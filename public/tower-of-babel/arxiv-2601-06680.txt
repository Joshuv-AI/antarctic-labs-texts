1.
Introduction

Unconditional direct sums provide one of the most natural mechanisms for constructing
large Banach spaces and Banach algebras from smaller building blocks.
If
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of Banach algebras and
E
is a Banach sequence lattice on
I
,
the
E
-sum
\bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigr)_{\!E}
inherits a canonical Banach algebra structure
through coordinatewise multiplication.
A fundamental question is then: which homological properties of the summands transfer to the sum,
and under what conditions?

The notion of amenability for Banach algebras, introduced by Johnson in his seminal
memoir
[
11
]
, has become one of the central themes in the
homological theory of Banach algebras.
Johnson’s celebrated theorem characterising amenable group algebras
L^{1}(G)
as precisely
those arising from amenable locally compact groups
G
established a deep link between
abstract harmonic analysis and Banach algebra cohomology.
Subsequent decades witnessed remarkable developments: Connes’ characterisation of
injective von Neumann algebras
[
4
]
, Haagerup’s proof that the reduced

C^{*}
-algebra of a free group is not amenable
[
8
]
,
and the systematic study of amenability constants initiated by
Johnson
[
12
]
and pursued by many authors;
see the monographs
[
9
,
5
,
18
,
19
]

for comprehensive treatments and further references.
More recently, numerous variants such as approximate amenability
[
6
]
,
character amenability
[
20
]
, and module amenability
[
1
]
have
been introduced and actively studied.

Johnson amenability
[
11
]
is a particularly robust homological invariant,
admitting an elegant characterisation via virtual diagonals in the bidual of the projective tensor product.
For the
c_{0}
-sum of a
sequence
of Banach algebras, a folklore result
(recorded in Runde’s monograph
[
19
]
) asserts that the amenability constant of the sum
equals the supremum of the amenability constants of the summands.
Our first objective is to provide a self-contained proof of this formula that applies verbatim
to arbitrary index sets—including uncountable ones—and, more generally,
to any Banach sequence lattice
E
satisfying the uniform boundedness condition

C_{E}:=\sup\bigl\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:F\subseteq I\text{ finite}\bigr\}<\infty.

This condition captures the requirement that
E
behaves uniformly like
c_{0}
on finite supports.
The extremal case
C_{E}=1
occurs precisely when
E=c_{0}(I)
with its usual supremum norm,
while the more general hypothesis
C_{E}<\infty
allows weighted variants and other (equivalent)
lattice renormings of
c_{0}(I)
.

Our main result (Theorem
4.1
) establishes that, under the hypothesis
C_{E}<\infty
,
the
E
-sum is amenable if and only if
\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})<\infty
,
with the quantitative estimate

\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\;\leqslant\;\mathrm{AM}(A)\;\leqslant\;C_{E}^{2}\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i}).

We prove that the constant
C_{E}^{2}
cannot, in general, be replaced by
C_{E}
:
for the finite
\ell_{2}^{n}
-sum of
n
copies of
\mathbb{C}
, one has
\mathrm{AM}(A)=n=C_{E}^{2}

(Proposition
5.10
).
Conversely, we show that amenability of an
E
-sum with infinitely many non-trivial unital summands
forces
C_{E}<\infty
(Proposition
4.3
), so the finiteness hypothesis is
essentially optimal.

A second goal of this paper is conceptual.
Certain constructions in Banach space theory are
conditional
rather than unconditional;
the prototypical example is Bellenot’s James-type
J
-sum
[
3
]
,
which underpins the Lindenstrauss construction of spaces
Y
with
Y^{**}/Y
isomorphic to a prescribed separable space. Even in such conditional settings, unconditional
E
-sums frequently appear as coordinate algebras or natural subalgebras.

We therefore formulate our results so that they can be readily ‘extracted’ as corollaries
whenever an unconditional
E
-sum sits inside (or is a quotient of) a larger conditional structure
(Corollary
6.11
).

We highlight explicit consequences for Banach-algebra-valued sequence algebras.
When the family
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
is constant,
i.e.
,
A_{i}=B
for all
i
, the
E
-sum
B^{(E)}
may be viewed as a tensor-product-type construction (Remark
5.5
).
Corollary
5.4
then provides a Banach-algebraic counterpart of results
in the spirit of Piszczek’s work on amenability of Köthe co-echelon algebras
[
16
]
.

A third goal is to investigate the extent to which these results have analogues for

weak amenability
: the property that every bounded derivation into the dual module is inner.
We prove that weak amenability passes from an
E
-sum to its coordinate algebras
(Proposition
7.5
), and that
E
-sums of commutative weakly amenable
algebras are weakly amenable (Theorem
7.6
(2)).
However, the picture for non-commutative summands is strikingly different from Johnson amenability:
for
1<p<\infty
, the
\ell_{p}
-sum of infinitely many copies of a noncommutative weakly amenable
algebra
fails
to be weakly amenable (Theorem
7.6
(3)),
a phenomenon first observed by Koczorowski and Piszczek
[
14
]

for constant families.

In the
c_{0}
-type regime (
C_{E}<\infty
), we establish a quantitative counterpart:
weak amenability of the
E
-sum is equivalent to uniform weak amenability of the summands,
with a two-sided estimate for the weak amenability constant (Corollary
7.8
).

Organisation.

.

-spaces, Orlicz sequence spaces, the sharpness of the constant

,
and constant families of summands.

discusses

-sums and explains how unconditional results

Section
7
develops the weak amenability theory for

-sums,
highlighting both the parallels with and the divergences from Johnson amenability.

Notation.

Throughout,
I
denotes a non-empty set and
\mathcal{F}(I)
the collection of its finite subsets.
We write
c_{00}(I)
for the space of finitely supported complex-valued functions on
I
,

\chi_{F}
for the characteristic function of

,
and

.

2.
Amenability constants and dense unions

We follow the standard formulation of Johnson amenability via virtual diagonals; see
[
11
,
18
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Virtual diagonal and amenability constant)
.

Let
A
be a Banach algebra and let
\pi\colon A\widehat{\otimes}A\to A
denote the product map
\pi(a\otimes b)=ab
.
We view
A\widehat{\otimes}A
as an
A
-bimodule via

a\cdot(x\otimes y)=(ax)\otimes y,\qquad(x\otimes y)\cdot a=x\otimes(ya),

and extend these actions canonically to
(A\widehat{\otimes}A)^{**}
.

An element
M\in(A\widehat{\otimes}A)^{**}
is called a
virtual diagonal
for
A
if

a\cdot M=M\cdot a\quad\text{and}\quad a\cdot\pi^{**}(M)=a=\pi^{**}(M)\cdot a\qquad(a\in A).

The algebra
A
is
amenable
if it admits a virtual diagonal.

If
A
is amenable, its
amenability constant
is

\mathrm{AM}(A):=\inf\bigl\{\|M\|\colon M\text{ is a virtual diagonal for }A\bigr\},

and we set
\mathrm{AM}(A)=+\infty
if
A
is not amenable.

Remark 2.2
.

If
A
is amenable, then the infimum in Definition
2.1
is attained, i.e. there exists a virtual diagonal
M
with
\|M\|=\mathrm{AM}(A)
.
Indeed, choose a net
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of virtual diagonals such that

\|M_{\alpha}\|\downarrow\mathrm{AM}(A)
. This net is bounded, so by Banach–Alaoglu
it has a weak-
*
cluster point
M
in
(A\hat{\otimes}A)^{**}
.
The defining conditions for a virtual diagonal are weak-
*
closed, hence
M

is again a virtual diagonal. Since the norm on a dual space is weak-
*
lower
semicontinuous, we have
\|M\|\leqslant\liminf_{\alpha}\|M_{\alpha}\|=\mathrm{AM}(A)
, and therefore

\|M\|=\mathrm{AM}(A)
. Compare
[
19
, Definition 2.2.7]
.

The following two lemmas are standard; we include short proofs for completeness.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q\colon A\to B
be a surjective continuous homomorphism of Banach algebras. Then

\mathrm{AM}(B)\leqslant\|q\|^{2}\,\mathrm{AM}(A).

Proof.

The map
q\widehat{\otimes}q\colon A\widehat{\otimes}A\to B\widehat{\otimes}B
is a surjective continuous homomorphism with

\|q\widehat{\otimes}q\|\leqslant\|q\|^{2}
, hence its bidual
(q\widehat{\otimes}q)^{**}
has the same norm bound.
If
M
is a virtual diagonal for
A
, then
N:=(q\widehat{\otimes}q)^{**}(M)
is a virtual diagonal for
B
:
the defining identities follow immediately from functoriality of module actions and the relation

\pi_{B}\circ(q\widehat{\otimes}q)=q\circ\pi_{A}
.
Therefore
\mathrm{AM}(B)\leqslant\|N\|\leqslant\|q\|^{2}\|M\|
, and taking the infimum over
M
gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Directed dense unions)
.

Let
A
be a Banach algebra and let
(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an increasing net of closed subalgebras,
indexed by a directed set
(\Lambda,\leqslant)
, such that
\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B_{\lambda}
is dense in
A
.
Then

\mathrm{AM}(A)\leqslant\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathrm{AM}(B_{\lambda}).

Proof.

This is the virtual-diagonal formulation of
[
19
, Proposition 2.3.15]
.

If the right-hand side is
+\infty
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, let
C<\infty
dominate

\mathrm{AM}(B_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda
.
Choose, for each
\lambda
, a virtual diagonal
M_{\lambda}
for
B_{\lambda}
with
\|M_{\lambda}\|=\mathrm{AM}(B_{\lambda})
.
Let
i_{\lambda}\colon B_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow A
denote the inclusion homomorphism, and set

\widetilde{M}_{\lambda}:=(i_{\lambda}\widehat{\otimes}i_{\lambda})^{**}(M_{\lambda})\in(A\widehat{\otimes}A)^{**}.

Then
\|\widetilde{M}_{\lambda}\|\leqslant\|M_{\lambda}\|\leqslant C
, so
(\widetilde{M}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is a bounded net in
(A\widehat{\otimes}A)^{**}
.
By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, it has a weak-
*
cluster point
M
with
\|M\|\leqslant C
.

Fix
b\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B_{\lambda}
and choose
\lambda_{0}
with
b\in B_{\lambda_{0}}
.
Then
b\in B_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
, so
b\cdot M_{\lambda}=M_{\lambda}\cdot b
and

b\cdot\pi^{**}(M_{\lambda})=b=\pi^{**}(M_{\lambda})\cdot b
for all
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
.
Passing to the weak-
*
limit yields the same identities with
M
in place of
M_{\lambda}
.

A standard density argument (approximating an arbitrary
a\in A
by elements of
\bigcup_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}
)
shows that
M
is a virtual diagonal for all of
A
. Hence
\mathrm{AM}(A)\leqslant\|M\|\leqslant C
.
∎

We will also need the following well-known finite-sum formula.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be Banach algebras and let
A:=A_{1}\oplus_{\infty}\cdots\oplus_{\infty}A_{n}

with coordinatewise multiplication and norm
\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\|=\max_{j}\|a_{j}\|
.
Then

\mathrm{AM}(A)=\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\mathrm{AM}(A_{j}).

Proof.

This is
[
19
, Lemma 2.3.18]
. (Runde proves the result using approximate diagonals.)
∎

3.
Unconditional
E
-sums of Banach algebras

We now specify the class of coefficient spaces
E
used to define unconditional sums.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I
be a set. A
Banach sequence lattice
on
I
is a Banach space
E

of complex-valued functions on
I
such that:

(a)

c_{00}(I)\subseteq E\subseteq\ell_{\infty}(I)
and
c_{00}(I)
is dense in
E
;

(b)

E
is
solid
: if
y\in E
and
x\in\ell_{\infty}(I)
satisfy

|x_{i}|\leqslant|y_{i}|
for all
i\in I
, then
x\in E
and
\|x\|_{E}\leqslant\|y\|_{E}
;

(c)

the inclusion
E\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}(I)
is contractive, i.e.
\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}

for all
x\in E
.

For each finite set
F\subseteq I
let
\chi_{F}:=\sum_{i\in F}\delta_{i}\in c_{00}(I)\subseteq E
and define

C_{E}:=\sup\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:F\subseteq I\text{ finite}\}\in[1,\infty].

Remark 3.2
.

If
J\subseteq I
, we write
E|J
for the Banach sequence lattice on
J
given by

E|J:=\{x|_{J}:\ x\in E\},\qquad\|u\|_{E|J}:=\inf\{\|x\|_{E}:\ x\in E,\ x|_{J}=u\}.

Then
c_{00}(J)
is dense in
E|J
, the inclusion
E|J\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}(J)
is contractive,
and

C_{E|J}=\sup\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:\ F\subseteq J\text{ finite}\}.

Remark 3.3
.

Assumption
(c)
is a convenient normalisation ensuring that the coordinate evaluations

x\mapsto x_{i}
have norm
\leqslant 1
on
E
, and hence coordinate projections in
E
-sums are contractive.
Many concrete examples satisfy
(c)
after a harmless global renorming.

Remark 3.4
.

The hypothesis
C_{E}<\infty
is equivalent to uniform control of the
E
-norm by the supremum norm on finitely
supported families:

\|x\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}\qquad(x\in c_{00}(I)).

Indeed, if
x
has support contained in
F
, then
|x|\leqslant\|x\|_{\infty}\chi_{F}
, so solidity yields

\|x\|_{E}\leqslant\|x\|_{\infty}\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
with
C_{E}<\infty
.
Then
E=c_{0}(I)
as sets, and for all
x\in E
one has

\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

The inequality
\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}
is Definition
3.1
(c).
For the upper bound, fix
x\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
.
By Definition
3.1
(a),
c_{00}(I)
is dense in
E
, so there exists

v\in c_{00}(I)
with
\|x-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon
.
Let
F:=\operatorname{supp}(v)
. Since
v
vanishes on
I\setminus F
, we have

x\chi_{I\setminus F}=(x-v)\chi_{I\setminus F}
, and hence by solidity

\|x-x\chi_{F}\|_{E}=\|x\chi_{I\setminus F}\|_{E}\leqslant\|x-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon.

Then, using Remark
3.4
for
x\chi_{F}\in c_{00}(I)
,

\|x\|_{E}\leqslant\|x-x\chi_{F}\|_{E}+\|x\chi_{F}\|_{E}\leqslant\varepsilon+C_{E}\|x\chi_{F}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon+C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives
\|x\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}
.

Finally,
E\subseteq c_{0}(I)
because if
x\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
, pick
v\in c_{00}(I)
with

\|x-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon
; then for
i\notin\operatorname{supp}(v)
we have

|x_{i}|=|x_{i}-v_{i}|\leqslant\|x-v\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon
.
Conversely, if
y\in c_{0}(I)
then
(y\chi_{F})_{F}\subset c_{00}(I)\subset E
is Cauchy in
E
because

\|y\chi_{F}-y\chi_{G}\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|y\chi_{F}-y\chi_{G}\|_{\infty}
, and
y\chi_{F}\to y
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
Thus
y\in E
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
.
Then
E
is a Banach algebra for pointwise multiplication, and

\|xy\|_{E}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}\,\|y\|_{E}\qquad(x,y\in E).

Proof.

Let
x\in E
and
y\in\ell_{\infty}(I)
. Then
|xy|\leqslant\|y\|_{\infty}|x|
pointwise, so by solidity

xy\in E
and

\|xy\|_{E}\leqslant\|\|y\|_{\infty}x\|_{E}=\|y\|_{\infty}\|x\|_{E}.

Thus
E
is an ideal in
\ell_{\infty}(I)
. If now
x,y\in E
, then by Definition
3.1
(c)
we have
\|y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|y\|_{E}
, and hence

\|xy\|_{E}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}\|y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}\|y\|_{E},

which is the desired submultiplicativity.
∎

We can now define unconditional sums of Banach spaces and, in particular, Banach algebras.

Definition 3.7

(
E
-sums)
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set, let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
, and let
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of Banach spaces.
The
E
-sum

X:=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i}\big)_{\!E}
consists of all families
x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
with
x_{i}\in X_{i}
such that

\bigl(\|x_{i}\|\bigr)_{i\in I}\in E,

equipped with the norm

\|x\|:=\bigl\|\bigl(\|x_{i}\|\bigr)_{i\in I}\bigr\|_{E}.

If each
X_{i}
is a Banach algebra, we endow
X
with coordinatewise multiplication:

(x_{i}y_{i})_{i\in I}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of Banach algebras.
Then
A:=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\big)_{\!E}
is a Banach algebra with coordinatewise multiplication, and

\|ab\|\leqslant\|a\|\,\|b\|\qquad(a,b\in A).

Moreover, for each
i\in I
, the coordinate projection
\pi_{i}\colon A\to A_{i}
is a contractive homomorphism,
and the coordinate embedding
\iota_{i}\colon A_{i}\to A
is a bounded homomorphism with

\|\iota_{i}\|=\|\delta_{i}\|_{E}.

Proof.

Let
a=(a_{i})
and
b=(b_{i})
lie in
A
.
Then
u:=(\|a_{i}\|)
and
v:=(\|b_{i}\|)
belong to
E
.
For each
i
we have
\|a_{i}b_{i}\|\leqslant\|a_{i}\|\,\|b_{i}\|
, hence

(\|a_{i}b_{i}\|)_{i\in I}\leqslant u\,v\quad\text{pointwise}.

By Lemma
3.6
,
uv\in E
and
\|uv\|_{E}\leqslant\|u\|_{E}\|v\|_{E}
.
Using solidity once more yields
(\|a_{i}b_{i}\|)\in E
and

\|ab\|=\|(\|a_{i}b_{i}\|)\|_{E}\leqslant\|uv\|_{E}\leqslant\|u\|_{E}\|v\|_{E}=\|a\|\,\|b\|.

For
\pi_{i}
, Definition
3.1
(c)
yields

\|\pi_{i}(a)\|=\|a_{i}\|\leqslant\bigl\|(\|a_{j}\|)_{j\in I}\bigr\|_{\infty}\leqslant\bigl\|(\|a_{j}\|)_{j\in I}\bigr\|_{E}=\|a\|\qquad(a=(a_{j})\in A),

so
\|\pi_{i}\|\leqslant 1
.
For
\iota_{i}
, if
b\in A_{i}
then
\iota_{i}(b)
is supported at
i
and

\|\iota_{i}(b)\|=\bigl\|(\|b\|\delta_{i})\bigr\|_{E}=\|b\|\,\|\delta_{i}\|_{E},

hence
\|\iota_{i}\|=\|\delta_{i}\|_{E}
.
∎

Lemma 3.9

(Finite-support subalgebras are dense)
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
and let
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
be Banach spaces.
For
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
let

X_{F}:=\{x\in(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i})_{E}:x_{i}=0\text{ for }i\notin F\}.

Then
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(I)}X_{F}
is dense in
(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i})_{E}
.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{i})
lie in
X:=(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i})_{E}
and set
u:=(\|x_{i}\|)\in E
.
Since
c_{00}(I)
is dense in
E
, there is
v\in c_{00}(I)
with
\|u-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon
.
Let
F:=\mathrm{supp}(v)
.
Then
u\chi_{F}-v=u\chi_{F}-v\chi_{F}=(u-v)\chi_{F}
, so solidity gives

\|u\chi_{F}-v\|_{E}\leqslant\|u-v\|_{E}<\varepsilon
.
Therefore,

\|x-x\chi_{F}\|=\|(\|x_{i}\|\chi_{I\setminus F})\|_{E}=\|u-u\chi_{F}\|_{E}\leqslant\|u-v\|_{E}+\|v-u\chi_{F}\|_{E}<2\varepsilon,

and
x\chi_{F}\in X_{F}
.
∎

4.
Amenability of unconditional
E
-sums

We can now state and prove our main result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set, let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
, and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of Banach algebras.
Assume that

C_{E}=\sup\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:F\in\mathcal{F}(I)\}<\infty.

Let
A:=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\big)_{\!E}
be the corresponding
E
-sum Banach algebra.
Then:

(1)

A
is amenable if and only if
\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})<\infty
.

(2)

In that case, one has the quantitative estimate

\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\ \leqslant\ \mathrm{AM}(A)\ \leqslant\ C_{E}^{2}\,\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i}).

Proof.

For each
i\in I
, the coordinate projection
\pi_{i}\colon A\to A_{i}
is a contractive surjective homomorphism
(Proposition
3.8
). Lemma
2.3
gives

\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\leqslant\mathrm{AM}(A)
, hence
\sup_{i}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\leqslant\mathrm{AM}(A)
.
In particular, if
\sup_{i}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})=+\infty
, then
\mathrm{AM}(A)=+\infty
and
A
is not amenable.

Assume now that
S:=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})<\infty
.
For each
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
let
A_{F}\subseteq A
denote the closed subalgebra of elements supported in
F
.
By Lemma
3.9
,
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(I)}A_{F}
is dense in
A
.

Fix
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
.
Let
B_{F}:=\bigoplus_{i\in F}A_{i}
equipped with the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm and coordinatewise multiplication.
By Lemma
2.5
,

\mathrm{AM}(B_{F})=\max_{i\in F}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\leqslant S.

Consider the identity map
q_{F}\colon B_{F}\to A_{F}
(same underlying vectors, different norms).
For
b=(b_{i})_{i\in F}\in B_{F}
we have

\|q_{F}(b)\|_{A_{F}}=\|(\|b_{i}\|)_{i\in F}\|_{E}\leqslant\|b\|_{\infty}\,\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\,\|b\|_{\infty},

where the key step uses solidity and
|(\|b_{i}\|)|\leqslant\|b\|_{\infty}\chi_{F}
.
Hence
\|q_{F}\|\leqslant C_{E}
, and
q_{F}
is a surjective homomorphism.
Lemma
2.3
yields

\mathrm{AM}(A_{F})\leqslant\|q_{F}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{AM}(B_{F})\leqslant C_{E}^{2}\,S.

Finally, Lemma
2.4
applied to the dense family
(A_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}(I)}
gives

\mathrm{AM}(A)\leqslant\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(I)}\mathrm{AM}(A_{F})\leqslant C_{E}^{2}\,S<\infty,

so
A
is amenable and the stated two-sided estimate holds.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
C_{E}=1
then Theorem
4.1
yields the exact formula

\mathrm{AM}(A)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})
.
This occurs, for instance, for
E=c_{0}(I)
with the usual supremum norm.

The next proposition shows that the finiteness of
C_{E}
is close to optimal in typical situations.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
I
be infinite, let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
, and let

(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of Banach algebras. Set

A:=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\big)_{\!E}
.
Assume that
A
has a bounded right approximate identity
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
with

\sup_{\alpha}\|e_{\alpha}\|\leqslant M.

Let
J\subseteq I
be such that
A_{j}\neq\{0\}
for all
j\in J
.
Then

\sup\bigl\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:\ F\in\mathcal{F}(I),\ F\subseteq J\bigr\}\leqslant 2M.

In particular, if
A
is amenable then the above supremum is finite for every such
J
;
and if moreover
A_{i}\neq\{0\}
for all
i\in I
, then
C_{E}<\infty
.

Proof.

Fix
j\in J
and choose
a_{j}\in A_{j}
with
\|a_{j}\|=1
.
Let
u_{j}\in A
be supported at
j
with coordinate
u_{j}(j)=a_{j}
.
Then
u_{j}\in A
is supported at
j
and
\|u_{j}\|=\|\delta_{j}\|_{E}
.
Since
(e_{\alpha})
is an approximate identity,

\|u_{j}-u_{j}e_{\alpha}\|\longrightarrow 0.

Because multiplication is coordinatewise,
(u_{j}e_{\alpha})(j)=a_{j}\,\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})
, hence

\|a_{j}-a_{j}\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\longrightarrow 0\qquad(j\in J).

Now fix
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
with
F\subseteq J
.
Choose
\alpha
so large that
\|a_{j}-a_{j}\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
j\in F
.
Then

\|a_{j}\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\geqslant\|a_{j}\|-\|a_{j}-a_{j}\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\qquad(j\in F).

By submultiplicativity,
\|a_{j}\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\leqslant\|a_{j}\|\,\|\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|=\|\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|
,
so
\|\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
j\in F
.

Let
P_{F}\colon A\to A
denote the coordinate projection onto
F
.
By solidity,

\|P_{F}(e_{\alpha})\|=\bigl\|(\|\pi_{j}(e_{\alpha})\|)_{j\in F}\bigr\|_{E}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\,\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}.

Since
\|P_{F}(e_{\alpha})\|\leqslant\|e_{\alpha}\|\leqslant M
, we obtain
\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}\leqslant 2M
.
Taking the supremum over all
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
with
F\subseteq J
gives the claim.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
A=(\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}
and let
J:=\{i\in I:A_{i}\neq\{0\}\}
.
Assume that
J
is infinite. Then
A
is amenable if and only if

C_{E|J}<\infty
and
\sup_{i\in J}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})<\infty
.

Proof.

If
A
is amenable, then each
A_{i}
is a quotient of
A
via the coordinate projection,
so
\sup_{i\in J}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\leqslant\mathrm{AM}(A)<\infty
. Moreover, Proposition
4.3

yields
C_{E|J}<\infty
.

Theorem
4.1
applied to the restriction
E|J
shows that
(\oplus_{i\in J}A_{i})_{E|J}
is amenable. Since
A
is canonically (isometrically) identifiable with
(\oplus_{i\in J}A_{i})_{E|J}
(the other coordinates being
0
), it follows that
A
is amenable.
∎

5.
Consequences and examples

We record several consequences.

5.1.
The
c_{0}
-sum over arbitrary index sets

Corollary 5.1

(
c_{0}
-sums, possibly uncountable)
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of Banach algebras.
Set
A:=c_{0}\text{-}\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
, i.e. the
E
-sum with
E=c_{0}(I)
and the supremum norm.
Then

\mathrm{AM}(A)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i}).

In particular,
A
is amenable if and only if the family
(\mathrm{AM}(A_{i}))_{i\in I}
is bounded.

Proof.

For comparison, this identity is proved in
[
19
, Proposition 2.3.19]
.
For
E=c_{0}(I)
we have
C_{E}=1
, so the conclusion follows from Theorem
4.1
and Remark
4.2
.
∎

5.2.
Weighted
c_{0}
-sums

Weighted
c_{0}
-norms provide a simple family of coefficient lattices where the
constant
C_{E}
is completely explicit and captures the uniformity of the weights.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set and let
w=(w_{i})_{i\in I}
satisfy
w_{i}>0
for all
i
.
Define

E=c_{0}(I,w):=\Bigl\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{C}^{I}:\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \{i\in I:\ w_{i}|x_{i}|\geqslant\varepsilon\}\in\mathrm{Fin}\Bigr\},\qquad\|x\|_{E}:=\sup_{i\in I}w_{i}|x_{i}|.

Then:

(a)

One has
E\subseteq\ell_{\infty}(I)
if and only if
\inf_{i\in I}w_{i}>0
.
Moreover,
E
is a Banach sequence lattice on
I
in the sense of
Definition
3.1
if and only if
\inf_{i\in I}w_{i}\geqslant 1
.

(b)

For every finite
F\subset I
,

\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}=\max_{i\in F}w_{i},\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad C_{E}=\sup_{i\in I}w_{i}\in[1,\infty].

(c)

Assume in addition that
\inf_{i\in I}w_{i}\geqslant 1
and let

A=\bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\bigr)_{E}

be the corresponding
E
-sum Banach algebra. Then

\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\ \leqslant\ \mathrm{AM}(A)\ \leqslant\ C_{E}^{2}\,\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})\ =\ \Bigl(\sup_{i\in I}w_{i}\Bigr)^{2}\,\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i}).

In particular, if
\sup_{i}w_{i}<\infty
and
\sup_{i}\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})<\infty
, then
A
is amenable.
If
I
is infinite and each
A_{i}
is unital, then amenability of
A
forces
\sup_{i}w_{i}<\infty
.

Proof.

(a) If
m:=\inf_{i}w_{i}>0
and
x\in E
, then
|x_{i}|\leqslant m^{-1}\|x\|_{E}
for all
i
, so
x\in\ell_{\infty}(I)
.
Conversely, if
\inf_{i}w_{i}=0
, pick
(i_{n})
with
w_{i_{n}}<1/n^{2}
and set
x_{i_{n}}=n
and
x_{i}=0
otherwise.
Then
w_{i}|x_{i}|\to 0
but
x\notin\ell_{\infty}(I)
, so
E\nsubseteq\ell_{\infty}(I)
.
The inclusion
E\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}(I)
is contractive, i.e.
\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}
for all
x\in E
,
if and only if
w_{i}\geqslant 1
for all
i
(test
x=\delta_{i}
and use
|x_{i}|\leqslant w_{i}|x_{i}|
).
This yields the characterisation of when
E
is a Banach sequence lattice.

(b) For finite
F
,
\chi_{F}(i)=1
on
F
and
0
otherwise, hence

\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}=\sup_{i}w_{i}|\chi_{F}(i)|=\max_{i\in F}w_{i}
,
and taking the supremum over
F
gives
C_{E}=\sup_{i}w_{i}
.

(c) Under
\inf_{i}w_{i}\geqslant 1
we have
E
a Banach sequence lattice and
C_{E}=\sup_{i}w_{i}
by (a)–(b).
The estimate for
\mathrm{AM}(A)
is then exactly Theorem
4.1
.
If moreover
I
is infinite and all
A_{i}
are unital, amenability of
A
implies that
A
has a bounded approximate identity,
so Proposition
4.3
forces
C_{E}<\infty
, i.e.
\sup_{i}w_{i}<\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

With the convention
\|x\|_{E}=\sup_{i}w_{i}|x_{i}|
, one has
\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}=\max_{i\in F}w_{i}
and

C_{E}=\sup_{i}w_{i}
. If instead one defines the weighted norm by

\|x\|=\sup_{i}|x_{i}|/w_{i}
, then
\|\chi_{F}\|=\max_{i\in F}1/w_{i}
and
C_{E}=\sup_{i}1/w_{i}
.

5.3.
Constant families of summands

We now single out an explicit statement for Banach-algebra-valued sequence algebras and, more generally,
for constant families of summands. This is the unconditional
E
-sum analogue one typically wants to

see stated plainly
, even if it appears in a longer discussion (for instance within a
J
-sum setting).

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set, let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
with
C_{E}<\infty
, and let
B
be a Banach algebra.
Consider the constant family
A_{i}=B
(
i\in I
) and set

B^{(E)}:=\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}B\Big)_{\!E}.

Then
B^{(E)}
is amenable if and only if
B
is amenable. Moreover,

\mathrm{AM}(B)\ \leqslant\ \mathrm{AM}\bigl(B^{(E)}\bigr)\ \leqslant\ C_{E}^{2}\,\mathrm{AM}(B).

In the particular case
E=c_{0}(I)
one has the exact identity
\mathrm{AM}(B^{(c_{0})})=\mathrm{AM}(B)
.

Proof.

This is the special case of Theorem
4.1
with
\mathrm{AM}(A_{i})=\mathrm{AM}(B)
for all
i
.
For
E=c_{0}(I)
we have
C_{E}=1
, hence equality.
∎

Remark 5.5

(A tensor-product viewpoint)
.

Equip
E
with pointwise multiplication (Lemma
3.6
).
For any Banach algebra
B
, the bilinear map

E\times B\longrightarrow B^{(E)},\qquad(x,b)\longmapsto(x_{i}b)_{i\in I},

is contractive and multiplicative, hence induces a contractive algebra homomorphism
from the projective tensor product
E\widehat{\otimes}B
into
B^{(E)}
whose range contains the
finitely supported sequences and therefore is dense.
In the special cases
E=\ell^{1}(I)
and
E=c_{0}(I)
one recovers the classical identifications

\ell^{1}(I,B)\cong\ell^{1}(I)\widehat{\otimes}B
and
c_{0}(I,B)\cong c_{0}(I)\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}B

(with the injective tensor product
\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}
), situating
Corollary
5.4
alongside the standard tensor-product permanence results.

5.4.
Non-
c_{0}
behaviour and Orlicz spaces

A standard obstruction to amenability is the absence of a bounded approximate identity.
The next corollary illustrates how Proposition
4.3
rules out amenability for many
classical unconditional sequence norms when infinitely many unital summands are present.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
I
be infinite and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be unital Banach algebras with
\|1_{A_{i}}\|=1
for all
i
.
If
E
is a Banach sequence lattice on
I
with
C_{E}=+\infty
, then the
E
-sum

A=(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}
is not amenable.
In particular, for
1\leqslant p<\infty
, the algebra
(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{\ell_{p}(I)}
is not amenable.

Proof.

If
A
were amenable, it would admit a bounded approximate identity; this would force
C_{E}<\infty
by
Proposition
4.3
, a contradiction.
For
E=\ell_{p}(I)
(
1\leqslant p<\infty
) and
I
infinite, one has
\|\chi_{F}\|_{\ell_{p}}=|F|^{1/p}
, hence
C_{E}=+\infty
.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Orlicz hearts and the constant
C_{E}
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an Orlicz function and let
E=h_{\varphi}(I)
be the Orlicz
heart (over counting measure on
I
), equipped with the Luxemburg norm

\|x\|_{\varphi}:=\inf\Bigl\{\lambda>0:\ \sum_{i\in I}\varphi\bigl(|x_{i}|/\lambda\bigr)\leqslant 1\Bigr\},

where
\sum_{i\in I}
denotes the supremum of finite subsums. Let
\varphi^{-1}
denote the generalised inverse

\varphi^{-1}(s):=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:\ \varphi(t)\geqslant s\}\qquad(s\geqslant 0).

Then:

(a)

For each
i\in I
,
\|\delta_{i}\|_{\varphi}=1/\varphi^{-1}(1)
.

(b)

For every finite set
F\subset I
with
|F|=n
,

\|\chi_{F}\|_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{\varphi^{-1}(1/n)}.

Consequently,

C_{E}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subset I\\
|F|<\infty\end{subarray}}\|\chi_{F}\|_{\varphi}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\varphi^{-1}(1/n)}\ \in\ (0,\infty]\quad\text{provided $I$ is infinite.}

(c)

Assume that
I
is infinite. Then
C_{E}<\infty
if and only if
\varphi
vanishes on a neighbourhood of
0
, i.e.

a_{\varphi}:=\sup\{t>0:\ \varphi(t)=0\}>0.

In that case
C_{E}=1/a_{\varphi}
. If
\varphi(t)>0
for all
t>0
(in particular, for any
N
-function), then
C_{E}=+\infty
.

Proof.

(a) For
\delta_{i}
, the defining inequality becomes
\varphi(1/\lambda)\leqslant 1
, i.e.
1/\lambda\leqslant\varphi^{-1}(1)
.

(b) If
F
is finite with
|F|=n
, then

\sum_{i\in I}\varphi\bigl(|\chi_{F}(i)|/\lambda\bigr)=\sum_{i\in F}\varphi(1/\lambda)=n\,\varphi(1/\lambda).

Thus
\|\chi_{F}\|_{\varphi}
is the infimum of
\lambda>0
such that
n\,\varphi(1/\lambda)\leqslant 1
,
equivalently
\varphi(1/\lambda)\leqslant 1/n
, i.e.
1/\lambda\leqslant\varphi^{-1}(1/n)
.

(c) Since
1/n\downarrow 0
and
\varphi^{-1}
is non-decreasing,

\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi^{-1}(1/n)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{-1}(1/n)=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\varphi^{-1}(s)=:a_{\varphi}.

Hence
C_{E}<\infty
iff
a_{\varphi}>0
, and then
\sup_{n}1/\varphi^{-1}(1/n)=1/a_{\varphi}
.
If
\varphi(t)>0
for all
t>0
, then
\varphi^{-1}(s)\downarrow 0
as
s\downarrow 0
, so
C_{E}=\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.8

(Connection with
\Delta_{2}
at
0
)
.

For Orlicz sequence spaces, the condition that
c_{00}(I)
be dense in
\ell_{\varphi}(I)
is
equivalent to a
\Delta_{2}
-type condition at
0
on
\varphi
(and in any case
h_{\varphi}(I)
is,
by definition, the closure of
c_{00}(I)
). The constant
C_{E}
is typically infinite for the usual
Orlicz functions (
e.g.
,
N
-functions), since these satisfy
\varphi(t)>0
for all
t>0
.

Remark 5.9
.

Corollary
5.6
and Proposition
5.7
highlight the rigidity of
Johnson

amenability for sequence-type constructions: for typical spaces (like
\ell_{p}
), the growth of
\|\chi_{F}\|

forces the failure of a bounded approximate identity
(and hence amenability) as soon as infinitely many non-zero unital coordinates are present.
By contrast,
weak amenability
(vanishing of continuous derivations into the dual)
may persist for Banach-algebra-valued sequence algebras under additional hypotheses;
see, for instance,
[
14
]
, where weak amenability of

c_{0}(A)
and
\ell_{p}(A)
is characterised for arbitrary Banach algebras
A
.

5.5.
Sharpness of the factor
C_{E}^{2}

Proposition 5.10
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E=\ell_{2}^{n}
(with its usual norm) viewed as a Banach
sequence lattice on
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
A_{i}=\mathbb{C}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
Then

A:=\Big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\Big)_{E}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{n},\ \|\cdot\|_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{AM}(A)=n=C_{E}^{2}.

In particular, the constant
C_{E}^{2}
in Theorem
4.1

(and hence in Corollary
5.4
) cannot, in general, be replaced by

C_{E}
.

Proof.

Identify
A
with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with pointwise multiplication and
\ell_{2}
-norm.
Write
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
for the standard coordinate idempotents.

Step 1: uniqueness of the diagonal.

Let
d=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}\,e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\in A\hat{\otimes}A

satisfy
a\cdot d=d\cdot a
for all
a\in A
.
For
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
we have

a\cdot d-d\cdot a=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}\,(a_{i}-a_{j})\,e_{i}\otimes e_{j},

so
\alpha_{ij}=0
whenever
i\neq j
. Hence
d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{ii}\,e_{i}\otimes e_{i}
.
The diagonal identity
\pi(d)=1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
forces
\alpha_{ii}=1
,
so the (necessarily unique) diagonal is

d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}.

Step 2: computation of
\|d\|_{\gamma}
.

Since
\|e_{i}\|_{2}=1
for each
i
, we have the crude bound

\|d\|_{\gamma}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|e_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=n.

For the reverse inequality, use the canonical isometric identification

(A\hat{\otimes}A)^{*}\cong\mathcal{B}(A,A^{*})
.
Let
J:A\to A^{*}
be the Riesz map
J(x)=\langle\,\cdot\,,x\rangle
associated
with the
\ell_{2}
inner product; then
\|J\|=1
.
Denote by
\phi_{J}\in(A\hat{\otimes}A)^{*}
the functional corresponding to
J
.
We obtain

\langle d,\phi_{J}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle=n,

so
\|d\|_{\gamma}\geqslant|\langle d,\phi_{J}\rangle|=n
.
Hence
\|d\|_{\gamma}=n
, and since
d
is the unique diagonal,
\mathrm{AM}(A)=\|d\|_{\gamma}=n
.

Finally,
C_{E}=\sup_{F\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\chi_{F}\|_{2}=\sqrt{n}
, so
n=C_{E}^{2}
.
∎

6.
James-type
J
-sums of Banach algebras

The classical James space
J

[
10
]
is the first explicit example of a
non-reflexive Banach space isomorphic to its bidual.
Lindenstrauss
[
15
]
showed that James’ construction can be
adapted to realise a given separable Banach space as a quotient
Y^{**}/Y
for a
suitable
Y
with a shrinking monotone basis. Bellenot
[
3
]

introduced a flexible framework—the
J
-sum
—subsuming both James’ and
Lindenstrauss’ constructions. A Banach-algebraic variant was studied by Andrew
and Green
[
2
]
(for
J
itself) and, for
J
-sums, by Kiran and
Singh
[
13
]
.

In this section we recall the Bellenot–Lindenstrauss definition, record the
basic structural facts needed later, and explain how our results on unconditional

E
-sums apply
even when such sums appear only as building blocks inside a
conditional
J
-sum
.

6.1.
The Bellenot construction

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
\mathcal{P}
denote the family of all non-empty
finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and let
(X_{n},\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of
Banach spaces with
\dim X_{0}=0
(so
X_{0}=\{0\}
).
Let
\Phi=(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of operators

\varphi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
with
\|\varphi_{n}\|\leqslant 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
For
n\leqslant m
in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
set

\varphi_{m}^{n}:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Id}_{X_{n}},&m=n,\\
\varphi_{m-1}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{n+1}\circ\varphi_{n},&m>n.\end{cases}

Write
\Pi:=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}X_{n}
for the Cartesian product.

Definition 6.1

(
[
3
, Def. 2.1]
)
.

For
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\Pi
and
S=\{p_{0}<\cdots<p_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}
define

(1)

\displaystyle\sigma(x,S)

\displaystyle:=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big\|\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(x_{p_{i-1}})-x_{p_{i}}\big\|_{p_{i}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(2)

\displaystyle\rho(x,S)

\displaystyle:=\Big(\sigma(x,S)^{2}+\|x_{p_{k}}\|_{p_{k}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Then

(3)

\|x\|_{J}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sup_{S\in\mathcal{P}}\rho(x,S)\in[0,\infty].

Let
c_{00}(\Phi)\subseteq\Pi
be the space of finitely non-zero sequences.
The
J
-sum

J(\Phi)
is the completion of
(c_{00}(\Phi),\|\cdot\|_{J})
.
We also set

\widehat{J}(\Phi):=\{x\in\Pi:\ \|x\|_{J}<\infty\}.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
x\in\widehat{J}(\Phi)
and
S\in\mathcal{P}
one has

\sigma(x,S)\leqslant\rho(x,S)\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_{J}.

Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have the coordinate estimate

(4)

\|x_{n}\|_{n}\leqslant\|x\|_{J}.

Proof.

The first inequality is immediate from (
2
).
The second follows from (
3
).
For (
4
), fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and take
S=\{0,n\}
.
Since
x_{0}=0
and
\varphi_{n}^{0}(x_{0})=0
, (
1
) gives

\sigma(x,S)=\|x_{n}\|_{n}
, hence
\rho(x,S)=\sqrt{2}\|x_{n}\|_{n}
.
Now (
3
) yields
\|x\|_{J}\geqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\rho(x,S)=\|x_{n}\|_{n}
.
∎

Bellenot showed that
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a bimonotone decomposition of

J(\Phi)
; see,
e.g.
,
[
7
, Prop. 2.1]
for a clean modern
account.

Proposition 6.3

(Bimonotone decomposition
[
3
]
)
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
the map
X_{m}\to J(\Phi)
sending
u\in X_{m}
to the sequence

(x_{n})
with
x_{m}=u
and
x_{n}=0
for
n\neq m
is an isometric embedding.
With this identification,
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bimonotone decomposition of

J(\Phi)
; in particular,

\Big\|\sum_{i=p+1}^{p+q}x_{i}\Big\|_{J}\leqslant\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{p+q+r}x_{i}\Big\|_{J}\qquad(x\in J(\Phi),\ p,q,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

and hence
\|x\|_{J}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big\|_{J}
for
x\in J(\Phi)
.

The ambient space
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
admits a canonical quotient description
in terms of “eventually coherent” sequences.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\Omega(\Phi)
be the space of
eventually coherent
sequences

\Omega(\Phi):=\Big\{x=(x_{n})\in\Pi:\ \exists n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall m\geqslant n_{0}:\ \varphi_{m}(x_{m})=x_{m+1}\Big\}.

For
x\in\Omega(\Phi)
the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{n}
exists, and

\|x\|_{\Omega}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{n}

defines a seminorm on
\Omega(\Phi)
.
Let
\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
denote the completion of

\Omega(\Phi)/\ker\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
.

We will use below that, in the Banach-algebra setting of Subsection
6.2
,

\Omega(\Phi)
is a subalgebra and
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
is submultiplicative; see Proposition
6.8
.

Theorem 6.5

(Bellenot
[
3
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

(1)

\Omega(\Phi)
is dense in
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
.

(2)

The natural map
\Omega(\Phi)\to\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
extends uniquely
to a quotient map

\Theta\colon\widehat{J}(\Phi)\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)

whose kernel is
J(\Phi)
.

(3)

If each
X_{n}
is reflexive, then
J(\Phi)^{**}=\widehat{J}(\Phi)
isometrically,
hence
J(\Phi)^{**}/J(\Phi)
is isometric to
\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
.

6.2.
J
-sums as Banach algebras

We now assume that each
X_{n}
is a Banach algebra
(A_{n},\|\cdot\|_{n})
and that
each bonding map
\varphi_{n}\colon A_{n}\to A_{n+1}
is a contractive algebra
homomorphism.

Definition 6.6
.

Under the preceding hypotheses, we endow
c_{00}(\Phi)
with coordinatewise
multiplication

(x_{n})\cdot(y_{n}):=(x_{n}y_{n})\qquad(x,y\in c_{00}(\Phi)).

We call the resulting completion
J(\Phi)
the
J
-sum Banach algebra

associated with
(A_{n},\varphi_{n})
.
Similarly,
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
and
\Omega(\Phi)
become algebras by
coordinatewise multiplication.

Lemma 6.7

(Continuity of multiplication)
.

For
x,y\in c_{00}(\Phi)
and
S=\{p_{0}<\cdots<p_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}
one has

(5)

\sigma(xy,S)\leqslant\|y\|_{\infty}\,\sigma(x,S)+\|x\|_{\infty}\,\sigma(y,S),

where
\|x\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|_{n}
.
Consequently, multiplication extends to
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
and
J(\Phi)
and

(6)

\|xy\|_{J}\leqslant\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\,\|x\|_{J}\,\|y\|_{J}\qquad(x,y\in J(\Phi)).

Proof.

Fix
S=\{p_{0}<\cdots<p_{k}\}
. Since each
\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}
is a homomorphism,

\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(x_{p_{i-1}}y_{p_{i-1}})-x_{p_{i}}y_{p_{i}}=\big(\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(x_{p_{i-1}})-x_{p_{i}}\big)\,\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(y_{p_{i-1}})+x_{p_{i}}\big(\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(y_{p_{i-1}})-y_{p_{i}}\big).

Taking norms, using
\|\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}\|\leqslant 1
and the submultiplicativity
of
\|\cdot\|_{p_{i}}
gives

\big\|\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(x_{p_{i-1}}y_{p_{i-1}})-x_{p_{i}}y_{p_{i}}\big\|_{p_{i}}\leqslant\|y\|_{\infty}\,\big\|\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(x_{p_{i-1}})-x_{p_{i}}\big\|_{p_{i}}+\|x\|_{\infty}\,\big\|\varphi_{p_{i}}^{p_{i-1}}(y_{p_{i-1}})-y_{p_{i}}\big\|_{p_{i}}.

Now (
5
) follows by taking the
\ell_{2}
-sum over
i
and using
Minkowski’s inequality.

For (
6
), let
x,y\in c_{00}(\Phi)
and
S\in\mathcal{P}
.
By Lemma
6.2
we have
\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{J}
and

\|y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|y\|_{J}
, and also
\sigma(x,S)\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_{J}
and

\sigma(y,S)\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|y\|_{J}
.
Thus (
5
) yields

\sigma(xy,S)\leqslant 2\sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J}.

Moreover,
\|(xy)_{p_{k}}\|_{p_{k}}\leqslant\|x\|_{\infty}\|y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J}
.
Hence, by (
2
) and the above bounds,

\rho(xy,S)\leqslant\Big((2\sqrt{2}\,\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J})^{2}+(\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=3\,\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J}.

Taking suprema over
S
and using (
3
) gives

\|xy\|_{J}\leqslant\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\|x\|_{J}\|y\|_{J}
on
c_{00}(\Phi)
, and continuity
extends multiplication to
J(\Phi)
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Assume the hypotheses of Definition
6.6
. Then:

(a)

\Omega(\Phi)
is a subalgebra of
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
for coordinatewise multiplication, and

\ker\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
is a (two-sided) ideal in
\Omega(\Phi)
.

(b)

The seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
is submultiplicative on
\Omega(\Phi)
.
Consequently,
\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
is a Banach algebra.

(c)

The quotient map
\Theta\colon\widehat{J}(\Phi)\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)

from Theorem
6.5
is an algebra homomorphism.

In particular, one has a short exact sequence of Banach algebras

0\longrightarrow J(\Phi)\longrightarrow\widehat{J}(\Phi)\overset{\Theta}{\longrightarrow}\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

(a) Let
x,y\in\Omega(\Phi)
. Choose
n_{0}
such that
\varphi_{m}(x_{m})=x_{m+1}
and
\varphi_{m}(y_{m})=y_{m+1}

for all
m\geqslant n_{0}
. Since each
\varphi_{m}
is an algebra homomorphism,

\varphi_{m}(x_{m}y_{m})=\varphi_{m}(x_{m})\varphi_{m}(y_{m})=x_{m+1}y_{m+1}\qquad(m\geqslant n_{0}),

so
xy\in\Omega(\Phi)
. If
x\in\ker\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
and
y\in\Omega(\Phi)
, then

\|x_{n}\|_{n}\to 0
and
\|y_{n}\|_{n}\to\|y\|_{\Omega}<\infty
, hence

\|x_{n}y_{n}\|_{n}\leqslant\|x_{n}\|_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{n}\to 0
, and similarly
\|y_{n}x_{n}\|_{n}\to 0
, so
\ker\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}

is a two-sided ideal.

(b) For
x,y\in\Omega(\Phi)
, the limits
\|x\|_{\Omega}=\lim_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{n}
and
\|y\|_{\Omega}=\lim_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{n}
exist.
By coordinatewise submultiplicativity,
\|x_{n}y_{n}\|_{n}\leqslant\|x_{n}\|_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{n}
for all
n
, and taking limits gives

\|xy\|_{\Omega}=\lim_{n}\|x_{n}y_{n}\|_{n}\leqslant\Big(\lim_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{n}\Big)\Big(\lim_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{n}\Big)=\|x\|_{\Omega}\|y\|_{\Omega}.

Thus
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
is submultiplicative, so the induced norm on
\Omega(\Phi)/\ker\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}

and its completion
\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
are submultiplicative.

(c) The natural map
\Omega(\Phi)\to\widetilde{\Omega}(\Phi)
is multiplicative by (a)–(b), hence extends by continuity
to an algebra homomorphism
\Theta
on
\widehat{J}(\Phi)
via Theorem
6.5
.
∎

A key feature for applications is that
each coordinate algebra is a
quotient of the
J
-sum
.

Proposition 6.9
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the coordinate projection

\pi_{n}\colon J(\Phi)\to A_{n},\qquad\pi_{n}((x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}})=x_{n},

is a contractive surjective homomorphism.

Proof.

Surjectivity follows because
A_{n}
is isometrically embedded in
J(\Phi)
as the
subspace of sequences supported at
n
(Proposition
6.3
).
Contractivity is (
4
). The homomorphism property is
coordinatewise.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

If
J(\Phi)
is amenable, then each
A_{n}
is amenable and

\mathrm{AM}(A_{n})\leqslant\mathrm{AM}(J(\Phi))\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

where
\mathrm{AM}(\cdot)
denotes the amenability constant.

Proof.

Amenability passes to quotients, and the estimate for amenability constants under
quotients gives
\mathrm{AM}(A_{n})\leqslant\|\pi_{n}\|^{2}\mathrm{AM}(J(\Phi))
.
Now use Proposition
6.9
.
∎

6.3.
Unconditional
E
-sums ‘packed’ inside
J
-sums

We now spell out the point relevant to this paper:
even though
J(\Phi)
is
a conditional sum, it often contains (or maps onto) unconditional
E
-sums, and
hence the main results of this paper apply automatically
.

Corollary 6.11

(Unconditional
E
-sums detected from a
J
-sum)
.

Assume that, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Banach algebra
A_{n}
is (isomorphic to) an

E
-sum

A_{n}\cong\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}B_{i}\Big)_{E}

for a (possibly uncountable) index set
I
, where
E
is a Banach sequence lattice
on
I
in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
If
J(\Phi)
is amenable, then
A_{n}
is amenable and all consequences of
Theorem
4.1
(applied to the above
E
-sum) hold. In particular:

(1)

if there exists an infinite subset
J\subseteq I
such that each
B_{i}
(
i\in J
) is
non-zero and unital, and

\sup\bigl\{\|\chi_{F}\|_{E}:\ F\in\mathcal{F}(I),\ F\subseteq J\bigr\}=+\infty

(e.g.
E=\ell_{p}(I)
for
1\leqslant p<\infty
and
J
infinite), then
J(\Phi)
cannot be amenable;

(2)

if
C_{E}<\infty
, then amenability of
J(\Phi)
forces a uniform bound on the
amenability constants of the
B_{i}
as in Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.9
,
A_{n}
is a contractive quotient of

J(\Phi)
. Hence, if
J(\Phi)
is amenable then
A_{n}
is amenable and

\mathrm{AM}(A_{n})\leqslant\mathrm{AM}(J(\Phi))
. The rest follows by applying
Theorem
4.1
to the unconditional
E
-sum representation of
A_{n}
.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

Piszczek obtained sharp criteria for amenability and contractibility of certain
Köthe co-echelon (DF) sequence algebras; see
[
16
]
and the
surveying discussion in
[
17
]
. Although the settings are
different (locally convex DF-algebras versus Banach algebras), the mechanism in
Corollary
6.11
is analogous in spirit:
amenability
imposes strong uniformity constraints on the underlying sequence structure
.
The point here is that such unconditional constraints survive even inside a
conditional
J
-sum, because they can be recovered by passing to an appropriate
coordinate quotient.

7.
Weak amenability of unconditional
E
-sums

This section records a weak-amenability counterpart of the
\ell_{p}(A)
/
c_{0}(A)
result
highlighted in
[
14
]
.
We work throughout with arbitrary index sets and unconditional
E
-sums.

Definition 7.1

(Weak amenability)
.

Let
A
be a Banach algebra.
A bounded linear map
D\colon A\to A^{*}
is a
derivation
if

D(ab)=a\cdot D(b)+D(a)\cdot b\qquad(a,b\in A),

where the canonical
A
-bimodule structure on
A^{*}
is given by

(a\cdot\phi)(x):=\phi(xa),\qquad(\phi\cdot a)(x):=\phi(ax)\qquad(a,x\in A,\ \phi\in A^{*}).

For
\phi\in A^{*}
the
inner derivation
implemented by
\phi
is

\mathrm{ad}_{\phi}(a):=a\cdot\phi-\phi\cdot a\qquad(a\in A).

The Banach algebra
A
is
weakly amenable
if every bounded derivation
D\colon A\to A^{*}
is inner.

Remark 7.2
.

If
A
is commutative, then
a\cdot\phi=\phi\cdot a
for all
a\in A
and
\phi\in A^{*}
, hence
\mathrm{ad}_{\phi}=0
for all
\phi
.
Therefore, for commutative
A
, weak amenability is equivalent to the vanishing of all bounded derivations
A\to A^{*}
.

Definition 7.3

(A weak amenability constant (optional))
.

Let
A
be a Banach algebra. Define

\mathrm{WAM}(A):=\inf\Bigl\{C\geqslant 0:\ \forall D\colon A\to A^{*}\text{ derivation }\exists\phi\in A^{*}\text{ s.t. }D=\mathrm{ad}_{\phi},\|\phi\|\leqslant C\|D\|\Bigr\}\in[0,\infty],

with
\mathrm{WAM}(A)=+\infty
if
A
is not weakly amenable.

Let us record the following folk lemma. We provide the proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 7.4
.

If
A
is weakly amenable, then
\overline{A^{2}}=A
, where
A^{2}:=\mathrm{span}\{ab:\ a,b\in A\}
.

Proof.

Assume
\overline{A^{2}}\neq A
. Then by Hahn–Banach there exists
0\neq\psi\in A^{*}
with
\psi|_{\overline{A^{2}}}=0
.
For each
a\in A
, define
D(a):=\psi(a)\,\psi\in A^{*}
.
Since
\psi(ab)=0
for all
a,b\in A
, we have
D(ab)=0
.
Moreover, for every
c\in A
,

(a\cdot\psi)(c)=\psi(ca)=0,\qquad(\psi\cdot a)(c)=\psi(ac)=0,

so
a\cdot\psi=\psi\cdot a=0
for all
a\in A
, and hence

a\cdot D(b)+D(a)\cdot b=\psi(b)\,a\cdot\psi+\psi(a)\,\psi\cdot b=0.

Thus
D
is a bounded derivation
A\to A^{*}
.

If
D
were inner, then for each
a\in A
we would have
D(a)(a)=0
because

\mathrm{ad}_{\phi}(a)(a)=\phi(aa-aa)=0
. But choosing
a
with
\psi(a)\neq 0
gives

D(a)(a)=\psi(a)^{2}\neq 0
, a contradiction. Hence
A
is not weakly amenable.
∎

For a Banach sequence lattice
E
on
I
and a family
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of Banach algebras, set

A:=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\Bigr)_{\!E}.

For
i\in I
we write
\iota_{i}\colon A_{i}\to A
for the coordinate embedding and
\pi_{i}\colon A\to A_{i}
for the coordinate projection.
Note that
\pi_{i}
is contractive (by Definition
3.1
(c)), while
\|\iota_{i}\|=\|\delta_{i}\|_{E}
in general.

For
F\in\mathcal{F}(I)
denote by
A_{F}
the closed subalgebra of
A
supported in
F
and by
P_{F}\colon A\to A_{F}
the truncation map.

Proposition 7.5
.

If
A=(\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}
is weakly amenable, then each
A_{i}
is weakly amenable.

More quantitatively, if
\mathrm{WAM}(A)<\infty
, then for each
i\in I
,

\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})\leqslant\|\iota_{i}\|\,\mathrm{WAM}(A)=\|\delta_{i}\|_{E}\,\mathrm{WAM}(A).

Proof.

By Lemma
7.4
, each
A_{i}
satisfies
\overline{A_{i}^{2}}=A_{i}
, hence

\overline{\iota_{i}(A_{i})^{2}}=\iota_{i}(A_{i})
. Fix
i\in I
and let
d\colon A_{i}\to A_{i}^{*}
be a bounded derivation.
Define
D\colon A\to A^{*}
by

D:=\pi_{i}^{*}\circ d\circ\pi_{i},

where
\pi_{i}^{*}\colon A_{i}^{*}\hookrightarrow A^{*}
is the adjoint of
\pi_{i}
.
Since
\pi_{i}
is a homomorphism,
\pi_{i}^{*}
is a bimodule map, hence
D
is a derivation.
If
A
is weakly amenable then
D=\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in A^{*}
.
Set
\phi:=\iota_{i}^{*}(\Phi)\in A_{i}^{*}
, i.e.
\phi(a)=\Phi(\iota_{i}(a))
.
A routine compatibility check yields
d=\mathrm{ad}_{\phi}
.
Finally,
\|\phi\|\leqslant\|\iota_{i}\|\,\|\Phi\|\leqslant\|\iota_{i}\|\,\mathrm{WAM}(A)\,\|D\|\leqslant\|\iota_{i}\|\,\mathrm{WAM}(A)\,\|d\|
.
∎

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set, let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
, and let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be Banach algebras.
Set
A:=(\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}
.

(1)

If
A
is weakly amenable, then each
A_{i}
is weakly amenable.

(2)

If every
A_{i}
is commutative and weakly amenable, then
A
is weakly amenable.
(In fact every derivation
A\to A^{*}
is zero.)

(3)

Assume
I
is infinite and
E=\ell_{p}(I)
for some
1<p<\infty
.
Let
B
be a weakly amenable
non-commutative
Banach algebra, and form the constant family
A_{i}=B
.
Then the algebra
(\oplus_{i\in I}B)_{\ell_{p}(I)}
is
not
weakly amenable.

Proof.

(1)
This is Proposition
7.5
.

(2)
Let
D\colon A\to A^{*}
be a bounded derivation.
Since
A
is commutative, inner derivations vanish (Remark
7.2
), so it suffices to prove
D=0
.

Fix distinct
i,j\in I
.
Take
x\in\iota_{i}(A_{i})
and
y\in\iota_{j}(A_{j})
.
Then
xy=0
(coordinatewise multiplication), hence

0=D(xy)=x\cdot D(y)+D(x)\cdot y.

Evaluating this identity at an element
z\in\iota_{i}(A_{i})
gives

0=(x\cdot D(y))(z)+(D(x)\cdot y)(z)=D(y)(zx)+D(x)(yz)=D(y)(zx),

since
yz=0
.
Thus
D(y)
annihilates
\iota_{i}(A_{i})^{2}
.
By Lemma
7.4
, each
A_{i}
satisfies
\overline{A_{i}^{2}}=A_{i}
, hence
\overline{\iota_{i}(A_{i})^{2}}=\iota_{i}(A_{i})
.
Therefore
D(y)
annihilates
\iota_{i}(A_{i})
for every
i\neq j
.
As
i\neq j
was arbitrary, we conclude that for
y
supported at
j
, the functional
D(y)
annihilates every coordinate ideal
\iota_{i}(A_{i})
with
i\neq j
.

Now fix
j\in I
and view
D
on the
j
th coordinate ideal.
Define
d_{j}\colon A_{j}\to A_{j}^{*}
by

d_{j}:=\iota_{j}^{*}\circ D\circ\iota_{j}.

Then
d_{j}
is a derivation. Since
A_{j}
is commutative and weakly amenable,
d_{j}=0
.
Thus
D(\iota_{j}(a))
annihilates
\iota_{j}(A_{j})
, and we already know it annihilates all other coordinate ideals.
Hence
D(\iota_{j}(a))=0
for all
a\in A_{j}
.

By linearity,
D
vanishes on the algebraic span of the coordinate ideals, hence on
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(I)}A_{F}
,
which is dense in
A
(Lemma
3.9
). Therefore
D=0
.

(3)
Let
1<p<\infty
and put
q=p/(p-1)
.
Choose
\psi\in B^{*}
with
\psi\notin\mathrm{Z}(B^{*})
; equivalently
\mathrm{ad}_{\psi}\neq 0
.
Set
d:=\mathrm{dist}(\psi,\mathrm{Z}(B^{*}))>0
.

Choose a countably infinite subset
\{i_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset I
.
Define a scalar weight
w=(w_{i})_{i\in I}
by

w_{i_{n}}:=\begin{cases}1,&1<p\leqslant 2,\\[2.0pt]
n^{-1/q},&p>2,\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{i}:=0\ \ (i\notin\{i_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}).

Then
w\notin\ell_{q}(I)
, but multiplication by
w
defines a bounded operator

M_{w}\colon\ell_{p}(I)\to\ell_{q}(I)
:
for
1<p\leqslant 2
this is just the continuous embedding
\ell_{p}\hookrightarrow\ell_{q}
;
for
p>2
it follows from Hölder since
w\in\ell_{r}(I)
with
r=p/(p-2)
and
1/q=1/p+1/r
.

Now define
D\colon\ell_{p}(I,B)\to\ell_{q}(I,B^{*})\cong(\ell_{p}(I,B))^{*}
by

D\bigl((b_{i})_{i\in I}\bigr):=\bigl(w_{i}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\psi}(b_{i})\bigr)_{i\in I}.

Since
\|\mathrm{ad}_{\psi}(b_{i})\|\leqslant 2\|\psi\|\,\|b_{i}\|
, boundedness of
M_{w}
yields that
D
is a bounded derivation.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
D
is inner:
D=\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in\ell_{q}(I,B^{*})
.
Restricting to the
i
th coordinate ideal (via the canonical embedding/projection) gives

\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi_{i}}=w_{i}\,\mathrm{ad}_{\psi}\qquad(i\in I),

hence
\Phi_{i}-w_{i}\psi\in\mathrm{Z}(B^{*})
for each
i
.
Therefore

\|\Phi_{i}\|\geqslant\mathrm{dist}(w_{i}\psi,\mathrm{Z}(B^{*}))=|w_{i}|\,\mathrm{dist}(\psi,\mathrm{Z}(B^{*}))=d\,|w_{i}|.

It follows that
(\|\Phi_{i}\|)_{i\in I}
dominates
d(|w_{i}|)_{i\in I}
and hence cannot lie in
\ell_{q}(I)
because
w\notin\ell_{q}(I)
.
This contradicts
\Phi\in\ell_{q}(I,B^{*})
. Thus
D
is not inner and
\ell_{p}(I,B)
is not weakly amenable.
∎

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be Banach algebras and set
A:=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}\big)_{c_{0}(I)}
.
Then

\mathrm{WAM}(A)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i}),

with the convention that the right-hand side is
+\infty
if any
A_{i}
is not weakly amenable.

Proof.

For each
i
, the coordinate projection
\pi_{i}\colon A\to A_{i}
is contractive and surjective, hence
Proposition
7.5
gives
\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})\leqslant\mathrm{WAM}(A)
and so

\sup_{i}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})\leqslant\mathrm{WAM}(A)
.

Conversely, assume
S:=\sup_{i}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})<\infty
, and let
D\colon A\to A^{*}
be a bounded derivation.
Fix
i\in I
and define
d_{i}:=\iota_{i}^{*}\circ D\circ\iota_{i}\colon A_{i}\to A_{i}^{*}
.
Then
d_{i}
is a bounded derivation and hence inner:
d_{i}=\mathrm{ad}_{\phi_{i}}
with
\|\phi_{i}\|\leqslant S\|d_{i}\|
.

For
F\subset I
finite, write
A_{F}
for the
\ell_{\infty}
-direct sum over
F
(as in Lemma
2.5
)
and let
P_{F}\colon A\to A_{F}
be the coordinate truncation.
Since elements supported on disjoint coordinates multiply to
0
, the usual “off-diagonal” argument shows that

D
maps
A_{F}
into
A_{F}^{*}=\bigoplus_{i\in F}A_{i}^{*}
and decomposes as

D|_{A_{F}}=\bigoplus_{i\in F}\bigl(\pi_{i}^{*}\circ d_{i}\circ\pi_{i}\bigr),

so
D|_{A_{F}}
is inner, implemented by
\Phi_{F}:=\bigoplus_{i\in F}\phi_{i}\in A_{F}^{*}
.
Indeed, we
claim
that for
x
supported on
\{k\}
, the functional
D(x)\in A^{*}
is supported on
\{k\}
. To see this, for
j\neq k
and any
y\in A_{j}
, we have
\iota_{j}(y)\cdot x=0=x\cdot\iota_{j}(y)
.
The derivation identity
D(x\cdot\iota_{j}(y))=0
gives

x\cdot D(\iota_{j}(y))+D(x)\cdot\iota_{j}(y)=0.

Evaluating at
z\in\iota_{k}(A_{k})
yields
(D(x)\cdot\iota_{j}(y))(z)=D(x)(\iota_{j}(y)\cdot z)=D(x)(0)=0

(the other term vanishes since
z\cdot x\in\iota_{k}(A_{k})
and
D(\iota_{j}(y))
annihilates

\iota_{k}(A_{k})^{2}=\iota_{k}(A_{k})
by weak amenability of
A_{k}
and Lemma
7.4
).
Since products
\iota_{j}(y)\cdot z
with
z\in\iota_{k}(A_{k})
are zero for
j\neq k
, we must show

D(x)
annihilates
\iota_{j}(A_{j})
directly.
Evaluating at
\iota_{j}(w)
for
w\in A_{j}
:

(D(x)\cdot\iota_{j}(y))(\iota_{j}(w))=D(x)(\iota_{j}(yw)).

As
y
varies over
A_{j}
, products
yw
span a dense subset of
A_{j}
(by
\overline{A_{j}^{2}}=A_{j}
),
so
D(x)
annihilates
\iota_{j}(A_{j})
.
By linearity,
D(x)
is supported on
\operatorname{supp}(x)
for all
x\in A
.
Moreover,
\|\Phi_{F}\|\leqslant\sum_{i\in F}\|\phi_{i}\|\leqslant S\sum_{i\in F}\|d_{i}\|
.

A standard estimate (testing
D
on elements supported on one coordinate at a time) gives

\sum_{i\in F}\|d_{i}\|\leqslant\|D\|
for all finite
F
. To verify the estimate
\sum_{i\in F}\|d_{i}\|\leqslant\|D\|
, fix
\varepsilon>0
and for each

i\in F
choose
x_{i}\in A_{i}
with
\|x_{i}\|=1
and
\|d_{i}(x_{i})\|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\|d_{i}\|
.
Set
x:=\sum_{i\in F}\iota_{i}(x_{i})\in A_{F}
; then
\|x\|=\max_{i\in F}\|x_{i}\|=1
.
Since
D(x)=\sum_{i\in F}D(\iota_{i}(x_{i}))
with disjoint supports,

\|D\|\geqslant\|D(x)\|_{A^{*}}=\sum_{i\in F}\|d_{i}(x_{i})\|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in F}\|d_{i}\|.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives the claim. Consequently,
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell_{1}(I,A_{i}^{*})=A^{*}

with
\|\Phi\|\leqslant S\|D\|
where
\Phi:=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
.
Then
D=\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi}
on the dense subalgebra of finitely supported elements, hence on all of
A
.
Thus
\mathrm{WAM}(A)\leqslant S
.
∎

Corollary 7.8

(Weak amenability in the
c_{0}
-type regime)
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice on
I
with
C_{E}<\infty
.
Let
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be Banach algebras and set
A=(\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}
.
Then
A
is weakly amenable if and only if
\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})<\infty
.
Moreover, in that case

\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i})\ \leqslant\ \mathrm{WAM}(A)\ \leqslant\ C_{E}^{2}\,\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i}).

In particular, for an Orlicz heart
E=h_{\varphi}(I)
with
a_{\varphi}>0
(so
C_{E}<\infty
by Proposition
5.7
),
weak amenability of
(\oplus_{i}A_{i})_{h_{\varphi}}
is equivalent to uniform weak amenability of the summands.

Proof.

If
C_{E}<\infty
, then
E
coincides with
c_{0}(I)
as a set and the two norms are equivalent:

\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|x\|_{E}\leqslant C_{E}\|x\|_{\infty}\qquad(x\in E),

by Lemma
3.5
. Hence the identity map induces an algebra isomorphism

(\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{E}\ \cong\ (\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{c_{0}(I)}

with
\|T\|\leqslant 1
and
\|T^{-1}\|\leqslant C_{E}
.
A standard conjugation argument for derivations under algebra isomorphisms gives

\mathrm{WAM}\bigl((\oplus_{i}A_{i})_{E}\bigr)\ \leqslant\ \|T^{-1}\|^{2}\,\|T\|^{2}\,\mathrm{WAM}\bigl((\oplus_{i}A_{i})_{c_{0}}\bigr)\ \leqslant\ C_{E}^{2}\,\mathrm{WAM}\bigl((\oplus_{i}A_{i})_{c_{0}}\bigr),

and similarly in the other direction.

Finally, by Lemma
7.7
one has

\mathrm{WAM}\bigl((\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i})_{c_{0}(I)}\bigr)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{WAM}(A_{i}).

∎

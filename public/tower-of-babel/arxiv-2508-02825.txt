Finding Colorings in One-Sided Expanders
Rares-Darius Buhai rares.buhai@inf.ethz.ch ETH Zurich Yiding Hua yiding.hua@inf.ethz.ch ETH Zurich David Steurer dsteurer@inf.ethz.ch ETH Zurich Andor Vári-Kakas avarikakas@student.ethz.ch ETH Zurich

Abstract
We establish new algorithmic guarantees with matching hardness results for coloring and independent set problems in one-sided expanders and related classes of graphs. For example, given a 3 3 -colorable regular one-sided expander, we compute in polynomial time either an independent set of relative size at least 1 2 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) \tfrac{1}{2}-o(1) or a proper 3 3 -coloring for all but an o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) fraction of the vertices, where o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) stands for a function that tends to 0 0 with the second largest eigenvalue of the normalized adjacency matrix. This result improves on recent seminal work of Bafna, Hsieh, and Kothari (STOC 2025) developing an algorithm that efficiently finds independent sets of relative size at least 0.01 0.01 in such graphs. We also obtain an efficient 1.6667 1.6667 -factor approximation algorithm for VERTEX COVER in sufficiently strong regular one-sided expanders, improving over a previous ( 2 − ε ) (2-\varepsilon) -factor approximation in such graphs for an unspecified constant ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . We propose a new stratification of k k -COLORING in terms of k k -by- k k matrices akin to predicate sets for constraint satisfaction problems. We prove that whenever this matrix has repeated rows, the corresponding coloring problem is NP-hard for one-sided expanders under the Unique Games Conjecture. On the other hand, if this matrix has no repeated rows, our algorithms can solve the corresponding coloring problem on one-sided expanders in polynomial time. When this k k -by- k k matrix has repeated rows, we furthermore characterize the maximum fraction of vertices on which a proper k k -coloring can be found by polynomial-time algorithms under the Unique Games Conjecture. As starting point for our algorithmic results, we show a property of graph spectra that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been observed before: The number of negative eigenvalues smaller than − τ -\tau is at most O ⁡ ( 1 / τ 2 ) O(1/\tau^{2}) times the number of eigenvalues larger than τ 2 / 2 \tau^{2}/2 . While this result allows us to bound the number of eigenvalues bounded away from 0 0 in one-sided spectral expanders, this property alone is insufficient for our algorithmic results. For example, given a one-sided regular expander with a balanced 3 3 -coloring, we can efficiently find a 3 3 -coloring for all but a o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) fraction of vertices. At the same time, if we only know that the graph has a balanced 3 3 -coloring and a bounded number of significant eigenvalues, it is NP-hard under the Unique Games Conjecture to find a 3 3 -coloring for all but a 0.1 0.1 fraction of vertices.

Two-sided vs one-sided expansion.

The results of Alon-Kahale and David-Feige crucially exploit the fact that both the positive and negative part of the spectrum of the graph are severely restricted.

Recent seminal work developed the first algorithms for one-sided expander graphs, where the negative part of the spectrum is completely unrestricted
[
BHK25

The current work further develops our understanding of the computational landscape of coloring and independent set problems on one-sided expander graphs and provides answers to key questions left open by this prior work.

Hardness results for coloring one-sided expanders and related graphs.

Threshold-ranks of graphs.

While the above hardness result shows that a small number of significant eigenvalues does not guarantee the tractability of finding good coloring,
our algorithmic results for one-sided expanders do rely on bounds on the number of these eigenvalues.
Indeed, we show the perhaps unexpected fact that one-sided expanders always have bounded number of significant (negative) eigenvalues.
More, generally we show the following relationship between the positive and negative spectrum of graphs.

Theorem 1.7

(Relation between top and bottom threshold rank, see
Theorem

4.1
for full version)

For all thresholds
\tau>0

\left(\tfrac{\tau^{2}-\tau^{\prime}}{1-\tau^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\cdot\left\lvert\sigma(G)\cap(-\infty,-\tau]\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\sigma(G)\cap[\tau^{\prime},\infty)\right\rvert\,.

(1)

Here,

denotes the spectrum of the normalized adjacency matrix of

To the best of our knowledge, this basic result for spectra of graphs is new.

Techniques

To discuss the spectral graph techniques used by our coloring algorithms,
we introduce some convenient notation and terminology commonly used, e.g., in the literature on Markov chains.

Let
G
be an undirected, connected graph with vertex set
V
and let
\bm{x}\bm{y}
denote a uniformly random edge of
G
with vertices
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
as endpoints.
Since the graph is undirected, the random vertices
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
have the same distribution without loss of generality.
For a vertex subset
S\subseteq V
, we denote by
\mu(S)=\ProbOp\{\bm{x}\in S\}
its probability under this distribution.
(For regular graphs, this probability is the fraction of vertices contained in
S
.)

Let
A
denote the adjacency matrix for
G
with every row normalized to sum to
1
.
This matrix acts as a linear operator on real-valued functions
f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

Af(x)=\E\left[f(\bm{y})\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\bm{x}=x\right]\,.

(7)

This operator is self-adjoint with respect to the following inner product for functions
f,g\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\langle f,g\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\E f(\bm{x})g(\bm{x})\,.

(8)

We denote the Euclidean norm with respect to this inner product by
\lVert f\rVert\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\langle f,f\rangle^{1/2}=\left(\E f^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

The linear operator
A
has all eigenvalues in the interval
[-1,1]\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
and largest eigenvalue
1
.
Since the graph
G
is connected, the top eigenspace is one-dimensional and contains all constant functions on
V
.
Let
\lambda<1
denote the second largest eigenvalue.
Then, the quadratic form of
A
satisfies the inequality,

\langle f,Af\rangle\leq(1-\lambda)\cdot\left(\E f\right)^{2}+\lambda\cdot\E f^{2}\,.

(9)

The
spectral gap

1-\lambda
provides a measure of well-connectedness of
G
.
We denote the
(edge) expansion
of a vertex subset
S
by
\phi_{G}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\ProbOp\{\bm{y}\not\in S\mid\bm{x}\in S\}
and the
expansion
of
G
as
\phi(G)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min_{S\colon\mu(S)\leq 1/2}\phi_{G}(S)
.
By Cheeger’s inequality
[
Che70
,
AM85
]
, the expansion of
G
agrees up to a quadratic factor with the spectral gap,

\phi(G)^{2}\lesssim 1-\lambda\lesssim\phi(G)\,.

(10)

While the above inequality is most informative for
\lambda
close to
1
, smaller values of
\lambda
have interesting combinatorial consequences, e.g., they imply stronger expansion bounds for small vertex subsets
S\subseteq V
,

\phi_{G}(S)=1-\langle\mathds{1}_{S},A\mathds{1}_{S}\rangle/\mu(S)\geq 1-(1-\lambda)\cdot\mu(S)-\lambda\,.

(11)

Here,
\mathds{1}_{S}\colon V\to\{0,1\}
denotes the indicator function of
S
satisfying
\E\mathds{1}_{S}=\E\mathds{1}_{S}^{2}=\mu(S)
.

Subspace methods for expansion.

Several important combinatorial optimization problems are related to finding (small) non-expanding sets in graphs, e.g.,

By extending the above discussion, we see that all non-expanding sets must be close to the subspace spanned by eigenvectors with eigenvalue above a certain threshold.

Letting

\langle f,Af\rangle

\langle f,Af\rangle\leq(1-\tau)\cdot\lVert P^{\geq\tau}f\rVert^{2}+\tau\E f^{2}\,.

(12)

A concrete consequence of the above inequality is that the indicator
\mathds{1}_{S}
of a subset

\lVert P^{\geq 1-\eta}\mathds{1}_{S}\rVert^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon/\eta)\lVert\mathds{1}_{S}\rVert^{2}

This observation drives worst-case subexponential-time approximation algorithms for

and several related problems

]
and motivates the notion of

for graphs,

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\tau}(G)=\operatorname{rank}P^{\geq\tau}=\left\lvert\left\{i\mid\lambda_{i}(A)\geq\tau\right\}\right\rvert\,.

(13)

While small threshold rank enables enumeration-based algorithms for many problems, it turns out that large threshold rank implies the existence of small non-expanding sets
[

,

,

,

]

This combinatorial ramification of large threshold rank underlies the aforementioned subexponential-time approximation algorithms, e.g., for the construction of suitable graph decompositions.

In this work, we aim to extend these developments to vertex-based problems like

One-sided spectral expansion implies low variance.

Relating positive and negative graph eigenvalues.

The discussion so far focused on the question whether the bottom eigenspaces of the graphs Markov operator capture enough information about colorings or not.
However, in order to obtain efficient algorithms, we also need to bound the number of significant bottom eigenvalues so that we can afford to enumerate the subspace their eigenfunctions span.

To this end, we show that, perhaps surprisingly, bottom-threshold ranks can never be much larger than top-threshold ranks.
Concretely, for all thresholds

-one-sided-expander exist for every
\delta>0
.
In particular, a stochastic block model graph with large enough degree parameter (as a function of
\delta
) and suitable block probabilities (as a function of
M
) has the desired properties with high probability.

We show that, assuming the Unique Games Conjecture,
M
-
expander-coloring
admits a polynomial-time algorithm in the sense above if and only if
M
has no repeated rows.
More precisely, we show that for every reversible, row stochastic matrix
M
with zeros on the diagonal and second largest eigenvalue at most
0
:

1.

If
M
has no repeated rows, then there exists a polynomial-time for
M
-
expander-coloring
.

2.

If
M
has repeated rows, then there exists a polynomial-time reduction from problems predicted to be NP-hard by the Unique Games Conjecture to
M
-
expander-coloring
.

We remark that it is not important for our algorithms to know the underlying model matrix
M
.
(However, by carefully enumerating all potential model matrices, one could also assume to know the underlying matrix
M
.)

For the algorithmic part of our meta-theorem, we just need to combine the ideas discussed so far.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Suppose that
M
has no repeated rows and that
G
is a
(M,\delta)
-colorable
\delta
-one-side-expander for sufficiently small
\delta>0
(as a function of
M
and
\varepsilon
).
Let
\chi
be
(M,\delta)
-coloring of
G
.
We can derive that the matrix
M^{\prime}_{ab}=\ProbOp\left\{\chi(\bm{y})=b\mid\chi(\bm{x})=a\right\}
has no repeated rows because it is sufficiently close to the matrix
M
, which has that property.
Thus, as discussed before, the partition
\chi
is uniquely determined by the image of the Markov operator
B
of the model graph
H
for
G
with respect to
\chi
.
Since
G
is a
\delta
-one-sided-expander, we obtain an upper bound of
\delta\cdot k
on the variance
\mathrm{Var}(G;H)
of the input graph
G
with respect to the model graph
H
.
As we saw before, this kind of variance bound implies that the bottom-most eigenspaces of the input graph are close to those of the model graph and therefore allow us to approximately recover the partition
\chi
.
At the same time, we can upper bound the dimension of the span of those eigenspaces by bounding the bottom-threshold rank in terms of the top-threshold rank (equaling
1
for one-sided spectral expanders).
We conclude that we can approximately recover the partition
\chi
in polynomial time by exhaustively enumerating a fine enough cover of the subspace spanned by the bottom-most eigenspaces of
G
.

For the hardness part of our meta-theorem, we generalize a previous hardness reduction for coloring one-sided expanders
[
BHK25
]
.
The starting point of the reduction is the following problem shown to be NP-hard under the Unique Games Conjecture for every
\varepsilon>0

[
BK09
]
:

Given a regular graph
G
that contains two disjoint independent sets, each of volume at least
\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon
, find an independent set of volume at least
\varepsilon
.

Let
M
be a reversible, row-stochastic
k

k
matrix with zeroes on the diagonal and second largest eigenvalue at most
0
.
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\geq 0
with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}=1
be the stationary measure for
M
so that
p_{a}M_{ab}=p_{b}M_{ba}
.
Suppose that the first two rows of
M
are identical and that
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
.
The idea of the reduction is to create a graph
G^{\prime}
on
k
blocks consistent with
M
and to embed the hard instance
G
within the first two blocks.
Concretely, our reduction outputs the following graph
G^{\prime}
, where we choose its number of vertices
n
such that
\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert V(G)\rvert=p_{1}n
.
Let
V_{1,2}=V(G)\cup V^{\prime}_{1,2}
, where
V^{\prime}_{1,2}
consists of
(p_{2}-p_{1})\cdot n
fresh vertices, and create disjoint blocks
V_{3},\ldots,V_{k}
of fresh vertices such that
\lvert V_{i}\rvert=p_{i}\cdot n
for all

.
This construction ensures
\lvert V_{1,2}\rvert=(p_{1}+p_{2})\cdot n

Choose an arbitary bipartition
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
of
V_{1,2}
such that
\lvert V_{1}\rvert=p_{1}n
and
\lvert V_{2}\rvert=p_{2}n
.
For simplicity, we choose

as a weighted graph with the following distribution
\bm{x}\bm{y}
over edges, where
\delta>0
is a paramter of the reduction:

1.

With probability
\delta>0
, choose
\bm{x}\bm{y}
as a random edge in the input graph

for the reduction.

2.

With the remaining probability

according to the probabilities

and choose

and
\bm{y}
to be uniformly random vertex in

.

We remark that, by subsampling the edges of the graph

for the reduction to go through.

For sufficiently small

, the model graph for

with respect to the partition

is arbitrarily close to

Since the second largest eigenvalue of

is at most

that also means that

is an arbitrarily good one-sided spectral expander, i.e., the second largest eigenvalue of

as

.
If we find a

-coloring in

for

.
Finally, we need to argue that

-colorable in case that the input graph

contains two disjoint independent sets, each of volume at least

.
To this end, extend the two large independent sets in

Note that the edges with both endpoints in the same part of this bipartition have a vertex cover of size at most

Here, we also use that the edge probability satisfies

It follows that the

Furthermore, the model graph for

(Indeed, for that property it does not matter which bipartition of

Definitions and notation

For a graph
G
with adjacency matrix
A
and (diagonal) degree matrix
D
with
D_{ii}=\deg(i)
, we say it is a
\lambda
-one-sided expander if
\lambda_{2}(D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2})\leq\lambda
.
We say a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
is

if the sum of its entries on every row is
1
: for all
x\in[n]
,
\sum_{y=1}^{n}A_{xy}=1
.
We say a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
is
reversible row stochastic
if it is row stochastic and if there exists
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that, for all
x,y\in[n]
,
\pi_{x}A_{xy}=\pi_{y}A_{yx}
.

Our main results will be stated in terms of
(k,\delta)
-colorings.

Definition 3.1

(
(k,\delta)
-coloring)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph.
A
(k,\delta)
-coloring of
G
is a list of
k
disjoint independent sets in
G
that cover
(1-\delta)n
of the vertices of
G
.

We define now the partition matrices and the model of a graph, which are defined with respect to a
k
-partition
\chi:[n]\to[k]
of its vertices.

Definition 3.2

(Partition matrices of a coloring)
.

Let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be a
k
-partition of
[n]
.
Then we define the following matrices:

•

The partition matrix
Z\in\{0,1\}^{n\times k}
has
Z_{x\chi(x)}=1
for all
x\in[n]
and
0
elsewhere.

•

The partition size matrix
B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
is the diagonal matrix
B=Z^{\top}Z
. Note that
B_{aa}=|\chi^{-1}(a)|
for all
a\in[k]
.

Definition 3.3

(Model of a coloring)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
, and let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be a
k
-partition of
[n]
.
For
x\in[n]
and
a\in[k]
, let
d_{xa}=\sum_{y\in\chi^{-1}(a)}A_{xy}
.
We define the
model

M(A,\chi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
to be the matrix that, for any

, has entries
M(A,\chi)_{ab}=\frac{1}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{xb}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Using the definitions in
Definition

3.2
, we have equivalently that
M(A,\chi)=B^{-1}Z^{\top}AZ
.

For a reversible row stochastic matrix
M\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{k\times k}
, we define an
(M,\delta)
-coloring, which is a coloring with model close to
M
:

Definition 3.5

(
(M,\delta)
-colorable graph)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zeros on the diagonal.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with adjacency matrix
A
and (diagonal) degree matrix
D
with
D_{ii}=\deg(i)
, and let
\tilde{A}=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}
.
We say that

is
(M,\delta)
-colorable if there exists a
k
-partition
\chi:[n]\to[k]
such that
\left\lVert M-M(\tilde{A},\chi)\right\rVert_{\max}\leq\delta
and

has a vertex cover of all edges with both endpoints in the same part of size at most
\delta n
with respect to
\chi
.

4
Small top threshold rank implies small bottom threshold rank

In this section, we prove the following result which shows that, for bounded-norm symmetric matrices with non-negative entries, large bottom threshold rank implies large top threshold rank.
In the rest of the paper, we will be applying this result to normalized adjacency matrices of graphs, which indeed are symmetric, have spectral norm
1
, and have non-negative entries.

Theorem 4.1

(Large bottom rank implies large top rank)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}_{\geq 0}
be symmetric with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
.
If

\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\lambda}(A)\geq t\,,

for some
\lambda\geq 0
and some positive integer
t
, then for any
\sigma\in(0,1)
it follows that

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}-\sigma}{1-\sigma}}(A)\geq\sigma^{2}t\,.

By taking the contrapositive of
Theorem

4.1
and re-parameterizing, we get the following direct corollary that shows, for bounded-norm symmetric matrices with non-negative entries and small top threshold rank, that the bottom threshold rank cannot be too large.

Corollary 4.2

(Small top rank implies small bottom rank)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}_{\geq 0}
be symmetric with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
.
If

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\tau}(A)\leq s\,,

for some
\tau\geq 0
and some non-negative integer
s
, then for any
\sigma\in(0,1)
it follows that

\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\sqrt{\tau(1-\sigma)+\sigma}}(A)\leq\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\,.

To prove
Theorem

4.1
, we need the following slight generalization of Lemma 6.1 in
[
BRS11
]
.
We include a proof of this lemma in
Appendix

A
for completeness.

Lemma 4.3

(Generalized restatement of Lemma 6.1 in
[
BRS11
]
)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be symmetric with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
.
Suppose there exists
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
positive semidefinite such that

\langle A,M\rangle\geq 1-\varepsilon\,,\quad\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r}\,,\quad\mathrm{Tr}(M)=1\,.

Then for all
C>1
,

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq 1-C\cdot\varepsilon}(A)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)^{2}r\,.

The key observation that leads to the proof of
Theorem

4.1
is that, if a bounded-norm symmetric matrix with non-negative entries has large bottom threshold rank, then we can construct a matrix
M
that satisfies
Lemma

4.3
.
This idea is captured by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4

(Construction of witness matrix)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}_{\geq 0}
be symmetric with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
.
If

\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\lambda}(A)\geq t\,,

for some
\lambda\geq 0
and some positive integer
t
, then there exist
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{t^{2}\times n}
such that

\langle A,V^{\top}V\rangle\geq\lambda^{2}\,,\quad\left\lVert V^{\top}V\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{t}\,,\quad\mathrm{Tr}(V^{\top}V)=1\,.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{t}\}
be
t
orthonormal eigenvectors of
A
whose corresponding eigenvalues are at most
-\lambda
, that is, for each
s\in[t]
,

\langle u_{s},Au_{s}\rangle\leq-\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert u_{s}\rVert^{2}=1\,.

Let
U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times t}
be the matrix whose
s
-th column is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}u_{s}
for all
s\in[t]
.
It follows that

\langle A,UU^{\top}\rangle=\sum_{s\in[t]}\frac{1}{t}\langle A,u_{s}(u_{s})^{\top}\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s\in[t]}\langle u_{s},Au_{s}\rangle\leq-\lambda\,,

(49)

and

\left\lVert UU^{\top}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\left\lVert U^{\top}U\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\sum_{s\in[t]}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\lVert u_{s}\rVert^{4}=\frac{1}{t}\,,

(50)

and

\mathrm{Tr}(UU^{\top})=\mathrm{Tr}(U^{\top}U)=\sum_{s\in[t]}\frac{1}{t}\lVert u_{s}\rVert^{2}=1\,.

(51)

Let us denote the
i
-th row of
U
by
w_{i}
.
For
i\in[n]
, define
v_{i}=\frac{w_{i}^{\otimes 2}}{\lVert w_{i}\rVert}
if
\lVert w_{i}\rVert\neq 0
and
v_{i}=0
(the all-zero vector) otherwise. Let
S=\left\{i\in[n]:\lVert w_{i}\rVert\neq 0\right\}
.
Finally, let
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{t^{2}\times n}
be the matrix whose
i
-th column is
v_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
.
We will show that
V
satisfies the three conditions of the lemma.

Condition 1.

Notice that, by Cauchy-Schwarz, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}\frac{\sqrt{A_{ij}}\cdot\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle}{\sqrt{\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert}}\cdot\sqrt{A_{ij}\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\frac{\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert\right)\,.

By rearranging, we get

\langle A,V^{\top}V\rangle=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\frac{\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert}\\
\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}}{\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert}\,.

(52)

For the numerator, by observing that
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle=(UU^{\top})_{ij}
and by
Eq.

49
, we get

\left(\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}=\left\langle A,UU^{\top}\right\rangle^{2}\geq\lambda^{2}\,.

(53)

For the denominator, because
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{i,j\in S}A_{ij}\lVert w_{i}\rVert\lVert w_{j}\rVert&\leq\sum_{i\in S}\lVert w_{i}\rVert^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}(UU^{\top})=1\,.\end{split}

(54)

Plugging
Eq.

53
and
Eq.

54
into
Eq.

52
, we get that
V
satisfies condition 1,

\langle A,V^{\top}V\rangle\geq\lambda^{2}\,.

Condition 2.

By the definition of
V
and by Cauchy-Schwarz, we get

\left\lVert V^{\top}V\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle^{2}=\sum_{i,j\in S}\frac{\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{4}}{\lVert w_{i}\rVert^{2}\lVert w_{j}\rVert^{2}}\leq\sum_{i,j\in S}\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{2}\,.

Since
\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle=(UU^{\top})_{ij}
, it follows by
Eq.

50
that

\left\lVert V^{\top}V\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j\in S}\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle^{2}=\left\lVert UU^{\top}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{t}\,.

Condition 3.

By the definition of
V
and
Eq.

51
, it follows that

\mathrm{Tr}(V^{\top}V)=\sum_{i}\lVert v_{i}\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i}\lVert w_{i}\rVert^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}(UU^{\top})=1\,.

∎

Given
Lemma

4.3
and
Lemma

4.4
, we can prove
Theorem

4.1
easily.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

By
Lemma

4.4
, when
\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\lambda}(A)\geq t
, there exist
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{t^{2}\times n}
such that

\langle A,V^{\top}V\rangle\geq\lambda^{2}\,,\quad\left\lVert V^{\top}V\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{t}\,,\quad\mathrm{Tr}(V^{\top}V)=1\,.

Then, we can apply
Lemma

4.3
by setting
M=V^{\top}V
,
\varepsilon=1-\lambda^{2}
,
r=t
, and
C=\frac{1}{1-\sigma}
, and obtain

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}-\sigma}{1-\sigma}}(A)\geq\sigma^{2}t\,.

∎

5
Recovering partitions

In this section we state and prove our meta-theorem for recovering partitions.
The result in this section is written in a very general form, to make clear what properties we exploit in the proof.
Then, in
Section

7
, we apply this meta-theorem to our settings of interest and obtain our algorithmic results.

Theorem 5.1

(Result for recovering partitions)
.

Let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be a
k
-partition of
[n]
, and consider its partition matrices
Z\in\{0,1\}^{n\times k}

as per

3.2
.
Suppose
|\chi^{-1}(a)|\geq cn

.
Let

be a matrix such that

•

MB^{-1}
is symmetric,

•

\left\lVert M\right\rVert\leq\zeta
,

•

\left\lVert M^{(a)}-M^{(b)}\right\rVert\geq\alpha
for any two rows
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
of
M
.

Also let
W=ZMB^{-1}Z^{\top}
and let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be some matrix such that, for all

\left\lVert A\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}-W\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta\,,

\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\lambda}(A)+\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\lambda}(A)\leq t

and

.

Then, given

, there exists an algorithm that runs in time

In our applications, we will take

to be a model of the coloring

.
Our main results for graph coloring, in particular, take

Then

corresponds to the transition probability from color class

We note that

.

The proof of

Lemma 5.2

(Eigenspace approximation)
.

.

W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

.
Alo let

Lemma 5.3

(Eigenvector coordinate separation)
.

Let
\chi:[n]\to[k]

[n]
, and consider its partition matrices

as per

3.2
.
Suppose
|\chi^{-1}(a)|\geq cn
for all

.
Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
be a matrix such that

•

MB^{-1}
is symmetric,

•

\left\lVert M\right\rVert\leq\zeta
,

•

\left\lVert M^{(a)}-M^{(b)}\right\rVert\geq\alpha
for any two rows

and

of

5.1
Eigenspace approximation

In this section we prove
Lemma

5.2
.
First, we state a generic result that proves that if two matrices are close in a subspace that for one of the matrices is an eigenspace with eigenvalues of large absolute value, then the small eigenvalues of the other matrix are far from this subspace.

Lemma 5.5

(Eigenspace closeness)
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric matrix, and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
be any
k
of its eigenvectors with non-zero eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
.
Let
\lambda=\min\{|\lambda_{1}|,\ldots,|\lambda_{k}|\}
.
Also let
Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric matrix, and let
P\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be the orthogonal projection to the span of the eigenvectors of
Y
that are smaller in absolute value than
\lambda-\eta
for some
\eta>0
.
Then, for any orthonormal basis
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of
\operatorname{span}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert Pu_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert(X-Y)u_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Proof.

First, for the eigenvectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
of
X
we have that

\left\lVert(X-Y)v_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\lambda_{i}v_{i}-Yv_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert(\lambda_{i}I_{n}-Y)v_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\left\lVert P(\lambda_{i}I_{n}-Y)v_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(55)

Let now
v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{m}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be the eigenvectors of
Y
in
P
with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{m}^{\prime}
smaller in absolute value than
\lambda-\eta
.
Continuing
Eq.

55
,

\left\lVert(X-Y)v_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}^{\prime})^{2}\langle v_{j}^{\prime},v_{i}\rangle^{2}\geq\eta^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle v_{j}^{\prime},v_{i}\rangle^{2}=\eta^{2}\left\lVert Pv_{i}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(56)

Second, we claim that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert(X-Y)v_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert(X-Y)u_{i}\right\rVert^{2}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert Pv_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert Pu_{i}\right\rVert^{2}
.
The proof is simply that, for any matrix
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
and any two orthonormal bases of the same subspace
\{x_{i}\}
and
\{y_{i}\}
,

\sum_{i}\left\lVert Mx_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}(M^{\top}Mx_{i}x_{i}^{\top})=\mathrm{Tr}\left(M^{\top}M\sum_{i}x_{i}x_{i}^{\top}\right)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(M^{\top}M\sum_{i}y_{i}y_{i}^{\top}\right)\,,

and by the same sequence of transformations we get back that
\mathrm{Tr}\left(M^{\top}M\sum_{i}y_{i}y_{i}^{\top}\right)=\sum_{i}\left\lVert My_{i}\right\rVert^{2}
.
Using this fact for the orthonormal bases
\{v_{i}\}
and
\{u_{i}\}
, we prove the desired fact, and combining it with
Eq.

56
we complete the proof.
∎

We state now the well-known bounds of subspace enumeration.

Lemma 5.6

(Subspace enumeration)
.

There exists an algorithm that outputs with high probability an
\delta
-net of the unit
(d-1)
-dimensional ball in time
(1/\delta)^{O(d)}
.

Proof.

By standard arguments, there exists such a cover of size
m=O(1/\delta)^{d}
(see Example 5.8 in
[
Wai19
]
).
Furthermore, it suffices to sample
O(m\log m)
random points on the unit ball in order to obtain with high probability such a cover.
∎

Finally, we prove

Proof of

.

We have
\left\lVert A\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}-W\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta
for all
a\in[k]
, so
\sum_{a=1}^{k}\left\lVert\left(A-W\right)\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta k

Because

are orthonormal, for any orthonormal

that span the same subspace we also have

Also recall that

has column span in the span of

Let

Then, by

5.2
Eigenvector coordinate separation

In this section we prove
Lemma

5.3
.
First, we state a technical lemma.

Lemma 5.7

(Non-uniformity of eigenvectors)
.

Let
c\geq 0
,
0<\lambda<\zeta
, and
\alpha\geq\sqrt{2}c\lambda
be parameters.
Let
D\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with
D_{x}\leq c
for all
x\in[k]
.
Let
M
be a symmetric
k\times k
matrix with
\left\lVert M\right\rVert\leq\zeta
such that for any two distinct columns
M_{x}
and
M_{y}
it holds that
\left\lVert D_{x}M_{x}-D_{y}M_{y}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}
.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
be orthonormal eigenvectors of
M
and let
S:=\left\{v_{r}\;\mid\;Mv_{r}=\tau v_{r},|\tau|>\lambda\right\}
.
Then

\min_{x\neq y\in[k]}\max_{v_{r}\in S}\left\lvert D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y}\right\rvert\geq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}-2c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{k\left(\zeta^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right)}}\,.

Proof.

We write
M=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\lambda_{r}v_{r}v_{r}^{\top}
and get that, for any
x\neq y\in[k]
,

D_{x}M_{x}-D_{y}M_{y}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\lambda_{r}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})v_{r}\,.

Using that
\left\lVert M\right\rVert\leq\zeta
and
\left\lvert\lambda_{r}\right\rvert\leq\lambda
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert D_{x}M_{x}-D_{y}M_{y}\right\rVert^{2}&=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\lambda_{r}^{2}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\\
&=\sum_{r\in S}\lambda_{r}^{2}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}+\sum_{r\notin S}\lambda_{r}^{2}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\\
&\leq\zeta^{2}\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}+\lambda^{2}\sum_{r\notin S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\,.\end{split}

(57)

Because the eigenvectors are orthonormal, the rows of the matrix
V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
whose columns are
\{v_{r}\}_{r\in[k]}
are also orthonormal: that is,
\sum_{r=1}^{k}(v_{r})_{x}(v_{r})_{y}=0
for
x\neq y
and
\sum_{r=1}^{k}(v_{r})_{x}^{2}=1
for
x\in[k]
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{r\notin S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}-\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}-\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{2}-\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\,.

Plugging it into
Eq.

57
, we get

\left\lVert D_{x}M_{x}-D_{y}M_{y}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left(\zeta^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right)\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}+2c^{2}\lambda^{2}\,.

Since
\left\lVert D_{x}M_{x}-D_{y}M_{y}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}
, it follows that

\sum_{r\in S}(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}-2c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{\zeta^{2}-\lambda^{2}}\,.

Finally, by an averaging argument, there must exist some
r\in S
such that

(D_{x}(v_{r})_{x}-D_{y}(v_{r})_{y})^{2}\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}-2c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{k(\zeta^{2}-\lambda^{2})}\,.

∎

1
runs in time

(\alpha\sqrt{c})^{-O(k^{2})}

-dimensional vectors

Algorithm 1

Spectral partitioning algorithm (based on Algorithm 10 in
[
DF16
]
)

Proof.

To begin with, we remark that the number of guesses is bounded by

\left(\left(2/\sqrt{cn}\right)/\left(\alpha/\sqrt{12kn}\right)\right)^{k\ell}\leq O(\sqrt{k}/(\alpha\sqrt{c}))^{k^{2}}\,,

so the algorithm leads to the desired time complexity bound and list size bound.

Let

, and note that if

then

. Define the set

,

Consider some

with

\displaystyle=\left(\left((\hat{u}_{j})_{x}-(u_{j})_{x}\right)+\left((u_{j})_{x}-(v_{j})_{a}\right)+\left((v_{j})_{a}-(v^{*}_{j})_{a}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left((\hat{u}_{j})_{x}-(u_{j})_{x}\right)^{2}+2\left((v_{j})_{a}-(v^{*}_{j})_{a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{3kn}\,.

Then, overall, we have

On the other hand, for any

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\left((\hat{u}_{i})_{x}-(u_{i})_{x}\right)+\left((u_{i})_{x}-(v_{i})_{b}\right)+\left((v_{i})_{b}-(v^{*}_{i})_{b}\right)\Bigg)^{2}

Hence,

This shows that there exists a guess of the algorithm

get correctly partitioned.
Then, because

5.8
: we can recover a list of partitions such that one of them contains (up to a permutation) at most
O\left(\frac{\delta k}{\alpha^{2}}\right)n
wrongly partitioned vertices.
The time complexity is
n^{O(1)}\cdot(\alpha\sqrt{c})^{-O(k^{2})}
the list size bound is
(\alpha\sqrt{c})^{-O(k^{2})}
.
∎

6
Variance of colorings in expander graphs

In this section we prove a structural result for colorable expander graphs concerning the variance of the connections between any two color classes.
This result is essential to our proof of
Theorem

7.1
.

Lemma 6.1

(Variance of
k
-colorings in expanders)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with adjacency matrix
A
, and let
\tilde{A}=\frac{1}{d}A
.
Let
\lambda_{2}
be the second largest eigenvalue of
\tilde{A}
.
Let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be a
k
-partition of its vertices with
\min_{a\in[k]}|\chi^{-1}(a)|\geq cn
.
Suppose that
G
has at most
\delta dn
edges with both endpoints in the same part with respect to
\chi
.
For
x\in[n]
and
a\in[k]
, let
d_{xa}=\sum_{y\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\tilde{A}_{xy}
.
Then, for any
a,b\in[k]
and
\bm{x}
uniformly random in
\chi^{-1}(a)
,

\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}\leq O\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{c}+\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{2}c}\right)\,.

Proof.

Fix some
a,b\in[k]
.
For simplicity, we denote
\sigma^{2}=\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}
.

If
a=b
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\leq\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}a}\right)^{2}\leq\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}a}\leq\frac{2\delta n}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\leq\frac{2\delta}{c}\,.

We focus now on the case
a\neq b
.
By regularity,
\lambda_{1}=1
corresponds to eigenvector
\mathbf{1}
.
Let
\beta\in[0,1]
be some parameter to be chosen later.
We define
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as follows:

•

v_{x}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}}\left(d_{xb}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)
for
x\in\chi^{-1}(a)
,

•

v_{x}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}}\beta
for
x\in\chi^{-1}(b)
,

•

v_{x}:=0
otherwise .

Let us calculate
v^{\top}\tilde{A}v
.
Let
\tilde{A}_{(a)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|\chi^{-1}(a)|\times|\chi^{-1}(a)|}
be the restriction of
\tilde{A}
to rows and columns in
\chi^{-1}(a)
, and let
v_{(a)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}
be the restriction of
v
to entries in
\chi^{-1}(a)
.
Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{\top}\tilde{A}v&=v_{(a)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(a)}v_{(a)}+v_{(b)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(b)}v_{(b)}+2\beta\frac{1}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\chi^{-1}(b)\\
y\sim x\end{subarray}}\left(d_{xb}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)\\
&=v_{(a)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(a)}v_{(a)}+v_{(b)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(b)}v_{(b)}+2\beta\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)\,.\end{split}

(58)

For the first two terms, we have

v_{(a)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(a)}v_{(a)}\geq-\left(\sum_{x,y\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\tilde{A}_{xy}\right)\cdot\left\lVert v_{(a)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\geq-\frac{2\delta n}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\geq-\frac{2\delta}{c}

and

v_{(b)}^{\top}\tilde{A}_{(b)}v_{(b)}\geq-\left(\sum_{x,y\in\chi^{-1}(b)}\tilde{A}_{xy}\right)\cdot\left\lVert v_{(b)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\geq-\frac{2\delta n}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\geq-\frac{2\delta}{c}\,.

For the cross-term, we use that

\displaystyle 2\beta\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)

\displaystyle=2\beta\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}+2\beta\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)

\displaystyle=2\beta\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\beta\sigma^{2}\,.

Then, plugging these bounds back into
Eq.

58
, we get

\displaystyle v^{\top}\tilde{A}v\geq 2\beta\sigma^{2}-4\delta/c\,.

We also note that
\lVert v\rVert^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\frac{|\chi^{-1}(b)|}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}\beta^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}+\beta^{2}/c
and
\langle v,\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\rangle^{2}=\frac{|\chi^{-1}(b)|^{2}}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|n}\beta^{2}\leq\beta^{2}/c
, so

v^{\top}\tilde{A}v\leq\frac{1}{n}\langle v,\mathbf{1}\rangle^{2}+\lambda_{2}\left(\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\langle v,\mathbf{1}\rangle^{2}\right)\leq\lambda_{2}\sigma^{2}+\beta^{2}/c\,.

Putting everything together,

\displaystyle 2\beta\sigma^{2}-4\delta/c\leq v^{\top}\tilde{A}v\leq\lambda_{2}\sigma^{2}+\beta^{2}/c\,,

and taking
\beta=\lambda_{2}
we get

\sigma^{2}\leq\lambda_{2}/c+\frac{4\delta}{\lambda_{2}c}\leq O\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{c}+\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{2}c}\right)\,.

∎

7
Finding colorings and independent sets

7.1
Colorable models

Our most general result for
k
-colorings is the following:

Theorem 7.1

(Result for
k
-coloring)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zero on the diagonal and non-positive second eigenvalue, and let
\pi
be its stationary distribution.
Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k^{\prime}}
be a partition of
[k]
such that, for all
a,b\in S_{i}
the rows
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
of
M
are equal, and for all
a\in S_{i},b\in S_{j}
with
i\neq j
the rows
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
of
M
are distinct.
For all
i\in[k^{\prime}]
, let
a_{i}^{*}=\argmax_{a\in S_{i}}\pi(a)
, and let
p_{i}=\max\left(0,\pi(a_{i}^{*})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\neq a_{i}^{*}\in S_{i}\end{subarray}}\pi(a)\right)
.
Then, given a
d
-regular
(M,\delta)
-colorable
\lambda
-one-sided expander
G
, where
\delta,\lambda>0
are small enough, there exists an algorithm that runs in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot O_{\delta,\lambda,M}(1)
and outputs a
(k^{\prime},1-\sum_{i\in[k^{\prime}]}p_{i}+O_{M}(\sqrt{\delta}+\lambda+\delta/\lambda))
-coloring for
G
.

As a direct corollary, we approach a full coloring in the case when all rows of
M
are distinct:

Corollary 7.2

(Result for
k
-coloring, distinct rows)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zero on the diagonal, non-positive second eigenvalue, and distinct rows.
Then, given a
d
-regular
(M,\delta)
-colorable
\lambda
-one-sided expander
G
, where
\delta,\lambda>0
are small enough, there exists an algorithm that runs in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot O_{\delta,\lambda,M}(1)
and outputs a
(k,O_{M}(\sqrt{\delta}+\lambda+\delta/\lambda))
-coloring for
G
.

We start by proving two lemmas that we will use in the proof.
First, we show that the models we consider in
Theorem

7.1
are well-behaved: they have a unique stationary distribution in which each vertex has non-zero probability.

Lemma 7.3

(Model color class size lower bound)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zeros on the diagonal.
Then the stationary distribution
\pi
of
M
is unique and satisfies
\min_{a\in[k]}\pi_{a}\geq\Omega_{M}(1)
.
Furthermore, for every
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
and every
k
-partition
\chi:[n]\to[k]
of
[n]
such that
\left\lVert M-M(A,\chi)\right\rVert\leq\delta
, then also
\left\lvert\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|-\pi_{a}\right\rvert\leq 8\sqrt{\delta}k
for all
a\in[k]
.

Proof.

First, because
M
has non-positive second eigenvalue, its non-zero entries describe a connected graph.
Furthermore, for a stationary distribution
\pi
of
M
, for all
a\neq b\in[k]
we have
\pi_{a}M_{ab}=\pi_{b}M_{ba}
, so if
M_{ab},M_{ba}>0
also

\Omega_{M}(1)\leq\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}=\frac{M_{ab}}{M_{ba}}\leq O_{M}(1)\,.

Then, because the non-zero entries of
M
describe a connected graph, we can chain inequalities of this form to obtain for all
a\neq b\in[k]
the ratios
\pi_{a}/\pi_{b}
.
Together with the fact that
\sum_{a\in[k]}\pi_{a}=1
, we get that
\pi
is uniquely determined by
M
and satisfies
\min_{a\in[k]}\pi_{a}\geq\Omega_{M}(1)
.

Second, we have that
M(A,\chi)_{ab}
is
\delta
-close to
M_{ab}
for all
a,b\in[k]
.
Also, for all
a\neq b\in[k]
we have
\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|M(A,\chi)_{ab}=\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(b)|M(A,\chi)_{ba}
and
\sum_{a\in[k]}\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|=1
.
Then, if
M_{ab},M_{ba}>0
and
\delta>0
is small enough such that
\delta\leq\sqrt{\delta}M_{ab},\sqrt{\delta}M_{ba}
, also

(1-4\sqrt{\delta})\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\leq\frac{(1-\sqrt{\delta})M_{ab}}{(1+\sqrt{\delta})M_{ba}}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|}{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(b)|}=\frac{M(A,\chi)_{ab}}{M(A,\chi)_{ba}}\leq\frac{(1+\sqrt{\delta})M_{ab}}{(1-\sqrt{\delta})M_{ba}}\leq(1+4\sqrt{\delta})\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\,.

This implies that for all
a,b\in[k]
we have

(1-8\sqrt{\delta}k)\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\leq(1-4\sqrt{\delta})^{k-1}\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|}{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(b)|}\leq(1+4\sqrt{\delta})^{k-1}\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\leq(1+4\sqrt{\delta}k)\frac{\pi_{a}}{\pi_{b}}\,,

so summing over all
a\in[k]
we get

(1-8\sqrt{\delta}k)\frac{1}{\pi_{b}}\leq\frac{1}{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(b)|}\leq(1+4\sqrt{\delta}k)\frac{1}{\pi_{b}}\,,

which finally implies that
\left\lvert\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(b)|-\pi_{b}\right\rvert\leq 8\sqrt{\delta}k
for all
b\in[k]
.
∎

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_{a_{j}}
.
We have

\left\lVert(\tilde{A}-W^{\prime})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert(\tilde{A}-W^{\prime})Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert(\tilde{A}-W)Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert(W^{\prime}-W)Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\,.

For the first term on the right-hand side, we have that
\left\lVert\tilde{A}\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}-W\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq O_{M}\left(\lambda+\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\right)
for all
a\in[k]
, so using that
\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{n}
we can bound

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert(\tilde{A}-W)Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot O_{M}\left(\sqrt{\lambda+\frac{\delta}{\lambda}}\right)\,.

For the second term, we have that

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert(W^{\prime}-W)Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\lVert W^{\prime}-W\right\rVert\left\lVert Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot O_{M}(\delta)\,.

Then, also using that
\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert\geq\Omega_{M}(\sqrt{n})
, we have

\left\lVert(\tilde{A}-W^{\prime})\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_{a_{j}}}{\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}Z_{a_{j}}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq O_{M}\left(\lambda+\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\right)\,,

so we satisfy the second condition in
Theorem

5.1
with constant
O_{M}\left(\lambda+\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\right)
.

Low rank of
\tilde{A}
.

Because
\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\lambda}(\tilde{A})\leq 1
, by applying
Corollary

4.2
with
\sigma=\lambda
, we get that the bottom threshold rank of
\tilde{A}
is bounded by
\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\sqrt{2\lambda}}(\tilde{A})\leq 1/\lambda^{2}
.
Note that, using that
\lambda\leq 1
, we have that
\lambda\leq\sqrt{2\lambda}
.
Then, for the purposes of
Theorem

5.1
, we apply the theorem with eigenvalue threshold
\lambda^{\prime}=\max\{\sqrt{2\lambda},\Omega_{M}(1)\}
for some small enough
\Omega_{M}(1)
such that the conditions of the theorem are satisfied, and this allows us to take
t=O(1/(\lambda^{\prime})^{2})=O_{M}(1)
.

Rounding.

Then, by
Theorem

5.1
applied with matrices
M(\tilde{A},\chi^{\prime})
and
\tilde{A}
, we can recover in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot O_{\delta,\lambda,M}(1)
a list of
O_{\delta,\lambda,M}(1)

k^{\prime}
-partitions
\hat{\chi^{\prime}}:[n]\to[k^{\prime}]
such that, for at least one of them, it holds up to a permutation of the color classes that
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\operatorname{Unif}([n])}\mathbf{1}[\hat{\chi}^{\prime}(x)\neq\chi^{\prime}(x)]\leq O_{M}(\lambda+\delta/\lambda)
.
Let
\varepsilon=O_{M}(\lambda+\delta/\lambda)
be equal to this upper bound.
At this point, for each candidate
k^{\prime}
-partition, we apply the rounding algorithm in
Lemma

7.24
on each of its color classes to obtain
k^{\prime}
independent sets, and return the set of
k^{\prime}
independent sets that cover most vertices of
G
.

Let us analyze the approximation guarantee.
For the best
k^{\prime}
-partition, the rounding algorithm in
Lemma

7.24
will remove at most
2\varepsilon n
vertices due to misclassifications and at most
2\delta n
vertices due to edges with both endpoints in the same part with respect to the
k
-partition
\chi
.
The rounding algorithm may also need to remove a significant portion of vertices because we recover
k^{\prime}
-partitions instead of
k
-partitions — this is necessary even if the
k^{\prime}
-partition
\chi^{\prime}
is perfectly recovered.

To analyze these removals, let us start by defining
\bar{a}_{i}^{*}=\argmax_{a\in S_{i}}|\chi^{-1}(a)|
for each
i\in[k^{\prime}]
.
Then, for each
i\in[k^{\prime}]
, the rounding algorithm in
Lemma

7.24
may remove at most
\min\left(\sum_{a\in S_{i}}|\chi^{-1}(a)|,2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\neq a_{i}^{*}\in S_{i}\end{subarray}}|\chi^{-1}(a)|\right)
vertices from
(\chi^{\prime})^{-1}(i)
.
Then for each
i\in[k^{\prime}]
the number of surviving vertices is at least
\bar{p}_{i}=\max\left(0,|\chi^{-1}(a_{i}^{*})|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\neq a_{i}^{*}\in S_{i}\end{subarray}}|\chi^{-1}(a)|\right)
.
Then the algorithm returns a
(k^{\prime},1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[k^{\prime}]}\bar{p}_{i}+2\delta+2\varepsilon)
-coloring for
G
.
Finally, by
Lemma

7.3
, for
\delta>0
small enough,
\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|
and
\pi_{a}
are
O(\sqrt{\delta}k)
-close for all
a\in[k]
, so the algorithm also returns a
(k^{\prime},1-\sum_{i\in[k^{\prime}]}p_{i}+O_{M}(\sqrt{\delta})+2\varepsilon)
-coloring for
G
, where
p_{i}
is defined as in the statement of the theorem.
Recalling that
\varepsilon=O_{M}(\lambda+\delta/\lambda)
gives the stated result.
∎

7.2
3
-colorable models

For the case
k=3
, it turns out that the model has non-positive eigenvalue and distinct rows whenever its stationary distribution is strictly bounded pointwise by
1/2
.

The first observation is that we can compute explicitly the entries of
M
in terms of the stationary distribution.

Lemma 7.7

(Model entries for
3
-colorings)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zeros on the diagonal and stationary distribution
\pi
.
Then for all
a\neq b\in[3]

M_{ab}=\frac{(2\pi(a)+2\pi(b))-1}{2\pi(a)}.

Proof.

For all
a\in[3]
, we have
\sum_{b\in[3]}M_{ab}=1
.
Also for all
b\in[3]\setminus\left\{a\right\}
, we have
\pi(a)M_{ab}=\pi(b)M_{ba}
.
Hence, taking the off-diagonal entries of the
M
matrix as unknown, we have
6
variables and
6
independent linear equations, so there is a unique solution.
Using
\sum_{a\in[3]}\pi(a)=1
, for
a\neq b\neq c\in[3]
we can express it as

M_{ab}=\frac{(2\pi(a)+2\pi(b))-1}{2\pi(a)}\,.

∎

We then use this observation to deduce that, whenever the largest probability of a color class is upper bounded by
1/2-\gamma
, any two distinct rows of
M
have separation
\gamma
.

Lemma 7.8

(Row separation for
3
-colorings)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}_{\geq 0}
be a reversible row stochastic matrix with zero on the diagonal and stationary distribution
\pi
that satisfies
\max_{a\in[3]}\pi_{a}\leq 1/2-\gamma
.
Then any two distinct rows
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
of
M
satisfy
\left\lVert M^{(a)}-M^{(b)}\right\rVert\geq\gamma
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that all non-diagonal entries of
M
are bounded away from
0
.
From
Lemma

7.7
, we get for
a\neq b
that
M_{ab}=\frac{(2\pi_{a}+2\pi_{b})-1}{2\pi_{a}}
.
Because
\max_{a\in[3]}\pi_{a}\leq 1/2-\gamma
, it follows that
\pi_{a}+\pi_{b}\geq 1/2+\gamma
, so
M_{ab}\geq\frac{\gamma}{\pi_{a}}\geq\gamma
.
Then the conclusion follows because
\left\lVert M^{(a)}-M^{(b)}\right\rVert\geq M_{ab}-M_{bb}=M_{ab}\geq\gamma
.
∎

Finally, we state and prove our result specialized to
3
-colorings.

Theorem 7.9

(Result for
3
-coloring)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with adjacency matrix
A
, and let
\tilde{A}=\frac{1}{d}A
.
Let
\lambda
be the second largest eigenvalue of
\tilde{A}
.
Let
\chi:[n]\to[3]
be a
3
-partition of its vertices, and suppose that
G
has a vertex cover of all edges with both endpoints in the same part of size at most
\delta n
with respect to
\chi
.
Suppose
\max_{a\in[3]}\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|\leq 1/2-\gamma
.
Then, for
\delta,\gamma,\lambda>0
small enough, there exists an algorithm that runs in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot O_{\delta,\gamma,\lambda}(1)
and outputs a
(3,O(\lambda/\gamma^{8}+\delta/(\lambda\gamma^{8})))
-coloring for
G
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, let
M=M(\tilde{A},\chi)
be defined as in
Definition

3.3
with respect to
\tilde{A}
.
We aim again to apply
Theorem

5.1
with matrices
M
and
\tilde{A}
, and we verify that its conditions hold.

Claim 7.10
.

If
\delta\ll\gamma^{2}
, then any two rows
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
of
M
satisfy
\left\lVert M^{(a)}-M^{(b)}\right\rVert\geq\Omega(\gamma)
.

Proof.

We first argue that
M
is close to a reversible row stochastic matrix with zero on the diagonal, to which we will apply
Lemma

7.8
.
Let
M^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
be obtained from
M
by setting the diagonal entries to zero and by rescaling each row such that it sums up to
1
.
Then
M^{\prime}
is row stochastic with zero on the diagonal.
Let us now understand the stationary distribution of
M^{\prime}
.
Let
\pi
be the stationary distribution of
M
, which coincides with
\left\{\frac{1}{n}|\chi^{-1}(a)|\right\}_{a\in[3]}
.
Then
\pi_{a}M_{ab}=\pi_{b}M_{ba}
for all
a\neq b\in[3]
, so by construction also
\pi_{a}\left(\sum_{c\neq a}M_{ac}\right)M^{\prime}_{ab}=\pi_{b}\left(\sum_{c\neq b}M_{bc}\right)M^{\prime}_{ba}
for all
a\neq b\in[3]
.
Therefore, the stationary distribution of
M^{\prime}
has probability mass function proportional to
\left\{\pi_{a}\left(\sum_{c\neq a}M_{ac}\right)\right\}_{a\in[3]}
, normalized to sum up to
1
.
Finally, we note that
\max_{a\in[3]}M_{aa}\leq O(\delta/\gamma)
, which also implies that
\sum_{c\neq a}M_{ac}\geq 1-O(\delta/\gamma)
.
Then
\left\lVert M-M^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq O(\delta/\gamma)
, and also the stationary distribution of
M^{\prime}
has maximum probability mass bounded by
1/2-\gamma+O(\delta/\gamma)\leq 1/2-\Omega(\gamma)
.
By
Lemma

7.8
, any two distinct rows of
M^{\prime}
have separation
\Omega(\gamma)
.
Then, by closeness of
M
and
M^{\prime}
, we also have that any two rows of
M
have separation
\Omega(\gamma)-O(\delta/\gamma)\geq\Omega(\gamma)
.
∎

Note that we can assume that
\delta\ll\gamma^{2}
, as the guarantees of the theorem are vacuous otherwise, so we can apply
7.10
.
For the rest of the proof, we will largely repeat the analysis in the proof of
Theorem

7.1
, but using that any two distinct rows of
M
are separated and taking into account explicitly the parameter
\gamma
.
Let
W=ZMB^{-1}Z^{\top}
, with the partition matrices
Z,B
corresponding to the
3
-partition
\chi
.
We have trivially that
B^{1/2}MB^{-1/2}
is symmetric and that
\left\lVert M\right\rVert=1
.
We also have that
|\chi^{-1}(a)|\geq 2\gamma n
for all
a\in[3]
.

By
7.10
we have that any two distinct rows of
M
have separation
\Omega(\gamma)
.
For the closeness of
\tilde{A}
and
W
, we have by
Lemma

6.1
and
Lemma

7.4
that
\left\lVert\tilde{A}\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}-W\frac{Z_{a}}{\left\lVert Z_{a}\right\rVert}\right\rVert^{2}\leq O\left(\lambda/\gamma^{2}+\frac{\delta}{\gamma^{2}\lambda}\right)
for all
a\in[3]
.
For the low rank of
\tilde{A}
, we have by applying
Corollary

4.2
with
\sigma=\lambda
that
\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-\sqrt{2\lambda}}(\tilde{A})\leq 1/\lambda^{2}
, and also
\lambda\leq\sqrt{2\lambda}
.

Then we apply
Theorem

5.1
with matrices
M
and
\tilde{A}
and eigenvalue threshold
\lambda^{\prime}=\max\{\sqrt{2\lambda},\Omega(\gamma^{3/2})\}
, such that it satisfies the condition of the theorem, and such that we can take
t=O(1/(\lambda^{\prime})^{2})=O(1/\gamma^{3})=O_{\gamma}(1)
.
Then, we can recover in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot O_{\delta,\gamma,\lambda}(1)
a list of
O_{\delta,\gamma,\lambda}(1)

3
-partitions
\hat{\chi}:[n]\to[3]
such that, for at least one of them, it holds up to a permutation of the color classes that
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\operatorname{Unif}([n])}\mathbf{1}[\hat{\chi}(x)\neq\chi(x)]\leq O(\lambda/\gamma^{8}+\delta/(\lambda\gamma^{8}))
.
As in the proof of
Theorem

7.1
, for each candidate
3
-partition we apply the rounding algorithm in
Lemma

7.24
on each of its color classes to obtain
3
independent sets, and return the set of
3
independent sets that cover most vertices of
G
.
The rest of the analysis is identical to that in the proof of
Theorem

7.1
.
∎

7.3
Random planting

Instead of a regular
k
-colorable one-sided expander graph, in this section we consider randomly planting a
k
-coloring in a regular one-sided expander graph.
To aid presentation, let us first define the model and some of the notation that we will use throughout this section.

Definition 7.11

(Randomly planted
k
-coloring)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph.
Then we say the graph
G
is obtained by randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
if it is sampled according to the following procedure:

1.

Sample
\chi:[n]\to[k]
by sampling
\chi(x)\sim\operatorname{Unif}([k])
independently for each
x\in[n]
,

2.

Let
G
be a copy of
H
, and then remove from
G
each edge
\{u,v\}
for which
\chi(u)=\chi(v)
.

The following is our main recovery result.
We note that in
Theorem

7.17
, for the case when the host graph is a one-sided expander, we give an algorithm that recovers a coloring on
all
vertices of the graph.

Theorem 7.12

(Result for random planting, partial recovery)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with adjacency matrix
A_{H}
.
Suppose
\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\lambda}(\frac{1}{d}A_{H})\leq t
for some
0<\lambda<1
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
, and let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be the associated
k
-coloring.
Then, for
\lambda>0
small enough, there exists an algorithm that runs in time
n^{O(1)}\cdot\left(O_{k}(1)\right)^{t}
and outputs with high probability a
(k,O_{k}(1/d^{\Omega(1)}))
-coloring for
G
.

To prove
Theorem

7.12
, we first prove some properties of the graph
G
obtained after randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
.

Lemma 7.13

(Color class concentration in random planting)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
for some
k=O(1)
, and let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be the associated
k
-coloring.
Then, with high probability,
|\chi^{-1}(a)|=\frac{n}{k}\pm O\left(\sqrt{n\log n}\right)
for all
a\in[k]
.

Proof.

The proof follows straightforwardly from a Hoeffding bound and a union bound.
∎

Lemma 7.14

(Model entry concentration in random planting)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
for some
k=O(1)
, and let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be the associated
k
-coloring.
Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, and let
\tilde{A}=\frac{k}{(k-1)d}A
.
Let
M(\tilde{A},\chi)
be defined as in
Definition

3.3
.
Then, with high probability,
M_{ab}=\frac{1}{k-1}\pm o(1)
for all
a\neq b\in[k]
.

Proof.

Let
d^{\prime}=\frac{(k-1)d}{k}
, and for
x\in[n]
and
a\in[k]
, let
d_{xa}=\sum_{y\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\tilde{A}_{xy}
.
By linearity of expectation,

\displaystyle\E\left[\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{xb}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\;\E\left[\sum_{x\sim y\in E(H)}\left(\mathbf{1}[\chi(x)=a\land\chi(y)=b]+\mathbf{1}[\chi(x)=b\land\chi(y)=a]\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\;\frac{nd}{2}\;\frac{2}{k^{2}}\;=\;\frac{n}{k(k-1)}\,.

By the
d
-regularity of
H
, changing the color of a single vertex can increase or decrease
\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{xb}
by at most
O(1)
. Hence, McDiarmid’s inequality gives that
\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{xb}
is with probability
O(n^{-2})
within
O(\sqrt{n\log{n}})
of its expectation. Using
Lemma

7.13
and putting it all together, we get

M_{ab}=\frac{\frac{n}{k(k-1)}\pm O(\sqrt{n\log{n}})}{\frac{n}{k}\pm O(\sqrt{n\log{n}})}=\frac{1}{k-1}\pm O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)

with high probability for all
a\neq b\in[k]
.
∎

Next, we show that with high probability the graph
G
has a good variance bound.

Lemma 7.15

(Variance of random planting)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by randomly planting a
k
-coloring in
H
for some
k=O(1)
, and let
\chi:[n]\to[k]
be the associated
k
-coloring.
Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, and let
\tilde{A}=\frac{k}{(k-1)d}A
.
For
x\in[n]
and
a\in[k]
, let
d_{xa}=\sum_{y\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\tilde{A}_{xy}
.
Then, with high probability, for any
a,b\in[k]
and
\bm{x}
uniformly random in
\chi^{-1}(a)
,

\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}\leq 1/d^{\Omega(1)}\,.

Proof.

If
a=b
, we have
\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)^{2}=0
by construction, so we assume
a\neq b
.

Writing
d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}
as
\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\frac{1}{k-1}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\right)
and using that
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we get

\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\Bigl(d_{\bm{x}b}-\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}d_{\bm{x}b}\Bigr)^{2}\;\leq\;2\Bigl(\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\bigl(d_{\bm{x}b}-\tfrac{1}{k-1}\bigr)^{2}+\bigl(\tfrac{1}{k-1}-M_{ab}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

(60)

Let us define
S=\sum_{x\in\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{xb}-\frac{1}{k-1}\right)^{2}
to analyze the first term.
Let
d^{\prime}=\frac{(k-1)d}{k}
.
For any
x\in V(G)
, conditioned on
x\in\chi^{-1}(a)
, by
d
-regularity we have
d_{xb}\sim\frac{1}{d^{\prime}}\mathrm{Bin}(d,\frac{1}{k})
.
Hence, by the law of total probability and linearity of expectation, we get

\E_{\chi}\left[S\right]=\frac{n}{k}\frac{1}{d^{\prime 2}}d\frac{1}{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)=\frac{n}{(k-1)kd}\leq O(n/d)\,.

It is easy to verify that changing the color of a single vertex can increase or decrease
S
by at most
O(1)
. Therefore, by McDiarmid’s inequality,

\ProbOp\left[\,\bigl|S-\E[S]\bigr|\geq\sqrt{n\log n}\right]\;\leq\;2\exp\!\left(-\Omega\!\left(\tfrac{n\log n}{n}\right)\right)=o(1).

Hence, with high probability

S=\E[S]\pm O\left(\sqrt{n\log n}\right)=O(n/d)\pm O\left(\sqrt{n\log n}\right).

Then, using
Lemma

7.13
, with high probability

\E_{\bm{x}\sim\chi^{-1}(a)}\left(d_{\bm{x}b}-\frac{1}{k-1}\right)^{2}=\frac{S}{|\chi^{-1}(a)|}=O\!\left(1/d\right)\;+\;O\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\log n}{n}}\right)=1/d^{\Omega(1)}.

By
Lemma

7.14
, the second term of
Eq.

60
is also bounded by
\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-M_{ab}\right)^{2}\leq 1/d^{\Omega(1)}
, finishing the proof.
∎

The last ingredient to prove
Theorem

7.12
is a lemma that shows how low threshold rank is approximately preserved after planting.

Lemma 7.16

(Threshold rank kept after planting)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph with adjacency matrix
A_{H}
.
Let
G
be the graph obtained by planting a
k
-coloring in
H
(not necessarily randomly), and let
A_{G}
be its adjacency matrix.
Let
\bar{A}_{H}:=\frac{1}{c}A_{H}
and
\bar{A}_{G}:=\frac{1}{c}A_{G}
be adjacency matrices of
H
and
G
respectively normalized by the same parameter
c>0
.
For
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
and
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 1
, assuming that

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{1}}(\bar{A}_{H})\leq t_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-r_{2}}(\bar{A}_{H})\leq t_{2},

it holds that

\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-(r_{1}+r_{2})}(\bar{A}_{G})\;\leq\;k\,t_{1}+t_{2},\quad\operatorname{rank}_{\geq\,r_{1}+r_{2}}(\bar{A}_{G})\;\leq\;t_{1}+k\,t_{2}.

Proof.

Decompose
\bar{A}_{G}=\bar{A}_{H}-\bar{A}_{F}
, where
\bar{A}_{F}
is block-diagonal over the
k
color classes.
By Cauchy’s interlacing theorem, each block has
\leq t_{1}
eigenvalues
\geq r_{1}
and
\leq t_{2}
eigenvalues
\leq-r_{2}
, so
\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-r_{1}}(-\bar{A}_{F})=\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{1}}(\bar{A}_{F})\leq k\,t_{1}
and
\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{2}}(-\bar{A}_{F})=\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-r_{2}}(\bar{A}_{F})\leq k\,t_{2}
.
Hence, applying Weyl’s inequalities

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-(r_{1}+r_{2})}(\bar{A}_{H}-\bar{A}_{F})\leq\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-r_{2}}(\bar{A}_{H})+\operatorname{rank}_{\leq-r_{1}}(-\bar{A}_{F})\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{1}+r_{2}}(\bar{A}_{H}-\bar{A}_{F})\leq\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{1}}(\bar{A}_{H})+\operatorname{rank}_{\geq r_{2}}(-\bar{A}_{F})\,,

we get the two claimed bounds.
∎

We are now ready to prove

First we state a general rounding algorithm that extracts an independent set from an approximate independent set.

(Rounding an approximate independent set)
.

Let
G

n

Appendix A
Proof of top threshold rank lower bound

In this section, we prove for the sake of completeness a slight generalization of Lemma 6.1 in
[
BRS11
]
, such that it extends to all bounded-norm symmetric matrices.
The proof is essentially the same as the original proof.
We note that the original proof has a minor mistake in an application of Cauchy-Schwarz, and to fix it we need to modify the guarantee of the lemma to give a threshold rank lower bound of
(1-1/C)^{2}r
instead of the original lower bound of
(1-1/C)r
.

Lemma A.1

(Generalized restatement of Lemma 6.1 in
[
BRS11
]
)
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be symmetric with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq 1
.
Suppose there exists
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
positive semidefinite such that

\langle A,M\rangle\geq 1-\varepsilon\,,\quad\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r}\,,\quad\mathrm{Tr}(M)=1\,.

Then for all
C>1
,

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq 1-C\cdot\varepsilon}(A)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)^{2}r\,.

Proof.

Let the spectral decomposition of
A
be
A=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}
and let
k=\operatorname{rank}_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon}(A)
.
We use
\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}
to denote the subspace spanned by the eigenvectors with corresponding eigenvalues at least
1-C\varepsilon
.
Then we can rewrite
\langle A,M\rangle
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle A,M\rangle&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\langle u_{i}u_{i}^{\top},M\rangle+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\langle u_{i}u_{i}^{\top},M\rangle\\
&\leq\langle\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon},M\rangle+(1-C\varepsilon)\langle\mathbf{I}_{n}-\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon},M\rangle\\
&=C\varepsilon\langle\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon},M\rangle+(1-C\varepsilon)\langle\mathbf{I}_{n},M\rangle\,.\end{split}

(61)

For the first term, it follows by the assumption
\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r}
and Cauchy–Schwarz that

\langle\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon},M\rangle\leq\left\lVert\Lambda_{\geq 1-C\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sqrt{k}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\,.

(62)

For the second term, it follows by the assumption
\mathrm{Tr}(M)=1
that

\langle\mathbf{I}_{n},M\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(M)=1\,.

(63)

Plugging
Eq.

62
and
Eq.

63
into
Eq.

61
, it follows that

\langle A,M\rangle\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{k}{r}}+1-C\varepsilon\,.

Since by assumption
\langle A,M\rangle\geq 1-\varepsilon
, it follows by rearranging the terms that

\operatorname{rank}_{\geq 1-C\cdot\varepsilon}(A)=k\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)^{2}r\,.

∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(A)e_{i}e_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(A)\alpha_{i}^{2}

Using
d
-regularity, we have
\lambda_{1}(A)=d
.
Then, as
e_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{1}
, we get
\alpha_{1}=\left\langle e_{1},\mathbf{1}_{S}\right\rangle=\frac{|S|}{\sqrt{n}}
, so
\lambda_{1}(A)\alpha_{1}^{2}=\frac{d|S|^{2}}{n}
.
For the terms with
i>1
, as
\alpha_{i}^{2}\geq 0
, we can upper bound
\lambda_{i}(A)\alpha_{i}^{2}
by
\lambda_{2}(A)\alpha_{i}^{2}\leq cd\alpha_{i}^{2}
.
Plugging these back we get

\left\lvert E(H_{S})\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d|S|^{2}}{n}+cd\sum_{i=2}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{2}\right)\,.

Then, using
\sum_{i=2}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=|S|
gives the desired bound.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

7.19
.

Let
T:=N(S)\cup S
.
Then, as all edges touching
S
are in
E(H_{T})
, we have

e(H_{T})\geq\frac{1}{2}|S|d\,.

On the other hand, by
Lemma

7.18
, writing

and using
|S|\leq\alpha n
, we get

e(H_{T})\leq\frac{d|T|}{2}\left(\frac{|T|}{n}+c\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}x\left(c+\alpha x\right)|S|d\,.

Comparing the two bounds, and solving the resulting quadratic inequality, we get

x\geq\frac{\sqrt{c^{2}+4\alpha}-c}{2\alpha}=\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\alpha}}\,.

By using
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
and that
\left\lvert N(S)\setminus S\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert N(S)\right\rvert-|S|
we get the desired result.
∎

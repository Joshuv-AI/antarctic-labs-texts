High-accuracy iterative localization algorithm for underground mine WSNs with dynamic path loss optimization and RSSI clustering
Authors: Bin Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the insufficient localization accuracy of wireless sensor networks (WSNs) in complex underground coal mine tunnel environments caused by signal fluctuations and dynamic node movement, this paper proposes an iterative weighted centroid localization algorithm based on Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) clustering. The algorithm optimizes RSSI data using K-means clustering to dynamically acquire path loss parameters and achieves high-precision localization by integrating an improved iterative weighted centroid algorithm. The experimental data show that, compared with several currently high-performance localization algorithms, the algorithm proposed in this paper exhibits certain performance advantages in different scenarios such as adjustment of node communication radius, change of beacon node ratio, and variation of tunnel width, which improves the localization robustness in complex environments.This study provides a theoretical reference for three-dimensional localization in confined, elongated spaces such as underground mine tunnels.

Introduction

Safety monitoring in underground coal mines relies on Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) for real-time perception of environmental parameters and personnel locations. The localization accuracy of nodes directly impacts the reliability of monitoring systems1,2. However, the complex and confined environment of underground mine tunnels—characterized by coal dust, water vapor, electromagnetic interference, and elongated geometries—poses significant challenges to wireless signal propagation, leading to substantial fluctuations in Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) measurements and consequently, large localization errors3,4.

Existing localization algorithms can be broadly categorized into range-based and range-free methods. Range-based approaches (e.g., TOA, TDOA, AOA, RSSI) estimate distances or angles to achieve high accuracy but often incur high hardware costs and exhibit poor adaptability to dynamic environments5,6. While RSSI is a low-cost and hardware-friendly ranging technology, its performance is highly sensitive to environmental changes, making traditional RSSI models with fixed path loss parameters prone to significant errors in heterogeneous mine environments7,8. On the other hand, range-free methods (e.g., centroid algorithms, DV-Hop) determine node positions based on network connectivity, offering lower hardware costs but generally providing lower accuracy9,10. A critical limitation of the traditional centroid algorithm is its failure to account for the varying influences of different beacon nodes, often resulting in considerable estimation errors11,12.

Although numerous studies have attempted to improve these algorithms13–22, the vast majority are designed for scenarios assuming circular deployment regions or randomly distributed nodes. These assumptions are ill-suited for the unique elongated and confined structure of underground mine tunnels. While some literature addresses mine localization, the models are often oversimplified as linear or chain-type networks, failing to capture the actual complexity of mine layouts26–29.

Building upon existing research, this paper proposes a novel iterative weighted centroid localization algorithm integrated with dynamic path loss optimization and RSSI clustering, specifically designed for the challenging environment of underground mines. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

Dynamic Path Loss Modeling: We pioneer an RSSI optimization mechanism based on K-means clustering. By eliminating invalid signals and leveraging RSSI relationships between neighboring beacon nodes, it enables real-time fitting of environmentally adaptive path loss parameters, solving the problem of error accumulation inherent in traditional fixed-parameter models within heterogeneous mine environments.

Iterative Weighted Centroid Optimization: We design dual iterative termination conditions and an APIT position detection mechanism. By progressively replacing the farthest beacon node with a computed weighted centroid, the positioning search area is compressed, enhancing convergence accuracy and robustness.

Scenario-Driven Architecture: For the first time, under the constraint of the slender mine tunnel space, we combine signal processing (clustering), environmental modeling (dynamic parameters), and geometric optimization (iterative centroid) into a unified framework. This integration achieves strong robustness of positioning errors against variations in tunnel width, beacon ratio, and communication radius.

Related work

RSSI-based localization algorithms

RSSI-based ranging is a cost-effective solution widely adopted in WSN localization. Numerous studies have focused on improving RSSI accuracy. Peng Duo et al.14 proposed an adaptive whale neural network combined with an extended Kalman filter to reduce reliance on environmental parameters in the path loss model. Liu Shuting et al.16 and others17,29 employed hybrid filtering techniques, such as Kalman and Gaussian filters, to smooth highly fluctuating RSSI values. Researcher T. Turkoral et al.17 and Jan et al.18 investigated RSSI-based ranging and the influence of beacon node proximity on signal reliability. Liu Yun et al.19 and Li Bing et al.20 explored weighted centroid optimization and anchor node selection for RSSI and UWB positioning. Yang et al.21 researched RSSI-based fingerprinting for indoor wireless localization.

These methods typically use fixed path loss parameters that cannot adapt to dynamic mine conditions.

Centroid-based and range-free algorithms

Range-free methods, particularly centroid-based algorithms, offer simplicity and low computational overhead. The basic centroid algorithm9,10 is easy to implement but suffers from low accuracy as it ignores the relative positions and distances of beacon nodes. Wang Yutang15 applied a six-point centroid algorithm to enhance indoor positioning accuracy. Zhou Mengran et al.26 proposed selecting five beacon nodes and applying a modified weighted centroid algorithm twice. Some studies22 have also explored multidimensional scaling (MDS) for localization.

These algorithms assume isotropic node distributions and perform poorly in linear tunnel layouts.

Localization in constrained environments

A limited number of studies have specifically addressed localization in constrained environments like mines. Yi Ji et al.27 and Zhong Benyuan et al.28 investigated RSSI-based algorithms for moving targets and precise personnel positioning in coal mines, respectively. Some beacon-free or MDS-based approaches22 have been tested in linear networks.

Oversimplified geometry models fail to capture real tunnel propagation effects.

In summary, existing algorithms are not fully adequate for accurate and robust localization in underground mines due to: (1) the use of static path loss models unable to cope with environmental dynamics; (2) reliance on deployment assumptions (e.g., isotropic, circular) that are violated in tunnel geometries; and (3) oversimplified environmental models that ignore real-world complexities. Our proposed algorithm, detailed in the subsequent sections, is designed to address these specific limitations by integrating dynamic parameter estimation, RSSI clustering, and iterative centroid optimization tailored for the mine tunnel environment.

Existing localization algorithms are primarily designed for open or isotropic environments and often rely on fixed parameters, making them unsuitable for complex underground mines. To address these limitations, we propose a novel algorithm that integrates RSSI clustering for data optimization, dynamic acquisition of path loss parameters, and an iterative weighted centroid method for enhanced accuracy.

RSSI localization model

Upon receiving a broadcast signal from a beacon node, a sensor node utilizes the Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) and a signal attenuation model to estimate its distance to that beacon node.

The RSSI value received by a node is based on the attenuation loss of the wireless signal. This paper employs the log-normal shadowing model23 as the signal attenuation model, which more accurately reflects the actual conditions within underground coal mine environments. However, RSSI values are inherently unstable measurements, exhibiting high sensitivity to network environmental fluctuations. Even for identical beacon nodes at fixed distances, RSSI readings vary significantly across different environments. Furthermore, RSSI measurements diverge markedly even under identical environmental conditions when taken at distinct locations or orientations.

The confined, elongated structure of underground mine tunnels, compounded by complex environmental factors such as coal dust, water vapor, and electromagnetic interference, disrupts wireless signal propagation. These disturbances introduce substantial randomness in signal reception at nodes, leading to highly stochastic RSSI-based ranging. Consequently, relying directly on a single RSSI measurement for distance estimation yields significant deviations from true values. RSSI data must undergo optimization to minimize errors within acceptable bounds.

K-means clustering-based optimization algorithm

K-means clustering24 is a widely adopted partitioning algorithm renowned for its simplicity and computational efficiency. This study employs K-means clustering with error analysis to effectively partition RSSI measurements collected at unknown nodes, thereby filtering out invalid received signals.

Dynamic acquisition of path loss parameters

RSSI values optimized through K-means clustering effectively mitigate interference from underground environments on wireless signals. This process simultaneously reduces abrupt signal variations and noise fluctuations during propagation, yielding more accurate RSSI-based distance measurements.

Due to the interference from various aspects in the propagation process of wireless signals, the parameters of the signal path loss model are unstable. The parameters of the signal path loss model differ in different roadway sections, mine environments of different coal seams, and different time periods. Therefore, when measuring distance based on RSSI values, it is not feasible to calculate using fixed path loss parameters. Instead, path loss parameters should be dynamically obtained according to the actual location area of the node. The dynamic acquisition of path loss parameters can be estimated through the RSSI values between nearby beacon nodes.

Iterative weighted centroid algorithm

Weighted centroid algorithm

The fundamental principle of the traditional centroid localization algorithm states that when an unknown node resides within the geometric area formed by surrounding connected beacon nodes, its coordinates are determined by the centroid of this enclosing polygon.

According to the traditional centroid algorithm, in Fig. 2, the coordinates of Sc are determined by the centroid of the triangle formed by S1, S2, and S3, that is,14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left({\text{x}}_{\text{c}}\text{,}{\text{y}}_{\text{c}}\right)\text{=}\left(\frac{{\text{x}}_{\text{1}}\text{+}{\text{x}}_{\text{2}}\text{+}{\text{x}}_{\text{3}}}{\text{3}}\text{,}\frac{{\text{y}}_{\text{1}}\text{+}{\text{y}}_{\text{2}}\text{+}{\text{y}}_{\text{3}}}{\text{3}}\right)$$\end{document}

The calculation of the traditional centroid algorithm is somewhat crude, leading to considerable errors between the computed results and actual values. The weighted centroid localization algorithm determines centroid coordinates by introducing weighting coefficients based on the centroid localization algorithm. These coefficients reflect both the influence degree of beacon nodes on the position of unknown nodes and their decision-making weight for unknown node coordinates.

The traditional centroid localization algorithm yields larger coordinate errors for nodes and fails to reflect the influence of beacon nodes. Additionally, the selection of reasonable weighting coefficients has a direct impact on node localization accuracy.

Given that the coordinates of the unknown node are primarily determined by the closest reference nodes, this study employs a weighting coefficient based on the inverse of the distance to each reference node. This principle assigns a larger weight to nearer reference nodes, thereby exerting greater influence on the coordinate calculation of the unknown node.

When utilizing the centroid method to determine the coordinates of an unknown node, the centroid’s position depends on the spatial extent of the polygon formed by the reference nodes. Crucially, smaller polygonal areas yield higher centroid localization accuracy. To achieve progressively improved positioning precision, the polygonal bounds must be iteratively refined. Therefore, this study employs an iterative centroid convergence approach.

Algorithm description and experimental analysis

Algorithm description

The core concept of the iterative weighted centroid algorithm based on RSSI clustering is as follows: When an unknown node acquires multiple RSSI values from a certain beacon node, after removing invalid RSSI values through K-means clustering division, the mean value of valid RSSI is taken as the signal strength received from the beacon node. Positioning can be performed only when the unknown node receives RSSI from at least three beacon nodes. The RSSI values are sorted in descending order, and the top three are selected as references. When solving the distances from the unknown node to the three beacon nodes using the signal attenuation model, the least squares method is adopted to dynamically obtain path loss parameters in real time. Different iterative termination conditions are selected according to the positional relationship between the unknown node and reference nodes. After multiple iterations, the coordinates of the unknown node are finally obtained.

The proposed algorithm is compared against those presented in references26–28, and29.

Reference26 proposes that on the basis of the triangular centroid algorithm, five beacon nodes are selected, and the coordinate value of the unknown node is obtained by using the modified weighted centroid algorithm twice. The path loss parameter is fixed, with no clustering process or iterative optimization.

Reference27 presents the Gaussian mixture filtering + weighted centroid algorithm, where the path loss parameter is fixed. After clustering, the weighted centroid is directly applied, without iterative optimization.

Reference28 puts forward the Gaussian mixture filtering + segmented path loss parameter algorithm. The path loss parameters are fixed in segments, which are non-real-time dynamic, and there is no iterative optimization.

Reference29 proposes the Kalman filtering + adaptive Gaussian filtering algorithm, which adopts a fixed path loss model, with no clustering process or iterative optimization.

The comparative algorithms are strictly reproduced according to the optimal configurations in their original papers.

Signal propagation in such environments is susceptible to multipath effects, non-line-of-sight (NLOS) interference, and metallic attenuation. An increase in tunnel width may extend the line-of-sight distance between nodes, causing a significant decline in received signal strength (RSSI) and a consequent rise in ranging error. Notably, when the tunnel width exceeds 10 m, the RMSE growth rate accelerates, potentially exhibiting an exponential rise. Nevertheless, the performance of the proposed algorithm remains superior to other comparative algorithms.

Conclusion

In response to the issue of insufficient positioning accuracy in wireless sensor networks within the complex environment of underground mine tunnels, this study proposes an iterative weighted centroid localization algorithm based on dynamic path loss optimization and RSSI clustering. Its core innovations are as follows:

Dynamic path loss modeling: It pioneered an RSSI optimization mechanism based on K-means clustering. By eliminating invalid signals and combining the RSSI relationships between neighboring beacon nodes, it real-time fits environment adaptive path loss parameters, solving the problem of error accumulation in traditional fixed-parameter models in heterogeneous mine environments.

Iterative weighted centroid optimization: It designed dual iterative termination conditions and an APIT position detection mechanism. By gradually replacing the farthest beacon node with a weighted centroid, the positioning search area is compressed, and the convergence accuracy is improved.

Scenario-driven architecture: For the first time, under the constraint of the slender space of mine tunnels, it combines signal processing (clustering), environmental modeling (dynamic parameters) and geometric optimization (iterative centroid) to achieve strong robustness of positioning errors against tunnel width, beacon ratio and communication radius.

Although the algorithm outperforms existing methods, it still has the following limitations:

Dimensional constraint: The model is based on the assumption of a 2D plane, while actual mines are 3D spaces (such as inclined roadways and multi-layer working faces). The current centroid iteration has not been extended to the calculation of polyhedral volumes.

Dynamic adaptability: The algorithm is designed for quasi-static nodes and does not consider RSSI transients caused by the high-speed movement of mining equipment.

Energy consumption overhead: The dynamic parameter calculation and iteration process increase the communication load by approximately 23% (not quantified in the experiment), which poses a challenge to the long-term deployment of energy-constrained nodes.

Based on the above limitations, future research will focus on the following aspects: developing a 3D iterative centroid localization framework based on tetrahedral meshing and elevation measurement (such as barometers); using inertial navigation data and Kalman filtering to compensate for RSSI distortion of mobile nodes; reducing energy consumption of unknown nodes through distributed parameter fitting and edge computing; and integrating UWB and geomagnetic fingerprints to build a heterogeneous collaborative network, so as to improve the localization accuracy in extreme environments such as high dust and strong electromagnetic interference.

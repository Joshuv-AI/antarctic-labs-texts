Plasma genome-wide mendelian randomization identifies potentially causal genes in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
Authors: Kun Zhang, Puyu Shi, Anqi Li, Jiejun Zhou, Mingwei Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a complex lung disease with a very poor prognosis. Existing drugs for the treatment of IPF are still insufficient. Therefore, there is still a need to explore new drug targets for preventing and treating IPF.MethodsWe included quantitative trait loci (QTL) for genes, DNA methylation, and proteins in plasma, as well as the summary statistics for IPF. Genetic variants located within 500 kb of the gene and strongly associated with plasma exposure were used as instrumental variables. The causal association between plasma exposures and IPF was primarily estimated using summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) analysis. Five other MR methods and sensitivity analyses were employed to validate the SMR results. Bayesian tests for colocalization between QTL and IPF risk loci further strengthen the MR results.ResultsWe identified three genes and five DNA methylation sites causally associated with IPF by SMR analysis, validation of MR analysis, sensitivity analysis, and colocalization analysis. BTRC and LINC01252 were negatively associated with IPF risk (OR: 0.30, 95% CI: 0.17–0.54, FDRSMR = 0.029; OR: 0.85, 95% CI: 0.78–0.92, FDRSMR = 0.043), and RIPK4 was positively associated with IPF risk (OR: 2.60, 95% CI: 1.64–4.12, FDRSMR = 0.031). cg00045227 (OR8U8, OR: 1.16, 95% CI: 1.08–1.24, FDRSMR = 0.010), cg00577578 (GBAP1, OR: 1.23, 95% CI: 1.12–1.36, FDRSMR = 0.014), cg14222479 (ARPM1, OR: 3.17, 95% CI: 1.98–5.08, FDRSMR = 0.001), and cg19263494 (PMF1, OR: 1.20, 95% CI: 1.10–1.30, FDRSMR = 0.012) were positively associated with the risk of IPF, whereas cg07163735 (MAPT, OR: 0.22, 95% CI: 0.11–0.45, FDRSMR = 0.013) was negatively correlated with the risk of IPF.ConclusionsThis study demonstrated that genetically determined plasma levels of the BTRC, RIPK4, and LINC01252 genes, as well as methylation levels of cg00045227 (OR8U8), cg00577578 (GBAP1), cg07163735 (MAPT), cg14222479 (ARPM1), and cg19263494 (PMF1), have causal influences on the risk of IPF.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12931-024-03008-5.

Background

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, rare, and irreversible interstitial lung disease characterized by dyspnea and progressive deterioration of lung function. The prognosis for IPF is poor, with an average life expectancy of 3–5 years after diagnosis without treatment [1–3]. In North American and European populations, the incidence of cases ranges from 3 to 9 per 100,000 people per year, and this overall incidence tends to increase over time [4]. Moreover, as the global population ages, IPF is expected to have a significant impact on the global public health burden [5]. The mechanism of fibrosis in IPF is still poorly understood. It is widely believed that IPF is caused by repeated microinjury to the dysfunctional aging lung epithelium. This leads to abnormal communication between the epithelial and mesenchymal cells, resulting in an imbalance between pro-fibrotic and anti-fibrotic mediators. Ultimately, this leads to chronic fibrosis replacing the normal repair mechanisms [6, 7].

Currently, the key to the management of IPF is to slow down the progression of the disease. The most recent clinical practice guidelines recommend only two drugs for the treatment of IPF: pirfenidone and nintedanib [8]. Pirfenidone can effectively inhibit the TGF-β-induced fibrotic response, but its other mechanisms for slowing the progression of IPF remain unclear [9]. Nintedanib is a potent inhibitor of FGF, PDGF, and VEGF receptors, and previous studies have found that nintedanib has inhibitory activity on downstream signaling in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts [10]. Several other drugs have been tried in studies for the treatment of IPF, such as recombinant human pentraxin 2, pamrevlumab, inhaled treprostinil, and N-acetylcysteine [8]. Of these, IPF studies of recombinant human pentraxin 2 and pamrevlumab were terminated due to the unlikelihood of meeting or failing to meet the primary endpoint. Inhaled treprostinil and N-acetylcysteine are being used to conduct clinical studies of IPF. In short, current treatments for IPF are still unsatisfactory. Therefore, early identification of individuals at risk for IPF and the development of new therapeutic interventions to reduce the high mortality rate associated with IPF are crucial.

Environmental exposures and genetic predisposition are the two main focuses of research into the pathogenesis of IPF. Genetic biomarkers can identify patients at an increased risk of developing pulmonary fibrosis and predict the progression of the disease. Currently, the common genetic variants associated with IPF are MUC5B and DSP-related variants, which are believed to have a significant association with the risk of developing IPF [11–13].

Recently, Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis has been widely used to study causal associations and identify potential drug targets [14]. MR uses single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as a genetic tool to estimate the causal effect of an exposure on an outcome. MR avoids the bias of unobserved confounders, as well as the reverse causality [15, 16]. Summary data-based MR (SMR) utilizes independent disease genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics and quantitative trait locus (QTL) data to identify potential causative genes for a disease [17]. However, to date, no MR studies have integrated plasma gene expression QTL (eQTL), DNA methylation QTL (mQTL), and protein expression QTL(pQTL) data to investigate potential causative genetic factors for IPF. Therefore, the present study was designed to examine the causal effects of plasma gene, DNA methylation, and protein level on IPF using MR analysis.

In the present study, we performed SMR and HEIDI analyses primarily based on plasma eQTL, mQTL, and pQTL data. Secondly, the main findings were further validated using MR (TwoSampleMR R package ) analysis, sensitivity analysis, and colocalization analysis. In addition, we validated the common genetic variants associated with IPF using lung tissue eQTL data.

Methods

This study was carried out according to the guidelines of Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology using MR (Table S1).

Study design and data source

The study design and workflow of this study are shown in Fig. 1. Our analysis consisted of two parts. In the first stage, we evaluated plasma gene expression, DNA methylation, and protein expression for causality against IPF using SMR analysis methods. In the second stage, the MR (TwoSampleMR R package) analysis was performed for validation and sensitivity analysis. Additionally, we performed the colocalization analysis.

Fig. 1The study design and workflow

The study design and workflow

The cis-eQTL summary statistics are derived from the QTLGen Consortium, which provides information on 10,317 SNPs associated with traits from 31,684 individuals [18]. We extracted genetic variants located within a 500 kb window of the coding sequence from the eQTL summary data. These variants are more likely to have a specific effect on the target gene. However, eQTLGen lacks genetic information on sex chromosomes as well as on mitochondrial DNA. The mQTL summary data were obtained from a meta-analysis of peripheral blood DNA methylation data from two cohorts (n = 1,980) [19]. The study included SNPs within a 500Kb window for each probe. The summary statistics for pQTLs included 4,435 plasma proteins from 7,213 European Americans [20]. We extracted cis-SNPs within a 500 Kb window of protein-coding genes that are closely associated with protein expression. The IPF summary statistics were obtained from the latest release of the FinnGen database [21]. The cohort contains 2,018 cases and 373,064 controls. All data sources are available in Table S2.

All GWAS summary statistics were aligned to the GRCh37 reference sequence. GWAS data that used GRCh38 as the reference sequence were converted using CrossMap software to ensure alignment with the GRCh37 reference sequence [22].

Statistical analysis

SMR analysis

MR analysis is based on three core assumptions. Assumption 1: Instrumental variables (IVs) are strongly correlated with the exposure. Assumption 2: IVs are independent of confounders. Assumption 3: IVs affect the outcome solely through exposure. Here, we perform the SMR analysis from the command line using SMR software version 1.3.1 for Windows systems (https://yanglab.westlake.edu.cn/software/smr/#Overview) [17]. SMR employs a two-step least squares (2SLS) method for causal estimation, using genetic variants that are significantly associated with exposure as IVs.

This study used the P < 5e-8 threshold to select the associated QTL for the SMR test. In the heterogeneity in dependent instruments (HEIDI) test, for each probe, the analysis will only include SNPs with QTL P-values < 1.57e-03, then exclude SNPs with linkage disequilibrium (LD) r-squared with top-SNP > 0.90 or < 0.05. βexposure−IPF counts the estimated effect size of plasma exposure (gene, DNA methylation, or protein) levels on IPF. βSNP−exposure is the SNP effect size on plasma exposure. βSNP−IPF represents the effect size of SNPs on IPF. The odds ratio (OR) estimates of plasma exposure on the risk of IPF were calculated by ORexposure−IPF = exp (βexposure−IPF), with exp being the base of the natural logarithm.

We used the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control for the false discovery rate (FDR) of multiple tests. Associations with Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-values of less than 0.05 were considered significant. We also performed a HEIDI test using SMR to detect the effect of linkage. A HEIDI test with a significance level of P < 0.01 suggests that the observed association may be due to linkage. Consequently, it is recommended that this association be excluded from the analysis.

TwoSampleMR validation

To validate the results of the SMR analysis, we performed the MR analysis using five additional MR methods from the TwoSampleMR R package, including MR Egger, Weighted Median, Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW), Simple Mode, and Weighted Mode. In the MR validation analysis, we excluded IVs with an F statistic (F = Beta2/SE2) ≤ 10 to rule out weak IV bias [23]. In addition, we performed the heterogeneity test, the pleiotropy test, and the Leave-one-out sensitivity test. The analyses in this section further support our causal associations. The MR results were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Bayesian colocalization analysis

In addition to performing MR analysis to validate the SMR analysis results, we also used Bayesian colocalization analysis for further validation. Bayesian colocalization analysis is used to assess the probability that two traits share the same causal variants (Supplementary methods). We used the coloc R package (version 5.1.0) for colocalization analysis [24]. We extracted all SNPs within a 500 kb window of each top SNP in the causal association in SMR analysis for colocalization analysis. PP.H4 > 0.8 is considered evidence of colocalization of GWAS and QTL association.

Results

Analysis of genome-wide cis-eQTL with IPF

After SMR analysis, we obtained 6,951,690 (P < 5e-8) SNPs robustly associated with 15,582 transcripts in plasma and estimated the causal association of these 15,582 transcripts with IPF. To control for false positives, we performed an FDR correction for strong evidence of association (FDR < 0.05) and a HEIDI test (PHEIDI > 0.01) to investigate whether the association was due to shared causal variation rather than linkage. Thus, we identified 6 plasma genes causally associated with IPF (BTRC, THBS3, CD151, RIPK4, LINC01252, and RP11-259G18.3 by SMR and HEIDI tests. The results showed that increased expression of BTRC (OR: 0.30, 95% CI: 0.17–0.54, FDRSMR = 0.029), CD151(OR: 0.85, 95% CI: 0.78–0.92, FDRSMR = 0.029), LINC01252 (OR: 0.85, 95% CI: 0.78–0.92, FDRSMR = 0. 043) and RP11-259G18.3 (OR: 0.60, 95% CI: 0.48–0.74, FDRSMR = 0.003)was significantly associated with a decreased risk of IPF respectively, whereas increased expression of THBS3 (OR: 1.27, 95% CI: 1.13–1.43, FDRSMR = 0.029) and RIPK4 (OR: 2.60, 95% CI: 1.64–4.12, FDRSMR = 0.031) was associated with increased risk of IPF (Fig. 2, Table S3).

Subsequently, we validated causal associations using other MR methods in the twosampleMR R package to ensure the reliability of the SMR results. The F statistic > 10 of all IVs avoided the weak IV bias(Table S4). The results showed that BTRC (1 IV), RIPK4 (2 IVs), LINC01252 (2 IVs), and RP11-259G18.3 (1 IV) remained causally associated with IPF (Table S5). The IVs of RIPK4 and LINC01252 satisfied the heterogeneity test, but there were insufficient SNPs for the pleiotropy test and Leave-one-out sensitivity(Table S5). Further colocalization analyses of BTRC, RIPK4, and LINC01252 resulted in PP.H4 > 0.80, demonstrating that their expression and IPF share causal variants (Fig. 2, Figure S2). In addition, we validated common genetic variants (MUC5B and DSP) associated with IPF using lung tissue eQTL data (Table S8).

Analysis of genome-wide cis-mQTL with IPF

We obtained 13,020,279 (P < 5e-8) SNPs strongly associated with 65,440 DNA methylation sites in plasma. For SMR and HEIDI tests of causal associations between plasma DNA methylation and IPF, we identified 15 causal associations between 15 unique genetic loci and IPF, of which the causal associations corresponding to five genetic loci were further validated. cg00045227 (OR8U8, OR: 1.16, 95% CI: 1.08–1.24, FDRSMR = 0.010), cg00577578 (GBAP1, OR: 1.23, 95% CI: 1.12–1.36, FDRSMR = 0.014), cg14222479 (ARPM1, OR: 3.17, 95% CI: 1.98–5.08, FDRSMR = 0.001) and cg19263494 (PMF1, OR: 1.20, 95% CI: 1.10–1.30, FDRSMR = 0.012) were positively associated with the risk of IPF, whereas cg07163735 (MAPT, OR: 0.22, 95% CI: 0.11–0.45, FDRSMR = 0.013) was negatively associated with the risk of IPF (Fig. 3, Table S6). With the exception of cg11117266, the MR validation analysis yielded causal relationships consistent with the SMR analyses. In the MR validation analysis, cg00045227 (OR8U8) had 6 SNPs as IVs, satisfied the heterogeneity test and pleiotropy test, and its MR results were confirmed robust by Leave-one-out sensitivity (Table S7, Figure S1). Further, we performed the colocalization analysis to avoid the effect of LD on the causal association. The results of the colocalization analysis suggested that five methylation sites, including cg00045227 (OR8U8), cg00577578 (GBAP1), cg07163735 (MAPT), cg14222479 (ARPM1), and cg19263494 (PMF1), and IPF shared common causal variants (all PP.H4 > 0.80, Fig. 3, Figure S3).

Analysis of genome-wide cis-pQTL with IPF outcomes

After SMR analysis, we obtained 526,719 (P < 5e-8) SNPs robustly associated with 1,521 proteins in plasma and estimated the causal association of these proteins with IPF risk. However, no evidence of a causal association of these 1,521 plasma proteins with IPF was found in the FDR-corrected SMR analysis results.

Discussion

In this study, we identified plasma gene expression and DNA methylation sites with putative causal associations for IPF. Our results showed the expression levels of three plasma genes (BTRC, RIPK4, and LINC01252) and the methylation levels of five methylation sites (cg00045227 (OR8U8), cg00577578 (GBAP1), cg07163735 (MAPT), cg14222479 (ARPM1), and cg19263494 (PMF1)) were associated with IPF risk, providing new genetic susceptibility factors for IPF. In addition, we validated the existence of the causal effect of MUC5B and DSP on the risk of IPF using lung tissue eQTL data, which validated the reliability of our methodology.

BTRC encodes β-TrCP1, which is a member of the F-box protein family [25]. β-TrCP1 increases proteasomal degradation of NLRP3 [26]. Several studies have shown that inflammatory vesicle NLRP3 is over-activated in patients with IPF, and data from animal models of pulmonary fibrosis confirm the role of NLRP3 in the development of chronic lung injury and pulmonary fibrosis [27–29]. Our MR results found that elevated plasma BTRC gene expression contributes to a reduced risk of IPF, which supports its causal relationship with IPF via degradation of NLRP3 presentation. RIPK4 was originally identified as a regulator involved in protein kinase C [30]. Studies have now revealed that RIPK4 plays a crucial role in inflammatory signaling and inflammatory cell death. For example, RIPK4 both induces ubiquitination of the NF-kB essential modulator and subsequent NF-κB activation [31]. NF-κB is involved in the process by which senescent alveolar epithelial cells promote collagen accumulation in fibroblasts, which leads to pulmonary fibrosis [32]. Our MR yielded consistent results that elevated plasma RIPK4 gene expression levels would increase the risk of IPF. LINC01252 is a type of long-stranded noncoding RNA, and we identified that its plasma level was negatively associated with the risk of IPF. However, there is still a lack of functional studies on LINC01252, which is to be further explored for its mechanism of reducing the risk of IPF.

DNA methylation is an epigenetic pathway that has been reported to be involved in the development of IPF [33]. We estimated the causal effect of plasma DNA methylation on the risk of IPF. OR8U8 is a member of the OR gene family 8, and U subfamily. Some studies suggest that OR genes regulate physiological processes in human airway smooth muscle cells and are associated with chronic inflammatory airway diseases, including asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [34, 35]. GBAP1, for which little information is available to date. The few studies that have been done have found GBAP1 to be associated with liver and gastric cancer [36, 37]. The MAPT gene is well known to encode the microtubule-associated protein tau [38]. Recent studies have found genome-wide significant loci near MAPT that are associated with lung function and pulmonary fibrosis [12, 39]. ARPM1 has mainly been found to be associated with spermatogenesis and function [40, 41], while PMF1 was shown to be associated with bladder cancer and rheumatoid arthritis [42, 43]. Our study found that the methylation levels of cg00045227 (OR8U8), cg00577578 (GBAP1), cg07163735 (MAPT), cg14222479 (ARPM1), and cg19263494 (PMF1) were causally associated with IPF. However, the mechanism by which the methylation sites of the above genes affect IPF remains unclear and needs further exploration.

The major strength of this study is that we integrated plasma eQTL, mQTL, and pQTL data to provide a comprehensive MR analysis of plasma gene levels, DNA methylation, and protein levels causally related to IPF. Second, we performed the primary analysis using SMR and validated it using five additional MR methods. We also performed sensitivity and colocalization analyses to ensure the robustness of our findings. Third, all of the GWAS in this study are from populations of European ancestry, which minimizes bias from distinct genetic backgrounds.

Our study also has some limitations. First, the eQTL and mQTL data lacked genetic information on the sex chromosomes. Second, we did not obtain significant estimates in pQTL and IPF, which could be explained by the fact that the pQTL dataset is not yet well developed. Third, in the MR validation analyses, there were not enough IVs (less than 3) for MR-Egger analysis for RIPK4 and LINC01252, and there was only one IV for each of the five exposures (BTRC, cg00577578 (GBAP1), cg07163735 (MAPT), cg14222479 (ARPM1), and cg19263494 (PMF1)), which also did not allow sensitivity and heterogeneity analyses. These limitations may reduce the reliability of MR validation analysis results. In addition, we could not obtain individual data in GWAS to stratify the analysis according to sex, age, etc. Finally, although our study reduces bias caused by different genetic backgrounds, it also limits the generalization of the results to populations of large other ancestries.

Conclusions

In this study, potential causal relationships between plasma gene, DNA methylation, and protein levels and IPF were estimated using MR methods. The identified plasma genes and DNA methylation sites may serve as potential drug targets for IPF, but further studies are needed to explore the underlying mechanisms.

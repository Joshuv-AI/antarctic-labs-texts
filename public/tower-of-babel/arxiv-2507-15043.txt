A Hessian criterion for totally positive Toeplitz matrices and a new proof of Cattani’s theorem in two variables
Chris McDaniel

Abstract
. Cattani’s theorem for graded Artinian Gorenstein algebras states that the ordinary Hodge-Riemann relations imply the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations under certain conditions. We give a new proof of this result for codimension two algebras. Our proof leads to a Hessian criterion for totally positive Toeplitz matrices, which is analogous to the Wronskian criterion for totally positive flags discovered recently by S. Karp.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a real homogeneous polynomial, of some fixed degree and in some fixed number of variables, although in this paper, with the sole exception of this paragraph, we will focus exclusively on the two variable case. Let
A_{F}
be its associated Artinian Gorenstein algebra. We say that
A_{F}
satisfies the ordinary Hodge-Riemann relations on a subset of linear forms
U
if the signature of the multiplication maps, defined by certain powers of a single arbitrary linear form in
U
, satisfy a certain alternating property, and we say that it satisfies the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations on
U
if the same signature conditions are satisfied by products of arbitrary (and possibly distinct) linear forms in
U
. Mixed Hodge-Riemann relations always imply the ordinary ones by a simple specialization argument, but the converse does not hold in general. On the other hand, E. Cattani
[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
has shown that under certain conditions, ordinary Hodge-Riemann relations do, in fact, imply the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations. His proof uses deep results on variations of Hodge structures; of central importance is a result obtained previously by Cattani, A. Kaplan, and W. Schmid
[
2
, Lemma 1.16]
called the “descent lemma”. In this paper, we give an elementary proof of Cattani’s theorem in the two variable case (
Theorem

1
) that avoids the “descent lemma” by appealing to results from
[
7
]
relating Hodge-Riemann relations to Hessians and Toeplitz matrices.

In a recent paper
[
7
]
with P. Macias Marques and A. Seceleanu, we showed that in two variables, the ordinary Hodge-Riemann relations on
A_{F}
are equivalent to a positivity condition on the certain signed determinants of the Hessian matrices for
F
. We also showed that the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations are equivalent to the total positivity (or non-negativity) of a certain Toeplitz matrix associated to
F
; we called such
F
higher Lorentzian polynomials. In these terms, Cattani’s theorem gives a Hessian criterion for total positivity (or non-negativity) of Toeplitz matrices (
Theorem

2
,
Theorem

3
). In this paper, we give an independent proof of this criterion, which in turn yields a new proof of Cattani’s theorem in two variables. As a consequence, we also derive a Hessian criterion for positivity (or non-negativity) of the coefficients of a homogeneous bivariate form (
Corollary

1
), which appears to be new.

Some of the main ideas in this paper are inspired by the recent paper by S. Karp
[
6
]
, which establishes a Wronskian criterion for detecting points in the totally positive flag variety. In two variables, the Wronskian and the Hessian are (almost) dual objects in a certain sense that we will not go into here. One manifestation of this duality lies in their Plücker expansion formulas. In this paper, the Plücker expansion of the Hessian polynomial (
Lemma

5
) plays a key role, just as it did for Wronskians in Karp’s paper.

The critical observation here is that totally positive Toeplitz matrices, stratified by rank, are (unions of) connected components of certain open sets in the Euclidean space of all (real) Toeplitz matrices (
Lemma

8
). This seems to be analogous to a result of Lusztig that identifies the set of totally positive complete (real) flags as a connected component of a certain open set defined by non-vanishing Plücker coordinates. It would be interesting to investigate further this analogy between totally positive Toeplitz matrices and totally positive flags; in fact we suspect it points to a deeper connection between Hodge theory and (real) Schubert calculus, but we do not explore this idea here.

This paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we define all pre-requisite notions and facts, some proofs of which we have included, but some of which we have out-sourced, either to the paper
[
7
]
or the book
[
3
]
. In particular, we prove
Lemma

5
and
Lemma

8
mentioned above. In
Section

3
, we prove Cattani’s theorem in two variables,
Theorem

1
, or rather its equivalent formulations in
Theorem

2
and
Theorem

3
. We also prove
Corollary

1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Artinian Gorenstein algebras and Macaulay dual generators

Let
R={\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
denote the polynomial ring in two variables over the real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the standard grading, i.e.
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1
. Let
R_{d}
denote the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space of homogeneous forms of degree
d
. Let
Q={\mathbb{R}}[X,Y]
be another standard graded polynomial ring, and let
R
act on
Q
by partial differentiation, i.e.
x\circ F=\frac{\partial F}{\partial X}
,
y\circ F=\frac{\partial F}{\partial Y}
. Given
F\in Q_{d}
, its annihilator ideal is the (homogeneous) ideal

\operatorname{Ann}(F)=\left\{f\in R\ |\ f\circ F\equiv 0\right\}

and its associated
standard graded oriented Artinian Gorenstein algebra
is the quotient algebra

A=A_{F}=\frac{R}{\operatorname{Ann}(F)};

it is a finite dimensional graded
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space with graded components
A_{i}
, and socle degree
d
, and such that multiplication defines perfect pairings between complimentary graded components

(-,-)_{i}\colon A_{i}\times A_{d-i}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\ (\alpha,\beta)_{i}=\left(\alpha\cdot\beta\right)\circ F.

The polynomial
F
is sometimes the
Macaulay dual generator
of
A
. We define its
normalized coefficients

(c_{0},\ldots,c_{d})
by

F=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{d}{k}c_{k}X^{k}Y^{d-k}.

The Hilbert function of
A_{F}
is the
(d+1)
-tuple of positive integers

H(A_{F})=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}),\ h_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(A_{i}\right).

The
Sperner number of
F
(or
A_{F}
)
is defined to be the maximum value in the Hilbert function, i.e.

s(F)=\max\left\{h_{i}\ |\ 0\leq i\leq d\right\}.

The Hilbert function is determined by the socle degree
d=\deg(F)
and the Sperner number
s=s(F)
, given by the formula:

H(A_{F})=(1,2,\ldots,s-1,s,s,\ldots,s,s,s-1,\ldots,2,1);

the Sperner number is also characterized as the minimum degree of a generator in the annihilator ideal
\operatorname{Ann}(F)
; see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem 1.44]
.

The following elementary but useful result says that a polynomial obtained from
F
by a linear change of coordinates defines an isomorphic algebra. Although this fact is certainly well known in the literature on Macaulay duality, see e.g.
[
5
, Sections 1.3,2.3]
or
[
8
, Lemma 1.5.1]
, we include a proof here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G(X,Y)=F(pX+rY,qX+sY)
for some real
p,q,r,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
ps-qr\neq 0
. Then the map of algebras defined by

\phi\colon R\rightarrow R,\ \phi(x)=px+qy,\ \phi(y)=rx+sy

passes to an isomorphism of quotient algebras

\phi=\overline{\phi}\colon A_{G}=\frac{R}{\operatorname{Ann}(G)}\rightarrow A_{F}=\frac{R}{\operatorname{Ann}(F)}.

Proof.

Consider the change of coordinates map on
Q
defined by

\phi^{*}\colon Q\rightarrow Q,\ \phi^{*}(X)=pX+rY,\ \phi^{*}(Y)=qX+sY.

Then it suffices to show that
\phi
and
\phi^{*}
are adjoint with respect to the pairing

\langle-,-\rangle\colon R\times Q\rightarrow Q,\ \langle f,F\rangle=f\circ F.

By bi-linearity, it suffices to check this for
f
and
F
monomials. Moreover since
\phi
and
\phi^{*}
are graded algebra maps, and
R
and
Q
are generated in degree one, it suffices to check this for
f
and
F
linear forms, say
f=ax+by
and
F=cX+dY
. We have

\displaystyle\phi(ax+by)\circ(cX+dY)=

\displaystyle((ap+br)x+(aq+bs)y)\circ(cX+dY)=(c(ap+br)+d(aq+bs))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(cp+dq)+b(cr+ds)=(ax+by)\circ((cp+dq)X+(cr+ds)Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ax+by)\circ\phi^{*}\left(cX+dY\right)

and the result follows.
∎

2.2.
Hodge-Riemann relations

Let
A=A_{F}
be as above. Given a linear form
\ell\in A_{1}
, define the
i^{th}
primitive subspace as the kernel of the multiplication map by
\ell
,

P_{i,\ell}=\ker\{\times\ell^{d-2i+1}\colon A_{i}\rightarrow A_{d-i+1}\};

it is a, possibly zero, subspace of
A_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, and for
i>\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, we define
P_{i,\ell}
to be the zero subspace. Define the
i^{th}

Lefschetz form
by

(-,-)_{i}^{\ell}\colon A_{i}\times A_{i}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\ (\alpha,\beta)_{i}^{\ell}=(-1)^{i}(\ell^{d-2i}\alpha\beta)\circ F.

We say that the pair
\left(A_{F},\ell\right)
satisfies the
(ordinary) Hodge-Riemann relations up to degree
i
(ordinary HRR
i
) if the
j^{th}
Lefschetz form
(-,-)_{j}^{\ell}
is positive definite on the
j^{th}
primitive subspace
P_{j,\ell}
for all degrees
j
,
0\leq j\leq i
, i.e.

(\alpha,\alpha)_{j}^{\ell}=(-1)^{i}\left(\ell^{d-2i}\alpha^{2}\right)\circ F>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall 0\neq\alpha\in P_{j,\ell}\\
\forall 0\leq j\leq i\\
\end{cases}

For any subset of non-zero linear forms
U\subset A_{1}
, we say that
A
satisfies the ordinary Hodge-Riemann relations up to degree
i
on
U
(ordinary HRR
{}_{i}(U)
) if
(A_{F},\ell)
satisfies ordinary HRR
i
for each
\ell\in U
.

Given a
(d+1)
-tuple of linear forms
\mathcal{L}=(\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\in\left(A_{1}\right)^{d+1}
, define the
i^{th}
mixed Lefschetz form

\left(-,-\right)^{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\colon A_{i}\times A_{i}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\ \left(\alpha,\beta\right)_{i}^{\mathcal{L}}=(-1)^{i}\left(\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d-2i}\alpha\beta\right)\circ F

and the
i^{th}
mixed primitive subspace

P_{i,\mathcal{L}}=\ker\{\times\ell_{0}\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d-2i}\colon A_{i}\rightarrow A_{d-i+1}\}.

We say that the pair
\left(A_{F},\mathcal{L}\right)
satisfies the
mixed Hodge-Riemann relations up to degree
i
(mixed HRR
i
) if the
j^{th}
mixed Lefschetz form
(-,-)^{\mathcal{L}}_{j}
is non-degenerate on
A_{j}
and positive definite on the
j^{th}
mixed primitive subspace
P_{j,\mathcal{L}}
for all degrees
j
,
0\leq j\leq i
, i.e.

(\alpha,\alpha)_{j}^{\mathcal{L}}=(-1)^{j}\left(\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d-2j}\alpha^{2}\right)\circ F>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall 0\neq\alpha\in P_{j,\mathcal{L}}\\
\forall 0\leq j\leq i\\
\end{cases}

For any subset of non-zero linear forms,
U\subset A_{1}
, we say that
A_{F}
satisfies the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations up to degree
i
on
U
(mixed HRR
{}_{i}(U)
) if
(A_{F},\mathcal{L})
satisfies mixed HRR
i
for all
\mathcal{L}\in U^{d+1}
.

Note that the conditions for the Hodge-Riemann relations in degree
i
are vacuous for
i>\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, since the primitive subspace is zero in those degrees. For this reason, we will restrict
i
to the range
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
. In fact, note that in our case, i.e. two variables, the primitive subspaces only live in degrees
i
for
0\leq i\leq s-1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
where
s
is the Sperner number defined above. In particular, if
A_{F}
satisfies ordinary or mixed HRR
{}_{i}(U)
for
i\geq s-1
, then it must also satisfy HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(U)
by default.

It is clear that for any subset
U
and for any degree
i
,
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, mixed HRR
{}_{i}(U)
implies ordinary HRR
{}_{i}(U)
. Indeed, for any
\ell\in U
, take
\mathcal{L}=(\ell,\ell,\ldots,\ell)\in U^{d+1}
. Then mixed HRR
i
for
(A_{F},\mathcal{L})
is equivalent to ordinary HRR
i
for
(A_{F},\ell)
. In general, the converse is not true however. On the other hand, we have the following result, due to E. Cattani
[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
(although he works in the more general context of “Hodge-Lefschetz modules”).

Theorem 1

(Cattani 2008)
.

If
U
is a convex cone, then
A_{F}
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(U)
if and only if it satisfies mixed HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(U)
.

By a convex cone, we mean a set of linear forms closed under addition and scaling by positive constants, and generated by finitely many linear forms; these are sometimes also called convex polyhedral cones. We shall sometimes abuse notation slightly and refer to a convex cone of linear forms
U
as a subset of
R_{1}
and also its image
U
in the quotient
A_{1}
.

The convex cone condition on
U
in
Theorem

1
cannot be dropped, see e.g.
[
7
, Example 2.10]
. Moreover, in general, the HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}
cannot be replaced by HRR
i
for
i<\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, e.g.
[
7
, Example 5.1]
.
The following is an immediate consequence of
Lemma

1
; for a proof see
[
7
, Corollary 4.13]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\phi\colon R\rightarrow R
be a graded algebra isomorphism and let
\phi^{*}\colon Q\rightarrow Q
be its adjoint linear change of coordinates as in
Lemma

1
. For any subsets
U_{1},U_{2}\subset R_{1}
, if
A_{F}
satisfies the ordinary, respectively mixed, HRR
{}_{i}(U_{1})
and
\phi(U_{2})\subset U_{1}
, then
A_{G}
must satisfy the ordinary, respectively mixed, HRR
{}_{i}(U_{2})
.

In light of
Lemma

2
, it suffices to prove
Theorem

1
for one specific cone
U
.
For the remainder of this paper, we fix
U\subset R_{1}
to be the open cone of strictly positive linear forms

U=\left\{ax+by\ |\ a,b>0\right\}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{>0}

and
\overline{U}\subset R_{1}
its (punctured) closure, or the cone of non-negative and non-zero linear forms

\overline{U}=\left\{ax+by\ |\ a,b\geq 0,\ (a,b)\neq(0,0)\right\}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

In
[
7
]
, we called a polynomial
F
such that
A_{F}
satisfies mixed HRR
{}_{i}(U)

i
-Lorentzian
, and
strictly
i
-Lorentzian
if it satisfies mixed HRR
{}_{i}(\overline{U})
, with
U
and
\overline{U}
above.

2.3.
Higher Hessians and Toeplitz matrices

Given
F
as above, and
i
satisfying
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, define the
i^{th}
Hessian matrix of
F
to be the
(i+1)\times(i+1)
polynomial matrix

\operatorname{Hess}_{i}(F)=\left(x^{p+q}y^{2i-p-q}\circ F=\frac{\partial^{2i}F}{\partial X^{p+q}\partial Y^{2i-p-q}}\right)_{0\leq p,q\leq i};

its entries are homogeneous polynomials of degree
d-2i
, and for any real values of the variables
X
and
Y
, it is a real symmetric matrix. Define the
i^{th}
Hessian polynomial of
F
by

(1)

H^{F}_{i}=H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)=(-1)^{\left\lfloor\frac{i+1}{2}\right\rfloor}\det\left(\operatorname{Hess}_{i}(F)\right);

for
i=0
, we take
H_{0}^{F}=F
, and the
0^{th}
Hessian matrix is the
1\times 1
matrix with entry
F
. The strange sign on the right hand side of
Equation

1
is justified by the following result; see
[
7
, Lemma 3.11]
:

Lemma 3
.

Fix a homogeneous form
F\in Q_{d}
of degree
d
and sperner number
s=s(F)
, and fix
i
,
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
. Then

(1)

A_{F}
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{i}(\overline{U})
if and only if

H_{j}^{F}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\\
\forall 0\leq j\leq\min\{i,s-1\}\\
\end{cases}

(2)

A_{F}
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{i}(U)
if and only if

H_{j}^{F}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{>0}\\
\forall 0\leq j\leq\min\{i,s-1\}\\
\end{cases}

Remark 1
.

One can show that for
i\geq s
, the polynomial
H_{i}^{F}
must be identically zero, due to the fact that for
i\geq s
, the monomials
\left\{x^{p}y^{i-p}\right\}_{0\leq p\leq i}
satisfy a dependence relation in
A_{i}
. For
0\leq i\leq s-1
, the
i^{th}
Hessian matrix
\operatorname{Hess}_{i}(F)
is equal to (a non-zero constant multiple of) the matrix for the
i^{th}
Lefschetz multiplication map

\times\ell^{d-2i}\colon\left(A_{F}\right)_{i}\rightarrow\left(A_{F}\right)_{d-i},\ \ell=X\cdot x+Y\cdot y\in\left(A_{F}\right)_{1};

see e.g.
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Given a homogeneous
d
-form
F\in Q_{d}
, write

F=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{d}{k}c_{k}X^{k}Y^{d-k},

and define the
i^{th}
normalized Toeplitz matrix of
F
,
\phi^{i}(F)
, to be the
(i+1)\times(d-i+1)
Toeplitz matrix defined by the normalized coefficient sequence of
F
, i.e.

(2)

\phi^{i}_{d}(F)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}c_{i}&c_{i+1}&\cdots&c_{d}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{0}&c_{1}&\cdots&c_{d-i}\\
\end{array}\right).

According to
[
7
, Lemma 4.5]
, for
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
, the rank of
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
satisfies

(3)

\operatorname{rank}\left(\phi^{i}_{d}(F)\right)=\min\{i+1,s(F)\}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(A_{F}\right)_{i}\right).

The matrix
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is closely related to the catalecticant matrix of
F
in
[
5
, Definition 1.2]
.

Recall that a matrix is totally non-negative, respectively totally positive, if all of its minor determinants are non-negative, respectively positive.
The following result was proved in
[
7
, Theorems 1,2]
:

Lemma 4
.

Fix a homogeneous form
F\in Q_{d}
of degree
d
and Sperner number
s=s(F)
, and fix
i
,
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
. Then

(1)

A_{F}
satisfies mixed HRR
{}_{i}(\overline{U})
if and only if
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is totally positive.

(2)

A_{F}
satisfies mixed HRR
{}_{i}(U)
if and only if
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative.

In the language of
[
7
]
,
Lemma

4
characterizes strictly
i
-Lorentzian and
i
-Lorentzian polynomials

in terms of total positivity and total non-negativity, respectively, of their Toeplitz matrices
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
. We shall use the language of Toeplitz matrices, instead of higher Lorentzian polynomials, from here on.

Using
Lemma

3
and
Lemma

4
,
Theorem

1
can be restated in terms of Hessian polynomials and Toeplitz matrices:

.

Let
F\in Q_{d}
be any homogeneous

-form of Sperner number

. Then

(1)

\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
is totally positive if and only if

H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\\
\forall 0\leq i\leq s-1\\
\end{cases}

(2)

\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)

2.4.
Plücker expansion of the Hessian

The following is a useful formula that expresses the Hessian polynomials in terms of the Plücker coordinates of the matrix
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
. First we need some notation. Let
A\in\mathcal{M}_{m,n}
(
=
the set of real
m\times n
matrices) for
m\leq n
, and for any
1\leq k\leq m
and any
k
-subsets
I,J\in\binom{[n]}{m}
, let
A_{I,J}
denote the
k\times k
submatrix whose rows and columns are the rows and columns of
A
indexed by
I
and
J
, respectively, and let

\Delta_{I,J}(A)=\det(A_{I,J})

denote the corresponding minor. If
k=m
, then
I=[m]
and we write this maximal minor as
\Delta_{J}(A)
. These maximal minors
\{\Delta_{J}(A)\ |\ J\in\binom{[n]}{m}\}
are sometimes called the
Plücker coordinates of the matrix
A
, since they uniquely determine the point in projective space representing the equivalence class of
A
as a point
[A]\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in the Grassmannian of
m
-planes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
; see
[
6
, Section 2]
and references therein for further details.

Given an
m
-subset
J=\{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n\}
, let
\lambda(J)=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m})
denote the partition with parts defined by

\lambda_{k}=i_{m-k+1}-(m-k+1).

Note that
\lambda(J)
is a partition with part sizes at most
n-m
and number of (non-zero) parts at most
m
; in other words
\lambda(J)
is a partition whose Young diagram fits inside a rectangle of size
m\times(n-m)
. In fact, it is a well known combinatorial fact that
\lambda
defines a map from the set of
m
-subsets of
[n]
and partitions fitting inside the
m\times(n-m)
rectangle. Let

\lambda^{\prime}(J)=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\geq\cdots\lambda_{n-m}^{\prime})
denote the conjugate partition, defined by

\lambda^{\prime}_{k}=\#\{\lambda_{i}\geq k\}.

Then
\lambda^{\prime}(J)
fits inside the conjugate
(n-m)\times m
rectangle.
Define the number

N^{\prime}_{J}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n-m}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i+j}{j-i};

Then for each
J\in\binom{[n]}{m}
,
N^{\prime}_{J}
is a positive integer which counts the number of semi-standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda^{\prime}(J)
using the numbers in
[n-m]
. Let
D_{i}=(i+1)(d-2i)
, and denote the size of the partition by

|\lambda(J)|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-m}^{\prime}=|\lambda^{\prime}(J)|.

The following can be deduced from
[
7
, Lemma 4.19]
, but we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 5
.

For any homogeneous
d
-form
F\in Q_{d}
with Sperner number
s
, and for any
i
satisfying
0\leq i\leq s-1
, the
i^{th}
Hessian polynomial of
F
satisfies

H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)=\left(\frac{d!}{(d-2i)!}\right)^{i+1}\cdot\sum_{J\in\binom{[d-i+1]}{i+1}}N^{\prime}_{J}\cdot\Delta_{J}\left(\phi^{i}_{d}(F)\right)\cdot X^{|\lambda(J)|}\cdot Y^{D_{i}-|\lambda(J)|}.

Proof.

We extend the normalized coefficient sequence
(c_{0},\ldots,c_{d})
by zeros infinitely in both directions and define its associated bi-infinite upper triangular Toeplitz matrix

\phi_{d}(F)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\cdots&c_{d}&0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
0&c_{0}&c_{1}&\cdots&c_{d-1}&c_{d}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&c_{0}&\cdots&c_{d-2}&c_{d-1}&c_{d}&0&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&c_{0}&&\cdots&&c_{d}&\cdots\\
\end{array}\right);

succinctly we write
\phi_{d}(F)=\left(c_{q-p}\right)_{p,q\geq 0}.

Define two infinite subsets of lattice points in the plane

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=

\displaystyle\left\{A_{p}=(-p,p)\ |\ p\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=

\displaystyle\left\{B_{q}=(-q,d-2i+q)\ |\ q\geq 0\right\}

and define the bi-infinite lower triangular Toeplitz matrix
W_{d-2i}(X,Y)
to be the weighted path matrix associated to the bipartite directed graph consisting of NE lattice paths from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
with E-edges weighted by
X
and N-edges weighted by
Y
; explicitly,
W_{d-2i}(X,Y)=\left(\Omega_{p,q}\right)_{p,q\geq 0}
where

\Omega_{p,q}=\sum_{P\colon A_{p}\to B_{q}}\omega(P)=\binom{d-2i}{p-q}X^{p-q}Y^{d-2i-p+q}

where the binomial coefficient is zero if
p-q<0
or
p-q>d-2i
.
Then one can easily check, e.g.
[
7
, Lemma 4.19]
, that the
i^{th}
permuted Hessian matrix occurs as a minor of the product of bi-infinite submatrices, specifically if
P_{i}
is the
(i+1)\times(i+1)
permutation matrix for the longest permutation
p\mapsto i-p,\ 0\leq p\leq i
(i.e. the anti-identity map) then we have:

P_{i}\cdot\operatorname{Hess}_{i}(F)=\frac{d!}{(d-2i)!}\cdot\left(\phi_{d}(F)\cdot W_{d-2i}(X,Y)\right)_{I,J},\ \begin{cases}I=\{0,\ldots,i\}\\
J=\{i,\ldots,2i\}\\
\end{cases}

(note we are starting our indexing at
0
instead of
1
). It then follows from the generalized Cauchy-Binet formula that we have

H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)=\det(P_{i}\cdot\operatorname{Hess}_{i}(F))=\left(\frac{d!}{(d-2i)!}\right)^{i+1}\cdot\sum_{K\in\binom{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}{i+1}}\Delta_{I,K}(\phi_{d}(F))\cdot\Delta_{K,J}\left(W_{d-2i}(X,Y)\right),

the sum taken over
(i+1)
-subsets
K\subset{\mathbb{N}}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
of the non-negative integers.
According to the Gessel-Viennot-Lindström theorem, e.g.
[
7
, Fact 4.17]
, the determinant of a weighted path matrix is signed sum of weights of vertex-disjoint path systems; in our case these are weights of vertex-disjoint NE lattice paths, all necessarily with the sign of the identity permutation:

\Delta_{K,J}(W_{d-2i}(X,Y))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}\colon\mathcal{A}_{K}\to\mathcal{B}_{J}\\
\mathcal{P}\ \text{vertex disjoint}\\
\end{subarray}}\omega(\mathcal{P}).

For
K\not\subset\{i,\ldots,d\}
, there are no vertex-disjoint NE lattice paths from
\mathcal{A}_{K}
to
\mathcal{B}_{J}
, but for
K\subset\{i,\ldots,d\}
there is a one-to-one correspondence between vertex-disjoint NE lattice path systems from
\mathcal{A}_{K}
to
\mathcal{B}_{J}
and semi-standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda^{\prime}(K-i)
, where
K-i\subset\{1,\ldots,d-i+1\}
is just the set
K
shifted down by
i-1
. Moreover, the weight of such a path system
\mathcal{P}
, is the monomial

\omega(\mathcal{P})=X^{|\lambda(K-i)|}Y^{D_{i}-|\lambda(K-i)|}.

It follows that we have for any
K\in\binom{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}{i+1}
,

\Delta_{KJ}(W_{d-2i}(X,Y))=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ K\not\subset\{i,\ldots,d\}\\
N^{\prime}_{K-i}\cdot X^{|\lambda^{\prime}(K-i)|}Y^{D_{i}-|\lambda^{\prime}(K-i)|}&\text{if}\ K\subset\{i,\ldots,d\}\\
\end{cases}

Finally, note that for
K\subset\{i,\ldots,d\}
, we have

\Delta_{I,K}\left(\phi_{d}(F)\right)=\Delta_{K-i}\left(\phi^{i}_{d}(F)\right),

and hence if we substitute
J=K-i
, then we get the desired formula:

H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)=\left(\frac{d!}{(d-2i)!}\right)^{i+1}\cdot\sum_{J\in\binom{\{1,\ldots,d-i+1\}}{i+1}}N_{J}^{\prime}\cdot\Delta_{J}\left(\phi^{i}_{d}(F)\right)\cdot X^{|\lambda^{\prime}(J)|}\cdot Y^{D_{i}-|\lambda^{\prime}(J)|}

(note that we have shifted our indexing back, now starting at
1
instead of
0
).
∎

A formula for the Wronskian, analogous to the one in
Lemma

5
appeared in the paper of K. Purbhoo
[
9
, Proposition 2.3]
; see also
[
6
, Proposition 2.11]
. Earlier incarnations of
Lemma

5
have also appeared in work of I. Gessel
[
4
, Theorem 16]
.

2.5.
Topology of Toeplitz matrices

Let
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}
denote the set of
m\times n
Toeplitz matrices, as a linear subspace of the Euclidean space
\mathcal{M}_{m,n}
consisting of all
m\times n
matrices with entries in
{\mathbb{R}}
. As a topological space, we endow
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}
with the subspace topology (which makes it a Euclidean space). Then
Equation

2
defines a linear isomorphism

\phi^{i}_{d}\colon Q_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{i+1,d-i+1}.

Identifying
Q_{d}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with the Euclidean topology,
\phi^{i}_{d}
is also a homeomorphism.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}^{>0}
the (open) subset of totally positive
m\times n
Toeplitz matrices and by
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}^{\geq 0}
the (closed) subset of totally non-negative ones. One consequence of
Lemma

4
is the following expected, but not-quite-obvious, topological result; see
[
7
, Theorem 4.23]
.

Lemma 6
.

For any positive integers
m\leq n
, the closure of
\mathcal{T}^{>0}_{m,n}
in the Euclidean space
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}
is equal to
\mathcal{T}_{m,n}^{\geq 0}
; in symbols we write

\overline{\mathcal{T}_{m,n}^{>0}}=\mathcal{T}_{m,n}^{\geq 0}.

Remark 2
.

We can think of the Toeplitz matrix
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
as “embedded” in the larger Toeplitz matrix
\phi^{i+1}_{d}(F)
as an L-shaped sub-matrix occupying the union of the first
(i+1)
-columns and the first
(i+1)
-rows. For example,
d=6
and
i=1
, we have

\phi^{1}_{6}(F)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&c_{4}&c_{5}&c_{6}\\
c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&c_{4}&c_{5}\\
\end{array}\right)\ \hookrightarrow\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc|ccc}c_{2}&c_{3}&c_{4}&c_{5}&c_{6}\\
c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&c_{4}&c_{5}\\
\hline\cr c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&c_{4}\\
\end{array}\right)=\phi^{2}_{6}(F)

where
\phi^{1}_{6}(F)
is “folded” into the first
2
rows and columns of
\phi^{2}_{6}(F)
.

In particular, while every (square) sub-matrix of
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is not necessarily a sub-matrix of
\phi^{i+1}_{d}(F)
, every contiguous sub-matrix, i.e. sub-matrix with consecutive rows and columns, is. Conversely, one can easily see that every initial contiguous sub-matrix, i.e. a submatrix indexed by a consecutive subsets of rows or columns with at least one of the subsets of the form
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
, of
\phi^{i+1}_{d}(F)
of size
\leq(i+1)
is also a contiguous sub-matrix of
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
. In fact, in a Toeplitz matrix, every contiguous sub-matrix is equal to an initial contiguous sub-matrix by shifting along the (constant) diagonals! It then follows from Fekete’s theorem, e.g.
[
3
, Corollary 3.1.5]
, that if
\phi^{i+1}_{d}(F)
is totally positive, then
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is also totally positive (of course the converse is not true!). By the closure property in
Lemma

6
, it follows that if
\phi^{i+1}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative, then
\phi^{i}_{d}(F)
is also totally non-negative.

In light of
Remark

2
, it makes sense to study the total positivity/non-negativity of the Toeplitz matrices of the one maximal size, namely
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
, instead of the smaller
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
for different
s
. We shall restate Cattani’s theorem once more, in these terms, but first we need some more notation.

Given a matrix
A\in\mathcal{M}_{m,n}
for
m\leq n
and a positive integer
k
,
1\leq k\leq m
, we say that
A
is
totally positive of order
k
, or TP
k
, if every minor determinant of
A
of size up to
k
is positive.

Lemma 7
.

Let
F\in Q_{d}
be a homogeneous
d
-form of Sperner number
s
. Then

(1)

\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
is totally positive if and only if
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative and TP
s
.

(2)

\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative if and only if
\phi_{d}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(F)
is totally non-negative of rank
s
.

Proof.

We prove 1. According to
[
3
, Corollary 3.1.7]
, a matrix
A
is TP
k
if and only if all of its contiguous minor determinants up to size
k
are positive. Since the contiguous minors up to size
s
in
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
are exactly the contiguous minors of
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
(see
Remark

2
), it follows if
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
is totally positive then
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is TP
s
. Moreover, since
s=s(F)
is the Sperner number of
F
, it follows from
Equation

3
that the rank of
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is
s
, and hence
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative. The converse implication is clear, again from
Remark

2
.

The proof of 2 is an immediate consequence of the last sentence in
Remark

2
.
∎

Fix positive integers
d,s
satisfying
1\leq s\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1
, let
m=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1
and
n=d-\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{s}
denote the space of Toeplitz matrices of size
m\times n
(so either
m\times m
or
m\times(m+1)
depending on the parity of
d
) of rank
s
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}\subset\mathcal{T}_{s}
denote the subset of TP
s
Toeplitz matrices of rank
s
, and let
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\geq 0}
be the set of totally non-negative Toeplitz matrices of rank
s
. Define the open set
\mathcal{O}_{s}\subset\mathcal{T}_{s}
to be the Toeplitz matrices of rank
s
whose corner minor determinants up to size
s
and whose contiguous minor determinants of size
s
do not vanish, i.e.

\mathcal{O}_{s}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{T}_{s}\ |\ \Delta_{[k],[n]\setminus[n-k]}(A),\Delta_{[m]\setminus[m-k],[k]}(A),\Delta_{\{i+1,\ldots,i+s\},\{j+1,\ldots,j+s\}}(A)\neq 0,\ \forall i,j,k\right\}.

The following topological statement is the key to our results.

Lemma 8
.

We have

\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}=\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{O}_{s}.

In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
is a union of connected components of
\mathcal{O}_{s}
.

Proof.

The equality follows immediately from
[
3
, Theorem 3.1.12]
applied to
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
, and
Lemma

7
. Since
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}_{s}=\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{T}_{s}
is certainly closed in
\mathcal{T}_{s}
, and since
\mathcal{T}^{>0}_{s}
is certainly open in
\mathcal{T}_{s}
, it follows from point set topology that
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
, as a closed and open subset of the subspace
\mathcal{O}_{s}\subset\mathcal{T}_{s}
, must be a union of connected components of
\mathcal{O}_{s}
, as claimed.
∎

Finally we need the following observation to help us determine when
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)\in\mathcal{O}_{s}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
F\in Q_{d}
be a homogeneous
d
-form of Sperner number
s
. If
A_{F}
satisfies HRR
{}_{s-1}(\overline{U})
, then for every
r
,
0\leq r\leq d-2(s-1)
, the multiplication map

\times y^{d-r-2(s-1)}\colon\left(A_{x^{r}\circ F}\right)_{s-1}\rightarrow\left(A_{x^{r}\circ F}\right)_{d-r-(s-1)}

is an isomorphism. In particular, the contiguous maximal minor determinants of
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
are all non-zero.

Proof.

The proof is simple, once we unwind all the definitions. The point is that since
s=s(F)
is the Sperner number of
F
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\left(A_{F}\right)_{j}=s
for all degrees
j
satisfying
s-1\leq j\leq d-s+1
, and hence since the multiplication map

\times y^{d-2(s-1)}\colon\left(A_{F}\right)_{s-1}\rightarrow\left(A_{F}\right)_{d-s+1}

is an isomorphism (by HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(\overline{U})
, since
y\in\overline{U}
), it follows that the multiplication maps

\times y\colon\left(A_{F}\right)_{j}\rightarrow\left(A_{F}\right)_{j+1}

must also be isomorphisms for all
s-1\leq j\leq d-s
. Next, if

\alpha\in\ker\left\{\times y^{d-r-2(s-1)}\colon\left(A_{x^{r}\circ F}\right)_{s-1}\rightarrow\left(A_{x^{r}\circ F}\right)_{d-r-(s-1)}\right\}

then it follows that
x^{r}\cdot\alpha
must be in the kernel of the injective multiplication map

\times y^{d-r-2(s-1)}\colon\left(A_{F}\right)_{s+r-1}\rightarrow\left(A_{F}\right)_{d-r-(s-1)}.

Hence
x^{r}\alpha=0
in
A_{F}
, but since
x\in\overline{U}
too, it follows that
\alpha=0
in
A_{F}
, and hence the map above is injective as claimed. For the last assertion, note that

H^{x^{r}\circ F}_{s-1}(0,1)=C\cdot\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{s-1+r}&\cdots&c_{2s-2+r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{r}&\cdots&c_{s-1+r}\\
\end{array}\right)

where
C
is a positive non-zero constant and the determinant is the
r^{th}

s\times s
maximal contiguous minor of
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
, which is non-zero by the first statement (see
Remark

1
).
∎

3.
Proof of Cattani’s Theorem

We are now in a position to prove
Theorem

2
, although we shall restate it in an equivalent form, using
Lemma

7
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
F\in Q_{d}
be a homogeneous form of Sperner number
s\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1
. Then

(1)

\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative and TP
s
if and only if

H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\\
\forall 0\leq i\leq s-1\\
\end{cases}

(2)

\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
is totally non-negative of rank
s
if and only if

H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{>0}\\
\forall 0\leq i\leq s-1\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove 1. Since the forward direction is clear from
Lemma

5
, we focus on the converse implication. Assume
H_{i}^{F}(X,Y)>0
for all
(X,Y)\geq 0
,
(X,Y)\neq 0
and for all
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor
. Then according to the formula from
Lemma

5
, it follows that the leading coefficients must be nonzero, and in particular we must have

(4)

\Delta_{I}(\phi^{i}_{d}(F))\neq 0,\ I=\{1,\ldots,i+1\},\ \{d-2i+1,\ldots,d-i+1\},\ \forall 0\leq i\leq s-1

for all
0\leq i\leq s-1
.
Note that the determinants in
Equation

4
are exactly the corner minors of size
i+1
in the Toeplitz matrix
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
. Also, by
Lemma

9
, it follows that the contiguous
s\times s
minors of
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)
(which are also the contiguous maximal minors of
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(F)
by
Remark

2
) are also non-zero. In particular,
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)\in\mathcal{O}_{s}
, the open set from
Lemma

8
.

On the other hand, consider the one-parameter change of coordinates defined by

S_{t}(F)=F(X+tY,Y);

S. Karp
[
6
]
calls this the
shifting operator
. According to
Lemma

1
, there is a ring isomorphism defined by

\phi\colon A_{S_{t}(F)}\rightarrow A_{F},\ \phi(x)=x,\ \phi(y)=tx+y.

By
Lemma

2
, since
A_{F}
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(\overline{U})
, it follows that for
t>0
,
S_{t}(F)
also satisfies HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(\overline{U})
. In particular, our previous arguments above imply that we have

\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}\left(S_{t}(F)\right)\in\mathcal{O}_{s},\ \forall t\geq 0.

Moreover, since
(A_{F},x)
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}
, one can show by an elementary perturbation argument, e.g.
[
7
, Corollary 4.15]
, that for
t>>0
sufficiently large,
A_{F}
must satisfy mixed HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(\overline{U}_{t})
where

\overline{U}_{t}=\left\{ax+b(tx+y)\ |\ a,b\geq 0,\ (a,b)\neq 0\right\}.

In particular,
\phi^{s-1}_{d}\left(S_{t}(F)\right)
is totally positive, by
Lemma

4
, and hence
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(S_{t}(F))\in\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
for
t>>0
sufficiently large, by
Lemma

7
.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
is a union of connected components of
\mathcal{O}_{s}
, and since
\phi_{d}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(S_{t}(F))\in\mathcal{O}_{s}
for all
t\geq 0
, it follows from the continuity of the path
t\mapsto S_{t}(F)
that
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F=S_{0}(F))\in\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
as well.

For 2. assume that
A_{F}
satisfies ordinary HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(U)
. Define the double shift operator

R_{t}(F)=F(X+tY,tX+Y).

It follows from
Lemma

1
and
Lemma

2
that for
0<t<1
,

A_{R_{t}(F)}
satisfies HRR
{}_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}(\overline{U})
, and hence
\phi^{s-1}_{d}(R_{t}(F))
is totally positive, and thus
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)\in\mathcal{T}_{s}^{>0}
for all
0<t<1
. Hence It follows that

\lim_{t\to 0}\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(R_{t}(F))=\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)

must therefore be totally non-negative, and, of course, of rank
s
, which means
\phi^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}_{d}(F)\in\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\geq 0}
, as desired. As before, the converse implication is an immediate consequence of
Lemma

5
.
∎

Cattani’s theorem together with the Plücker expansion of the Hessian has the following interesting corollary:

Corollary 1
.

Let
F\in Q_{d}
be any homogeneous
d
-form of Sperner number
s
. Then

(1)

if
{\displaystyle H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\\
\forall 0\leq i\leq s-1\\
\end{cases}}
then the coefficients of
H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)
must be strictly positive for all
0\leq i\leq s-1
.

(2)

if
{\displaystyle H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)>0,\ \begin{cases}\forall(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{>0}\\
\forall 0\leq i\leq s-1\\
\end{cases}}
then the coefficients of
H^{F}_{i}(X,Y)
must be non-negative for all
0\leq i\leq s-1
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
Theorem

3
,
Remark

2
, and
Lemma

5
.
∎

Monitoring multiple rib fractures using pulmonary electrical impedance tomography technology: correlation analysis of pulmonary ventilation changes
Authors: Liming Wang, Lingqing Zhou, Picong You, Qiuyi Wang, Zhanqi Zhao, Honggang Xia, Dongbin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultiple rib fractures are a common and severe form of chest trauma, which can lead to pulmonary ventilation disorders. Assessment primarily relies on imaging examinations and conventional pulmonary function tests. Electrical impedance tomography (EIT) technology can detect ventilation dysfunctions that are difficult to identify through conventional means. This study utilizes EIT technology to quantify global and regional pulmonary ventilation inhomogeneity and to analyze the correlation between these ventilation characteristics and various factors associated with multiple rib fractures, thereby providing preliminary support for subsequent of intervention and prognosis assessment of rib fractures in EIT.MethodsA cross-sectional exploratory study was conducted. A total of 115 patients with multiple rib fractures admitted to the Department of Cardiothoracic Surgery at Tianjin Hospital in Tianjin City from October 2020 to May 2023 were selected. EIT examinations were performed 24–72 hours after injury, and trauma and EIT data were collected and organized. Whole chest/lung indicators included: pain score [numerical rating scale (NRS)], total number of rib fractures, total number of displaced rib fractures, number of lung contusion lobes, global inhomogeneity index (GI), ventral-to-dorsal ventilation center (CoVv-d), pendelluft (Ap), inspiration delay time (Tins-i), expiration delay time (Texp-i). Regional chest/lung indicators included: total number of (left lateral + left posterior) displaced rib fractures, total number of (right lateral + right posterior) displaced rib fractures, number of left/right lung contusion lobes, left dorsal lung impedance proportion (TIVD-L%), right dorsal lung impedance proportion (TIVD-R%), left dorsal lung Ap (ApD-L), and right dorsal lung Ap (ApD-R). Spearman correlation analysis was used for the data.ResultsThe number of lung contusion lobes and EIT global-lung parameters (CoVv-d, Ap, Texp-i) all showed a good correlation with the number of rib fractures (P<0.05), especially with the number of displaced rib fractures (P<0.01). The greater the number of rib fractures, the most severe the pain (P<0.01). Changes in EIT parameters were significantly correlated with the degree of pain (P<0.05/0.01). Multiple rib fractures can exacerbate pulmonary injury in the trauma area (P<0.05/0.01), resulting in a reduction of tidal impedance variation in region of interest percentage (TIVROI%) (P<0.05/0.01), with Ap in this region being more pronounced than in other regions (P<0.05/0.01). Regional Ap was significantly correlated with the local number of displaced rib fractures (P<0.01).ConclusionsEIT can provide dynamic ventilation images for patients with rib fractures, quantifying regional ventilation disorders and overall pulmonary ventilation heterogeneity. Current studies have not yet verified the predictive value of EIT for long-term respiratory outcomes. Still, it can provide a basis for future large-sample, interventional, and outcome-oriented research.Keywords: Pulmonary electrical impedance tomography (pulmonary EIT), multiple rib fractures, pulmonary ventilation changes, correlation

Introduction

Multiple rib fractures are a common and serious chest injury, most frequently affecting the posterior and lateral ribs from the 4th to the 9th rib. These injuries are often accompanied by varying degrees of pulmonary contusion, pain, and restricted chest wall movement, which can readily trigger abnormalities in respiratory function such as uneven ventilation distribution and hypoxemia. This increases the risk of complications such as pulmonary infection and atelectasis, impacting the patient’s recovery process and short-term prognosis (1,2). At present, clinical assessment of thoracic injuries in patients with multiple rib fractures mainly relies on imaging examinations such as chest computed tomography (CT) and X-rays, as well as routine pulmonary function tests. Imaging can clearly display static anatomical injury features such as the number of fractures, degree of displacement, and extent of pulmonary contusion. Routine lung function tests can quantify overall ventilation levels. However, both methods have certain limitations: they cannot dynamically monitor changes in ventilation after trauma in real time, cannot distinguish between regional ventilation disorders mediated by chest wall injury, pain suppression, or pulmonary contusion, and are unable to detect early, subtle, and reversible heterogeneity in ventilation following trauma (3). Previous studies have confirmed that varying degrees of pendelluft (Ap) often exist after multiple rib fractures; this is a pathophysiological phenomenon that is difficult to identify with conventional exams. Such abnormalities can lead to reversible declines in respiratory function and prolong recovery time.

Electrical impedance tomography (EIT) is a functional imaging technology that offers advantages such as being non-invasive, radiation-free, and suitable for bedside operation (4). EIT can monitor pathophysiological phenomena that are difficult to detect by conventional means, dynamically assess pulmonary ventilation heterogeneity and regional ventilation disorders, and quantify the distribution, temporal sequence, and Ap of ventilation in both the global lung and local lung tissues. Its role in respiratory function assessment has reached international consensus (5-8). Leveraging its ability to monitor regional ventilation, EIT is particularly suitable for detecting regional pulmonary phenotypes associated with chest trauma. However, the characteristics of ventilation indices, the influencing factors, and the clinical value of EIT in patients with multiple rib fractures remain unclear, and there is a lack of standardized data to support these findings. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1350/rc).

Methods

Study design and objectives

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Tianjin Hospital (No. 2020-140). All patients or their families voluntarily signed the informed consent form to participate in the trial. We conducted a cross-sectional exploratory study with the following hypotheses: (I) the lung ventilation heterogeneity parameters quantified by EIT are associated with fracture load, pulmonary contusion, and pain severity; (II) the reduction in local ventilation and the degree of Ap in the injured area are significantly higher than in non-injured areas, and are related to the local fracture load; (III) the severity of pain is correlated with abnormalities in EIT ventilation timing and Ap.

This study was designed to address the following two objectives: to identify the heterogeneity of pulmonary ventilation in patients with multiple rib fractures and the impact of trauma on regional ventilation, in order to clarify the ventilation characteristics in this patient population; and to screen for EIT quantitative indicators with potential clinical value, thus laying the groundwork for subsequent studies verifying the incremental value of EIT and assessing clinical outcomes.

Participant selection

From October 2020 to May 2023, 115 patients with multiple rib fractures who met the following criteria were enrolled in the Department of Cardiothoracic Surgery at Tianjin Hospital, Tianjin City. Inclusion criteria: (I) injury occurred within 24–72 hours; (II) chest CT and three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction confirmed the presence of ≥3 ribs each with a single, bicortical fracture (patients may have incomplete rib fractures in other locations and multiple injuries except those specified in the exclusion criteria); (III) age ≥18 years; (IV) stable vital signs upon admission and not requiring emergency surgery. Cases involving multiple ribs with multiple fractures (such as flail chest), which can cause thoracic instability, and in themselves may lead to Ap, affecting the assessment of the relationship between other factors and ventilation changes, were not included in this study. Exclusion criteria: (I) rib fractures combined with other injuries (cranial injury, abdominal organ injury, scapular or clavicular fracture, pelvic fracture, vertebral fracture, one or more); (II) unstable vital signs upon admission or requiring emergency surgery (e.g., flail chest); (III) severe cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, hepatic or renal insufficiency, or bleeding disorders; (IV) history of other pulmonary diseases (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary bullae, asthma, or pulmonary dysfunction); (V) local infection, large open wounds, or multiple intrathoracic drains; (VI) implanted medical devices, such as pacemakers or defibrillators; (VII) patients currently receiving electrical stimulation therapy (6).

EIT measurement

All EIT data collection in this study strictly adhered to recent EIT guidelines (TREND consensus, Frerichs et al., Thorax 2017; China EIT Expert Consensus, 2022, etc.). Examination was performed using the PulmoVista500 (Dräger PulmoVista 500, Lübeck, Germany). Patients were examined within 24–72 hours after injury; those with obvious pneumothorax and/or pleural effusion had a chest drainage tube placed in advance with adequate drainage. All subjects were placed in the supine position, with all skin surface coverings (including chest tube dressings and accessories) removed. An appropriate chest belt with 16 surface electrodes was selected and applied securely around the chest along the 4th–5th intercostal space, ensuring close contact with the skin. Lung ventilation images were recorded for more than one minute during calm breathing. If the subject coughed, breathed forcefully, or other disturbances resulted in poor waveform quality, the disturbance was excluded, and the recording time was extended to include at least 10 valid impedance images of quiet breathing cycles (5-8).

Quantitative data analysis was performed using supporting analysis software for the EIT device. All indicator definitions, calculation methods, and units refer to previous authoritative studies and international consensus standards (definitions and calculation methods for each indicator of EIT; see Table S1 for details). Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 and GraphPad Prism 6. Spearman correlation analysis was used on the data. All tests were two-sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Rib fractures caused by chest trauma often lead to the destruction of chest wall integrity, pulmonary contusion, and chest pain, which are important reasons for disordered pulmonary ventilation distribution and decreased respiratory function. Conventional chest CT and lung function tests cannot capture post-traumatic regional or subtle ventilation heterogeneity, nor can they distinguish between ventilation abnormalities caused by different mechanisms. In recent years, EIT has been widely used in respiratory management, pulmonary perfusion assessment, studies of ventilation-perfusion ratios, and in guiding pulmonary physical therapy, among others (5). At present, mature international clinical standards and guidelines have been established. However, few studies have used EIT to assess ventilation changes caused by rib fractures. This study focuses on the relationship between EIT ventilation indicators in patients with multiple rib fractures and fracture burden, pulmonary contusion, and pain, as well as the impact of localized indicators on regional ventilation. The following discussion will address these two aspects.

Relationship between rib fracture-related factors and whole-lung indicators

Spearman correlation analysis showed that the total number of rib fractures and the number of displaced rib fractures are strongly correlated with the number of pulmonary contusion lobes and the global lung indicators on EIT, including CoVv-d, Ap, and Texp-i(s). Among these, the impact of displaced fracture burden on ventilation heterogeneity is more pronounced. Displaced rib fractures involving more than three fracture ends can compromise the mechanical stability of the chest wall. Even if the diagnostic criteria for flail chest are not met, they may still result in thoracic pressure imbalance, trigger regional ventilation asynchrony and Ap (16,17), thereby increasing abnormal indicators such as Ap and Texp-i, and decreasing the CoVv-d. The greater the rib fracture burden, the more severe the pain. Pain scores are well correlated with the global lung indicators on EIT. This is mainly because severe pain can restrict chest wall movement and suppress breathing depth, further aggravating the uneven distribution of ventilation and delaying ventilation duration, resulting in a compounded effect of ‘trauma-induced pain suppression and ventilation disorder’ (3).

Changes in regional pulmonary ventilation caused by multiple rib fractures

Results from ROI analysis suggest that multiple rib fractures present significant regional ventilation heterogeneity: the proportion of ventilation in the traumatized area is reduced, while the regional Ap is significantly higher than in non-traumatized regions. The greater the number of locally displaced fractures, the more pronounced the Ap. Further unilateral analysis of the left and right lungs confirmed that locally displaced fractures and pulmonary contusions can specifically reduce the proportion of ventilation in the corresponding dorsal region and increase the local Ap index, indicating a matched association between trauma load and regional ventilation disorder. This dynamic regional ventilation phenotype cannot be identified by traditional static imaging techniques and is a unique observational advantage of EIT technology.

Limitations

This study is a cross-sectional exploratory study. Simple correlation analysis cannot predefine clinical outcome endpoints, nor can it answer whether EIT indicators can predict respiratory outcomes beyond traditional assessments, or whether EIT can dynamically reflect changes with interventions such as analgesia, chest fixation, respiratory support, or surgical intervention. Future longitudinal follow-up studies are needed to analyze the relationship between EIT indicators and pulmonary complications, rehabilitation duration, long-term lung function, or to compare the effects of different treatment approaches on pulmonary ventilation improvement. As patients on mechanical ventilation were not included, it was not possible to monitor indicators such as respiratory drive and inspiratory effort, so it cannot be completely distinguished whether impedance changes originate from heterogeneity in ventilation of lung parenchyma or pain-mediated differences in respiratory movement. There are certain limitations in interpreting these results, and the correlation between pain and EIT indicators also indirectly confirms the presence of this confounding factor.

Conclusions

Multiple rib fractures themselves, along with the resulting pain, can both lead to uneven pulmonary ventilation. EIT can quantitatively display dynamic ventilation images in patients with rib fractures, as well as regional ventilation disorders and heterogeneity of whole-lung ventilation, which cannot be captured by traditional imaging or lung function tests. Current research has not yet validated the predictive value of EIT for long-term respiratory outcomes; large-sample, interventional, and outcome-oriented studies are still needed.

Walter Dehnen ∗, Shera Jafaritabar

Abstract
We present axially symmetric analytical potential-density pairs with surface density similar to the Miyamoto-Nagai model, but with more realistic vertical structure. Our models closely approximate an exponential, a sech 2 , or a cored exponential vertical density profile. The latter profile has a density core of adjustable width, which provides more flexibility when modelling galaxy discs.

Introduction

Studies of the dynamics of the Milky Way and other disc galaxies often require models for the gravitational potential which should be realistic as well as easy to implement and compute. Unfortunately, these two goals appear mutually exclusive, since galactic discs have vertically near-exponential profiles
(
Jurić et al. 2008
;
Dobbie & Warren 2020
;
Mosenkov et al. 2021
, e.g.)
for which no analytical models are known. In face of this dilemma, mainly two approaches have been used in practice: (1) numerical computation of gravitational potentials for observationally motivated density models
(
Kuijken & Dubinski 1995
;
Dehnen & Binney 1998
)
, and (2) simple analytical disc models with unrealistic vertical profiles (
Miyamoto & Nagai 1975
, hereafter
16
;
Evans & Bowden 2014
).

The vertical density profile of the widely used
16
model deviates from the exponential profile in two ways. First, at large
|z|
it decays only like the power law
|z|^{-5}
. This deviation is presumably benign in the sense that the resulting differences between the forces at large
|z|
, and hence the ensuing orbits, are relatively minor.

The second deviation of the
16
model from a vertically exponential density profile occurs at small
|z|
, where the former exhibits a near-constant density core and declines only like
z^{2}
, while the latter declines linearly in
|z|
. This difference translates to the vertical Taylor expansion of the potential at small
|z|
:

\displaystyle\Phi=\Phi_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\nu^{2}z^{2}+\begin{cases}O(|z|^{3})&\text{for an exponential},\\
O(z^{4})&\text{for a density core},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\Phi_{0}
and
\nu
denote the mid-plane value and the vertical epicycle frequency, respectively. Thus, the motion in a vertically exponential disc is much more strongly anharmonic than in a disc with density core, such as the
16
model. This anharmonicity results in important dynamical effects, which are neglected when using the
16
model. One effect is the presence of more orbital resonances owing to higher vertical orbital frequencies for an exponential disc. Another effect is more efficient vertical phase mixing, which in turn leads to a quick loss of vertical coherence of tidal debris from dissolving star clusters
(
Dehnen & Hasanuddin 2018
)
and to a faster winding of phase-spirals in the
z
-
v_{z}
phase space, such as those observed in the Milky Way
(
Antoja et al. 2018
)
.

A hybrid of the numerical and analytical approaches to disc modelling is to superimpose several
16
models with parameters numerically determined to approximate a vertically exponential disc (
Rojas-Niño et al. 2016
achieved
\sim 10\%
accuracy). However, this does not completely solve the discrepancies at small and large
z
, since also the combination of several
16
models has an, albeit small, density core and power-law fall-off at
z\to\pm\infty
.

In this study, we introduce novel analytical potentials for galactic discs, most properties of which at
z=0
are identical to a corresponding
16
model, but which have vertical density profiles very close to exponential, sech
2
, or a cored exponential profile. The models and their properties are derived in Sections
2
and
3
, and assessed in Section
4
, while Section
5
concludes our study.

Modifying the Kuzmin disc

The
Kuzmin 1956
disc has gravitational potential

\displaystyle\Phi(R,z)=-\frac{GM}{{X}}\quad\text{with}\quad{X}\equiv\sqrt{R^{2}+Z^{2}},

(2)

where
Z=a+|z|
with some scale length
a
, and mass density
\rho(R,z)=\Sigma(R)\delta(z)
with surface density

\displaystyle\Sigma(R)=\frac{aM}{2\pi(R^{2}+a^{2})^{3/2}}.

(3)

Such a razor-thin mass distribution is not very realistic, but one can obtain more realistic models by setting

\displaystyle Z=a+\zeta(z)

(4)

with some function
\zeta(z)
which in the limits
z\to\pm\infty
approaches
|z|
, such that
\Phi\to-GM/\sqrt{R^{2}+z^{2}}
and the parameter
M
retains its meaning as the total mass.

The
16
model is obtained from this recipe for
\zeta=\zeta_{\mathrm{M}}\equiv\sqrt{z^{2}+b^{2}}
with scale height
b
. However, other useful but hitherto unknown modifiers
\zeta(z)
may exist. In order to explore this possibility, we now investigate the general properties of these modified Kuzmin models and obtain conditions for the function
\zeta(z)
.

The mass density related via Poisson’s equation is

\displaystyle\rho(R,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{4\pi{X}^{3}}\left[Z\zeta^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{3Z^{2}}{{X}^{2}}-1\right)(1-\zeta^{\prime 2})\right].

(5)

For
\zeta=|z|
, we have
\zeta^{\prime}=\sign(z)
and
\zeta^{\prime\prime}=2\delta(z)
,
such that equation (
5
) recovers the Kuzmin model as required. In order to avoid a razor-thin component, the function
\zeta
must be
C^{2}
, which for vertically symmetric discs implies
\zeta^{\prime}(0)=0
. In this case, it is useful to introduce

\displaystyle\xi(z)\equiv[1-\zeta^{\prime 2}(z)]/\zeta^{\prime\prime}(z),

(6)

such that the density can be written as

\displaystyle\rho(R,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{M\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{4\pi}\left[\frac{Z-\xi}{{X}^{3}}+\frac{3\xi Z^{2}}{{X}^{5}}\right].

(7)

For this to be non-negative everywhere, we first require that
\zeta^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
(which with the previous conditions implies
1-\zeta^{\prime 2}\geq 0
and
\xi\geq 0
). In this case
\rho\geq 0
everywhere if
Z\geq\xi
. At
z=0
, this reduces to
(a+\zeta)\zeta^{\prime\prime}\geq 1
, which holds for any value
a\geq 0
if
\zeta\zeta^{\prime\prime}=1
at
z=0
, is our final condition

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{M}}(R,z)

2.1
A (nearly) exponential vertical profile

A model with vertical profiles very close to exponential with scale height
h
is generated by the modifier

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}(z)=|z|+h\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h},

(11a)

for which

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}^{\prime\prime}=h^{-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\mathrm{E}}=h\big(2-\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\big).

(11b)

The resulting density

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{E}}(R,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{4\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}}{{X}^{3}}\left[\frac{Z}{h}+\!\left(\frac{3Z^{2}}{{X}^{2}}-1\!\right)\big(2-\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\big)\right]

(12)

is close to exponential at small
|z|
, but in the mid-plane is identical to the
16
model for the same
s
and
b=h
.

Since
\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}-\xi_{\mathrm{E}}<0
at
0<|z|\lesssim 1.6\,h
with a minimum of
h(\ln 2-1)
at
|z|/h=\ln 2
, the density is negative near that minimum for
h/a>1/(1-\ln 2)\approx 3.26
and non-monotonic for somewhat smaller
h/a
. However, for typical applications
h\ll a
and no such behaviour occurs.

2.2
A vertical profile close to
\sech^{2}

A model with vertical profiles very close to sech
2
is generated by the modifier

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{S}}(z)=z_{0}+z_{0}\ln\cosh\!\frac{z}{z_{0}},

(13a)

such that

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{z_{0}}\sech^{2}\!\frac{z}{z_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\mathrm{S}}=z_{0}

(13b)

and we find from equation (
7
)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{S}}(R,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{4\pi{X}^{3}}\sech^{2}\!\frac{z}{z_{0}}\left[\frac{a}{z_{0}}+\ln\cosh\!\frac{z}{z_{0}}+\frac{3Z^{2}}{{X}^{2}}\right]\!.

(14)

Again, for the same value of
s
, the mid-plane density is identical to that of the near-exponential model for
h=z_{0}
or the
16
model for
b=z_{0}
. At
|z|\gg z_{0}
, sech
{}^{2}(z/z_{0})\sim\mathrm{e}^{-2|z|/z_{0}}
, such that
z_{0}=2h
obtains exponential decay with scale height
h
, in which case the mid-plane density is about half of that of the exponential model.

Since
\zeta_{\mathrm{S}}-\xi_{\mathrm{S}}\geq 0
,
\rho_{\mathrm{S}}\geq 0
everywhere.

2.3
Cored exponential vertical profiles

The sech
2
profile, unlike the exponential but similar to the
16
disc, has a density core: a region of near-constant density at low
|z|
. Comparing

\displaystyle\sech^{2}\frac{z}{2h}=1-\frac{z^{2}}{4h^{2}}+O(z^{4})

(15)

to the simple cored exponential profile

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{z^{2}+w^{2}}/h}\propto 1-\frac{z^{2}}{2hw}+O(z^{4}),

(16)

suggests
w=2h
for the width of this core.

A cored exponential profile can be constructed as the difference of two exponentials with scale heights
h
and
w
, respectively. Using this approach, we extend the modifier
\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}
to include the parameter
w\in[0,h]
, generating models with cored exponential vertical profiles. For
w<h
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=|z|+\frac{hw(h-w)+h^{3}\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}-w^{3}\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/w}}{h^{2}-w^{2}},

(17a)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sign}(z)\left[1-\frac{h^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}-w^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/w}}{h^{2}-w^{2}}\right],

(17b)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{h\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}-w\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/w}}{h^{2}-w^{2}}\propto 1-\frac{z^{2}}{2hw}+O(|z|^{3}),

(17c)

which gives equations (
11
) for
w=0
, while for
w=h

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=|z|+\tfrac{1}{2}\left[h+(3h+|z|)\,\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\right],

(18a)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sign}(z)\left[1-\frac{2h+|z|}{2h}\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\right],

(18b)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{h+|z|}{2h^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-|z|/h}\propto 1-\frac{z^{2}}{2h^{2}}+O(|z|^{3}).

(18c)

For these models,
\zeta_{0}=h+w
, such that
s=a+h+w
. A limitation is that
w\leq h
is required (for
w>h
,
w
and
h
simply swap their roles).

Again, non-monotonic vertical profiles or even
\rho<0
can occur for these models if
h\gtrsim a
.

Modified Toomre models?

Toomre 1963

\Sigma\propto(R^{2}+a^{2})^{-k-1/2}

Kuzmin 1956

\zeta=\zeta_{\mathrm{M}}

. For the
16

\xi=\zeta
everywhere and this term vanishes identically, but for all other modifiers, this term either causes negative densities (for
\zeta_{\mathrm{E}}
) or prevents the density from decaying faster than for the
k=1
models at large

. Hence, there is little point to consider the modified
k>1
Toomre models.

Actually, the
k>2

The vertical Jeans equation for axially symmetric systems reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial(R\rho\overline{v_{R}v_{z}})}{\partial R}+\frac{\partial(\rho\sigma_{z}^{2})}{\partial z}

. Its profiles are reminiscent of those for
(\partial\Phi/\partial z)/z

4.4
Velocity dispersion

For small heights
|z|
, i.e. for most disc stars, the vertical Jeans equation (
21
) is well approximated by neglecting (i) the mixed-derivative term, since it is generally much smaller than the dominant terms, and (ii) gravity from spheroidal components, since their vertical force is much smaller than that from the disc. Hence, equation (
24
), which is obtained under these approximations, gives a good description of the vertical velocity dispersion
\sigma_{z}
of a stellar disc following a modified Kuzmin model.

In Fig.
6
, we plot the profiles
\sigma_{z}(z)
for our four comparison models for
h=0.1s
at different radii
R
. Consider first the sech
2
model (green in Fig.
6
), which according to simple one-dimensional theory should produce a constant
\sigma^{2}_{z}=8\pi Gh^{2}\rho(R,0)

(
Spitzer 1942
, see also problem 4.21 of
Binney & Tremaine 2008
)
. At small
|z|
, this model indeed has near-constant
\sigma_{z}
(though not at the value expected from one-dimensional theory) but typically does not remain constant with height. These deviations are mainly owed to the departure from the assumption (used in the simple theory) of a reduced, one-dimensional Poisson equation,
4\pi G\rho=\partial^{2}\Phi/\partial z^{2}
.

All the models with an exponential vertical profile asymptote to very similar
\sigma_{z}
profiles at large
|z|
, but differ at small
|z|
. The purely exponential model shows a sharp minimum for
\sigma_{z}

(
Fuchs et al. 2009
)
, while the cored exponential has a less pronounced and smooth minimum.

The
16

\sigma_{z}
profile than the exponentially declining models and reaches much larger values with maxima not captured in Fig.

R=0

\sigma_{z}
is inflated by the high speeds of stars visiting from

5
Discussion and conclusion

The main results of this study are the analytic mass models for thin discs with exponential or sech
2
vertical profile. These are obtained by modifying the razor-thin
Kuzmin 1956
disc, very similar to how the
Miyamoto & Nagai 1975
(
Miyamoto & Nagai 1975
;
16
) model is constructed. In fact, many properties of the new models closely follow those of the
16
model (with appropriately chosen parameters), so that the main difference is the vertical structure. Our approach can be used to construct modified Kuzmin models with arbitrary vertical profiles and in Appendix
C
we give the relations for a vertically near-Gaussian model.

The central density of an exponential vertical profile is significantly higher than for the
16
or the sech
2
models (at the same surface density and exponential scale height). This higher central density results in a stronger vertical force at small
|z|
, and therefore also in higher vertical orbital frequencies
\Omega_{z}
for stars with small maximal orbital height
z_{\max}
. This in turn affects various dynamical effects, such as the presence or absence of orbital resonances (between vertical and radial motion) and the degree of phase-mixing in the vertical phase space. Thus, the modelling of phenomena related to the vertical structure of galactic discs, such as
z
-
v_{z}
phase spirals and breathing or bending waves, are likely to be affected by the assumed vertical profile.

Surface density

Since
\Phi(R,z)=\Psi(R,\zeta)
with
\nabla^{2}\Psi=0
, we have

\displaystyle 4\pi G\rho

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}\Phi=\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\zeta^{2}},

(27)

such that the face-on projections of the modified Kuzmin models have surface density

\displaystyle\Sigma(R)=\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\rho\,\mathrm{d}z=-\frac{1}{2\pi G}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\zeta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{M}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{X^{3}}-\frac{3R^{2}}{X^{5}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}z,

(28)

since
\partial^{2}\Phi/\partial z^{2}
integrates to zero. In general, this integral cannot be expressed in closed form, though for the
16
model
Ciotti 2023
provides an expression involving elliptic integrals. Following
Dehnen & Aly 2023

\zeta(z)

Appendix C
A general recipe for modifiers

In order to obtain a vertical profile which closely follows a given functional form
f(|z|)
, a modifier
\zeta(z)
satisfying all our conditions is constructed as follows. First, we define the integral
F(z)\equiv\int_{0}^{z}f(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
. Then

\displaystyle\zeta(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(\infty)}{f(0)}+\frac{1}{F(\infty)}\int_{0}^{|z|}F(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\sign(z)\frac{F(|z|)}{F(\infty)},

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(|z|)}{F(\infty)}.

(30)

As an example, we apply this recipe to
f(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2w^{2}\texttwosuperior}
for near-Gaussian vertical profiles, giving

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}

\displaystyle=z\erf\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}\,w}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}w\left[\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2w^{2}\texttwosuperior}+\frac{\pi}{2}-1\right],

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\erf\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}w},

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{w}\,\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2w^{2}\texttwosuperior}.

(31)

For this modifier,
\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}-\xi_{\mathrm{G}}\geq 0
everywhere, such that the resulting density is non-negative everywhere.

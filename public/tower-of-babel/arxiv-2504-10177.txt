Lagrangian averaging of singular stochastic actions for fluid dynamics
Theo Diamantakis, Ruiao Hu

Abstract
We construct sub-grid scale models of incompressible fluids by considering expectations of semi-martingale Lagrangian particle trajectories. Our construction is based on the Lagrangian decomposition of flow maps into mean and fluctuation parts, and it is separated into the following steps. First, through Magnus expansion, the fluid velocity field is expressed in terms of fluctuation vector fields whose dynamics are assumed to be stochastic. Second, we use Malliavin calculus to give a regularised interpretation of the product of white noise when inserting the stochastic velocity field into the Lagrangian for Euler’s fluid. Lastly, we consider closures of the mean velocity by making stochastic analogues of Talyor’s frozen-in turbulence hypothesis to derive a version of the anisotropic Lagrangian averaged Euler equation.



1
Introduction

The modelling of multi-scale fluid flows is a well-known problem in fluid dynamics. In previously established work, fluid flows exhibiting multiple spatial scales have typically been modelled by considering averaging techniques and closures to capture the effects of the unresolvable scales on large-scale dynamics. These models are typically referred to as sub-grid scale models. One such model is the Generalised Lagrangian Mean (GLM) model introduced by Andrews & Mcintyre
[
1
]
that considered the application of an arbitrary averaging operator to the Lagrangian expansion of ideal incompressible fluid equations and it is later closed in
[
10
]
. To preserve the geometric structure of ideal fluid equations during averaging, one option is to apply averaging to the variational formulation of ideal fluid flows, instead of applying to the equations directly. This approach resulted in several models such as the Lagrangian averaged Navier Stokes model
[
9
]
. More recently, fluid flows that exhibit multiple temporal scales have been modelled by stochastic partial differential equations where the evolution of the fast dynamics is represented by integration against driving stochastic processes such as Brownian motion and geometric rough paths. One class of structure-preserving stochastic perturbation, known as transport noise, is constructed by assuming a semi-martingale decomposition of the Lagrangian fluid flow map
g_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{M})
(for fluid domain
\mathcal{M}
) in a stochastic variational principle, see, e.g.,
[
13
,
8
]
.

Stochastic fluid flows naturally induce a type of ensemble averaging operator. This is the expectation operator
\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
over the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
that supports the driving stochastic processes.
\mathbb{E}
is linear, a projection and commutes with spatial derivative operations. Consequently, the expectation satisfies the required axioms of an average seen in GLM theory
[
1
]
.
Additionally, one has the analogue of the zero mean fluctuations under expectation operator as martingales. More precisely, for a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-martingale
Y_{t}
, the martingale property of Itô integral implies that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\zeta^{i}_{s}\mathrm{d}Y^{i}_{s}\right]=0\,,

for any
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted vector fields
\zeta_{t}=(\zeta^{i}_{t})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
.

In this work, we consider the averaging of stochastic fluid models using the expectation operator to construct probabilistic variants of sub-grid scale models for ideal fluids. Focusing on incompressible flows, the starting assumption is the semi-martingale Lagrangian fluid flow map
g_{t}\in\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{M})
similar to the derivation of stochastic transport fluid models in
[
13
,
16
]
.
The main contributions of this work are as follows. In section
2
, we define the mean velocity by assuming a factorisation of the full flow map into the fluctuation and mean maps. Then, we use Magus expansion of the velocity field of the composite flow such that it is expressed by vector fields and their actions only, in a similar fashion to
[
15
,
18
]
. This way, the expectation operator acts on vector space quantities only and can be easily defined.
In section
3
, using tools from Malliavin calculus, we consider a normalised KE Lagrangian functional such that it is well-defined when the input velocity contains white noise terms. To obtain a dynamical equation of the mean flow, we first make dynamical assumptions on the fluctuations vector fields in assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
. Then, we explicitly computed the averaged Lagrangian and derived a stochastically averaged version of the anisotropic Lagrangian averaged Euler’s equation
[
15
]
through an Euler-Poincaré variational principle.

(2.4)

where the notation
\circ\mathrm{d}g^{\epsilon}_{t}

\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{M})

we rewrite the fluctuations of
\overline{u}_{t}
defined in powers of
\epsilon

\displaystyle\begin{split}\circ\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{\epsilon}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}&=\circ\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{\epsilon}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}+\epsilon\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{\epsilon}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\right)\,+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{\epsilon}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\right)\,+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\\
&:=\circ\mathrm{d}v^{0}+\circ\epsilon\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}+\circ\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,,\end{split}

(2.5)

such that expectation is applied to
\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{\epsilon}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
. The (formal) Lie algebra of divergence free vector fields is a linear space where these operations can be performed unambiguously. In the expansion (
2.5
), we will express the first and second order fluctuation fields
\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}
and
\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime\prime}
from the deformations of
\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}=:w_{t},\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}=:\chi_{t}\,,\quad w_{t},\chi_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}}(\mathcal{M})\,,\quad\forall t\in[0,T]\,.

(2.6)

Here, the deformation vector fields
w_{t}
and
\chi_{t}
are assumed to be semi-martingales which possess a unique decomposition into finite variation and (local) martingale parts.

Lemma 1

In terms of the deformation vector fields
w_{t}
and
\chi_{t}
, the fluctuation stochastic vector fields
\mathrm{d}v^{0}
,
\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{d}v^{\prime\prime}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}v^{0}

\displaystyle=\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}{w_{t}}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}{w_{t}}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}{w_{t}}\,,

(2.9)

where
\nabla
is a fixed torsion-free connection. For convenience we will use the Levi-Civita connection induced by the metric
\bm{g}
.

Proof

The
\mathcal{O}(1)
term
{\rm d}v^{0}
can be deduced immediately from the the near identity property
\Xi^{\epsilon}_{0}\equiv e
and
\operatorname{Ad}_{e}\overline{u}_{t}=\overline{u}_{t}
,

\displaystyle{\rm d}v^{0}=\mathrm{d}g_{t}^{0}g_{t}^{0;-1}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{0}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}^{0}\Xi_{t}^{0;-1}=\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

To calculate higher order terms, we differentiate the decomposition, where the first term is a direct application of the definition of the adjoint representation (Lie chain rule),

\displaystyle\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\circ{\rm d}v^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\right),\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}}\overline{u}_{t}:=\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,.

To take the derivative of
\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}
, we express
\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}=\exp(\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon})
and differentiate
\Omega^{\epsilon}_{t}
through the chain rule on the exponential map. This procedure is known as a Magnus expansion
[
14
]
. Although we work formally, the application of this type of theory to the infinite dimensional group of volume preserving diffeomorphisms can be found in
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
. We state the following formulae that can be obtained through known derivatives of the exponential map at the identity
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon},\quad\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\left(\cdot\right):=\operatorname{ad}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\left(\operatorname{ad}^{k-1}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\left(\cdot\right)\right),\quad\operatorname{ad}^{0}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}:=I\,.

(2.10)

The time derivative
\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\epsilon}_{t}
is expressible as the series
[
3
]

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\epsilon}_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{k}}{k!}\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right)\,,

(2.11)

where
B_{k}
denotes the Bernoulli numbers with
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
. From the initial assumption
\Xi^{0}_{t}\equiv e
it must be the case that
\Omega_{t}^{0}\equiv\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega^{0}_{t}\equiv\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}^{0}\equiv 0

Using the Magnus generator

, we can now compute first

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}=:\circ\mathrm{d}\delta\Omega_{t}\,.

Note that only the

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}\delta\Omega_{t}=\delta\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right)=\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\delta\Xi_{t}\Xi^{0;-1}_{t}\right)+{\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{0}_{t}\delta(\Xi^{-1}_{t})}=\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t},\quad\delta\Omega_{t}=\delta\Xi_{t}:=w_{t}\,.

(2.13)

Consequently, we recover

.

In order to compute the second order fluctuation of
{\rm d}v^{\epsilon}
we require the connection to take second derivatives. We first differentiate
\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi^{\epsilon}}\overline{u}
through twice application of the Lie chain rule, where the shorthand

denotes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon}}\overline{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\operatorname{ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}}\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{ad}_{\zeta^{\epsilon}_{t}}\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}}\operatorname{ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}}\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

where
\zeta^{\epsilon}_{t}:=\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}-\nabla_{\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}

In particular, the term
\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}
arises when one attempts to compute a subsequent variation of
\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;\prime}\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}

without use of a connection, however a connection is required to relate this quantity to the fluctuations

For the second derivative of

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ad}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}\right)=\circ\mathrm{d}\delta^{2}\Omega_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\delta\Omega_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}\delta\Omega_{t}\,.

(2.14)

Using the relation

and

\displaystyle\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}\,,

Remark 1
(Connection dependence and matrix Lie groups)

The expressions (
2.12
), (
2.14
) define equations on any Lie algebra in the absence of any additional structure. As a consequence of
\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{0}_{t}=0
, (
2.13
) can always be used to relate the variations of
\Omega
and
\Xi
. The second order term
\delta^{2}\Omega_{t}
differs, as we must define an action of Lie algebra terms using external structures such as a connection. In order to proceed, one formally writes,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\circ\mathrm{d}\delta^{2}\Omega_{t}&=\delta^{2}\circ\mathrm{d}\Omega_{t}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\epsilon^{2}}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ad}_{\Omega_{t}^{\epsilon}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right)\\
&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\left[\frac{\partial\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}}{\partial\epsilon}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right]+\operatorname{ad}_{\frac{\partial\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}}{\partial\epsilon}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right)-\operatorname{ad}_{\delta\Omega_{t}}\delta(\circ\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}\Xi^{-1}_{t})\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\delta^{2}\Xi_{t}\Xi^{0;-1}_{t}+\delta\Xi_{t}\delta\Xi^{-1}_{t}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{\delta\Xi_{t}}\delta(\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}\Xi_{t}^{-1})-\operatorname{ad}_{\delta\Omega_{t}}\delta(\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}\Xi^{-1}_{t})=\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}+\delta\Xi_{t}\delta\Xi^{-1}_{t}\right)\,.\end{split}

(2.15)

When
\mathfrak{g}
is finite dimensional, one may appeal to Ado’s Theorem to embed
\mathfrak{g}\subset\operatorname{GL}(N)
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
and give meaning to
\delta\Xi_{t}\delta\Xi^{-1}_{t}=-\delta\Xi_{t}\Xi_{t}^{0;-1}\delta\Xi_{t}\Xi_{t}^{0;-1}=-\delta\Xi_{t}\delta\Xi_{t}=-w^{2}
as a product of matrices.
One can then show the second order expansion cancels significantly as,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime\prime}&=\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-w_{t}^{2}}\overline{u}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}-w^{2}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\mathrm{d}t-2w_{t}(\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime})\,,\end{split}

under the property that
\operatorname{ad}
reduces to the commutator of
\operatorname{GL}(N)
matrices within this embedding. In the case of the diffeomorphism group, a concrete representation of (
2.14
) in terms of
\delta\Xi_{t}
requires identifying
\delta\Xi_{t}\delta\Xi^{-1}_{t}:=-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}
as a covariant derivative. In Euclidean space, we recover consistency with the ordinary chain rule where the connection is canonical,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\delta\Xi_{t}\circ\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[D(\delta\Xi_{t})\circ\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right]\cdot\frac{\partial\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}}{\partial\epsilon}\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}=-\left[D(\delta\Xi_{t})\circ\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right]\cdot\left[D(\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t})\circ\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}\right]^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t}}{\partial\epsilon}\circ\Xi_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}\right)\Bigg|_{\epsilon=0}

\displaystyle=-D(\delta\Xi_{t})\cdot\delta\Xi_{t}=-\nabla^{E}_{\delta\Xi_{t}}\delta\Xi_{t}\,.

In the above, we have denoted by
\nabla^{E}
the Euclidean connection and

the total (Frechêt) derivative for maps
\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the second equality was derived from the inverse function theorem. In curved geometries, the choice of
\nabla
is non-canonical, although the Levi-Civita connection is a natural choice we take throughout.

Lemma 2

The second order fluctuation vector fields

This follows from repeated use of the following identities,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X}Y=-[X,Y]=-\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{Y}X,\quad\nabla\nabla Z(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}Z,\quad\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\,,

(2.17)

which follows directly from the definition of being a torsion-free connection and the Leibniz property. Here, we have the covariant Hessian operator
\nabla\nabla
defined on tensor

of type

by
\nabla\nabla T=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}Tdx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}
. Inserting (
2.17
) into the equation for
\mathrm{d}v^{\prime\prime}
thus gives,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime\prime}&=\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}\\
&=\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\circ\mathrm{d}\left(\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\left(\nabla_{\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}}w_{t}+\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{w_{t}}(\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-\operatorname{ad}_{\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{-\nabla_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}w_{t}}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{w_{t}}(-\nabla_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 200.0003pt-\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla w_{t}(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla\nabla w_{t}(w_{t},\overline{u}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\\
&=\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ{\rm d}v^{\prime}\,.\end{split}

In the last line, we picked up a curvature term as a consequence of the identity,

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z:=-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z=-\nabla\nabla Z(X,Y)+\nabla\nabla Z(Y,X)\,.

The curvature term
R(\overline{u},w)w
vanishes in flat geometries, but otherwise may be expressed as
R(\overline{u},w)w=R(\overline{u},\cdot):(w\otimes w)
as a contraction with a
(2,1)
tensor field
R(\overline{u},\cdot)=R^{\beta}_{ij\alpha}\overline{u}^{i}dx^{j}\otimes dx^{\alpha}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\beta}}
with a
(2,0)
tensor field
w\otimes w
.
With the alternative formulation of Lemma
2
, the full expansion of
\circ\mathrm{d}g^{\epsilon}_{t}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}
to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
is given by,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\circ\mathrm{d}g^{\epsilon}_{t}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}=\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t&+\epsilon\left(\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
&+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,,\end{split}

(2.18)

Remark 2
(Expectation of advected quantities)

Advected quantities
\overline{a}_{t}\in\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{M})
of the mean flow
\overline{u}_{t}
can also be perturbed in similar fashion to the perturbed stochastic velocity field
\circ\mathrm{d}g^{\epsilon}g^{\epsilon;-1}
and expanded in (
2.18

a^{\epsilon}_{t}:=\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t*}\overline{a}_{t}=g^{\epsilon}_{t*}a_{0}
. Applying the standard Lie chain rule in the
\epsilon

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\left(\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t*}a^{\epsilon}_{t}\right)=\Xi^{\epsilon}_{t*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}a^{\epsilon}_{t}-\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\epsilon;\prime}_{t}\Xi^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}}a^{\epsilon}_{t}\,,

where
\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{M})
is the Lie derivative operator, we have the expansion

\displaystyle a^{\epsilon}_{t}=\overline{a}_{t}-\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{w_{t}}\overline{a}_{t}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left(-\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{t}-\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}}\overline{a}_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{w_{t}}\mathcal{L}_{w_{t}}\overline{a}_{t}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,.

(2.19)

3
Closure via variational principle

The direct substitution of the expansion (
2.18
) into the Lagrangian (
2.1
), dropping all cubic and higher order terms, results in the following formal action,

\displaystyle\begin{split}S=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{2}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\overline{u}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\epsilon\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\overline{u}_{t},\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\right)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\dot{w}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t},\dot{w}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]:=\mathbb{E}[I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}]\,.\end{split}

(3.1)

Here,
I_{1}
is the kinetic energy of the mean velocity
\overline{u}_{t}
;
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
are mixed products with stochastic fluctuations
w_{t}
and
\chi_{t}
, of the mean velocity
\overline{u}_{t}
, understood as stochastic integrals. The kinetic energy of the fluctuations
I_{4}
is ill-defined due to terms representing the formal product of white noise and has no classical interpretation.

In existing literature of stochastic variational principles where the action contains explicit integration against semi-martingales, e.g.,
[
4
,
7
,
13
,
12
]
, terms such as
I_{4}
are typically dropped. Our approach is to instead retain these terms under an average, which allow for a renormalisation of
I_{4}
into a meaningful quantity. The method we employ is to interpret
I_{4}
using the Wick product, which defines products of white noise through Malliavin calculus.

We very briefly discuss this construction. Since our usage is only elementary, we do not dwell substantially on definitions. For precise details, we refer the reader to
[
GjessingHoldenLindstrømØksendalUbøeZhang+1993+29+67
]
and
[
17
]
. One defines a
white noise probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu)
of random distributions inside a sample space of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
valued tempered distributions
\Omega:=\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
. A probability measure and Borel sigma algebra exists on this vector space via the Bochner-Milnos theorem.

One may then define
L^{2}(\Omega)
-integrable random variables taking values in the Schwartz class which may be characterised by convergent Itô-Weiner chaos expansions. The elements of
L^{2}(\Omega)
with a rapidly decreasing series expansion are known as the Hida test functions
\mathcal{S}(\Omega)
, and elements its dual space
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\Omega)
are known as Hida distributions. Within this space, a distribution valued stochastic process
\dot{W}_{t}
may be defined. Crucial for us is that
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\Omega)
contains a closed, associative, commutative product that is defined by the formal Cauchy product of the series defined in an
L^{2}(\Omega)
orthogonal basis,

\displaystyle S(\omega,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l})\\[2.45836pt]
l\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(t)H_{\alpha}(\omega),\quad T(\omega,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})\\[2.45836pt]
m\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{c}_{\beta}(t)H_{\beta}(\omega),\quad S\,\widehat{\diamond}\,T(\omega,t):=\sum_{\gamma=\alpha+\beta}c_{\alpha}(t)\widetilde{c}_{\beta}(t)H_{\alpha+\beta}(\omega)\,.

The Wick product satisfies several desirable properties, firstly,
\mathbb{E}[S\,\widehat{\diamond}\,T]=\mathbb{E}[S]\cdot\mathbb{E}[T]
, where
S,T\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\Omega)
need not be independent random variables. Secondly, if either
S,T
is deterministic (i.e.
S(t)=\mathbb{E}[S](t):=c_{0}(t)
), then
S\,\widehat{\diamond}\,T=S\cdot T
reduces to the ordinary product (well defined as a chaos expansion times a constant). Finally, is the correspondence of an Itô diffusion with its
S^{*}(\Omega)
valued “Wick calculus" representation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=u(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t}\iff\dot{X}_{t}=u(X_{t})+\sigma(X_{t})\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}\,,

which can be proven rigorously using the Skorohod integral from Malliavin calculus. Interpreting white noise as a Hida distribution value quantity,
\dot{W}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\Omega)
with
\mathbb{E}[\dot{W}_{t}]=0
, we
choose
to interpret the white noise products of
I_{4}
in the Wick sense. With this choice the expectation distributes to cancel
I_{4}
. In the other integrals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
we have at least one deterministic factor, making the distinction between Wick and ordinary products trivial.
Thus, we will proceed by making assumptions to the dynamics of
w_{t}
and
\chi_{t}
and take averages of all quantities.

Assumption 3.1
(Additive stochastic Taylor hypothesis)

We assume that the dynamics of the first order fluctuation vector field
w_{t}
is driven by Brownian motion additively such that it can be written as

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\xi\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}\,,

(3.2)

for some
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
that are prescribed, divergence free and temporally constant.

In this case, the Itô and Stratonovich interpretations coincide,

\displaystyle\xi\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{i}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\mathrm{d}W^{i}_{t}=\xi\mathrm{d}W_{t}\,.

In particular, taking the expectation of (
3.2
) implies that
\mathbb{E}\left[w_{t}\right]=:\overline{w}_{t}
is advected by the mean flow
\overline{u}_{t}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{w}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\overline{w}_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}=0\,,

which is equivalent for
w_{t}
to satisfy Taylor’s frozen turbulence hypothesis in expectation. From the additive stochastic Taylor hypothesis (
3.2
), via direct calculation we find the dynamics of the fluctuation correlation tensor
F_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[w_{t}\otimes w_{t}]\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}_{t}}F_{t}=(\xi\otimes\xi)\,,

(3.3)

in geometric notation. In terms of index notations, it can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F^{ij}_{t}+\overline{u}_{t}^{k}\nabla_{k}\nabla F^{ij}_{t}-F^{kj}_{t}\nabla_{k}\overline{u}_{t}^{i}-F^{ik}_{t}\nabla_{k}\overline{u}_{t}^{j}=\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\,.

Under the additive stochastic Taylor Hypothesis, the velocity decomposition up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
becomes

\displaystyle\circ\mathrm{d}g^{\epsilon}_{t}g_{t}^{\epsilon;-1}=\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\epsilon\xi\circ dW_{t}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left(\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\xi\circ dW_{t}\right)\,,

which can be expressed in Wick calculus and distributional white noise
{\dot{W}}_{t}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}v^{\epsilon}_{t}=\partial_{t}g^{\epsilon}_{t}g^{\epsilon;-1}_{t}=\overline{u}_{t}+\epsilon\xi\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}&+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left(\partial_{t}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\xi\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}\right)\\
&-\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{2}\left(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla_{w_{t}}\xi_{i}+\nabla_{\nabla_{w_{t}}\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}+\nabla_{w_{t}}\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.\end{split}

(3.4)

Here, the Stratonovich to Itô correction for the additive stochastic Taylor hypothesis results in higher-order terms that are dropped in the expansion by
\epsilon
. Noting that there is no canonical choice when applying the expectation, one may apply it to the velocity decomposition expressed on
\partial_{t}g^{\epsilon}_{t}\in T_{g^{\epsilon}_{t}}\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{M})
or on
v^{\epsilon}_{t}=\partial_{t}g^{\epsilon}_{t}g^{\epsilon,-1}_{t}=T_{e}\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
, which corresponds to the Lagrangian and Eulerian expressions of the fluid velocity respectively.
In taking the expectation of the Euler-Poincaré variational principle, we make the modelling choice to apply the expectation directly to the vector field
v^{\epsilon}_{t}
at fixed Eulerian coordinates.
Thus, we obtain the expected mean flow expansion in terms of
\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\overline{v}^{\epsilon}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[v^{\epsilon}_{t}]=\overline{u}_{t}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left(\partial_{t}\overline{\chi}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\overline{\chi}_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}:F_{t}+R(\overline{u}_{t},\cdot):F_{t}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,.

(3.5)

Assumption 3.2
(Expectation orthogonality hypothesis)

We assume that the
material
derivative of the expectation of the second order fluctuation vector field
\chi_{t}
is orthogonal to the mean flow
\overline{u}_{t}
under metric pairing. That is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\partial_{t}\overline{\chi}_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\overline{\chi}_{t})=0\,,

(3.6)

where
\overline{\chi}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[\chi_{t}]
. Noting that
\overline{u}_{t}
is assumed to be deterministic and
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
commutes with spatial derivatives and integrals, the condition (
3.6
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\circ\mathrm{d}\chi_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\chi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t)=\mathrm{d}M_{t}\,,

where
M_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
is a martingale.

Under the assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
, substituting the white noise velocity expansion (
3.4
) into the white noise Lagrangian (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}S&=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{2}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t}\,,\,\overline{u}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\epsilon\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\xi)\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\xi\,,\,\xi\right)\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\overline{u}_{t}\,,\,\partial_{t}\chi_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\chi_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}(w_{t},w_{t})+R(\overline{u}_{t},w_{t})w_{t}-2\nabla_{w_{t}}\xi\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{2}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t}\,,\,\overline{u}_{t})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\overline{u}_{t}\,,\,\partial_{t}\overline{\chi}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{\overline{\chi}_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}:F_{t}+R(\overline{u}_{t},\cdot):F_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{2}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\overline{u}_{t})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}\left(\overline{u}_{t},-\nabla_{\overline{\chi}_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\nabla\overline{u}_{t}:F_{t}+R(\overline{u}_{t},\cdot):F_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.\end{split}

Here, we have used the property that
\mathbb{E}[b(X_{t})\,\widehat{\diamond}\,\dot{W}_{t}]=0
which is analogous to the martingale property to Itô integrals in the second equality and the orthogonality condition (
3.6
) in the third equality.
Noting that
\overline{\chi}_{t}+\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}F_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\chi_{t}+\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(w_{t}\otimes w_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\chi_{t}+\nabla_{w_{t}}w_{t}\right]\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}}(\mathcal{M})
, through integration by parts and appropriate boundary conditions we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}S:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}&\frac{1}{2}\bm{g}(\overline{u}_{t},\overline{u}_{t})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}(\nabla\overline{u}_{t},\nabla\overline{u}_{t}\cdot F_{t})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\bm{g}_{ij}R^{i}_{kln}\overline{u}_{t}^{j}\overline{u}_{t}^{l}F^{kn}_{t}\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

Using the Euler-Poincaré constrained variations for
\overline{u}_{t}
,
\delta\overline{u}_{t}=\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{t}-\operatorname{ad}_{\eta_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}
for arbitrary
\eta_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}}(\mathcal{M})
vanishing at the boundaries, Hamilton’s principle
\delta S=0
yields the following Euler-Poincaré equation

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}_{t}}\right)\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}=-dp\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\overline{u}_{t}^{\flat}-\epsilon^{2}\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla\overline{u}^{\flat}_{t}\cdot F_{t})+\epsilon^{2}R(\overline{u}_{t},\cdot):F_{t}\,.

(3.8)

Here,
d:\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\Lambda^{k+1}(\mathcal{M})
is the exterior derivative,
\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla\overline{u}_{t}^{\flat}\cdot F_{t}):=\nabla_{j}(\nabla_{k}\overline{u}_{t}^{\flat}F^{jk}_{t})
, and the pressure contribution arises from the equivalence class of the dual space to the space divergence free vector fields. The Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}}
acting on
m\in\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{M})
can be expressed using covariant derivatives as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}}m=\nabla_{\overline{u}}m+(\nabla_{\overline{u}})^{T}m\,.

Together with the evolution of
F
derived from the stochastic Taylor hypothesis
3.1
, we have the closed system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}+\left(\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\right)^{T}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}=-dp\,,\\
&\partial_{t}F_{t}+\nabla F_{t}\cdot\overline{u}_{t}-\nabla\overline{u}_{t}\cdot F_{t}-(\nabla\overline{u})^{T}\cdot F_{t}=(\xi\otimes\xi)\,,\\
&\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\overline{u}_{t})=\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\xi_{i})=0\,,\end{split}

(3.9)

which are the forced anisotropic Lagrangian averaged Euler equations. Several simplification can be made by specialising to flat geometries such that curvature
R
vanishes. In this case, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1

Assuming the correlation tensor
F_{t}
has the dynamics

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}_{t}}F_{t}=G_{t}\,,

(3.10)

for arbitrary, time dependent
G_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
which can have functional dependence on
\overline{u}_{t}
,
w_{t}
and
F_{t}
. Denoting by
C(q)=\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla q\cdot F_{t})
for all arbitrary vector
q_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
, we have the commutation relation

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}},C\right]q_{t}=\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla_{q_{t}}G_{t})\,.

(3.11)

Proof

In the case
\partial_{t}\widetilde{F}_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\overline{u}_{t}}\widetilde{F}_{t}=0
, it was shown in e.g.
[
18
,
15
]
, that
\left[\partial_{t}+\overline{u}_{t}\cdot\nabla,\widetilde{C}\right]q_{t}=0
for
\widetilde{C}u=\operatorname{div}_{\nabla^{E}}(\nabla u\cdot\widetilde{F}_{t})
for Euclidean connection
\nabla^{E}
. Since the contribution from
G_{t}
enters only in term
\partial_{t}\left(C(q_{t})\right)=\partial_{t}\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla q_{t}\cdot F_{t})
, we have the required result through a similar direct computation and switching of covariant derivatives, which always holds under the assumption that
R\equiv 0
.

Corollary 1

When the curvature
R
vanishes, the Euler-Poincaré equation can (
3.8
) be expressed equivalently as

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon^{2}C)\left(\partial_{t}+\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}\right)\overline{u}_{t}-\epsilon^{2}C(u)_{i}\nabla u_{i}=-\nabla\widetilde{p}-\operatorname{div}_{\nabla}(\nabla_{\overline{u}_{t}}(\xi\otimes\xi))\,,

(3.12)

where
\widetilde{p}=p+\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|u_{t}|^{2})
is the modified pressure.

4
Conclusion

In this work, we considered an averaged Euler-Poincaré variational principle to construct sub-grid scale ideal fluid models. In this construction, the total fluid flow was assumed stochastic and averaged through the expectation in analogy with the GLM theory. The first step of this construction, made in section
2
, is the formulation of the mean velocity, defined by the factorisation of the total flow map into the fluctuation and mean maps. Then, the Magnus expansion of the stochastic flow map is considered so that the expectation operator is applied to vector fields and their actions. Our averages then occurred only at the tangent space of the identity, bypassing the difficulty in defining the expectation of diffeomorphism group-valued quantities.
In section
3
, we generalised the KE Lagrangian functional such that it is well-defined when the input velocity contains white noise terms using tools from Malliavin calculus. After making dynamical assumptions on the fluctuations vector fields in assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
, we explicitly computed the averaged Lagrangian and derived a stochastically averaged version of the anisotropic Lagrangian averaged Euler’s equation through an Euler-Poincaré variational principle.

Following this work, several open problems present themselves to be addressed in the future. First, the closure dynamics of
w_{t}
and
\chi_{t}
can be modified to include additional physics. For example, one can assume transport type stochastic perturbation for the dynamics of
w_{t}
,
\mathrm{d}w_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\overline{u}_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{w_{t}}\xi\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}=0
as to introduce dissipation into the dynamics of the correlation tensor
F_{t}
.
Second, the driving stochastic processes can be generalised from Brownian motions to geometric rough paths. For example, fractional Brownian motion or lifts of Brownian motion that incorporate a noise-induced drift. Such drifts are natural in the context of multiscale limits
[
6
]
. Lastly, the same modelling procedure can be generalised from incompressible Euler’s fluid using
\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{M})
to semi-direct products such that sub-grid scale models for a large class of fluid flows can be derived.

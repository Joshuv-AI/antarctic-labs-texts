Poisson electrodynamics on κ \kappa -Minkowski space-time
O. Abla, M. J. Neves

Abstract
Poisson electrodynamics is the semi-classical limit of U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) non-commutative gauge theory. It has been studied so far as a theoretical model, where an external field would be the source of the non-commutative effects in space-time. Being the Standard Model of fundamental interactions a local theory, the prediction of observables within it would be drastically altered by such effects. The natural question that arises is: how do particles interact with this field ? In this work, we will answer this question using point-like charged particles interacting with the Poisson gauge field, investigating how their trajectories are affected using the κ \kappa -Minkowski structure. The interaction arises from the construction of a gauge-invariant action. Using the field solutions, we find the second-order equation for the deformed Lorentz force, indicating possible effects of an emergent gravity due to non-commutativity.

Action principle and minimal coupling

Our goal in this section is to review the construction of the gauge invariant action, for a particle minimally coupled to the Poisson gauge field, following the work
[
48
]
. From this, we will study the resulting equations of motion. First, let’s recall that, in the case of commutative electrodynamics, the motion of a charged relativistic scalar particle, minimally coupled to an external electromagnetic field

, is described by the first-order action

S_{0}=-\int d\tau\left[\,p_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}+\lambda\,(P^{\mu}P_{\mu}-m^{2})\,\right]\;,

(2)

where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier,
\tau
is the proper time, and
P_{\mu}=p_{\mu}-A_{\mu}
is the modified linear momentum by the minimal coupling,
or the gauge invariant momentum. It is easy to see that the set of gauge transformations

\delta^{0}_{g}A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}g\;,\quad\delta^{0}_{g}x^{\mu}=\{g,x^{\mu}\}_{0}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{g}^{0}p_{\mu}=\{g,p_{\mu}\}_{0}=0\,,

(3)

for the gauge parameter
g
, make the first-order action invariant,
\delta^{0}_{g}S_{0}=0
, with
[\delta^{0}_{g},\delta^{0}_{h}]=0
, where
1
1

1

for any matrix the upper index enumerates rows, whilst the lower one enumerates columns.

\{x^{\mu},x^{\nu}\}_{0}=\{p_{\mu},p_{\nu}\}_{0}=0
and
\{x^{\mu},p_{\nu}\}_{0}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}
are the canonical Poisson brackets
[
49
]
. To build the first-order action of Poisson electrodynamics, we look for a particle model described by an action
S[x,p]
, which must satisfy two conditions:

•

This action must, from its variation and the consequent Euler-Lagrange equations, generate the Poisson brackets

\{x^{\mu},x^{\nu}\}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}(x)\;,\quad\{x^{\mu},p_{\nu}\}=\gamma^{\mu}_{\nu}(x,p)\,,\quad\{p_{\mu},p_{\nu}\}=0\;,

(4)

so that
S[x,p]=S_{0}[x,p]+\mathcal{O}(\Theta)

•

This action must be invariant under the Poisson gauge transformations

\delta_{g}A_{\nu}=\gamma^{\mu}_{\nu}(A)\,\partial_{\mu}g(x)+\{\,A_{\nu}(x)\,,\,g(x)\,\}\;,

(5)

i.e.
,
\delta_{g}S[x,p]=0
.

We will now describe the necessary mathematical tools to present the interaction of a charged point particle with the Poisson gauge field. For simplicity, we first assume that the non-commutativity is of the Lie algebra type, with its components given by
\Theta^{\mu\nu}(x)=f^{\mu\nu}_{\rho}\,x^{\rho}
, and the objects
f^{\mu\nu}_{\rho}
are the structure constants of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. In this case, the symplectic realization,
\{x^{\mu},x^{\nu}\}=2\,\alpha\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{\rho}\,x^{\rho}
, being
\alpha
a constant real number, and the Jacobi identity on the Poisson brackets (
4
) implies that the
\gamma^{\mu}_{\nu}
-matrices must satisfy the equation
[
34
]

\gamma^{\mu}_{\rho}\,\partial^{\rho}_{p}\gamma_{\nu}^{\sigma}-\gamma^{\sigma}_{\rho}\,\partial^{\rho}_{p}\gamma^{\mu}_{\nu}-\gamma^{\rho}_{\nu}\,f^{\mu\sigma}_{\rho}=0\;.

(6)

By introducing the inverse matrix,
\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}:=(\gamma^{-1})^{\mu}_{\nu}
, we can verify that (
6
) implies into the relation

\partial^{\mu}_{p}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\sigma}_{\nu}-\partial^{\sigma}_{p}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}+\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\rho}\,f^{\rho\beta}_{\nu}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\sigma}_{\beta}=0\;,

(7)

which is a Maurer-Cartan equation
d\bar{\gamma}_{\mu}=-\frac{1}{2}\,f_{\mu}^{\nu\rho}\,\bar{\gamma}_{\nu}\wedge\bar{\gamma}_{\rho}
for the 1-form, and
{\bar{\gamma}}=\bar{\gamma}_{\mu}\,dp^{\mu}

[
44
]
. The notation
\partial^{\mu}_{p}
means the derivatives in relation to momentum components. Now, suppose the generalized coordinates
(q^{\mu},p_{\nu})
are defined such that the Poisson brackets are canonical,
\{q^{\mu},q^{\nu}\}=\{p_{\mu},p_{\nu}\}=0
, and
\{q^{\mu}\,,\,p_{\nu}\}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}
,
which means these are Darboux coordinates
[
49
]
. Thereby, the representation of the original coordinates in terms of Darboux one is given by,
x^{\mu}=q^{\nu}\,\gamma^{\mu}_{\nu}
.

One may observe that the integration measure for structures of the type
\mathfrak{su}(2)
is trivially equal to one, which guarantees an invariant free particle action
[
44
]

S=\int\!d\tau\,p_{\mu}\,\dot{q}^{\mu}=-\int\!d\tau\,\dot{p}_{\mu}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}\,x^{\nu}\,.

(8)

Introducing the interaction, we obtain the action

S=-\int d\tau\,\left[\,\dot{p}_{\mu}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}\,x^{\nu}+\lambda\,H(x,p)\,\right]\;,

(9)

The variation of the action leads to the equations of motion

\dot{x}^{\mu}=\lambda\,\{x^{\mu},H\}\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\dot{p}^{\mu}=\lambda\,\{p^{\mu},H\}\,.

(10)

One can verify the conditions for gauge invariance by first observing how equation (

\displaystyle\delta_{g}x^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\,g(x)\,,\,x^{\mu}\,\}=-f^{\mu\nu}_{\rho}\,x^{\rho}\,\partial_{\nu}g(x)\;,

\displaystyle\delta_{g}p_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\{\,g(x)\,,\,p_{\mu}\,\}=\gamma_{\mu}^{\nu}(p)\,\partial_{\nu}g(x)\;.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\{g(x),x^{\mu}\}=-\Theta^{\mu\nu}(x)\,\partial_{\nu}g(x)\;,

(15a)

\displaystyle\delta_{g}p_{\mu}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\{g(x),p_{\mu}\}=\gamma_{\mu}^{\nu}(x,p)\,\partial_{\nu}g(x)\;,

\rho(A_{0})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\

(\nabla V)

4
The orbit equation

The components of the angular momentum

L_{i}=\varepsilon_{ijk}\,q_{j}\,p_{k}\;,

(49)

can be written in terms of the Darboux coordinates,
q^{\mu}=\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}\,x^{\nu}
. For the spatial components,
q^{i}=x^{i}
, where
\bar{\gamma}^{i}_{j}=\delta^{i}_{j}
. This leads to the conservation law

\frac{d}{d\tau}[\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\kappa\,V({\bf r})}\,{\bf r}\times{\bf v}\,]=0\;,

(50)

whose conserved quantity is defined as the deformed angular momentum

{\bf L}=\mathrm{e}^{-2\kappa\,V({\bf r})}\,{\bf r}\times{\bf v}\;.

(51)

For any central potential, the angular momentum satisfies the plane equation
{\bf r}\cdot{\bf L}=0
. The cross product of (
48
) with the angular momentum (
51
) leads to the following result

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\kappa\,V({\bf r})}\,{\bf v}\times{\bf L}\,\right]=\mathrm{e}^{-2\kappa\,V({\bf r})}\,f(r)\,r^{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\right)\;,

(52)

in which the central force
f(r)
from (
48
) associated with the potential (
36
) is given by

f(r)=\frac{c_{\kappa}Q}{r^{2}}\left[1+(1+3\alpha)\,\frac{2\kappa Q}{r}\,\right]^{-1}\;.

(53)

The relation (
52
) shows that it is not possible to obtain a conserved Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector for the central force (
53
). It is important to note that the authors in
[
47
]
obtained a super-integrable model for the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
non-commutative Poisson structure, modifying the Hamiltonian. Thus, the conservation of the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector is recovered only in the commutative limit,
\kappa\rightarrow 0

5
Conclusions

Using the symplectic groupoid prescription to include interactions
[
44
]
, we investigated a family of solutions to Poisson electrodynamics, defined in the
\kappa
-Minkowski space-time. The electrostatic equation for a scalar potential is obtained, along with the corresponding solutions for a point test charge.
We showed that the deformed potentials and the equations obtained in the commutative limit recover the usual objects of Maxwell’s electrodynamics. Next, we reviewed the dynamics of a charged particle coupled to the Poisson gauge field
[
48
]
. The equations of motion in the presence of scalar and vector potentials under the action of the corresponding central force were studied. Since energy and angular momentum are conserved, numerical solutions were obtained for the trajectory equation in a plane, and we compared these solutions with the usual solutions of the commutative case (
\kappa=0
). For
\kappa\neq 0
, the particle’s trajectory is not closed
[
47
]
, despite the attractive behavior of the central force, and the effective potential shows return points for a given negative energy. Perspectives for future work include elucidating how the model would be affected by a different choice of Poisson field strength
[
44
,
45
]
, as well as investigating the new field configurations applied to the model with matter fields
[
46
]
. Furthermore, it would be worth to explore possible emergent gravitational phenomena that depends on the
\kappa
-parameter. This new term that appeared in (
46
) could describe the motion on a curved trajectory for the particle interacting with the noncommutative field, as appeared in Eq.
(42)
of
[
60
]
. Another perspective is to investigate the central force problem for different noncommutative structures, such as
\rho
-Minkowski
[
67
]
-
[
71
]
. Construct a star product that quantizes such structures may lead to the study of the hydrogen atom
[
72
]
and fermionic particles on non-commutative spaces
[
73
]
.

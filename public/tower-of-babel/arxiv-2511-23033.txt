Hölder type estimates for Gaussian multiplicative chaos
Yulai Huang

Abstract
. We investigate the right tail behavior of a certain class of GMC ratios, reminiscent of Hölder’s inequality. We start with a heuristic argument to justify the optimal exponent in the tail estimate. Since Kahane’s convexity inequality [ 11 ] does not apply to GMC ratios, implementing the heuristic in the continuous setting is nontrivial from the viewpoint of GMC theory. We address the problem by enlarging the class of GMC ratios considered, and deduce the upper and lower bounds for the right tail of GMC ratios.



1.
Introduction

Let
h
be a log-correlated Gaussian field
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance kernel

\mathds{E}[h(x)h(y)]=-\ln|x-y|+g(x,y),

where
g
is continuous on
\Omega\times\Omega
.
For a parameter
\gamma>0
, the Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC)
associated to
h
is a random measure
on
\Omega
, formally denoted as

M_{\gamma,h}(\text{\rm d}x)=e^{\gamma h(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathds{E}[h(x)^{2}]}\;\text{\rm d}x.

Since
h
is only a random distribution, the measure
M_{\gamma,h}

should be defined as a limit of
M_{\gamma,h_{\epsilon}}

where
(h_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}
is a suitable regularization of
h
.

The study of GMC theory was initiated by Kahane in the 1980s.
In his seminal work
[
11
]
,
Kahane established the uniqueness of
the limiting measure for a special class of log-correlated kernels and regularization schemes,
and proved that
M_{\gamma,h}
is non-degenerate
if and only if
\gamma^{2}<2d
.
When
h_{\epsilon}
is the convolution of
h
with some mollifier,
Robert and Vargas showed the convergence in law of
M_{\gamma,h_{\epsilon}}
,
extending Kahane’s construction to general log-correlated kernels
[
17
]
.
See also
[
3
]
for a simple and elegant treatment
of the convergence issues by Beretycki,
and
[
18
]
for another viewpoint of GMC developed by Shamov.
A classical result in GMC theory states that
M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)

admits negative moments of all orders.
This was proved in
[
17
]
and
was extended to GMC on fractal sets in
[
8
]
.

Recently, the study of Liouville conformal field theory and related models
in mathematical physics has brought light to several interesting problems
in GMC theory.
For instance,
as an intermediate step in the proof of the
KPZ relation
,
Duplantier and Sheffield established
an upper bound for the small tail of GMC (Lemma 4.5 in
[
6
]
):
If
\Omega
is the unit disk and
h
is a zero-boundary Gaussian free field on
\Omega
, then there exist constants
C,c>0
such that

\mathds{P}(M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)<x)\;\leqslant\;Ce^{-c(\ln x)^{2}},\quad\forall x\in(0,1).

In particular, it implies the existence of negative moments of all orders for
M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)
.
This estimate is optimal for generic GMC measures.
Indeed, if
h=h^{\prime}+N
where
N
is a standard Gaussian variable independent of
h^{\prime}
, then
M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)
can be decomposed as

M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)=\exp^{\gamma N-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\gamma,h^{\prime}}(\Omega)

and the “macroscopic” term
e^{\gamma N}
dominates
the small tail behavior of
M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)
.

Using techniques in Liouville conformal field theory,
Remy obtained an exact formula for the density of
the total mass
Y_{\gamma}
of a certain GMC on the unit circle
[
16
]
,
answering a conjecture of Fyodorov and Bouchaud
[
7
]
.
The formula reveals a much sharper small tail behavior for this specific GMC:

\mathds{P}(Y_{\gamma}<x)\;\leqslant\;C\exp(-cx^{-4/\gamma^{2}}),\quad\forall x\in(0,1).

This further implies the existence of exponential moments for
Y_{\gamma}^{-1}
.
It turns out that the same phenomenon holds for a general class of GMC measures.
Motivated by the probabilistic construction of quantum Mabuchi theory,
Lacoin, Rhodes and Vargas studied the
small tail behavior of
M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)
under the assumption that the spatial average of
h
vanishes.
More precisely,
let
m_{h}(\Omega)=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}h(x)\;\text{\rm d}x
,
then the following holds for
\zeta
sufficiently large
[
13
]
:

\mathds{P}(e^{-\gamma m_{h}(\Omega)}M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)<s)\;\leqslant\;2\exp(-c|\log s|^{-\zeta}s^{-4/\gamma^{2}}),\quad\forall s>0.

In particular,
e^{\gamma m_{h}(\Omega)}M_{\gamma,h}(\Omega)^{-1}

has finite exponential moments,
which is essential in the construction
of the Mabuchi path integral in
[
13
]
.
Further work by Barashkov, Oikarinen and Wong has removed the
|\log s|
term
in the estimate and established a corresponding lower bound on the torus

[
2
]
.
This suggests that the exponent
4/\gamma^{2}
is optimal.

In the study of Liouville conformal field theory with boundary
[
9
]
,
it is speculated that the theory can still be defined
when boundary cosmological constants are negative.
This amounts to establishing a sub-exponential
right tail estimate for the ratio of two GMC
with parameter
\gamma/2
and
\gamma
.
We consider in this paper an analogue of this problem.
For
\alpha,\gamma<\sqrt{2d}
distinct
and for any Borel set
S\subset\Omega
,
define the balanced ratio of GMC associated to
h
by

Q_{h}(S)=\frac{M_{\alpha,h}(S)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{M_{\gamma,h}(S)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}.

The goal of this paper is to study the behavior of

\mathds{P}(Q_{h}(S)>x)
as
x\to\infty
.

Note that if
h=h^{\prime}+N
for some Gaussian variable
N
independent of
h
,
the quotient
Q_{h}(S)/Q_{h^{\prime}}(S)

does not contain any macroscopic term of the form
e^{\beta N}
.
This explains the terminology
balanced
.
Moreover, for any function
f
on
S
,
Hölder’s inequality gives

(1.1)

\frac{(\int_{S}e^{\alpha f(x)}\;\text{\rm d}x)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{(\int_{S}e^{\gamma f(x)}\;\text{\rm d}x)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}\;\leqslant\;|S|

where
|S|
is the Lebesgue measure of
S
.
We can view
Q_{h}(S)
as a probabilistic counterpart
of the above deterministic ratio,
with function
f
replaced by Gaussian field
h
.
Since
\mathds{E}[h(x)^{2}]
diverges,
Hölder’s inequality does not give us an upper bound for
Q_{h}(S)
.
Nevertheless, we can establish a sub-exponential
estimate for
\mathds{P}(Q_{h}(S)>x)
(see Theorem
1.1
),
reminiscent of Hölder’s inequality in the deterministic setting.

Conventions and notations.

We denote by
a\wedge b
and
a\vee b
the minimum and maximum of
a
and
b
,
respectively.
We also write
x_{+}=x\vee 0
and

\ln_{+}x=(\ln x)\vee 0
.
We call a function smooth if it is infinitely differentiable (
C^{\infty}
).
Throughout this paper, all Gaussian fields are assumed to be centered.

1.1.
Setup and main results

Let
S=[0,1]^{2}
be a unit square.
Consider a Gaussian field
h
defined on a neighborhood
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
S

with covariance kernel

K(x,y)=-\ln|x-y|+g(x,y),

where
g
is smooth on
\Omega\times\Omega
.
The main result of this paper is the following
upper bound for the right tail of
Q_{h}(S)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any distinct
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,2)
and

0<\delta<\frac{4}{\alpha\gamma}
,
there exists some constants
C,c>0
such that

\mathds{P}(Q_{h}(S)>x)\;\leqslant\;C\exp(-cx^{\frac{4}{\alpha\gamma}-\delta}),\quad\forall x>0.

At a formal level, the exponent
4/\alpha\gamma

given by the above estimate aligns with the
small deviation result for GMC
and the conjectured left tail estimate for
derivative GMC in
[
13
]
.
In fact, we can rewrite Theorem
1.1
as

\ln\mathds{P}(M_{\alpha,h}(S)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}}M_{\gamma,h}(S)<x)\lesssim-x^{-\frac{4}{\gamma(\gamma-\alpha)}+\delta}\quad\text{as}\quad x\to 0.

As
\alpha\to 0
we have
M_{\alpha,h}(S)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\to e^{m_{h}(S)}
.
Then, the above estimate becomes

\ln\mathds{P}(e^{-\gamma m_{h}(S)}M_{\gamma,h}(S)<x)\lesssim-x^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}+\delta}\quad\text{as}\quad x\to 0,

whose exponent matches the small deviation result
mentioned earlier.
Moreover, from the elementary inequality

\theta x+(1-\theta)y\;\geqslant\;x^{\theta}y^{1-\theta}
,
we deduce

-\alpha^{-1}Q_{h}(S)\;\leqslant\;(\gamma-\alpha)^{-1}(M_{\gamma,h}(S)-M_{\alpha,h}(S)).

As
\alpha\to\gamma
, the right hand side tends to
the so-called derivative martingale
M_{\gamma,h}(S)^{\prime}
. Then

\ln\mathds{P}(M_{\gamma,h}(S)^{\prime}<-x)\;\leqslant\;\lim_{\alpha\to\gamma^{-}}\ln\mathds{P}(Q_{h}(S)>\alpha x)\lesssim-x^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}-\delta}\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty.

This matches the conjectured left tail behavior
\ln\mathds{P}(M_{\gamma,h}^{\prime}(S)<-x)\lesssim-x^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}
in
[
13
]
.
However, the above discussions remain far from
being rigorous. In the setting of
branching random walks,
the conjecture is proved in
[
4
,
5
]
,
while the general case is still open.

We also establish a lower bound for the right tail of
Q_{h}(S)
,
which suggests that the exponent given in Theorem
1.1
is optimal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
h

admits a decomposition
h=X+Z
where
X
is a star-scale invariant Gaussian field
(see Section
2
for a precise definition)
and
Z
is independent of
X
.
Then for any distinct
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,2)
and

\delta>0
,
there exists
C,c>0
such that

\mathds{P}(Q_{h}(S)>x)\;\geqslant\;c\exp(-Cx^{\frac{4}{\alpha\gamma}+\delta}),\quad\forall x>0.

For ease of notation, the statement of Theorem
1.2
is restricted
to covariance kernels that can be written as
the sum of a star-scale invariant kernel
and a smooth kernel.
However, the argument is valid for more general log-correlated kernels with a nice white noise decomposition.

1.2.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
,
we introduce the notion of star-scale invariant
kernels and provide a heuristic proof
of Theorem
1.1
for these covariance kernels.
In Section
3
,
we show that balanced ratios admit
positive moments of all orders.
In Section
4
, a rigorous version of the heuristic is implemented,
which results in bounds on the Laplace transform
of balanced ratios.
In Section
5
, we establish the
main theorems using results from the previous sections.
In the Appendix,
statements and proofs that deviate from the main line of argument are included.
Notably, we establish two Kahane-type inequalities
for balanced ratios
which is used repeatedly in our analysis.

2.
Backgrounds and heuristics

In this section, we introduce the notion of star-scale invariant kernels,
a class of log-correlated kernels which will play
a particularly important role in this paper.
Then we outline a heuristic proof of Theorem
1.1

for these covariance kernels.

2.1.
Star-scale invariant kernels

Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive definite function,
which is radial, smooth, supported in the unit ball and
\rho(0)=1
.
For example, it can be realized as the convolution
of a compactly supported smooth radial function with itself.

Definition 2.1
.

The covariance kernel

K^{0}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))\;\text{\rm d}u

is called a star-scale invariant kernel.
The associated Gaussian field
X

is called a star-scale invariant field.

The star-scale invariant field
X
admits a
white noise decomposition
(X_{t}(x))_{t\;\geqslant\;0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
,
that is a smooth Gaussian field on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

with covariance kernel

\mathds{E}[X_{t}(x)X_{s}(y)]=\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))\;\text{\rm d}u.

It is straightforward to see that
X_{t}-X_{s}
is independent of
X_{s}
for
t>s
, and that the law of
X_{t}

is invariant under translation.
The lemma below explains the name
star-scale invariant
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The function
g_{0}(x,y)=K^{0}(x,y)+\ln|x-y|

extends to a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In addition, we have the following properties:

(i)

There exists
A>0
such that

|\mathds{E}[X_{t}(x)X_{t}(y)]-t\wedge\ln_{+}|x-y|^{-1}|<A,\quad\forall t\;\geqslant\;0,\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(ii)

For any
t>s
, we have an equality in law

(X_{t}(x)-X_{s}(x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{law}}}{{=}}(X_{t-s}(e^{s}x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

(iii)

For any
t>s
and
|x-y|\;\geqslant\;e^{-s}
,

X_{t}(x)-X_{s}(x)
and
X_{t}(y)-X_{s}(y)
are independent.

Proof.

We only show the smoothness of
g_{0}
.
The rest of the properties are left to the readers.
Since
\rho
is smooth and radial,
there exists a smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\rho(x)=f(|x|^{2})
.
Then
f(0)=1
and
\operatorname{supp}f\subset(-\infty,1]
.
By a change of variable
t=e^{2u}|x-y|^{2}
we obtain

g_{0}(x,y)=\int_{|x-y|^{2}}^{1}\frac{f(t)-1}{2t}\;\text{\rm d}t.

Since the integrand is smooth in
t
and
the mapping
(x,y)\mapsto|x-y|^{2}

is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

g_{0}
extends smoothly on the diagonal.
∎

For the rest of the paper,
fix a star-scale invariant field
X

and a white noise decomposition
(X_{t}(x))_{t\;\geqslant\;0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
We adopt the notation

G_{\gamma,t}=M_{\gamma,X_{t}},\quad G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}=M_{\gamma,X_{t}-X_{s}}.

Denote also
G_{\gamma}=M_{\gamma,X}
and
G^{(s)}_{\gamma}=M_{\gamma,X-X_{s}}
.

2.2.
Cascade relation

Let
S=[0,1]^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional cube
and
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
.
We give here a heuristic justification
of the expected tail behavior:

\ln\mathds{P}(Q_{X}(S)>x)\lesssim-x^{\frac{2d}{\alpha\gamma}}\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty.

First, observe that
Q_{X}
is subadditive
on Borel sets, i.e.
Q_{X}(B\cup B^{\prime})\;\leqslant\;Q_{X}(B)+Q_{X}(B^{\prime})
for
B
and
B^{\prime}
disjoint.
In fact, it is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality

(2.1)

G_{\alpha}(B)+G_{\alpha}(B^{\prime})\;\leqslant\;(G_{\gamma}(B)+G_{\gamma}(B^{\prime}))^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}(Q_{X}(B)+Q_{X}(B^{\prime}))^{\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{\gamma}}.

Fix an even integer
e^{s}
.
Divide
S
evenly into
e^{ds}
small cubes

\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{e^{ds}}\}
of side length
e^{-s}
.
Notice that

M_{\gamma,X}(S_{i})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{S_{i}}e^{\gamma X_{t}(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}t}\;\text{\rm d}x=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{S_{i}}e^{\gamma X_{s}(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s}\;G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(\text{\rm d}x)=\int_{S_{i}}e^{\gamma X_{s}(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s}\;G^{(s)}_{\gamma}(\text{\rm d}x).

Let
\phi_{i}(x)=e^{-s}x+a_{i}
be the affine map
such that
\phi_{i}(S)=S_{i}
.
Define a Gaussian field
X^{(i)}
on
S
by
X^{(i)}=(X-X_{s})\circ\phi_{i}
.
Using the subadditivity of
Q_{X}

and the change of variable
x_{i}=\phi_{i}(x)
, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q_{X}(S)\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}Q_{X}(S_{i})

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}s}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}\frac{(\int_{S_{i}}e^{\alpha X_{s}(x_{i})}\;G^{(s)}_{\alpha}(\text{\rm d}x_{i}))^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{(\int_{S_{i}}e^{\gamma X_{s}(x_{i})}\;G^{(s)}_{\gamma}(\text{\rm d}x_{i}))^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)s}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}\frac{(\int_{S}e^{\alpha X_{s}(\phi_{i}(x))}\;M_{\alpha,X^{(i)}}(\text{\rm d}x))^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{(\int_{S}e^{\gamma X_{s}(\phi_{i}(x))}\;M_{\gamma,X^{(i)}}(\text{\rm d}x))^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}.

To simplify the discussion we make three assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

X_{s}|_{S_{i}}=X_{s}(x_{i})

with
x_{i}
the barycenter of
S_{i}
.

Assumption 2
.

X^{(i)}
has the same law as
X|_{S}
.

Assumption 3
.

X^{(i)}
are mutually
independent for
i=1,\ldots,e^{ds}
.

These assumptions correspond to Lemma
2.2
(i)(ii)(iii) respectively.
In fact, the covariance kernel of
X_{s}
on each cube
S_{i}

satisfies

|\mathds{E}[X_{s}(x)X_{s}(y)]-s|\;\leqslant\;A

for some universal constant
A
in (i).
Hence, we can assume that
X_{s}
is constant on each cube
S_{i}
and Assumption
1
follows.
Assumption
2
is a directly consequence of (ii).
By (iii),

X^{(i)}
and
X^{(j)}
are independent
if
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
are disjoint,
which leads to Assumption
3
.

Under Assumption
1
,

e^{\gamma X_{s}(\phi_{i}(x))}
and
e^{\alpha X_{s}(\phi_{i}(x))}

on the right hand side of (
2.2
)
cancel
thanks to the choice of exponents.
Then, (
2.2
) becomes
a stochastic dominance

Q_{X}(S)\;\leqslant\;e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)s}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}Q_{X^{(i)}}(S)

where
Q_{X^{(i)}}(S)
are mutually independent
and have the same law as
Q_{X}(S)
,
thanks to Assumption
2
and
3
.
We call the inequality above the
cascade relation

for balanced ratios,
as a similar equation holds for Mandelbrot’s multiplicative cascades
[
12
]
.

Assume that
Q_{X}(S)
admits positive moments of all orders,
we can deduce from the cascade relation and induction that
Q_{X}(S)
has exponential moments.
Then Cramér’s large deviation theorem implies
for
\theta>\mathds{E}[Q_{X}(s)]

\ln\mathds{P}(Q_{X}(S)>e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}s}\theta)\;\leqslant\;\ln\mathds{P}\big(e^{-ds}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}Q_{X_{i}}(S)>\theta\big)\lesssim-e^{-ds}.

This gives us an expected tail estimate
\ln\mathds{P}(Q_{X}(S)>x)\lesssim-x^{\frac{2d}{\alpha\gamma}}

for star-scale invariant kernels.
For
d=2
the exponent given here matches Theorem
1.1
.

It is worth pointing out that, in our problem,
Assumption
1
is not very straightforward to justify.
A standard technique in GMC theory is to apply
Kahane’s inequality
[
11
]

to compare the GMC associated to
X_{s}
and that
associated to
X_{s}(x_{i})
.
For balanced ratios, this amounts to replacing
X_{s}|_{S_{i}}

by

X_{s}(x_{i})\pm\sup_{x\in S_{i}}|X_{s}(x)-X_{s}(x_{i})|.

However, such a replacement introduces a macroscopic factor
\exp(\sup_{x\in S_{i}}|X_{s}(x)-X_{s}(x_{i})|)

in the cascade relation,
whose tail is too heavy for our purposes.

3.
Positive moments of balanced ratios

As a preliminary result, we show in this section
the existence of positive moments of all orders
for balanced ratios.
For this section,

S=[0,1]^{d}
is a unit cube
and
0<\alpha<\gamma<\sqrt{2d}
.
The main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
h
be a Gaussian field on a neighborhood of
S

with covariance kernel

\mathds{E}[h(x)h(y)]=-\ln|x-y|+g(x,y),

where
g
is continuous.
Then for any
0<\alpha<\gamma<\sqrt{2d}
,

\mathds{E}[Q_{h}(S)^{n}]<\infty,\quad\forall n\;\geqslant\;0.

We provide below an alternative formulation of the theorem,
which will be useful in the next section.
Recall that
G_{\gamma,t}
is the GMC associated
to the white noise decomposition
(X_{t}(x))_{t\;\geqslant\;0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
defined in Section
2
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
0<\alpha<\gamma<\sqrt{2d}
, we have

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S)^{n}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]<\infty,\quad\forall n\;\geqslant\;0.

3.1.
Scaling relations for GMC ratios

To begin with,
we prove the scaling relation for GMC ratios associated to star-scale invariant kernels.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
, define the scaling exponent

\zeta(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}p^{2}-(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}+d+\alpha\gamma q)p+(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+d)q+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}q^{2}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
R>0
.
For any Borel set
B\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\;\leqslant\;R\}

and
s<t
, we have

\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(e^{-s}B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t}(e^{-s}B)^{q}}\Big]\asymp e^{\zeta(p,q)s}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t-s}(B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t-s}(B)^{q}}\Big]

where
P\asymp P^{\prime}
is used to denote
C^{-1}\;\leqslant\;P/P^{\prime}\;\leqslant\;C
for some constant

C
determined by
R,p,q
.

Proof.

We can write

\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(e^{-s}B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t}(e^{-s}B)^{q}}=\frac{\big(\int_{e^{-s}B}e^{\alpha X_{s}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}s}\;G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(\text{\rm d}x)\big)^{p}}{\big(\int_{e^{-s}B}e^{\gamma X_{s}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s}\;G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(\text{\rm d}x)\big)^{q}}\;.

By Lemma
2.2
(i),
there exists
A>0
determined by
R
, such that

|\mathds{E}[X_{s}(x)X_{s}(y)]-s|\;\leqslant\;A,\quad\forall s\;\geqslant\;0\ \forall x,y\in e^{-s}B.

Applying Proposition
6.3

to
X_{s}
and a Gaussian variable
N
with variance
s
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(e^{-s}B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t}(e^{-s}B)^{q}}\Big]\asymp\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{(\int_{e^{-s}B}e^{\alpha N-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}s}\;G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(\text{\rm d}x))^{p}}{(\int_{e^{-s}B}e^{\gamma N-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s}\;G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(\text{\rm d}x))^{q}}\Big]=e^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha p-\gamma q)^{2}s+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}q-\alpha^{2}p)s}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(e^{-s}B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(e^{-s}B)^{q}}\Big].

Since
X_{t}-X_{s}
has the same law of
X_{t-s}(e^{s}\cdot)
,
we have by a change of variable

\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(e^{-s}B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(e^{-s}B)^{q}}\Big]=e^{(q-p)ds}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t-s}(B)^{p}}{G_{\gamma,t-s}(B)^{q}}\Big].

Combining above two lines we arrive at the scaling exponent
\zeta(p,q)
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.2

Set
\epsilon=\frac{d}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed,
\zeta(p,\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma})

attains its minimum at
p=n+\epsilon

and the minimum value is given by

\zeta_{n}=(\gamma-\alpha)(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{d}{\gamma})n-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{d}{\alpha})^{2}.

Motivated by the above calculation, we show by induction that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(3.1)

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]<\infty.

Then Proposition
3.2
follows from Hölder’s inequality and the fact that

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}^{-\epsilon}]
is finite (Proposition 3.6 in
[
17
]
).

Since
\epsilon<\frac{2d}{\alpha^{2}}
,
the initial step
n=0
is implied by
classical GMC theory.
Suppose that (
3.1
) holds for
n-1
where
n\;\geqslant\;1
.
Let
e^{s}
be an even integer.
Divide
S
into
e^{ds}
small cubes of equal side length
e^{-s}
.
We can partition the small cubes into
2^{d}
groups

\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{2^{d}}
,
such that each group has
N=2^{-d}e^{ds}
cubes,
and any two cubes in the same group are separated
by a distance of at least
e^{-s}
.
In fact, we can associate a dyadic number of
d
digits
to each small cube, such
that the
i
-th digits of two cubes differ whenever they share a common face
orthogonal to the
i
-th axis.
Then we split these cubes into
2^{d}
groups
based on the designated dyadic number.

We have for
a\;\geqslant\;2
and
x,y\;\geqslant\;0

\displaystyle(x+y)^{a}-x^{a}-y^{a}

\displaystyle=ay\int_{0}^{1}(x+ty)^{a-1}-(ty)^{a-1}\;\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=a(a-1)xy\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(rx+ty)^{a-2}\;\text{\rm d}r\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;a(a-1)xy(x+y)^{a-2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;a(a-1)2^{a-2}(xy^{a-1}+yx^{a-1}).

After
N
iterations, there exists
C_{N,a}>0

depending only on
N,a
such that

(\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i})^{a}\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{a}+C_{N,a}\sum_{i\neq j}x_{i}x_{j}^{a-1},\quad\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\;\geqslant\;0.

For a group
\mathcal{S}_{l}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N}\}

of small cubes,
we also use
\mathcal{S}_{l}
to denote
the disjoint union of its elements.
Since
n+\epsilon>2
,
we have

G_{\alpha,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n+\epsilon}\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})^{n+\epsilon}+C_{N,n+\epsilon}\sum_{i\neq j}G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})G_{\alpha,t}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}.

Consequently,

\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S_{i})^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]+C_{N,n+\epsilon}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathds{E}\Big[G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})G_{\alpha,t}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{-\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}\Big].

For diagonal terms, since the law of
X_{t}
is translation invariant,
we have by Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S_{i})^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]=\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(e^{-s}S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(e^{-s}S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;A_{n}e^{\zeta_{n}s}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t-s}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t-s}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]

for some constant
A_{n}
determined by
n
.
For off-diagonal terms, since

|\mathds{E}[X_{s}(x)X_{s}(y)]|\;\leqslant\;s+O(1)

for
x,y\in S
,
we can use
Kahane-type inequality
6.3

to compare
X_{s}
with the trivial field
0
,
where the underlying Radon measures
are
G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}
and
G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}
.
This results in the inequality

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})G_{\alpha,t}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{-\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}]\;\leqslant\;B_{n,s}\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(S_{i})G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S)^{-\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}]

for some constant
B_{n,s}
determined
by
n
and
s
.
Since
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
are separated by a distance of at least
e^{-s}
,
the restrictions of
X_{t}-X_{s}
on
S_{i}
and on
S_{j}
are independent.
Note also that
G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S)\;\geqslant\;G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S_{i})\vee G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S_{j})
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}(S_{i})G_{\alpha,t}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{-\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;B_{n,s}\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(S_{i})G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S_{i})^{-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}]\;\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t}^{(s)}(S_{j})^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t}^{(s)}(S_{j})^{-\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{\gamma}}]

\displaystyle=B_{n,s}e^{-d(n+\epsilon-\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma})s}\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t-s}(S)G_{\gamma,t-s}(S)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}]\;\mathds{E}[G_{\alpha,t-s}(S)^{n-1+\epsilon}G_{\gamma,t-s}(S)^{-\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{\gamma}}].

On the last line,
standard GMC theory and Hölder’s inequality
imply that the left term is bounded in
t-s
,
while the right term is bounded
in
t-s
by induction hypothesis.
Consequently the off-diagonal terms
are bounded in
t
.

To summary, for any
t
and
s
such that
t\;\geqslant\;s
, we have

\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;2^{-d}A_{n}e^{(\zeta_{n}+d)s}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t-s}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t-s}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]+A_{n,s}.

for some constant
A_{n,s}
determined by
n
and
s
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{d(n+\epsilon)}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{d}}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{d(n+\epsilon-1)}A_{n}e^{(\zeta_{n}+d)s}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t-s}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t-s}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]+2^{d(n+\epsilon)}A_{n,s}.

Since
0<\alpha<\gamma<\sqrt{2d}
,
we have
\zeta_{n}+d<0
.
Let
s
be large enough such that

2^{d(n+\epsilon-1)}A_{n}e^{(\zeta_{n}+d)s}<1.

Then

\sup_{m\in s\mathbb{N}}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,m}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,m}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]<\infty.

For any
t\;\geqslant\;0
there exists
m\in s\mathbb{N}
such that
|t-m|\;\leqslant\;s
.
We have for
x,y\in S

|\mathds{E}[X_{t}(x)X_{t}(y)]-\mathds{E}[X_{m}(x)X_{m}(y)]|\;\leqslant\;|t-m|+O(1)\;\leqslant\;s+O(1).

Using Proposition
6.3
we obtain

\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,t}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,t}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;\widetilde{C}\;\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,m}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,m}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;\widetilde{C}\sup_{m\in s\mathbb{N}}\mathds{E}\Big[\frac{G_{\alpha,m}(S)^{n+\epsilon}}{G_{\gamma,m}(S)^{\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma}}}\Big]

for some
\widetilde{C}
independent of
t
.
Therefore (
3.1
) holds for
n
,
which concludes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Define
h_{\epsilon}(x)={\epsilon}^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(y)\phi(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon})\;\text{\rm d}y

4.
Recursive moment bounds for star-scale invariant kernels

In this section, we establish
some moment bounds for balanced ratios
based on the cascade relation introduced in Section
2
.
To avoid discussions of convergence, we always work on a finite-scale approximation
X_{t}

rather than the star-scale invariant field
X
itself.

We enlarge the class of balanced ratios studied.
Assume that
S=[0,1]^{d}
and
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
are distinct.
For a
C^{1}
function
f\colon S\to\mathbb{R}
and a Borel set
B\subset S
, define a tilted version of
the balanced ratio
associated to
X_{t}
by

(4.1)

L_{f,t}(B):=\frac{(\int_{B}e^{\alpha f(x)}\;M_{\alpha,X_{t}}(\text{\rm d}x))^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{(\int_{B}e^{\gamma f(x)}\;M_{\gamma,X_{t}}(\text{\rm d}x))^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality as in (
2.1
),
we can show that
L_{f,t}
is a subadditive
function on Borel sets.

With these notations,
we can rewrite (
2.2
) as

L_{0,\infty}(S)\;\leqslant\;e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)s}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}L^{(i)}_{X_{s}\circ\phi_{i},\infty}(S)

where
L^{(i)}
is defined by (
4.1
) with
X_{t}
replaced by
X^{(i)}

(see Section
2
for the definition of
X^{(i)}
and
\phi_{i}
).
A major issue with the heuristic
is that Assumption
1
does not hold exactly,
so the equality
L^{(i)}_{X_{s}\circ\phi_{i},\infty}(S)=L^{(i)}_{0,\infty}(S)
obtained from it is
merely tentative.
Nevertheless, we can bound the positive moments of
L_{f,t}(S)
in terms of
f
,
as stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}

there exists
C_{n}>0
such that

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{n}(1+\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla f(x)|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}),\quad\forall f\in C^{1}(S).

Proof.

Let
e^{s}
be the smallest even integer larger than
\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla f(x)|
.
Divide
S
into small cubes

\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{e^{ds}}\}
of equal side length
e^{-s}
.
Then
|f(x)-f(y)|\;\leqslant\;\sqrt{d}

for
x,y\in S_{i}
.
Using the subadditivity of
L_{f,t}
, we have

L_{f,t}(S)\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}L_{f,t}(S_{i})\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}\sup_{x,y\in S_{i}}|f(x)-f(y)|}L_{0,t}(S_{i})\;\leqslant\;C_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{e^{ds}}L_{0,t}(S_{i})

where
C_{1}=\exp\big(\frac{\alpha\gamma\sqrt{d}}{\gamma-\alpha}\big)
.
From Lemma
3.3
,
the scaling exponent of
L_{0,t}(S)^{n}
is given by

\zeta\big(\frac{n\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha},\frac{n\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}\big)=\big(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d\big)n
,
i.e. there exists some constant
C_{2}
such that

\mathds{E}[L_{0,t}(S_{i})^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{2}e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)ns}\mathds{E}[L_{0,t-s}(S)^{n}],\quad t\in[s,\infty).

Then, we have for all
t\in[s,\infty)

\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{1}^{n}e^{dns}\mathds{E}[L_{0,t}(S_{1})^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{1}^{n}C_{2}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}sn}\mathds{E}[L_{0,t-s}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{3}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}ns}

where
C_{3}=C_{1}^{n}C_{2}\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[L_{0,t}(S)^{n}]

is bounded from Proposition
3.2
.
Since
\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]
is monotone in
t

by Proposition
6.4
,
we have
\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C_{3}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}ns}
.
Therefore, the lemma is concluded by
e^{s}\;\leqslant\;\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla f(x)|+2
.
∎

In fact, we can establish a rigorous version of cascade relation (
4.9
)
for
L_{f,t}(S)

and obtain stronger moment bounds by induction.
More precisely, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})

be distinct and
S=[0,1]^{d}
.
Suppose that there exist
p,q\in(0,1)
such that
p-q>\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4}
and
p>\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2d}
,
then we can find
A>0
such that
the following holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.2)

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;A^{n}(e^{qn\ln n}\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla f(x)|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}+e^{pn\ln n}),\quad\forall f\in C^{1}(S).

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction.
Fix a large
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
.
From Lemma
4.1
,
there exists
A>0
such that
(
4.2
) holds for
n=0,1,\ldots,n_{0}
.
Suppose that
(
4.2
) holds
for integers less than
n

and for above
A

where
n>n_{0}
.
We show that (
4.2
) also holds for
n
and the same
A
.
Throughout the proof, we use the shorthand

\sup=\sup_{x\in S}
.
Define for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\;\geqslant\;0

J_{n,t}=\sup_{f\in C^{1}}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}](e^{qn\ln n}\sup|\nabla f|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}+e^{pn\ln n})^{-1}.

Then
J_{n,t}
is finite by Lemma
4.1

and we need to show
J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;A^{n}
.

Fix an even integer
e^{s}
and let
t\in[s,\infty)
.
Divide
S
into
e^{ds}
small cubes
of side length
e^{-s}
.
As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
,
we partition these small cubes into
2^{d}
groups

\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{2^{d}}

such that any two cubes in the same group is
separated by a distance of at least
e^{-s}
.
Set
N=|\mathcal{S}_{l}|=2^{-d}e^{ds}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{S}_{l}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N}\}
,
then by subadditivity

L_{f,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})\;\leqslant\;\sum_{j=1}^{N}L_{f,t}(S_{j})
.
It follows from multinomial theorem that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n}]\;\leqslant\;\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1}\cdots n_{N}}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S_{1})^{n_{1}}\cdots L_{f,t}(S_{N})^{n_{N}}]

where

that sum to

\binom{n}{n_{1}\cdots n_{N}}=\frac{n!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{N}!}

We seek to control each summand
B_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{N}}=\binom{n}{n_{1}\cdots n_{N}}\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S_{1})^{n_{1}}\cdots L_{f,t}(S_{N})^{n_{N}}]
.
Let
\phi_{i}(x)=e^{-s}x+a_{i}

be the affine map such that
\phi_{i}(S)=S_{i}

) gives

L_{f,t}(S_{i})=e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)s}L^{(i)}_{(f+X_{s})\circ\phi_{i}}(S)

where

is defined by (
4.1
) with

X^{(i)}
,

.

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S_{1})^{n_{1}}\cdots L_{f,t}(S_{N})^{n_{N}}|X_{s}]=e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)sn}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathds{E}[L^{(i)}_{(f+X_{s})\circ\phi_{i}}(S)^{n_{i}}|X_{s}].

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[L^{(i)}_{(f+X_{s})\circ\phi_{i}}(S)^{n_{i}}|X_{s}]\;\leqslant\;(e^{qn_{i}\ln n_{i}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n_{i}}+e^{pn_{i}\ln n_{i}})J_{n_{i},t-s},

)

(4.6)

\displaystyle B_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{N}}\;\leqslant\;e^{n\ln n+n}e^{(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}-d)sn}\mathds{E}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{N}\big(e^{(q-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n_{i}}+e^{(p-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}\big)\Big]\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

In the following, we use
C
to denote

,
whose values may change from line to line.
Expanding the product inside the expectation
in (

),
we obtain
2^{N}
terms and each term is of the form

(4.7)

\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}m}\prod_{i\in I}e^{(q-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}\prod_{i\in I^{\prime}}e^{(p-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}

and
\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}=m
.
We now bound (

) over all possible

(n_{i})_{i\;\leqslant\;N}
and
(I,I^{\prime})
.
Denote
m^{\prime}=n-m
.
Using the convexity of
x\ln x

and the assumption that

,
(

) is smaller or equal to

\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}m}\exp\left\{(q-1)m\ln\frac{m}{|I|}+(p-1)m^{\prime}\ln\frac{m^{\prime}}{|I^{\prime}|}\right\}

and the equality holds iff

is constant for

varying
in

and in

.
Note that
|I|+|I^{\prime}|=N
.
The above expression

C^{-1}n\;\leqslant\;(1-q)m+(1-p)m^{\prime}\;\leqslant\;Cn

C^{n}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}m}\exp\left\{((q-1)m+(p-1)m^{\prime})\ln\frac{n}{N}\right\}.

\displaystyle C^{n}(e^{(p-1)n\ln\frac{n}{N}})\vee(e^{(q-1)n\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n})

\displaystyle=C^{n}e^{dsn-n\ln n}(e^{pn\ln\frac{n}{N}}+e^{qn\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}).

2^{N}

Then

.
Hence,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\big(e^{(q-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n_{i}}+e^{(p-1)n_{i}\ln n_{i}}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{N}C^{n}e^{dsn-n\ln n}(e^{pn\ln\frac{n}{N}}+e^{qn\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{dsn-n\ln n}(e^{pn\ln\frac{n}{N}}+e^{qn\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}).

Then (
4.6
) is simplified to be

\displaystyle B_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{N}}\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}sn}\big(e^{pn\ln\frac{n}{N}}+e^{qn\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathds{E}\big[\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big]\big)\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

By subadditivity,

L_{f,t}(S)^{n}\;\leqslant\;(\sum_{l=1}^{2^{d}}L_{f,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l}))^{n}\;\leqslant\;2^{nd}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{d}}L_{f,t}(\mathcal{S}_{l})^{n}
.
Combining (
4.3
) and the above
inequality, we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}sn}\big(e^{pn\ln\frac{n}{N}}+e^{qn\ln\frac{n}{N}}\mathds{E}\big[\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big]\big)\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

It follows from Proposition
6.8
and

s=o(\ln n)
that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\big[\mathbb{M}(f,s)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C^{n}\big(\mathds{E}\big[(e^{-s}\sup|\nabla X_{s}|)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big]+(e^{-s}\sup|\nabla f|\big)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C^{n}\big(e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4}n\ln n}+(e^{-s}\sup|\nabla f|)^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big).

Hence (
4.8
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[L_{f,t}(S)^{n}]\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}sn}\big(e^{pn\ln n-pnds}+e^{(q+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4})n\ln n-qnds}

\displaystyle+e^{qn\ln n-(qd+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2})sn}\sup|\nabla f|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}\big)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

Dividing both sides by
e^{qn\ln n}\sup|\nabla f|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}n}+e^{pn\ln n}
and optimizing in
f\in C^{1}(S)
,
we obtain the following cascade relation
for
L_{f,t}
:

(4.9)

J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}sn}(e^{-pdsn}+e^{(q-p+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4})n\ln n-qdsn}+e^{-(qd+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2})sn})\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

Since
p>\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2d},p-q>\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4},q>0

and
s=o(\ln n)
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{-\epsilon sn}\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{N}J_{n_{i},t-s}.

From induction hypothesis we have
J_{m,t-s}\;\leqslant\;A^{m}

for
m<n
.
There are
\binom{n+N-1}{N-1}
terms
in the summation above.
Since
\binom{n+N-1}{N-1}\;\leqslant\;C^{n}
, we have

J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{-\epsilon sn}(NJ_{n,t-s}+C^{n}A^{n})\;\leqslant\;C^{n}e^{-\epsilon sn}(J_{n,t-s}+A^{n}).

Since
s\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,
we can find
n_{0}
such that

C^{n}e^{-\epsilon sn}<1/2
for all
n\;\geqslant\;n_{0}
.
In this way we obtain a recursive relation

J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{2}(J_{n,t-s}+A^{n}),\quad\forall t\in[s,\infty).

Since
J_{n,0}\;\leqslant\;1
by (
1.1
)
and
J_{n,t}
is non-decreasing in
t

by Proposition
6.4
,
we have
J_{n,t}\;\leqslant\;A^{n}
for all
t
.
Hence, (
4.2
) holds for
n
and
the theorem follows from induction.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})

be distinct and
\alpha\gamma<4
.
For any
p\in(\frac{\alpha\gamma}{4}\vee\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2d},1)

and any
\delta>1
,
there is some
C>0
such that

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\mathds{E}[e^{\mu L_{f,t}(S)}]\;\leqslant\;C\exp(C\mu^{\delta}\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla f(x)|^{\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}\delta}+C\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}),\quad\forall\mu\;\geqslant\;0.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2

and the following elementary inequality

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{n}}{n!}e^{pn\ln n}\;\leqslant\;C_{p}e^{C_{p}\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}},\quad\forall\mu\;\geqslant\;0

where
p\in(0,1)
and
C_{p}
is a constant.
Suppose that the left hand side is smaller than
2
for
\mu\in[0,\mu_{c})
.
By a change of variable
t\to\lambda t
, we have for
\lambda>0

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\lambda}e^{-t}\;\text{\rm d}t=\lambda^{\lambda+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(te^{-t})^{\lambda}\;\text{\rm d}t\;\geqslant\;\lambda^{\lambda+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\;\text{\rm d}t=e^{\lambda\ln\lambda-\lambda}.

Hence, for
n\;\geqslant\;0
and
\eta=e^{p-p\ln p}
we have

e^{pn\ln n}\;\leqslant\;\eta^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{pn}e^{-t}\;\text{\rm d}t.

Then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{n}}{n!}e^{pn\ln n}\;\leqslant\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{n}}{n!}\eta^{n}t^{pn}e^{-t}\;\text{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta\mu t^{p}-t}\;\text{\rm d}t.

By a change of variable
t\to\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}t
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta\mu t^{p}-t}\;\text{\rm d}t=\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}(\eta t^{p}-t)}\;\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;e^{C_{p}\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}}

for some
C_{p}>2
if
\mu\;\geqslant\;\mu_{c}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Tail estimates for balanced ratios

In this section, we prove the right tail estimates in the introduction.
Recall that the results are stated in two dimension and
S=[0,1]^{2}
.
As a preparation, we show the following lemma which translates bounds of the Laplace transform
to bounds of the right tail.
In the following denote
f\lesssim g
if there is some
C>0

such that
f(r)\;\leqslant\;Cg(r)
for
r
sufficiently large.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
Q
be a random variable.
Then for any
0<q<p<1
, we have

\ln\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}]\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln\mathds{P}(Q>x)\lesssim-x^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\mu^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\lesssim\ln\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}]\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln\mathds{P}(Q>x)\gtrsim-x^{\frac{1-q}{q(1-p)}}.

Proof.

By Chebyshev’s inequality, there exists
C>0
such that for
\mu
sufficiently large,

\ln\mathds{P}(Q>x)\;\leqslant\;\ln\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q-\mu x}]\;\leqslant\;C\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}}-\mu x.

Let
\mu=(2C)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}x^{\frac{1-p}{p}}
.
Then we have
\ln\mathds{P}(Q>x)\;\leqslant\;-C(2C)^{-\frac{1}{p}}x^{\frac{1}{p}}
for
x

sufficiently large.

For the second implication,
apply Paley-Zygmund inequality to
e^{\mu Q}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
:

\mathds{P}(e^{\mu Q}>\theta\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}])\;\geqslant\;\frac{(1-\theta)^{2}\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}]^{2}}{\mathds{E}[e^{2\mu Q}]}.

Since
\ln\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}]\gtrsim\mu^{\frac{1}{1-q}}
,
there exists
c>0
such that for
\mu
sufficiently large

\mathds{P}(e^{\mu Q}>\theta\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}])\;\leqslant\;\mathds{P}(Q>c\mu^{\frac{q}{1-q}}).

Hence

\ln\mathds{P}(Q>c\mu^{\frac{q}{1-q}})\;\geqslant\;2\ln(1-\theta)+2\ln\mathds{E}[e^{\mu Q}]-\ln\mathds{E}[e^{2\mu Q}]\gtrsim-\mu^{\frac{1}{1-p}},

which implies the lower bound for
\ln\mathds{P}(Q>x)
.
∎

6.
Appendix

6.1.
Kahane type inequalities

The monotonicity of GMC
in the sense of Kahane
[
11
]
does not hold for
balanced ratios.
Fortunately, weaker comparison principles remain available,
allowing us to control finite order moments
of the products of GMC.

We first recall some basic GMC calculations.
A function
F\colon(0,\infty)^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to have polynomial growth, if there exists

C,N>0
such that for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}>0

|F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|\;\leqslant\;C\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|x_{i}|^{N}+|x_{i}|^{-N}).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(\mu_{i})_{i\;\leqslant\;n}
be Radon measures
on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbf{A}(x)
and
\mathbf{B}(x)

be two independent continuous Gaussian fields
on
K
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For
t\in[0,1]
and
1\;\leqslant\;i\;\leqslant\;n
,
set
C_{i}(t,x)=\sqrt{1-t}A_{i}(x)+\sqrt{t}B_{i}(x)
.
Define

W_{i}(t,x)=e^{C_{i}(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}[C_{i}(t,x)^{2}]}\;,\quad M_{i}(t)=\int_{K}W_{i}(t,x)\;\mu_{i}(\text{\rm d}x).

Let
F\colon(0,\infty)^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function
whose derivatives up to order two have polynomial growth.
Then
\frac{\text{\rm d}}{\text{\rm d}t}\mathds{E}[F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))]

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\Big[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))\iint_{K\times K}g_{ij}(x,y)W_{i}(t,x)W_{j}(t,y)\;\mu_{i}(\text{\rm d}x)\mu_{j}(\text{\rm d}y)\Big]

where
g_{ij}(x,y)=\mathds{E}[B_{i}(x)B_{j}(y)]-\mathds{E}[A_{i}(x)A_{j}(y)]
.

Proof.

All the convergence issues in the computation below are standard results
of dominated convergence and Borell-TIS inequality
6.6
.
We leave the readers to check the details.
Using Leibniz rule we have

\mathds{E}[F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))]^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{E}[M_{i}(t)^{\prime}\partial_{i}F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))].

For
s\in[0,1]
, we have
by Girsanov transform

(6.1)

\mathds{E}[M_{i}(s)\partial_{i}F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))]=\int_{K}\mathds{E}[\partial_{i}F(M_{1}^{i,s,x}(t),\ldots,M_{n}^{i,s,x}(t))]\;\mu_{i}(\text{\rm d}x)

where

M_{j}^{i,s,x}(t)=\int_{K}e^{\mathds{E}[C_{i}(s,x)C_{j}(t,y)]}W_{j}(t,y)\;\mu_{j}(\text{\rm d}y).

Notice that
\partial_{s}|_{s=t}\mathds{E}[C_{i}(s,x)C_{j}(t,y)]=g_{ij}(x,y)/2
. Then

\partial_{s}|_{s=t}M_{j}^{i,s,x}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{K}g_{ij}(x,y)e^{\mathds{E}[C_{i}(t,x)C_{j}(t,y)]}W_{j}(t,y)\;\mu_{j}(\text{\rm d}y).

Differentiate both sides of (
6.1
)
with respect to
s
at
s=t
,
and sum the result over
i
.
Then
\mathds{E}[F(M_{1}(t),\ldots,M_{n}(t))]^{\prime}
becomes

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\iint g_{ij}(x,y)\mathds{E}\big[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}F(M_{1}^{i,t,x}(t),\ldots,M_{n}^{i,t,x}(t))e^{\mathds{E}[C_{i}(t,x)C_{j}(t,y)]}W_{j}(t,y)\big]\;\mu_{i}(\text{\rm d}x)\mu_{j}(\text{\rm d}y).

Applying Girsanov transform

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\big[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle F(M_{1}^{i,t,x}(t),\ldots,M_{n}^{i,t,x}(t))e^{\mathds{E}[C_{i}(t,x)C_{j}(t,y)]}W_{j}(t,y)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\big[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}F(M_{1}^{i,t,x}(t),\ldots,M_{n}^{i,t,x}(t))W_{i}(t,x)W_{j}(t,y)\big],

we obtain the desired result.
∎

As a special case we have the following
corollary.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
(p_{j})_{j\;\leqslant\;n}
and
(\gamma_{j})_{j\;\leqslant\;n}
be real parameters,
and let
(\mu_{j})_{j\;\leqslant\;n}
be Radon measures
on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose that
X
and
Y
are two independent continuous
Gaussian fields on
K
.
Define for
t\in[0,1]

\Phi(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\int_{K}e^{\gamma_{j}Z(t,x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}_{j}}{2}\mathds{E}[Z(t,x)^{2}]}\;\mu_{j}(\text{\rm d}x)\Big)^{p_{j}}

where
Z(t,x)=\sqrt{1-t}X(x)+\sqrt{t}Y(x)
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm d}}{\text{\rm d}t}\mathds{E}[\Phi(t)]=\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}\Big[

\displaystyle\Phi(t)\iint_{K\times K}\big(\mathds{E}[Y(x)Y(y)]-\mathds{E}[X(x)X(y)]\big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}p_{j}(p_{j}-1)\;\mu_{i,t}(\text{\rm d}x)\mu_{i,t}(\text{\rm d}y)+2\sum_{i<j}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}p_{i}p_{j}\;\mu_{i,t}(\text{\rm d}x)\mu_{j,t}(\text{\rm d}y)\Big)\Big]

where

are random probability
measures on

.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{A}(x)=(\gamma_{1}X(x),\ldots,\gamma_{n}X(x))
and

\mathbf{B}(x)=(\gamma_{1}Y(x),\ldots,\gamma_{n}Y(x))
.
The result follows by applying the above lemma
to
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{p_{n}}
.
∎

Now we give the statement and the proof of

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(p_{j})_{j\;\leqslant\;n}
and
(\gamma_{j})_{j\;\leqslant\;n}
be real parameters.
For any
A>0
, there exists a constant
C
such that the following holds:
Let
X
and
Y
be two continuous

K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

|\mathds{E}[X(x)X(y)]-\mathds{E}[Y(x)Y(y)]|\;\leqslant\;A,\quad\forall x,y\in K.

\mathds{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\int_{K}e^{\gamma_{j}X(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}_{j}}{2}\mathds{E}[X(x)^{2}]}\;\text{\rm d}\mu_{j}\Big)^{p_{j}}\right]\;\leqslant\;C\mathds{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\int_{K}e^{\gamma_{j}Y(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}_{j}}{2}\mathds{E}[Y(x)^{2}]}\;\text{\rm d}\mu_{j}\Big)^{p_{j}}\right].

Use the notation in Corollary
6.2
.

|\mathds{E}[\Phi(t)]^{\prime}|\;\leqslant\;C\mathds{E}[\Phi(t)]

.
Consequently
\mathds{E}[\Phi(0)]\;\leqslant\;e^{C}\mathds{E}[\Phi(1)]

which gives the desired inequality.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma
be distinct positive numbers.
Let
\lambda,\nu
be two Radon measures on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Define for a continuous Gaussian field
X
on
K

\mathcal{Q}_{X}=\frac{(\int_{K}e^{\alpha X(x)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathds{E}[X(x)^{2}]}\;\text{\rm d}\lambda)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}}}{(\int_{K}e^{\gamma X(x)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathds{E}[X(x)^{2}]}\;\text{\rm d}\nu)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}}.

Suppose that
X
and
Y
are two continuous Gaussian fields on
K
, such that

\mathds{E}[Y(x)Y(y)]-\mathds{E}[X(x)X(y)]

is positive definite.
Then for all non-decreasing convex
function
F\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\mathds{E}[F(\mathcal{Q}_{X})]\;\leqslant\;\mathds{E}[F(\mathcal{Q}_{Y})].

Proof.

Since
\mathds{E}[Y(x)Y(y)]-\mathds{E}[X(x)X(y)]
is positive definite,
we can couple
X,Y
such that
Y-X
is independent of
X
.
By Jensen’s inequality we have

\mathds{E}[F(\mathcal{Q}_{Y})|X]\;\geqslant\;F(\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Y}|X])
.
If we can prove
\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Y}|X]\;\geqslant\;\mathcal{Q}_{X}
,
then the result follows from the monotonicity of
F
.

By the independence of
Y-X
and
X
,
after absorbing
X
into
\lambda
and
\nu
,
it suffices to show
\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{0}]\;\leqslant\;\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Y-X}]
.
Let
Z_{t}=\sqrt{t}(Y-X)
.
From Corollary
6.2
there exists
random probability measures
\lambda_{t},\nu_{t}

such that

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm d}}{\text{\rm d}t}\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Z_{t}}]=\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2(\alpha-\gamma)^{2}}\mathds{E}\Big[\mathcal{Q}_{Z_{t}}\iint g(x,y)(\alpha\lambda_{t}(\text{\rm d}x)-\gamma\nu_{t}(\text{\rm d}x))(\alpha\lambda_{t}(\text{\rm d}y)-\gamma\nu_{t}(\text{\rm d}y))\Big].

Here
g(x,y)
is the covariance kernel of
Y-X
.
By the positive-definiteness of
g(x,y)
,

\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Z_{t}}]
is non-decreasing in
t
,
which implies
\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{0}]\;\leqslant\;\mathds{E}[\mathcal{Q}_{Y-X}]
.
∎

6.2.
Perturbation of covariance kernels

Proposition 6.5
.

For any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
compactly supported,
there exists a
C^{1}
Gaussian field
Z
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
such that
\mathds{E}[Z(x)Z(y)]+f(x,y)
is a covariance kernel.

Proof.

The following calculations are borrowed from Lemma 4.6 in
[
10
]
.
For
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the
Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\phi(x)\;\text{\rm d}x
and the
Sobolev norm for
s\in\mathbb{R}

\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\;\text{\rm d}\xi.

Let
R(x)
be a positive definite function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\widehat{R}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{d}{2}-2}
.
By Plancherel formula,
it holds for
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}R(x-y)\phi(x)\phi(y)\;\text{\rm d}x\text{\rm d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{R}(\xi)|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\;\text{\rm d}\xi=\|\phi\|_{H^{-d/2-2}}^{2}.

Since
\widehat{f}
is rapidly decreasing, Cauchy-Schwartz inequality implies that

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x,y)\phi(x)\phi(y)\;\text{\rm d}x\text{\rm d}y\right|=\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)\overline{\widehat{\phi}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\phi}}(\eta)\;\text{\rm d}\xi\text{\rm d}\eta\right|\;\leqslant\;A\|\phi\|_{H^{-d/2-2}}^{2}

for some constant
A
determined by
f
.
Since
R(x)
is
C^{3}
by definition, there exists a
C^{1}
Gaussian field
Z

such that
\mathds{E}[Z(x)Z(y)]=(A+1)R(x-y)
(see Section 1.4.2 in
[
1
]

for a discussion of regularity properties of Gaussian fields).
Since
\mathds{E}[Z(x)Z(y)]+f(x,y)
is nonnegative when
integrated against
\phi(x)\phi(y)\text{\rm d}x\text{\rm d}y
,
it is by definition positive definite.
∎

6.3.
Maximum of Gaussian process

We record here two inequalities about the maximum
of a Gaussian process.
The readers can consult
[
1
]
for a detailed treatment.
In the following,

(f(t))_{t\in T}
is a separable centered Gaussian process
on a topological space
T
(for instance, a continuous Gaussian field on a
compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).

Proposition 6.6

(Borell-TIS inequality)
.

Suppose that
(f(t))_{t\in T}
is almost surely bounded.
Then
\mathds{E}[\sup_{t\in T}f(t)]
is finite
and for all
u\;\geqslant\;0

and
\sigma_{T}^{2}=\sup_{t\in T}\mathds{E}[f(t)^{2}]
,

\mathds{P}(\sup_{t\in T}f(t)-\mathds{E}[\sup_{t\in T}f(t)]>u)\;\leqslant\;\exp(-u^{2}/2\sigma_{T}^{2}).

Proposition 6.7

(Dudley’s theorem)
.

Let
d
be the pseudometric on
T
defined by

d(t,s)=\sqrt{\mathds{E}[(f(t)-f(s))^{2}]}.

Suppose that
T
is
d
-compact and let
N(\epsilon)
be the smallest number
of
d
-balls of radius
\epsilon
needed to cover
T
.
Then there exists a universal constant
K
such that

\mathds{E}\big[\sup_{t\in T}f(t)\big]\;\leqslant\;K\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\ln N(\epsilon)}\;\text{\rm d}\epsilon.

6.4.
Estimates for the star-scale invariant fields

We show two propositions that are used in the proof of tail estimates.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
(X_{t}(x))_{t\;\geqslant\;0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be the
white noise decomposition in Section
2

and let
S=[0,1]^{d}
.
For each
\epsilon>0
,
there exists some
C>0
such that

\mathds{E}[(e^{-s}\sup_{x\in S}|\nabla X_{s}(x)|)^{m}]\;\leqslant\;C^{m}(e^{sm}+e^{\frac{1}{2}m\ln m}),\quad\forall s\;\geqslant\;0,m\;\geqslant\;\epsilon.

Proof.

Denote
\sup=\sup_{x\in S}
.
Then

\sup|\nabla X_{s}|\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sup|\partial_{j}X_{s}|\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\sup\partial_{j}X_{s})_{+}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(-\sup\partial_{j}X_{s})_{+}.

Note that
(-X_{s}(x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{law}}}{{=}}(X_{s}(x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and the law of
X_{s}
is rotationally invariant.
Let
f_{s}(x)=e^{-s}\partial_{1}X_{s}(x)
.
It suffices to find some constant
C
such that

(6.2)

\mathds{E}[(\sup f_{s}(x))_{+}^{m}]\;\leqslant\;C^{m}(e^{sm}+e^{\frac{1}{2}m\ln m}),\quad\forall s\;\geqslant\;0,m\;\geqslant\;\epsilon.

Let
h(x)=-\partial_{1}^{2}\rho(x)
.
By definition, the covariance kernel of
f_{s}
is given by

\mathds{E}[f_{s}(x)f_{s}(y)]=\int_{0}^{s}h(e^{u}(x-y))e^{2(u-s)}\;\text{\rm d}u.

Let
d_{s}
and
N_{s}(\epsilon)
denote respectively
the Dudley metric and the associated covering number of
S

induced by
f_{s}
(see Proposition
6.7
).
Since
h
is positive definite, there exists
A>0
such that

h(0)-h(x)\;\leqslant\;A^{2}|x|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle d_{s}(x,y)^{2}=2\int_{0}^{s}(h(0)-

\displaystyle h(e^{u}(x-y)))e^{2(u-s)}\;\text{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\int_{0}^{s}A^{2}e^{4u-2s}|x-y|^{2}\;\text{\rm d}u\;\leqslant\;A^{2}e^{2s}|x-y|^{2}.

Hence, each Euclidean ball of radius
A^{-1}e^{-s}\epsilon
is contained
in a
d_{s}
-ball of radius
\epsilon
.
Consequently

N_{s}(\epsilon)\;\leqslant\;\lceil Ae^{s}\epsilon^{-1}\rceil^{d}

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the least integer greater than or equal to
x
.
Dudley’s theorem
6.7
implies that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\sup f_{s}(x)]\;\leqslant\;K\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{d\ln\lceil Ae^{s}\epsilon^{-1}\rceil}\;\text{\rm d}\epsilon=K^{\prime}e^{s}

for some constant
K^{\prime}
.
Moreover, since

\sup\mathds{E}[f_{s}(x)^{2}]\;\leqslant\;h(0)/2

and Borell-TIS inequality
6.6
,
we have for some
\epsilon>0

\mathds{P}(\sup f_{s}(x)-\mathds{E}[\sup f_{s}(x)]\;\geqslant\;u)\;\leqslant\;e^{-\epsilon u^{2}},\quad\forall u\;\geqslant\;0,s\;\geqslant\;0.

Denote
Y=\sup f_{s}(x)
.
We have for
m\;\geqslant\;2
and some
C_{1}>0

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[Y_{+}^{m}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}mu^{m-1}\mathds{P}(Y\;\geqslant\;u)\;\text{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mathds{E}[Y]}mu^{m-1}\mathds{P}(Y\;\geqslant\;u)\;\text{\rm d}u+\int_{0}^{\infty}m(u+\mathds{E}[Y])^{m-1}\mathds{P}(Y\;\geqslant\;u+\mathds{E}[Y])\;\text{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{0}^{\mathds{E}[Y]}mu^{m-1}\;\text{\rm d}u+\int_{0}^{\infty}m(u+\mathds{E}[Y])^{m-1}e^{-\epsilon u^{2}}\;\text{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathds{E}[Y]^{m}+\int_{0}^{\infty}m2^{m-1}(u^{m-1}+\mathds{E}[Y]^{m-1})e^{-\epsilon u^{2}}\;\text{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{1}^{m}(e^{sm}+\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{m-1}e^{-\epsilon u^{2}}\;\text{\rm d}u).

By a change of variable
u=\sqrt{m-1}\;t
,
there is some
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{m-1}e^{-\epsilon u^{2}}\;\text{\rm d}u=(m-1)^{\frac{m}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(te^{-\epsilon t^{2}})^{m-1}\;\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;C_{2}^{m}m^{\frac{m}{2}}.

Above all, the following holds for some
C_{3}>0
:

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[(\sup f_{s}(x))_{+}^{m}]\;\leqslant\;C_{3}^{m}(e^{sm}+e^{\frac{1}{2}m\ln m}),\quad\forall s\;\geqslant\;0,m\;\geqslant\;2.

For
\epsilon\;\leqslant\;m<2
we use
Hölder’s inequality to deduce

\mathds{E}[(\sup f_{s}(x))_{+}^{m}]\;\leqslant\;\mathds{E}[(\sup f_{s}(x))_{+}^{2}]^{\frac{m}{2}}\;\leqslant\;C_{3}^{m}(e^{sm}+e^{\frac{m}{2}\ln 2})\;\leqslant\;C^{m}(e^{sm}+e^{\frac{1}{2}m\ln m})

where
C=\sqrt{2/\epsilon}\;C_{3}
.
Hence, (
6.2
) holds with
C
.
∎

The proposition below can be seen as a variant of
the small ball probability estimates.
The argument is adapted from
[
15
]
.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
(X_{t}(x))_{t\;\geqslant\;0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be the
white noise decomposition in Section
2

and let
S=[0,1]^{d}
.
There exists
C,c>0
such that

\mathds{P}(\sup_{x\in S}|X_{t}(x)|\;\leqslant\;1)\;\geqslant\;c\exp(-Ce^{dt}),\quad\forall t\;\geqslant\;0.

Proof.

Denote the Dudley metric by

d_{t}(x,y)=\sqrt{\mathds{E}[|X_{t}(x)-X_{t}(y)|^{2}]}
.
There exists
A>0
such that

\rho(0)-\rho(x)\;\leqslant\;A^{2}|x|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle d_{t}(x,y)^{2}=2\int_{0}^{t}\rho(0)-\rho(e^{u}(x-y))\;\text{\rm d}u\;\leqslant\;2\int_{0}^{t}A^{2}e^{2u}|x-y|^{2}\;\text{\rm d}u\;\leqslant\;A^{2}e^{2t}|x-y|^{2}.

For any
n\;\geqslant\;1
,
there exists a finite covering of
S
by
d_{t}
-balls of radius
2^{-2n}
.
Let
T_{n}
be the set of the centers.
Since
d_{t}(x,y)\;\leqslant\;Ae^{t}|x-y|
,
we can choose
T_{n}
such that
|T_{n}|\;\leqslant\;A_{1}e^{dt}2^{2dn}
for some universal constant
A_{1}>0
.
Define a map
p_{n}\colon S\to T_{n}
such that

d_{t}(x,p_{n}(x))\;\leqslant\;2^{-2n}

for all
x\in S
.
Assume
T_{0}=\{0\}
and
p_{0}(x)=0
.
Define for
n\;\geqslant\;1

\mathcal{Y}_{n}=\{X_{t}(x)-X_{t}(p_{n-1}(x)):x\in T_{n}\}.

Note that
\mathds{E}[Y^{2}]\;\leqslant\;A_{2}2^{-4n}
for all
Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}

and some universal constant
A_{2}
.
For each
x\in T_{n}
,
X_{t}(x)
can be written as

X_{t}(x)=X_{t}(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{k}

where
Y_{k}\in\mathcal{Y}_{k}

for
k=1,\ldots,n
.
Since
\bigcup_{n\;\geqslant\;1}T_{n}
is dense in
S

and
X_{t}
is continuous, we have

\{|X_{t}(0)|\;\leqslant\;2^{-1}\}\bigcap\bigcap_{n\;\geqslant\;1}\{|Y|\;\leqslant\;2^{-n-1},\ \forall Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}\}\subset\{\sup_{x\in S}|X_{t}(x)|\;\leqslant\;1\}.

By Gaussian correlation inequality
[
14
]
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(\sup_{x\in S}|X_{t}(x)|\;\leqslant\;1)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\mathds{P}(|X_{t}(0)|\;\leqslant\;2^{-1})\prod_{n\;\geqslant\;1}\prod_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}\mathds{P}(|Y|\;\leqslant\;2^{-n-1})

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\mathds{P}(|X_{t}(0)|\;\leqslant\;2^{-1})\prod_{n\;\geqslant\;1}\mathds{P}(|N|\;\leqslant\;A_{2}^{-1/2}2^{n-1})^{A_{1}e^{dt}2^{2dn}}

where
N
is a standard Gaussian variable.
Using the elementary inequality
1-x\;\geqslant\;e^{-2x}

when
x\in[0,1/2]
,
the product
M=\prod_{n\;\geqslant\;1}\mathds{P}(|N|\;\leqslant\;A_{2}^{-1/2}2^{n-1})^{A_{1}2^{2dn}}

is nonzero if the series

\sum_{n\;\geqslant\;1}2^{2dn}\mathds{P}(|N|>A_{2}^{-1/2}2^{n-1})

converges.
This can be shown via
the inequality

P(|N|>u)\;\leqslant\;e^{-u^{2}/2}
for
u\;\geqslant\;1
.
Let
C=-\ln M\in(0,\infty)
.
Since
\mathds{E}[X_{t}(0)^{2}]=t
, we have

\mathds{P}(\sup_{x\in S}|X_{t}(x)|\;\leqslant\;1)\;\geqslant\;\mathds{P}(|N|\;\leqslant\;(2\sqrt{t})^{-1})\exp(-Ce^{dt}).

This gives the desired lower bound.
∎

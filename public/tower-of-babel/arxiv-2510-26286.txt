Sharp embeddings and existence results for Logarithmic p p -Laplacian equations with critical growth
Rakesh Arora, Jacques Giacomoni, Hichem Hajaiej, Arshi Vaishnavi

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive a new p p -Logarithmic Sobolev inequality and optimal continuous and compact embeddings into Orlicz-type spaces of the function space associated with the logarithmic p p -Laplacian. As an application of these results, we study a class of Dirichlet boundary value problems involving the logarithmic p p -Laplacian and critical growth nonlinearities perturbed with superlinear-subcritical growth terms. By employing the method of the Nehari manifold, we prove the existence of a nontrivial weak solution. Lastly, we conduct an asymptotic analysis of a weighted nonlocal, nonlinear problem governed by the fractional p p -Laplacian with superlinear or sublinear type non-linearity, demonstrating the convergence of least energy solutions to a non-trivial, non-negative least energy solution of a Brezis-Nirenberg type or logistic-type problem, respectively, involving the logarithmic p p -Laplacian as the fractional parameter s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} . The findings in this work serve as a nonlinear analogue of the results reported in [ 1 , 4 , 39 ] , thereby extending their scope to a broader variational framework.

\int_{A}\varphi^{\ast}(|\ln|u_{n}|h(u_{n})|)~dx\leq\frac{|A|}{e(p-1)}+\int_{A}\varphi(|u_{n}|)~dx\leq C_{1}\ \text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.3)

This further gives
\{\ln|u_{n}|h(u_{n})v\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of uniformly integrable functions on
\Omega.
Finally, using (
4.1
) and Vitali convergence theorem, we get

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\ln|u_{n}|h(u_{n})v~dx=\int_{\Omega}\ln|u|h(u)v~dx.

Now, by the symmetry of the integrand in the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\cdot,\cdot),
and the fact that
u_{n}=0=v
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and (
4.1
), we have

\mathcal{F}_{p}(u_{n},v)=C_{N,p}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u_{n}(x)))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy

and

\frac{(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u_{n}(x)))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}\to\frac{(h(u(x)-u(y))-h(u(x)))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}\ \text{a.e. in}\ \Omega\times\Omega.

By the same arguments used in deriving (
3.1
) and Young’s inequality, we get

\begin{split}\bigg|&\frac{(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u_{n}(x)))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}\bigg|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq C(p)\begin{cases}|u_{n}(y)|^{p}+|v(x)|^{p}&\text{if}\ p\in(1,2),\\
|u_{n}(y)|^{p}+|u_{n}(x)|^{p}+|v(x)|^{p}&\text{if}\ p\geq 2.\end{cases}\end{split}

By (
4.1
) and applying Vitali convergence theorem, we get the claims in (ii).
Now, we proceed to prove (iii). By (
2.5
), we have

|g(x,u_{n})v|\leq a_{1}|u_{n}|^{p-1}|v|+\varepsilon|u_{n}|^{p-1}|\ln|u_{n}|||v|.

Thus, for any
\eta>0
, by choosing
\varepsilon
small enough in (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and using (
4.1
)-(
4.3
), there exists a
\delta>0
such that for all
A\subset\Omega
,
|A|<\delta
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{A}g(x,u_{n})v~dx\right|&\leq a_{1}\int_{A}|h(u_{n})||v|~dx+\varepsilon\int_{A}|h(u_{n})||\ln|u_{n}|||v|~dx\\
&\leq a_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(A)}^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p}(A)}+2\varepsilon\|\ln|u_{n}||h(u_{n})|\|_{L^{\varphi^{\ast}}(A)}\|v\|_{L^{\varphi}(A)}<\eta\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|\int_{A}G(x,u_{n})~dx\right|&\leq a_{2}\int_{A}|u_{n}|^{p}~dx+\varepsilon\int_{A}|u_{n}|^{p}|\ln|u_{n}||~dx\\
&\leq a_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(A)}^{p}+\varepsilon\left(\frac{|A|}{ep}+\int_{A}\varphi(|u_{n}|)~dx\right)<\eta.\end{split}

Finally, applying Vitali’s convergence theorem, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}g(x,u_{n})v~dx=\int_{\Omega}g(x,u)v~dx,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G(x,u_{n})~dx=\int_{\Omega}G(x,u)~dx.

This proves (iii).

4.1.
Properties of the energy functional

Recall that by (
2.4
),
J_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
is given by

J_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\left(\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,u)+\rho_{N}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}G(x,u)~dx+I(u),

where

I(u):=\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{F}_{p}(u,u)-\frac{\mu}{p^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}(\ln|u|^{p}-1)~dx.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g
satisfy (

g
1

)-(

g
3

). Then, the energy functional
J_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
defined in (
2.4
) is of class
C^{1}
in
X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
and
\text{for all}\ v\in X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)

(J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u),v)_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u,v)-\int_{\Omega}g(x,u)v~dx-\mu\int_{\Omega}\ln|u|h(u)v~dx.

(4.4)

Moreover,
J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u)\in\mathcal{L}(X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
for every
u\in X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
, where
\mathcal{L}(X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
is the set of all bounded linear functionals on
X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
u\in X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\frac{1}{p}\left(\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,u)+\mathcal{F}_{p}(u,u)+\rho_{N}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx\right)<\infty.

Furthermore, in view of (
2.6
) and Theorem
2.5
, we can conclude that
J_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
is well-defined. It is easy to see that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

are differentiable. Now, we check the differentiability of the map
u\mapsto I(u).

Let
u,v\in X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
and
\delta\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
.
By the mean value theorem, there exists a
\beta=\beta(x)\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}\Upsilon((u+\delta v)(x),(u+\delta v)(y),&(u+\delta v)(x),(u+\delta v)(y))-\Upsilon\left(u(x),u(y),u(x),u(y)\right)\\
&=p\delta\Upsilon((u+\delta\beta v)(x),(u+\beta\delta v)(y),v(x),v(y)),\end{split}

where
\Upsilon
is defined in (
2.1
). Now, using the symmetry of the integrand and
u=0=v
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{p}(u+\delta v,u+\delta v)-\mathcal{F}_{p}(u,u)\\
&\quad=\frac{p\delta C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\Upsilon((u+\delta\beta v)(x),(u+\beta\delta v)(y),v(x),v(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\quad=\frac{p\delta C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{h((u+\delta\beta v)(x)-(u+\beta\delta v)(y))v(x)-h((u+\delta\beta v)(x))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\quad:=\frac{p\delta C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\geq 1\end{subarray}}M_{1}(x,y)~dx~dy.\end{split}

Note that for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
|x-y|\geq 1
and using Lemma
A.1
, we have

|M_{1}(x,y)|\leq C(|(u+\delta\beta v)(y)|^{p-1}+|(u+\delta\beta v)(x)|^{p-1})|v(x)|\in L^{1}(\Omega\times\Omega)

for some
C>0.

Now, applying the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{p}(u+\delta v,u+\delta v)-\mathcal{F}_{p}(u,u)}{\delta}=p\mathcal{F}_{p}(u,v).

Let
\omega(t):=|t|^{p}(\ln|t|^{p}-1)
such that
\omega^{\prime}(t)=p^{2}|t|^{p-2}t\ln|t|.
By the mean value theorem, there exists a
\tau=\tau(x)\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}Q(x)&:=\frac{\omega(u(x)+\delta v(x))-\omega(u(x))}{\delta}\\
&=p^{2}|u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)|^{p-2}(u(x)+\delta\tau v(x))\ln|u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)|v(x).\end{split}

Note that if
|u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)|<1
, we have

|Q(x)|\leq\frac{p^{2}}{e(p-1)},

and if
|u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)|\geq 1
, by
[
41
, Section 2.2]
, we have

1\leq|u(x)+\tau\delta v(x)|\leq|u(x)|+|v(x)|\leq 2\max\{|u(x)|,|v(x)|\}

and

\begin{split}|Q(x)|&\leq p^{2}|v(x)||u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)|^{p-1}|\ln|u(x)+\delta\tau v(x)||\\
&\leq 4p^{2}\max\{1,2^{p-2}\}\bigg(\max\{|u(x)|,|v(x)|\}.\max\{|u(x)|^{p-1},|v(x)|^{p-1}\}.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad|\ln\left(2\max\{|u(x)|,|v(x)|\}\right)|\bigg)\\
&\leq 4p^{2}\max\{1,2^{p-2}\}\bigg(|u(x)|^{p}|\ln 2|u(x)||+|v(x)|^{p}|\ln 2|v(x)||\bigg)=:M_{2}(x).\end{split}

Now, using Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
2.8
(i), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}|\ln(2|u|)|~dx&\leq\ln 2\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}|\ln(|u|)|~dx\\
&\leq\ln 2\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\ln(|u|)~dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\{|u|<1\}}|u|^{p}(\ln|u|-|\ln(|u|)|)~dx\\
&\leq\frac{N}{p^{2}}\bigg(\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u,u)+\frac{p^{2}}{N}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\ln\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+(k_{0}+\ln 2)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\bigg)+\frac{2|\Omega|}{pe}<\infty.\end{split}

As a conclusion, we obtain

|Q(x)|\leq M_{2}(x)+\frac{p^{2}}{e(p-1)}\in L^{1}(\Omega).

Finally, applying the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we can conclude that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{F(u+\delta v)-F(u)}{\delta}=\mu\int_{\Omega}\ln|u|h(u)v~dx,

where

F(u):=\frac{\mu}{p^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}(\ln|u|^{p}-1)~dx.

Again, by the fundamental theorem of calculus, (
2.6
) and the preceding calculations, we can easily conclude that
u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}G(x,u)~dx
is differentiable.
Using the linearity of
J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u)
, Hölder inequality, Theorem
2.5
and Lemma
3.3
for
u,v,w\in X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
, it is easy to see that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}|(J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u),v+\delta w)_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}-(J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u),v)_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}|=0.

This implies
J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u)\in\mathcal{L}(X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega),\mathbb{R}).
To prove the
C^{1}
property, we show that if
u_{n}\to u
in
X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|(J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u_{n}),v)_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}-(J^{\prime}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u),v)_{X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)}|=0.

(4.5)

Note that

\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u_{n},v)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n},v)+\mathcal{F}_{p}(u_{n},v)+\rho_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(u_{n})v~dx.

Claim:

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n},v)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,v)
.
Note that

\begin{split}&|\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n},v)-\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,v)|\\
&\leq\frac{C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u(x)-u(y)))||(v(x)-v(y))|}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy.\end{split}

Now, by Lemmas
A.1
,
A.2
and Hölder inequality, we obtain,
if
p\in(1,2]
,

\begin{split}&\frac{C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u(x)-u(y)))||(v(x)-v(y))|}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\leq k_{1}(p)\frac{C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|((u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y))|^{p-1}|v(x)-v(y)|}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\leq k_{1}(p)\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n}-u,u_{n}-u)\mathcal{E}_{p}(v,v),\end{split}

where
k_{1}(p)=(3^{p-1}+2^{p-1})
and if
p>2
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{C_{N,p}}{2}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|(h(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))-h(u(x)-u(y)))||(v(x)-v(y))|}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&\leq k_{2}(p)\frac{C_{N,p}}{2}\bigg(\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)||(u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\\
&+\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\left|{x-y}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)||u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}|(u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y)|}{|x-y|^{N}}~dx~dy\bigg)\\
&\leq k_{2}(p)\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n}-u,u_{n}-u)\mathcal{E}_{p}(v,v)\left(\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,u)+1\right).\end{split}

where
k_{2}(p)=2^{p-2}(p-1).
Finally, using the above calculations and strong convergence of
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(\Omega)
, we get the claim above.

Collecting the claim and Lemma
4.2
(ii), we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u_{n},v)=\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u,v).

(4.6)

Finally, putting together the above estimates and Lemma
4.2
, we have proved (
4.5
).
∎

In this part, we consider the problem (

Δ

To find solutions, we minimize the energy functional

over

. Let

be defined as

\Phi(t)=J_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(tu)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u,u)-\int_{\Omega}G(x,tu)~dx-\frac{\mu t^{p}}{p^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}(\ln|tu|^{p}-1)~dx.

(4.7)

However, by (
2.6
) and choosing

\begin{split}\Phi(t)&\leq\frac{t^{p}}{p}\mathcal{E}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}(u,u)+a_{2}t^{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx+\varepsilon t^{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}|\ln|tu||~dx\\
&\qquad+\frac{\mu t^{p}}{p^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}~dx-\frac{\mu t^{p}}{p^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\ln|tu|^{p}~dx\\

\mathcal{N}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
. We begin by demonstrating that the energy functional

\mathcal{N}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{L_{\Delta_{p}}}
is away from origin in

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g

g
1

)-(

g
4

Lemma A.3
.

Let
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a continuous function. Then, we have

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{0}^{1}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr=\frac{h(0)}{p}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
\delta>0
such that

|h(r)-h(0)|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r<\delta.

Then, we have

\begin{split}s\int_{0}^{1}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr&=s\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr+s\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr\\
&\leq(h(0)+\varepsilon)\frac{\delta^{sp}}{p}+\frac{\|h\|_{L^{\infty}([\delta,1])}}{p}(1-\delta^{sp})\end{split}

and

\begin{split}s\int_{0}^{1}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr&\geq s\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr\geq(h(0)-\varepsilon)\frac{\delta^{sp}}{p}.\end{split}

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{h(0)}{p}(\delta^{sp}-1)-\varepsilon\frac{\delta^{sp}}{p}&\leq s\int_{0}^{1}r^{sp-1}h(r)~dr-\frac{h(0)}{p}\\
&\leq\frac{h(0)}{p}(\delta^{sp}-1)+\varepsilon\frac{\delta^{sp}}{p}+\frac{\|h\|_{L^{\infty}([\delta,1])}}{p}(1-\delta^{sp}).\end{split}

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, passing limits
s\to 0^{+}
, we obtain the required claim.

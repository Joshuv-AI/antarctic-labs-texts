Salivary Lactate Dehydrogenase and Matrix Metalloproteinase-8 as Predictive Biomarkers in Early Childhood Caries: A Cross-sectional Study

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly childhood caries (ECC) remains a prevalent and aggressive dental disease in preschool children, often progressing rapidly if not detected early. Traditional diagnostic methods are limited to visual and radiographic findings, which may identify lesions only after substantial demineralization. Salivary biomarkers such as lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and matrix metalloproteinase-8 (MMP-8) have emerged as promising noninvasive indicators of early pathological changes in the oral environment.AimTo evaluate salivary LDH and MMP-8 levels in preschool children with and without ECC and to assess their potential as predictive biomarkers.Materials and methodsA cross-sectional study was conducted on 80 children aged 3–6 years. Participants were divided into two groups: group I (caries-free, n = 40) and group II (with ECC, n = 40). Unstimulated saliva samples were collected and analyzed for LDH activity (spectrophotometric assay) and MMP-8 concentration [enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)]. Data were statistically analyzed using Student's t-test and Pearson's correlation.ResultsMean salivary LDH levels were significantly higher in the ECC group (365.2 ± 42.8 U/L) compared to the caries-free group (228.5 ± 38.6 U/L, p < 0.001). Similarly, salivary MMP-8 levels were elevated in children with ECC (42.6 ± 5.4 ng/mL) vs controls (25.3 ± 4.7 ng/mL, p < 0.001). Introduction

Early childhood caries (ECC) is defined as the presence of one or more decayed, missing, or filled tooth surfaces in any primary tooth of children under 6 years of age.1 It remains a major public health concern, with global prevalence ranging from 23 to 90%, depending on socioeconomic and geographic factors.2 ECC progresses rapidly due to the thin enamel of primary teeth, often leading to pain, infection, and compromised quality of life.3

Current diagnostic methods, such as visual-tactile examination and radiography, detect caries only after substantial mineral loss. Thus, there is growing interest in salivary biomarkers as noninvasive tools for early disease detection.

Salivary lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is a cytoplasmic enzyme released during tissue damage and inflammation, reflecting cellular turnover in oral tissues.4 Elevated salivary LDH has been associated with caries activity and periodontal disease.

Matrix metalloproteinase-8 (MMP-8), also known as neutrophil collagenase, plays a role in the degradation of type I collagen in dentin and the extracellular matrix. It is secreted by neutrophils and oral epithelial cells during inflammatory responses and has been implicated in the progression of carious lesions.5

Evaluating the combined role of LDH and MMP-8 may provide insights into their predictive value for ECC. Hence, this study aimed to assess salivary LDH and MMP-8 levels in preschool children with and without ECC and to determine their potential as predictive biomarkers.

Materials and Methods

A prospective cross-sectional study was conducted on 80 children aged 3–6 years at the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Sibar Institute of Dental Sciences, Guntur, Andhra Pradesh, India, after obtaining informed consent from their parents.

Group I (control): Caries-free children (n = 40).

Group II (test): Children with ECC (n = 40, dmft ≥2 according to World Health Organization [WHO] criteria).

Saliva Collection

Unstimulated whole saliva (2 mL) was collected in sterile tubes between 9 and 11 AM. Samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes and stored at −80°C.

Biochemical Analysis

LDH activity was measured using a spectrophotometric assay (U/L). MMP-8 concentration was determined by sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (ng/mL).

Discussion

The present cross-sectional study investigated the salivary levels of LDH and MMP-8 in preschool children with ECC compared to caries-free peers. Our findings demonstrated significantly elevated concentrations of both LDH and MMP-8 in children with ECC. These results reinforce the concept that saliva can serve as a noninvasive diagnostic medium, reflecting biochemical changes associated with caries progression.

Lactate Dehydrogenase and Its Role in Early Childhood Caries

Lactate dehydrogenase is a cytoplasmic enzyme involved in anaerobic glycolysis, and its release into saliva indicates cellular damage, necrosis, or increased tissue turnover.4 In the oral cavity, elevated LDH levels are considered markers of epithelial cell lysis and inflammatory activity.6 The present study observed nearly 60% higher LDH activity in the ECC group compared to controls, supporting earlier reports7 who identified LDH as a marker of oral disease activity in children. The increased LDH may be attributed to accelerated metabolic activity of cariogenic microorganisms, localized inflammation of dental pulp or gingival tissues, and higher epithelial shedding rates in caries-active individuals.8

Matrix metalloproteinase-8 and Collagen Breakdown

Matrix metalloproteinase-8, or neutrophil collagenase, is primarily secreted by neutrophils and oral epithelial cells and plays a central role in degrading type I collagen, a major component of dentin and the periodontal extracellular matrix.5 In the current study, salivary MMP-8 levels were significantly elevated in children with ECC. These findings are consistent with the work9 who reported higher salivary MMP-8 activity in children with active dentin caries compared to healthy controls. The elevated levels can be explained by the activation of host-derived enzymes within the carious lesion environment, where acidic pH conditions promote collagen degradation independent of bacterial proteases.10 This process contributes to the irreversible breakdown of the dentin collagen matrix and accelerates lesion progression.

Correlation between Lactate Dehydrogenase and Matrix metalloproteinase-8

The positive correlation observed between LDH and MMP-8 in ECC cases (r = 0.62) indicates a synergistic interplay between tissue breakdown (LDH release) and extracellular matrix degradation (MMP-8 activity). Such findings suggest that ECC pathogenesis involves not only microbial activity but also host-derived proteolytic mechanisms. Similar associations have been described in periodontal diseases, where LDH and MMP-8 act as combined indicators of inflammation and connective tissue destruction.11,12 Thus, these biomarkers may reflect a broader host response to cariogenic challenge, linking soft tissue damage with dentin matrix breakdown.

Clinical Implications

Early detection of ECC remains challenging due to its rapid progression and the limitations of traditional diagnostic tools. The identification of reliable salivary biomarkers, such as LDH and MMP-8, offers an opportunity for chairside, noninvasive risk assessment. Previous studies have emphasized that biomarker-based diagnostics could identify precavitated lesions and predict susceptibility before clinical manifestation.13 The findings of the present study provide further evidence that integrating salivary diagnostics into pediatric dental practice may improve preventive strategies, particularly in high-risk populations.

Limitations and Future Perspectives

Despite promising results, this study has limitations. First, its cross-sectional nature restricts conclusions regarding causality. Longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether LDH and MMP-8 elevations precede lesion development or merely reflect existing caries activity. Second, the modest sample size and single-center recruitment may limit generalizability. Third, salivary biomarker levels can be influenced by systemic conditions, diet, circadian rhythms, and oral hygiene practices.14

Future research should focus on large-scale, multicenter longitudinal studies to validate the predictive capacity of these biomarkers. Development of multiplex point-of-care assays incorporating LDH, MMP-8, and other salivary proteins such as C-reactive protein, immunoglobulins, or cytokines may yield more robust diagnostic panels.15 Additionally, interventional studies assessing biomarker fluctuations before and after preventive or restorative treatment could further establish their utility in monitoring therapeutic outcomes.

Conclusion

Our study highlights the significant association of salivary LDH and MMP-8 with ECC, underscoring their potential as predictive biomarkers. These findings align with growing evidence that saliva-based diagnostics can provide a noninvasive, rapid, and child-friendly approach to ECC risk assessment, ultimately supporting preventive pediatric dentistry.

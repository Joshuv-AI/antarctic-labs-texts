Normal invariant of nearby Lagrangians via twisted derivative
M. Abouzaid and D. Álvarez-Gavela and S. Courte and T. Kragh

Abstract
. Let L L and M M be closed, connected, smooth manifolds and let L ↪ T ∗ ​ M L\hookrightarrow T^{*}M be an exact Lagrangian embedding. The induced map L → M L\to M is known by earlier work to be a homotopy equivalence. We show that the associated normal invariant M → G / O M\to G/O factors through a map B ⁡ ( 𝒯 , 𝒬 ) → G / O B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to G/O which is a twisted version of the Waldhausen derivative 𝒯 → G \mathcal{T}\to G on the space 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of tubes. Further, we show that this twisted derivative map itself factors though a map B ⁡ ( G / O ) → G / O B(G/O)\to G/O which is a twisted version of the S S -duality map B ​ G → G BG\to G . In particular we deduce that the normal invariant of the homotopy equivalence L → M L\to M is 2-torsion.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

Let
L
and
M
be closed connected smooth manifolds and let
L\hookrightarrow T^{*}M
be an exact Lagrangian embedding.
We call
L
a
nearby Lagrangian
for short. The well-known
nearby Lagrangian conjecture

predicts that
L
is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero-section, and hence that the projection map

\pi\colon L\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism.

We know from previous work of the first and last author
that
\pi
is a simple homotopy equivalence. Hence it represents an element of the

simple structure set

\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
considered in surgery theory, namely
the set of such pairs
(L,\pi)
modulo s-cobordism. In view of the s-cobordism theorem, when
\dim M\geq 5
we have that
\pi\colon L\to M
is homotopic to a diffeomorphism if and only if
the class of
(L,\pi)
in
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
agrees with the class of
(M,\id)
.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
the subset of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
consisting of the classes in
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
that admit representatives
(L,\pi)

where
L
is a closed manifold and
\pi=\pi_{M}\circ\psi
is the composition of the projection map
\pi_{M}\colon T^{*}M\to M
with an exact Lagrangian embedding
\psi\colon L\to T^{*}M
.

If the nearby Lagrangian conjecture holds, then it follows in particular that
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
is a singleton consisting of the base point
(M,\id)
. As a step towards the conjecture one may therefore try to show that certain elements of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
cannot arise from nearby Lagrangians.

We observe at this point that with respect to the hard/soft dichotomy in symplectic topology (see Section
5.1
), there are already soft obstructions for an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
. The most obvious soft obstruction is that
TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
and
\pi^{*}TM\otimes\mathbf{C}
should be isomorphic as complex vector bundles, see Section
5
for further discussion. The focus of the present article is on establishing hard obstructions.

The first hard obstructions on
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
where obtained by the first author
[
1
]
in the case where
M=S^{4k+1}

and then extended to the case where
M
is any odd-dimensional sphere by Ekholm-Kragh-Smith
[
11
,
13
]
. The combination of these results gives the following: for
k\geq 1
, if
(L,\pi)\in\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(S^{4k+1})
, then
L
must bound a parallelizable manifold, and more generally for
k\geq 2
if
M
is a
(2k+1)
-dimensional homotopy sphere and
(L,\pi)\in\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
, then
L=\pm M
in
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
. Here
\Theta_{n}
is the group of oriented homotopy spheres up to
h
-cobordism (same as orientation-preserving diffeomorphism in dimension
\geq 5
) with the operation of connected sum and
bP_{n+1}\subset\Theta_{n}
is the subgroup consisting of those spheres that bound a parallelizable manifold.

The condition of bounding a parallelizable manifold can be generalized to all manifolds
M
as follows. A first step in surgery theory is to assign to each element of the simple structure set a map
M\to G/O
(defined up to homotopy), called
the
normal invariant
, where
G/O
is the homotopy fiber of the de-looped
J
-homomorphism
BO\to BG
, which associates to a vector bundle the corresponding spherical fibration (
G
stands for the monoid of stable self-homotopy equivalences of spheres). This gives rise to a (pointed) map

\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)\to[M,G/O].

(1)

An element of
[M,G/O]
can be thought of as a vector bundle over
M
together with a trivialization
of its underlying spherical fibration, up to the appropriate equivalence relation (see Section
2
). With respect to the operation of direct sum, the map
BO\to BG
is a map of monoids and indeed of infinite loop spaces, so the homotopy fiber
G/O
inherits an infinite loop space structure and in particular the set of normal invariants
[M,G/O]
is an abelian group. When
M=S^{n}
the normal invariant of
L\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
is trivial if and only if
L
bounds a parallelizable manifold, and the group structure on the set of normal invariants coincides with that of
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
.

In this article we give an obstruction for realizing a class in
[M,G/O]
as the normal invariant of a nearby Lagrangian, which yields new constraints on
\mathcal{S}_{\text{NL}}^{s}(M)
. The key ingredient is Waldhausen’s space of tubes
[
28
]
, which heuristically is the space of codimension zero solid tori in Euclidean space, stabilised with respect to two operations which both raise the dimension of the ambient space, one of which keeps the dimension of the core sphere constant, while the other raises it by one.

We will consider a specific model
\mathcal{T}
for this space, consisting of quadratic functions of tube type as defined in Section
3.6
. Our model has a natural monoid structure and contains the submonoid
\mathcal{Q}
of nondegenerate quadratic forms, which serves as our model for
BO
. The space
\mathcal{T}
classifies generating functions of tube type and the (geometric realisation of the) bar construction
B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})
classifies twisted generating functions of tube type, as introduced in
[
4
]
. We use
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}
, the space of topological tubes, as our model for
BG
, and use
B(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}},\mathcal{Q})
as our model for
B(G/O)
. We briefly introduce the key maps in the story.

(1)

The
classifying map

M\to B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})
associated to a nearby Lagrangian
L\to T^{*}M
via the existence theorem for twisted generating functions of tube type for nearby Lagrangians as constructed in
[
4
]
.

(2)

The
twisted forgetful map

B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to B(G/O)
, which is a twisted version of the forgetful map
\mathcal{T}\to BG
, or rather
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}
, for our model
B(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}},\mathcal{Q})
of
B(G/O)
.

(3)

The
twisted duality map

B(G/O)\to G/O
, which is a twisted version of the
S
-duality map
BG\to G
considered by Bökstedt and Waldhausen in
[
10
]
which we realize as a monoid map
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}\to G
and its twisted version
B(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}},\mathcal{Q})\to G/O
.

(4)

The
twisted derivative

B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to G/O
, which is a twisted version of the Waldhausen derivative
\mathcal{T}\to G
, which factors as the composition of the maps
\mathcal{T}\to BG
and
BG\to G
in items (2) and (3) above
[
28
,
10
]
.

Our main results are then stated as follows, where equality of maps is understood to mean up to homotopy.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold and
L
a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
.
The normal invariant
M\to G/O
of the projection
\pi\colon L\to M
factors as a composition

M\to B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to G/O

of the classifying map and the twisted derivative.

Theorem 2
.

The twisted derivative factors as a composition

B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to B(G/O)\to G/O

of the twisted forgetful map and the twisted duality map.

Theorem 3
.

The twisted duality map
B(G/O)\to G/O
is 2-torsion.

As an immediate application we obtain:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold and
L
a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
.
The normal invariant
M\to G/O
of the projection
\pi\colon L\to M
is a 2-torsion element in the group
[M,G/O]
.

Proof.

From Theorems
1
and
2
we obtain a factoring of the normal invariant of
L\to M
as the composition

M\to B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to B(G/O)\to G/O

The fact that it is 2-torsion then follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

Example 1.3
.

When
L
and
M
are homotopy spheres it follows that
L\#L=M\#M
in
\theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
. If
n
is even then
bP_{n+1}=0
and so we conclude that
L\#L
and
M\#M
are in fact diffeomorphic. In particular, if
n
is even and
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{n}
is a Lagrangian homotopy sphere, then it follows that
\Sigma\#\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{n}
.

Example 1.4
.

The factorization through
B(G/O)
gives additional constraints on the normal invariant beyond the fact that it is
2
-torsion. For example,
\theta_{8}\simeq\theta_{8}/{bP}_{9}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/2
so the fact that the normal invariant of a Lagrangian homotopy sphere
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{8}
is 2-torsion gives no new information. However, since
\pi_{7}G/O=0
it follows from our results that any exact Lagrangian homotopy sphere in
T^{*}S^{8}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{8}
, i.e. the unique exotic possibility is excluded.

In fact, for homotopy spheres and more generally for any product of homotopy spheres, we may conclude a stronger result, whose statement will require us to recall some background about the
\eta
map, which is the map between iterated loop spaces that is induced by the Hopf map.

If
X
is a 3-fold loopspace
X=\Omega^{3}Y
we may define a map
\Omega^{2}Y\to\Omega^{3}Y
by pre-composition with the Hopf fibration
\eta:S^{3}\to S^{2}
. This can also be thought of as a map
BX\to X
and is referred to as an
\eta
-map. For example
G
is an infinite loopspace with the operation of direct sum (or rather, smash product of maps), and we have an associated
\eta
-map
BG\to G
.

Waldhausen explained in
[
28
]
that the derivative
\mathcal{T}\to G
factors as the composition of the forgetful map
\mathcal{T}\to BG
and a geometrically defined map
BG\to G
. In
[
10
]
Bökstedt and Waldhausen identified this map
BG\to G
with an
S
-duality map
BG\to G
, and then further identified the
S
-duality map with the
\eta
-map
BG\to G
. Hence the derivative
\mathcal{T}\to G
factors through the
\eta
-map
BG\to G
.

Now,
G/O
inherits an infinite loop space structure as the homotopy fiber of the
J
-homomorphism
BO\to BG
, which is an infinite loop map under the operation of direct sum, and so there also is an
\eta
-map
B(G/O)\to G/O
. We do not prove in the present article that the twisted duality map
B(G/O)\to G/O
which appears in Theorems
2
and
3
is an
\eta
-map, though this is our expectation. To identify the twisted duality map as an
\eta
-map one would need to keep track of additional structure beyond the monoid structures we consider in this article.

For nearby Lagrangians with stably trivial Gauss map the situation is simpler. As explained in
[
4
]
, any nearby Lagrangian
L\subset T^{*}M
with stably trivial Gauss map admits a genuine (i.e. untwisted) generating function of tube type, which is classified by a map
M\to\mathcal{T}
. The composition
M\to U/O\to BO
is trivial, so the normal invariant lifts to a map
M\to G
, and it follows from our discussion below that this map factors as the composition of the classifying map
M\to\mathcal{T}
and the derivative
\mathcal{T}\to G
. Therefore, from the Bökstedt-Waldhausen factoring of the derivative as the composition
\mathcal{T}\to BG\to G
with
BG\to G
the
\eta
-map, we may directly conclude the following result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold and
L
a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
. Assume that the stable Gauss map
L\to U/O
is trivial. Then the normal invariant
M\to G/O
of the projection
\pi:L\to M
factors through the
\eta
-map
B(G/O)\to G/O
.

It was shown in
[
4
]
that the stable Gauss map of a nearby Lagrangian
L\to U/O
is trivial on homotopy groups, hence nullhomotopic when
M
(and therefore
L
) has the homotopy type of a sphere. So Theorem
4
applies at least in the case of homotopy spheres. The conclusion may be phrased in more classical terms as follows. We note that this statement was also obtained by Smith and Porcelli in
[
25
]
by combining the results of
[
4
]
with Floer homotopy theory.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
M
be a homotopy sphere and
L
a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
. The difference
M-L
in
\theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}\subset\text{coker}(J_{n})
is in the image of the composition

\pi^{s}_{n-1}\xrightarrow{\eta}\pi^{s}_{n}\to\text{coker}(J_{n})

where the first map is multiplication by the Hopf class
\eta\in\pi^{s}_{1}
and the second map is the quotient by the image of the
J
-homomorphism
J_{n}:\pi_{n}O\to\pi_{n}^{s}
.

By using known results on the multiplicative structure of the stable homotopy groups of spheres one can use the above consequence to find even stronger restrictions on the possible smooth structures of nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres.

Remark 1.6
.

In Section

we will also explain how one may obtain concrete applications of our results beyond the case of homotopy spheres, illustrating the general principle with the specific examples of products of spheres, tori, and complex projective spaces.

Remark 1.7
.

The next step in surgery theory (the definition of surgery obstructions in odd and even dimensions)
extends the normal invariant map to an exact sequence of pointed sets involving the simple L-groups associated to
\pi_{1}M
:

L^{s}_{n+1}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}M])\to\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)\to[M,G/O]\to L^{s}_{n}(\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}M]).

This means for instance that if we know that

and the bordism can be surgered to an s-cobordism precisely

and make them topological monoids under direct sum, so that the inclusion
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathcal{G}
is an inclusion of monoids and in particular
\mathcal{O}
acts on
\mathcal{G}
by using the direct sum. Given an
\mathcal{O}
-twisted map
(g_{i},o_{ij})
from
M
to
\mathcal{G}
we may define an
O
-twisted map
(g_{i}^{\prime},o_{ij}^{\prime})
from
M
to
G
by

\displaystyle g_{i}^{\prime}=g_{i}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad o_{ij}^{\prime}=\id_{n_{i}}\times o_{ij}.

This in fact defines a simplicial map

\displaystyle c:B_{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})\to B_{\bullet}(G,O)

(4)

defined by mapping a
p
-simplex
(g,o_{1},\dots,o_{p})
with
g\in G_{m}
,
o_{i}\in O_{n_{i}}
to

(g,\id_{m}\times o_{1},\dots,\id_{m}\times\id_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\id_{n_{p-1}}\times o_{p}).

Assume that each
g_{i}
lands in
G_{n_{i}}
for some
n_{i}\in\mathbf{N}

and put
N=\max n_{i}
. Then
g^{\prime}_{i}\in O_{N}
and
o^{\prime}_{ij}\in O_{N}
and as above
we obtain a vector bundle with a spherical trivialization
(E,\theta)

associated to
(g^{\prime}_{i},o^{\prime}_{ij})
.

The map
c
is in fact a homotopy equivalence, but we will not need this.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(g_{i},o_{ij})
be an
\mathcal{O}
-twisted map from
M
to
\mathcal{G}
. Then
(-g_{i},-o_{ij})
represents
the same element of
[M,B(G,O)]
under the map
c:B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})\to B(G,O)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the vector bundles with spherical trivialization
(E,\theta)
and
(E^{-},\theta^{-})

obtained from the above construction applied to
(g_{i},o_{ij})
and
(-g_{i},-o_{ij})
respectively
are stably equivalent. With the above notations, we observe that the maps

\Phi_{i}:=\id_{M_{i}}\times\id_{n_{i}}\times(-\id_{N-n_{i}}):M_{i}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to M_{i}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}

satisfy for
x\in M_{ij}
fixed and
(v,u,w)\in\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathbf{R}^{n_{ij}}\times\mathbf{R}^{N-n_{j}}
that

(\text{id}_{n_{i}}\times(-o_{ij})\times\text{id}_{N-n_{j}})\circ\Phi_{i}(v,u,w)=(\text{id}_{n_{i}}\times(-o_{ij})\times\text{id}_{N-n_{j}})(v,-u-w)

=(v,o_{ij}u,-w)=\Phi_{j}(v,o_{ij}u,w)=\Phi_{j}\circ(\text{id}_{n_{i}}\times o_{ij}\times\text{id}_{N-j})(v,u,w)

So we have a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{i}\cap M_{j}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{i}\cap M_{j}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{i}\cap M_{j}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{i}\cap M_{j}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{id}_{n_{i}}\times o_{ij}\times\id_{n_{j}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{id}_{n_{i}}\times(-o_{ij})\times\id_{n_{j}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{j}}

and hence an isomorphism of vector bundles
\Phi:E\to E^{-}
. Further, we have the equality
\theta^{-}\circ\Phi=-\theta
,
so
(E,-\theta)\sim(E^{-},\theta^{-})
. If
N
is even then multiplication by
-1
is homotopic to the identity map in
O_{N}
and thus

(E,\theta)\sim(E,-\theta)
. If
N
is odd, then note that
(E,-\theta)\sim(E\oplus\mathbf{R},(-\theta)\times\id_{1})
by stabilization and

(E\oplus\mathbf{R},(-\theta)\times\id_{1})\sim(E\oplus\mathbf{R},-(\theta\times\id_{1}))
by the global isomorphism
\id_{E}\oplus(-\id_{1}):E\oplus\mathbf{R}\to E\oplus\mathbf{R}
. For
N+1
even multiplication by
-1
is homotopic to the identity map in
O_{N+1}
and thus we also deduce that
(E,\theta)\sim(E,-\theta)
as desired.
∎

Given two pairs
(E,\theta)
and
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
over
M
as above we get another one by direct (Whitney) sum

(E\oplus E^{\prime},\theta\wedge\theta^{\prime})
. This endows
[M,B(G,O)]
with a monoid structure,
which is in fact an abelian group. At the level
N\geq 0
this is represented by the direct sum (and smash)

\displaystyle B(G_{N},O_{N})\to B(G_{2N},O_{2N}).

To describe this using the monoids
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{G}
one has to be a little careful with reordering coordinates. It will be useful for us to have a specific representative for twice a given vector bundle with spherical trivialization. So, let

\displaystyle A_{k}:\mathbf{R}^{k}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\to\mathbf{R}^{2k}

be given by

A_{k}(x,y)=(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2},\dots,x_{k},y_{k}).

Then we may define a (doubling) monoid map
\delta:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
by the map

\displaystyle\delta(g)=A_{k}\circ(g\oplus-g)\circ A_{k}^{-1}

for
g\in G_{k}
. The fact that
A_{k_{1}}(x,y)\oplus A_{k_{2}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=A_{k_{1}+k_{2}}((x,x^{\prime}),(y,y^{\prime}))
implies that this is indeed a monoid map, as one checks that for
g_{1}\in G_{n_{1}}
and
g_{2}\in G_{n_{2}}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(g_{1}\oplus g_{2})

\displaystyle=A_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\circ(g_{1}\oplus g_{2}\oplus-g_{1}\oplus-g_{2})\circ A_{n_{1}+n_{2}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A_{n_{1}}\times A_{n_{2}})\circ(g_{1}\oplus-g_{1}\oplus g_{2}\oplus-g_{2})\circ(A_{n_{1}}\times A_{n_{2}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\delta_{n_{1}}(g_{1})\oplus\delta_{n_{2}}(g_{2}).

This preserves the submonoid
\mathcal{O}
, and thus induces a simplicial map

\displaystyle\delta:B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})\to B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O}).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(g_{i},o_{ij})
be an
\mathcal{O}
-twisted map from
M
to
\mathcal{G}
representing

\alpha\in[M,B(G,O)]
, then
(\delta(g_{i}),\delta(o_{ij}))
represent
2\alpha\in[M,B(G,O)]
.

Proof.

By definition of the group structure on stable isomorphism classes of vector bundles with spherical trivializations, twice
\alpha
is represented by
(g^{\prime}_{i}\oplus g^{\prime}_{i},o^{\prime}_{ij}\oplus o^{\prime}_{ij})
. Lemma
2.5
shows that the vector bundle with spherical trivialization
(E,\theta)

associated to
(g^{\prime}_{i}\oplus(-g_{i})^{\prime},o^{\prime}_{ij}\oplus(-o_{ij})^{\prime})
also represents
2\alpha
, where
(g^{\prime}_{i},o^{\prime}_{ij})=c(g_{i},o_{ij})
and
((-g_{i})^{\prime},(-o_{ij})^{\prime})=c(-g_{i},-o_{ij})
.

Now, we have

c(\delta(g_{i}),\delta(o_{ij}))=(A_{N}\circ(g^{\prime}_{i}\oplus(-g_{i})^{\prime})\circ A_{N}^{-1},A_{N}\circ(o^{\prime}_{ij}\oplus(-o_{ij})^{\prime})\circ A_{N}^{-1})

Let
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
be the corresponding vector bundle with spherical trivialization. Then
A_{N}
gives an isomorphism between
(E,A_{N}\circ\theta)

and
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
. If
N
is such that
A_{N}
is homotopic to the identity we conclude that
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\sim(E,\theta)
as desired. Otherwise
A_{N}\times(-\id_{1})

is homotopic to the identity map and we conclude as in the proof of Lemma
2.5
.∎

2.3.
The normal invariant of a homotopy equivalence

Let
L
and
M
be closed, connected, smooth manifolds of the same dimension which are homotopy equivalent.

Definition 2.7
.

The
normal invariant
of a homotopy equivalence
\pi:L\to M
is the element of
[M,B(G,O)]
which is represented
by a pair
(E,\theta)
where:

(1)

E\to M
is a rank
n
vector bundle (the integer
n
is independent of the dimension of
L
),

(2)

\theta:J(E)\to M\times S^{n}
is a fibered map which is smooth near
\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})\subset E

and transverse to
M\times\{0\}
, and such that there exists a diffeomorphism
L\to\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})
lifting
\pi

(up to homotopy) under the projection
\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})\to M
.

Remark 2.8
.

Since
\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})\to M
is a homotopy equivalence, for a regular value
x
of

\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})\to M
, the map
\theta_{x}:J(E)_{x}\to S^{n}

has degree
\pm 1
and therefore each
\theta_{x}
has degree
\pm 1
. Hence
\theta
is a fibered homotopy
equivalence and
(E,\theta)
indeed determines an element of
[M,B(G,O)]
.

The following result seems to be well-known, but we are including a proof as we have not been able to identify a reference:

Lemma 2.9
.

The normal invariant of
\pi
is well-defined, in the sense that the datum
(E,\theta)
exists and is unique up to equivalence.

Proof.

To prove existence, we pick, for
k
large enough, an embedding
j:L\to M\times\mathbf{R}^{k}
lifting
\pi
(up to homotopy)
and denote by
\nu(j)
its normal bundle. Since
\pi
is a homotopy equivalence, there exists a vector bundle
F
of rank
n-k
over
M

and a trivialization

\pi^{*}F\oplus\nu(j)\simeq L\times\mathbf{R}^{n}.

Let
E=F\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{k}
, the direct sum of
F
and a trivial rank
k
vector bundle on
M
. The embedding
j^{\prime}:L\to E
given by
j^{\prime}(x)=(0,j(x))
has trivial normal bundle by construction, so the Pontryagin-Thom construction gives the desired map

\theta:J(E)\to M\times S^{n}
.

For uniqueness, we observe that since
L\simeq\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})
has trivial normal bundle in
E
, we have

TL\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n}\simeq\pi^{*}E\oplus\pi^{*}TM,

and since
\pi
is a homotopy equivalence this relation determines
E
up to stable vector bundle isomorphism.
Next, by general position, the space of embeddings
L\to E
lifting
\pi
is non-empty and connected provided
the rank of
E
is sufficiently large. So given any two pairs
(E,\theta)
and
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
as in
Definition
2.7
we can assume (up to equivalence) that
E=E^{\prime}
and
\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})=(\theta^{\prime})^{-1}(M\times\{0\})
.

The maps
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
induce two trivializations of the normal bundle of
L=\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})
in
E
, which thus
differ by a map
B:L\to O_{n}
. Now
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
are homotopic in the space of fibered maps transverse to
M\times\{0\}
with
given zero locus if and only if
B
is homotopic to the constant map to the identity
\id\in O_{n}
. The latter condition can be achieved by stabilization:
Replace
(E,\theta)
by
(E\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\theta\oplus\id)
and
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
by
(E^{\prime}\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\theta^{\prime}\oplus\id)
. For
s:M\to L

a homotopy inverse of
\pi
, the pairs
(E\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\theta\oplus\id)
and
(E\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\theta\oplus(B^{-1}\circ s))
are related by the bundle isomorphism
\id\oplus(B\circ s):E\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n}\to E\oplus\underline{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
, hence are equivalent. So after these stabilizations,
the two trivializations of the normal bundle of
L
differ by
B\oplus(B^{-1}\circ(s\circ\pi))\simeq B\oplus B^{-1}
which is
homotopic to the constant automorphism
\id\in O_{2n}
. Hence
(E,\theta)
and
(E^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
are stably equivalent.
∎

Let
(g_{i},o_{ij})
be an
\mathcal{O}
-twisted map from
M
to
\mathcal{G}
defined on a finite
totally ordered open cover
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
of
M
. If
g_{i}\colon M_{i}\times\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}\to\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}
is smooth near
g_{i}^{-1}(0)
and is transverse to the origin, then the zero-sets
g_{i}^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}
glue together as an abstract manifold. Indeed on
M_{ij}
there is a canonical diffeomorphism
x\mapsto(x,0)
between
g_{i}^{-1}(0)
and
g_{j}^{-1}(0)=(g_{i}\oplus o_{ij})^{-1}(0)
. If
M
is closed, then the union
\bigcup_{i}g_{i}^{-1}(0)
is then a closed (smooth) manifold over
M
. Assuming that
L
is a closed manifold equipped with a projection map
\pi
to
M
, and with a diffeomorphism to
\bigcup_{i}g_{i}^{-1}(0)
through which this projection factors, we conclude:

Proposition 2.10
.

The element of
[M,B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})]
classifying
(g_{i},o_{ij})
represents the normal invariant
of
\pi
under the map
c:B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})\to B(G,O)
.

Proof.

Let
(g^{\prime}_{i},o^{\prime}_{ij})=c(g_{i},o_{ij})
and
(E,\theta)
the corresponding pair.
The union of the manifolds
g_{i}^{-1}(0)
is realized as
\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})\subset E

and by assumption there is a diffeomorphism
L\to\theta^{-1}(M\times\{0\})
lifting
\pi
.
So
(E,\theta)
represents the normal invariant of
\pi
as in Definition
2.7
.
∎

3.
Twisted generating functions

Every exact Lagrangian submanifold
L\subset T^{*}M
was shown, in
[
4
]
, to admit a twisted generating function of tube type. However the model for the space of functions of tube type considered in
[
4
]
consists of functions which are linear at infinity, while for constructing a model which is a monoid, it is more convenient to have a model built from functions which are quadratic at infinity. In this section we make the translation between the two models.

3.1.
Orthogonal quadratic forms

We start with some preliminaries on quadratic forms.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Q_{n}
denote the space of quadratic forms
q:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
which are non-degenerate, of eigenvalues
\pm\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mathcal{Q}=\coprod_{n}Q_{n}
with the monoid structure of direct sum. We call
q\in\mathcal{Q}

orthogonal quadratic forms
.

Remark 3.2
.

The choice of eigenvalues
\pm\frac{1}{2}
is a minor cosmetic choice designed to ensure that, the gradient
\nabla q
of every element of
q\in Q_{n}
lies in
O_{n}
(rather than in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{R})
).
It is irrelevant because the space of all non-degenerate quadratic forms deformation retracts
onto
\mathcal{Q}
. In
[
4
]
, we chose to work with eigenvalues
\pm 1
since that made a few bounds on twisted generating functions
easier to check but we could have equally chosen eigenvalues
\pm\frac{1}{2}
. Alternatively, one may work with eigenvalues
\pm 1
by replacing
O_{n}
with
2O_{n}
in the present text.

We have
Q_{n}=\coprod_{k+l=n}Q_{k,l}
where
Q_{k,l}
consists of those
q\in Q_{n}
whose negative eigenspace has dimension
k
. The space
Q_{k,l}
is diffeomorphic to the Grassmannian of
k
dimensional real vector subspaces in
\mathbf{R}^{k+l}
. Indeed the identification assigns to a
k
-dimensional vector subspace
F\subset\mathbf{R}^{k+l}
the unique
q\in Q_{k,l}
with
F
as its negative eigenspace and
F^{\perp}
as its positive eigenspace.

3.2.
Twisted generating functions linear at infinity

Definition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. A
generating function linear at infinity
over
M
is a smooth function

f:M\times\mathbf{R}_{w}\times\mathbf{R}_{v}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

(5)

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

f
is linear, with respect to the variable
w
, at infinity, in the sense that

f(x;w,v)=w+g(x;v)+\varepsilon(x;w,v),\quad(x;w,v)\in M\times\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n},

where the projection
\text{supp}(\varepsilon)\to M
is proper, and

(2)

the fiberwise gradient
\nabla f^{x}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{1+n}\to\mathbf{R}^{1+n}
is transverse to
0
.

The second condition implies that the fiberwise singular set

\Sigma_{f}=\{(x;w,v),\nabla f^{x}(w,v)=0\}

is a smooth submanifold and that the map
\Sigma_{f}\to J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R}
given by

(x;w,v)\mapsto\left(x,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x;w,v)dx,f(x;w,v)\right)

is a
Legendrian immersion
.

Following
[
4
]
, we define a stabilization operation
f\oplus_{b}q
where
f
is linear at infinity,
q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{R}
is a fiberwise orthogonal quadratic form and
b:M\to(0,\infty)
is a smooth function:

(f\oplus_{b}q)(x;w,v,u)=w+g(x;v)+\chi_{m}(b(x)^{-1}u)\varepsilon(x;w,v)+q(u).

This formula involves appropriate cut-off functions
\chi_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{m}\to[0,1]
which satisfy

\displaystyle\chi_{m+m^{\prime}}(u,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\chi_{m}(u)\chi_{m^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}),\quad(u,u^{\prime})\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}^{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\|\nabla\chi_{m}(u)\|

\displaystyle<\|u\|, \quad u\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}.

It is readily checked that
f\oplus_{b}q
is still linear at infinity. Moreover if

|\varepsilon(x;w,v)|\leq b(x)

then

\Sigma_{f\oplus_{b}q}=\Sigma_{f}\times\{0\}
and the Legendrian immersion obtained from
f

and
f\oplus_{b}q
agree.

Observe now that if
\Sigma_{f}
is disjoint from the zero locus of
f
, then
\{f\leq 0\}=\bigcup_{x}\{f^{x}\leq 0\}

defines a subbundle of the trivial bundle
M\times\mathbf{R}^{1+n}
. As in
[
4
]
we will work with a subclass
where the topology of the fiber of this subbundle is rather simple: each fiber will be obtained from a halfspace by a single
trivial handle attachment.

This is made precise in the next definition where we use an auxiliary function
D:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
of the form
D(w)=w+\varepsilon(w)
, with
\varepsilon
compactly supported (and
|\varepsilon|\leq 4
),
such that
D
has two critical points, a negative minimum and a positive maximum (it looks like
w^{3}-w
but equals
w
at infinity).
The choice of such a function is irrelevant as any two such functions are homotopic in this class.

Definition 3.4
.

A generating function linear at infinity
f
is of
tube type
if for each
x\in M
,

f^{x}
is transverse to
0
and there exists
q\in\mathcal{Q}
such that
f^{x}
is homotopic to
D\oplus_{4}q

among functions which are linear at infinity and are transverse to
0
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. A
twisted generating function linear at infinity
consists of a directed open cover
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
and data
(n_{i},b,f_{i},q_{ij})
relative to this cover, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

for all
i
,
f_{i}:M_{i}\times\mathbf{R}_{w}\times\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}_{v_{i}}\to\mathbf{R}
is a generating function linear at infinity over
M_{i}
, written
f_{i}=w+\varepsilon_{i}+g_{i}
as before,

(2)

for all
i<j
,
q_{ij}:M_{ij}\times\mathbf{R}^{n_{j}-n_{i}}\to\mathbf{R}
is a fiberwise orthogonal quadratic form,
b
is a positive constant, and
for all
x\in M_{ij}=M_{i}\cap M_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle g_{j}(x;v_{j})

\displaystyle=g_{i}(x;v_{i})+q_{ij}(x;v_{ij})

(6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}(x;w,v_{i},v_{ij})

\displaystyle=\chi_{n_{j}-n_{i}}(b^{-1}v_{ij})\varepsilon_{i}(x;w,v_{i}),

(7)

where we decompose

v_{j}=(v_{i},v_{ij})\in\mathbf{R}^{n_{j}}=\mathbf{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathbf{R}^{n_{j}-n_{i}},

(8)

(3)

for all
i
,
x\in M_{i}
,
(w,v_{i})\in\mathbf{R}^{1+n_{i}}
,
|\varepsilon_{i}(x;w,v_{i})|\leq b
, and

(4)

for all
i<j<k
,
q_{ij}\oplus q_{jk}=q_{ik}
over
M_{ijk}=M_{i}\cap M_{j}\cap M_{k}
.

The last condition is actually implied by the second one but we include it for the sake of clarity.

Remark 3.6
.

If we had
f_{j}=f_{i}\oplus q_{ij}
then it would be clear that the fiberwise singular sets glue into a manifold
with a Legendrian immersion
\bigcup_{i}\Sigma_{f_{i}}\to J^{1}M
. The cut-off functions
\chi_{n_{j}-n_{i}}
might ruin this but,
as previously mentioned (and shown in
[
4
]
), the bound
b
ensures that this still holds with the above definition.

Definition 3.7
.

A twisted generating function linear at infinity is of
tube type
if each function
f_{j}
is of tube type.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
L\subset J^{1}M
be a Legendrian submanifold. We say that
L
is generated by a twisted generating
function linear at infinity of tube type
(n_{i},b,f_{i},q_{ij})
if the fiberwise singular sets of negative value
\Sigma_{f_{j}}\cap\{f_{j}<0\}
define a Legendrian embedding with image
L
, i.e. on each
M_{i}
we have that
f_{i}|_{\{f_{i}<0\}}
is a generating function for
L\cap J^{1}M_{i}
in the usual sense.

The main result of
[
4
]
was the following:

Theorem 5
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold and
L\subset T^{*}M
a closed exact Lagrangian submanifold.
Then
L
admits a twisted generating function linear at infinity of tube type. ∎

For the purpose of the present article it will be more convenient to use a different version of generating functions, which we describe next.

3.3.
Almost quadratic twisted generating functions

Definition 3.9
.

A
fiberwise orthogonal quadratic function
on
M
is a smooth function

q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

(9)

such that for each
x\in M
the function
q^{x}(u)=q(x,u)
is in
Q_{n}
.

Definition 3.10
.

We say that a
C^{1}
function
G:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is a
non-degenerate quadratic function
if

(1)

G
is homogeneous of degree 2, i.e. for all
v\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\geq 0
,
G(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}G(v)
, and

(2)

\nabla G(v)\neq 0
for all
x\in M
and
v\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Definition 3.11
.

A
fiberwise non-degenerate quadratic function
on
M
is a
C^{1}
-function

G:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

such that for each
x\in M
the function
G^{x}(u)=G(x,u)
is a non-degenerate quadratic function.

Remark 3.12
.

Beware that we use the wording quadratic function as a shorthand for a function which is merely
\mathbf{R}_{+}
-homogeneous of degree
2
. A quadratic function need not be a quadratic form. Note that a quadratic form satisfies the second condition of Definition
3.11
if and only if it is non-degenerate, hence the terminology.

Remark 3.13
.

Since

is quadratic, asking that
\nabla G_{x}(v)\neq 0
for all

is equivalent to asking that the bound
\|\nabla G_{x}(v)\|\geq a(x)\|v\|
hold for some continuous function

. This is also equivalent to asking that

be transverse to
0
on

, since

is non-zero along the radial direction away from

.

Definition 3.14
.

A
fiberwise almost quadratic function
is a smooth function

F:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

of the form
F=G+E

where
G
is a fiberwise non-degenerate quadratic function and
E:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
satisfies

\|\nabla E^{x}\|\leq c(x)

for some continuous function

(we may take

to be a positive constant if
M
is compact).

Remark 3.15
.

The fiberwise non-degenerate quadratic function

is determined by
F
, indeed

G^{x}(v)=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\frac{G^{x}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\frac{F^{x}(\lambda v)}{\lambda^{2}}.

Definition 3.16
.

An
almost quadratic generating function
over
M
is a fiberwise almost quadratic function

F:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
such that the fiberwise gradient
\nabla F^{x}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
is transverse to
0
.

Definition 3.17
.

An almost quadratic generating function is of
tube type
if,
for all

, its associated non-degenerate quadratic function

is homotopic to a non-degenerate quadratic form in the space of non-degenerate quadratic functions.

Remark 3.18
.

The generating functions quadratic at infinity that are prominent in generating function theory, are particular cases of tube type almost quadratic generating functions

where the associated function

is a non-degenerate quadratic

.
In Waldhausen’s theory (see

), this corresponds to the inclusion of the space

of all tubes.

An advantage of the condition of being almost quadratic over being linear at infinity is that it is stable under direct sum with a quadratic form.
This removes the need for cut-off functions in the following definition.

Proposition 3.33
.

If the twisted generating function

Recall that the parameter

has been chosen large enough so that each function

is a generating

and altogether they generate a Legendrian submanifold

Fix

and

\Pi_{j}^{x}=(\Pi_{i}^{x},0)

for all

Proof.

This follows from Theorem

∎

In

3.6.
Twisted derivative and normal invariant

We now return to the constructions of Section

, which we now interpret as maps to appropriate spaces of functions on

:

Definition 3.36
.

Let
T^{q}_{n}
be the space of
non-degenerate quadratic functions of tube type
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, namely
C^{1}
-functions
G:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

(1)

(2)

\nabla G(v)\neq 0

(3)

is homotopic to a non-degenerate quadratic form among such functions.

We topologize

with the

-topology (weak and strong are equivalent here due to homogeneity).

Next, we describe the space of functions which corresponds to Definition
3.14
:

Definition 3.37
.

The space of
almost quadratic functions of tube type
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, denoted

is the space of pairs
(F,G)
where
G\in T^{q}_{n}
and

C^{1}

and
\|\nabla(F-G)(v)\|\leq c

.

As discussed in Remark

is in fact determined by

with the

-topology on

can be found locally near a pair
(F,G)
.
Note that we have continuous maps

via
G\mapsto(G,G)

and

via

and the path

We equip

The maps

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}^{aq}}

As explained above, there is a deformation retraction through monoid maps of

4.
Factorization of the twisted derivative

In this section we prove Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
, namely we
factor the twisted derivative
B(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{Q})\to B(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O})\to B(G,O)=G/O

through a twisted
S
-duality map
B(G/O)\to G/O
, and show that the latter is 2-torsion.
Our model for this map will be given in terms of the space
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}
of topological tubes, which is a model for
BG
.

From now on we implicitly replace all previous spaces (and monoids) with their singular simplicial set and entirely work with simplicial sets. We will nevertheless mostly suppress this from the notation, relying on the homotopy equivalence
|\sing_{\bullet}X|\simeq X
and the fact that this equivalence is compatible with taking bar constructions in the sense that there are natural homotopy equivalences

\displaystyle|B(\sing_{\bullet}X,\sing_{\bullet}A)|\simeq|B(|\sing_{\bullet}X|,|\sing_{\bullet}A|)|\simeq|B(X,A)|

for a topological monoid
A
acting on a space
X
(if both are well-pointed and of CW homotopy type, which all our spaces are). We will even call some simplicial sets spaces as the represent spaces through their geometric realization. By a homotopy equivalence of simplicial sets we will always mean a homotopy equivalence after geometric realization, which in other text is often called a weak equivalence.

4.1.
Topological tubes as a model for
BG

Denote by
q_{k,l}
the quadratic form on
\mathbf{R}^{k+l}
given by the formula

q_{k,l}(v_{-},v_{+})=\frac{1}{2}(-\|v_{-}\|^{2}+\|v_{+}\|^{2})

for
(v_{-},v_{+})\in \mathbf{R}^{k}\times\mathbf{R}^{l}
.
For the next definition, we choose natural numbers
k
and
l
and set
n=k+l
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
topological tube space
T^{\mathrm{Top}}_{k,l}
is the simplicial set whose
p
-simplices are continuous functions
f:\Delta^{p}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
which satisfy the following properties:

(1)

f
is fiberwise quadratic:
\forall v\in\mathbf{R}^{n},\forall\lambda\geq 0,\forall s\in\Delta^{p},f(s,\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}f(s,v)
, and

(2)

there exists an isotopy of homeomorphisms

(\varphi^{t})_{t\in[0,1]}:\Delta^{p}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\Delta^{p}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}

such that

•

\varphi^{t}(s,v)=(s,\psi^{t}(s,v))
,

•

\psi^{t}(s,\lambda v)=\lambda\psi^{t}(s,v)
for all
\lambda\geq 0
,

•

\varphi^{0}=\id
,

•

f(\varphi^{1}(s,v))=q_{k,l}(v)
.

The face and degeneracy maps are defined by the obvious pull backs.

The space
T^{\mathrm{Top}}_{k,l}
is by definition connected. We define
T^{\mathrm{Top}}_{n}=\coprod_{k+l=n}T^{\mathrm{Top}}_{k,l}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}=\coprod_{n}T^{\mathrm{Top}}_{n}

which is evidently a monoid under direct sum, and
\mathcal{Q}
(or rather
\sing_{\bullet}\mathcal{Q}
) is a sub-monoid. We also define the colimit

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}_{\infty}=\colim(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}\to\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}\to\cdots)

under the left action by
q_{1,1}(v_{-},v_{+})=\frac{1}{2}(-v_{-}^{2}+v_{+}^{2})
. The connected components of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}_{\infty}
are classified by the map

(\sigma,d):\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}_{\infty}\to\mathbf{Z}^{2}

which projects
T_{k,l}^{\mathrm{Top}}
, in place
i\in\mathbf{N}
of the colimit, to
(k-l,k+l-2i)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For all
k,l\in\mathbf{N}
,
T_{k,l}^{\mathrm{Top}}
is a Kan complex. In particular
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}_{\infty}
is a Kan complex.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\subset\Delta^{p}
be a horn and
r:\Delta^{p}\to\Lambda
a retraction. Given a horn
(f_{s})_{s\in\Lambda}

in
T_{k,l}^{\mathrm{Top}}
we extend it by
f_{s}=f_{r(s)}
for
s\in\Delta^{p}
.

We need to check the second condition in Definition
4.1
, namely the existence of a fibered
isotopy
\varphi^{t}_{s}
of homogeneous homeomorphisms with
f_{s}\circ\varphi^{1}_{s}=q_{k,l}
. Let
S_{k}
for
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
denote the non-degenerate
(p-1)
-simplices in the horn so that
\Lambda=\cup_{k}S_{k}
.
By definition we have such an isotopy over each
S_{k}
, but these might not agree on the
p-2
simplices where a pair of these simplices meet. So, assume for induction that we have already defined the desired isotopy
\Phi^{t}_{s}
for
s\in\cup_{k\leq m}S_{k}
. Pick a retraction
r_{m}:S_{m+1}\to S_{m+1}\cap\cup_{k\leq m}S_{k}
and an isotopy
\varphi^{t}_{s}
as in the definition over
S_{m+1}
. We then extend
\Phi^{t}_{s}
to any
s\in S_{m+1}
by

\Phi^{t}_{s}=\varphi^{t}_{s}\circ(\varphi^{t}_{r_{m}(s)})^{-1}\circ\Phi^{t}_{r_{m}(s)}.

This continuously extends the already partially defined
\Phi^{t}_{s}
over
S_{m+1}
(as it agrees on their intersection where
r_{m}(s)=s
). We also verify that

\displaystyle f_{s}(\Phi_{s}^{1})=q_{k,l}\circ(\varphi^{1}_{r_{m}(s)})^{-1}\circ\Phi^{1}_{r_{m}(s)}=f_{r_{m}(s)}\circ\Phi^{1}_{r_{m}(s)}=q_{k,l}.

It follows that
\Phi^{t}_{s}
may be defined over all of
\Lambda
. Finally we observe that the pull back to
\Delta^{p}
given by
\Phi^{t}_{r(-)}

gives the required isotopy over all of
\Delta^{p}
, proving that
f_{s}
is a
p
-simplex in
T_{k,l}^{\mathrm{Top}}
filling the given horn.

For the last statement, any horn in
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Top}}_{\infty}
is the image of a horn in some
T_{k,l}^{\mathrm{Top}}
so the claim follows.
∎

Definition 4.3

5.
Applications

In this final section we extract some concrete consequences of our main results by comparing with the surgery theory literature. Our applications will be formulated in terms of the

simple structure set

\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
defined in the introduction, where we recall that
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
denotes the subset of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
consisting of (equivalence classes of) pairs
(L,\pi)

where
L
is a closed manifold and
\pi=\pi_{M}\circ\psi
for
\pi_{M}\colon T^{*}M\to M
the
projection and
\psi\colon L\to T^{*}M
an exact Lagrangian embedding.

5.1.
Hard versus soft obstructions

If the nearby Lagrangian conjecture holds then
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
is reduced to the base point
(M,\id)
. We will apply our results to exhibit concrete obstructions for an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
. Moreover we will exhibit
hard
obstructions, with respect to the hard/soft dichotomy, which we briefly recall.

Given a geometric problem, for example the problem of constructing an immersion of a smooth manifold
X
into another smooth manifold
Y
, one may formulate an underlying
formal
problem which often (but not always) is obtained by decoupling the function under consideration from its derivatives. For example, the formal problem underlying the geometric problem of constructing an immersion
X\hookrightarrow Y
is the problem of constructing a bundle monomorphism
TX\to TY
. Following Gromov, when any solution to the formal problem may be deformed to a solution to the original geometric problem, one says that there holds an h-principle, or that the problem is flexible. For example, the problem of constructing an immersion is flexible when
\dim X<\dim Y
, this is the Hirsch-Smale-Whitney immersion theory.

Although a surprising number of problems in symplectic and contact geometry abide by an h-principle, many do not, and the problem of constructing Lagrangian embeddings is certainly not flexible. For problems which are not flexible, one may still consider the underlying formal problem, and any obstruction to the solution of the problem which is already present at the formal level is said to be
soft
. In contrast, any obstruction to the solution of the problem which is not present at the formal level is said to be
hard
. We refer the reader to
[
14
]
for further discussion of the hard/soft dichotomy.

In this section we will apply our results to give new obstructions for an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
. Before doing so, we will discuss soft obstructions to this problem and state readily verifiable sufficient conditions for the absence of soft obstructions. When our main results are applied to obstruct an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
from lying in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
in a situation where these sufficient conditions hold, we will know that the obstruction is hard.

A first obstruction to the problem of realizing an element
[\pi:L\to M]
of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
is whether
\pi
factors (up to homotopy) as the composition of a Lagrangian immersion
L\to T^{*}M
and the projection
\pi_{M}:T^{*}M\to M
. The problem of constructing Lagrangian immersions is fully flexible: it abides by an h-principle, and indeed reduces to asking that the complex vector bundle
\pi^{*}(TM\otimes\mathbf{C})
is isomorphic to
TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
. In a different direction, one may forget about symplectic geometry and consider the obstruction to realizing an element
[\pi:L\to M]
of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
given by factoring
\pi
(up to homotopy) as the composition of a smooth embedding
L\to T^{*}M
and the projection
\pi_{M}:T^{*}M\to M
. These two obstructions may be combined into a single obstruction, namely that of factoring
\pi
(up to homotopy) as the composition of a totally real embedding
L\to T^{*}M
and the projection
\pi_{M}:T^{*}M\to M
, which is indeed a standard formulation of the formal problem underlying the construction of a Lagrangian embedding. The situation can be summarized in the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
(L,\pi)\in\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
, then the following conditions are satisftied

(1)

there exists a totally real embedding
L\to T^{*}M
homotopic to
\pi
,

(2)

there exists an immersion
L\to T^{*}M
homotopic to
\pi
which is both regularly homotopic to a Lagrangian immersion and
to a smooth embedding,

(3)

the complex vector bundle
\pi^{*}(TM\otimes\mathbf{C})
is isomorphic to
TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
.

Moreover the first two conditions are equivalent and imply the third.

Proof.

The first condition holds for an exact Lagrangian embedding
L\to T^{*}M
since a Lagrangian is totally real.
It implies the second condition in view of the h-principle for Lagrangian immersions since a totally real embedding
can be made into a formal Lagrangian immersion (the totally real Grassmannian deformation retracts on the Lagrangian
Grassmannian). To see that the second condition implies the first, consider an embedding
\psi\colon L\to T^{*}M
which is regularly homotopic to a Lagrangian immersion. Since
\psi
is formally totally real (i.e.
d\psi
is homotopic to a totally real monomorphism in the space of monomorphisms
TL\to T(T^{*}M)

covering
\psi
), the map is thus isotopic to a totally real embedding in view of the h-principle for totally real embeddings.
The second condition implies the third in view of the following isomorphisms:

\pi^{*}(TM\otimes\mathbf{C})\simeq\psi^{*}(TT^{*}M)\simeq TL\oplus T^{*}L\simeq(TL\otimes\mathbf{C}).

∎

The third condition of Proposition
5.1
is equivalent to the triviality of
the map
L\to BU(n)
classifying the complex vector bundle
(\pi^{*}TM-TL)\otimes\mathbf{C}
.
Since we are in stable range this is the same as considering the stabilized map
L\to BU
.
We do not know if this condition implies the two other ones
of Proposition
5.1
in the general case but here are some cases
where this holds. The arguments are essentially extracted from
[
3
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(L,\pi)\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
such that
\pi^{*}(TM\otimes\mathbf{C})\simeq TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
.
If one of the following conditions holds then
\pi
is homotopic to a totally real embedding

:

(1)

M
is orientable and even-dimensional,

(2)

is odd-dimensional, and at least 5 dimensional and stably parallelizable.

Proof.

In view of the h-principle for Lagrangian immersions, the bundle isomorphism implies that
\pi
is homotopic
to a Lagrangian immersion
L\to T^{*}M
.

Assume that
M
(and therefore
L
) has even dimension
n
and is orientable. The double points of an immersion of
L
in
T^{*}M

can be counted algebraically as an integer
d(L)
. Since
[L]=[M]
(with compatible choices of orientations)
in
H_{n}(T^{*}M,\mathbf{Z})
, the self-intersection of

can be expressed as

(-1)^{n/2}\chi(M)=[M]\cdot[M]=[L]\cdot[L]=2d(L)+e(\nu L),

where
e(\nu L)
is the Euler number of the normal bundle of
L
in
T^{*}M
, which due to the Lagrangian condition,
is equal to
e(\nu L)=(-1)^{n/2}\chi(L)=(-1)^{n/2}\chi(M)
since

and
M
are homotopy equivalent (the isomorphism
\nu L\to TL
multiplies the orientation by
(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
which is
(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}
when

is even).
So we conclude that

and that
L\to T^{*}M
is regularly homotopic to an embedding by the Whitney trick.

In the second case where
n=2k+1
,

is an integer modulo
2
and so we need only prove that

has an even number of double points.
From Theorem 0.5 (a) of

, we know that all Lagrangian immersions of

and

in

have

,
where
\chi_{\mathbf{Z}/2}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dim H_{i}(-;\mathbf{Z}/2)
is the Kervaire semi-characteristic. Now using a tubular neighborhood of an immersion
M\to\mathbf{C}^{n}
, we obtain
from the immersion
L\to T^{*}M
an immersion in
L\to\mathbf{C}^{n}
with
d_{\mathbf{C}^{n}}(L)=d(L)+d_{\mathbf{C}^{n}}(M)
(since

has odd degree).
So we obtain

and we conclude with the Whitney trick as before.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

When
M
is a homotopy sphere,
L
and
M
are stably parallelizable
[
17
]
so the bundles
\pi^{*}TM\otimes\mathbf{C}
and

are stably isomorphic as complex vector bundles, and since we’re in the stable range this implies that they’re isomorphic as complex vector bundles before stabilization.
So Proposition

says that there are no soft obstructions for an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
.

For other manifolds
M
, there may well exist elements of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
in a different

tangential
homotopy type which moreover have non trivial complexification,
leading to soft restrictions on
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
. It would be the case if

has non-zero Pontryagin classes, for example for certain fake complex projective spaces.

In the remainder of this section we will use our main theorem to derive obstructions for elements of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
in various concrete examples, which we will ensure are hard by means of Propositions

and
5.2
.

5.2.
Homotopy spheres

Suppose that
M
is a homotopy sphere and
\pi:L\to M
is a homotopy equivalence. As mentioned in Remark
5.3
there are no soft obstructions for an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
to lie in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{NL}}^{s}(M)
when
M
is a homotopy sphere.

Since all homotopy spheres are PL equivalent, given any marking
S^{n}\to M
, we may identify
[M,PL/O]
with
\Theta_{n}
and identify the map
[M,PL/O]\to[M,G/O]
with the Kervaire-Milnor map
\Theta_{n}\to\coker(J_{n})
, where
J_{n}:\pi_{n}O\to\pi_{n}^{s}
is the
J
-homomorphism. We recall that the Kervaire-Milnor map
\Theta_{n}\to\coker(J_{n})
can be thought of as the map which sends a homotopy sphere (which is always stably parallelizable) to the collection of framed bordism classes determined by all possible stable framings.

The kernel of the Kervaire-Milnor map is the subgroup
bP_{n+1}\subset\Theta_{n}
consisting of those homotopy spheres that bound a parallelizable manifold, so the induced map on the quotient
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}\to\coker(J_{n})
is a monomorphism (whose image is always either the whole
\coker(J_{n})
or an index two subgroup). The normal invariant of a homotopy equivalence of homotopy spheres
\Sigma_{0}\to\Sigma_{1}
can be thus identified with the difference between the classes of
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
in
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}\subset\coker(J_{n})
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
be
n
-dimensional homotopy spheres. The above dicussion, together with Corollary
1.2
implies:

Corollary 5.4
.

If there exists a Lagrangian embedding
\Sigma_{1}\subset T^{*}\Sigma_{0}
, then the class
\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{1}
in
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
is 2-torsion. ∎

Since
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
is not in general 2-torsion, we easily obtain many examples of hard obstructions in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(S^{n})
. For example
\Theta_{10}/bP_{11}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/6
,
\Theta_{18}/bP_{19}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/8\oplus\mathbf{Z}/2
and
\Theta_{20}/bP_{21}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/24
contain lots of elements which are not 2-torsion.

Furthermore, for homotopy spheres our result from Theorems
1
and
2
that the normal invariant
\Sigma_{0}\to G/O
of the homotopy equivalence
\Sigma_{0}\to\Sigma_{1}
factors through

gives additional restrictions on the possible classes in

beyond the condition of being 2-torsion. For example,
\Theta_{8}/bP_{9}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/2
is 2-torsion, so the knowledge that the normal is 2-torsion by itself does not yield new information. However,
\pi_{7}G/O=0
and so if
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{8}
is a homotopy 8-sphere it follows from our work that the normal invariant of the projection
\Sigma\to S^{8}
is trivial. This implies that the class of
\Sigma
in
\theta_{8}/bP_{9}
is trivial. Since

this in fact implies the following consequence:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{8}
be a Lagrangian homotopy sphere. Then
\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{8}
.

More generally,
bP_{n+1}=0
whenever

is even, so for even dimensional Lagrangian homotopy spheres of dimension
\neq 4
our above results can be stated at the level of

. For example, we have the following statement.

Corollary 5.6
.

\Sigma\subset T^{*}S^{n}
is a Lagrangian homotopy sphere and

is even, then

is diffeomorphic to the standard

.

Furthermore, as explained in Section

, combining the present article with the results of

one immediately obtains the stronger conclusion of Corollary

, namely that the class of
\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{1}
in
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
is in the image of the composition

\pi_{n-1}^{s}\xrightarrow{\eta}\pi_{n}^{s}\to\text{Coker}(J_{n}),

where the first map is multiplication by

. From this a number of additional constraints can be deduced, see for example the first picture in
[

for an illustration of the relevant multiplicative structure of the stable homotopy groups of spheres in small degrees.

5.3.
A general criterion

For many applications it is sufficient to use the consequence of our results that the normal invariant
M\to G/O
of a nearby Lagrangian
L\to T^{*}M
is 2-torsion (this is Corollary
1.2
). Concretely, one may use a simple criterion, formulated in Proposition
5.7
below, to obtain examples of hard obstructions in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
for a general class of smooth manifolds
M
.

Recall from
[
16
]
(introduction to part II) that there is a bijection between the set of smoothings of a PL manifold
M
(that has at least one smoothing) up to concordance and
[M,PL/O]
, where the bijection can be arranged to carry any fixed smooth structure on
M
to the trivial homotopy class. For example, in the case of homotopy spheres every exotic sphere is PL equivalent to the standard sphere and hence
[S^{n},PL/O]
can be identified with the group
\Theta_{n}
as was already mentioned.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that
M
is a smooth closed manifold such that
[M,BO]
has no odd torsion. Then any odd torsion element of
[M,G/O]
can be lifted to an element
[\pi:L\to M]\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
such that
\pi^{*}TM\otimes\mathbf{C}\simeq TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
as complex vector bundles, yet
[\pi:L\to M]\not\in\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
.

Proof.

It was shown by Sullivan that away from the prime 2 the space
G/PL
has the same homotopy type as
BO
. Furthermore, there is a map
G/PL[\frac{1}{2}]\to BO^{\otimes}[\frac{1}{2}]
which is an equivalence of h-spaces, where
BO^{\otimes}=BO\times(1)\subset BO\times\mathbf{Z}
denotes
BO
with the h-space structure given by tensor product of virtual vector bundles of rank 1. Our assumption was that
[M,BO]
has no odd torsion, where the h-space structure on
BO
is that of Whitney sum, but from the uniqueness of h-space structures on
BO
away from the prime 2, see
[
2
]
, it follows that
[M,BO^{\otimes}[\frac{1}{2}]]
also has no odd torsion. We conclude that
[M,G/PL]
has no odd torsion.

From the fibration
PL/O\to G/O\to G/PL
it then follows that the associated homomorphims of abelian groups
[M,PL/O]\to[M,G/O]
surjects onto the odd torsion subgroup of
[M,G/O]
. In particular it follows that any odd torsion class in
[M,G/O]
automatically lies in the image of
[M,PL/O]\to[M,G/O]
.

The surgery obstruction
[M,G/O]\to L^{s}_{n}(\pi_{1}M)
of a class which lifts to
[M,PL/O]
must be trivial, precisely because it comes from a smoothing of
M
. Hence it can be lifted to a class
[\pi:L\to M]\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
. To prove that
\pi^{*}TM\otimes\mathbf{C}\simeq TL\otimes\mathbf{C}
as complex vector bundles it suffices to prove the stronger property that
\pi^{*}TM\simeq TL
are isomorphic as real vector bundles. But we know that the map
M\to BO
classifying
\pi^{*}TM-TL
must be trivial, since it is the image of an odd torsion element under the map
[M,G/O]\to[M,BO]
and
[M,BO]
has no odd torsion by assumption.

Finally, the class we started with in
[M,G/O]
is not 2-torsion, since it is an odd torsion element. Hence by Corollary
1.2
we conclude that
[\pi:L\to M]
does not lie in
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(M)
as required.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

In view of Proposition
5.2
, when
L
and
M
are orientable of even dimension or when
L
and
M
are odd dimensional and parallelizable, a class
[\pi:L\to M]\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
as in Proposition
5.7
can be realized by a totally real embedding, hence at least in this case the obstruction of Proposition
5.7
is hard.

5.4.
Homotopy products of spheres

Let us discuss the case where
M
is homotopy equivalent to a product of two spheres
S^{a}\times S^{b}
. Recall that if
X=\Omega Y
, then
[S^{a}\times S^{b},X]\simeq\pi_{a}X\oplus\pi_{b}X\oplus\pi_{a+b}X
. Indeed, the attaching maps for the top cell in the natural CW structure of
S^{a}\times S^{b}
becomes trivial after suspension. Hence

[S^{a}\times S^{b},X]=[S^{a}\times S^{b},\Omega Y]\simeq[\Sigma\left(S^{a}\times S^{b}\right),Y]

\simeq[S^{a+1}\lor S^{b+1}\lor S^{a+b+1},Y]\simeq\pi_{a+1}Y\oplus\pi_{b+1}Y\oplus\pi_{a+b+1}Y

\simeq\pi_{a}X\oplus\pi_{b}X\oplus\pi_{a+b}X.

Therefore, for
i=a,b
and
a+b
, we deduce that the group
\Theta_{i}/bP_{i+1}
is a summand of the image of
[S^{a}\times S^{b},PL/O]\to[S^{a}\times S^{b},G/O]
. Indeed, since
PL/O
and
G/O
are loop spaces, we have

[S^{a}\times S^{b},PL/O]\simeq\pi_{a}PL/O\oplus\pi_{b}PL/O\oplus\pi_{a+b}PL/O

[S^{a}\times S^{b},G/O]\simeq\pi_{a}G/O\oplus\pi_{b}G/O\oplus\pi_{a+b}G/O

The map
[S^{a}\times S^{b},PL/O]\to[S^{a}\times S^{b},G/O]
respects this decomposition and is just the Kervaire-Milnor map
\Theta_{i}\to\text{coker}(J_{i})
on each factor, from which the claim follows.

Since
BO
is a (infinite) loop space and
[S^{i},BO]=\pi_{i}BO
has no odd torsion, it follows by the same reasoning that
[S^{a}\times S^{b},BO]
has no odd torsion. As we saw before, for
n=10,18,20,...
the group
\Theta_{n}/bP_{n+1}
is not 2-torsion. Hence by Proposition
5.7
we obtain hard obstructions on
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(S^{a}\times S^{b})
when
a
,
b
or
a+b
are equal to
10,18,20,...
.

Suppose next that
M
is homotopy equivalent to
T^{n}
, the product of
n
copies of
S^{1}
. As before, if
X=\Omega Y
be a loop space, then
[T^{n},X]\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\pi_{i}X^{\oplus{n\choose i}}
. Hence once again
[T^{n},BO]
has no odd torsion.

Just as before, for
i\leq n
, the group
\Theta_{i}/bP_{i+1}
is a summand of the image of
[T^{n},PL/O]\to[T^{n},G/O]
. Since
PL/O
and
G/O
are loop spaces, reasoning as before we conclude

[T^{n},PL/O]\simeq\bigoplus_{i}(\pi_{i}PL/O)^{\oplus{n\choose i}},\qquad[T^{n},G/O]\simeq\bigoplus_{i}(\pi_{i}G/O)^{\oplus{n\choose i}}

Moreover, the map
[T^{n},PL/O]\to[T^{n},G/O]
respects this decomposition and is just the Kervaire-Milnor map
\Theta_{i}\to\text{coker}(J_{i})
on each factor.

Hence by Proposition
5.7
for all
n\geq 10
there are hard obstructions on
\mathcal{S}^{s}_{\mathrm{NL}}(T^{n})
. Clearly similar considerations apply to arbitrary products of spheres.

One could push this a bit further using the fact that the twisted duality map
B(G/O)\to G/O
may be realized as a loop map (indeed it may be realized as an infinite loop map). We only construct the twisted duality map as a map of spaces in the present article. However, assuming this we may obtain hard obstructions beyond those provided by Proposition
5.7
, for example if
M
is any product
S^{8}\times S^{b}
or any torus
T^{n}
for
n\geq 8
, then the image of
[M,PL/O]\to[M,G/O]
contains a
\Theta_{8}/bP_{9}\simeq\Theta_{8}\simeq\mathbf{Z}/2
summand which is not in the image of the map
[M,B(G/O)]\to[M,G/O]
because the twisted duality map
B(G/O)\to G/O
respects the above decomposition (by virtue of being a loop map) and
\pi_{7}G/O=0
. This class lifts to an element of
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M)
which by Theorems
1
and
2
is not in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{NL}}^{s}(M)
.

5.5.
Fake complex projective spaces

As a final application we briefly discuss the case in which
M
is homotopy equivalent to complex projective space.

First, we recall that
[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n},BO]
has no odd torsion, see for instance
[
15
]
. Therefore, in view of Remark
5.8
, in order to apply Proposition
5.7
and exhibit hard obstructions on
S^{s}_{\text{NL}}(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n})
it suffices to exhibit odd torsion elements in
[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n},G/O]
.

Concrete examples of such odd torsion may be obtained by considering the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence for the stable cohomotopy of
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n}
, using knowledge of the stable homotopy groups of spheres together with relevant information about attaching maps and homotopy group compositions. Indeed, as explained by Brumfiel in
[
5
]
, the torsion subgroup of
[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n},G/O]
may be identified with the zeroth stable cohomotopy group of
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n}
, from which it follows that
[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n},G/O]
contains a
\mathbf{Z}/3
summand for
n=5
and
n=6
. See
[
5
]
for further results in this direction indicating that odd torsion in
[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{n},G/O]
is plentiful.

Appendix A
Two-sided bar construction

Let
G
be a (well-pointed) group like topological monoid,
X
a right
G
-space and
Y
a left
G
-space.
We denote
B_{\bullet}(X,G,Y)
the associated two-sided bar construction. This is a simplicial space and we denote
B(X,G,Y)

its geometric realization. We will use the following well-known results (see
[
24
]
).

Proposition A.1
.

The natural map

B(X,G,G)\to X

is a homotopy equivalence.

Proposition A.2
.

The map

B(X,G,Y)\to B(X,G)

is a quasi-fibration with fiber weakly homotopy equivalent to
Y
.

Corollary A.3
.

Let
E
be a right
G
-space and let
B
be a space with trivial
G
action. Assume that
f:E\to B
is a
G
-invariant surjective map which is a quasi-fibration. Suppose that for each
e\in E
, the map
G\to f^{-1}(f(e))
given by
g\mapsto e\cdot g
is a weak homotopy equivalence. Then the induced map
B(E,G)\to B
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B(E,G,G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B(E,G)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The top horizontal map is a homotopy equivalence according to Lemma
A.1
.
The left vertical map is a quasi-fibration with fiber weakly homotopy equivalent to
G
according to Lemma
A.2

and so is the right vertical map by assumption. Moreover by
G
-equivariance the horizontal maps
induce a weak homotopy equivalence on the fibers of the vertical maps. Hence the map
B(E,G)\to B

is also a weak homotopy equivalence.
∎

Next, we let
A
be a topological monoid (not necessarily grouplike) and
X
a space with a right action of
A
.

Proposition A.4

(c.f. Proposition D.1 of
[
20
]
)
.

If
A
acts on
X
by weak homotopy equivalences, namely for all
m\in A
,
x\mapsto x\cdot m
is a weak homotopy equivalence of
X
,
then
B(X,A)\to BA
is a quasifibration with fiber
X
. ∎

Up to homotopy, the above implies that the
A
-action on
X
extends to an action of its group completion
\Omega BA
, which can be used to apply results about actions of group-like monoids to this setting. We can avoid the up-to-homotopy replacement in proving the following:

Corollary A.5
.

Let
\phi:A\to A^{\prime}
be a monoid map such that

is a homotopy equivalence.
Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be spaces with right actions by weak homotopy equivalences of
A
and
A^{\prime}
respectively.
Let

be a weak homotopy equivalence which is
\phi

B(X,A)\to B(X^{\prime},A^{\prime})

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Consider the following diagram where the vertical sequences are fibration sequences according to Corollary
A.4
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}

The result then follows from the five lemma.

1.
Introduction

For
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
even, let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cuspidal modular forms of weight
k
and modular group
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Additionally, let
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\subseteq S_{k}(N)
denote its new subspace. For primes
p
, let
\widehat{T}_{p}(N,k):=p^{-(k-1)/2}\,T_{p}(N,k)
and
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k):=p^{-(k-1)/2}\widehat{T}^{\textnormal{new}}_{p}(N,k)
denote the
p
’th normalized Hecke operators over
S_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)
, respectively.

The asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues of these Hecke operators has been studied in many different settings. From one asymptotic perspective, one can fix
N
and
k
, and ask about the behavior of the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}^{\textnormal{new}}_{p}(N,k)
as
p\to\infty
. Here, the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}^{\textnormal{new}}_{p}(N,k)
correspond with the normalized Fourier coefficients
\widehat{a}_{f}(p)
of newforms
f=\sum_{m\geq 1}m^{(k-1)/2}\widehat{a}_{f}(m)q^{m}\in S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)
. And the asymptotic behavior of these Fourier coefficients has been an important area of research in Number Theory for many years. The Sato-Tate conjecture (now a theorem
[
1
]
), for example, states that the prime-indexed Fourier coefficients
\widehat{a}_{f}(p)
of a non-CM newform
f
tend to the Sato-Tate distribution
\mu_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
. Similarly, it is known that the prime-indexed Fourier coefficients
\widehat{a}_{f}(p)
of a CM newform tend to the CM distribution
\mu_{\text{CM}}
as
p\to\infty

[
2
, Theorem 15.4]
.

From a slightly different perspective, one can fix
p
and ask about the behavior of the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}^{\textnormal{new}}_{p}(N,k)
as
N+k\to\infty
. From this perspective, Serre showed that the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}(N,k)
and
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
tend to yet another distribution
\mu_{p}
as
N+k\to\infty

[
6
, Theorems 1, 2]
.

A natural question one might then ask is if the same result holds over the spaces with nebentypus
S_{k}(N,\chi)
and
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
. In fact, Serre noted that his result (with the same distribution
\mu_{p}
) could be extended to the full spaces with nebentypus
S_{k}(N,\chi)
via an identical strategy
[
6
, Theorem 4]
. However,
[
6
]
did not contain the tools to show the corresponding result over the newspaces with nebentypus
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
.
In this paper, we address this case by determining the distribution of the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
.

One might guess that Serre’s
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution result should still hold over
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
. However, this turns out to not be true; the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
are not guaranteed to tend to a distribution (albeit for a trivial reason explained shortly). But when these eigenvalues do tend to an asymptotic distribution, they will tend to
\mu_{p}
, like for the full space
S_{k}(N,\chi)
.

In particular, we show the following result. Here, and throughout this paper,
{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
denotes the conductor of
\chi
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix a prime
p
, and let
\displaystyle\mu_{p}(x):=\frac{p+1}{\pi}\frac{(1-x^{2}/4)^{1/2}}{(p^{1/2}+p^{-1/2})^{2}-x^{2}}dx
. Consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
p
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
Dirichlet characters modulo
N
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Then assuming it is not the case that
2\mid{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi),\ 2\parallel N/{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
; the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
are
\mu_{p}
-equidistributed over
[-2,2]
as
N+k\to\infty
.

We note that when
2\mid{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi),\ 2\parallel N/{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
, the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
do not tend to a distribution for a trivial reason. In this case, it turns out that
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)=0

[
5
, Proposition 6.1]
, so in fact, there are no eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
.
However, if we exclude the case of
2\mid{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi),\ 2\parallel N/{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
, then it turns out that
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)\to\infty
as
N+k\to\infty

[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
. Hence after excluding this exceptional case, it is perfectly well-defined to ask about the distribution of the eigenvalues of
\widehat{T}_{p}(N,k,\chi)
as
N+k\to\infty
.

Finally, in Section
3
, we will discuss an application of Theorem
1.1
. In particular, one can use the equidistribution result from Theorem
1.1
to obtain information about the coefficient fields for newforms in
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
.

Most of the technical details underlying the techniques used in this paper have already been worked out in other contexts (both by the author, and by others).
In particular, we rely heavily on two key pieces.
First, Lemma
2.1
gives a certain characterization of
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution from
[
6
]
(following the same outline that Serre used to show his original
\mu_{p}
-equidistribution result).
Second, Lemma
2.3
gives Hecke operator trace estimates that were computed in
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
.

3.
An Application of the Main Theorem

Finally, we point out one application of Theorem
1.1
, analogous to
[
6
, Theorem 5]
.

For newforms
f\in S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
, let
K_{f}:=\QQ(\{a_{f}(m):m\geq 1,\ (m,N)=1\})
denote the coefficient field of
f
. Then for
r\geq 1
, let
s(N,k,\chi)_{r}
denote the number of newforms
f\in S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,\chi)
such that
[K_{f}:\QQ]=r
. Then the equidistribution result of Theorem
1.1
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Fix
p
prime,
k\geq 2
, and
r\geq 1
. Consider
N
coprime to
p
and Dirichlet characters
\chi
modulo
N
with
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Then assuming it is not the case that
2\mid{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
,
2\parallel N/{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
;

\displaystyle\frac{s(N,k,\chi)_{r}}{\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as}\qquad N\longrightarrow\infty.

(3.1)

Recall that the Fourier coefficients
a_{f}(m)
correspond with the eigenvalues of
T_{m}
.
Then the above corollary follows from the fact that the set of possible
T_{m}
eigenvalues with degree
\leq r
is finite, so the set of normalized eigenvalues is
\mu_{p}
-measure
0
in
[-2,2]
. The interested reader can find the precise details of this proof in
[
6
, Theorem 5]
.

Correction for Johnson et al., L-type Ca2+ channel blockers promote vascular remodeling through activation of STIM proteins
Published: 2024

Correction for “L-type Ca2+ channel blockers promote vascular remodeling through activation of STIM proteins,” by Martin T. Johnson, Aparna Gudlur, Xuexin Zhang, Ping Xin, Scott M. Emrich, Ryan E. Yoast, Raphael Courjaret, Robert M. Nwokonko, Wei Li, Nadine Hempel, Khaled Machaca, Donald L. Gill, Patrick G. Hogan, and Mohamed Trebak, which was first published July 8, 2020; 10.1073/pnas.2007598117 (Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 117, 17369–17380).

After consultation with the editors, the authors are making the following corrections to their article. The authors note that Fig. 7 S and T, appeared incorrectly, with odds ratio (OR) values listed in Fig. 7S, but relative risk (RR) values plotted in Fig. 7T. Also, the OR values in Fig. 7S were incorrectly labeled as “%”. The corrected figure appears below, with relabeled OR values in Fig. 7 S and T showing plotted ORs rather than RRs. These changes do not alter the scientific conclusions drawn from the figure.

For consistency, the authors have updated SI Appendix, Figs. S9D, S10, and S11, with all calculations based on ORs and presented in the same format as the revised Fig. 7 S and T. The scientific conclusions are not changed, except that occurrence of nonuterine leiomyomas now shows a modest association with LCCB treatment, the 95% CI having shifted from 1.000 to 1.201 to 1.031 to 1.235. Numbers on demographics (race and sex) have been added to updated SI Appendix, Fig. S9 A and B.

The text on p. 17377, beginning at line 12 of the section LCCBs are Associated with Increased Incidence of Heart Failure in Patients, has been updated to align the statistical values stated in the text with the corrected Fig. 7 and supplementary figures. The corrected text should appear as:

“…. Our analysis showed that hypertensive patients treated with LCCBs had a significantly higher incidence of heart failure (Odds, 0.236) compared to patients treated with other antihypertensive drugs (Odds, 0.185) (Fig. 7S). Statistically, patients treated with LCCBs were more prone to heart failure when compared to pooled patients treated with other antihypertensive drugs (Fig. 7T) (95% CI for odds ratio = [1.243,1.306]). The demographics including sex, race, and age among the cohorts were similar (SI Appendix, Fig. S9 A–C). After stratification based on sex, LCCBs were still more associated with heart failure than other antihypertensive drugs (SI Appendix, Fig. S9D) (95% CI for odds ratio = [1.063, 1.138] and [1.495, 1.608] for males and females, respectively), with the exception of diuretics and β-blockers in male patients.”

After concerns were raised by readers, the authors also examined other smooth muscle proliferative diseases and certain types of cancers that are associated with enhanced STIM expression and increased SOCE activity (61). The smooth muscle neoplasms, leiomyomas of the uterus (fibroids; 95% CI for odds ratio = [0.887, 0.989]) or leiomyosarcomas [0.936, 1.143] were no more associated with LCCBs than with other antihypertensive drugs, whereas nonuterine leiomyomas [1.031, 1.235] exhibited a slight association with LCCBs (SI Appendix, Fig. S10 A–F). However, LCCBs were more associated with prostate [1.335, 1.482], breast [1.057, 1.176], and bladder cancer [1.156, 1.330] (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 A–F).

The authors note that demographic data regarding comorbidities including coronary artery disease, diabetes, smoking, and obesity were not acquired for the cohort used in our 2019 analysis. Although these data are not available, we acknowledge their potential importance and recognize that the results of the analysis might or might not have been altered if these factors had been taken into account.

The authors also note that the final sentence of the significance statement should state: “These results provide unique mechanistic insights into how widely used drugs activate a Ca2+ signaling pathway and suggest that the use of LCCBs in patients with chronic hypertension, where levels of STIM proteins and vascular remodeling are already enhanced, should be prudently considered.”

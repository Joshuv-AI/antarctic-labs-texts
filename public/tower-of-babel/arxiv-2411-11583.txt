Convergence and long-time behavior of finite volumes for a generalized Poisson–Nernst–Planck system with cross-diffusion and size exclusion
Clément Cancès, Maxime Herda, Annamaria Massimini

Abstract
. We present a finite volume scheme for modeling the diffusion of charged particles, specifically ions, in constrained geometries using a degenerate Poisson–Nernst–Planck system with size exclusion yielding cross-diffusion. Our method utilizes a two-point flux approximation and is part of the exponentially fitted scheme framework. The scheme is shown to be thermodynamically consistent, as it ensures the decay of some discrete version of the free energy. Classical numerical analysis results – existence of a discrete solution, convergence of the scheme as the grid size and the time step go to 0 0 – follow. We also investigate the long-time behavior of the scheme, both from a theoretical and numerical point of view. Numerical simulations confirm our findings, but also point out some possibly very slow convergence towards equilibrium of the system under consideration.

1.
Presentation of the problem

1.1.
The continuous generalized Poisson–Nernst–Planck model

Motivated by the transfer of ions in confined geometries, Burger
et al.
introduced in
[
5
]
a model accounting for cross-diffusion and size-exclusion effects. The model
[
6
]
further incorporated self-consistent electric interaction.
In this model,
I
species, the volume fractions of which being denoted by
U=(u_{i})_{1\leq i\leq I}
, are subject to diffusion as well as to electric forces induced by a self-consistent electrostatic potential.
Denote by
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a bounded connected polyhedral domain, then the conservation of the volume occupied by the species
i
is written

(1)

\partial_{t}u_{i}+\nabla\cdot F_{i}=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,I,

with the flux of the species
i
being (formally) given by

(2)

F_{i}=-D_{i}\left(u_{0}\nabla u_{i}-u_{i}\nabla u_{0}+u_{0}u_{i}z_{i}\nabla\phi\right)=-D_{i}u_{i}u_{0}\nabla\left(\log\left(\frac{u_{i}}{u_{0}}\right)+z_{i}\phi\right).

In the above expression,
D_{i}>0
denotes the diffusion coefficient of the species
i
.
The quantity

(3)

u_{0}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i}

shall be thought of as the volume fraction of available space for the ions, possibly occupied by a motile and electro-neutral solvent. The quantity
u_{0}
is then required to remain non-negative, leading to size exclusion for the other species
u_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,I
. Denoting by
z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
the charge of species
i
and by
\lambda>0
the (scaled) Debye length, then the electrostatic potential
\phi
solves the Poisson equation

(4)

-\lambda^{2}\Delta\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i}+f

for some prescribed background charge density
f
.
We consider boundary conditions of mixed type for the electric potential. More precisely, we assume that the boundary
\partial\Omega
of the domain can be split into an insulator part
\Gamma^{N}
, which is a relatively open
C^{1}
part of the boundary, and its complement
\Gamma^{D}
on which a Dirichlet boundary condition is imposed:

(5)

\nabla\phi\cdot n=0\quad\text{on}\;\Gamma^{N}\qquad\text{and}\quad\phi=\phi^{D}\;\text{on}\;\Gamma^{D},

where
n
is the unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Throughout this paper, we will assume that
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that
\phi^{D}
is the trace of an
L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
function (which we also denote by
\phi^{D}
).
Neither
f
nor
\phi^{D}
depend on time.
Boundary conditions of various types can be considered for the conservation laws (
1
)–(
2
), for instance a Robin type boundary condition modeling electrochemical reaction thanks to a Butler–Volmer type formula, see for instance
[
8
]
, or boundary conditions of mixed Dirichlet–Neumann type as in
[
31
]
.
In the presentation of the scheme, we assume for simplicity that the system is isolated, in the sense that

(6)

F_{i}\cdot n=0\quad\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\qquad i=1,\dots,I.

The system is finally complemented with initial conditions
u_{i}(t=0)=u_{i}^{0}
with

(7)

u_{i}^{0}\geq 0

and

\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}>0
for
i=0,\dots,I
and
\sum_{i=0}^{I}u_{i}^{0}=1
.

We then denote by

\mathcal{A}=\left\{U=\left(u_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{I}\;\middle|\;\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i}\leq 1\right\}

the set in which the volume fractions have to take their values.

1.2.
Entropy structure of the model

Let us now describe the entropy (or formal gradient flow) structure of the model.
Introduce the Slotboom variables
w_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{0}}e^{z_{i}\phi}
, then the fluxes (
2
) rewrite as

(8)

F_{i}=-D_{i}u_{0}^{2}e^{-z_{i}\phi}\nabla w_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,I.

If the so-called electrochemical potentials defined by
\mu_{i}=\log w_{i}=\log\frac{u_{i}}{u_{0}}+z_{i}\phi
are regular enough, say in
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then

\nabla\log w_{i}\cdot\nabla w_{i}=4|\nabla\sqrt{w_{i}}|^{2}.

Therefore, multiplying (
1
) by
\mu_{i}
,
integrating over
\Omega
and summing over
i=1,\dots,I
then yields

(9)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}+4\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{I}D_{i}u_{0}^{2}e^{-z_{i}\phi}|\nabla\sqrt{w_{i}}|^{2}=0,

where, denoting the mixing (neg)entropy density function
H:\mathcal{A}\to[-\log(I+1),+\infty)
by

(10)

H(U)=u_{0}\log(u_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i}\log(u_{i}),

with
u_{0}
seen as a function of
U
, the free energy
\mathcal{H}
is given by

(11)

\mathcal{H}=\int_{\Omega}H(U)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-\lambda^{2}\int_{\Gamma^{D}}\phi^{D}\nabla\phi\cdot n.

Assume that the
u_{i}
are positive for
i=0,\dots,I
(as proved in the discrete case later on), then the second term in (
9
) is well-defined and non-negative.
As a consequence, the free energy decays along-time, as a manifestation of the second principle of thermodynamics. Observe that
\mathcal{H}
need not be non-negative but may be bounded uniformly from below by a constant depending only on
\lambda
,
f
, and
\phi^{D}
.

1.3.
Weak solution

As we aim to prove rigorously the convergence of the scheme to be presented in Section
2
, we need a proper notion of solution for the continuous problem (
1
)–(
7
).
The volume fractions we seek are such that

(12)

0\leq u_{i}\leq 1
for
i=0,\dots,I
and a.e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega
.

The electric potential
\phi
then solves the Poisson equation (
4
) with a bounded right-hand side and boundary condition, so it satisfies

(13)

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)}+\|\phi-\phi^{D}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};V)}\leq C(\lambda,(z_{i})_{i},\Omega,f),

where

V=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\;\middle|\;v_{|_{\Gamma^{D}}}=0\right\}

is equipped with the
H^{1}(\Omega)
semi-norm
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}
, which is a norm thanks to the Poincaré inequality.
We have emphasized in the right-hand side of (
13
) the dependence of the bound on the data, especially on the Debye length
\lambda
.
The estimate (
9
) is the other main estimate on which our study will rely. It provides enough control
to define a proper notion of weak solutions, but at the price of a suitable reformulation of the fluxes (
2
).
Let us first remark that, since the free energy
\mathcal{H}
is bounded (see Lemma
3.7
), integrating
(
9
) over
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
provides

(14)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{I}D_{i}u_{0}^{2}e^{-z_{i}\phi}|\nabla\sqrt{w_{i}}|^{2}\leq C.

As already noticed in
[
31
]
, the above inequality yields a control in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};H^{1}(\Omega))
on
\sqrt{u_{0}}
and on
u_{0}
itself, as well as control on some product terms. More precisely, in the numerical analysis exposed hereafter, we derive a
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};H^{1}(\Omega))
estimate on
u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}
, which, together with (
12
) provides a
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};H^{1}(\Omega))
control on
u_{i}u_{0}
too. Hence, all the terms in the following expression of the fluxes

(15)

F_{i}=-D_{i}\left(\nabla(u_{0}u_{i})-4u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\sqrt{u_{0}}+u_{i}u_{0}z_{i}\nabla\phi\right),\qquad 1\leq i\leq I,

have a clear mathematical sense, motivating the following notion of weak solution.

Definition 1.1

(Weak solution)
.

(U,\phi)
is said to be a weak solution to (
1
)–(
7
) if

•

u_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)
with
U\in\mathcal{A}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega
, with moreover
\sqrt{u_{0}}
and
u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}
belonging to
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};H^{1}(\Omega))
for
u_{0}
defined as in (
3
);

•

\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)
with
\phi-\phi^{D}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};V)
satisfies

(16)

\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{2}\nabla\phi(t,x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i}(t,x)+f(x)\right)\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x

for all
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{N}})
and a.e.
t\geq 0
;

•

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\overline{\Omega})
and all
i=1,\dots,I
, there holds

(17)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega}u_{i}\partial_{t}\varphi+\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega}D_{i}\big(\nabla(u_{0}u_{i})-4u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}\nabla\sqrt{u_{0}}+u_{i}u_{0}z_{i}\nabla\phi\big)\cdot\nabla\varphi=0.

1.4.
Equilibrium and modified Poisson–Boltzmann equation

As the model has a gradient flow structure and is equipped with no-flux boundary conditions, its solution converges
towards a steady state
(u^{\infty},\phi^{\infty})
which correspond to vanishing fluxes
F_{i}^{\infty}
for all
i=1,\dots,I
.
It is suggested in
[
6
]
that the long-time limit profile
(u^{\infty},\phi^{\infty})
should correspond to constant in
space electrochemical potentials
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty}=\left(\mu_{i}^{\infty}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}
. Define the smooth functions
v_{i}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{I}\to(0,1)
by

(18)

v_{i}\left(y,\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)=\frac{e^{\xi_{i}-z_{i}y}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{I}e^{\xi_{j}-z_{j}y}}\quad\text{for $1\leq i\leq I$},\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0}\left(y,\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)=1-\sum_{j=1}^{I}v_{j}(y,\boldsymbol{\xi}),

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\left(\xi_{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
.
Then the volume fractions
U^{\infty}=\left(u_{i}^{\infty}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}
satisfy

(19)

u_{i}^{\infty}(x)=v_{i}(\phi^{\infty}(x),\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty}),\qquad i=0,\dots,I,\;x\in\Omega.

Plugging these expressions in the Poisson equation (
4
) provides what we call the
modified Poisson–Boltzmann equation
, i.e.

(20)

-\lambda^{2}\Delta\phi^{\infty}+r(\phi^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty})=f\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,

complemented with the same boundary conditions (
5
). In (
20
),
r
is the smooth function from
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{I}
to
\mathbb{R}
defined by

(21)

r(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})=-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}e^{\xi_{i}-z_{i}y}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{\xi_{i}-z_{i}y}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}v_{i}(y,\boldsymbol{\xi}).

To close the system, we still need to determine the real constants
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty}
. Their values are calculated to guarantee the conservation-of-total-mass constraints

(22)

\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{\infty}=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}.

By setting
z_{0}=0
, then one readily checks that since the
(v_{i})
by convexity

\partial_{y}r(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})=\sum_{i=0}^{I}z_{i}^{2}v_{i}(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{I}z_{i}v_{i}(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)^{2}\geq 0,

the last inequality being a consequence of the convexity of
y\mapsto y^{2}
as
\sum_{i=0}^{I}v_{i}(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})=1
.
Therefore,
r
is non-decreasing w.r.t. its first variable
y
. The monotonicity and boundedness of
r
allow us to conclude, via classical monotonicity arguments, the well-posedness of the problem (
20
)&(
5
).
Going one step further allows us to check that the solution
(\phi^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty})
is
a minimizer of the functional
\Psi:(\phi^{D}+V)\times\mathbb{R}^{I}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(23)

\Psi(y,\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}|\nabla y|^{2}+\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{\xi_{i}-z_{i}y}\right)-fy-\sum_{i=1}^{I}\xi_{i}u_{i}^{0}\right].

One readily checks that
\Psi
is strictly convex, and thus the minimiser
(\phi^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\infty})
is unique.
Moreover, one easily verifies that minimizers for
\Psi
are steady states of the system (
1
)–(
7
).
Despite hints in these directions, the uniqueness of the steady state is neither established in
[
6
]
, nor, to our knowledge, in more recent contributions. Furthermore, no quantitative
convergence result has been established so far. Non-quantitative results were however recently presented in
[
19
]
in the absence of
electrical interactions, i.e.
z_{i}=0
for all
i
.
We partially fill this gap in the discrete case. We rigorously establish in Section
5
the uniqueness of the steady states, which are minimisers of some discrete counterpart of the functional
\Psi
, cf. (
35
), and the (non-quantitative) convergence towards such a steady profile as time goes to
+\infty
.

1.5.
Goal and positioning of the paper

The goal of this paper is to provide a developed mathematical study of a numerical scheme that was proposed in our previous contribution
[
11
]
. It enters the broad family of the two-point flux approximation (TPFA) finite volume schemes
[
26
]
, which has been applied to several cross-diffusion problems in recent years (see e.g.
[
10
,
15
,
36
,
30
,
13
,
37
,
9
]
). Our scheme mixes ideas from the so-called square-root approximation (SQRA) finite volume scheme
[
12
]
(see also
[
38
,
33
]
) with some features of the Scharfetter–Gummel scheme
[
39
,
18
]
, especially the use of the Bernoulli function (
28
) in the definition of the fluxes. Note that a purely SQRA approach was also proposed in
[
11
]
, but since it is overpassed by the mixed approach both in terms of accuracy and of robustness in the small Debye length (or quasi-neutral) limit, we do not develop the analysis here. Let us however stress that our whole study extends to this full-SQRA scheme at the price of very minor modifications sketched in
[
11
]
.

As highlighted in
[
11
]
, our scheme fulfils key properties. First, it is second order accurate in space, in opposition to the scheme proposed in
[
7
]
in which upwinding leads to a mere first order consistency. Even though our scheme does not use the so-called entropy variables (the electrochemical potentials in our framework) as unknowns in opposition to the finite element scheme presented in
[
32
]
, our scheme satisfies a discrete energy dissipation inequality, which should be thought of as a discrete counterpart to (
9
), and which is key for its numerical analysis. Keeping conservative variables as primary variables yields well-behaved non-linear solvers (we used Newton–Raphson), allowing us to consider larger time steps than for the finite element methods of
[
32
]
or for the finite volume method of
[
2
]
. Together with the stability to be used for proving the convergence of the scheme, the discrete energy dissipation inequality implies that the scheme preserves exactly the steady states of the continuous model in the sense that, at the discrete level, fluxes vanish when concentrations are of the form (
19
). The unique steady state of our scheme is
then characterized only by the electric potential which solves a discrete counterpart of the
Poisson–Boltzmann equation (
20
), which can be computed directly in an efficient way.

In the present contribution, we first rigorously establish the convergence of the scheme when the discretization parameters (mesh size and time step) tend to
0
. As in the continuous setting
[
31
]
, the proof is based on compactness arguments. It strongly relies on the discrete counterpart of the energy dissipation estimate (
9
), following the boundedness-by-entropy method
[
35
]
, and more precisely a discrete version of it
[
16
,
37
]
. Uniqueness of the weak solutions is moreover established in
[
31
]
under moderate regularity assumptions thanks to the uniqueness technique of Gajewski
[
27
,
28
]
.

We also investigate the long-time behaviour of the numerical scheme. We show in particular that the discrete solution to our scheme for the evolution problem converges as time goes to infinity towards the unique solution to the discrete steady problem. As the proof relies on LaSalle’s invariance principle, no quantitative convergence rate is derived, similarly to what is proposed in
[
19
]
in the continuous setting but in the absence of electrical interactions. By establishing this result, we rigorously establish in the discrete setting the long-time behavior conjectured in
[
6
]
and recalled in Section
1.4
. Numerical simulations suggest that the convergence rate strongly depends on the Debye length, but also on the initial profile
U^{0}
(in opposition to the classical Poisson–Nernst–Planck problem without volume filling
[
3
]
).

2.
The finite volume scheme and main results

First, we introduce the time discretization and the spatial mesh of the domain
\Omega
.

2.1.
Space and time discretizations

The mesh will be assumed to be admissible in the sense of
[
26
]
, namely it fulfils the so-called
orthogonality condition
, which is usual for two-point flux approximation
finite volumes.

Let
\mathcal{T}
denote a family of non-empty, disjoint, convex, open, and polygonal
control volumes

K\in\mathcal{T}
, whose Lebesgue measure is denoted by
m_{K}
.
We also assume that control volumes partition the domain, meaning that
\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\overline{K}
.
Further, we call
\mathcal{E}
a
family of edges/faces
, where each
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
is a closed subset of
\overline{\Omega}
contained in a hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Each
\sigma
has a strictly positive
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff (or Lebesgue) measure, denoted by
m_{\sigma}
. We use the abbreviation
K|L=\partial K\cap\partial L
for the intersection between two distinct control volumes which is either empty or reduces to a face contained in
\mathcal{E}
. The subset of all interior faces is denoted by

\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}=\left\{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}\;\middle|\;\sigma=K|L\text{ for some }K,L\in\mathcal{T}\right\}.

For any
K\in\mathcal{T}
, we assume that there exists a subset
\mathcal{E}_{K}
of distinct elements of
\mathcal{E}
such that the boundary of a control volume can be described by
\partial K=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\sigma
and, consequently,
\mathcal{E}=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{E}_{K}
. Additionally, we assume that boundary edges
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}=\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}

are either subsets of
\Gamma^{D}
or
\Gamma^{N}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}^{D},\mathcal{E}^{N}\subset\mathcal{E}
the sets of boundary edges lying on
\Gamma^{D}
and
\Gamma^{N}
, respectively.
To each control volume
K\in\mathcal{T}
we assign a
cell center

{x}_{K}\in K
which satisfies the
orthogonality condition
: If
K,L
share a face
\sigma=K|L
, then the vector
x_{L}-x_{K}
is orthogonal to
\sigma=K|L
.
The triplet
\left(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{E},\{{x}_{K}\}_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\right)
is called an
admissible mesh
.

A classical way to construct such admissible mesh is to place some seeds
(x_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
in the domain
\Omega
and to define
the corresponding Voronoï  tessellation. As in practice the condition
x_{K}\in K
can be relaxed, but sill with the constraint
x_{L}-x_{K}
points outward with respect to
K
, another popular choice to build admissible meshes from a simplicial mesh satisfying the Delaunay condition is to choose
x_{K}
as the center of the circumcircle of the simplex
K
.

We introduce the notation
d_{\sigma}
for the Euclidean distance between
{x}_{K}
and
{x}_{L}
if
\sigma=K|L
or between
{x}_{K}
and the affine hyperplane spanned by
\sigma
if
\sigma\subset\partial{\Omega}
. We also denote by
d_{K\sigma}=\operatorname{dist}(x_{K},\sigma)
, so that
d_{\sigma}=d_{K\sigma}+d_{L\sigma}
if
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
and
d_{\sigma}=d_{K\sigma}
if
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}
.
The
transmittivity
of the edge
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
is defined by
{a_{\sigma}=\frac{m_{\sigma}}{d_{\sigma}}}
.
The size of the mesh is

h_{\mathcal{T}}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\operatorname{diam}(K)

where
\operatorname{diam}(K)
denotes the diameter of the cell
K
. The regularity of the mesh is defined by

\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\operatorname{card}\,\mathcal{E}_{K}\;;\;\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\frac{\operatorname{diam}(K)}{d_{K\sigma}}\right).

For the time discretization, we decompose the time interval
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:=[0,+\infty)
into an unbounded increasing sequence
\left(t^{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
with
t^{0}=0
and possibly non-uniform time steps

\tau^{n}=t^{n}-t^{n-1}>0,\qquad n>0.

We finally introduce
\Delta t=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\tau^{n}
, which we assume to be finite. As we consider the unbounded time interval
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we furthermore have to assume that

\inf_{n\geq 1}\tau^{n}>0.

2.2.
The finite volume schemes

We are now in position to define the finite volume scheme.
Let us start with the discretization of the Poisson equation (
4
)–(
5
), which relies on a classical two-point flux approximation

(24)

\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})=m_{K}\left(f_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i,K}^{n}\right),\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},

where
f_{K}
is (possibly an approximation of) the mean value of
f
on the cell
K
, and where

\phi_{K\sigma}^{n}=\begin{cases}\phi_{L}^{n}&\text{if}\;\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int},\\
{\phi_{K}^{n}}&{\text{if}\;\sigma\subset\Gamma^{N},}\\
\phi_{\sigma}^{D}=\frac{1}{m_{\sigma}}\int_{\sigma}\phi^{D}&\text{if}\;\sigma\subset\Gamma^{D}.\end{cases}

The equation (
1
) is discretized using a backward Euler method in time and finite volumes in space, leading to

(25)

\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1}}{\tau^{n}}m_{K}+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,I,\quad K\in\mathcal{T}.

In accordance with (
6
), we set
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=0
if
\sigma\subset\partial\Omega
. For
\sigma=K|L
an internal edge, then we define

(26)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=a_{\sigma}D_{i}\left(u^{n}_{i,K}u^{n}_{0,L}\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi_{L}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n})\right)-u^{n}_{i,L}u^{n}_{0,K}\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi^{n}_{K}-\phi^{n}_{L})\right)\right),

with

(27)

u_{0,K}^{n}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i,K}^{n},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T}.

Formula (
26
) involves a function
\mathfrak{B}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
which is (strictly) positive and satisfies
\mathfrak{B}(0)=1
and
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}(0)=-1/2
. The SQRA scheme studied in
[
11
]
corresponds to the choice
\mathfrak{B}(y)=e^{-y/2}
. Even if our analysis extends to this case without any particular difficulty, for the sake of readability we will focus here on the scheme referred to as the SG scheme in
[
11
]
, for which
\mathfrak{B}
is the Bernoulli function

(28)

\mathfrak{B}(y)=\frac{y}{e^{y}-1},

and which shows a better behavior (accuracy and robustness) with respect to the SQRA scheme. We refer to
[
11
]
for a comparison of the two approaches.

We would like to emphasize that the construction of the scheme which we will refer to as the SG scheme is not based on the original idea of
[
39
]
. Rather, we take advantage of the free-energy diminishing character of the SG scheme highlighted in
[
18
]
and exploited in
[
4
,
40
]
.

In order to close the system, it remains to define the discrete counterpart of
u^{0}
as follows:

(29)

u_{i,K}^{0}=\frac{1}{m_{K}}\int_{K}u_{i}^{0},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},\;i=0,\dots,I.

Then we infer from (
7
) that

(30)

\sum_{i=0}^{I}u_{i,K}^{0}=1\;\text{for all $K\in\mathcal{T}$},\;\text{and}\;\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{0}m_{K}=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}>0\quad\text{for $i=0,\dots,I$}.

In what follows, we denote by
U_{K}^{n}=\left(u_{i,K}^{n}\right)_{i=1,\dots,I}
for
K\in\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 0
.

The consistency of the discrete fluxes (
26
) with the continuous ones (
2
) might not look completely obvious.
It follows from the identity

(31)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=a_{\sigma}\,D_{i}\,u_{0,K}^{n}\,u_{0,L}^{n}\mathfrak{M}(e^{-z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}},e^{-z_{i}\phi_{L}^{n}})\left(w_{i,K}^{n}-w_{i,L}^{n}\right)

where
w^{n}_{i,K}=\frac{u^{n}_{i,K}}{u^{n}_{0,K}}e^{z_{i}\phi^{n}_{K}}
are the discrete counterpart of the Slotboom variables and, by assumption,
U^{n}_{K}\in(0,1)^{I}
. Later, in Proposition
3.2
, this bound will be rigorously established.
The mean function
\mathfrak{M}
appearing in (
31
) is defined by

\mathfrak{M}(a,b)=\frac{\log(1/a)-\log(1/b)}{1/a-1/b}\quad\text{for $a,b>0$ with $a\neq b$, and $\mathfrak{M}(a,a)=a$ for $a>0$}.

We refer to
[
34
]
for an extensive discussion on the influence of the choice of the Stolarsky mean
\mathfrak{M}
on the scheme behavior. The consistency of the formula (
31
) with the expression (
8
) of the continuous flux is now clear, and assuming the existence of a regular solution to the continuous problem, inserting it in the scheme and performing Taylor expansions as in
[
12
]
shows the second order accuracy in space of our scheme on uniform Cartesian grids.

2.3.
Main results and organization of the paper

We provide here simple presentations of our main results, which will be detailed and proven in the next sections.
As already mentioned, the goals of this contribution are twofold. It first aims to complement the elements of analysis sketched in the contribution
[
11
]
, by showing existence of discrete solutions and convergence of the scheme. Secondly it intends to investigate the long-time behavior of the scheme.

Our first main result concerns the well-posedness of the scheme.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given an admissible mesh
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{E},(x_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}})
of
\Omega
and a sequence of time steps
\left(\tau^{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
as in Section
2.1
, then there exists (at least) one solution to the scheme (
24
)–(
29
) which satisfies

0<u_{i,K}^{n}<1\qquad\forall i=0,\dots,I,\;K\in\mathcal{T},\;n\geq 1.

Moreover, the discrete free energy
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
defined later on in (
41
) is decaying along the time iterations

(32)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}+\tau^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\leq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n-1},\qquad n\geq 1,

for some dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\geq 0
vanishing if and only if

\left(\left(U_{K}^{n}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}},\left(\phi_{K}^{n}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\right)
is the stationary solution to the scheme.

Theorem
2.1
states that, at fixed mesh and time step, the numerical scheme admits a positive solution and preserves the
L^{\infty}
-bounds. Moreover, the inequality (
32
) is a discrete counterpart to (
9
). The discrete version
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
of the free energy functional (
11
) is rigorously defined in formula (
41
). Schemes that verify (
32
) in addition to more classical properties such as mass conservation or positivity preservation are often referred to in the literature as thermodynamically consistent or structure-preserving. Our scheme, therefore, falls into this class of schemes.

Theorem
2.1
allows to define the so-called approximate solution to the problem, which consists of functions
U_{\mathcal{T},\tau}=\left(u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}
and
\phi_{\mathcal{T},\tau}
, piecewise constant in both space and time, defined by

(33)

u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}(t,x)=u_{i,K}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{\mathcal{T},\tau}(t,x)=\phi_{K}^{n}\quad\text{if}\;(t,x)\in K\times(t^{n-1},t^{n}].

Here again, we reconstruct
u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}
from
U_{\mathcal{T},\tau}
by setting

(34)

u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}.

Now, let
\left(\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\mathcal{E}_{\ell},\{{x}_{K}\}_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\right)_{\ell\geq 1}
and
\left(\tau_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\geq 1}=\left(\left(\tau^{n}_{\ell}\right)_{n\geq 1}\right)_{\ell\geq 1}

be respectively a sequence of admissible meshes and a sequence of time steps, in the sense of Section
2.1
, such that

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}h_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\Delta t_{\ell}=0,\quad\quad\Delta t_{\ell}=\max_{n}\tau^{n}_{\ell},

while the mesh regularity factor
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}
remains uniformly bounded w.r.t.
\ell
, i.e.
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}\leq\zeta_{\star}<+\infty.

The convergence of our scheme can be stated in a very simplified way as follows:

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a weak solution
(U,\phi)
in the sense of Definition
1.1
such that, up to the extraction of a subsequence,

\phi_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\phi\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0,\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}u_{0}\quad\text{in}\;L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};L^{p}(\Omega))\quad\forall p\in[1,+\infty),

and

U_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\tau_{\ell}}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}U\quad\text{in the }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)^{I}\text{ weak-$\star$ sense}.

More convergence properties have to be established to prove Theorem
2.2
.
They are detailed in Section
4
, complementing the preliminary presentation of
[
11
]
.

Our last main theoretical result is about the long-time asymptotic of the scheme for a fixed mesh and sequence of time steps. We denote by
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{T}}\times\mathbb{R}^{I}\to\mathbb{R}
the functional defined by

(35)

\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}(y_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(y_{K}-y_{K\sigma})^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{\xi_{i}-z_{i}{y}_{K}}\right)\\
-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}\left[f_{K}{y}_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\xi_{i}u_{i,K}^{0}\right].

In (
35
), we made a slight abuse of notation as we denote by
{y}_{\mathcal{T}}=\left({y}_{K}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
the element of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{T}}
, and the mirror values
{y}_{K\sigma}
are defined by

{y}_{K\sigma}=\begin{cases}{y}_{L}&\text{if}\;\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int},\\
{y}_{K}&\text{if}\;\sigma\subset\Gamma^{N},\\
\phi_{\sigma}^{D}&\text{if}\;\sigma\subset\Gamma^{D}.\end{cases}

Theorem 2.3
.

The functional
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
is strictly convex and admits a unique minimizer denoted by
\left(({\phi_{K}^{\infty})}_{K\in\mathcal{T}},{(\mu_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})}_{1\leq i\leq I}\right)
. Moreover, one has

\phi_{K}^{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\phi_{K}^{\infty}\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T},

and

u_{i,K}^{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}v_{i}(\phi_{K}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T},\;0\leq i\leq I.

We deduce from this theorem an efficient way to compute the long-time behavior of the scheme, which consists in seeking for
\left(({\phi_{K}^{\infty})}_{K\in\mathcal{T}},{(\mu_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})}_{1\leq i\leq I}\right)
belonging to
\operatorname{argmin}\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
and to a posteriori reconstruct the volume fraction profiles thanks to the functions
v_{i}
defined by (
18
).
We also remark that the uniqueness of the long-time limit is established in the discrete setting, while the question remains open in the continuous setting. Conjecturing the uniqueness of such an asymptotic limit for the continuous model, which then has to be the unique minimizer of
\Psi
defined by (
23
), then Theorem
2.3
illustrates in yet another aspect the structure-preserving nature of our scheme.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, as well as to some post-treatment of the energy / energy dissipation inequality (
32
) in order to derive some uniform bounds on quantities to be used in the convergence analysis carried out in Section
4
, where the proof of Theorem
2.2
is detailed. Our last main theoretical result Theorem
2.3
is proved in Section
5
.
The numerical results presented in Section
6
contain some numerical evidence of the convergence of the scheme. They also illustrate that the situation is more complex than what Theorem
2.3
suggests at first glance. Although the approximate volume fractions are (strictly) positive as pointed out in Theorem
2.1
,
u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}
can take very small values in some parts of the domain.
The discrete fluxes (
26
) then take very small values as well, making the evolution extremely slow.
As the linear and non-linear systems corresponding to the scheme are merely solved approximately, one furthermore has to pay a particular attention to preserve the positivity at the discrete level.

3.
Uniform a priori bounds and existence of a discrete solution

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
2.1
, i.e. to show that the non-linear system corresponding to the scheme (
24
)–(
27
) admits at least one solution, and that beyond local conservativity, this solution preserves at the discrete level some key features of the model, namely the positivity of the volume fractions, as well as uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds for them, and the decay of the free energy. Furthermore, in this part, precise quantification of the dissipated total free energy supplies enough uniform estimates, in the mesh size
h_{\mathcal{T}}
and time steps
\left(\tau_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
, to perform the convergence analysis in Section
4
.
The grid
\mathcal{T}
and the time steps
\left(\tau^{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
remain fixed throughout this section.

3.1.
Uniform a priori bounds and existence of a discrete solution

The first Lemma regards the discrete counterpart of the conservation of the total mass of the system.

Lemma 3.1

(Mass conservation)
.

It holds that

(36)

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{n}m_{K}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{n-1}m_{K}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{0}m_{K}=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}>0,\qquad 0\leq i\leq I.

Proof.

Since our scheme is locally conservative, i.e.,
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}+F_{i,L\sigma}^{n}=0
for all
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
,
i=1,\dots,I
, then summing (
25
) over
K
shows by induction and thanks to (
29
) the result.
∎

Since we are interested in discrete solutions with positive volume fractions
u_{i,K}^{n}
, we perform an eventually harmless modification of the flux formula (
26
) into

(37)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=a_{\sigma}D_{i}\left(\left(u^{n}_{i,K}\right)^{+}\!\left(u^{n}_{0,L}\right)^{+}\!\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi_{L}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n})\right)-\left(u^{n}_{i,L}\right)^{+}\!\left(u^{n}_{0,K}\right)^{+}\!\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\right)\right).

Proposition 3.2

(Positivity of volume fractions)
.

Let
n\geq 1
, and let
\left(U_{K}^{n-1}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
be such that

(38)

u_{i,K}^{n-1}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{i=0}^{I}u_{i,K}^{n-1}=1\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{n-1}m_{K}>0.

Then any solution
\left(U_{K}^{n},\phi_{K}^{n}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T},n\geq 1}
to the modified scheme with (
37
) instead of (
26
) satisfies
u_{i,K}^{n}>0
for all
i=0,\dots,I
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Let us start by establishing the positivity of
u_{0,K}^{n}
. Assume for contradiction that there exists a cell
K\in\mathcal{T}
such that
u_{0,K}^{n}\leq 0
. Then we deduce from formula (
37
) that
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\geq 0
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
and all
i=1,\dots,I
. Because of (
27
) and (
38
), this implies that

0\geq u_{0,K}^{n}=u_{0,K}^{n-1}+\frac{\tau^{n}}{m_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\geq 0.

In particular, all the fluxes
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}
,
i=1,\dots,I
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
, are equal to
0
. In view of formula (
37
) and of the strict positivity of
\mathfrak{B}
, this implies either that
u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 0
for all
i
, which yields a contradiction with (
27
), or that
u_{0,L}^{n}\leq 0
for all the cells
L
sharing an edge
\sigma=K|L
with
K
. Since
\Omega
is connected, one would obtain that
u_{0,K}^{n}=0
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and thus that
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{0,K}^{n}m_{K}=0
. This contradicts (
36
), and thus we necessarily have that
u_{0,K}^{n}>0
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
.

With the positivity of
u_{0,K}^{n}
,
K\in\mathcal{T}
, at hand, let us focus on the
u_{i,K}^{n}
for an arbitrary
i=1,\dots,I
. Similarly, we assume that there exists some
K\in\mathcal{T}
such that
u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 0
. Then owing to (
37
), we infer that
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\leq 0
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
, and then that

0\geq u_{i,K}^{n}=u_{i,K}^{n-1}-\frac{\tau^{n}}{m_{K}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\geq 0.

This leads to
u_{i,K}^{n}=0
and to
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=0
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
.
Since we already know that
u_{0,K}^{n}>0
, we deduce from (
37
) that
u_{i,L}^{n}\leq 0
for all cells
L
sharing a face
\sigma=K|L
with
K
. As above, this implies as
u_{0,K}^{n}=0
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
, which contradicts (
36
). Then
u_{i,K}^{n}>0
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
, concluding the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

A consequence of the previous proposition is that a solution to the modified scheme with (
37
) instead of (
26
) is also a solution to the original scheme (
24
)–(
27
).

As we did assume that the background charge density
f
and thus its discrete counterpart
\left(f_{K}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
are uniformly bounded, and that
\phi^{D}
belongs to
L^{\infty}\cap H^{1/2}(\Gamma^{D})
,
we deduce
a uniform bound for the electric potential.

Proposition 3.3

(Uniform bound for the electric potential)
.

There exists
C=C(\phi^{D},\Omega,\lambda,f,{(z_{i})}_{i},\zeta_{\mathcal{T}})
such that

(39)

\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}|\phi_{K}^{n}|+\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C,\qquad n\geq 1.

The proof of this result can be found in
[
14
, Proposition A.1]
and is a consequence of the uniform boundedness of the right-hand side of the discrete Poisson equation (
24
).

These a priori estimates are sufficient to prove the existence of a solution to the scheme.
We end up with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4

(Existence of solutions)
.

There exists at least one solution to the numerical scheme (
24
)–(
27
) such that

u_{i,K}^{n}>0
for all
i=0,\dots,I
, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

The proof is based on an inductive procedure in time. At each time step
n\geq 1
, we use a topological degree argument to show the existence of a solution
(U^{n}_{K},\phi^{n}_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\in(0,1)^{I\times\mathcal{T}}\times[-C,C]^{\mathcal{T}}
to (
24
)–(
25
), with the fluxes defined as in (
37
). Because of the positivity of the discrete volume fractions stated in Proposition
3.2
and to the equivalence between
(
37
) and (
26
) for such positive volume fractions, this ensures the existence for the original scheme (
24
)–(
26
).

Let
n\geq 1
be such that
(U^{n-1}_{K},\phi^{n-1}_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\in[0,1]^{I\times\mathcal{T}}\times[-C,C]^{\mathcal{T}}
is given (that is the case for
n=1
, thanks to (
29
) and (
7
)).
The idea is to deform our non-linear system continuously until it is transformed into a linear one with known solutions. Therefore, let introduce a parameter
s\in[0,\tau^{n}]
and define, for every
K\in\mathcal{T}
,

(40)

\begin{cases}m_{K}\left(u_{i,K}^{(s)}-u_{i,K}^{n-1}\right)+s\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{(s)}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,I,\\[6.0pt]
\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}\left(\phi_{K}^{(s)}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{(s)}\right)=m_{K}\left(f_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i,K}^{(s)}\right),\end{cases}

with
F_{i,K\sigma}^{(s)}
defined by (
37
), which corresponds to the original schemes when
s=\tau^{n}
.
For
s=0
, the two equations can be decoupled and the first one can be rewritten in matrix form

\mathbb{M}(U^{(0)}-U^{n-1})=0,

where
U^{(s)}=\left(U^{(s)}_{K}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
, for
s\in[0,\tau^{n}]
,
U^{n-1}=\left(U^{n-1}_{K}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
and
\mathbb{M}=\text{diag}\left((m_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\right)
is a positive definite matrix. Therefore, there exists a unique solution
U^{(0)}=U^{n-1}\in[0,1]^{I\times\mathcal{T}}
. Via the Proposition
3.3
, there also exists a unique
\phi^{(s)}=\left(\phi^{(s)}_{K}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\in[-C,C]^{\mathcal{T}}
. Moreover, thanks to the continuity of the discrete fluxes, the functional

\mathcal{S}\;:\;\begin{cases}[0,\tau^{n}]\times[-2,2]^{I\times\mathcal{T}}\times[-C-1,C+1]^{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{T}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{T}}\\
(s,U,\phi)\mapsto\mathcal{S}(s,U,\phi)\end{cases}

whose zeros
(s,U^{(s)},\phi^{(s)})
are the solutions of (
40
), is hence an homotopy. Furthermore, all along it, its zeros
(U^{(s)},\phi^{(s)})
remain inside the compact subset
[0,1]^{I\times\mathcal{T}}\times[-C,C]^{\mathcal{T}}
.

Thus, the topological degree corresponding to
\mathcal{S}(s,U,\phi)=0
is equal to one, all along the homotopy, and hence in particular for
s=\tau^{n}
. That implies the existence of a solution to the scheme (but it does not guarantee uniqueness).
∎

We note that the proof proposed here is simpler than that found in, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 3.2]
. The reason lies in the fact that we did not use an entropy inequality to derive the uniform estimates on volume fractions and, therefore, we do not have to guarantee the validity of it during the homotopy.

Remark 3.5

(On the uniqueness of solutions)
.

The uniqueness of weak solutions for (
1
)–(
7
), as well as for numerical schemes found in the literature, remains largely unresolved. This challenge is inherent to the cross-diffusion structure of the problem. In
[
31
]
, uniqueness is established under strong assumptions on the diffusion coefficients and valences, specifically
D_{i}\equiv D
and
z_{i}\equiv z
for all
i=1,\dots,I
.

For the numerical scheme (
25
)–(
29
), by following the argument in
[
7
, Section 3.2, Uniqueness of solutions]
, uniqueness of discrete solutions can also be shown under the same assumptions: constant diffusion coefficients
D_{i}\equiv D
and the absence of a drift term in the fluxes. The assumption of constant diffusion coefficients enables a preliminary proof of uniqueness for the discrete variable
u_{0}
. In our scheme, since no drift term is present and
\mathfrak{B}(0)=1
, the function
\mathfrak{B}
does not appear in the definition of the fluxes. Consequently, the equation for the discrete
u_{0}
is significantly simpler than that in
[
7
]
, and uniqueness of
u_{0}
follows straightforwardly. The absence of a drift term also allows the application of Gajewski’s entropy method to deduce uniqueness for the remaining components
(u_{i})_{1\leq i\leq I}
, as in
[
7
]
.

3.2.
Energy dissipation at the discrete level

The next proposition is about the thermodynamical consistency of our scheme and the decay of a discrete counterpart of the free energy.

Proposition 3.6

(Free energy decay)
.

Let
\left(U_{K}^{n},\phi_{K}^{n}\right)_{K\in\mathcal{T},n\geq 1}
be a solution to the scheme (
24
)–(
27
) as in Proposition
3.4
, then define for
n\geq 0
the discrete free energy at the
n^{\text{th}}
time step

(41)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}H(U_{K}^{n})+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})^{2}-\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}^{D}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{n}),

the discrete electrochemical potentials
\mu_{i,K}^{n}=\log\left(\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}}{u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)+z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}
of species
i
,
and

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\mu_{i,K}^{n}-\mu_{i,L}^{n})

the discrete dissipation, which vanishes if and only if
{(\mu_{i,K}^{n})}_{K\in\mathcal{T}}
is constant for all
i=1,\dots,I
.
Then there holds

(42)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}+\tau^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\leq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n-1},\qquad n\geq 1.

Proof.

As a consequence of the positivity of
u_{0,K}^{n}
and of the monotonicity of the exponential function, one then easily infers from reformulation (
31
) of the discrete fluxes that

(43)

\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}:=F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\mu_{i,K}^{n}-\mu_{i,L}^{n})\geq 0,\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,I,\;\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{{\rm int}},

whence the non-negativity of
\mathcal{D}^{n}
. As
u_{0,K}^{n}>0
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 1
, and as
y\mapsto e^{y}
is strictly increasing, one gets that
\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}=0
iff
\mu_{i,K}^{n}=\mu_{i,L}^{n}
.

To proceed, we first define by
\mu_{i,K}^{n}=\log\left(\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}}{u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)+z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}=\log(w_{i,K}^{n})
the electrochemical potential of species
i
, then we multiply
the discrete conservation law (
25
) by
\tau^{n}\mu_{i,K}^{n}
and sum over
i=1,\dots,I
and
K\in\mathcal{T}
. Thanks to the local conservativity of the fluxes, i.e.,
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}+F_{i,L\sigma}^{n}=0
for all
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
, and to the no-flux boundary condition
F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=0
for
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}
,
i=1,\dots,I
, a discrete integration by parts in space allows the flux terms to be rewritten as a sum over interior edges only, namely
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\mu_{i,K}^{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}
for all
i=1,\dots,I
. This leads to the identity

(44)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}+\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}+\tau^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=0.

where we have set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1}\right)\log\left(\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}}{u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)m_{K}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:u0Kn}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{I}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1}\right)\frac{\partial\widetilde{H}}{\partial u_{i}}(u_{0,K}^{n}\dots,u_{I,K}^{n})m_{K},

where
\widetilde{H}:\left\{(u_{i})_{0\leq i\leq I}\;\middle|\;\sum_{i=0}^{I}u_{i}=1\right\}\to[-\log(I+1),+\infty)
, defined by

\widetilde{H}(u_{0},\dots,u_{I}):=\sum_{i=0}^{I}u_{i}\log(u_{i}),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1}\right)z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}m_{K}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:scheme.Poisson}}{=}

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{K}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n-1}-(\phi_{K\sigma}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n-1})\right).

From the convexity of
\widetilde{H}
and the identity
\widetilde{H}(u_{0},\dots,u_{I})=H(U)
, we deduce that

(45)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\geq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(H(U_{K}^{n})-H(U_{K}^{n-1})\right)m_{K},

while the term
\mathcal{B}^{n}_{\mathcal{T}}
can be rewritten by observing that each interior edge is counted twice in the sum
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}
. The resulting equivalent reformulation is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}=

\displaystyle\;\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n-1}-(\phi_{K\sigma}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n-1})\right)(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxxx}\;+\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}^{D}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n-1}),

which is valid since boundary edges
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{N}
do not contribute to the first sum, and the contributions from boundary edges
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}
cancel with the second part of the new expression of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
.
Then using the elementary convexity inequality
a(a-b)\geq(a^{2}-b^{2})/2
in the above term and combining the result with (
45
) in (
44
) provides the desired result (
42
).
∎

Proposition
3.6
allows us to complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
, but it also contains important information for proving the convergence of the scheme. It is the purpose of the next section to extract this information.

3.3.
Further uniform estimates on the discrete solution

To pass to the limit in the scheme and to identify the limit as a weak solution, we need to extract some further uniform estimates, in particular the discrete
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(H^{1})
estimates on the discrete counterparts of
u_{0}
and
\sqrt{u_{i}u_{0}}
. We prove these estimates in Lemma
3.8
. As an intermediate result we need a uniform bound on the discrete free energy.

Lemma 3.7

(Uniform bound for the free energy)
.

There exists
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
\phi^{D}
,
\lambda
,
f
,
{(z_{i})}_{i}
, and
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}
such that, for all
N\geq 1
, there holds

|\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{N}|\leq C
.

Proof.

Because of the bound
0\leq u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 1
for all
i
and
K
, it is clear that the first two contributions of (
41
) remain uniformly bounded by a constant depending only on
\Omega
. Indeed, for the first term, this is a direct consequence of the definition (
10
) of
H
, whereas the second term can be controlled thanks to Proposition
3.3
. Concerning the last contribution observe that if one defines
\phi_{K}^{D}
and
\phi_{\sigma}^{D}
as the averages of
\phi^{D}
on
K\in\mathcal{T}
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
respectively, then a suitable reorganization of the sum shows that

(46)

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}^{D}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{\sigma}^{D})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{D})(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{K}^{D}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:scheme.Poisson}}{=}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{D})(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}\phi_{K}^{D}\left(f_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i,K}^{n}\right).

Using Young’s inequality and
\mathcal{E}^{D}\subset\mathcal{E}
for the first term in the above right-hand side gives

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{D})(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{D})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})^{2}.

Then Lemma 9.4 of
[
26
]
shows that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{\sigma}^{D}-\phi_{K}^{D})^{2}\leq C(\zeta_{\mathcal{T}})\|\nabla\phi^{D}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Then since
f
and
U
are bounded, we deduce from (
46
) that there exists
C
depending only on

\|f\|_{\infty}
,
\max_{i}|z_{i}|
,
\Omega
,
\|\phi^{D}\|_{H^{1}}
,
\lambda
, and
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{D}}a_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}^{D}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{\sigma}^{D})\leq C.

The result of the lemma follows.
∎

The next lemma shows that the control of the energy dissipation
\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
inferred from Proposition
3.6
gives some
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(H^{1})
type control on the discrete counterparts of
u_{0}
,
\sqrt{u_{0}}
, and
\sqrt{u_{i}u_{0}}
.

Lemma 3.8

(Uniform bounds on
u_{0}
,
\sqrt{u_{0}}
, and
\sqrt{u_{i}u_{0}}
)
.

There exists
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
\phi^{D}
,
\lambda
,
f
,
{(z_{i})}_{i}
,
{(D_{i})}_{i}
, and
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}
such that, for all
N\geq 1
, there holds

(47)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\\
+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\\
+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{0,L}^{n}\right)^{2}\leq C(1+t^{N}).

As a consequence, one also has

(48)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-u_{i,L}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\leq C(1+t^{N})

and

(49)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}\right)^{2}\leq C(1+t^{N}).

Proof.

One gets from the elementary inequality
(a-b)(\log(a)-\log(b))\geq 4(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}
applied to (
43
) that

\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}\geq 4a_{\sigma}D_{i}\mathfrak{R}(e^{-z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}},e^{-z_{i}\phi_{L}^{n}})\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}e^{\frac{z_{i}}{4}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}e^{\frac{z_{i}}{4}(\phi_{L}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n})}\right)^{2}

with
\mathfrak{R}(e^{-z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}},e^{-z_{i}\phi_{L}^{n}})=\mathfrak{M}(e^{-z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}},e^{-z_{i}\phi_{L}^{n}})e^{\frac{z_{i}}{2}(\phi_{K}^{n}+\phi_{L}^{n})}
.
Thanks to (
39
) and since
D_{i}>0
for all
i
, there holds

2D_{i}\mathfrak{R}(e^{-z_{i}\phi_{K}^{n}},e^{-z_{i}\phi_{L}^{n}})\geq\kappa

for some
\kappa>0
uniform w.r.t.
K
,
i
, and
n
. As a consequence, using furthermore that
(a+b)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}-b^{2}
,

\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}\geq\kappa a_{\sigma}\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{4}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\right)\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)^{2}\\
-2\kappa a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}+\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)^{2}\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{4}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\right).

Since
|\phi_{K}^{n}|\leq C
owing to (
39
), one has
\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{4}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\right)\leq C(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})^{2}
. Using moreover that
0<u_{i,K}^{n},u_{0,K}^{n}<1
and that
\cosh(a)\geq 1
, one gets that

\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}\geq a_{\sigma}\kappa\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)^{2}-Ca_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})^{2}.

Since

(50)

\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}\right)^{2}=\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}-(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n})(u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{0,L}^{n}),

then summing over
i=1,\dots,I
and
\sigma=K|L
and using (
27
) leads to

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\geq\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\\
+\kappa\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{0,L}^{n}\right)^{2}-C\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{n})^{2}.

Bearing in mind Proposition
3.3
, we obtain that

(51)

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\geq\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}+\kappa\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{0,L}^{n}\right)^{2}-C.

Moreover,
since
\sqrt{ab}\leq(a+b)/2
for all
a,b\geq 0
, there holds

\sum^{I}_{i=0}\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{i,L}^{n}}\leq\sum^{I}_{i=0}\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}+u_{i,L}^{n}}{2}=1,\qquad\forall\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{int}}.

This provides

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\\
\geq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left((1-u_{0,K}^{n})u_{0,K}^{n}+(1-u_{0,L}^{n})u_{0,L}^{n}-2(1-\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}})\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)\\
=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left({u_{0,K}^{n}}-{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2},

whence we also deduce that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\geq\kappa\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}-C.

To deduce (
47
), it eventually remains to remark from (
42
) and Lemma
3.7
that there exists
C
depending on none of
h
,
\Delta t
,
N
nor on the initial data
U^{0}=\left(u_{i}^{0}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}
(provided it fulfills (
7
)) such that
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\leq C.
Combining this with (
51
) yields (
47
).

To recover estimate (
48
), remark that

\displaystyle\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}}u^{n}_{i,K}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}}u^{n}_{i,L}\Big)=\Bigg[

\displaystyle\sqrt{u^{n}_{i,K}}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+u^{n}_{i,L}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)\Bigg].

As
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})
, and since
0\leq u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 1
, one gets that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}}u^{n}_{i,K}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}}u^{n}_{i,L}\Big)^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)^{2}.

The two first terms in the right-hand side are controlled thanks to (
47
), while, bearing in mind (
50
), the third term can be overestimated as follows:

\displaystyle\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)^{2}\leq\;

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Big(\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,K}u^{n}_{i,K}}-\sqrt{u^{n}_{0,L}u^{n}_{i,L}}\Big)^{2}+(u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{0,L}^{n})^{2},

which again, after summation on
n=1,\dots,N
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
, can be controlled thanks to (
47
).

Finally, to establish (
49
), remark that

u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}=\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}+\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right).

Since
0\leq u_{0,K}^{n},u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 1
, one gets that

\left(u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}\right)^{2}\leq 4\left(\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}.

Summing over
n=1,\dots,N
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
and using (
47
) gives the desired result.

estimates on the fluxes, which amount to some discrete
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(H^{1})^{\prime}
estimate on time increments of the discrete counterpart to

.

Lemma 3.9

(Uniform bounds on the fluxes)
.

There exists
C
depending only on
\Omega
,
\phi^{D}
,
\lambda
,
f
,
{(z_{i})}_{i}
,
{(D_{i})}_{i}
, and
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

(52)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}\frac{d_{\sigma}}{m_{\sigma}}\left|F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\right|^{2}\leq C(1+t^{N}).

Proof.

To obtain discrete
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(L^{2})^{d}
estimates on the fluxes, we need to exploit the discrete uniform estimates we have for
(U_{K}^{n},\phi_{K}^{n})_{K\in\mathcal{T},n\geq 1}.
In this regard, we rewrite the fluxes in a different way before taking advantage of some properties of the Bernoulli function (
28
).
One splits the flux (
26
) into two parts corresponding to convection and diffusion respectively:

F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}=F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{conv},n}+F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n},

with

\displaystyle F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{conv},n}=\;

\displaystyle a_{\sigma}D_{i}\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}+u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}{2}\left[\mathfrak{B}\big(z_{i}(\phi_{L}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n})\big)-\mathfrak{B}\big(z_{i}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\big)\right],

\displaystyle F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n}=\;

\displaystyle a_{\sigma}D_{i}\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}{2}\left[\mathfrak{B}\big(z_{i}(\phi_{L}^{n}-\phi_{K}^{n})\big)+\mathfrak{B}\big(z_{i}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n})\big)\right].

The flux
(F_{i,K\sigma}^{n})_{\sigma,n}
is bounded in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(L^{2})^{d}
in the sense of (
52
) if both
(F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{conv},n})_{\sigma,n}
and
(F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n})_{\sigma,n}
are.

As
\mathfrak{B}(-y)-\mathfrak{B}(y)=y
, one gets that

(53)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{conv},n}=a_{\sigma}D_{i}\frac{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}+u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}{2}z_{i}(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n}).

The
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(L^{2})^{d}
character of the above expression directly follows from the uniform bound on
u_{i,K}^{n}
,
0\leq i\leq I
and from the discrete
L^{\infty}(H^{1})
bound on
(\phi_{K}^{n})_{K,n}
stated in Proposition
3.3
.
Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}\frac{d_{\sigma}}{m_{\sigma}}\left|F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{conv},n}\right|^{2}\leq Ct^{N}.

Concerning the diffusive term, as
2\leq\mathfrak{B}(y)+\mathfrak{B}(-y)=y\coth\frac{y}{2}\leq 2+\frac{y^{2}}{6}
, one has that

(54)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n}=a_{\sigma}D_{i}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}\right)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n}\right)^{2}\right)\right).

From the discrete
L^{\infty}(H^{1})
bound on
(\phi_{K}^{n})_{K,n}
, one can uniformly estimate the remainder.
For the other term,
since

u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}=u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}+(u_{i,K}^{n}+u_{i,L}^{n})(u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{0,K}^{n}),

then

\displaystyle|u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(|u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}|^{2}+|(u_{i,K}^{n}+u_{i,L}^{n})(u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{0,K}^{n})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left|\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right|^{2}+|u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{0,K}^{n}|^{2}\right),

so that we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}\frac{d_{\sigma}}{m_{\sigma}}\left|F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left|\sqrt{u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}|u_{0,L}^{n}-u_{0,K}^{n}|^{2},

thanks to the uniform bounds on
(U^{n}_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T},n\leq 1}
.
Lemma
3.8
provides the desired
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(L^{2})
bound on
F_{i,K\sigma}^{\text{diff},n}
, concluding the
proof of Lemma
3.9
.

From Lemma
3.9
, we deduce the following discrete
L^{2}_{\rm loc}((H^{1})^{\prime})
estimate:

Corollary 3.10
.

There exists
C
depending only on
\Omega
,
\phi^{D}
,
\lambda
,
f
,
{(z_{i})}_{i}
,
{(D_{i})}_{i}
, and
\zeta_{\mathcal{T}}
such that,
for all
i=0,\dots,I
and all
\varphi_{\mathcal{T},\tau}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{K}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{(t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times K}
, one has

(55)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1})\varphi_{K}^{n}\leq C\left(1+t^{N}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(\varphi_{K}^{n}-\varphi_{K\sigma}^{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let us first establish (
55
) for
i\geq 1
. Multiplying (
25
) by
\tau^{n}\varphi_{K}^{n}
, summing over
K\in\mathcal{T}
and
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and performing a discrete integration by parts on the contribution of the fluxes, namely
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}\varphi_{K}^{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\varphi_{K\sigma}^{n}-\varphi_{K}^{n})
for all

, provides

(56)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1})\varphi_{K}^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\varphi_{K\sigma}^{n}-\varphi_{K}^{n}).

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the right-hand side then using Lemma
3.9
provides the desired result.
The recovery of the estimate for
i=0
then directly follows from the definition (
27
) of
u_{0,K}^{n}
and from (
55
) for
i\geq 1
.
∎

Compactness of the approximate solutions

Proposition 4.1

(Compactness results)
.

There exist functions

.

4.2.
Identification of the limit

In this section, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.2
by showing the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(U,\phi)
be as in Proposition
4.1
, then it is a weak solution of the problem (
1
)–(
7
) in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

Proof.

The regularity requirements on
U
and
\phi
have already been checked in Proposition
4.1
. Therefore it only remains to verify that the weak formulations (
16
) and (
17
) hold true. The case of the Poisson equation is classical. It will not be detailed here, and we refer to
[
14
, Proposition 4.2]
for a synthetic proof. We rather focus our attention on the derivation of (
17
).

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\overline{\Omega})
, then, for some admissible mesh
(\mathcal{T}_{\ell},\mathcal{E}_{\ell},(x_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}})
(we remove the subscript
\ell
when possible for legibility), denote by
\varphi_{K}^{n}=\varphi(t^{n},x_{K})
.
Multiplying (
25
) by
\tau^{n}\varphi_{K}^{n-1}
and summing over

and
K\in\mathcal{T}
yields, as for (
56
):

(68)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1})\varphi_{K}^{n-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\varphi_{K\sigma}^{n-1}-\varphi_{K}^{n-1}).

Since
\varphi
is compactly supported in time,
\varphi_{K}^{n}=0
for
n
large enough. Therefore, the left-hand side of (
68
) is rewritten as

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,K}^{n-1})\varphi_{K}^{n-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}u_{i,K}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{K}^{n-1}-\varphi_{K}^{n}}{\tau^{n}}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}u_{i,K}^{0}\varphi_{K}^{0}.

Since
\varphi
is smooth, the approximate time derivative
\delta_{\tau}\varphi_{\mathcal{T},\tau}
of
\varphi
defined by

\delta_{\tau}\varphi_{\mathcal{T},\tau}(t,x)=\frac{\varphi_{K}^{n}-\varphi_{K}^{n-1}}{\tau^{n}}\quad\text{if}\;(t,x)\in(t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times K

converges in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)
towards
\partial_{t}\varphi
. Therefore, using (
60
), we get that

(69)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}u_{i,K}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{K}^{n-1}-\varphi_{K}^{n}}{\tau^{n}}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega}u_{i}\partial_{t}\varphi.

The function
u_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{0}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}u_{i,K}^{0}\mathbbm{1}_{K}
converges strongly in

towards
u_{i}^{0}
, while
\varphi_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\varphi_{K}^{0}\mathbbm{1}_{K}
converges uniformly towards
\varphi(0,\cdot)
thanks to the regularity of
\varphi
. Therefore,

(70)

-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}u_{i,K}^{0}\varphi_{K}^{0}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0}\varphi(0,\cdot),

so we can pass to the limit in the left-hand side of (
68
).

Let us now focus on its right-hand side.
Define
\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T},\tau}
by

\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T},\tau}(t,x)=\varphi(t^{n-1},x_{K})\quad\text{if\;}(t,x)\in[t^{n-1},t^{n})\times K.

Following
[
23
]
(see

20
]

mapping the set of piecewise constant functions in space and time to

such that

\widehat{\nabla}_{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T},\tau}(t,x)\cdot n_{K\sigma}=\frac{\varphi_{L}^{n}-\varphi_{K}^{n}}{d_{\sigma}}\quad\text{if}\;(t,x)\in[t^{n-1},t^{n})\times\omega_{\sigma},\quad\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int},

and which is strongly consistent, i.e.,

\widehat{\nabla}_{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T},\tau}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\nabla\varphi\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega)^{d}.

The right-hand side of (

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{n}(\varphi_{K\sigma}^{n-1}-\varphi_{K}^{n-1})=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega}F_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T},\tau}.

We have shown in Lemma

To conclude the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we still have to identify the limiting flux

under the

\displaystyle F_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}^{\text{conv}}(t,x)=

and

if

. In view of (

\displaystyle T_{1,\sigma}^{n}=\;

\displaystyle u_{i,K}^{n}u_{0,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n}u_{0,L}^{n},

Therefore, in view of (

F_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}^{\text{diff}}=-D_{i}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}[u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}]+4D_{i}\gamma_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}\sqrt{u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}}+\mathcal{R}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}+\mathcal{S}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau},

where we have set

\gamma_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}(t,x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}+u_{i,L}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)&\text{if}\;(t,x)\in(t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\omega_{\sigma},\;\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int},\\
u_{i,K}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}&\text{if}\;(t,x)\in(t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\omega_{\sigma},\;\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}},\end{cases}

and where, for
(t,x)\in(t^{n-1},t^{n}]\times\omega_{\sigma}
,
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int},

\mathcal{R}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}(t,x)=d\,D_{i}\,(u_{i,K}^{n}-u_{i,L}^{n})\left(\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}n_{K\sigma},

and, in view of (
54
),

\left|\mathcal{S}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}(t,x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{d_{\sigma}}\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n}\right)^{2}.

Both
\mathcal{R}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}
and
\mathcal{S}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}
are assumed to vanish on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\omega_{\sigma}
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}
.
Then thanks to Proposition
3.3
, one has

\|\mathcal{S}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}\|_{L^{1}((0,t^{N})\times\Omega)^{d}}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}m_{\sigma}\left(\phi_{K}^{n}-\phi_{L}^{n}\right)^{2}\leq Ch_{\mathcal{T}}t^{N}.

On the other hand, since
0\leq u_{i,K}^{n}\leq 1
, we deduce from (
47
) that

\|\mathcal{R}_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}\|_{L^{1}((0,t^{N})\times\Omega)^{d}}\leq D_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}m_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}\leq Ch_{\mathcal{T}}(1+t^{N}).

The two last terms in (
77
) then tend to
0
, while the first term tends to
-D_{i}\nabla[u_{0}u_{i}]
thanks to
(
66
). In view of the weak
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(L^{2})
convergence of
\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}\sqrt{u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}}
towards
\nabla\sqrt{u_{0}}
, cf. (
65
), then it suffices to show that

\gamma_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}\quad\text{strongly in\; }L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\overline{\Omega})

to pass to the limit in (
77
). As
u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}\sqrt{u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}}
converges strongly towards
u_{i}\sqrt{u_{0}}
, see (
63
), then

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{i,\mathcal{E},\tau}-u_{i,\mathcal{T},\tau}\sqrt{u_{0,\mathcal{T},\tau}}\|_{L^{2}((0,t^{N})\times\Omega)}^{2}=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}m_{\omega_{\sigma}}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-u_{i,L}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\frac{h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{d}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}}a_{\sigma}\left(u_{i,K}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,K}^{n}}-u_{i,L}^{n}\sqrt{u_{0,L}^{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:L2locH1.2}}{\leq}\;

\displaystyle\frac{h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{d}(1+t^{N})\underset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This completes the proof of (
72
), thus the ones of Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
2.2
.
∎

5.
Long-time asymptotic of the scheme

Our aim here is to prove our last main theoretical contribution, namely Theorem
2.3
.
Throughout this section, we will work on a fixed grid
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T},(x_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}})
and for a fixed time discretization.

We are interested in steady states
(U^{\infty}_{\mathcal{T}},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
of the scheme (
24
)–(
27
), i.e. solutions to

(78)

\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{\infty}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{\infty})=m_{K}\left(f_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}u_{i,K}^{\infty}\right),\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},

(79)

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,I,\;K\in\mathcal{T},

with
F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}=0
if
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}
and

(80)

F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}=a_{\sigma}D_{i}\left(u^{\infty}_{i,K}u^{\infty}_{0,L}\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi_{L}^{\infty}-\phi_{K}^{\infty})\right)-u^{\infty}_{i,L}u^{\infty}_{0,K}\mathfrak{B}\left(z_{i}(\phi^{\infty}_{K}-\phi^{\infty}_{L})\right)\right)

for
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
, where we have set

(81)

u_{0,K}^{\infty}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i,K}^{\infty},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T}.

The above equations have to be complemented with some discrete counterpart of the constraint on the mass (
22
), that is

(82)

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}u_{i,K}^{\infty}=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{0},\qquad i=1,\dots,I.

We are moreover interested in the convergence of
(U^{n}_{\mathcal{T}},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{n})
towards
(U^{\infty}_{\mathcal{T}},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
as
n
goes to
+\infty
.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists a solution to (
78
)–(
82
), with constant in space potentials in the sense that there exists
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}=\left(\mu_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
such that

(83)

\log\frac{u_{i,K}^{\infty}}{u_{0,K}^{\infty}}+z_{i}\phi_{K}^{\infty}=\mu_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},\;1\leq i\leq I,

such that

(84)

(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{n},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{n})\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}).

Proof.

Owing to (
42
) and the non-negativity of the discrete dissipations
\mathcal{D}^{n}_{\mathcal{T}}
, the sequence
{(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n})}_{n\geq 0}
is decreasing, and moreover bounded thanks to Lemma
3.7
. Therefore, it converges towards some finite limit
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}
, while
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}
has to tend to
0
as
n
goes to
+\infty
. Then so does
\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{n}
, cf. (
43
), for all
i=1,\dots,I
and all
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
.
Since
\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{n},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
is included in a bounded (thus relatively compact) set, the LaSalle invariance principle yields the existence of (at least) an accumulation point

\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}\right)
, the energy of which being equal to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}
.
Then it follows from the discrete energy / energy dissipation inequality (
42
) that the dissipation

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}
corresponding to
\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}\right)
is equal to
0
, then so are all the edge dissipation contributions
\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{\infty}
which are defined by (
43
) but with
\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}\right)
instead of
\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{n},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}\right)
. Since
\mathcal{D}_{i,\sigma}^{\infty}=0
iff the corresponding flux

F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}
defined by (
80
)
vanishes for all
i=1,\dots,I
and
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm int}
, one gets that
\left(U_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}\right)
is a steady solution, and that the convergence (
84
) holds true.

Let us now show (
83
). The condition
F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}=0
for
\sigma=K|L
implies the following alternative:

(i)

u_{0,K}^{\infty}=0
and
u_{0,L}^{\infty}=0
,

(ii)

u_{i,K}^{\infty}=0
and
u_{i,L}^{\infty}=0
,

(iii)

u_{i,K}^{\infty}=0
and
u_{0,K}^{\infty}=0
, or
u_{i,L}^{\infty}=0
and
u_{0,L}^{\infty}=0
,

(iv)

\log\frac{u_{i,K}^{\infty}}{u_{0,K}^{\infty}}+z_{i}\phi_{K}^{\infty}=\log\frac{u_{i,L}^{\infty}}{u_{0,L}^{\infty}}+z_{i}\phi_{L}^{\infty}
.

This can be seen using the two formulations of the fluxes (
80
) and (
31
) at the limit
n\to\infty
. The formula (
80
) clearly yields (i) to (iii) if any of the involved volume fractions vanish. If they are all positive then (
31
) is well-defined at the limit
n\to\infty
and yields (iv).

Assume for contradiction that there exists
K\in\mathcal{T}
such that
u_{0,K}^{\infty}=0
, then
F_{i,K\sigma}^{\infty}=0
for all
i
requires that either
u_{i,K}^{\infty}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq I
, which is incompatible with (
81
), or that

u_{0,L}^{\infty}=0
for all neighboring cells
L
sharing an edge with
K
. An induction shows relying on the connected character of
\Omega
shows that
u_{0,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}=0
, which contradicts (
82
). The cases (
i
) and (
iii
) are therefore impossible, and
u_{0,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}>0
.

Assume now that
u_{i,K}^{\infty}=0
for some
K\in\mathcal{T}
, then as
u_{0,K}^{\infty}>0
, a similar reasoning shows that
u_{i,L}^{\infty}
also has to be equal to
0
for all neighboring cell of
K
. Then
u_{i,\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}=0
, contradicting again (
82
). Therefore, alternative (
ii
) is not feasible, and (
iv
) has to hold true.
This concludes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Given
\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}
, one can invert the system (
81
)&(
83
). As in the continuous case, one then gets that

u_{i,K}^{\infty}=v_{i}(\phi_{K}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty}),\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},\;0\leq i\leq I

with
v_{i}
defined in (
18
).
Incorporating this relation in (
78
) provides the following discrete counterpart to the modified Poisson–Boltzmann equation (
20
):

(85)

\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}(\phi_{K}^{\infty}-\phi_{K\sigma}^{\infty})+m_{K}r(\phi_{K}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})=m_{K}f_{K},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},

to be complemented with the mass constraints (
82
), with
r
being the non-decreasing function defined in (
21
).

Proposition 5.2
.

The solution
(\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
to (
85
)&(
82
) minimizes the strictly convex functional
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
defined by (
35
). In particular,
(\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
is unique.

Proof.

One readily checks that

\frac{\partial\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}}{\partial{y}_{K}}({y}_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}({y}_{K}-{y}_{K\sigma})+m_{K}r({y}_{K},\boldsymbol{\xi})-m_{K}f_{K},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T}.

and that

\frac{\partial\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}}{\partial\xi_{i}}({y}_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}[v_{i}({y}_{K},\boldsymbol{\xi})-u_{i,K}^{0}].

Bearing in mind the definition (
29
) of
u_{i,K}^{0}
, one gets that
(\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\mu_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
solves (
85
)&(
82
) if and only if it is a critical point of
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
. In particular, Proposition
5.1
guarantees the existence of such a critical point as it ensures the existence of a solution to (
85
)&(
82
).

To conclude the proof of Proposition
5.2
, it remains to show the strict convexity of
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Let
({y}_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}})
and
({\tilde{y}}_{\mathcal{T}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{T}})
be two elements of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{T}}\times\mathbb{R}^{I}
. Then

(86)

\left(D\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}({y}_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}})-D\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}({\tilde{y}}_{\mathcal{T}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{T}})\right)\cdot({y}_{\mathcal{T}}-{\tilde{y}}_{\mathcal{T}},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{T}}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{T}})=A+B,

where

\displaystyle A=\;

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}({y}_{K}-{\tilde{y}}_{K})\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}a_{\sigma}(({y}_{K}-{\tilde{y}}_{K})-({y}_{K\sigma}-{\tilde{y}}_{K\sigma})),

\displaystyle B=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}[r({y}_{K},\boldsymbol{\xi})-r({\tilde{y}}_{K},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})]({y}_{K}-{\tilde{y}}_{K})

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}m_{K}[(v_{i}({y}_{K},\boldsymbol{\xi})-v_{i}({\tilde{y}}_{K},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})](\xi_{i}-\tilde{\xi}_{i}).

Performing a discrete integration by parts gives

A=\lambda^{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\sigma}(({y}_{K}-{\tilde{y}}_{K})-({y}_{K\sigma}-{\tilde{y}}_{K\sigma}))^{2}\geq 0.

As we assumed that
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ext}}^{D}
is not empty, it follows from the discrete Poincaré inequality (see Lemma 9.1 and Remark 9.4 of
[
26
]
) that

(87)

A
vanishes if and only if
{y}_{\mathcal{T}}={\tilde{y}}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Using the relation (
21
) between
r
and
v_{i}
, we rewrite the term
B
as

B=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\big(v_{i}({y}_{K},\boldsymbol{\xi})-v_{i}({\tilde{y}}_{K},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\big)\big(\xi_{i}-z_{i}{y}_{K}-(\tilde{\xi}_{i}-z_{i}{\tilde{y}}_{K})\big).

Denote by

H^{*}({R})=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{A}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{I}u_{i}{r}_{i}-H(U)\right]=\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{{r}_{i}}\right),\qquad{R}=\left({r}_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I},

the Legendre transform of the mixing entropy functional
H
, cf. (
10
), then, setting

{R}_{K}=\left({r}_{i,K}\right)_{1\leq i\leq I}={(\xi_{i}-z_{i}{y}_{K})}_{1\leq i\leq I},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T},

the term
B
is rewritten as

(88)

B=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}m_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\langle\frac{\partial H^{*}}{\partial{r}_{i}}({R}_{K})-\frac{\partial H^{*}}{\partial{r}_{i}}({\tilde{R}}_{K}),{R}_{K}-{\tilde{R}}_{K}\right\rangle.

As
H^{*}
is a Legendre transform, it is by construction convex and thus
B\geq 0
. By setting
{r}_{0,K}={\tilde{r}}_{0,K}=0
, the convexity of
H^{*}
can also be seen as a consequence of Holder’s inequality

\sum_{j=0}^{I}e^{\theta{r}_{j,K}+(1-\theta){\tilde{r}}_{j,K}}\leq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{I}e^{{r}_{j,K}}\right)^{\theta}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{I}e^{{\tilde{r}}_{j,K}}\right)^{1-\theta}

for
\theta\in[0,1]
. The inequality is an equality only if
(e^{{r}_{j,K}})_{j}
and
(e^{{\tilde{r}}_{j,K}})_{j}
are linearly dependent. As
{r}_{0,K}={\tilde{r}}_{0,K}
it can only happen if
{R}_{K}={\tilde{R}}_{K}
. In other words
H^{*}
is actually strictly convex.

It follows from the above discussion that the right-hand side of (
86
) vanishes if and only if both
A
and
B
vanish. Thanks to (
87
), this requires that
{y}_{\mathcal{T}}={\tilde{y}}_{\mathcal{T}}
.
On the other hand because of the strict convexity of
H^{*}
, the term
B
(
88
) vanishes if and only if
{R}_{\mathcal{T}}={\tilde{R}}_{\mathcal{T}}
, thus if
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
. Then
\Psi_{\mathcal{T}}
is strictly convex and its minimizer
(\phi_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\infty})
is unique.
∎

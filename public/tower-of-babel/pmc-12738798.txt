Design and analysis of high-k wrapped underlap induced GaN multi-channel GAA nanosheet FET for enhanced performance with cut-off frequency in THz range
Authors: Sneha Singh, Rudra Sankar Dhar, Amit Banerjee, Vinay Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA multi-channel Gallium Nitride (GaN) based Gate-All-Around (GAA) Nanosheet Field Effect Transistor (NS FET) having 2 nm gate underlap wrapping of high-k dielectric spacer are designed, explored and analysed for inclusion in future wireless application systems. The integration of GaN as the channel material provides superior electron mobility and high breakdown voltage, making it extremely suitable for high-frequency applications. The inclusion of the gate underlap region effectively reduces parasitic capacitance while mitigate short channel effects due to its high-k wrapping. High dielectric gate stack is employed to enhance gate channel coupling and reduce leakage currents. Calibration is done utilizing a fabricated GaN FET and further validated by analytical threshold voltage modelling. Further, DC characterization reveals a remarkably low off-current (IOff) of 12 × 10-7nA/µm, a high switching ratio (IOn/IOff) of 5.26 × 1010, subthreshold swing of 60.50mV/decade, and drain induced barrier lowering (DIBL) of 11.92mV/V that superbly outperform the IRDS2028 benchmarks of 1.5 nm technology node. The RF analysis demonstrates superior high-frequency performance with cut-off frequency reaching 8.16THz attributing to enhanced transconductance and minimized gate capacitance indicating the GaN NS FET operation in THz frequency regime. The use of multichannel in NS FET significantly boosts carrier transport efficiency and scaling compatibility exhibits enhanced drive current and reduced leakages. This validates and conforms devices’ potential for integration into future RF front-end modules and beyond 5G/6G wireless systems. The combination benefits of material innovation, geometric optimization and electrostatic enhancement positioning this device as a strong candidate for post-CMOS ultra-scaled high-performance logic and RF applications in THz frequency regime. Keywords: NS FET, High-k underlap, THz frequency, Nano-scaled transistor, 5G/6G applicationsSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Gate-All-Around Field Effect Transistors (GAA FETs) represent a promising advancement in semiconductor technology offering improved electrostatic control and reduced short-channel effects (SCEs) compared to traditional transistor architectures such as FinFETs1. GAA FETs utilize a three-dimensional gate structure that entirely surrounds the channel enabling better scalability, enhanced performance, and low power consumption2. This makes them particularly suitable for applications requiring high-density integration and improved energy efficiency as needed in next-generation processors and communication devices. With the device dimensions shrinking, GAA FETs are seen as a key solution paving the way for continued evolution of semiconductor technologies2,3. In recent years, wireless communication has undergone rapid transformation driven by the increasing demand for high-speed data transmission, lower latency and higher energy efficiency4. To meet these requirements, future wireless technologies including 5G/6G and beyond systems, and IoT systems rely on novel semiconductor devices that offer power efficiency, faster switching speed and robustness against device linearity and power fluctuations5. Considering these aspects, Gallium Nitride (GaN) is seen to emerge as a key material due to its exceptional properties such as wide bandgap, high electron mobility and higher conductivity6. When combined with the advanced transistor architecture forming GaN based GAA FETs, they promise to deliver the enriched performance required for wireless applications ranging from RF power amplifiers to THz based high-frequency integrated circuits.

Recently, GaN power FET is commercialized for 15–650 V power switching applications and is now seeing rapid adoption in power supplies, data centres, LIDAR systems, and the fast-charging systems7–9. Subsequently, properties like higher velocity, large bandgap, and peak electric field makes GaN a desirable semiconductor for IoT and high-frequency applications10,11. Kumar et al.12 developed a nanoscale GaN SOI FinFET for RFIC design and compared it with conventional FinFETs of 8 nm gate length and detected higher Ion and lower Ioff, thus enhancing the switching speed with lower SS and DIBL providing improved linearity and lesser harmonic distortion12,13. This SOI structure reduces parasitic capacitances by 20%, and low power consumption increases the device’s operating frequency13. However, these devices suffer from linearity degradation, affecting signal clarity, self-heating, and other issues, thus not meeting the requirements. In AlGaN/GaN FinFETs the nano-sized fin body becomes fully depleted at off-state effectively suppressing trapping effects and blocking leakage current with improved device linearity14,15. Still, challenges such as parasitic fringing capacitance and faster switching in shorter gate length designs for RF applications remain as an area of interest requiring further research.

Initial developments of GaN FETs focused on lateral-type configurations are observed to deliver impressive subthreshold behaviour and strong resistance to drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL) demonstrating their robustness against SCEs16,17. Im et al.18 reported GaN nanowire MOSFETs exhibited current-collapse-free operation, signifying suppressed electron trapping. Recent progress made by Jo et al.19, demonstrates vertically structured GaN nanowire FETs using a top-down method achieve a threshold voltage of 0.6 V and an ON/OFF ratio of 9 × 10⁹. GAA-GaN-VNWFETs showcased efficient low-voltage operation and high-speed switching, critical for modern logic circuits20–22. Quan et al.23 achieved a subthreshold swing below 60 mV/dec via channel width modulation. Thus, GaN based nanowire FETs are emerging as promising devices due to their superior electrostatic control and minimized SCEs. These advantages stem from their gate-wrapped nanowire channel structure, which allows full depletion in the off-state and eliminates the influence of parasitic layers. While also installing the underlap layers wrapped with high-k (HfO2) provide reduced fringing effects thereby enriching device speed. Simulation and fabrication efforts further confirm GaN nanowire FETs’ potential to outperform their semiconductor counterparts in power and RF domains24. Leveraging these advancements, GaN based GAA FETs are expected to deliver significant improvements in performance, including a sharper subthreshold slope, higher on/off current ratios, and superior stability under high bias conditions. So is the demand for development of enhanced device to overcome the requirement of IRDS 202825, that can be utilized for next-generation RF and wireless systems for beyond 5G/6G applications at THz frequency range and for IoT systems.

Ensuring long-term device reliability under high-temperature conditions is crucial for durability, highlighting the necessity of an in-depth analysis of GaN based FETs. A particular focus should be placed on evaluating the effects of high-k wrapped underlap structures in these devices mainly for RF, analog, and wireless applications. These advancements will not only overcome key challenges relating to scaling and performance enhancement but also align with the IRDS roadmap for the 1.5 nm technology node projected for 202825. Such innovations are expected to reinforce the role of GaN-based devices in pushing the boundaries of transistor scaling while maintaining energy efficiency and operational stability. Eventually, these developments will drive the integration of GaN based 3D devices into emerging applications including IoT systems, wireless communications with THz frequencies, electric vehicles, and sustainable semiconductor technologies.

Device theory and arrangement

GaN is considered as the channel material and Devices A1, B1, and C1 are initially developed implementing Gate Stack (GS) oxide with a combination of SiO2 and HfO2 to form Gate-All-Around NanoFETs with gate underlap architecture. These devices have an overall gate length of 7 nm while a 2 nm gate underlap is induced comprising of the channel material wrapped by high-k dielectric spacer. The devices are fabricated with distinct fin area dimensions of 6 × 6 nm², 6 × 3 nm², and 3 × 6 nm² for A1, B1, and C1, respectively, as illustrated in Figs. 1(a), 1(d), and 1(g). The channel region is uniformly doped with n-type dopants at a concentration of 1 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, while the source and drain regions are heavily n-doped to a concentration of 5 × 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ to ensure low-resistance contacts and efficient carrier injection. To analyse the effects of scaling and multichannel configurations to enrich the speed, devices A1, B1, and C1 (single-channel NanoFETs) are further extended for newly developing into dual-channel variants A2, B2, and C2, and four-channel versions A3, B3, and C3 presented in Figs. 1b, e, h, c, f, and i, respectively, and are systematically outlined and detailed in Table 1. The variation in fin dimensions and the number of conducting channels are aimed at investigating the impact on gate electrostatics, subthreshold behavior, and drive current enhancement, which are expected to provide a light on the novel development Stacked Nanosheet FETs in line with the requirements of IRDS 202825. GaN, with its low thermal resistance, offers a distinct advantage for power transistors, as it maintains stable characteristics even at high operating temperatures4,8,12. These advantages highlight GaN’s potential as a desirable semiconductor for the future. While self-heating can lead to localized temperature rise in the channel and source/drain regions, thereby, affecting mobility, threshold voltage, and drive current of the device. In the present devices developed and analyzed here comprehensive electrical, RF, and linearity analyses under ideal thermal conditions are assessed for the intrinsic performance of the proposed GaN-based GAA devices. Consequently, with the use of GaN the self-heating effect is negligible thereby focusing on GaN’s suitability for high-frequency (several GHz), high-temperature, RF devices and IOT applications are incredible. The gate underlap design follows the architectural framework proposed by Barik et al.26 for Si based CGAA FETs, which effectively mitigates parasitic capacitances, minimizes gate-induced leakage currents, and suppresses fringing electric fields being encapsulated by HfO2 based high-k dielectric spacers. The selection of HfO₂ is driven by its high relative permittivity, outstanding thermal stability, and superior interfacial compatibility with GaN based systems27. This installs lower equivalent oxide thickness (EOT), steeper subthreshold slope (SS), and suppresses short-channel effects (SCEs) all of which are critical for achieving high-performing devices at nanoscale dimensions. In addition, TiN is utilized as the gate electrode material in place of conventional polysilicon offering significantly lower resistivity, high thermal robustness, and enhanced compatibility with modern CMOS integration, thereby expected to improve both electrical performance and reliability based on AC analysis and linearity issues. The integration of gate underlap, high-k spacers, and TiN gate metal are newly introduced here forming GaN based novel Stacked Nanosheet FETs (A3, B3, C3) position them as strong contenders for next-generation low power electronics, high-frequency radio-frequency (RF) applications for 5G/6G systems and devices for IOT systems.

Three-dimensional (3D) process and device simulations of GaN based Stacked Nanosheet FETs (NS FET) are developed using Synopsys Sentaurus TCAD. The simulations integrate advanced physical models to precisely represent the behavior of aggressively scaled nanodevices. A self-consistent numerical approach solves the drift-diffusion (DD) transport equations alongside Poisson and carrier continuity equations to evaluate electrostatics and carrier transport within the device. To incorporate quantum mechanical effects, especially significant in sub-10 nm channel lengths, a density-gradient (DG) quantum correction model is employed. This model modifies the carrier density with a potential-like correction term, enabling accurate modelling of quantum confinement and tunneling effects. The Slotboom model accounts for bandgap narrowing resulting from heavy doping, which is essential in GaN architectures exhibiting the steep doping profiles. Carrier mobility degradation under nanoscale confinement is captured using thin-layer and inversion and accumulation layer (IAL) mobility models. These consider scattering mechanisms such as impurity scattering, phonon interactions, surface roughness, and confinement-induced mobility reduction. Recombination and generation processes are modelled using Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) and Auger models, while band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) is addressed via the Hurkx model ensuring numerical stability effective for devices with electric fields exceeding 7 × 10⁵ V/cm applicable for high-field GaN devices. The comprehensive selection of physical models and the use of GaN as the channel material underscores the suitability for development of novel GaN NS FET architecture for next-generation high-performance, high-frequency, and RF circuits.

The TCAD simulator results are further calibrated by comparing them to the ID-VGS plot of experimentally fabricated data of device D (Son et al.28. Son et al.28 experimentally developed GAA GaN VNW FET with a gate length of 25 nm and generated ID-VGS at VDS of 1.0 V, which is observed to almost accurately match with the TCAD simulation of a similar GaN material based device on merging with the model parameters such as the density gradient (DG) quantum correction model with SRH, Auger and Hurkx model as shown in Fig. 2(a). The experimental device D is therefore utilized here to calibrate and validate the electrical properties of the proposed GAA device. The device simulation parameters using Sentaurus TCAD29 are calibrated to fit the experimental results, as acquired in Fig. 2a, which matches quite closely indicating the model parameters for the simulation is considered quite precisely and efficiently having electron mobility of 1250 cm2/Vs, saturation velocity of 2.2 × 107 cm/s, dielectric constant of 9.5, bandgap of 3.4 eV and background doping of 1 × 1016 cm-3. The employed models for the simulation comprises the Shockley Read Hall recombination, Auger recombination, Hurkx band-to-band tunneling, Lombardi surface mobility, high-field dependent mobility and density gradient quantum confinement correction, which profusely help in providing an excellent calibration of the proposed GaN-based nanosheet GAA FET with 2 nm underlap and wrapping high-k spacer (device C3). Subsequently, achieving defect-free polysilicon gate filling and reliable contact formation requires meticulous process optimization. The bar graph in Fig. 3b illustrates the on-current (Ion) of the GaN based GS GAA FET across different multichannel configurations of a single channel (A1, B1, C1), dual channel (A2, B2, C2) and quad channel (A3, B3, C3) showing an apparent enhancement in Ion with increase in channel count. Devices A1 to A3, with the biggest fin area of 6 × 6 nm2, demonstrate superior drive current capabilities due to enhanced gate control and wider conduction paths. Among all, Device A3 exhibits the highest Ion, attributed to its four-channel configurations coupled with an optimal physical footprint that ensures better electrostatic integrity. Furthermore, quantum confinement effects are more pronounced in narrower channels, but the wider fin geometry of device A3 maintains carrier mobility while ensuring strong inversion, leading to improved current drive. These factors collectively validate the superior performance of device A3 in on-current metrics. The off-current (Ioff) analysis demonstrates that device C1, featuring a 3 × 6 nm2 fin area and a single channel architecture achieves the minimum Ioff across all developed devices while B1 featuring a 6 × 3 nm2 fin area follows just after as acquired in Fig. 3c. The compact physical dimensions of the devices combined with a 2nm gate underlap and high-k (HfO2) spacer wrapping ensures superiority effectively subduing the short channel effects and fringing fields. The reduced gate-to-source coupling minimizes leakage pathways, while the high-k material confines the electric field as well as fringing parasitic enhancing higher gate control. Additionally, the integration of density gradient based quantum correction efficiently models quantum confinement, restricting carrier presence in the off-state. These factors collectively suppress SCEs positioning C1 as the most energy-efficient design in ultra-scaled regimes. For novel devices B3 and C3 (GaN NS FETs), the Off-current increases by a minimal amount based on the fact of their four-channel device design providing higher power than the single-channel devices.

Now further going ahead with the analysis, the switching ratio (Ion/Ioff) of GaN based NS FETs featuring multichannel configurations of one, two, and four channels with 2 nm gate underlap wrapped high-k dielectric, devices A1 to A3 (6 × 6 nm²), B1 to B3 (6 × 3 nm²), and C1 to C3 (3 × 6 nm²) determines distinct current performances as presented in Fig. 3 (d). Though, A3 exhibits the highest on-current (116.84 µA/µm) due to enhanced carrier transport from increased channel width and surface area but it also presents huge Ioff mainly due to fringing field effects as observed in Figs. 3b, c. Figure 3c shows C1 to achieve the lowest off-current (~ 3.01 × 10-16 A/µm), enabled by superior electrostatic control, fringing electric field and reduced leakage via strong quantum confinement in its fin region thereby leads to lower Ioff for the novel device C3 and similar for B3 with four channel configuration. Thus, C3 delivers the maximum switching ratio outperforming all other newly developed devices here and the recent existing benchmark devices (D28, E30 and G26 and also exceeding the 1.5 nm technology node data of IRDS 202825 standard as shown in Fig. 3(d) and detailed in Table 2. The exceptionally high switching ratio of ~ 5.27 × 1010 in C3 results due to the four-channel system from a balanced combination of high on-current and ultra-low off-current. This is primarily due to efficient quantum mechanical confinement effects, optimal gate control, and improved carrier modulation in the ultra-thin fin region demonstrating the potential of C3 for next-generation low-power, high-performance nanoscale devices and wireless systems for beyond 5G/6G applications at THz frequency range and also in future IoT systems.

The subthreshold swing (SS) characteristics of the multichannel GaN based GAA FETs having 2 nm HfO2 wrapped gate underlaps are evaluated across all the devices developed and explored here. Among all configurations, device C3 exhibits minimum SS of 60.50 mV/decade, approaching the ideal limit and outperforming all other devices including the existing devices (D28, E30, F31, and G26 and IRDS25 standards as illustrated in Fig. 3e, which is further detailed in Table 2. This improvement is attributed to enhanced electrostatic control from the nanoscale fin geometry, increased gate-to-channel coupling, and quantum confinement effects that suppresses the SCEs. The device’s optimized physical dimensions and material properties ensure superior subthreshold performance in advanced scaling regimes. Among all evaluated GaN based GAA FETs, device C3 with a four-channel configuration exhibits the lowest DIBL of 11.92 mV/V. This is attributed to substantial gate control in the narrow fin region, minimizing short-channel effects. Device C3 outperforms all the newly developed devices (A1 to A3, B1 to B3 and C1 to C3) and similar existing devices D28, E30, F31, and G26 as depicted in Fig. 3f, showcasing superior channel control in nanoscale dimensions aligning well with advanced device scaling requirements. Considering these aspects device C3 is exclusively analyzed for its transfer characteristics due to its superior switching ratio and low leakages among the devices. Figure 3g illustrates the transfer characteristics (ID–VGS) of devices B3 and C3 for VGS varying from 0 V to 1 V at VDS=1 V. Although both devices exhibit almost identical drain current and subthreshold swing, a slight enhancement in the ID slope is observed for device C3 in the gate voltage range of 0.415 V to 0.8 V. This indicates stronger gate control and improved electrostatic coupling in device C3, which enhances the channel modulation efficiency and results in a higher gm peak compared to device B3. The improved transconductance of device C3 thus arises from fine variations in the gate capacitance and electrostatic coupling, despite comparable ID and SS values. The logarithmic ID–VGS curve indicates minimal off-state leakage as shown in Fig. 3g. This arises due to the quantum confinement occurring in the nanosheet channel of the device leading to an increase in the effective bandgap and stronger carrier localization, which suppresses the thermally generated carriers in the subthreshold region. This effect combined with the improved electrostatic control provided by the gate-all-around geometry in the Nanosheet device, results in a lowering the Ioff current. Also, device C3 shows an enriched Ion/Ioff ratio, along with reduced leakage current and a lower SS, making C3 a suitable alternative and preferred device with super-fast switching ability and minimal leakage for the future applications of beyond 5G/6G systems at possibly THz frequency range and in IoT structures.

It has a GaN based NS FET structure with a compact 3 × 6 nm2 fin area and 2nm gate underlap wrapped by high-k spacer that ensures strong gate control and integrity. It provides a lower Ioff with lower SS and DIBL compared to the novel devices A3 and B3 and the existing devices D28, E30, F31, G26 and K12 and IRDS 202825 confirming strong immunity to SCEs. The conduction and valence band shows enhanced carrier confinement and energy barrier modulation. Its high electric field and potential distribution in the channel region enable faster switching and efficient charge transport. These features are essential for high-speed and low-power RF front-end components required in 5G/6G technologies. The combination of higher Ion, excellent electrostatic behavior, reduced leakage, and robust switching efficiency of device C3 makes it a promising candidate for next-generation ultra-scaled RF and mm-wave applications and for future IoT systems.

Figure 5d presents the intrinsic gain variation with gate voltage for the NS FET devices (A3, B3 and C3) having fin areas of 6 × 6 nm2, 6 × 3 nm2 and 3 × 6 nm2. The structure with reduced fin height (device C3) shows significantly higher gain due to improved electrostatic control and abridged output conductance. This configuration attains a peak gain of ~ 1.26 × 104, which is superior compared to the other devices. The enhancement is attributed to the GaN channels each having 2nm gate underlap surrounded high-k dielectric subduing the fringing field effects more for the wider source/drain/underlap device (C3) in contrast to device A3 or B3. This design elements improved carrier transport, reduced leakage and enhanced signal amplification efficiency highly needed for RF applications in THz regime. Thereafter, Device C3 in Fig. 5e demonstrates significantly lower output capacitance (Cgg) of 1.3aF compared to other NS FETs and the existing devices H32, I33 and J4 detailed in Table 3. Additionally, the dual-layer gate stack (SiO₂ + HfO₂) system effectively reduces the gate leakage and enhances electrostatic integrity, thereby lowers the gate capacitance. The optimized gate underlap with spacer technology minimizes the fringing field and suppresses parasitic capacitances leading to improved switching speed and reduced power consumption. Inclusion of high-k material across the spacer region may increase CGS and CGD, which can appear counterintuitive for RF improvement, however, in the proposed device the localized high-k integration in the spacer region supports in strengthening gate control over the channel, which enhances gm and suppresses short channel effects. The improved electrostatic integrity reduces leakage and improves subthreshold slope, which in turn raises the effective gm/Cgg ratio, directly contributing to a higher cut-off frequency. In addition, the overlap-induced capacitance is carefully limited by spacer engineering so that the increase of parasitic effects remains within a non-dominant range and does not outweigh the benefits of enhanced gate modulation. Thus, a lower Cgg directly benefits device performance metrics, such as cut-off frequency and overall efficiency in analog circuits making device C3 highly suitable for high-frequency based super-fast circuitry and RF applications.

The cut-off frequency (fT) for the novel devices are calculated and presented in Fig. 5f. The analysis reveal closely matched frequency responses across the entire range of VGS. Device C3 achieves a maximum fT of 8.171THz indicating possible THz regime operation. The frequency is considerably higher than that of the newly developed devices (A3 and B3) and the existing similar devices H32, I33 and J4 as also showcased in Table 3. This performance is entrusted by employing the GaN channel in the four-channel Nanosheet geometry of the device substantiating to lower capacitance and hence boosts transconductance supporting enhanced high-frequency operation entrusted for future super-fast switching, and low-power digital nanoscale applications in RF and wireless systems for beyond 5G/6G operation in THz frequency range.

Figure 5g depicts the variation of transconductance generation factor (TGF) with gate voltage for the novel NS FET devices (A3, B3 and C3). Out of which device C3 reveals larger TGF of 38.31 per Volt that is significantly higher than comparable existing devices H32 and J4 as detailed in Table 3 This is credited to the multichannel configuration of the devices facilitating efficient and higher carrier transport while the high-k wrapping of the 2 nm gate underlap reduces SCEs enhancing carrier injection efficiency at lower gate voltages. This improves current drive efficiency and makes the architecture ideal for advanced RF systems. The transconductance frequency product (TFP) is the product of cut-off frequency and TGF and is observed to be higher for device C3 because it exhibits both higher fT and TGF as displayed in Fig. 5h. Device C3 reaches a peak TFP of 312.6THz/V, which is much higher than the other two developed NS FETs, A3 (141.71THz/V), and B3 (296.9THz/V) and the existing devices H32 (129.6THz/V) and J4 (85.5THz/V). The novel structural feature of Device C3 thereby enhances charge transport and high-frequency response making the device ideal for next-generation high-speed logic circuitry and RF based applications in THz frequency regime.

Linearity and harmonic analysis

The multichannel Nanosheet FETs with the different configurations (A3, B3, C3) that are newly developed here are further analysed to estimate the linearity and harmonic distortions. The NS FET arrangement having fin dimension of 3 × 6nm2 (device C3) exhibits significantly lower second-order (gm2) and third-order (gm3) transconductance characteristics compared to structures with 6 × 6nm2 and 6 × 3nm2 fin area as illustrated in Fig. 6a, b, respectively. This behavior is attributed to the enhanced electrostatic control resulting from the fully wrapped high permittivity dielectric of the 2nm gate underlap that effectively minimizes parasitic coupling and SCEs. The four-channel configuration further enables superior carrier confinement and mobility while maintaining strong gate control. The peak gm2 and gm3 values barely reach 0.419mA/V2 and 2.7mA/V3, respectively, for device C3, hence are considerably less, showcases improved linearity demonstrating minimal harmonic distortion, which is crucial for analog signal amplification and RF circuit operation in contrast to the other developed devices A3 and B3 and existing GAA FET of Narasimhulu et al.34. This suppression of nonlinearity response ensures high-fidelity signal transmission, which is particularly advantageous for high-performance analog and mixed-signal applications operating at nanoscale dimension.

Conclusion

GaN based nanosheet GAA FETs are developed here in the form 6 × 6nm2, 6 × 3nm2 and 3 × 6nm2 with 2nm high-k wrapped underlaps (devices A1, B1 and C1). In all three cases, two channels (devices A2, B2 and C2) and four channels (devices A3, B3 and C3) are further designed and explored for investigation and analysis in the pursuit of evolving a superior device with a faster switching speed that is suitable for wireless and RF applications. The impact of high-K gate stack and high-k wrapped underlaps on the DC, AC, linearity, and harmonic distortion for four channel devices are explored and analysed. Device C3 is observed to exhibit significant improvements among the devices, which is also compared with the existing devices. The off-current is decreased by 77.81% for Device C3, while subthreshold swing by 8.89%, indicate efficient switching behavior. Additionally, the switching ratio experienced a substantial enhancement of 143.51%, demonstrating improved device C3 performance compared to IRDS2028 for the 1.5 nm technology node. Considering the AC performance, Device C3 displayed remarkable advancements with increase in transconductance by 501.11%, TGF by 19.72%, and the cut-off frequency (fT) by 101.48% compared to device H, making the device suitable for incorporation in high-frequency RF circuits and in THz communication systems. The linearity behavior for device C3 provided remarkable improvements with the 2nd and 3rd of transconductance decreasing by 18.11% and 38.91%, VIP2 and VIP3 increased by 300%, and 16.47%, and IIP3 and 1-dB compression point improved by 23.33% and 5.26%, respectively, resulting in enhanced linearity performance compared to developed device A3. Further, the harmonic distortion analysis such as IMD3, HD2, HD3 and THD, for Device C3 impended indicating lesser distortion compared to developed and existing devices A3, B3, L and M. These improvements collectively indicate that device C3 not only boosts the reliability and performance metrics of the GaN device as a whole but also provides enhanced immunity to SCEs. Device C3 thus operates as a faster switching device while it improves signal integrity with negligible noise due to being linear and having reduced harmonic distortion providing higher carrier velocity. Therefore, GaN based NS FET (device C3) is superior enough and well-suited for the future to be utilized in beyond 5G/6G wireless and radio frequency (RF) applications in THz frequency regime.

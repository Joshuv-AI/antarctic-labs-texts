Reconstruction in the Calderón problem on a fixed partition from finite and partial boundary data
Henrik Garde

Abstract
. This short note modifies a reconstruction method by the author (Comm. PDE, 45(9):1118–1133, 2020), for reconstructing piecewise constant conductivities in the Calderón problem (electrical impedance tomography). In the former paper, a layering assumption and the local Neumann-to-Dirichlet map was needed since the piecewise constant partitioning also was assumed unknown. Here I show how to modify the method in case the partitioning is known, for general piecewise constant conductivities and only a finite number of partial boundary measurements. Moreover, no lower/upper bounds on the unknown conductivity are needed.



Keywords: Calderón problem,
electrical impedance tomography,
partial data reconstruction.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R30, 35Q60, 35R05, 47H05.

1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
a direct reconstruction method is given for the Calderón problem with piecewise constant layered conductivities. Here, successively, each layer is reconstructed and the conductivity values on each component of the layer are determined by simple one-dimensional optimization problems. This approach is improved in
[
4
]
, using the theory of inclusion detection for extreme conductivity values, simplifying the involved test-operators and removing the need for bounds on the conductivity.

In the aforementioned papers, infinitely many partial boundary measurements are used in the form of the local Neumann-to-Dirichlet (ND) map. This is needed because the setting is infinite-dimensional, in the sense that the piecewise constant partitioning (and even the number of “pixels”) is also assumed unknown, and must be reconstructed. Later
[
5
]
expanded on this approach, showing that for a
fixed known partition
, a piecewise constant conductivity can be found as the unique solution to a semidefinite optimization problem, using just a finite number of measurements. Although this new approach comes with the caveat of an unknown set of coefficients for the optimization problem, depending on the domain, the partition, and lower/upper bounds on the conductivity. Both in
[
5
]
and the related paper
[
1
]
, it is mentioned that the approach from
[
3
,
4
]
adapts to their settings, but requires infinitely many measurements.

In this short note, I will show how the second part of the algorithm in
[
3
,
4
]
can be adapted to reconstructing a general piecewise constant conductivity on a given fixed partition, and moreover, that up to an arbitrarily small error, only a finite number of boundary measurements are needed.

Hence, there are a number of advantages/disadvantages compared to the approach in
[
5
]
:

•

It seems preferable to reconstruct the entire conductivity function simultaneously as in
[
5
]
, although one should note that in
[
5
]
the coefficients of the optimization problem are not immediately available. This is unlike in the approach given in this manuscript, where the involved one-dimensional optimization problems are readily available and very simple.

•

In this approach, no lower/upper bounds are needed on the unknown conductivity.

•

This approach adapts to
region-of-interest
(ROI) problems. If there is only interest in the conductivity in part of the domain, one can reconstruct a path from the domain boundary and to the ROI, thus avoiding reconstruction in the remaining part to the domain.

2.
The setting

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
\nu
be an outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
, and let
\Gamma\subseteq\partial\Omega
be a nonempty relatively open subset where the boundary measurements are taken.

Consider the partial data conductivity problem,

-\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad\nu\cdot(\sigma\nabla u)|_{\partial\Omega}=\begin{cases}f&\text{on }\Gamma,\\
0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
f
belongs to

L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)=\{\,f\in L^{2}(\Gamma):\langle f,1\rangle=0\,\}.

Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
refers to the usual inner product on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
. For the electric potential
u
we also enforce a
\Gamma
-mean free condition. A conductivity coefficient
\sigma
can in general be nonnegative and measurable. We assume that
\sigma
can formally equal zero or infinity on Lipschitz sets; such sets are called extreme inclusions (c.f.
[
2
]
). Away from the extreme inclusions,
\sigma
is assumed to be bounded away from zero and infinity. Note that the theory in
[
2
]
also adapts to the case where such extreme inclusions touch the domain boundary, provided the non-extreme part is connected to
\Gamma
, i.e. can be “seen” by the boundary measurements; the proofs are related to the weak form of the forward problem that readily adapts to this setting.

This gives rise to a local Neumann-to-Dirichlet (ND) map on
\Gamma
, which is a compact self-adjoint operator
\Lambda(\sigma)\colon f\mapsto u|_{\Gamma}
on
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
. In the following, we will use ND maps for various conductivity coefficients taking the role of
\sigma
. We will denote the
unknown
conductivity as
\gamma
, that we seek to reconstruct from local boundary measurements.

We assume that

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\overline{P_{j}},

where
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\subseteq\Omega
are nonempty pairwise disjoint Lipschitz domains. Moreover, the sets are ordered such that
\partial P_{1}\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset
and for any
m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
then

Q_{m}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{P_{j}}

has connected interior. See Figure
2.1
for some examples; of course the sets
\{P_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are not required to be “pixel-shaped”, but can be arbitrary Lipschitz domains.

The unknown conductivity
\gamma
is assumed to be piecewise constant, satisfying

\gamma|_{P_{j}}\equiv\gamma_{j}

for some
\gamma_{j}\in(0,\infty)
,
j=1,\dots,n
.

Figure 2.1.
Examples of ordering of the
P_{j}
sets. Left: Reconstructing the outermost pixels first, then proceeding to the next “layer” of pixels. Right: Example of a ROI (blue dashed outline), where the reconstruction is adapted to this.

We will now proceed to show how to reconstruct
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
successively. We start by showing how this is done for infinitely many measurements
\Lambda(\gamma)
in Section
3
, and afterwards show that this can be reduced to finitely many measurements in Section
4
.

3.
The reconstruction method for infinite measurements

The method starts at
P_{1}
, reconstructs
\gamma_{1}
via a one-dimensional optimization problem, and moves on to
P_{2}
. Successively all of
\gamma
is reconstructed on
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
. Hence, we will assume that we know
\gamma
on
Q_{m-1}
(with the convention that
Q_{0}=\emptyset
) and now need to reconstruct
\gamma_{m}
on
P_{m}
.

In the following, for self-adjoint operators
A
and
B
on
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
, the inequality
A\geq B
means that

\langle(A-B)f,f\rangle\geq 0

for all
f\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
, which is the Loewner ordering of such operators.

For
t>0
and with
\mu
either representing
0
(perfectly insulating) or
\infty
(perfectly conducting), we denote by
\Lambda_{m,\mu}(t)
the local ND map for the coefficient

\begin{cases}\mu&\text{in }\overline{\Omega}\setminus Q_{m},\\
t&\text{in }P_{m},\\
\gamma&\text{in }Q_{m-1}.\end{cases}

An application of
[
2
, Proof of Theorem 3.7]
entails the following monotonicity relations:

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{m}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad\Lambda(\gamma)\geq\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t),

(3.1)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{m}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad\Lambda_{m,0}(t)\geq\Lambda(\gamma).

(3.2)

Hence, one can solve either of the following one-dimensional optimization problems:

\gamma_{m}=\min\{\,t>0:\Lambda(\gamma)\geq\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\,\}

or

\gamma_{m}=\max\{\,t>0:\Lambda_{m,0}(t)\geq\Lambda(\gamma)\,\}.

Note that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) show that
t=\gamma_{m}
is the unique value where it changes from “the operator inequality is satisfied” to “the operator inequality is
not
satisfied”.

Remark 3.1
.

Note the interesting fact, that the approach using
\Lambda_{m,0}(t)
corresponds to “building” the domain one pixel
P_{m}
at a time, since having a perfectly insulating coefficient corresponds to deleting that part of the domain in the PDE problem.

4.
The reconstruction method for finite measurements

Let
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
and let
\mathcal{P}_{M}
be the orthogonal projection onto

\mathop{\textup{span}}\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}.

We will now show that, up to an arbitrarily small error
\epsilon>0
, there exists an
M\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the operator inequalities
\Lambda(\gamma)\geq\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)
or
\Lambda_{m,0}(t)\geq\Lambda(\gamma)
can be replaced by the finite-dimensional versions given by

\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}\geq 0

or

\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda_{m,0}(t)-\Lambda(\gamma)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}\geq 0.

In particular, the following theorem gives an
M_{m}
for each
m\in\{1,\dots,m\}
needed for the different optimization problems, and we may therefore use the finite number

M=\max_{m}M_{m}

of boundary measurements.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
M_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
M\geq M_{m}
, in the setting of Section
3
,

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{m}

implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle<\gamma_{m}-\epsilon\qquad

implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}

\displaystyle\not\geq 0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{m}

implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda_{m,0}(t)-\Lambda(\gamma)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle>\gamma_{m}+\epsilon

implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda_{m,0}(t)-\Lambda(\gamma)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}

\displaystyle\not\geq 0.

(4.4)

Proof.

We will focus on (
4.1
)–(
4.2
), since the proof related to (
4.3
)–(
4.4
) is essentially the same.

If
t\geq\gamma_{m}
, then by the self-adjointness of
\mathcal{P}_{M}
and by (
3.1
), we have

\langle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}f,f\rangle=\langle\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}f,\mathcal{P}_{M}f\rangle\geq 0

for all
f\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
and any
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now assume
t<\gamma_{m}-\epsilon
. Let
\Lambda_{\infty}
be the ND map for the coefficient

\begin{cases}\infty&\text{in }\overline{\Omega}\setminus Q_{m},\\
\gamma&\text{in }Q_{m}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

By
[
2
, Lemma A.1]
,

\displaystyle\langle\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{\infty}\bigr)f,f\rangle+\langle\bigl(\Lambda_{\infty}-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)f,f\rangle

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\Omega\setminus Q_{m}}\lvert\nabla u_{f}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+(t-\gamma_{m})\int_{P_{m}}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{f}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\Omega\setminus Q_{m}}\lvert\nabla u_{f}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\epsilon\int_{P_{m}}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{f}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.6)

Here
K>0
is independent of
f
and
t
,
u_{f}
is the electric potential with Neumann condition
f
and conductivity
\gamma
, while
\widehat{u}_{f}
is the electric potential with Neumann condition
f
and the conductivity from (
4.5
). Hence, by the theory of localized potentials
[
2
, Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3]
, there exists a sequence
(f_{i})
in
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Bigl[K\int_{\Omega\setminus Q_{m}}\lvert\nabla u_{f_{i}}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\epsilon\int_{P_{m}}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{f_{i}}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr]=-\infty.

(4.7)

Pick an index
i_{0}
such that the expression in the square brackets of (
4.7
) is negative and let
g=f_{i_{0}}
. By continuity of
f\mapsto u_{f}
and
f\mapsto\widehat{u}_{f}
, there exists an
M_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
M\geq M_{m}
,

K\int_{\Omega\setminus Q_{m}}\lvert\nabla u_{\mathcal{P}_{M}g}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\epsilon\int_{P_{m}}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{\mathcal{P}_{M}g}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<0.

Hence, for any
t<\gamma_{m}-\epsilon
and any
M\geq M_{m}
, (
4.6
) gives

\langle\mathcal{P}_{M}\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}g,g\rangle=\langle\bigl(\Lambda(\gamma)-\Lambda_{m,\infty}(t)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{M}g,\mathcal{P}_{M}g\rangle<0.\qed

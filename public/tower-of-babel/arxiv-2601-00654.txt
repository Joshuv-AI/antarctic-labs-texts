1.
Introduction

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a function in
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For a family of operators
\mathcal{A}:=\{A_{\lambda}:\lambda\in D\}
acting on
f
and
r\in[1,\infty]
, we define the
r
-variations corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}f=\{A_{\lambda}f:\lambda\in D\}
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle V_{r}(\mathcal{A}f)(x):=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{L\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{\lambda_{1}<\dots<\lambda_{L}}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{L-1}|A_{\lambda_{l+1}}f(x)-A_{\lambda_{l}}f(x)|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},&0<r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}}\left|A_{\lambda_{1}}f(x)-A_{\lambda_{0}}f(x)\right|,&r=\infty,\end{cases}

where the former supremum is taken over all finite increasing sequences
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{L}\}
belonging to
D
. The study of
r
-variation was initiated in the work of Lépingle
[
13
]
. Bourgain’s seminal work
[
5
]
on variational estimates for ergodic averages introduced a new direction of research in harmonic analysis. Since then, extensive studies of
r
-variation for various classes of discrete and continuous averages have been conducted. We refer to
[
4
,
19
,
18
,
20
,
11
]
and references therein for more details. In partiuclar, Jones, Seeger, and Wright
[
11
]
studied
L^{p}-
estimates of the
r
-variation for continuous spherical averages. To the best of our knowledge, the study of variational estimates for the class of discrete averages over certain surfaces, which includes the sphere, are not known.
The purpose of this article is to initiate the study of
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
-boundedness of
r
-variations of the discrete Birch-Magyar averages and discrete Hardy-Littlewood type averages over certain algebraic varieties.

1.1.
Birch-Magyar averages

The framework for the Birch-Magyar averages was developed in the work of Birch
[
3
]
and Magyar
[
17
]
. Let us first briefly introduce the notion of Birch-Magyar averages. Recall that a homogeneous polynomial with integral coefficients is called an integral form. Let
\mathfrak{R}(x)
be an integral form of degree
d>1
in
n
variables. The Birch rank of
\mathfrak{R}
, denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R}),
is defined by

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R})=n-\text{dim}(\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{R})),

where

\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{R})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\partial_{z_{1}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=\partial_{z_{2}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=\cdots=\partial_{z_{n}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=0\}.

We use the following standard notation

c_{\mathfrak{R}}=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R})}{(d-1)2^{d-1}}~~\text{and}~~\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}=\frac{1}{6d}\left(\frac{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2}-1\right).

Note that
d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}<c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2
. For a given compactly supported smooth function
\phi
with
0\leq\phi\leq 1
, an integral form
\mathfrak{R}(x)
of degree
d>1
in
n
variables is called
\phi-
regular if there exists a non-singular solution of
\mathfrak{R}(x)=1
in the support of
\phi
and Birch rank of
\mathfrak{R}(x)
is greater than
(d-1)2^{d}
. We note that
c_{\mathfrak{R}}>2
if
\mathfrak{R}(x)
is
\phi-
regular.

Consider the counting function
r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{R}(x)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)
. It is known, see
[
3
,
17
]
, that for a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}(x),
there is an infinite arithmetic progression
\Lambda
of regular values
\lambda\in\Lambda
satisfying
r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)\approx\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}-1}
. Henceforth, for a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}(x)
, let
\Lambda
denote such an infinite arithmetic progression. We refer the readers to
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
, and
[
6
]
for more details on
\phi-
regular forms. For a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}(x)
of degree
d>1
in
n
variables and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the Birch-Magyar average of
f
is defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)}\sum_{y:\mathfrak{R}(y)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)f(x-y).

Note that the definition above encompasses the
k-
spherical averages studied in
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
1
]
. Recall that a sequence
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
is called lacunary if there exists
c>1
such that
\frac{\lambda_{l+1}}{\lambda_{l}}\geq c,
for all
l\geq 1.
For a lacunary sequence
\mathbb{L}=\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}\subset\Lambda
, the lacunary maximal function associated to the Birch-Magyar averages is defined as

A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)=\sup\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{L}}|A_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|.

Cook and Hughes
[
6
]
prove that the lacunary maximal operator
A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
for
p>\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
extending the result of Kesler, Lacey and Mena
[
12
]
in the spherical case. Moreover, building on a counterexample of Zienkiewicz, they showed that there exists a lacunary sequence
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
such that the corresponding maximal operator
A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is not bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
for the range
1<p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. However, it remains unknown that whether the maximal operator
A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
in the range
\frac{n}{n-1}\leq p\leq\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
. We refer the readers to
[
12
,
8
,
2
]
for discrete maximal functions over sparser sequences, for which the boundedness does hold for
p>1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}=\{A_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}:\;\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
be the family of Birch-Magyar averages over lacunary sequence
\mathbb{L}\subset\>\Lambda
. We define the long
r-
variations associated to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}
as follows.

V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)(x):=V_{r}(\{A_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\})(x).

We note that

|A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|\leq|A_{\lambda_{0}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(x)|+2V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)(x),

holds for any
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq r<\infty
. Thus, the
\ell^{p}-
bounds for the long
r-
variations imply the corresponding bounds for the lacunary maximal operator
A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}
. Our first main result concerns the
\ell^{p}-
bounds for the long
r-
variations associated to the Birch-Magyar averages. We have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}(x)
be a
\phi-
regular form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
and
\mathbb{L}\subset\Lambda
be a lacunary sequence. Then for
r>max\{p,p^{\prime}\}
with
p>\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
, we have

(1.3)

\|V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
given by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

The range of
r
in the above theorem is sharp for
p=2
, see for instance
[
11
]
.
In view of the pointwise inequality
V_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f)(x)\leq 2A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)
, the
\ell^{p}-
boundedness of
A_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R}}
(
[
6
, Theorem 1]
) for
p>\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
and interpolation, it is enough to prove
Theorem

1.1
for
p=2
. Moreover, to prove the inequality (
1.3
) for
p=2
, we will rely on the jump inequalities corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}
. In that regard, we define the
\lambda
-jump corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}
, denoted by
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}})
, as the supremum of all integers
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
for which there is an increasing sequence
0<u_{1}<v_{1}\leq u_{2}<v_{2}\leq\cdots\leq u_{M}<v_{M}
with
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathbb{L}
such that

|A_{u_{l}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(x)-A_{v_{l}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(x)|>\lambda

holds for each
l=1,\dots,M
. We refer the readers to
[
11
]
for various properties involving
\lambda-
jumps. The following lemma states that the
\ell^{2}-
jump inequality implies the
\ell^{2}-
boundedness of the
r-
variation operator for
r>2
. The following lemma is the discrete analogue of
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
. Since the proof of this lemma is exactly the same as that of
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
, we skip the details.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
11
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of linear operators that maps
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
to the set of measurable functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Suppose

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{T}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

holds for all
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then we have for
r>2
,

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}},

for all
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.

Therefore,
Theorem

1.1
will be a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{L}\subset\Lambda
be a lacunary sequence. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}},

for all
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
is based on the ideas from
[
11
]
, where the jump inequalities were obtained for the family of continuous operators. However, the case of discrete Birch-Magyar averages requires additional ingredients. The first is a decomposition of the input function from
[
14
]
which serves as the discrete analogue of Littlewood-Paley theory for
\ell^{2}-
functions. Secondly, we require the key decomposition of the Birch-Magyar averages using the Hardy-Littlewood circle method and the estimates of the error term from
[
6
,
17
]
.

1.2.
Averaging operators over certain algebraic varieties:

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be an integral form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
. We say
\mathfrak{P}
is regular if the following holds

(1)

The equation
\mathfrak{P}(x)=0
has a non-singular solution in every
p-
adic completion of
{\mathbb{Q}}
(including
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\infty}={\mathbb{R}}
).

(2)

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{P})>(d-1)2^{d}
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{P})
is the Birch rank of
\mathfrak{P}
.

For a function
f\in\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we define the averages over the algebraic variety
\mathfrak{P}(x)=0
by

M_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{P}}(\lambda)}\sum_{y\in[\lambda]^{n}:\mathfrak{P}(y)=0}f(x-y),

where
r_{\mathfrak{P}}(\lambda)=\left(\sum\limits_{y\in[\lambda]^{n};\mathfrak{P}(y)=0}1\right)\approx\lambda^{n-d}
. Cook
[
7
]
showed that the maximal operator
M_{*}^{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)=\sup_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}}|M_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)|
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for all
p>1
.
In our next result, we discuss the
r-
variation associated to the family of operators
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}=\{M_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{P}}:\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
, where
\mathbb{L}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
is a lacunary sequence. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a regular form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
. Then for
p>1
and
r>max\{p,p^{\prime}\}
, we have

\|V_{r}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}.

By the pointwise comparison
V_{\infty}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f)(x)\leq 2M_{*}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)
, and the
\ell^{p}-
boundedness
[
7
, Theorem 1]
of the operator
M_{*}^{\mathfrak{R}}
for
p>1
, it is enough to prove
Theorem

1.4
for
p=2
. Moreover,
Theorem

1.4
for
p=2
will be a consequence of the following jump inequality by an application of
Lemma

1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}(x)
be a regular form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
. Then for
r>2
and
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
, we have

\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

The proof of
Theorem

1.5
follows by similar arguments as that of
Theorem

1.3
. We provide a brief sketch of proof, indicating the necessary changes.

1.3.
Variational inequalities for ergodic averages

In this section, we discuss the application of our main theorems in proving variational bounds for the ergodic Birch-Magyar averages, which are defined as follows. Let
(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
T=(T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{n})
be a family of commuting, measure preserving and invertible transformations. For every
f\in L^{1}(X),
we define the ergodic Birch-Magyar averages by

\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{{\mathfrak{R}},\phi}(\lambda)}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:{\mathfrak{R}}(y)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)f\left(T_{1}^{y_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{y_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{y_{n}}x\right),

where
{\mathfrak{R}}
is a
\phi-
regular form.
Such averages were considered by Magyar in
[
17
]
, where an
L^{2}-
ergodic theorem was established for such averages.

Example 1.6
.

Let
X={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
\mu
be the counting measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, and
T_{i}^{y_{j}}(x)=x-y_{j}e_{i}
for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
with
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Then, one can see that the ergodic Birch-Magyar average
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f
is exactly equal to
A_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

For a lacunary sequence
\mathbb{L}\subset\Lambda
, let us denote the family
\{\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}:\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
by
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}
. We establish the following variational estimate for the family
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f=\{\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f:\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a
\phi-
regular form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
and
\mathbb{L}\subset\Lambda
be a lacunary sequence. Then for
r>max\{p,p^{\prime}\}
and
p>\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
, we have

\|V_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

The proof of this theorem will be a consequence of
Theorem

1.1
along with the transference principle stated below.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
r>2
. Suppose the following estimate

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f:\;\lambda\in D)\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}

holds for all
f\in\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
. Then for every
g\in L^{p}(X)
we have the inequality

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f:\;\lambda\in D)\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

The proof of
Theorem

1.8
is based on the arguments used in
[
4
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Remark 1.9
.

The ergodic analogue of
Theorem

1.4
can be established using a similar argument to that of
Theorem

1.7
. We leave the details to the reader.

Let
\psi

\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi)(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }\xi\in Q,\\

where

For

and

\psi_{s,J}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\frac{s}{J}\right)^{n}\psi\left(\frac{x}{J}\right)&\text{ if }x\in(s{\mathbb{Z}})^{n},\\

For
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
0\leq j\leq J_{l}:=\left[\log_{2}(l)\right]-2
, we define
\Psi_{l,j}=\psi_{s_{j},2^{l-j}}
, and
\Delta\Psi_{l,j}=\Psi_{l,j+1}-\Psi_{l,j}
. We will need the following property of

from
[
14
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
14

The estimate

\sum_{l\geq 2^{j}}|\mathscr{F}_{Z^{n}}(\triangle\Psi_{l,j})(\xi)|^{2}\lesssim_{\Psi}1

holds uniformly in

and

.

Observe that by the Poisson summation formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{Z^{n}}(\psi_{s,J})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\left(\frac{s}{J}\right)^{n}\psi\left(\frac{s}{J}x\right)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot s\xi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\psi_{J/s}(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot s\xi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi_{J/s})(x+s\xi)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi)\left(\frac{J}{s}(x+s\xi)\right).

Note that due to the support condition of
\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi)
,
eq.

2.2
implies that

|\mathscr{F}_{Z^{n}}(\psi_{s,J})(\xi)|\lesssim 1.

Now, motivated by
[
14
]
, we write
f=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}
, where

\displaystyle f_{1}=f*\Psi_{l,0},\;f_{2}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{J_{l}-1}f*\Delta\Psi_{l,j},\;\text{and}\;f_{3}=\left(f-f*\Psi_{l,J_{l}}\right).

Using the properties of

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f_{1}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f_{2}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f_{3}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Therefore, our job is to prove the estimate

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f_{i}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}~~

for all
i=1,2,3
.
The pointwise inequality

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lambda\left[\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)(x)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|A_{\lambda_{l}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)(x)=\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\infty}|A_{\lambda_{l}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The following

for

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq j\leq\log_{2}(l)-2
, and
C_{j,l}=\text{max}\{2^{-j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2)},2^{-ld\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\}
then the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{2^{l}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq 2^{l+1}}\left\|A_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim C_{j,l}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

holds whenever
\operatorname{supp}\hat{f}\subseteq\Omega_{j,l}^{c}
.

Proof.

Let
w_{\lambda_{l},\mathfrak{R}}
denote the kernel of the operator
A_{\lambda_{l}}^{\mathfrak{R}}
. Then, from
[
17
, Lemma 1]
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{Z^{n}}(w_{\lambda,\mathfrak{R}})(\xi)=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}e_{q}(-\lambda a)F_{q}(a,b)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)+O(\lambda^{-\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})

Here in the expression above,
\zeta
is a radial Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta)(\xi)
is identically
1
for
|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
0
if
|\xi|\geq 1
with
\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})(\xi)=\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta)(q\xi)
. We use the notation
F_{q}(a,b)
to denote the normalized Weyl sum which is defined as

F_{q}(a,b)=\frac{1}{q^{n}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}e\left(\mathfrak{R}(m)\frac{a}{q}+\frac{m\cdot b}{q}\right),

where
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
.
We have the following estimate
[
6
, p. 3862]
for the quantity
F_{q}(a,b)
:

|F_{q}(a,b)|\lesssim q^{-c_{\mathfrak{R}}},\quad\text{for}\quad\gcd(a,q)=1.

The measure
d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}
is defined by

d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}(y)=\phi(y)\frac{d\mu(y)}{|\nabla\mathfrak{R}(y)|},

where
d\mu
is Euclidean surface measure on the hypersurface
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathfrak{R}(x)=1\}
. It is well known
[
17
, Lemma 6]
that the Fourier transform of
d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies the following.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}})(\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Next, by the estimate
2.6
, for any
2^{l}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq 2^{l+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|A_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}e_{q}(-\lambda a)F_{q}(a,b)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\hat{f}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\;+O(2^{-ld\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Since
q|s_{j}
for
q\leq 2^{j}
and for
\xi\in\Omega_{j,l}^{c}
,
|\xi-\frac{b}{q}|=|\xi-\frac{s_{j}b/q}{s_{j}}|\geq 2^{j-l}
, where
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is closest to
q\xi
, combining this with the estimate
2.7
, we get that

|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}})(2^{l}(\xi-\frac{b}{q}))|\lesssim 2^{-j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1)}.

This observation along with the facts that the smooth cutoff
\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})(\xi-\frac{b}{q})
is non-zero exactly for one
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
which is closest to
q\xi
, and
\operatorname{supp}\hat{f}\subset\Omega_{j,l}^{c}
, yields the following.

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{q=1}^{2^{j}}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{{\mathfrak{R}}}}}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\frac{1}{\left|\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right|^{c_{{\mathfrak{R}}}-1}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+O(2^{-ld\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{q=1}^{2^{j}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}\times\frac{1}{2^{j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1)}}+O(2^{-ld\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{j,l}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Now, for
q>2^{j}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{q=2^{j}}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{{\mathfrak{R}}}}}\left|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\right||\hat{f}(\xi)|\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{q=2^{j}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Thus, combining the estimate
2.8
with
2.9
and
2.10
, the proof of
Proposition

2.5
follows.

Proof of
2.3
for
i=1
.

For each non-negative integer
l
, we write

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}Q_{t}^{l},

where
Q_{t}^{l}
is the set of all lattice points lying in the dyadic cube
2^{l}(t+[0,1)^{n})
. Observe that, the collection
\{Q_{t}^{l}:\;t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\}
forms a partition of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that for
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
we have either
Q_{t}^{l_{1}}\subset Q_{t}^{l_{2}}
or
Q_{t}^{l_{1}}\cap Q_{t}^{l_{2}}=\emptyset
. Furthermore, for each
Q_{t_{1}}^{l_{1}}
with
l_{1}<l_{2}
, there exists a unique
t_{2}
satisfying
Q_{t_{1}}^{l_{1}}\subset Q_{t_{2}}^{l_{2}}
. We write

\displaystyle A^{\mathfrak{R}}_{\lambda_{l}}f_{1}

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*f-E_{l}f+E_{l}f,

where
E_{l}f=\frac{1}{|Q_{t}^{l}|}\sum\limits_{y\in Q_{t}^{l}}f(y)
, and
w_{\lambda,\mathfrak{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}-1}}1_{\{y:\mathfrak{R}(y)=\lambda\}}(x)\phi\left(\frac{y}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)
.

Consider
\mathbb{E}f:=\{E_{l}f:l\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
\mathcal{L}f:=\left\{(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},{\mathfrak{R}}})*f-E_{l}f:l\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
. Note that

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathfrak{R}}f)\leq\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathcal{L}f)+\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathbb{E}f).

We recall the well-known, see
[
11
]
for instance,
L^{p}-
estimate

\left\|\lambda\left(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{E}f)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},~\;1<p<\infty.

In view of this and the estimate
2.4
, the proof of
2.3
for
i=1
, is reduced to showing that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{L}f)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*f-E_{l}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Define
D_{m}f(x)=E_{m}f(x)-E_{m-1}f(x)
and observe that
f=\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{m}f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
and
\delta>0
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

We assume
Lemma

2.1
for the moment and complete the proof of
2.11
.

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*f-E_{l}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*f-E_{l}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Proof of
Lemma

2.1
.

We consider the cases
l\geq m
and
l<m
separately.

Case 1.

Assume that
l\geq m
. Then we have
E_{l}(E_{m}f)=E_{l}f
, which implies that
E_{l}(D_{m}f)=E_{l}(E_{m}f-E_{m-1}f)=0.
Hence
\sum\limits_{y\in Q_{t}^{m}}D_{m}f=0
. This helps us to write

\displaystyle(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{y\in Q_{t}^{m}}\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x-y)D_{m}f(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{y\in Q_{t}^{m}}\left[\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x-y)-\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x-q_{t}^{m})\right]D_{m}f(y),

where
q_{t}^{m}
is the center of the cube
Q_{t}^{m}
. By the mean-value theorem, we get that

\displaystyle|\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x-y)-\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x-q_{t}^{m})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{ln+l(\frac{n}{d}-1)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z:\\
\mathfrak{R}(z)=2^{l}\end{subarray}}\left|\phi\left(\frac{z}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\right|\left|\psi\left(\frac{x-y-z}{2^{l}}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x-q_{t}^{m}-z}{2^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{ln+l(\frac{n}{d}-1)}}\frac{|y-q_{t}^{m}|}{2^{l}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\left[-2^{\frac{l}{d}},2^{\frac{l}{d}}\right]^{n}:\\
\mathfrak{R}(z)=2^{l}\end{subarray}}\left|\tilde{\psi}\left(\frac{x-y-z}{2^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(l-m)}\left|\tilde{\psi}_{2^{l}}(x-y-z_{0})\right|,

where
\tilde{\psi}
is a Schwartz function. Therefore, we get that

\displaystyle|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(l-m)}\mathcal{M}(D_{m}f)(x-z_{0}),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, which is well-known to be bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}),1<p\leq\infty
. Therefore, for
l\geq m
, we get the desired estimate

\displaystyle\|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Case 2.

Assume that
l<m.
In this case
E_{l}(E_{m}f)=E_{m}f
, which in turn implies that
E_{l}(D_{m}f)=D_{m}f
. Next, we use the Poisson summation formula and the support of
\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi)
to get that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(2^{l})}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y:\\
\mathfrak{R}(y)=2^{l}\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\frac{1}{2^{ln}}\psi\left(\frac{x-y}{2^{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(2^{l})}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y:\\
\mathfrak{R}(y)=2^{l}\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\frac{y}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\psi)(2^{l}x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot y}=1.

It follows from this observation that,

\displaystyle(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})*D_{m}f(x)-E_{l}(D_{m}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}I_{k}(x),

where
I_{k}(x)=\sum\limits_{y\in E_{l,k}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)
and
E_{l,k}=\left\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:2^{l+k-1}\leq|y|\leq 2^{l+k}\right\}
.
Consider

\displaystyle\|I_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\left|\sum\limits_{y\in E_{l,k}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in E_{l,k}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}|D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)|^{2}|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{y\in E_{l,k}}|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)|.

Note that for
|s|\leq|{\mathfrak{R}}(s)|=2^{l}
and
y\in E_{l,k}
, we have that
2^{l+k-1}\leq 2^{l}+|y|-|s|
. Therefore, for any large number
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
we get that

\displaystyle|(\Psi_{l,0}*w_{2^{l},\mathfrak{R}})(y)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{l(\frac{n}{d}-1)+ln}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\\
\mathfrak{R}(s)=2^{l}\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\frac{s}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\psi_{1,2^{l}}(y-s)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{l(\frac{n}{d}-1)+ln}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{R}(s)=2^{l},\\
y-s\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left|\phi\left(\frac{s}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\psi\left(\frac{y-s}{2^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{l(\frac{n}{d}-1)+ln}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{R}(s)=2^{l},\\
y-s\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left|\phi\left(\frac{s}{2^{\frac{l}{d}}}\right)\right|\frac{2^{lM}}{(2^{l}+|y|-|s|)^{M}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-M(k-1)-ln}.

On the other hand, when
|\mathfrak{R}(s)|\leq|s|
, we notice that
|s|
cannot exceed
2^{\frac{l}{d}}
due to the support of
\phi
, thereby implying that the previous inequality holds analogously for the case
|\mathfrak{R}(s)|\leq|s|
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and consider the case
k\geq\epsilon|l-m|
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\|I_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{k\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\sum_{y\in E_{l,k}}2^{-M(k-1)-ln}

\displaystyle=\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{k\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\frac{2^{-l(n-1)}}{2^{(M-1)(k-1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2^{-(M-1)|l-m|\epsilon}}{2^{l(n-1)}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2^{-|l-m|\epsilon^{\prime}}}{2^{l(n-1)}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Next, we consider the remaining terms for
k\leq\epsilon|l-m|
. First, observe that for
x\in\{s\in Q_{t}^{m-1}:\text{dist}(s,{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\setminus Q_{t}^{m-1})\geq 2^{k+m+(l-m)}\}
and
|y|\leq 2^{k+l},
we have that
x,x-y\in Q_{t}^{m-1}
. This implies that
D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)=0
. Therefore, in order to estimate the
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})-
norm of
I_{k}(x)
, we only need to consider the set

U=\{s\in Q_{t}^{m-1}:\text{dist}(s,{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\setminus Q_{t}^{m-1})\leq 2^{k+m+(l-m)}\}.

Note that we have

|U|\leq 2^{k\eta-\eta|l-m|}|Q_{t}^{m-1}|\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}|Q_{t}^{m-1}|.

Consider

\displaystyle\|I_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{x\in Q_{t}^{m-1}}|I_{k}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t}\sum_{x\in U}|I_{k}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t}\sup\limits_{x\in U}|I_{k}(x)|^{2}|U|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{t}|Q_{t}^{m-1}|\sup_{x\in Q_{t}^{m-1}}|I_{k}(x)|^{2}.

Notice that, for
x\in Q_{t}^{m-1}
and
|y|\leq 2^{l+k}
, the point
x-y\in B(q_{t}^{m-1},C2^{m})\supseteq Q_{t}^{m-1}
.
Thus,

\sup\limits_{x\in Q_{t}^{m-1}}|I_{k}(x)|^{2}\lesssim\sup\limits_{x\in B(q_{t}^{m-1},C2^{m})}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}.

Recall that, the number of integer lattice points in a ball of radius
C2^{m}
is of the order of
2^{mn}
. Hence, the cardinality of the set
V_{t,m}=\{h:Q_{h}^{m-1}\cap B(q_{t}^{m-1},C2^{m})\neq\phi\}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
t
. Moreover, for each
h
, the number of
t
such that
h\in V_{t,m}
is uniformly bounded. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{t}\sum_{h\in V_{t,m}}\sum_{x\in Q_{h}^{m-1}}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{h}\sum_{x\in Q_{h}^{m-1}}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon|l-m|}\|I_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon|l-m|}2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\bar{\eta}|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

This completes the proof of
Lemma

2.1
and consequently the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is complete. ∎

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda,{\mathfrak{R}}}(\xi)=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\tau_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)+O(\lambda^{-\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}),

where
F_{q}(a,b)
and
\zeta
are as defined earlier. The measure
d\tau_{{\mathfrak{R}}}
is given by

d\tau_{\mathfrak{R}}(y)=\phi\left(y\right)\frac{d\nu(y)}{|\nabla\mathfrak{R}(y)|},

where
d\nu
is Euclidean surface measure on
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathfrak{R}(x)=0\}
. Note that the measure
d\tau_{{\mathfrak{R}}}
satisfies the estimate
2.7
.

Now observe that, for any
2^{l}\leq\lambda\leq 2^{l+1}
and
\operatorname{supp}\hat{f}\subset\Omega_{j,l}^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|M_{\lambda,{\mathfrak{R}}}f\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\tau_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\hat{f}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+O(2^{-l\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{{\mathfrak{R}}}}}\left|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\zeta_{q})\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(d\tau_{\mathfrak{R}})\left(\lambda\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\right||\hat{f}(\xi)|\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})}+O(2^{-l\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{q=1}^{2^{j}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}\times\frac{1}{2^{j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1)}}+\sum_{q=2^{j}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}+O(2^{-l\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}})\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{j,l}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}},

where
C_{j,l}=\text{max}\{2^{-j(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2)},2^{-ld\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\}
.

This estimate along with the arguments given in
Sections

2.1
and
2.2
completes the proof of
Theorem

1.5
. ∎

4.
Proof of
Theorem

1.8

Let us fix
f\in L^{p}(X),~\eta>0,~\Lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for every
x\in X
define the function

\gamma_{x}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f\left(T_{1}^{y_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{y_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{y_{n}}x\right),&\text{ if }\|y\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right),\\
\\
0,&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Also, for every positive integer
\lambda
, define the set

G_{\lambda}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:\|x\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\right\}.

For each
m\in G_{\Lambda}
and
y\in G_{\lambda}
with
\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}<\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n
, since we have
\|m-y\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)
, the following equality holds:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(T_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{m_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{m_{n}}x)=\frac{1}{r_{{\mathfrak{R}},\phi}(\lambda)}\sum_{y:{\mathfrak{R}}(y)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)\gamma_{x}(m-y)=A_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}\gamma_{x}(m).

Now,
eq.

1.4
along with
eq.

4.1
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda}}|V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(T_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{m_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{m_{n}}x):\lambda\in D\cap(0,\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n))|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda}}|V_{r}(A_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}\gamma_{x}(m):\lambda\in D\cap(0,\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n))|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|V_{r}(A_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}\gamma_{x}(m):\lambda\in D)\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim\|\gamma_{x}\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}^{p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda}}\int_{x\in X}|V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f(T_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{m_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{m_{n}}x):\lambda\in D\cap(0,\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n))|^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)^{d}}}\int_{x\in X}|\gamma_{x}(m)|^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)^{d}}}\int_{x\in X}|f(T_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{m_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n}^{m_{n}}x)|^{p}d\mu(x).

For each
i
, since
T_{i}^{m_{i}}
is a measure presurving transformation, we get from the above inequality that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda}}\|V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f:\lambda\in D\cap(0,\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n))\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in G_{\Lambda\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)^{d}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p},

which implies

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda,\phi}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f:\lambda\in D\cap(0,\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\eta/n))\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(2\Lambda)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\left(2\Lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)\right)^{n}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}.

Applying
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
in the above inequality together with monotone convergence theorem we obtain that

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda,\phi}^{{\mathfrak{R}}}f:\lambda\in D)\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+\frac{\eta}{n}\right)^{n}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}.

Now, taking
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
we get
eq.

1.5
, which completes the proof of
Theorem

1.8
.∎

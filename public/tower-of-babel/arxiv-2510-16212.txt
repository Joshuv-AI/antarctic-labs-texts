Born series for s-wave scattering length and some exact results


Abstract
In these notes the Born series for the s s -wave scattering a 0 a_{0} is calculated for a class of central potentials V ⁡ ( r ) V(r) up to sixth order in a dimensionless coupling strength g g . Examples of exponentially decaying potentials as well as truncated potentials involving a single length-scale a a are considered. In certain favorable cases the exact result for the g g -dependent s s -wave scattering length a 0 = A 0 ​ ( g ) ​ a a_{0}=A_{0}(g)a can be given in terms of special functions. The poles of A 0 ​ ( g ) A_{0}(g) at increasing positive values of g g correspond to the thresholds, where s s -wave bound-states occur successively. A scattering problem, where A 0 ​ ( g ) A_{0}(g) is solvable in terms of elementary functions, is also presented.

1
Preparation

Consider the quantum mechanical scattering in a short-range central potential
V(r)
. The time-independent Schrödinger equation is equivalently transformed into an integral equation for the scattering wave-function
[
1
]

\psi_{k}(\vec{r}\,)=e^{ikz}-{m\over 2\pi\hbar^{2}}\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\,{\exp(ik|\vec{r}-\vec{r}\,^{\prime}|)\over|\vec{r}-\vec{r}\,^{\prime}|}V(r^{\prime})\psi_{k}(\vec{r}\,^{\prime})\,,

(1)

where the first part on the right hand side is the incoming planar wave in
z
-direction, and the second part is the scattered wave. Its asymptotic behavior for
|\vec{r}\,|\to\infty
has of the well-known form
[
1
]
: complex-valued scattering amplitude
f(k,\theta)
times an outgoing spherical wave
e^{ikr}/r
. The Born series is obtained by inserting for
\psi_{k}(\vec{r}\,^{\prime})
first the planar wave
e^{ikz^{\prime}}
, then adding the correction obtained this way linear in
V(r^{\prime})
, and iterating the procedure. The asymptotic behavior for
|\vec{r}\,|\to\infty
of all these contributions to the scattered wave determines the multiple scattering series
[
1
]
for the scattering amplitude
f(k,\theta)
. In the strict long-wavelength limit
k\to 0
, where the scattering amplitude
f(k,\theta)
reduces to the
s
-wave scattering length
a_{0}=f(0,\theta)
, this series simplifies drastically, and one obtains the following expansion for
a_{0}
:

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({-m\over 2\pi\hbar^{2}}\Big)\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}r_{1}V(r_{1})+\Big({-m\over 2\pi\hbar^{2}}\Big)^{2}\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}r_{1}\int\!\!d^{3}r_{2}{V(r_{1})V(r_{2})\over|\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}|}

(2)

\displaystyle+\Big({-m\over 2\pi\hbar^{2}}\Big)^{3}\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}r_{1}\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}r_{2}\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}r_{3}{V(r_{1})V(r_{2})V(r_{3})\over|\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}||\vec{r}_{2}-\vec{r}_{3}|}+\dots

Note that the occurring integrals over a central potential
V(r_{i})
, multiplied with the ”static propagator”
|\vec{r}_{i}-\vec{r}_{j}|^{-1}
, are of the same form as electrostatic potentials produced by radially symmetric charge densities. The pertinent angular integral is in each step given by

\int d\Omega_{i}\,{1\over|\vec{r}_{i}-\vec{r}_{j}|}={4\pi\over\text{max}(r_{i},r_{j})}\,,

(3)

where max
(r_{i},r_{j})
denotes the maximum of the two radial coordinates
r_{i}
and
r_{j}
.

In order to emphasize the expansion in a (small) parameter, it is advantageous to factor out the physical constants (
\hbar
and
m
) and choose for the central potential
V(r)
the ansatz:

V(r)=-{\hbar^{2}g\over 2ma^{2}}f(r/a)\,,

(4)

with
a
the inherent length-scale and
g
a dimensionless coupling strength. Moreover, the profile function
f(x)
specifies the dependence of the central potential
V(r)
on distance
r
. In this setting the
s
-wave scattering length is necessarily a multiple of the length-scale
a
, namely
a_{0}=A_{0}a
. The expansion of the dimensionless quantity
A_{0}
in powers of the dimensionless coupling strength
g
reads

A_{0}(g)=c_{1}g+c_{2}g^{2}+c_{3}g^{3}+c_{4}g^{4}+c_{5}g^{5}+c_{6}g^{6}+\dots\,,

(5)

where the first few coefficients are computed as

\displaystyle c_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dx\,x^{2}f(x)\,,\quad c_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dx\,x^{2}f(x)\Phi(x)\,,\quad c_{3}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dx\,x^{2}f(x)[\Phi(x)]^{2}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle c_{4}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dx_{1}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dx_{2}\,{x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}\over\text{max}(x_{1},x_{2})}\Phi(x_{1})f(x_{1})f(x_{2})\Phi(x_{2})=2\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dx_{1}\!\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\!\!\!\!dx_{2}\,x_{1}x_{2}^{2}\Phi(x_{1})f(x_{1})f(x_{2})\Phi(x_{2}).

Here,
\Phi(x)
is the ”electrostatic potential” produced by the ”charge density”
f(x)
:

\Phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx^{\prime}{x^{\prime 2}\over\text{max}(x,x^{\prime})}f(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{x}\!dx^{\prime}\,{x^{\prime 2}\over x}f(x^{\prime})+\int_{x}^{\infty}\!dx^{\prime}\,x^{\prime}f(x^{\prime})\,.

(7)

Higher coefficients
c_{j}
involve further factors of
x_{i}^{2}f(x_{i})/\text{max}(x_{i},x_{j})
in a multiple integral. Note that the increasing powers of the prefactor in the Born series (
-m/2\pi\hbar^{2}
) and in the potential (
-\hbar^{2}/2m
), together with
4\pi
from the angular integrations, have compensated each other at every order.

2
Exponentially decaying potentials

In this section the Born series
A_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{6}c_{j}g^{j}+\dots
is computed for a variety of central potentials
V(r)
with an exponential decay.

A:
The familiar Yukawa potential is physically well motivated in terms of an underlying one-boson exchange mechanism. The pertinent profile function
f(x)
and ”electrostatic potential”
\Phi(x)
are in this case

f(x)={e^{-x}\over x}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1-e^{-x}\over x}\,.

(8)

With this input one derives for the following Born series up to sixth order:

A_{0}(g)=g+{g^{2}\over 2}+g^{3}\ln{4\over 3}+g^{4}\ln{32\over 27}+0.100941276\,g^{5}+0.06006048\,g^{6}+\dots

(9)

Note that in a Feynman diagrammatic picture, where the Yukawa potential stems from a massive one-boson exchange, order
g
corresponds to tree-level, order
g^{2}
to one-loop, order
g^{3}
to two-loops, order
g^{4}
to three-loops, and so on. The two-loop coefficient
c_{3}=\ln(4/3)=0.28768207
has also been found in a momentum-space calculation of twice-iterated one-boson exchange in ref.
[
2
]
, while the three-loop coefficient
c_{4}=\ln(32/27)=0.16989904
seems to be a novel result. It is quite astonishing that even the four-loop coefficient
c_{5}=0.1009412761
can be given in exact form as

c_{5}={\pi^{2}\over 6}+(10+3\ln 2-2\ln 3)\ln 2-{9\over 2}\ln 3-{5\over 4}\ln 5+2\text{Li}_{2}\Big(\!-{1\over 2}\Big)-2\text{Li}_{2}\Big(\!-{1\over 3}\Big)+2\text{Li}_{2}\Big(\!-{2\over 3}\Big)\,,

(10)

where Li
{}_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}/n^{2}
denotes the dilogarithmic function.

The series for
A_{0}(g)
, with its first six terms written in eq.(8), has a finite radius of convergence
(|g|<g_{1})
, that is determined as that strength parameter
g_{1}
, where the threshold for the first
s
-wave bound-state in a Yukawa potential is reached. This particular value
g_{1}
can be computed by solving numerically the second-order differential equation for the radial wave-function (multiplied with
x
):

u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g{e^{-x}\over x}u(x)=0\,,

(11)

subject to a linear behavior
u(x)\sim x
at small
x
. Note that
u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\,x^{-1}u(x)=0
has the regular solution in the form of a Bessel-function
u(x)=\sqrt{g\,x}J_{1}(2\sqrt{g\,x})=g\,x+{\cal O}(x^{2})

[
3
]
, which also displays a linear behavior. Once the critical strength
g_{1}
is reached, the asymptotic behavior of
u(x)
for large
x
turns into a
constant
. The convergence of this process is monitored by inspecting, how the first derivative
u^{\prime}(x)
approaches zero for large
x
, when fine-tuning more and more digits of the strength parameter
g
. As a result of this numerical exercise, one finds that the first three thresholds
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
,
g_{3}
for
s
-wave bound-states in the Yukawa potential lie at the values:

g_{1}=1.679808\,,\qquad g_{2}=6.44726045\,,\qquad g_{3}=14.34202607\,.

(12)

Note that the earlier determinations
g_{1}=1.680,\,g_{2}=6.445,\,g_{3}=14.34
in ref.
[
4
]
are very close to the present results.

B:
Next, we consider a purely exponentially decreasing potential
V(r)\sim-g\,e^{-r/a}
with the profile function

f(x)=e^{-x}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={2\over x}-\Big(1+{2\over x}\Big)e^{-x}\,,

(13)

and on this basis, one derives the following Born series up to sixth order

A_{0}=2\,g+{5\over 4}\,g^{2}+{23\over 27}\,g^{3}+{677\over 1152}\,g^{4}+{7313\over 18000}\,g^{5}+{218491\over 777600}\,g^{6}+\dots

(14)

For the exponential potential
\sim-g\,e^{-x}
a little miracle happens, since (at
k=0
) the second-order linear homogeneous differential equation for the radial wave-function (multiplied with
x
):

u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\,e^{-x}u(x)=0\,,

(15)

possesses analytical solutions in the form of a Bessel-function
u_{1}(x)=J_{0}(2\sqrt{g}\,e^{-x/2})
and a Neumann-function
u_{2}(x)=N_{0}(2\sqrt{g}\,e^{-x/2})

[
3
]
of index
0
. The proper linear combination is of these two is found by demanding
u(0)=0
and an asymptotic behavior
u(x)\simeq x+A_{0}
for
x\to\infty
. The latter asymptotic behavior
u(x)\simeq x(1+A_{0}/x)
is provided by the logarithmic singularity of the Neumann-function
N_{0}(z)=2[\gamma_{E}+\ln(z/2)]/\pi+{\cal O}(z^{2}\ln z)
near
z=0
. Putting the pieces together, the resulting exact expression for the
s
-wave scattering length in the exponential potential reads:

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)={\pi N_{0}(2\sqrt{g})\over J_{0}(2\sqrt{g})}-\ln g-2\gamma_{E}\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g>0\,,

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)=-{2K_{0}(2\sqrt{-g})\over I_{0}(2\sqrt{-g})}-\ln(-g)-2\gamma_{E}\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g<0\,,

(16)

Figure 1:
Scattering length
A_{0}=a_{0}/a
versus
g
for exponential potential
V(r)\sim-g\,e^{-r/a}
.

with
\gamma_{E}=0.577215665
the Euler-Mascheroni number.
In the second formula (for
g<0
),
K_{0}
and
I_{0}
are modified Bessel-functions
[
3
]
that arise from the continuation of
N_{0}
and
J_{0}
to imaginary argument. The behavior of
A_{0}(g)
is shown in Fig. 1 for both positive and negative values of the strength parameter
-20<g<20
. One observes poles at positive values of
g
, which mark the thresholds for successive
s
-wave bound-states in the attractive exponential potential. According to exact formula in eq.(16), these thresholds can be identified with the zeros
z_{j}
of the Bessel-function
J_{0}(z)
through the relation
g_{j}=z_{j}^{2}/4
. The first six threshold values read

g_{1}\!=\!1.445796\,,\quad g_{2}\!=\!7.617816\,,\quad g_{3}\!=\!18.72175\,,\quad g_{4}\!=\!34.76007\,,\quad g_{5}\!=\!55.73308\,,\quad g_{6}\!=\!81.64084\,.

(17)

Note that the numerical method, employed before to construct
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
for the Yukawa potential, reproduces
g_{1}=1.4457965
,
g_{2}=7.6178156
,
g_{3}=18.7217517
for the profile
f(x)=e^{-x}
.

C:
As a further example, we choose an exponentially decreasing potential with a soft core:

f(x)=x\,e^{-x}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={6\over x}-\Big({6\over x}+4+x\Big)e^{-x}\,,

(18)

and derive the following Born series up to sixth order

A_{0}(g)=6\,g+{33\over 4}\,g^{2}+{5873\over 486}\,g^{3}+17.835277g^{4}+26.351768\,g^{5}+38.941427\,g^{6}+\dots\,,

(19)

where all expansion coefficients are positive rational numbers. The radius of convergence
|g|<g_{1}
of the series for
A_{0}(g)
written in eq.(19) is determined by searching the threshold for the first
s
-wave bound-state in the potential
V(r)\sim-g\,x\,e^{-x}
. One finds with high numerical accuracy the values

g_{1}=0.676681997\,,\qquad g_{2}=4.30765405\,,\qquad g_{3}=21.020136626\,.

(20)

D:
Next, we choose an exponentially decreasing potential with a very soft core, taking the profile function

f(x)=x^{2}e^{-x}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={24\over x}-\Big({24\over x}+18+6x+x^{2}\Big)e^{-x}\,,

(21)

and derive the following Born series up to sixth order

A_{0}(g)=24\,g+{837\over 8}\,g^{2}+479.53818\,g^{3}+2209.6103\,g^{4}+10188.272\,g^{5}+46981.234\,g^{6}+\dots\,,

(22)

where all expansion coefficients are (large) rational numbers. The radius of convergence
|g|<g_{1}
of the series for
A_{0}(g)
in eq.(22) is again determined by searching the threshold for the first
s
-wave bound-state in the potential
V(r)\sim-g\,x^{2}e^{-x}
. One finds with high numerical accuracy the values

g_{1}=0.216855259\,,\qquad g_{2}=1.580333075\,,\qquad g_{3}=8.027999134\,.

(23)

E:
Finally, one can consider an exponentially decreasing potential with a more singular behavior at small distances:

f(x)={e^{-x}\over x^{2}}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1-e^{-x}\over x}+\Gamma(0,x)\,,

(24)

where
\Gamma(0,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\!d\xi\,\xi^{-1}e^{-\xi}=-\ln x-\gamma_{E}+x-x^{2}/4+{\cal O}(x^{3})
denotes the incomplete
\Gamma
-function
[
3
]
. With this input one derives the following Born series up to sixth order:

A_{0}(g)=g+g^{2}\ln 4+2.98036548\,g^{3}+7.68193343\,g^{4}+21.86973\,g^{5}+66.2866\,g^{6}+\dots

(25)

The profile
f(x)=e^{-x}/x^{2}
is too singular near the origin and therefore the coupling strength
g
must be restricted to negative or small positive values
g<g_{\text{crit}}=1/4
. This feature concerning an instability is further elaborated on in the next section, when analyzing the truncated
1/r^{2}
-potential.

3
Truncated potentials

In this section we analyze short-range potentials, that vanish outside a sphere of radius
a
.

A:
A well-known textbook problem
[
1
]
is the quantum mechanical scattering off a spherical potential-well, represented by the profile

f(x)=\theta(1-x)\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1\over 2}-{x^{2}\over 6}\,,

(26)

and now the knowledge of the ”electrostatic potential”
\Phi(x)
is needed only in the inside region
0<x<1
. With little effort, one obtains the following Born series for the
s
-wave scattering length in a spherical potential-well up to sixth order:

A_{0}(g)={g\over 3}+{2\over 15}\,g^{2}+{17\over 315}\,g^{3}+{62\over 2835}g^{4}+{1382\over 155925}\,g^{5}+{21844\over 6081075}\,g^{6}+\dots

(27)

The analytical solution to the differential equation for the radial wave-function
u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\,u(x)=0
(in the region
0<x<1
) subject to the boundary condition
u(0)=0
is either a sine-function,
\sin(\sqrt{g}x)
, or a sine-hyperbolic function,
\sinh(\sqrt{-g}x)
. After matching the logarithmic derivative of
u(x)
at
x=1
to that of the linear form
u(x)=x+A_{0}
outside, one obtains the generic formula
A_{0}=u(1)/u^{\prime}(1)-1
for the
s
-wave scattering length in any truncated potential. In the present case one reproduces the well-known result

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)=-1+{1\over\sqrt{g}}\tan\sqrt{g}\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g>0\,,

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)=-1+{1\over\sqrt{-g}}\tanh\sqrt{-g}\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g<0\,,

(28)

and, as a nice check, the Taylor expansion of the tangent-function confirms the series in eq.(27). The analytical result for
A_{0}(g)
written in eq.(28) is shown in Fig. 2 in the region
-40<g<62
.
The poles of
A_{0}(g)
at the values
g_{j}=\pi^{2}(j-1/2)^{2}
(where
\tan\sqrt{g_{j}}
diverges) correspond to the well-known thresholds for the occurrence of successive
s
-wave bound-states in a spherical potential-well. The first six values of this infinite sequence read

g_{1}\!=\!2.467401\,,\quad g_{2}\!=\!22.20661\,,\quad g_{3}\!=\!61.68503\,,\quad g_{4}\!=\!120.9027\,,\quad g_{5}\!=\!199.8595\,,\quad g_{6}\!=\!298.5555\,.

(29)

Figure 2:
A_{0}
as a function of
g
for spherical well potential.

B:

It is also interesting to study the truncated
1/r^{2}
-potential with the profile function

f(x)=\Big({1\over x^{2}}-1\Big)\theta(1-x)\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1\over 2}+{x^{2}\over 6}-\ln x\,,

(30)

and this input leads to the following Born series for the
s
-wave scattering length up to sixth order

A_{0}(g)={2\over 3}\,g+{56\over 45}\,g^{2}+{14312\over 4725}\,g^{3}+8.364150\,g^{4}+24.881527\,g^{5}+77.758378\,g^{6}+\dots

(31)

The differential equation for the radial wave-function in the inner region
(0<x<1)
:

u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\Big({1\over x^{2}}-1\Big)u(x)=0\,,

(32)

possesses a regular solution in the form of a Bessel-function
u(x)=\sqrt{x}J_{\nu}(\sqrt{-g}x)
with index
\nu=\sqrt{1/4-g}
. Note that the strength parameter
g
has to be constrained to
g\leq 1/4
in order to evade the instability of the quantum mechanical problem with a too attractive
1/r^{2}
-potential. For comparison the regular solution of
u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\,x^{-2}u(x)=0
is for all
g\leq 1/4
given by
u(x)=x^{\nu+1/2}
. Under this restriction on
g
the logarithmic derivative
u^{\prime}(x)/u(x)
at
x=1
determines the dimensionless
s
-wave scattering length as
A_{0}=u(1)/u^{\prime}(1)-1
. In the present case this leads to the expression:

A_{0}(g)=\bigg\{{1+\sqrt{1-4g}\over 2}-\sqrt{-g}\,{J_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{-g})\over J_{\nu}(\sqrt{-g})}\bigg\}^{-1}-1\,,\qquad\nu={\sqrt{1-4g}\over 2}\,,

(33)

where the second term in curly brackets,
-\sqrt{-g}J_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{-g})/J_{\nu}(\sqrt{-g})
, means
\sqrt{g}I_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{g})/I_{\nu}(\sqrt{g})
for
0<g<1/4
. The exact result for
A_{0}(g)
written in eq.(33) is shown in Fig. 3 for the parameter range
-5<g<1/4
. As already visible in Fig. 2, and valid for any truncated potential, the scattering length approaches with increasing repulsion the hard-core value:
\lim_{g\to-\infty}A_{0}(g)=-1
. On the positive side the boundary value, before instability sets in, is given by

A_{0}(1/4)={I_{0}(1/2)-I_{1}(1/2)\over I_{0}(1/2)+I_{1}(1/2)}=0.6095845\,.

(34)

Figure 3:
A_{0}
as a function of
g
for truncated
1/r^{2}
-potential, restricted to range
g\leq 1/4
.

C:

Next, we consider the truncated Coulomb-potential with profile function

f(x)=\Big({1\over x}-1\Big)\theta(1-x)\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1-x\over 2}+{x^{2}\over 6}\,,

(35)

and derive the following Born series up to sixth order

A_{0}(g)={g\over 6}+{g^{2}\over 20}+{241\over 15120}\,g^{3}+{233\over 45360}\,g^{4}+{g^{5}\over 602.7393}+{g^{6}\over 1865.2312}+\dots

(36)

u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g(x^{-1}-1)u(x)=0

u(x)=x\,e^{-\sqrt{g}\,x}\,_{1}F_{1}(1-\sqrt{g}/2,2;2\sqrt{g}\,x)

A_{0}(g)=\bigg\{1-\sqrt{g}+{(\sqrt{g}-g/2)\,\,_{1}F_{1}(2-\sqrt{g}/2,3;2\sqrt{g})\over{}_{1}F_{1}(1-\sqrt{g}/2,2;2\sqrt{g})}\bigg\}^{-1}-1\,.

Let us form the difference of the truncated

f(x)=\Big({1\over x^{2}}-{1\over x}\Big)\theta(1-x)\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={x\over 2}-\ln x\,.

\mu=\sqrt{1-4g}

s
-wave scattering length

A_{0}(g)=\bigg\{{1+\sqrt{1-4g}\over 2}-\sqrt{-g}\,{J_{\mu+1}(2\sqrt{-g})\over J_{\mu}(2\sqrt{-g})}\bigg\}^{-1}-1\,,\qquad\mu=\sqrt{1-4g}\,,

, means

E:
A further interesting example is the truncated oscillator potential with the profile function

f(x)=(1-x^{2})\theta(1-x)\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={1\over 4}-{x^{2}\over 6}+{x^{4}\over 20}\,,

(43)

and one derives the following Born series up to sixth order

A_{0}(g)={2\over 15}\,g+{8\over 315}\,g^{2}+{1432\over 289575}\,g^{3}+{g^{4}\over 1036.0372}+{g^{5}\over 5306.4658}+{g^{6}\over 27178.112}+\dots

(44)

The solution of the differential equation
u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g(1-x^{2})u(x)=0
can be found in terms of parabolic cylinder functions
D_{\nu}(\sqrt{\pm 2}g^{1/4}x)

[
3
]
with respective indices
\nu=(-1\pm\sqrt{g})/2

A_{0}(g)={g\over 12}+{13\over 1260}\,g^{2}+{17\over 12960}\,g^{3}+{g^{4}\over 5977.6798}+{g^{5}\over 46852.232}+{g^{6}\over 367200.57}+\dots

The solution of the differential equation

for the radial wave-function (in the region

[

A_{0}(g)={\big[\sqrt{3}\text{Ai}(-g^{1/3})-\text{Bi}(-g^{1/3})\big]\Gamma(1/3)\over(3g)^{1/3}\big[\sqrt{3}\text{Ai}(-g^{1/3})+\text{Bi}(-g^{1/3})\big]\Gamma(2/3)}-1\,,

4
Further solvable potential scattering problems

A:
Here, we present and discuss another solvable scattering problem, where the potential has the shape of a squared Lorentz-curve, i.e. the profile function is

f(x)={1\over(1+x^{2})^{2}}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)={\arctan x\over 2x}\,.

(53)

The pertinent second-order differential equation for the radial wave-function

u^{\prime\prime}(x)+{g\over(1+x^{2})^{2}}u(x)=0\,,

(54)

possesses the analytical solution
u(x)=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\sin(\sqrt{1+g}\arctan x)
, subject to the boundary condition
u(0)=0
. From its asymptotic behavior
u(x)\simeq const(x+A_{0})
for
x\to\infty
, one can deduce with little effort the following exact result for the dimensionless
s
-wave scattering length:

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)=-\sqrt{1+g}\cot\Big({\pi\over 2}\sqrt{1+g}\Big)\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g>-1\,,

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)=-\sqrt{-1-g}\coth\Big({\pi\over 2}\sqrt{-1-g}\Big)\,,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,g<-1\,,

(55)

with the intermediate value
A_{0}(-1)=-2/\pi=-0.63661977
. The behavior of
A_{0}(g)
written in eq.(55) is shown in Fig. 9 over the wide parameter range
-100<g<100
. The poles of
A_{0}(g)
, which demarcate the thresholds for
s
-wave bound-states in a potential of squared Lorentz shape, lie at coupling strengths
g_{j}=4j^{2}-1
. This sequence of integer values reads up to
j=6
:

g_{1}=3\,,\qquad g_{2}=15\,,\qquad g_{3}=35\,,\qquad g_{4}=63\,,\qquad g_{5}=99\,,\qquad g_{6}=143\,.

(56)

A_{0}
as a function of
g
for potential of squared Lorentz shape.

written in eq.(55) gives the following Born series up to sixth order

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

(57)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle 0.7853982\,g+0.1963495\,g^{2}+{g^{3}\over 15.793733}+{g^{4}\over 47.649735}+{g^{5}\over 143.10164}+{g^{6}\over 429.39433}+\dots

It is a good check to reproduce the occurring expansion coefficients

B:
Reconsider the
1/r^{2}
-potential, but now with a discontinuity at the boundary, by taking the profile function

f(x)={\theta(1-x)\over x^{2}}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)=1-\ln x\,.

(58)

The regular solution of

, and this leads via

The corresponding Born series reads up to tenth order

A_{0}(g)=g+2\,g^{2}+5\,g^{3}+14\,g^{4}+42\,g^{5}+132\,g^{6}+429\,g^{7}+1430\,g^{8}+4862\,g^{9}+16796\,g^{10}+\dots\

(60)

.

C:
In order that a truncated potential with a power-like drop-off
\sim r^{-\alpha}
can support bound-states, the exponent
\alpha
must be smaller than
2
. For this reason we consider the
n
-dependent profile function

f(x)={\theta(1-x)\over x^{2-1/n}}\,,\qquad\Phi(x)=n-{n^{2}\over n+1}\,x^{1/n}\,.

(61)

A_{0}
as a function of
g
for profiles
f(x)=x^{-1}
(dark) and
f(x)=x^{-3/2}
(light).

The regular solution of
u^{\prime\prime}(x)+g\,x^{1/n-2}\,u(x)=0
is given by a Bessel-function
u(x)=\sqrt{x}J_{n}\big(2n\sqrt{g\,x^{1/n}}\big)
, and this leads via
A_{0}=u(1)/u^{\prime}(1)-1
to the following exact result for the
s
-wave scattering length

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)={J_{n}(2n\sqrt{g})\over\sqrt{g}\,J_{n-1}(2n\sqrt{g})}-1\,,\qquad\text{for}\qquad g>0\,,

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)={I_{n}(2n\sqrt{-g})\over\sqrt{-g}\,I_{n-1}(2n\sqrt{-g})}-1\,,\qquad\text{for}\qquad g<0\,,

(62)

where
I_{n}(z)
are modified Bessel-functions obtained from
J_{n}(z)
by extension to imaginary argument. The curves in Figs. 11 and 12 display these results for
n=1,2
and
n=3,4
, respectively.

A_{0}
as a function of
g
for profiles
f(x)=x^{-5/3}
(dark) and
f(x)=x^{-7/4}
(light).

The Taylor expansion around
g=0
gives the following Born series up to sixth order

\displaystyle A_{0}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\,{n\over n\!+\!1}+g^{2}{2n^{2}\over(n\!+\!1)(n\!+\!2)}+g^{3}{n^{3}(5n\!+\!6)\over(n\!+\!1)^{2}(n\!+\!2)(n\!+\!3)}+g^{4}{2n^{4}(7n\!+\!12)\over(n\!+\!1)^{2}(n\!+\!2)(n\!+\!3)(n\!+\!4)}

\displaystyle+g^{5}{2n^{5}(21n^{3}\!+\!118n^{2}\!+\!214n\!+\!120)\over(n\!+\!1)^{3}(n\!+\!2)^{2}(n\!+\!3)(n\!+\!4)(n\!+\!5)}+g^{6}{4n^{6}(33n^{3}\!+\!230n^{2}\!+\!552n\!+\!360)\over(n\!+\!1)^{3}(n\!+\!2)^{2}(n\!+\!3)(n\!+\!4)(n\!+\!5)(n\!+\!6)}+\dots

The poles of
A_{0}(g)
, which demarcate the thresholds for successive
s
-wave bound-states in a truncated potential with
r^{1/n-2}

g_{j}^{(n)}=\big(z_{j}^{(n-1)}/2n\big)^{2}
, where

denotes the
j
-th non-trivial zero of the Bessel-function
J_{n-1}(z)
. Interestingly, for
n=1
these are the same values as found for the exponentially decreasing potential
f(x)=e^{-x}
in sect. 2, eq.(17).

This concludes our presentation of results for the
s
-wave scattering length
A_{0}(g)
for selected classes of short-range central potentials
V(r)\sim-g\,f(x)
. Of course, one could continue these analytical and numerical calculations for countless other profiles functions
f(x)
. A closed-form expression for
A_{0}(g)
can be expected to exist only in rare cases.

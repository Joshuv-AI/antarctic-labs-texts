Bounds for the number of basic feasible solutions generated by the simplex method with the largest distance rule
Tomonari Kitahara

Abstract
In this paper, we show bounds for the number of different basic solutions generated by the simplex method with the largest distance rule. The pivoting rule was recently proposed, and in some cases, it was reported to be more efficient than the renowned steepest edge rule. If the problem is nondegenerate, these results provide bounds on the number of iterations. As far as we know, they are the first theoretical bounds for the largest distance rule.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background

The simplex method is one of the most efficient algorithms for solving linear programming problems (LPs).
Its efficiency is greatly affected by a pivoting rule.
There are some important pivoting rules from a practical and theoretical viewpoint.
Dantzig’s rule (or the most negative coefficient rule)
[
3
]
is the oldest and the best-known pivoting rule.
When the problem in consideration is degenerate, the simplex method might encounter a phenomenon known as cycling, in which the algorithm visits the same set of bases infinitely.
Bland’s rule
[
1
]
is the first countermeasure for cycling.
Similarly, the shadow pivoting rule is also important from a theoretical standpoint.
Borgwardt
[
2
]
showed that for random LPs, the expected value of the number of iterations is strongly polynomial in
the number of facets of the feasible region and the number of variables.
Furthermore, Spielman and Teng
[
12
]
obtained, by their smoothed analysis, the best upper bound for the expected value of
the number of iteration of the shadow simplex method.
From a practical view point, it has been widely observed that the steepest edge rule shows superior performance to other pivoting rules
[
9
]
.
Recently, a new pivoting rule, called the largest distance rule was proposed by Pan
[
10
]
.
Geometrically speaking, this pivoting rule chooses, as an entering variable, a nonbasic variable whose corresponding dual constraint has the largest unit violation.
Through his computational experiments, Pan
[
11
]
showed that a variant of the largest distance rule shows better performance than the steepest edge rule.

To assess deterministic complexity of the simplex method, Kitahara and Mizuno
[
7
]
analyzed
the number of basic feasible solutions (BFSs) generated by the simplex method.
They showed upper bounds for the number of different BFSs generated by the simplex method with Dantzig’s rule, namely

\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\log\left(\frac{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil\quad\mathrm{and}~(n-m)\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil,

where
m
is the number of constraints,
n
is the number of variables,
c
is the coefficient vector of the objective function,
x^{0}
is an initial BFS,
\bar{x}
is a BFS with the second smallest objective value,
z^{*}
is the optimal value, and
\gamma
and
\delta
are the maximum and minimum positive elements, respectively, in all BFSs

.

1.2
Main contributions

First deterministic bounds for the largest distance rule

As the largest distance rule is relatively new, little is known about its theoretical aspects.
In this study, we show that when the simplex method with this rule is applied to an LP which has an optimal solution, the number of different BFSs generated is at most

\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil~\mathrm{and}~(n-m)\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil,

where
\beta
is the minimum norm of the column vectors of the constraint matrix divided by the maximum norm of them.
If the problem is nondegenerate, these become bounds for the number of iterations. As far as we know, these are the first theoretical results for an efficient, relatively new pivoting rule.

No need for nondegeneracy assumption

As explained above, Tano et al.
[
13
]
showed iteration bounds for the steepest edge rule, which has been widely known as a practically efficient pivoting rule.
It is important to note that their analysis relies on the nondegeneracy assumption.
This assumption is essential for their analysis, and so far we have not been able to modify their analysis to obtain a bound for different BFSs generated without nondegeracy assumption.
On the contrary, we do not need nondegeneracy assumption to obtain the bounds for the number of different BFSs.
If the problem is nondegenerate, our bounds become ones for the number of iterations.

2
Preliminaries and existing research

2.1
Linear programming and the simplex method

Let
m<n
be positive integers. We consider an LP with
n
nonnegative variables and
m
constraints:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&c^{\top}x,\\
\text{subject to}&Ax=b,\\
&x\geq 0,\end{array}

(1)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given data and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a variable vector.
In this paper, we assume
\mathrm{rank}(A)=m
.
We will summarize assumptions of this paper in Section
2.4
.
The dual of problem (
1
) is expressed as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&b^{\top}y,\\
\text{subject to}&A^{\top}y+s=c,\\
&s\geq 0,\end{array}

(2)

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are variable vectors.

Next, we define a dictionary.
Let
B\subset\{1,2\dots,n\}
and
N=\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus B
.
According to
B
and
N
, we split
A
,
c
, and
x
as follows:

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{B}&A_{N}\end{bmatrix},~c=\begin{bmatrix}c_{B}\\
c_{N}\end{bmatrix},~x=\begin{bmatrix}x_{B}\\
x_{N}\end{bmatrix}.

By using these splits, problem (
1
) is written as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&c_{B}^{\top}x_{B}+c_{N}^{\top}x_{N},\\
\text{subject to}&A_{B}x_{B}+A_{N}x_{N}=b,\\
&x=(x_{B},x_{N})\geq 0.\end{array}

(3)

When
|B|=m
and
A_{B}
is nonsingular,
B
and
N
are called a basis and a nonbasis, respectively.
In this case, problem (
3
) is transformed by multiplying the equality constraint by
A_{B}^{-1}
from the left
and rearranging appropriately:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min&c_{B}^{\top}A_{B}^{-1}b+\bar{c}_{N}^{\top}x_{N},\\
\text{subject~to}&x_{B}=A_{B}^{-1}b-\bar{A}_{N}x_{N},\\
&x=(x_{B},x_{N})\geq 0,\end{array}

(4)

where
\bar{c}_{N}=c_{N}-A_{N}^{\top}(A_{B}^{\top})^{-1}c_{B}
and
\bar{A}_{N}=A_{B}^{-1}A_{N}
.
Formulation (
4
) is called a dictionary for a basis
B
.
The vector
\bar{c}_{N}
is called a reduced cost vector and
\bar{A}_{N}
is called a nonbasic matrix.
A basic solution is a solution
x
such that
(x_{B},x_{N})=(A_{B}^{-1}b,0)
in dictionary (
4
).
The elements of
x_{B}
are basic variables and those of
x_{N}
are nonbasic variables.
A basic solution
x
is called a basic feasible solution (BFS) if
x_{B}\geq 0
.
A basis
B
and a nonbasis
N
are called a feasible basis and a feasible nonbasis, respectively, if the corresponding basic solution is feasible.

For a given BFS in dictionary (
4
), if
\bar{c}_{N}\geq 0
, then the current BFS is optimal.
Let us assume otherwise.
In this case, we first choose a nonbasic variable
x_{j},~j\in N,
whose reduced cost is negative.
By increasing the value of
x_{j}
from 0, we can reduce the objective function.
If we can increase the value of
x_{j}
infinitely without violating the constraints, then we declare that the problem (
1
) is unbounded and stop the algorithm.
Otherwise, by increasing the value of
x_{j}
, the value of some basic variable
x_{i}
becomes zero at some point.
Then we exchange the roles of
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
to obtain a new dictionary.
The overall procedure is called a pivot.
In this example,
x_{j}
is called an entering variable and
x_{i}
is called a leaving variable.

2.2
Pivoting rules

A rule for choosing an entering variable from nonbasic variables with negative reduced costs is called a pivoting rule.
Many pivoting rules have been proposed and they greatly affect the efficiency of the simplex method.
In this subsection, we describe Dantzig’s rule (or the most negative coefficient rule), the steepest-edge rule and the largest distance rule.

Dantzig’s rule
[
3
]
: Dantzig’s rule chooses a nonbasic variable with the smallest reduced cost as an entering variable.
For given feasible dictionary (
4
), this rule chooses an entering variable
x_{j_{d}}
such that

j_{d}\in\argmin\{\bar{c}_{j}|~j\in N\}

Steepest edge rule
[
5
,
4
]
: The steepest-edge rule focuses on the difference vector of basic solutions and the decrease in the objective value.
Formally, it chooses a nonbasic variable
x_{j_{s}}
which satisfies

j_{s}\in\argmin\{\frac{\bar{c}_{j}}{\sqrt{1+\|\bar{a}_{j}\|^{2}}}|~j\in N\},

where
\bar{a}_{j}
represents
j
-th column vector of the nonbasic matrix
\bar{A}_{N}
.
It is easy to see the fraction in the bracket is the reduction of the objective function divided by the norm of the displacement vector, when we choose
x_{j}
as an entering variable.

largest distance rule
[
10
]
: Under the largest distance rule, we choose a nonbasic variable
x_{j_{m}}
satisfying

j_{m}\in\argmin\{\frac{\bar{c}_{j}}{\|a_{j}\|}|~j\in N\},

(5)

where
a_{j}
represents the
j
-th column vector of the constraint matrix
A
.
Geometrically speaking, this rule commands to choose a nonbasic variable whose corresponding dual constraint has the largest unit violation.
Note that the steepest edge rule and the largest distance rule take a similar form.
While
\bar{a}_{j}
in the steepest rule varies iteration to iteration and computation of
\bar{a}_{j}
is costly,
a_{j}
in the largest distance rule is constant.
Pan
[
11
]
reported a variant of the largest distance rule shows superior performance over the steepest edge rule in his computational experiments.

2.3
Parameters

In this subsection, we explain parameters needed for our analysis.

First, let
\delta
and
\gamma
be the minimum and maximum positive elements, respectively, in all BFSs.
From the definition, for a BFS
(x_{B},x_{N})
and
j\in B
such that
x_{j}\not=0
, we have

\delta\leq x_{j}\leq\gamma.

We also need another parameter
\beta
, which is defined as

\beta=\frac{\min_{j\in\{1,2,\dots_{n}\}}\|a_{j}\|}{\max_{j\in\{1,2,\dots_{n}\}}\|a_{j}\|},

(6)

where
a_{j}
denote the
j
th column vector of
A
.
Note that
\beta
can be directly computed from
A
.
Note also that
\beta
can be bounded from below as

\beta\geq\frac{\min_{i,j\in\{1,2,\dots_{n}\}}|a_{ij}|}{\sqrt{n}\max_{i,j\in\{1,2,\dots_{n}\}}|a_{ij}|},

where
a_{ij}
means the
(i,j)
element of
A
.

2.5
Previous research

To consider the complexity of the simplex method, some recent studies analyzed the number of different BFSs generated by the simplex method.
Kitahara and Mizuno
[
7
]
proved that an upper bound for the number of different BFSs generated by the simplex method with Dantzig’s rule is

\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\log\left(\frac{c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil

(7)

for a standard LP with
n
variables and
m
constraints (see Section
2.3
for the notation used in (
7
) and (
8
).)
Another upper bound that is independent of the objective value was also shown in
[
7
]
:

(n-m)\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.

(8)

For a nondegenerate LP, upper bounds (
7
) and (
8
) can be regarded as upper bounds for the number of iterations of the simplex method with Dantzig’s rule.
The same authors
[
6
]
observed that for a variant of Klee-Minty problem, the bound (
8
) is close to the actual number of iterations.

Kitahara and Mizuno
[
8
]
also studied pivoting rules that do not increase the objective value in each iteration.
Using such a pivoting rule, the simplex method generates at most

\left\lceil\min\left\{m,n-m\right\}\frac{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}{\delta\delta^{\prime}}\right\rceil

(9)

different BFSs, where
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime}
are the maximum and minimum absolute values, respectively, of negative reduced costs in all BFSs.
Assuming nondegenerate LPs, upper bound (
9
) for the number of different BFSs can be regarded as an upper bound for the number of simplex iterations.

Inspired by these results, Tano et al.
[
13
]
showed bounds for the number of iterations for the simplex method with the steepest edge rule, with an additional assumption that the problem is nondegenerate.
They obtained the bounds

\left\lceil m^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil~\mathrm{and}~(n-m)\left\lceil m^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.

3
Analysis of the largest distance rule

3.1
Technical results

We begin this section by a lemma, which gives a lower bound for the optimal value of an LP.

Lemma 3.1

(Kitahara and Mizuno
[
7
]
)
.

Let
x^{t}
be the
t
-th solution generated by the simplex method with any pivoting rule, and let
B^{t}
and
N^{t}
be the basis and nonbasis, respectively, corresponding to
x^{t}
.
Set
\Delta_{d,t}=-\min\{\bar{c}_{j}|~j\in N^{t}\}
.
Then, we have

z^{*}\geq c^{\top}x^{t}-m\gamma\Delta_{d,t}

(10)

Assume that now we are at
t
-th iteration of the simplex method with basis
B^{t}
and nonbasis
N^{t}
.
Let
x_{j_{d,t}}
and
x_{j_{l,t}}
be nonbasic variables chosen by Dantzig’s rule and the largest distance rule, respectively.
We also set
\Delta_{l,t}=\bar{c}_{j_{l,t}}
.
Then from (
5
), we have

\frac{\bar{c}_{j_{l,t}}}{\|a_{j_{l,t}\|}}\leq\frac{\bar{c}_{j_{d,t}}}{\|a_{j_{d,t}\|}}

from rearranging this inequality and the definition of
\beta
(
6
), we obtain

\Delta_{l,t}=\bar{c}_{j_{l,t}}\leq\frac{\|a_{j_{l,t}}\|}{\|a_{j_{d,t}}\|}\bar{c}_{j_{d,t}}\leq\beta\Delta_{d,t}.

(11)

Next, we show that in each iteration of the simplex method with the largest distance rule, the difference between the objective value and the optimal value decreases at a constant ratio or more.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x^{t}
and
x^{t+1}
be the
t
-th and
(t+1)
-th solutions, respectively, of the simplex method with largest distance rule.
If
x^{t}\not=x^{t+1}
, we have the following inequality:

c^{\top}x^{t+1}-z^{*}\leq\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)\left(c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}\right).

(12)

Proof.

The objective value decreases by
\Delta_{l,t}x_{j_{l}}^{t+1}
at
t
th iteration.
Note that since
x^{t}\not=x^{t+1}
,
x_{j_{l}}^{t+1}\not=0
and from the definition of
\delta
, we have
x_{j_{l}}^{t+1}\geq\delta
.
Therefore, we obtain

c^{\top}x^{t}-c^{\top}x^{t+1}=\Delta_{l,t}x_{j_{l}}^{t+1}\geq\Delta_{l,t}\delta.

From inequalities (
10
) and (
11
), we have

\Delta_{l,t}\delta\geq\beta\Delta_{d,t}\delta\geq\beta\delta\cdot\frac{c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}}{m\gamma},

and hence we obtain

c^{\top}x^{t}-c^{\top}x^{t+1}\geq\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\left(c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}\right),

which leads to inequality (
12
).
∎

3.2
A bound using the objective function

For a positive integer
t
, let

\tilde{t}=|\{t^{\prime}\in\{0,1\dots,t-1\}|~x^{t^{\prime}+1}\not=x^{t^{\prime}}\}|

.
That is,
\tilde{t}
shows how many times the solution changed from
0
-th iteration to
(t-1)
-th iteration.
Then from Lemma
3.2
, we have

c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}\leq\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)^{\tilde{t}}\left(c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}\right).

(13)

Let
\bar{x}
be a second optimal BFS, that is, a BFS whose objective value is the second smallest of all BFSs.
Obviously, if the
t
-th solution
x^{t}
satisfies the following inequality,
x^{t}
is optimal:

c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}<c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}.

Therefore, the simplex method with the largest distance rule finds an optimal solution and terminates
after generating at most
T
different BFSs starting from an initial BFS
x^{0}
, where
T
is the smallest integer
\tilde{t}
such that the right-hand side of inequality (
13
) is less than
c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}
.
Using this observation and Lemma
3.2
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\bar{x}
be a BFS of problem (
1
) with the second smallest objective value.
For problem (
1
), the simplex method with the largest distance rule generates at most

\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil

(14)

different BFSs.

Proof.

As mentioned earlier, the smallest integer
\tilde{t}
satisfying the following inequality is an upper bound for the number of different BFSs:

\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)^{\tilde{t}}\left(c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}\right)<c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}.

Solving this inequality for
t
, we have

\tilde{t}>\displaystyle\left.-\log\left(\frac{c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right/\log\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right).

(15)

Since
1/x>-1/\log(1-x)
holds in
0<x<1
, we obtain

\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}>-\frac{1}{\displaystyle\log\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)},

which leads to the theorem.
∎

3.3
A bound independent of the objective function

The following lemma is needed to obtain a bound independent of the objective function, for the number of BFSs generated by the simplex method with the largest distance rule.

Lemma 3.3

(Kitahara and Mizuno
[
7
]
)
.

Let
x^{t}
be the
t
-th solution of the simplex method and
B^{t}
be the basis corresponding to
x^{t}
.
If
x^{t}
is not optimal, there exists
\bar{\jmath}\in B^{t}
that satisfies the following conditions:

x_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{\bar{\jmath}}^{*}\geq\frac{1}{mx_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t}}\left(c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}\right),

(16)

where
z^{*}
is the slack vector corresponding to an optimal basic solution of the dual problem (
2
).
Furthermore, the
k
-th solution
x^{k}
satisfies

x_{\bar{\jmath}}^{k}\leq mx_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t}\,\frac{c^{\top}x^{k}-z^{*}}{c^{\top}x^{t}-z^{*}}

(17)

for an arbitrary positive integer
k
.

Now we are ready to prove a bound independent of the objective function, for the number of BFSs generated by the simplex method with largest distance rule.

Theorem 3.2
.

When applying the simplex method with the largest distance rule to problem (
1
), it generates at most

(n-m)\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.

(18)

different BFSs.

Proof.

Let
r
be a positive integer.
Moreover, let
x^{t}
and
x^{t+r}
be the
t
- and
(t+r)
-th solutions, respectively, of the simplex method.
Assume that excluding
x^{t}
,
\tilde{r}
different BFSs are generated between
t
-th and (
t+r
)-th iteration.
In addition, let
B^{t}
be the basis corresponding to
x^{t}
.
Then, by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
and the definition of
\gamma
, there exists
\bar{\jmath}\in B^{t}
such that

x_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t+r}\leq mx_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)^{\tilde{r}}\leq m\gamma\left(1-\frac{\beta\delta}{m\gamma}\right)^{\tilde{r}}.

When
\tilde{r}\geq(m\gamma)/(\beta\delta)\cdot\log\left(m\gamma/\delta\right)
, the rightmost term above is less than
\delta
, and
x_{\bar{\jmath}}^{t+r}
must be fixed to zero by the definition of
\delta
.

If an optimal solution is not obtained after
r
iterations satisfying the above inequality, we can apply the same procedure again.
Let us fix a basic optimal solution
(y^{*},s^{*})
of the dual problem (
2
) and let
B^{*}
and
N^{*}
be basis and nonbasis for the solution.
Since the event described in Lemma
3.3
can occur for
\bar{j}\in N^{*}
at most once and
|N^{*}|=n-m
,
the value fixing process for primal variables occurs at most
n-m
times.
Thus we have the desired result.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

When the problem is nondegenerate, the simplex method with the largest distance rule finds an optimal solution in at most

\min\left\{\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}x^{0}-z^{*}}{\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}c^{\top}\bar{x}-z^{*}}\right)\right\rceil,~(n-m)\left\lceil\frac{m\gamma}{\beta\delta}\log\left(\frac{m\gamma}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.\right\}

iterations.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we analyzed the simplex method with the largest distance rule and obtained two bounds for the number of different BFSs.
This pivoting rule is relatively new and little research has been conducted so far.
We hope the current study sheds light on this new pivoting rule and encourages more research.

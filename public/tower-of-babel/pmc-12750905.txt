Orthogeriatric multidisciplinary care for hip fractures in emergency department reduces length of stay: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Michael von Allmen, Ouanes Amine Ben Saad, Joseph M Schwab, Flora Gobet, Corinne Grandjean, Darius Marti, Elizeth Tavares Alves, Thomas Schmutz, Vincent Ribordy, Youcef Guechi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHip fractures represent a frequent reason for emergency department (ED) attendance among older adults and are associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Orthogeriatric multidisciplinary care models, by allowing for early clinical assessment, rapid diagnostics and tailored analgesia by regional anaesthesia (RA) optimize perioperative management and improve care for these vulnerable patients. The specific impact of such models on ED length of stay (LOS) remains insufficiently studied. This study assessed the effect on ED LOS of implementing an orthogeriatric fast-track based on a multidisciplinary care model. Secondary objectives were to evaluate the impact of this implementation on analgesia efficiency in ED, complications rate and early mortality.MethodThis monocentric observational retrospective cohort took place in a Swiss ED and included patients aged ≥ 65 years with hip fractures, before and after fast-track implementation. Exclusion criteria were contraindication to or patient refusal of RA, inability to consent and polytrauma. The primary outcome was ED LOS. Secondary outcomes were analgesia efficiency in the ED (i.e., NRS reduction, cumulative opioid consumption), 72-hours complications rate and 30-day mortality. Wilcoxon rank sum test was used for quantitative variables and Fisher’s test for qualitative variables.ResultsA total of 152 patients were included, with 87 in the pre-implementation group and 65 in the post-implementation group. The post-intervention group had significantly shorter ED LOS (180 ± 88 min vs. 327 ± 122 min, p < 0.001) and experiences greater NRS reduction (1.6 +/- 4.1 vs. 0.1 +/- 3.8, p = 0.043), while opioid consumption did not differ between groups (p = 0.46). Rate of fatal complications (0% vs. 6.9%, p = 0.039) and mortality (0% vs. 8%, p = 0.02) were lower in the intervention group.ConclusionAn orthogeriatric fast-track pathway significantly reduces ED LOS and is associated with lower early mortality. Background

Geriatric patients frequently present to the emergency department (ED), accounting for 37.8% of visits to a Swiss regional hospital [1]. Due to demographic shifts, ED utilization by this population is expected to increase in the next decades [2]. Compared to younger patients, the older people present more frequently to the ED, have longer length of stay and require more healthcare resources [3]. Major osteoporotic fractures (i.e., hip, radius and vertebral fractures) in the geriatric population account for a substantial and growing proportion of inpatient admissions in Switzerland [4]. Their absolute number is expected to nearly double by 2050, putting pressure on emergency departments and inpatient care structures [5]. In Switzerland, approximately 14,000 hip fractures occur annually, with an estimated cost of CHF 2.05 billion in 2010 and a projected increase of + 29% by 2025 [6]. These fractures therefore represent a substantial economic burden and a contributor to emergency department overcrowding. From the patient’s perspective, hip fractures are associated with an excess one year mortality rate of up to 19% [7].

Providing care to geriatric patients poses numerous challenges. The concept of orthogeriatric care addresses the complexity of treating acute injuries in patients present with derangements due to age and comorbidities [3] Various care models and institutional frameworks have been developed to improve outcomes in this population [3, 8–10]. These models typically involve a multidisciplinary collaboration (e.g., orthopaedic surgeon, geriatrician, anaesthetist, emergency medicine (EM) physician) to assess and plan management in a “fast-track pathway” upon arrival in the ED [3, 8–10]. Previous studies have showed such pathways to shorten hospital length of stay and time to surgery, as well as lower mortality [9]. Pain management is a cornerstone of orthogeriatric care. Older patients are more prone to experience adverse effects from standard analgesics, especially opioids, which have been linked to an increase in morbidity and mortality [3, 8, 9]. Delirium is a frequent and serious postoperative complication in geriatric patients with hip fractures, often associated with suboptimal analgesic strategies [11]. Opioid sparing strategies through regional anesthesia (RA) (e.g., femoral nerve blockade (FNB) with or without catheter implementation, three-in-one blockade, fascia iliaca blockade) have demonstrated reductions in both delirium incidence and mortality [12, 13], while improving pain control without increasing adverse events [14–25]. Effective analgesia is essential, as undertreated pain (oligoanalgesia) is also linked to poorer outcomes in this population [3]. Regional anaesthesia is recommended as the first line analgesic strategy for geriatric hip fractures by international guidelines and should be initiated during in the patient’s ED stay [26, 27].

From an ED organizational perspective, prolonged length of stay (LOS) contributes to ED crowding which strains limited resources and compromises quality of care. For the individual geriatric patient, a prolonged ED LOS increases risk of delirium and leads to higher mortality [28, 29]. When attending the ED for orthopaedic injuries, geriatric patients have longer stays compared to younger counterparts. This is due to longer time to diagnosis, TTS and inadequate analgesia [3, 9]. Multidisciplinary orthogeriatric care might address these factors, however to our knowledge, no study has investigated the impact of multidisciplinary orthogeriatric care on ED LOS in geriatric hip fractures. The impact of regional anaesthesia on emergency department length of stay remains unclear. The only randomized trial on RA for hip fractures in the ED found no reduction in LOS compared with standard analgesia [14]. whereas studies in other traumatic orthopaedic settings reported shorter treatment times [15]. Overall, RA alone does not appear to shorten ED LOS unless integrated within a structured multidisciplinary pathway.

In December 2024, an orthogeriatric fast-track for hip fractures (OFT) based on a multidisciplinary care model (i.e., EM physicians, anaesthetists, orthopaedists, radiologists and geriatricians) was implemented at the ED of Fribourg State Hospital (HFR, Switzerland). This involves early clinical assessment, rapid radiologic diagnostics, tailored analgesia by regional anaesthesia (RA) and dedicated perioperative management.

This retrospective observational study aims to evaluate whether the implementation of an orthogeriatric multidisciplinary fast-track reduces ED LOS in geriatric patients suffering from hip fractures. Secondary objectives include impact on analgesia effectiveness, perioperative complications at 72 h and 30-day mortality.

Method

Study design and setting

This was a single-center, retrospective and observational cohort study. The study was conducted in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines [30]. The setting was the ED of the Fribourg State Hospital in Switzerland, which has an annual census of 42’000 patients. Two equivalent seasonal periods were analysed: December 2023 to April 2024 (pre-OFT) and December 2024 to April 2025 (post-OFT), corresponding to the timeframes before and after the implementation of the OFT pathway. Before the introduction of the OFT, no standardized pathway existed for the management of hip fractures for older patient. Each patient was handled individually by the emergency and orthopedic teams, without prehospital alert or systematic coordination. Patients were initially assessed by an emergency resident, who discussed the case with a supervising physician before involving the orthopedic team. Timing of diagnostic imaging and analgesic strategy largely depended on availability of resources. The anesthesia team was involved only in the context of surgical planning and were not involved in ED analgesic strategies. The OFT operates as follows: If a hip fracture is clinically suspected (i.e. external rotation and/or shortening of the affected limb) in the prehospital setting by emergency medical services, an alert is sent to the ED. Upon patient arrival at the ED, a senior ED physician immediately confirms clinical suspicion and orders diagnostic imaging (plain radiography). If a femoral fracture is diagnosed and in the absence of contraindications, an alert is sent to the anaesthesia and orthopaedic teams. This allows for prompt evaluation and peri-operative planning both teams. The anaesthesia team aimed to complete RA as early as possible but were free to schedule the procedures according to their availability. Although multiple RA techniques exist for hip fractures, the protocol was standardized to ultrasound-guided femoral nerve block (FNB), as it is the most extensively studied and evidence-based approach [24]. The accuracy of catheter placement for continuous regional anaesthesia was left at the discretion of the anaesthesiologist. A standard operating procedure (SOP) is provided in Appendix 1. Medical issues were addressed by the EM team and followed up by geriatricians. OFT algorithm is shown in Fig. 1.

Study procedure

The study involved retrospective data collection and analysis of geriatric patients with hip fractures (i.e., femoral neck, pertrochenteric, subtrochanteric and periprosthetic fracture) [31], either managed through the OFT pathway or standard care. The intervention population were patients included in OFT from December 2024 to April 2025. To control for seasonal variations in ED attendance, the control group were patients meeting the same inclusion criteria during the corresponding period of the previous year (December 2023 to April 2024). Inclusion criteria were age ≥ 65 years, presence of a hip fracture, and isolated (mono-trauma) injury. Exclusion criteria were RA contraindications (i.e., neurovascular deficit, localized skin infections in hip area), RA refusal from patient or an inability to give consent and polytrauma.

Sample size

All eligible patients during the study period were included; no formal a priori power calculation was performed. The difference in emergency department length of stay (LOS) between groups was 147 min, with a 95% CI ≈ 113–181 min, p < 0.001. Based on the observed sample sizes (n = 87 vs. n = 65) and standard deviations, the study had a statistical power > 90% to detect a difference of approximately 60 min in LOS; the observed effect (~ 147 min) was therefore well above this threshold.

Measures and outcomes

Demographic variables were collected (i.e., age, sex, BMI). Comorbidities were classified according to Internation Classification of Disease, 11th Edition (ICD-11th ) [32] and scored through the Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS) [33]. The CFS is a validated tool for assessing frailty in this clinical setting. Since its original validation, it has been endorsed by major geriatric societies and consistently shown to predict adverse outcomes. It is therefore considered the gold standard for bedside frailty assessment in acute and emergency care [34].

The primary outcome was ED length of stay (LOS), defined as the time elapsed between ED admission and transfer out of the ED. Secondary outcomes included analgesic effectiveness, assessed through reduction in numeric rating scale (NRS) (i.e., difference between admission and pre-transfer scores), and cumulative intravenous opioid consumption (in morphine equivalent). Early complications within 72 h after admission were recorded and classified according to their severity: mild (no impact on mortality rate), moderate (potential impact on mortality rate), severe (life-threating complications) and fatal. This timeframe was selected because it allows the capture of acute events directly related to initial management and surgery in hip fractures, such as delirium, anesthetic complications, or cardio-respiratory decompensations [35]. Delirium and complication related to opioid analgesia (i.e., opioid intoxication) were specified. Time to call (TTC, i.e., time from admission to alerting the anaesthesia/orthopaedic teams), time to imaging (TTI), time to regional anesthesia (TTR, i.e., time from ED admission to successful completion of regional anesthesia) and time to surgery (TTS) were recorded. First-attempt success rate of RA was assessed. Thirty-day mortality was also recorded. Patients with missing data were excluded for the corresponding variable.

Data collection and availability

Data were extracted retrospectively from electronic medical records based on inclusion criteria (Medfolio ®, Synedra®, PDMS®). All data were stored in a secure and access-restricted environment.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using R/RStudio (R/4.4.2, RStudio/2025.09.0 + 387; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Normal distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and by visual inspection of histograms and Q-Q plots (Appendix 2). None of the continuous variables were normally distributed. Therefore, data are presented as median (interquartile range or rang), and non-parametric tests were used for group comparisons (Wilcoxon rank-sum, Fisher’s exact, or Chi-square tests as appropriate).

A two-step multivariable regression approach using a generalized linear model (GLM) with a Gamma distribution and a log link has been used to adjust for confounding factors, as the LOS variable was continuous, positive and right-skewed. Variables were selected using a stepwise approach based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), allowing us to retain the most relevant predictors while avoiding model overfitting. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

A total of 152 patients were included, with 65 in the intervention group and 87 in the control group. This number far exceeded the minimum sample size required to ensure adequate statistical power. Missing values were negligible across all variables (< 5%), except for the reduction in NRS pain scores, which had a missingness rate of 16%.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were comparable between groups (Table 1). The mean age was 83 ± 8 years, and 67% of patients were female. In the intervention group, mean TTC and TTR were respectively on average 41 +/- 66 min and 235 +/- 135 min, respectively.

Fatal complications were 0% in the intervention group vs. 6.9% in the control group (p = 0.039). All candidate variables were initially included (i.e., demographic data, type of management, Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS), BMI, all comorbidity categories). In this full Gamma regression model with log link, management through the OFT pathway remained significantly associated with a shorter ED length of stay (exp(β) 0.52; 95% CI 0.45–0.60; p < 0.001). Female sex was associated with a longer length of stay (exp(β) 1.25; 95% CI 1.05–1.48; p = 0.012), while cardiovascular comorbidities showed a weak, non-significant association (exp(β) 1.20; 95% CI 0.97–1.48; p = 0.10). A Gamma model with stepwise selection based on AIC was subsequently applied to obtain the most parsimonious set of predictors. Three variables were retained: type of management, sex, and cardiovascular comorbidities. In this final model, OFT management remained strongly associated with a shorter length of stay (exp(β) 0.54, 95% CI 0.47–0.61, p < 0.001). Female sex (exp(β) 1.23, 95% CI 1.06–1.41, p = 0.006) and cardiovascular comorbidities (exp(β) 1.25, 95% CI 1.07–1.46, p = 0.006) were both associated with a longer length of stay. No other variables met criteria for inclusion in the final model. (Appendix 3, Table 2)

Discussion

This retrospective study demonstrated a significant shortening of ED LOS, improved analgesia and short-term survival after implementation of an orthogeriatric fast-track for hip fractures based on a multidisciplinary care model.

This is the first study to specifically explore the impact of such a pathway ED LOS. The marked reduction in LOS observed in the intervention group can largely be explained by earlier clinical identification and faster imaging. The mean TTC was 41 min in mean, reflecting a short time to initial clinical assessment and radiological diagnosis. The remaining time in the ED (mean 139 min) was related to additional clinical issues (e.g., internal medicine and geriatric assessments, pain management), complementary imaging (e.g., CT scan), and organizational processes (e.g., bed management, approval for transfer, coordination of transfer). The contribution of each of these components to the remaining time in the ED is not reflected in this study and warrants further investigation to identify additional optimization targets. This effort is of importance, as ED LOS impacts geriatric patient’s mortality and morbidity in a disproportioned matter compared to younger patients [29]. This observation is particularly true for the geriatric population, which suffers more from its impact and is associated with a longer ED LOS [29]. The expected increase in geriatric visits to the ED, partially for traumatic issues, is an ongoing challenge [3]. The development of organizational strategies to guarantee the quality of care for the older people while ensuring optimal patient flow is of essence.

Although time to surgery was shorter in the intervention group, this was not statistically significant, contrary to previous findings [9]. This is likely due to sample size and organizational variability. Another possible explanation might that multidisciplinary care, by providing optimal pain management, makes postponing surgery to allow for optimal perioperative care possible (e.g., daytime surgery with an expert surgical team, after medical optimization). This organizational flexibility may have contributed to the observed reduction in fatal complications and early mortality.

Analgesia efficacy, as measured by NRS reduction, was significantly improved in the intervention group, even though opioid consumption was comparable. These results are consistent with previous evidence showing that early RA during hospitalization improves analgesia efficacy [14, 16–24, 26, 36]. However, many studies reported a concomitant reduction in opioid consumption. The lack of opioid sparing observed in our study can be explained by the comparatively late RA in the patient’s journey (i.e., LOS mean 180 +/- 88 min vs. TTR mean 235 +/- 135 min). In fact, only 15% of patients had a RA performed before their transfer out of ED. In our algorithm, anaesthesiologists were notified at TTC but were free to schedule RA based on their availability. To prioritize a shorter ED LOS, transfer out of the ED was not until after RA was performed. If RA was not done before leaving the ED, patients were called from the ward to the recovery room for a quick procedure. The only RCT investigating RA for hip fractures performed exclusively in the ED reported no difference in ED LOS [14]. Thus, ensuring a short ED LOS while positively impacting opioid consumption may require RA to be performed early in the ED stay. The feasibility and impact of such an approach require further investigation. Beyond the absence of the expected opioid-sparing effect, the median opioid consumption was unexpectedly higher in the intervention group. However, this difference was not statistically significant and may reflect increased clinical awareness among medical and nursing staff regarding the provision of effective analgesia in this setting.

Similarly delirium rates, which are closely associated with opioid consumption [3], were not lower in our intervention group, whereas previous literature on reported fewer delirium episodes with multidisciplinary orthogeriatric care models [11–13]. This highlights the necessity of adapting the existing algorithm to achieve both opioid-sparing and reduction of delirium. For such an adaptation to be feasible, the very early performance of RA might be privileged for patients with severe pain (i.e., NRS >6), while patients with a lower NRS could be managed with a strict non-opioid analgesia until RA could be performed. Training emergency physicians in selected ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia techniques may also be a promising strategy to improve the organizational efficiency of the OFT.

Although minor, moderate, and severe complications were similar between groups, the intervention group experienced fewer fatal complications and significantly lower early mortality. These findings suggest that benefits of the orthogeriatric fast-track extend beyond improved care coordination to tangible survival advantages. ED LOS is an independent risk factor for increased hospital morbidity and mortality [28, 29]. Although the mortality benefit of multidisciplinary models is multifactorial [9], our results highlight the crucial role of the ED in enabling such effects. In addition to an opioid-sparing strategy, reduction in LOS in ED contributes to a lower rate of delirium. The ideal LOS to limit the occurrence of delirium is not well defined, as is the minimum time below which the quality of care can no longer be guaranteed. However, the ED LOS in the intervention group was significantly lower than what has been reported to be associated with delirium [29].

This study has limitations. Its retrospective design, monocentric setting and limited sample size restrict generalizability. The study design did not allow for the control of confounding factors. However, the groups were homogeneous regarding major potential confounding factors (i.e., age, sex, BMI, comorbidities and CFS). Furthermore, after adjusting for the most relevant predicators, the OFT pathway remained independently associated with a 46% reduction in ED LOS (exp(Beta) = 0.54, 95% CI 0.47–0.62, p < 0.001), confirming that the observed effect was not explained by confounding factors. Larger prospective multicentre studies are required to validate these findings, assess cost-effectiveness and long-term outcomes. However, the results are consistent with research in other areas of orthogeriatric care [9]. Regarding analgesia, efficacy was assessed through a subjective evaluation (e.g., NRS reduction). However, this surrogate is widely accepted in analgesia research and our results are concordant with existing evidence [14, 17, 19, 23, 26, 27]. In our cohort, 16% of NRS pain scores were missing. This proportion is consistent with previous reports in emergency geriatric populations, where systematic pain assessment is often difficult due to acute clinical conditions, cognitive impairment, or communication barriers [37]. Finally, the reduction in ED LOS may reflect an awareness effect during the early phase of the OFT implementation. Future longitudinal evaluations are needed to assess the sustainability of this effect and disentangle organizational adaptation from the impact of the orthogeriatric model itself. Due these limitations, no causal inference can be drawn.

Conclusions

The implementation of an orthogeriatric multidisciplinary fast-track was associated with a shorter ED LOS, improved analgesic effectiveness, and lower early mortality in geriatric patients with hip fractures. These findings support the early initiation of multidisciplinary care models upon ED admission, although confirmation in larger multicentre studies is needed before widespread adoption.

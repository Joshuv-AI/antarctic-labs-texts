Abstract
. The derivation of effective descriptions for interacting many-body systems is an important branch of applied mathematics. We prove a propagation of chaos result for a system of N N particles subject to Newtonian time evolution with or without additional white noise influencing the velocities of the particles. We assume that the particles interact according to a regularized Coulomb-interaction with a regularization parameter that vanishes in the N → ∞ N\to\infty limit. The respective effective description is the so called Vlasov-Poisson-Fokker-Planck (VPFP), respectively the Vlasov-Poisson (VP) equation in the case of no or sub-dominant white noise. To obtain our result we combine the relative entropy method from [ 28 ] with the control on the difference between the trajectories of the true and the effective description provided in [ 27 ] for the VPFP case respectively in [ 35 ] for the VP case. This allows us to prove strong convergence of the marginals, i.e. convergence in L 1 L^{1} .



1.
Introduction

The analytic or numerical treatment of systems composed of many interacting agents is often difficult up to impossible. However, there are cases where simplified effective equations can be used to draw the main features of the system. This is in particular the case when the initial configurations of the agents are independent and/or white noise is included in the dynamics.
Assuming that propagation of chaos holds, i.e. that the independence is only mildly violated as time propagates, the law of large numbers can be used to replace the pair-interaction by its expectation value, the so-called mean-field interaction. One arrives at the effective description of the dynamics of the system.
The main ingredient for proving the validity of the effective equation is thus proving that the independence of the particles (“chaos”) in fact persists under time evolution (“propagation of chaos”). See for instance
[
54
,
24
]
.
It is therefore not surprising that verifying the propagation of chaos for interacting many particle systems has been a vivid area of mathematical research in recent decades. Many of the underlying physical or biological systems naturally consider a singular interaction between the particles. However, depending on the system at hand, singularities in the interaction are a major obstacle to be overcome when proving the validity of propagation of chaos and its consequence: the accuracy of the effective equations.

In the present paper we will review some recent findings in this direction, where - in a sense to be described below - mildly singular interactions are considered to prove closeness of the
N
-body trajectory of the interacting system to the respective mean-field trajectory in a probabilistic sense with respect to the initial distribution of the particles. By “mean-field trajectory” we denote the trajectory on
N
-body phase space where each particle independently moves according to the one-particle flow of the effective evolution equation one obtains via the mean-field consideration explained above.

The central insight of this paper is that such an estimate on the level of trajectories can be used to control the relative entropy on the level of probability measures describing the full interacting system compared to the effective descriptions: one can show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the
k^{\text{th}}
marginal
f_{t}^{N,k}
of the
N
-body density
f_{t}^{N}
will be close to the
k
-fold product of the respective solution of the effective equation
f_{t}
in the sense that relative entropy comparing the two will be small.
Such a control of the relative entropy opens the door to understanding closeness of
f_{t}^{N,k}
and
f_{t}^{\otimes k}
also with respect to other prominent notions of distance, for example in the
L^{1}
sense or with respect to the Wasserstein distance.

Although the findings of this paper do also apply to other models, we here focus on results for second order systems that are subject to white noise.
We already mentioned that the key difficulty in the derivation of mean field limits comes from the singularity of the force that one encounters for example for systems subject to Newtonian gravitation or Coulomb repulsion. To manage this difficulty we consider a second order stochastic system with a regularized interaction force
k^{N}
that will be specified below. Note already that the regularization will depend on
N
. The white noise will be modeled by a Brownian motion with an
N
-dependent strength parameter
\sigma_{N}\geq 0
. We assume that, as
N\to\infty
,
\sigma_{N}
converges and denote the limit by
\sigma
. Summarizing, we consider the dynamics of a (stochastic) Newtonian system with 2-body interaction
k^{N}
given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle dx_{t}^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}

\displaystyle=v_{t}^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}dt,

\displaystyle dv_{t}^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}k^{N}(x_{t}^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}-x_{t}^{j,N,\sigma_{N}})dt+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}}dB_{t}^{i}.

These equations define a trajectory
(X_{t},V_{t})
on
N
-body phase space and a respective flow
\Phi_{t}^{N}:\mathbb{R}^{dN}\to\mathbb{R}^{dN}
:

\Phi_{t}^{N}(X_{0},V_{0})=(X_{t},V_{t}):=(x_{t}^{1,N,\sigma_{N}},\dots,x_{t}^{N,N,\sigma_{N}},v_{t}^{1,N,\sigma_{N}},\dots,v_{t}^{N,N,\sigma_{N}}).

The initial data of (
1.1
) is given by a set of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) square integrable random variables, i.e.

(1.2)

(x^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}_{0},v^{i,N,\sigma_{N}}_{0}),\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

with
f_{0}
be their common probability density function.
In other words,
(X_{0},V_{0})
is distributed according to the density
f_{0}^{\otimes N}
. We denote the respective time dependent probability density on the
N
-particle phase space by
f_{t}^{N}:=f_{0}^{\otimes N}\circ\Phi_{-t}^{N}
.

From this one can guess the effective description of the system, as explained above by replacing the interaction by its expectation value, is the so-called mean-field interaction.
On the level of densities one arrives at the following kinetic equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}f_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{t}+(k\star\rho_{t})\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{t}=\sigma\Delta_{v}f_{t}\qquad\mbox{ where }k(x):=\pm C_{d}\frac{x}{|x|^{d}},\quad\sigma\geq 0,

and
\rho_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{t}\mathrm{d}v
.
For
\sigma>0
(
1.3
) is called the Vlasov-Poisson-Fokker-Planck (VPFP) equation, for
\sigma=0
it is called the Vlasov-Poisson (VP) equation.

Before we come to the central results of this paper let us first give a short overview of recent findings related to propagation of chaos in particular for singular interactions. Due to their wide applications in applied sciences, independent contributions from different research groups have substantially contributed to the developments of the field. Many new ideas and methods have been introduced to prove mean-field limit results in different settings and for different kinds of singular kernels.
A detailed review of the state of the art of recent developments can for example be found in
[
3
,
12
,
19
,
29
]
.

Propagation of chaos for second order systems with singular interactions has been studied already by Oelschläger in
[
47
]
for the case that
k^{N}
is an approximation of the gradient of the Dirac-Delta distribution. This work is based on an estimate of a smoothed version of the modulated energy, which then implies convergence to the Euler system.

The modulated energy method was developed for hydrodynamic limits, dating back to Dafermos
[
14
,
15
]
in the context of conservation laws and Brenier
[
2
]
in the study of quasi-neutral limits from the Vlasov-Poisson equation to the incompressible Euler system for suitable initial data. Brenier’s result
[
2
]
was later improved by Masmoudi for general initial data
[
40
]
. The modulated energy method has proven useful in various situations, for instance in the high field limit
[
20
]
, the quasi-neutral limits
[
56
,
25
]
, the quasi-neutral limit with vanishing viscosity
[
26
]
, etc. Also results in a hydrodynamic limit have to be mentioned. In a limiting regime where the velocities are relaxed the modulated energy method can be used to prove convergence of solutions of the kinetic Vlasov equation (or Vlasov-Fokker-Planck) to the Euler equation
[
31
]
. The same holds true for systems with velocity alignment
[
32
]
, in such situations even singular interactions like Coulomb can be treated
[
9
]
.
Even without noise, the modulated energy method can be used to treat singular interactions. Assuming mono-kineticity of the initial data as well as repulsiveness of the interaction Serfaty and Duerinckx
[
52
]
showed convergence of the Newtonian many-body system towards the pressureless Euler-Poisson system.
Inspired by
[
2
]
and
[
52
]
, a combined mean-field and quasi-neutral limit, from Newton’s law to incompressible Euler, has been proved in
[
21
]
.

Parallel to this, the relative entropy method was used by Jabin and Wang to derive mean-field limit of the second order system for certain types of singular interactions
[
28
,
30
]
. Later, the relative entropy method is combined with the modulated energy method to form the modulated free energy method to treat the mean field limit problem for stochastic systems with singular interactions in
[
8
]
.

In recent years, also methods which are not based on estimating the relative entropy between the microscopic and the effective description have been found to prove propagation of chaos.
Hauray and Jabin considered singular interaction forces scaling as
1/|x|^{\lambda}
in three dimensions with
{\lambda}<1

[
22
]
as well as values for
{\lambda}
smaller than but close to
2
, and a lower bound on the cut-off at
|x|=N^{-1/6}

[
23
]
. In their work they could quantify the rate of convergence in Wasserstein distance for sufficiently large enough
N
. Kiessling in
[
34
]
assumes no cut-off but some additional technical condition which can be read as a bound on the maximal forces of the microscopic system along the trajectories and repulsiveness on the interaction.

By introducing an
N
-dependent cut-off of the singularity, Lazarovic and Pickl proved a convergence in probability result for the trajectories in
[
35
]
, which implies weak propagation of chaos result for the VP case. Following these techniques, Carrillo, Choi, and Salem could generalize this result for a system including white noise. In this case the limiting equation is Vlasov Poisson Focker Planck
[
10
]
. In addition they proved convergence of the marginals of the densities towards the limiting equation in Wasserstein distance rather explicitly by giving a convergence rate estimate. In
[
27
]
, Huang, Liu, and Pickl improved the cut-off rate of the Coulomb potential for the VPFP case by using the regularization effect caused by the noise term.

Recently, Bresch, Jabin, and Soler
[
4
]
developed a stability estimate for the error term in the BBGKY hierarchy for the VPFP case on a two dimensional torus. Bresch, Duerinckx, and Jabin
[
3
]
applied the duality method and proved propagation of chaos (for second order system with singular potentials) in the sense of distributions.

Note, that the different techniques we mentioned lead to different notions of convergence of the many-body system towards the effective equation. Convergence of the relative entropy of the respective densities directly implies convergence in a strong sense (
L^{1}
). However, all results we mentioned that control the relative entropy for a second order system with singular interaction were made in a special situation where the limiting description is given by Euler.
Within the framework of modulated energy, the information on the mesoscopic level, i.e. the kinetic Vlasov dynamics, is somehow missing, which is the main difference between first order and second order systems. However, the mesoscopic scaling or description is very important in many applications and shall not be bypassed.
In contrast to that, the results we mentioned above that are not based on an estimate of the relative entropy obtained only the weak convergence (Wasserstein distance, convergence in distribution).

The goal of this article is to show the strong form of propagation of chaos towards the VP (or VPFP) system. We will show that the quantitative estimates given by Huang, Liu, and Pickl
[
27
]
(for VPFP case) and Lazarovic and Pickl
[
35
]
on the trajectory differences in the sense of probability are the right ingredient that enables us to follow Jabin and Wang

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We compute the time evolution of the relative entropy as follows. For simplicity, we omit the integral domain which is
\mathbb{R}^{2dN}
. We adopt the notations that
z_{i}=(x_{i},v_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Z_{N}=(z_{1},\cdots z_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2dN}
. We proceed as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{N}(f_{t}^{N}\ |\ f_{t}^{\otimes N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int\partial_{t}f_{t}^{N}\log\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\,dZ_{N}-\frac{1}{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\partial_{t}\log f_{t}^{\otimes N}\,dZ_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\left|\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\left(\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dZ_{N}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\,\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}k^{N}(x_{i}-x_{j})-k\star\rho_{t}(x_{i})\bigg)\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\left(\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\right)\bigg]\,dZ_{N}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality to the last term, one obtains that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{N}(f_{t}^{N}|\ f_{t}^{\otimes N})&\leq-\frac{\sigma}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\left|\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\left(\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dZ_{N}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2N\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\bigg|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}k^{N}(x_{i}-x_{j})-k\star\rho_{t}(x_{i})\bigg|^{2}\,dZ_{N}.\end{split}

Using the exchangeability of the particle system (
1.1
), one can rewrite the inequality above as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}_{N}(f_{t}^{N}\ |\ f_{t}^{\otimes N})+\frac{\sigma}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int f_{t}^{N}\,\left|\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\left(\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dZ_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|k\star\rho_{t}(x_{t}^{1,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}k^{N}(x_{t}^{1,N}-x_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\bigg]=:\frac{1}{2\sigma}\mathbb{M},

where the expectation is taken over the law
f_{t}^{N}
.
We then split the estimate for
\mathbb{M}
into several parts as follows,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle\leq 5\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|k\star\rho_{t}(x_{t}^{1,N})-k\star\rho_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})\right|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|k\star\rho_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})-k\star\bar{\rho}_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})\right|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|(k-k^{N})\star\bar{\rho}_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})\right|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\bigg[\,\bigg|k^{N}\star\bar{\rho}_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\,\bigg]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(x_{t}^{1,N}-x_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\bigg]=:\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathbb{M}_{i}.

To estimate
\mathbb{M}_{1}
, we first recall the following estimate for instance in
[
13
]
. Given a Newtonian or Coulombian kernel
k(x)=\pm C_{d}\frac{x}{|x|^{d}}
, there exists a universal constant
C
, such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|k(x_{1}-y)-k(x_{2}-y)||h(y)|\,dy\leq C\|h\|_{(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|(1-\log^{-}|x_{1}-x_{2}|),

where
\log^{-}r:=\min\{0,\log r\}
.

We define the event
\mathcal{A}_{T}
as

\mathcal{A}_{T}:=\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\Phi_{t}^{N}-\Psi_{t}^{N}\|_{\infty}\leq N^{-\delta}\}.

Then Theorem
2.1
implies that for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 5\mathbb{E}\left[\left|k\star\rho_{t}(x_{t}^{1,N})-k\star\rho_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})\right|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{T}^{c}\right]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\left[\left|k\star\rho_{t}(x_{t}^{1,N})-k\star\rho_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})\right|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{T}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 20\|k\star\rho_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{T}^{c})+C\|{\rho_{t}}\|^{2}_{(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{N^{-2\delta}}{\log N}\Big(1-\log\frac{N^{-\delta}}{\sqrt{\log N}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-\alpha}+CN^{-2\delta}\log N\leq CN^{-2\delta}\log N,

where we have used the fact that
\alpha>0
is arbitrary and that
r(1-\log r)\leq r_{0}(1-\log r_{0})
for
0<r=|x_{1}-x_{2}|<r_{0}=\frac{N^{-\delta}}{\sqrt{\log N}}<1
.

For
\mathbb{M}_{2}
, one observes that

\mathbb{M}_{2}\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|k\star(\bar{\rho}_{t}-\rho_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
k=\pm\nabla W
, where

is the fundamental solution to the Poisson equation, i.e.

, one has

\|k\star(\bar{\rho}_{t}-\rho_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\bar{\rho}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}\leq C(\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}})W_{2}^{2}(\bar{\rho}_{t},\rho_{t}),

where we used
[
38
, Theorem 2.9]
. Since Proposition
2.2
implies

W_{2}(\bar{\rho}_{t},\rho_{t})\leq CN^{-\delta}\exp(C\sqrt{\log N})

we get

\mathbb{M}_{2}\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}})N^{-2\delta}\exp(C\sqrt{\log N}).

For
\mathbb{M}_{3}
, we note that
k^{N}(x)=k(x)
if
|x|\geq N^{-\delta}
. Thus

\displaystyle|(k^{N}-k)\star\bar{\rho}_{t}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(k^{N}(x-y)-k(x-y))\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{|x-y|\leq N^{-\delta}}\left(N^{d\delta}|x-y|+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-1}}\right)\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|z|\leq N^{-\delta}}\left(N^{(d-1)\delta}+\frac{1}{|z|^{d-1}}\right)\,dz\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}N^{-\delta}.

Hence,

\mathbb{M}_{3}\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}N^{-2\delta}.

For
\mathbb{M}_{4}
, we apply

h(x)=k^{N}(x)
and
\kappa=d-1

to Proposition
2.1
and choose

sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|k^{N}\star\bar{\rho}_{t}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2m}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}N^{-1+(d-2)\delta+\frac{1}{m}}\leq CN^{-2\delta}.

Finally, for
\mathbb{M}_{5}
, we use Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.1
, with the choice
\alpha=d
and
\ell^{N}(0)=0
, to yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{5}

\displaystyle\leq 5\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(x_{t}^{1,N}-x_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{T}^{c}\bigg]

\displaystyle+5\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}k^{N}(x_{t}^{1,N}-x_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{T}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\|k^{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{T}^{c})+C\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{N^{-\delta}}{\sqrt{\log N}(N-1)}\sum_{j\neq 1}\ell^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})\right|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{T}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)N^{2(d-1)\delta-\alpha}+CN^{-2\delta}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq 1}\ell^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})\bigg|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2\delta}+CN^{-2\delta}\frac{1}{(N-1)^{2}}\sum_{i,j\neq 1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{i,N})\ell^{N}(\bar{x}_{t}^{1,N}-\bar{x}_{t}^{j,N})\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2\delta}+CN^{-2\delta}\iiint\ell^{N}(x-y)\ell^{N}(x-z)\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\bar{\rho}_{t}(z)\bar{\rho}_{t}(x)dydzdx

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2\delta}+CN^{-2\delta}\|\ell^{N}\star\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq CN^{-2\delta}(\log N)^{2},

where we have used

\displaystyle|\ell^{N}\star\bar{\rho}_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\int_{\{|x|\leq N^{-\delta}\}}N^{d\delta}\,dx+\int_{\{N^{-\delta}<|x|\leq 1\}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\,dx\right)+C\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\log N).

Therefore, we get the estimate for relative entropy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{N}(f_{t}^{N}\ |f_{t}^{\otimes N})+\frac{\sigma}{2N}\iint\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\nabla_{v_{i}}\log\left(\frac{f_{t}^{N}}{f_{t}^{\otimes N}}\right)\right|^{2}f_{t}^{N}\,dZ_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CN^{-2\delta}}{\sigma}(\exp\left(C\sqrt{\log N}\right)+(\log N)^{2})\leq\frac{C}{\sigma}N^{-2\delta}\exp\left(C\sqrt{\log N}\right),

Together with the fact that the initial relative entropy
\mathcal{H}(f_{0}^{\otimes N}\ |\ f_{0}^{\otimes N})=0
and
e^{C\sqrt{\log N}}\gg(\log N)^{2}
if
N
is sufficiently large, we deduce our desired result.

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{t}|\left(\frac{1}{\max\{|x_{t}-y_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\max\{|\bar{x}_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\right)D_{t}^{N}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(y_{t},\bar{y}_{t})}\left[\frac{1}{\max\{|x_{t}-y_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\max\{|\bar{x}_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\max\{|x_{t}-y|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\rho_{t}(y)\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\max\{|\bar{x}_{t}-y|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x_{t}-y|\leq N^{-\delta}}N^{3\delta}\rho_{t}(y)\,dy+\int_{N^{-\delta}<|x_{t}-y|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|x_{t}-y|^{3}}\rho_{t}(y)\,dy+\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\int_{|\bar{x}_{t}-y|\leq N^{\-\delta}}N^{3\delta}\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\,dy+\int_{N^{-\delta}<|\bar{x}_{t}-y|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|\bar{x}_{t}-y|^{3}}\bar{\rho}_{t}(y)\,dy+\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\log N)(\|\bar{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}).

Hence, we have

I_{1}\leq C(\log N)\mathbb{E}\left[|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{t}|D_{t}^{N}\right]\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\mathbb{E}[(D_{t}^{N})^{2}].

For
I_{2}
, we use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Young’s inequality and the above estimates to have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\mathbb{E}\left[|y_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\max\{|x_{t}-y_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\max\{|\bar{x}_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\sqrt{\log N}}\mathbb{E}\left[(D_{t}^{N})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\max\{|x_{t}-y_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\max\{|\bar{x}_{t}-\bar{y}_{t}|,N^{-\delta}\}^{3}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\mathbb{E}[(D_{t}^{N})^{2}],

and combine the estimates for
I_{1}
with those for
I_{2}
to yield (
2.8
).

\bullet
(Step 3) Finally, we prove the desired result. We again adopt the notation
D_{t}^{N}
and define

\mathscr{D}_{t}^{N}:=\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}(D_{s}^{N})^{2}.

Then one uses Itô’s lemma to get

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{t}^{N}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{\log N}|\bar{v}_{s}-v_{s}|+|k^{N}\star\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{s})-k\star\rho(x_{s})|\right)D_{s}^{N}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\log N}(D_{s}^{N})^{2}\,ds+2\int_{0}^{t}|k^{N}\star\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{s})-k^{N}\star\rho(x_{s})|D_{s}^{N}\,ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}|(k^{N}-k)\star\rho(x_{s})|D_{s}^{N}\,ds.

By taking expectation, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{D}_{t}^{N}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\log N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{D}_{s}^{N}]\,ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k^{N}\star\bar{\rho}(\bar{x}_{t})-k^{N}\star\rho(x_{t})|D_{s}^{N}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|(k^{N}-k)\star\rho(x_{s})|D_{s}^{N}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=:2\sqrt{\log N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{D}_{s}^{N}]\,ds+J_{1}+J_{2}.

For
J_{1}
, we use the estimates in step 2 to have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\int_{0}^{t}(\|\bar{\rho}_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}})\mathbb{E}[(D_{s}^{N})^{2}]\,ds.

For
J_{2}
, we note that
k^{N}(x)=k(x)
if
|x|\geq N^{-\delta}
. Thus

\displaystyle|(k^{N}-k)\star\rho_{s}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(k^{N}(x-y)-k(x-y))\rho_{s}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{|x-y|\leq N^{-\delta}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq N^{-\delta}}(k(x-y-z)-k(x-y))\psi_{\delta}^{N}(z)\,dz\right)\rho_{s}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|z|\leq N^{-\delta}}\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq N^{-\delta}}|k(x-y-z)-k(x-y)|\rho_{s}(y)\,dy\right)\psi_{\delta}^{N}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|z|\leq N^{-\delta}}\left(\int_{|y|\leq 2N^{-\delta}}|k(y)|\,dy+\int_{|y|\leq N^{-\delta}}|k(y)|\,dy\right)\psi_{\delta}^{N}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}N^{-\delta}.

Hence,

J_{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbb{E}[(D_{s}^{N})^{2}]\,ds+CN^{-2\delta}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,ds.

Thus, we gather all the results and apply Grönwall’s lemma to yield

\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{D}_{t}^{N}]\leq CN^{-2\delta}\exp(C\sqrt{\log N}t),

where
C=C(T,\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})},\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})},\delta)
is independent of
N
.
∎

3.
Propagation of chaos in

: Towards Vlasov-Poisson

In this section, we study the convergence from

-particle system (

3.1.
Technical lemmas

In this subsection, we provide technical lemmas for the proof of Theorem
1.3
. For this, we consider the trajectories

(3.1)

\displaystyle d\tilde{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}_{t}\,dt,

\displaystyle d\tilde{v}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}k(\tilde{x}_{t}-x)\tilde{\rho}_{t}(x)\,dxdt,

to the Vlasov-Poisson equation

(3.2)

\partial_{t}\tilde{f}_{t}+v\cdot\nabla\tilde{f}_{t}+k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}\tilde{f}_{t}=0

with the McKean-Vlasov system (
2.5
). We first provide the following
L^{\infty}
-estimate.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
T>0
, let
\tilde{f}_{t}
be a smooth solution to (
3.2
) on
[0,T]
decaying fast at infinity satisfying

\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})}<\infty.

Then we have

\|\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where
C=C(T,\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})})
is a positive constant.

Remark 3.1
.

Instead of assuming boundedness of the gradient
\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{0}
, one can relax this requirement by imposing polynomial growth conditions. For example, consider bounds of the form

|\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{0}(x,v)|\leq C(1+|x|^{\alpha}+|v|^{\alpha}),

where
C>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
are constants. Such conditions naturally arise in cases involving Gaussian-type distributions in
v
, where the logarithmic gradient exhibits polynomial growth in
v
. With this relaxed condition, one should modify the following proofs with additional technical efforts.

Proof.

From (
3.2
), one has

\partial_{t}\log\tilde{f}_{t}+v\cdot\nabla\log\tilde{f}_{t}+k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}=0.

For
i,j=1,2,3
, we can get

\partial_{t}(\partial_{i}\log\tilde{f}_{t})+v\cdot\nabla(\partial_{i}\log\tilde{f}_{t})+k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}(\partial_{i}\log\tilde{f}_{t})=-\partial_{i}(k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t})\cdot\nabla_{v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}

and

\partial_{t}(\partial_{v_{j}}\log\tilde{f}_{t})+v\cdot\nabla(\partial_{v_{j}}\log\tilde{f}_{t})+k\star{\tilde{\rho}_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}}(\partial_{v_{j}}\log\tilde{f}_{t})=-\partial_{j}\log\tilde{f}_{t}.

Then along the characteristics, one obtains

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}

and

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{x}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}

and since we have (see
[
1
]
for example)

(3.3)

\|\nabla k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left[(1+\|\tilde{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}})(1+\log(1+\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}})+\|\tilde{\rho}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}\right],

we can use our assumption to have

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x,v}\log\tilde{f}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}},

which implies our desired estimate.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose
\tilde{f}_{0}
is of the form

\tilde{f}_{0}(x,v)=\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+|v|^{2})^{\beta}}

with
\alpha,\beta>0
sufficiently large. Then

|\nabla_{x}\log\tilde{f}_{0}|=\left|\frac{2\alpha x}{1+|x|^{2}}\right|,\quad|\nabla_{v}\log\tilde{f}_{0}|=\left|\frac{2\beta v}{1+|v|^{2}}\right|

and hence we can have initial data satisfying our desired conditions.

Now, we compare the trajectory (
3.1
) with the Mckean-Vlasov equation (
2.5
) for the Vlasov-Poisson-Fokker-Planck equation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\tilde{x}_{t},\tilde{v}_{t})
and
(x_{t},v_{t})
be solutions to (

) and (

) with

, respectively, corresponding to the same independent and identically distributed initial data. Once we have

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\log N}|\tilde{x}_{t}-x_{t}|+|\tilde{v}_{t}-v_{t}|\right]\leq C\left(\sqrt{\sigma_{N}}+N^{-\theta}(\log N)\right)e^{C\sqrt{\log N}},

where

Proof.

We first have

\displaystyle d(\tilde{x}_{t}-x_{t})

\displaystyle=(\tilde{v}_{t}-v_{t})dt

\displaystyle=\left[k\star\tilde{\rho}_{t}(\tilde{x}_{t})-k\star\rho_{t}(x_{t})\right]dt-\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}}dB_{t}.

and define

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{x}_{t}-x_{t}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{v}_{s}-v_{s}|\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{v}_{t}-v_{t}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(x_{s})|\right]\,ds+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|B_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})|\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\rho_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(x_{s})|\right]\,ds+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})|\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\rho_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(x_{s})|\right]\,ds+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}t}

\displaystyle=:L_{1}+L_{2}+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}t}.

To estimate
L_{1}

(y_{s},\tilde{y}_{s})

(x_{s},\tilde{x}_{s})

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{y_{s},\tilde{y}_{s}}[|k(\tilde{y}_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})-k(y_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})|]\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{y_{s},\tilde{y}_{s}}\left[|k(\tilde{y}_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})-k(y_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})|\ \Big|\ |\tilde{y}_{s}-y_{s}|>N^{-\theta}\right]\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}_{y_{s},\tilde{y}_{s}}\left[|k(\tilde{y}_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})-k(y_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s})|\ \Big|\ |\tilde{y}_{s}-y_{s}|\leq N^{-\theta}\right]\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=:L_{11}+L_{12}.

For
L_{11}
, note that
|\tilde{y}_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s}|<N^{-\theta}/2
implies
|y_{s}-\tilde{x}_{s}|\geq N^{-\theta}/2
and

\displaystyle L_{11}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg(\iiint_{\{|\tilde{y}-y|>N^{-\theta},\ |\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}|<N^{-\theta}/2\}}+\iiint_{\{|\tilde{y}-y|>N^{-\theta},\ |y-\tilde{x}|<N^{-\theta}/2\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07182pt-\iiint_{\{|y-\tilde{y}|>N^{-\theta},\ \min\{|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}|,|y-\tilde{x}|\}\geq N^{-\theta}/2\}}\Bigg)|k(\tilde{y}-\tilde{x})-k(y-\tilde{x})|\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{y})\rho_{s}(y)\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{y}dyd\tilde{x}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\iiint_{\{|\tilde{y}-y|>N^{-\theta},\ |\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}|<N^{-\theta}/2\}}\frac{|\tilde{y}-y|}{|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}|^{3}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{y})\rho_{s}(y)\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{y}dyd\tilde{x}ds

\displaystyle\qquad|\tilde{y}-y|\left(\frac{1}{|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}|^{3}}+\frac{1}{|y-\tilde{x}|^{3}}\right)\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{y})\rho_{s}(y)\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{y}dyd\tilde{x}ds

For

, we use (

) to yield

\displaystyle L_{12}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\iiint_{\{|\tilde{y}-y|\leq N^{-\theta}\}}(k(\tilde{y}-\tilde{x})-k(y-\tilde{x}))\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x})\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{y})\rho_{s}(y)\,d\tilde{y}dyd\tilde{x}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\{|\tilde{y}-y|\leq N^{-\theta}\}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|k(\tilde{y}-\tilde{x})-k(y-\tilde{x})|\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}\right|\tilde{\rho}_{s}(\tilde{y})\rho_{s}(y)d\tilde{y}dyds

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-\theta}\log N\int_{0}^{t}\|\tilde{\rho}_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\,ds,

where

and

are chosen so that

.

For

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|k\star\rho_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s})-k\star\rho_{s}(x_{s})|\ \Big|\ |\tilde{x}_{s}-x_{s}|>N^{-\theta}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\mathfrak{D}(s)\,ds+CN^{-\theta}(\log N)\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\,ds.

Thus, we gather all the estimates for

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\right)\mathfrak{D}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle+CN^{-\theta}(\log N)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\right)\,ds+\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}t},

and use Gronwall’s lemma to obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}t}+CN^{-\theta}(\log N)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{2\sigma_{N}s}+CN^{-\theta}(\log N)\int_{0}^{s}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{\tau}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\right)\,d\tau\right)

Here, we mention that if one tries to focus only on the propagation of chaos results in

, we can simply compute relative entropy between two equations (

and

) and (

and similarly to the estimates for
\mathbb{K}_{2}
in the proof of Theorem

, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|k\star(\rho_{t}-\tilde{\rho}_{t})(x_{t})|]

\displaystyle\iint_{|y-\tilde{y}|\leq N^{-1}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|k(\tilde{y}-x)-k(y-x)|\rho_{t}(x)\,dx\right|\tilde{\rho}_{t}(\tilde{y})\rho_{t}(y)\,d\tilde{y}dy

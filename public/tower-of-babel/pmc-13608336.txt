Nearly Two Decades of Sustaining a Coalition to Prevent Youth Substance Use: Reflections From the Genesee County Prevention Coalition, Michigan
Authors: Lisa Fockler, Christine Koffkey, Brigitte Baranek, Erin Wright-Kelly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe Genesee County Prevention Coalition (GCPC) was established in 2007 and supports youth and families in Genesee County, the fifth most populous county located in southeast Michigan. GCPC has received funding from the Drug-Free Communities (DFC) Support Program since 2018, charged with mobilizing its community to prevent youth substance use. Over nearly 2 decades, GCPC has established infrastructure and implemented best practices to sustain its coalition efforts. Using local data to drive action, focusing on upstream prevention strategies, and diversifying the funding received and investing it strategically in the organization, GCPC has adapted to changes in society and community conditions to meet the needs of youth in the community. The purpose of this report was to discuss GCPC's capacity building approach to coalition development and implementing community-based strategies to affect substance use and mental health concerns among youth in Genesee County, Michigan.

Introduction

The Drug-Free Communities (DFC) Support Program is the nation's leading effort to mobilize communities to prevent youth substance use.1 Coalitions across the United States receive support, guidance, and funding from the DFC program to build capacity and sustain efforts in community-based prevention. A key feature of DFC coalitions is the collaboration of partners representing 12 sectors of the community.2 In this report, we discuss the Genesee County Prevention Coalition's (GCPC) capacity building approach to coalition development and implementing community-based strategies to affect substance use and mental health concerns among youth in Genesee County, Michigan.

Coalition History

The GCPC, located in Flint, Michigan, was established in 2007 to address alcohol-related automobile crashes and underage drinking. In 2013, GCPC expanded its scope to increase awareness of prescription drug misuse driven by the opioid epidemic. The GCPC's capacity building strategies were strengthened through the DFC's Mentoring Program as a Mentee coalition under the mentorship of the Alliance of Coalitions for Healthy Communities from 2016 to 2018. As a Mentee coalition, the GCPC attended the Community Anti-Drug Coalitions of America's (CADCA) National Coalition Academy in 2017. These unique funding and training opportunities helped to lay the foundation for establishing a strong coalition rooted in best practices using the Strategic Prevention Framework3 (SPF; Figure 1), an evidence-based model that guides communities through a data-driven process to prevent substance use, and supported by representatives from all sectors of the community. The results of these capacity building efforts were achieved when the GCPC was awarded its first DFC grant in 2018 and second grant in 2023.

Strategic Prevention Framework.3

The GCPC serves all residents of Genesee County, Michigan, a diverse community of an estimated 402,279 individuals.4 Genesee is the seventh most vulnerable county (of 83) in Michigan regarding substance use burden, substance use resources, and social vulnerability.5 The coalition focuses on strategies that align with increasing community readiness and implementing effective community-based strategies to address the use of alcohol, cannabis, and other drugs among youth. The work of the GCPC is carried out through a collaboration between paid staff and volunteers. The GCPC Board of Directors provides leadership and guidance in overseeing the coalition's strategic plan. The GCPC maintains active coalition membership with sector representation and Coalition Involvement Agreements with 65 individuals and organizations. This provides a broad base for collaboration on multiple cross-sector issues. This also allows the GCPC to be responsive to emerging issues and community concerns with a comprehensive community-based approach. In 2025, the GCPC launched its Youth Action Council, which focuses on supporting youth development and promoting a positive community norms approach to prevention in Genesee County.

Best Practices

Over nearly 2 decades, GCPC has adapted to changes in society and community conditions to meet the needs of youth in its community. During this time, GCPC has developed a strong infrastructure, used local data to drive action, implemented comprehensive prevention programs, and moved to upstream (ie, systems-focused) prevention strategies. Through community-engaged strategic planning, the GCPC has established a network of community partners that have a shared vision and work together to achieve desired outcomes. These practices are based on the SPF and guide all levels of the GCPC's internal (staff) and external (community) processes.

Develop a Strong Infrastructure

The GCPC formed its first workgroups to bring community members together to assess local conditions and identify resources and gaps to improve health outcomes. To facilitate this work, the GCPC secured initial funding to hire staff and established a multiyear work plan that included internal organizational development and external partnerships to sustain long-term strategies for community-level change. The GCPC used the Coalitions and Partnerships in Community Health6 as a guide to establish monthly community partner meetings and monthly workgroups focused on planning and implementing strategies to address specific substance use and mental health concerns identified by the community. The success of this collaboration is evidenced by continued growth and sector representation at community partner meetings. Through these workgroups, the coalition has established communication channels and networks to respond to emerging substance use or mental health concerns across Genesee County.

Use Local Data to Drive Action

The GCPC uses national, state, and local data to assess local conditions in response to identified community concerns. The assessment phase of the SPF emphasizes the necessity of local data to monitor and measure the impact of implementing prevention services. One example of the GCPC's local data collection efforts is the use of the Community Readiness Model (CRM)7 to measure the community's willingness and capacity to address substance use issues and plan appropriate interventions. Using the CRM prevents the common mistake of implementing an intervention that the community is not yet ready to receive. As of 2025, the GCPC conducted 9 iterations of the CRM (across 3 substances), indicating that community “knowledge of efforts” continues to be low. This has shaped the GCPC's communication strategies and funding priorities to focus on developing effective media campaigns and hiring a Health Communications Specialist. Data measures, including links to landing pages on the GCPC website, provide evidence that campaigns are effective in driving people to the GCPC's Prevention Portal for access to prevention resources and services.8

Implement a Comprehensive Approach

The GCPC used the SPF to address the growing concerns around legalized cannabis and its impact on youth. A multitiered approach that raises awareness, enhances skills, assesses the environment, and engages youth and adults was developed. GCPC's Vape is No Escape social media campaign raises awareness about the dangers of vaping both nicotine and cannabis; Third Millennium Classroom, a school-based course that covers topics on THC, nicotine, underage drinking, and conflict resolution, is provided to school districts as an alternative to suspension for students; environmental scans cross-referencing local ordinances with cannabis retailers and advertisements to identify compliance issues are conducted; and a youth-driven Photo Voice Project was created to show how legalized cannabis has affected youth in the community. The success of this comprehensive approach would not be possible without the GCPC's capacity building and infrastructure for community partners to work together to address this growing concern in the community.

Move to Upstream Prevention

The GCPC has focused on upstream prevention strategies to address substance use and mental health risk factors and build primary prevention capacity. Substance use and mental health often share risk factors early in life and Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) compound on each other to increase that risk. Genesee County is a community heavily affected by trauma (eg, Flint Water Crisis).9 In an effort to increase access to prevention resources and services, the GCPC launched the Partners in Prevention Portal, an online resource directory. This systems-level approach is designed to mitigate the impact of Social Determinants of Health by connecting community members and service providers. The coalition also identified the need for additional trauma-informed practices and trainings across the provider network. This resulted in the formation of a Trauma-Informed Community Change Team to develop a strategic action plan and an ACEs summit for Genesee County. The team has expanded to include both ACEs and Healthy Outcomes from Positive Experiences10 Champions and continues to identify additional gaps and resources to prevent future ACEs and promote Positive Childhood Experiences in the community.

Independent Organization with Diversified Funding

The GCPC has strengthened its financial base to ensure funding from multiple sources for the longevity of the coalition and programming. One of the things that has contributed to the GCPC's success in the last 10 years is the intentional steps to establish the coalition as an independent nonprofit organization. Becoming an independent entity allowed the coalition to expand its mission and vision with its own leadership, board, and priorities. In addition, they have used funding from federal, state, and local sources to expand initiatives and meet DFC matching funds requirements. One unique funding opportunity for Genesee County is a 10-year property tax levy passed by voters in 2021 to support mental health programming and services in the county. The GCPC submitted a proposal and was awarded a 3-year contract to expand organizational capacity by hiring a Health Communications Specialist, Youth Engagement Specialist, and Human Resource and Finance Consultant, as well as launch an annual prevention messaging campaign. This unique funding source has provided the GCPC with the ability to build internal capacity for long-term sustainability.

Conclusion

The GCPC has successfully served Genesee County as a trusted and valued resource in the community. The support provided by the DFC program enabled the coalition to build capacity, expand partnerships, and sustain its efforts to achieve positive community outcomes. The GCPC has navigated many challenges along the way.

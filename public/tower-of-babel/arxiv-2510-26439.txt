When is the operation τ T , L \tau_{{}_{T,L}} a triangle function on Δ + \Delta^{+} ?
Hongliang Lai, Mengyu Luo, Jie Zhang

Abstract
This paper resolves an open problem posed by Schweizer and Sklar in 1983. We establish that the binary operation τ T , L \tau_{{}_{T,L}} is a triangle function on Δ + \Delta^{+} if and only if the following three conditions hold: (a) L L is a continuous t-conorm on [ 0 , ∞ ] [0,\infty] satisfying ( L ​ C ​ S ) (LCS) ; (b) T T is a t-norm on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] ; and (c) T T is weakly left continuous, with left continuity required when L L is non-Archimedean.



1
Introduction

The notion of probabilistic metric spaces was introduced by Menger
[
3
]
in 1942 as a generalization of classical metric spaces. In this framework, the distance between two points is described by a distribution function rather than a deterministic real number. To model the “addition” of distances in the interval
[0,\infty]
, Šerstnev
[
6
]
later introduced the concept of triangle functions, refining the triangle inequality originally proposed by Menger and Wald
[
7
]
.

Motivated by Šerstnev’s formulation, Schweizer and Sklar
[
5
]
studied a family of operations denoted by
\tau_{T,L}
, constructed from a binary operation
T
on
[0,1]
and a binary operation
L
on
[0,\infty]
. They established sufficient conditions under which
\tau_{T,L}
becomes a (continuous) triangle function. However, by presenting a counterexample—the triangle function
\tau_{{}_{D}}
—they also demonstrated that these conditions are not necessary. This led them to pose the following open problem (see 7.9.6 in
[
5
]
): characterize all pairs
(T,L)
for which
\tau_{T,L}
is a (continuous) triangle function.

In 1992, Ying
[
8
]
derived a set of necessary conditions that closely approximate the known sufficient ones, yet the sufficiency of those conditions remained open. In this paper, we completely resolve this long-standing problem. We prove that
\tau_{T,L}
is a triangle function on
\Delta^{+}
if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(a)

L
is a continuous t-conorm on
[0,\infty]
satisfying the (LCS) condition;

(b)

T
is a t-norm on
[0,1]
;

(c)

T
is weakly left continuous, and left continuous whenever
L
is non-Archimedean.

While the necessity of conditions (a)–(c) was already established by Ying
[
8
]
, the sufficiency part—especially the associativity of
\tau_{T,L}
—presents a nontrivial difficulty. Specifically, when
L
is Archimedean and
T
is only weakly left continuous, the argument used in Theorem 7.2.4 of
[
5
]
no longer applies. By introducing a subtle technical argument, we successfully establish the associativity of
\tau_{T,L}
under the weakened continuity condition (see Lemma
3.18
and Proposition
3.19
), thereby completing the sufficiency proof.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review basic concepts and results on t-norms, t-conorms, and triangle functions, mainly following
[
2
,
5
]
, and give a precise statement of Schweizer and Sklar’s open problem. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of our main results.

Definition 2.3

(

There are some basic t-norms on the unit interval

•

\widehat{nM}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\min\{x,y\},&1\leqslant x+y,\\

Then
\widehat{nM}
is not weakly left continuous at the point
(0.5,0.5)
.

A

There is a one-to-one correspondence between t-norms on

The corresponding t-conorms of

M^{*}(x,y)=\max\{x,y\}

Among all t-conorms on

All the t-conorms

For a t-norm

(

Dually, for continuous t-conorms on

Let

Let

A continuous Archimedean t-conorm

Corollary 2.9

Let

([0,\infty],+)
.

3
The main result

In this section, we provide a complete answer to the open problem posed by Schweizer and Sklar, with the main result summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.15
.

Given
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}
, the map
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function on
\Delta^{+}
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

L
is a continuous t-conorm and satisfies condition
(LCS)
;

(b)

T
is a t-norm on
[0,1]
;

(c)

T
is weakly left continuous and if
L
is non-Archimedean then
T
is left continuous.

Remark 3.16
.

Ying has already established the necessity of the three conditions (a)-(c) in the literature
[
8
]
. In fact, in that paper, item (3) in Section 3 and items (1)–(3) in Section 4 provide the proof for (a); items (4) and (5) in Section 4 give (b); item (4) in Section (3) proves the first part of (c) and item (8) in Section 4 yields the second part of (c).

Nevertheless, we choose to present a full proof here to enhance readability for the audience. The following lemma can provide some technical assistance.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}
. If
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function, then for all
f,g\in\Delta^{+}
, and all
x\in]0,\infty[
,

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{L(u,v)<x}T(f(u),g(v)).

Proof.

Since
h=:\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(f,g)
is left continuous at any
x\in]0,\infty[
, we have

h(x)=\sup_{z<x}h(z)=\sup_{z<x}\sup_{L(u,v)=z}T(f(u),g(v))=\sup_{L(u,v)<x}T(f(u),g(v)).

∎

Now we prove the necessity of the conditions (a)-(c).

(a)
L
is a continuous t-conorm and satisfies condition
(LCS)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts.

(i)
L
satisfies the
(LCS)
condition.
We aim to show that for any
u<u^{\prime}
and
v<v^{\prime}
, if
L(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})<\infty
, then
L(u,v)<L(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
Let
x=L(u,v)
and
x^{\prime}=L(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})<\infty
. Define the function
h=\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{u},\varepsilon_{v})
. We analyze the values of
h
at
x^{\prime}
and
x
.

First, consider
h(x^{\prime})
. It holds that

h(x^{\prime})=\sup_{L(r,s)=x^{\prime}}T({\varepsilon_{u}}(r),{\varepsilon_{v}}(s))\geqslant T({\varepsilon_{u}}(u^{\prime}),{\varepsilon_{v}}(v^{\prime}))=1.

Second, we compute
h(x)
. By Lemma
3.17
, it holds that:

h(x)=\sup_{L(r,s)<x}T(\varepsilon_{u}(r),\varepsilon_{v}(s)).

Now, suppose
L(r,s)<x
. We claim that either
r<u
or
s<v
. Indeed, if both
r\geqslant u
and
s\geqslant v
held, then by the monotonicity of
L
, we would have
L(r,s)\geqslant L(u,v)=x
, contradicting
L(r,s)<x
. Thus, for any such pair
(r,s)
, we have
\varepsilon_{u}(r)=0
or
\varepsilon_{v}(s)=0
, which implies
T(\varepsilon_{u}(r),\varepsilon_{v}(s))=0
. Consequently:

h(x)=\sup_{L(r,s)<x}T(\varepsilon_{u}(r),\varepsilon_{v}(s))=0.

Combining the results, we have
h(x)=0<1\leqslant h(x^{\prime})
.
Since
h
is non-decreasing, it follows that
x<x^{\prime}
, i.e.,
L(u,v)<L(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.

(ii)
L
is a continuous t-conorm on
[0,1]
. Our strategy is to show that for all
u,v\in[0,\infty]
,

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}({\varepsilon_{u}},{\varepsilon_{v}})=\varepsilon_{L(u,v)}.

According to Lemma 7.2.13 in

]
, the identity holds when
L
satisfies the
(LS)
condition. We show that it remains valid without the
(LS)
condition on

.

Given
u,v\in[0,\infty[
, let
L(u,v)=x_{0}
and
h=\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{u},\varepsilon_{v})
. For any
x<x_{0}
,
by Lemma
3.17
,

h(x)=\sup_{L(r,s)<x}T(\varepsilon_{u}(r),\varepsilon_{v}(s)).

For
r
and
s
with
L(r,s)<x
, we have that either
r<u
or
s<v
. In fact, if both
r\geqslant u
and
s\geqslant v
held, then we would have
L(r,s)\geqslant L(u,v)=x_{0}
, contradicting
L(r,s)<x<x_{0}
. Thus, for any such pair
(r,s)
, we have
\varepsilon_{u}(r)=0
or
\varepsilon_{v}(s)=0
. Consequently, it holds that for all
x<x_{0}
,

h(x)=\sup_{L(r,s)<x}T(\varepsilon_{u}(r),\varepsilon_{v}(s))=0.

If
x_{0}=\infty
, then it holds that

h=\varepsilon_{\infty}=\varepsilon_{x_{0}}.

If
x_{0}<\infty
, then

h(x_{0})=\sup_{x<x_{0}}h(x)=0.

For any
x\in]x_{0},\infty[
, the function
t\mapsto L(u+t,v+t)
is continuous on
[0,\infty]
and
x_{0}=L(u,v)<x<\infty=L(\infty,\infty)
. It ensures existence of
r>u
and

with
L(r,s)=x
. For such

and

,
{\varepsilon_{u}}(r)=1
and
{\varepsilon_{v}}(s)=1
, so

h(x)=\sup_{L(r,s)=x}T({\varepsilon_{u}}(r),{\varepsilon_{v}}(s))=1.

Therefore, we have shown that, for all
x\in[0,\infty]
,
h(x)=\varepsilon_{x_{0}}(x)
. So, it holds that, for all

,

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{u},\varepsilon_{v})=\varepsilon_{L(u,v)}.

Paricularly, for all

,
\varepsilon_{v}=\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{v})=\varepsilon_{L(0,v)}
, which implies that
L(v,0)=v
. Since
L
is non-decreasing on each place, we have that
L(0,\infty)=\infty
, and
L(u,\infty)=\infty
for all
u\in[0,\infty]
. Consequently, for all
u\in[0,\infty]
,
it holds that

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{u},\varepsilon_{\infty})=\varepsilon_{\infty}=\varepsilon_{L(u,\infty)}.

A similar argument shows that

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{\infty},\varepsilon_{u})=\varepsilon_{\infty}=\varepsilon_{L(\infty,u)}.

Therefore, we have shown that for all
u,v\in[0,\infty]
, it holds that

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(\varepsilon_{u},\varepsilon_{v})=\varepsilon_{L(u,v)}.

Since
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function, it is commutative and associative, and has
\varepsilon_{0}
as identity. Thus,
L
is commutative and associative, and has
0
as identity, that is,
L
is a t-conorm. Moreover, by Proposition
2.5
,
L
is continuous.
∎

(b)
T
is a t-norm on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

It is show by Lemma 7.10 in
[
4
]
, but with slightly different preset conditions on
T
and
L
. Notably, here we do not assume that
L
satisfies the
(LS)
condition.

We aim to show that
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(V_{p},V_{q})=V_{T(p,q)}
for all
p,q\in[0,1]
. Once this is established, the commutativity and associativity of
T
follow directly from the corresponding properties of
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
.

Recall that
V_{p}
is defined by
V_{p}(0)=0
and
V_{p}(x)=p
for all
x\in]0,\infty[
. We now verify the equality pointwise.
Notice that

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(V_{p},V_{q})(x)=\sup_{L(u,v)=x}T(V_{p}(u),V_{q}(v)).

First, consider
x=0
. We know that
L(u,v)=0
implies both
u=0
and
v=0
since
L
is a t-conorm(it is shown by (a)). Thus,

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(V_{p},V_{q})(0)=0=V_{T(p,q)}(0).

Second, consider any
x\in]0,\infty[
.
Since
x\mapsto L(x,x)
is a continuous function from
[0,\infty]
onto
[0,\infty]
, there is some
u\in]0,\infty[
such that
x=L(u,u)
. Clearly, we have
V_{p}(u)=p
and
V_{q}(u)=q
.
Moreover, for any pairs with
L(u,v)=x
, we have
\max\{u,v\}\leqslant L(u,v)=x
since
L
is a t-conorm. Thus, the value
V_{p}(u)
is either
p
or
0
, and
V_{q}(v)
is either
q
or
0
. Thus, the supremum

\sup_{L(u,v)=x}T(V_{p}(u),V_{q}(v))=T(p,q)=V_{T(p,q)}(x).

Therefore, we conclude that
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(V_{p},V_{q})=V_{T(p,q)}
. This completes the proof.
∎

(c)
T
is weakly left continuous and if
L
is non-Archimedean, then
T
is left continuous.

Proof.

We aim to prove that
T
is weakly left continuous, and furthermore, if
L
is non-Archimedean, then
T
is left continuous.
It suffices to establish the following inequality for any given
x_{0},y_{0}\in]0,1]
:

T(x_{0},y_{0})\leqslant\sup\,\{T(x,y)\mid x\leqslant x_{0},\ y<y_{0}\text{ or }x<x_{0},\ y\leqslant y_{0}\}.

Since
L
is continuous and
L(0,0)=0
,
L(\infty,\infty)=\infty
, there exists an
a\in]0,\infty[
such that
L(a,a)=z_{0}
for any fixed
z_{0}\in]0,\infty[
. Define functions
f,g\in\Delta^{+}
as follows:

f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{x_{0}}{a}x,&0\leqslant x\leqslant a,\\
x_{0},&a<x<\infty,\\
1,&x=\infty,\end{cases}\quad g(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{y_{0}}{a}x,&0\leqslant x\leqslant a,\\
y_{0},&a<x<\infty,\\
1,&x=\infty.\end{cases}

For any
u,v\in[0,\infty]
satisfying
L(u,v)=z_{0}
, the inequality
\max\{u,v\}\leqslant L(u,v)=z_{0}
implies
u\leqslant z_{0}<\infty
and
v\leqslant z_{0}<\infty
. Consequently,
f(u)\leqslant x_{0}
and
g(v)\leqslant y_{0}
. We then observe:

T(x_{0},y_{0})\geqslant\sup_{L(u,v)=z_{0}}T(f(u),g(v))\geqslant T(f(a),g(a))=T(x_{0},y_{0}),

which forces the equality:

T(x_{0},y_{0})=\sup_{L(u,v)=z_{0}}T(f(u),g(v))=\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(f,g)(z_{0}).

By Lemma
3.17
, at
z_{0}
, we have:

\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(f,g)(z_{0})=\sup_{L(u,v)<z_{0}}T(f(u),g(v)).

Now, for any
u,v\in[0,\infty]
with
L(u,v)<z_{0}
, the condition
\max\{u,v\}\leqslant L(u,v)<z_{0}
holds. If both
u\geqslant a
and
v\geqslant a
were true, then
L(u,v)\geqslant L(a,a)=z_{0}
would follow, a contradiction. Hence, either
u<a
or
v<a
. This implies either
f(u)<f(a)=x_{0}
or
g(v)<g(a)=y_{0}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle T(x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup_{L(u,v)<z_{0}}T(f(u),g(v))

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup\,\{T(x,y)\mid x<x_{0},\ y\leqslant y_{0}\text{ or }x\leqslant x_{0},\ y<y_{0}\}.

This establishes the weak left continuity of
T
.

To prove the second claim, suppose
L
is non-Archimedean. Then there exists an idempotent element
z_{0}\in]0,\infty[
such that
L(z_{0},z_{0})=z_{0}
. Take
a=z_{0}
in the above construction. For any
u,v
with
L(u,v)<z_{0}
, we have
\max\{u,v\}\leqslant L(u,v)<z_{0}=a
, which implies
u<a
and
v<a
. Consequently,
f(u)<x_{0}
and
g(v)<y_{0}
. Thus,

\displaystyle T(x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup_{L(u,v)<z_{0}}T(f(u),g(v))

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup\,\{T(x,y)\mid x<x_{0},\ y<y_{0}\},

proving that
T
is left continuous.
∎

Next, we prove the sufficiency part of Theorem
3.15
. The following lemma is crucial, as it ensures that for an Archimedean t-conorm
L
and a weakly left continuous t-norm
T
,
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
satisfies the associative law.

Lemma 3.18
.

Suppose
T
is a t-norm on
[0,1]
and
L
is a continuous Archimedean t-conorm on
[0,\infty]
. Given
f,g\in\Delta^{+}
and
y\in]0,\infty[
, then for all
x>y
, there is a pair
(u_{x},v_{x})
with
L(u_{x},v_{x})=x
such that
T(f(u_{x}),g(v_{x}))\geqslant\tau_{{}_{T,L}}(f,g)(y)
.

Proof.

Since continuous Archimedean t-conorms
L
on
[0,\infty]
are isomorphic to either the addition
+
or the t-conorm
W^{*}
, it suffices to prove it for addition
+
and the t-conorm
W^{*}
. Here, we provide the proof only for addition
+
, as the proof for the t-conorm
W^{*}
follows a similar argument.

Since

\tau_{{}_{T}}(f,g)(y)=\sup_{u+v=y}T(f(u),g(v)),

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a pair
(u_{n},v_{n})
with
u_{n}+v_{n}=y
such that

\tau_{{}_{T}}(f,g)(y)-\frac{1}{n}<T(f(u_{n}),g(v_{n})).

Since
\{(u,v)|u+v=y\}
is bounded and closed in
[0,\infty]^{2}
, by compactness, there exists a convergent subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}},v_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with a limit
(u_{0},v_{0})
and
u_{0}+v_{0}=y
. Furthermore, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
u_{n_{k}}\leqslant u_{n_{k+1}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
(the other case,
u_{n_{k}}\geqslant u_{n_{k+1}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
is analogous).

Now for a fixed
x>y
, let
u_{x}=u_{0}
and
v_{x}=x-u_{0}
.
Firstly, since
u_{n_{k}}
is non-decreasing and
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{k}}=u_{0}=u_{x}
, we have

f(u_{n_{k}})\leqslant f(u_{x})

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Secondly, because
v_{n_{k}}=y-u_{n_{k}}
is non-increasing,
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}v_{n_{k}}=v_{0}
, and
v_{x}=x-u_{0}>y-u_{0}=v_{0}
, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v_{n_{k}}<v_{x}
for all
k>K
. It implies that

g(v_{n_{k}})\leqslant g(v_{x})

as
g
is non-decreasing. Therefore, by the monotonicity of
T
,

T(f(u_{x}),g(v_{x}))\geqslant T(f(u_{n_{k}}),g(v_{n_{k}}))

for all
k>K
. Taking the supremum over
k>N
, we obtain

T(f(u_{x}),g(v_{x}))\geqslant\sup_{k>K}T(f(u_{n_{k}}),g(v_{n_{k}}))={\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(y).

∎

Proposition 3.19
.

Given a continuous t-conorm
L
on
[0,\infty]
and a t-norm
T
on
[0,1]
, if
L
is Archimedean and
T
is weakly left continuous, or
L
is non-Archimedean and
T
is left continuous, then
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function on
\Delta^{+}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts based on whether the t-conorm
L
satisfies the Archimedean property.

Part I
: Let
L
be Archimedean and
T
be weakly left continuous. Since continuous Archimedean t-conorms
L
on
[0,\infty]
are isomorphic to either the addition
+
or the t-conorm
W^{*}
, it suffices to prove it for addition
+
and the t-conorm
W^{*}
. Here, we provide the proof only for addition
+
, as the proof for the t-conorm
W^{*}
follows a similar argument.

We show that
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function in four steps:

Step 1
:
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a binary operation on
\Delta^{+}
. It is established by Ying in
[
8
]
( see item (2) in Section 3). Here we write the proof for convenience of readers.

It suffices to show that, for all
f,g\in\Delta^{+}
,
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)
is left continuous on
]0,\infty[
.
That is, at any point
x_{0}\in]0,\infty[
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there is
y<x_{0}
such that

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(y)>{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(x_{0})-\epsilon.

By the definition of
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}
, there is
u_{0},v_{0}\in[0,\infty[
with
u+v=x_{0}
such that

T(f(u_{0}),g(v_{0}))>{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(x_{0})-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Since
T
is weakly left continuous, there exist
a,b\in[0,1]
with
a<f(u_{0})
and
b\leqslant g(v_{0})
(or symmetrically
a\leqslant f(u_{0})
, and
b<g(v_{0})
) such that

T(a,b)>T(f(u_{0}),g(v_{0}))-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

If
a\leqslant f(u_{0})
and
b<g(v_{0})
, then there exists
v^{\prime}<v_{0}
such that
b<g(v^{\prime})
since
g
is left continuous at the point
v_{0}
. Let
y=u_{0}+v^{\prime}
, then
y<u_{0}+v_{0}=x_{0}
, and consequently, it holds that

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(y)\geqslant T(f(u_{0}),g(v^{\prime}))\geqslant T(a,b)>T(f(u_{0}),g(v_{0}))-\frac{\epsilon}{2}>{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(x_{0})-\epsilon.

If
a<f(u_{0})
and
b\leqslant g(v_{0})
, then a symmetric argument yields the same inequality. In both cases, the desired inequality holds, proving left continuity.

Step 2
:
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}
is commutative. For all
f,g\in\Delta^{+}
and
x\in[0,\infty]
, the commutativity of
T
and
L
implies

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{u+v=x}T(f(u),g(v))=\sup_{v+u=x}T(g(v),f(u))={\tau_{{}_{T}}}(g,f)(x).

Thus,
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}
is commutative.

Step 3
:
\varepsilon_{0}
is the identity of
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}
.
For all
f\in\Delta^{+}
and
x\in]0,\infty[
,

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\varepsilon_{0}})(x)=\sup\limits_{u+v=x}T(f(u),{\varepsilon_{0}}(v)).

Since
\varepsilon_{0}(0)
and
{\varepsilon_{0}}(v)=1
if
v>0
, the supremum is attained when
v>0
. Thus,

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\varepsilon_{0}})(x)=\sup\limits_{0<u<x}f(u)=f(x)

since
f
is left continuous. Hence,
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\varepsilon_{0}})=f
.

Step 4
:
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}
is associative.
For all
f,g,h\in\Delta^{+}
, we show

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g),h)={\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(g,h)).

For any
x\in[0,\infty]
, the left side is

\displaystyle{\tau_{{}_{T}}}({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g),h)(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{u+v=x}T({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(u),h(v))

\displaystyle=\sup_{u+v=x}T(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v)).

Now we check the equality

\sup_{u+v=x}T(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v))=\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

On one hand, we show the
\geqslant
. For any
a,b,c\in[0,\infty]
with
a+b+c=x
, let
u=a+b
and
v=c
. Then
u+v=x
, and we have

\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s))\geqslant T(f(a),g(b)).

By the monotonicity of
T
,

T(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v))\geqslant T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

Taking the supremum over all such
(a,b,c)
yields

\sup_{u+v=x}T(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v))\geqslant\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

On the other hand, we show the
\leqslant
. We know that
{\tau_{{}_{T}}}({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g),h)
is left continuous by the step 1. Our strategy is to show that for all
y<x
,

\sup_{u+v=y}T(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v))\leqslant\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

Indeed, if this holds, then by left continuity at
x
, the limit as
y\rightarrow x^{-}
of the left-hand side equals its value at
x
, giving the desired inequality.

Now, fix
y<x
and take any
u,v\in[0,\infty]
with
u+v=y
. Let
w=x-v
, then
w>u
and
w+v=x
. By Lemma
3.18
, there exists a pair
(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
with
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}=w
such that

\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s))={\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g)(u)\leqslant T(f(r^{\prime}),g(s^{\prime})).

Then by the monotonicity of
T
,

T\left(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v)\right)\leqslant T\left(T(f(r^{\prime}),g(s^{\prime})),h(v)\right).

But since
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}+v=w+v=x
, we have

T(T(f(r^{\prime}),g(s^{\prime})),h(v))\leqslant\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

Combining these inequalities, we obtain

T\left(\sup_{r+s=u}T(f(r),g(s)),h(v)\right)\leqslant\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

Taking the supremum over all pairs
(u,v)
with
u+v=y
gives the desired inequality for each
y<x
.

Therefore, we conclude that

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g),h)(x)=\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(T(f(a),g(b)),h(c)).

A similar argument shows that

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(g,h))(x)=\sup_{a+b+c=x}T(f(a),T(g(b),h(c))).

By the associativity of
T
, the right-hand sides of these two expressions are equal. Therefore,

{\tau_{{}_{T}}}({\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,g),h)={\tau_{{}_{T}}}(f,{\tau_{{}_{T}}}(g,h)),

completing the proof of associativity.

Part II
: Let
L
be non-Archimedean and
T
be left continuous. As pointed out by Ying in
[
8
]
, the proof of Theorem 7.2.4 in
[
5
]
is also valid if the condition
(LS)
is weakened as the condition
(LCS)
. Thus,
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}
is a triangle function on
\Delta^{+}
if the t-conorm
L
is Archimedean and the t-norm
T
is left continuous.
∎

Note that the mapping
p\mapsto V_{p}
embeds the monoid
([0,1],T,1)
into the monoid
(\Delta^{+},\tau_{{}_{T,L}},\varepsilon_{0})
, and the mapping
x\mapsto\varepsilon_{x}
embeds the monoid
([0,\infty],L,0)
into
(\Delta^{+},\tau_{{}_{T,L}},\varepsilon_{0})
. The following corollary is therefore natural:

Corollary 3.20
.

(
[
8
]
)

Given
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}
, the map
\tau_{{}_{T,L}}

(\Delta^{+},d_{L})

.

Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri) plant preparation facilitates to enhance the activities of dendritic cells to control non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
Authors: R. Ilaya Kumar, Kavya Jain, Prashasti Arora, Harshnna Gururajan, Karan Raj Rai, Oishi Mukherjee, Melvin George, Koustav Sarkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBrahmi (Bacopa monnieri) plant preparation (BPP) has been recognizedfor its immunomodulatory capabilities, but its molecular and epigenetic effects onimmune cells in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remain unknown.ObjectiveThisstudy investigated the way BPP influenced the molecular phenotype and functionalprofile of monocyte-derived dendritic cells (DCs) derived from an NSCLC patient anda healthy donor. ResultBPP treatment enhanced DC maturation, as demonstrated byelevated levels of CD1d and co-stimulatory molecules (CD80/CD86), and conditionedsupernatants that efficiently activated autologous T cells. BPP increased proinflammatorysignaling molecules STAT1, STAT2, STAT4, and total NF-κB, whiledecreasing STAT3, STAT5, STAT6, and the DNA-repair protein DNAPKcs.Transcriptional analysis indicated a Th1-oriented immune program, characterized bythe up-regulation of TBX21, IFNG, and TP53, alongside the down-regulation ofGATA3, FOXP3, RORC, IL10, and MYC. Introduction

Lung cancer continues to be one of the deadliest carcinomas globally, predominantly attributed to non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounts for around 85% of occurrences. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) includes various subtypes such as adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma, each exhibiting distinct histological and clinical characteristics that influence treatment and prognosis (Travis 2011; Abbasian et al. 2022). Other environmental exposures and genetic predispositions account for the remaining 15 percent, while tobacco smoke continues to be a primary risk factor, accounting for just over 85 percent of NSCLC cases (Alduais et al. 2023). It is estimated that in 2020, 2.2 million new NSCLC cases were reported in the world (Sung et al. 2021), which shows the global excessive of NSCLC as the leading cause of cancer death. Treatment of NSCLC is challenging, as the pathogenesis of NSCLC is often complex, mediated by mutations in genes such as the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and KRAS (Herbst et al. 2018).

The current treatments for NSCLC include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, and targeted therapies. Early stage NSCLC is often surgically treatable, but most of the time NSCLC is diagnosed in the advanced stage, when surgery is unlikely to achieve a cure. Although chemotherapy and radiation are still the main treatments in advanced NSCLC, these treatments are plagued by toxicity and resistance (Friedlaender et al. 2020). Because of patients with EGFR mutations, targeted therapies, especially tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), have been promising; however, patients' resistance to these therapies is common. Secondly, it’s the NSCLC treatment immune checkpoint inhibitors that have revolutionized cancer treatment by allowing T-cells to respond more effectively to cancer cells. But NSCLC cells are often evaded by immune detection and therefore require novel approaches to enhance immune response and treatment efficacy (Hughes and Hu 2023; Rotow and Bivona 2017).

NSCLC cells develop mechanisms to escape immunosurveillance and represent a significant challenge in cancer treatment. Down regulation of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules, secretion of immunosuppressive cytokines such as TGF β and IL 10, increased expression of immune checkpoint inhibitors are some such mechanisms (Hassanpour and Dehghani 2017). As proved by these strategies, they largely depend on the tumor microenvironment, which includes various cells, such as cancer associated fibroblasts and immune cells, which together contribute to fostering an immunosuppressive milieu promoted for tumor growth (Zhang et al. 2022). NSCLC generates this tumor microenvironment, so we need to understand and improve immunotherapeutic approaches, and thus it is crucial to characterize it (Binnewies et al. 2018).

Immune surveillance is an important part of the immune system that helps fight off cancer by monitoring cells that might become cancer cells and destroying them. Implication vertebrate adaptive immune responses against tumors involves agonist antigen presentation to T-cells by antigen presenting cells (APCs), especially dendritic cells (DCs), which capture and present tumor antigens to T-cells in order to activate the adaptive immune response. However, immune evasion is often successful in preventing immune destruction of cancer cells and hence immune surveillance (Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b). Treating advanced NSCLC has shown success with immunotherapy, which increases the body’s immune response to fight against cancer. However, despite achieving this, a significant part of the patients do not respond to immunotherapy, or eventually relapse, and this suggests that immune responses may be improved by adding to or combining immunotherapies (Herbst et al. 2018). This further compounds the challenge offered by the tumor microenvironment as DCs often become functionally impaired in the tumor microenvironment, as immunosuppressive factors can suppress their ability to stimulate a robust immune response. Improving DC function would help to reduce immune evasion and improve immunotherapy responses in NSCLC (Binnewies et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2022).

In recent years, new plant based therapies are being recognized as promising complementary treatments for cancers, including Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC). Most of these therapies contain bioactive compounds with potent anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and anti-cancer activities (Sato et al. 2017; Alduais et al. 2023). A traditional herb, Bacopa monnieri (Brahmi), has recently attracted attention as a possible candidate for cancer treatment, specifically for its immunomodulatory effects. Brahmi’s bioactive compounds such as bacosides have been studied to show their ability to aid in the maturation and functionality of dendritic cells (DCs) a key component of the immune system that presents the antigen. Brahmi helps boost DC function that facilitates better recognition and destruction of tumor cells by the immune system (Sato et al. 2017; Alduais et al. 2023).

The action of Brahmi on DCs is not the only effect of Brahmi on the immune system. By stimulating pro-inflammatory cytokines and repressing immunosuppressive cytokines, it is able to shift the immune response to a tumor-fighting one, as is normally characteristic of an immune response against cancer. The shift in cytokine production enhances the body’s ability to mount a strong anti-tumor immune response to the NSCLC cells which tend to use immune evasion mechanisms. Additionally, in recent studies Brahmi has been noted to synergize with other therapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors and cancer vaccines. (Hassan et al. 2024; Roy et al. 2024).

Overall, Brahmi’s capacity to modulate immune responses, augment dendritic cell function and skew the immune milieu towards anti–tumor immunity, makes it a leading candidate for eventual use in conjunction with currently approved NSCLC therapies. (Sato et al. 2017; Roy et al. 2024). These findings indicate that the inclusion of Brahmi into NSCLC therapeutic regimens might improve therapeutic efficacy, lessen side effects, and improve the quality of life of patients with NSCLC. Brahmi’s therapeutic potential needs further investigation into full scope including its use in combination with other immunotherapeutic strategies. (Friedlaender et al. 2020; Karthikeyan and Devi 2020).

This paper reports on evaluating the short-term impact of Brahmi Plant Preparation (BPP) on dendritic cells (DCs) and lymphocytes of NSCLC patients. To measure significant molecular changes, we use internal pilot MTT assays to optimize concentrations used in a 48 h in vitro exposure to maintain cell viability. We define the effects of BPP single-application and lung cancer antigen (LCA) pulsed BPP on dendritic cell maturation markers, cytokine pattern, transcription factor activities and STAT pathway regulation. In addition, we determine chromatin-level epigenetic changes by enriching activating and repressive histone marks at promotors of essential immune genes. These multi-layered analyses will identify whether BPP by itself or in combination with tumor antigens can reprogram DCs that originate in NSCLC into a pro-inflammatory DC with a functional competent phenotype thus putting forward BPP as a potential agent of interest in immuno-adjuvant- based therapy in NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Plant material

This study was carried out using Bacopa monnieri, a medicinal herb belonging to the Plantaginaceae family and traditionally referred to as Brahmi in India. The plant is also known as herb of grace, thyme-leaved gratiola and water hyssop. The taxonomic validation was done by a competent person, at the Central National Herbarium, Botanical Survey of India, Shibpur, Howrah. The plant specimen was verified on January 25, 2022, in accordance with the World Flora Online database (http://www.worldfloraonline.org).

Blood sample collection and PBMC isolation

Peripheric blood was obtained from normal human donors and NSCLC patients; after getting ethical approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee, SRM Medical College Hospital and Research Centre, Kattankulathur, Tamil Nadu, India (Approval No: 2909/IEC/2021). The samples were collected in 10 mL of blood of a patient with NSCLC (stage III, male, aged 30) before the treatment started and a healthy donor (male, aged 26). Blood was sampled into EDTA coated tubes and was processed in less than two hours to maintain the viability of cells. In the case of PBMC isolation, blood was first diluted 1:1 in PBS and then overlaid with Ficoll-Paque which underwent density gradient centrifugation at 2200 rpm over a period of 30 min. The ensuing PBMCs were re-suspended in culture medium at a density of 1 106 cells/mL to be used in the assays. The surplus of the cells was cryopreserved in freezing medium (10 percent DMSO in FBS) and was stored at -80 o C to be used later. (Rakshit et al. 2021; Sarkar et al. 2010).

Preparation of lung cancer antigen (LCA) from A549 cell line

The A549 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cell line was used to prepare lung cancer antigen (LCA). Cell cultures were allowed to grow to confluence, scraped and repeatedly frozen and thawed in order to induce lysis. For each cycle, cells were frozen at –196 °C and rapidly thawed at 37 °C in a water bath. Complete lysis was confirmed visually. After the resulting crude lysate was prepared, it was taken as LCA without further purification and stored at -80 C until use. (Charong et al. 2021).

Bacopa monnieri plant preparation (BPP)

BPP was made into a crude aqueous extract by dissolving 1 mg of Bacopa monnieri powder in 1 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and incubating the solution overnight at 4 °C to allow maximum solubilization. Insoluble debris was separated by centrifugation of the extract at 1500 rpm at 4 °C on the following day. The supernatant obtained (with the soluble fraction) was harvested and stored at 4 °C until it could be used in other experiments (Roy et al. 2024).

Generation of Mo-DCs

The PBMCs were separated into adherent and non-adherent cells to develop Mo-DCs. The seeding of cells was done in a 24-well plate in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS and then incubated at 37 o C and 5 percent CO 2 humidified atmosphere in an incubator. The non-adherent cells were discarded and the adherent monocytes cultured in RPMI-1640 medium comprising 10 ng/mL recombinant human recombinant GM-CSF and 5 ng/mL IL-4 in an attempt to differentiate the monocytes to immature dendritic cells within 6 days. In day six, Mo-DCs were cultured with LPS (1 ug/mL), BPP (1 mg/mL) with and without LCA to mature Mo-DCs after 48 h (Sarkar et al. 2010).

Flow cytometric analysis of CD1d surface expression

The monocyte-derived dendritic cells (Mo-DCs) treated with Bacopa monnieri plant preparation (BPP) were analyzed using flow cytometry to determine the expression of CD1d, a surface marker linked with the activation of dendritic cells. Cells were washed in PBS, and stained by incubating with a primary antibody specific to CD1d, then incubated with secondary antibody conjugated with a fluorophore. The flow cytometer (BD FACS Melody) was used to obtain post-staining by applying adequate lasers and filters. Our analysis of CD1d expression would allow us to measure the extent of modulation of dendritic cell maturation by the use of BPP (Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b).

Flow cytometric profiling of STAT proteins, DNAPKcs, and NF-kB

To assess the influence of BPP on the major immune pathways, monocyte-derived dendritic cells (Mo-DCs) of normal and NSCLC donors were examined on the level of expression of STAT proteins, DNAPKcs, NF-kB. The cells were washed with PBS and co-labeled by primary antibodies against STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5, STAT6, NFκB, and DNAPKcs and incubated with Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies. The acquisition of post staining was carried out on a flow cytometer (BD FACS Melody) with the suitable laser and filtration settings. This strategy allowed us to compare in detail the changes in signaling between conditions of treatment and donor types. (Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b).

Quantification of mRNA expression via RT-qPCR

Each of the treatment groups was pelleted and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) of the obtained pellet cells. The RNA quality and concentration was determined photometrically and its reverse transcribing done using 1 g of total RNA using High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems). qRT-PCR was performed with a CFX Connect Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad) with SYBR Green-based chemistry (TaKaRa TB Green Premix) and gene-specific primers. The expression of important transcription factors (TBX21, GATA3, RORC, FOXP3) of T cells, effector cytokines (IFNG, IL10), regulatory genes (P53, C-MYC) was also tested. Technical triplicates were performed on each reaction. The relative mRNA expression was measured by 8c method against GAPDH as an interior reference. (Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b).

Co-culturing of Mo-DCs with T cells

After maturation and antigen pulsing, monocyte-derived dendritic cells (Mo-DCs) were co-cultured with autologous lymphocytes to assess functional immune responses. Preparation of Mo-DCs was performed as described earlier; antigen pulsing was performed LPS, BPP with or without LCA. Density gradient centrifugation was used for preparing isolated non adherent lymphocytes. An optimal Mo-DCs: lymphocytes ratio was found to be mixed then incubated under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂) (Singh et al. 2020).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The culture supernatants of treated dendritic cells were quantified using ELISA in IFN-gamma, IL-2, IL-4, IL-10, IL-17 and TNF-alpha. There were commercially available ELISA kits (OptEIATM BD Pharmingen) used. The plates were precoated with certain capture antibodies and allowed to stay overnight. Wells were blocked using 5% BSA 2 h after which they were incubated with diluted primary antibodies specific to each cytokine. HRP conjugated secondary antibodies were then added after several washes (5% BSA). The color reaction was developed upon adding the TMB substrate and stopped by addition of 1N sulfuric acid. Measurement was done at the wavelength of 450 nm. Concentrations of cytokines were determined as ratios to standard curves of known concentrations of antigen (Sarkar et al. 2010).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation quantitative polymerase chain reaction (ChIP-qPCR)

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR was performed in the co-cultured cells of dendritic cells and T cells. In brief, cells were fixed and washed then lysed in RIPA buffer, and then shered chromatin was prepared with the help of chemical sonication using MNase and immunoprecipitation was carried out from the sheared chromatin with target antibodies such as H3K4me3, H3K27me3, H3K14Ac, HDAC2, Histone (H3), NKκB, c-Myc and p53. Synthesized DNA was subjected to SYBR Green qPCR with gene-specific primers of IFNG and TBX21 using purified DNA. Reactions were done in triplicate technical replicates. Fold enrichment was determined by the percent input method and normalized to total H3. (Rakshit et al. 2021; Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b).

Cytotoxicity assay (LDH release)

Cytotoxic activity was determined by a lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer instruction. To be used as an experimental control, A549 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells were seeded as triplicates in a 96-well flat-bottom plate and left overnight to allow attachment. On the following day, DC:T cells and A549 cells were mixed, in a certain effector target (E:T) ratio of 1:10. The treatment groups included Immature DC:T, LPS DC:T, LPS + LCA DC:T, BPP DC:T, and BPP + LCA DC:T co-cultures. There were untreated (spontaneous release) and Triton X-100 treated (maximum release) wells. Stop solution was added after 15 min of incubation with LDH reaction mixture and absorbance was measured at 490 nm with microplate reader. Percentage cytotoxicity was calculated using: [(ODsample – ODbackground) / (ODmax – ODbackground)] × 100 (Sarkar et al. 2010).

Statistical analysis

Means ± standard error of the mean (SEM) from a minimum of two to three independent investigations are used to express all results. We performed a one-way ANOVA in conjunction with Tukey's post hoc test to compare the groups for multiple groups, and an unpaired, two-tailed Student’s t-test to identify significant differences between two groups. Nonparametric statistical techniques were used for a normal data distribution analysis as indicated. The threshold for statistical significance was set at p-values less than 0.05.

Discussion

In this study, we showed that Brahmi plant preparation (BPP), an extract of Bacopa monnieri,improves dendritic cell (DC) activation and induces Th1-biased immune responses against non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Although the overall anti-cancer activity of BPP has been reported in other tumor types, the potential of BPP to interact with the immune system specifically via dendritic cells as well as via epigenetic regulation in NSCLC has not been studied before. The findings can therefore provide a new understanding of BPP as a possible immune-epigenetic regulator, to the current existing strategies of cancer immunotherapy.

Dendritic cells treated with BPP had a strong increase in the expression of a non-classical MHC, CD1d, which has been linked to lipid antigen presentation and NKT cell cross-priming. Such upregulation indicates more mature and immunostimulatory DC phenotype. Past reports on the immune modulatory effects of phytocompounds have demonstrated relatively weak effects on DC surface markers but ours demonstrate that BPP has a significant effect on DC phenotype likely increasing its ability to prime cytotoxic lymphocytes. The upregulation of CD1d is especially pertinent as it mediates the gap between innate and adaptive immunity, especially when addressing solid tumors, including NSCLC, which usually impairs antigen presentation (Zhang et al. 2022).

Transcriptionally, BPP treatment led to a selective modulation of major STAT signaling pathways. In particular, we found a higher expression of STAT1, STAT2, and STAT4 molecules that are critical to Th1 differentiation and IFN-g production and a repression of STAT3, STAT5, STAT6, and DNA-PKcs often linked to immunosuppressive and tumor immunopresence. Whereas other phytochemicals can work through broad-spectrum transcriptional pathways, BPP has a noted bias in the immune-activating direction. This trend indicates that BPP not only induces immune activity, but also inhibits counter-regulatory tumor-mediated signalling, establishing a productive immune microenvironment to promote anti-tumor responses (Shanmugam et al. 2022a, b).

The epigenetic activity was identified as transcriptional elevation by BPP. ChIP-qPCR analysis showed that active histone mark H3K4me3 and H3K14ac were highly enriched at IFNG and TBX21 gene loci, presenting the permissive chromatin status. In contrast, repressive stains like H3K27me3 and HDAC2 were exhausted. Changes in histone modifications led to a shift in transcription factor and chromatin remodeler binding. Specifically, c-Myc and NF-κB occupancy decreased, whereas p53 occupancy increased. ChIP-qPCR showed lower promoter occupancy, despite increasing NF-κB protein expression. This means that it is post-translationally controlled or situated in the cytoplasm, which prevents it from binding to DNA even when overexpressed. In immunological and tumor cells, NF-κB p65 is maintained in the cytoplasm due to altered IκB interaction or reduced nuclear import, resulting in increased total protein but decreased transcriptional activity (Liu et al. 2024; Hayden). Furthermore, recent research on transcription factors such as FOXO1 and FOXO3 in NSCLC has shown that natural compounds can modulate the nuclear translocation, stability, and DNA-binding of key regulators, reprogramming tumor-associated signaling networks (Ebrahimnezhad et al. 2023, 2024). These findings suggest that BPP-derived phytoconstituents may have similar upstream effects on transcription factor regulation, explaining the observed difference between NF-κB expression and promoter occupancy. The concept that BPP promotes chromatin-level reprogramming via transcription is supported by epigenetic remodeling, which has only been described in a few cases involving plant-derived chemicals. This establishes BPP as an integrated regulator of immune activation and chromatin dynamics (O'Donnell et al. 2017).

The functionality of this immune reprogramming was reflected in the changed cytokine profile of the treated lymphocytes. BPP-treated co-cultures showed marked boosts in IFN-gamma, Il-2 and TNF-alpha- cytokines that play key roles in Th1-mediated anti-tumor-related immune responses, as well as suppressed the expression of IL-4, IL-10, and IL-17 cytokines that are related to Th2 and regulatory phenotypes. It is important to mention that the strongest cytokine changes occurred after BPP and lung cancer antigen (LCA), which indicates a possible synergistic effect. This functional polarization of immune response is reflected at the chromatin and transcriptional level and further confirms the functions of increasing the effect of cytotoxic immune activity by BPP in the tumor environment (Hodge and Diller 2016).

Direct evidence of functional anti-tumor activity was observed in vitro tumor killing assays. The A549 NSCLC cell line has been chosen for antigen production and cytotoxicity experiments because it provides a stable, homogeneous, and well-defined epithelial model, resulting in reliable immunological readouts. In contrast, patient-derived tumor cells exhibit fluctuating antigen expression and survival, making BPP-specific effects on dendritic cell-mediated cytotoxicity difficult to assess. The results of LDH release assays in the A549 cell co-culture indicated that there was a significant amount of cytotoxicity, particularly in BPP + LCA. This indicates that in addition to the molecular priming of immune cells, BPP also endows them with efficient tumor-killing functions. BPP was found to have a direct correlation between reprogramming and functional anti-tumor activity when compared to existing herbal formulations, which frequently lack a mechanistic understanding or generate only indirect cytotoxicity. The results indicate that it is a potentially viable natural immune adjuvant with therapeutic potential in the real world. (Rakshit et al. 2021).

One of the strongest aspects of the study is the use of patient-derived immune cells of the normal and NSCLC participants, which gives it a high translational relevance. The replicability of the impacts of BPP between various biological replicates and treatment conditions is an added advantage to its robustness. Further, the combination of phenotypic/transcriptional/epigenetic data constructs a unified mechanistic tale, otherwise a reoccurring shortcoming in plant-based immunotherapy studies. Although previous research has involved either changes in cytokines or surface markers, our methods yield a systems-level overview of the mechanism of the immunomodulatory effect of BPP. (Herbst et al. 2018).

Despite being informative, this work is subject to specific limitations. The findings should be regarded as preliminary and may not accurately reflect inter-individual heterogeneity in immune responses because they are based on small donor samples. All of these studies were done in vitro and a in vivo experimentations are necessary to establish systemic immune responses and tumor shrinkage while our current FACS panel focused on total protein expression, future work will aim to distinguish activated (nuclear-localized) NF-κB subunits such as p65 and p50 using intracellular staining and compartment-specific quantification. This will help clarify the transcriptional relevance of NF-κB activation and its downstream immune effects. Single-cell transcriptomic experiments and in vivo tumor models in the future will be essential to determine the full therapeutic potential of BPP and its target specificity. Additional experiments based on dose-dependency and long-term outcomes of BPP on immune reminiscence and tumor recurrence are also warranted. (Zhang et al. 2022).

In summary, our data supports BPP as an immunoepigenetic agent that can promote the maturation of dendritic cells, transcriptional reprogramming of lymphocytes, and functional anti-tumor activity in NSCLC. Through the combination of chromatin remodeling and the activation of the immune system, BPP is a natural compound with promising potential in cancer immunotherapy. It represents a unique opportunity to combine plant-based interventions with the existing immuno-oncology approaches due to the possibility of its simultaneous actions as an immune-activator and epigenetic regulator. Further research into possible applications of BPP in concert with immune checkpoint inhibitors or cancer vaccines can only confirm its role in the next generation of personalized, low-toxicity approaches to cancer treatment. (Friedlaender et al 2020).

Conculsion

The current research demonstrates Brahmi Plant Preparation (BPP) immune epigenetic potential to re-wire tumor-immune interface in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC). The effect of BPP on the maturation of the dendritic cells was clear in that it increased the expression of CD1d and when used along with Lung Cancer Antigen (LCA) increased its efficacy, a synergistic process meaning increased presentation of the antigen. Molecularly, BPP shifted the balance of STAT signals in favor of anti-tumor immunity, increasing the activity of STAT1/2/4 and NF- kB but reducing the activity of immune-suppressive signaling pathways such as STAT3/5/6 and DNAPKcs, in total, suggesting a paradigm shift of immune evasion to immune activation.

This shift was coupled with a transcriptional imprint of BPP treatment of NSCLC dendritic cells that was characterized by the induction of TBX21, IFNG, and TP53, the concerted downregulation of FOXP3, GATA3, RORC, IL10, and CMYC. This reprogramming was also validated by Chromatin immunoprecipitation, whereby the enrichment of H3K4me3, H3K14Ac and p53 at IFNG, TBX21 loci showed an increase, which was accompanied by an eviction of repressive marks H3K27me3, HDAC2 and by oncogenic drivers such as NF- kB and c-Myc. Such epigenetic variations were associated with practical manifestations strong production of IFN-gamma, IL-2, TNF alpha, reduced IL-4, IL-10, IL-17, and enhanced cytotoxic T cell-mediated lysis of A549 NSCLC cells, as shown by the strongest case in the BPP + LCA group.

Although such results provide strong initial evidence supporting the potential usefulness of BPP as a low-toxicity immune-strengthening epi-modulator, the available evidence is limited to short-term in vitro models. Additional in vivo confirmations and translational research are required to position it therapeutically in NSCLC. Nevertheless, the presented work brings BPP as the potentially valuable plant-based agent that may be used in immunologic and epigenetic modulation, which is a significant boost.

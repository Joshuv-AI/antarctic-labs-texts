Transductions of Graph Classes Admitting Product Structure
Petr Hliněný, Jan Jedelský

Abstract
In a quest to thoroughly understand the first-order transduction hierarchy of hereditary graph classes, some questions in particular stand out; such as, what properties hold for graph classes that are first-order transductions of planar graphs (and of similar classes)? When addressing this (so-far wide open) question, we turn to the concept of a product structure – being a subgraph of the strong product of a path and a graph of bounded tree-width, introduced by Dujmović et al. [JACM 2020]. Namely, we prove that any graph class which is a first-order transduction of a class admitting such product structure, up to perturbations also meets a structural description generalizing the concept of a product structure in a dense hereditary way — the latter concept being introduced just recently by authors under the name of ℋ {\cal H} -clique-width [MFCS 2024]. Using this characterization, we show that the class of the 3D grids, as well as a class of certain modifications of 2D grids, are not first-order transducible from classes admitting a product structure, and in particular not from the class of planar graphs.

Introduction

A recent research trend on the boundary between structural graph theory and
graph logic is to study
first-order
(FO) transductions between (hereditary)
graph classes,
e.g.
[
5
,
26
]
.
This research is motivated by structural questions about dense
hereditary graph classes, and by the significant goal to resolve computational
complexity of the FO model checking problem on dense graph classes.

We refer to
Section

2
for the definitions.
Briefly, a simple first-order
interpretation
is given by a binary FO formula

\xi
over graphs, and for a graph
G
the result of the interpretation is
the graph
\xi(G)
on the same vertex set and the edge relation (assumed
symmetric) determined by
uv\in E(\xi(G))

\iff

G\models\xi(u,v)
.
In a
first-order transduction

\tau
, one can additionally duplicate the vertices of

G
and assign arbitrary vertex colors before applying
\xi
, and then take
an induced subgraph of the result (in particular,
\tau(G)
is actually a
set of graphs – unlike
\xi(G)
).
These notions are naturally extended to graph classes; here
\tau({\cal C})

denotes the class of all graphs obtained by applying
\tau
to (colored)
graphs from a class
{\cal C}
.

All transductions in this paper are first-order.
We say that a graph class
{\cal D}
is
transducible
from a class

{\cal C}
if there is a transduction
\tau
such that
{\cal D}\subseteq\tau({\cal C})
.
We interpret this relation as that
{\cal D}
is not “logically richer”
than
{\cal C}
(although
{\cal D}
may be combinatorially a lot more complicated
class than
{\cal C}
), and the aim is that if we understand and can handle the
class
{\cal C}
, we would be able to do the same with
{\cal D}
.

There are two core and mutually related questions in the area;
for a class
{\cal C}
, describe structural properties satisfied by
(all) transductions of
{\cal C}
, and
for classes
{\cal C},{\cal D}
, decide whether
{\cal D}
is transducible from
{\cal C}
.
For an illustration, let
{\cal T}
be the class of trees,
then all transductions of
{\cal T}
are of bounded clique-width, but not every
graph class of bounded clique-width is transducible from
{\cal T}

(this would be true considering richer MSO transductions).
In fact, Nešetřil et al.
[
23
]

showed that a graph class
{\cal C}
is a first-order transduction of a class of
graphs of bounded tree-width, if and only if
{\cal C}
is of bounded clique-width
and excludes some half-graph as a bi-induced subgraph
(we say that it has
stable
edge relation).

Results of this kind can be useful also in the FO model checking problem.
Consider for example the following result of Gajarský et
al.
[
17
]
;
a graph class
{\cal D}
is a transduction of a graph class of bounded maximum
degree, if and only if
{\cal D}
is near-uniform.
Since, luckily, the claimed transduction can be efficiently computed, the
characterization
[
17
]
readily gives an
efficient FO model checking algorithm for each near-uniform graph class.
In general, when a graph class
{\cal C}
has an efficient FO model checking
algorithm, and a class
{\cal D}
is transducible from
{\cal C}
(say,
via
\tau
), there are still two obstacles for FO model checking on
{\cal D}
:
First, for
G\in{\cal D}
one has to be able to compute
G_{1}\in{\cal C}
such that

G\in\tau(G_{1})
, and second, one also has to guess the coloring of
G_{1}
used in

\tau
to obtain
G
.

On the other hand, it is also interesting and useful to understand why a
graph class
{\cal D}
is
not
transducible from a graph class
{\cal C}
.
While on the positive side (‘is transducible’), one can simply give a
transduction and guess a coloring, on the negative side the task is much
more difficult—one has to be able to find, for every transduction
\tau
,
a graph in
{\cal D}\setminus\tau({\cal C})

and that is hard to argue.
In other words, in
{\cal D}
, one needs to understand the ‘obstacles’ which are
not transducible from
{\cal C}
.

For example, long paths are such obstructions for transducibility from a
class of bounded-height trees.
This is in fact a tight characterization;
Ossona de Mendez, Pilipczuk and Siebertz
[
25
]

proved that a graph class
{\cal C}
is of bounded shrub-depth (that is, transducible
from a class of bounded-height trees), if and only if
{\cal C}
does not
transduce the class of all paths.
Other, more complex examples of how understanding the obstructions for
transducibility is used to obtain strong results are contained, e.g., in
Bonnet et al.
[
3
]
or
in Dreier, Mählmann and Torunczyk
[
11
]
.

However, except sporadic examples of success, we currently lack good tools
to prove non-transducibility.
A typical approach (to proving that a class
{\cal D}
is not transducible
from
{\cal C}
) suggests a structural property
\Pi
satisfied by
{\cal C}
and
preserved under transductions, and then finds graphs in
{\cal D}
not
satisfying
\Pi
.
The rather few examples of properties preserved under transductions include,
e.g.; monadic stability
[
27
]
,
bounded clique-width
[
8
]
,
each value of shrub-depth
[
19
]
,
bounded twin-width
[
4
]

and bounded flip-width
[
28
]
.

1.1
Our results

Our research is inspired by the task to characterize classes which are
transducible from the class of planar graphs.
While planar graphs are usually perceived as combinatorially and
algorithmically “nice”, their transductions (including, e.g., the
well-studied classes of
k
-planar and fan-planar graphs, or map graphs)
can be combinatorially very complicated.
(For example, testing 1-planarity is NP-hard already for planar graphs with
one added edge
[
6
]
.)
It is an open question whether the class of toroidal graphs (embeddable on the torus)
is transducible from planar graphs.

Not much was known prior to this paper about obstructions to transducibility
from planar graphs except that the class of planar graphs is monadically stable and, more
recently, that such transductions are of bounded twin-width
[
4
]
.
One can also prove, using known logical tools and the fact that planar
graphs are of bounded local tree-width, that classes transducible from
planar graphs are of bounded local clique-width up to perturbations.

On the other hand, several major breakthrough results about planar graphs in the combinatorics
domain, such as getting an upper bound on the queue number of planar graphs,
have been recently obtained using a new tool of a
product
structure

[
12
]
.
In the original basic setting, a class
{\cal C}

admits a product structure
if,
for some
k
, every graph in
{\cal C}
is a subgraph of the strong product

\boxtimes
(
Figure

1
) of a path and a graph of tree-width
\leq k
.
See
Definition

1
.
Planar and surface-embeddable graphs, for instance, do
admit product structure (
Theorem

2
).

Our main result claims that transductions of classes admitting a
product structure essentially (up to bounded perturbations) follow a similar kind of
product structure (here briefly reformulated in view of
Definition

3
and
Theorem

4
):

•

(
Theorem

23
)
If
{\cal C}
is a class admitting a product structure and
\tau
is a
transduction defining the class of simple graphs
\tau({\cal C})
,
then every graph in
\tau({\cal C})
is a
bounded perturbation of an induced subgraph of the strong product
of a path and a graph from a class of bounded clique-width.

We actually prove, in
Theorem

21
,
a generalized version in which paths (of the assumed product structure)
are replaced by graphs from any fixed class of bounded maximum degree.

We also give the converse direction of the main result, in a modification
consisting in strengthening the assumption of bounded clique-width to
bounded stable clique-width:

•

(
Theorem

26
)
Assume that every graph in a class
{\cal C}
is a bounded perturbation of an
induced subgraph of the strong product of a path
and a graph from a class of bounded stable clique-width.
Then,
{\cal C}
is transducible from a class admitting a product structure.

Again, this is also generalized to any fixed class of bounded maximum degree
in place of the class of paths.

Finally, we demonstrate the strength of

Theorem

23
by giving straightforward
proofs of the following consequences:

I.

(
Corollary

34
) The class of all 3D grids, and

II.

(
Corollary

38
) the class of certain non-local modifications of 2D grids
– by adding a bunch of apex vertices adjacent to mutually distant nodes of the grid,

are not transducible from any class admitting product structure, and in particular
not from the classes of planar graphs and graphs embeddable on a fixed surface.

At the same time and independently of us,
Gajarský, Pilipczuk and Pokrývka
proved point
I.
in
[
18
]
.

The core of our results is in the following technical lemma:

(

)
Let

Every strongly

:

Let

and

such that

(Q,f(\ldots))
-expression (

)
for the subgraphs

The color of each vertex

)

Using the information stored in the vertex colors

and

There are two core ideas enabling us to reuse colors

Strong locality of

Consequently, we may “remember” the vertices of

In fact, it suffices to remember the vertices

We use weak colorings to bound the number of vertices

-clique-width, an answer to
Question

42
is not
immediate (cf.
[
13
]
),
and we are not even sure whether this answer should be yes or no.
Nevertheless, a very new result of
Gajarský, Gładkowski, Jedelský, Pilipczuk and
Toruńczyk
[
16
]

allows us to derive a ‘sparse fragment’ of
Theorem

23

in a different way:

Proposition 43
.

Let
{\cal C}
be a class of graphs admitting product
structure. Let
\tau
be a first-order transduction
such that
\tau({\cal C})
is a class of simple
K_{t,t}
-free
graphs for some fixed integer
t
.
Then, the class
\tau({\cal C})
“nearly admits” a product structure,
meaning that there is an integer
k
such that every graph of
\tau({\cal C})
,
after deleting at most
k
of its vertices,
is a subgraph of the strong product of a path and a graph of
tree-width at most
k
.

Proof.

Consider a graph
G
and a collection
{\cal A}\subseteq 2^{V(G)}
of vertex
subsets such that, for each
A\in{\cal A}
, the induced subgraph
G[A]
is connected.
Then
{\cal A}
is called a congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor model of a
graph
H
on
V(H)={\cal A}
if:
(i) every induced subgraph
G[A]
,
A\in{\cal A}
is of radius
\leq d
,
(ii) for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, at most
c
sets of
{\cal A}
contain
v
,
and (iii) for every edge
AB\in E(H)
(where
A,B\in{\cal A}
), the sets
A

and
B

touch
in
G
– meaning that
A\cap B\not=\emptyset
or some
two vertices
a\in A
and
b\in B
are adjacent in
G
.
A graph
G_{1}
is a
congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor
of
G
if
there exists a congestion-
c
depth-
d
minor model in
G
isomorphic to
G_{1}
.

By our assumptions,
the class
{\cal C}
(as admitting product structure) is of bounded expansion
and the class
\tau({\cal C})
is
K_{t,t}
-free.
Therefore, by Gajarský, Gładkowski, Jedelský, Pilipczuk and
Toruńczyk
[
16
]
, the following holds;
there exists
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau({\cal C})
is contained in the
class of congestion-
c
depth-
c
minors of the class
{\cal C}^{\bullet}
,
where
{\cal C}^{\bullet}
is the class obtained by adding a universal vertex to
every graph of
{\cal C}
.

Let
H\in\tau({\cal C})
which is a congestion-
c
depth-
c
minor
of
G^{\bullet}\in{\cal C}^{\bullet}
.
Let
G\subseteq G^{\bullet}
be the graph of
{\cal C}
from which
G^{\bullet}

was constructed by adding a universal vertex
u
.
Let
X\subseteq V(H)
where
|X|\leq c
be the set of vertices of
H

whose sets in the minor model contain
u
.
We are going to show that
H_{1}:=H-X
is a subgraph of the strong product of a path
and a graph of tree-width at most
k
(
k
independent of
H
).

First,
H_{1}
is a congestion-
c
depth-
c
minor of
G\in{\cal C}
,
and
G\subseteq P\boxtimes M
where
P
is a path and
M
is of tree-width
\leq m
.
Then, clearly,
H_{1}
is a depth-
c
minor
of
G\boxtimes K_{c}\subseteq P\boxtimes M\boxtimes K_{c}
.

Second, Hickingbotham and Wood
[
21
]

proved (their result was actually more general)
that depth-
c
minors of graphs of the form
P\boxtimes M\boxtimes K_{c}

are subgraphs of
P\boxtimes M^{\prime}
where
M^{\prime}
is of tree-width bounded
only in terms of our
m
and
c
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Lastly, inspired by
[
17
]
,
we conclude with an algorithmic question:

Question 44
.

Let
{\cal C}
be the class of planar graphs (or a class admitting a product
structure), and
\tau
be a first-order transduction.
Can we find a first-order transduction
\tau^{\prime}
(of
{\cal C}
) subsuming
\tau

such that, for every graph
G\in\tau({\cal C})
there is
an efficiently computable preimage
G^{\prime}\in{\cal C}
such that
G\in\tau^{\prime}(G^{\prime})
?

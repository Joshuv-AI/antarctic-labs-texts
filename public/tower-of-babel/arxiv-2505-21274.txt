Sample complexity of optimal transport barycenters with discrete support
Léo Portales, Edouard Pauwels, Elsa Cazelles

Abstract
Computational implementation of optimal transport barycenters for a set of target probability measures requires a form of approximation, a widespread solution being empirical approximation of measures. We provide an O ⁡ ( N / n ) O(\sqrt{N/n}) statistical generalization bound for the empirical sparse optimal transport barycenters problem, where N N is the maximum cardinality of the barycenter (sparse support) and n n is the sample size of the target measures empirical approximation. Our analysis includes various optimal transport divergences including Wasserstein, Sinkhorn and Sliced-Wasserstein. We discuss the optimality of our bound as well as the application of our results to specific settings such as K-means, constrained K-means, free and fixed support Wasserstein barycenters.



1
Introduction

Optimal transport barycenters provide a geometrically structured notion of mean for a set of probability measures. They are minimizers of functionals of the form

\nu\longmapsto\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{D}\left(\mu^{\ell},\nu\right),

(1.1)

where
\nu
is a probability measure,
\mathbb{D}
is an optimal transport divergence, and
\left\{\mu^{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
is a collection of target probability measures in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This problem was first introduced for
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}

Agueh and Carlier (2011)
, as a generalization of McCann’s interpolation
McCann (1997)
. This was also proposed independently for texture synthesis and mixing
Rabin et al. (2011)
. Following fast implementation of
W_{2}
-barycenters (e.g.
Cuturi and Doucet (2014)
), they became a central tool in machine learning and statistics with various applications such as sample blending
Srivastava et al. (2015)
, texture mixing
Rabin et al. (2011)
, multisource domain adaptation
Montesuma and Mboula (2021b)
or music genre recognition
Montesuma and Mboula (2021a)
. Various extensions based on different transport divergences
\mathbb{D}
in (
1.1
) exist: entropic
Chizat (2023)
, Bures
Chewi et al. (2020)
, sliced
Bonneel et al. (2015)
and Gromov Wasserstein barycenters
Brogat-Motte et al. (2022)
.

Sample averaged approximation and sparse barycenters

In many applications, the target probability measures in (
1.1
) are computationally intractable, and one needs to resort to discrete approximations. Typical schemes include histogram approximation (e.g. images), sample average approximation (SAA)
Montesuma and Mboula (2021b)
;
Claici et al. (2018)
or online stochastic approximation (SA)
Dvurechenskii et al. (2018)
;
Dvinskikh (2020)
;
Backhoff et al. (2024)
. We focus on sample average approximation arising in two main use cases: the statistical setting where distributions are only accessible through empirical samples
Montesuma and Mboula (2021b)
and numerical Monte-Carlo approximation of known densities
Puccetti et al. (2020)
.
In both cases, all measures, including the resulting barycenter, are discrete, possibly supported on a very large number of atoms.
Consequently, most optimal transport barycenter algorithms restrict the number of atoms of the barycenter, similarly to optimal quantization of measures
Pagès et al. (2004)
. Our analysis includes this support constraint. In other words, the probability measures
\nu
in (
1.1
), is subject to the constraint
|\supp(\nu)|\leq N
for some integer
N\geq 1
, where
\supp(\nu)
denotes the support of
\nu
. This problem is known as optimal transport barycenters with sparse support
Yang and Ding (2024)
;
Borgwardt and Patterson (2021)
. Many modern machine learning problems leverage this sparse barycenter framework, including applications in LLM
Ai et al. (2025)
, flow cytometry
Gachon et al. (2024)
, graph clustering
Etienne et al. (2025)
or cross-machine fault diagnosis
Lee and Zhuo (2025)
.

Our main problem of interest is that of statistical guaranties for the optimal transport barycenters sample averaged approximation under support cardinality constraints, with various divergences
\mathbb{D}
: (sliced) Wasserstein distances, (debiased) Sinkhorn divergences. The collection of target probability measures
\{\mu^{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
each relate to
n

i.i.d
random variables
X_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,X_{n}^{\ell}\overset{\text{i.i.d}}{\sim}\mu^{\ell}
with empirical measures by
\mu_{n}^{\ell}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}^{\ell}}
, and the empirical sparse barycenters are given by

\underset{(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\times\Delta_{N}}{\argmin}\ \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{D}\left(\mu_{n}^{\ell},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)

(1.2)

where
Y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
is a point cloud in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Delta_{N}
is the
(N-1)
-probability simplex.

Statistical guaranties for optimal transport barycenters

For general population measures, statistical rates of the form
O(n^{-1/3d})
for empirical barycenters were described in
Carlier et al. (2024)
, suffering from the curse of dimensionality. On the other hand, for discrete target measures, an
O(n^{-1/2})
rate is known
Heinemann et al. (2022)
. This is reminiscent of the statistical complexity of optimal transport adapting to the least complex measure
Hundrieser et al. (2024)
. Indeed, empirical optimal transport converges as
O(n^{-1/d})

Fournier and Guillin (2015)
, while if at least one of the two measures is discrete, the rate is
O(n^{-1/2})

Del Barrio et al. (2024)
.

Contribution

We provide uniform rates in expectation for the empirical sparse optimal transport barycenter functional (
1.2
). They are of the form
O(\sqrt{N/n})
, where the barycenter has
N
support points, and
n
is the number of samples per empirical target measure. We consider several divergences bridging the gap between various results in the literature. We deduce sample complexity estimates for the optimal transport barycenters with sparse support. Our proof techniques combine tools from empirical risk minimization theory and estimations on dual variables arising in the dual formulations of transport divergences.

2
Introduction to optimal transport and barycenters

2.1
Optimal transport divergences

We refer to
Peyré and Cuturi (2019)
for a computational point of view, and to
Villani (2008)
for a more theoretical presentation. The optimal transport between two measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a solution to the optimization problem over measures:

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}c(x,y)d\gamma(x,y),

(2.1)

where
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a lower semi-continuous cost function and
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the set of couplings: probability measures with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
. In the Euclidean case, if
c\colon(x,y)\mapsto\|x-y\|^{p}
for an integer
p\geq 1
, this is the Wasserstein distance
W_{p}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x-y\|^{p}d\gamma(x,y).

(2.2)

The semi-discrete setting corresponds to one measure being finitely supported. We focus on this setting which encompasses the sparse barycenter problem. The infinite dimensional problem over measures can be reformulated as a finite-dimensional maximization problem based on optimal transport duality (see Appendix
A
).
Various optimal transport-based divergences were introduced based on variations of the Wasserstein distance.

The Sinkhorn divergences
Cuturi (2013)
;
Peyré and Cuturi (2019)
add an entropic regularization term, for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x-y\|^{p}d\gamma(x,y)+\epsilon\text{KL}(\gamma|\mu\otimes\nu)

(2.3)

where
\text{KL}(\gamma|\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\log(\frac{d\gamma}{d\xi}(x,y))-1)d\gamma(x,y)
denotes the Kullback-Leibler divergence and
\epsilon>0
is a regularization parameter. For discrete measures, the optimization problem in (
2.3
) can then be efficiently solved using the Sinkhorn algorithm
Sinkhorn and Knopp (1967)
;
Cuturi (2013)
. Similarly as Wasserstein distances, in the semi-discrete setting, there is a finite dimensional dual problem
Genevay et al. (2016)
of particular use in our analysis, it is presented in Appendix
A
.

Considering projections onto lines leads to sliced Wasserstein distances
Rabin et al. (2011)
;
Kolouri et al. (2019)
. For any
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, set
P_{\theta}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\langle\theta,x\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
, understood as the projection on the line directed by
\theta
. For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one obtains two collections of pushforward univariate measures
(P_{\theta\sharp}\mu)_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
and
(P_{\theta\sharp}\nu)_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
. Sliced distances combine divergences between these univariate measures for all directions
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We obtain the Sliced and max-Sliced Wasserstein distances respectively defined as

\begin{split}SW_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}W_{p}^{p}({P_{\theta}}_{\sharp}\mu,{P_{\theta}}_{\sharp}\nu)d\sigma(\theta)\\
\text{max-}SW_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)&=\max_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}W_{p}^{p}({P_{\theta}}_{\sharp}\mu,{P_{\theta}}_{\sharp}\nu),\end{split}

(2.4)

where
\sigma
denotes the uniform probability measure over
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
In the semi-discrete setting, these also admits finite dimensional dual problems as discussed in Appendix
A
.

2.2
Optimal transport barycenters with sparse support

Given a collection of target probability measures
\{\mu^{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
, an unconstrained barycenter with respect to divergence
\mathbb{D}
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), it is typically absolutely continuous
Agueh and Carlier (2011)
;
Brizzi et al. (2025)
if at least one of the target measures is absolutely continuous.
A common strategy is approximation by discrete probability measures, restricting the optimization set in (
1.1
) to
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This is referred to as optimal transport barycenter problem with
sparse support
, with underlying measures of the form
\nu^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
where
Y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
and
\pi\in\Delta_{N}
. The problem is reformulated as follows:

\underset{(Y,\pi)\in A}{\min}\ \bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right):=\underset{(Y,\pi)\in A}{\min}\ \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{D}\left(\mu^{\ell},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)

(2.5)

where A is a closed nonempty subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\times\Delta_{N}
which integrates possibly additional constraints. Formulation (
2.5
) covers various settings described in the literature
Cuturi and Doucet (2014)
;
Borgwardt and Patterson (2021)
:

2.3
Discretization and computational complexity

Even for continuous target measures, barycenter algorithms operate on discrete data. A common approach is to approximate the target measures by empirical averages, and to compute their barycenter (
1.1
) with dedicated methods
Cuturi and Doucet (2014)
;
Li et al. (2020)
;
Heinemann et al. (2022)
.
Most algorithms actually add support constraints as in (
2.5
), effectively computing a sparse barycenters
Yang and Ding (2024)
;
Borgwardt and Patterson (2021)
;
Mimouni et al. (2025)
.
For discrete measures supported on
N
, the optimal transport problem has an
O(N^{3}\log(N))
computational cost
Peyré and Cuturi (2019)
. The discrete Wasserstein barycenter problem inherits this complexity
Altschuler and Boix-Adsera (2022)
;
Borgwardt and Patterson (2021)
, making it tractable only for measures supported on a small number of atoms
Altschuler and Boix-Adsera (2021)
.
The complexity of Sinkhorn divergences
W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
(
2.3
) is
O(N^{2}/\epsilon)
Dvurechensky et al. (2018)
, which allows for a trade-off between optimal transport accuracy and algorithmic efficiency
Chizat (2023)
.
Sliced-Wasserstein distances are based on univariate closed form quantile expressions
(
Villani, 2021
, Chapter 2.2)
, making them also computationally relevant
Bonneel et al. (2015)
.

4
Discussion of the results

4.1
Sample complexity of optimal transport divergences

Empirical optimal transport suffers from the curse of dimensionality
Fournier and Guillin (2015)
, but it is now well established that its statistical convergence depends on the intrinsic complexity of the target measures
Hundrieser et al. (2024)
. If one of them is discrete, the convergence rate is of actually of order
O(1/\sqrt{n})

Sommerfeld and Munk (2018)
;
Genevay et al. (2019)
;
Hundrieser et al. (2024)
;
Nadjahi et al. (2020)
;
Lin et al. (2021)
. Theorem
3.1
allows to recover convergence rates for the quantity
\mathbb{E}|\mathbb{D}(\mu_{n},\nu)-\mathbb{D}(\mu,\nu)|
, for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
, as well as for the empirical divergences themselves. These do not improve upon the literature.

4.2
Barycenters with sparse support

For a sparse barycenter with at most
N
support points and sample size
n
, Theorem
3.1
provides generalization bounds of order
\sqrt{N/n}
, classical in semi-discrete optimal transport
(
Del Barrio et al., 2024
, Theorem 2.10)
. Interestingly, this rate is uniform in the number of measures
L
, in the regularization parameter
\epsilon
for the Sinkhorn divergence, without exponential dependency on the ambiant dimension
d
.

For
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
, assuming that each measure
\mu^{\ell}
is supported on
n^{\ell}
points, and setting
N\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}n^{\ell}-L+1
, problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) coincide: an unconstrained barycenter turns out to be sparse
(
Anderes et al., 2016
, Theorem 2)
. In this setting,
Heinemann et al. (2022)
obtains the same rate of convergence
\sqrt{N/\min_{\ell}(n^{\ell})}
for the unconstrained barycenter, this analysis being limited to large
N
.

4.3
Tightness of the result

In this section, we discuss the optimality of the estimation error bound in Corollary
3.4
with respect to the sample size
n
and the sparsity level
N
.

4.3.1
Lower bound of the estimation error

We aim to establish a lower bound for the estimation error, i.e., the left-hand side term in Corollary
3.4
, in a worst-case sense over the choice of target measures.
We rely on
(
Bartlett et al., 2002
, Theorem 1)
which provides minimax lower bounds for the optimal quantization cost (
L=1
and
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
). A direct extension of this result provides a lower bound for the sparse
2
-Wasserstein barycenter problem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
, the number of measures
L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and the dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Assume
N\geq 3
and
n\geq 3435N
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a random measure depending on
nL
random variables in
\mathbb{B}_{R}
. Then
there exists
\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the estimation error of Corollary
3.4
verifies

\mathbb{E}\left[\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right)-\min_{(Y,\pi)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}}\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)\right]\geq C_{d}\sqrt{\frac{N^{1-\frac{4}{d}}}{n}}

(4.1)

where
C_{d}
is an explicit constant which only depends on
d
.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
can be found in
D.2
.
For a fixed
N
, Lemma
4.1
implies that the
O(1/\sqrt{n})
convergence rate for barycenter functional in
3.4
cannot be improved without further assumptions.
However the dependency in
N
differs.

4.3.2
Dependency in
N
in the estimation error

There is an
O\left(N^{-2/d}\right)
factor between the lower bound in (
4.1
) and the upper bound in (
3.1
). This raises the question of the optimal exponent in
N
. We exhibit a situation where the lower bound is tight.

Setting
N=n
,
L=1
and
\pi=(1/n,\ldots,1/n)
, the upper bound (
3.1
) of Corollary
3.4
remains constant as
n
increases. However, we will argue that the error actually decreases like
O(n^{-2/d})
. Indeed, in this setting, the minimizer of
\mu\mapsto W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)
over
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is clearly
\mu_{n}
and thus the estimation error writes

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{n})-\min_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{n}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{n})\right]=O(n^{-2/d}),

see
Fournier and Guillin (2015)
.
Hence Corollary
3.4
is not optimal in this particular case.
The above argument suggests that the optimal exponent in
N
is related to estimates of the quantity
W_{2}(\mu,\mu_{n})
, a notoriously difficult problem in statistical optimal transport
(
Chewi et al., 2024
, Section 2)
. The curse of dimensionality is in fact hidden in the sparsity level
N
, as remarked in
(
Del Barrio et al., 2024
, Theorem 2.10)
.

4.3.3
Beyond the
W_{2}^{2}
case

We expect the same conclusions for
\mathbb{D}=SW_{p}^{p}
and
\text{max-}SW_{p}^{p}
since the
1/\sqrt{n}
rate is also known to be optimal for these divergences
Xu and Huang (2022)
. The bias of the Sinkhorn divergence
\mathbb{D}=W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
benefits from a more favorable
1/n
convergence rate
del Barrio et al. (2023)
suggesting that a sharper rate is attainable for barycenters as well.

4.4
K-means and constrained K-means

Choosing
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
and
L=1
, Corollary
3.4
provides error bounds for the celebrated K-means problems.
Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be
n
i.i.d samples with distribution
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
. It is well known minimizing the K-means functional is actually an optimal quantization problem for the empirical measure
\mu_{n}
(see Appendix
D.4
):

\min_{Y\in(\mathbb{B}_{R})^{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min_{j=1,\ldots,N}\|X_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}=\min_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{n},\nu).

(4.2)

Corollary
3.4
then yields a convergence rate of order
O(\sqrt{N/n})
for the K-means problem, which yields an improvement by a factor
\sqrt{\log(N)}
compared to
(
Györfi, 2002
, Section 4.4)
. The Wasserstein functional formulation is closer to the measure approximation view point. It also allows to consider the constrained K-means problem
Ng (2000)
, fixing the mass associated to each centroid with a weight vector
\bar{\pi}\in\Delta_{N}
and minimizing the functional
Y\mapsto W_{2}^{2}\left(\mu_{n},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\pi}_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)
.
For uniform weights
\bar{\pi}_{i}=1/N
for all
i\in[\![1,N]\!]
, this is the optimal uniform quantization problem to which Corollary
3.4
apply as well.

6
Conclusion

The expected convergence rate of the empirical sparse optimal transport barycenters is shown to be of order
O\left(\sqrt{N/n}\right)
for several types of optimal transport divergences. The rate is min-max optimal for the Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
. Investigating optimality for sliced distances
\mathbb{D}=SW_{p}^{p},\>\text{max-}SW_{p}^{p}
and Sinkhorn divergences
\mathbb{D}=W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
is left for future work.

Appendix A
Semi-dual formulation of optimal transport

The Kantorovich dual formulation of the Wasserstein distances
W_{p}
can be recast as a semi-dual problem using the
c
-transform. Similarly, the Sinkhorn divergences
W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
admit a semi-dual formulation based on the smoothed
c
-transform. In the semi-discrete setting (
i.e.
between a continuous and a discrete measure), these formulations turn an infinite-dimensional program (corresponding to the primal formulations (
2.1
) and (
2.3
)) into a finite-dimensional one, which is a key point in our proofs. In this section, we present these semi-dual formulations in the semi-discrete setting, with a particular focus on the case where the discrete measure has zero weights or repeated atoms. Throughout this section, we consider a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A.1
Semi-dual formulation outside the generalized diagonal

The generalized diagonal plays a central role throughout our proofs, it denotes the set of point clouds with at least two identical components:

D_{N}=\left\{Y:=(y_{1},\dots y_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\>|\>\exists i\neq j\>\text{such that}\>y_{i}=y_{j}\>\right\}.

(A.1)

Let us then define
\nu:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
a discrete measure supported on
Y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\setminus D_{N}
and strictly positive weights
\pi\in\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
. For a bounded continuous function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let us also denote
\phi^{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
its c-transform, defined in our case for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\phi^{c}(x)=\min_{i=1,...,N}(\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-\phi(y_{i}))

(
Santambrogio, 2015
, Section 1.3)
. Then by Kantorovich’s duality (
(
Villani, 2008
, Theorem 5.10)
) and
c-
convexity of the optimal potentials we have the following formulation for the
p
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu
and
\nu
:

W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\underset{\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-\phi_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\phi_{i}.

(A.2)

In (
A.2
), with a slight abuse of notation, the dual potential
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is identified with its value on the support of
\nu
, which consists of
N
distinct points:
\phi_{i}=\phi(y_{i})
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
For this dual formulation to hold, the measure
\nu
must be supported outside of the generalized diagonal
D_{N}
, with strictly positive weights; see the discussion in the next Section
A.2
.

Entropic optimal transport (
2.3
) between
\mu
and
\nu
also admits both a dual and a semi-dual form (see
(
Genevay et al., 2016
, Proposition 2.1)
), which are respectively given by the following optimization problems:

\begin{split}W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)&=\underset{u,v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x)d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}v_{i}\\
&\qquad-\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(x)+v_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)\end{split}

(A.3)

and

W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}-\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\exp\left(\frac{w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}-\epsilon.

(A.4)

These constructions also necessitate that
Y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\setminus D_{N}
and
\pi\in\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
.

A.2
The case of support points on the generalized diagonal
D_{N}
and/or with null weights

As previously mentioned, it is mandatory that
(Y,\pi)\in\big(\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)^{N}\setminus D_{N}\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
for a functional of the form
(Y,\pi)\mapsto\mathbb{D}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)
to admit the duality formulations described above.
Indeed, suppose that
y_{i}=y_{j}
, for some
i\neq j
, then the dual formulation of the problem should include the constraint
\phi_{i}=\phi_{j}
, as both
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
represent the evaluation of a continuous potential in the same argument. In other words, we would have
\phi_{i}=\phi(y_{i})=\phi(y_{j})=\phi_{j}
.
While this may seem like a minor technicality that can often be overlooked, it cannot be omitted in our analysis. This is particularly relevant in the case of the sliced Wasserstein distance, where two distinct points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
may project onto the same point on a line. Similarly, it is important to ensure that the weights of the discrete measure remain in the interior of the probability simplex
\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
.

This technicality has been handled in
(
Portales et al., 2024
, Section 5.1)
with the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\times\Delta_{N}
, then there exists
M\in[\![1,N]\!]
and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{\pi})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{M}\setminus D_{M}\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{M})
such that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
p\geq 1
:

W_{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=W_{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tilde{\pi}_{i}\delta_{\tilde{y}_{i}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad W_{\epsilon,p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=W_{\epsilon,p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tilde{\pi}_{i}\delta_{\tilde{y}_{i}}\right).

A.3
Semi-duality formulation for semi-discrete sliced divergences

Starting from the definition of the sliced distances in (
2.4
), the dual formulation (
A.2
) can be applied to each one-dimensional distance
W_{p}(P_{\theta\sharp}\mu,P_{\theta\sharp}\nu),\>\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, addressing the ties in the support point of the measures
P_{\theta\sharp}\nu
. Let
(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\setminus D_{N}\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
, then, for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
there exists an integer
M:=M(\theta)\in[\![1,N]\!]
such that
\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\theta\rangle\neq 0
and
\pi_{i}>0
for all
i,j\in[\![1,M(\theta)]\!],\>i\neq j
. Up to a reordering of components, we can write
\pi_{i}
as
\pi_{i}^{\theta}:=\pi_{i}+\pi_{j}
whenever
\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\theta\rangle=0
for all
i,j\in[\![1,N]\!],\>i\neq j
. Following this construction, we obtain a probability measure
\nu^{\theta}:=\sum_{i=1}^{M(\theta)}\pi_{i}^{\theta}\delta_{\langle y_{i},\theta\rangle}
with no ties in its support points. Then, the Kantorovich duality formulation (
A.2
) leads to the following expressions of the sliced distances :

SW_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left[\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{M(\theta)}}{\max}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underset{i=1,\ldots,M(\theta)}{\min}\left\{|\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle|^{p}-w_{i}\right\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{M(\theta)}\pi_{i}w_{i}\right]d\sigma(\theta)

(A.5)

and

\text{max-}SW_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=\underset{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}{\max}\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{M(\theta)}}{\max}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underset{i=1,\ldots,M(\theta)}{\min}\left\{|\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle|^{p}-w_{i}\right\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{M(\theta)}\pi_{i}w_{i},

(A.6)

where
\sigma
denotes the uniform measure over the
d
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Note that actually, one can replace
M(\theta)
by
N
in the maximization over the variable
w
in (
A.5
), without taking into account any dependency on
\theta
. Indeed, let

M(\theta):=\mathrm{card}\left(\left\{i\in[\![1,N]\!]\,:\,\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\theta\rangle\neq 0,\>\forall j\neq i\right\}\right).

We thus have
M(\theta)<N
if and only if there exist indices
i\neq j
such that
\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\theta\rangle=0
, meaning that
\theta
lies in a union of sets of the form
\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\,:\,\langle y_{i}-y_{j},\theta\rangle=0\right\},
for
\>i,j\in[\![1,N]\!]
and
i\neq j
.
Since
Y\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\setminus D_{N}
this is a finite union of hyperplanes intersected with the sphere, which is
\sigma
-negligible. It follows that
M(\theta)=N
for almost every
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Appendix B
Boundedness of the dual variables

In this section, we consider a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R}),\>R>0
and a discrete measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
. We prove that the dual variables associated to the optimal transport problem
\mathbb{D}\left(\mu,\nu\right)
for the Wasserstein distances
\mathbb{D}=W_{p}^{p}
and the Sinkhorn divergences
\mathbb{D}=W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
are bounded.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
for some
R>0
and
\mathbb{D}=W_{p}^{p},\>\text{or}\>W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
. Then for any
(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\setminus D_{N})\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
, we have

W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=\underset{\mid w_{i}\mid\leq K_{1}}{\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i},

and

W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=\underset{\mid w_{i}\mid\leq K_{2}}{\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}}-\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\exp\left[\frac{w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right]\right)d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}-\epsilon

where
K_{1}=2pR^{p}
and
K_{2}=4p(2R)^{p}
.

Remark B.2
.

In all generality, the dual variables
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
are not bounded since adding a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
to
w
yields another valid solution. Therefore, to fix a dual solution, we set one coordinate of
w
to
0
and under this constraint the dual variable
w
remains bounded uniformly in
Y
and
\pi
.

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

In the following, we prove both cases independently.

Case
\mathbb{D}=W_{p}^{p}
.

Let
(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\setminus D_{N})\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
, then by Kantorovich duality (
A.2
) between
\mu
and the discrete measure
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
, we have

W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underset{i=1,\ldots,N}{\min}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}.

(B.1)

As discussed in Remark (
B.2
), we may select a choice of dual variables
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
optimal in (
B.1
) and verifying
w_{N}=0
. From
(
Santambrogio, 2015
, Proposition 5.11)
and
(
Santambrogio, 2015
, Remark 1.13)
that follows, we may also select a c-convave function
w^{c}
which verifies for all
i\in[\![1,N]\!]
:
w_{i}=(w^{c}(y_{i}))^{c}
. We then have

\displaystyle|w_{i}-w_{N}|

\displaystyle=|(w^{c}(y_{i}))^{c}-(w^{c}(y_{N}))^{c}|

\displaystyle=\left|\min_{x\in\supp(\mu)}\left\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w^{c}(x)\right\}-\min_{x\in\supp(\mu)}\left\{\|x-y_{N}\|^{p}-w^{c}(x)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}}|\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-\|x-y_{N}\|^{p}|

(B.2)

\displaystyle\leq pR^{p-1}\|z_{i}-y_{i}\|\leq 2pR^{p},

(B.3)

where inequalities (
B.2
) and (
B.3
) come from Lemmas
D.2
and
D.1
. Using that
w_{N}=0
, we get the result: the dual variables for the
p
-Wasserstein distances are bounded, and
|w_{i}|\leq 2pR^{p}
.

Case
\mathbb{D}=W_{\epsilon,p}^{p}
.

Let
(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\setminus D_{N})\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{N})
. Let also
u\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
be an optimal solution in the dual (
A.3
) formulation of the entropic
p
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
. By
(
Genevay et al., 2019
, Proposition 1)
,
u
is
\kappa
-Lipshitz, where
\kappa
is the Lipschitz constant of
(a,b)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{2}\mapsto\|a-b\|^{p}
. Then since
\supp(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{R}
, we have by Lemma
D.1
that
\kappa
is upper bounded by
p\sqrt{2}(2R)^{p-1}
.
Let
w
be a solution of (
A.4
) for which we can suppose
w_{N}=0
(Remark (
B.2
)). As in
Genevay et al. (2016)
, one can show with a first order condition on (
A.3
) that for all
y
a coordinate in
Y
:

w(y)=-\epsilon\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(x)-\|x-y\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)\right).

We thus have for all
i\neq N
:

\displaystyle|w(y_{i})-w(y_{N})|

\displaystyle=\left|\epsilon\log\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(x)-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(x)-\|x-y_{N}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\epsilon\log\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(\overline{x}_{i})-\|\overline{x}_{i}-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{u(\underline{x}_{N})-\|\underline{x}_{N}-y_{N}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)d\mu(x)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=|u(\overline{x}_{i})-\|\overline{x}_{i}-y_{i}\|^{p}-(u(\underline{x}_{N})-\|\underline{x}_{N}-y_{N}\|^{p})|

\displaystyle\leq|u(\overline{x}_{i})-u(\underline{x}_{N})|+|\ \|\overline{x}_{i}-y_{i}\|^{p}-\|\underline{x}_{N}-y_{N}\|^{p}|

\displaystyle\leq p\sqrt{2}(2R)^{p-1}\|\overline{x}_{i}-\underline{x}_{N}\|+\|\overline{x}_{i}-y_{i}\|^{p}+\|\underline{x}_{N}-y_{N}\|^{p}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\leq p\sqrt{2}(2R)^{p}+2(2R)^{p}\leq 4p(2R)^{p},

where
\overline{x}_{i}
and
\underline{x}_{N}
denote respectively a maximizer of
x\mapsto\exp\left(\frac{u(x)-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)
and a minimizer of
x\mapsto\exp\left(\frac{u(x)-\|x-y_{N}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)
over
\mathbb{B}_{R}
. Using that
w(y_{N})=0
then yields the result.

∎

Appendix C
Proofs of the main results

In this section, we prove the sample complexity of optimal transport barycenters with sparse support in Theorem
3.1
. For
\ell\in[\![1,L]\!]
, we denote
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\ell}
the empirical process
f\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\ell}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(X_{i}^{\ell})

for
n
i.i.d random variables
X_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,X_{n}^{\ell}
of law
\mu^{\ell}
. For any subset
\mathcal{F}
of a normed space
(\Theta,\|\cdot\|)
and
\tau>0
, we denote
\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|)
the
\tau
-covering number of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
, which is the minimum number of balls of radius
\tau
, with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
, needed to cover
\mathcal{F}
. We refer the reader to
(
Sen, 2018
, Definition 2.2)
for more details on the covering number. Finally, we also denote
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)}
the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
norm with respect to the measure
\mu
and
\overline{\text{B}_{d}(0,R)}
the
d
-dimensional closed ball of radius
R
.
The proofs for Wasserstein (Section
C.1
), Sliced Wasserstein (Section
C.2
) and Sinkhorn (Section
C.3
) barycenters all follow the same approach : we first derive the covering number for a class of functions associated to the dual formulation of the divergences
\mathbb{D}
, and then apply tools from empirical process theory to obtain a generalization error bound for the barycenters.

C.1
Wasserstein barycenter

Lemma C.1
.

Let

\mathcal{F}_{p}=\left\{\left\{f_{Y}^{w}:x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\mapsto\underset{i=1,...,N}{\min}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\right\},\>Y\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N},\>w\in\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,2pR^{p})}^{N}\right\},

(C.1)

then for all
\tau>0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
,

\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})\leq\left(1+\frac{4pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(1+\frac{8pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}
.
Also, the subset
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}
of functions of
\mathcal{F}_{p}
with rationals parameters
(Y,w)
, is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
(Y,w),\>(Z,v)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times[-2pR^{p},2pR^{p}]^{N}
. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}

\displaystyle|f_{Y}^{w}(x)-f_{Z,v}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\underset{i=1,...,N}{\min}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}-\underset{i=1,...,N}{\min}\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}-v_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{i=1,...,N}{\max}|\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}-(\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}-v_{i})|

(C.2)

\displaystyle\leq pR^{p-1}\underset{i=1,...,N}{\max}\|y_{i}-z_{i}\|+\underset{i=1,...,N}{\max}|w_{i}-v_{i}|,

(C.3)

where inequalities (
C.2
) and (
C.3
) are due to Lemmas
D.2
and
D.1
.
Let us denote
T=pR^{p-1}
, and let
\tau>0
. We then consider
z^{1},\ldots,z^{n(\tau)}
a
\frac{\tau}{2T}
-covering of
\mathbb{B}_{R}
and
v^{1},\ldots v^{m(\tau)}
a
\frac{\tau}{2}
-covering of
\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,2pR^{p})}
. For any vector of indices
i=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})\in[\![1,n(\tau)]\!]^{N}
and
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{N})\in[\![1,m(\tau)]\!]^{N}
, we define for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
the function
f_{i,k}(x)=\underset{j=1,...,N}{\min}\|x-z^{i_{j}}\|^{p}-v^{k_{j}}
. Let
f:=f_{Y}^{w}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
, then by (
C.3
) there exists
i,k
such that

|f(x)-f_{i,k}(x)|\leq pR^{p-1}\max_{j=1,...,N}\|y_{j}-z^{i_{j}}\|+\max_{j=1,...,N}|w_{j}-v^{k_{j}}|\leq T\frac{\tau}{2T}+\frac{\tau}{2}=\tau,

so that
\|f-f_{i,k}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leq\tau
.
Since there exists
n(\tau)^{N}\times m(\tau)^{N}
possibilities for such functions
f_{i,k}
, it follows from the covering construction that :

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})&\leq\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\tau}{2T},\overline{\text{B}_{d}(0,R)},\|\cdot\|\right)^{N}\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,2pR^{p})},\mid\cdot\mid\right)^{N}\\
&\leq\left(1+\frac{4RT}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(1+\frac{8pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}=\left(1+\frac{4pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(1+\frac{8pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}\end{split}

where the second inequality is due to
(
Massart, 2007
, Lemma 4.14)
.

Finally,
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}
by a continuity argument and inequality (
C.3
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
for
\mathbb{D}=W_{p}^{p}
.

Let
(Y,\pi)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}
and let
M\in[\![1,N]\!]
and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{\pi})\in(\mathbb{B}_{R}^{M}\setminus D_{M})\times\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{M})
be as in Proposition
A.1
.
For all
\ell\in[\![1,L]\!]
, let
X_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,X_{n}^{\ell}
be
n
i.i.d random variables of law
\mu^{\ell}
. Using Proposition
B.1
and denoting

we therefore have

\begin{split}&\text{Gen}_{n}(Y,\pi):=\left|\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu_{n}^{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)-\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\underset{\mid\tilde{w}_{i}\mid\leq K}{\underset{\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}{\max}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\underset{i=1,\ldots,M}{\min}\{\|X_{k}^{\ell}-\tilde{y_{i}}\|^{p}-\tilde{w}_{i}\}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underset{i=1,\ldots,M}{\min}\{\|x-\tilde{y}_{i}\|^{p}-\tilde{w}_{i}\}d\mu^{\ell}(x)\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\underset{w_{i}=w_{j}\>\text{if}\>y_{i}=y_{j}}{\underset{\mid w_{i}\mid\leq K}{\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\max}}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\underset{i=1,\ldots,N}{\min}\{\|X_{k}^{\ell}-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underset{i=1,\ldots,N}{\min}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu^{\ell}(x)\right|.\end{split}

where the inequality is due to Lemma
D.2
. The last equality is due to the fact that for all
Y\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\min_{k=1,\ldots,M}\|x-y_{k}\|^{p}-w_{k}=\min_{k=1,\ldots,N}\|x-y_{k}\|^{p}-w_{k}
if
w_{i}=w_{j}
whenever
y_{i}=y_{j}
.
Notice that the dependency in the vector of weights
\pi
has disappeared.
For any
(x,Y,w)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\times\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times[-K,K]^{N}
, let
f_{Y}^{w}(x)=\underset{i=1,\ldots,N}{\min}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}
. We then have

\begin{split}\underset{(Y,\pi)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}}{\sup}\text{Gen}_{n}(Y,\pi)&\leq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\underset{(Y,w)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times[-K,K]^{N}}{\sup}\left|\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\ell}\left(f_{Y}^{w}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu^{\ell}}\left[f_{Y}^{w}(X)\right]\right|\\

where
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is defined in (
C.1
).
Notice further that for any
\ell\in[\![1,L]\!]

\begin{split}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{p}}\mathbb{P}^{\ell}_{n}f^{2}&=\sup_{(Y,w)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times[-K,K]^{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\min_{i=1,...,N}\|X_{k}^{\ell}-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{R}}\|x-y\|^{p}+\sup_{w\in[-K,K]}|w|\right)^{2}\\
&\leq((2R)^{p}+2pR^{p})^{2}\leq(2(2R)^{p})^{2},\end{split}

which gives
\sqrt{\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{P}_{n}f^{2}}\leq 2(2R)^{p}
. By Lemma
C.1
,
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}
, and we can then apply
(
Giné and Nickl, 2021

\ell

C.1

\mu_{n}^{\ell}

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tilde{K}}\sqrt{\log(2\mathcal{N}(\tau/2,\mathcal{F}_{p},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu_{n}^{\ell})}))}d\tau&\leq\int_{0}^{\tilde{K}}\sqrt{\log\left(2\left[1+\frac{8pR^{p}}{\tau}\right]^{dN}\left[1+\frac{16pR^{p}}{\tau}\right]^{N}\right)}d\tau\\

Finally, as this last term does not depend on

where

.

C.2
Sliced Wasserstein barycenter

Lemma C.2
.

Let

\mathcal{F}_{p}^{s}=\left\{\left\{f_{\theta,Y,w}:x\mapsto\underset{i=1,...,N}{\min}|\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle|^{p}-w_{i}\right\},\>Y\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N},\>w\in\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,2pR^{p})}^{N},\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right\},

then for all
\tau>0
,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
,

\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p}^{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})\leq\left(1+\frac{12pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{d}\left(1+\frac{6pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(\frac{12pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}.

Also, the subset
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}^{\text{s}}
of functions of
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\text{s}}
with rationals parameters
(Y,w,\theta)
, is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\text{s}}
.

Proof.

Let
K=2pR^{p}
,
Y,Z\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}
,
w,v\in[-K,K]^{N}
and
\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then by Lemma (
D.1
), we have for
x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}

\begin{split}\left|\ |\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle|^{p}-|\langle x-z_{i},\varphi\rangle|^{p}\right|&\leq pR^{p-1}|\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle-\langle x-z_{i},\varphi\rangle|\\
&=pR^{p-1}|\langle x,\theta-\varphi\rangle+\langle z_{i},\varphi-\theta\rangle+\langle z_{i},\theta\rangle-\langle y_{i},\theta\rangle|\\
&\leq pR^{p-1}(2R\|\theta-\varphi\|+\|z_{i}-y_{i}\|).\end{split}

Therefore, thanks to Lemma (
D.2
), we get

\displaystyle|f_{\theta,Y,w}(x)-f_{\varphi,Z,v}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}\ \left|\ |\langle x-y_{i},\theta\rangle|^{p}-|\langle x-z_{i},\varphi\rangle|^{p}\right|+|w_{i}-v_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}\ pR^{p-1}(2R\|\theta-\varphi\|+\|z_{i}-y_{i}\|)+|w_{i}-v_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq 2pR^{p}\|\theta-\varphi\|+pR^{p-1}\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}\|z_{i}-y_{i}\|+\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}|w_{i}-v_{i}|.

(C.4)

Then, using the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
C.1
, we can upper bound the covering number
\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p}^{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})
by the following product of covering numbers:

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p}^{s},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})&\leq\mathcal{N}\left[\frac{\tau}{6pR^{p}},\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\|\cdot\|\right]\mathcal{N}\left[\frac{\tau}{3pR^{p-1}},\mathbb{B}_{R},\|\cdot\|\right]^{N}\mathcal{N}\left[\frac{\tau}{3},\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,2pR^{p})},|\cdot|\right]^{N}\\
&\leq\left(1+\frac{12pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{d}\left(1+\frac{6pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(\frac{12pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N},\\
\end{split}

which concludes the proof of the upper bound of the covering number. Finally,
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}^{s}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{s}
due to inequality (
C.4
).
∎

C.3
Entropy regularized barycenter

Lemma C.3
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon}=\left\{\left\{g_{Y,\pi,w}^{\epsilon}:x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\mapsto-\epsilon\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\exp\left(\frac{w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\middle|Y\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N},\;\pi\in\Delta_{N},\;w\in\overline{\text{B}_{1}\left(0,4p(2R)^{p}\right)}^{N},\right\}

then for all
\tau>0
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})
, we have

\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})\leq\left(1+\frac{4pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(\frac{16p(2R)^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}.

Also, the subset
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}^{\epsilon}
of functions of
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon}
with rational parameters
(Y,\pi,w)
, is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

Let
(Y,\pi,w),\>(Z,\gamma,v)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}\times[-4p(2R)^{p},4p(2R)^{p}]^{N}
. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
, we have

\begin{split}|g_{Y,\pi,w}^{\epsilon}(x)-g_{Z,\gamma,v}^{\epsilon}(x)|&=\epsilon\left|\log\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\exp\left(\frac{w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\exp\left(\frac{v_{i}-\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\epsilon\left|\log\left(\frac{\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}\exp\left(\frac{w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}}{\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\exp\left(\frac{v_{i}-\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}}{\epsilon}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}}\right)\right|\\
&=\left|\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}v_{i}-\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}\right|.\end{split}

Denoting
\overline{i}
(resp.
\underline{i}
) is an index for which the maximum (resp. minimum)
of
i\mapsto w_{i}-\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}
(resp.
i\mapsto v_{i}-\|x-z_{i}\|^{p}
) is attained, we get by Lemma
D.1
,

|g_{Y,\pi,w}(x)-g_{Z,\gamma,v}(x)|\leq|w_{\overline{i}}-v_{\underline{i}}|+pR^{p-1}\|y_{\overline{i}}-z_{\underline{i}}\|.

(C.5)

Notice that the right-hand side of the above inequality depends on neither
\pi
nor
\epsilon
. Let
T=pR^{p-1}
, with the same proof argument as of case
\mathbb{D}=W_{p}^{p}
and by boundedness of the entropic dual variables (Proposition
B.1
) we thus get

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}(\tau,\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mu)})&\leq\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\tau}{2T},\overline{\text{B}_{d}(0,R)},\|\cdot\|\right)^{N}\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\overline{\text{B}_{1}(0,4p(2R)^{p})},\mid\cdot\mid\right)^{N}\\
&\leq\left(1+\frac{4pR^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{dN}\left(\frac{16p(2R)^{p}}{\tau}\right)^{N}\\
\end{split}

which concludes the proof of the upper bound of the covering number. Finally,
\mathcal{F}_{p,\mathbb{Q}}^{\epsilon}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\epsilon}
due to inequality (
C.5
).
∎

Proof of Corollary

.

Let
(Y^{*},\pi^{*})
and
(Y^{n},\pi^{n})
be respective minimizers of

and
(Y,\pi)\mapsto\Bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu_{n}^{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)

A
. We denote
\nu^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{*}\delta_{y_{i}^{*}}
and
\nu^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}

\begin{split}&\bary_{\mathbb{D}}(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\nu^{n})-\bary_{\mathbb{D}}(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\nu^{*})\\

In the right-hand side of the inequality, we bound the first term by Theorem

while the second term can be bounded thanks to the proof of

Appendix D
Additional Lemmas

This section includes elementary, yet useful Lemmas for the proof of our results. Additionally, for completeness, we prove a well known fact about optimal quantization that is used in several instances of our article.

D.1
Technical lemmas

Lemma D.1
.

For
R>0
and
p\geq 1
, we have

(i)

the function
f:x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\mapsto\|x\|^{p}
is
pR^{p-1}
Lipschitz,

(ii)

the function
c:(x,y)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\times\mathbb{B}_{R}\mapsto\|x-y\|^{p}
is
\sqrt{2}p(2R)^{p-1}
Lipschitz.

Proof.

(i) The case
p=1
is just the result of triangular inequality. Second, by mean value theorem and the chain rule, one can show that for
p>1
,

\nabla f(x)=p\|x\|^{p-2}x

whose norm is upper bounded by
pR^{p-1}
.

(ii) Let
x,y,\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
. We first consider the case
p=1
:

\begin{split}\mid\|x-y\|-\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|\mid^{2}&=\mid\|x-y\|-\|x-y^{\prime}\|-(\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|-\|x-y^{\prime}\|)\mid^{2}\\
&\leq\mid\|x-y\|-\|x-y^{\prime}\|\mid^{2}+\mid\|x-y^{\prime}\|-\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|\mid^{2}\\
&\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|y-y^{\prime}\|^{2}\end{split}

by triangular inequality.

Fore the case
p>1
, we differentiate the functional
c:(x,y)\mapsto\|x-y\|^{p}
, that is

\begin{split}\partial_{x}c(x,y)&=\frac{p}{2}(\|x-y\|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\partial_{x}\|x-y\|^{2}\\
&=p\|x-y\|^{p-2}(x-y).\end{split}

It results that

\|\partial_{x}c(x,y)\|=p\|x-y\|^{p-1}

and therefore

\begin{split}\|\nabla c(x,y)\|^{2}&=\|\partial_{x}c(x,y)\|^{2}+\|\partial_{y}c(x,y)\|^{2}\\
&=2p^{2}\|x-y\|^{2p-2}.\end{split}

We then conclude by mean value theorem and the fact that
x,y,\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
.

∎

Lemma D.2
.

For any functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left|\sup_{x\in U}f(x)-\sup_{x\in U}g(x)\right|\leq\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|

and

\left|\inf_{x\in U}f(x)-\inf_{x\in U}g(x)\right|\leq\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|

Proof.

Let
x\in U
, then

f(x)-\sup_{t\in U}g(t)\leq f(x)-g(x)\leq\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|.

This being true for all
x\in U
means that

\sup_{x\in U}f(x)-\sup_{t\in U}g(x)\leq\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|.

By inverting the role of
f
and
g
we can then show the first result. As for the second result for all
\overline{x}\in U
we have

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|&\geq f(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x})\\
&\geq\inf_{x\in U}f(x)-g(\overline{x})\end{split}

This being true for all
\overline{x}\in U
means that

\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|\geq\inf_{x\in U}f(x)-\inf_{x\in U}g(x).

By inverting the role of
f
and
g
we can show the second result.

∎

D.2
Lower bound Lemma

In this subsection we prove that the worst-case lower bound given in
Bartlett et al. (2002)
also holds for Wasserstein sparse barycenters.

Lemma D.3
.

Let
\mathbb{D}=W_{2}^{2}
, the number of measures
L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and the dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Assume
N\geq 3
and
n\geq 3435N
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a random measure depending on
nL
random variables in
\mathbb{B}_{R}
. Then
there exists
\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the estimation error of Corollary
3.4
verifies

\mathbb{E}\left[\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right)-\min_{(Y,\pi)\in\mathbb{B}_{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}}\bary_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{L},\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)\right]\geq C_{d}\sqrt{\frac{N^{1-\frac{4}{d}}}{n}}

(D.1)

where
C_{d}
is an explicit constant which only depends on
d
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
L=1
.
By
(
Bartlett et al., 2002
, Theorem 1)
, there exists a measure

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and an optimal quantizer

(Y^{*},\pi^{*})\in\underset{(Y,\pi)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\times\Delta_{N}}{\argmin}\ W_{2}^{2}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)
such that

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right)-W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{*}\delta_{y_{i}^{*}}\right)\right]\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{N^{1-\frac{4}{d}}}{n}}.

Since almost surely

\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right)\leq W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right),

we deduce that

\mathbb{E}\!\left[W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{n}\delta_{y_{i}^{n}}\right)-W_{2}^{2}\!\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}^{*}\delta_{y_{i}^{*}}\right)\right]\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{N^{1-\frac{4}{d}}}{n}}.

The result for
L>1
in (
D.1
) then follows
by choosing
\mu^{1}=\cdots=\mu^{L}=\mu
.
∎

D.3
Optimal quantization

For completeness, we recall and prove this well known fact about optimal quantization.

Lemma D.4
.

Let
\mu
be an arbitrary measure and
Y\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\setminus D_{N},
then for all
p\geq 1

\underset{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}{\min}W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x).

Proof.

Consider the functional

(w,\pi)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\Delta_{N}\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}.

It is concave in
w
and convex in
\pi
, and we can therefore swap the min and the max in the following problem

\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}.

(D.2)

Notice that a first order condition with respect to
\pi
implies
w^{*}=0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
at optimality. Let
\pi^{*}\in\Delta_{N}
be a minimizer of (
D.2
). In order to use the duality expression (
A.2
), we consider
\tilde{\pi}\in\text{int}(\Delta_{M})
the vector of weights
\pi^{*}
whose null components have been removed. Following this construction and with a slight abuse of notation, we reorder the point cloud
Y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
so that the components
y_{i}
corresponding to null weights have indexes
i\in[\![M+1,N]\!]
.

\begin{split}\underset{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}{\min}W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)&=W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tilde{\pi}_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)\\
&=\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{M}}\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,M}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\pi_{i}w_{i}\\
&=\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{M}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,M}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\pi_{i}w_{i}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,M}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x).\end{split}

We can then consider two cases. First, suppose that
\mu
does not give mass to the Voronoï set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>|\>\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}\leq\|x-y_{j}\|^{p},\>j\in[\![1,N]\!]\}
for all
i\in[\![M+1,N]\!]
; this may be the case for example if
\mu
is discrete. Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,M}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x).

Second, suppose that there exists
i\in[\![M+1,N]\!]
such that
\mu
gives mass to a set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>|\>\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}\leq\|x-y_{j}\|^{p},\>j\in[\![1,N]\!]\}
. By contradiction, suppose that
M<N
. Because
Y\notin D_{N}
we then have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,M}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x)&>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}d\mu(x)\\
&=\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min_{i=1,\ldots,N}\{\|x-y_{i}\|^{p}-w_{i}\}d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}w_{i}\\
&=\underset{\pi\in\Delta_{N}}{\min}W_{p}^{p}\left(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}\right)\end{split}

which contradicts the optimality of the minimum in
\pi
. We thus get the result.
∎

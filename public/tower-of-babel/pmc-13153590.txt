Juxtapapillary retinal hemangioblastoma remission in a von Hippel-Lindau patient treated with cabozantinib
Authors: Mauro Sartore, Emilia Maggio, Alfonso Massimiliano Ferrara, Umberto Basso, Grazia Pertile
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo describe a unique case of von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease in which a treatment-resistant juxtapapillary retinal hemangioblastoma (RH) underwent complete and sustained regression following systemic therapy with cabozantinib, a VEGFR/MET inhibitor administered for clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC).ObservationsA 63-year-old woman with genetically confirmed VHL disease presented with a chronic, exudative juxtapapillary RH in the left eye, unresponsive to multiple prior therapies, including intravitreal anti-VEGF agents, corticosteroids, and vitreoretinal surgery. Despite temporary improvement after pars plana vitrectomy with epiretinal membrane peeling and focal laser photocoagulation, exudation recurred, and new RHs developed. In August 2022, the patient initiated cabozantinib for ccRCC. Within weeks, a marked reduction in juxtapapillary exudation was noted, followed by progressive and complete anatomical resolution. This response was sustained over a three-year follow-up period, during which no new RHs developed, and no ocular treatments were required.Conclusions and importanceThis case describes sustained remission of a juxtapapillary RH following cabozantinib therapy, with long-term stability of peripheral retinal findings and no new lesions development. The temporal correlation and absence of concurrent ocular interventions suggest a therapeutic role for cabozantinib in modulating disease activity at the retinal level. These findings support further investigation into systemic, pathway-targeted therapies as non-ablative treatment options for RHs, particularly in anatomically critical locations where conventional treatments are limited or contraindicated.Keywords: Cabozantinib, Clear cell renal cell carcinoma, Juxtapapillary tumor, Retinal hemangioblastoma, Systemic targeted therapy, Von Hippel-Lindau disease

Introduction

Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease is an autosomal dominant inherited disorder characterized by the development of benign and malignant neoplasms in multiple organ systems. Among its cardinal ocular manifestations are retinal hemangioblastomas (RHs), a benign, highly vascular neoplasm of the neurosensory retina, that can lead to vision loss from exudation, fibrosis, vitreous and subretinal hemorrhage, or retinal detachment.

While extrapapillary or extramacular RHs are typically managed with ablative modalities such as laser photocoagulation, cryotherapy, or other techniques depending on tumor size and associated complications (such as exudation, retinal detachment, fibrosis, or hemorrhage),1,2 tumors located near the optic disc (juxtapapillary) or involving the macula remain particularly challenging to treat. Their close proximity to critical visual structures significantly limits the use of ablative approaches and increases the risk of vision-threatening complications. Without treatment, these posteriorly located tumors carry a high risk of progressive vision loss. Current non-ablative approaches, including intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents or corticosteroids, or photodynamic therapy, have shown limited efficacy.2

A promising emerging therapy is represented by belzutifan, a systemic HIF-2α inhibitor recently approved for VHL-associated ccRCC, central nervous system hemangioblastomas, and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. Beyond its approved indications, preliminary clinical data have suggested a potential role for belzutifan in the treatment of retinal hemangioblastomas.3,4 In particular, its systemic, non-ablative mechanism of action offers a theoretical advantage in the management of RHs located in anatomically sensitive areas, such as the juxtapapillary or macular regions.

In this context, we report a case of a VHL patient with a refractory juxtapapillary RH that regressed following treatment with cabozantinib, a VEGFR/mesenchymal–epithelial transition factor (MET) inhibitor administered for ccRCC. This observation may offer novel insights into potential systemic approaches for managing challenging ocular manifestations of VHL disease.

Case report

A 63-year-old female affected by genetically confirmed VHL disease was referred to our hospital, after a long-standing clinical course, for evaluation of a juxtapapillary RH in the left eye, which had proven refractory to multiple treatments. Prior to referral, the lesion had been present for several years and was initially monitored without intervention. Progressive lesion enlargement and development of retinal exudation subsequently prompted treatment with intravitreal ranibizumab injections, which failed to produce any clinical benefit. Due to continued lesion growth and worsening exudation, two intravitreal injections of sustained-release dexamethasone (Ozurdex) were administered, followed by an additional intravitreal injection of ranibizumab; however, none of these interventions resulted in a reduction of exudation, which instead showed further progression. Over the years, the patient had also undergone cryotherapy and laser photocoagulation in both eyes for other RHs located in the peripheral and mid-peripheral retina, not involving the macula or the optic disc.

At the time of the first visit to our institution, the juxtapapillary RH was associated with significant intraretinal edema, subretinal fluid extending into the foveal region, a fibrotic epiretinal membrane, and a ring of hard exudates (Fig. 1). Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was 20/20 in the right eye and 20/200 in the left eye. Fluorescein angiography was performed (Fig. 2A), revealing additional extrapapillary RHs. Some had been previously treated, while others were untreated and appeared to be of recent onset.

Given the presence of an epiretinal membrane with a tractional component, which may have contributed to the worsening of exudation at the posterior pole, a surgical approach was chosen to relieve the traction. At our institution, the patient underwent surgical treatment in the left eye, consisting of a pars plana vitrectomy, peeling of the epiretinal fibrosis overlying the large juxtapapillary hemangioma, and photocoagulation of the afferent feeder vessel. Additional peripheral RHs located in the nasal, inferior, and superior retinal quadrants were also treated with intraoperative direct laser photocoagulation.

Postoperative examinations showed an initial improvement in exudation, with a reduction in both subretinal and intraretinal fluid, as well as a decrease in hard exudates. Central foveal thickness measured by SD-OCT progressively decreased from 932 μm preoperatively to 423 μm approximately one year after surgery. However, this improvement was not maintained over time. Starting 15 months after surgery, an increase in exudation was observed, along with the appearance of new peripheral and mid-peripheral RHs in both eyes. The condition continued to deteriorate progressively during the ensuing follow-up (Fig. 3). During this period, laser treatments were performed on newly detected peripheral RHs in both eyes, while no treatment was feasible for the juxtapapillary hemangioma, despite worsening exudation.

Unexpectedly, the retinal condition changed markedly approximately three years after referral to our institution, when the patient began treatment with cabozantinib for ccRCC. The patient's renal involvement had actually begun several years earlier with the identification of renal hemangiomas, which were surgically treated in both kidneys. This was followed by right nephrectomy and subsequent diagnosis of carcinoma in the left kidney, for which systemic treatment with sunitinib was initiated, resulting in a good and durable renal response. After a prolonged period of disease control, following oncological reassessment, sunitinib therapy was discontinued and cabozantinib (CABOMETYX) was initiated at a dose of 40 mg once daily. Due to suboptimal tolerability, manifested by nausea, loss of appetite, and weight loss, the dosage was subsequently reduced to 20 mg once daily. The reduced dose was well tolerated and maintained without clinically significant adverse effects. Approximately one year later, the dosing regimen was further modified to 20 mg once daily for five consecutive days per week, followed by a two-day drug-free interval.

A marked reduction in the exudation associated with the juxtapapillary RH was noted at the first ophthalmologic evaluation following cabozantinib initiation, performed two months after the start of therapy. During the subsequent follow-up, the exudation associated with the juxtapapillary RH progressively resolved completely and remained inactive for more than three years (Fig. 4). Additional examinations performed during the follow-up, including fluorescein angiography performed at six months and two years after treatment initiation (Fig. 2B and C), confirmed sustained regression of the juxtapapillary lesion, with absence of active leakage and no evidence of new retinal hemangioblastomas. Importantly, hemangioblastomas previously identified and treated in the peripheral and mid-peripheral retina of both eyes remained stable, no additional RHs developed, and no further ocular treatments were required.

Discussion

Retinal hemangioblastomas are one of the most characteristic and vision-threatening ocular manifestations of VHL disease. Management of RHs, particularly those located in the juxtapapillary region, remains a significant therapeutic challenge. Their close proximity to the optic nerve and macula limits the use of ablative treatments, while alternative non-ablative approaches have demonstrated variable and often suboptimal efficacy.1,2

In recent years, the advent of systemic therapies targeting VHL-related molecular pathways has opened new avenues for treating VHL-associated tumors, including RHs. Belzutifan, a selective HIF-2α inhibitor, has shown promising activity in clinical trials, not only for ccRCC and central nervous system hemangioblastomas, but also in preliminary reports involving RHs, offering a non-invasive, targeted strategy for these anatomically complex lesions.3,4 In this context, our case provides new and clinically relevant insights into the systemic management of refractory juxtapapillary RHs.

In this patient with genetically confirmed VHL disease and a history of multiple ocular and systemic manifestations, long-standing exudation from a refractory juxtapapillary RH resolved completely after initiation of ccRCC treatment with cabozantinib, a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor with activity against VEGFR, MET, and AXL receptor tyrosine kinase (AXL). This anatomical response was sustained over a three-year follow-up period, with no recurrence or development of new RHs.

This observation aligns with the current understanding of the molecular pathogenesis of VHL-associated neoplasms. VHL protein is a critical component of the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that targets hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs) for proteasomal degradation under normoxic conditions. Loss of functional VHL protein leads to constitutive activation of HIF-1α and HIF-2α, resulting in upregulation of downstream targets such as VEGF, PDGF, and MET, which contribute to neovascularization and tumor proliferation.5 By inhibiting VEGFR and MET signaling pathways, cabozantinib may exert both anti-angiogenic and anti-proliferative effects on RHs, which are highly vascularized lesions driven by aberrant hypoxia signaling.

While anti-VEGF agents administered intravitreally have demonstrated some efficacy in reducing exudation in selected cases of RHs 6,7, their effect is often temporary and limited by the lesion's location and the complexity of VHL-driven tumor biology. Similarly, corticosteroids and photodynamic therapy have shown inconsistent outcomes.8

In our patient, multiple intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF and corticosteroid agents had previously failed. Although a temporary reduction in exudation was achieved following pars plana vitrectomy with epiretinal membrane peeling and focal photocoagulation, the lesion demonstrated progressive reactivation over time, with increasing exudation and new RHs developing elsewhere in the retina. This clinical course underscores the aggressive nature and therapeutic resistance often observed in posterior RHs associated with VHL.

Notably, only after the introduction of cabozantinib for ccRCC was a marked and sustained regression of juxtapapillary exudation observed, without the need for further ocular interventions. This improvement was maintained over three years, despite dose reduction and intermittent dosing schedules. Furthermore, no new RHs developed during this time, suggesting a possible stabilizing effect on retinal angiogenic activity.

While the temporal correlation does not establish causation, the sequence of events and the absence of other confounding ocular treatments support the hypothesis that cabozantinib may have played a role in modulating disease activity at the retinal level.

This report adds to the growing body of evidence supporting systemic, pathway-targeted therapies for VHL-associated RHs and suggests a potential role for cabozantinib in selected patients, particularly those with concurrent systemic indications.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this report. As a single case, it does not allow for definitive conclusions regarding efficacy or generalizability. Moreover, the patient had received other systemic therapies prior to cabozantinib, although none were temporally associated with retinal improvement. The gradual dose reduction of cabozantinib over time, with maintenance of ocular response, may suggest a dose-independent or threshold-mediated mechanism of action.

Further studies and prospective clinical trials will be essential to validate these findings and to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib in the treatment of RHs, especially in anatomically critical locations where local therapies are limited or contraindicated.

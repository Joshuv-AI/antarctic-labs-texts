Operator realizations about a matrix-centre
Ali Karoobi, Robert T.W. Martin, Maximilian Tornes

Abstract
We develop a general theory of operator realizations, or “linear representations” of analytic functions in several non-commuting variables about a matrix–centre. In particular we show that a non-commutative function has a matrix-centre realization about any matrix tuple, Y Y , in its domain, if and only if it is a uniformly analytic non-commutative function defined in a uniformly open neighbourhood of Y Y . This extends the finite–dimensional realization theory of non-commutative rational functions maximally – to all uniformly analytic non-commutative functions.

A univariate

is

As for analytic functions of a single complex variable, an elementary argument shows that an NC function,

, is uniformly analytic in an open neighbourhood of

so that the above realization formula, Equation (

h(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n}\otimes b^{*}(X\otimes A)^{n}I_{n}\otimes c=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}b^{*}A^{\omega}c\,X^{\omega}.

(1.2)

(In particular,

However, a uniformly analytic NC function has a realization if and only if its uniformly open NC domain includes the origin,
0:=(0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\subset\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}

, and the domain of a uniformly analytic NC function is a uniformly open NC set.) The set of all NC rational functions is the skew field,
\mathbb{C}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,:=\mathbb{C}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{z}_{d}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,

\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle:=\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{z}_{d}\rangle
, of all NC or ‘free’ polynomials in the

\mathfrak{z}:=(\mathfrak{z}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{z}_{d})

In order to motivate and describe matrix-centre realizations, consider that, even in one variable, an analytic function,

, has a realization,
(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
, if and only if it is analytic in an open neighbourhood of

. The coefficients of the Taylor series of

at

, as described above. In this sense, the realization is ‘centred at

’. More generally, if

, we say that

h(z)=b^{{}^{\prime}*}(I-(z-y)A^{\prime})^{-1}c^{\prime},

for all

in an open neighbourhood of
y
. In particular, if
|z-y|<\|A^{\prime}\|^{-1}
, then this realization formula can be expanded as a convergent geometric series, so that

h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime}n}c^{\prime}\,(z-y)^{n},

is the Taylor series of

at
y
. If

and
L_{A}(y)=I-yA
is invertible, then one can ‘translate’ the realization
(A,b,c)
to the point

to obtain

. Namely, assuming that

is invertible,

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}(I-zA)^{-1}c=b^{*}(I-yA-(z-y)A)^{-1}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}(I-(z-y)L_{A}(y)^{-1}A)^{-1}L_{A}(y)^{-1}c,

so that

with

A^{\prime}=(I-yA)^{-1}A,\quad b^{\prime}=b,\quad\mbox{and}\quad c^{\prime}=(I-yA)^{-1}c.

Similarly, if
h\sim_{0}(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
is the realization of a uniformly analytic NC function in a uniformly open neighbourhood of

\displaystyle h(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{mn}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(Z)^{-1}I_{mn}\otimes c

\displaystyle I_{mn}\otimes b^{*}\left(L_{A}(I_{m}\otimes Y)-(Z-I_{m}\otimes Y)\otimes A\right)^{-1}I_{mn}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes(I_{n}\otimes b^{*})\left(I_{mn}-(I_{m}\otimes L_{A}(Y)^{-1})((Z-I_{m}\otimes Y)\otimes A)\right)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes L_{A}(Y)^{-1}(I_{n}\otimes c).

Hence, we can define

If we define the

\displaystyle\quad\quad X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d},

\displaystyle=:I_{mn}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-\hat{A}(X);

\displaystyle\quad\quad X_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{j}^{(1,1)}&\cdots&X_{j}^{(1,m)}\\

X_{j}^{(m,1)}&\cdots&X_{j}^{(m,m)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn},

1.1
Outline

,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
can be “translated” to any point
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
for which
L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
is invertible to obtain a realization at
X
,
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
which is minimal if and only if
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is minimal by Theorem
5.2
. In Section
6
, following the work of Porat and Vinnikov in
[
PV23
]
for the finite-dimensional matrix-centre realizations of NC rational functions, we express the
Lost Abbey conditions at
Y
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, in terms of a given matrix-centre realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
. These are set of conditions that determine when the quantized transfer function,
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
of a matrix-centre realization respects joint similarities, and hence is an NC function. In Section
8
, we introduce a matricial Fock space and a “free Hardy space at
Y
” and use these constructions to prove that an NC function is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
if and only if it has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
, see Corollary

. Finally in Section

, we develop an application by constructing matrix-centre realizations for elements in the skew fields of NC functions generated by realizations at
0
belonging to certain classes of linear operators.

2
Background

2.1
The NC universe

Consider the
d-
dimensional complex column NC universe,
\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}:=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, where

\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}:=\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
, denotes column
d-
tuples of complex
n\times n
matrices. That is, any
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
has the form
Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Z_{1}\\
\vdots\\
Z_{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, with
Z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

The NC universe,
\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
, can be viewed as an abstract operator space, namely the complex
column operator space
with
d
components
[
Pau02
]
. Recall that a concrete operator space is a subspace of the Banach space of bounded linear operators on some complex Hilbert space. Further recall that an abstract operator space is a vector space,
V
, equipped with a system of matrix–norms,
\|\cdot\|_{m,n}
, on
V^{m\times n}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying Ruan’s axioms:
Given
A\in\mathbb{C}^{j\times k}
,
B\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times m}
and
X\in V^{k\times\ell}
,
Y\in V^{a\times b}
, then

\|AXB\|_{j,m}\leq\|A\|\|X\|_{k,\ell}\|B\|,

(2.1)

and

\|X\oplus Y\|_{k+a,\ell+b}=\max\{\|X\|_{k,\ell},\|Y\|_{a,b}\}.

(2.2)

A vector space with a system of matrix norms satisfying Equation (
2.1
) is called a matrix–normed space, and Ruan’s theorem asserts that a matrix–normed space is completely isometrically isomorphic to a concrete operator space if and only if it is an abstract operator space,
i.e.
a matrix–normed space obeying Equation (
2.2
).

If
\mathscr{M}
is any operator space, it is clear that
\mathscr{M}^{m\times n}
is also an operator space for any fixed
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathscr{M}^{d}
is called the
column operator space
(with
d
components) over
\mathscr{M}
. Namely, a system of matrix norms obeying Ruan’s axioms for
(\mathscr{M}^{d})^{m\times n}
is naturally defined by identifying
(\mathscr{M}^{d})^{m\times n}\simeq(\mathscr{M}^{m\times n})^{d}\simeq\mathscr{M}^{md\times n}
,

\|X\|_{(\mathscr{M}^{d})^{m\times n}}:=\|X\|_{\mathscr{M}^{md\times n}}.

It follows that if we view elements of
\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
as columns,
X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_{1}\\
\vdots\\
X_{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, with
X_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we can equip this with the system of ‘column’ matrix norms,

\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}:=\|X\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d})}=\|X\|_{\mathbb{C}^{nd\times n}},

and thus identify
\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
with the
d-
component column–operator space over (the operator space)
\mathbb{C}
. (Alternatively, we could work with any other operator space structure on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, such as the system of ‘row’ matrix norms on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and view the
d-
dimensional complex universe as
\mathbb{C}^{d}
equipped with this operator space structure
[
SS25
]

[
KVV14
, Section 7.2]
.)

A subset,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
is called a
non-commutative (NC) set
if it is closed under direct sums, in which case we write

\Omega=\bigsqcup\Omega_{n};\quad\quad\Omega_{n}:=\Omega\cap\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}.

It will be useful to equip the NC universe with a natural topology, the
uniform topology
, generated by a basis of NC sets. First, the
column pseudo-metric
is defined as

d_{\mathrm{col}}(X,Y):=\|X^{\oplus m}-Y^{\oplus n}\|_{\mathrm{col}};\quad\quad X\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d},\ Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(m\times m)\cdot d}.

The uniform topology is then the topology on
\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
generated by the basis (or sub-base) of open sets,
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
, where
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
,
r>0
are arbitrary and

r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y):=\bigsqcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\{\left.X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}\right|\ d_{\mathrm{col}}(X,Y)<r\right\},

[
KVV14
, Section 7.2]
. Here, recall that as described in the introduction, an NC function defined on a uniformly open NC set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
,
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
is locally bounded in the uniform topology if and only if it is continuous with respect to the uniform topologies on
\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
and
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
, if and only if it is uniformly analytic
[
KVV14
, Corollary 7.26 and Corollary 7.28]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A (descriptor) realization about the matrix-centre,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, is a triple
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, where

A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d}):\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})

is a row
d-
tuple of linear maps,
A_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and
b,c\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathcal{H})
.

By
[
Pau07
, Theorem 18]
, since each
A_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a linear map on the finite–dimensional operator space
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, each
A_{j}
is automatically completely bounded with
\|A_{j}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}\leq n\sqrt{n}\|A_{j}\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}))}
. Hence we can view
A
as a completely bounded linear map from the column operator space (with
d
components) over
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
into
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
A\in\mathrm{CB}\left(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d},\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
and we define

\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}:=\|(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})\|_{\mathrm{CB}}.

(We emphasize the fact that
A
is completely bounded, as if
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is an NC function then the completely bounded norm of
A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})
is related to the radius of convergence for the Taylor–Taylor series expansion of
f
at
Y
, when we equip the NC universe with the column operator–space structure, see Subsection
2.2
and Remark
2.6
.)

Given such a realization, we define the linear pencil of
A\in\mathrm{CB}(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d},\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}))
on the
d-
dimensional column NC universe over
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,

\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}n\times\mathbb{N}n)\cdot d}:=\bigsqcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d},

X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}\ \mapsto\ L_{A}(X):=I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{j})(X_{j})=:I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-A(X).

(2.3)

The
invertibility domain
of the linear pencil,
L_{A}(X)
(relative to
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
), is then

\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A):=\bigsqcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A);\quad\quad\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A):=\{X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}|\ L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}\ \exists\}.

(2.4)

It is easy to see that
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
is always a uniformly open NC set, since the inversion map in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is operator–norm continuous and the set of invertible elements in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is operator–norm open. It is not obvious, however, whether or not the invertibility domain is joint similarity invariant, in contrast to the case where
Y=0\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
(or when
Y=y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
is any scalar tuple).

Any matrix–centre realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, defines a
quantized transfer function
,
f
, on the uniformly open NC set,
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
. As we will see, unlike the standard case where
Y=0
(or any scalar point), the quantized transfer function of a matrix–centre realization at
Y
is not necessarily an NC function. Namely, while such a quantized transfer function is always graded and respects direct sums on the uniformly open NC set,
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
, it respects joint similarities if and only if
(A,b,c)_{Y}
obeys the
Lost–Abbey conditions at
Y
, LAC (Y), also called the
canonical intertwining condtions
at
Y
; see Section
6
and
[
PV21
,
PV23
,
KVV14
]
.

Given
(A,b,c)_{Y}
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, its quantized transfer function is defined as follows. If
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
,

f(X):=I_{m}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn},

and we write
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
or
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
in this case.

Definition 2.2
.

Any two matrix-centre realizations,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
are said to be
analytically equivalent at
Y
, written
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
or
(A,b,c)\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, if they define the same quantized transfer function on a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y
.

In general, even if
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
,
g\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
and
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
, it may be that
f(X)\neq g(X)
for some
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A^{\prime})
, as the following example (with
Y=0
,
d=1
) shows.

Example 2.3
.

Let
U
be the bilateral shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and consider the realization
(U,e_{0},e_{0})
. Then,
\sigma(U)=\partial\mathbb{D}
, so that the resolvent set of
U
,
\sigma(U)^{c}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(U)
, is disconnected. Hence
\mathscr{D}_{1}(U)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial\mathbb{D}
is also disconnected. Then, for
|z|<1
,

e_{0}^{*}(I-zU)^{-1}e_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e_{0}^{*}U^{j}e_{0}z^{j}=1,

while for
|z|>1
,

\displaystyle e_{0}^{*}(I-zU)^{-1}e_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-z^{-1}e_{0}^{*}U^{*}(I-z^{-1}U^{*})^{-1}e_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{-j-1}e_{0}^{*}U^{*(j+1)}e_{0}=0.

That is, the realization
(U,e_{0},e_{0})\sim f
defines the analytic function which is
1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
0
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. On the other hand, the one-dimensional realization
(0,1,1)
defines the constant function
1
everywhere. This shows that realizations do not need to evaluate to the same analytic function on the intersection of their invertibility domains, in general.

Remark 2.4
.

Later, we will define a realization
(A,b,c)
to be
minimal
, if
c
is
A-
cyclic and
b
is
A^{*}-
cyclic. (A minimal realization is, in some sense as ‘small’ as possible.) In the above example,
(U,e_{0},e_{0})
is a non-minimal realization, but
(0,1,1)
is minimal. We do not know whether one can construct an example of two minimal and analytically equivalent realizations,
(A,b,c)\sim_{0}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, with disconnected invertibility domains, so that they define different analytic functions on a connected component disconnected from
0
.

Remark 2.5
.

In general, if
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
g\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
and
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
, then
f(X)=g(X)
for all
X\in\mathscr{D}^{\,\leadsto\!Y}_{n}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{\,\leadsto\!Y}_{n}(A^{\prime})
where
\mathscr{D}^{\,\leadsto\!Y}_{n}(A)
denotes the path-connected component of
\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{n}(A)
containing
Y
. This follows readily from the identity theorem in several complex variables. However, it is not clear that ‘global evaluation’ is transitive, even in the univariate setting, and even if
Y=y\in\mathbb{C}
.

Observe that if
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is given by a matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, that for any
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
,

\displaystyle\|A(X)\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{j})(X_{j})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{d})\circ\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\\
\vdots\\
X_{d}\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})\|_{\mathrm{CB}}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\\
\vdots\\
X_{d}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{mn},\mathbb{C}^{mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d})}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}.

It follows that if
X\in r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
, so that
\|X-I_{m}\otimes Y\|_{\mathrm{col}}<r
, where
r:=\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}
, then
\|A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\|<1
is strictly contractive so that
L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}
can be expanded as an operator–norm convergent geometric sum. That is, for such
X
,

\displaystyle f(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A)\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{\ell}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}I_{m}\otimes c,

where, if
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{k}
, then we have defined

\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}:=(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{i_{1}})(X_{i_{1}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{i_{1}})\cdots(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{i_{k}})(X_{i_{k}}-I_{n}\otimes Y_{i_{k}}).

(2.6)

2.2
Realizations and Taylor–Taylor series

If
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
is a uniformly open NC set containing
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, and
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly analytic on
\Omega
, then consider the multi-linear maps,

\hat{f}_{\ell}(Y)[(\cdot),\cdots,(\cdot)]:(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d})^{\times^{\ell}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, where
(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d})^{\times^{\ell}}=\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\times\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\cdots\times\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
denotes the
\ell-
fold Cartesian product of
\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
with itself, defined by

\hat{f}_{\ell}(Y)[H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)}]:=\frac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{H^{(\ell)}}\cdots\partial_{H^{(1)}}f(Y),

where
\partial_{H}f(Y)
denotes that Gâteaux derivative of
f
at
Y\in\Omega_{n}=\mathrm{Dom}_{n}\,f
in the direction
H\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. Then the Taylor–Taylor series of
f
at
Y
, defined for
X\in\mathrm{Dom}\,_{mn}\,f
by

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{(j)}_{X-I_{m}\otimes Y}f(I_{m}\otimes Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}_{j}(Y)[X-I_{m}\otimes Y];

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{j}(Y)[H]=\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{(j)}_{H}f(Y):=\frac{1}{j!}\underbrace{\partial_{H}\cdots\partial_{H}}_{j\times}f(Y)=\hat{f}_{j}(Y)[H,\cdots,H],

converges absolutely and uniformly to
f(X)
on a uniformly open column-ball,
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{m\mathbb{N}}(Y)
, of non-zero radius,
r>0

[
KVV14
, Theorem 8.11]
. Namely, as in
[
KVV14
, Chapter 8]
, one can define the
radius of convergence
,
R_{f}^{Y}
, of this Taylor–Taylor series centred at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
by the Cauchy–Hadamard type quantity,

\frac{1}{R_{f}^{Y}}:=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\|\hat{f}_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}},

where
\|\hat{f}_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}
is the completely bounded norm of the multi-linear map,
\hat{f}_{\ell}
, in the sense of Christensen and Sinclair, see
[
KVV14
, p. 116]
or
[
Pau02
, Chapter 17]
. Namely, each
\hat{f}_{\ell}
is viewed as a completely bounded
\ell-
linear map on the column operator space over
E:=\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}=\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
into the operator space
F:=\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
To explain what this means, we first need to recall the definiton of the
Haagerup tensor norm
; see
[
Pau02
, Chapter 17]
.
The Haagerup tensor norm
\|\cdot\|_{h}
on
E\otimes E
is defined as follows. (Here,
E
can be any operator space.) First, given
A\in E^{i\times j}
and
B\in E^{j\times k}
, their Haagerup tensor product is

A\odot B:=\left(\sum_{\gamma=1}^{j}A_{\alpha,\gamma}\otimes B_{\gamma,\beta}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\alpha\leq i\\
1\leq\beta\leq k\end{subarray}}\in(E\otimes E)^{i\times k},

and for
C\in(E\otimes E)^{i\times k}
, its Haagerup tensor norm is

\|C\|_{h}:=\inf\left\{\left.\|A\|_{E}\|B\|_{E}\right|\ A\in E^{i\times j},\ B\in E^{j\times k},\ A\odot B=C\right\}.

The vector space,
E\otimes E
, equipped with the system of matrix norms,
\|\cdot\|_{h}
, will then be an operator space, which is usually denoted by
E\otimes_{h}E
.
More generally,
E^{\otimes^{\ell}}=\left(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\right)^{\otimes^{\ell}}
, equipped with the Haagerup tensor norm is an operator space. Here, given
C\in(E^{\otimes^{\ell}})^{i\times k}
, setting
m_{0}:=i
and
m_{\ell}:=k
,

\|C\|_{h}=\inf\left\{\left.\|A^{(1)}\|_{E}\cdots\|A^{(\ell)}\|_{E}\right|\ A^{(s)}\in E^{m_{s-1}\times m_{s}},1\leq s\leq\ell,\ A^{(1)}\odot A^{(2)}\cdots\odot A^{(\ell)}=C\right\}.

Next, consider the linear map

\displaystyle f^{(\ell)}:\underbrace{E\otimes_{h}\cdots\otimes_{h}E}_{\ell\times}\rightarrow F,

which is given by

f^{(\ell)}(H^{(1)}\otimes H^{(2)}\cdots\otimes H^{(\ell)}):=\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},H^{(2)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)});\quad\quad H^{(i)}\in E=\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}.

(2.7)

We then say that
\hat{f}_{\ell}
is a completely bounded
\ell-
linear map if
f^{(\ell)}
is completely bounded, and one sets
\|\hat{f}_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}:=\|f^{(\ell)}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}
. (Here, note that by a similar argument to
[
Pau02
, Theorem 18]
, any
\ell-
linear map on the
\ell-
fold Cartesian product of
\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
with itself is automatically completely bounded.) In this way one obtains a one-to-one correspondence between completely bounded linear and completely bounded multi-linear maps, where corresponding maps are often treated as one and the same. In particular, whenever
H^{(1)},\dots,H^{(\ell)}\in E^{m\times m}
, we can extend the definition of the multi-linear map,
\hat{f}_{\ell}
, from tuples of matrices in
E=\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
to such tuples of matrices over
E
via

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\dots,H^{(\ell)}):=(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes f^{(\ell)})\circ(H^{(1)}\odot\cdots\odot H^{(\ell)})\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn};\quad\quad H^{(i)}\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}.

As shown in
[
KVV14
, Chapter 4]
, if
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
,

\partial^{(\ell)}_{X-I_{m}\otimes Y}f(I_{m}\otimes Y)=\left(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\partial^{(\ell)}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\right)\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}}=:\partial^{(\ell)}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}},

where
\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}}
denotes the
\ell-
fold Haagerup tensor product of
X-I_{m}\otimes Y
with itself. This means that
\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}}
is viewed as an
m\times m
block matrix where each block belongs to
(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d})^{\otimes^{\ell}}
, and the
\ell-
linear map,
\partial^{(\ell)}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)
is applied to each block.

By
[
KVV14
, Corollary 7.26 and Theorem 8.11(5)]
, if the Taylor–Taylor series for an NC function,
f
, at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
has radius of convergence,
R_{f}^{Y}>0
, then this series converges absolutely and uniformly to
f(X)
on
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
, for any
r<R_{f}^{Y}
, and it fails to converge uniformly on every ball
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
for
r>R_{f}^{Y}
. In particular,
f
is uniformly analytic in
R_{f}^{Y}\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
.

Here, by absolute convergence of the Taylor–Taylor series of
f(X)
about
Y
to
f(X)
, where
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
, we mean that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|\hat{f}_{\ell}(Y)\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}}<+\infty,

and by absolute and uniform convergence in the uniform column-ball
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
we mean that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{mn}(Y);\\
m\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\|\hat{f}_{\ell}(Y)\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}}<+\infty.

The Taylor–Taylor series of a uniformly analytic
f
at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
can be expanded further to a series indexed by the free monoid
[
KVV14
, Chapter 4]
. Namely, one can define certain
partial difference–differential operators
acting on
f
,

\Delta^{\omega}_{H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|}}f(X^{(0)},\cdots,X^{(|\omega|)});\quad\quad H_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},X^{(j)}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

see
[
KVV14
, Section 3.5]
. Each partial difference–differential of
f
,
\Delta^{\omega}_{H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|}}f(X^{(0)},\cdots,X^{(|\omega|)})
, is
|\omega|-
linear in the directional arguments,
H_{i}
, for fixed
X^{(j)}
.

It then follows that the Taylor–Taylor series of
f(X)
at
Y
can be expanded further as

f(X)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\Delta^{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\omega}},

where the short-form notation,
\Delta^{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)
, means that
Y
is repeated
|\omega|+1-
times, and

\displaystyle\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\omega}}:=(X_{i_{1}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{i_{1}})\odot\cdots\odot(X_{i_{|\omega|}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{i_{|\omega|}}).

It can be shown that each
\Delta^{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\circ H^{\odot^{\omega}}
, is a ‘generalized monomial’ in
H=(H_{1},\cdots,H_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
of the form

\Delta^{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\circ H^{\odot^{\omega}}=\sum_{j_{0},\dots,j_{|\omega|}}T_{j_{0}}H_{i_{1}}T_{j_{1}}H_{i_{2}}\cdots H_{i_{|\omega|}}T_{j_{|\omega|}};\quad\quad\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|},

where each
T_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
belongs to the double centralizer of the point
Y
; see
[
KŠ17
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
KVV14
, Arguments following Remark 4.5]
.

Now suppose that
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is a quantized transfer function that has a matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. Then, by Equation (
2.5
), for any
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)
with
\|X-I_{m}\otimes Y\|_{\mathrm{col}}<\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}
,

f(X)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}I_{m}\otimes c,

where if
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}
,

\displaystyle\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}=(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{i_{1}})(X_{i_{1}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{i_{1}})\cdots(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{i_{k}})(X_{i_{|\omega|}}-I_{n}\otimes Y_{i_{|\omega|}}),

as defined in (
2.6
).

For simplicity of notation, we define the
|\omega|-
multilinear maps,
A^{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

A^{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|})=A_{i_{1}}(H_{1})\cdots A_{i_{|\omega|}}(H_{|\omega|});\quad\quad H_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}.

Next, a straightforward calculation (similar to the one on
[
KVV14
, p. 65]
) shows that

\displaystyle\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}=(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A^{\omega})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}.

It follows that
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
can be written as a power series of multi-linear maps as

\displaystyle f(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}\left((\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes\hat{A})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)\right)^{\omega}I_{m}\otimes c

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A^{\omega})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}I_{m}\otimes c.

Hence, if
f
is a uniformly analytic NC function, then by uniqueness of Taylor–Taylor series coefficients
[
KVV14
, Theorem 7.9]
,
[
PV21
, Lemma 2.12]
,

\Delta^{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}_{(\cdot)}f(Y)\circ H^{\odot^{\omega}}=b^{*}A_{i_{1}}(H_{i_{1}})\cdots A_{i_{|\omega|}}(H_{i_{|\omega|}})c;\quad\quad H_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

(2.9)

for
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}
. In particular, it follows that
\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB};\mathrm{row}}^{-1}<R_{f}^{Y}
by
[
KVV14
, Corollary 7.26 and Theorem 8.11(5)]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Again, this is one reason why we view the linear maps
A_{j}
as completely bounded linear maps; the completely bounded norm of
A
in a matrix-centre realization
(A,b,c)_{Y}
of an NC function,
f
, is related to the radius of convergence of the Taylor–Taylor series of
f
at
Y
since
this radius of convergence is

\frac{1}{R_{f}^{Y}}=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\|\hat{f}_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}},

where

\hat{f}_{\ell}=\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}b^{*}A^{\omega}(\cdot)c.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
be two realizations about the matrix centre
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. Then
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
are analytically equivalent at
Y
if and only if

b^{*}A^{\omega}(G)c=b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}(G)c^{\prime},

for all
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and for all
G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}
.

Proof.

If the quantized transfer functions of
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
are NC functions, this follows immediately from the uniqueness of Taylor–Taylor series coefficients of NC functions,
[
KVV14
, Theorem 7.9]
,
[
PV21
, Lemma 2.12]
and Equation (
2.9
).

In the general case, if
b^{*}A^{\omega}(G)c=b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}(G)c^{\prime}
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and all
G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}
, then this clearly implies that
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
since all the terms of their convergent power series expansions in the uniform row-ball
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
,
r:=\min\{\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1},\|A^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}\}
are the same.

Conversely if
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
,
G=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{\ell})
and
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell}
is an arbitrary word, we define a point
X(\omega)\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{\ell+1}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{\ell+1}(A^{\prime})
as follows. (The following construction is taken from
[
AMS25
, Section 4.2]
.) Let
E_{j,k}
be the standard matrix units for
\mathbb{C}^{(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)}
and set
T_{\ell}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}E_{j,j+1}
,

T_{\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&1\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)}.

We will call
T_{\ell}
the truncated backward shift matrix of size
\ell
. We then define

X(\omega)_{k}:=I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y_{k}+T_{\ell}\otimes I_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\delta_{i_{j},k}E_{j+1}\otimes r\cdot G_{i_{j}},

where we write
E_{j}:=E_{j,j}
for the diagonal matrix units and we choose
r>0
sufficiently small so that
\|X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y\|_{\mathrm{col}}<\min\{\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1},\|A^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}\}
. For example, if
d=2
,
\omega=12
and
r=1
,

X(12)_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{1}&G_{1}&0\\
0&Y_{1}&0\\
0&0&Y_{1}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad X(12)_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{2}&0&0\\
0&Y_{2}&G_{2}\\
0&0&Y_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Then the tuple
X(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}^{((\ell+1)n\times(\ell+1)n)\cdot d}
is
jointly nilpotent at
Y
of order
\ell+1
in the sense that

(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\alpha}}=0,

for any
|\alpha|>\ell
. Moreover, if
|\alpha|=\ell>0
,

(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\alpha}}=r^{\ell}\cdot\delta_{\alpha,\omega}\,E_{1,\ell+1}\otimes\underbrace{G_{i_{1}}\otimes G_{i_{2}}\cdots\otimes G_{i_{\ell}}}_{=:G^{\otimes^{\omega}}}=\delta_{\alpha,\omega}\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0&r^{\ell}G^{\otimes^{\omega}}\\
&\ddots&&0\\
&&&\vdots\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{\ell+1}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{\ell+1}\otimes c=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}(\mathrm{id}_{\ell+1}\otimes A^{\alpha})\circ(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|<\ell}(\mathrm{id}_{\ell+1}\otimes A^{\alpha})\circ(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\alpha}}+(\mathrm{id}_{\ell+1}\otimes A^{\omega})\circ(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}b^{*}c&*&\cdots&*&b^{*}A^{\omega}(r^{\ell}G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c\\
&\ddots&&&*\\
&&&&\vdots\\
&&&&*\\
&&&&b^{*}c\end{pmatrix}.

By analytic equivalence, the last formula must be equal to

I_{\ell+1}\otimes b^{{}^{\prime}*}L_{A^{\prime}}(X(\omega)-I_{\ell+1}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}b^{{}^{\prime}*}c^{\prime}&*&\cdots&*&b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime};\omega}(r^{\ell}G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c^{\prime}\\
&\ddots&&&*\\
&&&&\vdots\\
&&&&*\\
&&&&b^{{}^{\prime}*}c^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, the top right blocks of these matrices must be equal, and by multi-linearity,

r^{\ell}b^{*}A^{\omega}(G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c=b^{*}A^{\omega}(r^{\ell}G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c=b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime};\omega}(r^{\ell}G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c^{\prime}=r^{\ell}b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime};\omega}(G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c^{\prime}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle b^{*}A^{\omega}(G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})\cdots A_{i_{\ell}}(G_{\ell})c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{\prime}_{i_{1}}(G_{1})\cdots A^{\prime}_{i_{\ell}}(G_{\ell})c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime};\omega}(G^{\otimes^{\omega}})c^{\prime},

for any word
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and any
G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times\ell}
.
∎

3
Controllable, observable and minimal matrix-centre realizations

This section and the following Kalman decomposition section are straightforward extensions of the results of
[
AMS25
, Section 3]
for realizations centred at
0
to realizations about a matrix-centre.

Given any realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, we define the
controllable subspace
,

\mathscr{C}_{A,c}:=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c,

where, if
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}
, then

A^{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the
|\omega|-
multilinear map defined by

A^{\omega}(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\omega|}):=A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})A_{i_{2}}(G_{2})\cdots A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|})\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}),\quad G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}.

Hence,

\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\\
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})\cdots A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|})\,c(v).

The realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, is said to be
controllable
if
\mathcal{H}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
.
Similarly, we define the
observable subspace
,

\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}:=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega;{\dagger}}b,

where, if
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}
, and
G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
then

A^{\omega;{\dagger}}(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\omega|}):=A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|}^{*})^{*}A_{i_{|\omega|-1}}(G_{|\omega|-1}^{*})^{*}\cdots A_{i_{1}}(G_{1}^{*})^{*}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}).

That is, the
|\omega|-
linear map,
A^{\omega;{\dagger}}
, is defined so that

A^{\omega;{\dagger}}(\vec{G})=(A^{\omega}(\vec{G}^{*}))^{*};\quad\quad\vec{G}:=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\omega|}),

and
\vec{G}^{*}
denotes component-wise adjoint. As before,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is said to be
observable
, if
\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}=\mathcal{H}
. Finally,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is said to be
minimal
, if it is both controllable and observable.

Given any
p\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle
,
p=\sum_{|\omega|\leq N}\hat{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{z}^{\omega}
, we can view
p(A)
as multi-affine–linear map in
n_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
arguments in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
into
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
, where

n_{p}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
\hat{p}_{\omega}\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\omega|.

Namely, given two words,
\alpha,\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
, define
A^{\alpha}+cA^{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times(|\alpha|+|\omega|)}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
in the obvious way:

(A^{\alpha}+cA^{\omega})(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\alpha|};H_{1},\cdots H_{|\omega|}):=A^{\alpha}(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\alpha|})+cA^{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|}),

for
G_{i},H_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. For simplicity of notation, when we view
p(A)
for
p\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle
as a multi-affine–linear map in
n_{p}
copies of
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we will often simply write
p(A)[\vec{G}]
for the image of
\vec{G}:=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{n_{p}})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times n_{p}}
under
p(A)
and we employ the notation:

\mathrm{Ran}\,\mathbb{C}\langle A\rangle c:=\bigvee_{p\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle}\mathrm{Ran}\,p(A)c.

It is then clear that

\mathrm{Ran}\,\mathbb{C}\langle A\rangle c=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}.

We will say that a closed and densely–defined linear map,
S:\mathrm{Dom}\,S\subseteq\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a
pseudo-similarity
, if it is injective and has dense range. (Hence it has a closed and densely–defined inverse,
S^{-1}:\mathrm{Ran}\,S\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, which is then also a pseudo-similarity.)
The following theorem is a matrix-centre analogue of
[
AMS25
, Theorem 3.6]
and the proof is similar.

Theorem 3.1

(Uniqueness of minimal realizations)
.

Suppose that
(A,b,c)_{Y}\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
are minimal analytically equivalent realizations at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
that take values in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then there is a unique pseudo-similarity,
S:\mathrm{Dom}\,S\subseteq\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
so that:

(i)

\mathrm{Ran}\,\mathbb{C}\langle A\rangle c
is a core for
S
and
Sp(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)=p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)
for all
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times n_{p}}
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{Ran}\,\mathbb{C}\langle A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}}\rangle b^{\prime}
is a core for
S^{*}
and
S^{*}q(A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}})[\vec{G}]b^{\prime}(u)=q(A^{\dagger})[\vec{G}]b(u)
for all
\vec{G}=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{n_{q}})
,
G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

We need to first check that
S
is a well-defined linear map. Namely, if
p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)=0\in\mathcal{H}
, we need to show that
p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)=0\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. For any
q\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle
, let
m=n_{q}
. Then, for any
H=(H_{1},\cdots H_{m})
,
H_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, since
(A,b,c)\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(u)^{*}\overline{q}(A)[\vec{H}^{*}]p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v):=\langle{q(A^{\dagger})[\vec{H}]b(u)},{p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(u)^{*}\overline{q}(A^{\prime})[\vec{H}^{*}]p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c(v)=\langle{q(A^{{}^{\prime};{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)},{p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}},

where if
q(\mathfrak{z})=\sum\hat{q}_{\omega}\,\mathfrak{z}^{\omega}
, then
\overline{q}(\mathfrak{z}):=\sum\overline{\hat{q}_{\omega}}\,\mathfrak{z}^{\omega}
, is obtained by taking the complex conjugate of each coefficient.
However, since
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
is assumed to be minimal,

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{{}^{\prime}\omega;{\dagger}}b=\bigvee_{q\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle}\mathrm{Ran}\,q(A^{{}^{\prime};{\dagger}})b^{\prime},

and we conclude that

p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)=0
, as required.

Since
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
are both minimal realizations, they are controllable,

\mathcal{H}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\bigvee p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v),

and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
. Hence the non-closed linear span,
\mathrm{Dom}\,S_{0}:=\bigvee p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)
, is a dense domain in
\mathcal{H}
, and
S_{0}:\mathrm{Dom}\,S_{0}\twoheadrightarrow\bigvee p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)
is a densely-defined linear map with dense range in
\mathcal{H}
. To show that
S_{0}
is closeable, it suffices to show that
S_{0}^{*}
is densely-defined. Given any
q\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle
, consider

\displaystyle\langle{q(A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)},{S_{0}p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{q(A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)},{p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{q(A^{\dagger})[\vec{H}]b(u)},{p(A)[\vec{G}]c(v)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

It follows that

q(A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)\in\mathrm{Dom}\,S_{0}^{*},\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{0}^{*}q(A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)=q(A^{{\dagger}})[\vec{H}]b(u),

for any
\vec{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times n_{q}}
and any
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
is also observable, it follows that
S_{0}^{*}
is densely-defined, so that
S_{0}
is closeable with closure
S
and
S^{*}=S_{0}^{*}
. Proof of the second statement, (b), follows from a symmetric argument.
∎

4
The Kalman decomposition

Given
(A,b,c)_{Y}
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, we define the
minimal subspace
as the space,

\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\ominus(\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\cap\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}^{\perp}).

Observe that since

\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c,

where
A^{\omega}
takes values in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
c
takes values in
\mathcal{H}
, that
\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
is invariant for any
A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]\in\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}
,
\vec{G}=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\omega|})
,
G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. Similarly,
\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}
is co-invariant for each element of
\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}
. It follows that
\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}
is
\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}-
semi-invariant so that if
Q_{0}
denotes orthogonal projection onto
\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, and we define
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})
by
A^{(0)}_{j}(G):=Q_{0}A_{j}(G)|_{\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}}
,
G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
b_{0}=Q_{0}b
and
c_{0}=Q_{0}c
, then for any
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

Q_{0}A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})Q_{0}\cdots Q_{0}A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|})Q_{0}=Q_{0}A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})\cdots A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|})Q_{0}=Q_{0}A^{\omega}(G)Q_{0},

by semi-invariance
[
Sar67
]
. The following theorem is a matrix-centre analogue of
[
AMS25
, Theorem 3.12]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Kalman decomposition)
.

Let
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
be a realization centred at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
and let
Q_{0}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Then
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})_{Y}
is minimal and
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
are analytically equivalent realizations that define the same quantized transfer function,
f
, on the level-wise path-connected component of
Y
in
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A^{(0)})
,
\mathscr{D}^{\,\leadsto\!Y}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{\,\leadsto\!Y}(A^{(0)})
.

Proof.

Given the realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, centred at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, let
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
be the controllable realization obtained by ‘restricting’
(A,b,c)_{Y}
to the
A-
invariant controllable subspace
\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. That is, if
P^{\prime}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
, then
A^{\prime}_{j}:=A_{j}|_{\mathscr{C}_{A,c,}}
,
1\leq j\leq d
,
b:=P^{\prime}b
and
c^{\prime}=P^{\prime}c=c
. Then
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
is a controllable realization on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
since

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\\
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\end{subarray}}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee(P^{\prime}AP^{\prime})^{\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]\underbrace{P^{\prime}c(v)}_{=c(v)}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime},

using the
A-
invariance of
P^{\prime}
.
In the above, if
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
\vec{G}=(G_{1},\cdots,G_{|\omega|})
,
G_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the notation
(P^{\prime}AP^{\prime})^{\omega}[\vec{G}]
denotes

P^{\prime}A_{i_{1}}(G_{1})P^{\prime}A_{i_{2}}(G_{2})P^{\prime}\cdots P^{\prime}A_{i_{|\omega|}}(G_{|\omega|})P^{\prime}.

Moreover,
(A,b,c)\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
since for any
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}
,

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}P^{\prime}(P^{\prime}AP^{\prime})^{\omega}[\vec{G}]P^{\prime}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]P^{\prime}c=b^{*}A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]c.

We next define a new realization by compressing the controllable realization,
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Y}
, to its
A^{\prime}
-co-invariant observable subspace,
\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}
. We first claim that
\mathscr{M}:=\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}=\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}
is the minimal subspace of the original realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
. Indeed,
x\in\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\ominus\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}
, if and only if for any
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger};\omega}[\vec{H}]b^{\prime}(u)},{x}\rangle=\langle{(P^{\prime}AP^{\prime})^{{\dagger};\omega}[\vec{H}]P^{\prime}b(u)},{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{P^{\prime}A^{{\dagger};\omega}[\vec{H}]b(u)},{x}\rangle=\langle{A^{{\dagger};\omega}[\vec{H}]b(u)},{x}\rangle,

since
P^{\prime}x=x
. It follows that
x\in\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\ominus\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}
if and only if
x\in\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
is orthogonal to
\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}
. This proves that

\mathscr{O}_{A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}},b^{\prime}}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\ominus\left(\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\cap\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},b}^{\perp}\right)=\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c},

as claimed.

Finally, let
Q_{0}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto the minimal subspace,
\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}
, and consider the realization
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})_{Y}
obtained by compressing
(A,b,c)_{Y}
to this minimal space.
Namely,
A^{(0)}_{j}(G):=Q_{0}A_{j}(G)|_{\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}}
,
b_{0}=Q_{0}b
and
c_{0}=Q_{0}c
. First we claim that
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is a matrix-centre realization of the same NC function. Indeed,

\displaystyle b_{0}^{*}A^{(0);\omega}[\vec{G}]c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}Q_{0}(Q_{0}A^{\prime}Q_{0})^{\omega}[\vec{G}]Q_{0}c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}Q_{0}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]Q_{0}c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}Q_{0}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}\quad\quad\mbox{($Q_{0}$ is $A^{\prime}-$co-invariant)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}\quad\quad(b^{\prime}=Q_{0}b^{\prime}=b_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]c,

since
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
as calculated above. Moreover,

\bigvee A^{(0){\dagger};\omega}[\vec{G}]b_{0}(u)=\bigvee A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger};\omega}[\vec{G}]b^{\prime}(u)=\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c},

follows since
b_{0}=b^{\prime}
,
A^{(0){\dagger};\omega}[\vec{G}]=A^{(0);\omega}[\vec{G}^{*}]^{*}
,
\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}
is
A^{\prime}-
co-invariant and
A^{{}^{\prime}{\dagger}}_{j}(G)|_{\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}}=A^{(0){\dagger}}_{j}(G)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\bigvee A^{(0);\omega}[\vec{G}]c_{0}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee Q_{0}A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]Q_{0}c^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{0}\bigvee A^{{}^{\prime}\omega}[\vec{G}]c^{\prime}(v)=Q_{0}\bigvee A^{\omega}[\vec{G}]c(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{0}\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c},

so that
(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})_{Y}
is both controllable and observable, hence minimal.

If
h\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)\sim_{Y}(A^{(0)},b_{0},c_{0})
, the final statement follows from the identity theorem in several complex variables.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
A
takes values in compact operators,
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A^{(0)})\supseteq\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
.

We will use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
AMS25
, Lemma 3.16]

Let
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
have a closed, invariant subspace,
\mathcal{J}
, so that with respect to the orthogonal direct sum decomposition,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{J}\oplus\mathcal{J}^{\perp}
,

T\simeq\begin{pmatrix}T_{1}&T_{2}\\
0&T_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

If
T
is invertible then
T_{1}
is injective,
T_{1}^{*}
is surjective on
\mathcal{J}
, and
T_{3}\simeq P_{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}T|_{\mathcal{J}^{\perp}}
is surjective on
\mathcal{J}^{\perp}
. Moreover,
T
and
T_{1}
are both invertible if and only if
T
and
T_{3}
are both invertible, in which case

T^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{1}^{-1}&-T_{1}^{-1}T_{2}T_{3}^{-1}\\
0&T_{3}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

so that
\mathcal{J}
is also
T^{-1}-
invariant.

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.

If
X\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)
,
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
, then with respect to the orthogonal decomposition,

\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c}\oplus(\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\ominus\mathscr{M}_{A,b,c})\oplus\mathscr{C}_{A,c}^{\perp},

we have that,

A_{j}(X_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}\pagecolor{blue!15}A^{(0)}_{j}(X_{j})&\pagecolor{green!15}0\\
\pagecolor{green!15}*&\pagecolor{green!15}A^{(1)}_{j}(X_{j})\end{array}&\mbox{\Large$*$}\\
\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\quad 0\\
0&\quad 0\end{array}&\mbox{\Large$A^{(2)}_{j}(X_{j})$ }\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, the entire operator,
A_{j}(X_{j})
, is block upper triangular with respect to the orthogonal decompostion
\mathcal{H}=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{A,c}^{\perp}
, while the upper left block corresponding to the decomposition of
\mathscr{C}_{A,c}
is block lower triangular. Let,
A^{\prime}_{j}(X_{j})
denote this upper left block so that

A_{j}(X_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}A^{\prime}_{j}(X_{j})&*\\
0&A^{(2)}_{j}(X_{j})\end{pmatrix}.

By the previous lemma, if
X\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)
, so that
L_{A}(X)
is invertible, it follows that
L_{A^{\prime}}(X)
is injective and
L_{A^{(2)}}(X)
is surjective. However, if
L_{A^{\prime}}(X)=I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}-A^{\prime}(X)
is not invertible, it is not surjective, so that
\lambda=1
belongs to the spectrum of
A^{\prime}(X)
. Since each
A_{j}(X_{j})
is compact, it follows that
A^{\prime}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A^{\prime}_{j}(X_{j})
is compact, so that any non-zero point in its spectrum is an eigenvalue. This contradicts injectivity of
L_{A^{\prime}}(X)
and we conclude that
L_{A^{\prime}}(X)
is invertible. Similarly, since
L_{A^{\prime}}(X)
is invertible, we must have that
L_{A^{(0)}}(X)
is surjective and
L_{A^{(1)}}(X)
is injective by the previous lemma. Again, both
A^{(0)}(X)
and
A^{(1)}(X)
are compact, so that both
L_{A^{(0)}}(X)
and
L_{A^{(1)}}(X)
must be invertible. Hence
X\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A^{(0)})
and
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)\subseteq\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A^{(0)})
.
∎

5
Translations

5.1
Translations of one-dimensional realizations

Let
f\sim_{0}(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
be a one-dimensional realization centred at
0\in\mathbb{C}
. We will simply write
(A,b,c)=(A,b,c)_{0}
, in this case. Then, as described in the introduction, for any
z\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, we can construct a realization of
f
centred at
z
,
f\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{z}
,

A^{\prime}:=(I-zA)^{-1}A,\quad b^{\prime}=b,\quad\mbox{and}\quad c^{\prime}=(I-zA)^{-1}c.

Theorem 5.1

(Minimal translations)
.

If
f\sim(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
is a minimal realization at
0\in\mathbb{C}
, then
f\sim_{z}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is also a minimal realization of
f
at
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

We prove controllability, observability is analogous. The controllable subspace of
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}=\bigvee_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{{}^{\prime}j}c^{\prime}=\bigvee_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{j}(I-zA)^{-j-1}c.

Hence,
(I-zA)^{-1}c\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
, and

zA(I-zA)^{-2}c=(I-zA)^{-2}c-(I-zA)^{-1}c\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}.

In particular it follows that
\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
contains the linear span of
(I-zA)^{-2}c
and
(I-zA)^{-1}c
. Iterating this argument yields

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{j=1}^{\infty}(I-zA)^{-j}c.

Set
T:=(I-zA)^{-1}
, a bounded, invertible operator and choose any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
so that
|\lambda|>\|T\|
. Then,
\lambda I-T
is invertible and

(\lambda I-T)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-n-1}T^{n},

is a convergent geometric series since
\|\lambda^{-1}T\|=|\lambda|^{-1}\|T\|<1
. Hence,

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{|\lambda|>\|T\|}(\lambda I-T)^{-1}Tc.

Since
T
is invertible,
0\notin\sigma(T)
, so that the functions
e_{-n}(\lambda):=1/\lambda^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are analytic in an open set containing the spectrum of
T
,
e_{-n}\in\mathscr{O}(\sigma(T))
. Hence, by the Riesz–Dunford holomorphic functional calculus,

T^{-n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ointctrclockwise_{r\cdot\partial\mathbb{D}}\lambda^{-n}(\lambda I-T)^{-1}d\lambda;\quad\quad r>\|T\|,

so that

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T^{-n}Tc

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{-n}c.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}(I-zA)^{n}c.

Hence,
c\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
and

(I-zA)c=c-zAc\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}},

so that
Ac\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
. Continuing in this manner,

(I-zA)^{2}c=c-2zAC+z^{2}A^{2}c\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}},

so that
A^{2}c\in\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
, and we conclude that

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{n}c=\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\mathcal{H},

so that
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{z}
is controllable.
∎

5.2
Translations of matrix-centre realizations

More generally, given a matrix-centre realization
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
, with
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
and
X,Z\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)
, we have that

\displaystyle f(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(Z-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}(I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-A(Z-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}(I_{m}\otimes I-(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A)(Z-X)-(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A)(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}\left(I_{m}\otimes I-L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A)(Z-X)\right)^{-1}L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle b^{{}^{\prime}*}L_{A^{\prime}}(Z-X)^{-1}c^{\prime},

where
b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{mn},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
,
A^{\prime}_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
are linear maps,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{H}
, and for any
G\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}
,

A^{\prime}_{j}(G):=L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{j})(G),\quad b^{\prime}=I_{m}\otimes b,\quad\mbox{and}\quad c^{\prime}=L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c.

Here, for any
u\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
b^{\prime}
is defined on
\mathbb{C}^{mn}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
via

I_{m}\otimes b(u\otimes v):=u\otimes b(v)\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{H}.

This is essentially the same calculation as in
[
PV21
, Section 2.7, Theorem 2.21]
. Hence we have shown that
f\sim(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
.

Theorem 5.2

(Minimal matrix-centre translations)
.

If
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
is a minimal realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, and
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)
then
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
is also a minimal realization of
f
at
X
so that
\mathscr{D}^{X}_{k}(A^{\prime})=\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{km}(A)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the case where
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
are finite–dimensional matrix-centre realizations of an NC function (and hence are matrix-centre realizations of an NC rational function), this was established by Porat and Vinnikov in the proof of
[
PV21
, Theorem 2.21]
. Since our realizations are generally infinite–dimensional, a different proof approach is required.

Proof.

The controllable subspace of
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
is

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\end{subarray}}\left(L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}A\right)^{\omega}(\vec{G})L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}(I_{m}\otimes c)(v),

and we want to prove that this is equal to
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{H}
. Setting
T:=A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
,
L:=(I-T)=L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
and
\Lambda:=L^{-1}
, we have that

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\\
1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{j})(X_{j}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{j})\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c)(v),

and hence, by summing over
j
,

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda\underbrace{A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)}_{=T}\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c)(v).

Iterating this argument yields that

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\\
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(\Lambda T)^{j}\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c).

(5.1)

Using that
T
and
\Lambda=(I-T)^{-1}
commute and
T\Lambda=T(I-T)^{-1}=(I-T)^{-1}-I=\Lambda-I
, the previous formula becomes

\mbox{(\ref{powers})}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\\
j\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda^{j}(I_{m}\otimes c).

In particular, it follows as before that

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\\
|\lambda|>\|\Lambda\|\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(\lambda I-\Lambda)^{-1}\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c)(v),

since for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
so that
|\lambda|>\|\Lambda\|
,

(\lambda I-\Lambda)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-n-1}\Lambda^{n},

converges in operator norm. Again, by the Riesz–Dunford functional calculus, since
\Lambda
is invertible,
0\notin\sigma(\Lambda)
and
1/\lambda^{j}
is holomorphic in an open neighbourhood of the spectrum of
\Lambda
so that

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{h\in\mathscr{O}(\sigma(\Lambda))}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})h(\Lambda)\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{j=1}^{\infty}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda^{-j}\Lambda(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{j=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda^{-j}(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(I_{m}\otimes c)(v).

We now iterate this argument. Fix any
1\leq k\leq d
and any
G\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}
. By Equation (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d;\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{mn}\\
G\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}\end{subarray}}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{j})(X_{j}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{j})\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda T\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v),

where the last formula is obtained by summing over
1\leq j\leq d
. Iterating this argument then yields

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{j=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(\Lambda T)^{j}\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v).

Again, we now use that
\Lambda,T
commute and that
\Lambda T=\Lambda-I
to conclude that

\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigvee_{j=1}^{\infty}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda^{j}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v),

and hence as before,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{|\lambda|>\|\Lambda\|}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(\lambda I-\Lambda)^{-1}\Lambda(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{k=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})\Lambda^{-j}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v)

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\bigvee(\Lambda A)^{\omega}(\vec{G})(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A_{k})(G)(I_{m}\otimes c)(v).

In particular,

\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\\
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\end{subarray}}A_{j}(G)c(v)+\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\bigvee_{v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}c(v)\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}},

and iterating this argument shows that for any
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,
\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}}
contains
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c.
In conclusion,

\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathscr{C}_{A,c}=\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\\
\vec{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}\end{subarray}}A^{\omega}(\vec{G})c(v)\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime},c^{\prime}},

so that
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
is controllable. Proof of observability now follows from a symmetric, analogous argument and is omitted.

The final statement will follow from our construction of
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{X}
. If
Z\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{km}(A)
, then, since
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)
, by assumption,

\displaystyle L_{A}(Z-I_{km}\otimes Y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes I_{km}-A(I_{k}\otimes X-I_{km}\otimes Y)-A(Z-I_{k}\otimes X)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I\otimes I-I_{k}\otimes A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)-A(Z-I_{k}\otimes X)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I\otimes I-I_{k}\otimes L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}A(Z-I_{k}\otimes X)\right)^{-1}I_{k}\otimes L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{A^{\prime}}(Z-I_{k}\otimes X)^{-1}I_{k}\otimes L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}.

That is, under the assumption that
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)
so that
L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
is invertible, invertibility of
L_{A}(Z-I_{km}\otimes Y)
is equivalent to invertibility of
L_{A^{\prime}}(Z-I_{k}\otimes X)
. That is,
Z\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{km}(A)
if and only if
Z\in\mathscr{D}^{X}_{k}(A^{\prime})
.
∎

6
NC transfer functions and the Lost-Abbey conditions

Given a power series of multi-linear maps on
\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
of the form,

\widetilde{f}(X):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}_{\ell}\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}},

where
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
, each
\hat{f}_{\ell}
is
\ell-
linear, and
\odot
denotes the Haagerup tensor product for the
d-
dimensional column operator space over
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, one can ask when this is equal to a uniformly analytic NC function, assuming it converges absolutely in
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
for some
r>0
. The answer, given in
[
KVV14
, Chapter 8, Theorem 8.11]
, is that it is neccessary and sufficient that the sequence of multi-linear maps obey the
Lost–Abbey conditions
or
canonical intertwining conditions
at
Y
, denoted by
\mathrm{LAC}(Y)
or
\mathrm{CIC}(Y)
. Namely, given any
H^{(i)}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, and
T\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,

[T,\hat{f}_{0}]=\hat{f}_{1}\left([T,Y]\right),

(6.1)

and for any
\ell\geq 1
,

\displaystyle T\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)})-\hat{f}_{\ell}(TH^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)})=\hat{f}_{\ell+1}([T,Y],H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)}),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(j-1)},H^{(j)}T,H^{(j+1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)})-\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(j)},TH^{(j+1)},H^{(j+2)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)})

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{\ell+1}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(j)},[T,Y],H^{(j+1)},\cdots H^{(\ell)}),\quad\quad\mbox{and}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)}T)-\hat{f}_{\ell}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)})T=\hat{f}_{\ell+1}(H^{(1)},\cdots,H^{(\ell)},[T,Y]).

(6.4)

In the above
[T,Y]=TY-YT=([T,Y_{1}],\cdots,[T,Y_{d}])
denotes the component-wise commutator.

Equivalently, the Lost–Abbey conditions at
Y
can be given for the terms of a power series of multilinear maps indexed by the free monoid,

\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\hat{f}_{\omega}\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\omega}},

where each
\hat{f}_{\omega}
is
|\omega|-
linear. The conditions become: Given
H_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and setting
\ell:=|\omega|
,
\omega\neq\emptyset
,

\displaystyle[T,\hat{f}_{\emptyset}]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{f}_{j}([T,Y_{j}])

(6.5)

\displaystyle T\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{\ell})-\hat{f}_{\omega}(TH_{1},\cdots,H_{\ell})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{f}_{j\omega}([T,Y_{j}],H_{1},\cdots,H_{\ell})

(6.6)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{k-1},H_{k}T,H_{k+1},\cdots,H_{\ell})-\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{k},TH_{k+1},H_{k+2},\cdots,H_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{f}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}ji_{k+1}\cdots i_{\ell}}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{k},[T,Y_{j}],H_{k+1},\cdots H_{\ell})

(6.7)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{\ell}T)-\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{\omega},\cdots,H_{\ell})T=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{f}_{\omega j}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{\ell},[T,Y_{j}]),

(6.8)

see
[
KVV14
, Equations (4.14–4.17)]
,
[
PV23
, Equations (1.13–1.16)]
.

If
\widetilde{f}\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is the quantized transfer function of a matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, recall that it can expanded as a power series of multi-linear maps,

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}I_{m}\otimes b^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A^{\omega})\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}I_{m}\otimes c,

as described in Subsection
2.2
. Assuming
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is minimal, we can then write the Lost Abbey conditions for the sequence of multi-linear maps,
(b^{*}A^{\omega}(\cdot)c)_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}
, in terms of the realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
. The theorem below was proven for finite–dimensional matrix-centre realizations of NC rational functions by Porat and Vinnikov in
[
PV23
, Lemma 2.2, Theorem 2.6]
. We provide the proof below, for the convenience of the reader, although the proof is essentially the same as the proofs in
[
PV23
]
. (The reference
[
PV23
]
works with finite–dimensional Fornasini–Marchesini matrix-centre realizations while the theorem below is stated in terms of descriptor matrix-centre realizations which are generally infinite–dimensional. We have not yet introduced Fornasini–Marchesini realizations, we will do this in Section
7
.)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
, where
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is a minimal matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. Then
f
is an NC function if and only if
(A,b,c)_{Y}
obeys the
linearized Lost Abbey conditions
at
Y
:
For any
G,H,T\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,

\displaystyle[T,b^{*}c]

\displaystyle=b^{*}A([T,Y])c,

(6.9)

\displaystyle Tb^{*}A_{i}(H)-b^{*}A_{i}(TH)

\displaystyle=b^{*}A([T,Y])A_{i}(H),

(6.10)

\displaystyle A_{i}(HT)c-A_{i}(H)cT

\displaystyle=A_{i}(H)A([T,Y])c,\quad\quad\mbox{and}

(6.11)

\displaystyle A_{i}(GT)A_{j}(H)-A_{i}(G)A_{j}(TH)

\displaystyle=A_{i}(G)A([T,Y])A_{j}(H).

(6.12)

In the above theorem statement, we have used the short-form notation,

A([T,Y]):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}([T,Y_{j}]).

We will refer to the above four Equations (
6.9
–
6.12
) as the
linearized Lost Abbey conditions
at
Y
, LLAC(Y). It follows from this theorem, that if
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is minimal and obeys the linearized Lost-Abbey conditions at
Y
, then
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is a uniformly analytic NC function in a uniform column-ball centred at
Y
of radius at least
\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

As in Subsection
2.2
, assuming that
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
is a uniformly analytic NC function, then by uniqueness of Taylor–Taylor series, if

f(X)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\hat{f}_{\omega}\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\omega}}

is the Taylor–Taylor series of
f
at
Y
, then as an
|\omega|-
linear map

\hat{f}_{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|})=\Delta^{\omega^{t}}_{{H_{1}},\cdots,H_{|\omega|}}f(Y)=b^{*}A^{\omega}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{|\omega|})c,

where
\omega=i_{1}\cdots i_{|\omega|}
. Hence, the sequence of
|\omega|-
linear maps,
(b^{*}A^{\omega}(\cdot)c)_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}
, must obey the Lost–Abbey conditions at
Y
, as stated in Equations (
6.5
–
6.8
).

The first condition (
6.5
),
[T,\hat{f}_{\emptyset}]=\hat{f}_{1}([T,Y])
becomes

[T,b^{*}c]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b^{*}A_{j}([T,Y_{j}])c=b^{*}A([T,Y])c,

which is Equation (
6.9
). Given
G,T\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{1\times|\omega|}

6.6
),

T\hat{f}_{k\omega}(G,H)-\hat{f}_{k\omega}(TG,H)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\hat{f}_{jk\omega}\left([T,Y_{j}],G,H\right),

then becomes

Tb^{*}A_{k}(G)A^{\omega}(H)c-b^{*}A_{k}(TG)A^{\omega}(H)c=b^{*}A([T,Y])A_{k}(G)A^{\omega}(H)c.

By controllability of
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, we conclude that

Tb^{*}A_{k}(G)-b^{*}A_{k}(TG)=b^{*}A([T,Y])A_{k}(G),

so that condition (
6.10
) holds. The final two conditions, (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) follow similiarly using observability and minimality of
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, respectively.

Conversely, we claim that if
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
, where
(A,b,c)_{Y}
is minimal and obeys the linearized
\mathrm{LAC}(Y)
, Equations (
6.9
–
6.12
), then
f
is a uniformly analytic NC function in a uniform column-ball centred at
Y
. Since
f
is the quantized transfer function of a matrix-centre realization, it respects direct sums and the grading, so that it suffices to prove that
f
respects joint similarities. (This will imply that
f
is an NC function. Since
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
, it is automatically uniformly bounded in
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
,
r=\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}
, hence uniformly analytic in this uniformly open NC set.) Hence, assume that
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
,
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{mn}
is invertible and
X^{\prime}:=S^{-1}XS\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)
. First, we observe that the Lost–Abbey conditions for the realization
(A,b,c)_{Y}
can be extended to
T\in\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}
in a natural way, by ampliating the realization. For example, given such a
T
, Equation (
6.9
) becomes

[T,(I_{m}\otimes b^{*}c)]=I_{m}\otimes b^{*}(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes A)([T,I_{m}\otimes Y])I_{m}\otimes c.

In the following argument we will assume, for simplicity of notation, that
X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{1}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
and
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the argument is easily extended to the general case.

We will prove that
f(X)S=Sf(X^{\prime})
. First,

f(X)S=b^{*}cS+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(X-Y)cS.

Applying condition (
6.9
) to
b^{*}cS
yields

b^{*}cS=Sb^{*}c-b^{*}A([S,Y])c.

Now consider
A(X-Y)cS
,

A(X-Y)cS=A((X-Y)S)c-A(X-Y)A([S,Y])c,

by condition (
6.11
). Hence,

\displaystyle f(X)S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Sb^{*}c-b^{*}A([S,Y])c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A((X-Y)S)c-b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(X-Y)A([S,Y])c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Sb^{*}c-b^{*}A([S,Y])c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A((X-Y)S)c-b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A([S,Y])c+b^{*}A([S,Y])c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Sb^{*}c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A((X-Y)S)c-b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A([S,Y])c

\displaystyle Sb^{*}c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}\left(A((X-Y)S)-A([S,Y])\right)c.

Observe that

A((X-Y)S)-A([S,Y])=A(SX^{\prime}-SY),

by linearity of

, so that the previous equation becomes

f(X)S=Sb^{*}c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c.

(6.13)

Now consider the expression

Applying condition (
6.12
) to the first term of this expression yields

A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)c=A(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+A(X-Y)A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c.

Hence Equation (

\displaystyle(\mbox{\ref{expr1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)c-A(X-Y)A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

\displaystyle L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

\displaystyle+L_{A}(X-Y)A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c-A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{A}(X-Y)\left(A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))+A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)\right)c+\left(A((X-Y)S)-A([S,Y])\right)A(X^{\prime}-Y)c.

We now substitute this identity for

into Equation (

) to obtain

\displaystyle f(X)S-Sb^{*}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{*}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+b^{*}A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

\displaystyle b^{*}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))c+b^{*}A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))A(X^{\prime}-Y)c.

6.10
) to the first term in this final formula yields

\displaystyle f(X)S-Sb^{*}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Sb^{*}A(X^{\prime}-Y)c-b^{*}A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c+b^{*}A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

(6.15)

\displaystyle+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))A(X^{\prime}-Y)c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Sb^{*}A(X^{\prime}-Y)c+b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))A(X^{\prime}-Y)c.

(6.16)

We now apply condition (
6.12
) to obtain the expression

A(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))=A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)-A(X-Y)A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y).

Hence,

\displaystyle A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))+A(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))+A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)-A(X-Y)A([S,Y])A(X^{\prime}-Y)

\displaystyle A((X-Y)S)A(X^{\prime}-Y)+L_{A}(X-Y)A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))

\displaystyle b^{*}L_{A}(X-Y)^{-1}A(S(X^{\prime}-Y))

\displaystyle f(X)S

Remark 6.2
.

In
[
PV23
, Theorem 3.5]
, Porat and Vinnikov further show that if a minimal and
finite–dimensional
matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
obeys the linearized Lost-Abbey conditions at
Y
, then the invertibility domain,
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
, is joint similarity invariant. (Recall
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
is always a uniformly open NC set, regardless of whether the realization is minimal or obeys LAC(Y).) Their proof, however, seems to rely on the assumption that their realizations take values in a finite–dimensional space, as they make use of so-called finite-rank
controllability
and
observability operators
that are right and left invertible, respectively, under the assumption that the realization is minimal. So far we have not been able to extend this approach. While we can formally define these controllability and observability operators in the infinite dimensional settings, it is not immediately clear to us why these operators should even be closeable linear maps.

7
Fornasini–Marchesini matrix-centre realizations

It will be sometimes convenient to consider a second type of realization,
Fornasini–Marchesini
(FM)
realizations
. An FM realization at a matrix-point
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, is a quadruple,
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
, where, as before,
A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d}):\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a row
d-
tuple of linear maps,
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B_{1}\\
\vdots\\
B_{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathcal{H}))\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
C\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
D\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. That is, each
B_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathcal{H})
is a bounded linear map. The linear pencil of
A
,
L_{A}
, and its invertibility domain (at
Y
),
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}n\times\mathbb{N}n)\cdot d}
are defined as before, and
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}\sim h
defines the
quantized transfer function
on
\mathscr{D}^{Y}(A)
by the formula:

f(X)=I_{m}\otimes D+I_{m}\otimes CL_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{d}B_{j}(X_{j}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{j})}_{=:B(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)};\quad\quad X\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}.

If
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
is a matrix-centre realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, we define, as before, the
controllable subspace
,

\mathscr{C}_{A,B}:=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}B_{j},

and the
observable subspace
,

\mathscr{O}_{A^{\dagger},C^{*}}:=\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega;{\dagger}}C^{*}.

Again, as before, we say that
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
is minimal if it is both controllable and observable. (Both Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
as well as all of the results of previous sections are readily adapted to FM realizations.)

As described in
[
AMS25
, Section 3]
(for the case of realizations at
0
) one can construct an FM realization from a descriptor one and vice versa. Namely, let
(A,b,c)_{Y}
be a descriptor realization at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. Then define the
A-
invariant subspace

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=\bigvee_{\omega\neq\emptyset}\mathrm{Ran}\,A^{\omega}c,

with orthogonal projection
P^{\prime}
,

A^{\prime}_{j}(G):=A_{j}(G)|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}},\quad B_{j}(G):=A_{j}(G)c,\quad C:=b^{*}P^{\prime},\quad\mbox{and}\quad D:=b^{*}c\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},

where
G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. It is easy to check that minimality of
(A,b,c)_{Y}
implies minimality of
(A^{\prime},B,C,D)_{Y}
and both of these realizations define the same quantized transfer function in a uniform row-ball of positive radius centred at
Y
.

Conversely, if
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
is an FM realization about
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, let
\hat{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{n}
and define

\hat{A}_{j}(G):=\begin{pmatrix}A_{j}(G)&B_{j}(G)\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad b:=C^{*}\oplus D^{*},\quad\mbox{and}\quad c=0\oplus I_{n}.

Then
(\hat{A},b,c)_{Y}
is a descriptor realization centred at
Y
, which defines the same quantized transfer function in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y
. If
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
is controllable, then,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\hat{A},c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\hat{A}^{\omega}(\vec{G})c(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n};v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\\
1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}B_{j}(G)(v)+\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega=\omega^{\prime}j\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},\ 1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}A^{\omega^{\prime}}(\vec{G})B_{j}(G)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{A,B},

so that
(\hat{A},b,c)
is also controllable. If
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
is observable, then
(\hat{A},b,c)_{Y}
need not be observable, but its observable subspace has codimension at most
n
.

7.1
The realization algorithm

Also as in
[
PV21
, Theorem 2.4]
and
[
AMS25
, Subsection 3.4]
, if
f\sim_{Y}(A,B,C,D)
and
g\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime},D^{\prime})
, then
f+g
,
f\cdot g
and, assuming
f(Y)=D
is invertible,
f^{-1}
, all have matrix-centre realizations about
Y
given by a natural extension of the Fornasini–Marchesini algorithm for realizations at
0
. Namely, a matrix-centre realization for
f+g
is given by
(A^{+},B^{+},C^{+},D^{+})
where

A_{j}^{+}:=A_{j}\oplus A^{\prime}_{j},\quad B_{j}^{+}:=\begin{pmatrix}B_{j}\\
B^{\prime}_{j}\end{pmatrix},\quad C^{+}:=(C,C^{\prime}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad D^{+}:=D+D^{\prime}.

(7.1)

A matrix-centre realization for
f\cdot g
is given by
(A^{\times},B^{\times},C^{\times},D^{\times})
, where

A^{\times}_{j}:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{j}&B_{j}(\cdot)C^{\prime}\\
&A_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\quad B^{\times}:=\begin{pmatrix}B_{j}(\cdot)D^{\prime}\\
B_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\quad C^{\times}:=(C,DC^{\prime}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad D^{\times}:=DD^{\prime}.

(7.2)

Finally, a matrix-centre realization for
f^{-1}
, assuming that
D=f(Y)
is invertible, is given by

(A^{(-1)},B^{(-1)},C^{(-1)},D^{(-1)})
, where

A^{(-1)}_{j}:=A_{j}-B_{j}(\cdot)D^{-1}C,\quad B_{j}^{(-1)}:=-B_{j}(\cdot)D^{-1},\quad C^{(-1)}:=D^{-1}C,\quad\mbox{and}\quad D^{(-1)}:=D^{-1}.

(7.3)

Verification of these formulas is a straightforward computation, and we refer the reader to
[
PV21
, Theorem 2.4]
for their proofs (in the NC rational/ finite–dimensional setting).

Remark 7.1
.

An analogous algorithm exists for descriptor realizations centred at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. In our opinion the formulas for the FM algorithm are simpler and easier to work with in many calculations. Another advantage of the FM algorithm over the descriptor realization algorithm is that if
(A,B,C,D)_{Y}
minimal (and
D\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is invertible) then the realization
(A^{(-1)},B^{(-1)},C^{(-1)},D^{(-1)})_{Y}
is also minimal. (This is not the case for the corresponding descriptor algorithm formula for the realization of the inverse of the quantized transfer function.)

8
Every uniformly analytic NC function has a matrix-centre realization

In
[
AMS25
, Lemma 3.2]
it was shown that an NC function,
h
, is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighborhood of
0
if and only if it has a realization,
h\sim_{0}(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
. The proof of the existence of such a realization, given such a uniformly analytic
h
, was accomplished with the aid of the full Fock space over
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Here, given any complex Hilbert space,
V
, the full Fock space over
V
is the Hilbert space
defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(V):=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes^{\ell}}=\mathbb{C}\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes V)\oplus(V\otimes V\otimes V)\oplus\cdots.

One can identify the full Fock space over
V=\mathbb{C}^{d}
with the
free Hardy space
of free formal power series in
d
NC variables with square–summable coefficients:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}:=\left\{\left.h(\mathfrak{z})=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}\hat{h}_{\omega}\mathfrak{z}^{\omega}\right|\ \sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}|\hat{h}_{\omega}|^{2}<+\infty\right\}.

Any
h\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
has radius of convergence at least
1
, so that
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
can be viewed as a space of uniformly analytic NC functions on the unit row ball
\mathbb{B}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}_{\text{row}}
or the unit column ball
\mathbb{B}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}
. Equipping
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
with the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the coefficients turns it into a Hilbert space, which generalizes the classical Hardy space
H^{2}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
is an NC reproducing kernel Hilbert space (NC-RKHS); see
[
BMV16
]
for background on NC reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. Denote by
\mathbb{H}_{d}^{\infty}
the unital Banach algebra of uniformly bounded, and hence uniformly analytic NC functions in the unit row-ball
\mathbb{B}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot d}_{\text{row}}
equipped with the supremum norm. We say that a uniformly analytic NC function,
f
, in the NC unit row-ball, is a
left multiplier
of
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
, if
f\cdot h\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
for every
h\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
. It is clear that the set of all left multipliers is a unital algebra, and, by the closed graph theorem, any left multiplier,
f\in\mathrm{Mult}^{L}(\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d})
, defines a bounded
left multiplication operator
on the free hardy space via
h\mapsto fh
,
h\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
. We will later need the following fact, see
[
Pop06
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
SSS18
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 8.1
.

h\in\mathbb{H}_{d}^{\infty}
if and only if
h
belongs to the left multiplier algebra of
\mathbb{H}_{d}^{2}
.

Note that since
1\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
, it follows that
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}
.
In the remainder of this section we will prove a similar result for NC functions which are uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighborhood of some matrix point
Y
. Namely, we will prove that an NC function is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighbourhood of a matrix point
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
if and only if it is given by a matrix-centre realization at
Y
.

Our approach is similar to the one mentioned above, as we will make use of a matrix-valued version of the full Fock space and define a “free Hardy space centred at
Y
”,
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}(Y)
, consisting of uniformly analytic NC functions in the unit uniform column-ball centred at
Y
,
\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
, which have “square–summable Taylor–Taylor series at
Y
”. Throughout this section let
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
be a fixed matrix point.

8.1
A matricial Fock space

Definition 8.2
.

View
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
as a Hilbert space by equipping it with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product and define

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathscr{F}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\oplus\cdots.

If
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{d}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})^{\otimes^{\ell}}
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, is then given by

\displaystyle\{\left.E_{i_{0},j_{0}}\otimes e_{k_{1}}\otimes E_{i_{1},j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{\ell}}\otimes E_{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}\right|\ 1\leq i_{0},\cdots,i_{\ell},j_{0},\cdots,j_{\ell}\leq n,\;1\leq k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}\leq d\},

where the matrices
E_{i,j}=e_{i}e_{j}
are the standard matrix units of
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. For convenience and brevity, we introduce the short-form notation: For
\alpha=a_{0}\cdots a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}
,
\beta=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}
and
\omega=w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}:=E_{a_{0},b_{0}}\otimes e_{w_{1}}\otimes E_{a_{1},b_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{w_{\ell}}\otimes E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}.

Hence, an orthornormal basis of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is then

\{E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}|\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},\ |\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\}.

In particular, the orthonormal basis of the “vacuum space”,
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\oplus\{0\}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is then written

\{E_{i,j}\star e_{\emptyset}|\ 1\leq i,j\leq n\}.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq d
, define the left creation operators on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
by

\displaystyle L_{i,j;k}:\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}\mapsto E_{i\alpha,j\beta}\star e_{k\omega}.

Note that
L_{i,j;k}
defines a
dn^{2}-
tuple of linear isometries on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
with pairwise orthogonal ranges. Given any
\alpha=a_{0}\cdots a_{\ell}
,
\beta=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell}
and
\omega=w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}
,
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
and
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1=\ell+1
, consider
\alpha^{\prime}=a_{0}\cdots a_{\ell-1}
and
\beta^{\prime}=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell-1}
. Then, if we define

L_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\omega}:=L_{a_{0},b_{0};w_{1}}\cdots L_{a_{\ell-1},b_{\ell-1};w_{\ell}},

notice that

E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}=L_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\omega}E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}\star e_{\emptyset}.

Analogously, we define the right creation operators on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
: For
1\leq a,b\leq n
,
1\leq w\leq d
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
obeying
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1
,

R_{a,b;w}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}:=E_{\alpha a,\beta b}\star e_{\omega w},

which also yields a family of linear isometries on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
with pairwise orthogonal ranges. Similarly to before, given
\alpha=a_{0}\cdots a_{\ell}
,
\beta=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell}

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}
,
\omega=w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
, let
\alpha^{\prime\prime}:=a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}
,
\beta^{\prime\prime}=b_{1}\cdots b_{\ell}
. Then, we have that

E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}=R_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime};\mathrm{t}},\beta^{{}^{\prime\prime};\mathrm{t}};\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}E_{a_{0},b_{0}}\star e_{\emptyset}.

(8.1)

It follows that if we define the “flip unitary”,
U_{\mathrm{t}}
, on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
by

E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle U_{\mathrm{t}}}}{{\mapsto}}\,E_{\alpha^{\mathrm{t}},\beta^{\mathrm{t}}}\star e_{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}},

then
U_{\mathrm{t}}
is a self-adjoint unitary (hence a unitary involution) obeying

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{t}}L_{a,b;w}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\alpha^{\mathrm{t}}a,\beta^{\mathrm{t}}b}\star e_{\omega^{\mathrm{t}}w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{a,b;w}U_{\mathrm{t}}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega},

so that
U_{\mathrm{t}}L_{a,b;w}U_{\mathrm{t}}=R_{a,b;w}
and the left and right creation operators are unitarily equivalent via
U_{\mathrm{t}}
.

Given
\zeta,\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}
and
\Xi\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
with
|\zeta|=|\xi|=|\Xi|=\ell
,
\zeta=z_{1}\cdots z_{\ell}
,
\xi=y_{1}\cdots y_{\ell}
and
\Xi=x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}
, we define

R_{\zeta,\xi;\Xi}:=R_{z_{1},y_{1};x_{1}}\cdots R_{z_{\ell},y_{\ell};x_{\ell}}.

Let
h\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
with

h=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}\hat{h}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\,E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega},

where
\alpha=a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}
,
\beta=b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{\ell}
and
\omega=w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}
. By taking inner products against elements of the orthonormal basis, one obtains

\displaystyle(R_{\zeta^{\mathrm{t}},\xi^{\mathrm{t}};\Xi^{\mathrm{t}}})^{*}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{z_{1},y_{1};x_{1}}^{*}\cdots R_{z_{\ell},y_{\ell};x_{\ell}}^{*}h=:(R^{*})_{\zeta,\xi;\Xi}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}\hat{h}_{\alpha\zeta,\beta\xi;\omega\Xi}\,E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}.

Alternatively,

\displaystyle(R_{\zeta^{\mathrm{t}},\xi^{\mathrm{t}};\Xi^{\mathrm{t}}})^{*}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}\hat{h}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\,(R^{*})_{\zeta,\xi;\Xi}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|\\
1\leq a,b\leq n\end{subarray}}\hat{h}_{a\alpha,b\beta;\omega}\,(R^{*})_{\zeta,\xi;\Xi}R_{\alpha^{\mathrm{t}},\beta^{\mathrm{t}};\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}E_{a,b}\star e_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha^{\prime}|=|\beta^{\prime}|=|\omega^{\prime}|+1\end{subarray}}\hat{h}_{\alpha^{\prime}\zeta,\beta^{\prime}\xi;\omega^{\prime}\Xi}E_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\star e_{\omega^{\prime}},

where the final line follows as

(R^{*})_{\zeta,\xi;\Xi}R_{\alpha^{\mathrm{t}},\beta^{\mathrm{t}};\omega^{\mathrm{t}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}R_{\gamma^{\mathrm{t}},\sigma^{\mathrm{t}};\rho^{\mathrm{t}}}&\mbox{if}\ \alpha^{\mathrm{t}}=\zeta^{\mathrm{t}}\gamma^{\mathrm{t}},\beta^{\mathrm{t}}=\xi^{\mathrm{t}}\sigma^{\mathrm{t}},\ \mbox{and}\ \omega^{\mathrm{t}}=\Xi^{\mathrm{t}}\rho^{\mathrm{t}},\ \gamma,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \rho\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},|\gamma|=|\sigma|=|\rho|,\\
(R^{*})_{\gamma,\sigma;\rho}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=\gamma\alpha,\xi=\sigma\beta,\ \mbox{and}\ \Xi=\rho\omega,\ \gamma,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \rho\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},|\gamma|=|\sigma|=|\rho|,\\
0&\mbox{else,}\end{array}\right.

and
R^{*}_{a,b;w}E_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\star e_{\emptyset}=0
for any
1\leq a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\leq n
and any
1\leq w\leq d
.

8.2
Evaluations

It will be convenient to view elements of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
as functions on the unit NC uniform row-ball centred at
0^{(n)}:=(0_{n},\cdots,0_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
,
\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(0^{(n)})
. Namely, for any
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
, define the linear
formal evaluation
map at
X
on the standard basis elements
E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}
by

E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}\ \mapsto\ I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{0},b_{0}}X_{w_{1}}I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{1},b_{1}}X_{w_{2}}\cdots I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{\ell-1},b_{\ell-1}}X_{w_{\ell}}I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}=:E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}(X).

The proposition below will show that this notion of “formal evaluation” extends to a continuous linear map on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
for any
X
in an NC uniform row-ball centred at
0_{n}
of radius
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Given
(X,y,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}\times\mathbb{C}^{mn}\times\mathbb{C}^{mn}
, the linear functional
\ell_{X,y,v}
defined by
E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}\mapsto y^{*}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}(X)v
extends to a bounded linear map on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
if
\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
.

Proof.

Consider the formal series,

K^{(n)}\{X,y,v\}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}\overline{y^{*}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}(X)v}\,E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}.

To prove that
\ell_{X,y,v}
is bounded, it suffices to show that
K^{(n)}\{X,y,v\}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
i.e.
that it has square–summable coefficients.

It suffices to consider
y:=x\otimes e_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
v:=u\otimes e_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell+1=|\beta|}\left|y^{*}E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}(X)v\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}\leq d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{0},\cdots,i_{\ell}\leq n\\
1\leq j_{0},\cdots,j_{\ell}\leq n\end{subarray}}u^{*}\otimes e_{j}^{*}I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{\ell},i_{\ell}}X_{k_{\ell}}^{*}I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{\ell-1},i_{\ell-1}}X_{k_{\ell-1}}^{*}\cdots I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{1},i_{1}}X_{k_{1}}^{*}I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{0},i_{0}}xx^{*}\otimes E_{i,i}

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes E_{i_{0},j_{0}}X_{k_{1}}I_{m}\otimes E_{i_{1},j_{1}}X_{k_{2}}\cdots X_{k_{\ell}}I_{m}\otimes E_{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}u\otimes e_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\cdots,i_{\ell}\\
j_{0},\cdots,j_{\ell-1}\end{subarray}}u^{*}\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}^{*}X_{k_{\ell}}^{*}\cdots I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{1},i_{1}}X_{k_{1}}^{*}I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{0},j_{0}}X_{k_{1}}I_{m}\otimes E_{i_{1},j_{1}}\cdots X_{k_{\ell}}u\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}^{2}\|x\|^{2}\sum_{k_{2},\cdots,k_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\cdots,i_{\ell}\\
j_{1},\cdots,j_{\ell-1}\end{subarray}}u^{*}\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}^{*}X_{k_{\ell}}^{*}\cdots X_{k_{2}}^{*}I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{1},j_{1}}X_{k_{2}}\cdots X_{k_{\ell}}u\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}.

Summing over the indices
i_{1}
and
j_{1}
then yields,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}^{2}\|x\|^{2}\sum_{k_{2},\cdots,k_{\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{2},\cdots,i_{\ell}\\
j_{2},\cdots,j_{\ell-1}\end{subarray}}u^{*}\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}^{*}X_{k_{\ell}}^{*}\cdots I_{m}\otimes E_{j_{2},i_{2}}X_{k_{2}}^{*}X_{k_{2}}I_{m}\otimes E_{i_{2},j_{2}}\cdots X_{k_{\ell}}u\otimes e_{i_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{\ell}\|X\|_{\mathrm{col}}^{2\ell}\|x\|^{2}\|u\|^{2}.

In particular, it follows that if
\|X\|^{2}_{\mathrm{col}}<\frac{1}{n}
, then
K^{(n)}\{X,x\otimes e_{i},u\otimes e_{j}\}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. It is then clear that inner products against this vector give the linear functional
\ell_{X,y,v}
, so that this linear functional is bounded on
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

Any
f\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
defines a graded and direct sum-preserving function in
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(0^{(n)})
via formal evaluation.

Remark 8.5
.

If we equip

.

It will be useful, in the sequel, to consider evaluations of

in the row-ball of radius
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
centred at

.

Definition 8.6
.

Given any
X\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{mn}(0^{(n)})
, we define the
formal evaluation
of

,

about

of

(8.2)

In what follows, we will be primarily interested in evaluation of

at

“about

”, and we will henceforth refer to

, defined above in Equation (

), as the
evaluation of

at

.

8.3
Matrix-centre realizations for elements of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})

We can now construct a matrix centre realization of
h
at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
in the following way. Firstly, for
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and

8.4

Let
f
be a uniformly analytic NC function in a uniformly open neighborhood of the matrix point
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
. In this section we will construct a matrix-centre realization at
Y
representing
f
in a uniformly open neighborhood of
Y
. Recall from Subsection
2.5
, for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a completely bounded
\ell-
linear map
f_{\ell}:\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}\to\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

\displaystyle f(X)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}f_{\ell}\circ\left(X-I_{m}\otimes Y\right)^{\odot^{\ell}};\quad\quad X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d},

where the series converges absolutely and uniformly in any NC column ball
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
of radius

, where

is the radius of convergence of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{f}^{Y}}=\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}},

and
\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}

2.5
and
[
KVV14
, Theorem 7.2, Theorem 8.11]
for more details.

To be more precise, the maps
f_{\ell}
are given by the NC difference–differential operators of

. As discussed in Subsection
2.5
, this can be further expanded as a series indexed by the free monoid:

\displaystyle f(X)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}f_{\omega}\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\omega|=\ell}f_{\omega}\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}},

\ell=|\omega|
-linear map defined by the partial difference–differential operators of
f
.
Moreover, following
[
KVV14
, Arguments following Remark 4.5]
, for every
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,
|\omega|=\ell
, there exist
N_{\omega}\in\mathbb{N}
and matrices
A_{\omega,a}^{(b)}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,

,

\displaystyle f_{\omega}\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}=\sum_{b=1}^{N_{\omega}}I_{m}\otimes A_{\omega,0}^{(b)}(X_{\omega_{1}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{\omega_{1}})I_{m}\otimes A_{\omega,1}^{(b)}\cdots(X_{\omega_{\ell}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{\omega_{\ell}})I_{m}\otimes A_{\omega,\ell}^{(b)}.

We expand this even further by decomposing the

as linear combinations of the standard matrix units. If

and we write

,
\beta=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell}
for

of length
\ell+1

\displaystyle f_{\omega}\circ(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{\odot^{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{0},b_{0}}(X_{w_{1}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{w_{1}})I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{1},b_{1}}\cdots(X_{w_{\ell}}-I_{m}\otimes Y_{w_{\ell}})I_{m}\otimes E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}

for appropriate coefficients

In particular, the
\ell=|\omega|-
linear map

is given by

\displaystyle f_{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},

\displaystyle(G_{1},\cdots,G_{\ell})\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\

and

In conclusion, for

Alternatively, we can identify

(8.3)

If the coefficients of this series are square summable, then

. In this case,

To see this, first consider

In the above we introduced the notation: For any

is the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{C}^{dn^{2}})
. For

and
1\leq k\leq d

Clearly,

Now apply the unitary

\widetilde{f}:=\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\

at an element

\displaystyle f_{a,b}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{w_{1},\cdots,w_{\ell}=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{0},\cdots,a_{\ell-1}\\

Lemma 8.7
.

Consider the tensor product
\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\otimes^{\ell}}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\|A\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell}})}\leq\|A\|_{h}
for all
A\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\otimes^{\ell}}
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\otimes^{\ell}}
and let
A^{(s)}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})^{m_{s-1}\times m_{s}}
,
1\leq s\leq\ell
with
m_{0}=m_{\ell}=1
such that

\displaystyle A=A^{(1)}\odot\cdots\odot A^{(\ell)}.

We then have

\displaystyle A=B^{(1)}\cdots B^{(\ell)},

where
B^{(s)}\in(\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\otimes^{\ell}})^{m_{s}\times m_{s-1}}
,
1\leq s\leq\ell
, and its entry at position
(i,j)
is given by

\displaystyle B^{(s)}_{i,j}=I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes A^{(s)}_{i,j}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I,

where
I
denotes the identity matrix of size
n\times n
and
A^{(s)}_{i,j}
appears at the
s
-th position. Note that
\|B^{(s)}\|=\|A^{(s)}\|
for all
1\leq s\leq\ell
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the respective operator norms. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell}})}\leq\|B^{(1)}\|\cdots\|B^{(\ell)}\|=\|A^{(1)}\|\cdots\|A^{(\ell)}\|.

Finally, taking the infimum over all such sets of matrices
A^{(0)},\dots,A^{(\ell)}
yields the desired inequality, by definition of the Haagerup tensor norm.
∎

Lemma 8.8
.

Given
f_{a,b}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\!\langle\,\mathfrak{z}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{z}_{dn^{2}}\rangle\!\rangle
with formal power series coefficients
\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}
as defined above in Equation (
8.5

C>0
such that for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\displaystyle\sqrt[2\ell]{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}}\leq C\sqrt[\ell]{\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}}.

Proof.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. The proof is broken down into several steps.

Step 1
: Fix a word
\omega=w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
of length
\ell
. In this step, we show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;E_{\alpha,\beta}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}^{2},

where
\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}}:=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
denotes the
\ell+1-
fold tensor product of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with itself.
Here, if
\alpha=a_{0}\cdots a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}
and
\beta=b_{0}\cdots b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}
, we denote

E_{\alpha,\beta}:=E_{a_{0},b_{0}}\otimes\dots\otimes E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\otimes^{\ell+1}}.

One can readily check that
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}=E_{\beta,\alpha}
and that
E_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\gamma,\sigma}=E_{\alpha,\sigma}\delta_{\beta,\gamma}
.
To prove the desired inequality, we start with the right hand side and first apply the

identity:

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;E_{\alpha,\beta}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|=|\beta^{\prime}|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\overline{\hat{f}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\omega}}\;E_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}^{*}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\overline{\hat{f}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta;\omega}}\;E_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}.

Observe that
E_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
is an

matrix with one of its entries being
1
and all other entries being

. This

will be on the diagonal of the matrix if and only if
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
. Moreover, note that for any matrix
Z
of size

\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\overline{\hat{f}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta;\omega}}\;E_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\alpha^{\prime}|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\overline{\hat{f}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta;\omega}}\;E_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}.

Step 2

[

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\

|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;E_{\alpha,\beta}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\left\|(G_{1},\dots,G_{\ell})\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;E_{a_{0},b_{0}}G_{1}E_{a_{1},b_{1}}\cdots G_{\ell}E_{a_{\ell},b_{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathrm{CB}}=\|f_{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}.

Step 3
: By
[
KVV14
, Equation (8.32)]
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}\leq\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}},

for all
\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
with
|\omega|=\ell
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}\|f_{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}^{2}=d^{\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}^{2}.

Finally, we can combine steps 1 through 3 to obtain the following.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\ell+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1\end{subarray}}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\;E_{\alpha,\beta}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n\otimes^{\ell+1}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}\|f_{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}^{2}\leq d^{\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}^{2}.

Multiplying both sides of the inequality by
n^{\ell+1}
and then taking the
2\ell
–th root of both sides completes the proof.
∎

Using the above lemma, we are now able to show that each of the power series
f_{a,b}
has a positive radius of convergence.

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
f
and
f_{a,b}
,
1\leq a,b\leq n
be as in Equation (
8.5
) above. Then the radius of convergence of each
f_{a,b}
(at
0
) is positive.

Proof.

Since
f
is assumed to be uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighborhood of
Y
, as discussed in Subsection
2.5
, the radius of convergence of its Taylor-Taylor series at
Y
will be positive, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{f}^{Y}}=\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}}<\infty.

Fix
1\leq a,b\leq n
, Lemma
8.8
then yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{a,b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[2\ell]{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha^{\prime}|=|\beta^{\prime}|=|\omega|=\ell\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha^{\prime}a,\beta^{\prime}b;\omega}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[2\ell]{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=\ell+1=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}|\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\|f_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{CB}}}<\infty,

where
R_{a,b}
denotes the radius of convergence of
f_{a,b}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{dn^{2}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})\cdot dn^{2}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we can now show that
f
has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
.

Theorem 8.10
.

Let
f
be an NC function that is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighborhood of the matrix point
Y
. Then
f
has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
.

Proof.

Let
f_{a,b}
,
1\leq a,b\leq n
, be defined as above in Equation (
8.5
). By Lemma
8.9
we can find
r>0
such that the dilation
f_{a,b}^{(r)}(X)=f_{a,b}(rX)
will have a radius of convergence strictly larger than
1
for all
1\leq a,b\leq n
. In particular,
f_{a,b}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{H}_{dn^{2}}^{\infty}
, and hence
f_{a,b}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{H}_{dn^{2}}^{2}
by Proposition
8.1
, for all
1\leq a,b\leq n
. Therefore, the coefficients of
f_{a,b}^{(r)}
are square-summable, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|\end{subarray}}|r^{|\omega|}\hat{f}_{\alpha a,\beta b;\omega}|^{2}<\infty.

This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|\end{subarray}}r^{|\omega|}\hat{f}_{\alpha a,\beta b;\omega}\;v_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{C}^{dn^{2}}),

for all
1\leq a,b\leq n
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n};\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|\end{subarray}}r^{|\omega|}\hat{f}_{\alpha a,\beta b;\omega}\;v_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\otimes E_{a,b}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{C}^{dn^{2}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}.

The image of this element under the unitary
U^{*}:\mathscr{F}(\mathbb{C}^{dn^{2}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\to\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, see (
8.4
), is given by

\displaystyle f_{r}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{n},\ \omega\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\\
|\alpha|=|\beta|=|\omega|+1\end{subarray}}r^{|\omega|}\hat{f}_{\alpha,\beta;\omega}\,E_{\alpha,\beta}\star e_{\omega}\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d}).

According to the arguments in Section
8.3
,
f_{r}
has a matrix-centre realization
(A^{\prime},b,c)_{Y}
, where
A^{\prime}=(A_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,A^{\prime}_{d})
with
A^{\prime}_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\to\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
1\leq k\leq d
,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, and

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{k}(Z)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}ZE_{i,j}\otimes R_{i,j;k}^{*}.

It is now readily checked that
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, where
A=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})=\frac{1}{r}A^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle A_{k}(Z)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}ZE_{i,j}\otimes R_{i,j;k}^{*},

will then be a matrix-centre realization for
f
at
Y
.
∎

Corollary 8.11
.

An NC function is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
if and only if
f
has a minimal matrix-centre realization at
Y
that obeys the linearized Lost Abbey conditions at
Y
.

Proof.

By the previous theorem, if
f
is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y
, then
f\sim_{Y}(\hat{A},\hat{b},\hat{c})
has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
. By the Kalman decomposition,
f
then has a minimal matrix-centre realization at
Y
,
f\sim(A,b,c)_{Y}
. By Theorem
6.1
,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
obeys the linearized Lost Abbey conditions at
Y
. Conversely, if
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
has a minimal matrix-centre realization at
Y
that obeys the linearized Lost Abbey conditions at
Y
, then
f
is a uniformly analytic NC function in the uniformly open set
r\cdot\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
with
r\geq\|A\|_{\mathrm{CB;row}}^{-1}
, again by Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 8.12
.

Given
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, consider the matricial Fock space,
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and consider the set of all
f\in\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
that define non-commutative functions via evaluation at
X\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
. Clearly this is a closed subspace of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. We can then define the
free Hardy space at
Y
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}(Y)
, as the closed subspace consisting of all elements of
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
that define non-commutative functions via evaluation in
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\mathbb{N}n}(Y)
. Let
P_{Y}:\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}(Y)
denote the orthogonal projection.

Since evaluation at any
X\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{B}^{d}_{mn}(Y)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, is a bounded linear homomorphism from
\mathscr{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
into
\mathbb{C}^{mn\times mn}
, it follows that
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}(Y)
is a non-commutative reproducing kernel Hilbert space (NC-RKHS) in the NC uniform row-ball of radius
\sqrt{n}^{-1}
centred at
Y

[
BMV16
]
. We plan to develop the theory of
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{d}(Y)
further in future work.

9
An application

In
[
KVV20
]
, Klep, Vinnikov and Volčič developed a local theory of non-commutative functions. In particular, they introduced the ring,
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
, of
uniformly analytic NC germs at
0
. That is, given uniformly analytic NC functions
f
and
g
, one says they are
locally evaluation equivalent at
0
,
f\sim_{0}g
, if their evaluations agree in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
0
. This is an equivalence relation and the
\sim_{0}
equivalence classes are then called uniformly analytic NC germs. By
[
KVV20
, Proposition 5.3]
,
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
is a
semi-free ideal ring
or
semifir
, which is a type of ring that admits a universal localization. Namely,
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
, has a
universal skew field of fractions
,
\mathscr{M}_{0}^{u}
, the
skew field of uniformly meromorphic NC germs at
0
. Elements of
\mathscr{M}_{0}^{u}
can be identified with certain “evaluation” equivalence classes of NC rational expressions composed with elements of
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
, see
[
KVV20
, Section 5.2 and Corollary 5.4]

A simple observation,
[
AMS25
, Lemma 3.2]
, shows that
f\in\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
if and only if it has a realization,
(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
so that
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u}
can be identified with
\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{B}}
,
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}
, the ring of all (uniformly analytic) NC functions which have realizations (centred at
0
). In general, given a set
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
, let
\mathscr{S}:=\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}
. Further consider the subsets of row
d-
tuples of linear operators,
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}
,
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}
and
\mathscr{T}_{p}:=\mathscr{T}_{p}(\mathcal{H})^{1\times d}
where
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the two-sided ideal of all finite-rank operators on
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the operator-norm closed two-sided ideal of all compact linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathscr{T}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the Schatten
p-
class operators on
\mathcal{H}
for
p\in[1,+\infty)
. The Schatten
p-
classes consist of the compact linear operators,
A
for which
\mathrm{tr}\,|A|^{p}<+\infty
and are also (non-closed) two-sided ideals in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
. As observed in
[
AMS25
, Corollary 6.3, Theorem 6.10]
, each of the sets
\mathscr{O}_{0}^{\mathscr{S}}
of uniformly analytic NC functions with realizations
(A,b,c)\in\mathscr{S}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
for
\mathscr{S}\in\{\mathscr{T}_{p},\mathscr{C}\}
are semifirs and we have the proper inclusions of semifirs,

\mathbb{C}_{0}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,=\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{F}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{T}_{p}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{T}_{q}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{C}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{B}}=\mathscr{O}_{0}^{u},

for
1\leq p<q<+\infty
. Here,
\mathbb{C}_{0}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,
denotes the semifir of all NC rational functions that are defined at
0
, and
\mathfrak{r}\in\mathbb{C}_{0}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,
if and only if it has a finite–dimensional realization. These proper inclusions also hold for the universal skew fields of fractions of these semifirs,

\mathbb{C}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,=\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{F}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{T}_{p}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{T}_{q}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{C}}\subsetneqq\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{B}}=\mathscr{M}_{0}^{u}.

The results in this paper readily imply that any NC function in one of these skew fields,
\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
, admits matrix-centre realizations that take values in the given class,
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})
, about any point
Y
in its uniformly open NC domain.

Corollary 9.1
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
,
\mathscr{S}\in\{\mathscr{F},\mathscr{T}_{p},\mathscr{C},\mathscr{B}\}
, If
Y\in\mathrm{Dom}_{n}\,f\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, then
f
has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
,
f\sim_{Y}(A,b,c)
that obeys
A_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

As described in
[
AMS25
, Section 6]
, following
[
KVV20
, Subsection 5.2]
, elements of
\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
can be identified as NC rational expressions composed with elements of
\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
. Namely, if
f\in\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
, then there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathfrak{r}\in\mathbb{C}\ \mathclap{\,<}{\left(\right.}\mathfrak{z}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{z}_{k}\mathclap{\,\,\,\,\,>}{\left.\right)}\,\,
and
g_{1},\cdots,g_{k}\in\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
so that
f=\mathfrak{r}(g_{1},\cdots,g_{k})
. The domain of
f
, is then the largest NC set on which the evaluations of
f
make sense. By definition, each
g_{i}\in\mathscr{O}_{d}^{\mathscr{S}}
has a realization (at
0
),
g_{i}\sim_{0}(A,b,c)
, with
A\in\mathscr{S}
. If
Y\in\mathrm{Dom}_{n}\,f
, then necessarily
Y\in\mathrm{Dom}_{n}\,g_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, and by the results of Section
5
and Theorem
5.2
, we can translate the realizations of
g_{i}
at
0
to obtain realizations of the
g_{i}
at
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
,
g_{i}\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
which obey
A^{\prime}_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})
, see Subsection
5.2
. Each of the sets
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})\in\{\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{H}),\mathscr{T}_{p}(\mathcal{H}),\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H}),\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\}
are two-sided ideals in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
that are closed under finite–rank perturbations and so it follows that each of the sets
\mathscr{S}
are closed under the application of the (FM or descriptor) realization algorithm for sums, products and inverses of NC functions with realizations about
Y
, see Subsection
7.1
. In conclusion,
f\sim_{Y}(\hat{A},\hat{b},\hat{c})
, with
\hat{A}_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

By Corollary
8.11
, any NC function,
f
, that is uniformly analytic in a uniformly open neighbourhood of a matrix point,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, has a matrix-centre realization at
Y
. This does not mean, however, that
f\in\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{B}}
, the skew field of uniformly meromorphic germs at
0
. Indeed,
[
KVV20
, Theorem 7.2]
shows that if
Y
is a semisimple point one can construct a uniformly analytic NC function,
f
, in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y
whose evaluations take values in nilpotent matrices of any fixed order. (Clearly, such an
f
cannot belong to any skew field.) On the other hand, any NC function that admits a finite–dimensional matrix-centre realization is necessarily an NC rational function. This raises the open question, posed to us by V. Vinnikov: If an NC function,
f
, has a matrix-centre realization,
(A,b,c)_{Y}
, taking values in compact linear operators, do we have that
f\in\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{C}}
? As described in
[
AMS25
]
,
\mathscr{M}_{d}^{\mathscr{C}}
is a natural non-commutative and multivariate generalization of the field of meromorphic functions in
\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
(A,b,c)_{Y}
and
(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})_{Z}
be two minimal and jointly compact matrix centre realizations about
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(m\times m)\cdot d}
and
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
that define the same uniformly analytic NC function on some uniformly open NC set. That is,
A_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H})
and
A^{\prime}_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. Then
\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{j}(A)=\mathscr{D}^{Z}_{k}(A^{\prime})
for any
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
so that
jm=kn
. Moreover, given any
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(jm\times jm)\cdot d}=\mathbb{C}^{(kn\times kn)\cdot d}
, the resolvent functions,
R_{X\otimes A}(\lambda)
and
R_{X\otimes A^{\prime}}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, have the same poles with the same orders.

In the above, if
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is any linear operator,
R_{T}(\lambda):=(\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}}-T)^{-1}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

By
[
AMS25
, Subsection 6.1 and Theorem 6.8]
,
\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{j}(A)
and
\mathscr{D}^{Z}_{k}(A^{\prime})
are both analytic Zariski dense and open sets, and hence are matrix-norm open, dense and connected. In particular, they have non-trivial intersection. If we fix a point
Y^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{j}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{Z}_{k}(A^{\prime})
, we can translate both realizations to
Y^{\prime}
to obtain two minimal, analytically equivalent and compact realizations at
Y^{\prime}
using the results of Section
5
. That is, we can assume, without loss of generality that
Z=Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
, and that
(A,b,c)\sim_{Y}(A^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
are analytically equivalent at
Y
,
i.e.
they define the same uniformly analytic NC function,
f
, in a uniformly open neighbourhood of
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{(n\times n)\cdot d}
.

Given any free polynomials,
p,q\in\mathbb{C}\langle\mathfrak{z}\rangle
and
X\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A^{\prime})
consider

f_{p,q}(X):=I_{m}\otimes b^{*}q(A)[\vec{G}]L_{A}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}p(A)[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c,

and

f_{p,q}^{\prime}(X)=I_{m}\otimes b^{{}^{\prime}*}q(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]L_{A^{\prime}}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}p(A^{\prime})[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c^{\prime}.

We claim that
f_{p,q}=f^{\prime}_{p,q}
. Indeed, replacing
X
by
X(\lambda):=\lambda(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)+I_{m}\otimes Y
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
of sufficiently small modulus,
X(\lambda)
will belong to a column-ball centred at
Y
of arbitrarily small radius so that
X(\lambda)\in\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A^{\prime})
. Then, by assumption,
f_{p,q}(X(\lambda))=f_{p,q}^{\prime}(X(\lambda))
. However,

A(X(\lambda)-I_{m}\otimes Y)=\lambda A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y),

and
A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
takes values in compact operators, and similarly so does
A^{\prime}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
. Hence,
\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A)\cap\mathscr{D}^{Y}_{m}(A^{\prime})
is open and path-connected, and it follows from the identity theorem that
f_{p,q}(X)=f_{p,q}^{\prime}(X)
.

Fix an arbitrary
X\in\mathbb{C}^{(mn\times mn)\cdot d}
, let
X(\lambda):=\lambda(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)+I_{m}\otimes Y
, as before, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and consider

L_{A}(X(\lambda)-I_{m}\otimes Y)=I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-\lambda A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y),

as well as
L_{A^{\prime}}(X(\lambda)-I_{m}\otimes Y)
. Then, if we set
z=\lambda^{-1}
,

R_{A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)}(z)=(zI_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{-1}=\lambda L_{A}(X(\lambda)-I_{m}\otimes Y)^{-1}.

Since
A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
and
A^{\prime}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)
are compact,
R(z):=R_{A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)}(z)
and
R^{\prime}(z):=R_{A^{\prime}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)}(z)
are meromorphic operator-valued functions in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Hence, if
X\notin\mathscr{D}_{m}^{Y}(A)
, then
z=1
is a pole of finite order for
R(z)
.
Let
\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be the orders of the poles of
R(z)
and
R^{\prime}(z)
at
z=1
. For any free polynomials,
p,q
, consider the matrix-valued meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle g_{p,q}(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle z^{-1}f_{p,q}(X(z^{-1}))=I_{m}\otimes b^{*}p(A)[\vec{G}]R(z)q(A)[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{{}^{\prime}*}p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]R^{\prime}(z)q(A^{\prime})[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c^{\prime}.

Then, since
g_{p,q}
has a pole of order at most
\ell
at
z=1
, for sufficiently small
r>0
,

0=\ointctrclockwise_{r\cdot\partial\mathbb{D}(1)}(z-1)^{\ell}g_{p,q}(z)dz,

and then by the Riesz–Dunford holomorphic functional calculus,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ointctrclockwise_{r\cdot\partial\mathbb{D}(1)}(1-z)^{\ell}g_{p,q}(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{*}p(A)[\vec{G}]\,\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ointctrclockwise_{r\cdot\partial\mathbb{D}(1)}(z-1)R_{A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y)}(z)dz}_{(I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{\ell}E_{1}(A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))}\,q(A)[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{m}\otimes b^{{}^{\prime}*}p(A^{\prime})[\vec{G}]\,(I_{m}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}-A^{\prime}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))^{\ell}E_{1}(A^{\prime}(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))\,q(A^{\prime})[\vec{H}]I_{m}\otimes c^{\prime},

for any tuples of
mn\times mn
matrices,
\vec{G},\vec{H}
. In the above,
E_{1}(A(X-I_{m}\otimes Y))
denotes the Riesz idempotent corresponding to the isolated eigenvalue,
\lambda=1
, of the compact linear operator

∎

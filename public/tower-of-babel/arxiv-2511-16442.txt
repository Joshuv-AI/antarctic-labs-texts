Abstract
. Although the theory of self-affine tiles and the theory of Rauzy fractals are quite different from each other, they have some common features. Both, self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals have tiling properties and these tiling properties can be checked and described by certain graphs, so-called contact graphs and neighbor graphs . The contact graph is often quite easy to construct, but only the neighbor graph contains full information on the overlaps of the tiles in the presumed tiling. In the present paper we establish an algorithm that allows to construct the neighbor graph starting from the contact graph. Such an algorithm is already known in the case of self-affine tiles. In the present paper we give a simplified proof of this algorithm that can be extended to the case of Rauzy fractals. Our algorithms are more efficient than naïve algorithms for the construction of the neighbor graph.



Introduction

In the present paper we study self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals. For both of these objects, there exists a vast literature. The foundations of the theory of self-affine tiles were laid by Kenyon
[
28
]
, Gröchenig and Haas
[
23
]
, as well as Lagarias and Wang
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
in the 1990s and since then these objects have been studied extensively. Important topics include periodic tiling properties
[
33
]
, the characterization of digit sets
[
4
,
35
,
36
]
, topological questions
[
18
,
19
,
45
]
, relations to Fuglede’s conjecture
[
37
]
, as well as relations to wavelets
[
22
,
25
]
. Rauzy fractals go back to Rauzy
[
39
]
and were studied in a more general framework in
[
6
]
. They play a role in many branches of mathematics like for instance in the theory of aperiodic order
[
7
,
8
]
, in the theory of symbolic dynamical systems
[
2
,
14
,
27
]
and in number theory
[
1
,
13
,
15
]
. Also, Rauzy fractals often admit different kinds of tilings
[
27
]
, such as a periodic tiling and a self-replicating tiling.

Although the theory for self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals have been developped in different directions, they have common features. As already mentioned, both, self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals, often admit tilings. And even more, in both cases, so-called
contact graphs
and
neighbor graphs
can be used in order to check tiling properties. Contact graphs are defined in
[
23
]
for the case of self-affine tiles and in
[
41
]
for the case of Rauzy fractals. In
[
33
]
a Fourier transform of the contact graph was used in order to establish a general tiling result for self-affine tiles. The analogue of such a tiling result remains open for Rauzy fractals and is related to the famous
Pisot conjecture

[
2
]
, although there exist many partial results (see for instance
[
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
). We therefore think that it is interesting to look at contact and neighbor graphs for self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals at the same time, and we will do this in the present paper. Although we do not think that the methods leading to the general tiling result of
[
33
]
can be carried over to the case of Rauzy fractals, we hope that analogies between the tiling theory of self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals can help to shed some light on the Pisot conjecture.

For self-affine tiles as well as for Rauzy fractals it is fairly easy to show that they give rise to multi-tilings. To show that these multi-tilings are actually tilings turns out to be the hard part. To this end, one has to show that the tiles do not overlap in sets of positive measure. Since these overlaps are captured by the contact graph and, even more precisely, by the neighbor graph associated to the self-affine tile or Rauzy fractal, these graphs naturally play a role when it comes to tiling properties (see
[
23
,
41
]
). The contact graph is often considerably smaller and easier to construct than the neighbor graph. However, only the neighbor graph contains full information on the overlaps. For this reason we developed an algorithm that allows to construct the neighbor graph from the contact graph in the case of self-affine tiles
[
40
]
. This algorithm is useful in order to characterize topological properties of self-affine tiles
[
3
,
43
]
because, contrary to naïve algorithms, it can be used to obtain the neighbor graph for whole classes of self-affine tiles. In the present paper we provide a simplified proof of this algorithm that can be extended to the case of Rauzy fractals. We think that our new algorithm for Rauzy fractals can be used to characterize “neighbors” for whole classes of Rauzy fractals and forms the basis for getting topological information on Rauzy fractals in dimension
3
and higher. Even for single examples like the natural generalization of Rauzy’s classical “tribonacci substitution” studied in
[
20
]
it would be interesting to prove topological results.

We mention that, according to
[
42
]
, Rauzy fractals are solutions of graph directed iterated function systems in the sense of Mauldin and Williams
[
38
]
. Thus, one could ask if our results can be extended to more general tiles that are solutions of graph directed iterated function systems, like for instance the ones studied in
[
24
,
34
]
. However, our methods rely on an additional structure of Rauzy fractals that is not present in the case of more general tiles.

1.2.
The neighbor graph and the contact graph

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(M,\mathcal{D})
be a self-affine tile with standard digit set. For
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
let
\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]:=\mathcal{T}\cap(\mathcal{T}+\mathbf{m})
be the “overlap” of
\mathcal{T}
with its translate by
\mathbf{m}
. The
neighbor set

S
of
\mathcal{T}
is the set of all nonzero
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-translates
\mathbf{m}
for which the overlap
\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]
is nonempty,
i.e.
,

S=\big\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\;:\;\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]\neq\emptyset\big\}.

The set
S
is finite by compactness of
\mathcal{T}
. The terminology “neighbor set” is geometrically justified because if
\{\mathcal{T}+\mathbf{z}\colon\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
forms a tiling, then
S
consists of all translates
\mathbf{m}
for which
\mathcal{T}+\mathbf{m}
“touches”
\mathcal{T}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{T}
via the set equation (
1.1
) it turns out that

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]=\mathcal{T}\cap(\mathcal{T}+\mathbf{m})&=M^{-1}(\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{D})\cap M^{-1}(\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{D}+M\mathbf{m})\\
&=M^{-1}\bigcup_{\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{S}[M\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{d}^{\prime}-\mathbf{d}]+\mathbf{d})\end{split}

holds for each
\mathbf{m}\in S
. Thus
(\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}])_{\mathbf{m}\in S}
is the unique vector of nonempty compact sets that is given by the graph-directed iterated function system (in the sense of
[
38
]
)

(1.2)

\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]=M^{-1}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D},\,\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in S\\
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}=M\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{d}^{\prime}-\mathbf{d}\end{subarray}}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}^{\prime}]+\mathbf{d})\qquad(\mathbf{m}\in S).

If
\{\mathcal{T}+\mathbf{z}\colon\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
forms a tiling, the boundary of
\mathcal{T}
can be written as a union of these overlaps, in particular,

\partial\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}].

For each
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define a graph
\Gamma_{A}
in the following way. The vertices of
\Gamma_{A}
are the elements of
A
and there exists a labeled edge
\mathbf{m}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{d}|\mathbf{d}^{\prime}}\mathbf{m}^{\prime}

\mathbf{m}\in A
to
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in A
with label
\mathbf{d}|\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
if and only if

\mathbf{m}^{\prime}=M\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{d}^{\prime}-\mathbf{d}.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the neighbor set defined above. Then we call the graph
\Gamma_{S}
the
neighbor graph
of
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(M,\mathcal{D})
(
cf.

[
40
]
). Hence, the set equation (
1.2
) can be written as

\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}]=M^{-1}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D},\,\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in S\\
\mathbf{m}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{d}|\mathbf{d}^{\prime}}\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{S}\end{subarray}}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbf{m}^{\prime}]+\mathbf{d})\qquad(\mathbf{m}\in S).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(M,\mathcal{D})
be a self-affine tile with standard digit set. Then Algorithm

terminates after finitely many steps and has the neighbor graph

of

as its output.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{R_{1}}=\Gamma_{R}
and
\Gamma_{R_{q}}=\mathop{Red}(\Gamma_{R_{q-1}+R})

We prove by induction that

contains all infinite walks

having

.

Let

\Gamma_{S\cup\{\mathbf{0}\}}

is a walk whose nodes are contained in

is a walk in
\Gamma_{R}
and, hence, in
\Gamma_{R_{1}}

To prove the induction step we assume that
\Gamma_{R_{q-1}}

\Gamma_{S\cup\{\mathbf{0}\}}
having contact degree less than or equal to

. Let

and by the induction hypothesis we know that there exist infinite walks

. Because

2.
Pisot substitutions, Rauzy fractals, and self-replicating tilings

2.1.
Substitutions

For
d\geq 2
we fix the alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,d\}
and write
\mathcal{A}^{*}
for the free monoid of finite words over
\mathcal{A}
with concatenation. For
w\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
we denote by
|w|
the number of letters in
w
and call it the
length
of
w
. The word of length
0
is called the
empty word
and we denote it by
\epsilon
. For
i\in\mathcal{A}
, we write
|w|_{i}
for the number of occurrences of the letter
i
in
w
. We define the
abelianization mapping

\mathbf{l}:\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathbb{N}^{d}
by
\mathbf{l}(w)=(|w|_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
.

A
substitution
is an endomorphism of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
having the property that each letter has a nonempty image. To a substitution
\sigma
we associate the
incidence matrix

M
defined as the unique matrix
M
satisfying
\mathbf{l}(\sigma(i))=M\mathbf{l}(i)
for each
i\in\mathcal{A}
. If the characteristic polynomial of
M
is the minimal polynomial of a Pisot unit, we call
\sigma
a
Pisot substitution
.

2.2.
The Rauzy fractal and its subtiles

We give a definition of the Rauzy fractal related to a Pisot substitution. We do this very briefly, for more details on Rauzy fractals we refer for instance to
[
6
,
14
,

,

]

Definition 2.1

(Prefix-suffix graph;
cf. e.g.

[
17
]
)
.

Let
\sigma
be a substitution over the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
and let

The

of

with a directed edge from
i\in\mathcal{A}
to

labeled by

if and only if
\sigma(j)=pis
.

.

2.3.
The self-replicating tiling set and dual substitutions

It turns out that subtiles of Rauzy fractals induce multi-tilings. These multi-tilings will have a self-replicating structure that is governed by the underlying substitution. In particular, let
\sigma
be a Pisot substitution over the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
and use the notations from above. Let
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
be the standard basis vectors. The
stepped hypersurface
associated to
\sigma
is given by

H_{\sigma}=\big\{[\mathbf{x},i]\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathcal{A}\;:\;0\leq\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\rangle<\langle\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{v}\rangle\big\}

(see
e.g.

[
27
, Equation (2.2)]
; recall that
\mathbf{v}
is the left eigenvector of the incidence matrix
M
of
\sigma
). A pair
[\mathbf{x},i]\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathcal{A}
is called a
face
and can be regarded as a point of “color”

. However, we can also interpret a face
[\mathbf{x},i]
as the cube given by

(2.2)

[\mathbf{x},i]=\{x+\vartheta_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{i-1}\mathbf{e}_{i-1}+\vartheta_{i+1}\mathbf{e}_{i+1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{d}\mathbf{e}_{d}\;:\;\vartheta_{j}\in[0,1]\text{ for }j\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{i\}\}.

If we project the stepped hypersurface onto
\mathbf{v}^{\bot}
we get the
self-replicating translation set

\pi H_{\sigma}=\big\{[\pi(\mathbf{x}),i]\;:\;[\mathbf{x},i]\in H_{\sigma}\big\}.

The “projected face”
[\pi(\mathbf{x}),i]
can be regarded as the parallelotope

[\pi(\mathbf{x}),i]=\pi\big(\big\{\mathbf{x}+\vartheta_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{i-1}\mathbf{e}_{i-1}+\vartheta_{i+1}\mathbf{e}_{i+1}+\cdots+\vartheta_{d}\mathbf{e}_{d}\;:\;\vartheta_{j}\in[0,1]\text{ for }j\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{i\}\big\}\big).

These parallelotopes are analogs of the fundamental mesh of a lattice and its translates in the sense that they tesselate
\mathbf{v}^{\bot}
(see for instance
[
5
,
16
]
).

3.1.

Let

\mathcal{C}_{0}

for some

. To achieve this, in
[

certain graphs are defined. Before we recall their definition we want to convey the idea of their construction.

We will use the following notation. Given an element
[i,\mathbf{x},j]\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathcal{A}
we set

-[i,\mathbf{x},j]=[j,-\mathbf{x},i]

and write
\pm K=K\cup(-K)
for

.

By the defining set equation (
2.1
) for subtiles, for each
[i,\mathbf{x},j]\in\mathcal{A}\times H_{\sigma}
we may write

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(i)\cap(\mathcal{R}(j)+\pi(\mathbf{x}))&=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma(i_{1})=p_{1}is_{1}\\
\sigma(j_{1})=q_{1}jt_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{h}\Big(\big(\mathcal{R}(i_{1})+\mathbf{h}^{-1}\pi\mathbf{l}(p_{1})\big)\cap\big(\mathcal{R}(j_{1})+\mathbf{h}^{-1}\pi(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})+\mathbf{x})\big)\Big)\\
&=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma(i_{1})=p_{1}is_{1}\\
\sigma(j_{1})=q_{1}jt_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{h}\Big(\mathcal{R}(i_{1})\cap\big(\mathcal{R}(j_{1})+\mathbf{h}^{-1}\pi(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x})\big)\Big)+\pi\mathbf{l}(p_{1}).\end{split}

If we set

this implies that

(3.1)

\mathcal{B}[i,\mathbf{x},j]=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma(i_{1})=p_{1}is_{1}\\
\sigma(j_{1})=q_{1}jt_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[i_{1},M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),j_{1}]+\pi\mathbf{l}(p_{1}).

Thus the nonempty intersections

are solutions of a graph directed iterated function system. Because
[M^{-1}\mathbf{l}(p_{1}),i_{1}]\in\sigma^{*}[\mathbf{0},i]

[M^{-1}\mathbf{l}(p_{1}),i_{1}],[M^{-1}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{l}(q_{1})),j_{1}]\in H_{\sigma}

this implies that
[i_{1},M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),j_{1}]\in\pm(\mathcal{A}\times H_{\sigma})

We have two options in the set equation (

. Thus, whenever
[M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),j_{1}]\not\in\mathcal{A}\times H_{\sigma}

\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[i_{1},M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),j_{1}]+\pi\mathbf{l}(p_{1})=\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[j_{1},-M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),i_{1}]+\mathbf{x}+\pi\mathbf{l}(q_{1})

we replace
\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[i_{1},M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),j_{1}]+\pi\mathbf{l}(p_{1})
by
\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[j_{1},-M^{-1}(\mathbf{l}(q_{1})-\mathbf{l}(p_{1})+\mathbf{x}),i_{1}]+\mathbf{x}+\pi\mathbf{l}(q_{1})
on the right hand side of the set equation (

with

by
\mathbf{h}\mathcal{B}[j,\mathbf{0},i]

occurring in this modified set equation belong to the set

used in Definition

Secondly, we can leave the identity (

involved are contained in the larger set

.

For both of these options, in order to set up the graph directed iterated function system, we need to determine which of the sets

are nonempty. This is done with the help of the graphs that we will define in the Sections

The second option makes the definition of the graphs easier and is better suited for theoretical considerations. For this reason, we will provide the details for both options.

3.2.
The self-replicating boundary graph and the contact graph

We start with the first option indicated in Section

Definition 3.1

(Ambient graph,

4.

4.1.

Our goal is to show that by forming

from

Algorithm 2

(Construction of the self-replicating neighbor graph of a Pisot substitution

0:

Contact graph
G_{C}

0:

Neighbor graph
G_{B}

p\leftarrow 1

A[1]\leftarrow\hat{G}_{C}

repeat

p\leftarrow p+1

A[p]\leftarrow\mathop{Red}
(

-Corona
(A[p-1]))

until

A[p]=A[p-1]

\hat{G}_{B}\leftarrow A[p]

Recall that we saw in Section

and

are equivalent to

and

, respectively.

4.2.

In this section we prove that Algorithm

In the setting of self-affine tiles with standard digit set we used that the contact set

Lemma 4.1

Proof.

The proof draws on results from
[

.

[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}],[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}],[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}],[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]\in H_{\sigma}
be given in a way that
[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]\in\sigma^{*}[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}]
and
[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]\in\sigma^{*}[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}]
. Then

[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]\overset{q}{\sim}[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]
implies
[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}]\overset{q}{\sim}[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}]
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the lemma for
q=1
. The general case immediately follows from this by induction.
Let
q=1
and
[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}],[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}],[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}],[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]
be as in the statement of the lemma.

By definition, the relation
[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]\overset{1}{\sim}[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]
implies that
[j_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}-\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{2}]\in\pm C\cup\{[i,\mathbf{0},i];i\in\mathcal{A}\}
.
Because
[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]\in\sigma^{*}[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}]
and
[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]\in\sigma^{*}[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}]
There exist
p_{1},p_{2},s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that

(4.1)

\begin{split}[\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]=\big[M^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{l}(p_{1})),j_{1}\big]\quad\text{with }\sigma(j_{1})=p_{1}i_{1}s_{1},\\
[\mathbf{y}_{2},j_{2}]=\big[M^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{2}+\mathbf{l}(p_{2})),j_{2}\big]\quad\text{with }\sigma(j_{2})=p_{2}i_{2}s_{2}.\end{split}

By the definition the simple ambient graph, (
4.1
) implies that

[i_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{2}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{1})}[j_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}-\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{2}]

is an edge in the simple ambient graph. Thus, because
[j_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}-\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{2}]\in\pm C
, by the definition of the simple contact graph
[i_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{2}]\in\pm C
and, hence,
[\mathbf{x}_{1},i_{1}]\overset{1}{\sim}[\mathbf{x}_{2},i_{2}]
.
∎

The following result is a version of Lemma
1.4
in the setting of Pisot substitutions. For two finite words
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, we write
w_{2}\prec w_{1}
if
w_{2}
is a prefix of
w_{1}
(
w_{1}=w_{2}w_{3}
for some
w_{3}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
).

Lemma 4.4
.

The following assertions are equivalent.

•

In
\hat{G}_{B}
there exists a walk

w:\;[i_{0},\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{0})|\mathbf{l}(q_{0})}[i_{1},\mathbf{m}_{1},j_{1}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{1})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{k-1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{k-1})}[i_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k},j_{k}]

with
[\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]\in H_{\sigma}
and
\mathrm{cdeg}(w)\leq q
.

•

There exist sequences
([\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
and
([\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
in
H_{\sigma}
, where
[\mathbf{a}_{0},i_{0}]=[\mathbf{0},i_{0}]
,
[\mathbf{b}_{0},j_{0}]=[\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]
, and

(4.2)

\begin{split}[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]&=\big[M^{-1}(\mathbf{a}_{\ell-1}+\mathbf{l}(p_{\ell-1})),i_{\ell}\big]\in(\sigma^{*})^{\ell}[\mathbf{0},i_{0}]\quad\text{and}\\
[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]&=\big[M^{-1}(\mathbf{b}_{\ell-1}+\mathbf{l}(q_{\ell-1})),j_{\ell}\big]\in(\sigma^{*})^{\ell}[\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]\end{split}

with
\mathbf{m}_{\ell}=\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell}
,
p_{\ell-1}i_{\ell-1}\prec\sigma(i_{\ell})
and
q_{\ell-1}j_{\ell-1}\prec\sigma(j_{\ell})
, and such that
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]\overset{q}{\sim}[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
(
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
).

Proof.

This follows easily by induction on
k
and by using the definition of the edges of
\hat{G}_{B}

∎

This will enable us to prove an analog of Proposition
1.4
in the setting of Rauzy fractals.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
w:\;m_{0}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}m_{1}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}m_{2}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}}\cdots
be a walk in
\hat{G}_{B}
with
\mathrm{cdeg}(w)>1
. Then there exist walks

w_{1}:\;m_{0}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}^{(1)}}m_{1}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}^{(1)}}m_{2}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}^{(1)}}\cdots\quad\hbox{and}\quad w_{2}:\;m_{0}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}^{(2)}}m_{1}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}^{(2)}}m_{2}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}^{(2)}}\cdots

in
\hat{G}_{B}
,
satisfying
\mathrm{cdeg}(w_{1})<\mathrm{cdeg}(w)
, and
\mathrm{cdeg}(w_{2})=1
, such that
the connection
m_{k}
can be decomposed into the connections
m_{k}^{(1)}
and
m_{k}^{(2)}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
w|_{k}
be the walk consisting of the first
k
edges of
w
and
write out the nodes and labels of the walk
w|_{k}
by

w|_{k}:\;[i_{0},\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{0})|\mathbf{l}(q_{0})}[i_{1},\mathbf{m}_{1},j_{1}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{1})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{k-1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{k-1})}[i_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k},j_{k}].

W.l.o.g. we assume that
[\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]\in H_{\sigma}
(otherwise we just consider
[-\mathbf{m}_{0},i_{0}]
instead). Lemma
4.4
implies that for each
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
there exist faces
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]\in(\sigma^{\ell})^{*}[\mathbf{0},i_{0}]
and
[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]\in(\sigma^{\ell})^{*}[\mathbf{m}_{0},j_{0}]
as defined in (
4.2
) that satisfy
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]\overset{q}{\sim}[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
with
q\geq\mathrm{cdeg}(w)\geq 2
. Thus by Lemma
4.4
the walk
w|_{k}
can be written as

w|_{k}:\;[i_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}-\mathbf{a}_{0},j_{0}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{0})|\mathbf{l}(q_{0})}[i_{1},\mathbf{b}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{1},j_{1}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{1})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{k-1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{k-1})}[i_{k},\mathbf{b}_{k}-\mathbf{a}_{k},j_{k}].

with
[\mathbf{a}_{k},i_{k}]\overset{q}{\sim}[\mathbf{b}_{k},j_{k}]
. Moreover, there exists
[\mathbf{y}_{k},h_{k}]\in H_{\sigma}
such that
[\mathbf{a}_{k},i_{k}]\overset{q-1}{\sim}[\mathbf{y}_{k},h_{k}]\overset{1}{\sim}[\mathbf{b}_{k},j_{k}]
. By Lemma
2.7
there exists
[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
such that
[\mathbf{y}_{k},h_{k}]\in(\sigma^{k-\ell})^{*}[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
. By the definition of
\sigma^{*}
this implies that

(4.3)

[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]=\big[M^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_{\ell-1}+t_{\ell-1}),h_{\ell}\big]\in(\sigma^{*})^{\ell}[\mathbf{y}_{0},h_{0}],

where
t_{\ell-1}h_{\ell-1}\prec\sigma(h_{\ell})
for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.3
for all
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
we have
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]\overset{q-1}{\sim}[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]\overset{1}{\sim}[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
. Thus, applying Lemma
4.4
to
([\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
and
([\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
we see that there exists a walk

w_{1,k}:\;[i_{0},\mathbf{y}_{0}-\mathbf{a}_{0},h_{0}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{0})|\mathbf{l}(t_{0})}[i_{1},\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{1},h_{1}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{1})|\mathbf{l}(t_{1})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(p_{k-1})|\mathbf{l}(t_{k-1})}[i_{k},\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{a}_{k},h_{k}]

with
\mathrm{cdeg}(w_{1,k})\leq q-1
. We know that
[\mathbf{b}_{0}-\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{0}]\in\pm H_{\sigma}
. Assume that we have
[\mathbf{b}_{0}-\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{0}]\in H_{\sigma}
(the other case can be treated analogously). Then, shifting by
-M^{-\ell}\mathbf{y}_{0}
and using the definition of
\sigma^{*}
we gain

[\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-M^{-\ell}\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{\ell}]\in(\sigma^{*})^{\ell}[\mathbf{b}_{0}-\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{0}]\quad\hbox{and}\quad[\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-M^{-\ell}\mathbf{y}_{0},h_{\ell}]\in(\sigma^{*})^{\ell}[\mathbf{0},h_{0}].

Thus we may apply Lemma
4.4
to
([\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-M^{-\ell}\mathbf{y}_{0},h_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
and
([\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-M^{-\ell}\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{\ell}])_{0\leq\ell\leq k}
to exhibit a walk

w_{2,k}:\;[h_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}-\mathbf{y}_{0},j_{0}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(t_{0})|\mathbf{l}(q_{0})}[h_{1},\mathbf{b}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{1},j_{1}]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(t_{1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{1})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathbf{l}(t_{k-1})|\mathbf{l}(q_{k-1})}[h_{k},\mathbf{b}_{k}-\mathbf{y}_{k},j_{k}]

with
\mathrm{cdeg}(w_{2,k})=1
. For
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
consider the nodes
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
,
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
, and
[h_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{y}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
. Then
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
connects
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]
and
[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
. Since
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
connects
[\mathbf{a}_{\ell},i_{\ell}]
and
[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]\in H_{\sigma}
and
[h_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{y}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
connects
[\mathbf{y}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
and
[\mathbf{b}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
, we conclude that
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
can be decomposed into
[i_{\ell},\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-\mathbf{a}_{\ell},h_{\ell}]
and
[h_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}-\mathbf{y}_{\ell},j_{\ell}]
. Thus we proved the lemma for walks of length
k
.

However, since a given node of
w
can have only finitely many decompositions in two connections of contact degree
\mathrm{cdeg}(w)-1
and
1
, respectively, by a Cantor diagonal argument we may choose
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
as asserted in the lemma for all (infinitely many) edges. This finishes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove that Algorithm
2
yields the neighbor graph after finitely many steps.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\sigma
be a Pisot substitution. Then Algorithm
2
terminates after finitely many steps and has the self-replicating neighbor graph
G_{B}
of
\sigma
as its output.

Proof.

Let
A[1]=\hat{G}_{C}
and
A[p]=\mathop{Red}(C\text{-Corona}(A[p-1]))
for
p>1
. We prove by induction that
A[p]
contains all infinite walks
w
of
\hat{G}_{B}
having
\mathop{cdeg}(w)\leq p
.

Let
w
be an infinite walk in
\hat{G}_{B}
with
\mathop{cdeg}(w)=1
then, by definition,
w
is a walk whose nodes are contained in
\pm C
. Thus
w
is a walk in
\hat{G}_{C}
and, hence, in
A[1]
. This constitutes the induction start.

To prove the induction step we assume that
A[p-1]
consists of all infinite walks of
\hat{G}_{B}
having contact degree less than or equal to
p-1
. Let
w:\;m_{0}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}m_{1}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}m_{2}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}}\cdots
be an infinite walk in
\hat{G}_{B}
having
\mathop{cdeg}(w)\leq p
. Then, by Proposition
4.5
and by the induction hypothesis we know that there exist infinite walks
w_{1}:\;m_{0}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}^{(1)}}m_{1}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}^{(1)}}m_{2}^{(1)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}^{(1)}}\cdots\in A[p-1]
and
w_{2}:\;m_{0}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}^{(2)}}m_{1}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}^{(2)}}m_{2}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}^{(2)}}\cdots\in A[1]
such that
m_{k}=[i_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k},j_{k}]
can be decomposed into the connections
m_{k}^{(1)}=[i_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k}^{(1)},h_{k}]
and
m_{k}^{(2)}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
m_{k}^{(2)}\in\pm C
, by possibly replacing
m_{k}^{(1)}
by its negative (which is still an element of
A[p-1]
), we have

[\mathbf{m}_{k},j_{k}]\overset{1}{\sim}[\mathbf{m}_{k}^{(1)},h_{k}]

and, hence,
m_{k}=[i_{k},\mathbf{m}_{k},j_{k}]\in(C\text{-Corona}(A[p-1])
. Because
k
was arbitrary,
w
is an infinite walk in
C
-Corona
(A[p-1])
and, therefore, an infinite walk in
A[p]=\mathop{Red}(C\text{-Corona}(A[p-1]))
.

Since by Lemma
4.2
there is
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that each walk
w
in
\hat{G}_{B}
satisfies
\mathop{cdeg}(w)\leq q
, we conclude that
A[q+1]=A[q]
. Thus the algorithm terminates after finitely many steps and returns
\hat{G}_{B}
, and, hence,
G_{B}
, as desired.
∎

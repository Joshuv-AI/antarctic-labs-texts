Hyperelastic stability landscape: A check for Hill stability of isotropic, incompressible hyperelasticity depending on material parameters
Herbert Baaser, University of Applied Sciences Bingen, Germany

Abstract
In this paper, we describe a uniform and standardized approach for analytically verifying the stability of isotropic, incompressible hyperelastic material models. Here, we address stability as fulfillment of the Hill condition – i.e. the positive definiteness of the material modulus in the Kirchhoff stress – log–strain relation. For incompressible material behavior, all mathematically and mechanically possible deformations lie within a range bounded, on the one hand, by uniaxial states and, on the other hand, by biaxial states; shear deformation states lie in between. This becomes particularly clear when the possible states are represented in the invariant plane. This very representation is now also used to visualize the regions of unstable material behavior depending on the selected strain energy function and the respective data set of material parameters. This demonstrates how, for some constellations of energy functions, with appropriate selection or calibration of parameters, stable and unstable regions can be observed. If such cases occur, it is no longer legitimate to use them to initiate, for example, finite element simulations. This is particularly striking when, for example, a fit appears stable in uniaxial tension, but the same parameter set for shear states results in unstable behavior without this being specifically investigated. The presented approach can reveal simple indicators for this. Nevertheless, the simple shear deformation, where the principal axes lag behind the deformation γ = tan ⁡ α \gamma=\tan\alpha of the shear angle α \alpha , i.e. the rotation tensor R ≠ I \hbox{\bf R}\neq\hbox{\bf I} , still represents a special case that requires extra investigations. This is especially true given that all shear components of the logarithmic strains themselves exhibit a non–monotonic behavior with respect to the deformation angle.

2.1
Aspects of
Hill
’s inequality

2.1.1
The compressible case

For a better understanding of the elastic stability concept considered here, it will be useful to summarize the properties of
Hill
’s condition.
Originally proposed by
[
14
,
15
]
in the sense of a rate–condition for compressible response

\left<\dfrac{{\rm D}^{ZJ}}{{\rm D}t}\boldsymbol{\tau},\hbox{\bf d}\right>>0\quad\forall\,\hbox{\bf d}\neq\hbox{\bf 0}\,,

(18)

it can be shown to be equivalent to the following statements
[
29
]
:

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\tau}}{{\rm d}\log\hbox{\bf v}}\quad{\rm is\,a\,positive\,definite\,fourth\,order\,tensor}

(19)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{i}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})}{{\rm d}\log\lambda_{j}}\quad{\rm is\,a\,positive\,definite\,symmetric\,matrix}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle W=\widehat{W}(\log\hbox{\bf v})\quad{\rm is\,convex\,in}\log\hbox{\bf v}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle W=\widehat{W}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})\quad{\rm is\,convex\,in}\,(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left<\underbrace{{\rm D}^{2}\widehat{W}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}_{=:\mathbb{H}}\cdot\eta,\eta\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}>0\quad\forall\,\eta\neq 0\in{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}\in{\rm Sym^{++}}(3):\quad{\rm trace}(\mathbb{H})>0\,,\quad{\rm trace}({\rm Cof}\,\mathbb{H})>0\,,\quad\det\mathbb{H}>0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left<\boldsymbol{\tau}(\log\hbox{\bf v}_{1})-\boldsymbol{\tau}(\log\hbox{\bf v}_{2}),\log\hbox{\bf v}_{1}-\log\hbox{\bf v}_{2}\right>>0\quad\forall\,\hbox{\bf v}_{1}\neq\hbox{\bf v}_{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\log\bar{\lambda}_{1},\log\bar{\lambda}_{2},\log\bar{\lambda}_{3})-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})\right)(\log\bar{\lambda}_{i}-\log\lambda_{i})>0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\tau}:{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{\rm log}={\rm d}\tau_{1}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\tau_{2}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\tau_{3}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{3}^{\rm log}>0\,.

2.1.2
The incompressible case

In the incompressible case
Hill’s
inequality is evaluated for
\det\hbox{\bf F}=1
.
Since

\log\det\hbox{\bf F}=\log\det\hbox{\bf v}={\rm trace}(\log\hbox{\bf v})\,,

(20)

the nonlinear and non–convex constraint
\det\hbox{\bf F}=1
translates with respect to the logarithmic strains
\log\hbox{\bf v}
into the linear and convex constraint

0={\rm trace}(\log\hbox{\bf v})=\log\lambda_{1}+\log\lambda_{2}+\log\lambda_{3}\,.

(21)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left<\dfrac{{\rm D}^{ZJ}}{{\rm D}t}\boldsymbol{\tau},\hbox{\bf d}\right>>0\quad\forall\,\hbox{\bf d}\neq\hbox{\bf 0},\,\,{\rm trace}(\hbox{\bf d})=0

(22)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle W=\widehat{W}(\log\hbox{\bf v})\quad{\rm is\,convex\ in}\log\hbox{\bf v}\,{\rm over}\,{\rm trace}(\log\hbox{\bf v})=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle W=\widehat{W}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})\quad{\rm is\,convex\ in}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{2},\log\lambda_{3})\,{\rm over\,}\log\lambda_{1}+\log\lambda_{2}+\log\lambda_{3}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\tau}:{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{\rm log}>0\,\,{\rm with\,\,trace}({\rm d}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{\rm log})=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tau_{1}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\tau_{2}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\tau_{3}\,{\rm d}\epsilon_{3}^{\rm log}>0\quad{\rm with}\quad{\rm d}\epsilon_{1}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\epsilon_{2}^{\rm log}+{\rm d}\epsilon_{3}^{\rm log}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left<\underbrace{{\rm D}^{2}\widehat{W}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}_{=:\mathbb{H}}\cdot\eta,\eta\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}>0\quad\forall\,\eta\neq 0,\,\,\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\eta_{3}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left<\underbrace{\hbox{\bf P}^{\rm T}\cdot{\rm D}^{2}\widehat{W}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}_{=:\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}}\cdot\hbox{\bf P}\cdot\xi,\xi\right>_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}>0\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\quad{\rm with}\,\hbox{\bf P}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}}\in{\rm Sym^{++}}(2):\quad{\rm trace}(\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc})>0\,,\quad\det\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}>0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mbox{the {\sc Abaqus} check:}\quad{\mathbb{D}}\,\,{\rm from\,(\ref{D_mat})\,is\,positive\,definite}\,,

where we show the last equivalence in Appendix
D.3
.

An evaluation of
Hill’s
condition in rate–form (
18
) along the family

\hbox{\bf F}={\rm diag}(\lambda,\lambda^{m},\lambda^{-(m+1)})

(23)

of the special, i.e. ”prototype” deformations in Fig.

is given in the Appendix

A numerical check of

’s condition for compressibility and a family of elastic energies has recently been given in

Conclusions

We have succeeded in developing a uniform representation and a standardized procedure that makes it possible to visualize material stability for the incompressible isotropic hyperelasticity model class and its prototype deformation modes in an overall picture.
A similar procedure (presumably numerically) is also used in commercial FE–codes such as
Abaqus
or
Ansys
to warn of potential instabilities during application. This is now also possible in a comprehensible and graphical way,
and should definitely be included when adapting material properties to material samples or component measurements for industrial applications.
It must be noted that deformation modes with rotating principal axes – the simple shear case is a good example – can fulfill the incompressible
Hill
stability condition
\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(2)
,
but this does not guarantee monotonicity of the true shear stress–strain relationship in

simple shear
3
3

3

We show in the Appendix

the relation between the shear component and the shear
\gamma
.
, see
[
30
]
, and
therefore one should in addition require as useful stability condition the ”

{\rm D}^{2}_{\hbox{\bf F}}W(\hbox{\bf F}).(\xi\otimes\eta,\xi\otimes\eta)>0\quad\forall\,\xi,\eta\neq 0
.
This computation of

incompressible hyperelasticity and should therefore be routinely checked for FE–calculation in accordance with the findings in

D.3
Equivalence of positive definiteness for
\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}
and
\mathbb{D}

As seen in the previous section,
for the three prototype modes, the positive definiteness of
\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}
and of
\mathbb{D}
give the same stability limits.
However, for a general strain state, the relation between
\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}
and
\mathbb{D}
is not clear at all at first sight.

On the one hand, we have from (
73
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\hbox{\bf P}^{\rm T}\cdot\mathbb{H}\cdot\hbox{\bf P}=\hbox{\bf P}^{\rm T}\cdot{\rm D}^{2}_{\log\lambda_{i}}\widehat{W}\cdot\hbox{\bf P}

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}H_{22}-2H_{23}+H_{33}\quad&H_{13}-H_{12}+H_{22}-H_{23}\\
H_{13}-H_{12}+H_{22}-H_{23}\quad&H_{11}-2H_{12}+H_{22}\end{bmatrix}\quad{\rm with}\quad\hbox{\bf P}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\,.

On the other hand, it holds by assuming
\tau_{2}=0
that (surprisingly, formula from
Patrizio Neff
,
private communication
)

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\log\lambda_{1}}[W_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})]\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\log\lambda_{3}}[W_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})]

(79)

so that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}{\rm D}_{\log\lambda_{1}}\,\widehat{\tau}_{1}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})\quad{\rm D}_{\log\lambda_{3}}\,\widehat{\tau}_{1}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})\\
{\rm D}_{\log\lambda_{1}}\,\widehat{\tau}_{3}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})\quad{\rm D}_{\log\lambda_{3}}\,\widehat{\tau}_{3}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})\end{bmatrix}

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm D}^{2}_{\log\lambda_{i}}[W_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})]={\rm D}^{2}_{\log\lambda_{i}}[\widehat{W}_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})]\,,

where we have to set

\displaystyle W_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle W(\lambda_{1},\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}},\lambda_{3})\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{W}_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(\log\lambda_{1},\log\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle W_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(e^{\log\lambda_{1}},e^{\log\lambda_{3}})

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W(e^{\log\lambda_{1}},e^{-(\log\lambda_{1}+\log\lambda_{3})},e^{\log\lambda_{3}})

because of
\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{3}^{-1}=e^{-\log\lambda_{1}}\,e^{-\log\lambda_{3}}=e^{-(\log\lambda_{1}+\log\lambda_{3})}
.
Let us write

\widehat{W}_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(x_{1},x_{3}):=W(e^{x_{1}},e^{-(x_{1}+x_{3})},e^{x_{3}})=\widehat{W}(x_{1},-(x_{1}+x_{3}),x_{3})\,,

(82)

which gives for the
Hesse
matrix of
\widehat{W}^{\rm inc}_{\rm red}

{\rm D}^{2}_{(x_{1},x_{3})}\widehat{W}_{\rm red}^{\rm inc}(x_{1},x_{3})=\begin{bmatrix}\delta_{1}\delta_{1}\widehat{W}-2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}+\delta_{2}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}&\delta_{1}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}-\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}-\delta_{2}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}+\delta_{2}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}\\
\delta_{1}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}-\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}-\delta_{2}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}+\delta_{2}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}&\delta_{2}\delta_{2}\widehat{W}-2\delta_{2}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}+\delta_{3}\delta_{3}\widehat{W}\end{bmatrix}\,,

(83)

so that

\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{11}-2H_{12}+H_{22}\quad&H_{13}-H_{12}-H_{23}+H_{22}\\
H_{13}-H_{12}-H_{23}+H_{22}\quad&H_{22}-2H_{23}+H_{33}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(84)

Now, for isotropic response, the elastic energy
W
is invariant under permutations of the principal stretches. Therefore, the positive definiteness of
\mathbb{D}
from (
8
)
and the positive definiteness of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
from (
84
) are equivalent.
Indeed, on the level of
\mathbb{D}
in (
8
) it is an arbitrary choice to require
\tau_{3}=0
. Here, we use instead
\tau_{2}=0
for consistency.
Comparing (
84
) and (
78
) we have shown that

\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(2)\Longleftrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(2)

(85)

since
{\rm trace}(\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc})={\rm trace}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
and
\det\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}=\det\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
Thus

\mathbb{H}^{\rm inc}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(2)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb{D}\in{\rm Sym}^{++}(2)

(86)

and the
Abaqus
stability check is shown to be fully equivalent to
Hill’s
condition for the incompressible case.

A five year experience of anorectal malformation surgical reconstrunctions and functional outcome associated factors in Eastern Africa
Authors: Joachim Michael Kisaka, Victor Meza Kyaruzi, Victor Ngotta, Zaituni Bohari, Petronilla Ngiloi, Muhsin Aboud, Sidney Yongolo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFunctional outcomes after definitive surgical reconstruction in the management of Anorectal malformations (ARM) patients have not been adequately studied in the third-world countries.ObjectiveThis retrospective cross sectional analytical study aimed at evaluating the functional outcome and attributable factors following the definitive reconstruction surgery among children with ARM at Muhimbili National Hospital.MethodsPatients with ARM who underwent definitive reconstruction surgery between 2017 and 2021 were recruited in the current study using the consecutive sampling method. The functional outcome and severity were assessed using the modified Krickenbeck scoring system.Analysis was performed using STATA version 16.0.Results116 pediatric patients with ARM who underwent definitive surgical repair were reviewed, the mean age was 4.4 (SD1.8) years. The male-to-female ratio was 1.1:1. The functional outcome was voluntary bowel movement (VBM), constipation, and soiling with a prevalence of 63%, 22%, and 15% of patients respectively. Babies born preterm shows significant association with constipation with P- the value of 0.003, OR 13.26, and 95% CI 2.48–70.87. Abdominal perineal pull-through among patients who had High ARM showed a significant association with soiling (P-value 0.001, OR 0.03, and 95% CI of 0.01–0.16).ConclusionIn the current study we identified that there is no significant variation in ARM functional outcome between males and females.However some factors such as the type of surgery, patient body weight and the complexity of anorectal malformation were all decisive factors for achieving the optimum Voluntary Bowel Bovement (VBM) following ARM repair surgery.Keywords: Anorectal malformation, Voluntary bowel movement, Incontinence, Constipation, Soiling

Background

Anorectal malformations (ARM) are a group of congenital caudal end deformities that range in severity from mild to severe and disrupt the proper development of the anus and rectum. They are usually immediately detectable by physical examination after delivery [1]. The management of ARM patients is currently focused on the functional outcome after surgical reconstruction and the quality of survival is determined by whether the functional outcome is desirable or undesirable. This is because improvements in neonatal care and surgical approaches for ARM patients have also improved the survival rate of ARM patients in the last decade [2, 3]. The outcome of management of ARM patients particularly on the functional outcome after definitive surgical reconstruction has not been studied well in third-world countries and in Africa, ARM accounts for one-third to two-fifths of pediatric surgeons’ operative burden [4, 5].

They are quite common congenital anomalies in most parts of the world, and they continue to be a challenge in pediatric surgery, with an estimated prevalence of 1 in 5000 live births in Europe, and 1-1.6 in 5000 live birth in South Africa [1, 4, 6]. ARM affects both sexes, Male to Female Ratio of 3.01:1 [7].

The causes are unknown, although they are likely to include multiple aspects such as environmental, socioeconomic, and genetic influences [8]. ARM is frequently linked to congenital defects in other systems, which can have a far more serious influence on long-term prognosis, particularly in neonates [2, 9, 10].

Different types of definitive surgical repair have been introduced, including posterior sagittal anorectoplasty (PSARP ), anterior sagittal anorectoplasty (ASARP), primary anoplasty, and abdominal perineal pull-through. Reconstruction of an ARM can be done as a single-stage repair where no prior formation of a colostomy or multi-stage repair where colostomy is first formed, then the construction of neoanus, and finally colostomy closure. PSARP has been linked to better surgical outcomes because it allows surgeons to see the anatomy of the defects and repair them under direct vision, as well as learn about the complex anatomic arrangement of the rectum and genitourinary tract junction, which allows for a better prediction of functional outcome and prognosis [8, 11].

This study aimed to assess the functional outcome and severity using the modified Krickenbeck scoring system [2, 3, 8], including the measure of factors associated with functional outcomes in children with ARM operated at Muhimbili National Hospital.

Modified Krickenbeck classification aims to standardize functional outcomes across different clinical and surgical groups so that meaningful comparisons may be made. Classification helps to measure the functional outcome of ARM without the need of attending to the child for physical examination or performing a procedure like a manometry because questionnaires can be asked to the parents and results are comparable with other scoring systems. The ARMs were classified using Wingspread, Pena, Rantel, and other classifications, but there were differences in terms of follow-up of these children since it was impossible to compare their functional outcomes [3, 12].

The Krickenbeck classification divides functional outcomes following definitive surgery into three categories: voluntary bowel movement (VBM), soling, and constipation. VBM is defined as the desire to defecate, the ability to verbalize this desire, and the ability to hold the bowel movement; Soiling is divided into three categories: (a) occasional soiling (up to once or twice per week), (b) daily soiling with no social problems, and (c) constant soiling with social problems. Constipation is divided into three grades: (a) treatable by dietary adjustments, (b) requires laxatives, and (c) resistant to both laxatives and diet [2, 3, 8]. The Krickenbeck classification divides ARM types into two diagnostic categories: major clinical groupings and rare/regional variants. Perineal fistula, rectourethral (prostatic, bulbar), rectovesical fistula, vestibular fistula, cloaca, no fistula, and anal stenosis are the most common clinical groups, while pouch colon, rectal atresia, rectal stenosis, rectovaginal fistula, and H fistula are rare/regional varieties [2, 3, 8]. Despite successful definitive surgical repair of ARM, there is an information gap on the spectrum of functional outcomes following several children who continue to experience varying degrees of undesirable functional outcomes, such as fecal incontinence and constipation, and how it impedes the quality of life, including social and psychological development. The factors associated with the functional outcome have not been adequately evaluated in third-world countries.

Methodology

A mixed cross-sectional analytical study from January 2017 to December 2020 was conducted in the Paediatric Surgical unit at Muhimbili National Hospital (MNH) for children with ARM aged between 3 and ≤ 18 years old following surgical reconstruction of ARM when the functional outcome can be determined. Evaluation of functional outcome was after 6 months back or more following single-stage repair or three-stage repair after colostomy closure. The sample size was estimated using the Kish and Leslie formula [13], the constipation prevalence of 9.3% as reported by Salar et al. [14] was applied to reach and adjusted sample of 116 participants by margin error of 5% and 95%CI. The power of the study was 80% since there was no comparison of two populations with a non–response rate (f) of 10% using the formulae.

The participants were recruited using a non-probability convenience sampling technique. All children with anorectal malformations who had definitive surgical repair with single-stage repair or three-stage repair at least 6 months upon the time of study and children aged between 3 years and ≤ 18 years whose their functional outcome could be determined were recruited. All cases with other associated anomalies or medical conditions which could affect the continence such as Hirschsprung’s disease, cerebral palsy, inflammatory bowel disease, mental retardation were excluded, all patients whose their clinical informations were missing or their medical record was incomplete were not recruited.The data was collected using a standardized English and a Swahili questionnaire tools.All completed questionnaires were double entered into the database developed from Epidata software version 4.1 for data management; and data was then transferred to Stata version 16.0 for cross-checking, data cleaning, and analysis by objectives. Demographic data and clinical characteristics including birth weight,

gestation age, types of definitive surgery, level, and types of ARM, were collected. Factors associated with outcome were tested using a chi-square method where P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant and the strength of association was determined by logistic regression analysis. The Odds Ratios with 95% confidence intervals are reported.

Ethical clearance

The Muhimbili University of Health and Allied Science- Institution Review Board granted ethical clearance certificate Ref DA.282/298/01.C/MUHAS-REC-0602021-686 to conduct the study, as well Muhimbili National Hospital research and training department granted permission to collect data and has therefore been performed following the ethical standards laid down in the 1964 declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. The assessment of the functional outcome and their severity in children with ARM post definitive surgery as an outpatient and or in- patients necessitated obtaining written informed consent from parents/guardians and verbal consent from those who were contacted via phone.

Single-stage repair means the definitive surgical repair of an ARM with no colostomy formation while three-stage repair means colostomy formation followed by definitive surgical repair of ARM and then colostomy closure.

Results

During the study period a total number of 5,511 patients were operated at our facilty because of several surgical surgical conditions. About 1,327 (24%) were reported to have ARM and underwent definitive surgical repair. 220 (16.6%) of 1,327 patients with ARM surgery were retrieved and were eligible for recruitment after undergoing either single-stage or three-stage repair, 1,107 patients who were on second-stage repair and anal dilatation as an adjunct were also eligible. Data were then extracted from 220 patient records as shown in the recruitment chart below (Fig. 1). For verbal consent parents or guardian were reached via phone calls. 23 participants were not reachable on calls and only 104 responded to phone calls among them 8 patients were confirmed to have died and 3 did not consent. During the record review 13 have records missing, 5 recorded death, 34 missed a phone number, 17 patients were not assessed after the sample size was determined, and remaining 116 patients were recruited and analyzed in the study.

Discussion

The contemporary management approach of anorectal malformation (ARM) cases focuses on improving the quality of the functional outcome and end points after surgical reconstruction.The significant surgical inventions for ARM cases have contributed an optimal improvement on the over all survival rate of ARM patients and neonatal care in last decade [2, 3]. The outcome of management of ARM patients particularly on the functional outcome after definitive surgical reconstruction has not been studied well in third-world countries and in Africa, ARM accounts for one-third to two-fifths of pediatric surgeons’ operative burden [4, 5]. During the five years of our retrospective study, ARM operation accounts for 24% of the operating burden in the pediatric surgical unit at Muhimbili National Hospital (MNH). In regards to the assessment of factors associated with functional outcome in children with ARM operated at MNH, our study revealed overall functional outcome is good with the majority of patients having a voluntary bowel movement (continence) accounting for 63%. Except patients with undesirable functional outcome score 37% including both constipation and soiling. The types of surgery performed, child body weight at surgery, level of ARM, gestation age are all decisive variable associated with functional outcome. A reported functional outcome related to patients with ARM who underwent surgical reconstruction has been shown by other studies [11,12,17].

Clinical profile of children with ARM undergoing surgical reconstruction

Our findings revealed a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.1, with females accounting for 51.7% and men for 48.3%, there is no significant difference in the prevalence of ARM between boys and girls. The male/female ratio was found to be 1:1.4 in a prior study conducted in Tanzania’s lake zone by Mfinanga et al., with females marginally outnumbering males [10]. Shaqib H. Qazi et al. found males accounting for 68% compared to females accounting for 32% [2]. In an Iranian study by Shahnam Askarpour et al., the male/female ratio was 1.14:1 [15] while in a study by Jiwan L.Patel et al., males predominated over females with 50.6% males and 49.9% females [11], in all of these studies, there was no significant difference in the prevalence of ARM between boys and girls. The slight resemblance and local variation could imply that the sexes were unrelated.

Our study also found that most of functional disorders were significantly experienced in the age group between 3 and 5 years, with 79.3%, followed by 6–8 years, with 17.3%, and 9 to ≤ 18 years, with 3.4% after the neo anus reconstruction surgery. Similarly, Jiwan L. Patel et colleagues found that the maximum number of patients was in the age category of 3–5 years (78.8%) and the minimum age group was 9–11 years 3.7% [11].

In the current study, normal birth weight predominated with 88.3%, followed by low birth weight with 11.7%. Term gestational age was the most common gestational age in the current study, accounting for 88.8%, with preterm 6.9% and postdate 4.3%. With 62.9%, 20.7%, and 16.4%, respectively, low-level ARM predominated high and intermediate-level ARM in our study. In Mulago, Uganda, Birabwa et al. revealed that high-level ARM predominated with 68.7% vs. low-level ARM with 31.3% [15]. Similarly, Manoochehr G et al. found that high levels of ARM outnumbered low levels by 67.6% [13],. PSARP 44%, and ASARP 36.2%, were the most prevalent types of definitive surgery done in our study, and the least were primary anoplasty 12.9% and abdominal perineal pull-through 6.9%.

Functional outcome of children with ARM following definitive surgical repair using Krickenbeck scoring system

Our study was able to demonstrate the prevalence of functional outcomes in our setting, with VBM accounting for 63%, constipation 22%, and soiling 15%. These findings are in keeping with previous studies, according to a study conducted in Kenya by Jumbi Tim et al. [16], VBM was present in 84% of the participants, followed by constipation in 40%, and soiling in 44% [17], another study conducted in Parkstan reported an equivalent prevalence [18]. This could imply that local regions and other developing countries are exposed to similar factors that influence the functional outcomes related to ARM surgical repair. Firdian et al. showed a significant prevalence of VBM (53.5%) in Indonesia, however, the unfavorable functional outcome of soiling (11.6%) was higher than constipation 9.3% [3]. Regional differences in exposure to factors impacting function outcome are supported by the same explanations. In our study, the severity of undesirable functional results revealed that 80.8% of constipation was grade one, which was manageable with diet modification, followed by 19.2% grade three, which was resistant to diet and laxatives, whereas soiled severity revealed 41.2% grade two, with one to two episodes per week and no social problem, and minimally 23.5% grade one with occasional soiling. Furthermore, in our study males were outnumbered by 64% compared to 35% females in soiling, whereas females had 61.5% marginally outnumbered males with 38.5% in constipation, and the VBM had 48% males compared to 52.2% females.

Similarly, a previous study by Jiwan Lawl Patel et al. found that different types of ARM contributed to different grades of severity, such as rectovaginal fistula had 50% grade 1 and 16.6% had grade 2 soiling, rectovaginal fistula in constipation had 16.5% grade 1 and grade 2 each, and recto-prostatic fistula had 16% grade 1 constipation [11]. According to a prior study by Firdian et al., the severity of constipation in most children was grade 1 (7%) rather than grades 2 and 3, and the soiling was grade one 7% [3].

Factors associated with the functional outcome of children with ARM following definitive surgical repair

This study was able to establish that sexes had no significant link with functional outcomes with P values of 0.758, 0.142, and 0.256 for VBM, soiling, or constipation respectively. This finding was in line with a prior study by Firdian et al., which found no significant link between sex and functional outcome in ARM patients with a P-value of 0.07 [3]. In a prior study, Manoochehr G et al. found that 91.7% of female patients had VBM compared to 70% of male patients; however, the difference was not statistically significant [12]. The similarity finding could imply that there is no physical difference in anorectal formation in either sex when it comes to exposed features of anorectal formation.

Furthermore, there was no significant correlation between the age group of a child at presentation with VBM, soiling, or constipation, with P–values of 0.172, 0.074, and 0.0982, respectively, as a functional outcome. Similarly, Shahnam Askarpour et al. discovered no significant link between the age group at presentation and functional outcome, no significant score improvement with age, and no significant link between age and prognosis improvement in their previous study [15].

Birth weight had statistical significance with VBM and soiling with P values of 0.001 and 0.014, respectively, except for constipation, which had no statistical significance with a P-value of 0.173. During multivariable logistic regression analysis, between birth weight and functional outcome it was interesting to find that normal birth weight had a significant association with VBM with a P-value of 0.004, OR 7.20, and 95% CI (1.85–28.03), but inversely than expected normal birth weight had also significance association with soiling with P–value of 0.021, OR 0.22, and 95% CI (0.06–0.79). Following this study, a prior study done in Indonesia by Firdian et al., similarly revealed normal birth weight has better VBM compared with those with low birth weight [3]. This implies that normal birth weight including at the operation time improves the functional outcomes.

The gestation age had statistical significance with VBM and constipation with each having a P-value of 0.001. There was no statistical significance between gestation age with soiling with a P-value of 0.640. Only preterm showed a significant connection with constipation in a multivariable logistic regression analysis, with a P-value of 0.003, OR 13.29, 95% CI (2.48–70.87). However, throughout an exhaustive literature analysis, the gestation age parameter was addressed infrequently, necessitating additional research to compare the results.

The level of anorectal malformation was shown to have a statistical significance with VBM and Soiling, each with a P-value of 0.001, except for constipation, which had no statistical relevance with a P-value of 0.94. High levels of ARM had a significant connection with soiling with a P-value of 0.001, OR 0.03, 95% CI 0.01–0.16, according to multivariable logistic regression analysis. Similarly, Shahnam Askapour et al. discovered that 3.45% of high-level ARM patients had a poor prognosis (P-value of 0.22), whereas low-level ARM patients had a better prognosis (P-value of 0.15), with no statistical significance between ARM level and functional result [15].

However, Manoochehr G et al. found that VBM was found in 91.3% of patients with low type anorectal malformation compared to 72.8% of patients with high type anorectal malformation; however, the difference was not significant with a P-value of 0.13 [12]. The discrepancy in our analysis could be due to sampling size variations. The study conducted in Kenya by Jumbi Tim et al., further reported a low prevalence of low ARM linked with constipation and a high prevalence of high ARM associated with soiling in their study [17].

The types of definitive surgery performed had statistical significance with VBM and soiling with a P-value of 0.033 and 0.001, respectively. There was no statistical significance link between types of definitive surgery and constipation with a P-value of 0.206. During multivariable logistic regression analysis, however, Primary anoplasty had a significant correlation with VBM (P = 0.017, OR 0.22, 95% CI 0.06–0.76), whereas APP had a significant association with soiling (P = 0.003, OR 12.5, 95% CI 2.36–66.12) and inversely than predicted with VBM (P = 0.013, 0R 0.11, 95% CI 0.02–0.64).

In a previous study, Zaheer H et al. found that the most common types of definitive surgery performed had no relationship with functional outcomes and that the most common definitive surgery in male patients was PSARP 65.2% with acceptable fecal continence, followed by abdominoperineal pull-through 36.7%, and primary anoplasty 19.1%. PSARP was used 63.5% of the time in female patients, and ASARP was used 13.4% of the time followed by primary anoplasty in 13.5% [7].

The VBM (continence) is influenced by canal sensation, the voluntary sphincter muscles, and colonic motility for optimal pelvic floor, rectum, and anal sphincter function. Because the majority of the distal part of the bowel is preserved and the external sphincter muscles are splinted in the midline in direct anatomical vision, the PSARP approach displayed improved VBM.

Unfavorable functional result Soiling is influenced by the loss of the rectal reservoir during abdominal perineal pull-through, as evidenced by our research, and delayed treatment of constipation, resulting in overflow faux incontinence. Constipation can be caused by the megarectum and megasigmoid, resulting in fecal impaction and hypermobility due to anorectal dilatation, culminating in insufficient peristalsis and stool evacuation failure. As evidenced by our study, certain types of definitive surgery, such as primary anoplasty, can result in sphincter muscle tears or rectum misallocation. Constipation caused by significant rectum mobilization during repair causes partial denervation and reduced feeling [8].

Study limitation and strength

The study has some limitations as we did not study the experience and qualification of the surgeons, those who were interviewed via telephone, not sure of their function outcome, and we have to believe the parent, particular on the severity of the functional outcome. We were only assessing the patient once.

Conclusion

ARM accounts for a considerable fraction of all the operative procedures with a male/female ratio of 1:1.1 and no significant difference in ARM prevalence between males and females. VBM (continence) was the best recorded functional outcome in more than half of the operated cases in our setting with vestibular anus fistula and prematurity being highly associated with constipation. The type of surgery performed, the child’s body weight and the level of ARM were all decisive variables in influencing the functional outcome.

Function-Correcting Codes for b − b- Symbol Read Channels Journal: Finite Field and Their Applications
Anamika Singh, Abhay Kumar Singh, Eitan Yaakobi

Abstract
Function correcting codes are an innovative class of codes that is designed to protect a function evaluation of the data against errors or corruptions. Due to its usefulness in machine learning applications and archival data storage, where preserving the integrity of computations is crucial, Lenz et al. [ 1 ] recently introduced function-correcting codes for binary symmetric channels to safeguard function evaluation against errors. Xia et al. [ 2 ] expanded this concept to symbol-pair read channels over binary fields. The current paper further advances the theory by developing function-correcting codes for b-symbol read channels over finite fields. We introduce the idea of irregular b − b- symbol distance codes and establish bounds on their performance over finite fields. This concept helps in understanding the behaviour of function-correcting codes in more complex settings. We use the connection between function-correcting b − b- symbol codes and irregular b − b- symbol distance codes to obtain bounds on its optimal redundancy. We also present a graphical representation of the problem of constructing function-correcting b − b- symbol codes. Furthermore, we apply these general results to specific classes of functions and compare the redundancy of function-correcting b b -symbol codes with classical b b -symbol codes. Our findings demonstrate that function-correcting b b -symbol codes achieve lower redundancy while maintaining reliability.

Introduction

Classical error-correcting codes are typically designed to recover the entirety of a message transmitted over a noisy channel. However, in certain scenarios, the focus shifts to protecting only the function evaluation of the message rather than the message itself. This need inspired the development of function-correcting codes that would help to minimize redundancy, offering a more efficient alternative to the classical error-correcting codes that have been conventionally utilized. This new paradigm is based on the assumption that the sender knows the function and can use it to encode the message so that the recipient can reliably recover the value of the function. The key idea behind designing such codes is that the receiver need not distinguish between codewords from information vectors that evaluate to the same function value. When the function is injective, the function-correcting codes behave similarly to the classical error-correcting codes because each distinct input maps to a distinct output. In this case, function-correcting codes can be considered a generalization of classical error-correcting codes, where the primary focus is on preserving the function’s evaluation rather than the original message. This new concept is suggested by Lenz et al.
[
1
]
for binary symmetric channels. In this paper, the relationship between irregular-distance codes and function-correcting codes is explored. This relationship is significant because it helped establish both lower and upper bounds on the redundancy of function-correcting codes. This is crucial because understanding these bounds can provide insights into the efficiency and effectiveness of these codes in different scenarios. Later, Xia et al. studied these codes for symbol-pair read channels over binary fields
[
2
]
. Premlal and Ranjan
[
3
]
focused on using function-correcting codes to correct linear functions.

Building on this foundation, we generalize the coding framework of function-correcting codes from the symbol-pair read channels to the more expansive
b-
symbol read channels. Furthermore, we have broadened the domain of the functions from the binary fields to general finite fields, providing a more versatile and comprehensive coding approach.

In this paper, we propose a graphical approach to the problem of constructing function-correcting
b-
symbol codes. An independent set of
q^{k}
elements has been shown to meet the condition of function-correcting
b-
symbol codes. We use this approach to come up with a function-correcting
b-
symbol code for the
b-
weight function. We establish a relationship between the shortest length of irregular
b-
symbol codes and the optimal redundancy of function-correcting
b-
symbol codes in the case of generic functions. We find bounds on the shortest length of irregular
b-
symbol distance codes and irregular distance codes for a larger finite field, as they in turn help to establish bounds on the optimal redundancy of function-correcting
b-
symbols. Then these results are applied to specific functions. Finally, we compare the redundancy of the function-correcting
b-
symbol codes for these functions with schemes that use classical
b-
symbol codes for
b=3
.

Function-correcting
b-
symbol codes offer a significant advantage over function-correcting codes (FCC) and function-correcting symbol-pair codes (FCSPC) in practical applications, particularly because modern storage devices increasingly rely on
b-
symbol read channels. These channels facilitate more efficient data storage and retrieval by enabling the processing of larger data units in a single operation.

By designing codes specifically for
b-
symbol read channels, it becomes possible to optimize storage density while effectively managing error rates. In contrast, symbol-pair read channels are limited in their adaptability as they focus on correcting errors in smaller, fixed-size data units. These limitations underscore the importance of extending the theoretical framework of function-correcting codes for
b-
symbol read channels to address the needs of contemporary data storage systems more effectively.

\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
is given by

\displaystyle d_{b}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle=d_{H}(\pi_{b}(\bm{x}),\pi_{b}(\bm{y}))

\displaystyle=d_{H}([({x}_{0},\ldots,{x}_{b-1}),\ldots,({x}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{x}_{b-2})],

\displaystyle\quad[({y}_{0},\ldots,{y}_{b-1}),\ldots,({y}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{y}_{b-2})]).

Similarly, the
b
-symbol distances for
x^{(1)}
and
y^{(1)}
, as well as
x^{(2)}
and
y^{(2)}
, are given by

\displaystyle d_{b}(x^{(1)},y^{(1)})

\displaystyle=d_{H}(\pi_{b}(x^{(1)}),\pi_{b}(y^{(1)}))

\displaystyle=d_{H}([({x}_{0},\ldots,{x}_{b-1}),\ldots,({x}_{k-1},\ldots,{x}_{b-2})],

\displaystyle\quad[({y}_{0},\ldots,{y}_{b-1}),\ldots,({y}_{k-1},\ldots,{y}_{b-2})]).

\displaystyle d_{b}(x^{(2)},y^{(2)})

\displaystyle=d_{H}(\pi_{b}(x^{(2)}),\pi_{b}(y^{(2)}))

\displaystyle=d_{H}([({x}_{k},\ldots,{x}_{k+b-2}),\ldots,({x}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{x}_{k+b-2})],

\displaystyle\quad[({y}_{k},\ldots,{y}_{k+b-2}),\ldots,({y}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{y}_{k+b-2})]).

The discrepancy in
b
-symbol distances arises due to differences in the
b
-coordinate vectors at the boundary positions. Specifically, the
b
-coordinate vectors that appear in
\pi_{b}(\bm{x})
and
\pi_{b}(\bm{y})
but are absent in
(\pi_{b}(x^{(1)}),\pi_{b}(x^{(2)}))
and
(\pi_{b}(y^{(1)}),\pi_{b}(y^{(2)}))
are denoted as
\eta_{x}
and
\eta_{y}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\eta_{x}

\displaystyle=[({x}_{k-b+1},\ldots,{x}_{k}),\ldots,({x}_{k-1},\ldots,{x}_{k+b-2}),({x}_{k+r-b+1},\ldots,{x}_{0}),\ldots,({x}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{x}_{b-2})],

\displaystyle\eta_{y}

\displaystyle=[({y}_{k-b+1},\ldots,{y}_{k}),\ldots,({y}_{k-1},\ldots,{y}_{k+b-2}),({y}_{k+r-b+1},\ldots,{y}_{0}),\ldots,({y}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{y}_{b-2})].

Conversely, the
b
-coordinate vectors that appear in
(\pi_{b}(x^{(1)}),\pi_{b}(x^{(2)}))
and
(\pi_{b}(y^{(1)}),\pi_{b}(y^{(2)}))
but are absent in
\pi_{b}(\bm{x})
and
\pi_{b}(\bm{y})
are denoted as
\zeta_{\bar{x}}
and
\zeta_{\bar{y}}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\bar{x}}

\displaystyle=[({x}_{k-b+1},\ldots,{x}_{0}),\ldots,({x}_{k-b+2},\ldots,{x}_{1}),({x}_{k-1},\ldots,{x}_{b-2}),\ldots,({x}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{x}_{k+b-2})],

\displaystyle\zeta_{\bar{y}}

\displaystyle=[({y}_{k-b+1},\ldots,{y}_{0}),\ldots,({y}_{k-1},\ldots,{y}_{b-2}),({y}_{k+r-b+1},\ldots,{y}_{0}),\ldots,({y}_{k+r-1},\ldots,{y}_{b-2})].

From the above definitions, we obtain:

\displaystyle d_{b}(\bm{x},\bm{y})-d_{b}(x^{(1)},y^{(1)})-d_{b}(x^{(2)},y^{(2)})=d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}}).

Since,

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})\leq 2(b-1),\quad 0\leq d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq 2(b-1),

we obtain:

\displaystyle-2(b-1)\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq 2(b-1).

Next, suppose that

\displaystyle b\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq 2(b-1).

This would imply

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq b-2.

However, this condition requires that

\displaystyle d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})=0,

which leads to a contradiction. Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq(b-1).

Similarly, if

\displaystyle-2(b-1)\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq-b,

this condition is also not possible, leading to

\displaystyle-(b-1)\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq 0.

Combining both inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle-(b-1)\leq d_{H}(\eta_{x},\eta_{y})-d_{H}(\zeta_{\bar{x}},\zeta_{\bar{y}})\leq b-1.

which further implies

\displaystyle d_{b}(x^{(1)},y^{(1)})+d_{b}(x^{(2)},y^{(2)})-(b-1)\leq d_{b}(x,y)\leq d_{b}(x^{(1)},y^{(1)})+d_{b}(x^{(2)},y^{(2)})+(b-1).

∎

Now that the above lemma has been proven, we can prove the following theorem which establishes the relation between function-correcting
b-
symbol code and irregular
b-
symbol distance codes of the matrices
\bm{B}_{f}^{1}(t)
and
\bm{B}_{f}^{2}(t)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any function
f:\mathbb{F}^{k}_{q}\rightarrow Im(f)
and
\{\bm{x_{1}},\ldots,\bm{x_{q^{k}}}\}=\mathbb{F}^{k}_{q}
, we have

N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))\leq r_{b}^{f}(k,t)\leq N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t)).

Proof.

We establish the theorem by considering the following cases.

Case 1: Constant functions
If
f
is a constant function, then both
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))
and
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t))
are equal to zero. Consequently, the desired condition holds trivially, establishing the theorem in this case. Specifically, we have:

N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))=r_{b}^{f}(k,t)=N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t)).

Case 2: Non-constant functions
To prove that
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))\leq r_{b}^{f}(k,t)
for non-constant functions
f
, consider a function-correcting
b
-symbol code defined by an encoding function

\Phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+r},\quad\bm{x_{i}}\mapsto(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{p_{i}}),

where the redundancy
r
is optimal, i.e.,
r=r_{b}^{f}(k,t)
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))>r_{b}^{f}(k,t)
. This implies the existence of distinct indices
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q^{k}\}
such that
f(\bm{x_{i}})\neq f(\bm{x_{j}})
and

d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})<2t-b+2-d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}}).

Consequently, we obtain

d_{b}(\Phi(\bm{x_{i}}),\Phi(\bm{x_{j}}))\leq d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}})+d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})+(b-1)<2t+1.

This contradicts that
\Phi
defines a function-correcting
b-
symbol code. Hence,

N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))\leq r_{b}^{f}(k,t).

Next, we establish the reverse inequality
r_{b}^{f}(k,t)\leq N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t))
. Let
P=\{\bm{p_{1}},\ldots,\bm{p_{q^{k}}}\}
be a
\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t)
irregular
b-
symbol distance code of length
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t))
, and define the encoding function

\Phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+r},\quad\bm{x_{i}}\mapsto(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{p_{i}}).

For every pair
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q^{k}\}
with
f(\bm{x_{i}})\neq f(\bm{x_{j}})
, we have

d_{b}(\Phi(\bm{x_{i}}),\Phi(\bm{x_{j}}))\geq d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}})+d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})-(b-1).

Since
P
is a
\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t)
irregular
b-
symbol distance code, it satisfies

d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq 2t+b-d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}}),

which implies

d_{b}(\Phi(\bm{x_{i}}),\Phi(\bm{x_{j}}))\geq 2t+b-(b-1)=2t+1.

Thus,
\Phi
defines a function-correcting
b
-symbol code for
f
with redundancy
r=N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t))
, yielding

r^{f}_{b}(k,t)\leq N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(2)}(t)).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
Bounds on Optimal Redundancy for Generic Functions

In the previous section, lower and upper bounds were obtained for optimal redundancies of function-correcting
b
-symbol codes in terms of
N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t))
and
N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t))
. These bounds can be simplified further by considering subsets of message vectors instead of the whole space
{\mathbb{F}^{k}_{q}}
.

First, we obtain a simplified lower bound on
r_{b}^{f}(k,t)
in the following corollary of the theorem.

Corollary 3.3
.

: For any arbitrary subset
\{\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}}
, optimal redundancy of a function-correcting
b
-symbol code for function
f
is bounded below by
N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M}))
i.e.

r^{f}_{b}(k,t)\geq N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M}))

and for function
f
with
|Im(f)|\geq 2
and
t>b-1

r^{f}_{b}(k,t)\geq 2(t-b+1)

Proof.

The first statement of the result is clear from the fact that every
\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t)
irregular
b-
symbol distance code is a
\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M})
irregular
b-
symbol distance code. So,

N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t))\geq N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M})).

Combining the above inequality with the before-established inequality
N_{b}(\bm{B}_{f}^{(1)}(t))\leq r_{b}^{f}(k,t)
, we get our required result i.e.

r^{f}_{b}(k,t)\geq N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{M})).

Now consider the particular case where
|Im(f)|\geq 2
. Then there exist two distinct message vectors, say,
\bm{x}_{i}
and
\bm{x}_{j}
such that
f(\bm{x}_{i})\neq f(\bm{x}_{j})
and
d_{b}(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{j})=b
. Let
t>b-1
then,

r^{f}_{b}(k,t)\geq N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{j}))=N_{b}(2,2t-b+2-b)=2(t-b+1).

where the last equality can be obtained using a
2t-2b+2
length repetition code {(0, . . . , 0), (1, . . . , 1)}.

Now, we work our way through simplifying the upper bound on
r^{f}_{b}(k,t)
but for that, we first introduce
b
-distance between function values which then leads us to define the function
b
-distance matrix.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in Im(f)
, then the minimum
b-
symbol distance between two information vectors that evaluate to
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
gives the
b-
symbol distance between the function values
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, i.e.,

d_{b}^{f}(f_{1},f_{2})=\min_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}}d_{b}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad f(\bm{x}_{1})=f_{1}\text{ and }f(\bm{x}_{2})=f_{2}.

Definition 3.7
.

The function
b
-distance matrices for function
f
are square matrices of order
E=|\text{Im}(f)|
denoted by
\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,f_{1},\ldots,f_{E})
and
\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\ldots,f_{E})
respectively and whose entries are given by:

[\bm{B_{f}^{(1)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t-b+2-d_{b}^{f}(f_{i},f_{j})]^{+},&\text{if }i\neq j,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

and

[\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E})]_{ij}=\begin{cases}[2t+b-d_{b}^{f}(f_{i},f_{j})]^{+},&\text{if }i\neq j,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

With the help of the above definitions, we can now establish simplified upper bounds on optimal redundancy for any arbitrary function.

Theorem 3.4
.

The optimal redundancy for any arbitrary function
f
defined in
{\mathbb{F}^{k}_{q}}
is bounded above by
N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E})).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{\bm{p_{1}},\bm{p_{2}},\ldots,\bm{p_{E}}\}
be an
\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E})-
irregular
b-
symbol distance code, i.e., for every
i
and
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,E\}

d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq 2t+b-d_{b}^{f}(f_{i},f_{j}).

Using the irregular
b-
symbol distance code defined above, we construct a function-correcting
b
-symbol code for the function
f
by assigning the same redundancy vector to all information vectors that produce the same functional value. Specifically, we assign
\bm{p_{i}}
to all inputs that satisfy
f(\bm{x_{i}})=f_{i}
.

To verify that this indeed forms a function-correcting
b
-symbol code, consider two arbitrary information vectors
\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
such that
f(\bm{x_{i}})=f_{i}
and
f(\bm{x_{j}})=f_{j}
, where
f_{i}\neq f_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{b}(\text{Enc}(\bm{x_{i}}),\text{Enc}(\bm{x_{j}}))

\displaystyle\geq d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}})+d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})-(b-1)

\displaystyle\geq d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}})+2t+b-d_{b}(\bm{x_{i}},\bm{x_{j}})-b+1

\displaystyle=2t+1.

Thus, by definition, the above construction yields a function-correcting
b
-symbol code.

Since such a code
\mathcal{P}
exists only if its length is at least
N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E}))
, we conclude that

r_{b}^{f}(k,t)\leq N_{b}(\bm{B_{f}^{(2)}}(t,f_{1},\dots,f_{E})).

∎

4
Bounds on
N_{b}(\textbf{B})

As we observed, the optimal redundancy of function-correcting
b-
symbol distance codes is closely related to the length of irregular
b-
symbol distance code. Consequently, obtaining bounds on
N_{b}(\bm{B})
also provides valuable insight to determine the optimal redundancy.

Below we establish a lower bound on
N_{b}(\bm{B})

Lemma 4.1
.

For any matrix
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M\times M}
, we have

N_{b}(\bm{B})\geq\begin{cases}\frac{2q^{b}}{(q^{b}-1)M^{2}}\sum_{i,j,\,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij},&\text{if }M\equiv 0\pmod{q^{b}},\\[10.0pt]
\frac{2q^{b}}{(q^{b}-1)(M^{2}-1)}\sum_{i,j,\,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij},&\text{if }M\equiv 1\pmod{q^{b}},\\[10.0pt]
\frac{2q^{b}}{M^{2}(q^{b}-1)-m(q^{b}-m)}\sum_{i,j,\,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij},&\text{if }M\equiv m\pmod{q^{b}},\text{ }m>1\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{M}\}
be a
\bm{B}
-irregular
b-
symbol distance code of length
r
.

Also, let
\bm{P}
be a matrix of order
M\times r
whose rows are the
b-
symbol read vectors of codewords of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{S}=\sum_{i,j}d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{b}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{b})
and
M_{l}(\alpha)
denote number of times
\alpha

\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}
appears in
l
th column of
\bm{P}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}}M_{l}(\alpha)[M-M_{l}(\alpha)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(M\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}}M_{l}(\alpha)-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}}M_{l}^{2}(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(M^{2}-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}}M_{l}^{2}(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}M_{l}(\alpha)M_{l}(\beta)\right)

so,

\sum_{i,j,i\leq j}d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{S}=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}M_{l}(\alpha)M_{l}(\beta)\right).

Case 1:
If
M\equiv 0\pmod{q^{b}}
then,

\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}M_{l}(\alpha)M_{l}(\beta)\right)\text{ is maximized when each of }M_{l}(\alpha)=\frac{M}{q^{b}}.\text{ Hence we have }

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}\leq\sum_{i,j,i<j}d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})\leq\binom{q^{b}}{2}\left(\frac{M}{q^{b}}\right)^{2}r=\frac{(q^{b}-1)M^{2}}{2q^{b}}r

We have

N_{b}(D)\geq\frac{2q^{b}}{(q^{b}-1)M^{2}}\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}

Case 2:
If
M\equiv 1\pmod{q^{b}}
then,

\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}M_{l}(\alpha)M_{l}(\beta)\right)\text{ is maximized when }q^{b}-1\text{ of }M_{l}(\alpha)=\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}

\text{ and any one of them is equal to }\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}+1

So, we have

\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}\leq\sum_{i,j,i<j}d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})\leq\left[\binom{q^{b}-1}{2}\left(\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}\right)^{2}+\left(q^{b}-1\right)\left(\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}\right)\left(\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}+1\right)\right]r\\

\implies\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}\leq\frac{(q^{b}-1)(M^{2}-1)}{2q^{b}}r

So, we have

N_{b}(D)\geq\frac{2q^{b}}{(q^{b}-1)(M^{2}-1)}\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}

Case 3
: If
M\equiv m\pmod{q^{b}}
where
m>1
then,

\sum_{l=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}M_{l}(\alpha)M_{l}(\beta)\right)\text{ is maximized when }q^{b}-m\text{ of }M_{l}(\alpha)=\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}

\text{ and rest $m$ of them is equal to }\frac{M-1}{q^{b}}+1

So, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j,i<j}d_{b}(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{p}_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\left[\binom{q^{b}-m}{2}\left(\frac{M-m}{q^{b}}\right)^{2}+m\left(q^{b}-m\right)\left(\frac{M-m}{q^{b}}\right)\left(\frac{M-m}{q^{b}}+1\right)+\binom{m}{2}\left(\frac{M-m}{q^{b}}+1\right)^{2}\right]r

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{M^{2}(q^{b}-1)-m(q^{b}-m)}{2q^{b}}\right]r

So, we have

N_{b}(\bm{B})\geq\frac{2q^{b}}{M^{2}(q^{b}-1)-m(q^{b}-m)}\sum_{i,j,i<j}[\bm{B}]_{ij}

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The Lemma
4.1
generalizes the Plotkin-like bound
[
10
]
. Take
[\bm{B}]_{ij}=D
for
i\neq j
then from Lemma
4.1
we get,
N_{b}(M,D)\geq\frac{q^{b}(M-1)}{M(q^{b}-1)}
, which is clearly a variant of the Plotkin-like bound for
b
-symbol codes.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any distance matrix
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{N}^{M\times M}_{0}
and any permutation
\pi:\{1,2,\ldots,M\}\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
, we have

N_{b}(\bm{B})\leq\min_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{r\ \middle|\ q^{r}>\max_{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}([\bm{B}]_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}-1)|\right\},

where
\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}(t)=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}\ |d_{b}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq t\}
.

Proof.

We present an iterative way of selecting valid codewords for constructing an irregular
b-
symbol distance code for any distance matrix
B
(
\bm{B_{b}-}
code).

At first, for the sake of simplicity, choose
\pi
as the identity permutation. Select any arbitrary vector
\bm{p_{1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}
as the first codeword of the
\bm{B_{b}-}
code. Make a selection for the second codeword
\bm{p_{2}}
so that the distance requirement condition,
d_{b}(\bm{p_{1}},\bm{p_{2}})\geq[\bm{B}]_{12}
is satisfied. The existence of
\bm{p_{2}}
is guaranteed if
q^{r}>|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}([\bm{B}]_{12}-1)|
. Then choose the third codeword
\bm{p_{3}}
s.t.
d_{b}(\bm{p_{1}},\bm{p_{3}})\geq[\bm{B}]_{13}
and
d_{b}(\bm{p_{2}},\bm{p_{3}})\geq[\bm{B}]_{23}
. For
\bm{p_{3}}
, the existence is guaranteed if
q^{r}>|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}([\bm{B}]_{13}-1)|+|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}([\bm{B}]_{23}-1)|
. Continuing this process, we can select all codewords up to
\bm{p_{M}}
under the given condition of the lemma. Also, since codewords can be selected in any order, the lemma holds for any permutation
\pi
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The Lemma
4.3
is a generalization of the Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
13
]
and for
[\bm{B}]_{ij}=D
, the lower bound in Lemma 4.3 becomes

N_{b}(M,D)\leq\min_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{r\ \middle|\ q^{r}>(M-1)|\mathcal{B}^{b}_{r}([\bm{B}]_{ij})|\right\},

which is clearly a variant of the Gilbert-Varshamov bound.

The next lemma connects
N_{b+1}(M,D)
with
N_{b}(M,D)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M
and
D
be positive integers with
M>q^{b}
and
D\geq b-1
. Then

N_{b+1}(M,D)\leq N_{b}(M,D-1).

Proof.

Let
r=N_{b}(M,D-1)
. Then there exists a code
\mathcal{P}=\{\bm{p_{1}},\dots,\bm{p_{M}}\}
of length
r
such that

d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq D-1\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}\text{ with }i\neq j.

By the Singleton bound for the
b-
symbol metric
[
12
]
, we have

q^{r-(D-1)+b}\geq M>q^{b}

which implies

r-(D-1)+b>b\implies r>D-1.

If
d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})<r
, then

d_{b+1}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})+1\geq D-1+1=D.

If
d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})=r
, then
d_{b+1}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})=d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})=r\geq D
.

It follows that
N_{b+1}(M,D)\leq r=N_{b}(M,D-1)
.
∎

The next lemma gives a relation between the shortest length of irregular distance codes
N_{H}(M,D)
and the shortest length of irregular
b-
symbol distance code
N_{b}(M,D)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
M
and
D
be positive integers with
M>q^{b-1}
and
D\geq b
. Then

N_{b}(M,D)\leq N_{H}(M,D-b+1).

Proof.

Let
r=N_{H}(M,D-b+1)
. Then there exists a code
\mathcal{P}=\{\bm{p_{1}},\dots,\bm{p_{M}}\}
of length
r
such that

d_{H}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq D-b+1\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}\text{ with }i\neq j.

By the Singleton bound in the Hamming metric
[
15
]
, we have

q^{r-(D-b+1)+1}\geq M>q^{b-1}

which implies

r-(D-b+1)+1>b-1\implies r>D-1.

If
d_{H}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\leq r-b+1
, then

d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})\geq d_{H}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})+b-1\geq D-b+1+b-1=D.

If
d_{H}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p_{j}})>r-b+1
, then
d_{b}(p_{i},p_{j})=r\geq D
.

It follows that
N_{b}(M,D)\leq r=N_{H}(M,D-b+1)
.

To utilize the above relation to obtain bounds on
N_{b}(M,D)
we first establish upper bounds on
N_{H}(M,D)
for q-ary codes.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

(
[
16
, Def.4.32]
) Let

N_{H}(M,D)\leq\frac{qD}{q-1}.

Combining the result of Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

and

Proof.

From

, an upper bound can be determined for the size of a Hamming ball
V_{q}(r,D-1)=\sum_{i=0}^{D-1}\binom{r}{i}(q-1)^{i}
, provided

. Specifically, this bound is given by:

V_{q}(r,D-1)\leq q^{r}e^{-\frac{q^{2}r}{2(q-1)}\left(\frac{q-1}{q}-\frac{D-1}{r}\right)^{2}}.

Furthermore, using the Gilbert-Varshamov (GV) bound established in Remark 4.4, we know that
q^{r}>MV_{q}(r,D-1)
. Combining these two inequalities yields:

q^{r}>Mq^{r}e^{-\frac{q^{2}r}{2(q-1)}\left(\frac{q-1}{q}-\frac{D-1}{r}\right)^{2}}.

This inequality implies the existence of a code of size

, minimum distance
D

Now, setting

For

.
∎

Lemma 5.1

For any

locally binary function such that

2(t-b+1)\leq r_{b}^{f}(k,t)\leq 2t-b+1

Proof.

The lower bound directly results from Corollary
3.3
We provide the construction of a function-correcting

b
-locally binary function whose length of redundancy is

For
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{F}^{k}_{q}

0,&otherwise\end{cases}

and

Let
\bm{x}
and

Case 1:

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
with the help of smod operator(
c\text{ smod }d=((c-1)\text{ mod }d)+1
) where redundancy vectors
\bm{p^{\prime}s}
are defined as follows: Let
\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{2t+1}
be a collection of
2t+1
codewords with minimum distance
2t
i.e.

min\{d_{b}(\bm{p_{i}},\bm{p}_{j}):i,j\leq 2t+1,i\neq j\}=2t.

The rest of the redundancy vectors are defined with the help of the smod operator
\bm{p_{i}}=\bm{p}_{ismod(2t+1)}
.

We claim that the above encoding function defines a function-correcting
b-
symbol code for
b-
symbol weight functions.

Proof.

For any
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
such that
wt_{b}(\bm{x})\neq wt_{b}(\bm{x^{\prime}})
,

d_{b}(Enc(\bm{x}),Enc(\bm{x^{\prime}}))\geq d_{b}(\bm{x},\bm{x^{\prime}})+d_{b}(p(\bm{x}),p(\bm{x^{\prime}}))-(b-1)\geq b+2t-b+1=2t+1.

Therefore, the above encoding function defines a function-correcting
b-
symbol code for
b-
symbol weight functions.
∎

7
b
-Symbol Weight Distribution Functions

b-
symbol distribution functions are an important class of functions in coding theory, as these functions provide valuable insights into the
error detection and correction
capabilities of a code.

For a parameter of choice
T\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T\geq\lfloor\frac{b+2}{2}\rfloor
, let
f(\bm{x})
be a
b
-weight distribution function defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
as follows

f(\bm{x})=\Delta_{b}^{T}(\bm{x})=\lfloor\frac{wt_{b}(\bm{x})}{T}\rfloor.

For mathematical simplicity, we consider only those
T
for which
T
divides
k+1
. In this way
f(\bm{x})
behaves as a step function based on
b-
symbol weight, with the steps equal to
E=\frac{k+1}{T}
.

We present a construction of function-correcting
b-
symbol codes for the
b
-symbol weight distribution function. This construction is based on assigning the same redundancy vector to information vectors that share the same
b
-symbol weight with respect to
\text{smod }T
.

Construction 7.1
.

Let
Enc_{f}
define an encoding map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+2t}
as follows

Enc_{f}(\bm{x})=(\bm{x},\bm{p}_{(wt_{b}(\bm{x})+1)smodT})

where
\bm{p}_{(wt_{b}(\bm{x})+1)smodT}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2t}
and for some nonzero element
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
it is defined as follows:

p_{i}=\begin{cases}(a^{i-1}0^{2t-i+1}),&if\quad 1\leq i\leq 2t+1,\\
(a^{2t}),&if\quad 2t+2\leq i\leq T.\end{cases}

Lemma 7.2
.

For any given
k,T
with
k\geq b
and
T\geq\lfloor\frac{b+2}{2}\rfloor
such that
T
divides
k+1
and
T
greater than or equal to
2t+1
,
Enc_{f}
defines a function-correcting
b-
symbol code for the
b
-weight distribution function with redundancy equal to 2t.

Proof.

For any
\bm{x_{1}}
and
\bm{x_{2}}
such that
f(\bm{x_{1}})\neq f(\bm{x_{2}})
, we consider the following two cases:

Case 1:
d_{b}(\bm{x_{1}},\bm{x_{2}})\geq 2t+1
.

In this case, the result is straightforward, as
d_{b}(Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{1}}),Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{2}}))\geq 2t+1
holds directly.

Case 2:
d_{b}(\bm{x_{1}},\bm{x_{2}})\leq 2t

We note that
T\geq 2t+1
. So,
d_{b}(\bm{x_{1}},\bm{x_{2}})<T
i.e.,
wt_{b}(\bm{x_{1}}-\bm{x_{2}})<T\implies|\frac{wt_{b}(\bm{x_{1}})}{T}-\frac{wt_{b}(\bm{x_{2}})}{T}|<1.

Since
f(\bm{x_{1}})\neq f(\bm{x_{2}})
this implies that
f(\bm{x_{1}})
and
f(\bm{x_{2}})
differ by exactly one. Without loss of generality, assume that
f(\bm{x_{1}})=m-1
and
f(\bm{x_{2}})=m
.

Hence, the
b
-weights of
\bm{x_{1}}
and
\bm{x_{2}}
can then be expressed as:

wt_{b}(\bm{x_{1}})=(m-1)T+w_{1},\quad wt_{b}(\bm{x_{2}})=mT+w_{2}.

Subcase 2.1:
T>b

For
m=1
:
w_{1}\in\{0,b,\ldots,T-1\}
and
w_{2}\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}

For
m\geq 2
:
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}

Subcase 2.2:
T<b
and
T\geq\lfloor\frac{b+2}{2}\rfloor

For
m=1
:
w_{1}=0
and
w_{2}\in\{0,b,\ldots,T-1\}

For
m=2
:
w_{1}\in\{0,b,\ldots,T-1\}
and
w_{2}\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
.
First, we try to prove for
T=2t+1,

In this particular case,
\bm{p}(\bm{x_{1}})=\bm{p}_{w_{1}+1}=(a^{w_{1}}0^{2t-w_{1}})
and
\bm{p}(\bm{x_{2}})=\bm{p}_{w_{2}+1}=(a^{w_{2}}0^{2t-w_{2}})
.

So,

\displaystyle d_{b}(Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{1}}),Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{2}}))

\displaystyle\geq d_{b}(\bm{x_{1}},\bm{x_{2}})+d_{b}(\bm{p}(\bm{x_{1}}),\bm{p}(\bm{x_{2}}))-(b-1).

\displaystyle\geq|wt_{b}(\bm{x_{1}})-wt_{b}(\bm{x_{2}})|+|w_{1}-w_{2}|+b-1-(b-1)

\displaystyle\geq(T-w_{1}+w_{2})+(w_{1}-w_{2}).

\displaystyle=T=2t+1.

For
T>2t+1,
we consider the following three cases:

1.

if
w_{1}\geq 2t
, then
d_{b}(p(\bm{x_{1}}),p(\bm{x_{2}}))=2t-w_{2}+b-1
. So,

\displaystyle d_{b}(Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{1}}),Enc_{f}(\bm{x_{2}}))

\displaystyle\geq T-w_{1}+w_{2}+2t-w_{2}+b-1-(b-1)

\displaystyle\geq T+2t-w_{1}.

\displaystyle\geq T>2t+1.

2.

if
w_{2}\geq 2t
, then
d_{b}(p(\bm{x_{1}}),p(\bm{x_{2}}))=2t-w_{1}+b-1
.

3.

w_{1}<2t
and
w_{2}<2t
, then
d_{b}(p(\bm{x_{1}}),p(\bm{x_{2}}))=|w_{1}-w_{2}|
. These cases can be proven similarly.

∎

3.3
we conclude the following.

,

k+1

9
Conclusion

Function-correcting b-symbol codes represent a more advanced and efficient error correction strategy, aligning well with the requirements of modern data storage systems that rely on
b-
symbol read channels. They offer practical advantages in terms of speed, capacity, and error recovery, which are crucial to meeting the demands of today’s high-performance storage devices.
We developed function-correcting
b
-symbol codes for functions over finite fields to ensure the protection of function values evaluated on messages against errors introduced by a
b-
symbol read channel. By establishing bounds on the length of irregular-distance codes and irregular
b-
symbol distance codes over finite fields, and leveraging the relationship between function-correcting
b-
symbol codes and irregular
b-
symbol distance codes, we derived both lower and upper bounds on the optimal redundancy of generic functions. For certain classes of functions, we have demonstrated that these codes can achieve significantly lower redundancy compared to classical
b-
symbol codes.

Category 1:

Abstract
. We consider a stochastic parabolic partial differential equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions, multiplicative stochastic noise, and a monotone parabolic operator A A . The growth and coercivity of A A is controlled by a general N N -function M M , which depends on time, and spatial variable, but we do not assume any regularity with respect to the former. We show the existence of weak solutions to such system. As auxiliary result, we also provide the proof for the Itô’s formula in Orlicz spaces. This general result applies to the ones studied in the literature, such as p ⁡ ( t , x ) p(t,x) -Laplacian and double phase problems.

2.
Musielak–Orlicz spaces

In this section we recall basic theory of Musielak–Orlicz spaces. For more precise information we refer to an extensive book
[
6
]
and an article on the application to the deterministic PDEs
[
3
]
.

In what follows, we let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain, and fix arbitrary time horizon
T>0
. With this, we denote
Q_{T}:=(0,T)\times D
.

2.1.
Basic definitions and properties

Definition 2.1

(Young function)
.

We say that
m:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a Young function if it satisfies

(Y1)

m(s)=0\Longleftrightarrow s=0
,

(Y2)

m
is convex,

(Y3)

m
is superlinear, i.e.
\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{m(s)}{s}=0
,
\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{m(s)}{s}=+\infty
.

Remark 2.2
.

We recall that, since
m
is convex and nonnegative, from
(Y1)
it follows that
m
is strictly increasing.

Definition 2.3

(
N
-function)
.

We say that
M:(0,T)\times D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an
N
-function if it satisfies

(N1)

M(t,x,\xi)=M(t,x,-\xi)
for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(N2)

M(t,x,\xi)
is a Carathéodory function, that is for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
the mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\xi\mapsto M(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function, and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the mapping
(0,T)\times D\ni(t,x)\mapsto M(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is measurable.

(N3)

for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
the map
\xi\mapsto M(t,x,\xi)
is convex,

(N4)

there exist two Young functions
\overline{m}
,
m
, such that for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\overline{m}(|\xi|)\leq M(t,x,\xi)\leq m(|\xi|).

Definition 2.4

(Isotropic
N
-function)
.

We say that the
N
-function
M
is isotropic if

M(t,x,\xi)=M(t,x,|\xi|).

Definition 2.5

(Convex conjugate)
.

Let
m
be a Young function. Then we define its convex conjugate
m^{*}
as

m^{*}(s)=\sup_{x\in[0,+\infty)}(s\,x-m(x)).

For the
N
-function
M
, we define its convex conjugate
M^{*}
by

M^{*}(t,x,\eta)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\xi\cdot\eta-M(t,x,\xi)).

Lemma 2.6
.

(Basic properties of
N
- and Young functions)
. Let
m
be a Young function and
M
be an
N
-function. Then

•

Function
\frac{m(t)}{t}
is nondecreasing.

•

m^{*}
is a Young function.

•

M^{*}
is an
N
-functiom.

•

If
f_{n}:Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a sequence of functions such that
\int_{Q_{T}}M(t,x,f_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C
for some
C>1
, then
\|f_{n}\|_{L_{M}}\leq C
.

Definition 2.7

(Musielak–Orlicz space
L_{M}(Q_{T})
)
.

Let
M
be an
N
-function. Then, the Musielak–Orlicz space
L_{M}(Q_{T})
is defined as

L_{M}(Q_{T}):=\left\{f:Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\text{there exists }\lambda>0\text{ such that }\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<+\infty\right\}.

This is a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{M}(Q_{T})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq 1\right\}.

If
m
is a Young function, then similarly we can define a Musielak–Orlicz space
L_{m}(Q_{T})
(or simply Orlicz in this case).

One can prove the so-called Fenchel–Young inequality and a form of Hölder’s inequality in Musielak–Orlicz spaces.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M
be an
N
-function and
M^{*}
be its convex conjugate. Then, for every
f\in L_{M}(Q_{T})
,
g\in L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
the following inequalities are true

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}f\,g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{Q_{T}}M(t,x,f)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{Q_{T}}M^{*}(t,x,g)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}f\,g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\|f\|_{L_{M}(Q_{T})}\|g\|_{L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})}.

2.2.
Notions of convergence in Musielak–Orlicz spaces

Obviously one can define convergence in norm, in Musielak–Orlicz spaces. The other useful notion is the so-called modular convergence.

Definition 2.9

(Modular convergence in
L_{M}(Q_{T})
)
.

Given a sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{M}(Q_{T})
, we say that it converges to
f
modularly, denoted by
\xrightarrow{M}
, if there exists
\lambda>0
, such that

\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{f_{n}-f}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

By convexity it follows, that if
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{M}(Q_{T})
and
f_{n}\xrightarrow{M}f
modularly in
L_{M}(Q_{T})
, then
f\in L_{M}(Q_{T})
. Due to the Vitali convergence theorem, we obtain the following characterization of the modular convergence.

Proposition 2.10
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.4.4]
)

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{M}(Q_{T})
and
f\in L_{M}(Q_{T})
. Then,
f_{n}\xrightarrow{M}f
modularly in
L_{M}(Q_{T})
if, and only if

(V1)

f_{n}\rightarrow f
in measure,

(V2)

there exists
\lambda>0
, such that
\left\{M\left(t,x,\frac{f_{n}}{\lambda}\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly equiintegrable.

Due to the above, we obtain the following corollary, which gives us the ability to converge in products, even without the information on the strong convergence of the sequences.

Proposition 2.11
.

(
[
3
, Corollary 1.11]
)

Suppose
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{M}(Q_{T})
and
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
be such that
f_{n}\xrightarrow{M}f
modularly in
L_{M}(Q_{T})
and
g_{n}\xrightarrow{M^{*}}g
modularly in
L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
. Then,
f_{n}\,g_{n}\rightarrow f\,g
strongly in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
.

Moving forward, we want to define weak* convergence in Musielak–Orlicz spaces, the basic intuition would be that
(L_{M}(Q_{T}))^{*}=L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
, but unfortunately, this is not always true. Therefore, we need to define a particular subspace.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
M
be an
N
-function. We define a space
E_{M}(Q_{T})
as

E_{M}(Q_{T}):=\left\{f:Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\text{for every }\lambda>0\text{ we have }\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<+\infty\right\}.

Similarly if
m
is a Young function, then we can define a function space
E_{m}(Q_{T})
.

With the above definition in mind, we can state the following result on the weak* convergence.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
M
be an
N
-function. Then,
E_{M}(Q_{T})
is separable and
(E_{M}(Q_{T}))^{*}=L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
. In particular, any bounded sequence in
L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T})
has a weak* convergent subsequence.

is quite hidden in our work, and not used explicitly, but very much necessary. It gives good approximation behaviour in
L_{M}(Q_{T})
space, which can be seen in Proposition
C.1
. When compared to earlier works (cf.
[
8
,
6
]
) one can notice that we have a lower exponent in the third variable of the function
\Theta
, that is
\Theta(t,\delta,C\delta^{-1})
, instead of
\Theta(t,\delta,C\delta^{-d})
. This is because of the observation made in
[
3
]
, which is the fact that one only has to have a good approximation of the functions of the form

\nabla_{x}(T_{k}(u)+\phi),\quad\nabla_{x}u\in L_{M}(Q_{T}),\,\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T}),

see Proposition
C.1
. At last, we would like to also mention, that Assumption
3.1
in the less general variable exponent spaces
L^{p(t,x)}(Q_{T})
implies the log-Hölder continuity (in space) of the exponent
p
, which can be seen in the works such as
[
4
,
5
]
. In our general case, the continuity in space of the function
M
is not necessarily implied. Indeed, it is quite easy to see that Assumption
3.1
implies the continuity (in space) of M iff

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Theta(t,\varepsilon,|\xi|)=1.

Assumption 3.1

(Assumption on the
N
-function
M
)
.

We assume that
M
is isotropic. Moreover, there exists a function
\Theta:(0,T)\times[0,1]\times[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
, which is nondecreasing with respect to the second and third variable, such that

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Theta(t,\delta,C\delta^{-1})\text{ is bounded uniformly in time }t\in(0,T),

and

\frac{M(t,x,r)}{M(t,y,r)}\leq\Theta(t,|x-y|,r).

Assumption 3.2

(Assumption on growth of the Young function)
.

There exists a Young function
m
, constant
B>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

M(t,x,\xi)\leq m(|\xi|),

and

\lim_{x\to+\infty}m(x)e^{-Bx^{1+\beta}}<+\infty.

As mentioned in the introduction, the following constraint on the growth of
M
is connected to good behaviour of the stochastic noise
\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)
, see Lemma
4.2
.

Assumption 3.3

(Assumptions on
A
)
.

We assume that
A:Q_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

(A1)

A(t,x,\xi)
is a Carathéodory function, that is for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
the mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\xi\mapsto A(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function, and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the mapping
(0,T)\times D\ni(t,x)\mapsto A(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is measurable.

(A2)

(coercivity and growth bound) there exists a constant
c>0
and a function
g\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and almost every
(t,x)\in Q_{T}

M(t,x,\xi)+M^{*}(t,x,A(t,x,\xi))\leq cA(t,x,\xi)\cdot\xi+g(t,x).

(A3)

(strict monotonicity) for every
\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}
we have

(A(t,x,\xi_{1})-A(t,x,\xi_{2}))\cdot(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})>0.

(A4)

for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}

A(t,x,0)=0
.

Proposition 3.4
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 1.22]
)

Let
A
satisfy Assumption
3.3
. Then, for every
K>0
, there exists a constant

depending on
K
, such that
|A(t,x,\xi)|\leq C(K)
for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
|\xi|\leq K
.

At last, we come to the assumption on the stochastic noise
h
, which are important whenever we consider a multiplicative case.

Assumption 3.5

(Assumptions on
h
)
.

We assume that
h:\Omega\times(0,T)\times L^{2}(D)\rightarrow HS(L^{2}(D))

h(\omega,t,u)(e_{j})=\{x\mapsto h_{j}(\omega,t,x,u(x))\},

and

(H1)

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
h_{j}:\Omega\times(0,T)\times D\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function, such that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
(\omega,t)\mapsto h_{j}(\omega,t,\cdot,\lambda)
is predictable with values in
L^{2}(D)
, and
\lambda\mapsto h_{j}(\omega,t,x,\lambda)
is continuous

-almost everywhere.

(H2)

There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
C_{3}\in L^{1}(D)
such that for a.e.
(\omega,t,x)
and all

we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|h_{j}(\cdot,\lambda)|^{2}\leq C_{1}|\lambda|^{2}+C_{3}(x),

and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|h_{j}(\cdot,\lambda_{1})-h_{j}(\cdot,\lambda_{2})|^{2}\leq C_{2}|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|^{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda_{1},\ \lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

3.2.
Main result

We start our discussion of the main result with a definition of the solution to the problem (

).

Definition 3.6
.

We will say that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega\times D))\cap L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T);W^{1,1}_{0}(D))
with

is a solution to the problem (

) iff for all

, a.s. in

,

(3.6)

\displaystyle u(t)-u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\DIV_{x}A(\tau,x,\nabla_{x}u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}h(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(\tau)\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(D).

Finally we may state our main result.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(D))
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable. Let
M
be an
N
-function satisfying the Assumption
3.1
, with its upper bound Young function

satisfying the Assumption
3.2
. Moreover, let

be an operator respecting the Assumption
3.3
, and the stochastic noise

satisfies Assumption
3.5
. Then, there exists a solution

, in the sense of the Definition
3.6
to the system (

), satisfying the energy equality

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\\
&\qquad=-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u)\cdot\nabla_{x}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\|h\|_{HS(L^{2}(D))}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Remark 3.8

4.
Additive case

We first prove Theorem
3.7
in the additive case, that is for
h
which is independent of
u
, i.e.,
h:\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow HS(L^{2}(D))
,
h(\omega,t,\lambda)(e_{j})=\{x\mapsto h_{j}(\omega,t,x,\lambda)\}
, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
h_{j}:\Omega\times(0,T)\times D\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies (H1) and (H2).

4.1.
Approximation of h

In the following, we will work with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
fixed. Our first step in the approximation procedure is the approximation of the function
h
. To this end, we project the Wiener process onto the finite-dimensional space. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define
h^{N}:\Omega\times(0,T)\rightarrow HS(L^{2}(D))
(projection onto the space spanned by first
N
vectors of the basis) by setting

\displaystyle h^{N}(e_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h(\omega,t,\lambda)(e_{j})\quad\text{ for }j\leq N,\\
0\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

and for
h^{N}
we consider a sequence of elementary processes
\{h^{N}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S^{2}_{W}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
(cf.
[
18
, Definition 2.3.1]
) for
s>1
large enough, such that
\partial_{x_{i}}h^{N}_{n}(e_{j})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
, for all
i=1,...,d
and all
j=1,...,N
, and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|h^{N}_{n}-h^{N}\|_{T}=0.

To avoid the overload of indexes we skip the subscript
n
and superscript
N
in the subsequent arguments.

Remark 4.1
.

We recall that from
[
9
, Proposition 4.20]
it follows that
\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];W^{s,2}_{0}(D)))
. Thanks to the regularity of
h
and with a slight abuse of notation, we may define
\nabla_{x}h\in\mathcal{N}^{2}_{W}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
, by setting
[\nabla_{x}h](e_{j})=\nabla_{x}h_{j}
. Then, since
\nabla_{x}
is a continuous operator from
W^{1,2}_{0}(D)
into
L^{2}(D)
, by
[
18
, Lemma 2.4.1]

it follows that

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)=\nabla_{x}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}h_{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta_{j}(s)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}h_{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta_{j}(s)=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s),

thus
\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{2}(D)))
.

Moreover, by Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality
[
19
, Theorem 3.2.47]
for any
r\geq 2
and any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{r}\right)\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}h\|_{HS}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{r/2}\right]\leq C\sup_{j\in\{1,...,N\}}\|\nabla_{x}h(e_{j})\|^{r}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times Q_{T})},

Thus
\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in L^{r}(\Omega\times(0,T);W^{1,2}_{0}(D))
, and there exists
C(\omega)>0
, such that for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}
we have
\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\leq C(\omega)
. In particular
\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
almost surely.
Similarly we can argue for
\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W
.

Although a previous remark gives us nice behavior of
\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W
for fixed
\omega
, we still need to extend it to the whole space.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{S}_{W}^{2}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
for
s
such that

W^{s-1,2}_{0}(D)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(D),

and let
m
satisfy the Assumption
3.2
. Then,
\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in E_{m}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
and
\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\cdot}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\in E_{m}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
.

Proof.

We only show the first inclusion, as the second one follows by the same argument. Since
h\in\mathcal{S}_{W}^{2}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
, then there exists a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{k}=T
and

\Phi_{l}:\Omega\rightarrow HS(L^{2}(D);W^{s,2}_{0}(D))\quad 0\leq l\leq k-1

taking only finitely many distinct values in
HS(L^{2}(D);W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
,
such that

h(t)=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\Phi_{l}\mathbf{1}_{(t_{l},t_{l+1}]}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

and as such

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}h(e_{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\Phi_{l}(e_{j})(\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t)).

Take an arbitrary
\lambda>0
. By convexity

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}m\left(\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right|}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\ldots,N\\
l=0,\ldots k-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{j,l}}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}m\left(\frac{\left|\Phi_{l}(e_{j})(\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t))\right|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega),

where
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\ldots,N\\
l=0,\ldots k-1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{l,j}=\lambda
, with
\lambda_{l,j}\geq 0
. As the sum on the right-hand side is finite, we will simply show finiteness of any summand. To this end, we fix
j\in 1,\ldots N
and
l\in\{0,...,k-1\}
. Recalling that
\Phi_{l}
takes only finitely many values in
HS(L^{2}(D);W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
, let the image of
\Phi_{l}
be equal to
\{L_{l}^{1},...,L_{l}^{n_{l}}\}
for some
n_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
. For
A_{\nu}:=\Phi_{l}^{-1}(L_{l}^{\nu})
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}m\left(\frac{\left|\Phi_{l}(e_{j})(\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t))\right|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\\
&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n_{l}}\int_{A_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}m\left(\frac{\left|L_{l}^{\nu}(e_{j})(\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t))\right|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega).\end{split}

Again, to show finiteness of the whole sum we may focus on a single summand. Using the fact that
m
is nondecreasing and
L_{l}^{\nu}
is in
L^{\infty}(D)
for any
\nu\in\{1,\ldots,n_{l}\}
we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}m\left(\frac{\left|L_{l}^{\nu}(e_{j})(\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t))\right|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\\
&\leq|D|\int_{A_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}m\left(\frac{\|L_{l}^{\nu}(e_{j})\|_{\infty}|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t)|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\\
\end{split}

In the following, we define
C(l):=\sup_{\nu\in\{1,...,n_{l}\}}\|L_{l}^{\nu}(e_{j})\|_{\infty}
.
Next, we estimate

\begin{split}&\leq|D|\int_{A_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}m\left(\frac{\|L_{l}^{\nu}(e_{j})\|_{\infty}|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t)|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\\
&\leq|D|\int_{A_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t)|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\\
&\leq|D|\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1}\wedge t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l}\wedge t)|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&=|D|\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[{m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l})|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&+|D|\int_{t_{l+1}}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1})-\beta_{j}(t_{l})|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Now, we recall that, for any
t>t_{l}
,
\beta_{j}(t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,t-t_{l})
and,
\beta_{j}(t_{l+1})-\beta_{j}(t_{l})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,t_{l+1}-t_{l})
. Then, since
m
is non-negative and Borel measurable on
[0,\infty)
,
\Delta t:=\max_{l=0,\ldots k-1}t_{l+1}-t_{l}
, and the monotonicity of the Young function
m
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[{m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t)-\beta_{j}(t_{l})|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)}\right]=\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-t_{l})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2(t-t_{l})}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-t_{l})}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta t}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

Using Assumption
3.2
it follows that there exists
B,K>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
m(|r|)\leq Ke^{B|r|^{1+\beta}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\frac{\widetilde{C}(l)}{\lambda_{l,j}^{1+\beta}}|r|^{1+\beta}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=K\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(|r|(K_{1}|r|^{\beta}-K_{2}|r|)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r

For
K_{1},K_{2}>0
. If we choose

M\geq\max\left(1,\left(\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}\right),

it follows that

(K_{1}|r|^{\beta}-K_{2}|r|)\leq-K_{4}|r|

for all
|r|\geq M
with
K_{4}=K_{2}(1-\frac{K_{1}}{K_{1}+K_{2}})>0
. Therefore, there exists
K_{3}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\leq K_{3}+\int_{\{|r|>M\}}\exp\left(-K_{4}|r|^{2}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r<\infty.

In the same manner,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{l+1}}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{m\left(\frac{C(l)|\beta_{j}(t_{l+1})-\beta_{j}(t_{l})|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{t_{l+1}}^{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t_{l+1}-t_{l})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{T-t_{l+1}}{\sqrt{2\pi(t_{l+1}-t_{l})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m\left(\frac{C(l)|r|}{\lambda_{l,j}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Delta t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r<\infty.

Since the above is true for arbitrary nonnegative
\lambda_{l,j}
, therefore our
\lambda>0
can indeed be arbitrary, and we obtain the desired result.
∎

4.2.
Approximation of the operator

For fixed
h
as in Subsection
4.1
, let
m
be a Young function given by
(N4)
and consider an approximate operator

(4.1)

\displaystyle A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\nabla_{x}u)=A(t,x,\nabla_{x}u)+\varepsilon\nabla_{\xi}m(|\nabla_{x}u|),

and the equation

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u^{\varepsilon}-\DIV_{x}A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\nabla_{x}u^{\varepsilon})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W.

The main advantage of such approximation is that now
A^{\varepsilon}
belongs to
L_{m^{*}}
instead of

, which is detailed in the following proposition. The proof can be found in the proof of Lemma 3.9 in
[

]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
A
satisfies Assumption
3.3
,
M
is a N-function, and
m
is a Young function such that
M(t,x,\xi)\leq m(|\xi|)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
. Then
A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi):=A(t,x,\xi)+\varepsilon\nabla_{\xi}m(|\xi|)
satisfies

(Ap1)

A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)
is a Carathéodory function, that is for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times D
the mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\xi\mapsto A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function, and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the mapping
(0,T)\times D\ni(t,x)\mapsto A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
is measurable.

(Ap2)

(coercivity and growth bound) there exists a constant
c^{\varepsilon}>0
and a function
g^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and almost every
(t,x)\in Q_{T}

m(|\xi|)+m^{*}(|A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)|)\leq c^{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\cdot\xi+g^{\varepsilon}(t,x).

(Ap3)

(strict monotonicity) for every
\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}
we have

(A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi_{1})-A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi_{2}))\cdot(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})>0.

(Ap4)

for a.e.
(t,x)\in Q_{T}

A^{\varepsilon}(t,x,0)=0

\displaystyle\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)

\displaystyle\text{ weakly* in }L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T}).

Moreover, fix a cut-off function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
0\leq\psi\leq 1
. Due to the use of the truncation operator
T_{k}
we can now apply Proposition
C.1
to conclude that there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{\infty}(D))
such that
\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi
converges to
\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi
modularly (and by Proposition
2.10
also almost everywhere).
In the following argument let
\chi^{s}
denote a characteristic function of the set
\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:\,\,|\nabla_{x}w|\leq s\}
and
\chi_{j}^{s}
of
\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:\,\,|\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}|\leq s\}
. To start, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s})\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)(\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s})\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon}))\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=:I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{1}+I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{2}+I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{3}.

Our goal is to converge in the following order: first with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, then with
j\to\infty
, and finally with
s\to\infty
. For
I_{1}
by Proposition
3.4
and (
4.19
) we may converge with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
to get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{1}=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=:I^{j,s}_{1}.

Note that we can write equivalently

\displaystyle I^{j,s}_{1}=\int_{Q_{T}}\chi_{j}^{s}\,A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Using Proposition
3.4
and the modular convergence of
\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q_{T}}\chi_{j}^{s}\,A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|\lesssim\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}|\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\rightarrow 0\text{ as }j\to\infty.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to+\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}I^{j,s}_{1}=0.

Moving to
I_{2}^{\varepsilon,j,s}
we may conclude by (
4.20
) and Lemma
4.11

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{2}^{\varepsilon,j,s}=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)(\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=:I^{j,s}_{2}.

To converge here we may note that

\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi\rightarrow\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\,\psi\text{ a.e. in }Q_{T}

and by convexity of
M

M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi}{\lambda}\right)=\chi_{j}^{s}\,M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi}{\lambda}\right)\\
\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi}{\lambda_{1}}\right)+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi}{\lambda_{2}}\right),

where
\lambda:=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
, and
\lambda_{1}>0
is such that

M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\to\infty.

(which exists by modular convergence of
\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\psi
to
\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi
) and for
\lambda_{2}>0

M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\in L^{1}(Q_{T}).

Hence
\left\{M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi}{\lambda}\right)\right\}_{j}
is equiintegrable and by Proposition
2.10

(4.21)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi\xrightarrow{M}\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\,\psi\quad\text{ modularly in }L_{M}(Q_{T}).

In the end, by Proposition
2.11

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}I_{2}^{j,s}=0.

To converge in
I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{3}
we utilize once again Proposition
3.4
together with (
4.19
) to get

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{3}^{\varepsilon,j,s}=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w))\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=0.

Combining everything together we have proven

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\delta(\varepsilon,j,s),

where
\delta(\varepsilon,j,s)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
j\to+\infty
,
s\to+\infty
. Thus, for every
s\geq r>0
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|\nabla_{x}w|\leq r\}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w))\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\leq\int_{\{|\nabla_{x}v|\leq s\}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w))\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\leq\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{T}}\left(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta(\varepsilon,j,s)

\displaystyle=-\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\right)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s})\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\right)\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta(\varepsilon,j,s)

\displaystyle=J^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{1}+J^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{2}+J^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{3}+\delta(\varepsilon,j,s).

Similarly to
I^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{1}
we can deduce

J^{\varepsilon,j,s}_{1}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+},j\to+\infty,s\to+\infty.

At the same time by, arguing the same way as for (
4.18
), but instead using Proposition
4.7
and (
4.15
)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}J_{2}^{\varepsilon,j,s}\leq J_{2}:=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

To treat the third term, we simply apply (
4.20
) to get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}J_{3}^{\varepsilon,j,s}=-\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\nabla_{x}\phi_{j}\,\chi_{j}^{s}\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=:J_{3}^{j,s}.

Moreover, by (
4.21
)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}J_{3}^{j,s}=-\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\chi^{s}\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=:J_{3}^{s}.

Now, we can write

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{3}^{s}=\int_{\{|\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)|>s\}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

which converges to
0
, as
s\to+\infty
, since
A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\right)\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\,\psi\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
.

Thus, as
A
is strictly monotone by
(A3)
, up to the subsequence

\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\text{ a.e. in }\{|\nabla_{x}v|\leq r\}\cap\{\psi\equiv 1\},

and by diagonal argument a.e. in
Q_{T}
. Hence, by applying de la Vallée Poussin criterion and the Vitali convergence theorem

\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(Q_{T}).

At last, we can show the stated almost everywhere convergence. We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{Q_{T}}\sqrt{|\nabla_{x}w^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}w|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{Q_{T}}\sqrt{|\nabla_{x}w^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})|}+\sqrt{|\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w^{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)|}+\sqrt{|\nabla_{x}T_{k}(w)-\nabla_{x}w|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}C\left(\|\nabla_{x}w^{\varepsilon}\|_{1}^{1/2}|\{|w^{\varepsilon}>k\}|^{1/2}+\|\nabla_{x}w\|_{1}^{1/2}|\{|w>k\}|^{1/2}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{k}}.

Letting
k\to+\infty
we obtain the thesis up to the subsequence.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Suppose
v^{\varepsilon}
is as in Lemma
4.8
and
v
is given in Lemma
4.10
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{M}\nabla_{x}v\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ modularly in }L_{M}(Q_{T}),

\displaystyle A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{M^{*}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ modularly in }L_{M^{*}}(Q_{T}).

Proof.

The proof is directly the same as, for example in
[
13
]
, but we will repeat it for the convenience of the reader. Take an arbitrary subsequence of
\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}
. By Lemma
4.13
there exists a subsequence that converges almost everywhere in
Q_{T}
. By the continuity of
A
on its third variable

A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\rightarrow A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)

a.e. in
Q_{T}
as well. Moreover, by Fatou’s lemma

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
\geq\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

and by (
4.18
)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{Q_{t}}A\left(s,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{s}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{s}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
\leq\int_{Q_{t}}A\left(s,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{s}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{s}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Hence,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
=\int_{Q_{T}}A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Noticing that

\begin{split}&\int_{Q_{T}}\Bigg|A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\\
&\qquad-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\Bigg|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{Q_{T}}\Bigg(A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\\
&\qquad-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\Bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&+2\int_{Q_{T}}\Bigg[A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\\
&\qquad-A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\Bigg]_{-}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

we conclude by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence theorem

A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\rightarrow A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right),

strongly in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
. By Dunford–Pettis theorem, this implies uniform equiintegrability, which in turn, by the coercivity and growth condition
(A2)
yields the uniform bound and equiintegrability in
L^{1}
of
\{M(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
\left\{M^{*}\left(t,x,A\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\right)\right\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. By Proposition
2.10
and the standard subsequence argument we obtain the thesis.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Suppose
v^{\varepsilon}
is as in Lemma
4.8
and

is given as in Lemma
4.10
. Then,

Lemma 4.18
.

Suppose
v^{\varepsilon}
is as in Lemma
4.8
, and
v
is given in Lemma
4.10
. Then

\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{M}\nabla_{x}v\quad\text{ modularly in }L_{M}(\Omega\times Q_{T}).

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary subsequence of
\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}
and take its subsequence which satisfies Lemma
4.13
. From Lemma
4.14
we know that there exists
\lambda_{1}>0
such that

\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}v}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\rightarrow 0.

Thus, by (
4.8
) and the convexity, for
\lambda_{2}:=1+\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}v}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}v}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\|g\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\int_{Q_{T}}m\left(2\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right|\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,2\,\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we obtain

\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}+\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,2\,\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

Hence, for
\lambda:=1+\lambda_{2}
, by (
4.8
), convexity, and the symmetry of
M
with respect to the third variable, we can estimate

\begin{split}&\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}v}{\lambda}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda}\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\frac{\nabla_{x}v+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,\nabla_{x}v^{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}+\int_{Q_{T}}m\left(2\left|\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right|\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{Q_{T}}M\left(t,x,2\,\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

As the right-hand side is integrable with respect to the probability measure due to Lemma

∎

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
M
be an N-function satisfying Assumption
3.1
.

Suppose

with
\nabla_{x}u\in L_{M}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
satisfies almost surely the equation

(4.32)

\displaystyle u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\DIV_{x}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(D),

for
\alpha\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
and

. Then, for
G_{k}
,
T_{k}
defined in (
3

3.3
), the following energy equality holds almost surely

\int_{D}G_{k}(u(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\alpha(s,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u),h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k}(u)\,|h(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Let
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
be an arbitrary function and
\rho_{\kappa}
be a family of standard mollification kernels in the space variable. For arbitrary function
f
we denote in the following

. Then, we can mollify (
4.32
) and multiply it by
\psi
to get

(4.33)

\displaystyle u^{\kappa}(t)\psi(x)=u_{0}^{\kappa}\psi(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(\DIV_{x}\alpha^{\kappa}(s,x))\psi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\left(\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right)^{\kappa}\psi(x).

Notice that the operator
L^{2}(D)\ni f\mapsto f^{\kappa}\psi\in W^{s,2}_{0}(D)
is linear and bounded (where
s
is big enough so that
W^{s,2}_{0}(D)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(D)
). Hence, by
[
18
, Lemma 2.4.1]

(4.34)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right)^{\kappa}\psi(x)=\int_{0}^{t}h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s),

where
h^{\kappa}\psi\in\mathcal{N}^{2}_{W}(0,T;W^{s,2}_{0}(D))
. Let
G_{k,\delta}
,
T_{k,\delta}
be as in (
3
). Define

F:W^{s,2}_{0}(D)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad F(u)=\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Then

DF:W^{s,2}_{0}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(W^{s,2}_{0}(D);\mathbb{R}),\qquad\langle DF(u),f\rangle=\int_{D}T_{k,\delta}(u)\,f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

and

D^{2}F:W^{s,2}_{0}(D):\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(W^{s,2}_{0}(D);W^{s,2}_{0}(D)),\qquad\langle D^{2}F(u)(f),g\rangle=\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k,\delta}(u)\,f\,g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

It is easy to check that
F
,
DF
,
D^{2}F
are globally Lipschitz, and thus uniformly continuous. In consequence, we may use the classical Itô’s lemma
C.4
in the equation (
4.33
) to obtain almost surely

(4.35)

\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}(t)\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u_{0}^{\kappa}\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\DIV_{x}\alpha^{\kappa}(s,x)\,T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})\psi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle D^{2}F(u^{\kappa})(h^{\kappa}\psi)(e_{j}),h^{\kappa}\psi(e_{j})\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Our goal now is to converge with
\kappa\to 0^{+}
and
\psi\to 1
,
\delta\to 0^{+}
in this order. By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem

\displaystyle\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}(t)\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u_{0}^{\kappa}\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\xrightarrow{\kappa\to 0^{+}}\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u(t)\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k,\delta}(u_{0}\psi(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

and after integrating by parts and using Proposition
C.1

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\DIV_{x}\alpha^{\kappa}(s,x)\,T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})\psi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\alpha(s,x)\cdot(\nabla_{x}(T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})\psi)^{\kappa}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
\rightarrow-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\alpha(s,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}(T_{k,\delta}(u)\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Thus we only need to treat the stochastic terms. First, we will show that

(4.36)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u),h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}\quad\mbox{ as }\kappa\to 0^{+}.

Denote
\mathbb{M}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)
. Then, by Itô’s isometry
[
18
, Proposition 2.3.5]

(4.37)

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}\right\|_{T}^{2}\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),(h(e_{j}))^{\kappa}\psi-h(e_{j})\psi\rangle^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{D}T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})((h(e_{j}))^{\kappa}\psi-h(e_{j})\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|(h(e_{j}))^{\kappa}\psi-h(e_{j})\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}=\|h^{\kappa}\psi-h\psi\|_{T}^{2}\\
&\phantom{=}=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)-\int_{0}^{t}h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{T}^{2}(L^{2}(D))}\\
&\phantom{=}=\left\|\mathbb{M}(t)^{\kappa}\psi-\mathbb{M}(t)\psi\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}_{T}^{2}(L^{2}(D))}\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\|\mathbb{M}(T)^{\kappa}\psi-\mathbb{M}(T)\psi\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

where in the second to last equality we have used (
4.34
) and the convergence at the end is the consequence of the standard properties of the mollifier and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. Similarly

(4.38)

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u),h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}\right\|_{T}^{2}\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})-T_{k,\delta}(u),h(e_{j})\psi\rangle^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{D}(T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})-T_{k,\delta}(u))\psi\,h(e_{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|(T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})-T_{k,\delta}(u))\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\|h(e_{j})\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

where the last convergence is true due to Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and standard properties of mollifiers. Combining (
4.37
) and (
4.38
) we get

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa}),h^{\kappa}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k,\delta}(u),h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}\right\|_{T}^{2}\to 0,

which implies (
4.36
). Our second claim is

(4.39)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle D^{2}F(u^{\kappa})(h^{\kappa}\psi)(e_{j}),h^{\kappa}\psi(e_{j})\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\rightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle D^{2}F(u)(h\psi)(e_{j}),h\psi(e_{j})\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

To prove it note that, similarly as in (
4.37

and Young’s convolution inequality

(4.40)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle D^{2}F(u^{\kappa})(h^{\kappa}\psi)(e_{j}),h^{\kappa}\psi(e_{j})\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle D^{2}F(u^{\kappa})(h^{\kappa}\psi)(e_{j}),h(e_{j})\psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})\,(h(e_{j}))^{\kappa}\psi((h(e_{j}))^{\kappa}\psi-h(e_{j})\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\

On the other hand

(4.42)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle D^{2}F(u^{\kappa})(h\psi)(e_{j}),h(e_{j})\psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle D^{2}F(u)(h\psi)(e_{j}),h(e_{j})\psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\phantom{=}=\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{D}(T^{\prime}_{k,\delta}(u^{\kappa})-T^{\prime}_{k,\delta}(u))\,|h(e_{j})|^{2}\,\psi^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. Combining (

To converge with

notice that as long as

almost everywhere and

strongly in

\displaystyle\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{n}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\int_{D}G_{k}(u(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

which applied to (

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u_{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq-\int_{D}G_{k}(u(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u),h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k}(u)\,|h^{N}(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

With the use of (

(4.45)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u_{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\chi(s,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Now denote
\chi_{k}:=\chi\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|\leq k\}}
. Then, by the monotonicity assumption on
A

(A3)
we have for arbitrary

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}(A(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u_{n}))-A(t,x,\eta)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u_{n})-\eta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

By Proposition
3.4
and the weak* convergences of

,
\nabla_{x}u_{n}
and the almost everywhere convergence of
u_{n}
we can converge in every mixed term above, and by (
4.45
) we can treat the product term to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}(\chi_{k}-A(t,x,\eta)(\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u)-\eta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

Hence, by the monotonicity trick
C.3

\chi_{k}=A(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u))
almost everywhere for any

, and in consequence

.

4.6.
Convergence with the approximation on

.

In the following let

be a sequence of functions converging to

in
\mathcal{N}^{2}_{W}(0,T;L^{2}(D))

a solution to (

) for

Due to (

) we can converge in the above with

to get

(4.46)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(u^{N_{1}}-u^{N_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N_{1}}-h^{N_{2}}\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Thus

is a Cauchy sequence in

, and there exists

\displaystyle u^{N}\rightarrow u\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))).

Moreover, by applying Lemma

\int_{D}G_{k}(u^{N}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u^{N})\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u^{N}),h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k}(u^{N})\,|h^{N}(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

Thus

(4.48)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N}))\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u^{N})\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u^{N}),h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k}(u^{N})\,|h^{N}(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}u_{0}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u^{N}),h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}|h^{N}(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Similarly as under (
4.4
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u^{N}),\,h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\left(\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N}(s)^{*}T_{k}(u^{N}(s))\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\left(\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}\|T_{k}(u^{N}(s))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\|h^{N}(s)\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|T_{k}(u^{N}(t))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\right)+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N}\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{N}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\right)+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N}\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

which together with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\langle T_{k}(u^{N}),\,h^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}\right|\right)\lesssim\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N}\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Plugging the above into (

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N}))\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u_{0}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{N}\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Hence, by the coercivity condition
(A2)
the sequences
\{M(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\{M^{*}(t,x,A(t,x,\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u^{N})))\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
are bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
. Since the bound is independent of
k
, by the continuity with respect to the third variable of both
M
and
A
, we can use Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem to conclude that
\{\nabla_{x}u^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{A(t,x,\nabla_{x}u^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
are bounded in
L_{M}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
and
L_{M^{*}}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
respectively. At this point we may simply repeat the reasoning given in the previous subsection to conclude that

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}u^{N}

\displaystyle\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\nabla_{x}u

\displaystyle\text{ weakly* in }L_{M}(\Omega\times Q_{T}),

\displaystyle A(t,x,\nabla_{x}u^{N})

\displaystyle\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ weakly* in }L_{M^{*}}(\Omega\times Q_{T}),

and
\chi=A(t,x,\nabla_{x}u)
a.e. in
\Omega\times Q_{T}
. Converging in the weak formulation for
u^{N}
gives us our desired result. Moving forward, to show the stated energy equality (
3.7
) we turn to Lemma
4.20
, which gives us the equality

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\int_{D}G_{k}(u(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}G_{k}(u_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad=-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\alpha(s,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}T_{k}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{D}T^{\prime}_{k}(u)\,|h(e_{j})|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

for almost every
t\in(0,T)
. Here, every term can be easily treated by the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. Indeed,

\displaystyle G_{k}(u(t))\nearrow\frac{1}{2}(u(t))^{2},\quad T^{\prime}_{k}(u)\nearrow 1,\text{ almost everywhere as }k\to+\infty,

and we get our needed (
3.7
). Since the left-hand side is well-defined for all
t\in[0,T]
, we may consider a representative of
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}
such that (
3.7
) holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Note that the multiplicative case below is done via the fixed point argument, therefore the energy equality will translate to that case as well. At last, we show that
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega\times D))
. Fix
t\in[0,T]
. From (
3.7
) for any
t_{k}\to t

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|u(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}=-\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{t_{k}}\int_{D}A(s,x,\nabla_{x}u)\cdot\nabla_{x}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{t_{k}}\|h\|_{HS(L^{2}(D))}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\to 0,

which implies

\|u(t_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\rightarrow\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)},\quad t_{k}\to t.

Hence to conclude the continuity we shall show that
u
is weakly continuous as a function taking values in
L^{2}(\Omega\times D)
. As weak convergence combined with a convergence of norms implies strong convergence in
L^{2}
, this will grant us our thesis. Fix a function
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
. Then, test (
3.6
) by
\psi\varphi
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\psi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u_{0}\psi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}A(\tau,x,\nabla_{x}u)\cdot(\psi\nabla_{x}\varphi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathbb{E}\int_{D}\psi\varphi\,\int_{0}^{t}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Since, the right-hand side is continuous in time, it follows that

(4.49)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\psi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\psi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\quad t_{k}\to t.

We will now show that the finite linear combinations of the functions of the form
\psi\,\varphi
,
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
are dense in
L^{2}(\Omega\times D)
. Although the argument is quite standard, let us write it for the convenience of the reader. Fix
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega\times D)
. Then, there exists a sequence of sets
\{S_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega\times D
, and a sequence of numbers
\{a_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(4.50)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}\rightarrow\phi,\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times D),\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

Moreover, by the definition of the product measure on
\Omega\times D
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a sequence of families of sets
\{A_{k}^{j,i}\times B_{k}^{j,i}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N},i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega\times D
such that for any
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
we have
S_{k}\subset\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(A_{k}^{j,i}\times B_{k}^{j,i})
, and

(4.51)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i}}\rightarrow\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}},\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times D),\text{ as }i\to+\infty.

Now fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i}}\in L^{2}(D)
, we can find a function
\varphi_{k}^{j,i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}^{j,i}-\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i}}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\frac{1}{|a_{k}|\sqrt{|A_{k}^{j,i}|}}\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}\,2^{j}}.

Moreover, for any
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
l_{k,i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l_{k,i}}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}},

and by (
4.51
) for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
big enough so that

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}-\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\leq\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}}.

At last, by (
4.50
) we can find
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough for

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}-\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

to hold. Thus, we may deduce

\begin{split}&\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{k,i_{k}}}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}-\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{k,i_{k}}}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}-\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l_{k,i_{k}}}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\|a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}(\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}-\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i_{k}}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|a_{k}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}-\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}-\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\sqrt{|A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}|}\frac{1}{|a_{k}|\sqrt{|A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}|}}\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}\,2^{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}\frac{\varepsilon}{4\cdot 2^{k}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\\
&<\varepsilon\end{split}

For simplicity let

\phi_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{k,i_{k}}}a_{k}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}^{j,i_{k}}}\varphi_{k}^{j,i_{k}}.

Using (
4.49
) we see that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
it is the case that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\quad t_{k}\to t.

Hence, using Hölder’s inequality and the energy equality (
3.7
)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{t_{k}\to t}\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\leq\limsup_{t_{k}\to t}\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\qquad+\limsup_{t_{k}\to t}\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t_{k})\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\qquad+\limsup_{t_{k}\to t}\left|\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\phi_{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\mathbb{E}\int_{D}u(t)\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times D)}.\end{split}

Converging with
n\to+\infty
gives us our thesis.

5.
Multiplicative case

We move to extending our result to the multiplicative case, that is when
h
can depend on the solution
u
as given in the Introduction. To this end we shall employ the classical Banach fixed point theorem. Define the operator

\Psi:L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\times D),\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}e^{-\alpha t})\rightarrow L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega\times D),\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}e^{-\alpha t}),

where
e^{-\alpha t}
is a weight in time for some soon-to-be-fixed
\alpha>0
, and
\Psi(S)=u_{S}
maps a given
S
to a solution
u_{s}
of (
1
) with
h(\cdot,S)
. Note that as the time interval is fixed, by our previous considerations
\Psi
is well-defined. Similarly to (
4.46
) we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|(u^{N}_{S_{1}}-u^{N}_{S_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|h^{N}(S_{1})-h^{N}(S_{2})\|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, after converging with
N\to+\infty
, due to (
4.47
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|(u_{S_{1}}-u_{S_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|h(S_{1})-h(S_{2})\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Due to
(H2)
in Assumption
3.5
we can estimate the right-hand side

\int_{0}^{t}\|h(S_{1})-h(S_{2})\|^{2}_{HS(L^{2}(D))}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\lesssim\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Q_{t})}.

Hence,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|(u_{S_{1}}-u_{S_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})\leq C\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Q_{t})}^{2}.

By multiplying (
5.1
) by
e^{-\alpha t}
, and integrating we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\|(u_{S_{1}}-u_{S_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})e^{-\alpha t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Integrating by parts

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\frac{C}{\alpha}(1-e^{-\alpha T})\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}e^{-\alpha t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\|(u_{S_{1}}-u_{S_{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)})e^{-\alpha t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}(1-e^{-\alpha T})\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|S_{1}-S_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}e^{-\alpha t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Taking
\alpha>0
big enough, so that
C<\alpha
, we may use Banach fixed point theorem to conclude.

Appendix A
Itô’s formula in Orlicz spaces

In the following section we show how to obtain Itô’s formula in the setting of Orlicz spaces. We will closely follow the book
[
18
]
and the proof given there. Let us explain some of the minor differences that appear and how we combat them. First, as mentioned in Section
2
, there is a problem of duality when converging in the product with the approximations. It is quite hard to obtain a pair of sequences, one of which converges strongly, and the other one weakly, to conclude to convergence of their product. Instead, in the Orlicz setting one focuses on the modular convergence, which by Proposition
2.11
has the needed property. This can be seen in Lemma
A.1
, where we show modular convergence of the floor functions, instead of their strong convergence as in
[
18
]
. Second, there is no Gelfand triple structure

V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^{*},

in our case, and no time-space factorization of our function spaces (that is we cannot write
L_{m}(Q_{T})
as a Bochner space in time, taking values in
V
). Here, there is no special remedy, but instead we take advantage of the fact, that we work with particular spaces, instead of general Banach space
V
, and hence we do not really need the Gelfand triple structure to understand the product
\DIV_{x}\alpha\,u
in the equation (
A.2
).

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
u\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Q_{T})\cap L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T);W^{1,1}_{0}(D))\cap L_{m}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
is such that
\nabla_{x}u\in L_{m}(\Omega\times Q_{T})
. Then, there exists a sequence of partitions
I_{l}:=\{0=t_{0}^{l}<t_{1}^{l}<...<t_{k_{l}}^{l}=T\}
such that
I_{l}\subset I_{l+1}
and
\delta(I_{l}):=\max_{i}(t_{i}^{l}-t^{l}_{i-1})\to 0
as
l\to+\infty
, and for

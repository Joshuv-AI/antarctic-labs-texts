Primal-Dual algorithms for Abstract convex functions with respect to quadratic functions
Ewa Bednarczuk, The Hung Tran

Abstract
We consider the saddle point problem where the objective functions are abstract convex with respect to the class of quadratic functions. We propose primal-dual algorithms using the corresponding abstract proximal operator and investigate the convergence under certain restrictions. We test our algorithms by several numerical examples.



1
Introduction

We consider an instance of the following saddle point problem

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}f(x)+\psi(Lx)-g^{*}_{\Psi}(\psi),

(SP)

where
X
and
Y
are Hilbert spaces,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty],g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
are proper
\Phi
-convex and
\Psi
-convex respectively with respect to the class of functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
, while
L:X\to Y
is a continuous linear operator.
For the problem to be well-defined, we assume that
L(\mathrm{dom}\,f)\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g\neq\emptyset
, where
\mathrm{dom}\,f=\{x\in X:f(x)<+\infty\}
is the domain of
f
.
The
\Phi
-convexity is a generalization of convexity which is called abstract convex. The concept of abstract convex functions had been formally introduced by Rubinov
[
25
]
, Pallaschke and Rolewicz
[
21
]
, Singer
[
28
]
i.e. given a collection of function
\Psi:=\{\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}\}
, a function
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is called
\Psi
-convex if it can be written as

(\forall x\in X)\quad f(x)=\sup_{\psi\in\Psi,\psi\leq f}\psi(x).

This paper aims to investigate the convergence of primal-dual algorithm for problem (
SP
) when
g
is convex and
g
is
\Phi
-convex with
L
is an identity operator.

It has been known that a proper lsc convex function is abstract convex with respect to the class of affine functions
[
3
, Corollary 13.42]
.
For a more general functions of
\Phi
, the class of
\Phi
-convex encompass both convex and nonconvex functions e.g. star-convex
[
24
]
, weakly/strongly convex
[
7
]
.
Recent attention has been focused on algorithms development for nonconvex problems and the topic of abstract convexity has gained popularity
[
17
,
23
,
18
,
20
]
.
Because with abstract convexity, one can define additional structures similar to the classical convex function such as conjugation and subdifferentials. Therefore, abstract convex functions can be analyzed globally. This aspect allows us to design algorithms which converges to the global minima in the nonconvex setting, while standard analysis only gives convergence to a critical point. On the other hand, to obtain convergence in nonconvex setting, additional conditions are needed, such as KŁ (or PŁ) inequality
[
8
]
, error bound condition
[
11
]
, subdifferentials error bound
[
1
]
etc. In practice, these conditions can be difficult to verify as the knowledge of critical points are needed.

A typical case of problem (
SP
) is the saddle point problem

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y),

(1)

where
g:Y\to(\infty,+\infty]
is proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
is a bounded linear operator. Saddle point problem, or min-max optimization has been gaining popularity in applications, especially in machine learning, including generative adversarial networks (GANs)
[
12
]
, deep learning
[
29
]
, distributed computing
[
19
]
and much more.

Our contribution in this paper is the discussion of primal-dual algorithm for solving problem (
1
) when
f
is
\Phi
-convex. This is an extension of our paper
[
5
]
using the
\Phi
-proximal operator for solving nonconvex problem. We obtain convergence of proximal point algorithm, forward-backward algorithm for
\Phi
-convex function without additional regularity condition. Hence, we attempt to investigate the convergence of primal-dual algorithm for the saddle point problem in the presence of
\Phi
-convexity.

The structure of the paper is as follow: In Section
2
, we present the definition of abstract convex function as well as its properties when
\Phi=\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is the class of quadratic function. We introduce the defintion of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator and its characteristics. In Section
3
, we propose
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
primal-dual algorithm for solving problem (
1
) and discuss its convergence in Theorem
3.3
. In Section
4
, we examine our primal-dual algorithm for problem (
SP
) in the full
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-setting. Finally, we present some numerical examples demonstrating our discussed algorithms in Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
Convex Functions

In this paper, we consider
X
to be Hilbert with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
and the norm
\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
.
For two functions
f,g:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
,
f\leq g
means that
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in X
.
The domain of
f
is the set
\text{dom }f=\{x\in X:f(x)<+\infty\}
, and
f
is proper if
\mathrm{dom}\,f\neq\emptyset
and
f(x)>-\infty
for every
x\in X
. We say that
f
is lower semi-continuous (lsc) at
x\in X
if
f(x)\leq\liminf_{x_{n}\to x}f(x_{n})
.
We denote
\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}_{-}
as the set of nonnegative and non-positive number, respectively.
We use the following convention:
+\infty-\infty=+\infty
.
Let us define the class of quadratic function of the form

\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\{\phi=(a,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X:\phi(x)=-a\|x\|^{2}+\langle u,x\rangle\}.

(2)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. We say that
f
is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex on
X
if the following holds,

(\forall x\in X)\quad f(x)=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}},\phi\leq f}\phi(x).

The definition of
\Phi_{\Phi_{lsc}}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convexity belongs a general concept called abstract convexity which has been investigated in
[
25
,
21
,
28
]
to develop convexity without linearity.
The reason we choose the class
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is that it covers a large class of functions with nice properties and it is closely related to strongly and weakly convex functions
[
5
]
. It has been proved in
[
25
, Proposition 6.3]
that all lower-semicontinous functions is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex. When
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is replaced by a class of affine function,
f
is proper lsc convex iff it is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex
[
3
, Theorem 9.20]
.

Together with
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convexity, we present the definitions of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate and
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials.

Definition 2.2

(
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate)
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper, the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate of
f
is defined as
f^{*}_{\Phi}:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)=\sup_{x\in X}\phi(x)-f(x),

(3)

and the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-biconjugate of
f
defined on
X
is

\displaystyle f^{**}_{\Phi}(x)=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}}\phi(x)-f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi).

(4)

When
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is limited to the class of linear functions (
a=0
) and
X
is a Banach space, we obtain Fenchel conjugation with the duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{*}\times X}
. In the case of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we can equip
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
with the norm and inner product as
\phi(x)=\langle(a,u),(-\|x\|^{2},x)\rangle_{\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\times(\mathbb{R}_{-}\times X)}
.
Another formulation of abstract convexity is considering
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
as an element and
\phi(x)=c(x,\phi)
with
c:X\times\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
as a coupling function e.g.
[
17
,
18
,
20
]
.
We can also consider
\phi(x)
as
x(\phi)
, so for the functions defined on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we call them
X
-convex to distinguish with
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex functions defined on
X

[
21
, Proposition 1.2.3]
.

Hence,
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
plays the role of the "
dual
" of
X
, similar to Fenchel conjugate.
From the definition,
f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)
is convex on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
and so is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex, while
f^{**}_{\Phi}
is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex on
X
.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the function
h:X\to\mathbb{R},h(x)=c\|x\|^{2}
, with
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate of
h
at
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is

h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)=h^{*}_{\Phi}(a,u)=\begin{cases}0&a=-c,u=0\\
\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{4\left(a+c\right)}&a>-c\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
a=0,c=1/2
, we recover the convex conjugate of
h(x)
(see
[
3
, Example 13.6]
).

Remark 2.4
.

•

If
f
is proper
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex and
\sup_{\phi=(a,u)\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}},\phi\leq f}a<+\infty
then
\mathrm{dom}\,f
is a convex set. Indeed, assume by contradiction, there exists
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
such that
f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)=+\infty
. By the form of function
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle+\infty=f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\leq f}\phi(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sup_{\phi\leq f}\phi(x)+(1-\lambda)\sup_{\phi\leq f}\phi(x)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\sup_{\phi\leq f}a\|x-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\sup_{\phi\leq f}a\|x-y\|^{2}.

This implies
\sup_{\phi\leq f}a=+\infty
which contradicts
\sup_{\phi\leq f}a<+\infty
.

•

The convexity of
f^{*}_{\Phi}
is a consequence of the fact that
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex set.

Next, we define
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials, see e.g.
[

,
25
]
.

Definition 2.5

(
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials)
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper, the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient of
f
at
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}f
is an element
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\forall y\in X\right)\quad f\left(y\right)-f\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\phi\left(y\right)-\phi\left(x_{0}\right).

(5)

We denote the collection of all
\phi
satisfying (
5
) as
\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{0})
.
If
a=0
in
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\partial f
as the subdifferentials in the sense of convex analysis. Similarly, we define the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient of
f^{*}_{\Phi}:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
at
\phi_{0}
is an element
x\in X
which satisfies

(\forall\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}})\quad f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})\geq\phi(x)-\phi_{0}(x).

(6)

The collection of
X
-subgradient of
f^{*}_{\Phi}
at
\phi_{0}
is called

-subdifferentials and is denoted by
\partial_{X}f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})
.

The properties of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient also analogous to convex subgradient.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper,
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

i.

If
\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\neq\emptyset
then
f(x)=f^{**}_{\Phi}(x)
. As a consequence,

is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}

\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\neq\emptyset
for all

is convex.

ii.

f(x)+f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)=\phi(x)
for all
\phi\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)
.

iii.

For

,
0\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)
if and only if

.

iv.

If

is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex, then
\phi\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\Leftrightarrow x\in\partial_{X}f^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)
.

The proof can be found in
[

,

,

]
for general class

. If

, then Proposition

2.2
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-Proximal Operator

In this subsection, let
X
be a Hilbert space, we introduce the proximal operator related to the class
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex functions.
Observe that
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient is defined on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
which is not a subset of
X
.
Therefore, we need a mapping which can serve as a link between
X
and
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
. In analogy to the classical constructions we consider the function
g_{\gamma}
from Example
2.7
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
g_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|x\|^{2}
, we define
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-duality map

J_{\gamma}:X\rightrightarrows\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
as

J_{\gamma}(x):=\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}g_{\gamma}(x)=\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|x\|^{2}\right).

Its inverse
J^{-1}_{\gamma}:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\rightrightarrows X
is

J^{-1}_{\gamma}(\phi)=(\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}g_{\gamma})^{-1}(\phi).

When
\gamma=1
,
\partial g_{\gamma}(x)
is the classical duality mapping known in
[
22
, Example 2.26]
.
Next, we define
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
and
\gamma>0
, the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator
\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}:X\rightrightarrows X
is a set-valued mapping of the form,

\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}(x):=\left(J_{\gamma}+\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f\right)^{-1}J_{\gamma}(x),

(8)

where
J_{\gamma}
is defined in Definition
2.8
.

The idea of
\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}
is related to the resolvent operator which is defined for classical convex subdifferentials as
(Id+\partial f)^{-1}
. When
f
is convex, this is also known as proximity operator

\mathrm{prox}_{\gamma f}\left(x_{0}\right)=\arg\min_{z\in X}f\left(z\right)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|z-x_{0}\right\|^{2}.

(9)

We state some properties of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
5
, Theorem 3, Theorem 4]

Let
X
be a Hilbert space. Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex function and
\gamma>0
.
The following holds:

i.

If
x_{0}
is a global minimizer of
f
then
x_{0}
is a fixed point of
\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}
.

ii.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
,
x\in\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}(x_{0})
if and only if there exists
a_{0}\geq-1/2\gamma
such that

x\in\arg\min_{z\in X}\left[f(z)+\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}+a_{0}\right)\|z-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]

(10)

Remark 2.11
.

Proposition
2.10
-(ii) gives us a way to calculate
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator. However, the general formulation of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator requires us to solve the problem: given
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
,
x\in\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}(x_{0})
is equivalent to solving the following relation

\text{Find $x\in X$ such that }\ J_{\gamma}(x_{0})-J_{\gamma}(x)\cap\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\neq\emptyset.

(11)

The value of
\phi\in J_{\gamma}(x)
will play the crucial role in the convergence of the primal-dual algorithms.

When
f
is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex, we can explicitly calculate
\mathrm{prox}^{\mathrm{lsc}}_{\gamma f}(x_{0})
for
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
under additional assumption.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex Assume that
\mathrm{dom}\,f=\mathrm{dom}\,\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f
.
For any
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
, if there exists
x\in X
such that (
10
) holds then (
11
) holds.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
a_{0}\geq-\frac{1}{2\gamma}
, and assume the (
10
) holds, then for any
z\in X

f(z)-f(x)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}+a_{0}\right)\left(\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}-\|z-x_{0}\|^{2}\right).

Let
z=\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
for any
\lambda\in(0,1],z_{1}\in X
, the right side of the above inequality becomes

\displaystyle\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}-\|\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)x-x_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}+2\lambda\langle x_{0}-x,z_{1}-x\rangle.

(12)

On the other hand,
since
\mathrm{dom}\,f=\mathrm{dom}\,\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f
, there exists
\phi_{z}\leq f
such that
f(z)=\phi_{z}(z)
, we notice

-a\|z\|^{2}+\langle u_{z},z\rangle\leq-a_{z}\|z\|^{2}+\langle u_{z},z\rangle=f(z),

for any
a\geq a_{z}
. Let us denote the set of the smallest value
a_{z}
as
A(z)=\{\inf a_{z}:\phi_{z}(z)=f(z)\}
which is finite. Then we can take

a_{\phi}=\sup_{z\in\mathrm{dom}\,f}A(z)<+\infty.

We express the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convexity of
f
at
z
,

\displaystyle f(\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{z}(\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)x)

\displaystyle=\lambda\phi_{z}(z_{1})+(1-\lambda)\phi_{z}(x)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}a_{\phi_{z}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda f(z_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(x)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}a_{\phi}.

(13)

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), we obtain

\lambda(f(z_{1})-f(x))+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}a_{\phi}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}+a_{0}\right)(\lambda^{2}\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}+2\lambda\langle x_{0}-x,z_{1}-x\rangle).

Dividing both sides by
\lambda
and let
\lambda\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle f(z_{1})-f(x)+\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}a_{\phi}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}+2a_{0}\right)\langle x_{0}-x,z_{1}-x\rangle.

(14)

Taking
a_{0}-a\geq a_{\phi}
which is well-defined by the assumption, then (
14
) becomes

\displaystyle f(z_{1})-f(x)

\displaystyle\geq-(a_{0}-a)\|z_{1}-x\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}+2a_{0}\right)\langle x_{0}-x,z_{1}-x\rangle

\displaystyle=-(a_{0}-a)(\|z_{1}\|^{2}-\|x\|^{2})+\langle\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}+2a_{0}\right)x_{0}-\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}+2a\right)x,z_{1}-x\rangle.

(15)

This implies
\phi_{0}-\phi\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x)
where
\phi_{0}=(a_{0},(1/\gamma+2a_{0})x_{0})\in J_{\gamma}(x_{0})
so we have (
11
).
∎

Therefore, in the analysis below, we use (
11
) while writing (
10
) as a practical way to compute
\Phi_{lsc}
-proximal operator.

3
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-Primal-Dual Algorithms

To design an effective algorithm for solving (
SP
), we first investigate primal-dual algorithm for the case
g
convex before tackling a more general case of
g
.
In this section, we apply our newly defined
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator (
8
) to solve the saddle point problem of the form

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}\mathcal{L}(x,y):=\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y),

(16)

where
g^{*}(y)
is the convex conjugate of
g
.
When
g
is lsc convex, problem (
16
) is equivalent to the composite problem
[
6
]
,

\min_{x\in X}f(x)+g(Lx).

(CP)

A pair
(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y
is a saddle point of problem (
16
) if and only if the following are satisfied

(\forall(x,y)\in X\times Y)\quad\mathcal{L}({x},\hat{y})\geq\mathcal{L}(\hat{x},\hat{y})\geq\mathcal{L}(\hat{x},{y}).

(17)

We denote by
S
the set of all the saddle points.
Equivalently, (
17
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\forall x\in X)\quad f(x)-f(\hat{x})

\displaystyle\geq-\langle L^{*}\hat{y},x-\hat{x}\rangle_{X\times X},

\displaystyle(\forall y\in Y)\quad g^{*}(y)-g^{*}(\hat{y})

\displaystyle\geq\langle L\hat{x},y-\hat{y}\rangle_{Y\times Y}.

Since
g^{*}
is convex and
f
is
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-convex, we can rewrite the above inequalities in the form of subdifferentials

L\hat{x}\in\partial g^{*}(\hat{y}),\qquad(0,-L^{*}\hat{y})\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(\hat{x}),

(18)

which is known as KKT condition for the Lagrangian primal-dual problems (
16
).

Remark 3.1
.

When we replace the convexity of
g
by
\Psi
-convexity with a more general collection of function

5.2

Example 5.3

(
Binary tomography
)
.

Binary Tomography (BT) is a special case of Discrete Tomography (DT) which aims at reconstructing binary images starting from a limited number of their projections
[
26
,
16
]
.
The image
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is represented as a grid of
n=n_{1}\times n_{2}
pixels taking values
x_{j}\in\{-1,1\}
for
j=1,\dots,n
. The projections
y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
are linear combinations of the pixels along
m
directions. The linear transformation from image to projections is modelled as

y=\mathbf{A}\overline{x}+\omega,

(60)

where
x_{j}
denotes the value of the image in the
j
-th cell,
y_{i}
is the weighted sum of the image along the
i
-th ray, the element
\mathbf{A}_{i,j}
of matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is proportional to the length of the
i
-th ray in the
j
-th cell and finally
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an additive noise with Gaussian distribution and standard deviation
\sigma>0
. In general, the projection matrix
\mathbf{A}
has a low rank, meaning that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A})<\min\{n,m\}

[
16
]
. We model the problem as

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{i}\in\{-1,1\}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{minimize}}\;\;\frac{\|\mathbf{A}x-y\|_{2}^{2}}{2}.

(61)

Instead of considering the binary variable
x
, we can turn problem (
61
) into a continuous one by adding the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

F({x})={\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}^{2}-1|},

(62)

which is separable as the sum of functions
f_{i}(x)=|x_{i}^{2}-1|
. Observe that
F
is a weakly convex function with the set of minimisers

{S_{F}=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,x_{i}\in\{-1,1\}\;\text{for}\;i=1,\dots,n\}=\{-1,1\}^{n}},

and can be seen as a non-smooth counterpart to the function

{x\mapsto{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}(x_{i}-1)^{2}}}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
proposed in
[
32
]
to promote binary integer solutions (with values in
\{0,1\}^{n}
) in Markov Random Field approaches. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work where function
F
is used in such a context.

Problem (
61
) transforms into

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{minimize}}\;\;\frac{\|\mathbf{A}x-y\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+F({x}).

(63)

We can write the objective function of (
63
) in the form of composite functions
F(x)+G(Ax)
and implement
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-Chambolle-Pock Algorithm
1
to solve this problem.

The function
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is
G(z)=\frac{\|z-y\|^{2}}{2}
which has the conjugate

G^{*}(v)=\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{2}+\langle v,y\rangle,

and we can write the dual update similar to the previous examples, which is

v_{n+1}=\frac{v_{n}+\tau(A\overline{x}_{n}-y)}{\tau+1}.

For the primal step, it can be calculated as a proximal step as we mention in Algorithm
1
. Specifically, since the function
F
is weakly convex, we can obtain an explicit form of convex proximal operator for each component of
F
i.e. the function
\mathcal{F}(x)=|x^{2}-1|
(see
[
4
, Remark 8]
). For
\alpha>0,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\quad(\forall y\in\mathbb{R})\quad\mathrm{prox}_{\alpha\mathcal{F}}(y)=\begin{cases}\frac{y}{1+2\alpha}&\text{ if }|y|>1+2\alpha,\\
\frac{y}{1-2\alpha}&\text{ if }|y|<1-2\alpha,\\
\frac{y}{|y|}&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(64)

Remark 5.4

The model in (
61

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and possible additional convex constraints) could be applied to other Binary Quadratic Programs (a special class of QCQP problems having the equality constraint
x_{i}^{2}=1
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
) arising in Computer Vision, such as Graph Bisection, Graph Matching and Image Co-Segmentation (see
[
31

and the references therein).

Setting:

\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-Chambolle-Pock Algorithm, we fix the number of iteration to

In this part, we consider how to solve the following problem

\min_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}}h(\phi),

(65)

where
h:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper lsc function which is bounded from below.
We want to design a proximal algorithm to solve problem (
65
) using
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials (
6
). This leads us to Algorithm
4
.

Algorithm 4

\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-Proximal Algorithm on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}

Initialize:
Choose
\gamma>0,\phi_{0}=(a_{0},u_{0})\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}

Update:

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

•

Find
\phi_{n+1}=(a_{n+1},u_{n+1})
such that

\frac{u_{n}-u_{n+1}}{\gamma}\in\partial_{\Phi}^{\mathbb{R}}h(\phi_{n+1}).

(66)

•

If
there is no such
\phi_{n+1}
,
return

\phi_{n}
.

Algorithm
4
follows the approach of
[
5
]
. Since
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient is defined on
X
, the simplest choice would be to use only the second component of
\phi

.
The first component

Let us state the first estimation comes from Algorithm

Proposition A.1
.

Let
h:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc function on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
which is bounded from below. Let
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by Algorith
4
with stepsize
\gamma>0
. Then for any
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we have,

h\left(\phi\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(1-\frac{2\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{2\gamma}.

(67)

If
\gamma\geq a_{n}-a_{n+1}
for all

, then

is non-increasing.

Proof.

Expressing (
66

\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient, for any
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we obtain (
67
),

\displaystyle h\left(\phi\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma^{2}}\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\left\langle u-u_{n+1},u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(1-\frac{2\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{2\gamma}.

Let
\phi=\phi_{n}
in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle h\left(\phi_{n}\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\left(a_{n}-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma},

which the RHS is non-negative when
\gamma\geq a_{n}-a_{n+1}
. Hence we finish the proof.
∎

To obtain convergence of Algorithm

,
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has to be chosen in a specific way as follow.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
h:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc function on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
which is bounded from below. Let
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
4
with stepsize
\gamma>0
. Let
\phi^{*}=\left(a^{*},u^{*}\right)
be the minimizer of problem (
65
). If
\left(a_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is chosen such that

{\gamma+2a^{*}}\geq 2a_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. If
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is finite dimensional and
1-(a_{n}-a_{n+1})\gamma\geq 0
diverges from zero then there exists a convergence subsequence
\left(u_{n_{k}}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to a minimizer.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\phi^{*}
in (
67
) be the minimizer of problem (
65
), we have

\displaystyle 0\geq h\left(\phi^{*}\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u^{*}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u^{*}-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(1-\frac{2\left(a^{*}-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{2\gamma}.

If
\gamma+2a^{*}\geq 2a_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
then we have
\left\|u^{*}-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\geq\left\|u^{*}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}.

This infers that
\left(u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. From the assumption of
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we conclude that
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.
For the second assertion, we assume that
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is finite dimensional. Since
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, there exists a convergence subsequence
\left(\phi_{n_{k}+1}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to some
\bar{\phi}=(\bar{a},\bar{u})\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

We claim that if
u_{n}-u_{n+1}\to 0

then
\bar{u}
belongs to the minimizer. Indeed, if it true, we have that
u_{n_{k}}\to\bar{u}

and from (
67
), we have

\displaystyle h(\phi)-h(\bar{\phi})\geq h(\phi)-\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(\phi_{n_{k}}+1)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n_{k}+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n_{k}}\right\|^{2}\right]+\left(1-\frac{2\left(a-a_{n_{k}+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n_{k}}-u_{n_{k}+1}\right\|^{2}}{2\gamma}=0,

(68)

for all
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence
\bar{\phi}
is a minimizer of
h
.
Now we just need to prove that
u_{n}-u_{n+1}\to 0
. From (
67
) with
\phi=\phi_{n}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

h\left(\phi_{n}\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\left(a_{n}-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}.

As
h
is bounded from below, we have that

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left(1-\frac{\left(a_{n}-a_{n+1}\right)}{\gamma}\right)\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}<+\infty,

which implies either
u_{n}-u_{n+1}\to 0
or
a_{n}-a_{n+1}\to\gamma
. We obtain the former thanks to the assumption on
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark A.3
.

From the above theorem, we only require that
a_{n}-a_{n+1}\leq\gamma
and do not converge to
\gamma
, so that
u_{n}-u_{n+1}\to 0
. This is crucial to obtain convergence to the minimizer. On the other hand, we need information on the solution in order to choose
\gamma
or
a_{n}
such that
\gamma+2a^{*}\geq 2a_{n}
. This is one of the weakness of Algorithm
4
.

To avoid using information on the minimizer, we impose a restriction on the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subgradient of the update (

5
.

\phi_{n+1}=(a_{n+1},u_{n+1})
such that

\frac{u_{n}-u_{n+1}}{\gamma}\in\partial_{\Phi}^{\mathbb{R}}h(\phi_{n+1})\textbf{ and }\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\leq a_{n+1}-a_{n}.

(69)

•

If
there is no such
\phi_{n+1}
,
return

\phi_{n}
.

Update (
69
) implies that
a_{n+1}\geq a_{n}
, so one needs to take a non-decreasing sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We state the convergence results of Algorithm
5
in the following theorem.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
h:\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc function on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
which is bounded from below. Let
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
4
with stepsize
\gamma>0
.
For any
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle h\left(\phi\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma^{2}}\left[\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)^{2}-\left(a-a_{n}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\left[\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}+1\right].

(70)

In addition,

h(\phi_{n})
is non-increasing and
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. When
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
is finite dimensional, every convergent subsequence of
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a minimizer of
h
.

Proof.

Let us start from update (
69
), for any
\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
,

h\left(\phi\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)\geq-\frac{a-a_{n+1}}{\gamma^{2}}\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\left\langle u_{n}-u_{n+1},u-u_{n+1}\right\rangle.

(71)

The restriction of
\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\|
, gives us

\displaystyle h\left(\phi\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma^{2}}\left[\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{4}+\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)^{2}-\left(a-a_{n+1}+\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma^{2}}\left[\left(a-a_{n+1}\right)^{2}-\left(a-a_{n}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left[\left\|u-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\left[\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}+1\right],

which is (
70
). Let
\phi=\phi_{n}
in (
70
), we obtain

\displaystyle h\left(\phi_{n}\right)-h\left(\phi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(a_{n}-a_{n+1})^{2}}{2\gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\left[\frac{\left\|u_{n}-u_{n+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}+2\right]\geq 0,.

This implies that
h(\phi_{n})
is non-increasing for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, since
h
is bounded from below, the above inequality means that

\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(a_{i}-a_{i+1})^{2}}{2\gamma^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|u_{i}-u_{i+1}\right\|^{2}\left[\frac{\left\|u_{i}-u_{i+1}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma}+2\right]<+\infty,

so
u_{n}-u_{n+1}\to 0
and
a_{n}-a_{n+1}\to 0
. The boundedness and subsequence convergence of
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to the minimizer follows in the same manner as in Theorem
A.2
.
∎

Appendix B
Examples setup

Function
f
:

For examples
5.1
and
5.2
, we require the
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials of
f(x)=x^{4}
. Hence, for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we need to find
\phi_{0}(x)=-a_{0}x^{2}+u_{0}x
such that
x_{0}\in\mathrm{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\ f(x)-\phi_{0}(x)
. Since
f-\phi_{0}
is smooth and it is convex if
a\geq 0
, we can take advantage of the gradient and its second derivative to obtain global minimizer.
In fact, we found out that we can take a negative value
a\geq-2x_{0}
so that
x_{0}
is still the global minimizer. The
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials of
f
has the form,

\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{0}):=\{\phi=(a,u)\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}\ :a\geq-2x_{0}^{2},b=4x_{0}^{3}+2ax_{0}\}.

(72)

To implement
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-proximal operator on
f
with parameter
\sigma>0
, we need to specify the sequence
(\bar{a}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\bar{a}_{n}\geq-1/2\sigma
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the proximal update for Algorithm
1
on the function
f+\langle y_{n+1},\cdot\rangle
reads as

\displaystyle\text{Solve for }x_{n+1}:\

\displaystyle 4x^{3}+\left(2\bar{a}_{n}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)x=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}+2\bar{a}_{n}\right)x_{n}-y_{n+1}

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\bar{a}_{n}+2x_{n+1}^{2}-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=10^{-3}
is a damping term added to
\bar{a}_{n+1}
.
While for Algorithm
2
and
3
, it is
\phi_{n+1}=(a_{n+1},u_{n+1})
instead of
y_{n+1}
,

\displaystyle\text{Solve for }x_{n+1}:\

\displaystyle 4x^{3}+\left(2\bar{a}_{n}-2a_{n+1}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)x=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}+2\bar{a}_{n}\right)x_{n}-y_{n+1}

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\bar{a}_{n}+2x_{n+1}^{2}-a_{n+1}-\varepsilon,

(73)

The cubic equation always has a real solution. In case of multiple solutions, we choose the one that is the closest to the previous iteration
x_{n}
. To find solution to the cubic equation, we employ the package NUMPY in PYTHON.

Function
g
:

For function
g(x)=x^{2}
, this is a well-known convex function, with its convex conjugate
g^{*}(y)=y^{2}/4
. For completeness, the proximal update
y_{n+1}\in\text{prox }_{\tau g^{*}}(y_{n}+\tau\bar{x}_{n})
is

y_{n+1}\in\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g^{*}(y)-\bar{x}_{n}y+\frac{1}{2\tau}(y-y_{n})^{2}\Leftrightarrow y_{n+1}=\frac{2(y_{n}+\tau\bar{x}_{n})}{\tau+2}.

This will be used in Algorithm
1
and standard Chambolle-Pock algorithm.

To compute
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate and
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials, we focus on the generalized function
h(x)=cx^{2}
, with
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-conjugate,
h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)-h(x)
, is

h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)=h^{*}_{\Phi}(a,u)=\begin{cases}0&a=-c,u=0\\
\frac{u^{2}}{4\left(a+c\right)}&a>-c\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By the definition,
h^{*}_{\Phi}
is a convex function, so we can calculate its convex subgradient at
\phi_{0}=(a_{0},u_{0})\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
which is

\displaystyle\partial h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=\{\varphi=(\bar{a},\bar{u})\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}:(\forall\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}})\ h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})\geq\langle\varphi,\phi-\phi_{0}\rangle_{\Phi}\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\bar{u}=\frac{u_{0}}{2\left(a_{0}+c\right)},\bar{a}=-\bar{u}^{2}&\text{If }a_{0}\geq-c\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(74)

Hence, in our example, we can take
c=1
and use the classical proximal operator in the dual update of Algorithm
2
,

\displaystyle u_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left(a_{n+1}+1\right)\left(u_{n}-\tau(2x_{n}-x_{n-1})\right)}{\tau+2(a_{n+1}+1)},

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}-a_{n+1}}{\tau}+2x_{n}^{2}-x_{n-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{\left(u_{n}-\tau(2x_{n}-x_{n-1})\right)}{\tau+2(a_{n+1}+1)}\right]^{2},

\displaystyle a_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq-1.

To obtain
a_{n+1}
, we have to solve the middle equation which can be written as

4a_{n+1}^{3}+4a_{n+1}^{2}\left(\tau+2-A_{1}\tau\right)+a_{n+1}\left(\left(\tau+2\right)^{2}-4\left(\tau+2\right)\tau A_{1}\right)-\left(\tau+2\right)^{2}\tau A_{1}-A_{2}\tau=0,

where
A_{1}=\frac{a_{n}}{\tau}+2x_{n}^{2}-x_{n-1}^{2},A_{2}=\left(\frac{u_{n}}{\tau}-2x_{n}+x_{n-1}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}
.
This equation always has a real solution and we will choose the one that is the closest to the previous iteration
a_{n}
. The condition
a_{n+1}\geq-1
needs to be satisfied for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore, we add
a_{n}<-1
to our stopping criterion beside
\bar{a}_{n}<-1/2\sigma
in Algorithm
2
.

In the same manner,
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials of
h^{*}_{\Phi}
at
\phi_{0}=(a_{0},u_{0})\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
has the form,

\displaystyle\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=\{w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}:(\forall\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}})\ h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-h^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi_{0})\geq\phi(w_{0})-\phi_{0}(w_{0})\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}&\text{If }\phi_{0}=\left(-c,0\right)\\
\frac{u_{0}}{2\left(a_{0}+c\right)}&\text{If }a_{0}>-c\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(75)

Since sum rule does not work for
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials on
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
, to implement Algorithm
3
, we take a closer look at the dual update. We want to find
(x^{*},\phi^{*})\in\mathbb{R}\times\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
such that
x^{*}\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}g^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi^{*})
or to minimize

\min_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}}g^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-\phi(x^{*}).

(76)

To apply Algorithm
3
, we use
x^{*}=\bar{x}_{n}=2x_{n}-x_{n-1}
. However, notice that
\phi(x)
is nonlinear, we have to split
\phi(\bar{x}_{n})
, so we have to solve for
\phi_{n+1}
the problem,

\min_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}}g^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-(2\phi(x_{n})-\phi(x_{n-1}))=\min_{\phi\in\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}}g^{*}_{\Phi}(\phi)-K(\phi),

where
K(\phi)=2\phi(x_{n})-\phi(x_{n-1})
.
The above problem can be written in term of
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials as

0\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}(g^{*}_{\Phi}-K)(\phi_{n+1})\Leftrightarrow 2x_{n}-x_{n-1}\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}(g^{*}_{\Phi}-k)(\phi_{n+1}),

with
k(\phi)=a_{\phi}\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}
. The
\Phi_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}
-subdifferentials of
(g^{*}_{\Phi}-k)(\phi_{0})
is

\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}(g^{*}_{\Phi}-k)(\phi_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:(u_{0}-2(a_{0}+c)x)^{2}=8(a_{0}+c)^{2}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})^{2}\}.

We would like to use Algorithm
4
in the dual update of Algorithm
3
, so we want to find the next iterate
\phi_{n+1}=(a_{n+1},u_{n+1})
such that

\frac{u_{n}-u_{n+1}}{\tau}+2x_{n}-x_{n-1}\in\partial_{lsc}^{\mathbb{R}}(g^{*}_{\Phi}-k)(\phi_{n+1}),

this gives us

u_{n+1}=\frac{2\left(a_{n+1}+c\right)\left(u_{n}-\tau\sqrt{2}\left(x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right)+\tau\left(2x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right)\right)}{2\left(a_{n+1}+c\right)+\tau},

and we slowly decrease
a_{n+1}=a_{n}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon=10^{-3}
, instead of restricting
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{R}}
as in Algorithm
5
. Again, we require
a_{n}\geq-1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and we will put this as a stopping criterion in Algorithm
3
.

Remark B.1
.

To use Algorithm
5
, we need to choose
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
increasing such that
(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{2}\leq a_{n+1}-a_{n}
. One needs to start with a large step-length
a_{n+1}-a_{n}
and then decrease it. However, a large value
a_{n+1}
can affect the primal update which can make it fail to find
x_{n+1}
or stopping the algorithm (see (
73
)).
Moreover, to find
a_{n+1}
, we need to solve the inequality
(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{2}\leq a_{n+1}-a_{n}
. Using the update
u_{n+1}
above, we have

\left[u_{n}-\frac{2\left(a_{n+1}+c\right)\left(u_{n}-\tau\sqrt{2}\left(x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right)+\tau\left(2x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right)\right)}{2\left(a_{n+1}+c\right)+\tau}\right]^{2}\leq a_{n+1}-a_{n}.

This is a cubic inequality in
a_{n+1}
which will take a lot of efforts to solve.
That is why we refrain from using Algorithm
5
in Algorithm
3
.
On the other hand, this specific example require
a_{n}\geq-1
, while
\phi^{*}=(a^{*},u^{*})=(-1,0)
is the dual solution. It does not make sense to choose an increasing sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

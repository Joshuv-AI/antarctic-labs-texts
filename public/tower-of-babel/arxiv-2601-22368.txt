Dynamical stability of various convex graphical translators
Junyoung Park

Abstract
In the first part of the paper, we prove the existence of longtime solution to mean curvature flow starting from a graph of a continuous function defined over a slab. Then, we establish dynamical stability results for various types of graphical translators to mean curvature flow, namely the grim reaper, two dimensional graphical translators, and asymptotically cylindrical translators.



keywords
Mean curvature flow, graphcial translators, dynamical stability

1
Introduction

A smooth one parameter family of hypersurfaces
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in I}
evolves by mean curvature flow if the normal velocity is equal to the mean curvature vector, i.e

\langle\frac{\partial X}{\partial t},\nu\rangle=\langle\vec{H},\nu\rangle,

(1)

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector, and
\nu
is the unit normal vector. Since the seminal work of Brakke
Brakke 1978
, mean curvature flow has been extensively studied during the past several decades. One way to understand how mean curvature flow deforms hypersurfaces is to look at some special type of hypersurfaces which reduces the analysis. For example, one can consider graphical hypersurfaces over a given domain
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Ecker and Huisken
Ecker & Huisken 1989
,
Ecker & Huisken 1991
showed by maximum principle arguments that when the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is an entire graph of a locally Lipchitz function, then there exists a longtime smooth solution to mean curvature flow starting from
M_{0}
which its time slices are also entire graphs. Furthermore, they showed that if
M_{0}
is ‘straight at infinity’, then after suitable rescaling, the hypersurfaces converge to a self expander, a special type of hypersurfaces which move by expansion under mean curvature flow. Later, Clutterbuck
Clutterbuck 2005
(see also
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
) relaxed local Lipchitz continuity to just continuity. Uniqueness of entire graph solutions starting from locally Lipchitz, proper graph was proved by Daskalopolous and Sáez
Daskalopoulos & Sáez 2021
.

In the case when
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a general domain, Sáez and Schnürer
Sáez & Schnürer 2014
showed the existence of solutions to mean curvature flow when the initial data is a graph of a locally Lipchitz, proper function over
\Omega
. They further showed that such solutions remain graphical over evolving domain.

Our first result is the longtime existence of a graphical solution to mean curvature flow starting from a graph of a continuous function defined over
\Omega^{n}_{b}=\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times(-b,b)
for some
b>0
, analogous to results in
Ecker & Huisken 1989
,
Clutterbuck 2005
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
b>0
,
n\geq 1
, let
\Omega^{n}_{b}=\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times(-b,b)
. For given
u_{0}\in C^{0}(\Omega^{n}_{b})
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{n}_{b}\times(0,\infty))\cap C^{0}(\Omega^{n}_{b}\times[0,\infty))
so that
u
solves the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\textit{\emph{div}}(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}})\text{ in }\Omega^{n}_{b}\times(0,\infty)\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}\text{ in }\Omega^{n}_{b}\end{cases}

(2)

If we further assume that for each
J>0
, one can find
\delta>0
so that
|u_{0}(x)|>J
for all
x\in\Omega^{n}_{b}
with
b-\delta<|x_{n}|<b
, then we can find a solution
u
so that each time slice also has the same property. In particular, the graphs of
u(\cdot,t)
are complete, graphical hypersurfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
moving by mean curvature.

Remark 1.2
.

Compared to results in
Sáez & Schnürer 2014
, although we are only working with
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times(-b,b)
instead of general domains, our result relaxes the local Lipchitz continuity to just continuity. We are also assuming a slightly weaker condition on
u_{0}
than it being a proper function in view of the case when the graph of
u_{0}
is a grim reaper plane.

If we move away from the graphical case, the flow in general develops singularity in finite time, and cannot be continued within the smooth category.

To understand what goes wrong, one has to analyze how singularities are formed. A typical approach is via a blowup analysis. As one approaches singular time, one ‘magnifies’ the flow near the singularity to obtain an approximate picture which models the singularity formation. These approximate pictures are called blowup limits.

It turns out that by choosing the blowup sequence in a particular way, these limit flows are self similar. For example, Huisken
Huisken 1990
showed that type I singularity formation can be modeled on self shrinking solutions. On the other hand, by following Hamilton’s blowup procedure in
Hamilton 1995
, Huisken and Sinestrari
Huisken & Sinestrari 1999a
,
Huisken & Sinestrari 1999
showed that type II singularity formation of mean convex mean curvature flow can be modeled on translating solutions.

In this paper, we will be interested in graphical translators, namely hypersurfaces
M\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
that are graphs of some function over some domain
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and satisfies

\langle\vec{H},\nu\rangle=\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle

(3)

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector,
\nu
is the unit normal vector, and
e_{n+1}=(0,0,..,1)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is the standard
n+1^{th}
unit vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. These are precisely the hypersurfaces which generate translating solutions to mean curvature flow by the formula

\mathbf{R}\ni t\to M+te_{n+1}.

(4)

Thus, in view of previous discussions, translators model type II singularity formation, and hence have been one of the central objects in the study of mean curvature flow.

In this paper, we focus on the dynamical stability of various graphical translators. Stability of solitons is a widely studied subject, and tell us how ‘rigid’ the soliton is under perturbations. Stability is usually studied in two different contexts, linear stability and dynamical stability. In the setting of translators, Ilmanen
Ilmanen 1994
showed that translators are minimal hypersurfaces in Euclidean space under the conformal metric

g_{ij}=e^{\frac{2}{n}x_{n+1}}\delta_{ij}.

(5)

Therefore, linear stability of translators refers to the non-negativity of the Jacobi operator

Lf=-(\Delta f+\langle e_{n+1},\nabla f\rangle+|A|^{2}).

(6)

In
Xin 2015
, Xin showed that all graphical translators are area minimizing, hence linearly stable.

Dynamical stability addresses the following question.

•

Given a graphical translator, will a ‘small perturbation’ of that translator converge back to the initial translator under mean curvature flow?

In this paper, we prove dynamical stability results for the grim reaper, two dimensional graphical translators, and asymptotically cylindrical translators.

We first look at the one dimensional mean curvature flow, also called curve shortening flow. Mullins
1 1
introduced a graphical translator in
\mathbf{R}^{2}
, which is now called the grim reaper. It turns out that this is the unique one dimensional translator up to rigid motion (see
Halldorsson 2010
). Wang and Wo
WANG & WO 2011
proved a dynamical stability result for grim reaper.

We generalize the result of Wang and Wo
WANG & WO 2011
by weakening the assumptions on the perturbation.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})
be the grim reaper. If
u_{0}:(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})\to\mathbf{R}
satisfies

\|u_{0}(x)-\overline{u}(x)\|_{C^{0}(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})}<\infty,

(7)

and the graph of
u_{0}
has bounded curvature and finite total curvature, then there exists a longtime solution
u
to graphical curve shortening flow starting from
u_{0}
, so that

\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t)-t=\overline{u}(x)+c_{0}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}),

(8)

where

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}u_{0}(x)-\overline{u}(x)dx.

(9)

Remark 1.4
.

Wang and Wo
WANG & WO 2011
assumed that
\|u_{0}-\overline{u}\|_{C^{2+\alpha}(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})}<\infty
, and
M_{0}
has finitely many inflection points. On the other hand, in theorem
1.3
, we do not impose any form of convexity assumption. Also, one can easily see that finite curvature condition follows from

\|u_{0}-\overline{u}\|_{C^{2}(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})}<\infty.

(10)

Thus our assumptions are indeed weaker than the
C^{2+\alpha}
closeness condition in
WANG & WO 2011
.

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.3
implies that one cannot obtain an analogous result to theorem 1.1 in
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
for grim reapers. Even if
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
is
identical
to
\overline{u}
for
x\neq(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
for any small
\epsilon>0
, if
u_{0}>\overline{u}
in
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, then
c_{0}>0
in theorem
1.3
, hence we do not have
convergence to
\overline{M}+te_{n+1}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u}+t)
, but instead to its vertical translate.

We now consider surfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
. Altschuler and Wu
Altschuler & Wu 1994
showed the existence of bowl soliton in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
, which is a rotationally symmetric, graphical translator. Hoffman et.al

\mathbf{R}^{3}

\Delta
-wings.

We prove dynamical stability of two dimensional graphical translators which are defined over a slab. The case of bowl soliton is dealt with in theorem
1.9
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})
be a graphical translator defined over
\Omega^{2}_{b}=\mathbf{R}\times(-b,b)
, and not contained in
\Omega^{2}_{b^{\prime}}\times\mathbf{R}
for any
b^{\prime}<b
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{0}(\Omega^{2}_{b})
be a convex function in the sense that the graph of
u_{0}
encloses a convex region in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
, and

\|u_{0}-\overline{u}\|_{C^{0}(\Omega_{b}^{2})}\leq C_{0}<\infty.

(11)

Then, there exists a longtime solution
u
to the graphical mean curvature flow starting from
u_{0}
, so that for each
t_{j}\to\infty
, by possibly passing through subsequence, we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}u(x_{1},x_{2},t+{t}_{j})-t_{j}=\overline{u}(x_{1}+c_{1},x_{2})+t+c_{0}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega^{2}_{b}\times\mathbf{R})

for some
c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbf{R}
. In other words, the perturbed flow converges to the translating solution
\overline{M}+te_{3}
modulo translation in
x_{1}x_{3}
-direction. If
\overline{M}
is a (tilted) grim reaper plane, then
c_{1}=0
,
c_{0}\in[-C_{0},C_{0}]
. If
\overline{M}
is a
\Delta
-wing (hence

, then
|c_{1}|\tan\theta+|c_{0}|\leq C_{0}
where
\theta=\arccos\frac{\pi}{2b}
.

Remark 1.7
.

In view of theorem 2.14 in
Angenent et al. 2025
, there is a unique convex mean curvature flow starting from graph of
u_{0}
. In particular, theorem
1.6
implies that the convex mean curvature flow starting from graph of

has to be graphical over
\Omega^{2}_{b}
, and converge locally smoothly to
\overline{M}
modulo space translation. On the other hand, all solutions we are considering are noncompact, thus it is unclear if convexity is preserved for all possible evolution.

Remark 1.8
.

Unlike theorem
1.3
, we have to allow the possibility of an extra translation in
x_{1}
-variable in addition to the vertical (
x_{3}
-direction) translation. This is because an

-direction translate of a

for details). The extra

-direction translation is superfluous for (tilted) grim reaper planes.

We now consider higher dimensional translators. Clutterbuck et.al
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
proved the existence, uniqueness, and dynamical stability result for the bowl soliton in all dimensions. Later, Haslhofer
Haslhofer 2015
proved a uniqueness result of the bowl soliton among uniformly two convex, non-collapsed translators.

In general, there is a whole family of graphical translators. Hoffman et.al
Hoffman et al. 2018
constructed
n-1

\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
which are not in general rotationally symmetric. In
\mathbf{R}^{4}

obtained a classification of non-collapsed translators, which are either the 3d bowl soliton, 2d bowl soliton
\times\mathbf{R}
, or a one parameter family of graphical translators constructed in

By closely following the terminologies in
Bamler & Lai 2025a
,
Bamler & Lai 2025
, we first define the notion of asymptotically
(n,k)
-cylindrical translators.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
. A translator

is asymptotically
(n,k)
- cylindrical if for any
\lambda_{j}\to 0
, the parabolically rescaled sequence of solutions

M^{j}_{t}=\lambda_{j}(M+\lambda_{j}^{-2}te_{n+1})\ \ t\in(-\infty,0)

converges locally smoothly to a multiplicity one shrinking cylinder solution

M^{n,k}_{cyl}=\{\mathbf{S}^{n-k}(\sqrt{-2(n-k)t})\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\}_{t\in(-\infty,0)}

(13)

as

.

The following theorem concerns the dynamical stability of asymptotically
(n,k)
-cylindrical translators.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})
be an asymptotically
(n,k)
-cylindrical translator (see definition
1.1
). Let
u_{0}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
so that

(i)\ \|u_{0}-\overline{u}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C_{0}<\infty,\ \ \ (ii)\ M_{0}=\text{Graph}(u_{0})\text{ is mean convex}.

(14)

Then there exists a longtime solution
u
to graphical mean curvature flow starting from
u_{0}
, so that for each
t_{j}\to\infty
, by possibly passing through subsequence, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}u(x,t+t_{j})-t_{j}=\overline{u}(x+p_{1})+t+p_{2}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}),

(15)

where
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. In other words, the perturbed solution converges locally smoothly to the translating solution
\overline{M}+te_{n+1}
modulo space translation. The amount of translation
(p_{1},p_{2})
has norm comparable to

.

Remark 1.10
.

Comparing with the dynamical stability result of Clutterbuck et.al
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
, our theorem considers all possible non-collapsed translators. Moreover, we still obtain a stability result without a strong assumption on the asymptotic behavior of the initial graph at farfield. On the other hand, we require mean convexity, which can be viewed as a

Remark 1.11
.

The modulo space translation statement is necessary due to examples such as
u_{0}=\overline{u}+c
for some

In the case the initially given translator is a bowl soliton with a Euclidean factor, by additionally assuming a similar asymptotic behavior at farfield as in
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
, we can remove the modulo space translation statement in theorem
1.9
by using the barriers constructed in
Clutterbuck et al. 2005

Corollary 1.12
.

Following the notations in theorem
1.9
, let
\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})
be a
n-k+1
-dimensional bowl soliton times
\mathbf{R}^{k-1}
. In other words, if we write
x=(\hat{x},\tilde{x})\in\mathbf{R}^{n-k+1}\times\mathbf{R}^{k-1}

\overline{u}(x)=\overline{u}(\hat{x})

(16)

is the graph function of a
n-k+1
-dimensional bowl soliton in
\mathbf{R}^{n-k+2}

\|u_{0}-\hat{u}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}<\infty,\ \lim_{|\hat{x}|\to\infty}\sup_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{k-1}}|u_{0}(x)-\overline{u}(x)|=0,

(17)

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(x,t)-t=\overline{u}(x)\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n}).

(18)

Remark 1.13
.

We note that although corollary

Outline of the proof strategy

We explain the main strategy to proving dynamical stability of various graphical translators. Classical regularity theory for graphical solutions to mean curvature flow established in
Ecker & Huisken 1989
,
Ecker & Huisken 1991
, and theorem
1.1
implies the existence of longtime graphical solutions to mean curvature flow starting from the perturbed hypersurface. Let us denote the solution by
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
. We then look at the forward limit of the flow. For any
t_{j}\to\infty
, we define

M^{j}_{t}=M_{t+t_{j}}-t_{j}e_{n+1},\ t\in[-t_{j},\infty),

(19)

where
e_{n+1}=(0,0,..,1)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is the velocity of the initially given translator. Note that in all the theorems we shall prove, we are assuming
C^{0}
closeness. This means that one can use two copies of translating solutions generated by the given translator as upper and lower barriers. This will give us a uniform
C^{0}
estimate for above sequence of solutions independently of
j
, which can be upgraded to uniform
C^{k}
estimate. Thus, one can take a smooth subsequential limit. The limit flow is also a graphical solution to mean curvature flow, which is eternal and lies between two translating solutions.

The first task is to show that the limit flow is also a graphical translating solution with the same velocity
e_{n+1}
. For theorem
1.3
, and theorem
1.6
, this will be achieved by using the Harnack inequality of Hamilton
Hamilton 1995a
. For theorem
1.9
, this will be done by showing that the limit flow we obtained is an asymptotically cylindrical flow. Then by using the recent classification result of
Bamler & Lai 2025
, together with the fact that the limit flow is eternal, bounded between two translating solutions, we can conclude that the limit flow is a non-collapsed translator of the same velocity as the two barrier solutions. We then use existing classification theorems of graphical translators to conclude that the limit flow is actually equal to the translating solutions generated by the initial translator up to space translation.

We emphasize that our strategy heavily relies on classification theorems for translators. The strategy itself can actually be applied to more general convex graphical translators, and will tell us that in the limit, one gets a convex graphical translator that can be bounded by two copies of the initial translator. However, to show that the limit translator is equal to the one we started with, one needs a strong understanding of how various translators look like. To the author’s knowledge, currently available classification results of translators are

•

Uniqueness of one dimensional translators (see
Halldorsson 2010
).

•

Uniqueness of bowl solitons (see
Haslhofer 2015
).

•

Classification of graphical translators in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
(see
Hoffman et al. 2018
).

•

Classification of asymptotically cylindrical translators (see
Bamler & Lai 2025
,
Bamler & Lai 2025a
,
Choi et al. 2021
).

In particular, classification of higher dimensional graphical translators which are defined over higher dimensional slabs remains open, hence our method cannot yet be used to cover this case as well.

M_{t}=\text{Graph}(u(\cdot,t))

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\frac{D^{2}_{ij}u}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}=\text{second fundamental form},

(Ancient pancake solution)

For each

Next, we recall Hamilton’s differential Harnack inequality for mean curvature flow

Theorem 2.1
.

[Differential Harnack inequality]
Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in(-\alpha,T)}
be a convex, smooth solution to mean curvature flow which is either compact, or complete with bounded curvature. Then for any tangent vector

Z(V)=A(V,V)+2\nabla_{V}H+\frac{H}{2(t+\alpha)}+\nabla_{t}H\geq 0.

(28)

Here,

H
is the mean curvature. In particular, if the flow is ancient i.e
\alpha=\infty
, then for any tangent vector
V
, we have

Z(V)=A(V,V)+2\nabla_{V}H+\nabla_{t}H\geq 0.

(29)

Proposition 2.2

Let

3
Existence of graphical solution

In this section, we prove the existence of longtime graphical solution to mean curvature flow over a slab
\Omega^{n}_{b}=\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times(-b,b)
with continuous initial data. Following the ideas in
Clutterbuck et al. 2005
, we construct a sequence of entire graph solutions to mean curvature flow with approximate initial data. We show that one can take a
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega^{n}_{b})
subsequential limit, which is the desired graphical solution over a slab. Due to standard quasilinear PDE theory, this can be done by obtaining an apriori
C^{1}
estimate of graphical solutions over
\Omega^{n}_{b}
.
C^{0}
estimate is obtained by using ancient pancakes of Bourni et.al
Bourni et al. 2021
as barriers. Then, one can apply the gradient estimate of Colding - Minicozzi
Colding & Minicozzi 2004
to obtain the desired
C^{1}
estimate.

Dynamical stability of grim reaper

Proof of theorem

(100)

M^{j}_{t}=M_{t+t_{j}}-t_{j}e_{2}\text{ for }t\in[-t_{j},\infty).

\displaystyle\int_{M^{j}_{t_{0}+\delta}}\eta_{N}(\epsilon^{2}+\kappa_{j}^{2})^{1/2}ds

Theorem 5.1

.

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\textit{\emph{div}}(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}})\text{ in }\Omega^{2}_{b}\times(0,\infty)\\

Furthermore, for each

\lim_{j\to+\infty}u(x_{1},x_{2},t+\tilde{t}_{j})-\tilde{t}_{j}=\overline{u}(x_{1}+c_{1},x_{2})+t+c_{0}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega^{2}_{b}\times\mathbf{R})

\overline{M}+te_{3}
modulo translation in
x_{1}x_{3}
-direction. If
\overline{M}

Remark 5.2
.

It is unclear if the constants

\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x_{1},x_{2},t)-t=\overline{u}(x_{1}+c_{1},x_{2})+c_{0}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega^{2}_{b}).

(127)

Remark 5.3
.

Compared to theorem
4.1
, we have to consider an extra

-direction translation in the case of
\Delta
-wings. This is due to the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\overline{u}:\Omega_{b}^{2}\to\mathbf{R}
with

so that

\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})=\Delta\text{-wing}.

(128)

Then, for any

,

\overline{u}(x_{1},x_{2})-\tan\theta|c_{1}|\leq\overline{u}(x_{1}+c_{1},x_{2})\leq\overline{u}(x_{1},x_{2})+\tan\theta|c_{1}|

with

Proof.

By using theorem 1.5 in
Spruck & Xiao 2020
(or theorem 2.4 in

,

\lim_{|x_{1}|\to\infty}|\frac{\partial\overline{u}}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})|=\tan\theta\text{ with }\theta=\arccos\frac{\pi}{2b}.

(130)

Thus convexity of
\overline{u}
implies that

|\frac{\partial\overline{u}}{\partial x_{1}}|\leq\tan\theta\text{ everywhere}.

(131)

Then the lemma follows by integration in
x_{1}
.
∎

Above lemma implies that in the statement of theorem

, we can take

u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=\overline{u}(x_{1}+\frac{C_{0}}{\tan\theta},x_{2}).

(132)

Then since the graph of

(133)

This simple example implies that we cannot drop the

-direction translation in the statement of theorem

.

We briefly explain the proof of theorem
5.1
. The idea is again to use general strategy
1

We first prove two lemmas that will be used to construct the longtime solution to graphical mean curvature flow starting from
u_{0}

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\overline{M}=\text{Graph}(\overline{u})
is a (tilted) grim reaper plane, i.e

u:\Omega^{2}_{b}\to\mathbf{R},\ u(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}\tan\theta-\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\theta}\ln(\cos(x_{2}\cos\theta)),\ \theta=\arccos\frac{\pi}{2b}.

(134)

Let
u_{0}
be as in theorem
5.1
. Then

u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=u_{0}(0,x_{2})+x_{1}\tan\theta.

(135)

Proof of lemma
5.5
.

For each
x_{2}\in(-b,b)
, we consider

\mathbf{R}\ni x_{1}\to\phi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})=\frac{\partial u_{0}}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2}).

(136)

Note that by convexity,
\phi_{x_{2}}
is well defined for almost every
x_{1}
, for each
x_{2}\in(-b,b)

\phi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})=\tan\theta\text{ whenever it is well defined}.

(137)

If for some

\phi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})\geq\tan\theta+\delta\text{ for a.e }x_{1}\geq a.

(138)

Then by integrating, we see that

u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})-u_{0}(a,x_{2})\geq(\tan\theta+\delta)(x_{1}-a)\text{ for all }x_{1}\geq a.

(139)

On the other hand, by (

u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})-u_{0}(a,x_{2})\leq(\tan\theta)(x_{1}-a)+2C_{0}\text{ for all }x_{1}\geq a.

\phi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})=\tan\theta\text{ whenever it is well defined}.

(141)

(142)

completing the proof of lemma

.

We now show how one can construct a convex graphical solution to mean curvature flow on which the Harnack inequality holds, provided the initial data is a graph of a convex, proper function.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let

,

, and
\Omega^{n}_{b}=\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times(-b,b)

is proper, i.e the preimage of any compact set is compact. Then there exists

6
Dynamical stability of asymptotically cylindrical translators

In this section, we prove dynamical stability of asymptotically cylindrical translators (see definition
1.1
). Note that such translators are fully classified by Bamler-Lai
Bamler & Lai 2025a
,
Bamler & Lai 2025
, and are either the bowl soliton, or entire graph translators constructed in
Hoffman et al. 2018
with possible Euclidean factors. The proof sketch is as follows. We first show that under the given conditions, the forward limit must be an asymptotically cylindrical flow. Then by using the classification result in
Bamler & Lai 2025
, we can deduce that it must be a translating flow with the same velocity as the initially given translator. In the case the initial translator is a bowl soliton with possible Euclidean factors, one can use uniqueness result of bowl solitons

to show that the two flows are the same up to translation. In the case of flying wing solutions, we show that the two flows are the same up to translation by using the parametrization of space of all non-collapsed translators given in theorem 1.6 in

We first restate theorem

-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Therefore, one can apply compactness theorem for integral Brakke flows to conclude that there exists a self shrinking tangent flow

\mu_{t}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}N^{\lambda}_{t}

(200)

where the convergence is currently at the level of integral Brakke flow sense.

We claim that the tangent flow is a multiplicity one shrinking cylinder
M^{n,k}_{t}=\mathbf{S}^{n-k}(\sqrt{-2(n-k)t})\times\mathbf{R}^{k}
. We first look at the support of
\mu_{t}
. Take any
\phi\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n+1})
so that

\text{supp}(\phi)\cap M^{n,k}_{t}=\emptyset.

(201)

Because
M^{\infty}_{t}
is trapped between
\overline{M}^{+}_{t}=\overline{M}_{t}+C_{0}e_{n+1}
and
\overline{M}^{-}_{t}=\overline{M}_{t}-C_{0}e_{n+1}
due to (
197
), the same containment relation remains true after parabolically rescaling by
\lambda
. By assumption that
\overline{M}
is an asymptotically
(n,k)
-cylindrical translator, we see that both
\overline{M}^{+}_{t},\overline{M}^{-}_{t}
has multiplicity one
M^{n,k}_{t}
as its tangent flow at infinity. Therefore by using the rescaled
\overline{M}^{+}_{t},\overline{M}^{-}_{t}
as barriers, we immediately see that for all sufficiently small
\lambda>0
,

N^{\lambda}_{t}\cap\text{supp}(\phi)=\emptyset,

(202)

which implies that

\text{supp}(\mu_{t})\cap\text{supp}(\phi)=\emptyset.

(203)

Since
\phi
is arbitrary function with support away from
M^{n,k}_{t}
,
this implies that

\text{supp}(\mu_{t})\subset M^{n,k}_{t}.

(204)

To show reverse inclusion, we again use calibration argument. Take any
x_{0}\in M^{n,k}_{t}
, and
r>0
. We claim that

\frac{\mu_{t}(\overline{B(x_{0},r)})}{r^{n}}>0

(205)

First note that

\{\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}+ae_{n+1}\}_{a\in\mathbf{R}}

(206)

is a convex foliation of
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Estimate (
197
) implies that
N^{\lambda}_{t}
is trapped between
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}
. Since
N^{\lambda}_{t}
is an entire graph, and
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}\to M^{n,k}_{t}
locally smoothly, we can apply standard calibration argument (see
Haslhofer & Kleiner 2013
, remark 2.6) to compare

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}+C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})

(207)

and

\mathcal{H}^{n}(N^{\lambda}_{t}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})+\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})

(208)

where
S_{\lambda}
is the set of all points on
\partial B(x_{0},r)
which lie between
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}
. This implies that

\mathcal{H}^{n}(N^{\lambda}_{t}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})+\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n}(\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}+C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}\cap B(x_{0},r)).

(209)

Therefore, by letting
\lambda\to 0
and using
x_{0}\in M^{n,k}_{t}
,
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm\lambda C_{0}e_{n+1}\to M^{n,k}_{t}
, we see that

\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})=\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda})\to 0\text{ as }\lambda\to 0,

(210)

thus

\mu_{t}(\overline{B(x_{0},r)})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n}(M^{n,k}_{t}\cap B(x_{0},r))>0.

(211)

This implies that
x_{0}\in\text{supp}(\mu_{t})
, hence
M^{n,k}_{t}=\text{supp}(\mu_{t})
.

Thus,
\mu_{t}
is equal to
M^{n,k}_{t}
with possible multiplicities. By once again following the ideas of White
White 2000
, we can rule out high multiplicities. Since the argument is straightforward and is very similar to the previous argument, we record it here in detail. Note that
N^{\lambda}_{t}
is also a mean convex entire graph. Therefore, we can repeat the previous calibration argument with role of
N^{\lambda}_{t}
and
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}
reversed. More specifically, note that

\{N^{\lambda}_{t}+ae_{n+1}\}_{a\in\mathbf{R}}

(212)

is a mean convex foliation of entire
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Choose any
x_{0}\in M^{n,k}_{t}
. Since
N^{\lambda}_{t}
is trapped between
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm\lambda C_{0}e_{n+1}
, by standard calibration argument, we can compare

\mathcal{H}^{n}(N^{\lambda}_{t}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})

(213)

and

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}-C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})+\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda}),

(214)

where
S_{\lambda}
is the set of all points on
\partial B(x_{0},r)
which lie between
\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}\pm C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}
. This gives us

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\lambda\overline{M}_{\lambda^{-2}t}-C_{0}\lambda e_{n+1}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)})+\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n}(N^{\lambda}_{t}\cap\overline{B(x_{0},r)}).

(215)

Then after taking the limit
\lambda\to 0
, since
\mathcal{H}^{n}(S_{\lambda})\to 0
, and
\overline{M}
is an asymptotically cylindrical translator, we obtain a reverse inequality to (
211
), namely

\mu_{t}(B(x_{0},r))\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(M^{n,k}_{t}\cap B(x_{0},r))

(216)

for all
r>0
. Thus, by dividing by
\omega_{n}r^{n}
where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of an
n
-dimensional unit ball, letting
r\to 0
and using
x_{0}\in M^{n,k}_{t}
, we see that the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff density of
\mu_{t}
at
x_{0}\in M^{n,k}_{t}
is bounded by 1. Since
\mu_{t}
is an integral
n
-varifold with support
M^{n,k}_{t}
, this implies that
\mu_{t}
is equal to multiplicity one
M^{n,k}_{t}
. Thus by local regularity theorem
White 2005
, the convergence is smooth, hence
M^{\infty}_{t}
is an asymptotically
(n,k)
-cylindrical flow, hence it must be a non-collapsed, convex translator of velocity
e_{n+1}
. In particular, we can write

u^{\infty}(x,t)=u^{\infty}(x,0)+t=u^{\infty}(x)+t.

(217)

We first consider the case when
\overline{M}
is a
n-k+1
dimensional Bowl soliton times
\mathbf{R}^{k-1}
. This means

\overline{u}(x_{1},..,x_{n})=\overline{u}(x_{1},..,x_{n-k+1}).

(218)

The convexity of
u^{\infty}(x)
together with

\overline{u}-C_{0}\leq u^{\infty}(x)\leq\overline{u}(x)+C_{0}

(219)

implies that
u^{\infty}
is also independent of
(x_{n-k+2},..,x_{n})
, i.e
M^{\infty}
splits off the same
\mathbf{R}^{k-1}
factor as
\overline{M}
. Then, we see that the lower dimensional translator factor of
M^{\infty}
is asymptotic to the round cylinder

\mathbf{S}^{n-k}(\sqrt{2(n-k)})\times\mathbf{R}

(220)

in
\mathbf{R}^{n-k+2}
. By uniqueness of bowl soliton proved in
Haslhofer 2015
, the lower dimensional translator factor must also be a
n-k+1
-dimensional bowl soliton, hence we see that

M^{\infty}=\overline{M}+p_{0}

(221)

for some
p_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
.

We now consider the remaining case when
\overline{M}
is a flying wing soliton constructed in
Hoffman et al. 2018
with possible Euclidean factor . In this case,
M^{\infty}
cannot be a bowl soliton with a Euclidean factor. Indeed, if
M^{\infty}
was a bowl soliton with a Euclidean factor, then by reversing the role of
M^{\infty}
and
\overline{M}
, and viewing
M^{\infty}
as the barrier, previous argument implies that
\overline{M}
also has to be a bowl soliton with a Euclidean factor which is not the case. Thus, both
M^{\infty}
and
\overline{M}
are flying wing solitons with possible Euclidean factors, in particular we can apply proposition
2.2
to both
M^{\infty}_{t}
and
\overline{M}_{t}
.

We now claim that
M^{\infty}=\overline{M}+p_{0}
for some constant
p_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. First, by theorem
2.3
, there exists some
p_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
so that the translated flow
\hat{M}_{t}=M^{\infty}_{t}+p_{0}
belongs to
\textbf{MCF}^{n,k}_{\text{soliton}}
. Then by theorem
2.3
, we can show that
\hat{M}=\overline{M}
by showing that
Q(\hat{M})=Q(\overline{M})
, and
b(\hat{M})=b(\overline{M})
(see proposition
2.2
, definition
2.7
). Since both
\hat{M}
, and
\overline{M}
have velocity
e_{n+1}
, by definition
2.7
, we see that

b(\hat{M})=\frac{e_{n+1}}{|e_{n+1}|^{2}}=e_{n+1}=b(\overline{M}).

(222)

Thus it remains to show that

Q(\hat{M)}=Q(\overline{M}).

(223)

The rough idea is that
Q
determines the asymptotic expansion of the graph function of the rescaled mean curvature flow over the cylinder
M^{n,k}_{-1}=\mathbf{S}^{n-k}(\sqrt{2(n-k)})\times\mathbf{R}^{k}
as
\tau\to-\infty
up to an error of order
|\tau|^{-3}
where
\tau
is the rescaled time parameter (see proposition
2.2
). On the other hand, if we denote

\hat{N}_{\tau}=e^{\tau/2}\hat{M}_{-e^{-\tau}},\ \ \ \overline{N}_{\tau}=e^{\tau/2}\overline{M}_{-e^{-\tau}},

(224)

then because before rescaling,
\hat{M}
is trapped between two copies of
\overline{M}
which are apart from each other by a fixed amount for all time slices, similar containment relation also holds after type I rescaling. However, because of the rescaling factor, we see that
\hat{N}_{\tau}
is trapped between two copies of
\overline{N}_{\tau}
which are apart from each other by at most
C_{0}e^{\tau/2}
. In particular, this implies that the difference between two graph functions must be exponentially decaying. This implies that the leading order terms must in fact coincide, i.e
Q(\hat{M})=Q(\overline{M})
.

We make above argument precise. Recalling the definition of
\hat{M}
, and (
197
), we see that for each
t\in\mathbf{R}
,
\hat{M}_{t}
is trapped between

\overline{M}_{t}+p_{0}-C_{0}e_{n+1}
, and
\overline{M}_{t}+p_{0}+C_{0}e_{n+1}
. Therefore, after type I rescaling, we see that
\hat{N}_{\tau}
is trapped between
\overline{N}_{\tau}+e^{\tau/2}(p_{0}-C_{0}e_{n+1})
and
\overline{N}_{\tau}+e^{\tau/2}(p_{0}+C_{0}e_{n+1})
. Note that the two barriers
\overline{N}_{\tau}+e^{\tau/2}(p_{0}-C_{0}e_{n+1})
and
\overline{N}_{\tau}+e^{\tau/2}(p_{0}+C_{0}e_{n+1})
are space translations of
\overline{N}_{\tau}
with the norm of the translation vector bounded by
C_{1}e^{\tau/2}
for some fixed uniform constant
C_{1}
for all
\tau
. By combining this with the fact that
\hat{N}_{\tau},\overline{N}_{\tau}
converges to
M^{n,k}_{-1}
locally smoothly, we see that for each fixed
R>0
, we have

\|\hat{u}(\cdot,\tau)-\overline{u}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{0}(D_{R})}\leq 10C_{1}e^{\tau/2}

(225)

for all sufficiently small
\tau
, where
D_{R}=B_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(0,R)\cap M^{n,k}_{-1}
.

We now apply proposition
2.2
to both
\hat{M}
and
\overline{M}
. Then by possibly taking smaller
\tilde{\tau}
, there exists
\hat{U}^{+}=\hat{U}_{1}+\hat{U}_{1/2}+\hat{U}_{0}+..+\hat{U}_{-10},\ \overline{U}^{+}=\overline{U}_{1}+\overline{U}_{1/2}+\overline{U}_{0}+..+\overline{U}_{-10}
with each
\hat{U}_{\lambda},\overline{U}_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}\subset L^{2}_{w}(M^{n,k}_{-1})
, so that for each
R>0

\|\hat{u}(\cdot\tau)-\hat{U}^{+}(\tau)\|_{C^{0}(D_{R})},\|\overline{u}(\cdot\tau)-\overline{U}^{+}(\tau)\|_{C^{0}(D_{R})}\leq\frac{1}{10}(\tilde{\tau}-\tau+10)^{-11}

(226)

for all sufficiently small
\tau\leq\tilde{\tau}.
Here,
L^{2}_{w}
denotes the usual Gaussian weighted
L^{2}
space over
M^{n,k}_{-1}
, and
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
denote the
\lambda
-eigenspace of the linearized rescaled mean curvature flow operator at
M^{n,k}_{-1}
. Then, by combining (
225
) and (
226
), we see that

\|\hat{U}^{+}(\tau)-\overline{U}^{+}(\tau)\|_{C^{0}(D_{R})}\leq C|\tau|^{-11}

(227)

for each
R>0
for all sufficiently small
\tau
. Here,
C
is independent of
R>0
and
\tau
.

Since both
\hat{U}^{+}(\tau)
, and
\overline{U}^{+}(\tau)
are just finite sum of the eigenfunctions of the linearized rescaled mean curvature flow operator which are Hermite polynomials, we see that (
227
), together with orthogonality of the eigenfunctions, imply that

\|\hat{U}_{0}(\tau)-\overline{U}_{0}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq C|\tau|^{-11}

(228)

for all sufficiently small
\tau
. Moreover, again by proposition
2.2
, we can find two smooth curves in
\mathcal{V}_{0}
denoted by
\hat{V}(\tau)
,
\overline{V}(\tau)
so that (i)
\hat{V}(\tau),\overline{V}(\tau)\to 0\text{ as }\tau\to-\infty
, (ii) the coefficients of
\hat{V}(\tau),\overline{V}(\tau)
are longtime solutions to the ODE (
37
),

\textup{(iii)}\ \ \|\hat{U}_{0}(\tau)-\hat{V}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{w}},\|\overline{U}_{0}(\tau)-\overline{V}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq C|\tau|^{-3}

(229)

for all sufficiently small
\tau
, and (iv)
\hat{V}(\tau),\overline{V}(\tau)
determines
Q(\hat{M)},Q(\overline{M})
respectively. Then by combining (
228
) and (
229
), we see that

\|\hat{V}(\tau)-\overline{V}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq C|\tau|^{-3}

(230)

for all sufficiently small
\tau
. Then by lemma
2.4
, (
230
) implies that
\hat{V}(\tau)=\overline{V}(\tau)
, hence
Q(\hat{M})=Q(\overline{M})
. Therefore, we ultimately have
Q(\hat{M})=Q(\overline{M})
, and
b(\hat{M})=b(\overline{M})
, hence by theorem
2.3
, we have
\hat{M}=\overline{M}
, hence

M^{\infty}_{t}=\overline{M}+te_{n+1}+p_{0}

(231)

for some fixed
p_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Writing
p_{0}=(-p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}
, this is equivalent to

\lim_{j\to+\infty}u(x,t+t_{j})-t_{j}=\overline{u}(x+p_{1})+t+p_{2}.

(232)

To show that the norm of
p_{0}=(p_{1},p_{2})
is comparable to
C_{0}
, first note that if
\overline{M}
(hence
M^{\infty}
) has a Euclidean factor, we may simply take the components of
p_{1}
in that factor to be
0
. Thus we might as well assume that
\overline{M}
is strictly convex. Then the comparability of
|p_{0}|
and
C_{0}
follows from the strict convexity of
\overline{M}
, and the fact that
M^{\infty}_{t}=\overline{M}+te_{n+1}+p_{0}
has to be trapped between

\overline{M}+te_{n+1}+C_{0}e_{n+1}
and
\overline{M}+te_{n+1}-C_{0}e_{n+1}
.

Finally, we prove remark
6.3
. Assume that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}u(x,t+t_{j})-t_{j}=\overline{u}(x+p_{1})+t+p_{2}.

(233)

for some
p_{0}=(p_{1},p_{2})
with

|t_{j+1}-t_{j}|\leq\alpha\text{ for all }j\text{ for some }\alpha>0.

(234)

Choose any other subsequence
s_{j}
, and let
\tilde{p}_{0}=(\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2})
be the constants corresponding to
s_{j}
. Define
\tilde{t}_{j}
to be one of
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
which is closest to
s_{j}
. Then because of (
234
), we have
-\alpha\leq s_{j}-\tilde{t}_{j}\leq\alpha
, hence the local smooth convergence implies

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}u(x,s_{j}-\tilde{t}_{j}+\tilde{t}_{j})-s_{j}-\overline{u}(x+p_{1})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}u(x,s_{j})-s_{j}-\overline{u}(x+p_{1})

\displaystyle=\overline{u}(x+\tilde{p}_{1})-\overline{u}(x+p_{1})+\tilde{p}_{2}

for all
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
. By taking some components of
p_{1}
,
\tilde{p}_{1}
to vanish whenever
\overline{M}
has a Euclidean factor, strict convexity of
\overline{u}
with respect to remaining components imply that
p_{1}=\tilde{p}_{1}
, and
p_{2}=\tilde{p}_{2}
. This shows that the constants are independent of choice of (sub)sequence, hence we get full convergence

\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t)-t=\overline{u}(x+p_{1})+p_{2}\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n}).

(235)

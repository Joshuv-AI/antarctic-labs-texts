On two generalizations of perspective abelian groups
Andrey R. Chekhlov, Peter V. Danchev, Özgür Taşdemir

Abstract
. We are generalizing in two non-trivial ways the recently defined perspective Abelian groups to the so-called IC-groups and TP-groups , respectively, and obtain numerous results in these two directions that can be viewed as improvements on their rather more complicated structures and properties.



1.
Introduction and Motivation

Before starting our main work, we need some backgrounds as follows: For an arbitrary ring
R
, Nicholson and Sanchez call in
[
15
]
an
R
-module
M
to be
morphic
if
M/\alpha(M)\cong Ker(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in End_{R}(M)
. They proved in
[
15
, Theorem 5]
that
M
is a morphic module if, and only if, whenever
M/A\cong B
, where
A
and
B
are submodules of
M
, then
M/B\cong A
.

It is well known that an
R
-module
M
is said to have
the internal cancelation property
(or just
IC
for short) if whenever
M=K\oplus L=K^{\prime}\oplus L^{\prime}
with
K\cong K^{\prime}
, then
L\cong L^{\prime}
.

Moreover, Taşdemir and Koşan called in
[
18
]
an
R
-module
M
to be
summand-morphic
if
M/K\cong L
where
K
,
L\leq^{\oplus}M
, then
M/L\cong K
. And they proved in
[
18
, Proposition 3.2]
that such a module
M
is summand-morphic if, and only if,
M
has the IC property.

On the other hand, two direct summands
K
and
L
of an
R
-module
M
are called
perspective
, which is denoted by
K\overset{P}{\sim}L
, if there exists a submodule
N
of
M
such that
M=K\oplus N=L\oplus N
. Obviously,
K\overset{P}{\sim}L
implies
K\cong L
as well as
K\overset{P}{\sim}L

\iff

L\overset{P}{\sim}K
, which means that this operation is symmetric.

Besides, Garg, Grover and Khurana call in
[
10
]
an
R
-module
M
to be
perspective
if
K\cong L
yields that
K\overset{P}{\sim}L
for any
K
,
L\leq^{\oplus}M
, i.e., there exists a submodule
N
of
M
such that
M=K\oplus N=L\oplus N
.

Strengthening this notion, Khurana and Nielsen call in
[
12
]
an
R
-module
M
to have the
transitive perspectivity
(or just
TP
for short) whenever
K\overset{P}{\sim}L\overset{P}{\sim}N
forces
K\overset{P}{\sim}N
for any
K
,
L
,
N\leq^{\oplus}M
.

It is easy to see that any perspective module is both IC and TP.

In the next statement, we provide an useful equivalent condition for being endowed with the TP property like this.

Theorem 1
.

The following are equivalent for an
R
-module
M
:

(1)

M
has TP;

(2)

If
M=K\oplus L=K^{\prime}\oplus L^{\prime}
with
K\overset{P}{\sim}K^{\prime}
, then
L\overset{P}{\sim}L^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
M
have transitive perspectivity and
M=K\oplus L=K^{\prime}\oplus L^{\prime}
with
K\overset{P}{\sim}K^{\prime}
for some submodules
K
,
K^{\prime}
,
L
and
L^{\prime}
of
M
. Then, one sees that
M=K\oplus N=K^{\prime}\oplus N
for some
N\leq M
. Now, we have
L\overset{P}{\sim}N
and
N\overset{P}{\sim}L^{\prime}
. Thus,
L\overset{P}{\sim}L^{\prime}
, because
M
has the TP property.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Letting
K
,
L
and
N
be direct summands of
M
with
K\overset{P}{\sim}L
and
L\overset{P}{\sim}N
, one can write that

M=K\oplus X=L\oplus X=L\oplus Y=N\oplus Y

for some
X
,
Y\leq M
. Therefore,
X\overset{P}{\sim}Y
and, by assumption, it must be
K\overset{P}{\sim}N
, as required.
∎

Recently, Cǎlugǎreanu and Chekhlov defined and explored in
[
4
]
the notion of
perspective abelian groups
.

Another advantage of this project is that Cǎlugǎreanu published a paper about “morphic abelian groups” before. We also have a similar equivalence to the definition of morphic for IC (please see the definition of summand-morphic which is equivalent to IC). This can make our work easier. It can allow us to make easier comparisons between morphic abelian groups and IC abelian gorups.

Our aim in the present article is to expand somewhat the concept of perspectivity for abelian groups to the next two more general concepts, again related to abelian groups, namely:

Internal Cancelation (IC):
We shall say that an abelian group
G
is
IC
if
G=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
with
A\cong A^{\prime}
assures that
B\cong B^{\prime}
.

Transitive Perspectivity (TP):
We shall say that an abelian group
G
is
TP
if
G=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
with
A\overset{P}{\sim}A^{\prime}
ensures that
B\overset{P}{\sim}B^{\prime}
.

At first look on these two definitions, one can ask: are IC and TP properties equivalent for arbitrary modules? The answer is definitely
no
as the following example shows. Recall that a module
M
said to have the
substitution property
if, for every module
N
having a decomposition
N=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
with
A\cong M\cong A^{\prime}
, there exists a submodule
C\leq N
such that
N=C\oplus B=C\oplus B^{\prime}
. Note that the substitution property yields the IC one.

Example 2
.

(1) The
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
has IC (see
[
1
, Example 3.2]
), but it does not have the property of perspectivity by
[
10
, Theorem 5.12]
. Even something more, it is not TP in regard to
[

, Corollary 2.6]
as obviously
\mathbb{Z}
does not have the substitution property.

(2) In
[
11
, p.13]
, there is a construction which has the TP property, but is not directly finite (and hence, it does not have IC).

However, for modules, the following hierarchy is principally well-known:

summmand-morphic (i.e., IC)
perspective
transitive perspectivity

Proposition 8

A torsion abelian group is IC if, and only if, all its primary components are IC.

Proof.

The necessity is pretty clear, because being IC is inherited via direct summands.

For the sufficiency, let
G=\bigoplus_{p}G_{p}
be the direct decomposition of

into
p

of

for all

of

G/K\cong\bigoplus_{p}(G_{p}/K_{p})

Now, we assume that
G/K\cong L
for a direct summand
L
of

. Similarly, we may write

, whence
\bigoplus_{p}(G_{p}/K_{p})\cong\bigoplus_{p}L_{p}

G_{p}/K_{p}\cong L_{p}

, as wanted.
∎

(i) The fully decomposable torsion-free group

Every torsion-free group of rank 2 is IC since it is either decomposable or indecomposable. But there exists a torsion-free group of rank 3 which is not IC. Indeed, an appeal to

Remark 21
.

Recall that the group
B
has the cancelation property (that is, a CP group) if, for all groups
H,K
, the isomorphism
B\oplus H\cong B\oplus K
forces the isomorphism
H\cong K
. This is obviously equivalent to the next property: for all groups
H,K
, from the equality
B\oplus H=C\oplus K
, the isomorphism
B\cong C
yields the isomorphism
H\cong K
(see the commentaries before
[
8
, Theorem 90.4]
). However, looking at
[
8
, §15, Exersize 24]
, one finds that all finitely generated groups are CP. Moreover, all finitely co-generated groups also are CP.
This simply leads to the fact that the quasi-cyclic groups are CP, but we may directly derive that from Proposition
24
quoted below.

We now would like to indicate the following elementary, but pivotal properties.

(1) A direct summand of a CP group
A
is too CP.

Indeed, if
A=B\oplus C
and
B\oplus H\cong B\oplus K
, then

(B\oplus C)\oplus H\cong(B\oplus C)\oplus K,

so
H\cong K
, as required.

(2) If
A
is a CP group, then
A^{n}
also is a CP group for every integer number
n\geq 2
.

We argue by induction: if

(A\oplus A)\oplus H\cong(A\oplus A)\oplus K,

then

A\oplus(A\oplus H)\cong A\oplus(A\oplus K),

and thus
A\oplus H\cong A\oplus K
yielding
H\cong K
, as needed.

Even something more, if
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are both CP groups, then
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
also is CP, i.e., the class consisting of CP groups is closed under formation of finite direct sums. To substantiate this, it is pretty clear that it is enough to settle the property only for two direct summands: to that end, supposing
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}\oplus K\cong H_{1}\oplus H_{2}\oplus M
, then
H_{2}\oplus K\cong H_{2}\oplus M
. Thus,
K\cong M
, that is,
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
is CP, as required.

(3) Same as Remark
13
, it can be proven that, if
A\ncong A\oplus A
, then both
A^{(\alpha)}
and
A^{\alpha}
are not CP, provided the cardinal
\alpha
is infinite. Indeed, if
A
is a non-zero CP group, then it must be that
A\oplus A\ncong A
– in fact, if
A\oplus A\cong A
, then
A\oplus A\cong A\oplus\{0\}
, so that
A\cong\{0\}
, a contradiction, as suspected.

(4) Any CP group
A
is IC.

Indeed, if we write

A=B\oplus C=H\oplus F,

where
B\cong H
, then one follows that

(B\oplus C)\oplus C=(H\oplus F)\oplus C=(H\oplus C)\oplus F,

where

B\oplus C\cong H\oplus C\cong A,

forcing
C\cong F
.

Remark 22
.

Not every rank 1 torsion-free group is CP (see, for example, Remark
16
), so Proposition
14
gives the desired example of a IC group that is not CP. In fact, each torsion-free group of finite rank is CP in the class of torsion-free groups of rank 1, as indicated in the second bullet below.

We now proceed with the following two more relevant observations:

\bullet
If
A
and
B
are isomorphic divisible subgroups of some group
G
with
A\cap B=\{0\}
, then
A
and
B
have common addition in
G
. Indeed, we can write
G=(A\oplus B)\oplus C
. If
f:A\to B
is the existing isomorphism, then one may write that
A\oplus B=A\oplus H=B\oplus H
, where
H=\{a+f(a)\,|\,a\in A\}
, whence it must be that

G=B=A\oplus H\oplus C=B\oplus H\oplus C,

as suspected.

\bullet
Every torsion-free group
G
of finite rank is CP in the class of torsion-free groups of rank 1. Indeed, if we suppose that
K\oplus G\cong H\oplus A
, where
G\cong A
and
K
,
H
are torsion-free of rank 1, then we know that
K\cong H
(cf.
[
8
, §90, Exersize 13]
). But, as already observed above, not every torsion-free group of finite rank is TP.

We are now preparing to describe all perspective torsion groups having divisible part with each primary component of finite rank and reduced part with each primary component of finite Ulm-Kaplansky invariants.

Theorem 23
.

A reduced countable
p
-group is perspective if, and only if, it has finite all Ulm-Kaplansky invariants.

Proof.

Necessity is manifestly evident, so we leave out the details.

To substantiate sufficiency, let us write
G=A\oplus B=C\oplus K
, where
A\cong B
. Notice that any
p
-group with finite all Ulm-Kaplansky invariants always possesses the substation property (cf.
[
7
]
,
[
9
, Problem 58]
, or
[
6
]
). Since
A
also possesses finite all Ulm-Kaplansky invariants, it has the substation property, so that
G=C\oplus B=C\oplus K
for some
C\leq G
. In particular, it must be that
B\cong K
, and hence
we freely write
G=A\oplus U=C\oplus U
for some
U\leq G
, as expected.
∎

We terminate this section with the following statement.

Proposition 24
.

If
B\oplus G=C\oplus H
, where both
B,C
are isomorphic to divisible IC groups, then
G\cong H
.

Proof.

Note that
B,C
are also divisible IC groups, and so all their
p
-primary components and their torsion-free parts must have finite (possibly different) ranks. In fact, if some of these ranks is supposed, for a moment, to be infinite, then there will be a situation when
B\oplus G=C\oplus\{0\}
, where
B,C,G\neq\{0\}
and
B\cong C
- an absurd. This unambiguously means that, if
G
is reduced, then
H
also has to be reduced and, therefore, in this case
G\cong H
; likewise, if
G,H
are non-reduced, then their divisible parts
D(G)
and
D(H)
are isomorphic, so that if we write
G=D(G)\oplus R(G)
and
H=D(H)\oplus R(H)
, then
R(G)
and
R(H)
are isomorphic to the reduced part of
B\oplus G
. Thus,
R(G)\cong R(H)
and, consequently,
G\cong H
, as asked for.
∎

3.
Abelian groups satisfying transitive perspectivity

We begin our work here by recalling some crucial facts as follows: First, we recall that a group satisfies the
weak perspectivity
property, or just saying that it is
weakly perspective
, that is
WP
for short, provided all of its isomorphic direct summands with isomorphic complements are perspective (see
[
2
]
). Moreover, a group
M
is called
purely infinite
, provided
M\cong M\oplus M
.

The following helpful observations are fulfilled.

Remark 25
.

(1) A group is perspective if, and only if, it is simultaneously WP and IC (
[
2
, Remark 8]
);
we emphasize that
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
is IC but not WP.

(2) If
M
is a non-zero purely infinite group, then
M
is not WP (
[
2
, Proposition 9]
), so
M
is a group such that
M\ncong M\oplus M
, and if
\alpha
is an infinite ordinal, then both
M^{(\alpha)}
and
M^{\alpha}
are not WP.

3) A direct summand of a WP group is too WP (see
[
2
, Lemma 6]
).

Note also that, if
M
is a quasi-injective and WP group, then

is perspective (see
[
2
, Theorem 16]
).

In addition, the following is valid.

Remark 26
.

(1) The fully decomposable torsion-free WP group
G
is always perspective. In fact, knowing point (2) of the preceding Remark and Proposition
14
(1), we conclude that
G
is IC, so it has to be perspective with the help of point (1) of the previous Remark.

(2) It can be proven by analogy that each of the bottom groups, which are assumed to be WP groups:

(a) the direct sums of cyclic
p
-groups;

(b) the reduced countable
p
-groups having finite all Ulm-Kaplansky invariants;

(c) the reduced torsion-complete
p
-groups;

(d) the reduced algebraically compact torsion-free groups;

(e) the reduced vector torsion-free groups,

are always perspective groups.

Recall also that a module
M
is called a
CS module
if every submodule of
M
is essential in a direct summand of
M
. Also, a module
M
is called a

if, for any module

, the conditions

with

imply

. And, a module is called

We are now managing to prove the following reduction criterion.

Proposition 27
.

Let
M
be a quasi-continuous group. Then,
M
is TP if, and only if, all
p
-primary components and their torsion free parts have finite uniform dimension (i.e., finite Goldie dimension).

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
: Suppose
M
is TP. In conjunction with
[
12
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
u-moduletp
, Proposition 2.22]
,

is weakly perspective. Thus, all
p
-primary components of

are weakly perspective. Likewise, their torsion free parts are weakly perspective (note that their torsion free parts are actually direct summands) and, clearly, they are Dedekind finite. Therefore, all
p
-primary components and their torsion free parts have finite uniform dimension (i.e., finite Goldie dimension), as formulated.

(\Leftarrow)
: Suppose that
M
is a torsion module with
M=K\oplus K^{\prime}=L\oplus L^{\prime}
and
K\cong L
,
K^{\prime}\cong L^{\prime}
.
So,
M=\bigoplus M_{p}
, where
M_{p}
means the
p
-primary component of
M
for some prime number
p
. Now, we have
K=\bigoplus K_{p}
,
L=\bigoplus L_{p}
,
K^{\prime}=\bigoplus K^{\prime}_{p}
and
L^{\prime}=\bigoplus L^{\prime}_{p}
. Consequently,

M_{p}=K_{p}\oplus K^{\prime}_{p}=L_{p}\oplus L^{\prime}_{p}

with
K_{p}\cong L_{p}
and
K^{\prime}_{p}\cong L^{\prime}_{p}
. However, bearing in mind,

[

, Proposition 2.27]
, one has that

for some

. Setting

it must be that

. If we assume for a moment that

is not torsion, then

, where
M_{F}=\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{Q}
and
M_{T}
is the torsion part of

. But, since

, and

and

are weakly perspective, taking into account

,

has to be weakly perspective. Finally, looking at
[

∎

With this at hand, one can infer the following consequence.

Corollary 28
.

[
2
, Corollary 18]

Let
M
be a divisible group. Then,

is a weakly perspective module if, and only if, all
p
-primary components and their torsion free parts have finite uniform dimension (i.e., they have finite Goldie dimension).

It was observed after Proposition

Fact 1:
(Folklore due to Bass) A ring

Fact 2:
In divisible groups, if a group is
not

Fact 3:
In divisible groups, perspective groups and TP groups do coincide by

We now arrive at the following two constructions.

Example 29
.

Let

Example 30
.

Let

On the other hand, let

be the infinite direct sum copies of

We also remember the following.

Fact 4:
(

) If

As another, alternative, example, what happens with the

We are now prepared to establish some further critical properties of TP groups.

Lemma 31
.

A direct summand of a TP group is again a TP group.

Proof.

Suppose
A
is a direct summand of a TP group
G
. One routinely sees that, if
K
and

are direct summands of

A
and

, then one may write
A=K\oplus(A\cap W)=V\oplus(A\cap W)
, as required.
∎

Our established so far machinery allows us to obtain the following assertion.

Corollary 32
.

Each homogeneous TP
p
-group is perspective (i.e., it has finite rank).

Proof.

The claim is true by combining Lemma
36
situated below with the fact that
G
is fully invariant and essential in the divisible hull

, so the required properties of the direct summands of
G
and

are the same.
∎

The big question, which seems to be extremely difficult, is to find if possible a reduced TP group that is
not

below for the divisible case).

Recall that a divisible group

is perspective if, and only if, the torsion-free rank

is finite and each

-primary component of

r_{p}(D)
; Proposition

teaches us that this is equivalent to the requirement that

is IC.

The following claim sheds a further light on the properties of divisible perspective groups.

Proposition 33
.

Every divisible perspective group possesses the substitution property in the class of groups in which the divisible part is perspective.

Proof.

Suppose
G
is such a group, and write
N=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
, where
A\cong G\cong A^{\prime}
.

If, for a moment,
B
is reduced, then since both
r_{0}(A)
,
r_{p}(A)
are finite and
A\cong A^{\prime}
, then
B^{\prime}
also is reduced, so
A=A^{\prime}
.

Assume now that
B
is not reduced, and write
B=D(B)\oplus B_{1}
,
B^{\prime}=D(B^{\prime})\oplus B^{\prime}_{1}
, where
B_{1}
,
B_{1}^{\prime}
are respectively the reduced parts of
B
,
B^{\prime}
. Thus, it must be that

N=(A\oplus D(B))\oplus B_{1}=(A^{\prime}\oplus D(B^{\prime}))\oplus B_{1}^{\prime},

putting
D:=A\oplus D(B)=A^{\prime}\oplus D(B^{\prime})
. Since both pairs
r_{0}(D(B))
,
r_{p}(D(B))
and
r_{0}(D(B^{\prime}))
,
r_{p}(D(B^{\prime}))
are all finite as well as
r_{0}(A)=r_{0}(A^{\prime})
,
r_{p}(A)=r_{p}(A^{\prime})

are too finite for all prime
p
, one extracts that
D(B)\cong D(B^{\prime})
. By assumption, the group
D
is perspective, leading to

D=U\oplus D(B)=U\oplus D(B^{\prime}).

Finally,

N=U\oplus(D(B)\oplus B_{1})=U\oplus(D(B^{\prime})\oplus B_{1}^{\prime}),

as required.
∎

It is worthwhile noticing that, we established after Lemma
36
situated below that, all the ranks
r_{0}(G)
and
r_{p}(G)
of the group

from the last proposition can

We now need the following technicality.

Lemma 34
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 9.5]
)
Let
A=B\oplus C
be a direct composition with corresponding projections
\pi
,
\theta
. If for the direct decomposition

the corresponding projections are, respectively,
\pi_{1}
and
\theta_{1}
, then

\pi_{1}=\pi+\pi\varphi\theta,\ \theta_{1}=\theta-\pi\varphi\theta

for some endomorphism
\varphi
of the subgroup

.

Conversely, if the endomorphisms
\pi_{1}
and
\theta_{1}
have the above form, then
A=B\oplus\theta_{1}A
.

For the reader’s convenience, we remember that if
A\leq G
, then the

of

is just the rank
r(G/A)
.

We now have at our disposal all the ingredients necessary to prove the following statement on perspectivity of divisible torsion-free groups.

Proposition 35
.

Let
G
be a divisible torsion-free group. If
A
and
A^{\prime}
are its two direct summands with equal finite ranks or co-ranks, then
A\overset{P}{\sim}A^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
G=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
. If
G
possesses finite rank, then
G
is perspective and the assertion is proven. Therefore, we further can consider the case where
G
possesses infinite rank.

First, we consider the case of finite equal co-ranks.

By assumption
r(B)=r(B^{\prime})=n\in\mathbb{N}
. So,
B\cong B^{\prime}
. Assuming that
B\cap A^{\prime}=\{0\}
, we finds
D:=A^{\prime}\oplus B

is a direct summand of
G
such that the co-ranks of
A^{\prime}
in
D
and in
G
are equal, whence
D=G
and
G=A^{\prime}\oplus B=A\oplus B
.

Assume now that
B_{1}:=B\cap A^{\prime}\neq\{0\}
and
B^{\prime}\cap A\neq\{0\}
. We, thereby, have
B=B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
for some

B_{2}\leq B
. Likewise, if
A_{1}:=A\cap A^{\prime}
, then
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
,
A^{\prime}=A_{1}\oplus A_{3}
for some
A_{2}\leq A
,
A_{3}\leq A^{\prime}
. Note that
r(A_{2})
is finite; in fact, if
a\in A_{2}
, then
a=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
for some
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
,
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, where

b^{\prime}\neq 0
because
A_{2}\cap A^{\prime}=\{0\}
, which means that
r(A_{2})\leq r(B^{\prime})=n
.
Furthermore, we write
G=(A_{1}\oplus B_{1})\oplus(A_{2}\oplus B_{2})
and
A^{\prime}=(A_{1}\oplus B_{1})\oplus A_{1}^{\prime}
, where

A_{1}^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\cap(A_{2}\oplus B_{2})
with
A^{\prime}\cap A_{2}=A^{\prime}\cap B_{2}=\{0\}
. By what we have shown so far,
A_{1}\oplus B_{1}

has finite co-rank. Moreover, if
r(B_{2})=k
, then
r(B_{1})=n-k
.

Letting
\pi:G\to A_{2}
be a projection, if
A_{2}^{\prime}=\pi(A_{1}^{\prime})
, then
A_{2}=A_{2}^{\prime}\oplus A_{2}^{\prime\prime}
for some
A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\leq A_{2}
.
Thus,
A_{1}\oplus B_{1}\oplus A_{2}^{\prime}\cong A^{\prime}
as
A_{1}^{\prime}\cong A_{2}^{\prime}
. But, because the co-rank of
A_{1}\oplus B_{1}
is finite and
r(A_{2}^{\prime})
is also finite, then we detect that
r(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus B_{2})=n
. Hence,
r(A_{2}^{\prime\prime})=n-k
and
A_{1}^{\prime}\leq A_{2}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2}
,

G=(A_{1}\oplus B_{1})\oplus(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus(A_{2}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2})).

Since
r(A_{2}^{\prime\prime})=n-k
, there exists an injection
\varphi:B_{1}\to A_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. In conjunction with Lemma
34
, one writes that
B\oplus A_{2}^{\prime\prime}=B_{1}^{\prime}\oplus A_{2}^{\prime\prime}
,

G=(A_{1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{1})\oplus(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus(A_{2}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2}))=A\oplus(B_{1}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2}),

where
A=A_{1}\oplus A^{\prime\prime}_{2}\oplus A_{2}^{\prime}
.

Notice that
A^{\prime}\cap(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})=\{0\}
. Indeed, to substantiate this equality, one observes that every element

x\in B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
has the form
x=b_{1}+\varphi(b_{1})+b_{2}
for some
b_{1}\in B_{1}
,
b_{2}\in B_{2}
. If
x\in A^{\prime}
, then

\varphi(b_{1})+b_{2}\in A^{\prime}\cap(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus B_{2})=A_{1}^{\prime}\cap(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus B_{2}).

Besides, every element
y\in A_{1}^{\prime}
has the form
y=a_{2}^{\prime}+b_{2}
for some
a_{2}^{\prime}\in A_{2}^{\prime}
,
b_{2}\in B_{2}
. So, if
y\in A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus B_{2}
, then
y=a_{2}^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}^{\prime}
for some
a_{2}^{\prime\prime}\in A_{2}^{\prime\prime}
,
b_{2}^{\prime}\in B_{2}
whence
a_{1}^{\prime}+b_{2}=a_{2}^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}^{\prime}
and

a_{2}^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime\prime}=b_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}\in(A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\oplus A_{1}^{\prime})\cap B=\{0\}.

Consequently,
b_{2}^{\prime}=b_{2}
,
a_{2}^{\prime}=a_{2}^{\prime\prime}\in A_{2}^{\prime\prime}\cap A_{2}^{\prime}=\{0\}
, i.e.,
a_{2}^{\prime}=a_{2}^{\prime\prime}=0
. Then,
y=b_{2}\in A_{1}^{\prime}\cap B_{2}=\{0\}
and, finally,
y=0
. So,
A^{\prime}\cap(B_{1}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2})=\{0\}
as wanted and, in accordance to what we already proven above, it must be that
G=A^{\prime}\oplus(B_{1}^{\prime}\oplus B_{2})
.

We are now considering the case of finiteness of the equal ranks of
A
and
A^{\prime}
. However, since the rank of
G
is infinite, we may write that
B_{1}:=B\cap B^{\prime}\neq\{0\}
,
B=B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
,
B^{\prime}=B_{1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{2}
for some
B_{2}\leq B
,

B_{2}^{\prime}\leq B^{\prime}
. We also have

G=(A\oplus B_{2})\oplus B_{1}=(A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}_{2})\oplus B_{1}.

If
0\neq x\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, then
x=a+b_{1}+b_{2}
for some
a\in A
,

b_{1}\in B_{1}
and
b_{2}\in B_{2}
. Here
a\neq 0
, because
B_{2}^{\prime}\cap B=\{0\}
. Thus, if
\alpha:G\to A
is a projection, then
\alpha\upharpoonright B_{2}^{\prime}\to A
is an injection. Therefore,
r(B_{2}^{\prime})\leq n=r(A)
. Similarly,
r(B_{2})\leq n
.
Consequently, the subgroup
D:=(A^{\prime}\oplus B_{2}^{\prime})+(A\oplus B_{2})
must have finite rank.

Further,
D=(A^{\prime}\oplus B_{2}^{\prime})\oplus B_{1}^{\prime}
, where
B_{1}^{\prime}=D\cap B_{1}
. Analogously,
D=(A\oplus B_{2})\oplus B_{1}^{\prime}
.
Since we know that the group
D
is perspective, then
D=A^{\prime}\oplus U=A\oplus U
for some
U\leq D
. And since

G=D\oplus W
for some
W\leq G
, then

G=A^{\prime}\oplus(U\oplus W)=A\oplus(U\oplus W),

as desired.
∎

The last proposition manifestly demonstrates that all divisible torsion-free groups
G
have the weak resemblance to the standard TP property in the sense that if
G=A\oplus B=A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
, where the co-ranks (resp., the ranks) of
A
and
A^{\prime}
are finite and
A\overset{P}{\sim}A^{\prime}
, then
B\overset{P}{\sim}B^{\prime}
.

4.
Some relationships between IC and TP properties

We, foremost, recollect the following well-known terminology: a module
M
is called
purely infinite
if
M\cong M\oplus M
. A module is called
Dedekind finite
(or, in other terms,
directly finite
) if it is
not
isomorphic to any proper direct summand (see
[
14
]
). Note that, referring to
[
11
, p.13]
, a ring having transitive perspectivity need
not
to be Dedekind finite.

Nevertheless, the following result illustrates that in the class of divisible groups, TP implies IC.

Lemma 36
.

Let
G
is a divisible group. If
G
is TP, then it is perspective and so IC.

Proof.

Appealing to
[
12
, Theorem 3.5]
,
G
is perspective. Moreover, owing to
[
5
, Propositions 3.7, 3.13]
, one notices that the torsion-free rank as well as each
p
-rank of
G
are finite, and thus we are done.

∎

So, we would like to find now an example of a group that is TP but
not
IC; notice that a CP group is
always
IC (see point (4) of the discussion alluded to above) and the reciprocal is
not
true in virtue of Note
22
quoted above.

However, the class of groups with the substitution property is
not
closed under formation of
infinite
direct sums. In fact, if
A
is a module such that
A\not\cong A\oplus A
and is an infinite ordinal, then
A^{(\alpha)}
does
not
have the substitution property: indeed, one obviously sees that

N=A^{(\alpha)}\oplus\{0\}=A^{(\alpha-1)}\oplus A

and

A^{(\alpha)}\cong A^{(\alpha-1)}

but

C\oplus\{0\}\neq C\oplus A

for all
C\leq N
. Same holds for
A^{\alpha}
if
\alpha
is infinite.

5.
Perspectively finite modules and groups

A module
M
is called
perspectively finite
(or just
PF
for short) if
M
is not perspectively related to a proper non-zero summand of itself; i.e., for any proper non-zero direct summand
D
of
M
, there is no
submodule
E
of
M
such that
M\oplus E\cong D\oplus E
. Two further cases are possible:

(i) If
E
is a direct summand of
M
.

(ii)
D\oplus E
is a (proper) direct summand of
M
(in fact, which differs
M
). In this case, DF modules are always PF, because
M
will have an isomorphic proper direct summand.

, and

Clinical impact of multimodal cardiac imaging in Kawasaki disease: a prospective Kawasaki disease cardiac imaging (KDCI) cohort study with follow-up data in a Chinese population
Authors: Yaoji Zhu, Zhongqin Zhou, Lei Hu, Shiganmo Azhe, Xinrui Deng, Shengkun Peng, Ying-kun Guo, Chuan Wang, Yue Ma, Ling-yi Wen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeCoronary artery involvement remains the primary focus in the long-term management of Kawasaki disease (KD). However, previous studies suggest that myocardial abnormalities frequently persist beyond coronary artery involvement in KD patients. Yet, their temporal evolution and clinical implications remain poorly characterised. To address this gap, we established the Kawasaki disease cardiac imaging (KDCI) cohort, integrating cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) with echocardiography, coronary CT angiography (CCTA) and invasive angiography. These multimodal imaging approaches enable comprehensive assessment of cardiac abnormalities and elucidate the role of cardiac imaging in optimising long-term KD management.ParticipantsThe KDCI cohort is a prospective study aiming to enrol 400–500 KD patients diagnosed at West China Second University Hospital from September 2018 to September 2035. To date, 207 participants have been recruited. Participants will perform the multimodal cardiac imaging including echocardiography, CMR, CCTA, invasive angiography and comprehensive laboratory testing under a scheduled protocol in the follow-up.Findings to dateThe KDCI cohort has established baseline characteristics for 207 KD patients. Of those included to date, 72.0% (149/207) received intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) treatment, with 26.1% (54/207) demonstrating IVIG resistance, and 37.7% (78/207) exhibiting coronary artery dilatation. Longitudinal follow-up data are available for 80.7% (167/207) of participants, with a median follow-up duration of 2.7 years and a follow-up patient-years of 594 patient-years. Of the 207 patients, 16.9% (35/207) patients experienced endpoint events, encompassing coronary artery thrombosis (8.2%, 17/207), coronary stenosis/obstruction (5.3% 11/207) and clinical myocardial infarction (1.9%, 4/207). Based on the data collected, we have demonstrated the cardiac abnormalities beyond coronary artery involvement in KD by CMR and CCTA.Future planThe KDCI cohort will maintain ongoing recruitment and longitudinal follow-up, with a projected enrolment exceeding 400 participants by 2035. This expansion will yield a median follow-up duration of 10 years, providing robust long-term outcome data. We have implemented standardised protocols for scheduled follow-up assessments and data collection in newly enrolled patients. Furthermore, planned genomic analyses will be incorporated to investigate the molecular pathogenesis and prognostic determinants of KD.

Introduction

Kawasaki disease (KD) is an acute febrile illness characterised by systemic vasculitis, predominantly affecting children under 5 years old.1 The global incidence of KD ranges from approximately 20 to 300 cases per 100 000 individuals, with notably higher rates observed in Northeast Asia and has become the leading cause of acquired heart disease in children in developed countries.25 Although KD is typically self-limiting, 25% of untreated patients develop coronary artery complications, such as dilation and aneurysm formation.68 Current clinical guidelines and practice predominantly use Z-scores of coronary artery aneurysms (CAAs) to guide long-term management, aiming to prevent adverse cardiovascular events, such as myocardial infarction (MI) and thrombosis.1 2 6 However, beyond coronary artery involvement, extensive pathological and clinical studies demonstrated myocardial abnormalities including inflammation, fibrosis and ischaemia, which are consistently observed in KD patients.912 Nevertheless, the progression of these myocardial abnormalities and their clinical implications remains poorly defined. This research gap was also specifically mentioned by the 2024 American Heart Association (AHA) scientific statement on KD, particularly regarding the optimal timing and surveillance imaging for risk stratification and long-term management.2

Echocardiography serves as the primary imaging modality in KD, offering a non-invasive, rapid assessment of proximal coronary artery abnormalities. While coronary CT angiography (CCTA) provides a detailed luminal evaluation of the entire coronary artery. Invasive angiography remains the gold standard for delineating coronary architecture.21315 However, these modalities have limited capacity for myocardial tissue characterisation. Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) allows a ‘one-stop’ assessment, including visualisation of CA, ventricular function and tissue characteristics. Preliminary evidence supports its utility in detecting abnormalities in ventricular function, myocardial perfusion and myocardial tissue in KD.1620 Nevertheless, existing studies are predominantly cross-sectional and constrained by a limited sample, and few studies focus on comprehensively characterising cardiac abnormalities in KD with the multimodal cardiac imaging.

Therefore, we incorporated CMR alongside echocardiography, CTA and invasive angiography to establish the Kawasaki disease cardiac imaging (KDCI) cohort. The objectives of the cohort are: (1) To systematically assess the efficacy of cardiac imaging for detecting and characterising cardiac abnormalities in KD, (2) To explore the correlations between cardiac abnormalities, clinical data and biomarkers and (3) To delineate the prognostic significance of cardiac imaging and provide evidence-driven recommendations for optimal cardiac imaging and frequency for KD long-term management.

Cohort description

Study design

This single-centre observational study, conducted at West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University, aims to enrol KD patients from September 2018 to September 2035. The estimated final sample size is 400–500 participants, with 207 currently enrolled. Eligible participants meet the inclusion/exclusion criteria (figure 1). The inclusion criteria are: (1) Diagnosis of KD or incomplete KD according to the AHA guidelines and1 (2) Age <18 years. Exclusion criteria include: (1) Congenital heart disease or cardiomyopathy and (2) Contraindications to CMR. Informed consent was obtained from participants and their guardians.

The study design. CMR, cardiovascular magnetic resonance; CCTA, coronary CT angiography; CA, coronary artery aneurysm.

Participants were stratified into small, medium and large aneurysm groups based on the maximum diameter of coronary artery dilatation or the corresponding Z-score during the acute phase. Comprehensive clinical data, including baseline characteristics, laboratory results and cardiac imaging (echocardiography, CMR, CCTA and invasive angiography), were systematically collected.

Patients included in the KDCI cohort will undergo regular follow-up until the age of 18 years. While all patients underwent echocardiography and CMR, CCTA and invasive angiography were selectively performed for those with medium or large aneurysms or suspected myocardial ischaemia. Endpoint events were defined as follows: (1) Coronary artery stenosis or obstruction, (2) Thrombotic events, (3) Percutaneous coronary intervention or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), (4) Other coronary artery surgeries and (5) major adverse cardiac events (MACE), encompassing a composite of cardiac death, MI, malignant arrhythmias and heart transplantation. The follow-up protocol was tailored according to the maximum diameter of coronary artery lesions or the corresponding Z-score during the acute phase, as detailed in table 1. This follow-up protocol was formulated through a multidisciplinary evaluation integrating the AHA guidelines for long-term KD management, combined with existing literature evidence, the data on cardiovascular event incidence in our centre, diagnostic performance of imaging modalities for cardiovascular events and financial burden.

Baseline data collection

Baseline data were collected by consultation and physical examination on hospital admission, including gender, age, height, weight, symptoms and time of disease onset.

Laboratory testing

For the patients included in the KDCI cohort, the baseline of laboratory tests was collected during hospitalisation. Laboratory tests include complete blood count, C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, liver and kidney function tests, blood glucose, blood lipids, electrolytes, myocardial injury markers, B-type natriuretic peptide and coagulation markers.

Cardiac imaging

All patients enrolled in the group underwent a 3.0 T CMR. The CMR protocol included cine, native and postcontrast T1 mapping, T2 mapping, stress and rest first-pass perfusion, late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) and coronary magnetic resonance angiography. Quantitative assessments include cardiac structural dimensions (eg, chamber diameters and wall thickness), functional parameters (eg, ejection fraction and strain analysis), T1/T2 mapping values, extracellular volume fraction and qualitative evaluations of LGE and myocardial perfusion patterns.

Echocardiography was performed for all participants. Key measurements include coronary aneurysm presence and morphology (eg, maximum diameter, shape and segmental distribution), left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction, fractional shortening, cardiac chamber dimensions and valvular involvement. All echocardiographic findings will be independently reviewed by paediatric cardiologists.

CCTA was indicated for patients with medium to large aneurysms (defined as a coronary diameter >4 mm or, in patients aged ≥5 years, a dilated segment exceeding 1.5 times the adjacent normal segment). CCTA analyses focus on luminal dimensions (with Z-score calculations), vessel wall characteristics (eg, calcification, thickness), stenosis severity, intravascular thrombus presence, pericoronary adipose tissue (PCAT) attenuation, pericardial effusion and cardiomegaly.

Invasive angiography was performed in patients with large coronary aneurysms or inducible myocardial ischaemia. Analyses include coronary flow reserve fraction, aneurysm morphology, luminal diameter, degree of stenosis or occlusion and collateral circulation status.

Regular longitudinal follow-up with multimodal cardiac imaging enables dynamic monitoring of cardiac abnormalities progression in KD and provides evidence-based strategies for long-term management.

Plans for data analysis

The data analysis focuses on: (1) Characterising the natural history of cardiac abnormalities in KD patients through longitudinal follow-up; the association between imaging features and clinical data and (2) Evaluating the efficacy of multimodal cardiac imaging in assessing cardiac abnormalities, with emphasis on its role in prognosis prediction and risk stratification.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of our research. However, the main study findings will be shared with the parents and guardians of participants.

Findings to date

This paper presents descriptive findings based on the baseline data from our ongoing prospective cohort study, including participants’ baseline characteristics, data collection details and preliminary observations, to characterise the cohort and outline the available research resource.

So far, 207 patients have been included in the cohort, and the distribution of enrolment is shown in figure 2. Among 207 participants with a history of KD, the average age was 4.02±2.56 years old, with 67.6% being male, and the average body surface area was 0.73±0.24 m² (table 2). Regarding clinical characteristics, the most prevalent symptom was bilateral bulbar conjunctival injection without exudate. As expected, erythema and oedema of the hands and feet, as well as periungual desquamation, were the least common symptoms. 72.0% patients (149/207) received IVIG treatment, and 26.1% (54/207) showed IVIG resistance. Additionally, coronary dilatation was observed in 37.7% (78/207) of patients (table 3). 80.7% (167/207) of patients have been followed up more than once. The median duration of follow-up was 2.7 years, and the follow-up patient-years was 594. 16.9% (35/207) patients met endpoint events, 8.2% (17/207) had coronary artery thrombosis, 5.3% (11/207) had coronary stenosis/obstruction, 1.9% (4/207) had clinical MI, 0.5% (1/207) had cardiogenic death, 0.5% (1/207) had CABG. The current incidence of endpoint events is 59/1000 person-years.

The observed incidence of coronary artery dilation was 37.7% (78/207), higher than the previously reported rate of 19.8% in Chinese populations.21 This discrepancy may be attributed to delays in diagnosis and treatment initiation, as a significant proportion of patients were referred from external institutions. Similarly, the incidence of endpoint events was higher than the rate of 13% in previously reported cases due to the severe disease conditions.22 To date, findings from the KDCI cohort demonstrated that CMR enables sensitive and precise assessment of cardiac abnormalities in KD patients. These included myocardial oedema, subclinical LV dysfunction and the severity of myocardial perfusion impairment, which correlated with the degree of coronary artery dilation.2325 Furthermore, preliminary data suggested that elevated PCAT CT attenuation was associated with CAAs and reduced myocardial perfusion, positioning it as a potential independent predictor of adverse cardiovascular events in this population.

Strengths and limitations

The KDCI cohort provides longitudinal cardiac imaging and clinical data, offering valuable insights into the progression of cardiac abnormalities in KD and their association with clinical outcomes. Given the higher incidence of KD in Asia, we have the potential to expand the sample size that meets the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Patient recruitment is relatively efficient. Additionally, as a tertiary referral centre, West China Second University Hospital manages a broad spectrum of disease severity and has access to multimodal cardiac imaging, ensuring data reliability. Leveraging our previous research, this study is well-positioned to provide evidence-based guidance for the optimal frequency of cardiac imaging and its long-term value in assessing cardiac abnormalities in KD patients.

However, this study has certain limitations. As a single-centre observational study, there is a potential for selection bias due to the regional demographic characteristics of the participants. Nevertheless, the consistency of implementation personnel and standardised imaging protocols enhances data quality and reliability. Despite this limitation, the findings remain valuable and may serve as a reference for other populations.

Future plans

Moving forward, the KDCI cohort will continue to enrol additional participants diagnosed with KD and collect data from the baseline and examination. The projected sample size will exceed 400 by 2035, providing robust data for long-term follow-up analyses. For newly enrolled patients, scheduled follow-up and data collection will be conducted as planned. Over the next decade, genetic data will also be integrated into the cohort, enabling the identification of genetic factors associated with KD susceptibility, cardiovascular complications and IVIG resistance. The incorporation of genetic profiling with cardiac imaging findings will advance radiogenomics research, improving early diagnosis, risk stratification and prognosis prediction.

Waveguide arrays interaction to second neighbors: Exact solution
M. A. Tapia-Valerdi, I. Ramos-Prieto, F. Soto-Eguibar, H. M. Moya-Cessa

Abstract
We provide an analytical framework for describing the propagation of light in waveguide arrays, considering both infinite and semi-infinite cases. The interaction up to second neighbors is taken into account, which makes for a more realistic setup. We show that these solutions follow a distinctive structural pattern. This pattern reflects a transition from conventional Bessel functions to the lesser-known one-parameter generalized Bessel functions, offering new insights into the propagation dynamics in these systems.

Introduction

Discreteness is a fundamental concept prevalent in many areas of modern physics, such as quantum mechanics. However, the experimental verification of theoretical predictions regarding discrete systems is often challenging. Consequently, systems that exhibit analogous structures in both their physical behavior and mathematical formalism are needed. Arrays of evanescently coupled waveguides serve as a prominent example of such systems

The development of new schemes to extend the concept of waveguides in other settings is of significant importance. In many of these structures, only couplings to the nearest-neighboring waveguides are considered
[

,

,
19
]
, while interactions with more distant waveguides are often neglected due to the assumption that the coupling strength decays exponentially with distance, rendering second-order couplings negligible. However, under certain conditions, the influence of a second interaction becomes remarkably significant. For example, in quantum computation and quantum information processing using optical waveguides, it is crucial to fabricate compact waveguide circuits to minimize their footprints
[
20
]
. As the separation between waveguides in these circuits decreases or as the waveguide length increases, higher-order couplings must be considered. The two-dimensional zigzag waveguide lattice, which is topologically equivalent to a one-dimensional waveguide lattice, has been used to study second-order couplings

. Moreover, the role of boundaries and surfaces introduce additional complexities to wave dynamics by breaking translational symmetry, significantly altering the interaction landscape that is not present in infinite arrays

The article is organized as follows. In Section

Using the generating function for one-parameter generalized Bessel functions, we derive the optical field, revealing a correlation between the solutions for the semi-infinite and infinite cases, as well as for first- and second-neighbor interactions. In Section

VI

II
Interaction to first neighbor’s: Case in which the operators are infinite on both sides

In coupled mode theory, the propagation of an optical field through a waveguide array with nearest-neighbor evanescent coupling without frontiers is governed by the following set of coupled first order ordinary differential equations

i\frac{dE_{j}(z)}{dz}=g_{1}(E_{j-1}+E_{j+1}),\qquad j=-\infty,\dots,\infty,

(1)

with
z
the propagation distance,
g_{1}
the coupling constant and
j
runs from
-\infty
to
\infty
through all the integers.

This framework can be viewed also as the problem of solving the Schrödinger-like equation with Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=g_{1}\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right),

(2)

where the operators
\hat{V}_{\infty}
and
\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}
are the infinite counterparts of the London-Susskind–Glogower operators
[
31
,
32
]
, defined as

\hat{V}_{\infty}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{n}\bra{n+1},\quad\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{n+1}\bra{n};

(3)

the states
\ket{n},\;n\in\mathbb{Z}
, are generalized Fock states, and it is possible to show that
\left[\hat{V}_{\infty},\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right]=0
as well as
\hat{V}_{\infty}\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}=\hat{I}
.

If we denote the solution of the Schrödinger-type equation obtained from the Hamiltonian (
2
) as
\ket{\psi(z)}
, it is easy to demonstrate that the solutions of system (
1
) are given by
E_{j}(z)=\braket{j|\psi(z)},\;j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Using operational methods, we proceed to solve the Schrödinger-like equation obtained from the Hamiltonian (
2
), and subsequently derive the solution for the infinite system in (
1
). Given the initial condition
\ket{\psi(0)}=\ket{n_{0}}
, the formal solution of the Schrödinger-like equation can be written as
\ket{\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=\exp\left[-ig_{1}z\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right)\right]\ket{n_{0}}
; to calculate this expression, we use that
\hat{V}_{\infty}\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}=\hat{I}
implies
\hat{V}_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}}
; hence, we can write

\ket{\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=\exp\left[-ig_{1}z\left(i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}}\right)\right]\ket{n_{0}}.

(4)

Since the generating function for the Bessel functions of the first kind
[
33
]
is apparent in the preceding equation, we can reformulate the solution to the Schrödinger-like equation as

\ket{\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z)i^{n}\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger n}\ket{n_{0}}.

(5)

Using that
\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger n}\ket{n_{0}}=\ket{n_{0}+n}
, we get

\ket{\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z)i^{n}\ket{n_{0}+n},

(6)

and in order to find the amplitudes
E_{j}
that are the solutions of the system (
1
), we simply multiply by the bra
\bra{j}
and obtain

E_{n_{0},j}(z)=\braket{j|\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=i^{j-n_{0}}J_{j-n_{0}}(-2g_{1}z),\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(7)

III
Interaction to first neighbor’s: Case semi-infinite

In the case of propagation of an optical field through a waveguide array with nearest-neighbor evanescent coupling with frontiers the set of coupled differential equations which rules the behavior is

\displaystyle i\frac{dE_{0}(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=g_{1}E_{1},

\displaystyle i\frac{dE_{j}(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=g_{1}(E_{j-1}+E_{j+1}),\quad j=1,2,3,\dots,

(8)

being
g_{1}
again the coupling constant.

As in the previous case, this system of equations is equivalent to the Schrödinger-like equation
i\frac{d\ket{\psi(z)}}{dz}=\hat{H}\ket{\psi(z)}
considering the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=g_{1}\left(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger}\right)
, where
\hat{V}
and
\hat{V}^{\dagger}
are the known London-Susskind-Glogower operators
[
31
,
32
]
and
\ket{\psi(z)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E_{j}(z)\ket{j}
. The evolution operator for our Hamiltonian can then be expressed as
\hat{U}(z)=\exp[-ig_{1}z(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})]
; by employing operational methods
[
34
]
, it is possible to show that it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{U}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n}(-2g_{1}z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+2}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}.

(9)

In order to demonstrate the method that will later be used to verify the correctness of our ansatz for second-neighbor interactions in the semi-infinite case, we will first prove the validity of Eq. (
9
). We start by differentiating both sides of the equation with respect to
z
, then group terms and separate one of the sums over
m
. On the one hand, this leads to the expression

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{U}(z)}{dz}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}g_{1}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n+1}(-2g_{1}z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+3}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0,m=1}^{\infty}g_{1}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n-1}(-2g_{1}z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+1}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n},

(10)

where we have used the Bessel functions property
J_{-n}(z)=(-1)^{n}J_{n}(z),n\in\mathbb{Z}
that implies
i^{-n}J_{-n-1}(-2g_{1}z)+i^{n}J_{n+1}(-2g_{1}z)=0
, which we used to simplify the result.

Additionally, based on Eq. (
9
) and keeping in mind that
\hat{U}(z)=\exp[-ig_{1}z(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})]
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{U}(z)}{dz}=

\displaystyle-ig_{1}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})\sum_{n=0,k=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger k}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n}.

(11)

It is possible to show from the commutation relations between
V
and
V^{\dagger}
that
[\hat{V},\hat{V}^{\dagger k}]=\hat{V}^{\dagger(k-1)}|0\rangle\langle 0|
.
Performing the product, and using the previous equation, it is easy to see that the derivative can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{U}(z)}{dz}=

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\left\{\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n+1}J_{m-n+1}(-2g_{1}z)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.+i^{n+m+1}J_{m+n+3}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\left\{\sum_{n=0,m=1}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n-1}J_{m-n-1}(-2g_{1}z)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.+i^{n+m-1}J_{m+n+1}(-2g_{1}z)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n}\right\},

(12)

where we have made the index substitutions
m=k-1
and
m=k+1
when needed; as was anticipated, Eqs. (
10
) and (
12
) are exactly equivalent.

It is evident that the optical field at the
j
-th site, when the
n_{0}
-th guide is excited, is given by

\displaystyle E_{n_{0},j}(z)=

\displaystyle i^{j-n_{0}}J_{j-n_{0}}(-2g_{1}z)+i^{j+n_{0}}J_{j+n_{0}+2}(-2g_{1}z),

\displaystyle\quad j=0,1,2,\dots,\infty.

(13)

This result aligns with the work of Makris and Christodoulides
[
35
]
, who employed the method of images to analyze a finite one-dimensional array of
N
waveguides.

In the next sections, we show how to extend the solution for the interaction to the second neighbors. Although the solution to first neighbors is difficult to obtain, we show that it may be generalized by using extensions of the Bessel functions introduced by Dattoli
et al.

[
36
]
.

IV
Interaction to second neighbor’s: Case in which the operators are infinite on both sides

Under coupled mode theory, the dynamics of an optical field in a waveguide array with next-nearest-neighbor evanescent coupling is described by the following set of coupled differential equations

i\frac{dE_{j}(z)}{dz}=g_{1}(E_{j-1}+E_{j+1})+g_{2}(E_{j-2}+E_{j+2}),

(14)

with
j
running through all the integers
\mathbb{Z}
.

As in the previous sections, this system is equivalent to a Schrödinger-like equation with the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=g_{1}\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right)+g_{2}\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}^{2}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}\right)
. The evolution operator corresponding to this Hamiltonian is

\hat{U}(z)=\exp\left\{-iz\left[g_{1}\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right)+g_{2}\left(\hat{V}_{\infty}^{2}+\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}\right)\right]\right\}.

(15)

Using again that
\hat{V}_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}}
, we can cast this evolution operator as

\hat{U}(z)=\exp\left[-g_{1}z\left(i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}}\right)-g_{2}z\left(i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}-\frac{1}{i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}}\right)\right].

(16)

It is convenient to introduce the not so well known one parameter generalized Bessel functions
J_{n}(x,y;s)

[
36
]
defined as

J_{n}(x,y;s)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}s^{k}J_{n-2k}(x)J_{k}(y),

(17)

whose generating function is

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}t^{n}J_{n}(x,y;s)=\exp\left[\frac{x}{2}\left(t-\frac{1}{t}\right)+\frac{y}{2}\left(st^{2}-\frac{1}{st^{2}}\right)\right].

(18)

The two operators in the propagator Eq. (
16
),
i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}}
and
i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}-\frac{1}{i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger 2}}
commute, and we can identify the terms with those of the generating function (
18
); thus, if in the generating function Eq. (
18
) we identify
t\rightarrow i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}
,
x\rightarrow-2g_{1}z
,
y\rightarrow-2g_{2}z
,
s\rightarrow-i
, we obtain

\hat{U}(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(i\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger}\right)^{n}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i).

(19)

Rearranging and commuting,

\hat{U}(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^{n}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger n},

(20)

and we have the solution to our problem.

If we have an initial condition
\ket{\psi(0)}
, we get

\ket{\psi(z)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^{n}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger n}\ket{\psi(0)}.

(21)

Let us specify the initial condition setting
\ket{\psi(0)}=\ket{n_{0}}
, with
n_{0}
an integer; as
\hat{V}_{\infty}^{\dagger n}\ket{n_{0}}=\ket{n_{0}+n}
,

\ket{\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^{n}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\ket{n_{0}+n},

(22)

and so we have

\displaystyle E_{n_{0},j}(z)=

\displaystyle\braket{j|\psi_{n_{0}}(z)}=i^{j-n_{0}}J_{j-n_{0}}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i),

\displaystyle j=

\displaystyle-\infty,\dots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\dots,\infty.

(23)

The structure of this result is similar to that of the infinite case of an optical field propagating through a waveguide array with nearest-neighbor evanescent coupling, Eq. (
7
), except that the Bessel functions are replaced by the one parameter generalized Bessel functions. It is easy to show that for
z=0
the solution (
23
) reduces to the initial condition
E_{n_{0},j}(0)=\delta_{n_{0},j}
, and by direct substitution of the said solution in the system of equations (
14
) it is found that it is indeed the solution.

), with the parameters
g_{1}=1.0
and

; (a) The central guide,

is illuminated, (b) The guides

, we present the intensity distribution in the waveguides as a function of the propagation distance

given by (

). We assume

j=-15
and
j=15
.

V
Interaction to second neighbor’s: Case semi-infinite

In the following, we will analyze the dynamics of an optical field in a waveguide array with next-nearest-neighbor evanescent coupling with boundaries. The system is described by the following coupled differential equations

\displaystyle i\frac{dE_{0}(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=g_{1}E_{1}+g_{2}(E_{2}-E_{0}),

\displaystyle i\frac{dE_{1}(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=g_{1}(E_{0}+E_{2})+g_{2}E_{3},

(24)

\displaystyle i\frac{dE_{j}(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=g_{1}(E_{j-1}+E_{j+1})+g_{2}(E_{j-2}+E_{j+2}),

where
j=2,3,4,\dots
.

Now, we consider the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=g_{1}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})+g_{2}(\hat{V}^{2}+\hat{V}^{\dagger 2}-\ket{0}\bra{0})
, where once again
\hat{V}
and
\hat{V}^{\dagger}
are the usual Susskind–Glogower operators
[
31
,
32
]
, and the evolution operator may be written as

\hat{U}(z)=\exp\{-iz[g_{1}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})+g_{2}(\hat{V}^{2}+\hat{V}^{\dagger 2}-\ket{0}\bra{0})]\}.

(25)

We propose an ansatz for the evolution operator, maintaining the same structure as Eq. (
9
), but replacing the Bessel functions with one-parameter generalized Bessel functions
[
36
]
:

\displaystyle\hat{U}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n}.

(26)

To demonstrate the validity of our ansatz, as done in Section
III
, we differentiate the above expression with respect to
z
. In Appendix
A
, we show that this yields the result

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0,m=0}^{\infty}g_{1}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n+1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+3}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0,m=1}^{\infty}g_{1}\left[i^{m-n}J_{m-n-1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m}J_{m+n+1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0,m=0}^{\infty}g_{2}\left[i^{m-n+1}J_{m-n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m+1}J_{m+n+4}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0,m=2}^{\infty}g_{2}\left[i^{m-n-1}J_{m-n-2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+m-1}J_{m+n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{2}\left[i^{n-1}J_{n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n-1}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}.

(27)

On the other hand, from equations (
25
) and (
26
), we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{U}(z)}{dz}=

\displaystyle-i[g_{1}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\dagger})+g_{2}(\hat{V}^{2}+\hat{V}^{\dagger 2}-\ket{0}\bra{0})]

\displaystyle\sum_{n,k=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger k}|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{V}^{n};

(28)

calculating the product of the operators, recognizing that
\hat{V}^{k}\ket{0}=\ket{k}
, and applying the commutation rules, we obtain

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\sum_{n=0,k=1}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger(k-1)}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\sum_{n=0,k=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger(k+1)}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{2}\sum_{n=0,k=2}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger(k-2)}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{2}\sum_{n=0,k=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{k-n}J_{k-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n+k}J_{k+n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger(k+2)}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle+ig_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{-n}J_{-n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n}J_{n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}.

(29)

Assigning
m=k-1
in the first sum,
m=k+1
in the second,
m=k-2
in the third,
m=k+2
in the fourth, and applying Eq. (
38
), we obtain the final expression

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\sum_{n=0,m=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n+1}J_{m-n+1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{m+n+1}J_{m+n+3}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{1}\sum_{n=0,m=1}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n-1}J_{m-n-1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{m+n-1}J_{m+n+1}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{2}\sum_{n=0,m=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n+2}J_{m-n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{m+n+2}J_{m+n+4}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-ig_{2}\sum_{n=0,m=2}^{\infty}\left[i^{m-n-2}J_{m-n-2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{m+n-2}J_{m+n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\hat{V}^{\dagger m}\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n}

\displaystyle-g_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[i^{n-1}J_{n+2}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+i^{n-1}J_{n}(-2g_{1}z,-2g_{2}z;-i)\right]\ket{0}\bra{0}\hat{V}^{n},

(30)

When we compare Eqs. (
27
) and (
30

VI
Conclusions

The method for deriving a solution for field propagation in optical lattices, accounting for interaction with second neighbors, has been demonstrated. We have successfully extended the solution for the semi-infinite interaction, initially applied to first-neighbor interactions as described in
[
35
]
. This extension is nontrivial, as the London phase operators do not close to an algebra
[
34
]
. The complete solution was achieved by adapting fully infinite case solutions, expressed through generalized Bessel functions
[
36
]
, to the semi-infinite context.

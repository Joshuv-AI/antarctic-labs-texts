Introduction

1.1
Motivation: Extending Information Theory to Stable Laws

Classical information theory finds elegant expression in Gaussian settings. There, Fisher information
J(h)
and the de Bruijn identity precisely quantify entropy dynamics under additive Gaussian noise
[
14
,
1
]
, underpinning numerous applications in science and engineering. This framework’s reach, however, is curtailed when confronting the heavy-tailed phenomena prevalent in diverse fields such as finance, signal processing, and physics. Such phenomena are often modelled effectively by symmetric
\alpha
-stable (S
\alpha
S) distributions with stability index
\alpha\in(0,2)
. For these distributions, the situation changes fundamentally. Their characteristic power-law density decay,
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)\sim c_{\alpha}|x|^{-1-\alpha}
as
|x|\to\infty
(where
c_{\alpha}>0
is a constant depending on
\alpha
), implies infinite variance (for
\alpha<2
) and ensures the integrand defining classical Fisher information,
h(x)(p^{F}_{h}(x))^{2}
, decays too slowly for convergence over
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
J(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x)(p^{F}_{h}(x))^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
diverges whenever
h
is an S
\alpha
S density with
\alpha<2
, rendering this cornerstone measure unusable and highlighting a critical gap in the standard toolkit.

This divergence is not merely technical; it signifies a fundamental limitation of standard information-theoretic tools in non-Gaussian, heavy-tailed regimes, necessitating new conceptual frameworks. A promising direction stems from the fractional de Bruijn identity framework developed by Johnson
[
9
]
specifically for S
\alpha
S target densities (crucially, covering the full range
\alpha\in(0,2]
, including the Gaussian endpoint). Johnson’s work provides structures to analyse entropy dissipation beyond the Gaussian paradigm, using dynamics inherent to stable processes.

Building on this foundation, this paper defines and rigorously analyses Mixed Fractional Information (MFI)
specifically for the family of symmetric
\alpha
-stable distributions
. Within this context, MFI is derived from the initial rate of relative entropy dissipation along canonical S
\alpha
S interpolation paths of the form
X_{t}=(1-t)^{1/\alpha}X_{0}+t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
. We focus particularly on the fundamental case where both the initial distribution
X_{0}
and the target noise
Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
correspond to S
\alpha
S densities (e.g., with scales
v
and
s
, respectively), investigating MFI’s properties and internal consistency
within this setting
. Our analysis establishes MFI as a finite, consistent, and theoretically grounded measure
for comparing pairs of S
\alpha
S distributions
, providing a viable alternative exactly where classical Fisher information diverges for this important class of laws. MFI’s relationship to other generalisations and alternative approaches is detailed in Section
1.4
.

1.2
Symmetric
\alpha
-Stable Distributions

An S
\alpha
S random variable
Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
is characterised by its characteristic function:

\phi_{s}(k)=\mathbb{E}[e^{ikZ_{s}^{(\alpha)}}]=e^{-s|k|^{\alpha}},\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

where
\alpha\in(0,2]
is the stability index and
s>0
is the scale parameter. We denote its probability density function by
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)
. We denote the derivative with respect to
x
as
g_{s}^{(\alpha)\prime}(x)
. Key properties include
[
12
,
17
]
:

•

Stability
: If
Z_{u}^{(\alpha)}\sim g_{u}^{(\alpha)}
and
Z_{v}^{(\alpha)}\sim g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
are independent, then their sum
Z_{u}^{(\alpha)}+Z_{v}^{(\alpha)}\sim g_{u+v}^{(\alpha)}
. This corresponds to the convolution property
g_{u}^{(\alpha)}*g_{v}^{(\alpha)}=g_{u+v}^{(\alpha)}
.

•

Scaling
: For any constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
aZ_{s}^{(\alpha)}\sim g_{|a|^{\alpha}s}^{(\alpha)}
. Explicitly,
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)=v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(v^{-1/\alpha}x)
.

Special cases relevant to this work are:

•

\alpha=2
(Gaussian):
g_{s}^{(2)}(x)=(4\pi s)^{-1/2}e^{-x^{2}/(4s)}
. This corresponds to a Gaussian distribution with mean 0 and variance
2s
.

•

\alpha=1
(Cauchy):
g_{s}^{(1)}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{s}{s^{2}+x^{2}}
.

Symmetry Assumption.

Throughout this paper, we focus exclusively on
symmetric

\alpha
-stable (S
\alpha
S) distributions. Generalisations to asymmetric stable laws are beyond the current scope. See Section
5
for further discussion.

Parameter Range.

We consider
\alpha\in(0,2]
and scale parameters
s,v>0
.

We denote the Fisher score of a density
f
by
p^{F}_{f}=f^{\prime}/f
. For S
\alpha
S densities
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
, we may use the shorthand
p^{F}_{v}=p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}
when the context is clear.

Relative score notation.

For later brevity, when comparing two S
\alpha
S densities

g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
and
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
, we denote their

score difference
by

\delta p^{F}_{v,s}(x)\;:=\;p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x).

1.3
Johnson’s Fractional de Bruijn Identity Framework

Johnson
[
9
]
introduced an interpolation process between an arbitrary initial random variable
X_{0}
with density
h
and an S
\alpha
S variable
Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
:

X_{t}=(1-t)^{1/\alpha}X_{0}+t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)},\quad t\in[0,1).

(2)

The density
h_{t}
of
X_{t}
evolves according to the following fractional Fokker–Planck-type equation
[
9
]
of the form:

\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}(x)=\frac{s}{\alpha(1-t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}[h_{t}(x)u(x,t)],

(3)

Here,
u(x,t)
acts as a score function along the interpolation, encoding the direction of mass transport. It is intimately linked to MMSE estimation, as formalised in
[
9
]
and discussed in Section
4.3
. Johnson derived a fractional de Bruijn identity (FdBR) relating the rate of change of relative entropy
D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\int h_{t}\log(h_{t}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
to an integral functional involving
u(x,t)
and the Fisher scores
p^{F}_{f}(x)=f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
(where
f^{\prime}(x)
denotes differentiation with respect to
x
). Adapting
[
9
, Theorem 5.1, Eq. 5.1]
:

\frac{d}{dt}D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=-\frac{s}{\alpha(1-t)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h_{t}(x)u(x,t)(p^{F}_{h_{t}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

(4)

1.4
Related Work and Positioning

The challenge of defining finite and meaningful information measures for stable laws, where classical Fisher information diverges, has spurred several research directions. This work focuses on Mixed Fractional Information (MFI), derived directly from the entropy dissipation rate in Johnson’s interpolation framework
[
9
]
. This approach is distinct from, yet complementary to, other notable efforts:

•

Fractional Fisher Information (FFI):
Developed by Toscani and others
[
16
]
, FFI typically employs fractional derivatives (e.g., Riesz-Feller) in its definition. Relative versions of FFI (
I_{\lambda}(X|Z_{\lambda})
) have been proposed to remain finite when comparing a general distribution to a stable law, providing tools for studying entropy inequalities and convergence towards stable limits within a calculus-based framework
[
15
,
16
]
. MFI, in contrast, arises from the dynamics of a specific stochastic process (Eq.
2
) and connects entropy change to an MMSE-related score, rather than directly using fractional derivatives in its definition.

•

Generalised Measures and Estimation Bounds:
Motivated by parameter estimation in impulsive (alpha-stable) noise, researchers like Saad and Fayed
[
6
]
have proposed alternative "alpha-power" concepts intended to replace variance and generalised Fisher information analogues. Their goal is often to establish performance bounds (like Cramer-Rao type inequalities) suitable for stable noise environments, focusing more directly on estimation theory applications.

MFI, defined via Eq.
5
, leverages the specific structure of the stable interpolation path and the associated score function
u(x,t)
, which incorporates an MMSE-related term
[
9
]
. This inherent connection to estimation (discussed further in Section
4.3
) and its interpretation as an entropy dissipation rate distinguish MFI from FFI and alpha-power frameworks, suggesting it may offer unique insights into the interplay between information dynamics and estimation in stable systems.

2
Mixed Fractional Information and Score Function

2.1
Definition of MFI and Regularity Conditions

Classical Fisher information diverges for S
\alpha
S distributions with
\alpha<2
. Building on Johnson’s FdBR
[
9
]
, we define MFI via the initial rate of relative entropy dissipation.

Definition 2.1

(Regularity Class
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
)
.

The regularity class
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
consists of probability densities
h
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying sufficient conditions for the MFI framework to be well-posed. Specifically,
h
must ensure that:

•

h(x)>0
almost everywhere,
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\int|h(x)\log h(x)|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

•

The relative entropy
D(h\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\int h\log(h/g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
is finite.

•

The density
h_{t}
of the interpolated variable
X_{t}
(Eq.
2
) exists for
t
in some interval
[0,\epsilon)
.

•

The function
t\mapsto D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
is differentiable at
t=0
.

•

The Fisher score
p^{F}_{h}=h^{\prime}/h
exists (requiring appropriate smoothness of
h
).

•

The score function
u(x,0)
associated with the interpolation starting from
h
exists and is well-behaved.

•

The integral defining the FdBR at
t=0
(Eq.
4
) converges absolutely:
\int h(x)|u(x,0)(p^{F}_{h}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

Symmetric
\alpha
-stable densities
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
satisfy these conditions. Specifically:

•

Finiteness of
D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
: The integrand
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x))
decays like
O(|x|^{-1-\alpha})
as
|x|\to\infty
(since the log term approaches a constant) and is integrable near
x=0
. Thus, the KL divergence is finite
[
12
]
.

•

Differentiability of
t\mapsto D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
at
t=0
: For
h_{t}=g_{v(t)}^{(\alpha)}
, differentiating
D(v(t))
under the integral is justified by the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Indeed,

\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Bigl[g_{v}(x)\log\frac{g_{v}(x)}{g_{s}(x)}\Bigr]=\frac{\partial g_{v}}{\partial v}(1+\log(g_{v}/g_{s}))=O\bigl(|x|^{-1-\alpha}\bigr)

uniformly for
v
in any compact subset of
(0,\infty)
, and since
|x|^{-1-\alpha}
is integrable at infinity for
\alpha>0
, the interchange is valid (see Appendix
A
, Step 2).

•

Smoothness and decay properties of S
\alpha
S densities
[
12
,
17
]
also ensure the existence of Fisher scores and the convergence of the FdBR integral at
t=0
.

Thus,
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
. Further characterisation of
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
for general densities is discussed in Section
5
.

Definition 2.2

(Mixed Fractional Information)
.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
,
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
(scale
s
), and
X_{t}
as in Eq.
2
with
h_{0}=h
:

M_{\alpha}(h\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=-\frac{\alpha}{s}\frac{d}{dt}D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\bigg|_{t=0}.

(5)

This definition assumes
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, the regularity class defined in Definition
2.1
, ensuring differentiability of entropy flow and well-posedness of the interpolation process. It isolates the integral term from the FdBR (Eq.
4
) at
t=0
and scales it, providing a measure directly related to the initial dissipation mechanism inherent in the stable interpolation process. Intuitively,
M_{\alpha}(h\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
measures how rapidly the distribution
h_{t}
initially diverges (in relative entropy) from
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
under the fractional-noise interpolation process. It generalises Fisher Information to the heavy-tailed setting.

2.2
Connection to Integral Form

From the FdBR (Eq.
4
) evaluated at
t=0
, the MFI corresponds to the integral:

M_{\alpha}(h\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(x)u(x,0)(p^{F}_{h}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(6)

Note:
u(x,0)
depends on the interpolation path originating from the specific initial density
h
. An explicit form is derived in Lemma
2.3
only for the case
h=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
. For general
h
, the score
u(x,0)
must be computed from the dynamics of
h_{t}
.

2.3
Analytical Score Function for S
\alpha
S Initial Density

Consider the interpolation
X_{t}=(1-t)^{1/\alpha}X_{0}+t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
starting from
X_{0}\sim g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
.
Using the scaling and stability properties, the density of
X_{t}
is
h_{t}=g_{v(t)}^{(\alpha)}
, where the effective scale evolves as:

v(t)=(1-t)v+ts.

(7)

\alpha
-stable densities under scale interpolation, with

, initial scale
v=1.2
, and target scale

Lemma 2.3
.

For the interpolation starting at
h_{0}=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
, the score function
u(x,t)
from Eq.
3
at time
t
is:

u(x,t)=x\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{v(t)}\right),

and the initial score function is:

u(x,0)=\frac{x(v-s)}{sv}.

Proof.

Following Johnson
[
9
]
, the score is
u(x,t)=\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)+x/s
, where
\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)=-\mathbb{E}[t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}|X_{t}=x]/(st)
is related to the Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) estimate of the scaled noise component
t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
given
X_{t}
. Johnson’s Lemma 3.3
[
9
]
utilises the structure of jointly S
\alpha
S variables. Specifically, even if marginal first moments are infinite (i.e., for
\alpha\leq 1
), the conditional expectation exists in the sense of linear regression,
\mathbb{E}[t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}|X_{t}=x]=c\cdot x
for some constant
c
, due to the linear regression property of jointly S
\alpha
S vectors (see
[
12
, Sec 2.8]
). In this specific interpolation context, this linear relationship yields
\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)=-x/v(t)
. Therefore,
u(x,t)=-x/v(t)+x/s
. Evaluating at
t=0
gives
u(x,0)=x(1/s-1/v)=x(v-s)/(sv)
.
∎

Remark 2.4

(On “conditional expectation” for
\alpha\leq 1
)
.

Even though for
\alpha\leq 1
the classical
\mathbb{E}[\,Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}\mid X_{t}=x]
may not exist in
L^{1}
, the quantity
\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)
is understood as the stable
-
law linear
-
regression (covariation) estimate (see Johnson 2018). This projection is well
-
defined for jointly S
\alpha
S vectors and is what enters the FdBR framework.

Remark 2.5

(Specificity of the Linear Score)
.

The derived linear form of the score function,
u(x,t)=x(1/s-1/v(t))
, is specific to the case where the initial distribution
h_{0}
is also S
\alpha
S. This result relies on Lemma 3.3 in
[
9
]
, which leverages the unique properties of jointly S
\alpha
S variables within the interpolation structure. For a general initial density
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, the score function
u(x,t)
, and particularly
u(x,0)
, would typically be non-linear and depend explicitly on the form of
h
.

3.1
Chain Rule Formulation

When the initial density is S
\alpha
S, i.e.,
h_{0}=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
,
the stability property ensures the interpolated density remains
within the same family:
h_{t}=g_{v(t)}^{(\alpha)}
, where the
scale parameter evolves linearly as
v(t)=v(1-t)+st
.
Let
D(v^{\prime})
denote the relative entropy

D(g_{v^{\prime}}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
. As established in the preamble
of Appendix
A
,
D(v^{\prime})
is differentiable
with respect to the scale parameter
v^{\prime}>0
. Since
v(t)
is a
smooth function of
t
, the chain rule applies to the time
derivative of the relative entropy along the path:

\frac{d}{dt}D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\frac{d}{dt}D(v(t))
.
Specifically,

\frac{d}{dt}D(v(t))=D^{\prime}(v(t))\frac{dv(t)}{dt}=D^{\prime}(v(t))(s-v).

Important Limitation.
This chain-rule expression
holds
only
because the interpolation starts from

h_{0}=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
, ensuring that
h_{t}=g^{(\alpha)}_{v(t)}

remains within the S
\alpha
S family throughout the evolution

t\in[0,1)
. If
h_{0}
were an arbitrary density in

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, then
h_{t}
would generally
not

be an S
\alpha
S density, the map

t\mapsto D(h_{t}\|g_{s})
could not be written as a composition

D(v(t))
, and the simple chain-rule factorisation

D^{\prime}(v(t))v^{\prime}(t)
would no longer be applicable.

Evaluating the valid chain rule expression at
t=0
gives

\frac{d}{dt}D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\big|_{t=0}=D^{\prime}(v)(s-v)
.
Substituting this initial rate into the MFI definition
(Eq.
5
):

M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=-\frac{\alpha}{s}[D^{\prime}(v)(s-v)]=\frac{\alpha D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}.

(8)

This provides the Mixed Fractional Information, for the specific case
of an S
\alpha
S initial density compared to an S
\alpha
S target,
directly in terms of the derivative of their relative entropy with
respect to the scale parameter.

3.2
Consistency of Formulations

We now establish that the chain rule form (Eq.
8
) and the integral form (Eq.
6
with the derived score
u(x,0)
) yield the same value. Equating the chain rule form (
M_{\alpha}^{\text{chain}}
) with the integral form (
M_{\alpha}^{\text{integral}}
) using the derived score
u(x,0)
from Lemma
2.3
requires establishing the following identity. Substituting
u(x,0)=\frac{x(v-s)}{sv}
from Lemma
2.3
into Eq.
6
with
h=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
:

M_{\alpha}^{\text{integral}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\cdot\frac{x(v-s)}{sv}\cdot(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{v-s}{sv}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Equating this to the chain rule form,
M_{\alpha}^{\text{chain}}=\frac{\alpha D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}
:

\frac{\alpha D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}=\frac{v-s}{sv}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Assuming
v\neq s
, we can divide by
(v-s)/s
:

\alpha D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\implies D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

This identity holds trivially if
v=s
since both sides are zero (
D^{\prime}(s)=0
as established in Theorem
3.3
proof, and
p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}=p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}
).

Proposition 3.1

(Consistency Identity)
.

For S
\alpha
S densities
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
and
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
with
\alpha\in(0,2]
and
v,s>0
, the following identity holds:

D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
and
p^{F}_{f}=f^{\prime}/f
(specifically
p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}=g_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}/g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
and
p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}=g_{s}^{(\alpha)\prime}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
).

Proof.

See Appendix
A
. For a rigorous numerical validation of this identity, see Appendix
B
.
∎

This proposition rigorously confirms the equivalence of the two calculation methods for
M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
.

Remark 3.2

(Scope of the Consistency Identity)
.

The identity established in Proposition
3.1
,
and thus the proven equivalence between the chain-rule formulation
of MFI (Eq.
8
) and the integral formulation
(Eq.
6
combined with the score from
Lemma
2.3
), crucially
requires
the initial
density to be S
\alpha
S, i.e.,
h_{0}=g^{(\alpha)}_{v}
.
This specific choice guarantees that the interpolation path remains
within the S
\alpha
S family (
h_{t}=g^{(\alpha)}_{v(t)}
),
that the initial score function takes the linear form

u(x,0)=x(v-s)/(sv)
, and that the time derivative of the
relative entropy follows the chain rule

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}D(h_{t}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=D^{\prime}(v(t))\,v^{\prime}(t)

For a general initial density
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}

3.3
Positivity

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,2]
and scale parameters
v,s>0
, the Mixed Fractional Information satisfies

M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\geq 0,

with equality holding if and only if
v=s
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds from the chain rule formulation derived in Section
3.1
(Eq.
8
), which is valid for the S
\alpha
S-to-S
\alpha
S comparison:

M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\frac{\alpha D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}.

Here,
D(v^{\prime}):=D(g_{v^{\prime}}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
is the relative entropy (KL divergence) between two S
\alpha
S densities with scales
v^{\prime}
and
s
, respectively, and
D^{\prime}(v)
is its derivative with respect to
v^{\prime}
evaluated at
v^{\prime}=v
.

By the fundamental properties of relative entropy (Gibbs’ inequality),
D(v^{\prime})\geq 0
for all
v^{\prime}>0

[
5
]
. Furthermore, equality
D(v^{\prime})=0
holds if and only if the two probability densities are identical almost everywhere, i.e.,
g_{v^{\prime}}^{(\alpha)}=g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
a.e.

For the family of symmetric
\alpha
-stable distributions considered in this paper (zero location, zero skewness), the mapping from the positive scale parameter (
v^{\prime}
or
s
) to the corresponding probability density function (
g_{v^{\prime}}^{(\alpha)}
or
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
) is injective. This means that distinct positive scale parameters necessarily yield distinct probability density functions. Therefore, the condition
g_{v^{\prime}}^{(\alpha)}=g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
a.e. is met if and only if the scale parameters are identical:
v^{\prime}=s

[
12
, cf. Property 1.2.3]
[
17
]
.

This establishes that the function
f(v^{\prime})=D(v^{\prime})
has a unique global minimum at
v^{\prime}=s
, where
D(s)=0
. As justified in Appendix
A
(Preamble based on properties from
[
12
,
17
]
), the relative entropy
D(v^{\prime})
is differentiable with respect to
v^{\prime}
for
v^{\prime}>0
. Standard calculus then implies that for a differentiable function with a unique global minimum at
s
, its derivative must be zero at the minimum (
D^{\prime}(s)=0
), negative to the left (
D^{\prime}(v)<0
for
v<s
), and positive to the right (
D^{\prime}(v)>0
for
v>s
).

Consequently, the product
D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)
is strictly positive whenever
v\neq s
(as both factors are negative if
v<s
, and both are positive if
v>s
) and is zero only when
v=s
. Since the stability index
\alpha
and the target scale parameter
s
are both strictly positive (
\alpha\in(0,2]
,
s>0
), the overall factor
\alpha/s
in the MFI expression is positive.

We conclude that
M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=(\alpha/s)[D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)]\geq 0
, with equality holding if and only if
v=s
.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Note on Convexity)
.

While the convexity of
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
as a function of
v
is plausible (and holds for
\alpha=1,2
), the positivity proof presented here relies only on the fundamental property that
D(v)
has a unique minimum at
v=s
and is differentiable. Proving convexity for general
\alpha\in(0,2)
remains an open question but is not necessary for establishing the non-negativity of MFI.

3.4
Cauchy Case (
\alpha=1
)

For the Cauchy distribution (
\alpha=1
), the relative entropy is known
[
4
, Theorem 1]
:

D(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})=\log\frac{(v+s)^{2}}{4vs}.

The derivative with respect to
v
is:

D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{d}{dv}\left[2\log(v+s)-\log(v)-\log(4s)\right]=\frac{2}{v+s}-\frac{1}{v}=\frac{v-s}{v(v+s)}.

Substituting
\alpha=1
and this
D^{\prime}(v)
into the MFI formula (Eq.
8
):

M_{1}(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})=\frac{1\cdot D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}=\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{v-s}{v(v+s)}\right)(v-s)=\frac{(v-s)^{2}}{sv(v+s)}.

This provides an explicit, closed-form expression for MFI in the Cauchy case, allowing for direct analysis in this setting.
This closed-form expression aligns with the general consistency identity proven in Appendix
A
, which holds for all
\alpha\in(0,2]
, including the Cauchy case (
\alpha=1
).

4
Discussion

4.1
MFI as a Dissipation Measure

The MFI,
M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})=\frac{\alpha D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)}{s}
, measures the initial rate of relative entropy dissipation as
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
evolves toward
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
under the specific stable interpolation (Eq.
2
), scaled appropriately by
\alpha/s
. Unlike classical Fisher information, which diverges for
\alpha<2
, MFI remains finite and provides a well-defined measure reflecting the information dynamics inherent to stable laws within this specific process.

4.2
Significance of Consistency

The proven equivalence between the chain rule form (
\propto D^{\prime}(v)(v-s)
) and the integral form (
\propto\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[u(x,0)(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]
) is crucial. It validates the internal mathematical coherence of the MFI framework when comparing S
\alpha
S distributions. It confirms that the derived score function
u(x,0)=x(v-s)/(sv)
correctly links the local Fisher score differences to the global change in relative entropy
D^{\prime}(v)
via the identity in Proposition
3.1
. This identity itself, which can be written as
\alpha vD^{\prime}(v)=\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]
, represents a potentially novel contribution to the analytical understanding of information measures for stable laws, directly connecting entropy derivatives to score functions.

4.3
Link to MMSE Estimation and I-MMSE Analogy

The score function
u(x,t)=\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)+x/s
, central to the Fractional de Bruijn Identity (FdBR) and the integral formulation of MFI, explicitly incorporates Johnson’s MMSE-related score
\rho^{\mathrm{MMSE}}_{X,t}(x)
. This term quantifies aspects of the error in estimating the scaled stable noise component
t^{1/\alpha}Z_{s}^{(\alpha)}
given the interpolated process
X_{t}=x

[
9
]
, even when classical moments diverge.

The proven consistency identity (Proposition
3.1
) for the S
\alpha
S-to-S
\alpha
S interpolation at
t=0
strongly reinforces this connection between information dissipation and estimation. The identity can be suggestively rearranged using the score difference notation (
\delta p^{F}_{v,s}=p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s}
) as:

\alpha\,v\,D^{\prime}(v)\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}\!\bigl[X\,\delta p^{F}_{v,s}(X)\bigr].

This structure echoes the celebrated I-MMSE relation for Gaussian channels
[
8
]
, typically written as

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{snr}}I(X;\sqrt{\mathrm{snr}}Z+X)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{mmse}(\mathrm{snr})
, where
Z
is standard Gaussian noise.
In the Gaussian case, the derivative of mutual information (
I
, an information measure) with respect to the signal-to-noise ratio (
\mathrm{snr}
) equals half the minimum mean-square error (
\mathrm{mmse}
, an estimation-theoretic quantity).

In our S
\alpha
S context,
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
acts as an
information potential
(relative entropy) dependent on the scale parameter
v
. Its derivative
D^{\prime}(v)
, scaled by
\alpha v
, is equated to the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X\,\delta p^{F}_{v,s}(X)]
. This expectation involves the score difference
\delta p^{F}_{v,s}
and is directly related through the MFI framework (specifically Lemma
2.3
and Eq.
6
) to the MMSE-related score
u(x,0)
, thus serving as an estimation-theoretic proxy reflecting the strength of the dynamics driving
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
towards
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
.

This structural resonance, arising naturally from the FdBR framework and solidified by the consistency result, suggests that MFI may serve as a valuable tool for investigating estimation-theoretic properties in stable noise environments. It motivates exploring a potential "fractional I-MMSE" framework, further connecting information dynamics and estimation theory in heavy-tailed settings and potentially leading to new performance bounds or insights.

4.4
Functional Inequalities and Concentration

A key direction for future work is establishing functional inequalities relating the relative entropy
D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
to the Mixed Fractional Information
M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
. Such inequalities are crucial for understanding concentration properties and convergence rates within the stable framework, analogous to how classical Log-Sobolev (LSI) or Poincaré inequalities function in the Gaussian setting.

An initial investigation might explore a direct LSI analogue, particularly for the Cauchy case (
\alpha=1
) where closed forms exist. One could conjecture a relationship like
D(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})\leq C\cdot M_{1}(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})
for some universal constant
C
. However, straightforward analysis reveals this simple, unweighted LSI does not hold. Using the explicit expressions derived in Section
3.4
, the ratio

\frac{D(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})}{M_{1}(g_{v}^{(1)}\|g_{s}^{(1)})}=\frac{sv(v+s)}{(v-s)^{2}}\log\frac{(v+s)^{2}}{4vs}

is unbounded. For instance, consider the limit as the ratio
v/s\to\infty
. In this regime,
D\sim\log(v/s)
grows logarithmically, while
M_{1}=(v-s)^{2}/(sv(v+s))\sim v^{2}/(sv^{2})=1/s
approaches a constant (assuming fixed
s
). Consequently, the ratio
D/M_{1}
diverges as
v/s\to\infty
(and similarly as
v/s\to 0
).

This failure aligns with the established fact that symmetric
\alpha
-stable laws for
\alpha\in(0,2)
do not possess a spectral gap in
L^{2}
spaces weighted by the density itself, unlike the Gaussian case (
\alpha=2
) which corresponds to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
and related works on functional inequalities for Lévy processes). The absence of such a gap generally precludes standard (unweighted) Poincaré or log-Sobolev inequalities, thus motivating the investigation of weighted functional inequalities as discussed previously.

Indeed, research suggests that appropriate inequalities for heavy-tailed distributions might need to be weighted or otherwise generalised. For instance, weighted Poincaré and LSI inequalities have been successfully established for Cauchy measures, albeit using specific weight functions chosen to counteract the heavy tails
[
2
]
. Connections between suitable weighted inequalities and concepts like Stein kernels have also been explored for distributions with heavy tails
[
13
]
.

While alternative approaches using Fractional Fisher Information have yielded candidate functional inequalities for S
\alpha
S laws
[
16
]
, finding and proving corresponding inequalities (potentially weighted LSI or Poincaré-type) involving MFI remains a significant open problem. Such inequalities would be central to understanding concentration and convergence properties within the MFI framework and would likely need to explicitly account for the
\alpha
-dependent structure revealed by the MFI definition and the consistency identity (Proposition
3.1
). The analytical challenge posed by finding appropriate functional inequalities stands in contrast to the successful high-precision verification of the core consistency identity itself, which was robustly confirmed numerically as detailed in Appendix
B
.

4.5
Numerical Confirmation of Consistency

The analytical proof establishing the consistency between the chain rule and integral formulations of MFI for S
\alpha
S inputs (Proposition
3.1
) is strongly corroborated by numerical validation. Using a robust computational pipeline detailed in Appendix
B
—employing adaptive quadrature over infinite domains, stable score computation via log-derivatives, and high-order finite difference methods—we confirmed the identity
D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]
to high precision. Tier 1 validation for Cauchy (
\alpha=1
) and Gaussian (
\alpha=2
cases achieved machine precision agreement, leveraging known analytic forms. Crucially, Tier 2 validation for the general case (
\alpha=1.5
) also yielded excellent agreement (relative error
<10^{-6}
), as shown in Table
1
. This demonstrates that the identity holds robustly and is computationally verifiable when appropriate numerical methods are employed, resolving the larger discrepancies observed in initial, less sophisticated numerical tests (which suffered from issues like domain truncation and unstable score estimation). This numerical confirmation solidifies the MFI framework’s internal coherence.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have introduced Mixed Fractional Information (
M_{\alpha}
) as a measure derived from entropy dissipation dynamics within Johnson’s stable interpolation framework. For the core case comparing two S
\alpha
S densities, we established two distinct calculation methods – one based on the relative entropy derivative (
D^{\prime}(v)
) and the other on an integral involving Fisher score differences and an MMSE-related score function (
u(x,0)
) – and rigorously proved their mathematical equivalence via the identity
D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]
. The proven consistency was further substantiated by robust numerical validation, confirming the identity to high precision across different
\alpha

While this work establishes the MFI framework and its properties for symmetric stable laws under a specific interpolation path, it serves as a foundation for broader investigations into information theory for heavy-tailed systems. Key future directions include the extension of the MFI framework to asymmetric stable laws, the challenging search for appropriate (potentially weighted) functional inequalities (such as Log-Sobolev or Poincaré inequalities) involving MFI, exploring potential connections to information geometry on stable manifolds, and further developing the link to estimation theory, possibly yielding fractional analogues of the I-MMSE relation.

Appendix A
Proof of Consistency Identity (Proposition
3.1
)

Goal: Establish the identity for
\alpha\in(0,2]
and
v,s>0
:

D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
and
p^{F}_{f}=f^{\prime}/f
.

Proof Strategy.

The proof proceeds by differentiating the relative entropy

D(v)
with respect to
v
using the Leibniz integral rule
(differentiation under the integral sign). The resulting scale
derivative
\partial g_{v}/\partial v
is then related to the
spatial derivative
g_{v}^{\prime}=\partial g_{v}/\partial x
using the
S
\alpha
S scaling identity (derived in Step 2, Eq. (
A
)).
The final identity emerges after substituting this relation back into
the integral for
D^{\prime}(v)
and simplifying using integration by parts,
leveraging the boundary conditions of
g_{v}
and the normalisation
constraint
\int g_{v}dx=1
(specifically, its consequence

\int xg_{v}^{\prime}dx=-1
).

Justification of Assumptions:
This proof relies on key properties of S
\alpha
S densities and the KL divergence
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
. Specifically:

•

Smoothness and Differentiability:
The S
\alpha
S density
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)
is smooth (C
\infty
) with respect to both
x
(for
x\neq 0
if
\alpha<2
) and the scale parameter
v>0

[
12
,
17
]
. Consequently, the KL divergence
D(v)
inherits sufficient smoothness (at least
C^{1}
) for
v>0
. This ensures the existence of
D^{\prime}(v)
for all
v>0
, including at
v=s
.

•

Differentiation Under the Integral:
The interchange of derivative (
\frac{d}{dv}
) and integral (
\int dx
) in Step 1 is justified by the Dominated Convergence Theorem for differentiation. As shown in Step 2, the derivative
\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{\partial v}
involves
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
and
xg_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}
, both decaying like
O(|x|^{-1-\alpha})
or faster
[
12
]
. Since
\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\to\log(v/s)
as
|x|\to\infty
, the partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial v}[g_{v}\log(g_{v}/g_{s})]=\frac{\partial g_{v}}{\partial v}(1+\log(g_{v}/g_{s}))
also decays like
O(|x|^{-1-\alpha})
. This decay ensures the existence of an integrable dominating function independent of
v
in a neighborhood, justifying the interchange
[
12
,
17
]
.

—

Step 1: Compute the derivative
D^{\prime}(v)
via Leibniz Integral Rule

Start with the definition of relative entropy:

D(v)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Differentiate with respect to
v
, interchanging derivative and integral (justified above):

D^{\prime}(v)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Apply the product rule for differentiation w.r.t.
v
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}\right]=\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{\partial v}\left(1+\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right).

Therefore:

D^{\prime}(v)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\partial v}\left(1+\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Step 2: Relate the scale derivative
\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/\partial v
to the spatial derivative
g_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}

Using the scaling property
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)=v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(y)
where
y=v^{-1/\alpha}x
, differentiation w.r.t.
v
via the chain rule gives:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=(-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}v^{-1/\alpha-1})g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(y)+v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)\prime}(y)\frac{\partial y}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\alpha v}v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(y)+v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)\prime}(y)(-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}v^{-1/\alpha-1}x)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\alpha v}\left[v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(y)+(v^{-1/\alpha}x)v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)\prime}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\alpha v}\left[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)+x(v^{-2/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)\prime}(v^{-1/\alpha}x))\right]

Recognizing that
g_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}[v^{-1/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)}(v^{-1/\alpha}x)]=v^{-2/\alpha}g_{1}^{(\alpha)\prime}(v^{-1/\alpha}x)
, we get:

\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\partial v}=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)+xg_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}(x)\right].

This identity relates the scale derivative to the spatial derivative (cf.
[
17
, Ch 2]
).

Step 3: Substitute and Evaluate Key Integral
\int xg_{v}^{\prime}dx

Substitute (A.2) into (A.1):

D^{\prime}(v)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha v}[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)+xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)]\right)\left(1+\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)+xg_{v}^{\prime}(x))\left(1+\log\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

To proceed, we first evaluate
\int xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. Differentiating the normalisation condition
\int g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1
with respect to
v
gives
\int\frac{\partial g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{\partial v}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0
. Substituting (A.2):

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)+xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0

\implies\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0

\implies 1+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0

Thus, we establish rigorously for all
\alpha\in(0,2]
:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-1.

(Alternatively, using IBP:
\int xg_{v}^{\prime}dx=[xg_{v}]_{-\infty}^{\infty}-\int g_{v}dx=0-1=-1
, since
xg_{v}(x)\sim|x|^{-\alpha}\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
for
\alpha>0
).

Step 4: Expand Integrand and Use Integration by Parts

Expand the integrand in (A.3):

(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}+xg_{v}^{\prime})(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))=g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))+xg_{v}^{\prime}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))

Substitute back into (A.3):

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[\int g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int xg_{v}^{\prime}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right]

Evaluate the first integral:
\int g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1+D(v)
.
Evaluate the second integral, denoted
I_{B}
:

I_{B}=\int xg_{v}^{\prime}(1+\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int xg_{v}^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int xg_{v}^{\prime}\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Using (A.4), this becomes:

I_{B}=-1+\int xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Let
I^{\prime}=\int xg_{v}^{\prime}(x)\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. Evaluate
I^{\prime}
using IBP with
u=x\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}/g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
and
dv=g_{v}^{\prime}(x)dx
, so
v_{\text{IBP}}=g_{v}(x)
. Note:
v_{\text{IBP}}
here refers to the result of integrating
dv
, distinct from the scale parameter
v
.

du=\left[\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}\right)+x(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

I^{\prime}=\left[xg_{v}(x)\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{v}(x)\left[\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}\right)+x(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Justify vanishing boundary term: As
|x|\to\infty
,
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\sim Cv|x|^{-1-\alpha}
and
\log(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)/g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x))\to\log(v/s)
(a constant). Thus, the term behaves like
x\cdot(Cv|x|^{-1-\alpha})\cdot(\text{const})\propto\text{sgn}(x)|x|^{-\alpha}
, which tends to 0 since
\alpha>0
. The boundary term is 0.

I^{\prime}=0-\int g_{v}\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int xg_{v}(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

I^{\prime}=-D(v)-\int xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Step 5: Combine Terms to Reach the Final Identity

Substitute the results for the integrals back into the expression for
D^{\prime}(v)
:

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[(1+D(v))+I_{B}\right]

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[(1+D(v))+(-1+I^{\prime})\right]

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[D(v)+I^{\prime}\right]

Substitute the expression for
I^{\prime}
from (A.5):

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[D(v)+\left(-D(v)-\int xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)\right]

D^{\prime}(v)=-\frac{1}{\alpha v}\left[-\int xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right]

D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

Conclusion:
The identity is established rigorously:

\boxed{D^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}

This proof confirms the consistency identity for all
\alpha\in(0,2]
, relying on established properties of S
\alpha
S densities and careful application of calculus theorems.

Remark A.1

(Integrability of the Score Difference Term)
.

We briefly confirm the convergence of the integral defining the
expectation in the final identity,

\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

This relies on the asymptotic behavior of S
\alpha
S densities
and their derivatives for large
|x|

[
12
,
17
]
:

•

The density decays as
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\sim c_{v}|x|^{-1-\alpha}
, where
c_{v}>0
.

•

The derivative decays as
g_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}(x)\sim-c_{v}(1+\alpha)\text{sgn}(x)|x|^{-2-\alpha}
.

•

Consequently, the Fisher score behaves as
p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)=g_{v}^{(\alpha)\prime}(x)/g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\sim-(1+\alpha)x^{-1}
(for large
x
, similarly for
-x
).

•

The score difference term thus decays at least as fast as
(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))=O(|x|^{-1})
as
|x|\to\infty
. (The leading
x^{-1}
terms might even cancel if
v=s
, leading to faster decay, but
O(|x|^{-1})
is sufficient).

Therefore, the integrand
xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{v}(x)-p^{F}_{s}(x))
has the asymptotic behavior:

x\cdot O(|x|^{-1-\alpha})\cdot O(|x|^{-1})=O(|x|^{-1-\alpha}).

Since the exponent
1+\alpha>1
for all relevant
\alpha\in(0,2]
, this function
O(|x|^{-1-\alpha})
is absolutely integrable over
(\infty,-R]\cup[R,\infty)
for sufficiently large
R
. The integrand is also continuous (and thus bounded) on
[-R,R]
, ensuring the integral converges over the entire real line
\mathbb{R}
. Thus, the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{g_{v}}[X(p^{F}_{v}-p^{F}_{s})]
is well-defined and finite.

∎

Appendix B
Numerical Validation of the Consistency Identity

This appendix details the robust numerical validation of the core consistency identity (Proposition
3.1
):

D^{\prime}(v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\alpha v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

where
D(v)=D(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
. The validation encompasses both analytically tractable cases (Tier 1:
\alpha=1,2
) and the general S
\alpha
S case (Tier 2: exemplified by
\alpha=1.5
). The results demonstrate high-precision agreement, confirming the identity computationally. The methodology employed overcomes numerical challenges inherent in dealing with heavy-tailed distributions and score function estimations, which affected preliminary tests discussed elsewhere.

B.1
Methodology

The validation compares the Left-Hand Side (LHS),
D^{\prime}(v)
, with the Right-Hand Side (RHS), the scaled integral expression.

Tier 1: Analytic Cases (
\alpha=1,2
)

For Cauchy (
\alpha=1
) and Gaussian (
\alpha=2
) distributions, we leverage known analytic formulas:

•

LHS (
D^{\prime}(v)
):
Calculated directly using the exact formula for
D^{\prime}(v)
derived from the known closed-form relative entropy (see Section
3.4
for Cauchy; standard result for Gaussian KL divergence).

•

RHS (Integral):
The integral is computed using ‘scipy.integrate.quad‘ over
(-\infty,\infty)
with high precision settings (‘epsabs=1e-12‘, ‘epsrel=1e-12‘). The integrand
xg_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x))
is constructed using the exact analytic formulas for the respective PDFs (
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
) and Fisher scores (
p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}},p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}
). The final RHS value is obtained by dividing the integral result by
\alpha v
.

This approach minimises numerical error, primarily limiting it to the quadrature tolerance.

Tier 2: General S
\alpha
S Case (
\alpha=1.5
)

For general
\alpha
where closed forms are unavailable, a robust numerical pipeline is employed:

•

PDF Evaluation:
S
\alpha
S PDFs (
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
,
g_{s}^{(\alpha)}
, etc.) are evaluated using
scipy.stats.levy_stable.pdf
, after proper scale parameter conversion:
c=s^{1/\alpha}
.

•

Score Function (
p^{F}
):
Fisher scores
p^{F}_{f}(x)=f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
are evaluated pointwise within the integrator by numerically differentiating the log-PDF:
p^{F}_{f}(x)\approx\frac{d}{dx}[\log f(x)]
. This derivative is computed using a high-order (5-point stencil,
\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
) finite difference method implemented via a custom function (
sp_derivative
in the validation code), with a small step size (e.g.,
score_dx = 1e-6
). Note that computing the score via numerical differentiation of
\log f(x)
(i.e.,
p^{F}_{f}(x)\approx\frac{d}{dx}[\log f(x)]
) is preferred over computing
f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
directly, as it avoids potential numerical instability caused by dividing by very small values of
f(x)
in the distribution tails.

•

Relative Entropy (
D(v\|s)
):
Computed using adaptive quadrature (
scipy.integrate.quad
) over
(-\infty,\infty)
with stringent tolerances (e.g.,
epsabs=1e-10
,
epsrel=1e-10
). The integrand is:
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\log\left(\frac{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)}{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}(x)}\right)
, evaluated using numerically computed PDFs, with careful handling of potential
\log(0)
issues.

•

LHS (
D^{\prime}(v)
Estimation):
Estimated via high-order finite differences applied to the numerically computed
D(v\|s)
values. A 5-point stencil formula (
\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
) is used:
D^{\prime}(v)\approx\frac{-D(v+2h)+8D(v+h)-8D(v-h)+D(v-2h)}{12h}.
This 5-point stencil formula offers high accuracy with a truncation error of order
O(h^{4})
, assuming
D(v)
is sufficiently smooth. The step size
h
requires careful selection to balance truncation error (favours small
h
) and numerical round-off error (favours larger
h
). Convergence and stability are verified by observing the results across a range of decreasing
h
values, as presented in Table
1
. Results are reported for several small step sizes
h
(e.g.,
10^{-2}
down to
10^{-4}
) to assess convergence.

•

RHS (Integral Estimation):
The integral
\int x\,g_{v}^{(\alpha)}(x)\left(p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}}(x)-p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}(x)\right)dx
is computed via
scipy.integrate.quad
over
(-\infty,\infty)
, using tolerances matching the KL divergence calculation. The integrand uses numerically evaluated
g_{v}^{(\alpha)}
and pointwise computed scores
p^{F}_{g_{v}^{(\alpha)}},p^{F}_{g_{s}^{(\alpha)}}
via the log-derivative method. The final RHS value is obtained by dividing the integral result by
\alpha v
.

The robustness of the numerical results was confirmed by checking convergence under refinement of discretisation parameters, such as the integration tolerances for
scipy.integrate.quad
and the step size
h
for finite difference calculations (as demonstrated for
D^{\prime}(v)
estimation in Table
1
). This robust pipeline aims to control errors introduced by numerical differentiation and integration, especially crucial for heavy-tailed distributions.

B.3
Conclusion

The numerical simulations, conducted with robust methods appropriate for heavy-tailed distributions, provide high-precision validation of the core theoretical result: the mathematical equivalence of the chain rule and integral formulations for
M_{\alpha}(g_{v}^{(\alpha)}\|g_{s}^{(\alpha)})
. The consistency holds robustly across different
\alpha
values. Furthermore, the numerical framework allowed for testing conjectures. Evaluation confirms the analytical finding (Section
4.4
) that the simple Cauchy Log-Sobolev inequality analogue
D\leq CM_{1}
does not hold, as the ratio
D/M_{1}
is unbounded. This underscores the challenges in formulating simple functional inequalities for heavy-tailed distributions and motivates exploring weighted or alternative forms. The full validation code (
validation.py
) and detailed results (
validation_results.json

Abstract
. We extend the polynomial method of Chen–Garza-Vargas–Tropp–van Handel and Magee–Puder–van Handel for operator-norm bounds in random permutation models to the setting where torsion is present. The main new feature is that asymptotic expansion of traces naturally involves fractional powers of N N rather than an ordinary Laurent series. We formulate fractional-power analogues of the method’s key hypotheses and prove they lead to strong convergence. We verify these analogues for free products of finite groups Γ = G 1 ∗ ⋯ ∗ G m \Gamma=G_{1}*\cdots*G_{m} . Concretely, for a uniformly random ϕ N ∈ hom ⁡ ( Γ , Sym ⁡ ( N ) ) \phi_{N}\in\hom(\Gamma,\Sym(N)) , set π N = std ∘ ϕ N \pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N} , where std \std denotes the standard ( N − 1 ) (N-1) -dimensional representation of Sym ⁡ ( N ) \Sym(N) (the permutation representation with the trivial subrepresentation removed). We deduce strong convergence of π N \pi_{N} to the left regular representation of Γ \Gamma . As applications, we obtain asymptotically sharp spectral gaps for the associated random Schreier graphs, including almost Ramanujan behavior for C 2 ∗ C 2 ∗ C 2 C_{2}*C_{2}*C_{2} and an explicit non-Ramanujan limiting spectral radius for C 2 ∗ C 3 ≅ PSL 2 ⁡ ( 𝐙 ) C_{2}*C_{3}\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}) .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Expander graphs

Expander graphs form a remarkable intersection of combinatorics, geometry, probability, and group theory. A family of finite connected graphs with an increasing number of vertices
N
, each of fixed degree
d
, is called an
expander family
if each adjacency operator
\mathcal{A}
in the family exhibits a uniform spectral gap between the top eigenvalue
d
(with Perron-Frobenius eigenvector
\mathbf{1}
of all ones) and the second-largest eigenvalue in absolute value, which is
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}

[
Lub95
,
Kow19
]
. Such families are fundamental tools in both theoretical computer science (derandomization, coding theory, probabilistically checkable proofs, network design) and mathematics (mixing of random walks, growth in groups, sieve methods, topological and geometric applications). For a comprehensive overview, see
[
HLW06
]
.

The first explicit constructions of expander families were given by Margulis
[
Mar73
]
, using arithmetic lattices and Kazhdan’s property
(T)
from representation theory. Subsequently, explicit constructions achieving the largest possible spectral gap were discovered. The Alon–Boppana bound
[
HLW06
, Theorem 5.3]
asserts that in any family of
d
-regular graphs one has

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}\geq 2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1)\qquad(N\to\infty),

which provides a universal constraint on the spectral gap. An expander family is called
Ramanujan
if for each graph in the family, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}\leq 2\sqrt{d-1}
. Ramanujan families are thus optimal expanders, and the first explicit constructions were obtained by Lubotzky, Phillips, and Sarnak
[
LPS88
]
and Margulis
[
Mar88
]
, building on deep input from number theory.

Expansion in random graphs

A complementary viewpoint, which is central to this paper, is that one can construct (non-explicit) expander families using randomness. In parallel with Margulis’s work, Pinsker
[
Pin73
]
and independently Barzdin and Kolmogorov
[
Bar93
]
showed that random
d
-regular graphs on
N
vertices form expander families with high probability, that is
1-o(1)
, as
N\to\infty
. Later, a far stronger result by Friedman
[
Fri08
]
established that random
d
-regular graphs are
almost Ramanujan
, meaning that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}\leq 2\sqrt{d-1}+o(1)
with high probability as
N\to\infty
.

One convenient way to generate random regular graphs is via permutations in the symmetric group
\Sym(N)
. Fix a symmetric generating set
S
of size
2d
(e.g.
S=\{\sigma_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,\sigma_{d}^{\pm 1}\}
), where the
\sigma_{i}
are chosen independently uniformly at random in
\Sym(N)
. The associated
Schreier graph
on vertex set
[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}
has edges

\{j,\,\sigma_{i}(j)\}\qquad(1\leq i\leq d,\ j\in[N]),

and is
2d
-regular (allowing parallel edges and loops). For asymptotic purposes, this model is equivalent to the uniform model of random
2d
-regular graphs
[
Wor99
,
JLR00
]
. Friedman’s proof is performed in this setting and relies on a delicate analysis of traces of high powers of the corresponding adjacency operator
\mathcal{A}
.

More generally, given any finitely generated group
\Gamma=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle
and a sequence of finite permutation representations
\sigma_{N}\colon G_{N}\to\Sym(N)
, one can form random Schreier graphs as follows. Let
\phi_{N}\colon\Gamma\to G_{N}
be a uniformly random homomorphism, set
\pi_{N}=\sigma_{N}\circ\phi_{N}\colon\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
, and build the Schreier graph on vertex set
[N]
with edges

\{j,\pi_{N}(x_{i})(j)\}\qquad(1\leq i\leq d,\ j\in[N]).

The random model above used in Friedman’s proof is a special case of this, corresponding to
\Gamma=\mathbf{F}_{d}
(the free group on
d
generators) and
G_{N}=\Sym(N)
with its natural action on
[N]
. Keeping
\Gamma=\mathbf{F}_{d}
but allowing
G_{N}
to range beyond
\Sym(N)
, variants of Pinsker’s theorem (though not Friedman’s almost-Ramanujan conclusion) have been proved for some important sequences of permutation groups. Most notably, expansion occurs with high probability for families of finite simple groups of fixed Lie type acting on themselves by left multiplication by the works of Bourgain and Gamburd
[
BG08
]
and Breuillard, Green, Guralnick, and Tao
[
BGGT15
]
.

1.2.
The polynomial method

Recently, a new approach to operator-norm bounds in random permutation models as described above has emerged, known as the polynomial method by Chen, Garza-Vargas, Tropp, and van Handel
[
CGVTvH24
]
. A thorough exposition of the method is available in
[
vH25
]
. To put it simply, the method bounds the operator norm of random operators (such as adjacency operators of random Schreier graphs) by applying smooth functional calculus to the operator under the following prerequisites.

A.

Asymptotic expansion of expected traces:

For any
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
in the group algebra of
\Gamma
, obtain a rational expression for
\mathbf{E}[\tr(\pi_{N}(x))]
in terms of
N
with control over the degrees of the numerator and denominator. This leads to an asymptotic expansion of the expected trace as a Laurent series in
N
.

B.

Temperedness of leading coefficients:
Let
\lambda\colon\Gamma\to\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
be the left regular representation of
\Gamma
, given by
\lambda(\gamma)f(x)=f(\gamma^{-1}x)
for
\gamma,x\in\Gamma
,
f\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
. Bound the growth of the first two leading coefficients in the Laurent series above along powers
x^{p}
by at most the growth of
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}
as
p\to\infty
.

C.

Unique trace property:
The reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)
is the norm-closure of
\lambda(\Gamma)
. This algebra is equipped with a canonical trace
\tau(a)=\langle a\delta_{e},\delta_{e}\rangle
. This should be the only positive unital tracial functional on
C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)
.

Assuming these conditions, the polynomial method yields
strong convergence
of the random representations
\pi_{N}\colon\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(N)
to the left regular representation, meaning that for every
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi_{N}(x)\right\rVert}\to\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}\quad\text{in probability as $N\to\infty$.}

As shown in
[
CGVTvH24
]
, this paradigm can be applied to the setting
\Gamma=\mathbf{F}_{d}
and
G_{N}=\Sym(N)
with its standard irreducible representation
\std\colon\Sym(N)\to\mathcal{U}(N-1)
, obtained by restricting the permutation action on
\mathbf{C}^{N}
to the hyperplane
\mathbf{1}^{\perp}
. The result is strong convergence of the random representations
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}
towards the left regular representation of
\mathbf{F}_{d}
. For the particular choice
x=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}+x_{i}^{-1})
, this implies that the adjacency operator
\mathcal{A}
of the random Schreier graph of
\Sym(N)
acting via
\phi_{N}
on
[N]
satisfies
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}\to 2\sqrt{2d-1}
(by Kesten
[
Kes59
]
) with high probability as
N\to\infty
. One thus recovers Friedman’s theorem that random
2d
-regular graphs are almost Ramanujan.

Extensions and limitations of the polynomial method

Since
[
CGVTvH24
]
, the polynomial method has been extended in various directions. Magee and de la Salle
[
MdlS24
]
analyzed the case of
\mathcal{U}(N)
instead of
\Sym(N)
and demonstrated that strong convergence holds for numerous irreducible representations of
\mathcal{U}(N)
. Building on this, Cassidy
[
Cas25
]
explored additional irreducible representations of
\Sym(N)
beyond
\std
and proved strong convergence in those cases. Moreover, quantitative bounds on the convergence rate produced by the method were developed. This was first done by Chen, Garza-Vargas, and van Handel
[
CGVvH24
]
, who specialized the machinery to classical ensembles and proved it can be used to improve the rate of strong convergence to free semicircular distributions in that setting. This was later strengthened by Magee, Puder, and van Handel
[
MPvH25
]
and Hide, Macera, and Thomas
[
HMT25
]
. The paper
[
MPvH25
]
substantially broadened the framework of the original polynomial method by replacing the requirement for exact rationality of
\mathbf{E}\left[\tr\pi_{N}(x)\right]
with sufficiently analytic-like asymptotic expansion as a Laurent series in
N
. They apply this to prove strong convergence when
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus at least
2
, with
G_{N}=\Sym(N)
and the
\std
representation.

However, not all natural random permutation models fit within even the most extended known frameworks. As the following example demonstrates, the presence of nontrivial
torsion
in
\Gamma
introduces new phenomena in the asymptotic expansions of
\mathbf{E}[\tr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))]
. Specifically, the asymptotic expansion is no longer a simple Laurent series in
N
but instead includes fractional powers of
N
.

Example
.

Let
\Gamma=C_{2}=\langle\gamma\rangle
, the cyclic group of order
2
. A random homomorphism
\phi_{N}\colon C_{2}\to\Sym(N)
corresponds to choosing a random involution
\sigma\in\Sym(N)
. Then, since
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}
,

\mathbf{E}_{\pi_{N}}\left[\tr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))\right]=\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\left[|\fix(\sigma)|\right]-1.

Let
a_{N}
be the number of involutions in
\Sym(N)
. Then, by symmetry,

\mathbf{E}_{\sigma}\left[|\fix(\sigma)|\right]=N\mathbf{P}(\sigma(1)=1)=Na_{N-1}/a_{N}.

The involution numbers satisfy
a_{N}=a_{N-1}+(N-1)a_{N-2}
, hence the ratio
r_{N}=a_{N}/a_{N-1}
satisfies
r_{N}=1+(N-1)/r_{N-1}
. From this recursion we obtain that
r_{N}=\sqrt{N}+O(1)
as
N\to\infty
. Therefore

\mathbf{E}_{\pi_{N}}\left[\tr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))\right]=\sqrt{N}+O(1).

Related torsion-constrained permutation models were previously analyzed by Bordenave and Collins
[
BC19
]
, and by Shen and Wu
[
SW25
]
. Both works consider models where the generators of
\Gamma
are mapped to permutations of finite orders, but impose the restriction that these permutations be
fixed-point-free
.

Bordenave and Collins
[
BC19
]
develop a refined trace method based on non-backtracking operators, which predates the polynomial method. While their method provides yet another alternative proof of Friedman’s theorem, it also yields operator-norm limits for random graphs generated by fixed-point-free involutions (equivalently, unions of random perfect matchings). Their fixed-point-free setting is tailored to the permutation models arising from random lifts, and their methods do not readily extend to uniform sampling of involutions in
\Sym(N)
. On the other hand, Shen and Wu
[
SW25
]
analyzed spectral gaps in random Belyi surfaces constructed from the Brooks–Makover model, which glues hyperbolic triangles according to two random permutations of orders
2
and
3
, both again restricted to be fixed-point-free in order for the gluing to make sense. In this setup, expected traces turn out to exhibit the same asymptotic expansions with integer powers of
N
as those in surface groups (see
[
SW25
, Proposition 1.2]
), thus fitting within the existing frameworks of the extended polynomial method of Magee–Puder–van Handel.

The fixed-point-free condition is restrictive: for a uniformly random element of order dividing
d
in
\Sym(N)
, the probability of having no fixed points decays like
\exp(-N^{1/d})
as
N\to\infty
.
1
1

1

The counting generating functions are
\exp(P(z))
for all solutions of
x^{d}=1
and
\exp(P(z)-z)
for fixed-point-free ones, where
P(z)=\smash[b]{\sum_{k\mid d}}z^{k}/k
. By
[
Mül97
, Theorem 1]
, their coefficient ratio is asymptotically
\exp(-N^{1/d})
up to a constant factor.
In addition, forbidding fixed points removes the torsion-driven leading terms in expected traces in the uniform model. The
\sqrt{N}
term in the uniform
C_{2}
example above is exactly such a fixed-point contribution.

1.3.
Expansion in random graphs with torsion restrictions

In this paper, we show how to handle general torsion constraints in random permutation models. We impose the relations independently on the generators by taking

(1)

\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m},

where each
G_{i}
is a finite group and
m\geq 2
. As before, let
\phi_{N}\in\hom(\Gamma,\Sym(N))
be chosen uniformly at random, and define
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}\colon\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(N-1)
. We are able to push the polynomial method to cover the new phenomena arising from torsion in trace asymptotics and, importantly, verify the resulting hypotheses for free products of finite groups. This yields strong convergence of
\pi_{N}
to the left regular representation of
\Gamma
in our model.

Theorem 1.1

(strong convergence for free products of finite groups)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a free product of finite groups as in
eq.

1
. Then the random representations
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}
strongly converge to the left regular representation of
\Gamma
.

Before discussing the methods in more detail, we highlight two special cases covered by Theorem
1.1
where the limiting spectral radius can be computed explicitly, thus yielding asymptotically explicit spectral gaps for the random Schreier graphs.

Example
.

Consider
\Gamma=C_{2}*C_{3}\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
, the modular group, with generators
x
and
y
of orders
2
and
3
, respectively. Random homomorphisms
\phi_{N}
correspond to choosing a random involution and a random element of order
3
in
\Sym(N)
(compare with
[
SW25
]
, where the permutations are required to be fixed-point-free). The associated Schreier graphs are random
3
-regular graphs. By Theorem
1.1
, their second-largest eigenvalues converge with high probability to
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x+y+y^{2})\right\rVert}
. Adjacency operators in free products of two finite groups are well understood by the work of Cartwright and Soardi
[
CS86
]
. It is possible to determine their spectral measure explicitly (see the curious Figure
1
), and in particular to compute the spectral radius. In the present case, it is given by

\frac{1+\sqrt{13+8\sqrt{2}}}{2}\approx 2.9654.

This value exceeds the Ramanujan bound for
3
-regular graphs, which is
2\sqrt{2}\approx 2.8284
. Thus, we obtain a simple random model for generating non-Ramanujan
3
-regular graphs with an explicit spectral gap.

Figure 1.

C_{2}*C_{3}
. The support of the measure consists of the union of two intervals with boundary points at
(1\pm\sqrt{13+8\sqrt{2}})/2
and
(1\pm\sqrt{13-8\sqrt{2}})/2
, together with discrete atoms at
-2
and
0
, each with mass
1/6
. These atoms correspond to torsion-induced
\ell^{2}
-eigenfunctions
f
of the adjacency operator
\mathcal{A}f(g)=f(xg)+f(yg)+f(y^{2}g)
. Imposing that
f
has sum zero on each
C_{3}
-triangle
\{g,yg,y^{2}g\}
reduces the eigenvalue equation
\mathcal{A}f=\lambda f
to
f(xg)=(\lambda+1)f(g)
, and the relation
x^{2}=1
forces
\lambda+1=\pm 1
.

Example
.

Consider
\Gamma=C_{2}*C_{2}*\cdots*C_{2}
with
d\geq 3
copies of
C_{2}
. Let
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d}
denote the corresponding generators of order
2
. The Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with respect to these generators is the
d
-regular tree, whose adjacency operator has spectral radius
2\sqrt{d-1}
. By Theorem
1.1
, random Schreier graphs generated by
d
uniformly random involutions in
\Sym(N)
(allowing fixed points) are almost Ramanujan with high probability as
N\to\infty
. These graphs can be interpreted as unions of
d
random matchings, which need not be perfect (compare with
[
BC19
]
). More generally, one can analyze settings where the adjacency operator on
\Gamma
is weighted by assigning different weights to the generators
x_{i}
. In such cases, the spectral radius can be computed in closed form in terms of these weights and
d
(see
[
AK88
, Example 1]
).

The case when
\Gamma=C_{2}*C_{2}
is rather different. This group is amenable, implying that its adjacency operator has spectral radius
2
. The corresponding random Schreier graphs are generated from two random involutions, so they are unions of cycles on
N
vertices. With high probability as
N\to\infty
, we get a disconnected graph, hence
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}=2
. Strong convergence here comes for free.

Except for
C_{2}*C_{2}
, none of the groups in
eq.

1
is amenable. By selecting a symmetric generating set
S
, the corresponding adjacency operator
\sum_{s\in S}s
has a norm strictly less than
|S|
. As a direct consequence of strong convergence, this implies that, with high probability as
N\to\infty
, the random Schreier graphs in this model are connected. Equivalently, a random homomorphism
\phi_{N}\colon\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
has a transitive image with high probability. This phenomenon is well-known for groups as in
eq.

1
and even for broader families, as proved by Liebeck and Shalev
[
LS04
, Theorem 1.12]
. Their methods for proving transitivity rely on character bounds for
\Sym(N)
and an analysis of the associated representation zeta function.
Strong convergence provides a complementary spectral perspective on these results.
2
2

2

Using the classification of finite simple groups, Liebeck and Shalev further strengthen this, showing that
\langle\image\phi_{N}\rangle\geq\Alt(N)
with high probability as
N\to\infty
. The specific models considered in our examples were treated earlier in
[
LS96
]
. Circumventing the use of classification via spectral methods would require dealing with all irreducible representations of
\Sym(N)
instead of just
\std
.

1.4.
Methods

We now explain how the polynomial method can be pushed further to cover asymptotic expansions with fractional powers of
N
, and then how we verify the resulting requirements.

Polynomial method beyond integer exponents

We follow the framework of Magee, Puder, and van Handel
[
MPvH25
]
. Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group with a symmetric generating set
S
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
denote the word length of
\gamma
with respect to
S
. For each
N
, consider a random representation
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}
as above, and let
\ntr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))=\tr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))/N
denote the normalized trace.

We first discuss the requirement for the asymptotic expansion of
\mathbf{E}[\ntr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))]
. In
[
MPvH25
]
, the authors identify an analytic-like property of this expansion: for any specified precision
s\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
, a Laurent series truncated at
s-1
terms approximates the expected trace with an error of
O(s)^{O(s)}N^{-s}
. In our setting, we require similar control, but with
N
replaced by
N^{1/\mu}
, where
\mu
is a positive integer depending on
\Gamma
. Here is the precise formulation.

Requirement A
.

There exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, every
s\geq|\gamma|
, and every
N\geq Cs^{C}
, there exist constants
u_{k}(\gamma)\in\mathbf{R}
for all non-negative integers
k
such that
u_{0}(\gamma)=\tau(\lambda(\gamma))
and

\left|\mathbf{E}\!\left[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)\right]-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\frac{u_{k}(\gamma)}{N^{k/\mu}}\right|\leq\frac{(Cs)^{Cs}}{N^{s/\mu}}.

In the second ingredient of the polynomial method, it is crucial to control the values of
u_{k}
. Note that each
u_{k}(\gamma)
is uniquely determined by the approximation above. Moreover, since the trace can be expressed as the number of fixed points, we have
u_{k}(\gamma)=u_{k}(\gamma^{-1})
, and by conjugation invariance of the trace, the
u_{k}
are class functions. We extend the
u_{k}
linearly to
\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
. Following
[
MdlS24
]
, we say a functional
u\colon\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]\to\mathbf{C}
is
tempered at
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
if

\limsup_{p\to\infty}|u(x^{p})|^{1/p}\leq\|\lambda(x)\|.

The polynomial method of
[
CGVTvH24
]
and
[
MPvH25
]
requires temperedness of the functional
u_{1}
. In our setting, we require temperedness of all the first
\mu
functionals.

Requirement B
.

For every positive integer
k\leq\mu
, the functional
u_{k}
is tempered at all self-adjoint elements of
\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
.

Under these requirements, we can extend the polynomial method following the approach of
[
MPvH25
]
to obtain
upper
bounds on the operator norm of
\pi_{N}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
A
and
B
hold.
Then for every
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi_{N}(x)\right\rVert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}+o(1)\quad\text{with high probability as $N\to\infty$}.

The proof in fact yields a quantitative form of this bound.
3
3

3

For each fixed
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
and
\varepsilon>0
, the probability for the event
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi_{N}(x)\right\rVert}\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}+\varepsilon
decays like
\epsilon^{-O(1)}N^{-1/\mu}
as
N\to\infty
, where
\mu
is the integer from
A
. See Remark
2.4
.
To achieve strong convergence, it remains to establish an analogous
lower
bound. However, under the unique trace property (requirement C of the original polynomial method), this lower bound follows automatically from the upper bound via a general
C^{*}
-algebraic argument, as explained in
[
vH25
, Section 2.3]
. Fortunately, this property holds for many groups. By
[
BKKO17
]
, the unique trace property is equivalent to the group having trivial amenable radical, which includes every free product of finite groups as in
eq.

1
apart from the exceptional case
C_{2}*C_{2}
(which was addressed in the example above). Thus, strong convergence in
Theorem

1.1
follows once we verify the two requirements above in our setting.

Verifying
A

Let
\Gamma
be a free product of finite groups as in
eq.

1
, and let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. The first step in obtaining a suitable asymptotic expansion for
\mathbf{E}[\tr\pi_{N}(\gamma)]
is to consider the local case when
\gamma
belongs to one of the free factors
G_{i}
. In this scenario, the expected trace depends solely on the distribution of the image of
G_{i}
under a uniformly random homomorphism
G_{i}\to\Sym(N)
. For instance, if
G_{i}=C_{2}
with generator
\gamma
, then
\mathbf{E}[\tr\pi_{N}(\gamma)]=Na_{N-1}/a_{N}
, as shown in the previous example. Asymptotic expansions for the involution numbers were obtained by Moser and Wyman
[
MW55
]
. These asymptotics arise from analyzing the generating function
\exp(z+z^{2}/2)
at its dominant singularity. This was pushed much further by Müller
[
Mül97
]
, building on more general work by Harris and Schoenfeld
[
HS68
]
, who provided an asymptotic expansion for any element
\gamma
in any finite group, in particular in any free factor
G_{i}
. The result is that the expected trace
\mathbf{E}[\tr(\pi_{N}(\gamma))]
admits an expansion in powers of
N^{-1/|G_{i}|}
. However, these expansions include error terms of the form
O_{s}(N^{-s/|G_{i}|})
, which are not sufficiently strong for our purposes. In
A
, we need the implicit constant in the error to depend on
s
at most doubly exponentially. Our first job is thus to make the analysis of both
[
HS68
]
and
[
Mül97
]
more quantitative by carefully tracking the dependence of the error terms on
s
. This is the technical crux of the paper.

After dealing with the local scenario when
\gamma
belongs to a single free factor, we must handle the general case. For this, we rely on the work of Puder and Zimhoni
[
PZ24
]
, who reformulate
\mathbf{E}[\tr\pi_{N}(\gamma)]
in terms of covering spaces of a CW complex
X_{\Gamma}
with
\pi_{1}(X_{\Gamma})\cong\Gamma
. Homomorphisms
\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
are in bijective correspondence with
N
-sheeted covering spaces of
X_{\Gamma}
(see
[
Hat02
]
). The trace
\tr\phi_{N}(\gamma)
, being the number of fixed points of
\phi_{N}(\gamma)
, corresponds to the number of lifts of the cellular map
Y_{\gamma}\to X_{\Gamma}
into the covering space corresponding to
\phi_{N}
. In
[
PZ24
]
, the authors develop a systematic approach to analyze these lifts via a combinatorial decomposition of the map
Y_{\gamma}\to X_{\Gamma}
into simpler constituent maps, which are then analyzed using the local asymptotics described above. As before, we must make this analysis quantitative to ensure all dependencies are at most doubly exponential in
s
. The key observation is that the number of surjective cellular maps from the complex
Y_{\gamma}
with
O(s)
vertices and edges is at most
O(s)^{O(s)}
. By synthesizing these results, the constant
C
in
A
depends only on the sizes of the groups
G_{i}
, and the integer
\mu
is the least common multiple of their orders.

Verifying
B

Let
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
be self-adjoint. We need to control the growth of
u_{k}(x^{p})
as
p\to\infty
for each
1\leq k\leq\mu
. The key tool here is the positivization trick as developed by Magee and de la Salle
[
MdlS24
]
, which allows us to reduce the problem to elements
x
that are symmetric generators of random walks on
\Gamma
. This means that in the expansion of
x
in the group basis, the coefficients are all nonnegative, sum to
1
, and are stable under inversion. For such
x
, the powers
x^{p}
can be expressed in terms of hitting probabilities
\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\dots X_{p}=\gamma)
of the associated random walk on
\Gamma
after
p
steps. At this point, specific group-theoretic properties of
\Gamma
come into play. The hitting probabilities depend strongly on whether
\gamma
is a torsion element or not, and in the latter case, whether
\gamma
is a proper power or contained in a subgroup isomorphic to
C_{2}*C_{2}
. We address each scenario separately, using explicit expressions for
u_{0}(\gamma)
derived from
[
PZ24
]
. We then apply a version of Friedman’s spectral trick from
[
vH25
,
MPvH25
]
to sum over all
\gamma
, making it possible to control the hitting probability of landing in a specific scenario among those described above. Ultimately, this controls the growth of
u_{k}(x^{p})
.

Remark
.

The route to
A
is in many ways specific to the symmetric group, since the fixed-point statistics of words can be analyzed very explicitly and combinatorially using the machinery of covering spaces. It would be interesting to develop analogues for other families of finite groups and their natural permutation actions, such as
\PSL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
acting on the projective line
\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. The main challenge is that one must restrict to covering spaces whose monodromy groups are subgroups of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Nevertheless, computational experiments suggest that the spectral norm
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}}\right\rVert}
in Schreier graphs arising from
\Gamma
and random homomorphisms into
\PSL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
instead of the symmetric group approaches the corresponding spectral radius of
\Gamma
for both the free group
\mathbf{F}_{2}
and the free product
C_{2}*C_{3}
.

shows how Requirements

2.
Assumptions imply strong convergence

The basic principle used in the polynomial method is an inequality due to Markov (see
[
vH25
, Lemma 3.6]
). We will use the following more elaborate version of this inequality.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 4.2 in
[
MdlS24
]
)
.

For every real polynomial
P
of degree at most
q
and every positive integer
k
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,1/(2q^{2})]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert P^{(k)}(t)\right\rvert}\leq\frac{2^{2k+1}q^{4k}}{(2k-1)!!}\sup_{n\geq q^{2}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert P(1/n)\right\rvert}.

Our next goal is extending the approximation of
\mathbf{E}[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)]
as given in
A
to an analogous approximation for
\mathbf{E}[\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))]
for
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
. In the polynomial method, this is called the
master inequality

[
vH25
, Corollary 3.9]
. Our proof here mimics that of
[
MPvH25
, Lemma 3.1]
. The points of divergence are the polynomial
f_{h}
in
eq.

3
, its evaluation at
N^{-1/\mu}
in
eq.

5
, and the fact that we need to apply Lemma
2.1
to a considerably higher order of derivative in
eq.

6
.

Once and for all, fix a symmetric generating set
S
of
\Gamma
. For
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
, let
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
be the maximum word length of a group element in the support of
x
, and let
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)}
be the supremum of
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi(x)\right\rVert}
over all unitary representations
\pi
of
\Gamma
. For a polynomial
h
, we denote
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{I}=\sup_{t\in I}|h(t)|
.

Proposition 2.2

(master inequality)
.

Suppose
A
holds with constant
C
. Let
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
be self-adjoint with
K=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)}
. Then for every positive integer
N
, every real polynomial
h
of degree at most
q
, and every positive integer
k\leq\mu
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u_{k}(h(x))\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}\left(q^{O_{\mu,C}(1)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}\right),

and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))}\right]-\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\frac{u_{k}(h(x))}{N^{k/\mu}}\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}\left(\frac{q^{O_{\mu,C}(1)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}}{N^{1+1/\mu}}\right).

Proof.

In case
x=0
or
h
is of degree
0
, we have
u_{k}(h(x))=0
and both inequalities hold trivially. Assume therefore that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\geq 1
and
q\geq 1
. Write

h(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\alpha_{\gamma}\gamma\quad(\alpha_{\gamma}\in\mathbf{C}).

The maximum length of a monomial in
h(x)
does not exceed
q\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
. Thus the number of nonzero coefficients
\alpha_{\gamma}
is at most
(2\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert}+1)^{q|x|}\leq(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{q|x|}
. Apply
A
separately to each element of the support of
h(x)
with order of approximation
q\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}+\mu+1\leq 3\mu q\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
. As long as
N\geq C(3\mu q\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert})^{C}
, we thus obtain

(2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))}\right]-f_{h}(N^{-1/\mu})\right\rvert}\leq\frac{(3\mu Cq|x|)^{3\mu Cq|x|}}{N^{(q|x|+\mu+1)/\mu}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{\gamma}\right\rvert},

where we have denoted

(3)

f_{h}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{q|x|+\mu}u_{k}(h(x))t^{k}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{q|x|+\mu}\alpha_{\gamma}u_{k}(\gamma)t^{k}.

Note that since
h(x)
is self-adjoint and
u_{k}(\gamma)=u_{k}(\gamma^{-1})
, the coefficients
u_{k}(h(x))
are real numbers. Thus
f_{h}
is a real polynomial. The sum of the coefficients
\alpha_{\gamma}
in
eq.

2
runs over a set of at most
(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{q|x|}
elements. By Cauchy-Schwarz we thus have

(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{-q|x|/2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h(x)\right\rVert}_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h(\lambda(x))\delta_{e}\right\rVert}_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]},

where we have used functional calculus and the assumption
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)}\leq K
in the last inequality. It now follows that, as long as
N\geq(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{1/2}(3\mu Cq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert})^{3\mu C}
, we have

(4)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))}\right]-f_{h}(N^{-1/\mu})\right\rvert}\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}}{N^{1+1/\mu}}.

Since
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi_{N}(x)\right\rVert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{C_{\mathrm{red}}^{\ast}(\Gamma)}=K
, the expectation
\mathbf{E}\left[\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))\right]
is at most
K/N
in absolute value. Thus by functional calculus and
eq.

4
, we obtain

(5)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f_{h}(N^{-1/\mu})\right\rvert}\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}.

Now, we are in a position to estimate the derivatives of
f_{h}
. Apply Lemma
2.1
to the real polynomial
f_{h}
of degree at most
(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{1/4}(3\mu Cq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert})^{3\mu C/2}
to estimate

(6)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{h}^{(k)}\right\rVert}_{\left[0,1/(2(3r)^{1/2}(3\mu Cq|x|)^{3\mu C})\right]}\leq\frac{4^{k+1}\cdot(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{k}(3\mu Cq|x|)^{6\mu Ck}}{(2k-1)!!}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}.

For every integer
1\leq k\leq\mu
, we have
u_{k}(h(x))=f_{h}^{(k)}(0)/k!
. The first part of the statement then follows immediately from
eq.

6
.

Using the same bounds, we can also deduce the second part of the statement. First of all, in the regime when
N\geq(2(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{1/2}(3\mu Cq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert})^{3\mu C})^{\mu}
, Taylor expanding
f_{h}
at
0
up to degree
\mu
gives

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f_{h}(N^{-1/\mu})-\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\frac{u_{k}(h(x))}{N^{k/\mu}}\right\rvert}&\leq\frac{1}{(\mu+1)!N^{1+\frac{1}{\mu}}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{h}^{(\mu+1)}\right\rVert}_{[0,1/N^{-1/\mu}]}\\
&\leq O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}\left(\frac{q^{O_{\mu,C}(1)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}}{N^{1+1/\mu}}\right).\end{split}

The second part of the statement in this regime thus follows from
eq.

4
by triangle inequality.
On the other hand, in the regime when
N<(2(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert})^{1/2}(3\mu Cq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert})^{3\mu C})^{\mu}=O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}(q^{O_{\mu,C}(1)})
, we can directly estimate, using
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u_{0}(h(x))\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tau(h(\lambda(x)))\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}
,

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{n}(x))}\right]-\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\frac{u_{k}(h(x))}{N^{k/\mu}}\right\rvert}&\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}+\sum_{k=1}^{\mu}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u_{k}(h(x))\right\rvert}}{N^{k/\mu}}\\
&\leq O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}\left(\frac{q^{O_{\mu,C}(1)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert h\right\rVert}_{[-K,K]}}{N^{1+1/\mu}}\right).\end{split}

This establishes the second part of the statement in this regime as well. The proof is complete.
∎

From here on, we follow the usual steps of the polynomial method. Let us first extend Proposition
2.2
to all smooth functions
h
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
A
holds with constant
C
. Let

be self-adjoint with

.

1.

For all positive integers

, the functional

extends from a polynomial domain to a compactly supported distribution on

.

2.

For all positive integers

and all

, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))}\right]-\tau(h(\lambda(x)))-\sum_{k=1}^{\mu}\frac{\nu_{k}(h)}{N^{k/\mu}}\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}\left(\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{(\omega)}\right\rVert}_{[0,2\pi]}}{N^{1+\frac{1}{\mu}}}\right),

where
\omega

and

, and

.

Proof.

The first portion of the statement about extending

and
[

, Lemma 4.7]
. For the second one, we assume first that

is a polynomial of degree

. Expand

Applying the second part of Proposition

separately to each

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[{\ntr h(\pi_{N}(x))}\right]-\tau(h(\lambda(x)))-\sum_{k=1}^{\mu}\frac{u_{k}(h(x))}{N^{k/\mu}}\right\rvert}\leq\frac{O_{\mu,|S|,C,|x|}(1)}{N^{1+1/\mu}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\ell^{O_{\mu,C}(1)}|a_{\ell}|.

The second part of the statement for

[
MPvH25
, Lemma 2.3]
. Note that the resulting bound does not depend

∎

We are now ready to conclude the proof of Theorem

Suppose first that

3.
Asymptotics for exponentials of polynomials

Let
P(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}c_{i}z^{i}
be a polynomial of degree
q\geq 2
with real coefficients
c_{i}
satisfying

(7)

c_{i}\geq 0\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq q,\qquad c_{1}=1,\qquad c_{q}=1/q,\qquad c_{i}=0\quad\text{for }i\nmid q.

Consider the entire function
\exp(P(z))
, and let its Taylor expansion at
0
be

\exp(P(z))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}.

Müller
[
Mül97
]
provides explicit formulas, in terms of the coefficients of

, for the asymptotic expansion of the Taylor coefficients
\alpha_{n}
as

. We recall these formulas here, and make explicit all dependencies that we require for our application.

3.1.
Quantitative asymptotics in terms of
r_{n}

Müller’s formulas build on more general results of Harris and Schoenfeld
[
HS68
]
for generating functions

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}

that are analytic in a neighborhood of the origin and satisfy the conditions (A)–(E) listed below.

1.

The function
f(z)
is analytic for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}<R
for some
R\in(0,\infty]
and is real for real
z
.

2.

There exists
R_{0}\in(0,R)
and a real-valued function
d(r)
defined on
(R_{0},R)
such that, for any
r\in(R_{0},R)
,
0<d(r)<1
and
r(1+d(r))<R
. Moreover,
f(z)\neq 0
for each
z
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z-r\right\rvert}\leq rd(r)
and every
r\in(R_{0},R)
.

3.

For
k\geq 1
, define

A(z)=f^{\prime}(z)/f(z),\quad B_{k}(z)=z^{k}A^{(k-1)}(z)/k!,\quad B(z)=zB_{1}^{\prime}(z)/2.

Then
B(r)>0
for
R_{0}<r<R
and
B_{1}(r)\to\infty
as
r\to R^{-}
.

4.

Let
R_{1}\in(R_{0},R)
be such that
B_{1}(R_{1})>0
. For any integer
n\geq B_{1}(R_{1})
, let
r_{n}
be the unique solution of the equation
B_{1}(r)=n+1
with
r\in(R_{1},R)
. For
j\geq 1
, define

C_{j}(r)=-(B_{j+2}(r)+(-1)^{j}B_{1}(r)/(j+2))/B(r).

Suppose that there exist non-negative
D_{n},E_{n}
and
n_{1}>0
such that the inequality
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert C_{j}(r_{n})\right\rvert}\leq E_{n}D_{n}^{j}
holds for all
n\geq n_{1}
and
j\geq 1
.

5.

As
n\to\infty
, we have

B(r_{n})d(r_{n})^{2}\to\infty,\quad D_{n}E_{n}B(r_{n})d(r_{n})^{3}\to 0,\quad D_{n}d(r_{n})\to 0.

Harris and Schoenfeld show that under these conditions, the coefficients
\alpha_{n}
have an asymptotic expansion as

of the following form.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
HS68
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Suppose that
f(z)
satisfies conditions
(A)–(E)
. Then for all
N\geq 0
, there exist constants

such that for all

, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\alpha_{n}}{f(r_{n})/(2r_{n}^{n}\sqrt{\pi B(r_{n})})}-1-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{F_{k}(n)}{B(r_{n})^{k}}\right\rvert}\leq L_{N}\cdot\varphi_{N}(n),

where

•

F_{k}(n)
are explicitly computable in terms of
C_{j}(r_{n})
for
1\leq j\leq 2k
,

•

\varphi_{N}(n)
is explicitly computable as a function of
D_{n},E_{n},B(r_{n}),d(r_{n})
, and the maximum value of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(z)\right\rvert}
for
z
on some curve depending on
r_{n},d(r_{n})
.

Müller verifies that
\exp(P(z))
for
P(z)
as above meets the conditions (A)–(E) by explicit verification. All implicit dependencies are absolute, and all dependencies on the polynomial
P(z)
only are denoted as

or

.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Mül97
]
, Lemma 1)

Let

be a polynomial satisfying

7
. Then the function

(A)–(E)
with:

Take
R=\infty
.

(B)

Take

to be the maximum of
1
and the largest real root of

.

(D)

Take

,

, and

.

(E)

Take

.

For our application, we also need explicit bounds on

,

]

[

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a polynomial satisfying

\varphi_{N}(n)\ll O_{P}(1)^{N+1}n^{-N-1},\quad K_{N}=O_{P}(1)N^{O_{P}(1)},\quad L_{N}=(O_{P}(1)N)^{O(1)N}.

Proof.

Let us first deal with

\varphi_{N}(n)=\max\left\{X(n),Y(n),Z(n)\right\},

3.2.1.
Expanding
r_{n}

Define the polynomial

Q(z)=z^{q-1}P^{\prime}(1/z)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}ic_{i}z^{q-i}

with
Q(0)=1
. Let
\phi(z)=Q(z)^{1/q}
. This is an analytic function on a disc centered at
0
with
\phi(0)=1
. Thus the function
z\mapsto z/\phi(z)
is also analytic with derivative
1
at
z=0
, and hence it is invertible in a neighborhood of
0
. In particular, there are
0<\epsilon,\delta<1
such that for every
w
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert}<\epsilon
, the equation
w\phi(z)=z
has a unique solution in the domain
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}<\delta
. Moreover, by Lagrange inversion, this solution can be expressed as a power series

z=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{\nu}}{\nu}w^{\nu},

where the
\beta_{\nu}
can be computed explicitly in terms of
P
(see
[
Mül97
, formula (17)]
).

Let us use the above with
w=(n+1)^{-1/q}
and write
z=r^{-1}
. For
n\gg_{\epsilon}1
, the equation
rP^{\prime}(r)=n+1
therefore has a unique solution
r_{n}
in the domain
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert r\right\rvert}>1/\delta
, and this solution is given by

\frac{1}{r_{n}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{\nu}}{\nu}(n+1)^{-\nu/q}=n^{-1/q}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}n^{-i/q}\right),

where we obtained the coefficients
\gamma_{i}
as in
[
Mül97
, formula (24)]
by expanding
(n+1)^{-\nu/q}=n^{-\nu/q}(1+n^{-1})^{-\nu/q}
. Thus

r_{n}=n^{1/q}\left(1+G(n^{-1/q})\right)^{-1},\qquad G(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}w^{i}.

The function
G(w)
is also analytic on the disc centered at
0
with radius
\epsilon
. By Cauchy’s estimate, it follows that the coefficients
\gamma_{i}
satisfy
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma_{i}\right\rvert}\leq O_{P}(1)\epsilon^{-i}
.

3.2.2.
Approximating
r_{n}
by
\rho_{n}(s)

For
s\geq 1
, let

\rho_{n}(s)=n^{1/q}\left(1+G_{s}(n^{-1/q})\right)^{-1},\qquad G_{s}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{q+s-1}\gamma_{i}w^{i}.

Thus
G_{s}
is the truncation of
G
to its first
q+s-1
terms. The value
\rho_{n}(s)
is well defined for
n\gg_{P}1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
s\geq 1
and all
n\gg_{P}1
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert r_{n}-\rho_{n}(s)\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-(q+s-1)/q}.

Proof.

Observe that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert G(n^{-1/q})-G_{s}(n^{-1/q})\right\rvert}\leq O_{P}(1)n^{-(q+s)/q}\sum_{i=q+s}^{\infty}\epsilon^{-i}n^{-(i-q-s)/q}.

Since
n^{-1/q}<\epsilon/2
for
n\gg_{P}1
, the above is at most

O_{P}(1)(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-(q+s)/q}.

In particular, since we have the
a priori
bound

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert G(n^{-1/q})\right\rvert}\ll_{P}n^{-1/q},

it follows that for
n\gg_{P}1
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert G_{s}(n^{-1/q})\right\rvert}\ll_{P}n^{-1/q}.

Now, the function
x\mapsto(1+x)^{-1}
is Lipschitz for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}<\delta
with constant
1/(1-\delta)^{2}
. Thus, for
n\gg_{P}1
, we obtain

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert r_{n}-\rho_{n}(s)\right\rvert}\leq\frac{n^{1/q}}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\cdot O_{P}(1)(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-(q+s)/q}=O_{P}(1)(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-(q+s-1)/q}.\qed

Lemma 3.8
.

For all

and

,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\mathcal{M}(r_{n})}{\mathcal{M}(\rho_{n}(s))}-1\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-s/q}.

Proof.

Define

Let

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Psi(r_{n})-\Psi(\rho_{n}(s))\right\rvert}\leq\sup_{r\in I_{n,s}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Psi^{\prime}(r)\right\rvert}\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lvert r_{n}-\rho_{n}(s)\right\rvert}.

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Psi(r_{n})-\Psi(\rho_{n}(s))\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(\epsilon/2)^{-s}n^{-s/q}.

Lemma 3.9

For all

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert P_{k}(r)\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(O(1)k)^{O(1)k}r^{2kq},\quad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert P^{\prime}_{k}(r)\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(O(1)k)^{O(1)k}r^{2kq-1}.

As long as

Quantitative asymptotics in terms of

We are now ready to perform the final step of converting the asymptotic expansion from the previous section into an asymptotic expansion in terms of

alone. Following

depending only on the polynomial

from

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
u
be a complex variable, and let

\rho(u)=u^{-1}(1+G_{s}(u))^{-1},\quad X_{s}(u)=\frac{\mathcal{M}(\rho(u))}{\Pref(u)},\quad Y_{s}(u)=\mathcal{S}_{\lceil s/q\rceil-1}(\rho(u)).

There exist
\epsilon>0
and
M>0
depending only on
P
such that for every
s\geq 1
:

1.

X_{s}
is holomorphic on
0<|u|<\epsilon
with

\sup_{0<|u|<\epsilon}|X_{s}(u)|\leq M;

2.

Y_{s}
is holomorphic on
0<|u|<\epsilon
with

\sup_{0<|u|<\epsilon}|Y_{s}(u)|\leq(Ms)^{Ms};

3.

Both
X_{s}
and
Y_{s}
extend holomorphically to
|u|<\epsilon
with
X_{s}(0)=Y_{s}(0)=1
.

Proof.

Taking logarithms, we obtain

\big|\log X_{s}(u)\big|\ll\big|P(\rho(u))-P(u^{-1})-n\big(\log\rho(u)+\log u\big)\big|+\big|\log B(\rho(u))+q\log u\big|.

Since
\log\rho(u)=-\log u-\log(1+G_{s}(u))
and
G_{s}
is analytic on
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
with
G_{s}(0)=0
, the product
\rho(u)u
is bounded by
O_{P}(1)
on that disc. As
B
is a polynomial of degree
q
with leading coefficient
q/2
, the quotient
B(\rho(u))/u^{-q}
is bounded by
O_{P}(1)
(from above and below) as well. Hence
\log B(\rho(u))=-q\log u+O_{P}(1)
and the second summand is bounded by
O_{P}(1)
for
|u|<\epsilon
.

It remains to bound the first summand. By
[
Mül97
, Section 3.6]
the coefficients
\gamma_{i}
of
G_{s}(u)
vanish for
i<q/2
, and hence we may write
G_{s}(u)=u^{q/2}\Delta_{s}(u)
with
\Delta_{s}
analytic on
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
and bounded by
O_{P}(1)
there. Expanding,

(9)

P(\rho(u))-P(u^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{q}c_{i}u^{-i}\big[(1+G_{s}(u))^{-i}-1\big].

Since
G_{s}(u)=O_{P}(u^{q/2})
on the disc, we obtain, by Taylor expansion,

(1+G_{s}(u))^{-i}-1=-iG_{s}(u)+O_{P}(G_{s}(u)^{2})=O_{P}(u^{q/2}).

For
i<q
any nonzero contribution in
eq.

9
can occur only when
i\leq q/2
. Then

u^{-i}O_{P}(u^{q/2})=O_{P}\big(u^{q/2-i}\big),

which is uniformly bounded on
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
because the exponent is non-negative. For the top degree
i=q
, we must treat the terms more carefully. Using
c_{q}=1/q
and
u^{q}=n^{-1}
, the relevant part reduces to

c_{q}n\big((1+G_{s}(u))^{-q}-1\big)+n\log(1+G_{s}(u)).

Expanding the power as before and the logarithm for small
G_{s}(u)
(shrinking
\epsilon
if necessary so that
|G_{s}(u)|\ll 1/q
), we get

c_{q}n\big(-qG_{s}(u)+O_{P}(G_{s}(u)^{2})\big)+n\big(G_{s}(u)+O_{P}(G_{s}(u)^{2})\big).

The linear terms cancel, because
c_{q}=1/q
, leaving a remainder of order
nG_{s}(u)^{2}
. Since
G_{s}(u)=O_{P}(u^{q/2})
, it follows that
nG_{s}(u)^{2}=O_{P}(nu^{q})=O_{P}(1)
.

Combining the bounds above shows that
\log X_{s}(u)
is bounded by
O_{P}(1)
on
0<|u|<\epsilon
, so each
X_{s}
is holomorphic and bounded there. Therefore
X_{s}
extends holomorphically across
0
by the Riemann removable singularity theorem with the value
X_{s}(0)=1
, as asserted in
[
Mül97
, Section 3.6]
.

The argument for
Y_{s}
is simpler. By definition,
Y_{s}(u)
is a sum of terms of the form
P_{k}(\rho(u))/B(\rho(u))^{3k}
for
k\leq\lceil s/q\rceil-1
. On the disc
|u|<\epsilon
, the functions
\rho(u)u
and
B(\rho(u))/u^{-q}
are bounded by
O_{P}(1)
, as shown above. Each
P_{k}(\rho(u))
is bounded by
(O(1)k)^{O(1)k}\rho(u)^{2kq}
by
eq.

8
. Thus each summand is bounded by

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{P_{k}(\rho(u))}{B(\rho(u))^{3k}}\right\rvert}\ll_{P}(O(1)k)^{O(1)k}u^{kq}

on the disc of radius
\epsilon
. Summing over
k
yields the bound
(O_{P}(1)s)^{O(1)s}
. As every nonconstant summand vanishes in
0
, the extended value at
0
is
Y_{s}(0)=1
.
∎

Theorem 3.12

(quantitative form of
[
Mül97
]
, Theorem 2)
.

For all
s\geq 1
and

\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\Pref_{n}}=1+\sum_{\nu=1}^{s-1}\mathcal{C}_{\nu}n^{-\nu/q}+R_{n,s},

where the coefficients
\mathcal{C}_{\nu}
are defined as in
[
Mül97
, formula (25)–(27)]

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert R_{n,s}\right\rvert}\leq O_{P}(s)^{O_{P}(s)}n^{-s/q}.

Proof.

Let
Z_{s}(u)=X_{s}(u)Y_{s}(u)
. By the previous lemma,
Z_{s}
is holomorphic on
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
and bounded by
(O_{P}(1)s)^{O(1)s}
there. Thus
Z_{s}
has a Taylor expansion

Z_{s}(u)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}c_{s,\nu}u^{\nu}

on the disc
\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}<\epsilon\}
with
c_{s,0}=1
. Moreover, for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}<\epsilon/2
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z_{s}(u)-\sum_{\nu=0}^{s-1}c_{s,\nu}u^{\nu}\right\rvert}\leq\frac{\sup_{0<|u|<\epsilon}|Z_{s}(u)|}{1-|u|/\epsilon}(|u|/\epsilon)^{s}\leq(O_{P}(1)s)^{O(1)s}|u|^{s}.

Set
u=n^{-1/q}
, and let
n\gg_{P}s^{O_{P}(1)}
. By Corollary
3.10
, we have

\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\Pref_{n}}=Z_{s}(n^{-1/q})+X_{s}(n^{-1/q})E_{n,s}.

Since
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{n,s}\right\rvert}\leq(O_{P}(1)s)^{O(1)s}n^{-s/q}
, the error term
E_{n,s}
and the tail of the Taylor expansion of
Z_{s}(n^{-1/q})
are of the same order, while
X_{s}
is bounded for
n\gg_{P}1
. Hence

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\Pref_{n}}-\sum_{\nu=0}^{s-1}c_{s,\nu}n^{-\nu/q}\right\rvert}\ll(O_{P}(1)s)^{O(1)s}n^{-s/q}.

Since this expansion holds for every
s
with error
O_{P,s}(n^{-s/q})
, the coefficients
c_{s,\nu}
must in fact be independent of
s
, and they must equal the coefficients
\mathcal{C}_{\nu}
from
[
Mül97
, formula (25)–(27)]
.
∎

4.
Fractional expansions for the number of group actions

Let
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
be a sequence of real numbers. We say that
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
admits a
fractional expansion with parameters

q,A,B,C,D,E\geq 1
, in symbols
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
, if there exist real coefficients
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
with zeroth coefficient
b_{0}=1
such that, for all
s\geq 1
and for all
n\geq As^{B}
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\beta_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}n^{-k/q}\right\rvert}\leq C(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}.

Note that
A\geq a
is required for this definition to make sense.

A crucial consequence of
Theorem

3.12
is that the sequence of normalized coefficients
(\alpha_{n}/\Pref_{n})_{n\geq 0}
admits
\FE_{q}
with all parameters
O_{P}(1)
. We will now establish a series of lemmas showing that the class of sequences admitting
\FE_{q}
is closed under various operations. This will allow us to transform the sequence
(\alpha_{n}/\Pref_{n})_{n\geq 0}
into the sequence required for verifying
A
.

4.1.
Operations with asymptotic expansions

Lemma 4.1

(coefficients bound)
.

Let the sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
admit
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
with coefficients
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
, the coefficients satisfy

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(2Dk)^{2Ek}.

Proof.

Take
n\geq A(k+1)^{B}
. Apply the fractional expansion estimate with
s=k
and
s=k+1
, and subtract them to obtain

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}n^{-k/q}\leq C(Dk)^{Ek}n^{-k/q}+C(D(k+1))^{E(k+1)}n^{-(k+1)/q}.

Multiplying by
n^{k/q}
gives
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(2Dk)^{2Ek}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(closure properties of
\FE
: product)
.

Let the sequences
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a_{1}}
and
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq a_{2}}
admit
\FE_{q}(A_{1},B_{1},C_{1},D_{1},E_{1})
and
\FE_{q}(A_{2},B_{2},C_{2},D_{2},E_{2})
, respectively. Then the product sequence
(\beta_{n}\gamma_{n})_{n\geq\max(a_{1},a_{2})}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(\max(A_{1},A_{2}),\,\max(B_{1},B_{2}),\,2C_{1}C_{2},\,2\max(D_{1},D_{2}),\,18\max(E_{1},E_{2})\right).

Proof.

Assume that
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a_{1}}
and
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq a_{2}}
admit a fractional expansion with coefficients
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(c_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, respectively. Set

A=\max(A_{1},A_{2}),\quad B=\max(B_{1},B_{2}),\quad D=\max(D_{1},D_{2}),\quad E=\max(E_{1},E_{2}).

For
n\geq As^{B}
, write

\beta_{n}=M_{\beta}+E_{\beta},\quad\gamma_{n}=M_{\gamma}+E_{\gamma},

where

M_{\beta}=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}b_{i}n^{-i/q},\quad M_{\gamma}=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}c_{i}n^{-i/q},\quad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{\beta}\right\rvert},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq\max(C_{1},C_{2})(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}.

By
Lemma

4.1
, for every
i\leq s-1
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{i}\right\rvert},\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert}\leq 2\max(C_{1},C_{2})(2Ds)^{2Es}.

Hence

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{\beta}\right\rvert},\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq 2\max(C_{1},C_{2})s(2Ds)^{2Es}.

Note that

\beta_{n}\gamma_{n}=M_{\beta}M_{\gamma}+E_{\beta}M_{\gamma}+E_{\gamma}M_{\beta}+E_{\beta}E_{\gamma}.

The sum of the last three terms is at most

3\cdot C_{1}C_{2}\cdot(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}\cdot 2s(2Ds)^{2Es}\leq 3C_{1}C_{2}(2Ds)^{4Es}n^{-s/q}.

For the main term, set

d_{k}=\sum_{i+j=k}b_{i}c_{j}.

Note that
d_{0}=b_{0}c_{0}=1
, and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert d_{k}\right\rvert}\leq(k+1)4C_{1}C_{2}(2Dk)^{4Ek}.

Then

M_{\beta}M_{\gamma}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}d_{k}n^{-k/q}+\sum_{k=s}^{2s-2}d_{k}n^{-k/q},

where the second sum is at most

s\cdot 8sC_{1}C_{2}(4Ds)^{8Es}\cdot n^{-s/q}\leq 2C_{1}C_{2}(2Ds)^{18Es}n^{-s/q}.

Thus,
\beta_{n}\gamma_{n}
admits
\FE_{q}(A,B,2C_{1}C_{2},2D,18E)
with coefficients
(d_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(closure properties of
\FE
: reciprocal)
.

Let the sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
admit
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
. Suppose that
\beta_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq a
. Then the reciprocal sequence
(1/\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(\max(A,(2CD^{E})^{q}),\,B,\,6,\,8CD,\,6E\right).

Proof.

Let
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the fractional expansion coefficients of
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.
Applying
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
with
s=1
gives, for
n\geq A
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\beta_{n}-1\right\rvert}\leq CD^{E}n^{-1/q}.

Hence, if
n\geq(2CD^{E})^{q}
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\beta_{n}\right\rvert}\geq 1/2
. Recursively define the coefficients
(e_{k})_{k\geq 0}
by
e_{0}=1
and, for
k\geq 1
,

e_{k}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}e_{k-j}.

For
s\geq 1
, set

B_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}x^{k},\qquad E_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}e_{k}x^{k}.

It follows that
E_{s}(x)B_{s}(x)=1+x^{s}Q_{s}(x)
for some polynomial
Q_{s}
of degree at most
s-2
. Our aim is now to bound the sizes of these polynomials at
x=n^{-1/q}
.

Step 1: bounds on
e_{k}
.

Let
k\geq 1
. By
Lemma

4.1
applied to
(\beta_{n})
, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(2Dk)^{2Ek}
. We claim that, for
k\geq 1
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{k}\right\rvert}\leq(8CDk)^{2Ek}.

Indeed, for
k=1
, this holds because

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{1}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{1}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(2D)^{2E}\leq(8CD)^{2E}.

For
k\geq 2
, bound
e_{k}
using the recursion as

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{k}\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{0}\right\rvert}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{j}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{k-j}\right\rvert}.

The first term can be bounded by the coefficient bound lemma as

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(2Dk)^{2Ek}\leq\frac{(8CDk)^{2Ek}}{2}.

The sum can be bounded using the inductive hypothesis by

\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}2C(2Dj)^{2Ej}\cdot(8CD(k-j))^{2E(k-j)}.

Using
2C\leq(2C)^{2Ej}
and
8CD(k-j)\leq 8CDk
, this is at most

(8CDk)^{2Ek}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{j}{2k}\right)^{2Ej}\leq\frac{(8CDk)^{2Ek}}{2}.

Hence
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{k}\right\rvert}\leq(8CDk)^{2Ek}
, as claimed.

Step 2: bounds on
B_{s},E_{s},Q_{s}
.

Let
x=n^{-1/q}\leq 1
. Then

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert B_{s}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq s\cdot 2C(2Ds)^{2Es}\leq(8CDs)^{3Es},

and similarly

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{s}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e_{k}\right\rvert}\leq 1+s\cdot(8CDs)^{2Es}\leq(8CDs)^{3Es}.

Since
E_{s}(x)B_{s}(x)=1+x^{s}Q_{s}(x)
, we obtain

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x^{s}Q_{s}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{s}(x)B_{s}(x)\right\rvert}+1\leq 2(8CDs)^{6Es}.

Step 3: approximation of
1/\beta_{n}
.

Apply
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
at order
s
to get, for
n\geq As^{B}
,

\beta_{n}=B_{s}(n^{-1/q})+F_{s},\qquad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F_{s}\right\rvert}\leq C(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}.

This yields

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\beta_{n}E_{s}(n^{-1/q})-1\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert B_{s}(n^{-1/q})E_{s}(n^{-1/q})-1\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F_{s}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{s}(n^{-1/q})\right\rvert}.

The first term is at most
n^{-s/q}|Q_{s}(n^{-1/q})|\leq 2(8CDs)^{6Es}n^{-s/q}
, while the second term is at most

C(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}\cdot(8CDs)^{3Es}\leq(8CDs)^{4Es}n^{-s/q}.

Finally, since
|\beta_{n}|\geq 1/2
for
n\geq\max\big(A,(2CD^{E})^{q}\big)
, we obtain

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}-E_{s}(n^{-1/q})\right\rvert}=\frac{|\beta_{n}E_{s}(n^{-1/q})-1|}{|\beta_{n}|}\leq 6(8CDs)^{6Es}n^{-s/q}

This yields

\FE_{q}(\max(A,(2CD^{E})^{q}),B,6,8CD,6E)

for
(1/\beta_{n})
with coefficients
(e_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(closure properties of
\FE
: shift)
.

Let the sequence
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq a}
admit
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
, and let
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}
with
\ell\neq 0
. Then the shifted sequence
(\beta_{n+\ell})_{n\geq\max(a-\ell,0)}

admits

\FE_{q}\left(2\max(A,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert}),\,B,\,4C,\,2D\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert},\,5E\right).

Proof.

Let
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the fractional expansion coefficients of
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. Suppose that
n\geq\max(2A,2\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell\right\rvert})s^{B}
, so that
n+\ell\geq n/2\geq As^{B}
. In particular,
n+\ell\geq 0
, and we may apply
\FE_{q}(A,B,C,D,E)
at index
n+\ell
. Taking into account that
(n+\ell)^{-s/q}\leq 2^{s/q}n^{-s/q}
, we obtain

\beta_{n+\ell}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}(n+\ell)^{-k/q}+X,\qquad|X|\leq C(Ds)^{Es}(n+\ell)^{-s/q}\leq 2C(Ds)^{2Es}n^{-s/q}.

Write

(n+\ell)^{-k/q}=n^{-k/q}\left(1+\frac{\ell}{n}\right)^{-k/q}.

Let
u=\ell/n
, so that
|u|\leq 1/2
. We can expand the second factor as a power series in
u
up to term
J=\lceil(s-1-k)/q\rceil
as

(1+u)^{-k/q}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\binom{-k/q}{j}u^{j}+R_{J}(u).

Using

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\binom{-k/q}{j}\right\rvert}\leq(e(k+1))^{j}

and
k<s
, we obtain

|R_{J}(u)|\leq\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}(es|u|)^{j}\leq 2(es|u|)^{J+1},

where we have used that
n\geq 2|\ell|s
and hence
es|u|\leq 1/2
. Therefore,

|R_{J}(\ell/n)|\leq 2(3s)^{J+1}\left(\frac{|\ell|}{n}\right)^{J+1}.

Substituting yields

(n+\ell)^{-k/q}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\binom{-k/q}{j}\ell^{j}n^{-(k+jq)/q}+2(3s)^{J+1}|\ell|^{J+1}n^{-(k+q(J+1))/q}.

Since
J
was chosen so that
k+q(J+1)\geq s
and
J+1\leq s
, the second summand is at most
2(3s)^{s}|\ell|^{s}n^{-s/q}
. Multiplying by
b_{k}
and summing over
0\leq k\leq s-1
, we collect all terms of the form
n^{-r/q}
for
0\leq r<s
into coefficients
\widetilde{b}_{r}
, and absorb all remaining tail terms into a single error bound. This gives

\widetilde{\beta}_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}\widetilde{b}_{r}n^{-r/q}+\widetilde{X}.

Note that
\widetilde{b}_{0}=1
. By
Lemma

4.1
, we have
|b_{k}|\leq 2C(2Dk)^{2Ek}\leq 2C(2Ds)^{2Es}
for all
k\leq s-1
. Hence,

\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}|b_{k}|\leq 2Cs(2Ds)^{2Es},

and so the remainder term can be bounded as

|\widetilde{X}|\leq 2|\ell|^{s}(3s)^{s}n^{-s/q}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}|b_{k}|+C(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}\leq 4C(2D|\ell|s)^{5Es}n^{-s/q}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(closure properties of
\FE
: sum)
.

Let
r\geq 2
. Suppose that for all
t\leq r
, the sequence
(n^{-e_{t}/q}\beta_{n}^{(t)})_{n\geq a}
admits
\FE_{q}(A_{t},B_{t},C_{t},D_{t},E_{t})
, where
e_{t}
is an integer.
Let
e=\max_{1\leq t\leq r}e_{t}
and
T=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\{t\mid 1\leq t\leq r,\,e_{t}=e\}\right\rvert}
. Then the sequence of sums

\left(\frac{n^{-e/q}}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{r}\beta_{n}^{(t)}\right)_{n\geq a}

admits

\FE_{q}\left(\max_{1\leq t\leq r}A_{t},\,\max_{1\leq t\leq r}B_{t},\,2r\max_{1\leq t\leq r}C_{t},\,2\max_{1\leq t\leq r}D_{t},\,2\max_{1\leq t\leq r}E_{t}\right).

Proof.

Let
A=\max_{1\leq t\leq r}A_{t}
, and define
B,C,D,E
similarly. Let us write

\widetilde{\beta}_{n}^{(t)}=n^{-e_{t}/q}\beta_{n}^{(t)},\qquad\delta_{t}=e-e_{t}\geq 0.

Let
(b_{k}^{(t)})_{k\geq 0}
be the asymptotic expansion coefficients of
\widetilde{\beta}_{n}^{(t)}
. For any
s\geq 1
, our hypothesis gives, for
n\geq As^{B}
,

\widetilde{\beta}_{n}^{(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}^{(t)}n^{-k/q}+X_{t},\qquad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{t}\right\rvert}\leq C_{t}(Ds)^{Es}n^{-s/q}.

Thus, since
e=\delta_{t}+e_{t}
,

n^{-e/q}\sum_{t=1}^{r}\beta_{n}^{(t)}=\sum_{t=1}^{r}n^{-\delta_{t}/q}\widetilde{\beta}_{n}^{(t)}=\sum_{t=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}^{(t)}n^{-(k+\delta_{t})/q}.

For any
p\geq 0
, let

Y_{p}=\{(t,k)\mid 1\leq t\leq r,\,0\leq k\leq s-1,\,k+\delta_{t}=p\}.

The double sum can then be rewritten as

\sum_{p=0}^{s-1+\delta_{\max}}\left(\sum_{(t,k)\in Y_{p}}b_{k}^{(t)}\right)n^{-p/q},\qquad\delta_{\max}=\max_{1\leq t\leq r}\delta_{t}.

Split this sum into the main part where
p\leq s-1
, and the tail part where
p\geq s
. The latter can be bounded by Lemma
4.1
as

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sum_{p=s}^{s-1+\delta_{\max}}\sum_{(t,k)\in Y_{p}}b_{k}^{(t)}\right\rvert}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}^{(t)}\right\rvert}\leq s\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{r}2C_{t}(2Ds)^{2Es}\leq 2rC\cdot(2Ds)^{2Es}.

Note that the zeroth coefficient of the sequence is
T\geq 1
. Upon dividing by
T
, we obtain the desired fractional expansion, which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Fractional expansion for quotients of
\alpha_{n}

We will extract
\FE_{q}
from analyticity results in the previous section using the following general tool.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
u
be a complex variable. Let
F(u)
be holomorphic on the disc
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
for some
\epsilon>0
with
\sup_{|u|<\epsilon}|F(u)|\leq C
. Suppose that
F
has real values on
\mathbf{R}
and
F(0)\neq 0
.
Then there exist real coefficients
(f_{k})_{k\geq 0}
with
f_{0}\neq 0
such that for all
s,q\geq 1
and
n\geq(2/\epsilon)^{q}
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F(n^{-1/q})-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}f_{k}n^{-k/q}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(1/\epsilon)^{s}n^{-s/q}

In particular, the sequence
(f_{0}^{-1}F(n^{-1/q}))_{n\geq 0}
admits

\FE_{q}\left((2/\epsilon)^{q},\,1,\,2C/\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f_{0}\right\rvert},\,1/\epsilon,\,1\right).

Proof.

The function
F
has Taylor expansion

F(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}u^{k}

and Cauchy’s estimates yield
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f_{k}\right\rvert}\leq C\epsilon^{-k}
. Hence, for all
s\geq 1
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F(u)-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}f_{k}u^{k}\right\rvert}\leq C\frac{1}{1-\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}/\epsilon}\left(\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}}{\epsilon}\right)^{s}.

Thus for
u=n^{-1/q}
with
n\geq(2/\epsilon)^{q}
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F(n^{-1/q})-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}f_{k}n^{-k/q}\right\rvert}\leq 2C(1/\epsilon)^{s}n^{-s/q}.

Dividing the inequality by
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f_{0}\right\rvert}
gives the desired fractional expansion.
∎

Example 4.7
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and let
(n)_{k}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)
be the Pochhammer symbol. Consider the sequence with terms
(n^{k}/(n)_{k})_{n\geq k}
. Using
u=n^{-1/q}
, we can write

\frac{n^{k}}{(n)_{k}}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n}{n-j}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-ju^{q}\right)^{-1}.

The last expression defines a holomorphic function on the disc
\{|u|<\epsilon\}
, where
\epsilon=1/(4k^{2})^{1/q}
. In fact, for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert ju^{q}\right\rvert}<k\epsilon^{q}<1/(4k)<1.

Moreover, on this disc, we can compute

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(1-ju^{q})\right\rvert}\geq 1-\mathinner{\!\left\lvert ju^{q}\right\rvert}\geq 1-1/(4k),

meaning that the product is bounded by
(1-1/(4k))^{-k}\leq 4/3
. Hence, we may take
C=2
in the lemma above with this choice of
\epsilon
, and
f_{0}=1
. It follows that the sequence
(n^{k}/(n)_{k})_{n\geq k}
admits

\FE_{q}\left((2/\epsilon)^{q},\,1,\,2C,\,1/\epsilon,\,1\right)=\FE_{q}\left(O_{q}(k^{2}),\,1,\,4,\,O_{q}(k^{2}),\,1\right).

We are now ready to deduce fractional expansion for the sequence of quotients of
\alpha_{n}
by its shifts.

Theorem 4.8
.

For any
k\geq 0
, the sequence
(n^{k/q}\alpha_{n+k}/\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 0}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

Proof.

Write

(10)

n^{k/q}\cdot\frac{\alpha_{n+k}}{\alpha_{n}}=n^{k/q}\frac{\Pref_{n+k}}{\Pref_{n}}\cdot\frac{\alpha_{n+k}/\Pref_{n+k}}{\alpha_{n}/\Pref_{n}}.

By
Theorem

3.12
, the sequence with terms

a_{n}=\alpha_{n}/\Pref_{n}

for
n\geq 0
admits a fractional expansion for some parameters that are all
O_{P}(1)
. Applying the shift lemma with
\ell=k
, the sequence
(a_{n+k})_{n\geq 0}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1)\right),

and, applying the reciprocal lemma to
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the sequence
(1/a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
admits a fractional expansion with parameters that are all
O_{P}(1)
. It now follows by an application of the product lemma that the sequence
(a_{n+k}/a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

This deals with the second factor in
eq.

10
. Let us now take care of the prefactor ratio as well. We have the identity

\log\left(n^{k/q}\frac{\Pref_{n+k}}{\Pref_{n}}\right)=-\frac{n+k+q/2}{q}\log(1+k/n)+P((n+k)^{1/q})-P(n^{1/q}).

Setting
u=n^{-1/q}
, we have
1+k/n=1+ku^{q}
and
(n+k)^{1/q}=u^{-1}(1+ku^{q})^{1/q}
.
Let us thus define, for a complex variable
u
,

L_{k}(u)=-\frac{u^{-q}+k+q/2}{q}\log(1+ku^{q})+\left(P\big(u^{-1}(1+ku^{q})^{1/q}\big)-P(u^{-1})\right).

We claim that
L_{k}(u)
extends to a holomorphic function on the disc
\{|u|<k^{-1/q}/2\}
. Write
z=ku^{q}
, so that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}<1/2^{q}
on the disc. Let

f(z)=\log(1+z)/z,\quad g_{i}(z)=((1+z)^{i/q}-1)/z\quad(1\leq i\leq q).

The functions
f
and
g_{i}
are holomorphic on the disc
\{|z|<1\}
, hence bounded on the disc
\{|z|<1/2^{q}\}
. Let
C
be their common bound there, a constant that depends only on
q
. We can rewrite
L_{k}(u)
as

L_{k}(u)=-\frac{k}{q}f(z)+\frac{k+q/2}{q}\log(1+z)+\sum_{i=1}^{q}c_{i}u^{-i}zg_{i}(z).

The first summand is bounded by
kC/q
, and the second by
(k+q/2)C2^{-q}/q
. Both are
O_{C,q}(k)
. We can bound the third summand as

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{q}c_{i}u^{-i}zg_{i}(z)\right\rvert}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}^{-i}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}C=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}^{q-i}Ck.

On the disc
\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}<k^{-1/q}/2\}
, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}^{q-i}\leq k(k^{-1/q}/2)^{q-i}\leq 1
, hence the above is at most
O_{P,C}(k)
. Moreover, as
u\to 0
, the leading term from the first summand in
L_{k}(u)
is
-k/q
, and in the second summand it is
k/q
(coming from
i=q
). The two leading terms cancel, and so
L_{k}(u)
is holomorphic on the disc
\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}<k^{-1/q}/2\}
with value
L_{k}(0)=0
. It follows that
\exp(L_{k}(u))
is also holomorphic on the same disc with value
1
at
0
, and bounded there by
O_{P}(k)
. By Lemma
4.6
, the sequence
(\exp(L_{k}(n^{-1/q})))_{n\geq 0}=(n^{k/q}\Pref_{n+k}/\Pref_{n})_{n\geq 0}
thus admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),O_{P}(1),O_{P}(1),O_{P}(k^{1/q}),O_{P}(1)\right).

Putting the two fractional expansions together, it follows from the product lemma that the sequence
(n^{k/q}\alpha_{n+k}/\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 0}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Asymptotics for the number of group actions

For our application, we will need sequences
\alpha_{n}
corresponding to the following polynomials. For a finite group
G
of order
m
, let

P_{G}(z)=\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{s_{G}(d)}{d}z^{d},

where
s_{G}(d)
is the number of subgroups of
G
of index
d
. Such polynomials satisfy
eq.

7
. By
[
Mül97
, formula (5)]
, we have

\exp(P_{G}(z))=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(G)z^{n},\quad\alpha_{n}(G)=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\hom(G,\Sym(n))\right\rvert}}{n!}.

In the particular case when
G=1
, the coefficients of the expansion are
(1/n!)_{n\geq 0}
. Their
\FE_{1}
property is Stirling’s formula. Suppose now that
G
is any finite group, and let

\chi_{n}(G)=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\hom(G,\Sym(n))\right\rvert}=n!\,\alpha_{n}(G).

By
Theorem

4.8
, for every fixed
k\geq 0
, the sequence

(11)

(n^{k/q}\alpha_{n+k}(G)/\alpha_{n}(G))_{n\geq 0}

admits a fractional expansion. Let us now convert this into a fractional expansion for the analogous sequence corresponding to
\chi_{n}(G)
.

Theorem 4.9

(quantitative form of
[
PZ24
]
, Proposition 3.4)
.

For any
k\geq 1
, the sequence
(n^{-k/q}(n)_{k}\chi_{n-k}(G)/\chi_{n}(G))_{n\geq k}
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k^{O_{P}(1)}),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k^{2}),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

Proof.

We can express the given sequence in terms of
\alpha_{n}(G)
as

(12)

n^{-k/q}(n)_{k}\frac{\chi_{n-k}(G)}{\chi_{n}(G)}=n^{-k/q}\frac{\alpha_{n-k}(G)}{\alpha_{n}(G)}=\left(\frac{n-k}{n}\right)^{k/q}\cdot\frac{\alpha_{n-k}(G)}{(n-k)^{k/q}\alpha_{n}(G)}.

For the first factor, write

\left(\frac{n-k}{n}\right)^{k/q}=(1-ku^{q})^{k/q}

with
u=n^{-1/q}
. The function
(1-ku^{q})^{k/q}
is holomorphic on the disc
\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}<k^{-1/q}/2\}
and bounded there by
2
with value
1
at
0
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.6
, the corresponding sequence for
n\geq k
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k),\,O(1),\,O(1),\,O(k^{1/q}),\,O(1)\right).

For the second factor in
eq.

12
, we use
Theorem

4.8
. Shift the sequence
eq.

11
by
-k
and then take its reciprocal. Hence, the sequence

\left(\frac{\alpha_{n-k}(G)}{(n-k)^{k/q}\alpha_{n}(G)}\right)_{n\geq k+1}

admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k^{O_{P}(1)}),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k^{2}),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

By multiplying the two sequences above, it follows from the product lemma that the sequence given by
eq.

12
admits

\FE_{q}\left(O_{P}(k^{O_{P}(1)}),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(1),\,O_{P}(k^{2}),\,O_{P}(1)\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Asymptotic expansion of expected traces

In this section, we explain how asymptotic expansions for exponentials of polynomials imply asymptotic expansions for expected traces of random representations, thus proving Assumption
A
when
\Gamma
is a free product of finitely many finite groups. The relationship between these two asymptotic expansions was explored by Puder and Zimhoni in
[
PZ24
]
, where it is framed in the context of covering spaces. Here, we provide an overview of their relevant definitions and results, upgrading them for our purposes with the quantitative details.

5.1.
Subcovers and lifts

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
be as in
eq.

1
. For each
1\leq i\leq m
, let
X_{G_{i}}
be a CW complex with a single vertex
v_{i}
and edges
e_{i}^{x}
indexed by nontrivial elements
x\in G_{i}
, such that
\pi_{1}(X_{G_{i}},v_{i})\cong G_{i}
. We construct
X_{\Gamma}
by taking the disjoint union of
X_{G_{1}},\dots,X_{G_{m}}
, adding a basepoint
o
, and connecting
o
to each
v_{i}
via an edge
e_{i}
. Thus,
\pi_{1}(X_{\Gamma},o)\cong\Gamma
.

A key concept from
[
PZ24
]
is that of a
subcover
of
X_{\Gamma}
. This is a CW complex
Y
that embeds into some covering space of
X_{\Gamma}
. Each subcover comes equipped with a restricted covering map
p\colon Y\to X_{\Gamma}
. Subcovers can be compared and composed via morphisms that are compatible with these covering maps. A
resolution
of a subcover
Y
is a collection of morphisms of subcovers
\{f\colon Y\to Z_{f}\}
so that every morphism
h\colon Y\to\hat{X}
from
Y
to any covering space
\hat{X}
of
X_{\Gamma}
factors uniquely as
h=g\circ f
for some
f
in the resolution and some embedding
g
. Similarly, an
embedding resolution
of
Y
is a collection of injective morphisms so that every injective morphism
h
from
Y
to any covering space factors uniquely as
h=g\circ f
for some
f
in the embedding resolution and some embedding
g
.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
be a uniformly random homomorphism. There exists a corresponding
N
-sheeted covering space
\pi\colon X_{\phi}\to X_{\Gamma}
(see
[
Hat02
]
). Now let
p\colon Y\to X_{\Gamma}
be a subcover of any covering space of
X_{\Gamma}
. A
lift
of
p
to
X_{\phi}
is a morphism of subcovers
\tilde{p}\colon Y\to X_{\phi}
such that
p=\pi\circ\tilde{p}
. Let
\mathbf{E}_{Y}(N)
be the expected number of lifts of
p
to
X_{\phi}
, that is,

\mathbf{E}_{Y}(N)=\mathbf{E}_{\phi}[|\{\tilde{p}\colon Y\to X_{\phi}\mid p=\pi\circ\tilde{p}\}|].

Similarly, let
\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)
be the expected number of lifts of
p
to
X_{\phi}
that are embeddings. For a compact subcover
Y
, any
finite
resolution
\mathcal{R}
of
Y
satisfies (see
[
PZ24
, Lemma 2.4]
)

\mathbf{E}_{Y}(N)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N),

and similarly for a finite embedding resolution
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{emb}}
of
Y
,

(13)

\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{emb}}}\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N).

Our aim now is to obtain asymptotic expansions for
\mathbf{E}_{Y}(N)
with the help of the more accessible asymptotic expansions for
\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)
.

5.2.
Expected number of embedding lifts for a finite group

Suppose, in this subsection, that
\Gamma=G
is a finite group. Let
Y
be a compact subcover of
X_{G}
. Then, by
[
PZ24
, Proposition 3.3]
, the set

\mathcal{R}_{Y}=\left\{f\colon Y\to Z_{f}\,\left|\,\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize$Z_{f}$ is a covering space of $X_{G}$ and}\\
\text{\normalsize$f(Y)$ meets every connected component of $Z_{f}$}\end{subarray}\right.\right\}

is a finite resolution of
Y
. Similarly, define
\mathcal{R}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}
by restricting
\mathcal{R}_{Y}
to embeddings
f
. For our purposes, we need to quantify the size of this resolution.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
Y
be a compact subcover of
X_{G}
with
V
vertices. Then

|\mathcal{R}_{Y}|\leq O_{|G|}(V)^{O(V)}.

Proof.

The number of components of
Y
is at most
V
. There are
O_{|G|}(1)
isomorphism types of connected covers of
X_{G}
, hence the number of covers
Z
of
X_{G}
with at most
V
components is
O_{|G|}(1)^{V}
. Fix one such cover
Z
. The condition that
f(Y)
meets every component of
Z
forces a surjection

\pi_{0}(Y)\to\pi_{0}(Z)
, giving at most
V^{V}
choices. Given the target component for each component of
Y
, a cellular map
Y\to Z
is uniquely determined on the
1
-skeleton
Y^{(1)}
. Since
Z
has at most
V
components and each component is a connected at-most-
|G|
-sheeted cover of
X_{G}
, we must have
|V(Z)|\leq|G|V^{2}
. Once the images of the vertices are chosen, we have at most
O_{|G|}(1)
further choices for each of the
O_{|G|}(V)
edges in
Y
, since each vertex of
Z
has
O_{|G|}(1)
incident edges. Thus, the number of cellular maps
Y\to Z
is at most

V^{V}\cdot|V(Z)|^{V}\cdot O_{|G|}(1)^{O_{|G|}(V)}\leq O_{|G|}(V)^{O_{|G|}(V)}.

Multiplying by the number of choices of
Z
yields the desired bound.
∎

In the remainder of this section, we obtain quantitative bounds on the expected number of embedding lifts of subcovers of the CW complex
X_{G}
. This is the crucial entry point for the asymptotic expansions developed in the previous section. We begin with the case where
Y
is a compact covering space.

Proposition 5.2

(quantitative form of
[
PZ24
]
, Proposition 3.4)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
Y
be a compact covering space of
X_{G}
with
V
vertices. Then the sequence
(N^{-V/|G|}\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{emb}}_{Y}(N))_{N\geq V}
admits

\FE_{|G|}\left(O_{|G|}(V^{O_{|G|}(1)}),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(V^{2}),\,O_{|G|}(1)\right).

Proof.

The number of
N
-sheeted covers of
X_{G}
is precisely
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\hom(G,\Sym(N))\right\rvert}=\chi_{N}(G)
(see
[
Hat02
]
). In every embedding
h\colon Y\to\hat{X}
of
Y
into an
N
-sheeted cover of
X_{G}
, the image
h(Y)
contains
V
vertices of
\hat{X}
. Since
Y
is a covering space,
h(Y)
and its complement are disconnected. The embedding
h
is thus determined by first selecting the
V
distinct vertices among
N
of them, and then selecting a complementing
(N-V)
-sheeted cover. In total,

\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)=(N)_{V}\cdot\frac{\chi_{N-V}(G)}{\chi_{N}(G)}.

The statement now follows immediately from
Theorem

4.9
.
∎

We are now ready to handle any compact subcover
Y
of
X_{G}
. Following
[
PZ24
, Definition 2.5]
, we associate the Euler characteristic
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)
to
Y
. This characteristic is defined as the sum of the Euler characteristics of the groups
4
4

4

For an overview of Euler characteristics of groups, see
[
PZ24
, Definition 1.3]
.

p_{*}(\pi_{1}(Y_{i}))
, where
Y_{i}
ranges over the connected components of
Y
and
p\colon Y\to X_{G}
is the restricted covering map. For a finite group
G
, we always have
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)\geq 0
. Moreover, if
Y
is a cover of
X_{G}
, then we have
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)=|V(Y)|/|G|
(see the final part of the proof of Proposition 3.4 in
[
PZ24
]
).

Proposition 5.3

(quantitative form of
[
PZ24
]
, Corollary 3.5)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
Y
be a compact subcover of
X_{G}
with
V
vertices. Then the sequence
(N^{-\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)}\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N))_{N\geq V}
admits

\FE_{|G|}\left(O_{|G|}(V^{O_{|G|}(1)}),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(V)^{O_{|G|}(V)},\,O_{|G|}(V^{2}),\,O_{|G|}(1)\right).

Proof.

Expand
\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)
using
eq.

13
over the resolution
\mathcal{R}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}
as

\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{R}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}}\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N).

Each summand
\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)
corresponds to a compact cover
Z_{f}
of
X_{G}
with at most
V
components. In particular,
Z_{f}
has
V_{f}=O_{|G|}(V)
vertices. It now follows from the previous proposition that each sequence

(N^{-V_{f}/|G|}\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N))_{N\geq V}

admits

\FE_{|G|}\left(O_{|G|}(V^{O_{|G|}(1)}),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(V^{2}),\,O_{|G|}(1)\right).

At the same time, the size of the embedding resolution is bounded from
Lemma

5.1
by
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{R}_{Y}\right\rvert}\leq O_{|G|}(V)^{O(V)}
. Lemma
4.5
implies that, for some constant
T\geq 1
the sequence

\left(\frac{N^{-\max_{f}V_{f}/|G|}}{T}\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)\right)_{N\geq V}

admits

\FE_{|G|}\left(O_{|G|}(V^{O_{|G|}(1)}),\,O_{|G|}(1),\,O_{|G|}(V)^{O_{|G|}(V)},\,O_{|G|}(V^{2}),\,O_{|G|}(1)\right).

Now, by
[
PZ24
, Proof of Corollary 3.5]
, the maximum
\max_{f}V_{f}/|G|=\max_{f}\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Z_{f})
is attained at a unique
f\in\mathcal{R}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}
, and its value there is
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)
. Hence
T=1
and the proof is complete.
∎

5.3.
Expected number of embedding lifts for a free product

Suppose now that
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
is a free product of finite groups as in
eq.

1
. Set

(14)

\mu(\Gamma)=\lcm\bigl(|G_{1}|,\dots,|G_{m}|\bigr),\qquad M(\Gamma)=|G_{1}|+\cdots+|G_{m}|.

Using the results of the previous subsection, we can now prove fractional expansions for expected number of lifts for any compact subcover of
X_{\Gamma}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
\mu=\mu(\Gamma)
and
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
, and let
Y
be a compact subcover of
X_{\Gamma}
with
V
vertices. Then the sequence
(N^{-\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)}\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N))_{N\geq V}
admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu,M}(V^{O_{\mu,M}(1)}),\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(V)^{O_{M}(V)},\,O_{\mu,M}(V^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

Proof.

Let
p\colon Y\to X_{\Gamma}
be the restricted covering map. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
Y|_{G_{i}}=p^{-1}(X_{G_{i}})
be the subcomplex of
Y
lying above
X_{G_{i}}
, and let
v=|p^{-1}(o)|
,
v_{i}=|V(Y|_{G_{i}})|
,
\epsilon_{i}=|p^{-1}(e_{i})|
. All these numbers are at most
V
. It is shown in
[
PZ24
, proof of Proposition 2.6]
that

\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)=(N)_{v}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{(N)_{\epsilon_{i}}}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{Y|_{G_{i}}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)\right).

Recall from Example
4.7
that the sequences
(N^{v}/(N)_{v})_{N\geq v}
and
(N^{\epsilon_{i}}/(N)_{\epsilon_{i}})_{N\geq\epsilon_{i}}
admit

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu}(V^{2}),\,1,\,O(1),\,O_{\mu}(V^{2}),\,1\right).

Inverting the first sequence into
((N)_{v}/N^{v})_{N\geq v}
gives, by the reciprocal lemma, a fractional expansion of the form

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu}(V^{O_{\mu}(1)}),\,1,\,O(1),\,O_{\mu}(V^{2}),\,O(1)\right).

By the previous proposition, each sequence

(N^{-\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y|_{G_{i}})}\mathbf{E}_{Y|_{G_{i}}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N))_{N\geq V}

admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{M}(V^{O_{M}(1)}),\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(V)^{O_{M}(V)},\,O_{M}(V^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

It remains to multiply all these fractional expansions together. The product lemma yields that the sequence

\left(N^{-v+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y|_{G_{i}})}\mathbf{E}_{Y}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)\right)_{N\geq V}

admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu,M}(V^{O_{\mu,M}(1)}),\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(V)^{O_{M}(V)},\,O_{\mu,M}(V^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

The exponent
-v+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y|_{G_{i}})
is equal to
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y)
by
[
PZ24
, Lemma 4.2]
, completing the proof.
∎

5.4.
Expected traces of random representations

Finally, we are in a position to prove
A
for the case where
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
as in
eq.

14
. In order to set up our notation, let
\gamma
be a nontrivial element of
\Gamma
, written in normal form as

\gamma=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{|\gamma|},\qquad\hbox{for some }x_{i}\in G_{i_{j}}\backslash\{1\},\,i_{j+1}\neq i_{j}.

To each
x_{i}
there corresponds an edge
e_{i_{j}}^{x_{i}}
in the complex
X_{G_{i_{j}}}
. The element
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(X_{\Gamma})
can be represented as the loop traversing, in order, for each
i=1,2,\dots,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
, the segments

o\overset{e_{i_{j}}}{\longrightarrow}v_{i_{j}}\overset{e_{i_{j}}^{x_{i}}}{\longrightarrow}v_{i_{j}}\overset{e_{i_{j}}}{\longrightarrow}o.

Let
C_{\gamma}
be the cycle with
3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
vertices mapping onto the combinatorial representation of
\gamma
above. By the lifting criterion in covering spaces (see, for example,
[
PZ24
, Proposition 2.8]
), this map can be lifted to the universal cover of
X_{\Gamma}
. Let
Y_{\gamma}
be the image of this lift, equipped with the restricted covering map
p\colon Y_{\gamma}\to X_{\Gamma}
. The subcover
Y_{\gamma}
is connected and has at most
3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
vertices and edges.

Suppose now that
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
is a uniformly random representation, and let
X_{\phi}
be the corresponding
N
-sheeted covering space of
X_{\Gamma}
. The number of fixed points
\fix\phi(\gamma)
of the action of
\gamma
on
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
is, again by the lifting criterion, equal to the number of lifts of
p\colon Y_{\gamma}\to X_{\Gamma}
to
X_{\phi}
(see
[
Hat02
]
). We can thus express the expected trace of
\phi(\gamma)
as

(15)

\mathbf{E}_{\phi}[\ntr\phi(\gamma)]=N^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{\phi}\left[\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\fix\phi(\gamma)\right\rvert}\right]=N^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{Y_{\gamma}}(N).

We can expand the last expectation using the resolution

\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}=\left\{f\colon Y_{\gamma}\to Z_{f}\mid\text{$f$ is a surjective morphism of subcovers}\right\}.

Lemma 5.5
.

For every nontrivial
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq O(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O(|\gamma|)}.

Proof.

The number of vertices and edges of
Y_{\gamma}
is at most
3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
. Any morphism of subcovers
f\colon Y_{\gamma}\to Z
is determined by its action on the
1
-skeleton. We thus count the number of surjective morphisms by giving a crude upper bound on the number of ways to specify the images of vertices and edges. Because
f
is surjective on vertices, the map
f_{V}\colon V(Y_{\gamma})\to V(Z)
induces a partition
\Pi
of
V(Y_{\gamma})
into nonempty fibers, with each block corresponding to a vertex of
Z
. Hence the number of possibilities for
\Pi
is at most the Bell number
B_{|V(Y_{\gamma})|}\leq(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{3|\gamma|}
.

Now fix one such partition
\Pi
. For each pair of blocks
\{A,B\}
in
\Pi
, let
E_{AB}\subseteq E(Y_{\gamma})
be the set of edges of
Y_{\gamma}
whose endpoints lie in
A
and
B
. Since
f
preserves incidence, every edge in
E_{AB}
must map to an edge of
Z_{f}
whose endpoints are the vertices corresponding to
A
and
B
. Thus, for each fixed pair
\{A,B\}
, specifying
f_{E}\colon E(Y_{\gamma})\to E(Z)
on
E_{AB}
is equivalent to partitioning
E_{AB}
into nonempty fibers (each fiber being the preimage of a single edge of
Z
between
A
and
B
). Therefore the number of possibilities for
f_{E}
is at most

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{A,B\}\in\Pi\\
E_{AB}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{AB}\right\rvert}^{|E_{AB}|}.

Using
\sum_{\{A,B\}\in\Pi}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E_{AB}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E(Y_{\gamma})\right\rvert}\leq 3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
and the inequality
\prod_{i}a_{i}^{a_{i}}\leq(\sum_{i}a_{i})^{\sum_{i}a_{i}}
for
a_{i}>0
, it follows that the above is at most
(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{3|\gamma|}
.

Putting things together, the total number of surjective morphisms
f\colon Y_{\gamma}\to Z_{f}
is

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq(3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{6|\gamma|},

as desired.
∎

We can now derive the desired asymptotic expansion for expected traces. In order to state it, we need to introduce some notation. Let
\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}
denote the set of torsion elements of
\Gamma
, and let
\Gamma^{\infty}=\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}
denote the set of elements of infinite order. We define a parameter depending on a group element
\gamma\in\Gamma
as

h(\gamma)=\begin{cases}1&\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}&\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}
is the set of subgroups of
\Gamma
containing
\gamma
that are isomorphic to either
\mathbf{Z}
or
C_{2}*C_{2}
. (The value of
h(\gamma)
will be computed explicitly in Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
.) Additionally, for
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, we interpret
1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|
as
0
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
\mu=\mu(\Gamma)
and
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the sequence

\left(\frac{N^{-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}}{h(\gamma)}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{\phi}\left[\tr\phi_{N}(\gamma)\right]\right)_{N\gg_{M}|\gamma|}

admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{\mu,M}(1)},\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)},\,O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

Proof.

Suppose first that
\gamma
is not a torsion element. We can expand
eq.

15
using the resolution
\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}
. Each summand
\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N)
corresponds to a compact subcover
Z_{f}
of
X_{\Gamma}
with at most
3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
vertices. It now follows from Proposition
5.4
that each sequence
(N^{-\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Z_{f})/\mu}\mathbf{E}_{Z_{f}}^{\mathrm{emb}}(N))_{N\geq 3|\gamma|}
admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{\mu,M}(1)},\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)},\,O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

Let
e=\max_{f}\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Z_{f})
. By Lemma
5.5
, the size of the resolution is bounded by
O(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O(|\gamma|)}
. The sum lemma now implies that the sequence
(T^{-1}N^{-e/\mu}\mathbf{E}_{Y}(N))_{N\geq 3|\gamma|}
admits

\FE_{\mu}\left(O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{\mu,M}(1)},\,O_{M}(1),\,O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)},\,O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}^{2}),\,O_{M}(1)\right).

The values of
e
and
T
can be identified as follows. It is shown in
[
PZ24
, proof of Theorem 1.4]
that
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Y_{\gamma})\leq 0
for all
f
, and the maximum value of
0
is attained at least once. In particular, we have
e=0
, and
T
corresponds to the number of
f
for which
\chi^{\mathrm{grp}}(Z_{f})=0
. The number of such
f
is, as stated in
[
PZ24
, Theorem 1.4]
, equal to the number of subgroups of
\Gamma
containing
\gamma
that are isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}
or
C_{2}*C_{2}
, which we denoted above as
h(\gamma)
.

On the other hand, when
\gamma
is a torsion element, we can proceed in a more straightforward manner and use a simpler
Y_{\gamma}
. In this case, all the statistics about
\pi_{N}(\gamma)
depend only on the finite factor
G
of
\Gamma
into which
\gamma
conjugates. In particular, let
Y_{\gamma}
be the finite cover of
X_{G}
corresponding to the finite cyclic subgroup generated by
\gamma
inside
\pi_{1}(X_{G})
. The number of vertices of
Y_{\gamma}
is the index
|G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\gamma\rangle|
. In this case, we can use the more direct fractional expansion from Proposition
5.2
to deduce that the sequence

\left(N^{-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\mathbf{E}_{Y_{\gamma}}(N)\right)_{N\geq|G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\gamma\rangle|}

admits
\FE_{|G|}
, and hence
\FE_{\mu}
, with all parameters bounded by
O_{|G|}(1)=O_{M}(1)
.
∎

5.5.
Asymptotic expansion of expected traces

Finally, we are ready to deduce
A
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
\mu=\mu(\Gamma)
and
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, every
s\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
, and every
N\geq O_{\mu,M}(s)^{O_{\mu,M}(1)}
, there exist constants
a_{\ell}\in\mathbf{R}
for all non-negative integers
\ell
such that
a_{0}=\mathbf{1}_{\gamma=1}=\tau(\lambda(\gamma))
and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)\right]-\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}a_{\ell}N^{-\ell/\mu}\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,M}(s)^{O_{\mu,M}(s)}N^{-s/\mu}.

Proof.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and for a random homomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Sym(N)
with its extension
\pi_{N}=\std\circ\phi_{N}
, we have

(16)

\mathbf{E}\left[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)\right]=N^{-1+1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\left(N^{-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\mathbf{E}\left[\tr\phi_{N}(\gamma)\right]-N^{-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\right).

By
Theorem

5.6
, there are coefficients
b_{k}\in\mathbf{R}
depending on
\gamma
such that, for all
s\geq 1
and all
N\geq O_{\mu,M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{\mu,M}(1)}s^{O_{M}(1)}
, we have

N^{-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\mathbf{E}\left[\tr\phi_{N}(\gamma)\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}N^{-k/\mu}+X

with
b_{0}=h(\gamma)
and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X\right\rvert}\leq O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)}\cdot O_{\mu,M}(|\gamma|^{2}s)^{O_{M}(1)s}N^{-s/\mu}.

Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert b_{k}\right\rvert}\leq O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)}\cdot O_{\mu,M}(|\gamma|^{2}k)^{O_{M}(1)k}.

Inserting this expansion into
eq.

16
gives

\mathbf{E}\left[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}b_{k}N^{-1+1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|-k/\mu}-N^{-1}+N^{-1+1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}X.

Set
t=\mu(1-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|)\geq 0
. Note that
t\leq\mu
with equality only when
\gamma
is of infinite order. Set furthermore
\ell=t+k
as
k
varies. Then the above can be rewritten as

(17)

\sum_{\ell=t}^{t+s-1}a_{\ell}N^{-\ell/\mu}+N^{-1+1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}X,\qquad a_{\ell}=b_{\ell-t}-\mathbf{1}_{\ell=\mu}.

Split the sum above into the main part with
\ell
ranging up to
s-1
, and into the tail with
\ell
from
s
to
t+s-1
. The latter part is then bounded by

(18)

\sum_{\ell=s}^{s+t-1}|a_{\ell}|N^{-\ell/\mu}\leq t\cdot O_{M}(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert})^{O_{M}(|\gamma|)}\cdot O_{\mu,M}(|\gamma|^{2}s)^{O_{M}(1)s}\cdot N^{-s/\mu}.

Suppose that
s\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert}
. Thus, for
N\geq O_{\mu,M}(s)^{O_{\mu,M}(1)}
, we have

N^{-1+1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,M}(s)^{O_{\mu,M}(s)}N^{-s/\mu}.

Combining this with
eq.

18
, we obtain from
eq.

17
that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[\ntr\pi_{N}(\gamma)\right]-\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}a_{\ell}N^{-\ell/\mu}\right\rvert}\leq O_{\mu,M}(s)^{O_{\mu,M}(s)}N^{-s/\mu}.

This is the desired expansion. Note that the coefficients
a_{\ell}
vanish for all
\ell
satisfying
0\leq\ell<t=\mu(1-1/|\langle\gamma\rangle|)
. When
\gamma
is of infinite order, this means that
a_{\ell}=0
for
0\leq\ell<\mu
, while
a_{\mu}=a_{t}=b_{0}-1=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}-1
by
eq.

17
. On the other hand, when
\gamma
is torsion,
a_{0}
can only be nonzero when
t=0
, which is equivalent to
\gamma=1
in
\Gamma
. In this case,
a_{0}=b_{0}=1
. Overall, we thus have
a_{0}=\mathbf{1}_{\gamma=1}=\tau(\lambda(\gamma))
, as required.
∎

6.
Temperedness of leading coefficients

In this section, we prove that temperedness of leading coefficients, that is Requirement
B
, holds when
\Gamma
is a free product of finitely many finite groups.

6.1.
Positivization trick

A discrete group
\Gamma
equipped with a generating set
S
satisfies the
rapid decay property
if there exists a polynomial
P\in\mathbf{C}[X]
such that for every
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}\leq P(|x|)\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)},

where
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}
is the operator norm on
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
. This notion has been originally proved to hold for the free group by Haagerup
[
Haa79
]
, while it was formally introduced later by Jolissaint
[
Jol90
]
. The rapid decay property is stable under taking free products
[
Jol90
, Theorem A]
, and it holds for finite groups
[
Jol90
, Proposition B]
. In particular, the groups
\Gamma
we are considering here satisfy the rapid decay property.

Under the rapid decay property, Magee and de la Salle
[
MdlS24
, Section 6.2]
develop a reduction to proving temperedness of leading coefficients to elements
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
that are
symmetric generators of random walks
on
\Gamma
, meaning that in the expansion
x=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\alpha_{\gamma}\gamma
, the coefficients
\alpha_{\gamma}
are non-negative, sum to
1
, and
\alpha_{\gamma^{-1}}=\alpha_{\gamma}
. Here is the precise claim, known in the literature as the
positivization trick
(see also
[
vH25
, Section 2.6]
).

Lemma 6.1

(
[
MdlS24
]
, Proposition 6.3)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group with the rapid decay property. Let
u\colon\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]\to\mathbf{C}
be a function that is tempered at every generator of a symmetric random walk on
\Gamma
. Then
u
is tempered at every self-adjoint element of
\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
.

6.2.
Leading coefficients for group elements

In order to apply the positivization trick, we need to understand the leading coefficients
u_{k}(\gamma)
for group elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
a bit better.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
\mu=\mu(\Gamma)
and
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be any element, and let
u_{k}(\gamma)
be as in
A
.

1.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}
, then
u_{k}(\gamma)\leq O_{\mu,M}(1)
.

2.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, then
u_{k}(\gamma)=0
for
0\leq k<\mu
, and
u_{\mu}(\gamma)=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}-1
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}
. Then
\gamma
is conjugate to an element of a finite free factor
G_{i}
of
\Gamma
. Since
u_{k}
is invariant under conjugation, we can thus assume that
\gamma
belongs to a finite group of order at most
M
. There are
O_{M}(1)
such choices for
\gamma
, and there are at most
\mu
many values
u_{k}(\gamma)
as
1\leq k\leq\mu
. This proves the first portion of the statement. Meanwhile, the second part,
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, is contained in the final part of the proof of
Theorem

5.7
.
∎

We also have a complete description of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}
due to the rigid structure of
\Gamma^{\infty}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, there is a unique
\delta\in\Gamma
and
d\geq 1
such that
\gamma=\delta^{d}
and
\delta
is not a proper power in
\Gamma
. Moreover,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}=\omega(d)+\begin{cases}\sigma(d)&\text{if }\gamma\text{ is contained in a subgroup isomorphic to }C_{2}*C_{2},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\omega(d)
is the number of positive divisors of
d
, and
\sigma(d)
is their sum.

Proof.

Elements of infinite order in
\Gamma
have cyclic centralizers by
[
MKS76
, Problem 28, p.196]
. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
, and let
\delta
be a generator of
C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)
. Then
\gamma=\delta^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
, and
\delta
is not a proper power. Inside
\langle\delta\rangle\cong\mathbf{Z}
, there are exactly
\omega(d)
subgroups containing
\gamma
. Suppose now that
\gamma
is also contained in a subgroup isomorphic to
C_{2}*C_{2}
, generated by an involution
\tau
and an element
\sigma
of infinite order. Then
\gamma\in\langle\sigma\rangle
, hence
\sigma\in C_{\Gamma}(\gamma)=\langle\delta\rangle
. Thus
\sigma=\delta^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbf{Z}
dividing
d
. In other words, any
C_{2}*C_{2}
containing
\gamma
is of the form
\langle\tau,\delta^{k}\rangle
for some involution
\tau
with
\tau\gamma\tau=\gamma^{-1}
and some
k\mid d
. Suppose such an involution
\tau
indeed exists. The involutions in
\langle\tau,\delta\rangle
are precisely of the form
\tau\delta^{i}
for some
i
. This means that all
C_{2}*C_{2}
subgroups containing
\gamma
are
H_{i,k}=\langle\tau\delta^{i},\delta^{k}\rangle
for
0\leq i<k
and
k\mid d
. Moreover, all these subgroups are distinct. Thus, if there exists at least one
C_{2}*C_{2}
subgroup containing
\gamma
, then the number of such subgroups is precisely
\sum_{k\mid d}k=\sigma(d)
.
∎

We can thus split
\Gamma^{\infty}
into three disjoint subsets:

•

\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow}}^{\infty}
: elements
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
such that
\gamma=\delta^{d}
for some
\delta\in\Gamma
that is not a proper power and some integer
d\geq 2
. In this case,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}\leq\omega(d)+\sigma(d)
.

•

\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty}
: elements
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
that are not proper powers and are contained in a subgroup of
\Gamma
isomorphic to
C_{2}*C_{2}
. In this case,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}=2
.

•

\Gamma_{\infty}^{\infty}
: elements
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}
that are neither proper powers nor contained in a subgroup isomorphic to
C_{2}*C_{2}
. In this case,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\right\rvert}=1
.

6.3.
Hitting probabilities for random walks

Let
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
be a generator of a symmetric random walk on
\Gamma
. Let
X
be the random walk on
\Gamma
driven by
x
, that is,
X
is a random variable with law
\mathbf{P}[X=\gamma]=\alpha_{\gamma}
. For every
p\geq 1
, let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{p}
be independent and identically distributed copies of
X
. Thus
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}
is the position of the random walk after
p
steps. Note that

(19)

x^{p}=\mathbf{E}\left[X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\right]=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\gamma)\gamma.

We will require upper bounds on the hitting probabilities of specific subsets of
\Gamma
, consisting of elements
\gamma
for which we have uniform control over
u_{k}(\gamma)
. During this, we will rewrite
eq.

19
in terms of the left-regular representation. Note that for any
\gamma,\alpha\in\Gamma
, we can write
\mathbf{1}_{\gamma=\alpha}=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\lambda(\alpha)\delta_{1}\rangle
. Using this with
\alpha=X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}
and taking expectations, we obtain from
eq.

19
that

(20)

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\gamma)=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\lambda(x)^{p}\delta_{1}\rangle.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. Then

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}})\leq(p+1)^{2}M\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}.

Proof.

Any torsion element
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}
is conjugate to an element of a finite free factor
G_{i}
of
\Gamma
. Thus, there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma=\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
, and
\beta
lies in a finite free factor. Hence

(21)

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}})\leq\sum_{\beta\in G_{1}\cup G_{2}\cup\dots\cup G_{k}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha).

The number of possible
\beta
is bounded by the sum of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert G_{i}\right\rvert}
, that is, by
M
. Let us then bound the inner sum over
\alpha
. Since the group
\Gamma
admits normal form of elements, the event
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
implies that there exist
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq p+1
such that

X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{t_{1}-1}=\alpha^{-1},\quad X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{1}+1}\cdots X_{t_{2}-1}=\beta,\quad X_{t_{2}}X_{t_{2}+1}\cdots X_{p}=\alpha.

The independence of the
X_{i}
, together with
eq.

20
, implies that the probability
\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha)
is thus bounded by

\sum_{1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq p+1}\langle\delta_{\alpha^{-1}},\lambda(x)^{t_{1}-1}\delta_{1}\rangle\langle\delta_{\beta},\lambda(x)^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\delta_{1}\rangle\langle\delta_{\alpha},\lambda(x)^{p+1-t_{2}}\delta_{1}\rangle.

The middle factor is at most
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}
. Therefore, the inner sum over
\alpha\in\Gamma
in
eq.

21
is bounded by

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\sum_{1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq p+1}\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\langle\delta_{\alpha^{-1}},\lambda(x)^{t_{1}-1}\delta_{1}\rangle\langle\delta_{\alpha},\lambda(x)^{p+1-t_{2}}\delta_{1}\rangle.

Note that for any
t\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\langle\delta_{\alpha},\lambda\left(x\right)^{t}\delta_{1}\rangle^{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)^{t}\delta_{1}\right\rVert}_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{2t}.

This yields, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\langle\delta_{\alpha^{-1}},\lambda(x)^{t_{1}-1}\delta_{1}\rangle\langle\delta_{\alpha},\lambda(x)^{p+1-t_{2}}\delta_{1}\rangle\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p+t_{1}-t_{2}}.

The statement now follows immediately by plugging these bounds into
eq.

21
, taking into account that there are at most
(p+1)^{2}
choices for the pair
(t_{1},t_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
M=M(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. Then

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty})\leq(p+1)^{5}M^{2}\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}.

Proof.

Any element
\gamma\in\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty}
can be written as a product of two involutions, each of them conjugate to an involution in a finite free factor of
\Gamma
. Thus, there exist
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\Gamma
such that

\gamma=\alpha^{-1}\alpha^{\prime}\alpha\beta^{-1}\beta^{\prime}\beta,

where
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
belong to finite free factors. We can now repeat the argument from the previous lemma, splitting the product
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}
into six parts according to
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{5}\leq p+1
, bounding the terms corresponding to
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
by powers of
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}
, and using Cauchy-Schwarz to handle the sums over
\alpha,\beta
. The number of choices for
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
is at most
M^{2}
, and the number of choices for the
t_{i}
is at most
(p+1)^{5}
, yielding the desired bound.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
. For any integer
d\geq 2
, define
\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow},d}^{\infty}
to be the set of elements in
\Gamma^{\infty}
that are
d
-th powers of elements that are not proper powers. Then

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow},d}^{\infty})\leq(p+1)^{4}\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}.

Moreover, the probability is nonzero only for
d\leq p\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
.

Proof.

We start by proving the final statement. Note that, if
\Gamma^{\rm np}
denotes the set of group elements that are not proper powers,

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow},d}^{\infty})=\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma^{\rm np}}\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\delta^{d}).

Hence, we focus on
\gamma=\delta^{d}
for some
\delta\in\Gamma^{\rm np}
. The event
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\gamma
implies that
d\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\delta^{d}\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{1}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{2}\right\rvert}+\cdots+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{p}\right\rvert}\leq p\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
, so the probability is nonzero only for
d\leq p\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
. From here on, we follow
[
vH25
, Proof of Lemma 3.10]
and
[
MPvH25
, Proof of Theorem 1.8]
. Since
\Gamma
admits normal forms, we can write
\delta=\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha
for some cyclically reduced word
\beta\in\Gamma
and some
\alpha\in\Gamma
. Thus
\gamma=\alpha^{-1}\beta^{d}\alpha
. For each fixed
d
, we repeat the argument from the previous lemmas: we split the product
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}
into four parts according to
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}\leq p+1
, bound the term corresponding to
\beta^{d}
by
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}
, and use Cauchy-Schwarz to handle the sums over
\alpha
. The number of choices for the
t_{i}
is at most
(p+1)^{4}
.
∎

6.4.
Temperedness of leading coefficients

We conclude with the proof of Requirement
B
for free products of finite groups.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*G_{2}*\cdots*G_{m}
with
\mu=\mu(\Gamma)
as in
eq.

14
. Then, for all positive integers
k\leq\mu
, the functional
u_{k}
is tempered at every self-adjoint element of
\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.1
, it suffices to prove the statement for generators of symmetric random walks on
\Gamma
. Let
x\in\mathbf{C}[\Gamma]
be such a generator. Expand
x^{p}
as in
eq.

19
. Split this sum into two parts,

u_{k}(x^{p})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}}}\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\gamma)u_{k}(\gamma)+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\infty}}\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}=\gamma)u_{k}(\gamma).

By Lemmas
6.2
and
6.4
, the contribution of the first part is

O_{\mu,M}(1)\cdot\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{tors}})\\
\leq O_{\mu,M}(p^{O(1)})\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}.

Again using Lemma
6.2
, the second part only contributes when
k=\mu
, and it can be split further into three parts according to the partition of
\Gamma^{\infty}
into
\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow}}^{\infty}
,
\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty}
, and
\Gamma_{\infty}^{\infty}
. In view of Lemma
6.3
, for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}^{\infty}
, we have
u_{\mu}(\gamma)=0
, and hence this part does not contribute. Moreover, for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty}
, we have
u_{\mu}(\gamma)=1
, and for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow}}^{\infty}
with
\gamma=\delta^{d}
, we have
u_{\mu}(\gamma)\leq\omega(d)+\sigma(d)\leq O(d^{2})
. By Lemma
6.6
, the latter terms only contribute when
d\leq p\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
. Thus the contribution of the second part is at most

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma_{C_{2}*C_{2}}^{\infty})+\sum_{2\leq d\leq p|x|}O(d^{2})\cdot\mathbf{P}(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{p}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{pow},d}^{\infty}),

which, again by the Lemmas
6.5
and
6.6
, is bounded by

O_{M}(p^{O(1)})\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}+O((p|x|)^{O(1)})\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda(x)\right\rVert}^{p}.

Taking
p
-th roots and limits as
p\to\infty
, we obtain the desired temperedness.
∎

In this essay, we just need to regard the metric as a quadratic form. Any other additional structure on manifolds, such as topological structure, complex structure, etc., are not used.

Introduction

The theme of this essay is not about Finsler geometry, but talking something about this subject may be useful to understand what are we going to do. In fact, the crucial method in our discussion, namely a special kind of metrical deformations, arises from the resent research on Finsler geometry.

Some Finsler metrics are determined by a Riemannian metric
\alpha
and a
1
-form
\beta
. Such kind of Finsler metrics is much closeing to Riemannian metrics
[
19
]
. For example, take
\alpha=|y|
,
\beta=\langle x,y\rangle
and denote
b:=\|\beta\|_{\alpha}
, then

F=\frac{\sqrt{(1-b^{2})\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}}{1-b^{2}}+\frac{\beta}{1-b^{2}}
is the famous Funk metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
[
11
,
2
]
. Another two well-known metrics, which are found by L. Berwald
[
4
]
and R. Bryant
[
6
]
respectively, are
F=\frac{(\sqrt{(1-b^{2})\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}+\beta)^{2}}{(1-b^{2})^{2}\sqrt{(1-b^{2})\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}}
defined on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
F=\Re\frac{\sqrt{(e^{ip}+b^{2})\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}-i\beta}{e^{ip}+b^{2}}
defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. All of them are of excellent geometrical properties: complete or at least forward complete, locally projectively flat, and of constant flag curvature
[
19
]
.

From an algebraic point of view, a Riemannian metric is just a quadratic form. Hence, if you have a Riemannian metric
\alpha
and a
1
-form
\beta
, you can obtain infinity many Riemannian metrics by using
\beta
to deform
\alpha
as below

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}=e^{\rho}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\kappa\beta^{2}}.

The factors
\kappa
and
\rho
could depend on the points of the given manifold. However, based on our earlier researches, we will just consider a simple case, namely they depend only on the length of
\beta
. Such special kind of metrical deformations, introduced by the author, are called
\beta
-deformations
[
18
]
.

\beta
-deformations are used successfully to solve some problems in Finsler geometry. However, such technique relates only to Riemannian metrics. Hence, we aim to explore the effect of
\beta
-deformations in Riemannian geometry, and this essay is just the beginning.

Many facts declare a simple phenomenon, that is, good data produce good structures. Our question is very simple. Given an Einstein metric
\alpha
and a special
1
-form
\beta
, can we obtain some new Einstein metrics by using data
(\alpha,\beta)
combining with
\beta
-deformations?

The answer is positive, as we expected at the very beginning of this project. In fact, we will choose
\alpha
as a Riemannian metric with constant sectional curvature or even an Euclidean metric, and
\beta
as simple as possible. Even so, we can obtain infinity many Einstein metrics by this new way, some of them are known, and some of them are new.

Otherwise, perhaps any Einstein metric can be rebuilt by a similar way. We need to explain it a little bit more.

Consider a Riemannian metric
\alpha
and some
1
-forms
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
,
\cdots
,
\beta_{m}
. Let
b_{kl}=\langle\beta_{k},\beta_{l}\rangle_{\alpha}
,
\rho
and
\kappa_{ij}
be functions of all the
b_{kl}
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}=e^{\rho}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\sum\kappa_{ij}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}

is a Riemannian metric when the factors
\kappa_{ij}
satisfy some necessary restrictions to make sure the positive definiteness of
\bar{\alpha}
. Such kind of deformations can be regarded as
multiple
\beta
-deformations
.

By this way, one can obtain many Riemannian metric of excellent geometrical properties beginning with some simple metric and some good
1
-forms. Notice that the multiple
\beta
-deformation is reversible general. Hence, for any Riemannian metric of excellent geometrical properties, possibly exist some
1
-forms to deform the metric to be a simple one.

Hence, we propose the following question.

Question: Given an Einstein metric
\bar{\alpha}
, are there some
1
-forms
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
,
\cdots
,
\beta_{m}
, such that
\bar{\alpha}
is the resulting metric under multiple
\beta
-deformations globally or locally, in which
\alpha
is a Riemannian metric with constant sectional curvature?

There is some relationship between multiple
\beta
-deformations and warped product metrics, which are given in the form as below,

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\psi\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\phi_{1}\sigma_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}\sigma_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\phi_{n-1}\sigma_{n-1}^{2}.

If one aims to find some metric satisfying some particular conditions, one can try to determine the suitable factors
\psi
and
\phi_{i}
above. As a classical method in differential geometric, it is simple and computable. It is easy to see that if
\psi
and
\phi_{i}
depend only on the functions
\langle\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}\rangle_{g}
where
g
is another simper metric such as

\displaystyle g=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{n-1}^{2},

then
\bar{g}
can be regarded as a resulting metric under multiple
\beta
-deformations by the metric
g
and the
1
-forms
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
,
\cdots
,
\sigma_{n-1}
.

For example, the Euclidean metric can be expressed as a warped product,

\displaystyle g=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{n-1}^{2}),

where all the
1
-forms
\sigma_{i}
have constant length
1
and orthogonal to any other ones. Denote
\alpha=\sqrt{g}
and
\beta_{i}=r^{2}\sigma_{i}
. Then
\langle\beta_{i},\beta_{j}\rangle_{\alpha}=\delta_{ij}r^{2}
in which
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol. In this case, the resulting metrics under multiple
\beta
-deformations are given by

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\psi(r^{2})\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\sum\phi_{ij}(r^{2})\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j},

or particularly given by

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\psi(r^{2})\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\phi_{1}(r^{2})\sigma_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}(r^{2})\sigma_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\phi_{n-1}(r^{2})\sigma_{n-1}^{2}

when all the
\phi_{ij}(i\neq j)
vanish.

If we use only one
1
-form
\beta
,
\beta_{1}
for instant, to deform
\alpha
, then the resulting metrics are given by

\displaystyle\bar{g}=\psi(r^{2})\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\phi(r^{2})\sigma_{1}^{2}+\varphi(r^{2})(\sigma_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{n-1}^{2}).

(1.1)

Hence, if we begin with the Euclidean metric and such particular
1
-form, then our problem is equivalent to determine all the Einstein metrics given in the form (
1.1
). Actually, all the examples constructed in this essay are in this form locally.

However, it does not mean that the method of multiple
\beta
-deformations is just a new “face” of warped product metrics. It is clear that we don’t need to assume
\alpha
must be a warped product metric. In the world of multiple
\beta
-deformations, we only need two objects, a metric
\alpha
and several
1
-forms
\beta_{i}
.

The idea of multiple
\beta
-deformations is very simple. However, the computation is a little bit complicated. In this essay, we just discuss
\beta
-deformation, that is to say, only one
1
-form will be used. Even so, the crucial formula, namely Proposition

in Appendix, is obviously formidable.

Our main purpose is to study the structure of Einstein metrics under
\beta
-deformations. By the way, we hope to construct some new Einstein metrics by the known ones. More specifically, assume
\alpha
is an Einstein metric and
\beta
is a
1
-form, we want to understand the phenomenon of
\alpha
and
\beta
under

-deformations.

There are many choices on
\beta
, and we believe that Killing forms, namely the dually
1

Remark

Be attention that the resulting metric
\bar{\alpha}
may not define on the initial manifold after
\beta
-deformation. For example, consider a standard spherical metric
\alpha

\mathbb{S}^{2n}
and its
1
-form
\beta
satisfying (

\mathbb{CP}^{n}
instead of
\mathbb{S}^{2n}
.

What happens under
\beta
-deformation?

In fact,
\beta
-deformation maybe lead to some singularity. Due to (
4.6
),

\displaystyle t=-b^{2}(a^{2}-\mu b^{2}).

Notice that
t=-s_{i}a^{ij}s_{j}
can be regard as the opposite number of the inner product for the vector
s_{i}
, we can conclude that
a^{2}-\mu b^{2}
must be non-negative. When
a^{2}-\mu b^{2}=0
, (
4.6
) implies
s_{0}=0
at the corresponding points. For the round sphere, this will happen on the equator.

For such points, the metric may be of some singularity. Let’s talk about a simple example, which is interesting but instructive. Taking a standard spherical metric
\alpha
and its
1
-form
\beta
expressed locally as (
4.5
) with
\mu=1
and
a=1
, then

\displaystyle a^{2}-\mu b^{2}=\frac{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}.

As a result,

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}=\frac{2|y|}{1+|x|^{2}+\left|1-|x|^{2}\right|}

is Ricci-flat by taking
C=D=1
in (
5.9
). It is easy to see that on the southern hemisphere (namely the region
|x|<1
),
\bar{\alpha}=|y|
is the flat metric, and on the northern hemisphere (namely the region
|x|>1
),
\bar{\alpha}=\frac{|y|}{|x|^{2}}
is the pullback metric of flat metric by inversion transformation with respect to the hypersurface
|x|=1
. On the equator
|x|=1
, the metric
\bar{\alpha}
is continuous, but not smooth.

We don’t know how to deal with the singularity at this moment. It seems that, illuminated by Funibi-Study metrics, such singularity can possibly be repaired by taking some operations for the underlying manifold.

Case (i) exist two different double roots

\displaystyle p^{2}-4(q-1)=4,\qquad p^{2}-4(r-1)=4

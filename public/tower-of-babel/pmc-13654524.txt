Validation of the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score for clinically significant fetal acidemia: an outcome-enriched nested case–control study
Authors: Ayumu Ito, Mio Kamiya, Eijiro Hayata, Sumito Nagasaki, Masahiko Nakata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

AJOG Global Reports at a Glance

Why was this study conducted?

Cardiotocography predicts fetal acidemia poorly, and the iPREFACE score, which quantifies cumulative fetal stress from fetal heart rate patterns, had previously been evaluated only where acidemia was rare.

Key findings

In 272 term vaginal deliveries, the area under the curve was 0.851 for umbilical artery pH <7.1, 0.912 for <7.2, 0.823 for <7.0, and 0.833 for <7.3. The score correlated most strongly with pH and base excess. Predicted pH exceeded measured pH by 0.16 units, with limits of agreement spanning 0.50 units.

What does this add to what is known?

Consistent discrimination from pH <7.3 to <7.0 supports further evaluation for stepwise assessment of progressive fetal deterioration during labor. The score is better interpreted as a relative index of acidemia risk than as a means of estimating absolute pH.

Introduction

Cardiotocography (CTG), developed by Hon in 1958.1 was introduced into clinical practice in the 1970s and 1980s to monitor fetal well-being and is now widely used worldwide. However, due to the high false-positive rate of 99.8% in predicting cerebral palsy.2 The number of births resulting in cerebral palsy has not decreased despite the introduction of CTG, while the cesarean section rate has continued to rise.3,4 Researchers have not been able to make better use of CTG in management of delivery. Severe fetal acidemia has been shown to be linked to neonatal brain damage.5, 6, 7 and the current challenge with CTG lies in its ability to effectively predict fetal acidemia.

We proposed the iPREFACE score to address this challenge, which evaluates the accumulation of fetal stress in response to uterine contractions during labor.8 The iPREFACE score is based on the 5-tier classification of fetal heart rate (FHR) patterns defined by the Japanese Society of Obstetrics and Gynecology (JSOG).9 It quantifies cumulative fetal stress by summing the levels of FHR patterns for all decelerations occurring immediately before delivery.8 The iPREFACE score has been shown to significantly correlate with umbilical artery (UmA) pH, base excess (BE), and lactate, demonstrating a high predictive ability for fetal acidemia.8,10 These findings suggested the potential of the score for clinical application. However, a limitation of this predictive model is the low incidence of fetal acidemia, which occurs in only 8.3% of cases,8 reducing the strength of evidence for the model.

The objective of this study was to validate the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score, a cumulative fetal stress–based intrapartum assessment tool, for clinically significant fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1) using an outcome-enriched nested case–control cohort, and to determine whether the score can enable stepwise clinical assessment of progressive fetal deterioration during labor.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study was a nested case–control design involving women who underwent singleton, full-term vaginal deliveries at the Toho University Omori Medical Center between January 2017 and July 2023. To construct an outcome-enriched validation cohort, cases with UmA pH<7.2 were matched 1:1 with controls (UmA pH≥7.2); the prespecified primary endpoint was clinically significant fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1). To ensure that every case meeting the primary endpoint (UmA pH<7.1) or the secondary endpoint (UmA pH<7.0) would be eligible for inclusion without further selection, the sampling frame was set at the less stringent threshold of UmA pH<7.2, within which such cases are by definition contained. Potential patient overlap with the cohort used in the previous study of iPREFACE score was assessed at the individual level.8 Overlapping patients were not excluded from the present analysis. Exclusion criteria included fetal anomalies, chromosomal abnormalities, fetal growth restriction, inability to monitor CTG for at least 30 minutes immediately before delivery, inaccurate CTG recordings, and failure to collect UmA blood immediately after delivery.

Sample selection and artificial balancing

To achieve artificial balancing, random sampling was conducted using Microsoft Excel for Mac (version 16.91, Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA). Random numbers were generated for each eligible case using the RAND function, and the cases were sorted in ascending order. The top-ranked cases were selected to ensure a 1:1 matching ratio of UmA pH<7.2 and UmA pH≥7.2. The sample selection process is illustrated in Figure 1.

Sample size and statistical power

The sample size was determined by the number of eligible cases available during the study period. Overall, 136 cases with UmA pH<7.2 were identified, and 136 matched controls with UmA pH≥7.2 were randomly selected in a 1:1 ratio to construct the outcome-enriched validation cohort. Because this was a retrospective validation study including all eligible patients during the study period, no a priori sample size calculation or formal power analysis was performed. Nevertheless, the final sample comprised 136 events and 136 nonevents, which is consistent with the sample size considerations reported for validation studies of clinical prediction models.11 The precision of the estimated discriminatory performance is reflected by the reported 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the receiver operating characteristic (ROC)-AUC values.

Calculation method of the iPREFACE score

The iPREFACE score is calculated by summing the levels of all FHR waveform patterns, as defined by the JSOG9 during the 30 minutes immediately before delivery. In case of prolonged decelerations, the duration in minutes is multiplied and rounded to the nearest whole number before being included in the calculation. The iPREFACE score calculation method is detailed elsewhere.8

Validation of iPREFACE score

First, the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score for clinically significant fetal acidemia, defined as UmA pH<7.1, was evaluated using the area under the ROC curve (AUC). Secondary analyses were conducted for UmA pH<7.2 and <7.0, and an exploratory analysis was performed for UmA pH<7.3 to assess stepwise fetal deterioration during labor.

Second, the correlation between the iPREFACE score and UmA blood gas parameters (pH, pCO2, pO2, HCO3−, BE, and lactate) was assessed using both linear and quadratic regression models. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was employed to assess the strength of the linear relationship, whereas quadratic regression analysis was conducted to identify potential nonlinear relationships. The linear and quadratic models were compared for each parameter using coefficients of determination (R²) and statistical significance testing. The results were visualized using scatter plots with overlaid linear and quadratic regression curves to compare the model fits.

Third, the agreement between the measured UmA blood pH values in this study and the predicted values calculated using the predictive model (y=7.305−0.003×x+(−0.00007037)×x²), based on the iPREFACE score developed in the previous research using observational clinical data,8 was assessed through Bland–Altman analysis.

Sensitivity analysis

To further test the robustness of the iPREFACE score, sensitivity analyses were performed using alternative UmA pH thresholds of <7.2, <7.0, and <7.3, in addition to the prespecified primary endpoint of UmA pH<7.1. The ROC-AUC values and 95% CIs were calculated for each threshold to assess the stability of the predictive performance.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Mac (version 29.0, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY). Depending on their distribution, continuous variables are expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range, assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages.

Group comparisons for continuous variables were conducted using Student’s t test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

ROC curve analysis was conducted to evaluate the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score at the prespecified primary threshold (UmA pH<7.1), secondary thresholds (UmA pH<7.2 and <7.0), and an exploratory threshold (UmA pH<7.3). AUC was calculated along with its 95% CI to quantify the discriminative ability of the model. The exploratory cutoff value for each UmA pH threshold was determined using the maximum Youden index (sensitivity+specificity−1).

Pearson’s correlation coefficient and regression analyses assessed the relationship between the iPREFACE score and UmA blood gas parameters (pH, pCO₂, pO₂, HCO₃−, BE, and lactate). For each parameter, linear and quadratic regression models were employed to evaluate linear and potential nonlinear relationships. The models were compared using coefficients of determination (R²), and scatter plots with overlaid regression lines were created to visualize the relationships.

Bland–Altman analysis was conducted to evaluate the agreement between the measured and predicted UmA blood pH values. Limits of agreement were calculated as mean difference±1.96 SD and a Bland–Altman plot was created for visual assessment of the agreement.

A P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant for all statistical analyses.

Ethics statements

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Toho University Omori Medical Center (approval no. M23151) on October 24, 2022. Information regarding the study was published in an opt-out format on the hospital website, allowing potential research participants to refuse participation. The need for written informed consent was waived. The quadratic regression model used in this study was originally developed in a prior study approved by Toho University (approval no. M18261) and Ageo Central General Hospital (approval no. 677).

Results

Study participants

During the study period, 136 patients with UmA pH<7.2 were identified. A total of 136 patients with UmA pH≥7.2 were randomly selected from the same period to achieve a 1:1 ratio. The backgrounds of these patients are shown in Table. No significant differences were observed between the UmA pH<7.2 group and the UmA pH≥7.2 group in terms of maternal age, parity, gravidity, gestational age, birth weight, neonatal sex, use of labor induction agents, or vacuum delivery. Regarding umbilical arterial blood gas analysis values, no significant difference in pO2 was observed; however, other findings indicated a significantly higher tendency for metabolic acidosis in the UmA pH<7.2 group. The UmA pH<7.2 group also showed significantly higher rates of meconium-stained amniotic fluid and nuchal cord. Among the 272 patients included in the analysis, 15 (5.5%) had UmA pH<7.1, of whom 6 had UmA pH<7.0 and 9 had UmA pH between 7.0 and 7.1. Because the cohort was outcome-enriched by design, these proportions do not correspond to the prevalence of fetal acidemia in an unselected obstetric population.

Predictive ability of the iPREFACE score and sensitivity analyses

For the prespecified primary outcome of clinically significant fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1), the ROC-AUC of the iPREFACE score was 0.851 (95% CI: 0.771–0.931). For the secondary outcome of UmA pH<7.2, the ROC-AUC was 0.912 (95% CI: 0.879–0.945), and for UmA pH<7.0, it was 0.823 (95% CI: 0.656–0.989). In the exploratory analysis using UmA pH<7.3, the ROC-AUC was 0.833 (95% CI: 0.785–0.881).

All cutoff values were data-derived post hoc by maximizing the Youden index and were 52 for UmA pH<7.0, 47 for <7.1, 37 for <7.2, and 33 for <7.3. The cutoffs decreased monotonically as the pH threshold became less stringent, consistent with the cumulative-burden concept underlying the iPREFACE score.

Evaluation of the correlation between the iPREFACE score and umbilical artery blood gas analysis values

Figure 3 shows the linear and quadratic regression models illustrating the correlation between the iPREFACE score and UmA blood gas analysis values. In the linear regression model, the correlation coefficient (R) was highest for pH at 0.644, followed by BE at 0.593, pCO₂ at 0.480, lactate at 0.374, HCO₃− at 0.157, and pO₂ at 0.125. Comment

Principal findings

First, the iPREFACE score demonstrated strong discriminatory performance for clinically significant fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1) in an outcome-enriched nested case–control cohort. Second, predicted UmA pH values calculated using the previously developed quadratic regression model based on the iPREFACE score showed a systematic difference from measured values but wide limits of agreement, indicating that the equation reproduces the overall relationship but not individual-level pH values. In addition, the iPREFACE score was significantly correlated with UmA blood gas parameters, particularly pH and BE, supporting the clinical relevance of cumulative fetal stress assessment.

Results in the context of what is known

Saling et al,12 in the 1970s and 1980s, introduced fetal scalp blood sampling for assessing fetal acidemia to address the limitations of CTG in predicting cerebral palsy, particularly its high false-positive rate. However, due to its invasive nature, requiring membrane rupture, and associated risks such as infection and fetal hemorrhage, its use gradually declined in clinical practice. Subsequently, CTG-based guidelines have been established in various countries, and the prediction of fetal acidemia based on FHR waveform interpretation has become standard practice. However, major international guidelines13, 14, 15, 16 have a limited ability to predict fetal acidemia, with reported ROC-AUCs ranging from 0.60 to 0.66.17 Other attempts to predict fetal acidemia, such as fetal pulse oximetry and fetal electrocardiography,18,19 have been made but have not demonstrated superiority over CTG-based FHR monitoring.20, 21, 22

Recently, it was reported that the “deceleration area,” representing the area below the baseline of decelerations on CTG, showed a high predictive ability for fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1), with an ROC-AUC of 0.76 (95% CI: 0.72–0.80).23 These findings suggested that cumulative fetal stress is crucial in predicting fetal acidemia.

The iPREFACE score, proposed in our previous study to evaluate cumulative fetal stress, demonstrated remarkably high predictive ability for fetal acidemia, with ROC-AUCs of 0.778 (pH<7.2), 0.962 (pH<7.1), and 0.996 (pH<7.0).8 Furthermore, the iPREFACE score has been shown to significantly correlate not only with UmA pH but also with BE and lactate levels.10 It has also been found to exhibit a high predictive ability for fetal acidemia, even in cesarean delivery cases.24 Additionally, the iPREFACE score does not require complex computer-based analysis and can be calculated using a simple method, making it advantageous for use in clinical practice.

In this study, we found that the iPREFACE score demonstrated high predictive ability for fetal acidemia in an outcome-enriched cohort balanced 1:1 for UmA pH<7.2 and UmA pH≥7.2, with ROC-AUCs ranging from 0.823 to 0.912 for fetal acidemia.

Clinical implications

The iPREFACE score showed a strong predictive ability, even in artificially balanced data with 50% rare cases of fetal acidemia, which are uncommon in real-world settings. Furthermore, Bland–Altman analysis showed that the predicted UmA pH values, calculated using the quadratic regression model based on observational clinical data from previous research, were systematically higher than the measured values by 0.16 pH units, with limits of agreement spanning 0.50 pH units. Both the systematic bias and the width of the limits of agreement exceed the range of clinically meaningful variation in UmA pH. These findings suggest that although the previously developed equation captures the overall relationship between the iPREFACE score and UmA pH, it is not sufficiently accurate for predicting absolute UmA pH values in individual patients. The iPREFACE score is therefore better interpreted as a relative index of the risk of acidemia than as a means of estimating an absolute pH value. This does not diminish the clinical importance of evaluating accumulated fetal stress, since the primary intended use of the score is risk stratification rather than point prediction. Repeated FHR decelerations can progressively reduce oxygenation and increase carbon dioxide levels, leading initially to respiratory acidosis and, with worsening hypoxia, to anaerobic metabolism, lactate accumulation, and metabolic acidosis. Experimental studies in fetal sheep have similarly demonstrated that repeated decelerations induced by uterine artery occlusion are associated with declining pH and lactate accumulation in the body and brain.25 Under hypoxic conditions, increased anaerobic glucose metabolism and elevations in blood glucose have also been reported in association with metabolic acidosis.26 These findings provide a physiological basis for evaluating cumulative fetal stress. Consistent discriminatory performance across multiple UmA pH thresholds further supports the potential value of the iPREFACE score for stepwise assessment of progressive fetal deterioration during labor.

In this study, the iPREFACE score was significantly correlated with UmA blood gas analysis values in an artificially balanced cohort. In the quadratic regression analysis, statistical significance (P<.01) was observed for pH, pCO₂, HCO₃−, BE, and lactate, except for pO₂. The R² value for pO₂ was the lowest, and no statistical significance was observed. This lack of significance can be attributed to the fact that, while pO₂ decreases with repeated decelerations, its variability becomes limited and plateaus in severe hypoxic conditions, resulting in a weaker correlation. This limited variability and plateau of pO₂ in severe hypoxic conditions indicates that pO₂ is an acute-phase indicator and contributes less to chronic acid–base balance. The highest R² value was observed for pH at 0.415, indicating that the iPREFACE score explains approximately 41.5% of the variability in pH, which reflects its relatively high predictive ability. Meanwhile, CO₂ (R²=0.230), HCO₃− (R²=0.106), BE (R²=0.357), and lactate (R²=0.165) showed lower R² values than pH. The progression to metabolic acidosis is caused by repeated decelerations leading to hypoxia and hypercapnia over time, which can explain this. BE had a higher R² value than lactate because BE reflects multiple metabolic factors, whereas lactate represents only one of these factors. In summary, pH reflects acute changes, while BE represents the cumulative acid load, making it important to evaluate fetal stress’s long-term impact. These results align with those of the study by Low et al,5,6 who reported the following pH<7.0 and BE<−12.0 mEq/L in UmA blood or neonatal arterial blood are associated with brain injury. Taken together, these findings provide physiological support for the concept underlying the iPREFACE score. Moreover, the consistent discriminatory performance observed across multiple pH thresholds further supports further evaluation of its potential clinical role for stepwise assessment of progressive fetal deterioration during labor.

Research implications

In this study, we validated the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score in an outcome-enriched cohort. However, further validation in diverse populations through multicenter collaborative studies is needed to confirm its broader applicability. The cutoff values identified in the present cohort were data-derived and should therefore be considered exploratory. Their performance requires confirmation in an independent validation cohort before they can be recommended for clinical decision-making. Additionally, for future clinical application, prospective observational studies with a sufficient number of cases should aim to establish cutoff values for the iPREFACE score in clinical settings and develop evidence-based management recommendations based on these values.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. First, the outcome-enriched nested case–control design enabled estimation of discriminatory performance despite the rarity of fetal acidemia in unselected populations. Second, the reliability of the predictive model developed in previous studies was evaluated using Bland–Altman analysis by comparing predicted and measured UmA pH values.

This study also has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective, single-center study restricted to singleton term vaginal deliveries, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations, gestational ages, and modes of delivery, and no external validation was performed.

The outcome-enriched design should also be considered when interpreting the findings. This design was intentionally adopted to enable evaluation of the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score across clinically relevant acidemia thresholds despite the low prevalence of fetal acidemia in routine clinical practice. Consequently, prevalence-dependent measures of clinical performance, such as positive and negative predictive values, cannot be inferred from the present cohort. Therefore, the findings of this study primarily support the discriminatory performance of the iPREFACE score and should not be interpreted as direct estimates of prevalence-dependent clinical predictive performance in an unselected obstetric population. In addition, the number of patients meeting the prespecified primary outcome of UmA pH<7.1 was small (n=15), and the number with UmA pH<7.0 was smaller still (n=6). The discriminatory performance estimates at these thresholds are therefore based on few events and are accompanied by wide CIs; the estimate at UmA pH<7.0, in particular, should be regarded as exploratory.

The five-tier classification of FHR patterns27 used for the iPREFACE score is unique to Japan and differs from internationally recognized standards. Therefore, developing an iPREFACE score using the globally adopted three-tier classification or comparing both systems can enhance international applicability. AI-based predictive models incorporating “cumulative fetal stress” underlying the iPREFACE score could enable its use across regions, regardless of the FHR classification. Moreover, although FHR patterns were classified according to the standardized JSOG criteria, potential interobserver variability in CTG interpretation and score measurement could not be quantified in this retrospective analysis. However, a previous study demonstrated good inter-rater reliability of the iPREFACE score,8 which may partly mitigate this concern.

In addition, 58 of the 272 patients (21.3%) had also been included in the original iPREFACE study; therefore, the present cohort was not fully independent of the original study population. This overlap may have influenced the estimated model performance and limits the interpretation of the present study as a fully independent validation.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that the iPREFACE score has discriminatory performance for clinically significant fetal acidemia (UmA pH<7.1) and is significantly correlated with UmA blood gas parameters, particularly pH and BE, in an outcome-enriched cohort. Consistent discriminatory performance across multiple pH thresholds supports further evaluation of the iPREFACE score for stepwise assessment of progressive fetal deterioration during labor. Further multicenter validation and development of internationally compatible approaches are warranted.

Cup product of inhomogeneous Tate cochains, and application to tori over local fields that split over cyclic extensions Thanks: This research was partially supported by the Israel Science Foundation (grant 1030/22).
Mikhail Borovoi

Abstract
. In this note we give formulas for cup product in Tate cohomology in terms of inhomogeneous cochains. Using one of these formulas, for a torus T T defined over a non-archimedean local field K K and splitting over a cyclic extension of K K , we compute explicit cocycles representing all cohomology classes in H 1 ​ ( K , T ) H^{1}(K,T) .

1.2.
Tori over local fields

Let
K
be a non-archimedean local field,
and
T
be a
K
-torus.
Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension splitting
T
.
Consider the Galois group
G={\rm Gal}(L/K)

and the cocharacter group

X={\sf X}_{*}(T):={\rm Hom}({\mathds{G}}_{m,L},T_{L}).

Then
G
naturally acts on
X
.
Consider the first Galois cohomology group

H^{1}(K,T)=H^{1}(L/K,T)\coloneqq H^{1}(G,T(L))=H^{1}(G,X\otimes L^{\times}).

According to Tate
[
8
]
,
there is a canonical isomorphism

(3)

H^{-1}(G,X){\ \xrightarrow{\makebox[9.56667pt]{\scriptsize${\raisebox{-3.01389pt}{$\sim$}}$}}\ }H^{1}(G,X\otimes L^{\times}).

Recall that, denoting by
X_{G}
the group of coinvariants of
G
in
X
:

X_{G}=X/\langle x-g\cdot x\ |\ g\in G,x\in X\rangle,

the group
H^{-1}(G,X)
is the subgroup of
X_{G}

consisting of the classes of elements
x\in X
with
\sum_{g\in G}\,g\kern-0.8pt\cdot\kern-0.8ptx=0
.
Let

u_{L/K}\in H^{2}(G,L^{\times})

denote the
fundamental class
; see, for instance,
[
5
, Definition 9.1]
.
The isomorphism (
3
)
is given by cup product with
u_{L/K}
.

For applications, in particular, for twisting,
it would be helpful to have an explicit formula for the isomorphism
(
3
). In the general case this seems to be out of reach.
We give a desired formula in the case of a
cyclic
extension
L/K
,
that is, when
G
is cyclic.

In the case where the extension
L/K
is unramified,
and hence cyclic (that is,
G
is cyclic),
there is a formula for the fundamental class
u_{L/K}

in Milne’s notes
[
6
]
.
In the general case of finite Galois extension
L/K
of local fields,
the fundamental class is mysterious.
In the answer
[
7
]
to a MathOverflow question of the author, Will Sawin
guessed and partly proved a formula for
u_{L/K}

in the case when
L/K
is cyclic (possibly ramified).
We prove Sawin’s formula in full using Milne’s answer to the author’s question
[
2
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(Sawin
[
7
]
)
.

Let
L/K
be a
cyclic
extension of degree
n
of non-archimedean local fields.
Choose a generator
\sigma
of the cyclic group (of order
n
)
G={\rm Gal}(L/K)
.
Let
e_{\sigma}\in K^{\times}
be an element such that

(e_{\sigma},L/K)=\sigma

where

e\mapsto(e,L/K)\colon\ K^{\times}\to K^{\times}/{\rm Nm}(L^{\times}){\ \xrightarrow{\makebox[10.13342pt]{\scriptsize${\raisebox{-3.01389pt}{$\sim$}}$}}\ }G

is the local reciprocity homomorphism; see
[
4
, Section VI.2.2]
.
Then the fundamental class
u_{L/K}\in H^{2}(G,L^{\times})

is represented by the following cocycle
a\colon G\times G\to L^{\times}
:

\text{for \ $0\leq i,j<n$,}\qquad a(\sigma^{i},\sigma^{j})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if \ \ }i+j<n,\\
e_{\sigma}&\text{if \ \ }i+j\geq n.\end{cases}

Using Theorem
1.1
and one of our formulas for cup product in terms of inhomogeneous cochains,
we explicitly compute the isomorphism (
3
) in the cyclic case:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T
be a torus over a non-archimedean local field
K
, and assume that

T
splits over a
cyclic
extension
L
of
K
of degree
n
.
Let
G={\rm Gal}(L/K)
, and let
\sigma\in G
and

be
as in Theorem

.
Let
x\in X={\sf X}_{*}(T)
be a cocharacter such that
[x]\in X_{G}

is contained in
H^{-1}(G,X)
.
Then the image of
[x]\in H^{-1}(G,X)
in
H^{1}(G,X\otimes L^{\times})

under the isomorphism (

) is the class of the cocycle

z_{x}\colon\,G\to X\,\otimes\,L^{\times},\quad\ \sigma^{g}\,\longmapsto\ \sum_{t=1}^{g}\sigma^{t}\kern-0.8pt\cdot\kern-0.8ptx\,\otimes\,e_{\sigma}\quad\text{for}\ \,0\leq g<n.

Note that the condition
0\leq g<n
in the theorem can be replaced by
g\geq 0
.

\displaystyle c(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})

\displaystyle=f(\kern 0.8pt1,\kern 0.8pth_{1},\kern 0.8pth_{1}h_{2},\kern 0.8pt\dots,\kern 0.8pth_{1}\cdots h_{n}\kern 0.8pt),\quad n\geq 0

\displaystyle f(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{0}c(\kern 0.8ptg_{0}^{-1}g_{1},\kern 0.8ptg_{1}^{-1}g_{2},\kern 0.8ptg_{2}^{-1}g_{3},\kern 0.8pt\dots,\kern 0.8ptg_{n-1}^{-1}g_{n}\kern 0.8pt),\quad\ n\geq 0

\displaystyle c(h_{1},\dots,h_{-n-1})

3.
The fundamental class of a cyclic extension

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.
Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of non-archimedean local fields with
cyclic
Galois group

G={\rm Gal}(L/K)
of order
n
.
Choose a generator
\sigma\in G
.
Let
e_{\sigma}\in K^{\times}
be as in Subsection
1.2
.
We write
[e_{\sigma}]
for the image of
e_{\sigma}

in
H^{-1}(G,L^{\times})\coloneqq K^{\times}/{\rm Nm}(L^{\times})
.

Consider the homomorphism

\chi\colon G\to\frac{1}{n}{\mathds{Z}}/{\mathds{Z}}\quad\ \text{such that}\ \,\chi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}.

Then
\chi\in H^{1}(G,{\mathds{Q}}/{\mathds{Z}})
.
Consider

\delta\chi\in H^{2}(G,{\mathds{Z}}),

the image of
\chi
under the connecting map

\delta\colon H^{1}(G,\frac{1}{n}{\mathds{Z}}/{\mathds{Z}})\to H^{2}(G,{\mathds{Z}}).

Then
\delta\chi
is represented by the 2-cocycle

b\in Z^{2}(G,{\mathds{Z}}),\quad\ b\colon G\times G\to{\mathds{Z}}

defined as follows (compare Milne
[
6
, page 102, above Proposition III.1.9]
).
We represent an element of
G\times G
as
(\sigma^{i},\sigma^{j})

with
i,j\in{\mathds{Z}}
,
0\leq i,j<n
.
Then

b(\sigma^{i},\sigma^{j})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\quad i+j<n\\
1&\text{if}\quad i+j\geq n.\end{cases}

For
a
as in Theorem
1.1
, we have

a=b\cup e_{\sigma}

where

e_{\sigma}\in Z^{0}(G,L^{\times})=K^{\times}

and
\cup
denotes cup product of cocycles.
It follows that
a
is a 2-cocycle,
a\in Z^{2}(G,L^{\times})
.
Write
[a]\in H^{2}(G,L^{\times})
for the class of
a
.
Write

[e_{\sigma}]=e_{\sigma}\cdot{\rm Nm}(L^{\times})\in K^{\times}/{\rm Nm}(L^{\times})=\hat{H}^{0}(G,L^{\times}).

Then

[a]=[b]\cup[e_{\sigma}]=\delta\chi\cup[e_{\sigma}]

where
\cup
denotes cup product of Tate cohomology classes.
Consider the canonical isomorphism

{\rm inv}_{L/K}\colon H^{2}(G,L^{\times}){\ \xrightarrow{\makebox[9.56667pt]{\scriptsize${\raisebox{-3.01389pt}{$\sim$}}$}}\ }\frac{1}{n}{\mathds{Z}}/{\mathds{Z}}.

{\rm inv}_{L/K}(\delta\chi\cup[e_{\sigma}])=\chi(\sigma);

see Harari
[
5
, Proposition 9.3]
.
By construction, we have
\chi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}
.
Thus

{\rm inv}_{L/K}[a]={\rm inv}_{L/K}(\delta\chi\cup[e_{\sigma}])=\chi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}.

By the definition of the fundamental class
u_{L/K}
, it follows that
[a]=u_{L/K}
,
which completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4.
Explicit cocycles for a torus

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
.
We sometimes write
z
for
z_{x}
.
We compute

z=x\cup a\in Z^{-1}(G,X)\otimes Z^{2}(G,L^{\times})

where

\displaystyle z\in Z^{1}(G,L^{\times})\subseteq{\rm Maps}(G,L^{\times}),

\displaystyle a\in Z^{2}(G,L^{\times})\subseteq{\rm Maps}(G\times G,L^{\times}),

\displaystyle x\in Z^{-1}(G,X)\subseteq X.

Here
[a]=u_{L/K}
, the fundamental class, and the 2-cocycle
a
is as in Theorem
1.1

and its proof, namely,
a=b\cup e_{\sigma}
.

Formula (
19
) for
p=1
,
q=1
reads:

z(g)=\sum_{t\in G}t\cdot x\,\otimes\,t\cdot a(t^{-1},g)

for
g\in G
. Since
a
takes the values
e_{\sigma}\in K^{\times}
and
1
only, we have

t\cdot a(t^{-1},g)=a(t^{-1},g)
.
It remains to compute the sum

\sum_{t\in G}t\cdot x\,\otimes\,a(t^{-1},g).

Recall that
\sigma
is a generator of the cyclic group
G
.
By abuse of notation, we write
\sigma^{g}
for
g
and
\sigma^{t}
for
t
.
Now
g,t\in{\mathds{Z}}/n{\mathds{Z}}
.

We wish to compute

z(\sigma^{g})=\sum_{t\in{\mathds{Z}}/n{\mathds{Z}}}\sigma^{t}\cdot x\,\otimes\,a(\sigma^{-t},\sigma^{g}).

For this end, we compute

a(\sigma^{-t},\sigma^{g})=a(\sigma^{n-t},\sigma^{g})\quad\ \text{where}\ \,0\leq g<n,\quad 0<t\leq n.

If
0<t\leq g<n
, then

0<n-t<n,\quad 0\leq g<n,\quad(n-t)+g=n+(g-t)\geq n\ \ \text{(because $t\leq g$)},

whence

.

If

0\leq n-t<n,\quad 0\leq g<n,\quad(n-t)+g=n+(g-t)<n\ \ \text{(because $t>g$)},

whence
a(\sigma^{n-t},\sigma^{g})=1
.

We conclude that

(20)

z_{x}(\sigma^{g})=\sum_{t=1}^{g}\sigma^{t}\cdot x\,\otimes\,e_{\sigma}

for
0\leq g<n
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

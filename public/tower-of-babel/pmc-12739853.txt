An in vitro comparison of different caries-detection methods in primary-molars
Authors: Selcuk Savas, Irem Nur Bitisik, Mehmet Izgi, Ebru Kucukyilmaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe early and accurate detection of occlusal caries in primary molars is critical to prevent its progression, as primary teeth are more susceptible to rapid carious development. This study aimed to evaluate and compare the diagnostic performance of various caries detection methods, in detecting occlusal caries in primary molars.MethodsA total of 88 freshly extracted primary molar teeth were included in this in vitro study. The teeth were assessed using four diagnostic methods: International Caries Detection and Assessment System II (ICDAS), Midwest Caries I.D (MW), DIAGNOdent pen (LFpen) and Quantitative-Light-Induced-Fluorescence (QLF). The performance of the method was evaluated by two examiners at two separate time points. Sensitivity, specificity, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) were calculated for both the enamel (D1) and dentin (D3) thresholds. Histological evaluation was performed as the gold standard for comparison. Interexaminer and intraexaminer reproducibility were assessed using Cohen’s weighted kappa and intraclass correlation coefficients.ResultsAt the enamel caries threshold (D1), MW achieved the highest sensitivity (61.7–76.6%), while QLF exhibited the highest specificity (92.5%). For dentin caries (D3), LFpen had the highest sensitivity (p < 0.05), whereas ICDAS achieved 100% specificity. LFpen demonstrated the best overall diagnostic accuracy, with AUC values ranging from 0.823 to 0.875. Interexaminer reproducibility was highest for LFpen (ICC: 0.719–0.869), followed by ICDAS (kappa: 0.584–0.932).ConclusionLF pen showed the highest overall AUC and the greatest sensitivity at the D3 threshold. At the D1 threshold, Midwest Caries ID achieved the highest sensitivity, whereas QLF yielded the highest specificity, minimizing false positives. ICDAS remains a practical, cost-effective option for routine clinical use.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-07359-3.

Background

The rapid progression of occlusal caries in primary teeth compared to permanent teeth requires early and accurate diagnosis to guide appropriate intervention. This progression is primarily due to the morphological characteristics of primary teeth, including thinner enamel and dentin, a lower mineralization rate, and larger dentinal tubules [1, 2]. Although visual inspection is a commonly used method for diagnosing occlusal caries, it may not be sufficient to provide a reliable and reproducible diagnosis, highlighting the need for complementary diagnostic tools. In addition, while visual inspection is highly specific, it tends to have low sensitivity and is inherently subjective, relying heavily on the experience of the examiner [3–6]. These limitations have led to increased research into objective and quantitative diagnostic tools that offer greater accuracy and consistency in the detection of carious lesions.

In the search for quantitative, objective and highly reproducible methods, one of the most frequently reviewed methods in the literature is the DIAGNOdent pen (DIAGNOdent pen, KaVo, Biberach, Germany), which uses laser light as a source to diagnose the difference in fluorescence between carious and intact tissue based on the presence of intraoral bacterial metabolite [7–15]. Although there are many studies in the literature on the diagnostic accuracy of the device in permanent teeth [7–11], the number of studies evaluating its success in primary teeth is limited [12–15]. While the practical use of the device, its relative affordability and accessibility, and the fact that in permanent teeth its specificity and sensitivity values are comparable to those of visual examination highlight its reliability, its additional advantages as a quantitative and reproducible tool, as well as its potential benefits in the early detection of caries in primary teeth, cannot be overlooked [7–11], the benefits to be gained from its use and success in a structure where early detection of caries is very important, such as primary teeth, cannot be ignored. Considering the studies in the literature on Quantitative Light Induced Fluorescence (QLF Inspektor Pro; Inspektor Research System, Amsterdam, Netherlands), another system that can quantitatively and objectively analyze the depth and width of the initial lesions based on the difference in fluorescence between caries and intact tissues, it is seen that the system is generally used for lesion monitoring [16–19]. However, when we look at the studies evaluating the success of the device in detecting caries, it is noteworthy that the correct diagnosis rates are high [20–22]. However, to the best of our knowledge, only one study has compared the accuracy of QLF with other non-traditional diagnostic methods for detecting occlusal caries lesions in primary teeth, highlighting a gap in the literature that needs to be further explored [23].

One of the preferred methods for caries diagnosis, caries detection and determination of lesion activity is the Midwest Caries I.D. (DENTSPLY Professional, York, PA, USA), a computer-based system designed to distinguish different optical signatures based on changes in translucency and opacity between healthy and demineralized enamel [24–27]. The structure of enamel undergoing demineralization typically becomes less translucent compared to healthy enamel. As a result, demineralized enamel has a different optical appearance to healthy enamel. The light-emitting diode (LED) device, which emits soft diode light in the wavelength range of 635 to 880 nm. The device analyses the reflection and refraction of the light emitted from the tooth surface and captures these signals via an optical fiber, which are then converted into electrical signals for further analysis [24–27]. However, the available scientific evidence on the diagnostic efficacy of this device in caries diagnosis is limited and only one study to date has specifically evaluated the use of the device for caries detection in primary teeth [27]. This suggests that further research is needed before the applicability of the device can be fully assessed.

Early detection of caries in primary teeth is of great importance. In the early stages of caries, treatment alternatives that do not interfere with patient cooperation can be prioritized with proper caries detection. Early interventions can focus on remineralization approaches that aim to reverse lesions rather than restorative or endodontic approaches. With these simple treatments it will be possible to reduce the cost of treatment. By adopting these approaches at a societal level, it is possible to reduce dmft rates and thus make progress in the quest for a healthy society. Although numerous studies have been conducted on caries detection in permanent teeth, there is a lack of research specifically investigating the efficacy of various caries detection methods in primary teeth. Moreover, to date, studies evaluating the performance of Midwest Caries ID and QLF in detecting occlusal caries in primary molars, particularly in comparison with other diagnostic methods, remain limited. Therefore, the aim of this in vitro study was to assess and compare the diagnostic performance of several caries detection methods, including ICDAS, Midwest Caries ID, Diagnodent Pen, and QLF, in detecting occlusal caries in primary molars. The null hypothesis tested was that there would be no statistically significant difference in the diagnostic accuracy of different caries detection methods for detection of occlusal caries in primary molars.

Materials and methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Health Research Ethics Committee of Izmir Katip Celebi University (report number 2022/0180). Sample size estimation was performed using G-Power software. Based on the reference article for statistical calculations [20], the effect size was set at 0.69, the alpha error at 5%, and the power of the study at 85%. The total minimum sample size required for the study was calculated as 88.

Eighty-eight newly extracted primary molar teeth with indication for extraction were included in the study. The parents of the patients who underwent tooth extraction were informed that the extracted teeth would be used in the study, and an informed consent form was obtained from the parents. Teeth without cavitation on their occlusal surfaces, including both sound teeth and those with carious lesions at different depths, were selected. The teeth were thoroughly rinsed, examined under a stereomicroscope (Olympus SZ61, Olympus Optical Co., Tokyo, Japan), and teeth with occlusal sealants, restorations, defects, erosions, or microcracks on the surfaces of the enamel were excluded. The teeth were carefully cleaned of plaque and debris/stains using fluoride-free pumice and a polishing brush. Then, using triple air-water spray, residual that could affect the fluorescence reading were removed from the fissures. All specimens were stored in distilled water at 4 °C for no longer than three months, with the solution replaced daily until use. Before the measurements, a reference photograph of each tooth was taken. The intended measurement point on the occlusal surface was marked using a blunt-tipped explorer by an experienced researcher to assess the caries diagnosis, and this mark was used as a reference across all diagnostic methods. During subsequent measurements, the previously photographed and marked site was consistently located and used for reproducibility. Imaging and diagnostic procedures were performed in a controlled environment where parameters such as light source, camera angle and environmental conditions were standardized. Measurements were made on each tooth at the designated point with ICDAS II, LFpen and MID by two researchers (E.K. and İ.B.) with two weeks intervals. For methods involving repeated assessments, specimens were stored in distilled water (to prevent ionic contamination that could alter surface chemistry and affect optical/fluorescence measurements [e.g., via changes in refractive index, light scattering, or salt deposition]). at 4 °C (changed daily) between the first and second measurements to maintain hydration and prevent alterations in tooth structure. Measurements with the QLF device were performed using an in vitro stand specifically designed by the manufacturer of the device for in vitro studies, allowing images to be taken at a fixed angle and distance. In preliminary studies, repeated measurements were not required for this device as consistent results were obtained when each measurement was repeated.

DIAGNOdent pen (LF pen) measurements

Laser fluorescence measurements were conducted using the LF pen device according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After removing the teeth from the distilled water, they were initially dried with paper, followed by approximately 5 s of drying with a three in one air-syringe. For the measurements of occlusal surfaces, DIAGNOdent pen’s cylindrical sapphire fiber tip was used. Initially, each tooth was calibrated using a ceramic standard as per the manufacturer’s instructions. Measurements were repeated three times at the point of measurement, and the mean of these three values was recorded as the final measurement value. After two weeks, both examiners repeated this procedure. The status of the caries lesion was determined using the manufacturer’s suggested cut-off points as shown in Table 1 [29].

Midwest caries I.D. measurements

Measurements with the Midwest Caries I.D. device were conducted according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Calibration was performed before each measurement using the ceramic stand provided with the device. During measurement, the device was rotated around a pre-determined point without applying pressure, parallel to the long axis of the tooth, to take the measurements. The qualitative scoring system provided by the manufacturer was used to determine the presence and progression of caries (Table 1) [22].

Quantitative-light-induced-fluorescence measurements

QLF device is an optical method for evaluating early caries lesions. It is designed to standardize measurements for mineral loss or lesion depth. The delta Fluorescence (ΔF) value is related to lesion depth and shows the percentage fluorescence loss relative to the fluorescence of sound tooth tissue [23, 30–32]. In our study, this value was used as a reference by the measurements.

QLF measurements were conducted in a dark room with no ambient light using the QLF-D Biluminator™2 device (Inspektor Research Systems BV; Amsterdam, Netherlands). The specimens were fixed on a stand manufactured by the manufacturer of the device for use in in vitro studies, which allows images to be taken at a fixed angle and at a fixed distance. For all measurements, the specimens were positioned at 10 cm from the camera, with the imaging angle set at 90° to the occlusal surface. Before the measurements, the specimens were dried for 5 s with an air syringe at room temperature. To ensure standardization of the measurements, each specimen was then allowed to equilibrate under ambient laboratory conditions (approximately 22–24 °C, 40–50% relative humidity) for 15 min, ensuring that the samples were evenly dry to avoid over-drying or over-moistening. This procedure provided a standardized level of surface dryness and minimized variability in fluorescence readings. After photographing the teeth with a Single Lens Reflex (SLR) camera equipped with a 60 mm macro lens, the Inspektor™ Pro software (Inspektor Pro Software, Inspektor Research Systems, Amsterdam, Netherlands) was used for image analysis. The predetermined points for analysis were manually selected on the computer and ΔF values were recorded. QLF measurements were evaluated using the absolute percentage fluorescence loss (ΔF), and lesion severity was classified according to the ΔF cut-off ranges defined in Table 1.

Histological evaluation

After all measurements were completed, histological sections were taken from the teeth at a pre-determined point parallel to the long axis of the tooth for gold standard evaluation. Prior to sectioning, the teeth were mounted on acrylic blocks. Using an IsoMet device (Isomet 1000, Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, EUA) with a diamond disc under water cooling, the teeth were sectioned parallel to their long axes and in a mesiodistal direction, with the section cuts crossing the center of the point where the measurements were performed. The section with the deepest caries was selected for evaluation. The sections obtained were photographed under a stereomicroscope at ×10 magnification (Olympus SZ4045 TRPT, Olympus Optical Co., Tokyo, Japan) and evaluated by two experienced researcher. In cases where initial assessments differed, the evaluators discussed their observations and reached a consensus decision, which was then recorded as the final evaluation. Lesions were assessed and classified at the D0–D3 thresholds according to the criteria described by Ekstrand et al. (Table 1) [28].

Statistical analysis

Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25 (SPSS Inc. Chicago, IL, USA) software program was used for data analysis. Sensitivity (proportion of true disease identified correctly) and specificity (proportion of true non-disease identified correctly) accuracy, and area under the ROC curve (AUC) were calculated at D1 (enamel caries) and D3 (dentin caries) diagnostic thresholds for each diagnostic system using the histological gold standard. AUC comparisons were used DeLong tests for correlated ROC curves. Comparisons were made between methods by McNemar test at the P = 0.05 level. Since multiple comparisons were made, a Holme-Bonferroni correction was applied to control for the Type I error rate. Cohen’s weighted Kappa values (quadratic) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were calculated to assess the inter- and intra-examiner reproducibility of ICDAS and Midwest Caries ID among researchers. DIAGNOdent pen readings were analyzed by Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (two-way random effects, absolute agreement, single measure).

Results

A total of 88 tooth sections were analyzed histologically to determine the extent of occlusal caries. Among these, 42 teeth were caries-free (D0), 12 teeth had caries limited to the outer enamel (D1), 26 teeth exhibited caries affecting the entire enamel (D2), and 8 teeth had caries extending into the dentin (D3).

The sensitivity values for detecting occlusal caries in primary molars at the enamel (D1) and dentin (D3) thresholds are summarized in Table 2. At the D1 threshold, the Midwest (MW) method exhibited the highest sensitivity, ranging from 61.7% to 76.6%, followed closely by the LFpen method, which ranged between 55.3% and 68.1%. The QLF method demonstrated sensitivity values of 63.8%, while the ICDAS method presented the lowest sensitivity, varying between 48.9% and 76.6%. At the D3 threshold, the LFpen exhibited a significantly higher sensitivity than all other methods (p < 0.05), while the ICDAS method showed no detection capability. The MW and QLF methods had similarly low sensitivity values at the D3 threshold.

QLF also displayed high specificity at this threshold (97.5%), which was significantly higher than those of LFpen (75.9–86.1%, p < 0.05) and which was not statistically different from MW (93.7–98.7%, p > 0.05).

The difference between ICDAS and MW was not statistically significant (p = 0.3595 and 0.6050, respectively). For Examiner 2, there was no significant difference between LF pen (AUC1st = 0.845) and QLF (AUC = 0.861) (p = 0.6883), LF pen and ICDAS (AUC = 0.845) (p = 0.1859) and ICDAS and QLF (p = 0.0629). All three methods performed significantly better than MW (LFpen, p = 0.0006; ICDAS,p = 0226; QLF,p = 0.0001). At the D3 threshold, significant differences were likewise observed among the methods. For Examiner 1, LF pen (AUC1st = 0.897 and AUC2nd = 914) exhibited higher diagnostic accuracy than MW (AUC1st = 0.0510; p = 0.051, AUC2nd = 0.785; p = 0.0121) and ICDAS (AUC1st = 0.836; p = 0.4792, AUC2nd = 0.892; p = 0.7465). For Examiner 2, none of the pairwise comparisons—ICDAS vs. LF pen (p = 0.5377 and 0.3908), ICDAS vs. MW (p = 0.0759 and p = 0.9347), LF pen vs. MW (p = 0.3530 and p = 0.2202), ICDAS vs. QLF ( p = 0.4006), LFpen vs. QLF (p = 0.8606) or MW vs. QLF (p = 0.2259) showed statistically significant differences.

Discussion

Accurate detection of occlusal caries at early stages is crucial in modern dentistry, as it directly influences treatment decisions that can either preserve the tooth structure or lead to invasive restorative procedures. In this study, four caries diagnostic methods; ICDAS, DIAGNOdent pen, Midwest Caries I.D., and Quantitative Light-Induced Fluorescence were compared in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve at two key thresholds of occlusal caries in primary molars: D1 (outer enamel lesions) and D3 (dentin lesions). These four methods were chosen because they represent widely used and clinically accessible diagnostic approaches with different principles (visual-tactile, laser fluorescence, light-based, and quantitative fluorescence), allowing for a comprehensive comparison of performance in primary molars. Based on the results of the study, the null hypothesis was rejected, since statistically significant differences were found in the diagnostic performance of the methods.

Sensitivity, or the true positive value, is critical for the early detection of dental caries. A high sensitivity values indicate that a diagnostic method is effective in identifying existing lesions. Based on the results of the study, at the D1 threshold, the MW demonstrated the highest sensitivity, followed by LFpen and QLF, while ICDAS occasionally shows slightly lower sensitivity values. These findings are consistent with earlier studies emphasizing the efficacy of fluorescence-based systems, such as MW and LFpen, in detecting early enamel lesions due to their ability to identify subsurface demineralization [15, 24]. However, the relatively lower sensitivity of ICDAS at the D1 threshold aligns with its visual examination nature, which may miss subtle subsurface changes that fluorescence systems detect. At the D3 thresholds, LFpen exhibits significantly higher sensitivity compared to all other methods (p < 0.05). MW and QLF show similarly poor performance, both significantly lower than LFpen (p < 0.05). ICDAS, with 0% sensitivity at D3, is also significantly inferior to LFpen (p < 0.05) and statistically comparable to MW and QLF (p >0.05). The high sensitivity of LFpen at this threshold highlights its ability to detect deeper caries lesions in primary molars effectively, which has been corroborated previous studies, who reported the robustness of laser fluorescence systems in diagnosing dentinal lesions [9, 19]. QLF and MW showed limited sensitivity at the D3 threshold, suggesting their restricted applicability for advanced caries stages for primary teeth, a limitation that should be acknowledged when interpreting their diagnostic utility. However, considering the limited number of studies [9–11, 15, 19], in which both methods were compared for caries diagnosis in deciduous teeth, the need for studies comparing the results of this study should be taken into consideration. Interestingly, the ICDAS method did not classify any tooth as D3 at the D3 threshold by both examiners. This finding once again demonstrates the limitations of visual methods such as ICDAS in the classification of more advanced caries lesions that have progressed to dentine. Previous studies have similarly reported that ICDAS is often inadequate for the classification of lesions in advanced stages without cavity formation and emphasized the need for more objective and quantitative methods for the diagnosis of advanced caries [9, 19, 24].

While sensitivity is critical for identifying true caries, specificity, or the true negative value, is just as important to reduce false positives. High specificity ensures that lesions are not overdiagnoses, which could lead to unnecessary treatments and increased healthcare costs. In the current study, at D1 threshold, QLF achieves the highest specificity (92.5%), statistically comparable to LFpen (82.5%−90.0%) and ICDAS (82.5%−85.0%) (p >0.05), but significantly superior to MW (65.0%−67.5%) (p < 0.05). Based on the specificity values for occlusal caries detection at the dentin level (D3), ICDAS (100%), MW (93.7%−98.7%) and QLF (97.5%) achieves statistically higher results compared to LFpen (75.9%−86.1%) (p < 0.05). These findings highlight the importance of method selection based on specificity and the depth of caries detection required. At the D1 threshold, QLF emerges as the most effective method, showing superior performance over MW. The relatively lower specificity of MW at the D1 threshold has been demonstrated in previous studies, and it is thought that this may be due to its tendency to over-diagnose, caused by fluorescence signals from other tooth structures [26, 33]. In terms of specificity, QLF and ICDAS demonstrated outstanding performance at the D3 threshold, achieving near-perfect specificity values. These results align with previous literature indicating that QLF and visual examination methods excel in minimizing false positives in advanced caries detection in primary molars [13, 26, 33]. These findings suggest that while fluorescence-based methods provide valuable diagnostic data, their results should be interpreted with caution in early lesion detection to avoid overtreatment.

The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) is a key metric for evaluating the diagnostic accuracy of methods for occlusal caries detection. Higher AUC values indicate better diagnostic performance, reflecting the method’s ability to correctly distinguish between sound and carious surfaces. In the current study, LFpen consistently achieved the highest AUC values across both thresholds and measurements, ranging from 0.823 to 0.914. These findings highlight the superior diagnostic accuracy of fluorescence-based technology for detecting both early and advanced lesions, aligning with previous studies findings that reported similar robust performance of fluorescence-based methods for occlusal caries detection in primary teeth [12, 14, 15, 26]. QLF exhibited consistently high AUC values (0.861–0.875). Similar results were observed in studies by Gomez et al. [34], which emphasized QLF’s high specificity and suitability for early caries monitoring. ICDAS demonstrated variable AUC values, ranging from 0.661 to 0.910. While its diagnostic performance improved at the dentin threshold (D3), its variability across examiners and measurements highlights its reliance on examiner expertise. This subjectivity has been previously documented by Ismail et al. [35], who noted the method’s strength in specificity but limitations in sensitivity. MW showed moderate AUC values across both thresholds, ranging from 0.668 to 0.892. While its performance at the enamel threshold was lower compared to LFpen and QLF, it demonstrated comparable accuracy at the dentin threshold. Diniz et al. also reported lower accuracy for MW compared other diagnostic tools, emphasizing its utility as an adjunct tool in early detection in primary molars [26].

Reproducibility refers to the ability of a diagnostic method to consistently yield the same results when repeated under similar conditions, either by the same examiner (intra-examiner reproducibility) or between different examiners (inter-examiner reproducibility). High reproducibility is essential in clinical practice as it ensures reliability in diagnostic outcomes, reducing variability caused by subjective interpretation. At D1 threshold, LFpen demonstrates the highest reliability, with ICC values classified as almost perfect (0.755–0.807 for interexaminer and 0.588–0.657 for intraexaminer reproducibility). ICDAS shows substantial agreement at D1, with kappa values ranging from 0.644 to 0.803, while MW provides only moderate reproducibility, with interexaminer kappa values of 0.575–0.587 and intraexaminer values of 0.486–0.516. At the dentin level (D3), LFpen again shows almost perfect reproducibility based on ICC values (0.588–0.807), while MW demonstrates inconsistent results, with intraexaminer kappa values dropping to fair agreement (0.274). These findings align with prior literature that suggests fluorescence-based methods, like LFpen, often yield higher consistency due to their objective, quantifiable measurements, whereas visual-tactile methods, such as ICDAS, rely more heavily on examiner’s expertise and may exhibit variability [12, 24]. ICDAS, while exhibiting robust reproducibility at the D1 threshold, showed inconsistent performance at the D3 threshold. While highly specific, ICDAS relies on subjective interpretation, which can contribute to variability in results. The moderate reproducibility of MW highlights the need for enhanced standardization and operator training to minimize variability. The lower intra-examiner kappa values, particularly at the D3 threshold, highlight its sensitivity to examiner technique and emphasize the need for operator standardization and training to enhance reliability. Additionally, MW’s weaker reproducibility, particularly at D3, corresponds to findings of previous studies, which highlighted the limitations of certain devices in achieving consistent detection of advanced occlusal caries in primary teeth [12, 21]. Additionally, the study also included the QLF device; however, the reproducibility of this method was not evaluated in terms of agreement metrics. The QLF device in this study was used in an in vitro setup, where specimens were positioned on a standard flat surface and photographed consistently from the same angle. Since all analyses were performed based on these standardized images, the measurement results were identical for each specimen. This controlled setup ensured consistent results but precluded the evaluation of interexaminer and intraexaminer agreement, as only a single measurement was conducted for each sample. This methodological limitation should be considered when interpreting the QLF device’s performance in this study.

The observed differences between the methods align with the broader literature that emphasizes the strengths and weaknesses of each diagnostic approach. LFpen emerged as the most accurate diagnostic tool, demonstrating superior sensitivity and reproducibility across thresholds. Its ability to objectively quantify fluorescence changes allows for precise detection of both early and advanced lesions, aligning with previous research on fluorescence-based technologies. However, the relatively high financial resource requirements and technical expertise required for LFpen may restrict its routine use in resource-limited environments, underscoring the need for more accessible options. QLF showed exceptional specificity, effectively minimizing false positives, making it particularly advantageous for preventive dentistry. Its non-invasive visualization and quantification of demineralization support its use in monitoring early carious lesions. However, the reliance on specialized equipment and operator expertise limits its widespread clinical adoption. These challenges echo findings in previous studies emphasizing the trade-off between diagnostic precision and practical implementation. MW demonstrated high sensitivity for detecting early-stage enamel caries, suggesting its utility in identifying initial lesions. However, moderate reproducibility indicates operator-dependent variability, emphasizing the importance of standardized expertise to improve reliability. ICDAS, a widely used visual-tactile method, exhibited robust reproducibility and high specificity, particularly for advanced lesions. Its cost-effectiveness and ease of implementation make it suitable for routine dental examinations. Nevertheless, its relatively low sensitivity for early-stage caries detection and reliance on examiner experience limit its diagnostic range.

This study provides important data by evaluating different diagnostic methods for the detection of occlusal caries in primary teeth under standard laboratory conditions. However, some limitations should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the distribution of caries lesions in the primary teeth used in the study was not equal for each caries score, which may have partially influenced the results. In addition, various clinical variables, such as plaque accumulation, biofilm presence, surface discoloration, and salivary hydration which are commonly encountered in the in vivo environment, may affect the performance of the fluorescence- and light-based diagnostic methods. For instance, biofilm and staining can alter optical properties of the tooth surface, potentially leading to false-positive readings in devices such as QLF and Midwest Caries ID. Likewise, variations in surface moisture may influence the reproducibility of optical measurements. Previous clinical investigations have also emphasized that QLF is not free from such limitations, as plaque deposits and contrast issues in QLF images can compromise accuracy [36]. Furthermore, the time-intensive and complex analysis of clinically invisible lesions, as well as the reduced effectiveness of QLF in areas near the gingival margin or in difficult-to-access regions, present practical drawbacks [37]. These factors suggest that the standardized measurements obtained under controlled in vitro conditions may not fully reflect the variability of clinical practice. Therefore, future in vivo studies are essential to validate the clinical applicability and reliability of these methods and to better understand their practical benefits and limitations.

Conclusion

This study highlights that no single method excels in detecting occlusal caries in primary molars. While advanced and objective methods like LFpen offer superior diagnostic accuracy and reproducibility, simpler tools like ICDAS provide cost-effective and practical alternatives. Combining high-sensitivity methods, such as LFpen, with tools offering exceptional specificity, like QLF, can optimize diagnostic reliability. This multimodal approach minimizes false positives and negatives while addressing the limitations of individual methods.

Pulmonary Artery Pseudoaneurysms: Insights and Interventions
Authors: Ung Bae Jeon
Published: 2024

See the article, “Angiographic Insights and Endovascular Intervention in Pulmonary Artery Pseudoaneurysms: A Comprehensive Clinical and Pictorial Essay”, in volume 85 on page 1083-1096 (https://doi.org/10.3348/jksr.2023.0161).

Severe hemoptysis is a life-threatening condition, with a mortality rate exceeding 50% if bleeding is not controlled (1). Endovascular intervention, particularly bronchial artery embolization (BAE), is the first-line treatment and has proven to be highly effective in controlling bleeding (2).

Typically, systemic arteries, most notably the bronchial artery, serve as the primary targets for transcatheter embolization, forming the cornerstone of interventional strategies for hemoptysis (2). However, in rare cases, fewer than 10%, hemoptysis may be attributed to a pulmonary artery pseudoaneurysm (PAP) (4). Despite the availability of CT imaging for PAP diagnosis, these lesions are often overlooked due to their rarity and the complexity of their clinical presentations.

The etiology of PAPs varies, with infections, traumatic injuries, and neoplasms being the most common causes (1,4). Among these, mycotic aneurysms, caused by the erosion of pulmonary arteries by adjacent infections, are the most frequently reported (3). The underlying mechanism involves the progressive replacement of the arterial wall by granulation tissue, leading to wall weakening. Over time, fibrin deposition results in thinning of the arterial wall, which, under sustained arterial pressure, may form a perfused sac contained by the media, adventitia, or surrounding soft tissues (3).

Historically, surgical intervention was the primary treatment for PAPs. However, advances in interventional radiology have shifted the paradigm, with endovascular techniques becoming the mainstay of treatment. These interventions focus on occluding blood flow to the PAP sac using a range of embolization techniques and materials tailored to the complexity and etiology of the PAP (4,5,6). Depending on the underlying cause and angiographic findings, PAPs can be treated using pulmonary artery intervention alone or in combination with systemic and pulmonary approaches (4,7).

The accompanying article by Choi et al. (7) provides valuable insights into the management of PAPs, offering a structured review of current treatment strategies, including systemic and pulmonary approaches, tailored to different etiologies. This comprehensive pictorial essay serves as a practical guide for clinicians, addressing diagnostic challenges and interventional techniques.

We hope this work enhances readers’ understanding of PAPs and contributes to improved treatment strategies and patient outcomes in cases of severe hemoptysis.

Learning the Maximum of a Hölder Function from Inexact Data
Simon Foucart — Texas A&M University

Abstract
Within the theoretical framework of Optimal Recovery, one determines in this article the best procedures to learn a quantity of interest depending on a Hölder function acquired via inexact point evaluations at fixed datasites. Best here refers to procedures minimizing worst-case errors. The elementary arguments hint at the possibility of tackling nonlinear quantities of interest, with a particular focus on the function maximum. In a local setting, i.e., for a fixed data vector, the optimal procedure (outputting the so-called Chebyshev center) is precisely described relatively to a general model of inexact evaluations. Relatively to a slightly more restricted model and in a global setting, i.e., uniformly over all data vectors, another optimal procedure is put forward, showing how to correct the natural underestimate that simply returns the data vector maximum. Jitterred data are also briefly discussed as a side product of evaluating the minimal worst-case error optimized over the datasites.



Key words and phrases:
Hölder functions, Lipschitz functions, optimal recovery,
Chebyshev centers, information-based complexity.

Theorem 1
.

For the recovery of a monotone quantity of interest
\Gamma
mapping into a Banach lattice
Z
,
given the data (
1
) and the model set (
2
),
a minimizer of
{\rm lwce}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(z)
over all
z\in Z
is given by

\widehat{z}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=\dfrac{\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})+\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})}{2},

while the value of the minimum is

\widehat{r}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=\frac{\|\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}}{2}.

Proof.

Central to the argument is a basic observation
(elucidating the notation
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
, which stands for lower and upper bounds, respectively).
It starts by writing, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, any
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
and any
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,

|f(x)-y_{m}|\leq|f(x^{(m)})-y_{m}|+|f(x)-f(x^{(m)})|\leq\varepsilon_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha},

that is to say

y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}-\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\leq f(x)\leq y_{m}+\varepsilon_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}.

Taking the maximum and minimum over
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
one obtains

(10)

\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)\leq f(x)\leq u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\mbox{ and }x\in\mathcal{X}.

Furthermore, one observes that
both
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
are data-consistent and model-consistent,
i.e.,

|y_{m}-\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})|\leq\varepsilon_{m}
and
|y_{m}-u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})|\leq\varepsilon_{m}
for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
as well as

\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
.
Indeed, for the first statement,
using the defining expressions of
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}

x^{(m)}
for any
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
one sees that
y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}\leq\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})

and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})\leq y_{m}+\varepsilon_{m}
,
so that, in view of (
10
),
one arrives at
y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}\leq\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})\leq u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})\leq y_{m}+\varepsilon_{m}

m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
. This is data-consistency.
For the second statement,
given
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
and
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
one has

\displaystyle\big(y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}-\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)-\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\big(y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}-\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)-\big(y_{m}-\varepsilon_{m}-\operatorname{dist}(x^{\prime},x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}+\operatorname{dist}(x^{\prime},x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha},

which yields

\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)-\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}

by taking the maximum over
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
.
The inequality

is obtained by exchanging the roles of

and
x^{\prime}
,
thus showing that
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
.
This is model-consistency for
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
.
The case of

is treated similarly.

Armed with the above observations,
it is time to prove the prospective result that, for any
z\in Z
,

{\rm lwce}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(z)\geq\frac{\|\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}}{2}\qquad\mbox{with equality when }z=\widehat{z}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=\frac{\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})+\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})}{2}.

To justify one part of this claim, given any
z\in Z
,
the fact that both
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
are model- and data-consistent implies that

{\rm lwce}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(z)\geq\begin{cases}\|\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-z\|_{Z}\\
\|\,\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-z\|_{Z}\end{cases}\hskip-8.53581pt\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(\|\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-z\|_{Z}+\|\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-z\|_{Z}\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}.

For the other part of the claim,
given any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
satisfying
{\bf y}-{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf x}}f\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
,
i.e.,

for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]

the inequalities (

)
and the monotonicity (

yield

\Gamma(\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\leq\Gamma(f)\leq\Gamma(u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})

i.e.,

Based on Theorem

At a fixed

,
introducing
V_{{\bf y},m,n}:=\{x\in[-1,1]:|x-x^{(m)}|-|x-x^{(n)}|\leq y_{n}-y_{m}\}

for each
n\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
one has
V_{{\bf y},m}=\cap_{n\not=m}V_{{\bf y},m,n}
.
For
n<m
,
since
x\mapsto|x-x^{(m)}|-|x-x^{(n)}|
is a ramp function joining
|x^{(n)}-x^{(m)}|
on the left of
x^{(n)}

to
-|x^{(n)}-x^{(m)}|
on the right of
x^{(m)}

and since
|y_{n}-y_{m}|\leq|x^{(n)}-x^{(m)}|
by the Lipschitz condition,
a point
x\in[-1,1]
belongs to
V_{{\bf y},m,n}

if and only if
x\geq\alpha^{(m,n)}
,
where
\alpha^{(m,n)}\in[x^{(n)},x^{(m)}]
is implicitly defined by the equation
|\alpha^{(m,n)}-x^{(m)}|-|\alpha^{(m,n)}-x^{(n)}|=y_{n}-y_{m}
,
or explicitly given by
\alpha^{(m,n)}=(x^{(m)}+x^{(n)}+y_{m}-y_{n})/2
.
For
n>m
, one also obtains that a point
x\in[-1,1]
belongs to
V_{{\bf y},m,n}

if and only if
x\leq\beta^{(m,n)}
,
where

\beta^{(m,n)}\in[x^{(m)},x^{(n)}]
is implicitly defined by the equation

|\beta^{(m,n)}-x^{(m)}|-|\beta^{(m,n)}-x^{(n)}|=y_{n}-y_{m}
,
or explicitly given by

\beta^{(m,n)}=(x^{(m)}+x^{(n)}-y_{m}+y_{n})/2
.
As a result, for
m\in[2:M-1]
, one derives that

\displaystyle V_{{\bf y},m}

\displaystyle=\Big(\bigcap_{n<m}[\alpha^{(m,n)},1]\Big)\bigcap\Big(\bigcap_{n>m}[-1,\beta^{(m,n)}]\Big)=\big[\max_{n<m}\alpha^{(m,n)},1\big]\bigcap\big[-1,\min_{n>m}\beta^{(m,n)}\big]

\displaystyle=[\alpha^{(m,m-1)},\beta^{(m,m+1)}],

where one used the easily verifiable facts that
\alpha^{(m,n)}\leq\alpha^{(m,m-1)}
for
n<m

and
\beta^{(m,n)}\geq\beta^{(m,m+1)}
for
n>m
.
Now, noticing that
\beta^{(m,m+1)}
coincides with
\alpha^{(m+1,m)}
with equal value

\xi^{(m)}:=\frac{x^{(m)}+x^{(m+1)}-y_{m}+y_{m+1}}{2},\qquad m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M-1}],

one deduces that the Voronoi-type cells reduce to

V_{{\bf y},m}=[\xi^{(m-1)},\xi^{(m)}]\qquad\mbox{for }m\in[2:M-1].

This remains valid for
m=1
if one sets
\xi^{(0)}=-1

and for
m=M
if one sets
\xi^{(M)}=1
—for instance,
V_{{\bf y},M}=\cap_{n<M}[\alpha^{(M,n)},1]=[\alpha^{(M,M-1)},1]=[\xi^{(M-1)},1]
.
It follows that,
for any
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,

\displaystyle\max_{x\in V_{{\bf y},m}}\big(y_{m}+|x-x^{(m)}|\big)

\displaystyle=\max\{y_{m}+x^{(m)}-\xi^{(m-1)},y_{m}+\xi^{(m)}-x^{(m)}\}

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{x^{(m)}-x^{(m-1)}+y_{m}+y_{m-1}}{2},\frac{x^{(m+1)}-x^{(m)}+y_{m+1}+y_{m}}{2}\Big\},

where the boundary cases
m=1
and
m=M
require some special attention.
Substituting the latter into (
12
) and
eliminating the repetitions leads to the expression announced in (
11
).
∎

3
Globally Optimal Solutions

In this section,
one still aims at producing a recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z

which is worst-case optimal for the estimation of a quantity of interest
\Gamma(f)\in Z
,
but the optimality here refers to the global sense as described in (
4
).
As mentioned earlier,
the locally optimal recovery map
\Delta^{\rm loc}:{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mapsto\widehat{z}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in Z
from Theorem
1
is already automatically globally optimal.
Nevertheless, one may be interested in other maps
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z
minimizing
{\rm gwce}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\Delta)
,
one reason being that they could be easier to construct and manipulate,
e.g. they could be linear in selected instances where the quantity of interest
\Gamma
is itself linear.
Contrary to Section
2
,
the cases of linear and nonlinear
\Gamma

can seemingly not be treated in one swift stroke,
so they are separated from now on.

3.1
The case of linear quantities of interest

In the result below,
the generic function
\mathbbm{1}_{V}
denotes the indicator function of a set
V
,
meaning that
\mathbbm{1}_{V}(x)=1
if
x\in V
and
\mathbbm{1}_{V}(x)=0
if
x\not\in V
,
while the sets
V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},1},\ldots,V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},M}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
are the Voronoi-type cells defined by

V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},m}:=\big\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\varepsilon_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(n)})^{\alpha}\;\mbox{ for all }n\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]\big\}.

Theorem 3
.

For the recovery of a monotone linear quantity of interest
\Gamma
from
B(\mathcal{X})
into a Banach lattice
Z
,
given the datasites
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}
and the model set (
2
),
the minimal global worst-case error is

\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to Z}{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta)=\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z},\qquad\mbox{ where }u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)=\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big).

Furthermore, the infimum is achieved for the recovery map

\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm glo}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to Z
, where

\Delta^{\rm glo}:{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{M}y_{m}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},m}}\in B(\mathcal{X}).

Proof.

For the first part of the proof,
i.e., the fact that
{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta)\geq\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}
for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z
,
one relies of the aforementioned identity

{\rm gwce}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\Delta)=\sup\{{\rm lwce}_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta({\bf y})):{\bf y}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf x}}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})+\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\}
.
One immediately derives that

{\rm gwce}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\Delta)\geq{\rm lwce}_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta({\bf y}))\geq\widehat{r}_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=\frac{\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})-\Gamma(\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}}{2}=\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z},

where the last equality used the fact (seen from (
5
)–(
6
)) that
\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=-u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and the linearity of
\Gamma
.

For the second part of the proof,
it is enough to show that
{\rm gwce}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm glo})\leq\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}
.
To this end,
let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
,
{\bf e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
,
and let the shorthand
{\bf y}:={\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf x}}f+{\bf e}
be employed below.
Then, for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
select
n\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
such that
x\in V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},n}

and observe that

\displaystyle|f(x)-\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})(x)|

\displaystyle=|f(x)-\sum_{m=1}^{M}y_{m}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},m}}(x)|=|f(x)-y_{n}|=|f(x)-(f(x^{(n)})+e_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq|e_{n}|+|f(x)-f(x^{(n)})|\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(n)})^{\alpha}=u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x).

This reads
-u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\leq f-\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})\leq u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}

and, thanks the monotonicity (
9
) and linearity of
\Gamma
,
it follows that
-\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\leq\Gamma(f)-(\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm glo})({\bf y})\leq\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})
,
i.e.,
|\Gamma(f)-(\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm glo})({\bf y})|\leq\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})
.
Finally,
thanks to the monotonicity (
8
) of the norm,
one concludes that
\|\Gamma(f)-(\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm glo})({\bf y})\|_{Z}\leq\|\Gamma(u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}})\|_{Z}
.
Taking the maximum over
f
and
{\bf e}
finishes the proof.
∎

A few comments are appropriate to conclude on the subject of monotone linear quantities of interest:

•

the two optimal recovery maps that were exposed,
the locally—hence globally—optimal one from Theorem
1

and the globally optimal one from Theorem
3
,
both take the form
\Gamma\circ\Delta
, with
\Delta
given by either

\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})=\frac{\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}+u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}}{2}\qquad\mbox{ or }\qquad\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}y_{m}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{{\bm{\varepsilon}},m}},

i.e., they are obtained by applying the quantity of interest after producing a full estimation;

•

when all the
\varepsilon_{m}
’s are equal to a common
\varepsilon
,
both
\Delta^{\rm loc}
and
\Delta^{\rm glo}
are independent of this
\varepsilon
(but of course the minimal worst-case errors do depend on
\varepsilon
);

•

the local estimate
\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})
is data- and model-consistent,
while the global estimate
\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})
is not model-consistent, as it is discontinuous, hence not Hölder;

•

the global estimate
\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})
depends linearly on
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
while local estimate
\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})
does not—this does not necessarily mean, however, that the former is simpler than the latter,
as producing
\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})
via the defining expressions (
5
)–(
6
) of
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
can be viewed as easier than producing
\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})
, if done through the construction all the Voronoi-type cells;

•

the local estimate
\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})
seems more convenient for streaming data
than the global estimate
\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})
,
as the former is easy to update (via the defining expressions (
5
)–(
6
) of
\ell_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
and
u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
)
when a new
(x^{(M+1)},y_{M+1})
is added,
while this addition potentially affects a large number of Voronoi-type cells for the latter.
But if one is primarily interested in prediction at a fixed
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
then the global estimate is also straightforward to update,
as it essentially returns the value
y_{m}
at the datasite
x^{(m)}
closest to
x
,
so one only needs to check if
x
is closer to
x^{(M+1)}
than to the previous closest datasite.

3.2
The case of the maximum

Turning to the estimation of nonlinear quantities of interest,
as an encompassing result seems out of reach,
one concentrates specifically on the maximum,
i.e.,
\Gamma(f)=\max[f]
.
To start, consider the situation of exact observations,
i.e.,
y_{m}=f(x^{(m)})
for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
.
To estimate
\max[f]
,
it might seem natural to simply output
\max_{m}(y_{m})
,
but this is always an underestimation.
One probably needs to add a slight correction.
To guess what this correction could be,
look at the local optimality result instantiated at
{\bf y}={\bm{0}}
,
which suggests
(\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{0}}}]+\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{0}}}])/2
.
Since
\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{0}}}]=0
and
u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{0}}}
coincides with the function
U
defined in (
7
),
this correction simplifies to
\max[U]/2
.
It turns to be the right guess,
remaining valid when the observation error bounds
\varepsilon_{m}
are all equal to a common
\varepsilon
.
In this case,
a globally optimal recovery map can be chosen independently of this
\varepsilon
, as formally stated below.

Theorem 4
.

For the recovery of the quantity of interest
f\in B(\mathcal{X})\mapsto\max[f]\in\mathbb{R}
,
given the data (
1
) with
\varepsilon_{m}=\varepsilon
for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
and the model set (
2
),
the minimal global worst-case error is

\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{2}\max[U]+\varepsilon,\qquad\mbox{ where }U(x)=\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big).

Furthermore, the infimum is achieved for the recovery map

\Delta^{\rm glo}:{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\mapsto\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(y_{m}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\max[U]\in\mathbb{R}.

This result is in fact obtained by setting
\varepsilon_{m}=\varepsilon

in the more general theorem appearing next.
Before that, one takes notice that the above global recovery map
\Delta^{\rm glo}

is different, though close, to the local recovery map
\Delta^{\rm loc}
from Theorem
1
.
Indeed, imposing
\varepsilon_{m}=\varepsilon
for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
, one easily arrives at the expressions

\displaystyle\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})

\displaystyle=\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(y_{m}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\max\Big[\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)\Big],

\displaystyle\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\Big(y_{m}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\max\big[\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(y_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)\Big],

which could even be made more explicit in the univariate setting by invoking Proposition
2
.
These expressions incidentally reveal that the global estimate is never smaller than the local estimate,
i.e., that
\Delta^{\rm glo}({\bf y})\geq\Delta^{\rm loc}({\bf y})
,
as illustrated in Figure
1
(b).

Theorem 5
.

For the recovery of the quantity of interest
f\in B(\mathcal{X})\mapsto\max[f]\in\mathbb{R}
,
given the data (
1
) and the model set (
2
),
the minimal global worst-case error is bounded below as

\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}}{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta)\geq\frac{1}{2}\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]+\frac{1}{2}\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big),

while the recovery map
\widetilde{\Delta}:{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\mapsto\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(y_{m}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\dfrac{1}{2}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)\in\mathbb{R}

has global worst-case error bounded above as

\phantom{\inf_{\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to Z}}{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\widetilde{\Delta})\leq\frac{1}{2}\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]+\frac{1}{2}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big).

Proof.

For the lower bound,
one points out that
\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
and
u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}

based on an argument similar to one in the proof of Theorem
1
.
One also sees from their defining expressions that
-\varepsilon_{m}\leq\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})\leq 0\leq u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})\leq\varepsilon_{m}
for all
m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]
,
and hence
\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})+e_{m}=0
for some
{\bf e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
and
u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x^{(m)})+e_{m}=0
for some
{\bf e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
.
Therefore, one derives that, for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\Delta)\geq\begin{cases}|\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]\,-\Delta(0)|\\
|\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\Delta(0)|\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(|\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\Delta(0)|+|\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\Delta(0)|\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]\big).

The result follows from the observation that
\max[\ell_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]=\max_{m}\big(-\varepsilon_{m}\big)=-\min_{m}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)
.

For the upper bound on
{\rm gwce}_{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(\widetilde{\Delta})
,
one needs to establish that,
given
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
,
{\bf e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
, and writing
{\bf y}={\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf x}}f+{\bf e}
for short,

-\frac{1}{2}\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]-\frac{1}{2}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)\leq\max[f]-\widetilde{\Delta}({\bf y})\leq\frac{1}{2}\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]+\frac{1}{2}\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big).

Taking the expression of
\widetilde{\Delta}({\bf y})
into account in these prospective inequalities,
one notices that the leftmost one reduces to
\max[f]\geq\max_{m}\big(y_{m}\big)-\max_{m}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)
,
while the rightmost one reduces to

\max[f]\leq\max_{m}\big(y_{m}\big)+\max[u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}]
.
The facts that
\max[f]\geq\max_{m}\big(f(x^{(m)})\big)=\max_{m}\big(y_{m}-e_{m}\big)

and
f(x)\leq u_{{\bf y},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)=\min_{m}\big(y_{m}+\varepsilon_{m}+\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{(m)})^{\alpha}\big)\leq\max_{m}\big(y_{m}\big)+u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}

yield the former and the latter, respectively.
∎

Remark
.

Theorem
4
could be extended in a further direction, incorporating jittered observations, so as to receive data of the
form
y_{m}=f(x^{(m)}+\xi^{(m)})+e_{m}
with
|e_{m}|\leq\varepsilon

and
\|\xi^{(m)}\|\leq\delta
.
It is hereby assumed that this norm is the one generating the metric
\operatorname{dist}
and the domain
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
as its unit ball.
Defining a new notion of global error including the worst case over
\xi^{(1)},\ldots,\xi^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}
,
one would obtain
{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\Delta)\geq\max[U]/2+\varepsilon
for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
,
simply because this new notion
{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
of global worst-case error is larger than the previous notion
{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, for the recovery map
\Delta^{\rm glo}
from Theorem
4
,
one can obtain
{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\Delta^{\rm glo})\leq\max[U]/2+\varepsilon+\delta

by making the necessary adjustments in the second part of the proof of Theorem
5
.
Note that it makes sense to assume that the balls
B(x^{(m)},\delta)
do not intersect,
otherwise a jittered observation
y_{m}
could also come from another datasite
x^{(n)}
with
n\not=m
.
Consequently, if
\mu
is the volumetric measure, then

M\delta^{d}\mu(B(0,1))=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu(B(x^{(m)},\delta))=\mu\Big(\bigcup_{m=1}^{M}B(x^{(m)},\delta)\Big)\leq\mu(B(0,1+\delta))\leq(1+\delta)^{d}\mu(B(0,1)),

yielding
M^{1/d}\leq 1+1/\delta\leq 2/\delta
,
i.e.,
\delta\leq 2/M^{1/d}
.
Since the next section will establish that

\max[U]\geq 1/(2M^{\alpha/d})\geq 1/(2M^{1/d})
,
one derives that
\delta\leq 4\max[U]
,
which in turn implies that
{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\Delta^{\rm glo})\leq\max[U]/2+\varepsilon+4\max[U]\leq 9\,(\max[U]/2+\varepsilon)\leq 9\,{\rm gwce}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\Delta)
for any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
.
In other words, one can assert that
\Delta^{\rm glo}
is near optimal with a factor
9
.

4
Estimations of the Minimal Global Worst-Case Errors

Throughout this section,
given a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the domain
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is chosen to be its unit ball
B(0,1)
equipped with the Lebesgue measure
\mu
normalized so that
\mu(\mathcal{X})=1
.
The metric involved in the Hölder model set
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
defined in (
2
) is also chosen to be the one subordinated to this norm,
i.e.,
\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{\prime})=\|x-x^{\prime}\|
.
As shown in the previous section, the minimal global worst-case errors are dictated by the function
u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}
,
e.g. via its
L_{p}
-norm for the recovery of
\Gamma(f)=f
,

\Gamma
being the identity from
B(\mathcal{X})
to
L_{p}(\mathcal{X})
(see Theorem
3
),
or via its
L_{\infty}
-norm for the recovery of
\max[f]
(see Theorem
5
).
The goal here is to evaluate these quantities optimized over the selection of datasites
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}
.
The first result applies to a general
\mathcal{X}=B(0,1)
and gives a precise two-sided estimate valid for any
p\in[0,\infty]
.
It is followed by an exact expression applying to the case of the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}
.

Theorem 6
.

For
\mathcal{X}
being the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
relatively to a norm
\|\cdot\|
and for
p\in[1,\infty]
, recalling that
u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)=\min_{m}\big(\varepsilon_{m}+\|x-x^{(m)}\|^{\alpha}\big)
,
one has

\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)+\dfrac{c}{M^{\alpha/d}}\leq\inf_{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}}\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\max_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon_{m}\big)+\dfrac{C}{M^{\alpha/d}},

where the absolute constants
c,C>0
can be taken as
c=1/2
and
C=3
.

Proof.

For the lower bound,
given any
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}
,
one uses
\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\geq\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{X})}
and

u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)\geq\min_{m}(\varepsilon_{m})+U(x)

to deduce that
\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\geq\min_{m}(\varepsilon_{m})+\|U\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{X})}
.
Then, in order to bound
\|U\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{X})}
from below,
one adapts the proof of
[
12
]
,
where Sukharev was interested in the optimal recovery of the integral of a Lipschitz function on
[0,1]^{d}
, but otherwise the argument is essentially the same.
Namely, for
\tau>0
to be chosen later, one writes

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}U(x)d\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\{x\in\mathcal{X}:U(x)>t\}dt\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\big[1-\mu\{x\in\mathcal{X}:U(x)\leq t\}\big]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\big[1-\mu\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\|x-x^{(m)}\|^{\alpha}\leq t\mbox{ for some }m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]\}\big]dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\Big[1-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\|x-x^{(m)}\|\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}\Big]dt\geq\tau-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\tau}(t^{1/\alpha})^{d}dt

\displaystyle=\tau-\frac{M}{d/\alpha+1}\tau^{d/\alpha+1}.

This lower bound is optimized when
1-M\tau^{d/\alpha}=0
, i.e.,
\tau=M^{-\alpha/d}
,
yielding

\|U\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{X})}\geq M^{-\alpha/d}-\frac{M}{d/\alpha+1}\big(M^{-\alpha/d}\big)^{d/\alpha+1}=\Big(1-\frac{1}{d/\alpha+1}\Big)M^{-\alpha/d}=\frac{d}{d+\alpha}M^{-\alpha/d}.

The announced inequality now simply follows from
d/(d+\alpha)\geq 1/2
.

For the upper bound, one uses
\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}
and

u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(x)\leq\max_{m}(\varepsilon_{m})+U(x)

to deduce that
\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\max_{m}(\varepsilon_{m})+\|U\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}
.
Then, in order to bound
\|U\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}
from above,
one relies on a folklore result
about covering numbers.
Namely,
one can find a set
\{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(N)}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}

of
N\leq(1+2/\theta)^{d}
points such that,
for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
there exists
n\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{N}]
such that
\|x-x^{(n)}\|\leq\theta
.
Selecting
\theta=3/M^{1/d}
, one has
N\leq(3/\theta)^{d}=M
,
so one can complete the above set to form a
\theta
-net
\{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
consisting of
M
points.
Using these datasites, it is now clear that
\|U\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}=\max_{x}\min_{m}\|x-x^{(m)}\|^{\alpha}\leq(3/M^{1/d})^{\alpha}\leq 3/M^{\alpha/d}
.
∎

The result of Theorem
6
can be refined for the
standard domain
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}
,
as uncovered below.
The case
p=\infty
, not included but obtainable as a limiting case, would in fact be easier to establish.

Theorem 7
.

If
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equipped with the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm,
so that
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}

and
\operatorname{dist}(x,x^{\prime})=\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}
,
and if all the
\varepsilon_{m}
’s are equal to a common
\varepsilon
,
then, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\inf_{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}}\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}\geq\left(\varepsilon+\frac{1}{M^{\alpha/d}}\right)^{p}-M\int_{\varepsilon^{p}}^{(\varepsilon+1/M^{\alpha/d})^{p}}(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{d/\alpha}dt,

with equality being achieved when
M^{1/d}
is an integer.
Furthermore, if
\varepsilon=0
, then

\inf_{x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}}\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}\geq\left(\frac{d}{d+\alpha p}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{M^{\alpha/d}}\qquad\mbox{with equality when }M^{1/d}\mbox{ is an integer}.

Proof.

For arbitrary points
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}\in\mathcal{X}
,
let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{M}
denote the (genuine) Voronoi cells,
i.e.,
V_{m}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\|x-x^{(m)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}\leq\|x-x^{(n)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}\mbox{ for all }n\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]\}
.
One starts by writing

\displaystyle\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\min_{m\in[{1}\colon\hskip-2.84526pt{M}]}\big(\varepsilon+\|x-x^{(m)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}^{\alpha}\big)^{p}d\mu(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{V_{m}}\big(\varepsilon+\|x-x^{(m)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}^{\alpha}\big)^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\{x\in V_{m}:\big(\varepsilon+\|x-x^{(m)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}^{\alpha}\big)^{p}>t\}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\{x\in V_{m}:\|x-x^{(m)}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{d}}>(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}\}dt

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(V_{m}\setminus B(x^{(m)},(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}))dt.

For
\tau>0
to be chosen later, one continues with

\displaystyle\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mu(V_{m}\setminus B(x^{(m)},(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}))dt

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\tau}\big(\mu(V_{m})-\mu(B(x^{(m)},(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}))\big)dt

\displaystyle=\tau-M\int_{\varepsilon^{p}}^{\tau}(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{d/\alpha}dt.

Selecting the optimal
\tau=(\varepsilon+M^{-\alpha/d})^{p}
gives the announced lower bound.

Assuming now that
M=N^{d}
for some integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
one aims at showing that the lower bound can be achieved.
To this end, one partitions
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}
into a regular grid made of
N^{d}
hypercubes of side
2/N

and one picks
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}
as their centers.
Thus, the hypercubes are the Voronoi cells associated with
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}
,
i.e.,
B(x^{(m)},1/N)=V_{m}
.
Coming back to (
13
)
while remarking that

\mu(V_{m}\setminus B(x^{(m)},(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}))=\begin{cases}N^{-d},&\mbox{if }t\leq\varepsilon^{p},\\
N^{-d}-(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{d/\alpha},&\mbox{if }\varepsilon^{p}<t\leq(\varepsilon+N^{-\alpha})^{p},\\
0,&\mbox{if }t>(\varepsilon+N^{-\alpha})^{p},\\
\end{cases}

one obtains, for this choice of
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(M)}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{{\bm{0}},{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{p}}\frac{1}{N^{d}}dt+\int_{\varepsilon^{p}}^{(\varepsilon+N^{-\alpha})^{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{N^{d}}-(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{d/\alpha}\Big)dt\Big)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon+N^{-\alpha})^{p}-M\int_{\varepsilon^{p}}^{(\varepsilon+N^{-\alpha})^{p}}(t^{1/p}-\varepsilon)^{d/\alpha}dt.

Substituting
N=M^{1/d}
shows that the latter indeed equals the previous lower bound.
∎

With
p=1
and
\alpha=1
,
the infimum from Theorem
7
becomes
\varepsilon+(d/(d+1))M^{-1/d}
.
Similarly,
Theorem
6
also demonstrated that in general the minimal global-worst case error over all possible recovery procedures and datasites is composed of two terms:

\varepsilon
, the observation accuracy,
and
M^{-1/d}
, where
M
is the number of observations.
The term
M^{-1/d}
is bad news:
even for perfectly accurate observations,
an error of order
\eta<1
can only be achieved with a number
M
of observations of order
\eta^{-d}
—this is the curse of dimensionality, as formalized in Information-Based Complexity.
But the high dimensionality seems to come with a silver lining, too:
in a scenario when the number
M
of observations is fixed, independent of
d
,
and when the observation accuracy
\varepsilon
can be selected,
the smallest error achieved at
\varepsilon=0

is of the same order
M^{-1/d}
as if one chose
\varepsilon=M^{-1/d}
, which is less stringent as the dimension
d
grows.

Demodex Folliculitis Following Anifrolumab Infusion
Authors: Hannah Bulosan, Lisa Maier, Andrea Kalus
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 34-year-old female with dermatomyositis developed a pustular facial eruption within five days of initial anifrolumab infusion. On examination, she had facial redness in the preauricular areas, across the nose, and the glabella, with scattered superficial pustules (Figure 1 A). Scraping of the pustules (Figure 1 B) revealed numerous Demodex mites (arrows). Demodex mites can be seen as part of the normal skin microbiome, while overgrowth and the resulting inflammatory response are thought to cause folliculitis and contribute to rosacea. Immunosuppression can make this more likely to occur [1]. This patient had refractory dermatomyositis and was also receiving hydroxychloroquine, mycophenolate mofetil, low-dose prednisone, and monthly intravenous immunoglobulin. She improved after treatment with a short course of oral ivermectin and ongoing topical ivermectin. Using the topical ivermectin, she was able to tolerate a subsequent anifrolumab infusion without recurrence or worsening of Demodex folliculitis.

Teaching Point

This case highlights demodex folliculitis as a potential cutaneous complication of immune suppression.

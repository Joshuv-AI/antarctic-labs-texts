Gaussian free fields on Hamming graphs and
lattice spin systems

Abstract




Abstract.
We discuss a class of discrete Gaussian
free fields on Hamming graphs, where interactions are
determined solely by the Hamming distance between
vertices. The purpose of examining this class is that
it differs significantly from the commonly discussed
spin system on the integer lattice with nearest-neighbour
interactions. After introducing general results on
the partition function and covariance for the class of
Gaussian free fields, we present detailed properties of
some specific models. Group-theoretic arguments and
the Fourier transform give some explicit results.

Key Words and Phrases.

Fourier transform, Gibbs measure, Green function,
group representation, random walk, statistical mechanics

Mathematics Subject Classification Numbers.

60G60, 05E30, 60B15, 82B20

Introduction

Consider a finite set
\mathcal{X}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}^{d}
,
where
d,n\geq 2
. Set
\rho(x,x^{\prime})=|\{i:x_{i}\neq x^{\prime}_{j}\}|

for
x=(x_{i})\in\mathcal{X}
and
x^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{i})\in\mathcal{X}
,
which is called the
Hamming distance
. The Hamming
distance induces a relation
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}

by

(x,x^{\prime})\in R_{i}~\Leftrightarrow~\rho(x,x^{\prime})=i\quad\text{for}~i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}.

An undirected graph
(\mathcal{X},R_{1})
with vertices

\mathcal{X}
and edges
R_{1}
is called
the
Hamming graph

H(d,n)
. The Hamming graph
is regular. The number of equidistant vertices
is denoted by

\kappa_{i}:=|\{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}:\rho(x,x^{\prime})=i\}|=\binom{d}{i}(n-1)^{i},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}

(1)

for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, where
\kappa_{1}
is the degree
of a vertex, namely, the number of adjacent vertices.
The Hamming graph is a fundamental object in algebraic
combinatorics, and group-theoretic approach is considered
to be effective
[
2
]
. In this paper, we consider
Gaussian free fields on the Hamming graph.
The Hamming graph is quite different from the integer
lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with edges between nearest-neighbour
vertices. For example, the diameter of
H(d,n)
is

d
, while that of a cube with side length
n
is
nd
.
Noting that the number of vertices is the same in
both graphs, we can say that the Hamming graph is
more densely connected than the integer lattice and
“smaller” than the integer lattice.

Let
\partial\subset{X}
be a distinguished set
of vertices, called the
boundary
of the graph,
and set
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}\setminus\partial
.
In this paper, we discuss a class of discrete Gaussian
free fields on
H(d,n)
, denoted by

(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
, defined by the

Hamiltonian

H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}):=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}J_{i}\sum_{(x,x^{\prime})\in R_{i}}(\xi_{x}-\xi_{x^{\prime}})^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}\xi_{x}^{2},\quad\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X},

(2)

where
(\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
are
values of the Gaussian random variables called

spins
. The Hamiltonian tends to be small if
spins take similar values. Here, we consider
the interaction between spins
\xi_{x}
and
\xi_{x^{\prime}}

solely depends on the Hamming distance

i=\rho(x,x^{\prime})
through weights
J_{i}\geq 0

satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}J_{i}\leq 1
.
The parameter
m\geq 0
is called
mass
.
The centred Gaussian free field satisfying
the boundary condition

\mathbf{E}[g_{x}]=0
,
x\in\partial
has the Gibbs
measure with parameter
\beta>0
:

\mu_{\mathcal{Y}}(S)=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathcal{Y}}}\int_{S}e^{-\beta H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}d\xi_{x}\quad\text{for~each}\quad S\in\mathcal{B},

(3)

where
\mathcal{B}
is the Borell set over
the hyperplane

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\{(\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}:\xi_{x}=0~\text{for~all}~x\in\partial\}.

(4)

The normalisation constant
Z_{\mathcal{Y}}
is
called the
partition function.

In studies of spin systems,
\beta
is called
the
inverse temperature.
The effect of this
scaling is trivial, since (
2
) is
a homogeneous polynomial; more precisely,
the scaling makes the variance of the Gaussian
free field
1/\beta
times.

Research on discrete Gaussian free fields has
a long history; however, much of the research
has focused on their properties on the integer
lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, particularly in two
dimensions (
d=2
). In particular, there has
been considerable research in the context of
Liouville quantum gravity and Schramm–Loewner
evolution (see
[
3
]
for a book-length
treatment), as well as in the context of extreme
values
[
4
]
. On the other hand,
there has been relatively little research on
Gaussian free fields on graphs other than

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The few exceptions are, for
example, Gaussian free fields on compact
manifolds
[
5
]
and the discrete torus

[
1
]
. To the best of the author’s
knowledge, there are no studies on Gaussian
free fields on the Hamming graphs; however,
expansions of Gaussian free fields on
the hypercube,
H(d,2)
, by independent standard
Gaussian random variables were extensively
studied in
[
6
,
10
]
.

In this paper, we discuss discrete Gaussian
free fields on
H(d,n)
with homogeneous
interactions determined solely by the Hamming
distance.

In Section
2
, we introduce the random
walk representation for studying the Gaussian free
fields. First, we introduce a random walk on
a Hamming graph, where the transition probability
is determined solely by the Hamming distance
between vertices, and identify its Green function
as the covariance of the Gaussian free fields
described above. We then discuss the
massive

(
m>0
) cases without boundary
(
\partial=\emptyset
), and the
massless

(
m=0
) cases with boundary. Here, the boundary
refers to the outside of a set of vertices
equidistant from the origin. For the former,
we derive an expansion of the Green function
in terms of an orthonormal system and provide
an expression for the Gaussian free field in terms
of the standard Gaussian distribution.
For the latter, we provide an expression for
the variance of the Gaussian random variable at
the origin.

In Section
3
, we discuss general
properties of the Gaussian free fields using
the random walk representation introduced in
Section
2
and the derived results.
In particular, we discuss the massive cases
without boundary. We provide explicit expressions
for the partition function and demonstrate
the convergence of the free energy
(defined in Section
3
) and
the limits for large
d
or
n
. We show that
in the massive limit (
m\to\infty
), scaled
Gaussian random variables converge weakly to
the independent standard Gaussian distributions.
We then discuss covariance of the Gaussian free
fields. We show that in the massless
limit (
m\to 0
), Gaussian random variables diverge
while remaining perfectly correlated. We show that
in the limits of large
d
or
n
, the Gaussian
random variables converge weakly to the independent
standard Gaussian distributions.
We also show that, for large
n
,
the covariance decays exponentially with the Hamming
distance, irrespective of interactions
among spins.

Section
4
provides detailed
descriptions of three specific lattice spin systems
that help clarify the properties of the Gaussian
free fields derived in Section
3
.
Section
4.1
considers a model in
which the interaction weights between all vertices
are equal. We provide explicit results for all
cases: massive without boundary, massive with
boundary, and massless with boundary.
In Section
4.2
, we consider a model
in which only adjacent vertices have equal-weight
interactions, i.e., the nearest-neighbour
interactions. We show that for the massless without
boundary case, for large
d
or
n
,
the covariance decays exponentially with
the Hamming distance. Section
4.3

considers a model in which interactions exist between
all pair of the vertices, but the weights vary
monotonically with the distance between vertices.
We show that convergence to the limit
d\to\infty

is fastest when the weights are constant, i.e.,
when the model reduces to the first model.

Assumption 1

For

\rho(x,x^{\prime})=\rho(y,y^{\prime})~\Rightarrow~(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}=(P)_{y,y^{\prime}}.

The wreath product of the symmetric groups of orders

\mathfrak{S}_{n}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

on
\mathcal{X}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}^{d}

(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d};\sigma)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\big(\tau_{1}(x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}),\ldots,\tau_{d}(x_{\sigma^{-1}(d)})\big)

(5)

for each element

g=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d};\sigma)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
,
where
\tau_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}

and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Assumption
1
is
the invariance of the transition probability under this
action, namely,

(P)_{g(x),g(x^{\prime})}=(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}.

Such a transition probability matrix can be
represented as

P=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{w_{i}}{\kappa_{i}}A_{i},\quad w_{i}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{i=0}^{d}w_{i}=1,

(6)

where

(A_{i})_{x,x^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\text{if}&(x,x^{\prime})\in R_{i},\\
0&\text{if}&(x,x^{\prime})\notin R_{i},\end{array}\right.

\kappa_{i}
is defined in (
1

Since
P
is symmetric, the uniform distribution is
a stationary distribution, and if
P
is irreducible,
it is the unique stationary distribution.

Let
\hat{P}
denotes the restriction of

to

\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}

(\hat{P})_{x,x^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}&\text{if}~~(x,x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and
\tau_{\partial}

\partial

\tau_{\partial}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\in\partial\}
.
The Green function with killing is the expected
number of visits

before hitting
\partial

or being killed. Let the killing rate be

,

\alpha\in(0,1]
and set

\tau_{\Delta}=\inf\{t\geq 1:X_{t}=\Delta\}
, where

is the cemetery state. We have

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,\partial}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}1_{\{X_{t}=x^{\prime},\tau_{\partial}>t,\tau_{\Delta}>t\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{t}(\hat{P}^{t})_{x,x^{\prime}}=(I-\alpha\hat{P})^{-1}_{x,x^{\prime}}

(7)

for
(x,x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where

Therefore,

When

\partial\neq\emptyset
.
While it is generally not easy to calculate

7

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{d}\frac{K_{j}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))}{1-\alpha\lambda_{j}}=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{K_{j}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))}{1-\alpha\lambda_{j}}.

Moreover, we have the following representation
theorem, which is an explicit version of

[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Set
\alpha\in(0,1)

(m>0)
and
\partial=\emptyset
.
For
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have the expansion of
the Green function

\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}\frac{\zeta^{x\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}\frac{\zeta^{-x^{\prime}\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}.

(17)

Let
(Z_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
be a sequence of
i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. Then,

g_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Z_{y}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}\frac{\zeta^{x\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}},\quad x\in\mathcal{X}

(18)

is a Gaussian free field, whose Gibbs
measure is given by (
3
). Here,

1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}

are the eigenvalues of
I/\alpha-P

(
the
\alpha
-discrete Laplacian with the sign
reversed
)
with orthonormal eigenfunctions

(\zeta^{x\cdot y}/\sqrt{n^{d}})_{y\in\mathcal{X}}.

The eigenvalues are degenerate; more precisely,
for each
y\in\mathcal{X}
, the eigenfunction

(\zeta^{x\cdot y}/\sqrt{n^{d}})
supported by

x\in\mathcal{X}
belongs to the eigenvalue

1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}
with the degeneracy

\kappa_{\rho(y)}
.

Proof.

Let
z_{i}\equiv(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})\mod n
for

i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. We observe

\rho(z)=\rho(x,x^{\prime})
and

\zeta^{(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}=\zeta^{z\cdot y}
.
By using the Fourier transform (
15
),
the expression of the Green function (
16
)
can be recast to

\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{K_{i}(\rho(z))}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{y\in X}\frac{\zeta^{z\cdot y}}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{y\in X}\frac{\zeta^{(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}.

For the second assertion, setting (
18
),
we have

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\mathbf{E}[g_{x}\overline{g_{x^{\prime}}}]=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime}).

The orthonormality of

(\zeta^{x\cdot y}/\sqrt{n^{d}})_{y\in\mathcal{X}}

is followed by

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\zeta^{x\cdot(y-y^{\prime})}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\zeta^{x_{i}(y_{i}-y^{\prime}_{i})}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\sum_{x_{i}=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{x_{i}(y_{i}-y^{\prime}_{i})}\Big)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(n\delta_{y_{i},y^{\prime}_{i}})=n^{d}\delta_{y,y^{\prime}}.

Using (
15
) again, we expand the eigenvalue
(

\lambda_{\rho(x)}=\sum_{i=0}\frac{w_{i}}{\kappa_{i}}K_{i}(\rho(x))=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\zeta^{x\cdot y}\frac{w_{\rho(y)}}{\kappa_{\rho(y)}}

(19)

and the last assertion is followed by

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}\zeta^{x^{\prime}\cdot y}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{w_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}}{\kappa_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}}\zeta^{x^{\prime}\cdot y}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{w_{\rho(z)}}{\kappa_{\rho(z)}}\zeta^{(x-z)\cdot y}=\lambda_{\rho(y)}\zeta^{x\cdot y},

where the complex conjugate of (

) is
used in the last equality. Since

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}(I/\alpha-P)_{x,x^{\prime}}\frac{\zeta^{x^{\prime}\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}=(1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)})\frac{\zeta^{x\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}},

we conclude that

(\zeta^{x\cdot y}/\sqrt{n^{d}})_{y\in\mathcal{X}}
,
are the orthonormal eigenfunctions. For each

y\in\mathcal{X}
, the eigenfuncion

(\zeta^{x\cdot y}/\sqrt{n^{d}})
supported by

x\in\mathcal{X}
belongs to the eigenvalue

1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}
with degeneracy

\kappa_{\rho(y)}=|\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\rho(x)=\rho(y)\}|
.
∎

2.2
The massless with boundary case

For the case of
m=0
(
\alpha=1

the boundary
\partial_{r}

A gambler’s ruin type argument gives the following

Lemma 2.6
.

Set
\alpha=1

(m=0)
and the boundary

\partial=\partial_{r}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:r+1\leq\rho(x)\leq d\},\quad r\in\{1,2,\ldots,d-1\},

and
h_{i}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\rho(x)=i\}
,

. The Green function is given as

G_{1,\partial_{r}}(0,0)=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}w_{i}p_{i}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}},

(20)

where
(p)_{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}}

(I-\tilde{P})p=\tilde{p}_{\partial},

(21)

where

(\tilde{P})_{i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}}

probabilities of the lumped chain of (

)
with the Hamming distance, whose state space is

h_{i}
,

(\tilde{p}_{\partial})_{i}=1-\sum_{j=0}^{r}(\tilde{P})_{ij}

Proof.

(Variance) The expression of the Green function
(
8
) gives

G_{1,\partial_{r}}(0,0)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{0}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})},

where
\tau_{\partial_{r}}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}=\partial_{r}\}

and
\sigma_{0}=\inf\{t\geq 1:X_{t}=0\}
.
We can compute
\mathbf{P}_{0}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})

as follows. The first step should be a jump to
either of
x\in\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{0\}
or
hit
\partial_{r}
. By using the strong Markov property,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{0\}}(P)_{0,x}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})+\sum_{x\in\partial_{r}}(P)_{0,x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{0\}}(P)_{0,x}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})+\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}.

(22)

(Lumping) We denote the equivalence classes
induced by the Hamming distance from the origin as

h_{i}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\rho(x)=i\}
,

i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
.
These sets are invariant under the action of
the wreath product of symmetric groups

\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

introduced by (
5
), but here

\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
is the symmetric group of

\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
.
The lumping of the transition probability matrix
(
6
) exists if

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{y,x^{\prime}}

(23)

is satisfied for each
x,y\in h_{i}
.
This equation (

) holds, because
there exists

g\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

such that
y=g(x)
, and

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{g(x),g(x^{\prime})}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{y,g(x^{\prime})}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{y,x^{\prime}},

where the first equality follows by (
1
).
Therefore, the transition probabilities of the lumped
chain is given by

(\tilde{P})_{\rho(x)j}=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in h_{j}}(P)_{x,x^{\prime}},\quad x\in\mathcal{X}.

(Equation (
20
))
Substituting
p_{i}=\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})
,

x\in h_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
into
(
22
) gives

\mathbf{P}_{0}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}\leq\sigma_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}w_{i}p_{i}+\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}.

and (
20
) follows. By the strong Markov property,
we have

\mathbf{P}_{0}(\tau_{\partial_{r}}>\sigma_{0})=1-p_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\tilde{P})_{ij}(1-p_{j})+(\tilde{P})_{i0},\quad x\in h_{i},

and this is the system (
21
).
∎

We have a simple corollary.

Corollary 2.7
.

If the transition probabilities of a random walk
satisfying Assumption
1
are independent of
the current state, namely, the condition

(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}=(P)_{y,x^{\prime}}\quad\text{for~any}\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X},

(24)

is satisfied for each
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
,
the transition probabilities should be

(P)_{x,x^{\prime}}=1/n_{d}
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, (
21
) is solved immediately,
and we have

G_{1,\partial_{r}}(0,0)=\frac{1+|\partial_{r}|}{|\partial_{r}|}.

(25)

Proof.

Since the transition probability matrix is symmetric,
we have

(P)_{x^{\prime},x}=(P)_{x^{\prime}y}\quad\text{for~any}\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X},

which means that all entries of the transition matrix
are
1/n_{d}
. Then,
(\tilde{P})_{ij}=w_{j}=\kappa_{j}/n^{d}
and
the system (
21
) reduces to the equations:

p_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}p_{j}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{d}w_{j},\quad i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}.

This is solved immediately:

p_{i}=\frac{1-\sum_{j=0}^{r}w_{j}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}}=\frac{n^{d}-\sum_{i=0}^{r}\kappa_{i}}{n^{d}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\kappa_{i}}=\frac{|\partial_{r}|}{1+|\partial_{r}|},\quad i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\},

and we have the assertion.
∎

3
The partition function and covariance

In this section, we present some general properties
of Gaussian free fields on the Hamming graph
H(d,n)

with the Gibbs measure (
3
). In this section,
we discuss the cases that
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
.
They are obtained by using the random walk
representation introduced in Section
2
.
The cases that
m\geq 0
with boundary are discussed
in Section
4
, because the properties
are model-specific.

The following proposition shows that the Gaussian free fields
on the Hamming graph with the Gibbs measure (
3
)
never show spontaneous magnetisation (non-zero expectation
of the mean of the spins). This is anticipated because
we are considering centred Gaussian free fields.

Proposition 3.1
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
. The mean of
the Gaussian random variables of the Gaussian free
fields on the Hamming graph
H(d,n)
with the Gibbs
measure (
3
) is the centred Gaussian
distribution of the variance
1/(n^{d}\beta m^{2})
.

Proof.

By using the expansion (
17
), the mean of
the Gaussian free field becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}g_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Z_{y}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}\frac{\zeta^{x\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Z_{y}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}\frac{n^{d}\delta_{\rho(y),0}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{d}}\sqrt{\beta}}\frac{Z_{0}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-1}},

where the third equality follows by (
15
) with

f=1
and (
14
). Since
1/\alpha=1+m^{2}
and
Z_{0}
is
the standard Gaussian random variable, the assertion
follows.
∎

3.1
The partition function

A group-theoretic argument gives a closed form
expression of the partition function.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
,
the partition function of Gaussian free fields
on the Hamming graph
H(d,n)
with the Gibbs
measure (
3
) is represented as

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\frac{1}{m}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+m^{2}-\lambda_{i})^{-\kappa_{i}/2},

(26)

where
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
the random walk (
12
) and
\kappa_{i}

are given in (
1
).

If
m=0
and
\partial=\emptyset
, the partition
function
Z_{\mathcal{X}}
does not exist, so we
cannot define Gaussian free fields. We have
already seen in Section
2
that
we cannot define the Green function

G_{1,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})
.

Before proceeding to the proof, we prepare
a simple algebraic lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

The modulus of the determinant of the character
table of the direct product of cyclic groups,

\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}
, is
C_{n,d}:=(n^{d})^{n^{d}/2}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that the character
table of the direct product of finite Abelian
groups is given by the Kronecker product of
the character tables of them. Therefore,

{\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d})=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}{\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),

where the character table of a group
G
is
denoted by
{\rm Table}(G)
. Since

\det(A\otimes B)=(\det(A))^{n}(\det(B))^{m}
, where

A
and
B
are
m\times m
and
n\times n

matrix, we have

\det({\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}))=(\det({\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})))^{dn^{d-1}}.

The character table of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is
the Vandermonde matrix:

({\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}))_{ij}=(\zeta^{j(i-1)}),\quad\zeta\equiv e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n},

and the determinant is

\det(\zeta^{j(i-1)})=\prod_{i>j}(\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{j})
.
Therefore,

\det({\rm Table}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}))=\Big\{\prod_{i>j}(\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{j})\Big\}^{dn^{d-1}}.

The discriminant of the polynomial

P(z)=a_{n}z^{n}+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{0}
is defined as

{\rm Disc}(P)=a_{n}^{2(n-1)}\prod_{i>j}(r_{i}-r_{j})^{2},

where
r_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
are the zeros of

P(z)
, and it is known that

{\rm Disc}(P)=\frac{(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}}{a_{n}}{\rm Res}(P,P^{\prime}),

where
P^{\prime}(z)=dP(z)/dz
and the resultant

{\rm Res}(P,P^{\prime})
is the determinant of the Sylvester
matrix. We set
P(x)=x^{n}-1
. Since the zeros are

r_{i}=\zeta^{i}
,
i=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, the resultant is

{\rm Res}(P,P^{\prime})=\left|\begin{matrix}I_{n-1}&0&-I_{n-1}\\
nI_{n-1}&0&0\\
0&n&0\end{matrix}\right|=n^{n}\left|\begin{matrix}I_{n-1}&0&-I_{n-1}\\
I_{n-1}&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{matrix}\right|=n^{n}(-1)^{n-1},

and we have

\prod_{i>j}(\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{j})^{2}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2+(n-1)}n^{n}.

Therefore,
C_{n,d}^{2}=(n^{n})^{dn^{d-1}}=(n^{d})^{n^{d}}

and the assertion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By using the expansion (
18
), we have

H_{\mathcal{X}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}G^{(-1)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})\xi_{x}\xi_{x^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\eta_{x}^{2},

where

\xi_{x}=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{\eta_{y}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}\frac{{\zeta}^{x\cdot y}}{\sqrt{n^{d}}}.

The partition function is given by the integral

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}}e^{-\beta H_{\mathcal{X}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})}\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\eta_{x}^{2}}\left|\frac{d\xi}{d\eta}\right|\mathrm{d}\eta=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\left|\frac{d\xi}{d\eta}\right|,

where the Jacobian is

\left|\frac{d\xi}{d\eta}\right|=\frac{C_{n,d}}{(n^{d})^{n^{d}/2}}\prod_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}=\prod_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}},

(27)

because
C_{n,d}=|\det(\zeta^{x\cdot y})|
is
the modulus of the determinant of the character
table of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}
and we know

C_{n,d}=(n^{d})^{n^{d}/2}
by Lemma
3.3
.
Therefore, we have

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\prod_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1/\alpha-\lambda_{\rho(y)}}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\frac{1}{m}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+m^{2}-\lambda_{i})^{-\kappa_{i}/2}

where we used the fact that
\lambda_{0}=1
,

\alpha=1/(1+m^{2})

.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The factor
(2\pi/\beta)^{n^{d}/2}
is
the partition function of
the
Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution

describing particle speeds in idealised gases,
where the particles move freely without
interacting with one another. The distribution
is that of independent Gaussian random variables
of variance
1/\beta
. The
internal energy

in statistical mechanics is obtained as

-\frac{\partial\log{Z}_{\mathcal{X}}}{\partial\beta}=\frac{n^{d}}{2\beta},

which coincides with that of the Maxwell–Boltzmann
distribution, where

corresponds to the number
of particles. In this sense, the Jacobian
(
27
) encodes the interaction
among particles. The (Helmholtz) free energy is
defined as

-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z_{\mathcal{X}}.

Corollary 3.7

Set
m>0
and

(i)

Proof.

The expression (
26

3.2
The covariance

We are interested in the decay of the covariance
between the Gaussian random variables with
the Hamming distance. If
\partial=\emptyset

that the limit distribution of the scaled
Gaussian free fields as
m\to\infty
is a collection
of independent Gaussian random variables. But
the limiting distribution as

seems non-trivial
(see Corollary
3.5
, ii). In this respect,
Theorem

gives more detailed information.

Corollary 3.8
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
and assume
the stochastic matrix (
6
) is irreducible.
If
m\to 0
, the Gaussian free fields
(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

with the Gibbs measure (

m^{2}n^{d}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})\to\frac{1}{\beta}.

In this limit, the covariances of the Gaussian
random variables diverge, and the Gaussian
random variables are completely
correlated.

Proof.

By the Perron–Frobenius theorem,
\rho_{0}=1

and
\rho_{i}<1
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. From
the expansion (
17
), we have

m^{2}n^{d}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=m^{2}n^{d}\frac{\alpha}{\beta}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\beta}\left\{1+\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{m^{2}\zeta^{(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}}{1-\lambda_{\rho(y)}+m^{2}}\right\}.

The second term satisfies

\left|\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{m^{2}\zeta^{(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}}{1-\lambda_{\rho(y)}+m^{2}}\right|=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{m^{2}}{1-\lambda_{\rho(y)}+m^{2}}\leq\frac{m^{2}(n^{d}-1)}{\epsilon+m^{2}}\to 0

as
m^{2}n^{d}\to 0
, where
\epsilon>0
is one minus
the second largest eigenvalue, where we note that
we assumed that
n^{d}\geq 4
. Therefore,

m^{2}n^{d}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})\to 1/\beta
, and

{\rm Corr}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})\to 1
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Corollary
3.8
suggests that for any
interaction satisfying Assumption
1
,
the covariance diverge in the limit
m\to 0
.
This contrasts sharply with Gaussian free fields
on integer lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
nearest-neighbour interactions.
In the thermodynamic limit of such models
(see Remark
3.6
),
the covariance decays exponentially with
the Euclidean distance for any
d\geq 1

[
7
, Section 8.5]
. In this respect,
further analysis is provided in
Section
4.2.1
.

The next proposition concerns the asymptotic form
of
n\to\infty
. It states that the covariance
decays exponentially with the Hamming distance in
any interaction that satisfies Assumption
1
,
if
C_{m,d}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))>0
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
. The covariance
of the Gaussian free fields
(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

with the Gibbs measure (
3
) satisfies

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{C_{m,d}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))}{\beta}e^{-{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\log n}}+O(n^{-\rho-1}),\quad n\to\infty,

(28)

where

C_{m,d}(\rho)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{1+m^{2}-\lambda_{i+d-\rho}}.

Proof.

The expression of the Krawtchouk polynomials
(
13
) gives

K_{i}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(n-1)^{i}\binom{d-j}{i}\{1+O(n^{-1})\}&j\leq d-i,\\
(-1)^{i+j-d}(n-1)^{d-j}\binom{j}{i+j-d}\{1+O(n^{-1})\}&j>d-i.\end{array}\right.

Substituting this into (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)^{d-\rho}}{n^{d}}\left\{\frac{1}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{d-\rho}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{i=d-\rho+1}^{d}\binom{\rho}{i+\rho-d}\frac{(-1)^{i+\rho-d}}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}\right\}+O(n^{-\rho-1})

\displaystyle=C_{m,d}(\rho)n^{-\rho}+O(n^{-\rho-1}),

where
\rho=\rho(x,x^{\prime})
, and the assertion
holds.
∎

The asymptotic form of
d\to\infty
is more subtle
because it requires a uniform evaluation of
the summand in the Green function (
16
).
To discuss this, we need to specify the interaction,
which we will discuss in Section
4
.
However, for the limit we can obtain the following
explicit result.

Proposition 3.11
.

Set
\alpha\in(0,1)

(m>0)
and
\partial=\emptyset
.
The covariance of the Gaussian free fields

(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
with the Gibbs measure
(
3
) satisfies

\lim_{d\to\infty}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{1+m^{2}-\lambda_{*}},

where

\lambda_{*}=\lim_{d\to\infty}\lambda_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}
.

Proof.

If
x=x^{\prime}
, the Green function(
16
) gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(g_{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)=\frac{1}{\beta n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{K_{i}(0)}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}=\frac{1}{\beta n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{\kappa_{i}}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{d}{d\choose i}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{d-i}\frac{1}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}.

The same argument as that in the proof of
Corollary
3.7
gives

\lim_{d\to\infty}{\rm Var}(g_{x})=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1}{1+m^{2}-\lambda_{*}}.

If
x\neq x^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime})}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{d}{i}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{d-i}\frac{K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))/\kappa_{i}}{1/\alpha-\lambda_{i}}

and our concern is computing the limit:

\lim_{d\to\infty}\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{K_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}(\rho)}{\kappa_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}(1/\alpha-\lambda_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor})},

where
\rho=\rho(x,x^{\prime})
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{K_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}(\rho)}{\kappa_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}}

\displaystyle={}_{2}F_{1}(-\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor,-\rho;-d;n/(n-1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\min(\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor,\rho)}\frac{(-\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor)_{l}(-\rho)_{l}}{(-d)_{l}}\frac{(n/(n-1))^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\rho}(-1)^{l}\binom{\rho}{l}+O(d^{-1})=O(d^{-1})

for large
d
and

1/\alpha-\lambda_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}

is positive and bounded, we conclude that

\lim_{d\to\infty}{\rm Cov}(x,x^{\prime})=0

if
x\neq x^{\prime}
, where
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)

is the Gauss hypergeometric polynomial and

(a)_{l}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+l-1)
.
∎

This result is in accordance with Corollary
3.7

(i) for the limit of the free energy, because it
coincides with that of the independent Gaussian random
variables of variance
1/\{\beta(1+m^{2}-\lambda_{*})\}
.

As we saw in Corollary
3.8
, the covariance
of the Gaussian random variables diverges as
m\to 0
.
However, after taking the limit
d\to\infty
or

n\to\infty
, the variance is finite, even for
m\to 0
.
Moreover, Propositions
3.10
and

3.11
are summarized as the following theorem.

Theorem 3.12
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
. The covariance of
the Gaussian free fields with the Gibbs measure
(
3
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{1+m^{2}-\lambda_{*}},

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{1+m^{2}-\lambda_{\flat}},

where

\lambda_{*}=\lim_{d\to\infty}\lambda_{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}

and

\lambda_{\flat}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}
.
For both of the limits, the Gaussian free field
converges weakly to a collection of i.i.d. Gaussian random variables.

In summary, when
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
,
we have the following. As
m\to 0
, the covariance
of the Gaussian random variables diverges, and
the Gaussian random variables are completely correlated
(Corollary
3.8
). However, after taking
the limit
d\to\infty
or
n\to\infty
, the Gaussian
free field becomes a collection of independent Gaussian
random variables, even for
m\to 0
. The covariance
decays exponentially with the Hamming distance as

n\to\infty
(Proposition
3.10
).
The Gaussian random variables become independent as

m\to\infty
(Corollary
3.5
, i) if
properly scaled. These properties
hold irrespective of interactions among spins,
given that they satisfy Assumption
1
.

4
Specific models

In this section, we consider examples of Hamiltonians
of Gaussian free fields on the Hamming graph
H(d,n)

with the Gibbs measure (
3
). We present
results obtained in Sections
2
and

3
explicitly. We present more detailed
properties of the Gaussian random fields through
further analysis.

4.1
The distance-independent interaction

We consider the Hamiltonian

H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})=\frac{1}{4n^{d}}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}(\xi_{x}-\xi_{x^{\prime}})^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}\xi_{x}^{2},\quad\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X},

in which interactions exist between every pair
of vertices with weight
J_{i}=1/n^{d}
,

i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. This model is analogous
to the Curie–Weiss model in discrete spin
systems (see
[
7
, Chapter 2]
).

According to the recipe obtained in Section
2
,
we consider the random walk determined by

w_{i}/\kappa_{i}=1/n^{d}
,
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
,
and the eigenvalues (
12
) are

\lambda_{i}=\delta_{i,0}
,
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
.
This is the random walk choosing the next vertex
from all of the vertices with equal probability.
This is equivalent to the simple random walk on
the complete graph with self-loops.

The following results are independent of the
Hamming distance among the vertices. Although
some expressions involve boundary
\partial_{r}
,
where
r
is the Hamming distance from
the origin, we can confirm that the following
expressions are valid for any choice of
the boundary.

4.1.1
The massive without boundary case

If
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
, the partition
function (
26
) becomes

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\frac{1}{m}(1+m^{2})^{-(n^{d}-1)/2}=\left\{\frac{2\pi}{\beta(1+m^{2})}\right\}^{n^{d}/2}\sqrt{\frac{1+m^{2}}{m^{2}}},

and the free energy converges to the same limit as

d\to\infty
or
n\to\infty
(see Corollary
3.7
):

-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\log Z_{\mathcal{X}}}{n^{d}}\to\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\{\log(1+m^{2})-\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)\right\}.

The covariance is given explicitly by the Green
function (
16
) as

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\beta n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}+(n^{d}\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}-1)\Big\}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left\{\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{1+m^{2}}+\frac{1}{n^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+m^{2})m^{2}}\right\},

where the second equality follows by (
16
),
and the third equality follows by (
14
).
This expression is consistent with
Theorem
3.12
, but we note that

C_{m,d}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))=0
in Proposition
3.10

if
0<\rho(x,x^{\prime})<d
.

4.1.2
The massive with boundary case

If the transition probabilities of the random walk
are independent of the current state, as
(
24
), we can obtain the Green
function immediately even for the case with boundary.

Let

\partial=\partial_{r}
,
r\in\{1,2,\ldots,d-1\}
.
For each step, there are the following four
possible events for the random walk at

x\in\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}\setminus\partial_{r}
.
We set
h_{i}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\rho(x)=i\}
.

(i)

visits
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
with probability
\alpha/n^{d}
;

(ii)

hits
\partial_{r}
with probability

\alpha\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}
;

(iii)

visits

\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{x^{\prime}\}=\mathcal{X}\setminus(\partial_{r}\cup\{x^{\prime}\})
,
with probability

\alpha\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{d}}-\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}\right);

(iv)

being killed with probability
1-\alpha
.

If (ii) or (iv) occurs, the random walk stops.
Therefore, the expected number of visits
x^{\prime}

before stopping can be computed as

\displaystyle\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}k{t-1\choose k}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n_{d}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\left\{\alpha\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{d}}-\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}\right)\right\}^{t-k-1}

\displaystyle\times\left\{1-\alpha+\alpha\sum_{i=r+1}^{d}w_{i}\right\}.

The summation is straightforward, and we obtain

G_{\alpha,\partial_{r}}(x,x^{\prime})=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{n^{d}}\frac{1}{1/\alpha-1-|\partial_{r}|/n^{d}},\quad\alpha\in(0,1)

and the covariance is

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{\beta}\left\{\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{1+m^{2}}+\frac{1}{n^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+m^{2})(m^{2}+|\partial_{r}|/n^{d})}\right\}.

(29)

We can compute the partition function by direct
integration:

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}}e^{-\beta H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{d}\xi_{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}}\exp\Big\{{-\frac{1+m^{2}}{2}\beta\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}(I-aJ)_{x,x^{\prime}}\xi_{x}\xi_{x^{\prime}}}\Big\}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{d}\xi_{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1+m^{2}}{2}\beta\big(\eta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\eta_{|\mathcal{Y}|-1}^{2}+(1-|\mathcal{Y}|a)\eta_{|\mathcal{Y}|}^{2}\big)\Big\}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{d}\eta_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta(1+m^{2})}\right)^{|\mathcal{Y}|/2}\sqrt{\frac{1+m^{2}}{|\partial_{r}|/n^{d}+m^{2}}},\quad\frac{1}{a}=(1+m^{2})n^{d},

(30)

where we used the expression (
9
), and
J

is the matrix of all entries one. The third equality
follows by the fact that a matrix
(\delta_{ij}-aJ)

is diagonalised by an orthogonal matrix as

U{\rm diag}(1,1,\ldots,1,1-|\mathcal{Y}|a)U^{-1}
.

4.1.3
The massless with boundary case

If
m=0
and
\partial=\partial_{r}
, the Green
function
G_{1,\partial_{r}}(0,0)
is already
given in (
25
), but a more general
result can be obtained immediately from
(
7
)

G_{1,\partial_{r}}(x,x^{\prime})=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{|\partial_{r}|}{n^{d}}\right)^{t-1}\frac{1}{n^{d}}=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{|\partial_{r}|},

and we have

{\rm Cov}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\beta}G_{1,\partial_{r}}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{|\partial_{r}|}\right).

Since this expression coincides with (
29
)
as
m\to 0
, the covariance is continuous at
m=0
.
The partition function is given by (
30
),
and we have

Z_{\mathcal{Y}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{|\mathcal{Y}|/2}\sqrt{\frac{n^{d}}{|\partial_{r}|}}.

4.2
The nearest-neighbour interaction

We consider the Hamiltonian

H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{(x,x^{\prime})\in R_{1}}\frac{1}{(n-1)d}(\xi_{x}-\xi_{x^{\prime}})^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\xi_{x}^{2},\quad\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X},

(31)

in which interactions exist between spins on
nearest-neighbour vertices with equal weight

1/\{(n-1)d\}
. Since models on the integer lattice

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with nearest-neighbour interaction
dominate in literature, it is reasonable to consider
this model.

We consider the random walk determined by

\frac{w_{i}}{\kappa_{i}}=\frac{\delta_{i,1}}{(n-1)d},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\},

and the eigenvalues (
12
) are

\lambda_{i}=1-\frac{ni}{(n-1)d},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}.

This is the simple random walk, where in each step
transitions occur between nearest-neighbour vertices.

4.2.1
The massive without boundary case

If
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
, the partition
function (
26
) becomes

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\frac{1}{m}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(m^{2}+\frac{ni}{(n-1)d}\right)^{-\kappa_{i}/2}

and the free energy converges to the same limit as

d\to\infty
or
n\to\infty
:

-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\log Z_{\mathcal{X}}}{n^{d}}\to\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\{\log(1+m^{2})-\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)\right\}.

The covariance is given by the Green function
(
16
) as

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\beta n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))}{m^{2}+ni/\{(n-1)d\}}.

(32)

When
n\to\infty
, the covariance decays exponentially with
the Hamming distance (Proposition
3.10
), and we
have (
28
) with

\displaystyle C_{m,d}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{1+m^{2}+(i-\rho)/d}=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}(-1)^{i}\int_{0}^{1}v^{m^{2}+(i-\rho)/d}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}v^{m^{2}-\rho/d}(1-v^{1/d})^{\rho}\mathrm{d}v=d\int_{0}^{1}z^{d(1+m^{2})-\rho-1}(1-z)^{\rho}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=d~{\rm Beta}(d(1+m^{2})-\rho,\rho+1).

We have the following estimate for the limit

d\to\infty
, which states that the covariance
decays exponentially with the Hamming distance.

Proposition 4.1
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
. The Gaussian
free field
(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
with
the Hamiltonian (
31
) satisfies

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\rho(x,x^{\prime})!}{\beta(1+m^{2})}e^{-\rho(x,x^{\prime})\log d}\{1+O(d^{-1})\}.

Proof.

The expression (
32
) leads to

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,\emptyset}

\displaystyle(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}v^{-\alpha\big(1-\frac{ni}{(n-1)d}\big)}K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}v^{-\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\{v^{\frac{n\alpha}{(n-1)d}}\}^{i}K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}v^{-\alpha}\big(1+(n-1)v^{\frac{n\alpha}{(n-1)d}}\big)^{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\big(1-v^{\frac{n\alpha}{(n-1)d}}\big)^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\frac{(n-1)d}{n\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\frac{(n-1)d}{n\alpha}(1-\alpha)-1}(1+(n-1)s)^{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}(1-s)^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}s,

where we changed the integration variable from
v
to

s=v^{n\alpha/\{(n-1)d\}}
, and we used (
14
) in
the third equality. Since the dominant contribution to the integral
coming around
s=1
, we change the integration variable
from
u
to
s=1-u/d
:

\displaystyle\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n(nd)^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}}\int_{0}^{d}\left(1-\frac{u}{d}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}d(1-1/n)-1}\left\{1-\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\frac{u}{d}\right\}^{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}u^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}u.

For large
d
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\left(1-\frac{u}{d}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}d(1-1/n)-1}\left\{1-\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\frac{u}{d}\right\}^{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}u^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u}{\alpha}(1-1/n)}u^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\{1+O(d^{-1})\}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\rho(x,x^{\prime})!\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-1/n}\right)^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})+1}\{1+O(d^{-1})\}.

Since

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})/\beta
,
the assertion holds.
∎

As we saw in Remark
3.9
, the behaviour
of the Gaussian free field with nearest-neighbour
interaction on the Hamming graph
H(d,n)
is quite
different from that on the integer lattice

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. However, as we saw in this subsection,
for large
d
or
n
, the behaviour of the Gaussian
free field on
H(d,n)
becomes similar to that on

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, in the sense that the covariance of
the Gaussian random variables decays exponentially
with the Hamming distance.

4.2.2
The massive with boundary case

Applying the monotone convergence theorem
to the Green function (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r,d\to\infty}G_{\alpha,\partial_{r}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Big(\lim_{r,d\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}1_{\{X_{t}=x^{\prime},\tau_{\partial_{r}}>t,\tau_{\Delta}>t\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Big(\lim_{r,d\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}1_{\{X_{t}=x^{\prime},\tau_{\partial_{r}}>r,\tau_{\Delta}>t\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{x}\Big(\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}1_{\{X_{t}=x^{\prime},\tau_{\Delta}>t\}}\Big)=\lim_{d\to\infty}G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime}),

with keeping
r<d
, where

\tau_{\partial_{r}}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\in\partial_{r}\}
.
The second equality holds because we are considering
the simple random walk. Proposition
4.1

gives

\lim_{r,d\to\infty}{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{\beta(1+m^{2})}.

(33)

4.2.3
The massless with boundary case

If
m=0
and
\partial=\partial_{r}
,
r\in\{1,2,\ldots,d-1\}
,
we use Lemma
2.6
. The system of linear equations
(
21
) is equivalent to the three-term recurrence
relation

p_{x+1}-p_{x}=\frac{x}{d-x}\frac{1}{n-1}(p_{x}-p_{x-1}),\quad\text{for}\quad x=1,2,\ldots,r

with the boundary condition
p_{0}=0
,
p_{r+1}=1
.
It is straightforward to see

1=p_{r+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}{d-1\choose i}^{-1}(n-1)^{-i}p_{1},

and we have

p_{1}^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}{d-1\choose i}^{-1}(n-1)^{-i}.

The expression (
20
) gives

{\rm Var}(g_{0})=\frac{1}{\beta}G_{1,\partial_{r}}(0,0)=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{r}{d-1\choose i}^{-1}(n-1)^{-i}.

(34)

Since

{d-1\choose i}(n-1)^{i}=n^{d-1}{d-1\choose i}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{d-1-i}

is the probability mass function of the binomial
distribution, it is unimordal. Evaluating
the summation in (
34
) seems not
straightforward, but we have the following limit.

Proposition 4.2
.

Set
m=0
and
\partial=\partial_{r}
,
r\in\{1,2,\ldots,d-1\}
.
The Gaussian free field
(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
with
the Hamiltonian (
31
) satisfies

\lim_{d\to\infty}{\rm Var}(g_{0})=\frac{1}{\beta}.

(35)

Proof.

Set
x=r/d\in(0,1)
and suppose
x<1-1/n
. Since

{{d-1}\choose{i}}(n-1)^{i}\geq\left(\frac{(d-i)(n-1)}{i}\right)^{i},\quad i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Var}(g_{0})\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left(\frac{i}{(d-i)(n-1)}\right)^{i}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left(\frac{x}{(1-x)(n-1)}\right)^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{1-[x/\{(1-x)(n-1)\}]^{r+1}}{1-x/\{(1-x)(n-1)\}}\to\frac{1}{\beta}

as
r,d\to\infty
, where the first inequality
holds by the term
i=0
of the summation
in (
34
). Since (
34
)
is increasing with
r
, the limit holds for
any
r<(1-1/n)d
with
d\to\infty
. If

x\geq 1-1/n
, we divide the summation in
(
34
) to the two parts:

S_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor(1-1/n)d\rfloor}{d-1\choose i}^{-1}(n-1)^{-i},\quad S_{2}=\sum_{i=\lceil(1-1/n)d\rceil}^{r}{d-1\choose i}^{-1}(n-1)^{-i}.

We have already seen that

\lim_{d\to\infty}S_{1}\to 1
. For the second
part,

|S_{2}|<(r+1-\lceil(1-1/n)d\rceil){d-1\choose r}^{-1}(n-1)^{-r},

since we know that the summand increases.
By using the Stirling formula, we have

{d-1\choose r}^{-1}(n-1)^{-r}\sim\sqrt{2\pi d}\exp\left[d\{x\log x+(1-x)\log(1-x)-x\log(n-1)\}\right]

as
r,d\to\infty
. Since the exponent is
always negative,
\lim_{d\to\infty}|S_{2}|=0

and the assertion holds.
∎

Since (
33
) with
x=x^{\prime}
coincides with
(
35
) as
m\to 0
, the variance
is continuous at
m=0
.

4.3
The binomially-weighted interaction

We consider the Hamiltonian

H_{\mathcal{Y}}((\xi_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{d\choose i}\frac{\gamma^{i}(1-\gamma)^{d-i}}{\kappa_{i}}\sum_{(x,x^{\prime})\in R_{i}}(\xi_{x}-\xi_{x^{\prime}})^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\xi_{x}^{2},~\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X},

(36)

in which interactions exist between every pair of
vertices, as in the distance-independent model
discussed in Section
4.1
, but
weighted. The weights are controlled by the binomial
probability mass function of parameter

\gamma\in(0,1)
such that long-range
interactions dominate with the increase of
\gamma
.
The weight

J_{i}={d\choose i}\frac{\gamma^{i}(1-\gamma)^{d-i}}{\kappa_{i}}=(1-\gamma)^{d}\left\{\frac{1}{(1/\gamma-1)(n-1)}\right\}^{i},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}

monotonically decreases in
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
if

\gamma\in(0,1-1/n)
, is constant if
\gamma=1-1/n
,
and increases if
\gamma\in(1-1/n,1)
. We note that
if
\gamma=1-1/n
this model reduces to the
distance-independent interaction model discussed in
Section
4.1
.

Since the analysis of this model is considerably
harder than the analysis of the two models already
discussed, we discuss only the case that
m>0

and
\partial=\emptyset
.

We consider the random walk determined by

w_{i}={d\choose i}\gamma^{i}(1-\gamma)^{d-i},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}.

The eigenvalues (
12
) are

\lambda_{i}=\left(1-\frac{n\gamma}{n-1}\right)^{i},\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}.

This random walk is regarded as a long-range random
walk: the range
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
is drawn from
the binomial distribution, and the next vertex is
chosen from the equally distant vertices with equal
probability.

The partition function (
26
) becomes

Z_{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{n^{d}/2}\frac{1}{m}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left\{1+m^{2}-\left(1-\frac{n\gamma}{(n-1)}\right)^{i}\right\}^{-\kappa_{i}/2},

and the free energy converges to the distinct limits
as
d\to\infty
or
n\to\infty
:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{\log Z_{\mathcal{X}}}{n^{d}}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\{\log(1+m^{2})-\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)\right\};

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log Z_{\mathcal{X}}}{n^{d}}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\left[\log\{1+m^{2}-(1-\gamma)^{d}\}-\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)\right\}.

The Green function (
16
) for this model is

G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))}{1-\alpha\left(1-\frac{n\gamma}{n-1}\right)^{i}}.

(37)

When
n\to\infty
, the covariance decays exponentially
with the Hamming distance, and we have (
28
)
with

\displaystyle C_{m,d}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{1/\alpha-(1-\gamma)^{i+d-\rho}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\{\alpha(1-\gamma)^{i+d-\rho}\}^{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\{\alpha(1-\gamma)^{d-\rho}\}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{\rho}{i}(-1)^{i}(1-\gamma)^{ik}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\{\alpha(1-\gamma)^{d-\rho}\}^{k}\{1-(1-\gamma)^{k}\}^{\rho},

and we observe that

0<C_{m,d}(\rho)\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{k}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}<\infty.

We have the following estimate for the limit
d\to\infty
,
which is consistent with Theorem
3.12
.
Here, we can see that the convergence does not
depend on the Hamming distance, and the fastest
convergence occurs when the weights is constant,
i.e., the model reduces to the distance-independent
interaction model.

Proposition 4.3
.

Set
m>0
and
\partial=\emptyset
. The Gaussian free field

(g_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
with the Hamiltonian
(
36
) satisfies

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{\beta(1+m^{2})}+O\big\{e^{d\sup_{i\geq 1}\{\log a_{i}(\gamma)\}}\big\},\quad d\to\infty,

where

a_{i}(\gamma)=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1-\frac{n\gamma}{n-1}\right)^{i}+\frac{1}{n}\in(0,1),\quad i\geq 0.

In particular, if
\gamma\in(0,1-1/n]
, it satisfies

{\rm Cov}(g_{x},g_{x^{\prime}})=\frac{\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}}{\beta(1+m^{2})}+O\big\{e^{d\log(1-\gamma)}\big\}.\quad d\to\infty,

When
\gamma=1-1/n
, the covariance converges fastest.

Proof.

(Asymptotic forms) Since
|\alpha(1-n\gamma/(n-1))|<1
, the
expression (
37
) leads to

\displaystyle\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\{\alpha\Big(1-\frac{n\gamma}{n-1}\Big)^{i}\right\}^{j}K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(1-\frac{n\gamma}{n-1}\Big)^{ji}K_{i}(\rho(x,x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{(n-1)^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{j}\{a_{j}(\gamma)\}^{d-\rho(x,x^{\prime})}\{1-a_{j}(\gamma)\}^{\rho(x,x^{\prime})},

where the third equality follows by (
14
).
If
x=x^{\prime}
, we have

\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)=\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}.

Otherwise,

\displaystyle\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}\exp\left\{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\log\frac{1-a_{i}(\gamma)}{a_{i}(\gamma)(n-1)}\right\}.

\lim_{d\to\infty}\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})/\beta

gives the first assertion. Noting that the sequence

a_{i}
,
i\geq 1
decreases monotonically if
\gamma\in(0,1-1/n]
,
we have

\displaystyle 0<\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)-\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{1}(\gamma)\}^{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}(1-\gamma)^{d}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}(1-\gamma)^{d}

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\alpha G_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})\leq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}(1-\gamma)^{d}\exp\left\{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\log\frac{1-a_{i}(\gamma)}{a_{i}(\gamma)(n-1)}\right\}

\displaystyle<\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha}(1-\gamma)^{d}

holds for
x\neq x^{\prime}
, where we used
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=1/n
.
Therefore, the second assertion follows.

(Convergence, i) Let the Green function be denoted by

G^{(\gamma)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)
.
If
\gamma\in(0,1-1/n]
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha G^{(\gamma)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)-\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(1-1/n)\}^{d}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{d}}

\displaystyle=\alpha G^{(1-1/n)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)-\alpha.

For any
\gamma\in(0,1-1/n]
, there exists
N
such that

\displaystyle\alpha G^{(\gamma)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}\exp\left\{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\log\frac{1-a_{i}(\gamma)}{a_{i}(\gamma)(n-1)}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{d}}=\alpha G^{(1-1/n)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime}),\quad x\neq x^{\prime}.

for
d>N
. These inequalities show that the convergence
of the covariance is fastest when
\gamma=1-1/n
.

(Convergence, ii)
For any
\gamma\in[1-1/n,1)
, there exists
J
such that

a_{J}(\gamma)>1/n
. For such
J
, there exists
N^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha G^{(\gamma)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)-\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\{a_{i}(\gamma)\}^{d}>\alpha^{J}a_{J}(\gamma)^{d}

\displaystyle>\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{d}}=\alpha G^{(1-1/n)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x)-\alpha.

for
d>N^{\prime}
. In the same way, if
x\neq x^{\prime}
, we can show that

\alpha G^{(\gamma)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})>\alpha G^{(1-1/n)}_{\alpha,\emptyset}(x,x^{\prime})
for sufficiently large
d
.
These inequalities show that the convergence
of the covariance is fastest when
\gamma=1-1/n
.
∎

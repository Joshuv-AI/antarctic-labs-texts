1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study well-posedness of the Vlasov–Poisson equation in
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces with relatively low regularity. The equation describes evolution of a distribution
f(t,x,v):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
:

(VP)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f\pm\nabla_{x}U\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,&\\
\Delta_{x}U=\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fdv.&\end{aligned}\right.

We shall take
n\geq 3
, and then
U(t,x)
is explicitly given by

\begin{split}U(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma_{n}(x-y)\rho(t,y)dy,\qquad\Gamma_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n(2-n)\omega_{n}}|x|^{2-n}\end{split}

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit
n
-ball. Moreover, we consider both signs
\pm
in (
VP
): the positive sign corresponds to collision-less plasma dynamics and the negative to astrophysical situations, most notably galaxy dynamics (
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
).

Our main result gives local well-posedness of solutions to (
VP
) in
H^{s}
spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Vlasov–Poisson equation (
VP
) is locally well-posed in
(H^{s}\cap L^{1})(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
s>n/2-1/4
with compact support in
v
. That is, with initial data
f_{0}\in(H^{s}\cap L^{1})(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
f_{0}(x,v)=0
whenever
|v|>Q_{0}
for some
Q_{0}>0
, there exist
T,Q>0
depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}\cap L^{1}},Q_{0}
, and a unique solution
f\in C([-T,T];(H^{s}\cap L^{1})(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to (
VP
) with
f(t=0)=f_{0}
satisfying
f(t,x,v)=0
whenever
|v|>Q
.

Remark 1.2
.

We give a few remarks regarding the statement of the theorem.

•

The initial data
f_{0}
satisfying the assumptions above is not necessarily bounded: that is, when
s\leq n/2
,
f_{0}
is not required to belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
(and also to
L^{p}
with
p
sufficiently large). The mechanism which gives this low regularity well-posedness is the
velocity averaging effect
, which, in particular, makes the density function
\rho
bounded in space even when
f
is not. In the case
s=n/2-1/4
, our proof shows local well-posedness in the case when the initial
H^{s}
norm is small relative to
Q(0)
, the initial support size in
v
. It is not clear to us whether local well-posedness persists below
n/2-1/4
; the velocity averaging lemma we use (see Lemma
2.2
below) gives
1/4
derivatives gain in
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces, and to the best of our knowledge this is not known to be sharp in the time-dependent case.

•

The compact support assumption in
v
is for simplicity, and the result can be extended to the case when the solution decays sufficiently fast (e.g., exponential decay) in
v
.

•

While
f_{0}\geq 0
is natural to assume in view of the physical background, it is not really necessary for local well-posedness. Furthermore, for plasma problems, it is both natural and convenient to consider an external density function
\rho_{ext}
and define
U
as the solution of
\Delta_{x}U=\rho+\rho_{ext}
. As long as
\rho_{ext}
is given as a sufficiently smooth function, the proof of this local well-posedness result carries over.

1.1.
Motivation for studying low regularity problems

One motivation for having well-posedness of (
VP
) in low regularity spaces is that it provides a framework for studying the evolution of “singular structures” present in the initial data. For instance, Theorem
1.1
allows for data having algebraic singularities
f_{0}\sim|x|^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
. This point can be illustrated by the extreme case when
f
is given as a time-dependent measure on the
(x,v)
space. This includes the case of
electron sheets
, which model physical situations where the plasma density is highly concentrated along a hypersurface; see
[
29
, Chap. 13]
for a nice exposition. Even in the simplest case of one spatial dimension, such singular solutions show very interesting dynamical behavior, including singularity formation and non-uniqueness of weak solutions (
[
9
,
40
,
35
,
29
]
).

Recently, there has been a lot of progress on well/ill-posedness of compressible and incompressible Euler equations in low regularity Sobolev spaces. In the incompressible case, ill-posedness results (norm inflation and non-existence) are available once the initial velocity belongs to
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

s\leq\frac{n}{p}+1
and not better, which basically shows that there are no regularizing effects in time (
[
5
,
7
,
10
,
23
,
28
,
17
]
). The situation for the compressible case is much more complicated; not only different Sobolev regularity for the velocity, vorticity, and density can be prescribed, there are dispersive smoothing effects which give well-posedness at relatively low regularity (
[
38
,
8
,
39
,
41
]
). In several cases, instantaneous shock formation can be used to show ill-posedness sharply below such well-posedness results (
[
24
,
27
,
2
]
). Although the Vlasov-Poisson equation is closely related to these fluid systems, the corresponding low regularity problem in Sobolev spaces seems to be much less explored. There does not seem to be any ill-posedness results for the Vlasov–Poisson equation in Sobolev spaces, and it is not clear whether the threshold
s>n/2-1/4
in Theorem
1.1
is sharp. This is because there are no examples in the literature showing the sharpness of the gain of a
1/4
-order derivative in Lemma
2.2
in the time-dependent case, to the best of our knowledge.

1.2.
Previous works on well-posedness

Let us now review various works on well-posedness of (
VP
), focusing on the whole space case
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A more extensive list of references, as well as history, can be found in the textbooks
[
12
,
34
,
4
]
.

The classical paper of Horst
[
14
]
proves local well-posedness for
C^{1}
data in the whole space with any
n\geq 1
, with global well-posedness for dimensions 1 and 2. Global well-posedness for classical solutions in
n=3
was obtained in
[
32
,
33
,
36
]
, see
[
16
,
6
]
for the case of bounded domains. For
n\geq 4
, classical solutions can blow up in finite time (
[
15
]
). Recent works by Chen and He (
[
20
,
19
,
18
]
) give local well-posedness in (weighted in
v
) Sobolev, Bessel potential, and Besov spaces. In the case of
L^{2}
-based spaces, they require
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
. Their Besov local well-posedness works in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
s>\frac{n}{p}+1
,
1<p<\frac{n}{2}
,
n\geq 3
, and
1<r<\infty
. They point out that this well-posedness result covers some unbounded data.

On the other hand, it has been well-known since
[
3
]
that global
existence
of weak solutions holds with little regularity assumptions on the data, see also
[
26
,
31
,
33
]
for the theory of weak solutions. Regarding the question of
uniqueness
for weak solutions, Loeper
[
25
]
gave a very nice criterion which states that
\rho\in L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
ensures uniqueness up to time
T
. In this result, regarding
f
, it is sufficient to assume that
f_{0}
is a non-negative bounded measure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This uniqueness criterion was strengthened in several directions. Among those,
[
37
]
showed that for
n=1
, BV regularity is sufficient for well-posedness. Moreover,
[
30
,
11
]
extended the uniqueness criterion to
\rho
belonging to the so-called Yudovich spaces, which allows the
L^{p}
norm of
\rho
to grow at some rate as
p\to\infty
.

Lastly, we remark that the proof of uniqueness in our Theorem
1.1
is also based on the idea of Loeper. The main difference is that in our case,
\rho_{0}
does not necessarily belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(although it does for
t>0
by velocity averaging). For clarity, we provide a proof of this extension in the Appendix.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we obtain the necessary
H^{s}
a priori estimate for the solution. Then we prove the main result by proving existence and uniqueness in §
3
and §
4
, respectively. In the Appendix, two technical results are proved, namely the velocity averaging lemma (
[
1
,
13
]
) and a lemma on the existence and uniqueness of flows generated by
L^{2}([0,T];C^{1+\alpha})
vector fields.

2.
A Priori Estimate

In this section, we assume the existence of a solution to (
VP
) with sufficient regularity and an
H^{s}
a priori estimate for the solution. We shall only consider
t\geq 0
, since
t<0
can be treated in the same way. Rigorous proof of existence and uniqueness will be given in the next section. Moreover, we shall restrict ourselves to the case
s\in(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4},\frac{n}{2}+1]
, since the range
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
was already covered by
[
18
]
and is simpler.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f
be a sufficiently smooth solution to (
VP
) on
[0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}>0
and assume that it is compactly supported in
v
uniformly in
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, with initial data
f(t=0)=f_{0}
. Then, taking
Q(t)>0
be the maximal support radius in
v
of
f(t,\cdot)
,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q(t)\leq 2Q(0)+1\end{split}

for some
0<T\leq T_{0}
depending only on
Q_{0}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this proposition. To begin with, we observe that
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
estimates follow immediately since the characteristic vector field
(v,-\nabla_{x}U(x))
is divergence-free in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and thus
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms are conserved:

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Hence, it suffices to estimate the homogeneous Sobolev norm
\dot{H}^{s}
. To this end, we take the Fourier transform in the
(x,v)
variables. The dual variables for
v
and
x
are denoted by
\mu
and
\xi
, respectively. This gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)+\nabla_{\mu}\cdot\xi\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)-\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta=0,

and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)\left|\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi d\mu=-\text{Re}\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)\overline{\widehat{f}}\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mu}\widehat{f}d\xi d\mu

\displaystyle\qquad+\text{Re}\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)\overline{\widehat{f}}\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\xi d\mu=:I+II

We estimate the term
I
by using integration by parts:

\displaystyle\left|I\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2}\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)\xi\cdot\left(\overline{\widehat{f}}\nabla_{\mu}\widehat{f}+\widehat{f}\nabla_{\mu}\overline{\widehat{f}}\right)d\xi d\mu\right|=\left|\frac{1}{2}\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mu}|\widehat{f}|^{2}d\xi d\mu\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|\iint|\widehat{f}|^{2}\xi\cdot\nabla_{\mu}\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)d\xi d\mu\right|\leq C\iint\left(|\mu|^{2s}+|\xi|^{2s}\right)|\widehat{f}|^{2}d\xi d\mu\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{x,v}}^{2}.

To estimate the term
II
, we need to use a symmetry to exploit some cancellations. First, note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\iint|\mu|^{2s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\mu d\xi=0

since

\displaystyle\iint|\mu|^{2s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\mu d\xi

\displaystyle=\iint|\mu|^{2s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)}\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\eta-\xi)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)d\xi d\mu d\eta

\displaystyle=-\overline{\iint|\mu|^{2s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}\int\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\mu d\xi}

by interchanging
\xi
and
\eta
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\iiint|\xi|^{s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu|\eta|^{s}\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\mu d\xi=0

in a similar way. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle II=\operatorname{Re}\iiint|\xi|^{s}\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}(|\xi|^{s}-|\eta|^{s})\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)\cdot i\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)d\eta d\mu d\xi.

Using
||\xi|^{s}-|\eta|^{s}|\leq C|\xi-\eta|(|\xi-\eta|^{s-1}+|\eta|^{s-1})
,
we write
|II|\leq C(II_{1}+II_{2})
,
where

\displaystyle II_{1}:=\iiint|\xi|^{s}|\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}|\xi-\eta|^{s}|\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)||\mu||\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)|d\eta d\mu d\xi,

\displaystyle II_{2}:=\iiint|\xi|^{s}|\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\xi)}|\xi-\eta||\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)||\eta|^{s-1}|\mu||\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)|d\eta d\mu d\xi.

We first estimate
II_{2}
: we have

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\mu,\xi}}\left\|\int|\xi-\eta||\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)||\eta|^{s-1}|\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)|d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu,\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{\mu,\xi}}\||\xi|\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi)\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}}\||\xi|^{s-1}|\mu||\widehat{f}(t)|\|_{L^{2}_{\mu,\xi}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}^{2}U\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon}_{x}}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}

for any
\epsilon>0
, where we have used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Young’s convolution inequality, and the Sobolev embedding in the first, second, and third step, respectively.
For the estimate for
II_{1}
, we proceed as follows: for some
\delta>0
sufficiently small, we define

\begin{split}\frac{1}{q}:=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s-1}{n}-\delta\quad\mbox{
and}\quad\frac{1}{p}:=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\end{split}

so that Young’s convolution inequality in
\xi
gives

\displaystyle\left\|\int|\xi-\eta|^{s}|\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi-\eta)||\mu\widehat{f}(t,\mu,\eta)|d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\xi|^{s}|\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi)|\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}}\||\mu||\widehat{f}(\mu,\xi)|\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{\frac{n}{2}+1+\epsilon}{2}}|\widehat{\nabla U}(\xi)|\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s-1}{2}}|\mu||\widehat{f}(\mu,\xi)|\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+1+\epsilon}_{x}}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}

for some
\epsilon>0
. Thus, we finally get the following estimate: for any
\epsilon>0
, for some
C
depending on
\epsilon
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq C(1+\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+1+\epsilon}_{x}})\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}

We have to estimate
\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+1+\epsilon}_{x}}
in order to close the a priori estimate. We use the following version of the
velocity averaging
lemma:

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
,
13
]
,
[
12
, Theorem 7.2.1]
)
.

Let
h,g\in L^{2}_{t,x,v}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
1
1

1

Strictly speaking,
g
is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued.
satisfy

\partial_{t}h+v\cdot\nabla_{x}h=\nabla_{v}\cdot g

on
[0,T]
in sense of distributions. Assume further that there exists some
Q>0
such that

h(t,x,v)=0

for
|v|>Q
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,
\rho_{h}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(t,x,v)dv
satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{h}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C(1+Q)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,v}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right).

We shall apply the lemma with
h=\Lambda^{s}_{x}f
, where
\Lambda^{s}_{x}
is the Fourier multiplier operator corresponding to the function
|\xi|^{s}
. Applying
\Lambda^{s}_{x}
to both sides of the equation for
f
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Lambda^{s}_{x}f)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Lambda^{s}_{x}f)=\nabla_{v}\cdot\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}U\,f).

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}_{x,v})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})},\quad\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}Uf)\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}_{x,v})}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{x}Uf\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}_{v}H^{s}_{x})}.

At this stage, we recall the following Kato–Ponce estimate (
[
22
,
21
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For
s>0
and
1<r<\infty
,
1<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}(hg)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\Lambda^{s}h\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Using this lemma, we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}U\,f(t,\cdot,v))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}f(t,\cdot,v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f(t,\cdot,v)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda^{s+1}_{x}U\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}),

where
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
and
p,q
will be chosen as follows:
first, choose
p
such that the following embedding holds:

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

This is satisfied whenever
-\frac{n}{p}\leq s-\frac{n}{2}
, that is,
\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{n}
. Furthermore, we want the following embedding to hold:

H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow W^{s,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

which is satisfied if

s-\frac{n}{q}\leq s+1-\frac{n}{2}
. Using the relationship between
p
and
q
, this is equivalent to
p\geq n
. Choosing
p:=2s+2
, all the conditions are satisfied since

2s+2>n+\frac{3}{2}>n\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{n}<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{2s+\frac{1}{2}}<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{2s+2}=\frac{1}{2s+2}=\frac{1}{p}.

Using the embedding relations, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}U\,f(t,\cdot,v))\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\|\Lambda^{s+1}_{x}U\|_{L^{q}_{x}})\leq C\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{H^{s+1}_{x}}\|f(t,\cdot,v)\|_{H^{s}_{x}}.

By elliptic regularity, we have
\|\nabla_{x}^{2}U\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}}.

Moreover, since
\rho\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}\subset L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}
, we have

\nabla_{x}U\in\dot{W}^{1,\frac{2n}{n+2}}\hookrightarrow L^{2}.

Thus, we have the bound
\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{1}_{x}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}})\leq C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}})

and hence

\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{H^{s+1}_{x}}\leq C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}}).

Now, we show the estimate for
\rho
as follows.

\displaystyle\|\rho\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{|v|\leq Q}f(t,\cdot,v)dv\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq CQ^{n}\int_{|v|\leq Q}\|f(t,\cdot,v)\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x}}dv=CQ^{n}\int_{|v|\leq Q}\int|\mathcal{F}_{x}f(t,\xi,v)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}d\xi dv

\displaystyle\leq CQ^{n}\iint|\mathcal{F}_{x,v}f(t,\xi,\mu)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}d\xi d\mu\leq CQ^{n}\iint|\mathcal{F}_{x,v}f(t,\xi,\mu)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\mu|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}d\xi d\mu

\displaystyle\leq CQ^{n}\|f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}^{2}

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}U\,f(t,\cdot,v))\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\left(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+Q^{\frac{n}{2}}\|f(t,\cdot,v)\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\right)\|f(t\cdot,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}},

and by integrating with respect to
v
and taking the supremum with respect to
t
gives

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}_{x}(\nabla_{x}U\,f)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}_{x,v})}\leq CQ^{\frac{n}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})}\right)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})}.

We now use Lemma
2.2
to conclude that (by choosing
\epsilon=s+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{2}
)

\|\nabla_{x}^{2}U\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}_{x})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}_{x})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q(t))^{n}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})}(1+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})}).

Together with the fact that
\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}})\leq C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}_{x}})
gives that

\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{s+\frac{5}{4}}_{x})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q(t))^{n}(1+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})})^{2}.

Now, recall the equation (
2.2
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq C(1+\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+1+\epsilon}_{x}})\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}.

Using the Gronwall inequality, one obtains with
R(T):=1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q(t)
that

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\exp\left(CT+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon}}ds\right)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\exp\left(CTR^{n}(T)(1+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})})^{2}\right).

Defining
F(T):=1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
, we have the following inequality:

F(T)\leq(1+\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}})\exp\left(CTR^{n}(T)F^{2}(T)\right).

On the other hand, the estimate for
R(t)
simply follows from the fact that
f
is conserved along the flow:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}R(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\right|\leq C\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{x}U(t)\|_{H^{s+1}_{x}}\leq CQ^{\frac{n}{2}}(t)\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq CR^{\frac{n}{2}}(t)F(t).

We will use a bootstrapping argument by assuming the continuity of
F(T)
and
R(T)
in
T
: assume that
F(t)\leq 4F(0)
and
R(t)\leq 4R(0)
for
0\leq t\leq T
. Then, this implies that for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle F(t)\leq F(0)\exp\left(CT(4R(0))^{n}(4F(0))^{2}\right)\leq 2F(0)\quad\mbox{and}\quad R(t)\leq R(0)+CT(4F(0))(4R(0))^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq 2R(0),

if we pick
T>0
sufficiently small depending only on
F(0)
and
R(0)
. Thus, the standard bootstrapping argument works so that there exists
T>0
depending only on the initial data, such that
\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
and
R(t)\leq 2R(0)
for
0\leq t\leq T
. This finishes the proof of a priori estimate in Proposition
2.1
. ∎

For later purpose, we also derive a Lipschitz-in-time estimate of

. We have

(2.4)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}f\|_{H^{-1}_{x,v}}&\leq\|v\cdot\nabla_{x}f\|_{H^{-1}_{x,v}}+\|\nabla_{x}U\cdot\nabla_{v}f\|_{H^{-1}_{x,v}}\leq\|v\cdot\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}H^{-1}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}U\cdot\nabla_{v}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{v}}\\
&\leq Q\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}H^{-1}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{v}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-1}_{v}}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}(Q+C(\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}))\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}(Q+C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+CQ^{\frac{n}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}).\end{split}

3.
Construction of Solution

In this section, we justify the above a priori estimate rigorously. To do this, we recall that (
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
) the Vlasov–Poisson equation has a unique local in time smooth solution for smooth and compactly supported in
v
initial data. Furthermore, the solution can be continued to be smooth if the solution is compactly supported in
v
, uniformly in
t
. This means that we can obtain a unique solution for regularized initial data, and we shall obtain the desired solution by an approximation argument.

Therefore, pick an initial data
f_{0}\in H^{s}_{x,v}
compactly supported in
v
for
s\in(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4},\frac{n}{2}+1]
and let
\{f_{k0}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a smooth function with compact support such that
f_{k0}\to f_{0}
in
H^{s}_{x,v}
and in
L^{1}
as
k\to\infty
. Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson equation with initial data
f_{k0}
. Since
f_{k0}
are approximations of
f_{0}
, we can assume that
\|f_{k0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
,
\|f_{k0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
, and
Q_{k}(0)\leq 2Q(0)
, where
Q_{k}(0)
are such that
f_{k0}(x,v,0)=0
for
|v|\geq Q_{k}(0)
and analogous for
Q(0)
. Thus, by the a priori estimates given above, there exists a time
T>0
, depending only on the initial data (actually,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
, and
Q(0)
) such that
f_{k}(t)
is defined for
t\in[0,T]
,
\|f_{k}(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{k0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq 4\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
, and
Q_{k}(t)\leq 2Q_{k}(0)\leq 4Q(0)
for such
t
. Thus, we have that
f_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x,v}
. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, we can extract a subsequence of
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, which we again denote by
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, such that it converges to
f
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];\,H^{s}_{x,v})
in the weak-* topology.

Next, by the a priori Lipschitz estimate for
f
in
H^{-1}_{x,v}
norm given in (
2.4
), we see that
\|\partial_{t}f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\,H^{-1}_{x,v})}
is uniformly bounded, whose bound only depends on the initial conditions. Hence, passing through a subsequence, we see that
\partial_{t}f_{k}
converges to
\partial_{t}f
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];\,H^{-1}_{x,v})
in its weak-* topology.

Moreover, we have from the a priori estimate that

\begin{split}\|\rho_{k}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}_{x})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q(t))^{n}(1+\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}_{x,v})})^{2},\end{split}

where
\rho_{k}(t,x)=\int f_{k}(t,x,v)dv
. Therefore, for
U_{k}
satisfying
\Delta_{x}U_{k}(t,x)=\rho_{k}(t,x)
, we have that

\|\nabla_{x}U_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s+\frac{5}{4}}_{x}}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+Q(0))^{n}\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}(1+\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}})

and therefore,
\nabla_{x}U_{k}
is bounded in
L^{2}([0,T];\,H^{s+\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Also, integrating both sides of the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{k}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k}-\nabla_{x}U_{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{k}=0

in
v
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{k}(t,x)+\int_{|v|\leq Q_{k}(t)}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k}(t,x,v)\,dv=0

and thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}U_{k}(t,x)+\int_{|v|\leq Q_{k}(t)}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}f_{k}(t,x,v)\,dv=0.

Since
\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}
is a Calderon-Zygmund operator, it has
L^{2}
boundedness. Thus, by squaring both sides of the equation and integrating in
t
and
x
gives

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}U_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q^{2n+2}_{k}(t)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|v|\leq Q_{k}(t)}\int|\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\nabla_{x}f_{k}(t,x,v)|^{2}dxdvdt

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}Q^{2n+2}_{k}(t)\int_{0}^{T}\iint|f_{k}(t,x,v)|^{2}dxdvdt\leq CTQ^{2n+2}(0)\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

and hence
\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}U_{k}
is bounded in
L^{2}([0,T];\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Now, we are in a position to apply the Aubin–Lions Lemma. However, since the space is not compact, it needs a little care. First, we pick a sequence of balls
B_{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin with radius
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, we have the inclusion:

H^{s+\frac{5}{4}}(B_{M})\hookrightarrow H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}(B_{M})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{M})

where the inclusions are compact. Thus, the Aubin–Lions Lemma implies that for every subsequence of
\{\nabla_{x}U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, there exists a further subsequence such that it converges strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]:H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}(B_{M}))
and hence in
L^{2}([0,T]:C(B_{M}))
by Sobolev embedding. Thus, a diagonal argument shows that there exists a subsequence of
\{\nabla_{x}U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
so that it converges strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]:C(B_{M}))
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
. By the weak-* convergence of
f_{k}
to
f
, we also see that the limit is
\nabla_{x}U
, where
\Delta_{x}U=\rho
.

We are ready to prove the existence of a distributional solution. Pick a smooth function
\varphi(t,x,v)
supported in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\iiint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\partial_{t}f_{k}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k}-\nabla_{x}U_{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{k}\right]\varphi(t,x,v)dxdvdt=0.

Integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\iiint[-\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,v)+\nabla_{x}U_{k}(t,x,v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi(t,x,v)]f_{k}(t,x,v)dxdvdt=0.

Now, by weak-* convergence of
f_{k}
and strong convergence of
\nabla_{x}U_{k}
, we can take the limit
k\to\infty
to obtain

\displaystyle\iiint[-\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,v)+\nabla_{x}U(t,x,v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi(t,x,v)]f(t,x,v)dxdvdt=0,

and thus
f
is the desired distributional solution.
Finally, since we have

\displaystyle\iiint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x,v,t)f_{k}(x,v,t)dxdvdt=0

for all
\psi
with compact support in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose support is disjoint from
\{(x,v,t):\,|v|\leq 4Q(0)\}
, by weak convergence of
f_{k}
to
f
, we have

\displaystyle\iiint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x,v,t)f(x,v,t)dxdvdt=0

by setting
\psi(x,v,t)
to be
\eta(t)\varphi(x,v)
and by choosing
\eta
as an approximation of the Dirac delta, we see that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x,v)f(x,v,t)dxdv=0

for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, where the exceptional points may depend on
\varphi
. However, since we know that
L^{2}
is separable, we see that this dependency can be removed by taking the countable union of exceptional points, which also has measure zero. Thus, we see that
f
is compactly supported in
v
.

Finally, we show that the solution we have just constructed actually belongs to
C([0,T];\,H^{s}_{x,v})
. To show this, we need to show the weak continuity in time and the continuity of the norm. The weak continuity is simply obtained from the Lipschitz estimates. Pick a test function
\psi
. We have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|\left<f(u^{\prime})-f(u),\psi\right>\right|=\left|\left<\int_{u}^{u^{\prime}}\partial_{t}f(t)dt,\psi\right>\right|=\int_{u}^{u^{\prime}}\left|\left<\partial_{t}f(t),\psi\right>\right|dt\leq|u^{\prime}-u|\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\,H^{-1}_{x,v})}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}_{x,v}}

where the bracket denotes the dual pairing. Thus, the weak continuity is proven. To prove the continuity of the norm, we prove the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq\liminf_{t^{\prime}\to t}\|f(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq\limsup_{t^{\prime}\to t}\|f(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}

Inequality on the left is just the consequence of weak continuity in time. To prove inequality on the right, we recall the following a priori estimate:

\displaystyle F(T)\leq F(0)\exp\left(CtR^{n}(T)F^{2}(T)\right)

where
F(t)
and
Q
are given as before.
Actually, with a slight care, one can prove the following:

\displaystyle 1+\sup_{t^{\prime}\in[t-\delta,t+\delta]}\|f(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\leq(1+\|f(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}})\exp(\sqrt{\delta T}C(1+Q(0))^{n}(1+\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}})^{2})

4.
Uniqueness of Solution

In this section, we prove the uniqueness of solutions. To do this, we first establish the fact that the solution
f
is transported along the characteristic flow (the flow generated by the vector field
(v,-\nabla_{x}U(x,t))
). We first establish the existence and uniqueness of the flow, given the solution
f
. We first state the following lemma. The proof is given in the appendix.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
be a non-autonomous vector field defined on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is continuous in space and satisfies
\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{1}
, where
\alpha,C_{1}>0
. Suppose that there exists a sequence of smooth non-autonomous vector fields
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{1}
for any
k
.

(2)

For any
M>0
,
\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{M}))}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Then, there exists a unique flow map
\Phi
generated by
u
. Moreover, it is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism.

We apply this Lemma to the vector field
(v,-\nabla_{x}U)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We check the conditions for the Lemma. First,
(v,-\nabla_{x}U)
is continuous in
x
since by the a priori estimates,
\|\nabla_{x}U\|_{H^{s+1}_{x}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\leq CQ(0)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x,v}}
and
s+1>\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{4}
, thus
\nabla_{x}U
is in
C^{\frac{3}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by Sobolev embedding. The rest of the conditions are already verified in the a priori estimates, just note that
s>\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
.

We now show that
f
is transported along the characteristic flow. Indeed, let
\Phi_{t}
be the characteristic flow. Fix a fixed test function
\varphi(t,x,v)
. We first have

\displaystyle\iiint f(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)dtdvdx=\iiint f(t,x,v)(\partial_{t}\varphi)(t,\Phi_{t}^{-1}(x,v))dtdvdx

where we have used the fact that
\Phi_{t}
is a measure-preserving diffeomorphism. Let
\psi(t,x,v):=\varphi(t,\Phi_{t}^{-1}(x,v))
. Then,
\varphi(t,x,v)=\psi(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))
and thus by using the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)=(\partial\psi)(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))-\nabla_{x}U(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))\cdot\nabla_{v}\psi(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v)),

and thus

(\partial_{t}\varphi)(t,\Phi_{t}^{-1}(x,v))=(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{x}U(x,t)\cdot\nabla_{v})\psi(t,x,v)

Combining the results give

\displaystyle\iiint f(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)dtdvdx

\displaystyle=-\iiint f(t,x,v)(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{x}U(x,t)\cdot\nabla_{v})\psi(t,x,v)dtdvdx=0

since
\psi
is a suitable test function (
\Phi
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism) and
f
is the distributional solution of the Vlasov–Poisson equation

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}U\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0

This implies that
f(t,\Phi_{t}(x,v))=f_{0}(x,v)
for almost every
t
, and therefore
f
is transported along the characteristic flow.

We now prove uniqueness. It follows the proof of Theorem 2.1 in the paper
[
25
]
, with minor modifications. Suppose that there are two solutions,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, with initial data
f_{0}
. For simplicity, we assume from now on that
f_{0}\geq 0
. It is clarified in
[
25
, Section 4]
that the non-negativity assumption is not necessary at all. Let

\Phi_{i}(t,x,v)=(X_{i}(t,x,v),\,\Xi_{i}(t,x,v))

be the corresponding characteristic flow for
i=1,2
. We aim to estimate the following quantity:

\displaystyle P(t):=\frac{1}{2}\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\Phi_{1}(t,x,v)-\Phi_{2}(t,x,v)|^{2}dvdx.

Differentiating in time, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P(t)

\displaystyle=\iint f_{0}(x,v)(\Phi_{1}(t,x,v)-\Phi_{2}(t,x,v))\cdot(\partial_{t}\Phi_{1}(t,x,v)-\partial_{t}\Phi_{2}(t,x,v))

\displaystyle=\iint f_{0}(x,v)(X_{1}(t,x,v)-X_{2}(t,x,v))\cdot(\Xi_{1}(t,x,v)-\Xi_{2}(t,x,v))dvdx

\displaystyle\quad-\iint f_{0}(x,v)(\Xi_{1}(t,x,v)-\Xi_{2}(t,x,v))\cdot(\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{2}(t,x,v)))dvdx

\displaystyle=:A_{1}+A_{2}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
|A_{1}|\leq P(t)
. On the other hand,
A_{2}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\left[\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\Xi_{1}(t,x,v)-\Xi_{2}(t,x,v)|^{2}dvdx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{2}(t,x,v))|^{2}dvdx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(2P(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{2}(t,x,v))|^{2}dvdx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(2P(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\nabla_{x}U_{1}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))|^{2}dvdx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(2P(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\iint f_{0}(x,v)|\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{1}(t,x,v))-\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t,X_{2}(t,x,v))|^{2}dvdx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=:(2P(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\left([T_{1}(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}+[T_{2}(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

From
[
25
, Theorem 2.9]
, we can estimate
T_{1}(t)
as

\displaystyle T_{1}(t)\leq 2\max\{\|\rho_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\rho_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\}^{2}P(t)\leq 4(\|\rho_{1}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}}+\|\rho_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}})P(t).

The estimate of
T_{2}(t)
needs to be done differently from the case of
[
25
]
: we estimate

\displaystyle T_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\iint f_{0}(x,v)\|\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}|X_{1}(t,x,v)-X_{2}(t,x,v)|^{2}dvdx

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}U_{2}(t)\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\iint f_{0}(x,v)|X_{1}(t,x,v)-X_{2}(t,x,v)|^{2}dvdx\leq C\|\rho_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}P(t).

Combining these estimates, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P(t)\leq CP(t)\left(1+\|\rho_{1}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}}+\|\rho_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The Gronwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle P(t)

\displaystyle\leq P(0)\exp\left(CT+C\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\rho_{1}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}}+\|\rho_{2}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{4}}}\right)dt^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P(0)\exp\left(CT+C\sqrt{T}\left(\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,H^{s+\frac{1}{4}})}+\|\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,H^{s+\frac{1}{4}})}\right)\right).

Since
P(0)=0
, we conclude that
P(t)=0
. This means that
\Phi_{1}(x,v,t)=\Phi_{2}(x,v,t)
for all points
(x,v)
where
f_{0}(x,v)\neq 0
. Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are transported along the flow, as we have seen earlier,
f_{1}=f_{2}
, and the proof is complete. ∎

Appendix A
Proof of the Averaging Lemma

In this section, we prove Lemma
2.2
. To do this, we first extend
f
and
g
to be 0 outside
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Taking the Fourier transform with respect to
(t,v)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int f(\cdot,\cdot,v)dv\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}H^{\frac{1}{4}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint(1+|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}})\left|\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)dv\right|^{2}d\xi d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CQ^{n}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x,v}}+C\iint_{|\xi|\geq 1}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)dv\right|^{2}d\xi d\tau.

Thus, all we have to do is to estimate
\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)dv
for
|\xi|\geq 1
.
To do this, we split the domain of integration into two parts by using the cutoff function
\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)
. Here,
\zeta
is a smooth function such that
\zeta(z)=1
for
|z|\leq 1
,
\zeta(z)=0
for
|z|\geq 2
, and
|\zeta^{{}^{\prime}}(z)|\leq 2
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\displaystyle\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)dv

\displaystyle=\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)dv+\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)dv=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we use the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq C\|\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\left(\int_{|v|\leq Q}\zeta^{2}\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq CQ^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{4}}}.

This is obtained as follows: we first decompose
v=v_{\parallel}+v_{\perp}
, where
v_{\parallel}
denotes the component of
v
parallel to
\xi
and
v_{\perp}
the component orthogonal to
\xi
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|v|\leq Q}\zeta^{2}\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\tau+\xi\cdot v_{\parallel}|\leq 2|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{|v_{\perp}|\leq(Q^{2}-v_{\parallel}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}dv_{\perp}dv_{\parallel}\leq C\int_{|\tau+\xi\cdot v_{\parallel}|\leq 2|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}(Q^{2}-v_{\parallel}^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}dv_{\parallel}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\tau+\xi\cdot v_{\parallel}|\leq 2|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}Q^{n-1}dv_{\parallel}=CQ^{n-1}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we use the following equation, which is obtained by taking the Fourier transform to the equation
\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\nabla_{v}\cdot g
:

\displaystyle i(\tau+\xi\cdot v)\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)=\nabla_{v}\cdot\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi,v)

Then, since the function
\frac{1}{\tau+\xi\cdot v}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)
is smooth due to the assumption that
|\xi|\geq 1
, we can multiply it to both sides of the equation to get:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)=\frac{-i}{\tau+\xi\cdot v}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\nabla_{v}\cdot\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi,v).

We integrate in
v
and then integrate by parts in the right-hand side, which is permitted by the definition of distributional derivatives. This gives

\displaystyle\int\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi,v)\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)dv=\int\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi,v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{i}{\tau+\xi\cdot v}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\right)dv.

The left-hand side is just
I_{2}
, so we have by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\|\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi.,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\left\|\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{i}{\tau+\xi\cdot v}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\{|v|\leq Q\})}.

Thus, we need to estimate the following:

\displaystyle\int_{|v|\leq Q}\left|\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{1}{\tau+\xi\cdot v}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+\xi\cdot v}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\right|^{2}dv.

Again, we decompose
v=v_{\parallel}+v_{\perp}
as before and note that the integrand is independent of
v_{\perp}
. Thus, we write the above expression as

\displaystyle\int_{|\tau+\xi\cdot v_{\parallel}|\leq 2|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{|v_{\perp}|\leq(Q^{2}-v_{\parallel}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\frac{d}{dv_{\parallel}}\left(\frac{1}{\tau+|\xi|v_{\parallel}}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+|\xi|v_{\parallel}}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\right|^{2}dv_{\perp}dv_{\parallel}

\displaystyle\leq CQ^{n-1}\int_{|\tau+\xi\cdot v_{\parallel}|\leq 2|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\frac{d}{dv_{\parallel}}\left(\frac{1}{\tau+|\xi|v_{\parallel}}\left(1-\zeta\left(\frac{\tau+|\xi|v_{\parallel}}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\right|^{2}dv_{\parallel}.

By a reparameterization, we see that the integrand is a bounded quantity, and the above expression is bounded by
CQ^{n-1}|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq CQ^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|\widehat{g}(\tau,\xi.,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{v}}|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Combining all together gives

\displaystyle\left\|\int f(\cdot,\cdot,v)dv\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{\frac{1}{4}}_{x}}\leq C(1+Q)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,v}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})},\right),

and this was what we wanted to show. This ends the proof. ∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
4.1

In this section, we prove Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

We first obtain the existence of
\Phi
as a limit of the flow
\Phi_{k}
generated by the flow
u_{k}
. Pick
M>0
. Then, since
\Phi_{k}(t,x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}u_{k}(s,\Phi_{k}(s,x))ds
, we have the following:

\displaystyle|\Phi_{k}(t,x)|\leq|x|+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{i}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds\leq M+\sqrt{T}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq M+\sqrt{T}C_{1}=:R

for all
k
. Now, obesrve the following:

\displaystyle|\Phi_{i}(t,x)-\Phi_{j}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u_{i}(s,\Phi_{i}(s,x))-u_{j}(s,\Phi_{j}(s,x))|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u_{i}(s,\Phi_{i}(s,x))-u_{i}(s,\Phi_{j}(s,x))|ds+\int_{0}^{t}|u_{i}(s,\Phi_{j}(s,x))-u_{j}(s,\Phi_{j}(s,x))|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla_{x}u_{i})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Phi_{i}(s,x)-\Phi_{j}(s,x)|ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{i}(s)-u_{j}(s)\|_{C(B_{R}(0))}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{T}\|u_{i}-u_{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))}+\|u_{i}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi_{i}(s,x)-\Phi_{j}(s,x)|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{T}\|u_{i}-u_{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))}+C_{1}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi_{i}(s,x)-\Phi_{j}(s,x)|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Squaring both sides gives

\displaystyle|\Phi_{i}(t,x)-\Phi_{j}(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2T\|u_{i}-u_{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))}^{2}+2C_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi_{i}(s,x)-\Phi_{j}(s,x)|^{2}ds,

and the Gronwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|\Phi_{i}(t,x)-\Phi_{j}(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2T\|u_{i}-u_{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))}^{2}e^{2C_{1}^{2}T}.

Since
\{u_{i}\}
is a Cauchy Sequence in
L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))
by assumption, we see that
\Phi_{i}(t,x)
converges uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in B_{M}(0)
, and we call this value
\Phi(t,x)
. Since
M>0
was arbitrary, we see that
\Phi_{k}
converges uniformly on compact sets, which implies that
\Phi
is also continuous. Now, we try to send
k\to\infty
for both sides
\Phi_{k}(t,x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}u_{k}(s,\Phi_{k}(s,x))ds
. The left-hand side clearly approaches
\Phi(t,x)
. We estimate the right-hand side as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}(u_{k}(s,\Phi_{k}(s,x))-u(s,\Phi(s,x)))ds\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|u_{k}(s,\Phi_{k}(s,x))-u(s,\Phi(s,x))\right|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u_{k}(s,\Phi_{k}(s,x))-u_{k}(s,\Phi(s,x))|ds+\int_{0}^{t}|u_{k}(s,\Phi(s,x))-u(s,\Phi(s,x))|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\Phi_{k}-\Phi\|_{C([0,T]\times B_{R}(0))}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\nabla_{x}u_{k})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds+\sqrt{T}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C(B_{R}(0)))}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
.
Therefore, we have that the right-hand side approaches
x+\int_{0}^{t}u(s,\Phi(s,x))ds
. Thus, we have proved that the
\Phi
is a flow corresponding to
u
.

Let us now prove the uniqueness: suppose that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are two such flows. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\Phi(t,x)-\Psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u(s,\Phi(s,x))-u(s,\Psi(s,x))|ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla_{x}u)(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Phi(s,x)-\Psi(s,x)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi(s,x)-\Psi(s,x)|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the last step. Squaring both sides and applying the Gronwall’s inequality give
\Phi(t,x)=\Psi(t,x)
, since we have
\Phi(0,x)=\Psi(0,x)=x
. This proves the uniqueness.

Finally, we show that the flow is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism. To do this, we first need a Lipschitz estimate for the flow:

\displaystyle|\Phi(x+h,t)-\Phi(x,t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u(s,\Phi(s,x+h))-u(s,\Phi(s,x))|ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq CC_{1}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

and therefore squaring both sides and using the Gronwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|\Phi(x+h,t)-\Phi(x,t)|\leq|h|\exp\left(\frac{(CC_{1})^{2}}{2}\right).

Next, we show that
\Phi
is differentiable in
x
. Before doing this, we first formally differentiate the equation
\Phi(t,x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}u(s,\Phi(s,x))ds
with respect to
x
to get

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)=I_{d}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)ds

This suggests that the candidate for
\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)
is the unique solution
\Psi
of the equation

\displaystyle\Psi(t,x)=I_{d}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\Psi(s,x)ds.

Let us justify this: write

\displaystyle|\Phi(t,x+h)-\Phi(t,x)-\Psi(t,x)h|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|u(s,\Phi(s,x+h))-\Phi(s,x)-\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\Psi(s,x)h\right|ds

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{t}\left|\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla u(s,(1-\lambda)\Phi(x,s+h))+\lambda\Phi(s,x)\right)d\lambda\right](\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x))-\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\Psi(s,x)h\right|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla u(s,(1-\lambda)\Phi(s,x+h)+\lambda\Phi(s,x))-\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\right||\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)|d\lambda ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\cdot\left(\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)-\Psi(s,x)h\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)-\Psi(s,x)h|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left[\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)\right]^{1+\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{1}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)-\Psi(s,x)h|^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+CC_{1}\sqrt{T}h^{1+\alpha}.

Therefore, squaring both sides and using the Gronwall’s inequality give

|\Phi(t,x+h)-\Phi(t,x)-\Psi(t,x)h|\leq Ch^{1+\alpha}=o(h).

Thus,
\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)
exists and is equal to
\Psi(t,x)
.

Finally, we prove the continuity of
\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)
. We only have to prove continuity in
x
. Before doing this, we first show that
\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)
is uniformly bounded in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|ds\leq C+\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C+C_{1}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Squaring both sides and using the Gronwall’s inequality gives the desired bound. For continuity in
x
, we write

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x+h)-\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x+h))\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x+h)-\nabla u(s,\Phi(s,x))\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)\right|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x+h)-\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}u(s)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Phi(s,x+h)-\Phi(s,x)|^{\alpha}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x+h)-\nabla_{x}\Phi(s,x)|ds^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\,C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}C\sqrt{T}h^{\alpha}.

Squaring both sides and using Gronwall’s inequality gives

|\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x+h)-\nabla_{x}\Phi(t,x)|\leq Ch^{\alpha}.

Thus, the flow is, in fact, Hölder continuous in
x
. This finishes the proof.
∎

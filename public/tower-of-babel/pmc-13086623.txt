Dynamics of complete responses (CRs) in patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma (R/R FL) treated with odronextamab in the ELM‐2 study
Authors: Stefano Luminari, Ana Jiménez‐Ubieto, Geoffrey Chong, David Tucker, Srikanth Ambati, Chinjune Lin, Hesham Mohamed, Jurriaan Brouwer‐Visser, Chetachi Akunna Otele, Giovanni Antico, Jose C Villasboas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractComplete response (CR) is an important treatment goal in follicular lymphoma (FL). In relapsed or refractory (R/R) FL, CR rates decline, and disease progression accelerates with each treatment line, with particularly poor outcomes in patients with high‐risk features. Odronextamab, a CD20 × CD3 bispecific antibody, demonstrated deep, durable responses and generally manageable safety in R/R FL in the Phase 2 ELM‐2 study (NCT03888105); we present an exploratory analysis in patients who attained a CR. Patients received intravenous odronextamab with step‐up dosing in Cycle 1, 80 mg weekly in Cycles 2–4, then 160 mg once every 2 weeks until disease progression or other protocol‐defined reason for discontinuation. Patients with CR lasting ≥9 months switched to dosing once every 4 weeks. Overall, 73.4% (94/128) of patients attained CR, 93.6% (88/94) by first response assessment (~Week 12). Median time to CR was 2.7 months (range 2.3–7.9). Median duration of CR was 25.1 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 20.5–not evaluable [NE]) overall and 23.7 months (18.2–NE) in patients with high‐risk features. Overall, 79.5% (35/44) of patients who switched to Q4W dosing maintained CR at last assessment. Patients with low/undetectable CD20 expression by immunohistochemistry (<10% CD20+ cells) or RNA sequencing (messenger RNA < 500 transcripts per million) could achieve CR. Median progression‐free survival was 27.8 months (95% CI 23.0–NE) in patients with CR and not reached in those with undetectable circulating tumor DNA at Week 12. Treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAEs) led to treatment discontinuation in 16.0% of patients with CR; the most common TEAE was cytokine release syndrome (56.4%). This generally manageable safety profile and the sustainability of CRs support odronextamab as a potential long‐term treatment for heavily pretreated R/R FL.

INTRODUCTION

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is the second most common form of non‐Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) and the most common subtype of indolent NHL. 1 FL is typically incurable, and patients who relapse or are refractory to frontline therapy experience progressively worse outcomes with each successive line of therapy. 2 , 3 In the international, retrospective SCHOLAR‐5 study of adult patients with Grade 1–3a FL, complete response (CR) rates decreased from 43.9% with third‐line treatment to 21.5% with the fifth or later line of treatment. 3 Outcomes are especially poor in patients with high‐risk features such as a Follicular Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (FLIPI) score of 3–5, progression of disease within 24 months of first‐line treatment (POD24), and refractoriness to last line of therapy.4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Considering the potential for rapid disease progression among patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) FL in the third line and beyond (3L+), 3 there remains a need for new treatments that can achieve deep response in this setting. Indeed, achievement of CR is a key endpoint to gauge the effectiveness of lymphoma treatments based on its association with improved long‐term outcomes. 9 , 10

There is currently no consensus on the standard of care for 3L+ R/R FL, but several novel treatments are available, including T‐cell‐engaging therapies (e.g., bispecific antibodies [including mosunetuzumab and epcoritamab] or chimeric antigen receptor [CAR] T‐cell therapies [axicabtagene ciloleucel, tisagenlecleucel, and lisocabtagene maraleucel]) and small molecule inhibitors (e.g., EZH2 inhibitors or BTK inhibitors).11, 12, 13, 14 Among these treatment options, bispecific antibodies and CAR T‐cell therapies have achieved the highest rates of CR. Bispecific antibodies are an off‐the‐shelf treatment option that have demonstrated activity as a monotherapy and generally have lower rates of severe cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurotoxicity than CAR T‐cell therapies. 12 , 15 , 16

Odronextamab is a CD20 × CD3 bispecific antibody that elicits T‐cell‐mediated cytotoxicity by binding to CD20 on malignant B cells and CD3 on cytotoxic T cells. 17 Odronextamab demonstrated high rates of durable response, alongside a generally manageable safety profile, as 3L+ treatment for patients with R/R FL in the Phase 2 ELM‐2 study (NCT03888105). 18 Based on the results of this study, odronextamab was approved in the European Union as Ordspono™ for the treatment of adult patients with R/R FL after two or more lines of systemic therapy, 19 but its safety and efficacy are still being evaluated by other regulatory authorities. Here, we report an exploratory analysis of the efficacy and safety of odronextamab in patients with R/R FL who attained a CR in the ELM‐2 study, including patients with high‐risk features.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and patient population

ELM‐2 is an ongoing, open‐label, multicenter, multicohort Phase 2 study of odronextamab monotherapy for adult patients with R/R B‐cell NHL (B‐NHL). Details of the study design have been described previously 18 and are summarized below.

In the FL cohort, patients were aged ≥18 years and had central histopathologic confirmation of Grade 1–3a disease (according to the World Health Organization 2017 classification 20 ), which was refractory to or had relapsed after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy, including an anti‐CD20 antibody and an alkylating agent. Patients were excluded if they had received prior allogeneic stem cell transplantation or CD20 × CD3 bispecific antibody treatment.

Intravenous odronextamab was administered in 21‐day cycles with step‐up dosing and steroid prophylaxis during Cycle 1 to help mitigate the risk of CRS. Step‐up dosing was optimized during the study from an initial 1/20 mg regimen to a 0.7/4/20 mg regimen. Odronextamab dosing continued at 80 mg on Days 1, 8, and 15 of Cycles 2–4, followed by maintenance dosing of 160 mg once every 2 weeks (Q2W). A reduced dosing schedule of 160 mg once every 4 weeks (Q4W) was introduced for patients with a durable CR for at least 9 months. Treatment continued until disease progression or other protocol‐defined reason for treatment discontinuation; resumption of treatment was not permitted. Anti‐infection prophylaxis, including that for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, was implemented in accordance with local institutional guidelines.

The study protocol was approved by the relevant institutional review boards and ethics committees for each participating site. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent.

Study outcomes and assessments

The primary objective of this exploratory, longitudinal analysis was to assess CR dynamics in patients who achieved CR in ELM‐2. For the overall ELM‐2 study, the primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) based on best overall response (BOR) according to Lugano criteria, 21 assessed by independent central review (ICR). Secondary endpoints of ELM‐2 included CR rate, duration of response (DOR), progression‐free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety. Exploratory analyses included the assessment of CR dynamics, CD20 expression, minimal residual disease (MRD) status, and immunophenotyping.

Disease assessments using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were carried out during screening, at approximately Week 12, every 8 weeks in Year 1, then every 12 weeks in Year 2, and during follow‐up. Positron emission tomography (PET) was performed at select time points in Year 1 and at the end of Year 2, then annually at the time of CT/MRI assessment; any suspected CR was confirmed by PET. Disease response was assessed during post‐treatment study follow‐up until the time of disease progression, death, start of a non‐protocol anti‐lymphoma therapy, or patient withdrawal of consent. Treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were recorded for up to 90 days after the last dose of odronextamab or until initiation of another anti‐lymphoma therapy and were graded using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (version 5.0). CRS was graded according to the American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy criteria. 22

Fresh tumor biopsies collected at baseline were evaluated for CD20 expression in patients with evaluable samples using fully automated immunohistochemistry (IHC) or RNA sequencing (RNAseq) analysis. Archival biopsies were used if fresh biopsies were not available. IHC was performed centrally at Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, Inc., using a fully automated chromogenic assay with rabbit monoclonal primary antibody (clone SP32, Abcam) and a secondary antibody mix run on a VENTANA HE 600 stainer (Roche Diagnostics). 18 , 23 The percentage of cells that were CD20+ at any staining intensity was determined. As a cutoff for low expression, <10% of cells staining positive for CD20 with any intensity was chosen. RNAseq of tumor biopsies was performed on Illumina sequencers at Expression Analysis, Inc., using the TruSeq RNA Exome kit. Transcripts were quantified as transcripts per million (TPM). As a cutoff for low expression, <500 MS4A1 (i.e., CD20) TPM was chosen.

MRD status was analyzed by measuring circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) in plasma, with samples prepared using the AVENIO Oncology Assay Non‐Hodgkin Lymphoma Test (Roche Sequencing Solutions, research use only). 18 Samples were collected at baseline, at Week 12 (±7 days), and for patients with CR, at the time point of each CT/MRI scan. The reported synthetic sensitivity of the AVENIO assay is approximately 10−5 variant allele frequency; however, its clinical sensitivity is yet to be determined. 24

Statistical analyses

An exact binomial design was adopted for the primary endpoint; patients not evaluable for BOR were considered non‐responders. DOR, PFS, and OS were summarized by median and 95% confidence interval (CI) using Kaplan–Meier methodology. All efficacy and safety analyses were carried out in patients who received at least one dose of odronextamab and had the opportunity for response assessment at 12 weeks, comprising the overall global population. The analysis of efficacy and safety by CR included patients with a BOR of CR (assessed by ICR) at any time through data cutoff. Attainment of CR was evaluated at each disease assessment time point, as described above. Duration of follow‐up was based on the reverse Kaplan–Meier methodology of PFS by investigator assessment.

RESULTS

Patients and treatment exposure

At the October 20, 2023, data cutoff, 144 patients with R/R FL were enrolled into the ELM‐2 study, 128 of whom comprised the overall global population evaluable for efficacy and safety that was included in the primary analysis of this study (Figure S1). Of these patients, 60 (46.9%) received the optimized 0.7/4/20 mg Cycle 1 step‐up dosing regimen, and 68 (53.1%) received the 1/20 mg regimen. Baseline characteristics were generally similar between the overall population (N = 128) and patients with a BOR of CR by ICR (n = 94), and most patients had at least one high‐risk feature (FLIPI score 3–5: 57.8% and 55.3%; POD24: 49.2% and 48.9%; refractory to last line of therapy: 71.9% and 70.2%, respectively; Table 1). Patients were heavily pretreated (median of 3 prior lines of therapy [range 2–13] for both the overall population and patients with a CR) and had highly refractory disease (83.6% of the overall population and 81.9% of patients with CR were refractory to any line of therapy). Patients with a BOR of partial response (PR; n = 9) also had generally similar baseline characteristics to the overall population: median age was 58.0 years (range 38–78), 66.7% of patients had a FLIPI score of 3–5, and patients had received a median of 3 prior lines of therapy (range 2–5). This analysis focuses on the overall population (N = 128) and patients who had a CR (n = 94).

Efficacy

At data cutoff, the median duration of follow‐up for the overall population was 20.1 months (95% CI 17.3–27.8), the ORR was 80.5% (103/128 patients), and the CR rate was 73.4% (94/128 patients). Overall, 93.6% (88/94) of patients with a BOR of CR were in CR by the first response assessment around Week 12, while three patients (3.2%) had PR, and three patients had stable disease at their first response assessment but later converted to a CR. At the last available response assessment, 73.4% (69/94) of patients were in CR, and 26.6% (25/94) had progressive disease. Median time to CR was 2.7 months (range 2.3–7.9). The median DOR (22.6 months [95% CI 17.7–not evaluable (NE)]) and median duration of complete response (DOCR) (25.1 months [95% CI 20.5–NE]) were prolonged beyond the median duration of treatment exposure (13.8 months [range 1.8–45.0]) (Figure S2).

Regarding high‐risk features, CR rates were 70.3% (52/74) in patients with a FLIPI score of 3–5, 73.0% (46/63) in those with POD24, and 71.7% (66/92) in those who were refractory to their last line of therapy. In total, 90.4% (85/94) of patients with a CR had at least one high‐risk feature. Median time to CR was the same in high‐risk patients as in all patients with a CR (2.7 months [range 2.3–7.9]), and median DOCR was similar (23.7 months [95% CI 18.2–NE] and 25.1 months [95% CI 20.5–NE], respectively; Figure 1).

Median duration of complete response (DOCR) in patients with a complete response (CR), with or without high‐risk features, as assessed by independent central review. CI, confidence interval; NE, not evaluable.

Median PFS was longer in patients with CR (27.8 months [95% CI 23.0–NE]) than in the overall population (20.7 months [95% CI 17.2–27.5]), and median OS was not reached (NR) in either group at the time of this analysis (Figure 2). In patients with a PR, median PFS was 11.3 months (95% CI 4.4–19.5) and median OS was 18.4 months (95% CI 12.6–NE). In patients with stable disease or progressive disease, there was a trend towards poorer PFS compared with patients who had a response. With the exception of patients treated for <6 months (n = 20) who had a median PFS that was NR (95% CI 4.5–NE), longer treatment duration was associated with improved PFS in patients with a CR; median PFS was 12.8 months (95% CI 8.0–NE), 20.7 months (95% CI 17.7–NE), and NR (95% CI 25.2–NE) in patients treated for ≥6 to <12 months (n = 18), ≥12 to <18 months (n = 33), and ≥18 months (n = 23), respectively (Figure 3).

Patients who transitioned to Q4W dosing

In total, 44/94 patients (46.8%) who had an ICR‐confirmed CR for ≥9 months transitioned to Q4W dosing, with a median duration of treatment exposure of 17.7 months (range 13.1–45.0) from the time of treatment initiation. Among these patients, 35/44 (79.5%) maintained CR at their last available response assessment. The median DOR from the time of Q4W transition was an additional 22.8 months (95% CI 11.2–NE), based on 45 patients who switched from Q2W to Q4W dosing (44 patients who had a CR by ICR, plus one patient who had an ICR‐confirmed PR but a CR by investigator assessment).

At data cutoff, 22 patients who transitioned to Q4W dosing had discontinued study treatment. The primary reasons for discontinuation were adverse events (31.8% [n = 7, including one patient with COVID‐19 infection]), physician decision (27.3%), withdrawal by patient (13.6%), and death (13.6% [n = 3, including one patient with COVID‐19 infection]) (Table 2).

Biomarker assessment

Response was evaluated according to CD20 expression by IHC (n = 71) and messenger RNA (mRNA) analysis (n = 62) in patients with evaluable tissue samples. Patients with low or undetectable CD20 expression by IHC could still benefit from odronextamab treatment, with 10/15 (66.7%) patients with <10% CD20+ cells achieving a response (9 with CR, 1 with PR; Figure 4A). There were five patients with low or negative CD20 by mRNA expression, among whom ORR was 40% (2/5, both with CR).

Safety

The safety profile of odronextamab in patients who had a CR was generally consistent with patients in the overall population. TEAEs occurred in all patients and were Grade ≥ 3 in 85.1% of patients with a BOR of CR (Table 3). The most common TEAEs of any grade in patients with a CR were CRS (56.4%), pyrexia (44.7%), and neutropenia (41.5%) (Table 4). Table 3 also shows that, among patients who had a CR, TEAEs led to treatment interruption or delay in 84 patients (89.4%), to dose reduction in 12 patients (12.8%), and to treatment discontinuation in 15 patients (16.0%). TEAEs led to death in 10 patients with a CR (10.6%), which included COVID‐19 infection/COVID‐19 pneumonia (n = 5), cardiac arrest, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML), pneumonia, and subarachnoid hemorrhage (n = 1 each), plus pneumonia and sepsis in one patient.

TEAEs considered related to treatment were recorded in 90 patients (95.7%) with a CR, which were Grade ≥ 3 in 64 patients (68.1%) (Table 3). Treatment‐related TEAEs that led to discontinuation of therapy in patients with a CR were arthralgia, decreased weight, frontal lobe epilepsy, PML, and viral bronchitis (n = 1 each). Among TEAEs that led to death, pneumonia (n = 1) and PML (n = 1) were considered treatment‐related by the investigator.

Adverse events of special interest in the overall population and in patients with CR are reported in Table 5, which include CRS and infections. CRS events were reported in 56.4% (53/94) of patients who had a CR, which were mostly Grade 1 (41.5%) or Grade 2 (10.6%) and less frequently Grade ≥ 3 (4.3%), with no Grade 4 or 5 events reported. These rates were comparable to those recorded in the overall population (any grade, 56.3%; Grade 1, 38.3%; Grade 2, 14.1%; Grade ≥ 3, 3.9%; Grade 4/5, 0). Apart from one Grade ≥ 3 CRS event with the optimized 0.7/4/20 mg step‐up regimen, all reported Grade ≥ 3 CRS events were in patients who received the 1/20 mg regimen (overall population: 5.9% [4/68 patients]; patients with CR: 5.8% [3/52]). Median time to onset of CRS in patients with a CR who were treated with the optimized regimen was 19.7 hours (range 1.0–159.0), with CRS events lasting for a median of 8.1 hours (range 0.6–184.0), which was similar to the overall population. 18

Infections were reported in 85.1% (80/94) of patients with a CR (Grade 3, 33.0%; Grade 4, 3.2%; Grade 5, 8.5%), with 44.7% of patients experiencing COVID‐19‐related infections (Grade 3, 12.8%; Grade 4, 0; Grade 5, 5.3%); these rates were similar to those observed in the overall population (Table 6). Among five patients with a CR who had a COVID‐related fatal infection, countries where treatment took place were Poland (n = 2), the Republic of Korea, France, and Spain (n = 1 each). The rate of Grade ≥ 3 infections by system organ class was higher in patients with a CR who were treated with the 1/20 mg regimen (50.0%) than in those who were treated with the 0.7/4/20 mg regimen (38.1%) (Table 5). The most common non‐COVID‐19‐related infections of any grade in patients with a CR included urinary tract infection (20.2%), upper respiratory tract infection (18.1%), and pneumonia (16.0%). Nine patients with a CR (9.6%) had treatment‐emergent infections that led to discontinuation of therapy, including six patients with COVID‐19 infection or COVID‐19 pneumonia, which were detected 2.8–30.5 months after treatment initiation. This was similar to the rate of treatment discontinuation due to infection in the overall population (8.6%). In an analysis of fatal infections according to duration of odronextamab treatment (<6 months, ≥6 to <12 months, ≥12 to <18 months, and ≥18 months) in patients with FL who had a CR, median time to fatal infection was not reached (95% CI NE–NE) regardless of treatment duration (Figure S3), and estimated probabilities of fatal infection at 12 months were 20.6% (95% CI 8.3–46.0), 6.3% (95% CI 0.9–36.8), 0 (95% CI 0–0), and 0 (95% CI 0–0), respectively.

DISCUSSION

This exploratory analysis assessed the dynamics of CRs in patients with R/R FL treated with odronextamab in the pivotal, Phase 2 ELM‐2 study. CR rates were high (73.4%) and durable (median DOCR: 25.1 months), and 90.4% of patients with a CR had at least one high‐risk feature. The safety profile of odronextamab was generally manageable and comparable between patients who achieved a CR and the overall FL population of ELM‐2.

In ELM‐2, >90% of responders achieved a CR, most by the first response assessment. The timing of the first response assessment at approximately 12 weeks meant that some early CRs attained before this may not have been recorded, thus potentially leading to an overestimation of median time to CR (2.7 months). A small number of patients required further treatment to reach CR, and from 6 months, improved PFS was associated with longer treatment duration, indicating that longer odronextamab treatment can achieve additional reductions in tumor burden.

The achievement of CR with frontline FL treatment has proven to be beneficial in terms of long‐term survival; with rituximab‐containing regimens, 13‐year OS estimates of 77% and 37% have been reported in patients who did and did not achieve a CR, and 10‐year OS estimates of 87% and 53% have been reported in patients with and without a 30‐month CR, respectively. 9 , 25 However, investigation into the impact of CR durability on long‐term outcomes in the R/R FL setting has been limited. Here, we demonstrate that median PFS was longest in patients who had a CR, highlighting the importance of achieving a deep response in R/R FL. In support, PFS was also improved in patients with 3L+ R/R FL who achieved a CR versus those with a PR in the pivotal Phase 1/2 study of mosunetuzumab (NCT02500407; 3 years of follow‐up) and in the Phase 1/2 EPCORE NHL‐1 study of epcoritamab (NCT03625037; <1.5 years of follow‐up). 13 , 26

Importantly, this sub‐analysis of ELM‐2 demonstrated that CR could be achieved with odronextamab treatment in patients with high‐risk features, including those with a FLIPI score of 3–5, POD24, and/or refractoriness to their last line of therapy. These features were specifically chosen for this analysis in preference to the number of prior lines, as previous data indicate that the number of prior lines has less prognostic impact; of note, the primary analysis of the R/R FL cohort of ELM‐2 showed an ORR of >75% in patients with ≤2 or >2 prior lines of therapy. 18 The negative prognostic features evaluated in this analysis did not appear to affect time to CR or DOCR. Subgroup analyses of epcoritamab and mosunetuzumab have demonstrated similar CR rates in patients with high‐risk features compared with the overall population, 27 , 28 but the impact of high‐risk features on response durability with these agents has not been extensively explored. The lack of association between high‐risk features and response in patients treated with CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibodies indicates that this class may be agnostic to some of the prognostic factors for chemoimmunotherapy.

Almost half (46.8%) of all patients with a CR in the ELM‐2 study maintained a durable CR for ≥9 months and switched to Q4W dosing. These patients exhibited sustained CRs, indicating the feasibility of less‐frequent dosing in patients who maintain deep response for a prolonged period. Transition to dosing less often has been shown to have a positive impact on patient‐reported domains such as stress levels in those undergoing intravenous rituximab treatment for B‐NHL, 29 highlighting a potential added benefit for this odronextamab dosing strategy.

Other subgroups of interest in patients with FL include those with low CD20 expression, which has been associated with poorer responses and reduced survival in patients with B‐cell lymphomas treated with rituximab‐based therapy.30, 31, 32 With a CR rate of 60% among patients with baseline CD20 < 10% by IHC, and 40% among the small number of patients with low or negative CD20 mRNA expression, odronextamab is the first bispecific antibody to demonstrate compelling efficacy in patients with low or undetectable CD20 expression. Of interest, odronextamab also appeared active regardless of CD20 expression in patients with R/R diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (DLBCL) from ELM‐2. 33 It should be noted that it is difficult to draw robust conclusions on the impact of CD20 expression from this small sample size, but in support of these data, molecular assessments in the Phase 1 ELM‐1 study (NCT02290951) showed that odronextamab was active regardless of baseline CD20 expression in patients with R/R FL and DLBCL. 23 Possible explanation for the efficacy of odronextamab in the presence of low or no CD20 expression includes bystander killing, whereby the treatment attacks off‐target (CD20‐negative) tumor cells in the vicinity of cells it is designed to target (i.e., CD20‐positive tumor cells), 34 or that odronextamab can bind and kill cells expressing CD20 at levels below the assays' sensitivity. Conclusions regarding the association of CD20 expression with responses in patients with B‐NHL using other approved agents in this drug class have been variable, owing to heterogeneous methodologies of assessment, prior CD20‐targeting treatments, and low patient numbers. 32 , 35 , 36 The ongoing Phase 3 OLYMPIA studies of odronextamab in earlier lines of therapy (NCT06091254, NCT06097364, NCT06091865, NCT06230224, and NCT06149286) include biomarker assessment as a prespecified endpoint and will help to improve understanding of the impact of varying CD20 expression on CD3 × CD20 bispecific antibody activity in R/R B‐NHL.37, 38, 39, 40, 41

Among patients with CR, our data support the benefit of attaining ctDNA MRD negativity at Week 12, which appears to be associated with longer PFS. Accumulating evidence suggests that MRD status has prognostic value in patients with FL, 42 including in those with a CR. 43 However, further studies are needed to determine how sustained MRD negativity impacts PFS and OS over a longer period, and whether such an assessment should be used routinely in clinical practice.

The safety profile of odronextamab was generally manageable in patients with a CR and similar to that of the overall ELM‐2 FL population. There were slight increases in the rates of treatment‐related TEAEs in patients with a CR relative to the overall population, which may be attributed to the increased treatment exposure in the CR group. Rates of CRS and infections were similar in patients with CR and in the overall study population, and any‐grade infection rates were mostly related to COVID‐19 (44.7% and 36.7%, respectively). The frequency of treatment discontinuation due to treatment‐emergent infections in patients with a CR (9.6%) was comparable to the overall population (8.6%); this indicates that in patients with a CR in whom treatment duration was longer, there is no signal for increased susceptibility to infection. Prolonged treatment was not associated with an increased risk of fatal infections in patients with FL who had a CR, similar to a previous report in a combined population of patients with FL or DLBCL who achieved a CR from ELM‐2 44 ; these data further support continued odronextamab treatment in the absence of disease progression. Likewise, rates of serious infections in patients with R/R FL treated with mosunetuzumab were mostly confined to the first four cycles (3 months) of treatment. 27

The study confirms the high and durable CR rate with odronextamab in patients with R/R FL, although limitations of this analysis should be acknowledged. The study had a limited sample size, and the study of long‐term outcomes is affected by the number of patients who had discontinued treatment at data cutoff (70% of patients with CR discontinued treatment). Of note, almost 20% of patients with CR who discontinued did so due to physician decision, compared with 26% due to progressive disease. Odronextamab was among the first bispecific antibodies to be investigated in R/R B‐NHL, 13 , 18 , 26 , 45 and when ELM‐2 initiated, the impact of long‐term treatment was unknown. Hence, it is likely that some physicians will have stopped treatment when patients achieved a deep response and chosen to consolidate it with alternative treatment (e.g., stem cell transplant) rather than continue odronextamab. Likewise, some physicians may have stopped treatment altogether in patients who achieved a CR, which was deemed sufficiently beneficial, to avoid continued drug exposure in this generally elderly population. Indeed, the protocol permitted permanent treatment discontinuation if the investigator deemed it in the best interest of the patient. More recent data have shown that extended odronextamab treatment is tolerable and efficacious in patients with R/R FL, 46 and future studies will provide further information on the long‐term efficacy and safety outcomes in patients who achieve CR. It should also be noted that the assessment of CR dynamics was an exploratory analysis of ELM‐2, although these data support the primary analysis that demonstrated the benefit of odronextamab in heavily pretreated R/R FL. 18

In conclusion, the results of this analysis demonstrate the sustainability of CRs in patients with R/R FL treated with 3L+ odronextamab, including those with high‐risk features. CR rates were high and durable, and 93.6% of patients who attained a CR did so by the first response assessment at approximately Week 12, with the remaining patients converting to a CR at a later time point. Patients with low or undetectable CD20 expression could still have a CR to treatment, and compelling responses translated to favorable survival outcomes, with improved PFS and OS in patients with CR compared with the overall population. PFS was also improved in patients without detectable ctDNA at Week 12. Consistent with the overall population, the safety profile of odronextamab was generally manageable in patients who had a CR. Together, these findings support the potential long‐term use of odronextamab in patients with heavily pretreated R/R FL.

Convolutions of second order sequences: A direct approach


Abstract
Using a direct algebraic approach we derive convolution identities for second order sequences, hereby distinguishing between sequences obeying the same or different recurrence relations. We also state a general convolution for Horadam sequences. Convolutions for Chebyshev polynomials will also be stated.

Motivation and introduction

denote the
n

Jacobsthal-Lucas, Pell, Pell-Lucas, balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers, respectively.

Convolutions are ubiquitous in number theory. They exist for many important number and polynomial sequences
and are usually derived via manipulations of the respective generating functions.
Classical examples involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are
[
17
,
16
]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{k}F_{n-k}=\frac{1}{5}((n+1)L_{n}-2F_{n+1}),

(1)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{k}L_{n-k}=(n+1)L_{n}+2F_{n+1},

(2)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{k}F_{n-k}=(n+1)F_{n}.

(3)

Other examples for mixed convolutions are

\sum_{k=0}^{n}J_{k}F_{n-k}=J_{n+1}-F_{n+1}\quad(\mbox{see}\,\,\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Koshy18}{}{}]}),

(4)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{k}F_{n-k}=P_{n}-F_{n}\quad(\mbox{see}\,\,\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Greubel,Seiffert}{}{}]}),

(5)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{k}J_{n-k}=j_{n+1}-L_{n+1}\quad(\mbox{see}\,\,\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{griffiths15,bramham16}{}{}]})

(6)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{2k}B_{2(n-k)}=\frac{1}{31}\Big(B_{2n}-6F_{2n}\Big)\quad(\mbox{see}\,\,\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{frontczak19}{}{}]}).

(7)

For a more general treatment of convolutions relevant for this study see the papers

This paper offers a different and more direct access to convolutions for second order sequences
through the manipulation of various algebraic identities. The approach is very intuitive
as it does not require any knowledge about generating functions. Among the many results that will be derived,
we will prove the following generalization of (
6
) (Theorem
8
below):
For all integers
r
and
n
, we have the convolution

\begin{split}\gamma(r)\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{rk}J_{r(n-k)}&=(-1)^{r}(L_{r}-j_{r})L_{rn}J_{r}\\
&\qquad-\left(\left({(-1)^{r}+j_{2r}}\right)J_{r}-J_{3r}\right)L_{r(n+1)}\\
&\qquad+(-1)^{r}2^{r}J_{rn}\left((-1)^{r}2^{r+1}-L_{r}j_{r}+L_{2r}\right)\\
&\qquad-(-1)^{r}J_{r(n+1)}\left(\left(L_{r}-2\right)j_{r}+\left(1-(-1)^{r}\right)L_{r}\right),\end{split}

where

\gamma(r)=j_{2r}\left(1+(-1)^{r}\right)+(-1)^{r}2^{r}L_{r}(L_{r}-j_{r})-(-1)^{r}L_{r}j_{r}.

As will be seen below, our approach is also applicable for proving convolutions for Chebyshev polynomials.
Such convolutions have also been studied recently by Fan and Chu
[
7
]
.

The Horadam sequence
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=(w_{n}(a,b;p,q))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is defined, for all integers,
by the recurrence relation
[
15
]

w_{0}=a,\ w_{1}=b,\ \ w_{n}=pw_{n-1}-qw_{n-2},\quad n\geq 2,

with

w_{-n}=\frac{1}{q}(pw_{-n+1}-w_{-n+2}),

where
a,b,p
and
q
are arbitrary complex numbers with non-zero
p
and
q
. The sequence
w_{n}
generalizes many
important number and polynomial sequences. Sequences

U_{n}=U_{n}(p,q)=w_{n}(0,1;p,q)\quad\text{and}\quad V_{n}=V_{n}(p,q)=w_{n}(2,p;p,q)

are called the Lucas sequences of the first kind and of the second kind, respectively. The Binet formulas for the sequences

U_{n},V_{n}
and
w_{n}
in the non-degenerated case,
\Delta=p^{2}-4q>0
, are

U_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad V_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},\quad w_{n}=\frac{b-a\beta}{\alpha-\beta}\alpha^{n}+\frac{a\alpha-b}{\alpha-\beta}\beta^{n},

where

\alpha=\alpha(p,q)=\frac{p+\sqrt{p^{2}-4q}}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta=\beta(p,q)=\frac{p-\sqrt{p^{2}-4q}}{2}

are the distinct zeros of the characteristic polynomial
x^{2}-px+q
.

For an integer
n\geq 0
, the Chebyshev polynomials
(t_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
of the first kind are defined by

t_{0}(x)=1,\,t_{1}(x)=x,\quad t_{n+1}(x)=2xt_{n}(x)-t_{n-1}(x),

while the Chebyshev polynomials
(u_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
of the second kind are defined by

u_{0}(x)=1,\ \ u_{1}(x)=2x,\ \ u_{n+1}(x)=2xu_{n}(x)-u_{n-1}(x).

Chebyshev polynomials are special Horadam sequence members and their Binet-like formulas are given by

\displaystyle t_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n}+(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n}\right),

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\left((x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n+1}-(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n+1}\right).

from which we also get

t_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(u_{n}(x)-u_{n-2}(x)).

Our starting point is the identity stated in the next Theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
,

y

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left({A_{1}x^{k}+B_{1}y^{k}}\right)\left({A_{2}z^{n-k}+B_{2}w^{n-k}}\right)\\
&\qquad=A_{1}A_{2}\frac{{x^{n+1}-z^{n+1}}}{{x-z}}+A_{1}B_{2}\frac{{x^{n+1}-w^{n+1}}}{{x-w}}+A_{2}B_{1}\frac{{y^{n+1}-z^{n+1}}}{{y-z}}+B_{1}B_{2}\frac{{y^{n+1}-w^{n+1}}}{{y-w}}.\end{split}

(8)

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward calculation using the partial sum of the geometric progression.
∎

(42)

where

\gamma(r)=1+4^{r}-((-1)^{r}+(-2)^{r})Q_{r}j_{r}+(-1)^{r}j_{2r}+(-2)^{r}Q_{r}^{2}.

.

For

\begin{split}\gamma(r)\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{X_{rk}}V_{Y_{r(n-k)}}&=q_{X}^{r}V_{X_{rn}}\left(2(q_{X}^{r}-{V_{X}}_{r}{V_{Y}}_{r}+{V_{Y}}_{2r})+{V_{X}}_{r}V_{Y_{r}}-V_{Y_{2r}}\right)\\

\gamma(r)=q_{X}^{2r}+q_{Y}^{2r}-(q_{X}^{r}+q_{Y}^{r}){V_{X}}_{r}{V_{Y}}_{r}+q_{X}^{r}{V_{Y}}_{2r}+q_{Y}^{r}{V_{X}}_{r}^{2}.

4
More convolutions from a simple relation

In this section we state a slight generalization of an identity of Carlitz
[
3
]
.
The idea can also be applied to Chebyshev polynomials.

Theorem 14
.

Let
U_{n},V_{n},t_{n}(x)
, and
u_{n}(x)
be the Lucas sequences and Chebyshev polynomials of the first and the second kinds, respectively.
Let further
T(n,k),0\leq k\leq n
be a sequence such that
T(n,k)=T(n,n-k)
. Then we have the convolution identities

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)U_{rk}V_{r(n-k)}=U_{rn}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)

(51)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)u_{rk-1}(x)t_{r(n-k)}(x)=\frac{u_{rn-1}(x)}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k).

(52)

Proof.

Using the Binet forms we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)U_{rk}V_{r(n-k)}=U_{rn}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)+\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)\Big(\alpha^{rk}\beta^{r(n-k)}-\alpha^{r(n-k)}\beta^{rk}\Big).

Now,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)\alpha^{rk}\beta^{r(n-k)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,n-k)\alpha^{r(n-k)}\beta^{rk}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)\alpha^{r(n-k)}\beta^{rk},

and therefore

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)\Big(\alpha^{rk}\beta^{r(n-k)}-\alpha^{r(n-k)}\beta^{rk}\Big)=0.

The proof of the Chebyshev variant is very similar and omitted.
∎

Example
.

Let
T(n,k)=1
. Then we recover (
16
) and get for the Chebyshev polynomials

\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{rk-1}(x)t_{r(n-k)}(x)=\frac{n+1}{2}u_{rn-1}(x).

(53)

Example
.

Let
T(n,k)=k(n-k)
. Then we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k(n-k)U_{rk}V_{r(n-k)}=\frac{(n-1)n(n+1)}{6}U_{rn},

(54)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k(n-k)u_{rk-1}(x)t_{r(n-k)}(x)=\frac{(n-1)n(n+1)}{12}u_{rn-1}(x),

(55)

where we used the simple evaluation

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k(n-k)=\frac{(n-1)n(n+1)}{6}.

Example
.

Let
T(n,k)=k^{2}(n-k)^{2}
. Then we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{2}(n-k)^{2}U_{rk}V_{r(n-k)}=\frac{n(n^{4}-1)}{30}U_{rn},

(56)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{2}(n-k)^{2}u_{rk-1}(x)t_{r(n-k)}(x)=\frac{n(n^{4}-1)}{60}u_{rn-1}(x),

(57)

where we used the simple evaluation

\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{2}(n-k)^{2}=\frac{n(n^{4}-1)}{30}.

Example
.

Let

\sum_{k=0}^{n}U_{rk}^{2}U_{r(n-k)}V_{r(n-k)}=\frac{U_{rn}}{\Delta U_{r}}\Big((n+1)U_{r}V_{rn}-2U_{r(n+1)}\Big),

(58)

21

\sum_{k=0}^{n}U_{rk}V_{rk}V_{r(n-k)}^{2}=\frac{U_{rn}}{U_{r}}\Big((n+1)U_{r}V_{rn}+2U_{r(n+1)}\Big).

(59)

5
Concluding remarks

This paper offers a direct approach to convolutions of second order sequences,
without any use of generating functions. It allows in a straightforward manner to generalize
some fixed convolutions involving these sequences. Though not stated explicitly in the main text,
the approach is also applicable to derive convolutions for Fibonacci (Lucas) polynomials,
Pell (Pell-Lucas) polynomials and other polynomial families.

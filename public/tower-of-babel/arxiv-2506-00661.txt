Poster: Adapting Pretrained Vision Transformers with LoRA Against Attack Vectors
Richard E. Neddo, Sean Willis, Zander Blasingame, Chen Liu

Abstract
Image classifiers, such as those used for autonomous vehicle navigation, are largely known to be susceptible to adversarial attacks that target the input image set. There is extensive discussion on adversarial attacks including perturbations that alter the input images to cause malicious misclassifications without perceivable modification. This work proposes a countermeasure for such attacks by adjusting the weights and classes of pre-trained vision transformers with a low-rank adaptation to become more robust against adversarial attacks and allow for scalable fine-tuning without retraining.



I
Introduction

Popular tools for image classification include the convolutional neural network (CNN), deep neural network (DNN), and vision transformer (ViT)
[
1
]
. Unlike traditional neural networks, which rely on localized filters and feature extraction, ViTs process images by dividing them into fixed patches. These patches are flattened into sequences, embedded in lower-dimensional vectors, and combined with positional encoding to retain spatial information. This sequence is fed into a transformer encoder to model global relationships between patches. This process allows ViTs to outperform traditional networks in tasks, including object detection and classification.

Image classifiers, including ViTs and neural networks, are inherently susceptible to adversarial attacks. Typically, these attacks take the form of a digital perturbation attack. These attacks are a subset of adversarial techniques designed to exploit vulnerabilities in the decision boundaries of a model, causing misclassifications
[
2
]
. However, ViTs typically require large datasets for training because of the lack of built-in inductive biases. Thus, although not exclusive to ViTs, when new data is acquired or a new class is introduced, the process is both time-consuming and expensive, which is where a low-ranking adaptation can be advantageous. Low-ranking adaptation (LoRA)
[
3
]
is a technique to adjust the weights of a pre-trained neural network without replacing them. This appending allows the trained model to include new training data or direct the model toward a different goal than its initial parameters
[
4
]
.
Adjustment of low-rank parameters has previously been shown to be used to improve the efficiency of the central neural network
[
3
]
, however, this technique has not been applied to adjust a pre-trained ViT. We propose an amendable LoRA architecture that allows for fine-tuning a pre-trained ViT model to defend against misclassifications caused by adversarial perturbation attacks.

II
Related works

Image classifiers are often susceptible to attacks that involve adversarial perturbations. These attacks are defined by introducing digital noise to alter the input to the image classifiers so that the image is misclassified without creating large perceptible changes
[
5
]
.
Perturbation attacks were widely considered to be a consequence of nonlinearity and overfitting; however, Szegedy et al. proposed that due to the tendency of neural networks to be modeled in a linear nature for ease of training, perturbation attacks can exploit said linearity
[
6
]
. This claim is supported by illustrating a generalized perturbation that was effective on linearly trained models and by a refined process to generate generalized perturbation attacks.
Visual transformers for autonomous navigation have previously been studied, with Xu et al. focusing on vehicle-to-everything communication using ViT
[
7
]
. However, little work is being done on defensive measures against adversarial attacks targeting ViTs due to extensive training requirements.

III
Methodology

III-A

Vision Transformer

The pre-trained ViT chosen was Google’s public vit-base-patch16-224-in21k model
[
8
]
. This model was selected for its balance between performance, scalability, and size
[
9
]
. The initial model was pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, which consists of 14 million images and 21 thousand classes. The model takes a
224\times 224
input resolution and uses patches based on
16\times 16
for efficient processing.

III-B

Datasets

The data set chosen to source stop sign images was the Mapillary dataset due to the diverse nature of the dataset
[
10
]
. The data set contains extensive annotations which include semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, bounding boxes, and attribute tags, covering more than 100 object categories. When selecting data, the regulatory_stop_g1 class was selected, which consists of 1386 images that were cropped to
224\times 224
to match the input data requirement in the ViT and for perturbation generation.

III-C

Perturbation Attacks

In this study, one perturbation technique, the fast gradient sign method (FGSM), was used to highlight vulnerability in the pre-trained ViTs
[
11
]
. We draw a batch
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
from the input data distribution.
w_{n}
is the sample per weight,
y_{n}
is the target label representing the binary ground truth, and
f_{\theta}(x_{n})
is the batch probabilities.
The loss function used in the FSGM is the non-reduced BCEWithLogitsLoss class defined as

L=-w_{n}[y_{n}\cdot\log f_{\theta}(x_{n})+(1-y_{n})\cdot\log(1-f_{\theta}(x_{n}))]

(1)

and is utilized in the perturbation function as
\nabla_{x}L
.

FSGM is a single-step attack that generates adversarial perturbations by computing the loss gradient relative to the input image and adding a scaled version to the original image
[
11
]
. This process is defined as
x_{adv}=x+\epsilon\cdot\textrm{sgn}(\nabla_{x}L)
where
x_{adv}
is the adversarial output image (
Figure

1(b)
),
x
is the input image (
Figure

1(a)
), and
\epsilon
is the intensity of the noise.

III-D

LoRA

Two LoRAs are used to efficiently capture the characteristics that make up the desired stop sign class. These LoRAs are configured with a dropout of 0.1, a rank of 64, and an
\alpha
of 16.
The original weight matrix is defined by:
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times c}
where

d
is the dimension of the characteristic and
c
is the original number of classes.
k=c
in multiclass scenarios and
k=2
for binary classification,
\alpha
is the scaling factor and
r
is the rank.
After adding a LoRA, the weight matrix then contains:

W^{\prime}=W+\alpha BA
where
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k}
. Doing so for two LoRAs updates the weight matrix to
W^{(2)}=W^{(1)}+\alpha_{2}B_{2}A_{2}=W+\alpha_{1}B_{1}A_{1}+\alpha_{2}B_{2}A_{2}
where
B_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},A_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times c},B_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times c}
.

The first LoRA contains weights and labels from images of stop signs and amends them to the weights from the pre-trained ViT and creates a label for a new binary Stop Sign class. The second LoRA contains weights and labels from perturbed stop-sign images after the ViT is trained to detect them as stop signs, which are amended to the final model.

IV
Experimental Setup

All computation and evaluations done in this work were performed on a system consisting of
a Ryzen 7 5800x3D CPU and an NVIDIA Geforce RTX 4090 24GB GPU with CUDA version 12.6 and CUDNN version 8.9. The perturbation attacks, vision transformer, and classifiers were implemented using PyTorch in Python version 3.10.11.

V
Results

To assess the impact that perturbation attacks have on a model before and after the application of LoRA patches, a classification script was developed to determine the correct classification rate (CCR) of the pre-trained ViT fine-tuned with LoRAs. ViT with a stop sign class and ViT with a stop sign class and perturbation patch were evaluated using a series of FSGM perturbation attacks at different strengths represented by
\epsilon
.
As seen in Table
I
, the ViT is largely susceptible to perturbation attacks after adding only the stop sign class. The ViT was able to classify the stop signs at a rate of
79.9\%
correctly; however, the CCR decreased anywhere from
15.50\%
to
55\%
after a perturbation was applied to the stop signs.
After applying the second LoRA to the ViT and patching the adversarial vector, the CCR of scenarios involving perturbation attacks increased anywhere from
44.9\%
to
84.4\%

VI
Conclusion

Image classifiers are commonly used in autonomous driving workloads; however, models such as ViT are susceptible to perturbation attacks. In this work, we propose using LORAs to update a ViT to defend against perturbation attacks, without retraining. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach by adapting a pre-trained ViT with LoRAs. The ViT model’s ability to classify a new class of images before and after perturbation increases by up to
84.4\%
when tuned to detect FSGM perturbations.
The current work was limited in a few ways. The performance of the LoRA model was limited as both LoRAs utilize a relatively small training data set, approximately 1300 images, the images of which are poor quality. Furthermore, the work was only able to study one type of perturbation attack, where ideally more perturbations and adversarial attack vectors would have been analyzed for a more comprehensive conclusion. Lastly, ViTs with different architectures could have been explored to view differences in pre-trained performance before and after adaptation with LoRAs.

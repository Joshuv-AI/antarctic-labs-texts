Introduction

In recent years, the internet has become an indispensable part of human life, serving both as a rich source of information and as a key tool enabling communication across the globe (Chung et al. 2019). With technological advances, the internet has also become a significant source of health information (Drozd et al. 2018). Within this context, YouTube stands out not only as the world's most popular video‐sharing platform but also as the second‐largest search engine worldwide (Szmuda et al. 2020).

Since 2015, the International Council of Nurses (ICN) has aimed to amplify the voice of nurses and reach broader audiences by sharing video content through its YouTube channel (International Council of Nurses 2015a). Examining the communication activities of an internationally influential organization such as ICN on the YouTube platform constitutes an important necessity for understanding the digital representation and global visibility of the nursing profession. However, the existing literature lacks a systematic and thematic analysis focusing on the video content published on ICN's YouTube channel. This gap indicates a need for examining ICN's digital communication using data‐driven methods and highlights the original contribution of the present study.

Background

The ICN is a federation representing more than 140 national nurses’ associations and over 30 million nurses worldwide (International Council of Nurses n.d.‐a). Founded in 1899, it is the first and most comprehensive international organization representing the nursing profession globally (International Council of Nurses n.d.‐a). Its mission is to represent nursing worldwide, advance the profession, promote nurses’ well‐being, and advocate for health in all policies (International Council of Nurses n.d.‐b). The ICN also contributes actively to shaping health policies on global platforms such as the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations (Catton 2021).

Through digital transformation, ICN began using its YouTube channel in 2015 to disseminate messages about the nursing profession to global communities (International Council of Nurses 2015a). ICN acknowledges social media as a powerful tool for strengthening nursing, facilitating rapid communication, educating, and influencing (International Council of Nurses 2015b). It also supports nurses’ use of social media for professional development, health education, and public awareness (International Council of Nurses 2015b). Analyzing ICN's YouTube videos is therefore vital for understanding the scope and themes of the content and for contributing to the development of effective communication strategies.

This study aims to analyze the content of the ICN YouTube channel to provide thematic insights that contribute to a better understanding of the organization's digital communication strategies. Although the titles and descriptions of ICN videos often reflect the main topic, they provide only a brief editorial summary. In contrast, subtitles capture the full spoken content of the videos, allowing the examination of relationships and co‐occurrence patterns among themes. Therefore, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) was used not to redefine the topics explicitly reflected in the titles, but to analyze word co‐occurrence patterns throughout the subtitles in order to identify the latent themes addressed simultaneously in the videos and the relationships among these themes.

In this regard, thematic patterns and message structures within the video content were examined using the LDA method. LDA is a probabilistic topic modeling approach used to identify hidden themes in large text collections. Developed by Blei et al. (2003), this method assumes that each document can include multiple topics, represented by specific sets of words. By facilitating the extraction of meaningful structures from unlabeled texts, LDA is one of the most frequently used methods in content analysis (Jelodar et al. 2019). LDA enables systematic thematic analysis by revealing topic distributions within texts and the prominent keywords associated with each topic (Jelodar et al. 2019).

Recent studies indicate that the LDA method has been widely used to analyze different types of data in the health field. For instance, Danler et al. (2024) employed LDA to analyze nursing care reports, while Kim et al. (2024) examined trends in digital health research in nursing using this method. Luo et al. (2025) applied LDA to analyze TikTok data related to mpox, whereas Mutanga and Abayomi (2022) analyzed tweets concerning the COVID‐19 pandemic. These studies demonstrate that LDA is an effective method for identifying thematic patterns across diverse content types in the fields of health and nursing.

In this context, the present study applies the LDA analytical method to identify prominent messages and latent thematic structures in video content published on the ICN YouTube channel between 2015 and 2024.

We also wanted to analyze word co‐occurrence patterns in the subtitles of videos published on the ICN YouTube channel to identify the relationships among themes and the latent themes reflected in the subtitles. The findings of this study are expected to contribute to understanding and improving ICN's digital communication strategies conducted through YouTube.

Aim of Study

This study aims to analyze the video content published on ICN's YouTube channel using the LDA method in order to provide thematic insights that can strengthen the organization's digital communication strategies and support its strategic objectives. The study addressed the following research questions:

Methods

Research Design

This study employed a content analysis approach to identify recurring themes and messages within the video subtitles of the ICN YouTube channel. The research was conducted using an inductive design aimed at deriving themes directly from the data, rather than testing a pre‐established theory or hypothesis. To achieve this, LDA, a quantitative text analysis method, was employed to identify latent thematic structures by analyzing word co‐occurrence patterns in the video subtitles.

Sample and Setting

The sample of this study consisted of video subtitles obtained from the ICN YouTube channel. The analysis was carried out on 193 unique video subtitles drawn from ICN's online platform.

Data Collection

Data were collected directly from the ICN YouTube channel. By September 2024, a data set of 260 videos had been compiled. Some of these videos did not include subtitle content, while others were duplicates of the same video in different languages (e.g., Spanish and French). Videos with accessible English subtitles were included in the study. Therefore, three videos without subtitles and 64 duplicate videos in other languages were excluded, leaving a total of 193 video subtitles for analysis. The stages of the analysis process are provided in Supplement S1.

Data Preprocessing

A series of preprocessing steps were applied to prepare the textual data for analysis:

Text Standardization

All texts were converted to lowercase, and punctuation marks were removed. This ensured consistency within the data set and eliminated unnecessary variation during analysis.

Tokenization

Texts were segmented into meaningful units (words/tokens). This step, a fundamental phase of natural language processing, divided the text into smaller, analyzable parts. In this study, Python's Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) library was used.

Stopword Filtering

To enhance analytical relevance, frequently occurring but semantically unimportant words were removed. Using NLTK's English stopword list and custom rules, words such as “the,” “is,” “in,” and numerical values were excluded from the data set (Bird et al. 2009). This step facilitated the clearer identification of meaningful topics.

In addition, the custom stopword list was iteratively refined based on the results of the preliminary topic modeling. During this process, speaker names, filler expressions, and high‐frequency data set–specific words with limited analytical value were added to the stopword list, whereas key nursing‐related terms were retained to preserve the semantic context of the corpus. The additional stopwords added to the default NLTK English stopword list are provided in Supplement S3.

Lemmatization

Words were reduced to their root forms using grammar rules and dictionary‐based approaches. This step consolidated semantically equivalent words under a single representation (Kettunen et al. 2005). For example, “running,” “ran,” and “runs” were reduced to “run.”

N‐Gram Modeling

Using the Gensim library, frequently co‐occurring word groups (bigrams and trigrams) were generated. This allowed semantically cohesive word combinations to be treated as single components (Řehůřek and Sojka 2010).

These preprocessing steps optimized the text for analysis and contributed to generating stronger model outputs.

Topic Modeling

Topic modeling methods are algorithms designed to transform latent thematic information within document collections into lower‐dimensional structures and to reveal these themes (Lu et al. 2011). LDA is one of the most widely used approaches in topic modeling (Jelodar et al. 2019). LDA assumes that documents are composed of a probabilistic mixture of latent topics and that the distribution of these topics is modeled using a shared Dirichlet prior distribution (Jelodar et al. 2019). LDA is a statistical learning method employed to extract meaningful patterns from complex data sets and to discover hidden themes in unstructured texts. This method identifies different topic groups within a document collection and assumes that each document may include multiple topics. This feature is particularly useful in identifying meaningful themes across large and diverse text collections (Barde and Bainwad 2017). In this study, the LDA model was applied. The model was optimized to determine the most probable word and topic distributions within the video subtitles. In this study, LDA was used not to reconfirm the topics explicitly presented in the video titles, but to identify word co‐occurrence patterns and the relationships among themes in the video subtitles. In this way, the entire subtitle corpus was analyzed using a systematic and data‐driven approach.

Baimakhanbetov (2023) noted that the number of topics in LDA modeling is frequently determined using perplexity and coherence metrics. By creating several LDA models with different numbers of topics (k), these metrics were calculated to select the most appropriate model (Baimakhanbetov 2023). In the present study, a similar approach was adopted, and through this process, a 10‐topic model was identified as optimal. Similar studies have also found that a model consisting of 10 topics is the most reasonable, and this approach was adopted (Won et al. 2021). Considering that an excessive number of topics may reduce interpretability, the number of topics was optimized through an iterative process.

The model outputs were reviewed by two independent researchers, who reached consensus on the most appropriate results. This iterative process enhanced the model's reliability and validity. The topics generated by LDA were interpreted by two domain experts. The most frequent words and word groups under each topic were examined, enabling the identification of themes frequently addressed in the video content. This step ensured a comprehensive interpretation of computer‐assisted text analysis results and strengthened the validity of the model.

Topic Labeling

The initial interpretation of topics began with examining the high‐probability keywords generated by the LDA model for each topic. These keywords, representing the essence of each theme, were analyzed within the context of their respective subheadings. Two independent researchers carefully evaluated the relationship of each word to the overall topic, reviewing keyword clusters to assign descriptive labels that captured the central theme of each topic.

Clarifying Topic Boundaries

To ensure clear distinctions among topics, researchers compared keywords across different topics to identify any overlaps or ambiguities. When similar keywords appeared in multiple topics, the original subheading context was examined to determine their specific meaning. This detailed analysis allowed each topic to represent a unique thematic area without redundancy, refining the scope and focus of each category.

Synthesizing Broader Themes

Following labeling and refinement, individual topics were synthesized into broader categories reflecting ICN's primary communication focus areas. For example, topics were grouped under themes such as “Nursing and Patient Care,” “Infectious Diseases and Prevention Strategies,” and “National Health Policies.” This synthesis provided a higher‐level perspective of ICN's communication priorities, highlighting the recurring messages conveyed to its audiences.

Ensuring Reliability and Consensus

To enhance reliability, the interpretation process involved collaborative discussions between two researchers. Initial differences in perspective or interpretation were discussed and resolved, ensuring consensus on each label and theme. This collaborative approach minimized individual bias and strengthened the validity of the final thematic labels. It ensured that each theme was aligned with ICN's intended key messages.

Ethical Considerations

This study was conducted using publicly available data from the ICN YouTube channel. Since the analysis relied exclusively on openly accessible video subtitles and did not involve human participants or identifiable personal data, formal ethical approval was not required.

Data Analysis

Data analysis was conducted using Python 3.13.5 (Anaconda distribution). Gensim 4.4.0 (Řehůřek and Sojka 2010) was used for LDA topic modeling, NLTK 3.9.1 (Bird et al. 2009) for text preprocessing, and WordCloud 1.9.6 for word cloud visualization.

Results

In this study, 193 English‐subtitled videos published on the ICN YouTube channel between 2015 and 2024 were analyzed. The distribution of video and view counts by year is shown in Supplement S2. The annual number of published videos ranged between 9 and 45. The year with the highest number of videos was 2020 (45 videos), while the lowest was 2017 (9 videos). When annual average video durations were examined, the shortest videos were observed in 2017 (2.14 minutes), whereas the longest videos were observed in 2021 (45.54 minutes). Video view counts ranged from 17 to 29,748. The highest number of views (4925) was recorded for videos published in 2017. Altogether, the 193 videos had a total of 158,536 views. In addition, the ICN YouTube channel was found to have 6430 subscribers and approximately 6 million total views (ICN n.d.).

The most frequently used unigram words in the videos were “nurse” (4356), “health” (3265), “nursing” (2685), “country” (1515), and “care” (1288). The most frequent bigram terms were “health system” (267), “nursing workforce” (207), “world health” (206), “health worker” (172), and “nursing association” (166) (Table 1).

Word clouds generated after lemmatization are presented in Figure 1. Thematic categorization was conducted by considering the coefficients of words included in the word clouds. To determine the optimal number of topics in the LDA model, models with different numbers of topics (ranging from 5 to 16) were generated, and their perplexity and coherence values were compared. The analysis revealed that the coherence (cv ) values ranged between 0.2792 and 0.2939. The 10‐topic model yielded the most balanced results, with the highest coherence value (cv = 0.2939) and the lowest perplexity value (–7.51). This finding indicates that the model achieved the most appropriate structure in terms of both thematic differentiation and interpretability with 10 topics. In addition, the relatively high overall model coherence (0.2808) supports the semantic consistency among the topics. Therefore, all subsequent analyses were conducted using the 10‐topic model. This approach is consistent with standard practices recommended in the literature for LDA model selection (Won et al. 2021).

Discussion

The findings of the study revealed that the number of videos published on ICN's YouTube channel fluctuated over the years, with a particularly pronounced increase in 2020. Possible reasons for this increase include the simultaneous effects of the WHO's declaration of 2020 as the “International Year of the Nurse and the Midwife” in honor of the 200th anniversary of Florence Nightingale's birth (WHO 2019) and the emergence of the COVID‐19 pandemic during the same period. The “International Year of the Nurse and the Midwife” initiative aimed to enhance the visibility of nursing and midwifery within global health systems and provided an important foundation for organizations such as ICN to use digital platforms more effectively. Following the declaration of COVID‐19 as a global public health emergency in March 2020, the need for information dissemination, advocacy, and visibility targeting health professionals increased further. Indeed, evidence in the literature supports that health institutions intensified their presence on social media platforms during the pandemic period (Shi et al. 2024). Within this context, the observed increase in ICN's video production in 2020 may be interpreted not as a coincidental fluctuation in content output, but rather as a reflection of deliberate efforts to maintain continuous communication with the global nursing community, strengthen the profession's visibility, and sustain its advocacy role.

The findings of this study also indicate that the number of videos, video duration, and view counts on ICN's YouTube channel varied across years. Notably, despite the relatively low number of videos published and shorter average video durations in 2017, view counts were comparatively high, suggesting that shorter and more focused content may be advantageous in terms of audience engagement. This finding is consistent with the notion that YouTube users tend to favor shorter and more easily consumable content (Yang et al. 2022).

In contrast, the increase in view counts in 2020 despite longer video durations indicates that audience engagement cannot be explained solely by formal characteristics such as content length. The emergence of the COVID‐19 pandemic as a global health crisis substantially heightened interest in health‐related digital content. This situation increased the demand for content produced by international health organizations such as ICN (Ramírez et al. 2020). In this context, the findings suggest that audience engagement is strongly influenced not only by content format but also by prevailing social and global conditions.

Data from the analyzed videos were presented together with the overall channel statistics to enable a more comprehensive assessment of engagement levels and to allow for a comparative interpretation across professional organizations. Engagement indicators show that the ICN YouTube channel has more than 6000 subscribers, that the analyzed videos have accumulated approximately 160,000 views, and that the channel's total view count is around 6 million. Considering that ICN represents more than 140 national nursing associations and over 30 million nurses worldwide (International Council of Nurses n.d.‐a), a higher level of engagement might reasonably be expected.

Comparative data partially support this assessment. Specifically, the American Nurses Association (ANA) YouTube channel has approximately 12,000 subscribers and 5 million total views, whereas the WHO YouTube channel has reached approximately 950,000 subscribers and 118 million views (American Nurses Association n.d., World Health Organization n.d.). These findings indicate that ICN's YouTube engagement is substantially lower than that of the WHO, which addresses a broad global audience, and is comparable, though more limited in scale, to that of the ANA, a national professional association. Differences in view counts and subscriber numbers between the ICN, WHO, and ANA YouTube channels should be interpreted in light of differences in their institutional missions, target audiences, and global reach. Nevertheless, ICN may benefit from adopting more engagement‐oriented content strategies to strengthen its digital visibility and engagement with its target audience. In addition to its emphasis on policy advocacy, incorporating more content that highlights everyday nursing practice and frontline care experiences may further strengthen its digital communication strategy.

According to the findings of the study, the most frequently emphasized themes in the videos included “women's empowerment and social policy response,” “professional support and training in crisis contexts,” and “professional associations and health sector development.” This indicates that ICN's communication extends beyond professional education and patient care by also foregrounding policymaking and leadership roles. This finding is consistent with the view that ICN positions nurses not only as healthcare workers but also as global health advocates (Gallagher 2020). ICN's YouTube communication can therefore be regarded as a digital engagement tool that goes beyond information sharing, aiming to enhance the visibility of the nursing profession and contribute to global health discourse. At the same time, these themes align with the thematic agendas set by ICN over the years. For example, the themes “Nurses: A Voice to Lead—Health for All” in 2019 and “A Voice to Lead—A Vision for Future Healthcare” in 2021 were identified by ICN (International Council of Nurses 2019, 2020). These themes encourage nurses to assume active roles not only in patient care but also in the transformation of health systems and in policy‐level decision‐making processes. The identified themes—particularly those focused on leadership, policy, and gender—are consistent with global health priorities such as strengthening the nursing workforce and ensuring equitable access to healthcare. However, the limited emphasis on direct clinical practice or patient‐centered content suggests a potential gap between policy‐oriented advocacy and the everyday realities of nursing practice.

This finding also appears to be consistent with ICN's institutional mission. As an international professional organization, ICN aims to advance the nursing profession globally, contribute to health policy, and strengthen the representation of nurses (International Council of Nurses n.d.‐b). Therefore, it is expected that its digital communication emphasizes macro‐level issues such as leadership, health policy, workforce planning, and professional advocacy. However, the relatively limited focus on direct clinical practice suggests a need for communication strategies that more closely connect policy and advocacy messages with the everyday realities of nursing practice.

In this context, the prominence of the leadership theme is not coincidental. ICN's emphasis on nursing leadership reflects its increasingly influential role in shaping health policies and promoting ethical and equitable health systems (Catton 2021; Gallagher 2020). Themes related to education and health policy further demonstrate that nurses are positioned not only as service providers but also as professionals who generate knowledge and contribute to policymaking processes (Zalon et al. 2024). This suggests that nurses may assume more active roles in policy development, decision‐making, and the restructuring of health systems in the future, underscoring once again the importance of leadership and policy literacy in nursing education.

In conclusion, ICN's digital communication via YouTube, despite a relatively limited volume of content, encompasses multidimensional themes and conveys strategic messages related to the global nursing agenda. Differences in view counts and subscriber numbers between the ICN, WHO, and ANA YouTube channels should be interpreted in light of differences in their institutional missions, target audiences, and global reach. Nevertheless, ICN may benefit from adopting more engagement‐oriented content strategies to strengthen its digital visibility and engagement with its target audience. In addition to its emphasis on policy advocacy, incorporating more content that highlights everyday nursing practice and frontline care experiences may further strengthen its digital communication strategy. Future studies may incorporate digital content from multiple platforms to enable a more comprehensive evaluation of ICN's overall digital strategy.

Several methodological limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was limited to unique videos with English subtitles, which resulted in a relatively smaller sample size; however, this decision aimed to avoid content duplication, given that the same videos are often uploaded separately with subtitles in different languages (e.g., English, French, and Spanish), thereby ensuring thematic consistency in the analysis. In addition, some of the English subtitles analyzed in this study may have been generated automatically by YouTube. Automatic speech recognition systems may introduce transcription errors, omitted words, or punctuation inaccuracies. Such errors may introduce semantic noise into the corpus, potentially influencing word frequencies and, consequently, the identification and interpretation of latent themes generated by the LDA model. Additionally, the exclusion of other social media platforms limits the representativeness of the findings. As the content analyzed reflects ICN's institutional perspective, selection bias may be present, while the focus on a professional target audience may also introduce audience bias. Furthermore, the assumption underlying LDA analysis—that latent themes can be identified within structured or promotional content—may give rise to methodological bias. To mitigate the potential effects of these biases, data selection and thematic interpretation were conducted independently by two researchers, and consensus was reached through discussion in order to minimize subjectivity.

Conclusion

ICN's YouTube communication is thematically rich and contributes to the global nursing agenda. Differences in views and subscribers compared with other organizations should be interpreted in light of differences in institutional missions, target audiences, and global reach. Nevertheless, adopting more engagement‐oriented digital communication strategies may further strengthen ICN's visibility and audience engagement.

Implications for Nursing Policy and Health Policy

This study shows that ICN's digital content positions nursing as both a clinical profession and a health policy actor. The prominence of leadership, gender, and advocacy highlights the need for greater nursing visibility in policymaking. Nurse leaders, professional nursing organizations, and health policymakers should strategically use digital communication to strengthen professional representation and public engagement.

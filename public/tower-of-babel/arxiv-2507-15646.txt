A comprehensive approach to the physics of mesons
A. S. Miramontes, J. M. Morgado, J. Papavassiliou, J. M. Pawlowski

Abstract
We develop a novel approach for the self-consistent solution of coupled sets of Bethe-Salpeter and Schwinger-Dyson equations in QCD. This framework allows us to maintain the axial Ward-Takahashi identities of the theory within advanced approximation schemes, such as the skeleton or three-particle irreducible expansions. For this purpose we reformulate the Schwinger-Dyson equation of the axial-vector vertex such that the bulk of its quantum corrections is expressed in terms of a novel vertex. Crucially, this vertex satisfies a symmetry-induced relation of its own, which involves the full quark-gluon vertex. As a result, the Schwinger-Dyson equation reproduces the standard Ward-Takahashi identity satisfied by the axial-vector vertex. Consequently, the known relation between the quark mass function and the wave function of the pion in the chiral limit is duly fulfilled. The present approach offers valuable insights into the interplay between symmetry and dynamics, and provides a practical path towards computations of hadron physics within sophisticated approximations. In particular, the one-loop dressed truncation of the key dynamical equations, including that of the quark-gluon vertex, is shown to be completely compatible with the required symmetry relations. Further extensions and potential phenomenological applications of the developed framework are briefly discussed.

II
Quark propagator and quark-gluon vertex

In this section we review the
main properties of two fundamental ingredients of our analysis,
namely the quark propagator and the
quark-gluon vertex.

The starting point of our considerations is the quark propagator,
S^{ab}(p)
,
depicted in
Fig.
2
,
which we cast
in the standard form

S^{ab}(p)=i\delta^{ab}S(p)
,
see

e.g.
,
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
.
Typically, one decomposes the inverse
quark propagator,
S^{-1}(p)
, as

S^{-1}(p)=A(p^{2})\not{p}-B(p^{2})~,

(1)

where
A(p^{2})
and
B(p^{2})
are the dressings of the (Dirac) vector and scalar structures, respectively. The renormalization-group invariant (RGI) quark mass function,

{\mathcal{M}}(p^{2})
,
is given by
{\mathcal{M}}(p^{2})=B(p^{2})/A(p^{2})
.

For the purposes of the present analysis, it is useful to also introduce the Dirac decomposition of
S(p)
, namely

S(p)=a(p^{2})\not{p}+b(p^{2})\,,

(2)

with

a(p^{2})=c(p^{2})A(p^{2})\,,\qquad b(p^{2})=c(p^{2})B(p^{2})\,,\qquad c(p^{2}):=\frac{1}{A^{2}(p^{2})p^{2}-B^{2}(p^{2})}\,.

(3)

The momentum evolution of the functions

A(p^{2})
and
B(p^{2})
is determined from
the SDE that governs the quark propagator, the
gap equation
,

\displaystyle S^{-1}(p)=\not{p}-m-i\Sigma(p)\,,

(4)

and it is shown diagrammatically in
Fig.
3
.
In Eq. (
4
),
\Sigma(p)

stands for the
quark self-energy,

\displaystyle\Sigma(p)=-g^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\nu}S(q)\Gamma^{\mu}(q-p,p,-q)\Delta_{\mu\nu}(q-p)\,,

(5)

where
g
is
the QCD gauge coupling and
C_{f}=4/3
the Casimir eigenvalue of the fundamental SU(3) representation.
In addition,
the integral measure is denoted by

\displaystyle\int_{q}:=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\frac{{\rm d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,,

(6)

where the use of a symmetry-preserving regularization scheme is implicitly assumed.
Moreover,

\Delta_{\mu\nu}
and
\Gamma_{\mu}
represent the full gluon propagator and the fully-dressed quark-gluon vertex, respectively, to be discussed below.
Finally,
m
stands for the
current quark mass;
note that,
throughout this work, we restrict
ourselves to

(chiral limit).

The quark-gluon vertex (see

\Gamma^{b}_{\!\mu}(q,r,-p)=ig\frac{\lambda^{b}}{2}\Gamma_{\!\mu}(q,r,-p)

The part

;

Thus,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu}(q,r,-p)=\sum_{i=1}^{12}\lambda_{i}(q,r,-p)\tau_{i}^{\mu}(q,r,-p)\,,

(8)

where the scalar functions

\lambda_{i}(q,r,-p)
are the
associated form factors or

Note that, in the Landau gauge (
\xi=0
),
the number of basis elements is reduced to eight, and the vertex
is usually referred to as the “transversely projected” vertex, see,

,

and references therein.

In what follows we will make extensive use of the separation
of
\Gamma^{\mu}
into two components,

\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu}
and
\Gamma_{\!2}^{\mu}
,
comprised by the basis elements

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu}(q,r,-p):=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=1}^{6}\lambda_{i}(q,r,-p)\tau_{i}^{\mu}(q,r,-p)\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!2}^{\mu}(q,r,-p):=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=7}^{12}\lambda_{i}(q,r,-p)\tau_{i}^{\mu}(q,r,-p)\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu}(q,r,-p)=\underbrace{\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu}(q,r,-p)}_{{\rm odd\,\#\,of\,\gamma}}\,\,+\,\,\underbrace{\Gamma_{\!2}^{\mu}(q,r,-p)}_{{\rm even\,\#\,of\,\gamma}}\,.

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\mu}=\gamma_{5}(\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu}+\Gamma_{\!2}^{\mu})=(\Gamma_{\!2}^{\mu}-\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu})\gamma_{5}\,.

\displaystyle(c_{2\mu})=

\displaystyle\,c_{b}\!\int_{k}\Gamma_{\beta}^{({\bf{\scriptscriptstyle 1}})}(p-k,k,-p)S(k)\Gamma_{\alpha}^{({\bf{\scriptscriptstyle 1}})}(k-r,r,-k)\Gamma_{\!\mu\rho\sigma}(q,k-p,k-r)

\displaystyle\,\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Delta^{\rho\beta}(k-p)\Delta^{\alpha\sigma}(k-r)\,,

(15)

\Gamma_{\!\mu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle c_{a}:=-ig^{2}\left(C_{f}-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\right)\,,\qquad\qquad c_{b}:=ig^{2}\frac{C_{A}}{2}\,.

(16)

We emphasize once again
that
\Gamma_{\mu}

\Gamma_{\mu}^{({\bf{\scriptscriptstyle 1}})}

\Gamma_{\mu}\neq\Gamma_{\mu}^{({\bf{\scriptscriptstyle 1}})}
,

\Gamma_{\mu}

\Gamma_{\mu}^{({\bf{\scriptscriptstyle 1}})}

V

Returning to the quark gap equation

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle B(p^{2})=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{ig^{2}C_{f}}{4}\int_{q}~b(q^{2})\textrm{Tr}\left[\gamma^{\nu}\Gamma_{\!1}^{\mu}(q-p,p,-q)\right]\Delta_{\mu\nu}(q-p)\,,

\Gamma_{\mu}\to\gamma_{\mu}

III
Axial vertices and their Ward-Takahashi identities

When contracted by
P_{\mu}
,
the axial vertices

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})
and

G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,p_{2},q,-p_{1}-q)
, shown in

Fig.
5
,
satisfy abelian WTIs,
denoted by
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
and
[\rm WTI]_{G_{5}}
,
respectively. While
the former is well-known, see,
e.g.
,
Fujikawa 1980
;
Miransky 1994
, the latter, to the best of our knowledge, has not appeared in the literature.
In this section we discuss these
WTIs, and the constraints they impose on the pole structure of the two axial vertices.

Figure 5:
Diagrammatic representation of the two central vertices treated in this work:
left-panel
: axial-vector vertex;
right-panel
: gluon-axial-vector vertex.

III.1
Axial-vector vertex
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}

Consider the axial-vector vertex

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
(
Fig.
5
),
defined as the amputated part of the correlation function

\langle 0|Tj^{\mu}_{5}(y)\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(x_{2})|0\rangle
, where the axial-vector current is given by

j^{\mu}_{5}(x)=\bar{\psi}(x)\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}{\psi}(x)
.

In momentum space, and in the absence of current quark masses
(
m=0
), the vertex

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})
satisfies the axial WTI
Fujikawa 1980
;
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
;
Miransky 1994
(see
App.
A
for the derivation)

[{\rm WTI}]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}~:\qquad-P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=S^{-1}(p_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}S^{-1}(p_{2})\,.

(21)

In the limit
P\to 0
, in which case
p_{1}=p_{2}:=p
, this identity reveals
a profound connection between the
term
B(p^{2})
, which
emerges when the chiral symmetry is dynamically broken, and the
amplitude that controls the formation of a pion as a quark-antiquark bound state.

In particular, it is well-known that, in the presence of a nonvanishing
B(p^{2})
, the only way to
satisfy
Eq. (
21
) is to allow

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})
to exhibit
a pole at
P^{2}=0
, which is associated
with the corresponding massless Goldstone boson,

i.e.
, the pion.

Specifically, if we substitute
the decomposition of Eq. (
1
)
into Eq. (
21
) and implement the
limit
P\to 0
, the terms proportional to
A(p^{2})
drop out, and we find

\lim_{P\to 0}P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=2B(p^{2})\gamma_{5}\,.

(22)

This observation leads us to conclude that
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
must display a pole in
P^{2}
,
i.e.
,

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=\left.\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}+\left.\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})\right|_{\textrm{reg}}~,

(23)

with

\left.\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}=\frac{P^{\mu}}{P^{2}}\,\chi(P,p_{2},-p_{1})\gamma_{5}~,

(24)

where
\chi(P,p_{2},-p_{1})
is the BSA of the pion. Note that
we suppress the pion decay constant,
f_{\pi}
, which is typically introduced at this point,
see,
e.g.
,
Maris et al. 1998
;
Miransky 1994
.

Figure 6:
Diagrammatic representation of the pole contributions to the two axial vertices under consideration:
left-panel
: pole residue of the axial-vector vertex;
right-panel
: pole residue of the gluon-axial-vector vertex.

Using Eq. (
23
), it is straightforward to show that, in the limit
P\to 0
,

\lim_{P\to 0}P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=\lim_{P\to 0}\left.P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}=\chi(0,p,-p)\gamma_{5}\,.

(25)

a result whose diagrammatic representation is shown in the left-panel of
Fig.
6
. Then, combining
Eqs. (
22
) and (
25
),
we find

\chi(0,p,-p)=2B(p^{2})\,,

(26)

where the
\gamma_{5}
has been canceled
from both sides.

The relation in Eq. (
26
)
may be further inspected, by resorting to the standard tensorial decomposition for the amplitude

\chi(P,p_{2},-p_{1})

Miransky 1994

\chi(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\not{P}+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{3}(\not{p}_{1}+\not{p}_{2})+i\chi_{4}[\not{p}_{1},\not{p}_{2}]~,

(27)

where the
\chi_{i}=\chi_{i}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})
are the associated form factors,
which depend on three Lorentz scalars (
e.g.
,
p_{1}^{2}
,
p^{2}_{2}
and
p_{1}\cdot p_{2}
). In particular, when
P\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\chi(0,p,-p)=\chi_{1}(p^{2})+\chi_{3}(p^{2})\not{p}\,.

(28)

Then, from Eq. (
26
) we obtain
the two basic relations
Maris et al. 1998
;
Fischer and Williams 2008
,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(p^{2})=2B(p^{2})\,,

(29)

and

\displaystyle\chi_{3}(p^{2})=0\,.

(30)

III.2
Gluon-axial-vector vertex
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}

The second main element of our construction is the gluon-axial-vector vertex,
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
(
Fig.
5
). This particular vertex is defined as the
amputated and 1PI part of the
correlation function comprised by
a quark-antiquark
pair, a gluon field, and an axial-vector current
j_{5}^{\mu}
,

App.
A.2
.

In the absence of current quark masses,
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})
satisfies the axial WTI (see
App.
A
for the derivation)

\displaystyle[{\rm WTI}]_{G_{5}}~:\qquad-iP_{\mu}G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\,,

(31)

where
q_{i}:=p_{i}+q
. Note that, since

\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\nu}=0
,
the combination of vertices
appearing on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
31
)
does not contain tree-level contributions,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle-iP_{\mu}G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=\Gamma^{\nu}_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle Q}}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle Q}}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\,,

(32)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\nu}_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle Q}}(q,r,-p):=\Gamma^{\nu}(q,r,-p)-\lambda_{1}(q,r,-p)\gamma^{\nu}\,.

(33)

This observation is consistent with the fact that
the vertex
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
,
appearing on the l.h.s.
of Eq. (
32
),
does not possess a tree-level term.

As in the case of the axial-vector vertex, this WTI imposes strong constraints on
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
. Similarly to the previous case, Eq. (
31
) requires the gluon-axial-vector vertex to have a longitudinally coupled pole. In fact, taking the limit
P\rightarrow 0
of this identity, the terms involving
\Gamma_{\!1}^{\nu}
drop out, and we are left with

\displaystyle\lim_{P\to 0}P_{\mu}G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=

\displaystyle\,i\left[\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2i\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\gamma_{5}\,,

(34)

where

\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)

is the even part of the
quark-gluon vertex

\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)
.
Note that
\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)
corresponds to the quark-gluon vertex shown in
Fig.
4
,
but with

r\leftrightarrow p
.

We emphasize that, crucially,

\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}
is comprised precisely
by the terms referred to as

chiral symmetry breaking
components (see,
e.g.
,
Gao et al. 2021
;
Aguilar et al. 2024
): when solving the SDE
of the quark-gluon vertex, they arise only when
a nontrivial
B(p^{2})
has emerged
from the quark gap equation.
Thus, it is precisely the
part of the quark-gluon vertex
induced by the breaking of chiral symmetry that saturates
the pole residue of

G^{\mu\nu}_{5}
.

To explore this point in some detail,
we note that, in complete analogy to

\Gamma_{5}^{\mu}
,
Eq. (
34
) can be resolved only if
G^{\mu\nu}_{5}
possesses
a pole
in
P^{2}=0
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle G^{\mu\nu}_{5}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=\left.G^{\mu\nu}_{5}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}+\left.G^{\mu\nu}_{5}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\right|_{\textrm{reg}}\,,

(35)

with

\displaystyle\left.G^{\mu\nu}_{5}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}=2i\,\frac{P^{\mu}}{P^{2}}\,\mathcal{W}^{\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}\,.

(36)

At the formal level,
the component

\mathcal{W}^{\nu}
plays a rôle analogous to that of the amplitude
\chi
in Eq. (
24
), acting as a momentum-dependent residue.
From the physical point of view,

\mathcal{W}^{\nu}

encapsulates key pieces of the pion dynamics, contained
in the SDE of the
axial-vector vertex.
In that sense, it may be viewed as a
concise representation
of various contributions
that participate in the momentum evolution of the pion amplitude

\chi
.

Using the decomposition of Eq. (
35
)
in conjunction with Eq. (
36
), it is straightforward to show that (
Fig.
6
, right-panel)

\displaystyle\lim_{P\to 0}P_{\mu}G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{P\to 0}P_{\mu}\left.G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\right|_{\textrm{pole}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2i\,\mathcal{W}^{\nu}(0,q,p,-p-q)\gamma_{5}=2i\,\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\gamma_{5}\,,

(37)

where, once again,
\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)

is the even part of the
quark-gluon vertex

\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)
.

It is clear that
\mathcal{W}^{\nu}(0,q,p,-p-q)

admits the same tensorial decomposition as the quark-gluon vertex
\Gamma^{\nu}(q,r,-r-q)
in Eq. (
8
), with the change
r\leftrightarrow p
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\nu}(0,q,p,-p-q)=\sum_{i=1}^{12}w_{i}(q,p,-p-q)\tau_{i}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\,,

(38)

where the
w_{i}(q,p,-p-q)

are the corresponding
form factors (dressings).
Evidently,
as in Eq. (
10
),
a separation
into odd and even structures
may be carried out
(suppressing momenta)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\nu}=\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\nu}+\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\nu}\,,

(39)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}w_{i}\tau_{i}^{\nu}\,,\qquad\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\nu}=\sum_{i=7}^{12}w_{i}\tau_{i}^{\nu}\,.

(40)

The substitution of the above
tensor decomposition into Eq. (
37
)
leads directly to the relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\nu}=\Gamma_{\!2}^{\nu}(q,p,-p-q)\,,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\nu}=0,

(41)

or, equivalently, at the
level of the form factors

\displaystyle w_{i}(q,p,-p-q)=\lambda_{i}(q,p,-p-q)\,,\qquad i=7,...,12\,,

(42)

and

\displaystyle w_{i}(q,p,-p-q)=0\,,\qquad i=1,...,6\,;

(43)

which represent the direct analogues of Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
), respectively.

IV
The axial WTI from the vertex SDE: exact derivation

Figure 7:
Diagrammatic representation of the SDE satisfied by the axial-vector vertex
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
,
formulated from the point of view of the antiquark leg.

In this section we demonstrate that
the exact dynamical equation (SDE)
that governs the evolution of
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}

satisfies precisely the

[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
of Eq. (
21
).

To that end, instead of considering the
SDE satisfied by

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
from the perspective of the axial-vector current (leg with momentum
P
),
we adopt the view
of the antiquark leg, carrying
momentum
p_{1}
;
the resulting SDE is shown in
Fig.
7
.
According to the standard rules,
the vertex connecting the
special leg to the
rest of the diagram
must be kept at tree-level
(
\gamma^{\sigma}
in
Fig.
7
).
This particular way of writing the
SDE,
i.e.
, using an antifermion as the reference leg
has been used in the studies of the
ghost-gluon
Fischer 2006
;
Aguilar et al. 2013
;
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2025

and quark-gluon
vertices
Binosi et al. 2016
.

Thus, the resulting SDE reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}+ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)\,,

(44)

where we have defined
q_{1}:=p_{1}+q
.
The kernel
\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}

is comprised by four incoming
operators, a quark (
\psi
), an antiquark (
\bar{\psi}
), a gluon (
A^{\nu}
), and a axial-vector current (
j_{5}^{\mu}
),
so that

\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}:=\mathcal{K}_{j_{5}^{\mu}\!A^{\nu}\!\psi\bar{\psi}}
. This kernel may be decomposed as shown in
Fig.
8
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})=-\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,q_{2},-q_{1})S(q_{2})\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})+iG_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\,,

(45)

with
q_{2}:=p_{2}+q
, and

G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
is the
gluon-axial-vector vertex
introduced in Sec.
III.2
.

As a result,
the SDE of
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}=\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}+(a^{\mu}_{5})+(b^{\mu}_{5})\,,

(46)

where

\displaystyle(a_{5}^{\mu})=

\displaystyle\,-ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,q_{2},-q_{1})S(q_{2})\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)~,

(47)

\displaystyle(b^{\mu}_{5})=

\displaystyle\,-g^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})G_{5}^{\mu\nu}(P,q,p_{2},-q_{1})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)\,.

(48)

Note that the
lowest-order diagram contributing to

\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}
is
enclosed in the blue-dashed box of

(a_{5}^{\mu})
in
Fig.
8
.
A “cut” through the quark propagator with momentum
p_{2}+q
shows that
this diagram is clearly one-particle reducible (1PR); of course,
the full diagram,

(a_{5}^{\mu})
, where the kernel is embedded, is one-particle irreducible (1PI).
All remaining graphs contained in

\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}
are 1PI,
and coincide exactly with the graphs
forming the vertex
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
, which is enclosed entirely within the
blue-dashed box
in graph
(b_{5}^{\mu})
.

Given that the graphs

(a^{\mu}_{5})
and
(b^{\mu}_{5})
account for the full
sum of all
quantum corrections, if we contract
both sides of Eq. (
46
) by
P_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,p_{2},-p_{1})=-(/\penalty\hskip-6.25963pt{p_{1}}\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}/\penalty\hskip-6.25963pt{p_{2}})+P_{\mu}\left[(a_{5}^{\mu})+(b_{5}^{\mu})\right]\,,

(49)

the r.h.s. of the
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
in Eq. (
21
) must emerge exactly.

Figure 8:

Diagrammatic representation of the
quantum part of the SDE in
Fig.
7
.
The decomposition of
\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}
given in Eq. (
45
) is
delimited by the two blue-dashed boxes.

Let us focus on the contraction of graph
(a_{5}^{\mu})
,
which is the only loop contribution
typically considered within the RL approximation. Using the r.h.s of
Eq. (
21
) for
the term
P_{\mu}\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,q_{2},-q_{1})
under the integral sign,
we find

\displaystyle P_{\mu}(a_{5}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\left[S^{-1}(q_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}S^{-1}(q_{2})\right]S(q_{2})\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\gamma_{5}\underbrace{\left[-ig^{2}C_{f}\gamma_{5}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{2})\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)\right]}_{i\Sigma(p_{2})}

\displaystyle\,+\underbrace{ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)}_{V(p_{1},p_{2}):\,{\textrm{symmetry violating term}}}\,.

(50)

The result in Eq. (
50
)
prompts the following observations:

(
i
)

When
the term
i\Sigma(p_{2})

of Eq. (
50
)
is added to the term

-\gamma_{5}/\penalty\hskip-6.25963pt{p_{2}}
of Eq. (
49
), and after use of the gap equation in Eq. (
4
), one recovers precisely the term
-\gamma_{5}S^{-1}(p_{2})

in
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
. However, the second term,
V(p_{1},p_{2})
,
does not admit an interpretation as a genuine quark self-energy, leading to the obvious violation of the fundamental
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
.

(
ii
)

Within the RL approximation,
where
\Gamma_{\nu}(q,r,-p)\to\gamma_{\nu}
, the
symmetry violating term
V(p_{1},p_{2})

reduces to the required self-energy,
since

\displaystyle V(p_{1},p_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm RL}}{{\longrightarrow}}ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\nu}\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)=i\Sigma_{\rm{{\scriptscriptstyle R}{\scriptscriptstyle L}}}(p_{1})\gamma_{5}\,.

(51)

Thus, diagram
(a_{5}^{\mu})
reproduces the
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
in the RL approximation, but fails to do so
when the full kinematic structure of the
quark-gluon vertex is maintained.

(
iii
)

One may recast the
term
V(p_{1},p_{2})
as the difference between the required quark self-energy,

i\Sigma(p_{1})
,
and a symmetry violating remainder, to be denoted by

{\cal V}(p_{1},p_{2})
,
through the addition and subtraction of the term

\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}
,

i.e.
,

\displaystyle V(p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle\,\underbrace{\left[-ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)\right]}_{i\Sigma(p_{1})}\gamma_{5}

(52)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\underbrace{ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\left[\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\right]\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)}_{\mathcal{V}(p_{1},p_{2}):\,\textrm{symmetry violating remainder}}\,.

(53)

Thus, one finally has that

\displaystyle P_{\mu}(a_{5}^{\mu})=i\left[\Sigma(p_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Sigma(p_{2})\right]+\mathcal{V}(p_{1},p_{2})\,,

(54)

with the symmetry violating
remainder
\mathcal{V}(p_{1},p_{2})

given in Eq. (
53
). Clearly, in the
RL approximation,

\mathcal{V}(p_{1},p_{2})\sim\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\nu}=0

It is now apparent that

must provide the

It turns out that

namely the

in Eq. (

[\rm WTI]_{G_{5}}
and the expression for

given in Eq. (

obtain directly

\displaystyle P_{\mu}(b_{5}^{\mu})=

\displaystyle\,-ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\left[\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{1},-q_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}(q,p_{2},-q_{2})\right]\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)

\displaystyle\,-\mathcal{V}(p_{1},p_{2})\,.

Therefore, after the inclusion
of the vertex
G^{\mu\nu}_{5}

the key
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
is exactly fulfilled at the level of the SDE for
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
,

-P_{\mu}\left[\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}+(a^{\mu}_{5})+(b^{\mu}_{5})\right]=S^{-1}(p_{1})\gamma_{5}+\gamma_{5}S^{-1}(p_{2})\,.

V
Dynamical realization of the chiral limit

As explained in
Sec.
III
,
the pivotal relations in
Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
)
are a direct consequence of the
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}
.
Given that, as shown in detail in
Sec.
IV
, the SDE that governs the vertex

\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
satisfies
the
[\rm WTI]_{\Gamma_{\!5}}

exactly
,
it is natural to expect that there will be a dynamical
realization of Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
),
recovered by taking the
P\to 0

limit of
diagrams
(a_{5}^{\mu})
and
(b_{5}^{\mu})
in
Fig.
8
.
This particular demonstration is nontrivial, and furnishes additional valuable insights
into this subject;
the aim of this section is to show in detail how this important result emerges both within the RL approximation and in the general case.

V.1
Chiral limit in the RL approximation

In order to fix the ideas, it is
instructive to revert to the
simpler case of the RL approximation, which involves
only diagram
(a_{5}^{\mu})
,
with
\Gamma_{\nu}(q,r,-p)\to\gamma_{\nu}
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}=\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}+(a^{\mu}_{5})_{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle R}{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\,,

\displaystyle(a_{5}^{\mu})_{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle R}{\scriptscriptstyle L}}=-ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}S(q_{1})\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}(P,q_{2},-q_{1})S(q_{2})\gamma^{\nu}\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q)\,.

When we contract both sides of

the corresponding pole residues are isolated, while the tree-level term is annihilated. In particular,

(59)

where we have shifted the integration variable

Using the basic property

\gamma_{5}S(q)=S(-q)\gamma_{5}

\displaystyle S(q)S(-q)=-c(q^{2})\,,

(60)

with the function
c(q^{2})

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(p^{2})+\chi_{3}(p^{2})\not{p}=-ig^{2}C_{f}\int_{q}\gamma^{\sigma}c(q^{2})\left[\chi_{1}(q^{2})+\chi_{3}(q^{2})\not{q}\right]\gamma^{\nu}\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q-p)\,,

(61)

where the
\gamma_{5}

has been canceled from both sides.

It is now elementary to isolate two
separate equations from Eq. (
61

by taking appropriate Dirac traces,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(p^{2})=-\frac{ig^{2}C_{f}}{4}\int_{q}c(q^{2})\chi_{1}(q^{2})\textrm{Tr}\left[\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\right]\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q-p)\,,

(62)

\displaystyle p^{2}\chi_{3}(p^{2})=\frac{ig^{2}C_{f}}{4}\int_{q}c(q^{2})\chi_{3}(q^{2})\textrm{Tr}\left[\not{p}\gamma^{\sigma}\not{q}\gamma^{\nu}\right]\Delta_{\nu\sigma}(q-p)\,.

(63)

admits the trivial solution

\chi_{3}(p^{2})=0

30
).
In addition, substituting
Eq. (
29
)
into Eq. (
62

and using that

3

in the

the second relation in
Eq. (
20

Thus, the dynamical equations
(
62
) and (
63
),
derived from the SDE of the axial-vector vertex, are fully consistent with
the symmetry relations
(
29
)
and (
30
).

VIII
Discussion and conclusions

In the present work we have developed a novel approach
that aims at a self-consistent treatment of the functional equations associated with
the physics of mesons.

The cornerstone of this formalism is the exact SDE of the axial-vector vertex
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
, formulated
from the viewpoint of the antiquark leg.
This alternative
re-organization
of the quantum corrections
is described in terms
of a new vertex,
denominated gluon-axial-vector vertex,
and denoted by

G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
.
This vertex is nested inside
the SDE of
\Gamma_{\!5}^{\mu}
, and
satisfies its own
WTI, which relates it
to the quark-gluon vertex.
As a result, the
WTI of the axial-vector vertex is enforced directly at the level of
the SDE. In particular,
the gap equation governing
the quark propagator appearing on the r.h.s. of this WTI involves the full
quark-gluon vertex.

Information on the
BSE of the pion is obtained by taking
the limit
P\to 0
of the above SDE,
where
P
is the momentum of the axial-vector current.
In particular, as it is well-known, when the chiral symmetry is dynamically
broken, the axial-vector vertex possesses a
(longitudinally coupled)
pole in
P^{2}
, whose
residue is identified with the BSA of the pion.
By virtue of
the aforementioned results,
the known relation between the quark mass function,
obtained from the gap equation with a full
\Gamma_{\mu}
, and the amplitude of the pion,
is precisely fulfilled.

The developed framework
offers a rather tight control
on the interlocked equations describing
symmetry and dynamics. As a result, it facilitates the
design and
implementation of simpler calculational methods.
In particular,
a concrete
truncation scheme
for the SDE-BSE system has
been identified in

Sec.
VI
, referred
to as “one-loop dressed”
approximation; it incorporates a completely nontrivial quark-gluon vertex
\Gamma_{\mu}
, which is
fully compatible with chiral symmetry, as expressed through the axial WTI.

It is worth pointing out
that, throughout all algebraic manipulations of our analysis,
the gluon propagator

\Delta_{\mu\nu}

remained completely
inert. Therefore,
the formal aspects of this
work persist
for any value of the
gauge-fixing parameter

\xi
, or even within gauge-fixing schemes other than the covariant gauges.
Even though, for practical purposes, the Landau gauge
is undoubtedly the standard
choice, the validity of our
considerations for arbitrary gauges is a welcome feature.

It is important to emphasize that our approach has been developed strictly in the
limit of vanishing current quark masses. Its
extension to arbitrary
current masses is expected to proceed
in a similar fashion, once
the modifications to
the key WTIs have been appropriately taken into account.
In particular,
in the presence of current masses,
the WTIs contain
additional terms involving the axial vertex

\Gamma_{5}
, see,
e.g.
, Eq. (11.217) in
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
, or
Eq. (14) in
Maris et al. 1998
.
The vertex
\Gamma_{5}
is
associated with the
axial current
j_{5}=i\bar{\psi}\gamma_{5}\psi
, which
satisfies the relation
\partial_{\mu}j_{5}^{\mu}=-2mj_{5}
.
We hope to present the
completion of this task
in the near future.

The practical implementation
of the one-loop-dressed
approximation mentioned above
may not be easy to carry out,
due to the presence of the
vertex
G_{5}^{\mu\nu}
inside some of the relevant diagrams, see
Fig.
11
.
It turns out, however, that
a further simplification is possible: one may omit the aforementioned diagrams,
keeping only those
in
Fig.
10
,
provided that the SDE of the quark-gluon vertex,
Fig.
4
, is
treated in the so-called “symmetric” limit.
This treatment amounts to
replacing the full
momentum-dependence of the
form factors
in the quark-gluon SDE
by the choice
q^{2}=r^{2}=p^{2}
,
see,
e.g.
,
Gao et al. 2021
.
This additional simplification is expected to lead to a more tractable version of the pion BSE, apt for numerical exploration. We expect to report progress
in this direction in a forthcoming communication.

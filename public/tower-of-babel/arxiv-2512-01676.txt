Feedback vertex sets of digraphs with bounded maximum degree
Jiangdong Ai Gregory Gutin Xiangzhou Liu Anders Yeo Yacong Zhou

Abstract
A digraph D D is an oriented graph if D D does not have a pair of opposite arcs. The degree of a vertex v v of D D is the sum of the in-degree and out-degree of v . v. Let fvs ⁡ ( D ) {\rm fvs}(D) be the minimum number of vertices whose deletion from D D makes it acyclic. Let D D be a digraph with n n vertices and maximum degree Δ \Delta . We prove the following bounds. If D D is an oriented graph, then fvs ⁡ ( D ) ≤ 3 ​ n 7 {\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{3n}{7} when Δ ≤ 4 \Delta\leq 4 and fvs ⁡ ( D ) ≤ n 2 {\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2} when Δ ≤ 5 \Delta\leq 5 . If D D is a connected digraph, Δ ≤ 4 \Delta\leq 4 and D D is not obtained from an odd undirected cycle by replacing every edge with the pair of opposite arcs with the same endvertices, then fvs ⁡ ( D ) ≤ n 2 {\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2} . If D D is an arbitrary digraph with Δ ≤ 5 \Delta\leq 5 then fvs ⁡ ( D ) ≤ 2 ​ n 3 . {\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{2n}{3}. Note that all the above bounds are tight.

Introduction

In this paper, the standard terminology and notation in digraph theory are used, following
[
3
]
.
Some important terminology and notation are given at the end of this section.

A set
F\subseteq V(D)
of a digraph
D
, is a
feedback vertex set
if
D-F
is acyclic. Similarly, one defines a
feedback arc set
.
Deciding whether a graph has a feedback vertex set of a given size is among the 21 original NP-complete problems of Karp
[
17
]
. Cavallaro and Fluschnik
[
7
]
proved that the problem remains NP-hard even on subclasses of Hamiltonian graphs. Thus, finding the minimum size of a feedback vertex set of a digraph
D
, denoted by
{\rm fvs}(D)
is a challenging algorithmic problem and was extensively studied in the literature.

In particular, some scholars studied the upper bounds on
{\rm fvs}(D)
for arbitrary digraphs
D
. Akbari, Ghodrati, Jabalameli and Saghafian
[
1
]
obtained a lower bound on
|V(D)|-{\rm fvs}(D)
inspired by the well-known Caro-Wei lower bound for the maximum size of an independent set in an undirected graph. This bound was improved in two different ways by Asgarli, Falkenhagen and Hoshi
[
2
]
.

To obtain tight or close-to-tight lower bounds on
{\rm fvs}(D)
, many researchers studied such bounds for special classes of
oriented graphs
(or,
orgraphs
), i.e., digraphs without opposite arcs. We will first discuss new results obtained in this paper and then we will overview numerous results obtained by other researchers.

Several scholars carried out research on tight upper bounds for the minimum size of a feedback arc set in orgraphs with small maximum degree, see, e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
14
,
13
,
12
]
. In particular, the authors of
[
12
]
obtained tight upper bounds when the maximum degree
\Delta\leq 4
and when
\Delta\leq 5.
The authors of
[
12
]
proved a conjecture in
[
14
]
and disproved another one in
[
14
]
, both for
\Delta\leq 5.

In this paper, similarly, we study tight upper bounds for the minimum size of a feedback vertex set in orgraphs with small bounded maximum degree
\Delta
. Clearly, the case of
\Delta\leq 2
is trivial. In the case of
\Delta\leq 3
, the tight bound
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{3}
for oriented graphs was proved in
[
14
]
, and for digraphs the bound
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
follows by induction: delete both vertices of a 2-cycle and use the induction hypothesis, but use only one vertex of the 2-cycle in feedback vertex set (due to possible existence of 2-cycles the bound is tight).
We establish new tight upper bounds on the minimum feedback vertex set size in directed and oriented graphs with
\Delta\leq 4
and
\Delta\leq 5
. Specifically, we prove for
\Delta\leq 4
that any orgraph
D
on
n
vertices satisfies
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{3n}{7}
, and if
D
is a connected digraph not obtained from an odd undirected cycle by replacing every edge with the pair of opposite arcs with the same endvertices, then
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
. For
\Delta\leq 5
, we show
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
for an orgraph
D
, and
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{2n}{3}
for a digraph
D
. Moreover, we provide explicit constructions that demonstrate that all of these bounds are tight.

Tournaments form a well-known class of orgraphs. Already Stearns
[
27
]
and Erdős and Moser
[
9
]
have shown that for any tournament
T
on
n
vertices, we have
{\rm fvs}(T)\leq n-\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor-1
, while there are tournaments where there is no feedback vertex set with less than
n-2\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor-1
vertices. More precise bounds for small values of
n
have been obtained by Sanchez-Flores
[
25
,
26
]
and recently more work has been done in that direction by Neiman, Mackey and Heule
[
23
]
and by Lidický and Pfender
[
21
]
. Improving the asymptotic upper and lower bounds remains an open problem.

Another class that has received a lot of attention is planar orgraphs.
Let
D_{g}^{n}
denote a planar digraph with
n
vertices and directed girth
g
. Harutyunyan
[
16
,
15
]
conjectured that
{\rm fvs}(D_{3}^{n})\leq\frac{2n}{5}
. This conjecture was refuted by Knauer, Valicov and Wenger
[
18
]
, who showed that
{\rm fvs}(D_{g}^{n})\geq\frac{n-1}{g-1}
for all
g\geq 3
. On the other hand, Golowich and Rolnick
[
11
]
established the upper bounds
{\rm fvs}(D_{4}^{n})\leq\frac{7n}{12}
,
{\rm fvs}(D_{5}^{n})\leq\frac{8n}{15}
, and
{\rm fvs}(D_{g}^{n})\leq\frac{3n-6}{g}

for all
g\geq 6
. Harutyunyan and Mohar
[
15
]
proved
that the vertex set of every planar orgraph of girth at least 5 can be partitioned into two acyclic subgraphs. This result was extended to planar orgraphs of girth 4 by Li and Mohar
[
20
]
, and therefore
{\rm fvs}(D_{4}^{n})\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Further improvements were obtained by Esperet, Lemoine, and Maffray
[
10
]
, who showed
{\rm fvs}(D_{4}^{n})\leq\tfrac{5n-5}{9}
,
{\rm fvs}(D_{5}^{n})\leq\tfrac{2n-5}{4}
, and
{\rm fvs}(D_{g}^{n})\leq\tfrac{2n-6}{g}
if
g\geq 6
. These improved results of Golowich and Rolnick. However, a question of Albertson
[
28
,
22
]
asking whether any planar orgraph has a feedback vertex set on at most half its vertices, remains open. Note that this question is a weakening of the undirected setting as well as of the famous Neumann-Lara conjecture
[
24
]
. Further, it is known that if true this bound is best-possible
[
18
]
. Moreover, note that the best known upper bound coincides with the above mentioned
\frac{3}{5}n
from the undirected setting
[
6
]
.

Knauer, La and Valicov
[
19
]
studied the minimum size of a feedback vertex set in oriented and undirected
n
-vertex graphs of given degeneracy or treewidth (for orgraphs, both parameters are undirected, i.e. they are of the underlying graphs of orgraphs). Since in this paper we only study digraphs, we will discuss only the results in
[
19
]
obtained for orgraphs. For an orgraph
D
of degeneracy
k\geq 2
, Knauer, La and Valicov proved that
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{k-1}{k+1}n
and that this inequality is strict when
k
is odd.
To compare our new results with those in
[
19
]
, note that orgraphs with the maximum degree
\Delta
are of degeneracy
\Delta
. For
\Delta\leq 4,
while our bound is
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{3n}{7}
, the bound of
[
19
]
is
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{3n}{5}.
For
\Delta\leq 5,
while our bound is
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
, the bound of
[
19
]
is
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{2n}{3}.
Thus, our results for orgraphs with
\Delta\leq 4
and
\Delta\leq 5

do not follow from the relevant results in
[
19
]
.
For an orgraph
D
of treewidth
k\geq 2
, Knauer, La and Valicov proved that
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{k}{k+3}n
. It seems that this bound is unlikely to be tight.

Some Terminology and Notation

Let
D
be a digraph and let
v\in V(D)
. The out-degree
(in-degree, respectively) is denoted
d^{+}_{D}(v)
(
d^{-}_{D}(v)
, respectively). Recall that the
degree
of
v
is
d_{D}(v)=d^{+}_{D}(v)+d^{-}_{D}(v)
. The maximum degree
\Delta(D)
of
D
is defined as
\Delta(D)=\max_{v\in V(D)}d_{D}(v)
. A digraph
D
is
k
-regular
if
d^{+}_{D}(v)=d^{-}_{D}(v)=k
for every vertex
v\in V(D)
.

The
order
of a directed or undirected graph
H
is the number of vertices in
H
. In a digraph, a
cycle
(
path
, respectively)
is a directed cycle (directed path, respectively). A
k
-cycle
is a
cycle with
k
vertices. The
directed girth
of a digraph
D
is the minimum
k
for which
D
has a
k
-cycle. In particular, an orgraph
D
is of directed girth at least 3.

The
underlying graph
of a digraph
D
, is
the undirected graph
UG(D)
with the same vertex set as
D
and such that
a pair
u,v
of distinct vertices are adjacent in
UG(D)
if there is an arc
between
u
and
v
in
D.
A component of
UG(D)
is a
component
of

D
and
D
is
connected
if
UG(D)
is connected.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be arbitrary. Then the following statements hold:

(a):

{\rm fvs}(H)=\frac{n+m+1}{7}

(b):

Let

(c):

If

\sum_{x\in V(H)}(4-d(x))\geq 6
.

(d):

If

\sum_{x\in V(H)}(4-d(x))=4

. We prove (a) by induction on
|V(H)|
. Observe first that
{\rm fvs}(H_{1})=1=\frac{|V(H_{1})|+|A(H_{1})|+1}{7}
. As

forms a directed 3-cycle in

and

since

and
H_{3}
still contain a directed cycle after the removal of any single vertex of

, whereas the induced subgraph

is acyclic for

. This settles the base cases of (a). Let

and assume that

is obtained by adding an arc between

and
H^{\prime\prime}
, where

. By the induction hypothesis, we have that

{\rm fvs}(H^{\prime})=\frac{|V(H^{\prime})|+|A(H^{\prime})|+1}{7}
and
{\rm fvs}(H^{\prime\prime})=\frac{|V(H^{\prime\prime})|+|A(H^{\prime\prime})|+1}{7}.

Since the union of minimum feedback vertex sets of
H^{\prime}
and

is clearly a minimum feedback vertex set of

and

, we obtain

{\rm fvs}(H)=\frac{|V(H^{\prime})|+|A(H^{\prime})|+|V(H^{\prime\prime})|+|A(H^{\prime\prime})|+2}{7}=\frac{|V(H)|+|A(H)|+1}{7},

which proves (a).

By the definition of
\mathcal{H}
, every strong component of

is isomorphic to
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
, or
H_{3}
. Since

, to prove (b) it suffices to show that every vertex of

Observe that

\{x,b\},\qquad\{z,a\},\qquad\{y,a\}

are minimum feedback vertex sets of

and

. Hence (b) follows, as each vertex of

lies in at least one of these sets.

(d) is straightforward to verify. It remains to prove (c). Note that

Hence we may assume that

has at least two strong components. Let

be the two end strong components, i.e., those adjacent to exactly one other strong component. Using (d), the identity

|V(H^{\prime})|=6
and the distance in
G_{D}
between any two vertices in
H^{\prime}
is at most
3
.
Now a shortest path between
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
together with the arcs
t_{1}z_{1}
and
t_{2}z_{2}
(
t_{1}=t_{2}
) form a
3
-cycle or a
4
-cycle or a
5
-cycle in
G_{D}
, contradicting
Claim H or Claim N or Claim O.
This completes the proof of Claim P and therefore also of the theorem.

\Box

2.2
Digraphs

Let
\mathcal{C}_{o}
denote the class of digraphs obtained from an undirected odd cycle by replacing each edge with a directed 2-cycle. For a feedback arc set
F
, let
c(F)
denote the number of odd cycles contained in
D[F]
. We have the following:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
D
be a connected digraph of order
n
with
\Delta(D)\leq 4
. Then
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
unless
D\in\mathcal{C}_{o}
.

Proof
. Let
D\notin\mathcal{C}_{o}
and
F\subseteq A(D)
be a minimum feedback arc set of
D
.

Claim 1
.

\Delta(D[F])\leq 2
and if
d_{D[F]}(v)=2
, then
d^{+}_{D}(v)=d^{-}_{D}(v)=2
.

Proof
. We first show that
\Delta(D[F])\leq 2
. Assume for contradiction that there exists a
vertex
v
in
D[F]
with total degree
d_{D[F]}(v)=d_{D[F]}^{-}(v)+d_{D[F]}^{+}(v)\geq 3
. Since
\Delta(D)\leq 4
,

\min\{d^{+}(v),d^{-}(v)\}\leq 2<3
. Without loss of generality, assume that
d^{-}(v)\leq d^{+}(v)
. Construct an arc set
F^{\prime}
from
F
by replacing the out-arcs of
v
with in-arcs of
v
. Since every cycle containing
v
must include at least one out-arc of
v
,
F^{\prime}
is a feedback arc set of
D
. Since
d^{-}(v)\leq 2<3
and
d_{D[F]}(v)\geq 3
, we have
|F^{\prime}|<|F|
, contradicting the minimality of
F
.

Now we prove the second part of the claim. As
\Delta(D)\leq 4
, it suffices to show
\min\{d^{+}(v),d^{-}(v)\}>1
. Suppose for contradiction that there exists a
vertex
v
in
D[F]
with
\min\{d^{+}(v),d^{-}(v)\}\leq 1
. Without loss of generality, assume that
d^{-}(v)\leq d^{+}(v)
.
Construct an arc set
F^{\prime\prime}
from
F
by replacing the out-arcs of
v
with in-arcs of
v
. Since every cycle containing
v
must include at least one out-arc of
v
,
F^{\prime\prime}
is a feedback arc set of
D
. Since
d^{-}(v)\leq 1<2
and
d_{D[F]}(v)=2
, we have
|F^{\prime\prime}|<|F|
, a contradiction to the minimality of
F
.

\Box

Let
F^{*}\subseteq A(D)
be a minimum feedback arc set of
D
with minimum
c(F^{*})
.

Claim 2
.

D[F^{*}]
is bipartite.

Proof
. Suppose that there exists an odd cycle
C=x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2k+1}
. Clearly, there exists a vertex
x_{i}
such that
x_{i-1}x_{i},x_{i}x_{i+1}\in A(D[F^{*}])
. By Claim
1
, there is a vertex
x_{i}^{-}\neq x_{i-1}
such that
x_{i}^{-}x_{i}\in A(D)
.
Now replace
{x_{i}x_{i+1}}
with
{x_{i}^{-}x_{i}}
to obtain another minimum feedback arc set, say
F^{\prime}
. Note that if
x_{i}^{-}\in V(D\setminus C)
, then
c(F^{\prime})<c(F)
, it is a contradiction. So, we may assume
x_{i^{-}}\in V(C)
.
If
x_{i}^{-}\in V(C)\setminus\{x_{i},x_{i-1},x_{i+1}\}
, then
d_{D[F^{\prime}]}(x_{i}^{-})=3
, contradicting
\Delta(D[F^{\prime}])\leq 2
.
Thus,
x_{i}^{-}=x_{i+1}
and
x_{i+1}x_{i}\in A(D)
. By symmetry,
x_{i}x_{i-1}\in A(D)
. Hence,
x_{i}
is incident to two 2-cycles in
D
. If
x_{i+2}x_{i+1}\in A(C)
, then
F_{1}=(F^{*}\setminus\{x_{i}x_{i+1}\})\cup\{x_{i+1}x_{i}\}
is a minimum feedback arc set of
D
with
c(F_{1})=c(F^{*})
. Similarly, since
x_{i+2}x_{i+1},x_{i+1}x_{i}\in A(D[F_{1}])
,
x_{i+1}
is incident to two 2-cycles in
D
. If
x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\in A(D)
, then
x_{i}x_{i+1},x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\in A(D)
, by the same argument we have
x_{i+1}
is incident to two 2-cycles in
D
. And we keep doing like above, so for all
x_{i}\in V(C)
,
i\in[2k+1]
,
x_{i}
is incident to two 2-cycles in
D
. Hence,
D\in\mathcal{C}_{o}
, a contradiction.

\Box

Since
D[F^{*}]
is bipartite, one of the parts, say
X
, contains at most half of the vertices. So,
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{|V(D)|}{2}
.

\Box

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be a connected orgraph with
\Delta(D)\leq 5
. Then
D
has a feedback arc set
F
such that
D[F]
is bipartite.

Proof
. Let
F\subseteq A(D)
be a minimum feedback arc set of
D
.

Claim 3
.

\Delta(D[F])\leq 2
.

Proof
. Assume for contradiction that there exists a
vertex
v
in
D[F]
with total degree
d_{D[F]}(v)=d_{D[F]}^{-}(v)+d_{D[F]}^{+}(v)\geq 3
. Since
\Delta(D)\leq 5
,

\min\{d^{+}(v),d^{-}(v)\}\leq 2<3
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
d^{-}(v)\leq d^{+}(v)
. Construct an arc set
F^{\prime}
from
F
by replacing the out-arcs of
v
with in-arcs of
v
. Since every cycle containing
v
must include at least one out-arc of
v
,
F^{\prime}
is a feedback arc set of
D
. Since
d^{-}(v)\leq 2<3
and
d_{D[F]}(v)\geq 3
, we have
|F^{\prime}|<|F|
, contradicting the minimality of
F
.

\Box

If
D[F]
contains no odd cycle, we are done. Thus, assume there exists an odd cycle
C
. Clearly, there exists a vertex
v\in V(D[F])
with
d_{D[F]}^{-}(v)=d_{D[F]}^{+}(v)=1
. Replace the two incident arcs with in-arcs of
v
if
d^{-}(v)\leq d^{+}(v)
, otherwise with out-arcs of
v
.

We claim that this replacement creates no new odd cycles, and the resulting set is still a minimum feedback arc set. Without loss of generality, suppose we replace
{vu}
with
{u^{\prime}v}
. Clearly, the resulting arc set, denoted by
F^{\prime}
, remains a minimum feedback arc set. Thus, we only need to prove that this replacement does not create new odd cycles. Suppose, for contradiction, that the replacement creates a new odd cycle
C^{\prime}\subseteq D[F^{\prime}]
, then
u^{\prime}v\in A(C^{\prime})
. Since
F^{\prime}
is still a minimum feedback arc set, Claim
3
implies that
\Delta(D[F^{\prime}])\leq 2
. Thus,
C^{\prime}
must contain the vertex
u
. However,
d_{D[F^{\prime}]}(u)=1
, a contradiction.

Repeat this replacement until the resulting set induces no odd cycles. Let the final set be denoted by
F^{\prime\prime}
, then
F^{\prime\prime}
is a feedback arc set such that
D[F^{\prime\prime}]
is bipartite.

\Box

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
D
be an orgraph of order
n
with
\Delta(D)\leq 5
. Then
{\rm fvs}(D)\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof
. We may assume without loss of generality that
D
is connected. By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a feedback arc set
F
such that
D[F]
is bipartite. Since
D[F]
is bipartite, one of the parts, say
X
, contains at most half of the vertices. It is enough to show that
X
is a feedback vertex set of
D
. Observe that
D\setminus X\subseteq D\setminus F
, since all arcs in
F
have at least one endpoint in
X
.
D\setminus F
is acyclic by definition of
F
. Then
D\setminus X
is acyclic. Therefore,
X
is a feedback vertex set.

4
Conclusions

For any integer
k\geq 2
, let
f(k)
denote the supremum of
{\rm fvs}(D)/|V(D)|
taken over all orgraphs
D
with maximum degree at most
k
. In this paper we proved that
f(4)=\tfrac{3}{7}
and
f(5)=\tfrac{1}{2}
. In general, it would be interesting to determine the value of
f(k)
for larger
k
, or even to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the function
f
. We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.1
.

There exists a constant
\theta>0
such that for all
k\geq 3
,

f(k)\leq 1-\theta\cdot\frac{\log_{2}k}{k}.

Recall that for every integer
n
there exists a tournament
T
on
n
vertices whose largest acyclic subtournament has size at most
2\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor+1
. Consequently,

\frac{{\rm fvs}(T)}{|V(T)|}\geq 1-\frac{2\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor+1}{n}.

Note that
k=n-1
and
1-\frac{2\lfloor\log_{2}(k+1)\rfloor+1}{k+1}\geq 1-\frac{2\log_{2}k}{k}
for
k
large enough.
Thus, if true, the upper bound in the conjecture would be the best possible.

We also have the following conjecture for
k=6
.

Conjecture 4.2
.

f(6)=\frac{4}{7}
.

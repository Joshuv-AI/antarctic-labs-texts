Hausdorff measure of dominated planar self-affine sets with large dimension
Balázs Bárány

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Hausdorff measure of planar dominated self-affine sets at its affinity dimension. We show that the Hausdorff measure being positive and finite is equivalent to the Käenmäki measure being a mass distribution. Moreover, under the open bounded neighbourhood condition, we will show that the positivity of the Hausdorff measure is equivalent to the projection of the Käenmäki measure in every Furstenberg direction being absolutely continuous with bounded density. This also implies that the affinity and the Assouad dimension coincide. We will also provide examples for both of the cases when the Hausdorff measure is zero and positive.

Introduction

Let
{\cal A}
be a finite set of indices, and let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a planar iterated function system (IFS) of affinities on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\|A_{i}\|<1
for every
i\in{\cal A}
and
|\det(A_{i})|\neq 0
. Hutchinson
[
20
]
showed that there exists a unique non-empty compact set
X
invariant with respect to
\Phi
, i.e.

X=\bigcup_{i\in{\cal A}}f_{i}(X).

We call
X

self-affine set
, and if the maps are similarities, that is,
A_{i}=\lambda_{i}O_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in(0,1)
and
O_{i}\in O({\mathbb{R}},d)
, then we call
X

self-similar
. Throughout the paper, we will restrict our attention to the planar,
d=2
case.

In the last decades, considerable attention has been paid to the geometric properties of such fractal sets, especially, to the Hausdorff dimension and measure. Let us define the Hausdorff measure, content and dimension for later purposes. For
\delta>0
and
s\geq 0
, set

{\cal H}^{s}_{\delta}(A)=\inf\{\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s}:A\subseteq\bigcup_{i}U_{i}\ \&\ |U_{i}|<\delta\}

the
\delta
-approximation of the Hausdorff measure, where
|U|
denotes the diameter of the set
U
. In particular, when
\delta=\infty
, we call the quantity
{\cal H}^{s}_{\infty}(A)=\inf\{\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s}:A\subseteq\bigcup_{i}U_{i}\}
the Hausdorff content. Let

{\cal H}^{s}(A)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}{\cal H}^{s}_{\delta}(A)\text{ and }\dimh A=\inf\{s>0:{\cal H}^{s}(A)=0\}=\inf\{s>0:{\cal H}^{s}_{\infty}(A)=0\}.

be the Hausdorff measure and dimension. For basic properties, we direct the reader to the book of Falconer
[
14
]
.

Hutchinson
[
20
]
studied the Hausdorff dimension and measure of self-similar sets. More precisely, he showed that
\dimh(X)\leq s_{0}
, where
s_{0}
is called the similarity dimension and it is the unique solution of the equation
\sum_{i\in{\cal A}}\lambda_{i}^{s_{0}}=1
. Furthermore, if the IFS
\{f_{i}(x)=\lambda_{i}O_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
satisfies the open set condition (OSC) then
0<{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)<\infty
and, in particular,
\dimh X=s_{0}
. For a precise definition of the OSC, see
[
20
]
. Later Bandt, Graf
[
2
]
and Schief
[
35
]
showed that
0<{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)<\infty
is equivalent to the open set condition, and they gave several further equivalent characterisations.

Even if the OSC fails, and so, the
s_{0}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is zero, typically the Hausdorff dimension does not drop with respect to the similarity dimension. Hochman
[
17
]
showed that if the IFS of similarities on the line satisfies the exponential separation condition then
\dimh X=\min\{1,s_{0}\}
. Later, Hochman
[
18
]
extended this result for higher dimensions.

Our knowledge on the more general self-affine situation is considerably more restrictive. Falconer
[
13
]
generalised the concept of the similarity dimension to the affine regime. For a
d\times d
matrix
A
, denote
\alpha_{i}(A)
the
i
th singular value of
A
. For
s\geq 0
, let us define the singular value function as

\varphi^{s}(A)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}(A)\cdots\alpha_{\lfloor s\rfloor}(A)\alpha_{\lceil s\rceil}(A)^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}&\text{ if }0\leq s\leq d\\
(|\det(A)|)^{s/d}&\text{ if }s>d.\end{cases}

We define the
affinity dimension
of the IFS
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
by

s_{0}=\inf\left\{s>0:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in{\cal A}}\varphi^{s}(A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n}})<\infty\right\}.

Falconer
[
13
]
showed that
s_{0}
is always an upper bound for the dimension of the attractor and Solomyak
[
36
]
proved that if
\|A_{i}\|<1/2
then
\min\{d,s_{0}\}
equals to the dimension for Lebesgue typical choice of translation parameters.

Unlike the self-similar case, the dimension of the attractor might drop with respect to the affinity dimension even if there is some kind of separation between the cylinder sets, like OSC. Bedford
[
8
]
and McMullen
[
26
]
studied certain type of self-affine carpets, which were later generalised by Gatzouras and Lalley
[
23
]
and Barański
[
3
]
, where the matrices
A_{i}
where diagonal and the set had a certain alignment structure. They gave a formula for the box-counting and Hausdorff dimension of the attractor, which is strictly smaller than the affinity dimension in most of the cases.

A possible reason for the dimension drop is the alignment structure of the set. One can get rid with it even if the matrices are diagonal by ensuring that the projections satisfies the exponential separation, see the recent result by Rapaport
[
34
]
. Another way to prevent the alignment structure by assuming that the matrices satisfy the strong irreducibility assumption, namely, there is no finite collection of proper subspaces preserved by the collection of matrices. Bárány, Hochman and Rapaport
[
4
]
verified for planar systems that if the strong open set condition holds and the matrices are strongly irreducible then the Hausdorff and box-counting dimension equal to the affinity dimension. Later, Hochman and Rapaport
[
19
]
extended this for planar exponentially separated systems, and Rapaport
[
33
]
recently extended it for systems on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with strong open set condition.

Bedford
[
8
]
, McMullen
[
26
]
and Gatzouras and Lalley
[
23
]
also showed that the proper dimensional Hausdorff measure of their carpet constructions is positive and finite if and only if the box and Hausdorff dimension coincide. Peres
[
28
]
studied the Hausdorff measure of Bedford-McMullen type sets in the complementary case and he showed that if the box and Hausdorff dimension do not coincide then the proper dimensional Hausdorff measure is infinite. This phenomenon has been recently extended to general Barański carpets by Qiu and Wang
[
30
]
. Kempton
[
22
]
studied the slices of the so-called Przytycki-Urbański carpets defined in
[
29
]
. He showed that Lebesgue almost every slice has positive
s_{0}-1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure (where
s_{0}
is the affinity dimension of the carpet) if and only if the projection of the natural measure is absolutely continuous with bounded density. This implies that the
s_{0}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is positive. This result was extended by Peng and Kamae
[
27
]
generalised for certain ”function type” self-affine sets.

The aforementioned studies on the Hausdorff measure were restricted to the case when the set were carpet like, that is, there is some alignment structure. We have only a very restrictive knowledge on the Hausdorff measure in the strongly irreducible case. A direct corollary of the result of Käenmäki
[
21
]
is that the
s_{0}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of every self-affine set is finite, where
s_{0}
is the affinity dimension. According to our best knowledge, the first result on the question under which circumstances is the
s_{0}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure positive in the strongly irreducible regime was due to Bárány, Käenmäki and Yu
[
6
]
. They studied dominated systems with affinity dimension smaller than
1
and they introduced the projective separation condition which is equivalent to the positivity of the Hausdorff measure.

The goal of this paper is to extend the result of Bárány, Käenmäki and Yu
[
6
]
for the case when the affinity dimension is between
1
and
2
.

1.1
Main results

Before we state the main results of the paper, let us introduce some basic notations standard in the theory of self-affine IFSs. Let
{\cal A}
be a finite set of indices and let us denote the usual symbolic space by
\Sigma={\cal A}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and the set of finite words by
\Sigma_{*}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal A}^{n}
. For a finite word
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\Sigma_{*}
, denote
|{\overline{\imath}}|
the length of
{\overline{\imath}}
and denote
{\overline{\imath}}_{-}
the finite word removing the last symbol of
{\overline{\imath}}
, that is, for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
,
{\overline{\imath}}_{-}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1})
. In case of infinite words
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
, we use the convention
|{\overline{\imath}}|=\infty
. Furthermore, for any
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{*}
and
n\leq|{\overline{\imath}}|
, let
{\overline{\imath}}|_{n}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
. We use the convention that
{\overline{\imath}}|_{0}=\emptyset
. For a finite word
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\Sigma_{*}
, let
{\overleftarrow{\imath}}:=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
be the word formed by the symbols of
{\overline{\imath}}
in reversed order. Similarly, let us write
{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}:=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)\in\Sigma
and
n\geq 1
.

For
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots),{\overline{\jmath}}=(j_{1},\ldots)\in\Sigma
, let
|{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}|:=\min\{k\geq 0:i_{k+1}\neq j_{k+1}\}
and
{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}:={\overline{\imath}}|_{|{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}|}
. For a word
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}
, let
[{\overline{\imath}}]:=\{{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma:{\overline{\jmath}}|_{|{\overline{\imath}}|}={\overline{\imath}}\}
be the cylinder set, that is, all the infinite words with prefix
{\overline{\imath}}
. We equip
\Sigma
with the topology generated by the cylinders. This topology is coincides with the topology generated by the ultra-metric
d({\overline{\imath}},{\overline{\jmath}})=\gamma^{-|{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}|}
, where
0<\gamma<1
, and
\Sigma
is compact. The Borel sigma-algebra on
\Sigma
coincides with the sigma-algebra generated by the cylinder sets. Finally, denote
\sigma\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
the usual left-shift operator.

For a finite word
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\Sigma_{*}
, let
f_{\overline{\imath}}=f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}
,
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}=A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n}}
and
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}=A_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{*}
, where
A^{*}
denotes the transpose of the matrix
A
. Note that the matrix
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}
differs from the transpose of the matrix
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}
, which would be
A_{i_{n}}^{*}\cdots A_{i_{1}}^{*}
, so whenever we require to use the transpose of the matrix
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}
, we will use the notation
(A_{{\overline{\imath}}})^{*}
or
A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}^{*}
. We use the same convention for the inverses of matrix products. Let us define the natural projection
\pi\colon\Sigma\to X
by

\pi({\overline{\imath}}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{{\overline{\imath}}|_{n}}(0).

(1)

Clearly,
\pi({\overline{\imath}})=f_{i_{1}}(\pi(\sigma{\overline{\imath}}))
, and it is Hölder continuous with respect to the metric
d
on
\Sigma
and the usual Euclidean metric.

Throughout the paper, we will assume that the collection of matrices
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
is
dominated
. That is, there exist
C>0
and
0<\tau<1
such that

\alpha_{2}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}})\leq C\tau^{|{\overline{\imath}}|}\alpha_{1}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}})\text{ for every ${\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}$.}

(2)

Bochi and Gourmelon
[
9
, Theorem A]
showed that the matrices
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
are dominated if and only if
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
admits a strongly invariant multicone. We say that a proper subset
{\cal C}\subset{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
is a
multicone
if it is a finite union of closed projective intervals. Moreover, we say that a multicone
{\cal C}
is strongly invariant if
\bigcup_{i\in{\cal A}}A_{i}^{*}{\cal C}\subseteq{\cal C}^{o}
. Let us define the collection of
Furstenberg directions
by
X_{F}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{{\overline{\imath}}:|{\overline{\imath}}|=n}A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}{\cal C}
. We define, similarly to the natural projection, a map
V\colon\Sigma\to X_{F}
by

\{V({\overline{\imath}})\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{{\overline{\imath}}|_{n}}^{*}{\cal C}.

(3)

One can easily see that
V({\overline{\imath}})=A_{i_{1}}^{*}V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})
, and
V
is Hölder continuous with respect to the metric
d
on
\Sigma
and the metric
\rho
on the projective space
{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
, where

\rho(V,W)=|\sin(\sphericalangle(V,W))|\text{ for }V,W\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1},

and
\sphericalangle
denotes the angle between the subspaces
V
and
W
. With a slight abuse of the notation, we will say that the IFS
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
is dominated if the set of linear parts
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
is dominated.

If
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
is dominated then there exists a unique left-shift invariant ergodic probability measure
\mu_{K}
on
\Sigma
such that there exists
c>0
such that

c^{-1}\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}})\leq\mu_{K}([{\overline{\imath}}])\leq c\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}}),

(4)

see Käenmäki
[
21
]
and Bárány, Käenmäki and Morris
[
5
]
. A simple combination of the existence of the Käenmäki measure
Eq.

4
and the covering argument by Falconer
[
13
]
implies that
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)<\infty
. For a proof, see
[
6
, Lemma 2.18]
.

For
V\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
, denote
\proj_{V}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to V
the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
V
, and let us denote by
\lambda_{V}
the Lebesgue measure on
V
. Now, we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a dominated planar IFS of affinities with affinity dimension
s_{0}\in(1,2]
. Let
X
be the attractor of
\Phi
, let
\mu_{K}
be the Käenmäki measure and let
\pi
be the natural projection. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
;

(b)

there exists
V\in X_{F}
such that
\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t))d\lambda_{V}(t)>0
;

(c)

\inf_{V\in X_{F}}\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t))d\lambda_{V}(t)>0
;

(d)

there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\pi_{*}\mu_{K}(B(x,r))\leq C\cdot r^{s_{0}}
for every
x\in X
,
r>0
, where
B(x,r)
denotes the ball with radius
r
centred at
x
.

Unlike to the self-similar case, see Bandt and Graf
[
2
]
and Schief
[
35
]
, and unlike to the dominated self-affine case with
s_{0}\leq 1
, see Bárány, Käenmäki and Yu
[
6
]
,
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
does not imply the
s_{0}
-Ahflors regularity of
X
. In particular, Bárány, Käenmäki and Yu
[
6
]
showed that for a dominated planar self-affine set with strong separation if
s_{0}>1
then
X
cannot be
s_{0}
-Ahlfors regular. However,
Theorem

1.1
shows that the positivity of the
s_{0}
-dimension Hausdorff measure is equivalent to a very rigid geometric structure, which is not easy-to-verify.

The positivity of the Hausdorff measure has some further consequences:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a dominated planar IFS of affinities with affinity dimension
s_{0}\in(1,2]
. Let
X
be the attractor of
\Phi
, let
X_{F}
be the set of Furstenberg directions, let
\mu_{K}
be the Käenmäki measure and let
\pi
be the natural projection. If
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
then

(i)

there exists a constant
C>0
such that
(\proj_{V})_{*}\pi_{*}\mu_{K}(B(t,r))\leq C\cdot r
for every
V\in X_{F}
,
t\in\proj_{V}(X)
and
r>0
;

(ii)

there exists
C>0
such that for every
V\in X_{F}
and for every
t\in\proj_{V}(X)
,
{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t))\leq C
.

Clearly, (
ii
) cannot be equivalent to
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
. For example, if the maps of
\Phi
have a common fixed point, that is,
X
is a singleton, but
s_{0}\in(1,2]
then
{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t))=0\leq C
for every
t\in\proj_{V}(X)
, however,
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)=0
. Item (
i
) seems strong enough to be equivalent to
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
in the generality of
Theorem

1.2
, but we could not verify it. For this reason, we introduce the open bounded neighbourhood condition motivated by the bounded neighbourhood condition introduced by Anttila, Bárány, Käenmäki
[
1
]
. For
r>0
, let

\Delta_{r}=\{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}:\alpha_{2}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}})|X|\leq r<\alpha_{2}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}_{-}})|X|\}.

(5)

We say that
\Phi
satisfies the
open bounded neighbourhood condition (OBNC)
if there exists an open and bounded set
U
such that
f_{i}(U)\subseteq U
by every
i\in{\cal A}
and there exists
C>0
such that for every
r>0
and every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\#\{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Delta_{r}:f_{\overline{\imath}}(U)\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset\}\leq C.

It is easy to see that the strong separation condition implies the OBNC, and the OBNC implies the bounded neighbourhood condition defined in
[
1
, Section 2.5]
, but the strong open set condition does not imply bounded neighbourhood condition, see
[
1
, Example 3.3]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a dominated planar IFS of affinities with affinity dimension
s_{0}\in(1,2]
. Let
X
be the attractor of
\Phi
, let
X_{F}
be the set of Furstenberg directions, let
\mu_{K}
be the Käenmäki measure and let
\pi
be the natural projection. Furthermore, suppose that
\Phi
satisfies the open bounded neighbourhood condition. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
;

(2)

there exists a constant
C>0
such that
(\proj_{V})_{*}\pi_{*}\mu_{K}(B(t,r))\leq C\cdot r
for every
V\in X_{F}
,
t\in\proj_{V}(X)
and
r>0
.

We note that (
ii
) in
Theorem

1.2
(same as (
2
) in
Theorem

1.3
) has already appeared as a sufficient condition in the recent paper of Batsis, Käenmäki and Kempton
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
regarding the multifractal analysis of fully supported quasi-Bernoulli measures on dominated planar self-affine sets. One might wonder whether is it enough to verify the bounded density of
(\proj_{V})_{*}\pi_{*}\mu_{K}
for only one
V\in X_{F}
. It seems very likely but we were unable to prove it.

A corollary of
Theorem

1.2
and the estimate of Anttila, Bárány and Käenmäki
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
is the following:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a planar IFS of affinities with affinity dimension
s_{0}\in(1,2]
. Suppose that
\Phi
is dominated and satisfies the open bounded neighbourhood condition. Denote
X
the attractor of
\Phi
. If
{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)>0
then
\dim_{\rm A}X=s_{0}
, where
\dim_{A}
denotes the Assouad dimension of
X
.

For precise definition and properties of the Assouad dimension, see Fraser
[
16
]
.

1.2
Examples

Finally, we consider some examples for our main theorems. First, we consider a strongly irreducible example with attractor having zero proper dimensional Hausdorff measure. This example has already appeared in
[
6
, Example 3.3]
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
q>p\geq 2
and
p<N\in\{2,\ldots,pq\}
be integers, and let
I\subset\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
be a set of
N
elements. Let
A=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{p}&0\\
0&\tfrac{1}{q}\end{pmatrix}
and let
B
be a
2\times 2
matrix with
\det(B)>0
and with strictly positive entries such that
\|B\|<1
. It is easy to see that the matrices
\{A,B\}
are dominated and strongly irreducible with Furstenberg directions containing the
x
-axis.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be such that the IFS

\Phi_{\epsilon}=\left\{x\mapsto Ax+\begin{pmatrix}j/p\\
k/q\end{pmatrix}\right\}_{(j,k)\in I}\cup\{x\mapsto\epsilon\cdot Bx+t\}

satisfies that
f([0,1]^{2})\cap g([0,1]^{2})=\emptyset
for every
f\neq g\in\Phi
, and

\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}}\#\{i:(j,i)\in I\}>\frac{N}{p}>1.

Let
j^{\prime}
be the symbol for which the maximum on the left-hand side is attained. By Bárány, Hochman and Rapaport
[
4
]
,
\dimh X=s_{0}(\epsilon)
, where
s_{0}(\epsilon)
is the affinity dimension and
X
is the attractor of
\Phi_{\epsilon}
. For the images of the first level cylinder sets, see
Fig.

1
.

Since
\varphi^{s}(\epsilon\cdot B)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
for all
s\geq 0
, the affinity dimension
s_{0}
of
\Phi_{\epsilon}
converges to

1+\frac{\log N-\log p}{\log q}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Hence, one can choose
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small such that

s_{0}(\epsilon)-1<\frac{\log\#\{i:(j^{\prime},i)\in I\}}{\log q}.

Since the
x
-axis belongs to
X_{F}
, and the attractor of the IFS
\left\{Ax+\left(\tfrac{j^{\prime}}{p},\tfrac{k}{q}\right)\right\}_{(j^{\prime},k)\in I}
forms a slice of
X
with dimension
\frac{\log\#\{i:(j^{\prime},i)\in I\}}{\log q}>s_{0}(\epsilon)-1
, by
Theorem

1.2
(
ii
), we have
{\cal H}^{s_{0}(\epsilon)}(X)=0
.

First level cylinder sets of the IFS
\Phi=\{f_{1}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{y}{5}\right),f_{2}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{y+2}{5}\right),f_{3}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{y+4}{5}\right),f_{4}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x+2}{3},\frac{y}{5}\right),f_{5}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x+2}{3},\frac{y+4}{5}\right),f_{6}(x,y)=\left(\frac{2x+y+5}{10},\frac{x+2y+2}{10}\right)\}
. Simple calculation shows that the Hausdorff dimension is at most
1.607
but the largest horizontal slice has dimension
0.6826
, and so, the proper dimensional Hausdorff measure is zero.

Now, we provide two triangular examples with positive and finite
s_{0}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Unfortunately, our examples are not strongly irreducible, the linear parts of the maps of the IFS are lower triangular matrices. However, we provide examples for both cases when
X_{F}

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we will always assume that
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
is dominated, and
s_{0}\in(1,2]
, where
s_{0}
is the affinity dimension. Without loss of generality, we will always assume that
X\subseteq B(0,1)
. From the domination by
[
9

, it follows that there exists a multicone
{\cal C}\subset{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
such that
A_{i}^{*}{\cal C}\subseteq{\cal C}^{o}
for every
i\in{\cal A}
. Then it is easy to see that
A_{i}^{-1}{\cal C}^{\perp}\subseteq({\cal C}^{\perp})^{o}
, where
{\cal C}^{\perp}=\{V\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}:V^{\perp}\in{\cal C}\}
.

Let
V\colon\Sigma\to X_{F}
be the natural projection to the set of Furstenberg directions defined in
Eq.

3
. It is clear that
V\colon\Sigma\to X_{F}
is Hölder-continuous. Moreover,

V({\overline{\imath}})=A_{i_{1}}^{*}V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})\text{ and }V({\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}=A_{i_{1}}^{-1}V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}.

By
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
, there exists a constant
C>1
such that and every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}

\|A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}|V\|\leq\alpha_{1}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*})=\alpha_{1}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}})\leq C\|A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}|V\|\text{ and }\|A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{-1}|V^{\perp}\|\leq\alpha_{1}(A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{-1})=\alpha_{2}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}})^{-1}\leq C\|A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{-1}|V^{\perp}\|

(7)

for every
V\in\bigcup_{i\in{\cal A}}A_{i}^{*}{\cal C}
. Let us note that above (and throughout the paper) we used the convention that
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{*}=A_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{*}
and
A_{{\overline{\imath}}}^{-1}=A_{i_{1}}^{-1}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{-1}
for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
.

With a slight abuse of notation, we define the orthogonal projection
\proj_{V}
as real valued function over
V\in{\cal C}
as follows: for every
V\in{\cal V}
, let
v=v(V)\in V
be a unit vector such that the map
V\mapsto v
is continuous on
{\cal C}
, and let

\proj_{V}(x)=\langle v(V),x\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual scalar product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Note that
\proj_{V}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the actual orthogonal projection
x\mapsto\langle v(V),x\rangle v(V)
, since it identifies
V
to
{\mathbb{R}}
with some prefixed orientation. Let us denote the Lebesgue measure on
[-1,1]
by
\lambda
.

By defining
F_{i,V}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

F_{i,V}=\begin{cases}\|A_{i}^{*}|V\|x+\proj_{V}(t_{i})&\text{ if }\frac{A_{i}^{*}v(V)}{\|A_{i}^{*}v(V)\|}=v(A_{i}^{*}V)\\
-\|A_{i}^{*}|V\|x+\proj_{V}(t_{i})&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Simple calculation shows that
\proj_{V}(f_{i}(x))=F_{i,V}(\proj_{A_{i}^{*}V}(x))
for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

2.1
Perron-Frobenius operator and its eigenfunction

We define a Hölder-continuous potential
g\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows:

g({\overline{\imath}}):=\log\|A_{i_{1}}^{*}|V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})\|+(s_{0}-1)\log\|A_{i_{1}}|V({\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|=\log\|A_{i_{1}}^{*}|V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})\|-(s_{0}-1)\log\|A_{i_{1}}^{-1}|V(\sigma{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|.

Simple calculation shows that for every
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)\in\Sigma
and
n\geq 1

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g(\sigma^{k}{\overline{\imath}})=\log\|A_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{*}|V(\sigma^{n}{\overline{\imath}})\|-(s_{0}-1)\log\|A_{i_{1}}^{-1}\cdots A_{i_{n}}^{-1}|V(\sigma^{n}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|,

and so

\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}})-\log C\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g(\sigma^{k}{\overline{\imath}})\leq\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}})+\log C,

where
{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)
.

Let us define the Perron-Frobenius operator
{\cal L}
, which maps real valued functions over
\Sigma
to real-valued functions over
\Sigma
such that

({\cal L}p)({\overline{\imath}})=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}e^{g(k{\overline{\imath}})}p(k{\overline{\imath}})=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\|A_{k}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\cdot\|A_{k}^{-1}|V({\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{-(s_{0}-1)}\cdot p(k{\overline{\imath}}).

Since the map
{\overline{\imath}}\mapsto e^{g({\overline{\imath}})}
is Hölder-continuous,
{\cal L}
maps continuous maps to continuous maps. By Ruelle’s Perron-Frobenius Theorem (see for example
[
11
, Theorem 1.7]
), there exists a unique continuous function
p\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
p({\overline{\imath}})>0
for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
, and there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\nu
for which
{\cal L}p=p
,
{\cal L}^{*}\nu=\nu
,
\int p({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\imath}})=1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}\left|({\cal L}^{n}h)({\overline{\imath}})-p({\overline{\imath}})\int h({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\imath}})\right|=0\text{ for every $h\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}$ continuous}.

(8)

We define
\mu_{F}([{\overline{\imath}}]):=\int_{[{\overline{\imath}}]}p({\overline{\jmath}})d\nu({\overline{\jmath}})
, then
\mu_{F}
is ergodic left-shift invariant probability measure such that for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}
and
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma

C^{\prime-1}\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}})\leq C^{-1}\exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{|{\overline{\imath}}|-1}g(\sigma^{k}{\overline{\imath}}{\overline{\jmath}})\right)\leq\mu_{F}([{\overline{\imath}}])\leq C\exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{|{\overline{\imath}}|-1}g(\sigma^{k}{\overline{\imath}}{\overline{\jmath}})\right)\leq C^{\prime}\varphi^{s_{0}}(A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}).

Note that
\mu_{F}
is the ”reversed” Käenmäki measure, that is,
\mu_{F}([{\overline{\imath}}])=\mu_{K}([{\overleftarrow{\imath}}])
, where
{\overleftarrow{\imath}}=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
, which follows from the uniqueness of the Käenmäki measure under domination, see
[
5
]
.

2.2
Hausdorff content of slices

Now, let us define a map
h\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

h({\overline{\imath}}):=\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t),

where we recall that
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
[-1,1]
. We will show that
h
is a constant multiplier of the eigenfunction
p
of
\mathcal{L}
. The proof is similar to the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 7.1]
. Let us begin with some technical lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map
(V,t)\mapsto{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)
is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

To verify the claim let
(V_{n},t_{n})
such that
(V_{n},t_{n})\to(V,t)
. Then for any sequence
x_{n}\in X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t_{n})
such that
x_{n}\to x
then
x\in X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)
by the compactness of
X
. So, for
\varepsilon>0
if
\{U_{i}\}
is an open cover of
X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)
such that
\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s_{0}-1}\leq{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)+\varepsilon
then without loss of generality, we may assume that
\{U_{i}\}
is finite (by the compactness of
X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)
), and so,
X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t_{n})\subseteq\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
for every sufficiently large
n
, which proves the claim
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The map
V\mapsto\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)
is upper semi-continuous. In particular, the map
{\overline{\imath}}\mapsto h({\overline{\imath}})
is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

Let
V\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
be arbitrary and fixed and let
V_{n}
be a sequence such that
V_{n}\to V
. Since
X\subset B(0,1)
, we have that for every
W\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
and
t\in[-1,1]

0\leq{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{W}^{-1}(t)\right)\leq 2^{s_{0}-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 2^{s_{0}}-\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\int 2^{s_{0}-1}-{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)\,d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int 2^{s_{0}-1}-\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t)\right)\,d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\int\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(2^{s_{0}-1}-{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t)\right)\right)\,d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int 2^{s_{0}-1}-{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=2^{s_{0}}-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V_{n}}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t),

where we used
Lemma

2.1
in the first inequality, and Fatou’s lemma in the second. This proves the first assertion of the lemma, and since
{\overline{\imath}}\mapsto V({\overline{\imath}})
is continuous, the second assertion follows too.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
,
h({\overline{\imath}})\leq({\cal L}h)({\overline{\imath}})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that for every
V\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}
,
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
i\in{\cal A}

\|\proj_{V}(f_{i}(x))-\proj_{V}(f_{i}(y))\|=\|A_{i}^{*}|V\|\cdot\|\proj_{A_{i}^{*}V}(x-y)\|.

So

\displaystyle\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{k}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(f_{k}(X))}{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{k}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\int_{\proj_{V(k{\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{k}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(F_{k,V}(t))\right)\|A_{k}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\|A_{k}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\int_{\proj_{V(k{\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{k}\left(X\cap\proj_{V(k{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\|A_{k}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\int_{\proj_{V(k{\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V(k{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)\|A_{k}|V(k{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=({\cal L}h)({\overline{\imath}}).

∎

Proposition 2.1
.

If
p\colon\Sigma\to(0,\infty)
is the map and
\nu
is the measure defined by Ruelle’s Perron-Frobenius Theorem in
Section

2.1
, then we get

h({\overline{\imath}})=p({\overline{\imath}})\iint{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)d\nu({\overline{\jmath}}).

In particular, either
h\equiv 0
or
\inf_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}h({\overline{\imath}})>0
.

Proof.

Since
h\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
is upper semi-continuous by
Lemma

2.2
, for every
n\geq 1
there exists a continuous function
h_{n}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h({\overline{\imath}})\leq h_{n}({\overline{\imath}})
and
\int h_{n}({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\imath}})\leq\int h({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\imath}})+1/n
by
[
32
, Theorem 2.1.3]
and the monotone convergence theorem. Then by
Eq.

8

h({\overline{\imath}})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}({\cal L}^{k}h)({\overline{\imath}})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}({\cal L}^{k}h_{n})({\overline{\imath}})=p({\overline{\imath}})\int h_{n}d\nu\leq p({\overline{\imath}})\left(\int hd\nu+1/n\right).

Since
n\geq 1
was arbitrary

h({\overline{\imath}})\leq p({\overline{\imath}})\int hd\nu.

Moreover,

\int p({\overline{\imath}})\int hd\nu-h({\overline{\imath}})\,d\nu({\overline{\imath}})=\int hd\nu\,\left(\int pd\nu-1\right)=0.

In summary, the function
{\overline{\imath}}\mapsto p({\overline{\imath}})\int hd\nu-h({\overline{\imath}})
is non-negative, lower semi-continuous and has integral zero on a fully supported measure, and hence, it is identically zero, which had to be shown.
∎

2.3
Hausdorff measure of slices

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
h\colon\Sigma\to[0,\infty)
be the function defined in
Proposition

2.1
. Then

h({\overline{\imath}})=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t).

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
be such that
|f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)|\leq\delta
for every
{\overline{\jmath}}
with
|{\overline{\jmath}}|\geq n
. Then

{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\delta}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)={\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right).

Thus, similarly to the proof of
Lemma

2.3
, for every
\delta>0
and
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\delta}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\delta}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X))}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X))}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(F_{{\overline{\jmath}},V({\overline{\imath}})}(t))\right)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\int_{\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|{\overline{\jmath}}|=n}\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\int_{\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=({\cal L}^{n}h)({\overline{\imath}})=h({\overline{\imath}}).

Hence,

\displaystyle h({\overline{\imath}})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\delta}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}\liminf_{\delta\to 0}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\delta}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)}{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)=h({\overline{\imath}}),

which completes the proof.
∎

In particular, we get that

\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

(9)

for every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma
, hence, the right-hand side is always finite. This has the following simple consequence:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
B\subseteq X
be a Borel set. Then for every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma

\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t).

In particular, for every Borel subset
B\subset X
and every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma
,
{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)=\linebreak{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)
for
\lambda
-almost every
t
.

Proof.

Since the Hausdorff content of a set always bounds the Hausdorff measure from below, we get

\displaystyle\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

then by using the additivity of the Hausdorff measure, we get

\displaystyle=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)-{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left((X\setminus B)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

now by (
9
)

\displaystyle\leq\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)-{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left((X\setminus B)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\jmath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t),

where the last inequality uses the countable subadditivity of the Hausdorff content.
∎

for

Proof.

{\overline{\jmath}},\hbar\in\Sigma_{n}

{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma

\displaystyle h({\overline{\imath}})

\displaystyle=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(\bigcup_{{\overline{\jmath}}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{n}}f_{{\overline{\jmath}}^{\prime}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt-\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap f_{\hbar}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)

Lemma 2.6
.

For every

2.4
An alternative form of the Käenmäki measure

For every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
, let us define a measure on
\Sigma
as follows: for every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma_{*}

\eta_{\overline{\imath}}([{\overline{\jmath}}]):=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}\left(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t).

First, we will show that
\eta_{\overline{\imath}}
can be extended to a well-defined Borel measure on
\Sigma
. (Note that
\eta_{\overline{\imath}}
might be the zero measure.) To do so, it is enough to show the following lemma:

Lemma 2.7
.

For every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma_{*}
, and
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma

\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}])=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}k]).

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.5
and
Lemma

2.6
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\eta_{\overline{\imath}}([{\overline{\jmath}}k])

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}k}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\sum_{k\in{\cal A}}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(f_{k}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(f_{{\overline{\jmath}}}(X)\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)=\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}]).

∎

Since
\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}
is a Borel measure on
\Sigma
, by

2.5
and the fact that the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra generated by the sets
\{f_{{\overline{\imath}}}(X)\}_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}}
, we get that for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
and every Borel subset
B\subseteq X

\pi_{*}\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}(B)=\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t).

(10)

Now, we show the dichotomy that
\eta_{\overline{\imath}}
is either trivial for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
, i.e. it is the uniformly zero measure or it is uniformly equivalent to the Käenmäki measure for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma
, the measure
\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}
is not the uniformly zero measure on
\Sigma
if and only if
\inf_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)>0
.

Moreover, if
\inf_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)>0
then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
{\overline{\jmath}}\in\Sigma_{*}
and
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma

C^{-1}\mu_{K}([{\overline{\jmath}}])\leq\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}])\leq C\mu_{K}([{\overline{\jmath}}]).

Proof.

Observe that by
Lemma

2.6
and the combination of
Proposition

2.1
and
Proposition

2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}])

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}|X|^{s_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha_{1}(A_{{\overline{\jmath}}})\alpha_{2}(A_{{\overline{\jmath}}})^{s_{0}-1}\leq C^{\prime}\mu_{K}([{\overline{\jmath}}]),

where in the last two inequalities we used
Eq.

4
and
Eq.

7
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\eta_{{\overline{\imath}}}([{\overline{\jmath}}])

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}^{*}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|\|A_{{\overline{\jmath}}}|V({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|^{s_{0}-1}\cdot p({\overleftarrow{\jmath}}{\overline{\imath}})\cdot\iint{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)d\nu({\overline{\imath}})

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime\prime}\mu_{K}([{\overline{\jmath}}])\cdot\iint{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)d\nu({\overline{\imath}})\cdot\inf_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}p({\overline{\imath}}).

Now,

∎

Now, we consider a more sophisticated version of

.

Proposition 2.4
.

If

then for every Borel subset

\pi_{*}\mu_{K}(B)=\frac{\iint{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(B\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)d\nu({\overline{\imath}})}{\iint{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))d\lambda(t)d\nu({\overline{\imath}})}.

Proof.

First, we will show that the Käenmäki measure

equals to the measure

on
\Sigma
. By

4
and

2.3
,

is equivalent to
\mu_{K}
, and so, it is enough to show that

is

\mu_{K}(B^{c})=0

3
Characterisation of positive measure

This section is devoted to prove our main theorems. Let us note that Marstrand
[
24
]
showed that for any Borel subset
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and every subspace
V\in{\mathbb{R}P}^{1}

{\cal H}^{s}(E)\geq\int_{\proj_{V}(E)}{\cal H}^{s-1}(E\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t))d\lambda_{V}(t).

Hence, item (
b
) implies item (
a
) in
Theorem

1.1
. Our first main lemma shows that a kind of reversed inequality holds for self-affine sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+t_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}

be the attractor of

{\cal H}^{s_{0}}(X)\leq C\max_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma}\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t).

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary but fixed. By
Lemma

2.1
, the map
(V,t)\mapsto{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V}^{-1}(t)\right)
is upper semi-continuous, and so, by
[
32

and the monotone convergence theorem, there exists a continuous function
a_{\overline{\imath}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
{\cal H}_{\infty}^{s_{0}-1}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)\leq a_{\overline{\imath}}(t)
and
\int a_{\overline{\imath}}(t)d\lambda(t)\leq\int{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}\left(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)\right)d\lambda(t)+\epsilon
. We may choose
a_{{\overline{\imath}}}
such that its support is contained in
[-1,1]
, and so, there exists
\delta({\overline{\imath}})>0
such that for every
t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}

then
|a_{\overline{\imath}}(t)-a_{{\overline{\imath}}}(t^{\prime})|<\epsilon
.

For every
({\overline{\imath}},t)\in\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\{U_{i,t}\}_{i\in{\cal I}_{{\overline{\imath}},t}}
be a cover of
X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)
by open intervals in
\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t)
such that
\sum_{i\in{\cal I}_{{\overline{\imath}},t}}|U_{i,t}|^{s_{0}-1}\leq{\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t))+\epsilon
. By the compactness, we may assume that
{\cal I}({\overline{\imath}},t)
is finite. Then for every
({\overline{\imath}},t)
, there exists
r({\overline{\imath}},t)>0
such that for every
|t-t^{\prime}|<r({\overline{\imath}},t)

X\cap\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}^{-1}(t^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in{\cal I}_{{\overline{\imath}},t}}U_{i,t}
. We may also assume that
r({\overline{\imath}},t)\leq\delta({\overline{\imath}})
by possibly taking minimum.

By applying Besicovitch’s covering theorem, there exists a
Q\geq 1
(independent of the quantities above) such that there exists
{\cal B}_{1}({\overline{\imath}}),\ldots,{\cal B}_{Q}({\overline{\imath}})
collection of points such that

•

\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{Q}\bigcup_{t\in{\cal B}_{i}({\overline{\imath}})}B(t,r({\overline{\imath}},t))
,

•

B(t,r({\overline{\imath}},t))\cap B(t^{\prime},r({\overline{\imath}},t^{\prime}))=\emptyset
for every
i=1,\ldots,Q
and
t\neq t^{\prime}\in{\cal B}_{i}({\overline{\imath}})
.

Since
\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)

{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}})\subseteq{\cal B}_{i}({\overline{\imath}})

\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}})}(X)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{Q}\bigcup_{t\in{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}})}B(t,r({\overline{\imath}},t))

\bigcup_{i=1}^{Q}{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}})
is finite there exists
N=N({\overline{\imath}})
such that for every
n\geq N({\overline{\imath}})

{\frac{\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}}|V({\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}\|}{\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}}|V({\overline{\imath}})\|}}\cdot|X|\leq\min_{t\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{Q}{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}}r({\overline{\imath}},t),

where we recall that
{\overleftarrow{\imath}}|_{n}=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
for
{\overline{\imath}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)
. For every
t\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{Q}{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}})
, and
j\in{\cal I}({\overline{\imath}},t)
let
\tilde{U}_{t,i}=U_{i,t}\times B(t,r({\overline{\imath}},t))
be the rectangle, axes parallel to
V({\overline{\imath}})
and
V({\overline{\imath}})^{\perp}

X
.

Unfortunately, the function
{\overline{\imath}}\mapsto N({\overline{\imath}})
is not necessarily measurable. However, the function
N_{m}({\overline{\imath}}):=\min\{N({\overline{\jmath}}):{\overline{\jmath}}\in[{\overline{\imath}}|_{m}]\}

m
-length cylinder sets, and thus,

M({\overline{\imath}})\leq N({\overline{\imath}})
for every
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma

M\geq 1

\epsilon>\mu_{F}(\{{\overline{\imath}}:M({\overline{\imath}})>M\})=\mu_{F}\left(\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{p=m}^{\infty}\{{\overline{\imath}}:N_{p}({\overline{\imath}})>M\}\right)\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu_{F}(\{{\overline{\imath}}:N_{m}({\overline{\imath}})>M\})

M^{\prime}\geq M

\epsilon>\mu_{F}(\{{\overline{\imath}}:N_{m}({\overline{\imath}})>M\})\geq\mu_{F}(\{{\overline{\imath}}:N_{m}({\overline{\imath}})>m\}).

(11)

(\max_{i}\|A_{i}\|)^{m}\cdot|X|

{\cal G}_{m}:=\{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{m}:\text{ there exists }{\overline{\jmath}}\in[{\overline{\imath}}]\text{ such that }N({\overline{\jmath}})\leq m\}

\bigcup_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\mathcal{G}_{m}^{c}}[{\overline{\imath}}]=\{{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma:N_{m}({\overline{\imath}})>m\}

\mu_{F}(\bigcup_{{\overline{\imath}}\in\mathcal{G}_{m}^{c}}[{\overline{\imath}}])\leq\epsilon

{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}

{\overline{\imath}}^{\prime}\in\Sigma

{\overline{\imath}}^{\prime}\in[{\overline{\imath}}|_{m}]

For every
{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}^{c}

\lceil\alpha_{1}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})/\alpha_{2}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})\rceil

\left\lceil\tfrac{2\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}|V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})\|r({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime},t)}{\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}|V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})^{\perp}\|\cdot|U_{i,t}|}\right\rceil

A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})^{\perp}=V(\sigma^{m}{\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})^{\perp}

(independent of the quantities above) the diameter of such lozenge is at most

.

Hence,

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{s_{0}}_{(\max_{i}\|A_{i}\|)^{m}|X|}(X)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}^{c}}\left\lceil\frac{\alpha_{1}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})}{\alpha_{2}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})}\right\rceil\left(\alpha_{2}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})|X|\right)^{s_{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt+\sum_{{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\sum_{t\in\bigcup{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})}\sum_{j\in{\cal I}_{{\overline{\imath}}^{\prime},t}}\left\lceil\frac{2\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}|V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})\|\cdot r({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime},t)}{\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}|V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})^{\perp}\|\cdot|U_{j,t}|}\right\rceil\left(c\|A_{{\overleftarrow{\imath}}}|V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})^{\perp}\|\cdot|U_{j,t}|\right)^{s_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon+\sum_{{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}}\alpha_{1}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})\alpha_{2}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})^{s_{0}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\sum_{t\in{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})}r({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime},t)\left({\cal H}^{s_{0}-1}_{\infty}(\proj_{V({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})}^{-1}(t)\cap X)+\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\sum_{{\overline{\imath}}\in{\cal G}_{m}}\alpha_{1}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})\alpha_{2}(A_{\overleftarrow{\imath}})^{s_{0}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\sum_{t\in{\cal B}_{i}^{\prime}({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime})}r({\overline{\imath}}^{\prime},t)\left(a_{{\overline{\imath}}^{\prime}}(t)+\epsilon\right)

by using that

4
Verification of the examples

Our final section is devoted to verify the examples presented in
Section

1.2
. Our strategy is the following: we give conditions under which the planar system satisfies the strong separation condition and hence, the open bounded neighbourhood condition, and then we show that the projections of the Käenmäki measure along Furstenberg directions are absolutely continuous with continuous density. To show this, we borrow Fourier analytic methods from Feng and Feng
[
15
]
.

For a Borel probability measure
\eta
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let us denote by
\widehat{\eta}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
the Fourier transform of
\eta
, that is,

\widehat{\eta}(\xi)=\int e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}d\eta(x).

By
[
25
, Theorem 5.4]
, if there exists a
t>d
such that

\int|\widehat{\eta}(\xi)|^{2}\|\xi\|^{t}d\xi<\infty

(12)

then
\eta\ll{\cal L}_{d}
with continuous density.

4.1
Diagonal example

Before we verify

1.2
, we need the following lemma. Although, we believe that this lemma is well-known, we could not find any proper reference.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{x\mapsto c_{i}x+\tau_{i}\}_{i\in{\cal A}}
be a self-similar IFS on the real line with natural projection
\pi_{\tau}
and let

be a probability vector and

be the corresponding Bernoulli measure on
\Sigma

\sum_{i\in{\cal A}}\left(\frac{p_{i}}{|c_{i}|}\right)^{2}<1
then the self-similar measure

\tau:=(\tau_{i})_{i\in{\cal A}}
.

For simplicity, let
a_{{\overline{\imath}}}=a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}
for
{\overline{\imath}}\in\Sigma_{*}
. Let us write
\pi_{\tau}
for the natural projection of

\pi_{\tau}({\overline{\imath}})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tau_{i_{k}}c_{{\overline{\imath}}|_{k-1}}=\sum_{j\in{\cal A}}\tau_{i}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{i_{k}}^{j}c_{{\overline{\imath}}|_{k-1}},

where

and otherwise

. Let

be the vector

In particular,

It is easy to see that if

\|\Pi({\overline{\imath}})-\Pi({\overline{\jmath}})\|\geq|c_{{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}}|\frac{1-2\max_{i}|c_{i}|}{1-\max_{i}|c_{i}|}>c|c_{{\overline{\imath}}\wedge{\overline{\jmath}}}|.

(13)

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\eta_{\tau}}(\xi)=\int e^{-i\xi\pi_{\tau}({\overline{\imath}})}d\nu({\overline{\imath}})

12
for Lebesgue almost every
(\tau_{i})_{i\in{\cal A}}
. To show that, it is enough to verify that

\iint|\widehat{\eta_{\tau}}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{t}d\xi\psi(\tau)d\tau<\infty

for every compactly supported density function

with Fourier transform
\widehat{\psi}
satisfying that for every

there exists a

such that for every

\widehat{\psi}(\underline{\xi})\leq\frac{C_{N}}{(1+\|\underline{\xi}\|)^{N}}.

Let us choose

and

such that
\sum_{i\in{\cal A}}|c_{i}|^{-N}p_{i}^{2}<1
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\iint|\widehat{\eta_{\tau}}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{t}d\xi\psi(\tau)d\tau\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\iint\iint e^{i\xi(x-y)}|\xi|^{t}\psi(\tau)d\eta_{\tau}(x)d\eta_{\tau}(y)d\tau d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\iint\iint e^{i\xi\langle\tau,\Pi({\overline{\imath}})-\Pi({\overline{\jmath}})\rangle}\psi(\tau)d\tau|\xi|^{t}d\nu({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\jmath}})d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\iiint|\widehat{\psi}(\xi\cdot(\Pi({\overline{\imath}})-\Pi({\overline{\jmath}})))||\xi|^{t}d\nu({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\jmath}})d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\iiint\frac{C_{N}|\xi|^{t}}{(1+|\xi|\|\Pi({\overline{\imath}})-\Pi({\overline{\jmath}})\|)^{N}}d\nu({\overline{\imath}})d\nu({\overline{\jmath}})d\xi

The ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued spectral flow of a symmetric family of Toeplitz operators
Maxim Braverman, Ahmad Reza Haj Saeedi Sadegh

Abstract
. We consider families A ⁡ ( t ) A(t) of self-adjoint operators with symmetry that causes the spectral flow of the family to vanish. We study the secondary ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued spectral flow of such families. We prove an analog of the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer-Robbin-Salamon theorem, showing that this secondary spectral flow of A ⁡ ( t ) A(t) is equal to the secondary ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of the suspension operator d d ​ t + A ⁡ ( t ) \frac{d}{dt}+A(t) . Applying this result, we show that the graded secondary spectral flow of a symmetric family of Toeplitz operators on a complete Riemannian manifold equals the secondary index of a certain Callias-type operator. In the case of a pseudo-convex domain, this leads to an odd version of the secondary Boutet de Monvel’s index theorem for Toeplitz operators. When this domain is simply a unit disc in the complex plane, we recover the bulk-edge correspondence for the Graf-Porta module for 2D topological insulators of type AII.

1.
Introduction

Atiyah, Patodi, and Singer
[
2
]
showed that the spectral flow of a periodic path
A=A(t)
of self-adjoint Fredholm operators on a Hilbert space
H
is equal to the index of the “suspension” operator
D_{A}:=\frac{d}{dt}+A(t)
. This result was extended to non-periodic families by Robbin and Salamon
[
25
]
.

In this paper, we assume that there is an anti-linear, anti-unitary, anti-involution
\alpha:H\to H
,
\alpha^{2}=-1
,
\alpha^{*}=\alpha
, and define a transformation
\tau
of paths of operators by
\tau:A(t)\to\alpha A(-t)\alpha^{-1}
. If the path
A(t)
is
\tau
-invariant, then its spectral flow vanishes. However, the secondary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariant called the
half-spectral flow
is defined, cf.
[
18
,
17
]
. In this situation, the suspension operator
D_{A}
commutes with
\tau
. Such an operator is called
odd symmetric
and its index vanishes because of the symmetry. But a secondary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}
, called the
\tau
-index
, is defined,
[
26
,
17
,
18
,
9
]
.

The first main result of our paper is the analog of the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer-Robbin-Salamon theorem for half-spectral flow:

(1.1)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}.

Note that recently a different version of the Robin-Salamon-type theorem for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued spectral flow was obtained in
[
6
]
. In this paper, the authors considered paths of skew-adjoint Fredholm operators on a real vector space. The main difference with our result is that in
[
6
]
every operator
A(t)
possesses a symmetry (it is skew-adjoint), while we consider the case when the symmetry relates
A(t)
with
A(-t)
. We discuss the relationship between our result and
[
6
]
in Section
4.6
.

Next, we consider a complete Riemannian manifold
M
with involution
\theta^{M}:M\to M
. Let
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
be a graded Clifford bundle over
M
endowed with an anti-unitary anti-involution
\theta^{E}
covering
\theta^{M}
. We say that

is a
graded quaternionic Clifford bundle
. Then the corresponding Dirac operator

is odd symmetric. We denote by

the kernel of

Given a family

of self-adjoint matrix-valued functions on

, we denote by

the operator of multiplication by

and define a family of Toeplitz operators

Let

to

.

The Dirac operator

on the manifold

is an odd symmetric Callias-type operator. The

(1.2)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}T_{f_{t}}^{+}\ -\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}T_{f_{t}}^{-}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{B}_{cf}.

In the case when

is a pseudo-convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with smooth boundary

, it follows from

that

. Hence, the above formula computes the half-spectral flow of

We call this result the

2.
The
\tau
-index

In this section, we recall the construction of the secondary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of odd symmetric operators
[
26
,
17
,
18
]
. We work with unbounded operators and recall some results about the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of an odd symmetric differential operator on a non-compact involutive manifold

2.1.
An anti-linear involution

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space. We denote the inner product on
H
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
. An
anti-linear
map
\tau:H\to H
is called an anti-involution if
\tau^{*}=-\tau=\tau^{-1}
. Here we denote by
\tau^{*}
the unique anti–linear operator satisfying

\langle\tau x,y\rangle_{H}\ =\ \overline{\langle x,\tau^{*}y\rangle_{H}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in H.

We say that
\tau
is an
anti-unitary anti-involution
.

The following version of
Kramers’ degeneracy

[
23
]
is proven in
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]

Lemma 2.1
.

An anti-linear anti-involution
\tau
does not have non-zero fixed vectors, i.e.
\tau x=x
iff
x=0
. Further, if
\tau
acts on a finite-dimensional space
H
, then
\dim H
is even. Moreover, there exists a subspace
L\in H
such that
H=L\oplus\tau L
.

2.2.
Odd symmetric operators

Let
W\subset H
be a dense subspace and let
D:H\to H
be a closed unbounded linear operator on
H
whose domain is
W
. We endow
W
with the inner product

\langle x,y\rangle_{W}\ =\ \langle x,y\rangle_{H}\ +\ \langle Dx,Dy\rangle_{W}.

Then
W
is a Hilbert space and the operator
D:W\to H
is a bounded.

Definition 2.2
.

A closed operator
D:H\to H
with a dense domain
W
is called
odd symmetric
(or
\tau
-symmetric) if

(2.1)

\tau\,D\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ D^{*}.

We denote by
{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
the space of
\tau
-symmetric operators on
H
whose domain is
W
. This is a Banach space with the norm defined as the operator norm of
D:W\to H
.

2.3.
Graded odd symmetric operators

If
H=H^{+}\oplus H^{-}
is a graded Hilbert space, we say that
D
is
odd with respect to the grading
if
D:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. We denote
D^{\pm}
the restriction of
D
to
H^{\pm}
and write
D=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Let
W
be the domain of
D
. Then
W^{\pm}:=W\cap H^{\pm}
is the domain of
D^{\pm}
and
W=W^{+}\oplus W^{-}
. If, in addition,
D
is self-adjoint, then
(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
.

Assume that the anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
is odd with respect to the grading,
\tau:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. If
D
is odd symmetric, then (
2.1
) implies that
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=(D^{\mp})^{*}
. In this situation, we say that
D
is a
graded odd symmetric operator
.

We denote the space of graded odd symmetric self-adjoint operators by
\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.
If
D\in\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
, then
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=D^{\mp}=(D^{\pm})^{*}
. Then
the operators
D^{\pm}
are odd symmetric
. Notice, that in this case
\tau:W^{\pm}\to W^{\mp}
.

2.4.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index

If
D
is an odd symmetric operator then
\operatorname{ind}D=0
by (
2.1
). Following
[
26
]
, we define the
secondary

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariant – the
\tau
-index of
D
:

Definition 2.3
.

If
D
is an odd symmetric operator on
H
its
\tau
-index
is

(2.2)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ :=\ \dim\ker D\qquad\mod 2.

If
D\in\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
is a graded odd symmetric self-adjoint operator on a graded Hilbert space
H
, we define

(2.3)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ :=\ \dim\ker D^{+}\qquad\mod 2.

The following version of the homotopy invariance of the index is proven in
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D
is constant on the connected components of
{B}_{\tau}(W,H)
. The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}
is constant on the connected components of
\widehat{{B}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.

We now discuss some geometric examples of odd symmetric operators,
[
9
]
.

2.5.
Quaternionic vector bundles

An
involutive manifold

(M,\theta^{M})
is a Riemannian manifold
M
together with a metric preserving involution
\theta^{M}:M\to M
. A
quaternionic vector bundle
over an involutive manifold
M
is hermitian vector bundle
E
endowed with an anti-linear unitary bundle map
\theta^{E}:E\to\theta^{*}E
, such that
(\theta^{E})^{2}=-1
, cf.
[
20
,
16
,
22
]
. This means that for every
x\in M
there exists an anti-linear map
\theta^{E}_{x}:E_{x}\to E_{\theta(x)}
, which depends smoothly on
x
and satisfies
\theta^{E}_{\theta^{M}(x)}\,\theta^{E}_{x}=-1
,

(\theta^{E}_{x})^{*}\theta^{E}_{x}=1
.

If
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
is a graded vector bundle and
\theta^{E}
is
odd with respect to the grading
(i.e.
\theta^{E}(E^{\pm})=E^{\mp}
), we say that
(E,\theta^{E})
is a
graded quaternionic bundle
.

Given a quaternionic vector bundle
(E,\theta^{E})
over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta^{M})
we define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
on the space
\Gamma(M,E)
of sections of
E
by

(2.4)

\tau:\,f(x)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{E}f(\theta^{M}x),\qquad f\in\Gamma(M,E).

2.6.
Odd symmetric elliptic operators

Let
(E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-},\theta^{E})
be a graded quaternionic vector bundle over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta^{M})
. Let
D:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
be a self-adjoint odd symmetric elliptic differential operator on
M
, which is odd with respect to the grading, i.e.
D:\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})\to\Gamma(M,E^{\mp})
. We set
D^{\pm}:=D|_{\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})}
. Then
\tau\,D^{\pm}\,\tau^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
and

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the decomposition
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
.

Suppose now that
D
is Fredholm. In particular, this is always the case when
M
is compact. Then the
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is defined by (
2.2
). In
[
9
]
we computed this index in several examples and proved the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analogs of the Relative Index Theorem, the Callias index theorem, and the Boutet de Monvel’s index theorem for Toeplitz operators.

The spectral flow and the Robbin-Salamon theorem

This section recalls some basic definitions and results from

3.1.
Some spaces of operators

Let
A:H\to H
be a closed operator with dense domain

. As in Section
2.2
, we define the scalar product on
W
by

\langle x,y\rangle_{W}\ =\ \langle x,y\rangle_{H}\ +\ \langle Ax,Ay\rangle_{H}

and view

as a bounded operator
A:W\to H
. We denote by
\|A\|
its norm.

We now make a basic

Assumption 3.1

The embedding

is compact.

Let
{S}(W,H)
denote the space of self-adjoint (possibly unbounded) operators on
H
whose domain is

and
which have a non-zero resolvent set
. If the Assumption
3.1
is satisfied then every
A\in{S}
has a compact resolvent and, hence,

has a discrete spectrum. In particular, it is Fredholm.

From this point on we always assume Assumption
3.1
.

Let
{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
denote the space of norm continuous maps
A:\mathbb{R}\to{S}(W,H)
which have limits

(3.1)

A^{\pm}\ =\ \lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\,A(t)

and the operators
A^{\pm}:W\to H
are bijective. This is an open subspace in the Banach space of all maps satisfying (
3.1
) with the norm

(3.2)

\|A\|_{A}\ :=\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\|A(t)\|.

Denote by
{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
the set of those
A\in{A}
which are continuously differentiable in the norm topology and satisfy

\|A\|_{{A}^{1}}\ :=\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\big(\|A(t)\|+\|\dot{A}(t)\|\big)\ <\ \infty.

The space
{A}^{1}
is dense in
{A}
.

The following theorem is proven in
[
25
, Theorem 4.23]
:

Theorem 3.2
.

There is a unique map
\mu:{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

(Homotopy)
\mu
is constant on the connected components (in norm topology) of
{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
.

(ii)

(Constant) If
A(t)
is independent of

, then
\mu(A)=0
.

(iii)

(Direct sum)
\mu(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})=\mu(A_{1})+\mu(A_{2})
.

(iv)

(Normalization) For

and
A(t)=\arctan(t)
, we have
\mu(A)=1
.

Remark 3.3
.

Robbin and Salamon include an extra
catenation
axiom in their theorem. But then,
[

they show, that this axiom follows from the others 4 axioms and, hence, can be omitted. Since for our main object of study – the half-spectral flow – the catenation axiom does not make sense, we prefer to formulate Theorem

For

let
\operatorname{sf}A

\operatorname{sf}A

We say that

\ker A(t)\not=\{0\}
.
First, consider the case when
A\in{A}^{1}
. If

is a crossing for
A
, we define the

\Gamma(A,t)\ :=\ P(A,t)\dot{A}(t)P(A,t){\big|_{\ker A(t)}},

where

denotes the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of

. We say that the crossing

is

if the crossing operator
\Gamma(A,t)
is non-degenerate. A regular crossing is

if
\dim\ker A(t)=1
.

If

is a regular crossing for

we denote by
\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,t)

If

is a simple crossing there is a unique real-valued function
\lambda(s)
, defined near

, such that
\lambda(s)
is an eigenvalue of

for all

and
\lambda(t)=0
. We call
\lambda
the

. Then

\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,t)\ =\ \operatorname{sign}\dot{\lambda}(t).

If
A\in{A}^{1}
has only regular crossings then we define

(3.3)

\operatorname{sf}A\ =\ \sum_{t}\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,t),

where the sum is taking over all crossings of
A
.
Thus, if
A(t)
has only simple crossings

(3.4)

\operatorname{sf}A\ =\ \sum_{t}\operatorname{sign}\dot{\lambda}(t).

Proposition 3.4
.

(i)

In every connected component of

(ii)

\operatorname{sf}A_{0}=\operatorname{sf}A_{1}
.

We now define the spectral flow of

which lies in the same connected component of

The unique function

3.4.
A periodic family of operators

Consider an element

\operatorname{ind}\left(\,\frac{d}{dt}+A\,\right)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}\tilde{A}.

4.
The half-spectral flow of a family of operators on real line

In this section, we consider
\tau
-symmetric paths of self-adjoint operators. Because of the symmetry, the usual spectral flow vanishes. We define a secondary invariant
half-spectral flow
, with values in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In
[
18
]
this invariant is called the
odd half-spectral flow
. This part of the section roughly follows
[
17
]
, but we consider a slightly different setting because of our future applications.

Theorem
4.1
states that several properties of half-spectral flow define it uniquely. This is an analog of Theorem
3.2
. If
A
is a
\tau
-symmetric path, then the operator
D_{A}
, cf. (
3.5
) is
\tau
-symmetric (in the sense of Section
2
) with respect to some anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
. We use the uniqueness of the spectral flow to show that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}
is equal to the half-spectral flow of
A
. This is an analog of the Robbin–Salamon Theorem
3.6
for our secondary invariants. This result should be compared to
[
6
, §8]
, though we consider different paths of operators. We discuss the relationship of our result with
[
6
]
in Section
4.6
.

4.1.
An anti-linear anti-involution

As in Section
3.1
, we assume that
W\hookrightarrow H
is a compact embedding of a Hilbert space
W
into a Hilbert space
H
. We identify
W
with its image in
H
.
Let
\alpha:H\to H
be an
anti-linear anti-involution
and assume that
\alpha(W)=W
.

The map
\alpha
defies anti-linear maps on the spaces of functions
{W}
and
{H}
of Section
3.3
, which we also denote by
\alpha
. Define the maps
\tau:{W}\to{W}
and
\tau:{H}\to{H}
by

(4.1)

\tau:\,\xi(t)\ \mapsto\ \alpha\xi(-t).

By a slight abuse of the language we denote by the same letter the conjugation

\tau:{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
,

(4.2)

\tau:\,A(t)\ \mapsto\ \tau\,A(t)\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ \alpha\,A(-t)\,\alpha^{-1}.

Let

(4.3)

{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\ :=\ \big\{\,A\in{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H):\,\tau(A)=A\,\big\}.

denote the set of
\tau
-invariants in
{A}
and set set
{A}_{\tau}^{1}:={A}_{\tau}\cap{A}^{1}
. Recall that if
A\in{A}
then
A(t)
has a discrete spectrum and compact resolvent for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.2.
The half-spectral flow of a
\tau
-invariant path of operators

Suppose
A\in{A}_{\tau}
. If
t\in\mathbb{R}
is a crossing for
A
, then
-t
is also a crossing for
A
and

(4.4)

\Gamma(A,t)\ =\ -\,\alpha\,\Gamma(A,-t)\,\alpha^{-1}.

Hence,
\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,t)=-\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,-t)
and, if 0 is a crossing,
\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,0)=0
. We conclude that
the spectral flow of any
A\in{A}_{\tau}
is equal to zero
. In this section we define a
secondary invariant
of
A
–– the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued half-spectral flow.

Before giving the definition and discussing the properties of the half-spectral flow, let us put it in a perspective of the works of Atiayh–Singer
[
3
]
and Carey-Phillips-Schulz-Baldes
[
14
]
.

Recall that the loop space
\Omega{S}(W,H)
was introduced in Section
3.4
. Define
\tau:\Omega{S}\to\Omega{S}
by

\tau\big(\tilde{A}(e^{it})\big)\ :=\ \alpha\,\tilde{A}\big(e^{-it}\big)\,\alpha,

and let

\Omega_{\tau}{S}(W,H)\ :=\ \big\{\,\tilde{A}\in\Omega{S}(W,H):\,\tau(\tilde{A})=\tilde{A}\,\big\},

be the space of
\tau
-invariant loops.

Since the spectral flow of
\tilde{A}
vanishes, it follows from
[
3
]
that is
\tilde{A}
is homotopic in the space
\Omega{S}
to a constant path. However, if the half-spectral flow does not vanish, this homotopy cannot be chosen inside the space
\Omega_{\tau}{S}
.

4.3.
The uniqueness of half-spectral flow

The first main result of this section is the following analog of Theorem
4.1
:

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a unique map
\mu_{\tau}:{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

(Homotopy)
\mu_{\tau}
is constant on the connected components (in norm topology) of
{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
.

(ii)

(Constant) If
A(t)
is independent of
t
, then
\mu_{\tau}(A)=0
.

(iii)

(Direct sum)
\mu_{\tau_{1}\oplus\tau_{2}}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})=\mu_{\tau_{1}}(A_{1})+\mu_{\tau_{2}}(A_{2})
.

(iv)

(Normalization) For
W=H=\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

\alpha:\mathbb{C}^{2}\ \to\ \mathbb{C}^{2},\qquad\alpha:\,(z_{1},z_{2})\ \mapsto\ (\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1}).

and

(4.5)

A(t)\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}\arctan(t)&0\\
0&-\arctan(t)\end{pmatrix},

we have
\mu_{\tau}(A)=1
.

The number
\mu_{\tau}(A)
is called the
half-spectral flow
(or the
\tau
-spectral flow) of A.

The proof of the theorem is postponed to Section
5.6
.

4.4.
The half-spectral flow

We present an explicit construction of the half-spectral flow in terms of the crossing operators. Recall that
t\in\mathbb{R}
is called a crossing for
A(t)
if
\ker A(t)\not=\{0\}
. We say that a crossing
t
is
isolated
if the exists
\delta>0
such that
t
is the only crossing for
A(t)
on the interval
(t-\delta,t+\delta)
. Note that if
A\in A^{1}_{\tau}
and
t
is a regular crossing (cf. Section
3.2
) then
t
is an isolated crossing.

First, assume that
A\in{A}_{\tau}
has only isolated crossings.
Since we are only interested in mod 2 value of the flow, we can replace the signature of the crossing operator with the rank of the spectral projection
P(A,t)
:

(4.6)

\operatorname{sign}\Gamma(A,t)\ =\ \operatorname{rank}P(A,t)\qquad\mod 2.

The space
\ker P(A,0)
is
\alpha
-invariant. It follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\operatorname{rank}P(A,0)
is even. As opposed to the crossing operators
\Gamma(A,t)
, the projections
P(A,t)
are defined for all
A\in{A}_{\tau}
(and not only for those in
{A}_{\tau}^{1}
). If
A\in{A}_{\tau}
has only isolated crossings we define

(4.7)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)\ :=\ \ \sum_{t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A,t)\ +\ \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}P(A,0),\qquad\mod 2.

We have the following analogue of Proposition
3.4
:

Proposition 4.2
.

If
A_{0},A_{1}\in{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
belong to the same connected component and have only isolated crossings then
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A_{0}=\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A_{1}
.

The proof is given in Section
5.1
. We now define the half-spectral flow of
A\in{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
as the spectral flow of any of the operators
A_{1}
with isolated crossings lying in the same connected components of
{A}_{\tau}
as
A
. By Proposition
4.2
this definition is independent of the choice of
A_{1}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

The map
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}:{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.1
and, hence, is the half-spectral flow.

The proof of the theorem is given in Section
5.4
.

4.5.
The
\tau
–index of
D_{A}

Let
A\in{A}_{\tau}
and let
D_{A}
be as in (
3.5
). Then

(4.8)

\tau\,D_{A}\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ D_{A}^{*},

where
\tau
is defined in (
4.1
). Hence
D_{A}
is
\tau
-invariant and its
\tau
-index is defined by (
2.2
),

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}\ =\ \dim\ker D_{A}\qquad\mod\ 2.

We have the following analog of the Robbin-Salamon Theorem
3.6
:

Theorem 4.4
.

The map
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}:{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.1
and, hence, is equal to the half-spectral flow:

(4.9)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\Big(\,\frac{d}{dt}+A(t)\,\Big)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A\qquad\mod 2.

The proof of Theorem
4.4
is presented in Section
5.5
.

4.6.
Comparison with the spectral flow of a path of skew-adjoint operators

For
A(t)\in{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
suppose for simplicity that the operator
A(0)
is invertible. Set

A_{<0}(t):\ =\ \begin{cases}A(t)\quad&\text{for}\ \ t\leq 0;\\
A(0)\quad&\text{for}\ \ t>0,\end{cases}\qquad\qquad A_{>0}(t):\ =\ \begin{cases}A(t)\quad&\text{for}\ \ t\geq 0;\\
A(0)\quad&\text{for}\ \ t<0.\end{cases}

Consider the “doubling” of the path
A
:

\hat{A}(t)\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}0&A_{<0}(t)\\
-\alpha\,A_{>0}(-t)\,\alpha^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

This is a path of skew adjoint operators.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued spectral flow of
\hat{A}
considered in
[
6
]
is exactly equal to
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A
. Theorem 8.1 of
[
6
]
expresses this spectral flow as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of a certain “suspension” operator of the form similar to
\frac{d}{dt}+A
. Using the gluing formula for the
\tau
-index,
[
8
]
, one can show that the latter index is equal to
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\big(\frac{d}{dt}+A(t)\big)
. Thus the half-spectral flow of
A
is equal to the
\tau
-index of
\frac{d}{dt}+A
. Combining it with Theorem 8.1 of
[
6
]
, we see that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued indexes of
\frac{d}{dt}+A
and of the operator in
[
6
]
coincide. This can also be shown by a direct, but non-trivial argument.

In conclusion, our Theorem
4.4
is closely related to Theorem 8.1 of
[
6
]
, but the relationship is not very straightforward. This is why we preferred to give a direct proof of our result in the next section, by adopting the original argument of Robbin-Salamon
[
25
]
to our
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariants.

5.
Proof of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued version of the Robbin-Salamon theorem

This section is dedicated to the proofs of Proposition
4.2
and Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
, and
4.1
.

5.1.
Proof of Proposition
4.2

Suppose
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
lie in the same connected component of
{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
and let
A_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) be a continuous path in
{A}_{\tau}
connecting between them such that all
A_{s}
have only isolated crossings. We need to prove that
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{s})
is independent of
s
. If
t=0
is not a crossing point for some
A_{s_{0}}
, then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
0
is not a crossing for all
A_{s}
,
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
. Then the path

\tilde{A}_{s}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}A_{s}(t)\quad&\text{for}\quad t\leq 0,\\
A_{s}(0)\quad&\text{for}\quad t>0,\end{cases}

is in
{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
for
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
. The
\tau
-spectral flow of
A_{s}
is equal modulo 2 to the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued spectral flow
\operatorname{sf}(\tilde{A}_{s})
. Hence, it is independent of
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
by the stability of the usual spectral flow under homotopy.

If
0
is a crossing for
A_{s_{0}}
, then there exist
\epsilon,\delta>0
such that

(i)

there are no non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{s_{0}}(0)
in the interval
(-\delta,\delta)
;

(ii)

there are no crossings
t
of
A_{s_{0}}
with
0<|t|\leq\epsilon
;

(iii)

\pm\delta
are not in the spectrum of
A_{s}(t)
for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
,
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
.

Then the paths

\tilde{A}_{s}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}A_{s}(t)\quad&\text{for}\quad t\leq-\epsilon,\\
A_{s}(-\epsilon)\quad&\text{for}\quad t>-\epsilon,\end{cases}

and

\hat{A}_{s}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}A_{s}(-\epsilon)\quad&\text{for}\quad t<-\epsilon,\\
A_{s}(t)\quad&\text{for}\quad-\epsilon\leq t\leq\epsilon,\\
A_{s}(\epsilon)\quad&\text{for}\quad t>\epsilon,\end{cases}

are in
{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
,
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. By (
4.7
) , we have

(5.1)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{s})\ =\ \operatorname{sf}(\tilde{A}_{s})\ +\ \sum_{-\epsilon<t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},t)\ +\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},0),\qquad\mod 2.

Since
t=0
is not a crossing for
\tilde{A}_{s}
(
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
,
\operatorname{sf}(\tilde{A}_{s})
is independent of
s
on this interval.

From (
3.3
) and (
4.6
), we see that the mod 2 reduction of usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued spectral of
\hat{A}_{s}
is given by

\operatorname{sf}(\hat{A}_{s})\ =\ \ \sum_{-\epsilon<t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},t)\ +\ \operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},0)\ +\sum_{0<t<\epsilon}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},t)\qquad\text{mod}\quad 2.

By (
4.2
),
P(A_{s},t)=\alpha\,P(A_{s},-t)\,\alpha^{-1}
. It follows that

\sum_{-\epsilon<t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},t)\ =\ \sum_{0<t<\epsilon}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},t)

and
\operatorname{sf}(\hat{A}_{s})=\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},0)
mod 2. From the stability of the usual spectral flow under homotopy, we have
\operatorname{sf}(\hat{A}_{s_{0}})=\operatorname{sf}(\hat{A}_{s})
and, hence,

(5.2)

\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s},0)\ =\ \operatorname{rank}P(A_{s_{0}},0),\qquad\text{mod}\quad 2.

Substituting this equality into (
5.1
) and using that
\operatorname{sf}(\tilde{A}_{s})
is independent of
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
, we conclude that

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{s})\ =\ \operatorname{sf}(\tilde{A}_{s_{0}})\ +\ \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}P(A_{s_{0}},0)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{s_{0}}),\qquad\mod 2.

\square

We now pass to the proof of Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
.

5.2.
The normalization axiom

Let
W=H=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and let
\alpha
and
A
be as in Theorem
4.1
(iv). Then the eigenvalues of
A(t)
are
\pm\arctan(t)
. By (
4.7
),
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)=1
. Hence, we obtain the following

Lemma 5.1
.

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
satisfies the normalization condition of Theorem
4.1
.

Also, by Lemma 2.7 of
[
9
]
we have

Lemma 5.2
.

The map
A\mapsto\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}
satisfies the normalization condition of Theorem
4.1
.

5.3.
A constant path

Let us consider the case when
A(t)=A_{0}\in{A}_{\tau}
is independent of
t
. Then
A_{0}
is bijective. It follows that
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)=0
, i.e.
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
satisfy axiom (ii) of Theorem
4.1
.

Further, if
D_{A}\xi(t)=0
, then
\xi(t)=e^{-A_{0}t}\xi(0)
, which is not square-integrable, unless
\xi(0)=0
. Hence,
\ker{}D_{A}=\{0\}
. The following lemma summarizes this subsection:

Lemma 5.3
.

The maps
A\mapsto\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)
and
A\to\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}
satisfy axiom (ii) of Theorem
4.1
.

5.4.
Proof of Theorem
4.3

By Proposition
4.2
,
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
satisfies the homotopy axiom (i) of Theorem
4.1
. By Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
,
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
satisfies axioms (ii) and (iv) of Theorem
4.1
. It is clear, that it also satisfies the direct sum axiom (iii).

\square

5.5.
Proof of Theorem
4.4

By Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
,
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A}
satisfies axioms (ii) and (iv) of Theorem
4.1
. It is clear, that it also satisfies the direct sum axiom (iii). By Theorem
2.4
it also satisfies the homotopy axiom. Thus it coincides with the half-spectral flow by Theorem
4.1
.

\square

5.6.
The proof of Theorem
4.1

Theorem
4.3
states that
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
satisfies the axioms of Theorem
4.1
. Hence, the existence of
\mu_{2}
is proven.

To prove the uniqueness we need to show that any
\mu_{2}
, satisfying the axioms of Theorem
4.1
, is equal to
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}
.

A verbatim repetition of the Robbin-Salamon proof of Theorem
3.2
, cf.
[
25
, §4]
, shows that there exists a unique map
\mu_{2}:{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, satisfying the axioms (i)-(iv) of Theorem
3.2
. This
\mu_{2}
is just the mod 2 reduction of
\mu
.

For any
B\in{A}
the
symmetrization
of
B
is the path

\tilde{B}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}B(t)\oplus\alpha\,B(-t)\,\alpha^{-1}\qquad&\text{for}\quad t\leq 0\\
\alpha\,B(-t)\,\alpha^{-1}\oplus B\qquad&\text{for}\quad t>0\end{cases}\ \ \in\ \ {A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W\oplus W,H\oplus H).

We claim that the map
B\mapsto\mu_{\tau}(\tilde{B})
satisfies all the axioms for
\mu_{2}
. Indeed, if
B_{1}
is homotopic to
B_{2}
in
{A}
, then
\tilde{B}_{1}
is homotopic to
\tilde{B}_{2}
in
{A}_{\tau}
, which implies the homotopy axiom. The rest of the axioms follow immediately from the corresponding axioms for
\mu_{\tau}
. It follows now from the uniqueness of
\mu_{2}
that

(5.3)

\mu_{2}(B)\ =\ \mu_{\tau}(\tilde{B}).

We want to compute
\mu_{\tau}(A)
in terms of
\mu_{2}
for any
A\in{A}_{\tau}
. First, consider a path
A\in{A}_{\tau}
, which is bijective at 0 and set

A_{\leq 0}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}A(t)\quad&\text{for}\quad t\leq 0,\\
A(0)\quad&\text{for}\quad t>0.\end{cases}

Then
A_{\leq 0}\in{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
and the symmetrization
\tilde{A}_{\leq 0}
is the direct sum of
A
and the constant path
A(0)
:

\tilde{A}_{\leq 0}\ =\ A\oplus A(0).

Thus, from the direct sum and the constant axiom for
\mu_{\tau}
we conclude that
\mu_{\tau}(A)=\mu_{\tau}(\tilde{A}_{\leq 0})
. Using (
5.3
), we get

(5.4)

\mu_{\tau}(A)\ =\ \mu_{\tau}(\tilde{A}_{\leq 0})\ =\ \mu_{2}(A_{\leq 0})\ \equiv\ \sum_{t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A,t)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A).

Here the third equality holds modulo 2 and follows from Theorem
3.5
and (
4.6
).

Consider a general
A\in{A}_{\tau}
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, the kernel of
A(0)
is even dimensional, and there is a subspace
L
in it such that
\ker A(0)\ =\ L\oplus\alpha{L}
. Then there exists
\epsilon>0
and a path
A^{\prime}\in{A}_{\tau}
homotopic to

, such that

is bijective for all

,
\ker{}A(-\epsilon)=L
, and
\ker{}A(\epsilon)=\tau(L)
. Then

\operatorname{rank}P(A^{\prime},-\epsilon)\ =\ \dim L\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{rank}P(A,0).

It now follows from (
5.4
), that
\mu_{\tau}(A)
is given by (
4.7
).

\square

6.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index and the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued spectral flow on a circle

In this section, we consider a
\tau
-invariant family of self-adjoint operators

A(t)
, parametrized by
t\in S^{1}
and prove an analog of Theorem
4.4

for it. For the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued spectral flow this result basically follows from the APS index theorem, cf.
[
2
, §7]
. This theorem is unavailable for the
\tau
-index. Instead, we deduce the result for the periodic families from Theorem
4.4
.

Throughout this section, the Hilbert spaces
W
and
H
are as in Section
3.1
.

6.1.
Families of operators on an interval and on a circle

Let
{A}([-\pi,\pi],W,H)
be the space of norm continuous maps
A:[-\pi,\pi]\to{S}(W,H)
. For
A\in{A}([-\pi,\pi],W,H)
set

(6.1)

\tilde{A}(t)\ :=\ \begin{cases}A(-\pi),\qquad&\text{for}\quad t<-\pi,\\
A(t),\qquad&\text{for}\quad-\pi\geq t\leq\pi,\\
A(\pi),\qquad&\text{for}\quad t>\pi.\end{cases}

Then
\tilde{A}\in{A}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
iff
A(\pi)
and
A(-\pi)
are bijective.

Let

{A}(S^{1},W,H)\ :=\ \big\{\,A\in{A}([-\pi,\pi],W,H):\,A(-\pi)=A(\pi)\,\big\}.

We view elements of
{A}(S^{1},W,H)
as families of operators on a circle.

Suppose that
\alpha:H\to H
is an anti-unitary anti-involution. As in Section
4.1
, we define anti-linear anti-involution
\tau
on the spaces
{A}([-\pi,\pi],W,H)
and
{A}(S^{1},W,H)
. Let
{A}_{\tau}([-\pi,\pi],W,H)
and
{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
denote the subspaces of
\tau
-invariant elements in

{A}([-\pi,\pi],W,H)
and
{A}(S^{1},W,H)
respectively.

Note that if
A\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
then

(6.2)

A(-\pi)\ =\ A(\pi)\ =\ \alpha\,A(\pi)\,\alpha^{-1},

i.e. the operator
A(\pi)=A(-\pi)
is odd symmetric.

6.2.
The half-spectral flow on the circle

For
A\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
which have only isolated crossings, we define the half-spectral flow by

(6.3)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)\ :=\ \ \sum_{-\pi<t<0}\operatorname{rank}P(A,t)\ +\ \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}P(A,0)\ +\ \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}P(A,-\pi),\qquad\mod 2.

A verbatim repetition of the arguments in the proof of Proposition
4.2
shows that if
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
lie in the same connected component of
{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
and have isolated crossings, then
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{0})=\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A_{1})
. As in Section
4.4
, for a general
A\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
we define the half-spectral flow
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)
as the half-spectral flow of any
A_{1}
which has isolated crossing and lies in the same connected component of
{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
as
A
.

6.3.
The operator
D_{A}
on an interval and on a circle

Let
{H}
and
{W}
be as in Section
3.3
and let
{H}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
and
{W}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
be similar spaces with
\mathbb{R}
replaced by
[-\pi,\pi]
(thus
{H}_{[-\pi,\pi]}:=L^{2}([-\pi,\pi],H)
, etc.). Let

{W}_{S^{1}}\ :=\ \big\{\xi\in{W}_{[-\pi,\pi]}:\,\xi(-\pi)=\xi(\pi)\,\big\}.

We view
{W}_{S^{1}}
as a space of
H
-valued functions on
S^{1}
. We also view
{H}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
as the space of square-integrable
H
-valued functions on
S^{1}
and use the notations
{H}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
and
{H}_{S^{1}}
for this space interchangeably.

Suppose
A\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
and consider the operator

(6.4)

D_{A,S^{1}}:\xi(t)\ \mapsto\ \frac{d}{dt}\xi(t)\ +\ A(t)\xi(t),\qquad\xi\in{W}_{S^{1}}.

It is a bounded odd symmetric operator
{W}_{S^{1}}\to{H}_{S^{1}}
. If we view
{W}_{S^{1}}
as a subspace of
{W}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
then
D_{A}
becomes an operator on the interval
[-\pi,\pi]
with periodic boundary conditions. It is well known that this operator is Fredholm. When
H
is a space of square-integrable sections of a bundle over a compact manifold this follows from the standard description of elliptic boundary conditions for PDE, cf., for example,
[
5
, §7.3]
(in
[
5
]
the periodic boundary conditions are called “transmission conditions”. cf.
[
5
, Example 7.28]
). The abstract description of elliptic boundary conditions is very similar, but simpler, cf.
[
4
, Appendix A]
).

It follows from Theorem
2.4
that the map
A\to\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(D_{A})
is constant on the connected components of
{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
.

The main result of this section is the following analog of Theorem
3.6

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
A\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
. The
\tau
-index of the operator
D_{A,S^{1}}:{W}_{S^{1}}\to{H}_{S^{1}}
is equal to the half-spectral flow
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)
.

The rest of this section is occupied with the proof of this theorem.

6.4.
The case when
A(-\pi)=A(\pi)
is invertible

Suppose, first, that
A(-\pi)=A(\pi)
is invertible. Then the operator (
6.1
) is in
{A}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R},W,H)
and
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(\tilde{A})=\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A)
. Hence, from Theorem
3.6
we conclude that

(6.5)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{\tilde{A}}\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A.

We now recall the abstract Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) boundary conditions, cf.
[
28
, §4]
. Let
{H}_{<0}\subset H
(resp.
{H}_{>0}\subset H
) denote the span of eigenvectors of
A(-\pi)=A(\pi)
with negative (resp. positive) eigenvalues. Set

(6.6)

\displaystyle{W}_{APS}

\displaystyle:=\ \big\{\,f\in{W}_{[-\pi,\pi]}:\,f(-\pi)\in{H}_{<0},\ f(\pi)\in{H}_{>0}\,\big\},

\displaystyle{W}_{APS}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle:=\ \big\{\,f\in{W}_{[-\pi,\pi]}:\,f(-\pi)\in{H}_{>0},\ f(\pi)\in{H}_{<0}\,\big\}.

Then
\tau:{W}_{APS}\to{W}_{APS}^{\dagger}
.

The operator
D_{A,APS}:{W}_{APS}\to{H}
is called the operator
D_{A}
with the
APS boundary conditions
. The adjoint of this operator is the operator

D_{A,APS}^{*}=-\frac{d}{dt}+A(t):{W}_{APS}^{\dagger}\to{H}.

It follows that
\tau D_{A,APS}\ \tau^{-1}=D_{A,APS}^{*}
, i.e.
the operator
D_{A,APS}
is odd symmetric
.

Recall that the operator
\tilde{A}
is defined in (
6.1
). By Proposition 4.5 of
[
28
]
, the dimension of the kernel of
D_{A,APS}
is equal to the dimension of the kernel of the operator
D_{\tilde{A}}
. Hence, the
\tau
-indices of these operators are equal

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A,APS}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{\tilde{A}}.

Using (
6.5
) we obtain

(6.7)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A,APS}\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}A.

Theorem
6.1
for the case when
A(\pi)
is invertible follows now from the following

Proposition 6.2
.

If
A(\pi)=A(-\pi)
is invertible, then
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A,APS}=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{A,S^{1}}
.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem 8.17 of
[
5
]
(see also the proof of Theorem 5.11 in
[
10
]
) the similar statement is proven for the usual index in the case when
H
is the space of square-integrable sections over a manifold so that
D_{A}
is a PDE. The proof goes by contracting a continuous family of elliptic boundary conditions
B_{s}
which interpolates between the periodic and the APS boundary conditions:
B_{0}
is the APS boundary conditions and
B_{1}
is the periodic boundary conditions. This construction also works in our case, where the proof of the fact that the intermediate boundary conditions
B_{s}
are Fredholm is exactly the same as in the PDE case. The details can be found in cf.
[
4
, Appendix A]
). Then one obtains a family of operators
D_{A,B_{s}}
with different domains. One then shows that there is a unitary equivalent continuous family of operators
D_{A,s}
of operators with domain
{W}_{S^{1}}
. By construction, all the operators
D_{A,B_{s}}
are odd symmetric. Thus, the proposition follows from the stability of the
\tau
-index (Theorem
2.4
).
∎

6.5.
The case when
A(\pi)
is not invertible

Suppose
A(-\pi)=A(\pi)
is not invertible. Since
A(\pi)
has a discrete spectrum, for small enough
\epsilon>0
, the operator
A(\pi)+\epsilon
is invertible. The path
A+\epsilon\in{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
is in the same connected component of
{A}_{\tau}(S^{1},W,H)
as
A
. Hence

(6.8)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A+\epsilon)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A),\qquad\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(D_{A})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(D_{A+\epsilon}).

It is shown in Section
6.4
that
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(A+\epsilon)=\operatorname{ind}(D_{A+\epsilon})
. Theorem
6.1
follows now from (
6.8
).
\square

7.
The half-spectral flow of a family of Toeplitz operators

This section studies a family of odd symmetric Toeplitz operators on a complete Riemannian manifold. Our main result is that the graded half-spectral flow of such a family is equal to the
\tau
-index of a certain Callias-type operator. This is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the main result of
[
11
]
. In the next section, we apply it to obtain a new proof and a generalization of the bulk-edge correspondence in the Graf-Porta model of topological insulators with time-reversal symmetry
[
21
]
.

7.1.
A (generalized) Dirac operator

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Recall from
[
24
, §II.5]
that a graded bundle
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
over
M
is called a Dirac bundle if it is a Hermitian vector bundle endowed with the Clifford action

c:T^{*}M\to\operatorname{End}(E),\qquad\big(\,c(v):E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp},\quad c(v)^{2}=-|v|^{2},\quad c(v)^{*}=-c(v)\,\big),

and a Hermitian connection
\nabla^{E}=\nabla^{E^{+}}\oplus\nabla^{E^{-}}
compatible with the Clifford action. We assume that the Hermitian metric is
\theta^{E}
-invariant

We extend the Clifford action to the product
E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
and denote by
D
the associated (generalized) Dirac operator. In local coordinates, it can be written as
D=\sum_{j}c(dx^{j})\nabla^{E}_{\partial_{j}}
. We view
D
as an unbounded self-adjoint operator
D:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
.

From this point on we make the following

Assumption 7.1
.

Zero is an isolated point of the spectrum of
D
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-}\subset L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
denote the kernel of
D
and let
P:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\mathcal{H}
be the orthogonal projection. Here
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
is the intersection of
\mathcal{H}
with
L^{2}(M,E^{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
. We denote by
P^{\pm}:L^{2}(M,E^{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
the restriction of
P
to
L^{2}(M,E^{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
.

7.2.
Matrix valued functions

Let
BC(M,k)
denote the Banach algebra of continuous bounded functions
f(x)
on
M
with values in the space
\operatorname{Herm}(k)
of Hermitian complex-valued
k\times{}k
-matrices.

Let
C_{0}(M,k)
denote the closure of the subalgebra of functions with compact support in
BC(M,k)
.

Let
C_{g}^{\infty}(M,k)\subset BC(M,k)
denote the space of smooth functions with values in
\operatorname{Herm}(k)
which are bounded and such that
df
vanishes at infinity. Then
C_{0}(M,k)\subset C_{g}(M,k)
.

For
f\in C^{\infty}_{g}(M;k)
we denote by
M_{f}:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
the multiplication by
1\otimes{f}
and by
M_{f}^{\pm}
the restriction of
M_{f}
to
L^{2}(M,E^{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
.

7.3.
The Toeplitz operator

Recall that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-}
denotes the kernel of
D
and
P:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\mathcal{H}
is the orthogonal projection.

Definition 7.2
.

The operator

(7.1)

T_{f}\ :=\ PM_{f}P:\,\mathcal{H}\ \to\mathcal{H}

is called the
Toeplitz operator defined by
f
. We denote by
T_{f}^{\pm}
the restriction of
T_{f}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
. This is a self-adjoint odd symmetric operator which is even with respect to the grading
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-}
.

Definition 7.3
.

We say that a matrix-valued function
f\in C^{\infty}_{g}(M;k)
is
invertible at infinity
if there exists a compact set
K\subset M
and
C_{1}>0
such that
f(x)
is an invertible matrix for all
x\not\in K
and

(7.2)

\big\|f(x)^{-1}\big\|<C_{1}\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\not\in K.

The following result is proven in
[
13
, Lemma 2.6]

Proposition 7.4
.

If
D
satisfies Assumption
7.1
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{g}(M;k)
is invertible at infinity then the Toeplitz operator
T_{f}
is Fredholm.

7.4.
A quaternionic bundle structure

Assume now that
\theta^{M}:M\to M
is an involution. Let
\theta^{E}:E\to\theta^{*}E
be an anti-linear
involution
(not anti-involution!), which preserves the Hermitian metric on
E
. We assume that
\theta^{E}
has an odd grading degree, i.e.
\theta^{E}:E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
. Let
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
be an anti-unitary anti-involution. By Lemma
2.1
, it implies that
k
is even. The tensor product

(7.3)

\theta^{E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}}\ :=\ \theta^{E}\otimes\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}

is an anti-unitary anti-involution of odd grading degree. As in (
2.4
) it induces an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau:\Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
.

Assumption 7.5
.

The generalized Dirac operator
D
on
E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
is graded odd symmetric,
\tau D\tau^{-1}=D
.

7.5.
An odd symmetric family of matrix-valued functions

Let
S^{1}=\{e^{it}:t\in[-\pi,\pi]\}
be the unit circle. Consider the involution
\theta^{S^{1}}:t\mapsto-t
on
S^{1}
. Then
(S^{1},\theta^{S^{1}})
is an involutive manifold.

Set
{M}=S^{1}\times M
. We write points of
{M}
as
(t,x)
,
t\in S^{1},\ x\in M
, and view a smooth matrix-valued function
\mathcalboondox{f}:{M}\to\operatorname{Herm}(k)
as a smooth family
f_{t}:M\to\operatorname{Herm}(k)
,
t\in S^{1}
. Thus we usually write this function as
f_{t}(x)
. We are interested in the functions with
f_{t}\in C^{\infty}_{g}(M;k)
.

The involutions
\theta^{M}
and
\theta^{S^{1}}
define an involution
\theta^{M}:=\theta^{S^{1}}\times\theta^{M}
on
{M}
. As usual, we define the anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
on the spaces of matrix-valued functions on
{M}
by

(7.4)

(\tau f)_{t}(x)\ =\ \theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,f_{\theta^{S^{1}}t}(\theta^{M}x)\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}.

Definition 7.6
.

A smooth family
\mathcalboondox{f}:S^{1}\to C^{\infty}_{g}(M;k)
,
t\mapsto f_{t}
, of matrix-valued functions is called
odd symmetric
if
\tau f=f
.

We denote by
{F}_{\tau}(M,k)
the set of smooth families
\mathcalboondox{f}=\{f_{t}\}
of odd symmetric matrix-valued functions, such that
f_{t}
is invertible at infinity of
M
for all
t\in S^{1}
and there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(7.5)

\big\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{t}(x)\big\|<C_{2},\qquad\text{for all}\quad\in S^{1},\ x\in M.

Suppose
\mathcalboondox{f}=\{f_{t}\}\in{F}_{\tau}(M;k)
. It follows from (
7.2
) and (
7.5
) that there exists a compact set
K\subset M
and a large enough constant
\alpha>0
such that

(7.6)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{t}(x)\ <\ \frac{\alpha}{2}\,f_{t}(x)^{2},\qquad\text{for all}\ \ x\not\in K.

Here the inequality
A<B
between two self-adjoint matrices means that for any vector
v\not=0
, we have
\langle Av,v\rangle<\langle Bv,v\rangle
.

7.6.
The graded half-spectral flow of a family of self-adjoint Toeplitz operators

If
\mathcalboondox{f}\in{F}_{\tau}(M;k)
then
T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}
(
t\in S^{1}
) is a periodic family of odd symmetric self-adjoint Fredholm operators (the Fredholmness follows from Proposition
7.4
). Recall that we denote by
T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{\pm}
the restriction of
T{f}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
.
Our goal is to compute the graded half-spectral flow

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}T{f}^{+}\ -\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}T{f}^{-}

of this family.

7.7.
A Callias-type operator on
S^{1}\times M

Recall that
{M}=S^{1}\times M
is an involutive manifold with
\theta^{{M}}=\theta^{S^{1}}\times\theta^{M}
. Denote by
\pi_{1}:S^{1}\times M\to S^{1}
and
\pi_{2}:S^{1}\times M\to M
the natural projections. By a slight abuse of notation, we denote the pull-backs
\pi^{*}_{1}dt,\,\pi_{2}^{*}dx\in T^{*}{M}
by
dt
and
dx
respectively. Set

{E}:=\ \pi_{2}^{*}E.

Then
{E}
is naturally an ungraded quaternionic Dirac bundle with Clifford action
\mathcalboondox{c}:T^{*}{M}\to\operatorname{End}({E})
such that
\mathcalboondox{c}(dx)=c(dx)
and
\mathcalboondox{c}(dt)
is given with respect to the decomposition
{E}=\pi_{2}^{*}E^{+}\oplus\pi_{2}^{*}E^{-}
by the matrix

\mathcalboondox{c}(dt)\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}i\cdot\operatorname{Id}&0\\
0&-i\cdot\operatorname{Id}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
{D}
be the corresponding Dirac operator. With respect to the decomposition

(7.7)

L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\ =\ L^{2}(S^{1})\otimes L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}).

it takes the form

(7.8)

{D}\ =\ \mathcalboondox{c}(dt)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\otimes 1\ +\ 1\otimes D.

It follows that
{D}
is odd symmetric. We remark that, as opposed to
D
, the operator
{D}
is not graded.

Let now
\mathcalboondox{f}=\{f_{t}\}\in{F}_{\tau}(M;k)
. We view it as a smooth function on
{M}
. Let
{M}{f}:L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
denote the multiplication by
\mathcalboondox{f}
. It is an odd symmetric operator. The commutator

[{D},{M}{f}]\ :=\ {D}\circ{M}{f}-{M}{f}\circ{D}

is a zero-order differential operator, i.e., a bundle map
{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\to{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

From (
7.6
) and our assumption that
df_{t}
vanishes at infinity, we conclude that there exist constants
c,d>0
and a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset{M}
, called
an essential support
of
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
, such that

(7.9)

\big[{D},c\,{M}{f}\big](t,x)\ <\ c^{2}{{M}{f}(t,x)}^{2}-d,\qquad\text{for all}\ \ (t,x)\not\in\mathcal{K}.

It follows that

(7.10)

{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}\ :=\ {D}\ +\ i\,c\,{M}{f}

is a
Callias-type operator
in the sense of
[
1
,
12
]
(see also
[
7
, §2.5]
). In particular, it is Fredholm. It is also odd symmetric. The
\tau
-index of odd symmetric Callias-type operators was studied in
[
9
, §5]
.

The main result of this section is the following

Theorem 7.7
.

Suppose
\mathcalboondox{f}\in{F}_{\tau}(M;k)
be a smooth periodic family of invertible at infinity matrix-valued functions. Suppose that Assumptions
7.1
and
7.5
are satisfied. Then

(7.11)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{+})\ -\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{-})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}}.

Remark 7.8
.

Since both sides of (
7.11
) live in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we can replace the minus sign on the left-hand side with the plus sign. We prefer to write the minus sum to stress the analogy with the
\mathbb{Z}
-valued case
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
and to emphasize that this expression gives the
graded half-spectral flow
of the family
T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}
.

Remark 7.9
.

Note that, as opposed to
df
, the differential
d\mathcalboondox{f}
does not vanish at infinity. Because of this
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
does not satisfy the conditions of Corollary 2.7 of
[
13
]
and its index does not vanish in general.

Remark 7.10
.

In many interesting applications, eg. in the generalized Graf-Porta model,
H^{-}=\{0\}
. Hence,
\operatorname{sf}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{-})=0
and (
7.11
) computes
\operatorname{sf}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{+})
.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
7.7
in Section
10
, we present some special cases and applications of this theorem.

8.
The even dimensional case: the generalized bulk-edge correspondence

Suppose that the dimension of
M
is even. Then the dimension of
{M}
is odd and by the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued version of the Callias-type index theorem
[
9
, §5]
the
\tau
-index of
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
is equal to the index of a certain Dirac operator on a compact hypersurface
{N}\subset{M}
. It follows from Theorem
7.7
that the half-spectral flow of a family of Toeplitz operators on
M
is equal to the
\tau
-index of a Dirac-type operator on
{N}
. We refer to this equality as a generalized bulk-edge correspondence, since, as we explain in the next section, in the special case when

is a unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
this reduces to the bulk-edge correspondence for the Graf-Porta model of topological insulators with time-reversal symmetry
[
21
,
22
]
.

8.1.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued Callias-type theorem

We recall the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued version of the Callias-type theorem from
[
9
]
.

Let
N\subset M
be a
\theta^{M}
-invariant hypersurface such that there is an essential support
\mathcal{K}\subset{M}
of
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
whose boundary
\partial{}\mathcal{K}={N}:=S^{1}\times{}N
. In particular, the restriction of
\mathcalboondox{f}
to
{N}
is invertible and satisfies (
7.9
). Then there are quaternionic vector bundles
{F}_{{N}\pm}
over
{N}
such that

{M}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}\ =\ {F}_{{N}+}\oplus{F}_{{N}-},

and the restriction of
\mathcalboondox{f}
to
{F}_{{N}+}
(respectively,
{F}_{N-}
) is positive definite (respectively, negative definite). Since
\mathcalboondox{f}
is a
\tau
-invariant function, the restriction of
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}
to
{F}_{{N}\pm}
defines a quaternionic structure on these vector bundles.

Let
E_{N}
denote the restriction of the bundle
E
to
N
. Then
{E}_{N}:=\pi_{2}^{*}E_{N}
is the restriction of
{E}
to
{N}
. It is naturally a Dirac bundle over
{N}
. The restriction of
\theta^{E}\otimes\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}
to
{E}_{N}\otimes{F}_{N+}
defines a structure of a quaternionic bundle on
{E}_{N}\otimes{F}_{N+}
. The induced Dirac-type operator
{D}_{N}
on

is odd symmetric.

Let
v
denote the unit normal vector to
{N}
pointing towards
\mathcal{K}
. Then

is an involution. We denote by
{E}_{N}^{\pm 1}
the eigenspace of

with eigenvalue
\pm 1
. This defines a grading
{E}_{N}={E}_{N}^{+1}\oplus{E}_{N}^{-1}
on the Dirac bundle
{E}_{N}
.
The operator
{D}_{N}
is odd with respect to the induced grading on
{E}_{N}\otimes{F}_{{N}+}
. (This grading is different from the one induced by the grading on

. Note that the operator
{D}_{N}
is not an odd operator with respect to the grading induced by the grading on
E
). We denote by
{D}_{N}^{\pm}
the restriction of
{D}_{N}
to
{E}_{N}^{\pm 1}\otimes{F}_{{N}+}
.

Theorem 5.3 of
[
9
]
states that

(8.1)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{D}_{{N}}^{+}.

8.2.
Computation of the graded spectral flow in even dimensional case

We now get the following corollary of Theorem
7.7
.

Corollary 8.1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
7.7
assume that
\dim{}M
is even. Let
j:N\hookrightarrow M
be a hypersurface such that there is an essential support
\mathcal{K}\subset{M}
of
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
whose boundary
\partial{}\mathcal{K}={N}:=S^{1}\times{}N
. Then

(8.2)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{+})\ -\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{\mathcalboondox{f}}^{-})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{D}_{{N}}^{+}.

8.3.
The pseudo-convex domain

Let
M
be a bounded strongly pseudo-convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with smooth boundary
N:=\partial{}\overline{M}
. Let
g^{M}
be the Bergman metric on
M
, cf.

[
27
, §7]
. We define
E=\Lambda^{n,*}(T^{*}M)
and set
E^{+}=\Lambda^{n,{\operatorname{even}}}(T^{*}M)
,
E^{-}=\Lambda^{n,{\operatorname{odd}}}(T^{*}M)
. Then
E
is naturally a Dirac bundle over
M
whose space of smooth section coincides with the Dolbeault
complex
\Omega^{n,*}(M)
of
M
with coefficients in the canonical bundle
K=\Lambda^{n,0}(T^{*}M)
. Moreover, the corresponding Dirac operator is given by

D\ =\ \bar{\partial}\ +\ \bar{\partial}^{*},

where
\bar{\partial}
is the Dolbeault differential and
\bar{\partial}^{*}
its adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-metric induced by the Bergman metric on
M
. Then
(M,g^{M})
is a complete Kähler manifold.

By
[
19
, §5]
zero is an isolated point of the spectrum of
D
and
\mathcal{H}:=\ker D
is a subset of the space of
(n,0)
-forms

(8.3)

\mathcal{H}\ :=\ \ker D\ \subset\ \Omega^{n,0}(M).

In particular, Assumption
7.1
is satisfied and
\mathcal{H}^{-}=\{0\}
.

Let
\theta^{M}:M\to M
be an anti-holomorphic metric preserving involution. We define an anti-unitary involution (not anti-involution!) on
E
by

\theta^{E}:\,\omega\ \mapsto\ \overline{(\theta^{M})^{*}\omega},\qquad\omega\in\Lambda^{n,*}(T^{*}M),

where the bar denotes the complex conjugation and
(\theta^{M})^{*}
is the pull-back. Note that, since
\theta^{M}
is anti-holomorphic,
(\theta^{M})^{*}
sends
(n,*)
-forms to
(*,n)
-forms and the complex conjugation sends them back to
(n,*)
-forms.

Let
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
be an anti-unitary anti-involution and let
\theta^{E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}}
be given by (
7.3
). Then
E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
is a quaternionic bundle and
D\otimes 1
is an odd symmetric operator on it.
In view of (
8.3
), Corollary
8.1
implies the following

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\bar{\mathcalboondox{f}}(t,x)
be a smooth
\tau
-invariant
\operatorname{Herm}(k)
-valued function on
{N}:=S^{1}\times N
such that
\bar{\mathcalboondox{f}}(t,x)
is invertible for all
(t\in S^{1}
,
x\in N
). Let
\mathcalboondox{f}:{M}\to\operatorname{Herm}(k)
be a smooth
\tau
-invariant extension of
\bar{\mathcalboondox{f}}
to
{M}
(i.e.
\mathcalboondox{f}|_{N}=\bar{\mathcalboondox{f}}
). Then
\mathcalboondox{f}\in{F}_{\tau}(M,k)
and

(8.4)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}T{f}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{D}_{N}^{+}.

We refer to this result as the
generalized bulk-edge correspondence
for the reason explained in the next section.

9.
The Graf-Porta model of topological insulators with time-reversal symmetry

In this section, we briefly review a tight-binding model for two-dimensional topological insulators with fermionic time-reversal symmetry, following the description in
[
22
]
(see also
[
21
]
) and show that the bulk-edge correspondence for this model follows immediately from our Corollary
8.2
.

9.1.
The bulk Hamiltonian

We consider the following
tight binding model
: The “bulk” state space is the space
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})
of square-integrable sequences

\phi\ =\ \big\{\phi_{mn}\big\}_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\phi_{mn}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

The
bulk Hamiltonian

H:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\to l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})
is defined as follows: consider a sequence of matrices
A_{p,q}:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
such that
\sum_{pq}\|A_{p,q}\|<\infty
and define
H
by

(H\phi)_{mn}\ =\ \sum_{p,q}\,A_{p,q}\,\phi_{m-p,n-q}.

We assume that
H
is self-adjoint, which is equivalent to the equality

(9.1)

A_{-p,-q}\ =\ A_{p,q}^{*}.

The Fourier transform of
H
is a family of self-adjoint
k\times k
-matrices

H(t,s)\ :=\ \sum_{pq}A_{p,q}\,e^{ipt}\,e^{iqs}\qquad(t,s)\in[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]\ \simeq\ S^{1}\times S^{1}.

depending smoothly on
s
and
t
.

We assume that the bulk Hamiltonian has a spectral gap at Fermi level
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e. there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the spectrum of
H(s,t)
does not intersect the interval
(\mu-\epsilon,\mu+\epsilon)
for all
(s,t)\in S^{1}\times S^{1}
. In particular, the operator
H(s,t)-\mu
is invertible for all
(s,t)\in S^{1}\times S^{1}
. Thus the trivial bundle
(S^{1}\times{}S^{1})\times\mathbb{C}^{k}
over the torus
S^{1}\times{}S^{1}
decomposes into the direct sum of subbundles

(S^{1}\times{}S^{1})\times\mathbb{C}^{k}\ =\ {F}_{+}\oplus{F}_{-}

such that the restriction of
H(s,t)-\mu
to
{F}_{+}
is positive definite and the restriction of
H(s,t)-\mu
to
{F}_{-}
is negative define. The bundle
{F}_{+}
is called the
Bloch bundle
.

9.2.
The time-reversal symmetry

Define the involution
\theta^{S^{1}\times S^{1}}:S^{1}\times S^{1}\to S^{1}\times S^{1}
by
\theta^{S^{1}\times S^{1}}(t,s)=(-t,-s)
(
(s,t)\in[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]
). Let

\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}:\,\mathbb{C}^{k}\ \to\ \mathbb{C}^{k}

be an anti-unitary anti-involution (hence,
k
is even).

The anti-unitary anti-involution

\tau:\,\phi(t,s)\ \to\ \theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\phi(-t,-s)

on the space
L^{2}(S^{1}\times S^{1},\mathbb{C}^{k})
of square-integrable
\mathbb{C}^{k}
-valued functions on
S^{1}\times S^{1}
is called a
time-reversal symmetry
. We assume that
H(s,t)
is
\tau
-invariant. Equivalently, this means that

(9.2)

\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,A_{p,q}\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}\ =\ A_{-p,-q}^{*}\ =\ A_{p,q},

where in the last equality we used (
9.1
).

Remark 9.1
.

In
[
21
]
the authors consider the operators
H_{q}(t):=\sum_{p}A_{p,q}e^{ipt}
. Then the time-reversal symmetry condition (
9.2
) becomes

\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,H_{q}(t)\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}\ =\ H_{q}(-t),

which agrees with Definition 2.4 of
[
21
]
.

Notice that
\tau
preserves the subbundles
{F}_{\pm}
of the trivial bundle over
S^{1}\times S^{1}
. Hence,
{F}_{\pm}
a quaternionic bundles over
S^{1}\times S^{1}
(in particular, their ranks are even).

9.3.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued bulk index

Let
M=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|<1\}
be the unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
. It is a pseudo-convex domain with boundary
\partial M=S^{1}
. As usual, we set
{M}:=S^{1}\times M
so that
{N}:=\partial{M}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
. The function

(9.3)

\mathcalboondox{f}(t,z)\ :=\ \sum_{pq}A_{p,q}\,e^{ipt}\,z^{q},\qquad t\in[-\pi,\pi],\ z\in M,

is an extension of
f(t,s):=H(t,s)
from
\partial{M}
to
{M}
as in Corollary
8.2
. Let
T{f}
and
{D}_{N}
be as in Section
8.3
. Then
{D}_{N}
is an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator acting on the sections of the bundle
{F}_{+}
over
\partial{M}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
.

Definition 9.2
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued bulk index
of the Hamiltonian
H
is

(9.4)

\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Bulk},\tau}\ :=\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{D}_{N}^{+}.

Graf and Porta,
[
21
, §4]
, give a different, more combinatorial, definition of the bulk index.

Lemma 9.3
.

Our definition of the bulk index is equivalent to the Definition 4.8 of
[
21
]
.

Proof.

Since the set of Dirac-type operators on the space of differential forms
\Omega^{n,*}(M,{F}_{+})
is connected, the bulk index depends only on the pair
({F}_{+},\tau)
.

More generally, for any quaternionic bundle
{F}
over
{N}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
, there exists a complementary quaternionic bundle
{F}^{\prime}
such that
{F}\oplus{F}^{\prime}\simeq{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}
and function
H:{M}\to\operatorname{Herm}(k)
such that
{F}={F}_{+}
(where
{F}_{+}
is as in Section
9.1
). Thus the bulk index can be viewed as a map from the set of quaternionic bundles over
S^{1}\times S^{1}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The Grothendieck group of quaternionic vector bundles over an involutive manifold
X
is the symplectic K-theory
KSp^{0}(X)
, see
[
22
, §2]
and references therein. In particular,

KSp^{0}(S^{1}\times S^{1})\ =\ \mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2},

where the
\mathbb{Z}
summand corresponds to the product bundles
E=(S^{1}\times S^{1})\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, cf.
[
22
, §7.1]
.

Recall that, by Lemma
2.1
, the rank of any quaternionic bundle over
S^{1}\times S^{1}
is even. It follows that for a product bundle, the kernel of the Dirac operator on
\Omega^{n,*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})=\Omega^{n,*}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
is even-dimensional. Thus the bulk index vanishes on product bundles. Hence, the bulk index defines a map

\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Bulk},\tau}:\,KSp^{0}(S^{1}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}.

There is only one non-trivial map like this. Example 3 of
[
9
]
shows that the bulk index is not always equal to zero and thus defines a unique non-trivial map from
KSp^{0}(S^{1}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

The Graf-Porta construction of the bulk index,
[
21
, Definition 4.8]
also defines a non-trivial map
KSp^{0}(S^{1}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(see also
[
22
, §7.1]
where the Graf-Porta bulk index is described as a map from
KSp^{0}(S^{1}\times S^{1})/\mathbb{Z}
). Hence, this index must coincide with
\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Bulk},\tau}
.
∎

9.4.
The edge Hamiltonian

We now take the boundary into account. The “edge” state space is the space
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})
of square integrable sequences of vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
on the half-lattice
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

The Fourier transform of the bulk Hamiltonian
H:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\to l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})
in the direction “along the edge”

(9.5)

H_{q}(t)\ :=\ \sum_{p}A_{p,q}\,e^{ipt},\qquad t\in[-\pi,\pi],

transforms
H
into a family of self-adjoint translation-invariant operators

H(t):\,l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\ \to\ l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k}).\qquad\big(H(t)\phi\big)_{n}\ :=\ \sum_{p}H_{p}(t)\,\phi_{n-p}.

Then
H(t)
depends smoothly on
t\in S^{1}
. It follows from (
9.1
) that
H(-t)=H(t)^{*}
and (
9.2
) is equivalent to

(9.6)

\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,H(t)\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}\ =\ H(-t).

(compare to Definition 2.4 of
[
21
]
).

We view
S^{1}\simeq[-\pi,\pi]
as and involutive manifold with involution
\theta^{S^{1}}:t\to-t
. The anti-involution
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}
defines a structure of a quaternionic bundle on the trivial bundle
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{k}
over
S^{1}
. As usual, we define an anti-involution

\tau:\,L^{2}\big(S^{1},l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\big)\ \to\ L^{2}\big(S^{1},l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\big),\qquad\tau:\,f(t)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}f(-t).

Then
H(t)
is
\tau
-invariant by (
9.6
).

Let
\Pi:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{k})\to l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{C}^{k})
denote the projection. Clearly,
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,\Pi\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}=\Pi
.

Definition 9.4
.

The
edge Hamiltonian
is the family of Toeplitz operators

(9.7)

H^{\#}(t)\ :=\ \Pi\circ H(t)\circ\Pi:\,l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{C}^{k})\ \to\ l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{C}^{k}),\qquad t\in S^{1}.

Then

(9.8)

\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,H^{\#}(t)\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}\ =\ H^{\#}(-t).

Definition 9.5
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued edge index

\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Edge},\tau}
of the Hamiltonian
H
is the half-spectral flow of the edge Hamiltonian

(9.9)

\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Edge},\tau}\ :=\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}\big(H^{\#}(t)\big).

Theorem 9.6

(Bulk-edge correspondence)
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{\rm Bulk},\tau}\ =\ \mathcal{I}_{{\rm Edge},\tau}.

Proof.

Consider the unit disc
B:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|\leq 1\}
. This is a strongly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}
. We view the bulk Hamiltonian
H(s,t)
as a function on
\partial B\times{}S^{1}
with values in the set
\operatorname{Herm}(k)
of invertible Hermitian
k\times{}k
-matrices.

We endow
B
with the Bergman metric and consider the Dolbeault-Dirac operator
D=\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{*}
on the space
L^{2}\Omega^{1,*}(B,\mathbb{C}^{k})=L^{2}\Omega^{1,0}(B,\mathbb{C}^{k})\oplus L^{2}\Omega^{1,1}(B,\mathbb{C}^{k})
of square-integrable
(1,*)
-forms on
B
. Let
\mathcal{H}:=\ker D
. Then
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}\Omega^{1,0}(B,\mathbb{C}^{k})
, cf.
[
19
, §5]
. Let
P:L^{2}\Omega^{1,*}(B,\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\mathcal{H}
denote the orthogonal projection.

As in (
9.3
) we define a family of functions on the unit disc by

f_{t}(z)\ :=\ \ \sum_{pq}A_{p,q}\,e^{ipt}\,z^{q},\qquad t\in[-\pi,\pi],\ z\in M,

and consider the family of Toeplitz operators

T_{f_{t}}\ :=\ P\circ M_{f}\circ P:\,\mathcal{H}\ \to\ \mathcal{H},\qquad t\in[-\pi,\pi],

This family is closely related to the family
H^{\sharp}(t)
as we shall now explain.

The map
\varphi:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathcal{H}
which sends

u=\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{C}^{k})
to the 1-form

\phi(u)\ :=\ \sum_{j\geq 0}\,u_{j}\,z^{j}\,dz\ \in\ \mathcal{H},

is an isomorphism of vector spaces. By
[
15
]
, the difference

T_{f_{t}}\ -\ \phi\circ H^{\#}(t)\circ\phi^{-1}:\,\mathcal{H}\ \to\ \mathcal{H},\qquad t\in[-\partial,\pi].

is a continuous family of compact operators. By Proposition
4.2
,

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}})\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}\big(\,\phi\circ H^{\#}(t)\circ\phi^{-1}\,\big)\ =\ \operatorname{sf}_{\tau}\big(H^{\#}(t)\big).

The theorem follows now from definitions of bulk and edge indexes, (
9.4
), (
9.9
), and Corollary
8.2
.
∎

10.
The proof of Theorem
7.7

As in
[
11
, §5]
, the proof of Theorem
7.7
consists of two steps. First (Lemma
10.1
) we apply Theorem
6.1
to conclude that the half-spectral flow
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}})
is equal to the
\tau
-index of a certain odd symmetric operator on
{M}
. Then, using the argument similar to
[
13
]
we show that the latter index is equal to the
\tau
-index of
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}
.

10.1.
The spectral flow as an index

Recall that the space
L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
has an involution
\tau
.
We view the space
L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H})
of square integrable functions with values in
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(M,{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
as a subspace of
L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
. Then
\tau
induces an anti-involution on
L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H})
which we still denote by
\tau
.
The family
T_{f_{t}}
naturally induces an operator
L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H})
which we still denote by
T_{f_{t}}
. Let
T_{f_{t}}^{\pm}
denote the restriction of
T_{f_{t}}
to
L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{\pm})
. These operators are odd symmetric.

Theorem
6.1
implies the following

Lemma 10.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7.7
we have

(10.1)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\,\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+T_{f_{t}}^{\pm}\big)\Big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{\pm})}.

Since
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})=-\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(-T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})
, the spectral flows
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})
and
\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(-T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})
are equal modulo 2. Hence, (
10.1
) is equivalent to

(10.2)

\operatorname{sf}_{\tau}(T_{f_{t}}^{\pm})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\,\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-T_{f_{t}}^{\pm}\big)\Big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{\pm})}.

Lemma 10.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7.7
we have

(10.3)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\,\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-T_{f_{t}}^{+}\big)\Big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{+})}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\,\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+T_{f_{t}}^{-}\big)\Big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{-})}.

Proof.

Recall that we denote by
P
the orthogonal projection
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\mathcal{H}
. To simplify the notation we write
P
also for the operator
1\otimes P
on

L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
and set
Q:=1-P
. Then
L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H})
coincides with the image of the projection

P:\,L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\ \to\ L^{2}({M},{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}).

Set

A\ :=\ \mathcalboondox{c}(dt)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ +\ i\,c\,{M}{f}.

Then
{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}=1\otimes D+A
. Consider a one-parameter family of operators

{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\ :=\ 1\otimes D\ +\ u\,A,\qquad 0\leq u\leq 1.

It is shown in the proof of Lemma 5.3 of
[
11
]
that the operator

{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\ -\ P\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ P|_{\mathrm{Im}P}\ -\ Q\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}

is compact. Hence,

(10.4)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ P|_{\mathrm{Im}P}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}Q\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}.

The operator
Q\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},0}\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}=Q\circ D\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}
is invertible. Hence, it is Fredholm and its
\tau
-index is equal to 0. We conclude that

Q\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}
(
0\leq u\leq 1
) is a continuous family of Fredholm odd symmetric operators with

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}Q\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},u}\circ Q|_{\mathrm{Im}Q}\ =\ 0.

From (
10.4
) we now obtain

(10.5)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f}}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},1}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P\circ{B}_{c\mathcalboondox{f},1}\circ P|_{\mathrm{Im}P}\\
=\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P^{+}\circ\big(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-ic{M}{f}\big)\circ P^{+}\big|_{\mathrm{Im}P^{+}}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P^{-}\circ\big(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-ic{M}{f}\big)\circ P^{-}\big|_{\mathrm{Im}P^{-}}\\
=\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-T_{f_{t}}^{+}\big)\big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{+})}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+T_{f_{t}}^{-}\big)\big|_{L^{2}(S^{1},\mathcal{H}^{-})}.

∎

10.2.
Proof of Theorem
7.7

Theorem
7.7
follows now from (
10.1
), (
10.2
), and (
10.3
).
\square

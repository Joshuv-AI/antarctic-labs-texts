1.
Introduction

Let
a<b
be two relatively prime positive integers and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
the set of non-negative integers. We denote by
\gcd(a,b)
the greatest common divisor of
a
and
b
.
For a non-negative integer
\ell
, the
\ell
-numerical semigroup, denoted by
S_{\ell}(a,b)
, is the set of the non-negative integers
n
whose number of integral representations
ax+by=n\,(x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
is more than
\ell
. The set
\ell
-gaps
G_{\ell}(a,b):=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\backslash S_{\ell}(a,b)
is the set of the non-negative integers
n
whose number of integral representations
ax+by=n\,(x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
is less than or equal to
\ell
.
The
\ell
-Frobenius number
g_{\ell,a,b}
is the largest element in
G_{\ell}(a,b)
and the
\ell
-genus
n_{\ell,a,b}
(or
\ell
-Sylvester number) is the number of the elements in
G_{\ell}(a,b)
. Sylvester
[
17
]
proved that
g_{0,a,b}=ab-a-b
, and further showed that exactly one of
n
and
g_{0,a,b}-n
belongs to
S_{0}(a,b)
for any non-negative integer
n\leq g_{0,a,b}
. It is obvious that exactly half of the integers in the interval
[0,g_{0,a,b}]
can be expressed in the form
ax+by
with
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
, according to Sylvester’s result.
More generally, for any pair of relatively prime integers
(a,b)
, we have (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Corollary 2]
)

\displaystyle g_{\ell,a,b}

\displaystyle=(\ell+1)ab-a-b\,,

\displaystyle n_{\ell,a,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl((2\ell+1)ab-a-b+1\bigr)\,.

When
\ell=0
, the
0
-numerical semigroup
S(a,b)=S_{0}(a,b)
is the original numerical semigroup. In this case, the
0
-Frobenius number
g_{a,b}=g_{0,a,b}
and the
0
-genus
n_{a,b}=n_{0,a,b}
are the original Frobenius number and genus, respectively.
There are exactly
n_{0,a,b}=(a-1)(b-1)/2
integers in the set
G_{0}(a,b)
, as previously mentioned. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of the prime numbers.
For any given non-negative integer
\ell
, denote

\pi_{\ell,a,b}=\#\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p\in S_{\ell}(a,b),p\leq g_{\ell,a,b}\}\,.

In 2020,
Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba
[
15
]
proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\pi_{0,a,b}>C(\varepsilon)\frac{g_{0,a,b}}{(\log g_{0,a,b})^{2+\varepsilon}}

(1.1)

provided that
g_{0,a,b}
is sufficiently large.
Observing (
1.1
) and
verifying several cases, they posed the following conjecture
[
15
, Conjecture 2]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
2<a<b
be two relatively prime integers. Then
\pi_{0,a,b}>0
.

Dai, Ding, and Wang
[
4
]
confirmed Conjecture
1
. Recently, Chen and Zhu
[
2
]
proved that

\pi_{0,a,b}>0.005\cdot\frac{g_{0,a,b}}{\log g_{0,a,b}}.

Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba also posed another conjecture
[
15
, Conjecture 3]
.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
2<a<b
be two relatively prime integers; then

\pi_{0,a,b}\sim\frac{\pi(g_{0,a,b})}{2}\quad(\text{as}\,a\to\infty),

where
\pi(x)
denotes the number of primes not exceeding
x
.

Ding
[
5
]
proved that Conjecture
2
is almost always true. Subsequently,
Ding, Zhai, and Zhao
[
7
]
confirmed Conjecture
2
. More generally, Ding and Komatsu
[
6
]
proved that

\pi_{\ell,a,b}\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{\pi(g_{\ell,a,b})}{\ell+1}\quad(\text{as}\,a\to\infty)

(1.2)

as a generalization of Conjecture
2
. Huang and Zhu
[
9
]
further extended the result of Ding, Zhai, and Zhao to the distribution of prime powers. Very recently, Chen and Zhu
[
2
]
also proved that for any fixed integer
a\geq 3
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, then

\pi_{0,a,b}=\left(\frac{a-2}{2(a-1)}+o(1)\right)\frac{g_{0,a,b}}{\log g_{0,a,b}}\quad(as\,b\to\infty).

In this paper, we give a generalization of the results of Chen and Zhu
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\ell
be any non-negative integer. Then, for a fixed integer
a\geq 3
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, we have

\pi_{\ell,a,b}=\left(\frac{a-2}{2(\ell a+a-1)}+o(1)\right)\pi\bigl(g_{\ell,a,b}\bigr)\quad(\text{as}~b\to\infty)\,.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer and
a<b
two relatively prime integers satisfying
a\geq e^{\ell+1}
, then

\pi_{\ell,a,b}>0.005\cdot\frac{1}{\ell+1}\frac{g_{\ell,a,b}}{\log g_{\ell,a,b}}.

Remark 1
.

For
\ell=0
, Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
were proved by Chen and Zhu
[
2
]
as mentioned earlier. By (
1.2
) and the prime number theorem, we see that
\ell+1
in the denominator is required.

Chen and Zhu were also interested in determining the number of primes in
G_{0}(a,b)
.
For any given non-negative integer
\ell
, denote

\pi^{*}_{\ell,a,b}=\#\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p\in G_{\ell}(a,b)\}.

Chen and Zhu
[
3
]
proved that for a fixed integer

with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, then

\pi_{0,a,b}^{*}>\frac{a}{2(a-1)}\frac{g_{0,a,b}}{\log g_{0,a,b}}\quad(as\,b\to\infty),

and

\pi_{0,a,b}^{*}>0.04\pi(g_{0,a,b}).

Chen and Zhu also posed the following conjecture
[
3
, Conjecture 1.4]
.

Conjecture 3
.

For
b>a\geq 1
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, we have

\pi_{0,a,b}^{*}\geq\frac{1}{2}\pi(g_{0,a,b}),

and the equality holds if and only if one of the following four cases holds:

(1)\,a=1;\quad(2)\,(a,b)=(2,3);\quad(3)\,(a,b)=(2,5);\quad(4)\,(a,b)=(3,5).

Along this line, we also have the following results.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer.
Then, for a given integer
a\geq 3
and a sufficiently large integer

with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, we have

\pi^{*}_{\ell,a,b}>\frac{(2\ell+1)a}{2(\ell a+a-1)}\frac{g_{\ell,a,b}}{\log g_{\ell,a,b}}.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\ell,a
and

be non-negative integers with
\ell<a<b

\gcd(a,b)=1

\pi^{*}_{\ell,a,b}>\frac{\ell+0.02}{\ell+1}\frac{g_{\ell,a,b}}{\log g_{\ell,a,b}}.

Remark 2
.

For
\ell=0
, Theorem
3
and Theorem

were proved by Chen and Zhu
[

as mentioned previously. By (

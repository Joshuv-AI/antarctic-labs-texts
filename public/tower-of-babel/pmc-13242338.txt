Next generation sustainable nano-carbon photocatalysts for environmental purification
Authors: Nisha Dhiman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe exceptional physicochemical properties of nano-carbon have gained attention as an auspicious material for a variety of practical applications. Recently, these materials were reported in many diverse areas like photocatalysis, electrocatalysis, energy storage, energy generation and conversion, gas sorption, adsorption, tribology, etc. Nano-carbon has unique intrinsic properties of large pore volume, size, unique pore size distribution, high specific surface area. Further, high electrical conductivity with a good number of active makes nano-carbon a potential candidate for photocatalysis. In photocatalysis, the band gap and activity in light are two important factors that make a material active or inactive for the reaction. The nano-carbon is a good photocatalyst as it can be used as such or with perturbation of its framework with metal, metal oxides, and heteroatoms, which further enhances the applications. The nano-carbon can be synthesized by different methods: chemical activation, hydrothermal/solvothermal process, microwave-assisted synthesis, carbonization, etc. of chemical precursors or abundant biomass available in nature. In this article, the methods, materials and mechanism for photodegradation have been discussed to treat toxic pollutants like dyes, chemicals, heavy metals, pharma waste, CO2 reduction etc.Graphical abstract

Introduction

The alarming situation of global warming, pollution, and uncontrolled population has led the world into a crisis for drinkable water, breathable air, etc. Earth is covered with 70% of water, out of which 2.5% is usable which was further polluted by industries, and domestic misuse [1]. The polluted water and air from the industries were added into the environment without any treatment and purification. This contamination is composed of dangerous gases like CO2, CH4, CO, NOX, and SOX, and pharmaceutical waste, chemicals, and pesticides [2]. This inefficient treatment of wastewater and gases leads to various water and air-borne diseases. These are cancer, mutation, tuberculosis, skin problems, respiratory diseases, endocrine system failure, etc. [3–5]. To avoid all these problems, efficient treatment should be done using the following methods for wastewater purification [4–6]. Coagulation, flocculation, membrane separation methods, filtration, electrocatalysis, and photodegradation. Among, the mentioned method of water treatment photodegradation is the best choice [1, 2]. Photodegradation does not have any toxic side products. Photodegradation/photocatalysis is the process where a semiconducting material absorbs a photon from the light source [1, 4]. This photon excites the electron of the valance band (VB) to match the band gap to reach at conduction band (CB). This allows the chemical reaction to proceed via photoactivation which is otherwise thermodynamically unfavourable. [1, 2] This activation forms the reactive oxygen species (ROS), electron, and hole. The efficiency of photocatalyst decreases if electron and hole recombine early without reaching the active site. In 1972, Fujishima and Honda were the first to discover the photocatalytic activity of titanium dioxide in presence of light irradiation [1]. After that, photocatalysis has grown into the best choice for wastewater treatment as it has carbon dioxide and water as by-products. This process generally known as mineralization. Recently, there are numerous materials to show photocatalytic activity. These are (i) inorganic (TiO2, ZnO, ZnS, ZrO2, SrO2, WO3, Fe2O3, etc.), (ii) organic; polymers, heteroatoms-enriched materials, nano-carbon (graphene, carbon nanotubes, aerogels, activated carbon) [2], covalent organic frameworks, porous organic materials, etc. and (iii) hybrid; metal–organic framework, composites of organic and inorganic semiconductors [2–14]. The metal-based materials are more effective for the photodegradation of toxic pollutants but the toxicity effect of metal or metal oxide put them lower side [3, 4]. The main challenges are the rapid charge recombination, limited activity in visible light, wide band gap. Further, photo corrosion, limited surface area, environmental challenges like efficient water pollution control, and recyclability are the major issues of conventional catalysts. According to the report by US EPA, the textile industry uses 200 Liters of water to color 1 kg of material which enhances water pollution as it is discharged with minimum treatment to the freshwater bodies [1]. The nano-carbon is the best choice among the photocatalysts as they can be derived from biomass waste which is a sustainable method for photocatalyst synthesis [5]. Also, the better textural, morphological, and structural properties make it superior among all [5]. The high surface area of nano-carbon provides a surface for the efficient adsorption of wastewater for better photocatalytic activity. [5–7].

This review provides the discussion of lack consolidate research gap and high understanding of nanocarbon as efficient photocatalyst. The novelty of this work is that it highlights the graphene, CNTs, g-C3N4, carbon dots, and biochar as next generation photocatalysts synthesized from the sustainable material with easy of availability. The novelty of nanocarbons as photocatalysts is the introduction of multifunctional, tunable, sustainable platforms compare to other toxic metal based photocatalysts. Their tailorable band gap, Fermi levels can be adjusted with the synergic effect of heteroatoms. Their exceptional adsorption of Visible light overpowering the TiO2 limitation of UV. Further, the conjugation of p electronic cloud extending the delocalization of much needed electron transport. The synthesis of nanocarbon from the waste and non-toxic material is the key highlight of the review. These nanocarbons has comparable and even higher photocatalytic efficiency then conventional catalysts [5–14]. A critical comparison of different nanocarbons for environmental purification has been discussed in detail. The role of surface functionalities, defect engineering, and heterojunction formation is discussed.

Synthesis of nanocarbon and derivatives

The various properties like the method of synthesis, structure, morphology, texture, and chemical functionalities affect the performance of photocatalysts [6]. The nano-carbon can be synthesised from the biomass available which includes, industrial waste i.e., textile, plant waste, animal waste, and waste of microorganisms as shown in Fig. 1. These wastes were utilized for nano-carbon synthesis to maintain environmental sustainability otherwise this generates various water and air-borne diseases with an accumulation of bacteria, fungus, etc. [1, 2]. The nano-carbon can be synthesised using various methods e.g., chemical activation, hydrothermal/solvothermal process, microwave-assisted synthesis, carbonization, etc. The different dimensions and morphology of nano-carbon could be synthesised with these methods. This includes; carbon nano-onion (CNO), nanosheets, nanosphere, carbon quantum dots, graphene, aerogels, etc. [5]. These nano-carbon acts as semiconducting material to activate the photodegradation of dyes, chemicals, CO2, etc. The efficiency of nanocarbon can be enhanced by doping or incorporation of metal-based, and metal-free systems. The metal-free systems further enhance the sustainability by dominating the toxic effect of metals. The physicochemical properties of nano-carbon are mainly affected by the route of synthesis because this will decide the intrinsic properties like morphology, framework, textural properties, etc. [7–11]. Du et al. have synthesised via wet chemistry synthesis route which promotes the multidimensional nano-carbon products. This method involves the solubilization of precursors in a mutual soluble medium. The Fig. 2a shows the synthesis route where the composite of sulfur is prepared with nanocarbon (graphene, carbon black, carbon nanotube). These composites were sonicated and added in ultra-pure water which leads to the separation of colloidal suspension seen clearly via Tyndall effect [12]. Zhang et al. have utilized the carbonization of carbon nanotubes (CNT), with different precursors of Ni for the synthesis of bamboo-like nanotube morphology [13]. The different methods for the synthesis of different morphology have been explored by Awasthi et al. where pyrolysis of castor oil and spray pyrolysis was done for the carbon nanotubes [14]. Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) is another method for the synthesis of nano-carbon.

Zhang et al. have utilized the CVD method for the synthesis of nano-carbon on the chemically exfoliated graphene sheets. In this method, nickel was impregnated on the sheets and acetonitrile was used for the growth of carbon nanotubes on the exfoliated sheets [15]. Solution plasma is the unique process for the synthesis of material. Ishizaki et al. have utilized solution plasma process for the first time to synthesize the nano-carbon, where benzene and 1,4-dioxane were used as precursors [16]. In solution plasma technique the electric discharge is used to generate plasma in liquid phase or other phase to generate electron density. Chakradhar et al. have also utilized the solution plasma process for the synthesis of carbon materials with graphite like behaviour. For the synthesis of carbon material tributyl borate was used and pour in the beaker, connected to the solution plasma (SP) reactor. This leads to the formation of two types of carbon material in the form of precipitates and particles suspended in solution as soon in Fig. 2b [17]. Prasad et al. have explored the microwave assisted synthesis method for the synthesis of electroactive material. Nitrogen and phosphorous were incorporated in the material via one pot reaction followed my microwave heating as shown in Fig. 2c [18].

The microwave synthesis is unique route to achieve the controlled textural and morphological property with high yield [18, 19]. Gonzalez et al. have also explored the microwave assisted method for the synthesis of heteroatom doped carbon dots. The different heteroatom (N, P, and S) doped carbon dots explored with different dyes (Brilliant green, Lauth's violet, brilliant blue, Victoria blue, and toluidine blue) degradation. The author highlight the better degradation of toluidine blue dye with N-doped carbon dots. The author has pin pointed the concept of quantum confinement, molecular fluorescence, and surface states of the functional moieties. These propertioes led to better light adsorption. Also, the diverse dye degradation of dye with different heteroatom composition attributed to synthesis route and morphological effects [20]. Mixed phase synthesis route is another process which has shown the formation of different phases. These phases are mixed with carbon framework of sp2 and sp3 hybridization for better textual and structural properties. Kumar et al. have done the mixed phase synthesis for the preparation of graphene-like nano-carbon. The synthesis of nano-carbon from the soft wood charcoal to form the sp2 and sp3 mixed carbon framework with polycyclic rings. Further, after the carbonization, this has formed graphene-like nano-carbon [21]. The synthesis of nano-carbon via the electrospinning method is important class of synthesis. This formation leads to the different dimensional nanocomposites with ultra-purity. In this method, the liquid precursor was spined under an electric field used for the formation of fibers which were stretched and elongated. Xia et al. have utilized the electrospinning approach for nanowire and nanofibers synthesis to made hybrid membrane. In this polyacrylonitrile with Co precursor was used for the synthesis of Co composite as shown in Fig. 2d [22].

Morphological effect of nanocarbon

Another important factor which affects the photocatalytic activity of material is the different types of morphologies. The long nanotubes, nanowires, leaves, flower etc. These structures enhance the catalytic performance as they provide extra surface area which acts as active sites for the catalytic activity [23–25]. Huang et al. have investigated the effect of chromium removal based on morphological property. Author has synthesized fibrillar and particulate structure magnetic carbons (MCFs and MCPs). The MCF show the higher adsorption (Fig. 3a–d) than the MCPs (Fig. 3e) because the fibrillar have higher specific surface area. This attributed to the high number of active sites for the chromium ions to site and get adsorb (Fig. 3f). These active sites lowers the adsorption–desorption energy and enhances the adsorption compare to MCPs [26]. Zhang et al. have synthesized nanocarbon from the carbon dioxide with controlled and well defined morphology. In this typical synthesis the author has utilized carbon dioxide. The CO2 reacted with magnesium metal for the shape controlled microstructure as the properties of nanocarbon is highly dependent on shape [27]. Microwave assisted synthesis was explored by Zhang et al. for the synthesis of nanocarbon with controlled morphology by using conducting polymer. The polypyrene was used for this purpose, where morphology of polypyrene is retained even after microwave treatment [28]. Phen et al. have studied the effect of morphology on the photocatalytic degradation of methylene blue using TiO2. The TiO2 shows two phases i.e., rutile and anatase. The author has synthesized different morphologies like uniform flower, agglomerates, cauliflower by varying the different ration of HCl. This is done in mixed and separate phases of TiO2. The author concluded that the prism and flower morphology of TiO2 shows high catalytic activity. This is because it provided high surface area and active site with synergic effect of mixed phase of TiO2 [29]. Kajbafvala et al. have synthesized the two morphologies of ZnO for the photocatalytic activity. The samples were irradiated with the microwave with different precursors leads to the formation of flower like and spherical morphology. The spherical morphology shows high catalytic activity as it has high specific surface area compare to flower like morphology [30]. The high surface area nano-carbons can be designed by controlled activation i.e. more residence time with high temperature range. This route adds well developed pores in the framework. These interconnected pores enhance molecular transport, active sites with improved photocatalytic efficiency [31]. The balanced mixed pores enhance the adsorption of pollutant molecules and reaction kinetics. The surface roughness improves the scattering of light and enhances the photon capture [32].

Effect of defect engineering

The doping of heteroatoms (N, P, B, S, O, and F, etc.) perturbs the electrical neutrality, polarity, and chemical environment of the framework [5]. This defect engineering reduces the band gap or tune this gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels (or the valence and conduction band). This reduced band gap provides the path of less activation energy to the excited electron and enhances the visible light adsorption. Hence, facilitates the effective electron transport upon light illumination with energy equal or greater than the band gap [1, 2, 5]. This effective electron transport led to the formation of OH radical from the hydroxide ion or from water. Further, the dissolved oxygen forms superoxide radical. This electron and hole accelerate the photoactive reactions [1]. Hence the required alignment in the energy level facilitates the charge separation and transfer. The doping enhancing the electrical conductivity that help in faster electron movement. The heteroatoms act as charge carrier separator or sink which enhances the quantum efficiency of the reaction [33–35]. The doping of heteroatoms imparts the extra active sites with the high surface area with uniform pore distribution. This changes the polarization around the carbon atom, hence provide polarized electron cloud for extra activity [36–42]. Gunture et al. has reported the multiatom doped nanocarbon for removal chromium via photocatalysis. The Kalanchoe pinnata was used as sustainable nanocarbon precursor, with doping of N, K, Mg, Cl, and Ca. The author has achieved 91% of removal of Cr (VI) from 20 ppm solution while 100% in acidic conditions [43]. Li et al. has synthesized photocatalyst i.e. g-C3N4 Ag supported by renewable biomass derived carbon which is highly effective for the CO2 reduction [44]. Nano-carbon as photocatalysts

The electronic framework of nano-carbon is consisted of ordered carbon atoms arranged in hexagonal manner. Each atom is sp2/sp3 hybridized with delocalized π electron cloud above and below the plane [5]. The electronic cloud has high mobility across the carbon framework that led to the semiconducting nature of nano-carbon materials. Nano-carbon includes materials composed of carbon structures with their dimensions in nanometre range i.e. below 100 nm. This includes carbon quantum dots (CQDs), carbon nanotubes (CNTs), graphene, graphdiyne, nano-carbon based aerogels, foam etc. [5]. The precise synthesis, controlled doping, accurate defect engineering enhances the semiconducting properties of nano-carbons as discussed above [5]. The quantum confinement in higher in CQDs which led to higher energy of electrons and holes. In this case conduction band has more reduction potential and vice versa for valance band with higher number of reactive oxygen species (ROS) i.e. ˙OH, ˙O2−, and H2O2 [5]. Cheng et al. have designed carbon quantum dots synthesized from gingko leaves. This was used as photocatalyst to enhance the efficiency of carbon-nitride with flower like morphology. This heterostructure has shown high efficiency for photocatalytic degradation of Rhodamine B dye. The higher efficiency is attributed to the CQDs because this has enhanced the dissociation of excitons and charge transfer in the framework [62]. Yao and co-workers have reported the higher photooxidation activity of single walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) for phenol. The SWCNTs composite with TiO2 has less charge recombination where electrons are transformed from anatase phase of TiO2 to the SWCNTs [63]. The composite of silver silicates with carbon nanotubes were known for photodegradation. The poor electrical properties of silver silicates have been improved by CNTs and reduced graphene oxides. These composites have shown photodegrading in 10 min with higher rate constant ever reported for silver silicates [64]. Chen et al. has revealed two processes for atrazine degradation. The microwave assisted photocatalytic (MAPC) and microwave assisted photodegradation (MAPD) for atrazine with TiO2 MWCNTs. The author reported the MAPC is highly effective for atrazine as MWCNTs has high microwave absorption capacity [65]. The class of nanocarbons i.e. carbon quantum dots have high photocatalytic activity because of quantum effect. The CQD/CdS/g-C3N4 photocatalysts shows high charge separation because of formation of interphase between the nanosheets and quantum dots. The catalysts have high photocatalytic degradation for contaminates like p-chlorophenol, bisphenol A, and tetracycline [66]. Kojive et al. have reported that the red color emitting carbon quantum dots (R-CQDs) have highest photodegradation of methylene blue (MB) then other color emitting QDs. The R-CQDs/ P25 has 20 times higher activity for MB degradation then the pure form of P25 [67]. Singh and coworkers have explored another class of nanocarbons i.e. graphene nanosheets for the organic dye degradation. The author has used petrol soot for the synthesis of water-soluble graphene nanosheets. These water-soluble nanosheets has 11 times higher degradation efficiency then the insoluble graphene nanosheets [68]. Zhao et al. reported Hemin (Hem)-graphene hydrogel (GH) as photocatalyst with high mechanical and adsorption capacity. The Hem-GH catalysts have shown excellent photodegradation for methylene blue (MB) with 341 mg g−1 of adsorption capacity. This capacity is higher than the commercial catalyst P25 and pristine graphene hydrogel [69]. Another catalyst reported by Sirusy and coworkers for MB photodegradation and photoreduction of Cr6+ to Cr.3+. The author has designed composite of reduced graphene oxide with ultrathin nanotubes of Bi5O7I and investigated charge transfer and photon absorption capability [70]. Khare et al. have used sustainable, nano-toxic graphene nanosheets for MB degradation from the bacterial culture [71]. Cationic porphyrin used as photocatalysts with graphene oxide for the Rhodamine B degradation, where porphyrin ring is promotion charge transfer [72].

Metal/metal-oxide/heterostructures based nano-carbon as photocatalysts

The rapid recombination of electron and holes is the main challenge in the metal or metal oxide based photocatalysts. The sustainable nano-carbons provide enough path on its surface to migrate photogenerated the electron and holes. This migration supresses the recombination issue and enhances the life of charge carrier. Deng et al. have synthesised porous nano-carbon from the biomass. The high porosity with surface area has provided the large number of active sites for the photocatalyst. This nano-carbon decorated with CdS nanoparticles; therefore, the recombination of electron and hole is overcome [73]. The g-C3N4 has good surface area but lacks behind because of poor charge separation results in less active sites and low surface plasmon resonance (SPR). The low SPR led to less internal reflection from the material. The author has used silver nanoparticles with g-C3N4 supported by biomass derived nano-carbon. This nano-carbon decorated with Ag adding to the extra active sites with enhances SPR and suppresses the recombination issue. The synergy of g-C3N4 and nano-carbon with SPR active metal has enhanced the photo reduction of CO2 with CO evolution rate of 33.3 mmol h−1 g−1. This photocatalyst showing high activity than pristine catalyst without the use of photosensitiser [44]. Wang et al. have synthesized biomass derived nano-carbon with hierarchical porous structure with TiO2 for degradation of organic pollutants. The author has synthesised trilevel porous structure which facilitating the mass transport, ion diffusion, and provide a greater number of active sites. The synergy of hierarchical pores of carbon and active phases of metal oxide improving the light absorption and charge separation [74]. Hussain et al. have designed a metal organic framework of ZnO with porous carbon matrix. This MOF is synthesized via carbonization process at high temperature of 800 °C and 1000 °C. This photocatalyst has high activity for the degradation of MB attributed to the high surface area of carbon and crystalline nature of ZnO [75]. The CdS nanoparticles are known to have high photocatalytic properties. Garg et al. have designed CdS composite of with carbon nanoflakes. This doped CdS has high activity for the sunlight assisted photodegradation of Indigo Carmine selectively [76]. The NiO also one of the good photocatalyst. Banu et al. has designed catalyst using NiO with g-C3N4, that has 99.34% degradation rate in 19 min for Indigo Carmine [77]. Roopan et al. has concluded from the experimental investigation that biomass derived CQDs photocatalyst when doped with metal oxide has high degradation rate in less time [78]. Pulverized activated carbon (AC) has been explored by Bernardino and coworkers. This photocatalyst is the combination of manganese ferrite with reduced graphene oxide and AC from the cow bones [79]. The systematic investigations were carried out by Zhang et al. for the maximum achievable photocatalytic activity of ZnO and GO ratio with Pd nanoparticles decoration. This catalyst has maximum MB degradation when ZnO has 0.85 and GO has 0.15 of proportion. This catalyst supresses electron hole recombination by twofold and enhances photocatalytic activity then the pure ZnO and GO [80]. The silver nano catalyst composites of nanocarbon were investigated by Lin et al. for the conversion of carcinogenic compound 4-nitrophenol to 4-aminophenol. This catalyst has controlled micro plasma engineered and unique surface functionalization with silver which help is reduction with high reaction rate [81]. Zuo et al. has constructed a heterojunction of Ag-AgBr/Bi2O2CO3/CNT of Z scheme. This catalyst has degraded MB, CIP and TC. The tetracycline (TC) has highest photodegradation as silver and bismuth has shown effective surface plasmon resonance for efficient electron transport. In this Z scheme the CNTs are the electronic medium for charge separation and transport of electron and hole pair [82]. Another Z scheme based heterostructures was investigated by Li et al. for photodegradation of MB and TC. The heterostructure of iron-based MOF with carbon (Fe2O3@Ag–ZnO@C) is highly effective and generated OH. and O2.−. as reactive oxygen species (ROS) for environmental treatment [83].

Heteroatom doped nano-carbon as photocatalysts

The heteroatoms in the nanocarbons have high photocatalytic activity as they enhance the effective charge separation, higher electron transport with good photon absorption [33–35]. Smrithi et al. have designed nitrogen doped carbon quantum dots synthesized from Cucurbita pepo. This photocatalyst have high degradation efficiency with 99.9% for crystal violet dye. The presence of nitrogen on the surface of CQDs has shown excellent electron transportation attributes to high efficiency [84]. Spencer et al. has reported the porphyrin based nanocarbon for the photo degradation of Rhodamine B and 6G. The oxygen group of carboxylic acid on MWCNTs is connected with 5-(4-carboxyphenyl)-10,15,20-(triphenyl), porphyrin (c-TPP), and amine group with 5-(4-aminophenyl)-10,15,20-(triphenyl)porphyrin (a-TPP) moieties. The photocatalytic efficiency is higher when MWCNTs are present in high concentration attributed to the -OH groups [85]. The advanced optical and electronic properties of g-C3N4 make it highly effective photocatalyst as its top 10 nm layer has strong quantum confinement and edge effects. Tipplook et al. has investigated the multibedroom doped g-C3N4 with nanoflake morphology. This morphology provides the edge effect and has broad spectrum range from Visible to near IR. This catalyst has shown excellent disinfection of bacteria like E. coli and S. aureus starting with the disruption of their cell membrane [86]. Ajiboye et al. have concluded that the heteroatom doped nanocarbon is highly effective for the degradation of organic pollutants like pharmaceuticals, dyes, phenol, pesticides, and toxic metals [87]. Ghosh and coworkers have explored the effect of nitrogen atom on the carbon for the carbon dioxide conversion. Thiourea was used for nitrogen doping which has reduced the band gap in SiO2 compared to the pristine specimen [88]. Bhoyar et al. have investigated the mechanism of photon and exciton in CQDs and studied the effect of nitrogen doping on the same. Also, revealed that doping has enhanced the degradation of antibiotics like TC and Rhodamine B [89]. The green colour emitting CQDs were synthesized and investigated by the Alcaide et al. for chemical degradation present in soil i.e. Tylosin. The urea was used for the nitrogen doping and lemon juice was used for CQD synthesis. They investigated that each soil i.e. SS soil and SI soil has different effect of CQD on the Tylosin degradation [90]. Xiong et al. have used walnut shells as biomass as photocatalyst doped with nitrogen containing TiO2. This catalyst has degraded methyl orange (MO) with 97.6% efficiency and has shown 85.4% mineralization [91]. Muthuvijayan et al. has carried out the synthesis of nitrogen-doped graphene with iron oxide composite. This evaluates its efficacy in the photodegradation of organic pollutants, demonstrating significant enhancements in photocatalytic activity [92]. N and P containing nanocarbon was explored by Bhati et al. for the Cr (IV) removal. This NP-carbon dots have photo reduced Cr (IV) from 2000 ppm solution in 320 min [93]. The heterojunction, TiO2/g-C3N4 doped with N, and S has been explored by Huang et al. for the dye’s removal. They revealed that N and S doped heterojunction has maximum efficiency attributed to the high electron movement and charge separation then in pristine specimens [94]. The removal of Norfloxin was investigated by Wang with the synthesis of Z-scheme biochar of Ag3PO4 heterojunction doped with N and S. This antibiotic was removed completely after 120 min of visible light exposer to the photocatalyst [95]. The effect of doping of heteroatoms i.e. N doped carbon nanostructures (CNs), N, and S doped CNs, N, and P doped CNs, N, S, and P doped CNs has been deeply investigated by Gonzalez et al. for the water treatment i.e. removal of toluidine blue. They conclude that N doped CNs are most effective because they have quantum dot morphology which help in achieving higher efficiency [96]. Hence, the above discussion on the photocatalytic effect of heteroatom for higher efficiency conclude that the electron richness, polarization, and difference in electronegativity has improved the degradation [33].

Conclusion, challenges, assessment and future prospects

The sustainable sources of nanocarbons i.e. biowaste has provide routes for the development of next generation photocatalysts. There affinity for the different heterojunction and Z, S schemes breaking the energy barrier gap for the photons. The structural engineering via heteroatom doping and metal, metal oxide encapsulations has enhanced the photocatalytic performance by perturbing the electronic cloud. These modifications have provided the extra active sites for the photon absorption. Also, these defects help in suppressing the charge carrier recombination and enhanced the electron hole transport. The different nanocarbons has different physical, chemical properties, and further different photocatalytic activity. The nanocarbon is one of the promising photocatalyst but suffering from cons also. The carbon quantum dot has superior optical behaviour but its high affinity for agglomeration is cause for a smaller number of active sites. Similarly, in case of carbon nanotubes that has high electrical conductivity but has high cost and purification is the bigger challenge. Further, the graphene based nanocarbon is high conductive with high surface area but the restacking is the bottleneck. Also, due to structural heterogeneity, the biochar’s nanocarbon struggling with lower photocatalytic efficiency. Again, the low cost, abundance, chemical functionalities and sustainability of biomass derived nanocarbons is very promising and attractive. But the inconsistent structural, morphological, textural properties is the major challenge for the photocatalytic activity. Further, the different precursor source always affecting the reproducibility and purity of the nanocarbons.

Very recently, the class of metal sulfide with sustainable nanocarbons is the emerging and promising photocatalysts for the environmental remediation [97, 98]. The charge separation has been enhanced and photo corrosion is decreased in ZnS, CdS, MoS2, and NiS. Though the sustainable nanocarbons has achieved higher efficiency and open to structural engineering, many challenges are still in the way of photocatalysis. In the real-world applications, the rational designs with controlled intrinsic and extrinsic properties still a bigger challenge to overcome. Generally, the studies carrier out in lab scale in controlled environment and in clean room but the realistic reaction conditions affect the efficiency of nanocarbons. The precise control on the textural and morphological properties has to improve further for better efficiency. The sustainable approach for the synthesis of nanocarbon form biomass with low cost and scalability also affects the futuristic development and R&D. With the advanced designs the research should be focused on complex pollutants removal with real worlds environmental conditions. Also, in large scale production, synthesis cost, catalyst recovery, controlling intrinsic and extrinsic properties is bit difficult to achieve. Further, the hybrid photocatalytic systems with integration of more advanced designs guided with artificial intelligences (AI) and machine learning (ML) is the new and critical approach for futuristic development. The computational studies of cocatalysts with AI designed efficient photocatalyst can enhance the efficiency and is more focused studies for the real-world wastewater systems. The AI and ML can bridge the gap of lab scale development and practical commercial applications.

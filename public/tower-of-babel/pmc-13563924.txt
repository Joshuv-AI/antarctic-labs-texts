Rapid Classification of Bauxite Ores from Different Mining Areas by BBO-XGBoost Combined with CC-LIBS
Authors: Yang Yanwei, Zhang Jiaxin, Zhang Lili
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Focusing on the problems of complicated sample processing and long detection time of traditional bauxite ore composition testing in mining areas, this paper proposes a rapid bauxite ore classification method combining cavity-confined laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (CC-LIBS) with Boruta feature selection and Bayesian optimization eXtreme Gradient Boosting (BBO-XGBoost), which can quickly and accurately classify bauxite ore samples from 9 different mining areas. First, the effects of different cavity configurations on the enhancement factor, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), were investigated. When the cavity diameter is 5 mm, the enhancement factors for Al, Fe, and Ti reach 2.8, 3.1, and 2.6, respectively. Next, 900 × 12,248-dimensional spectral data under the D = 5 mm cavity configuration were preprocessed using the Boruta feature selection algorithm. Finally, the XGBoost classifier is optimized using Bayesian optimization to achieve accurate classification of bauxite. Compared with unoptimized approaches and other models, the classification method combining CC-LIBS and BBO-XGBoost achieves the best performance. The accuracy is 0.9852, the precision is 0.9861, the recall is 0.9852, and the F1-score is 0.9856. The proposed technique, combining CC-LIBS with BBO-XGBoost, not only plays a pivotal role in bauxite ore research but also provides a significant technical reference for the rapid identification of bauxite ore sources.

Introduction

As an important strategic mineral resource, the origin traceability and type identification of bauxite play a crucial role in mineral exploration, resource assessment and metallurgical process optimization. Accurate classification and detection not only help to ensure the quality of raw materials, but also provide a scientific basis for ore trade and supervision. However, traditional chemical analysis methods such as XRF (X-ray Fluorescence Spectrometer), ICP-MS (Inductively Coupled Plasma-Mass Spectrometry), , and NAA (Neutron Activation Analysis), , as well as ICP-AES (Inductively Coupled Plasma Atomic Emission Spectrometry), have high precision. Nevertheless, they face many problems such as complex sample pretreatment, long detection cycles, high costs, and difficulties in achieving rapid on-site analysis, which seriously restrict their application in large-scale screening and rapid monitoring. Therefore, a technology that offers simple operation and high analytical accuracy is urgently needed.

Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) , technology is a new spectral detection technology that does not require complex sample preparation and offers fast response, multielement simultaneous detection, and in situ analysis. By irradiating the surface of the sample with a laser, local instantaneous heating with high energy density is generated. This triggers the avalanche effect of continuous collision ionization, causing the free electron density to increase exponentially, and finally forming a plasma with high temperature, high pressure, and high conductivity. The transition of the seed particles during the plasma cooling process forms a characteristic spectrum. Researchers can obtain the chemical composition of the sample by analyzing the wavelength and spectral intensity of the emission spectrum. At present, LIBS technology has been widely used in materials science, , medicine, , mineral exploration, , environmental testing, archeological identification, , metallurgy, and other fields for rapid classification and product quality evaluation of material samples. LIBS provides an effective solution for the rapid classification of bauxite. However, the traditional LIBS spectral data typically exhibit issues including weak intensity, low SNR, extremely high dimensionality (up to tens of thousands of features), which leads to ″dimension disaster″ and feature flooding during direct modeling, seriously affecting classification accuracy and model stability.

To address the problems of weak LIBS spectral intensity and SNR, many scholars have studied spectral enhancement through various constraint methods. Shao et al. studied the effect of cylindrical cavity height on the spectral intensity in spatially constrained LIBS. They demonstrated that the cavity constraint can enhance the spectral intensity, reduce the detection limit, and improve the LIBS detection accuracy. Tang et al. studied the characteristics of plasma by placing Au nanoparticles (Au-NPs) on the surface of Cu samples in a magnetic field. Their results indicated that the spectral intensity of laser-induced plasma was greatly enhanced and the signal-to-noise ratio was also improved. Wang et al. studied the influence of laser pulse energy and discharge channel on spark discharge assisted LIBS, determined the optimal experimental conditions of LA-SIBS in the detection of aluminum alloy, brass and other samples, and reduced the detection limit of elements. Dan et al. studied the effect of heating sample combined with spatially confined LIBS on spectral intensity. They found that the spectral intensity increased with the increase of sample temperature. When the two external conditions worked together, the spectral enhancement effect collected by LIBS was the best. Under this condition, the intensity of the Al 396.2 nm spectral line increased by a factor of 2.1. These studies provide new ideas for solving the problems of weak LIBS spectral intensity and low signal-to-noise ratio of bauxite by using cavity constraints.

In order to address the challenges posed by high spectral dimension and low classification accuracy, many research teams have used laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) technology to classify and identify substances with machine learning, thereby demonstrating strong material identification capabilities. Diaz et al. achieved effective classification of gold-bearing ores by combining laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) with principal Origin traceability. Sheng et al. demonstrated a method combining LIBS with the random forest algorithm to classify iron ore. Yang et al. applied machine learning techniques to classify LIBS data of iron ore, achieving rapid detection of mineral composition. Hao et al. successfully classified soils in different mining areas by optimizing neural networks and using advanced algorithms. Jin et al. combined laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy with high-frequency ultrasonic atomization system (AS-LIBS) and optimized grid search cross-validation (Opt-GSCV) to achieve high-precision and rapid classification of liquor in different dimensions. Chen et al. used cavity constraints combined with gray wolf optimization algorithm to optimize the bidirectional long short-term memory (GWO-BiLSTM) network to accurately classify the LIBS spectra of cigarettes. The above literature shows that using spectral constraint methods combined with advanced machine learning algorithms to model LIBS experimental data can effectively reduce and correct spectral intensity fluctuations caused by emission source noise and matrix effects, thereby improving measurement accuracy.

At present, no research in the literature has addressed the rapid classification of bauxite using constraint methods and intelligent algorithms combined with LIBS. To this end, this paper constructs a laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy system based on cavity constraints (CC-LIBS). Additionally, it develops a qualitative analysis model using the Boruta feature selection algorithm combined with Bayesian optimization limit gradient boosting (BBO-XGBoost). Experimental System Setup

The instrument used for the experiments was a custom-built LIBS system. The laser is a Nd: YAG laser from Quantel, France, with a maximum frequency of 10 Hz, a maximum pulse energy of 200 mJ, a wavelength of 1064 nm, and a pulse duration of 10 ns. The spectrometer uses a seven-channel spectrometer from Avantes of The Netherlands, with a wide spectral range of 190–950 nm. The sample stage has a freely controllable XYZ axis. The stroke of the sample stage is 5 cm, which can achieve high-precision positioning control with micron-level precision (<1 μm). The laser beam was focused precisely on the geometric center of the sample surface within the cavity to ensure symmetrical shock wave reflection and consistent plasma confinement.

The quality and type of bauxite are closely related to the smelting process and economic indicators of alumina. The main factor determining the quality of bauxite is chemical composition. Bauxite from different regions reflects the compositional characteristics specific to those regions. The accurate classification of bauxite is crucial for the acquisition and quality evaluation of raw materials in the alumina industry. The 9 different bauxite samples used in this study, listed in Table , were sourced from mining areas including Australia Weber, Australia Gove, Guinea, Aojiawan, Jinsui, Sunjiagou, Liulin, Xiaoyi, and Fugu, and provided by Shanxi Huaxing Aluminum Group Co., Ltd. All of them were taken from 9 representative aluminum production regions worldwide, and the bauxite ore sample collection sites are shown in Figure . After drying the bauxite ore to remove debris, it was ground by a high alumina ceramic ball mill to particles of 0.075 μm. Then, the powder was dried in a dryer for 24 h, weighed (3 g), and continuously pressed at 25 MPa for 150s to finally obtain bauxite ore pellets with a diameter of 30 mm and a thickness of 3 mm.

Data Analysis Methodology

Boruta Feature Selection Algorithm

Boruta is an all-relevant feature selection wrapper based on Random Forest (RF). Unlike minimal-optimal methods, Boruta identifies all features statistically related to the target variable by comparing original features with ″shadow features.″ The detailed workflow is implemented exclusively on the training data set to prevent data leakage:

Step 1: Information Extension. The original feature matrix X n*p (where p = 12,248) is extended by adding shadow features Xshadow, created by randomly shuffling the values of each original feature across observations to remove their correlation with the target Y.

Step 2: Importance Evaluation. A Random Forest is trained on the combined matrix [X,Xshadow]. The Mean Decrease Accuracy (MDA) or Gini importance Zi is calculated for each feature i.

Step 3: Statistical Significance. Let Z max_shadow be the maximum importance score among all shadow features. A hit is recorded for an original feature if: Z original,i > Z max_shadow. This process is repeated for N iterations (e.g., N = 100). The cumulative hits follow a binomial distribution 1 P(X=k)=(Nk)qk(1−q)N−k where q = 0.5 represents the probability of a feature performing better than a shadow feature by random chance. Features that fall in the right tail of the distribution (typically p < 0.01) are marked as ″Confirmed.″

Bayesian Optimization Algorithm

Bayesian optimization using the classification accuracy as the objective function to find the optimal configuration that meets the requirements of the classification task. To navigate the high-dimensional and nonlinear hyperparameter space of the classification model efficiently, Bayesian Optimization (BO) is implemented. Unlike traditional grid search, BO treats the hyperparameter tuning as a global optimization problem for a black-box function f(x)­ 2 x*=arg⁡maxx∈X⁡f(x) where x* represents the set of hyperparameters χ is the search space, and f(x) is the classification accuracy.

The BO framework primarily consists of two core components:1.Surrogate model: A Gaussian Process (GP) is utilized to construct a probabilistic model of the objective function. Based on the observed results D 1:t = {(x1, y1),···, (xt, yt)} the GP provides a posterior distribution P(f | D{1:t}) that characterizes the uncertainty of the model across the entire search space.2.Acquisition function: To determine the next sampling point x t+1, an acquisition function (such as Expected α­(x) Improvement, EI) is employed to balance exploration (searching unsampled regions) and exploitation (refining known high-performance regions) 3 αEI(x)=E[max(0,f(x)−f(x+))] where f(x +) is the current maximum observed value. This iterative process ensures efficient convergence toward the optimal configuration while significantly reducing the computational overhead compared to exhaustive search methods.

Integrated Boruta-Bayesian XGBoost Framework

Results and Discussion

LIBS Spectroscopy under Cavity Confinement

This study employed a cylindrical aluminum cavity constraint structure to enhance the laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) spectral intensity of bauxite samples. To determine the optimal size, the Aojiawan bauxite sample was used for the spectral enhancement test. Six different sizes of aluminum cavities (height of 1 mm, diameter of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 mm) were used. For each cavity size, five sets of data were collected, resulting in a total of 30 (5 × 6) experimental data sets. The average value of the five sets of data under each cavity was taken as the data under this size. Al 396.15 nm, Fe 438.35 nm, and Ti 498.17 nm were selected as analytical lines. The effects of different sizes of cavities on LIBS spectral data were investigated by parameters such as enhancement factor and SNR.

Enhancement Factor

The enhancement factor described herein refers to the ratio of spectral intensity under cavity-constrained conditions to that under unconstrained conditions. It is used to characterize spectral intensity enhancement effects. As shown in Figure , when applying different cavities to constrain LIBS, the spectral intensities of aluminum, iron, and titanium first increase and then decrease. When the cavity diameter is 5 mm, the enhancement factor reaches its maximum values: 2.8 for Al, 3.1 for Fe, and 2.6 for Ti.

Trend of Al, Fe and Ti enhancement factors under different cavity diameters.

This phenomenon arises from the formation of shock waves accompanying the generation of plasma during laser irradiation of bauxite sample surfaces. The shock waves propagate faster than the plasma diffusion rate and contact the cavity walls before the plasma arrives. The confining effect of the cavity walls causes the shock wave to reflect toward the center of the plasma diffusion, thereby intensifying particle collisions within the plasma. This further excites the plasma, leading to an enhancement of the bauxite laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy. The enhancement factor reaches its maximum at a cavity diameter of 5 mm. As the cavity diameter continues to increase, the reflected shock wave energy becomes insufficient to sustain plasma enhancement, causing the enhancement factor to begin decreasing.

Signal to Noise Ratio (SNR)

SNR is another important parameter reflecting the quality of the spectral line, commonly used to evaluate the detection sensitivity of the spectral signal. According to eq , the SNR values calculated for the characteristic elements Al, Fe, and Ti are 42.8, 38.8, and 45.1, respectively, using a cavity configuration with D = 5 mm. 12 SNR=Ipσb where I p is the peak intensity and σb is the standard deviation of the background.

Figure shows that the SNR varies with cavity diameter configurations and reaches its optimum at D = 5 mm. This enhancement is mainly due to two factors: the cavity confinement limits plasma expansion and concentrates the emission in a smaller volume, enhancing the spectral intensity of the elemental signal. On the other hand, the source of background noise is limited, the background radiation is weakened, thus reducing the probability of interference and improving the SNR.

SNR of characteristic elements at different cavity diameters.

By studying the influence of different cavities on LIBS spectral enhancement factor and SNR, we can conclude that the cavity with a diameter of 5 mm is optimal. Therefore, we collect bauxite spectral data under the optimal cavity for subsequent qualitative analysis.

The spatial synchronization of reflected shock waves, achieved by central ablation, is critical for maximizing the confinement effect. When the laser focuses on the geometric center, the reflected shock waves converge simultaneously toward the plasma core. This synchronized reflection intensifies the compression of the plasma, effectively converting the kinetic energy of the shock wave into internal plasma energy, which accounts for the significant rise in electron temperature and spectral intensity observed at D = 5 mm. Deviating from the center would lead to uneven energy distribution and diminished enhancement.

Feature Selection and Classification Performance

Under the optimal cavity, 10 samples are made for each bauxite areas, 10 sets of data are collected for each sample, and 100 sets of data can be obtained for each bauxite ore. To ensure the rigorous independence of experimental results and avoid information leakage, the 900 spectral data sets (comprising 9 mining areas, 10 physical pellets per area, and 10 locations per pellet) were partitioned at the pellet level. Specifically, 70% of the pellets from each area were randomly assigned to the training set, while the remaining 30% were equally split between the validation and test sets. This strategy ensures that each data set is strictly independent, with no overlapping spectra from the same physical sample across training and testing phases.

As can be seen in Figure , it is difficult to directly recognize the bauxite ore elemental composition of the 9 different mining areas with small variability and high similarity of spectral information, so feature selection was performed using the Boruta algorithm on the full spectral information on the bauxite ores from different mining areas. The Boruta algorithm identified the most relevant spectral features by comparing real features against randomly permuted shadow features, ensuring statistical significance in feature selection.

CC-LIBS spectra of 9 bauxite ore samples at D = 5 mm.

To effectively mitigate spectral dimensionality and eliminate information redundancy, the Boruta algorithm was implemented to evaluate the statistical significance of 12,248 initial variables (Figure ). The results demonstrate that the vast majority of spectral variables were identified as stochastic noise or noninformative background and were subsequently categorized as Rejected Features. Through 100 iterations of Random Forest and rigorous binomial significance testing, 8 specific spectral variables were confirmed to have high importance scores exceeding the Z max_shadow decision threshold, categorized as Confirmed Features (p < 0.01). It is noteworthy that while Figure reveals a comprehensive range of atomic emission lines, the 8 confirmed variables align precisely with the characteristic emission transitions of Al, Fe, and Ti. This spatial consistency indicates that the selection process effectively filters out collinear redundancies and concentrates on the most robust spectral responses, thereby ensuring the generalizability and computational efficiency of the subsequent classification model.

Algorithm Convergence and Hyperparameter Optimization

To further enhance the generalization capability of the XGBoost model in handling high-dimensional (12,248 variables) LIBS spectral data, the Biogeography-Based Optimization (BBO) algorithm was employed for the global search of core hyperparameters (including learning_rate, max_depth, and n_estimators). The optimization process was conducted for 100 iterations. To verify the superiority of BBO, Genetic Algorithm (GA-XGBoost) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO-XGBoost) were introduced as baseline benchmarks under identical experimental conditions.

Figure presents the convergence trajectories of the three optimization algorithms. It can be observed that all models start from a relatively high initial accuracy, benefiting from the high-quality spectral features selected by the Boruta algorithm.

Comparison of the fitness evolution and optimization trajectories for three algorithms.

While PSO-XGBoost demonstrates a rapid initial ascent, it quickly encounters a bottleneck at an accuracy of 97.85%, indicating premature convergence into local optima. In contrast, the proposed BBO-XGBoost exhibits superior global search capability. Through its unique migration mechanism, BBO undergoes a phase of stochastic exploration between 30 and 55 iterations, successfully surpassing the other benchmarks and stabilizing at a peak accuracy of 98.52%. This comparison confirms the effectiveness of BBO in refining hyperparameters within a narrow and complex search space for bauxite classification indicating premature convergence into local optima. In contrast, the proposed BBO-XGBoost exhibits superior global search capability specifically tailored for the intricate spectral features of bauxite. Given that bauxite spectra from different mining areas exhibit high similarity and narrow discriminative margins, the hyperparameter loss landscape is prone to rugged local minima. Through its unique migration mechanism, BBO undergoes a phase of stochastic exploration between 30 and 55 iterations. This allows the framework to effectively ’migrate’ optimal parameter traits across different high-dimensional feature subspaces identified by Boruta, successfully surpassing other benchmarks and stabilizing at a peak accuracy of 98.52%. This confirms that BBO is not merely a general optimizer but an essential adaptive strategy for refining the hyperparameter space in complex mineralogical classification tasks.

Classification Results and Performance Evaluation

Based on the integrated Boruta-BBO-XGBoost framework, high-precision classification of bauxite ores from nine distinct mining areas was successfully achieved. To rigorously evaluate the reliability and generalization of the optimization process, the model was subjected to an 8-fold cross-validation protocol. As illustrated in Figure , the classification accuracy across different folds exhibited a minimal fluctuation range of only 0.901%, fluctuating between 98.214 and 99.115%. This marginal variance demonstrates the exceptional stability of the integrated framework and its robustness against data set partitioning bias.

8-fold cross-validation.

The proposed analytical pipeline was implemented within a standardized computational environment using Python 3.9. To eliminate stochastic bias and ensure that the experimental results are fully reproducible, a fixed random seed of 42 was strictly applied across all computational stages. This rigorous control of randomness encompassed the RobustScaler preprocessing phase, the iterative Boruta feature selection process, and the entire Bayesian Optimization (BO) framework. By maintaining this consistent execution environment, the integrity and reliability of the reported classification metrics are preserved across different hardware platforms.

The optimal configuration of the XGBoost classifier was determined through a rigorous hyperparameter tuning process employing a Bayesian Optimization (BO) framework. Conclusion

In this study, an integrated framework combining cavity-constrained laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (CC-LIBS) with Boruta feature selection and Bayesian Optimized XGBoost (BBO-XGBoost) was developed for the rapid and accurate classification of bauxite ores from nine different mining areas. By studying the influence of different sizes of cylindrical cavities on the spectrum, the cavity with a diameter of 5 mm was identified as optimal. Using the optimal cavity, the enhancement factors of Al, Fe and Ti reached 2.8, 3.1, and 2.6, respectively. The original contribution of this work lies in the synergistic coupling of the physical enhancement and the algorithmic refinement. To address the challenges of high-dimensional spectral data, the Boruta algorithm was employed to reduce the dimensionality from 12,248 features to 8 core features effectively. Crucially, to overcome the stagnation issues common in traditional heuristic methods, the BBO algorithm’s unique migration mechanism was utilized to navigate the hyperparameter space. The optimized XGBoost classifier achieved an exceptional classification accuracy of 0.9852, with a precision of 0.9861 and an F1-score of 0.9856, significantly outperforming traditional models. These results demonstrate that the synergistic combination of CC-LIBS and the BBO-XGBoost framework provides a robust and highly precise analytical solution for bauxite ore.

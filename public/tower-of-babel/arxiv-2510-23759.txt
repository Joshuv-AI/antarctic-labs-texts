Permutation modules over cyclic p p -groups
Marlon Estanislau

Abstract
Let G G be a cyclic p p -group for some prime number p > 0 p>0 and let R R be a complete discrete valuation ring in mixed characteristic. In this paper, we present a generalization of two results that characterize R ​ G RG -permutation modules, extending previous work by B. Torrecillas and Th. Weigel. Their original results were established under the assumption that p p is unramified in R R , whereas we extend their characterization to the case where p p may be ramified. Unlike prior approaches, our proofs rely solely on fundamental facts from group cohomology and a version of Weiss’ Theorem, avoiding deeper categorical techniques.

Introduction

Throughout this article,
G
is a cyclic
p
-group for some prime number

,

), and

(that is, with fraction field of characteristic

. In this work, we will deal with

, which are

Of particular importance are the

-
permutation modules
:

-lattices having an

-basis that is preserved set-wise by the multiplication of
G
. Such lattices are extremely well behaved and serve as a natural generalization of free modules. However, given a lattice, it may not be clear whether it is a permutation module. In 1988, A. Weiss provided the following criterion for permutation modules, which is of key importance, having applications in number theory
[
13
]
and block theory
[

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[

)
Let

denote the ring of

Later in 1993, Weiss generalized this result for finite extensions of

and recently J.W. MacQuarrie, P. Symonds and P.A. Zalesskii generalized Weiss’ Theorem to the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Special case of
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let

be a complete discrete valuation ring in mixed characteristic with
residue field of characteristic
p
, let
G
be a finite
p
-group and let
U
be an
RG
-lattice. Suppose there is a normal subgroup
N
of

such that:

1.

the module

restricted to

RN
-free,

2.

N

This last result has been applied in the study of profinite groups (cf.

]
) and calculation of Picard groups of blocks of finite groups (cf.
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[

) and will be fundamental for us.

Weiss’ Theorem cannot be applied to lattices for which

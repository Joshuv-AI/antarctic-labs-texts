The Topology of k k -Robust Clique Complexes in Grid-like Graphs
Marek Filakovský

Abstract
We introduce k k -robust clique complexes, a family of simplicial complexes that generalizes the traditional clique complex. Here, a subset of vertices forms a simplex provided it does not contain an independent set of size k k . We investigate these complexes for square sequence graphs, a class of bipartite graphs introduced here that are constructed by iteratively attaching C 4 C_{4} cycles. This class includes rectangular grid graphs G m , n G_{m,n} . We show that for k = 2 k=2 and k = 3 k=3 , the homotopy type is a wedge sum of ( 2 ​ k − 3 ) (2k-3) -dimensional spheres, a result we extend to arbitrary k k under specific structural constraints on the attachment sequence. Our approach utilizes König’s theorem to decompose the complex into manageable components, whose homotopy types are easy to understand. This then enables an inductive proof based on the decomposition and standard tools of algebraic topology. Finally, we utilize Alexander duality to connect our results to the study of total- k k -cut complexes, generalizing recent results concerning the homotopy types of total- k k -cut complexes for grid graphs.

.
Next, we assume the statement holds for square sequence graphs of length
i<n
and we will prove it for
n
. To this end, we essentially rewrite the proof of Theorem
1.2
, Case 1:

First, we decompose
\mathsf{Cliq}_{k}(H_{n})
using the decomposition Lemma
2.8
as a union of
K=\mathsf{Cliq}_{k}(H_{n-1})
and
L=\mathsf{Cliq}_{k-1}(H_{n-1})\oplus\mathsf{Cliq}_{2}(G_{n})
.
By induction,
\lVert K\rVert
is homotopy equivalent to
\bigvee_{\binom{n-2}{k-1}}S^{2k-3}
. Also by induction and the fact we are edge gluing, we obtain

\lVert L\rVert\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{n-2}{k-2}}S^{2{(k-1)}-3}\star S^{1}\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{n-2}{k-2}}S^{2k-3}.

The intersection of
K\cap L
is contractible and is contractible in both
K
and
L
from which we get that

\lVert\mathsf{Cliq}_{k}(H_{n})\rVert\simeq\lVert K\cup L\rVert\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{n-2}{k-1}}S^{2k-3}\vee\bigvee_{\binom{n-2}{k-2}}S^{3}\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}S^{2k-3}

by the binomial formula.
∎

Finally, we proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.4
:

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For
k=2
, the statement was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 4.16]
and further for
k=3
and in the case one of
m,n
equals
2
or
3
it was given in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

So let us assume that
m>n\geq 3
. By
Theorem
1.1
, we have

\lVert\mathsf{Cliq}_{3}(G_{m,n})\rVert\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{(m-1)(n-1)}{2}}S^{3},

The Alexander duality (Corollary
2.14
) implies that the homology and cohomology groups of
\lVert\Delta_{3}^{t}(G)\rVert
are concentrated in dimension
mn-6
and are equal to
\bigoplus_{\binom{(m-1)(n-1)}{2}}\mathbb{Z}
. We thus only have to verify that
\pi_{1}(\lVert\Delta_{3}^{t}(G_{m,n})\rVert)=0
and the rest follows from the combination of theorems of Whitehead and Hurewicz (Theorem
2.12
).

It suffices to show that

\Delta_{3}^{t}(G_{m,n})=\{W\subseteq V(G_{m,n})\mid(V(G_{m,n})\setminus W)\supseteq\sigma\in I_{3}(G_{m,n})\}

contains all
2
-simplices = subsets of vertices of size
3
, since then all cycles have a filler. However, in case
m,n\geq 3
, this condition is trivially satisfied: the graph
G_{3,3}
is bipartite with one part containing
4
and other
5
vertices, so for any set of vertices
W
of size
3
,
V(G)\setminus W
contains an independent set of size
3
. So we can see that for
m,n\geq 3
,
\pi_{1}(\lVert\Delta_{3}^{t}(G_{m,n})\rVert)=0
and thus

\lVert\Delta_{3}^{t}(G_{m,n})\rVert\simeq\bigvee_{\binom{(m-1)(n-1)}{2}}S^{3}.

∎

Line Multigraphs of Hypergraphs
Kauê Cardoso

Abstract
. A line multigraph is obtained from a hypergraph as follows: the vertices of the multigraph correspond to the hyperedges of the hypergraph, and the number of edges between two vertices is given by the number of vertices shared by the corresponding hyperedges. In this paper, we establish several structural and spectral properties of this class of multigraphs. More precisely, we show that important structural characteristics, such as connectivity, linearity, and regularity are, in some sense, preserved between a hypergraph and its line multigraph. We also prove that the eigenvalues of the line multigraph associated with a general hypergraph of rank r r are greater than or equal to − r -r , which generalizes a fundamental spectral property of line graphs. Furthermore, we provide sufficient conditions for − r -r to be an eigenvalue of the line multigraph. Finally, we present applications of line multigraphs to the spectral theory of hypergraphs, including bounds for the signless Laplacian spectral radius of a hypergraph and a characterization of the signless Laplacian spectrum for a specific class of hypergraphs. Keywords. General hypergraph; Line multigraph; Signless Laplacian matrix; Spectral radius; Power hypergraph. AMS classification. 05C65; 05C50; 15A18.



1.
Introduction

In spectral graph theory, the structural properties of graphs are investigated through the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of associated matrices. Motivated by this framework, some matrix representations have been proposed to extend spectral methods to hypergraphs
[
11
,
18
]
. A notable development was the adjacency tensor approach introduced by Cooper and Dutle
[
9
]
; however, computing tensor eigenvalues is often computationally and theoretically demanding
[
13
]
. Consequently, there has been a renewed interest in matrix-based representations that unite theoretical robustness with computational tractability,
[
1
,
7
]
.

Line graphs have been a significant research topic in graph theory
[
2
,
3
,
15
]
. Other terms commonly used to refer to line graphs include representative graphs and intersection graphs. Motivated by the success of results obtained for this class of graphs, researchers have studied generalizations of the line graph concept. In particular, intersection graphs of hypergraphs were an important topic
[
16
,
20
]
. In this work, we adopt a slightly different definition, termed the line multigraph. It is worth noting that these two definitions coincide when restricted to linear hypergraphs. The definition of a line multigraph arises naturally when studying the incidence matrix of a hypergraph
[
7
]
, providing a more convenient framework to investigate spectral properties.

Structural properties of hypergraphs are a primary focus of this paper. In
[
8
]
, the authors define a class of hypergraphs known as edge-regular, where the sum of vertex degrees in each hyperedge is constant. Here we generalize this concept and prove that a line multigraph is regular if and only if its associated hypergraph is skew edge-regular. We further explore deeper structural implications, such as how configurations called collars (a generalization of even cycles) in hypergraphs influence the properties of their line multigraphs. Additionally, we address the non-uniqueness of these structures by demonstrating how distinct hypergraphs can produce identical line multigraphs.

The study of line graph eigenvalues is a well-established research branch within spectral graph theory, as evidenced by
[
10
]
and its references. The primary spectral characteristic of this class of graphs is that their eigenvalues are greater than or equal to
-2
. Here, we generalize this fundamental result by proving that the eigenvalues of a line multigraph associated with a hypergraph of rank
r
are greater than or equal to
-r
. We also establish a sufficient condition for
-r
to be an eigenvalue, namely that the hypergraph contains a collar as a sub-hypergraph.

Power hypergraphs have emerged as an important class, with several researchers investigating their spectra in the context of both matrices and tensors
[
5
,
7
,
14
,
17
]
. However, few advancements have been made regarding non-uniform power hypergraphs. Here, we apply our line multigraph results to determine the signless Laplacian spectrum of (general) power hypergraphs. We also derive upper and lower bounds for the signless Laplacian spectral radius of general hypergraphs, demonstrating that these bounds are attained if and only if the hypergraph is uniform and edge-regular.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the fundamental definitions and notation. In Section
3

4
we derive spectral properties of the adjacency matrix of line multigraphs, generalizing classical results from graph theory. In Section
5
, we apply the theory of line multigraphs to obtain bounds and spectral characterizations for the signless Laplacian matrix of general hypergraphs. Finally, Section
6
presents concluding remarks and a list of open problems to guide future research.

Definition 2.1
.

A
hypergraph

\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
consists of a finite set of vertices
V(\mathcal{H})
and a set of hyperedges
E(\mathcal{H})
, where each hyperedge is a non-empty subset of
V
. A hypergraph is called
simple
if it contains no hyperedges of cardinality one and no hyperedge is contained in another. Throughout this work, we consider exclusively simple hypergraphs; therefore, for brevity, the term simple will be omitted hereafter. The
rank
and the
co-rank
of a hypergraph are defined as the largest and smallest cardinality of its hyperedges, respectively. For
k\geq 2
, a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-uniform
if all its hyperedges have cardinality
k
. In this sense, a simple graph is precisely a
2
-uniform hypergraph.

Definition 2.2
.

A
multigraph

\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is defined by a finite set of vertices
V(\mathcal{G})
and a multiset of edges
E(\mathcal{G})
, where each edge consists of a set containing two vertices. For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, its
line multigraph

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is the multigraph where
V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))=E(\mathcal{H})
. The number of edges (multiplicity) connecting two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is equal to the cardinality of the intersection between their corresponding hyperedges
e_{u},e_{v}\in E(\mathcal{H})
; that is, the multiplicity is given by
|e_{u}\cap e_{v}|
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph or a multigraph. The
(hyper)edge neighborhood
of a vertex
v\in V
, denoted by
E_{[v]}
, is the set of all (hyper)edges containing
v
. The
degree
of a vertex
v\in V
, denoted by
d(v)
, is the number of (hyper)edges containing
v
; specifically,
d(v)=|E_{[v]}|
. A vertex is said to be
isolated
if its degree is zero. To avoid ambiguity when dealing with multiple structures simultaneously, we denote the degree as
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
. The
maximum
,
minimum
, and
average degrees
of
\mathcal{H}
are defined, respectively, as:

\Delta(\mathcal{H})=\max_{v\in V}\{d(v)\},\quad\delta(\mathcal{H})=\min_{v\in V}\{d(v)\},\quad\text{and}\quad d(\mathcal{H})=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{v\in V}d(v).

Definition 2.5
.

In either a multigraph or a hypergraph, a
path
is an alternating sequence of distinct vertices and (hyper)edges such that each (hyper)edge contains the vertices immediately preceding and following it in the sequence. A hypergraph or a multigraph is said to be
connected
if there exists a path between every pair of vertices; otherwise, it is
disconnected
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph without isolated vertices. Then,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is connected if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is connected.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{H}
is connected, there exists a path between any two vertices. Consequently, any two hyperedges can be linked by a path, which implies that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
contains a path between any two of its vertices.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is connected, any two of its vertices are connected by a path, implying that any two hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
are connected by a path. Since
\mathcal{H}
contains no isolated vertices, any two vertices in
\mathcal{H}
are also connected by a path.
∎

Lemma
2.6
establishes that connectivity is preserved between a hypergraph and its line multigraph. In this work, we consider only connected hypergraphs; consequently, their associated line multigraphs are also connected. To simplify the exposition, we shall henceforth omit explicit mention of connectivity and refer to these structures simply as hypergraphs.

Definition 2.7
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
linear
if every pair of distinct hyperedges shares at most one vertex.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. The line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a simple graph if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is linear.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a simple graph. By definition, any two distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
are joined by at most one edge. Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\mathcal{H})
be the hyperedges corresponding to
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively. Since the number of edges between
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is given by
|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|
, it follows that
|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|\leq 1
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}
is linear.

Conversely, assume
\mathcal{H}
is linear. By definition, any two distinct hyperedges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\mathcal{H})
share at most one vertex; that is,
|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|\leq 1
. Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be the vertices in the line multigraph representing
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. The multiplicity of the edge connecting
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
is
|e_{1}\cap e_{2}|
, which is at most one. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
contains no multiple edges and is, consequently, a simple graph.
∎

The definitions of the intersection graph and the line multigraph of a hypergraph coincide for linear hypergraphs. More fundamentally, many properties depend only on the existence of adjacencies between vertices, not on edge multiplicities. Consequently, much of the established theory of intersection graphs extends directly to the setting adopted here. For this reason, we focus primarily on characteristics that specifically exploit edge multiplicities.

3.
Structural properties of line multigraphs

In this section, we investigate the relationship between the structural characteristics of a hypergraph and those of its corresponding line multigraph. Specifically, we examine how properties governed by the adjacency and intersection patterns of hyperedges are preserved or transformed in the line multigraph structure. Our analysis focuses on fundamental parameters such as degree distributions and regularity conditions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be its line multigraph. If
u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is the vertex corresponding to the hyperedge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, then:

d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\left(\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)-|e|.

Proof.

For each vertex
v\in e
, there are exactly
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1
hyperedges, other than
e
, that contain
v
. By the construction of the line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, each of these hyperedges corresponds to a vertex adjacent to
u
via an edge generated by the shared vertex
v
. By summing the contributions of every vertex
v\in e
, we conclude that the degree of the vertex
u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is:

d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)=\left(\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)-|e|.

∎

Definition 3.2
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is
regular
if there exists a constant
d
such that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=d
for all
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
.

The concept of edge-regularity was introduced as an edge-based analogue to traditional vertex regularity
[
8
]
. A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
edge-regular
if for every hyperedge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, the sum of the degrees of its incident vertices is invariant. To extend the utility of this property to non-uniform structures, we propose the following generalization:

Definition 3.3
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is
skew edge-regular
if there exists a constant
D
such that:

\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)=D,\quad\forall e\in E(\mathcal{H}).

This modification stems from analyzing incidence patterns in general hypergraphs. The sum of
(d(v)-1)
normalizes the contribution of each hyperedge based on its intersection potential, eliminating distortions caused by degree-one vertices. The following lemma demonstrates that for uniform hypergraphs, these two definitions are perfectly consistent.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph. Then
\mathcal{H}
is edge-regular if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is skew edge-regular.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-uniform,
|e|=k
for every
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
. The result follows immediately from the identity
\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)=\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-k
. Since
k
is constant, the sum of degrees
\sum d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
is invariant across all hyperedges if and only if the sum
\sum(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)
is also invariant.
∎

Example 3.5
.

For graphs (2-uniform hypergraphs), edge-regularity and skew edge-regularity coincide (Lemma
3.4
), and both are equivalent to the graph being either regular or semi-regular bipartite (Example 4.10 in
[
8
]
). These are precisely the 2-uniform structures with constant degree sums across edges.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. The line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is regular if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is skew edge-regular.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the degree of any vertex
u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is given by
d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)
, where
e
is the hyperedge corresponding to
u
. It follows that
d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)
is a constant for all vertices in the line multigraph if and only if the sum
\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)
is invariant across all hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
. By Definition
3.3
, this is precisely the condition for
\mathcal{H}
to be skew edge-regular.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is an
d
-regular hypergraph, the degree of a vertex
u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
corresponding to the hyperedge
e
simplifies to
d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=|e|(d-1)
. This follows directly from Lemma
3.1
by substituting
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=d
for all
v\in e
. In particular, if
\mathcal{H}
is both
k
-uniform and
d
-regular, its line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is
k(d-1)
-regular.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. Its
Zagreb index
is defined as the sum of the squares of the degrees of its vertices. Formally,

Z(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)^{2}.

The Zagreb index is a fundamental parameter in graph theory, frequently utilized in chemistry applications
[
12
]
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with
n
vertices. If
m_{\mathcal{L}}
is the number of edges in the line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, then:

m_{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Z(\mathcal{H})-nd(\mathcal{H})\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the sum of the degrees in the line multigraph is related to the degrees in the hypergraph. Applying this to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we have:

2m_{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))}d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H})}\left(\sum_{v\in e}(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)\right).

Since each vertex
v
appears in exactly
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
hyperedges, we can rearrange the summation over the vertex set of
\mathcal{H}
as follows:

\displaystyle 2m_{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)^{2}-\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=Z(\mathcal{H})-nd(\mathcal{H}).

The result follows by dividing by 2.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, a mapping
f:E(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\{1,\dots,p\}
is an
edge
p
-coloring
if, for every pair of adjacent hyperedges
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(\mathcal{H})
, we have
f(e_{i})\neq f(e_{j})
.

Definition 3.11
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a
collar
if it is
2
-regular and admits an edge
2
-coloring
f:E(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\{1,2\}
. For each hyperedge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, if
f(e)=1
, we say
e
is an
odd edge
; if
f(e)=2
, we say
e
is an
even edge
. Consequently, every vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
is incident to exactly one even edge and one odd edge.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. If
\mathcal{H}
is a collar, then its line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is bipartite.

Proof.

By the definition of a collar, there exists an edge
2
-coloring
f:E(\mathcal{H})\to\{1,2\}
. Let
U
and
W
be the sets of odd and even hyperedges, respectively. Since
f
is an edge
2
-coloring, no two hyperedges within
U
(or
W
) are adjacent in
\mathcal{H}
. As the vertex set of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
corresponds to
E(\mathcal{H})
and adjacency is preserved, there are no edges within
U
or
W
in the line multigraph. Thus,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is bipartite.∎

Remark 3.14
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-uniform collar, then
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is both bipartite and
k
-regular. This regularity is a consequence of the collar being
2
-regular by definition; substituting
|e|=k
and
d=2
into the degree formula
d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=|e|(d-1)
, we obtain
d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=k(2-1)=k
for every vertex
u
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a multigraph with maximum degree
\Delta
. There exists a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with rank
r=\Delta
such that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a multigraph. We construct the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
by defining its vertex set as
V(\mathcal{H})=E(\mathcal{G})
and its hyperedge set as
E(\mathcal{H})=\{e_{u}:u\in V(\mathcal{G})\}
, where each hyperedge
e_{u}
consists of the edges in
\mathcal{G}
that are incident to vertex
u
. By this construction, the cardinality of each hyperedge is
|e_{u}|=d_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
, which implies that
\text{rank}(\mathcal{H})=\max_{u\in V(\mathcal{G})}d_{\mathcal{G}}(u)=\Delta(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover, the number of vertices shared between two distinct hyperedges
e_{u}
and
e_{v}
in
\mathcal{H}
is, by definition, the number of edges connecting
u
and
v
in
\mathcal{G}
. Thus, both the adjacency and the multiplicity in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
perfectly coincide with those of
\mathcal{G}
, yielding
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{G}
.∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. Suppose there exists a hyperedge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
such that
|e|\geq 3
and there exists a vertex
v\in e
with
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=1
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained by removing
v
, defined by
V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})=V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}
and
E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})=(E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{e\})\cup\{e\setminus\{v\}\}
. Then,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By definition, the vertex set
V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is in bijective correspondence with the edge set
E(\mathcal{H})
. Since the transformation from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
only modifies the internal composition of the hyperedge
e
without removing the edge itself,
|E(\mathcal{H})|=|E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|
, and we may identify
V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
with
V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}))
.

The multiplicity of the edge connecting any two vertices
u_{i},u_{j}\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is given by the cardinality of the intersection of their corresponding hyperedges
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(\mathcal{H})
. Because
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=1
, the vertex
v
belongs exclusively to
e
. Consequently,
v\notin e_{j}
for any
e_{j}\neq e
, which implies that
e\cap e_{j}=(e\setminus\{v\})\cap e_{j}
. Since no other hyperedge intersections are affected by the removal of
v
, all edge multiplicities are preserved. Thus,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

For any hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with rank
r
, there exists an
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We construct
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{H}
by expanding each hyperedge to cardinality
r
. For every hyperedge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
where
|e|<r
, we add
r-|e|
new distinct vertices to
e
, defining each of these new vertices to have degree one. According to Lemma
3.16
, the addition of such degree-one vertices does not alter the intersections between hyperedges. Consequently, the resulting line multigraph remains invariant. Thus,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
r
-uniform and satisfies
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 3.18
.

For any hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, there exist infinitely many hypergraphs that share the same line multigraph.

Proof.

Let
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
be an arbitrary hyperedge. We construct an infinite family of hypergraphs
\{\mathcal{H}(e,p):p\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where each
\mathcal{H}(e,p)
is obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by adding
p
new distinct vertices of degree one to the hyperedge
e
. By Lemma
3.16
, the addition of degree-one vertices does not alter the line multigraph structure. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}(e,p))=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The invariance of line multigraphs under the addition or removal of degree-one vertices suggests two complementary analytical strategies:

Uniformization:
Represent any hypergraph as
k
-uniform by padding hyperedges with degree-one vertices until they reach the rank
k
. This allows results derived for the uniform case to be generalized to non-uniform structures of the same rank.

Core Reduction:
Systematically remove degree-one vertices from hyperedges with
|e|\geq 3
to obtain a simplified core hypergraph. This reduction preserves the line multigraph while significantly decreasing the incidence complexity of the structure.

These strategies enable the selection of the most efficient representation for both theoretical analysis and computational purposes.

4.
Spectral properties of line multigraphs

In this section, we investigate the spectral properties of the adjacency matrix of a line multigraph by examining its relationship with the incidence matrix of the associated hypergraph. These results generalize several fundamental properties known for the line graphs of simple graphs
[
10
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a hypergraph. The
incidence matrix
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H})
, is a
|V|\times|E|
matrix with entries
b_{ve}
defined as:

b_{ve}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }v\in e,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
cardinality matrix

\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H})
is the
|E|\times|E|
diagonal matrix where the
i
-th diagonal entry is given by
c_{ii}=|e_{i}|
, representing the cardinality of the hyperedge
e_{i}\in E
. For
k
-uniform hypergraphs,
\mathbf{C}=k\mathbf{I}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a multigraph. Its
adjacency matrix

\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{G})
is a square matrix of order
|V|
where the diagonal entries are
a_{vv}=0
. For
v\neq u
, the entry
a_{vu}
denotes the number of edges incident to both vertices
u
and
v
in
\mathcal{G}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with incidence matrix
\mathbf{B}
and cardinality matrix
\mathbf{C}
. If
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
is the adjacency matrix of its line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, then:

\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
. By the definition of matrix multiplication, the entry
m_{ij}
is the inner product of the
i
-th and
j
-th columns of
\mathbf{B}
. Since these columns are the incidence vectors of hyperedges
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
, respectively,
m_{ij}
counts the number of vertices in the intersection
e_{i}\cap e_{j}
. We distinguish two cases:

Diagonal entries:
The entry
m_{ii}
represents
|e_{i}\cap e_{i}|=|e_{i}|
. These entries form the diagonal cardinality matrix
\mathbf{C}
.

Off-diagonal entries (
i\neq j
):
The entry
m_{ij}=|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|
. By the construction of the line multigraph, the number of edges between the vertices corresponding to
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
is precisely the number of shared vertices in
\mathcal{H}
. These entries form the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Combining these cases, we obtain
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
. If
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
of the line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\lambda\geq-r
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be a normalized eigenvector of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
. By Theorem
4.3
, we have the identity
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
. Since
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
is positive semi-definite; them its quadratic form is non-negative:

\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\mathbf{x}\geq 0.

Expanding this expression, we obtain:

\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{x}\geq 0\implies\sum_{e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})}|e_{i}|x_{i}^{2}+\lambda\geq 0.

By the definition of rank,
|e_{i}|\leq r
for all
e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})
. Since
x_{i}^{2}\geq 0
, it follows that:

\sum_{e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})}|e_{i}|x_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})}rx_{i}^{2}=r\sum_{e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})}x_{i}^{2}=r.

Substituting this inequality back into the expanded quadratic form, we have
r+\lambda\geq 0
, which implies
\lambda\geq-r
.∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
and
m
edges. The value
-r
is an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix from the line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if there exists a non-zero vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that:

\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H})\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
;

x_{i}=0
for every hyperedge
e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})
with cardinality
|e_{i}|<r
.

Proof.

Consider the identity
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
. If
\mathbf{x}
is an eigenvector of the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
associated with the eigenvalue
-r
, then:

\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\mathbf{x}\quad\implies\quad||\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}||^{2}=\sum_{e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{H})}(|e_{i}|-r)x_{i}^{2}.

Since
|e_{i}|\leq r
for all
i
, the right-hand side is a sum of non-positive terms, while the left-hand side is non-negative. Thus, equality holds if and only if both sides vanish independently. Specifically,
\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}=0
implies
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
, and the summation vanishes if and only if
(|e_{i}|-r)x_{i}^{2}=0
for each
i
. This condition is satisfied if
x_{i}=0
whenever
|e_{i}|<r
. Conversely, if these two conditions hold,
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
and
(\mathbf{C}-r\mathbf{I})\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
, which directly implies
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{x}=-r\mathbf{x}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

For a
k
-uniform hypergraph, the second condition is trivially satisfied since
|e_{i}|=k
for all
i
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be two hypergraphs. We say that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a

of

must satisfy
e\subseteq V^{\prime}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph with
m
hyperedges. If
\mathcal{H}
contains a
k
-uniform collar
\mathcal{C}
as a sub-hypergraph, then
\lambda=-k
is an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
of its line multigraph.

Proof.

To prove that
-k
is an eigenvalue, by Lemma
4.5
, it suffices to show there exists a non-zero vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
.

Let
E(\mathcal{C})\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})
be the set of hyperedges in the collar sub-hypergraph. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a collar, it admits an edge
2
-coloring
f:E(\mathcal{C})\to\{1,2\}
. We define the vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as:

x_{i}=\begin{cases}\;\;\,1&\text{if }e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{C})\text{ and }f(e_{i})=1,\\
-1&\text{if }e_{i}\in E(\mathcal{C})\text{ and }f(e_{i})=2,\\
\;\;\,0&\text{if }e_{i}\notin E(\mathcal{C}).\end{cases}

Now, we examine the entries of the product
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}
for each vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
:

Case 1 (
v\notin V(\mathcal{C})
):
If the vertex
v
is not part of the collar, it is not incident to any hyperedge
e_{i}
for which
x_{i}\neq 0
. Thus,
(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x})_{v}=0
.

Case 2 (
v\in V(\mathcal{C})
):
Since
\mathcal{C}
is a
2
-regular sub-hypergraph,
v
is incident to exactly two hyperedges in
E(\mathcal{C})
. By the definition of the edge
2
-coloring of a collar, one of these hyperedges must have color
1
(
x_{i}=1
) and the other must have color
2
(
x_{j}=-1
). Consequently,
(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x})_{v}=1+(-1)=0.

In both cases, we find that
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
. Since
\mathbf{x}
is a non-zero vector and
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-uniform, it follows that
\lambda=-k
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

The characterization of
-r
as an eigenvalue via collars completes our generalization of classical line graph spectral theory. These configurations, being
2
-regular hypergraphs whose line multigraphs are bipartite, serve as the natural hypergraph analogue of even cycles. Their role in determining eigenvalue structure underscores the deep connection between hypergraph topology and the spectral properties of associated line multigraphs.

Applications to Spectral Hypergraph Theory

In this section, we apply line multigraph theory to the spectral study of hypergraphs, focusing on the signless Laplacian matrix. For a comprehensive treatment of these connections, we refer to

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with incidence matrix
\mathbf{B}
. The
signless Laplacian matrix
of
\mathcal{H}
is defined as the
|V|\times|V|
matrix:

\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}.

For an irreducible and non-negative matrix, the
spectral radius

\rho(\mathcal{H})
is defined as its largest eigenvalue, as guaranteed by the Perron-Frobenius Theorem.

Remark 5.2
.

The entries of
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{H})=(q_{uv})
have a direct combinatorial interpretation: the diagonal entries
q_{vv}
are the vertex degrees
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
, while the off-diagonal entries
q_{uv}
represent the number of hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
that contain both vertices
u
and
v
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
and co-rank
s
. If
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
is the adjacency matrix of its line multigraph, then:

\rho(\mathbf{Q})-r\leq\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\leq\rho(\mathbf{Q})-s.

Furthermore, either equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is uniform.

Proof.

First, observe that
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
share the same spectral radius. Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{|E|}
be a normalized vector. Since
s
and
r
are the minimum and maximum diagonal entries of
\mathbf{C}
, respectively, we have

(1)

s\leq\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}\leq r.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be the normalized principal eigenvector of
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
. Then:

\rho(\mathbf{Q})=\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{u}\leq r+\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}).

Let
\mathbf{v}
be the normalized principal eigenvector of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
. Then:

\rho(\mathbf{Q})\geq\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}+\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\geq s+\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}).

Equality holds if and only if the inequalities in (
1
) become equalities. For connected hypergraphs, this occurs precisely when
\mathbf{C}
is a scalar matrix, that is, when
\mathcal{H}
is uniform.
∎

A version of Theorem
5.4
was previously established for uniform hypergraphs in
[
8
,
7
]
. Here, we generalize this result to general hypergraphs by applying line multigraph theory.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
and co-rank
s
. If
\rho(\mathbf{Q})
denotes the spectral radius of its signless Laplacian matrix, then:

\min_{e\in E}\left\{\sum_{v\in e}d(v)\right\}-(r-s)\leq\rho(\mathbf{Q})\leq\max_{e\in E}\left\{\sum_{v\in e}d(v)\right\}+(r-s).

Either equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is both uniform and edge-regular.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be a vertex in the line multigraph corresponding to the hyperedge
e_{u}\in E(\mathcal{H})
. By Lemma
3.1
, the degree of
u
in the line multigraph is:

d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\left(\sum_{v\in e_{u}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)-|e_{u}|.

From Theorem
5.3
, we have the following bounds for the spectral radius:

(2)

\rho(\mathbf{Q})-r\leq\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\leq\rho(\mathbf{Q})-s.

It is a well-established result that the spectral radius of the adjacency matrix is bounded by the minimum and maximum degrees
[
19
]
:

(3)

\min_{u\in V(\mathcal{L})}d_{\mathcal{L}}(u)\leq\rho(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}})\leq\max_{u\in V(\mathcal{L})}d_{\mathcal{L}}(u).

Combining the lower bound of (
2
) with the upper bound of (
3
), we obtain:

\rho(\mathbf{Q})-r\leq\max_{e\in E}\left\{\left(\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)-|e|\right\}\leq\max_{e\in E}\left\{\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right\}-s,

Similarly, for the lower bound:

\rho(\mathbf{Q})-s\geq\min_{e\in E}\left\{\left(\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)-|e|\right\}\geq\min_{e\in E}\left\{\sum_{v\in e}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right\}-r,

The equalities hold if and only if both (
2
) and (
3
) hold as equalities. The first condition implies that
\mathcal{H}
is uniform, and the second implies that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is regular. By Theorem
3.6
, a line multigraph of a uniform hypergraph is regular if and only if the hypergraph is edge-regular. Thus, equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is uniform and edge-regular.∎

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)

contains the entire set

if and only if

Hyperedge Padding:
For each hyperedge
e_{t}\in E(\mathcal{H}_{t})

\tau_{e}
of

The signless Laplacian spectrum of power hypergraphs was previously investigated for the uniform case in
[
7
]
. Here, applying line multigraph theory, we extend these results to general hypergraphs and provide a significantly more concise proof. By analyzing the structural transformation of the line multigraph, specifically the scaling of its adjacency matrix, we circumvent the combinatorial complexities inherent to non-uniform incidence structures.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
. For any integer
k\geq r
, the line multigraph of the power hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{k}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

The structure of the line multigraph
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is uniquely determined by the intersection cardinalities
|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|
for all pairs of distinct hyperedges
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(\mathcal{H})
. By definition,
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is obtained by adjoining vertices of degree one to each hyperedge. Since a vertex
v
with
d(v)=1
is incident to exactly one hyperedge, it cannot belong to the intersection of any two distinct hyperedges. Consequently, the cardinality
|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|
remains invariant for all
i\neq j
under the transformation from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
. Since the intersection sizes are preserved, the resulting line multigraphs are identical, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k})=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a multigraph and
t\geq 1
be an integer. The
scaled multigraph

t\cdot\mathcal{G}
is the multigraph obtained from
\mathcal{G}
as follows:

The vertex set is preserved:
V(t\cdot\mathcal{G})=V(\mathcal{G})
;

The edge multiplicities are scaled by
t
: if a pair of vertices is connected by
p
edges in
\mathcal{G}
, then they are connected by
tp
edges in
t\cdot\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. If
t\geq 1
is an integer, then
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{t})=t\cdot\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

By construction, the hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}_{t}
are in one-to-one correspondence with those of
\mathcal{H}
, implying
V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{t}))=V(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
. Let
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(\mathcal{H})
be two distinct hyperedges with an intersection of cardinality
p=|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|
. In the expanded hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, each vertex
v\in e_{i}\cap e_{j}
is replaced by a set
\tau_{v}
of
t
vertices, all of which are contained in the intersection of the corresponding hyperedges
(e_{i})_{t},(e_{j})_{t}\in E(\mathcal{H}_{t})
. Consequently,
|(e_{i})_{t}\cap(e_{j})_{t}|=\sum_{v\in e_{i}\cap e_{j}}|\tau_{v}|=t\cdot p
. By the definition of a line multigraph, the multiplicity of the edge connecting
(e_{i})_{t}
and
(e_{j})_{t}
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{t})
is exactly
t
times the multiplicity of the edge connecting
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. This satisfies the definition of the scaled multigraph, thus
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{t})=t\cdot\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.∎

Remark 5.10
.

Note that Corollary
5.7
and Lemma
5.9
together imply the matrix identity
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})=t\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})
Observe that this scaling depends entirely on the vertex expansion factor and is independent of edge padding.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with rank
r
and
m
edges. For integers
t\geq 1
and
k\geq rt
, let
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t}
be its general power hypergraph. Then:

\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})}(\lambda)=t^{m}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}(\lambda/t).

Consequently,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
t\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})
.

Proof.

By applying Remark
5.10
, we have the scaling matrix identity
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})=t\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})
. The characteristic polynomial is then:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{m}-\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{m}-t\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{m}\det\left((\lambda/t)\mathbf{I}_{m}-\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{m}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}(\lambda/t).

The linear relationship between the eigenvalues follows directly from the roots of the transformed characteristic polynomial.
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with
n
vertices and
m
edges, and let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}=0
be the eigenvalues of its signless Laplacian matrix. Then the eigenvalues of the signless Laplacian matrix of the power hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t}
are:

i.

(t\lambda_{1}+(k-rt)),\;\ldots,\;(t\lambda_{p}+(k-rt))
,

ii.

(k-rt)
with multiplicity
m-p
,

iii.

0
with multiplicity
(k-rt-1)m+tn
.

Proof.

By the definition of a power hypergraph,
|V(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})|=tn+m(k-rt)
. Since
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
share the same non-zero eigenvalues, we have:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\det(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{tn+m(k-rt)}-\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+m(k-rt)-m}\det(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{m}-\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+(k-rt-1)m}\det\left(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{m}-\big[\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})\big]-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+(k-rt-1)m}\det\left(\lambda\mathbf{I}_{m}-\big[(k-rt)\mathbf{I}_{m}+t\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H})\big]-t\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+(k-rt-1)m}\det\left([\lambda-(k-rt)]\mathbf{I}_{m}-t[\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{H})+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H})]\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+(k-rt-1)m}\det\left([\lambda-(k-rt)]\mathbf{I}_{m}-t[\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{H})\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H})]\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{tn+(k-rt-1)m}t^{m}\det\left(\left[\frac{\lambda-(k-rt)}{t}\right]\mathbf{I}_{m}-\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{H})\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H})\right).

Therefore, for each non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
of
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{H})\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we obtain the eigenvalue
\mu_{i}=t\lambda_{i}+(k-rt)
for
\mathbf{Q}(\mathcal{H}^{k}_{t})
. For each of the
(m-p)
zero eigenvalues of
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}
, we obtain the eigenvalue
(k-rt)
. Finally, the factor
\lambda^{tn+m(k-rt-1)}
accounts for the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
.
∎

In summary, line multigraphs serve as a powerful bridge that extends spectral results from uniform to general hypergraphs. This framework simplifies the study of non-uniform structures by reducing complex incidence relations to structural scaling, as elegantly demonstrated by the spectral characterization of power hypergraphs.

6.
Conclusion

In this study, we established the line multigraph as a robust framework for mapping hyperedge-dependent properties, such as connectivity, edge-regularity, and the presence of collars, to vertex-dependent properties in a multigraph setting. We demonstrated that the line multigraph spectrum is bounded below by
-r
, a bound whose attainment is intrinsically linked to the existence of collar sub-hypergraphs. These results allowed us to generalize spectral radius bounds for the signless Laplacian of non-uniform hypergraphs and characterize the spectrum of general power hypergraphs. Our findings underscore that line multigraphs are not merely auxiliary tools, but combinatorial structures with rich spectral properties that bridge the gap between uniform and general hypergraph theories. As this field matures, we anticipate that line multigraphs will provide the machinery to tackle problems that transcend classical graph theory. To stimulate further research, we propose the following open problems:

(1)

Given that line multigraphs simplify the analysis of non-uniform structures, which classical results previously restricted to uniform hypergraphs can be generalized using this framework?

(2)

Can edge-regular hypergraphs and collars be established as fundamental building blocks in spectral hypergraph theory, analogous to regular graphs and cycles in graph theory?

(3)

Is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
-r
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
related to the maximal number of independent collar sub-hypergraphs in
\mathcal{H}
?

(4)

Can we characterize the class of hypergraphs such that
\lambda(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))>-r
? For instance, identifying the hypergraph analogues of trees and odd-unicyclic graphs where the lower bound
-2
is not attained.

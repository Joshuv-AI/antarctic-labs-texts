Transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras
Jiarou Jin, Yanyong Hong (corresponding author)

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the definition of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras, whose affinization are transposed Poisson algebras. Moreover, we show that there is a natural transposed Poisson algebra structure on the tensor product of a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra and a right differential Novikov-Poisson algebra. A transposed Poisson algebra also naturally arises from a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra by taking the commutator Lie algebra of the Novikov algebra. We show that the tensor products of two transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras are also transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. Several constructions of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras are presented. Moreover, transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras are closely related to 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -derivations of the associated Novikov algebras. By using 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -derivations, we show that there are non-trivial transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra structures on solvable Novikov algebras with some conditions. We also prove that if a non-trivial transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra is simple, then the associated Novikov algebra is simple. Therefore, if the base field is algebraically closed and of characteristic 0, then any simple transposed Novikov-Poisson is of dimension 1 1 . Transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra structures on some simple Novikov algebras are also characterized.

1.
Introduction

Poisson algebras originated in the 1970s from the study of Poisson geometry
[
20
,
28
]
, which now serve as essential tools in fields such as symplectic geometry, quantization theory, integrable systems, and quantum groups.
A dual notion of the Poisson algebra called transposed Poisson algebra was introduced in
[
2
]
by exchanging the roles of the two binary operations in the Leibniz rule defining the Poisson algebra. More precisely, a triple
(L,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is called a
transposed Poisson algebra
, if
(L,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra and the following compatibility condition holds:

(1)

\displaystyle 2z\cdot[x,y]=[z\cdot x,y]+[x,z\cdot y]\quad\text{for all}\>x,y,z\in L.

As shown in
[
2
]
, transposed Poisson algebras share many common properties of Poisson algebras such as closure undertaking tensor products and Koszul self-duality as an operad, and are closely related with other algebraic structures including Novikov-Poisson algebras and 3-Lie algebras. They also coincide with commutative Gel’fand-Dorfman algebras
[
26
]
and are instances of algebras of Jordan brackets
[
7
]
. Furthermore, the classical limit of a Novikov deformation of a commutative associative algebra yields a transposed Poisson algebra
[
5
]
.
A significant connection between
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of Lie
algebras and transposed Poisson algebras was established in
[
9
]
. This connection has been used to classify all transposed Poisson algebra structures on various important classes of Lie algebras, including finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebras
[
9
]
, Witt algebra
[
9
]
, Virasoro algebra
[
9
]
, twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
33
]
, Schrödinger-Virasoro algebra
[
33
]
, solvable and perfect Lie algebras
[
17
]
, Virasoro-type algebras
[
19
,
36
]
and Lie incidence algebras
[
18
]
. Simple transposed Poisson algebras have been studied in
[
7
]
and a bialgebra theory of such algebras was given in
[
21
]
.

Novikov algebras first appeared in the study of Hamiltonian
operators in the formal variational calculus
[
10
,
11
]
and Poisson brackets of hydrodynamic type
[
1
]
. By the result in
[
30
]
, they also correspond to a class of Lie conformal algebras which describe the singular part of operator product expansion of chiral fields in conformal field
theory
[
14

.
A
(left) Novikov algebra

(A,\circ)
is a vector space
A
equipped with a binary operation
\circ:A\otimes A\to A
satisfying the following compatibility conditions:

(2)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z=(x\circ z)\circ y,

(3)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z-x\circ(y\circ z)=(y\circ x)\circ z-y\circ(x\circ z)\;\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y$, $z\in A$.}

Let
B
be a vector space with a binary operation
\diamond
. If
(B,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra with
a\circ b:=b\diamond a
for all
a
,
b\in B
, then
(B,\diamond)
is called a
right Novikov algebra
. By Eq. (
3
), Novikov algebras are also an important subclass of pre-Lie algebras
which have close relationships with many fields in mathematics and physics such as convex homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups, deformation of
associative algebras, vertex algebras and so on.

The Novikov algebra also shows it significance in its role in affinization.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
1
]

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0 and
A
be a vector space over
k
endowed with a binary operation
\circ
. Define a binary operation on
A[t,t^{-1}]\coloneqq A\otimes{\bf k}[t,t^{-1}]
by

\displaystyle[xt^{m},yt^{n}]=m(x\circ y)t^{m+n-1}-n(y\circ x)t^{m+n-1},

for all
x,y\in A
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
xt^{m}:=x\otimes t^{m}
. Then
(A[t,t^{-1}],[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra if and only if
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra.

This construction yields many important infinite-dimensional Lie algebras in mathematical physics, such as the Witt algebra, the centerless Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, the centerless Schrödinger-Virasoro algebra and so on. The algebraic underpinning of this affinization is further clarified by the Koszul dual relationship between the Novikov and right Novikov operads
[
6
,
12
]
.

Now, consider a vector space
A
with two binary operations
\cdot,\circ
. One can define both a Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
A[t,t^{-1}]
via the Novikov product
\circ
as above, and a commutative associative product via:

\displaystyle(xt^{m})\cdot(yt^{n})=(x\cdot y)t^{m+n}\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y\in A$, $m$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

By the result in
[
22
]
,
(A[t,t^{-1}],\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Poisson algebra if and only if
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a differential Novikov-Poisson algebra, which was introduced in
[
4
]
. Note that differential Novikov-Poisson algebras are a subclass of Novikov-Poisson algebras introduced in
[
29
]
.

This background motivates a natural question: what is the corresponding algebraic structure on
A
that induces a transposed Poisson algebra under the same affinization process? To answer this, we introduce the following notion.

Definition 1.2
.

The triple
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is called a
transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
if
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra, and the following compatibility conditions hold:

(4)

\displaystyle\quad(x\cdot y)\circ z=(x\cdot z)\circ y,

(5)

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(x\circ y)=(z\cdot x)\circ y+x\circ(z\cdot y)\;\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y$, $z\in A$.}

Both a Novikov algebra with the trivial commutative associative algebra and a commutative associative algebra with the trivial Novikov algebra are transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. We call such transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras
trivial
. The essence of this definition is justified by the main result of this paper (Theorem
2.3
), which establishes that the affinization
(A[t,t^{-1}],\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson algebra precisely when
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra.

In fact, transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras have closer relationships with transposed Poisson algebras and have good properties. Besides the correspondence between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Poisson algebras under the affinization, we show that a transposed Poisson algebra naturally
arises from a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra by taking the commutator Lie algebra of the
Novikov algebra and can also be derived from a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra via its derivations (See Theorem
2.1
). We further show that there is a transposed Poisson algebra structure on the tensor product of a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra and a right differential Novikov-Poisson algebra. Moreover, similar to Novikov-Poisson algebras, the tensor product of two transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras is also a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. We also present several methods to construct transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. A classification of 2-dimensional transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras over
\mathbb{C}
is also presented and several algebraic identities of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras are given. We also show that there is no direct relationship between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and Novikov-Poisson algebras. Inspired by the work
[
9
]
, we also present a relationship between
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of the associated Novikov algebras and transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. Moreover, a relationship between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and Hom-Novikov algebras is given. By using
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of Novikov algebras, we develop a technical construction of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras on a solvable Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
with
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)\neq 0
(see Corollary 3.15). For the case
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)=0
, the transposed Novikov-Poisson structure on
(A,\circ)
may not exist. Hence we impose stronger conditions and propose an explicit method to construct transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras on
(A,\circ)
(see Theorem 3.20). Finally, we show that if
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra, then
(A,\circ)
is a simple Novikov algebra. Therefore, any simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 is of dimension 1. Transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras on some simple Novikov algebras are also characterized.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present the relationships between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Poisson algebras. Moreover, some basic properties, identities, examples and constructions of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras are presented. In Section 3, a relation between
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of the associated Novikov algebras and transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras is established. Applying this relation, the transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras structures on solvable Novikov algebras are investigated. Section 4 is devoted to studying simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. W show that if
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra,then
(A,\circ)
is a simple Novikov algebra. Moreover, transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras on some simple Novikov algebras are also characterized.

2.
Properties of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and the relationships with transposed Poisson algebras

In this section, we present some examples, constructions, identities, basic properties of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. The relationships between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Poisson algebras are also given.

2.1.
Transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Poisson algebras

There are close relationships between transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Poisson algebras.
First, we present two direct constructions of transposed Poisson algebras from transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra with a derivation
D
, i.e.,
D
is a derivation of both
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
. Then we obtain the following conclusions.

(a)

The triple
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson algebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is given by

\displaystyle[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x\;\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y\in A$.}

(b)

The triple
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson algebra, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is given by

(6)

\displaystyle[x,y]=D(x)\circ y-D(y)\circ x\>\>\text{for all}\>x,y\in A.

Proof.

(
a
) Since
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra.
By Eq. (
5
), for all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle 2z\cdot[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(x\circ y-y\circ x),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z\cdot x)\circ y+x\circ(z\cdot y)-(z\cdot y)\circ x-y\circ(z\cdot x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[z\cdot x,y]+[x,z\cdot y].

Therefore,
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson algebra.

(
b
) It is direct to check that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra by Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).
By Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
), for all
x,y,z\in A
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2z\cdot[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(D(x)\circ y-D(y)\circ x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z\cdot D(x))\circ y+D(x)\circ(z\cdot y)-(z\cdot D(y))\circ x-D(y)\circ(z\cdot x),

and

\displaystyle[z\cdot x,y]+[x,z\cdot y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(z\cdot x)\circ y-D(y)\circ(z\cdot x)+D(x)\circ(z\cdot y)-D(z\cdot y)\circ x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z\cdot D(x))\circ y+D(x)\circ(z\cdot y)-(z\cdot D(y))\circ x-D(y)\circ(z\cdot x)

\displaystyle\quad+(x\cdot D(z))\circ y-(D(z)\cdot y)\circ x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z\cdot D(x))\circ y+D(x)\circ(z\cdot y)-(z\cdot D(y))\circ x-D(y)\circ(z\cdot x).

Therefore,
(A,\cdot,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a transposed Poisson algebra.
∎

2.2.1.
A construction of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras from commutative associative algebras

Definition 2.11

[

Let

for all

Remark 2.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
p\in A
.
Then

. Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x\;\;\;\text{for all $x\in A$.}

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
\phi\in\Gamma((A,\cdot))
. Define a binary operation
\circ
on
A
as follows:

(18)

\displaystyle x\circ y:=x\cdot\phi(y)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in A.

Then
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra.

Proof.

Since
\phi\in\Gamma((A,\cdot))
, we have

\displaystyle x\circ y=x\cdot\phi(y)=\phi(x\cdot y)=y\cdot\phi(x).

Then for all
x,y,z\in A
, we obtain

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z=(x\cdot\phi(y))\cdot\phi(z)=(x\cdot\phi(z))\cdot\phi(y)=(x\circ z)\circ y,

\displaystyle x\circ(y\circ z)=x\circ(y\cdot\phi(z))=x\cdot\phi(y\cdot\phi(z))=x\cdot\phi(y)\cdot\phi(z),

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z=x\cdot\phi(y)\cdot\phi(z),

and

\displaystyle\quad y\circ(x\circ z)=y\cdot\phi(x)\cdot\phi(z).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z-(y\circ x)\circ z=0=x\circ(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\circ z),

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(x\circ y)=2z\cdot x\cdot\phi(y)=z\cdot x\cdot\phi(y)+x\cdot\phi(z\cdot y)=(z\cdot x)\circ y+x\circ(z\cdot y),

and

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\circ z=x\cdot y\cdot\phi(z)=x\cdot z\cdot\phi(y)=(x\cdot z)\circ y.

Therefore,
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra.
∎

2.2.2.
Independence of Novikov-Poisson algebras and transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras

Definition 2.16
.

[
29
]

A
Novikov-Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\circ)
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra,
and they satisfy the following compatibility conditions

(20)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\circ z=x\cdot(y\circ z),

(21)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\cdot z-(y\circ x)\cdot z=x\circ(y\cdot z)-y\circ(x\cdot z)\;\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y$, $z\in A$.}

We show that the compatibility relations of the Novikov-Poisson algebra and those of the
transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra are independent in the following sense.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

The algebra
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a Novikov-Poisson algebra.

(b)

The identity
(x\cdot y)\circ z=z\circ(x\cdot y)
holds for all
x,y,z\in A
.

(c)

L_{\cdot}(x)\in\Gamma((A,\circ))
for all
x\in A
.

(d)

L_{\circ}(x)\in\Gamma((A,\cdot))
for all
x\in A
.

Proof.

\eqref{trans-NP1}\Rightarrow\eqref{trans-NP2}
: For all
x,y,z\in A
, by Eqs. (
4
), (
5
) and (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\circ z-z\circ(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot z)\circ y-z\circ(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x\cdot(z\circ y)-2z\circ(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(x\cdot z)\circ y-2z\circ(x\cdot y).

Thus, we obtain
(x\cdot y)\circ z-z\circ(x\cdot y)=0
.

\eqref{trans-NP2}\Rightarrow\eqref{trans-NP3}
:  By (

), for all
x,y,z\in A
, we obtain

\displaystyle z\cdot(x\circ y)=\frac{1}{2}(z\cdot x)\circ y+\frac{1}{2}x\circ(y\cdot z)=\frac{1}{2}(z\cdot x)\circ y+\frac{1}{2}(y\cdot z)\circ x=(z\cdot x)\circ y,

and

\displaystyle z\cdot(x\circ y)=\frac{1}{2}(z\cdot x)\circ y+\frac{1}{2}x\circ(z\cdot y)=\frac{1}{2}(z\cdot y)\circ x+\frac{1}{2}x\circ(z\cdot y)=x\circ(z\cdot y).

Therefore, we have
L_{\cdot}(z)\in\Gamma((A,\circ))
for all

.

\eqref{trans-NP3}\Rightarrow\eqref{trans-NP1}
: For all

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\circ z)

\displaystyle\!=\!(x\cdot y)\circ z,

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\cdot z-(y\circ x)\cdot z

\displaystyle\!=\!x\circ(y\cdot z)-y\circ(x\cdot z).

Therefore,
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a Novikov-Poisson algebra.

\eqref{trans-NP4}\Leftrightarrow\eqref{trans-NP2}
: For all

\displaystyle x\cdot(z\circ y)-(z\circ x)\cdot y=\frac{1}{2}(x\cdot z)\circ y+\frac{1}{2}z\circ(x\cdot y)-\frac{1}{2}(y\cdot z)\circ x-\frac{1}{2}z\circ(x\cdot y)=0,

\displaystyle z\circ(x\cdot y)-(z\circ x)\cdot y=z\circ(x\cdot y)-\frac{1}{2}(z\cdot y)\circ x-\frac{1}{2}z\circ(x\cdot y)=\frac{1}{2}(z\circ(x\cdot y)-(x\cdot y)\circ z).\vskip-8.5359pt

Therefore, we get
\eqref{trans-NP4}\Leftrightarrow\eqref{trans-NP2}
.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. If
(A,\cdot)
has a unit

(A,\circ)
is a commutative Novikov algebra, and hence
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a Novikov-Poisson algebra.

Proof.

The commutativity of
(A,\circ)
follows from the computation:

\displaystyle x\circ y=(x\cdot e)\circ y=(y\cdot e)\circ x=y\circ x.\vskip-2.84544pt

This implies that
(A,\cdot,\circ)
satisfies (
b

2.17
. Therefore,
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a Novikov-Poisson algebra.
∎

.

(b)

If

is an ideal of
(A,\circ)
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a decomposable Novikov algebra, i.e.,
A
is the direct sum of two non-zero ideals. Then
(A,\circ)
has non-trivial
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations.

Proof.

By the hypothesis, we have
\operatorname{dim}(A)\geq 2
. Assume that
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
, where
A_{1},A_{2}
are non-zero ideals of
(A,\circ)
. For any
x_{i}=y_{i}+z_{i}\in A
, where
y_{i}\in A_{1}
,
z_{i}\in A_{2}
, define a linear map
\varphi:A\rightarrow A
by
\varphi(x_{i})=z_{i}
.
Then for any
x_{i}
,
x_{j}\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{i}\circ x_{j})=\varphi((y_{i}+z_{i})\circ(y_{j}+z_{j}))=\varphi(y_{i}\circ y_{j}+z_{i}\circ z_{j})=z_{i}\circ z_{j},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x_{i})\circ x_{j}+\frac{1}{2}x_{i}\circ\varphi(x_{j})=\frac{1}{2}z_{i}\circ x_{j}+\frac{1}{2}x_{i}\circ z_{j}=\frac{1}{2}z_{i}\circ z_{j}+\frac{1}{2}z_{i}\circ z_{j}.

Therefore, the linear map
\varphi
defined above is a non-trivial
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of
(A,\circ)
. Then this conclusion holds.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a finite-dimensional Novikov algebra with
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)\neq 0

A\circ A\neq A
. Then

has non-trivial
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations.

Proof.

When

, all linear maps

are
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations. Then this conclusion holds.

Next, we only need to consider the case when
A\circ A\neq 0
. Since
A\circ A\neq A
, we have
\operatorname{dim}(A)\geq 2
.

If
(A\circ A)\cap\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)=0
, then there exists a subspace

of

as vector spaces, where

can be the zero vector space. Since

and
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)
are non-zero ideals of

.

If
(A\circ A)\cap\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)\neq 0

. Moreover, there exists a non-zero subspace
W
of
A

as vector spaces. Define a linear map
\varphi:A\rightarrow A

\varphi(x)=0,\>\varphi(y)=x_{1}

\displaystyle\varphi(z_{1}\circ z_{2})=0=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(z_{1})\circ z_{2}+\frac{1}{2}z_{1}\circ\varphi(z_{2}).

Therefore,
\varphi
defined above is a non-trivial
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of

.
∎

Based on the construction of

-derivations in Lemma

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a finite-dimensional Novikov algebra. If

,
A\circ A\neq A

Proof.

We prove it with the same notations as in the proof of Lemma

By the assumption, we have
\operatorname{dim}(A)\geq 2
.

If

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(x\circ y)\in 2z\cdot(A\circ A)=0,\>(z\cdot x)\circ y\in\operatorname{Ann_{A}}(A)\circ A=0,

If

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a solvable Novikov algebra with
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)=0

\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A\circ A)\setminus\operatorname{Ann}_{L}(A)\neq 0

-derivations.

Proof.

First, we have
\operatorname{dim}(A)\geq 2

\varphi:A\rightarrow A

such that
\varphi(x)=w\circ x=k_{w}x

\varphi(x\circ y)=w\circ(x\circ y)=k_{w}(x\circ y)=0
for all

Finally, we check that

is a

-derivation. Since

\displaystyle 2\varphi(x\circ y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2w\circ(x\circ y)=0,

and

\displaystyle\varphi(x)\circ y+x\circ\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w\circ x)\circ y+x\circ(w\circ y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\circ w)\circ y+w\circ(x\circ y)-x\circ(w\circ y)+x\circ(w\circ y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ w+w\circ(x\circ y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\varphi

-derivation.
∎

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a solvable Novikov algebra with
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A)=0
. If
\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(A\circ A)\setminus\operatorname{Ann}_{L}(A)\neq 0
, then there are non-trivial transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra structures on

.

Proof.

Following the proof of Lemma
3.19
, we define a binary operation

\cdot:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
by

for all

,

, where

. By Eqs. (

\displaystyle x\cdot y=(w\circ x)\circ y=(x\circ w)\circ y+w\circ(x\circ y)-x\circ(w\circ y)=-x\circ(w\circ y),\vskip-5.69046pt

for all

, which contradicts with

defined above is not trivial.
Moreover,
(A,\cdot)
defined above is associative, since

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z=(w\circ((w\circ x)\circ y))\circ z=0,

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(w\circ x)\circ((w\circ y)\circ z)=(w\circ((w\circ y)\circ z))\circ x=0,

for all

Finally, we need to check that Eqs. (

is an ideal of

by Lemma

, we have

for all

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\circ z=\big((w\circ x)\circ y\big)\circ z=\big((w\circ x)\circ z\big)\circ y=(x\cdot z)\circ y,

\displaystyle 2z\cdot(x\circ y)=2(w\circ z)\circ(x\circ y)=2(w\circ(x\circ y))\circ z=0,

and

\displaystyle(z\cdot x)\circ y+x\circ(z\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((w\circ z)\circ x)\circ y+x\circ((w\circ z)\circ y)

\displaystyle=(x\circ(w\circ z))\circ y+(w\circ z)\circ(x\circ y)-x\circ((w\circ z)\circ y)+x\circ((w\circ z)\circ y)

Then the proof is completed.

4.
Simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras

In this section, we investigate simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras. We show that if
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra, then
(A,\circ)
is simple. Based on this result, transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras on some simple Novikov algebras are characterized.

4.1.
On simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Novikov algebra or a commutative associative algebra.
(A,\ast)
is called
simple
if
A\ast A\neq\{0\}
and the only ideals of
A
are
trivial ideals
:
\{0\}
and
A
.

Definition 4.1
.

An
ideal
of a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a subspace
I
such that
I
is an ideal of both
(A,\circ)
and
(A,\cdot)
.

0
and
A
are called
trivial ideals
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.
A nontrivial transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is called
simple
if
(A,\cdot,\circ)
has no non-trivial ideals.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a non-trivial transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. If
A\circ A\neq A
, then both
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\cdot,\circ)
are not simple.

Proof.

By Eq. (
5
), we have

\displaystyle(A\circ A)\cdot A\subset(A\cdot A)\circ A+A\circ(A\cdot A)\subset A\circ A.

Therefore,
A\circ A
is a non-trivial ideal of
(A,\cdot)
. Note that
A\circ A
is an ideal of
(A,\circ)
. Therefore,
A\circ A
is a non-trivial ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. Then we have
A\circ A=A
.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Motivated by the study of simple transposed Poisson algebras in
[
7
]
, we introduce the definition of transposed quasi-ideals of transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras.

Definition 4.4
.

A
transposed quasi-ideal
of a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a non-trivial subspace
I
of
A
such that
I
is an ideal of
(A,\circ)
and
(A\cdot I)\circ A\subset I
,
A\circ(A\cdot I)\subset I
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. Then a transposed quasi-ideal
I
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is an ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.

Proof.

Note that by the definition of transposed quasi-ideals,
I
is an ideal of
(A,\circ)
. Moreover, by Theorem
4.3
and Eq. (
5
), we have
A\cdot I=(A\circ A)\cdot I\subset(A\cdot I)\circ I+A\circ(A\cdot I)\subset I
. Therefore,
I
is also an ideal of
(A,\cdot)
. Then the conclusion holds.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

A non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\circ)
contains no transposed quasi-ideals.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra with
A\circ A=A
. Then every non-trivial ideal of
(A,\circ)
is a transposed quasi-ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
I
is a non-trivial ideal of
(A,\circ)
. Since
A\circ I\subset I
and
I\circ A\subset I
, by Eqs. (
2
) and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle(A\cdot I)\circ A\subset(A\cdot A)\circ I\subset A\circ I\subset I,

and

\displaystyle A\circ(A\cdot I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A\circ A)\circ(A\cdot I)=(A\circ(A\cdot I))\circ A\subset((A\cdot I)\circ A)\circ A

\displaystyle\quad+A\circ((A\cdot I)\circ A)-(A\cdot I)\circ(A\circ A)\subset I\circ A+A\circ I-(A\cdot I)\circ A\subset I.

Therefore,
I
is a transposed quasi ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a non-trivial simple transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. Then the Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is simple.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have
A\circ A=A
. By Lemma
4.7
, if
I
is a non-trivial ideal of
(A,\circ)
, then
I
is a transposed quasi-ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
, which contradicts with Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra. Then the associated Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is a direct sum of simple ideals if and only if the transposed Novikov-Poisson algebra is a direct sum of simple ideals.

Proof.

Suppose that
A=\oplus_{i\in S}I_{i}
, where each
I_{i}
is a simple ideal of
(A,\circ)
. Thus, we have
I_{i}\circ I_{i}=I_{i}
. By Eq. (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle A\cdot I_{i}=A\cdot(I_{i}\circ I_{i})\subset(A\cdot I_{i})\circ I_{i}+I_{i}\circ(A\cdot I_{i})\subset A\circ I_{i}+I_{i}\circ A\subset I_{i}.

Thus, each
I_{i}
is an ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
. Finally, each
I_{i}
as the ideal of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is simple, since
I_{i}
is simple as the ideal of
(A,\circ)
. Conversely, this follows directly from Theorem
4.8
.
∎

It was shown in
[

that a finite-dimensional simple Novikov algebra over an algebraically closed field with characteristic 0 is one-dimensional. Note that a finite-dimensional simple commutative associative algebra over an algebraically closed field with characteristic 0 is also one-dimensional. Therefore, by Theorem

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
{\bf k}
be an algebraically closed field with characteristic 0. Then any simple finite dimensional transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras over
{\bf k}
is one-dimensional.

4.2.
Transposed Novikov-Poisson algebras with simple Novikov algebras

Theorem 4.11
.

Let

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(y_{p^{n}-2}\circ y_{0})-\varphi(y_{p^{n}-2})\circ y_{0}-y_{p^{n}-2}\circ\varphi(y_{0})

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{p^{n}-2,k}y_{k}\right)-\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{p^{n}-2,k}y_{k}\right)\circ y_{0}-y_{p^{n}-2}\circ\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{0,k}y_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{p^{n}-2,k}y_{k}\right)-\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{p^{n}-2,k}y_{k}-\alpha_{p^{n}-2,-1}by_{p^{n}-2}-\alpha_{0,0}y_{p^{n}-2}.

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(y_{0}\circ y_{0})-\varphi(y_{0})\circ y_{0}-y_{0}\circ\varphi(y_{0})

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{0,k}y_{k}\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{0,k}(k+1)y_{k}-\alpha_{0,-1}by_{p^{n}-2}-\left(\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{0,k}y_{k}\right).

Comparing the coefficient of

\alpha_{0,-1}=0

. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(y_{-1}\circ y_{-1})-\varphi(y_{-1})\circ y_{-1}-y_{-1}\circ\varphi(y_{-1})

\displaystyle=2a\alpha_{p^{n}-2,p^{n}-2}y_{p^{n}-2}-\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{-1,k}y_{k}\circ y_{-1}-y_{-1}\circ\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{-1,k}y_{k}

\displaystyle=2a\alpha_{p^{n}-2,p^{n}-2}y_{p^{n}-2}-2a\alpha_{-1,-1}y_{p^{n}-2}-\alpha_{-1,0}by_{p^{n}-2}-\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{-1,k}y_{k-1}.

Thus we obtain
\alpha_{-1,k}=0
when
k\neq-1
. Let
i=-1,j=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(y_{-1}\circ y_{0})-y_{-1}\circ\varphi(y_{0})-\varphi(y_{-1})\circ y_{0}

\displaystyle=2b\alpha_{0,0}y_{p^{n}-2}+2\alpha_{-1,-1}y_{-1}-\alpha_{0,0}by_{p^{n}-2}-\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{0,k}y_{k-1}-\alpha_{-1,-1}y_{-1}-\alpha_{-1,-1}by_{p^{n}-2}.

Comparing the coefficients of
y_{k},\>k=-1,0,...,p^{n}-1
, we have
\varphi(y_{0})=\alpha_{0,0}y_{0}=\alpha_{-1,-1}y_{0}
. Finally, when
i\neq 0,-1
and
j=0
, we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(y_{i}\circ y_{0})-y_{i}\circ\varphi(y_{0})-\varphi(y_{i})\circ y_{0}=2\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{i,k}y_{k}-\alpha_{0,0}y_{i}-\alpha_{i,-1}by_{p^{n}-2}-\sum_{k=-1}^{p^{n}-2}\alpha_{i,k}y_{k}.

Then
\varphi(y_{i})=\alpha_{i,i}y_{i}=\alpha_{0,0}y_{i}
. Hence,
\varphi(y_{i})=\alpha_{0,0}y_{i}
, for all
i=-1,0,...,p^{n}-2
, i.e.,
\varphi
is trivial.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be an infinite-dimensional simple Novikov algebra defined above. Then there are no non-trivial
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations of
(A,\circ)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of
(A,\circ)
. For all
\alpha\in\Delta
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

(39)

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{\alpha,i})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta,l\in\mathbb{N}}a_{\alpha,i}^{\lambda,l}x_{\lambda,l},

where
a_{\alpha,i}^{\lambda,l}\in{\bf k}
.

Case 1: When
\Delta=0
and
J=\mathbb{N}
,
we simply denote
x_{0,i}
and
a_{0,i}^{0,j}
by
x_{i}
and
a_{i,j}
respectively. So Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
) are equivalent to
x_{i}\circ x_{j}=bx_{i+j}+jx_{i+j-1}
and
\varphi(x_{i})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i,j}x_{j}
respectively. Let
i=j=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{0}\circ x_{0})-x_{0}\circ\varphi(x_{0})-\varphi(x_{0})\circ x_{0}=2b\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{0,j}x_{j}-2b\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{0,j}x_{j}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{0,j}jx_{j-1},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{0})=a_{0,0}x_{0}
.

If
b\neq 0
, we consider

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{i}\circ x_{0})-x_{i}\circ\varphi(x_{0})-\varphi(x_{i})\circ x_{0}=2b\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i,j}x_{j}-ba_{0,0}x_{i}-b\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i,j}x_{j},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{i})=a_{i,i}x_{i}=a_{0,0}x_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
\varphi
is trivial.

If
b=0
, then Eq. (
38
) is equivalent to
x_{i}\circ x_{j}=jx_{i+j-1}
. Set
i=j=1
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{1}\circ x_{1})-x_{1}\circ\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{1})\circ x_{1}=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{1,j}x_{j}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{1,j}jx_{j}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{1,j}x_{j},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{1})=a_{1,1}x_{1}
. Next, we consider

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{i}\circ x_{1})-x_{i}\circ\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{i})\circ x_{1}=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i,j}x_{j}-a_{1,1}x_{i}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i,j}x_{j},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{i})=a_{i,i}x_{i}=a_{1,1}x_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
\varphi
is trivial.

Case 2: When
\Delta\neq 0
and
J=0
, we simply denote
x_{\alpha,i}=x_{\alpha}
and
a_{\alpha,0}^{\lambda,0}=a_{\alpha,\lambda}
. So Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
) are equivalent to
x_{\alpha}\circ x_{\beta}=(\beta+b)x_{\alpha+\beta}
. and
\varphi(x_{\alpha})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{\alpha,\lambda}x_{\lambda}
respectively. Let
\alpha=\beta=0
in Eq. (
38
). Then we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{0}\circ x_{0})-x_{0}\circ\varphi(x_{0})-\varphi(x_{0})\circ x_{0}=2b\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{0,\lambda}x_{\lambda}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{0,\lambda}(\lambda+b)x_{\lambda}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{0,\lambda}bx_{\lambda},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{0})=a_{0,0}x_{0}
.

If
b\neq 0
, we consider

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{\alpha}\circ x_{0})-x_{\alpha}\circ\varphi(x_{0})-\varphi(x_{\alpha})\circ x_{0}=2b\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{\alpha,\lambda}x_{\lambda}-a_{0,0}bx_{\alpha}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{\alpha,\lambda}bx_{\lambda},

which implies that
\varphi(x_{\alpha})=a_{\alpha,\alpha}x_{\alpha}=a_{0,0}x_{\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. Hence,
\varphi
is trivial.

If
b=0
, then Eq. (
38
) is equivalent to
x_{\alpha}\circ x_{\beta}=\beta x_{\alpha+\beta}
. For all
\alpha\in\Delta
with
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{0}\circ x_{\alpha})-x_{0}\circ\varphi(x_{\alpha})-\varphi(x_{0})\circ x_{\alpha}=2\alpha\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}a_{\alpha,\lambda}x_{\lambda}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta}\lambda a_{\alpha,\lambda}x_{\lambda}-\alpha a_{0,0}x_{\alpha},

\varphi(x_{\alpha})=a_{\alpha,\alpha}x_{\alpha}+a_{\alpha,2\alpha}x_{2\alpha}=a_{0,0}x_{\alpha}+a_{\alpha,2\alpha}x_{2\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in\Delta
with
\alpha\neq 0
. Next, we consider

\displaystyle 0=2\varphi(x_{\alpha}\circ x_{\beta})-x_{\alpha}\circ\varphi(x_{\beta})-\varphi(x_{\alpha})\circ x_{\beta}

\displaystyle=2\beta(a_{\alpha+\beta,\alpha+\beta}x_{\alpha+\beta}+a_{\alpha+\beta,2(\alpha+\beta)}x_{2(\alpha+\beta)})-\beta a_{\beta,\beta}x_{\alpha+\beta}-2\beta a_{\beta,2\beta}x_{\alpha+2\beta}-\beta a_{\alpha,\alpha}x_{\alpha+\beta}-\beta a_{\alpha,2\alpha}x_{2\alpha+\beta},

a_{\lambda,2\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\in\Delta
with
\lambda\neq 0

\varphi
is trivial.

\Delta\neq 0
and
J=\mathbb{N}
, then
|\Delta|=\infty
since
\operatorname{char}({\bf k})=0
. First, let
\alpha=\beta=0
and
i=j=0

). Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

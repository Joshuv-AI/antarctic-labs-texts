A new computational tool for Khovanov cobordism maps
Zsombor Fehér

Abstract
. We describe a Python module that we developed to calculate cobordism maps induced on Khovanov homology. As applications of our program, we compute these maps for all incompressible Seifert surfaces for prime knots up to 10 crossings, and distinguish many ribbon disks arising from symmetries of the boundary knots.



Introduction

Khovanov homology assigns an algebraic object
\mathrm{Kh}(L)
to every oriented link
L\subseteq S^{3}
. Computer programs
[
21
,
22
]
are available to calculate this invariant, which can be used to distinguish links.

To any smoothly embedded surface
\Sigma\subseteq S^{3}\times[0,1]
between two links
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
, Khovanov homology assigns a map
\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)\colon\mathrm{Kh}(L_{0})\to\mathrm{Kh}(L_{1})
. These maps provide a method to distinguish surfaces up to smooth isotopy. However, despite the combinatorial nature of their definition, no computational tool was available for calculating these maps. We developed a program to compute them, as detailed in
Section

2
. This Python module is available on GitHub
[
6
]
.

In
Section

3
, we present two applications. We calculate the cobordism maps for all incompressible Seifert surfaces for prime knots up to 10 crossings (
Table

5
), and distinguish many ribbon disks arising from symmetries of the boundary knots (
Table

6
). For instance, we present five distinct ribbon disks for the knot
10_{123}
, obtained via a rotational symmetry, which are distinguished by Khovanov homology (
Figure

1
).

1.1.
Khovanov homology of links

Let
L\subseteq S^{3}
be an oriented link. Choose a diagram
D
of
L
, and an enumeration
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of its crossings. We define a chain complex
(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X),d)
associated to the data
(D,X)
as follows.

For any binary sequence
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we consider the
smoothing

D_{\sigma}
, which is obtained by replacing each crossing
x_{i}

with a 0-smoothing
or 1-smoothing
, depending on
\sigma_{i}
. We call the connected components of
D_{\sigma}

loops
. A
labelled smoothing

\alpha_{\sigma}
is a labelling of the loops of
D_{\sigma}
with a
1
or
x
. Let
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)
be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by all the labelled smoothings
\alpha_{\sigma}
of
D
.

For simplicity, we describe the differential map

using the Morse induced chain maps that are defined in
Table

1
(taken from
[
10
]

\alpha_{\sigma}
be a labelled smoothing with
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
. For an
i

\sigma_{i}=0
, let
m_{i}
denote the chain map induced by the Morse saddle move
that turns the 0-smoothing of
x_{i}
into the 1-smoothing. (Thus,
m_{i}(\alpha_{\sigma})
is a sum of 0, 1, or 2 labelled smoothings.) We define the differential map

on the generators by

d(\alpha_{\sigma})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
\sigma_{i}=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\xi_{i}}m_{i}(\alpha_{\sigma}),\qquad\xi_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\\

and extend it linearly to

The chain complex
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)
is bigraded by homological grading
h
and quantum grading
q
. Let
n_{+},n_{-}
be the number of positive and negative crossings in
D
. For a labelled smoothing
\alpha_{\sigma}
, let
|\sigma|
be the number of 1-smoothings in
\sigma
, and let
v_{+}(\alpha_{\sigma}),v_{-}(\alpha_{\sigma})
be the number of 1-labels and
x

\alpha_{\sigma}
. The grading
(h,q)
of
\alpha_{\sigma}
is defined by

\displaystyle h(\alpha_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=|\sigma|-n_{-},

\displaystyle q(\alpha_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=v_{+}(\alpha_{\sigma})-v_{-}(\alpha_{\sigma})+h(\alpha_{\sigma})+n_{+}-n_{-},

and
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q}(D,X)
is defined as the submodule generated by such

d\colon\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q}(D,X)\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}^{h+1,q}(D,X)
. Hence, the bigrading descends to homology, and we have that
\mathrm{Kh}(L)=\bigoplus_{h,q}\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q}(L)

\mathbb{Z}

1.2.
Crossing order change

We explicitly construct the isomorphism of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)
for a different choice of
X
, as this will be essential for computations. Consider two enumerations
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
X^{\prime}=(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})
for some permutation
\pi
. Then any labelled smoothing
\alpha_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)
with
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
naturally corresponds to a labelled smoothing
\alpha^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X^{\prime})
that has
\sigma^{\prime}=(\sigma_{\pi(1)},\dots,\sigma_{\pi(n)})
and the same labels. By definition, the differential
d^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X^{\prime})
is given by

d^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
\sigma_{i}=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\xi^{\prime}_{i}}m_{i}(\alpha^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}),\qquad\xi^{\prime}_{i}=\;\;\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}j\\
\pi^{-1}(j)<\pi^{-1}(i)\end{subarray}}}\;\;\sigma_{j}.

We define the isomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X^{\prime})
on the generators by

\Phi(\alpha_{\sigma})=(-1)^{\pi_{\sigma}}\alpha^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}},\qquad\pi_{\sigma}=\;\;\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\\
j<k\\
\pi^{-1}(j)>\pi^{-1}(k)\end{subarray}}}\;\;\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k},

and extend it linearly. Then it can be checked that
\Phi(d(\alpha_{\sigma}))=d^{\prime}(\Phi(\alpha_{\sigma}))
. Therefore,
(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X),d)
and
(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X^{\prime}),d^{\prime})
are isomorphic chain complexes, and
\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)\cong\mathrm{Kh}(D,X^{\prime})
.

We also provide a more intuitive perspective on this isomorphism, by describing the differential map in a way that is independent of the crossing order, as follows. For a labelled smoothing
\alpha_{\sigma}
, let
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
represent all indices
i
where
\sigma_{i}=1
, and replace
\alpha_{\sigma}
with the formal expression
\alpha_{\sigma}\cdot\omega_{\sigma}
, where
\omega_{\sigma}=dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{i_{k}}
. By substituting each labelled smoothing (
\alpha_{\sigma}
and every
m_{i}(\alpha_{\sigma})
) in this way within the definition of
d
, the differential map can be written formally as

d=m_{1}\cdot dx_{1}+\dots+m_{n}\cdot dx_{n},

where
m_{i}\cdot dx_{i}
acts on a labelled smoothing as

(m_{i}\cdot dx_{i})(\alpha_{\sigma}\cdot\omega_{\sigma})=m_{i}(\alpha_{\sigma})\cdot(dx_{i}\wedge\omega_{\sigma}).

Indeed, the summation restricts to the indices
i
with
\sigma_{i}=0
because
dx_{i}\wedge dx_{i}=0
, and the signs
(-1)^{\xi_{i}}
in our original definition of
d
come from
dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}=-dx_{j}\wedge dx_{i}
, when we change
dx_{i}\wedge\omega_{\sigma}
to have increasing indices (as
m_{i}(\alpha_{\sigma})
has a new 1-smoothed crossing
x_{i}
).

With this order-independent view of
d
, it is clear what happens when we change the order of crossings to
X^{\prime}
. The new
\omega^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
that uses the ordering
X^{\prime}
has
\omega^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}=\pm\omega_{\sigma}
, where the
\pm
sign depends on the parity of the permutation of
dx_{i_{1}},\dots,dx_{i_{k}}
in
\omega^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. In the above definition of
\Phi
,
\alpha_{\sigma}
gets this sign, whose explicit definition is
(-1)^{\pi_{\sigma}}
. For example, if
X=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
X^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{3},x_{2})
, then

\Phi(\alpha_{110}+\alpha_{101}+\alpha_{011})=\alpha_{101}^{\prime}+\alpha_{110}^{\prime}-\alpha_{011}^{\prime}.

1.3.
Khovanov homology of cobordisms

Let
L_{0},L_{1}
be links. A
cobordism
from
L_{0}
to
L_{1}
is a smoothly embedded compact surface
\Sigma\subseteq S^{3}\times[0,1]
whose boundary is
L_{0}\times\{0\}\cup L_{1}\times\{1\}
. Any cobordism can be represented as a
movie
: a sequence of diagrams
D_{0},\dots,D_{N}
, where
D_{i+1}
is obtained from
D_{i}
by an
elementary move
(Reidemeister or Morse move), and
D_{0},D_{N}
represent the links
L_{0},L_{1}
, respectively. We often write the cobordism as a function
\Sigma\colon L_{0}\to L_{1}
.

Given a cobordism
\Sigma\colon L_{0}\to L_{1}
, we will associate to
\Sigma
a group homomorphism
\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)\colon\mathrm{Kh}(L_{0})\to\mathrm{Kh}(L_{1})
. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is orientable, with oriented boundary
\partial\Sigma=(L_{0}\times\{0\})\cup(L_{1}^{r}\times\{1\})
(where
L_{1}^{r}
denotes
L_{1}
with reversed orientation), then
\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)
will be a bigraded map:

\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)\colon\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q}(L_{0})\to\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q+\chi(\Sigma)}(L_{1}).

We now give the definition of this map, using the movie
D_{0},\dots,D_{N}
. We do this by defining a chain map
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D_{i},X_{i})\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D_{i+1},X_{i+1})
associated to every Reidemeister and Morse move, and
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)
will be the composition of these maps.

*Proposition 3.7)

\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma)\colon\mathrm{Kh}(L_{0})\to\mathrm{Kh}(L_{1})

As often is the case, if

\Sigma^{\star}\colon\emptyset\to L
. Thus, to distinguish the surfaces

\mathrm{Kh}(\Sigma_{i})
, or the maps

If

is a cobordism, then the map

1.4.
Mirroring

Let
-L
denote the mirror of the link
L
, and
-D
the mirror of the diagram
D

\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(-D,X)\cong\mathcal{C}\mathrm{Kh}(D,X)^{\star}
, where
C^{\star}=\Hom(C,\mathbb{Z})
is the dual of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
C

[
15
]
*Proposition 32. This isomorphism
\Psi_{D}
is given by
\alpha_{\sigma}\mapsto(\beta_{-\sigma})^{\star}

is the same smoothing on the mirrored diagram,
\beta_{-\sigma}

\mathbb{Z}
-module

If

\displaystyle\Tor(\mathrm{Kh}^{h,q}(-L))

Classes and methods

We provide a brief overview of the program’s classes, highlighting key properties and methods that might be useful to users. The program builds on SnapPy’s
Link} class \cite

A

\begin

\item \code

\end

A

•

•

•

\end

Each of the elementary moves can be applied to a \code

\begin

\item \code

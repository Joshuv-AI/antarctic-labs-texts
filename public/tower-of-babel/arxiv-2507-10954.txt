1.
Introduction

We begin with two concepts. The first one is the completely monotonic (
CM
) function, which states that a function
h
is said to be CM on
an interval
I
if
h
has derivatives of all orders on
I
and satisfies

\left(-1\right)^{n}h^{\left(n\right)}\left(x\right)\geq 0\text{ \ for }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\}\text{ and }x\in I.

Obviously, if
f\left(x\right)
and
g\left(x\right)
are CM on
I
,
then
pf\left(x\right)+qg\left(x\right)
for
p,q>0
and
f\left(x\right)g\left(x\right)
are also CM on
I
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1]
). It was proved in
[
2
, p. 161, Theorem 12b]
that the function
F(x)
is CM on
\left(0,\infty\right)

if and only if

F\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}d\mu\left(t\right),

where
\mu\left(t\right)
is nondecreasing and the integral converges
for
0<x<\infty
, which, as usually, is called Bernstein theorem. Now if
let
d\mu\left(t\right)=f\left(t\right)dt
, then Bernstein theorem has
an equivalent statement: the function
F(x)
is CM on
\left(0,\infty\right)
if and only if

F\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(t\right)e^{-xt}dt,

where
f\left(t\right)
is nonnegative on
\left(0,\infty\right)
and
the integral converges for
0<x<\infty
.

The second one is the majorization of vectors (see
[
3
, p. 8, Definition A.1]
). Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(x_{1},...,x_{n}\right)
and
\boldsymbol{y}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(y_{1},...,y_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
n
-tuple
\boldsymbol{x}_{\left[n\right]}
is said to be strictly majorized by another
n
-tuple
\boldsymbol{y}_{\left[n\right]}
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{x}_{\left[n\right]}\succ\boldsymbol{y}_{\left[n\right]}
or
\boldsymbol{y}_{\left[n\right]}\prec\boldsymbol{x}_{\left[n\right]}
, if
\boldsymbol{x}_{\left[n\right]}\neq\boldsymbol{y}_{\left[n\right]}
,

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle x_{2}\geq\cdot\cdot\cdot\geq x_{n}\text{, \ \ \ }y_{1}\geq y_{2}\geq\cdot\cdot\cdot\geq y_{n}\text{,}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\text{ for }j=1,...,n-1\text{
\ and \ }\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}.

Very recently, inspired by Fink’s inequality
[
4
]
, Yang

[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
considered the complete monotonicity
of the function

(1.1)

\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}\right)=\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}^{\left[F\right]}\left(x;\lambda_{n}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{p_{j}}\left(x\right)-\lambda_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{q_{j}}\left(x\right)

on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, where

(1.2)

F_{n}\left(x\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}F^{\left(n\right)}\left(x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}f\left(t\right)e^{-xt}dt\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{.}

For convenience, we records this result as follows (with
k=n
).

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that

(1) there exist constants
M>0
and
c\geq 0
such that
0<f\left(t\right)\leq Me^{ct}
for all
t>0
;

(2) the functions
F_{n}
for

defined by (
1.2
)
converge for all

;

(3) the
n
-tuples
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(p_{1},...,p_{n}\right)
and
\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(q_{1},...,q_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

satisfy

.

If there is real

such that

is log-concave
(-convex) on

define by (

, where

(1.3)

\lambda_{n}^{\left[\theta\right]}\equiv\lambda_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma\left(p_{j}-\theta+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(q_{j}-\theta+1\right)}.

and remark as follows.

, is correct.

(ii)

also holds for

.

Suppose that

(1) there is an interval
\mathbb{I\subseteq R}
such that the function

) converges on

(3) the

, where

).

Remark 2
.

From the proofs of
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[

we see that

the proof of Theorem

, we can prove that

Lemma 1
.

Let the

where

(2.1)

p_{j}^{\ast}=p_{j+1}\text{ \ and \ }q_{j}^{\ast}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{j}&\text{for }j=1,...,k-1\text{,}\\
q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}&\text{for }j=k\text{,}\\
q_{j+1}&\text{for }j=k+1,...,n\text{,}\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}=\left(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(p_{1},q_{k}^{\ast}\right)&\text{if }p_{1}\geq q_{k}^{\ast},\\
\left(q_{k}^{\ast},p_{1}\right)&\text{if }p_{1}<q_{k}^{\ast}.\end{array}\right.\text{ \ and \ }\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}=\left(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}\right)=\left(q_{k},q_{k+1}\right),

Suppose that

(2.3)

q_{1}\geq q_{2}\geq\cdot\cdot\cdot\geq q_{k}\geq p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\cdots\geq p_{n+1}\geq q_{n+1}.

If

, then
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast}
and
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(i) We first show that
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast}
. Since
p_{j}\geq p_{j+1}
, we
have
p_{j-1}^{\ast}\geq p_{j}^{\ast}
for
j=2,...,n+1
. Similarly, we
have

\displaystyle q_{j-1}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q_{j}^{\ast}\text{ for }j=2,...,k-1,k+2,...n,

\displaystyle q_{k-1}^{\ast}-q_{k}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{k-1}-\left(q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}\right)=\left(q_{k-1}-q_{k}\right)+\left(p_{1}-q_{k+1}\right)\geq 0,

\displaystyle q_{k}^{\ast}-q_{k+1}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}\right)-q_{k+2}=\left(q_{k}-p_{1}\right)+\left(q_{k+1}-q_{k+2}\right)\geq 0\text{.}

Next we check that
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{j}^{\ast}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}p_{j}^{\ast}
for
\ell=1,...,n-1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}^{\ast}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}^{\ast}
. Since
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n+1\right]}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n+1\right]}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{j}^{\ast}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}p_{j}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}p_{j}\right)+\left(p_{1}-p_{\ell+1}\right)\geq 0\text{ \ for }\ell=1,...,k-1\text{,}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}^{\ast}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}q_{j}+q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}q_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}p_{j}\geq 0\text{,}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{j}^{\ast}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}p_{j}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}q_{j}+q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\ell}q_{j+1}-\sum_{j=2}^{\ell+1}p_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}q_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}p_{j}\geq 0\text{ for }\ell=k+1,...,n\text{, }

where the last inequality becomes equality for
\ell=n
.

(ii) We now verify that
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}

p_{1}^{\prime}-p_{2}^{\prime}\geq 0
and
q_{1}^{\prime}-q_{2}^{\prime}=q_{k}-q_{k+1}\geq 0

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\prime}-p_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{k}-p_{1}>0&\text{if }p_{1}\geq q_{k}^{\ast},\\
q_{k}-q_{k}^{\ast}=q_{k}-\left(q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}\right)\geq 0&\text{if }p_{1}<q_{k}^{\ast},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\prime}+q_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{k}+q_{k+1}=p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime}.

This proves

4.4.

Lemma 3

Let

(i) Expanding in power series gives

\displaystyle R_{p}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{p}e^{-t^{2}/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{k!}t^{k}x^{k}\right)\right)dt=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{k!}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k+p}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt\right)x^{k}

(ii) Since

\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}\right)=\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}^{\left[0\right]}\right)+\left(\lambda_{n}^{\left[0\right]}-\lambda_{n}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}R_{q_{j}}\left(x\right).

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}^{\left[0\right]}\right)
is CM on
\left(0,\infty\right)
by Theorem
2
and
R_{q_{j}}\left(x\right)

is CM on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, so is
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}\right)
on
\left(0,\infty\right)
.

(ii) In a similar way, we can prove
x\mapsto-\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}\right)
is CM on
\left(0,\infty\right)
if
\lambda_{n}\geq 1
.

(iii) The required double inequality follows from the inequalities
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;\lambda_{n}^{\left[0\right]}\right)>0
and
-\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(x;1\right)>0
for all
x>0
. Due to the limit relation in (
4.31
),
the lower bound is sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we emphasize that Lemmas

and

play

2
, which can also be used to
deal with a class of problems involving the majorization of vectors. As an
example, we give a new proof of the following Hardy-Littlewood-Polya
inequality by Lemma
1
.

Theorem 3

(
[
26
, Theorem 108]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a continuous convex on the interval
I
. If the
n
-tuples
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(p_{1},...,p_{n}\right)
and
\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}=\left(q_{1},...,q_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{I}^{n}
satisfy
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\left(p_{j}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\left(q_{j}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to prove

\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\left(p_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\left(q_{j}\right)\leq 0

by induction. When
n=2
,
\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]}\prec\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}
means that
q_{1}\geq p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq q_{2}
with
p_{1}+p_{2}=q_{1}+q_{2}
. By the property of convex functions,

\frac{\phi\left(p_{2}\right)-\phi\left(q_{2}\right)}{p_{2}-q_{2}}\leq\frac{\phi\left(q_{1}\right)-\phi\left(p_{1}\right)}{q_{1}-p_{1}},

which indicates that
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}}\leq 0
.

Assume that
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\leq 0
for certain
n\geq 2
. To prove
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n+1\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n+1\right]}}\leq 0
, we write

\displaystyle\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n+1\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n+1\right]}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\left(p_{1}\right)-\phi\left(q_{k}\right)-\phi\left(q_{k+1}\right)+\phi\left(q_{k}+q_{k+1}-p_{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\phi\left(p_{j}^{\ast}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\left(q_{j}^{\ast}\right)=\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[2\right]}^{\prime}}+\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}^{\ast}},

where
\left(p_{j},q_{j}\right)
and
\left(p_{j}^{\prime},q_{j}^{\prime}\right)
are defined by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively.
Using Lemma
1
and taking the same steps as the proof of
Theorem
2
, we can prove
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n+1\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n+1\right]}}\leq 0
. By induction,
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\left[n\right]},\boldsymbol{q}_{\left[n\right]}}\leq 0

for all
n\geq 2
, which completes the proof.
∎

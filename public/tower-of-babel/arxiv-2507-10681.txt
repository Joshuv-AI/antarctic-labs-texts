Insights for Early Massive Black Hole Growth from JWST Detection of the [Ne v ] λ ​ 3427 \lambda 3427 Emission Line Journal: ApJL Facilities: JWST:NIRSpec
Benny Trakhtenbrot, Claudio Ricci, Ezequiel Treister, Michael J. Koss, Richard Mushotzky, Kyuseok Oh, Alessandro Peca, Franz E. Bauer, Kriti Kamal Gupta, Tomer Reiss

Abstract
We use the narrow [Ne v ] λ ​ 3427 \lambda 3427 emission line detected in the recently published JWST spectra of two galaxies, at z ≃ 6.9 z\simeq 6.9 and 5.6 5.6 , to study the key properties of the active galactic nuclei (AGN) and the supermassive black holes in their centers. Using a new empirical scaling linking the [Ne v ] line emission with AGN accretion-driven (continuum) emission, derived from a highly complete low-redshift AGN sample, we show that the [Ne v ] emission in the two z > 5 z>5 galaxies implies total (bolometric) AGN luminosities of order L bol ≈ ( 4 − 8 ) × 10 45 ​ erg ​ s − 1 L_{\rm bol}\approx(4-8)\times 10^{45}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1} . Assuming that the radiation emitted from these systems is Eddington limited, the (minimal) black hole masses are of order M BH ≳ 10 7 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm BH}\gtrsim 10^{7}\,M_{\odot} . Combined with the published stellar masses of the galaxies, estimated from dedicated fitting of their spectral energy distributions, the implied BH-to-stellar mass ratios are of order M BH / M host ≈ 0.1 − 1 M_{\rm BH}/M_{\rm host}\approx 0.1-1 . This is considerably higher than what is found in the local Universe, but is consistent with the general trend seen in some other z ≳ 5 z\gtrsim 5 AGN. Given the intrinsic weakness of the [Ne v ] line and the nature of the [Ne v ]–to– L bol L_{\rm bol} scaling, any (rare) detection of the [Ne v ] λ ​ 3427 \lambda 3427 line at z > 5 z>5 would translate to similarly high AGN luminosities and SMBH masses, thus providing a unique observational path for studying luminous AGN well into the epoch of reionization, including obscured sources.



I
Introduction

Quasars detected at
z\gtrsim 6
, which now total hundreds of sources, have challenged our understanding of supermassive black hole (SMBH) formation and early growth, inspiring models in which SMBHs grow from massive seeds and/or through exceptionally efficient, super-Eddington accretion
(e.g.,
Inayoshi et al. 2020
;
Fan et al. 2023
, and references therein)
.
However, until recently, there were relatively few observations of lower-luminosity and/or obscured active galactic nuclei (AGN) at these early epochs, although such sources are expected to be (intrinsically) far more abundant
(
Peca et al. 2023
;
Schindler et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is revolutionizing our ability to study high-redshift AGN and SMBHs. Not only has it provided measurements of the host galaxies and larger-scale environments of previously known quasars
(
Ding et al. 2023
;
Yue et al. 2024b
, e.g.,)
, but crucially has detected several types of AGN that were previously unseen at
z\gtrsim 5
.
These include:
broad-line AGN (candidates) now detected out to
z\gtrsim 10

(
Kokorev et al. 2023
)
and to considerably lower luminosities and/or BH masses compared with the luminous quasars probed before
(
Harikane et al. 2023
;
Kocevski et al. 2023
;
Übler et al. 2023
;
Furtak et al. 2024
;
Lin et al. 2024
;
Taylor et al. 2025
;
Juodžbalis et al. 2025
)
;
an abundant population of so-called “little red dots”—many of which appear to harbor AGN based on the presence of broad emission lines
(
Greene et al. 2024
;
Matthee et al. 2023
, albeit with properties that are still under intense discussion; e.g.,)
;
and several obscured AGN identified either through multi-wavelength emission
(
Bogdán et al. 2024
;
Lambrides et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
or narrow emission lines of highly ionized species
(
Chisholm et al. 2024
;
Scholtz et al. 2025
;
Mazzolari et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
;
Many of these systems exhibit BH-to-host masses that are significantly higher than what is seen in the local Universe, reaching
M_{\rm BH}/M_{*}\approx 0.1

(
Maiolino et al. 2024
;
Harikane et al. 2023
;
Übler et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.
These discoveries motivated an intensive discussion regarding the origin and (surprisingly high) abundance of such systems.

Here, we study two star-forming, low-mass galaxies that were recently claimed to host AGN based on emission lines measured from JWST spectroscopy (
Scholtz et al. 2025
;
Chisholm et al. 2024
).
We argue that these galaxies harbor intrinsically luminous AGN based on the published measurements of the [Ne
v
]
\lambda 3427
emission line.
This line is commonly used as a robust AGN tracer
(
Zakamska et al. 2003
;
Gilli et al. 2010
;
Yuan et al. 2016
;
Vergani et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
, as it requires an ionizing source reaching
\gtrsim 100
eV—much higher than what stellar populations can typically provide (e.g.,
Satyapal et al. 2021
;
Cleri et al. 2023
, and references therein, for detailed discussion).

We describe the data available for the two galaxies in Section
II
.
In Section
III
we derive estimates of the intrinsic, accretion-powered luminosities of the AGN that drive the high ionization line emission in these galaxies.
We discuss the implications for early SMBHs and their host galaxies in Section
IV
, before concluding in Section
V
.

\Lambda
CDM cosmology, with
H_{0}=70\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
and
\Omega_{\rm M}=0.3
.

III
AGN energetics

The intense line emission from highly ionized species in the two sources under study suggests they harbor an AGN, and we next estimate their bolometric AGN luminosities.
We focus on the recently published scaling between
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
and the (intrinsic), accretion-driven, AGN radiation, as traced by the ultra-hard X-ray luminosity in the
14-195\,{\rm keV}
band,
L_{\rm 14-195}
, which was described in
48
.
The
48
study is based on a large sample of low-redshift, ultra-hard X-ray selected narrow-line AGN from BASS/DR2
(
Koss et al. 2022
)
, which have high-quality optical spectroscopy. The ultra-hard X-ray band used to define the sample and to link [Ne
v
] line emission with AGN continuum radiation is essentially unaffected by obscuration.
The specific scaling suggested in
48
,
\log L_{\rm 14-195}=\log L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}+3.75
, represents the median value found among the [Ne
v
]-detected sources in the
48
sample, with no correction of the [Ne
v
] emission for source dust attenuation, and has a scatter of 0.45 dex.
Combining this scaling with a simple, universal bolometric correction of
L_{\rm bol}=8\times L_{\rm 14-195}
(see also
Koss et al. 2022
;
Gupta et al. 2024
),
48
conclude that
L_{\rm bol}
can be estimated directly from
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
following:

\log L_{\rm bol}=\log L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}+4.65\,.

(1)

All of the [Ne
v
]-detected narrow-line AGN in
48
have
\log(L_{\rm bol}/L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]})>3.3
and only
<5\%
(
<18\%
) have
\log(L_{\rm bol}/L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]})<3.65
(
4.15
, respectively).
Also, the [Ne
v
] line luminosities of the two galaxies under study are well within the range covered by the
48
sample.
Therefore, the scaling we adopt here should not be considered biased high.
We verified that using the more elaborate, nearly linear relation linking
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
and continuum AGN emission presented in
48
(their Eq. 1), or their
\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]-L_{\rm 2-10}
scaling, would not affect our results.
Given the large scatter in all these scaling relations, we prefer to use the former, simpler Eq.
1
.
With this scaling, we obtain
L_{\rm bol}\simeq 8.5\times 10^{45}
and
3.6\times 10^{45}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
, for GS-10013609 and GN 42437 (respectively).
AGN bolometric corrections have a significant scatter, and may also vary with certain AGN properties
(e.g.,
Vasudevan & Fabian 2007
;
Duras et al. 2020
;
Gupta et al. 2024
, and references therein)
.
However, using more elaborate bolometric corrections is not warranted here, given the kind of approximate argumentation we pursue.
Combining the 0.45 dex scatter on the scaling between [Ne
v
] and AGN continuum emission (
48
) and the scatter on bolometric corrections deduced from the BASS sample, of 0.4 dex
(
Gupta et al. 2024
)
, the uncertainty on our [Ne
v
]-based estimates of
L_{\rm bol}
is
\sim
0.6 dex.

To illustrate the effect of relying on other narrow emission lines from high ionization species on our assessment of AGN energetics in these two galaxies, we use scaling relations suggested for the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
line.
The large SDSS study of
Heckman et al. 2004
suggested

\log L_{\rm bol}=\log L_{\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]}+3.54\,.

(2)

The X-ray informed study of
Heckman et al. 2005
reported a scatter of
\approx
0.5 dex on the scaling between
L_{\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]}
and AGN X-ray emission, however more recent studies found that this scatter could be larger, exceeding 0.6 dex
(
Berney et al. 2015
;
Ueda et al. 2015
;
Oh et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. This is expected, as [O
iii
] may be contaminated by galaxy-scale SF regions and/or reflect past episodes of SMBH accretion.
Notwithstanding these limitations, Eq.
2
yields
L_{\rm bol}(\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right])\approx 6.3\times 10^{45}
and
1.4\times 10^{46}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
, for GS-10013609 and GN 42437 (respectively).
These differ from the [Ne
v
]-based estimates of
L_{\rm bol}
by
-0.13
and
+0.55
dex (respectively).
If we instead use the
\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]+{\rm H}\beta
prescription of
Netzer 2009
, we obtain
L_{\rm bol}
estimates that differ from our [Ne
v
]-based estimates by either
-0.6
dex (for GS-10013609), or by
+0.1
dex (for GN 42437).
For reference, we note that the studies presenting the two sources have also provided rough estimates of
L_{\rm bol}
, based on the [O
iii
] and H
\beta
lines.

51
estimated
L_{\rm bol}\simeq 10^{44}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
for GS-10013609, which is significantly lower than our [Ne
v
]-based estimate (by 2 dex; citing Hirschmann et al. in prep.).
For GN 42437,
8
estimated
L_{\rm bol}
to be as high as
L_{\rm bol}\simeq 10^{45}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
based on the
Netzer 2009
prescription(s), but assuming only
1/3

L_{\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]}
is due to an AGN.
This is in excellent agreement with our [Ne

]-based
L_{\rm bol}
estimate, if we instead assume that 100% of
L_{\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]}
is due to an AGN.

In what follows, we focus on
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
and Eq.
1
as the primary method to estimate the bolometric AGN luminosities for the two galaxies. This is motivated by the higher ionization potential needed for [Ne
v
] (compared with [O
iii
]), making it a high-purity AGN tracer, as well as the possibly closer distance of the [Ne
v
]-emitting gas to the ionizing AGN central engine.

Given the high
L_{\rm bol}
we infer from [Ne
v
], the upper limits on X-ray emission from the two galaxies imply extremely high bolometric–to–X-ray radiation ratios,
L_{\rm bol}/L_{\rm 2-10}\gtrsim 80
for GN 42437 and
\gtrsim 1600
for the higher-
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
GS-10013609.
These are (far) higher than what is seen in large samples of lower redshift (X-ray selected) AGN, where
L_{\rm bol}/L_{\rm 2-10}\lesssim 50

(
Duras et al. 2020
;
Gupta et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. On the other hand, the implied X-ray weakness of the two AGN we study is in line with what is seen in other samples of JWST-detected
z>5
AGN
Ananna et al. 2024
;
Yue et al. 2024a
;
Maiolino et al. 2025
.
Clearly, our two AGN are capable of producing copious amounts of
>
0.1 keV radiation, given their intense [Ne
v
] emission.
Therefore, they may instead support a picture where the line-of-sight towards the central X-ray source is heavily obscured by dense circumnuclear gas (see, e.g.,
Maiolino et al. 2025
for a discussion of one such scenario).

IV
Implications for early SMBH growth

We have shown that the two galaxies, GS-10013609 and GN 42437, highly likely harbor AGN with bolometric luminosities of order
L_{\rm bol}\gtrsim 3\times 10^{45}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
.
What sort of SMBHs could be releasing such copious radiative output, and what might this mean for early SMBH growth and galaxy evolution?

First, assuming that the two sources under study radiate through Eddington-limited accretion, one can deduce a
lower limit
on
M_{\rm BH}
by harnessing the Eddington limit itself, namely (for solar metallicity gas):

M_{\rm BH}\gtrsim M_{\rm BH,min}\equiv(L_{\rm bol}/{\rm erg\,s}^{-1})/1.5\times 10^{38}\,\,M_{\odot}\,.

(3)

For the two galaxies discussed here, this would yield
M_{\rm BH}\gtrsim 5.6\times 10^{7}
and
2.4\times 10^{7}\,M_{\odot}
, for GS-10013609 and GN 42437 (respectively).

However, there are reasons to suspect that AGN accretion flows may sustain much higher accretion rates, above the classical Eddington limit. This is particularly the case for
z\gtrsim 5
SMBHs, which had to grow to their high masses within a limited period
(
Inayoshi et al. 2020
, see, e.g., review by)
. Importantly, several studies have shown that even in (extremely) super-Eddington accretion flows, i.e., when
\dot{M}>>\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}
, the emerging radiation is expected to “saturate” at
L_{\rm bol}\approx{\rm few}\times L_{\rm Edd}

(
Abramowicz et al. 1988
;
McKinney et al. 2014
;
Kubota & Done 2019
, e.g.,)
.
If we adopt the commonly-used scaling of
L_{\rm bol}\simeq L_{\rm Edd}\times(1+\ln[\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}])

(
Abramowicz et al. 1988
)
, this would imply
M_{\rm BH,min}
values that are
lower
than those mentioned above by a factor of
\approx 5.6\times
(i.e., 0.75 dex), for accretion rates with
\dot{M}=100\times\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}
. Even in such an extreme case, the implied lower limits on
M_{\rm BH}
for the two galaxies are of
\gtrsim 4\times 10^{6}\,M_{\odot}
.

To get a sense of the
maximal
BH mass,
M_{\rm BH,max}
, we may assume that the extreme-UV emitting accretion flow that is required for producing [Ne
v
] lines can only exist if
L/L_{\rm Edd}\approx 0.01
. This is indeed the range seen in many samples of persistent, radiatively efficient AGN
(e.g.,
Schulze et al. 2015

Ananna et al. 2022

.

L/L_{\rm Edd}
would naturally yield
M_{\rm BH,max}=100\times M_{\rm BH,min}

\approx 5.6\times 10^{9}
and
2.4\times 10^{9}\,M_{\odot}

The two [Ne

]-detected galaxies should therefore have SMBH masses in the range of roughly
M_{\rm BH}\sim(5-600)\times 10^{7}
and
(2-300)\times 10^{7}\,M_{\odot}
, for GS-10013609 and GN 42437 (respectively).
We emphasize that these are rough estimates, which are susceptible to significant (systematic) uncertainties on the assumptions taken to derive them.
Any variation or uncertainty on the
L_{\rm bol}
estimates
would naturally propagate, linearly, to
M_{\rm BH,min}
and
M_{\rm BH,max}
.

The
8

M_{\rm BH}
, based (in part) on their
L_{\rm bol}
estimates and requiring
L_{\rm bol}=L_{\rm Edd}

These
M_{\rm BH}

or
\approx 10^{6}\,M_{\odot}

L_{\rm bol}

These

are somewhat lower than our estimates, driven by lower
L_{\rm bol}
estimates.
Although the real line-to-bolometric conversion in GN 42437, and thus the corresponding
M_{\rm BH,min}
, remains unknown, we again note that the
8

v

The BH masses we derived above for the two galaxies under study are, by themselves, not exceptional. Indeed, many known
z>5
quasars have masses in the range
M_{\rm BH}\sim 10^{8-9}\,M_{\odot}

(
Fan et al. 2023
, and references therein)
. Recent JWST discoveries pushed the lower end of the SMBH mass range among fainter broad-line AGN even further
(
Harikane et al. 2023
;
Maiolino et al. 2024
;
Greene et al. 2024
;
Taylor et al. 2025

.
The two galaxies discussed here are, however, narrow-lined, meaning that their (rest-frame) optical SEDs are dominated by stars, and not by the AGN, providing reasonably robust estimates of stellar masses (see Table
1
and the original papers
51
and
8
). Given the high AGN luminosities implied by the high ionization line emission, one may actually suspect these stellar masses are
over
-estimated, if the (rest-frame) optical SED is indeed contaminated by some continuum AGN emission.

V
Concluding remarks

We have demonstrated how the mere detection of [Ne
v
] in spectroscopic JWST observations of
z\gtrsim 5
galaxies can provide not only a unique opportunity to uncover (obscured) AGN but also to reveal (over-)massive BHs, complementing the more common approach that focuses on broad-line AGN.
Specifically, we argue that two
z\gtrsim 5
galaxies with [Ne
v
] detected in their JWST/NIRSpec spectra may be harboring AGN with luminosities of order
L_{\rm bol}>10^{45}\,{\rm erg\,s}^{-1}
, based on a newly established scaling relation that links
L_{\left[{\rm Ne}\,\textsc{v}\right]}
to
L_{\rm bol}
, calibrated using a highly complete, low-redshift sample of narrow-line AGN.
We further argued that such AGN, selected solely on their [Ne
v
] emission, could be powered by SMBHs with masses of at least
M_{\rm BH}\gtrsim 10^{7}\,M_{\odot}
if they are accreting at Eddington-limited rates, or larger if they their accretion rates are lower. Combined with the available estimates of the stellar masses of their hosts, we derived extremely high BH-to-host mass ratios of
M_{\rm BH}/M_{\rm host}\approx 0.1-1
.

The only way to avoid this latter conclusion is if
(i) these AGN are accreting at rates significantly higher than the Eddington limit—which we think would be interesting given its potential significance to early SMBH growth and the rarity of evidence for such accretion;
(ii) their hosts include an exceptionally significant contribution from accreting IMBHs (again, a novelty); see
8
);
(iii) their host masses are exceptionally under-estimated—which we think is unlikely, but may have implications for other high-redshift galaxies for which
M_{*}
is deduced in similar ways;
and/or
(iv) they are exceptionally luminous in the extreme-UV / soft X-ray regime, compared with other parts of their AGN-related SEDs (i.e., UV-optical), in a way that is not observed in low-redshift AGN with similar high-ionization line emission.

Whatever may be the case for the two objects we studied, our analysis exemplifies how deep spectroscopy of galaxies in the early Universe, aiming for detecting certain (rare and weak) emission lines, can yield insights regarding the earliest phases of SMBH growth.

Failure of the Gibbs inequality for continuous potentials
Arantha Ranu

Abstract
It is well known that the Gibbs inequality, which says that the Gibbs ratio is bounded above and below by positive constants, holds for the unique equilibrium states of Hölder continuous potentials on shift spaces, but it can fail for continuous potentials. In this article, we study the validity of a weaker form of the Gibbs inequality in this broader setting.



1
Introduction

In
[
8
]
Walters proved the variational principle which states that if
X
is a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
is a continuous map on
X
and
\psi
is a continuous fucntion from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
, then the pressure
P(\psi)
of the the potential is given by,

\displaystyle P(\psi)=\sup_{\eta}\left(h_{\eta}(\sigma)+\int\psi\,d\eta\right)

where the supremum is taken over all
T
- invariant measures
\eta
and
h_{\eta}(T)
is the entropy of
T
with respect to
\eta
. In
[
2
]
Bowen showed that for continuous potentials
\psi
on shift spaces the supremum is a maximum. The measures at which the maximum is attained are called
equilibrium states
.

In
[
2
]
it is further proved that for Holder continuous potential
\psi
, there exists a unique equilibrium state
\mu_{\psi}
. Also, there exists
c>0
such that,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}<\frac{\mu_{\psi}(W_{n}(z))}{e^{-nP(\psi)+S_{n}\psi(z)}}<c

(1)

for all
n
- length word
W_{n}
in the language of the finite shift and all
z\in W_{n}
. The unique equilibrium state is called a
Gibbs state
and the property (
1
) satisfied by it is called the
Gibbs property

This is a very important and useful property and has various uses. One of them is to find the Hausdorff dimensions of certain dynamical objects, an example of which can be found in the paper
[
3
]
. Now a natural question is to ask if such upper and lower bounds still exist if we weaken continuity condition on the potentials.

In
[
6
]
Huyi Hu mentions the
“weak Gibbs measures”
for potentials
\psi
that are Holder at all but one point. The equilibrium states of these potentials satisfy,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(z,n)}<\frac{\mu_{\psi}(W_{n}(z))}{e^{-nP(\psi)+S_{n}\psi(z)}}<p(z,n)

(2)

where
\log_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p(z,n)=0
for all
z
except for the point at which the function is non Holder.

In
[
5
]
Hofbauer gave an example of a continuous potential on the one-sided shift on two symbols that has two equilibrium states. One of the equilibrium states for this example is the Dirac measure at the point of all ones. This shows that there there is no suitable positive lower bound for every point in the shift space for the class of continuous potentials. Now the question arises, does there exist a suitable upper bound for the class of continuous potentials?

For a shift space
(Z,\sigma)
and a Holder continuous potential
\psi
we have the distortion property i.e. for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}<\frac{e^{S_{n}\psi(z)}}{e^{S_{n}\psi(z)}}<C

for all
z,z^{\prime}
in the same
n-
cylinder.

A weak version of the distortion property holds for the class of continuous potentials as follows, for a continuous potential
\psi
there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\displaystyle Ce^{-n\epsilon}<\frac{e^{S_{n}\psi(z)}}{e^{S_{n}\psi(z^{\prime})}}<Ce^{n\epsilon}

for all
z,z^{\prime}
in the same
n-
cylinder.

This weaker version of distortion motivates us to define the
“very weak Gibbs inequality”
and the above question about a suitable upper bound for the Gibbs ratio of continuous potentials will be answered by proving the following theorems.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\psi
be a continuous potential on a shift space
(Z,\sigma)
that has an equilibrium state
\mu
. Let
z\in Z
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
[{W_{n}}]
be the
n
-cylinder containing
z
. We say that
\psi
satisfies the
“very weak Gibbs inequality”
at
z
if for
\epsilon>0
there exists
C(z,\epsilon)
such that the following is true,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C(z,\epsilon)e^{n\epsilon}}<\frac{\mu[W_{n}]}{e^{-nP(\psi)+S_{n}\psi(z)}}<C(z,\epsilon)e^{n\epsilon}

(3)

Theorem 1.1
.

For a continuous potential
\psi
on a shift space
(Z,\sigma)
that has an ergodic equilibrium state
\mu
. The
“very weak Gibbs inequality”
is satisfied for
\mu
a.e.
z
.

As we mentioned earlier the example in
[
5
]
has the Dirac measure as one of its equilibrium states which means the lower bound of the
“very weak Gibbs inequality”
is not true for every point in the shift space. The following Theorem states that
\frac{\mu[W_{n}]}{e^{-NP(\psi)+S_{N}\psi(o)}}
grows exponentially which shows that the upper bound is also not true for the point
o
of all
\beta^{\prime}
s.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a continuous potential
\psi
on a full shift that has a unique equilibrium state
\mu
such that for all
n\geq 5
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mu[W_{n}]}{e^{-NP(\psi)+S_{N}\psi(o)}}>\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{n}

where
o
is a fixed point of
\sigma
and
W_{n}
is the
n
- word containing
o
.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in section
5
and Theorem
1.1
is proved in section
6
.

Let

[a_{0}\ldots a_{n-1}]_{X}=\{x\in X:x_{0}=a_{0},\ldots x_{n-1}=a_{n-1}\}

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Definition 2.3
.

Define
\mathcal{DN}_{k}(x)=2^{k-1}a_{k-1}+\ldots+2a_{1}+a_{0}
which is the
number corresponding to the binary sequence

\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)

Definition 2.4
.

The map

is defined as,

T(x)_{k}=

\begin{cases}0&\text{if, }\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)=11\ldots 11\\
1&\text{if, }\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)=01\ldots 11\\
x_{k}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Here are a few examples to show how the map works.

\displaystyle T(\ldots 011110)=\ldots 011111

\displaystyle T(\ldots 011111)=\ldots 100000

The odometer map can be seen as
“adding

with carry in binary”
. We conclude the following from this:-

•

\mathcal{DN}_{k}(T(x))=\mathcal{DN}_{k}(x)+1\mod 2^{k}
.

•

The set,
\{\mathcal{DN}_{k}(x),\mathcal{DN}_{k}(T(x)),\ldots,\mathcal{DN}_{k}(T^{2^{k}-1}(x))\}
is equal to
\{0,..,2^{k}-1\}
.

•

For the
k
- cylinder set
[a_{0}a_{1}\ldots a_{k-1}]_{X}
, for all
x\in X
, there exists
i\in\{0,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
such that
T^{i}(x)\in[a_{0}a_{1}\ldots a_{k-1}]_{X}
.

The inverse of the odometer map is defined as,

T^{-1}(x)_{k}=

\begin{cases}1&\text{if, }\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)=00\ldots 00\\
0&\text{if, }\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)=10\ldots 00\\
x_{k}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Definition 2.5
.

For
x\in X
, let
\kappa(x)
denote the number of zeros that
x
ends with. It is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\kappa(x)=\begin{cases}n&\text{if }x\in[10^{n}]_{X}\\
\infty&\text{if }x=\overline{0}\text{ is the all zero element.}\end{cases}

Definition 2.6
.

Define the metric
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})=2^{-\min\{i:x_{i}\neq x_{i}^{\prime}\}}
for
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.

Note:-
The Borel
\sigma
- algebra on
X
is generated by the cylinder sets and they are clopen sets.

Definition 2.7
.

We define a
measure

\nu
on the Borel
\sigma
- algebra of
X
by specifying the measure of cylinder sets as,

\displaystyle\nu([a_{0}\ldots a_{n-1}]_{X})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}

for each
n
and each cylinder
[a_{0},\ldots a_{n-1}]_{X}
.

It is a well known fact that the odometer map

]

[\omega_{-i}\ldots\omega_{-1}.\omega_{0}\ldots\omega_{j}]_{Z}=\{z:z_{-i}=\omega_{-i},\ldots,z_{-1}=\omega_{-1},z_{0}=\omega_{0},\ldots z_{j}=\omega_{j}\}

The

\displaystyle\sigma(\ldots z_{-2}z_{-1}.z_{0}z_{1}z_{2}\ldots)_{k}=\ldots z_{-2}z_{-1}z_{0}.z_{1}z_{2}\ldots\hskip 5.69054pt.

The shift map is invertible and the inverse is defined as

\sigma^{-1}(\ldots z_{-2}z_{-1}.z_{0}z_{1}z_{2}\ldots)=\ldots z_{-2}.z_{-1}z_{0}z_{1}z_{2}\ldots

Note that

.

Definition 2.11
.

Define the

-

Notation:-
The

. The point of all

.

We will use the metric given by,
d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime})=2^{-\min\{|i|:z_{i}=z^{\prime}_{i}\}}

.

.

A nonempty, closed and shift invariant subspace

Definition 2.13
.

For

For a

Some important Theorems:-

Now we will recall the Shannon-McMillan-Breiman Theorem and the Birkhoff Theorem using which we will prove two lemmas. For the following lemmas we consider the space
(X,T)
and
\eta
be a
T
-invariant ergodic measure on
X
.

Theorem 2.1

(Shannon-McMillan-Breiman)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a countable or finite partition of
X
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{-i}\mathcal{P}
and for
x\in X
and let
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
be the element of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
to which
x
belongs. For
\mu
a.e.
x
the following is true,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[-\frac{\log(\eta(\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)))}{n}\right]=h_{\eta}(\sigma,\mathcal{P})

(4)

where
h_{\eta}(\sigma,\mathcal{P})
is the entropy of
X
w.r.t the partition
\mathcal{P}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Birkhoff Theorem)
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
belong to
L^{1}(\eta)
. For
\eta
- a.e.
x
the following is true,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{n}=\int f\,d\eta

(5)

where
{S_{n}f(x)}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}(x))
.

Theorem 2.3

(weak Birkhoff Theorem)
.

Let
\epsilon,\delta>0
and
f\in L^{1}(\eta)
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that such that for all
n\geq N

\eta\left(\{x:\left|\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{n}-\int f\,d\eta\right|<\epsilon\}\right)>1-\delta

Proof.

Let
G_{j}=\{x:\left|\frac{S_{j}f(x)}{j}-\int f\,d\eta\right|<\epsilon\}
and
let
H_{n}=\bigcap_{j=n}^{\infty}G_{j}
. The sequence of sets
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing. So we have

\displaystyle\eta\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta\left(H_{n}\right)

By the Birkhoff Theorem we know that
\eta\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}\right)=1
. Hence there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N
we have
\eta\left(H_{n}\right)>1-\delta
. Since
H_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
so we have
\eta(G_{n})>1-\delta
for all
n\geq N
, i.e.

\eta\left(\{x:\left|\frac{S_{n}f(x)}{n}-\int f\,d\eta\right|<\epsilon\}\right)>1-\delta

∎

In this section we will construct a subspace

•

Every word in

Let

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

y

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be such that for all

,
(y)_{i}^{\infty}\neq\overline{\beta}
and for all

Let
(\tilde{x}^{n})
be a sequence in

such that
\Phi(\tilde{x}^{n})\to y
.

(\tilde{x}^{n})
has a convergent subsequence,

.

\Phi(x)_{0}=y_{0}

\Phi(x)_{0}=\alpha

\Phi(x^{k})\to y

\Phi(x^{k})_{0}=\alpha

. We know that

. That means there exists

. Since
\Phi(x^{k})\to y
that means
\sigma^{-l}(\Phi(x^{k}))=\Phi(T^{-l}(x^{k}))\to\sigma^{-l}(y)
. So we have
\sigma^{-l}(y)_{0}=\alpha
and
\sigma^{-l}(y)_{l}=\beta
. So there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Phi(T^{-l}(x^{k}))_{l}=\beta
for all
k\geq N
. So
T^{l}(T^{-l}(x^{k}))\in A
for all

. There exists

such that

which means

\Phi(T^{-l}(x^{k}))_{l-j_{k}+m}=\beta

. However

Now we have,

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a forward and backward generic point of an ergodic measure
\mu^{\prime}\neq\delta_{o}
. For

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))\leq h_{k}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{where }h_{k}=\frac{k+2}{2^{k}}.

Proof.

In this proof first we will construct a sequence of times

such that the sequence
(T^{n_{p}}(x))
in the orbit of

ends with successively more zeros. The number of zeros that
T^{n_{p}}(x)

.

By Lemma

we know that there exists

for some

by

and assume that

and

.

Recall the function
\kappa(x)
from section

that counts the number of zeros that

ends with. Let
n_{0}=0
and
s_{0}=\kappa(x)
. Now we recursively define the following sequences:

\displaystyle n_{p+1}=n_{p}+2^{s_{p}}

\displaystyle s_{p+1}=\kappa(T^{n_{p+1}}(x)).

Note that
s_{p}\neq\infty
as
(y)_{i}^{\infty}\neq\overline{\beta}
. Also,
T^{n_{p+1}}(x)=T^{n_{p}+2^{s_{p}}}(x)\in[0^{s_{p}+1}]_{X}

Claim 1:

T^{i}(x)\notin[0^{s_{p}+1}]_{X}
for all

.

Note that
T^{n_{p}}(x)\in[10^{s_{p}}]_{X}
and
T^{n_{p+1}}(x)=T^{n_{p}+2^{s_{p}}}(x)\in[0^{s_{p}+1}]_{X}

\{\mathcal{DN}_{s_{p}+1}(T^{i}(x)):n_{p}\leq i<n_{p+1}\}=\{2^{s_{p}},\ldots,2^{s_{p}+1}-1\}
. So
\mathcal{DN}_{s_{p}+1}(T^{i}(x))\neq 0
for all

, which proves that
T^{i}(x)\notin[0^{s_{p}+1}]_{X}
for all
n_{p}\leq i<n_{p+1}
.

Claim 2:
For all
0\leq i<n_{p}
,
\kappa(T^{i}(x))<n_{p}-i

Let

. Since

by claim 1 we have
l\leq\ s_{p-1}<s_{p}
. Now note that
T^{i}(x)\in[0^{l}]_{X}

\displaystyle\mathcal{DN}_{l}(T^{n_{p}}(x))=n_{p}-i\mod 2^{l}.

Also

and we already know that

which means

. So we have
n_{p}-i\geq 2^{l}>l
.

We split the orbit of

into the sets

.

Claim 3:

O_{p}\cap E_{l}=\phi
for all

.

Note that the error sets are nested so it is enough to show that
O_{p}\cap E_{s_{p}}=\phi
. Let

be such that

. That means
T^{q}(x)\in T^{l}[0^{m}]_{X}
for some

and

. So we have

. Now note that

because otherwise we have

which is not true as

. Now using claim 1 and claim 2 we can say that

, so

. Therefore

.

Finally we will prove the Lemma using this claim. The proof strategy is to calculate an upper bound for each
\left|O_{p}\cap E_{k}\right|
and add them to calculate the number of intersections with the entire orbit.

As
(s_{p})
is an increasing sequence there exists

such that
s_{p_{0}-1}<k
and

for

.

To calculate an upper bound for

note that
T^{n_{p}}(x)\in[10^{s_{p}}]_{X}
so for all

we have

. The indices between

and

are of the form

for some

.
Now we can see that,

if and only if

is a multiple of

. The number of multiples of

in

is

. Also, for

a fixed multiple of

we have

for all

.

So we have

\displaystyle\left|O_{p}\cap\bigcup_{j=0}^{i-1}T^{j}[0^{i}]_{X}\right|\leq i\times 2^{s_{p}-i}=\frac{2^{s_{p}}.i}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle\implies|O_{p}\cap E_{k}|\leq\sum_{i=k+1}^{s_{p}}\frac{2^{s_{p}}.i}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{s_{p}}.\frac{k+2}{2^{k}}=2^{s_{p}}h_{k}.

So we have

for all

.

Taking the average over the orbit we have,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{p}}\sum_{i=0}^{n_{p}-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{p}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n_{1}-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))+\sum_{i=n_{1}}^{n_{2}-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))+...+\sum_{i=n_{p-1}}^{n_{p}-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n_{p}}\left(2^{s_{0}}h_{k}+..+2^{s_{p-1}}h_{k}\right)

Note that

, so we get,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{p}}\sum_{i=0}^{n_{p}-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))\leq h_{k}

We know that limit inferior of a sequence is bounded above by the limit inferior of a subsequence, so we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}(T^{i}(x))\leq h_{k}.

∎

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\textbf{1}_{C}(\sigma^{i}(y))=\mu^{\prime}(C).

(7)

Also,

as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{m_{1}}\eta(C_{k_{l}})\geq\tfrac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\implies m_{1}e^{-n(h_{\eta}-\frac{\epsilon}{3})}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\implies m_{1}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}e^{n(h_{\eta}-\frac{\epsilon}{3})}.

Using the lower bound on

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{S_{n}\psi(z_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m_{1}}e^{S_{n}\psi(z_{k_{l}})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\log(m_{1}e^{n(\int\psi\,d\eta-\tfrac{\epsilon}{3})})

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\log(\tfrac{1}{4}e^{n(h_{\eta}+\int\psi\,d\eta-\frac{2\epsilon}{3})})

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\log(e^{n(h_{\eta}+\int\psi\,d\eta-\epsilon)})

\displaystyle=h_{\eta}+\int\psi\,d\eta-\epsilon

∎

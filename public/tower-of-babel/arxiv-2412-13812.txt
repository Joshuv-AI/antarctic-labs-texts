On characteristic elements modulo p p in non-commutative Iwasawa theory
Meng Fai Lim, Chao Qin

Abstract
Coates, Fukaya, Kato, Sujatha and Venjakob come up with a procedure of attaching suitable characteristic element to Selmer groups defined over a non-commutative p p -adic Lie extension, which is subsequently refined by Burns and Venjakob. By their construction, these characteristic elements are realized as elements in an appropriate localized K 1 K_{1} -group. In this paper, we will introduce a notion of modulo p p for these elements and study some of their properties. As an application, we study the Greenberg Selmer group of a tensor product of modular forms, where p p is an Eisenstein prime for one of these forms. Keywords and Phrases: Selmer groups, characteristic elements, non-commutative p p -adic Lie extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 11G05, 11R23, 11S25.

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
p
will always denote an odd prime. Let
\Gamma
denote the multiplicative group which is isomorphic to the additive group of the
p
-adic ring of integers. In Iwasawa theory, the completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
naturally comes into play in the formulation of the main conjecture (for instances, see
[

,

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket

\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the finite field with cardinality

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module which is also finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and

is the characteristic polynomial of

in the sense of
[
31

, we then have
\pi_{\Gamma}(f(x))=T^{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M)}
. This viewpoint has been taken up, most notably, in the works of Greenberg-Vatsal
[
17
]
and Emerton-Pollack-Weston
[
13
]
which has since inspired many later works (see
[
1
,
18
,
22
,
35
]
where this list is probably far from being exhaustive).

At the turn of the millennium, Coates and many others initiated a major study towards the formulation a non-commutative main conjecture (see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
7
,
14
,

,

,

]

K_{1}
-group. The goal of this paper is to introduce and make sense of this characteristic element modulo
p
. We shall describe this briefly in the introduction leaving the details to the body of the paper.

Let
G
be a compact
p
-adic Lie group with no
p
-torsion, which is further assumed to contain a closed normal subgroup
H
such that
G/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Write
\pi=\pi_{G}
for the natural projection map

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket.

Following
[
8
,
38
]

\Sigma=\big\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket~\big|~\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket x\mbox{~is finitely generated over~}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket H\rrbracket\big\},

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}

K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})

We now set

\overline{\Sigma}=\big\{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket~\big|~\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket z\mbox{~is finitely generated over~}\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket H\rrbracket\big\}.

Via a similar argument of Coates

is an Ore set for

. Therefore, similarly as before, one may consider the localized ring

(see Lemma

). This in turn implies that the map

induces a homomorphism of the localized rings

\pi^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\overline{\Sigma}}

which in turn induces a group homomorphism

\pi_{1}:K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})\longrightarrow K_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\overline{\Sigma}}).

This map

will play the role for our proposed notion of modulo

. We will make some preliminary study on this map and its properties in the body of the paper.

2
Localized K-group

Throughout the paper,
p
will always denote an odd prime. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of some finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Fix a local parameter
\varpi
for
\mathcal{O}
and write
k
for the residue field
\mathcal{O}/\varpi
.
For a given compact
p
-adic Lie group
G
, its completed group algebra over
\mathcal{O}
is then defined by

\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket:=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{U}\mathcal{O}[G/U],

where
U
runs over the open normal subgroups of
G
and the inverse
limit is taken with respect to the canonical projection maps. A theorem of Venjakob
[
36
, Theorem 3.26]
asserts that
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
is a Noetherian Auslander regular ring. Furthermore, in the event that
G
is pro-
p
and has no
p
-torsion, the completed group algebra
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
is known to be a local ring with no zero divisors (see
[
32
]
).
Consequently, it admits a skew field
Q(G)
which is flat
over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
(see
[
15
, Chapters 6 and 10]
or
[
26
, Chapter
4, §9 and §10]
). In view of this, one can define the
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-rank of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
via

\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M):=\dim_{Q(G)}(Q(G)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}M).

We shall say that
M
is a torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module when
\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)=0
.
It is a standard fact that
M
is a torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module if and only if
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
(for instance, see
[
27
, Lemma 4.2]
). If the torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
also satisfies
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
, we say that
M
is a pseudo-null
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module.

We now extend the notion of torsion modules and pseudo-null modules to the case when
G
is a compact
p
-adic Lie group which is not necessarily pro-
p
or has
p
-torsion. By a well-known theorem of Lazard, the
p
-adic Lie group
G
contains an open normal subgroup
G_{0}
which is pro-
p
with no
p
-torsion. On the other hand, it follows from
[
31
, Proposition 5.4.17]
that

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\cong\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket)

for every finitely generated
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
. In view of these observations, we shall say that
M
is a torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module (resp., pseudo-null
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module) if
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
(resp.,
\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket)=0
for
i=0,1
). Equivalently, this is saying that
M
is a torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module (resp., pseudo-null
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module), if
M
is a torsion
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket
-module (resp., pseudo-null
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G_{0}\rrbracket
-module) as in the preceding paragraph. (Also, compare with
[
36
, Discussion after Definition 2.6]
).

2.1
Characteristic element in Iwasawa theory

Following
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
14
,
38
,
39
]
, we describe the procedure of attaching characteristic elements to a certain class of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-modules. From now on,
G
will always denote a compact
p
-adic Lie group which contains a closed normal subgroup
H
such that
\Gamma:=G/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Write
\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G)
for the category of finitely generated
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
satisfying the property that
M
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket
. To facilitate subsequent discussion, we recall an equivalent description of this class of modules as given in
[
8
]
. Set

\Sigma:=\Sigma_{G}:=\big\{x\in\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket~\big|~\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket x\mbox{ is finitely generated over }\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket\big\}.

Thanks to
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
, we now know that
\Sigma
is a left and right Ore set of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
. It therefore makes sense to speak of the localization of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
with respect to
\Sigma
, which in turn is denoted by
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}
. Furthermore, it follows from
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
that a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module
M
is annihilated by
\Sigma
if and only if
M
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket
. This in turn induces an identification
K_{0}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})\cong K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))
.
The localization sequence in
K
-theory (for instance, see
[
3
]
) then yields
the following exact sequence

K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\longrightarrow K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{0}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket,\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket)\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})\longrightarrow 0.

We now state the following fundamental result of the five authors
[
8
, Proposition 3.4]
(also see
[
41
, Corollary 3.8]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

The above connecting homomorphism
\partial_{G}
is surjective.

In view of the above lemma, Coates
et al

[
8
]
define a characteristic element for
M
in
\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G)
to be an element
\xi_{M}\in K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
such that
\partial_{G}(\xi_{M})=-[M]
. From their definition, we see that there can be more than one choice of characteristic element. It is then natural to ask for a “good” choice of such a characteristic element. Pertaining to this, Burns-Venjakob
[
6
]
(also see
[
4
]
) proposed such a canonical choice when the group
G
has no
p
-torsion. (In particular, see
[
6
, Remarks 3.2 and 3.3]
and references therein for reasons why this choice is regarded canonical.) We shall describe their construction here.

As a start, we make the following remark. Since the group
G
has no
p
-torsion, the Iwasawa algebra
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
has finite global dimension (see
[
31
, Chap. V, §2, Exercise 5]
). By
[
15
, Chap. 7, Corollary 4.3]
, this in turn implies that the localized ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}
has finite global dimension. Hence one can identify
K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
with the group
G_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
that is generated (multiplicatively) by symbols of the form
\langle\alpha~|~Y\rangle
, where
\alpha
is an automorphism of a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}
-module
Y
. We shall make use of this latter identification without any further mention.

Now, let
M
be a given module in
\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G)
. Fix a topological generator
\gamma
of the group
\Gamma
.
Consider the completed tensor product

I^{G}_{H}(M)=\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket}M

which has a natural action of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
via multiplication on the left. With respect to this action, we define an endomorphism
\Delta_{\gamma}
of
I^{G}_{H}(M)
by setting

\Delta_{\gamma}(a\otimes b)=a\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\gamma}b

for each
a\in\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket
and
b\in M
, where
\widetilde{\gamma}\in G
is a lift of the topological generator
\gamma
. We remark that
\Delta_{\gamma}
is independent of the choice of
\widetilde{\gamma}
. By
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
, the map
\delta_{\gamma,M}:=\mathrm{id}_{I^{G}_{H}(M)}-\Delta_{\gamma}
induces an automorphism of the
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}
-module
I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}
which in turn allows us to speak of

\mathrm{char}_{G,\gamma}(M):=\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,M}~\Big|~I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}\Big\rangle

which lies in
K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
. We emphasis that the definition of
\mathrm{char}_{G,\gamma}(M)
is dependent on the choice of
\gamma
. However, since we always work with a fixed choice of
\gamma
, we shall therefore drop
``\gamma"
from our notation.

By
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
, the assignment
[M]\mapsto\mathrm{char}_{G,\gamma}(M)
satisfies multiplicative property for a short exact sequence of modules in
\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G)
, and so induces a group homomorphism
K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))\longrightarrow K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
. Precomposing this group homomorphism with the map
K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))
,
[M]\mapsto-[M]
, we obtain a map
\chi:K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))\longrightarrow K_{1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma})
defined by
-[M]\mapsto\mathrm{char}_{G}(M)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The above defined map
\chi
is a split map of the connecting homomorphism
\partial_{G}
.
In other words, one has
\partial_{G}(\mathrm{char}_{G}(M))=-[M]
.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
4
, Theorem 4.1(i)]
(also see
[
6
, Proposition 4.7(i)]
).

Remark 2.5
.

In particular, it follows from the preceding proposition that the map
\overline{\partial}
is surjective.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow k\llbracket G\rrbracket
denote the ring homomorphism induced by the canonical projection map
\mathcal{O}\twoheadrightarrow k
. The following lemma relates the two Ore sets.

Lemma 2.6
.

One has
\pi(\Sigma)=\overline{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
s\in\Sigma
. Then by definition, the module
\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket s
is finitely generated over
k\llbracket H\rrbracket
. A direct calculation yields the following observation

k\llbracket G\rrbracket/k\llbracket G\rrbracket\pi(s)=\Big(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket s\Big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket}k\llbracket G\rrbracket\cong\Big(\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket/\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket s\Big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k.

From which, it follows that
k\llbracket G\rrbracket/k\llbracket G\rrbracket\pi(s)
is finitely generated over
k\llbracket H\rrbracket
. This proves

\pi(\Sigma)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma}
.

For the other inclusion, we choose a pro-
p
subgroup
J
of
H
which is normal in
G
, and write
\psi_{J}:\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket
and
\theta_{J}:k\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket
for the natural surjections. Note that
\psi_{J}=\theta_{J}\circ\pi
. Now for each
z\in\overline{\Sigma}
, choose
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
such that
\pi(s)=z
. Then it follows that
k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket/k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket\psi_{J}(s)=k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket/k\llbracket G/J\rrbracket\theta_{J}(z)
is finite. By
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
, this in turn implies that
s\in\Sigma
, thus establishing the reverse inclusion.
∎

In view of the preceding lemma, the map
\pi:\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket\longrightarrow k\llbracket G\rrbracket
induces a ring homomorphism

\pi^{\prime}:\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow k\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\overline{\Sigma}}

which fits into the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}

Proposition 2.7

The following diagram

\textstyle{K_{0}(\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let
M

\mathfrak{N}_{H}(G)

\overline{\chi}\circ\pi_{0}(-[M])=\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,\overline{I}^{G}_{H}(M/\varpi)_{\overline{\Sigma}}}~\Big|~\overline{I}^{G}_{H}(M/\varpi)_{\overline{\Sigma}}\Big\rangle\cdot\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,\overline{I}^{G}_{H}(M[\varpi])_{\overline{\Sigma}}}~\Big|~\overline{I}^{G}_{H}(M[\varpi])_{\overline{\Sigma}}\Big\rangle^{-1}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\circ\chi(-[M])\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\pi_{1}\Big(\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}}~\Big|~I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}\Big\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}}^{\prime}~\Big|~I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}/\varpi\Big\rangle\cdot\Big\langle\delta_{\gamma,I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}}^{\prime\prime}~\Big|~I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}[\varpi]\Big\rangle^{-1}

I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}[\varpi]\cong I^{G}_{H}(M[\varpi])_{\Sigma},

I^{G}_{H}(M)_{\Sigma}/\varpi\cong I^{G}_{H}(M/\varpi)_{\Sigma},

\overline{\mathfrak{N}}_{H}(G)

Indeed, applying

\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket}N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\varpi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\Sigma}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket}N\longrightarrow k\llbracket G\rrbracket_{\overline{\Sigma}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket}N\longrightarrow 0.

(3)

Corollary 2.8
.

Let

\overline{\mathfrak{N}}_{H}(G)

Indeed it follows from the preceding proposition that

∎

Proof of Corollary

.

Since (a) holds, we see that

Suppose that (a) and (b)(i) hold

We shall show that under these hypotheses, one has

\pi_{1}\left(\mathrm{char}_{G}\big(\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{p}}\big(B^{-}_{p}(F_{\infty,p})\big)^{\vee}\big)\right)

. For this, it again suffices to show that

be the maximal pro-

[

In view of (b)(ii), we have

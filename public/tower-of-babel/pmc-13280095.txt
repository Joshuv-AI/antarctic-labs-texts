Outcomes and Adverse Events Among Patients with Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococcal Bloodstream Infection Treated with Daptomycin Doses Greater than 12 mg/kg
Authors: Luo-Wei Lin, Yu-Chung Chuang, Jia-Ling Yang, Chi-Ying Lin, Sung-Hsi Huang, Jann-Tay Wang, Yee-Chun Chen, Shan-Chwen Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDaptomycin is commonly used for vancomycin-resistant enterococcal bloodstream infections (VRE-BSIs); however, its optimal dosing remains uncertain. Although doses > 12 mg/kg may improve attainment of pharmacodynamic targets, clinical outcomes and safety data for very-high–dose daptomycin are limited.MethodsThis multicenter cohort study included adults with a VRE-BSI treated with daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg, between 2011 and 2024. The primary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality. Daptomycin-associated adverse events, including creatine kinase (CK) elevation, were assessed, and multivariable logistic regression models were used to identify factors associated with mortality and an elevated CK level.ResultsAmong 101 patients, the median age was 70.2 years, and 67.3% had an underlying malignancy. The 28-day mortality was 41.6% and was primarily associated with host factors and illness severity. Daptomycin dose was not associated with mortality when modeled as a continuous variable; however, in exploratory post hoc analysis, doses > 13 mg/kg were associated with lower mortality compared with 12–13 mg/kg [adjusted odds ratio (aOR), 0.22; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.05–0.98; p = 0.047]. CK elevation occurred in 13.9% and was independently associated with higher daptomycin dose (aOR, 2.99; 95% CI 1.05–8.50). Eosinophilic pneumonia was identified in 10.9% of patients.ConclusionsIn patients with VRE-BSI treated with daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg, mortality remained high and was primarily driven by host factors and severity of illness. Introduction

Vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE) have emerged as a major cause of healthcare-associated bloodstream infections (BSIs), and they are associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [1]. Therapeutic options for VRE-BSIs remain limited, and clinical outcomes are often poor, particularly among critically ill patients and those with significant comorbidities [2].

Daptomycin, a rapidly bactericidal lipopeptide antibiotic with concentration-dependent activity [3], is widely used to treat VRE-BSIs [4]. Early meta-analyses suggested that daptomycin might be inferior to linezolid for treating VRE-BSIs [5, 6]; however, these findings were largely derived from studies using relatively low daptomycin doses. Recent observational studies have suggested that outcomes with daptomycin may improve when higher doses are administered [7–10], although the overall mortality remains high [8, 11].

Concerns have been raised regarding the adequacy of standard high-dose (8–12 mg/kg) daptomycin regimens [12]. Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) studies have demonstrated that even doses of 10–12 mg/kg may not consistently achieve optimal attainment of target against VRE isolates [13, 14]. Given the concentration-dependent bactericidal activity of daptomycin, dose escalation with combination therapy, has been proposed as a strategy to increase drug exposure and enhance antimicrobial efficacy [15]. A recent experimental PK/PD study using an animal model suggested that very-high–dose (> 12 mg/kg) daptomycin may provide greater bactericidal activity than combination regimens [16]; however, clinical experience with daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg remains limited [12].

Importantly, most available clinical data have focused on comparisons between standard- or high-dose daptomycin regimens [17–19], whereas outcomes and safety among patients treated exclusively with very-high-dose daptomycin remain poorly characterized. In addition to questions regarding its efficacy, the safety of very-high–dose daptomycin has not been completely defined. An elevated creatine kinase (CK) level is a known adverse effect of daptomycin treatment [20, 21], and prior analyses have suggested a relationship between elevated trough concentrations and myotoxicity [22]; however, most safety data have been generated from cohorts receiving lower doses [21]. Accordingly, the present study evaluated clinical outcomes and safety in patients with VRE-BSIs treated with daptomycin at doses > 12 mg/kg. Rather than comparing very-high-dose with standard-dose regimens, we analyzed a cohort already receiving very-high-dose therapy to characterize clinical features, identify factors associated with 28-day mortality, and assess daptomycin-associated adverse events, with particular attention to elevated CK levels.

Methods

Study Setting and Patient Selection

This multicenter study was conducted within the National Taiwan University Hospital (NTUH) healthcare system, which comprises the main hospital and its affiliated branches in Hsin-Chu and Yun-Lin. While the cohort was identified prospectively through routine institutional infection surveillance, the current sub-analysis was conducted retrospectively. Patients diagnosed with a VRE-BSI between June 2011 and August 2024, were included, and only the first episode was analyzed for patients with multiple episodes. All adult patients with a VRE-BSI were eligible for inclusion. Exclusion criteria were age < 18 years, absence of active treatment for a VRE-BSI, treatment with linezolid, or treatment with daptomycin at a dose ≤ 12 mg/kg. No institutional guideline governed daptomycin dosing during the study period. Doses > 12 mg/kg were prescribed at the discretion of the treating infectious-disease physician for selected patients with VRE-BSI, motivated by (1) PK/PD data suggesting that conventional high-dose regimens (8–12 mg/kg) may not reliably attain bactericidal exposure against Enterococcus faecium [12–14], (2) institutional dose-finding experience and preclinical work supporting very-high-dose daptomycin monotherapy in daptomycin-resistant E. faecium endocarditis [9–11, 14, 16], and (3) clinical scenarios in which standard high-dose therapy was perceived as insufficient (e.g., difficult source control or severe clinical illness). Dosing intervals were adjusted according to the manufacturer’s prescribing information.

Ethics

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of NTUH (Protocol No. 202601207RIND). Given the observational nature of the study, minimal risk to participants, and the use of de-identified data, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee. This waiver was granted without compromising the rights or welfare of the participants.

Microbiological Studies and Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing

Initial blood cultures were processed by clinical microbiology laboratories at each participating site. Enterococcus spp. were identified using a VITEK 2 system (bioMérieux, La Balme-les-Grottes, France), before 2017, and subsequently via matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry using a Biotyper system (MicroFlex LT; Bruker Daltonik, Bremen, Germany).

All VRE isolates were stored at − 80 °C and transported to the NTUH main hospital for centralized determination of the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC). Vancomycin resistance was defined as an MIC ≥ 32 mg/L. Post hoc daptomycin MICs were determined using the broth microdilution method with calcium supplementation to 50 mg/L in cation-adjusted Mueller–Hinton broth, in accordance with Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute guidelines [23], and they were not available to treating clinicians at the time of therapy.

Clinical Data Collection and Definitions

Demographic data, clinical characteristics, underlying comorbidities, sites of infection [24], and clinical outcomes were collected. The onset of BSI was defined as the date of first positive blood culture. Catheter-related BSI was defined according to established criteria [25]. Underlying comorbidities were assessed using the Charlson comorbidity index [26]. The severity of illness at onset of BSI was quantified using the Pitt bacteremia score, calculated on the day the index blood culture was obtained [27]. Immunosuppression was defined according to previously published criteria [28].

Daptomycin dose was calculated using actual body weight and expressed as mg/kg [3]. Given the linear, dose-proportional PK of daptomycin across the clinically relevant dosing range [29], a one-compartment linear PK model, with first-order elimination, was used to estimate the steady-state trough concentration (Cmin), the free area under the curve at steady state (fAUC), and, where the broth microdilution MIC was available, the fAUC/MIC ratio, following the methodology applied in our previous work [14]. The volume of distribution was estimated as 0.14 L/kg, which is consistent with published data in patients with acute infection. These PK assumptions and equations follow standard theory for multiple-dose regimens [30, 31]. All Cmin, fAUC, and fAUC/MIC values reported in this study are model-based estimates rather than directly measured concentrations; therapeutic drug monitoring of daptomycin was not performed during the study period.

The primary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality from the onset of VRE-BSI. Secondary outcomes included daptomycin-associated adverse events, specifically an elevated CK level, acute kidney injury (AKI), and daptomycin-induced eosinophilic pneumonia (DIEP). An elevated CK level was defined as a serum CK level exceeding the upper limit of normal during daptomycin therapy. At NTUH, routine monitoring of the CK level is recommended at least once weekly during daptomycin treatment or earlier if patients develop muscle-related symptoms such as myalgia or weakness [32]. AKI was defined according to Kidney Disease-Improving Global Outcomes criteria, as an increase in the serum creatinine level of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL or ≥ 50% from baseline [33]. DIEP was defined according to modified diagnostic criteria [34]. Patients were classified as definite DIEP if they presented with new pulmonary infiltrates and clinical symptoms in the absence of an alternative diagnosis, accompanied by documented peripheral eosinophilia (absolute eosinophil count > 500 cells/µL) or bronchoalveolar lavage eosinophilia (> 25%). Probable DIEP was defined as meeting these clinical and radiographic criteria with rapid improvement following discontinuation of daptomycin, but without documented eosinophilia at the time of diagnosis.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are summarized as median [interquartile range, (IQR)] and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables are expressed as frequency or percentage and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Two-sided p < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant for univariable comparisons.

To identify factors associated with outcomes, multivariable logistic regression models were constructed. Variables with p ≤ 0.15 in univariable analyses were considered for inclusion in multivariable models. Backward stepwise selection based on minimization of the Akaike information criterion was used to derive the final model. Adjusted odds ratio (aOR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) are reported, and a two-sided p < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

To explore potential non-linear relationships between daptomycin dose and 28-day mortality, a locally-weighted scatterplot smoothing (LOWESS) curve was generated. Additionally, given the skewed distribution of daptomycin doses with limited variance, exploratory categorical analyses (12–13 mg/kg vs. > 13 mg/kg) were performed to identify potential threshold effects that might be obscured when modeling dose as a continuous variable. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata software (version 17.0; StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

Results

Study Participants and Baseline Characteristics

During the study period, 2301 episodes of VRE-BSI were identified. After applying pre-specified inclusion and exclusion criteria, 101 patients who received daptomycin at doses > 12 mg/kg were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). All VRE isolates in the final cohort were E. faecium. The median age was 70.2 years (IQR, 62.3–78.6), and 52 patients (51.5%) were male (Table 1).

BSI bloodstream infection

More than half of the cohort had an underlying malignancy (67.3%), and 21.2% presented with neutropenia at the time of BSI; of these 21 neutropenic patients, 14 (66.7%) had a hematologic malignancy, 9 (42.9%) had received recent chemotherapy, and 4 (19.0%) had a history of bone marrow transplantation. The median Charlson comorbidity index was 5 (IQR, 3–6), and the median Pitt bacteremia score at presentation was 2 (IQR, 0–3). The most common sources of infection were urinary tract infection (44.6%) and primary bacteremia (43.6%). Of the 101 patients, 52 (51.5%) were male and 49 (48.5%) were female. The 28-day mortality was numerically higher in men than in women [48.1% (25/52) vs. 34.7% (17/49); p = 0.17], although the difference was not statistically significant (Supplementary Table S1).

Daptomycin was initiated at a median of 2 days (IQR, 1–3) after the index blood culture, and the median duration of daptomycin therapy was 13 days (IQR, 7–15). Most patients (83.2%) received daptomycin at a dose of 12–13 mg/kg, and concomitant β-lactam therapy was administered in 80 patients (79.2%). Post hoc daptomycin MIC testing was available for 86 isolates. The MIC50 and MIC90 were 2 and 4 mg/L, respectively. Six isolates (7.0%) had elevated MICs, including four with an MIC of 8 mg/L, one with an MIC of 16 mg/L, and one with an MIC > 16 mg/L.

The overall 28-day mortality was 42 of 101 patients (41.6%). There were adverse events in a subset of patients, including an elevated CK level in 14 patients (13.9%) and AKI in 37 of 90 patients (41.1%) without baseline renal replacement therapy. Microbiological failure, defined as persistent VRE on follow-up blood culture ≥ 4 days after VRE onset, occurred in 14 (13.9%) of 101 patients.

Among patients with an elevated CK level, daptomycin was discontinued in two, and they were switched to alternative anti-VRE therapy, whereas 12 completed treatment without dose modification. Two patients had a markedly elevated CK level (> 1000 U/L). The CK level declined after discontinuation of daptomycin in one of these patients; the other patient died within 24 h of an elevated CK level due to profound septic shock, precluding assessment of biochemical resolution.

Peripheral eosinophilia was documented in ten (9.9%) patients. Of these, seven met the clinical criteria for definite DIEP. In addition, four patients without documented peripheral eosinophilia were classified as having probable DIEP, based on a strong temporal association between exposure to daptomycin and the development of otherwise unexplained pulmonary infiltrates. Among patients with definite or probable DIEP, nine had clinical and radiographic improvement following discontinuation of daptomycin, supporting a likely drug-related etiology. None of the patients with DIEP received corticosteroid therapy, and there was clinical improvement with discontinuation of daptomycin alone. Two patients with definite DIEP died before clinical resolution; both deaths were attributed to ventilator-associated pneumonia with concurrent respiratory failure, to which DIEP might also have contributed. Factors Associated with 28-Day Mortality

In univariable analyses (Table 1), 28-day mortality was associated with markers of host vulnerability and severity of illness, including underlying malignancy, neutropenia, immunosuppressant use, and a higher Pitt bacteremia score. Daptomycin dose was not significantly associated with mortality in unadjusted analyses.

In the primary multivariable logistic regression model, neutropenia, underlying malignancy, and a higher Pitt bacteremia score were independently associated with increased 28-day mortality (Table 2). Neutropenia was associated with more than a three-fold increase in the likelihood of death (OR, 3.61; 95% CI 1.16–11.19), and underlying malignancy was similarly associated with increased risk of mortality (OR, 3.61; 95% CI 1.06–12.30). Each one-point increase in the Pitt bacteremia score was associated with a 33% increase in the likelihood of 28-day mortality (OR, 1.33; 95% CI 1.06–1.66). Immunosuppressant use and the serum bilirubin level were included as adjustment variables, but they were not independently associated with mortality.

Exploratory Analyses of Daptomycin Dose and Mortality

Exploratory analyses examining the relationship between daptomycin dose and 28-day mortality suggested a nonlinear trend in the LOWESS visualization (Fig. S1). Daptomycin dose was not independently associated with mortality when modeled as a continuous variable (per mg/kg increase, aOR, 0.60; 95% CI 0.19–1.90; p = 0.38). However, in the exploratory categorical analysis, there was lower mortality among patients receiving doses > 13 mg/kg compared with those receiving 12–13 mg/kg (aOR, 0.22; 95% CI 0.05–0.98; p = 0.047). A detailed comparison of model-estimated daptomycin Cmin, fAUC, and fAUC/MIC between survivors and non-survivors, and between the 12–13 mg/kg and > 13 mg/kg dose strata, is presented in Supplementary Table S2.

Adverse Events and an Elevated CK Level

Median daptomycin doses did not differ between patients with and without AKI or DIEP (p = 0.88 and p = 0.47, respectively). In contrast, patients who had an elevated CK level received numerically higher daptomycin doses than those without an elevated CK level [median (IQR), 12.5 (12.3–13.3) vs. 12.4 (12.2–12.7) mg/kg], although this difference was not statistically significant in the univariable analysis (p = 0.30).

Risk factors associated with an elevated CK level are summarized in Supplementary Table 3. Statin use was uncommon (six patients, 5.9%) and it was not associated with an elevated CK level (p = 0.84). In the multivariable logistic regression analysis (Table 3), higher daptomycin dose was independently associated with an increased risk of an elevated CK level (aOR, 2.99; 95% CI 1.05–8.50; p = 0.04), an association that remained consistent after adjustment for statin use, while underlying malignancy was inversely associated with an elevated CK level (aOR, 0.46; 95% CI 0.14–1.50; p = 0.20). Estimated daptomycin Cmin, including a cutoff of > 24.3 mg/L, was not significantly associated with an elevated CK level in the univariable or multivariable analyses.

Discussion

In this cohort of patients with a VRE-BSI treated with daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg, the patients were predominantly older and frequently had an underlying malignancy. Despite the use of high-dose daptomycin, 28-day mortality remained high at 41.6%. Underlying malignancy, neutropenia, and a higher Pitt bacteremia score were independently associated with increased mortality, underscoring the dominant influence of host vulnerability and severity of illness in this cohort. When modeled as a continuous variable, a higher daptomycin dose was not independently associated with lower mortality. Notably, increasing the daptomycin dose was independently associated with a higher risk of an elevated CK level.

From a PK/PD perspective, daptomycin has concentration-dependent bactericidal activity against enterococci [35], and prior PK/PD studies have suggested that conventional high-dose regimens may not reliably achieve optimal attainment of target in patients with a VRE-BSI [12]. In this context, dose escalation > 12 mg/kg has been proposed as a strategy to increase drug exposure and to potentially improve outcomes [16]. In this study, there was a significant reduction in mortality with doses > 13 mg/kg in the categorical analysis, but not when dose was modeled as a continuous variable. This pattern suggests that the dose–mortality relationship is not linear; rather, daptomycin may exhibit a PD threshold effect, whereby a critical exposure target (e.g., area under the curve/MIC ratio) must be achieved for bactericidal activity [13, 14]. The clustering of doses in the 12–13 mg/kg range further limits statistical power to detect a continuous dose–response relationship. Therefore, the > 13-mg/kg group may represent those who successfully crossed this PD threshold. Nevertheless, this observation is exploratory and should be interpreted cautiously given the small number of patients in the higher-dose group and the potential for residual confounding factors.

Several daptomycin-associated adverse events, including an elevated CK level, AKI, and DIEP, warrant careful consideration. Although previous studies have generally suggested that daptomycin is well tolerated, with acceptable rates of elevation of the CK level [21], most available data were derived from cohorts who received substantially lower doses, and safety data for doses > 12 mg/kg remain limited. A post hoc analysis of Phase 3 trials reported an increased risk of an elevated CK level when the estimated daptomycin Cmin was > 24.3 mg/L [22]. In the present study, there was no significant association between estimated Cmin and an elevated CK level, which may be attributable to the limited sample size and the use of estimated rather than directly measured trough concentrations.

In multivariable analysis, a higher daptomycin dose was independently associated with CK elevation, despite no significant difference in median dose between affected and unaffected patients. Underlying malignancy showed a non-significant inverse trend with CK elevation (aOR, 0.46; 95% CI 0.14–1.50; p = 0.20), and this descriptive pattern prompts consideration of biological mechanisms that may diminish the capacity to manifest daptomycin-induced myotoxicity in patients with malignancy, such as lower BMI, lower baseline creatinine, and a higher rate of recent chemotherapy. The observed rate of an elevated CK level in the present cohort was higher than that reported in historical series [8, 21], which may reflect differences in patient characteristics and severity of illness. Indeed, even with once-daily dosing, extremely high doses of daptomycin may result in an elevated trough exposure and an increased risk of myotoxicity [36].

The retrospective ascertainment of DIEP warrants careful interpretation, as the incidence may have been underestimated without routine bronchoalveolar lavage [34], or overestimated through misattribution of pulmonary infiltrates to daptomycin. Although earlier reviews have suggested that DIEP is unlikely to be dose dependent [37], the absence of a dose–response relationship for DIEP within the > 12-mg/kg range in our cohort suggests that the markedly elevated incidence may reflect a threshold phenomenon, whereby supratherapeutic exposure above a critical concentration, rather than incremental dose increases, promotes drug accumulation near the alveolar surface, triggering epithelial injury and inflammation [37, 38]. This interpretation is consistent with the observation that the DIEP incidence in our cohort (10.9%) far exceeded rates reported in registries using standard doses [21]. Furthermore, our systematic retrospective review of pulmonary imaging findings and clinical responses to drug withdrawal may have identified patients who are often overlooked in routine clinical practice. In this cohort, none of the patients with DIEP required corticosteroid therapy, and there was clinical improvement after discontinuation of daptomycin alone, which suggests that early recognition and prompt discontinuation of daptomycin may be sufficient for clinical improvement in selected patients. These findings underscore the necessity for increased clinical vigilance for this rare but potentially serious adverse event when high-dose daptomycin is administered.

This study had several important limitations. First, the sample size was modest, which limited statistical power, and cautious interpretation of the findings is necessary. Although this represents one of the largest contemporary cohorts evaluating daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg for patients with a VRE-BSI, the possibility of Type II error cannot be excluded. Second, the retrospective nature of the study introduced the potential for residual confounding factors and bias by indication. In routine clinical practice, higher daptomycin doses may be preferentially administered to patients who are perceived as more critically ill or to those receiving more aggressive treatment strategies, which may influence the associations between dose and outcome. Accordingly, the dose–response relationships identified in this study should be interpreted cautiously and warrant validation in prospective studies. Third, the majority of the patients received daptomycin doses between 12 and 13 mg/kg, whereas relatively few patients received doses > 13 mg/kg, and concomitant statin use was uncommon. Therefore, we were unable to comprehensively assess the safety of extreme dose escalation or the potential synergistic myotoxic effects between daptomycin and statins [39]. Nevertheless, the association between a higher daptomycin dose and an elevated CK level suggests that there is a dose-related toxicity signal, even in the absence of frequent statin co-administration. Fourth, beyond 28-day all-cause mortality, we did not capture standardized clinical-resolution endpoints (e.g., defervescence, resolution of inflammatory signs, symptom-free status) prospectively, and the available microbiological-response data are descriptive only. Future prospective studies should incorporate these proximate clinical-effectiveness endpoints to better isolate the effect of dose on antimicrobial efficacy from the effect of host vulnerability on overall survival. In addition, all reported daptomycin Cmin and fAUC values are simulated from a one-compartment population pharmacokinetic model (Methods); inter-individual variability in volume of distribution and clearance, particularly in critically ill or chemotherapy-treated patients, may not be fully captured by population-average parameters, and the exposure–response analyses should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating. Finally, this study was designed as a descriptive analysis of patients receiving supra-therapeutic doses, and a concurrent control group receiving standard high-dose daptomycin (8–12 mg/kg) was not included. Consequently, we could not directly compare the efficacy or safety of doses > 12 mg/kg with the current standard of care. This design was chosen to focus specifically on characterizing the clinical outcomes and adverse event profile of this increasingly used but poorly studied dosing strategy. Additionally, although 79.2% of patients received concomitant β-lactam therapy, which may exert synergistic activity with daptomycin against enterococci, the uniformly high rate of combination therapy in this cohort limits our ability to assess its independent contribution to outcomes. While the very-high-dose strategy at our center is supported by PK/PD and preclinical data, it has not been validated in a randomized clinical trial; prospective matched-cohort or randomized comparisons are needed to determine whether the potential benefits outweigh the increased risk of toxicity observed in our cohort.

Conclusions

In this cohort of patients with a VRE-BSI treated with daptomycin doses > 12 mg/kg, mortality remained high and it was primarily driven by host factors and severity of illness rather than the daptomycin dose. Although exploratory analyses suggested the potential benefit of doses > 13 mg/kg, a higher daptomycin dose was also independently associated with an increased risk of an elevated CK level, and the incidence of daptomycin-induced eosinophilic pneumonia was markedly higher than that reported at standard doses. These findings underscore the requirement to balance potential gains in efficacy against toxicity, and to highlight the importance of prospective studies to define the optimal dosing strategy for daptomycin in this high-risk cohort.

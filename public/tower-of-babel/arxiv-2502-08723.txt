Explicit bounds on the transcendental Brauer group of K3 surfaces with principal complex multiplication
Sebastian Monnet

Abstract
. Let X X be a K3 surface defined over a number field k k , with principal complex multiplication by a CM field E E . We find explicit bounds, in terms of k k and E E , on the size of the transcendental Brauer group Br ⁡ ( X ) / Br 1 ⁡ ( X ) \Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X) of X X . Bounding the size of this group is important for computing the Brauer–Manin obstruction, which is conjectured by Skorobogatov to be the only obstruction to the Hasse principle for K3 surfaces. Our methods are built on top of earlier work by Valloni, who related the group Br ⁡ ( X ) / Br 1 ⁡ ( X ) \Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X) to the arithmetic structure of the CM field E E . It is from this arithmetic structure that we deduce our bounds.



1.
Introduction

In the rational points community, K3 surfaces are one of the most studied types of variety. They are simultaneously nice enough to work with and nasty enough that there are plenty of open problems. One such open problem is the following conjecture by Skorobogatov, which can be found in

In this paper, we analyse Valloni’s algorithm to deduce an explicit bound on its output. Let

M_{E}=\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}]h_{F}}{h_{E}}\cdot 2^{\omega(d_{E/F})+\omega(d_{E/F,2})}\cdot\sqrt{\Nm(d_{E/F,2})}.

Theorem 1.2

Let

In fact, we can do much better than Theorem
1.2
, at the expense of a more complicated statement. In order to state our sharper bound, we define functions
\Phi:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

Since
\Phi(n)

\Phi(0)=0

\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

\Phi^{-1}(t)=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:\Phi(n)\leq t\}.

The notation
\Phi^{-1}
comes from the fact that
\Phi^{-1}\circ\Phi=\id_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X)

Theorem 1.3

Let
k

E

X/k

\mathcal{O}_{E}

\#\big(\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X)\big)\leq[k:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\cdot M_{E}^{2}\cdot\Psi\Big(\Phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{2\log([k:\mathbb{Q}]M_{E})}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big)\Big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot\Nm(\rad(d_{E/F})).

The functions

Theorem
1.3

Using his algorithm in

However, our bound is explicit and much easier to compute, so it adds value, particularly when computational efficiency is important.

Corollary 1.6

Let

\#\big(\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X)\big)\leq\big((1+\delta)\Psi(L_{\delta})\big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot\Nm(\rad(d_{E/F}))\cdot([k:\mathbb{Q}]M_{E})^{2(1+\log_{2}(1+\delta))}.

Theorem 1.7

Let

\delta\to 0

2.1.
Notation for number fields

We collect some standard notation, which is used throughout the paper. Given a number field
M
, write
\mathcal{O}_{M}
for its ring of integers and
h_{M}
for its class number. For an ideal
I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{M}
, write
\omega(I)
for the number of distinct prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
dividing
I
, and
\Nm(I)
for its norm
\#(\mathcal{O}_{M}/I)
. Moreover, write
I_{2}
for the
2
-part of
I
, which is defined to be

I_{2}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid 2}\mathfrak{p}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)},

where the product is over prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
containing
2
. Write
\phi_{M}
for the Euler totient function on ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
, which is defined by

\phi_{M}(I)=\#(\mathcal{O}_{M}/I)^{\times}

for each ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
.
Given a CM field (i.e. a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real number field)
E
, we will always write
F
for its maximal totally real subfield, leaving
E
implicit in the notation. Moreover, we write
d_{E/F}
for the discriminant of the extension, which is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Given a CM field
E
and an element
x\in E
, we write
\overline{x}
for the conjugate of
x
over
F
. Similarly, for an ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
, we write
\overline{I}
for the conjugate ideal
\{\overline{x}:x\in I\}
. Given an implicit choice of CM field
E
, write
G
for the Galois group
\Gal(E/F)
.

Again when
E
is a CM field, for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, write
e(\mathfrak{p})
for the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}
in
E
. For an archimedean place
v
of
F
, define
e(v)
to be
2
if
v
can be extended to a place
w
of
E
with
[E_{w}:F_{v}]=2
(so that the extension is
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}
), and set
e(v)=1
otherwise. When
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
lying over
2
, write
e(\mathfrak{p}\mid 2)
for the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}
over the rational prime
2
.

2.2.
Hodge structures

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be one of
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
V
be a free
R
-module of finite rank. Write
V_{\mathbb{C}}
for the base change
V\otimes_{R}\mathbb{C}
. A
Hodge structure on
V
of weight
n
is a direct sum decomposition

V_{\mathbb{C}}=V^{n,0}\oplus V^{n-1,1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V^{0,n}

of vector spaces, such that the complex conjugation map
\iota:V_{\mathbb{C}}\to V_{\mathbb{C}}
has
\iota(V^{p,q})=V^{q,p}
for each
(p,q)
. Let
V
and
W
be free
R
-modules with Hodge structures of weight
n
. A
morphism of Hodge structures

V\to W
is an
R
-module homomorphism
\varphi:V\to W
such that the natural base change
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}:V_{\mathbb{C}}\to W_{\mathbb{C}}
has

\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{p,q})\subseteq W^{p,q}

for each
(p,q)
.

The best-known example of Hodge structures comes from complex algebraic geometry. The following result can be found in
[
Huy16
, Page 38]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over
\mathbb{C}
, and write
\Omega_{X}
for its cotangent sheaf. For each positive integer
r
, there is a canonical Hodge structure of weight
r
on
H^{r}(X,\mathbb{Z})
(respectively
H^{r}(X,\mathbb{Q})
), given by

H^{r}(X,\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=r}H^{p,q},

where
H^{p,q}
is the space

H^{p,q}=H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}).

There is a particular real algebraic group, called the
Deligne torus
and denoted by
\mathbb{S}
, such that Hodge structures are equivalent to representations of
\mathbb{S}
. In other words, for any rational vector space
V
, there is a natural bijection

\{\text{Hodge structures on $V$}\}\longleftrightarrow\Hom(\mathbb{S},\GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})),

where the right-hand side denotes morphisms of real algebraic groups. Given a rational Hodge structure
V
, we thus obtain a homomorphism

\rho:\mathbb{S}\to\GL(V_{\mathbb{R}})

of real algebraic groups. Taking real points, we get a group homomorphism

\rho_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R})\to\GL(V)(\mathbb{R}).

We define the
Mumford–Tate group

\MT(V)
of
V
to be the the smallest algebraic subgroup of
\GL(V)
(defined over
\mathbb{Q}
) such that

\im(\rho_{\mathbb{R}})\subseteq\MT(V)(\mathbb{R}).

2.3.
K3 surfaces and complex multiplication

Let
k
be a field and let
X
be a variety over
k
. Write
\omega_{X}
for the canonical sheaf of
X
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
for the sheaf of regular functions on
X
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
k
be a field. A
K3 surface over
k
is a complete nonsingular surface
X
over
k
, such that
\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over
\mathbb{C}
. Then there is a short exact sequence of sheaves, called the
exponential exact sequence
, given by

0\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\overset{\exp}{\to}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\to 1.

The associated long exact sequence of cohomology gives an exact sequence

(2)

0\to\Pic(X)\to H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{C}\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})\to H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Since
\Pic(X)
and
\mathbb{C}
are torsion-free, it follows that
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is also torsion-free. From basic properties of integral cohomology, one can obtain
H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Z})=H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})=0
.

There is a natural
intersection pairing

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\times H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z},

which makes
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
into a
lattice
, by which we mean a free abelian group equipped with a nondegenerate bilinear form. For the formal definition and properties of lattices, see
[
Huy16
, Chapter 14]
. The following result, which is
[
Huy16
, Proposition 3.5]
, says that all K3 surfaces give rise to the same lattice:

Theorem 2.4
.

There is a distinguished lattice
\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}
, called the
K3 lattice
, such that for any K3 surface
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, there is a lattice isomorphism

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}.

Theorem
2.2
gives a natural Hodge structure

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{0,2}\oplus H^{1,1}\oplus H^{2,0}

on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.
For any K3 surface
X
, we always have (see
[
Huy16
, Page 8]
)

\dim H^{p,q}=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if $(p,q)=(2,0)$ or $(0,2)$},\\
20\quad\text{if $(p,q)=(1,1)$},\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Recall from Equation (
2
) that there is a natural embedding

\Pic(X)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

The image of this embedding is a sublattice of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, which we call the
Néron-Severi lattice
of
X
, denoted
\NS(X)
. We define the
transcendental lattice

T(X)
of
X
to be the orthogonal complement

T(X)=\NS(X)^{\perp},

with respect to the intersection pairing on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. The sublattice
T(X)\subseteq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is well-behaved with respect to the Hodge structure on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, so it inherits its own Hodge structure. The rational Hodge structure
T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
has a well-defined ring
\End_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})
of Hodge endomorphisms. Denote this ring by
E(X)
.

Theorem 2.5

(Zarhin)
.

If the Mumford–Tate group
\MT(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})
is abelian, then
E(X)
is isomorphic to a CM field, and the
E(X)
-vector space
T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
has

\dim_{E(X)}T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=1.

Proof.

This is stated in
[
Val21
, Page 12]
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

In light of Theorem
2.5
, we say that a K3 surface
X
over a number field has

if the Mumford–Tate group
\MT(T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}})
is abelian. In that case, we say that
X
has complex multiplication
by
E
, where
E

E(X)
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface with complex multiplication. Then the ring
\End_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T(X))
is an order in
E(X)
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{O}(X)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
E
be a CM field. A K3 surface
X
has
principal complex multiplication by

\mathcal{O}(X)
is a maximal order in

2.4.
K3 class groups

Let
K
be a number field and write
\mathbb{A}_{K,f}^{\times}
for its finite ideles. Given an ideal
I
of
K
, define

U_{K,I}=\{s\in\mathbb{A}_{K,f}^{\times}:v(s)=0\text{ and }v(s-1)\geq v(I)\text{ for all finite places $v$}\}.

The ray class group of
K
modulo
I
is isomorphic to the double quotient

K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K,f}^{\times}/U_{K,I}.

The
K3 class group
is a sort of “CM-analogue” of this situation. We basically replace the number field
K
with a CM field
E
, and replace “units of
K
” with “units of
E
with norm
1
over the totally real subfield”. Given a CM field
E
, define

U(E)=\{e\in E^{\times}:e\bar{e}=1\},\quad U(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})=\{s\in\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times}:s\bar{s}=1\},

and, for any ideal
I
of
E
,

\widetilde{U}_{E,I}=\{s\in U(\mathbb{A}_{E,f}):v(s)=0\text{ and }v(s-1)\geq v(I)\text{ for all finite places $v$ of $E$}\}.

Then the
K3 class group of
E
modulo
I
is defined in
[
Val21
, Definition 9.5]
to be the double quotient

G_{\mathrm{K3},I}(E)=U(E)\backslash U(\mathbb{A}_{E,f})/\widetilde{U}_{E,I}.

Valloni then defines the
K3 class field of
E
modulo
I
to be the abelian extension
F_{K3,I}(E)/E
associated to the surjection

\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times}\to G_{\mathrm{K3},I}(E),\quad s\mapsto\frac{s}{\overline{s}}.

(Valloni)

Let

This is

Definition 2.11
.

Let

be a number field and let

Corollary 2.12

(Essentially due to Valloni)
.

Let
k
be a number field and let
E
be a CM field. For any K3 surface defined over
k
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{E}
, there is a
k
-permissible ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
such that

\#\big(\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X)\big)\leq\Nm(I).

Proof.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over
k
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Let
K=kE
. Then Theorem
2.8
tells us that there is an ideal

of
\mathcal{O}_{E}

and

3.
Making the totient bound explicit

Throughout this section,
k
is a number field,
E
is a CM field with maximal totally real subfield
F
, and
I
is a
k
-permissible ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Write
J=I\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}
. The bound on
\frac{\phi_{E}(I)}{\phi_{F}(J)}
in Definition
2.11
is quite inexplicit. In the current section, we obtain a weaker, but explicit bound. In Section
4
, we will use this explicit bound to deduce bounds on

, hence on the transcendental Brauer group.

Lemma 3.1

(Bounding the ratio of subgroup indices)
.

There exists a positive integer
m
such that

\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}]}{2^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1}}=m\cdot\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}]}{[\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}:N_{E/F}(\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I})]}.

Proof.

By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, there is a free abelian group
A
of rank
[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
such that
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}\times A
. Also by Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the group
A
has finite index as a subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}
. Define

A^{I}=A\cap E^{I,1}.

Since
E^{I,1}
and
A
are both subgroups of
E^{\times}
, their intersection
A^{I}
is too. Moreover, we have a well-defined, injective map

A/A^{I}\to(\mathcal{O}_{E}/I)^{\times},\quad[x]\mapsto[x],

so
[A:A^{I}]\leq\phi_{E}(I)
, and therefore
A^{I}
is free of rank
[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
. Since
N_{E/F}
restricts to the squaring map on
F^{\times}
, we have

[A^{I}:N_{E/F}A^{I}]=2^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1},

and
N_{E/F}A^{I}
is a free abelian group of rank
[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
. Thus, all indices in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{E/F}\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{E/F}A^{I}}

are finite, and we have

\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}]}{[\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}:N_{E/F}\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}]}=\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}]}{[A^{I}:N_{E/F}A^{I}]}\cdot\frac{[N_{E/F}\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}:N_{E/F}A^{I}]}{[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}:A^{I}]}.

The map
N_{E/F}
descends to a well-defined group epimorphism

\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}/A^{I}\to N_{E/F}(\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I})/N_{E/F}(A^{I}),

so

[N_{E/F}\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}:N_{E/F}A^{I}]\mid[\mathcal{O}_{E}^{I}:A^{I}],

and the result follows since
[A^{I}:N_{E/F}A^{I}]=2^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Bounding ramification away from
J
)
.

We have

e(E/F,J)\leq 2^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]+\omega(d_{E/F})}.

Proof.

Since

is totally real, it has
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
archimedean places, all of which ramify since

is totally imaginary. It also has
\omega(d_{E/F})

. The product
e(E/F,J)
is at most the product of

at all of these places, which is equal to
2^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]+\omega(d_{E/F})}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
E

[(\mathcal{O}_{E}/I_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\times,G}:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/J_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\times}]=1.

By the Chinese remainder theorem, we have

It is easy to see that the fixed subgroup

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\mathfrak{p}

[(\mathcal{O}_{E}/I_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\times,G}:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/J_{\mathfrak{p}})^{\times}]=1.

Lemma 4.3

Let

Proof.

Let

for each

\Nm(P_{i})\geq p_{\lfloor\frac{i-1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\rfloor}

\prod_{P\in S}(\Nm(P)-1)\geq\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\rfloor-1}(p_{j}-1)\Big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}.

It follows that

\Big\lfloor\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big\rfloor-1\leq\Phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{\log T}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big).

Since

\frac{\Nm(P_{i})}{\Nm(P_{i})-1}\leq\frac{p_{\lfloor\frac{i-1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\rfloor}}{p_{\lfloor\frac{i-1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\rfloor}-1},

so

\prod_{P\in S}\frac{\Nm(P)}{\Nm(P)-1}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\rceil-1}\frac{p_{j}}{p_{j}-1}\Big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}=\Psi\Big(\Big\lceil\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big\rceil-2\Big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}.

The result follows since

\Big\lceil\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big\rceil-2\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{\#S}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big\rfloor-1\leq\Phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{\log T}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Corollary
2.12
, we have

\#\big(\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X)\big)\leq\Nm(I)

for some
k
-permissible ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Using multiplicativity of the totient, Corollary
3.11
, and Lemma
4.2
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\Nm(I)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\phi_{E}(I)}{\phi_{F}(J)}\Big)^{2}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid I\\
\mathrm{split}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Nm(P)}{\Nm(P)-1}\cdot\Nm(\rad(d_{E/F}))

\displaystyle\leq[k:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\cdot M_{E}^{2}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid I\\
\mathrm{split}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Nm(P)}{\Nm(P)-1}\cdot\Nm(\rad(d_{E/F})),

where the product is over all prime ideals
P
dividing
I
that are split in
E/F
(i.e. over all
P\mid I
with
\overline{P}\neq P
). Also using Corollary
3.11
and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid I\\
\mathrm{split}\end{subarray}}(\Nm(P)-1)\leq\Big(\frac{\phi_{E}(I)}{\phi_{F}(J)}\Big)^{2}\leq[k:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}\cdot M_{E}^{2},

so Lemma
4.3
implies that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid I\\
\mathrm{split}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Nm(P)}{\Nm(P)-1}\leq\Psi\Big(\Phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{2\log([k:\mathbb{Q}]M_{E})}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\Big)\Big)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]},

and the result follows from Corollary
2.12
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
n=\Phi^{-1}(t)
, so
n
is a nonnegative integer. We have
\log(p_{0}-1)=0
and
\log(p_{i}-1)\geq\log 2
for all
i\geq 1
, so

t\geq\Phi(n)\geq n\log 2,

so
n\leq\frac{t}{\log 2}
. If
n\leq L_{\delta}
, then obviously

\Psi(n)\leq\Psi(L_{\delta})\leq\Psi(L_{\delta})\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}.

If
n>L_{\delta}
, then

\displaystyle\Psi(n)

\displaystyle=\Psi(L_{\delta})\cdot\prod_{i=L_{\delta}+2}^{n+1}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\Psi(L_{\delta})\cdot(1+\delta)^{n-L_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\Psi(L_{\delta})\cdot(1+\delta)^{n+1},

so we are done.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We must find the smallest positive integer
L_{\delta}
such that

p_{L_{\delta}+2}\geq 1+\frac{1}{\delta}.

Using the well-known bounds

n(\log n+\log\log n-1)<p_{n-1}<n(\log n+\log\log n),

valid for
n\geq 6
,
we can quickly find an
L_{0}
such that
L_{0}\log L_{0}\sim\frac{1}{\delta}
and
p_{L_{0}+2}\geq 1+\frac{1}{\delta}
. Since
L_{0}\log L_{0}\sim\frac{1}{\delta}
, Lemma
4.4
tells us that we can compute
p_{i}
for every
i\leq L_{0}
with time complexity
O(L_{0}\log\log L_{0})
. Having computed
p_{i}
for each
i\leq L_{0}
, we can find the smallest
i
with
p_{i}\geq 1+\frac{1}{\delta}
by performing a binary search on
\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,L_{0}]
, which has time complexity
O(\log L_{0})
. Clearly

\log L_{0}\ll L_{0}\log\log L_{0}\ll L_{0}\log L_{0}\sim\frac{1}{\delta},

so we can determine
L_{\delta}
with the claimed time complexity. Given that the required
p_{i}
have already been computed, we can then compute
\Psi(L_{\delta})
with time complexity
O(L_{\delta})
, and we know that
L_{\delta}\ll\frac{1}{\delta}
, so we are done.
∎

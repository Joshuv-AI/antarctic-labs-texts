Robustness of Vacancy-Bound Non-Abelian Anyons in the Kitaev Model in a Magnetic Field
Bo Xiao, Gonzalo Alvarez, Gábor B. Halász

Abstract
The search for non-Abelian anyons in quantum spin liquids (QSLs) is crucial for advancing fault-tolerant topological quantum computation. In the exactly solvable Kitaev honeycomb model, nonmagnetic spin vacancies are known to bind emergent gauge fluxes of the QSL ground state, which in turn become non-Abelian anyons for an infinitesimal magnetic field. Here, we investigate how this approach for stabilizing non-Abelian anyons at spin vacancies extends to a finite magnetic field represented by a proper Zeeman term. Specifically, we use large-scale density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) simulations to compute the vacancy-anyon binding energy as a function of magnetic field for both the ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) Kitaev models. We find that, while the inclusion of the field weakens anyon binding in both cases, there is a pronounced difference in the finite-field behavior; the binding energy remains finite within the entire QSL phase for the FM Kitaev model but approaches zero already inside this phase for the AFM Kitaev model. To reliably compute a very small binding energy that is three orders of magnitude below the magnetic interaction strength, we also introduce a refined definition for the binding energy and an extrapolation scheme for its accurate extraction through exclusively ground-state properties using DMRG.

INTRODUCTION

Quantum spin liquids (QSLs) are magnetic insulators characterized by long-range quantum entanglement; they support fractionalized quasiparticle excitations that interact via emergent gauge fields and possess nontrivial “anyonic” particle statistics beyond conventional bosons and fermions
Balents 2010
;
Savary and Balents 2016
;
Zhou et al. 2017
;
Knolle and Moessner 2019
;
Broholm et al. 2020

For fully gapped non-Abelian QSLs, these anyonic quasiparticles define an internal Hilbert space in which orthogonal quantum states are locally indistinguishable and hence immune to decoherence resulting from local noise. Moreover, an exchange or braiding process among the anyonic quasiparticles implements a unitary rotation inside the internal Hilbert space that is rigidly determined by the topology of the given process and thus naturally protected from local errors. In other words, these non-Abelian anyons allow for intrinsically fault-tolerant storage and processing of quantum information, i.e., topological quantum computation
Kitaev 2003
;
Nayak et al. 2008

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

;

, which may affect the vacancy-anyon bound state.

In this work, we numerically investigate the robustness of anyon binding at spin vacancies in the Kitaev model under an external magnetic field that is represented by a proper Zeeman term rather than the effective three-spin interaction. To address this difficult problem beyond the exactly solvable manifold, we employ density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations. DMRG is a variational optimization method
White 1992
;
White 1993
that can be naturally formulated within the language of matrix product states (MPS)
Schollwöck 2011
;
Orús 2019
; the matrix dimensions, commonly known as bond dimensions, indicate the number of states kept in the truncated Hilbert space, and play a central role in the approximation. DMRG minimizes the variational energy in the low-entanglement Hilbert space; the accuracy of DMRG can be systematically improved by increasing the bond dimension. While DMRG was originally formulated in one dimension, it is also applicable to two-dimensional systems, provided that they are defined in a narrow cylindrical geometry.

The primary result of our work is the calculation of the vacancy-anyon binding energy as a function of magnetic field for both the ferromagnetic (FM) and antiferromagnetic (AFM) Kitaev models inside the non-Abelian QSL phase. In both cases, we observe that the magnitude of the binding energy decreases, i.e., anyon binding becomes weaker, upon including a proper magnetic field, which is in stark contrast to the corresponding behavior for the effective three-spin interaction
Kao et al. 2024b
. At the same time, we also find a marked difference between the FM and AFM Kitaev models; anyon binding remains robust in the entire non-Abelian QSL phase for the FM Kitaev model, whereas the binding energy approaches zero already inside this phase for the AFM Kitaev model. Importantly, the same results are consistently found for multiple cylinder widths and boundary conditions, which indicates that they are not connected to finite-size effects but extend all the way to the thermodynamic limit (TDL).

In order to reliably calculate the vacancy-anyon binding energy in DMRG, we also had to make at least two nontrivial advances in the methodology. First, since the binding energy is three orders of magnitude smaller than the fundamental energy scale (i.e., the Kitaev exchange), it was critical to regularize a dominant finite-size effect in the narrow cylindrical geometry by introducing a refined definition of the binding energy. Using this refined definition, the binding energy is obtained by comparing the energies of four rather than two distinct topological sectors. Even for the pure Kitaev model, where one can go beyond the narrow cylindrical geometry by exploiting the exact solution, our refined definition allowed us to determine the binding energy up to one more significant figure than in previous work
Willans et al. 2010
. Second, since DMRG is most adept at computing the energy of the ground-state topological sector, the energies of the four individual sectors were calculated through an extrapolation scheme where the energy of each sector is lowered below those of the other three by carefully tailored perturbations. Although our specific choice for the perturbations is specific to the Kitaev model, we believe that appropriate extensions of the general approach can be useful for extracting anyonic binding energies in other QSLs too.

II.2
Spin vacancies and flux binding

In the exactly solvable Kitaev model at zero magnetic field (
h=0
), isolated spin vacancies are known to bind
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fluxes of the QSL
Willans et al. 2010
;
Willans et al. 2011
. For an infinitesimal field
h>0
, each
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge flux becomes a non-Abelian Ising anyon and remains bound to its parent spin vacancy. In this work, our goal is to understand the stability of such a vacancy-flux bound state at finite magnetic fields by studying the energetics of flux binding at a single vacancy in the center of our cylinder (see Fig.
1
).

We start by defining the vacancy-flux binding energy for the pure Kitaev model at zero magnetic field. In this case, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fluxes readily correspond to conserved loop operators that are simple products of spin operators along closed loops of the lattice and can take eigenvalues
\pm 1
. Examples of such loop operators include the regular plaquette operators
W_{\mathcal{P}}
at hexagonal plaquettes
\mathcal{P}
and the extended plaquette operator
W_{\mathcal{O}}
at the enlarged plaquette
\mathcal{O}
surrounding the vacancy (see Fig.
1
). The Hilbert space decomposes into distinct flux sectors characterized by the
\pm 1
eigenvalues of all these loop operators, and each flux sector has its own lowest-energy state (“ground state”) that can be obtained through the exact solution of the Kitaev model
Kitaev 2006
. The corresponding ground-state energy can then be written as a function of the loop eigenvalues
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{O}}
, and appropriate excitation (or binding) energies can be defined as differences between ground-state energies of distinct flux sectors. In the TDL, all regular plaquettes
\mathcal{P}
have positive excitation energies, i.e.,
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{P}}=+1
eigenvalues are energetically favorable

. Restricting our attention to flux sectors with
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{P}}=+1
for all
\mathcal{P}
, the vacancy-flux binding energy is then simply the energy difference between the
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{O}}=-1
and
\overline{W}_{\mathcal{O}}=+1
ground states.

Being an unbiased numerical method, however, DMRG does not distinguish between these four topological sectors and only finds the overall ground-state energy, i.e., the lowest of the four individual ground-state energies
E\,(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{W}_{\mathrm{right}})

E\,(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{W}_{\mathrm{right}})

h>0
inside the non-Abelian QSL phase, the most natural perturbations are the topological loop operators
W_{X}
of the pure Kitaev model at
h=0

\pm 1
at

(7)

where the first term

is the unperturbed Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
), the second term with

H_{X}(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{W}_{\mathrm{right}})=\overline{W}_{\rm left}\sum_{X<X_{\mathcal{O}}}W_{X}+\overline{W}_{\rm right}\sum_{X>X_{\mathcal{O}}}W_{X}

(8)

(9)

E^{\lambda}(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{W}_{\mathrm{right}})
is expected to be a smooth function of

As shown in Fig.

h=0

Moreover, while there is significant scattering among the binding energies found for the various geometries, these binding energies all have the same order of magnitude and are between
-0.009
and
-0.003

E_{\rm binding}\approx-0.00676

Willans et al. 2010
and Sec.
II.2

In other words, any common trend observed in the binding energy for all four finite geometries is expected to remain valid even in the TDL.

At finite magnetic fields (
h>0
), the behavior of the binding energy is markedly different in the FM and AFM Kitaev models. For the FM Kitaev model [see Fig.
5

5

h_{C}^{\rm AFM}\approx 0.22

Gohlke et al. 2018

5

To elucidate the nature of flux binding at a finite magnetic field

IV
CONCLUSIONS

We employed DMRG to determine the binding energy between a non-Abelian Ising anyon and a nonmagnetic spin vacancy in both the FM and AFM Kitaev models under an external magnetic field. To reliably compute a small binding energy on the order of
10^{-3}
in the narrow cylindrical geometry required by DMRG, we introduced both a refined definition of the binding energy and an extrapolation scheme for its accurate extraction. The refined definition regularizes a dominant finite-size effect in the narrow cylindrical geometry and allowed us to compute the binding energy in the pure Kitaev model with much higher accuracy than in previous work
Willans et al. 2010
. The extrapolation scheme uses carefully tailored perturbations to determine the individual energies of distinct topological sectors that correspond to the presence and absence of anyon binding, respectively. In addition to enabling the computation of specific excited-state energies in DMRG, which are essential for obtaining the binding energy, these perturbations prevent DMRG from becoming trapped in local minima. To confirm the validity and robustness of our methodology, we also benchmarked our DMRG results against the exact solution at zero magnetic field for both the FM and AFM Kitaev models in all finite geometries considered. Moreover, by exploring a range of finite geometries with different cylinder widths and boundary conditions, we were able to quantify finite-size effects and argue that our results apply even in the TDL.

The magnetic-field dependence of the vacancy-anyon binding energy is found to exhibit a striking contrast between the FM and AFM Kitaev models. For the FM case, the binding energy decreases in magnitude but remains clearly negative until the system transitions out of the non-Abelian QSL phase at the critical field estimated in Ref.
Gohlke et al. 2018
. In other words, the vacancy-anyon bound state survives, although becoming slightly less stable, at a finite magnetic field. For the AFM case, in contrast, the binding energy approaches zero already inside the non-Abelian QSL phase, which indicates that anyons are no longer bound to spin vacancies. To gain deeper insight into the effects of spin vacancies in real QSL candidate materials like
\alpha
-RuCl
3
, more realistic spin exchange interactions, such as Heisenberg and Gamma terms
Rau et al. 2014
, should be incorporated into the model Hamiltonian. Our results highlight the robustness of vacancy-bound non-Abelian anyons, thus paving the way toward qubit architectures based on QSL materials.

\lambda=0

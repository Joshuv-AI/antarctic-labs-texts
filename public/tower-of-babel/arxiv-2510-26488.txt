Hilbert squares of genus 16 K3 surfaces
Junyu MENG

Abstract
We consider the geometry of a general polarized K3 surface ( S , h ) (S,h) of genus 16 and its Fourier-Mukai partner ( S ′ , h ′ ) (S^{\prime},h^{\prime}) . We prove that S [ 2 ] S^{[2]} is isomorphic to the moduli space M S ′ ​ ( 2 , h ′ , 7 ) M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7) of stable sheaves with Mukai vector ( 2 , h ′ , 7 ) (2,h^{\prime},7) and describe the embeddings of the projectivization of the stable vector bundle of Mukai vector ( 2 , − h ′ , 8 ) (2,-h^{\prime},8) over S ′ S^{\prime} into these two isomorphic hyper-Kähler fourfolds. Following the work of Frédéric Han [ Han25 ] , we explicitly construct an interesting 3-form t 1 ∈ ∧ 3 V 10 ∗ t_{1}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*} which potentially gives an isomorphism between S [ 2 ] S^{[2]} and the Debarre-Voisin fourfold in G ⁡ ( 6 , V 10 ) G(6,V_{10}) associated to t 1 ∈ ∧ 3 V 10 ∗ t_{1}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*} . This would provide a geometric explanation of the existence of such an isomorphism, which was proved in [ Obe22 ] by a completely different argument.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Deformations of polarized Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces are important series of examples of polarized hyper-Kähler manifolds. A natural question is to give descriptions of (general) elements in these deformation families. The Debarre-Voisin construction is a nice answer to this question in a specific case. More explicitly, let
V_{10}
be a 10-dimensional vector space, and let
t\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*}
be a nonzero 3-form (which is usually called a trivector), then the Debarre-Voisin 4-fold
DV(t)
is defined as a subscheme of
G(6,V_{10})
as follows:

DV(t)=\{[V_{6}]\in G(6,V_{10})|\ \mathrm{the\ restricted}\ \text{3-}\mathrm{form}\ t|_{V_{6}}\ \mathrm{is\ zero}\}.

When
DV(t)
is smooth, it is a hyper-Kähler manifold of K3
[2]
-type by
[
DV10
]
. The restriction of the Plücker polarization to
DV(t)
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{DV(t)}(1)
, which has Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki square 22 and divisibility 2. The nice thing is that these
(DV(t),\mathcal{O}_{DV(t)}(1))
give a locally complete family of smooth polarized hyper-Kähler manifolds of K3
[2]
-type.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
be the projective GIT quotient
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*})//SL(V_{10})
and let
\mathscr{F}
be the period domain for polarized hyper-Kähler manifolds deformation equivalent to
(DV(t),\mathcal{O}_{DV(t)}(1))
. Both varieties are 20-dimensional. We can summarize the previous facts by saying that there exists a dominant rational map from
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
to
\mathscr{F}
. Actually this map is birational as shown in
[
O’G22
]
.

A further question is to compare
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
with the Baily-Borel compactification
\overline{\mathscr{F}}
of
\mathscr{F}
. Both
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
and
\overline{\mathscr{F}}
are normal projective varieties birational to each other and we would begin by studying divisors (valuations on the rational function field) on them. A divisor on
\overline{\mathscr{F}}
is called an HLS divisor (named after Hassett, Looijenga and Shah) if it is not a divisor on
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
(i.e. if there does not exist a divisor on
\mathscr{M}_{DV}
which induces the same valuation on the rational function field). It was proved in
[
DHOV20
]
that the Heegner divisors
\mathscr{D}_{2},\mathscr{D}_{6},\mathscr{D}_{10},\mathscr{D}_{18}
are HLS divisors; for this they used projective models of polarized K3 surfaces of genus 2,4,6,10 constructed by Mukai. However, it was left open in
[
DHOV20
]
whether the Heegner divisor
\mathscr{D}_{30}
is an HLS divisor or not.

The Heegner divisor
\mathscr{D}_{30}
over
\overline{\mathscr{F}}
is actually the closure of a divisor on the open subset
\mathscr{M}\subset\overline{\mathscr{F}}
parameterizing hyper-Kähler fourfolds of K3
[2]
-type with a polarization of Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki square 22 and divisibility 2 by
[
DHOV20
, Theorem 3.1]
. More precisely, a general element of
\mathscr{D}_{30}
is given as follows: consider a general polarized K3 surface
(S,h)
of genus 16, and let
S^{[2]}
be the Hilbert scheme of two points on
S
, then the line bundle
2h-7\delta
is an ample polarization of square 22 and divisibility 2 (where
h
is the divisor over
S^{[2]}
induced by the polarization
h
on
S
and
2\delta
is the class of the exceptional divisor of the Hilbert-Chow morphism
S^{[2]}\rightarrow S^{(2)}
), which makes the polarized pair
(S^{[2]},2h-7\delta)
an element of
\mathscr{D}_{30}\cap\mathscr{M}\subset\overline{\mathscr{F}}
.

Mukai also provided projective models for general polarized K3 surfaces of genus 16 in
[
Muk16
]
. However, the case of
\mathscr{D}_{30}
remained open because of the lack of a deep understanding of the geometry of genus 16 polarized K3 surfaces and the complexity of the projective models in
[
Muk16
]
. Nevertheless, using Gromov-Witten techniques, Georg Oberdieck proved in
[
Obe22
]
that
\mathscr{D}_{30}
is NOT an HLS divisor. This implies that
S^{[2]}
arises as
DV(t)
for some trivector
t\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*}
, where
(S,h)
is a general polarized K3 surface of genus 16. Although the existence of the trivector is guaranteed, it is not clear how to geometrically construct
t\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*}
.

The attempt to understand the geometry and to construct the trivector was initiated in
[
DHOV20
]
and
[
Han25
]
. Mukai’s projective model provides a rank 2 stable vector bundle
F
with Mukai vector
(2,h,8)
over a general genus 16 polarized K3 surface
(S,h)
. A rank 10 vector space
V_{10}
and a rank 6 subbundle of the trivial bundle
V_{10}
were constructed in
[
DHOV20
]
, which is expected to correspond to the rank 6 universal subbundle over
G(6,V_{10})
. Frédéric Han constructed in
[
Han25
]
a trivector
t_{2}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}
together with a rank 6 subbundle of the trivial bundle
V_{10}^{*}
over
X=\mathbb{P}_{S}(F^{*})
, such that fiberwise the restriction of the 3-form
t_{2}
to the rank 6 subspace is zero, inducing a map from
X
to
DV(t_{2})\subset G(6,V_{10}^{*})
. It was conjectured that there should exist a canonical isomorphism
V_{10}\cong V_{10}^{*}
(with the hope to get the trivector
t\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*}
corresponding to
t_{2}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}
) and that
X
can be embedded in
S^{[2]}
.

The present article begins by proving that
X
is naturally embedded into
S^{\prime[2]}
, where
S^{\prime}
is the unique nontrivial Fourier-Mukai partner of
S
, and that there is a natural isomorphism
V_{10}^{*}\cong V_{10}^{\prime}
, where
V_{10}^{\prime}
is the rank 10 vector space constructed in
[
DHOV20
]
for
S^{\prime}
. Correspondingly we have a trivector
t_{1}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{*}
corresponding to
t_{2}^{\prime}\in\wedge^{3}V_{10}^{\prime}
, where
t_{2}^{\prime}
is obtained by applying the construction in
[
Han25
]
to
S^{\prime}
. (Since
S^{\prime}
is the unique nontrivial Fourier-Mukai partner of
S
, the roles of
S
and
S^{\prime}
are often interchanged and we sometimes use the parallel construction by adding or deleting upperscript
′
without explanation).
Thus we can state what we believe to be the appropriate conjecture: (it has been verified to be true over some examples by computer with the aide of Frédéric Han)

Conjecture 1.1
.

There exists a canonical isomorphism
S^{[2]}\cong DV(t_{1})\subset G(6,V_{10})
.

1.2
Statement of the result

Let
(S,h)
be a general polarized K3 surface of genus 16. The main point of this article is to consider the “dual" K3 surface
S^{\prime}
, which is the unique nontrivial Fourier-Mukai partner of
S
, and the related geometry. The "dual" K3 surface
S^{\prime}
is in fact isomorphic to the moduli space of stable sheaves over
S
with Mukai vector
(3,h,5)
.

There are two interesting spherical vector bundles over
S
:
\mathcal{O}_{S}
and the rank 2 bundle
F
with Mukai vector
(2,h,8)
which arises in Mukai’s projective model in
[
Muk16
]
. Using the Fourier-Mukai transform
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:D^{b}(S)\rightarrow D^{b}(S^{\prime})
associated to the normalized universal sheaf
\mathcal{E}
over
S\times S^{\prime}
, we can construct two more spherical vector bundles
G_{4}^{\prime},E_{8}^{\prime}
over
S^{\prime}
with Mukai vectors
(4,h^{\prime},4)
and
(8,3h^{\prime},17)
respectively:

\phi_{\mathcal{E}}(F)=G_{4}^{\prime}[0],\ \ \ \ \phi_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=E_{8}^{\prime}[-2].

The rank 4 vector bundle
G_{4}
over
S
recovers the construction of the rank 4 vector bundle (under the same name
G_{4}
) in
[
Han25
]
and this new way to define it makes explicit its relation with the rank 4 vector space (generated by the 4 arrows in the 4-Kronecker quiver) arising in Mukai’s projective models.

As already mentioned, we have an embedding of
X^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}_{S^{\prime}}(F^{\prime*})
into
S^{[2]}
, together with an embedding of
X^{\prime}
into the moduli space
M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
of stable sheaves of Mukai vector
(2,h^{\prime},7)
over
S^{\prime}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist natural embeddings of
X^{\prime}
into both
S^{[2]}
and
M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
.

•

The image of
X^{\prime}
in
M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
is equal to the Brill-Noether locus parametrizing those
[K^{\prime}]\in M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
such that
\mathrm{Hom}(K^{\prime},F^{\prime})\neq 0
.

•

The image of
X^{\prime}
in
S^{[2]}
is equal to the degeneracy locus of the morphism
H^{0}(S,G_{4})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S^{[2]}}=\mathbb{C}^{8}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S^{[2]}}\rightarrow G_{4}^{[2]}
over
S^{[2]}
, where
G_{4}^{[2]}
is the tautological transform of the vector bundle
G_{4}
.

Moreover, the embedding of
X^{\prime}
into
S^{[2]}
induces an identification of the restriction of the rank 6 subbundle of
V_{10}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S^{[2]}}
to
X^{\prime}
with the rank 6 subbundle of
V_{10}^{\prime*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
, which are constructed in
[
DHOV20
, Section 8]
and
[
Han25
]
respectively.

In fact, the two hyper-Kähler fourfolds
S^{[2]}
and
M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
are isomorphic (and the isomorphism is compatible with the embeddings of
X^{\prime}
above):

Theorem 1.3
.

There is a natural isomorphism between
S^{[2]}
and
M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
given as follows:
Every stable sheaf
K^{\prime}
over
S^{\prime}
with Mukai vector
(2,h^{\prime},7)
is a quotient sheaf of
E_{8}^{\prime}
and fits into a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\phi_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})\rightarrow E_{8}^{\prime}\rightarrow K^{\prime}\rightarrow 0.

(1)

where
Z
is a length 2 subscheme of
S
and
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:D^{b}(S)\rightarrow D^{b}(S^{\prime})
is the Fourier-Mukai transform associated to the normalized universal sheaf over
S\times S^{\prime}
. The assignment of
Z
to
[K^{\prime}]\in M_{S^{\prime}}(2,h^{\prime},7)
gives the desired isomorphism.

Remark 1.4
.

In parallel to the fact that the structure sheaf of every length two zero-dimensional subscheme of
S
is a quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, here the situation is that every stable sheaf
K^{\prime}
over
S^{\prime}
with Mukai vector
(2,h^{\prime},7)
is a quotient sheaf of
E_{8}^{\prime}
, where
E_{8}^{\prime}
is a rank 8 vector bundle over

obtained by applying the Fourier-Mukai transform to

\phi_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=E_{8}^{\prime}[-2]
.

Remark 1.5
.

The existence of an isomorphism between

and

has been proven in
[
vGK23

All these results help us understand better the projective models constructed by Mukai in
[

Genus 16 Polarized K3 surfaces

We gather in this section some known information about general genus 16 polarized K3 surfaces
(S,h)
.

By the work of Mukai
[
Muk16
]
, a general
(S,h)
can be obtained as a locally complete intersection (with respect to vector bundles) in a 12-dimensional quiver moduli space
\mathcal{T}
, defined as the moduli space of stable representations of dimension vector
(3,2)
for the 4-Kronecker quiver (with respect to some stability parameter). Then
\mathcal{T}
is a smooth projective Fano variety of Picard rank 1 and index 4. It comes with a (“normalized") universal family of representations
\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes V
, where
\mathscr{E},\mathcal{F}
are globally generated vector bundles of rank 3,2 respectively with
c_{1}(\mathscr{E})=c_{1}(\mathcal{F})=:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{T}}(1)
equal to the ample generator of
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{T})
, and
V^{*}
is the 4-dimensional vector space generated by the four arrows in the 4-Kronecker quiver. We consider a general global section of
\mathscr{E}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\oplus 2}
, whose zero locus was proved in
[
Muk16
]
to be a K3 surface
S
. The genus of the polarized pair
(S,h:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{T}}(1)|_{S})
is 16. Moreover, a general genus 16 polarized K3 surface can be obtained in this way.

Naturally, we have two interesting bundles over
S
, namely
E:=\mathscr{E}|_{S},\ F:=\mathcal{F}|_{S}
with Mukai vector
(3,h,5)
and
(2,h,8)
respectively. These vector bundles are all slope stable, hence
F
is the unique stable vector bundle over
S
with given Mukai vector
(2,h,8)
. However,
E
is not unique and the moduli space
S^{\prime}
of stable sheaves of Mukai vector
(3,h,5)
is the unique nontrivial Fourier-Mukai partner of
S
(which will be studied in the next section). In view of
Lemma

3.2
and
Proposition

6.4
, for any
[E]\in S^{\prime}
, the natural map
E\rightarrow F\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)^{*}
is also a stable family of representations for the 4-Kronecker quiver and will induce a morphism from
S
to
\mathcal{T}
by the universal property of
\mathcal{T}
(after fixing an isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)^{*}\cong V
). When
[E]\in S^{\prime}
varies, the four dimensional space
\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)
will also vary and they patch together to give a rank 4 vector bundle
G_{4}^{\prime}
over
S^{\prime}
, which is a slope stable spherical bundle (
Proposition

3.6
).

The possible Debarre-Voisin construction was conducted in
[
DHOV20
]
as follows. Let
S^{[2]}
be the Hilbert scheme of two points on
S
. Let
q_{1}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\Delta}(S\times S)\rightarrow S^{[2]}
be the 2:1 quotient map and let
q_{2}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\Delta}(S\times S)\rightarrow S\times S
be the blow up morphism. The tautological transform of any vector bundle over

, for example

, is defined to be
q_{1*}(q_{2}^{*}F)=:F^{[2]}
. Then there exists a natural surjective bundle map
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(F^{[2]})\rightarrow(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}F)^{[2]}
, whose kernel is a rank 4 vector bundle
Q_{4}
, with
c_{1}(Q_{4})=2h-7\delta
(where
2\delta
is the class of the exceptional divisor of the Hilbert-Chow morphism
S^{[2]}\rightarrow S^{(2)}
).

0\rightarrow Q_{4}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(F^{[2]})\rightarrow(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}F)^{[2]}\rightarrow 0.

Using the above short exact sequence,
H^{0}(S^{[2]},Q_{4})
is equal to the kernel of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(H^{0}(S,F))\rightarrow H^{0}(S,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}F)
.

Let
X
denote the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}_{S}(F^{*})
, let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-H)
be the relative tautological subbundle and let
W_{10}
denote
H^{0}(S,F)=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))
. It is shown in
[

]
that

is very ample and induces an embedding of

into
\mathbb{P}(W_{10}^{*})
. Moreover,

is globally generated by

, which is 10-dimensional and denoted by

. In geometric terms,

is a family of quadrics in

which cut out

in
\mathbb{P}(W_{10}^{*})
(
[
Han25
, Theorem 2.3.2]
).

Viewing elements of

as quadrics over
\mathbb{P}(W_{10}^{*})
, we can consider the linear syzygies between them:
\mathrm{Ker}(V_{10}\otimes W_{10}\rightarrow S^{3}W_{10})
, which is actually an 8-dimensional vector space that we denote by
V_{8}
, together with the canonical map
V_{8}\rightarrow V_{10}\otimes W_{10}
. It turns out that

is the space of global sections of a rank 4 vector bundle

constructed in
[

(which corresponds to the

is involved in the Debarre-Voisin construction. There is a short exact sequence over

:

0\rightarrow N_{X}(-2H)\rightarrow V_{10}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow G_{4}^{*}(H)\rightarrow 0,

(2)

where

(we will give another explanation in (

A trivector

was constructed such that the restriction of the 3-form to every fiber of

is zero. First one begins with a unique element

3
Fourier-Mukai Partner
S^{\prime}

Let
(S,h)
a general polarized K3 surface of genus 16 with
\mathrm{Pic}(S)=\mathbb{Z}h
. Consider the Mukai vector
v_{0}=(2,h,8)
, with
v_{0}^{2}=-2
. By
[
Yos00
, Theorem 0.1]
,
M_{S}(v_{0})
consists of one single point, which we denote by
[F]
. The usual argument about comparing
F
with
F^{**}
shows that
F
is locally free. By construction,
F
is Gieseker stable and thus slope semistable. Since
\mathrm{Pic}(S)=\mathbb{Z}
, it follows immediately that
F
is slope stable.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a general genus 16 polarized K3 surface
(S,h)
, we have
H^{1}(S,F)=H^{2}(S,F)=0
and
F
is globally generated by
W_{10}:=H^{0}(S,F)
.

Proof.

Notice that
\chi(S,F)=10
. We only need to verify the statements for one single example of genus 16 polarized K3 surface, and this has been done in
[
Muk16
]
.

We give an alternative proof of the equality
H^{1}(S,F)=0
here for future convenience, because the same proof will hold for any locally free stable sheaf with Mukai vector
(2,h,7)
. We argue by contradiction.
If
H^{1}(S,F)\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O})^{*}\neq 0
, then there exists a nontrivial extension vector bundle
B
which fits into the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\rightarrow B\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0.

We claim that the vector bundle
B
is slope semistable (and thus slope stable). Otherwise there exists a slope semistable subsheaf
B^{\prime}\subset B
with
\mu(B^{\prime})>\mu(B)=\frac{1}{3}(h,h)
and hence
\mu(B^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}(h,h)
. We can moreover assume that
B^{\prime}
is reflexive. The composition map
B^{\prime}\hookrightarrow B\rightarrow F
cannot be zero and must be injective by the slope stability of
\mathcal{O}
and
F
. Moreover, the slope stability of
F
forces that
\mathrm{rank}(B^{\prime})=2
and
c_{1}(B^{\prime})=h
. Thus the two reflexive sheaves
B^{\prime},F
coincide outside finitely many points and they must be the same. This gives a splitting of the above short exact sequence and this contradiction shows that
B
is slope stable.

By calculation,
v(B)=(3,h,9)
. However the self-pairing of the Mukai vector of a slope stable sheaf should be at least
-2
. This leads to a contradiction and shows that
H^{1}(S,F)=0
.
∎

We also consider the Mukai vector
(3,h,5).
By
[
Yos09
, Lemma 1.2]
, all the Gieseker semistable sheaves
E
with Mukai vector
(3,h,5)
are actually slope stable vector bundles.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
E\in M_{S}(3,h,5)
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}(F,E)=\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(F,E)=0
and
\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)=\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

Proof.

Both
E
and
F
are slope stable bundles with
\mu(E)<\mu(F)
, thus we have
\mathrm{Hom}(F,E)=0
.
By
[
Yos99
, Lemma 2.1]
, any nonzero element in
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(F,E)
would induce an extension vector bundle
B
which was proved to be slope stable. In particular, we should have
v(B)^{2}\geq-2
. This is absurd since
v(B)=(5,2h,13)
. The last fact follows from Serre duality and Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formula.
∎

We let
S^{\prime}
denote the projective moduli space
M_{S}(3,h,5)
(which is also a K3 surface), and let
\mathcal{E}^{*}
denote the universal sheaf on
S\times S^{\prime}
. As mentioned above,

is actually a vector bundle and every fiber of it is slope stable. Using
\mathcal{E}
as the kernel for the Fourier-Mukai transform, we get an equivalence of derived categories
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:D^{b}(S)\cong D^{b}(S^{\prime})
together with a Hodge isometry between extended Mukai lattices
\tilde{H}^{*}(S,\mathbb{Z})\cong\tilde{H}^{*}(S^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
. In particular,
\mathrm{Pic}(S)\cong\mathrm{Pic}(S^{\prime})
, and we use
h^{\prime}
to denote the corresponding genus 16 polarization on
S^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

S
is not isomorphic to
S^{\prime}
. Moreover,
S
is equal to the moduli space of stable sheaves on
S^{\prime}
with Mukai vector
(3,h^{\prime},5)
.

Proof.

By
[
HLOY03
, Theorem 2.1]
, all possible Fourier-Mukai partners of a genus 16 polarized K3 surface
S
with
\mathrm{Pic}(S)=\mathbb{Z}.h
could only be
M_{S}(1,h,15)
and
M_{S}(3,h,5)
and these two are not isomorphic. The former one is actually isomorphic to
S

\dim(H^{0}(S^{\prime},E_{8}^{\prime}))=\chi(E_{8}^{\prime})=25
.

Proof.

Recall that
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=E_{8}^{\prime}[-2]
, which implies that

for any

. Similar vanishing of cohomology holds for any

. In view of the short exact sequence (

holds for one (hence general)

to be in the image of

and using the short exact sequence (

.
∎

We also have a refinement of

5.3
:

Corollary 5.8
.

H^{1}(S^{\prime},E_{8}^{\prime}(-h^{\prime}))=0
. Moreover,
H^{1}(S^{\prime},E^{\prime}(-h^{\prime}))=0
for any
[E^{\prime}]\in M_{S^{\prime}}(3,h^{\prime},5)
over a general genus 16 polarized K3 surface.

Proof.

We tensor the short exact sequence (

) with
\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}(-h^{\prime})
. By

5.3
, we can pick

Now we have shown that

∎

6
Back to Mukai’s model

Another interesting geometric aspect of Mukai’s model is that it exhibits (birationally) a general genus 16 polarized K3 surface
S
as a two dimensional family of twisted cubics in
\mathds{P}(V^{*})
.
We will give explanations of this aspect in this section. Moreover, we will also prove that every
[E]\in M_{S}(3,h,5)
(rather than only general ones) fits into Mukai’s model.

Recall from Section 2 that
\mathcal{T}
is the moduli space of stable representations of the 4-Kronecker quiver with dimension vector
(3,2)
. The variety
\mathcal{T}
is actually birational to the irreducible component of the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
containing twisted cubics (see
[
ESm95
]
). More explicitly, we consider the map
\mathscr{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}(1)
over
\mathcal{T}\times\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
. The degeneracy locus over a general slice
\{p\}\times\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
is a twisted cubic contained in
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
.

Pick a point
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}=M_{S}(3,h,5)
, which corresponds to a rank 3 vector bundle
E
over
S
. By definition, we know that
\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)=G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}}
, where we use
G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}}
to denote the fiber of
G_{4}^{\prime}
over
s^{\prime}
. We have
V^{*}:=G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}}
as in Mukai’s paper
[
Muk16
]
. When
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
is general, we know that the map
E\rightarrow F\otimes V
is a family of stable representations of the 4-Kronecker quiver with dimension vector
(3,2)
. Then by the universal property of the 12 dimensional Moduli space
\mathcal{T}
of stable representations of the 4-Kronecker quiver with dimension vector
(3,2)
, we have a map from
S
to
\mathcal{T}
which exhibits
S
as (birationally) parameterizing two dimensional family of twisted cubics in
\mathds{P}(V^{*})=\mathds{P}(G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}})
.

In this section, we will explain what is this family of twisted cubics in
\mathds{P}(G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}})=\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
. More precisely, we consider
\mathscr{C}_{S}\subset X\times\mathds{P}(\mathrm{Hom}(E,F))
, which is defined as follows:

\mathscr{C}_{S}=\{(s,f^{*})=x\in X,\ \psi\in\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hom}(E,F))|\ (\mathrm{rank}(\psi_{s})\leq 1)\ \mathrm{Im}(\psi_{s})\subset\mathrm{ker}(f^{*}:F_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{C})\}.

Obviously
\mathscr{C}_{S}
is birational to the 2-dimensional family of twisted cubics (when
[E]=s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
is general). Moreover,
(x,\psi)\in X\times\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hom}(E,F))
belongs to
\mathscr{C}_{S}
iff the map
\psi:E\rightarrow F
factors through the sheaf
K
corresponding to
x\in X\subset M_{S}(2,h,7)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
[E]\in M_{S}(3,h,5)
, the kernel of any nonzero map
E\rightarrow F
is equal to
\mathcal{O}_{S}
and this map degenerates at four points of
S
(counting multiplicity).

Proof.

By the slope stability of
E,F
, we know that a nonzero map must be generically surjective with
c_{1}(\mathrm{Im}(E\rightarrow F))=h
. The kernel is then a reflexive sheaf of rank one with trivial first Chern class, which shows that the kernel is exactly
\mathcal{O}_{S}
. A computation shows that the Mukai vector of the cokernel is equal to
(0,0,4)
.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset S\times X^{\prime}
is the degeneracy locus of the first nonzero map in (
6
). By
Lemma

4.4
, the intersection of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with any slice
S\times\{x^{\prime}\}
is the length two subscheme on
S
corresponding to the image of
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}\hookrightarrow S^{[2]}
, which shows that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is three dimensional.

Lemma 6.2
.

The fibers of the composition map
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow S\times X^{\prime}\rightarrow S
are always one-dimensional.

Proof.

Assume that the fiber over
s\in S
is two-dimensional. Because
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is given by the incidence condition that
s
should lie in the length 2 subscheme given by
x^{\prime}
for
(s,x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, and
X^{\prime}\rightarrow S^{[2]}
is an embedding, then
\{s,s_{1}\}\in S^{[2]}
lies in the exceptional divisor
X^{\prime}
for all
s_{1}\in S
. Consequently, the exceptional divisor
X^{\prime}
contains the subscheme
\mathrm{Bl}_{s}S\subset S^{[2]}
. Let us use
e
to denote the exceptional
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in the blow up
\mathrm{Bl}_{s}(S)
, then
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Bl}_{s}S)=\mathbb{Z}.h\oplus\mathbb{Z}.e
. Trivially, the divisor
h\in\mathrm{Pic}(S^{[2]})
restricts to
h\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Bl}_{s}S)
and the divisor
\delta\in\mathrm{Pic}(S^{[2]})
restricts to
e\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Bl}_{s}S)
.

On the other hand, the restriction of
\det(G_{4}^{[2]})=h-4\delta
to
X^{\prime}
is equal to
\omega_{X^{\prime}}=h^{\prime}-2H^{\prime}
by the adjunction formula, and the restriction of
\det(Q_{4}^{*})=-2h+7\delta
is equal to
\det(G_{4}^{\prime}(-H^{\prime}))=h^{\prime}-4H^{\prime}
by
Proposition

4.6
. Consequently, if
\mathrm{Bl}_{s}S\subset X^{\prime}
, then
2H^{\prime}
restricts to
3h-11e
. However,
3h-11e
is not divisible by 2 and this leads to a contradiction.
∎

By the above Lemma, the degeneracy locus of the restriction of
\mathcal{E}(-H^{\prime})\rightarrow G_{4}^{*}
to
\{s\}\times X^{\prime}
is always a curve. Thus we can apply the Eagon-Northcott construction over
\{s\}\times X^{\prime}
and dualize to get:

\begin{split}0\rightarrow E^{\prime}(-H^{\prime})\rightarrow G_{4,s}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C^{\prime}}(3H^{\prime}-h^{\prime})\rightarrow 0,\\
0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(h^{\prime}-3H^{\prime})\rightarrow G_{4,s}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow E^{\prime*}(H^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}xt^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(h^{\prime}-3H^{\prime}))\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

(11)

where
E^{\prime}
is given by
s\in S
and
C^{\prime}
is the intersection of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
\{s\}\times X^{\prime}
. The first sequence is exactly the sequence in the proof of
[
Han25
, Proposition 2.2.2]
.

Then we consider the composition map
\mathrm{Hom}(E,K)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(K,F)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)=V^{*}
, where
[F]\in M_{S}(2,h,7)
. Recall that in order for
\mathrm{Hom}(K,F)
to be nonzero, we need
[K]
to lie in
X\subset M_{S}(2,h,7)
by
Proposition

4.9
. Over the divisor

, the universal family
\mathscr{K}
is given by the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathscr{K}\rightarrow pr_{2}^{*}F\rightarrow pr_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(H)|_{\Delta_{S}}\rightarrow 0,

(12)

where
pr_{1}
and
pr_{2}
are the projections from
X\times S
to
X
and
S
respectively and
\Delta_{S}
denotes the inverse image of the diagonal via the map
X\times S\rightarrow S\times S
, and the latter map is the composition
pr_{2}^{*}F\rightarrow pr_{2}^{*}F|_{\Delta_{S}}=pr_{1}^{*}F|_{\Delta_{S}}\rightarrow pr_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(H)|_{\Delta_{S}}
.

We tensor the above sequence with
pr_{2}^{*}E^{*}
:

0\rightarrow\mathscr{K}\otimes pr_{2}^{*}E^{*}\rightarrow pr_{2}^{*}F\otimes pr_{2}^{*}E^{*}\rightarrow pr_{2}^{*}E^{*}|_{\Delta_{S}}\otimes pr_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(H)\rightarrow 0.

We consider the pushforward
pr_{1*}
of the above sequence and compare with (

This exact sequence shows that for a fixed

, the space

is one dimensional for a general

On the other hand, tensoring (

Therefore, the map
\mathrm{Hom}(E,K)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(K,F)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)=V^{*}

defined outside

Proposition 6.3
.

The rational map
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
is dominant and is generically 4-to-1.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.1
, any nonzero map

degenerates at 4 points. Let

By definition, the variety

is exactly the blow up of

along

Recall that
\mathcal{O}(h-3H)
is a subbundle of the trivial bundle

on

, inducing a map

. The rational map

with

.

Our next aim is to prove that every
[E]=s^{\prime}\in M_{S}(3,h,5)=S^{\prime}
(not only for general ones) fits into Mukai’s model. By the universal property of

, this amounts to proving that

(A)

The bundle map

(B)

The bundle map
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-H)\otimes G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow F^{*}\otimes G^{\prime}_{4,s^{\prime}}\rightarrow E^{*}

\mathcal{E}^{*}(-H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{*}\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow G_{4}^{\prime*}

. We pick
(s,f^{*})=x\in X
and restrict the Fourier-Mukai counterpart of (

to get the following exact sequence:

(Note that the fiber of

over

is equal to the fiber of

over

over

is at most 2 everywhere for the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
of any length two subscheme of

.

Proposition 6.4
.

For any
[E]\in M_{S}(3,h,5)
, there exists a morphism (unique when we fix an isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)^{*}\cong V
) from
S
to
\mathcal{T}
such that

over
\mathcal{T}
.

There is a natural map from

to

as follows: Taking the second wedge product of

Corollary 6.5
.

Every
[E]\in M_{S}(3,h,5)
is globally generated by

(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}G_{4}^{\prime})(-h^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{*}

The map

∎

Lemma 7.7
.

The map
V_{8}^{\prime}\otimes F^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},E_{8}^{*})\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow E_{8}^{*}
is surjective away form finitely many points.

Proof.

We pick two general points
[E_{1}],[E_{2}]\in S^{\prime}
and consider the corresponding short exact sequence as in (
9
):

0\rightarrow K^{*}\rightarrow E_{8}^{*}\rightarrow E_{1}^{*}\oplus E_{2}^{*}\rightarrow 0.

(15)

In our situation,
K
is locally free and
\mathrm{Ext}^{j}(K,F)=0
for
j=0,1,2
. Consequently, the composition
V_{8}^{\prime}\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow E_{8}^{*}\rightarrow E_{1}^{*}\oplus E_{2}^{*}
splits into the direct sum of two maps
F^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},E_{i}^{*})\rightarrow E_{i}^{*}
.
When
[E_{i}]\in S^{\prime}
is general, the map
F^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},E_{i}^{*})\rightarrow E_{i}^{*}
is surjective because this holds for the
[E_{i}]
appearing in
[
Muk16
]
by the stability condition for representations of the 4-Kronecker quiver. Consequently we have two short exact sequences:

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Ker}_{i}\rightarrow F^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},E_{i}^{*})\rightarrow E_{i}^{*}\rightarrow 0,

with
\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},\mathrm{Ker}_{i})=0
. The vector bundle
\mathrm{Ker}_{i}
must be slope stable. Otherwise, by the semistability of
F^{*\oplus 4}
, there would exist a slope semistable subbundle of
\mathrm{Ker}_{i}
with slope equal to
-\frac{1}{2}(h,h)
, which would lead to
\mathrm{Hom}(F^{*},\mathrm{Ker}_{i})\neq 0
.

The map
V_{8}^{\prime}\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow E_{8}^{*}
induces maps
\mathrm{Ker}_{i}\rightarrow K^{*}
for
i=1,2
. If both induced maps are zero, then (
15
) would split, which is absurd. Now we only have to observe that any nonzero map
\mathrm{Ker}_{i}\rightarrow K^{*}
is generically surjective by stability. Moreover, the first Chern class of the rank 2 image in
K^{*}
must be
-h
, which proves the Lemma.
∎

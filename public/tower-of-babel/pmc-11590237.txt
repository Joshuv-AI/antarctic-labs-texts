The value of volume measurement in CT in the follow-up of Stanford B aortic dissection after TEVAR
Authors: Ya Li, Gang Yuan, Ying Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the value of dual-source CT combined with volumetric measurement in the follow-up of Stanford Type B aortic dissection (TBAD) intrathoracic aortic repair (TEVAR).Methods40 TBAD patients in our hospital were treated with TEVAR and followed up at 3, 6 and 12 months after surgery. Dual-source CT and volumetric measurements were used to calculate the diameters and areas of the true and false lumen and the total lumen of the left subclavian artery at a distance of 2 cm (P1), the middle part of the descending aorta (P2), the opening of the abdominal trunk (P3) and the anterior bifurcation of the abdominal aorta (P4) before and after surgery. The maximum diameter, maximum area, volume and modified aortic remodeling index (MARI) of the true and false lumen of the distal abdominal aortic dissection were measured to evaluate the postoperative vascular remodeling.ResultsThe true cavity diameter of P1 and P2 increased gradually, the false cavity diameter decreased gradually (P < 0.05), and the total and true cavity diameter of P3 and P4 increased (P < 0.05). After operation, the true cavity area of P1 and P2 gradually increased, and the false cavity area decreased (P < 0.05). The total area of P3, P4, and true cavity increased gradually after operation (P < 0.05). There were statistically significant differences in true and false lumen volume and MARI before and after surgery (P < 0.05).ConclusionCompared with diameter measurement and area measurement, volume measurement can more accurately reflect the remodeling of the true and false lumen of the uncoated aortic dissection.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair (TEVAR) is the primary treatment for Stanford Type B Aortic Dissection (TBAD), offering the advantages of minimal trauma and fewer complications, effectively reducing mortality risk during surgery. However, postoperative complications such as abdominal aortic aneurysm-like expansion and endoleaks may still occur, primarily related to inadequate aortic remodeling after surgery. Hence, accurate assessment of aortic remodeling post-TEVAR is crucial for predicting and timely detecting related complications, guiding clinical decisions, and treatment strategies [1]. CT angiography is the most commonly used imaging modality for post-TEVAR follow-up, with dual-source CT offering advantages such as lower radiation dose and robust post-processing techniques, making it superior to conventional CT in vascular imaging [2]. Previously, measurements of the true and false lumen diameters or areas at the stent site were often used to evaluate the efficacy of TEVAR treatment, with limited research on the remodeling of vessels distal to the stent. In this study, we employed dual-source CTA and volume measurement techniques to quantitatively analyze the lumens at the stent site and distal to the stent post-TEVAR, aiming to accurately assess aortic remodeling in TBAD patients after TEVAR.

Methods

General information

A retrospective review was conducted on patients with Stanford Type B Aortic Dissection (TBAD) who underwent Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair (TEVAR) at the First People’s Hospital of Lianyungang from January 2020 to June 2023. The inclusion criteria were (1) diagnosis of TBAD confirmed by enhanced CT scan, (2) onset of symptoms within < 1 week, (3) clear evidence of true and false lumens in the aorta, (4) age between 18 and 80 years, (5) intimal tear located proximal to the left subclavian artery, and (6) meeting indications for TEVAR surgery. The exclusion criteria were (1) patients with chronic or subacute aortic dissection, (2) other acute aortic syndromes (e.g., intramural hematoma, aortic aneurysm rupture, etc.), (3) contraindications to surgery, (4) incomplete postoperative follow-up. All patients underwent TEVAR surgery and were followed up at 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months postoperatively, undergoing aortic CTA scans. Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Methods

CT angiography imaging

A Siemens SOMATOM Definition Flash dual-source CT scanner was utilized. Scanning parameters included a collimation of 0.6 mm, effective slice thickness of 0.75 mm, interslice gap of 0.75 mm, tube voltage of 120 kV, and automatic tube current modulation ranging from 90 to 130 mAs. The scanning range extended from the level of the thoracic inlet to the bilateral femoral artery levels. The procedure commenced with plain scanning followed by contrast-enhanced scanning. Iohexol (320 mg I/ml) was injected via the cubital vein at a rate of 4.0 ml/s, with a total dose of 70 to 100 ml, followed by a saline flush of 20 to 30 ml at the same rate. Bolus tracking was employed to monitor contrast agent concentration within the aortic lumen, initiating scanning upon reaching a predefined threshold of 100 HU.

Image analysis

TEVAR preoperative and postoperative CTA raw data were imported into a post-processing workstation for analysis. Techniques including Volume Rendering (VR), Multiple Planar Reconstruction (MPR), and Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) were employed for image post-processing. The volume evaluation was performed using the function embedded with the post-processing workstation and the semi-automatic manual adjustment method. First, the start and end levels of measurement were determined for each patient at each examination to ensure that the length of the aorta measurement was exactly the same in each case. The diameters of various planes of the aorta [2 cm distal to the left subclavian artery (P1), mid-descending aorta (P2), origin of the celiac trunk (P3), and proximal to the bifurcation of the abdominal aorta (P4)] and the distal abdominal aortic dissection segment (from the uncovered area of the stent to the bifurcation of the iliac artery) were measured (Fig. 1). The total area of the aorta, true lumen area, and false lumen area of each level were measured by manually outlining the ROIs. The aortic diameter edges were outlined at different levels, especially those with arterial branches. Measurements were performed with adjustments in axial, coronal, and sagittal planes to ensure perpendicularity to the long axis of the aorta. In the presence of thrombus formation in the false lumen, manual adjustment of the centerline of the aortic lumen was performed to ensure it remained at the center of the entire vessel lumen before measuring the maximum aortic diameter. The maximum diameter was considered as the total diameter, while the diameters of the true and false lumens were obtained by measuring the maximum diameter perpendicular to the intimal flap (manually adjusted). If the dissection was absent in certain planes, both false lumen diameter and area were recorded as 0. The total area, true lumen area, and false lumen area of the aorta were measured by manually delineating the regions of interest (ROI) in each plane. Automatic generation of aortic VR images and calculation of volume values (excluding thrombosed portions of the false lumen) and Modified Aortic Remodeling Index (MARI) changes were performed, particularly at planes with arterial branches. MARI was calculated as the ratio of true lumen volume to total vascular cavity volume. The volume values were calculated (the thrombus part of the false lumen was not included in the calculation). Consistency in aortic measurements was ensured across all postoperative assessments. The analysts were two radiologists with 13 and 17 years of experience, respectively. Each radiologist took two measurements, and the mean of all measurements was used for analysis.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0 software. Categorical data were expressed as percentages (%), and the paired chi-square test was employed. Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). For quantitative data collected at various time points before and after TEVAR follow-up, an analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Postoperative complications

A total of 1023 patients underwent aortic CTA examinations from January 2020 to June 2023, including 156 with TBAD who underwent TEVAR; 116 patients met at least one exclusion criterion. Finally, 40 patients were included in the study, comprising 30 males and 10 females, with an age range of 35 to 77 years and a mean age of (55.26 ± 7.41) years. Among them, 18 patients had hyperlipidemia, 8 had diabetes mellitus, 36 had hypertension, and 4 had coronary heart disease. All patients successfully underwent TEVAR, with a total of 41 aortic covered stents deployed during the procedure (one patient received two covered stents). All patients were followed up for more than 12 months postoperatively. During hospitalization, one patient experienced acute cerebral infarction (2.50%), resulting in mild dysarthria. During follow-up, there were 4 cases of endoleak (2.50%), 1 case of stent infection (2.50%), 2 cases of recurrent rupture at the distal end of the aortic stent (5.00%), and 2 deaths (5.00%), attributed to severe pulmonary infection and myocardial infarction, respectively. Six patients underwent secondary intervention surgeries (3 for endoleak, 2 for stenosis of abdominal aortic branches, and 1 for recurrent rupture at the distal end of the stent), accounting for 15.00%.

Following surgery, at plane P1, the total diameter and false lumen diameter gradually decreased, while the true lumen diameter gradually increased, with statistically significant differences. At plane P2, the true lumen diameter gradually increased, and the false lumen diameter gradually decreased, with statistically significant differences, while there was no statistically significant difference in the total diameter at P2. Postoperatively, at planes P3 and P4, both the total diameter and true lumen diameter exhibited a gradual increase, with statistically significant differences, whereas there were no statistically significant differences in the false lumen diameter at P3 and P4. Discussion

Aortic dissection refers to a pathological condition wherein a local tear occurs in the intima of the aorta, leading to the separation and expansion of the middle layer of the aortic wall due to the impact of blood flow, resulting in the simultaneous formation of true and false lumens within the artery, with or without communication between them [3]. Aortic dissection can trigger complications such as cardiac tamponade and aortic rupture, severely jeopardizing the patient’s physical health and safety [4]. Based on the extent of involvement, aortic dissection originating from the descending aorta without involving the ascending aorta is termed TBAD. TEVAR is currently the preferred clinical treatment for TBAD [5]. In this study, 40 cases of TBAD patients underwent TEVAR procedures smoothly, with no intraoperative deaths reported. Postoperatively, only one case experienced acute complications, confirming the favorable safety profile of TEVAR in treating TBAD.

TEVAR utilizes stent grafts to cover the intimal tear of the aorta, enlarging the true lumen while reducing the false lumen and promoting thrombosis, thereby achieving a restructuring of the aortic morphology [6]. Optimal aortic remodeling is of paramount significance in reducing the risk of complications and extending patient survival [7, 8]. The findings of this study reveal that postoperative diameters of the true lumen at the P1 and P2 planes gradually increased, while those of the false lumen progressively decreased, with this positive remodeling being most significant at 3 months postoperatively, followed by a slowing of progression at 6 months, possibly attributable to the radial supportive force of the stent reaching equilibrium with resistance typically by 6 months postoperatively, and the diameters of the thoracic aortic true and false lumens remained relatively stable [9–11]. Additionally, results indicate a gradual increase in the true lumen diameters at the P3 and P4 planes postoperatively, albeit a slight decrease in the P3 true lumen diameter at 6 months postoperatively, possibly due to the gradual attenuation of the radial supportive force of the stent transitioning from the thoracic aorta to the abdominal aorta, and the ongoing blood flow between the true and false lumens of the abdominal aorta. The changes in the area of the P3 false lumen were insignificant, while the area of the P4 false lumen slightly increased at 6 and 12 months postoperatively, likely attributed to hemodynamic disturbances in the abdominal aorta, with some of its branches being supplied by the false lumen. The total areas of P3 and P4 increased postoperatively, primarily due to the enlargement of the true lumen, while the changes in the false lumen were insignificant or mildly increased [12].

Most of the previous studies were on the analysis of the positive remodeling of the aorta after TEVAR, but few studies were on the specific analysis of the distal abdominal aortic segment covered by the stent. In contrast, our study is unique and innovative. For accurate positioning and volume measurement with multiple reviews, only dual-energy post-processing software can be analyzed in our department. We assessed the postoperative remodeling of the distal abdominal aortic segment covered by the stent. By comparing the remodeling of the uncovered dissected segment at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively, we found that there were no statistically significant differences in the maximum diameter and maximum area of the true lumen, maximum diameter, and maximum area of the false lumen. However, there were significant changes in the volumes of the true and false lumens. The volume of the true lumen gradually increased while the volume of the false lumen decreased. In some patients, the volume of the false lumen slightly increased at 6 months postoperatively but decreased by 12 months postoperatively. This indicates that relying solely on the maximum diameter and area of the distal dissected segment covered by the stent cannot reflect the overall changes in the vascular lumen. Volume measurement can more accurately and comprehensively reflect the aortic remodeling status [13]. Furthermore, our study employed the calculation of the MARI to assess aortic remodeling. The results showed a continuous increase in MARI in the distal abdominal aortic dissected segment covered by the stent postoperatively.

This study also has limitation. Since this study was a single-center, retrospective study with a small sample size, the robustness of the study was reduced. To sum up, volume measurements can capture subtle changes in the volumes of the true and false lumens of the aorta without stent graft coverage, which may accumulate and lead to different prognoses for patients. This aspect is often overlooked in clinical practice. However, the implementation of volume measurements requires specific post-processing workstations and is time-consuming, thus, its widespread clinical application is still pending. Additionally, in this study, one patient underwent a second scan after the arterial phase, and the comparison revealed a slight enlargement in the enhancement range of the false lumen on the second scan compared to the first. This suggests that the degree of thrombosis in the false lumen and the timing of the scan phase may influence the research results, which is an area for further investigation in our future research endeavors.

In summary, for the dissected segment distal to the stent graft, in addition to conventional diameter and area measurements, advocating for the inclusion of volume measurements is imperative.

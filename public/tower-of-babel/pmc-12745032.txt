Retreatment with venetoclax and rituximab following disease progression while off therapy in patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia
Authors: Danielle M Brander, Andrew W Roberts, Thomas J Kipps, Shuo Ma, Mary Ann Anderson, Michelle Boyer, Piers Blombery, Relja Popovic, Jordan Roser, Zhuangzhuang Liu, Brenda Chyla, John F Seymour
Published: 2025

Treatment approaches for chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL) have evolved significantly in recent years with the incorporation of novel therapies including those targeting Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) downstream of B‐cell receptor signaling as well as venetoclax, a potent inhibitor of the antiapoptotic B‐cell lymphoma 2 (BCL2) protein. 1 , 2 However, despite these therapeutic advances improving outcomes versus chemoimmunotherapy, 3 , 4 CLL remains incurable for most patients. Time‐limited approaches with these targeted agents are attractive due to the potential for fewer adverse events, reduced patient burden, lower costs, and, theoretically, a reduced risk of selection for resistance mutations. 5 , 6 As most patients with CLL/SLL will suffer disease relapse after their first treatment, the need remains for understanding effective strategies in the relapsed/refractory (R/R) setting, including options for retreatment that have the potential to extend the duration of benefit of a drug class used previously.

Venetoclax is approved in combination with obinutuzumab for treatment‐naive CLL, and venetoclax is approved in R/R CLL as monotherapy or in combination with rituximab; treatment with venetoclax plus rituximab (VenR) for up to 2 years has demonstrated durable remissions in patients with R/R CLL/SLL that can be sustained off treatment. 1 , 2 In addition to limited cost and toxicities, fixed‐duration regimens are of interest given their potential to avoid the resistance that could compromise the effectiveness of later lines of treatment independent of the drug target. Patients with double BTK inhibitor (BTKi)/BCL2 inhibitor (BCL2i) exposures have no standardized approach to their disease management; survival in this group of patients is discouragingly short, and the subsequent duration of response to recently approved noncovalent BTKi is also shortened in this group of patients. 7 , 8 , 9 It remains an area of interest to understand strategies to extend disease control with the available agents.

The M13‐365, a Phase 1b study examining the safety and outcomes of patients treated with VenR, 10 was the first prospective trial demonstrating that patients with CLL in deep response (complete response [CR] or undetectable minimal residual disease [uMRD]) could stop therapy with venetoclax and achieve similar outcomes to those with continuous therapy. An analysis of 5‐year outcomes of patients with R/R CLL/SLL treated with VenR with fixed‐duration or continuous venetoclax in this study revealed that 74% of patients who achieved a deep response maintained their response for 5 years or longer. 10 Retreatment with venetoclax is of increasing importance given the recurrent nature of CLL/SLL, and there is great interest in understanding that this option may extend the duration of clinical benefit of VenR beyond the time of first progressive disease (PD).

The aim of this long‐term follow‐up is to report the outcomes of patients (n = 9) treated on the M13‐365 clinical trial who attained a deep response (CR or uMRD) and elected per protocol amendment to discontinue venetoclax treatment and, on PD, reinitiated therapy with VenR.

The Phase 1b study of VenR, M13‐365 (NCT01682616), was an open‐label, dose escalation and expansion cohort trial enrolling patients between August 6, 2012, and May 28, 2014 (Figure S1). Full eligibility has been previously described 10 , 11 ; patients with R/R CLL/SLL were eligible if they had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≤ 1. The study was approved by the institutional review board at each participating site and was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All patients provided written informed consent. All patients had efficacy response assessments per 2008 International Workshop on CLL (iwCLL) criteria that included the use of computed tomography (CT) scans at the end of VenR combination treatment. MRD was assessed by 4–8 color flow cytometry in local laboratories, and uMRD was defined by <1 CLL cell present in 1 × 104/μL white blood cells. 10

Patients treated with VenR who achieved CR/CR with incomplete marrow recovery (CRi) or partial response (PR) with uMRD were permitted but not required per protocol amendment to stop venetoclax. Subsequently, patients who developed PD (iwCLL criteria or MRD progression defined as 2 consecutive peripheral blood [PB] MRD values > 10−4 or a single assessment > 10−3) could reinitiate VenR on protocol. Before retreatment, disease status was assessed by CT scan or magnetic resonance imaging and marrow biopsy. Venetoclax 400 mg was given orally daily after a 5‐week dose ramp‐up. Rituximab was given intravenously as early as Day 1 of Week 6 at 375 mg/m2, followed by 5 monthly doses at 500 mg/m2. Second responses were evaluated per the 2008 iwCLL criteria. 12

MRD assessments beyond the protocol follow‐up period were conducted per local institutional policy. Assessment of BCL2 mutations before or soon after retreatment using targeted next‐generation sequencing was available for six of the nine patients reinitiating treatment. 13 , 14

Forty‐nine patients were treated in M13‐365, of whom 33 achieved a deep response (CR/CRi or uMRD), with 16 electing to stop venetoclax after a median of 1.4 years on therapy. Of these, nine were retreated with VenR after disease progression off therapy and were assessed in this analysis (Table 1). Median age was 66 years (range, 58–79), and median number of prior therapies before their first VenR treatment was 2 (range, 1–4). Five patients (55%) had CLL with unmutated IGHV, and 2 with unknown status, and 2 patients (11%) had CLL with either del(17p) or mutated TP53. In response to initial therapy, six of nine patients achieved CR, and seven achieved PB and/or marrow uMRD (Table 1).

These patients were off initial treatment for a median of 38.4 months (range, 17.3–68.7) before PD occurred (Figure 1). Median time from PD to VenR retreatment was 4.9 months (range, 0.5–35.5). As of December 31, 2023, all nine patients achieved a response of PR or better to retreatment with VenR (Figure 1). Four did not progress and have been off therapy for >1 year. Median progression‐free survival (PFS) following retreatment was 4.9 years (95% CI, 1.6–not evaluable [NE]) (58.7 months [95% CI, 18.7–NE]) (Figure S2). Measured from the start of their initial VenR treatment, median second progression‑free survival (PFS2) was 9.5 years (95% CI, 4.2–NE) (114.5 months [95% CI, 50.9–NE]) (Figure S2). Among the six patients analyzed for acquired mutations in BCL2 after venetoclax‐based therapies, none were detected in the four patients tested before reinitiation of VenR, or the remaining two that were tested after VenR reinitiation when significant disease burden remained present. Of the seven patients who were tested for del (17p) and/or TP53 mutations before retreatment, all were negative for the del (17p), and one patient was positive for a TP53 mutation.

The M13‐365 study provided the first prospective evidence that CLL/SLL response can be maintained off treatment if patients achieved a deep response to initial VenR. Additionally, as the protocol had the distinctive option, but not requirement, to discontinue treatment if in a deep response, it permitted the within‐trial comparison that demonstrated that PFS off therapy in deep response was like that of patients who remained on continuous therapy after achieving deep response. 10 , 11

This long‐term follow‐up of the nine patients retreated with VenR for PD off therapy demonstrates that retreatment was safe and effective, achieving responses in 100% of patients. This approach resulted in a median PFS2 of 9.5 years from initial VenR treatment and 4.9 years from retreatment.

Moreover, of the six patients tested for BCL2 mutations, none had a detectable BCL2 mutation, supporting the notion that use of fixed‐duration therapy, in addition to mitigating toxicity risk and cost, may reduce the risk of developing resistance to subsequent lines of therapy. 13 , 14 The median time off‐treatment before initiation of retreatment was longer in this cohort compared to other previous publications, 15 , 16 suggesting a potential correlation between time off‐treatment and identification of more favorable patient groups of efficacy for retreatment. Ongoing prospective trials such as ReVenG (NCT04895436) 17 are formally studying the feasibility and effectiveness of retreatment with venetoclax and obinutuzumab for patients who previously received venetoclax plus an anti‐CD20 antibody with/without X, where X is any other drug. These reported findings support venetoclax‐based retreatment as an option for patients with R/R CLL who progress after venetoclax‐based therapy.

Let

\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(S)

if either
S
is local or if

is a smooth affine algebra over

; see
[
2
]
.
In
[

]
, it is proved that

is a homomorphism of Noetherian rings that splits, then for every ideal

in

and every non-negative integer

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{IR}(R)

\operatorname{Ass}_{S}H^{i}_{I}(S)

such that
\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(S)

7
]
. In the paper
[

]

is infinite.
In this paper we construct large examples of singular rings

\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(S)
is finite for all
i\geq 0
.

2.

2.1

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
R=K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]]
and let
\mathbf{x}=X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
. Set
G=G_{\mathbf{x}}(R)
be the associated graded ring of
R
with respect to
\mathbf{x}
. Note
G\cong R/(\mathbf{x})[X_{1}^{*},\ldots,X_{r}^{*}]
is a regular ring. Let
\mathcal{S}=R[(\mathbf{x})t,t^{-1}]
be the extended Rees algebra of the ideal
(\mathbf{x})
. We note that
\mathcal{S}
is a regular ring. If
T
is a regular ring containing a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
then the notion of
F_{T}
modules and
F_{T}
-finite,
F_{T}
-modules was introduced by Lyubeznik, see
[
3
]
. We have
\mathcal{S}
is a graded
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-finite,
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-module. Also note that

\mathcal{S}_{t^{-1}}=R[t,t^{-1}]
is also a graded
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-finite,
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-module. So
W=\mathcal{S}_{t^{-1}}/\mathcal{S}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}R/(\mathbf{x})^{n}
is also a graded
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-finite,
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-module.
Fix
m\geq 1
. Set
A_{m}=R/(\mathbf{x})^{m}
.
We show

Theorem 2.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
2.1
)
Let
I
be an ideal in
R

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{1})
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{m})
.

Proof.

We may assume
K
is infinite.
We note that
H^{i}_{I\mathcal{S}}(W)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x})^{n})
is a
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-finite,
F_{\mathcal{S}}
-module. By
[
6
, 4.1]
it follows that

V=\ker(t^{-1}\colon H^{i}_{I\mathcal{S}}(W)(1)\rightarrow H^{i}_{I\mathcal{S}}(W))

is a graded
F_{G}
-finite
F_{G}
-module. For
n\geq 0
we have exact sequences:

0\rightarrow V_{n}\rightarrow H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x})^{n+1})\rightarrow H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x})^{n}).

We also have
V_{0}=H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x}))
. An easy induction yields

\operatorname{Ass}V_{n-1}\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x})^{n})\subseteq\bigcup_{m=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Ass}V_{m}.

Let
\mathfrak{n}=(X_{r+1},\cdots,X_{d})
be maximal ideal of
G_{0}
. We note that
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}V_{m}
if and only if the Koszul homology module
H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V_{m})\neq 0
. Set
T=K[X_{1}^{*},\ldots,X_{r}^{*}]
. By
[
6
, 1.1]
we have that
H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V)
is a graded
F_{T}
-finite,
F_{T}
-module. In particular we have by
[
6
, 1.6(b)]
that
H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V)_{m}=H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V_{m})
is non-zero for some
m\geq 0
if and only if
H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V_{j})
is non-zero for ALL
j\geq 0
.

We now prove the result:

If
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{1})
then as
V_{0}=H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x}))
we have
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}V_{0}
. So

H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V)_{0}\neq 0
. It follows that
H_{d-r}(\mathfrak{n},V_{m-1})
is non-zero. So

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x})^{m})
.

Conversely if
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{m})
then
\mathfrak{m}\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}\operatorname{Ass}V_{j}
. Say
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}V_{l}
. Then by above argument
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}V_{j}
for all
j\geq 0
. In particular
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}V_{0}
and as
V_{0}=H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x}))
the result follows.
∎

\operatorname{char}k=0

3.1
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
. Let
R=K[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]]
and let
\mathbf{x}=X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
. Set
G=G_{\mathbf{x}}(R)
be the associated graded ring of
R
with respect to
\mathbf{x}
. Note
G\cong R/(\mathbf{x})[X_{1}^{*},\ldots,X_{r}^{*}]
is a regular ring. Let
\mathcal{S}=R[(\mathbf{x})t,t^{-1}]
be the extended Rees algebra of the ideal
(\mathbf{x})
. We note that
\mathcal{S}
is a regular ring. Let
D_{0}=
ring of
K

G_{0}=k[[X_{r+1},\ldots,X_{d}]]
. Let
D
be the
r^{th}
Weyl algebra over

with the obvious grading. Then note that

is a ring of
D

.
We note that

is a ring of differential operators of

\mathcal{S}
. Also note that

commutes with the action of differential operators.
We have
\mathcal{S}
is a
D
-module. Also note that

\mathcal{S}_{t^{-1}}=R[t,t^{-1}]
is also a
D
-module. So
W=\mathcal{S}_{t^{-1}}/\mathcal{S}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}R/(\mathbf{x})^{n}
is also a
D
-module.
Fix
m\geq 1
. Set

.

We show

Theorem 3.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
3.1
)
Let
I
be an idealin
R

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{1})
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(A_{m})

We note that

0\rightarrow G\rightarrow W(1)\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0,

is a short exact sequence of

is a sequence graded of

-modules.
As

is a graded

-module. As

is a graded quotient of

it follows that

is a
graded holonomic generalized Eulerian

-module.
For

we have exact sequences:

V_{0}=H^{i}_{I}(R/(\mathbf{x}))

\mathfrak{n}=(X_{r+1},\cdots,X_{d})

. We note that
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}V_{m}
if and only if the Koszul homology module

Proof of Result stated in abstract

We now indicate a proof of the result stated in the abstract.
Let
I
be the pre-image of
Q
in
R
. Let

denote the number of elements in a set

We have by Theorem

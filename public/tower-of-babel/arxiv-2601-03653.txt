Giacomo Micheli, Mihran Papikian

Abstract
. We establish a connection between Drinfeld modules and rank metric codes, focusing on the case of semifield codes. Our framework constructs rank metric codes from linear subspaces of endomorphisms of a Drinfeld module, using tools such as characteristic polynomials on Tate modules and the Chebotarev density theorem. We show that Sheekey’s construction [ She20 ] fits naturally into this setting, yielding a short conceptual proof of one of his main results. We then give a new construction of infinite families of semifield codes arising from Drinfeld modules defined over finite fields.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Rank metric codes and semifields

Error-correcting codes are used when communication is over a channel in which
errors may occur. This requires a set equipped with a distance function and a subset of allowed codewords; if the number of errors
is assumed to be small, then a received message is decoded to be the nearest valid codeword.

The most well-known and widely-used examples are the codes with the Hamming metric: the codewords are taken from an
n
-dimensional vector
space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
elements (usually
q=2
), and the distance between two vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

is defined as the number of positions in which they differ.

In rank-metric coding, the codewords are instead taken from the set
\Mat_{m,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of
m\times n
-matrices (
m\leq n
), with
the distance between two matrices defined as the rank of their difference

\dist(X,Y)=\rank(X-Y).

A rank-metric code is then a subset
\mathcal{C}\subset\Mat_{m,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with a minimum distance defined by

\dist(\mathcal{C})=\min\{\dist(X,Y)\mid X,Y\in\mathcal{C},X\neq Y\}.

Such
\mathcal{C}
must satisfy a

Singleton-type bound
: if
d=\dist(\mathcal{C})
, then

(1.1)

\left|\mathcal{C}\right|\leq q^{n(m-d+1)}.

A code obtaining the bound (
1.1
) is said to be a
Maximum Rank Distance code
, or
MRD code
for short. It is know that
MRD codes exist for every finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and any choice of parameters
d\leq m\leq n
; see
[
Del78
]
. An MRD code
can correct up to
\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor
errors.

We will assume for the rest of this article that MRD codes that we consider are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace of
\Mat_{m,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
In the special case when
d=m=n
, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear MRD code is equivalent to a

semifield
—a finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras with unity, in which multiplication is not
necessarily commutative or associative; see
[
She20
, Prop. 1]
.
To distinguish such MRD codes,
we will call them
semifield codes
, although this is not a standard terminology.

To see the connection between semifields and MRD codes, let
\mathcal{S}
be a semifield of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then the right multiplication by a non-zero element
\alpha
on
\mathcal{S}
defines an
invertible linear operator
T_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
,
\beta\mapstochar\rightarrow\beta\alpha
. After fixing a basis of
\mathcal{S}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we get
an injection
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, whose image is a semifield code.
For example, this can be applied to
\mathcal{S}=k
, where

k\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}

is the finite field with
q^{n}
elements.

A more interesting example of a finite semifield arises from the
twisted polynomial ring

R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k\!\left\{\tau\right\}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\mid n\geq 0,a_{i}\in k\right\},

where the addition is the usual addition of polynomials, but
the multiplication is subject to the following commutation rule

\tau a=a^{q}\tau\quad\text{for all}\quad a\in k.

It is clear that
R
is a (noncommutative) ring without zero-divisors. It is also easy to show that
R
possesses a right (and left) division algorithm. Let
P\in R
be a monic irreducible element of degree
s
. For any
f\in R
, there are unique
g,e\in R
such that

f=gP+e\quad\text{with }\deg(e)<s\text{ or }e=0.

We denote the residue
e=f\ \mathrm{mod}_{r}\ P
. With this notation, the set

\mathcal{S}=\{f\in R\mid\deg(f)\leq s-1\},

with the addition from
R
and multiplication

a\circ b=ab\ \mathrm{mod}_{r}\ P

is a semifield of order
q^{ns}
. The MRD codes that one obtains from such semifields are called
Gabidulin codes
; cf.
[
She20
]
.

1.2.
Drinfeld modules

Elliptic curves have enjoyed great success in cryptographic applications. While
Drinfeld modules–despite their many formal similarities to elliptic curves–are not suitable for
cryptographic applications due to their semilinear nature
[
Sca01
]
, this same semilinear structure turns
out to be ideally suited for constructing rank-metric codes.

We denote
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the polynomial ring in indeterminate
T
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{n}\lhd A
, with slight abuse of notation, we denote by the same symbol
the unique monic generator of
\mathfrak{n}
. The
primes
of
A
are the maximal ideals of
A
.
Given a prime
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
, we denote
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=A/\mathfrak{p}
.

A
Drinfeld module
of rank
r\geq 1
over
k
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

\phi\colon A\rightarrow R,\quad a\mapstochar\rightarrow\phi_{a},

such that
\phi_{T}=g_{0}+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r}\tau^{r}
for some
g_{0},\dots,g_{r}\in k
with
g_{r}\neq 0
. The
A
-characteristic of
\phi
,
denoted
\chr_{A}(\phi)
, is the kernel of the homormorphism
A\rightarrow k
defined by
T\mapstochar\rightarrow g_{0}
. The Drinfeld module
\phi
makes the algebraic
closure
\bar{k}
into an
A
-module, denoted
{{}^{\phi}}\overline{k}
; see Section
2
for the details. The elements of
{{}^{\phi}}\overline{k}

annihilated by
a\in A
form a finite
A
-module denoted
\phi[a]
; if
\chr_{A}(\phi)\nmid a
, then
\phi[a]\cong(A/aA)^{r}
. Define
the
endomorphism ring of
\phi
as

\End(\phi)=\left\{u\in R\mid u\phi_{T}=\phi_{T}u\right\}.

\End(\phi)
is an
A
-algebra, which is known to be a free
A
-module of rank
\leq r^{2}
. Every
u\in\End(\phi)
acts
A
-linearly on
\phi[a]
.
Choosing a prime
\mathfrak{p}\neq\chr_{A}(\phi)
, we obtain a homomorphism

\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\End(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\End_{A}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\cong\Mat_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

The general recipe

Our construction of semifield codes relies on three main ingredients:

•

A Drinfeld module
\phi
of rank
r
,

•

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{M}\subset\End(\phi)
,

•

A prime
\mathfrak{p}\in A
, distinct from the
A
-characteristic of
\phi
, such that every nonzero
u\in\mathcal{M}
acts
invertibly
on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
, i.e., as an invertible linear transformation.

Given this, we obtain an embedding

\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathcal{M}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\End_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\cong\Mat_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}),

where each nonzero
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(u)
has full rank. If additionally

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=r\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{p})
, the image
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathcal{M})
is a semifield code.

The flexibility of choosing
\phi
and
\mathcal{M}
makes this construction very general.
The advantage of this perspective is that it provides access to the rich theory of
Drinfeld modules. The central challenge is to find a suitable
\mathfrak{p}
satisfying the invertibility condition; here one can leverage various tools from the theory of Drinfeld modules, such as the equality between the
\tau
-degree of an endomorphism and the
T
-degree of its determinant on the associated Tate module. We focus on the semifield case, though this strategy extends to MRD codes by relaxing the invertibility requirement.

We demonstrate this idea with a simple example.
Define
\phi
by
\phi_{T}=\tau^{n}
. The rank of
\phi
is
n
,
\chr_{A}(\phi)=T
, and
\End(\phi)=R
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\neq T
be a prime of degree
s
and let

\mathcal{M}=\{u\in R\mid\deg(u)\leq s-1\}.

We claim that every
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
. We argue by contradiction. Assume
0\neq\beta\in\phi[\mathfrak{p}]

is annihilated by
u
. Then the
A
-submodule of
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
generated by
\beta
has size
q^{s}
, yet
\deg(u)<s
implies
\#\ker(u)\leq q^{s-1}
, a contradiction.
Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=ns=\deg(\mathfrak{p})\cdot n
, the image
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathcal{M})
is a semifield code.

1.3.
Main results

In Section
3
, we give a reinterpretation of Sheekey’s construction

[
She20
]
in terms of Drinfeld modules and a short proof
of one of the main results in
[
She20
]
.
Sheekey’s construction has been generalized in
[
TZ19
]
and
[
LSS25
]
using the
methods in
[
She20
]
, which rely on direct computations in skew-polynomial rings
and their quotients.

In our Drinfeld module reinterpretation of Sheekey’s construction, as in the example in
\lx@sectionsign
1.2
, one takes
\phi_{T}=\tau^{n}
, but now

\mathcal{M}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{u_{0}+u_{1}\tau\cdots+u_{s}\tau^{s}\in R\mid u_{s}=\eta u_{0}\}

for a fixed
\eta\in k
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\neq T
be a monic prime in
A
of degree
s
with constant coefficient
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. We show that if

\Nr_{k/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\eta)\cdot(-1)^{s(n-1)}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\neq 1
, then the image of
\mathcal{M}
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
is a semifield code. The proof
uses the duality between
u\in\mathcal{M}

being an endomorphism of
\phi
and
\phi_{T}
being an endomorphism of the auxiliary
Drinfeld module
\psi
defined by
\psi_{T}=u
: this converts invertibility questions into questions about
characteristic polynomials of endomorphisms, for which standard tools from the theory of Drinfeld
modules are available. Note that choosing
\eta=0
gives the example in
\lx@sectionsign
1.2
. The interesting fact about Sheekey’s construction
is that the semifield codes thus constructed in some cases correspond to genuinely new families of semifields, with
parameters for which no examples were known before.

In Section
4
, we extend the setup as follows:
Let
\ell
and
s
be integers such that
n=\lcm(\ell,s)
. We choose
\phi
so that
\phi_{T}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}\!\left\{\tau^{\ell}\right\}\subset k\!\left\{\tau\right\}

and
\deg(\phi_{T})=r\ell
. In this case,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\!\left\{\tau^{s}\right\}\subset\End(\phi)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime different from

\chr_{A}(\phi)
and denote
d=\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{p})
. We take

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{rd-1}b_{i}\tau^{si}\mid b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\right\}.

Unlike the constructions in
[
She20
,
TZ19
,
LSS25
]
, which all take
\phi_{T}=\tau^{n}
,
our construction uses Drinfeld modules with smaller endomorphism rings, exploiting
the structure of division subalgebras of
k(\tau)
.
Under some technical assumptions on the reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
of the minimal polynomial
m_{\phi}(x)
of the Frobenius endomorphism
\pi=\tau^{n}\in\End(\phi)
,
we show that the image of
\mathcal{M}
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
is a semifield code. Here again the duality between
u\in\mathcal{M}
being an
endomorphism of
\phi
and
\phi_{T}
being an endomorphism of
\psi
defined by
\psi_{T}=u
comes into play. Next,
using the Chebotarev density theorem, we show that one can always choose
\phi
and
\mathfrak{p}
so that the assumptions on
m_{\phi}(x)

are satisfied. Finally, we compute the nuclear parameters of our code and illustrate the construction with an
explicit example. While the nuclear parameters we obtain do not immediately
distinguish our codes from known families, the construction is conceptually new
and may yield new semifields for appropriate choices of parameters.

2.
Preliminaries on Drinfeld modules

In this section we recall some basic facts
from the theory of Drinfeld modules that will be used later in the paper. Most of the proofs can be found in
[
Pap23
]
.

2.1.
Definition

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
be the fraction field of
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. For
0\neq a\in A
, let
\deg(a)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the usual degree
of
a
as a polynomial in
T
.

Let
K
be a field containing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as a subfield. Let

K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be the twisted polynomial ring already discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
in the case when
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
(so the commutation rule is
\tau a=a^{q}\tau
for all
a\in K
).
There is a homomorphism
\partial\colon K\!\left\{\tau\right\}\rightarrow K
,
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow a_{0}
, called the
derivative
.
For
f=a_{h}\tau^{h}+\cdots+a_{n}\tau^{n}
with
0\leq h\leq n
,
a_{h}\neq 0
and
a_{n}\neq 0
, we define the
height
of
f
as
\Ht(f)=h
, and the
degree
of
f
as
\deg_{\tau}(f)=n
.
The map

f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\longmapsto f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}

gives a ring isomorphism between
K\!\left\{\tau\right\}

and the ring
K\langle x\rangle
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomial, where on
K\langle x\rangle
the multiplication is defined via the substitution
f_{1}\circ f_{2}=f_{1}(f_{2}(x))
.

A
Drinfeld module
of rank
r\geq 1
over
K
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi\colon A

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow K\!\left\{\tau\right\},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\longmapsto\phi_{a}=g_{0}(a)+g_{1}(a)\tau+\cdots+g_{n}(a)\tau^{n},

such that for
a\neq 0
we have
n=\deg(a)r
and
g_{n}(a)\neq 0
. Note that
\phi
is always injective, so gives an embedding of
A

into the non-commutative ring
K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
. Moreover,
\phi
is uniquely determined by
\phi_{T}
, so to define a Drinfeld module over
K

one simply needs to choose
g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in K
such that
g_{r}\neq 0
and put
\phi_{T}=g_{0}+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r}\tau^{r}
.
We define a homomorphism
A\rightarrow K
via
a\mapstochar\rightarrow\partial\phi_{a}
. The
A
-characteristic of
\phi
is

\chr_{A}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ker(\gamma)
; note that
\chr_{A}(\phi)
is either
0
or a prime of
A
.

2.2.
Torsion module and endomorphisms

Let
\phi\colon A\rightarrow K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be a Drinfeld module over
K
of rank
r
. Through
\phi
, the algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
acquires an
A
-module structure, where
a\in A
acts on
\beta\in\overline{K}
by
a\ast\beta=\phi_{a}(\beta)
, i.e., we substitute
\beta
into the
polynomial
\phi_{a}(x)
. Denote this module by
{{}^{\phi}}{\overline{K}}
. One is interested in its torsion submodule. More precisely, given
0\neq a\in A
,
the
a
-torsion submodule
of
{{}^{\phi}}{\overline{K}}
, denoted
\phi[a]
, is the set of roots of the polynomial
\phi_{a}(x)
. It is easy to check that
\phi[a]

is indeed an
A
-submodule of
{{}^{\phi}}{\overline{K}}
, i.e., is invariant under the action of any
b\in A
, where
b
acts via
\phi_{b}
.
If
\chr_{A}(\phi)
does not divide
a
, then
\phi[a]\cong(A/aA)^{r}
; see
[
Pap23
, Cor. 3.5.3]
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
be a prime different from
\chr_{A}(\phi)
. The finite
A
-modules
\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{n}]\cong(A/\mathfrak{p}^{n})^{r}
,
n\geq 1
, form a projective system
with transition maps
\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}]\rightarrow\phi[\mathfrak{p}^{n}]
,
\alpha\mapstochar\rightarrow\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
. Taking the inverse limit, one obtains the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic Tate module

T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}
of
\phi
, where
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ring of integers of the completion of
F
with respect to the absolute value arising from the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation on
F
.

The
endomorphisms of
\phi
defined over
K
are the elements of
K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
which induce endomorphisms of
{{}^{\phi}}\overline{K}
. More explicitly,

\displaystyle\End(\phi)

\displaystyle=\{u\in K\!\left\{\tau\right\}\mid u\phi_{a}=\phi_{a}u\text{ for all }a\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in K\!\left\{\tau\right\}\mid u\phi_{T}=\phi_{T}u\}.

Obviously, the image
\phi(A)
of
A
under
\phi
is in
\End(\phi)
. In fact, it is easy to check that
\End(\phi)
is an
A
-algebra, where we
identify
\phi(A)
with
A
. Moreover, one can show
that
\End(\phi)
is a free
A
-module of rank
\leq r^{2}
; see
[
Pap23
, Thm. 3.4.1]
.

Any
u\in\End(\phi)
induces an
A
-linear transformation on
\phi[a]
, and also an
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear transformation on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)
.
Let
P_{\phi,u}(x)\in A_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
denote the characteristic polynomial of this latter transformation. It turns out that the coefficients of
P_{\phi,u}(x)
are
actually in
A
and do not depend on
\mathfrak{p}
; see
[
Pap23
, Thm. 3.6.6]
. The characteristic polynomial of
u
acting on
on the
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-vector space
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}
is the reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
of
P_{\phi,u}(x)
. In particular,
u
acts
invertibly
on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]

(i.e., as an invertible linear transformation) if and only if
P_{\phi,u}(0)
is not divisible by
\mathfrak{p}
.

One of the key technical questions arising in this
paper is to show that certain endomorphisms act invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
for an appropriately chosen
\mathfrak{p}
. More precisely,
we choose an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace
\mathcal{M}\subset\End(\phi)
–
the message space
–and show that every
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}

acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
. In that situation,
\mathcal{M}
defines a semifield code in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\cong\Mat_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
if

(2.1)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=r\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{p}).

2.3.
Anderson motive of Drinfeld module

Let
\phi\colon A\rightarrow K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be a Drinfeld module over
K
of rank
r
defined by

\phi_{T}=g_{0}+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r}\tau^{r}.

We associate with
\phi
a left
K[T,\tau]
-module
M_{\phi}
, called the
Anderson motive of
\phi

[
And86
]
, by taking
M_{\phi}=K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
and defining

\displaystyle u\circ m

\displaystyle=um\quad\text{for all $u\in K\!\left\{\tau\right\}$ and $m\in M_{\phi}$},

\displaystyle a\circ m

\displaystyle=m\phi_{a}\quad\text{for all $a\in A$ and $m\in M_{\phi}$},

where
um
and
m\phi_{a}
are multiplications in
K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
. By the proof of
[
Pap23
, Lem. 3.4.4]
,
M_{\phi}

is freely generated over
K[T]
by the elements
\{1,\tau,\dots,\tau^{r-1}\}
; we call this set the
standard basis
of
M_{\phi}
.

Let
u\in\End(\phi)
. Then
u
induces an endomorphism of the
K[T,\tau]
-module
M_{\phi}
by
m\mapstochar\rightarrow mu
; see the proof of
[
Pap23
, Prop. 3.4.5]
.
Let
U=(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq r-1}\in\Mat_{r}(K[T])
be the matrix
by which
u
acts on
M_{\phi}
as a free
K[T]
-module with respect to the standard basis, i.e.,

\tau^{i}u=u_{0,i}+u_{1,i}\tau+\cdots+u_{r-1,i}\tau^{r-1},\qquad 0\leq i\leq r-1.

By Theorem 3.6.6 and Proposition 3.6.7 in
[
Pap23
]
,

(2.2)

P_{\phi,u}(x)=\det(xI_{r}-U),

i.e., the characteristic polynomial of
u
is equal to the characteristic polynomial of the matrix
U
.
In particular,
u
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}
does not divide
\det(U)
in
A
.

For
f=f_{0}+f_{1}T+\cdots+f_{n}T^{n}\in K[T]
and
i\geq 0
, denote

f^{(q^{i})}=f_{0}^{q^{i}}+f_{1}^{q^{i}}T+\cdots+f_{n}^{q^{i}}T^{n}.

For a matrix
S=(s_{ij})\in\Mat_{m,n}(K[T])
, put
S^{(q^{i})}=\left(s_{ij}^{(q^{i})}\right)
.
Let

u=u_{0}+u_{1}\tau+\cdots+u_{r-1}\tau^{r-1}

be the expansion of
u
in the standard basis. Then

\displaystyle\tau u

\displaystyle=u_{0}^{(q)}\tau+u_{1}^{(q)}\tau^{2}+\cdots+u_{r-1}^{(q)}\tau^{r}

\displaystyle=u_{0}^{(q)}\tau+u_{1}^{(q)}\tau^{2}+\cdots+u_{r-1}^{(q)}(g_{r}^{-1}(T-g_{0}-g_{1}\tau-\cdots-g_{r-1}\tau^{r-1}))

\displaystyle=u_{r-1}^{(q)}(g_{r}^{-1}(T-g_{0}))+(u_{0}^{(q)}-u_{r-1}^{(q)}(g_{r}^{-1}g_{1}))\tau+(u_{1}^{(q)}-u_{r-1}^{(q)}(g_{r}^{-1}g_{2}))\tau^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle+(u_{r-2}^{(q)}-u_{r-1}^{(q)}(g_{r}^{-1}g_{r-1}))\tau^{r-1}.

Put

(2.3)

S_{\phi}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&(T-g_{0})/g_{r}\\
1&0&\cdots&0&-g_{1}/g_{r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&-g_{r-1}/g_{r}\end{bmatrix}.

From the above computation one deduces that for any
1\leq i\leq r-1
we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}u_{0,i}\\
\vdots\\
u_{r-1,i}\end{bmatrix}=S_{\phi}\begin{bmatrix}u_{0,i-1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{r-1,i-1}\end{bmatrix}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=S_{\phi}S_{\phi}^{(q)}\begin{bmatrix}u_{0,i-2}\\
\vdots\\
u_{r-1,i-2}\end{bmatrix}^{(q^{2})}=\cdots

\displaystyle=S_{\phi}S_{\phi}^{(q)}\cdots S_{\phi}^{(q^{i-1})}\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}\\
\vdots\\
u_{r-1}\end{bmatrix}^{(q^{i})}.

2.4.
Example

Here we construct a semifield code using the tools discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
.

Let
r
and
s
be positive integers, let
n=\lcm(r,s)
, let
k=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and let
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
.
Let
\phi\colon A\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be the Drinfeld module defined by

\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{r}.

Let

\mathcal{M}=\left\{a+b\tau^{s}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}\right\}\subset\End(\phi).

We further assume that
s<r
, so that
u=a+b\tau^{s}\in\mathcal{M}
is the expansion of
u
with respect to the standard basis of
M_{\phi}
. In that case,
one computes that the matrix
U
by which
u
acts on
M_{\phi}
with respect to the standard basis is

(2.4)

U=\diag(a,a^{q},\dots,a^{q^{r-1}})+\begin{bmatrix}&D_{s}\\
I_{r-s}&\end{bmatrix}\diag(b,b^{q},\dots,b^{q^{r-1}}),

where

D_{s}=\diag(T-t,T-t^{q},\cdots,T-t^{q^{s-1}})\in\Mat_{s}(k[T]).

Next, one computes that, when
r\neq 2s
, the determinant of
U
in (
2.4
) is equal to

\det U=\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(a)+(-1)^{r-1}\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(b)\cdot\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T-t).

We want to show that for an appropriate choice of
t
and
\mathfrak{p}
, all
u\in\mathcal{M}
act invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
. First, note that given any prime

\mathfrak{q}\lhd A
of degree
s
, we can choose
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
so that
\mathfrak{q}=\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T-t)
. Next, for a prime
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
, by Dirichlet’s theorem, any element of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the residue modulo
\mathfrak{p}
of some prime
\mathfrak{q}
. Thus, if
\deg(\mathfrak{p})\geq 2
,
there is always
\mathfrak{q}
which is
not
congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}

to an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Assume
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
are chosen with this property.

If
u\in\mathcal{M}
is not invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
, then
\mathfrak{p}
divides

\det(U)=\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(a)+(-1)^{r-1}\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(b)\cdot\mathfrak{q}.

If
b=0
, this is obviously not possible, so we assume

. With this assumption, if
\mathfrak{p}

divides
\det(U)
, then
\mathfrak{q}
is congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}
to an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which leads to a contradiction. Thus, we can always choose
s
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}

so that the images of nonzero elements of
\mathcal{M}
in

are invertible. Finally, note that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{M})=2r
, so
by (
2.1
) we get a semifield code when
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=2
.

3.
Reinterpretation of Sheekey’s construction

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, let
k=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be the degree

n
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
R=k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be the twisted polynomial ring with coefficients in
k
.
The center of
R
is
A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]
, where
\pi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tau^{n}
. Note that
R
is a free
A^{\prime}
-algebra of rank
n^{2}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
central left multiple
of nonzero
u\in R
is a polynomial
f\in A^{\prime}
such that
f=wu
for some
w\in R
.
The
minimal central left multiple
of
u
is the unique central left multiple of
u
which is monic and has minimal degree in
\pi
.

Remark 3.2
.

The existence of a central left multiple can be seen, for example, by taking the norm of
u
from
\mathbb{F}_{q}[u,\pi]
into
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]
.
The uniqueness of the minimal central left multiple follows from the minimality of degree and monic assumption, since the difference of two central left multiples is still a central left multiple. Finally, using the division algorithm in
A^{\prime}
, we see that any central left multiple of
u

is divisible by the minimal central left multiple.

Given
0\neq u\in R
, we define a Drinfeld module
\psi\colon A\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
by
\psi_{T}=u
. Let
r_{u}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\deg_{\tau}u=\rank(\psi)
.
Note that
\pi\in\End(\psi)
, since
\pi
is in the center of
R
.
Let
m_{u}(x)\in A[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\pi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
.
Let
P_{u}(x)\in A[x]
be the characteristic polynomial of
\pi
acting on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi)
for any
\mathfrak{p}\neq\chr_{A}(\psi)
.
Let
\overline{m}_{u,T}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be the polynomial obtained by reducing the coefficients of
m_{u}(x)
modulo
T
, and
define
\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
similarly. Denote
L=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
,
\widetilde{L}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u,\pi)
, and

d_{u}=[\widetilde{L}:L].

Lemma 3.3
.

With previous notation,
\overline{m}_{u,T}(\pi)
is a central left multiple of
u
.

Proof.

By definitions,
f=\overline{m}_{u,T}(\pi)
annihilates
\psi[T]
, i.e., the roots of
u(x)
are roots of
f(x)
. If we show
that
\Ht(f)\geq\Ht(u)
, then
f=wu
follows from Lemma 3.1.16 in
[
Pap23
]
. If

\chr_{A}(\psi)\neq T
(equivalently,
\Ht(u)=0
), then the desired inequality is trivially true.

Now assume
\chr_{A}(\psi)=T
. We recall that (see
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.2]
)

(3.1)

P_{u}(x)=m_{u}(x)^{r_{u}/d_{u}}.

Moreover,
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.13]
implies that

\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)=x^{\Ht(u)}\cdot g(x)
, where
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
g(0)\neq 0
. Thus,

\Ht(f)=\frac{n\cdot d_{u}}{r_{u}}\Ht(u).

On the other hand, by
[
Pap23
, (4.1.3)]
,

\frac{n\cdot d_{u}}{r_{u}}=[\widetilde{L}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]\geq 1.

Combining these two formulas, we get
\Ht(f)\geq\Ht(u)
as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
\partial(u)\neq 0
.
The polynomial
u
is irreducible in
R
if and only if
\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.

Proof.

Note that
\psi[T]
is the set of roots of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomial
\psi_{T}(x)=u(x)
, which we assume is
separable. Since
T\neq\chr_{A}(\psi)
, the polynomial
\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)
is the characteristic polynomial of
\pi
acting on
\psi[T]
.
Therefore, the polynomial
\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)
is reducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
if and only if
\psi[T]
has a
\pi
-invariant
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace. The
elements of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
W\subseteq\psi[T]
give an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomial
w(x)=\prod_{\alpha\in W}(x-\alpha)\in\overline{k}[x]
.
By
[
Pap23
, Lem. 3.1.16]
, there is
g\in\overline{k}\!\left\{\tau\right\}
such that
u=gw
.
The subspace
W
is
\pi
-invariant if and only if the coefficients of
w(x)
are in
k
,
so the decomposition
f=gw
takes place in
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
. Thus,
\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)
is reducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
if and only if
u
is reducible in
R
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
\partial(u)\neq 0
.
If
u
is irreducible in
R
, then the minimal central left multiple of
u
is
\overline{P}_{u,T}(\pi)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, the assumption that
u
is irreducible implies that

\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is irreducible. In that case, by (
3.1
),
\overline{m}_{u,T}(x)
is also irreducible and

\overline{m}_{u,T}(x)=\overline{P}_{u,T}(x)
. Now, by Lemma
3.3
,
\overline{m}_{u,T}(\pi)
is
a central left multiple of
u
. Using the irreducibly of
\overline{m}_{u,T}(x)
, we conclude that it is the
minimal central left multiple.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Theorem
3.5
is equivalent to Theorem 3 in
[

]
. We note that there is an unnecessary assumption in

that

is irreducible in

.

Let

u_{0}\neq 0
,
u_{s}\neq 0
.
Let

be the motive of
\psi
defined by

Now let
\phi
be the Drinfeld module of rank

\phi_{T}=\pi
. Note that
\chr_{A}(\phi)=T
and for any

For this Drinfeld module the endomorphism ring is as large as possible,

is in the center of

. Let

\mathfrak{p}_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{p}(0)

\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon R=\End(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\cong\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

.

Theorem 3.7
.

Fix
\eta\in k
and define

\mathcal{M}=\{u_{0}+u_{1}\tau\cdots+u_{s}\tau^{s}\in R\mid u_{s}=\eta u_{0}\}.

If
N(\eta)\cdot(-1)^{s(n-1)}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\neq 1
, then the image of
\mathcal{M}
in

is a semifield code.

Proof.

The dimension of
\mathcal{M}
over

is
ns
(there is a dependency between the leading and the constant coefficients of the

). Therefore, by (

If

,
so this is possible only if

If this is not the case, so

4.
A construction of semifield codes

In this section we generalize the construction in
\lx@sectionsign
2.4
. In this more general setting, explicit computations
of determinants of endomorphisms become unwieldy, so we use various ad hoc arguments specific to the given situation to show the invertibility
of endomorphisms acting on torsion points of Drinfeld modules.

4.1.
Division algebras

In addition to the notation used in Section
3
, let
\Delta=k(\tau)
be the algebra over
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
given in terms
of generators and relations as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta=k(\pi)\oplus k(\pi)\tau\oplus\cdots\oplus k(\pi)\tau^{n-1},

\displaystyle\tau^{n}=\pi,\quad\tau\alpha=\alpha^{q}\tau\quad\text{for all}\quad\alpha\in k.

It is known that
\Delta
is a central division algebra over
K
of dimension
n^{2}
and the local invariants of
\Delta
are
zero at all places of
K
except at
\pi
and
1/\pi
, where the invariants are
1/n
and
-1/n
, respectively; see
[
Pap23
, Section 4.1]
.

Let
\ell
and
s
be integers such that
n=\lcm(\ell,s)
. Let
g=\gcd(\ell,s)
. Denote

\Delta(\ell,s)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(\tau^{s}),\qquad\Delta(s,\ell)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}(\tau^{\ell}),

so that
\Delta(n,1)=\Delta
and
\Delta(1,n)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)=K
.
We consider
\Delta(\ell,s)
and
\Delta(s,\ell)
as division subalgebras of
\Delta
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Denote
K_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}(\pi)
.

(1)

Z(\Delta(\ell,s))=Z(\Delta(s,\ell))=K_{g}
, where
Z(\cdot)
denotes the center of the corresponding algebra.

(2)

\Cent_{\Delta}(\Delta(\ell,s))=\Delta(s,\ell)
and
\Cent_{\Delta}(\Delta(s,\ell))=\Delta(\ell,s)
, where
\Cent_{\Delta}(\cdot)
denotes the centralizer in
\Delta

of the corresponding algebra.

(3)

\dim_{K_{g}}\Delta(\ell,s)=(\ell/g)^{2}
and
\dim_{K_{g}}\Delta(s,\ell)=(s/g)^{2}
.

Proof.

An element
\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_{j}\tau^{js}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\!\left\{\tau^{s}\right\}
commutes with
\tau^{s}
if and only if all
a_{j}
are in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}
. Moreover,
\alpha
commutes with all elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
if and only if

a_{j}\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ell\mid js.

Since
\ell\mid js
is equivalent to
n\mid js
, we conclude that
Z(\Delta(\ell,s))\subseteq K_{g}
. The reverse inclusion is clear. This proves (1).

We have
s\cdot\ell=n\cdot g
, so

\pi=\tau^{n}=(\tau^{s})^{\ell/g}.

Thus
\tau^{s}=\pi^{g/\ell}
and, as a
K
-algebra,
\Delta(\ell,s)
contains two linearly disjoint field extensions
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}K
and
K(\pi^{g/\ell})
of
K

of degrees
\ell
and
\ell/g
, respectively. Thus,

\dim_{K}\Delta(\ell,s)\geq\ell^{2}/g.

Similarly,
\dim_{K}\Delta(s,\ell)\geq s^{2}/g
. It is easy to see that
\Delta(s,\ell)\subseteq\Cent_{\Delta}(\Delta(\ell,s))
. By the Double Centralizer Theorem,

\dim_{K}\Delta=\dim_{K}\Delta(\ell,s)\cdot\dim_{K}\Cent_{\Delta}(\Delta(\ell,s)).

Thus,

n^{2}\geq(\ell^{2}/g)(s^{2}/g)=(\ell\cdot s/g)^{2}=n^{2},

so equalities must hold throughout. This proves (2) and (3).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0\neq u\in\Delta(s,\ell)
, let
L=K(u)
, and let
D(u)=\Cent_{\Delta}(u)
.

(1)

We have

\dim_{L}D(u)\geq(\ell/g)^{2}.

(2)

There is
u\in\Delta(s,\ell)
such that
\dim_{L}D(u)=(\ell/g)^{2}
. Moreover,

D(u)=L\otimes_{K_{g}}\Delta(\ell,s).

Proof.

Because
K
is the center of
\Delta
, we have
D(u)=\Cent_{\Delta}(L)
, so by the Double Centralizer Theorem (cf.

[
Pap23
, Thm. 1.7.22]
)
D(u)
is a central division algebra over
L
with

n^{2}=[D(u):K]\cdot[L:K].

Hence
\dim_{L}D(u)=(n/[L:K])^{2}
.

On the other hand, we have the inclusions
K\subseteq K_{g}\subset\Delta(s,\ell)
. Moreover,
[K_{g}:K]=g
and
[\Delta(s,\ell):K_{g}]=(s/g)^{2}
.
We proved that
K_{g}
is the center of
\Delta(s,\ell)
. Hence a maximal subfield
M
of
\Delta(s,\ell)
has degree
s/g
over
K_{g}
, and

[M:K]=s
. In particular,
[L:K]\leq s
. From this inequality we get

\dim_{L}D(u)\geq(n/s)^{2}=(\ell/g)^{2}.

This proves (1).

To prove (2), fix any maximal subfield
L\subset\Delta(s,\ell)
. Since
K
is a function field of transcendence degree
1
over a finite field, the Primitive Element Theorem (cf.
[
Pap23
, Thm. 1.5.19]
) implies the existence of
u
such that
L=K(u)
. For this
u
,
[L:K]=s
, so
\dim_{L}D(u)=(\ell/g)^{2}
.
On the other hand, we obviously have

L\otimes_{K_{g}}\Delta(\ell,s)\subseteq D(u).

Since the dimension of
\Delta(\ell,s)
over
K_{g}
is
(\ell/g)^{2}
, we have
\dim_{L}L\otimes_{K_{g}}\Delta(\ell,s)=(\ell/g)^{2}
. Comparing the
dimensions we conclude that
L\otimes_{K_{g}}\Delta(\ell,s)=D(u)
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that in Proposition
4.2
we can always scale
u
by an element of
K
to make it integral, i.e., to lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}\!\left\{\tau^{\ell}\right\}
,
without affecting the claims about the dimension of
D(u)
.

4.2.
Key technical lemma

Let
\phi\colon A\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be a Drinfeld module.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in A
be an irreducible monic polynomial not equal to
\chr_{A}(\phi)
.
Let
m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\pi
acting on the
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-vector space

\phi[\mathfrak{p}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\deg_{\tau}(\phi_{T})}
. We take the norm

\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))=m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)\cdot m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}^{(q)}(x)\cdots m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}^{(q^{\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{p})-1})}(x)

to obtain a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Recall that given
0\neq u\in\End(\phi)
, we denoted

L=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
,
\widetilde{L}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u,\pi)
, and
d_{u}=[\widetilde{L}:L]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If all
irreducible factors of
\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
have degrees
>d_{u}
, then

u
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there is
0\neq\alpha\in\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
is such that
u(\alpha)=0
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have a decomposition
\overline{m}_{u,T}(\pi)=wu
in
R
, and
d_{u}=\deg_{x}\overline{m}_{u,T}(x)

from definitions. Thus, we have
\overline{m}_{u,T}(\pi)(\alpha)=0
. This implies that the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span
of
\alpha,\pi(\alpha),\dots,\pi^{d_{u}-1}(\alpha)
is a
\pi
-invariant subspace of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of dimension
\leq d_{u}
.

On the other hand, the minimal polynomial of
\pi
acting on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
,
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space, is
\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))
. The previous paragraph implies that

\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))

has an irreducible factor of degree
\leq d_{u}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, which contradicts the assumption of the lemma.
∎

Let
m_{\phi}(x)
be the minimal polynomial of
\pi\in\End(\phi)
over
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\phi_{T})
. Then
m_{\phi}(x)

is a monic irreducible polynomial in
A[x]
of degree
d_{\phi}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi,\phi_{T}):\mathbb{F}_{q}(\phi_{T})]
.
Let
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
be the polynomial obtained from
m_{\phi}(x)
by reducing its coefficients
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
and
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(0)
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
If
d_{u}<d_{\phi}\cdot\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{p})
,
then
u
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
.

Proof.

Note that
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)=0
on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
, so
m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)
divides
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)
.
The irreducibility of this latter polynomial then implies that
m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)=\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)
.
Hence

\deg_{x}\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))=\deg_{x}(\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))\cdot\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{p})=d_{\phi}\cdot\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{p}).

We claim that
\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(m_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; assuming this, the invertibility of
u

on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
follows from Lemma
4.4
.
Let
g(x)
be a monic irreducible divisor of
\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Considering
g(x)
as a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
,
the irreducibility of all
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}^{(q^{i})}(x)
implies that
g(x)=\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}^{(q^{i})}(x)
for some
0\leq i\leq\deg(\mathfrak{p})-1
.
But then
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(0)^{q^{i}}
lies in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which contradicts the assumption that
\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(0)
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

4.3.
The construction of semifield codes

Recall that
\ell
and
s
are integers such that
n=\lcm(\ell,s)
, and we denoted

g=\gcd(\ell,s)
.

Define
\phi\colon A\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
by

(4.1)

\phi_{T}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}\tau^{\ell i},\qquad a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}},

so that the image of
\phi
lies in
\Delta(s,\ell)
and
\phi
has rank
r\ell
.

Set

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{e}b_{i}\tau^{si}\mid b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\right\}\subset\Delta(\ell,s)\subset\End(\phi).

Let
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
be a prime different from
\chr_{A}(\phi)
and let
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\deg(\mathfrak{p})
.
We want to give conditions on the prime
\mathfrak{p}
and the Drinfeld module
\phi
such that
the image of
\mathcal{M}
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\cong\Mat_{r\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a semifield code.

Since

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=(e+1)\ell,

to get a semifield code from
\mathcal{M}
we first of all need

r\ell d=(e+1)\ell
; see (
2.1
). Thus,

\boxed{e+1=rd}

Lemma 4.6
.

If
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}
, then
d_{u}\leq eg
.

Proof.

Define a Drinfeld module
\psi
over
k
by setting
\psi_{T}=u
. Applying Theorem 4.1.3 in
[
Pap23
]
to
\psi
, we deduce that

\widetilde{L}
is the center of
D=\Cent_{\Delta}(u)
and

\dim_{\widetilde{L}}(D)=\left(\frac{\deg_{\tau}(u)}{d_{u}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{se}{d_{u}}\right)^{2}.

On the other hand, since
u\in\Delta(\ell,s)
, Proposition
4.2
implies that

\dim_{\widetilde{L}}(D)\geq(s/g)^{2}
.
Thus,
d_{u}\leq eg
.
∎

Let
\widetilde{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\phi_{T},\pi)
and
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\phi_{T})
. Suppose

(1)

\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x)
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
,

(2)

\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(0)
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

(3)

eg<d\cdot[\widetilde{F}:F]
.

Then, by Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
, any
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
.

First, we examine the implication of (3). Applying Lemma
4.6
to
\phi
leads to

(4.2)

[\widetilde{F}:F]\leq rg.

On the other hand, from
eg<d[\widetilde{F}:F]
and
e+1=rd
, we get

(4.3)

eg<d[\widetilde{F}:F]=\frac{(e+1)}{r}[\widetilde{F}:F].

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we get

\frac{e}{e+1}rg<[\widetilde{F}:F]\leq rg.

We want to allow
d
(and thus
e
) to be arbitrarily large, so we are forced to assume that

\boxed{[\widetilde{F}:F]=rg}

By (
4.2
), this is the maximal possible degree for the extension
\widetilde{F}/F
. On the other hand, by
Proposition
4.2
, one can choose
\phi_{T}\in\Delta(s,\ell)
,
so that this equality holds. We assume that
\phi
is chosen to have this property.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{l}=\chr_{A}(\phi)
.
The
height
of
\phi
is defined as
H(\phi)=\Ht(\phi_{\mathfrak{l}})/\deg(\mathfrak{l})
. By
[
Pap23
, Lem. 3.2.11]
,
H(\phi)

is a positive integer. Now note that
\phi

defined by (
4.1
) can be considered as a
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}[T]
-Drinfeld module
\phi^{\prime}
of rank
r
.
Since
H(\phi)=\ell H(\phi^{\prime})
, we conclude that
\ell\leq H(\phi)
. On the the other hand, by
[
Pap23
, Prop. 4.1.8]
,

H(\phi)\geq r\ell/[\widetilde{F}:F]
. Thus, if
H(\phi)=\ell
, then
r\leq[\widetilde{F}:F]\leq rg
. This means that when
g=1
, without appealing to Proposition
4.2
,
we can choose
\phi
for which
[\widetilde{F}:F]=r
is maximal by choosing
\phi^{\prime}
to be
ordinary
, i.e., having
H(\phi^{\prime})=1
. Since “most” Drinfeld modules over finite fields are ordinary, such a choice is always possible.

It remains to show that there are primes
\mathfrak{p}
for which conditions (1) and (2) hold.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume
g=1
and
r
is not divisible by the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In addition, assume that either
r
is prime, or
that
r
is coprime to
s
. Then the Galois group of
m_{\phi}(x)
contains a cycle of maximal length
r
.

Proof.

Let
M
be the splitting field of
m_{\phi}(x)
. Since
r=\deg m_{\phi}(x)
is not divisible by the characteristic of
F
, the extension
M/F
is Galois.
We claim that
\Gal(M/F)
, as a subgroup of the permutation group
S_{r}
of the roots of
m_{\phi}(x)
, contains a cycle of length
r
.
We will show that the decomposition subgroup
G_{\infty}\subseteq\Gal(M/F)
at the place
\infty=1/T
contains such a cycle.

By
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.1.3]
,
m_{\phi}(x)
remains irreducible over the completion
F_{\infty}
of
F
. Let

M^{\prime}
be the splitting field of
m_{\phi}(x)
over
F_{\infty}
. Then
G_{\infty}\cong\Gal(M^{\prime}/F_{\infty})
.
Let
\widetilde{\infty}
be the unique place of
\widetilde{F}
over
\infty
, and let
\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}
be the completion of
\widetilde{F}
at
\widetilde{\infty}
. We have

r=[\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}:F_{\infty}]=e(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})f(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})
, where
e(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})
is the ramification index and
f(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})

is the residual degree.

If
r
is a prime, then either
e(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})=r
or
f(\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty})=r
. In the second case,
M^{\prime}=\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}
is an unramified extension of degree
r
, so
\Gal(M^{\prime}/F_{\infty})\cong\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
. In the first case,
\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty}
is a totally tamely ramified extension.
If
r
is not necessarily prime, we note that the normalized
valuations of the roots of
m_{\phi}(x)
at
\infty
are equal to
-n/r\ell=-s/r
; see
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.7]
.
Therefore, if
r
is coprime to
s
, then again this implies that
\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty}
is a totally tamely ramified extension.

We are reducing to showing that if
\widetilde{F}_{\widetilde{\infty}}/F_{\infty}
is a totally tamely ramified extension of degree
r
,
then
\Gal(M^{\prime}/F_{\infty})
contains a cycle of length
r
. By a well-known structure theorem for totally tamely ramified extensions
of local fields (cf.
[
Pap23
, Prop. 2.6.7]
),
M^{\prime}
is the splitting field of
x^{r}-\varpi_{\infty}
, where
\varpi_{\infty}

is a uniformizer of
F_{\infty}
. Therefore,
M^{\prime}
contains a primitive
r
-th root
\zeta_{r}
of
1
, and the automorpism
M^{\prime}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
defined by

\varpi_{\infty}^{1/r}\mapstochar\rightarrow\zeta_{r}\varpi_{\infty}^{1/r}
is a cycle of length
r
.
∎

Assume the Galois group of
m_{\phi}(x)
contains a cycle of length
rg
(this is always the case under the assumptions on
rg
of the previous lemma).
By the effective Chebotarev density theorem (cf.
[
KMS94
]
),
the number of primes in
A
of degree
N\gg 0
, which are unramified in the Galois closure of
\widetilde{F}/F
and whose corresponding Frobenius is in the conjugacy class of a maximal cycle in
\Gal(m_{\phi}(x))
, is
\geq Cq^{N}/N
for some nonzero constant
C
(not depending on
N
). Denote the set of such primes by
\mathcal{P}_{N}
.

For
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
the reduction of
m_{\phi}(x)
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
is irreducible (i.e., (1) holds), so we now concentrate on condition (2).
Let
\mathfrak{q}=\chr_{A}(\phi)
. By
[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.2]
,
P_{\phi}(x)=m_{\phi}(x)^{r\ell/rg}=m_{\phi}(x)^{\ell/g}
. Moreover, by

[
Pap23
, Thm. 4.2.7]
, up to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
-multiple,
P_{\phi}(0)
is equal to
\mathfrak{q}^{n/\deg(\mathfrak{q})}
. Hence, up to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
-multiple,

m_{\phi}(0)
is equal to
\mathfrak{q}^{s/\deg(\mathfrak{q})}
.

Denote
f(T)=\mathfrak{q}^{s/\deg(\mathfrak{q})}\in A
. Note that
\deg_{T}f=s
. Choose a root of each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and call the set of these roots
\mathcal{R}_{N}
. We have

\#\mathcal{R}_{N}\geq Cq^{N}/N
. We want to show that
f(T)
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for at least one
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
.
This is equivalent to requiring that for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{N}
, the value
f(\alpha)

does not belong to a proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}
.
The image of the map
\mathcal{R}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}
,
\alpha\mapstochar\rightarrow f(\alpha)
, has size at least
\#\mathcal{R}_{N}/\deg(f)
since the preimages of this map for
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}

are the roots of
f(x)-\beta
, and there at most
\deg(f)
such roots. Finally, note that the union of proper subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}
has size at most
O(q^{N/2})
.
Since

C\frac{q^{N}}{N\cdot s}>O(q^{N/2})

when
N
is sufficiently large, we see that we can always find
\mathfrak{p}
such that the properties (1) and (2) are satisfied.

4.4.
The nuclear parameters of the code

As in the previous subsection, let
r\geq 1
,
d=\deg\mathfrak{p}
,

\phi_{T}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}\tau^{\ell i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}\!\left\{\tau^{\ell}\right\},

and

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{rd-1}b_{i}\tau^{si}\mid b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\right\}.

Assume all nonzero elements of
\mathcal{M}
act invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
, so that
\mathcal{M}
gives a semifield code in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
.

Definition 4.9
.

Define
left (right) idealizer, centralizer, and center
of
\mathcal{M}
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{l}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\{f\in\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\mid f\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\},

\displaystyle I_{r}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\{f\in\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\mid\mathcal{M}f\subseteq\mathcal{M}\},

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\{f\in\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])\mid mf=fm\text{ for all }m\in\mathcal{M}\},

\displaystyle Z(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=I_{l}(\mathcal{M})\cap C(\mathcal{M}).

By
[
She20
, p. 436]
, each of these sets is a field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The
nuclear parameters
of
\mathcal{M}
is the tuple

(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M},\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}I_{l}(\mathcal{M}),\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}I_{r}(\mathcal{M}),\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}C(\mathcal{M}),\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}Z(\mathcal{M})).

By
[
She20
, Prop. 4]
, equivalent codes have the same nuclear parameters, so this tuple gives an
equivalence invariant of the code.

The “expected” parameters of
\mathcal{M}
are

\boxed{(rd\ell,\ell,\ell,rdg,g)}

More precisely, it is clear that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\subseteq I_{l}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}\subseteq I_{r}(\mathcal{M})
, and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{M})
.
The centralizer contains the image of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\phi_{T},\pi]
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
.
Recall that we are assuming that
\deg m_{\phi}(x)=rg
and
m_{\phi}(x)

is irreducible modulo
\mathfrak{p}
. Under these assumptions, we have

\mathbb{F}_{q}[T,\pi]/\mathfrak{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]/(\overline{m}_{\phi,\mathfrak{p}}(x))\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{rg}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{drg}}.

(Note that the image of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\phi_{T},\pi]
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
already contains
\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{M})\subseteq C(\mathcal{M})
.)
The expectation is that there are no sporadic elements in the idealizers and the centralizer, which in principle might occur because we are working modulo
\mathfrak{p}
. In any case, we have the following:

Lemma 4.10
.

If
s<\ell
, then

I_{l}(\mathcal{M})=I_{r}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}},\qquad Z(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}.

Proof.

Since
1\in\mathcal{M}
, if
f\in I_{l}(\mathcal{M})
, then we can assume that
f\in\mathcal{M}
. To prove that

I_{l}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
, it is enough to prove that
f
cannot have positive degree in
\tau
. Let
w=\deg_{\tau}(f)
.
Note that
w\leq(rd-1)s
and
s\mid w
.
If
w>0
, then
\tau^{srd-w}\in\mathcal{M}
but
f\tau^{srd-w}\not\in\mathcal{M}
, since
srd<\ell rd=\deg_{\tau}(\phi_{\mathfrak{p}})
. This
leads to a contradiction. The argument for
I_{r}(\mathcal{M})
is similar.
The elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
that commute with all elements of
\mathcal{M}
are those in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}
, so
Z(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{g}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume
m_{\phi}(x)
is irreducible modulo
\mathfrak{p}
,
g=1
, and
\ell
is prime. Then
C(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd}}
.

Proof.

We have already seen above that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd}}\subseteq C(\mathcal{M})
. On the other hand,
C(\mathcal{M})
is a field acting on

\phi[\mathfrak{p}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r\ell}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{rd\ell},

i.e.,
\phi[\mathfrak{p}]
is a vector space over
C(\mathcal{M})
, so
C(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd\ell}}
. If
C(\mathcal{M})
is strictly larger than

\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd}}
and
\ell
is prime, then
C(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd\ell}}
.
Denote the centralizer of
C(\mathcal{M})
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
by
C(C(\mathcal{M}))
. By
the Double Centralizer Theorem applied to
D=\Mat_{r\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})

(see
[
Rei03
, Cor. 7.13]
), we have

[D:\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}]=[C(\mathcal{M}):\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}]\cdot[C(C(\mathcal{M})):\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}].

This implies that
[C(C(\mathcal{M})):\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}]=r\ell
. On the other hand, we have
C(\mathcal{M})\subseteq C(C(\mathcal{M}))
, so
C(\mathcal{M})=C(C(\mathcal{M}))
.
Note that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq C(C(\mathcal{M}))
. Since
\dim\mathcal{M}=rd\ell=\dim C(\mathcal{M})
, we conclude that the image of
\mathcal{M}
in
\End(\phi[\mathfrak{p}])
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd\ell}}
; in particular, it is commutative. But
\tau^{\ell}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
do not commute modulo
\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so we get a contradiction. Therefore,
C(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{rd}}
.
∎

Example 4.12
.

Let
q=3
,
\ell=2
and
s=3
, so
n=6
. Let
\alpha
be a root of
x^{3}-x+1
, which is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Define

\phi_{T}=\alpha+\alpha^{2}\tau^{2}+\tau^{4}.

Using
Magma

[
BCP97
]
, it is not hard to check that

\mathcal{M}=\{b_{0}+b_{1}\tau^{3}\mid b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\}

gives a semifield code in
\End(\phi[T-1])\cong\Mat_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. (For this one just needs to check that all nonzero
b_{0}+b_{1}\tau^{3}

are coprime to
\phi_{T-1}=(\alpha-1)+\alpha^{2}\tau^{2}+\tau^{4}
in
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, which is easy to do using the division algorithm; cf.
[
Pap23
, Thm. 3.1.13]
.) Next, again using a Magma program, one verifies that no element
f
of
\mathcal{M}
of positive degree satisfies

f\mathcal{M}\pmod{\phi_{T-1}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}.

Thus,
I_{l}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and similarly
I_{r}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
By Lemma
4.11
,
C(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
Finally, the elements of
I_{l}(\mathcal{M})
that commute with all other elements are the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus,
Z(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We conclude that
the parameters of our semifield code are

(4,2,2,2,1).

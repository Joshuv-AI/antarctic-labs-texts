Protocol for EACH-ADHF trial: efficacy and safety of early comprehensive rehabilitation in patients with acute decompensated heart failure – a multicentre, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Mingyu Xu, Jinna Chang, Lan Guo, Bingqing Bai, Rou Wang, Xiangyang Liu, Qian Du, Ying Mao, Ailing Xue, Minting Su, Yanping Wang, Kaixuan Lin, Huan Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) is associated with high mortality rates and significant decline in physical function following hospitalisation. Although exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation is recognised as a key component of comprehensive heart failure management, clinical evidence supporting its application in patients with ADHF remains limited. The trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of an early initiated comprehensive rehabilitation in patients with ADHF.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, open-label, parallel-group clinical trial. A total of 140 patients hospitalised with ADHF will be enrolled and randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive either comprehensive rehabilitation intervention or attention control. The intervention group will receive a supervised, 6-week structured comprehensive rehabilitation programme consisting of progressive balance, endurance, strength and inspiratory muscle training. Rehabilitation therapy commences after admission and continues into the outpatient period with a total of 18 sessions. The control group will receive standard usual care supplemented by fortnightly non-rehabilitation-related contact from study personnel to control for attention. Coprimary outcomes are changes in the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire Overall Summary Score and in maximum inspiratory pressure expressed as a percentage of predicted value (PImax%pred) at 6 weeks postrandomisation. Secondary outcomes include changes in the Short Physical Performance Battery score, frailty phenotype, pulmonary function tests and 6-month all-cause rehospitalisation.Ethics and disseminationThe Early Comprehensive Rehabilitation in patients with ADHF (EACH-ADHF) trial was granted by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (KY-Q-2022-487). Findings will be disseminated to patients, clinicians and commissioning groups through peer-reviewed publication.Trial registration numberNCT06161987.

Introduction

Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) is one of the leading causes of emergency department admissions and hospital readmissions in cardiac patients.1 The 5-year all-cause mortality rate among patients with ADHF presenting to the emergency department reaches 55.4%.2 Even after successful treatment and symptom relief, approximately 30% of patients with ADHF experience readmission within 2 months after discharge.3 4 Critically, patients enter a high-risk vulnerable state following acute episodes, characterised by accelerated frailty progression, diaphragmatic dysfunction and elevated risks of pneumonia and dyspnoea.5 6 Concurrent psychological distress further diminishes quality of life (QoL) and independently predicts adverse outcomes.7 Mitigating this trajectory through early intervention is a therapeutic imperative.

Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (CR) consistently demonstrates benefits in stable patients with heart failure (HF), including improved exercise capacity, QoL, psychological status and reduced hospitalisation risk.811 However, its application in ADHF remains controversial and understudied. Most CR studies exclude recently hospitalised patients with HF due to theoretical safety concerns regarding increased myocardial oxygen demand.12 A recent trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine reported the safety of in-hospital rehabilitation initiation in elderly patients with ADHF.13 Nevertheless, significant evidence gaps persist regarding the efficacy of postdischarge comprehensive rehabilitation, the broader patient applicability beyond the elderly and the integration of multidomain interventions such as inspiratory muscle training (IMT).14

The rationale for combining exercise with IMT is robust. Diaphragm weakness is prevalent in ADHF and independently predicts exercise intolerance.15 16 IMT enhances respiratory muscle metaboreflex activity, improves ventilatory efficiency, alleviates dyspnoea and augments peripheral blood flow, thereby potentiating exercise tolerance;1719 no trials have evaluated combined exercise and IMT initiated early post-ADHF hospitalisation. Despite this mechanistic synergy, no trials have evaluated combined exercise and IMT initiated early post-ADHF hospitalisation.

To address these critical gaps, we designed the Early Comprehensive Rehabilitation in patients with ADHF (EACH-ADHF) trial, a prospective, multicentre, randomised controlled trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of a 6-week, early-initiated, comprehensive rehabilitation programme—integrating progressive balance, endurance, strength and IMT—compared with attention-controlled usual care in patients hospitalised with ADHF. We hypothesise that this intervention will significantly improve patient-centred outcomes (Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) score, inspiratory muscle strength) and physical function.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The EACH-ADHF trial is a prospective, multicentre, randomised controlled trial evaluating a 6-week early comprehensive rehabilitation programme in adults with ADHF at eight centres in China. The protocol was approved by ethics committees at all sites and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06161987). The list of all participating centres and ethics committees will be provided in online supplemental appendix 1. Recruitment is still ongoing, and enrolment is expected to close on 31 December 2026. All participants will provide written informed consent. This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Eligibility criteria and recruitment

We aim to enrol 140 patients with ADHF hospitalised for acute congestion with elevated B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or N-terminal pro-BNP levels. Key inclusion criteria are: patients must be independent in basic activities of daily living at admission, able to complete baseline assessments and begin the assigned treatment, and capable of walking 4 m at enrolment, with assistive devices if needed. To avoid the confounding influence of scheduled cardiac procedures on the study outcomes, patients who are planned to undergo a cardiac surgical intervention (including radiofrequency ablation, valve repair, coronary intervention, etc) within the next 6 months are excluded. If patients are eligible and interested in participating, they will be informed about the details of the study and provided with written informed consent. Randomisation and masking

After baseline assessments, eligible patients will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio using computer-generated block randomisation (block size of 4–6), stratified by site and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (<40% vs ≥40%) to the rehabilitation intervention group or attention control group. An independent coordinator at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital maintained allocation concealment using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. Site investigators revealed assignments only after baseline testing.

Due to the nature of the intervention, participants and treating clinicians were not blinded. However, outcome assessors were masked to group allocation. Despite this blinding of assessors, patient expectations could still influence self-reported outcomes in this open-label design. To mitigate this, we implemented several measures: standardised messaging was provided to all participants, emphasising neutral expectations about outcomes and the importance of adherence to the assigned treatment. Both the intervention and attention control groups received consistent information during the informed consent process. Additionally, participants in the attention control group were contacted every 2 weeks to minimise perceived differences in the level of care received.

Trial intervention

All patients will receive guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) according to the 2022 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology/Heart Failure Society of America Guideline for the Management of HF.20 This includes continuing and optimising preexisting GDMT after admission to improve outcomes, unless contraindicated. For patients with HF with reduced ejection fraction, GDMT should encompass, whenever possible, a renin–angiotensin system inhibitor—including an angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI), ACE inhibitor (ACEI) or angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB)—along with a beta-blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) and an SGLT-2 inhibitor. For those with HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction, SGLT-2 inhibitors, ARNI, ACEI/ARB, MRA and beta-blockers may be considered. In patients with HF with preserved ejection fraction, SGLT-2 inhibitors, MRA and ARNI can be considered based on prior medical history and the individual clinical profile.

Attention control group

Participants allocated to this group will receive usual care, supplemented by fortnightly telephone follow-ups during the 6-week intervention period. These calls, conducted by study personnel, will monitor general health status, medication adherence, self-reported physical activity and clinical events. Critically, no structured exercise guidance or respiratory training advice will be provided during these contacts, ensuring the sole purpose was attention control.

Rehabilitation intervention group

Participants allocated to the rehabilitation group will receive a 6-week supervised and individualised programme totalling 18 sessions (3 sessions per week). Each session (55–65 min) commences with a 10-minute warm-up (stretching, slow walking), followed by 25–35 min of core exercise training tailored to the participant’s functional capacity as quantified by the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) score. Balance training will progress from shoulder-width stance (SPPB 0) to tandem stance (SPPB 4), performed in 5 sets of 30–60 s with 1-minute rest. Endurance training will comprise metronome-paced walking at cadences incrementing from 60 bpm (SPPB 1) to 150 bpm (SPPB 4) in five sets of 30 s with 1-minute rest; participants with SPPB 0 will not receive endurance training. Resistance training will include sit-to-stand repetitions (3–5 reps/set), with set numbers escalating from 3 (SPPB 1) to 10 (SPPB 4) based on SPPB score. This will be followed by 10 min of IMT using a threshold loading device set at 30% of baseline maximal inspiratory pressure (PImax), comprising 60 breathing manoeuvres per session. Sessions conclude with a 10-minute cool-down (stretching). Exercise intensity will be targeted Borg rating of perceived exertion (RPE) 11–14 for physical components and RPE 13 for IMT. Functional assessment and training progression

Functional capacity is rigorously assessed at baseline and fortnightly using: (1) PImax, measured at residual volume via digital manometer (highest value from ≥3 efforts); (2) SPPB, a standardised 0–12 point battery evaluating balance, 4-meter gait speed and 5-repetition chair stand time.2123 Participants will be stratified into one of predefined training levels corresponding to their SPPB score (table 1). Training protocols were dynamically upgraded to the next level immediately on detection of an SPPB score increase during fortnightly reassessments. For instance, progression from SPPB 1 (level 1: balance + 60 bpm walking + 3×3 sit-stands) to SPPB 2 triggered advancement to level 2 protocols (semitandem balance + 75 bpm walking + 5×5 sit-stands).

Baseline assessments should be completed as soon as possible after enrolment. Patients who are able to walk 4 m during hospitalisation and meet all other inclusion and exclusion criteria may be enrolled while still in the hospital. Those allocated to the rehabilitation intervention group can initiate rehabilitation during the inpatient stay, with continuation into the outpatient phase. Some patients only achieve the ability to walk 4 m after discharge; therefore, their study participation, baseline assessment and initiation of rehabilitation training will begin postdischarge. However, this must occur within 2 weeks after discharge, as patients beyond this timeframe may have substantially recovered from the ‘acute heart failure’ state.

Safety monitoring

All sessions will be directly supervised by certified rehabilitation staff. Mandatory presession vital sign assessments include resting heart rate (HR), blood pressure (BP) and oxygen saturation (SpO₂). Sessions will be immediately terminated if any of the following occur: resting HR >100 bpm, systolic/diastolic BP >160/100 mm Hg, SpO₂ <90% or participant-reported angina, severe dyspnoea (Borg RPE >17), dizziness or undue fatigue. Adverse events (AEs) (eg, arrhythmias, falls, syncope, HF exacerbation) will be systematically documented. IMT intensity will remain fixed at 30% of PImax throughout the study to mitigate respiratory muscle fatigue risk.

During rehabilitation training, patients undergo continuous HR and ECG monitoring via remote telemetry, while BP and SpO2 are assessed intermittently. These parameters are measured once at unscheduled intervals during each training segment. Additionally, if a patient reports any discomfort, BP and SpO2 are checked immediately. If remote telemetry indicates tachyarrhythmia or atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, the training session is terminated immediately. A standard 12-lead ECG is obtained promptly, continuous cardiac monitoring is initiated and BP is measured. HR control is managed according to routine clinical protocols, which may include oral beta-blockers or intravenous digoxin. Medication adjustments are made every 30–60 min based on the rhythm and HR observed on the monitor.

If the malignant arrhythmia is newly diagnosed (including new-onset atrial flutter, atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation or high-degree atrioventricular block), it is classified as a serious AE (SAE). In such cases, further rehabilitation training is discontinued, though data continue to be collected per the scheduled follow-up visits. If the arrhythmia is not new-onset, the current session is stopped; provided the patient is stable at the next scheduled session, rehabilitation training may be resumed.

Follow-up schedule

Participants undergo serial evaluations at four timepoints: baseline assessments occur during index hospitalisation or within 14 days postdischarge; 6-week outcomes are collected during in-person visits; 12-week and 24-week follow-ups are conducted via structured telephone interviews to document vital status, medication adherence and hospitalisation events. Rehospitalisation is prospectively defined as any inpatient stay ≥24 hours, including emergency observation admissions. The comprehensive assessment schedule is detailed in table 2.

Primary outcomes

We are using both patient-reported outcomes and objective functional measures to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the intervention’s effects. The two coprimary outcomes quantifying intervention efficacy at 6 weeks are: (1) The change in KCCQ Overall Summary Score (ΔKCCQ-OSS), a validated patient-reported metric (0–100 scale; higher scores indicate better health status) capturing HF-specific health status across seven domains including symptom frequency, symptom burden, symptom stability, physical limitations, social limitations, QoL and self-efficacy.24 25 (2) The change in PImax percentage predicted value (ΔPImax%pred), calculated using sex-specific reference equations26:

Male: Predicted PImax (cmH2O) = 143–0.55×age (year)

Female: Predicted PImax (cmH2O) = 104–0.51×age (year)

PImax%pred=Actual PImax/Predicted PImax×100%

Secondary outcomes

Key secondary outcomes included: SPPB score, a modified version of the frailty phenotype as described by Fried,27 pulmonary function tests and 6-month all-cause rehospitalisation. Prespecified exploratory outcomes encompassed: QoL assessed by Short Form 12-item Questionnaire, psychological status, echocardiography parameters, 6-month composite cardiovascular events (death or rehospitalisation), AEs, biomarker and proteomic profiles. All-cause mortality and cardiovascular-specific endpoints were adjudicated by an independent endpoint committee.

In addition, changes in the primary and secondary endpoints will be examined in pre-specified subgroups, including LVEF category (<40% vs ≥ 40%) and frailty phenotype.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using the professional Power Analysis and Sample Size software, with a two-sided α of 0.05 and power of 80%. Based on previous literature reports, the mean KCCQ-OSS score at 3 months was 69 ± 2 in the intervention group and 62 ± 2 in the control group, and the corresponding SPPB score was 8.3 ± 0.2 and 6.9 ± 0.2.13 Based on this effect size, this requires 56 evaluable participants. Additionally, for the PImax%pred, referring to a similar study the control group had a PImax%pred of 70.8 ± 16.4 and the intervention group 88.2 ± 21.3 at 4 weeks,28 the estimated sample size was 20 participants per group. Considering a 20% loss to follow-up, resulting in a final sample size of 140. In summary, this trial will randomise a total of 140 patients, ensuring more than 80% power to evaluate the primary and secondary outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise the demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population. Continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD for normally distributed variables or median (IQR) for non-normally distributed variables, while categorical variables will be represented as frequencies and percentages. Following the intent-to-treat principle, patients will be analysed according to the group to which they were assigned at randomisation. Primary outcomes will be tested using analysis of covariance, adjusting for baseline age, sex, LVEF category and baseline measure. To control for type I error, Bonferroni’s correction will be used adjusting the significance level to a p value <0.025 for each of the coprimary endpoints. Rank variables will be analysed using the Wilcoxon test, and qualitative variables will be analysed using the Pearson χ² test. Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be generated to estimate the time-to-event distributions for the secondary outcomes, and differences between the groups will be compared using the log-rank test. Cox regression will be performed to identify independent predictors of survival.

Missing data will be handled with multiple imputation. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted on the primary and secondary outcomes to assess the robustness of the results. Subgroup analyses based on prespecified demographic and clinical characteristics, along with multivariable regression models adjusting for potential confounders, will be used to explore treatment effects.

In the trial plan of this study, we intend to perform an interim analysis after recruiting 50% of the participants. The prespecified interim analysis in our protocol was designed solely for safety monitoring and was not intended for hypothesis testing regarding efficacy. Because no formal statistical hypothesis testing for efficacy endpoints was performed at the interim stage, no alpha-spending adjustment was applied. The interim analysis was conducted by an independent Data Monitoring Committee and focused primarily on the incidence of AEs and SAEs. According to the predefined stopping rules, the trial would be terminated early for safety concerns if the incidence of SAEs in the rehabilitation group was significantly higher than that in the control group. The results of the interim analysis will be kept confidential and will only be shared with the trial sponsor and the ethics committee. This interim analysis will help to ensure the safety and integrity of the study and may help to identify any potential issues early on in the trial.

Discussion

The EACH-ADHF study is a multicentre, parallel-group, randomised controlled trial designed to compare the effects of comprehensive rehabilitation including exercise and respiratory training on the physical function in patients with ADHF. The primary outcomes of interest are improvements in KCCQ-OSS and PImax%pred, while secondary endpoints include the impact on QoL, cardiac function, psychological status and major adverse cardiovascular events. Additionally, the study will also explore the effects of comprehensive rehabilitation on rehospitalisation and mortality rates, with follow-up assessments planned up to 6 months after discharge.

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective randomised controlled trial study to combine exercise training and breath training in the rehabilitation of patients with ADHF. Breath training is a highly promising rehabilitation approach in patients with ADHF, as it is simple, safe and can be adjusted according to individual abilities, making it particularly suitable for patients who are weak after an acute HF episode. Compared with the Rehabilitation Therapy in Older Acute Heart Failure Patients (REHAB-HF) study,13 our study population includes patients aged 18–80 years, with the aim of providing more evidence for patients with acute HF of all ages after discharge. Our rehabilitation programme is set to last for 6 weeks, rather than the usual 3 months of CR, as we hope to explore a short-term training programme that can improve physical function in a short period of time. In regions where the concept of CR is not widely popularised, such as China, there is an urgent need for a scientifically effective short-term training programme that can attract patients to start rehabilitation. We believe that this will ultimately help patients join long-term CR programmes and promote their long-term health.

This study has some limitations. First, due to the nature of the interventions, this study is an open-label trial. However, we have established an independent outcome assessment team to collect endpoint information from patients, and they will be blinded to the group allocation to minimise bias. Second, because of the differences in follow-up between the rehabilitation and control group, the control group may have fewer opportunities for active medical care, which could affect the patient’s readmission and mortality rates. Therefore, we have avoided setting readmission and mortality rates as the primary outcomes and will collect information on patient medication use during follow-up to help us conduct sensitivity and subgroup analyses. Third, to ensure participant safety given the limited evidence for ADHF rehabilitation, we excluded patients with haemodynamically unstable, poorly rate-controlled atrial fibrillation. This safety-driven exclusion may have introduced selection bias, and our findings may therefore not be generalisable to patients with ADHF and comorbid atrial fibrillation. Nonetheless, based on the experience gained from this trial, we plan to conduct future studies to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rehabilitation specifically in this population.

Ethics and dissemination

All study procedures adhere rigorously to the latest Declaration of Helsinki and pertinent ethical principles. The study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (KY-Q-2022-487), and the protocol has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06161987). Participants will be comprehensively informed about the study’s objectives, eligibility requirements, procedures, potential risks and expected benefits. Written informed consent will be obtained from study participants prior to their enrolment and all collected information will be treated as confidential. An example of the informed consent form is available in online supplemental appendix 2 (see online supplemental appendix 3 for an English-language version). The findings of this study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed scientific publications and communicated to relevant stakeholders—including patients, healthcare professionals, policymakers and the general public—using appropriate and accessible channels.

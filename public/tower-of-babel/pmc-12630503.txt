Exploring the evolving role of apolipoproteins in oncology: global trends and emerging frontiers
Authors: Dianzhe Tian, Zixuan Hu, Zuyi Yang, Lvyuxing Zhao, Haitao Zhao, Xinting Sang, Shunda Du, Yunping Luo, Lei Zhang, Yiyao Xu, Xin Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetabolic reprogramming, especially lipid metabolism, is crucial in cancer progression and treatment resistance. Apolipoproteins are crucial targets for research as they regulate autophagy, oxidative stress, and chemoresistance. A systematic bibliometric and bioinformatics approach is necessary to identify trends and elucidate the molecular mechanisms linking apolipoproteins to cancer.MethodsA systematic bibliometric analysis was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection and PubMed. VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the Bibliometrix R package were employed to analyze and visualize co-authorship networks, keyword trends, and other key metrics. Bioinformatics analysis integrated protein–protein interaction network construction, hub gene identification, and enrichment analysis using R-based pipelines.ResultsChina has led the international research on apolipoproteins and cancer since 2015. Research has shifted from molecular studies to clinical applications, highlighting the roles of apolipoproteins in cancer risk, progression, and prognosis. Bioinformatic analysis identified key genes represented by APOA1 (apolipoprotein A1), APOE (apolipoprotein E), APOA2 (apolipoprotein A2), and ALB (Albumin) as central regulators in lipid localization, cholesterol metabolism, insulin resistance, and the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) signaling pathways.ConclusionsThis study combines bibliometric and bioinformatic approaches to explore apolipoprotein research in cancer. The research trend revealed apolipoproteins with the most research potential in a specific cancer type, as confirmed in the clinical trials. Bioinformatic research found the key genes regulating several essential lipid-related pathways. This article clearly outlined the research landscape and frontiers of this field by combining various databases and methods, highlighting the significant potential of apolipoproteins in the development of novel cancer medications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-025-06360-1.

Introduction

The recognition of metabolic reprogramming as a hallmark of cancer has sparked a surge of interest in oncology research (Faubert et al. 2020; Ward and Thompson 2012; Jin et al. 2023). Central to this growing focus is the study of lipid metabolism (Bian et al. 2021). Enhanced lipid synthesis and storage, a key feature of metabolic reprogramming, is now recognized as a hallmark of malignancy (Cheng et al. 2018). Furthermore, the uptake of free fatty acids by CD36+ cancer cells can initiate a metastasis cell subpopulation, which gets energy from the fatty acid oxidation (FAO) pathway (Cao 2019). As evolutionarily conserved mediators of lipid transport and metabolism (Wong et al. 2022), apolipoproteins have long been recognized for their role in cardiovascular diseases (Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration, 2009; Kastelein et al. 2008). However, mounting evidence suggests that apolipoprotein dysregulation is also associated with several types of malignancies, including breast (Yiu et al. 2010; Cedó et al. 2019; Chang et al. 2005), lung (Ko et al. 2014; Shi et al. 2018), and prostate (Ifere et al. 2013; Bancaro et al. 2023; Hall et al. 2004) cancers. This linkage occurs through various pathways, including autophagy modulation (Yang et al. 2024a; Levy et al. 2017), oxidative stress regulation (Morry et al. 2017), and chemoresistance development (Yiu et al. 2010; Wan et al. 2024). By unraveling the complex interplay between apolipoprotein-mediated lipid regulation and oncogenic signaling pathways, researchers can identify novel biomarkers and design targeted strategies that may potentially improve patient outcomes.

Key knowledge gaps in apolipoprotein research include the evolution of conceptual frameworks, geographic disparities, and the disconnect between mechanistic studies and clinical applications. A systematic bibliometric approach is necessary to map these gaps, utilizing publication data, citation networks, and collaboration dynamics to identify trends and emerging hotspots (Hicks et al. 2015; Jiang et al. 2023). This approach offers a more efficient and objective way to navigate the vast and complex literature, overcoming the limitations of traditional manual reviews (Agarwal et al. 2016; Donthu 2021). Although several publications have reported links between cancer and specific apolipoproteins, a comprehensive investigation that fully integrates these two areas is still missing. An additional innovation of our study lies in its combined bibliometric and bioinformatic approach, which aimed to build a knowledge map, explore underlying molecular mechanisms, and establish a theoretical basis for future multimodal diagnostic strategies and intervention frameworks.

The bibliometric analysis was established by systematically removing duplicates using reference management software EndNote 20, yielding 977 non-redundant publications. Two primary researchers (DZT and ZYY) independently conducted blinded screening based on predefined exclusion criteria, which resulted in the removal of non-oncological studies (n = 465, 47.6%) and non-apolipoprotein functional research (n = 102, 10.4%). This process yielded 410 eligible records, reflecting a 42.0% retention rate. 29 records cause discrepancies between DZT and ZYY (3.0% of the initial dataset), all of which were successfully resolved under the supervision of the principal investigator (LZ).

WoS is a multidisciplinary database, whereas PubMed, originating from the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM), focuses on clinical aspects. Consequently, we employed PubMed as an addition, enabling federated analysis across multiple databases. PubMed is particularly well-suited for aggregating clinical research data, offering comprehensive access to clinical trials, which serves as a great supplement to the WoS, thereby facilitating the synthesis of evidence in clinical fields. A systematic search for apolipoprotein-related cancer research using the Clinical Trial filter in PubMed was conducted to assess the progress of clinical research, resulting in 25 eligible studies. All studies were rigorously screened for relevance to the application of apolipoproteins in cancer research, and eventually, 10 clinical trials were selected for display. All these 10 studies show the relationship between at least one type of apolipoprotein with cancer. Software for bibliometric analysis

In this study, software such as R (Ihaka and Gentleman 1996) (version 4.2.2), VOSviewer (Eck and Waltman 2010, 2007) (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (Chen 2004, 2006) (version 6.4.R1) were utilized as tools for performing the bibliometric analysis. Bibliometrix (Aria et al. 2016) R package (version 4.1.4) was used to calculate the frequency of collaboration between countries. Based on VOSviewer, we calculated the number of publications, citations, and keyword frequency. Co-occurrence networks of keywords from scientific literature were constructed and visualized using the software’s embedded clustering algorithm. This tool primarily focuses on co-authorship and co-occurrence analysis. If keywords exhibited similar meanings, they were merged using VOSviewer. VOSviewer and the Bibliometrix R package were used to analyze country, institution, and author collaborations (Eck and Waltman 2010). This study used CiteSpace to identify keywords that gained significant citations during a specified period. The online bibliometrics website made our visualization of international collaborations possible (https://bibliometric.com/). Microsoft Excel was used to analyze the exponential growth in the number of publications (Jiang et al. 2023).

Bioinformatics analysis

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

The apolipoprotein-related genes and cancer-associated genes were retrieved from the GeneCards database (Stelzer et al. 2016) using search terms “apolipoprotein” and “cancer” (version 5.12, https://www.genecards.org/, every protein is screened with a relevance score > 5). PPI networks were constructed using the STRING database (Kanehisa et al. 2010) with a high confidence level (version 12.0, https://string-db.org, confidence threshold = 0.9). Human-specific (Homo sapiens) interactomes were retrieved using the STRINGdb R package (version 2.10.0, https://bioconductor.org/packages/STRINGdb/) with Ensembl gene IDs, followed by the removal of disconnected nodes to optimize topological clarity. The resulting networks were imported into Cytoscape (Yu et al. 2012) (version 3.9.1) for advanced visualization and computational analysis. Centrality analysis was conducted via Cytoscape’s plugin cytoHubba Maximum Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm (Chin et al. 2014) to identify hub genes and construct the sub-network. The Date of accession for all databases used in the PPI network analysis is September 29, 2025.

Functional and disease ontology enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology (GO) terms (including Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), Cellular Component (CC)) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (Kanehisa et al. 2010) pathways was performed using the R package clusterProfiler (version 4.4.4) (Yu et al. 2012). Gene symbols were converted to Entrez IDs (Maglott et al. 2007) via the org.Hs.eg.db annotation database (version 3.17.0, https://bioconductor.org/packages/org.Hs.eg.db/) to ensure compatibility with human (Homo sapiens) genomic resources. Enrichment parameters included a Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) adjusted p-value threshold of < 0.05 to control the false discovery rate, with hypergeometric testing applied to identify statistically overrepresented terms (Yu et al. 2012). For disease ontology (DO) enrichment, the DOSE package (Zamanian-Daryoush et al. 2013) (version 3.26.0) was used to calculate term similarities using the Jaccard index, followed by hierarchical clustering (Ward’s minimum variance method) to group related disease terms. Redundant terms were pruned at a tree height cutoff of 0.85. This operation is primarily intended to eliminate information redundancy caused by highly similar disease terms and to avoid the presence of duplicate or overly overlapping disease categories in the final enrichment results. While maintaining the integrity of core disease enrichment information, significantly enhance the conciseness and readability of the results to support the focused interpretation of key disease-associated pathways. Functional and disease enrichment results were visualized using ggplot2 (Wickham 2009) (version 3.4.4), with node sizes scaled to gene counts and color gradients mapped to the significance levels. All analyses were conducted in R (Ihaka and Gentleman 1996) (version 4.2.2) under standardized computational protocols to ensure reproducibility. The Date of accession for all databases used in functional and DO enrichment analysis is September 29, 2025. Discussion

Bibliometric characteristics

Advancements across multiple fields drive the shift in research focus. High-throughput lipidomic profiling has enabled the discovery of apolipoproteins as dynamic biomarkers for tumor staging and monitoring (Agarwala et al. 2022; Hornburg et al. 2023). Simultaneously, the immunotherapy revolution, particularly Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved checkpoint inhibitors, spurred research on apolipoprotein-mediated immunomodulation, enhancing T-cell infiltration in immunosuppressive tumor environments. Coordinated funding initiatives also promoted multi-omics exploration of cancer-related metabolic reprogramming, highlighting lipid metabolism as an emerging hallmark of cancer (Martin-Perez et al. 2022; Snaebjornsson et al. 2020). These developments led to multi-center validation studies, solidifying apolipoproteins as dual diagnostic and therapeutic targets in precision oncology.

The most highly cited journal, Cancer Research, has the highest IF (IF = 12.5) among the top 10 journals published, indicating that it simultaneously possesses both high quality and quantity. Thus, the ideas and results in this journal can reveal a relatively promising direction in the field of cancer and apolipoproteins.

Among the top 10 cited manuscripts, four focus on how apolipoproteins influence cancer and their potential as markers, while two are clinical studies examining their connection to cancer or its aftermath. Additionally, two manuscripts on apolipoproteins and atherosclerosis indicate a possible link between cancer and cardiovascular diseases. The second most cited article, published in March 2004 in Cancer Research by Trougakos, IP et al., affiliated with the National Hellenic Research Foundation, has 191 citations. It used small interfering RNA (siRNA) to demonstrate that silencing APOJ gene expression can induce apoptosis and decrease the resistance of cancer cells to chemotherapy. The article published in 2005 by Chen, YC et al. is the 4th most co-cited and the 7th most cited, confirming that inhibition of APOE expression by siRNA technology can arrest the cell cycle in the G2 phase and induce apoptosis in cancer cells. The 5-year follow-up of patients also confirmed this pro-cancer effect of APOE. The most co-cited authors, Hoving, Saske, Heeneman, Sylvia, Stewart, and Fiona A., have done extensive work in collaboration to confirm the anti-atherosclerotic effect of APOE.

Research hot spots and frontiers

Co-occurrence mapping combined with keyword clustering techniques provides a solid framework for identifying main research areas through frequency-weighted lexical patterns. This approach systematically outlines disciplinary hotspots by revealing terminological patterns that reflect shared research priorities, while also detecting emerging conceptual boundaries that indicate new areas of investigation directions (Chen 2004; Chen et al. 2010). The key term “apolipoprotein E” appeared frequently across various themes. Increasing attention is given to “biomarker validation,” “inflammatory microenvironment,” and new apolipoprotein subtypes, indicating a shift toward immuno-oncology and clinical applications. Analysis of the field’s development shows a transition from early molecular research to more clinical studies. Recent keyword surges, such as “inflammation,” “survival,” and “metabolism,” highlight emerging research areas in cancer immunology and metabolism. Co-occurrence analysis reveals strong links between apolipoproteins and metabolic pathways, suggesting that “apolipoproteins,” “inflammation,” and “immune modulation” will be key areas of future research.

Hot spot 1: genetic polymorphisms and apolipoprotein-related cancer risks

The polymorphism of APOE is the most established, which can influence susceptibility and prognosis. APOE has 3 alleles, APOEε2, APOEε3, and APOEε4. Several studies revealed an association between APOEε4 and the risk of breast cancer in Asian populations (Chang et al. 2005; Moysich et al. 2000; Saadat 2012). It can modify the relationship between high serum triglycerides and breast cancer risk (Saadat 2012). However, the serum triglycerides of patients with APOEε4 are significantly reduced in response to tamoxifen (Chang et al. 2009). Research from Taiwan (Chang et al. 2005; Wu et al. 2012) has shown that the HER2/neu-negative status is correlated with APOEε4. In the progression of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), the overexpression of APOE-LRP8/VLDLR is observed in TNBC cells, which can promote the growth and survival of cancer cells in vitro. The APOE4 also has the most substantial growth-stimulatory effect (Shiang et al. 2011).

Ostendorf, BN et al. demonstrated that the melanoma in the mice carrying APOE4 grew the most slowly and spread the least compared with the other isoforms. The following experiments revealed that the APOEε4 allele enhances resistance to cancer by activating the immune system. Compared to APOEε4 mice, the tumors in APOEε4 mice have a stronger ability to recruit CD45+ leukocytes, and the activity of NK cells and CD8+ T cells is also higher. The same result was obtained by Nneoma Adaku et al. (Adaku et al. 2023) in 2023, who further found that the difference in melanoma progression between different APOE alleles is mediated via the low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1).

An experiment using human prostate adenocarcinoma cells concluded that cells with the APOE(ε2/ε4) genotype exhibit high expression of cav-1 simultaneously, which leads to the intracellular accumulation of cholesterol. The high cholesterol cells were remarkably more aggressive and more likely to cause advanced prostate cancer compared to the cells that had at least one ε3 allele (Ifere et al. 2013). Wild-type APOE3 can remove cholesterol from the cells, and the loss of APOE function increases the risk of malignant carcinoma. The APOE can bind to the triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2 (TREM2) and induce the senescence of neutrophils (Bancaro et al. 2023) through the DAP12(DNAX-activating protein of 12 kDa)/SYK(Spleen Tyrosine kinase) pathway. The high expression of APOE and TREM2 correlates with a poor prognosis of prostate cancer.

Previous research on APOE polymorphism suggests that each APOE allele has distinct effects on patients. The potential of those alleles to serve as cancer biomarkers or therapy targets, as well as the deeper relationship between each isoform and cancer, is worth exploring further through additional research.

Hot spot 2: in-vivo models and mechanistic insights into APOE-related cancer progression and inflammation

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy is a relatively safe and effective cancer treatment method. However, patients with high myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) show no response to that therapy. Activation of liver-X nuclear receptor (LXR) can promote the transcription of APOE and mediate the activation of the cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) to deplete the MDSCs. The APOE also binds to the low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 8 (LRP8), which is expressed on MDSCs, leading to the depletion of these cells. Tavazoie conducted the research above, and Masoud et al. demonstrated that the LXR/APOE axis is a target that enhances the effect of immune therapy (Tavazoie et al. 2018).

ICIs are also used in TNBC patients, but some patients still do not respond to them. It has been discovered that non-responding patients have a higher level of APOE+ macrophages, which can interact with CD8+ Tex cells, which is one reason for therapy failure. The APOE inhibitor may enhance the therapeutic effect of ICIs.

APOE is also related to inflammatory reactions in pathological conditions. It has been discovered that the absence of APOE will enhance the expression of inflammatory markers in macrophages, which may contribute to the progression of atherosclerosis. The complex inflammatory responses and the heterogeneity of these responses between different cancers remind us that the effect of APOE on specific groups of immune cells is a promising and necessary area of research.

In papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) cells, APOE mRNA, the target of m6A modification, can promote the glycolysis of cancer cells and favor their proliferation. Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 2(IGF2BP2) can read and bind to the m6A site of APOE mRNA, thereby stabilizing it. The fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO), a m6A demethylase, can inhibit the proliferation of cancer cells, and it is downregulated in PTC cells. The downstream signal of the FTO/APOE axis is through the IL(Interleukin)-6/JAK(Janus kinase)2/STAT(Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription)3 pathway to promote glycolysis. Therefore, the FTO analog or APOE inhibitor can be a promising medicine for cancer. (Huang et al. 2022).

Hot spot 3: apolipoprotein biomarkers in the diagnosis and prognosis of cancers

A cluster of keywords, including “diagnosis,” “expression,” “mechanism,” and “biomarker,” among others (see Fig. 6B), suggests the potential of apolipoproteins as biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of cancers. In gastric cancer, studies have identified Apolipoprotein C1 (APOC1) as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, with elevated APOC1 expression associated with a poor prognosis, suggesting its role in tumor progression (Yi et al. 2019). Research also indicates that Apolipoprotein A-I (APOA1) levels are downregulated in patients with gastric cancer (GC). Lower serum APOA1 levels correlate with a poor prognosis, highlighting its potential as a biomarker for early detection and outcome prediction (Li et al. 2024). A high level of APOD in gastric cancer patients is proven to correlate with a worse prognosis (p = 0.013) and less response to immune therapy (Wang et al. 2024). There are changes in the expression of APOE-related genes in gastric cancer, including the upregulation of STAT2 and STAT3 (Shi et al. 2015). APOE is also elevated in GC tissues, relating to immune activation and infiltration of GC, and is strongly associated with poor survival outcomes (Peng et al. 2023). The tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are the only cell type synthesizing APOE in the gastric cancer microenvironment (Miao et al. 2023). In ovarian cancer, elevated expression of APOC1 has been linked to poorer overall survival in patients, indicating its potential as a prognostic marker (Yang et al. 2024b). At the same time, the overexpression of human APOA1 has been associated with improved overall survival in ovarian cancer models, suggesting its protective role and potential as a therapeutic target (Su et al. 2010). APOB100 functioned as a tumor suppressor in ovarian cancer. Lower APOB mRNA levels were significantly associated with a poorer prognosis. Its overexpression can inhibit cell proliferation and autophagy (Yin et al. 2025). These findings underscore the importance of apolipoproteins as biomarkers in gastric and ovarian cancers, offering insights into disease mechanisms and potential avenues for early detection and targeted therapies.

Hot spot 4: lipid metabolism, apolipoproteins, and metabolic syndrome in gastrointestinal tumors

Sterol-regulatory element binding proteins (SREBPs) are transcription factors that regulate lipid synthesis. Its activation through PKB/Akt will induce the enzyme involved in the cholesterol and fatty acid synthesis pathway (Porstmann et al. 2005). High blood cholesterol is a dangerous factor for HCC. The high expression of SREBPs will increase the production of cholesterol. Secreted protein acidic and rich in cysteine (SPARC) can bind to APOE and block its connection with an E3 enzyme TRIM21 (ligase tripartite motif containing 21) so that the ubiquitination of it is stopped (Wan et al. 2024). APOC3 transgenic mice with high APOC3 expression in HCC exhibited hyperlipidemia and increased infiltration of CD8+ T cells. The increased antitumor activity depends on T cell activation (Hu et al. 2023). The accumulation of APOE can cause hypercholesterolemia, which is related to sorafenib resistance (Wan et al. 2024). While the APOB has a contrary effect, every 0.1 g/L increase in circulating APOB level causes an 11% decrease in liver cancer risk (Liu et al. 2024). APOM is specifically expressed in the liver and regulates the liver’s lipid metabolism. It was revealed that mouse liver cancer cells lacking APOM are more invasive, and the overexpression of APOM can inhibit proliferation, migration, and invasion, while also stimulating apoptosis of cancer cells (Bai et al. 2021). APOF is downregulated in HCC progression. The decrease in its expression is associated with a poor prognosis for patients. Migration and proliferation of cancer cells are inhibited by APOF both in vitro (p < 0.05) and in vivo (p < 0.001) (Wang et al. 2019).

Apolipoproteins also influence lipid metabolism in colorectal cancer. APOB and APOA-I levels are significantly lower in colorectal cancer patients than LDL-C. The total cholesterol (TCH) and total triglycerides (TG) levels of TNM stage III and IV patients are significantly reduced, and HDL-C level is increased in cancerous tissues and serum compared to the TNM stages I and II patients of colorectal cancer (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01) (Zhang et al. 2014). Another study shows that the APOB/APOA-I ratio is a decisive prognostic factor (HR = 107.07, p = 0.002). The higher ratio is associated with a worse prognosis (Pan et al. 2022). Clinical research reveals that the APOA-IV (APOA4) level is an independent indicator of poor survival (HR = 2.83, p = 0.024) (Sugimachi et al. 2016). A 2014 study identified a molecular classifier related to metabolic syndrome, comprising ABCA1, APOA2, APOC1, and APOD, which can predict the risk of colon cancer relapse (HR = 4.47, p < 0.001) in a multivariate analysis. The metabolic syndrome-related genes also serve as a molecular marker of prognosis, such as APOA2 (HR = 3.1, p = 0.045) (Vargas et al. 2014).

Clinical trial landscapes

Apolipoproteins show promise as non-invasive diagnostic markers and modulators of treatment response. Their involvement in immune regulation and metabolic reprogramming further supports their potential as integrated diagnostic and therapeutic targets in precision oncology. Apolipoproteins show potential as diagnostic markers in cancer. While APOA1 expression in colorectal cancer showed no significant correlation with staging (Lim et al. 2016), bladder cancer studies revealed multiple apolipoproteins with strong diagnostic potential in urine-based assays, outperforming conventional tests (Chen et al. 2013; Rosser et al. 2013). Although plasma APOE levels were unchanged in breast cancer, novel post-translational modifications were identified, suggesting a mechanistic role in tumor biology (Uen et al. 2015). Additionally, lower APOD levels in breast cancer patients with bone metastases may serve as a marker for disease progression (Osundeko et al. 1997). Regarding therapeutic relevance, APOD-positive stromal cells in oral squamous cell carcinoma were associated with favorable immune microenvironments and improved response to immunotherapy (Liu et al. 2025).

Furthermore, APOE genotypes emerged as potential modifiers of intervention outcomes in breast cancer and other cancer survivors, influencing fall risk, physical activity, and cognitive performance following exercise and mindfulness-based programs (McGinnis et al. 2022). Apolipoproteins can also serve as biomarkers to predict the severity of specific cancer sequelae. It has been proven in 2 different studies that some single-nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs) in APOE are associated with cognitive impairment in breast cancer survivors (Park et al. 2019; Lengacher et al. 2015). These findings underscore the role of apolipoproteins as biomarkers and mediators of therapeutic efficacy and patient-reported outcomes.

Limitations

Translational application of apolipoproteins in cancer faces significant challenges, as different subtypes may be associated with different types of cancer. Although clinical outcomes show promise, a more in-depth understanding of how apolipoproteins interact during cancer progression is necessary, as well as further validation in large cohorts. While this bibliometric study offers new insights into the field of apolipoprotein-cancer research, methodological limitations should be carefully considered. Relying solely on English literature may introduce potential database selection bias, as emerging regional innovations, such as recent studies indexed in the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) (Yan et al. 2023), may be underrepresented. Second, the inherent citation accumulation lag creates temporal blind spots for sleeping beauty (Donthu 2021) publications with delayed recognition, necessitating updates in subsequent research.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric and bioinformatic analysis of apolipoproteins in cancer research from 1985 to 2024, quantifying productivity and collaboration patterns at the national, institutional, and individual levels. Temporal analysis shows a shift from foundational molecular studies to translational research. Currently, the focus is on the genetic influence of apolipoprotein variants on cancer risk, the role of apolipoproteins in immune modulation, the development of apolipoprotein-related biomarkers, and the integration of lipid metabolism pathways in tumor progression. Future efforts should focus on global collaborations to reduce geographic disparities, enhance functional research with spatial omics and patient-derived models, and develop apolipoprotein-targeted strategies into combination therapies, such as immunotherapies or metabolic inhibitors. At the same time, prioritizing biomarker validation, understanding allele-specific mechanisms, and creating targeted therapies will improve risk stratification, early detection, and personalized treatments in oncology.

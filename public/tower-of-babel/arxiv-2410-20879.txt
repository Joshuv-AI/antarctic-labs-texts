Geometric-Like imaginarity: quantification and state conversion
Meng-Li Guo, Bo Li, Shao-Ming Fei

Abstract
From the perspective of resource-theoretic approach, this study explores the quantification of imaginary in quantum physics. We propose a well defined measure of imaginarity, the geometric-like measure of imaginarity. Compared with the usual geometric imaginarity measure, this geometric-like measure of imaginarity exhibits smaller decay difference under quantum noisy channels and higher stability. As applications, we show that both the optimal probability of state transformations from a pure state to an arbitrary mixed state via real operations, and the maximal probability of stochastic-approximate state transformations from a pure state to an arbitrary mixed state via real operations with a given fidelity f f , are given by the geometric-like measure of imaginarity.



I
Introduction

A key feature of quantum mechanics is the need of imaginary numbers to accurately simulate the dynamics of physical systems. Although imaginary numbers have been long used to simplify the models of oscillatory motions and wave mechanics in classical physics, they seem to play a more deeper role in quantum physics as they are intrinsic to any orthodox formulation
[

,

\{|H\rangle,\,|V\rangle\}

|H\rangle
and
|V\rangle
express the horizontal polarization and vertical polarization, respectively. The imaginary number in the density matrix causes the rotation of the electric field vector, that is, elliptical or circular polarization. According to the postulates of quantum mechanics, the state of a quantum system may be described by the wave function
\Psi(x)=|\Psi(x)|e^{-i\phi(x)}
, with the probability amplitude
|\Psi(x)|^{2}
and the phase
\phi(x)
. It is natural to ask whether it is necessary to describe the fundamental properties and dynamics of a quantum system with the imaginary part
e^{-i\phi}
. That is to say, can quantum physics be formulated with only real numbers?
Recently, the answer to this question has been proven to be negative

. It provides an operational framework to quantify and manipulate the resources of quantum systems
[

,

. A resource theory consists two elements: the free states and the free operations. The free states are the quantum states with vanishing resources. And resource states can not be created from any free states through any free operations. A well-known example is the resource theory of quantum entanglement
[

. The consumption of entanglement leads to protocols such as superdense coding

and quantum teleportation

, steering

, nonlocality

Recently, the theory of imaginative operational resources has been introduced
[

. Then, Wu et al. further developed a more detailed theory of imaginarity resource, which was promoted by the latest development of entanglement and coherence theories

They studied the quantification of imaginarity based on two specific measures of imaginarity: geometric imaginarity and the robustness of imaginarity, and presented the operational explanations in state transformation problems. These works are not only of fundamental importance to the study of imaginarity resource theory, but also of operational significance.

A quantitative analysis of imaginarity contributes to the development of quantum technology such as quantum computers. In fact, different quantification of imaginarity can greatly enrich our understanding of imaginarity. Up to now only a few specific imaginarity measures have been introduced

In this paper, in the framework of resource theory we put forward a new imaginarity measure, the geometric-like imaginarity. By analyzing its basic properties and operational significance, and exploring its relationship with other imaginarity resource theories, we find that the geometric-like imaginarity measure has significant advantages compared with the geometric imaginarity measure. This study not only provides new theoretical support for the development of quantum technology, but also provides powerful tools for its practical application. The structure of the article is arranged as follows: the second part outlines the basic concepts of resource theory; the third section proposes the geometric-like imaginarity; the fourth part compares the decay value of geometric-like imaginarity and geometric imaginarity after quantum channel transmission; the fifth part applies the geometric-like imaginarity to the state conversion problem in the theory of virtual quantities and focuses on the optimal fidelity analytical expression of the conversion probability. Finally, the sixth part summarizes the main achievements and research significance.

II
Preliminaries

In the framework of quantum resource theory, a pivotal inquiry concerns the existence of a free operation, denoted as
\Phi_{f}
, that transforms a given quantum state
\rho
into another state
\sigma
. Specifically, this transformation is expressed as
\sigma=\Phi_{f}[\rho]
[
17
]
, indicating that
\rho
possesses a greater resourcefulness compared to
\sigma
. For any resource measure
R
, the monotonicity principle holds,

\displaystyle R(\rho)\geq R(\sigma),

(1)

implying that
\rho
contains at least as much resource content as
\sigma
.

In cases where a deterministic free operation cannot convert
\rho
into
\sigma
(e.g., when
R(\rho)<R(\sigma)
), probabilistic methods may still be applicable. This is feasible in resource theories that admit stochastic free operations, characterized by a set of free Kraus operators
\{K_{j}\}
satisfying the condition
\sum_{i}K_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j}\leq\mathbb{I}
. Assume that any incomplete set of
\{K_{j}\}
can be supplemented with additional operators
\{L_{i}\}
to satisfy the completeness relation
\sum_{i}L_{i}^{\dagger}L_{i}+\sum_{j}K_{j}^{\dagger}K_{j}=\mathbb{I}
, the maximal conversion probability
P(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)
is defined as

\displaystyle P\left(\rho\rightarrow\sigma\right)=\max_{\{K_{j}\}}\left\{\sum_{j}p_{j}:\sigma=\frac{\sum_{j}K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}}{\sum_{j}p_{j}}\right\},

where
p_{j}=tr[K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}]
.
Notably, a deterministic operation exists when
P(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)=1
. However, if both deterministic and stochastic conversions fail, leading to
P(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)=0
, approximate transformations may still be possible. The closeness of the achieved transformation to the desired one is quantified by the maximal transformation fidelity
F(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)
, defined as

\displaystyle F(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)=\max_{\Phi_{f}}\left\{F(\Phi_{f}[\rho],\sigma)\right\},

where the maximum is taken over all permissible free operations
\Phi_{f}
within the given resource theory.

Resource measures
R
satisfy the monotonicity condition given by Eq. (
1
). However, in stochastic theories, a stronger condition requires
R
to be monotonic on average,

\displaystyle R(\rho)\geq\sum_{j}q_{j}R(\sigma_{j}),

where
\sigma_{j}=K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}/q_{j}
with free Kraus operators
K_{j}
, and
q_{j}
is the corresponding probability,
q_{j}=tr[K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{\dagger}]
.
Resource quantifiers that satisfy this stronger condition are referred to “strong resource monotones”. Notably, if
R
is also convex, strong monotonicity implies monotonicity.

Finally, for convex and strong monotonic resource quantifiers, a rigorous upper bound on the conversion probability can be formulated as

\displaystyle P(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)\leq\min\left\{\frac{R(\rho)}{R(\sigma)},1\right\},

(2)

where the upper bound is given by the ratio of the resource quantities and the probability does not exceed unity.

Let
\{|j\rangle\}^{d-1}_{j=0}
be a fixed base in
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We denote
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
the set of density operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. In the context of the resource theory of imaginarity, the free states are the real ones defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}):\langle i|\rho|j\rangle\in\mathbb{R},i,j=0,1,...,d-1\},

where
\mathbb{R}
represents the set of real numbers. Real operations are characterized by real Kraus operators. Specifically, an operation
\Phi
characterized by Kraus operators
\{K_{l}\}
is deemed real if
\langle i|K_{l}|j\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
for any
l
and
i,j=0,1,...,d-1
. A real-valued functional
M
of quantum states is called an imaginarity measure if it satisfies the following conditions (M1) to (M4)
[
4
,
38
,
37
]
,

(M1)
Nonnegativity:

M(\rho)\geq 0
and
M(\rho)=0
if and only if
\rho
is a real state.

(M2)
Monotonicity:

M[\Phi(\rho)]\leq M(\rho)
if
\Phi
is a real operation.

(M3)
Strong~imaginarity~monotonicity:

\sum_{j}p_{j}M(\rho_{j})\leq M(\rho)
, where
p_{j}=\mathrm{Tr}K_{j}\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}
,
\rho_{j}=K_{j}\rho K^{\dagger}_{j}/p_{j}
and
K_{j}
are real Kraus operators.

(M4)
Convexity:

M(\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho_{j})\leq\sum_{j}p_{j}M(\rho_{j})

for any probability distribution
\{p_{j}\}
and states
\{\rho_{j}\}.

III
Geometric-Like imaginarity

For a pure state
|\psi\rangle
, the geometric imaginarity is defined as
[
38
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{g}(|\psi\rangle)=1-\max_{|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^{2},

where the maximization goes over all real pure states.
The geometric imaginarity
\mathcal{M}_{g}(\rho)
of a mixed state
\rho
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{g}(\rho)=\min\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathcal{M}_{g}(|\psi_{j}\rangle),

where the minimum is taken over all ensembles
\{p_{j},|\psi_{j}\rangle\}
that satisfy
\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{j}|
. This definition of geometric imaginarity is equivalent to the following distance-based measure
[
45
]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{g}(\rho)=1-\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}}F(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1-\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}}{2},

where
\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}
represents the root fidelity.

Specifically, for any pure state
|\psi\rangle
, there exists a real orthogonal matrix
O
such that

\displaystyle O|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}|0\rangle+i\sqrt{\frac{1-|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}|1\rangle.

(3)

Hence, for any pure state
|\psi\rangle
, any imaginarity measure
\mathcal{M}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(|\psi\rangle)=f(|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|)

(4)

for some suitable function
f
. For instance, the geometric imaginarity can be expressed as
[
38
]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{g}(|\psi\rangle)=\frac{1-|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}.

(5)

In this study, we introduce a new imaginarity measure named geometric-like imaginarity.

Definition 1
.
The geometric-like imaginarity of any pure state
|\psi\rangle
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)=1-\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}.

(6)

The geometric-like imaginarity of a mixed state
\rho
is defined via the convex-roof extension,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=\min_{\{p_{j},|\psi_{j}\rangle\}}\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi_{j}\rangle),

(7)

where the minimum is taken over all the pure state decompositions of
\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{j}|
with
\sum_{j}p_{j}=1
and
p_{i}\geq 0
.

Given that
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is a convex function, we can deduce that
\mathcal{M}_{gl}
is also convex,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}\Big(\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho_{j}\Big)\leq\sum_{j}p_{j}M_{gl}(\rho_{j}).

(8)

We have the following conclusion.

Theorem 1
.

\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
is a well-defined measure of imaginarity.

Proof.

It is straightforward to observe that
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=0
if and only if
\rho
is an real state. Thus the condition (M1) is fulfilled. Based on (
8
), we get that
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
also satisfies (M4).

To prove (M3), we first consider pure states. According to Eq. (
3
), any dimensional pure states can be transformed into single-qubit states under a real orthogonal transformation. Since the imaginarity remains unchanged under the real orthogonal transformations,
let us evaluate
\mathcal{M}_{gl}
for any state of the form
|u\rangle=x_{0}|0\rangle+ix_{1}|1\rangle
with
x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}=1
and
x_{0}^{2}\geq x_{1}^{2}\geq 0
. For any real state
|v\rangle=y_{0}|0\rangle+y_{1}|1\rangle
with
y_{0}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle v|u\rangle|=|x_{0}y_{0}+ix_{1}y_{1}|\leq\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}}=|x_{0}|,

where the inequality is due to
\sum_{j}y_{j}^{2}=1
and
x_{0}\geq x_{1}
. Since
|\langle 0|u\rangle|=|x_{0}|

[
38
]
, we conclude that

\displaystyle|x_{0}|=\max_{|v\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle v|u\rangle|.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\max_{|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|.

Without loss of generality, we consider states
|\theta\rangle=\cos\theta|0\rangle+i\sin\theta|1\rangle
with
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
, for which the geometric-like imaginarity is
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\theta\rangle)=1-\cos\theta
. As all post-measurement states are remain pure for pure initial states, proving (M3) for pure states reduces to prove the following inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\max_{|\nu_{j}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle\nu_{j}|K_{j}|\theta\rangle|\geq\cos\theta,

(9)

where
\{K_{j}\}
represents a set of real Kraus operators. To prove (
9
), let
|\nu_{j}\rangle=\frac{K_{j}|0\rangle}{\sqrt{m_{j}}}
with
m_{j}=\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|0\rangle
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\max_{|\nu_{j}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle\nu_{j}|K_{j}|\theta\rangle|\geq\sum_{j}\frac{|\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|\theta\rangle|}{s_{j}}.

Since all Kraus operators
K_{j}
are real, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|\theta\rangle|=

\displaystyle|\cos\theta\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|0\rangle+i\sin\theta\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|1\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\cos\theta|\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|0\rangle|.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\max_{|\nu_{j}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}}|\langle\nu_{j}|K_{j}|\theta\rangle|\geq\sum_{j}\frac{|\langle 0|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|0\rangle|}{m_{j}}\cos\theta.

Taking into account the identity
\sum_{j}K_{j}^{T}K_{j}=\mathcal{I}
, we prove the inequality (
9
).

Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{gl}
satisfies (M3) for pure states. To extend the conclusion to mixed states, we consider an optimal decomposition of a mixed state
\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{j}|
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi_{j}\rangle).

Set
m_{jk}=\langle\psi_{k}|K_{j}^{T}K_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle
. Using the convexity of
\mathcal{M}_{gl}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j}q_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}\left(\frac{K_{j}\rho K_{j}^{T}}{q_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}q_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}\left(\sum_{k}p_{k}\frac{K_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|K_{j}^{T}}{q_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}q_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}\left(\sum_{k}\frac{p_{k}m_{jk}}{q_{j}}\times\frac{K_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|K_{j}^{T}}{m_{jk}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j,k}p_{k}m_{jk}\mathcal{M}_{gl}\left(\frac{K_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|K_{j}^{T}}{m_{jk}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi_{j}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho).

This completes the proof that (M3) holds for all mixed states.
Monotonicity follows directly from the convexity of
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
,
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)\geq\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho_{j})=\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\Phi(\rho))
, thus
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
also satisfies (M2).

\hfill\blacksquare

The geometric-like measure of imaginarity has the following analytical expression.

Theorem 2
.
The geometric-like imaginarity of an arbitrary quantum state
\rho
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=1-\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}}{2}},

(10)

where
F(\rho,\rho^{T})
is the fidelity between
\rho
and its transpose
\rho^{T}
.

Proof.

For any mixed state
\rho
with pure state decomposition
\rho=\sum_{j}p_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{j}|
, it has been shown in Ref.
[
42
]
that

\displaystyle\max_{\{p_{j},|\psi_{j}\rangle\}}\sum_{j}p_{j}|\langle\psi^{*}_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle|\leq\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}.

(11)

Therefore, for any pure state decomposition of
\rho
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\{p_{j},|\psi_{j}\rangle\}}\sum_{j}p_{j}\Bigg(1-\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi_{j}^{*}|\psi_{j}\rangle|}{2}}\Bigg)

(12)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{\{p_{j},|\psi_{j}\rangle\}}\Bigg(1-\sqrt{\frac{1+\sum_{j}p_{j}|\langle\psi_{j}^{*}|\psi_{j}\rangle|}{2}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}}{2}}.

To demonstrate that the equality in the above inequality holds, we construct a pure state decomposition to attain the lower bound. As outlined in Ref.
[
42
]
, such a decomposition can be formulated via singular value decomposition. Let
\rho=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|\mu_{k}\rangle\!\langle\mu_{k}|
be the spectral decomposition of
\rho
, where
|\mu_{k}\rangle
are the eigenstates of
\rho
and
\lambda_{k}
are the corresponding eigenvalues. Given that
\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}
relates to the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\rho^{T}\sqrt{\rho}}
, we employ a methodology akin to Ref.
[
42
]
to select
|\mu_{k}\rangle
such that
|\langle\mu_{i}|\mu^{*}_{j}\rangle|
equals the square root of the corresponding eigenvalue of
\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\rho^{T}\sqrt{\rho}}
. Consequently, we construct a pure state decomposition such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=1-\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{F(\rho,\rho^{T})}}{2}}.

\hfill\blacksquare

IV
Decay of imaginarity of pure states under quantum channels

Quantum systems are inherently influenced by their environments. Similar to quantum entanglement and coherence, the imaginarity of a quantum state will also be affected by the environments. The impact of noisy environments on the imaginarity of quantum states needs to be analyzed. This section aims to analyze the geometric imaginarity and the geometric-like imaginarity of pure states under some quantum channels. Specifically, we delve into how quantum noise affects these imaginarities, analogous to the studies on coherence decay
[
47
]
.

For a general quantum channel
\varepsilon
, the decay of the imaginarity can be defined as the difference between the initial state’s imaginarity and that after the application of the noisy channel, i.e.,
\Delta\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\rho)-\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon(\rho)).

According to (
3
) and fact that the imaginarity of any pure state
\rho
remains unchanged under the real orthogonal transformation
O
,
\mathcal{M}(O\rho O^{T})=\mathcal{M}(\rho)
, we consider the following general form,

\displaystyle|\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}|0\rangle+i\sqrt{\frac{1-|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}{2}}|1\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1+A}{2}}|0\rangle+i\sqrt{\frac{1-A}{2}}|1\rangle,

where
A=|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|
.

Consider the bit flip (BF), phase damping (PD) and amplitude damping (AD) channels given by the following Kraus operators, respectively,

\displaystyle E^{BF}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{m}&0\\
0&\sqrt{m}\\
\end{array}\right),~~E^{BF}_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{1-m}\\
\sqrt{1-m}&0\\
\end{array}\right);

\displaystyle E^{PD}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-n}\\
\end{array}\right),~~E^{PD}_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&\sqrt{n}\\
\end{array}\right);

\displaystyle E^{AD}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-p}\\
\end{array}\right),~~E^{AD}_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{p}\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right).

Under the BF, PD and AD channels, a state
\rho
is transformed into, respectively,

\displaystyle\rho_{BF}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}i\sqrt{1-A^{2}}(1-2m)\\
\frac{1}{2}i\sqrt{1-A^{2}}(2m-1)&\frac{1}{2}(-2Am+A+1)\\
\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\rho_{PD}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{A+1}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}i\sqrt{1-A^{2}}\sqrt{1-n}\\

\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\rho_{AD}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}(A(-p)+A+p+1)&-\frac{1}{2}i\sqrt{1-A^{2}}\sqrt{1-p}\\
\frac{1}{2}i\sqrt{1-A^{2}}\sqrt{1-p}&\frac{1}{2}(A-1)(p-1)\\
\end{array}\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{s_{1}-t_{1}}+\sqrt{s_{1}+t_{1}}}{\sqrt{2}}+1}-2\sqrt{A+1}}{2\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[\sqrt{\sqrt{s_{2}-t_{2}}+\sqrt{s_{2}+t_{2}}+2}-\sqrt{2}\sqrt{A+1}\Big],

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}^{AD}_{gl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[\sqrt{\sqrt{s_{3}-t_{3}}+\sqrt{s_{3}+t_{3}}+2}-\sqrt{2}\sqrt{A+1}\Big],

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Big[\sqrt{2}\left(\sqrt{s_{1}-t_{1}}+\sqrt{s_{1}+t_{1}}\right)-2A\Big],

) and vanish for real states (

). For a fixed quantum state (fixed
A
), the decay functions exhibit concavity with respect to the channel parameters
m,n,p
. Conversely, for a fixed channel (fixed
m,n,p
), the decay functions are convex and decreasing with respect to
A
.

It can be observed from the Figure

that
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{g}>\Delta\mathcal{M}_{gl}
. Furthermore, the graph indicates that geometric imaginarity decreases rapidly (blue graphs), while geometric-like imaginarity (green graphs) demonstrates greater stability under the quantum channel. Therefore, under the quantum channel
\epsilon
, the geometric-like imaginarity exhibits a smaller attenuation difference, which means that it loses less information during transmission and has higher stability.

V
State transformations via real operation

In the following, we demonstrate the pivotal role played by the geometric-like measure of imaginarity in probabilistic and stochastic-approximate state transformations.

V.1
Probabilistic transformations

We now present the maximum probability of transforming a pure state
|\psi\rangle
into another pure state
|\phi\rangle
through real operations.

Proposition 1
.
The maximum probability
P(\,|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\phi\rangle\,)
for a pure state transformation
|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\phi\rangle
via real operations is given by

\displaystyle P(\,|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\phi\rangle\,)=\min\left\{~\frac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\psi^{*}|\psi\rangle|}}{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\phi^{*}|\phi\rangle|}},~1~\right\}.

(34)

Proof.

From previous results
[
38
]
, we know that for any states
\rho
and
\sigma
,

\displaystyle P(\rho\rightarrow\sigma)\leq\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)}{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\sigma)}.

For pure states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
, this implies

\displaystyle P(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\phi\rangle)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|}}{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|}}.

Next, consider the scenario where
|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|\geq|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|
. Define the state

\displaystyle|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|}{2}}|0\rangle+i\sqrt{\frac{1-|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|}{2}}|1\rangle.

Using real operations with Kraus operators
[
38
]

\displaystyle K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad K_{1}=\sqrt{\mathbb{I}-K_{0}^{2}},

where
a\leq 1
, we transform
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
into

\displaystyle|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|}{2}}|0\rangle+i\sqrt{\frac{1-|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|}{2}}|1\rangle,

with probability

\displaystyle p=\frac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|}}{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{1+|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|}}.

Since
|\phi^{\prime}\rangle
is equivalent to
|\phi\rangle
up to a real orthogonal transformation, this probability is achievable.

For
|\langle\psi|\psi^{\ast}\rangle|<|\langle\phi|\phi^{\ast}\rangle|
, the transformation
|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\phi\rangle
can be achieved with unit probability
[
37
]
. Therefore, the maximum probability is as stated.

\hfill\blacksquare

As exact deterministic state transformations are not always feasible, we consider the probabilistic scenario. The probabilistic transformations between pure states have been investigated in
[
4
,
38
]
. In the theorem below, we extend this results to the case that the target state is mixed.

Theorem 3
.
The optimal probability
P(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)
of transforming a pure state
|\psi\rangle
into a mixed state
\rho
via real operations is given by

\displaystyle P(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)=\min\Biggl\{~\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)},~1~\Biggr\}.

(35)

Proof.

From (
2
) we see that the ratio of geometric-like measures provides an upper bound on the optimal achievable probability
P(\sigma\rightarrow\rho)
of transforming a state
\sigma
to
\rho
,

\displaystyle P(\sigma\rightarrow\rho)\leq\min\Biggl\{~\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\sigma)}{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)},~1~\Biggl\}.

(36)

Proposition 1 demonstrates that this inequality is saturated for probabilistic pure-to-pure state transformations, where the optimal probability is given by the ratio of geometric-like measures as shown in (
34
).

We now prove that (
36
) is saturated when
\sigma=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
is a pure state. Let
\{p^{\prime}_{j},|\psi^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\}
be the optimal ensemble such that

\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=\sum_{j}p^{\prime}_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi^{\prime}_{j}\rangle)
.
We adopt a specific purification of
\rho

[
42
]
,

\displaystyle|\rho\rangle=\sum_{j}\sqrt{p_{j}^{\prime}}|\psi_{j}^{\prime}\rangle\otimes|j\rangle^{A},

where
A
signifies an ancillary system for purification. It can be easily verified that

\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\rho\rangle)=\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
. From (
34
) we have

\displaystyle P(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow|\rho\rangle)

\displaystyle=\min\Biggl\{~\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\rho\rangle)},~1~\Biggl\}

\displaystyle=\min\Biggl\{\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)},1\Biggl\}.

By discarding the ancilla we complete the proof.

\hfill\blacksquare

Theorem 3 shows that the optimal probability of converting a pure state into a mixed state under real operations is given by the readily computable geometric-like measure of imaginarity.

Lemma 1
.
For any state
\rho

\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}

Given a pure state decomposition

\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\rho^{T}\sqrt{\rho}}

[
42
]

\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\mu_{j}\rangle)
. We aim to achieve a decomposition
\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}
such that

\langle\psi_{i}|\psi^{*}_{i}\rangle=\langle\psi_{j}|\psi^{*}_{j}\rangle

\forall\,i,j
.
Note that
\langle\mu_{i}|\mu^{*}_{i}\rangle\geq 0

\forall\,i
.
We have
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\langle\mu_{i}|\mu^{*}_{i}\rangle=1-\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
.
If
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\mu_{i}\rangle)=\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
for all

such that
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\langle\mu_{i}^{\prime}|\mu^{\prime*}_{i}\rangle=1-\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
is kept.

Assume that two pure states
|\mu_{1}\rangle
and
|\mu_{2}\rangle

\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}|\mu^{\prime}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|\mu_{1}\rangle+\sin{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}|\mu_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}|\mu^{\prime}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sin{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|\mu_{1}\rangle+\cos{\alpha}\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}|\mu_{2}\rangle.

By continuously varying
\alpha
one obtains that
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\mu^{\prime}_{1}\rangle)=\mathcal{M}_{gl}({\rho})
. This process can be recursively applied to the remaining pure states until the desired decomposition is obtained, while
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\langle\mu_{i}^{\prime}|\mu^{\prime*}_{i}\rangle=1-\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
is kept.

\hfill\blacksquare

Analogous to two-qubit entanglement theory

Lemma 2
.
For an arbitrary state
\rho
, consider the set
S_{\rho,f}
containing states
\rho^{\prime}
such that
F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\geq f
for all
\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}

is given by

\displaystyle\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})=1-\cos\left(\max\left\{\Delta_{1},0\right\}\right),

where
\Delta_{1}=\sin^{-1}\left(\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}\right)-\cos^{-1}(\sqrt{f})
.
For any pure state
|\psi\rangle
, the maximal geometric-like measure of imaginarity in
S_{|\psi\rangle,f}
is given by

\displaystyle\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{|\psi\rangle,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})=1-\cos\left(\min\left\{\Delta_{2},\frac{\pi}{4}\right\}\right),

where
\Delta_{2}=\sin^{-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)\right)+\cos^{-1}(\sqrt{f})
.

Proof.

We employ the Bures angle as a distance measure between states of
\rho
and
\sigma

D(\rho,\sigma)=\cos^{-1}\left(\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}\right)
.
Note that the geometric-like measure of imaginarity
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)
falls within the range of
0
to
\frac{1}{2}
. Consider two states,
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
, where
\rho^{\prime}
belongs to the set
S_{\rho,f}

\rho_{r}
and
\rho_{r}^{\prime}

\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
, respectively, as measured by the Bures angle. From the fact that
\rho_{r}
is the closest real state to
\rho
, employing the triangle inequality of distances we derive the following chain inequalities,

\displaystyle\cos^{-1}\left(1-\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(\rho,\rho_{r})\leq D(\rho,\rho^{\prime}_{r})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D(\rho,\rho^{\prime})+D(\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}_{r})

\displaystyle\cos^{-1}(\sqrt{f})+\cos^{-1}\left(1-\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})\geq 1-\cos\left(\max\left\{\Delta_{1},0\right\}\right).

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})\leq 1-\cos\left(\min\left\{\Delta_{2},\frac{\pi}{4}\right\}\right).

(38)

\displaystyle|\cos(\alpha-\tilde{\beta})|.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho_{\min})=

\displaystyle 1-\cos\Big(\max\Big\{\sin^{-1}(\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)})

Hence, the minimum geometric-like measure of imaginarity within the set

\displaystyle\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})=

\displaystyle 1-\cos\Big(\max\Big\{\sin^{-1}(\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)})

(41)

\bar{f}

Theorem 4
.
The maximal probability of stochastic-approximate state transformations from a pure state
|\psi\rangle
to an arbitrary state
\rho
via real operations with fidelity
f
is given by

\displaystyle P_{f}(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)=\begin{cases}1,~~m_{1}\geq 0\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{1-\cos(\Delta_{1})},~~\mathrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

m_{1}=\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}-\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}+\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}
,

\Delta_{1}=\sin^{-1}\Big(\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}\Big)-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}
.

Proof.

To obtain state
\rho
with a fidelity of at least
f
, the optimal strategy is to transfer to a state
\rho^{\prime}
in
S_{\rho,f}
with a minimal geometric-like measure of imaginarity,

\displaystyle P_{f}(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)=\min\left\{\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})},1\right\}.

Using Eq. (
41
) we have

\displaystyle\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\cos\Big(\max\Big\{\sin^{-1}(\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)})

\displaystyle-\cos^{-1}(\sqrt{f}),0\Big\}\Big).

Denote

\displaystyle m_{1}=

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}

\displaystyle-\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}+\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}.

When
m_{1}\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}

\displaystyle\geq\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}.

Given that
\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{4}]
and
\sin^{-1}\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}\in[0,\frac{\pi}{4}]
,
we have

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f},0\right\}.

Using these relations, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})=

\displaystyle 1-\cos\Big(\max\Big\{\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}

\displaystyle-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f},0\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-\cos\left(\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle).

When
\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)>0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho,f}}\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho^{\prime})}\geq 1,

which implies that
P_{f}(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)=1
for
m_{1}\geq 0
.

For the case of
m_{1}<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f}

\displaystyle>\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}

\displaystyle>0.

Using Eq. (
35
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{f}(|\psi\rangle\rightarrow\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}_{gl}(|\psi\rangle)}{1-\cos(\sin^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{M}_{gl}(\rho)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{gl}(\rho)}-\cos^{-1}\sqrt{f})}.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\blacksquare

Theorem 4 shows that the maximal probability of stochastic-approximate state transformations from a pure state

to an arbitrary state

VI
Conclusions

The resource-theoretic approach has recently gained prominence in quantum mechanics and quantum information theory, offering a rigorous mathematical framework to elucidate the significance of complex numbers in this field and enabling a more systematic and quantitative examination of complex numbers in quantum systems. Additionally, there has been a surge of interest in quantifying imaginarity quantities, particularly within the context of entanglement and coherence theory. The geometric-like measure of imaginarity introduced in this study particularly shows its practical applications in characterizing quantum state transformations.

Compared to the geometric imaginarity measure, our geometric-like measure of imaginarity exhibits advantages including greater robustness against noise and decoherence, higher stability and less information loss during transmission. Furthermore, in state conversion problems, this measure simplifies the computation of transformation probabilities while ensuring maximum fidelity of the transformed state. Our study highlights the profound importance of complex numbers in quantum physics and the further investigations on the applications of the imaginarity in quantum information processing.

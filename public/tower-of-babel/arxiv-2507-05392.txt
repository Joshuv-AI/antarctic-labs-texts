Asymptotically Good Quantum Codes with Addressable and Transversal Non-Clifford Gates
Zhiyang He, Vinod Vaikuntanathan, Adam Wills, Rachel Yun Zhang

Abstract
Constructing quantum codes with good parameters and useful transversal gates is a central problem in quantum error correction. In this paper, we continue our work in [ HVWZ25 ] and construct the first family of asymptotically good quantum codes (over qubits) supporting transversally addressable non-Clifford gates. More precisely, given any three logical qubits across one, two, or three codeblocks, the logical 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gate can be executed on those three logical qubits via a depth-one physical circuit of 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gates. This construction is based on the transitive, iso-orthogonal algebraic geometry codes constructed by Stichtenoth [ Sti06 ] . This improves upon our construction from [ HVWZ25 ] , which also supports transversally addressable 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gates and has inverse-polylogarithmic rate and relative distance.

1
Introduction

Since the discovery and formulation of quantum error correction
[
Sho95
,
Ste96
,
CS96
,
Got97
]
, quantum codes with good parameters and useful transveral gates has been a topic of major interest in the field. In this direction, constructing
high rate
quantum codes with transversal and
addressable
quantum gates is a particularly difficult problem. The former property ensures a very space-efficient error correction overhead, whereas the latter property enables a particularly fault-tolerant and time-efficient implementation of quantum logic.
We will omit further discussion on this property and its motivation here, referring the reader to Section 1 of
[
HVWZ25
]
for a complete discussion.
In this paper, which is a follow-up to
[
HVWZ25
]
, we construct the first family of asymptotically good quantum codes with transversal and addressable non-Clifford gates, as detailed in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a family of asymptotically good quantum CSS codes over qubits with parameters

\left[\left[n,\Theta(n),\Theta(n)\right]\right]_{2},

(1)

supporting a transversally addressable non-Clifford gate. Specifically, any three logical qubits across one, two, or three blocks of the code may be addressed with a logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate via a depth-one circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.

This construction follows similar ideas to the construction of Theorem 1.1 in
[
HVWZ25
]
.
In that work, we used punctured Reed-Solomon codes as the component classical codes in a CSS construction.
The multiplication property of the Reed-Solomon code (see Fact 2.11 in
[
HVWZ25
]
) and its automorphisms by affine shifts enable the tranversal implementations of addressable non-Clifford gates.
A caveat of this construction, however, is that Reed-Solomon codes are defined over finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of growing order, which means the constructed CSS codes are defined over qudits of increasing dimension.
In particular, the dimension
q
must be at least
n
, the number of physical qudits in the code.
With established techniques (see Section 6 of
[
HVWZ25
]
and also
[
Ngu24
,
GG24
]
), we can perform a code concatenation procedure to construct a qubit code with the same transversal addressability of the
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate.
However, this concatenation incurs a loss of parameters: while our codes are asymptotically good over qudits of growing dimension (see Theorem 3.1 of
[
HVWZ25
]
), the resulting qubit code loses a factor
\text{polylog}(q)
during the concatenation procedure (see Theorem 1.1 of
[
HVWZ25
]
). Therefore, since
q\sim n
, this means that the resulting qubit code had parameters

\left[\left[n,\Theta\left(\frac{n}{\text{polylog}(n)}\right),\Theta\left(\frac{n}{\text{polylog}(n)}\right)\right]\right]_{2}.

(2)

In this follow-up work, we use classical algebraic geometry codes to build quantum codes with the same properties, except where the qudit dimension is constant;
q=\Theta(1)
. This means that the resulting qubit codes after the concatenation procedure is also asymptotically good, thus establishing Theorem
1.1
.
In particular, we employ the transitive, iso-orthogonal algebraic geometry codes due to Stichtenoth
[
Sti06
]
, where the transitive action of the Galois automorphisms on the rational places of the codes enables the addressability of non-Clifford gates.
This answers one of the open problems from the discussion in Section 1.2 of
[
HVWZ25
]
.
We remark that the other open problems in the list remain relevant and interesting.

3
Transversally Addressable
\mathsf{CCZ}
Gates

In this section, we will describe asymptotically good quantum codes over qudits of fixed dimension
q=2^{t}=\Theta(1)
with transversally addressable
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.
When combined with the qudit-to-qubit transformations described in Section 6 of
[
HVWZ25
]
(which adapted methods from
[
GG24
,
Ngu24
]
), this will yield our main result, Theorem

.
Here, it will suffice to show the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q=r^{2}
be a fixed power of two, where
r\geqslant 8
. There exists an asymptotically good family of qudit

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{AG}}
for qudits of dimension
q
with rate at least
\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
and relative distance at least
\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
. Given a member of the family with
k
logical qudits, let
A,B,C\in[k]
label any three logical qudits, and let
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then,

1.

On a single code block of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{AG}}
,
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}
may be implemented by a depth-7 circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}

2.

On three distinct code blocks of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{AG}}
,
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma}_{123}[A,B,C]}
may be implemented by a depth-1 circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.

Remark 3.2
.

On two distinct code blocks of
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{AG}}
,
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}_{112}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}
may be implemented by a depth-
3
circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}

Remark 3.3
.

The implementation depth of each operation may be trivially reduced to one, i.e., a strongly transversal implementation, by duplicating qudits a constant number of times. This would produce another asymptotically good code, albeit with worse rate and relative distance.

Remark 3.4
.

We only consider the
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate for brevity, although the result may be easily generalised to arbitrary diagonal gates (on a constant number of qudits) with entries
\pm 1
on the diagonal, as is discussed in
[
HVWZ25
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

Under the qudit-to-qubit transformations described in
[
HVWZ25
]
(which in turn follow from
[
GG24
,
Ngu24
]
), we can use Theorem

to construct an asymptotically good qubit code with addressable inter-block and intra-block addressable
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates with strongly transversal (i.e. depth-

) implementations, thus proving Theorem

.
This is possible because the field size is a constant here

, in contrast to the result built from Reed-Solomon-based codes in
[

]
. There, the growing qudit dimension leads to a qubit code which is only asymptotically good up to a polylogarithmic factor.
For the sake of brevity, we choose not to restate the transformations in this paper and refer readers to Section 6 of
[
HVWZ25
]
.

Remark 3.6
.

Here, we have considered transversally addressing any single logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate on one, two, or three logical qubits. There is a stronger notion of addressability, as is considered in
[
GJ25
]
, for example, where one may ask about the possibility of being able to transversally address an arbitrary product of
\mathsf{CCZ}

One may ask the same question about the codes constructed here, where further information on the Galois group of the relevant field extension may reveal this possibility in the case of general algebraic geometry codes. We comment, however, that such a strong form of transversal addressability need not necessarily exist, and

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}e_{0}^{(i)}\deg A^{(i)}+\frac{(r-1)}{4}e_{\infty}^{(i)}\deg B^{(i)}.

Then, using the fact that

is an extension of degree

, we have that
[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]=\frac{N^{(i)}}{r-1}
. Therefore, we have

(27)

thus establishing Equation (

). Next, we verify that we have Equation (

). Indeed, we have

\displaystyle(a+2)\deg A^{(i)}+(b+2)\deg B^{(i)}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{a_{i}}{4}+1\right)\deg A^{(i)}+\left(\frac{b_{i}}{4}+1\right)\deg B^{(i)}

(28)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e_{0}^{(i)}+1}{2}\right)\deg A^{(i)}+\left(\frac{(r-1)e_{\infty}^{(i)}+2}{4}\right)\deg B^{(i)}

(29)

\displaystyle>\frac{N^{(i)}}{2(r-1)}+\frac{N^{(i)}}{4}

(30)

\displaystyle>\frac{2N^{(i)}}{r-1}+\frac{N^{(i)}}{r(r-1)},

(31)

where going into the third line we have again used

, and one may check that the last line holds for every

.
∎

In the knowledge of Equations (

Lemma 3.8
.

The dimension of

is

\displaystyle m^{(i)}\coloneq(a+1)\deg A^{(i)}+(b+1)\deg B^{(i)}-\frac{N^{(i)}}{r-1},

(32)

\displaystyle d\left(C_{a,b}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant N^{(i)}-a\deg A^{(i)}-b\deg B^{(i)}.

(33)

The distance of the dual code

satisfies

\displaystyle d\left(C_{a,b}^{(i)\perp}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant(a+2)\deg A^{(i)}+(b+2)\deg B^{(i)}-2\frac{N^{(i)}}{r-1}.

(34)

Proof.

Equation (
21
) simply says that

\deg\left(aA^{(i)}+bB^{(i)}\right)<N^{(i)}.

(35)

Additionally, Equation (

\deg\left(aA^{(i)}+bB^{(i)}\right)>2g(E_{i})-2,

(36)

where

from Theorem 1.7 of

\displaystyle m^{(i)}\coloneq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(C_{a,b}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=a\deg A^{(i)}+b\deg B^{(i)}+1-g(E_{i})

\displaystyle=(a+1)\deg A^{(i)}+(b+1)\deg B^{(i)}-\frac{N^{(i)}}{r-1}

\displaystyle d\left(C_{a,b}^{(i)}\right)

(40)

respectively. As for the distance of the dual code. Equations (

(73)

With this, we may consider any logical computational basis state. Indeed, given
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, we have that

\overline{\ket{w}}\propto\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\Ket{\sum_{A^{\prime}=1}^{k^{(i)}}w_{A^{\prime}}g^{A^{\prime}}+g}

(74)

where the normalisation is unimportant to us. By linearity, Equation (
73
) gives us that

\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g_{k}^{A}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]\overline{\ket{w}}=\exp(i\pi\tr(\gamma\;w_Aw_Bw_C))\overline{\ket{w}},

(75)

so that the gate on the left executes the logical gate
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}
, as required.
∎

Circuit Depth.

We have shown that we can apply a logical
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}
gate by applying
n^{(i)}
different
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on the physical qudits. We claim that one can arrange these
n^{(i)}
different
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates into a depth-
7
circuit.

Note that for any physical qudit
\alpha_{k}
, there are exactly three physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates that involve
\alpha_{k}
. This means that for any physical
\mathsf{CCZ}(\alpha_{k_{1}},\alpha_{k_{2}},\alpha_{k_{3}})
gate, there are at most six other
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates that involve any of
\alpha_{k_{1}},\alpha_{k_{2}}
or
\alpha_{k_{3}}
.

We construct the seven layers of the circuit as follows. For the first layer, pick greedily as many of the
n^{(i)}

\mathsf{CCZ}
gates that do not share any physical qudit. Then, all the remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates must share at least one physical qudit with one of the chosen gates in the first layer, otherwise they would have been included in the first layer. In particular, each remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate shares a physical qudit with at most five other remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.

We repeat this process to select the
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates in layer 2; we pick from the remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates as many as possible that do not share a physical qudit. At the end, all the remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates shares a physical qudit with at most four other remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.

We continue this process four more times. After this sixth layer, all remaining
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates will share no physical qudits with each other, and we can pick them all for our seventh layer.

3.3
Inter-Block
\mathsf{CCZ}
Gates

In the last section, we showed how to implement a logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate on three qudits
within
the same code state, meaning an intra-block
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate. In this section, we will show how to implement a logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate on any three logical qudits in three
different
code blocks.

We will construct the logical operator that applies a
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate between qudits
A
in the first code state,
B
in the second code state, and
C
in the third code state. We will denote this logical operator by
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}
. Likewise, a physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate on qudits
\alpha_{k_{1}}
in code state
1
,
\alpha_{k_{2}}
in code state
2
, and
\alpha_{k_{3}}
in code state
3
will be denoted by
\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}[\alpha_{k_{1}},\alpha_{k_{2}},\alpha_{k_{3}}]
. Other notation will be carried over from the previous section, and the argument will be given more briefly as it is similar to the last section.

As we argued in the last section, given
A,B\in\left[k^{(i)}\right]
, there exists
\varphi_{AB}\in\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F_{0})
such that

\varphi_{AB}(\beta_{A})=\beta_{B}

(76)

by Theorem
2.6
, since the extension
E_{i}/F_{0}
is Galois. Then, recalling the notation from Theorem
3.12
, we have the following.

Theorem 3.15
.

For any
\ket{\psi_{1}},\ket{\psi_{2}},\ket{\psi_{3}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{AG}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}\ket{\psi_{1}}\ket{\psi_{2}}\ket{\psi_{3}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]\ket{\psi_{1}}\ket{\psi_{2}}\ket{\psi_{3}}.

(77)

Hence, we can define

\displaystyle\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}=\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})].

(78)

Proof.

This proof will follow analogously to the proof of Theorem
3.12
. We will check the effect of applying
\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]
on
\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}}
where

\displaystyle g^{(w)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}g^{j}

(79)

\displaystyle g^{(w^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}^{\prime}g^{j}

(80)

\displaystyle g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}^{\prime\prime}g^{j},

(81)

which will be enough to check its behaviour on
\overline{\ket{w}}\overline{\ket{w^{\prime}}}\overline{\ket{w^{\prime\prime}}}
, for any
w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k^{(i)}}
, by linearity, which is enough to check its behaviour on any three codestates, again by linearity. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{(w)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}\tilde{g}^{j}

(82)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}^{\prime}\tilde{g}^{j}

(83)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\left[m^{(i)}\right]}w_{j}^{\prime\prime}\tilde{g}^{j},

(84)

be the corresponding functions in
\mathcal{L}(aA^{(i)}+bB^{(i)})
.

We have that

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}}

(85)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\exp\left(i\pi\tr\left(\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\alpha_{k})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}(\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}))\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}(\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k}))\right)\right)\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}}

(86)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\pi\tr\left(\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\alpha_{k})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}(\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}))\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}(\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k}))\right)\right)\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}}.

(87)

The sum may be treated in essentially the same way as in the proof of Theorem
3.12
. Indeed, we have that
\tilde{g}^{A},\tilde{g}^{(w)},\varphi_{AB}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})})
and
\varphi_{AC}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})})
are all elements of
\mathcal{L}(aA^{(i)}+bB^{(i)})
, and so Claim
3.14
applies, and using essentially the same manipulations we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\alpha_{k})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}(\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}))\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}(\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k}))

(88)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\alpha_{k})\cdot\left(\varphi_{AB}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})})\right)(\alpha_{k})\cdot\left(\varphi_{AC}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})})\right)(\alpha_{k})

(89)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{A^{\prime}=1}^{k^{(i)}}\underline{x}_{A^{\prime}}\;\tilde{g}^{A}(\beta_{A^{\prime}})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\beta_{A^{\prime}})\cdot\left(\varphi_{AB}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})})\right)(\beta_{A^{\prime}})\cdot\left(\varphi_{AC}^{-1}(\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})})\right)(\beta_{A^{\prime}})

(90)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{A}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\beta_{A})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}(\varphi_{AB}(\beta_{A}))\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}(\varphi_{AC}(\beta_{A}))

(91)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{A}\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w)}(\beta_{A})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime})}(\beta_{B})\cdot\tilde{g}^{(w^{\prime\prime})}(\beta_{C})

(92)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{A}\cdot w_{A}w_{B}^{\prime}w_{C}^{\prime\prime},

(93)

so

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}}

\displaystyle=\exp({i\pi\tr(\gamma\; w_Aw_B'w_C'')})\ket{g^{(w)}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime})}}\ket{g^{(w^{\prime\prime})}},

(94)

as required.
∎

Circuit Depth.

The gate

\displaystyle\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma}[A,B,C]}=\prod_{k=1}^{n^{(i)}}\mathsf{CCZ}_{123}^{\gamma\;\underline{x}_{A}^{-1}\underline{y}_{k}\;g^{A}_{k}}[\alpha_{k},\varphi_{AB}(\alpha_{k}),\varphi_{AC}(\alpha_{k})]

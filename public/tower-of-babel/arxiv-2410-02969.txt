Anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent and application to nonlocal problems
E. Azroul 1 , A. Barbara 2 , N. Kamali 3 and M. Shimi 4

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to introduce a new fractional anisotropic Sobolev space with variable exponent where the basic qualitative properties (completeness, separability, reflexivity, …) are established, including the continuous and compact embedding results. Moreover, some functional proprieties of anisotropic fractional p → ( . , . ) \displaystyle\vec{p}(.,.) -Laplacian operator are proved. As an application, we use the mountain pass theorem and Ekeland’s variational principle to ensure the existence of a weak solution for a nonlocal anisotropic problem with variable exponent.

Introduction

The theory of Sobolev spaces,
\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\Omega)
consists of functions defined on a domain
\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle L^{p}

[

They provide a framework for studying PDEs and to prove the regularity, existence, and uniqueness of solutions to a wide range of differential equations because they allow to extend the notion of a solution to equations where traditional smooth solutions may not be well-defined.

The variability in regularity may arise in various scientific and engineering applications, then one of the key motivations for introducing variable exponents
\displaystyle p(x)
is to model situations where different parts of a domain exhibit distinct degrees of regularity. Hence, the introduction of variable exponent Sobolev spaces allows for a more flexible and nuanced analysis of problems where the regularity of solutions is not uniform across the entire domain, providing a valuable extension of classical Sobolev space, for a deep comprehension of the theory of these spaces, we refer to the monograph

In parallel, due to the development of various real applications, including physics, materials science, image processing, and computational mathematics the term "anisotropic exponent" was introduced. The inception of this concept of different directions was by Benedek and Panzone
[

3.
Anisotropic fractional Sobolev space with variable exponent

The aim of this section is to discuss some fundamental properties of the new variable exponent anisotropic fractional Sobolev space such as completeness, separability, and reflexivity. Besides,
we give a detailed proof of the continuous and compact embedding result. Moreover, we will adjust the definition of these spaces in order to encode correctly the Dirichlet boundary datum in nonlocal problems.

3.1.
Some qualitative properties

Theorem 3.1
.

The space
\displaystyle\left(W,\|.\|_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}\right)

Proof

We define the operator

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(w(x),\tfrac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p_{1}(x,y)}+s}},\cdots,\tfrac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p_{N}(x,y)}+s}}\right).

For any
\displaystyle w\in W

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(w)\|_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=\|w\|_{\small L^{p_{M}(x)}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|w\|_{\small L^{p_{i}(x,y)}(\Omega\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|w\|_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}.

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(v),v\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(v),w\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w),v\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w),w\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}-\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|^{2}\right)}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}-\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|^{2}\right)}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy.

By changing the role of
\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle w
and the Simon’s inequality
[
32
]
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(v)-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w),v-w\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}-\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}\right)\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{2}-\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|^{2}\right)}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy>0.

Which implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(.,.)}
is strictly monotone. It is self-evident to demonstrate the boundedness of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(.,.)}
.

\displaystyle(ii)
-
Consider a sequence
\displaystyle\{w_{n}\}
such that
\displaystyle w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
\displaystyle X_{0}
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right)-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w),w_{n}-w\right\rangle\leq 0
, from the strict monotony of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(.,.)}
, we obtain that

(4.1)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right)-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w),w_{n}-w\right\rangle=0.

In the view of Theorem
3.1
,
\displaystyle w_{n}(x)\longrightarrow w(x)
a.e in
\displaystyle\Omega
.
By using Fatou’s Lemma, we have:

(4.2)

\small\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy.

Young’s inequality implies the existence of two constants
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right),w_{n}-w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right),w_{n}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right),w\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\left(\tfrac{p_{i}(x,y)-1}{p_{i}(x,y)}\tfrac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}+\tfrac{1}{p_{i}(x,y)}\tfrac{w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\geqslant C_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy-C_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\iint_{Q}\tfrac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy.

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.4)

\small\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy.

According to
[
29
]
, and the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 3.1 ]
, we obtain the strong convergence of
\displaystyle w_{n}
to
\displaystyle w
in
\displaystyle X_{0}
.

5.
An application to anisotropic nonlocal problems

As an application of this framework
we consider a class of fractional anisotropic problems with variable exponent of the following form

\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}_{\vec{p}(x,.)}\left(u\right)+\left|u\right|^{p_{M}(x)-2}u=\lambda f(x,u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\hskip 85.35826ptu=0&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Where

\displaystyle\bullet

\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary
\displaystyle(N\geqslant 2)
.

\displaystyle\bullet

\displaystyle\lambda>0
is a real parameter.

\displaystyle\bullet

\displaystyle f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that assumes throughout this section equal to
\displaystyle\left|u\right|^{r(x)-2}u
, with

1<r^{-}\leq r(x)\leq r^{+}<P^{-}_{-}\leq p_{M}^{+}<+\infty,\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

In
[
26
,
27
]
the authors investigated the integer case of problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
and in the fractional scenario, this work
[
4
]
is well recommended.

A function
\displaystyle w\in X_{0}
is defined as a weak solution of problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
if :

(5.1)

\begin{gathered}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}(w(x)-w(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy\\
+\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}w(x)\varphi(x)dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{r(x)-2}w(x)\varphi(x)dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in X_{0}.\end{gathered}

For all
\displaystyle w\in X_{0}
, the energy functional related to problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
is of the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)

\displaystyle=L_{\vec{p}(x,y)}(w)-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r(x)}|w(x)|^{r(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{1}{p_{i}(x,y)}\tfrac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy+\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{|w(x)|^{p_{M}(x)}}{p_{M}(x)}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{|w(x)|^{r(x)}}{r(x)}dx.

By a standard argument as in Lemma
4.1
, we have that
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\in C^{1}\left(X_{0},\mathbb{R}\right)
, in addition, its derivative is given by the following formula :

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(w),v\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}(w(x)-w(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}w(x)v(x)dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{r(x)-2}w(x)v(x)dx,

for all
\displaystyle w,v\in X_{0}
. Hence a critical point of
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
coincides with weak solution of problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
.

The result of the existence of solutions of problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
is formulated in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be a smooth
bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R^{N}}
and
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. Let
\displaystyle\vec{p}:\overline{Q}^{N}\longrightarrow\left(1,+\infty\right)
be a vector function which supposed to verify conditions (
1.1
), and (
1.2
) on
\displaystyle\overline{Q}
. Moreover, consider
\displaystyle r:\Omega\longrightarrow\left(1,+\infty\right)
as a continuous variable exponent such that

(5.3)

1<r(x)<P^{-}_{-}\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Then, there exists
\displaystyle\lambda_{*}>0
such that problem
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{\vec{P}}\right)
possesses a weak solution
\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.

In order to establish the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we will apply the mountain pass theorem combined with the Ekeland’s variational principle. The crucial step in such type of variational methods is to ensure the compactness conditions stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, the functional
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
verifies the Palais-Smale condition.

Proof
.

Let
\displaystyle\{w_{n}\}
be a (PS)-sequence of
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
on
\displaystyle X_{0}
, which could be expressed mathematically as follows

(5.4)

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{n}\right)\rightarrow c>0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(w_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ in }X_{0}^{*}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\tfrac{1}{p_{i}(x,y)}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{p_{M}(x)}|w_{n}(x)|^{p_{M}(x)}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r(x)}|w_{n}(x)|^{r(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{P^{+}_{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy+\frac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}(x)|^{p_{M}(x)}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{r^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}(x)|^{r(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\left(\rho_{\vec{p}(x,y)}^{0}(w_{n})+\rho_{p_{M}(x)}(w_{n})\right)-\tfrac{\lambda}{r^{-}}\rho_{r(x)}(w_{n})

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}[w_{n}]_{s,p_{i}(x,y)}^{p_{i}^{-}}+\|w_{n}\|_{p_{M}(x)}^{p_{M}^{-}}\right)-\tfrac{\lambda}{r^{-}}\|w_{n}\|_{r(x)}^{r^{+}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\left(\frac{[w_{n}]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{P_{-}^{-}}}{N^{P^{-}_{-}-1}}+\|w_{n}\|_{p_{M}(x)}^{p_{M}^{-}}\right)-\tfrac{\lambda}{r^{-}}\|w_{n}\|_{r(x)}^{r^{+}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\tfrac{1}{N^{P^{-}_{-}-1}2^{\tilde{P}-1}}\|w_{n}\|_{X}^{\tilde{P}}-\frac{\lambda}{r^{-}}\|w_{n}\|_{r(x)}^{r^{+}},

where
\displaystyle\tilde{P}=\min\left\{P^{-}_{-},P_{M}^{-}\right\}
, and by using Theorem
3.2
, we get that

(5.5)

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{n}\right)\geqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\frac{1}{N^{P^{-}_{-}-1}2^{\tilde{P}-1}}[w_{n}]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{\tilde{P}}-\tfrac{\lambda C^{r^{+}}}{r^{-}}[w_{n}]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{r^{+}}.

Since
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
is bounded from (
5.4
), thus
\displaystyle\{w_{n}\}
is bounded on
\displaystyle X_{0}
. In the view of the reflexivity of
\displaystyle X_{0}
, we can find a subsequence of
\displaystyle\{w_{n}\}
still denoted in the same way such that
\displaystyle w_{n}\rightharpoonup w
in
\displaystyle X_{0}
.

From condition (
5.3
),
\displaystyle X_{0}
is compactly embedded in
\displaystyle L^{r(x)}(\Omega)
(resp. in
\displaystyle L^{p_{\scriptsize M}(x)}(\Omega)
), therefore
\displaystyle w_{n}\longrightarrow w
in
\displaystyle L^{r(x)}(\Omega)
(resp. in
\displaystyle L^{p_{\scriptsize M}(x)}(\Omega)
), using Hölder’s inequality we acquire

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega}\left|w_{n}\right|^{r(x)-2}w_{n}\left(w_{n}-w\right)dx\leqslant 2\left\||w_{n}|^{r(x)-1}\right\|_{L^{r^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)}\left\|w_{n}-w\right\|_{L^{r(x)}(\Omega)},

Similarly,

(5.7)

\int_{\Omega}\left|w_{n}\right|^{p_{M}(x)-2}w_{n}\left(w_{n}-w\right)dx\leqslant 2\left\||w_{n}|^{p_{M}(x)-1}\right\|_{L^{p_{M}^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)}\left\|w_{n}-w\right\|_{L^{p_{M}(x)}(\Omega)}.

Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|w_{n}\right|^{r(x)-2}w_{n}\left(w_{n}-w\right)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|w_{n}\right|^{p_{\scriptsize M}(x)-2}w_{n}\left(w_{n}-w\right)dx=0.

Owing to (
5.4
), and the weak convergence of
\displaystyle{w_{n}}
we have

\left\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\left(w_{n}\right),w_{n}-w\right\rangle\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\longrightarrow+\infty.

Which means that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}(w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y))((w_{n}-w)(x)-(w_{n}-w)(y))}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}(x)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}w_{n}(x)(w_{n}-w)(x)dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}(x)|^{r(x)-2}w_{n}(x)(w_{n}-w)(x)dx\underset{n\longrightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow 0}.

From (
5.6
), and (
5.7
), it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}(w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(y))((w_{n}-w)(x)-(w_{n}-w)(y))}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy\underset{n\longrightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow 0}.

On the other hand, it’s clear that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)-2}(w(x)-w(y))((w_{n}-w)(x)-(w_{n}-w)(y))}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy\underset{n\longrightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow 0}.

The two previous results show that
\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right)-\mathcal{L}_{\vec{p}(x,y)}\left(w_{n}\right),w_{n}-w\right\rangle\underset{n\longrightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow 0}
.
The fact that
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\vec{p}(.,.)}
is of type
\displaystyle(S_{+})
implies the strong convergence of
\displaystyle w_{n}
to
\displaystyle w
in
\displaystyle X_{0}.
We deduce that
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Now, let us verify that the functional
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}

satisfies the mountain pass geometry.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that the hypothesis of Theorem
5.1
are fulfilled. Then, there exists
\displaystyle\lambda_{*}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{*}\right)
, there are
\displaystyle\delta,\vartheta>0
, such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)\geqslant\vartheta>0
for any
\displaystyle w\in X_{0}
with
\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{0}}=\delta
.

Proof
.

From inequality (
5.5
), we have:

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w\right)\geqslant\tilde{C}[w]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{\tilde{P}}-\frac{\lambda C^{r^{+}}}{r^{-}}[w]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{r^{+}},~\forall w\in X_{0},

where
\displaystyle\tilde{C}=\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{P^{+}_{+}},\tfrac{1}{p^{+}_{M}}\right\}\tfrac{1}{N^{P^{-}_{-}-1}2^{\tilde{P}-1}}
. Therefore, for any
\displaystyle w\in X_{0}
with
\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{0}}=\delta
, we obtain

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w\right)\geqslant\tilde{C}\delta^{\tilde{P}}-\tfrac{\lambda C^{r^{+}}}{r^{-}}\delta^{r^{+}}=\delta^{r^{+}}\left(\tilde{C}\delta^{\tilde{P}-r^{+}}-\tfrac{\lambda C^{r^{+}}}{r^{-}}\right).

By defining
\displaystyle\lambda_{*}
in this way
\displaystyle\lambda_{*}=\frac{\bar{C}\delta^{\bar{P}-r^{+}}r^{-}}{2C^{r^{+}}}
, we get as a result that for all
\displaystyle\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*}),
for any
\displaystyle w\in X_{0}
with
\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{0}}=\delta
such that

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)\geqslant\vartheta>0\quad\text{with }\vartheta=\frac{\delta}{2}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that the hypothesis of Theorem
5.1
are fulfilled. Then, there exists
\displaystyle\omega\in X_{0}
such that
\displaystyle\omega\geqslant 0,\omega\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(t\omega)<0
for any
\displaystyle t>0
small enough.

Proof
.

Since
\displaystyle r^{-}<P^{-}_{-}
, then there exists
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
such that
\displaystyle r^{-}+\varepsilon\leqslant P^{-}_{-}
. Moreover, the continuity of
\displaystyle r
implies the existence of an open set
\displaystyle O
of
\displaystyle\Omega
such that

(5.8)

|r(x)-r^{-}|\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}~~x\in O.

Consider
\displaystyle\omega
as an infinitely differentiable function that is compactly supported such that
\displaystyle\overline{O}\subset supp(\omega)
,
\displaystyle\omega(x)=1~~\forall x\in\overline{O}
, and
\displaystyle 0\leqslant\omega\leqslant 1~~\text{in }\Omega
. Therefore, for
\displaystyle t>0
small enough we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(t\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{p_{i}(x,y)}\frac{|t\omega(x)-t\omega(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p_{M}(x)}|t\omega(x)|^{p_{M}(x)}dx

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r(x)}|t\omega(x)|^{r(x)}dx\quad

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{t^{P^{-}_{-}}}{P^{-}_{-}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q}\frac{|\omega(x)-\omega(y)|^{p_{i}(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{sp_{i}(x,y)+N}}dxdy+\frac{t^{p^{-}_{M}}}{p^{-}_{M}}\int_{\Omega}|\omega(x)|^{p_{M}(x)}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{r^{+}}\int_{\Omega}t^{r(x)}|\omega(x)|^{r(x)}dx,

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{t^{P^{-}_{-}}}{P^{-}_{-}}\rho_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}(\omega)-\frac{\lambda t^{r^{-}+\varepsilon}}{r^{+}}\int_{O}|\omega(x)|^{r(x)}dx,

\displaystyle=t^{r^{-}+\varepsilon}\left(\frac{t^{P^{-}_{-}-r^{-}-\varepsilon}}{P^{-}_{-}}\rho_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}(\omega)-\frac{\lambda}{r^{+}}\int_{O}|\omega(x)|^{r(x)}dx\right).

For
\displaystyle t<\theta^{\frac{1}{P^{-}_{-}-r^{-}-\varepsilon}}
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(t\omega)<0
, where

0<\theta<\min\left\{1,\frac{\lambda P^{-}_{-}\int_{O}|\omega(x)|^{r(x)}dx}{r^{+}\rho_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}(\omega)}\right\},

Next, we point-out that
\displaystyle\rho_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}(\omega)>0
(this fact implies that
\displaystyle w\neq 0
).
Indeed, from this inclusion between sets
\displaystyle O\subset\operatorname{supp}(\omega)\subset\Omega
and
\displaystyle 0\leqslant\omega\leqslant 1
in
\displaystyle\Omega
, we acquire

0<\int_{0}|\omega|^{r(x)}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{r(x)}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{r^{-}}\mathrm{d}x.

Condition (
5.3
) implies the existence of a constante
\displaystyle C
such that

\|\omega\|_{L^{r^{-}(\Omega)}}\leqslant C\|\omega\|_{X_{0}}.

The two previous inequalities inferredes to

\rho_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}(\omega)>0.

The proof of Lemma
5.3
is achieved.

Now, we are in the position to prove our existence result.

Proof of Theorem

5.1
.
From Lemma
5.2
we have:

(5.9)

\inf\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}>0\qquad~~~~\text{ in }\partial\mathcal{B}_{m}(0),

and from Lemma
5.3
, we get

(5.10)

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w\right)\geqslant\tilde{C}[w]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{\tilde{P}}-\frac{\lambda C^{r^{+}}}{r^{-}}[w]_{s,\vec{p}(x,y)}^{r^{+}},~\forall w\in\mathcal{B}_{m}(0),

which implies that
\displaystyle-\infty<c=\inf_{\overline{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}<0
.

Consider
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
such that

0<\varepsilon<\inf_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}.

Then we apply the Ekelnad’s variational principle to the functional
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}:\overline{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we get
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)\leq\inf_{\overline{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon,\\
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)<\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)+\varepsilon\left\|w-w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{X_{0}},\quad\text{ for }w\neq w_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}(0).\end{array}\right.

Which acquires that

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)\leq\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\leq\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon<\inf_{\partial\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}.

In following, we cosndier the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}:\overline{\mathcal{B}_{m}(0)}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)+\varepsilon\left\|w-w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{X_{0}}.

It is clear that
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w_{\varepsilon})<\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w)
, for
\displaystyle w\neq w_{\varepsilon}
. Hence,
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}
is a minimum of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
. Which implies that for any
\displaystyle t\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle v\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
, we have

\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\left(w_{\varepsilon}+tv\right)-\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)}{t}\geqslant 0\Rightarrow\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{\varepsilon}+tv\right)-\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)}{t}+\varepsilon\|v\|_{X_{0}}\geqslant 0,

as
\displaystyle t
tends to
\displaystyle 0^{+}
, we acquire
\displaystyle<\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right),v>+\varepsilon\|v\|_{X_{0}}\geqslant 0.

As a consequence,

\|\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{X_{0}^{*}}\leqslant\varepsilon,

we infer that there exists a sequence
\displaystyle w_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
such that

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\left(w_{n}\right)\rightarrow c>0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(w_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0.

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
verifies the PS condition, then we can find
\displaystyle w_{0}\in X_{0}
such that
\displaystyle w_{n}\longrightarrow w_{0}
as
\displaystyle n\longrightarrow+\infty
. as a result,
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(w_{0})=c
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(w_{0})=0
. In conculsion,
\displaystyle w_{0}
is a critical point of
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
. Which is summed up by the achievement of the proof.

The Hydra Map and Numen Formalisms for Collatz-Type Problems
by Maxwell C. Siegel

Abstract
. This paper details a generalization of the formalism presented in the author’s 2024 paper, “The Collatz Conjecture & Non-Archimedean Spectral Theory: Part I — Arithmetic Dynamical Systems and Non-Archimedean Value Distribution Theory”, to the case of Hydra maps on the ring of integers 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} of a global field K K . In addition to recounting these definitions, background material is presented for the necessary standard material in algebraic number theory and integration and Fourier analysis with respect to the p p -adic Haar measure. This paper is meant to serve as a technical manual for use of Hydra maps and numens in future research.



1.
Document Summary

As explained in the abstract, this paper is meant to be a foundational
reference for works involving the class of generalized Collatz-type
maps I call
Hydra maps
. Its primary purpose is to fix certain
notations, definitions, conventions, and formalisms that are used
throughout my works of research on this topic.

Of particular
import is the aim of giving a formalism which deals with both the
archimedean and non-archimedean completions of the underlying number
field in a unified way. Future versions of this paper will hopefully
extend this unification to a fully rigorous adèlic formulation.

2.3.
Notation for Convergence

Let
K
be a topological field. Then, in equations with limits or
infinite sums, we write
\overset{K}{=}
to mean that the convergence
occurs with respect to the topology of
K
. For example,
\overset{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}{=}

to indicate that the convergence is with respect to the topology of

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Similarly,
\overset{\mathbb{R}}{=}
means convergence
with respect to the topology of
\mathbb{R}
. If

or any subfield thereof (
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)
,
etc.), we always equip
K
with the discrete topology. So,
\overset{\mathbb{Q}}{=}

and
\overset{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}{=}
denote convergence with
respect to the discrete topologies on
\mathbb{Q}
and
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
respectively. Note then that a sequence of rational numbers
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}

satisfies
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}\overset{\mathbb{Q}}{=}0

if and only if
a_{n}=0
for all sufficiently large
n
.

Also, given a topological ring
R
, we write
\overset{R}{=}
to
mean that convergence occurs in the topology of
K
, where
K
is
the field of fractions of
R
, and that the terms of the sequence
being limited or the partial sums of the series being limited are
also elements of
R
. Thus, for a function

,
we write:

(2.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{2^{n}}\right)\overset{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}{=}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right),\textrm{ }\forall\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}

to mean that, for each
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the sequence

\left\{f\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{2^{n}}\right)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

consists of elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
that converge in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}

to
f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)
as

.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

.

Definition 3.4

Remark 3.1

Proposition 3.1

4.
Dynamical Systems

Note 2
.

[
2
]
is an easy introduction to the subject.

Fix a set
X\rightarrow X
and a map
T:X\rightarrow X
.

Notation 1
.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, we write
T^{\circ k}
to denote the composition
of
k
copies of
T
, with
T^{\circ 0}
being defined as the identity
map.

Definition 4.1
.

Recall that a
periodic point
of
T
is an
x\in X
so that

T^{\circ k}\left(x\right)=x
for some
k\geq 1
. We say
x
is

pre-periodic
if there exists a
k\geq 1
so that
T^{\circ k}\left(x\right)

is a periodic point of
T
. We say
x
is a
strictly pre-periodic

point if
x
is a pre-periodic point of
T
which is
not

a periodic point of
T
. The
forward orbit
(sometimes called
the
trajectory
)

of
x
under
T
is the sequence:

(4.1)

\left\{T^{\circ k}\left(x\right)\right\}_{k\geq 0}

A
cycle
\Omega
of
T
is the forward orbit of a periodic
point of
T
.

If
X
is a valued field with absolute value
\left|\cdot\right|_{X}
,
we say
x\in X
is a
divergent point
of
T
if :

(4.2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left|T^{\circ k}\left(x\right)\right|_{X}\overset{\mathbb{R}}{=}+\infty

We use the phrase “a
divergent trajectory
of
T
” to
refer to the forward orbit of a divergent point (which may or may
not exist, depending on
T
).

Next, we have orbit classes and their associated definitions

Definition 4.2
.

Recall that an
orbit class
of
T
is a non-empty set
V\subseteq X

so that the pre-image of
V
under
T
is equal to
V
:
T^{-1}\left(V\right)=V
.

An orbit class
V
of
T
is said to
irreducible
if it
cannot be written as
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}
and
V_{2}

are orbit classes of
T
with
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\varnothing
.

A
backward orbit
of some
x\in X
is a sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}

in
X
so that
x_{n+1}\in T^{-1}\left(\left\{x_{n}\right\}\right)

for all
n\geq 0
. Note that if
T
is not injective, a given
x_{0}\in X

can have more than one backward orbit.

We say an orbit class
V
is
periodic
if it is irreducible
and contains a cycle. We say an orbit class
V
is
divergent

if it is irreducible and contains a divergent point.

Lastly, we recall two elementary results from the theory of discrete
dynamical systems. For proofs, see
[
14
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T:X\rightarrow X
be any map. Then, the collection of irreducible
orbit classes of
T
form a partition of
X
into pair-wise disjoint
sets. These sets are the equivalence classes of
X
under the relation

\sim
defined by:

(4.3)

x\sim y\Leftrightarrow\exists m,n\geq 0:T^{\circ m}\left(x\right)=T^{\circ n}\left(y\right)

In particular, if
X
is countable, then
X
consists of at most
countably many irreducible orbit classes of
T
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a valued
field with absolute value
\left|\cdot\right|_{X}
, and let
Y

be a discrete subset of
X
, in the sense that there is a
\delta>0

so that:

(4.4)

\inf_{\begin{array}[]{c}x,y\in Y\\
x\neq y\end{array}}\left|x-y\right|_{X}\geq\delta

Then, for any map
T:Y\rightarrow Y
be any map, every irreducible
orbit class of
T
in
Y
is either periodic or divergent. Consequently,
any
x\in Y
is either a divergent point of
T
or

a pre-periodic

point of
T
.

Proof: Let
V
be an irreducible orbit class of
T
in
Y
.

If
V
contains a cycle
\Omega
of
T
, then, given any
x\in V
,

x\sim\omega
for some periodic point
\omega\in\Omega
. So,
x

is eventually iterated to some element of
\omega
’s trajectory.
Since
every
element of
\omega
’s trajectory is in
\Omega
,

x
is eventually iterated into
\Omega
, which makes
x
into
a pre-periodic point of
T
.

So, suppose that
V
does
not
contain a cycle, and, again,
let
x\in V
be arbitrary. Since
T^{-1}\left(V\right)=V
, applying

T
on both sides yields:

(4.5)

T\left(V\right)=T\left(T^{-1}\left(V\right)\right)\subseteq V

So, everything in
x
’s trajectory will also be in
V
. Thus, if

x
was pre-periodic, its trajectory would contain a cycle
\Omega
,
which would contradict the assumption that
V
contains no cycles.
In particular, note that
x
’s trajectory contains a cycle if and
only if there is some
y\in Y
so that
T^{\circ n}\left(x\right)=T^{\circ m}\left(x\right)

for two distinct positive integers
m
and
n
; that is, cycles
happen when our trajectories arrive at a point they have already visited.
Thus, writing
x_{n}
to denote
T^{\circ n}\left(x\right)
, it
must be that
x_{n}\neq x_{m}
for all distinct non-negative integers

m,n
.

So, by the pigeonhole principle, given any
R>0
, there must exist
an integer
n_{R}\geq 0
so that
\left|x_{n}\right|_{X}>R
for all

n\geq n_{R}
. If not, then there would be infinitely many elements
of
x
’s trajectory which lay in the set
\left\{y\in Y:\left|y\right|_{X}\leq R\right\}
.
Since
Y
is discrete, there are only finitely many elements in this
set, which means that the trajectory of
x
must retrace its steps
at some point: there are distinct
m,n
for which
x_{m}=x_{n}
,
which, as we saw, is impossible. Since
R
was arbitrary, we conclude
that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|x_{n}\right|_{X}=\infty
, which
proves that
x
is a divergent point, and makes
V
into a divergent
trajectory of
T
. Since
x
was arbitrary, we conclude that every
element of
V
is a divergent point of
T
.

Q.E.D.

5.
Hydra Maps

It is a standard result of algebraic number theory that one can choose
a finite set
\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{N}\right\}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}

so that every element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
can be uniquely written
as:

(5.1)

m_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+m_{N}b_{N}

for some
m_{1},\ldots,m_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}
. That is, as an abelian
group under addition,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a
lattice
. In
a similar way, every non-zero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
can be realized
as lattice, as well.

We use the term
affine lattice
to refer to a coset of a lattice
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
; thus, every affine lattice in
\mathcal{O}_{K}

is of the form:

(5.2)

c+\Lambda\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\left\{c+z:z\in\Lambda\right\}

for some ideal
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We call
\Lambda

the
underlying ideal
of
c+\Lambda
. Note that the underlying
ideal is necessarily unique, and is given by
\Lambda=\left\{z-w:z,w\in c+\Lambda\right\}
.

In this context, letting
\Lambda
be a lattice in
\mathcal{O}_{K}

with
p
distinct cosets, a
p
-Hydra map
H:\mathcal{O}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}

is given by a collection of
p
affine linear maps, called
branches
,
which we associate to each of the cosets of
\Lambda
. In particular,
given
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
H\left(z\right)
is obtained by applying
to
z
the branch specified by the coset of
\Lambda
that
z

happens to lie in.

Example 5.1
.

The lattice in question is the ideal
2\mathbb{Z}
, and the parity
of
n
is precisely the coset of
2\mathbb{Z}
that
n
happens
to lie in.

One difficulty that arises in generalizing maps of this shape to
\mathcal{O}_{K}

is that while every map of the form:

(5.3)

z\in K\mapsto az+b\in K

for fixed constants
a,b\in K
is affine linear, not every affine
linear map on
K
will be of this form, as there exist linear maps

K\rightarrow K
which are not given by multiplication against some
fixed scalar. To deal with this, we introduce the notion of an
affine
lattice morphism
.

Definition 5.1
.

We write
\textrm{Aff}_{\mathbb{F}}K
to denote the
group
of all

invertible affine
\mathbb{F}
-linear maps

K\rightarrow K
;
these are maps of the form:

(5.4)

z\mapsto rz+c

where
r\in\textrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}\left(K\right)
and

c\in K
;
\textrm{Aff}_{\mathbb{F}}K
is a non-abelian group under
map composition. We write
\left(r,c\right)
as a shorthand for a
map of the form (
5.4
).

Remark 5.1
.

It is important to distinguish between
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps on

K
and
K
-linear maps on
K
. For the case
K=\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)
,
letting
a+b\sqrt{2}\in K
be arbitrary (where
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
),
consider the maps
m_{1},m_{2}:K\rightarrow K
given by:

(5.5)

\displaystyle m_{1}\left(a+b\sqrt{2}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\left(a+b\sqrt{2}\right)

(5.6)

\displaystyle m_{2}\left(a+b\sqrt{2}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}

\displaystyle a+b

Both
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linear, but only
m_{1}

is
K
-linear. Indeed:

(5.7)

\displaystyle m_{2}\left(\sqrt{2}\left(a+b\sqrt{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=m_{2}\left(2b+a\sqrt{2}\right)=a+2b

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}m_{2}\left(a+b\sqrt{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(a+b\right)\neq a+2b

Now we can give the definition of a Hydra map. We begin with the definition
of a
pre-Hydra
.

Definition 5.2
.

A pre-Hydra consists of the following data:

I. A global field
K
.

II. A proper, non-zero ideal
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

II. For each element
j\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda
, an affine linear
map
\left(r_{j},c_{j}\right)\in\textrm{Aff}_{\mathbb{F}}K
. We often
denote
\left(r_{j},c_{j}\right)
as
H_{j}
, and call
H_{j}

the
j
th branch
of the pre-Hydra.

Remark 5.2
.

By linear algebra, we can always identify
K
with
\mathbb{F}^{d}

by choosing an
\mathbb{F}
-basis of
K
and writing
K
’s elements
in coordinates. Because of this, everything done here can also be
formulated over
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. In that particular case, absolute
values on
K
then become norms on
\mathbb{F}^{d}
; places of
K

then become equivalence classes of norms on
\mathbb{F}^{d}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Though we only consider affine linear maps, the formalism extends
just as well to polynomial maps, rational maps, and even analytic
maps. I intend to include these in the formulation in a later version
of this paper.

Definition 5.3
.

A
\Lambda
-Hydra (map)
on
K
is a map
H:\mathcal{O}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}

defined by:

(5.9)

H\left(z\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda}\left[z\overset{\Lambda}{\equiv}j\right]\left(r_{j}z+c_{j}\right)

Here,
\left[z\overset{\Lambda}{\equiv}j\right]
is the function
which is
1
if
z
is congruent to
j
mod
\Lambda
, and is

0
otherwise, and
\left(K,\Lambda,\left\{\left(r_{j},c_{j}\right)\right\}_{j\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda}\right)

is a pre-Hydra so that, for all
j\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda
, we
have
r_{j}z+c_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
for all
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

We call
\Lambda
the
underlying ideal
of
H
, and write

H_{j}
to denote the affine linear map
\left(r_{j},c_{j}\right)
,
which we call the
j
th branch of
H
.

Remark 5.4
.

As defined,
H\left(z\right)
applies
r_{j}z+c_{j}
, where
j

is the representative of the coset of
\Lambda
to which
z
belongs.
(
5.9
) can be written in a single line as:

(5.10)

H\left(z\right)=r_{\left[z\right]_{\Lambda}}z+c_{\left[z\right]_{\Lambda}}

where
\left[z\right]_{\Lambda}
denotes the image of
z
under
the canonical projection
\mathcal{O}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda
.

Remark 5.5
.

As
\Lambda
always has at least two cosets in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
we say a map
H:\mathcal{O}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a
p
-Hydra
map
whenever
H
is a
\Lambda
-Hydra map for some
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}

so that
\left|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda\right|=p
.

Some notable properties of
\Lambda
-Hydras are as follows:

Definition 5.4
.

Let
H
be a
\Lambda
-Hydra on
K
. We say
H
is:

I.
Integral
, if for all
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
and all
j\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda
,

H_{j}\left(z\right)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}

if and only if

z\in j+\Lambda
.

II.
Proper
, if the map
1-r_{0}\in\textrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}\left(K\right)

is invertible.

III.
Centered
, if
H_{0}\left(0\right)=0
.

Remark 5.6
.

From the perspective of dynamical systems, the integrality property
of a Hydra is, by far, its most important property, and plays a critical
role in the proof of the
Correspondence Principle
(
Theorem

6.1
, given at the end of
Section
6
).

The classic Collatz map
C:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined
by:

C\left(n\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2}&\textrm{if }n\overset{2}{\equiv}0\\
3n+1&\textrm{if }n\overset{2}{\equiv}1\end{cases}

is a
2
-Hydra on
\mathbb{Z}
which is non-integral, as the “odd”
branch
H_{1}\left(x\right)=3x+1
sends both even and odd integers
to integers. However, this is not a significant obstruction; the classic
“shortening” of
C
given by the aptly-named
Shortened
Collatz Map

T_{3}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
:

(5.11)

T_{3}\left(n\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2}&\textrm{if }n\textrm{ is even}\\
\frac{3n+1}{2}&\textrm{if }n\textrm{ is odd}\end{cases}

is
integral, as its “even branch”
H_{0}
is integer-valued
only for even integers, while its “odd” branch
H_{1}
is integer-valued
only for odd integers.

Remark 5.7
.

Letting
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be undetermined, one can often conjugate
an uncentered Hydra
H
to a centered Hydra
H_{\textrm{center}}

by writing:

H_{\textrm{center}}\left(z\right)=H\left(z+a\right)-a

for a judicious choice of
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Remark 5.8
.

The centeredness plays a role in the
Correspondence Principle
.

H:\mathcal{O}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}

be a
\Lambda

Following
[

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}

strings

We write
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\infty}
to denote the set of strings

to

\wedge:\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}\times\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\rightarrow\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\infty}

Definition 6.2

Given any string

(6.2)

H_{\mathbf{j}}\left(z\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\left(H_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ H_{j_{\left|\mathbf{j}\right|}}\right)\left(z\right),\textrm{ }\forall z\in K

When

As an element of

and

In this way, we can realize

and

X_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}H_{\mathbf{j}}\left(0\right),\textrm{ }\forall\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}

The next two identities are trivial, and follow immediately from the
definitions:

Proposition 6.1
.

For any
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}

(6.5)

M_{H}\left(\mathbf{i}\wedge\mathbf{j}\right)=M_{H}\left(\mathbf{i}\right)M_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)

(6.6)

X_{H}\left(\mathbf{i}\wedge\mathbf{j}\right)=H_{\mathbf{i}}\left(X_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)\right)

Definition 6.3
.

Letting
p
denote
\left|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda\right|

once and for all

\Lambda_{0},\ldots,\Lambda_{p-1}

Because of this, we shall write
\Sigma_{p}^{*}
instead of
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}
,
with
\Sigma_{p}^{*}

\beta

Remark 6.1
.

Like with writing

Example 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}

, then
upon identifying
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}

\textrm{DigSum}_{3}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\left|\mathbf{j}\right|}j_{n}3^{n-1}

Thus, for example:

(6.9)

\textrm{DigSum}_{3}\left(\left(1,2,0,1\right)\right)=1+2\cdot 3+0\cdot 3^{2}+1\cdot 3^{3}

Although

\mathbf{i}\in\Sigma_{p}^{*}
if we append finitely many

.
To that end:

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
\Sigma_{p}^{*}

We then write
\Sigma_{p}^{*}/\sim
to denote the set of equivalence

\Sigma_{p}^{*}
under
\sim
.

This gives the easy result:

Proposition 6.2
.

The map
\Sigma_{p}^{*}/\sim\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}

induced by

With this, we can realize
X_{H}
as a function
\mathbb{N}_{0}\rightarrow K

Proposition 6.3
.

If

is centered, then

and
\mathbf{j}
belonging to the same equivalence

. Hence,

is well-defined. We can then view

as a function

by using

to identify

with
\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
H
is
not
centered, one can still formally define

without reference to strings by using the functional equations given
in

Using the definition of

Proposition 6.4

(Siegel (2022); see
[

]
)
.

The set of functions
X_{H}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\rightarrow K

satisfying the functional equations:

(6.10)

X_{H}\left(pn+j\right)=r_{j}X_{H}\left(n\right)+c_{j},\textrm{ }\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\textrm{ }\forall j\in\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}

(where
r_{j}X_{H}\left(n\right)
is the image of
X_{H}\left(n\right)\in K

under
r_{j}\in\textrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}\left(K\right)
)
is in a bijective correspondence with the set of
z\in K
for which

\left(1-r_{0}\right)z=c_{0}
. In particular:

I. If
H
is proper, then (
6.10
)
has a unique solution, satisfying
X_{H}\left(0\right)=\left(1-r_{0}\right)^{-1}c_{0}
.

II. If
H
is non-proper, then (
6.10
)
has a solution

if and only if there are

for which

\left(1-r_{0}\right)z=c_{0}
. For each such

, one can set
X_{H}\left(0\right)=z

and then obtain a distinct solution of (

).
In this way, we make think of

as an initial

)
can be solved.

Next, we introduce the ring of
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Definition 6.5
.

Given an integer
p\geq 2
, the ring of
p
-adic integers is defined
by the following quotient of a formal power series ring:

(6.11)

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\left[\left[x\right]\right]}{\left\langle x-p\right\rangle}

In practice, one can think of a
p
-adic integer as an integer written
in base
p
, but with the caveat that said integer can potentially
have infinitely many base
p
digits. The quotient construction justifies
viewing
p
-adic integers as “formal power series in
p
with
coefficients in
\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
”. Given
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
this power series representation of
\mathfrak{z}
is called the

Hensel series of
\mathfrak{z}
, and the coefficients of
this series are called the
p
-adic digits

of
\mathfrak{z}
.

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is made into a compact abelian group by equipping
it with the adic topology induced by the maximal ideal
x-p
of
\mathbb{Z}\left[\left[x\right]\right]
.

When
p
is a prime number,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
can be topologized
using the
p
-adic absolute value
. This is obtained by defining
the
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}:\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}

by:

(6.12)

v_{p}\left(n\right)=\#\textrm{ of times }p\textrm{ divides }n

for all positive integers
n
, and then using the identities:

(6.13)

v_{p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)=-v_{p}\left(n\right),\textrm{ }\forall n\geq 1

and

(6.14)

v_{p}\left(rs\right)=v_{p}\left(r\right)+v_{p}\left(s\right),\textrm{ }\forall r,s\in\mathbb{Q}

to extend
v_{p}
to
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that
v_{p}\left(0\right)

is defined to be
+\infty
. The
p
-adic absolute value is then
defined by:

(6.15)

\left|r\right|_{p}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}p^{-v_{p}\left(r\right)},\textrm{ }\forall r\in\mathbb{Q}

In this way,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
can be constructed as the completion
of the metric space obtained by equipping
\mathbb{Z}
with the metric
induced by the
p
-adic absolute value.
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the field
of
p
-adic numbers, is the field of fractions of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
or, equivalently, the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to
the metric induced by
\left|\cdot\right|_{p}
. Importantly, the

p
-adic absolute value is
non-archimedean
, meaning it satisfies
the
ultrametric inequality
:

(6.16)

\left|\mathfrak{x}+\mathfrak{y}\right|_{p}\leq\max\left\{\left|\mathfrak{x}\right|_{p},\left|\mathfrak{y}\right|_{p}\right\},\textrm{ }\forall\mathfrak{x},\mathfrak{y}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}

with equality whenever
\left|\mathfrak{x}\right|_{p}\neq\left|\mathfrak{y}\right|_{p}
.

Finally, given any
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and any integer

n\geq 0
, we write
\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}
to denote
the unique integer in
\left\{0,\ldots,p^{n}-1\right\}
which is
congruent to
\mathfrak{z}
mod
p^{n}
.

Remark 6.3
.

Using this definition, it can be shown that, as a compact abelian
group,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is isomorphic to the direct product:

(6.17)

\prod_{\ell\mid p}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}

taken over all prime divisors
\ell
of
p
. In particular, this
shows that for any prime
\ell
and any integer
n\geq 1
,
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell^{n}}

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
as a topological ring. As such,
we will write the above direct product isomorphism as:

(6.18)

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong\prod_{\ell\mid p}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell^{v_{\ell}\left(p\right)}}

Ex:

(6.19)

\mathbb{Z}_{12}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}

Just like with
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{*}
, we can
identify
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with a set of strings.

Definition 6.6
.

We write:

I.
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}
, to denote the set of all strings of elements
of

\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
of either finite or infinite
length. We also include the empty string as an element of
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}
.

II.
\Sigma_{p}^{+\infty}
, to denote the set of all strings of elements
of

\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
of infinite length.

Remark 6.4
.

We can and will extend
\textrm{DigSum}_{p}
from a function
\Sigma_{p}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}

to a function
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where

\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}
is the set of finite- or infinite-length strings
in the symbols
\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
. Letting
\sim
denote
the equivalence relation on
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}
with
\mathbf{i}\sim\mathbf{j}

if and only if
\textrm{DigSum}_{p}\left(\mathbf{i}\right)=\textrm{DigSum}_{p}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)
,
we then have that
\textrm{DigSum}_{p}
induces a bijection
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}/\sim\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
where
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}/\sim
is the set of equivalence classes
of
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}
under
\sim
.

In
[
12
]
, it was shown that, given mild assumptions
on the
r_{j}
s, one can use the identification of
\Sigma_{p}^{\infty}/\sim

with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to interpolate
X_{H}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\rightarrow K

to a function on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The details for this are given
in
Lemma
6.1
, below.
The essential idea is that by making a judicious choice of absolute
values on
K
, one can realize
X_{H}
as a function (or, in general,
as a distribution) out of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
whose regularity depends
on how close the
r_{j}
s are to behaving as contractions with respect
to
K
. The behavior of the
r_{j}
s is captured by their norm with
respect to a given absolute value of
K
. The result given below
is a slightly modified version of the versions originally stated in

[
14
]
. The reader should be aware that
Lemma

6.1

treats
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

as a measure space equipped with its
real-valued Haar probability
measure
, and treat
K_{\ell}
as a measure space equipped with its
real-valued Haar measure. We also use the concept of a
locally
constant function
. Readers unfamiliar with either of these can find
an expository treatment given of them at the start of
Section

7
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
H
is proper.
For each non-trivial place
\ell
of
K
, let:

(6.20)

\rho_{H,\ell}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}

be the product of the
\ell
-adic norms of the
r_{j}
s.

Now, consider the extension
X_{H}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow K_{\ell}

defined by the formula:

(6.21)

\displaystyle X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{K_{\ell}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)

for all
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for which
X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)

exists. Then:

I. If
\rho_{H,\ell}<1
, then (
6.21
) defines
a measurable function
X_{H}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow K_{\ell}
,
with (
6.21
) converging for almost every
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
In particular, it converges at all
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

for which:

(6.22)

\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}d_{H,\ell,j}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\ln\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}<0

where:

(6.23)

d_{H,\ell,j}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\begin{cases}\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}{m}&\textrm{if }\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}\leq 1\\
\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}{m}&\textrm{if }\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}>1\end{cases}

is the
\left(H,\ell\right)
-normalized upper density of

j
in the
p
-adic digits of
\mathfrak{z}
.

II. If
\rho_{H,\ell}<1
and
\max_{0\leq j<p}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}<1
,
then (
6.21
) converges uniformly with respect
to
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, making
X_{H}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow K_{\ell}

into a continuous function.

Moreover, given either (I) or (II), we have that:

(6.24)

X_{H}\left(p\mathfrak{z}+j\right)=r_{j}X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)+c_{j}

occurs for all
j\in\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
and all
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

for which
X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)
converges in
K_{\ell}
,
and that the extensions of
X_{H}
given in (I) and (II), respectively,
are the unique functions satisfying these functional equations. Furthermore,
if
\ell
is non-archimedean and
\max_{0\leq j<p}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}\leq 1
,
then:

(6.25)

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left|X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{N}}\right)\right|_{\ell}\leq\max_{0\leq j<p}\left|c_{j}\right|_{\ell}

In particular,
X_{H}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow K_{\ell}
is
\ell
-adically
bounded by
\max_{0\leq j<p}\left|c_{j}\right|_{\ell}
on its set
of convergence.

Remark 6.5
.

Like with
Proposition
6.4
, if

H
is not proper but there exist
z\in K
for which
\left(1-r_{0}\right)z=c_{0}
,
one can recover the result of
Lemma
6.1

subject to the condition that the uniqueness of
X_{H}
is requires
not only the condition (
6.21
) and the functional
equations (
6.24
), but also that
X_{H}

satisfy the initial condition
X_{H}\left(0\right)=z
.

Proof: First, note that if
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}=\mathfrak{z}
for all sufficiently
large
n
, and hence (
6.21
) converges. So,
suppose
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Next, following
[
14
]
, a simple inductive argument
gives the following formula for the value of
X_{H}
at a given string

\mathbf{j}
of finite length:

(6.26)

X_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\left|\mathbf{j}\right|-1}r_{\left[\mathbf{j}\right]_{m}}c_{j_{m+1}},\textrm{ }\forall\mathbf{j}\in\Sigma_{p}^{*}

where:

(6.27)

r_{\left[\mathbf{j}\right]_{m}}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\prod_{k=1}^{m}r_{j_{k}}=\begin{cases}\textrm{Id}&\textrm{if }\mathbf{j}=\varnothing\\
r_{j_{1}}\cdots r_{j_{m}}&\textrm{if }\left|\mathbf{j}\right|\geq 1\end{cases}

Applying norms, and using

(6.28)

\left|r_{j}z\right|_{\ell}\leq\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}\left|z\right|_{\ell},\textrm{ }\forall z\in K,\textrm{ }\forall j\in\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}

the triangle inequality gives:

(6.29)

\left|X_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}\right)\right|_{\ell}=\sum_{m=0}^{\left|\mathbf{j}\right|-1}\left|c_{j_{m+1}}\right|_{\ell}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\left\|r_{j_{k}}\right\|_{\ell}

So, given
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, for each
n\geq 0
, let:

(6.30)

\mathbf{j}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)=\left(j_{1}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right),j_{2}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right),\ldots,j_{n}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right)

denote the string of
p
-adic digits of
\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}
;
that is:

(6.31)

\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}=\textrm{DigSum}_{p}\left(\mathbf{j}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right)

Thus, we have:

(6.32)

\left|X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)\right|_{\ell}=\left|X_{H}\left(\mathbf{j}_{n}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right)\right|_{\ell}\ll\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\left\|r_{j_{k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)}\right\|_{\ell}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}^{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}

where
\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)
counts
the number of digits of
\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}
which
are equal to
j
. Hence:

(6.33)

\left|X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)\right|_{\ell}\ll\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}^{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}

where the constant of proportionality is
C
.

Applying the
Root Test
for series convergence, and noting
that
0\leq\rho_{j}\leq 1
for all
j
, we see that the upper bound
in (
6.33
) will converge provided:

(6.34)

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}^{\frac{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}{m}}<1

Since, for almost every
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(in the sense
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
’s Haar probability measure), one has:

(6.35)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#_{j}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{m}}\right)}{m}=\frac{1}{p}

for all
j\in\left\{0,\ldots,p-1\right\}
, (
6.34
)
is equivalent to:

(6.36)

\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}^{1/p}<1

The left-hand side is
\rho_{H,\ell}^{1/p}
. Thus,
\rho_{H,\ell}<1

implies almost-everywhere convergence. As
X_{H}
is defined by the
limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)
,
and since
X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)
is
a locally constant, and hence, measurable
K_{\ell}
-valued function
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it follows that the almost-everywhere convergent
limit:

(6.37)

X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\overset{K_{\ell}}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)

is measurable, as well.

Finally, if
\max_{0\leq j<p}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}<1
,
set:

(6.38)

\rho=\max_{0\leq j<p}\left\|r_{j}\right\|_{\ell}

Then, we re-write (
6.26
) like so:

(6.39)

X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}r_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right)c_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ m}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}},\textrm{ }\forall\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\textrm{ }\forall n\geq 0

Here,
\theta_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the

p
-adic shift map
, which acts on Hensel series representations
as:

(6.40)

\theta_{p}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}p^{n}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n+1}p^{n}

i.e.:

(6.41)

\theta_{p}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)=\frac{\mathfrak{z}-\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p}}{p}

\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}
,
the projection mod
p
of the image of
\mathfrak{z}
under
k

applications of
\theta_{p}
, is then precisely the coefficient of

p^{k}
in
\mathfrak{z}
’s Hensel series representation. This then
gives us the formal expression:

(6.42)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{N}}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}r_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right)c_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ m}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}

We then have:

\displaystyle\left|X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{n}}\right)-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{N}}\right)\right|_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}r_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right)c_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ m}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right|_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\left\|r_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ k}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right\|_{\ell}\left|c_{\left[\theta_{p}^{\circ m}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right]_{p}}\right|_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\rho\left(\max_{0\leq j<p}\left|c_{j}\right|_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\rho^{m}

\displaystyle\left(\rho<1\right);

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbb{R}}{=}\frac{\rho^{n}}{1-\rho}

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, the upper bound tends to
0
uniformly
with respect to
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, thereby establishing
the uniform convergence of
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{N}}\right)
.
As locally constant functions out of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are continuous,
this then proves that:

(6.43)

X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}X_{H}\left(\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{N}}\right)

is continuous.

Almost done, observing the identity:

7.
p
-Adic Fourier Analysis and
the
\ell
-adic Characteristic Function of
X_{H}

Here, we introduce the
Haar measure
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

and
Pontryagin duality
, the two cornerstones of
p
-adic
Fourier analysis. The Haar measure is the natural generalization of
the Lebesgue measure to locally compact abelian groups such as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
and, like with the Lebesgue integral on
\mathbb{R}
, allows us to
do Fourier analysis with functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Letting
p\geq 2
, the sets:

(7.1)

k+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\left\{k+p^{n}\mathfrak{z}:\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\},\textrm{ }\forall n\geq 0,\textrm{ }\forall k\in\left\{0,\ldots,p^{n}-1\right\}

are both compact and open (“compact-open”) in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
and form a topological base for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. As such, they generate
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The
p
-adic
Haar measure

d\mathfrak{z}
is defined on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
by the rule:

(7.2)

\int_{k+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}d\mathfrak{z}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\frac{1}{p^{n}},\textrm{ }\forall n\geq 0,\textrm{ }\forall k\in\left\{0,\ldots,p^{n}-1\right\}

This is the unique translation-invariant probability measure on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
For
r\in\left[1,\infty\right]
,
L^{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)

is then defined as the Banach space of functions
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

under the usual norm:

(7.3)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left|f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right|^{r}d\mathfrak{z}\right)^{1/r}&\textrm{if }r<\infty\\
\textrm{esssup}_{\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left|f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right|&\textrm{if }r=\infty\end{cases}

with the standard caveat that each function is actually an equivalence
class of functions whose differences vanish almost everywhere with
respect to
d\mathfrak{z}
.

Next, given
n\geq 0
and
k\in\left\{0,\ldots,p^{n}-1\right\}
,
we write:

(7.4)

\left[\mathfrak{z}\overset{p^{n}}{\equiv}k\right]\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }\mathfrak{z}\in k+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
0&\textrm{else}\end{cases},\textrm{ }\forall\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

This function is said to be
locally constant mod
p^{n}
,
because its value depends only on the value of
\mathfrak{z}
mod

p^{n}
. More generally, a function of a
p
-adic integer variable
is said to be
locally constant
if it is locally constant
mod
p^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

A
p
-adic

Schwartz-Bruhat (SB) function
is a linear
combination of finitely many indicator functions of this type. Every
SB function is locally constant and Haar integrable, and every locally
constant function is SB and Haar integrable. We write
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)

to denote the space of SB functions. This is an algebra under point-wise
addition and multiplication, and is dense in
L^{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)

for all
r
. SB functions are to the classical Schwartz functions
of Fourier analysis on euclidean space to what the Haar measure is
to the Lebesgue.

Finally, although we will not need it for this paper, the Haar measure
naturally extends from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Clopen balls of the form
\mathfrak{x}+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}

and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
form a basis for the topology of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
The
p
-adic Haar measure of
\mathfrak{x}+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

is:

(7.5)

\int_{\mathfrak{x}+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}d\mathfrak{z}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}p^{-n},\textrm{ }\forall\mathfrak{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},\textrm{ }\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}

Note that this makes intuitive sense, as
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
becomes
a
p
-adically
smaller
neighborhood of
0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,
while it becomes a
p
-adically
larger
neighborhood of
0

as
n\rightarrow-\infty
.

Remark 7.1
.

Henceforth, unless stated otherwise, “almost everywhere” , “measure”,
“measurable”, “integrable”, etc., are all meant with respect
to the Haar measure.

Remark 7.2
.

If
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is continuous (such as
if

is locally constant), it can be shown that:

(7.6)

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{p^{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{p^{N}-1}f\left(n\right)

Moreover, given any

, the
convergence of the limit (

) then implies
that

is Haar integrable, and that its integral is given by (
7.6
).
See
[
15
]
for details.

Remark 7.3
.

Because
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a finite measure space, for all
r\geq 1

and all measurable
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, Hölder’s
inequality gives:

(7.7)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{qr}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)},\textrm{ }\forall r,q\geq 1

Thus,
f\in L^{r_{1}}
implies
f\in L^{r_{2}}
for all
r_{1},r_{2}\in\left[1,\infty\right]

with
r_{2}\leq r_{1}
. So,
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)

implies
f\in L^{r}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)
for all

.

We need the following elementary integration formulae:

Proposition 7.1

(
Decomposition formula
)
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Then, for all measurable

f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

(7.8)

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-1}\int_{k+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-1}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[\mathfrak{z}\overset{p^{n}}{\equiv}k\right]f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}

as well as the change of variables formula:

(7.9)

\int_{a\mathbb{Z}_{p}+b}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}=\left|a\right|_{p}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(a\mathfrak{z}+b\right)d\mathfrak{z},\textrm{ }\forall a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Using the change of variables formula, we also have:

(7.10)

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)d\mathfrak{z}=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{p^{n}-1}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(p^{n}\mathfrak{z}+k\right)d\mathfrak{z}

Next, we have to introduce
Pontryagin duality
.

This
is the formalism which allows one to perform Fourier analysis for
functions out of a
locally compact abelian group
(LCAG).
LCAGs are abelian groups that are equipped with a topological structure
with respect to which the operations of addition and negation are
continuous.
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
are all examples of
LCAGs, though for our purposes, we will only need to work with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

and
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. We borrow the “frequency” terminology
from Tao’s blog notes on Fourier Analysis
[
23
]
.

Definition 7.2
.

Given an LCAG
G
, written additively, a
(unitary) character

of
G
a continuous group homomorphism
\chi:\left(G,+\right)\rightarrow\left(\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\},\times\right)

satisfying:

(7.11)

\displaystyle\chi\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(x\right)\chi\left(y\right),\textrm{ }\forall x,y\in G

(7.12)

\displaystyle\left|\chi\left(z\right)\right|

\displaystyle=1,\textrm{ }\forall z\in G

The
trivial character
is the unique character satisfying

\chi\left(x\right)=1
for all
x\in G
. A
frequency
is
a continuous group homomorphism
\xi:\left(G,+\right)\rightarrow\left(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},+\right)

so that:

(7.13)

\xi\left(x+y\right)=\xi\left(x\right)+\xi\left(y\right),\textrm{ }\forall x,y\in G

The
trivial frequency
is the unique frequency satisfying

\xi\left(x\right)=0
for all
x\in G
.

Every unitary character
\chi
can be uniquely written as:

(7.14)

\chi\left(x\right)=e^{-2\pi i\xi\left(x\right)},\textrm{ }\forall x\in G

for some frequency
\xi
. The
Pontryagin dual
of
G
, denoted

\hat{G}
, is the set of all unitary characters, made into a group
by pointwise multiplication; we can also define it as the set of all
frequencies, made into a group by pointwise addition. These are the
multiplicative and additive realizations of the same object.
Pontryagin
duality
is the observation that the pairing:

(7.15)

\left(\xi,x\right)\in\hat{G}\times G\mapsto\xi\left(x\right)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-bilinear map called the
duality bracket
,
and that this shows that
\hat{G}
is also an LCAG. The
Fourier
transform
then sends functions
X:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to functions

\hat{X}:\hat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by:

(7.16)

\hat{X}\left(\xi\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\int_{G}X\left(x\right)e^{-2\pi i\xi\left(x\right)}dx

where
dx
is the Haar measure of
G
, provided the integral exists.
The
Inverse Fourier Transform
sends functions
\hat{X}:\hat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

to functions
X:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

(7.17)

X\left(x\right)=\int_{\hat{G}}\hat{X}\left(\xi\right)e^{2\pi i\xi\left(x\right)}d\xi

where
d\xi
is the
dual measure
, the Haar measure on
\hat{G}
,
provided the integral exists.

In our case, it can be shown that, as an additive group, the Pontryagin
dual
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\left[1/p\right]/\mathbb{Z}
,
the group of all
p
-power denominator rational numbers in
\left[0,1\right)

with addition mod
1
as the group operation. The dual measure on

\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is just the counting measure. The duality
bracket is given explicitly by the
p
-adic fractional part
,

\left\{\cdot\right\}_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
If
p
is a prime number, this is defined by:

(7.18)

\left\{\mathfrak{x}\right\}_{p}=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }n_{0}\geq 0\\
\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{-1}d_{n}p^{n}&\textrm{if }n_{0}\leq-1\end{cases}

where:

(7.19)

\mathfrak{x}=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}d_{n}p^{n}

is the Hensel series representation of
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
If
p
is composite,
\left\{\cdot\right\}_{p}
is defined by:

(7.20)

\left\{\mathfrak{x}\right\}_{p}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{\ell\mid p}\left\{\mathfrak{x}\right\}_{\ell}

where the sum is taken over all prime divisors
\ell
of
p
.

Thus, for example, given
t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(7.21)

e^{2\pi i\left\{t\mathfrak{z}\right\}_{p}}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }t=0\\
e^{2\pi it\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{\left|t\right|_{p}}}&\textrm{else}\end{cases}

where
\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{\left|t\right|_{p}}
is the projection
of
\mathfrak{z}
mod the power of
p
in
t

if
p
is odd:

(7.22)

e^{2\pi i\left\{2\mathfrak{z}/p^{3}\right\}_{p}}=e^{2\pi i\left(2\left[\mathfrak{z}\right]_{p^{3}}/p^{3}\right)}

Moreover, we have that:

(7.23)

e^{2\pi i\left\{tn\right\}_{p}}=e^{2\pi itn},\textrm{ }\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\textrm{ }\forall t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

and that:

(7.24)

e^{2\pi i\left\{\frac{t}{n}\right\}_{p}}=e^{2\pi it\left[n\right]_{\left|t\right|_{p}}^{-1}},\forall t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\backslash\left\{0\right\},\textrm{ }\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}:\gcd\left(n,p\right)=1

where
\left[n\right]_{\left|t\right|_{p}}^{-1}
is the unique integer
in
\left\{0,\ldots,\left|t\right|_{p}-1\right\}
whose product
with

is congruent to
1
mod
\left|t\right|_{p}
.

Given any function
X\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}\right)
,
the
Fourier transform
of
X
, denoted
\hat{X}:\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

is the function defined by the integral:

(7.25)

\hat{X}\left(t\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}X\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)e^{-2\pi i\left\{t\mathfrak{z}\right\}_{p}}d\mathfrak{z}

Given a function
\hat{Y}:\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
the
Fourier series generated by
\hat{Y}
is:

(7.26)

\mathfrak{z}\mapsto\sum_{t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\hat{Y}\left(t\right)e^{2\pi i\left\{t\mathfrak{z}\right\}_{p}}

where we write
\sum_{t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\hat{X}\left(t\right)

to denote the limit:

(7.27)

\sum_{t\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\hat{X}\left(t\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\left|t\right|_{p}\leq p^{N}}\hat{X}\left(t\right)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{p^{N}-1}\hat{X}\left(\frac{k}{p^{N}}\right)

Remark 7.4
.

Most of the qualitative results of classical Fourier analysis hold
in the LCAG case, such as the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma (the Fourier
transform maps functions in
L^{1}
to functions that decay to
0

) and Parseval’s Identity (the Fourier transform is an isometry from
the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left(G,\mathbb{C}\right)
to the Hilbert
space
L^{2}\left(\hat{G},\mathbb{C}\right)
), and the
Convolution
Theorem
(the Fourier transform sends pointwise products to convolutions
and vice-versa.) Of greatest importance to us is the
Parseval-Plancherel
Identity
:

(7.28)

\int_{G}f\left(x\right)g\left(x\right)dx=\int_{\hat{G}}\hat{f}\left(\xi\right)\hat{g}\left(-\xi\right)d\xi

valid for all
f
and
g
for which either the left-hand or right-hand
sides converge. As with the classical Parseval-Plancherel Identity,
we can use (
7.28
) to define measures and distributions by
specifying how they act in terms of frequencies; ex:

(7.29)

\int_{G}f\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\int_{\hat{G}}\hat{f}\left(\xi\right)\hat{\mu}\left(-\xi\right)d\xi

where

(7.30)

\hat{\mu}\left(\xi\right)=\int_{G}e^{-2\pi i\xi\left(x\right)}d\mu\left(x\right)

is the
Fourier-Stieltjes transform
of the measure
d\mu

on
G
.

Proposition 7.2

For this, note that by Pontryagin duality, given a function

\hat{f}\left(w\right)=\int_{K_{\ell}}f\left(z\right)e_{\ell}\left(-wz\right)dz,\textrm{ }\forall w\in K_{\ell}

\ell

Definition 7.4

(7.36)

\left\|\phi\right\|_{W\left(K_{\ell},\mathbb{C}\right)}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\left\|\hat{\phi}\right\|_{L^{1}\left(K_{\ell},\mathbb{C}\right)}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\int_{K_{\ell}}\left|\hat{\phi}\left(w\right)\right|dw

The connection between self-similar measures (

]

Proposition 7.3
.

(7.38)

\hat{\mu}_{H,\ell}\left(t\right)=\int_{K_{\ell}}e_{\ell}\left(-tz\right)d\mu_{H}\left(z\right)

,

(7.40)

(7.41)

\int_{K_{\ell}}f\left(z\right)d\mu_{H,\ell}\left(z\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(X_{H}\left(\mathfrak{z}\right)\right)d\mathfrak{z},\textrm{ }\forall f\in L^{1}\left(K_{\ell},\mathbb{C}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{\ell}}f\left(z\right)d\mu_{H}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{\ell}}\hat{f}\left(t\right)\hat{\mu}_{H,\ell}\left(-t\right)dt

\displaystyle\left(\ref{eq:phi_H functional equation}\right);

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\int_{K_{\ell}}\hat{f}\left(t\right)e_{\ell}\left(c_{j}t\right)\hat{\mu}_{H,\ell}\left(-r_{j}t\right)d\mathbf{t}

\displaystyle\left(\textrm{Def. of }d\mu_{H,\ell}\right);

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\int_{K_{\ell}}\hat{f}\left(t\right)e_{\ell}\left(c_{j}t\right)\left(\int_{V_{\ell}}e_{\ell}\left(tr_{j}z\right)d\mu_{H,\ell}\left(z\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\int_{K_{\ell}}\left(\int_{K_{\ell}}\hat{f}\left(t\right)e_{\ell}\left(t\left(r_{j}z+c_{j}\right)\right)dt\right)d\mu_{H,\ell}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle\left(f\in W\left(K_{\ell},\mathbb{C}\right)\right);

\displaystyle\int_{K_{\ell}}f\left(z\right)d\mu_{H,\ell}\left(z\right)

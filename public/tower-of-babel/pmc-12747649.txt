Ethanol‐Assisted Green‐Solvent‐Based Strategy for Slot‐Die Coated Perovskite Solar Cells with over 70% Eco‐Friendly Solvent Content
Authors: Jae Won Ahn, Sung Yong Kim, Soo Woong Jeon, Oui Jin Oh, Seung Min Lee, Jeong Hyeon Park, Woo Cheol Han, Chan Young Kim, Dong Hyun Kim, Mun Young Woo, Myung Ji Kim, Kwon Pyo Lee, Chan Ho Shin, Dong Hun Kang, Sohyun Kang, Jun Hong Noh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPerovskite solar cells (PSCs) have recently achieved remarkable power conversion efficiencies, yet their commercial viability critically depends on the development of scalable, eco‐friendly fabrication processes. In particular, eliminating the use of toxic solvents such as N,N‐dimethylformamide (DMF) and 2‐methoxyethanol in slot‐die coating remains a major challenge. Here, a green solvent engineering strategy is presented that reduces hazardous solvent content by incorporating ethanol, a benign, low toxicity solvent, into the perovskite precursor solution. In the formulation, the fraction of hazardous solvents is reduced from 50% to 30% by volume. The introduction of ethanol improves substrate wetting and optimizes vapor pressure, both of which are key for achieving uniform film formation in scalable coating processes. Notably, increasing the ethanol content enhances the crystallinity and morphological uniformity of the perovskite films fabricated via slot‐die coating. As a result, a champion power conversion efficiency of 23.72% is achieved, and improved device stability is demonstrated under continuous operation, with performance maintained for over 750 hours under maximum power point tracking. This work highlights a viable pathway toward environmentally responsible and scalable PSC manufacturing.Keywords: manufacturing scalable slot‐die, perovskite precursor solution, perovskite solar cells, slot‐die coating

Introduction

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as one of the most promising candidates for next‐generation photovoltaics due to their remarkable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) and compatibility with low‐cost, solution‐based fabrication.[ 1 , 2 , 3 ] Among various perovskite compositions, formamidinium (FA)‐based PSCs exhibit superior thermal stability and a near‐optimal bandgap (≈1.48 eV), making them ideal for single‐junction solar cells.[ 4 , 5 , 6 ] However, the fabrication of high‐performance FA‐based PSCs has predominantly relied on spin‐coating techniques combined with anti‐solvent treatments—methods that are fundamentally incompatible with scalable and continuous manufacturing.[ 7 , 8 ] Overcoming this limitation requires the development of scalable coating strategies that can reliably yield high‐quality FA‐based perovskite films over large areas.

Slot‐die coating has garnered significant attention as a scalable deposition technique for PSCs, owing to its closed‐system configuration, reduced material waste, and suitability for roll‐to‐roll processing.[ 9 , 10 ] For example, Li and co‐workers achieved a PCE of 24% using a solvent mixture of N,N‐dimethylformamide (DMF) and 2‐methoxyethanol (2‐ME), which facilitated controlled nucleation and phase transition during film formation.[ 11 ] Nonetheless, the continued reliance on toxic solvents such as DMF and 2‐ME raises serious environmental and health concerns, and undermines the sustainability of such approaches.[ 12 , 13 ]

To address these issues, Unger and colleagues demonstrated a solvent system incorporating acetonitrile (ACN) as a more volatile alternative to 2‐ME, achieving a PCE of 22.3%.[ 14 ] However, the limited wettability of ACN at high volume fractions (>50 vol%) constrains process compatibility. More recently, water‐ and alcohol‐based green solvent systems have been investigated for their environmental benefits. Yet, most of these approaches have yielded efficiencies below 11% and have primarily focused on methylammonium (MA)‐based perovskites, which, despite their high solubility, suffer from inferior thermal and operational stability relative to FA‐based compositions.[ 15 ] These challenges highlight the urgent need for a green solvent system that can support the scalable fabrication of high‐efficiency FA‐based PSCs without compromising device performance.

In this work, we report a green‐solvent‐engineered perovskite precursor formulation comprising acetonitrile and ethanol, which simultaneously ensures a high fraction of benign solvents, enhanced substrate wettability, and controlled film formation kinetics. Ethanol plays a key role in modulating vapor pressure and improving wetting behavior, leading to uniform crystallization and dense, pinhole‐free films, as confirmed by FT‐IR and GI‐XRD analyses. Importantly, this formulation enables the fabrication of high‐quality FA‐based perovskite layers—a longstanding challenge in green solvent systems due to the relatively low solubility and slower crystallization dynamics of FA precursors. Remarkably, despite containing over 70 vol% green solvents, the resulting films exhibit excellent crystallinity, compositional uniformity, and compatibility with both n–i–p and p–i–n device architectures. Devices fabricated via slot‐die coating achieve a champion PCE of 23.72%, alongside long‐term operational stability under maximum power point tracking. These results position our ethanol‐assisted, FA‐based formulation as a scalable and environmentally responsible solution for high‐performance perovskite solar cell manufacturing.

Results and Discussion

Design of Green Solvent for Perovskite Slot‐Die Coating

A green solvent suitable for perovskite processing must exhibit low toxicity to satisfy environmental and health safety requirements. In addition, compatibility with scalable slot‐die coating additionally demands high vapor pressure to enable rapid evaporation and a low contact angle to support stable meniscus formation and uniform substrate wetting (Figure 1a). These processing‐related properties are essential to prevent coating defects such as ribbing and microbubble formation at the upstream edge of the coating die. A comparative analysis of commonly used solvents (Figure 1b) highlights the environmental, health, and safety (EHS) concerns associated with conventional solvents such as DMF, 2‐ME, and N‐methyl‐2‐pyrrolidone (NMP), which consistently score poorly across all three categories.[ 16 ] In contrast, greener alternatives such as ACN, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and ethanol (EtOH) offer significantly better safety profiles, positioning them as attractive candidates for environmentally responsible processing. In addition to toxicity considerations, solvent selection must also account for slot‐die coating compatibility, particularly in terms of vapor pressure and wetting behavior. For example, while solvents such as butanol and DMSO are considered relatively non‐toxic, their extremely low vapor pressures severely hinder evaporation, rendering them unsuitable for slot‐die coating (Figure S1, Supporting Information).[ 17 ] Conversely, although ACN is both volatile and environmentally benign, prior studies have reported that increasing its content beyond 50 vol% leads to poor process stability and degraded device performance. [ 14 ] This is primarily attributed to its large contact angle on FTO/SnO2 substrates, which results in insufficient substrate wetting and unstable meniscus formation during coating (Figure S2, Supporting Information).

Toxic solvents such as DMF, 2‐ME, and NMP show high risk across all categories, while green solvents like ACN and EtOH offer more favorable EHS profiles. c) PCEs of slot‐die coated FA‐based perovskite solar cells as a function of green solvent ratio.

In response to these limitations, we developed a hybrid solvent system that incorporates a high fraction of green solvents—specifically ACN and EtOH—while minimizing the use of toxic solvents such as 2‐ME and NMP. This formulation was deliberately designed to exceed 70 vol% green solvent content, as shown in the shaded region of the ternary diagram (Figure 2a). The goal was to create a system that balances precursor solubility, evaporation behavior, and substrate wettability while maintaining environmental safety. As highlighted in Figure 1c, our solvent design not only meets this green solvent threshold but also, as discussed in later sections, enables substantially higher PCE than previously reported formulations with lower green solvent content.

To evaluate the process compatibility of the designed solvent system, we systematically examined the solubility and wetting behavior of FA‐based perovskite precursors as a function of ethanol content in ACN‐based formulations. The ternary phase diagram in Figure 2a maps the solubility of 1 M precursor solutions within the compositional space defined by ACN, EtOH, and a combined toxic solvent corner composed of 2‐ME and NMP. Green and red dots represent soluble and insoluble formulations, respectively, while the green‐shaded region highlights compositions containing ≥70 vol% green solvents (ACN + EtOH)—the target for environmentally responsible processing.

To aid interpretation, formulations are denoted as ExAy, where E and A refer to the volume fractions (%) of ethanol and ACN, respectively. For instance, E0A50 indicates 0 vol% EtOH and 50 vol% ACN, while the remainder is composed of 2‐ME and NMP. The detailed solvent fractions for each sample are provided in Table S1 (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 2b, precursor solutions remained fully soluble up to 20 vol% EtOH but showed visible precipitation at 30 vol% (e.g., E30A50), indicating a solubility limit near this threshold. In comparison, isopropanol (IPA), another green solvent candidate, exhibited a much narrower solubility window: precursor solutions were stable only up to 10 vol% IPA, with black precipitates forming at 20 vol% (Figure S3, Supporting Information). These results demonstrate that EtOH enables a broader processing window and higher green solvent incorporation than IPA, making it a more effective co‐solvent for FA‐based perovskite precursor systems.[ 18 ]

We next assessed the wetting behavior of these formulations by measuring the contact angles of precursor solutions on FTO substrates (Figure 2c). The ethanol‐free solution (E0A50) exhibited a relatively high contact angle of 39.4°, whereas increasing the ethanol content progressively reduced the contact angle to 27.5° at 20 vol% ethanol (E20A50), indicating enhanced substrate wetting. In contrast, the formulation containing 70 vol% ACN and no ethanol (E0A70) showed a significantly higher contact angle of 43.1°, confirming the detrimental effect of excessive ACN on wetting. The improved wettability observed with ethanol‐containing formulations plays a critical role in stabilizing the meniscus during slot‐die coating. Ethanol enhances substrate wetting, which helps maintain a stable coating front and suppresses microbubble formation at the upstream die lip. These effects are essential for achieving uniform, rib‐free film formation during large‐area slot‐die processing.[ 19 ]

Enhancing the Quality of FA‐Based Perovskite Thin Films using Ethanol‐Mixed Solvent

To investigate how high green solvent content affects perovskite film formation, we formulated a series of mixed solvent systems containing over 70 vol% green solvents (ACN + EtOH). FA‐based perovskite films, (FAPbI3)0.95(MAPbBr3)0.05, were fabricated using representative solvent compositions and systematically characterized to evaluate the impact of solvent formulation on film morphology, crystallinity, and chemical interactions. Figure 3a presents scanning electron microscopy (SEM) top‐view images of perovskite films fabricated with different solvent formulations. As the EtOH content increases from E0A50 to E20A50, the grain size becomes larger and more uniform. Specifically, the E20A50 film exhibits well‐defined grains averaging ≈1 µm in diameter, whereas the control film (E0A50) shows submicron grains below 0.5 µm (Figure S4, Supporting Information). In contrast, the E0A70 film—with high ACN content and no EtOH—exhibits poorly defined grain boundaries, likely due to residual PbI2 and δ‐phase inclusions.

a) Top‐view SEM images of perovskite films fabricated from precursor solutions with varying ethanol and ACN contents. b) X‐ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the corresponding perovskite films. c) Enlarged view of the (100) peak region from (b), highlighting shifts in peak position. d) Full‐width at half maximum (FWHM) of the (100) diffraction peak. e) Grazing‐incidence XRD (GI‐XRD) profiles at different incident angles (αi = 0.1° to 2.0°) of perovskite films as a function of ethanol content. f) FT‐IR spectra of precursor solutions with different ethanol contents, showing variation in –OH stretching vibration (3100–3600 cm−1), indicating ethanol incorporation. g) FT‐IR spectra highlighting C═O stretching near 1655 cm−1, reflecting changes in solvent–precursor interaction as a function of ethanol content.

X‐ray diffraction (XRD) patterns in Figure 3b confirm that EtOH‐rich formulations enhance crystallinity. The full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the (100) diffraction peak narrows systematically with increasing EtOH content, as shown in Figure 3c,d, indicating reduced structural disorder and larger coherent domains.[ 20 ] Additionally, the PbI2 diffraction peak at 12.6°—associated with incomplete conversion—was prominent in E0A50 and E0A70 but significantly suppressed in the E20A50 film, suggesting improved phase purity with EtOH addition. Conversely, higher ACN content alone resulted in both broader FWHM and increased PbI2 signal, indicating lower overall film quality.[ 21 ] Grazing‐incidence XRD (GI‐XRD) analysis (Figure 3e) revealed depth‐dependent crystallographic uniformity. The E20A50 film showed minimal peak shift across incidence angles, indicating a homogeneous lattice structure from surface to bulk. In contrast, the E0A50 film exhibited noticeable peak shifts, implying non‐uniform strain distribution and compositional inhomogeneity.[ 22 ] To further assess optoelectronic quality, the Urbach energy (Eu) was calculated from UV–vis absorption spectra (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The E20A50 film exhibited a lower Eu than the control, reflecting fewer sub‐bandgap trap states and lower structural disorder—both beneficial for device performance.

Fourier‐transform infrared (FT‐IR) spectroscopy was used to probe solvent–solute interactions within the precursor (Figure 3f,g). The C═O stretching vibration of the NMP–PbI2 adduct remained at ≈1655 cm−1 regardless of EtOH content, confirming that EtOH does not disrupt the coordination between NMP and PbI2. However, the O─H stretching vibration exhibited a blueshift from 3345 to 3376 cm−1 upon EtOH addition, indicating secondary interactions involving EtOH without perturbing the primary coordination structure. These results suggest that EtOH improves solvent volatility and wetting behavior without compromising precursor complexation. In support of this, spin‐coating experiments (Figure S6, Supporting Information) revealed that solvent evaporation occurred more rapidly as the EtOH content increased, further confirming its role in enhancing volatility. This accelerated evaporation is expected to promote more effective gas quenching during slot‐die coating, thereby assisting uniform nucleation and film formation under scalable conditions. In addition, previous contact angle measurements (Figure 2c) demonstrated that increasing EtOH content significantly reduced the contact angle of the precursor solution on FTO substrates—from 39.4° (E0A50) to 27.5° (E20A50)—enhancing substrate wetting and enabling more stable meniscus formation during coating. This improved wetting behavior, combined with preserved coordination structure, facilitates uniform film formation during slot‐die processing.

AFM analysis further confirmed improved surface morphology. The E20A50 film exhibited a root mean square (RMS) roughness of 25.2 nm, substantially smoother than the 43.1 nm observed for the E0A50 film (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Smoother morphology is advantageous for charge extraction and minimizes interfacial recombination. Finally, X‐ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis (Figure S8, Supporting Information) showed that the Pb0 peak—indicative of metallic lead defects—decreased with increasing EtOH content. Given that Pb0 species act as deep trap sites detrimental to both efficiency and long‐term stability, their suppression highlights the positive role of EtOH in passivating defect states and improving device robustness.[ 23 , 24 ] Collectively, these findings demonstrate that EtOH incorporation into green solvent‐rich formulations improves solubility, wettability, crystallinity, morphological uniformity, and optoelectronic quality of slot‐die‐coated FA‐based perovskite films. These improvements support the viability of such formulations for scalable, high‐efficiency perovskite solar cell manufacturing.

Device Performance of n‐i‐p Structure Perovskite Solar Cell

To evaluate device performance under high green solvent conditions, we fabricated PSCs using precursors containing over 70 vol% green solvents (ACN + EtOH). The device structure employed in this study was glass/FTO/SnO2/perovskite/2′,7,7′‐tetrakis[N,N‐di(4‐methoxyphenyl)amino]−9,9′‐spirobifluorene (Spiro‐OMeTAD)/Au, as illustrated in Figure 4a. The J–V curves of these devices are shown in Figure 4b, and the corresponding performance parameters are summarized in Table 1 . Increasing the EtOH content led to enhanced device performance, with the highest PCE of 23.72% obtained for the E20A50 formulation. External quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Figure 4c) confirmed that the short‐circuit current density (JSC ) remained nearly unchanged across all conditions (24.34, 24.40, and 24.44 mA cm−2), indicating that the observed PCE improvements primarily originated from increases in open‐circuit voltage (VOC ) and fill factor (FF). Figure S9 (Supporting Information) shows that, over 1000 s, the steady‐state power output (SPO) of E20A50 exhibits higher operational stability than that of E0A50.

To elucidate the origin of these enhancements, which are strongly affected by trap‐mediated recombination, we investigated the recombination behavior and trap state density in the devices. Light‐intensity dependent VOC analysis (Figure 4d) revealed a lower ideality factor of 1.12 for the E20A50 target device compared to 1.66 for the E0A50 control device, indicating suppressed non‐radiative recombination. Consistently, space‐charge‐limited current (SCLC) measurements (Figure S10, Supporting Information) confirmed a lower trap density in the E20A50 film. E0A50 shows a trap density of 5.41 × 1014 cm−3, whereas E20A50 shows 4.84 × 1014 cm−3, confirming trap reduction. Furthermore, time‐resolved PL (TRPL) measurements (Figure 4e) showed extended carrier lifetimes with increasing EtOH content, further supporting reduced trap‐assisted recombination.[ 25 ] Complementary dark J–V measurements (Figure S11, Supporting Information) also exhibited a lower leakage current in the E20A50 device, highlighting improved diode characteristics attributable to lower defect density.[ 26 ]

Altogether, these results demonstrate that even with a green solvent content exceeding 70 vol%, the incorporation of EtOH into the precursor formulation significantly enhances film quality—achieving higher crystallinity, reduced trap states, and more uniform interfaces. These improvements directly translate into superior device performance, underscoring the potential of EtOH‐assisted green solvent systems for scalable, high‐efficiency perovskite solar cell fabrication via slot‐die coating. To evaluate the suitability of our formulation for scalable manufacturing, we assessed the performance distribution of 16 cells fabricated across a 2.5 cm × 10 cm substrate area (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The E20A50 condition yielded a narrow efficiency distribution, indicating excellent film uniformity and process reproducibility. In contrast, devices fabricated using the E0A50 formulation exhibited a broad efficiency spread ranging from 12% to 20%, highlighting significant variability in film quality (Figure 4f). Cross‐sectional SEM analysis of the same devices (Figure S13, Supporting Information) revealed that the film thickness variation across the substrate remained within 10%, further supporting the consistency of slot‐die coating with the EtOH‐containing precursor. These results indicate that the E20A50 formulation improves device performance and enables reliable and uniform deposition over large areas. This is essential for scalable slot‐die coating of high efficiency PSCs.

Device Performance of p‐i‐n Structure Perovskite Solar Cell

To evaluate the versatility and scalability of the target precursor formulation—previously shown to improve crystallinity and device performance in n–i–p architectures—we further examined its applicability to p–i–n structured PSCs fabricated via slot‐die coating. The p–i–n devices were constructed with a layer sequence of glass/ITO/NiOx/self‐assembled monolayer (SAM)/perovskite/C60/SnO2/Ag, and the perovskite layers were deposited using the same E0A50 (control) and E20A50 (target) conditions (Figure 5a).[ 27 ] The J–V curves of these devices are presented in Figure 5b, and the corresponding parameter values are summarized in Table 2 . The E20A50 device achieved a PCE of 21.4%, outperforming the control device (E0A50), which exhibited a PCE of 20.6%. The statistical box plots of the PCE distributions for devices fabricated from E0A50 and E20A50 inks are shown in Figure S14 (Supporting Information). This improvement was primarily driven by a higher VOC , while the JSC remained nearly identical between the two devices, as confirmed by EQE measurements (Figure 5c). These results align with the trends observed in n–i–p devices, highlighting the generalizability of the green solvent‐based strategy.

To assess long‐term operational stability, we conducted maximum power point tracking (MPPT) measurements under continuous 1‐sun illumination (Figure 5d). The initial efficiency was 21.4% for E20A50 and 20.6% for E0A50, and was evaluated by fabricating the structure shown in Figure 5a and encapsulating it with a natural curing resin. The E0A50 device exhibited a significant drop in efficiency, retaining only 80% of its initial PCE after ≈350 h. In contrast, the E20A50 device maintained 80% of its initial performance for over 750 h, underscoring its superior operational durability. These findings underscore that, despite containing over 70 vol% green solvents, the introduction of EtOH enhances both the initial performance and long‐term durability of PSCs, validating its potential for scalable, stable, and high‐efficiency slot‐die coated devices.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a formulation based on green solvents, containing more than 70 % environmentally benign ACN and EtOH, for scalable slot‐die coating of perovskite solar cells. We reduced the fraction of the relatively toxic solvents, 2‐ME and NMP, from 50% to 30% in the ink formulation. By systematically tuning the EtOH content, we demonstrated improved solubility, enhanced substrate wetting, and accelerated solvent evaporation—all critical parameters for high‐quality film formation. Notably, the optimized formulation with 20 vol% EtOH (E20A50) enabled perovskite films with larger grain size, improved crystallinity, reduced trap density, and enhanced optoelectronic properties. As a result, devices fabricated with this formulation achieved PCEs of 23.72% in the n–i–p structure and 21.4% in the p–i–n structure, alongside significantly improved operational stability. The E20A50 devices also exhibited narrow performance distributions across large areas, underscoring their compatibility with scalable manufacturing. Importantly, these results demonstrate that even with a high fraction of green solvents, careful solvent engineering can overcome processability challenges and unlock high‐performance, stable perovskite devices. This work provides a promising route toward environmentally sustainable and industrially viable PSC production.

Pensez : Less Data, Better Reasoning – Rethinking French LLM
Huy Hoang Ha

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in various natural language processing tasks. However, achieving strong performance in specialized domains like mathematical reasoning and non-English languages often requires extensive training on massive datasets. This paper investigates a contrasting approach: strategic fine-tuning on a small, high-quality, bilingual (English-French) dataset to enhance both the reasoning capabilities and French language proficiency of a large language model. Rather than relying on scale, we explore the hypothesis that targeted data curation and optimized training can achieve competitive, or even superior, performance. We demonstrate, through targeted supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on only 2,000 carefully selected samples, significant improvements in mathematical reasoning. Specifically, Pensez 7B exhibits an increase in accuracy of the base model up to 20 points on the AIME25 and a 12 points increase on a French MATH level 5 benchmark. These results challenge the prevailing assumption that massive datasets are a prerequisite for strong reasoning performance in LLMs, highlighting the potential of strategic data curation and optimized fine-tuning for enhancing both specialized skills and multilingual capabilities. Our findings have implications for the efficient development of high-performing, multilingual LLMs, especially in resource-constrained scenarios.

2. Bilingual Enhancement: We show that a balanced bilingual training approach (1:1 English-French ratio) enhances performance in both languages, addressing the common imbalance in multilingual LLMs.

3. Resource Availability: We release our curated dataset, training code, and fine-tuned model to facilitate reproducibility and further research in this area.

2.2
Data filtering

Our objective is to construct an optimally compact and high-quality dataset that minimizes computational resource demands during model training while retaining linguistic robustness. To achieve this, we implement a three-stage filtering pipeline, systematically refining an initial corpus into a final set of 2,000 samples, evenly distributed across two languages-English and French (1,000 samples per language). This curated selection is guided by three principal criteria:
Length
,
Language Purity
, and
Diversity
. These criteria are enforced following an initial quality assurance phase during data collection, ensuring that only high-fidelity samples proceed to subsequent refinement stages.

Length

Given that the target model is designed to handle a context length of 16,384 tokens, we filter out all samples exceeding this token count. Tokenization is performed using the tokenizer of the base model,
Qwen2.5 7B Instruct
, to ensure consistency with the training framework.

Language Purity

To verify linguistic integrity, we employ the FastText language identification model
[
22
]
. For each language, we establish a confidence threshold of 0.95, ensuring that only samples with high linguistic homogeneity are retained.

Diversity

To quantify diversity, we categorize each question into distinct task types using the Llama 3.3 70B Instruct model. The dataset is deliberately biased toward reasoning-related tasks to strengthen the model’s reasoning capabilities, while the remaining tasks are distributed evenly to maintain a balanced representation across other categories.

Following the application of these filters, our aim is to construct a final dataset comprising 2,000 samples, balanced evenly across English and French subsets (1,000 samples per language). However, due to an insufficient number of French samples—falling short of the target 1,000—we augment the French subset by randomly selecting additional samples from the English corpus and designating them as French for subsequent processing. This adjustment ensures the dataset meets the desired size and language distribution prior to the data transformation stage. Detailed sample counts for each subset, including the adjusted totals, are presented in
Table 2
. All of the questions are deduplicated to ensure the diversity of the dataset.

Data augmentation

To ensure a robust bilingual dataset capable of supporting advanced reasoning, we augment the curated samples through translation and enrich their reasoning content. To enable bilingual training across English and French, we translate the English samples into French using the Llama 3.3 70B Instruct model. This translation step ensures that the dataset maintains an equitable representation of both languages, with 1,000 samples per language. By aligning the linguistic content, we enhance the model to generalize reasoning capabilities across linguistic boundaries, enhancing its cross-lingual robustness. To this end, we utilize the Llama 3.3 70B Instruct model to generate concise reasoning chains for these samples. This augmentation ensures a uniform structural format across the dataset, seamlessly integrating tasks ranging from straightforward daily-life scenarios to complex reasoning challenges. Performance

Sample-efficiency

Figure

While not achieving the highest scores, Pensez 7B consistently ranks second across various benchmarks. This demonstrates that the model effectively extends its learning to reasoning skills while maintaining strong performance on knowledge tasks. Detailed scores are presented in Table

Math Reasoning

The Pensez 7B model achieves strong performance on the MATH Hard lv5 benchmark, closely approaching the leading score despite being trained on substantially fewer samples—approximately 400 times less than DeepSeek R1 7B. In contrast, DeepSeek R1 7B demonstrates superior performance on the AIME25 benchmark, indicating that its extensive training enhances capability in specific English mathematical tasks, potentially at the cost of generalization (see Table

Reasoning Abilities

In French reasoning tasks, Pensez 7B performs competitively on the BBH and GPQA diamond benchmarks, consistently ranking among the highest-scoring models. Its performance extends to English reasoning tasks, though DeepSeek R1 7B exhibits an advantage in select English evaluations, suggesting a divergence in training outcomes between mathematical specialization and linguistic versatility.

Reading Comprehension

In tasks requiring deep linguistic processing, such as BoolQA and BBH in French, Pensez 7B achieves near-top performance, underscoring its capability for nuanced understanding and multi-step reasoning—skills critical for advanced language applications.

Knowledge-Based Performance

Pensez 7B outperforms DeepSeek R1 7B in knowledge-intensive benchmarks, including MMLU and Trivia, across both French and English. This disparity suggests that DeepSeek R1 7B’s extensive training may lead to overfitting on mathematical reasoning, limiting its generalization to diverse knowledge domains, whereas Pensez 7B maintains balanced proficiency.

Overall Performance

Trained on only 2,000 samples, Pensez 7B matches or exceeds DeepSeek R1 7B’s performance in French reasoning and consistently outperforms it in knowledge tasks across both languages. While DeepSeek R1 7B’s extensive training improves English math and reasoning, it leads to overfitting and reduced knowledge capabilities. This underscores Pensez 7B’s efficiency and balanced skill development with minimal data.

4.3
Analysis

While Pensez demonstrates significant improvements in reasoning capabilities after SFT, a closer examination of its generated responses reveals a recurring pattern: a tendency towards excessive self-reflection and, in some cases, an inability to terminate the reasoning process, even when a correct answer has been identified (
Figure 8
). This phenomenon, which we refer to as ”overthinking,” presents a critical challenge to the model’s practical utility and provides valuable insights into the dynamics of reasoning in LLMs. This behavior is not exclusive to models trained for reasoning; similar tendencies can be observed in general instruction-following models like Qwen2.5 7B Instruct, as shown in Figure 5 and Figure 6.

Quantitative Analysis of Reflection

To quantify this ”overthinking,” we analyzed the frequency of ”reflection tokens” within the generated reasoning chains. We identified a set of keywords indicative of self-reflection: ”wait”, ”recheck”, ”retry”, ”alternatively”, ”however”, ”verify”, ”actually”, ”let me think”, ”let me verify”. We counted the occurrences of these keywords in both correct and incorrect predictions. More details on the reflection analysis can be found in
Table 3
.

Figure 7
, reveals a clear trend: incorrect predictions exhibit substantially higher average reflection counts than correct predictions. This counterintuitive finding suggests that while self-reflection can be beneficial for reasoning, excessive self-reflection is often associated with incorrect answers. The model, in essence, gets ”stuck” in a loop of re-evaluation, potentially losing track of the core reasoning path or introducing errors through repeated consideration of alternative (and often incorrect) possibilities.

Distribution of Response Types and Overthinking:

Figure 5
and
Figure 6
further illuminate this issue by showing the distribution of correct, incorrect, and ”incorrect (out of context)” responses for Pensez 7B, DeepSeek R1 7B and Qwen2.5 7B Instruct on MMLU-French and MATH Hard lv5 (French). We define ”incorrect (out of context)” responses as those where the model deviates significantly from the expected format or reasoning process, often due to excessive self-reflection or getting stuck in loops. A significant proportion of incorrect answers fall into this ”out of context” category, particularly for Pensez 7B. This suggests that while the model can often identify the correct answer (as evidenced by the non-zero ”correct” percentage), its inability to regulate its reasoning process frequently leads it astray.

Connection to Test-Time Compute and Task Difficulty

This ”overthinking” behavior directly relates to the concept of test-time compute. While Pensez is designed to leverage extended reasoning (akin to increased test-time compute), the model cannot currently effectively regulate this extended reasoning. It demonstrates the potential for improved performance with more thinking time, but also highlights the crucial need for mechanisms to control and terminate the reasoning process appropriately. This finding underscores the importance of not only increasing test-time compute but also optimizing its utilization.

Moreover, comparing model behavior between
Figure 5
(less challenging) and
Figure 6
(more challenging) also suggests that task difficulty plays a role. In the more challenging task, all of the models tend to be easier to overthink and give more out-of-context answers.

5.1
Sample-efficient reasoning

Multilingual LLMs

While many LLMs exhibit some degree of multilingual capability
[

,

,

Aya, BLOOM and mT5 represent further efforts to create massively multilingual models. Pensez contributes to this area by demonstrating that a balanced bilingual approach (1:1 English-French ratio) can improve performance in both languages, suggesting a path towards more equitable multilingual LLM development.

Achieving robust mathematical reasoning remains a significant challenge for LLMs. Several approaches have been explored, including pre-training on specialized mathematical corpora

,

Data-Efficient LLM Training

The prevailing paradigm in LLM training has emphasized scaling up both model size and training data
[
48
,
41
]
. However, recent work has begun to explore the potential for data efficiency. The ”Superficial Alignment Hypothesis,” as explored in LIMA
[
70
]
, suggests that a relatively small number of high-quality examples (around 1,000) can be sufficient to align a model’s output format with user preferences. This challenges the assumption that massive datasets are always necessary for strong performance. Our work extends this line of inquiry by demonstrating that data efficiency is not only possible but advantageous for eliciting reasoning capabilities, a domain traditionally thought to require extensive training data. This is line with concurrent with S1
[
34
]
and LIMO
[
65
]
as they also use small dataset for math reasoning model.

,

,

, self-consistency

, and tree-search methods

all aim to allocate more computational resources to individual problem-solving instances. The OpenAI o1 model

Future Work

Building upon the promising results achieved with
Pensez 7B
, our future research will focus on several key directions to further enhance its reasoning capabilities, address current limitations, and expand its applicability.

Reinforcement Learning with GRPO

We plan to leverage Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
[
45
]
to refine Pensez’s reasoning processes. GRPO offers an efficient and effective approach to reinforcement learning, and we hypothesize that it will further improve the model’s ability to generate coherent, logical, and accurate reasoning chains. Preliminary investigations suggest GRPO may also mitigate the observed tendency towards repetitiveness or ”overthinking” in extended reasoning chains, a known challenge in LLMs.

Enhanced Agentic Capabilities

We aim to extend Pensez’s capabilities beyond purely text-based reasoning by incorporating tool use and promoting more agent-like behavior. This will involve exploring methods for integrating external tools, such as web search, database search or symbolic solvers, into the model’s reasoning process. The goal is to create a more versatile and adaptable reasoning system capable of tackling problems that require interaction with external resources.

Domain Expansion to Reasoning-Intensive Fields

While
Pensez 7B
has demonstrated strong performance in mathematical reasoning, we intend to expand its application to other domains that demand sophisticated reasoning skills. A particular area of interest is the medical domain, where accurate diagnosis, treatment planning, and clinical decision-making rely heavily on complex logical inference and integration of diverse knowledge sources. Adapting Pensez to the medical domain will require curating specialized datasets and potentially developing novel reasoning strategies tailored to the unique challenges of this field.

Impact Statement

Language models with strong reasoning capabilities offer transformative potential for human productivity, from facilitating complex decision-making to accelerating scientific discovery. However, state-of-the-art reasoning models often cater primarily to English-speaking users, neglecting the unique needs and linguistic landscape of other communities. Furthermore, despite promising advances in European-focused LLMs such as CroissantLLM, EuroLLM, and Mistral, these models often lack robust reasoning abilities at smaller, readily deployable scales.

Recognizing this critical gap, Pensez aims to democratize access to both advanced reasoning and high-quality French language understanding within a single, efficient model. By strategically fine-tuning a smaller LLM on a carefully curated bilingual dataset, Pensez makes sophisticated reasoning accessible to a wider audience, particularly those for whom French is a primary language. This work seeks to not only enhance model capabilities but also to ensure that the benefits of AI are equitably distributed across languages and communities, thereby maximizing its positive societal impact.

Intraoperative findings and predictors for length of hospital stay among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy: a multi-centre prospective study
Authors: Anthony Kagimu, Ogbu E Ngim, Yakubu Sunday Bot, Isaac Edyedu, Grace Robert Iga, Theoneste Hakizimana, Joshua Muhumuza
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGeriatric patients commonly pose a significant challenge, mainly in emergency settings where the time to optimize the high-risk patient with other associated co-morbidities is inadequate, which predisposes these patients to a number of adverse outcomes postoperatively. There is paucity of data on intraoperative findings and predictors for length of hospital stay among elderly surgical patients in Uganda. This study assessed the intraoperative findings and predictors for length of hospital stay among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy at Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals in eastern, northern and western Uganda, respectively.MethodsThis was a prospective observational cohort in which elderly patients (≥ 65 years) that underwent emergency exploratory laparotomy were enrolled and followed up until time of discharge to assess for the number of days spent in hospital. The intraoperative findings were documented during surgery per study participant. SPSS version 26 was used for analysis, with modified Poisson regression done to determine the significant predictors. P < 0.05 was considered significant.ResultsOf the 86 participants, 29.1% were above 80 years of age. Majority were male (74.4%). The most common diagnosis was intestinal obstruction (55.8%) followed by gut perforation (30.2%). Of the 77 that survived, prolonged hospital stay was seen in 35.1%. The median length of hospital stay was 7 days, while the mean was 8.57 days. In the multivariable analysis, American association of anesthesiology class III and having a perforation were associated with prolonged hospital stay (P < 0.05 for all).ConclusionOver one third of the study participants had a prolonged hospital stay. Optimization of patients should focus on improving the American association of anesthesiology score, in order to create chances of early discharge and reduce cases of prolonged hospital stays.Keywords: Elderly surgery, Emergency laparotomy, Intraoperative findings, Prolonged hospital stay, Uganda

Background

The burden of postoperative adverse outcomes following abdominal surgeries is approximated at about 65.8% among elderly patients and 37.4% in non-elderly patients (Chua and Chan 2020). This is associated with an increased length of hospital stay since complications have been associated with prolonged hospital stay (Onen et al. 2022; Endeshaw et al. 2023). In the light of the previous studies, it is observed that the majority of the elderly patients who present to surgical care units usually have one or more associated co-morbidities that significantly compromise postoperative healing, delaying the recovery process; the common ones being diabetes and hypertension, among others (Fan et al. 2020; Ogbuanya et al. 2023; Endeshaw et al. 2023).

Emergency abdominal surgeries have been associated with unfavorable surgical care outcomes mainly because these patients often present with deranged body systems. These derangements are at times worsened by delays in receiving care that can be patient related or healthcare system related (Aakre et al. 2020; Ogwal et al. 2020; Ogbuanya et al. 2023). All the above contribute to the number of days spent in hospital.

The increasing number of the elderly brings concerns with hospitalization and length of stay (LOS). LOS usually refers to the number of days that a patient spends in hospital and uses health care facilities during this period (Orooji et al. 2021). Length of stay is the simplest and most useful indicator of hospital activity which is used to assess hospital care management, hospital planning, and quality of patient care; also, it can be considered as an important factor for hospital management to examine the efficiency and effectiveness of hospital services (Orooji et al. 2021). Since health centers, including hospitals, faced with resource constraints, reducing the length of hospital stay is a desirable and effective strategy to reduce unnecessary hospitalization cost.

Increased postoperative length of hospital stay is associated with increased risk for hospital-acquired infections, including surgical site infection (SSI) (Kebede et al. 2024). It has been recognized in the literature examining costs of hospital-acquired infections that length of hospital stay and hospital-acquired infection are interdependent, sharing some risk factors such as age, surgery, and severity of illness for which the patient was hospitalized (Kebede et al. 2024). In a recent study examining the interdependency of hospital-acquired infection and length of hospital stay, Hassan et al. reported that extending the length of stay by one day increased the probability of developing an infection by 1.37%, and conversely, that the development of infection increased average length of stay by 9.32 days (Hassan et al. 2010).

Intraoperative abdominal findings are diverse and are associated with a sizable portion of adverse surgical early outcomes in the elderly patients, which significantly predispose them to morbidity and mortality. Geriatric patients commonly pose a significant challenge, mainly in emergency settings where the time to optimize the high-risk patient with other associated co-morbidities is inadequate, which predisposes these patients to a number of adverse outcomes postoperatively (Halle-Smith et al. 2021; McCann et al. 2020; Menegozzo et al. 2019).

Despite the fact that a significant number of elderly patients in Uganda require emergency laparotomy, no study has described the common intraoperative findings in this category of patients, nor the predictors for length of hospital stay following surgery in this category of patients. This paucity of data is a tangible gap in the knowledge, for which reason this study aimed to asses the intraoperative findings and predictors for length of hospital stay among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy at Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals.

Methods

Study design and period

This study was a prospective observational cohort that covered a period of three months in which elderly patients (> 65 years) that underwent emergency exploratory laparotomy were enrolled and followed up until time of discharge to assess the number of days spent in hospital. The emergency laparotomy intraoperative findings were documented per study participant.

Study setting

The study sites were Jinja, Lira, Hoima, and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals, wherein the study participants were recruited. Hoima and Fort Portal Regional Referral Hospitals are located in western Uganda in Hoima and Fort Portal cities, respectively, while Lira Regional Referral Hospital is located in northern Uganda in Lira City. Jinja Regional Referral Hospital is located in Eastern Uganda in Jinja City. All these hospitals have consultant surgeons, senior house officers, medical officers and other health workers attached to surgical wards. The departmental patient flow per day varies linearly and continuously from one hospital to another due to the different dynamics of each city and the population demographics. All these hospitals receive a sizeable number of elderly patients that undergo laparotomies, which was the target of this study.

Study population

We targeted patients aged 65 years and older with emergency abdominal pathology that needed to be addressed by an emergency exploratory laparotomy. All patients aged 65 years and above undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy that gave permission to take part in the research were included. The participants who were not sure of their age were requested to avail their national identity card. Patients with mental illness, involved in poly-trauma or confirmed metastatic abdominal malignancy were excluded.

The sampling procedure was consecutive recruitment of study participants.

Recruitment and data collection procedure

Any patient aged 65 years and older due to undergo laparotomy as decided by the attending surgeon was approached and explained the objective of the study and why his or her participation was required. Any participant who accepted to participate and fulfilled the eligibility criteria signed an informed consent document. The baseline characteristics of the patients were assessed and documented in the data collection form. After consent, the patient underwent the surgery as recommended by the attending surgeon, and the study team documented the intraoperative findings during the course of the operation. When clarity was needed, the attending surgeon helped to clarify the intraoperative findings. Follow-up was done till the time of discharge. The number of days spent in hospital were documented at discharge. Prolonged hospital stay referred to staying in the hospital for a duration above the median of the population, which was found to be 7 days in this study.

Study variables

The study variables included independent variables like socioeconomic characteristics of elderly patients, such as sex, age, alcohol consumption, and cigarette smoking. Clinical characteristics of elderly patients included: body mass index, MUAC, time to presentation, time from presentation to surgery, duration of surgery, ASA class, wound class, blood transfusion, intraoperative findings, cadre of operating surgeon, hemoglobin levels, WBCs, and platelets. The study dependent variable was length of hospital stay.

Quality control

A training session was held at the start of the study and in between the study period to refresh the research assistants about the special category of elderly surgical patients and how best these patients could be managed in the respective local settings. We employed coded patient numbers for proper patient identification to prevent participant duplication. Data collection tools were well re-checked to confirm the adequate completeness of study participant data prior to their discharge. Case files and other relevant documents of the patients were referred to so as to gather any additional relevant information.

Data analysis plan

Microsoft office excel was used to create excel sheets with a summary of the collected data. The Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA, version 26.0 for Windows) was used for data cleaning and later data analysis. The percentages and frequencies of the intra-operative findings were computed as a fraction of all the elderly patients that underwent emergency laparotomies, and the data was represented in a table. The mean and median length of hospital stay was computed. The percentages and frequencies of patients with prolonged hospital stay was computed, and data was presented in a table. Prolonged hospital stay referred to staying in the hospital for a duration above the median. Modified Poisson regression with a 95% confidence interval and a P value of ≤ 0.05 was used at levels of bivariate and multivariate analysis. Variables with P ≤ 0.05 were considered significant predictors of prolonged hospital stay among elderly patients who underwent emergency laparotomies.

Ethical considerations and consent

All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was granted by Kampala International University western campus Research Ethics Committee (KIU-2024 492). All participants gave written informed consent as evidenced by the participant’s signature.

Discussion

Regarding the intraoperative findings, the commonest diagnoses were intestinal obstruction (55.8%) and gut perforation (30.2%). The main causes of intestinal obstruction were adhesions and hernia, each accounting for 37.5% of the intestinal obstruction cases. The commonest type of hernia was inguinal accounting for 77.8% of the cases. The commonest perforation site was gastroduodenal, seen in 76.9% of the perforations.

According to the study conducted in China, intraoperative findings included hernia (1.7%), cholecystitis (2.5%), and abdominal wall hernia (7.1%) (Joo et al., 2017) which conditions were also seen among our study participants. However, contrary to our findings, Joo et al. reported lower percentages of hernia cases. Moreover, we did not have cases operated due to cholecystitis in our study population. The possible explanation for not having surgery due to cholecystitis could be because a number of patients with conditions in the hepatobiliary system are referred for endoscopic procedures or to the hepatobiliary surgeons who were not available at the study centres at the time of the study.

In East Africa, particularly in a Tanzania hospital-based prospective study, intestinal adhesions were noted as an intraoperative finding leading to small and large bowel obstruction, and other intraoperative findings included abdominal tumours, hernias, and volvulus (Mbembati and Melkizedeck 2024) which is comparable to the findings in this study where intestinal adhesions were among the commonest cause of intestinal obstruction among the elderly.

On the other hand, in Singapore, ileal diverticulum perforation was associated with small bowel perforation (Rajaguru and Sheong 2021). This is inconsistent with our findings where we didn’t register any elderly patients with ileal diverticulum. This can be explained by the racial difference. According to a study by (Sagar et al. 2006), congenital birth defects were considered a significant risk factor for ileal diverticulum (Sagar et al. 2006).

Our findings indicate that the main causes of emergency laparotomy among elderly patients are intestinal obstruction and perforation. The fact that these are the commonest findings, highlight the need for all surgeons attending to elderly patients to be well versed with the surgery of intestinal obstruction and bowel perforation, in order to ensure the most appropriate interventions among these elderly patients.

Predictors for prolonged hospital stay among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy

Regarding the predictors for prolonged hospital stay among elderly patients undergoing emergency exploratory laparotomy, having a high American association of anaesthesiology (ASA) score and having a perforation were found to be significant independent predictors. In agreement with our findings, in China, American Society of Anaesthiologists III and IV were associated with adverse outcomes among elderly patients following abdominal surgeries, with a notable hazard ratio of 2.61 in patients that present with emergency abdominal surgical diseases (Chua and Chan 2020). According to the study conducted in China by (Song et al. 2020), ASA above III was associated with longer hospital stay which is in agreement with our study.

An ASA class III or IV indicates that the patient has severe systemic disease, thus significantly increasing their risk for surgical complications because several underlying medical issues can complicate the care for anesthesia and the recovery process itself, with higher rates of possible complications like wound infection, cardiovascular, pulmonary complications, and longer hospital stay than those with lower ASA classification.

A gut perforation greatly increases the danger of complications and death because it allows intestinal contents to pass into the abdominal space, which can lead to a severe inflammatory response, resulting in peritonitis, sepsis, and multi-organ failure as a possible result if not treated, with death occurring in severe instances. In agreement, a study conducted by (Busch and Gutzwiller 2011) revealed that perforation was associated with a higher reintervention rate and increased length of hospital stay which is in agreement with this study.

Our findings highlight that a patient with a high ASA score, a marker of a patient’s preoperative physical health, is more likely to have a prolonged hospital stay. This calls for proper optimization of these patients to ensure that they have a better physical health before surgery. But also, ensuring the patients understand the risk as well as the prognosis in addition to the possibility of having a prolonged hospital stay followings surgery. Similarly, the presence of a perforations should alert the health care team about the potential for prolonged hospital stay.

Study limitation

The study was limited to government funded regional referral hospitals, yet the care for elderly patients undergoing surgery may differ significantly if the patients are in a private hospital, or a lower-level health facility; which limits the generalizability of our findings. Also, some other variables that could have had effect on the length of hospital stay were not assed. For example, the exact contribution of complications was not assessed; however, a table showing the rate of complications in those who had prolonged hospital stay and those who did not, has been included as Table 5.

Conclusion

Over one third of the study participants had a prolonged hospital stay. The independent predictors for prolonged hospital stay were, having a high American association of anesthesiology score and having a perforation. All elderly patients who present with a high American association of anesthesiology score or a perforation should be informed that they have a high possibility of staying longer in hospital, which will help them to have realistic expectations and proper planning.

The commonest diagnoses were intestinal obstruction and perforation. All doctors attending to elderly patients requiring surgical care should be well versed with the surgery of intestinal obstruction and gut perforations, since these were the commonest. Another study including private hospitals and lower-level health facilities should be conducted, to yield results that may be generalizable to all hospitals in Uganda.

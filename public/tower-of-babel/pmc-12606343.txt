Optimization of A comprehensive dispatching system based on ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight power dispatching strategy
Authors: Jingwei Ming, Zhiqiang Xie, Haikun Teng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this study, we explored an optimization method for a comprehensive power dispatching system based on the fusion of ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy. Firstly, the limitations of the existing scheduling system are introduced. Then, the proposed optimization methods are elaborated in detail, including the basic principle of ant colony algorithm, the design of dynamic weight scheduling strategy, and the fusion mode of the two. A large number of experimental data prove that this method is superior to the traditional scheduling method. Experimental results show that the integrated scheduling system optimization method based on ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy significantly improves the scheduling efficiency and resource utilization. Specifically, the method reduces the average dispatch time by 20% and improves the resource utilization by 15% when dealing with large-scale power dispatching problems. This indicates that the method has high practical value and can provide strong support for the optimization of scheduling system in related fields.

Introduction

In modern power systems, with large-scale access to renewable energy and the continuous development of the power market, power dispatching is facing unprecedented challenges1. Traditional dispatching methods have been difficult to adapt to the increasingly complex and changeable power environment. Therefore, developing an efficient and intelligent power-dispatching system is particularly important. As an optimization algorithm to simulate the foraging behaviour of ants in nature, the ant colony algorithm has the advantages of global solid search ability and good robustness, which provides a new idea for solving power dispatching problems. However, some limitations exist in relying solely on the ant colony algorithm for power dispatching, such as slow convergence speed and easy falling into local optimal solution2.

In today’s complex and changeable business environment, the integrated scheduling system is faced with extremely complex scheduling tasks. Taking the field of logistics and distribution as an example, it is necessary to weigh multiple factors such as vehicle deployment, distribution route planning, cargo loading and unloading sequence, and traffic conditions at different times. Manufacturing production scheduling is no exception, taking into account many aspects such as raw material supply, equipment operation status, order urgency, and worker scheduling. When the traditional scheduling method copes with such a complex task system, its efficiency and effectiveness are difficult to meet the actual demand, and there are often distribution delays, increased production costs, and low resource utilization. In this context, new optimization strategies are urgently needed to improve the performance of the integrated dispatching system. With its self-organizing and distributed search characteristics, the ant colony algorithm shows great potential in solving complex combinatorial optimization problems, and can gradually explore a near-optimal scheduling scheme by simulating the foraging behavior of ant colonies. The dynamic weight scheduling strategy can flexibly adjust the weight of each scheduling factor according to the real-time status of the task and the operation of the system, so as to make the scheduling decision more targeted and adaptable.

In order to solve the above problems, this paper proposes optimization research on a comprehensive scheduling system based on the fusion of ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy. This study focuses on the field of power dispatching, aiming to improve the efficiency and stability of power dispatching by combining ant colony algorithm with dynamic weight scheduling strategy. In practice, the global search advantage of ant colony algorithm is used to find the optimal scheduling scheme of the power system, and the dynamic weight scheduling strategy is integrated to optimize the information exchange between ants, accelerate the convergence process, and avoid the local optimal trap3,4.

This study aims to provide innovative solutions for optimizing and scheduling power systems using advanced algorithms and technologies. To demonstrate our method’s scientific value and practical application potential in this field, we plan to establish a high-complexity, multi-source, multi-load, and multi-constraint power system model, which will be a model that intersects artificial intelligence and power engineering. We will use this model for simulation analysis and systematically evaluate the advantages of our proposed method in terms of scheduling efficiency, system stability, and operational feasibility by comparing it with traditional power dispatch methods. Specifically, we will conduct in-depth research on the integration of an ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy, which is an innovative attempt to combine artificial intelligence algorithms with power scheduling requirements. We will not only analyze the scientific basis of this fusion mechanism theoretically, but also verify its actual effectiveness in power dispatch through carefully designed experiments, including comparative experiments and sensitivity analysis, to fully understand its performance boundary.

As a heuristic algorithm that simulates ant foraging behavior, ant colony algorithm has shown advantages in solving complex discrete optimization problems such as traveling salesman problem and resource scheduling by virtue of the positive feedback mechanism of pheromones, and has been widely used in resource scheduling scenarios such as power resource scheduling and logistics resource scheduling. However, the traditional ant colony algorithm has some shortcomings, such as lack of initial pheromones, easy to fall into local optimum, and low efficiency in large-scale problems. The dynamic weight scheduling strategy dynamically adjusts the weight parameters of the algorithm according to the real-time status of the system, such as load changes and power generation equipment operating parameters in the power dispatching system, or referring to the status of the task queue and node health in the task scheduling, so as to enhance the search orientation. In deep learning scenarios such as multi-task learning, object detection, generative adversarial network training, and time series prediction, as well as practical applications such as 302 dispatching servers to deal with emergencies, DSP (Digital Signal Processor) instruction scheduling, and PCDN (P2P Content Delivery Network) system running task scheduling, dynamic weight optimization has achieved good results, overcoming the limitations of traditional static weight allocation. The combination of ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy brings a new direction for the optimization of integrated scheduling system, which is expected to guide ant colony search by dynamically updating weights, improve the performance of ant colony algorithm, and improve the efficiency and stability of integrated scheduling system, but there are still challenges in improving algorithm efficiency to cope with large-scale complex problems, in-depth exploration of new application fields, and research of new optimization strategies, which require further research.

Due to its strict requirements for real-time performance, security and load balancing, the power dispatching system is significantly different from the general integrated dispatching system. In the research on the optimization of the integrated dispatching system based on the ant colony algorithm and the dynamic weight scheduling strategy, the power dispatching system needs to ensure real-time reliability at all times, and the dispatching strategy is required to be able to respond to the change of power grid status in milliseconds and quickly adjust the power generation and transmission schemes. In terms of safety constraints, it is necessary to strictly follow the safety regulations of power grid operation, such as line transmission capacity limitations, node voltage stability ranges, etc., to avoid risks such as overload and voltage collapse; The dynamic load balancing requirements need to comprehensively consider the real-time fluctuations of electricity load in different regions, the intermittency and randomness of new energy power generation, and optimize the distribution of power generation resources through dynamic weight adjustment to maintain the balance between power supply and demand. These particularities bring unique challenges and optimization space for the application of ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy.

In this study, an ant colony algorithm based on dynamic weight scheduling strategy was proposed to optimize the integrated scheduling system. The ant colony algorithm simulates ant foraging, and the power dispatching scheme is analogous to the path, and the pheromone concentration represents the scheme selection probability, but the traditional ant colony algorithm has problems such as lack of initial pheromones and easy to fall into local optimum. The dynamic weight scheduling strategy enhances the search orientation by monitoring the operation status of the power system in real time, such as load changes and power generation equipment parameters, and dynamically adjusting the weight parameters of the algorithm. After the combination of the two, the runtime updates the weights according to the real-time status, the ant colony searches for the scheduling scheme under the guidance of the weights, and the pheromone updates affect the subsequent selection to achieve system optimization. In order to verify its effectiveness, the typical power system is selected as a case, the detailed operation data such as power generation power and electricity demand are collected, and the algorithm is used to optimize the dispatching scheme, and compared with the traditional method and the single ant colony algorithm, the efficiency indicators such as the generation time and system power loss of the dispatching scheme, as well as the stability indicators such as voltage offset and frequency fluctuation range are evaluated.

Introduction and analysis of ant algorithm

Basic ideas and principles of ant algorithm

The ant algorithm, derived from observing ant foraging behavior, simulates the behavior of ants releasing pheromones while searching for food. The pheromones secreted by ants on the pathway gradually dissipate with time, forming multiple pheromone pathways. Later, ants tend to select these pathways probabilistically, whereas pathways with higher pheromone concentrations are more likely to be selected5. This ingenious mechanism facilitates the collective intelligence of the ant colony in discerning and adhering to the most optimal path, showcasing the algorithm’s adeptness in mimicking nature’s problem-solving strategies.

The dynamic weight scheduling strategy injects dynamic adaptability into the search process of the ant colony algorithm. In a scheduling system, the priority of tasks, the availability of resources, and the real-time status of the system are constantly changing. The dynamic weight scheduling strategy can adjust the task’s weight according to this real-time information to guide the ant colony algorithm to more intelligently bias towards the current optimal solution in the search process while avoiding falling into the local optimum. The dynamic nature of this strategy ensures that the algorithm can flexibly respond to the uncertainty in the scheduling environment and improve the overall scheduling efficiency. The integrated scheduling system integrating these two methods can effectively deal with the scheduling problem under multi-objective and multi-constraint conditions, which not only improves the efficiency and quality of scheduling but also enhances the robustness and adaptability of the system.

This process leads to the spontaneous concentration of ant colonies on the shortest path, which embodies ant algorithms’ self-organization and optimization characteristics7,8. This phenomenon underscores the algorithm’s efficiency and its ability to emulate the intricate decision-making processes observed in nature, highlighting its potential applications in a myriad of complex optimization scenarios.

Implementation of ant algorithm

In this ant foraging scenario, ants depend on the pheromone concentration on the path to determine their travel route. At the initial time t = 0, the pheromone concentration of all paths is 0, and the ants are evenly distributed at points B and D, with 15 ants on each possible path. Since the distance of BH and DH is equal, while the distance of BC and DC is equal, but the latter has a shorter distance, this affects the accumulation speed of pheromones11,12.

When t = 1, more ants chose this pathway due to the shorter BC and DC pathways, resulting in the pheromone concentration reaching 30 on the BC pathway compared to 15 on the BH pathway. This difference further influenced the selection of subsequent ants, making more new ants choose BC pathways with higher pheromone concentrations.

As this positive feedback mechanism continues to work over time, the pheromone concentration on the shortest path BCD will increase, attracting more ants to choose this path13. On the contrary, the pheromone concentration on the longer paths BH and DH increased slowly, eventually gradually causing ants to abandon these paths14. Through pheromone accumulation and volatilization, ants realized the spontaneous discovery and selection of the shortest path. This process embodies an aspect of swarm intelligence: simple interactions between individuals can lead to complex and compelling collective behavior.

Research method of dynamic weight dispatching problem

Deterministic algorithm

In this study, it is a highly innovative method to combine ant colony optimization (ACO) with dynamic weight scheduling strategy. Although ant colony algorithm has unique advantages in solving combinatorial optimization problems, it is easy to fall into local optimum and slow convergence speed in complex scheduling scenarios. The dynamic weight scheduling strategy can flexibly adjust the weights of each scheduling factor according to the real-time status of the scheduling task, resource status, and system operation stage to enhance the system adaptability. In this paper, the fusion framework is constructed to integrate the dynamic weight scheduling strategy into the pheromone update and path selection process of the ant colony algorithm, and adjust the pheromone update and path selection probability according to the dynamic weight. After analysis and comparison, the combination method has significant advantages over ACO alone, which can not only greatly improve the optimization effect, such as reducing the distribution cost by 15% and shortening the delivery time by 10% in logistics distribution scheduling, but also accelerating the convergence speed and shortening the running time by 20%, and at the same time enhancing the ability to resist local optimality, and increasing the number of successful jumps out of the local optimal by 30%, which provides a better solution for the optimization of the integrated scheduling system and has broad application prospects.

The core of a deterministic algorithm lies in using mathematical means to solve batch dispatching problems. Its foundation is often based on mathematical induction, and the accurate solution of problems is realized by constructing mathematical models. Deterministic algorithms include linear programming, dynamic programming and branch-and-bound method15. Approximation algorithm

Heuristic algorithm

The heuristic algorithm can obtain the approximate optimal solution of the problem at a low cost or within reasonable time and space constraints. The advantages of such algorithms are that they are easy to understand and modify, but their disadvantages include insufficient stability, limited efficiency, and only a limited number of solutions16. The heuristic algorithm is unsuitable for all problems and needs to be redesigned when the problem changes. In the study of batch dispatching problems, a heuristic algorithm was used initially for single-machine batch dispatching problems. Subsequently, various heuristic algorithms were developed to adapt to dispatching scenarios17.

The First-Fit (FF) algorithm first randomly sorts all workpieces and then, starting with the first workpiece puts it into an existing batch that can accommodate it. A new batch is created to accommodate the workpiece if there is no suitable batch. This process is repeated until all workpieces are assigned to the batch on the machine18. The Best-Fit First (BFF) algorithm is similar to the FF algorithm in that all artefacts are randomly sorted first. Then, starting with the first workpiece is placed in the first batch that can accommodate it and has the smallest remaining capacity. If there is no such batch, create a new batch. Continue this operation until all workpieces are assigned to the batch on the machine. The First-Fit Largest Processing Time (FFLPT) algorithm first arranges the workpieces in descending order according to the processing time and then divides them into batches according to the principle of the FF algorithm until all the workpieces are classified into each batch. The core idea of this algorithm is to group workpieces with extended processing time into the same batch as much as possible to optimize the overall dispatching efficiency19,20.

Meta-heuristic algorithm

In order to comprehensively evaluate the advantages of the proposed method, we selected advanced meta-heuristics such as particle swarm optimization algorithm (PSO) and genetic algorithm (GA) to compare the performance, efficiency and applicability. For example, in the large-scale project resource scheduling scenario, the scheme found by the proposed method can shorten the project duration by 15% and reduce the resource waste by 20% on average, compared with the average shortening of the construction period and the reduction of resource waste by 15% for PSO, 8% for GA and 12% for resource waste. This is due to the fact that the dynamic weight scheduling strategy can adjust the search direction of the ant colony according to the real-time state of the task, and more accurately approximate the global optimal solution. In terms of efficiency, taking the shop floor scheduling problem as an example, the average convergence algebra of the combination of ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight scheduling strategy is 30% less than that of GA and 20% less than that of PSO. In terms of applicability, in the face of different types of scheduling tasks such as resource-constrained logistics and distribution scheduling or time-constrained production scheduling, the proposed method can adapt to the characteristics of the task by reasonably adjusting the dynamic weight parameters, while the PSO is not effective in dealing with discrete scheduling tasks, and GA is prone to premature convergence under complex constraints. In comprehensive comparison, the ant colony algorithm and the dynamic weight scheduling strategy have obvious advantages in performance, efficiency and applicability, which provides a better choice for the optimization of the integrated scheduling system.

Ant colony optimization algorithm (ACO) is an intelligent optimization algorithm inspired by the foraging behavior of ants in nature. The core mechanism simulates ants releasing pheromones, attracting more ants to move along the path with high pheromone concentration, forming a positive feedback effect. The ACO algorithm has been applied to multiple NP-hard problems, such as batch dispatching problems, and has shown good performance21. However, ACO algorithms usually provide a general strategy, and for specific problems, further analysis and customized design are still needed. The framework of the ACO algorithm includes critical steps such as initialization, pheromone update, path selection, etc., and the optimal solution to the problem is found by simulating the behavior of an ant colony22.

Particle swarm optimization (PSO) is a neighborhood search-based metaheuristic algorithm proposed by Eberhart and Kennedy23. Inspired by the foraging behavior of birds, the PSO algorithm finds the optimal solution by simulating the social interaction between individuals. Because of its simple structure and easy implementation, the PSO algorithm has been widely used in many fields.

Genetic algorithm (GA) is a meta-heuristic algorithm that simulates the evolutionary process of organisms. GA directly operates on structural objects, has good implicit parallelism and global optimization ability, and can automatically adjust the search range to improve efficiency24. The framework of genetic algorithms includes critical steps such as selection, crossover, and mutation to optimize solutions by simulating natural selection and genetic mechanisms.

Research on dynamic weight dispatching problem without arrival time

Problem description and lower bound

In this study, the model of the power dispatching system adopts a hierarchical distributed architecture, and the system as a whole can be divided into three levels: the bottom-level operation unit, the middle-level regional dispatching center and the top-level central dispatching center. As the core foundation of the power system, the bottom layer is composed of the power generation side, the transmission network and the power consumption side. The power generation side includes thermal power, hydropower, wind power, photovoltaic and other power supply types, and thermal power is dominated by stable baseload supply, but the adjustment ability is limited when the load changes rapidly. Wind power and photovoltaic rely on natural conditions, and the output is obviously intermittent and unpredictable. The transmission network includes key equipment such as transmission lines and transformers, and the transmission capacity of the lines, the impedance characteristics, and the transformation ratio parameters of the transformers directly affect the efficiency and safety of power transmission. The electricity consumption side is subdivided into industrial, commercial, residential and other categories according to user attributes, with industrial users having large and periodic electricity consumption, while residential users show a concentrated trend of electricity consumption in the morning and evening peaks, and the dynamic change characteristics of various types of user loads are significant.

The middle-level regional dispatching center plays a key role as a data hub, and preliminarily integrates and analyzes scattered data by collecting real-time information such as unit output and equipment status on the power generation side, line power flow and node voltage of the transmission network, and load demand on the power consumption side. The top-level central dispatch center receives the data uploaded by the regional dispatch center, takes the ant colony algorithm as the core optimization tool, and combines the dynamic weight scheduling strategy to comprehensively consider the real-time reliability, security constraints and dynamic load balancing requirements. In terms of safety constraints, the constraints such as the thermal stability limit of the line and the safety interval of the node voltage are strictly followed. In terms of dynamic load balancing, the power distribution of each power source is dynamically adjusted according to the fluctuation of new energy output and the change of user load. The optimal dispatching instructions generated by the central dispatching center are fed back to the bottom layer through the regional dispatching center to realize the collaborative control of power generation equipment and transmission network, and the close correlation and collaborative operation between all levels and components form a complete power dispatching system model.

Ant colony optimization algorithm

Pheromone

How pheromones are defined and tuned is crucial when Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) is applied to a specific problem. This usually needs to be tailored to the characteristics of the problem, such as in dispatching problems, where pheromones may be related to the dispatching order of artefacts or the machine’s load.

In our research, ants selected suitable artefacts from the list of unprocessed artefacts through repeated iterations and gradually formed their solutions. The process involves the accumulation and volatilization of pheromones and the behavior of ants to make choices based on pheromone concentrations. Candidate list

In the ACO algorithm, we need to deal with a challenge to build a solution to the problem efficiently. Each must choose from a vast collection of unscheduled artefacts. To solve this problem, we introduce the concept of candidate lists.

A candidate list is a curated collection of artefacts, which contains artefacts in the current partial solution that have the potential to become components of the optimal solution. In this way, we limit the search scope of ants only to consider those artefacts that are more likely to bring reasonable solutions, thus avoiding wasting time in a hopeless search space28,29.

The advantage of building a candidate list is that it significantly reduces the dimension of the search space and speeds up the search process. However, this also poses the risk that the potential optimal solution may be inadvertently excluded if the candidate list is not selected correctly. Therefore, designing a candidate list strategy is crucial to reduce the search space while maintaining the solution quality effectively. For the ant colony algorithm, the pheromone volatility coefficient is generally set between 0.1 and 0.9, which is tested by a large number of experiments, and the value is too large will cause the ants to rely too much on past information, and the search is easy to fall into the local optimum. If the value is too small, the algorithm will search for randomness and the convergence speed will be slow. The pheromone heuristics are usually between 1 and 4, which determines how much attention the ants attach to the pheromone concentration when selecting the path, and the higher the value, the more inclined the ants are to choose the path with high pheromone concentration, which helps to converge quickly but may miss the global optimal solution. For the dynamic weight scheduling strategy, the weight update frequency depends on the frequency of the scheduling task change, for example, in the logistics and distribution scenario where the traffic flow changes frequently, the weight is updated every 15–30 min to respond to the task dynamics in a timely manner. The weight adjustment range is controlled between 0.1 and 0.3, the amplitude is too small to effectively adapt to task changes, and too large a magnitude may lead to system instability. These parameter settings are comprehensively determined through theoretical analysis, simulation experiments and practical application scenario tests, which directly affect the performance of the integrated scheduling system, and reasonable parameter settings can enable the system to achieve more efficient resource allocation and task scheduling in the face of complex scheduling tasks, and improve the overall operation efficiency.

Experimental design

In order to fully evaluate the performance and robustness of the algorithm, we carefully designed a series of test cases that were randomly generated based on the parameter space of the number of artefacts (n), size (s), and number of machines (m), ensuring the breadth and depth of the experimental design. Specifically, we consider four levels of workpiece count (20, 40, 100, 150), three levels of machine count (1, 3, 6), and three levels of workpiece size, corresponding to small, mixed, and large sizes, with ranges of1,15, [1, 40], and [15, 35], respectively. Combining these parameter levels, we construct 36 unique and representative data configurations, each generating 10 independent random instances to cover the algorithm’s performance in different scenarios.

The system of our study is equipped with 6 synchronous generators, covering 4 thermal power units (the rated power of a single unit is 300 MW, 400 MW, 500 MW and 600 MW respectively, the adjustment response time is 5–10 s, the minimum technical output is 25%−30% of the rated power, and the climbing rate is 5% of the rated power/minute, which is suitable for the dual roles of “base load + peak shaving” in dynamic weight scheduling) and 2 hydropower units (the rated power of a single unit is 200 MW and 350 MW, the adjustment response time is ≤ 3 s, and the minimum output can be as low as 10% of the rated power. Integrate a total of 12 renewable energy units in 3 categories, including 5 photovoltaic power stations (2 single station rated power of 50 MW, 2 each of 100 MW and 1 of 150 MW, with output fluctuation range of 0–100%, equipped with 5% rated power energy storage buffer units, prediction error controlled within ± 8%), 4 wind farms (including 2 onshore wind power units (single unit 2.5 MW × 20 units) and 2 offshore wind power units (single unit 4 MW × 25 units), cut out wind speed 25 m/s, output prediction period 15 min, fluctuation range ± 15%), and 3 biomass energy units (single unit rated power 20 MW, output stability ≥ 90%). Configure 18 transformers to build a multi-level voltage network, including 6 main network transformers (220 kV/110 kV, capacity 100MVA-200MVA, short-circuit impedance 8%−10%, connecting synchronous units and backbone network) and 12 distribution network transformers (110 kV/10 kV, capacity 20MVA-50MVA, short-circuit impedance 4%−6%, distributed in load centers, connected to renewable energy and user side); The total peak load is 1800 MW, including three types of asynchronous motor clusters, with industrial loads accounting for 40% (100 high-voltage motors, power 2 MW-5 MW, power factor 0.85–0.9, load fluctuation ± 5%, concentrated in industrial parks), commercial loads accounting for 30% (200 low-voltage motors, power 0.1 MW-1 MW, power factor 0.8–0.85, daytime load accounting for 70%, distributed in commercial areas), residential load accounting for 30% (500 household motors, power ≤ 0.05 MW, power factor 0.75–0.8, evening peak (18:00–22:00) load accounting for 60%); There are a total of 25 power supply nodes in the entire system, forming a multi-energy complementary network, including 6 main power supply nodes (corresponding to synchronous generators, connected to the 220 kV backbone network, node impedance 0.05–0.1 pu, supporting ± 10% voltage regulation), new energy 12 nodes (photovoltaic/wind farm grid connection points, connected to the 110 kV distribution network, equipped with SVG dynamic reactive power compensation, node voltage deviation ≤ ± 5%), 7 energy storage nodes (including lithium battery energy storage (50 MW/200MWh) and pumped hydro storage (100 MW/400MWh).

Conclusion

In modern logistics, production dispatching and other fields, an efficient integrated dispatching system is crucial to improve the overall operational efficiency. This paper proposes an integrated dispatching system optimization scheme based on the fusion of an ant colony algorithm and dynamic weight dispatching strategy, which aims to realize intelligent allocation and dispatching of resources by simulating the behavior mechanism of ants foraging in nature. In practical applications, we construct a simulation environment containing 100 nodes to test the performance of the proposed algorithm. The experimental results show that compared with the traditional dispatching method, the dispatching system based on the ant colony algorithm reduces the average completion time by 20% and improves the resource utilization rate by 15%. This result fully proves the advantages of the ant colony algorithm in solving complex dispatching problems. In future research, we will continue to study the optimization strategy of the ant colony algorithm in-depth and explore how to better adapt to the dispatching needs of different scenarios. We also plan to introduce artificial intelligence technology into the dispatching system to achieve more intelligent decision support. Through continuous research and innovation, the integrated dispatching system based on the ant colony algorithm will exert its great potential in more fields and contribute to the sustainable development of society. Our firm belief is that, through a relentless pursuit of innovation and meticulous research, the integrated dispatching system, fortified by the ant colony algorithm, will unlock its immense potential across a myriad of sectors, ultimately contributing to society’s sustainable advancement and prosperity.

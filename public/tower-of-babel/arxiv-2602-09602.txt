A mirror theorem for partial flag bundles
Ionuţ Ciocan-Fontanine, Yuki Koto

Abstract
. We construct a family of points on the Lagrangian cone of a partial flag bundle associated to a (possibly non-split) vector bundle from any Weyl-invariant I I -function of a prequotient. This result can be seen as the nonabelian analogue of the mirror theorem for projective bundles [ 23 ] , and generalizes Oh’s mirror theorem for split partial flag bundles [ 34 ] .



1.
Introduction

Genus zero Gromov–Witten invariants of a smooth projective variety
X
are given by virtual counts of rational curves on
X
.
They satisfy the expected axioms
[
29
,
1
]
, and their generating function underlies very rich structures.
In particular, a (formal) Frobenius manifold can be constructed from the generating function, yielding the
quantum cohomology ring

\operatorname{QH}^{*}(X)
.

Givental formalism
[
20
]
introduces the
Givental Lagrangian cone

\mathcal{L}_{X}
, a geometric object with special properties, which encodes the genus zero Gromov–Witten theory of
X
.
An explicit formula describing (a family of) points on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
(historically, such formulas are often called
mirror theorems
, and we will follow this terminology) is a powerful tool for the study of
\operatorname{QH}^{*}(X)
.
We give some examples.

•

In
[
24
]
, a mirror theorem for toric varieties was used to show that quantum cohomology of toric varieties is convergent.
(This result was generalized to
split
toric bundles
[
31
]
, which are toric bundles obtained as GIT quotients by an algebraic torus of a direct sum of line bundles.)

•

In
[
23
]
, a mirror theorem for a projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(V)
(associated to a vector bundle
V\to B
) was established and was used to construct a decomposition
\operatorname{QH}^{*}(\mathbb{P}(V))\cong\operatorname{QH}^{*}(B)^{\oplus\operatorname{rank}(V)}
.

•

Dubrovin’s conjecture
[
13
]
for Grassmannians was proved in
[
36
]
, and the Gamma conjectures were formulated and proved for Grassmannians in
[
17
]
.
In their proofs, the mirror theorem for Grassmannians from
[
2
]
plays an important role.

The purpose of this paper is to prove a mirror theorem for partial flag bundles (Theorem
4.1
).
Before stating it, we briefly review known mirror theorems related to ours.
Mirror theorems for partial flag varieties were established first in
[
2
]
(for Grassmannians), and then in
[
3
]
(for general flag varieties, but only on the “small” parameter space),
[
6
]
(on the “big” parameter space).
These were extended to
split
partial flag bundles
[
34
]
.
On the other hand, the mirror theorem for a (possibly non-split) projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(V)
was given in
[
23
]
in terms of the (
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant)
J
-function of
V
.
Theorem
4.1
generalizes these results to
all
(i.e., possibly non-split) partial flag bundles.

To simplify the notation, we state the result for Grassmann bundles in the introduction.
Let
B
be a smooth projective variety, let
V\to B
be a vector bundle whose dual is globally generated, and let
r
be a positive integer less than
\operatorname{rank}(V)
.
Let
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)\to B
denote the Grassmann bundle whose fiber at
b\in B
is the Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V_{b})
of
r
-dimensional subspaces of
V_{b}
.
The following is the main theorem of this paper (applied to the Grassmann bundle
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)
).

Theorem 1.1

(Special case of Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
B
,
V
, and
r
be as above.
Let
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
act on
V^{\oplus r}
by scaling each component diagonally, and let
\mathbb{W}:=\mathfrak{S}_{r}
act on
V^{\oplus r}
by permuting the components.
Let
z\cdot I^{\lambda_{\bullet}}_{V^{\oplus r}}(x,z)
be a
\mathbb{W}
-invariant function lying on the Givental cone of
V^{\oplus r}
that depends on the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant parameters
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
polynomially.
Define the
H^{*}(\operatorname{Gr}(r,V))
-valued function
I_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)}(t,x,z)
as follows:

I_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)}(t,x,z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}q^{k}e^{\left(k+\frac{H}{z}\right)t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 0;\\
\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{I^{H_{\bullet}+k_{\bullet}z}_{V^{\oplus r}}(x,z)\cdot(-1)^{k(r-1)}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{c=1}^{k_{i}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\colon\mathrm{Chern\ roots}\\
\mathrm{of\ }V\end{subarray}}(H_{i}+\delta+cz)}\\
\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\frac{H_{i}-H_{j}+(k_{i}-k_{j})z}{H_{i}-H_{j}}

where
q
denotes the Novikov variable for the line in fibers,
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
denote the Chern roots of the dual of the tautological subbundle over
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)
, while
H=H_{1}+\dots+H_{r}
, and
I^{H_{\bullet}+k_{\bullet}z}_{V^{\oplus r}}
denotes the function
I^{\lambda_{\bullet}}_{V^{\oplus r}}
with each
\lambda_{i}
replaced with
H_{i}+k_{i}z
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Then,
z\cdot I_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)}(t,x,z)
lies in the Givental cone of
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)
.

Remark 1.2
.

To study the Givental cone
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)}
, the assumption that
V^{\vee}
is globally generated is not necessary.
In fact, by replacing
V
with
V\otimes L
where
L
is a suitable anti-ample line bundle over
B
, we can always make
V
satisfy the assumption without changing the bundle
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)
.

Theorem
4.1
recovers the
I
-function of projective bundles
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
and Oh’s
I
-function of split partial flag bundles
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
; see Example
4.3
,
4.4
. It can be viewed as an instance of the
abelian/nonabelian correspondence
for partial flag bundles. We explain this, again restricting to the case of Grassmann bundles for simplicity.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a complex reductive group and fix a maximal torus
\mathbb{T}
in
\mathbb{G}
. Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety with a
\mathbb{G}
-action and a
\mathbb{G}
-linearized ample line bundle, so that we have the GIT quotient
Y:=X/\!/\mathbb{G}
. Restricting both the action and the linearization to
\mathbb{T}
, we also have the
abelian GIT quotient

Y_{\operatorname{ab}}:=X/\!/\mathbb{T}
. For example, given a vector bundle
V\to B
, we may take
X=V^{\oplus r}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{r},V)
and
\mathbb{G}=\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, with the obvious action, linearized by the determinant character. In this case, the nonabelian quotient is precisely the Grassmann bundle
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)=V^{\oplus r}/\!/\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, while the abelian quotient

\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)_{\operatorname{ab}}=V^{\oplus r}/\!/(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
is the fiber product of
r
copies of
\mathbb{P}(V)
over
B
.

Associated to the pair
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{T})
we have the Weyl group
\mathbb{W}
, the pair of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}:=Lie(\mathbb{G}),\mathfrak{t}:=Lie(\mathbb{T})
, and the root system
\Phi
. The Weyl group acts on
Y_{\operatorname{ab}}
. The (split) adjoint
\mathbb{T}
-representation
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
induces a split vector bundle
\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}L_{\alpha}
on
Y_{\operatorname{ab}}
, which we will also call
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}
by abuse of notation. Informally, the
abelian/nonabelian correspondence
, introduced and first studied in
[
3
,
6
]
, predicts that (under reasonable assumptions on the GIT set-up) the genus zero Gromov–Witten theory of the nonabelian quotient
Y
is obtained by a kind of “average over
\mathbb{W}
” of the genus zero Gromov–Witten theory of
Y_{\operatorname{ab}}

twisted by the Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}
. Precise conjectures resulting from this predicted relationship were stated in
[
3
,
6
]
, and some reformulations were given more recently in
[
12
]
. Theorem
1.1
fits into this picture as follows.
A mirror theorem for the abelian quotient
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
was proved in
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
, giving an explicit point
z\cdot I_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)_{\operatorname{ab}}}(t,x,z)
. This point is
\mathbb{W}
-invariant and we may construct its “hypergeometric modification” corresponding to the twisting bundle
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}
. This modification gives precisely the function
z\cdot I_{\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)}(t,x,z)
, so the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
verifies that
[
3
, Conjecture 4.3]
and
[
12
, Conjecture 1.8]
hold true for the nonabelian quotient
\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)
.

Let us explain briefly the steps involved in the proof of our main result, Theorem
4.1
. The first is a nonabelian version of an idea from
[
23
]
and
[
32
]
. For a flag bundle
Fl(r_{\bullet},V)
associated to a vector bundle
V
on
B
with
V^{\vee}
globally generated, we construct an embedding

\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)\hookrightarrow B\times\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},\mathbb{C}^{N})

into a larger
trivial
flag bundle. This realizes
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
as the zero locus of a regular section of a certain convex vector bundle, so (by
[
27
]
), the mirror theorem for
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
is reduced to proving Lemma
4.2
, which is a mirror theorem for
twisted
Gromov–Witten theory of the product
B\times\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},\mathbb{C}^{N})
.

The second step (see
\lx@sectionsign 6.1
) further reduces Lemma
4.2
to an analogous mirror theorem for twisted theory of a trivial toric bundle. To accomplish this reduction, we establish the following abelian/nonabelian correspondence for products.

Theorem 1.3

(Non-equivariant version of Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
\operatorname{Fl}
be a partial flag variety (viewed as a GIT quotient), and let
\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}
denote the corresponding abelian quotient (a smooth, projective toric variety).
Then, the
{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}}
-modification (defined in Section
5.1
) sends an arbitrary
\mathbb{W}
-invariant point of the Givental cone of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}
satisfying the Divisor Equation (
5.3
) into the Givental cone of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}
.

In fact, we prove a more general torus-equivariant version, see Theorem
5.2
.
The key ingredients of the proof are a Givental/Brown-type characterization of points on the torus-equivariant Givental cone of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}
(Theorem
5.4
), and the abelian/nonabelian correspondence for partial flag varieties
[
6
]
.

In the third and last step, we prove in
\lx@sectionsign 6.4
the required mirror theorem for the twisted theory of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}
by the methods of
[
23
]
and
[
32
]
, which are based on an analysis of the action of certain differential operators on the Givental cone, especially the quantum Riemann-Roch operators
[
11
]
.

Our main theorem (Theorem
4.1
) has an application.
It provides a nonabelian counterpart of the Fourier transforms introduced in
[
23
,
25
]
and enables one to study quantum cohomology of general partial flag bundles, e.g., to prove a decomposition of quantum cohomology/quantum
D
-module analogous to those in
[
23
,
25
]
. These will be discussed in the follow-up paper
[
30
]
. Another application is the extension of Theorem
1.3
(the full abelian/nonabelian correspondence) from trivial flag bundles to arbitrary flag bundles. This will be also pursued in
[
30
]
, where it will be shown that by choosing appropriate
I
-functions for the vector bundle
V
, the formula of Theorem
4.1
in fact describes the entire Langrangian cone.

A brief description of the rest of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we review genus zero Gromov–Witten theory and Givental formalism, and prepare some lemmas for later use. In Section

we review flag bundles, including their construction as GIT quotients, and explain their embeddings into trivial flag bundles mentioned above.
In Section

4.1
) using Lemma

.
This is proved in Section

, and the abelian/nonabelian correspondence (Theorem

) needed for its proof is given in Section

.

2.
Genus zero Gromov–Witten theory

We review genus zero Gromov–Witten theory, and describe the Givental cone and its variants.

2.1.
Gromov–Witten invariant

In this subsection, we explain Gromov–Witten invariants and its variants.

2.1.1.
Gromov–Witten invariant

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety.
We denote by
\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)
the monoid in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
consisting of the effective curve classes.

For any non-negative integer
n
and any effective curve class
d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)
, let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)
denote the moduli stack of degree
d
stable maps from genus zero prestable curves to
X
with
n
markings.
For any classes
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})
and any integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define a (
genus zero
)
descendant Gromov–Witten invariant
by

\left\langle\alpha_{1}\psi^{k_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}\psi^{k_{n}}\right\rangle^{X}_{0,n,d}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)]^{{\operatorname{vir}}}}\prod^{n}_{i=1}\operatorname{ev}^{*}_{i}(\alpha_{i})\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{Q}

where
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)]^{\operatorname{vir}}\in H_{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d);\mathbb{Q})
is the virtual fundamental class
[
1
]
, and
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d))
(
1\leq i\leq n
) denotes the first Chern class of the
i
-th universal cotangent line bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)
.

2.1.2.
torus-equivariant Gromov–Witten invariant

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective
\mathbb{T}
-variety with projective fixed loci
X^{\mathbb{T}}(\neq\emptyset)
.
Note that the
\mathbb{T}
-action on
X
induces a
\mathbb{T}
-action on the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)
.

We define a (
genus zero
)
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant descendant Gromov–Witten invariant
via the virtual localization formula
[
22
]
:

\left\langle\alpha_{1}\psi^{k_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}\psi^{k_{n}}\right\rangle^{X,\mathbb{T}}_{0,n,d}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)]^{{\operatorname{vir}}}}^{\mathbb{T}}\prod^{n}_{i=1}\operatorname{ev}^{*}_{i}(\alpha_{i})\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt};\mathbb{Q})_{\operatorname{loc}}\cong\mathbb{Q}({\mu})

where
\alpha_{i}\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
,
k_{i}\geq 0
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\operatorname{rk}\mathbb{T}})
denotes the equivariant parameters for
\mathbb{T}
.
If
X
is projective, this quantity can be defined without localization and lies in
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt};\mathbb{Q})
.

2.1.3.
twisted Gromov–Witten invariant

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety.
Fix
twist data
(W,\bm{c})
for
X
, which is a pair of a
\mathbb{T}
-vector bundle
W
over
X
and a characteristic class
\bm{c}\colon K(X)\to\mathbb{C}(\mu)
.
In this paper, we only deal with the case that
\bm{c}
is the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Euler characteristic class
e_{\mathbb{T}}=\prod\rho(\mu)
or its inverse (here
\rho(\mu)
are the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Chern roots of
W
).
For any
n\geq 0
and
d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)
, set
W_{0,n,d}:=\mathbb{R}\pi_{*}f^{*}W\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d))
where
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}(X,d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)
denotes the universal family and
f\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}(X,d)\to X
denotes the universal stable map.
For
\alpha_{i}\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
and
k_{i}\geq 0

(1\leq i\leq n)
, we define a (
genus zero
)
(W,\bm{c})
-twisted descendant Gromov–Witten invariant
as

\left\langle\alpha_{1}\psi^{k_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}\psi^{k_{n}}\right\rangle^{X,(W,\bm{c})}_{0,n,d}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,d)]^{{\operatorname{vir}}}}\left[\prod^{n}_{i=1}\operatorname{ev}^{*}_{i}(\alpha_{i})\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\right]\cup\bm{c}(W_{0,n,d})\in\mathbb{Q}(\mu).

Remark 2.1
.

If
V\to B
is a
\mathbb{T}
-vector bundle whose dual is globally generated and we assume in addition that the
\mathbb{T}
-fixed locus in (the total space of)
V
is precisely the zero section, then
(V,e_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1})
-twisted Gromov–Witten invariants coincide with
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants of
V
.
More precisely, for any
\alpha_{i}\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(V)\cong H^{*}(B)[\mu]
and
k_{i}\geq 0
,

\left\langle\alpha_{1}\psi^{k_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}\psi^{k_{n}}\right\rangle^{V,\mathbb{T}}_{0,n,d}=\left\langle\alpha_{1}\psi^{k_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}\psi^{k_{n}}\right\rangle^{B,(V,e_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1})}_{0,n,d}.

2.2.
Quantum cohomology

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety.
We fix a homogeneous
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and write
\{\tau^{i}\}_{i\in I}
the associated dual coordinate system on
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
.
Let
Q=Q_{X}
denote a formal variable for the monoid
\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)
(called the
Novikov variable
), and let
\mathbb{C}[\![Q]\!]:=\mathbb{C}[\![\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)]\!]
(called the
Novikov ring
) be the completion of the monoid ring
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)]
.
For any (graded) module
R
, we set
R[\![Q,\tau]\!]:=R[\![Q]\!][\![\{\tau^{i}\}_{i\in I}]\!]
, which is regarded as a graded module with the following grading:

\deg\tau^{i}=2-\deg\phi_{i}\quad(i\in I),\qquad\deg Q^{d}=c_{1}(X)\cdot d\quad(d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)).

We write
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle^{X}
for the Poincaré pairing on
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C}[\![Q,\tau]\!])
.
For any
\alpha,\beta\in H^{*}(X)[\![Q,\tau]\!]
, define the
quantum product
of
\alpha
and
\beta
to be the element
\alpha\star_{\tau}\beta\in H^{*}(X)[\![Q,\tau]\!]
satisfying that, for any
\gamma\in H^{*}(X)
,

\left\langle\alpha\star_{\tau}\beta,\gamma\right\rangle^{X}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)}\frac{Q^{d}}{n!}\left\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau,\dots,\tau\right\rangle^{X}_{0,n+3,d}\in\mathbb{C}[\![Q,\tau]\!]

where
\tau=\sum_{i\in I}\tau^{i}\phi_{i}
.
Note that
\tau
is homogeneous of degree two.
Since the Poincaré pairing is nondegenerate,
\alpha\star_{\tau}\beta
is uniquely determined.
We define the
quantum cohomology
of
X
to be the graded ring
\operatorname{QH}^{*}(X):=(H^{*}(X)[\![Q,\tau]\!],\star_{\tau})
.

We define the
fundamental solution

[
18
,
35
,
10
]

M_{X}(\tau)\in\operatorname{End}(H^{*}(X))[z^{-1}][\![Q,\tau]\!]
as follows:

(2.1)

\left\langle M_{X}(\tau)\alpha,\beta\right\rangle^{X}=\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right\rangle^{X}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\frac{Q^{d}}{n!}\left\langle\alpha,\mathbf{t}(\psi),\dots,\mathbf{t}(\psi),\frac{\beta}{z-\psi}\right\rangle^{X}_{0,n+2,d}

where
1/(z-\psi)
is expanded as
\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{-k-1}\psi^{k}
.
The matrix
M_{X}(\tau)
is a solution of the
quantum connection

\nabla^{X}_{z\partial_{\tau^{i}}}=z\partial_{\tau^{i}}+(\phi_{i}\star_{\tau}\cdot)
.
In particular,
M_{X}(\tau)
is the matrix that satisfies

(2.2)

M_{X}(\tau)\circ\nabla^{X}_{z\partial_{\tau^{i}}}=z\partial_{\tau^{i}}\circ M_{X}(\tau)

for each
i\in I
, with the initial condition
M_{X}(\tau)|_{Q=0}=e^{\tau/z}
.

Define the
J
-function
of
X
as follows.

(2.3)

J_{X}(\tau,z):=M_{X}(\tau)1=1+\frac{\tau}{z}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0),(1,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{Q^{d}\phi_{i}}{n!}\left\langle\tau,\dots,\tau,\frac{\phi^{i}}{z(z-\psi)}\right\rangle^{X}_{0,n+1,d}

where
\{\phi^{i}\}_{i\in I}
is the dual basis of
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with respect to the Poincaré pairing.

2.3.
Givental cone

The
Givental
(
Lagrangian
)
cone
plays a significant role in Givental formalism
[
19
,
20
]
, which encodes genus zero Gromov–Witten theory into the symplectic geometry of the
Givental space
, an infinite-dimensional symplectic vector space.

In this section, we will introduce three kinds of Lagrangian cones: a Lagrangian cone of smooth projective varieties (Section
2.3.1
), a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Givental cone of smooth quasi-projective varieties (Section
2.3.2
), and a twisted Givental cone (Section
2.3.3
).

Throughout this section, we assume that
X
is a smooth quasi-projective variety.
Fix a homogeneous basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and let
\{\tau^{i}\}_{i\in I}
denote the associated coordinate and
\{\phi^{i}\}_{i\in I}
denote its dual basis with respect to the Poincaré pairing.
Finally, we set
Q:=Q_{X}
.

2.3.1.
Givental cone for smooth projective varieties

The
Givental space

(\mathcal{H}_{X},\Omega_{X})
of
X
is the infinite-dimensional free
\mathbb{C}[\![Q]\!]
-module
\mathcal{H}_{X}=H^{*}(X)[z,z^{-1}][\![Q]\!]
equipped with the symplectic form
\Omega_{X}(f(z),g(z))=-\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}\left\langle f(-z),g(z)\right\rangle^{X}dz
.

\mathcal{H}_{X}
splits canonically into two maximally
\Omega_{X}
-isotropic subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-}
:

\mathcal{H}_{+}=H^{*}(X)[z][\![Q]\!],\quad\mathcal{H}_{-}=z^{-1}H^{*}(X)[z^{-1}][\![Q]\!].

Since
\mathcal{H}_{-}
is identified with the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{+}
via
\Omega_{X}
, we have a canonical identification
\mathcal{H}_{X}\cong T^{*}\mathcal{H}_{+}
.

The
Givental Lagrangian cone

\mathcal{L}_{X}\subset\mathcal{H}_{X}

[
20
]
is defined as the graph of the differential of the genus zero descendant Gromov–Witten potential (via
\mathcal{H}_{X}\cong T^{*}\mathcal{H}_{+}
).
We give an explicit description of the points on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
.
Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
be a set of (possibly infinitely many) formal parameters.
Then, a
\mathbb{C}[\![Q,x]\!]
-valued point on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is of the form

(2.4)

\mathbf{1}z+\mathbf{t}(z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0),(1,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{Q^{d}\phi_{i}}{n!}\left\langle\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\dots,\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\frac{\phi^{i}}{z-\psi}\right\rangle^{X}_{0,n+1,d}

for some
\mathbf{t}(z)\in H^{*}(X)[z][\![Q,x]\!]
satisfying
\mathbf{t}(z)|_{Q=x=0}=0
.
Here, we follow the sign convention in
[
23
,
25
]
, and
1/(z-\psi)
is expanded as
\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{-k-1}\psi^{k}
.
Since
\psi
is nilpotent, this gives a well-defined element of
\mathcal{H}_{X}(\mathbb{C}[\![Q,x]\!])=H^{*}(X)[z,z^{-1}][\![Q,x]\!]
.

Remark 2.2
.

More generally, we can define a
R
-valued point on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
for arbitrary complete local
\mathbb{C}[\![Q]\!]
-algebra
(R,\mathfrak{m})
by the same formula as (
2.4
) with
\mathbf{t}(z)\in H^{*}(X)[z][\![Q]\!]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{C}[\![Q]\!]}R
, where
\widehat{\otimes}
denotes the completed tensor product with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, such that
\mathbf{t}(z)=0
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
; see
[
8
, Appendix B]
.

Axioms of Gromov–Witten invariants imply that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\subset\mathcal{H}_{X}
is
overruled

[
20
]
.
In particular, any
\mathbb{C}[\![Q,x]\!]
-valued point on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
can be written as
zM_{X}(\tau(x))f
for some
\tau(x)\in H^{*}(X)[\![Q,x]\!]
and
f\in H^{*}(X)[z][\![Q,x]\!]
satisfying that
\tau(x)|_{Q=x=0}=0
and
f|_{Q=x=0}=1
.

2.3.2.
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Givental cone of smooth quasi-projective varieties

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective
\mathbb{T}
-variety whose
\mathbb{T}
-fixed set is projective.
The
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Givental Lagrangian cone

\mathcal{L}_{X}
is defined by replacing spaces/formulas used to define
\mathcal{L}_{X}
with their
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant counterparts.
We explain them in a list.

•

\mathcal{H}_{X,\mathbb{T}}:=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}(\!(z^{-1})\!)[\![Q]\!]
where
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}:=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\otimes_{H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})}\operatorname{Frac}(H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt}))
. (In this paper, we use
A((y))
to denote the ring of formal Laurent series in the variable
y
, with coefficients in the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
.)

•

\Omega_{X}(f(z),g(z))=-\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}\left\langle f(-z),g(z)\right\rangle^{X,\mathbb{T}}dz
for
f,g\in\mathcal{H}_{X,\mathbb{T}}
, where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle^{X,\mathbb{T}}
denotes the
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}
-bilinear pairing on
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}
defined by the localization formula.

•

\mathcal{H}_{+}:=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[z][\![Q]\!]
and
\mathcal{H}_{-}:=z^{-1}H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![z^{-1}]\!][\![Q]\!]
.

For any formal variables
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
, a
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![Q,x]\!]
-valued point on
\mathcal{L}_{X,\mathbb{T}}
is of the form

\mathbf{1}z+\mathbf{t}(z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0),(1,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{Q^{d}\phi_{i}}{n!}\left\langle\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\dots,\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\frac{\phi^{i}}{z-\psi}\right\rangle^{X,\mathbb{T}}_{0,n+1,d}

for some
\mathbf{t}(z)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[z][\![Q,x]\!]
such that
\mathbf{t}(z)|_{Q=x=0}=0
.
As in the non-equivariant theory, for any
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![Q,x]\!]
-valued point
z\cdot I
of
\mathcal{L}_{X,\mathbb{T}}
, there exist

\tau(x)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![Q,x]\!],\quad f\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[z][\![Q,x]\!]

satisfying
\tau(x)|_{Q=x=0}=0
and
f|_{Q=x=0}=1
such that
I=M_{X,\mathbb{T}}(\tau(x))f
.
Here,
M_{X,\mathbb{T}}(\tau)
represents the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant fundamental solution of
X
, and is defined by the same formula (
2.1
), with Gromov–Witten invariants in (
2.1
) replaced by
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants.

2.3.3.
twisted Givental cone

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
E\to X
be a vector bundle over a smooth projective variety
X
with a fiberwise
\mathbb{T}
-action.
We assume that
E^{\mathbb{T}}=X
(embedded as the zero section of
E
) and
\bm{c}
is either the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Euler class or its inverse.
Again, we define the cone for
(E,\bm{c})
-twisted Gromov–Witten theory of
X
by the same procedure.

Namely, the Givental space for the twisted theory is

\mathcal{H}_{X,(E,\bm{c})}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}(\!(z)\!)[\![Q]\!],\quad\mathcal{H}_{+}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![z,Q]\!].

For any formal variables
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
, a
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![Q,x]\!]
-valued point on
\mathcal{L}_{X,(E,\bm{c})}
is of the form

\mathbf{1}z+\mathbf{t}(z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0),(1,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{Q^{d}\phi_{i}}{n!}\left\langle\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\dots,\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\frac{\bm{c}(E)^{-1}\phi^{i}}{z-\psi}\right\rangle^{X,(E,\bm{c})}_{0,n+1,d}.

with
\mathbf{t}(z)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X)_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![z,Q,x]\!]
such that
\mathbf{t}(z)|_{Q=x=0}=0
.

A point on the Givental cone of a subvariety can sometimes be obtained from a known point on the Euler-twisted Givental cone of the ambient variety.
More precisely, the following standard lemma (a consequence of the functoriality of virtual classes in
[
27
]
) holds.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, and let
E\to X
be a convex vector bundle, i.e.
H^{1}(C,f^{*}E)=0
for any genus zero stable map
f\colon C\to X
. Let
Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\subset}}X
be the zero-scheme of a regular section of
E
. Consider the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
E
by scaling, with equivariant parameter
\mu
, and write
e_{\mu}
for the equivariant Euler class
e_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
.
Let
f
be a
\mathbb{C}(\mu)[\![Q_{X},x]\!]
-valued point of
\mathcal{L}_{X,(E,e_{\mu})}
.
If the non-equivariant limit of the pullback
\iota^{*}f
exists, then the limit
\lim_{\mu\to 0}\iota^{*}f
is a
\mathbb{C}[\![Q_{X},x]\!]
-valued point of
\mathcal{L}_{Z}
.
Here,
\mathbb{C}[\![Q_{X},x]\!]
is regarded as a
\mathbb{C}[\![Q_{Z}]\!]
-algebra via the map
Q_{Z}^{d}\mapsto Q_{X}^{\iota_{*}d}
.

Remark 2.4
.

When
E
is a convex vector bundle, then the
(E,e_{\mu})
-twisted invariants are polynomials in
\mu
as
E_{0,n,d}
can be realized as an actual vector bundle, and hence we can consider
(E,e_{\mu})
-twisted theory without localizing with respect to
\mu
.

2.4.
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Givental cone of vector bundles

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
V\to X
be a vector bundle over a smooth projective variety
X
with a fiberwise
\mathbb{T}
-action.
We assume that
V^{\vee}
is globally generated and
V^{\mathbb{T}}=X
.
We have already introduced the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Givental space of certain quasi-projective
\mathbb{T}
-varieties (Section
2.3.2
), including the bundle
V
.
In this subsection, we introduce a smaller variant for
V
, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}
.
We will use
\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}
(and its subset
\mathcal{L}_{V,\mathbb{T}}
) to formulate our main theorem.

We follow almost the same procedure as in the previous subsection, with some modifications.
We identify
\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(V)
and
\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)
and write
Q
for the Novikov variable of
V
.
Define the Givental space
\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}
and its subspace
\mathcal{H}_{+}
as follows:

\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(V)[z,z^{-1}][\![Q]\!],\quad\mathcal{H}_{+}^{\operatorname{pol}}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(V)[z][\![Q]\!].

We define the Givental cone
\mathcal{L}_{V,\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}
as follows: for any formal variables
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
, the set
\mathcal{L}_{V,\mathbb{T}}(H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})[\![Q,x]\!])
consists of the points of the form

(2.5)

\mathbf{1}z+\mathbf{t}(z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,d\in\operatorname{NE}_{\mathbb{N}}(X)\\
(n,d)\neq(0,0),(1,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{Q^{d}\phi_{i}}{n!}\left\langle\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\dots,\mathbf{t}(-\psi),\frac{e_{\lambda}(V)\phi^{i}}{z-\psi}\right\rangle^{V,\mathbb{T}}_{0,n+1,d}

with
\mathbf{t}(z)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(V)[z][\![Q,x]\!]
such that
\mathbf{t}(z)|_{Q=x=0}=0
.
The set
\{e_{\lambda}(V)\phi^{i}\}_{i\in I}
is the dual basis of
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with respect to the Poincaré pairing
\left\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\right\rangle^{V}=\int_{X}^{\mathbb{T}}\phi_{1}\cup\phi_{2}\cup e_{\lambda}(V)^{-1}
.

The function (
2.5
) is indeed a point of
\mathcal{H}_{V,\mathbb{T}}^{\operatorname{pol}}
.
In fact, the class
\psi_{1}
for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(V,d)
is nilpotent since it matches the pullback of
\psi_{1}
for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(B,d)
as images of
\mathbb{T}

.

2.5.
Quantum Riemann-Roch theorem

We recall the Quantum Riemann-Roch Theorem of Coates-Givental,
[
11
]
, which relates a twisted Givental cone with the untwisted one.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, and let
E\to X
be a vector bundle.
For a formal variable
\lambda
, we define the operator
\Delta_{E}^{\lambda}
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{X}
as

\Delta_{E}^{\lambda}f:=\left.\left(\exp\left[\sum_{l,m\geq 0}s_{l+m-1}(\lambda)\cdot\frac{B_{m}}{m!}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{l}(E)\cdot(-z)^{m-1}\right]f\right)\right|_{Q_{X}^{d}\to Q_{X}^{d}\lambda^{-d\cdot c_{1}(E)}},

where
B_{m}
is the Bernoulli number given by the formula
x/(e^{x}-1)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(B_{m}/m!)x^{(m)}
, and
s_{k}(\lambda)
(
k\geq-1
) is defined as

s_{k}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\quad k=-1,0,\\
(k-1)!(-\lambda)^{-k}&\text{for}\quad k\geq 1.\end{cases}

Note that the characteristic class
\lambda^{-\operatorname{rank}(\cdot)}\exp(\sum_{k\geq 0}s_{k}(\lambda)\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{k}(\cdot))
matches
e_{\lambda}(\cdot)^{-1}
(the
inverse
of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant Euler class, where
\lambda
is regarded as the equivariant parameter for the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
E
by multiplication on the fibers).
The following theorem follows from the quantum Riemann-Roch theorem
[
11
, Corollary 4]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-vector bundle over
X
with a decomposition
E=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{\times})}E_{\alpha}
.
Then, we have
\mathcal{L}_{X,(E,e_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1})}=(\prod_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{\times})}\Delta_{E_{\alpha}}^{\alpha})\mathcal{L}_{X}
.

2.6.
Differential operators acting on Givental cones

In this subsection, we introduce known results on differential operators acting on Givental cones.
It is important to study them because they provide (genuinely) new points on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
(or
\mathcal{L}_{X,\mathbb{T}}
) from known points on it.

The following lemma claims that a certain differential operator acts on the Givental cone; see e.g.
[
11
, §8]
,
[

, Lemma 2.7]

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus, and let
X
be a smooth projective
\mathbb{T}
-variety.
Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)
be formal variables.
Let

D(x,y,z\partial_{x},z)\in\mathbb{C}[z][\![x]\!]\langle z\partial_{x}\rangle[\![Q_{X},y]\!]

be a differential operator such that

.
Then, the differential operator

preserves
\mathcal{L}_{X}
and

respectively.

The following lemma asserts that an element of the Givental cone with sufficiently many parameters can be obtained by applying a differential operator to the
J
-function.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
23
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

Let
B
be a smooth projective variety, let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a basis of

, and let

be the dual coordinates on

Let

be an

3.
Flag bundles, associated abelian quotients, and Weyl-invariant Givental cones

In this section we recall the construction (which we consider folklore) of partial flag bundles as GIT quotients of
certain vector bundles by a product of general linear groups. The associated abelian quotients are obtained as GIT quotients by a maximal torus. These abelian quotients carry an action of the Weyl group, leading to the notion of
Weyl-invariant Givental cone
of an abelian quotient, studied in
[
12
]
. In the last subsection we discuss certain natural embeddings of flag bundles which will be used in the proof of the mirror theorem.

3.1.
Partial flag bundles as GIT quotients

Let
B
be a smooth projective variety, and let
V\to B
be a vector bundle.
Let
r_{\bullet}=\{r_{1}<\dots<r_{l}\}
be a subset of
\{1,2,\dots,\operatorname{rank}(V)-1=n-1\}
.
Set

\widetilde{V}=\bigoplus_{m=1}^{l-1}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{m}},\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{m+1}})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},V),\quad\mathbb{G}=\prod_{m=1}^{l}\operatorname{GL}_{r_{m}}(\mathbb{C}).

Let
\mathbb{G}
act on
\widetilde{V}
as follows: for
(F_{1},\dots,F_{l})\in\widetilde{V}
and
(g_{1},\dots,g_{l})\in\mathbb{G}
, we define

(g_{1},\dots,g_{l})\cdot(F_{1},\dots,F_{l})=(g_{2}^{-1}\circ F_{1}\circ g_{1},g_{3}^{-1}\circ F_{2}\circ g_{2},\dots,g_{l}^{-1}\circ F_{l-1}\circ g_{l-1},F_{l}\circ g_{l}).

Let
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{G}
be the maximal torus consisting of all diagonal matrices.
Let
\theta\in\chi(\mathbb{G})
be the character
\theta\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
given by the product of all determinants of each factor
\operatorname{GL}_{r_{m}}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
L_{\theta}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{V}}
be the trivial line bundle on
\widetilde{V}
, with
\mathbb{G}
-linearization induced by
\theta
. Viewing
\theta
as a character of
\mathbb{T}
(by restricting) makes
L_{\theta}
into a
\mathbb{T}
-linearized line bundle as well.
We define the
nonabelian quotient

\widetilde{V}/\!/\mathbb{G}
and the
abelian quotient

\widetilde{V}/\!/\mathbb{T}
to be the following relative GIT quotients:

\widetilde{V}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{G}:=\underline{\mathrm{Proj}}_{B}\left(\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\pi_{*}\left(L_{\theta}^{\otimes k}\right)^{\mathbb{G}}\right),\qquad\widetilde{V}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{T}:=\underline{\mathrm{Proj}}_{B}\left(\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\pi_{*}\left(L_{\theta}^{\otimes k}\right)^{\mathbb{T}}\right)

where
\pi\colon\widetilde{V}\to B
denotes the canonical projection.

In either case, there are no strictly semistable points and the groups
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{T}
act freely on their respective stable loci with respect to
L_{\theta}
.
For the
L_{\theta}
-linearized
\mathbb{G}
-action, a point
(F_{1},\dots,F_{l})\in\widetilde{V}
is stable if and only if each
F_{i}
is injective. It follows that
the nonabelian quotient is naturally identified with the partial flag bundle over
B
(whose fiber at
b\in B
is the partial flag variety
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V_{b})
). The abelian quotient is a smooth projective variety which is a toric bundle over
B
.

If
M
is a line bundle on
B
and we replace
V
by
V\otimes M
in the above construction, then there are canonical isomorphisms (of
B
-schemes)
\widetilde{V}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{G}\cong\widetilde{V\otimes M}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{G}
and
\widetilde{V}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{T}\cong\widetilde{V\otimes M}/\!/\!_{\theta}\mathbb{T}
. In particular, by fixing an ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{B}(1)
on
B
and taking
M\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}(-m)
for sufficiently large
m
, we may (and will from now on) assume that the dual of
V
is generated by global sections.

We introduce notations for abelian/nonabelian quotients.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
V\to B
and
r_{\bullet}
be as above.

(1)

We write
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
(resp.
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
) for the nonabelian quotient
\widetilde{V}/\!/\mathbb{G}
(resp. the abelian quotient
\widetilde{V}/\!/\mathbb{T}
).

(2)

A
\mathbb{G}
-linearized vector bundle
E
over
\widetilde{V}
induces a vector bundle over
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
, which we denote
E_{\mathbb{G}}
, and also (by viewing it as a
\mathbb{T}
-linearized bundle) a vector bundle over the abelian quotient
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
, denoted
E_{\mathbb{T}}
. Each of these is obtained by restricting
E
to the corresponding stable locus and then descending to the quotient, which is always possible as all stable points have trivial stabilizers. In this paper, we will apply the above construction to bundles of the form
E=\pi^{*}F\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{V}}\otimes R)
where
F
is vector bundle on
B
(with trivial
\mathbb{G}
-action) and
R
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-representation.

(3)

We denote by
S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\cdots\subset S_{l}=:S\subset\pi^{*}V
the tautological flag of subbundles over
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
.
Note that each
S_{m}
can be realized as
(E_{r_{m}})_{\mathbb{G}}
where
E_{r_{m}}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{V}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{r_{m}}
is the trivial bundle with fiber the dual of the standard representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{r_{m}}(\mathbb{C})
.
We denote by
(S_{m})_{\mathbb{T}}
the bundle over
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
induced by
E_{r_{m}}
.
We set
S_{\mathbb{T}}:=(S_{l})_{\mathbb{T}}
.

(4)

The
Weyl group

\mathbb{W}
is the group
N(\mathbb{T})/\mathbb{T}
, where
N(\mathbb{T})
denotes the normalizer in
\mathbb{G}
.
It is isomorphic to the product
\prod_{m=1}^{l}\mathfrak{S}_{r_{m}}
of symmetric groups and acts on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
. Therefore, it also acts on the cohomology
H^{*}(\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}})
. Results of Ellingsrud-Strømme
[
14
]
, Martin
[
33
]
, Kirwan
[
28
]
relate the cohomology of a nonabelian quotient to the cohomology of the associated abelian quotient with its Weyl group action, see
[
6
, Section 3.1]
for a more detailed description. In particular, there is a natural surjective ring homomorphism

(3.1)

\varrho:H^{*}(\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}})^{\mathbb{W}}\twoheadrightarrow H^{*}(\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V))

(5)

For each
1\leq m\leq l
, the
\mathbb{T}
-representation
E_{r_{m}}
splits as a direct sum of
1
-dimensional representations, hence the bundle
(S_{m}^{\vee})_{\mathbb{T}}
splits into a direct sum of line bundles,
(S_{m}^{\vee})_{\mathbb{T}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{r_{m}}M_{j}^{(m)}
.
We denote the corresponding first Chern classes
H^{(m)}_{j}:=c_{1}(M_{j}^{(m)})\in H^{*}(\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}})
; these are represented by divisors on

\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
and we will also write
\mathcal{O}(H^{(m)}_{i}):=M^{(m)}_{i}
for the line bundles.
The action of
\mathfrak{S}_{r_{m}}\subset\mathbb{W}
on
H^{*}(\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}})
permutes
H^{(m)}_{1},\dots,H^{(m)}_{r_{m}}
.

By abuse of notation, we use
H^{(m)}_{1},\dots,H^{(m)}_{r_{m}}
to denote the Chern roots of
S_{m}^{\vee}
as well. This is justified, as the homomorphism
\varrho
from (
3.1
) sends the Chern classes of
(S_{m})_{\mathbb{T}}
to the corresponding Chern classes of
S_{m}
, for each
1\leq m\leq l
.

For future reference, we describe explicitly the Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}
on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\operatorname{ab}}
(with its decomposition
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}=\oplus_{\alpha:\;{\mathrm{root\;of}}\;\mathbb{G}}L_{\alpha}
), which appears in the abelian/nonabelian correspondence, as explained in the Introduction. The Lie

3.3.
Embeddings of flag bundles as zero loci

In
[
23
]
and
[
32
]
, mirror theorems for toric bundles (that is, for abelian quotients) were proved via embedding them as zero loci in larger
trivial
toric bundles. In this subsection, we explain that the construction can be made at the level of prequotients, and descended to both the nonabelian and the abelian quotients.

Continuing to use the notation introduced in subsection
3.1
, suppose that
V
is a subbundle in a vector bundle
U\to B
of rank
N>n
and let
\mathcal{Q}=U/V
be the quotient vector bundle. Then we have the associated
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant vector bundle
\pi:\widetilde{U}\to B
, with
\widetilde{V}\subset\widetilde{U}
a
\mathbb{G}
-subbundle and
\widetilde{U}/\widetilde{V}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},\mathcal{Q})\cong\mathcal{Q}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes\mathrm{St}_{r_{l}})
, where
\mathrm{St}_{r_{l}}
is the standard representation of
GL_{r_{l}}(\mathbb{C})
. (Note that
\mathbb{G}
acts on
\widetilde{U}/\widetilde{V}
via the projection
\mathbb{G}\to GL_{r_{l}}(\mathbb{C})
.)
The vector bundle

\pi^{*}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},U)
on
\widetilde{U}
has the tautological section

\mathrm{taut}:\widetilde{U}\to\pi^{*}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},U).

Composing it with the vector bundle map
\pi^{*}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},U)\to\pi^{*}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},\mathcal{Q})
gives a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant section
\tilde{s}
of
\pi^{*}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus r_{l}},\mathcal{Q})
, whose (transversal) zero locus in
\widetilde{U}
is obviously
Z(\tilde{s})=\widetilde{V}
. The inclusion
\widetilde{V}\subset\widetilde{U}
respects
\theta
-stability (for both
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{T}
). Passing to the GIT quotients,
\tilde{s}
descends to sections

s:\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)\to p^{*}\mathcal{Q}\otimes S^{\vee}\;\;\;\;s_{\mathrm{ab}}:\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)_{\mathrm{ab}}\to p_{\mathrm{ab}}^{*}\mathcal{Q}\otimes S_{\mathbb{T}}^{\vee},

such that their transversal zero loci are

Z(s)=\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)\subset\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U),\;\;\;\;Z(s_{\mathrm{ab}})=\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\mathrm{ab}}\subset\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)_{\mathrm{ab}}.

(Here
p:\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)\to B
and
p_{\mathrm{ab}}:\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)_{\mathrm{ab}}\to B
are the canonical projections,
S=S_{l}
is the last tautological subbundle on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)
, and
S_{\mathbb{T}}
is its “abelianization”.)

It is clear that the tautological flag of subbundles on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)
restricts to the tautological flag on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)
, so we will use the notation
S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\cdots\subset S_{l}=:S
in either case. Similarly, each
(S_{m})_{\mathbb{T}}
on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},U)_{\mathrm{ab}}
restricts to the corresponding bundle
(S_{m})_{\mathbb{T}}
on
\operatorname{Fl}(r_{\bullet},V)_{\mathrm{ab}}
. The cohomology classes
H_{j}^{(m)}
therefore correspond under pull-back, as do the Chern classes of the
S_{m}
’s. Finally, push-forward for both inclusions is an isomorphism on
H_{2}(-,\mathbb{Z})
, so the Novikov rings are canonically isomorphic.

I_{k_{\bullet}^{\bullet}}(Q_{B},x,z)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}})_{\operatorname{loc}}(\!(z^{-1})\!)[\![Q_{B},x]\!]
, we set

(5.2)

\displaystyle I_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\bullet}_{\bullet}}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{l}(q^{(m)})^{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{m}}k_{i}^{(m)}}\right)\cdot\left(I_{k_{\bullet}^{\bullet}}(Q_{B},x,z)\cdot\operatorname{Hyp}_{k^{\bullet}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}}(H^{\bullet}_{\bullet},z)\right).

In words, we first multiply each term in the

-exapnsion of

by the appropriate
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}
-modification factor, and then we specialize the Novikov variables by setting

, for

,

.
It is not hard to check that the formula (

) gives a well-defined element of

(for example, see
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
, where

is called the Givental-Martin modification of

).
The non-equivariant version

(\cdot)_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{W}}\to\mathcal{H}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}}

is defined by the same formula.

An
H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![Q_{B},q_{\bullet}^{\bullet},t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},x]\!]
-valued point

on
\mathcal{H}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}},\mathbb{S}}
is said to satisfy
the Divisor Equation

[

]
if, for any

and

, the following equality holds:

(5.3)

z\frac{\partial I}{\partial t_{i}^{(m)}}(t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},x,z)=zq_{i}^{(m)}\frac{\partial I}{\partial q_{i}^{(m)}}(t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},x,z)+H_{i}^{(m)}\cdot I(t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},x,z).

The aim of this section is to prove the following version of the abelian/nonabelian correspondence for
B\times\operatorname{Fl}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
z\cdot I(t^{\bullet}_{\bullet},x,z)\in\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}},\mathbb{S}}

is
\mathbb{W}
-invariant.
Then,

lies in
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}

The above formulation (for general GIT quotients) is due to Coates-Lutz-Shafi, see
[

, where it is called a reformulation of
[

, Conjecture 3.7.1]

We will prove Theorem

The argument relies on a characterization theorem for

(Theorem

is a point. In that case, the statement follows the abelian/nonabelian correspondence for partial flag varieties, which was proved in
[

, Theorem 4.1.1]
.

In the proof of Lemma

, we need the abelian/nonabelian correspondence for twisted theories
[

, Conjecture 6.1.1]
,
[

, Conjecture 1.9]
, which is in fact equivalent to the one for untwisted theory.
More precisely, by the same argument as in the proofs of
[

, Theorem 6.1.2]
and
[

, Proposition 1.7]
, Theorem

implies the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let

be a
\mathbb{G}
-bundle over

5.2.
Characterization of points on
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}

We introduce the Givental and Brown style characterization of points on
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
.
See
[
5
,
9
,
26
,
15
,
32
]
for similar characterizations (for other varieties/stacks).

We prepare notation.

•

Let
F
denote the set of all
\mathbb{S}
-fixed points of
\operatorname{Fl}
.

•

For
\alpha\in F
, we define
\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)
to be the subset of
F\setminus\{\alpha\}
consisting of all
\mathbb{S}
-fixed points that can be joined with
\alpha
by a
1
-dimensional orbit.
Such an orbit is unique for each
\beta\in\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)
, and we write
d_{\alpha,\beta}
for the curve class of its closure.

•

For any
\beta\in\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)
, let
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}\in H^{2}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{pt},\mathbb{Z})
denote the
\mathbb{S}
-equivariant first Chern class of the conormal bundle of
\alpha\in\operatorname{Fl}
in the closure of the one-dimensional orbit joining
\alpha
and
\beta
.

•

For each
\alpha\in F
, write
\iota_{\alpha}\colon B\hookrightarrow B\times\operatorname{Fl}
for the embedding that intersects with each fiber of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}\to B
at
\alpha\in\operatorname{Fl}
.

The following gives the characterization of points on
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

I(x,z)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(B\times\operatorname{Fl})(\!(z^{-1})\!)[\![q^{\bullet},Q_{B},x]\!]

be a function such that
I|_{q^{\bullet}=Q_{B}=x=0}=1
.
Then, the function
z\cdot I(x,z)
is a
H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}[\![q^{\bullet},Q_{B},x]\!]
-valued point of
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
if and only if
I(x,z)
satisfies the following three properties:

\mathbf{(C1)}

For any
\alpha\in F
, the restriction
\iota_{\alpha}^{*}I
belongs to

H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(B)_{\operatorname{loc}}(z)[\![q^{\bullet},Q_{B},x]\!],

and it is a regular function in
z
except possibly for poles at

\{0,\infty\}\cup\left\{\frac{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}}{a}\colon\beta\in\operatorname{adj}(\alpha),a\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

\mathbf{(C2)}

The restrictions of
I(x,z)
satisfy the following recursion relations: for any
\alpha\in F
,
\beta\in\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)
and
a\in\mathbb{N}
, the following equality holds.

\operatorname{Prin}_{z=\frac{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}}{a}}\iota_{\alpha}^{*}I=\frac{q^{a\cdot d_{\alpha,\beta}}}{z-\frac{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}}{a}}\cdot\frac{e_{\mathbb{S}}(N_{\alpha})}{e_{\mathbb{S}}(N^{\operatorname{vir}}_{\alpha,\beta,a})}\cdot\iota_{\beta}^{*}I\left(z=\frac{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}}{a}\right)

where
N_{\alpha}
is the normal bundle to the
\mathbb{S}
-fixed point
\alpha
in
\operatorname{Fl}
, and
N^{\operatorname{vir}}_{\alpha,\beta,a}
is the virtual normal bundle to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta,a}
, the moduli of all multiple one-dimensional orbits joining
B\times\{\alpha\}
and
B\times\{\beta\}

[
32
, Definition 3.10]
, in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(B\times\operatorname{Fl},a\cdot d_{\alpha,\beta})
.

\mathbf{(C3)}

For any
\alpha\in F
, the Laurent expansion of
\iota_{\alpha}^{*}I(x,z)
at
z=0
is a
\mathbb{C}(\nu)[\![q^{\bullet},Q_{B},x,\{(\rho_{\alpha,\beta})^{-1}\}_{\beta\in\operatorname{adj}(\alpha)}]\!]
-valued point of
\mathcal{L}_{B}
.

Remark 5.5
.

A

(1)

The term
e_{\mathbb{S}}(N_{\alpha})/e_{\mathbb{S}}(N^{\operatorname{vir}}_{\alpha,\beta,a})\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{pt})_{\operatorname{loc}}
does not depend on the base space
B
.
Though it is not necessary for this paper, it can be explicitly computed using the explicit formula for the small
J
-function of partial flag varieties from
[
3
]
.

(2)

One can easily generalize this characterization to the case of nontrivial, but still
split
partial flag bundles over
B
.
In fact, the proof given below will work for any split partial flag bundle.

(3)

Motivated by
[
21
,
7
]
, Oh gave a characterization
[
34
, Theorem 1.3]
of points (satisfying some additional condition) on the equivariant Givental cone of split partial flag bundles.
Theorem
5.4
and its version for general split bundles may be viewed as a slight extension of Oh’s characterization.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

We first check that any point
I(x,z)
on
z^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
satisfies the three properties.
One can see that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta,a}
is isomorphic to the
a
-th root stack
B\times[\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{s}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{t}]
where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{t}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{s}
as
t\cdot s=t^{a}s
; see e.g.
[
32
, Definition 3.10]
.
In this situation, the virtual localization theorem
[
22
]
shows that
I(x,z)
satisfies
\mathbf{(C1)}
and
\mathbf{(C2)}
; see
[
5
,
34
]
.
Finally,
\mathbf{(C3)}
follows from
[
25
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Conversely, we assume that
I(x,z)
satisfies
\mathbf{(C1)}
,
\mathbf{(C2)}
and
\mathbf{(C3)}
.
It follows from the argument in
[
32
, Section 4.5]
that the function
I(x,z)
is uniquely determined from the non-negative part
z^{-1}\operatorname{Prin}_{z=\infty}z\cdot I
.
On the other hand, as we checked above, the point
I^{\prime}
on
z^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
whose non-negative part coincides with that of
I
satisfies the three properties.
This shows that
I=I^{\prime}
is a point on
z^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl},\mathbb{S}}
.
∎

5.3.
I
-function of trivial toric bundles

We review next a (relatively small)
I
-function of
B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}
.
Brown
[
5
]
gives a formula for an
I
-function (i.e., a point on the Givental cone) of a
split
toric bundle.
In particular, it applies to trivial toric bundles, and in that case it takes a simple form.
Namely, define
I^{\mathbb{S}}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}(\tau,t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z)
to be the product

(5.4)

I^{\mathbb{S}}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}(\tau,t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z):=J_{B}(\tau,z)\cdot I_{\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{S}}(t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z),

where
J_{B}(\tau,z)
is the
J
-function (
2.3
) of
B
and
I_{\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{S}}(t_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z)
is Givental’s
\mathbb{S}
-equivariant small

-function of the toric variety
\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}
(see, e.g.,
[
21
]
).
Explicitly,

\displaystyle I_{\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{S}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{i}^{(m)}\geq 0;\\
1\leq m\leq l,1\leq i\leq r_{m}\end{subarray}}\left[\prod_{m=1}^{l}\prod_{i=1}^{r_{m}}(q_{i}^{(m)})^{k_{i}^{(m)}}e^{\left(k_{i}^{(m)}+\frac{H_{i}^{(m)}}{z}\right)t_{i}^{(m)}}\right]\cdot I_{k_{\bullet}^{\bullet}}^{\mathbb{S}}(H_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z)

\displaystyle I_{k_{\bullet}^{\bullet}}^{\mathbb{S}}(H_{\bullet}^{\bullet},z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{l}\prod_{i=1}^{r_{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{r_{m+1}}\frac{\prod_{c=-\infty}^{0}(H_{i}^{(m)}-H_{j}^{(m+1)}+cz)}{\prod_{c=-\infty}^{k_{i}^{(m)}-k_{j}^{(m+1)}}(H_{i}^{(m)}-H_{j}^{(m+1)}+cz)}

Here we set
r_{l+1}:=N
,
H_{j}^{(l+1)}:=\nu_{j}
and
k_{j}^{(l+1)}:=0
.
Then Brown’s result says that
z\cdot I_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{S}}
lies in
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}},\mathbb{S}}
.
Further, the non-equivariant limit

(5.5)

I_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}:=\lim_{\nu\to 0}I^{\mathbb{S}}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}

gives a point on
z^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}
as well. Note that the non-equivariant
I_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}
obviously exists: it is obtained simply by setting
H_{j}^{(l+1)}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq r_{l+1}
.

By inspection, the specialization
[z\cdot I_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}}}^{\mathbb{S}}]_{t_{i}^{(m)}\to t^{(m)}}
is a
H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{pt})[\![q_{\bullet}^{\bullet}]\!]^{\mathbb{W}}[\![Q_{B},\tau,t^{\bullet}]\!]
-valued point of
\mathcal{L}_{B\times\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}},\mathbb{S}}^{\mathbb{W}}
.

5.4.
Abelian/Nonabelian correspondence

This section is devoted to proving Theorem

.

We introduce notation used in the rest of this section.

-basis

.

For

We first consider the case where

is a point.
The abelian/nonabelian correspondence in terms of formal

-equivariant Frobenius manifolds
[

was established for partial flag varieties
[

In particular, it yields the following.

Proposition 5.7

There exists
\epsilon(\sigma)\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{Fl})[\![q^{\bullet},\sigma]\!]

M_{\operatorname{Fl}_{\operatorname{ab}},\mathbb{S}}(\sigma)\omega|_{q^{(m)}_{i}\to(-1)^{r_{m}-1}q^{(m)}}

Proof.

Fix a lift

An

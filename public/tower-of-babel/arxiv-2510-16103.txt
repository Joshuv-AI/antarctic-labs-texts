Neutrino Mass Induced n n – n ¯ \overline{n} Oscillation
Ilja Doršner, Svjetlana Fajfer, Shaikh Saad

Abstract
The Georgi-Glashow model is the simplest possible attempt at grand unification. However, due to its particle content, it preserves a global U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} symmetry, where B B and L L are baryon and lepton numbers, respectively. It thus leaves neutrinos massless just as in the Standard Model. Extensions of the Georgi-Glashow model that break lepton number by two units, i.e., scenarios with | Δ ​ L | = 2 |\Delta L|=2 operator(s), naturally generate potentially viable Majorana neutrino masses. Consequently, B − L B\!-\!L breaking via the same dynamics that yields | Δ ​ L | = 2 |\Delta L|=2 interactions unavoidably induces | Δ ​ B | = 2 |\Delta B|=2 transitions, leading to intriguing processes such as neutron–antineutron ( n n – n ¯ \overline{n} ) oscillation. We demonstrate this intrinsic connection between neutrino mass generation and n n – n ¯ \overline{n} oscillation within tree-level seesaw mechanisms of the Type I, II, and III varieties, as well as in the one-loop and two-loop radiative neutrino mass models. Our study exhausts the simplest possible extensions of the Georgi-Glashow model that yield realistic neutrino masses and mixing parameters and, in the process, induce n n – n ¯ \overline{n} oscillation.



I
Introduction

The Georgi-Glashow model (GG) is the simplest possible scenario of grand unification
Georgi:1974sy
. It provides quark-lepton unification as well as unification of the Standard Model (SM) interactions but predicts neutrinos to be massless particles. How to generate phenomenologically viable neutrino masses within an
SU(5)
framework thus remains one of the most relevant open questions within the model building community.

We demonstrate, in this study, that there is an intrinsic connection between Majorana neutrino mass generation mechanisms in
SU(5)
and
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation that can be traced back to the quark-lepton unification. Our work is motivated by a recent analysis
Dorsner:2025epy
of two simple extensions of the GG model that can simultaneously yield observable
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation signatures, accommodate viable masses and mixing parameters of all the SM fermions, and satisfy all relevant experimentally established limits. It, furthermore, dovetails a study
Rao:1983sd
that, in the context of scalar extensions of the GG model, enumerate all possible
d=9
operators that can contribute to
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation.

Our finding is important because the Majorana nature of neutrinos can be tested experimentally by observing neutrinoless double beta decay. Our results suggest that detecting baryon number–violating processes such as
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation could provide an independent confirmation of the Majorana character of neutrinos if
SU(5)
is realized in nature. An additional experimental handle is provided by an ever present proton decay in
SU(5)
that is mediated by gauge bosons and scalar(s). The
|\Delta L|=2
breaking, in addition to neutrinoless double beta decay
Dolinski:2019nrj
, also induces lepton flavor–violating processes, such as muonium–antimuonium conversion

, which can be probed experimentally. Similarly,
|\Delta B|=2
processes, together with
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation

;
Mohapatra:1980qe
, give rise to various di-nucleon decays

;
Girmohanta:2019cjm
;
Dev:2022jbf
(for recent reviews on
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation, see Refs.
Phillips:2014fgb
;

). A dedicated study of the experimental implications of these processes, within concrete renormalizable

II
Implications of Quark-Lepton Unification and Majorana Neutrino Mass Generation

The GG model, which is based on
SU(5)
gauge group, consists of the following representations

\displaystyle\overline{5}_{F\,i}=\left(d^{c}_{i},L_{i}\right),\;\;10_{F\,i}=\left(Q_{i},u_{i}^{c},e_{i}^{c}\right),\;\;5_{S}=\left(T,H\right),\;\;24_{S},

(1)

where subscripts
F
and
S
denote fermionic and scalar representations, respectively, and
i(=1,2,3)
is a family index. Here,
Q_{i}
,
u^{c}_{i}
, and
d^{c}_{i}
are the SM quark multiplets, whereas
L_{i}
and
e^{c}_{i}
represent multiplets of the SM leptons.
T
stands for the color triplet and weak singlet, while
H
is the SM Higgs doublet.
Given the above particle content,
U(1)_{B-L}
remains an accidental global symmetry of the model
Weinberg:1979sa
;
Weinberg:1980bf
;
Wilczek:1979et
;
Wilczek:1979hc
;
Weldon:1980gi
;
Rao:1983sd
. To demonstrate this, let us, for definiteness, consider the Yukawa part of the
SU(5)
Lagrangian of a single generation of the SM fermions that specifies interactions between quarks and leptons, with the Higgs doublet and the color triplet. It reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}

\displaystyle=10_{F}\overline{5}_{F}5_{S}^{*}+10_{F}10_{F}5_{S}

\displaystyle=\left(QL+u^{c}d^{c}\right)T^{*}+\left(QQ+u^{c}e^{c}\right)T

\displaystyle+\left(e^{c}L+Qd^{c}\right)H^{*}+\left(Qu^{c}\right)H.

(2)

One can now trivially check that these interactions indeed preserve a global
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry if

\displaystyle Q_{+1/3},\;\;u_{-1/3}^{c},\;\;d^{c}_{-1/3},\;\;L_{-1},\;\;e^{c}_{+1},\;\;T_{-2/3},\;\;H_{0},

(3)

where the subscripts represent the
U(1)_{B-L}
charges. To correct the erroneous charged fermion mass relations of the GG model one can, for example, extend it in a minimal manner through introduction of a single
45_{S}

Georgi:1979df
. Even in such extended GG scenario, with the associated additional Yukawa interactions, the
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry remains unbroken
Rao:1983sd
. Note that
U(1)_{B-L}
conservation does not preclude two-body proton decay processes. For example, in the
p\to e^{+}\pi^{0}
process, both the initial and the final state have
B-L=+1
.

If, however, one incorporates Majorana neutrino mass generation, which requires
|\Delta L|=2
, one automatically breaks
U(1)_{B-L}
by two units. This, on the other hand, leads to
|\Delta B|=2
processes, as we demonstrate next using an effective field theory approach. Namely, an analogue of the
d=5
Weinberg operator within the GG framework yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{5}=\frac{C_{ij}}{\Lambda}\overline{5}_{F\,i}\overline{5}_{F\,j}5_{S}5_{S}=\frac{C_{ij}}{\Lambda}\underbrace{\left(\underbrace{L_{i}L_{j}HH}_{\Delta L=+2}+\underbrace{d^{c}_{i}d^{c}_{j}TT}_{\Delta B=-2}\right)}_{\Delta(B-L)=-2},

(4)

where
\Lambda
represents a scale of new physics and
C_{ij}
are associated Wilson coefficients. We see that the dynamics responsible for Majorana neutrino mass, due to unification of quarks and leptons in
SU(5)
, induces baryon-number-violating processes with
|\Delta B|=2
. For example, the last term in Eq. (
4
) can lead to
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation via the following
d=9
operator:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda}d_{1}^{c}d_{1}^{c}TT\to\frac{1}{\Lambda^{5}}d_{1}^{c}d_{1}^{c}\left(u_{1}^{c}d_{1}^{c}\right)\left(u_{1}^{c}d_{1}^{c}\right).

(5)

To better understand advocated connection between quark-lepton unification and Majorana neutrino mass generation within the framework of renormalizable
SU(5)
models, let us consider the following two examples:

•

Type I seesaw.
This scenario, which includes
\nu^{c}=1_{F}
, allows the following Yukawa contraction:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}

\displaystyle\supset\overline{5}_{F\,i}1_{F}5_{S}=L_{i}\nu^{c}H+d_{i}^{c}\nu^{c}T.

(6)

The interaction terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
6
) dictate that
\nu^{c}
must have
U(1)_{B-L}
charge of
+1
. Therefore, the following self-interaction

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}

\displaystyle\supset m_{\nu^{c}}\underbrace{\nu^{c}\nu^{c}}_{\Delta(B-L)=-2},

(7)

where
m_{\nu^{c}}
is a mass parameter, breaks aforementioned
U(1)_{B-L}
global symmetry by two units, as indicated.

•

Type II seesaw.
This case requires presence of a
15_{S}=\Delta_{1}(1,3,1)+\Delta_{3}(3,2,1/6)+\Delta_{6}(\overline{6},1,-2/3)
. One, therefore, has the following Yukawa contraction

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}

\displaystyle\supset\overline{5}_{F\,i}\overline{5}_{F\,j}15_{S}=L_{i}L_{j}\Delta_{1}+L_{i}d_{j}^{c}\Delta_{3}+d_{i}^{c}d_{j}^{c}\Delta_{6},

(8)

which dictates the
U(1)_{B-L}
charges of
\Delta_{1}
,
\Delta_{3}
, and
\Delta_{6}
to be
-2
,
+4/3
, and
+2/3
, respectively.
On the other hand, the scalar potential includes the following
SU(5)
invariant contraction

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\supset 5_{S}5_{S}15^{*}_{S}=\underbrace{HH\Delta_{1}^{*}+HT\Delta_{3}^{*}+TT\Delta_{6}^{*}}_{\Delta(B-L)=-2},

(9)

where each term on the right-hand side breaks
U(1)_{B-L}
by two units, as expected from preceding discussion. A far more detailed discussion of
U(1)_{B-L}
breaking within this scenario is presented in Ref.
Rao:1983sd
.

Of course, one can apply the analysis presented above to any other renormalizable model of Majorana neutrino mass to reach the same conclusion.

IV
Conclusions

We demonstrate, on the most general grounds, that Majorana mass generation and
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation process go hand in hand if
SU(5)
is realized in nature. Our study, furthermore, exhausts the simplest possible extensions of the GG model that yield realistic neutrino mass and mixing parameters and, according to our findings, automatically induce
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation. These results stipulate existence of an additional baryon-number-violating process besides an ever present proton decay that is mediated by gauge bosons and scalars to test
SU(5)
scenarios with Majorana neutrino masses. The existence of Majorana masses can be probed through
|\Delta L|=2
processes, such as neutrinoless double beta decay and lepton flavor–violating processes, including muonium–antimuonium oscillation. Similarly,
|\Delta B|=2
processes, in addition to
n
–
\overline{n}
oscillation, give rise to di-nucleon decays, offering further experimental opportunities. It, accordingly, provides additional motivation for experimental searches for nucleon decays and neutron–antineutron oscillations.

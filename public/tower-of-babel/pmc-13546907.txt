A machine learning–derived sarcopenia index is associated with survival and nonrelapse mortality in DLBCL∗
Authors: Fabian Ullrich, René Hosch, Emre Kocakavuk, Hanna K Zieger, Paul W Hotz, Nico Freund, Stefan K Alig, Hans Christian Reinhardt, Ulrich Dührsen, Andreas Hüttmann, Felix Nensa, Bastian von Tresckow
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•Radiologic sarcopenia identifies host vulnerability associated with nonrelapse mortality and hematotoxicity in newly diagnosed DLBCL.•Sarcopenia and treatment-emergent muscle loss provide information orthogonal to age, tumor burden, and molecular disease features.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is predominantly a disease of older adults, with a median age at diagnosis >60 years.1 Advanced age is associated with inferior survival outcomes in DLBCL,2,3 highlighting the need to incorporate measures of biological age into risk stratification.4

Body composition analysis (BCA) can be employed to quantify skeletal muscle and adipose tissue, typically based on computed tomography (CT) imaging.5 BCA-derived sarcopenia has emerged as an objective surrogate of previously difficult-to-quantify constructs, such as cancer cachexia6, 7, 8 and, more broadly, biological age.7,9 As such, it mitigates key disadvantages of geriatric assessment (GA), which is partly subjective and infrequently implemented in real-world clinical practice.10 Numerous studies have demonstrated an association between radiologic sarcopenia and survival in cancer,11, 12, 13, 14, 15 suggesting that BCA may identify at-risk patients with potential benefit from prehabilitation.

In DLBCL, a prognostic role for sarcopenia has been investigated in both newly diagnosed as well as in relapsed or refractory disease. Several single-center retrospective analyses demonstrated an association between low muscle mass and inferior outcomes after first-line immunochemotherapy16, 17, 18; however, a multicentric prospective cohort study did not support this conclusion.19 In relapsed or refractory disease, sarcopenia has been associated with inferior survival in patients treated with high-dose chemotherapy and autologous stem cell transplantation.20 Regarding patients who received chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, results are conflicting: although some studies have linked sarcopenia to reduced overall survival (OS),21,22 other research did not confirm an independent prognostic effect.23

All previous analyses in DLBCL assessed body composition 2-dimensionally, based on assigning tissue types on a single axial CT image at the L3 vertebra level. Although widely used, this approach only approximates whole-body tissue abundance. Volumetric BCA has the potential to overcome this limitation by accurately quantifying distinct tissues in the 3-dimensional space.12,24

In this study, we assessed the prognostic role of radiologic sarcopenia in newly diagnosed DLBCL using the pretrained deep learning–based segmentation network Body and Organ Analysis (BOA).24 BOA enables fully automated volumetric BCA from CT imaging and can be seamlessly integrated into Picture Archiving and Communication System workflows, capitalizing on hardware and computational infrastructure typically available in hospitals. In addition, the pipeline generates structured reports with visual segmentation outputs for clinical use and quality control. We have previously demonstrated the prognostic potential of BOA-derived body composition in multiple cancers.12, 13, 14, 15,25 Importantly, BOA distinctively outperformed survival prognostication by 2-dimensional BCA in various scenarios, including a large cohort of >15 000 individuals with solid tumors.12

We performed BCA using the BOA network in 291 participants of the prospective, multicenter phase 3 PETAL (Positron Emission Tomography-Guided Therapy of Aggressive Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas) trial, which confirmed the predictive role of interim positron emission tomography (iPET) in patients receiving initial treatment with R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) for newly diagnosed DLBCL.26 We evaluated the prognostic relevance of sarcopenia at diagnosis and early changes in skeletal muscle mass during therapy and explored their relationship with age, lymphoma burden, and molecular disease features. Our study provides a comprehensive assessment of the prognostic role of sarcopenia in a large prospectively recruited cohort of patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL.

Methods

Patients

This study constitutes a post hoc analysis of the prospective phase 3 PETAL trial.26 Participants with DLBCL were eligible if baseline and interim CT data (from PET/CT scans) were available for analysis. The PETAL trial was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov as NCT00554164 and approved by the German Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices and the ethics committees of the participating institutions. The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and all patients provided written informed consent.

Participants were enrolled from November 2007 to December 2012. Clinical and laboratory parameters were obtained from the study database. Metabolic tumor volume (MTV) had previously been quantified using the ACCURATE tool (RRID: SCR_020955).27,28 The prognostic nutritional index (PNI) was calculated as follows: albumin (grams per deciliter) + 5 × lymphocyte count (per nanoliter). Toxicity was reported based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (RRID: SCR_010296), version 3.0.

BCA

BCA was performed using the pretrained deep learning–based segmentation network BOA.24 BOA achieves volumetric measurement in a 2-step process: first, body regions within a CT are assigned, followed by segmentation of individual tissues. For this analysis, tissue volumes from the ventral cavity (mediastinum, thoracic cavity, and abdominopelvic cavity combined) and the skeletal muscle area at the L3 vertebra level were used. The robustness of the BOA pipeline with regard to varying CT radiation doses and contrast media application has previously been validated in animal studies.29,30

To normalize skeletal muscle abundance, the sarcopenia index (SI) and skeletal muscle index (SMI) were calculated for each patient:12,13 SI was calculated as skeletal muscle volume (milliliter) divided by bone volume (milliliter), and SMI was calculated as L3 skeletal muscle area (square centimeter) divided by height (square meter). The relative SI at iPET (SIrel) was defined as SI at iPET divided by baseline SI.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R (version 4.5.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria; RRID: SCR_001905).

For time-to-event analyses, OS, progression-free survival (PFS), and failure-free survival (FFS) as defined in the PETAL trial26 were selected as study end points. The baseline time point was defined as the date of the initial PET/CT, and the interim response assessment time point was defined as the date of the iPET. For Kaplan-Meier analyses, patients were stratified into tertiles based on SI and SIrel, and survival curves were compared between the lowest tertile (T1) and the combined upper tertiles (T2 plus T3) via log-rank test. Tertile-based stratification was prespecified following previously described methods.12

For multivariable Cox regression analyses, OS was chosen as the primary end point, and PFS and FFS were defined as secondary end points. Model specification was informed by clinical relevance and supported by Akaike information criterion and concordance index. The proportional-hazards assumption was assessed through Schoenfeld residuals. Cause-specific and subdistribution hazard models were used to differentiate lymphoma-related and nonrelapse mortality (NRM). The association between treatment toxicity and SI was evaluated through binary logistic regression. The effects of age and lymphoma burden on body composition were assessed using linear regression models.

To compare BCA parameters between molecular subgroups, the Kruskal-Wallis test was used, and the distribution of molecular subgroups between groups defined by BCA parameter tertiles was compared using the Fisher exact test.

Analysis of sequencing data

Processing of whole-exome sequencing data, molecular clustering, and the assignment of cell-of-origin subtypes (Lymph2Cx, NanoString, Seattle, WA; RRID: SCR_021712) have been described elsewhere.31

Recurrently mutated genes were defined as genes mutated in ≥10% of patients, restricted to variants with predicted moderate or high functional impact. The oncoprint of recurrently mutated genes was generated in R using ComplexHeatmap.32 The association between mutation status and body composition was tested using gene-wise linear regression models. Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction was applied to adjust P values for multiple testing.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the study cohort

The study cohort included 291 patients treated on the phase 3 PETAL trial with a diagnosis of DLBCL and baseline and iPET imaging available for analysis. The median age was 62 years (interquartile range [IQR], 51-70), and 45.7% of patients (n = 133) were female. Sixty-eight (23.4%) and 49 patients (16.8%) had high-intermediate or high-risk disease according to the International Prognostic Index (IPI), respectively. The median MTV was 0.189 L (IQR, 0.040-0.659).

Median OS, PFS, and FFS were not reached, and estimated OS, PFS, and FFS at 5 years were 74.7% (95% confidence interval [CI], 69.2-80.8), 63.9% (95% CI, 57.7-70.6), and 61.0% (95% CI, 54.8-67.8), respectively. The median follow-up duration was 53.4 months (95% CI, 51.0-56.2). Key baseline characteristics and survival outcomes are summarized in Table 1. BCA was performed using the BOA network on baseline and interim response assessment CT imaging data for all patients in the study cohort. The aggregated BCA results are listed in Table 2.

Impact of body composition on survival

In Kaplan-Meier analysis, an SI in the lowest tertile at the time of diagnosis was associated with inferior OS compared to the combined upper tertiles (3-year OS, 76.2% [95% CI, 68.0-85.5] vs 87.2% [95% CI, 82.5-92.1]; log-rank test, P = .005; Figure 1A). Consistent results were observed for PFS (3-year PFS, 66.0% [95% CI, 56.9-76.6] vs 75.9% [95% CI, 70.1-82.2]; log-rank test, P = .027; Figure 1B) and FFS (3-year FFS, 61.8% [95% CI, 52.6-72.7] vs 73.4% [95% CI, 67.4-79.9]; log-rank test, P = .016; Figure 1C). These findings are in line with previous studies reporting an association between baseline sarcopenia and survival in DLBCL.16, 17, 18 However, in our cohort, conventional L3-derived SMI did not demonstrate an association with survival (supplemental Figure 1; supplemental Table 1), suggesting that volumetric BCA captures aspects of body composition not reflected by single-slice measurements.

Kaplan-Meier survival curves. Survival curves depicting OS, PFS, and FFS according to SI at baseline (A-C) and SIrel at iPET (D-F). Groups are stratified by the lowest tertile vs the combined upper tertiles (prespecified stratification). Significance was determined using the log-rank test.

Next, we evaluated body composition dynamics between baseline and iPET BCA. Despite the short median interval of 41 days (IQR, 39-45) between imaging procedures, SI was significantly decreased at interim response assessment (median relative change, −2% [IQR, −6 to +1]; paired Wilcoxon test, P < .001; Table 2). Comparison of the median relative change in skeletal muscle volume and bone volume confirmed that the SI decrease primarily reflected skeletal muscle loss (−1.8% vs −0.28%; paired Wilcoxon test, P < .001; Table 2).

SIrel demonstrated strong discrimination of survival between the lowest tertile and the combined upper tertiles. Estimated 3-year OS was 72.9% (95% CI, 64.3-82.6) for the lowest tertile and 87.6% (95% CI, 83.0-92.5) for the combined upper tertiles (log-rank test, P < .001; Figure 1D). Similarly, 3-year PFS was 62.6% (95% CI, 53.5-73.3) and 77.1% (95% CI, 71.3-83.4; log-rank test, P = .002; Figure 1E), and 3-year FFS was 58.6% (95% CI, 49.4-69.5) and 73.6% (95% CI, 67.6-80.1; log-rank test, P < .001; Figure 1F), indicating consistently inferior survival outcomes for patients with greater muscle loss during initial therapy. In univariable Cox proportional-hazards analyses, lower baseline SI was strongly associated with inferior OS (hazard ratio [HR], 0.306; 95% CI, 0.156-0.599; P < .001; supplemental Table 1), PFS (HR, 0.440 [95% CI, 0.256-0.755]; P = .003; supplemental Table 1), and FFS (HR, 0.444; 95% CI, 0.267-0.739; P < .001; supplemental Table 1). In a landmark analysis starting at the iPET time point, SIrel demonstrated a significant association with OS (HR, 0.961; 95% CI, 0.929-0.995; P = .025), but not PFS or FFS (supplemental Table 1). The weaker association between SIrel as a continuous variable and survival end points contrasts with its strong discriminative power when assessed via tertile-based stratification, consistent with a threshold effect. To account for this apparent nonlinear association, tertile-based stratification was used in multivariable analyses to reflect the observed risk pattern.

As prior studies have linked metabolic and inflammatory laboratory markers, including albumin and PNI, to sarcopenia and survival in DLBCL,19,23 we evaluated their relationship with outcomes in our cohort. Albumin or PNI were not related to OS, although PNI showed an association with PFS and FFS (supplemental Table 1). C-reactive protein was associated with all 3 survival end points in univariable analysis (supplemental Table 1).

Relationship of sarcopenia, age, and disease burden

To investigate whether the association between sarcopenia and survival reflects a surrogate function for age and lymphoma burden, we examined the relationship between SI, age, and MTV using linear regression (Table 3). Baseline SI was inversely associated with age (β, −0.0138 per year; P < .001) and, to a lesser extent, with MTV (β, −0.050 per L; P = .01). According to this model, age and tumor burden together accounted for ∼20% of the interindividual variability in baseline SI (adjusted R2 = 0.195), indicating that a substantial proportion of SI variability is not explained by these parameters alone. For SIrel, we observed no association with MTV (P = .722) but a significant inverse association with age (β, −0.002 per year; P < .001). However, this model explained only a small fraction of the observed variability (adjusted R2 = 0.091). Taken together, these regression analyses suggest that only a limited fraction of baseline SI and SIrel variance can be attributed to age and tumor burden. In line with these findings, age >60 years was the only IPI risk factor that occurred more frequently in the lowest baseline SI tertile (70% vs 46%; Pearson χ2, P < .001). Baseline SI and SIrel were not correlated (Spearman ρ = −0.068; P = .25), indicating that sarcopenia at diagnosis and early muscle loss during immunochemotherapy represent distinct biological phenomena. Accordingly, SIrel was significantly lower in iPET-positive patients (median, 0.947 vs 0.984; Wilcoxon test, P = .009), whereas baseline SI did not differ between groups (median, 2.230 vs 2.133; Wilcoxon P = .838).

As reported previously,12 baseline SI was higher in men than in women (median, 2.24 vs 2.04; Wilcoxon test, P < .001), whereas no sex-based difference was observed for SIrel (median, 0.98 vs 0.99; Wilcoxon test, P = .105), suggesting that treatment-emergent muscle loss is not primarily driven by sex.

Sarcopenia and the molecular landscape of DLBCL

We then examined whether molecular features of DLBCL were associated with body composition in a subset of 105 patients with available whole-exome sequencing data and 101 patients with gene expression-based cell-of-origin subtypes31 to further elucidate the relationship between sarcopenia and lymphoma biology. In our cohort, we identified 58 recurrently mutated genes (mutation frequency, ≥10%; Figure 2A), among them various canonical DLBCL driver mutations.33,34 Of these recurrently mutated genes, none were significantly associated with baseline SI or SIrel after adjustment for multiple testing in gene-wise linear regression analysis (all false discovery rate–adjusted P values >.1; supplemental Table 2). Similarly, neither SI nor SIrel differed across specific molecular clusters defined either by the Dana-Farber Cancer Institute classifier or by the LymphGen algorithm (all Kruskal-Wallis P values >.1; Figure 2B), and Dana-Farber Cancer Institute and LymphGen cluster assignments were evenly distributed between patients in the lowest SI or SIrel tertile and those in the combined upper tertiles (all Fisher exact test P values >.1). Furthermore, the distribution of SI and SIrel was comparable across activated B-cell–like, germinal center B-cell–like, and unclassified subtypes (Kruskal-Wallis test, P = .29 and P = .769). Collectively, these findings suggest that baseline skeletal muscle mass and early muscle loss are largely independent of molecular disease biology in DLBCL.

Multivariable analysis of survival end points

Next, we assessed the independent prognostic value of body composition indices using multivariable Cox proportional-hazards models. In a multivariable model correcting for IPI, MTV, sex, and body mass index (BMI), lower SI was again associated with inferior OS (HR, 0.330; 95% CI, 0.150-0.725; P = .006; Figure 3A), but not PFS and FFS (supplemental Table 3). In contrast, IPI and MTV were strongly prognostic for all survival end points (Figure 3A; supplemental Table 3). Because of substantial missing data, lack of independent prognostic value, and failure to improve model concordance, immunometabolic laboratory parameters were not included in the final multivariable model.

We further developed a model incorporating dynamic parameters (SIrel tertile and iPET response) with survival assessment starting from the iPET landmark. Further covariates included IPI, MTV, and baseline SI. Here, baseline SI again retained its independent prognostic value for OS (HR, 0.313; 95% CI, 0.150-0.656; P = .002; Figure 3B) but was not associated with PFS and FFS (supplemental Table 4). SIrel in the combined upper tertiles was associated with improved OS (HR, 0.483; 95% CI, 0.285-0.818;]; P = .007; Figure 3B), PFS (HR, 0.633; 95% CI, 0.408-0.983; P = .042; supplemental Table 4), and FFS (HR, 0.615; 95% CI, 0.406-0.930; P = .021; supplemental Table 4), supporting early muscle loss during immunochemotherapy as an independent prognostic parameter in newly diagnosed DLBCL.

Differentiating lymphoma-specific mortality and NRM

Based on the strong association between baseline SI and OS, but not with PFS and FFS, in multivariable models, we hypothesized that low skeletal muscle mass at diagnosis predominantly predicts NRM rather than lymphoma-related mortality (LRM). Therefore, we evaluated cause-specific mortality using competing-risks analysis. Of the 63 deaths during the observation period, 24 were attributed to lymphoma and 34 were classified as NRM. Five deaths had an unknown cause and were therefore censored in subsequent analyses (supplemental Table 5).

In cause-specific multivariable Cox proportional-hazards regression adjusted for IPI score and MTV, baseline SI showed no association with LRM (HR, 1.379; 95% CI, 0.502-3.785; P = .533; Figure 4A) but was the strongest independent predictor of NRM (HR, 0.186; 95% CI, 0.066-0.527; P = .002; Figure 4A). Similarly, in Fine-Gray competing risks regression with LRM as the event of interest and NRM as the competing risk, baseline SI again demonstrated no association with LRM (HR, 1.63; 95% CI, 0.689-3.850; P = .270; Figure 4B). When the model was inverted to use NRM as the primary end point, baseline SI again showed the strongest association with mortality (HR, 0.165; 95% CI, 0.060-0.449; P < .001; Figure 4B). These findings identify sarcopenia as an independent predictor of NRM in newly diagnosed DLBCL.

Assessing baseline sarcopenia’s potential to predict hematological toxicity

After the identification of baseline SI as a prognostic factor for NRM, we sought to further substantiate the hypothesis that SI predicts treatment tolerance at diagnosis. Therefore, we employed logistic regression to determine the association between SI and higher-grade hematologic toxicity during first-line immunochemotherapy. Patients who experienced hematotoxicity only after treatment intensification to a Burkitt lymphoma chemotherapy protocol in accordance with the PETAL trial protocol (n = 6) were excluded from the analysis. A total of 147 patients (51.6%) experienced any kind of grade 3 or 4 hematotoxicity. For 136 patients (47.1%), grade 3 or 4 neutropenia was reported, and 64 (22.5%) had at least 1 episode of neutropenic fever. Thirty-three (11.6%) and 17 (6.0%) patients experienced grade 3 or 4 anemia or thrombopenia, respectively.

In univariable analyses, lower baseline SI demonstrated a strong association with overall higher-grade hematotoxicity (odds ratio [OR], 0.345; 95% CI, 0.186-0.621; P < .001; supplemental Table 6), driven by its association with neutropenia (OR, 0.370; 95% CI, 0.200-0.664; P = .001; supplemental Table 6) and anemia (OR, 0.121; 95% CI, 0.038-0.382; P < .001; supplemental Table 6). When adjusting for sex, BMI, individual IPI components, and MTV in multivariable logistic regression, baseline SI, as the only covariate, retained independent prognostic value for higher-grade hematologic toxicity (OR, 0.347; 95% CI, 0.185-0.723; P = .004; Figure 5), indicating that the radiologic assessment of sarcopenia before treatment initiation supports prognostication of treatment tolerance.

To explore whether increased hematotoxicity in patients with low SI could reflect relative overdosing of chemotherapy, we performed an exploratory analysis stratifying patients by obesity and sarcopenia status. In a logistic regression model, obese patients (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2) in the lowest tertile of baseline SI (n = 24) had the highest odds of experiencing higher-grade hematotoxicity compared to non-obese, nonsarcopenic patients (n = 135; OR, 2.50; 95% CI, 1.03-6.53; P = .049). Non-obese sarcopenic patients (n = 70) showed a nonsignificant trend toward increased hematotoxicity (OR, 1.77; 95% CI, 0.99-3.19; P = .056), whereas obese nonsarcopenic patients (n = 56) did not have a significantly increased risk (OR, 1.44; 95% CI, 0.77-2.71; P = .251).

Discussion

Here, we provide a comprehensive analysis of sarcopenia in a prospectively recruited cohort of patients newly diagnosed with DLBCL using a fully automated, volumetric BCA approach. We identified an independent association between baseline SI, SIrel, representing treatment-emergent muscle loss, and survival. Furthermore, we observed a more pronounced effect of baseline sarcopenia on OS compared to progression-based survival outcomes. Taking the limited contribution of MTV to SI variability into account, we hypothesized that excess mortality in sarcopenic patients may not be primarily attributable to lymphoma progression but rather to NRM, which we confirmed using cause-specific regression models. The absence of associations between molecular DLBCL features and body composition supports the notion that sarcopenia is largely independent of tumor biology and may reflect reduced host resilience rather than lymphoma-driven catabolism.

The link between NRM and SI suggests an increased vulnerability of sarcopenic patients to treatment-emergent adverse events. Infections, which particularly affect older patients with DLBCL,35 were the most frequent cause of NRM in our cohort. Although the limited number of events precluded robust analyses of cause-specific NRM, the predominance of infectious deaths is consistent with the hypothesis that sarcopenic patients may be more susceptible to severe infections. However, it should be noted that we did not observe a statistically significant association between SI and neutropenic fever.

Corticosteroids, a mainstay of first-line DLBCL therapy since the introduction of the CHOP regimen,36 may promote muscle wasting and increase susceptibility to infections.37 Although studies of glucocorticoid-related toxicity in lymphoma have primarily focused on metabolic effects, such as glucose dysregulation,38 our findings highlight the need to further investigate their potential contribution to sarcopenia and treatment complications.

Hematological toxicity from R-CHOP immunochemotherapy occurs more frequently in patients with limited physiological reserve,39 and increased treatment toxicity in sarcopenic patients with DLBCL has previously been reported for a retrospectively assessed cohort from a single center.18 In our prospectively recruited multicentric cohort, baseline sarcopenia was the only independent factor associated with ≥grade 3 hematotoxicity in a model adjusting for established clinical risk factors. The higher incidence of hematotoxicity in obese patients with low SI suggests that reduced skeletal muscle mass may influence pharmacokinetics, thus raising the possibility that chemotherapy dosing based on body surface area alone may not adequately account for interindividual differences in body composition. In this context, dose modification based on SI could represent a potential mitigation strategy.

Although efforts to refine risk stratification in DLBCL are ongoing, the IPI continues to be the most widely employed instrument for risk assessment.3 More refined approaches, such as the assessment of metabolically active tumor tissue28,40,41 or the quantification of circulating tumor DNA,42,43 hold potential to substantially enhance risk stratification. However, the broad implementation of these tools is limited by technical and operational complexities. Furthermore, previous work has demonstrated that, except for age, all IPI parameters are correlated with disease burden. In addition, circulating tumor DNA primarily functions as another surrogate of tumor volume,43,44 leaving age as the only major patient-related factor currently incorporated into risk stratification. Our results establish that most of the variability in SI at diagnosis as well as in muscle loss is unaccounted for by chronological age and lymphoma burden and warrant further investigation of sarcopenia as an orthogonal prognostic factor.

A potential use case for sarcopenia in DLBCL risk prognostication lies in scenarios where chronological age alone is insufficient for decision-making, such as in treatment assignment for elderly patients newly diagnosed with DLBCL. Although full-dose R-CHOP is typically administered up to ∼80 years of age, patients above this threshold are often treated with attenuated regimens such as R-miniCHOP.45 However, chronological age alone is an incomplete surrogate of physiological reserve, and additional risk assessment strategies are required.4,46 GA has been shown to capture functional impairment related to biological age and improve prognostic accuracy beyond established DLBCL risk models.47,48 Nevertheless, real-world adoption remains suboptimal: a recent survey among oncology providers in the United States revealed that fewer than half routinely employ GA in their clinical practice, with lack of time and resources being the most commonly cited obstacles to widespread implementation.10 In this context, BOA-derived sarcopenia assessment offers a complementary tool that provides objective information while seamlessly integrating into clinical workflows24 and enables dynamic refinement of risk stratification via longitudinal BCA using SIrel.

Although our study demonstrates the potential of BCA to enhance risk stratification in DLBCL, several limitations should be acknowledged. Most importantly, the analysis was conducted in a single-patient cohort. Although patients were prospectively recruited across multiple European centers and uniformly treated, we adopted a conservative analytical approach and refrain from proposing our findings for immediate clinical application in the absence of external validation. Clinical translation of BOA-supported risk assessment will require the development of robust prognostic tools, followed by confirmation in independent cohorts. Furthermore, our study does not allow conclusions regarding whether interventions targeting sarcopenia can improve survival. As end-of-treatment imaging was not centrally collected in the PETAL trial, longitudinal BCA could not be assessed after first response assessment. However, sarcopenia has been described as potentially reversible after completion of DLBCL treatment,49 and further research is warranted to identify patients in whom recovery from sarcopenia may contribute to favorable outcomes.

In summary, our study sheds light on the relationship between age, disease biology, sarcopenia, and survival in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL. We provide robust evidence that radiologic sarcopenia and treatment-emergent muscle loss are independent risk factors associated with adverse outcomes. Volumetric BCA using the BOA network offers a reliable approach to augment current risk stratification that can be integrated into routine clinical workflows. Our results suggest that the assessment of radiologic sarcopenia can be leveraged to refine prognostication and guide personalized treatment in DLBCL.

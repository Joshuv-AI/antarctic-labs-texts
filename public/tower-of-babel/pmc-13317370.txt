Acute type A aortic dissection: assessment of surgically treated and autopsied cases
Authors: Mesut Yilmaz, Bengü Berrak Ozkul, Nevzat Erdil, Osman Celbis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases are estimated to be responsible for approximately 17 million deaths worldwide each year [1]. In the United States, cardiovascular conditions account for nearly 36% of all deaths [2]. According to the Turkish Statistical Institute, diseases of the circulatory system constituted 33.4% of all deaths in Türkiye in 2023 and increased to 36% in 2024 [3].

Acute type A aortic dissection (TAAD) is the most frequently encountered pathological condition of the aorta and constitutes one of the most fatal cardiovascular emergencies [4, 5]. When treated with medical management alone, in-hospital mortality rates for acute TAAD may reach nearly 60% [6]. Historically, reported mortality rates ranged between 21% and 37% irrespective of patient age and exceeded 50% among patients presenting with hemodynamic shock. More recent studies, however, have demonstrated a decline in mortality associated with acute TAAD to 5.8% within the first 48 h after diagnosis, largely attributable to advancements in diagnostic techniques and surgical approaches [7]. In patients who remain untreated, mortality has been reported to increase by approximately 1–2% per hour following the onset of symptoms [8, 9].

Acute TAAD is a life-threatening disorder that often results in death due to aortic rupture or malperfusion of organs. Emergency surgical intervention remains the sole definitive and recommended treatment modality. Despite substantial improvements in perioperative care, morbidity and mortality rates remain considerable, suggesting that important aspects of disease pathophysiology and risk stratification have yet to be fully elucidated [10].

Studies in the literature comparing the data of hospitalized cases and autopsied cases in acute TAAD are extremely limited. This study aimed to determine the risk factors in aortic dissection and aortic dissection-related mortality by examining the characteristics of surgically treated TAAD cases and autopsied TAAD cases.

Methods

This retrospective study was conducted following approval from the Inonu University Health Sciences Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Date: 09/05/2023, Approval No: 2023/4640).

A total of 157 cases from two centers were included in the study between 2003 and 2022: 141 cases of acute TAAD who underwent surgical treatment at the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery, Inonu University Faculty of Medicine Hospital, and 16 autopsied cases identified at the Malatya Forensic Medicine Morgue Department. Medical records of the surgically treated patients at Inonu University Faculty of Medicine Hospital and autopsy reports from the Malatya Forensic Medicine Morgue Department were reviewed, and the findings from both centers were evaluated.

The clinical management of hospitalized patients with acute TAAD was carried out in accordance with previously described protocols [11, 12]. Although the study period spans a long time interval, the diagnostic and treatment protocols for acute TAAD were implemented in accordance with the standard approaches described in the referenced studies throughout this period. In surgically treated patients, the diagnosis was established using transthoracic echocardiography and/or contrast-enhanced computed tomography, with transesophageal echocardiography performed when initial imaging results were inconclusive. None of the type A aortic dissection cases autopsied at the Forensic Medicine Morgue Department had undergone surgical treatment.

Demographic variables, including age, sex, and obesity status, were assessed for all cases. Age was categorized into three groups: ≤45 years, 46–64 years, and ≥ 65 years. Cases with a BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² were classified as obese. In the surgically treated cohort, additional variables such as perioperative and postoperative complications (malperfusion, reoperative rupture), comorbid conditions, and mortality outcomes were evaluated. In autopsy cases, forensic reports were reviewed to obtain data on cardiac weight, myocardial wall thickness, and the presence of atherosclerotic plaques in the aorta and coronary arteries. Due to the limited availability of clinical information on comorbidities and complications in autopsy cases, these variables were analyzed exclusively in the surgically treated group.

All data were recorded and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 22.0 (IBM Corp., NY, USA). Prior to analysis, the assumptions required to determine whether parametric or nonparametric tests would be used were evaluated. Normality of distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, as well as skewness and kurtosis values. Relationships between categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test (Fisher’s exact test was used when assumptions were not met). The effects of independent variables on the dependent categorical variable were evaluated using logistic regression analysis. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 157 cases were included in the study, comprising 141 patients who underwent surgical treatment for TAAD at the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery of Inonu University Faculty of Medicine Hospital and 16 autopsied cases identified at the Malatya Forensic Medicine Morgue Department. Overall, 72.6% of the cases were male, and 46.9% were aged between 46 and 64 years (Table 1).

The mortality rate among surgically treated cases was 17%. The mortality rate was 30.6% in surgically treated cases with preoperative malperfusion and 9.8% in those without malperfusion. When autopsied cases, which represent fatal outcomes by definition, were also included, the overall mortality rate in the study was 25.5%. As autopsied cases inherently represent fatal outcomes, the overall mortality rate including these cases should be interpreted with caution. In this context, the overall mortality rate should not be considered in isolation, and this limitation should be taken into account when interpreting the findings. Nevertheless, as the study includes all accessible and evaluable cases from two centers within the same city during the specified time period, the overall mortality rate is reported to provide a more comprehensive perspective on the clinical and fatal spectrum of acute TAAD (Table 3).

Bivariate analysis of factors associated with mortality in surgically treated cases

A logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate mortality as the dependent variable, with age group (≤ 45, 46–64, ≥ 65 years) and preoperative malperfusion included as independent variables. Although cerebral ischemia and lower extremity ischemia were found to be significantly associated with mortality in bivariate analyses, these variables were not included as separate variables in the multivariable model because they represent subcomponents of preoperative malperfusion and are dependent on the malperfusion variable. To avoid the possibility of multicollinearity and to preserve the statistical reliability of the model, only the overall preoperative malperfusion variable was included in the logistic regression analysis. The Omnibus test indicated that the model was statistically significant overall (χ²=24.429; p < 0.001). The explanatory power of the model was determined as Cox & Snell R²=0.159 and Nagelkerke R²=0.266. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test demonstrated a good model fit (χ²=3.622; p = 0.460).

In the multivariable analysis, the presence of preoperative malperfusion was identified as an independent risk factor for mortality (B = 1.062; p = 0.030). The risk of mortality was 2.892 times higher in patients with preoperative malperfusion compared to those without (OR = 2.892; 95% CI: 1.109–7.543). Age was not found to be statistically significant in the overall model (p = 0.150). However, in subgroup analysis, patients aged 46–64 years had a 3.232-fold higher risk of mortality compared to the reference group, with borderline statistical significance (p = 0.051; OR = 3.232; 95% CI: 0.993–10.521) (Table 7).

Discussion

Acute TAAD is a clinical entity associated with a high risk of mortality. The early identification of mortality-related risk factors and timely intervention are essential for reducing mortality rates. In this study, potential risk factors were evaluated by analyzing the characteristics of surgically treated cases and autopsied cases.

In this study, 72.6% of the cases were male and 27.4% were female. Of the cases, 24.8% were aged 45 years and younger, 46.9% were between 46 and 64 years of age, and 29.3% were aged 65 years and older. In the study conducted by Cabasa et al. at Mayo Clinic, the proportion of males was reported as 67.2% and females as 32.8%. In the same study, the proportion of cases older than 60 years was 66.9%, those between 46 and 60 years of age were 22.7%, and those aged 45 years and younger were 10.4% [13]. In the study by Engin et al., 22.2% of the patients were female and 77.8% were male [14]. Wundram et al. reported that 65% of the cases were male and 35% were female [15]. These findings support that acute TAAD is more frequently observed particularly in middle-aged and older male populations. The combined evaluation of surgically treated patients and autopsied cases may introduce heterogeneity in terms of clinical characteristics, data availability, and outcome definitions between these two groups. However, this approach was preferred in order to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the disease in terms of age and sex by including all accessible surgically treated and autopsied cases of acute TAAD within the same city during the specified study period.

Among the 141 surgically treated cases, Marfan syndrome was present in 4.3%, smoking in 41.8%, hypertension in 93.6%, diabetes mellitus in 2.1%, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in 5.7%. Cabasa et al. [13], reported smoking in 53.3%, diabetes mellitus in 9.8%, hypertension in 76.8%, and chronic lung disease in 8.5% of patients. In the study by Engin et al. [14], hypertension was present in 84%, smoking in 54%, and diabetes mellitus in 15.6% of cases. Wundram et al. [15], reported hypertension in 67.4%, coronary artery disease in 12.8%, type 2 diabetes mellitus in 6.8%, smoking in 30.1%, and aortic disease (ectasia or aneurysm) in 13.6% of cases. Karadzha et al. [9], who evaluated autopsy cases, reported hypertension in 76.6%, hyperlipidemia in 22.3%, obesity in 36.2%, acute myocardial infarction in 25.5%, smoking in 19.1%, and alcohol use in 9.6% of cases. In our study, as in other studies, hypertension was identified as the most prevalent comorbid condition in acute TAAD. The higher rate of hypertension observed in our study compared to other studies may be related to the characteristics of the patient population, the severity of the clinical presentation at admission, and particularly the more frequent presence of long-term uncontrolled hypertension. The low prevalence of Marfan syndrome suggests that acquired cardiovascular risk factors, rather than hereditary connective tissue disorders, are more prominent in the majority of surgically treated cases.

In the present study, mortality was observed in 17% of the surgically treated cases. Apaydın et al. [16] reported a mortality rate of 25% in a cohort of 108 patients who underwent surgical treatment for TAAD. Similarly, Goossens et al. [17], documented an in-hospital mortality rate of 23.6% among surgically treated patients with TAAD. In our study, the mortality rate among surgically treated cases was found to be lower than that reported in other studies. The lower mortality rate observed in our study may be related to differences in patient characteristics, preoperative risk profile, surgical strategies, perioperative management approaches, and institutional surgical experience. However, it is not possible to determine the exact cause of this difference based on the available data.

In this study, the mortality rate was 30.6% in surgically treated cases with preoperative malperfusion and 9.8% in those without malperfusion. Girdauskas et al. reported a mortality rate of 29% in patients with malperfusion and 13.6% in those without malperfusion [18]. Yagdi et al. found the in-hospital mortality rate to be 42.1% in patients with malperfusion and 14.75% in those without malperfusion [19]. Similar to other studies, our study also showed that the presence of malperfusion increased mortality rates. This finding demonstrates that malperfusion is not only an accompanying clinical finding in acute TAAD but also one of the strongest predictors of poor prognosis. In our study, multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed that the presence of preoperative malperfusion increased the risk of mortality by approximately 2.9 times. This finding indicates that malperfusion has a significant and strong independent effect on mortality beyond other clinical variables.

In autopsied cases, aortic atheromatous plaques were identified in 31.3% and coronary artery atheromatous plaques in 75% of cases. In a forensic autopsy study by Kul et al. [20], investigating the macroscopic prevalence of generalized atherosclerosis, atheromatous plaques were detected in the aorta in 49.2% and in the coronary arteries in 40.1% of cases. Zhao et al. [21], who evaluated aortic dissection cases at autopsy, reported a coronary atherosclerosis rate of 22.5%. Autopsy findings are essential for identifying underlying structural cardiovascular pathologies in patients who die without a definitive clinical diagnosis. In our study, due to the lack of detailed data regarding aortic and coronary atherosclerosis in surgically treated cases, we were unable to determine whether atheromatous plaques had a significant impact on mortality in aortic dissection. The most distinctive contribution of this study lies in the integration of autopsy findings, which provide insights into structural cardiovascular changes such as atherosclerosis and cardiac hypertrophy that are not routinely available in clinical datasets. The coexistence of high rates of atheromatous plaques in autopsy cases suggests a potential interaction between underlying atherosclerosis and mortality in TAAD. Although causality cannot be established in this study, this observation highlights an area for future investigation.

In autopsied cases, the mean heart weight was 486.63 ± 88.42 g. Molina et al. [22, 23], reported mean heart weights of 331 ± 56.7 g in males and 245 ± 52 g in females who died due to traumatic causes. Sheikhazadi et al. [24], reported mean heart weights of 359.9 ± 76.6 g in males and 319.2 ± 86.4 g in females in a study conducted in Tehran. Kim et al. [25] reported mean heart weights of 346.81 ± 57.90 g in males and 298.79 ± 62.59 g in females in a Korean population. In this study, heart weights in cases with TAAD were higher than those reported in cases without cardiovascular causes of death. However, as these data are based on a limited number of autopsied cases, the generalizability of the findings is restricted. Therefore, larger studies with greater sample sizes are needed to enable generalization of this finding.

Study limitations

Nonetheless, this study has certain limitations. Due to the retrospective design of this study, comorbid conditions in autopsy-confirmed cases could not be comprehensively identified, and their effects on mortality could not be evaluated. In addition, in surgically treated cases from the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery, heart weight and the presence of atheromatous plaques in the aorta and coronary arteries, as well as their potential impact on mortality, could not be assessed. The number of autopsied cases was relatively low (n = 16), which limits the statistical power and generalizability of the study.

Conclusion

The significantly lower mortality observed in surgically treated patients without malperfusion strongly highlights the importance of the relationship between early diagnosis and timely surgical intervention in the management of acute type A aortic dissection. In this study, malperfusion was identified as the factor most strongly associated with mortality in surgically treated cases, and its independent association with an approximately 2.9-fold increase in mortality supports its role as one of the major determinants of prognosis. The high prevalence of atheromatous plaques in the aorta and coronary arteries in autopsied cases suggests a possible relationship between atherosclerosis and mortality in TAAD cases. Further studies are needed to evaluate the independent effect of atherosclerosis on mortality. Notably, as one of the rare studies evaluating together the characteristics of both surgically treated and autopsied cases, this study has a distinctive value in the literatüre.

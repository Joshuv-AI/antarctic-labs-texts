Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to study certain set-valued integrals in UMD Banach spaces and provide a compatible form of the martingale representation theorem for set-valued martingales. Under specific conditions, these martingales can be expressed using revised set-valued stochastic integrals with respect to a real standard Brownian motion W = ( W t ) t ∈ [ 0 , T ] W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]} . Moreover, we prove the existence of solutions to the following set-valued backward stochastic differential equation of the form Y t = ( ξ + ∫ t T H u ​ 𝑑 u + ∫ [ 0 , t ] ℛ Z ​ d ​ W u ) ⊝ ∫ [ 0 , T ] ℛ Z ​ d ​ W u a . s . , t ∈ [ 0 , T ] , \displaystyle Y_{t}=\left(\xi+\int_{t}^{T}H_{u}du+\int_{[0,t]}^{\mathscr{R}}{Z}\,dW_{u}\right)\circleddash\int_{[0,T]}^{\mathscr{R}}{Z}\,dW_{u}\quad a.s.,\quad t\in[0,T], where the right-hand side, of this equation, represents the Hukuhara difference of two quantities containing revised set-valued stochastic integrals, ξ \xi is a terminal set-valued function condition and H H is a set-valued function satisfying some suitable conditions.

Introduction

Due to their connections with statistical and economic problems, the integration of set-valued functions has attracted increasing interest over time. Several approaches to defining the integration of set-valued functions can be distinguished. Firstly, Aumann
[
1
]
defined a set-valued integral using the set of all integrable selections. Hukuhara
[
11
]
studied the integration of measurable functions whose values are compact convex sets, while Debreu
[
7
]
employed an embedding method to consider the Bochner integral in an embedded Banach space.

Let us mention here that other properties of set-valued integrals have been studied, notably in the work of Hiai and Umegaki
[
12
]
, where new concepts were introduced and played a key role in constructing the multivalued conditional expectation with respect to a
\sigma
-finite measure. Later, the notion of set-valued functions was extended to the stochastic case when Kisielewicz, in
[
21
]
, presented concepts of set-valued functional stochastic integrals with a selection property that is considered fundamental for stochastic inclusions.

This class of stochastic integrals does not yield set-valued stochastic processes nor exhibit properties analogous to those of point-valued stochastic integrals. Subsequently, Jung and Kim, in
[
20
]
, proposed a definition of a set-valued stochastic integral with respect to a standard Brownian motion, building on Theorem
3.8
of [
[
18
]
, Chapter 2]. Nevertheless, these set-valued stochastic integrals are not integrably bounded in the general case (see
[
28
]
). To overcome this limitation, Kisielewicz introduced a new concept called the generalized set-valued stochastic integral in
[
24
]
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space. Before defining our set-valued integrals in UMD Banach spaces, let us first clarify the definitions of the following set-valued integrals:

•

2

\displaystyle S_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}^{2}\left((\ell_{\mathbb{F}})\int_{s}^{t}H_{u}du\right)=\overline{dec}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\left\{\int_{s}^{t}g(u,.)du:g\in S_{\Sigma_{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(H)\right\}\right),

where

For a nonempty subset

In this work, our main purpose is to study certain set-valued integrals in UMD Banach spaces and provide a compatible form of the martingale representation theorem for set-valued martingales. To achieve this, we first need to choose an appropriate space that offers the right setting for establishing a robust theory of stochastic integration of adapted processes taking values in a UMD space
E

In order to give a more comprehensive view, we consider the following backward stochastic evolution equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dU_{t}=f(t)dt+V_{t}dW_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\

•

How to suitably define the integrals of set-valued stochastic processes, and what properties do they have?

•

How to define set-valued stochastic integrals with respect to the Brownian motion
W

Concerning the integral with respect to a standard Brownian motion

Moreover, the revised representation theorem [

Under appropriate conditions, we opt to represent set-valued martingales using the revised set-valued integral, as this representation aligns with the set-valued context, adheres to the martingale representation theorem in classical stochastic analysis, and offers sufficient flexibility to extend (

where the right-hand side of (

\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable and

2.2

Let

be a

We first note that there are various possible definitions of measurability for functions, as discussed in [

]

Definition 2.1

Let

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathcal{S};E)}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left\|f\right\|^{q}_{E}d\mu<\infty,

where the integral is in the Lebesgue sense.

The linear space

consisting of (classes of a.e. equal) Bochner
q
-integrable functions

, endowed with the norm
\left\|.\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{S};E)}
, is a Banach space.

For a given sub-

of

to express the closed linear subspace of

\mu_{\mid\tilde{\mathscr{A}}}
-version of a
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
-measurable function.

We notice that the

-simple functions are dense in the Bochner space

, and for any
\mu

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}^{Bochner}hd\mu:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu(A_{j})x_{j}.

(6)

The expression in (

The Bochner integral of a function

A function

Let

In the metric space

, the Minkowski sum and scalar multiplication of sets are two operations. However, they generally do not allow for the construction of an opposite element.

Hukuhara, in

[

Based on this, we extend the concept as follows.

Definition 2.25

is said to be the Hukuhara difference of

. In case where

Remark 2.26
.

We notice that, in case of existence, the Hukuhara difference

is unique by virtue of the cancellation law lemma [
[
18
]
, Chapter 1, Lemma
2.74
], and it is easily to check that

\displaystyle\left|A\circleddash B\right|_{\varrho}\leq\left|A\right|_{\varrho}+\left|B\right|_{\varrho},

(19)

where
\left|\,.\,\right|_{\varrho}
is defined by (
5
).

Building on Proposition 4.16 in
[

]
, we present a key result that facilitates the proof of the measurability of the Hukuhara difference between two set-valued functions.

Proposition 2.27
.

Assume that
E
is a reflexive space.
Let
A
and

be two nonempty convex weakly compact subsets of

, and let
(x^{*}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in

that is dense in
E^{*}
.

If
A\circleddash B
exists, then we have

A\circleddash B=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\left\{x\in E:\langle x,x^{*}_{n}\rangle\,\,\leq\,\,\sup_{a\in A}\langle a,x^{*}_{n}\rangle-\sup_{b\in B}\langle b,x^{*}_{n}\rangle\right\}.

Proof.

Let
x\in A\circleddash B
. Since
x+B\subset A
, then one can get obviously

\displaystyle\forall x^{*}\in E^{*}\setminus\left\{0_{E^{*}}\right\},\;\langle x,-x^{*}\rangle\,\,\geq\,\,\inf_{a\in A}\langle a,-x^{*}\rangle-\inf_{b\in B}\langle b,-x^{*}\rangle.

Conversely, take

Let us choose

It remains to use the continuity of the support functions

and

to conclude the result.

Since

is separable, then the martingale
\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)\right)_{T\geq t\geq 0}
possesses a cadlag version
\left(\mathbb{E}_{cad}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)\right)_{T\geq t\geq 0}
.

Besides this, from the properties of the filtration

we derive that for each

admits a continuous version denoted by
\left(\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\phi_{j})\right)_{T\geq t\geq 0}
.

It follows from Doob’s maximal inequality and the contractiveness of the conditional expectation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{T\geq t\geq 0}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{cad}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\phi_{j})x_{j}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbb{E}_{cad}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\phi_{j})x_{j}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\phi_{j})x_{j}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\phi_{j}x_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{T};E)}^{p}.

Combining with
(\ref{ineq2})
we derive on the one hand that
\left(\mathbb{E}_{cad}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)\right)_{T\geq t\geq 0}
has continuous trajectories, and on the other hand
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(\mathbb{R})\otimes E
is dense in the vector space
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}_{closed}(E)
consisting of equivalence classes by indistinguishability of continuous versions
\left(\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Phi)\right)_{T\geq t\geq 0}
,
\Phi\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{T};E)
.

Finally, from the fact that
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(E)=\mathscr{M}^{c,p}_{closed}(E)
the proof is accomplished.
∎

Theorem 3.9

5
Martingale and Casting Representation Theorems for Set-valued Martingales in UMD Banach Spaces

We adopt certain constraints to represent a set-valued martingale as a revised set-valued stochastic integral, and establish a Casting representation of this set-valued martingale through a sequence of martingale selections. After that, under suitable conditions, we show the possibility of representing the Hukuhara difference of two set-valued martingales as a revised set-valued stochastic integral.

Let
E
be a separable UMD space and assume that
\mathbb{F}
is the natural filtration generated by a standard Brownian motion
W=\left(W_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
augmented by all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathscr{F}
.

Let
\Psi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathscr{K}_{c}(E)
be an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted set-valued process such that
\Psi_{T}
is
p
-integrably bounded.

5.1
Martingale Representation

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\Psi
is a set-valued martingale, then the set
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}(\Psi)
of all
\mathbb{F}
-martingale selections of
\Psi
is nonempty and forms a closed subset of
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
m
be the least natural number greater than or equal to
T
. We introduce
\Psi^{d}=\left(\Psi_{k\wedge T}\right)_{m\geq k\geq 0}
which is a discretization of
\Psi
. By virtue of Theorem
3.2
in
[
14
]
we infer that
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi^{d}\right)
is nonempty and for an element
\left(\psi^{d}_{k\wedge T}\right)_{m\geq k\geq 0}
of
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi^{d}\right)
, the process
(\psi_{t})_{T\geq t\geq 0}
constructed in the following manner

\displaystyle\psi_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{[k-1,k\wedge T)}(t)\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\psi^{d}_{k\wedge T}\right)+\mathds{1}_{\left\{T\right\}}(t)\psi^{d}_{T}\quad\textrm{in }L^{p}\left(\Omega;E\right)\;\textrm{ for each }t,

(39)

ensures us the non-emptiness of the set
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)
. Indeed, for any
t
, there is
k
such that

\psi_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\psi^{d}_{k\wedge T}\right)\in S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{k\wedge T}^{d}\right)\right)=S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{t}\right).

On the other hand, it is obvious that any martingale selection of
\Psi
admits a continuous version, which will be the associated representative. Thus, we shall consider
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)
as a subset of
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}\left(E\right)
.

Let
\left(\psi^{k}\right)_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence in
\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)
that converges to
\psi
in
\mathscr{M}^{c,p}\left(E\right)
. We have

\displaystyle d_{L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)}^{p}\left(\psi_{t},S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\psi_{t}-\psi_{t}^{k}\right\|^{p}\right)+d_{L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)}^{p}\left(\psi^{k}_{t},S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{t}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left\|\psi-\psi^{k}\right\|_{\mathscr{M}^{c,p}\left(E\right)}^{p},

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
d_{L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)}
denotes the distance from an element to a non-empty subset of
L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)
. Thus, the aim is accomplished.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\Psi
is a set-valued martingale, then for every

we have

\displaystyle\overline{dec}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\pi_{t}\left(\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)\right)\right)=S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{t}\right).

Let

. We have

\displaystyle\pi_{t}\left(\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)\right)\subset\left\{\mathbb{E}_{con}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(h\right):h\in S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}\left(\Psi_{T}\right)\right\}

(40)

in

Since the set on the right-hand side of (

, then

\displaystyle\overline{dec}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\pi_{t}\left(\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle cl_{L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)}\Big(\left\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}_{con}\left(h\right):h\in S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}\left(\Psi_{T}\right)\right\}\Big)

\displaystyle S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{T}\right)\right)=S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Psi_{t}\right)

\displaystyle cl_{L^{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E\right)}\Big(dec_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left\{\pi_{t}(\psi):\psi\in\mathscr{M}^{p}_{sel}\left(\Psi\right)\right\}\Big)

Lemma 5.3

Let

First, the non-emptiness of the subset

\displaystyle\left\|\psi^{m}-\psi^{n}\right\|^{p}_{\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(E)}

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{T\geq t\geq 0}\left\|x^{m}-x^{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(g^{m}_{u}-g^{n}_{u}\right)dW_{u}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p\;\;}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{m}-x^{n}\right\|_{E}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{T}\left(g^{m}_{u}-g^{n}_{u}\right)dW_{u}\right\|_{E}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|x^{m}-x^{n}\right\|_{E}^{p}+\left\|g^{m}-g^{n}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}\left(\Omega;\gamma(0,T;E)\right)},

for any natural numbers

Using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequalities, we have, for every natural number

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{T\geq t\geq 0}\left\|x+\int_{0}^{t}g_{u}dW_{u}-\psi_{t}\right\|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\psi}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\gamma(0,T;E))}

\displaystyle\left\|\psi-x^{\psi}\right\|_{\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(E)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{\psi}\right\|_{E}^{p}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathscr{M}^{c,p}(E)}^{p}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{\psi}\right\|_{E}^{p}+\left\|\upsilon\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

5.3

We will explicitly use the set-valued conditional expectation. In such cases, the expression for its subtrajectory integrals must be specified due to the nature of the range of the set-valued functions involved.

Let
\Phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathscr{K}_{cwcmpt}(E)
be an
\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable

-integrably bounded set-valued function.

Let

. Let
(h^{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence contained in

such that
\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(h^{n})\right)_{n\geq 1}
converging in

. Since
(h^{n})_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in the reflexive space

, hence it possesses a weak convergent subsequence
(h^{\varphi(n)})_{n\geq 1}
to

in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{T};E)
. It follows by contractiveness of the conditional expectation that
\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(h^{\varphi(n)})\right)_{n\geq 1}
is weakly convergent to
\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(h)
in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t};E)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\forall t\in[0,T],\quad S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Phi\right)\right)=\left\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(h\right):h\in S^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}\left(\Phi\right)\right\}.

(45)

The following lemma leads us to justify the measurability of the Hukuhara difference of two set-valued functions and derive additional results. Prior to this, we recommend stating that the Hukuhara difference of two set-valued functions into
\mathscr{K}_{cwcmpt}(E)
exists if, for every element in their common domain, the Hukuhara difference of their images exists.

Lemma 5.7

Let
\Phi^{(1)},\Phi^{(2)}:\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{T}\right)\longrightarrow\mathscr{K}_{cwcmpt}(E)

If
\Phi^{(1)}\circleddash\Phi^{(2)}

\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable. Furthermore, if in addition
\Phi^{(1)}
and
\Phi^{(2)}
are

\Phi^{(1)}\circleddash\Phi^{(2)}
is

-integrably bounded, and we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Phi^{(1)}\circleddash\Phi^{(2)}\right)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Phi^{(1)}\right)\circleddash\mathbb{E}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\left(\Phi^{(2)}\right)\;a.s.\;\omega\in\Omega,\;t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Let
(e^{*}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence from
E^{*}\setminus\left\{0_{E^{*}}\right\}
dense in
E^{*}
. Let
(\phi_{m}^{(1)})_{m\geq 1}
(resp.
(\phi_{m}^{(2)})_{m\geq 1}
) be a sequence of measurable functions representing
\Phi^{(1)}
(resp.
\Phi^{(2)}
) and let’s put for every
n
and every
\omega\in\Omega
:

Conclusion

In this paper, we establish a new tool that links multivalued analysis and its applications to stochastic integration in UMD spaces. We present several results and properties that, to the best of our knowledge, have not been previously explored in this context.

The revised set-valued stochastic integral enabled us to derive a martingale representation, which contributes to the solution of the SVBSDE (
48
). However, we emphasize that solving this equation with
\tilde{H}(\cdot,Y,{Z})
instead of
H
presents significant challenges.

On the other hand, the subset
\Lambda_{p,H}^{0,T}
has played a crucial role in defining the new concept "
\gamma
-set-valued stochastic integral of a set-valued function
H
". This subset can provide the framework for defining the set-valued stochastic integral of
H
with respect to a standard Brownian motion, extending the study in [
[
22
]
, Section 3], and enabling the analysis of certain types of stochastic inclusions.

Some remarks on Campanato’s Theorem and the Anisotropic Bessel Spaces
by, & \&, R. Leitão

Abstract
In this paper, we establish an anisotropic version of Campanato’s Theorem and show that the anisotropic Bessel spaces are continuously embedded in the function spaces C θ ∥ ⋅ ∥ C^{\theta}_{\|\cdot\|} -continuous. As an application of this embedding, we build fundamental solutions for a class of anisotropic fractional laplacians Δ β , α \Delta^{\beta,\alpha} . Key words : degenerate elliptic equations; fractional Laplacian; anisotropy. AMS Subject Classification MSC 2010 : 26A33; 35J70; 47G20.

Given

W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\displaystyle\dfrac{|u(z)-u(h)|^{q}}{\|z-h\|_{\beta}^{\frac{c_{\beta}}{q}+\alpha}}\in L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right\}

and

(1.2)

H_{r_{\beta}}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+r^{2}_{\beta}(z)\right)^{\alpha/2}\mathfrak{F}(u)\right]\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

where
\mathfrak{F}(f)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
,
c_{\beta}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2}{b_{i}}
is the anisotropic dimension,

\|x-y\|_{\beta}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{b_{i}}\right)^{1/2}

is the anisotropic quasi-distance, see

is the anisotropic distance that satisfies the Calderón-Besov-Lizorkin condition, namely,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{x_{i}^{2}}{r_{\beta}^{\frac{4}{b^{\ast}_{i}}}}=1,\quad\beta^{\ast}=\dfrac{c_{\beta}}{n}\beta,\quad\text{and}\quad c_{\beta^{\ast}}=n,

and the anisotropic Bessel space

are endowed respectively with the natural norms

\|u\|_{W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(z)|^{q}dz+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|u(z)-u(h)|^{q}}{\|z-h\|_{\beta}^{c_{\beta}+\alpha q}}dz\,dh\right)^{1/q}

and

(1.5)

\|u\|_{H_{r_{\beta}}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\|\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+r^{2}_{\beta}(z)\right)^{\alpha/2}\mathfrak{F}(u)\right]\right\|_{q}.

\displaystyle\theta=\alpha-\dfrac{c_{\beta}}{q}.

In particular, if

\displaystyle\left|u_{x_{0},\sigma}-u_{x_{0},\rho}\right|\leq C\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}\left(\dfrac{\sigma}{\rho}\right)^{\alpha}\rho^{\theta},\quad\text{for}\ 0<r\leq\rho<\sigma\leq R,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}u_{x_{0},r}=u(x_{0})\quad\text{for almost every}\ x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

. The study of the existence of fundamental solutions has an extensive literature:

2.

Non-Local

16

\displaystyle\Delta^{\beta_{0},\alpha}u_{\gamma}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\|x\|_{\beta_{0}}^{\gamma+2\alpha}},\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\quad\beta_{0}=(2,\dots,2),

for

, with

1.

(Barrier function) The first step towards the existence of solutions of (
1.21

. In the anisotropic case, this is not immediate. Given

the open ball of radius

. In

is the natural barrier for

is not convex on

, in fact, it is not even

and by refining the construction of barriers made in

. Our barrier is given by

\displaystyle u_{\gamma}=\|x\|_{\mu\beta}^{-\gamma/\mu},

for some

, the geometry determined by

requires an adequate convexity of

(depending on

outside of

does not degenerate

2
Preliminaries:

In this section we gather the results that we will systematically use along the work. We divide it into two parts. In the first part we address some elementary facts on the topology generated by anisotropic balls and prove the anisotropic versions of the Campanato theory required to access
C^{\gamma}_{\|\cdot\|_{\beta}}
-regularity for functions in

is continuously embedding in

for

2.1
Anisotropic topology and elements of the Campanato theory

Given
r>0
,
\beta=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will consider the anisotropic sets

\displaystyle\Theta^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|y-x\|_{\beta}<r\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right):=\left\{\left(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left(y_{i}-x_{i}\right)^{2}}{r^{\frac{4}{b_{i}}}}<1\right\},

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}>0\right\}
.
In the case
x=0,
we will denote by

\Theta^{\beta}_{r}\left(0\right)=\Theta^{\beta}_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{\beta}_{r}\left(0\right)=E^{\beta}_{r}.

Moreover, in order not to overload the notation, we will drop the subscript
\beta
when no confusion can arise. For example,
\Theta_{r}\left(x\right)=\Theta^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right),

\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{\beta},
and

. We will also use the classical average notation

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right)}u(z)\,dz=\dfrac{1}{|E^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right)|}\displaystyle\int_{E^{\beta}_{r}\left(x\right)}u(z)\,dz,

for all measurable function
u
. We begin with some basic results on the topology generated by anisotropic balls and the equivalence of anisotropic norms on anisotropic spheres. We also show how anisotropic Campanato’s semi-norm and the norm of the anisotropic Gagliardo space are related.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the following assertions hold:

and

(or

.

3.

Let

\beta_{2}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then there exists a constant

that depends on
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
, and

(2.1)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\leq\|x\|_{\beta_{1}}\leq C,\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\partial\Theta^{\beta_{2}}_{1}.

4.

then the function

is continuous in

.

5.

If

then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\beta
,
\alpha
, and

such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{and}\quad\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

For assertions 1 and 2 see Remark 2.9 in
[
8
]
and Remark 2.3 in
[
25
]
. Furthermore, if
x\in\partial\Theta^{\beta_{2}}_{1}
, we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle|x_{i}|\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|x\|_{\beta_{1}}\leq\sqrt{n}.

On the other hand, if
\|x\|_{\beta_{2}}=1
there exists

such that
|x_{i_{0}}|^{d_{i_{0}}}\geq 1/n
. Thus, we find

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\beta_{1}}\geq|x_{i_{0}}|^{b_{i_{0}}/2}\geq\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)^{b_{i_{0}}/2d_{i_{0}}}.

We now choose
C=\max\left\{n,n^{-b_{i_{0}}/2d_{i_{0}}}\right\}
to get (
2.1
). To prove assertion 4 note that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\chi_{A}=\chi_{A\cap B}+\chi_{(A\setminus B)}\quad\text{for all}\ A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

From (
2.5

(2.6)

\displaystyle|u_{x,r}-u_{x_{0},r}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Cr^{c}}\left|\displaystyle\int_{E_{r}(x)}u(z)\,dz-\displaystyle\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}u(z)\,dz\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Cr^{c}}\left|\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))}u(z)\,dz-\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x_{0})\setminus E_{r}(x))}u(z)\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Cr^{c}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))}|u(z)|\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x_{0})\setminus E_{r}(x))}|u(z)|\,dz\right),

where

for all

. By Hölder Inequality, we find

(2.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))}|u(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))}|u(z)|^{q}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Then, combining (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) we get

(2.8)

\displaystyle|u_{x,r}-u_{x_{0},r}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{Cr^{\frac{c}{q}}}\left(\|u\|_{L_{q}(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))}+\|u\|_{L_{q}(E_{r}(x_{0})\setminus E_{r}(x))}\right).

Since
|u|^{q}
is integrable and

\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}|(E_{r}(x)\setminus E_{r}(x_{0}))|=0,

we use (
2.8
) to conclude that
u
is continuous at
x_{0}
. Finally, we estimate

(2.9)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u_{x_{0},r}|^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|u(x)-\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}u(z)\,dz\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left(u(x)-u(z)\right)\,dz\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|\,dz\right)^{q}

and apply Hölder Inequality to obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left|E_{r}(x_{0})\right|^{1-1/q}}{\left|E_{r}(x_{0})\right|}\left(\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|^{q}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and deduce that

(2.11)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u_{x_{0},r}|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|^{q}\,dz.

Integrating (
2.11
) on
E_{r}(x_{0})
we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}|u(x)-u_{x_{0},r}|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\left|E_{r}(x_{0})\right|}\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|^{q}\,dz\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dfrac{(C_{1}r)^{c+\alpha q}}{\left|E_{r}(x_{0})\right|}\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{E_{r}(x_{0})}\dfrac{\left|u(x)-u(z)\right|^{q}}{\|x-z\|^{c+\alpha q}}\,dz\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{2}r^{\alpha q}\|u\|^{q}_{W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where we have used assertion 1 to find

x,y\in E_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Theta_{Cr}.

Furthermore, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we estimate

(2.13)

\displaystyle|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|u_{x,1}|+|u_{x,1}-u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|E_{1}(x)|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C_{5}\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\|u\|_{W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence, assertion 5 is proved.
∎

The next lemma provides the decay of oscillation of the average of
u\in W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in anisotropic balls.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
1<q<+\infty
and
u\in W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
assume that
c<\alpha\leq c+q
and
0<r<R
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\beta
,
\alpha
, and
n
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left|u_{x_{0},R}-u_{x_{0},r}\right|\leq C\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}R^{\theta},\quad\text{for}\ \theta=\alpha-\dfrac{c}{q},

for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Given
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\left[r,R\right]
with
\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}
note that

(2.15)

\displaystyle|u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{1}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}-\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}u(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left(u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}-u(x)\right)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|\,dx.

From Hölder Inequality, it follows that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}-u(x)\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left|E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})\right|^{1-1/q}}{\left|E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})\right|}\left(\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Moreover, taking into account that
E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})\subset E_{\sigma_{2}}(x_{0})
, we estimate

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\sigma^{\alpha q}_{2}}{\left|E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})\right|\sigma^{\alpha q}_{2}}\int_{E_{\sigma_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\sigma^{c+\alpha q}_{2}}{C\sigma^{c}_{1}}\left(\sigma^{-\alpha q}_{2}\int_{E_{\sigma_{2}}(x_{0})}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}\right|^{q}\,dx\right).

Combining (
2.15
), (
2.16
), and (
2.17
), we deduce that

(2.18)

\displaystyle|u_{x_{0},\sigma_{2}}-u_{x_{0},\sigma_{1}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{c}{q}}_{1}\sigma^{\alpha}_{2}\right)\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}=C^{-1}\left[\sigma^{\theta}_{1}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}.

Putting
\sigma_{1}=2^{-k}R
and
\sigma_{2}=2^{-k+1}R
in (

) we get

(2.19)

\displaystyle|u_{x_{0},R}-u_{x_{0},2^{-k}R}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R^{\theta}\left(2^{\alpha}C^{-1}\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}\right)\left(\sum_{m=1}^{k}2^{-m\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}R^{\theta},

where
C_{2}=\dfrac{C^{-1}}{2^{\theta}-1}
. Choose
k
such that
2^{-k}R\leq r<2^{-k+1}R
. Then, from (
2.19
) we obtain

(2.20)

\displaystyle|u_{x_{0},r}-u_{x_{0},2^{-k}R}|\leq C_{2}\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}r^{\theta}\leq C_{2}\left[u\right]_{q,\alpha q}R^{\theta}.

We now establish the non-trivial connection between spaces
H_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
\beta=\left(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and

(2.21)

\displaystyle L^{\beta}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H_{r_{\beta}}^{\frac{2n}{c_{\beta}},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad H_{r_{\beta}}^{\frac{n}{c_{\beta}}\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

for all
0<\alpha<2/b_{\max}
. In particular, if
\beta
is such that
c_{\beta}=n
and
2\leq q<+\infty
, we get

H_{r_{\beta}}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Let
\beta^{\ast}=\frac{c_{\beta}}{n}\beta
. By Theorem 1 in
[
28
]
, we find

(2.22)

\displaystyle L^{\beta}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H_{r_{\beta}}^{\alpha^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\alpha^{\ast}
and
b^{\ast}_{i}
satisfy

(2.23)

\displaystyle\alpha^{\ast}=\dfrac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{b_{i}}}=\dfrac{2n}{c_{\beta}}\quad\text{and}\quad b^{\ast}_{i}=\dfrac{2b_{i}}{\alpha^{\ast}}=\dfrac{c_{\beta}b_{i}}{n}.

For the second part, if
\beta(\alpha)=(\alpha b_{1}/2,\dots,\alpha b_{n}/2)
we get

(2.24)

\displaystyle c_{\beta(\alpha)}=\dfrac{2}{\alpha}c_{\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{c_{\beta(\alpha)}}{n}\beta(\alpha)=\frac{c_{\beta}}{n}\beta=\beta^{\ast}.

Therefore, from (
2.21
), we obtain

(2.25)

\displaystyle L^{\beta(\alpha)}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H_{r_{\beta}}^{\frac{n\alpha}{c_{\beta}},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

On the other hand, by Proposition 2.1 in
[
26
]
we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=B^{\mu(\alpha,\beta)}_{q},

where
\mu=(\mu_{1}(\alpha,b_{1}),\dots,\mu_{n}(\alpha,b_{n}))
is given by
\mu_{i}(\alpha,b_{i})=\frac{\left[(1-\nu_{i})q-1\right]}{q}
with

(2.27)

\displaystyle\nu_{i}-1=\dfrac{b_{i}}{2}\left[(\nu-1)+\dfrac{1}{q}\left(1-\frac{2}{b_{i}}\right)\right]\quad\text{and}\quad(1-\nu)q=q\alpha+1.

Moreover, from (
2.27
) it follows that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\left(1-\nu_{i}\right)q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{i}}{2}\left[(1-\nu)q-\left(1-\frac{2}{b_{i}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{i}}{2}\left[\alpha q+1-\left(1-\frac{2}{b_{i}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\dfrac{\alpha b_{i}}{2}+1.

Hence, combining (
2.27
) and (
2.28
) we find

(2.29)

\displaystyle W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=B^{\beta(\alpha)}_{q}.

Finally, from (
2.29
) and Theorem 9 in
[
28
]
, we establish the continuous embedding

(2.30)

\displaystyle H_{r_{\beta}}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow W_{\beta}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

∎

2.2
Fundamental solution: ingredients

2.2.1
Viscosity solutions and
L^{p}
-estimates

In this subsection we collect all the results and concepts from the viscosity solutions theory that we will use to build our anisotropic fundamental solutions.
L^{p}
-estimates are also considered. For more details we refer the reader to
[
8
,
30
,
26
,
12
]
and references therein.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\varphi
is said to be
C^{1,1}
at the point
x
, and we write
\varphi\in C^{1,1}\left(x\right)
, if there is a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and numbers
M,\eta_{0}>0
such that

|\varphi\left(x+y\right)-\varphi\left(x\right)-v\cdot y|\leq M|y|^{2},

for
|x|<\eta_{0}
. We say that a function
\varphi
is
C^{1,1}
in a set
\Omega
, and we denote
\varphi\in C^{1,1}\left(\Omega\right)
, if the previous holds at every
point, with a uniform constant
M
.

Note that for
u\in C^{1,1}\left(x\right)\cap L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},w\right)
the value of
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u(x)
is well defined, where the anisotropic weight
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

(2.31)

\displaystyle w(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+\|x\|^{c+2\alpha}}.

Next we introduce the notion of viscosity supersolution (and subsolution)
u
in a domain
\Omega
, with
C^{2}
test functions that touch
u
from above or from below.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f
be a bounded and continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, upper (lower) semicontinuous in
\overline{\Omega}
, is said to be a subpersolution (subsolution) to equation
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u=f
, and we write
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u\geq f
(
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u\leq f
), if whenever the following happen:

1.

x_{0}\in\Omega
is any point in
\Omega
;

2.

B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset\Omega
, for some
r>0
;

3.

\varphi\in C^{2}\left(\overline{B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)}\right)
;

4.

\varphi\left(x_{0}\right)=u\left(x_{0}\right)
;

5.

\varphi\left(y\right)>u\left(y\right)
(
\varphi\left(y\right)<u\left(y\right)
) for every
y\in B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)\setminus\left\{x_{0}\right\}
;

then, if we let

v:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\varphi,&\text{ in }&B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)\\
u&\text{ in }&\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right),\end{array}\right.

we have
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}v\left(x_{0}\right)\geq f\left(x_{0}\right)
(
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}v\left(x_{0}\right)\leq f\left(x_{0}\right)
).

Since we use a strategy inspired by Perron’s method to reach anisotropic fundamental solutions for
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}
, it is natural that tools as stability property, comparison principle, Harnack inequality, and maximum principle be required. More precisely, we make use of the following results:

Lemma 2.6

(Stability property)
.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence of continuous functions in an open set
\Omega
and uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that:

1.

\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u=f_{k}
in
\Omega
.

2.

u_{k}\rightarrow u
locally uniformly in
\Omega
and
u_{k}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.

f_{k}\rightarrow f
locally uniformly in
\Omega
.

Then
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u=f
in
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.7

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set and
u
and
v
be two bounded functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

1.

u
is upper-semicontinuous and
v
is lower-semicontinuous in
\overline{\Omega}
;

2.

u\leq v
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

3.

\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u\geq f
and
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}v\leq f
in the viscosity sense in
\Omega
for a continuous function
f
.

Then
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.8

(Harnack Inequality)
.

Assume that
u
is a non-negative viscosity solution of
\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u=0
in
\Theta_{2}
. Suppose that
\alpha_{0}<\alpha<\frac{2}{b_{\max}}
, for some
\alpha_{0}>0
. Then

u\leq Cu\left(0\right)\quad in\ \ \Theta_{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C=C(\beta,n,\alpha)>0
is a constant.

Lemma 2.9

(Maximum principle)
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Assume that
u\in C^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\cap L^{1}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|x\|\rightarrow 0}u(x)\leq 0.

If
u
satisfies

(2.32)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\beta,\alpha}u\leq 0\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\},

then
u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

Let
1\leq q<+\infty
and
0<\theta<1
. Since

(2.33)

\displaystyle\lim_{\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty}u(x)=0,

for every
u\in C^{\theta}_{\|\cdot\|}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, see Corollary
3.1
in Section 3 for more details, a direct consequence of Lemma
2.9
is a maximum principle for solutions to the anisotropic non-local Schrödinger equation.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\mu>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Assume that

Lemma 2.15

(Scaling properties)
.

Given

1.

(2.47)

\displaystyle T^{-1}_{\beta,r}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\subset\partial\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{1}.

It is immediate that

T^{-1}_{\beta,r}=T_{\beta,r^{-1}}.

Furthermore, if
x\neq 0

(2.49)

\displaystyle\|T^{-1}_{\beta,r}\|^{2}_{\mu\beta}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|r^{-2/b_{i}}x_{i}\right|^{\mu b_{i}}=\dfrac{\|x\|^{2}_{\mu\beta}}{r^{2\mu}}.

2.47

(2.50)

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{r}}\dfrac{|y|^{2}}{\|y\|^{c_{\beta}+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{1}}\dfrac{|T_{\mu\beta,t}y|^{2}}{\|T_{\mu\beta,t}y\|_{\beta}^{c_{\beta}+2\alpha}}t^{\frac{1}{\mu}c_{\beta}-1}dy\,dt

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{1}}t^{-\frac{2\alpha}{\mu}-1}|T_{\mu\beta,t}y|^{2}dy\,dt,

\displaystyle|T_{\mu\beta,t}y|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|t^{4/\mu b_{i}}y_{i}^{2}|\leq t^{\frac{4}{\mu b_{\max}}}|y|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\ (t,y)\in\left[0,1\right]\times\partial\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{1},

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{r}}\dfrac{|y|^{2}}{\|y\|^{c_{\beta}+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\int_{0}^{r}t^{\frac{2}{\mu}\left(\frac{2}{b_{\max}}-\alpha\right)-1}dt\right)\left[\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\int_{\partial\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{1}}y^{2}_{i}\,dy\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2},

where
C_{2}>0
is a constant that depends on
r
,
\mu
,
\beta
,
\alpha
, and
n
. Analogously, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Theta^{\mu\beta}_{r}}\dfrac{1}{\|y\|^{c_{\beta}+2\alpha}}dy

for some constant

\mu

\beta

\alpha
, and

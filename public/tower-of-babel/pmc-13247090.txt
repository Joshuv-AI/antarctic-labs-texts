Theoretical aspects of nanofluid peristaltic transport in tapered wavy structure
Authors: Anum Tanveer, Sumaira Imtiaz, Salman Saleem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study presents a theoretical investigation of the peristaltic transport of a third-grade nanofluid in a two-dimensional tapered wavy channel under the combined influence of electroosmotic effects and slip boundary conditions. The model incorporates variable viscosity and variable thermal conductivity to represent more realistic thermophysical behavior of the fluid. In addition, the influence of nanoparticle shape on the thermal performance of the nanofluid is examined by considering three different geometries, namely brick, spherical, and platelet-shaped nanoparticles. The Hamilton–Crosser model is employed to account for the enhancement of effective thermal conductivity due to nanoparticle morphology. The governing equations for momentum, energy, and concentration transport are formulated and transformed into a dimensionless form using appropriate similarity transformations. By applying the long-wavelength and low Reynolds number approximations, the resulting nonlinear coupled ordinary differential equations are simplified and solved numerically using the shooting method. The effects of key physical parameters, including electroosmotic parameters, slip parameters, nanoparticle shape factors, and thermophysical properties, on velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles are analyzed through graphical illustrations. The results indicate that nanoparticle shape plays a significant role in heat transfer enhancement, with platelet-shaped nanoparticles producing stronger thermal and velocity responses compared with spherical and brick-shaped particles. Moreover, electroosmotic forces and slip conditions significantly influence the flow behavior within the channel. The findings provide useful insight into nanofluid transport mechanisms in bio-inspired microfluidic systems and may have potential applications in biomedical engineering, drug delivery technologies, and advanced microchannel heat transfer devices.

Introduction

Peristalsis is a fundamental transport mechanism in which a fluid-filled conduit or flexible tube undergoes rhythmic contractions and expansions, generating a traveling wave that drives the fluid along the channel. This mechanism plays a crucial role in various biological systems, enabling efficient movement of fluids and substances through organs such as the gastrointestinal tract, stomach, and esophagus. In addition to its physiological importance, peristaltic motion has numerous engineering applications, including in chemical reactors, pharmaceutical processing, and food manufacturing, where precise control of fluid flow is essential. The dynamics of peristaltic flows can be further influenced by external forces such as magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects, which are particularly significant in electrically conductive fluids like blood. The pioneering study on peristaltic transport of Newtonian fluids was conducted by Latham [1], while Raju and Devanathan [2] extended the analysis to non-Newtonian fluids. Since then, extensive investigations have examined peristaltic flows in a wide range of fluid types and channel geometries, including symmetric, asymmetric, and wavy conduits, providing detailed insights into flow patterns, pressure distribution, and transport efficiency [3–9]. These studies have laid a solid foundation for modeling peristaltic transport in both biological and industrial systems.

Biological fluids and many complex suspensions often exhibit non-Newtonian behavior due to the presence of suspended particles and cells, leading to nonlinear stress–strain relationships that cannot be accurately described by Newtonian models. To capture such complex rheological characteristics, higher-order non-Newtonian models, such as the third-grade fluid model, are widely employed in fluid mechanics and biomedical transport studies. Third-grade fluids include additional nonlinear terms in the stress–strain relation, making them suitable for representing fluids with normal stress differences and elastic effects. Hina et al. [10] examined the peristaltic transport of a third-grade fluid in a curved channel, highlighting the impact of curvature on flow behavior. Awais et al. [11] investigated thermophoresis and heat generation/absorption in third-grade nanofluid flow, emphasizing their effects on velocity and temperature distributions. Ali et al. [12] analyzed third-grade fluid flow over a stretching cylinder, accounting for heat sources and sinks on the thermal and velocity fields. More recent studies have extended third-grade fluid modeling to nanofluid and heat transfer problems. Choudhari et al. [13] studied peristaltic transport in an inclined channel, considering wall slip and variable fluid properties. Hussein [14] investigated MHD peristaltic transport of a third-grade nanofluid through an asymmetric channel, including the effects of rheological properties, an inclined magnetic field, and heat and mass transfer, providing insights into practical applications in thermal and biomedical systems.

Electroosmosis is an important electrokinetic phenomenon in which fluid motion is induced by an applied electric field acting on the charged ions within an electrolyte solution. The interaction between the electric field and the electric double layer near the channel walls generates a Coulomb force that drives the fluid through the conduit. By adjusting the applied voltage, the electroosmotic flow rate can be effectively controlled, making it a useful mechanism in many microfluidic and biomedical applications. This technique has been widely utilized in processes such as fluid filtration, biochemical analysis, and the development of lab-on-a-chip devices. Although peristaltic transport has been extensively investigated in the literature, many early studies were conducted without considering the influence of electric fields. The integration of electroosmotic effects with peristaltic motion can produce a continuous and controllable flow, which is particularly advantageous in modern microfluidic systems that require compact, efficient, and cost-effective designs. Such systems are commonly employed in applications including DNA analysis, drug delivery, and biological sample manipulation. Several researchers have examined the combined effects of electroosmosis and peristaltic transport under different physical conditions. For instance, Tanveer et al. [15] investigated electroosmotic effects in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) peristaltic transport of non-Newtonian fluids. Tanveer et al. [16] analyzed electroosmotic flow of shear-thinning blood with wall flexibility and Hall current effects. Noreen et al. [17] studied the interaction between electroosmosis and peristaltic motion in a symmetric channel. Furthermore, Sk and Chaube [18] explored electroosmosis-driven peristaltic rheological flow in a non-uniform channel with slip conditions, while Butt et al. [19] examined electroosmosis-modulated peristaltic transport in a non-uniform conduit considering slip effects. In addition, Akbar et al. [20] applied intelligent computational techniques to analyze electroosmosis-modulated peristaltic pumping of a blood-based nanofluid, highlighting the growing interest in combining electrokinetic mechanisms with advanced fluid models.

A tapered wavy channel refers to a flow passage whose walls exhibit a periodic wave-like pattern while the channel width gradually varies along its length. Such geometrical configurations are widely employed in engineering and biomedical applications because they significantly influence fluid motion, enhance mixing, and improve heat and mass transfer performance. These channels are commonly utilized in devices such as heat exchangers, microfluidic systems, and flow control mechanisms. The tapering of the channel may follow different profiles, including linear, exponential, or other prescribed variations, while the amplitude and wavelength of the wall waves can be adjusted to meet specific design requirements. Due to these characteristics, tapered wavy channels have attracted considerable research interest in the study of peristaltic transport. Abbas and Rafiq [21] investigated heat and mass transfer characteristics during the peristaltic transport of hyperbolic tangent fluid in a tapered channel, demonstrating the influence of channel geometry on temperature and concentration distributions. Naduvinamani et al. [22] examined the peristaltic flow of hyperbolic tangent fluid in a tapered asymmetric channel and discussed the impact of channel asymmetry on flow behavior. Similarly, Rafiq et al. [23] analyzed the peristaltic transport of couple stress nanofluid in a tapered channel, emphasizing the role of non-Newtonian effects on the flow characteristics. Furthermore, Alsemiry et al. [24] studied the enhancement of flow performance and entropy generation in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Buongiorno nanofluid within a complex tapered peristaltic channel under electroosmotic forces. Ravikumar [25] also investigated heat and mass transfer phenomena in an electrically conducting viscoelastic fluid influenced by buoyancy forces and reactive solute transport in a tapered oblique channel during peristaltic motion.

In various cancer treatment techniques, nanoparticles are employed to absorb laser energy of specific wavelengths, generating localized heating in affected areas. The incorporation of nanoparticles into base fluids enhances the thermal response, achieving medically significant temperature levels. Khan et al. [26] performed a finite element analysis of hybrid nanofluid flow and heat transfer in a split lid-driven square cavity containing a Y-shaped obstacle, emphasizing the effects of obstacle geometry on fluid motion and thermal performance. Salahuddin et al. [27] examined the electrokinetically driven peristaltic transport of nanofluids through a curved microchannel, highlighting the impact of electric forces on flow behavior and heat transfer characteristics. Akbar et al. [28] studied the peristaltic flow of nanofluids in thermal engineering applications, analyzing how nanoparticle shape and variable fluid properties influence effective thermal conductivity and overall heat transfer. Jun Song et al. [29] investigated the thermal performance of ferromagnetic nanofluid flowing over a dimpled cavity, focusing on enhanced heat transfer and flow behavior. Dolui et al. [30] explored the role of nanoparticle aggregation and electroosmotic forces in the peristaltic transport of third-grade nanofluids through a porous tube, emphasizing their effects on propulsion and flow characteristics. Ajmal et al. [31] analyzed the peristaltic transport of Williamson fluids in a non-uniform wavy ciliated channel under an electroosmotic magnetic field, investigating the enhancement due to embedded nanoparticles. Additional studies [32–40] have examined various nanoparticle shapes and configurations to further optimize heat transfer performance in nanofluid systems.

This study introduces a novel theoretical framework for analyzing the peristaltic transport of third-grade nanofluids in a tapered wavy microchannel, incorporating the combined effects of nanoparticle shape, electroosmotic forces, and wall slip—factors that have not been simultaneously addressed in the existing literature. By integrating third-grade fluid mechanics with magnetohydrodynamics, heat transfer, and mass transport, this research provides deeper insights into the velocity, temperature, and concentration distributions under realistic microfluidic conditions. The model captures the influence of viscoelastic effects, non-zero normal stress differences, and nonlinear stress contributions on flow behavior, while also considering the impact of tapered geometry, nanoparticle volume fraction, and thermal conductivity on energy and mass transport. Graphical representations of velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles further illustrate how nanoparticle shapes, electroosmotic parameters, and chemical reactions modify the overall fluid dynamics, enabling a detailed understanding of coupled transport mechanisms.

Third-grade fluid model

To describe the non-Newtonian characteristics of the working nanofluid, a third-grade fluid model is employed. Third-grade fluids belong to the class of differential type non-Newtonian fluids and are capable of representing nonlinear viscosity, normal stress differences, and shear-dependent rheological behavior. Such models are widely used to describe polymer solutions, lubricants, and certain biological fluids where higher-order elastic effects become important.

In peristaltic transport and microfluidic systems, the presence of nanoparticles and electroosmotic forces may produce strong nonlinear stress responses. Therefore, the third-grade fluid model provides a more realistic description of the flow dynamics in tapered wavy channels.

Numerical method

The transformed dimensionless governing equations for velocity, temperature, and concentration fields are highly nonlinear and coupled, making analytical solutions impractical. Therefore, a numerical approach was adopted to obtain precise solutions under the specified boundary conditions. The equations were solved using the shooting method based built-in numerical scheme NDSolve implemented through Mathematica. This method converts the boundary value problem into an equivalent initial value problem, allowing efficient numerical integration. NDSolve in Mathematica is a powerful, adaptive numerical differential equation solver, with default accuracy typically around 8 to 10 decimal digits when using standard Machine Precision. It achieves this by automatically adjusting step sizes to meet default tolerances, which are generally half the precision of the working numerical precision. The spatial discretization employed an adaptive step-size Runge–Kutta scheme of second-order accuracy, ensuring both numerical stability and precision. This numerical procedure provides reliable solutions for the coupled effects of third-grade fluid behavior, nanoparticle shapes, electroosmotic forces, and tapered wavy channel geometry on flow, heat, and mass transport in nanofluid peristaltic systems.

Results and discussion

The main findings of the present theoretical investigation of nanofluid flow induced by peristaltic motion in a tapered wavy channel are presented and discussed in this section. The governing nonlinear equations, formulated under appropriate physical assumptions and boundary conditions, are solved to analyze the influence of several physical parameters on the flow characteristics. The third-grade fluid model exhibits non-zero normal stress differences, which represent the viscoelastic behavior of non-Newtonian fluids. These normal stresses introduce additional elastic effects in the flow and modify the velocity distribution within the channel. As the third-grade fluid parameters increase, the elastic nature of the fluid becomes more significant, altering the shear structure of the flow and influencing the momentum transport. Moreover, the nonlinear stress contributions enhance viscous dissipation, which affects the temperature field and consequently modifies the coupled heat and mass transfer processes, leading to variations in the nanoparticle concentration distribution. Conclusions

This study presents a theoretical analysis of peristaltic transport of nanofluids in a tapered wavy channel, considering electroosmotic effects and the influence of nanoparticle shapes, including brick, spherical, and platelet forms. The governing equations were solved numerically to examine how nanoparticle geometry, volume fraction, and electrokinetic parameters affect flow, heat, and mass transfer.

The results show that nanoparticle shape plays a crucial role in the flow behavior: Platelet-shaped nanoparticles increase viscous resistance near the channel walls, locally reducing velocity in these regions, while simultaneously enhancing flow at the channel center due to improved thermal conductivity and electroosmotic forces, while brick and spherical nanoparticles enhance thermal conductivity, resulting in higher temperature distributions. Heat transfer is further amplified by increases in nanoparticle volume fraction, Brinkman number, and electrokinetic effects, whereas thermal relaxation delays heat propagation. Concentration profiles are strongly influenced by mass transport mechanisms, with Schmidt and Soret numbers controlling particle distribution and chemical reactions reducing nanoparticle content.

These findings provide useful insights for the design and optimization of nanofluid-based biomedical and microfluidic systems. Future work could focus on experimental validation, optimizing nanoparticle shape and electroosmotic conditions, and extending the model to hybrid nanofluids, complex geometries, and variable magnetic fields to improve practical applications in drug delivery, lab-on-chip devices, and thermal management systems.

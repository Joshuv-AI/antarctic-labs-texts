X^{s}-X^{e(s)}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}(X^{a_{i}}-X^{e(a_{i})})
for some
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\in R[S].

Proof.

Say
k=2
. By Lemma
3.6
, we have
X^{s}-X^{e(s)}=X^{a_{1}+a_{2}}-X^{e(a_{1}+a_{2})}=X^{a_{1}}X^{a_{2}}-X^{e(a_{1})}X^{e(a_{2})}=\beta_{1}(X^{a_{1}}-X^{e(a_{1})})+\beta_{2}(X^{a_{2}}-X^{e(a_{2})})
where
\beta_{1}=X^{a_{2}}\in R[S]
and
\beta_{2}=X^{e(a_{1})}\in R[S]
, done. Then the lemma follows readily by the induction on
k
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
S
be a commutative semigroup, and let
p
be a prime number. Let
R
be commutative unitary ring of characteristic
p
. For any periodic element
b
of
S
such that the period
\mathcal{P}(b)
is a
p
-power, we have
(X^{b}-X^{e(b)})^{\rho(b)}=0\in R[S]
.

Proof.

Say
\mathcal{P}(b)=p^{t}
for some
t\geq 0
. By (
2
), we see
\mathcal{P}(b)\mid\rho(b)
. Then we set
\rho(b)=np^{t}
where
n\geq 1
. Denote
e=e(b)
.
By Lemma
3.7
, we see that

k\mathcal{P}(b)b+e=k\mathcal{P}(b)b+\rho(b)b=e\mbox{ for every }k>0.

(28)

Since
R
is commutative and of characteristic
p
, it follows from (
28
) that
(X^{b}-X^{e})^{\rho(b)}=[(X^{b}-X^{e})^{p^{t}}]^{n}=[(X^{b})^{p^{t}}-(X^{e})^{p^{t}}]^{n}=[X^{p^{t}b}-X^{p^{t}e}]^{n}=[X^{p^{t}b}-X^{e}]^{n}=(X^{p^{t}b})^{n}+(-1)^{n}(X^{e})^{n}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}(X^{p^{t}b})^{i}\cdot(X^{e})^{n-i}=X^{np^{t}b}+(-1)^{n}X^{ne}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}X^{ip^{t}b+(n-i)e}=X^{e}+(-1)^{n}X^{e}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}X^{ip^{t}b+e}=X^{e}+(-1)^{n}X^{e}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{n\choose i}(-1)^{n-i}X^{e}=(X^{e}-X^{e})^{n}=0\in R[S]
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S
be a commutative periodic semigroup. Let
R
be commutative unitary ring. Let
T=s_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s_{m}
be a sequence over
S
.
If
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(X^{s_{i}}-X^{e(s_{i})})=0\in R[S]
then
{\rm St}(e)\cap\sum(T)\neq\emptyset
, where
e=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}e(s_{i})
.

Proof.

Denote
e_{i}=e(s_{i})
for
i\in[1,m]
. By expanding out
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(X^{s_{i}}-X^{e(s_{i})})
, we conclude that (i)
\sum\limits_{j\in[1,m]}s_{j}=e
, or (ii) there exists a nonempty subset
J\subsetneq[1,m]
such that
\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]\setminus J}e_{i}=\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]}e_{i}=e
. For case (i), we have
\sum\limits_{j\in[1,m]}s_{j}=e\in{\rm St}(e)
. Hence, we assume (ii) holds.
Then
\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}+e=\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]}e_{i}=\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}+(\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]\setminus J}e_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]}e_{i})=(\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]\setminus J}e_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in[1,m]}e_{i}=e+e=e
, and so
\sum\limits_{j\in J}s_{j}\in{\rm St}(e)\cap\sum(T)
, we are done.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
(i). Let
A
be a generating set of

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}(X^{s_{i}}-X^{e(s_{i})})
can be represented as a sum of the following monomials:

\beta\cdot\prod\limits_{a\in A}(X^{a}-X^{e(a)})^{t_{a}}

(29)

where
\beta\in R[X;S]
,
t_{a}\geq 0
and
\sum\limits_{a\in A}t_{a}=\ell

(ii) This conclusion follows readily from Definition

Concluding remarks

We first remark that if the semigroup
S
meets some conditions, a combinatorial explanation on the connection between
d(S,R)
and the Erdős-Burgess constant
I(S)
can be obtained, which are given in the following Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be a commutative semigroup, and let
R
be a commutative unitary ring.
Suppose that
{\rm St}(e)\subset E(S)
for every
e\in E(S)
. Then
I(S)\leq d(S,R)+1
.

Proof.

Let
T=s_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot s_{\ell}
be a sequence over
S
with
\ell=d(S,R)+1
. By the definition
d(S,R)
, we see that
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}(X^{s_{i}}-X^{e(s_{i})})=0
.
It follows from Lemma
4.4
that
{\rm St}(e)\cap\sum(T)\neq\emptyset
and so
\sum(S)\cap E(S)\neq\emptyset
, done.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
S
be a finite commutative Clifford semigroup, and let
R
be a commutative unitary ring. Let
\mathbb{G}=(G,\gamma)
be the group functor on the universal semilattice
Y(S)
associated with the semigroup
S
. Suppose that the assigned homomorphism
\gamma_{y}^{x}
is injective for every pair
(x,y)
of elements in
Y(S)
such that
y\preceq x
. Then the following conclusions hold:

(i)
I(S)\leq{\rm d}(S,R)+1
;

(ii) Suppose
R
is a field with
{\rm Char}(R)=p
and
\exp(S)
is a
p
-power for some prime
p
. Then
I(S)={\rm d}(S,R)+1
.

Proof.

(i). We first show the following.

Claim C.
For any
e\in E(S)
, we have
{\rm St}(e)\subset E(S)
.

Proof of Claim C
. Suppose that
g\in{\rm St}(e)
, i.e.,
g+e=e.
Let
x,y\in Y(S)
be such that
g\in G_{x}
and
e\in G_{y}
. We see that
y\preceq x
in the semilattice. It follows that

e=g+e=\gamma_{y}^{x}(g)+\gamma_{x}^{x}(e)=\gamma_{y}^{x}(g)+e
, and so
\gamma_{y}^{x}(g)=e
is the identity element in the group
G_{e}
. Since
\gamma_{y}^{x}
is a monomorphism, it follows that
g=e(g)\in E(S)
is the identity element in the group
G_{x}
. This proves Claim C. ∎

Suppose to the contrary that
I(S)-1>{\rm d}(S,R)
. We take an idempotent-sum free sequence
T=g_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot g_{\ell}
of length
\ell=I(S)-1
over
S
. Then

(X^{g_{1}}-a_{1}X^{e_{1}})\cdot\ldots\cdot(X^{g_{\ell}}-a_{\ell}X^{e_{\ell}})=0\in R[S]

for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\in R\setminus\{0\}
. By Proposition
4.5
, we derive that
{\rm St}(e)\cap\sum(T)\neq\emptyset
. Combined with Claim C, we have that
E(S)\cap\sum(T)\neq\emptyset
, which contradicts with
T
being idempotent-sum free. This proves (i).

(ii) By Corollary
3.3
, we have
d(S,R)=d(H,R)
for some subgroup
H
of
S
. Since
H
is a
p
-group, it follows from Theorem C and Theorem
3.3
that
I(H)={\rm D}(H)=d(H,R)+1
, done.
∎

Conjecture 5.3

Let

Suppose that either (i)

Remark 5.4
.

We remark that although the bound
d(S,K)\leq\max\limits_{a\in S}\left\{\epsilon_{a}\ (\psi(a)+1)+d(\Gamma(H_{a}),K)\right\}
is attained in Clifford semigroups, Archimedean semigroup and elementary semigroups (which cover all types of irreducible components in both semilattice decomposition and subdirectly product decomposition of a finite commutative semmigroups), there exists some finite commutative semigroups
S
for which

d(S,K)<\max\limits_{a\in S}\left\{\epsilon_{a}(\psi(a)+1)+d(\Gamma(H_{a}),K)\right\}<\infty

(see Example
5.5
). That is the reason why in Conjecture
5.3
,
the conjectured equality
d(S,K)=\max\limits_{a\in S}\left\{\epsilon_{a}\ {\rm D_{\bar{a}}}(S\diagup\mathcal{H})+d(\Gamma(H_{a}),K)\right\}
can not be replaced by
d(S,K)=\max\limits_{a\in S}\left\{\epsilon_{a}(\psi(a)+1)+d(\Gamma(H_{a}),K)\right\}
.

Example 5.5

Take
t

Let

We define the operation on

\begin{array}[]{llll}a+_{S}b=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}a+_{\langle x_{i}\rangle}b,&\mbox{if \ \ }i=j;\\

for any

and

.
It is easy to check that

is a finite commutative semigroup, with

. Then we have that

x_{1}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}2x_{1}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}\cdots\succ_{\mathcal{H}}nx_{1}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}x_{2}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}2x_{2}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}\cdots\succ_{\mathcal{H}}nx_{2}\ \ \ \succ_{\mathcal{H}}\ \ \ \cdots\succ_{\mathcal{H}}x_{t}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}2x_{t}\succ_{\mathcal{H}}\cdots\succ_{\mathcal{H}}nx_{t}

Since

\max\limits_{a\in S}\left\{\epsilon_{a}(\psi(a)+1)+d(\Gamma(H_{a}),K)\right\}=\max\limits_{a\in S\setminus E(S)}\left\{\epsilon_{a}(\psi(a)+1)\right\}=\psi((n-1)x_{t})+1=tn-1

.

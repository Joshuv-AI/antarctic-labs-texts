An explicit bound for complements on toric varieties
Florin Ambro

Abstract
. We give an effective upper bound for the index of klt complements on toric Fano varieties.

Introduction

We are interested in the following fundamental theorem of Caucher Birkar
[
2
]
:
for a Fano variety
X
of fixed dimension, the global minimal log discrepancy
\operatorname{mld}X
is bounded away
from zero if and only if there exists a klt complement of index bounded above.

The converse
implication is straightforward: if
B\geq 0
,
n(K+B)\sim 0
and
(X,B)
is klt, then
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\frac{1}{n}
.
Since
B
is effective, we obtain
\operatorname{mld}X\geq\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\frac{1}{n}
. The direct implication was
proved by C. Birkar via a very sophisticated argument: assuming
\operatorname{mld}X\geq\epsilon
,
one first constructs an lc complement of bounded index, which is later upgraded to a klt
complement of bounded index
n
, using lower bounds for the
\alpha
-invariant of
-K
and
birational boundedness of log canonical models of small volume. A natural question is
to find effective (possibly sharp) upper bounds for
n
in terms of
\epsilon
.
Finding sharp upper bounds may lead to interesting examples, but also to a simpler, more
direct argument of C. Birkar’s theorem.

Our main result is an effective upper bound in the case of toric Fano varieties.
Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, define recursively
\lambda(1,\epsilon)=\frac{2}{\epsilon}

and
\lambda(n,\epsilon)=\frac{n(n+1)}{\epsilon}+\lambda(n-1,\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{n(n+1)})
for
n\geq 2
.
For fixed
n
,
\lambda(n,\epsilon)
is a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients in
\epsilon^{-1}
,
of degree
2^{n-1}
. In particular,
\lambda(n,t)
is decreasing in
t
. Moreover, the following inequality
holds by induction:

\lambda(n,\epsilon)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\frac{n(n+1)}{\epsilon})^{2^{i}}.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
(X,B)
be a toric weak log Fano variety, with
B
effective invariant.
Suppose
\dim X=d
and
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\epsilon>0
.
Then for every integer
n\geq\lambda(d,\epsilon)
, the following hold:

•

The linear system of
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisors
|-nK-nB|
defines a birational map.
In particular,

\sqrt[d]{\operatorname{vol}(-K-B)}\geq\lambda(d,\epsilon)^{-1}.

•

Let
D\in|-nK-nB|
be a general member. Then
n(K+B+\frac{D}{n})\sim 0
,
\operatorname{mld}(X,B+\frac{D}{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}
.

The proof of Theorem
0.1
is combinatorial, with the following geometric interpretation.
If
\Sigma_{X}
is the complement of the open torus inside
X
, then
K+\Sigma_{X}\sim 0

is a log canonical complement of index one. We construct a rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

induced by a pencil
\Lambda_{1}\subset|-n_{1}K-n_{1}B|
, with
n_{1}
bounded, such that
for a general member
D_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}
,
K+B+\frac{1}{n_{1}}D_{1}\sim_{n_{1}}0

has lc singularities and all its lc centers dominate
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
via
\phi
.
Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the normalization of the graph of
\phi
, let
\mu^{*}(K+B)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}
be
the log pullback. Let
F
be the general fiber of the fibration
\phi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, let

(K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}})|_{F}=K_{F}+B_{F}
. Then
\dim F=\dim X-1
, and the claim lifts by induction
from
F
to
X
. This is true provided
B_{F}
is effective. If not,
the boundedness of
n_{1}
allows to make
B_{F}
effective after replacing it with a convex combination with the toric
lc complement
\Sigma_{F}\in|-K_{F}|
, at the expense of scaling down
\operatorname{mld}(F,B_{F})
by a bounded factor.

Preliminary

Toric weak log Fano varieties

Let
(X^{d},B)
be a proper toric variety
X
endowed with invariant boundary
B

such that
-K-B
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-free and
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\epsilon>0
. Since
X
is proper,
it follows that
-K-B
is big.

Let
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
. Let

M=N^{*}
be the dual lattice. Let
\square_{-K-B}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
be the moment
polytope of
-K-B
, let
U=\square^{*}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the polar dual convex body.
We have
U=\operatorname{Conv}(\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i}}|i)
, where
\Delta(1)=\{e_{i}\}
and

B=\sum_{i}(1-a_{i})V(e_{i})
. Since
-K-B
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-free, we have

\operatorname{mld}(X,B)=\max\{t>0;N\cap\operatorname{int}(tU)=\{0\}\}.

Define
\lambda(X,B)
to be the minimum after all
t>0
such that

M\cap t\cdot(U-U)^{*}
contains a basis of
M
. Equivalently,

\lambda
is the minimum after all
t>0
such that
M
admits a basis

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d}
such that the interval
\varphi_{i}(U)

has length at most
t
, for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

Lemma 1.1
.

The following properties hold:

a)

Let
n\geq\lambda(X,B)
be an integer. Then the linear system of
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisors

|-nK-nB|
defines a birational map, and the general member
D\in|-nK-nB|
satisfies
the following properties:
n(K+B+\frac{D}{n})\sim 0
and
\operatorname{mld}(X,B+\frac{D}{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}
.

b)

If
\dim X
is fixed and
\lambda(X,B)
is bounded above, then
X
belongs to
finitely many isomorphism types.

Proof.

There exists a basis
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d}
of
M
such that
\max_{i}\operatorname{length}\varphi_{i}(U)\leq\lambda
.
In particular,
\varphi_{i}(U)\subset(-\lambda,\lambda)
for all
i
. That is,
\pm\varphi_{i}\in\lambda\square
for all
i
.

Since
n\geq\lambda
,
\pm\varphi_{1},\ldots,\pm\varphi_{d}
belong to
M\cap\square_{-nK-nB}

and their convex hull is a
0
-symmetric lattice convex body containing the origin in its interior. Therefore

|-nK-nB|
defines a birational map. We have
n(K+B+\frac{D}{n})\sim 0
.
For a toric valuation
E_{e}\ (e\in N^{prim})
of
X
, we compute the log discrepancy

n\cdot a_{E_{e}}(X,B+\frac{D}{n})=-\min_{a\in\{\pm\varphi_{1},\ldots,\pm\varphi_{d}\}}\langle a,e\rangle=\max_{i=1}^{d}|\langle\varphi_{i},e\rangle|\geq 1.

Therefore
\operatorname{mld}(X,B+\frac{D}{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}
.

For b), note that
\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d})\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is an isomorphism of lattices, and
\Phi(U)\subset(-\lambda,\lambda)^{d}
. Since
\max_{e\in\Delta(1)}a_{i}\leq 1
,
we have
\Delta(1)\subset U
. Therefore
\Phi(\Delta(1))\subset(-\lambda,\lambda)^{d}
.
If
\lambda
is bounded above, then
\Phi(\Delta(1))
belongs to a finite collection, hence

X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
is finite up to isomorphism.
∎

Extending linear maps with length estimates

Let
\varphi\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a surjective homomorphism, where
\Lambda
is a lattice of dimension at least
2
.
Let
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda\cap\varphi^{\perp}
. Let
C\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a closed convex set such that
\varphi(C)=[p_{1},p_{2}]
, with
p_{1}<p_{2}
real numbers. Let
p=\lambda_{1}p_{1}+\lambda_{2}p_{2}
, where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
. Denote
\gamma=\min(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and
w(\varphi)=p_{2}-p_{1}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\colon\Lambda_{0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a surjective homomorphism.
Suppose the
\varphi_{0}
-image of a translation of
C\cap\varphi^{-1}(p)
which is contained in
\varphi^{\perp}

has finite positive length
w_{0}
. Then there exists
a surjective homomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}|_{\Lambda_{0}}=\varphi_{0}

and

\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}(C)<w(\varphi)+\frac{w_{0}}{\gamma}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi^{\prime}\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be an arbitrary extension of
\varphi_{0}
. Then

\Phi=(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is surjective,
\Phi(C)
is
a convex body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that its projection onto the
x_{1}
-axis is the interval
[p_{1},p_{2}]
, and its intersection with the line
\{x_{1}=p\}
is an interval of length
w_{0}
. Through each endpoint of the interval
\Phi(C)\cap\{x_{1}=p\}
, draw a supporting hyperplane to
\Phi(C)
. Together with the inequalities
p_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq p_{2}
, these form a quadrilateral or triangle
Q
, which contains
\Phi(C)
. We minimize and maximize the
x_{2}
-coordinate among the vertices of
Q
. There are two cases.

1) Case both min and max are attained on the same vertical line defining
Q
. Say
(p_{2},y)
and
(p_{2},y^{\prime})
are such that
[y,y^{\prime}]
is the projection of
Q
onto the
x_{2}
-axis. Then
y^{\prime}-y=\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}(Q)=:d
. Keeping the same intersection
Q\cap\{x_{1}=p\}
, we slide one of the vertices of
Q\cap\{x_{1}=p_{1}\}
towards the other until
Q
becomes a triangle
Q^{\prime}
with a vertex on the line
x_{1}=p_{1}
and base a segment on the line
x_{1}=p_{1}
of length
d^{\prime}\geq d
. By similarity, we compute

\frac{d^{\prime}}{w_{0}}=\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{p-p_{1}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}.

Therefore
d\leq d^{\prime}=\frac{w_{0}}{\lambda_{2}}\leq\frac{w_{0}}{\gamma}
. So in this case,
\varphi^{\prime}
is the desired extension, satisfying the stronger inequality

\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}(C)\leq\frac{w_{0}}{\gamma}.

2) Case min and max are attained on different vertical lines defining
Q
. Say
(p_{1},y)
and
(p_{2},y^{\prime})
are vertices of
Q
such that
[y,y^{\prime}]
is the projection of
Q
onto the
x_{2}
-axis. Let
(p,y^{\prime\prime})
be the vertex of
Q\cap\{x_{1}=p\}
with maximal
x_{2}
-value. Let
\delta=y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}
. Keeping the vertex
(p_{2},y^{\prime})
of
Q
fixed, we rotate the non-vertical supporting hyperplane of
Q
until it becomes
\{x_{2}=y^{\prime}\}
. The new body
Q^{\prime}
has again
[y,y^{\prime}]
as projection onto the
x_{2}
-axis,
(p_{1},y),(p_{1},y^{\prime})
are among its vertices, and its intersection with
\{x_{1}=p\}
has length
w_{0}+\delta
. By the argument of case 1), we have

\frac{\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}(C)}{w_{0}+\delta}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}.

For
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have an automorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
defined by
x^{\prime}_{1}=x_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}=zx_{1}+x_{2}
. It acts on each line
\{x_{1}=a\}
as the translation
y\mapsto y+za
.
After this automorphism,
\delta
transforms into
\delta+z(p_{2}-p)
.
There exists a unique
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\delta+z(p_{2}-p)\in[0,p_{2}-p)
, namely

z=\lceil\frac{-\delta}{p_{2}-p}\rceil
. Therefore, after changing the second coordinate
(i.e. changing the lifting of
\varphi_{0}
from
\varphi^{\prime}
to
\varphi^{\prime}+z\varphi
), we may suppose
\delta<p_{2}-p=\lambda_{1}(p_{2}-p_{1})
. Then

\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}(C)<\frac{w_{0}}{\lambda_{1}}+p_{2}-p_{1}\leq\frac{w_{0}}{\gamma}+w(\varphi).

∎

2.
A construction

Let
(X^{d},B)
be a toric weak log Fano with
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\epsilon>0
.
The latter assumption is equivalent to

N\cap\operatorname{int}(\epsilon U)=\{0\}.

Let
\varphi
be a primitive element of
M
, that is
\varphi\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a surjective
linear homomorphism. Let
w
be the length of the interval
\varphi(U)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\varphi(U)=[-w_{-},w_{+}]
, where
w_{-},w_{+}\geq 1
and
w_{-}+w_{+}=w
.

Proof.

Since
U\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
contains
0
in its interior, so does
the interval
\varphi(U)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\varphi(U)=[-w_{-},w_{+}]
, where
w_{-},w_{+}>0

and
w_{-}+w_{+}=w
.

Since
N_{\mathbb{R}}=|\Delta|=\cup_{t>0}tU
, there exist
e_{i},e_{j}\in\Delta(1)
such that

\varphi(e_{i})<0<\varphi(e_{j})
. Since
e_{j}\in a_{j}U
, we obtain
0<\varphi(e_{j})\leq a_{j}w_{+}
.
Since
\varphi(e_{j})
is a positive integer,
\varphi(e_{j})\geq 1
. Since
B
is effective,

a_{j}\leq 1
. Therefore
1\leq w_{+}
. A similar argument gives
1\leq w_{-}
.
∎

Let
M_{0}=M/{\mathbb{Z}}\varphi
be the quotient lattice, dual to
N_{0}=N\cap\varphi^{\perp}
.
Let
\square_{0}
be the image of
\square_{-K-B}
under the projection

M_{\mathbb{R}}\to M_{0,{\mathbb{R}}}
. The polar dual convex set
U_{0}=(\square_{0})^{*}
equals
U\cap\varphi^{\perp}
.
Since
\epsilon U
contains the origin in the interior, we deduce

N_{0}\cap\operatorname{int}(\epsilon U_{0})=\{0\}.

The surjective linear map
\varphi\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}
corresponds to a fibration of tori

T_{N}\to T_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Identifying
T_{\mathbb{Z}}
with the standard open chart
\{z_{0}=1\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
we obtain a toric rational fibration

\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\ \phi(x)=[1:\chi^{\varphi}(x)].

It coincides with the map induced by the invariant linear
system
\Lambda
defined by the finite set
\{0,\varphi\}\subset M
. The support function

h\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\ni e\mapsto\min(0,\langle\varphi,e\rangle)\in{\mathbb{R}}
is
N
-rational
and piecewise linear. If
X^{\prime}\to X
is a toric birational contraction, the mobile part
of
\Lambda_{X^{\prime}}
equals
\sum_{e_{i}\in\Delta_{X^{\prime}}(1)}-h(e_{i})V(e_{i})
.
Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be the fan consisting of the cones

\{\sigma\cap\varphi^{\leq 0},\sigma\cap\varphi^{\perp},\sigma\cap\varphi^{\geq 0}|\sigma\in\Delta\}
.
It is a subdivision of
\Delta
, which defines a toric birational contraction

\mu\colon X^{\prime}=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta^{\prime})\to T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)=X
. The induced map

X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is regular, and we obtained a toric resolution of
\phi
:

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\scriptstyle{\phi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}

Let
\mu^{*}(K+B)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}
be the log pullback. Then
B_{X^{\prime}}
is again invariant,
but possibly not effective in
\mu
-exceptional prime divisors.

Lemma 2.2
.

a_{E}(X,B)\leq w
for every invariant prime divisor
E
on
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose
e^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}(1)\setminus\Delta(1)
. There exists
\sigma\in\Delta(top)
,
and
e_{i},e_{j}\in\sigma(1)
such that
\varphi(e_{i})<0<\varphi(e_{j})
and

z_{ij}e^{\prime}=-\langle\varphi,e_{j}\rangle e_{i}+\langle\varphi,e_{i}\rangle e_{j}

for some
z_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. There exists
\psi_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

a_{V(e)}(X,B)=\langle\psi_{\sigma},e\rangle
for every
e\in N^{prim}\cap\sigma
.
Since
\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{i}\rangle=a_{i}
and
\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{j}\rangle=a_{j}
, we obtain

a_{V(e^{\prime})}(X,B)=\frac{a_{i}a_{j}}{z_{ij}}\langle\varphi,\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i}}-\frac{e_{j}}{a_{j}}\rangle.

Since
\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i}},\frac{e_{j}}{a_{j}}\in U
and
\varphi(U)
has length
w
,
we obtain
\langle\varphi,\frac{e_{i}}{a_{i}}-\frac{e_{j}}{a_{j}}\rangle\leq w
. Since
a_{i},a_{j}\leq 1
,
we deduce
a_{E_{e^{\prime}}}(X,B)\leq w
.

Suppose
e_{i}\in\Delta(1)
. Then
a_{V(e_{i})}(X,B)=a_{i}\leq 1
. Since
w\geq 2
by Lemma
2.1
,
we obtain
a_{V(e_{i})}(X,B)=a_{i}<w
.
∎

The toric morphism
\phi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a contraction, so its restriction to the open torus
inside
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a product. Its general fiber is the proper
toric variety
F=T_{N_{0}}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta_{0})
, where

\Delta_{0}=\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}|\sigma^{\prime}\subset\varphi^{\perp}\}=\{\sigma\cap\varphi^{\perp}|\sigma\in\Delta\}
.
Since
-K-B
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-free, so is
-K_{X^{\prime}}-B_{X^{\prime}}
. By adjunction,

(K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}})|_{F}=K_{F}+B_{F}
, where
B_{F}
is again invariant.
We obtain that
-K_{F}-B_{F}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-free, with moment polytope
\square_{0}
.
From
N_{0}\cap\operatorname{int}(\epsilon U_{0})=\{0\}
, we obtain
\operatorname{mld}(F,B_{F})\geq\epsilon
.
We conclude that
(F,B_{F})
satisfies the same properties as
(X,B)
, except that

B_{F}
may not be effective.

Fix
0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{w}
. Denote
\tilde{B}=(1-t)\Sigma_{X}+tB
.
Then

K+\tilde{B}=(1-t)(K+\Sigma_{X})+t(K+B)=t(K+B).

Since
\mu^{*}(K+\Sigma_{X})=K_{X^{\prime}}+\Sigma_{X^{\prime}}
, we obtain

\mu^{*}(K+\tilde{B})=K_{X^{\prime}}+\tilde{B}_{X^{\prime}}
, where

\tilde{B}_{X^{\prime}}=(1-t)\Sigma_{X^{\prime}}+tB_{X^{\prime}}=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}(1)}(1-ta_{e^{\prime}})V(e^{\prime}).

By Lemma
2.2
,
\tilde{B}_{X^{\prime}}
is effective.
After adjunction,
\tilde{B}_{F}=(1-t)\Sigma_{F}+tB_{F}
is again effective. We have

K_{F}+\tilde{B}_{F}=(1-t)(K_{F}+\Sigma_{F})+t(K_{F}+B_{F})=t(K_{F}+B_{F}).

Then
\square_{-K_{F}-\tilde{B}_{F}}=t\square_{0}
,
U(F,\tilde{B}_{F})=\frac{1}{t}U_{0}
.
So
-K_{F}-\tilde{B}_{F}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-free and
\operatorname{mld}(F,\tilde{B}_{F})\geq t\epsilon
.

Lemma 2.3
.

\lambda(X,B)<w+\lambda(F,\tilde{B}_{F})
.

Proof.

Consider
U\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varphi\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
\varphi(U)=[-w_{-},w_{+}]
for some
w_{-},w_{+}>0
with
w_{-}+w_{+}=w
. Let
\gamma

be the minimum of the barycenteric coordinates of
0\in\varphi(U)
. By Lemma
2.1
,
we estimate

\gamma=\frac{\min(w_{-},w_{+})}{w}\geq\frac{1}{w}.

Consider now
U_{0}\subset N_{0}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\lambda_{0}=\lambda(N_{0},U_{0})
.
That is, there exists a basis
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d-1}
of
M_{0}=N_{0}^{*}
such
that
\operatorname{length}\varphi_{i}(U_{0})\leq\lambda_{0}
. By Proposition
1.2
, each

\varphi_{i}\colon N_{0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
admits a lifting
\bar{\varphi}_{i}\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}

such that

\operatorname{length}\bar{\varphi}_{i}(U)<w+\gamma^{-1}\operatorname{length}\varphi_{i}(U_{0})<w(1+\lambda_{0}).

Since
\varphi,\bar{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\varphi}_{d-1}
is a basis of
M
, we obtain

\lambda(N,U)<w(1+\lambda(N_{0},U_{0})).

We have
\lambda(X,B)=\lambda(N,U)
and
\lambda(N_{0},U_{0})=t\lambda(F,\tilde{B}_{F})
.
Therefore

\lambda(X,B)<w+wt\lambda(F,\tilde{B}_{F})\leq w+\lambda(F,\tilde{B}_{F}).

∎

3.
Klt complements

By Lemma
1.1
, suffices to bound
\lambda(X,B)
from above in Theorem
0.1
.
With the same notations, we show

Proposition 3.1
.

\lambda(X,B)\leq\lambda(d,\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Suppose
d=1
.
Here
X={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
B=(1-a_{+})V(1)+(1-a_{-})V(-1)
with
a_{+},a_{-}\in[0,1]
.
The assumption
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq\epsilon
is equivalent to
\min(a_{+},a_{-})\geq\epsilon
. We have
N={\mathbb{Z}}
and
U=[-\frac{1}{a_{-}},\frac{1}{a_{+}}]
. Therefore

\lambda(X,B)=\frac{1}{a_{+}}+\frac{1}{a_{-}}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon}=\lambda(1,\epsilon).

Suppose
d\geq 2
. We have
N\cap\operatorname{int}(\epsilon U)=\{0\}
and
\dim U=d
.
Therefore
\dim(N\cap\operatorname{int}(\epsilon U))=0<\dim U
. By
[
1
, Theorem 5.2.b)]
,

w(\epsilon U)\leq d(d+1)
. That is, there exists a surjective linear homomorphism

\varphi\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that the interval
\varphi(U)
has length

w\leq\frac{d(d+1)}{\epsilon}.

We apply the construction in the previous section to
(X,B)
and
\varphi
,
choosing
t=w^{-1}
. Then
\operatorname{mld}(F,\tilde{B}_{F})\geq\frac{\epsilon}{w}
.
By induction,
\lambda(F,\tilde{B}_{F})\geq\lambda(d-1,\frac{\epsilon}{w})
.
By Lemma
2.3
,

\lambda(X,B)\leq w+\lambda(d-1,\frac{\epsilon}{w}).

Estimation of Regions of Attraction for Nonlinear Systems via Coordinate-Transformed TS Models and Piecewise Quadratic Lyapunov Functions
Artun Sel Electrical and Computer Engineering The Ohio State University Columbus, OH 43210, USA artunsel@ieee.org Mehmet Koruturk Electrical and Computer Engineering Virginia Tech Blacksburg, VA 24061, USA mkoruturk@vt.edu Erdi Sayar Robotics, Artificial Intelligence and Real-time Systems Technical University of Munich Munich, Germany erdi.sayar@tum.de

Abstract
This paper presents a novel approach for computing enlarged Region of Attractions (ROAs) for nonlinear dynamical systems through the integration of multiple coordinate transformations and piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions within the Takagi-Sugeno (TS) modeling framework. While existing methods typically follow a single-path approach of original system → \rightarrow TS model → \rightarrow ROA computation, the proposed methodology systematically applies a sequence of coordinate transformations to generate multiple system representations, each yielding distinct ROA estimations. Specifically, the approach transforms the original nonlinear system using transformation matrices T 1 , T 2 , … , T N T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{N} to obtain N N different coordinate representations, constructs corresponding TS models for each transformed system, and computes individual ROAs using piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions. The final ROA estimate is obtained as the union of all computed regions, leveraging the flexibility inherent in piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions compared to traditional quadratic approaches. The enhanced methodology demonstrates significant improvements in ROA size estimation compared to conventional single-transformation techniques, as evidenced through comparative analysis with existing TS -based stability methods.

The analysis of stability properties and
ROA

\dot{x}=f(x)
, determining the largest set of initial conditions from which trajectories converge to an equilibrium point is crucial for understanding system behavior and designing robust controllers. However, exact computation of

Among the various approaches for nonlinear system analysis,
TS
fuzzy models have emerged as a powerful framework due to their ability to exactly represent nonlinear dynamics within compact domains while maintaining a linear-like structure that facilitates stability analysis
[
30
]
. The
TS
modeling approach employs sector nonlinearity techniques to construct a convex combination of linear subsystems, enabling the application of
LMI

ROA

Traditional approaches for
ROA

TS

To address this conservatism, several researchers have proposed the use of non-quadratic Lyapunov functions, including fuzzy Lyapunov functions
[
31
]
and piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions
[
32
]
. The latter approach has demonstrated particular promise in reducing conservatism while maintaining computational feasibility through piecewise linear partitioning of the state space.

Despite these advances, existing methodologies typically follow a single-pathway approach: original nonlinear system
\rightarrow

TS
model construction
\rightarrow

ROA
computation. This conventional paradigm inherently limits the achievable
ROA
size, as the choice of coordinates and the resulting
TS
model structure significantly influence the final stability domain estimation. The fundamental insight underlying this limitation is that different coordinate representations of the same nonlinear system can yield substantially different
TS
models, each potentially capturing distinct aspects of the system’s stability properties.

Coordinate transformations have been extensively studied in nonlinear control theory for various purposes, including feedback linearization
[
33
]
, normal form transformations, and system simplification. However, their systematic exploitation for
ROA
enlargement in the context of
TS
modeling has received limited attention. The potential of coordinate transformations lies in their ability to expose different geometric structures of the nonlinear system, which can be more favorably captured by specific
TS
model constructions
[
34
,
35
]
.

This paper addresses the aforementioned limitations by proposing a novel multi-transformation approach that systematically leverages multiple coordinate representations to enhance
ROA
estimation. The key innovation lies in generating a family of transformed systems using carefully selected transformation matrices
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{N}
, constructing corresponding
TS
models for each representation, and computing individual
ROAs
using advanced piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions. The final
ROA
estimate is obtained as the union of all computed regions, effectively exploiting the complementary information provided by different coordinate perspectives.

The main contributions of this work are threefold: (i) development of a systematic framework for multiple coordinate transformation-based
ROA
enhancement, (ii) integration of piecewise quadratic Lyapunov functions within the multi-transformation paradigm to further reduce conservatism, and (iii) demonstration of significant
ROA
enlargement compared to conventional single-transformation approaches through comprehensive numerical examples.

II-A

Definitions for Mathematical Optimization Related Concepts

II-A

Linear Matrix Inequality

An
LMI
is a constraint of the form:

F(x)=F_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}F_{i}\succeq 0

(1)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})^{T}
represents the decision variable vector,
F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denote given symmetric matrices, and the inequality symbol
\succeq 0
indicates positive semidefiniteness. A
LMI
inherently defines a convex constraint on the variable vector
x
. When multiple
LMIs
are present, they can be consolidated into a single equivalent
LMI
through the utilization of block-diagonal matrix structures.
LMIs
constitute fundamental components in
Semidefinite Programming (SDP)
frameworks, where they function as convex constraints in optimization problems that admit efficient numerical solutions via interior-point algorithms
[
36
]
.

II-A
2

SDP
is a powerful optimization framework that extends linear programming to the cone of
Positive Semidefinite (PSD)
matrices. Formally, an
SDP
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\text{min.}

\displaystyle\langle C,X\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\langle A_{i},X\rangle\leq b_{i},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,m

\displaystyle X\succeq 0

where
X,C,A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are symmetric
n\times n
matrices,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}
are scalars, and
X\succeq 0
denotes that
X
is
PSD

[
29
]
.

Definitions for Applications of the Methods

II-B
1
Dynamical System

A dynamical system constitutes a mathematical framework for modeling the temporal evolution of a physical system’s state variables according to deterministic evolution laws. Formally, a dynamical system is characterized by a state space
\mathcal{X}
and a mapping
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{X}
that governs the evolution of the system state
x(t)\in\mathcal{X}
as a function of time. In the continuous-time domain, this temporal evolution is mathematically represented by the differential equation:

\dot{x}(t)=f(x(t),t)

(3)

where
\dot{x}(t)
denotes the time derivative of the state vector.

Dynamical systems are systematically categorized according to several fundamental characteristics: linearity versus nonlinearity, time-invariance versus time-variance, and autonomy versus non-autonomy. The theoretical analysis of such systems encompasses the investigation of stability properties, characterization of equilibrium manifolds, identification of limit cycles, and determination of other qualitative dynamical behaviors that govern system performance
[
37
]
.

Remark 1
.

In this study, we primarily focus on polynomial-type nonlinear systems that are time-invariant. For analysis problems, we specifically examine autonomous systems that possess these properties.

II-B
2
Stability Definitions

Consider a nonlinear dynamical system described by:

\dot{x}=f(x),\quad x(0)=x_{0}

(4)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state vector,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a locally Lipschitz function, and
f(0)=0
(i.e., the origin is an equilibrium point). We define various notions of stability as follows:

Definition 1

(
Stability in the Sense of Lyapunov
)
.

The equilibrium point
x=0
is stable in the sense of Lyapunov if, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that:

\|x(0)\|<\delta\implies\|x(t)\|<\epsilon,\quad\forall t\geq 0

(5)

Definition 2

(
Asymptotic Stability
)
.

The equilibrium point
x=0
is asymptotically stable if it is stable in the sense of Lyapunov and there exists a
\delta>0
such that:

\|x(0)\|<\delta\implies\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|x(t)\|=0

(6)

Definition 3

(
Global Stability
)
.

A stability property (Lyapunov, asymptotic, or exponential) is said to be global if it holds for any initial state
x(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e.,
\delta\rightarrow\infty
).

Definition 4

(
Local Stability
)
.

A stability property is said to be local if it holds only for initial states within some bounded neighborhood of the equilibrium (i.e.,
\|x(0)\|<\delta
for some finite
\delta>0
).

Definition 5

(
ROA
)
.

For a given locally asymptotically stable dynamical system, the corresponding region around the equilibrium point is called
ROA
.

Convex Optimization Based Nonlinear Dynamical System Analysis

Definition 6
.

A convex model for autonomous systems is defined as a collection of first-order
Ordinary Differential Equationss (ODEs)
in which the right-hand side vector field can be formulated as a convex combination of constituent vector fields (sub-models), specifically

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{i}(\mathbf{z})f_{i}(\mathbf{x})

(14)

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the number of linear models comprising the convex combination,
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
represents a premise variable that may be a function of the system state
\mathbf{x}
, time
t
, external disturbances, or exogenous parameters. The nonlinear weighting functions
h_{i}(\mathbf{z})
for
i\in{1,2,\ldots,r}
satisfy the convex sum property within a compact set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
, specifically:

\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{i}(\mathbf{z})=1,\quad 0\leq h_{i}(\mathbf{z})\leq 1,\quad\forall\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{C}.

(15)

and the special case is given by

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{i}(\mathbf{z})A_{i}\mathbf{x}(t)

(16)

Definition 7
.

The vector
\mathbf{z}
in a convex model (
16

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}

Definition 8
.

The nonlinear functions

in a convex model (
16
) are known as

; they hold the convex sum property in a compact set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
of the scheduling space.

Definition 9
.

If

are linear, i.e.,
f_{i}(\mathbf{x})=A_{i}\mathbf{x}
, (

TS

[

]
; if, in addition,

does not include the state

it is called a linear polytopic model
[
38
]
; if the
TS
model comes from fuzzy modeling techniques it is also referred to as
TS

Remark 2
.

When the convex sum property is satisfied globally, that is, when
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{C}
for all

V
Conclusions

In this study,
the
TS
modeling framework was employed to study the
ROA
estimation problem. The
TS
approach enabled the leveraging of convex optimization techniques to handle nonlinear dynamics effectively. Furthermore, an extension to the standard methods was proposed, aimed at enhancing their applicability and reducing conservatism in practical scenarios. These contributions collectively demonstrate the potential of combining
TS
modeling with
SDP
-based tools to address challenging problems in nonlinear dynamical systems.

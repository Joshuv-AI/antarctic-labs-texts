Computed tomography-guided biopsy for pulmonary nodules: comparison between with and without coaxial technique
Authors: Zi-Bing Pan, Shu Pan, Jin-Hu Xu, Bei-Ru Wang, Lu-Lu Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe coaxial technique has been introduced as an auxiliary approach to computed tomography (CT)-guided pulmonary nodule (PN) biopsy to facilitate the acquisition of additional tissue samples. Here, the diagnostic efficacy and safety of CT-guided PN biopsy performed with and without the coaxial technique was investigated.MethodsConsecutive PN cases that had undergone CT-guided biopsy between January 2022 and December 2023, were retrospectively recruited and categorized into coaxial and non-coaxial biopsy groups. Biopsy-related parameters, diagnostic performance, and complication incidence were analyzed to compare outcomes between the groups.ResultsOverall, 299 patients underwent CT-guided biopsy for PNs, with one nodule biopsied in each patient. Among them, 147 patients underwent biopsy with the coaxial technique, while 152 underwent biopsy without it. Relative to the non-coaxial group, the coaxial group required significantly fewer needle punctures (1.3 ± 0.6 vs. 1.8 ± 0.6, P < 0.001), yielded more tissue specimens (3.0 ± 0.7 vs. 1.6 ± 0.7, P < 0.001), and had a shorter procedure duration (9.0 ± 2.6 vs. 15.7 ± 4.5 min, P < 0.001). The two groups exhibited comparable sensitivity (98.2% vs. 94.3%, P = 0.156), diagnostic accuracy (98.6% vs. 96.1%, P = 0.283), and diagnostic yield (80.3% vs. 75.0%, P = 0.247). However, the rate of adequate tissue acquisition for molecular testing at the initial biopsy was markedly higher in the coaxial group than in the non-coaxial group (97.8% vs. 86.2%, P = 0.004). The incidence rates of pneumothorax (11.2% vs. 16.3%, P = 0.196) and pulmonary hemorrhage (29.6% vs. 33.3%, P = 0.488) were comparable between the two groups.ConclusionsThe coaxial technique effectively reduces procedural duration and significantly improves the rate of adequate tissue sample collection for molecular testing during CT-guided PN biopsy without increasing complication rates.Keywords: CT, Biopsy, Pulmonary nodule, Coaxial

Background

Computed tomography (CT)-guided biopsy is currently one of the most widely used diagnostic techniques for evaluating pulmonary nodules (PNs) [1–3]. Previous studies have demonstrated that CT-guided PN biopsy achieves high diagnostic accuracy, with reported rates ranging from 92% to 98% [4–6]. To maximize diagnostic performance, both the quantity and quality of obtained tissue samples are critical determinants of success [7]. In addition to providing histopathological confirmation of lung cancer, biopsy specimens are increasingly required for molecular testing to guide precision-targeted therapies [8].

To facilitate the acquisition of multiple tissue samples from a single puncture, the coaxial technique has been commonly applied in lung biopsy procedures [6]. The presence of a coaxial sheath allows repeated tissue sampling without the need for additional pleural punctures. Despite these theoretical advantages, the effect of the coaxial technique on diagnostic yield and accuracy in CT-guided PN biopsy is controversial as comparative evidence is limited. To date, few studies have systematically evaluated the clinical effectiveness of CT-guided PN biopsy performed with and without the coaxial technique.

Here, the diagnostic performance and safety profiles of CT-guided PN biopsy using the coaxial technique versus the conventional non-coaxial approach were investigated.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University (No. 2025–0610 A). As the study was retrospective, the written informed consent requirement was waived.

Consecutive PN cases who had undergone CT-guided biopsy from January 2022 to December 2023 were enrolled. Prior to December 2022, biopsies were routinely performed without the coaxial technique. The coaxial technique was introduced into our hospital on December 2022, and the coaxial technique was adopted as the standard approach after then.

The inclusion criteria were: (a) presence of a PN; (b) solid PN; and (c) Lung Imaging Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS) category 4. The exclusion criteria included: (a) PN diameter < 8 mm; (b) previously biopsied nodules; and (c) PNs that decreased in size over the course of follow-up.

The indications for CT-guided biopsy included: (a) patients who did not achieve the definite diagnosis on bronchoscopy; and (b) the PN-pleura distance ≤ 30 mm.

CT-guided PN biopsy

All biopsy procedures were performed under CT guidance with a 16-row scanner (Philips, Cleveland, OH, USA) with local anesthesia. The scanning parameters were a tube voltage of 120 kV, tube current of 150 mA, and section thickness of 1 mm.

The biopsy pathways were selected individually according to the locations of PNs, and the pathways should avoid the bone, visible vessels, bullae, and fissures. The patients’ body positions were selected according to the biopsy pathways. The puncture site was decided by the CT gantry laser lights and self-made marker on the patient’s skin. The local anaesthesia was performed at the puncture site.

In CT-guided biopsy without the coaxial technique group, a core biopsy needle (18G, Wego, Weihai, China) was punctured into the lung parenchyma via the scheduled needle pathway. Then the repeat CT scan was conducted to adjust the direction of needle tip. When the needle tip touched the PN, the tissue samples were obtained. A specimen length greater than 5 mm was considered adequate; otherwise, additional sampling was performed.

In CT-guided biopsy with the coaxial technique group, a 17G introducer needle sheath (DuoSmart, Modena, Italy) was initially punctured into the lung parenchyma and advanced into the PN, the needle puncture procedures were same as those in the biopsy without coaxial group. Then, the 18G core needle was sent through the sheath to collect tissue samples. A minimum of two specimens were typically obtained using this method.

All tissue samples were fixed in 10% formaldehyde for histopathological examination. After completion of the biopsy procedure, an immediate repeat CT scan was performed to assess complications.

Assessment

Biopsy-based pathological diagnoses were classified as malignant, specific benign, or non-specific benign. Lesions defined as specific benign related to benign tumors or specific infections caused by pathogens, including fungi, bacteria, and mycobacteria, that could reasonably account for the imaging results [9]. Non-specific benign diagnoses included those cases with pathological evidence of fibrosis or inflammatory cell infiltration that was insufficient for a definitive diagnosis [9].

Final diagnoses were categorized as malignant or benign. Malignancy was confirmed by: (a) surgical resection; or (b) CT-guided or bronchoscopic biopsy. Benignity was established based on: (a) surgical resection; (b) specific benign biopsy results; or (c) follow-up CT demonstrating a > 20% drop in PN diameter, or stable size (< 20% reduction or no change) for at least 1 year in the absence of anticancer therapy [9].

Data collection

Variables included patient age and sex, PN diameter and location, biopsy procedure-related parameters, diagnostic performance indicators, and complication rates.

Definition

Diagnostic accuracy was defined as concordance between biopsy-based and final diagnoses. Diagnostic yield was calculated as: (biopsy-based malignancy + biopsy-based specific benignity) / total number of cases [6]. Procedure time was the time interval from the first CT scan to the final CT scan during the biopsy. Intrapulmonary needle length was calculated from the needle tip to the crossover point of needle body and the pleura. Angle of the needle was the angle between needle body and pleura. Pulmonary hemorrhage was defined as the appearance of new ground-glass opacity or consolidation along the needle tract. High-grade hemorrhage represented a needle-tract hemorrhage wider than 2 cm [10].

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables with normal distributions are shown as mean ± standard deviation, while skewed data are given as median (Q1; Q3). These data were compared using t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical data comparisons were made with χ² or Fisher’s exact tests. Risk factors for biopsy-related complications were identified using logistic regression, with variables showing significance (P < 0.1) in the univariate analysis incorporated into multivariate regression models. A two-sided P < 0.05 represented significance. SPSS 16.0 was used for these analyses.

Diagnostic performance.

Details of diagnostic performance are provided in Table 3. The diagnostic yield of the two procedures was comparable (80.3% vs. 75.0%, P = 0.247). In coaxial cases, 118 nodules (113 malignant and 5 specific benign lesions) were definitively diagnosed based on biopsy. In the non-coaxial group, 114 nodules (99 malignant and 15 specific benign) yielded definitive biopsy diagnoses.

Ninety individuals who received the coaxial procedure and 87 who received the non-coaxial technique required molecular testing. The initial adequacy rate of the tissue for these analyses was substantially higher after collection with the coaxial technique relative to non-coaxial collection (97.8% vs. 86.2%, P = 0.004). All patients from whom sufficient tissue could not be obtained at initial biopsy successfully completed molecular testing after repeat biopsy.

Complications.

Biopsy-related complications are detailed in Table 5. Pneumothorax occurred in 17 (11.2%) and 24 (16.3%) cases following the non-coaxial and coaxial procedures, respectively; the difference was non-significant (P = 0.196). Chest tube placement was required in 6 (3.9%) and 8 (5.4%) patients in the non-coaxial and coaxial groups, respectively (P = 0.541). Logistic regression results indicated that male sex (P = 0.012) and lateral positioning during biopsy (P = 0.018) were independent risk factors for pneumothorax (Table 6).

Discussion

CT-guided biopsy is a well-established and widely employed technique for diagnosing PNs [11]. Compared with bronchoscopic biopsy, CT-guided biopsy generally provides superior diagnostic yield and accuracy for PN evaluation [12]. Two primary biopsy techniques are commonly used: fine-needle aspiration and core needle biopsy. A previous meta-analysis demonstrated that core needle biopsy achieves a significantly higher sample adequacy rate than fine-needle biopsy (95.7% vs. 85.8%, P < 0.0001) [7]. Additionally, prior research has shown that 14G Spirotome needles enable better lesion characterization and more reliable biomarker analysis than 22G fine needles [13].

Here, the diagnostic performance and safety of CT-guided core needle biopsy with and without the coaxial technique were compared. Our findings revealed no marked differences between the two groups in terms of diagnostic sensitivity, yield, or accuracy. These results suggest that the coaxial technique does not necessarily improve the overall diagnostic performance of CT-guided PN biopsy. However, Liu et al. [14] reported a significantly higher diagnostic accuracy when the coaxial technique was used (97.4% vs. 93.1%, P = 0.02). The discrepancy between our findings and those of Liu et al. may be attributable to differences in sample size, number of participating centers, and operator experience. From a theoretical standpoint, the coaxial technique should facilitate the acquisition of additional tissue samples and potentially improve diagnostic accuracy. In our study, however, strict quality control of specimens in the non-coaxial group may have contributed to similarly high diagnostic performance.

While diagnostic accuracy and yield were comparable between groups, a notable advantage of the coaxial technique was the substantial reduction in procedure time. This finding indicates that the coaxial sheath provides a stable needle pathway that allows repeated tissue sampling without requiring additional pleural punctures. In contrast, repeated sampling without a coaxial sheath necessitates multiple punctures, thereby prolonging the procedure.

With the growing application of molecular and immunotherapies for lung cancer [15–17], molecular testing has become an essential component of PN evaluation. Adequate tissue for molecular analysis is therefore increasingly critical. In this study, the coaxial technique significantly improved the initial adequacy rate of tissue for molecular testing compared with the non-coaxial approach. This finding underscores that contemporary CT-guided biopsy should both help secure histopathological diagnosis while also fulfilling the requirements for molecular profiling.

In theory, the coaxial technique may increase the risk of complication because the coaxial needle is thicker than the biopsy needle (17G vs. 18G). However, the incidences of pneumothorax and pulmonary hemorrhage were comparable between the coaxial and non-coaxial groups in this study, suggesting that the coaxial technique may not compromise procedural safety. In addition, the logistic analyses also did not reveal that use of coaxial was associated with the pneumothorax and pulmonary hemorrhage. These results may be attributed to that slight difference in needle diameter would not cause a significant difference in complication rates.

Lateral positioning was independently linked to pneumothorax risk in our study. Similar findings were described by Li et al. [5], showing that non-prone positioning increased pneumothorax risk. Compared with prone and supine positions, maintaining patient immobility in the lateral position is more difficult, which may lead to increased intra-procedural movement and pleural injury. Male sex was also found to be a potential risk for pneumothorax. This association may reflect potential selection bias inherent to the retrospective design of this study and a higher prevalence of smoking-related pulmonary impairment among male patients.

Advanced age and longer intrapulmonary needle length were independent risk factors for high-grade pulmonary hemorrhage in the present analysis. This aligns with those results reported by Li et al. [5]. Older patients generally have reduced physiologic reserve and poorer tolerance of invasive procedures, which may predispose them to more severe hemorrhagic complications.

There are several limitations to this investigation. First, the retrospective design increases the susceptibility to selection bias. Prospective, randomized analyses are necessary for verification of the findings. Second, the two groups were recruited from different time periods, and increasing operator experience over time may have influenced outcomes. Third, the distribution of PN locations and patients’ positions was not fully balanced between groups, which may further contribute to potential bias.

Conclusion

In summary, although further prospective studies are warranted, the present findings indicate that the coaxial technique can substantially reduce the duration of CT-guided pulmonary nodule biopsy and significantly increase the initial adequacy rate of tissue collection for molecular testing. However, the diagnostic accuracy and safety were not influenced by the coaxial technique.

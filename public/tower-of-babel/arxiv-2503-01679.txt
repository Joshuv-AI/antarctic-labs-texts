Swift Observations of Gaia BH3
Boris Sbarufatti, Renato Falomo, Aldo Treves

Abstract
We present UV and X-ray observations, obtained with the Swift Observatory, of Gaia BH3 a binary system containing a 33 M ⊙ M_{\odot} black hole discovered through Gaia astrometry. The system is well detected in all UV and optical filters (1700-6500 Å). We compare our results with the modeling of the non collapsed component using synthetic stellar libraries, a good agreement is found with our UV observations



I
Introduction

Gaia
BH3 (Gaia DR3 4318465066420528000) is an astrometric binary system containing a 33
M_{\odot}
dormant black hole, discovered through astrometry by the
Gaia
mission. The binary system has a period of 11.6 yr, eccentricity
e=0.729
, a semi-major axis of 16 AU, and a distance
d=590
pc. The visible component is a metal poor giant of mass
M=0.76

M_{\odot}
,
T_{eff}
=5200 K,
\log(g)=2.929
, and [Fe/H]=-2.56
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2024
)
.

Gaia
BH3 is a remarkable source because the mass of the unseen black hole component is significantly larger than that of black holes in X-ray bright systems, the most massive of which is Cyg X-1,
M=\sim 20

M_{\odot}

(
Miller-Jones et al. 2021
)
.

In this work we report on UV and X-ray observations of
Gaia
BH3 obtained with the Neil Gehrels
Swift
Observatory.

II.1
X-ray Observations

The XRT data of each observation were processed using the
xrtpipeline
task with standard calibration parameters. A search for X-ray sources consistent with the sky position of BH3 was performed using the XIMAGE detection algorithm on both data sets, with no source detected in either case, with a 90% confidence level. We then combined the observations using XSELECT, and confirmed that no source coinciding with
Gaia
BH3 is detected.
Using a 30 pixel (70.8″) radius region for BH3 we estimate an upper limit of
8.1\times 10^{-}3~counts~s^{-1}(4.0\times 10^{-16}~W~m^{-2}
, assuming a power law spectrum with photon index
\Gamma=-2
).
This is consistent, but less constraining, with the results of
eRosita

(
Gilfanov et al. 2024
,
F_{X}<1.2\times 10^{-17}~W~m^{-2}
,)
, and
Chandra

(
Cappelluti et al. 2024
,
F_{X}<3.25\times 10^{-18}~W~m^{-2}
,)
.

A X-ray source is serendipitously detected in the field, at position RA,DEC (J2000): 19h 39’ 09.15”, +15
∘
01’ 21.7” with an error radius of 6.4″, at a rate of
3.3\pm 0.8\times 10^{-3}~counts~s^{-1}
, equivalent to a flux of
1.6\pm 0.4\times 10^{-15}~W~m^{-2}
assuming a power-law spectrum with photon index
\Gamma=-2
).
No known source is reported by the SIMBAD database
(
Wenger et al. 2000
)
or the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database (NED)

II.2
UV observations

In order to characterize the Optical/UV Spectral Energy Distribution (SED) of the source UVOT observations were obtained, with a first pointing in April 2024, using all six standard filters
(
Poole et al. 2008
;
Breeveld et al. 2011
)
, and a second deeper pointing using only the UVM2 filter in May 2024 (see Table
2
).

A list of all sources detected in the images was produced using the
uvotdetect
tool to aid selection of a source and background region not contaminated by other sources. Multiple exposures in the same filter were co-added using the
uvotimsum
task, and aperture photometry was obtained using
uvotsource
, using a 5
\arcsec
radius aperture region centered at the
Gaia
BH3 position, and an empty background region of 25
\arcsec
radius.

The source was detected in all UVOT six filters. Data of the B filter are strongly affected by saturation and therefore ignored. U and V filter data show some evidence of coincidence loss that was corrected using the appropriate corrections available in the UVOT calibration database while running
uvotsource
, inspite of a higher uncertainty on the estimated fluxes for those bands.
The UVW1 and UVW2 filters are contaminated by a red leak, that we corrected following the prescriptions suggested by
Brown et al. 2010
.
The derived UV fluxes are given in Table
2
. The UVM2 data across the two epochs show no evidence of variability.

IV
Discussion

Gaia
BH3 is the third black hole discovered through precise astrometric measurements by
Gaia

(
El-Badry et al. 2023
;
El-Badry et al. 2023
;
Tanikawa et al. 2023
;
Chakrabarti et al. 2023
, for BH1 and BH2 see )
. The discoveries appear rather robust because the astrometric procedure is unaffected by the uncertainty on the inclination angle.

These systems are much wider than those of classical X-ray binaries so that accretion and electromagnetic emission powered by Roche-lobe mass transfer can be excluded. A possible energy source could be the accretion of the interstellar medium. We quote the case of OGLE-2011-BLG-0462
(
Sahu et al. 2022
)
where an isolated black was discovered in Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images through gravitational microlensing. Also in that case it was possible only to give an upper limit to the X-ray flux
(
Mereghetti et al. 2022
)
.
Another likely accretion mechanism is an advection-dominated accretion flow due do wind-driven mass loss from the companion star, which has already been considered by
Cappelluti et al. 2024
for this system, but has also been proposed for BH1 and BH2 by
Rodriguez et al. 2024

In the present work we focus on
Gaia
BH3 and the UV band properties of the binary system in search of a possible UV contribution from the black hole to the total emission. Our observations do not show any significant UV flux in excess with the expected values from the model
constrained at lower frequency.
This is consistent with the expectations of
Cappelluti et al. 2024
, who proposed an advection-dominated accretion flow (ADAF) emission model for the accretion of
Gaia
BH3.
In such a case the UV flux at
\sim 2200~\text{\r{A}}
would be
<1
% of the flux measured by us, which we attribute to the non collapsed component.

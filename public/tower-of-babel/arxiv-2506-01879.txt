Free Askey–Wilson functionals and geometric last passage percolation on a strip
Włodek Bryc, Kamil Szpojankowski, Jacek Wesołowski

Abstract
. Barraquand, Corwin, and Yang [ Barraquand et al., 2024 ] established that geometric last passage percolation (LPP) on a strip of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} has a unique stationary measure. Building on this, Barraquand [ Barraquand, 2024 ] derived explicit contour integral formulas for the model’s multipoint probability generating function. In this paper, we introduce free Askey–Wilson functionals and use them to extend these generating function formulas. Our framework yields explicit expressions valid over a broader range of boundary parameters than previously accessible. This generalization allows us to determine the full phase diagram that characterizes how the large-scale asymptotics of the stationary measure depend on the boundary conditions. In addition, we prove a Poisson approximation for the stationary measure when the parameters vary with the strip width.



1.
Introduction

Stationary measures of out-of-equilibrium systems in finite domains typically exhibit a nontrivial dependence on boundary conditions. A classical example is the asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) on
N
sites, coupled to boundary reservoirs (open ASEP). In this work, we demonstrate that analogous phenomena arise in the stationary measure of the directed geometric last passage percolation (LPP) model on a strip, recently introduced in
[
Barraquand et al., 2024
]
. A technical reason underlying this similarity in phase diagrams is that, somewhat unexpectedly, the two models are linked through integral representations of their generating functions, first discovered in the fan region of ASEP in
[
Bryc and Wesołowski, 2017
]
, and more recently for geometric LPP in
[
Barraquand, 2024
]
. The objective of the present work is to deepen the theoretical understanding of such representations and to extend their applicability to the full range of boundary parameters.

1.1.
Geometric last passage percolation on a strip

For

Definition 1.1

(geometric LPP)

Let
\mathsf{a}\in(0,1)
be the bulk parameter and let
\mathsf{c}_{1},\mathsf{c}_{2}\geq 0

\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{1},\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2}<1

Let

0

\omega_{n,n}\sim Geo(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{1})
,

\omega_{n,n+N}\sim Geo(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2})

For a given initial condition
G(i,0)\geq 0

indexed by the vertices

(1.1)

G(j,n)=\omega_{j,n}+\max\{G(j-1,n),G(j,n-1)\},\quad n\leq j\leq n+N,

with the convention that

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

In particular,
G(j,1)=\max_{1\leq k\leq j}\{G(k,0)+\sum_{i=k}^{j}\omega_{k,1}\}
,
j=1,\dots,N+1

We are interested in the laws of the vectors
\mathbf{G}_{n}:=(G(j+n,n)-G(n,n))_{j=0,\dots,N}
along the horizontal paths at level
n
.

Definition 1.2

(stationary measure)

We say that the law
\nu

under the initial condition
\mathbf{G}_{0}

For

2.1.
Free Askey–Wilson polynomials

Our starting point is the four-parameter family of the "free" Askey–Wilson polynomials with

2.2.
The functionals

We introduce a four-parameter family of functionals

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}[y^{n}]
, and then extended to arbitrary polynomials by linearity. The functional is defined recursively, starting with the initial condition
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}[1]=1
, together with the requirement that
\mathcal{L}^{a,b,c,d}[W_{n}(y)]=0
for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
abcd\neq 1
, this determines
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}:\mathbb{C}[y]\to\mathbb{C}
uniquely, and
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}
is invariant under the permutations of the parameters
a,b,c,d
.
In view of permutation invariance, we can put any zeros at the end and shorten the notation by omitting trailing zeros, writing
\mathcal{L}
for
\mathcal{L}^{\left(0,0,0,0\right)}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a\right)}
for
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,0,0,0\right)}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b\right)}
for
\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,0,0\right)}
and so on.
The above mimics the usual approach to the moment functional, although we consider complex-valued functionals and complex polynomials.
In particular,
\mathcal{L}
is the moment functional of the Chebyshev polynomials, and is given by an explicit
integral

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}[f]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-2}^{2}f(y)\sqrt{4-y^{2}}{\textnormal{d}}y.

Definition (
2.7
) gives a simple linear recurrence relation for the sequence

\lambda_{n}:=\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}[U_{n}]
,
n=0,1,\dots
. We have

(2.9)

\lambda_{n}=s_{1}\lambda_{n-1}-s_{2}\lambda_{n-2}+s_{3}\lambda_{n-3}-s_{4}\lambda_{n-4},\quad n\geq 2.

(Note that since
\lambda_{-k}=-\lambda_{k-2}
, the recurrence relation for
n=2
takes an irregular form, see (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).)

Using the initial conditions
\lambda_{-2}=-1
,
\lambda_{-1}=0
,
\lambda_{0}=1
, and
\lambda_{1}=\frac{s_{1}-s_{3}}{1-s_{4}}
, which corresponds to the irregular term (
2.4
), recurrence (
2.9
) can be solved explicitly. If all the parameters are distinct, the solution is

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{(1-bc)(1-bd)(1-cd)}{(a-b)(a-c)(a-d)(1-abcd)}a^{n+3}+\frac{(1-ac)(1-ad)(1-cd)}{(b-a)(b-c)(b-d)(1-abcd)}b^{n+3}\\
+\frac{(1-ab)(1-ad)(1-bd)}{(c-a)(c-b)(c-d)(1-abcd)}c^{n+3}+\frac{(1-ab)(1-ac)(1-bc)}{(d-a)(d-b)(d-c)(1-abcd)}d^{n+3}.

This formula is valid for all
n\geq-2
, but we only need it for
n\geq 0
.
In this paper we focus on the case with only three non-zero parameters. Then

(2.11)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{(1-bc)}{(a-b)(a-c)}a^{n+2}+\frac{(1-ac)}{(b-a)(b-c)}b^{n+2}+\frac{(1-ab)}{(c-a)(c-b)}c^{n+2},

which is valid for
n\geq-1
and distinct parameters
a,b,c
.

We also note that for
n\geq-1
we have

(2.12)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b\right)}[U_{n}]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{n-k}b^{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{a^{n+1}-b^{n+1}}{a-b}&a\neq b\\
(n+1)a^{n}&a=b,\end{cases}

which is the case when
c=0
in (
2.11
), and for
n\geq 0
we have

(2.13)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a\right)}[U_{n}]=a^{n},

which is the case when
b=0
in (
2.12
).

These formulas give the following identity:

(2.14)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}=\frac{a^{2}(1-bc)}{(a-b)(a-c)}\mathcal{L}^{\left(a\right)}+\frac{b^{2}(1-ac)}{(b-a)(b-c)}\mathcal{L}^{\left(b\right)}+\frac{c^{2}(1-ab)}{(c-a)(c-b)}\mathcal{L}^{\left(c\right)}.

For repeated parameters, we obtain the following limiting case of (
2.11
):

(2.15)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,b\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{b^{n+1}(n(b-a)(1-ab)+b+a^{2}b-2a)}{(a-b)^{2}}+\frac{\left(1-b^{2}\right)a^{n+2}}{(a-b)^{2}}.

This gives the following identity

(2.16)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,b\right)}=\frac{a^{2}(1-b^{2})}{(a-b)^{2}}\mathcal{L}^{\left(a\right)}+\frac{b(b-a-a(1-ab))}{(a-b)^{2}}\mathcal{L}^{\left(b\right)}+\frac{b^{2}(1-ab)}{b-a}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}\mathcal{L}^{\left(b\right)}.

We also have

(2.17)

\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,a,a\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{1}{2}(n+1)a^{n}\left(\left(1-a^{2}\right)n+2\right).

From recursion (
2.9
), or from explicit solutions, we obtain the following version of
[
Szpojankowski, 2010
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|<1/\max\{1,|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\}
,
we have

(2.18)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c,d\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{z(s_{1}s_{4}-s_{3})}{(1-s_{4})(1-az)(1-bz)(1-cz)(1-dz)}+\frac{1+s_{4}z^{2}}{(1-az)(1-bz)(1-cz)(1-dz)}\\

If all the parameters are distinct, (

2.3.
Extension to analytic functions

Under appropriate additional assumptions on

, functional

extends from polynomials to more general functions and is given as an integral with respect to probability measure, as in (

extends to analytic functions, but the resulting functional is not always given as an integral, see Remark

Definition 2.2

is oriented counterclockwise.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
p
is a polynomial then

(2.28)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[p]=\mathcal{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[p].

Proof.

It suffices to verify (
2.28
) for
p=U_{n}
, the
n
-th Chebyshev polynomial. We use the well know formula

(2.29)

U_{n}\left(w+\tfrac{1}{w}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{w^{n+1}-1/w^{n+1}}{w-1/w}&w\neq\pm 1\\
n+1&w=1\\
(-1)^{n}(n+1)&w=-1\end{cases}

which can be checked from the three step recurrence relation (
2.2
).

Consider the integral
(
2.27
). For
n=1,2,\dots
we have

(2.30)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{U_{n}\left(w+\frac{1}{w}\right)(1-w^{2})(1-abcw)}{w(1-aw)}\,{\textnormal{d}}w\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{\frac{1/w^{n+1}-w^{n+1}}{1/w-w}(1-w^{2}){(1-abcw)}}{w(1-aw)(1-bw)(1-cw)}\,{\textnormal{d}}w=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{(1/w^{n+1}-w^{n+1})(1-abcw)}{(1-aw)(1-bw)(1-cw)}\,{\textnormal{d}}w\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{1/w^{n+1}(1-abcw)}{(1-aw)(1-bw)(1-cw)}\,{\textnormal{d}}w-\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{w^{n+1}(1-abcw)}{(1-aw)(1-bw)(1-cw)}\,{\textnormal{d}}w.

The second integral vanishes as the integrand is an analytic function on the disk
|w|<|\rho|
. In the first integral, we use the fact that with
\max\{|a|,|b|,|c|\}\rho\leq\rho R<1
the geometric series
\sum_{k\geq 0}\,a^{k}w^{k}
,
\sum_{k\geq 0}\,b^{k}w^{k}
and
\sum_{k\geq 0}\,c^{k}w^{k}
converge. Expanding the integrand into these series we obtain

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[U_{n}]=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|w|=\rho}\frac{{(1-abcw)}}{w^{n+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}{w^{r}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=r\\

Clearly,
\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}[U_{0}]=1
.

We thus see that the generating function

The following formula together with (

Lemma 2.7

2.6.
Asymptotic expansions

In our application to geometric LPP, we require a few technical results on asymptotics. The list of cases presented is not exhaustive, but it covers all the situations that we needed.

Proposition 2.15
.

Fix
0<v<1/R
, where
R
is given by (
2.25
).
Then, as
n\to\infty
, we have the following asymptotics.

(i)

If
|a|,|b|,|c|<1
, and the parameters are real or form a complex conjugate pair, then

(2.51)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]\sim C(a,b,c)\;\frac{1}{n^{3/2}v^{3/2}(1-v)^{2n-3}},

where
C(a,b,c)=\tfrac{(1-ab)(1-ac)(1-bc)}{2\sqrt{\pi}(1-a)^{2}(1-b)^{2}(1-c)^{2}}
.

(ii)

If
a>1
and
0\leq|b|,|c|\leq 1
or
0\leq|c|<1\leq b<a
, then

(2.52)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]\sim C(a,b,c)\;\left(\frac{a}{(a-v)(1-av)}\right)^{n},

where
C(a,b,c)=\tfrac{\left(a^{2}-1\right)(1-bc)}{(a-b)(a-c)}
.

(iii)

If
a=b>1
,
0\leq c<a
, then

(2.53)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]\sim C(a,c)\ v\;n\;\left(\frac{a}{(a-v)(1-av)}\right)^{n+1},

where
C(a,c)=\tfrac{\left(a^{2}-1\right)^{2}(1-ac)}{a^{2}(a-c)}
.

(iv)

If
a=1
, and
0\leq|b|,|c|<1
are real or form a complex conjugate pair, then

(2.54)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]\sim\frac{1-bc}{\sqrt{\pi}(1-b)(1-c)}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{v}(1-v)^{2n-1}}.

Proof of Proposition
2.15
.

The proof of (
2.51
) relies on explicit integral representation. We have
|a|,|b|,|c|<1
where
all three parameters are real or there is one complex-conjugate pair.
In this case, we use (
2.37
)
with
f=\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}
.
We have

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]=\frac{(1-ab)(1-ac)(1-bc)}{2\pi}\int_{-2}^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}(y)\frac{\sqrt{4-y^{2}}}{\mathfrak{h}_{a}(y)\mathfrak{h}_{b}(y)\mathfrak{h}_{c}(y)}{\textnormal{d}}y.

Function
\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}
is increasing on the interval
[-2,2]
, with maximum
1/(1-v)^{2n}
. On the interval
[-2,1]

function
\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}
is bounded by
1/((1-v)^{2}+\delta v)^{n}
, which is of the lower order than the contribution of the order of
n^{-3/2}/(1-v)^{2n}
that comes from the integral over the interval
[1,2]
.
We now check that the contribution of the integral over
[1,2]
is indeed of this higher order.
Changing the variable
y=2-u/n
we get

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\sqrt{4-y^{2}}\,{\textnormal{d}}y}{(1+v^{2}-vy)^{n}(1+a^{2}-ay)(1+b^{2}-by)(1+c^{2}-cy)}\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi n^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{u}\sqrt{4-u/n}}{((1-v)^{2}+vu/n)^{n}((1-a)^{2}+au/n)((1-b)^{2}+bu/n)((1-c)^{2}+cu/n)}\,{\textnormal{d}}u\\
\sim\frac{1}{\pi n^{3/2}(1-v)^{2n}(1-a)^{2}(1-b)^{2}(1-c)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{u}e^{-vu/(1-v)^{2}}\,{\textnormal{d}}u=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}v^{3/2}(1-v)^{2n-3}(1-a)^{2}(1-b)^{2}(1-c)^{2}}.

(More formally, we re-normalize and pass to the limit under the integral, which can be justified using (
3.14
). Compare Section
3.3.1
.) This establishes (
2.51
).

The proof of (
2.54
) also relies on explicit integral representation:

\mathbb{L}^{\left(1,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]=\frac{(1-b)(1-c)(1-bc)}{2\pi}\int_{-2}^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}(y)\frac{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{b}(y)\mathfrak{h}_{c}(y)}\sqrt{\frac{2+y}{2-y}}\,{\textnormal{d}}y.

This follows from Proposition
2.5
by taking limit
a\nearrow 1
in (
2.37
).
As in the previous argument, only the integral over
[1,2]
contributes to the limit.
Changing the variable
y=2-u/n
we get

\frac{(1-b)(1-c)(1-bc)}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{(1+v^{2}-vy)^{-n}}{(1+b^{2}-by)(1+c^{2}-cy)}\sqrt{\frac{2+y}{2-y}}\,{\textnormal{d}}y\\
=\frac{(1-b)(1-c)(1-bc)}{2\pi\sqrt{n}}\int_{0}^{n}\frac{((1-v)^{2}+vu/n)^{-n}}{((1-b)^{2}+bu/n)((1-c)^{2}+cu/n)}\sqrt{\frac{4+u/n}{u}}\,{\textnormal{d}}u\\
\sim\frac{(1-b)(1-c)(1-bc)}{\pi\sqrt{n}(1-v)^{2n}}\int_{0}^{n}\frac{\left(1+\frac{vu}{(1-v)^{2}n}\right)^{-n}}{((1-b)^{2}+bu/n)((1-c)^{2}+cu/n)}\frac{{\textnormal{d}}u}{\sqrt{u}}\\
\sim\frac{1-bc}{\pi\sqrt{n}(1-v)^{2n}(1-b)(1-c)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-vu/(1-v)^{2}}\frac{{\textnormal{d}}u}{\sqrt{u}}=\frac{1-bc}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{v}\sqrt{n}(1-b)(1-c)}\frac{1}{(1-v)^{2n-1}}.

(Here we omitted some details.)

To determine the asymptotic for the remaining cases, we use the generating function

(2.55)

H(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right].

From the discussion at the end of the proof of Proposition
2.6
, we know that

1/\mathfrak{h}_{v}
is analytic on the interior of the ellipse
\gamma_{v}
.
To proceed, we fix
\rho<1/R
such that
|v|<\rho
and
\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]
is well defined. We now consider
|z|<\rho
small enough to ensure convergence of the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n-1}}{(1+v^{2}-vy)^{n}}=\tfrac{1}{(1+v^{2}-vy)}\frac{1}{1-\tfrac{z}{(1+v^{2}-vy)}}=\frac{1}{1-z+v^{2}-vy}=\frac{1}{v}\frac{1}{\tfrac{1-z}{v}+v-y},

uniformly in
y
in the closed region
\bar{D}_{\rho}
enclosed by the ellipse
\gamma_{\rho}
. This series converges for
|z|<(1-v)^{2}
. Indeed, write
y\in\bar{D}_{\rho}
as
y=w+1/w
with
|w|\geq\rho
. Then

|1+v^{2}-vy|=|(1-vw)(1-v/w)|\geq(1-v|w|)(1-v/|w|)=1+v^{2}-v(|w|+1/|w|)\geq 1+v^{2}-2v
as
|w|\leq 1
.

Let
B(z)
be the smaller root of the quadratic equation
B+1/B=\tfrac{1-z}{v}+v
. That is,

B(z)=\mathfrak{u}(\tfrac{1-z}{v}+v)
.
Explicitly,

(2.56)

B(z)=\frac{1+v^{2}-z-\sqrt{\left((1-v)^{2}-z\right)\left((1+v)^{2}-z\right)}}{2v}.

Since
|v|<1/R
and
B(z)\to v
as
z\to 0
, we can choose
\delta>0
with
2\delta<(1-v)^{2}
such that for
|z|<2\delta
, we have
|B(z)|<1/R
and the series (
2.55
) converges.
Thus, noting that

(2.57)

\frac{1}{\tfrac{1-z}{v}+v-y}=\frac{1}{B(z)+1/B(z)-y}=B(z)\frac{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{B(z)}(y)}

we get

H(z)=\frac{B(z)}{v}\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\frac{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{B(z)}}\right].

By Proposition
2.6

we obtain an explicit formula

(2.58)

H(z)=\frac{B(z)}{v}\frac{1-abcB(z)}{(1-aB(z))(1-bB(z))(1-cB(z))}.

To determine the asymptotics, we write

(2.59)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|z|=\delta}\frac{H(z)}{z^{n}}\,{\textnormal{d}}z

and analyze the asymptotics using (
2.58
). Since
|B(z)|<1
, from (
2.58
) we see that the integrand does not have poles when
|a|,|b|,|c|\leq 1
. If a parameter, say
a>1
is real, then there is a pole at

.
This pole is in the disk

, as
z_{a}>0
and

z_{a}-(1-v)^{2}=-\frac{(a-1)^{2}v}{a}<0.

In the following proofs, we further decrease
\delta
so that
\delta<z_{a}
if
a>1
.

Note that if there is another pole
z_{b}
with
1<b<a
then
z_{b}-z_{a}=v\left(a+\frac{1}{a}-b-\frac{1}{b}\right)>0
, so
z_{a}
is closer to the origin.

Proof of (
2.52
): With
a>1
, function
H(z)
given by (
2.58
)
has a simple pole at
z=z_{a}
, at the root of equation

and no other poles in the disk
|z|<(1-v)^{2}
.
Thus

\Psi(z)=(z-z_{a})H(z)
has a series expansion in the disk
|z|<(1-v)^{2}
and

\Psi(z_{a})=\frac{\left(a^{2}-1\right)(bc-1)}{(a-b)(a-c)}.

Inserting the series
\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
and
\frac{1}{z-z_{a}}=\frac{-1}{z_{a}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(z/z_{a})^{j}
into (
2.59
), we get

(2.60)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|z|=\delta}\frac{\Psi(z)}{(z_{a}-z)z^{n}}\,{\textnormal{d}}z=\frac{-1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}z_{a}}\oint_{|z|=\delta}\,\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(z/z_{a})^{j}\,{\textnormal{d}}z\\
=\frac{-1}{z_{a}}\sum_{k+j=n-1}a_{k}/z_{a}^{j}=\frac{-1}{z_{a}^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}z_{a}^{k}\sim\frac{-1}{z_{a}^{n}}\Psi(z_{a})=z_{a}^{-n}\frac{\left(a^{2}-1\right)(1-bc)}{(a-b)(a-c)}.

Proof of (
2.53
): Here
a=b>1
with
0\leq c<a
. Then

(2.61)

H(z)=\frac{B(z)}{v}\frac{1-a^{2}cB(z)}{(1-aB(z))^{2}(1-cB(z))}

has a pole of order 2 at

.

As discussed above, the pole lies within the disk
|z|<(1-v)^{2}
. Since a potential pole
z_{c}
with
c<a

\Psi(z):=(z-z_{a})^{2}H(z)
has a series expansion at
z_{a}

\Psi(z_{a})=\frac{\left(a^{2}-1\right)^{2}v(1-ac)}{a^{2}(a-c)}.

Inserting the series
\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
and
\frac{1}{(z-z_{a})^{2}}=\frac{1}{z_{a}^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)(z/z_{a})^{j}
into (

(2.62)

\mathbb{L}^{\left(a,b,c\right)}\left[\mathfrak{h}_{v}^{-n}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\oint_{|z|=\delta}\frac{\Psi(z)}{(z_{a}-z)^{2}z^{n}}\,{\textnormal{d}}z=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}z_{a}^{2}}\oint_{|z|=\delta}\,\frac{1}{z^{n}}\,(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k})\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)(z/z_{a})^{j}\\
=\frac{1}{z_{a}^{2}}\sum_{k+j=n-1}a_{k}(j+1)/z_{a}^{j}=\frac{1}{z_{a}^{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}(n-k)z_{a}^{k}\\
=\frac{n}{z_{a}^{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}z_{a}^{k}-\frac{1}{z_{a}^{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}ka_{k}z_{a}^{k}\sim\frac{n}{z_{a}^{n+1}}\Psi(z_{a})-o(n/z_{a}^{n}).

do not contribute to the asymptotic of

=\frac{\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{c}_{2}}{\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2}}\;\frac{\mathsf{c}_{1}^{2}-t_{N}^{2}}{\mathsf{c}_{1}^{2}-1}\;\frac{(\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{a})^{N}}{(\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{a}t_{N}^{2})^{N}}\sim\exp\left(\frac{2\mathsf{a}s}{\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{a}}\right),

which ends the proof. (See
[
Mukherjea et al., 2006
, Theorem 2]
.)

Proof of Theorem

Next, we use (

+\frac{\mathsf{c}_{2}^{2}t_{N}^{2}-1}{\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2}-\mathsf{c}_{1})}\;\frac{\mathsf{c}_{2}^{N}}{(\mathsf{c}_{2}-\mathsf{a})^{N}(1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2})^{N}}+\mathbf{1}_{\mathsf{c}_{1}>t_{N}}\frac{\mathsf{c}_{1}^{2}-t_{N}^{2}}{\mathsf{c}_{1}^{2}-\mathsf{c}_{1}\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2}}\;\frac{\mathsf{c}_{1}^{N}}{(1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{1})^{N}(\mathsf{c}_{1}-\mathsf{a}t_{N})^{N}}.

attains its minimum at

(with a constant that does not depend on

Since

, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(1-\mathsf{a}/\mathsf{c}_{2})(1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2})}{(1-\mathsf{a}t_{N})^{2}}=\frac{1+\mathsf{a}^{2}-\mathsf{a}(\mathsf{c}_{2}+1/\mathsf{c}_{2})}{1+\mathsf{a}^{2}-2\mathsf{a}}<1.

So

and the contribution of the integral to the limit of

For

as

and in order to have an atom we must have

\phi_{N}(s)\sim\frac{(\mathsf{c}_{2}^{2}t_{N}^{2}-1)(\mathsf{c}_{2}-\mathsf{c}_{1})}{(\mathsf{c}_{2}^{2}-1)(\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2}-\mathsf{c}_{1})}\left(\frac{1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2}}{1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2}t_{N}^{2}}\right)^{N}\sim e^{2s\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2}/(1-\mathsf{a}\mathsf{c}_{2})}\;\mbox{ as $N\to\infty$},

Stabilities of the Kleitman diameter theorem
Yongjiang Wu, Yongtao Li, Lihua Feng , Jiuqiang Liu, Guihai Yu

Abstract
Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a family of subsets of [ n ] [n] . The diameter of ℱ \mathcal{F} is the maximum size of symmetric differences among pairs of its members. Resolving a conjecture of Erdős, Kleitman determined the maximum size of a family with fixed diameter, which states that a family with diameter s s has cardinality at most that of a Hamming ball of radius s / 2 s/2 . Specifically, if ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} is a family with diameter s s , then for s = 2 ​ d s=2d , | ℱ | ≤ ∑ i = 0 d ( n i ) |\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i} ; for s = 2 ​ d + 1 s=2d+1 , | ℱ | ≤ ∑ i = 0 d ( n i ) + ( n − 1 d ) |\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i}+{n-1\choose d} . This result is known as the Kleitman diameter theorem, which generalizes both the Katona union theorem and the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem. In 2017, Frankl provided a complete characterization of the extremal families of Kleitman’s theorem and provided a stability result. In this paper, we determine the extremal families of Frankl’s theorem and establish a further stability result of Kleitman’s theorem. This solves a recent problem proposed by Li and Wu. Our findings constitute the second stability for the Kleitman diameter theorem.

Introduction

Extremal set theory
is an important topic of combinatorics, and it always deals with the problems on families of subsets of an
n
-element set. One of the central problem is to determine or estimate the maximum size of a family that satisfies certain constraints, including the restricted intersection or union.
To begin with, we fix the following notation.
For two integers
a\leq b
, let
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
, and simply let
[n]=[1,n]
. Let
2^{[n]}
denote the power set of
[n]
. For any
0\leq k\leq n
, let
\binom{[n]}{k}
denote the collection of all
k
-element subsets of
[n]
. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
k
-
uniform
if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
t
-
intersecting
if
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\geq t

for all
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
. If
t=1
,
\mathcal{F}
is simply called
intersecting
. Two families
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are
isomorphic
if there exists a permutation
\sigma
on
[n]
such that
\mathcal{G}=\left\{\{\sigma(x):x\in F\}:F\in\mathcal{F}\right\}
, and we write
\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{G}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
if
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to a subfamily of
\mathcal{G}
.

1.1
Restricted intersection: Erdős-Ko-Rado’s theorem

Erdős, Ko and Rado
[
14
]
determined the maximum possible size of an
intersecting family of
k
-subsets of
[n]
.
This result is one of the cornerstones of extremal set theory,
and since then, a vast number of results
of a similar flavour have been investigated for a range of different mathematical structures; see the surveys
[
13
,
22
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős, Ko and Rado
[
14
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k
be integers. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
be an intersecting family. Then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}.

For
n\geq 2k+1
, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}:x\in F\right\}
for some
x\in[n]
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
that satisfies
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}:x\in F\right\}
is called a
full star
centered at
x
, and
\mathcal{F}
is called
EKR
if

\mathcal{F}
is contained in a full star. Such a family is a “trivial” example of intersecting family. If
\mathcal{F}
is not EKR, then
\cap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{F}
is called
non-trivial
.
In 1967, Hilton and Milner
[
29
]
determined the maximum size of a non-trivial
k
-uniform intersecting family.

Theorem 1.2

(Hilton and Milner
[
29
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k+1
be integers. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
be an intersecting family.
If
\mathcal{F}
is not EKR, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}-\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}+1.

This bound is substantial smaller than the bound
given by Theorem
1.1
. Moreover,
Hilton and Milner identified the extremal families.
Namely, the equality in Theorem
1.2
holds
if and only if

\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{HM}(n,k)
, where
\mathcal{HM}(n,k)\overset{\rm def}{=}\big\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}:1\in F,F\cap[2,k+1]\neq\emptyset\big\}\cup\{[2,k+1]\}
;
and when
k=3
,

\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{HM}(n,3)
or
\mathcal{T}(n,3)
, where
\mathcal{T}(n,3)\overset{\rm def}{=}\big\{F\in\binom{[n]}{3}:|F\cap[3]|\geq 2\big\}
.

In 2017, Han and Kohayakawa
[
27
]
showed a further stability of Theorem
1.2
.
A family
\mathcal{F}
is called
HM
if

\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to a subfamily of
\mathcal{HM}(n,k)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Han and Kohayakawa
[
27
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
n\geq 2k+1
be integers. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
be an intersecting family. If
\mathcal{F}
is neither EKR nor HM, and if
k=3,\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{T}(n,3)
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}-\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}-\binom{n-k-2}{k-2}+2.

Recently, the current authors
[
42
]

provided a unified approach to revisiting
Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.
Apart from the above results,
many stability results (together with a wide variety of different methods) for the
Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem have been obtained in the literature; see,
e.g.,
[
12
,
26
,
30
,
33
,
34
,
37
,
36
,
38
,
40
]
. Moreover, the stability results for
t
-intersecting families are also well-studied in recent years
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
24
]
.
Besides being interesting in their own right, such stability
results can often be served as key ingredients for determining the exact Turán-type results for set families.
In this paper, we shall pay attention to the stability results on the extremal problems for non-uniform families with restricted union and symmetric difference.

1.2
Restricted union: Katona’s theorem

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
s
-
union
if
|F\cup F^{\prime}|\leq s
for all
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
.
Obviously,
\mathcal{F}
is
t
-intersecting if and only if the family
\mathcal{F}^{c}=\{F^{c}=[n]\backslash F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is
(n-t)
-union. Since
|\mathcal{F}^{c}|=|\mathcal{F}|
,
determining the maximum size of a

t
-intersecting family
is equivalent to determining the maximum size of the corresponding
s
-union family.
The maximum size of an
s
-union family on
[n]
can be easily obtained when
s=0,1,n-1
and
n
.
In 1964, Katona
[
32
]
determined the maximum size of
s
-union families for
2\leq s\leq n-2
. (The original statement of Katona
[
32
]
is written under the condition on intersections of sets, instead of the union of sets).

Theorem 1.4

(Katona
[
32
]
)
.

Let
2\leq s\leq n-2
be an integer. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is
s
-union.

(i)

If
s=2d
for an integer
d\geq 1
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}
.

(ii)

If
s=2d+1
for an integer
d\geq 1
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{d}
.

The extremal families achieving the bound are also determined
[
32
]
. Namely,
the equality in (i) holds only for

\mathcal{K}(n,2d)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq d\right\}
;
The equality in (ii) holds only for

\mathcal{K}(n,2d+1)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq d\right\}\cup\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{d+1}:y\in F\right\}
for some
y\in[n]
.
The original proof of Theorem
1.4

primarily relies on a theorem on shadows of intersecting families.
Alternative methods of Katona’s theorem
can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
31
,
41
]
.
For more related generalizations, we refer the interested readers to
[
5
,
20
,
21
,
34
]
and the references therein.

In 2017, Frankl
[
16
]
determined the
sub-optimal
s
-union families for
2\leq s\leq n-2
.

Theorem 1.5

(Frankl
[
16
]
)
.

Let
2\leq s\leq n-2
be an integer. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is
s
-union.

(i)

If
s=2d
for
d\geq 1
and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{K}(n,2d)
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-d-1}{d}+1.

(ii)

If
s=2d+1
for
d\geq 1
and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{K}(n,2d+1)
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{d}-\binom{n-d-2}{d}+1.

The extremal families of Theorem
1.5
are provided as follows.
The equality in (i) holds if and only if there exists
D\in\binom{[n]}{d+1}
such that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}(n,2d)
, where

\mathcal{H}(n,2d)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq d-1\right\}\cup\{D\}\cup\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{d}:F\cap D\neq\emptyset\right\},

or when
s=4
,
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}(n,4)
or
\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)
, where

\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq 1\right\}\cup\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{2}:F\cap[2]\neq\emptyset\right\}\cup\big\{\{1,2,i\}:i\in[3,n]\big\}.

The equality in (ii) holds if and only if there exist
D\in\binom{[n]}{d+1}
and
y\in[n]\backslash D
such that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}(n,2d+1)
, where

\mathcal{H}(n,2d+1)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq d\right\}\cup\{D\}\cup\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{d+1}:y\in F,F\cap D\neq\emptyset\right\},

or when
s=5
,
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}(n,5)
or
\mathcal{T}(n,5)
, where

\mathcal{T}(n,5)\overset{\rm def}{=}\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq 2\right\}\cup\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{3}:|F\cap[3]|\geq 2\right\}.

Note that there are two possibilities in the cases
s=4
and
5
.

In 2024, Li and Wu
[
39
]
further determined the third optimal
s
-union families.

Theorem 1.6

(Li and Wu
[
39
]
)
.

Let
4\leq s\leq n-2
be an integer. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is an
s
-union family such that
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{K}(n,s)
and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{H}(n,s)
.
Then the following holds.

(i)

If
s=2d
for
d\geq 2
and further
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)
for the case
s=4
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-d-1}{d}-\binom{n-d-2}{d-1}+2.

(ii)

If
s=2d+1
for
d\geq 2
and further
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{T}(n,5)
for the case
s=5
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{d}-\binom{n-d-2}{d}-\binom{n-d-3}{d-1}+2.

For
s=2
, we shall mention that a
2
-union family must be a subfamily of
\mathcal{K}(n,2)
or
\mathcal{H}(n,2)
. For
s=3
, if a
3
-union family
\mathcal{F}
is neither a subfamily of
\mathcal{K}(n,3)
nor of
\mathcal{H}(n,3)
,
then
|\mathcal{F}|
is maximized by

\left\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{b\},\{c\},\{a,b,c\}\right\}
. Moreover, the extremal families attaining the above bounds are characterized in
[
39
]
. Here, we do not describe them for simplicity.

1.3
Restricted diameter: Kleitman’s theorem

For convenience of readers,
the following notations are consistent with the previous work
[
17
]
.
Let
A,B
be two subsets of
[n]
. The
symmetric difference
of
A
and
B
is defined as

A+B:=(A\backslash B)\cup(B\backslash A).

Sometimes,
2^{[n]}
is considered as a metric space with the distance
d(A,B)=|A+B|
for every
A,B\subseteq[n]
.
The
diameter
of
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, denoted by
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
, is defined as
{\rm max}\{d(A,B):A,B\in\mathcal{F}\}
.
For
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
and
S\subseteq[n]
, the
translation
of
\mathcal{F}
by
S
is defined as

\mathcal{F}+S:=\{F+S:F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Note that
|\mathcal{F}+S|=|\mathcal{F}|
and
\Delta(\mathcal{F}+S)=\Delta(\mathcal{F})
.

In 1966, Kleitman
[
35
]
proved the following theorem originally conjectured by Erdős
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.7

(Kleitman
[
35
]
)
.

Suppose that
2\leq s\leq n-2
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
.

(i)

If
s=2d
for
d\geq 1
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}.

(ii)

If
s=2d+1
for
d\geq 1
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{d}.

Since

|A+B|\leq|A\cup B|
, the
s
-union assumption on
\mathcal{F}
implies
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
.
Thus, Theorem
1.7

extends Theorem
1.4
.
The combinatorial proof of Kleitman
[
35
]
transfers Theorem
1.7
into Theorem
1.4
by using the so-called down-shift operation (see Subsection
3.2
).
In 2020, Huang, Klurman and Pohoata
[
31
]
presented an elegant algebraic proof by the Cvetković spectral bound on independence number, and they also showed several extensions and generalizations of Theorem
1.7
to other allowed distance sets consisting of consecutive integers.
The Kleitman theorem can be viewed as an isodiametric inequality for discrete hypercubes. Many generalizations have been considered, such as the
n
-dimensional grid
[m]^{n}
with Hamming distance
[
2
,
18
]
, as well as

[m]^{n}
and the
n
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with Manhattan
distance
[
1
,
8
,
11
]
.

In 2017, Frankl
[
17
]
proved the following result, which is a diameter extension of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.8

(Frankl
[
17
]
)
.

Let
2\leq s\leq n-2
be an integer. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
. Then the only family achieving the equality of Theorem
1.7
is the translation of
Katona’s family
\mathcal{K}(n,s)
. Moreover, the following stability result holds.

(i)

If
s=2d
and
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,2d)
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-d-1}{d}+1.

(ii)

If
s=2d+1
and
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,2d+1)
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq d}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{d}-\binom{n-d-2}{d}+1.

Theorem
1.8
is the first level stability of Kleitman’s theorem. Frankl’s proof of Theorem
1.8
is combinatorial and it is mainly based on the use of his previous result of Theorem
1.5
. In 2023, Gao, Liu and Xu
[
25
]
gave a stability result for Kleitman’s theorem in the case
s=2d+1
by a linear algebra method.

Theorem 1.9

(Gao, Liu and Xu
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family with
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq 2d+1
. Then

(i)

either
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n\choose i}-2{n-5d-1\choose d}
,

(ii)

or
\mathcal{F}
is contained in some translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,2d+2)
. Furthermore, in this case,

•

either
\mathcal{F}
is contained in some translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,2d+1)
,

•

or
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{d}{n-1\choose i}-{n-d-2\choose d}+1
.

2
Main results

This paper establishes an affirmative answer to Problem
1.10
. To achieve this, our primary challenge lies in characterizing the extremal configurations of Theorem
1.8
, which serves as a crucial stepping stone toward the complete solution.
Prior to detailing our main theorems, we first introduce essential notations.

•

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer,
R\in\binom{[n]}{d+2}
and
y\in R
. Define

\mathcal{R}(n,2d)=\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq d-2\right\}\cup\{R\}\cup\left\{F\subseteq[n]:y\in F,|F|=d-1\text{ or }d\right\}\cup\left\{F\subseteq[n]:y\notin F,F\cap R\neq\emptyset,|F|=d-1\text{ or }d\right\}.

•

For any
y\in[3,n]
, define

\mathcal{R}^{*}(n,4)=\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq 1\}\cup\{\{1,2\},\{1,y\},\{2,y\}\}\cup\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|=3,y\in F,F\cap[2]\neq\emptyset\}\cup\{\{1,2,i\}:i\in[3,n]\}

and

\mathcal{U}^{*}(n,4)=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{y\}\}\cup\{\{y,i\},i\in[n]\backslash\{y\}\}\cup\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|=2,y\notin F,F\cap[2]\neq\emptyset\}\cup\{\{1,2,y\}\}\cup\{\{1,2,y,i\}:i\in[n]\backslash\{1,2,y\}\}.

Consider a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with diameter
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
. The structural properties of
\mathcal{F}
exhibit fundamentally different behaviors across different ranges of
s
:

•

Trivial cases: For
s\in\{0,1\}
, the family
\mathcal{F}
is necessarily contained in a translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,0)
and
\mathcal{K}(n,1)
, respectively. For
s\in\{n-1,n\}
, we immediately obtain the cardinality bounds
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2^{n-1}
and
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2^{n}
.

•

Non-trivial cases: The case
s=2
can be derived from Theorem
1.8
. For
s\in[3,n-2]
,
the case becomes considerably more intricate
and presents substantial technical challenges.
These cases require sophisticated combinatorial analysis to establish the desired characterization.

Recall that
\mathcal{K}(n,2)=\left\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|\leq 1\right\}
and
\mathcal{H}(n,2)=\left\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\right\}

for
a\neq b\in[n]
. Let
c\in[n]\backslash\{a,b\}
and
\mathcal{V}(n,2)=\left\{\{a\},\{b\},\{c\},\{a,b,c\}\right\}.
Our first result studies the families with diameter at most two.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq 2
. Then
\mathcal{F}
must be contained in a translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,2)
,
\mathcal{H}(n,2)
or
\mathcal{V}(n,2)
.

The following theorem characterizes the extremal families of Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
3\leq s\leq n-2
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translation of
\mathcal{K}(n,s)
.
Then the following three statements hold.

(i)

For
s=3
, the only family achieving equality of Theorem
1.8
is the translation of
\mathcal{H}(n,3)
.

(ii)

For
s=4
, the families achieving equality of Theorem
1.8
are the translations of
\mathcal{H}(n,4)
,
\mathcal{R}(n,4)
,
\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)
,
\mathcal{R}^{*}(n,4)
,
\mathcal{U}^{*}(n,4)
.
For
s=2d
(
d\geq 3
), the families achieving equality of Theorem
1.8
are the translations of
\mathcal{H}(n,2d)
and
\mathcal{R}(n,2d)
.

(iii)

For
s=5
, the families achieving equality of Theorem
1.8
are the translations of
\mathcal{H}(n,5)
and
\mathcal{T}(n,5)
. For
s=2d+1
(
d\geq 3
), the only family achieving equality in Theorem
1.8

3.2.1
Down-shift operator

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family and
j\in[n]
. The
down-shift operation

S_{j}
, also known as the squashing operation, discovered by Kleitman
[
35
]
, is defined as follows:

S_{j}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{S_{j}(F):F\in\mathcal{F}\right\},

where

S_{j}(F)=\begin{cases}F\backslash\{j\}&\text{if }j\in F\text{ and }F\backslash\{j\}\notin\mathcal{F},\\
F&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Observe that
S_{y}(\mathcal{R}(n,2d))=\mathcal{H}(n,2d)
,
S_{y}(\mathcal{R}^{*}(n,4))=\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)
,
S_{y}(\mathcal{U}^{*}(n,4))=\mathcal{H}^{*}(n,4)
and
S_{c}(\mathcal{V}(n,2))=\mathcal{H}(n,2)
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called a
complex
if
E\subseteq F\in\mathcal{F}
implies that
E\in\mathcal{F}
. If we start with a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfying
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, by repeatedly applying the down-shift operations

to
\mathcal{F}
for each

, then we end up with a complex family.

The following lemma plays a crucial role in our subsequent proofs.

Lemma 3.1

(Kleitman
[
35
]
; Frankl
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family and
j\in[n]
. Then

|S_{j}(\mathcal{F})|=|\mathcal{F}|
and
\Delta\left(S_{j}(\mathcal{F})\right)\leq\Delta(\mathcal{F})
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
is a complex, then
|E\cup F|\leq|\Delta(\mathcal{F})|
for all
E,F\in\mathcal{F}
.

5
Concluding remarks

Extending the stability of Erdős-Ko-Rado’s theorem, Han and Kohayakawa
[
27
]
, Kostochka and Mubayi
[
36
]
, Kupavskii
[
37
]
, Huang and Peng
[
30
]
and Frankl and Wang
[
23
]
recently investigated a series of stability results for the
k
-uniform intersecting families by exploring different structural parameters.
In particular, solving a question of Han and Kohayakawa
[
27
]
,
Huang and Peng
[
30
]
completely established the third full stability for Erdős-Ko-Rado’s theorem. Recently,
Ge, Xu and Zhao
[
26
]
developed a linear algebraic method to show the stability result at the
t
-th level for any integer
4\leq t\leq k-2
under a slightly stronger condition
n>3.618k
.

Along with the motivation of Han and Kahayakawa
[
27
]
, the second author and Wu
[
39
]
presented the second level stability of Katona’s theorem for non-uniform families with restricted union. In this paper, we answered a problem proposed in
[
39
]
, and we established the second level stability for Kleitman’s diameter theorem; see Figure
1
.
In light of the aforementioned works on intersecting families,
it is also an interesting and challenging problem to show the
t
-th level stability of Kleitman’s diameter theorem for any integer
t
.
Different from the classical problem on intersecting families, the problem on families with restricted diameter is relatively more difficult and complicated since the families we considered are non-uniform.
We believe that some efficient ideas and methods should be developed to avoid the complicated case analysis for
establishing the
t
-th stability of Kleitman’s diameter theorem.

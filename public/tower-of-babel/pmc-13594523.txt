Cxcl9 as a Marker of Adipose Tissue Changes Caused by Bariatric Surgery and Psoriasis
Authors: Zheng Zhou, Ling Kuang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBariatric surgery has been shown to improve psoriasis, but the underlying molecular mechanisms remain unclear. This study aimed to investigate shared molecular mechanisms between bariatric surgery-induced adipose changes and psoriasis using bioinformatics approaches.MethodsTranscriptomic data from adipose tissue before and after bariatric surgery and from psoriatic lesions were obtained from the GEO database. Common differentially expressed genes (co-DEGs) were identified and subjected to GO/KEGG enrichment analysis, protein–protein interaction network construction, and machine learning-based hub gene selection. Diagnostic performance and immune infiltration characteristics were evaluated.ResultsCo-DEGs were significantly enriched in the IL-17 signaling pathway, defense response to bacterium, response to lipopolysaccharide, cytolysis, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix. CXCL9 was identified as a key hub gene via multiple machine learning algorithms, showing good diagnostic performance in external validation (AUC > 0.85). Immune infiltration analysis revealed that CXCL9 expression was positively correlated with M1 macrophage and CD8⁺ T cell abundance, and negatively correlated with M2 macrophages.ConclusionCXCL9, through its involvement in the IL-17 signaling pathway and lipopolysaccharide response, may serve as a critical bridge linking adipose tissue inflammation and psoriatic skin immunity, providing a novel molecular target for the improvement of psoriasis following bariatric surgery.Keywords: bariatric surgery, psoriasis, CXCL9, adipose–skin axis

Introduction

Psoriasis is an immune-mediated chronic, relapsing inflammatory disease, obesity is widely recognized as an important independent risk factor for psoriasis.1,2 Obesity changes the microenvironment of adipose tissue, leading to massive infiltration of various immune cells (eg CD8+ T cells, Th17 cells), further amplifying inflammatory signals and providing a “ground” that favors the development and progression of psoriasis.3 Thus, there is a complex two-way pathophysiological relationship between obesity and psoriasis. Bariatric surgery, including sleeve gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, is currently the most effective and permanent intervention for morbid obesity.

Bariatric surgery profoundly affects adipose tissue and the immune system through the following mechanisms: 1. Intensive remodeling of adipose tissue: Postoperatively, adipocyte size is significantly reduced and adipose tissue passes through fibrotic degradation and angiogenesis.4 More importantly, the composition of immune cells within adipose tissue undergoes a fundamental change: the phenotype of macrophages switches from proinflammatory M1-dominant to antiinflammatory M2-dominant, and regulatory T cells increase. This “resetting” of the immune microenvironment from proinflammatory to anti-inflammatory is thought to be key to improving psoriasis;5 2. Improved adipokine profile: After surgery, levels of proinflammatory factors in plasma (eg TNF-α, IL-6, C-reactive protein) significantly decrease, while anti-inflammatory factors (eg adiponectin) increase.6 These systemic improvements directly affect the pathological process of psoriasis.

Among the chemokine pathways implicated in both conditions, the CXCL9/CXCR3 axis is of particular interest. CXCL9 is an interferon-γ-inducible chemokine that recruits CXCR3-expressing Th1 cells, CD8⁺ T cells, and natural killer cells to inflamed tissues. In psoriasis, CXCL9 is markedly upregulated in lesional skin and promotes the epidermal recruitment of pathogenic T cells, while blockade of the CXCL9/CXCR3 axis attenuates psoriasiform inflammation in preclinical models. In parallel, CXCL9 and its receptor have been implicated in adipose tissue inflammation and macrophage polarization in obesity, suggesting that this axis may act at the interface of metabolic and cutaneous immunity. In the present study, CXCL9 emerged as the top-ranked hub gene across multiple machine learning algorithms and was positively correlated with M1 macrophage and CD8⁺ T cell infiltration, supporting its role as a molecular bridge between adipose inflammation and psoriatic immunity.7

Although clinical observations have consistently shown that bariatric surgery ameliorates psoriasis severity, the molecular basis of this phenomenon remains elusive. Previous studies have characterized the obesity–psoriasis link largely at the epidemiological level or within single datasets, focusing either on adipose tissue inflammation or on psoriatic skin immunity in isolation.8–11 Critically, no study has systematically integrated transcriptomic profiles of adipose tissue before and after bariatric surgery with those of psoriatic lesions to identify shared molecular mediators of the adipose–skin axis. To address this gap, the present study, to our knowledge, is the first to identify common differentially expressed genes across these two tissue contexts, and to combine enrichment analysis, protein–protein interaction networks, and multiple machine learning algorithms to pinpoint robust hub genes, whose diagnostic performance was further confirmed in independent external cohorts. This integrative design distinguishes our work from prior single-dataset bioinformatics analyses and provides a mechanistic framework for understanding how adipose tissue remodeling after bariatric surgery may translate into cutaneous immune improvement. Therefore, this study aims to systematically investigate and integrate transcriptomic data from adipose tissue before and after bariatric surgery and from psoriatic lesions using bioinformatics methods, with the following goals: 1. Identify differentially expressed genes common to both conditions (co-DEGs) as potential molecular bridges; 2. To investigate the biological processes and signaling pathways in which these common genes are involved, with a special focus on pathways related to the immune system; 3. Check key hub genes using PPI networks and machine learning methods and validate their diagnostic efficiency for psoriasis or bariatric outcomes; 4. Construct regulatory networks of transcription factor (TF)-hub genes and PPI-mRNA in order to discover upstream regulatory mechanisms; 5. To perform immune infiltration analysis to investigate the relationship between hub gene expression and the number of immune cells in adipose tissue and psoriatic lesions, and ultimately to elucidate potential ways of modulating the immune microenvironment.

Respondents and Methods

Data Acquisition and Curation

Transcriptomic profiles pertaining to bariatric surgery interventions and psoriatic pathology were systematically retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository. Eligible GEO datasets were identified using the keywords “bariatric surgery adipose tissue” and “psoriasis”. Inclusion criteria were: (1) transcriptomic profiling of subcutaneous adipose tissue before and after bariatric surgery, or of lesional and non-lesional skin from psoriasis patients; (2) availability of platform annotation; (3) at least three samples per group. Datasets lacking normalized or raw expression data were excluded. Specifically, datasets GSE72158 and GSE59034 characterize metabolic alterations in human subcutaneous adipose tissue; these cohorts encompass 42 individuals assessed at two distinct temporal intervals (baseline and one-year post-operation), alongside a matched subset of 16 obese subjects evaluated both pre- and post-surgically. Regarding cutaneous inflammation, datasets GSE78097 and GSE34248 provide comprehensive sampling, including biopsies from mild (n=14) and severe (n=13) plaque psoriasis lesions, healthy control skin (n=6), as well as paired lesional and non-lesional specimens from 14 psoriatic patients. To ensure robustness, GSE59034 (bariatric) and GSE34248 (psoriasis) were designated as external validation cohorts. Platform heterogeneity was noted: GSE78097 and GSE34248 utilized the GPL570 array, whereas GSE72158 and GSE59034 were generated on the GPL10588 and GPL11532 platforms, respectively. Preliminary Data Processing and Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs)

Raw microarray datasets were imported into the R statistical environment (version 4.4.1). Sample expression profiles were annotated using corresponding platform files, wherein probe identifiers were mapped to gene symbols to convert the probe-level matrix into a gene-level matrix. In instances where a single probe matched multiple gene names, only the foremost gene entry was retained. Batch effects and technical variations were corrected utilizing the `normalizeBetweenArrays` function within the “limma” package. The distribution of standardized expression values across samples is illustrated via boxplots in Figure 2a and b, while principal component analysis (PCA) results are depicted in Figure 2c and d.

Normalized expression matrices. Box plots (A and B) and PCA diagrams (C and D) of the GSE72158 and GSE78097 datasets.

Abbreviation: PCA, principal component analysis.

Grouping metadata for both disease cohorts were established. Differential expression analysis was subsequently performed on the bariatric surgery and psoriasis transcriptomic matrices separately using the “limma” pipeline. This workflow involved constructing a design matrix, fitting linear models, and applying empirical Bayes moderation to identify significant DEGs for each condition. To refine the candidate gene list, we applied stringent filtering criteria: an adjusted P-value < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 0.585.

Visualization of the resulting DEGs was achieved through heatmaps and volcano plots generated by the “pheatmap” package. Furthermore, to pinpoint shared molecular mechanisms, a Venn diagram was constructed using the “VennDiagram” package to isolate the intersection of DEGs between the two diseases. These common co-expressed genes constituted the core dataset for all downstream analyses.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Enrichment Assessments

To annotate the human genome, we employed the “org.Hs.eg.db” R package to convert gene symbols from the co-expressed differentially expressed genes (DEGs)—identified in the preceding differential analysis of both diseases—into ENTREZ IDs. Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed on these shared DEGs using the “ClusterProfiler” package. Statistical significance was defined by thresholds of adjusted p-value < 0.05 and q-value < 0.05. Visualization of the functional enrichment outcomes was achieved via the “ggplot2”, “pathview”, and “enrichplot” packages. Finally, we utilized the “cnetplot” function within the “BiocManager” ecosystem to isolate and graphically represent the top four GO terms and the top eight KEGG pathways.

Construction of the Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) Network

The overlapping set of co-expressed differentially expressed genes was submitted to the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org) to map inter-genic associations. A confidence threshold of 0.4 was applied to filter interactions, generating a preliminary PPI network that was subsequently exported for downstream analysis. Visualization and topological interrogation were performed using Cytoscape software; within this framework, the fifteen most significant hub genes were identified based on the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) algorithm.

Identification of Feature Genes via Machine Learning Approaches

Fifteen top-ranked co-expressed genes, initially prioritized by the Maximum Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm, underwent further refinement to pinpoint critical feature genes associated with bariatric surgery and psoriasis. This selection process employed two distinct machine learning models: Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and Random Forest (RF). Specifically, LASSO and RF were applied independently to identify disease-specific key markers for each condition. Subsequently, the overlapping genes common to both diseases were determined by intersecting the resulting gene sets using Venn diagram analysis.

Assessment of Diagnostic Utility for Signature Genes via External Cohorts

The diagnostic potential of pivotal signature genes, identified through machine-learning frameworks across both pathologies, was quantified using the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC). This metric spans from 0.5 to 1.0, wherein values approaching unity signify superior discriminatory power and enhanced gene-specific accuracy. Independent validation cohorts were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO): GSE59034 for bariatric surgery (comprising 16 cases and 16 controls) and GSE34248 for psoriasis (consisting of 14 cases and 14 controls). Leveraging the “pROC” and “ggplot2” R packages, we generated ROC curves for these critical genes in both conditions, computing AUC statistics alongside their corresponding 95% confidence intervals to substantiate diagnostic efficacy. Furthermore, violin plots coupled with Student’s t-tests were constructed to corroborate the diagnostic relevance of these signature genes within the primary training datasets, GSE72158 and GSE78097.

Elucidation of Gene-Gene and Transcription Factor-Gene Regulatory Architectures

To dissect the functional interplay among identified biomarkers—defined here as pivotal characteristic genes—we leveraged the GeneMANIA web resource to map their underlying gene-gene interaction networks. Concurrently, we reconstructed the transcription factor (TF)-gene regulatory landscape by integrating data from the JASPAR repository (https://jaspar.genereg.net/) within the NetworkAnalyst framework. Subsequently, to pinpoint the most influential regulatory hubs, we applied the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm via the cytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape, thereby isolating the core transcription factors driving the observed regulatory dynamics.

Immune Cell Infiltration Profiling

Expression matrices for 45 co-expressed differentially expressed genes were extracted from the bariatric surgery cohort (GSE72158) and the psoriasis validation dataset (GSE78097). We quantified immune cell infiltration levels within these two disease cohorts utilizing the “CIBERSORT” algorithm implemented in R. Visualization of these compositional data was achieved through bar charts, box plots, and heatmaps generated via the “ggplot2” package. Furthermore, we computed correlation coefficients and corresponding P-values linking target genes to specific immune subsets using the “cor.test” function, with the resulting associations depicted as lollipop plots.

Results

Identification and Screening of Differentially Expressed Genes in Two Distinct Pathologies

Analysis of the bariatric surgery cohort (GSE72158), comprising 42 diseased and 42 control specimens, yielded 157 differentially expressed genes (DEGs); specifically, 43 transcripts exhibited upregulation, whereas 114 demonstrated downregulation (Figure 3a and b). Regarding the psoriasis dataset (GSE78097), which included 27 patient samples alongside 6 healthy controls, we identified a total of 3,060 DEGs, consisting of 1,721 upregulated and 1,339 downregulated genes. These findings were derived using stringent criteria (P < 0.05; |log2 fold change| > 1) and are visually represented via volcano plots in Figure 3c and d. Subsequent Venn diagram analysis of the overlapping gene sets from both conditions revealed 45 co-expressed DEGs (Figure 4a).

Our Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment assessment revealed distinct functional patterns among the co-expressed differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Regarding biological processes, these transcripts were predominantly associated with the IL-17 signaling cascade, antibacterial defense mechanisms, and the modulation of epithelial cell proliferation. Furthermore, significant enrichment was observed for responses triggered by bacterial constituents, specifically lipopolysaccharides, alongside cytolysis-related activities (Figure 4c–f). In terms of cellular component localization, the identified gene set showed a marked concentration within the collagen-rich extracellular matrix (Figure 4g and h).

To identify potential candidate genes, we generated a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network comprising 45 co-expressed genes via the STRING database. Subsequently, the Maximum Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm was applied to rank node importance, yielding the top 15 hub genes (Figure 5a).

Feature Genes Were Identified Through the Application of Machine Learning Methodologies

Specifically, we conducted further refinement on the top 15 candidate genes associated with bariatric surgery and psoriasis, respectively, utilizing two distinct algorithms: Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and Random Forest (RF). Following LASSO-based screening for bariatric surgery (Figure 5b and c) and psoriasis (Figure 5d and e), the overlapping set revealed a single characteristic gene: CXCL9. Subsequently, RF analysis was performed under the threshold of Mean Decrease Gini > 0.8 (or alternatively set to 1) for both conditions—bariatric surgery (Figure 5f) and psoriasis (Figure 5g). The intersection derived via Venn diagram analysis yielded eleven characteristic genes: AQP9, HMOX1, CCND1, MMP9, CXCL9, S100A9, HAVCR2, ANG, IRF8, APOE, and SPP1. Finally, by intersecting the gene sets obtained from both machine learning approaches, we isolated one pivotal characteristic gene: CXCL9 (Figure 5h).

External Validation of Diagnostic Performance for Candidate Genes

To rigorously assess the diagnostic utility of identified signature genes, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using independent external cohorts, with the area under the curve (AUC) serving as the primary metric for evaluation. In the bariatric surgery dataset (GSE59034), CXCL9 demonstrated robust discriminatory power, yielding an AUC of 0.902 (95% CI: 0.773–1.000; Figure 6a). Similarly, analysis of the psoriasis cohort (GSE34248) confirmed the strong diagnostic potential of CXCL9, with an AUC of 0.908 (95% CI: 0.779–1.000; Figure 6b). Consequently, CXCL9 was identified as a shared biomarker exhibiting significant diagnostic efficacy (AUC > 0.7) for both pathologies. Furthermore, differential expression analyses visualized via violin plots in the training sets—GSE72158 for bariatric surgery (Figure 6c) and GSE78097 for psoriasis (Figure 6d)—corroborated these findings, revealing statistically significant distinctions (p < 0.05) that underscore the clinical relevance of this biomarker.

Elucidation of Gene-Gene and Transcription Factor-Gene Regulatory Networks

To dissect the functional interplay among identified biomarkers, we leveraged the GeneMANIA database to construct a comprehensive gene-gene interaction network. As illustrated in Figure 7a, this visualization maps the protein-protein associations linked to our biomarkers, highlighting neighboring genes that exhibit the most frequent co-variation. Notably, CXCL9 emerges as a central hub within a intricate web of interactions, connecting with adjacent shared genes through diverse mechanisms including co-expression, physical binding, predictive modeling, genetic interplay, co-localization, pathway participation, and shared protein domain architecture. Functionally, these interactions are predominantly enriched in processes governing cytokine activity, chemokine receptor signaling, cellular responses to chemokines, as well as the migration and chemotaxis of neutrophils and granulocytes. These findings further underscore the pivotal role of immune cell-mediated inflammation in the pathogenesis of both diseases, positioning CXCL9 as a potential master regulator driving their developmental mechanisms. Furthermore, to identify upstream regulatory drivers, we employed the NetworkAnalyst platform to generate a transcription factor (TF)-gene interaction network (Figure 7b). By applying the Maximum Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm via the cytoHubba plugin, we pinpointed critical regulatory nodes. Our analysis ultimately validated FOXC1, YY1, EN1, MEF2A, and NFIC as the primary transcription factors orchestrating the expression of these key signature genes (Figure 7c).

Correlation Analysis of Key Characteristic Genes and Immune Cell Infiltration

Functional enrichment profiling of co-expressed genes revealed a pronounced accumulation within the IL-17 signaling cascade. This observation suggests that the identified gene set plays a pivotal role in modulating immune responses and inflammatory mechanisms. To delineate variations in immune cell infiltration between case and control cohorts across both pathologies, we quantified the proportional composition of 22 distinct immune cell populations for each sample using the CIBERSORT algorithm (Figure 8a–d). Comparative analysis indicated that, relative to healthy controls, both bariatric surgery patients and individuals with psoriasis exhibited a concordant elevation in Macrophages and CD4+ T cells. Specifically, within the psoriasis dataset, the case group displayed significant upregulation of follicular helper T cells, M0/M1 macrophages, and activated dendritic cells (p < 0.05), alongside marked downregulation of activated NK cells, resting mast cells, and M2 macrophages (p < 0.05). Conversely, in the bariatric surgery cohort, cases showed significant increases in γδ T cells and activated NK cells (p < 0.05).

Discussion

This study represents the first systematic bioinformatics investigation aimed at deciphering the shared molecular landscape between bariatric surgery-induced adipose tissue remodeling and psoriasis. By integrating transcriptomic data from both conditions, we identified a robust set of common differentially expressed genes (co-DEGs) that were significantly enriched in the IL-17 signaling pathway, the defense response to bacterium, the response to lipopolysaccharide (LPS), cytolysis, regulation of epithelial cell proliferation, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix. Through a rigorous multi-step screening pipeline that combined protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis with three distinct machine learning algorithms (LASSO, random forest, and SVM-RFE), we pinpointed CXCL9 as a pivotal hub gene. Its diagnostic performance, validated in external cohorts with area under the curve (AUC) values consistently exceeding 0.85, underscores its potential as a cross-disease biomarker. Moreover, immune infiltration analysis using CIBERSORT revealed that CXCL9 expression was strongly positively correlated with the abundance of pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages and CD8⁺ T cells, while being negatively correlated with anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages. These findings collectively provide a molecular rationale for the well-documented clinical observation that weight loss achieved through bariatric surgery can lead to significant amelioration of psoriatic inflammation.

The co-DEGs were heavily enriched in the IL-17 signaling pathway and the response to LPS, which together form a mechanistic bridge linking obesity-associated adipose inflammation with psoriatic skin pathology. The IL-17 pathway is the cornerstone of psoriasis immunopathogenesis: IL-17A, secreted primarily by Th17 cells, γδ T cells, and type 3 innate lymphoid cells, acts on keratinocytes to induce the production of chemokines such as CXCL8 and CCL20, antimicrobial peptides, and matrix metalloproteinases, thereby promoting neutrophil infiltration, epidermal hyperplasia, and dermal remodeling.12 In parallel, obesity is characterized by a state of chronic low-grade inflammation; adipose tissue-resident immune cells, including γδ T cells, are known sources of IL-17, and their abundance correlates with the degree of obesity and insulin resistance.13 A seminal study by Winer et al demonstrated that IL-17 neutralization in obese mice reduces adipose tissue inflammation and improves metabolic parameters.14 The convergence of these pathways in our analysis suggests that bariatric surgery, by reducing total fat mass and altering the adipose immune landscape, may systemically lower IL-17 levels, thereby interrupting a key inflammatory driver shared by both conditions. The enrichment of “defense response to bacterium” and “response to lipopolysaccharide” adds another critical dimension. Obesity is closely linked to increased intestinal permeability and metabolic endotoxemia, where circulating LPS derived from gut Gram-negative bacteria activates Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) on adipocytes and adipose-resident macrophages, triggering a cascade of pro-inflammatory cytokine release including IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β.15 This LPS-driven inflammation is further amplified by the presence of CD14⁺ monocytes and macrophages that respond to LPS via the MD-2/TLR4 complex. Similarly, in psoriasis, skin barrier dysfunction allows microbial products such as LPS to penetrate the epidermis and activate TLR4 on keratinocytes, leading to increased production of IL-17, IL-23, and antimicrobial peptides (eg, β-defensins), thus perpetuating the inflammatory cycle.16 Our data support the hypothesis that bariatric surgery may break this vicious loop by reducing systemic endotoxemia, improving gut barrier integrity, and attenuating the LPS-TLR4 signaling axis in both adipose tissue and skin. The enrichment of “cytolysis” likely reflects the cytotoxic activities of natural killer (NK) cells, CD8⁺ T cells, and neutrophils, which are prominent in both inflamed adipose tissue (where they contribute to adipocyte death and release of damage-associated molecular patterns) and psoriatic lesions (where they mediate keratinocyte apoptosis and secrete perforin and granzymes).17 Intriguingly, recent single-cell studies have identified a population of CD8⁺ tissue-resident memory T cells that co-express granzyme B and CXCL9 in both obese adipose tissue and psoriatic skin, further supporting a shared cytotoxic program.18

Equally compelling is the enrichment of “collagen-containing extracellular matrix” and “regulation of epithelial cell proliferation,” which highlights overlapping tissue remodeling processes. In psoriasis, excessive keratinocyte proliferation leads to acanthosis, while dermal fibroblasts produce collagen and fibronectin, contributing to the thickened and fibrotic dermis characteristic of chronic plaques.19 Simultaneously, matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) such as MMP-1, MMP-3, and MMP-9 are upregulated in psoriatic lesions, driving matrix turnover and angiogenesis.20 In obese adipose tissue, pathological fibrosis is a hallmark: pericellular collagen deposition by activated fibroblasts and adipocyte progenitors constrains adipocyte expansion, leading to cellular stress, hypoxia, and lipotoxicity.21 The resulting fibrotic microenvironment promotes the recruitment and activation of pro-inflammatory macrophages. Bariatric surgery is known to reverse this fibrotic phenotype within months: a longitudinal biopsy study by Fieldborg et al demonstrated decreased expression of collagen genes (COL1A1, COL6A3) and reduced fibrosis area in subcutaneous adipose tissue after weight loss.22 Our findings suggest that the molecular programs governing extracellular matrix remodeling are not tissue-specific but rather reflect a systemic shift in the balance between matrix synthesis and degradation. The gene shared between the two conditions likely include both matrix-degrading enzymes (eg, MMP9, MMP13) and matrix-remodeling regulators (eg, TIMP1, LOX). On the epithelial proliferation front, the identification of genes such as KRT16 (commonly upregulated in psoriasis) in the co-DEG set may indicate that similar proliferative signaling pathways—such as the STAT3 and MAPK cascades—are activated both in psoriatic keratinocytes and in adipose progenitor cells during pathologic remodeling.23 Thus, the “epithelial” context may extend to the adipocyte progenitor niche.

Our analysis identified CXCL9 as the most robust hub gene, a finding with multifaceted functional implications. CXCL9 (monokine induced by gamma interferon, MIG) is a CXC chemokine that binds exclusively to the receptor CXCR3, which is expressed on activated Th1 cells, CD8⁺ T cells, NK cells, and a subset of plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Its expression is tightly regulated by interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) through the JAK-STAT1 signaling pathway, and to a lesser extent by TNF-α and LPS via NF-κB.24 In the context of obesity, numerous studies have demonstrated that obese adipose tissue harbors an increased number of CD8⁺ T cells, which secrete IFN-γ and thereby stimulate adipocytes, preadipocytes, and stromal vascular cells to produce CXCL9.25 Elevated CXCL9 in turn recruits more CXCR3⁺ CD8⁺ T cells and Th1-polarized CD4⁺ T cells, establishing a positive feedback loop that perpetuates IFN-γ production and M1 macrophage polarization. Intriguingly, single-cell RNA-sequencing of human adipose tissue has revealed that CXCL9 is predominantly expressed by a subset of pro-inflammatory macrophages (CD68⁺CD163−) and by CD34⁺ adipose progenitor cells under IFN-γ stimulation.26 Bariatric surgery, by reducing total T cell infiltration and IFN-γ levels, effectively disrupts this loop, leading to a steep decline in CXCL9 expression within the adipose tissue. In parallel, within psoriatic skin, the same CXCL9/CXCR3 axis is heavily engaged: immunohistochemical staining shows CXCL9 deposition in the dermal papillae and CXCR3⁺ T cells clustering around blood vessels.27 Serum CXCL9 levels correlate positively with PASI score and can predict treatment response to anti-TNF-α therapy.28 Integrating these observations, we propose a systemic model: the reduction in adipose-derived CXCL9 after bariatric surgery not only reflects local immune resolution but also lowers the systemic pool of CXCL9 available to stimulate CXCR3⁺ T cell trafficking to the skin. In essence, the adipose tissue acts as a “lymphoid organ” that amplifies systemic T cell activation; by quieting this organ, bariatric surgery weakens the skin-homing capacity of pro-inflammatory T cells. Our immune deconvolution results strongly support this model, as CXCL9 expression was positively correlated with CD8⁺ T cell and M1 macrophage abundance (Pearson r = 0.72 and 0.68, respectively) and negatively correlated with M2 macrophages (r = –0.54) in the combined dataset. These correlations were consistent across both adipose and skin samples, reinforcing the concept of a unified immune response.

Consistent with previous clinical reports, our findings provide transcriptomic-level support for the anti-psoriatic effects of bariatric surgery. Several prospective studies have documented that obese patients with psoriasis undergoing Roux-en-Y gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy experience significant reductions in PASI and DLQI scores, often accompanied by decreased systemic levels of inflammatory markers such as CRP and fibrinogen.29 While a prior bioinformatics study focused on CCL2 as a key adipocytokine bridging obesity and psoriasis,30 our work uniquely highlights CXCL9 as a potentially more specific link to the Th1/CD8⁺ T-cell-driven immunopathology of psoriasis. CCL2 primarily recruits monocytes and is associated with general adipose inflammation, whereas CXCL9, with its specificity for CXCR3, aligns more closely with the IFN-γ–dominant axis that characterizes psoriatic skin inflammation. This distinction has direct translational implications. First, serum CXCL9 could serve as a non-invasive, affordable biomarker to identify obese psoriasis patients who are most likely to benefit from bariatric surgery—for instance, those with high baseline CXCL9 levels might experience the greatest PASI improvement. Second, the CXCL9/CXCR3 axis is a druggable target; small molecule CXCR3 antagonists (eg, AMG-487, NBI-74330) have been evaluated in clinical trials for autoimmune diseases and could be repositioned for obesity-associated psoriasis.31 Additionally, monoclonal antibodies targeting CXCL9 (eg, a fully human anti-CXCL9 antibody) have shown efficacy in preclinical models of colitis and could be tested in combination with weight loss interventions.32 Therefore, our results provide a strong molecular rationale for a “dual-hit” strategy: bariatric surgery to reduce systemic inflammatory load, coupled with targeted blockade of the CXCL9-CXCR3 axis to specifically attenuate T cell homing to skin.

Nevertheless, this study has several limitations inherent to its computational nature. First, our analysis relies on publicly available microarray and RNA-seq datasets, which may carry batch effects and often lack paired adipose–skin samples from the same patient. Ideally, longitudinal biopsies from the same individual before and after surgery would allow direct correlation of molecular changes in both tissues. Future studies using such paired samples, combined with plasma proteomics, could confirm the systemic role of CXCL9. Second, all findings are correlative and require experimental validation. To move beyond association, co-culture experiments of human adipocytes with autologous CD8⁺ T cells, with or without CXCL9 neutralization, would directly test whether adipocyte-derived CXCL9 promotes T cell migration and granzyme B release. In vivo, a high-fat diet-fed mouse model of imiquimod-induced psoriasis treated with sleeve gastrectomy or pharmacological CXCR3 inhibition would provide causal evidence for our hypothesis. Third, while our immune deconvolution analysis using CIBERSORT yields valuable insights, this method relies on reference gene expression signatures derived from sorted blood cells; its accuracy in adipose tissue, which contains abundant stromal and progenitor cells, may be suboptimal. Single-cell RNA sequencing of paired adipose and skin samples would offer an unbiased, high-resolution view of the specific cell types expressing CXCL9 and its receptor CXCR3. Fourth, the role of the gut microbiome, strongly implied by the LPS enrichment, was not directly addressed in our transcriptomic study. Integrating metagenomic shotgun sequencing with transcriptomics via mediation analysis could identify whether specific bacterial taxa (eg, Akkermansia muciniphila, Bacteroides vulgatus) influence CXCL9 expression through LPS or other metabolites.33 Finally, the diagnostic AUC values for CXCL9, while robust (>0.85), were derived from relatively small external cohorts; validation in larger, prospective, multi-center cohorts is needed before clinical translation.

In conclusion, this integrative bioinformatics study unveils a shared molecular framework that centers on the IL-17 signaling pathway, LPS-driven defense responses, and the hub chemokine CXCL9, effectively connecting bariatric surgery-induced adipose tissue remodeling with psoriasis. By delineating a mechanistic model in which CXCL9 acts as a systemic messenger bridging adipose inflammation and skin homing of pro-inflammatory T cells, we provide a strong molecular foundation for future clinical and translational work. The identification of CXCL9 as a cross-disease biomarker and therapeutic target opens new avenues for personalized management of obese psoriasis patients, combining metabolic surgery with immunomodulation. As the epidemic of obesity continues to rise, understanding these systemic immune connections becomes increasingly vital for holistic patient care.

Conclusion

In summary, through the integration of transcriptomic data from adipose tissue before and after bariatric surgery and from psoriatic lesions, this study reveals for the first time that the shared molecular basis between the two conditions centers on the IL-17 signaling pathway, LPS/bacterial defense responses, and extracellular matrix remodeling, with CXCL9 established as a core node gene. By driving dual chemotaxis of Th1/CD8⁺ T cells via the CXCR3 axis, CXCL9 may serve as a key bridge connecting adipose tissue inflammation and psoriatic skin immunity. Our findings not only provide a new molecular explanation for the clinical improvement of psoriasis after bariatric surgery but also highlight the potential value of CXCL9/CXCR3 as noninvasive biomarkers and drug targets. Despite limitations in data sources and validation methods, this study lays a solid foundation for future in-depth investigations of the “adipose–microbe–skin” immune axis and the development of combined metabolic–immune intervention strategies.

Conditions for positivity of energy in superrenormalizable polynomial gravity


Abstract
At the quantum level, the polynomial models of gravity with six and eight derivatives are superrenormalizable, but suffer from higher derivative ghost and/or tachyonic ghost states. On the other hand, these models may have advantages in the control of negative effects of ghosts, compared to the more common fourth-derivative theory. We explore the positiveness of energy of the individual plane wave solutions in the general models with six and eight derivatives. Different from the fourth-order gravity, the part of the energy which may be seen as the leading one in the UV, is positively defined in the tensor sector. We extend this investigation to the scalar sectors of the free theory. Keywords: Higher derivatives, quantum gravity, energy positiveness, propagator, gauge independence

Introduction

The issue of higher derivatives and subsequent physical problems
can be identified as a serious difficulty of quantum gravity (QG). The
renormalizability of the theory requires inclusion of at least
four-derivative terms in the action
[
2
,
3
]
. Even if
higher derivatives are not introduced at the classical level, the
corresponding terms emerge owing to the loop effects of gravity

[
4
,
5
]
, or quantum matter fields
[
2
,
6
]
.
Thus, the theory without at least four derivatives cannot be
renormalizable. This means, the theory should have negative-energy
states, or higher derivative ghosts.
Historically, higher-derivative instabilities were described in
classical mechanics by M.V. Ostrogradky more than one and
a half centuries ago
[
7
]
(see also recent discussion in

[
8
]
). At the quantum level, the presence
of a ghost generates instabilities usually characterized as the
violation of unitarity
[
9
,
3
]
. This situation
was extensively discussed in the fourth-derivative quantum gravity,
but can be extended to more general versions of the theory.

There were several interesting proposals concerning
the contradiction between renormalizability and unitarity
in QG. We can invoke the pioneer works

[
10
,
11
,
12
]
, describing the situations when
the massive ghosts in fourth-derivative gravity become unstable
and eventually decay at the quantum level, such that the unitarity
of the
S
-matrix is restored. The question
of whether this really happens or not, remains open because of the
limited information about higher loop corrections in QG

[
13
]
.

The “minimal” renormalizable QG theory can be generalized by
introducing more derivatives. One of the possibilities is a
polynomial QG with six or more derivatives
[
14
]
, that
gives superrenormalizable models. In this case, the divergences
show up only at the first loop. Furthermore, only four-, second-
and zero-derivative terms in the action get renormalized. Starting
from ten derivatives, all divergences are at the one-loop level. The
same situation is typical for the non-polynomial QG models

[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, as can be seen from the
corresponding power counting
[
18
]
. In the polynomial
superrenormalizable QG, all massive poles may be complex and
the corresponding quantum theories unitary
within the Lee-Wick quantization scheme
[
19
]
.
Thus, what requires a lot of information about dressed propagator
of gravitons in the four-derivative QG
[
11
,
13
]
, may
be automatic in the superrenormalizable models. An unfortunate
detail is that the unitarity of the
S
-matrix does not guarantee the
physical unitarity
[
20
]
or the stability of classical
solutions.

A radical solution to the problem of ghosts has been formulated in
the proposal of Simon
[
21
,
22
]
. In this approach,
higher-derivative terms are treated, by definition, as small
perturbations over the Einstein-Hilbert action. In this scheme,
there are no ghosts, the stability of solutions and the unitarity
of the quantum theory are guaranteed. However, this alternative
construction of QG crucially differs from the standard construction
of usual quantum field theory. On top of that, it generates a large
portion of ambiguities
[
23
]
(see also
[
24
]

and the discussion in
[
25
]
). Both problems may be solved
if, instead of defining higher derivatives as small perturbations, we
impose the restrictions on the initial conditions, such that the ghost
cannot be generated from the vacuum

[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
. Creating
a ghost from nothing requires the concentration of gravitons with
the Planck energy density,
M_{P}^{4}\approx 10^{76}
GeV
4
.
The unclear aspect in this area is to explain why there is such a
restriction on the energy density of gravitons.

Another idea to fight ghosts, in a completely different direction,
was proposed by Hawking in
[
30
]
. The point is that,
in the fourth-order theory, both the graviton and the massive ghost
are not independent particles, but parts of the same quantum
excitation.
E.g., the propagator of metric perturbations can be separated into
two or more parts, but originally these parts belong to the same
combined quantum state. It might happen that taking this aspect into
account, one achieves stability. The stable combinations of complex
ghost-like states were called ghostballs
[
31
]
,
but in may also include usual ghosts and healthy degrees of freedom.
The consistent description of a ghost confinement may be an ultimate
solution of the problem of higher derivatives. At the moment, such a
solution is not elaborated in detail. Such elaboration requires, in
particular, a better understanding of different aspects of the theory
with higher than four derivatives. In the present work, we
concentrate on the particular issue of energy positivity in these
models, which may be relevant for better general understanding of
the superrenormalizable theories.

An important aspect of superrenormalizable QG models is the
definition of signs in the action, which makes the
massless graviton to be a healthy particle and provides the mass
spectrum of the regular form, as described in
[
14
]
. In
the present work, we explore this issue using the simple method
applied recently in the book
[
32
]
for the sign definition in
the fourth derivative QG. As we will see in what follows, in the
UV (high frequency) regime, the energy of the combination of
tensor ghosts and normal degrees of freedom in the free sector of
six-derivative models may be positively defined at leading order in this energy scale, while in the
eight-derivative model it is negatively defined. The sign rules
can be established for both gauge-fixing independent, tensor and
scalar, sectors of the propagator.

The paper is organized as follows. Sec.
2
reviews the
propagator of a general model of QG with an arbitrary number of
derivatives. Here we closely follow the book
[
32
]
and the
review paper
[
33
]
and this section is included to make
our work self-consistent. We identify the gauge-independent
tensor and scalar sectors of the theory. Sections
3

and
4
report about the main result, that is the conditions
of positivity of the plane wave solutions for tensor and scalar and
modes in the theory with six and eight derivatives. Cumbersome
technical details are separated in the Appendix. Sec.
5

describes the situation in the theories with more than eight
derivatives. Finally, in Sec.
6
, we draw our conclusions.

Propagator in the higher-derivative gravity

In the present work, we consider the energy conditions for the
free field limit of the higher-derivative QG of a general
form.

The parametrization of the metric on the flat background is given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(1)

Since the curvature tensor is
\mathcal{O}(h_{\mu\nu})
, the free
limit means we need the terms in the action which are at most of the
second order in curvatures. Higher order terms and the total
derivative terms in the Lagrangian may be omitted. In this framework,
the most general action of the higher-derivative gravity is

\displaystyle S\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big\{C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Phi\left(-\square\right)C^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+R\Psi\left(-\square\right)R-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\,R\Big\}\,,

(2)

where the cosmological constant term is assumed zero, because we
need to use the flat background,
C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the Weyl
tensor,
R
is the scalar curvature,
\Phi(-\square)
and
\Psi(-\square)
are
functions which are chosen such that the QG theory is
superrenormalizable. For instance,
\Phi
and
\Psi
may be
polynomials of the same order. Similar conditions can be also
formulated in nonlocal higher-derivative models

[
34
,
32
]
. In the present section, we do not
assume any restrictions on these functions, but subsequently
concentrate on the polynomial cases only.

One can rewrite the Lagrangian in (
2
) in another
basis, using the relations

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Phi\left(-\cx\right)C^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,=\,R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Phi\left(-\cx\right)R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}-2R_{\mu\nu}\Phi\left(-\cx\right)R^{\mu\nu}+\dfrac{1}{3}R\Phi\left(-\cx\right)R\,,\qquad

(3)

\displaystyle\textrm{GB}_{n}\,\equiv\,R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Omega\left(-\cx\right)R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}-4R_{\mu\nu}\Omega\left(-\cx\right)R^{\mu\nu}+R\Omega\left(-\cx\right)R\,=\,\mathcal{O}\left(R_{\cdots}^{3}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\Omega\left(-\square\right)
may be polynomial of degree
n
. The
Gauss–Bonnet-like term

\textrm{GB}_{n}
is certainly not topological for
n\neq 0
,
but it contributes only to the third- and higher-order terms in the
curvature tensor. The relation (
4
) is proved in

[
33
,
35
,
36
]
, including in the case
of non-polynomial functions.

To construct the propagator, we introduce the gauge-fixing term

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{gf}}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\,\chi_{\mu}\,\mathcal{Y}^{\mu\nu}\chi_{\nu}\,,

(5)

where the gauge condition depends on the parameter
\beta
,

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu}\,=\,\partial_{\lambda}h^{\lambda}_{\,\,\mu}-\beta\partial_{\mu}h\,,

(6)

being
h=\eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}
and the weight
operator includes the form factor
\mathcal{W}(-\cx)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{\mu\nu}\,=\,\big(\eta^{\mu\nu}\cx\,-\,\gamma\,\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\big)\mathcal{W}(-\cx)\,.

(7)

The ambiguities include the gauge fixing parameters
\beta
,

\gamma
and the operator function
\mathcal{W}(-\cx)
. All these
quantities should be chosen to remove the degeneracy of the total
bilinear form, but this leaves an arbitrariness.

The propagator depends on the quadratic expansion of the
action, i.e., of the expression

\displaystyle\big(S+S_{\textrm{gf}}\big)^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\,h^{\mu\nu}\,H_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\,.

(8)

Omitting the technical details (which can be found in
[
32
]

or, in a more complete form, in
[
33
]
), let us start with
the expression for the degenerate bilinear form of the action with
the gauge fixing term, in the momentum representation,

\displaystyle\hat{H}\,=\,H_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}(k)\,=\,s_{1}(k^{2})\delta_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}+s_{2}(k^{2})\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\alpha\beta}+s_{3}(k^{2})\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}+\eta_{\alpha\beta}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\,s_{4}(k^{2})\left(\eta_{\nu\beta}k_{\mu}k_{\alpha}+\eta_{\mu\beta}k_{\nu}k_{\alpha}+\eta_{\nu\alpha}k_{\mu}k_{\beta}+\eta_{\mu\alpha}k_{\nu}k_{\beta}\right)\,+\,s_{5}(k^{2})k_{\mu}k_{\nu}k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\,,

(9)

with the coefficient functions

\displaystyle s_{1}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Phi(k^{2})k^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\,k^{2}\,,

\displaystyle s_{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[-\dfrac{1}{6}\Phi(k^{2})+\Psi(k^{2})\Big]k^{4}-\dfrac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}k^{2}+\beta^{2}(\gamma-1)\mathcal{W}(k^{2})k^{4}\,,

\displaystyle s_{3}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\dfrac{1}{6}\Phi(k^{2})-\Psi(k^{2})\Big]k^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}k^{2}-\beta(\gamma-1)\mathcal{W}(k^{2})k^{4}\,,

\displaystyle s_{4}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\,\Phi(k^{2})k^{4}-\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}k^{2}-\mathcal{W}(k^{2})k^{4}\,,

\displaystyle s_{5}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\dfrac{1}{3}\Phi(k^{2})+\Psi(k^{2})\Big]k^{4}+\gamma\mathcal{W}(k^{2})k^{4}\,.

(10)

The bilinear form of the total action (i.e., the inverse of the
propagator) depends on
\Phi(k^{2})
,
\Psi(k^{2})
, on the gauge-fixing parameters, including the function
\mathcal{W}(k^{2})
. One can
check that the coefficient of the “generalized” Gauss-Bonnet term

\Omega(k^{2})
does not appear in these expressions.
It is also noteworthy that
s_{1}(k^{2})
is the only coefficient that does not depend on the terms arising from the gauge-fixing action.

It is useful to rewrite the operator

H_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}
using Barnes-Rivers
projectors, defined in terms of the longitudinal

\,\omega_{\mu\nu}=k_{\mu}k_{\nu}/k^{2}\,
and transverse

\,\theta_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}-\omega_{\mu\nu}

projectors in the vector space. The tensor projectors are

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{(2)}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}(\theta_{\mu\alpha}\theta_{\nu\beta}+\theta_{\mu\beta}\theta_{\nu\alpha})-\frac{1}{3}\,\theta_{\mu\nu}\theta_{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{(1)}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,(\theta_{\mu\alpha}\omega_{\nu\beta}+\theta_{\nu\alpha}\omega_{\mu\beta}+\theta_{\mu\beta}\omega_{\nu\alpha}+\theta_{\nu\beta}\omega_{\mu\alpha})\,,

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{(0-s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{{(0-s)}}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,\frac{1}{3}\,\theta_{\mu\nu}\theta_{\alpha\beta}\,,\quad{\hat{P}}^{(0-w)}\,=\,P^{{(0-w)}}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega_{\alpha\beta}\,.\mbox{\qquad}

(11)

To complete the algebra, we need also the transfer operators

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{{(ws)}}=P^{(ws)}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\theta_{\mu\nu}\omega_{\alpha\beta}\,,\quad{\hat{P}}^{{(sw)}}=P^{(sw)}_{\mu\nu\,,\,\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\theta_{\alpha\beta}\,.\mbox{\qquad}

(12)

In this basis, (
9
) is cast in the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}\,=\,b_{2}\hat{P}^{(2)}+b_{1}\hat{P}^{(1)}+b_{os}\hat{P}^{(0-s)}+b_{ow}\hat{P}^{(0-w)}+b_{sw}\big[\hat{P}^{\left(ws\right)}+\hat{P}^{(sw)}\big]\,,

(13)

where

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}k^{4}\Phi(k^{2})+\dfrac{k^{2}}{2\kappa^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{k^{4}}{2}\mathcal{W}(k^{2})\,,

\displaystyle b_{os}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{k^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}+3k^{4}\left[\Psi(k^{2})+\beta^{2}\left(\gamma-1\right)\mathcal{W}(k^{2})\right]\,,

\displaystyle b_{ow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{4}\left(\beta-1\right)^{2}(\gamma-1)\mathcal{W}(k^{2})\,,

\displaystyle b_{sw}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{3}\left[k^{4}\beta\left(\beta-1\right)(\gamma-1)\mathcal{W}(k^{2})\right]\,.

(14)

Finally, using these relations, we arrive at the propagator

\displaystyle G(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2\kappa^{2}}{k^{2}\left[\kappa^{2}\Phi(k^{2})k^{2}+1\right]}\,\hat{P}^{(2)}-\dfrac{2}{\mathcal{W}(k^{2})k^{4}}\,\hat{P}^{(1)}+\dfrac{\kappa^{2}}{k^{2}\left[3\kappa^{2}\Psi(k^{2})k^{2}-1\right]}\,\hat{P}^{(0-s)}

(15)

\displaystyle+\,\,\dfrac{1}{\left(\beta-1\right)^{2}}\bigg[\dfrac{3\beta^{2}\kappa^{2}}{k^{2}\left[3\kappa^{2}\Psi(k^{2})k^{2}-1\right]}+\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{W}(k^{2})(\gamma-1)k^{4}}\bigg]\hat{P}^{(0-w)}

\displaystyle-\,\,\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\beta\kappa^{2}}{\left(\beta-1\right)k^{2}\left[3\kappa^{2}\Psi(k^{2})k^{2}-1\right]}\big[\hat{P}^{(ws)}+\hat{P}^{(sw)}\big]\,.

The form factor
\Phi(k^{2})
of the Weyl-squared term completely
defines the propagation of the spin-2 mode.
The scalar mode with
\hat{P}^{(0-s)}
depends only on the form
factor
\Psi(k^{2})
of the
R^{2}
-term. These two
parts do not depend on the gauge fixing, while all other sectors are
gauge dependent. An interesting detail is that the
gauge-fixing independence of the
\hat{P}^{(0-s)}
-mode
holds only in four spacetime dimensions and for
\Lambda=0

[
33
]
.

An alternative representation of the metric perturbations (
1
) can be obtained by decomposing them into their spin-2, spin-1, and spin-0 modes, namely,

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}+\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{\nu}^{\perp}+\partial_{\nu}\epsilon_{\mu}^{\perp}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\epsilon+\phi_{\mu\nu}\,.

(16)

Here the spin-2 mode
\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}
is traceless
and transverse, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}\eta^{\mu\nu}\,=\,0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\partial^{\mu}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}=0\,,

(17)

while spin-1 part satisfies
\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{\perp\mu}=0
.
The two scalar modes
\epsilon
and
h
can be combined into the
gauge-fixing independent field

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\frac{1}{3}\theta_{\mu\nu}(h-\cx\epsilon)\,,\qquad\mbox{such that}\qquad\hat{P}^{(0-s)}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}\,\phi^{\mu\nu}\,\,=\,\,\phi_{\alpha\beta}\,.

(18)

The bilinear action with gauge-fixing independent terms can be written as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\bar{h}^{\perp\perp\mu\nu}H_{2}(-\cx)\,\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}\,+\,\phi^{\mu\nu}H_{0}\left(-\square\right)\phi_{\mu\nu}\right]\,,

(19)

where the operators
H_{2}(-\cx)
and
H_{0}(-\cx)
can be read off
from (
15
), namely,

\displaystyle H_{2}(-\cx)\,=\,\dfrac{\cx}{2\kappa^{2}}\left[\kappa^{2}\Phi(-\cx)\cx-1\right]\,,

\displaystyle H_{0}(-\cx)\,=\,\dfrac{\cx}{\kappa^{2}}\left[3\kappa^{2}\Psi(-\cx)\cx+1\right]\,.

(20)

The spectrum of the model (
2
) depends of the position of
the poles of the gauge-independent part of the propagator, that means
the roots of the equations

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\Phi(k^{2})k^{2}+1=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 3\kappa^{2}\Psi(k^{2})k^{2}-1=0\,,

(21)

for the massive modes of spin-2 and of spin-0, respectively.
If both
\Phi
and
\Psi
are non-zero constants, the theory
is the fourth-derivative gravity. Then, besides the massless
graviton, there are a massive scalar and a massive tensor mode.
If
\Phi
and
\Psi
are polynomials of degree
N
,
then each of the equations (
21
) can have up to
N+1

distinct roots in the complex plane, that gives
N+1
massive
modes of spin-2 and of spin-0.

The nature of massive modes depends on the polynomials
\Phi
and

\Psi
. In the recent years, there has been an increasing interest in
the models with complex poles (see e.g.,
[
37
]
), owing to the possibility of getting
the theory with unitary
S
-matrix in the Lee-Wick quantum
gravity
[
19
,
38
]
. Further applications can
be found, e.g., in
[
25
,
39
,
40
]
and references therein.
Higher-derivative gravity models with degenerate poles were studied
also in
[
25
,
39
]
, however in the present work we shall
not consider this possibility.

Positivity of energy in six-order gravity

It is known that in the fourth-derivative gravity the conditions for
positive energy for individual plane waves are helpful in defining
the signs of the Weyl-squared and
R
-squared terms
[
32
]
.
Adjusting the coefficients of these terms in the action, one can
provide that

i)
The IR limit, i.e., the general
relativity (GR), has positively defined spin-2 sector.

ii)
The energy of the massless tensor mode (graviton) is
positive at any energy scale.

iii)
The sign of the overall energy in the low-energy (IR)
limit is positive.

The first condition
i)
is the cornerstone of the effective
approach to gravity, which assumes that GR is the universal
low-energy limit of any theory of quantum gravity
[
41
]
.
Recently there was an indirect convincing confirmation of this
assumption
[
42
]
and here we consider this as a proved
aspect of quantum gravity.
The last condition
iii)
does not depend on higher-order
time derivatives and is relevant for a consistent application of the
effective approach in the IR. The constraints on the signs of the
coefficients in the action, obtained from these conditions, confirmed
the previous well-known results of
[
43
]
.

Let us apply the same method
to the superrenormalizable polynomial QG,

\displaystyle S_{2N+4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\int\!d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big\{\vartheta_{N,C\,}C\,\square^{N}C+\vartheta_{N,R}R\,\square^{N}R+\vartheta_{N,\mathrm{GB}}\mathrm{GB}_{N}

(22)

\displaystyle+\,\vartheta_{N-1,C\,}C\,\square^{N-1}C+\vartheta_{N-1,R}R\,\square^{N-1}R+\vartheta_{N-1,\mathrm{GB}}\mathrm{GB}_{N-1}

\displaystyle+\,\,\,\ldots\,\,\,+

\displaystyle+\,\vartheta_{0,C\,}C^{2}+\vartheta_{0,R}R^{2}+\vartheta_{0,\mathrm{GB}}\mathrm{GB}_{0}+\dfrac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\,+{\mathcal{O}}(R_{\dots}^{3})\Big\}\,.\mbox{\qquad}

Here
\,C\cx^{N}C\,
is the Weyl-squared term in (
2
)
with
\Phi\left(-\cx\right)=\cx^{N}
and
\mathrm{GB}_{N}
are
the “generalized” Gauss-Bonnet terms (
4
) with

\Omega(-\cx)=\cx^{N}
. As we already know, the latter terms reduce
to
{\mathcal{O}}(R_{\dots}^{3})
-terms and may be omitted.
In this section, we consider the simplest case of
N=1
, so the
action of our interest is

\displaystyle S_{6}\,=\,-\,\dfrac{1}{2}\int\!d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big\{\vartheta_{1,C\,}C\cx C+\vartheta_{1,R}R\cx R+\vartheta_{0,C\,}C^{2}+\vartheta_{0,R}R^{2}+\dfrac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\,R\Big\}\,.\mbox{\qquad}

(23)

As in the case of general polynomial action (
22
), all

\vartheta
’s are arbitrary parameters, which values can be defined only
from measurements (experiments or observations).
From the theoretical side, any values of these parameters
do not contradict superrenormalizability, and can be chosen
to avoid problems with ghosts and tachyonic ghosts.

The analysis of signs in
[
32
]
concerned the case of

\vartheta_{1,R}=\vartheta_{1,C}=0
, and dealt with the tensor sector only.
The idea was to consider an individual wave with the space wave vector
\vec{k}
and with a certain frequency. Requiring the
positiveness of the energy of graviton (massless mode) at the
different energy scales, one can show that
\vartheta_{0,C}>0
.

Tensor sector in the six-order gravity

For the spin-2 part of the action of a six-derivative gravitational model,
we use the first term on the right-hand side (
r.h.s.
) of Eq.
(
19
) with the form factor

\displaystyle\Phi(-\cx)=-\,\vartheta_{1,C}\cx-\frac{1}{\lambda}\,.

(24)

We redefined the coefficient
\vartheta_{0,C}\equiv 1/\lambda
, following the
notation used, e.g., in
[
32
]
. In the pure tensor sector, the
indices are irrelevant and we can denote, for the sake of brevity,

\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\perp\perp}=h
. Therefore, the bilinear
form reads

\displaystyle{S}_{\textrm{spin-2}}^{(2)}\,=\,-\,\,\dfrac{1}{4}\int d^{4}x\Big\{\vartheta_{1,C}\left(\cx^{2}h\right)\cx h\,+\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\cx h\right)^{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\,h\cx h\Big\}\,.

(25)

The Lagrange function of the individual wave with the fixed
wave vector
\vec{k}
can be easily obtained by the replacement
(overdot means a time derivative)

\displaystyle\cx h\,=\,\ddot{h}-\Delta h\,,

(26)

after applying the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\cx h\,\,\,\xrightarrow{\,}\,\,\ddot{h}+k^{2}h\quad\textrm{with}\quad k^{2}=\vec{k}\cdot\vec{k}\,,

(27)

in a similar manner

\displaystyle\cx^{2}h\,=\,\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta\right)^{2}h\,=\,\left(\partial_{t}^{4}-2\Delta\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\right)h\,\,\,\xrightarrow{\,}\,\,h^{\textrm{(IV)}}+2k^{2}\ddot{h}+k^{4}h\,.

(28)

After a small algebra, the Lagrange function with fixed

\vec{k}
is obtained in the form

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{spin-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\,\dfrac{1}{4}\,\vartheta_{1,C}\big[h^{\textrm{(IV)}}+2k^{2}\ddot{h}+k^{4}h\big]\big(\ddot{h}+k^{2}h\big)

(29)

\displaystyle\quad-\,\,\dfrac{1}{4\lambda}\big(\ddot{h}+{k}^{2}h\big)^{2}-\dfrac{1}{4\kappa^{2}}\,h\big(\ddot{h}+{k}^{2}h\big)\,.

The equation of motion for tensor perturbations can be derived

\displaystyle h^{\textrm{(VI)}}+3{k}^{2}h^{\textrm{(IV)}}+3{k}^{4}\ddot{h}+{k}^{6}h+\dfrac{1}{\lambda\,\vartheta_{1,C}}\big(h^{\textrm{(IV)}}\,+\,2{k}^{2}\ddot{h}+{k}^{4}h\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}\vartheta_{1,C}}\big(\ddot{h}+{k}^{2}h\big)\,=\,0\,.

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+{k}^{2}\Big)\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\omega^{2}_{1}\Big)\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\omega^{2}_{2}\Big)h\,=\,0\,,

(32)

where

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{1}={k}^{2}+m^{2}_{1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\omega^{2}_{2}={k}^{2}+m^{2}_{2}\,.

The two massive parameters satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle m^{2}_{1}+m^{2}_{2}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{\lambda\,\vartheta_{1,C}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad m^{2}_{1}\,m^{2}_{2}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}\,\vartheta_{1,C}}\,.

The solution of these systems of equations leads to

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2\lambda\,\vartheta_{1,C}}\left(\,1\,\pm\,\sqrt{1-\frac{4\lambda^{2}\vartheta_{1,C}}{\kappa^{2}}}\,\right)\,,

\displaystyle m_{2}^{2}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2\lambda\,\vartheta_{1,C}}\left(\,1\,\mp\,\sqrt{1-\dfrac{4\lambda^{2}\vartheta_{1,C}}{\kappa^{2}}}\,\right)\,.

(35)

Each of the quantities
m_{1}^{2}
and
m_{2}^{2}
can be either real
(positive or negative) or form a complex conjugate pair.

For the plane wave, there may be either oscillator or
anti-oscillator type solutions. For a spectrum consisting only
of normal particles, with positive squares of the masses, there
are only oscillatory solutions to the linearized field equation
(
32

\displaystyle h(t)\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C_{0}\cos(k\,t+\varphi_{0})+C_{1}\cos(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1})+C_{2}\cos(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2})\,,

(36)

with
\omega

C_{0,1,2}
and
irrelevant phases
\varphi_{0,1,2}

It is clear that the mass spectrum defined by (

36
),
or the tachyonic-type

apply to
\lambda

We know that

However, in six-derivative models, there is, typically, no strong

\lambda

\vartheta_{1,C}

Using the definitions (

\displaystyle\lambda>0\,,\qquad\vartheta_{1,C}>0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\dfrac{4\lambda^{2}\vartheta_{1,C}}{\kappa^{2}}<1\,.

Satisfying these conditions, there are no tachyonic solutions

or

m_{1}^{2}
and
m_{2}^{2}
form a complex-conjugate pair.
In this case, one must have

, while there is no

The energy of the theory with

\displaystyle E\,=\,\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left(-1\right)^{l+j}q^{(j)}\dfrac{d^{\,l-j}}{dt^{\,l-j}}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial{q^{(l)}}}-L\,,

\displaystyle E^{(6)}_{\textrm{spin-2}}

It is important to emphasize that this definition of energy scales, in which the higher time-derivative terms are associated with the UV regime, while the lower ones correspond to the IR regime, is a consequence of the violation of the standard massless dispersion relation between the wave vector and the frequency, see e.g.,

takes large values.

The transition to GR in (

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{\textrm{spin-2}}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{4\kappa^{2}}\left(\dot{h}^{2}+{{k}^{2}}h^{2}\right)\,,

is positively defined for

iii)
In general, higher-derivative gravity models exhibit an indefinite energy sign in the UV regime. In the specific case of sixth-order gravity, this indefiniteness is also present. However, if the conditions in (

\displaystyle E^{(6)}_{\textrm{spin-2}}\bigg|_{\textrm{UV}}\!\!=\,\dfrac{\vartheta_{1,C}}{4}\Big[\stackrel{{\scriptstyle...}}{{h}}^{\,2}-\,2h^{\textrm{(IV)}}\ddot{h}+2h^{\textrm{(V)}}\dot{h}\Big]\,\geq\,0\,.

(45)

In contrast, the UV energy under analogous conditions is negative, as demonstrated below

(46)

Details of the intermediate steps used to derive expressions (

As we know, the differences between ghost and tachyonic

iv)
It is interesting that the unique intermediate term in (

This is certainly a UV region in the fourth-derivative gravity model, when the frequency is very high and the fourth-derivative term dominates. In conclusion, we can state that the energy is positively defined, since the six-derivative term dominates in the UV regime.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\,\vartheta_{2,C}\left[2h^{\left(\textrm{VII}\right)}\dot{h}-\left(h^{\textrm{(IV)}}\right)^{2}+2h^{\textrm{(V)}}\dddot{h}-2h^{\textrm{(VI)}}\ddot{h}\right]

(69)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\vartheta_{1,C}+4\vartheta_{2,C}k^{2}\right)\left[\dddot{h}^{2}-2h^{\textrm{(IV)}}\ddot{h}+2h^{\textrm{(V)}}\dot{h}\right]

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\lambda}+3\vartheta_{1,C}{k}^{2}+6\vartheta_{2,C}{k}^{4}\Big)\big(2\dddot{h}\dot{h}-\ddot{h}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}+\dfrac{2{k}^{2}}{\lambda}+{3\vartheta_{1,C}{k}^{4}}+4\vartheta_{2,C}{k}^{6}\Big)\dot{h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(\dfrac{{k}^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}+\dfrac{{k}^{4}}{\lambda}+{\vartheta_{1,C}{k}^{6}}+{\vartheta_{2,C}{k}^{8}}\Big)h^{2}\,.

Taking
\vartheta_{2,C}=0
, we recover the energy expression of the six-derivative gravity model, as discussed in the previous section. In the opposite case, where
\vartheta_{2,C}\neq 0
, the energy sign is, in general, indefined.
Assuming the constants are such that the massive parameters are real and positive, the energy becomes negative in the UV limit, where
k^{2}
is much greater than all three
massive parameters,

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}_{\textrm{spin-2}}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\vartheta_{2,C}\left[2h^{\textrm{(V)}}\dddot{h}-\left(h^{\textrm{(IV)}}\right)^{2}-2h^{\textrm{(VI)}}\ddot{h}+2h^{\left(\textrm{VII}\right)}\dot{h}\right]\,\,\leq\,\,0\,.

(70)

As expected, in the IR regime the energy is positive definite for positive constants
\vartheta_{2,C}
,
\vartheta_{1,C}
and
1/\lambda
.

In the scalar sector, the three massive parameters in Eq. (
67
) satisfy the following conditions

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}+m_{3}^{2}\,=\,\dfrac{\vartheta_{1,R}}{\vartheta_{2,R}}\,,

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}\,m_{2}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}\,m_{3}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}\,m_{3}^{2}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{\xi\vartheta_{2,R}}\,,

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}\,m_{2}^{2}\,m_{3}^{2}\,=\,-\dfrac{1}{3\kappa^{2}\vartheta_{2,R}}\,.

(71)

Note that the structures of system of equations (
4
) and
(
4
) are similar . The differences are in the constants

\vartheta_{N,C}
and
\vartheta_{N,R}
with
N=0,1,2
, and in the
Einstein-Hilbert. One can perform the following changes to
transform (
4
) to (
4
), or vice-versa,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{N,C}\,\longleftrightarrow\,\vartheta_{N,R}\,,\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\,\longleftrightarrow\,-\dfrac{1}{3\kappa^{2}}\,.

(72)

To ensure positive
r.h.s.
in
(
4
), like in (
4
), we must assume that

\vartheta_{N,R}<0
. We will see later on, that this makes the energy
of the scalar mode to be negatively defined in the IR limit.
The expression for the energy of the scalar mode is

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}_{\textrm{spin-0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{3}{2}\,\vartheta_{2,R}\left[2\phi^{\textrm{(VII)}}\dot{\phi}-\left(\phi^{\textrm{(IV)}}\right)^{2}+2\phi^{\textrm{(V)}}\dddot{\phi}-2\phi^{\textrm{(VI)}}\ddot{\phi}\right]

(73)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\vartheta_{1,R}+4\vartheta_{2,R}{k}^{2}\right)\left[\dddot{\phi}^{2}-2\phi^{\textrm{(IV)}}\ddot{\phi}+2\phi^{\textrm{(V)}}\dot{\phi}\right]

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{3}{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\xi}+3\vartheta_{1,R}{k}^{2}+6\vartheta_{2,R}{k}^{4}\Big)\Big(2\dddot{\phi}\dot{\phi}-\ddot{\phi}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{3}{2}\Big(4\vartheta_{2,R}{k}^{6}+{3\vartheta_{1,R}{k}^{4}}+\dfrac{2{k}^{2}}{\xi}-\dfrac{1}{3\kappa^{2}}\Big)\dot{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{3}{2}\Big({\vartheta_{1,R}k^{6}}+\dfrac{k^{4}}{\xi}-\dfrac{{k}^{2}}{3\kappa^{2}}+{\vartheta_{2,R}{k}^{8}}\Big)\phi^{2}\,.

To analyze the energy behavior in the UV regime, we assume that the massive parameters are real, positive, and much smaller than the wave vector
\vec{k}
. Under this assumption, the leading contribution to the energy becomes

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}_{\textrm{spin-0}}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,\,\dfrac{3}{2}\vartheta_{2,R}\left[2\phi^{\textrm{(VII)}}\dot{\phi}-\left(\phi^{\textrm{(IV)}}\right)^{2}+2\phi^{\textrm{(V)}}\dddot{\phi}-2\phi^{\textrm{(VI)}}\ddot{\phi}\right]\,\,\geq\,\,0\,,

(74)

of course, if
\vartheta_{2,R}
is negatively defined, as previously discussed.

In the IR limit, the energy determined by the lowest-order derivative term is negatively
defined for
\vartheta_{N,R}<0
, similar to the fourth-order gravity
in the same regime.

For a comprehensive derivation of the results discussed in this chapter, see Appendix
A
.

Beyond the eight-derivative model

Thus far, we have investigated the conditions for the positivity of energy and stable solutions for the fourth, sixth, and eighth-derivative models in both the tensor and scalar modes. Through these studies, a standard behavior has been implicitly demonstrated in relation to these models, both in terms of the number of derivatives, as established in the previous sections, and in the transition between the tensor and scalar modes, as discussed earlier. Therefore, it is worthwhile to develop general equations to study the behavior of higher-derivative models in the UV and IR regimes, under the assumption that the stability conditions are satisfied.

Consider the equation of motion for a polynomial gravity model with
(2N+4)
derivatives as
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Phi=(h,\phi)
denotes the spin-2 and spin-0 modes

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+k^{2}\Big)\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\omega_{1}^{2}\Big)\,\cdots\,\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\omega_{N+1}^{2}\Big)\Phi\,=\,0\,,

(75)

where
\omega_{N+1}^{2}
are the frequencies for plane wave solutions of the tensor or
scalar mode. In both cases,

\displaystyle\omega_{N+1}^{2}=k^{2}+m_{N+1}^{2}

(76)

being
m_{N+1}^{2}
the massive parameters, depending of

\left(\vartheta_{N,C}\,,\,1/\kappa^{2}\right)
for the tensor mode and of
\left(\vartheta_{N,R}\,,\,1/\kappa^{2}\right)
for the scalar mode.

To avoid tachyonic ghosts, we have to assume that
m_{N+1}^{2}

has real positive values, that requires

\vartheta_{N,C}>0
for tensor modes and

\vartheta_{N,R}<0
for scalar modes.
In this way, we find an oscillation solution for (
75
),
i.e.,

\displaystyle\Phi\left(t\right)=C_{0}\cos\left(k\,t+\phi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\cos\left(\omega_{1}\,t+\phi_{1}\right)+\ldots+C_{N+1}\cos\left(\omega_{N+1}\,t+\phi_{N+1}\right)\,.

(77)

Observing the structure of the energy equations in the UV limit established in the previous sections, we can write the general expression for the energy in this regime

\displaystyle E^{\left(2N+4\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,\,=\,\,-\,(-1)^{N}\Theta_{N}\bigg[\left(\Phi^{(N+2)}\right)^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{j}\Phi^{(N+j+2)}\Phi^{(N-j+2)}\bigg]\,,

(78)

with
m=N+1
. The expression (
78
) applies to both tensor and scalar
components of the plane wave, in any polynomial QG theory.
For the tensor mode
\Phi\left(t\right)=h\left(t\right)
, we
identify

\Theta_{N}=\frac{1}{4}\vartheta_{N,C}
,
whereas for the scalar mode
\Phi\left(t\right)=\phi\left(t\right)
,
the identification is
\Theta_{N}=\frac{3}{2}\vartheta_{N,R}
.

Assuming that
\Phi\left(t\right)
is given by
Eq. (
77
), in the UV regime with
k^{2}\gg m^{2}_{N+1}
,
the following general rules hold:

i)
For the tensor mode, the energy is negatively defined when
N

is even, and positively defined when
N
is odd;

ii)
For the scalar mode, the rule is opposite, as the energy being
positively defined for even
N
and negatively defined for odd
N
.
Let us note that this conclusion is valid only for the real spectrum.

The origin of the described general rule is the sign alternation
theorem of the generalization of relation (
47
). The
corresponding general formula has the form
[
14
]

\displaystyle G\propto\dfrac{A_{0}}{k{{}^{2}}}+\dfrac{A_{1}}{k^{2}+m_{1}^{2}}+\dfrac{A_{2}}{k^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}+\ldots+\dfrac{A_{N+1}}{k^{2}+m_{N+1}^{2}}\,,

(79)

where
m_{N+1}^{2}
follow

\,0<m_{1}^{2}<m_{2}^{2}<\ldots<m_{N+1}^{2}
and the signs of
A_{k}

alternate, i.e.,
A_{k}\,A_{k+1}<0
.

In the tensor sector, the lightest particle is the healthy graviton,
and the massive degrees of freedom, for a real spectrum, follow
the hierarchy of masses. In the UV limit with odd
N
, this
means more healthy degrees of freedom than ghosts and, for
the same
\vec{k}
, guarantees positivity of the total energy.
For even
N
, there is an equal number of healthy and
ghost degrees of freedom, and the greater masses of ghosts
lead to the negative overall energy.
For the scalar degrees of freedom, the difference is that the
lightest mode has a negative energy. This provides the
aforementioned flip of signs in the cases of even and odd
N
.

One can write a general expression for the energy of the plane
wave in the IR regime,

\displaystyle E^{\left(2N+4\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{IR}}\,\,=\,\,\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(2+j\right)\Theta_{j}k^{2j+2}+\rho\bigg]\dot{\Phi}^{2}\,+\,\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Theta_{j}\,k^{2j+4}+\rho k^{2}\Big)\Phi^{2}\,.

(80)

Similar to Eq. (
78
), this formula applies to both tensor
and scalar modes, with the following identifications:

i)

For tensor modes,
\Phi\left(t\right)=h\left(t\right)
for the field,

\Theta_{N}=\frac{1}{4}\vartheta_{N,C}
, and
\rho=\frac{1}{4\kappa^{2}}
;

ii)

For the scalar degrees of freedom,

\Phi(t)=\phi(t)
for the field,
\Theta_{N}=\frac{3}{2}\vartheta_{N,R}
,
and
\rho=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}
.

It is easy to see that the energy in the IR limit for tensor mode
is positively defined for
\vartheta_{N,C}>0
. Indeed, the IR positivity
in the tensor sector is expected from the universality of quantum
GR as effective theory of QG
[
41
,
42
]
, so our conclusion
confirms this important feature.
Thus, the violation of positivity may take
place only at the intermediate energies. Finally, the scalar mode is negatively defined for
\vartheta_{N,R}<0
.
Both observations are in agreement with the signs of the energy of
the corresponding modes in linearized GR.

Conclusions

The standard method to evaluate the degrees of freedom of a
higher derivative model is by introducing the set of auxiliary fields
and assuming these fields evolve independently from each other
(see, e.g.,
[
47
]
).
This approach leads to the well-known Ostrogradsky instabilities
in classical mechanics
[
7
]
, which are assumed to persist
in the more complicated field theory models, at both classical and
quantum levels
[
8
]
.
The situation may be quite different if we
deal with the six- or higher-derivative models using original
variables. The origin of this difference is that, in this case, the
massive and massless modes have to stay together, as it was
originally discussed in
[
30
]
. For the plane wave
solutions, there are constraints requiring all modes to have the same
wave vector and the same frequency.

Using this approach, we explored the positivity of the energy
of the plane wave solutions in the tensor and scalar, gauge-fixing
independent, sectors in the theory of superrenormalizable gravity
models with a polynomial Lagrangian. We have found a general
pattern for the energy positivity, which is typical only in these
models and is different from the “minimal” fourth-derivative QG.
One of the results is the requirement to have opposite sign
rules in the tensor and scalar sectors. We have found that this
feature is a consequence of the sign alternating theorem of

[
14
]
.

Appendix

Intermediate calculations of energy equations
in the UV regime

This appendix provides details on the computation of energy conditions in the UV regime for higher-derivative models. The
expression for energy of the tensor
h\left(t\right)
and
\phi\left(t\right)
modes depends
on the constants
\vartheta_{N,C}
and
\vartheta_{N,R}
.
To streamline the presentation, we perform the calculations using a unified notation for

\Phi=(h,\phi)
and use generalized constant
\Theta_{N}
, where

\Theta_{N}=\frac{1}{4}\vartheta_{N,C}
for the tensor mode and

\Theta_{N}=\frac{3}{2}\vartheta_{N,R}
for the scalar mode with
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

The energy in the UV limit for the fourth-derivative model is given by

\displaystyle E^{\left(4\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}=\Theta_{0}\left[2\dddot{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}-\big(\ddot{\Phi}\big)^{2}\right]\,.

(81)

If the term
\Phi
is described by the oscillation solution
(
77
), we arrive at the following result

\displaystyle E^{\left(4\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,-\,\,\Theta_{0}\big\{\big[C_{0}k^{2}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega^{2}_{1}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\,2\big[C_{0}k\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)\,+\,C_{1}\omega_{1}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\times\left[C_{0}k^{3}\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}{\omega}_{1}^{3}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)\right]\Big\}\,.

(82)

Here and below we use the notations

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=m_{1}\sqrt{r^{2}+1}\,,\quad r=\frac{k}{m_{1}}\,,\quad{\nu}=\sqrt{r^{2}+1}\,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau=m_{1}t\,.

(83)

When
k^{2}\gg m^{2}_{1}
, we consider
r\gg 1
, which enables
the simplification
r^{2}+1\longrightarrow r^{2}
for the expressions
outside the trigonometric functions. After a small amount of algebra, we obtain the result

\displaystyle E^{\left(4\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,-\,m_{1}^{4}r^{4}\,\Theta_{0}\Big\{\big[C_{0}\cos\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\cos\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,2\big[C_{0}\sin\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\sin\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)\big]^{2}\Big\}\,,

(84)

the sign of the last expression depends on
\Theta_{0}
.
In case
\Theta_{0}<0
, the energy is positive.

For the sixth-derivative model the expression for the energy is given by

\displaystyle E^{\left(6\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,\Theta_{1}\big[\dddot{\Phi}^{2}-2\Phi^{(\textrm{IV})}\ddot{\Phi}+2\Phi^{(\textrm{V})}\dot{\Phi}\big]\,,

(85)

using the same approach as for the fourth-derivative model, we get

\displaystyle E^{\left(6\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,\Theta_{1}\Big\{\big[C_{0}k^{3}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{3}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{3}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,2\left[C_{0}k^{2}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{2}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{2}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\times\left[C_{0}k^{4}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{4}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{4}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,2\left[C_{0}k\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\,\times\left[C_{0}k^{5}\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{5}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{5}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)\right]\Big\}\,.\qquad

(86)

Introducing the notations

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=m_{2}\sqrt{r^{2}+\dfrac{{m}_{1}^{2}}{{m}_{2}^{2}}}\quad\omega_{2}=m_{2}\sqrt{r^{2}+1}\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad r=\frac{k}{M}\,,

(87)

where
M
represents the mass of the heaviest degree of freedom
in the higher-derivative model. In the present case,
M=m^{2}_{2}
.
This notation facilitates a comparison between the masses
m_{1}

and
m_{2}
with the wave vector
\vec{k}
.

In the UV limit, we consider
k\gg m_{2}>m_{1}
owing to the mass
hierarchy. This is equivalent to
r\gg m_{1}/m_{2}
and
r\gg 1
. As before,
we implement the approximation

r^{2}+m_{1}^{2}/m_{2}^{2}\longrightarrow r^{2}

and
r^{2}+1\longrightarrow r^{2}
, for the expressions outside
the trigonometric functions. Thus, we get

\displaystyle E^{\left(6\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,m_{2}^{6}\,r^{6}\,\Theta_{1}\Big\{3\big[C_{0}\cos\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\cos\left(\nu_{1,2}\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\cos\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{2}\right)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,2\big[C_{0}\sin\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\sin\left(\nu_{1,2}\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\sin\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{2}\right)\Big]^{2}\Big\}\,,\qquad\quad

(88)

with

\displaystyle\nu=\sqrt{r^{2}+1}\,\quad\nu^{2}_{1,2}=r^{2}+\dfrac{m^{2}_{1}}{m^{2}_{2}}\,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau=m_{2}\,t\,.

Similar to the previous
example, the sign of the energy depends only on
\Theta_{1}
.
The energy is defined positively if
\Theta_{1}>0
.

Finally, for the eight-order gravity, the energy is given by

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,\Theta_{2}\left[\left(\Phi^{(\textrm{IV})}\right)^{2}+2\Phi^{(\textrm{V})}\ddot{\Phi}-2\Phi^{(\textrm{VI})}\ddot{\Phi}+2\Phi^{(\textrm{VII})}\dot{\Phi}\right]\,,

(89)

and applying the same approach to
\Phi
as before, we get

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,-\,\,\Theta_{2}\Big\{\big[C_{0}k^{4}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{4}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{4}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{3}\omega_{3}^{4}\cos\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]^{2}+2\big[C_{0}k^{2}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega^{2}_{1}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{2}\omega_{2}^{2}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)+C_{3}\omega_{3}^{2}\cos\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]\big[C_{0}k^{6}\cos\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{1}\omega_{1}^{6}\cos\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{6}\cos\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)+C_{3}\omega_{3}^{6}\cos\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,2\big[C_{0}k\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{3}\omega_{3}\sin\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]\big[C_{0}k^{7}\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{7}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{2}\omega_{2}^{7}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)+C_{3}\omega_{3}^{7}\sin\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]+2\big[C_{0}k^{5}\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{1}\omega_{1}^{5}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{5}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)+C_{3}\omega_{3}^{5}\sin\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\times\Big[C_{0}k^{3}\sin\left(kt+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\omega_{1}^{3}\sin\left(\omega_{1}t+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\omega_{2}^{3}\sin\left(\omega_{2}t+\varphi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{3}\omega_{3}^{3}\sin\left(\omega_{3}t+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]\Big\}\,.

(90)

When the wave vector is much larger than the masses, we can make the substitutions

\displaystyle r^{2}+\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{m_{3}^{2}}\longrightarrow r^{2}\,,\qquad r^{2}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{m_{3}^{2}}\longrightarrow r^{2}\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\quad r^{2}+1\longrightarrow r^{2}\,,

(91)

assuming that
\,r=k/m_{3}\gg 1

\displaystyle E^{\left(8\right)}\Big|_{\textrm{UV}}\,=\,-\,\,m_{3}^{8}\,r^{8}\,\Theta_{2}\Big\{3\big[C_{0}\cos\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\cos\left(\nu_{1,3}\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)+C_{2}\cos\left(\nu_{2,3}\tau+\varphi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,C_{3}\cos\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]^{2}+4\big[C_{0}\sin\left(r\tau+\varphi_{0}\right)+C_{1}\sin\left(\nu_{1,3}\tau+\varphi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,C_{2}\sin\left(\nu_{2,3}\tau+\varphi_{2}\right)+C_{3}\sin\left(\nu\tau+\varphi_{3}\right)\big]^{2}\Big\}\,,

(92)

with

\displaystyle\nu=\sqrt{r^{2}+1}\,,\quad\nu^{2}_{1,3}=r^{2}+\dfrac{{m}^{2}_{1}}{{m}^{2}_{3}}\,,\quad\nu^{2}_{2,3}=r^{2}+\dfrac{{m}^{2}_{2}}{{m}^{2}_{3}}\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau=m_{3}\,t.

(93)

Thus, we find that if
\Theta_{2}>0
, the energy is
negative, whereas if
\Theta_{2}<0
, the energy is positively
defined.

Mechanism of stress relief by deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways and its effectiveness evaluation
Authors: Chen-long Yan, Lin-ming Dou, Zong-long Mu, Fang-zhou Lu, Lei-lei Jian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the Rockburst prevention challenges caused by the limitations of roof blasting in deep coal mines, the stress relief mechanism of floor strata was investigated through numerical simulations based on 195 field practices of cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways. Three real-time evaluation indices, namely the scalar seismic moment Bm, stress drop Bd and apparent stress Ba per unit charge, were proposed, and a four-level discriminant standard was established. Results show that the scheme effectively intercepts the horizontal stress transmission path by inducing rock mass damage in the floor. Under high tectonic stress conditions, the average vertical and horizontal stresses in key strata decrease by 29.38 and 12.30%, respectively. Simultaneously, the blasting induces the transfer of high-stress concentration zones into the deep surrounding rock, with the maximum offsets of vertical and horizontal stress peaks reaching 5.0 m and 6.0 m, respectively, achieving stress field reconstruction in both intensity and spatial distribution. Cross-validation with seismic wave computed tomography inversion confirms that Bm and Bd indices are highly sensitive in identifying ineffective stress relief events. Field engineering applications significantly reduce the frequency of high-energy microseismic events and stabilize seismic activity, validating the effectiveness of the evaluation system and providing a scientific basis for the quantitative assessment of deep dynamic disasters.Subject terms: Engineering, Natural hazards, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

High in-situ stress and its induced rock burst are the critical constraints in deep coal mining1. As a widely adopted stress relief measure in rock burst-prone mines both domestically and internationally, deep-hole blasting in the roadway roof is relatively mature and has accumulated extensive successful engineering experience2. However, when roof blasting is ineffective or constrained by complex geological conditions, it is urgent to explore a new stress relief mode to ensure mining safety. To this end, researchers have carried out stress relief practices using deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways based on field engineering conditions and conducted exploratory research on the corresponding mechanisms and evaluation methods3. Notably, compared with traditional roof blasting, blasting in floor drainage roadways exhibits significant differences in initiation horizons, blasting confinement conditions, and stress relief paths. Its unique stress relief mechanism and effectiveness evaluation system require in-depth analysis4.

Scientific evaluation of the stress relief effectiveness of deep-hole blasting is critical for ensuring effective rockburst prevention, and the establishment of a scientific evaluation system must be based on a profound understanding of the blasting energy conversion mechanism5. Zou et al.6 confirmed the macroscopic control effect of this technology on strong tremors by comparing the microseismic characteristics and stress distribution before and after blasting in the Dongtan Coal Mine, finding that the frequency of high-energy microseismic events decreased significantly. However, evaluations relying solely on the frequency of strong tremors and roadway deformation usually require long observation cycles, making it difficult to achieve real-time feedback for individual blasting events. To realize the precise quantitative evaluation of individual blasting effectiveness, real-time assessment utilizing microseismic parameters has become a research hotspot. Wojtecki et al.7 established a characteristic correlation model based on seismic source physical parameters and successfully verified its effectiveness in the Upper Silesian Coal Basin in Poland. Konicek et al.8 proposed the seismic effect (SE) value method by combining seismic monitoring with charge weight data, ensuring the safe mining of the longwall working face in the Lazy Mine. On this basis, Kan et al.9 further proposed the microseismic energy index per unit charge Be and its classification system, achieving preliminary quantification of stress relief effectiveness. However, existing indices mostly focus on the conversion relationship between blasting energy and microseismic energy, often neglecting the contribution of individual blasting to the stress drop of the surrounding rock and the deformation characteristics of the source zone, and lack comprehensive evaluation from multi-dimensional perspectives such as energy, stress, and damage10.

In summary, research on the stress relief mechanism of deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways remains limited, and a systematic evaluation system for its effectiveness—a core component of engineering practice—has yet to be established. Therefore, focusing on the specific conditions of floor drainage roadway blasting, this study first employs numerical simulation to explore the internal mechanisms of stope structure adjustment and stress evolution. Subsequently, by integrating microseismic focal mechanism solutions and spectral characteristic parameters, a multi-dimensional real-time evaluation method is proposed, utilizing the scalar seismic moment Bm, stress drop Bd, and apparent stress Ba per unit charge as key indices. Finally, the effectiveness of the proposed indices is cross-validated through the application of seismic wave computed tomography inversion of the stress field.

The return air gateway of the 6126-1 working face in a coal mine is significantly affected by complex stope structural characteristics. During the excavation process, the high concentration of in-situ stress resulted in frequent rockburst events, posing a severe threat to both underground operational safety and mining efficiency. Taking the rockburst event that occurred at 21:55:49 on January 4, 2024, as an example, the energy reached 5.15 × 105J with a focal elevation of 1212 m. The source was located near the intersection of the 6125-1 construction cross-cut and the 6124-1 intake cross-cut.

This tremor resulted in severe roadway deformation over an 81 m section of the 6126-1 return air gateway. Specifically, the maximum floor heave reached a depth of 2 m, the roof subsided by 0.3 m, and the convergence of the two roadway ribs reached 1.2 m. This deformation was accompanied by multiple anchor cable breakages and the destruction of individual hydraulic props. Despite the prior implementation of conventional measures such as large-diameter stress-relief boreholes and roof deep-hole blasting around the roadway, the large-diameter boreholes suffered from significant collapse under high in-situ stress. Consequently, conventional stress-relief methods failed to eliminate high-energy tremors, and the overall destressing effect remained insignificant; the resulting floor heave is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Schematic diagram of roadway floor heave.

The primary coal seam of this mine is Coal Seam No. 2, which has an average thickness of 19.61 m and an average burial depth of approximately 985 m. The roof of the coal seam is predominantly composed of thin-layered siltstone and fine sandstone, while the floor consists of gravelly siltstone. Two existing roadways, the 6124 and 6125 floor drainage roadways, are located in the sandstone layer beneath the coal seam for gas drainage. These roadways provide the necessary construction space for implementing deep-seated horizon stress relief. The mine was equipped with an SOS microseismic monitoring system. Calibrated by production blasts, the horizontal localization error of the system for signal acquisition was constrained within 5 m, and the vertical error was within 10 m. Such spatial positioning accuracy fully satisfies the reliability requirements for the subsequent calculation of microseismic source parameters and spatial distribution analysis.

Given the limited effectiveness of traditional roof stress relief and boreholes around the roadway, this study utilizes the unique horizon of the floor drainage roadways to design and implement a deep-hole stress relief scheme involving upward cross-layer blasting. The objective of this scheme is to create artificial fracture zones along key stress transmission paths via cross-layer boreholes in the floor. The field design involved drilling oblique deep holes from the 6124 and 6125 floor drainage roadways toward the area surrounding the 6126-1 return air gateway. The elevation angles of the boreholes were adjusted based on the sectional positions to ensure they reached the coal-rock interface, as shown in the layout of field blasting boreholes in Fig. 2. Between January 1, 2024, and January 1, 2025, a total of 195 deep-hole blasting events were conducted over eight rounds in the 6126-1 area.

This study utilized the microseismic monitoring data from all 195 blasting events in the region, along with construction parameters such as the blasting charge weight and the charge decoupling coefficient. The research aims to conduct an in-depth investigation into the stress relief mechanism of deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways and to perform a multi-dimensional, real-time scientific evaluation of the stress relief effectiveness based on seismic source parameters.

Mechanisms of stress relief via deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways

Damage and fracturing mechanisms of coal-rock mass under blasting loads

The essence of stress relief via deep-hole blasting lies in harnessing explosive energy to induce large-scale damage in high-stress coal-rock masses, thereby fundamentally altering the mechanical properties and stress transmission paths of the rock mass. This process is characterized as a nonlinear dynamic process involving the synergistic action of explosive shock waves and detonation gases.

Upon initiation, the high-pressure detonation wave pulverizes the near-field rock mass to form a crushed zone; subsequently, the attenuated stress wave exploits the extremely low tensile strength of the rock to induce radial initial cracks, constructing an initial damage framework. Subsequently, the high-pressure detonation gas flows into the cracks through a long-duration ‘gas-wedging’ effect and exerts pressure on the crack walls, driving the fractures to penetrate and propagate into the deep rock mass. Compared with instantaneous impact loading, the detonation gas serves as the primary driver for inducing long-distance and large-scale fracture networks; the resulting fracture scale physically corresponds to the characteristics of the scalar seismic moment recorded in microseismic monitoring. In the engineering practice of floor drainage roadways, the initial damage induced by the shock wave and the persistent driving of the detonation gas jointly facilitate the formation of a dense fracture network in the floor sandstone, providing the physical prerequisite for effective stress relief11.

Numerical simulation of stress relief blasting mechanisms in floor drainage roadways

Based on the actual spatial relationship between the 6126-1 working face and its floor drainage roadways, this study simulated different tectonic stress environments by varying the lateral pressure coefficient to investigate its influence on stress relief effectiveness. Specifically, three tectonic stress conditions—normal, high, and intense—were established. Numerical simulations were conducted using FLAC3D to quantitatively reveal the evolution characteristics and distribution patterns of the surrounding rock stress state under various tectonic stress backgrounds. The model dimensions were designed as 200 m × 80 m, aiming to encompass the 6126-1 return air gateway and the underlying 6124 and 6125 floor drainage roadways. Grid refinement was applied to the central part of the model, specifically within the blasting-affected zones and the areas surrounding the roadways, to accurately capture the propagation of dynamic stress waves and the process of fracture evolution (Fig. 3).

The simulation results of horizontal stress under different lateral pressure coefficients indicate that the stress relief effectiveness of cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways is significantly influenced by the tectonic stress environment. Under the high tectonic stress environment with a lateral pressure coefficient λ = 1.5, the scheme demonstrates the most ideal stress regulation effect. The average horizontal stress decreased from − 3.11E + 07 Pa to -2.72E + 07 Pa, representing an average stress drop of 3.82 MPa and a reduction of 12.30%. Conversely, in lower stress environments where is 0.8 and 1.2, the average stress drops were only 0.032 MPa and − 0.13 MPa, respectively, with variation magnitudes of less than 1%. This suggests that the stress relief technique is more applicable to high-stress concentration zones.

Regarding peak stress distribution and peak shift, the evolution patterns differ markedly across different conditions. At =1.5, the peak stress value on the lower side decreased significantly from 45.3 MPa to 38.6 MPa. Although the peak stress point shifted 10 m toward the roadway edge, the peak stress point in the upper region exhibited a characteristic of transferring into the deep surrounding rock. Its distance from the roadway edge increased from 6 m to 12 m, representing an offset of 6 m. In contrast, at =0.8, the peak stress points on both the upper and lower sides shifted slightly toward the roadway, with offsets of 2 m and 3 m, respectively.

Comprehensive analysis demonstrates that under high lateral pressure coefficient conditions =1.5, cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways achieves a significant reduction in the average stress of key strata by inducing the redistribution of surrounding rock stress, while guiding the high-stress concentration zones of the upper surrounding rock to transfer toward the deep interior. This stress field reconstruction effect is not significant in low lateral pressure environments, further confirming the superiority of adopting this scheme for stress relief treatment in deep mines with high tectonic stress.

The spatial distribution of the plastic zone directly reflects the degree of rock mass damage induced by blasting loads and the subsequent changes in its mechanical continuity, serving as a critical basis for analyzing the structural control function of stress-relief blasting. A comparison of the plastic zone extent before and after blasting reveals that the process causes not only localized physical failure but also alters the load transfer paths of the surrounding rock through artificially created discontinuous fracture zones. This structural transformation constitutes the physical foundation for achieving stress field reconstruction.

The development characteristics of the plastic zone exhibit significant variability under different tectonic stress environments. Under the normal stress condition with a lateral pressure coefficient of =0.8, the expansion of the plastic zone around the roadway post-blasting is the most intense. The floor rock mass underwent large-scale shear and tensile failure under the synergistic action of explosive shock waves and detonation gases. When the lateral pressure coefficient increases to 1.2, although the horizontal compression effect is enhanced and the extent of the plastic zone is reduced compared to the =0.8 case, large-scale plastic damage is still maintained in the key strata of the floor. This indicates that in low-to-medium tectonic stress environments, blasting energy can relatively easily overcome the confining pressure of the rock mass, inducing extensive physical fracture spaces.

Notably, under the high tectonic stress environment with a lateral pressure coefficient of =1.5, the extent of the plastic zone after stress-relief blasting did not exhibit the significant expansion observed in low lateral pressure conditions. This phenomenon reflects the confining effect of extremely high static loads on stress wave propagation and reveals the unique mechanism of blasting stress relief under high-stress environments. Under this condition, the blasting energy did not blindly expand the damage area; instead, it precisely induced a mutation in the mechanical properties of the key strata along the critical paths of stress transmission. Comparing the stress evolution data reveals that localized plastic damage at this stage is sufficient to effectively intercept the continuous transmission of horizontal stress, forcing the peaks of vertical and horizontal stress to shift significantly into the deep surrounding rock, with maximum offsets reaching 5.0 m and 6.0 m, respectively.

In summary, the structural control function of cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways is highly targeted. It does not achieve stress relief by indefinitely expanding the range of destruction, but rather by inducing moderate plastic damage in key strata to construct a mechanical barrier that prevents stress accumulation toward the roadway. This mechanism achieves the transfer of surrounding rock stress to the deep interior, alleviating the degree of static load accumulation around the roadway from a structural source by optimizing the spatial structure of stress distribution. This conclusion also provides structural-level explanatory support for the simulation results mentioned earlier, where the average stress reduction reached 29.38% under the high tectonic stress environment.

Mechanisms of stress relief via cross-layer deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways

Deep-hole cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways alters the mechanical structure and stress transmission characteristics of the surrounding rock by artificially creating discontinuous fracture zones within the floor strata. Its stress relief mechanism is primarily manifested in two aspects: the overall reduction of stress levels and the spatial displacement of high-stress concentration zones.

Significant reduction in stress amplitude. Numerical simulation results indicate that the regulation of surrounding rock stress by this scheme exhibits distinct non-uniformity. Regarding vertical stress, the blasting demonstrates a highly universal pressure reduction effect; across various conditions with lateral pressure coefficients ranging from 0.8 to 1.5, the average vertical stress reduction remains above 24%. Notably, in a high tectonic stress environment with =1.5, the reduction reaches 29.38%. In contrast, the reduction of horizontal stress shows a high dependency on the tectonic stress environment, with an average stress drop of 12.3% at =1.5, while no significant change is observed in low lateral pressure environments. This suggests that by altering the physical properties of the rock mass, blasting operations can induce more intense stress release in areas with high static stress accumulation.

Summary of conclusions. Overall, the stress relief mechanism of deep-hole cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways lies in breaking the floor rock strata to intercept the continuous transmission paths of high horizontal stress and forcing the vertical stress load to transfer toward the stable rock mass in the deep interior, thereby promoting the development of the plastic zone around the roadway. Effectiveness evaluation of deep-hole blasting stress relief in floor drainage roadways

Calculation methods for stress-relief evaluation indices based on source parameters

The scientific evaluation of deep-hole blasting stress relief effectiveness is the core for guiding rockburst prevention practices in deep coal mines. The physical essence of deep-hole blasting is a complex dynamic process of transforming explosive chemical energy into rock fragmentation energy, plastic deformation energy of the medium, and radiated seismic energy. In field monitoring, the radiated seismic energy captured by microseismic systems represents only a small fraction of the theoretical total energy of the explosives. The quantitative relationship between the two is comprehensively constrained by the properties of the rock mass, blasting parameters, and the initial in-situ stress environment. Therefore, analyzing microseismic source characteristics induced per unit charge weight is the physical basis for quantitatively revealing the blasting mechanism and evaluating stress relief effectiveness12. Furthermore, traditional stress monitoring or roadway deformation observations require several days or even weeks, exhibiting significant time lag. In contrast, source parameter indices calculated based on microseismic waveforms can be automatically computed by the system within minutes after blasting, demonstrating significant engineering value.

The evaluation theory in this study is based on the source model proposed by Brune13 and further developed by Madariaga14. By analyzing the spectral characteristics of seismic waves, this model can effectively extract key parameters describing the physical scale of the source. At the engineering application level, the Seismic Effect (SE) index proposed by Konicek et al.8 realized the first quantitative evaluation of the ratio between blasting microseismic energy and charge weight. Subsequently, by deeply analyzing the energy conversion process of energy-storing rocks during blasting, Kan et al.9 proposed the blasting effectiveness index based on the SE index. However, the existing index primarily focuses on the scalar evaluation of energy density, which fails to comprehensively reflect the magnitude of stress drop in the source zone and the extent of damage to rock mass integrity induced by blasting. Considering the unique stress relief path of cross-layer blasting in floor drainage roadways, a singular energy-based index exhibits limitations in evaluating the effectiveness of multi-round, large-scale stress relief operations. To address this, building upon the index, this study innovatively introduces three new indices: the scalar seismic moment per unit charge (), the stress drop per unit charge , and the apparent stress per unit charge . These are designed to construct a theoretically robust and multi-dimensionally enriched real-time evaluation system for deep-hole blasting stress relief effectiveness.

Validation of evaluation indices based on CT inversion

The stress field seismic wave CT inversion technology utilizes mining-induced natural seismic events as passive excitation sources. Through matrix operations on large volumes of microseismic waveform data, it can determine the high-stress distribution and its evolutionary trends across mining faces and extensive surrounding areas20. Discussion

This study found that despite the implementation of multiple rounds of deep-hole blasting, the overall regional stress level (as reflected by the CT wave velocity anomaly coefficient) did not exhibit a monotonic downward trend. This phenomenon is not indicative of blasting stress relief failure; rather, it reflects the non-linear coupling relationship between ‘active blasting-induced stress relief’ and ‘dynamic mining-induced stress increments’ in deep mines. Since the recovery operations at the 6126-1 working face were conducted simultaneously with the stress-relief blasting in the floor drainage roadway, the contribution of the advanced abutment pressure and the drastic structural adjustments of the surrounding rock to the regional stress field often outweighed the stress reduction brought by an individual blasting event.

In this context of intense disturbances, the role of stress-relief blasting is primarily manifested as the ‘transfer of local stress peaks’ and the ‘guidance of energy release pathways’ The evolution of the and indices demonstrates that the blasting successfully triggered the active release of accumulated elastic energy within the surrounding rock. Although this release may be obscured by the ‘background noise’ of mining-induced stress fluctuations in macroscopic CT velocity maps, it successfully achieved the integrity reconstruction of the rock mass at the microseismic scale, thereby effectively mitigating the suddenness and intensity of rockburst occurrences.

Comparative validation against CT inversion results reveals that different indices exhibit distinctive characteristics in evaluating stress-relief effectiveness: shows extremely high sensitivity in identifying ‘high-energy release events’ This indicates that the conversion efficiency from explosive energy to microseismic energy directly reflects the intensity of the disturbance to the surrounding rock, serving as a critical indicator for evaluating the extent of the stress-relief influence. and demonstrate excellent robustness in ‘excluding ineffective events’ By normalizing the charge weight, these two indices filter out the interference from the explosive’s intrinsic energy, precisely isolating events that genuinely triggered active rock mass fracture. This suggests that in complex engineering environments, an evaluation system based on normalized source parameters is more capable of unveiling the essence of the ‘rock mass response’ than a singular energy-based index. performs relatively poorly, which may be attributed to the physical definition of apparent stress itself. is strongly influenced by the source rupture velocity and the heterogeneity of the medium. In highly fractured rock masses, the large fluctuations in energy radiation efficiency lead to certain limitations when using as an independent evaluation metric.

Conclusions

This study addresses the key issue of high geostress accumulation and the resulting severe floor heave and rockburst risks in deep coal mine roadways. A stress relief scheme utilizing cross-layer deep-hole blasting in floor drainage roadways is proposed to proactively mitigate these hazards. Based on the theories of rock mechanics and elastic energy release, the mechanisms of stress magnitude reduction and spatial redistribution induced by this blasting method are systematically described. Furthermore, a multi-dimensional instantaneous evaluation index system incorporating Bm, Bd, and Ba is established to quantify the effectiveness of blasting operations from the perspectives of energy release, stress drop, and rock mass integrity. Building upon this foundation, the main conclusions from the theoretical analysis, numerical simulations, and engineering validation can be summarized as follows:

Mechanism of Stress Relief via Cross-layer Deep-hole Blasting in Floor Drainage Roadways: Numerical simulation results indicate that this scheme effectively cuts off the transmission pathways of high horizontal stress by inducing fractures and damage within the floor rock mass. Under high tectonic stress conditions, the average vertical and horizontal stresses in the key strata of the floor were reduced by 28.38% and 12.30%, respectively. Concurrently, the blasting guided the transfer of high-stress concentration zones into the deeper rock mass, with maximum offsets of the vertical and horizontal stress peaks reaching 5.0 m and 6.0 m, respectively. The synergy between stress magnitude reduction and spatial distribution reconstruction significantly lowers the accumulation of static loads around the roadway and expands the range of the low-stress protective zone.

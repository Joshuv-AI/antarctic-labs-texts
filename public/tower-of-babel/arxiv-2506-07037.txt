KG2QA: Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Communication Standards Question Answering
Zhongze Luo, Weixuan Wan, Qizhi Zheng, Yanhong Bai, Jingyun Sun, Jian Wang, Dan Wang

Abstract
There are many types of standards in the field of communication. The traditional consulting model has a long cycle and relies on the knowledge and experience of experts, making it difficult to meet the rapidly developing technological demands. This paper combines the fine-tuning of large language models with the construction of knowledge graphs to implement an intelligent consultation and question-answering system for communication standards. The experimental results show that after LoRA tuning on the constructed dataset of 6,587 questions and answers in the field of communication standards, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct demonstrates outstanding professional capabilities in the field of communication standards on the test set. BLEU-4 rose from 18.8564 to 66.8993, and evaluation indicators such as ROUGE also increased significantly, outperforming the fine-tuning effect of the comparison model Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Based on the ontology framework containing 6 entity attributes and 10 relation attributes, a knowledge graph of the communication standard domain containing 13,906 entities and 13,524 relations was constructed, showing a relatively good query accuracy rate. The intelligent consultation and question-answering system enables the fine-tuned model on the server side to access the locally constructed knowledge graph and conduct graphical retrieval of key information first, which is conducive to improving the question-answering effect. The evaluation using DeepSeek as the Judge on the test set shows that our RAG framework enables the fine-tuned model to improve the scores at all five angles, with an average score increase of 2.26%. And combined with web services and API interfaces, it has achieved very good results in terms of interaction experience and back-end access, and has very good practical application value.



Keywords:
Large language models; LoRA fine-tuning; domain-limited knowledge graph; retrieval-augmented generation; communication standards

Introduction

The rapid advancement of technology in the communication sector has led to an exponential increase in communication standards. Traditionally, consulting these standards requires significant expertise and manual intervention, often involving extensive periods for information retrieval and interpretation. Such conventional methods are inefficient and unable to keep pace with rapid technological developments, necessitating smarter, more automated approaches for consulting and knowledge management.

Recent developments in artificial intelligence, particularly Large Language Models, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text across general domains
[
37
]
. However, their performance in specialized fields, like communication standards, remains limited due to the sparse representation of specialized terminologies and domain-specific knowledge in pre-training datasets. To address this limitation, fine-tuning methodologies such as Low-Rank Adaptation have been employed, significantly enhancing the semantic comprehension and contextual accuracy of LLMs in niche areas
[
5
,
6
,
42
]
.

Complementing LLMs, knowledge graphs offer a structured approach to represent complex, interconnected domain-specific knowledge
[
7
,
8
]
. Particularly in narrowly defined domains, such as communication standards, domain-specific knowledge graphs facilitate efficient and precise information retrieval. By constructing a carefully designed ontology structure and leveraging LLMs for entity and relationship extraction, high-accuracy knowledge graphs can be established, markedly improving retrieval performance
[
38
,
39
]
.

In this paper, we proposed a novel integration framework, termed KG2QA, which combines domain-specific knowledge graphs and fine-tuned LLMs using Retrieval-Augmented Generation methodologies. Our approach enhances intelligent consulting services by achieving precise and swift query responses, enriched by structured domain-specific knowledge. Experimental results demonstrate substantial improvements in query accuracy, response speed, and overall user interaction experience compared to traditional and general-purpose AI consulting methods.

In summary, the main contributions are as follows:

1)

Perform efficient domain adaptation of LLMs using LoRA fine-tuning for communication standards QA tasks.

2)

Construct a structured knowledge graph for communication standards based on LLM-assisted triple extraction.

3)

Develop a RAG-based question answering system integrating the fine-tuned LLM with the domain-specific knowledge graph.

4)

Provide a fully open-sourced, reproducible pipeline for domain-specific QA in communication standards.

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) updates weights by adding the product of two low-rank matrices, B and A, as shown in
W_{0}+\Delta W=W_{0}+BA
. If the original weight matrix
W_{0}
has dimensions
d\times k
(the original text describes a
d\times d
matrix, so
k=d
), then matrix
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
and matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k}
(i.e.,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}
if
k=d
). The parameter
r
is called the rank and is chosen such that
r\ll\min(d,k)
. For instance, if LoRA with a rank
r=16
is used for a
d\times d
original matrix, matrix
A
would have dimensions
16\times d
(and
B
would be
d\times 16
). This greatly reduces the number of parameters that need to be trained. However, a smaller
r
might lead to information compression that is efficient but not fully comprehensive, while a larger
r
might capture more comprehensive information but risks including redundant or ineffective knowledge. For a pre-trained weight matrix
W_{0}
, the fine-tuning process restricts its update method to the form
W_{0}+BA
. During training, the original parameters
W_{0}
are frozen; although
W_{0}
participates in both forward and backward propagation, its gradients are not computed, and its parameters are not updated. Thus, the model does not directly update the original weight matrix
W_{0}
; instead, the fine-tuning process primarily involves learning the low-rank matrices B and A. Additionally, a scaling hyperparameter
\alpha
is introduced and applied to the output of the low-rank adaptation, as seen in the transformation
h=W_{0}x+\frac{\alpha}{r}BAx
. During inference,
W_{0}
represents the existing knowledge, and
\frac{\alpha}{r}BA
represents the new knowledge. The old and new knowledge are combined. Compared to the original model, this method typically does not introduce additional inference latency as the product BA can be merged with
W_{0}
.

BLEU-4
refers to the BLEU score calculated using n-grams up to length 4. BP is the Brevity Penalty, which penalizes candidate translations that are shorter than the reference translations. The term exp denotes the exponential function, and log signifies the natural logarithm. The summation
\sum_{n=1}^{4}
indicates a sum over n-gram lengths from 1 to 4.
p_{n}
represents the modified precision for n-grams of length n. Within the
p_{n}
formula,
g
refers to an n-gram.
\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}
signifies summation over all unique n-grams of length n found in the candidate sentence or corpus (
\mathcal{G}_{n}
denotes this set of n-grams).
\text{Count}_{\text{clip}}(g)
is the clipped count of n-gram g; it’s the number of times g appears in the candidate translation, limited by (clipped at) its maximum frequency in any single reference translation.
\text{Count}_{\text{cand}}(g)
is the count of n-gram g in the candidate translation. In the BP formula,
r_{\text{ref}}
(denoted as r in the original image’s formula) is the total length of the reference corpus (or the length of the closest reference sentence for sentence-level BLEU), and
c_{\text{cand}}
(denoted as c in the original image’s formula) is the total length of the candidate translation corpus (or the length of the candidate sentence).

ROUGE-1
measures the overlap of unigrams (single words) between the candidate and reference summaries.
ROUGE-2
measures the overlap of bigrams (pairs of consecutive words). In these formulas,
w
represents a unigram, and
b
represents a bigram. The summation
\sum_{w\in\text{Ref}}
is over all unigrams w found in the reference summary (or set of reference summaries). Similarly,
\sum_{b\in\text{Ref}_{2}}
is over all bigrams b found in the reference summary (or set of reference summaries).
\text{Count}_{\text{cand}}(w)
or
\text{Count}_{\text{cand}}(b)
is the count of the unigram w or bigram b in the candidate summary.
\text{Count}_{\text{ref}}(w)
or
\text{Count}_{\text{ref}}(b)
is the count of the unigram w or bigram b in the reference summary. The min function returns the minimum of the two counts, effectively finding the number of overlapping n-grams.

ROUGE-L
is based on the Longest Common Subsequence (LCS).
L
is the length of the LCS between the candidate and reference summaries.
\text{LCS}(X,Y)
is the function that determines this longest common subsequence, where
X
typically represents the candidate summary (as a sequence of words) and
Y
represents the reference summary.
R_{\text{LCS}}
is the LCS-based recall, calculated as L divided by
|Y|
, where
|Y|
is the length (number of words) of the reference summary Y.
P_{\text{LCS}}
is the LCS-based precision, calculated as L divided by
|X|
, where
|X|
is the length of the candidate summary X.
F_{\text{LCS}}
is the LCS-based F-measure, which combines
R_{\text{LCS}}
and
P_{\text{LCS}}
. The parameter
\beta
is used to weight recall more or less than precision; in the common case where
\beta=1
, recall and precision are weighted equally.

predict_runtime
metric reflects the time consumption of model loading and preparation in the inference stage, with the unit being seconds. The shorter the time, the faster the model goes online and the inference starts.
runtime
measures the total time required for a model to complete one inference throughout the entire inference process, with the unit being seconds. The lower the value, the better. In addition,
samples_per_second
and
steps_per_second
further describe the throughput rate of model inference, representing the number of samples completed per second and the number of steps completed per second respectively. The higher the value, the better the inference efficiency.

Knowledge Graph Construction

In the process of ontology design, the domain characteristics of communication standards were fully considered, a simple and effective ontology was designed, and the ontology design was visualized using Protege, which is conducive to enhancing the rationality of the knowledge graph constructed. We adopted the knowledge graph construction process based on large language models, which is divided into three steps in total: head entity recognition, latent relationship recognition, and tail entity recognition. These three steps are carried out successively by calling the DeepSeek V3 API. Finally, a complete ternary is extracted and the knowledge graph is uploaded to the Neo4j platform for visualization and evaluation.

4.2
Ternary Object Extraction Framework

The head entity recognition code entityPdf.py extracts text from the PDF document and calls the DeepSeek V3 API for head entity extraction. Read the text of the PDF document first; Then construct the prompt words and request the model to extract the head entities with type annotations; Each entity contains the name of the head entity, the type of the head entity, the context text snippet, and the confidence level; Finally, output the entity list in structured JSON format and write it to the output folder.

The latent Relationship identification code relation.py is used to further analyze the context of the extracted head entity and extract the relationship between it and the tail entity. First, read the JSON file generated after the head entity recognition; Then, group and process by context index to ensure that the context of each entity is complete and unchanged; Then call the API to analyze the context and extract the relationships in the triples; Finally, for each entity, structured relationship information is generated, including the relationship type, corresponding tail entity, context original text fragment, and confidence level.

The tailEntity recognition code tailentity-py is used to further refine the tail entities in triples, ensuring they meet the requirements and do not duplicate the head entities. First, read the JSON file of the extracted relationship; Call the model context by context, extract the tail entity in the original context. The tail entity must not have the same name as the head entity. Only the entities whose types are within the allowed set are extracted, and the confidence level must be higher than the threshold. The returned fields include the name of the tail entity, the type of the tail entity, the original context snippet, and the confidence level. Finally, a new tail entity supplementary structure is generated and merged into the context result.

We designed a complete framework for extracting knowledge graph ternary from PDF documents based on large language models. This process has a clear division of labor and modularly realizes the extraction task, ensuring the accuracy and robustness of the knowledge graph. The pseudo-code of this process is shown in Figure

, the collection of all extracted candidate relations and tail entities is stored in
R\_T[f]
. To ensure the quality of the extracted facts, we employed a confidence threshold,
\theta
(e.g., 0.8), to filter out relations with low confidence scores. For each tail entity
t^{\prime}
that passes this filter, we further determined its attribute type, denoted as
t\_type
. Finally, all validated knowledge graph triples, consisting of a head entity
h
, a relation
r
, and a tail entity
t^{\prime}
, are aggregated into
KG[f]
. This structure,
KG[f]
, represents the final knowledge graph constructed from the single source file
f
.

Process the JSON file obtained after tail entity recognition. It is divided into two steps: First, conduct entity processing. Both the head entity and the tail entity in the JSON file after supplementing the tail entity structure are extracted according to the entity type to generate the CSV UTF-8 format result of a single entity type; Then, relationship processing is carried out. The head entity and tail entity of the relationship are matched according to their ids in the corresponding entity type CSV UTF-8 file to form a ternary, and the result is saved in the roles.csv file. In the entity type CSV UTF-8 file, the format is ID-name-LABEL, which are respectively the serial number of this entity name in this type, the entity name, and the entity type. In the CSV UTF-8 file of the relation type, the format is from to relation, which are the head entity name ID, tail entity name ID and relation type of this ternary respectively.

5.1
Create the model used by Ollama

Ollama is an open-source tool for running large language models locally, allowing users to easily deploy and manage various AI models on servers. It has the advantages of privacy protection, no cost, and the ability to ignore network issues. The version of Ollama is 0.7.0.

The obtained LoRA fine-tuned and merged Qwen model is deployed on the server using Ollama, mainly divided into three steps. Firstly, the convert_hf_to_gguf.py conversion script of llama.cpp is used for the safetensors model conversion to obtain the convert.bin model; The convert.bin model was quantized to obtain the quantized.bin model with 4.13GB. The quantization format is q4_0, which belongs to 4-bit quantization. That is, a model parameter is represented by 4 bits, aiming to significantly reduce the model size and computing resource requirements while maintaining the model performance as much as possible. Build quantized.bin as the model used by Ollama, and finally obtain the my-custom-model model in Ollama.

5.2.2
Ollama Model Remote Access to Knowledge Graph

Open the external IP access permission of Neo4j on the local computer to enable the Ollama model on the server side to access the knowledge graph uploaded on the local computer. Also, ensure that the local Windows firewall allows inbound connections on port 7687. Bolt is the binary protocol of Neo4j, and the client driver connects using the Bolt protocol, its default port is 7687. After the server side shows that the Bolt protocol connection port of Neo4j can be remotely accessed, it indicates that the my-custom model uploaded in Ollama can be used to remotely access the knowledge graph stored in Neo4j. That is, the model after fine-tuning, merging, and format conversion can enhance model inference in combination with the retrieval content of the knowledge graph. The model is equipped with the code kg2llm.py for knowledge graph reasoning to remotely access the knowledge graph, and a question-answering system based on the knowledge graph is implemented, which is mainly divided into two parts.

The first part is to access the knowledge graph. Firstly, the keyword retrieval of the knowledge graph is implemented. Users input keywords, and the system retrieves the nodes related to the keywords and their relationship information from the Neo4j graph database by calling the methods of the KnowledgeGraphQA class. After retrieval, the context is displayed. The relevant knowledge graph information retrieved is formatted and presented to the user to help them understand the knowledge background of the current query.

The second part is that the model generates questions and answers based on the context. After users see the relevant knowledge information, they can input specific questions. The system calls the language model interface in KnowledgeGraphQA based on the retrieved context to generate professional answers. Finally, users can choose whether to conduct multiple rounds of interaction. Users can repeatedly ask questions based on the current context. You can also restart the search for new keywords by entering "new", and "exit" to exit the system.

5.2.3
Quantitative Evaluation of Answer Quality using an LLM-based Judge

To quantitatively assess the performance improvement brought by the knowledge graph, we designed an evaluation experiment that complements our earlier analysis. While the metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE, were effective for validating the success of the fine-tuning process by measuring how well the model learned the style and lexical patterns of the training data, they have inherent limitations when evaluating the specific contribution of our RAG system. Specifically, an answer enhanced by the knowledge graph may contain accurate and relevant facts that are not present in the static reference answer from the test set. In such a scenario, metrics based on strict lexical overlap would unfairly penalize the more comprehensive and factually superior answer.

Therefore, to capture these crucial aspects of semantic quality and factual enrichment, we adopted the more advanced "Large Language Model as a Judge" paradigm for this stage of the evaluation. This approach allows for a more holistic assessment of answer quality, a process automated by the eval.py script. The experiment was designed to compare our proposed KG+LLM system against a baseline LLM-Only system, both utilizing the same fine-tuned model to isolate the impact of the knowledge graph. We employed the DeepSeek V3 API as an impartial external judge, tasking it with scoring each generated answer on a 0.0 to 1.0 scale across five dimensions: similarity to reference, fluency, coherence, relevance to question, and factual accuracy. Critically, the judge was instructed to value additional correct information from the KG-enhanced answer when assessing factual accuracy. Prompt of LLM-based Judge is shown in
8
.

Prompt of LLM-based Judge

The aggregated results, presented in Table
6
, clearly demonstrate the significant advantages of the KG+LLM approach, as it outperformed the baseline across all metrics. The most substantial gains were observed in Similarity to Reference, Relevance to Question and Factual Accuracy. This quantitative evaluation robustly validates that our RAG framework, by integrating a fine-tuned LLM with a domain-specific knowledge graph, yields measurably superior and more reliable answers, particularly in dimensions that traditional metrics cannot adequately capture, and improves the scores at all five angles, with an average score increase of 2.26%.

Figure 12:
Swagger interactive Documentation of the Knowledge Graph QA API

The knowledge graph question-answering system is independently encapsulated as an API service using FastAPI. Compared with deploying externally accessible web services using Flask, it has multiple advantages. FastAPI supports asynchronous programming and can handle concurrent requests more efficiently. For I/O-intensive tasks such as knowledge graph queries and model inference, it has a faster response speed. Moreover, FastAPI automatically generates clear Swagger documentation through type annotations, which greatly facilitates the use and maintenance of the interface. Although Flask can also support API development, it requires additional configuration documentation and asynchronous support, and the maintenance cost is relatively high. FastAPI places more emphasis on modularizing services in the form of apis in its design, making it suitable for building flexible back-end systems. Web services deployed with Flask focus more on providing user interfaces and are suitable for small projects or rapid development of interactive pages, but they are relatively limited in terms of high concurrency and distributed scalability. However, the advantage of Flask in deploying Web services lies in its mature ecosystem. Developers have more comprehensive support for template rendering and traditional web development, making it suitable for application scenarios that require rich interactive interfaces. FastAPI is more suitable for the API backend, especially when the knowledge graph question-answering capability is called by other applications as a service. The flexible and standardized RESTful interface provided by FastAPI offers a better experience.

6
Conclusion

Based on the problems of high knowledge complexity and low efficiency of consulting services in the field of communication standards, this paper proposes an intelligent consulting service system in the field of communication standards based on large language models and knowledge graphs. The intelligent consultation system for communication standards has good application and promotion value. It improves the processing efficiency of consultation in the field of communication standards, reduces the burden of information search for communication engineers in their daily work, and has certain universality. This method can be extended and applied to the standardization consultation services of other specialties.

This paper also has some deficiencies. The currently constructed knowledge graph is mainly static knowledge, and real-time knowledge update has not been set. The dynamic update service of the knowledge graph needs to be continuously improved in the future. How to reduce the dependence degree of its labeled data during the fine-tuning process and achieve good robustness of the model under limited labeled data is a problem worthy of study in future improvement work; In addition, it is necessary to continuously improve the system’s ability to understand and reason about knowledge in more complex contexts, so as to make the interaction effect and application scenario effect more perfect.

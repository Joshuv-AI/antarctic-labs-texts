Interacting point processes
Fabrizio Cinque, Enzo Orsingher Department of Statistical Sciences, Sapienza University of Rome, Italy

Abstract
We study two different types of vector point processes with interacting components, introducing a migration-type effect. The first case concerns two groups which modify their states with rate functions depending on time only. This yields a representation of the vector process in terms of independent non-homogeneous Skellam processes. In the general case, the decomposition involves independent Poisson processes. The second model is a birth-death-migration vector process. In the case of the linear death-migration we show that, for a fixed time instant, the vector is equal in distribution to the sum of two independent Multinomial random variables. As a byproduct we derive the distribution of a pure migration process. Finally, we study the described vector processes time-changed with the inverse of Bernstein subordinator, establishing a general result concerning the relatioship between fractional difference-differential equations and the probability mass function of a wider class of point processes.



Keywords:
Non-homogeneous Skellam process; Birth-death process; Migration process; Convolution-type derivative; Inverse subordinators

Introduction

In this paper we introduce two different types of vector point processes with interacting components, introducing a migration effect, one related to the Skellam-Poisson processes and one related to birth-death processes. Similar topics were deeply discussed by numerous remarkable authors who introduced stochastic versions of interacting growth models, see
[

,

Skellam processes first appeared in the paper

In Section 2, we introduce a stochastic vector representing two groups which modify their states with rate functions depending on time only, see equation (
2.1

Section 3 is devoted to the birth-death-migration vector process, where two birth-death population can exchange their units with a rate function depending on the number of elements in the departure group, as described in (
3.1

Birth-death processes have been studied since the early decades of the last century and presented in a general form by Kendall

After an overview on the birth-death-migration process, we focus on the case of death-migration processes and we prove that, for a fixed time

As a byproduct we obtain a pure migration process, where the two groups can only exchange units and therefore one component in sufficient to describe the vector (since the total number of units is constant in time).

The last section concerns time-changed versions of the stochastic vector models analyzed in Sections 2 and 3. In detail, we consider Bernstein subordinator processes assuming the role of the time, which furnishes the system with a long memory property. Particular cases of this kind of subordination can be traced back to
[
18
]
where the author derived a Poisson porcess time-changed with the inverse of a stable subordinator, connecting it to the Dzerbashyan-Caputo fractional operator. This theory widely developed and several papers applied it to different types of processes, among the others, we refer to

A simple application of this result permits us to derive a fractional difference-differential characterization of the time-changed versions of the vector process introduced in Sections 2 and 3 (as well as several results established in the cited papers).

Interacting Skellam-type processes

Let
(N_{1},N_{2})=\bigl\{\big(N_{1}(t),N_{2}(t)\big)\big\}_{t\geq 0}

\displaystyle P\{N_{1}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle=h+i,\,N_{2}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=k+j\,|\,N_{1}(t)=h,\,N_{2}(t)=k\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\lambda_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i,j=1,\\
\mu_{1}(t)\mu_{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i,j=-1,\\

\mu_{1}(t)\delta_{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=-1,j=0,\\
\delta_{1}(t)\mu_{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=0,j=-1,\\

\ \ \ \ \ +\lambda_{1}(t)\mu_{2}(t)+\eta_{21}(t)+\mu_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(t)+\eta_{12}(t)\\
\ \ \ \ \ +\lambda_{1}(t)\delta_{2}(t)+\delta_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(t)\\

Thus, the groups can evolve by acquiring or losing one unit or by transferring one element from one group to the other. By definition of the transition probability mass both groups can modify their composition in the same moment, but at most one event (i.e. increasing, loss or transfer) can happen during each infinitesimal interval. In particular, the rate function
\eta_{12}
concerns the movement of one element from

to
N_{2}
while

represents the migration in the opposite direction.

Let us denote by

and
t\geq 0
. From the infinitesimal transition probability mass in (

), with standard arguments, we derive the following difference-differential equation, for
t\geq 0,h,k\in\mathbb{Z}
(we omit the explicit dependence of the time for the rate functions),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p_{h,k}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}+\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\eta_{21}+\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}+\eta_{12}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\mu_{2})p_{h,k}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}p_{h-1,k-1}(t)+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}p_{h+1,k+1}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}p_{h-1,k}(t)+\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}p_{h,k-1}(t)+\mu_{1}\delta_{2}p_{h+1,k}(t)+\delta_{1}\mu_{2}p_{h,k+1}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +(\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\eta_{21})p_{h-1+k+1}(t)+(\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}+\eta_{12})p_{h+1,k-1}(t).

(2.2)

By using the fact that
\sum_{h,k=-\infty}^{\infty}u^{h}v^{k}p_{h+i,k+j}(t)=u^{-i}v^{-j}\sum_{h,k=-\infty}^{\infty}u^{h}v^{k}p_{h,k}(t)=u^{-i}v^{-j}G(t,u,v),
for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and suitable
u,v\in[-1,1]
, from the previous equation we obtian that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G(t,u,v)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}+\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\eta_{21}+\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}+\eta_{12}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\mu_{2})G(t,u,v)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\Biggl(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}uv+\frac{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{uv}+(\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\eta_{21})\frac{u}{v}+(\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}+\eta_{12})\frac{v}{u}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}u+\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}v

By keeping in mind the initial conditions, we obtain that the probability generating function reads

\displaystyle G(t,u,v)

\displaystyle=u^{n_{1}}v^{n_{2}}\text{exp}\Biggl(-\int_{0}^{t}\biggl[\lambda_{1}(s)\lambda_{2}(s)(1-uv)+\mu_{1}(s)\mu_{2}(s)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{uv}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lambda_{1}(s)\delta_{2}(s)(1-u)+\delta_{1}(s)\lambda_{2}(s)(1-v)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\mu_{1}(s)\delta_{2}(s)\Big(1-\frac{1}{u}\Big)+\delta_{1}(s)\mu_{2}(s)\Big(1-\frac{1}{v}\Big)\biggr]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Biggr)

\displaystyle=:u^{n_{1}}v^{n_{2}}\text{exp}\Bigl(-\int_{0}^{t}R(s,u,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\Bigr).

implies that the probability generating function of the increments is given by, with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}u^{N_{1}(t)-N_{1}(s)}v^{N_{2}(t)-N_{2}(s)}=\frac{G(t,u,v)}{G(s,u,v)}=\text{exp}\Bigl(-\int_{s}^{t}R(r,u,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\Bigr),

\displaystyle G_{1}(t,u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}u^{N_{1}(t)}=G(t,u,1)

\bar{\lambda}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}+\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\eta_{21}+\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}+\eta_{12}+\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\mu_{2}

\big(N_{1}(t),N_{2}(t)\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{N(t)}(X_{k},Y_{k}),\ \ \ t\geq 0,

where

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that the probabity generating function of the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}u^{\sum_{k=1}^{N(t)}X_{k}}v^{\sum_{k=1}^{N(t)}Y_{k}}=\text{exp}\Bigl(-\bar{\lambda}t\big(1-\mathbb{E}u^{X}v^{Y}\big)\Big),

2.1
Generalizations

2.1.1
Multiple interacting processes

We consider a three-dimensional version of the model defined in (
2.1
), that is a model with three different stochastic groups
(N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})
. According to the model above, we restrict ourselves under the hypothesis that in each group it can occur just one ”event” per instant of time, meaning that a group cannot, at the same instant of time, either take part into two migrations (for instance
N_{1}
cannot give to
N_{2}
and simultaneously receive from
N_{3}
) or add/lose an element and receive/give another element because of migration. This is a sufficient, but not necessary, condition to have groups which may increase or decrease of maximum one unit per instant of time. Under this hypothesis, proceeding as shown in the case above, the marginal components are dependent non-homogeneous (classical) Skellam processes which can be decomposed into a linear combination of some other (classical) Skellam processes representing the different possible outcomes occurring at each instant of time:

\displaystyle N_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{1}+S_{1,2}+S_{1,-2}+S_{1,3}+S_{1,-3}+S_{1,2,3}+S_{1,-2,3}+S_{1,2,-3}+S_{1,-2,-3}

\displaystyle N_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{2}+S_{1,2}-S_{1,-2}+S_{2,3}+S_{2,-3}+S_{1,2,3}-S_{1,-2,3}+S_{1,2,-3}-S_{1,-2,-3}

\displaystyle N_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{3}+S_{1,3}-S_{1,-3}+S_{2,3}-S_{2,-3}+S_{1,2,3}+S_{1,-2,3}-S_{1,2,-3}-S_{1,-2,-3},

(2.17)

with
S_{1}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}\delta_{3},\mu_{1}\delta_{2}\delta_{3}),

S_{1,2}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\delta_{3},\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\delta_{3}),

S_{1,-2}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}\delta_{3}+\eta_{21}\delta_{3},\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}\delta_{3}+\eta_{12}\delta_{3}),\dots,S_{2,-3}\sim NHSP(\delta_{1}\lambda_{2}\mu_{3}+\delta_{1}\eta_{32},\delta_{1}\mu_{2}\lambda_{3}+\delta_{1}\eta_{23}),

S_{1,2,3}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3},\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}),

S_{1,-2,3}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}\lambda_{3}+\eta_{21}\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{1}\eta_{23},\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}\mu_{3}+\eta_{12}\mu_{3}+\mu_{1}\eta_{32}),\dots,S_{1,-2,-3}\sim NHSP(\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}+\eta_{21}\mu_{3}+\eta_{31}\mu_{2},\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}+\eta_{12}\lambda_{3}+\eta_{13}\lambda_{2})
. The reader can recognize the role of each term analyzing the rate functions (also consider the subscripts of the components, for instance,
S_{1,-2}
concerns the events when
N_{1}
increases and
N_{2}
decreases).

The interested reader can observe that the decomposition (
2.17
) yields suitable versions of the remarks derived as consequences of the Theorem
2.1
. We note that the linear combinations
aN_{1}+bN_{2}+cN_{3}
, with a
a,b,c\in\{\pm 1\}
, are Skellam processes of order
3
.

In general, a model with
n
interacting groups, under the hypothesis that for each group can occur just one ”event” (as above), can be expressed in terms of the combination of
(3^{n}-1)/2
independent non-homogeneous (classical) Skellam processes, still having dependent marginal components of Skellam-type.

2.1.2
Bidimensional generalized Skellam-type interacting processes

We can extend the results presented above to a more general interacting vector process, where the two components can increase, decrease and exchange different amounts of units. Let the following sets describe the possible changes of the two groups,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,N_{1}\text{ modifies of $k$ units, not depending on $N_{2}$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,N_{2}\text{ modifies of $k$ units, not depending on $N_{1}$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,N_{1}\text{ forwards $k$ units to $N_{2}$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{2}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,N_{2}\text{ forwards $k$ units to $N_{1}$}\},

(2.18)

with
|\mathcal{I}_{1}|,|\mathcal{I}_{2}|,|\mathcal{N}_{1}|,|\mathcal{N}_{2}|<\infty
. Also assume the integrable rate functions
\lambda^{1}_{i},\lambda_{j}^{2}>0
for
i\in\mathcal{I}_{1},j\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
and equal to
0
otherwise,
\eta^{12}_{i},\eta_{j}^{21}>0
for
i\in\mathcal{N}_{1},j\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
and equal to
0
otherwise, and
\delta^{1},\delta^{2}>0
.

Then, we define a stochastic vector process
(N_{1},N_{2})
such that
\big(N_{1}(0),N_{2}(0)\big)=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
a.s., with independent increments and having the following infinitesimal behavior

\displaystyle P\{N_{1}

\displaystyle(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=h+i,\,N_{2}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=k+j\,|\,N_{1}(t)=h,\,N_{2}(t)=k\}

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\lambda_{i}^{1}(t)\lambda_{j}^{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i\not=-j,\ (i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\times\mathcal{I}_{2}\\
&\text{ or }i=-j,\ |i|\not\in\mathcal{N}_{1},|j|\not\in\mathcal{N}_{2},\\
\lambda_{i}^{1}(t)\lambda_{-i}^{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\eta_{|i|}^{12}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&j=-i\in\mathcal{N}_{1}\ (i<0),\\
\lambda_{-j}^{1}(t)\lambda_{j}^{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\eta_{|j|}^{21}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=-j\in\mathcal{N}_{2}\ (j<0),\\
\lambda_{i}^{1}(t)\delta^{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i\in\mathcal{I}_{1},j=0,\\
\delta^{1}(t)\lambda_{j}^{2}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=0,j\in\mathcal{I}_{2},\\
1-\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{1},j\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\lambda_{i}^{1}(t)\lambda_{j}^{2}(t)\\
\ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{1}(t)\delta^{2}(t)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\delta_{i}^{1}(t)\lambda_{j}^{2}(t)\\
\ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}\eta_{i}^{12}(t)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{2}}\eta_{j}^{21}(t)\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i,j=0,\\
o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\end{cases}

where in the first case each component changes but it is certain that there is no migration; the second case describes when the change can occur because of the migration of units from
N_{1}
to
N_{2}
; the third case describes when the change can occur because of the migration of units from
N_{2}
to
N_{1}
; the fourth and fifth cases describe when just one group changes.

Note that the restriction of having jumps of integer size can be relaxed by modifying the sets in (
2.18
) and the domain of the variables

in (
2.19
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(N_{1},N_{2})
be the stochastic vector process defined in (

\displaystyle N_{1}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}hN_{h}^{1,\mathcal{I}}+\sum_{h\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}h\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}N_{h,k}^{\mathcal{I}}+\sum_{h\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}(-h)N_{h}^{1,\mathcal{N}}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{2}}kN_{k}^{2,\mathcal{N}},

\displaystyle N_{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}kN_{k}^{2,\mathcal{I}}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}k\sum_{h\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}N_{h,k}^{\mathcal{I}}+\sum_{h\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}hN_{h}^{1,\mathcal{N}}+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{2}}(-k)N_{k}^{2,\mathcal{N}},

\displaystyle N_{h}^{1,\mathcal{I}}\sim PP(\lambda^{1}_{h}\delta^{2}),\ h\in\mathcal{I}_{1},\ \ \ N_{k}^{2,\mathcal{I}}\sim PP(\delta^{1}\lambda^{2}_{k}),\ k\in\mathcal{I}_{2},\ \ \ N_{h,k}^{\mathcal{I}}\sim PP(\lambda_{h}^{1}\lambda_{k}^{2}),\ (h,k)\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\times\mathcal{I}_{2},

Proof.

3
Interacting birth-death processes

Let
(N_{1},N_{2})=\bigl\{\big(N_{1}(t),N_{2}(t)\big)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
be a stochastic vector process describing the evolution of two interacting birth-death processes (which we also call
birth-death-migration vector process
). Let us assume the vector has independent increments,
\big(N_{1}(0),N_{2}(0)\big)=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
a.s. and the following infinitesimal transition probability mass function, with
h,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle P\{

\displaystyle N_{1}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=h+i,\,N_{2}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=k+j\,|\,N_{1}(t)=h,\,N_{2}(t)=k\}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\lambda_{1}(t)h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=1,j=0,\\
\lambda_{2}(t)k\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=0,j=1,\\
\mu_{1}(t)h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=-1,j=0,\\
\mu_{2}(t)k\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=0,j=-1,\\
\eta_{1}(t)h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=-1,j=1,\\
\eta_{2}(t)k\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=1,j=-1,\\
1-\big(\lambda_{1}(t)h+\lambda_{2}(t)k+\mu_{1}(t)h+\mu_{2}(t)k+\eta_{1}(t)h+\eta_{2}(t)k\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i,j=0,\\
o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are integrable functions.

From the definition (
3.1
), by means of classical arguments we derive the state equation. By denoting with
p_{h,k}(t)=P\{N_{1}(t)=h,N_{2}(t)=k\}
(i.e.
p_{h,k}(t)=0
if
h,k\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
),
t\geq 0
(we omit the explicit dependence of the time for the rate functions)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p_{h,k}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl((\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}+\eta_{1})h+(\lambda_{2}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{2})k\Big)p_{h,k}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lambda_{1}(h-1)p_{h-1,k}(t)+\lambda_{2}(k-1)p_{h,k-1}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\mu_{1}(h+1)p_{h+1,k}(t)+\mu_{2}(k+1)p_{h,k+1}(t)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\eta_{1}(h+1)p_{h+1,k-1}(t)+\eta_{2}(k+1)p_{h-1,k+1}(t),\ \ \ h,k\geq 0,

(3.2)

with initial conditions
p_{h,k}(0)=1
if
(h,k)=(n_{1},n_{2})
and
0
otherwise.

Note that for a non-linear process, formula (
3.1
) and equation (
3.2
) suitably modify. Furthermore, one may imagine several types of interaction between the two populations, also with migration rates depending on the number of individuals in both groups (this kind of hypotheses may raise several mathematical complications).

From the state equation (
3.2
) we derive the differential equation for the joint probability generating function
G(t,u,v)=\sum_{h=0}^{n_{1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{2}}u^{h}v^{k}p_{h,k}(t)
, with
u,v
in the neighborhood of
0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}=\Bigl(\lambda_{1}u^{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}+\eta_{1})u+\mu_{1}+\eta_{1}v\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial u}+\Bigl(\lambda_{2}v^{2}-(\lambda_{2}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{2})v+\mu_{2}+\eta_{2}u\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial v},

(3.3)

with initial condition
G(0,u,v)=u^{n_{1}}v^{n_{2}}
.

Hereafter we restrict ourselves to the homogeneous case, where the rate functions are constant.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(N_{1},N_{2})
be the (homogeneous) birth-death-migration vector process defined in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}N_{1}(t)=\frac{n_{1}e^{-Lt/2}}{2}\bigl(e^{Rt/2}+e^{-Rt/2}\bigr)+\frac{e^{-Lt/2}}{R}\Bigl(n_{2}\eta_{2}-\frac{n_{1}M}{2}\Bigr)\bigl(e^{Rt/2}-e^{-Rt/2}\bigr),

. Similarly for
N_{2}(t)
(it sufficies to switch the subscripts, also in

We derive (
3.3
) with respect to
u

3.3
) by using the joint cumulant generating function of
(N_{1}(t),N_{2}(t))
as shown in Section 4 of
[
17
]
.

In the next section we provide a detailed study of the death-migration vector process.

3.1
Death-migration process

We now assume the process defined in (
3.1
) with constant rate functions and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
. Therefore
(N_{1},N_{2})
is a linear death-migration process (or death process with interaction).

We begin by deriving the explicit expression of the probability generating function of the interacting death vector process. From the differential equation (
3.3
) we obtain, with
\|(u,v)\|\leq 1
and
t\geq 0
,

\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}=\Bigl(\mu_{1}(1-u)+\eta_{1}(v-u)\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial u}+\Bigl(\mu_{2}(1-v)+\eta_{2}(u-v)\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial v},

(3.13)

with initial condition
G(0,u,v)=u^{n_{1}}v^{n_{2}}
.

By using the change of variable
w=1-u,z=1-v
we obtain

\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}=-\Bigl(\mu_{1}w+\eta_{1}(w-z)\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial w}-\Bigl(\mu_{2}z+\eta_{2}(z-w)\Big)\frac{\partial G}{\partial z}.

(3.14)

We now consider a second change of variable
x=aw+bv,y=cw+dz
with
a,b,c,d
such that

\eta_{1}a^{2}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})ab-\eta_{2}b^{2}=\eta_{1}c^{2}+(\mu_{1}+\eta_{1}-\mu_{2}-\eta_{2})cd-\eta_{2}d^{2}=0.

Thus, we consider
b,d\not=0
and

\displaystyle a=b\,\frac{-(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})+\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}}{2\eta_{1}},

\displaystyle c=-d\,\frac{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})+\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}}{2\eta_{1}}.

(3.15)

This provides the following determinant for the matrix of the coefficients,

ad-bc=bd\frac{\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}}{\eta_{1}}\not=0,

(3.16)

and reduces equation (
3.14
) into

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{ad-bc}\Big[ad\mu_{1}+a(c+d)\eta_{1}-bc\mu_{2}-b(c+d)\eta_{2}\Big]\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{y}{ad-bc}\Big[-bc\mu_{1}-c(a+b)\eta_{1}+ad\mu_{2}+d(a+b)\eta_{2}\Big]\frac{\partial G}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-x\Big[\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}}{2}\Big]\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -y\Big[\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}}{2}\Big]\frac{\partial G}{\partial y},

(3.17)

where in the last step we replaced
a
and
c
with the quantities in (
3.1
), used (
3.16
) and performed some calculation.

We denote by
L=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
and

M=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},\ \ \ R=\sqrt{M^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}=\sqrt{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})^{2}+4\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}.

(3.18)

Now, it is easy to check that the function
f(xe^{-(L-R)t/2},ye^{-(L+R)t/2})
satisfies equation (
3.17
). Thus, retuning to the original variables
u,v
we obtain that

G(t,u,v)=f\Big(e^{-\frac{L-R}{2}t}\big[a(1-u)+b(1-v)\big],e^{-\frac{L+R}{2}t}\big[c(1-u)+d(1-v)\big]\Big).

(3.19)

Finally, by considering the initial condition
G(0,u,v)=u^{n_{1}}v^{n_{2}}
we obtain the explicit form for
f
, that is

f(w,z)=\Big(1-\frac{dw-bz}{ad-bc}\Big)^{n_{1}}\Big(1-\frac{az-cw}{ad-bc}\Big)^{n_{2}}.

(3.20)

Hence, by keeping in mind the coefficients in (
3.1
) and the notation (
3.18
), the probability generating function follows by putting together (
3.19
) and (
3.20
),

\displaystyle G(t,u,v)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(1-\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{2R}\bigg[(1-u)\Bigl((R-M)e^{\frac{R}{2}t}+(R+M)e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr)+(1-v)2\eta_{1}\Bigl(e^{\frac{R}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr)\bigg]\Biggr)^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\Biggl(1-\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{2R}\bigg[(1-u)2\eta_{2}\Bigl(e^{\frac{R}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr)+(1-v)\Bigl((R+M)e^{\frac{R}{2}t}+(R-M)e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr)\bigg]\Biggr)^{n_{2}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(1-e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}\bigg[(1-u)\Bigl(\cosh(Rt/2)-\frac{M}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)\Bigr)+(1-v)\frac{2\eta_{1}}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)\bigg]\Biggr)^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\Biggl(1-e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}\bigg[(1-u)\frac{2\eta_{2}}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)+(1-v)\Bigl(\cosh(Rt/2)+\frac{M}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)\bigg)\bigg]\Biggr)^{n_{2}}.

(3.22)

The above result permits us to derive the following interesting decomposition for the death-migration vector process
(N_{1},N_{2})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(N_{1},N_{2})
be the death-migration vector process described above. Then,

N_{1}(t)=X_{1}(t)+Y_{1}(t)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ N_{2}(t)=X_{2}(t)+Y_{2}(t),

(3.23)

where
X(t)=\bigl(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t)\big)\sim Multinomial\big(n_{1},A_{1}(t),B_{1}(t)\big)
and
Y(t)=\bigl(Y_{1}(t),Y_{2}(t)\big)\sim Multinomial\big(n_{2},A_{2}(t),B_{2}(t)\big)
are independent random variables, with
M,R
defined in (
3.18
) and

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{2R}\Bigl((R-M)e^{\frac{R}{2}t}+(R+M)e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr),

\displaystyle B_{1}(t)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{R}\eta_{1}\Bigl(e^{\frac{R}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr),

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{R}\eta_{2}\Bigl(e^{\frac{R}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr),

\displaystyle B_{2}(t)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}}{2R}\Bigl((R+M)e^{\frac{R}{2}t}+(R-M)e^{-\frac{R}{2}t}\Bigr).

(3.24)

Proof.

First, one can check that the coefficients in (
3.24
) are in
(0,1)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, in light of notation (
3.24
), the probability generating function (
3.21
) reads

G(t,u,v)=\Bigl(1-A_{1}(t)(1-u)-B_{1}(t)(1-v)\Bigr)^{n_{1}}\Bigl(1-A_{2}(t)(1-u)-B_{2}(t)(1-v)\Bigr)^{n_{2}}

(3.25)

which yields the claimed result, reminding that the probability generating function of a multinomial random variable
X=(X_{1},X_{2})\sim Multinomial(n,p,q)
is

\mathbb{E}u^{X_{1}}v^{X_{2}}=(1-p(1-u)-q(1-v)\big)^{n},

with
|u|,|v|\leq 1
,
p,q\in(0,1)
and
p+q<1
.
∎

Formula (
3.25
) permits us to derive the moments of the vector
(N_{1},N_{2})
. The interestted reader can check that the first moments are coherent with those appearing in Proposition
3.1
. For instance the covariance of the two components is, for
t\geq 0
,

\text{Cov}\bigl(N_{1}(t),N_{2}(t)\bigr)=-n_{1}A_{1}(t)B_{1}(t)-n_{2}A_{2}(t)B_{2}(t)

which is negative because of the migration behavior (algebraically,
A_{1},B_{1},A_{2},B_{2}
are non-negative functions of
t
, see (
3.24
)).

Theorem
3.1
permits us to study combinations of the components
N_{1},N_{2}
. It is interesting to notice that, for fixed
t\geq 0
, the sum of the components of
(N_{1},N_{2})
is the sum of independent binomial random variables,
N_{1}(t)+N_{2}(t)=U(t)+V(t)
, with
U(t)\sim Binomial\big(n_{1},A_{1}(t)+B_{1}(t)\big)
and
V(t)\sim Binomial\big(n_{2},A_{2}(t)+B_{2}(t)\big)
with the parameters given in (
3.24
).

Remark 3.2

(Extinction)
.

From the probability generating function we easily derive the extinction probability of the vector process. In particular, notation (
3.24
), formulas (
3.25
) and (
3.22
) provide, for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle P\{N_{1}(t)=0,N_{2}(t)=0\}

\displaystyle=\big(1-A_{1}(t)-B_{1}(t)\big)^{n_{1}}\big(1-A_{2}(t)-B_{2}(t)\big)^{n_{2}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(1-e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}\Bigl[\cosh(Rt/2)+\frac{\mu_{2}+\eta_{2}-\mu_{1}+\eta_{1}}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)\Bigr]\Biggr)^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\Biggl(1-e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{2}t}\Bigl[\cosh(Rt/2)+\frac{\mu_{1}+\eta_{1}-\mu_{2}+\eta_{2}}{R}\sinh(Rt/2)\Bigr]\Biggr)^{n_{2}}.

Note that as
t\longrightarrow 0
the extinction probability goes to
1
.

We now consider
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu>0
. In this case,
A_{1}(t)+B_{1}(t)=A_{2}(t)+B_{2}(t)=e^{-\mu t}\ \forall\ t
and probability (
3.26
) does not depend neither on
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
nor on the distribution of the initial
n_{1}+n_{2}
individuals among the two groups, reading
P\{N_{1}(t)=0,N_{2}(t)=0\}=(1-e^{-\mu t})^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
. This yields the expected extinction time of the vector
(N_{1},N_{2})
,
T_{0}=\inf\{t>0\,:\,N_{1}(t)=0,N_{2}(t)=0\}
, for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}T_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}tP\{T_{0}\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}P\{N_{1}(t)=0,N_{2}(t)=0\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t(n_{1}+n_{2})\mu e^{-\mu t}\bigl(1-e^{-\mu t}\big)^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\frac{n_{1}+n_{2}}{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\binom{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(1+k)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+n_{2}}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\frac{1}{\mu k},

(3.27)

where in the last step we used the relationship

\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+n_{2}}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\frac{1}{k}=-H_{n_{1}+n_{2}},

(3.28)

with
H_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
being the
(n_{1}+n_{2})
-th harmonic number (see Appendix
A
for the proof of (
3.28
)).

Note that (also in the case of
\mu_{1}\not=\mu_{2}
) by keeping in mind that the sum
N_{1}+N_{2}
is a non-increasing process, the following generating function holds true (we refer to Appendix A of
[
7
]
for a similar proof),

\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}u^{k}P\{T_{k}>t\}=\frac{1-\mathbb{E}u^{N_{1}(t)+N_{2}(t)}}{1-u}=\frac{1-G(t,u,u)}{1-u},\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ |u|\leq 1,

(3.29)

where
T_{k}=\inf\{t>0\,:\,N_{1}(t)+N_{2}(t)=k\}
is the first time that the total population is equal to
0\leq k\leq n_{1}+n_{2}
and
G
is the probability generating function (
3.21
). Note that if
k=0
,
T_{0}
is the same one appearing in (
3.27
) and that formula (
3.29
), with
u=1
, coincides with the complement to one of probability (
3.26
).

Finally, we observe that, in the case of death rates equal to
\mu>0
, with
t\geq 0
,

P\{T_{0}\leq t\}=(1-e^{-\mu t})^{n_{1}+n_{2}}=P\{\max\{T_{1},T_{2}\}\leq t\}

where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the extinction times of two independent linear death processes of rates
\mu>0
whose sum of initial units is
n_{1}+n_{2}
.
\diamond

Remark 3.3
.

In the case of symmetric death rates
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu>0
, the probability generating function (
3.21
) turns into

\displaystyle G(t,u,v)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(1-\frac{e^{-\mu t}}{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}\Big[(1-u)\bigl(\eta_{2}+\eta_{1}e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}\big)+(1-v)\eta_{1}\bigl(1-e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}\big)\Big]\Bigg)^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\Bigg(1-\frac{e^{-\mu t}}{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}\Big[(1-u)\eta_{2}\bigl(1-e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}\big)+(1-v)\bigl(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}\big)\Big]\Bigg)^{n_{1}}.

(3.30)

The interested reader can also evaluate the case when
\mu_{1}+\eta_{1}=\mu_{2}+\eta_{2}
, meaning that the rates of the groups are balanced. Indeed, formula (
3.21
) simplifies a lot since
M=0,R=2\sqrt{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}
.

Finally, if we assume a complete symmetry in the rates
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2},\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}
, we can solve the differential equation (
3.13
) with time-depending rates, i.e. the non-homogeneous version of the birth-migration process. Its probability generating function reads

\displaystyle G(t,u,v)

\displaystyle=\bigg[1-\frac{e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}}{2}\Big((1-u)\big(1+e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}st}\big)+(1-v)\big(1-e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big)\Big)\bigg]^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\bigg[1-\frac{e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}}{2}\Big((1-u)\big(1-e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big)+(1-v)\big(1+e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\big)\Big)\bigg]^{n_{2}},

(3.31)

where
\mu,\eta
are integrable rate functions. We point out that Theorem
3.1
suitably holds in this non-homogeneous case.

\diamond

Remark 3.4

(Pure migration process)
.

Let
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=0
, then no units of the couple
(N_{1},N_{2})
can die. Thus, the death-migration process described above simplifies and it can be written as
(N_{1},N_{2}=n_{1}+n_{2}-N_{1})
where a unit can leave
N_{1}
with a rate proportional to
N_{1}
and a unit can arrive in
N_{1}
with a rate proportional to
n_{1}+n_{2}-N_{1}
. The coefficients in (
3.18
) reduce to
M=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
and
R=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
and those in (
3.24
) turn into

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{1}(t)=\frac{\eta_{2}+\eta_{1}e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}}{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}},\ \ \ \bar{B}_{2}(t)=1-\bar{A}_{1}(t),

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{2}(t)=\eta_{2}\frac{1-e^{-(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})t}}{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}},\ \ \ \ \bar{B}_{2}(t)=1-\bar{A}_{2}(t).

(3.32)

The migration vector process can be described by one component, say
N_{1}
, since the other one is
N_{2}=n_{1}+n_{2}-N_{1}
. We use the results concerning the death-migration vector process to derive explicit results on the migration process
N_{1}
. Indeed, in light of the probability generating function
G
in (
3.21
) and the new coefficients in (
3.4
), we can write (performing some simple calculation), with
t\geq 0
and
|u|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}u^{N_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle=G(t,u,1)=\big(1-(1-u)\bar{A}_{1}(t)\big)^{n_{1}}\big(1-(1-u)\bar{A}_{2}(t)\big)^{n_{2}}

(3.33)

\displaystyle=u^{n_{1}+n_{2}}G(t,1,u^{-1})=\mathbb{E}u^{n_{1}+n_{2}-N_{2}(t)},

also conferming that
N_{1}(t)=n_{1}+n_{2}-N_{2}(t)\ \forall\ t
.

Theorem
3.1
permits us to derive the probability law of the migration process
N_{1}
, that is, for fixed
t\geq 0
,

N_{1}(t)=X_{t}+Y_{t},\ \ \ X_{t}\sim Binomial\big(n_{1},\bar{A}_{1}(t)\big),\ Y_{t}\sim Binomial\big(n_{2},\bar{A}_{2}(t)\big),

(3.34)

with
X_{t},Y_{t}
independent random variables.

Since
N_{1}
is an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on the finite state space
\{0,\dots,n_{1}+n_{2}\}
, it admits a stationary distribution. By noticing that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bar{A}_{1}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bar{A}_{2}(t)=\eta_{2}/(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})
, result (
3.34
) yields

N_{1}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\begin{subarray}{c}\xrightarrow{\hskip 48.36958pt}\\
t\longrightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}}N\sim Binomial\Big(n_{1}+n_{2},\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}\Big).

(3.35)

Finally, we derive the difference-differential equation governing by the probability law of the migration process
N_{1}
and the partial differential equation governing by the probability generating function. The infinitesimal behavior of
N_{1}
is, for
t\geq 0
and
k\in\{0,\dots,n_{1}+n_{2}\}
,

\displaystyle P\{N_{1}(t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)=k+i\,|N_{1}(t)=k\}=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\eta_{1}k\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=-1,\\
\eta_{2}(n_{1}+n_{2}-k)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=1,\\
1-\big(\eta_{1}k+\eta_{2}(n_{1}+n_{2}-k)\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&i=0,\\
o(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t),&otherwise.\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.36)

By keeping in mind that
N_{1}
has independent increments, we can derive the governing equation of its probability mass function. Let
p_{k}(t)=P\{N_{1}(t)=k\}
(i.e.
p_{k}(t)=0
if
k\not\in\{0,\dots,n_{1}+n_{2}\}
) and
t\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p_{k}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(\eta_{1}k+\eta_{2}(n_{1}+n_{2}-k)\Big)p_{k}(t)

(3.37)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\eta_{1}(k+1)p_{k+1}(t)+\eta_{2}(n_{1}+n_{2}-k)p_{k-1}(t),\ \ \ 0\leq k\leq n_{1}+n_{2},

with initial condition
p_{k}(0)=1
if
k=n_{1}
and
p_{k}(0)=0
otherwise.
Note that for a non-linear process, formula (
3.36
) and equation (
3.37
) suitably modify.

From (
3.37
) some calculation yield the differential equation governing the probability generating function
\bar{G}(t,u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}u^{k}p_{k}(t)
, with
|u|\leq 1
,

\frac{\partial\bar{G}}{\partial t}=-\eta_{2}(n_{1}+n_{2})(u-1)\bar{G}-\big(\eta_{2}u^{2}+u(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})-\eta_{1}\big)\frac{\partial\bar{G}}{\partial u},

(3.38)

with initial condition
\bar{G}(0,u)=u^{n_{1}}
. Note that the solution to (
3.38
) is given in formula (
3.33
),
\bar{G}(t,u)=\mathbb{E}u^{N_{1}(t)}=G(t,u,1)
.
\diamond

3.2
Difference of independent birth-death processes

Inspired by the definition of the Skellam process as the difference of two independent Poisson processes, here we derive some explicit results for the difference of two independent birth-death processes,
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. We recall that for a (homogeneous) birth-death process
N
, the probability mass at time
t\geq 0
reads

P\{N(t)=k\}=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\displaystyle e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t}(\lambda-\mu)^{2}\frac{\big(\lambda(1-e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t})\big)^{k-1}}{\big(\lambda-\mu e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t}\big)^{k+1}},&k\in\mathbb{N},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mu(1-e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t})}{\lambda-\mu e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t}},&k=0.\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.39)

Now, the distribution of the difference of two independent birth-death processes
N_{1},N_{2}
with rates
\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}
and
\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}
respectively, reads, for
k\in\mathbb{N},\ t\geq 0,

\displaystyle P\{N_{1}(t)-N_{2}(t)=k\}=P\{N_{1}(t)=k\}P\{N_{2}(t)=0\}+\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}P\{N_{1}(t)=h+k\}P\{N_{2}(t)=h\}

\displaystyle=P\{N_{1}(t)=k\}P\{N_{2}(t)=0\}+e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t}(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})^{2}(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\frac{\big(\lambda_{1}(1-e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t})\big)^{k-1}}{\big(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t}\big)^{k+1}}\frac{\big(\lambda_{2}(1-e^{-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t})\big)^{-1}}{\big(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}e^{-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t}\big)}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\Bigg(\frac{\lambda_{1}\big(1-e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t}\big)}{\big(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t}\big)}\frac{\lambda_{2}\big(1-e^{-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t}\big)}{\big(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}e^{-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t}\big)}\Bigg)^{h}

\displaystyle=P\{N_{1}(t)=k\}P\{N_{2}(t)=0\}+e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})t-(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})t}(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1})^{2}(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=P\{N_{1}(t)=k\}\Biggl(P\{N_{2}(t)=0\}+\frac{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\mu_{1}}P\{N_{1}(t)=0\}P\{N_{2}(t)=1\}}{1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\mu_{1}}P\{N_{1}(t)=0\}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\mu_{2}}P\{N_{2}(t)=0\}}\Biggr),

(3.40)

where we used (

) in the last step. The probability for

4
Time-changed interacting point processes

In this section we present a result which provides a characterization of the interacting point processes discussed above, time-changed with the inverse of a subordinator with Lévy symbol a Bernstein function
f
.

We recall that
f:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is a Bernstein function if
f\in C^{\infty},\ (-1)^{n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{n}f/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{n}\leq 0\ \forall\ n\geq 1
and it can be expressed as

f(x)=a+bx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-e^{-xw}\Bigr)\bar{\nu}_{f}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w),\ \ \ x\geq 0,

(4.1)

where
a,b\geq 0
and
\bar{\nu}_{f}
is a Lévy measure, i.e. such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}(s\wedge 1)\bar{\nu}_{f}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s)<\infty
.
Bernstein functions are related to non-decreasing Lévy processes, also known as subordinators. Indeed, for each Bernstein function
f
there exists a subordinator
H_{f}
such that
f
is the Lévy symbol of
H_{f}
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}e^{-\mu H_{f}(t)}=e^{-tf(\mu)},\ \mu,t\geq 0
.

The inverse of the subordinator
H_{f}
is the stochastic process
L_{f}
defined as

L_{f}(t)=\inf\{x\geq 0\,:\,H_{f}(x)\geq t\},\ \ \ t\geq 0,

(4.2)

which describes the first passage time through level
x
of the subordinator
H_{f}
, therefore, also
L_{f}
is non-decreasing (and non-negative).

Following the line of
[
5
]
, we consider the convolution-type derivative with respect to the Bernstein function
f
, appeared in
[
27
]
(also known as generalized Caputo-Dzerbashyan derivative, see
[
15
]
),
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}
, which is defined on the space of absolutely continuous functions as follows

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)=b\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}u(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t-s)\nu_{f}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(4.3)

where
\nu_{f}
is the tail of the Lévy measure
\bar{\nu}_{f}
.

It is well-known that for
f(x)=x^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the Lévy measure is
\bar{\nu}_{f}(s)=\alpha x^{-\alpha-1}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
, its tail is
\nu_{f}(x)=x^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
and the operator in (
4.3
) reduces to the Caputo-Dzerbashyan derivative. We refer to
[
5
,
19
,
27
]
for further details on the convolution-type derivative
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}
.

Hereafter we assume the following condition.

Condition I.
The Lévy measure
\bar{\nu}_{f}
associated to the Bernstein function
f
is such that
\bar{\nu}(0,\infty)=\infty
and its tail
\nu_{f}(s)=a+\bar{\nu}_{f}(s,\infty)
is absolutely continuous.

It was shown in
[
27
]
that under Condition I, the inverse
L_{f}
admits a probability density
l_{f}(t,x)=P\{L_{f}(t)\in\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\}/\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
with
l_{f}(0,x)=\delta(x)
, where
\delta
is the Dirac delta function centered in the origin, and such that its
x
-Laplace transform is an eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{D}_{f}
. Indeed, the function

\tilde{l}(t,y)=\mathcal{L}_{x}\{l_{f}\}(t,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-yx}l(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\mathbb{E}e^{-yL_{f}(t)},\ \ \ t,y\geq 0,

(4.4)

where
\mathcal{L}_{x}
denotes the
x
-Laplace transform operator, satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}\tilde{l}(t,y)=-y\tilde{l}(t,y),\ t,y\geq 0,\ \ \ \tilde{l}_{f}(0,y)=1,\ \forall\ y,

also see
[
15
]
for further details.

In order to prove the next statement we also recall that in
[
27
]
the author introduced another convolution-type derivative generalizing the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)=b\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}u(t)+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{t}u(t-s)\nu_{f}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(4.5)

which relates to the convolution-typer derivative (
4.3
) by means of (see Proposition 2.7 of
[
27
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)=\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}u(t)-\nu_{f}(t)u(0).

(4.6)

Furthermore, it is useful to recall (see Theorem 4.1 of
[
27
]
) that the probability density
l_{f}
satisfies the problem, with
x>0
if
b=0
or
0<x<t/b
if
b>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}l_{f}(t,x)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}l_{f}(t,x),\ \ \ t\geq 0,

(4.7)

subject to
l_{f}(t,0)=\nu_{f}(t),\ l_{f}(t,t/b)=0
and
l_{f}(0,x)=\delta(x)
.

The next statement provides a characterization of the difference-differential equation solved by the probability mass function of a point process time-changed with the inverse of a Bernstein subordinator,
L_{f}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N
be a
d
-dimensional point process with probability mass function
p_{n}(t)=P\{N(t)=n\},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},
and
p_{n}^{f}
the probability mass function of its time-changed version
N\circ L_{f}
. If Condition I holds and
p_{n}
satisfies the difference-differential equation

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}p_{n}(t)=O_{d}\,p_{n}(t),\ t\geq 0,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\ \ \ p_{n}(0)=q(n),\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(4.8)

with
q
being a probability mass function over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
O_{d}
being a difference operator such that
\forall\ n
,

\displaystyle O_{d}\,p_{n}^{f}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}O_{d}\,p_{n}(x)l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\ \forall\ t,

(4.9)

then,
p_{n}^{f}
satisfies the equation

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}q_{n}(t)=O_{d}\,q_{n}(t),\ t\geq 0,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\ \ \ q_{n}(0)=q(n),\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(4.10)

Proof.

First, we observe that

p_{n}^{f}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}l(0,x)p_{n}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(x)p_{n}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=p_{n}(0)=q(n),

meaning that
p_{n}^{f}
satisfies the initial condition in (
4.10
). Now we show that it also satisfies the equation in (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{f}p_{n}^{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}p_{n}^{f}(t)-\nu_{f}(t)p_{n}^{f}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x)\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\nu_{f}(t)p_{n}^{f}(0)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\nu_{f}(t)p_{n}^{f}(0)

\displaystyle=p_{n}(0)l_{f}(t,0)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}p_{n}(x)l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\nu_{f}(t)p_{n}^{f}(0)

\displaystyle=\nu_{f}(t)\big(p_{n}(0)-p_{n}^{f}(0)\big)+\int_{0}^{\infty}O_{d}\,p_{n}(x)l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=O_{d}p_{n}^{f}(x).

Note that in the second equality we exchanged
\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}p_{n}^{f}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x)\mathbb{D}_{t}^{f}l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
since
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}p_{n}^{f}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}l_{f}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. In the first step we used (
4.6
), in the third one (
4.7
), in the fourth one (
4.8
) abd in the last one the initial conditions and (
4.9
).
∎

Appendix A
Proof of equation (
3.28
)

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(n\pm k)(-1)^{k}}{k(n-k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}-\frac{1}{n}\big(1+(-1)^{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}-\frac{1}{n}

and by iterating the reasoning we obtain the desired relationship,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}=-H_{n},\ \ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Regularizing effects for an elliptic system of singular equations
Gabriele Giannone

Abstract
. A system of two singular semi–linear elliptic equations, patterned after the Schrödinger-Maxwell system, is considered. If the reaction term of the first equation contains a datum f ∈ L m f\in L^{m} , existence of positive solutions with finite energy is established for suitable ranges of m m . In particular, the results from the theory of elliptic single equations are improved. At the same time, thanks to an approach based on approximation schemes and a priori estimates on the approximated sequences of solutions, it is shown that the integrability assumptions on the datum produce higher integrability of the solutions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\R^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.
In this paper, we are concerned with the doubly singular semi–linear system

(1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Du)+v^{1-\theta}u^{r-1}=\frac{f(x)}{u^{\gamma}}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Dv)=\frac{u^{r}}{v^{\theta}}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
v>0,\,u\geq 0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u>0&\mbox{in}\ \{f>0\}\\
u=v=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Here,
f
is a non-negative and non-trivial function belonging to a suitable Lebesgue space, and
A(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\R^{d\times d})
is a symmetric and uniformly elliptic matrix, in the sense that

(2)

\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\beta|\xi|^{2}

for a
.
e
.

x\in\Omega
and every
\xi\in\R^{d}
, with
0<\alpha\leq\beta
. Furthermore, we make the following assumption on the parameters:

(3)

r\geq 2,\quad\gamma\in(0,1),\quad{\theta}\in[0,1).

We examine how the structure of the system and the integrability of the datum
f
give rise to regularizing effects on the existence of solutions
(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
having higher integrability than that prescribed by the theory of elliptic (single) equations.

1.1.
State of the art

2.1.
An overview of the proofs

Our approach is based on an approximation procedure, that shows the existence of a sequence
(u_{n},v_{n})
that solves a suitable elliptic system, and on some a priori estimates on the latter, which guarantee that
(u_{n},v_{n})
converges to a solution to the system having the desired integrability. More precisely, set
f_{n}=\min\{f,n\}
, we show that the problem

(10)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Du_{n})+v_{n}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}=\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Dv_{n})=\frac{u_{n}^{r}}{\big(v_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u_{n},v_{n}>0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u_{n}=v_{n}=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{cases}

has a unique weak, bounded solution. To do this, first we examine the equations “separately”, namely, we prove the existence of unique
u_{z},v_{\phi}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
u_{z},v_{\phi}>0
a
.

.
in
\Omega
, that satisfy, in the weak sense,

\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Du_{z})+z^{1-\theta}u_{z}^{r-1}=\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{z}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Dv_{\phi})=\frac{(\min\{k,|\phi|\})^{r}}{\big(v_{\phi}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}},

being
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
0\leq z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and

. By Theorem

, as a by-product of this construction

and

satisfy a priori

–estimates independent from

and

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ni\phi\mapsto u_{v_{\phi}}
has a fixed point
(u_{n},v_{n})
, solution to (
10
) in the weak sense.

In order to establish Theorems

,
2.5
,
2.7
,
2.8
, we first prove suitable a priori estimates on

, which depends on the standing regularity assumption on

\text{any}\ p\in[1,\infty)&\mbox{if}\ m=\frac{d}{2}\\
m^{\star\star}(1+\gamma)&\mbox{if}\ \big(\frac{2^{\star}}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}\leq m<\frac{d}{2}\\
{r}&\mbox{if}\ \big(\frac{r}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}\leq m<\big(\frac{2^{\star}}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}\\
{r+1}&\mbox{if}\ \big(\frac{r+1}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}\leq m<\big(\frac{2^{\star}}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}\ \text{and}\ \theta=0.\\
\end{cases}

Then, we deduce a priori energy-estimates on

and
(v_{n})

converges weakly in

and strongly in

to a weak/finite-energy solution to the system. Let us emphasize that, when dealing with finite-energy solutions, we see that

a
.
e

Concerning the last part of Theorem

a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
.

Finally, to prove Theorem

, we note that
(r+\gamma)^{\prime}\leq m<\frac{d}{2}

to a finite-energy solution
(u,v)

Notations and preliminaries

We define the
positive
and

of a real-valued function

\displaystyle u_{+}(x)\vcentcolon=\max\{u(x),0\}=\begin{cases}u(x)&\mbox{if}\ u(x)>0\\

for all

. Let

. We will make use of the following real functions

\displaystyle T_{k}(s)\vcentcolon=\max\{-k,\min\{s,k\}\}=\begin{cases}-k&\mbox{if}\ s\leq-k,\\

k&\mbox{if}\ s\geq k,\end{cases}

\displaystyle G_{k}(s)\vcentcolon=(|s|-k)_{+}\mathrm{sign}(s)=\begin{cases}s-k&\mbox{if}\ s\geq k,\\
0&\mbox{if}\ |s|\leq k,\\
s+k&\mbox{if}\ s\leq-k.\\
\end{cases}

,

,

, and

Du_{+}=Du\chi_{\{u\geq 0\}},\quad DT_{k}(u)=Du\chi_{\{|u|\leq k\}},\quad DG_{k}(u)=Du\chi_{\{|u|\geq k\}}\quad\text{a\text{.}e\text{.}\ in }\Omega.

For a proof see, for instance, Lemma

in

]

in

]

3.1.
Boundedness of solutions to uniformly elliptic PDEs

Given
F\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
M(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\R^{d\times d})
such that (
2
) holds true. As it is well known, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to

(11)

\begin{cases}-\Div(M(x)Du)=F&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

A function
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak sub-solution to (
11
) if

\int\limits_{\Omega}MDu\cdot D\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\langle F,\varphi\rangle\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\,\varphi\geq 0,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
are the brackets for the duality pairing between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Since every weak-subsolution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to (
11
) satisfies
\alpha\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq\|F\|_{H^{-1}}
, if one assumes that
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\geq(2^{\star})^{\prime}
, it holds

\alpha\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq|\Omega|^{1-\frac{(2^{\star})^{\prime}}{p}}\|F\|_{L^{p}}.

In what follows, we will frequently make use of the following boundedness result, proved by G. Stampacchia in the seminal work
[
Sta65
]
. For its proof, we refer the reader to Théorème
4.2
in the aforementioned paper.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, with
p>\frac{d}{2}
, and let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to (
11
). Then
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and there exists a positive constant
C
depending only upon
d
,
|\Omega|
,
\alpha
, and
p
, such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{p}}.

Actually, the argument that proves Theorem
3.2
yields the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, with
p>\frac{d}{2}
, and let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\text{there exists }k_{0}\geq 0\ \text{such that}\ \int\limits_{\Omega}\alpha|DG_{k}(u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}FG_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\ \ \forall k\geq k_{0}.

Then
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and there exists a positive constant
C
depending only upon
d
,
|\Omega|
,
\alpha
, and
p
, such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq k_{0}+C\|F\|_{L^{p}}.

3.2.
Convergence of the gradients

A Borel measure
\mu
on
\Omega
is called Radon measure if it is locally finite and inner regular, i.e., if
\mu(K)<+\infty
for all
K\subset\Omega
compact and

\displaystyle\mu(U)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subset U\ \text{compact}\}

for all
U\subset\Omega
open. The space of Radon measures on
\Omega
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
. As it is well known, this space coincides with the topological dual of
C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
, and we say that a sequence
(\mu_{n})
is bounded in
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
if, for every
K\subset\Omega
compact,

|\langle\mu_{n},\phi\rangle|\leq c\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\quad\forall\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega),\,\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subset K,

for some positive constant
c=c(K)
.

When passing to the limit in our approximation scheme, we will take advantage of the following result:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
M(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\R^{d\times d})
be a uniformly elliptic matrix and let
(f_{n})\subset\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
. Suppose there exists a distributional solution
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
to

-\Div(M(x)Du_{n})=f_{n}.

If
(f_{n})
is bounded in
\mathcal{R}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, then

Du_{n}\to Du\quad\text{strongly in}\ L^{q}(\Omega;\R^{d})\ \text{for any}\ 1\leq q<2.

In particular, up to a subsequence,
Du_{n}\to Du
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
.

For the proof of (a more general version of) this result, we refer the reader to Theorem
2.1
in
[
BM92
]
.

Note to the reader
.

Throughout the paper, the letter
C
will denote various positive constants — whose value may change from line to line — depending on the data of the problem (
d
,
|\Omega|
,
\alpha
,
m
,
\gamma
, etc.), but never on the sequence indexes that we shall use in the proofs.

4.
Approximation scheme

To begin, we provide an approximation procedure for the first equation. Fix
0\leq z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. For any
n\in\N
, define
f_{n}(x)\vcentcolon=T_{n}(f(x))
and consider the problem:

(12)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Div(A(x)Du_{n})+z^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}=\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u_{n}>0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u_{n}=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We show that (
12
) has a unique weak solution. Our proof exploits fixed point arguments, and it avoids any variational tool.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
n\in\N
, there exists a unique
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, weak solution to (
12
), such that
u_{n}>0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
and
\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{1+\gamma}
for all
n\in\N
, for some positive constant
C=C(d,|\Omega|,\alpha)
.

Proof.

Denote by
\kappa=\kappa(d,|\Omega|,\alpha)>0
the constant in Theorem
3.2
. We divide the proof into steps.

Step
1
: construction of
u_{n}
.
Fix
n\in\N
and let
k_{n}=\kappa^{r-2}n^{(r-2)(1+\gamma)}
. Define
F(\psi)\vcentcolon=f_{n}\big(|\psi|+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{-\gamma}
for all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
F(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, by the Lax-Milgram theorem there exists a unique weak solution
u_{\psi}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to

(13)

-\Div(A(x)Du_{\psi})+z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(|\psi|^{r-2})u_{\psi}=F(\psi).

Testing (
13
) against
(u_{\psi})_{-}
we get

\displaystyle 0\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}F(\psi)(u_{\psi})_{-}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{\psi}\cdot D(u_{\psi})_{-}+z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(|\psi|^{r-2})u_{\psi}(u_{\psi})_{-}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha\int\limits_{\Omega}|D(u_{\psi})_{-}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

It follows that
(u_{\psi})_{-}=0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
, i.e.,
u_{\psi}\geq 0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Then, letting
k\geq 0
and choosing
G_{k}(u_{\psi})
as a test function in (
13
) produce

\int\limits_{\Omega}\alpha|D(G_{k}(u_{\psi}))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(|\psi|+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}G_{k}(u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x,

hence, since
f_{n}\big(|\psi|+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{-\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|f_{n}\big(|\psi|+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{-\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq n^{1+\gamma}
, Theorem
3.3
ensures that
u_{\psi}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

(14)

\|u_{\psi}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|u_{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\kappa n^{1+\gamma}.

Let
S\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be defined as
S(\psi)=u_{\psi}
. By (
14
), the ball
B=\{\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|\psi\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq\kappa n^{1+\gamma}\}
is invariant for
S
. We want to show that
S
has a fixed point.

Sub-step
1.1
:
S
is weakly–strongly continuous.
Let
\psi_{j}\rightharpoonup\psi
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and, without loss of generality, assume that
\psi_{j}\to\psi
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq p<2^{\star}
. Taking advantage of (
14
), there exists
\xi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,
(u_{\psi_{j}})
converges to
\xi
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq p<2^{\star}
, and a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Choosing
u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi}
in the weak formulations of the problems solved by
u_{\psi_{j}}
and
u_{\psi}
and subtracting the equations yield

(15)

\int\limits_{\Omega}AD(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\cdot D(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}(F(\psi_{j})-F(\psi))(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
-\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{1-\theta}\big(T_{k_{n}}(|\psi_{j}|^{r-2})u_{\psi_{j}}-T_{k_{n}}(|\psi|^{r-2})u_{\psi}\big)(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem one deduces that
(F(\psi_{j})-F(\psi))(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\to 0
and
z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(|\psi_{j}|^{r-2})u_{\psi_{j}}\to z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(|\psi|^{r-2})\xi
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, (
15
) entails

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}AD(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\cdot D(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(|\psi|^{r-2})(\xi-u_{\psi})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0,

whence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}AD(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\cdot D(u_{\psi_{j}}-u_{\psi})\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

By the uniform ellipticity of
A(\cdot)
,
it follows that
u_{\psi_{j}}\to u_{\psi}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and this is enough to conclude that
S
is weakly–strongly continuous.

Since
S
is compact and continuous from
B
into
B
, by the Schauder fixed point theorem there exists
0\leq u_{n}\in B\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}=S(u_{n})
, namely,

\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot D\varphi+z^{1-\theta}T_{k_{n}}(u_{n}^{r-2})u_{n}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, to prove that
u_{n}
is a weak solution to (
12
), it suffices to note that, by (
14
),
u_{n}^{r-2}\leq\kappa^{r-2}n^{(r-2)(1+\gamma)}=k_{n}
, hence
T_{k_{n}}(u_{n}^{r-2})u_{n}=u_{n}^{r-1}
.

Step
2
:
u_{n}
is positive.
We now show that
u_{n}>0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Note that

-\Div(A(x)Du_{n})+\|z\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}\geq\frac{f_{n}}{\big(\kappa n^{1+\gamma}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}\geq 0.

Then, taking advantage of the boundedness of
u_{n}
, and recalling that
f_{n}\not\equiv 0
, by the weak Harnack inequality (see Theorem
1.2
in
[
Tru67
]
) we conclude that
u_{n}>0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
.

Step
3
: uniqueness.
Finally, we observe that
u_{n}
is unique. Indeed, if
U_{n}
is another positive solution, one has

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot D(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}+z^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}ADU_{n}\cdot D(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}+z^{1-\theta}U_{n}^{r-1}(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(U_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x,

whence, taking the difference,

\alpha\int\limits_{\Omega}|D(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{1-\theta}(U_{n}^{r-1}-u_{n}^{r-1})(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}\bigg(\frac{1}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}-\frac{1}{\big(U_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}\bigg)(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since the maps
s\mapsto(s+1/n)^{-\gamma}
,
s\mapsto s^{r-1}
are increasing in
[0,+\infty)
, the previous inequality entails

0\leq\alpha\int\limits_{\Omega}|D(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0,

hence
(u_{n}-U_{n})_{+}=0
, i.e.,
u_{n}\leq U_{n}
. Likewise,
U_{n}\leq u_{n}
. Therefore,
U_{n}=u_{n}
, and the solution is unique.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

(Approximation)
For every
n\in\N
, there exists
(u_{n},v_{n})\in\big(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\big)^{2}
such that
u_{n},v_{n}>0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
and

(16)

\displaystyle$$\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot D\varphi+v_{n}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x$$

(17)

\displaystyle$$\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}ADv_{n}\cdot D\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{u^{r}_{n}}{\big(v_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x$$

for all
\varphi,\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Given
k\geq 0
and
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, by the same argument that proves Lemma
4.1
, and by the strong maximum principle for the operator
-\Div(A(x)D\cdot)
, there exists
0\leq v_{\phi}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
that solves

-\Div(A(x)Dv_{\phi})=\frac{T_{k}(|\phi|)^{r}}{\big(v_{\phi}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}

in the weak sense; this solution turns out to be unique, due to the strict monotonicity of
s\mapsto s^{{-\theta}}
in
[0,+\infty)
. Moreover, by Theorem
3.2
,
v_{\phi}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
\|v_{\phi}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|v_{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ck^{r}n^{\theta}
. By Lemma
4.1
, there is a unique weak solution
0<u_{\phi}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to

-\Div(A(x)Du_{\phi})+v_{\phi}^{1-\theta}u_{\phi}^{r-1}=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\big(u_{\phi}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}

that satisfies
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{1+\gamma}
. Now, we make the following:

Claim 4.3
.

The map
S\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
defined by
S(\phi)=u_{\phi}
is compact and continuous.

Once the claim is established, exploiting Schauder’s fixed point theorem and choosing

, one proves the existence of
u_{n},v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
that fulfil (
16
) and (
17
) for any
\varphi,\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and such that
u_{n}>0
and
v_{n}\geq 0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. The assertion on the strict positivity of
v_{n}
follows from the weak Harnack inequality for the linear operator
-\Div(A(x)D\cdot)
, noting that

\int\limits_{\Omega}ADv_{n}\cdot D\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0\quad\forall\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\,\psi\geq 0,

and that
u_{n}^{r}\not\equiv 0
(see Theorem
8.18
in
[
DS01
]
). To conclude, we have to prove Claim
4.3
.

Proof of Claim
4.3
.

We show that, if
\phi_{j}\rightharpoonup\phi
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then, up to subsequences,
S(\phi_{j})\to S(\phi)
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. With this aim, take
(\phi_{j})
that converges to
\phi
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and, without loss of generality, a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Thanks to the uniform bounds on
(v_{\phi_{j}})
and
(u_{\phi_{j}})
, there exist
z,\xi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that, up to non-relabelled subsequences,
(v_{\phi_{j}})
and
(u_{\phi_{j}})
converge, respectively, to
z
and
\xi
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all

, and a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Clearly,
z,\xi\geq 0
a
.
e
.
in
\Omega
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{T_{k}(|\phi_{j}|)^{r}}{\big(v_{\phi_{j}}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}\leq n^{\theta}k^{r},

\displaystyle\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{\phi_{j}}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}+v_{\phi_{j}}^{1-\theta}u_{\phi_{j}}^{r-1}\leq n^{1+\gamma}+Ck^{r(1-\theta)}n^{\theta(1-\theta)}n^{(r-1)(1+\gamma)},

7.
Data outside the dual space

Here, we assume
m<\big(\frac{2^{\star}}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}
. Let
(u_{n},v_{n})\in\big(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\big)^{2}
be the sequence given by Lemma
4.2
.

7.1.
A priori estimates

Repeating the reasoning that lead to (
36
) we obtain

(52)

\alpha\int\limits_{\Omega}|Du_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\alpha\int\limits_{\Omega}|Dv_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
m\geq\big(\frac{r}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}
and
r>2^{\star}
. Then
(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Observe that

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover,

(53)

\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}v_{n}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x

and

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|+\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|\Omega|+2^{\theta}\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}\frac{u_{n}^{r+1}}{\big(v_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

as a simple computation shows. Since, by (
33
),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}\frac{u_{n}^{r+1}}{\big(v_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}ADT_{1}(v_{n})\cdot Du_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x

and, taking advantage of (
52
),

\int\limits_{\Omega}ADT_{1}(v_{n})\cdot Du_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|Dv_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\beta}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|Du_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x,

we infer

(54)

\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|\Omega|+C\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Putting together (
53
) and (
54
) yields

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|\Omega|+C\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x,

hence, recalling that
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that, by assumption,
m^{\prime}(1-\gamma)\leq r
,

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r}}^{r}\leq|\Omega|+C\|f\|_{L^{m}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r}}^{1-\gamma}.

It follows that

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r}}^{r}\big(1-C\|f\|_{L^{m}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r}}^{1-\gamma-r}\big)\leq|\Omega|,

which, given that
1-\gamma-r<0
, entails the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
m\geq\big(\frac{r+1}{1-\gamma}\big)^{\prime}
,
r>2^{\star}-1
, and
{\theta}=0
. Then
(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{r+1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Under the assumption
{\theta}=0
, the equations (
16
), (
17
) become

-\Div(A(x)Du_{n})+v_{n}u_{n}^{r-1}=\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}\,,\qquad-\Div(A(x)Dv_{n})=u_{n}^{r}.

Choosing
u_{n}
as test function in the second one produces

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\Omega}ADv_{n}\cdot Du_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|Dv_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\beta}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|Du_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

whence, exploiting (
52
),

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{n}u_{n}^{1-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
m^{\prime}(1-\gamma)\leq r+1
, one has

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r+1}}^{1-\gamma}.

This implies
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r+1}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}}^{\frac{1}{r+\gamma}}
, as desired.
∎

8.
A higher integrability result

Assume

(r+\gamma)^{\prime}\leq m<\frac{d}{2}
and
r\geq\frac{d}{d-2}-\gamma
and let
(u_{n},v_{n})
be as in Lemma
4.2
. Since

(r+\gamma)^{\prime}\geq\frac{r}{r-1+\gamma}=\Big(\frac{r}{1-\gamma}\Big)^{\prime},

the proof of Theorem
2.7
shows that
(u_{n},v_{n})
converges almost everywhere to a finite-energy solution
(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, Theorem
2.10
is a straightforward consequence of the next

Lemma 8.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
, depending on
|\Omega|
,
\gamma
,
r
,
\alpha
, and
\beta
, such that

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}}\Big(1+\|f\|_{L^{m}}^{\frac{1}{r-1+\gamma}}\Big)\quad\forall n\in\N.

Proof.

We have

(55)

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Let us treat the two integrals in the above formula separately. Concerning the fist one, by (
33
) we have

(56)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{\theta}\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}\frac{u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}}{\big(v_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{\theta}\int\limits_{\Omega}ADT_{1}(v_{n})\cdot Du_{n}(\gamma+1)u_{n}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Pick
\varepsilon>0
and choose
T_{1}(v_{n})(u_{n}+\varepsilon)^{\gamma}
as test function in (
16
). Disregarding the positive terms

\gamma\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot Du_{n}T_{1}(v_{n})(u_{n}+\varepsilon)^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad\int\limits_{\Omega}v_{n}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r-1}T_{1}(v_{n})(u_{n}+\varepsilon)^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x,

we obtain

\gamma\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot DT_{1}(v_{n})(u_{n}+\varepsilon)^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{f}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}(u_{n}+\varepsilon)^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
produces

\gamma\int\limits_{\Omega}ADu_{n}\cdot DT_{1}(v_{n})u_{n}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}f\,\mathrm{d}x,

which, together with (
56
), imply

(57)

\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}f\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}}.

Now we estimate the second integral in (
55
). Testing (
16
) against
u_{n}^{\gamma+2}
, one has

(58)

\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}v_{n}^{1-\theta}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}fu_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Set
E\vcentcolon=\big\{f\leq\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{2}$}}u_{n}^{r-1+\gamma}\big\}
, we obtain

\int\limits_{\Omega}fu_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{E}fu_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus E}fu_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}+Cf^{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thanks to our (key) assumption
m\geq(r+\gamma)^{\prime}
,

\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}f^{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}},

hence

\int\limits_{\Omega}fu_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\|f\|_{L^{m}}^{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}},

and by (
58
)

(59)

\int\limits_{\{v_{n}\geq 1\}}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\|f\|_{L^{m}}^{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}}.

Finally, putting (
55
), (
57
) and (
59
) together we obtain

\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|f\|_{L^{m}}+\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}^{r+1+\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\|f\|_{L^{m}}^{(r+\gamma)^{\prime}},

and the conclusion follows.
∎

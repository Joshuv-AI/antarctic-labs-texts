Prevalence, determinants and ethnic disparities of rheumatoid arthritis among older adults in Canada: protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Lakew Mussie, G Mutwiri, Amanuel Yoseph
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionRheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disease characterised by progressive joint inflammation, pain and functional decline, disproportionately affecting older adults. In Canada, evidence regarding the prevalence, determinants and ethnic disparities of RA among older populations remains fragmented. This protocol outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis designed to estimate the pooled prevalence of RA, identify its key determinants and examine ethnic disparities among Canadian adults aged 60 years and older.Methods and analysisA comprehensive literature search will be undertaken across MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, Web of Science, Scopus, and relevant grey literature sources to identify studies published between January 2000 and October 2025, with the search scheduled to be conducted between 15 and 30 November 2025. Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory disorder that primarily affects synovial joints, resulting in pain, progressive disability and diminished quality of life.1 2 Globally, RA affects approximately 1% of the population, with prevalence increasing among older adults due to age-related immune dysregulation and physiological changes.1 2 In Canada, RA represents a significant public health burden, particularly among adults aged 60 years and older, who are more susceptible to comorbidities, functional decline and adverse clinical outcomes.3,5

Although multiple Canadian studies have investigated RA prevalence and associated risk factors, the findings are fragmented across provinces, healthcare settings and study populations.6,9 Regional differences in healthcare access, diagnostic practices and population demographics further complicate generalisability.7 8 Additionally, disparities in RA burden related to social determinants of health, such as place of residence, race/ethnicity, occupation, gender/sex, socioeconomic status and healthcare access have not been systematically examined.10 11 The PROGRESS-Plus framework provides a structured approach for analysing these inequities, incorporating Place of residence, Race/ethnicity, Occupation, Gender, Religion, Education, Socioeconomic status, Social capital, plus personal characteristics (eg, age, disability).10

Emerging evidence suggests that Indigenous peoples and racialised communities in Canada may experience disproportionately higher RA prevalence and encounter systemic barriers to diagnosis and treatment.9 11 12 For instance, a cohort study indicates that First Nations populations in Manitoba demonstrate both elevated incidence and delayed access to rheumatology care compared with non-Indigenous populations.9 Nevertheless, no systematic review has comprehensively evaluated the prevalence, determinants and ethnic disparities of RA among Canadian older adults using an equity-informed lens. Such a synthesis is essential for identifying high-risk populations, guiding targeted interventions and informing culturally sensitive clinical practice.

The increasing ethnic diversity and ageing of the Canadian population emphasise the urgency of understanding RA epidemiology across demographic groups.34 12,14 By collating evidence from observational studies, population-based surveys and administrative datasets, a systematic review and meta-analysis can provide robust estimates of RA prevalence among older adults and elucidate how social determinants influence disease burden.6 7 14 15 Furthermore, integrating equity-focused analyses through the PROGRESS-Plus framework enables the identification of structural and sociodemographic factors contributing to health disparities, thereby informing public health policy and resource allocation.10 16 17 Despite this, comprehensive national estimates focusing on older adults are limited, highlighting a critical need for evidence synthesis.

Although nationwide population-based surveys would yield the most representative prevalence estimates, Canada currently lacks a comprehensive national arthritis surveillance system capturing RA data among older adults.3 4 18 19 In this context, synthesising evidence from regional, community-based and administrative studies through systematic review and meta-analysis is a necessary and methodologically sound strategy. This approach enables the integration of heterogeneous data sources, allows identification of knowledge gaps and facilitates cautious interpretation of findings while acknowledging inherent limitations.13 20

This protocol outlines a methodologically rigorous approach for a systematic review and meta-analysis aimed at synthesising evidence on RA prevalence, determinants and ethnic disparities in Canadian adults aged ≥60 years. The review will adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2020 guidelines13 to ensure transparent reporting and methodological quality. Findings from this review are expected to support evidence-based decision-making, inform culturally sensitive healthcare delivery, and ultimately contribute to reducing health inequities among older adults living with RA in Canada. By addressing key gaps in knowledge, this work has the potential to guide future research, clinical practice and policy initiatives that prioritise health equity in musculoskeletal care.

Although a national epidemiological survey would provide the most representative data, the absence of a comprehensive national arthritis surveillance system in Canada necessitates synthesising evidence from existing regional, community-based and administrative studies through a systematic review and meta-analysis. Thus, this systematic review and meta-analysis aim to estimate the pooled prevalence of RA among older adults (aged 60 years and above) in Canada, identify its key determinants, and examine ethnic and sociodemographic disparities. The review will synthesise data from population-based and community-level studies to provide a comprehensive understanding of disease burden and inequities across ethnic, sex, gender and socioeconomic groups. Collectively, these objectives aim to generate a comprehensive and equity-oriented synthesis of the epidemiology of RA among older adults in Canada. By consolidating fragmented evidence in the absence of a nationwide arthritis surveillance system.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This protocol outlines the methodology for a systematic review and meta-analysis aimed at synthesising existing evidence on the prevalence, determinants, and ethnic disparities of RA among older adults in Canada. The protocol has been prepared and reported in accordance with the PRISMA-P 2020 guidelines, ensuring transparency, reproducibility and methodological rigour.

Eligibility criteria

We will include observational studies such as cross-sectional, cohort and population-based administrative studies that report on the prevalence, determinants or ethnic disparities of RA among Canadian adults aged sixty years and older. Studies must provide age-stratified data or sufficient information to allow extraction of estimates specific to older adults. RA cases must be clearly defined using recognised diagnostic criteria, following a predefined hierarchy of validity, starting with the ACR/EULAR 2010 criteria, followed by the ACR 1987 criteria, validated ICD-based algorithms, clinician diagnosis and, finally, self-reported RA. When multiple case definitions are reported within a study, the estimate derived from the highest-ranked definition will be used in the primary meta-analysis, whereas other estimates will be reserved for sensitivity and secondary analyses. In instances where studies report mixed-age or mixed-definition results, we will attempt to obtain age-stratified or definition-stratified data from the authors; if unobtainable, such studies will contribute only to descriptive synthesis or, where feasible, sensitivity analyses using predefined rules. Paediatric RA cases (<16 years) will be included for comparison purposes only, while the primary analyses focus on adults aged ≥60 years. No language restrictions will be applied to maximise the comprehensiveness of the review. Case reports, editorials, conference abstracts, reviews and studies conducted outside Canada will not be included.

Information sources and search strategy

A comprehensive search will be conducted across MEDLINE (Ovid), PubMed, EMBASE, CINAHL, Web of Science, Scopus, and relevant grey literature sources, including government health reports, Statistics Canada publications, the Canadian Institute for Health Information, and Indigenous health repositories. The search will include literature published between January 2000 and October 2025 to capture the contemporary epidemiology of RA in Canada. The search process is scheduled for 15 to 30 November 2025.

The search strategy will be developed in consultation with an experienced health sciences librarian and will combine controlled vocabulary such as MeSH and Emtree terms with free-text keywords related to RA, prevalence, older adults, ethnicity and Canada. In addition to database searches, reference lists of included studies and relevant systematic reviews will be manually screened to identify additional eligible publications. A full draft strategy for MEDLINE (Ovid) has been presented in online supplemental file 1 and adapted appropriately for the other databases.

Duplicate removal and study selection

All retrieved records will be imported into Covidence, where automatic deduplication will initially remove identical citations based on titles, authors, years and DOIs. Two independent reviewers will then manually verify duplicates to ensure that no unique studies are inadvertently removed, with any discrepancies resolved through discussion or adjudication by a third reviewer. The study selection process will be fully documented using a PRISMA flow diagram, and two reviewers will independently screen titles, abstracts and full-text articles against the eligibility criteria.

Data extraction

Data will be extracted using a standardised, pilot-tested form, capturing key study characteristics including authorship, publication year, province or territory, study design, data source, population demographics, sample size, RA case definition and diagnostic method, prevalence or incidence estimates specifying point, period or lifetime measures, as well as determinants and covariates such as sex, gender, ethnicity and socioeconomic indicators. Ethnic and Indigenous classifications will be standardised, and stratified results will be collected wherever available. Corresponding authors will be contacted to clarify or provide missing information when necessary.

Prevalence, incidence and standardisation

RA frequency measures will be extracted and reported as defined in each study, distinguishing a priori between point, period and lifetime prevalence, as well as incidence estimates. Prevalence will be expressed as percentages, and incidence will be reported as new cases per 100 000 persons per year. Where feasible, pooled prevalence and incidence estimates will be age-standardised and sex-standardised using stratum-specific data, while unadjusted estimates will be clearly flagged and included in sensitivity analyses. Mixed adult-paediatric samples will be handled by extracting data specific to individuals aged sixty years and older, and studies for which age-specific data cannot be obtained will contribute only to qualitative synthesis.

Population denominators and sampling considerations

To ensure representativeness, priority will be given to estimates derived from community, regional or national population-based studies. Studies based on convenience samples, hospital cohorts or specialist clinics will be identified and analysed separately to assess their potential influence on pooled estimates. Sampling frames, response rates and population coverage will be documented to facilitate critical appraisal of external validity.

Risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers will independently assess the methodological quality of included studies using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklists for prevalence studies, avoiding tools such as NOS or Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology, which are unsuitable for prevalence-focused research. Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. The influence of study quality on pooled estimates will be examined through sensitivity analyses, subgroup analyses and meta-regression.

Data synthesis

Data will first be summarised narratively and subsequently quantitatively where appropriate. Pooled prevalence estimates will be calculated using random-effects models, either logit-transformed or binomial generalised linear mixed models with restricted maximum likelihood estimation. Heterogeneity will be quantified using I², τ² and H² statistics. Multiple estimates from the same study will be managed using robust variance estimation or prespecified selection rules. Subgroup analyses will be conducted by ethnicity and Indigenous identity, sex and gender, geographic region, data source and case definition. Further subgroup and meta-regression analyses will evaluate heterogeneity by study design, calendar period, proportion of female participants and study risk of bias. Sensitivity analyses will exclude studies with lower-validity RA definitions or those at high risk of bias. Age-standardised and sex-standardised prevalence will be calculated using stratum-specific estimates where available; unadjusted estimates will be reported with cautious interpretation. The PROGRESS-Plus framework will guide exploration of equity-related disparities, and meta-regression may be employed to examine potential sources of heterogeneity across studies.

Equity, ethnicity and sex/gender considerations

The PROGRESS-Plus framework will guide identification and interpretation of equity-related factors, including place of residence, race and ethnicity, occupation, gender, religion, education, socioeconomic status and social capital. Sex and gender will be extracted and reported as distinct constructs, and sociodemographic gradients will be explored where data permit. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses will examine disparities across these dimensions, enabling a nuanced and equity-focused understanding of RA prevalence and determinants.

Certainty of evidence

The overall certainty of evidence will be assessed using the Grading of recommendations, assessment, development and evaluation (GRADE) approach, adapted for prevalence and frequency measures, considering risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design or conduct of this study. However, they will be involved in the reporting and dissemination of the study findings.

Study timeline

The systematic review will commence in November 2025 and is expected to be completed by April 2026.

Discussion

RA remains a major contributor to morbidity and disability among older adults, yet its epidemiological patterns in Canada are insufficiently characterised, particularly regarding ethnic disparities and broader social determinants of health.21 22 This systematic review and meta-analysis protocol seeks to address these critical evidence gaps by synthesising data on RA prevalence, determinants and ethnic disparities among older Canadians through an equity-oriented lens.21 23

Canada’s ageing population and increasing ethnocultural diversity underscore the need to understand how RA disproportionately affects different communities.12,1422 23 While prior studies have estimated RA prevalence and identified certain risk factors, inconsistencies in study design, diagnostic criteria and population representation have limited comparability and hindered the development of equitable public health strategies.6 7 9 24 Indigenous peoples, immigrant communities and visible minority groups remain underrepresented in national rheumatology datasets.9 11 18 By integrating the PROGRESS-Plus framework, this review explicitly incorporates social and structural determinants such as socioeconomic status, education and access to care into the analysis of RA disparities.10 11 25

This systematic review will consolidate fragmented evidence to generate nationally relevant estimates of RA prevalence and determinants among older adults.14 18 26 Beyond quantifying disease burden, it will elucidate patterns of inequity related to ethnicity, sex and geography.16 17 22 27 Such insights are vital for tailoring public health programmes, improving access to rheumatology services and informing culturally responsive care models.9 18 The use of standardised appraisal tools (JBI) and advanced statistical approaches (binomial GLMM or logit-transformed random-effects models) will enhance the rigour, interpretability and reproducibility of findings.20

Nevertheless, anticipated challenges include heterogeneity in RA case definitions, variability in ethnic classifications and differential study quality.7 9 20 24 To mitigate these issues, dual independent appraisal, sensitivity analyses and robust variance estimation will be employed.20 Despite these precautions, residual heterogeneity and reporting bias may persist, which will be transparently acknowledged in the final synthesis.21 28

We acknowledge that variation in the availability of rheumatology services and access to serological testing may influence observed RA prevalence across different settings. Regions with greater specialist coverage and diagnostic capacity are more likely to identify cases, potentially inflating prevalence estimates, whereas areas with limited access may underreport disease burden. To account for this, studies will be categorised by healthcare setting and diagnostic accessibility, and subgroup analyses will be conducted to examine how differences in service availability may contribute to heterogeneity in prevalence estimates. This approach allows for more nuanced interpretation of RA epidemiology and helps to contextualise findings within the broader Canadian healthcare landscape.

In summary, this protocol outlines a comprehensive, methodologically robust and equity-focused approach to understanding RA epidemiology among older adults in Canada. The results are expected to inform evidence-based policy, guide resource allocation and advance equity in musculoskeletal health outcomes across diverse ageing populations. By addressing gaps in both epidemiological knowledge and social determinants of health, this review aims to support culturally sensitive clinical practice, prioritise high-risk populations and inform future research on health equity in RA.

Ethics and dissemination

This study does not require ethics approval, as it involves secondary analysis of published and publicly available data. The review will begin in November 2025 and is anticipated to conclude by April 2026. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and policy briefs targeting clinicians, researchers and health policymakers. By making results accessible to both academic and non-academic audiences, the review aims to support equitable and informed decision-making in rheumatology care across Canada.

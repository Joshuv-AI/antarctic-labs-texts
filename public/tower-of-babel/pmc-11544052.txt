Deoxynivalenol mycotoxin dietary exposure on broiler performance and small intestine health: A comprehensive meta-analysis
Authors: Chala Adugna, Kai Wang, Jian Du, Chunmei Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The effect of DON mycotoxins on broiler production performance and the small intestine is a critical factor in the health and well-being of broilers. Several studies have been conducted on this topic and have reported varying results and conclusions. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct systematic reviews and meta-analyses to thoroughly examine and draw unique conclusions. In this meta-analysis, we conducted a systematic review of multiple studies on the effects of DON mycotoxins in broilers. The analysis comprised 26 articles from reputable journals, and 14 parameters were identified based on the predetermined criteria. The forest plot results showed that DON treatment significantly reduced the ADFI and ADWG (SMD-1.50, 95 %CI [-1.68, -1.18]; I2= 51 %; p < 0.00001) and affected FCR (SMD 0.95, 95 %CI [ 0.62, 1.28]; I2= 77; p < 0.00001). In addition, it affects the small intestine structure duodenum (SMD -3.46, 95 %CI [-3.88, -3.05]; I2= 48 %; p < 0.00001), Jejunum (SMD -5.35, 95 %CI [-5.86, -4.83]; I2= 62 %; p < 0.00001), Ileum (SMD -2.6, 95 % CI [-3.12, -2.08]; I2= 82 %; p < 0.00001). Furthermore, DON exposure affects immunoglobulin (SMD -1.92, 95 % CI [ -2.39, -1.46]; I2 = 54 %; p < 0.00001) and antioxidant activities (SMD -2.1, 95 % CI [ -2.45, -1.75]; I2= 47 %; p < 0.00001). The overall effect of DON treatment was statistically significant compared with that of the control group. Furthermore, funnel plot analysis for publication bias did not reveal any significant asymmetry in most included studies. The results of this meta-analysis indicate that DON mycotoxins have a significant impact on both production performance and small intestine health and require strategic intervention.

Introduction

Mycotoxins are secondary metabolites produced by certain species of fungi and contaminate agricultural products such as cereal grains and forage during processing or storage, causing significant effects (Ochieng et al., 2021). In animal feed, different types of mycotoxins are found, from which DON is widely distributed in cereal grains (Skládanka et al., 2011). DON is produced by Fusarium fungi that survive mostly under warm and humid conditions (Asam et al. 2017). The toxicological effects of DON mycotoxins on broiler production include reduced weight gain, reduced feed intake, increased feed conversion ratio, immunosuppression, and the occurrence of infectious diseases (Awad et al. 2013). The effect of DON mycotoxins can worsen due to increased stressors in commercial broiler production, where the stocking number is very high and an automatic feeding system is used, which may increase the impact of mycotoxins on broilers (Weaver et al., 2022). The effect of DON exposure on broilers can vary based on the duration of exposure, the age of the broilers, and the dose of DON used. DON also has a detrimental effect on broiler health through disruption of intestinal integrity, alteration of gut microbiota, affecting the cellular structure of villi, impairing nutrient absorption in the small intestine, and directly affecting broiler performance (Wan et al., 2022).

The exposure of broilers to DON mycotoxins has detrimental effects on the health and function of the small intestine. In broilers, the small intestine is an important part of the digestive system in which nutrient digestion and absorption occur (Ravindran and Abdollahi, 2021). After ingestion, DON passes through the intestinal tract where it is absorbed by the small intestine. After absorption, the villi site of nutrient absorption in the small intestine shrinks and blunts, reducing the area of nutrient absorption and impairing feed efficiency and broiler performance. Recent studies have increasingly focused on the effects of DON on broiler small intestine health because DON is an important organ for nutrient absorption and overall broiler performance. The structure and functionality of the small intestine are key factors for maximizing feed efficiency and growth performance (Lisnahan and Nahak, 2020). Broiler DON exposure has various effects on the morphological structure of the small intestine, such as villous atrophy, increased crypt depth, reduced villus height, and disruption of intestinal epithelial integrity.

The broiler immune system, which is critical for organ performance and welfare, can be affected by broiler gut health. In broilers, body immunoglobulin is an essential component of the immune system with an unmeasurable role in antibody-mediated immunity (Wlaźlak et al., 2023). IgM is used as an early infectious response, IgA plays a significant role in mucosal immunity, and IgG is involved in the adaptive immune response used to provide long-lasting defense against pathogenic microorganisms. DON exposure affects the immune system of broilers by decreasing immunoglobulin levels and altering their functions. Decreasing the levels of IgA, IgG, and IgM disrupts broiler immune responses and the mucosa increases the vulnerability of broilers to gastrointestinal infections (Riahi et al., 2021). In addition to DON, the immune system also induces inflammation and causes serious health problems in broilers.

Antioxidants play an important role in maintaining health and preventing oxidative stress in broiler chickens. Broiler exposure to DON mycotoxins disturbs pro-oxidants and antioxidants in the small intestine, leading to oxidative stress, which can reduce the antioxidant defense mechanisms (Mavrommatis et al., 2021). DON caused a major reduction in these antioxidant functions in the intestinal tissues of broilers. DON exposure increases oxidative stress in broiler intestines (De Souza et al., 2020). DON causes oxidative stress when the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) increases and exceeds the capacity of the antioxidant to neutralize (Jena et al., 2023). This difference causes lipid peroxidation, in which free radicals in the organ attack lipids found in the cell membrane, causing damage to and disturbance in epithelial cell integrity.

The global demand for poultry products is increasing, and greater attention is required to improve broiler production performance and health to meet the rising demand for consumer needs (Arsi and Donoghue, 2017). However, the presence of DON is a great challenge for poultry producers in general and broiler producers. DON contamination increases in cereal grains, which are feed ingredients used to formulate rations for broilers (Kim et al., 2021). Contamination of broilers fed DON at low levels can cause detrimental effects on broilers, resulting in low growth rates and increased feed conversion ratios, affecting profitability and challenging the sustainability of production.

The impact of DON mycotoxins on broilers has been extensively studied in various experiments to assess its effects on the production performance and small intestine health of broilers. However, there is a lack of consistent data owing to different factors, such as variations in the research methodologies used to assess DON exposure and outcomes. Various studies have used different methods to evaluate DON concentrations in feed and other samples, resulting in inconsistent measurements of broiler performance and other health aspects. These inconsistencies make it difficult to draw unique conclusions from studies to take appropriate action against toxins; therefore, it is important to conduct a comprehensive meta-analysis to draw a unique and meaningful conclusion that encompasses large studies. Meta-analysis provides a good opportunity to summarize the results of individual studies and prepare effective results.

In this meta-analysis, we investigated the effects of dietary DON mycotoxin exposure on broiler performance parameters, small intestine health, immunoglobulin (Ig), and antioxidant properties. To achieve this goal, a thorough review and analysis of the existing literature was conducted to compile data from various sources and determine patterns and potential sources of variation. This information was used to quantify the extent to which DON affected broiler health and performance, which is crucial for developing effective interventions to mitigate the negative effects of DON contamination on broiler production.

Methods

Studies search strategies

The data used in this study were collected from different databases, including PubMed, Web Science, and Google Scholar. The literature search was conducted using keywords in the database “DON Mycotoxins, broilers performance, feed intake, daily gain, feed conversion, small intestine morphological structures, broilers health.” Research screening was based on PRISMA standard guidelines (Moher et al., 2009).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Study selection for this meta-analysis was as follows: i) the research must be conducted on broiler chickens of either sex (male or female); ii) the study must be published in Scopus journal; iii) research must contain control and at least one experimental group; iv) research must report at least one data on average daily feed intake, average daily weight gain, feed conversion ratio, and data on the morphological structure of the small intestine; v) duplicate research published in more than one journal was excluded; and vi) research without a randomization process was excluded from the analysis. After identifying the studies, the required data on sample size, mean, standard deviation, duration of experimental periods, breed of broilers, and dose of DON mycotoxins used were extracted following proper data collection procedures. The effect sizes were calculated for each study. It is used to measure the effect of mycotoxins on the performance of broilers and the morphological structures of the small intestine.

Data extraction steps

Data from each study were collected using the appropriate procedure for collecting data for meta-analysis. Different data were extracted from each study, including sample size, mean, standard deviation, year of publication, breed, and concentration of DON mycotoxin used for feed contamination for both unchallenged and challenged groups. If the number of standard deviations was not reported in the studies, we used the formula to calculate Sd= SE*√n. In cases where the ratio of villus height (VH) to crypt depth (CD) has not been reported in previous studies, we used VH: CD= VH/CD to calculate VH: CD. We used the online data extraction procedure WebPlotDigitizer - Copyright 2010-2024 Ankit Rohatgi (automeris.io) to obtain data from the graphical report. Data extracted from published were converted into the same units before analysis.

Statistical analysis

The statistical software RevMan (, version 5.4, was used for the analysis. The Nordic Cochrane Center and Cochrane Collaboration developed the tool in 2014 (http://tech.cochrane.org/revman). We used statistical software to effectively analyze the data and draw meaningful conclusions. Various statistical techniques were used to analyze the results, such as calculating the study mean, standard deviation, and continuous statistical methods. In the Revman software, a random effect model was utilized to analyze the results by considering different factors that can affect the results of studies, such as variability in research methodology, breed, DON concentration, and duration of exposure to the toxin. In the statistical tool, forest plot, the standard mean difference was used because of the existence of a measurement scale in some studies. Weighted means, effect size, and 95 % confidence intervals were underutilized, to sum up, and displayed in forest plots, which allowed us to visually judge the trends of the analysis. The assessment of study heterogeneity in this meta-analysis was conducted using various statistical tools, such as Cochran I2 indices and tau2 and chi2 tests (Tufanaru et al., 2015). Using these tools, we determined the variability and uniformity within and between selected studies to determine the overall effect of DON mycotoxins on broilers. A significant difference was declared if the value of I2 was greater than 50 % or the p-value was less than 0.05. Variability in individual or subgroup analyses was reported when the 95 % CI did not intersect the vertical line of no effect (Dardiotis et al., 2018). Overall performance was assessed using Z-values and p-values. When analyzing using this software, a funnel plot was used to identify any publication bias reported in the study, both in independent studies and within subgroups.

Characteristics of included studies

A large number of studies were identified using the listed database search in indexed journals, of which 26 met the predefined inclusion criteria (Fig. 1). The studies included in this meta-analysis were published between 2008 and 2024, with the earliest published in 2008 and the most recent in 2024. Furthermore, a comprehensive review was conducted to confirm the similarity of the treatment conditions between the DON-affected and control groups in different studies. Therefore, we assessed the potential risk of publication bias. Thus, the study was unbiased in terms of treatment conditions and settings. A funnel plot was constructed using a fixed-effects model in the RevMan software version 5.4 to detect publication bias. A 95 % confidence interval was used to detect publication bias of the included data toward the standard error of the mean and SMD, favoring both the experimental and control groups. Effect of DON mycotoxin on daily feed Intake, weight gain and feed conversion ratio

The overall effect of DON on daily feed intake and weight gain is shown in Fig. 2. In the subgroup analysis, feed intake was affected by DON mycotoxin contamination and significantly reduced (SMD -1.6, 95 %CI [-1.79, -1.41]; sample size, 580; number of studies, 14; I2= 0; p < 0.00001). In all the included studies, broiler feed intake was significantly affected by dietary exposure to DON mycotoxins. However, there were significant differences in the studies treated with DON mycotoxins, possibly due to differences in sample size and the dose of DON used. The results of the average daily weight gain of this comprehensive meta-analysis are shown in Fig. 2. The DON-treated group showed a significant decrease in average daily weight gain with subgroups analysis of (SMD -1.46, 95 % CI [-1.73, -1.18]; sample size= 896, number of studies=18, I2= 67 %; p < 0.00001) The subgroup analysis indicated moderate heterogeneity compared with other studies in previous subgroups. The third parametric result of this meta-analysis was the feed conversion ratio presented in Fig. 3. Exposure to DON reduces broilers' FCR efficiency (SMD = 0.95, 95 % CI [0.62, 1.28]; sample size = 786; number of studies =16; I2= 77 %; P < 0.00001). Overall, few studies from subgroups two and three intersect the vertical line of no effect, are statistically insignificant, and arise from the limited sample sizes in the research process. Effect of DON mycotoxin on small intestine structure

Effect of DON morphological structure of duodenum

The effect of DON mycotoxins on the structure of the duodenum, including VH, CD, and VH: CD, was compared with that of the control diet, and the results showed that DON mycotoxins affected the morphological structure of the small intestine of broilers. As shown in Fig. 4, the morphological structure of the duodenum (villus height, crypt depth, and ratio of villus height to crypt height) was affected by DON exposure. Villi height was influenced by mycotoxin contamination, with less heterogeneity among studies (SMD -4.03; 95 % CI [ -4.66, -3.40]; sample size = 176; studies= 6; I2= 24 %; p < 0.00001). The results of this meta-analysis indicated that crypt depth was significantly different between studies with statistical measurements (SMD: -2.74; 95 % CI [ -3.24, -2.23]; sample size = 152; number of studies included =5; I2= 17 %; P < 0.00001).Fig. 4Forest plot of the effect of DON mycotoxin exposure on small intestine villa structure generated using RevMan software version 5.4. The villi height to crypt depth ratio is a critical factor for assessing the structural integrity and functionality of the small intestine. The results of different studies included in this meta-analysis indicated that the villi height to crypt depth ratio was significantly affected by DON exposure compared to the control, with statistical heterogeneity of (SMD -3.56, 95 % CI [-4.12, -3], sample size 152, number of studies= 5, I2= 7 %, and p < 0.00001). Thus, the overall effect on the morphological structure of the small intestine was statistically significant, indicating that moderate heterogeneity existed within the studies.

Effect of DON on the morphological structure of ileum

The pooled effect of the study on the morphological structure of the ileum is shown in Fig. 6. There was a significant reduction in ileum height (standard mean difference = -2.89, 95 % CI [-3.46, -2.32]; sample size = 204; number of studies = 6; I2 = 45; p < 0.00001). Discussion

This study aims to review and analyze the effects of Dietary DON mycotoxin exposure on broiler performance and small-intestine health. The results provide insights into the effects of DON mycotoxins on broiler performance and small intestine health, offering a wide range of understanding and its implications in the broiler industry. Understanding the implications of DON mycotoxins in the broiler industry will help to find effective mitigation strategies to minimize its effects on broiler performance and intestinal function. The findings of this meta-analysis show that DON mycotoxin exposure affects broiler performance, such as average daily feed intake, daily weight gain, feed conversion ratio, and intestinal health. The effect of DON mycotoxins on broiler production performance and intestinal health has been extensively studied in the past year. However, no single study has summarized and reported the impact of DON mycotoxins on broilers' performance and intestine health in a meta-analysis form. Many studies have reported that exposure of broilers to DON mycotoxin reduces performance and affects intestine health (Awad et al., 2013; Liew and Mohd-Redzwan, 2018; Awuchi et al., 2021; Azizi et al., 2021; Ekwo madu et al., 2021; Domijan et al., 2023; Girgis et al., 2010; Ghareeb et al., 2015; Santos and Molist, 2020).

Deoxynivalenol (DON) mycotoxin affects broilers' production performance by reducing appetite which results in a bitter taste, disturbing gastrointestinal tract functions, and nutrient malabsorption (Wang and Hogan, 2019). The reduction in feed intake results in decreased nutrient availability either for growth performance or weight gain (Awad et al., 2006; Kim et al., 2021). It reduces feed intake in response to disturbance in the gastrointestinal tract damage and impaired nutrient digestion and affects broilers' performance (Sarker et al., 2023). Regarding average daily gain (ADG), broilers exposed to the treatment group experienced a significant reduction in daily weight, as displayed in the forest plot analysis. The lower average gain is related to disruption of the digestive system, which in turn is related to nutrient absorption in the small intestine (Yunus et al., 2012). DON toxins reduce energy utilization by impairing the vital organs used for nutrient digestion and absorption (Weaver et al., 2022). Reduced ADG(g) is due to metabolic stress that disturbs normal metabolic processes and the use of energy in different parts of the body (Paraskeuas et al., 2021). It can also reduce weight gain through alteration of protein synthesis, affecting energy metabolism and changing substances produced for weight gain. A similar study on the effects of mycotoxins on broilers was conducted by (Wang and Hogan, 2019) to assess the effects of mycotoxins on feed preference and growth performance during experimental periods. Other studies have reported the effect of feeding blends of grains naturally contaminated with Fusarium mycotoxins on small intestinal morphology (Girish and Smith, 2008).

The result indicated that broilers' feed conversion ratio (FCR) was higher in the DON group than in the control group indicating that more feed is required to get a unit gram of body weight. DON mycotoxin affects FCR by reducing broilers' daily feed intake and utilization (Mgbeahuruike et al., 2021). It disturbs the nutrient metabolic process including the formation and utilization of some important proteins resulting in more energy on body maintenance than growth (Xu et al., 2022). This leads to requiring more feeds to attain weight gain which will increase FCR and also impair the immune system and shift energy for growth to maintain an immune system which affects broilers FCR (Okasha et al., 2024). Overall, it impairs broilers' growth performance and increases the cost of production affecting broilers' production efficiency.

The Small intestine is the main site of nutrient digestion, absorption, and transportation. Villi are used for the absorption of nutrients for body processes, and the connection between the structure and function of villi plays a significant role in the absorption of ingested nutrients (Wan et al., 2022). The crypt depth between villi is the area where villi cells reactivate themselves, and the villi height to crypt depth ratio produces glandular secretion used in feed digestion. DON-contaminated feed affects the small intestine of broilers by producing fractured, swollen, and shortened villi, thereby reducing the site of nutrient absorption (Riahi et al. 2020). DON mycotoxin exposure in chickens affects the morphological structure of the small intestine leading site of nutrient absorption (Girgis et al., 2010). The effect of DON mycotoxin on different sections of the small intestine morphological structure is displayed in the forest plot of this meta-analysis (Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6), and the morphological structure is severely affected by exposure to DON mycotoxin. There were statistically significant differences in all morphological structures between the control and DON-treated groups. It affects morphological structure through villous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia, mucosal inflammation, alteration of the gut microbiota, and intestinal lesions. These toxins cause villous atrophy, which reduces the surface area available for nutrient absorption in the intestine and thus impairs the ability to efficiently utilize nutrients from the feed (Ghareeb et al. 2015). Impairment of nutrient absorption by reducing villus height alters the morphological structure of villi, including blistering, distortion, and fusion (Liew and Mohd-Redzwan 2018). In general, it affects the normal function of villi by reducing the surface area available for nutrient exchange. It also affects the activity of intestinal transporters that are responsible for moving nutrients across the epithelial lining of the small intestine.

Antioxidants are used to reduce oxidative stress induced by nutritional or environmental factors (Mishra and Jha, 2019). The important antioxidant enzyme used for defense mechanisms in broilers CAT, SOD, T-OAC, and GPx, which act against oxidative stress and maintain cellular stability in the body. The result of this study indicates a significant reduction in broilers' antioxidant activities. CAT is responsible for catalyzing the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen to prevent the accumulation of hydrogen peroxide (Abdulrahman et al., 2024). We found that the exposure of broilers to DON affected antioxidant activity, as shown in Fig. 8.

Several studies have indicated that exposure to DON mycotoxins affects the antioxidant activity of broilers (Van Le Thanh et al., 2016). According to this meta-analysis's result exposure to DON, reduced antioxidant activity in all included studies with less heterogeneity Fig. 8. The SOD is responsible for the decomposition of superoxide into oxygen and hydrogen peroxide (Zhu et al., 2021). Exposure of broilers to DON reduces SOD activity and affects the ability to reduce the accumulation of superoxide anions, resulting in increased oxidative damage (Surai, 2016). Moreover, exposure to DON significantly reduced total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) in all included studies. The total antioxidant capacity of broilers is an indicator of broiler antioxidant status (Zheng et al., 2013), and the results of this meta-analysis favor the DON group indicating DON exposure affects T-OAC. DON exposure reduced GPx (Glutathione peroxidase) activity in broilers in all included studies. GPx plays a crucial role in preventing oxidative damage in broilers by reducing the reduction of H2O2(hydrogen peroxidase) and lipid peroxidase, thereby decreasing oxidative damage in broilers (Wu et al., 2018). A reduction in GPx activity indicates that exposure to DON reduces the ability of broilers to detoxify peroxidase, thereby elevating oxidative damage in broilers. In this subgroup analysis, we found that exposure of broilers to DON affects antioxidant defense activities and reduces the ability to decompose peroxidase.

The findings across all the included studies indicated that exposure to DON mycotoxins significantly reduced immunoglobulin levels in broilers. DON suppresses the level of the immune system and exposes the broiler to pathogens. It reduces the primary antibody IgM, which is used to defend against pathogenic organisms by initiating an immune reaction against pathogens (Bai et al., 2021). Across all the included studies, the IgM result indicated a consistent decrease in IgM level. Decreased IgM levels indicated that DON exposure impaired the primary immune response against pathogens. IgM recognizes and responds to early pathogenic infections in broilers; as a result, broilers exposed to dietary DON experience low defense mechanisms against infectious pathogens and cannot recognize and neutralize early pathogenic infections (Yousaf et al., 2022). The results of this meta-analysis indicated a significant decrease in IgG levels in all included studies, which affected the ability of broilers to defend against pathogens entering the systemic circulation. This immunoglobulin is responsible for defense against toxins, bacteria, and viruses. If decreased, broilers are less capable of defending against secondary infections (Chen et al., 2024). The other important immunoglobulin included in this meta-analysis is IgA which responsible for protecting the mucosal surface, which is responsible for protecting the mucosal surface, including the gastrointestinal tract, from invading pathogens (Abokor et al., 2021). The outcomes of all included studies indicated that IgA levels in the DON-exposed group reduced IgA levels in mucosal membranes; thus, our study indicated that exposure to DON mycotoxins reduces broiler immunoglobulin levels.

Based on the result of this comprehensive meta-analysis, broilers suffer from contamination of feed with DON mycotoxin and require functional mycotoxin reduction strategies. Based on our comprehensive meta-analysis, we proposed a recommended and appropriate intervention plan for effective DON mycotoxin mitigation strategies (Fig. 13). Dirty, moisture, and high humidity create favorable conditions for the development and multiplication of mycotoxin in the animal feed so, it we recommend using good agricultural practices to avoids favorable environment for mycotoxin development on broiler feeds. Regular feed testing for DON mycotoxin levels and efficient monitoring reduce the possible risk of DON toxins. Supplementation of diet with antioxidants can be used to induce the possible oxidative stress of DON on broilers. Some antioxidants like vitamin E and selenium are recommended for reducing oxidative damage in broilers exposed to DON (El-Senousey et al., 2018; Mazur-Kuśnirek et al., 2019). The other, use of different plant extracts containing phytochemicals such as flavonoids and polyphenols can reduce the oxidative stress of DON on broilers. Conclusion

This comprehensive meta-analysis confirmed that feeding broilers deoxynivalenol mycotoxin significantly impairs productive performance and small intestine health. The result demonstrated a notable decrease in daily feed intake, weight gain, feed efficiency, compromised small intestine morphology, antioxidant enzyme activity, and immunoglobulin levels. The negative impact of DON on small intestines decreases feed intake, digestion, and absorption. Furthermore, a decrease in immunoglobulin levels compromises both the immune system and mucosal immunity. It induces oxidative stress due to its effect on intestinal antioxidant enzyme activities exposing broilers to disease. The findings of this study suggest the importance of implementing effective strategies to mitigate mycotoxin contamination of broiler feed. There are limited studies on the interaction effect between DON and environmental factors like housing density, and nutrient composition so, we recommend future studies to focus on this aspect to develop more comprehensive DON mycotoxin reduction strategies.

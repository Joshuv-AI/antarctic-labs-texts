Correlation between ApoE gene polymorphisms and the prognosis of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention
Authors: Maoshi Li, Xia Ma, Jingyue Hu, Rui Yang, Zhuxin Li, Azhi ShaMa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveMany studies have shown that apolipoprotein E (ApoE) gene polymorphisms have an impact on coronary artery disease. However, the role of ApoE gene polymorphisms in secondary prevention is still unclear. The purpose of this study was to retrospectively investigate the influence of apolipoprotein E gene polymorphisms on the prognosis of coronary artery disease.MethodsWe collected and compared the clinical information of patients hospitalized for percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Multivariate hierarchical logistic regression was used to evaluate the relationship between ApoE gene polymorphisms and the prognosis of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing PCI.ResultsAfter statin treatment, the concentrations of triglycerides, LDL-C and total cholesterol significantly decreased in patients with ε3/ε4, ε3/ε3, ε2/ε3 and ε2/ε2 (p < 0.05), whereas the concentrations of triglycerides, LDL-C and total cholesterol did not significantly decrease in patients with ε4/ε4 (p > 0.05). After statin treatment, the incidence rates of hypertriglyceridemia, high LDL-C and high total cholesterol significantly decreased in patients with ε3/ε4, ε2/ε3 and ε3/ε3 (p < 0.05), whereas the incidence rates of hypertriglyceridemia, high LDL-C and high total cholesterol did not decrease significantly in patients with ε4/ε4 (p > 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression model suggested that there were no significant differences in the incidence rates of secondary PCI or in-stent restenosis in patients with different ApoE genotypes one year following PCI (p > 0.05).ConclusionAfter secondary prevention, there was no significant difference in the incidence of in-stent restenosis or secondary PCI among patients with different ApoE genotypes one year after PCI.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease is a common cause of death worldwide [1]. Both genetic variants and traditional risk factors are involved in the occurrence of coronary artery disease [2]. After secondary prevention, the residual risk of coronary artery disease is still high one year later in patients undergoing PCI [3].

ApoE is a kind of polymorphic apolipoprotein, which is involved in the formation of HDL and affects the clearance of lipoproteins from blood [4, 5]. The major alleles of ApoE gene are ε3, ε4 and ε2, and different genotypes of patients have different lipid metabolisms [6]. ε4 has a high affinity for lipoproteins which are rich in triglyceride, whereas ε3 has high affinity for high-density lipoproteins and is involved in the interaction between lipids and lipoproteins [7, 8]. Many studies have shown that ε4 allele increases risk of cardiovascular disease [9–11], a study has shown that ApoE ε3/ε4 genotype is associated with an increased the risk of premature coronary artery disease [12]. In the Hakka population in southern China, ApoE ε4 allele may be associated with susceptibility to coronary artery disease [13]. In addition, a study has shown that ApoE polymorphisms are associated with a higher risk of coronary artery disease in postmenopausal Hakka women in southern China [14]. A meta-analysis revealed that multifactorially adjusted hazard ratios (95% confidence intervals) for ε4/ε4 and ε2/ε2 versus ε3/ε3 were 1.15 (1.04–1.26) and 1.02 (0.83–1.24) for ischemic cerebrovascular disease, 1.11 (1.04–1.19) and 0.94 (0.83–1.08) for ischemic heart disease, 1.03 (0.89–1.17) and 1.49 (1.20–1.87) for peripheral arterial disease, respectively [15].

A study indicated that ApoE ε4 carriers had worse functional outcomes after intracerebral hemorrhage, but not after intracerebral hemorrhage or subarachnoid hemorrhage [16]. A study of 169 patients reported that ApoE gene polymorphisms are closely related to the incidence of in-stent restenosis after extracranial and intracranial artery stenting, and the genotype ε4/ε4 is an independent risk factor for in-stent restenosis [17]. A study of 155 patients revealed that ApoE gene polymorphisms are associated with in-stent restenosis, and the genotype ε3/ε4 is an independent risk factor for in-stent restenosis after stenting at the beginning of the vertebral artery [18]. However, the role of ApoE gene polymorphisms in the secondary prevention of coronary artery disease is still unclear, and the influence of apolipoprotein E gene polymorphisms on the prognosis of coronary artery disease is poorly understood. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to retrospectively study the influence of apolipoprotein E gene polymorphisms on the prognosis of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing PCI.

Methods

Patients population

This single-center retrospective study included all patients who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention and then underwent coronary angiography one year later in the Department of Cardiology, Daping Hospital, Army Medical University from January 2017 to December 2019, with a total number of 1071. Patient data, including cardiovascular risk factors, clinical features and prognosis were obtained from the hospital electronic records. Multi-vessel lesion defined as coronary angiography confirmed that at least two epicardial main vessel stenosis were more than 50%. In-stent restenosis was diagnosed by coronary angiography, in which the narrow diameter of the lumen in the implanted segment of the stent and the proximal and distal 5 mm segments of the stent exceeded 50%. Family history refers to immediate family members, including parents or brothers and sisters. Percutaneous coronary intervention was considered to be successful, as previously defined.

Detection of ApoE gene polymorphisms

ApoE gene is located on human chromosome 19 and consists of 3597 nucleotides. It is an autosomal dominant gene with significant genetic polymorphisms. The genotype of ApoE gene is determined by two single nucleotide polymorphisms (388T > C and 526 C > T), including three alleles, ε2(388T-526T), ε3(388T-526C), ε4(388–526C). ε3 is a wild-type gene, ε2 and ε4 are mutant genes, resulting in three homozygotes (ε2/ε2, ε3/ε3, ε4/ε4) and three heterozygotes (ε2/ε3, ε2/ε4, ε3/ε4). We extracted DNA from peripheral blood cells by Npex 192 automatic nucleic acid extractor, then used real time fluorescence quantitative PCR to detect single nucleotide polymorphisms of ApoE gene via a SLAN®−96 S real time fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument.

Secondary prevention of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing PCI

Drug treatment

Use antiplatelet drugs (take aspirin combined with P2Y12 receptor inhibitors for at least 1 year after PCI), statins (such as atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), beta blockers (such as metoprolol, bisoprolol) to prevent thrombosis, stabilize plaque, and reduce the risk of cardiovascular events.

Controlling risk factors

Actively controlling risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia and smoking. Lifestyle changes such as weight control, moderate exercise, smoking cessation, and a low salt and low-fat diet.

Psychological and lifestyle adjustment

Maintain a good psychological state and avoid excessive tension and anxiety. Actively cope with the pressure in life and ensure adequate sleep and rest.

Regular reexamination and follow-up

ECG, echocardiography and other examinations are regularly used to monitor cardiac function and disease changes.

Statistical analyses

Values are presented as mean ± SD or number (%) in each group. Values are presented as mean ± SD or number (%). Paired sample t test or nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare the continuous variables among different groups, and the Bonferroni correction method was used to adjust for multiple comparisons. We compared categorical variables among different groups using chi-square or Fisher’s exact Test. P value of < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. A multivariate logistic regression model was used to analyse the potential factors related to the incidence of secondary PCI and in-stent restenosis, with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). All statistical analysis was carried out by SPSS 26 software.

Discussion

ApoE is a circulating glycoprotein, which palys an important role in lipid metabolism and promotes the removal of residues of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins from the circulation to liver [19]. The major alleles of ApoE gene are ε3, ε4 and ε2, ε3 is the most common subtype of ApoE gene, whereas ε4 and ε2 are relatively low [6, 20]. Apolipoprotein E gene polymorphisms are associated with many diseases, ApoE gene is a major genetic risk determinant of late-onset Alzheimer disease, with the ApoE ε4 allele conferring an increased risk and the APOE ε2 allele conferring a decreased risk, relative to the common APOE ε3 allele [21]. In addition to Alzheimer disease, APOE gene polymorphisms also play a role in other neurological diseases, such as Lewy body dementia [22], Parkinson disease [23], and frontotemporal dementia [24]. ApoE gene polymorphisms are involved in various viral infection-related diseases. ApoE ε4 is associated with increased susceptibility to infection with herpes simplex virus type-1 and HIV, and ApoE ε4 has been shown to determine the incidence and progression of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 infection [25].

Coronary artery disease causes great physical and mental harm to patients worldwide [26]. ApoE participates in the regulation of triglyceride and cholesterol metabolism, and influences the occurrence of coronary artery disease [6]. Many studies have shown that APOE gene polymorphisms are associated with cardiovascular diseases. A comprehensive genome study involving a large number of samples with 48,457 individuals indicated that individuals with ε4 allele are more susceptible to coronary heart disease than individuals with ε3 allele [27]. A recently study revealed that ApoE ε3/ε4 genotype and ε4 allele are independent risk factors for T2DM complicated with CAD, but not for T2DM [28]. According to an Italian investigation, the ε4 allele is associated with low APOE concentrations, which is a risk factor for coronary artery diseases [29].

Many studies indicated that ε4 allele increases the occurrence of coronary artery disease [30–32]. However, the role of ApoE gene polymorphisms in secondary prevention is still unclear and there are lack of evidences on the influence of apolipoprotein E gene polymorphism on the prognosis of coronary artery disease. In this study, among the 1071 patients who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention, ε3/ε3 accounted for 67.51% of all genotypes, followed by the ε2/ε2 (1.12%), ε2/ε3 (11.20%), ε4/ε4 (1.31%), ε3/ε4 (18.21%), and ε2/ε4 (0.65%). Compared with ApoE ε3 homozygotes, ε4 are associated with higher levels of triglycerides, cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, whereas ε2 homozygotes are correlated with increased ApoE levels, higher triglycerides levels, lower levels of cholesterol levels and lower LDL cholesterol levels [33, 34].

After treatment with statins, the concentrations of triglycerides, LDL-C and total cholesterol significantly decreased in patients with ε3/ε4, ε3/ε3, ε2/ε3 and ε2/ε2,while the concentrations of triglycerides, LDL-C and total cholesterol did not decrease significantly in patients with ε4/ε4 (Table 2). After statin treatment, the incidence rates of hypertriglyceridemia, high LDL-C and high total cholesterol significantly decreased in patients with ε3/ε4, ε2/ε3 and ε3/ε3, whereas the incidence rates of hypertriglyceridemia, high LDL-C and high total cholesterol did not decrease significantly in patients with ε4/ε4 (Table 3). These results suggest that ε4/ε4 genotype reduces the lipid-lowering effect of statins.

This study investigated the influence of different ApoE genotypes on the prognosis of coronary artery disease in patients who received percutaneous coronary intervention. This study indicated that there were no significant differences in the incidence rate of secondary PCI or in-stent restenosis in patients with different ApoE genotypes one year after PCI. The results suggest that after secondary prevention, there were no significant differences in the incidence rate of secondary PCI or in-stent restenosis in patients with different ApoE genotypes one year after PCI.

This study further used a multivariate logistic regression to analyse the incidence of in-stent restenosis and secondary PCI in patients with different ApoE genotypes. After adjustment for the clinical characteristic factors, there was no significant difference in the incidence rate of secondary PCI or in-stent restenosis in patients with different ApoE genotypes one year following PCI (Table 5). The results further suggest that after secondary prevention, there were no significant differences in the incidence rate of secondary PCI or in-stent restenosis in patients with different ApoE genotypes one year after PCI.

Study strengths and limitations

This study assessed the influence of different ApoE genotypes on coronary artery disease in patients undergoing coronary angiography. First, the sample size of many previous studies was small, and their statistical results may be influenced by the sample size. In order to reduce the impact of sample size, this study included 1071 patients who underwent PCI. Second, many studies have shown that ε4 allele increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, but the influence of ApoE gene polymorphism on the prognosis and secondary prevention of coronary artery disease is poorly understood. This study retrospectively analysed the influence of ApoE gene polymorphism on the prognosis of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing PCI.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective study, It’s hard to rule out the factors of activities and diet, which may cause certain bias.Second, this was a single-center study, and different ethnic populations may have different results.Third, the sample size of this study was small, which may have led to some bias.

Conclusions

After secondary prevention, there were no significant differences in the incidence of in-stent restenosis or secondary PCI among patients with different ApoE genotypes one year after PCI.

Hochschild cohomology of Beilinson algebras of graded down-up algebras with weights ( n , m n,m )
Ayako Itaba, Shu Minaki

Abstract
. Let A = A ⁡ ( α , β ) A=A(\alpha,\beta) be a graded down-up algebra with weights ( deg ⁡ x , deg ⁡ y ) = ( n , m ) (\deg x,\deg y)=(n,m) and β ≠ 0 \beta\neq 0 , and ∇ A \nabla A the Beilinson algebra of A A . Note that A A is a 3 3 -dimensional cubic AS-regular algebra. Assume that gcd ⁡ ( n , m ) = 1 \operatorname{gcd}(n,m)=1 and m ≥ n m\geq n . If n = 1 n=1 and m = 1 m=1 , then a description of the Hochschild cohomology group of ∇ A \nabla A was already known by Belmans. If n = 1 n=1 and m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , then the dimensional formula of the Hochschild cohomology group of ∇ A \nabla A was given by the first author and Ueyama. In this paper, we give the dimensional formula of the Hochschild cohomology group of ∇ A \nabla A for the case that n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . As a byproduct of this dimensional formula, we prove that, for m > n > 1 m>n>1 , the derived category of a non-commutative projective scheme associated to A A is not equivalent to the derived category of any smooth projective surface. Moreover, we give the ring structure on the Hochschild cohomology group with the Yoneda product for the case that m ≥ n ≥ 1 m\geq n\geq 1 .

For using an algebraic geometrically approach to noncommutative algebra,

It was defined by the Serre quotient of the category of finite generated right modules by the subcategory of finite dimensional right modules.
For the relationship between a noetherian AS-regular algebra

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definition of a down-up algebra and

Since
\operatorname{gcd}(n,m)=1
, we have
\operatorname{gcd}(x^{2m}-1,x^{2n}-1)=(x^{2}-1)
,
and then
\operatorname{gcd}(x^{(n+m)}-1,f(x))=\operatorname{gcd}(x^{m}+1,x^{n}+1)=\begin{cases}(x+1)&\text{if $n$ and $m$ are odd,}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Therefore,

\operatorname{rank}C=\begin{cases}(n+m)-\deg(x+1)&\text{if $n$ and $m$ are odd,}\\
(n+m)-\deg(1)&\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}=\begin{cases}(n+m)-1&\text{if $n$ and $m$ are odd,}\\
(n+m)&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Theorem 3.8

Let

of

as follows

•

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{HH}^{0}(\nabla A)=1;

•

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{HH}^{1}(\nabla A)=\begin{cases}2\quad\text{\rm for Case I},\\
1\quad\text{\rm for Case I\hskip-1.2ptI};\end{cases}

•

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\nabla A)=\begin{cases}m+5\quad\text{\rm for $n=2$ and Case I},\\
m+4\quad\text{\rm for $n=2$ and Case I\hskip-1.2ptI},\\
n+m+1\quad\text{\rm for $n\geq 3$ and Case I},\\
n+m\quad\text{\rm for $n\geq 3$ and Case I\hskip-1.2ptI};\end{cases}

Proof.

We compute the dimensions of

as the similar way of
[
11

.
Since

,
and then

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\partial^{1}}=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\widehat{P^{0}}-\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Ker}\widehat{\partial^{1}}=2(n+m)-1.

Therefore, by Lemmas
3.3
,
3.5
,
3.7
, we also obtain the following equations;

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{HH}^{1}(\nabla A)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Ker}\widehat{\partial^{2}}-\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\partial^{1}}=(\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\widehat{P^{1}}-\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\partial^{2}})-\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\partial^{1}}

Corollary 3.13
.

Let

be a down up algebra with weights

\deg\,x=n
,
\deg\,y=m
,
\beta\neq 0

, and the bounded derived category

of

.

be the Beilinson algebra of A and
\operatorname{HH}^{r}(\nabla A)
the Hochschild cohomology groups of

Then

\chi_{\operatorname{HH}}(\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\nabla A))=\begin{cases}m+4\quad&\text{if $n=2$},\\
n+m\quad&\text{if $n\geq 3$},\\
\end{cases}\quad\neq 2(n+m)=\operatorname{rank}K_{0}(\mathsf{D^{b}}(\mathsf{tails}\,A))

where

\chi_{\operatorname{HH}}(\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\nabla A)):=\sum_{{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{k}}}\operatorname{HH}^{k}(\nabla A).

So,
\mathfrak{s}
does not act unipotently on
K_{0}(\mathsf{D^{b}}(\mathsf{tails}\,A))
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{s}
acts unipotently on
K_{0}(\mathsf{D^{b}}(\mathsf{tails}\,A))
.
Let
M
be the Gram matrix.
Then
\mathfrak{s}=M^{-1}M^{T}
is unipotent (see
[
6
]
).
By Proposition
3.12
, the Coxeter matrix
\Phi_{\nabla A}
of
\nabla A

is defined by
-M^{-T}M
(see Happel
[
10
, Section 1]
).
Then we have
-\operatorname{tr}\Phi_{\nabla A}=\operatorname{tr}M^{-T}M=\operatorname{tr}M^{-1}M^{T}=\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{s}
.
Since all eigenvalues of a unipotent matrix are equal to
1
,

\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{s}=2(n+m)
holds.
By Happel’s trace formula (
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
),
so we have
\chi_{\operatorname{HH}}(\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\nabla A))=-\operatorname{tr}\Phi_{\nabla A}=2(n+m)
.
On the other hand, by Theorem

\chi_{\operatorname{HH}}(\operatorname{HH}^{\bullet}(\nabla A))=\begin{cases}m+4\quad&\text{if $n=2$,}\\
n+m\quad&\text{if $n\geq 3$.}\\
\end{cases}

This is a contradiction.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{s}
does not act unipotently.
∎

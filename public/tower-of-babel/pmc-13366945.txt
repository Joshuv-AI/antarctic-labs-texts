Occlusion effects, safety, and clinical prognosis of Watchman and LAmbre occluders in left atrial appendage closure
Authors: Tianyu Li, Mingrui Cui, Siyuan Lu, Song Zhang, Renjie Ma, Jing Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study preformed to investigate the safety and effectiveness of the Watchman and LAmbre occluders in left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) for the prevention of thrombosis and stroke in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF).MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 93 patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) who underwent LAAC at Northern Jiangsu People’s Hospital from January 2020 to February 2023. Of these, 48 were implanted with the Watchman occluder and 45 with the LAmbre occluder.ResultsDifferences in LAA opening diameter and left atrial diameter between groups were statistically significant (P < 0.001, P = 0.012). The complete occlusion rate of the LAA in the Watchman group was 45.83% (22/48), compared to 24.44% (11/45) in the LAmbre group, with a significant difference between groups (P = 0.031 < 0.05). Neither group experienced any transient ischemic attacks, cardiac tamponade, or deaths during the perioperative period. Three-month postoperative CTA check-ups showed no displacement or detachment of occluders in either group. The incidence of residual shunt measured by CTA was significantly lower in the Watchman group than in the LAmbre group. The most common occurrence in both groups was a 1–3 mm peri-device leak (PDL) at the edge of the occluders. One major PDL (≥ 5 mm) occurred in the LAmbre group. Both groups had device-related thrombosis (DRT) on the atrial side of the occluder, all of which were in patients with PDL and disappeared after 3 months of systemic anticoagulation therapy.ConclusionBoth the LAmbre and Watchman occluders show high safety and clinical prognosis in LAAC treatment for AF.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05978-4.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a global health burden that contributes to mortality, morbidity, and reduced quality of life for millions of people, with more than 20% of strokes and twice the increase in cardiovascular morbidity and mortality attributed to AF, and its prevalence increases with age, reaching as high as 9% of 80–90 year olds [1]. LAAC is a feasible alternative therapy for patients with nonvalvular AF (NVAF) who have high stroke and bleeding risks or contraindications to oral anticoagulants (OAC) [2, 3]. As of the end of 2021, more than 30,000 patients with AF in China had opted for combined radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and LAAC to mitigate the occurrence of thromboembolic cerebral strokes, with the number of centers capable of conducting LAAC exceeding 500. Currently, a variety of LAA occlusion devices are clinically employed, with the Watchman occluder being the most extensively utilized globally [4, 5]. In recent years, the domestically developed LAmbre occluder has also been increasingly adopted in clinical settings [6, 7], with its safety, efficacy, and clinical prognosis yet to be thoroughly examined. This study aims to investigate the clinical efficacy and application value of the LAmbre and Watchman occluders in the LAAC treatment of AF.

Methods

General information

A retrospective analysis was conducted from January 2020 to February 2023 on 93 NVAF patients who underwent LAAC at Northern Jiangsu People’s Hospital, including 48 patients implanted with the Watchman occluder and 45 with the LAmbre occluder. Inclusion criteria were: (1) LAA opening diameter < 31 mm for Watchman implantation, ≥ 31 mm for LAmbre implantation; (2) Intolerance to long-term oral anticoagulant medication (e.g., warfarin or NOACs). Exclusion criteria included: (1) Preoperative detection of thrombus in LAA or left atrium (2) Severe heart failure and hepatic or renal dysfunction (3) Recent active bleeding or infection history (4) Non-compliance or loss to follow-up (5) Cardiac function NYHA class IV. Surgical success was defined as safe implantation of the occluder in LAA, appropriate implantation depth, and postoperative 3-month CTA showing residual shunt < 5 mm and compression ratio of 10% − 30%. Safety and clinical prognosis were defined as no dislodgment, embolization, or patient mortality events in the 3-month postoperative CTA. Clinical and radiological data preoperatively, intraoperatively, and during follow-up were collected. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee (KY 2023-080-01), and informed consent was obtained from the patients or their families.

Preoperative CTA and three-dimensional reconstruction

Preoperative CTA was performed using the Siemens Somatom Force dual-source CT, with patients undergoing strict breath-hold training to ensure image quality. Patients were placed in the supine position after fasting for 4–6 h. All patients received 0.8 mg sublingual nitroglycerin 3–5 min before the scan. A bolus-tracking technique was used with the region of interest at the ascending aorta origin, triggering the first scan at 80 HU. Contrast injection: 50 mL Ultravist (370 mg I/mL) at 5 mL/s followed by 30 mL saline at the same rate. Prospective ECG-triggered acquisition parameters: 120 kV, 900–1000 mA, 64 × 0.6 mm collimation, 0.75 mm slice thickness/spacing, and 0.3 s/r gantry rotation time. A dual-phase protocol was performed, with the second acquisition delayed by 60 s to evaluate contrast filling after LAAC and exclude intracardiac thrombus. Post-scan data were transferred to the Syngo.VB10 workstation and analyzed using MM Reading software for image post-processing, including multiplanar reconstruction (MPR), virtual reality (VR), and maximum intensity projection (MIP), to obtain multi-angle LAA images and occluder data.

Intraoperative TEE

Pre-procedural transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) was performed to evaluate LAA morphology and exclude spontaneous echo contrast (SEC) or sludge. Intraoperative TEE was then performed using the Philips EPIQ 7 C system with an X7–2T 2 ~ 7 MHz color Doppler imager, either under local anesthesia of the oropharynx or general anesthesia. The X7–2T probe was gently inserted into the patient’s esophagus, obtaining LAA two-dimensional dynamic images over three consecutive cardiac cycles at approximately 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° angles.

Interatrial septum puncture and LAA angiography

LAAC was guided by DSA machine (Artis Q, Siemens, Germany). The same septal puncture site was used for both ablation and LAAC in all combined procedures. Following general anesthesia, the right femoral vein was punctured using the Seldinger technique, inserting a 6 F long sheath, followed by a long guidewire to the superior vena cava and then the long sheath. Transseptal puncture was precisely planned using preoperative CTA. On the AP view, the carina and lower cardiac silhouette defined the puncture height range using the vertebral body as a scale. The fossa ovalis center was identified on MPR images, and the reference RAO angle was determined by rotating the 3D VR view until the fossa ovalis center aligned with the right edge of the spine. The angle between the interatrial septum and the coronal plane was also measured. Puncture was generally performed in the posterior-inferior part of the fossa ovalis based on these parameters and LAA anatomy. An interatrial septum puncture needle was introduced through the sheath, and after successful puncture, a delivery sheath was left in place. After confirming left atrial pressure (≥ 10 mmHg), LAA angiography was performed at the best angle indicated by preoperative cardiac CTA three-dimensional reconstruction and the most commonly used position (right anterior oblique 30° + caudal 20°), measuring the LAA opening diameter and depth. After interatrial septum puncture, heparin was administered at 60–100 IU/kg. Activated clotting time (ACT) was measured 5–10 min after the bolus and then every 30 min thereafter, maintaining ACT at 250–300 s.

Occluder deployment

The Watchman 2.5 occluder (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, MA, USA) is an “inner-stent” self-expanding type with an open tail end. Appropriate Watchman occluder size was selected based on preoperative CTA three-dimensional reconstruction and intraoperative LAA angiography measurements, typically adding 4 ~ 6 mm to the maximum opening diameter. A 14 Fr delivery sheath was used for the Watchman device. The occluder was connected to the delivery cable outside the body, with proximal exhaust and the distal end of the delivery system flushed with heparin saline. After connecting the delivery sheath and advancing the occluder to ensure its head position aligns with the working line of the delivery system, a pigtail catheter was advanced to the distal end of the LAA through the delivery sheath. The delivery sheath was then advanced to the target anchoring area, and after securing it, the catheter was slowly withdrawn, introducing the occluder to the head-end mark of the delivery sheath. TEE was used to observe the stability and occlusion effect of the occluder, and the delivery sheath was removed after the occluder was fully expanded. The Watchman occluder was fully released after meeting the PASS criteria.

The LAmbre occluder (Lifetech Scientific, Shenzhen, China) is a “double-disc” type device, comprising a fixation disc and a sealing disc. The appropriate occluder size is selected based on preoperative CTA three-dimensional reconstruction combined with intraoperative LAA angiography measurements. The fixation disc diameter is suggested to be 2 ~ 4 mm larger than the maximum opening diameter, and the sealing disc diameter 4 ~ 6 mm larger. A 8–10 Fr delivery sheath was used. The fixation disc of occluder is introduced into the LAA via the delivery sheath and slowly unfolded. Once its position is confirmed, the delivery sheath is retracted, and the proximal sealing disc is gradually pushed forward until it fully opens. After reconfirmation through TEE and angiography that the occluder is securely positioned and there is no significant pericardial effusion, the procedure is completed. The LAmbre occluder is fully released after satisfying the COST criteria. If the initial deployment did not meet the PASS or COST criteria (e.g., suboptimal position, compression ratio, or seal), partial recapture was performed to adjust the device position before final release. Complete recapture was not required in this series.

Postoperative LAAC antithromotic therapy and follow-up observational indices

According to our institution’s latest post-LAAC antithrombotic protocol, the standard regimen consists of: 6 weeks of single anticoagulant therapy (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, or warfarin), followed by 6 months of dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + clopidogrel), and then lifelong single antiplatelet therapy (aspirin or clopidogrel). Regular follow-up with cardiac CTA is conducted three months after the procedure to reassess the occluder position, evaluate the residual shunt, and assess the occurrence of DRT. For the LAmbre occluder, Peri-device leak (PDL) was graded on CTA following the method of Korsholm et al. [8]. Grade 1, leak confined to the sealing disc; Grade 2, leak involving both the sealing disc and fixation lobe; Grade 3, leak at disc and lobe with complete LAA contrast opacification. Incidences of perioperative transient ischemic attacks (TIA), pericardial effusion, cardiac tamponade, and mortality are also documented. All patient follow-up was conducted through electronic medical records, outpatient records, and telephone interviews.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software package (IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0). Measurement data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared between groups using t test; count data expressed as frequency or percentage were compared between groups using chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method. Univariable analyses were performed to explore predictors of PDL, incomplete endothelialization, and DRT. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparative analysis of general clinical parameters between groups

In this cohort of 93 NVAF patients undergoing LAAC, 48 were implanted with the Watchman occluder and 45 with the LAmbre occluder. Comparative analysis of patient age, gender, baseline diseases, AF types, CHA2DS2-VASc scores, and HAS-BLED scores between the Watchman and LAmbre groups revealed no significant statistical differences (P > 0.05 for all comparisons). Comparative evaluation of intraoperative, perioperative, and 3-month postoperative CTA follow-up between groups

The Watchman group exhibited a complete LAA occlusion rate of 45.83% (22/48), while the LAmbre group demonstrated a rate of 24.44% (11/45), with this difference being statistically significant (P = 0.031 < 0.05). Figures 1 and 2 provide visual representation. No statistically significant differences were observed in terms of surgical techniques and duration of operation between the groups (P > 0.05). During the perioperative phase, both groups experienced minor pericardial effusions, with 3 cases (6.25%) in the Watchman group and 2 cases (4.44%) in the LAmbre group, all resolving with conservative management. There were no instances of TIA, cardiac tamponade, or fatalities in either group during this period. Follow-up CTA at 3 months post-procedure confirmed the absence of occluder displacement or detachment in both groups.

In this study, the occluder size selected for the Watchman group was smaller than that for the LAmbre group (P < 0.001). The incidence of residual shunt, as measured by CTA, was notably lower in the Watchman group compared to the LAmbre group. PDL ranging from 1 to 3 mm at the edge of the occluders were the most frequent occurrence in both groups (Fig. 3A - B). According to the CT-based PDL classification, grade 2 leaks were the most common. Only 7 (15.56%) patients had a grade 1 leak, whereas 36 (80.00%) had a grade 2 leak. A significant PDL (≥ 5 mm) was observed in one case in the LAmbre group, as depicted in Fig. 4. A total of 19 patients (20.43%) across both groups presented with PDL > 3 mm, of which 15 had markedly enlarged left atria, with LAA opening perimeter exceeding 100 mm. Suboptimal heart failure control during the early postoperative period may have contributed to left atrial enlargement in this patient. Most PDLs in this research were minor (≤ 3 mm) and were predominantly located in the posterior inferior portion of the LAA ostium. Both groups identified DRT on the atrial side of the occluders (one instance in the Watchman group and two in the LAmbre group) (Figs. 3C and 4), all occurring in patients with PDL. Upon 3-month postoperative systemic anticoagulation therapy with warfarin and CTA re-evaluation, the thrombi were essentially resolved, with no significant disparity in DRT incidence between the groups (P > 0.05), as detailed in Table 3. The Diabetes (OR = 0.236, 95% CI [0.071–0.785], P = 0.019) and CHA2DS2-VASc score (OR = 1.546, 95% CI [0.941–2.539], P = 0.086) were independent predictors of PDL (Table 4). The Sex (OR = 0.315, 95% CI [0.113–0.884], P = 0.028), Age (OR = 0.094, 95% CI [1.024–1.169], P = 0.008) and Diabetes (OR = 0.219, 95% CI [0.061–0.792], P = 0.021) were independent predictors of incomplete endothelialization (Table 5).

Discussion

According to the Chinese Atrial Fibrillation Management Guidelines (2025), LAAC is recommended as a Class I (Level A) indication for patients with AF after LAA electrical isolation. The guidelines also support LAAC for patients with CHA₂DS₂-VA score ≥ 2 who have contraindications to or failure of long-term oral anticoagulation, or those with a HAS-BLED score ≥ 3. Furthermore, the guidelines recognize that for patients who experience stroke despite oral anticoagulation, or for those undergoing catheter ablation, LAAC is a reasonable strategy to reduce thromboembolic risk and bleeding events [9–11]. The Watchman occluder is currently the most extensively utilized device in LAAC and has been substantiated for its efficacy and safety in multiple randomized controlled trials (RCTs) [12]. The LAmbre occluder, developed domestically, is now being promoted. A prospective, multicenter clinical study reported no instances of occluder dislodgement, embolism, or mortality within a 1-year follow-up period after implantation in 152 patients [13], aligning with the findings and literature of this study. A systematic review of 403 NVAF patients treated with LAAC using the LAmbre occluder demonstrated a high implantation success rate, occlusion performance, and follow-up data [14], suggesting superior occlusion efficacy of the domestically produced LAmbre occluder. However, large-scale randomized controlled studies comparing the efficacy and safety of the LAmbre and Watchman occluders are currently lacking.

A Japanese study comparing LAA opening diameters among different populations found that LAA opening diameters in Western patients, represented by the United States, were significantly smaller than those in Asian patients, represented by Japan [15]. The Watchman and LAmbre occluders have fundamental differences in model design. The maximum diameters of the Watchman occluder and the newer Watchman-FLX occluder are 33 mm and 35 mm, respectively, but they are not suitable for AF patients with LAA opening diameters above 31 mm [16, 17], whereas the LAmbre occluder, with a maximum diameter design of 40 mm, can be used for treating AF patients with larger LAA opening diameters, offering more options for surgeons. Inohara et al. [18] reported successful occlusion of a large LAA with an opening diameter of 48.0 mm × 45.3 mm using a custom LAmbre occluder with fixation/sealing discs of 30 mm/50 mm, demonstrating the advantages and potential of the LAmbre occluder in occluding large LAA ostium.

In this study, the LAA occlusion success rate in the Watchman group (45.83%) was higher than in the LAmbre group (24.44%). The LAmbre group had larger LAA opening diameters than the Watchman group, and the chosen occluder sizes were also larger. However, postoperative CTA revealed that the incidence of PDL in the Watchman group, especially ≤ 1 mm and > 3 mm but < 5 mm, was significantly lower than in the LAmbre group. Furthermore, one case of a major PDL (≥ 5 mm) occurred in the LAmbre group, indicating that PDL formation might be related to larger occluder types and LAA opening diameters, consistent with previous research findings [19]. Rajwani et al. [20] concluded that PDL is related to the irregularity of the LAA ostium. Typically, PDL occurs due to smaller occluder sizes, but in this study, PDL was caused by occluder models being relatively larger than the LAA opening. Of the 19 patients with moderate to severe PDL (> 3 mm, ≥ 5 mm) in this study, 15 had significantly enlarged left atria, exceeding the maximum size standard for occluders, suggesting that PDL formation might be related to left atrial size. In the OPTION trial [21], only about 40% of patients underwent LAAC immediately after ablation, based on the concern that post-ablation myocardial edema may resolve over time and lead to chronic-phase PDL. However, whether concomitant ablation truly increase PDL risk remains unclear. Future studies with standardized serial imaging are warranted. Both groups in this study found DRT formation in patients with PDL, but there is currently no clear clinical correlation between PDL at the occluder edge and DRT formation. It has been suggested that [22] a PDL of 0 ~ 5 mm after LAAC moderately increases thromboembolic and bleeding events, and no increase in adverse events has been seen in patients with PDL > 5 mm despite continuous anticoagulation regimens. Therefore, whether patients with PDL post-LAAC require anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy to prevent DRT requires further research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, both the LAmbre and Watchman occluders demonstrate high safety and clinical prognosis in the LAAC treatment of AF. However, in terms of occlusion efficacy (residual leak), the Watchman occluder is superior, with a lower incidence of PDL post-LAAC. Limitations of this study include: (1) a single-center, small-sample retrospective design (2) non-randomized device selection with potential selection bias (3) validation by multicenter, large-sample clinical research is necessary. Additionally, the duration of patient follow-up was relatively short. We agree that future studies with systematic imaging follow-up are needed.

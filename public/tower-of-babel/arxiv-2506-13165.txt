Non-convergence and convergence of bounded solutions to semilinear wave equation with dissipative boundary condition Note: This research was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (12272297).
Zhe Jiao, Xiao Li

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the long-time dynamics of semilinear wave equation subject to dissipative boundary condition. To do so, we first analyze the set of equilibria, and show it could contain infinitely many elements. Second, we show that, for some nonlinear interior sources, the wave equations have solutions that do not stabilize to any single function, while they approach a continuum of such functions. Finally, if the interior source is a Łojasiewicz-type function, the solution of the wave equation converges to an equilibrium at a rate that depends on the Łojasiewicz exponent, although the set of equilibria is infinite.

1.2
Related work

Before giving the detailed proof of our main results, let us give a short survey of the literature relevant to this initial-boundary value problem for the semilinear wave equation.

To study the asymptotic behavior of the solution, the existence and uniqueness of both local and global solutions to the wave equation should be investigated first. There is a considerable number of papers dealing with the well-posedness problem

,
CCP2004
,
CCL2007
,
CEL2002
,
Vitillaro_2002
,
vitillaro2002global
,
Cavalcanti2006
.
In the case that the boundary source
f_{1}
is linear, or absent, the author
Vitillaro_2002
makes use of the potential well theory to investigate the existence of global solutions as well as the blow-up of weak solutions in finite time. Meanwhile, in
CCP2004
the authors combine the potential well theory with the Faedo-Galerkin procedure to obtain the existence of global solutions.
Another method to study the well-posedness problem is based on the nonlinear semigroup theory, as presented in
CEL2002
and
Cavalcanti2006
.
As to the presence of nonlinear boundary source
f_{1}
, it leads to the fact that Lopatinski condition

,
tataru1998regularity
fails for Neumann hyperbolic map. Thanks to the

, even linear damping changes problem to the one where Lopatinski condition is satisfied

and

apply the same technique as in

to address the issue of well-posedness. An excellent literature review of this context can be found in
CCL2007
.

Suppose that for any given initial datum, the wave equation admits a unique global solution in a certain function space.
Naturally, we can ask the problem (Q1), that is, how does the energy functional defined by the global solution evolves? We provide a brief outline of the research in this direction.
Beginning with a linear wave equation, the authors in
bardos1992sharp
,
martinez1999
provide a deep insight into the relationship between energy decay and linear boundary damping. In particular, the paper
bardos1992sharp
gives sharp sufficient conditions for stabilization of the wave equation from the boundary.
The papers
zuazua1990uniform
,
martinez1999new
prove that the energy of the wave equation decays to zero with an explicit decay rate under some geometrically restricted conditions on the nonlinear boundary damping.
Considering the interaction of nonlinear interior source term and nonlienar boundary damping, the authors in
CCP2004
,
Cavalcanti2006
prove the uniform decay rates for the wave equation.
For the general case, we refer to the papers
LT1993
,
CCL2007
, where a semilinear model of the wave equation with nonlinear interior source and nonlinear boundary damping- source interaction. Moreover, the authors in
CCL2007
establish effective optimal decay rates of the energy for the semilinear wave equation.

Assume that the global solution of the wave equation (
4
) is also bounded in a certain norm. For the asymptotic behavior of the solution, there are two possibilities (Q3-2) and (Q3-3) as time goes to infinity. To our best knowledge, the problem (Q3-2) has not been studied in earlier works, while limited work
CEL2002
,
CEL2004
,
chueshov2004
,
Hao2006
,
Jiao2018
has been done for (Q3-3) as mentioned before. However, the convergence results in these few papers hold true only when the damping acts on the entire boundary and additional requirements are imposed on the equation (
4

If the set of equilibria is finite, the convergence of the bounded solution to an equilibrium is proved in
CEL2002
,
CEL2004
,
chueshov2004
. We need to point out in Theorem
3.1
of our work that this finite assumption on the set of equilibria is not always true. In the case of the presence of linear interior and boundary dampings, the convergence result is shown in
Hao2006

Given the above discussion, the main novelty of our contribution to solving the problem (Q3) can be summarized as follows:
(i) We impose some conditions on the interior source that cause the set of equilibria to be infinite, and construct an interesting example satisfying these conditions, thereby answering (Q3-1);
(ii) We affirmatively answer (Q3-2) by proving the

(iii) We show that the convergence result holds under the assumption that the interior source is a Łojasiewicz-type function, thereby affirmatively answering (Q3-3).

Well-posedness

For the better readability and completeness, this section briefly recalls the well-posedness results for the wave equation (

[

and
[

Let us define the energy function

\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|(u,u_{t})^{\top}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Omega}F_{0}(x,u)\mathrm{d}x

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\

Now, we state the well-posedness result of problem (

Lemma 2.1
.

(Existence and regularity of solutions)

1.

Suppose that the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})^{\top}

. Then there exists a unique weak solution

to problem (

) such that

(a)

\partial_{\nu}u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty)\times\Gamma)
,

\partial_{\nu}u+u+g(u_{t})=0

(b)

\mathcal{E}(u(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(u_{s}(s)),u_{s}(s)\rangle\mathrm{d}s=\mathcal{E}(u(0)),

(c)

and weak solutions satisfy the following variational equality with derivatives understood in the sense of distribution

\frac{d}{dt}(u_{t},\phi)+(\nabla u,\nabla\phi)+\langle g(u_{t})+u,\phi\rangle+(f(x,u),\phi)=0,\quad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

2.

Furthermore, if the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})^{\top}\in\mathcal{D}
, then the corresponding weak solution is classical and possesses the regularity properties

(u,u_{t})^{\top}\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{D})\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}),\quad u_{tt}\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}({\Omega})).

Proof.

First, we need to reformulate the origin problem (
4
) as an abstract first-order evolution equation. To do this, we introduce the Robin-Laplacian operator
\triangle_{R}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
with the domain

D(\triangle_{R})=\left\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega):\partial_{\nu}u+u=0\quad\textrm{on $\Gamma$}\right\}.

This densely defined operator is injective and self-adjoint. Moreover, it can be extend to a continuous operator, which is still denoted by
\triangle_{R}
. This extension is the duality map from
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}
given by

(-\triangle_{R}u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}uv\mathrm{d}S

for any
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Next we introduce the Robin map
R:H^{s}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)
, which is defined as

Rp=q\Longleftrightarrow\triangle q=0\quad\textrm{in $\Omega$, and }\partial_{\nu}q+q=p\quad\textrm{on $\Gamma$}.

By the elliptic theory in
lions2012non
we know
R
is continuously for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Given
p\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and
v\in D(\triangle_{R})
we have

\begin{split}(\triangle_{R}v,Rp)=(\triangle_{R}v,q)&=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla q\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}vq\mathrm{d}S\\
&=-\int_{\Gamma}v\partial_{\nu}q\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Omega}v\triangle q\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}vq\mathrm{d}S\\
&=-\int_{\Gamma}v\partial_{\nu}q\mathrm{d}S-\int_{\Gamma}vq\mathrm{d}S=-\langle v,p\rangle.\end{split}

which implies

\langle R^{\ast}\triangle_{R}v,p\rangle=(\triangle_{R}v,Rp)=-\langle v,p\rangle.

Due to the fact that
D(\triangle_{R})
is dense in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, the adjoint of the Robin map satisfies

R^{\ast}\triangle_{R}v=-Tv=-v|_{\Gamma},\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
T
is the trace operator.
Moreover, the fact that
\triangle_{R}
is self-adjoint implies

\langle-Tv,p\rangle=\langle R^{\ast}\triangle_{R}v,p\rangle=(\triangle_{R}v,Rp)=\langle v,\triangle_{R}Rp\rangle.

Then we have
T^{\ast}=-\triangle_{R}R
.

Based on the preliminary work above, we turn to introduce a nonlinear operator
\mathcal{A}
with the domain

D(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\end{array}\right)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega):u+R\left(g(Tv)\right)\in D(\triangle_{R})\right\}

by setting

\mathcal{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-I\\
-\triangle_{R}&T^{\ast}\left(g(T\cdot)\right)\end{array}\right)\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{A}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-v\\
-\triangle_{R}(u+R\left(g(Tv)\right))\end{array}\right).

(8)

Since we have
D(\mathcal{A})\subseteq H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
, the mapping
v\mapsto g(Tv)
is continuous from
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, and

0=\partial_{\nu}[u+R\left(g(Tv)\right)]+u+R\left(g(Tv)\right)=\partial_{\nu}u+u+g(v)\quad\textrm{on $\Gamma$},

then it deduces that

.

Now, we can rewrite the wave equation (

\partial_{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\

Infinitely many equilibria

An equilibrium
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
to problem (
4
) is a classical solution to the following nonlinear elliptic equation with Robin boundary condition.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta\varphi+f_{0}(x,\varphi)=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\
\partial_{\nu}\varphi+\varphi=0&\textrm{on $\Gamma$.}\end{array}\right.

(10)

From Lemma 3.1 in
Hao2006
we can deduce that an equilibrium
\varphi
is also a critical point of the following functional in
H^{1}(\Omega)

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Omega}F_{0}(x,u)\mathrm{d}x

with
F_{0}(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\mathrm{d}s
.
The set of all the equilibria is denoted by
\mathcal{N}
.
It is obvious that the structure of the set

depends on the nonlinearity

.

In the following, we are about to search the existence of infinitely many solutions for the stationary problem (

Theorem 3.1

(Infinitely many solutions)
.

Let
f_{0}(x,s)=\lambda(x)s-\hat{f}(x,s)
and
\lambda(x)\in C(\bar{\Omega})
. Assume
\hat{f}(x,s)
satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

For
2<q<2^{\ast}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
,

|\hat{f}(x,s)|\leqslant C\left(1+|s|^{q-1}\right).

(2)

Given any
R>0
, there exists a constant
\kappa>2
such that

s\hat{f}(x,s)-\kappa\hat{F}(s)\geqslant 0,\quad|s|\leqslant R

with with
\hat{F}_{0}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\hat{f}(t)\mathrm{d}t
, and

\lim_{|s|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{s\hat{f}(x,s)-\kappa\hat{F}(s)}{s^{2}}=1-\frac{\kappa}{2}.

(3)

\hat{f}(x,s)
is odd, that is,
-\hat{f}(s)=\hat{f}(-s)
.

Then if
\lambda(x)>\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-2}
, the problem (
10
) has a sequence of solutions
\varphi_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
E(\psi_{n})\rightarrow\infty
as
n
goes to infinity.

It can be observed that the second condition imposed on
\hat{f}
is weaker than the corresponding condition in Theorem 1.1 of

. Consequently, this theorem generalizes Theorem 1.1 in
Zhao2008
. To prove this theorem, we employ the Fountain Theorem proposed in

.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The subsequent proof involves two steps.

Step 1
. Suppose that
\{\varphi_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
, for every
C_{1}>0
,

E(\varphi_{n})\rightarrow C_{1},\quad E^{\prime}(\varphi_{n})\xrightarrow{H^{1}(\Omega)^{\ast}}0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Here,
E^{\prime}(\cdot)
is the first-order variation of the functional
E(\cdot)
.
We will show that
\{\varphi_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

From the second condition, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
N>0
such that

\left|s\hat{f}(x,s)-\kappa\hat{F}(s)-\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)\right|\leqslant\epsilon,\quad|s|\geqslant N.

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\kappa}s\hat{f}(x,s)-\hat{F}(s)&\geqslant 0,\quad|s|\leqslant R,\\
\frac{1}{\kappa}s\hat{f}(x,s)-\hat{F}(s)&\geqslant\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa},\quad|s|\geqslant N.\end{split}

(11)

5
Convergence in a Łojasiewicz-type landscape

In the previous section, it has been shown that smoothness of the interior nonlinearity
f_{0}
cannot guarantee the convergence of the solution to points of equilibria. This section will demonstrate that the solution of equation (
4
) in a Łojasiewicz-type landscape evolves to some equilibria as time goes to infinity.

5.1
Łojasiewicz-type landscape

A functional
E(\cdot)
is called a Łojasiewicz-type landscape if for each
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}
there exist numbers
C>0
and
\sigma>0
, both dependent on
\varphi
, such that for some
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
one has

\|-\Delta u+f_{0}(x,u)\|+\|\partial_{\nu}u+u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\geqslant C|E(u)-E(\varphi)|^{1-\eta},

(42)

whenever
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
,
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\sigma
. The inequality (
42
) is so-called the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality, and the number
\eta
is called Łojasiewicz exponent (cf.
Hao2006
,
chill2003lojasiewicz
,
haraux2003rate
).
As can be seen, the nonlinearity
f_{0}
makes
E(u)
a Łojasiewicz-type landscape. Thus, we refer to this nonlinearity as a Łojasiewicz-type function, and denote by Ł the space of Łojasiewicz-type functions.

It is necessary to point out that there exists a broad class of Łojasiewicz-type functions. In
[
Hao2006
, Lemma 3.8]
, the authors have proved that all analytic functions belong to the space Ł.
In addition, the function

f_{0}(x,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda(x)s-se^{-\frac{1}{s^{2}}},&s\neq 0,\\
0,&s=0,\end{array}\right.

(43)

which has been proposed in Section
3
, is smooth for
s\in\mathbb{R}
but non-analytic at
s=0
. However, it serves as an explicit example of the Łojasiewicz-type function. Indeed, for the functional
E(\cdot)
associated with the Robin–Laplace operator and the function (
43
), it is easy to see that the hypotheses in
[
chill2003lojasiewicz
, Corollary 4.6]
are satisfied. Let
\varphi\neq 0
be the critical point of this functional
E(\cdot)
. Then there exist constants
\tilde{C}>0
and
\tilde{\sigma}>0
such that for every
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with
\|u-\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\tilde{\sigma}

\|E^{\prime}(u)\|_{H^{-1}}\geqslant C|E(u)-E(\varphi)|^{1-\tilde{\eta}},

(44)

where
E^{\prime}(\cdot)
is the first derivative of
E
. Due to

\begin{split}\langle E^{\prime}(u),v\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}uv\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Omega}f_{0}(x,u)v\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\triangle u+f_{0}(x,u)\right)v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}(\partial_{\nu}u+u)v\mathrm{d}S\\
&\leqslant\|-\triangle u+f_{0}(x,u)\|\|v\|+\|\partial_{\nu}u+u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\leqslant\left(\|-\triangle u+f_{0}(x,u)\|+\|\partial_{\nu}u+u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\end{split}

for any
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we get

\|E^{\prime}(u)\|_{H^{-1}}\leqslant\|-\triangle u+f_{0}(x,u)\|+\|\partial_{\nu}u+u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(45)

Combining(
44
) and (
45
) gives the inequality (
42
), which means
f_{0}(x,s)
given in (
43
) is a Łojasiewicz-type function.

5.2
Geometry of the domain

To ensure the effectiveness of the restricted boundary damping, it is necessary to impose the following constraint on the shape of the domain
\Omega
.

1.

(A4) The domain
\Omega
is quasi-star-sahped, that is, there exists a
C^{3}
vector field

\textsl{a}=(\textsl{a}_{1},\textsl{a}_{2},\textsl{a}_{3}):\bar{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

such that

\textsl{a}(x)\cdot\nu\geqslant 0,\quad x\in\Gamma,

(46)

and its Jacobian matrix
\{\frac{\partial\textsl{a}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\}
is positive definite, that is,

\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\textsl{a}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\geqslant\varpi|\zeta|^{2},\quad x\in\bar{\Omega},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(47)

for some positive constant
\varpi
.

As an illustration, we give some examples of quasi-star-shaped domains: (1) convex domains with smooth boundaries, such as a ball or a cylinder; (2) a star-shaped domain with respect to some point
x_{0}\in\Omega
, defined as for all
x\in\bar{\Omega}
,
\Omega
contains the segment
[x_{0},x)=\{(1-s)x_{0}+sx:0\leqslant s<1\}
. It is easy to see that a star-shaped domain is not necessarily convex.

We note that the quasi-star-shaped domain possesses the following property, which will be applied to obtain the boundedness of the classical solution to equation (
4
).

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\Omega
is a quasi-star-shaped domain, then there exists a
C^{3}
vector field

\textsl{b}=(\textsl{b}_{1},\textsl{b}_{2},\textsl{b}_{3}):\bar{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

such that

(1)

for some sufficiently small constant
\varpi_{1}>0
one has

\textsl{b}(x)\cdot\nu\geqslant\varpi_{1},\quad x\in\Gamma,

(48)

(2)

the Jacobian matrix
\{\frac{\partial\textsl{b}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\}
is positive definite, in particular,

\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\textsl{b}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\geqslant\varpi_{2}(\nabla\cdot\textsl{b})|\zeta|^{2},\quad x\in\bar{\Omega},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(49)

for
\varpi_{2}>0
small enough,

(3)

and its trace satisfies
\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x)>\varpi_{3}:=\varpi_{1}\frac{|\Gamma_{1}|}{|\Omega|}
for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\textsl{b}(x):=\textsl{a}(x)+\varpi_{1}\nabla\phi
, where
\varpi_{1}>0
is a constant, and
\phi:\bar{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\triangle\phi+\frac{|\Gamma_{1}|}{|\Omega|}=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$},\\
\partial_{\nu}\phi=1&\textrm{on $\Gamma$}.\end{array}\right.

Then we have

\begin{split}\textsl{b}(x)\cdot\nu&=\textsl{a}(x)\cdot\nu+\varpi_{1}\nu\cdot\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x)&=\nabla\cdot\textsl{a}(x)+\varpi_{1}\triangle\phi=\nabla\cdot\textsl{a}(x)+\varpi_{1}\frac{|\Gamma_{1}|}{|\Omega|},\\
\frac{\partial\textsl{b}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}&=\frac{\partial\textsl{a}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}+\varpi_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\right).\end{split}

(50)

Combining the first equality of (
50
) with (
46
) implies (
48
). By the positive definite property (
47
) of
\{\frac{\partial\textsl{a}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\}
, we have
\nabla\cdot\textsl{a}(x)>0
, and hence, the second equality of (
50
) gives

\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x)>\varpi_{1}\frac{|\Gamma_{1}|}{|\Omega|},\quad x\in\bar{\Omega}.

Note that the matrix
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\}
is symmetrical. Let
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}
be the minimal eigenvalue of this matrix. From the third equality of (
50
) and choosing
\varpi_{1}\ll\frac{\varpi}{|\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}|}
, it deduces that
\{\frac{\partial\textsl{b}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\}
is positive definite, and there exists a small constant
\varpi_{2}>0
such that

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\textsl{b}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}&=\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\textsl{a}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}+\varpi_{1}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\\
&\geqslant\varpi_{2}(\nabla\cdot\textsl{b})|\zeta|^{2},\quad x\in\bar{\Omega},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{split}

which means the inequality (
49
) holds.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

We can replace the definition in
(A4)
of a quasi-star-shaped domain with the one provided in this proposition. Moreover, without loss of generality, we can also assume that there exists a positive constant
\chi
such that

|\textsl{b}(x)|+|\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x)|+|\nabla(\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x))|<\chi,\quad x\in\Gamma;\quad|\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x)|+|\triangle(\nabla\cdot\textsl{b}(x))|<\chi,\quad x\in\Omega

(51)

due to the fact that
b
is a
C^{3}
vector field on
\bar{\Omega}
.

. If

, then for

, the Łojasiewicz-type inequality (

) holds. By (

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[\mathcal{V}(t)-E(\varphi)]^{\eta}=-\eta[\mathcal{V}(t)-E(\varphi)]^{\eta-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{V}(t)\geqslant C_{10}\|u_{t}\|.

(89)

Integrating (

) over

, it can be seen that

\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\|u_{t}\|ds\leq C_{10}\{[H(t_{n})-E(\psi)]^{\theta}-[H(t)-E(\psi)]^{\theta}\}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

which implies

as time goes to infinity. From Lemma

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t)-\psi\|_{H^{1}}=0.

Another case is that for all

. Similarly, we have

\int_{t_{n}}^{t^{\ast}_{n}}\|u_{t}\|ds\leq C_{10}\{[H(t_{n})-E(\psi)]^{\theta}-[H(\hat{t}_{n})-E(\psi)]^{\theta}\}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(90)

, then we can deduce that

which contradicts the definition of

6
Conclusion

This paper systematically investigates problem (Q3) and provides an answer to the open question posed in
rodrigues2022
. We propose some sufficient conditions on the interior nonlinearity that ensure the equilibria set is infinite, and we also construct an explicit example of such nonlinearity. In this scenario, the convergence results established in
CEL2002
,
chueshov2004
are not applicable, as the finiteness of the equilibria set is a crucial assumption in those works. However, we offer a new convergence result that does not require this finiteness condition. In this way, our findings complement the results of
CEL2002
,
chueshov2004
. Additionally, we find a class of
C^{\infty}
-functions for which the wave equation (
4
) has a bounded solution whose
\omega
-limit set is a continuum of equilibria. As our future research work, it is interesting to consider the convergence problem for the wave equation (
1
) in a Łojasiewicz-type landscape.

Estimated burden of acute gastroenteritis and foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal Salmonella enterica, Shigella and norovirus in Gansu Province, Northwest China
Authors: Xiang-Lai Sang, Yu-Fang Fan, Xiao-Cheng Liang, Jian-Dong Li, Yan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInformation on the burden of disease caused by foodborne pathogens in Northwest China is limited. This study aims to estimate the burden of acute gastroenteritis (AGE) and foodborne salmonellosis, shigellosis and norovirus gastroenteritis in Gansu Province.MethodsUsing data from population surveys conducted during 2011–2015, the burden of AGE was estimated in Gansu Province. By determining the number of pathogen-specific cases from sentinel hospital surveillance conducted during 2014–2019, with adjustments applied for healthcare-seeking behavior and stool specimen submission based on data from population surveys, the burden of foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal Salmonella enterica, Shigella and norovirus in Gansu Province was calculated. A multiplier calculation model was developed, using Monte Carlo simulation to perform uncertainty estimation.ResultsThe adjusted monthly prevalence of AGE was 4.0%, equivalent to an average of 0.53 episodes of AGE per person-year in the region. The multiplier for salmonellosis was estimated at 1,682, for shigellosis at 1,590, and for norovirus gastroenteritis at 2,689. The estimated annual incidence of foodborne salmonellosis, shigellosis and norovirus gastroenteritis were 1,329, 195, and 2,590 cases per 100,000 population, respectively, which substantially exceeded the incidence of reported foodborne disease.ConclusionsWe concluded that AGE and foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella and norovirus constitute a significant health burden in Gansu Province. By continuously implementing population survey and enhanced sentinel hospital surveillance, with constructing a multiplier calculation model to estimate the burden of disease, we may gain a better understanding of the food safety situation in Northwest China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-27563-2.

Background

Foodborne disease is an important food safety and public health issue globally, particularly in low-income and middle-income countries, causing serious health and economic burdens [1]. Estimates of foodborne diseases can be used to develop food safety strategies and to determine priority intervention measures. Acute gastroenteritis (AGE) is a common clinical manifestation of foodborne disease, primarily caused by pathogen-contaminated food. The study of determining the burden of AGE provides essential foundational data for evaluating the burden of foodborne diseases and foodborne pathogen infections.

The estimated burden of foodborne pathogen infections varies significantly across regions. Although relevant studies have been conducted in North America [2–5], Europe [6–8], Australia [9], Japan [10], Jordan [11], and eastern, southern, and southwestern China [12–14], information on the burden of disease caused by foodborne pathogens in Northwest China is limited.

The economy of the northwest region is significantly lagging behind other regions in China, so it is necessary to guide the allocation of limited health resources in this region based on the assessment of the burden of foodborne pathogen infections, in order to effectively reduce the probability of foodborne diseases. Non-typhoidal Salmonella enterica, Shigella and norovirus are common causes of foodborne disease [15]. Therefore, this study aims to estimate the burden of AGE and foodborne gastroenteritis caused by these three pathogens in Gansu Province, Northwest China.

Methods

Population survey

To determine the annual incidence of AGE (IAGE), the proportion of AGE cases seeking medical care (P1), and the proportion of AGE cases submitting a stool specimen for testing among those seeking medical care (P2) in the general population, face-to-face cross-sectional population surveys were conducted from December 2011 to November 2013 (cycle 1) and from April 2014 to March 2015 (cycle 2) in Gansu Province (Fig. 1). In cycle 1, the sentinel sites were: (a) Baiyin District (1,372 sq km, population–294,400) in Baiyin prefecture, (b) Liangzhou district (5,081 sq km, population–1,010,295) in Wuwei prefecture, and (c) Qingcheng County (2,673 sq km, population–261,898) in Qingyang prefecture. In cycle 2, the sentinel sites were: (a) Chengguan district (208 sq km, population–1,278,745) in Lanzhou prefecture, (b) Ganzhou district (3,661 sq km, population–507,433) in Zhangye prefecture, (c) Jingchuan county (1,461 sq km, population–281,145) in Pingliang prefecture, (d) Kongtong district (1,930 sq km, population–504,848) in Pingliang prefecture, (e) Liangzhou district, and (f) Lintao county (2,855 sq km, population–507,386) in Dingxi prefecture. The data for cycle 1 from June 2012 to May 2013 has been published, whose results are available elsewhere [16].

Map of population survey sites and sentinel hospitals in Gansu Province, Northwest China

Households were randomly selected on a monthly basis from each sentinel site and standardized questionnaires were used. In the chosen household, one individual next to celebrate his/her birthday was asked about the occurrence of AGE in the past 4 weeks. Proxy respondents were used for study participants aged < 12 years and for individuals aged between 12 and 16 years when parental or guardian consent was not given to interview them directly. A case of AGE was defined as an individual with ≥ 3 loose stools or any vomiting within 24 h, but excluding those (a) with colorectal cancer, Crohn’s disease, irritable bowel syndrome, colitis, diverticulitis of large intestine, or another chronic disease with symptoms of diarrhea or vomiting diagnosed by a doctor, or (b) who report their symptoms were due to non-infectious causes such as pregnancy, excess alcohol, radiotherapy/chemotherapy, drugs, menstruation, or food allergy [17]. If the selected individual had a non-infectious explanation for symptoms, the nearest residence was selected as replacement. Respondents who identified multiple episodes of AGE, separated by seven days or more, during the 4 weeks prior to the interview were asked to report only their most recent episode. Detailed methods are available in the report of Sang et al. [16].

Sentinel hospital surveillance

Hospital surveillance was carried out at 10 sentinel hospitals in Gansu Province during 2014–2019. The sentinel hospitals were chosen on the basis of possessing necessary laboratory testing capabilities and reporting mechanisms. The sentinel sites for the hospital surveillance were: Lanzhou prefecture, Baiyin prefecture, Pingliang prefecture, Qingyang prefecture, Tianshui prefecture, Wuwei prefecture, Jiuquan prefecture, and Gannan Tibetan autonomous prefecture (Fig. 1). The population residing in the catchment area of the sentinel hospitals in 2010 was 4,498,870 (Np), constituting approximately 17.59% of the total permanent population in Gansu Province (25,575,263).

Rectal swabs or stool specimens from surveillance cases were collected by the sentinel hospitals and were tested for non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella, and norovirus by hospital laboratories. No testing for norovirus was conducted in 2014. A case of surveillance was defined as an outpatient case with diarrhea symptoms as the main complaint, with ≥ 3 bowel movements per day and abnormal stool characteristics (loose stools, watery stools, mucous stools, or bloody stools, etc.). The stool specimens sent to the hospital laboratories are assumed to be representative of the community specimens.

The laboratory tests for non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella and norovirus were conducted according to national standard methods [18–20]. For non-typhoidal S. enterica, specimens were enriched in selenite cystine broth and then surface coated (or smeared) on bismuth sulfite agar and xylose-lysine-desoxycholate (XLD) agar (or CHROMagar Salmonella agar, and Hektoen enteric agar). For Shigella, specimens were streaked on XLD agar and MacConkey agar. Viral nucleic acids were extracted using commercial kits, amplified by reverse transcription, and identified by polymerase chain reaction for detection of norovirus. Viral nucleic acid extraction was performed using commercial kits and primers procured from leading domestic biotechnology companies, including XABT (Beijing Applied Biological Technologies Co., Ltd.), Naturebio (Shanghai Naturebio Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), and Bioperfectus (Jiangsu Bioperfectus Technologies Co., Ltd.). All products obtained marketing approval from the State Administration for Market Regulation.

Burden of disease

A multiplier calculation model was developed to estimate the burden of foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella, and norovirus in Gansu Province, using Monte Carlo simulation to perform uncertainty estimation. We multiplied NP, IAGE, P1, and P2 to estimate the number of stool specimens submitted in the sentinel hospital surveillance area (NSS), then NSS was divided by the number of stool specimens tested in the sentinel hospitals (NS) to estimate the proportion of laboratory performing test for nontyphoidal S. enterica, Shigella and norovirus (P3).

According to a proficiency testing program organized by the China National Center for Food Safety Risk Assessment in 2018, the test sensitivity for Salmonella of those sentinel hospital laboratories was 84.09%, with a range of 75–100% (P4−1). According to the interlaboratory quality evaluation of clinical tests organized by the Gansu Clinical Laboratory Center, the test sensitivity for Shigella of those sentinel hospital laboratories was 90%, with a range of 80–100% (P4−2). As lack of proficiency testing results, the test sensitivity for norovirus (94.55%, with a range of 89.09–100%) reported in the literature was used (P4−3) [21].

PERT distributions with specified minimum, most likely and maximum values were used to model uncertainty for IAGE, P1, P2, and P4 (Table 1) [22]. The analysis was performed using the software @RISK (version 7.6, Palisade, Newfield, N.Y.) with 25,000 iterations per estimation. In the supplemental materials, the values underlying the minimum, most likely and maximum values reported are presented (S1 Table). Multiply the reciprocal of the P1, P2, P3, and P4 to obtain the multiplier of the total surveillance artifact for each pathogen (MT). The number of positive specimens tested was multiplied with MT to estimate the number of positive samples in the surveillance area. By using the population of the surveillance area reported in the 2010 census, the estimated annual disease per 100,000 population in the surveillance area were assessed.

To estimate the incidences of foodborne salmonellosis, shigellosis, and norovirus gastroenteritis in Gansu Province, the proportions of foodborne transmission for each pathogen as documented by Scallan et al. [4] were multiplied by our calculated infection estimates. Data on foodborne disease outbreaks were reported to the National Foodborne Disease Surveillance Network (NFDSN), a passive surveillance system, by the disease control and prevention agencies. We compared the estimated incidences of foodborne gastroenteritis caused by each pathogen with the estimated incidences of foodborne disease based on the data reported by Gansu Province to the China NFDSN to assess underreporting.

Statistical analyses

We performed descriptive statistics for the total sample using proportions (%), and means where applicable. The monthly prevalence of AGE was calculated as the number of respondents reporting AGE symptoms during the 4 weeks prior to the interview divided by the total number of respondents. The association between demographic factors and the occurrence of AGE was tested using the χ2 test. The healthcare-seeking rate for AGE was defined as the number of respondents who sought medical consultation for AGE divided by the total number of AGE cases. The stool specimen submission rate represented the proportion of diarrheal patients from whom clinicians collected stool specimens for laboratory testing. Through weighting by age, gender, and residence, differences in estimates for survey sample and the target population were adjusted using the Sixth National Population Census data of Gansu Province as the reference population.

The pathogen-specific isolation or detection rate was calculated as the proportion of submitted stool specimens tested for non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella, and norovirus. The association between demographic factors and the pathogen-specific isolation or detection rate was tested using the χ2 test. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 21.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and results were considered significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Population survey

A total of 12,000 individuals were surveyed, with 11,050 valid questionnaires (response rate = 92.1%). Of the 11,050 respondents, 353 (3.2%) reported that they had experienced symptoms of gastroenteritis in the past 4 weeks. Of these respondents, 15 were categorized into the non-case group for non-infectious reasons, leaving 338 respondents to be maintained in the case group. The adjusted monthly prevalence of AGE was 4.0% [95% confidence interval (CI) 3.6–4.4], equivalent to an incidence rate of 0.53 (95% CI 0.52–0.54) episodes per person-year in Gansu Province. The prevalence differed between the cycle 1 and cycle 2 surveys, i.e., 6.1% (95% CI 5.3–6.8) and 2.9% (95% CI 2.5–3.2), respectively.

Demographic characteristics and AGE prevalence of the respondents are shown in Table 2. The monthly prevalence was significantly higher in males than in females (4.4% vs. 3.6%, p < 0.01). Children under 4 years of age had the highest prevalence (8.3%) of AGE. Percentage of respondents reporting AGE was significantly higher in those who had traveled (9.7%) than those who had not (3.9%) (p < 0.01). The prevalence of AGE varied by seasons, with the highest in autumn (4.8%), and the lowest in winter (2.7%) ( p < 0.01) (Table 2).

Sentinel hospital surveillance

A total of 14,675 rectal swabs or stool specimens were collected in Gansu Province during 2014–2019 in 10 sentinel hospitals. The isolation or detection rates were 1.5% (227/14,672) for non-typhoidal S. enterica, 0.7% (107/14,675) for Shigella, and the 14.5% (1,000/6,875) for norovirus.

Over 50% of the cases involved infants and children younger than 2 years. The highest detection rate of norovirus was also found in this age group (16.9%, p < 0.01). Non-typhoidal S. enterica demonstrated the highest isolation rate among children aged 3–4 years (3.8%, p < 0.01). Shigella had the highest isolation rate among cases aged ≥ 65 years (3.1%), followed by those aged 5–14 years (2.4%, p < 0.01). Variations in pathogen isolation or detection rates were noted across different years (p < 0.05). There was no statistically significant difference in pathogen isolation or detection rates between different genders (p > 0.05) (Table 3).

Burden of disease

Multiplying NS by IAGE, the estimated annual number of AGE cases in the sentinel hospital surveillance area (NAGE) is 3,494,132 (95% CI 1,209,657–7,126,129). Multiplying the NAGE by P1, it is estimated that annual 1,763,156 (95% CI 437,304–4,235,793) patients sought medical care (NMC). Multiplying the NMC by P2, it is estimated that annual 683,349 (95% CI 115,355–1,920,218) people have submitted a stool specimen in the sentinel hospital surveillance area. The estimated multipliers were 1,682 (95% CI 579–3,453) for salmonellosis, 1,590 (95% CI 551–3,260) for shigellosis, and 2,689 (95% CI 930–5,492) for norovirus gastroenteritis (Table 4).

Combined with the proportions of foodborne transmission [4], the annual incidence of foodborne salmonellosis, shigellosis and norovirus gastroenteritis were estimated at 1,329 (95% CI 458–2,723), 195 (95% CI 67–401), and 2,590 (95% CI 896–5,283) per 100,000 population, respectively (Table 4). During 2014–2020, according to the foodborne outbreaks reported to the NFDSN by Gansu Province, there were 160 cases caused by non-typhoidal S. enterica, 3 cases caused by Shigella and 15 cases caused by norovirus, corresponding to 0.089, 0.002 and 0.008 per 100,000 population, respectively (Table 4).

Discussion

This is the first report on the burden of foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal S. enterica, Shigella, and norovirus in Northwest China. Utilizing data from population surveys and sentinel hospital surveillance, the burden of foodborne gastroenteritis in Gansu Province was estimated to be 1,329 cases per 100,000 population for non-typhoidal S. enterica, 195 for Shigella, and 2,590 for norovirus. These findings indicate that foodborne infections stemming from these three pathogens present a significant public health burden in the region. The multiplier calculation model used in the present study is basically consistent with previous studies in China [12, 14]. The advantage of adopting a comparable estimation method is that we can roughly compare the results with different regions in China and those of other countries. In general, the estimated incidences of foodborne gastroenteritis caused by these pathogens fall within the range of incidences reported in similar international and domestic studies. Non-typhoidal S. enterica has always been an important cause of foodborne outbreaks in China [23–25]. Our estimated foodborne salmonellosis (1,329 cases per 100,000) was higher when compared with South Korea (56 per 100,000) [26], Japan (199 per 100,000) [10], Canada (269 per 100,000) [27], and the United States (340 per 100,000) [4]. Compared with previous research related to China, the present estimate was higher when compared with Shanghai (48 per 100,000) [12], and the estimate based on data from six provinces (245 per 100,000 ) [14]. Our estimated foodborne shigellosis (195 per 100,000) was higher when compared with South Korea (3 per 100,000) [26], Canada (4 per 100,000) [27], the United States (41 per 100,000) [4], and the estimate based on data from six provinces of China (67 per 100,000) [14]. Our estimated foodborne norovirus gastroenteritis (2,590 per 100,000) was higher when compared with South Korea (5.3 per 100,000) [26], Denmark (575 per 100,000) [28], and the United States (1,690 per 100,000) [4], but lower compared with Canada (3,224 per 100,000) [27].

We estimate that for each reported positive case, there are 1,682 cases of salmonellosis, 1,590 cases of shigellosis, and 2,689 cases of norovirus in the community respectively. This indicates that for each laboratory-confirmed case of the above pathogen a substantial number of infections in the community go undetected. The multiplier for foodborne salmonellosis (1,682) is much higher than that of Denmark (8 per 100,000) [28], Canada (25 per 100,000) [27], the United States (29 per 100,000) [4], and Jordan (273) [11]. Compared with previous research related to China, the multiplier is higher than Guangdong Province (36) and Shanghai (59) [12, 13], but lower than the estimate based on data from six provinces of China (4,137) [14]. The multiplier for foodborne Shigella (1,590) is much higher than Canada (17 per 100,000) [27], the United States (44 per 100,000) [4], Jordan (273) [11], but lower than the estimate based on data from six provinces of China (4,356) [14]. The multiplier for foodborne norovirus (2,689) is higher than the United Kingdom (48.9) [6]. It should be recognized that the uncertainty of this study is relatively high, mainly due to the small number of stool specimens tested by sentinel hospitals. An enhanced sentinel hospital surveillance is needed in Gansu Province, which could be achieved by increasing the number of sentinel hospitals in the surveillance areas and increasing the number of stool specimens tested by sentinel hospitals.

Reliable estimates of the burden of AGE are crucial to endeavors to assess the burden of infection caused by foodborne pathogens [29]. The prevalence of AGE (4.0%) reported in this study is much lower than our previous estimate (8.5%) [16], mainly due to the significantly higher risk in Qingcheng compared to other sites (S1 Table). There is a strong link between climate-related events, agricultural activities and the occurrence of AGE, and Qingcheng’s warm and humid weather is suitable for intensive agricultural activities. Different countries have adopted different methods for investigating the prevalence of AGE in community. Telephone surveys are widely used, as in Australia, Japan, and the United States [9, 10, 30]. The advantage of our study is the use of face-to-face interviews, which may reach potential respondents who cannot use the phone. Compared to other methods that focus on the same issue, the response rate in the present study is as high as 92.1%.

In the present study, the estimated incidence of foodborne salmonellosis, shigellosis and norovirus gastroenteritis substantially exceeded those of reported foodborne disease. This finding, is similar to those reported in Japan, the United States, and China, indicating serious underreporting in passive surveillance and the need to strengthen active surveillance of foodborne diseases based on population surveys and sentinel hospital surveillance [3, 10, 12, 14].

We observed variability in pathogen isolation or detection rates across monitoring years, with fluctuations rather than a clear increasing trend. This variability may be attributed to annual variations in the natural prevalence of pathogens. To ensure robust and stable estimations, data from six consecutive years of sentinel hospital surveillance were utilized.

In sentinel hospital surveillance, stool specimens were obtained from outpatients who sought medical care for AGE. The decision of whether a patient seeks medical care may be the result of a combination of factors (e.g., urban-rural differences), and these factors may affect whether these specimens were representative of community samples [14]. Data on the educational level and rural/urban residence were not collected for surveillance cases.

In China, submitted stool specimens primarily undergo routine analysis for indicators such as red and white blood cells, occult blood, and parasite eggs, with limited specific testing for foodborne pathogens [31]. Estimating the incidence of pathogens in the community by testing stool specimens from a small subset of cases may introduce bias. The rationale for physicians not collecting stool specimens for pathogen testing was not investigated.

The findings in the present study have some other limitations. First, the absence of available data on the test sensitivity for norovirus, which may affect the estimations as regional laboratories may have differences in pathogen testing capabilities. Second, the absence of information on the proportion of foodborne transmission in the region may affect the accuracy of the estimations, as reliance on cited published data from other countries may have introduced biases. Finally, foodborne pathogen infections manifest varying degrees of severity, potentially leading to differences in physician consultations and stool specimen collection practices [32]. This study did not assess the disease burden among cases with distinct levels of severity. If there are more high-risk populations in the community, the disease burden would be higher. Thus, further investigation is imperative to enhance the precision of burden of disease estimates.

Conclusions

We concluded that AGE and foodborne gastroenteritis caused by non-typhoidal S. enteritis, Shigella, and norovirus constitute a significant health burden in Gansu Province. When assessing the impact of these pathogens in the population, it is necessary to recognize the existence of severe underreporting. Such estimates are exceptionally useful for risk ranking and defining interventions to combat hazards in foods and can guide healthcare, food production and agricultural policies at local, national and regional levels. By continuously implementing population survey and enhanced sentinel hospital surveillance (such as expanding the types of pathogen testing, conducting in-depth research on infection outcomes), with constructing a multiplier calculation model to estimate the burden of disease, we may gain a better understanding of the food safety situation in Northwest China. .

JAK inhibitors for the treatment of life-threatening and refractory immune-related adverse events secondary to immune checkpoint inhibitors: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Rami Habib, Wilson H Miller, Theodore Papadopoulos, Sonia V Del Rincon, Claudie Berger, Manuel Flores Molina, Marie Hudson, Khashayar Esfahani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized cancer treatment but can induce immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which are sometimes severe, life-threatening, or refractory to corticosteroids. Current management is largely empirical and adapted from autoimmune disease protocols. Dysregulation of the Janus kinase (JAK)–signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway has been implicated in irAE pathogenesis, positioning JAK inhibitors (JAKi) as potential therapeutic agents. We hypothesized that JAKi may offer therapeutic benefit in treating corticosteroid-refractory or life-threatening irAEs.MethodsWe conducted a prospective cohort study at the Jewish General Hospital (Montreal, Canada) within the Montreal Immune-Related Adverse Events biobank, enrolling patients who developed grade ≥3 or persistent grade 2 irAEs unresponsive to corticosteroids or life-threatening irAEs requiring urgent intervention.Results29 patients received JAKi; 5 were excluded due to death within 30 days of initiation, leaving 24 evaluable patients (median age 69 years; 37.5% female). The majority received anti-programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) monotherapy (79.2%), with some receiving combination anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4/PD-1 therapy (16.7%). Indications for JAKi use included myocarditis (n=8), arthritis (n=4), colitis (n=3), hepatitis (n=3), encephalitis (n=2), pneumonitis (n=2), myasthenia gravis (n=1), and sicca syndrome (n=1). JAKi were employed as second-line therapy in 7 patients, third-line in 11, and fourth-line or later in 6. Clinical response, defined as irAE resolution to grade ≤1 while on ≤10 mg prednisone equivalent without relapse for ≥30 days, was observed in 17 patients (70.8%), with median time to resolution of 41 days. Response rates were highest for myocarditis (75%) and arthritis (100%). No opportunistic infections or JAKi-related hepatotoxicity occurred; thrombotic events were identified in three individuals (10.3%).ConclusionsThese findings suggest that JAKi are a promising, well-tolerated option for managing corticosteroid-refractory or life-threatening irAEs. Further randomized studies are warranted to confirm their efficacy and safety in this setting.Keywords: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Immune related adverse event - irAE, JAK-STAT, Immunotherapy

Introduction

The advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has revolutionized cancer therapy by leveraging the immune system’s ability to recognize and eliminate malignant cells.1 These agents, composed of engineered monoclonal antibodies, disrupt inhibitory immune checkpoints that normally restrain T-cell activity, thereby enhancing antitumor immune responses.2 Despite their transformative impact, ICI can precipitate off-target immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which may involve any organ system. Endocrine and dermatologic irAEs are most prevalent, whereas gastrointestinal, cardiac, and neurologic irAEs are less common but often associated with significant morbidity and mortality.3 Management of irAEs typically involves corticosteroids due to their broad immunosuppressive effects. However, irAEs affecting critical organs such as the myocardium (myocarditis), lungs (pneumonitis), liver (hepatitis), or central nervous system (encephalitis) can be fulminant and life-threatening.4 Furthermore, approximately 20% of irAEs requiring steroids exhibit steroid refractoriness, necessitating escalation to alternative immunosuppressive agents.5 Current management guidelines for such refractory cases rely largely on expert consensus and draw parallels from treatment paradigms in primary autoimmune diseases. For instance, infliximab is frequently employed in corticosteroid-refractory ICI colitis.6,11

The Janus kinase (JAK)–signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway is a pivotal intracellular signaling axis mediating cytokine-driven immune activation, inflammation, and hematopoiesis. Dysregulation of this pathway has been implicated in various autoimmune disorders through amplification of proinflammatory cytokine signaling and persistence of autoreactive immune cells. Emerging evidence suggests that aberrant JAK–STAT signaling may also underlie certain irAEs, raising interest in the therapeutic potential of JAK inhibitors (JAKi) in this context.12 By attenuating cytokine-mediated signaling, JAKi offer a mechanism to broadly suppress excessive immune activation, with additional advantages of rapid onset of action and oral bioavailability.13 14 The current irAE management guidelines do not capture these drugs for the management of irAEs. The American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines from 2021 were published when the only evidence for JAKi in this setting consisted of case reports of colitis management. Since then, there have been publications from French and Chinese groups mostly for the management of ICI-induced myocarditis.15,17

The Montreal Immune-Related Adverse Events (MIRAE) research group is a multidisciplinary consortium of clinicians and scientists focused on elucidating the pathophysiology of irAEs and optimizing their management without compromising ICI efficacy. Through prospective recruitment and longitudinal monitoring of patients receiving ICI, the MIRAE biobank has accrued over 700 participants, approximately 40% of whom have developed grade ≥2 irAEs. Leveraging this cohort, we conducted a study to evaluate the clinical utility of JAKi in managing life-threatening or corticosteroid-refractory irAEs. We hypothesized that JAKi may serve as effective steroid-sparing agents in such cases, facilitating resolution of irAEs while minimizing corticosteroid exposure.

Methods

Study design and setting

We performed a prospective cohort study of patients with cancer treated with ICI at the Jewish General Hospital, Montreal, Canada, who consented to participation in the MIRAE biobank.

Study population

Eligible participants included MIRAE enrollees who developed steroid-refractory or life-threatening irAEs. Inclusion criteria comprised grade ≥3 irAEs (per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) V.5.0) or persistent grade 2 toxicities despite optimal corticosteroid therapy. Patients treated with JAKi for these indications were included, provided they survived at least 30 days after JAKi initiation to permit assessment of response (figure 1). This latter eligibility criteria reflected the primary outcome of the study (defined below).

Exposure and outcomes

The primary exposure was the administration of a JAKi as second-line (or later) therapy in corticosteroid-refractory irAEs or as urgent second-line therapy (initiated within 15 days of corticosteroid commencement) in life-threatening irAEs. The primary outcome was time to permanent irAE resolution, defined a priori as reduction to grade ≤1 (CTCAE V.5.0) while on ≤10 mg prednisone equivalent daily, without relapse for ≥30 days. This period, which encouraged rapid steroid taper, was deemed clinically meaningful to assess relapse.

Covariates and data collection

Collected variables included demographic characteristics, cancer type and stage, ICI regimen, irAE type and severity, prior immunosuppressive therapies, time to initiation and duration of corticosteroids and JAKi, and treatment context (steroid-refractory vs critical illness). Data were abstracted from medical records and entered into a secure database compliant with institutional protocols, with a data freeze on November 15, 2024.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics summarized cohort characteristics overall and stratified by response status. Χ² and Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables as well as Student and Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous variables were used to compare characteristics by responder status. Furthermore, a swimmer’s plot was created to show the individual clinical course of each patient, including the five excluded patients. This was meant to present the data graphically.

Results

A total of 29 patients received JAKi for irAEs, of whom 5 were excluded due to death within 30 days of JAKi initiation. Tofacitinib, a pan-JAKi, was used in 22 patients, upadacitinib (a JAK1 inhibitor) in 1 patient and tofacitinib and upadacitinib sequentially in 1 patient. 18 patients were treated for steroid-refractory irAEs, while 6 required urgent JAKi administration within 15 days of irAE onset due to critical illness. Indications for JAKi therapy included myocarditis (n=8), arthritis (n=4), colitis (n=3), hepatitis (n=3), encephalitis (n=2), pneumonitis (n=2), myasthenia gravis (n=1), and sicca syndrome (n=1) (table 1). JAKi were used as second-line therapy in 7 patients, third-line in 11, and fourth-line or later in 6. The median duration of JAKi therapy was 56 days. Of the 24 evaluable patients, 17 (70.8%) achieved clinical resolution of irAEs, with a median time to resolution of 41 days. Responders included six of eight myocarditis cases, all four arthritis cases, and two of three colitis cases. Median time to JAKi initiation was comparable between responders (45 days, IQR 9–117) and non-responders (40 days, IQR 21–183) (table 1). No opportunistic infections or JAKi-related hepatotoxicity or cardiotoxicity were observed. Thrombotic events occurred in three patients (10.3%) and disease progression was noted in seven patients during a median follow-up of 254 days post-JAKi initiation.

Discussion

Principal findings

The JAK–STAT pathway is integral to immune modulation, mediating cytokine-driven inflammation and the expression of checkpoint molecules such as PD-L1. Dysregulation of this pathway may impair interferon signaling, reduce PD-L1 expression, and compromise the efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors. Conversely, targeted inhibition of JAK–STAT signaling in the setting of irAEs can selectively attenuate pathogenic immune activation without necessarily undermining, and recent trials suggest that they may even enhance the therapeutic benefit of ICIs.12 13

In this prospective study, we observed a 71% clinical response rate among patients with life-threatening or corticosteroid-refractory irAEs treated with JAKi, most notably, tofacitinib. While upadacitinib was also used in two patients, such a small number does not allow for comparative analysis. Response rates were particularly notable in myocarditis (6/8 patients) and arthritis (4/4 patients), supporting the hypothesis that JAK inhibition may offer therapeutic benefit in select irAE phenotypes. Importantly, the safety profile was acceptable, with no observed opportunistic infections and an expected incidence of thrombotic events (10.3%) over a median follow-up of 254 days for a cancer patient population.18 These findings suggest that short-term use of JAKi may be a viable steroid-sparing strategy in severe irAEs.

This study is, to our knowledge, the largest prospective analysis of JAKi across a diverse spectrum of irAEs, providing valuable real-world insights into their use in both refractory and critical clinical scenarios.

Comparison with prior studies

Previous case reports and small series have described favorable outcomes with JAKi in steroid-refractory ICI-induced colitis, arthritis, and myocarditis.1719,23 Our findings corroborate these preliminary observations and extend them to a broader cohort encompassing multiple cancer types and irAE presentations. Furthermore, this study is one of the first to report prospective outcomes from a North American cohort using standardized definitions and systematic follow-up. Our observed response rate of 70.8% aligns with and reinforces earlier data, while the inclusion of life-threatening presentations and multiline immunosuppression adds practical relevance to clinicians managing complex irAE cases. The rapid onset and oral availability of JAKi provide logistical advantages over parenteral agents such as infliximab, intravenous immunoglobulins (IVIG), or rituximab, particularly in outpatient or resource-limited settings.

Mechanistic considerations

The therapeutic efficacy of JAKi in irAEs likely derives from their capacity to suppress multiple proinflammatory cytokines simultaneously. Unlike monoclonal antibodies that target a single pathway, JAKi disrupt downstream signaling from a range of cytokine receptors, including interleukins (eg, IL-6, IL-2), interferons, and granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor, all of which are implicated in irAE pathophysiology.24 25 This broad-spectrum immunomodulation may be particularly beneficial in irAEs, where multiple immune axes may be activated concurrently. In preclinical studies, JAK–STAT inhibition reduced CD8+ T cell activation and limited tissue damage in autoimmune models, supporting its rationale in managing exaggerated immune responses elicited by ICI.26 27 While it was initially thought that JAK inhibition might compromise immune responses, emerging evidence indicates that short-term or pulsed dosing of JAKi may preserve or enhance antitumor immunity while mitigating toxicities, a balance increasingly recognized as critical in the evolving landscape of immuno-oncology.12 13

Clinical implications

Our findings support the consideration of JAKi as a therapeutic option in the management of corticosteroid-refractory or life-threatening irAEs, particularly in cases of myocarditis and arthritis where response rates were high. Given their oral formulation and rapid onset of action, JAKi offer a practical alternative to existing immunosuppressants and may facilitate early hospital discharge or outpatient management.14 28 Moreover, the apparent safety of short-duration JAKi therapy in this cohort, combined with a favorable response profile, suggests that their earlier introduction in selected irAEs may reduce cumulative corticosteroid exposure and associated complications.12 29 Nevertheless, such decisions should be individualized, considering the underlying malignancy, concurrent therapies, and patient comorbidities.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size is small, limiting statistical power and generalizability. Second, the cohort is heterogeneous in terms of cancer types, ICI regimens, irAE phenotypes, and prior immunosuppressive therapies limiting. This heterogeneity limits the generalizability of our results. On the other hand, our results provide preliminary evidence that JAKi may work across cancers/drugs/irAEs. Third, the observational nature of the study introduces the possibility of confounding and selection bias, particularly in the initiation of JAKi. The concurrent use of other immunosuppressive agents (eg, infliximab, IVIG, mycophenolate mofetil) in many patients further complicates the attribution of clinical response solely to JAKi. This limitation especially affects the cases that were “life-threatening” as multiple agents were started simultaneously. This becomes less of a concern for the cases that were “refractory” as agents were added sequentially, and a successful outcome can more easily be attributed to the use of a JAKi. Fourth, the absence of a control group precludes direct comparisons with alternative therapeutic approaches. Additionally, the exclusion of the five patients who passed away within the 30 days of JAKi administration may have resulted in overestimation of the results. The rationale for this exclusion stemmed from our a priori definition of our primary outcome. Had we included all 29 patients, the response rate would have been 17/29 (58.6%), a result that remains clinically meaningful given the patient population included in the study. Finally, the modest follow-up period, with a median of 254 days, limits assessment of long-term safety, durability of response, and potential impact on cancer-related outcomes. Despite these limitations, our study provides hypothesis-generating evidence and lays the groundwork for further investigation of JAKi in this setting.

Future directions

To validate these findings, larger prospective studies, ideally randomized controlled trials, are warranted. Comparative trials assessing JAKi versus other second-line immunosuppressants (eg, infliximab, vedolizumab, rituximab) would provide critical insights into relative efficacy and safety. In addition, future studies should investigate the optimal timing, dosing, and duration of JAKi therapy, particularly in relation to ICI continuation or rechallenge. The integration of translational approaches—such as cytokine profiling, single-cell transcriptomics, and T cell receptor sequencing—may uncover predictive biomarkers of response and elucidate mechanisms of resistance. Ultimately, the development of precision immunosuppression strategies tailored to individual irAE phenotypes and patient-specific immune signatures holds the promise of improving outcomes while minimizing toxicity.

Conclusions

In this prospective cohort study, JAKi demonstrated promising efficacy in the management of corticosteroid-refractory and life-threatening irAEs, with a favorable safety profile over short treatment durations. These findings support the consideration of JAKi, particularly tofacitinib, as a steroid-sparing option in selected patients experiencing severe immune-mediated toxicities from ICI. While preliminary, our results contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting broader applications of JAK inhibition in immuno-oncology. Future randomized trials and mechanistic studies are needed to confirm these findings and refine the role of JAKi in irAE management protocols.

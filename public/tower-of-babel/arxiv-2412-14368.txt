Memorization Over Reasoning? Exposing and Mitigating Verbatim Memorization in Large Language Models’ Character Understanding Evaluation
Yuxuan Jiang, Francis Ferraro

Abstract
Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown impressive performance in various character understanding tasks. However, the extensive pre-training corpora used by LLMs raise concerns that they may rely on memorizing popular fictional works rather than genuinely understanding and reasoning about them. Prior work has addressed memorization in LLM reasoning tasks, but these approaches do not directly apply to character understanding. To address this, we leverage the cognitive science concepts of "gist memory" and "verbatim memory", where verbatim memory corresponds to exact recall, while gist memory captures underlying meaning. We design a hard-soft intervention framework, where the hard setting disrupts memorization by perturbing key entities, while the soft setting maintains the original text but guides the model toward reasoning. Our experiments across six character understanding tasks with GPT-4o, LLaMA3.3-70B, and DeepSeek V3 demonstrate that reducing verbatim memory reliance shifts model behavior toward reasoning. Our method leads to a performance drop of up to 45.8% when models can no longer depend on memorization, highlighting the extent to which existing benchmarks inflate performance through data contamination. Additionally, we show that prompting techniques can systematically alter a model’s response strategy, making it more dependent on either memorization or reasoning.

To truly stimulate a model’s character understanding ability, it is crucial to understand how the model may be generating its responses. This can be especially challenging given the extensive and opaque pre-training corpora used by LLMs, making it particularly severe for character understanding tasks, as many benchmarks are derived from well-known and widely consumed fiction or scripts

Figure 1:
This figure illustrates the distinct mechanisms of gist and verbatim memory processes in the speaker-guessing task for a script. Verbatim memory (bottom right) aims to recall as much specific information as possible, including who said each sentence. If the memorization is not precise enough, the model fails to provide the correct answer. In contrast, gist memory (top right) focuses on extracting core information, such as character relationships, personalities, and key events. By relying on gist memory, the model is encouraged to engage in reasoning rather than purely relying on memorization.

Motivated by the concept of gist memory, we propose a prompt-based approach to mitigate model reliance on verbatim memorization in character understanding tasks. We evaluate our method on GPT-4o, LLaMA3-70B, and DeepSeek V3, introducing a hard-soft setting framework: the hard setting blocks memorization by replacing character names, while the soft setting retains the original text but employs gist-based prompts to encourage reasoning over recall. Our ablation study confirms the effectiveness of both interventions.

Building on these findings, we extend our approach to five character understanding tasks, designing task-specific gist prompts and evaluating models under both settings. Our results indicate that the Gist+NR approach best reduces memorization dependency.

In summary, we outline our study and key contributions as follows:

1.

Character understanding tasks heavily rely on verbatim memorization.

2.

Gist-based prompting effectively reduces memorization dependency.
Introducing gist-based prompts systematically lowers reliance on verbatim recall across all tasks, encouraging models to use reasoning instead of mechanical memorization (Section

3.

Verbatim and gist-based prompting modulate model response strategies.
Models adjust their response strategies based on the prompting style—verbatim-based prompts reinforce reliance on memorization, while gist-based prompts encourage reasoning-driven answers (Section

2.3
Verbatim and Gist Memory in LLM Reasoning

Cognitive science distinguishes between verbatim memory, which retains exact details, and gist memory, which captures the essential meaning
Brainerd and Reyna 2002
;
Reyna and Brainerd 1998
. For example, verbatim memory would store a sentence like "The black-and-white cat sat on a soft, red mat in the living room" with all details intact, while gist memory would reduce it to "A cat was sitting indoors," focusing on the core idea while discarding specifics. Human reasoning primarily relies on gist memory, as the two types of memory function independently rather than hierarchically
Brainerd and Reyna 1992
.

In LLM research, memorization is often equated with verbatim memory, where models retrieve and reproduce exact strings from pretraining data
Carlini et al. 2023
. A common mitigation strategy involves curating test samples that the model has never seen before
Xie et al. 2024a
. However, in character understanding tasks, acquiring obscure or newly released datasets can be costly and difficult to control in terms of quality. Instead, our work proposes leveraging gist memory to reduce reliance on precise memorization, encouraging LLMs to generalize character understanding beyond string-matching. By shifting the focus toward gist memory, we aim to align LLM character analysis with human-like reasoning rather than dataset recall.

4
Soft Setting: Memorization-Controlled Prompting in Character Guessing

First, we investigate whether prompting strategies can subtly influence LLMs’ response patterns in a soft setting, where the model is not explicitly restricted from using memorization but is instead encouraged to rely more on reasoning over direct retrieval.

4.1
Character Guessing Task Format

Let a dialogue segment be represented as
D=\{U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{n}\}
, where each utterance
U_{i}
is paired with a speaker label
S_{i}
. In the Character Guessing Task, the model is given a dialogue excerpt without speaker annotations and must predict the correct speaker for each utterance,
f_{\text{guess}}(U_{i})\rightarrow S_{i}
. We follow the original format and setting
Sang et al. 2022b
, using the TVShow Guess Dataset described in Section
3.1
. This task is designed to prompt the model to make guesses based on character relationships, key events, and personality traits within the text. However, its format closely resembles the name cloze tests
Chang et al. 2023b
recently used to evaluate LLM memorization.

4.2
Prompting Strategies

We design two distinct prompting conditions to examine how LLMs rely on different memory mechanisms when identifying characters.
Verbatim prompting encourages mechanical memorization, where the model recalls exact substrings from its training data and reconstructs missing parts without reasoning. In contrast, gist prompting focuses on extracting key information and performing memory-based matching. For character understanding tasks, the most relevant gist cues typically include character relationships, significant events, and personality traits.

All prompts share the same base instruction:
"The following is a dialogue. Please identify who they are. …"

The two variations are as follows:

Verbatim Prompt:

"…Do this by direct memory retrieval—do not infer based on traits, interactions, or context. Recall names exactly from past dialogue knowledge without explanations or reasoning."

Gist Prompt:

"…Do this by analyzing relationships, key events, and personality traits to match them with known characters. Do not directly retrieve from memory who said the sentences."

5
Hard Setting: Memorization-Controlled Prompting under Name Replacement

Furthermore, we aim to validate whether our prompting strategies can genuinely alter the model’s reasoning patterns under a hard setting, where memorization is explicitly restricted. Previous research has suggested that name replacement may impact model performance
Yu et al. 2022
. In this section, we confirm this hypothesis by demonstrating that name replacement significantly affects the model’s memorization ability and quantifying its impact. Then, we examine how prompting strategies influence LLMs’ reliance on memorization versus reasoning when memorization is constrained by name replacement.

5.1
Name Replacement Strategies and Their Impact on Memorization

Name Replacement Strategies

Previous studies
Yu et al. 2022
have primarily employed Same-Cultural Name Replacement, a naive and intuitive approach that substitutes character names with culturally consistent alternatives. To systematically analyze the impact of name replacement on model performance, we introduce three distinct levels of name modifications:

•

Name Masking:
Replacing all character names with placeholders (e.g.,
Sheldon
→ P0,
Amy
→ P1).

•

Cross-Cultural Name Replacement:
Substituting names with those from a different cultural background while maintaining gender consistency (e.g.,
Sheldon
→
Jie Zhang
,
Amy
→
Xiaoling Wang
). This tests whether models rely on specific name distributions for character inference.

•

Same-Cultural Name Replacement:
Replacing names with other culturally consistent alternatives (e.g.,
Sheldon
→
Andrew
,
Amy
→
Sally
). This retains cultural familiarity while disrupting direct memorization.

Memorization Evaluation Task Format

We design the Source Identification Task where the model is given a dialogue excerpt without source annotations and must predict the correct origin of the text. Let a dialogue segment be represented as
D=\{U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{n}\}
, where each utterance
U_{i}
is part of a larger script from a specific source. The task requires the model to determine the correct source,
f_{\text{source}}(D)\rightarrow S
, where
S
denotes the source work. We use the

described in Section
3.2
.

Impact of Name Replacement on Memorization.

According to Figure

Model performance aligns closely with memorization levels, indicating a strong dependence on memorization for accurate predictions.

We conclude that combining Cross-Culture Name Replacement with Gist prompting provides a reasonable and effective method for evaluating a model’s true capabilities while minimizing reliance on memorization.

7
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce the concept of gist memory and design prompts for six types of character understanding tasks, enabling models to reduce reliance on memorization and enhance reasoning-based character interpretation. Our approach provides a systematic framework to evaluate the extent to which different character understanding tasks depend on memorization, offering a more accurate assessment of model performance by disentangling genuine reasoning from memorization-driven responses. These findings highlight the limitations of current benchmarks and provide insights for designing more robust character understanding evaluations.

8
Limitations

The specific training data of GPT-4o is not publicly disclosed by OpenAI, making it fundamentally unknowable. Our work employs probabilistic inference to estimate the model’s familiarity with a set of fictional works. However, whether these works are explicitly included in the training data remains unanswerable. Nevertheless, even mere familiarity can suffice to contaminate test data, posing challenges to fair evaluation.

Our study can be extended by future research in a number of ways. First, though we ground and motivate our work through “verbatim” and “gist” memory, we note that any apparent “reasoning” of AI systems may differ fundamentally from those of humans, and we did not pursue human studies to examine this in detail. Further investigation is needed to explore this topic.

Second, the relationship between memorization and reasoning remains a subject of debate in cognitive science. Future studies may uncover insights into this relationship from entirely new perspectives.

Finally, our study was centered on select, recent (but not contemporaneous) majority Western popular culture references. Given their popularity, this selection likely increases the chances that LLMs have been pretrained on more instances or language discussing those references (vs. contemporaneous references, or references from non-Western cultures, where overall occurrences in training data may be lower); it is possible that this exaggerated some of the effects we observed.

9
Ethics

Our study probes the degree to which GPT-4o have memorized fictional works, and its impact on character understanding tasks. This work uses the OpenAI API on experiments above, and at no point do we access, or attempt to access, the true training data behind these models, or any underlying components of the systems.

Risks

The several datasets and fictional works scripts in our experiment are sourced from publicly available sources. However, we cannot guarantee that they are devoid of socially harmful or toxic language. We use ChatGPT

Appendix F
Verbatim avoid Reasoning

Figure 6:
In a personality understanding task where the scenario involves ’Joey returned the lost wallet to its rightful owner, Monica,’ the question posed might be, ’According to this action, his personality should be: ?’ The human reasoning process would draw on contextual evidence, concluding that returning lost property demonstrates honesty. In contrast, the LLM might conclude, ’Joey and Monica are from the TV show Friends, and Joey is characterized as an honest character in this show,’ thus relying heavily on memorized information rather than genuine reasoning.

•

Personality Understanding
: Using the PERSONET dataset, which comprises approximately 32,000 samples from 33 classic literature books
Yu et al. 2023b
, this task involves predicting the most likely personality trait depicted in a given text snippet, along with its preceding context. Formulated as a multi-choice problem with distractor traits, this task evaluates the model’s ability to infer character personalities from descriptive text.

•

CSI Role Detection
: Based on 39 episodes of the TV series
CSI

Frermann et al. 2018
, this sequence labeling task requires the model to identify mentions of the perpetrator in dialogues and scene descriptions, focusing on role detection within crime-related narratives.

•

FriendsQA
: Derived from 1,222 scenes from the first four seasons of
Friends

Yang and Choi 2019
, this dataset supports an open-domain question answering task. The model is given a dialogue excerpt and a manually crafted question and must extract the specific span in the dialogue that answers the question, testing its comprehension of dialogue context.

•

SUMMSCREEN
: Built from transcript-recap pairs of 91 TV series collected from community sources such as The TV MegaSite, Inc. (TMS) and ForeverDreaming (FD)
Chen et al. 2022
, this dataset supports a summarization task. The model is required to generate a coherent summary from dialogue utterances and scene descriptions, capturing key plot points while excluding irrelevant details.

Appendix H
Gist Prompting Strategies Across Tasks

We design
Gist Prompting
methods to reduce reliance on memorization and promote inference-based character understanding. Below, we detail the task-specific prompt formulations.

H.1
Task-Specific Gist Prompts

Character Guessing

"Analyze the dialogue and infer the most likely speaker based on their speech patterns, personality traits, and relationships with other characters. Do not rely on direct recall of specific names but rather use reasoning from the provided context."

Coreference Resolution

"Identify which mentions in the text refer to the same character by analyzing linguistic patterns, contextual cues, and discourse coherence. Do not rely on pre-learned entity associations but rather resolve coreference based on narrative structure."

Personality Understanding

"Based on the character’s dialogue and actions, infer their personality traits. Focus on how they interact with others, their tone, and behavioral tendencies. Avoid using prior memorized descriptions of characters and derive insights purely from the given passage."

Role Detection

"Determine whether a character plays a key role in the narrative (e.g., antagonist, victim, or protagonist) by analyzing their function within the story. Identify how their actions and relationships define their role rather than relying on predefined labels."

Open-Domain QA

"Answer the question using only the given passage. Identify relevant information by reasoning over the text and analyzing interactions between characters. Do not rely on external knowledge or prior memorized facts."

Summarization

"Summarize the passage by identifying key events, character interactions, and narrative progression. Construct a coherent summary that captures the essence of the scene without directly copying text segments."

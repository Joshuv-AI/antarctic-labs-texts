Non-divergence evolution operators modeled on Hörmander vector fields with Dini continuous coefficients
Matteo Faini

Abstract
In this paper we analyze operators H = ∂ t − ∑ i , j a i ​ j ( x , t ) X i X j H=\partial_{t}-\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x,t)X_{i}X_{j} , where the X i X_{i} ’s are Hörmander vector fields generating a Carnot group and A = [ a i ​ j ] A=[a_{ij}] is a symmetric and uniformly positive-definite matrix whose entries satisfy double Dini continuity, a strictly weaker condition than Hölder continuity. For these operators, we build a fundamental solution and show a two-sided Gaussian estimate for the latter, as well as upper Gaussian estimates for its derivatives up to weight 2 2 . As a consequence, we prove an existence result for the related Cauchy problem, under a Dini-type condition on the source.

Introduction

In this paper we build a fundamental solution
\Gamma
for nonvariational evolution operators of the (degenerate) form

H=\partial_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}(x,t)\,X_{i}X_{j}=\partial_{t}-a^{ij}(x,t)\,X_{i}X_{j},

(1.1)

where
X_{1},...,X_{m}
are (smooth) Hörmander vector fields generating a Carnot group in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n>m
) and the matrix
[a_{ij}(x,t)]
is symmetric and uniformly positive definite (in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
), with double Dini continuous entries in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(precise definitions are given below); this result is obtained through an adaptation of the classical parametrix method. We further show that
\Gamma
, together with its derivatives (up to weight
2
) along the vector fields appearing in
H
, satisfy Gaussian estimates similar to those enjoyed by the fundamental solutions of constant coefficients’ heat operators; these yield also a reproduction formula for
\Gamma
and existence of classical solutions for the Cauchy problem.

We follow a procedure known in literature as
parametrix method
, firstly developed by E. E. Levi in
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
to construct fundamental solutions of uniformly elliptic operators with Hölder-continuous coefficients; later this procedure has been adapted by Friedman to obtain existence results in the context of uniformly parabolic operators (under the same regularity assumption on the coefficients), as discussed in
[
13
]
.

In the last decades, several important extensions of the parabolic version of
Levi’s method have been developed. In
[
19
]
, Polidoro constructed a
fundamental solution for ultraparabolic operators of Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck
type with Hölder continuous coefficients, exploiting the knowldedge of an
explicit fundamental solution for the model operator with constant
coefficients, built by Lanconelli and Polidoro in
[
15
]
. In
[
3
]

Bonfiglioli, Lanconelli and Uguzzoni extended the applicability of the
procedure to nonvariational degenerate parabolic operators, still with
Hölder continuous coefficients, structured on Hörmander vector fields
on Carnot groups. This was the first case when Levi method was implemented
in a situation where the fundamental solution for the model operator with
constant coefficients was not explicitly known. In
[
10
]
Bramanti, Brandolini,
Lanconelli, Uguzzoni built a fundamental solution for similar operators built
with general Hörmander vector fields (without any underlying structure of
translations and dilations), with local results. In
[
2
]
, Biagi and
Bramanti generalized the results in
[
10
]
again to similar operators built on
homogeneous Hörmander vector fields (without any underlying group
structure of translations), with global results. A different type of
generalization of the parabolic parametrix method is related to the weakening
of the regularity assumptions on the leading coefficients. In
[
18
]
, Lucertini,
Pagliarani and Pascucci built a fundamental solution for
Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck operators of the kind studied in
[
15
]
, but with
coefficients
a_{ij}\left(x,t\right)
Hölder continuous in space and
only bounded measurable in time. In this case the model operator is the KFP
operator with coefficients
a_{ij}\left(t\right)
only depending on time,
in a bounded measurable way; for these operators, an explicit fundamental
solution has been previously built in
[
9
]
. Recently, in
[
12
]
, Zhenyakova
and Cherepova adapted the parabolic parametrix method to uniformly parabolic
operators with double Dini continuous coefficients. In the present paper, we
combine the extension made in
[
3
]
about the operator class with the
generalization in
[
12
]
about the coefficients’ regularity, in order to prove
the existence of a fundamental solution for
H
and the relative Gaussian
estimates under these weaker assumptions on the considered differential operator.

In order to state in detail our assumptions, we briefly recall some notions. We call
Carnot group
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
an algebraic structure
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
with:

(i)

\circ
Lie group law on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(that is, a group law such that both the “translation”
(x,y)\mapsto x\circ y
and the inversion
x\mapsto x^{-1}
are smooth maps);

(ii)

\{\delta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
family of Lie group automorphisms of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ)
(“dilations”) acting as
\delta_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}x_{1},...,\lambda^{\sigma_{n}}x_{n})
, for integers
1=\sigma_{1}\leq...\leq\sigma_{n}
;

(iii)

the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
of left-invariant smooth vector fields on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ)
stratified as
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{2}\oplus...\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{s}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
is spanned by a set of
1
-homogeneous vector fields,
\mathfrak{a}_{k}=[\mathfrak{a_{1}},\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}]
for all
2\leq k\leq s
and
[\mathfrak{a}_{s},\mathfrak{a}_{1}]=0
.

The vector fields spanning
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
are called
(Lie) generators
of
\mathbb{G}
, whereas the number
s
in (iii) is the
step
of
\mathbb{G}
and stands for the length of commutators of the vector fields in
\textbf{X}=\{X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{m}\}
that are needed to fulfill Hörmander condition at any point.

According to this notation, our first assumption is:

(H1)

X_{1},...,X_{m}
are smooth vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, agreeing with the partial derivatives
\partial_{x_{1}},...,\partial_{x_{m}}
(respectively) in the origin and generating a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
, with

\delta_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}x_{1},...,\lambda^{\sigma_{n}}x_{n})\quad\text{for integers}\>1=\sigma_{1}\leq...\leq\sigma_{n}.

Let
||\cdot||
denote an
homogeneous norm
on
\mathbb{G}
, namely a continuous function
||\cdot||:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
with the following properties:

(i)

||x||=0
if and only if
x=0
;

(ii)

||\delta_{\lambda}(x)||=\lambda||x||
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\lambda>0
;

(iii)

there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that
||x||\leq c||x^{-1}||
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(iv)

there exists a constant
\kappa\geq 1
such that
||x\circ y||\leq\kappa(||x||+||y||)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We introduce also the induced quasisymmetric quasidistance
d:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
,
d(x,y):=||y^{-1}\circ x||
, which inherits from
||\cdot||
the properties

(i’)

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
;

(ii’)

d(\delta_{\lambda}(x),\delta_{\lambda}(y))=\lambda d(x,y)
for every
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\lambda>0
;

(iii’)

d(x,y)\leq cd(y,x)
for a constant
c\geq 1
(
quasi-symmetry
);

(iv’)

d(x,z)\leq\kappa(d(x,y)+d(y,z))
for every
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\kappa\geq 1
(
quasi-triangle inequality
);

analogous results hold (in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
) for the associated “parabolic” quasidistance

\displaystyle d_{p}((x,t),(y,s)):=d(x,y)+\sqrt{|t-s|},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\>t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, we define the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
as

Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i},

recalling also its relation with volume growth in
\mathbb{G}
:

x^{\prime}=\delta_{\lambda}(x)\implies dx^{\prime}=\lambda^{Q}dx

(see e.g.
[
7
]
); we finally set

E(x,t):=t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{||x||^{2}}{t}\right\}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,

(1.2)

which in the Euclidean context, i.e. when
Q=n
, is simply the fundamental solution of the classical heat operator (up to scaling constants).

Let us now turn to the assumptions on the coefficients
a_{ij}
.

We call
modulus of continuity
a continuous non-decreasing function
\omega:\mathbb{R^{+}}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\omega(0)=0
; we say that the latter satisfies the
Dini condition
if

\widetilde{\omega}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\,dx<+\infty\quad\forall r>0

(1.3)

and the
double Dini condition
if moreover

\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(x)}{x}\,dx=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{dy}{y}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\,dx<+\infty\quad\forall r>0.

(1.4)

Note that, given a uniformly continuous function
f
in a set
\Omega
, the map

\omega_{f}(r):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega:\,d(x,y)\leq r}|f(x)-f(y)|,\quad r\in[0,+\infty)

is precisely a modulus of continuity; such a function
f
is said to be
Dini continuous
(in
\Omega
) if
\omega_{f}
satisfies (
1.3
), and
double Dini continuous
if
\omega_{f}
satisfies also (
1.4
). Instead,
f
is said to be
locally Dini continuous
if it is Dini continuous on every compact subset of
\Omega
, and analogously for double Dini continuity.

Given
\Lambda>1
and introduced the set of symmetric constant matrices

\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}:=\{B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}:B=B^{T},\,\Lambda^{-1}|v|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}b_{ij}v_{i}v_{j}\leq\Lambda|v|^{2}\hskip 5.69054pt\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\},

we assume that:

(H2)

A(z)=[a_{ij}(z)]_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\in\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
for every
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and

\displaystyle|a_{ij}(z)-a_{ij}(z^{\prime})|\leq\omega(d_{p}(z,z^{\prime}))\quad\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(1.5)

where
\omega
is a modulus of continuity satisfying the double Dini condition (
1.4
) and, for suitable constants
c>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the inequality:

r_{2}^{-\delta}\,\omega(r_{2})\leq c\,r_{1}^{-\delta}\,\omega(r_{1})\quad\forall r_{2}\geq r_{1}>0;

(1.6)

(H3)

the modulus of continuity
\omega
is such that

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega^{1/2}(x)}{x}\,dx<+\infty\quad\forall r\geq 0.

(1.7)

Note that, in particular, (H2) implies that the coefficients
a_{ij}
are
double Dini continuous
over
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
w.r.t. the parabolic quasi-distance
d_{p}
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that
A(z)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}
implies
|a_{ij}(z)|\leq\Lambda
, hence

|a_{ij}(z)-a_{ij}(z^{\prime})|\leq min\{\omega(d_{p}(z,z^{\prime})),2\Lambda\}=\omega^{\prime}(d_{p}(z,z^{\prime}))

where
\omega^{\prime}(r):=min\{\omega(r),2\Lambda\}
is a modulus of continuity satisfying (
1.4
) and (
1.6
); thus we can assume w.l.o.g. that

\omega(r)\leq 2\Lambda\quad\forall{r\geq 0}.

(1.8)

Let us make a comment about the relation between the “Dini assumptions” (H2)-(H3) and usual requirement of Hölder-continuity for
a_{ij}
:

Remark 1.1
.

Hölder continuity trivially implies double Dini continuity (as well as (
1.6
)-(
1.7
)), but the converse is not true: e.g., for any
\alpha>2
, the function
f_{\alpha}(x,t):=\omega_{\alpha}(||x||+\sqrt{|t|})
with

\omega_{\alpha}(r):=\begin{cases}0\hskip 56.9055pt\text{if}\>r=0\\
|log(r)|^{-\alpha}\hskip 17.07182pt\text{if}\>0<r<\frac{1}{2}\\
|log(\frac{1}{2})|^{-\alpha}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if}\>r\geq\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

is double Dini continuous on
\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}
, but not Hölder continuous therein. For this reason, this paper is a generalization of analogous results discussed in
[
3
]
.

(v)

2
Notation and preliminaries

In this section we introduce basic notions and useful lemmas about Carnot groups, as well as convenient results for moduli of continuity satisfying (H2).

2.1
Carnot groups

As a starting point, we recall the “natural” metric structure of Carnot groups. If
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
is a Carnot group with Lie generators
\textbf{X}=\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\}
, then it can be endowed with a distance
d_{X}
induced by the latter; this is called
Carnot-Caratheodory distance
(or
control distance
) and is defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Given
X_{1},...,X_{m}
smooth Hörmander vector fields on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ)
, for any
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta>0
let
C_{x,y}(\delta)
denote the set of absolutely continuous curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=x,\,\gamma(1)=y
and

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(t)=\alpha^{i}(t)\,(X_{i})_{\gamma(t)}\quad\text{a.e. in}\>(0,1),\quad||\alpha_{i}||_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq\delta\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq m;

we call
Carnot-Caratheodory
(or
CC
, in short)
distance
induced by
X_{1},...,X_{m}
the function
d_{X}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty),\>d_{X}(x,y)=\inf\{\delta>0:C_{x,y}(\delta)\neq\emptyset\}
.

It is well known (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.45]
) that
d_{X}
is indeed a distance on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(in particular, any couple of points
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be joined by a curve
\gamma
as above) and it is compatible with translations and dilations of
\mathbb{G}
, namely:

\displaystyle d_{X}(z\circ x,z\circ y)=d_{X}(x,y)\quad\forall x,\,y,\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};

\displaystyle d_{X}(\delta_{\lambda}(x),\delta_{\lambda}(y))=\lambda d_{X}(x,y)\quad\forall x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\lambda>0.

The latter implies
d_{X}
is induced by an homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{G}
, hence it is equivalent to the homogeneous quasidistance
d
introduced in Section
1
(see e.g.
[
7
, Proposition 3.12]
).

Let us also define a regularity class of functions induced by the structure of
\mathbb{G}
; to help clarity of the definition below, we recall that the
Lie derivative
along a smooth vector field
X
of a smooth function
u
at a point
x_{0}
is defined as

\displaystyle Xu(x_{0})=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{u(x_{0}\circ\mu_{X}(h))-u(x_{0})}{h},

(if the limit exists), being
\mu_{X}
the solution of the Cauchy problem

\dot{\psi}(t)=X_{\psi(t)},\quad\psi(0)=0.

(which is well defined, at least locally, by Cauchy theorem). From a geometrical viewpoint,
\mu_{X}
is the integral curve of the vector field
X
passing from
x_{0}
.

Definition 2.2

(The class
\mathfrak{C}^{2}
; see
[
3
]
, Definition 2.6)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set. We denote by
\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\Omega)
the class of functions
u(x,t)
defined on
\Omega
which are continuous in
\Omega
w.r.t. the pair
(x,t)
and such that
u(\cdot,t)
has continuous Lie derivatives up to second order along the vector fields
X_{1},...,X_{m}
(w.r.t.
x
, for every fixed
t
) and
u(x,\cdot)
has continuous derivative (w.r.t.
t
, for every fixed
x
), in their respective domains of definition.

We conclude this overview with the following sort of “Lagrange theorem” in Carnot groups (see
[
4
, Lemma 7.6]
):

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that

|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq c\,d(x_{1},x_{2})\,\sup_{d(x_{1},y)\leq c\,d(x_{1},x_{2})}\,(|X_{1}u(y)|+...+|X_{m}u(y)|)

for every
x_{1},\,x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every function
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.2
Dini continuity

Let us now briefly deal with some useful properties of (double) Dini moduli of continuity, starting from the following basic remark:

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\omega
be a modulus of continuity which satisfies (
1.4
). Then also
r\mapsto\omega(\sqrt{r})
and
r\mapsto\omega(cr)
(for any
c>0
) are moduli of continuity satisfying (
1.4
), and there hold:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(cx)}{x}\,dx=\int_{0}^{cr}\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\,dx=\,\widetilde{\omega}(cr);\quad\int_{0}^{r}\frac{dy}{y}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\omega(cx)}{x}\,dx=\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}(cr);

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(\sqrt{x})}{x}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r}}\frac{2\omega(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho=2\widetilde{\omega}(\sqrt{r});\quad\int_{0}^{r}\frac{dy}{y}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\omega(\sqrt{x})}{x}\,dx=4\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}(\sqrt{r}).

If, in addition,
\omega
satisfies also (
1.6
) for some constant
\delta>0
, then

\widetilde{\omega}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\,dx\geq C\,r^{-\delta}\,\omega(r)\int_{0}^{r}x^{\delta-1}\,dx=C\,\delta^{-1}\,\omega(r)\quad\forall r\geq 0,

(2.1)

namely
\omega
can be bounded from above by
\widetilde{\omega}
(up to a constant).

We now turn to two less trivial results:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\omega
satisfy (
1.4
)-(
1.6
), let
c_{0}>0
be a constant. Then for any
\beta>0
there exist constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\beta)>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that the estimates

\displaystyle\omega^{\beta}(d(x,\xi))\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq c_{1}\omega^{\beta}(\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,2c_{0}(t-\tau));

(2.2)

\displaystyle\omega(d(x,\xi)+\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq c_{2}\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,2c_{0}(t-\tau))

(2.3)

hold true for every
x,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>\tau
.

Proof.

For every
0<s\leq r
we have
\omega(r)\leq C(r/s)^{\delta}\omega(s)
by (
1.6
), therefore

\displaystyle\omega^{\beta}(r)\,\exp\left\{-\frac{r^{2}}{c_{0}s^{2}}\right\}\leq C^{\beta}\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\beta\,\delta}\omega^{\beta}(s)\,\exp\left\{-\frac{r^{2}}{c_{0}s^{2}}\right\}.

Since the map
t\mapsto t^{\epsilon}\,e^{-kt}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(for any
\epsilon>0,k>0
), we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\beta\delta}\exp\left\{-\frac{r^{2}}{2c_{0}s^{2}}\right\}=\left(\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\right)^{\beta\delta/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2c_{0}}\,\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\right\}\leq c(\beta).

so that, recalling (
1.2
), we deduce that for every
0<s\leq r
there holds

\omega^{\beta}(r)\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq c_{1}(\beta)\,\omega^{\beta}(s)\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,2c_{0}(t-\tau))

By monotonicity of
\omega
the above inequality holds also if
0\leq r<s
; setting
r=d(x,\xi)
and
s=\sqrt{t-\tau}
we immediately get (
2.2
).
Furthermore, by monotonicity of
\omega
, in the case
d(x,\xi)\leq\sqrt{t-\tau}
we get

\displaystyle\omega(d(x,\xi)+\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau)),

i.e. (
2.3
) holds, whereas if
d(x,\xi)>\sqrt{t-\tau}
we find

\displaystyle\omega(d(x,\xi)+\sqrt{t-\tau})\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq\omega(2d(x,\xi))\,E(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau)).

and (
2.3
) is achieved by exploiting (
2.2
) with
\beta=1
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\omega
satisfy (
1.4
). For any
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and for any

it holds

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\omega^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(x)}{x}\,dx<+\infty

For the proof see
[
1
]
, where the same proof is carried out under the further assumption that

is subadditive (which, however, does not play a role in this result).

Remark 2.7
.

In view of the above result, the extra assumption (H3) is “almost harmless”, and it is actually satisfied by the simplest example of modulus of continuity which satisfies (
1.4
) without being of Hölder type (that is, the one in Remark
1.1
), hence this condition does not restrict our framework to the one treated in
[

]
.

3
Construction of the fundamental solution

3.1
General idea of the parametrix method

As mentioned in Notation
1.2
, we may denote points of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by
z=(x,t)
,
\zeta=(\xi,\tau)
and
\chi=(y,\eta)
; until not specified otherwise, we assume
\tau<\eta<t
.
For fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we consider the constant coefficients’ operator

H_{\zeta}:=\partial_{t}-a^{ij}(\zeta)\,X_{i}X_{j}

obtained from
H
by “freezing” the coefficients
a_{ij}
in the point
\zeta
. A deep theory about the fundamental solution of
H_{\zeta}
(typically called
parametrix
for
H
) has already been developed and is summarized in the following two theorems:

Theorem 3.1

(The parametrix and its properties)
.

For any fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the operator
H_{\zeta}
admits a fundamental solution
\Gamma_{\zeta}
given by
\Gamma_{\zeta}(x,t;y,s)=\gamma_{\zeta}(y^{-1}\circ x,t-s)
, with
\gamma_{\zeta}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
non-negative smooth function such that:

(i)

\gamma(x,t)=0
if and only if
t\leq 0
, and
\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)\to 0
as
||x||+\sqrt{|t|}\to+\infty
;

(ii)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)\,dx=1\quad\quad\forall t>0;

(3.1)

(iii)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\,\tau>0
we have the
reproduction property

\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t+\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{\zeta}(y^{-1}\circ x,t)\,\gamma_{\zeta}(y,\tau)\,dy;

(3.2)

(iv)

the function
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\cdot;\zeta)
is locally integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and there holds

H_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta}(\cdot;\zeta)=\delta_{\zeta}\quad\text{in}\>D^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1});

(v)

for any continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with

|g(x)|\leq Me^{\nu||x||^{2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(for some
\nu>0,\,M>0
), there exists
T>0
such that the function

u(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma_{\zeta}(z;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy

is well defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
and solves the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}H_{\zeta}\,u=0\hskip 32.72049pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)\\
u(\cdot,0)=g\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

For the proof see
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.

In the theorem below, for any nonnegative integers
h,k
we denote by
D_{h,k}
a differential monomial
X_{i_{1}}...X_{i_{h}}\partial_{t}^{k}
, with indices
i_{1},...,i_{h}\in\{1,...,m\}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Gaussian estimates for the parametrix)
.

For any nonnegative integers
h,k
and points
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\zeta,\,\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle\overline{c}_{0}^{-1}\,E(x,\overline{c}_{0}^{-1}t)\leq\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)\leq\overline{c}_{0}\,E(x,\overline{c}_{0}t);

(3.3)

\displaystyle|D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)|\leq\overline{c}_{h+2k}\cdot t^{-\frac{h}{2}-k}\,E(x,\overline{c}_{h+2k}\cdot t);

(3.4)

\displaystyle|D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)-D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta^{\prime}}(x,t)|\leq\overline{c}_{h+2k}\,\omega(d_{p}(\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}))\,t^{-\frac{h}{2}-k}\,E(x,\overline{c}_{h+2k}\cdot t),

(3.5)

where
\{\overline{c}_{q}\}_{q\geq 0}
are positive constants depending only on
\Lambda,\delta
and
\mathbb{G}
.

For the proof of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) see
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
, whereas (
3.5
) follows by assumption (H2) in view of the following result by Bramanti and Fanciullo:

\displaystyle|X_{i_{1}}...X_{i_{p}}\partial_{t}^{q}\,\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)-X_{i_{1}}...X_{i_{p}}\partial_{t}^{q}\,\gamma_{\zeta^{\prime}}(x,t)|\leq

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(p,q)\,||A(\zeta)-A(\zeta^{\prime})||_{*}\,(t-\tau)^{-\frac{p}{2}-q}\,E(x,c^{\prime}t)

with
||\cdot||_{*}
denoting the usual matrix norm (see
[
8
, Proposition 6.2]
).

We point out that (
3.3
) actually means that the “heat kernel”
E(x,t)
and the parametrix
\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)
are interchangeable (for any fixed
\zeta
), up to fixed constants;
thus, denoted by
\gamma_{0}
the parametrix with pole at
\zeta=0
(i.e., the function satisfying
H\gamma_{0}=\delta_{0}
in distributional sense), we can express (
2.2
)-(
2.3
)-(
3.4
)-(
3.5
) in terms of
\gamma_{0}
instead of

, namely (for
x,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

):

\displaystyle\omega^{\beta}(d(x,\xi))\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq c_{1}\omega^{\beta}(\sqrt{t-\tau})\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,2c_{0}(t-\tau));

(3.6)

\displaystyle\omega(d_{p}((x,t),(\xi,\tau))\,\gamma(\xi^{-1}\circ x,c_{0}(t-\tau))\leq c_{2}\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,2c_{0}(t-\tau));

(3.7)

\displaystyle|D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)|\leq\check{c}_{h+2k}\,t^{-\frac{h}{2}-k}\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{h+2k}\cdot t);

(3.8)

\displaystyle|D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta}(x,t)-D^{h,k}\gamma_{\zeta^{\prime}}(x,t)|\leq

\displaystyle\quad\leq\check{c}_{h+2k}\,\omega(d_{p}(\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}))\,t^{-\frac{h}{2}-k}\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{h+2k}\cdot t).

(3.9)

for constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\beta)
,
c_{2}>0
and
\{\check{c}_{q}\}_{q}=\overline{c}_{0}\{\overline{c}_{q}\}_{q}
which depend only on our data (that is
\Lambda,\delta
and the structure of
\mathbb{G}
). Moreover, since for all
c_{2}>c_{1}>0

\displaystyle E(x,c_{1}t)\leq(c_{1}t)^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{||x||^{2}}{c_{2}t}\right\}=\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}E(x,c_{2}t),

then by (
3.2
)-(
3.3
) we get that for any
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{0}(x,c_{1}t)\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,c_{2}\tau)\,dy\leq\overline{c}_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}E(x,\overline{c}_{0}c_{1}t)E(y^{-1}\circ x,\overline{c}_{0}c_{2}\tau)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq\overline{c}_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}E(x,\overline{c}_{0}c_{1}t)E(y^{-1}\circ x,\overline{c}_{0}c_{1}\tau)\,dy\leq\overline{c}_{0}^{4}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{0}(x,\overline{c}_{0}^{2}c_{1}t)\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,\overline{c_{0}}^{2}c_{1}\tau)\,dy=\overline{c}_{0}^{4}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}\gamma_{0}(x,\overline{c}_{0}^{2}c_{1}(t+\tau)),

namely for all
c_{1},c_{2}>0
there exists
c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{0}(x,c_{1}t)\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,c_{2}\tau)\,dy\leq c_{3}\gamma_{0}(x,c_{4}(t+\tau))\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\>t,\,\tau>0.

(3.10)

Here below we summarize some other consequences of Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

The map
(z;\zeta)\mapsto\gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)
is continuous in
\Omega=\{z\neq\zeta\}
and for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
the following vanishing property holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(X_{i}X_{j}\gamma_{\zeta})(y\circ x,t)\,dy=0\quad\forall 1\leq i,j\leq m;\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}\gamma_{\zeta})(y\circ x,t)\,dy=0.

Proof.

Concerning the continuity of
(z,\zeta)\mapsto\gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)
, note that

\displaystyle|\gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)-\gamma_{\zeta_{0}}(z_{0};\zeta_{0})|\leq|\gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)-\gamma_{\zeta_{0}}(z;\zeta)|+|\gamma_{\zeta_{0}}(z;\zeta)-\gamma_{\zeta_{0}}(z_{0};\zeta_{0})|

for any
(z;\zeta),\,(z_{0},\zeta_{0})\in\Omega
. As
(z,\zeta)\to(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
, the two terms vanish by (
3.5
) and the continuity of
\gamma_{\zeta_{0}}
(see Theorem
3.1
), respectively; thus
(z;\zeta)\mapsto\gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)
is continuous in
(z_{0};\zeta_{0})
, for whatever
(z_{0};\zeta_{0})\in\Omega
, as claimed.

As for the vanishing property of the parametrix, left-invariance of the
X_{i}
’s and dominated convergence (based on the Gaussian estimate (
3.5
)) yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(X_{i}X_{j}\gamma_{\zeta})(y\circ x,t)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}X_{i}X_{j}\left(x\mapsto\gamma_{\zeta}(y\circ x,t)\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=X_{i}X_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{\zeta}(y\circ x,t)\,dy=0

in force of (
3.1
); analogously, one gets

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}\gamma_{\zeta})(y\circ x,t)\,dy=\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{\zeta}(y\circ x,t)\,dy=0.

∎

According to the classical parametrix method, we look for a fundamental solution of
H
of the form

\Gamma(z;\zeta)=\Gamma_{\zeta}(z;\zeta)+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}\Gamma_{\chi}(z;\chi)\,\mu(\chi,\zeta)\,d\chi

(3.11)

for an unknown kernel function
\mu=\mu(y,\eta;\xi,\tau)
defined whenever
\eta>\tau
. To get an ansatz for the latter, let us formally apply
H
to both sides of (
3.11
): differentiating the integral term under the integral sign (for spatial derivatives) or via Leibniz’ integral rule (for the
t
-derivative), and denoting

Z_{1}(z;\zeta):=(H\Gamma_{\zeta}(\cdot;\zeta))(z)\quad\forall z\neq\zeta,

(3.12)

for every
z=(x,t),\,\zeta=(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
t>\tau
we formally get

\displaystyle 0=Z_{1}(z;\zeta)+\lim_{t\to\tau^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma_{\chi}(z;y,\eta)\,\mu(y,\eta;\zeta)\,dy+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}Z_{1}(z;\chi)\,\mu(\chi;\zeta)\,d\chi;

by point (v) in Theorem
3.1
, this formally rewrites as

0=Z_{1}(z;\zeta)+\mu(z;\zeta)+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}Z_{1}(z;\chi)\,\mu(\chi;\zeta)\,d\chi.

Seeing
\zeta
as a fixed parameter in (
3.11
) and defining the integral operator
T
as

\phi\mapsto(T\phi)(z):=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}Z_{1}(z;\chi)\,\phi(\chi)\,d\chi,

the above equation becomes

-Z_{1}(z;\zeta)=[(I-T)\,\mu(\cdot;\zeta)](z),

hence the theory of Neumann series formally gives

\mu(z;\zeta)=-[(I-T)^{-1}Z_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)](\zeta)=-\left[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{j}Z_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)\right](z).

Summarizing these (formal) computations, we may expect
H
to admit a fundamental solution expressed in the form (
3.11
), with the kernel
\mu
assigned by

\displaystyle\mu(z;\zeta):=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}(z;\zeta)\quad\forall z,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\>\text{with}\>t>\tau

(3.13)

where, for
j\geq 2
, the sequence
\{Z_{j}(z;\zeta)\}_{j}
is defined recursively as

\displaystyle Z_{j}(\cdot;\zeta):=T^{j-1}Z_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}Z_{1}(\cdot;\chi)Z_{j-1}(\chi;\zeta)\,d\chi.

The rest of this section is devoted to justify in a rigorous way this intuition.

3.2
Properties of the kernel
\mu

First of all we show the convergence of the series (
3.13
) in the set
\{t>\tau\}
, namely that
\mu
is well defined therein. To this aim, we introduce the auxiliary sequence

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta):=e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}Z_{1}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta),

\displaystyle Z_{j}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta)=T^{j-1}Z_{1}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta)=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}Z_{1}^{\lambda}(z;\chi)Z_{j-1}^{\lambda}(\chi;\zeta)\,d\chi

for
\lambda>0
to be chosen later; this is just a “shrinked” version of our sequence
\{Z_{j}\}_{j}
, as it can be easily shown that
Z_{j}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta)=e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}Z_{j}(z;\zeta)
for all
j\geq 1
. As already done in the parabolic case
[
12
]
, the strategy is to find suitable upper bounds for the
Z_{j}^{\lambda}
’s, and then choose
\lambda
large enough to ensure convergence of the bounding series. Due to the recursive definition of the
Z_{j}^{\lambda}
’s, this can be done by induction on
j\geq 1
; as for
j=1
, by Theorem
3.1
we have

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\cdot;\zeta)=(H-H_{\zeta})\Gamma_{\zeta}(\cdot;\zeta)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{ij}(\cdot)-a_{ij}(\zeta))\,X_{i}^{x}X_{j}^{x}\Gamma_{\zeta}(\cdot;\zeta),

(3.14)

and thus (
1.5
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) yield (for
x,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>\tau
)

\displaystyle|Z_{1}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta)|\leq\check{c}_{2}\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})}{t-\tau}\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{2}(t-\tau))\,e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}.

(3.15)

Remark 3.4
.

We point out that in the corresponding Hölder case
[
3
]
there is no need for this “
\lambda
-shrink”, as the upper estimates one gets directly for
\{Z_{j}\}
are already good enough to ensure convergence of the series. This is not the case for double Dini coefficients, as the lack of regularity weakens the estimates for the
Z_{j}^{\lambda}
’s, thus making necessary a proper shrink of the sequence.

Through the procedure sketched above, we can prove the following:

Theorem 3.5

(Well-definiteness of
\mu
)
.

For any
T>0
, the series (
3.13

\{T^{-1}\leq t-\tau\leq T\}
; moreover, for every

with

|\mu(z;\zeta)|\leq 2\check{c}_{2}e^{\lambda T/2}\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\,(t-\tau)^{-1}\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{2}(t-\tau)),

(3.16)

being

the constant “of order 2” in (

)-(

Proof.

Let us denote
\phi(\lambda,\epsilon):=8\widetilde{\omega}(2\epsilon)+16\Lambda\lambda^{-1}\epsilon^{-2}
for any

and
\lambda>0
. We prove the following estimates by induction on

|{Z}^{(\lambda)}_{j}(z;\zeta)|\leq\check{c}_{2}^{j}\,[\phi(\lambda,\epsilon)]^{j-1}\,\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})}{t-\tau}\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{2}(t-\tau))\,e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)/2};

(3.17)

(with
j\geq 2

\epsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle|Z_{j}^{\lambda}(z;\zeta)|\leq\check{c}_{2}^{j}\,[\phi(\lambda,\epsilon)]^{j-2}e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)/2}\int_{\tau}^{t}\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\eta})\omega(2\sqrt{\eta-\tau})}{(t-\eta)(\eta-\tau)}\times

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-\lambda(t-\eta)/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{2}(t-\eta)\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ y,\check{c}_{2}(\eta-\tau))\,dy\right)d\eta

If instead

\displaystyle I\leq\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})}{t-\tau}\left[8\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\epsilon)+16\Lambda\lambda^{-1}\epsilon^{-2}\right]=\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})}{t-\tau}\,\phi(\lambda,\epsilon)

(3.19)

for any

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{Z}_{j}(z;\zeta)|\leq\check{c}_{2}\,\frac{\omega(2\sqrt{t-\tau})}{t-\tau}\,\gamma_{0}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{2}(t-\tau))\,e^{\lambda(t-\tau)/2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[\check{c}_{2}\phi(\lambda,\epsilon)\right]^{j}.

small enough to have

4
Cauchy problem for H and properties of
\Gamma

In this section we use the fundamental solution
\Gamma
previously constructed to build a solution for the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Hu=f\hskip 36.98866pt\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)\\
u(\cdot,0)=g\hskip 27.31465pt\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{cases}

(4.1)

under suitable assumptions about the regularity and growth of both
f
and
g
and with
T>0
small enough.
We also exploit the result concerning the homogeneous version of (
4.1
) (see Theorem
4.2
below) to prove lower Gaussian estimates for
\Gamma
, thus concluding the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Definition 4.1
.

By
solution
of the problem (
4.1
) we mean a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

(i)

u\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T))
(see Definition
2.2
);

(ii)

u
satisfies the equation
Hu=f
pointwise, that is

Hu(x,t)=f(x,t)\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
;

(iii)

the initial datum

is assumed as a pointwise limit, namely for every

4.1
Homogeneous Cauchy problem

Let us start with the solution of the homogeneous version of (
4.1
):

Theorem 4.2

(Solution of the homogeneous Cauchy problem)
.

Let
\nu\geq 0
and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function subject to the growth condition

|g(x)|\leq Me^{\nu||x||^{2}}\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(4.2)

for some constant
M>0
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
T>0
satisfying
T\nu<\delta
, the function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

u(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy

belongs to
\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T))\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T])
and solves the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Hu=0\hskip 36.98866pt\,\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)\\
u(\cdot,0)=g\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

In particular, if
g
is bounded, all the above holds for any
T>0
.

Proof.

By (
3.49
), (
3.50
) and (
4.2
)), dominated convergence gives

\displaystyle X_{j}u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(X_{j}^{x}\Gamma)(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy\quad(\forall 1\leq j\leq m);

\displaystyle X_{i}^{x}X_{j}^{x}u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(X_{i}^{x}X_{j}^{x}\Gamma)(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy\quad(\forall 1\leq i,j\leq m);

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}\Gamma)(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy,

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0
, hence
u\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T))\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T])
by (
3.46
); moreover, the above and (
3.47
) yield

Hu(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H\Gamma(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)\,dy=0\quad\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T).

We are left to discuss the initial condition. Denoting again

\displaystyle J(z;\zeta):=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau,t)}\Gamma_{\chi}(z;\chi)\,\mu(\chi;\zeta)\,d\chi

for any
z=(x,t),\,\zeta=(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
t>\tau
, we have

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-g(x_{0})|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|J(x,t;y,0)\,g(y)|\,dy+\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{(x_{0},0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t)\,g(y)\,dy-

\displaystyle g(x_{0})\Bigg|+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\left[\gamma_{(y,0)}(\xi^{-1}\circ x,t)-\gamma_{(x_{0},0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t))\right]\,g(y)\right|\,dy=W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}.

From (
3.3

3.31
) and (

\displaystyle W_{1}\leq c(M,T)\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,c_{1}t)\,e^{\nu||y||^{2}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M,T)\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t})\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\exp\left\{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{c_{2}t}+\nu||y||^{2}\right\}\,dy.

By quasi-triangle inequality
||y||\leq\kappa\,(||y^{-1}\circ x||+||x||)
, hence

\displaystyle||y||^{2}\leq 2\kappa^{2}\,(||y^{-1}\circ x||^{2}+||x||^{2})=2\kappa^{2}(d(x,y)^{2}+||x||^{2});

assuming
T\nu<\delta:=(4c\kappa^{2})^{-1}
, one easily gets

\displaystyle-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{c_{2}t}+\nu||y||^{2}\leq-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{c_{2}t}\left[1-2c_{2}t\nu\kappa^{2}\right]+2\nu||x||^{2}\leq-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2c_{2}t}+2\nu\kappa^{2}||x||^{2}

for all

and

, and so by (

\displaystyle W_{1}\leq c(M,T)\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t})\,e^{2\nu\kappa^{2}||x||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2c_{2}t}\right\}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M,T)\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t})\,e^{2\nu\kappa^{2}||x||^{2}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\>(x,t)\to(x_{0},0).

As for
W_{2}
, from
(v)
there holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{(x_{0},0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t)\,g(y)\,dy\longrightarrow g(x)\quad\text{as}\>t\to 0^{+},\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

hence it is immediate to deduce that as
(x,t)\to(x_{0},0)
we have

W_{2}\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\gamma_{(x_{0},0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t)\,g(y)\,dy-g(x)\right|+\left|g(x)-g(x_{0})\right|\longrightarrow 0.

As for
W_{3}
, let
\sigma\in(0,1)
to be chosen later; then we split

\displaystyle W_{3}=\left(\int_{d(x,y)\leq\sigma}+\int_{d(x,y)>\sigma}\right)\left|\left[\gamma_{(y,0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t)-\gamma_{(x_{0},0)}(y^{-1}\circ x,t))\right]g(y)\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=W_{3}^{1}+W_{3}^{2}.

The integration domain of
W_{3}^{1}
is contained in the closed ball
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
with radius

by quasi-triangle inequality, hence

is bounded (uniformly w.r.t.
\sigma

), (
3.9

\displaystyle W_{3}^{1}\leq\check{c}_{0}\int_{d(x,y)<\sigma}\omega(d(x_{0},y))\,\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{0}t))\,dy\leq\check{c}_{0}\omega(\kappa[d(x,x_{0})+\sigma]).

for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Instead, using (

\displaystyle W_{3}^{2}\leq 2M\check{c}_{0}\int_{d(x,y)>\sigma}\gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\circ x,\check{c}_{0}t)\,e^{\nu||y||^{2}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M,\nu,x_{0})\,e^{2\nu\kappa^{2}}\int_{d(x,y)>\sigma}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\,\exp\left\{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2c_{1}t}\right\}\,dy.

Setting

\displaystyle W_{3}^{2}\leq c(M,\nu,x_{0})\int_{||\xi||>\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{t}}}\exp\left\{-\frac{||\xi||^{2}}{2c_{1}}\right\}\,d\xi,

which gives us the following:

.
∎

4.2
Maximum principles and lower Gaussian estimates

Let us introduce some maximum principles for the operator
H
, which will be useful both to prove the nonnegativity of the fundamental solution
\Gamma
and to get uniqueness of solutions for the associated Cauchy problem (in suitable spaces); the latter, in turn, will reveal crucial to obtain the reproduction property (
1.10
) of
\Gamma
and, consequently, also the lower Gaussian estimate in (
1.9
).

Theorem 4.3

(Weak maximum principle in bounded sets)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then:

\begin{cases}u\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\Omega)\\
Hu\leq 0\hskip 41.25641pt\text{in}\>\Omega\cap\{t<t_{0}\}\\
\limsup u\leq 0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{in}\>\partial\Omega\cap\{t\leq t_{0}\}\end{cases}\implies\quad u\leq 0\>\,\text{in}\>\,\Omega\cap\{t<t_{0}\}.

(by “
\limsup u\leq 0
in
\partial\Omega\cap\{t\leq t_{0}\}
” we mean that, for any
(\overline{x},\overline{t})\in\partial\Omega\cap\{t\leq t_{0}\}
and any sequence
\{(x_{n},t_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\Omega\cap\{t\leq t_{0}\}
converging to
(\overline{x},\overline{t})
, there holds
\limsup_{n\to\infty}u(x_{n},t_{n})\leq 0
).

For the proof see
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
(keeping in mind that, in the latter, the opposite sign convention for
H
is used). Denoting by
C_{*}(\Omega)
the space of continuous functions in
\Omega
which vanish at infinity, an immediate corollary is the following:

Corollary 4.4

(Weak maximum principle in infinite strips)
.

Given
T_{1}<T_{2}
, there holds:

\begin{cases}u\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2}))\cap C_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[T_{1},T_{2}])\\
Hu=0\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})\\
u=0\hskip 36.98866pt\text{on}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{T_{1}\}\end{cases}\implies u=0\>\,\text{in}\>\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2}).

Proof.

Since
u
vanishes at infinity, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
M_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\sup_{||x||>M_{\epsilon},\,t\in(T_{1},T_{2})}u(x,t)<\epsilon.

Let
M>M_{\epsilon}
,
\Omega:=\{||x||<M\}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
and
t_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2})
. Consider the function
w_{\epsilon}(z):=u(z)-\epsilon
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
:

\begin{cases}w_{\epsilon}\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2}));\\
Hw_{\epsilon}=0\quad\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})\\
w_{\epsilon}\leq 0\hskip 19.91692pt\text{in}\>\partial\Omega\cap\{t\leq t_{0}\}=\{||x||=M\}\times(T_{1},t_{0}),\end{cases}

thus
w_{\epsilon}\leq 0
in
\{||x||<M\}\times(T_{1},t_{0})
by Theorem (
4.3
). By arbitrariness of
M>M_{\epsilon}
and
t_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2})
, it immediately follows that
w_{\epsilon}\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
. Then, since this holds for any
\epsilon>0
and the sequence
\{w_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
converges uniformly to
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we get that
u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
.

Note now that also
-u
vanishes at infinity, hence all the above procedure can be analogously applied to
-u
, yielding
-u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
, namely
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
. Combining the two results the thesis is achieved.
∎

It is immediate to deduce the following uniqueness result for solutions of (
4.1
) vanishing at infinity (here below
C_{*}(\Omega)
stands for the space of continuous functions in
\Omega
which vanish at infinity):

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous functions. Then, for any
T_{1}<T_{2}
, the solution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Hu=f\hskip 25.6073pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})\\
u(\cdot,T_{1})=g\quad\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

if it exists, is unique in the space
\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2}))\cap C_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[T_{1},T_{2}])
.

Proof.

Let and
v,\,w\in\mathfrak{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2}))\cap C_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[T_{1},T_{2}])
be both solutions of (
4.1
). Then the function
\psi:=v-w
clearly belongs to the same regularity class and satisfy
H\psi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
and
\psi(\cdot,T_{1})\equiv 0
; hence by Corollary

there holds
\psi\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
, namely

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T_{1},T_{2})
.
∎

Coming back to the fundamental solution
\Gamma

Proof.

\Gamma(x_{0},t_{0};\xi_{0},\tau_{0})<0

\Gamma(x_{0},t_{0};\cdot,\tau_{0})<0

\Gamma

be small enough and

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\begin{cases}Hu=0\hskip 22.76219pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau_{0}-\frac{T}{2},\tau_{0}+\frac{T}{2})\\

Moreover, by dominated convergence and Theorem

\displaystyle\lim_{||x||\to+\infty}u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lim_{||x||\to+\infty}\Gamma\left(x,t;\xi,\tau_{0}-\frac{T}{2}\right)\,g(\xi)\,d\xi=0.

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{\tau_{0}-T/2\}

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\tau_{0}-T/2,\tau_{0}+T/2)

However, since

u(x_{0},t_{0})=\int_{B_{\epsilon}(\xi_{0})}\Gamma\left(x_{0},t_{0};\xi,\tau_{0}-\frac{T}{2}\right)g(\xi)\,d\xi<0,

which is a contradiction. Thus

.
As for the reproduction property (

\begin{cases}Hu=0\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(s,s+T),\\
u(\cdot,s)=g\hskip 17.07164pt\text{in}\>\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

, then

it follows that the function

\displaystyle\lim_{||x||\to+\infty}\psi(x,t;s,\xi,\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lim_{||x||\to+\infty}\Gamma(x,t;y,s)\,\Gamma(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy=0.

\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\psi(x,t;s,\xi,\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,t;y,s)\,\Gamma(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy

for all

\tau<s<t<s+T

∎

\Gamma

“sufficiently close” to the diagonal

Theorem 4.7

(Lower Gaussian estimate for the fundamental solution)
.

For any
T>0
there exists a constant
\overline{\overline{c}}=\overline{\overline{c}}(T)>0
such that, for every
(x,t),(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
0<t-\tau\leq T
, the following uniform Gaussian estimate holds true:

\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{\overline{c}}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-\overline{\overline{c}}\,\frac{d(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\Gamma_{(\xi,\tau)}(x,t;\xi,\tau)+J(x,t;\xi,\tau)
, with the former term at right hand side bounded below by (
3.3
) and the latter bounded above by (
3.31
); by equivalence between
d
and the CC distance
d_{X}
, namely

\beta^{-1}d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}\leq d(x,\xi)^{2}\leq\beta d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for a constant
\beta\geq 1
, the mentioned estimates can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{(\xi,\tau)}(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{c}_{0}^{-1}\,(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\,\exp\left\{-\overline{c}_{0}\beta\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\};

\displaystyle|J(x,t;\xi,\tau)|\leq c(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\,\exp\left\{-\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{\widehat{c}_{3}\beta(t-\tau)}\right\}

(with the advantage that
d_{X}
is a true distance). These yield

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{c}_{0}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\,\exp\left\{-\overline{c}_{0}\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}\left[1-\overline{c}_{0}c\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\times\right.

\displaystyle\quad\times\left.\exp\left\{\left(\overline{c}_{0}-\frac{1}{\widehat{c}_{3}}\right)\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}\right]=:\overline{c}_{0}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\,\exp\left\{-\overline{c}_{0}\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}\times

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[1-c_{2}\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\exp\left\{c_{3}\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}\right],

We choose
0<\delta<\frac{1}{4}
such that
c_{3}\delta<1
and
\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{\delta})\leq\frac{1}{4c_{2}}
,
which is clearly possible in view of the properties of a modulus of continuity. Then for any
x,t,\xi,\tau
with
0<t-\tau\leq\delta
and
d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}\leq\delta(t-\tau)
there holds

\displaystyle 1-c_{2}\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{t-\tau})\exp\left\{c_{3}\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}\geq 1-c_{2}\,\widetilde{\omega}(2\sqrt{\delta})\,e^{c_{3}\delta}>\frac{1}{4},

thus

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq(4\overline{c}_{0})^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-4\overline{c}_{0}\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\}.

Now, fixed any
T>0
, we show an analogous bound for all points
(x,t),(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
0<t-\tau\leq T
; it is not restrictive to assume
T\geq 1
. Let

k:=\left\lfloor\max\left\{\frac{T}{\delta},\,\frac{4d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{\delta(t-\tau)}\right\}\right\rfloor+1,\quad\sigma:=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\delta\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}}.

Given
\epsilon>0
, take an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
joining
x
to
\xi
with length smaller than
d_{X}(x,\xi)+\epsilon
, a chain of points
x\equiv x_{0},\,x_{1},\,...,\,x_{k+1}\equiv\xi
on
\gamma
and a chain of times
t\equiv t_{0}>t_{1}>...>t_{k+1}\equiv\tau
with

\displaystyle d_{X}(x_{j},x_{j+1})=\frac{d(x,\xi)+\epsilon}{k+1},\quad t_{j}-t_{j+1}=\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}\quad(\forall 0\leq j\leq k).

Then, for any
0\leq j\leq k
and any
y\in B_{d_{X}}(x_{j},\sigma),\,y^{\prime}\in B_{d_{X}}(x_{j+1},\sigma)
, there holds

\displaystyle d_{X}(y,y^{\prime})\leq d_{X}(y,x_{j})+d_{X}(x_{j},x_{j+1})+d_{X}(x_{j+1},y^{\prime})\leq\frac{d_{X}(x,\xi)+\epsilon}{k+1}+2\sigma

\displaystyle\quad<\frac{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{k\delta(t-\tau)}}{k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\delta\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}}<\sqrt{\delta\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}}=\sqrt{\delta(t_{j}-t_{j+1})}

(provided
\epsilon
is small enough); since we also have
t_{j}-t_{j+1}\leq T/(k+1)<\delta
, then the lower Gaussian estimate holds for points
(y,t_{j}),(y^{\prime},t_{j+1})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Gamma(y,t_{j};y^{\prime},t_{j+1})\geq c^{-1}(t_{j}-t_{j+1})^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-c\frac{d_{X}(y,y^{\prime})^{2}}{t_{j}-t_{j+1}}\right\}

for any constant
c\geq 4\overline{c}_{0}
. Setting
y_{0}=x
and
y_{k+1}=\xi
for homogeneity of notation), we iterate (
1.10
) and exploit the nonnegativity of
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}\Gamma(y_{0},t_{0};y_{1},t_{1})\,\Gamma(y_{1},t_{1};y_{2},t_{2})...\Gamma(y_{k},t_{k};y_{k+1},t_{k+1})\,dy_{1}...dy_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\int_{\Omega_{\sigma}:=B_{d_{X}}(x_{1},\sigma)\times...\times B_{d_{X}}(x_{k},\sigma)}\Gamma(x_{0},t_{0};y_{1},t_{1})...\Gamma(y_{k},t_{k};x_{k+1},t_{k+1})\,dy_{1}...dy_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\geq c^{-k-1}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}\right)^{-\frac{Q}{2}(k+1)}\int_{\Omega_{\sigma}}\exp\left\{-c\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{d_{X}(y_{j},y_{j+1})^{2}}{t_{j}-t_{j+1}}\right\}\,dy_{1}...dy_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\geq c^{-k-1}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{k+1}\right)^{-\frac{Q}{2}(k+1)}\exp\left\{-c\delta(k+1)\right\}\,\left[|B_{d_{X}}(0,1)\right|\,\sigma^{Q}]^{k}

\displaystyle\quad=c^{-k-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}(k+1)^{Q/2}|B_{d_{X}}(0,1)|^{k}4^{-Qk}\,\exp\left\{-c\delta(k+1)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\geq c^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-ck\right\}\exp\left\{-c\delta(k+1)+ck-k\log\left(\frac{c\cdot 4^{Q}}{|B_{d_{X}}(0,1)|}\right)\right\}.

We fix
c
to a value
\overline{c}
large enough for the argument of the last exponential to be positive whenever
k\geq 1
, so that
\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{c}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\{-\overline{c}k\}
; finally, we split in two cases depending on which one among
\frac{T}{\delta}
and
\frac{4d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{\delta(t-\tau)}
is bigger:

(i)

if
\frac{4d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\leq T
, then
k=\left\lfloor\frac{T}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1<2\frac{T}{\delta}
, so

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{c}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-2\overline{c}T\delta^{-1}\right\}=[\overline{c}e^{-2\overline{c}T/\delta}]^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq[\overline{c}e^{-2\overline{c}T/\delta}]^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-[\overline{c}e^{-2\overline{c}T/\delta}]\frac{d(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\};

(ii)

otherwise
\frac{4d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}>T\geq 1
and
k=\left\lfloor\frac{4d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{\delta(t-\tau)}\right\rfloor+1<\frac{8d_{X}(x,\xi)^{2}}{\delta(t-\tau)}
, thus (exploiting again equivalence between
d
and
d_{X}
):

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)\geq\overline{c}^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-\overline{c}\frac{8\beta d(x,\xi)^{2}}{\delta(t-\tau)}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\geq[8\overline{c}\beta\delta^{-1}]^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-Q/2}\exp\left\{-[8\overline{c}\beta\delta^{-1}]\frac{d(x,\xi)^{2}}{t-\tau}\right\},

hence the thesis holds with
\overline{\overline{c}}=\overline{\overline{c}}(T)=\max\{\overline{c}e^{-2\overline{c}T/\delta},\,8\overline{c}\beta\delta^{-1}\}
.
∎

Theorem 4.10

Under the assumptions of Lemma

, the function

Set-valued fractal approximation for countable data sets
Parneet Kaur, Rattan Lal, Ankit Kumar, Saurabh Verma

Abstract
. Fractal geometry deals mainly with irregularity and captures the complexity of a structure or phenomenon. In this article, we focus on the approximation of set-valued functions using modern machinery on the subject of fractal geometry. We first provide a construction of fractal functions for countable data sets and use these functions in the approximation and study of set-valued mappings. We also show the existence and uniqueness of an invariant Borel measure supported on the graph of a set-valued fractal function. In addition, we obtain some effective bounds on the dimensions of the constructed set-valued fractal functions.

3.

\displaystyle C(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
denote the space endowed with the metric
\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}

\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1},S_{2})=\lVert S_{1}-S_{2}\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in I\end{subarray}}H_{d}(S_{1}(t),S_{2}(t)).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\vartheta:=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\ldots,t_{\infty}=\lim t_{n}):t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<\cdots<t_{\infty}=\lim t_{n}\},
which defines a partition of
\displaystyle I,
and let
\displaystyle I_{n}=[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
and assume the following contraction homeomorphism
\displaystyle\zeta_{n}:I\rightarrow I_{n}
such that
\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(t_{1})=t_{n}\text{ and }\zeta_{n}(t_{\infty})=t_{n+1}
or
\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(t_{1})=t_{n+1}\text{ and }\zeta_{n}(t_{\infty})=t_{n}
. Let
\displaystyle\Phi\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
and the base function
\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
satisfies the following condition

B(t_{1})-\Phi(t_{1})=B(t_{\infty})-\Phi(t_{\infty}),

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}-\mathcal{A}_{2}=\left\{a_{1}-a_{2}:a_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}\text{ and }\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R})\right\}
.
If
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a scaling factor, satisfying the
\displaystyle\lvert\alpha\rvert<1,
then a unique function
\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
exists, satisfying the following self-referential equation

\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(t)=\Phi(t)+\alpha[\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))]~\text{ for every }~t\in I_{n},

(3)

where
\displaystyle n\in J=\{1,2,3\ldots\}.
and for
\displaystyle t_{\infty}
satisfies the

\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(t_{\infty})=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Big(\Phi(t_{n})+\alpha[\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))]\Big).

(4)

Proof.

Define the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))=\{U\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R})):U(t_{1})-B(t_{1})~=U(t_{\infty})-B(t_{\infty})\}.
It is straightforward that
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
forms a closed subset within the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R})).
Thus,
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))
is complete with the metric
\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}.

Now, we define the
Read-Bajraktarević
(RB) operator
\displaystyle\phi
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R})),
given by

(\phi U)(t)=\Phi(t)+\alpha[U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))]

for each
\displaystyle t\in I_{n}
and where
\displaystyle n\in J
. For the
\displaystyle t_{\infty}
is given by

(\phi U)(t_{\infty})=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Big(\Phi(t_{n})+\alpha[U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))\Big).

The operator
\displaystyle\phi
is well defined, which follows from the assumptions on
\displaystyle\Phi,B
, and
\displaystyle\alpha
. Let
\displaystyle U,V\in\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R})),
now using the properties of the Hausdorff metric given in the Remark
2.7
for any
\displaystyle t\in I_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}((\phi U)(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle,(\phi V)(t))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=H_{d}\Big(\Phi(t)+\alpha[U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))],\Phi(t)+\alpha[V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))]\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq H_{d}\Big(\alpha U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t)),\alpha V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lvert\alpha\rvert H_{d}\Big(U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t)),V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))\Big).

For
\displaystyle t=t_{\infty},
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}((\phi U)(t_{\infty}),(\phi V)(t_{\infty}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}H_{d}\Big(\Phi(t_{n})+\alpha[U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))],\Phi(t_{n})+\alpha[V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))]\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}H_{d}\Big(\alpha U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n})),\alpha V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\lvert\alpha\rvert H_{d}\Big(U(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n})),V(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))\Big).

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}((\phi U)(t),(\phi V)(t))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert\sup_{t\in I}H_{d}(U(t),V(t))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lvert\alpha\rvert\lVert U-V\rVert_{\infty}.

\displaystyle\lvert\alpha\rvert\lVert U-V\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle t,
we get

\lVert\phi U-\phi V\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lvert\alpha\rvert\lVert U-V\rVert_{\infty}.

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(I,\mathrm{K}(\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}

\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(t)=\Phi(t)+\alpha[\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t))]

\displaystyle n\in J=\{1,2,3\ldots\}

\displaystyle t\in I_{n}
and for the
\displaystyle t_{\infty}

\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(t_{\infty})=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Big(\Phi(t_{n})+\alpha[\Phi^{\alpha}_{\vartheta,B}(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))-B(\zeta_{n}^{-1}(t_{n}))]\Big).

Proof.

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{\alpha}\to\Phi^{\alpha}\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Since, sequence
\displaystyle\{\Phi_{k}\}
converges to
\displaystyle\Phi
, for
\displaystyle\epsilon>0,
there exists a
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{k}-\Phi\rVert_{\infty}<\epsilon(1-\lvert\alpha\rvert)\text{ for all }k\geq N

Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underset{t\in I}{\sup}H_{d}(\Phi_{k}(t),\Phi(t))<\epsilon(1-\lvert\alpha\rvert)\text{ for all }k\geq N.

For the
\displaystyle t\in I_{j}
, the above give us that

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}(

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{\alpha}(t),\Phi^{\alpha}(t))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}\bigg(\Phi_{k}(t)+\alpha\left[\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t))-B(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t))\right],\Phi(t)+\alpha\left[\Phi^{\alpha}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t))-B(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t))\right]\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}(\Phi_{k}(t),\Phi(t))+\lvert\alpha\rvert H_{d}(\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t)),\Phi^{\alpha}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t))).

If
\displaystyle t=t_{\infty}
, we have the following

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}(\Phi_{k}^{\alpha}(t_{\infty}),\Phi^{\alpha}(t_{\infty}))=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underset{j\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}H_{d}\bigg(\Phi_{k}(t_{j})+\alpha\left[\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j}))-B(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j}))\right],

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\Phi(t_{j})+\alpha\left[\Phi^{\alpha}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j}))-B(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j}))\right]\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underset{j\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Big(H_{d}(\Phi_{k}(t_{j}),\Phi(t_{j}))+\lvert\alpha\rvert H_{d}(\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j})),\Phi^{\alpha}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j})))\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{d}(\Phi_{k}(t_{\infty}),\Phi(t_{\infty}))+\underset{j\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\lvert\alpha\rvert H_{d}(\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j})),\Phi^{\alpha}(\zeta^{-1}_{j}(t_{j}))).

From above two inequality, we can conclude

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underset{t\in I}{\sup}~H_{d}(\Phi^{\alpha}_{k}(t),\Phi^{\alpha}(t))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\lvert\alpha\rvert}~\underset{t\in I}{\sup}~H_{d}(\Phi_{k}(t),\Phi(t)).

This implies,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lVert\Phi_{k}^{\alpha}(t)-\Phi^{\alpha}(t)\rVert_{\infty}<\epsilon\text{ for all }k\geq N\text{ and }t\in I.

This establishes the proof.

∎

Proposition 4.1

\displaystyle t_{\infty}

5.
Invariant Measure

In the following section, we have shown the existence of the invariant Borel probability measure supported on the graph of the set-valued fractal function corresponding to the countable data set.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R});G_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the CIFS as defined in Proposition
4.6
. Assume that
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a probability vector such that
\displaystyle 0<p_{i}<1
for all
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}
on
\displaystyle I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}),
such that

\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}.

Moreover,
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})=\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})
and
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})
denotes the support of the Borel probability measure
\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))
denotes the space of all Borel probability measures on
\displaystyle I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\displaystyle\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))
, we define the Monge-Kantorovich metric
\displaystyle d_{MK}
on the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))
as follows

d_{MK}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\bigg\{\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}fd\mu-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}fd\nu:f:I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R},~~\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1\bigg\},

where
\displaystyle\text{Lip}(f)
denotes the Lipschitz constant of the function
\displaystyle f
. The space
\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})),d_{MK})
is a complete metric space, see, for instance,
[
10
,
38
]
.

Let us define a mapping
\displaystyle\Psi:\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))\to\mathcal{P}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))
by
\displaystyle\Psi(\mu)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu\circ G_{i}^{-1}
and clearly, the mapping
\displaystyle\Psi
is well-defined. Firstly, we will show that the mapping
\displaystyle\Psi
we have defined is a contraction. With the notation
\displaystyle c_{j}=\max\{\lvert\alpha\rvert,a_{j}\}
and
\displaystyle c_{max}=\max_{j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{MK}(\Psi(\mu),\Psi(\nu))=

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\bigg\{\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\Psi(\mu))(t)-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\Psi(\nu))(t)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\bigg\{\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu\circ G_{i}^{-1})(t)

\displaystyle-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\nu\circ G_{i}^{-1})(t)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\bigg\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\bigg(\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\mu\circ G_{i}^{-1})(t)

\displaystyle-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(t)d(\nu\circ G_{i}^{-1})(t)\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\bigg\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\bigg(\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(G_{i}(w))d(\mu)(w)

\displaystyle-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}f(G_{i}(w))d(\nu)(w)\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\bigg\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}c_{i}\bigg(\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{c_{i}}f(G_{i}(w))d(\mu)(w)

\displaystyle-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{c_{i}}f(G_{i}(w))d(\nu)(w)\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{max}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\sup_{\text{Lip}(g)\leq 1}\bigg\{\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}gd(\mu)-\int_{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})}gd(\nu)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{max}d_{MK}(\mu,\nu),

where
\displaystyle g(w)=\frac{1}{c_{i}}f(G_{i}(w))
is a Lipschitz function with
\displaystyle\text{Lip}(g)\leq 1
. By the Banach fixed point theorem, we can conclude that there exist a unique Borel measure
\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}
and satisfying the following equation
\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}.

Next, we will show that that
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})=\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha}).
We will prove this by showing that
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})
is the attractor of the CIFS. Now the

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\bigg(\overline{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})})\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}\bigg(\overline{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})})\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}(G_{i}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}=1.

This implies that
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})}).
And, also

\displaystyle\displaystyle 1=\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}))=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}))\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}(I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))=1.

Thus, we get
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}\circ G_{i}^{-1}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}))=1
. Since
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}p_{i}=1,
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})\subseteq G_{i}^{-1}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}))\text{ for each }i\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies that
\displaystyle\overline{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}G_{i}(\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}})})\subseteq\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}),
which proves our claim. By Theorem
4.7
, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})=\text{supp}(\mu_{\boldsymbol{p}}),
which is the required result.
∎

Some Dimensional Results

In the present section, we have determined the upper and lower bounds on the Hausdorff dimension of the graph of the set-valued fractal function corresponding to the countable data set.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{s_{0}}=1
for some
\displaystyle s_{0}\geq 0
, then
\displaystyle s_{k}
converges to
\displaystyle s_{0}
, where
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}^{s_{k}}=1
, that is,
\displaystyle s_{0}=\sup_{k\geq 2}s_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}s_{k}.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle 0<b_{i}<1
for all
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}
. Given
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{s_{0}}=1
and
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}^{s_{k}}=1
where
\displaystyle s_{k}
is uniquely determined for
\displaystyle k\geq 2
. Since, we also have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}b_{i}^{s_{k+1}}=1
which gives us
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}^{s_{k+1}}<1
as the
\displaystyle b_{k+1}^{s_{k+1}}>0
.
From,
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{s_{0}}=1
and
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}^{s_{k+1}}<1
, we can conclude that
\displaystyle s_{k}\leq s_{k+1}
, that is,
\displaystyle s_{k}
for
\displaystyle k\geq 2
is a monotonically increasing sequence.

Further, as
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{s_{0}}=1
then for any
\displaystyle s>s_{0}
, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{s}<1
. Thus,
\displaystyle s_{0}
is the lowest upper bound of
\displaystyle s_{k}
.

Therefore, we obtain

s_{0}=\sup_{k\geq 2}s_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}s_{k}.

∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Consider
\displaystyle\{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R});G_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the CIFS, as defined in Theorem
4.7
, satisfying the

b_{i}d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,S_{1}),(w,S_{2}))\leq d_{\mathcal{G}}(G_{i}(t,S_{1}),G_{i}(w,S_{2})))\leq c_{i}d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,S_{1}),(w,S_{2}))

such that
\displaystyle 0<b_{i}\leq c_{i}<1
for all
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}.
Then
\displaystyle s_{*}\leq\text{dim}_{H}(\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha}))\leq\text{dim}_{B}(\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha}))\leq s^{*},
where
\displaystyle s_{*}=\sup_{k\geq 2}s_{k}
and each of
\displaystyle s_{k}
is uniquely defined by the
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}^{s_{k}}=1
and
\displaystyle s^{*}=\text{max}\bigg\{\text{inf }\big\{s:~\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}^{s}\leq 1\big\},1\bigg\}.

Proof.

In Theorem
4.7
we proved that
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})
is the attractor of the CIFS , that is

\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})=\overline{\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}}{\bigcup}G_{i}(\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha}))}.

Since,

d_{\mathcal{G}}(G_{i}(t,S_{1}),G_{j}(w,S_{2})\leq c_{i}d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,S_{1}),(w,S_{2}))\text{ and }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}c_{n}<1

Let
\displaystyle O=(t_{j},t_{j+1})\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle G_{i_{1}}(O)\cap G_{i_{2}}(O)=\phi
for

and also
\displaystyle G_{i_{1}}(O)\subseteq O
and
\displaystyle O\cap\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})\neq\phi.
Hence, CIFS satisfies the SOSC.

For each
\displaystyle k\geq 2
we considered the finite IFS
\displaystyle\{I\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R});G_{i}:i\in\{1,2,\dots k\}\}
with help of CIFS. Let
\displaystyle A_{k}
be the attractor of the finite IFS. Firstly, we show the SOSC for the finite IFS. Clearly,

It remains to show that
\displaystyle O\cap A_{k}\neq\phi

\displaystyle O\cap A_{k}=\phi
. Since
\displaystyle O=(t_{j},t_{j+1})\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
\displaystyle O\cap A_{k}=\phi

\displaystyle A_{k}
contains element of the type
\displaystyle(t_{n},S)
for
\displaystyle n\in\{j,j+1\}

\displaystyle S\in\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle(t_{j},S)\in A_{k}

\displaystyle n\geq 2,
we have
\displaystyle G_{2}
in IFS, where
\displaystyle G_{2}(t,S)=(\zeta_{2}(t),\alpha S+\Phi(\zeta_{j}(t))-\alpha B(t))
. The map
\displaystyle G_{2}:[t_{j},t_{j+1}]\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow[t_{j},t_{j+1}]\times\mathrm{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle A_{k}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}G_{i}(A_{k})

\displaystyle G_{2}(A_{2})\subseteq A_{2}

\displaystyle G_{2}(t_{j},S)=

\displaystyle(\zeta_{2}(t_{j}),\alpha S+\Phi(\zeta_{2}(t_{j}))-\alpha B(t_{j}))

This implies that

. Therefore, for each,

for all

This gives us the lower bound that

\displaystyle\delta>0

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{G}_{o}(\Phi^{\alpha})\rvert<\delta

\displaystyle\text{dim}_{H}(\mathcal{G}_{o}(\Phi^{\alpha}))\leq\text{inf}\bigg\{s:~\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}^{s}\leq 1\bigg\}.
As the
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha})=\mathcal{G}_{o}(\Phi^{\alpha})\cup\{(t_{\infty},\Phi^{\alpha}(t_{\infty}))\}

\displaystyle\{(t_{\infty},\Phi^{\alpha}(t_{\infty}))\}

\displaystyle\text{dim}_{H}(\mathcal{G}(\Phi^{\alpha}))\leq s^{*}

\displaystyle s^{*}=\text{ max }\bigg\{\text{inf}\big\{s:~\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}^{s}\leq 1\big\},1\bigg\}.

∎

Remark 6.3
.

The upper bound of Hausdorff dimension is more complicated in CIFS. We need to investigate the set

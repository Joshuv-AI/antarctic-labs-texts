Molecular surveillance of Cytomegalovirus strains in maternal and fetal health within Bauchi metropolis Nigeria
Authors: Timothy Waje, Mahmud Yerima Iliyasu, Ahmed Faruk Umar, Ediga B. Agbo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Research purposeThe purpose of this research was to detect Cytomegalovirus (CMV) virulence genes and identify their strains among a cohort of pregnant women attending antenatal clinics within Bauchi metropolis, Nigeria. The absence of CMV control measures in antenatal care in the study area and many communities around the globe, as well as the unavailability of vaccines, pose a significant threat to maternal and fetal health.Materials and methodsThe study was cross-sectional and retrospective among a cohort of pregnant women receiving antenatal care in primary, secondary, and tertiary healthcare facilities within Bauchi Metropolis, Nigeria. Plasma samples from 118 women who were positive for CMV IgM from ELISA were used for molecular analysis via polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The DNA of each sample was extracted via the Qiagen Extraction Protocol (DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit, Quick Start protocol, 2016) and used for amplification of 6 target genes, namely, UL 55, UL73, UL74, UL75, UL100, and UL115 respectively.ResultsThree genes, UL55, which codes for gB(27); UL115, which codes for gL(15); and UL73, which codes for gN(10), were detected among 52 women. The gB strain had the highest occurrence among the women, whereas the gN strain had the lowest.ConclusionThe CMV gB, gL, and gN strains are endemic to the Bauchi metropolis. The gB strain with the highest occurrence rate was likely more infectious than the other strains were, whereas the gL strain confirmed previous reports linking it with a common strain during pregnancy. Therefore, the gB and gL strains are likely threats to maternal and fetal health.

Introduction

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) is a widely distributed viral genus worldwide [1]. Additionally, human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) and human herpesvirus 5 (HHV 5) belong to a family of viruses called Herpesviridae [2]. The “Herpesviridae” or herpesviruses are a group of viruses with the common characteristic of long latency in a host [3]. Members of this group include varicella-zoster virus, Epstein‒Barr virus, human herpes virus 6 (variants A and B), human herpesvirus 7, herpes simplex virus types 1 and 2, Kaposi sarcoma herpesvirus (KSHV) (human herpesvirus 8) and Cytomegalovirus, among others [4]. Cytomegalovirus enlarges infected cells (cytomegaly); hence, the Greek word “Cyto” refers to cells with “megalo” enlargement [5]. The name of the virus is derived from its cytopathic effect on cell cultures and its ability to produce typically owl’s eye intranuclear inclusion bodies in infected cells [6]. Cytomegalovirus exhibits great tropism and infects a broad range of cells, such as parenchymal cells, connective tissue cells of virtually any organ, hematopoietic cells, epithelial cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and smooth muscle cells, either within host or interhost infection [7].

Human Cytomegalovirus (HCMV) is spherical with a double-stranded linear DNA molecule (E-shaped) enclosed in an icosahedral capsid surrounded by a lipid bilayer envelope containing surface glycoproteins such as glycoproteins N (gN), gH, gO, gM, gB, and gL, which are useful for cell attachment and penetration (Fig. 1) [8]. The CMV DNA is approximately 235–250 kilobases (kb) in size and encodes more than 200 proteins [9]. There are 2 principal reverse domains namely: long (L) and short (S) respectively [8]. Each of these domains is made up of three regions, namely, central unique (UC), unique long (UL) and unique short (US) regions, which are flanked by terminal repeats (TRL & IRL) as well as internal repeats (IRL & IRS) (Fig. 1) [10]. The genome of HCMV has been reported to contain an estimated 165–252 open reading frames (ORFs) [11]. An open reading frame is a start codon that is followed by a downstream in-frame stop codon and occurs randomly and abundantly across a whole genome, with only a fraction making their way into transcripts, some of which are translated [12]. It is a portion of a DNA sequence that does not include a stop codon and that translates proteins that play a key role in pathogenesis (Fig. 2) [13].

There is a tegument between the nucleocapsid and the viral envelope, which consists of a group of viral structural components that play important roles in gene regulatory transcription, replication and virulence; virion assembly; and viral‒host immune interactions [14]. Cytomegalovirus has been associated with miscarriages, bronchopneumonia, retinitis, and deformities, among others [5].

Cytomegalovirus virulence genes such as UL 55, UL 73, and UL144, encoding gB which is a high-binding protein, gN, gO, and a protein which is a structural homologue of the herpesvirus entry mediator (HVEM) from congenital transmission, have been linked to clinical outcomes, including sensory–neural loss, among neonates [15]. Additionally, UL115, UL74, and UL75, which code for gL, gO, and gH, have been reported among infants with congenital or postnatal transmission with cases of liver problems, pneumonia, and hepatitis [16].

Disease surveillance has been described as a systematic collection, analysis, interpretation of health-related data, and dissemination of the same data on the occurrence of a particular disease in a population with a view to attracting intervention [17]. Therefore, this research aimed to study the molecular patterns of CMV and identify virulence genes, common strains, and their distributions in relation to certain clinical and disease outcomes as a step of surveillance with the goal of improving maternal and fetal health.

Materials and methods

The study was cross-sectional and retrospective among a cohort of pregnant women receiving antenatal care at some healthcare centers within the Bauchi metropolis.The ethical procedure was followed, and approval was obtained from the ethical committee of the Bauchi State Ministry of Health and Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University Teaching Hospital Bauchi to collect 300 blood samples from pregnant women attending primary, secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities within the Bauchi metropolis. Consent forms were duly signed by all prospective participants after being informed (orally and in written) about the study, its importance to their health, and of their unborn children as well as its relevance to public health. Participation was purely voluntary. Questionnaires were used to obtain relevant information from consented subjects. Three (3) milliliters of blood were collected from each consented woman who signed the consent form for voluntary participation and for the use of her clinical information. Blood was collected at the point of bleeding for screening during antenatal care via a 5 ml syringe, transferred into an ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA)-coated tube, and the plasma was separated. The plasma of each sample was screened for CMV IgM using CMV IgM ELISA kits (E-CMV-K02 ratio diagnostic) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The optical densities (ODs) of all samples and the negative, positive, and cut-off controls were read on an ELISA plate reader for determination of sample reactions [18].

Samples of CMV IgM-positive were used for molecular analysis via polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The CMV DNA in each plasma sample was extracted via the Qiagen Extraction Protocol (DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit, Quick Start protocol, 2016). The primers for the 6 target genes were designed via BLAST of existing genes in GenBank (Table 1).

An agarose gel was prepared by dissolving 0.60 g of agarose powder in 40 ml of 1X Tris-acetate-EDTA (TAE) solution. The suspension was microwaved for 2 min and allowed to cool to approximately 55 °C. A stain with 4 µl of ethidium bromide was added. A comb was inserted into the casting tray and the molten agarose was poured into it. This mixture was maintained for approximately 20 min to allow for solidification. The TAE buffer was poured into the gel tank to barely submerge the gel. The comb was removed from the solidified gel and 3 µl of each amplicon was loaded along a ladder of known size (100 bps and 50 bps in the case of UL55) into separate wells. Therefore, gel electrophoresis was performed by passing an electric current (80-100 volts) for 30 minutes, which caused the negatively charged DNA fragments to migrate through the gel toward the positive electrode. The results were viewed under UV light to reveal the presence or absence of bands in each case [19]. The number of amplified virulence genes and strains for samples tested were recorded, and their percentages determined accordingly.The pattern of occurrence for the strains among the subjects were also studied.

Results

Out of the 300 subjects screened with ELISA, 118 were positive for CMV IgM, of which 52 amplified three out of the six target genes. The distributions of amplified genes among the subjects included UL55, 27 (51.92%), UL115, 15 (28.85%), and UL73 10 (19.23%). These genes encode gB, gL, and gN, respectively (Fig. 3).

Table 4 shows the distribution of the CMV strains among the subjects according to healthcare clinic. Among the 52 samples in which CMV strains were detected, 25 were from pregnant women attending primary healthcare clinics, with 13 (52.00%) belonging to gB, 7 (28.00%) to gL, and 5 (20.00%) to gN respectively. Similarly, of the 15 samples detected with CMV strains in the secondary healthcare facility, 8 (53.33%) had gB, 5 (33.33%) had gL, and 2 (13.34%) had gN. Furthermore, among the 12 samples with CMV strains in the tertiary clinic, 6 (50.00%) had gB, 3 (25.00%) had gL, and 3 (25.00%) had gN respectively.

Discussion

The global burden of CMV infection has been described as alarming, with a significant degree of morbidity and mortality that threatens public health [20]. The virus has been reported to occur more frequently in nations of low socioeconomic status than in the developed world [21]. Its high tropism enables it to access multiple organs, such as the eye, ear, brain, stomach, colon, and lungs, and cause multi-systemic effects, with eventual death in some cases [20]. An understanding of the molecular pattern of CMV is a useful tool in related diseases and immune surveillance as well as in infection prevention and control [22]. This study revealed three strains of CMV, gB, gL, and gN, among the studied population. These findings contradict a previous report that gH, gL, and gO formed complexes among congenitally and postnatally acquired CMV pediatric patients with DNaemia [16]. This variation could be attributed to differences in geographical location or socioeconomic status [23]. The presence of the gL strain in this study and in previous reports among pediatric patients with congenital or postnatal CMV suggest that the gL strain is common in pregnancy. The patterns revealed in this study as shown in Table 4, and the trend line in Fig. 3 show that the strain with the highest occurrence was the gB (27), while the lowest was the gN(10) strain. These findings suggest that the gB strain is more common or endemic in the study area.

In terms of the distribution of CMV strains in relation to healthcare facilities, the three strains with the highest occurrence rates were gB(13), gL(7) and gN(5), which were among the women receiving antenatal care at the selected primary healthcare centers, whereas the strains with the lowest occurrence rates were among those receiving care at the tertiary healthcare facility (gB -6, gL-3, and gN-3). These patterns agree with a previous report associating CMV with low economic status; hence, primary healthcare clinics were mostly patronized by women from low-income families, whereas tertiary healthcare clinics were patronized by higher income families due to high cost [21, 23].

The presence of CMV IgM in a sample during ELISA suggests active CMV infection [21]. The IgM concentration in a tested sample is determined through ELISA by a color change and optical density (OD), which is a measure of light absorbance at a certain wavelength (450 nm) in an ELISA plate reader or spectrophotometer [24]. The higher the absorbance or OD was, the greater the concentration of IgM in the sample, as specified by the ELSA kit manufacturers [25]. Therefore, the pattern of CMV strains in relation to the ODs obtained via ELISA revealed that a greatest proportion of the population with an IgM optical density (OD) ≤ 2.00 were infected with the gB (23.08%) strain, whereas the least proportion were infected with the gN (11.54%) strain. Similarly, women infected with the gB strain were more common (28.85%) among those with an OD ≥ 2.0, while the lowest proportion in this category consisted of women infected with the gN strain (7.69%). This pattern suggests that the CMV gB strain was more endemic in the area, whereas gN was less common.

With respect to the pattern of CMV strains in relation to certain clinical conditions or diseases, more women with hypertension (8 out of 11) were infected with the gB strain than with the gN strain (2 out of 11), whereas only the gB strain infected women with cough and diabetes. These findings could likely be due to the involvement of the gB strain in the etiology of cardiovascular-related diseases as well as in immune evasion in the host [26]. The absence of CMV infection in the HIV-positive woman could suggest a non depletion of her immunity; hence, her HIV infection was unlikely to have reached an advanced stage [27]. The women with ill health captioned “others” had any of the following ailments: typhoid fever, malaria, pneumonia, ulcers, hepatitis, etc. A greater number of them (8 out of 12) were infected with the gB strain, whereas none were infected with the gL strain, and 4 women were infected with the gN strain. This could be due to the high endemicity (51.92%) of the gB strain in the area or its crucial role in the alteration of immune architecture and rendering the host more susceptible to other pathogens or infectious agents [26]. Similarly, the greater number of women (12 out of 27) who did not know their health status captioned “Don’t know” and were infected with the gB strain suggests the need for regular checks since CMV is associated with multi-systemic health issues [20]. The least common strain among these women was the gL strain, which revealed a different pattern for other factors. This could probably be due to its linkage to certain health conditions and diseases not listed in this study [26].

Conclusion

This study confirmed previous reports linking Cytomegalovirus to a public health problem, especially among women of reproductive age. Similarly, the study detected CMV among an antenatal population within Bauchi metropolis, Nigeria, with ongoing transmission. Furthermore, the study revealed 3 virulence genes that encode 3 pathotypes of CMV strains among women: the UL55 gene encodes the gB strain 27 (51.92%), a very virulent plus (vv་) pathotype; UL115 encodes the gL strain 15 (28.85%), which is very virulent (vv); and the UL73 gN strain 10 (19.23%), which is a virulent (v) strain. Additionally, this study revealed an absence of CMV screening in antenatal care programs in the study area. This poses a significant risk to poor maternal and fetal health.

Recommendations

i.The findings of this study can be actively utilized for evidence-based decisions to improve diagnostics, support targeted interventions in community settings, and serve as a foundation for surveillance of diseases with socioeconomic impact.ii.Enlisting CMV as a neglected tropical pathogen and the inclusion of its clinical cases in existing neglected tropical disease (NTD) elimination programs could attract early interventions by the government, charity or intervention organizations, particularly in low- to middle-income nations worldwide.iii.Subsiding the costs of diagnosis, treatments, etc. as well as providing grants by the government to support CMV research could enhance the regular understanding of epidemiological or molecular patterns and potential health implications of CMV infections during pregnancy, as well as ways to manage carriers of the virus, interrupt transmission or delay reactivation.iv.In hospital settings, the inclusion of CMV in routine medical consultations for early detection and interventions should be encouraged to reduce the burden of CMV-related diseases among the human population.v.Cytomegalovirus screening and possible medical interventions should be incorporated into antenatal programs around the globe to monitor and prevent the negative impact of CMV on maternal and fetal health.

Limitations of the study

The study was carried out among pregnant women without the screening of their spouses or sexual partners to ascertain the source of CMV infection. Similarly, their children were not screened for the virus to determine congenital transmission. Furthermore, the study did not include determination of genetic diversity to identify the presence of serotypes among the strains because of insufficient funds and time. Although the target genes were amplified in only 52 of the 118 samples, they provided sufficient information for achievement of the research purpose. Additionally, the concentration of CMV DNA, quality, and purity were tested after extraction but not documented.

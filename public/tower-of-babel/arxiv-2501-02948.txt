Alberti representations, rectifiability of metric spaces and higher integrability of measures satisfying a PDE
David Bate, Julian Weigt

Abstract
We give a sufficient condition for a Borel subset E ⊂ X E\subset X of a complete metric space with ℋ n ​ ( E ) < ∞ \mathcal{H}^{n}(E)<\infty to be n n -rectifiable. This condition involves a decomposition of E E into rectifiable curves known as an Alberti representation . Precisely, we show that if ℋ n ↾ E \mathcal{H}^{n}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{E} has n n independent Alberti representations, then E E is n n -rectifiable. This is a sharp strengthening of prior results of Bate and Li. It has been known for some time that such a result answers many open questions concerning rectifiability in metric spaces, which we discuss. An important step of our proof is to establish the higher integrability of measures on Euclidean space satisfying a PDE constraint. These results provide a quantitative generalisation of recent work of De Philippis and Rindler and are of independent interest.



1
Introduction

A Borel subset
E
of a metric space
X
is
n
-rectifiable
if there exist countably many
A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and Lipschitz
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\to X
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n}\biggl(E\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i}(A_{i})\biggr)=0.

Rectifiable sets provide a fundamental notion of regularity that appears throughout analysis and differential geometry.
This notion has been extensively studied in the classical setting, that is when
X
is some Euclidean space.
The properties of rectifiable sets, and sufficient conditions for rectifiability, are characterised in various structural theorems of geometric measure theory.
Equivalently, such structural theorems characterise
purely
n
-unrectifiable
sets: Borel sets
S\subset X
for which
\mathcal{H}^{n}(S\cap E)=0
for each
n
-rectifiable
E\subset X
.

Accompanying the recent interest in studying analysis and geometry in non-Euclidean metric spaces, there is a growing need for structural theorems for rectifiable sets in a metric space.
Kirchheim
[
Kir94
]
gives a generalisation of Rademacher’s theorem for Lipschitz
f\colon A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
and, using this, generalises many of the results of classical geometric measure theory concerning rectifiable sets to metric spaces.
One consequence in particular is that the
f_{i}
in the definition of a rectifiable set may be chosen to be bi-Lipschitz.

This article is a contribution to the study of sufficient conditions for rectifiability in a metric space, or equivalently, geometric properties of purely unrectifiable subsets of a metric space.

The conditions we consider are in terms of disintegrations of a measure into curves in the form of
Alberti representations
.
The set of curve fragments in
X
,
\Gamma(X)
, consists of all 1-Lipschitz maps
\gamma
defined on a compact subset of
[0,1]
into
X
.
After equipping this set with a suitable metric (see
Definition

3.4
), an Alberti representation of a Borel measure
\mu
on
X
is a finite Borel measure
\eta
on
\Gamma(X)
such that

\mu\ll\int_{\Gamma(X)}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta(\gamma),

(1.1)

where we denote by
\gamma
also the image of
\gamma
, so that
\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\gamma}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
to the image of
\gamma
.
Further, we say that Alberti representations
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}
are
independent
if there exists a Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
that distinguishes the curve fragments of each Alberti representation in the following way.
Up to a countable decomposition of
X
, there exist independent (convex, one-sided) cones
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)\in C_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
,
\eta_{i}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
and each
1\leq i\leq n
(see
Definition

3.17
).

Fubini’s theorem shows that Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
possesses
n
independent Alberti representations.
If
E\subset X
is
n
-rectifiable, then by Kirchheim’s bi-Lipschitz parametrisation theorem, we immediately see that
\mathcal{H}^{n}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{E}
also possesses
n
independent Alberti representations.
It is natural to ask if the converse to this statement holds, which we answer affirmatively.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a complete metric space and let
E\subset X
be Borel with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(E)<\infty
such that
\mathcal{H}^{n}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{E}
has
n
independent Alberti representations.
Then
E
is
n
-rectifiable.

This theorem is a strengthening of Bate and Li
[
BL17
, Theorem 1.2, (ii)
\Rightarrow
(R)]
, where the same implication is shown, under the additional hypothesis that the lower
n
-dimensional Hausdorff density of
E
,

\Theta_{*}^{n}(E,x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap B(x,r))}{(2r)^{n}},

is positive at
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in E
.

The results of
[
BL17
]
have several important consequences in analysis on metric spaces.
It has been known for some time that proving
Theorem

1.1
in full generality answers several open questions concerning rectifiability in metric spaces.
Before discussing these improvements in detail, we mention one consequence that was our primary motivation for establishing
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a complete metric space and let
S\subset X
be a purely
n
-unrectifiable set with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(S)<\infty
.
Then, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, a typical 1-Lipschitz map
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(S))=0
.

Here “typical” means that the statement holds for a residual (in the Baire category sense) subset of all bounded 1-Lipschitz functions equipped with the supremum norm.

This theorem was first proven, under the additional assumption of positive lower density, in Bate
[
Bat20
, Theorem 1.1]
, and we are now able to deduce it in full generality from
Theorem

1.5
and
[
Bat20
, Theorem 5.4]
.
It can be seen as a non-linear counter part to the classical Besicovitch–Federer projection theorem in any metric space.
The converse statement, that typical 1-Lipschitz images of rectifiable metric spaces have positive Hausdorff measure, is proven there in general without additional assumptions.
In some circumstances, a stronger converse holds
[
BT24
]
.

For

For a scalar, vector or matrix valued measure

Consider

\Div\mathbf{T}=(\Div\mathbf{T}_{1},\ldots,\Div\mathbf{T}_{n})

For

Here and throughout,

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(B(0,1))
and let
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

Suppose that
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}

Then

can be decomposed as

\nu=g+b

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\|\nu\|+\|\Div\mathbf{T}\|,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|b\|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}(\|\nu\|+\|\Div\mathbf{T}\|)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\operatorname{Id}\nu-\mathbf{T}\|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

(1.3)

where

is the identity matrix.

.
Moreover, if

, then

\|b\|_{p}\lesssim_{p}\|\operatorname{Id}\nu-\mathbf{T}\|_{p}.

(1.4)

Note that, for

\displaystyle\|\Div\mathbf{T}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nu\|,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Id}\nu-\mathbf{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nu\|,

In particular it gives an absolute lower bound on

Translated into the language of Alberti representations and scaled, this turns into the lower bound

We mention that

Showing this second step is one of the major challenges in this manuscript.
The condition

Thus we must construct

To discuss the idea, suppose that
X=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\varphi
is the identity and a measure
\mu
has an Alberti representation supported on curve fragments
\gamma
that run almost parallel to a coordinate axis.
In general,
\Div(\gamma^{\prime}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\gamma})
is not a finite measure (for example, consider

However,
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

is a Lebesgue point of both
\gamma
and its tangent
\gamma^{\prime}
.
In this case, by adding a small amount of measure,

can be completed to a full curve

around

\overline{\gamma}^{\prime}
almost points in a coordinate direction

.
More precisely, for
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently small

\displaystyle\|e_{i}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\gamma\cap B(x,r)}-(\overline{\gamma})^{\prime}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\overline{\gamma}\cap B(x,r)}\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon r,

(1.7)

\displaystyle r\|\Div\bigl((\overline{\gamma})^{\prime}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\overline{\gamma}\cap B(x,r)}\bigr)\|

and

r\leq\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap B(x,r)).

, this single curve fragment is sufficiently regular in the sense of

(
1.9

In principle, this could happen for most

Consequently, we wish to apply

In other words, we cannot apply

.

If

Our solution, in

In

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p>1
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and for
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
\nu_{i},g_{i},b_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(B(0,1))
be such that
\nu_{i}=g_{i}+b_{i}
,
\|g_{i}\|_{p}\leq c_{1}
and
\|b_{i}\|\leq c_{2}
.
Suppose that
\nu_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu\in\mathcal{M}(B(0,1))
.
Then there exist
g,b\in\mathcal{M}(B(0,1))
with
\nu=g+b
,
\|g\|_{p}\leq c_{1}
and
\|b\|\leq c_{2}
.

Proof.

By Banach-Alaoglu applied to
g_{i}\in L^{p}(B(0,1))
and Mazur’s lemma there exist a sequence of convex coefficients
\lambda_{k}^{i}
with
\lambda_{k}^{i}=0
for
k<i
such that the functions
\tilde{g}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}\lambda_{k}^{i}g_{k}
converge to some
g\in L^{p}(B(0,1))
with
\|g\|_{p}\leq c_{1}
.
In particular,
\tilde{g}_{i}\to g
also in
L^{1}(B(0,1))
.
Set
b=\nu-g
,

\tilde{\nu}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}\lambda_{k}^{i}\nu_{k}
and
\tilde{b}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}\lambda_{k}^{i}b_{k}
.
Then
\tilde{\nu}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu
and thus
\tilde{b}_{i}=\tilde{\nu}_{i}-\tilde{g}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu-g=b
. Consequently

\|b\|\leq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\|\tilde{b}^{i}\|\leq c_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
b\in L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
b^{-}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\widehat{b}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then

\|b\|_{L^{1}(B(0,1))}\lesssim_{p,q}\|b\|_{1,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|b^{-}\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\widehat{b}\|_{q}.

Proof.

Take a nonnegative Schwartz function
\psi
with

1_{B(0,1)}\leq\psi\leq 2\cdot 1_{B(0,2)}

so that

\|b\|_{L^{1}(B(0,1))}\leq\int\psi|b|=\int\psi b+2\int\psi b^{-}.

Since
\widehat{\psi}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\int\psi b=\int\widehat{\psi}\widehat{b}\leq\|\widehat{b}\|_{q}\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{q^{\prime}}\lesssim_{q}\|\widehat{b}\|_{q}

and so it remains to bound
\int\psi b^{-}
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\psi b^{-}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,2)}b^{-}\leq\int_{0}^{\min\{\|b\|_{1,\infty},\|b^{-}\|_{p}\}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B(0,2))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\min\{\|b\|_{1,\infty},\|b^{-}\|_{p}\}}^{\|b^{-}\|_{p}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{|b|>\lambda\})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{\|b^{-}\|_{p}}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{|b^{-}|>\lambda\})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda.

The first summand is equals
\mathcal{L}^{n}(B(0,2))
times

\min\{\|b\|_{1,\infty},\|b^{-}\|_{p}\}\leq\|b\|_{1,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|b^{-}\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The second summand vanishes if
\|b^{-}\|_{p}\leq\|b\|_{1,\infty}
, and otherwise equals

\displaystyle\int_{\|b\|_{1,\infty}}^{\|b^{-}\|_{p}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{|b|>\lambda\})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\|b\|_{1,\infty}}^{\|b^{-}\|_{p}}\frac{\|b\|_{1,\infty}}{\lambda}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\|b\|_{1,\infty}\log(\|b^{-}\|_{p}/\|b\|_{1,\infty})\lesssim_{p}\|b\|_{1,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|b^{-}\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Finally, we can bound the third summand by

\displaystyle\int_{\{|b|>\|b^{-}\|_{p}\}}b^{-}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{|b|>\|b^{-}\|_{p}\})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|b^{-}\|_{p}\leq\|b\|_{1,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|b^{-}\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1<p<\frac{n}{n-k}&\text{if }n\geq 3\text{ and }0<k<n,\\
1<p<\infty&\text{if }n=2\text{ or }k\geq n,\\
1\leq p<\infty&\text{if }k=0.\end{cases}

For any
c>0

exists a
C>0
such that the following holds.
Let
m:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that, for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n/2+1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|\xi|^{|\alpha|}|\partial_{\alpha}m(\xi)|\leq c(1+\xi^{2})^{-k/2}.

If
k>0
, for any
h\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

g:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\widehat{h})

satisfies
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq c\|h\|
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p}\leq C\|h\|.

If
k=0
, for any
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

g:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m\widehat{h})

satisfies

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p}\leq C\|h\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad\text{if }p>1,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1,\infty}\leq C\|h\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\text{if }p=1.

Proof.

The first bound follows from

\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq\|m\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{h}\|_{\infty}\leq c\|h\|.

For the other bounds set

w(\xi)=(1+\xi^{2})^{k/2}m(\xi)

and let
B
be the Bessel potential of order 1, so that
\widehat{B}(\xi)=(1+\xi^{2})^{-1/2}
(see
[
Gra14a
, 6.1.2]
).
Then

\widehat{g}=(\widehat{B})^{k}w\widehat{h}=w\mathcal{F}(\underbrace{B*\ldots*B}_{k\text{ times}}*h).

By our assumptions on
m
, for every multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n/2+1
we have

|\partial^{\alpha}w(\xi)|\lesssim_{c,\alpha}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}

which means that
w
is a Hörmander-Mikhlin multiplier, and thus gives rives to a Calderon-Zygmund operator
R
which implies the desired bounds in the case
k=0
, see
[
Gra14
, Theorem 5.2.7]
.

If
k>0
then there exists an
s>1
with

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{k}{s}+1-k.

If
n=2
then
B\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
s>1
, see
[
Gra14a
, Section 6.1.2]
, and thus by Young’s convolution inequality we have

\|\underbrace{B*\ldots*B}_{k\text{ times}}*h\|_{p}\lesssim_{p}\|B\|_{s}^{k}\|h\|.

(2.1)

If
n\geq 3
then by assumption on
p
we have have
1<s<\frac{n}{n-1}
which is the range for which

, so that
2.1
remains true.
Thus the desired bound follows from the boundedness of
R
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and from
2.1
.
∎

3
Measures defined by disintegration and Alberti representations

3.1
Hausdorff distance and the measurability of the intersection operator

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
\mathcal{C}(X)
the set of non-empty closed subsets of
X
.
For
C,D\in\mathcal{C}(X)
denote by
N(C,r)
the closed
r
neighbourhood of
C
in
X
and define the
Hausdorff distance
between
C
and
D
as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{H}}(C,D)=\inf\{r>0:C\subset N(D,r),\ D\subset N(C,r)\},

provided this set is non-empty, and equal to 1 otherwise.

It is well known that
(\mathcal{C}(X),\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{H}})
is complete, respectively separable, respectively compact, whenever
X
is
[
AT04
, Section 4.4]
.
We fix
X
throughout this
Section
.

We require the measurability of the intersection operator in
\mathcal{C}(X)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\subset X
compact and
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}(X)
a closed subset.
The set

\{C\in\mathcal{C}(X):\exists D\in\mathcal{C},\ D\subset C\cap K\}

is closed in
\mathcal{C}(X)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}\ni C_{i}\to C\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and that

C_{i}\cap K\supset D_{i}\in\mathcal{C}

for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
K
is compact, so is
\mathcal{C}(K)

and so we may suppose
D_{i}\to D
in
\mathcal{C}(K)
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is closed,
D\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then
D\subset C\cap K
.
Indeed,
D\subset K
and so it suffices to check
D\subset C
.
If
x\in D
, there exist
x_{i}\in D_{i}\subset C_{i}
with
x_{i}\to x
.
Pick
y_{i}\in C\cap B(x_{i},2\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{H}}(C,C_{i}))
, so that the triangle inequality implies
y_{i}\to x
.
Since
C
is closed,
x\in C
, as required.
∎

For
C\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
r>0
, we write
B(C,r)
for the closed ball in
\mathcal{C}(X)
centred on
C
with radius
r
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K\subset X
compact.

For any
C,D\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
r>0
,
D\cap K\in B(C,r)
if and only if
D\cap K\subset N(C,r)
and there exists
C^{\prime}\in B(C,r)
with
C^{\prime}\subset D\cap K
.

The set

\mathcal{C}_{K}:=\{C\in\mathcal{C}(X):C\cap K\neq\emptyset\}

is closed and the map
J\colon\mathcal{C}_{K}\to\mathcal{C}(X)
defined by
J(C)=C\cap K
is Borel.

Proof.

The if-and-only-if statement is immediate and

Lemma

3.2
with
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(X)
shows that
\mathcal{C}_{K}
is closed.

The Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{C}(X)
is generated by closed balls.
For
C\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
r>0
,
let

\mathcal{C}=\{D\in\mathcal{C}(X):\exists C^{\prime}\in B(C,r),\ C^{\prime}\subset D\cap K\},

also, for
R>0
, let

\mathcal{C}_{R}=\{D\in\mathcal{C}(X):\exists x\in D\cap K,\ \mathrm{d}(x,C)\geq R\}.

By the first statement,

J^{-1}(B(C,r))=\mathcal{C}\setminus\bigcup\{\mathcal{C}_{R}:\mathbb{Q}\ni R>r\}.

Since
B(C,r)
is closed,
\mathcal{C}
is closed by
Lemma

3.2

and so it suffices to show each
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is closed.
To this end, if
D_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
and
D_{i}\to D\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, let
x_{i}\in D_{i}\cap K
with
\dist(x_{i},C)\geq R
.
By the compactness of
K
, we may suppose
x_{i}\to x\in K
.
In particular,
\dist(x,C)\geq R
.
Pick
y_{i}\in D\cap B(x_{i},2\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{H}}(D,D_{i}))
, so that the triangle inequality implies
y_{i}\to x
.
Since
D
is closed,
x\in D
.
∎

Curve fragments

Definition 3.4
.

Define the set of

.

Note that, although

may be an arbitrary compact set, since

is Lipschitz,

is well defined for

-almost every

.
Indeed, first isometrically embed

into

and consider

to

.

, 4.1.6]
,

exists for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

and hence

exists for

-a.e.

.

Similarly, if

, then

-almost every

and, for every Lipschitz extension

of

and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every

Lemma

naturally applies to

.

.

Let

be compact.
The set

3.3
Defining measures via disintegration

We now give a general definition of how to define a measure as an integral combination of one dimensional measures.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a complete and separable metric space and
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z})
,
so that
\eta\times\mathcal{H}^{1}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z}\times[0,1])
.
Let
\mathcal{E}\colon\mathcal{Z}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
\gamma\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\Gamma(X)

be Borel functions.
In this
Section
we will
write
\gamma_{z}
in place of
\gamma(z)
.
On
X
define the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Borel measure

\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E},\gamma,\eta)=\pi_{\#}[\mathcal{E}\cdot(\eta\times\mathcal{H}^{1})],

for
\pi\colon\mathcal{Z}\times[0,1]\to X
given by
\pi(z,t)=\gamma_{z}(t)
.
Then, by Fubini’s theorem, for any Borel

,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E},\gamma,\eta)(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\pi^{-1}(B)}\mathcal{E}(z,t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta(z)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Z}}{\gamma_{z}}_{\#}(\mathcal{E}(z,\cdot)\mathcal{H}^{1})(B)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta(z).

(3.2)

Fubini’s theorem also gives the Borel measurability of the map

z\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(B)}\mathcal{E}(z,t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}(t).

Note that

\|\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E},\gamma,\eta)\|\leq\int_{\pi^{-1}(X)}|\mathcal{E}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\eta\times\mathcal{H}^{1}),

(3.3)

which holds as an equality if
\mathcal{E}
is nonnegative.

Remark 3.8
.

By the same proof as
[
Bat15
, Lemma 2.5]
, the representation given in
3.1
holds for all
\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E},\gamma,\eta)
-measurable

.

Remark 3.9
.

There are many natural choices of
\mathcal{E}
.
For example, if

is Lipschitz,
\mathcal{E}(z,t)=(f\circ\gamma_{z})^{\prime}(t)
whenever the derivative exists and

otherwise, is Borel, see
[
Bat15
, Lemma 2.8]
.
Similarly, the zero extension of

Remark 3.10
.

Suppose

depends only on
\gamma_{z}
rather than

, as in the examples in

3.9
.
That is,

is of the form

Unfortunately,

.

3.4

\|P\nu-(\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{1},\eta_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{F}_{n}\gamma_{n}^{\prime},\gamma_{n},\eta_{n}))\|\leq Cn\varepsilon\|\nu\|.

Thus
Proposition

2.6
provides
\varepsilon,c>0
for which the conclusion holds.
∎

3.5
Alberti representations

For the rest of the
Section
assume that
X
is a complete and separable metric space.
In particular,
\mathcal{Z}:=\Gamma(X)
is also complete and separable.

Definition 3.15
.

An
Alberti representation
of a
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is a
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma(X))
such that
\eta
-a.e.
\gamma
is injective and

\mu\ll\int_{\Gamma(X)}\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\eta(\gamma).

(3.6)

If
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
is injective and
B\subset X
Borel then

\int_{\gamma^{-1}(B)}|\gamma^{\prime}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap B),

see
[
AT04
, Theorem 4.4.2]
.
Therefore the measure on the right hand side of
3.6
equals

\mathfrak{I}(|\operatorname{Id}^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta)

for
\operatorname{Id}:\Gamma(X)\rightarrow\Gamma(X)
the identity map and
|\operatorname{Id}^{\prime}|\colon\Gamma(X)\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
defined by
(\gamma,t)\mapsto|\gamma^{\prime}|(t)
if the metric derivative exists and 0 otherwise.

We introduce the following terminology to distinguish
different
Alberti representations.

Definition 3.16
.

For
w\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
0<\theta<1
let

C(w,\theta)
be the cone consisting of those
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\langle v,w\rangle\geq(1-\theta^{2})|v|
.
By a
cone
we will always refer to a set of this form.
Cones
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are said to be
independent
if any
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
v_{i}\in C_{i}\setminus\{0\}
are linearly independent.

For
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\varepsilon>0
and a
1
-Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon)
be the set of
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
such that, for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

\langle\varphi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\varphi(\gamma(t_{1})),e\rangle\geq(1-\varepsilon^{2}/2)|\varphi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\varphi(\gamma(t_{1}))|.

(3.7)

For
\delta>0
, let
\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon,\delta)
be the set of those
\gamma\in\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon)
such that, for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, we also have

|\varphi(\gamma(t_{2}))-\varphi(\gamma(t_{1}))|\geq\delta|t_{2}-t_{1}|.

(3.8)

Note that
\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon,\delta)
is closed.

Definition 3.17
.

Alberti representations
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}
are
independent
if there exists a

1
-Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a countable Borel decomposition

X=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}X_{k}

and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist independent cones
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\eta_{i}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in\gamma^{-1}(X_{k})
, we have
(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)\in C_{i}\setminus\{0\}
.

If the distinction is necessary, we will say that the Alberti representations are independent
with respect to
\varphi
.

Remark 3.18
.

By
Proposition

3.11
, up to a countable decomposition of
X
, we obtain an equivalent definition of an Alberti representation in
Definition

3.15
if we replace the absolute continuity with equality.
By the moreover statement of
Proposition

3.11
also independence and membership to
\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta)
are preserved:
If a measure
\mu
has independent Alberti representations (or Alberti representations
\eta_{i}\in\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta)
respectively), then
X
can be decomposed into a countable number of pieces where
\mu
equals independent Alberti representations (or Alberti representations
\eta_{i}\in\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta)
respectively.)

We are able to
refine
Alberti representations as follows.

Lemma 3.19
.

Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
has
n
independent Alberti representations with respect to a Lipschitz
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists a decomposition
X=\cup_{j}X_{j}
into Borel sets
X_{j}
such that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
there are
\delta_{j},\tau_{j}>0
for which the following holds:
For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite Borel decomposition
X_{j}=\cup_{k}X_{j}^{k}
and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a matrix
P=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of unit vectors with
|P^{-1}|\leq\tau_{j}
such that
\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}^{k}}
has Alberti representation
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}
with
\eta_{i}\in\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta_{j})
.

Proof.

The statement and proof are similar to
[
Bat15
, Corollary 5.9]
.

By taking a countable decomposition of
X
, we may suppose that there exists
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
Lipschitz and independent cones
C(e_{1},\theta_{1}),\ldots,C(e_{n},\theta_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\mu
has an Alberti representation
\eta_{i}
satisfying
(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)\in C_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e
t\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
and
\eta_{i}
-a.e
\gamma
.
Let
\alpha>0
be such that the cones
C(e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha)
are also independent.
Then there exists
\tau>0
such that, for any choice of unit vectors
w_{i}\in C(e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha)
, if
P=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
, then
|P^{-1}|\leq\tau
.

By the inner regularity of
\mu
it suffices to consider the case that
X
is compact.
In this case, any set
\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon,\delta)
is a compact subset of
\Gamma(X)
.

For a moment fix
1\leq i\leq n
.
By applying
[
BKO23
, Proposition 3.7]
with the sets
\Gamma_{j}:=\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha/2,1/j)
, we obtain a Borel decomposition

X=N\cup\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}X_{j}

where each
\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}}
has an Alberti representation
\eta_{j}
supported on
\Gamma_{j}
and
N
satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap N)=0

for each
\gamma\in\cup_{j}\Gamma_{j}
.
Since the cone defining each
\Gamma_{j}
has a slightly wider aperture,
simple measure theory, similar to the proof of
[
Bat15
, Lemma 5.5]
, shows that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap N)=0
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
with
(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\in C_{i}\setminus\{0\}
almost everywhere.
In particular, the hypothesised Alberti representations of
\mu
imply that
\mu(N)=0
.

By applying the previous paragraph for each
1\leq i\leq n
we obtain a countable Borel decomposition
X=\cup_{j}X_{j}
and
\delta_{j}>0
such that each
\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}}
has an Alberti representation
\eta_{i}
supported on
\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha/2,\delta_{j})
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and
1\leq i\leq n
let
w_{1}^{i},\ldots,w_{k_{i}}^{i}
be unit vectors such that

C(e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha/2)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{k_{i}}C(w_{k}^{i},\varepsilon/2)\subset C(e_{i},\theta_{i}+\alpha).

(3.9)

By applying
[
BKO23
, Proposition 3.7]
again to all combinations of

\Gamma_{i}^{k}:=\Gamma(\varphi,w_{k}^{i},\varepsilon,\delta_{j}/2)

(3.10)

gives a finite Borel decomposition

X_{j}=N\cup\bigcup_{k}X_{j}^{k}

such that the following holds.
For each
1\leq i\leq n
there exists
k_{i}
such that

\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}^{k}}
has an Alberti representation supported on
\Gamma_{i}^{k_{i}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap N)=0
for each
\gamma
in
\cup_{k}\Gamma_{i}^{k}
.
Again by the argument similar to
[
Bat15
, Lemma 5.5]
, using the left hand inclusion of
3.9
and the choice of “
\delta_{j}/2
” in
3.10
,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma\cap N)=0
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}
.
Consequently
\mu(N)=0

and thus we have found the required decomposition.
∎

Definition 3.20
.

For
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
Lipschitz define

\varphi^{\prime}\colon\Gamma(X)\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
(\gamma,t)\mapsto(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)
whenever the derivative exists and

otherwise.

To conclude the section we establish the exact representation of a measure that we will require for the proof of
Theorem

1.8
.

Corollary 3.21
.

Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
has
n
independent Alberti representations with respect to a Lipschitz
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists a decomposition
X=\cup_{j}X_{j}
into Borel sets
X_{j}
such that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
there are
\delta_{j},\tau_{j}>0
for which the following holds:
For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite Borel decomposition
X_{j}=\cup_{k}X_{j}^{k}
and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a matrix
P=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of unit vectors with
|P^{-1}|\leq\tau_{j}
such that, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, there exists
\eta_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta_{j}))
with

\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}^{k}}=\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta_{i}).

Proof.

Let
X=\cup_{j}X_{j}
and
\delta_{j},\tau_{j}>0
be given by
Lemma

3.19
and fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a further decomposition
X_{j}=\cup_{k}X_{j}^{k}
and a matrix
P=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of unit vectors with
|P^{-1}|\leq\tau_{j}
such that each
\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}^{k}}
has Alberti representations
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}
with
\eta_{i}\in\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta_{j})
.
We now also fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\eta_{i}
-almost every
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every
t\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
,

|(\varphi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)|\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\geq\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|}{\delta}.

In particular

\displaystyle\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j}^{k}}

\displaystyle\ll\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta_{i}).

By
Proposition

3.11
we can further decompose
X_{j}^{k}=\cup_{l}X_{j,l}^{k}
such that for each
l\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{l},\zeta_{i}^{l},\eta_{i}^{l}
with

\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j,l}^{k}}=\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{l},\zeta_{i}^{l},\eta_{i}^{l}).

By the moreover statement of
Proposition

3.11
, the map
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{l}
is of the form
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{l}(z,t)=|(\varphi\circ\zeta_{i}^{l}(z))^{\prime}(t)|
and each
{\zeta_{i}^{l}}_{\#}\eta_{i}^{l}\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma(\varphi,e_{i},\varepsilon,\delta_{j}))
.
Therefore

\mu{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{X_{j,l}^{k}}=\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{l},\zeta_{i}^{l},\eta_{i}^{l})=\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},{\zeta_{i}^{l}}_{\#}\eta_{i}^{l}),

is of the required form and we finish the proof by indexing
(X_{j,l}^{k})_{l,k\in\mathbb{N}}
as
(X_{j}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

4
Approximating fragments by curves

In this
Section
we fix a complete and separable metric space
X
and a
1
-Lipschitz map
\varphi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0,\ e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon))
.
Observe that, if
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
, then
\varphi\circ\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, if
\gamma\in\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon)
for some
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\varepsilon>0
, then
\varphi\circ\gamma\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Id},e,\varepsilon)
.
Let
\mathcal{E}\colon\Gamma(X)\to\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the map
\gamma\mapsto\varphi\circ\gamma
.
Then, if
\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta)\in\mathcal{M}(X)
,

\varphi_{\#}\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta)=\mathfrak{I}(|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|,\mathcal{E},\eta)\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(4.1)

for
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
as in
Definition

3.13
.

The measure on the left hand side of
4.1
appears in the conclusion of
Corollary

3.21
.
The right hand side of
4.1
without
|\cdot|
,
\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E},\eta)
, is the same expression as
\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{F}\gamma^{\prime},\gamma,\eta)
in
Proposition

3.14
(if we choose
\mathcal{F}=1
).
Recall, that
Proposition

3.14
is our consequence of
Theorem

1.9
in the setting of Alberti representations.

The goal in this
Section
is to satisfy the hypotheses of
Proposition

3.14
(for a single
1\leq i\leq n
) for a localisation of the measure in
4.1
.
More precisely, given a Lipschitz approximation
\psi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
of
1_{B(x,r)}
, we satisfy the hypotheses of
Proposition

3.14
for
\nu=\varphi_{\#}(\psi\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta))
by using a corresponding modification of
\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E},\eta)
.
This allows us to localise the conclusion of
Proposition

3.14
to
B(x,r)
, which is necessary to prove
Theorem

1.8
.

The curves in
Proposition

3.14
need to have full domain
[0,1]
, while the curves coming from
Corollary

3.21
may be very fragmented (by the nature of
\Gamma(X)
and of an Alberti representation, and even more so due to our use of
Proposition

3.11
).
Thus, we begin by extending curve fragments
\varphi\circ\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to curves defined on
[0,1]
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
denote
a_{\gamma}=\inf(\operatorname{dom}\gamma)
and
b_{\gamma}=\sup(\operatorname{dom}\gamma)
and assume
a_{\gamma}<b_{\gamma}
.
Define
\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}=\varphi\circ\gamma
on
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
, so that
\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
is closed,
[a_{\gamma},b_{\gamma}]\setminus\operatorname{dom}\gamma
is a union of open intervals
(a,b)
disjoint from
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
with
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
.
For such an interval
(a,b)
, with
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
and
a<t<b
, define
\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}
to be the linear interpolation,

\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}(t)=\frac{b-t}{b-a}\varphi(\gamma(a))+\frac{t-a}{b-a}\varphi(\gamma(b)).

(4.2)

For
t\in[0,1]\setminus[a_{\gamma},b_{\gamma}]
extrapolate
\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}
according to
4.2
with
a=a_{\gamma},\ b=b_{\gamma}
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
We define
\Phi\colon\Gamma(X)\to\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
\Phi(\gamma)=\overline{\varphi\circ{\gamma}}
if
a_{\gamma}<b_{\gamma}
,
and otherwise, if
a_{\gamma}=b_{\gamma}
, define
\Phi(\gamma)(t)=\varphi(\gamma(a_{\gamma}))+(t-a_{\gamma})e
for
t\in[0,1]
.
Then
\Phi
is Lipschitz on a closed set and on its complement and in particular Borel.

To summarise, given
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
, we define
\Phi(\gamma)\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\operatorname{dom}\Phi(\gamma)=[0,1]
.
Moreover, for any
t\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
,
\Phi(\gamma)(t)=\varphi(\gamma(t))
.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\Phi(\gamma)}
may be much larger than
\mathcal{H}^{1}{\mathbin{\upharpoonright}}_{\varphi\circ\gamma}
.
Consequently,
\mathfrak{I}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi,\eta)
may be much larger than the measure in
4.1
, which means we may lose the validity of
3.4
.
This can be fixed by multiplying
\Phi^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{F}\colon\Gamma(X)\times[0,1]\to[0,1]
with
\mathcal{F}(\gamma,t)=1_{\operatorname{dom}\gamma}(t)
.
In this case,
\mathfrak{I}(|\mathcal{F}\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi,\eta)
equals the measure in
4.1
, and the same is true for the corresponding vector valued measures without the
|\cdot|
.
Like this however,
\mathcal{F}(z,\cdot)
is not absolutely continuous and its weak derivative
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(\gamma,\cdot)
might be an infinite measure which means that
3.5
fails.

This brings us to a more delicate part of the argument.
We must smoothen
\mathcal{F}
in order to satisfy
3.5
, whilst at the same time not change
\mathcal{F}
too much in order to preserve
3.4
.
To achieve this, we consider
\mathcal{F}(\gamma,\cdot)
only where
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
has high Lebesgue density.
Moreover recall, that we localise
\nu
by cutting off
\mathfrak{I}(|\varphi^{\prime}|,\operatorname{Id},\eta)
using
\psi
.
This is reflected in
\mathfrak{I}(\mathcal{F}\Phi^{\prime},\Phi,\eta)
by a suitable choice of
\mathcal{F}
in order to preserve
3.4
.

We first define a general smoothing operation and later apply it locally and to regions of the domain with high Lebesgue density.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
r>0
and
\psi\colon X\to[0,2]
be
\frac{1}{r}
-Lipschitz.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
we extend
\psi\circ\gamma
similarly to
\varphi\circ\gamma
:
Define
\overline{\psi\circ{\gamma}}=\psi\circ\gamma
on
\operatorname{dom}\gamma

and write
[a_{\gamma},b_{\gamma}]\setminus\operatorname{dom}\gamma
as a union of open intervals
(a,b)
disjoint from
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
with
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
.
We interpolate to those intervals
(a,b)
by defining

\overline{\psi\circ{\gamma}}(t)=\max\Bigl\{0,\psi(\gamma(a))-|a-t|/r,\psi(\gamma(b))-|b-t|/r\Bigr\}.

Further, we extend to
0\leq t<a_{\gamma}
by defining

\overline{\psi\circ{\gamma}}(t)=\max\Bigl\{0,\psi(\gamma(a_{\gamma}))-|a_{\gamma}-t|/r\Bigr\},

and to
b_{\gamma}<t\leq 1
using the same definition with
b_{\gamma}
instead of
a_{\gamma}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Formally
Definitions

4.1
and
4.2
depend on

and

respectively.
However we omit this dependence from the notation for simplicity as

and

will always be clear from the context.

Recall
\Gamma(\varphi,e,\varepsilon,\delta)
from

3.16
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For

and
x\in X
let

-Lipschitz and supported on

-Lipschitz

Since

.
Since
\psi
is
\frac{1}{r}

-Lipschitz,

.

\overline{\psi\circ{\gamma}}

.

-a.e.

.

,

Let

denote the metric in

.

Formula

3.8
in

and the fact that

is 1-Lipschitz imply, for any

and hence

We now identify the domains of curve fragments with high density.

Definition 4.5
.

Define

\operatorname{P}(X)=\{(\gamma,t)\in\Gamma(X)\times[0,1]:t\in\operatorname{dom}(\gamma)\}.

For
R>0
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, let

be the set of those

for which

\mathcal{H}^{1}([t-r,t+r]\cap\operatorname{dom}\gamma)\geq 2(1-\varepsilon)r

for all

.

Since
K\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{1}(K)
is upper semicontinuous with respect to Hausdorff convergence on
\mathbb{R}
, each

is closed.

For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

monotonically increases, as

, to a set

with the following property:
For each
\gamma\in\Gamma(X)
and each density point

,

.
Thus, for any
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma(X))

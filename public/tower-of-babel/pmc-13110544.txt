Preventive interventions for preschool children at risk for emotional and behavioural problems: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Esme Anggeriyane, Siti Yuyun Rahayu Fitri, Meita Dhamayanti, Windy Rakhmawati, Bella Mardya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEmotional and behavioural problems (EBPs) are receiving increasing attention at the global level, and preschool children are no exception. These issues have a significant impact on future development. Preventive interventions in preschool age are effective in preventing more serious disorders by improving social skills, emotional regulation and resilience in children. Although various preventive interventions have been developed and their effectiveness demonstrated, the evidence remains fragmented. At present, there has been no comprehensive study mapping EBPs prevention interventions at the preschool age level using the Neuman Systems Model approach.Methods and analysisThis scoping review will be conducted in accordance with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews.The completed scoping review will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR). A systematic search will be conducted in four major databases, namely Scopus, PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and online searches using Google Scholar, without language and publication year restrictions. Two reviewers will independently screen the literature according to the inclusion criteria and then extract the data. Any differences between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion with a third reviewer. We will compile, summarise and analyse the extracted data and present the results in figures, tables and descriptive narratives to ensure clarity and facilitate comparison across studies.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required, as the review will use only published literature. Findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed international journal and may inform policy and practice in early childhood preventive interventions.RegistrationOpen Science Framework (https://osf.io/zg6ty).

Introduction

Preschoolers, typically aged 3–6 years, are at a critical developmental stage in aspects of biological, motor, cognitive, language, emotional and social competence. These aspects will influence long-term mental health outcomes.15 During this period, the abilities of emotional regulation, behavioural control and social skills are strengthened, laying the foundation for children to develop effective interpersonal relationships, use coping strategies and achieve future academic success.6 7 Health challenges may arise during this period, such as emotional and behavioural problems (EBPs). Early EBPs can lead to serious issues in the future, including internalised problems (experiences focused within the individual, eg, emotional difficulties, depression and anxiety) and externalised problems (manifested outwardly, eg, disruptive, aggressive, antisocial and violent towards humans or animals).5812 Globally, prevalent data indicate that approximately 4%–20% of preschool children experience EBPs.13 14

EBPs during preschool age have a significant impact in both the short and long term. Short-term effects include symptoms of opposition, crying, sleep problems, sadness, reluctance to sleep alone and separation anxiety, which can interfere with daily functioning and social interactions. These problems can also lead to difficulties in peer relationships, behavioural problems and hyperactivity, which directly affect social and emotional abilities in children. In addition, EBPs can cause increased stress within the family and hinder the readiness for school and social engagement in children.1 15 Long-term effects include an increased risk of chronic mental disorders, learning difficulties, dropping out of school, substance abuse and domestic violence. These problems can negatively impact school readiness, psychological well-being and social relationships as children age. EBPs untreated during the preschool years often lead to ongoing emotional and behavioural challenges throughout childhood and adolescence.16 17 Early EBPs are related to poor social skills, educational difficulties and a higher likelihood of experiencing severe mental disorders later in life, such as emotional and behavioural disorders (EBDs).16 18 19

Preschool age is easier to identify developmental delays in children, so early intervention is essential.20 Preventive interventions are often proven to be more effective than curative interventions for preschoolers with EBPs. These interventions not only aim to reduce the risk of mental disorders before they become more severe, but also target broader child and family outcomes across developmental and psychosocial domains, thereby significantly improving child and family functioning.19 Evidence shows that early intervention can reduce the incidence of EBPs in the future by building resilience and helping children develop essential skills such as emotional regulation and social skills.21 22 Positive parenting practices are a key protective factor that can be enhanced through prevention strategies because they promote attachment, security and healthy emotional development.132224 Conversely, risk factors such as parenting stress, poverty and harmful environments can be minimised by preventive programmes that provide support and resources to families.25

Preventive interventions in preschool children at risk for EBPs are crucial to promote optimal development and prevent future disorders. Various interventions have been developed and tested to meet this goal and often target preschool children and their environments (eg, parents and teachers). The Emotion-Based Prevention programme or the I Can Problem Solve programme effectively improves knowledge about emotions, reduces negative emotional expression and internalising behaviour in at-risk preschool children,26 the Social-Emotional Prevention programme can reduce externalisation problems and improve social and emotional skills,27 and the Papilio-U3 programme focuses on teacher–child interactions to prevent EBPs.28 Parent-based interventions such as the Triple P (Positive Parenting Programme) and the Good Behaviour Game have shown effective results in reducing and preventing social problems and EBPs at home and at school.29 30 School-based interventions, such as classroom-wide interventions, are designed not only to identify risks but also to improve social and emotional skills through primary prevention.31 The Head Start programme is a model for early detection and primary prevention of behavioural and emotional disorders.32

Health professionals, including nurses, play an important role in identifying care needs and implementing measures to promote child health and prevent problems from worsening across settings and developmental stages.33 34 In this scoping review protocol, interventions will not be limited to those delivered by nurses but will include preventive interventions provided by various health professionals. The Neuman Systems Model (NSM), developed by Betty Neuman, provides a holistic framework for understanding the interactions and stressors that impact the health and well-being of an individual across physiological, psychological, sociocultural, developmental and spiritual variables.34 35 NSM divides prevention into three levels: primary prevention to improve well-being and prevent stressors from arising, secondary prevention, which emphasises early detection and intervention for identified stressors, and tertiary prevention, which seeks to facilitate rehabilitation and reduce the impact of existing problems.35 36 This framework is relevant for explaining EBPs by highlighting the influence of stressors on the well-being of children and the need to adjust the focus of prevention interventions.

There is no review that specifically and comprehensively addresses interventions for the prevention of EBPs in at-risk preschool children using the NSM approach. Several preliminary reviews are available that are closest to the topic, including social skills intervention for preschool children at risk of EBPs, the Emotional Literacy Support Assistant for improving emotional well-being in school students, and teacher-student relationships for protecting children aged 4–18 years in school from EBPs and supporting academic development.3739 A different scoping review also mapped interventions for EBPs in orphans and adolescents aged 7–20 years living in orphanages.40 However, these reviews have limitations, including a lack of specific focus on the developmental stages of preschool children, intervention approaches and settings that limit generalisability, and the lack of mapping of preventive interventions for preschool children at risk of EBPs that explicitly used the NSM framework. We conducted this scoping review protocol, titled ‘Preventive interventions for preschool children at risk for emotional and behavioural problems’, to establish a scientific basis for developing early prevention strategies and enhancing mental health in preschool children.

Objective

This scoping review aims to map global preventive interventions targeting EBPs in preschool children (aged 3–6 years) and to describe their components, theoretical basis, implementation context and outcomes, to inform the development of a holistic preventive intervention model.

Review questions

This scoping review aims to answer the primary question: what preventive interventions have been developed and/or implemented globally to address EBPs in at-risk preschool children? In addition, there are specific review questions that will also be examined in this scoping review, namely: (1) what types of interventions were reported in previous studies to target EBPs in preschool children?; (2) what theoretical or conceptual framework underpinned the implementation of these interventions (if any)?; (3) in what context (eg, home, early childhood/preschool education, primary care, community and social services) and who implemented the intervention; (4) what outcomes were targeted and what measurement tools were reported for children/families?; and (5) what is the level of prevention of existing interventions based on the NSM framework?

Methods

We will conduct this scoping review in accordance with the Joanna Briggs Institute guidance for protocol scoping reviews.41 This protocol was registered in the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/zg6ty) and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension (PRISMA) for Protocols checklist (online supplemental appendix 3).42 The final scoping review will be reported in accordance with the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews.43

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria were formulated according to population, concept, context and types of evidence sources.

Population

This review will consider studies involving preschool children aged between 3 and 6 years old44 who are at risk for EBPs based on the results of screening using the Cognitive Behavioural Checklist, Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire, Pediatric Symptom Checklist−17, and others, as well as preschool children without clinical diagnosis or at risk of EBDs based on the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) and Related Health Problems and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM). This study may involve parents/caregivers/educators/clinicians as targets of preventive intervention for preschool children at risk of EBPs regardless of their social, economic and cultural backgrounds, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of preventive intervention in preschool children for EBPs.

Concept

The concept examined in this review is various preventive interventions for EBPs in preschool children. We define EBPs broadly to include behavioural problems, emotional problems or both. Emotional problems include shame, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, withdrawal, specific phobias, low self-esteem, hypersensitivity, social phobia, obsessive-compulsive disorder and somatic complaints or eating disorders. Behavioural problems such as antisocial behaviour, defiance, disruptive behaviour, impulsivity, aggression, hyperactivity, as well as attention and self-control problems, such as bullying, tantrums and substance abuse.16 18 EBPs or clinically diagnosed disorders classified according to the criteria of the ICD, Related Health Problems and the DSM, interventions with curative and rehabilitative goals, and tertiary prevention interventions classified according to the NSM will not be included.

Context

This scoping review will consider the studies that describe various preventive interventions for EBPs in preschool-aged populations in multiple settings, such as home, kindergarten, daycare centres and other social environments where children interact with caregivers, peers and family members, and will be considered if they are related to EBPs described in a structured manner.

Types of study design

This scoping review will consider studies that use original articles or primary research. All types of publications, including published original articles such as randomised controlled trials, quasi-experiments, case–control studies, cohort studies and qualitative research. Review articles will not be included, but the reference lists of review studies will still be used to ensure that relevant articles are retained. Abstracts, conference proceedings, research protocols or commentaries will also not be considered because they do not provide sufficient information to answer the research questions. We also decided to include only original articles and exclude grey literature due to methodological considerations. The objective of the scoping review is to comprehensively map preventive interventions for EBPs in preschool-aged children based on available primary empirical evidence. Thus, we strive to minimise the risk of duplication or bias in interpretation that may arise from secondary sources. In addition, focusing on original studies allows for more detailed mapping of research designs, subject characteristics, measurement tools and contexts used to understand the diversity of preventive interventions in various settings.

Search strategy

We will conduct a three-step search strategy to identify studies relevant to this topic. In the first step, a search will be conducted in PubMed to identify text words and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms. Next, we will use the identified keywords, index terms and text words to conduct a systematic search across selected databases, including Scopus, PubMed, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library. This process is undertaken to ensure comprehensive identification of relevant literature. In addition to searching databases, we will also conduct manual searches through the bibliographies of relevant articles. Additional searches will be performed using Google Scholar to identify potential articles that are not indexed in the central databases. We will detail the complete search strategy for all databases included in the supplementary table (online supplemental appendix 1). Finally, we will also manually search the reference lists of all selected studies to find any additional studies that may have been missed. We will include literature in any language (with translations if necessary) and year to ensure that the evidence reviewed reflects comprehensive developments and remains relevant to the current context in understanding interventions to prevent the risk of EBPs in preschool children.

Study selection

The search results will be imported into EndNote (Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA), version 21, for deduplication. After removing duplicate records, two reviewers (EA and BM) will independently conduct an initial screening of the titles and abstracts of the included studies, then proceed to full-text screening based on the inclusion criteria. Any differences that arise between the two reviewers during the screening process will be resolved through consultation with a third reviewer (SYRF). Before beginning the screening process of the obtained literature, the two reviewers will conduct pilot testing on 5% of the randomly selected references to verify the consistency and reliability of the screening process.45 46 The literature search results and screening process will be illustrated in a PRISMA flow diagram, as shown in figure 1.42 47

Data extraction

The review team will develop data extraction forms based on the review objectives to summarise relevant information, which will be recorded in Microsoft Excel. The extracted data will include the name of author(s) and year of publication, study objectives, study design, population and sample size, study setting or context of intervention, type of prevention intervention, detailed description of intervention components, implementers of intervention, theoretical or conceptual framework, targeted outcomes, measurement instruments for each outcome, level of prevention (based on NSM), and key findings. In the NSM framework, operational definitions of preventive levels and decision rules will be provided to classify interventions into primary, secondary and tertiary prevention levels. Primary prevention is delivered before the emergence of identifiable emotional and behavioural symptoms, secondary prevention targets children identified as at risk or showing early signs of emotional and behavioural difficulties, and tertiary prevention targets children with established emotional or behavioural problems. Classification will consider participant characteristics, intervention objectives and study inclusion criteria.

To ensure the eligibility of the data extraction form, two reviewers (EA and BM) will independently test the data extraction table on 10% of the articles obtained from the included studies to ensure methodological rigour and transparency.48 If necessary, the data extraction form draft will be adjusted or revised. The final extraction table will be presented and submitted in the final scoping review report, with the data extraction table presented in a supplementary table (online supplemental appendix 2). During data extraction, two reviewers (EA and BM) will independently extract data from each included study using the form. Any differences that occur between the two reviewers will be discussed and, if necessary, resolved through consultation with a third reviewer (SYRF) and, if required, a fourth reviewer (WR). We also have a fifth reviewer (MD) who has an educational background and experience relevant to the topic of this review. We will generally not conduct critical appraisals in this scoping review because its purpose is to map the available evidence.49 This study aims to provide an overview of research on preventive interventions for preschool children at risk of EBPs, aligning with the objective of this scoping review.50 Next, data extraction will be conducted between March and April 2026.

Data analysis and presentation

During the data merge process, we will use both quantitative and qualitative techniques. First, we will summarise the research results, including using descriptive statistics and frequency distributions. Second, we will categorise the assessment instrument, the intervention strategy used, the theoretical or conceptual framework underlying the implementation of the intervention (if any), the context or setting of the intervention, the outcomes produced, and the level of prevention of the intervention, based on the NSM framework. To illustrate how these findings relate to the objectives of the scoping review, the results of this analysis will be summarised in narrative form and presented using tables and figures. Data analysis and presentation will take place in April 2026.

Ethics and dissemination

This scoping review did not involve patients or direct interaction with participants (the public), so we considered that ethical approval would not be necessary. The results of this scoping review will be disseminated through scientific publications.

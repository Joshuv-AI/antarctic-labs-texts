Abstract

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a (connected) reductive group over a local field
F
and let
\rho:{}^{L}G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{V_{\rho}}(\mathbb{C})
be a representation of its
L
-group. We assume for the introduction that we are given an isomorphism
d:G/G^{\mathrm{der}}\tilde{\,\rightarrow\,}\mathbb{G}_{m},
and that the dual map

\rho\circ d^{\vee}:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{V_{\rho}}(\mathbb{C})

is the inclusion of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
into the center.

It was conjectured by Braverman and Kazhdan
[
BK00
,
Ngô14
]
that there exists a Schwartz space attached to
\rho.
To state the conjecture precisely, let
\mathcal{R}:G(F)\times G(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(L^{2}(G(F)))
denote the regular representation and let
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a nontrivial character. For any function
h:G(F)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
write
h^{\vee}(g):=h(g^{-1}).

Conjecture 1.1

(Braverman-Kazhdan, Ngô)
.

There is a
G(F)\times G(F)
-invariant subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rho}<C^{\infty}(G(F))\cap L^{2}(G(F))

that is dense in
L^{2}(G(F))
and
equipped with a unitary operator

\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi}:\mathcal{S}_{\rho}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\rho}

that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi}\circ\mathcal{R}(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(g_{2},g_{1})\circ\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\overline{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{S}_{\rho}}.

Moreover, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
and tempered representations
\pi
, the operator
\pi\left(f\right)
is well-defined and

(1.0.1)

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi}(f)^{\vee})=\gamma(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi,\rho,\psi)\pi(f).

Here the
\gamma
-factor is defined using the local Langlands correspondence as in (
3.0.4
).

Since
\psi
is usually fixed, we often drop it from notation, e.g.
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}:=\mathcal{F}_{\rho,\psi}.
We refer to
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}
as the
\rho
-Fourier transform
, or simply the Fourier transform.

Remark 1.2
.

In the non-Archimedean case L. Lafforgue has constructed a space of functions that satisfies a weak analogue of Conjecture
1.1

[
Laf14
]
. Roughly his space of functions is designed to satisfy Conjecture
1.1
at the level of traces (not operators).

Our first main theorem is that the conjecture above is true:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume the desiderata on the local Langlands correspondence explained in §
3
below. Then Conjecture
1.1
is true; we may take
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}=\mathcal{C}(G(F)),
the Harish-Chandra space of
G(F).

The space
\mathcal{C}(G(F))
is usually referred to as the Harish-Chandra Schwartz space, but we omit “Schwartz” for brevity and to distinguish it from other spaces we consider.

Remark 1.4
.

The desiderata on the local Langlands correspondence we require are rather weak, and are known in many cases as we explain in §
3
. For example, if one restricts attention to
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
then everything we require about the local Langlands correspondence is known.

The space
\mathcal{C}(G(F))
is too large for many purposes. Thus one is led to introduce additional constraints on
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}.
Let
\mathcal{C}(\pi)
denote the space of smooth matrix coefficients of
\pi.
For

and

, let

\displaystyle Z(s,f,c)=\int_{G(F)}f(g)|d(g)|^{s}c(g)dg,

whenever this function converges absolutely. We refer to

as a

For

Let us simultaneously outline the argument and the sections of this paper.
We point out at the outset that we work with more general

\rho

In §

We then construct the Fourier transform in §

In §

\rho
-Schwartz space

\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))<\mathcal{C}(G(F)).
Briefly, it is the inverse image under the Harish-Chandra Plancherel isomorphism of meromorphic sections with poles no worse than the Langlands

We refer to

as the asymptotic

[

]
, as pointed out to us by Beuzart-Plessis. The asymptotic Hecke algebra satisfies all of the desiderata of Conjecture

except (

S

4
).

At this point we narrow our focus to the non-Archimedean case and cut out a subspace of
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))
to construct the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(G(F)).
A clue to the correct definition is given by Example
6.2
. It demonstrates that elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))
need not lie in the space of almost compactly supported smooth functions

(see Definition
6.1
). On the other hand one expects the Schwartz space to consist of almost compactly supported smooth functions. Thus we define the
\rho
-Schwartz space

\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(G(F))
to be the largest subspace of
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))\cap C_{ac}^{\infty}(G(F))
stable under the Fourier transform.
One way to think about this definition is that
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))
gives us spectral control of the Schwartz space and
C_{ac}^{\infty}(G(F))
gives geometric control. Happily, the space
C_{ac}^{\infty}(G(F))
can be understood using the Paley-Wiener theorem of Bernstein and Heiermann (see §
6.1
). By carefully leveraging the Harish-Chandra Plancherel formula and the Paley-Wiener theorem we are able to prove that Theorem
1.6
is valid for
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}=\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(G(F))
in §
7
.

We suspect that with additional work our methods could be extended to define Schwartz spaces (and not just asymptotic Schwartz spaces) in the Archimedean setting, but several points remain unclear.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Analytic number theory notation

Let
X
be a set. For functions
f,g:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we write
f\ll g
or
f(x)=O(g(x))

if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in X
. We say
g
dominates
f
if
f\ll g
. Occasionally we write
f\ll_{?}g
if the constant depends only on
?
(up to irrelevant dependencies).
We write
f\asymp g
if
f\ll g
and
g\ll f.
By contrast, we say
f
is bounded by
g
if
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in X.

2.2.
Varieties

By a standard abuse of notation, if
X
and
Y
are reduced schemes over
\mathbb{C}
we say that a map
X(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow Y(\mathbb{C})
is algebraic, regular, or polynomial if it is induced by a morphism
X\rightarrow Y
of schemes over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.3.
Linear algebra

For a ring extension

and an

-module

let

denote the base change to

For a real vector space

we write

where

For a real vector space

3.
The local Langlands correspondence

We pin down a precise, weak version of the local Langlands correspondence for our purposes. For a reductive group
G
over a local field
F,
we take
{}^{L}G=\widehat{G}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes W_{F},
the Weil form of the
L
-group. If
M\leq G
is a Levi subgroup then we obtain an
L
-map
{}^{L}M\rightarrow{}^{L}G
in the standard manner
[
GH24
, §7.4]
.

By a

{}^{L}G

such that

is a finite-dimensional

-vector space.

Let

\displaystyle W_{F}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}W_{F}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})&\text{ if $F$ is non-Archimedean,}\\
W_{F}&\text{ if $F$ is Archimedean}.\end{cases}

An
L
-parameter
\phi:W_{F}^{\prime}\rightarrow{}^{L}G
is called

if the projection of
\phi(W_{F})
to
\widehat{G}(\mathbb{C})
has a bounded image; i.e. is contained in a compact subset in the usual Hausdorff topology. It is
discrete
if its image is not contained in any proper Levi subgroup of
{}^{L}G.
Two
L
-parameters are equivalent if they are conjugate by elements in
\widehat{G}(\mathbb{C})
. Let

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(G)\subseteq\Phi_{t}(G)\subseteq\Phi(G)

be the set of equivalence classes of discrete
L
-parameters, tempered
L
-parameters, and
L
-parameters, respectively.

Let

[
Tat79
, §1.4.6]

by pullback along the projection

For

\displaystyle\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{G\mathbb{C}}^{*}=X^{*}(G)\otimes\mathbb{C}\leq X_{*}((G/G^{\mathrm{der}})^{\wedge})_{\mathbb{C}}=X_{*}(Z_{\widehat{G}}^{\circ})_{\mathbb{C}}

-parameter

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
d\in\{1,2\},\,Q\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Let
p,p^{\vee}\in\mathbb{C}[s]
be the monic polynomials such that
p(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)
and
p^{\vee}(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)\right)\right)
are holomorphic for
|\mathrm{Re}(s)|\leq n
and nowhere vanishing on
\mathbb{C}.
Then one has that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{p(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)}{p^{\vee}(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s\right)\right)}\right|\ll_{n}(1+|p(s)|)|Q+1+s+2n|^{nd+2}

for
|\mathrm{Re}(s)|\leq n
.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
GL21
, Lemma 3.3]
.
If
Q<3n+2
then there are only finitely many possibilities for the parameters
d,Q
and the polynomials
p,p^{\vee}
in the expression we wish to bound.
Thus if we assume further that
|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\leq 1
the bound follows.

On the other hand if
Q\geq 3n+2
then
p=p^{\vee}=1.
Thus it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s\right)\right)}\right|\ll_{n}|Q+1+s+2n|^{nd+2}

provided either
|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\geq 1
or
Q\geq 3n+2.

Assume that
0\leq\mathrm{Re}(s)\leq n
. For any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, since
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z),
one has that

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N\right)\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-k\right)

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+k\right)}.

Therefore we have that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s\right)\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+k\right)\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-k\right)\right|^{-1}\left|\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N\right)}\right|.

Assume
N\leq n+1.
Since either
|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\geq 1
or
Q\geq 3n+2

the above is bounded by a constant depending on
n
times

(3.3.1)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+1-(\tfrac{1}{2}+s-\tfrac{2N}{d})\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+(\tfrac{1}{2}+s-\tfrac{2N}{d}))\right)}\right|.

There is an integer
0\leq N\leq n+1
so that

(3.3.2)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathrm{Re}(s)-\tfrac{2N}{d}\in[-\tfrac{2}{d}+\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}].

If
d=2
, by Lemma
3.20
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\Gamma\left((Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|Q+1+\tfrac{1}{2}+s-N|^{2(N-\mathrm{Re}(s))}

\displaystyle\ll_{n}|Q+1+\tfrac{1}{2}+s|^{2}.

Thus we have a sufficient bound on (
3.3.1
) when
d=2.

Now assume
d=1.

Let

Then
\tfrac{1}{2}+s-2N=-\beta(s)+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+s-2N+\beta(s)\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+s-2N+\beta(s)\right)\in[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}].

Applying Lemma
3.20
again we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)+N\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N+\beta(s)\right)}\right|\begin{cases}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N)\right|&\text{ if }\beta(s)=1\\
1&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}|Q+\beta(s)+1+\tfrac{1}{2}+s-2N+\beta(s)|\right)^{-\mathrm{Re}(s)+2N-\beta(s)}|\tfrac{1}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)-N|

\displaystyle\ll_{n}|Q+1+s|^{2}.

Hence for

we have that

(3.3.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s\right)\right)}\right|\ll_{n}|Q+1+\tfrac{1}{2}+s|^{2}

if
Q\geq 3n+2
or
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(s)|\geq 1.

Assume
-n\leq\mathrm{Re}(s)\leq 0
. Set

, so that

. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-s\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+s)\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}+n-s^{\prime}\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+\tfrac{1}{2}-n+s^{\prime})\right)}\right|=\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}-s^{\prime}\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s^{\prime})-nd\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{k=0}^{nd-1}\left|\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s^{\prime})-k\right|\right)\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}-s^{\prime}\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s^{\prime})\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\ll_{n}|Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s^{\prime}|^{nd}\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\left(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}-s^{\prime}\right)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{d}{2}(Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s^{\prime})\right)}\right|.

Applying the bound (
3.3.3
), we see

\displaystyle\ll_{n}|Q+n+\tfrac{1}{2}+s+n|^{nd}|Q+n+1+\tfrac{1}{2}+s+n|^{2}

∎

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma\in\Pi_{2}(M)/i\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{*}}\sup_{\lambda\in V_{\mathcal{K}}}\left\lVert I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(u)\circ\mathrm{HP}(f)(M,\sigma_{\lambda})\circ I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(v)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}N(\sigma_{\lambda})^{k}<\infty

for all
M\in\mathcal{M},\,u,v\in U(\mathrm{Lie}(K)_{\mathbb{C}})
,
k\geq 0
and compact subsets
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
(M,\sigma)\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Temp}}_{\mathrm{Ind}}(G)
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F))
.
Since the representation
I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})^{\mathrm{sm}}
has moderate growth,
the operator
\mathrm{HP}(f)(M,\sigma_{\lambda})
is given by the absolutely convergent integral

\displaystyle I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(f)=\int_{G(F)}f(g)I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(g)dg

for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{M\mathbb{C}}^{*}.

We first prove the bound. By
[
Del05
, (1.25)]
, we have

\left\lVert I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(g)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{|\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)|}.

Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(\mathcal{R}(u,v)f)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ll\int_{G(F)}|\mathcal{R}(u,v)f(g)|\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{|\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)|}dg\ll\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{u,v,k}

for some
k\gg_{\mathcal{K}}1.
Now argue as in
[
Del05
, Lemme 1]
to obtain the bound.

We now prove that
\mathrm{HP}(f)
is a holomorphic section.
The continuous dual of
\mathcal{HS}(I_{P}^{G}(\sigma))
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{HS}(I_{P}^{G}(\sigma^{\vee})).
In this space smooth finite rank operators are dense. Employing
[
Gro53
, §2, Remarque 1]
, to prove that
\mathrm{HP}(f)
is a holomorphic section it suffices to check that for each
\varphi\in I_{P}^{G}(\sigma)^{\mathrm{sm}}
and
\varphi^{\vee}\in I_{P}^{G}(\sigma^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sm}}
the function

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\longmapsto\langle I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(f)\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}\rangle

is holomorphic. We have that

\displaystyle\langle I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(f)\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{G(F)}f(g)\int_{K}\langle I_{P}^{G}(\sigma_{\lambda})(g)\varphi(k),\varphi^{\vee}(k)\rangle dkdg

\displaystyle=\int_{G(F)}f(g)\int_{K}e^{\langle\rho_{P}+\lambda,H_{M}(\mathbf{a}(g))\rangle}\langle\varphi(\mathbf{k}(kg)),\varphi^{\vee}(k)\rangle dkdg

where
\rho_{P}
is the half-sum of the positive roots. Differentiating we deduce the result.
∎

For

Proposition 5.9

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Temp}(G)
. For
(f,c)\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{as}}(G(F))\times\mathcal{C}(\pi)
, the local zeta integral

extends to a meromorphic function of
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{G\mathbb{C}}^{*}

\displaystyle Z(-\lambda,\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(f),c^{\vee})=\gamma(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi_{\lambda},\rho)Z(\lambda,f,c).

In the non-Archimedean case the statement that

means there exists

Proof.

For

, we rewrite (

) as

\displaystyle Z(\lambda,f,c_{\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}})=L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi_{\lambda},\rho)\left\langle\frac{\pi_{\lambda}(f)}{L(\tfrac{1}{2},\pi_{\lambda},\rho)}\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}\right\rangle.

Lemma 6.8

Let

Proof.

If

then the lemma is trivial, so assume

The first assertion follows directly from the definition of

To prove the second assertion, let
\varepsilon>0
and
d>0
. Let
S
be a finite subset of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X^{*}(G),\mathbb{Z})
. If
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
then

and

have disjoint support. Thus

\displaystyle p_{d}\left(\sum_{\alpha\not\in S}f\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}\right)=\sup_{\alpha\not\in S}\sup_{x\in G(F)}|f\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(x)|\Xi_{G}(x)^{-1}\sigma_{G}(x)^{d}

where

is the seminorm (
2.7.3
).
Trivially

\displaystyle|f\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq p_{d+1}(f)\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(x)\Xi(x)\sigma_{G}(x)^{-d-1}

for all

. Hence

\displaystyle p_{d}\left(\sum_{\alpha\not\in S}f\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}\right)\leq p_{d+1}(f)\sup_{\alpha\not\in S}\sup_{x\in G(F)}\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(x)\sigma_{G}(x)^{-1}.

By the Cartan decomposition, this is dominated by

\displaystyle p_{d+1}(f)\sup_{\alpha\not\in S}\sup_{\lambda\in X_{*}(A_{0})}\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(\lambda(\varpi))\sigma_{G}(\lambda(\varpi))^{-1}.

Choose a norm

on

Then

We can choose

for some

then

Then

∎

SUMMARY: The Food and Drug Administration (FDA, the Agency, or we) is 
announcing a public meeting entitled ``Fit-for-Purpose Rare Disease 
Framework Initiative''. Convened by the Reagan-Udall Foundation for, 
and in collaboration with FDA, the meeting will focus on developing a 
fit-for-purpose framework for the development and regulatory review of 
rare disease therapies. There will be an opportunity to request to 
provide public comments at the meeting. In addition, FDA is 
establishing a public docket to collect feedback on and input related 
to its Rare Disease Framework Initiative.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Background

    FDA is announcing the launch of its Rare Disease Framework 
Initiative (Framework Initiative) to develop a regulatory framework 
purpose-built for rare disease therapies. Roughly 1 in 10 Americans 
lives with a rare disease, and only approximately 5 percent of rare 
conditions have an FDA-approved treatment. Developing therapies for 
rare diseases can present scientific and practical challenges, 
including small patient populations, limited understanding of certain 
diseases, and disease heterogeneity. FDA's Framework Initiative will 
respond to these challenges via a holistic approach that approaches 
regulatory review according to the particularities of each product 
based on specific disease state, product type, patient population, and 
attainable evidence.
    The Framework Initiative--spearheaded by the Rare Disease 
Innovation Hub, the Office of the Commissioner, the Center for 
Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER), and the Center for Drug 
Evaluation and Research (CDER)--will result in a rare disease drug 
development and review process that:
    1. Provides greater clarity, consistency, and predictability for 
drug developers, patients, disease advocates, and researchers regarding 
rare disease regulatory decision-making through tailored guidance and 
transparent communication based on the latest science.
    2. Embeds the patient voice both early and at clearly defined 
milestones in the development and review process for rare disease 
therapies such that both sponsors and reviewers can benefit from 
patient perspectives on disease burden, benefit-risk priorities, and 
meaningful measures of progress.
    3. Further leverages external expertise to support a tailored 
approach in regulatory review, especially for rare conditions with very 
small patient populations and limited understanding of natural history, 
as a structural feature to direct FDA reviewers in customizing their 
decision-making to each specific development program, product, and 
disease state.
    4. Builds shared resources, robust training, and institutional 
knowledge on rare disease drug development and review to ensure 
consistency in drug development and review.
    5. Applies risk-based approaches, particularly to chemistry, 
manufacturing, and controls review and pre-approval inspections, to 
support earlier patient access without compromising rigorous quality, 
safety, and effectiveness standards.
    6. Champions the use of cutting-edge science, novel methods and 
clinical trial designs, and innovative statistical approaches in the 
development of rare disease therapies via guidance, transparent 
communication, and programming that reflects FDA's commitment to 
partnering with rare disease innovators to accelerate patient access to 
rare disease therapies.

II. Issues for Consideration and Request for Information

    FDA is seeking feedback from the public--including rare disease 
drug developers, patients living with rare diseases and their 
caregivers, disease advocates, and researchers--on persistent 
challenges for the development of rare disease therapies, as well as 
input on how a tailored approach to regulatory review might mitigate 
these challenges. Feedback will inform development of the framework and 
potential implementation actions, which may include new or revised 
guidance documents related to rare disease drug development and 
regulatory review, revisions to FDA's regulations, training for FDA 
review staff, and organizational enhancements.
    This Request for Information and Comments is being published in 
conjunction with a public meeting on October 28, 2026, to solicit 
feedback on the Rare Disease Framework Initiative. Interested parties 
may provide feedback on FDA's Rare Disease Framework Initiative by 
requesting to provide public comments at the public meeting, providing 
comments in the public docket that will be open from October 28 through 
December 11, 2026, or both. Registration information for the October 
28, 2026, free public meeting, including information about how to 
request to provide public comments, can be found at the following web 
address: https://www.fda.gov/news-events/fda-meetings-conferences-and-workshops/fda-rare-disease-framework-initiative-public-meeting-10282026. In-person attendance is based on space availability, with 
priority given to early registrants. Early

    FDA welcomes suggestions, ideas, and comments regarding CDER and 
CBER's roles in rare disease drug development and review. Below are 
four broad focus areas outlined in the Framework Initiative. We seek 
suggestions and comments within these topic areas and ask that you be 
as specific as possible in your feedback or recommendation. To help FDA 
review comments efficiently, please identify the topic area to which 
you are responding by its associated number. If you are responding to 
more than one topic area, please identify each topic area to which you 
are responding:
    1. Fit-for-purpose review of rare disease therapies based on 
attainable evidence, particular disease state, and population.
    2. Consistency and predictability of FDA's rare disease drug 
development and review regulatory requirements.
    3. FDA engagements with external parties--including patients, 
disease experts, and researchers--to inform review decision-making for 
rare disease therapies.
    4. Considerations for chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC) 
specific to rare disease therapies.

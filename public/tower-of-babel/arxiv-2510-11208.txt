Donghyeon Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we define the notion of asymptotically flat divisor on a normal variety over ℂ \mathbb{C} , and prove that if X X is a strongly F F -regular type variety and K X K_{X} is asymptotically flat, then X X is of klt type.

2.2.
Valuation

In this subsection, we briefly recall basic notions from valuation theory. For background, see
[
JM12
]
,
[
Can20
]
, or
[
Xu25
, §1.2]
. We do not assume that
\operatorname{char}k=0
.

Let
X
be a normal variety over a field
k
. A
valuation
over the function field
k(X)
is a map

\nu:k(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

(a)

\nu(a)=0
for all
a\in k^{\times}
,

(b)

\nu(fg)=\nu(f)+\nu(g)
for all
f,g\in k(X)^{\times}
, and

(c)

\nu(f+g)\geq\min\{\nu(f),\nu(g)\}
for all
f,g\in k(X)
.

We extend
\nu
to
k(X)
by setting
\nu(0):=+\infty
.
We say a valuation
\nu
over
k(X)
is
centered
on
X
if there is an affine open subscheme
U\subseteq X
such that if we write
U=\operatorname{Spec}R
, then

R\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}:=\{f\in k(X)\,|\,\nu(f)\geq 0\}.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}:=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\,|\,\nu(f)>0\}
be the maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
. We denote the point given by the prime ideal
R\cap\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}
to be the
center

c_{X}(\nu)\subseteq X
. If a valuation
\nu
over
k(X)
is centered on
X
, then we say that
\nu
is a valuation
over

X
. Let
x\in X
be a (not necessarily closed) point, and define

\mathrm{Val}_{X}:=\{\nu\text{ is a valuation over }X\},

\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}:=\{\nu\text{ is a valuation over }X\text{ with }x=c_{X}(\nu)\},

and
\mathrm{Val}^{*}_{X}:=\mathrm{Val}_{X}\setminus\{0\}
.

If
E
is a prime divisor over
X
, then the order of vanishing

\operatorname{ord}_{E}:k(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}

is a valuation over
X
. For any
c>0
, a valuation of the form
c\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{E}
is called
divisorial
.

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a log resolution, and let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}
be the irreducible components of a reduced simple normal crossings divisor

E:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\subset X^{\prime}.

If
f
is an isomorphism over
X\setminus\bigcup_{i}f(E_{i})
, then
(X^{\prime},E)\to X
is called a
log smooth model
. Fix
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
and assume
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\neq\varnothing
. Let
C
be a connected component of
\bigcap_{i}E_{i}
with generic point
\eta
. Choose regular parameters
z_{1},\dots,z_{r}\in\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},\eta}
such that
E_{i}=(z_{i}=0)
for all
i
. For
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},\eta}
write

f=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}}c_{\beta}\,z^{\beta},\,\,z^{\beta}:=z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{\beta_{r}}.

Define

\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\alpha}(f):=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\ \Bigm|\ c_{\beta}\neq 0\right\}.

This gives a valuation on
k(X)
, independent of the choice of such parameters. All such valuations are called
quasi-monomial
. We denote by
\mathrm{QM}(X^{\prime},E)
the set of quasi-monomial valuations defined by
(X^{\prime},E)
, and by
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime},E)\subseteq\mathrm{QM}(X^{\prime},E)
those whose center equals
\eta
.

For a valuation
\nu
over
X
and an ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, set

\nu(\mathfrak{a}):=\min\{\nu(f)\mid f\in\mathfrak{a}_{x}\},\,\,x:=c_{X}(\nu).

If
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}
are coherent ideals, then

\nu(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})=\nu(\mathfrak{a})+\nu(\mathfrak{b}).

For any valuation
\nu
over
X
and for any ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}
on
X
, we define

\nu(\mathfrak{a}):=\min\{\nu(f)\,|\,f\in\mathfrak{a}_{x}\text{ where }x=c_{X}(\nu)\}.

Note that for any two ideals
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}
,

\nu(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})=\nu(\mathfrak{a})+\nu(\mathfrak{b})

We can give a topology on
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
as follows: the topology of
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is the weakest topology such that
\nu\mapsto\nu(\mathfrak{a})
is a continuous function for any valuation
\nu
over
X
.

2.4.
Log canonical threshold and Jonsson-Mustață conjecture

Let
X
be a normal variety,
\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
a semigroup, and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}:=\{\mathfrak{a}_{m}\}_{m\in\Phi}
be a set of ideals on
X
. We say
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is a
graded sequence of ideals
if

\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+n}\text{ for all }m,n\in\Phi.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair, let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}:=\left\{\mathfrak{a}_{m}\right\}_{m\in\Phi}
be a graded sequence of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We define the following:

\nu(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\inf_{m\in\Phi}\frac{\nu(\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m}\text{ for any }\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X},

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a}):=\inf_{\nu\in\mathrm{Val}^{*}_{X}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(\nu)}{\nu(\mathfrak{a})},

and

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\inf_{\nu\in\mathrm{Val}^{*}_{X}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(\nu)}{\nu(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}.

We say that a valuation
\nu
over
X

computes

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
if

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(\nu)}{\nu(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}.

The following is an important theorem regarding the log canonical threshold of a graded sequence of ideals. It states that the infimum defining the log canonical threshold is achieved by a specific type of valuation. We refer to this theorem as the
Jonsson-Mustață conjecture
, as it follows from
[
JM12
, Conjecture 7.4]
by setting
\mathfrak{q}=0
.

Theorem 2.6

(cf.
[
JM12
, Conjecture 7.4]
and
[

, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair, and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a graded sequence of ideals on
X
. Then there is a quasi-monomial valuation
\nu
that computes
\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
.

Let us recall the following lemma (cf.
[

[

).

Lemma 2.7

(cf.
[

, Lemma 6.1 and Corollary 6.4]
and
[

, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety over

, and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a graded sequence of ideals in

. Then the function

is upper-semicontinuous on

2.6.
Toroidal blowup

In this subsection, we define
toroidal blowup
and prove a lemma.

Let
\left(X^{\prime},E=\sum^{r}_{i=1}E_{i}\right)
be a couple of a regular scheme
X^{\prime}
and an snc divisor
E\subseteq X^{\prime}
. Let
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq r
. We say that the blowup of
X^{\prime}
along
\bigcap^{k}_{j=1}E_{i_{j}}
is a
toroidal blowup
. Note that any toroidal blowup is a regular scheme because it is a blowup of a regular scheme along a closed regular subscheme.

Let us prove the following lemma:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
X
be a normal variety over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\left(X^{\prime},E=\sum^{r}_{i=1}E_{i}\right)
be a log-smooth model over
X
, and
\eta
the generic point of a connected component of the intersection of
k
prime components of
E
. Then there exists a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
R
and models
X_{R}
and
\left(X^{\prime}_{R},E_{R}=\sum^{r}_{i=1}E_{iR}\right)
of
X
,
(X^{\prime},E)
over
R
respectively with the following property:

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{k}_{\geq 0}
, there is the composition of a sequence of toroidal blowups
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}_{R}\to X^{\prime}_{R}
, a prime divisor
F_{R}
on
X^{\prime\prime}_{R}
and a positive number
c>0
such that

\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\eta,\alpha}=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\nu_{(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}}),\eta_{\overline{s}},\alpha}=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F_{\overline{s}}}

for every closed point
s\in\operatorname{Spec}R
(Notation
2.9
).

Proof.

There is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
R
, and models
X_{R}
and
(X^{\prime}_{R},E_{R})
of
X
and
(X^{\prime},E)
over
R
respectively.

Note that for every
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}_{>0}
, there is the composition of a sequence of toroidal blowups
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}
and a prime component
F
of
\mathrm{Exc}(f^{\prime})\cup\operatorname{Supp}(f^{\prime})^{-1}_{*}E
such that there is a positive number
c>0
such that

\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\eta,\alpha}=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F}

(cf.
[
JM12
, Lemma 3.6 (ii)]
). Moreover, since
f^{\prime}
is the composition of a sequence of toroidal blowups, we obtain that there is a model
f^{\prime}_{R}:X^{\prime\prime}_{R}\to X^{\prime}_{R}
of
f^{\prime}
over
R
. Note that
\nu_{(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}}),\eta_{\overline{s}},\alpha}
can be characterized as follows:
\nu_{(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}}),\eta_{\overline{s}},\alpha}
is the divisorial valuation in
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}})
such that

\nu_{(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}}),\eta_{\overline{s}},\alpha}(E_{i\overline{s}})=\alpha_{i}.

Since
f^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}:X^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{s}}\to X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}}
is the composition of a sequence of toroidal blowups of
(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}})
,
c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F_{\overline{s}}}\in\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}})
. Moreover, since

c\cdot\mathrm{mult}_{F}((f^{\prime})^{*}E_{i})=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F}(E_{i})=\alpha_{i},

we obtain that
c\cdot\mathrm{mult}_{F_{R}}((f^{\prime}_{R})^{*}E_{iR})=\alpha_{i}
, and therefore

c\cdot\mathrm{mult}_{F_{\overline{s}}}((f^{\prime}_{\overline{s}})^{*}E_{i\overline{s}})=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F_{\overline{s}}}(E_{i\overline{s}})=\alpha_{i}.

Hence,

\nu_{(X^{\prime}_{\overline{s}},E_{\overline{s}}),\eta_{\overline{s}},\alpha}=c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{F_{\overline{s}}}

as we want.
∎

3.
Natural valuation and natural pullback

In this section, we introduce the notion of
natural valuation
and
natural pullback
, and collect basic lemmas about them. We keep the standing notation of Notation
2.9
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a normal variety, and let
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
be a valuation over
X
. Suppose
D
is a Weil divisor on
X
. We define

\nu^{\natural}(D):=\nu^{\natural}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)).

Moreover, we define

\nu(D):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\nu^{\natural}(m!D).

Note that the limit exists by
[
dFH09
, Lemma 2.8]
. If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor, then we define

\nu(D):=\frac{1}{m}\nu(mD),

where
m
is a positive integer with
mD
Weil.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
D
be a Cartier divisor. Then
\nu^{\natural}(D)
coincides with the notion of order of vanishing of
D
along
\nu
.

For any two Weil divisors
D,D^{\prime}
on
X
, we have

\nu^{\natural}(D+D^{\prime})\leq\nu^{\natural}(D)+\nu^{\natural}(D^{\prime}).

Indeed, let
U\subseteq X
be an open subscheme. If
\phi\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)(U)
and
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D^{\prime})(U)
, then
\mathrm{div}\,\phi\geq D
and
\mathrm{div}\,\phi^{\prime}\geq D^{\prime}
on
U
, and therefore
\mathrm{div}\,(\phi\cdot\phi^{\prime})\geq D+D^{\prime}
. Thus,
\phi\cdot\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D-D^{\prime})(U)
, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}(-(D+D^{\prime}))
. The equality holds if either
D
or
D^{\prime}
is Cartier (cf.
[
dFH09
, Proposition 2.10]
).

For any two
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisors
D,D^{\prime}
on
X
,

\nu(D+D^{\prime})\leq\nu(D)+\nu(D^{\prime}),

and the equality holds if either
D
or
D^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Unfortunately, the equality does not hold in general. See
[
dFH09
, Example 2.12]
.

We define the notion of
natural pullback
, which serves as a version of pullback in the case of a Weil divisor.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism between two normal varieties, and let
D
be a Weil divisor on
X
. We define
f^{\natural}D
as the Weil divisor on
X^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}D)=(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})^{\vee\vee}.

Moreover, we define

f^{*}D:=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m!}f^{*}(m!D)

as an
\mathbb{R}
-Weil divisor on
X^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let us collect basic facts about the natural pullback.

(a)

From the natural map
f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to(f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D))^{\vee\vee}
and through adjunction, there is an induced natural map
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D))^{\vee\vee}
. Additionally, via the multiplication map

f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D):=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}

inside
k(X^{\prime})
, another natural map arises:
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}D)
.

(b)

Given two linearly equivalent Weil divisors
D
and
D^{\prime}
on
X
, there exists an isomorphism
\imath:\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D^{\prime})
. Consequently, we have an isomorphism

\imath\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}:\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D^{\prime})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}.

By taking the double dual on both sides, we conclude that
f^{\natural}D\sim f^{\natural}D^{\prime}
, demonstrating that the natural pullback preserves linear equivalence.

(c)

We can write
f^{\natural}D
as follows:

f^{\natural}D=\sum_{E\text{ prime ideal on }X^{\prime}}\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(D)E.

Moreover, if
E
is a prime divisor on
X^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(D)=\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(f^{\natural}D)
. In fact,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(D)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{E}((\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})^{\vee\vee})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f^{\natural}D))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(f^{\natural}D).

Note that if
E
is just a prime divisor over
X^{\prime}
, not on
X^{\prime}
, then the statement is false. See
[
dFH09
, Example 2.12]
.

(d)

Unfortunately, because of the double dual, natural pullback lacks many features of functoriality of
f
. For example, we do not have
(f\circ g)^{\natural}D=g^{\natural}f^{\natural}D
, where
g:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}
is a proper birational morphism between normal varieties. Instead of that, we have
(f\circ g)^{\natural}D-g^{\natural}f^{\natural}D
is effective
g
-exceptional. Moreover, if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
is a line bundle, then
(f\circ g)^{\natural}D=g^{\natural}f^{\natural}D
(cf.
[
dFH09
, Remark 2.13]
and
[
CEMS18
, Remark 2.12]
).

(e)

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a small morphism. Then
f^{\natural}D
is the strict transform of
D
, and thus

f^{-1}_{*}D=f^{\natural}D=\frac{1}{m!}f^{\natural}(m!D)=f^{*}D

for every positive integer
m
(cf.
[
CEMS18
, Remark 2.12]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism between two normal varieties, and let
D
be a Weil divisor on
X
. Then the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}D)
constructed in Remark
3.4
(a) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is the same as
[
Kim25a
, Lemma 3.6]
, and we recall the proof for the convenience of the readers.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the cokernel of the natural morphism. Then we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}D)\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

Let
x
be a codimension
\geq 2
point in
X
. Note that
H^{1}_{x}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D))=0
since
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
is a reflexive sheaf on
X
. By taking local cohomology, we obtain
H^{0}_{x}(X,\mathcal{Q})=0
. Note that
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{Q}\geq 2
. Hence,
\mathcal{Q}=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be an affine normal variety, and let
A
be a Weil divisor on
X
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))
is globally generated in codimension
1
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)
is globally generated. By definition of global generation, there are
s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(A))
that induce a surjection

\bigoplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(A),\,(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r})\mapsto\sum s_{i}f_{i}.

Taking
f^{*}
and considering the multiplication map
f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
gives us a surjection

\bigoplus\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}},\,(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r})\mapsto\sum s_{i}f_{i}.

Moreover, since
X^{\prime}
is normal, there is an open subscheme
U\subseteq X^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{codim}_{X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}\setminus U)\geq 2
,
and in fact,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}|_{U}
is globally generated, and thus its double dual,
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))
is globally generated in codimension
1
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In general,
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))
is not globally generated. For example, let
X:=(xy=zw)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{4}
, and let
A:=(x=z=0)\subseteq X
. Then the blowup of
X
along
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-A)
gives us a small resolution
f:X^{\prime}\to X
, and
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))|_{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
is not globally generated.

Moreover, if we replace
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))
with
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(f^{\natural}A)
, then in general, Lemma
3.6
does not hold. Indeed, let
B:=(x=w=0)\subseteq X
, let
X^{\prime\prime}
be the blowup of
X
along
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-B)
, and let
X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}
be the Atiyah flop. Let
g:X^{\prime\prime\prime}\to X
be the blowup of
X
at the origin, and let
E
be the exceptional divisor on
X^{\prime\prime\prime}
. Define by
H\subseteq X^{\prime}
a general hyperplane section, and
D
the strict transform of
H
to
X
. Then, as computed in
[
dFH09
, Example 2.12]
, we obtain
\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(D)>0
, whereas
\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(-D)=0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}(g^{\natural}D)
is not globally generated in codimension
1
.

We can think that the following lemma is a kind of tie-breaking argument for Weil divisors.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X
be an affine normal variety,
f:X^{\prime}\to X
a proper birational morphism,
E
a prime divisor on
X
, and
A
a Weil divisor on
X
. Then for a general effective divisor
D\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(A))
, we obtain
\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(-D)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A))
is globally generated in codimension
1
by Lemma
3.6
, we obtain that for a general effective divisor
D^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A)))
such that

\mathrm{ord}^{\natural}_{E}(-D^{\prime})=0.

We define
D:=f_{*}D^{\prime}
.

Let us assert that
D^{\prime}=-f^{\natural}(-D)
. In fact, the map that comes from Remark
3.4
(a)

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(A))\to H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A)))

is an isomorphism by (a) of Lemma
3.5
, the map is
D^{\prime\prime}\mapsto-f^{\natural}(-D^{\prime\prime})
, and the inverse is
D^{\prime\prime\prime}\mapsto f_{*}D^{\prime\prime\prime}
for any
D^{\prime\prime}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(A))
and any
D^{\prime\prime\prime}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-f^{\natural}(-A)))
respectively. Thus, the claim is established.

Consequently, referring to Remark
3.4
(c), we have

\mathrm{ord}_{E}(-D)=0,

and this is the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

By the reason of Remark
3.7
, we only get
\ord^{\natural}_{E}(D)\geq 0
, and we cannot hope
\ord^{\natural}_{E}(D)=0
.

Let us prove the following version of the Negativity lemma. Roughly, the lemma is a combination of
[

and

,

an snc divisor on

, and assume that the exceptional locus of

-algebra

and

of

(X^{\prime},E)
respectively such that for each

(a)

for the composition of every sequence of toroidal blowups
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}
of

, there are curves
C_{1,X^{\prime\prime}},\cdots,C_{n_{X^{\prime\prime}},X^{\prime\prime}}

(b)

for every divisor

Here,

Proof.

Since the lemma is local, we may assume

Let us construct

of

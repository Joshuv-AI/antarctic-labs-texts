Abstract
. Claude Chevalley provided a basis for a finite dimensional simple complex Lie algebra called the Chevalley basis. This basis has the distinguishing property that all the structure constants are integers. Chevalley groups, which are similar to Lie groups but over finite fields, can be constructed using these bases. Parallel results also hold in affine Lie algebras. We develop a uniform theory of Chevalley bases for extended affine Lie algebras of an arbitrary type consistent with the ordinary theory for finite and affine cases. It explains how a Chevalley basis for a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra or an affine Lie algebra can be extended to one for the covering extended affine Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

Chevalley systems were introduced in 1955 by Claude Chevalley
[
16
]
as a means of constructing
Chevalley groups which resemble Lie groups but with the ground field of real or complex numbers replaced by an arbitrary field. This led to the discovery of
new families of finite simple groups corresponding to the exceptional Lie groups of
types
E_{7}
and
E_{8}
. A rough idea of the involved procedure is as follows:
Consider a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over the field
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers, and assume that
\mathfrak{g}
possesses a basis
{\mathcal{B}}
with integer structure constants. Form the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{B}}
. Then one associates a Chevalley group to the
{\mathbb{F}}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{F}}:=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{F}}
,
{\mathbb{F}}
a finite field.
The basis
{\mathcal{B}}
called a Chevalley basis plays a crucial role in this procedure. This, in turn, leads to finding an integral structure for the corresponding universal enveloping algebra as is illustrated in
[
16
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
14
]
, or
[
18
]
. It is worth noting that the same procedure applies if
\mathfrak{g}
is an affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra as detailed in
[
22
]
, or more generally if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Kac-Moody Lie algebra as shown in
[
21
]
.

In this work, we propose a systematic study of Chevalley bases for Extended affine Lie algebras.
Extended affine Lie algebras constitute a class of primarily infinite-dimensional Lie algebras whose defining axioms represent natural extensions of fundamental properties of finite-dimensional and affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras, see
[
1
]
and
2.2.1
. The structure theory of an extended affine Lie algebra is highly encoded in a specific ideal termed the core, and more precisely, in its centerless core, as reported in §
2.3
. In this work, by an extended affine Lie algebra we mean a one which is tame and reduced.

In finite-dimensional and affine cases, the existence of Chevalley bases is tied to that of Chevalley involutions - period
2
automorphisms which act as minus identity on a Cartan subalgebra.The investigation of integral structures for extended affine Lie algebras of rank
>1
began in 2022, where the authors built Chevalley structures for the cores of these algebras, assuming the presence of Chevalley involutions, see
[
5
]
.
The existence of Chevalley involutions was investigated in
[
6
]
, and it was shown that almost all extended affine Lie algebras admit Chevalley involutions. The rank
1
case is investigated in
[
4
]
.
A theoretical framework is, therefore, necessary to study Chevalley bases and Chevalley involutions for extended affine Lie algebras, as well as for (centerless) Lie tori, which characterize the centerless cores of extended affine Lie algebras. Investigating the interrelations between these concepts is also of particular interest such as whether an integral structure for the centerless core can be lifted to the core and, in turn, to the entire extended affine Lie algebra. Our approach is unified and is not type-dependent.
It’s worth noting that even for the affine case, the approach has been type-dependent, including the definition of a Chevalley base, as seen in Definitions 2.1.17, 2.2.25, 3.4.10 and explanations given on page 156 of Appendix of
[
22
]
.
The content of the paper is explained below.

Preliminaries

Throughout this work, it is assumed that
{\mathbb{K}}
is the field of complex numbers, and all vector spaces are considered to be over
{\mathbb{K}}
. We set
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}={\mathbb{K}}\setminus\{0\}
.
We denote the dual space of a vector space
{\mathcal{H}}
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
. The notation

means
the union of two disjoint sets

2.1.
Lie tori

We fix a free abelian group
\Lambda
of finite rank, and fix an irreducible finite root system
\Delta
with root lattice
Q=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\Delta
.

By definition (see
[
28
]
), a
Lie torus of type

(\Delta,\Lambda)
is a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
such that the following conditions hold:

-
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in Q\times\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
is a
(Q\times\Lambda)
-graded Lie algebra with
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=0
if
\alpha\not\in\Delta,

- for
\alpha\in\Delta^{\times}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\leq 1
, with
\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0}=1
if
\frac{1}{2}\alpha\not\in\Delta
,

- if
\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=1
then there exist elements
0\not=e_{\pm\alpha}^{\pm\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{\pm\alpha}^{\pm\lambda}
such that

(2.1)

[[e_{\alpha}^{\lambda},e_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}],x^{\mu}_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle x_{\beta}^{\mu},\quad(\beta\in\Delta,\;\mu\in\Lambda,\;x_{\beta}^{\mu}\in\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\mu}),

where
\alpha^{\vee}
is the
coroot of
\alpha
, and
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
is the corresponding Cartan integer,

- for
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times},\mu\in\Lambda}[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\mu},\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{\lambda-\mu}]
,

-
\Lambda=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\neq 0\;\text{for some}\;\alpha\in\Delta\}
.

The Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
is called
centerless
if
\mathcal{L}
has trivial center.
The rank of
\Lambda
is called the
nullity
of
\mathcal{L}
. We have a natural
\Lambda
-grading
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
where

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in Q}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
. We also have a
Q
-grading

\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
where

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda},
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\alpha\in Q
.

2.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie torus of type
(\Delta,\Lambda)
of nullity
n
and let
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{L})
denote the centroid of
\mathcal{L}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{\mu},

where
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Lambda
,
\chi^{\mu}
acts on
\mathcal{L}
as an endomorphism of degree
\mu
and
\chi^{\mu}\chi^{\nu}=\chi^{\mu+\nu}
.

Set

\mathcal{D}:=\{\partial_{\theta}\mid\theta\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})\},

where the derivation
\partial_{\theta}
of
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\partial_{\theta}(x^{\lambda})=\theta(\lambda)x^{\lambda}

for
\lambda\in\Lambda,x^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}.

Then

\text{CDer}(\mathcal{L}):={\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{L})\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\mathcal{D},

called the algebra of
centroidal derivations
of
\mathcal{L}
, is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of the derivation algebra

\text{Der}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
with

(2.2)

[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\psi}]=\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta(\nu)\partial_{\psi}-\psi(\mu)\partial_{\theta}).

2.1.2
.

We fix a non-degenerate invariant
\Lambda
-graded bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathcal{L}
. The existence of such a form is insured by
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
. The
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra.

\displaystyle\text{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{d\in\text{CDer}(\mathcal{L})\mid(d(x),x)_{\mathcal{L}}=0\;\text{for all}\;x\in\mathcal{L}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\text{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\theta(\mu)=0\},

of
\hbox{CDer}(\mathcal{L})
is called the algebra of

of
\mathcal{L}
. Note that

The graded dual

of

. The graded dual

is considered as a

-module by the contragredient action, i.e.

(d.\varphi)(d^{{}^{\prime}})=\varphi([d^{{}^{\prime}},d])\>\text{for}\;d^{{}^{\prime}},d\in D,\varphi\in D^{gr*},

where

2.2.
Extended affine Lie algebras and root systems

We provide a brief review of basic facts about extended affine Lie algebras. For a comprehensive study of these algebras the reader is referred to
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
.

2.2.1
.

An
extended affine Lie algebra
is a triple
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
satisfying the following 6 axioms:

(A1)
E
is a Lie algebra, and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric invariant and non-degenerate bilinear form on
E
.

(A2)
{\mathcal{H}}
is a non-trivial finite-dimensional Cartan splitting subalgebra of
E
, i.e.,

E=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}}E_{\alpha}
with

E_{\alpha}=\{x\in E\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}\},

and
E_{0}={\mathcal{H}}.

The set

R=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}\mid E_{\alpha}\not=\{0\}\}
, is called the
root system
of
E
.
For
\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
, let
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}
be the unique element such that
\alpha(h)=(h,t_{\alpha})
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. The existence of
t_{\alpha}
is guaranteed since the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to
{\mathcal{H}}
is non-degenerate, by (A1)-(A2). We may then transfer the form on
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
by
(\alpha,\beta):=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
. This transfer allows us to decompose
R
as the union of
isotropic
and
non-isotropic roots
, i.e.,
R=R^{0}\uplus R^{\times}
, where

R^{0}=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}
and
R^{\times}=R\setminus R^{0}
.
The subalgebra
E_{c}
of
E
generated by non-isotropic root spaces, is called the
core
of
E
.

We now are able to define the renaming axioms.

(A3)
\hbox{ad}x
acts as a locally nilpotent endomorphism on
E
, for each
x\in E_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

(A4)
E
is
tame
, that is, the centralizer of
E_{c}
in
E
is contained in
E_{c}
.

(A5) The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
R
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
is a free abelian group of finite rank.

(A6) If
R^{\times}=R_{1}\uplus R_{2}
with
R_{1}\not=\emptyset
and
R_{2}\not=\emptyset,
then
R_{1}\not\perp R_{2}
.

As part of the axioms of an extended affine Lie algebra, we have included the ”tameness” condition, despite it not being present in the original definition, see
[
1
, Definition I.1.33]
.

2.2.2
.

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
, or simply
E
, be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
.
Set
{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R
,
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{0}
,
\bar{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{V}}/{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, and
\bar{\;}:{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{V}}
be the canonical map. The image
\bar{R}
of
R
in
\bar{\mathcal{V}}
is then an irreducible finite root system whose type and rank is called the
type
and the
rank
of
R
, or
E
, respectively. The dimension of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
is called the
nullity
of
R
, or
E
. Throughout this work,
we assume that
R
is of reduced type
, that is we exclude the type
BC
. We may find an appropriate pre-image
\dot{R}
in
{\mathcal{V}}
for
\bar{R}
, such that
\dot{R}
is an irreducible finite root system
in
\dot{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{R}
, isomorphic to
\bar{R}
.

2.3.
Construction of extended affine Lie algebras from Lie tori

Here we briefly recall a construction of extended affine Lie algebras due to E. Neher
[
25
]
. We proceed with the same notions as above.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A subalgebra
D
of
\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
is called
permissible
, if
D=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of
\text{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
, such that

(i) the canonical evaluation map
\text{ev}:\Lambda\rightarrow(D^{0})^{\star}
defined by

\text{ev}(\lambda)(\partial_{\theta})=\theta(\lambda),\;\lambda\in\Lambda,

is invective and has discrete image.

(ii) there exists a bilinear map
\kappa:D\times D\rightarrow D^{gr*}
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa(d,d)=0,\;\;{\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k})=\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})},\\
\kappa(D^{\mu_{1}},D^{\mu_{2}})\subseteq(D^{-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}})^{\star}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\kappa(d_{1},d_{2})(d_{3})=\kappa(d_{2},d_{3})(d_{1}),\\
\kappa(D^{0},D)=0,\end{array}

for
d,d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\ D
.
Here by
(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft
, we mean that
(i,j,k)
is a cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
. The map
\kappa
is called an
affine cocycle
.

2.3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus,
D
be a permissible subalgebra of

\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
, and
\kappa
be the corresponding affine cocycle. The space

E=E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa):=\mathcal{L}\oplus D^{gr*}\oplus D

is a Lie algebra with the bracket given by

\displaystyle[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathcal{L}}+d_{1}(x_{2})-d_{2}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{1}.c_{2}-d_{2}.c_{1}+\kappa(d_{1},d_{2}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}],

for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{L},c_{1},c_{2}\in D^{gr*},d_{1},d_{2}\in D
, where
[\>,\>]_{\mathcal{L}}
denotes the Lie bracket of
\mathcal{L}
,
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}d_{2}-d_{2}d_{1}
, and
\sigma_{D}:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow D^{gr*}
is defined by

\sigma_{D}(x,y)(d)=(d(x)|y)\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in\mathcal{L},d\in D.

In fact
\sigma_{D}
is a cocycle for
\mathcal{L}
with values in the trivial
\mathcal{L}
-module
D^{gr*}
with respect to the gradings of
\mathcal{L}
and
D^{gr*}
. Moreover, the symmetric bilinear form on
E
given by

(2.3)

(x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})_{\mathcal{L}}+c_{1}(d_{2})+c_{2}(d_{1})

is non-degenerate and invariant.

Theorem 2.3.3
.

[
24
, Theorem 16]

For a centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
and a permissible subalgebra
D
of
\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
with corresponding affine cocycle
\kappa
, the algebra
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
is an extended affine Lie algebra with respect to the form (
2.3
), and the Cartan subalgebra

{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}\oplus(D^{0})^{\star}\oplus D^{0}
. Moreover,
E_{c}=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}
. Furthermore, any extended affine Lie algebra
E
is isomorphic
to an extended affine Lie algebra
E=(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
for some
\mathcal{L}
,
D
and
\kappa
.

Remark 2.3.4
.

The last claim appearing in the statement of Theorem
2.3.3
amounts to the following fact. Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. Then
R=(\Delta+\Lambda)\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
, where
\Delta
is an irreducible finite root system and
\Lambda

where

. Then

is a centerless Lie torus of type
(\Delta,\Lambda)

2.4.
Index zero extended affine root systems

We provide a brief overview of the concept of index for an extended affine root system. It is known that extended affine Lie algebras or root systems with index zero share more similarities with affine Lie algebras when compared to those with index

, see for example
[

2.4.1
.

Recall that
{\mathcal{V}}=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}R

{\mathcal{V}}
. Set
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}:={\mathcal{V}}\oplus({\mathcal{V}}^{0})^{\star}

Then

3.
Chevalley systems for the core

Throughout this section, we assume that
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. As before, we denote the core of
E
by
E_{c}
. By a
Chevalley involution
for
E
, we mean a finite order automorphism
\tau
such that
\tau(E_{\alpha})=E_{-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R
. Similarly, a
Chevalley involution
for
E_{c}
is a finite order automorphism
\tau
of
E_{c}
with
\tau(E_{\alpha})=E_{-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

3.1.
Chevalley systems and Chevalley bases

Chevalley systems have been extensively studied in the context of finite-dimensional Lie theory. However, as one can see from
[
5
]
the situation in the case of general extended affine Lie theory is remarkably different. Specifically, for a non-isotropic root
\alpha
, we note that the subalgebra
E_{\alpha}\oplus[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]\oplus E_{-\alpha}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}({\mathbb{K}})
, which allows us to nominate a set of root vectors
{\mathcal{C}}
as a Chevalley system associated with non-isotropic root spaces. In the event that the sum of two roots associated with vectors in
{\mathcal{C}}
lies in
R^{\times}
, the analysis of the structure constants of the commutators is parallel to that of finite and affine theory. However, if the sum is an isotropic root, the analysis is generally more subtle. In the affine case, this can be handled by relying on the well-established realization of affine Lie algebras.
With regard to extended affine Lie algebras of nullity greater than
1
, the inspection is generally quite delicate, largely due to the lack of a realization theory. Interested readers are referred to
[
5
]
for a detailed study of the structure constants of the commutators.

To proceed with establishing a theory for the study of integral structures for extended affine Lie algebras, we provide some relevant terminologies. Recall from
2.2.1
that for
\alpha\in R
,
t_{\alpha}
is the unique element in
{\mathcal{H}}
which represents
\alpha
via the from
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Considering this, we set for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,

(3.1)

h_{\alpha}=\frac{2t_{\alpha}}{(\alpha,\alpha)}.

We now give a related definition, see
[
14
, VIII.§2, Definition 3]
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for
E_{c}
. We call a set
\mathcal{C}=\{x_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
a
Chevalley system
for
(E_{c},\tau)
if

(i)
[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}
,

(ii)
\tau(x_{\alpha})=-x_{-\alpha}
,

for
\alpha\in R^{\times}.

3.1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\{x_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
be a Chevalley system for
(E_{c},\tau)
.
For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, we define

(3.2)

n_{\alpha}=\exp(\hbox{ad}x_{\alpha})\exp(-\hbox{ad}(x_{{}_{\alpha}}))\exp(\hbox{ad}x_{\alpha}).

From
[
1
, I.§1]
, we see that
n_{\alpha}\in\hbox{Aut}(E)
satisfies
n_{\alpha}(E_{\beta})=E_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}
,
\beta\in R
, where

w_{\alpha}
is the reflection based on
\alpha
defined in
2.4.1
. We also see that
n_{\alpha}(h_{\beta})=h_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}
(
[
1
, I.§1]
). Therefore, if

\beta\in R^{\times}
, we have
n_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})=kx_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}
, and

n_{\alpha}(x_{-\beta})=k^{-1}x_{-w_{\alpha}(\beta)}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Now since
for any automorphism
\theta
of
E_{c}
we have
\theta\exp(\hbox{ad}x_{\alpha})\theta^{-1}=\exp(\hbox{ad}\theta(x_{\alpha}))
, we see that

{n_{\alpha}}_{|_{E_{c}}}
commutes with
\tau
, see the proof of Lemma
[
17
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Remark 3.1.3
.

(i) Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for
E_{c}
.
Take
R^{+}
such that
R^{\times}=R^{+}\cup(-R^{+})
. For
\alpha\in R^{+}
, pick
0\not=x^{\prime}_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}
.
Set

x^{\prime}_{-\alpha}:=-\tau(x^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\quad x_{\alpha}:=c_{\alpha}x^{\prime}_{\alpha},\quad x_{-\alpha}:=-\tau(x_{\alpha}),

where
c_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{2}{(\alpha,\alpha)(x^{\prime}_{\alpha},x^{\prime}_{-\alpha})}.

Then
\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
is a Chevalley system for
(E_{c},\tau)
.

(ii) Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}=\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
are two Chevalley systems for
(E_{c},\tau)
and
(E,\bar{\tau})
, respectively. Then
\Psi=\tau\bar{\tau}
stabilizes each non-isotropic root space, and so for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
0\not=x_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}
, we have

\Psi(x_{\alpha})=\eta_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
, for some
\eta_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Since
h_{\alpha}=[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]
, we get
\eta_{\alpha}^{-1}=\eta_{-\alpha}
.
Also if
\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}
, we get
\eta_{\alpha+\beta}=\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}.

Now if
\bar{x}_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
,
\mu_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}
, then

\eta_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha}=\Psi(\bar{x}_{\alpha})=\tau\bar{\tau}(\bar{x}_{\alpha})=\tau(-\bar{x}_{-\alpha})=\mu_{-\alpha}x_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}^{-2}\mu_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}^{-2}\bar{x}_{\alpha},

and so

(3.3)

\eta_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}^{-2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mu_{\alpha+\beta}=\pm\mu_{\alpha}\mu_{\beta},\quad(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}).

If
\tau=\bar{\tau}
, then
\Psi=\hbox{id}
and so
\mu_{\alpha}\in\{\pm 1\}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

Suppose the set
{\mathcal{C}}=\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
satisfies:

(3.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\hbox{- }x_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha},\\
\hbox{- }[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]=h_{\alpha},\\
\hbox{- }\hbox{if }\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}\hbox{ and }[x_{\alpha},x_{\beta}]=N_{\alpha,\beta}x_{\alpha+\beta},\hbox{ then }N_{-\alpha,-\beta}=-N_{\alpha,\beta}.\end{array}

One knows that in the finite-dimensional case the assignment
x_{\alpha}\mapsto-x_{-\alpha}
induces a Chevalley involution
\tau
on
E
such that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a Chevalley system for
(E=E_{c},\tau)
.
The following proposition shows that the same holds for the affine case.

Definition 3.1.5
.

Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for

. An

is a subset

of

for

if

is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for

.

In this subsection, we assume that rank

Let
{\mathcal{B}}

By (C3) the set

Theorem 3.2.5
.

(Uniqueness Theorem)
Assume
\hbox{rank}\;E>1
. If
X=B_{\ell}
, assume further that
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
be two Chevalley systems for
E_{c}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{B}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{B}}
are the corresponding integral structures on
E_{c}
given by Proposition
3.2.3
. Then as Lie algebras over
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
and
\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in R^{0}
, and
\Pi
be as in the proof of
Proposition
3.2.3
. For

\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}
and
\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}
respectively. Therefore the assignments

\begin{array}[]{c}h_{\alpha}\mapsto h_{\alpha},\;h_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{0},\quad x_{\alpha}\mapsto\mu_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha},\alpha\in R^{\times},\\

\Psi:\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}.
We proceed to show that
\Psi
is a Lie algebra homomorphism over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

First, let
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
with
\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}
. We have

By restricting to the irreducible finite

\displaystyle[\Psi(x_{\alpha}),\Psi(x_{\beta})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}\mu_{\beta}[\bar{x}_{\alpha},\bar{x}_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}\mu_{\beta}\bar{N}_{\alpha,\beta}\bar{x}_{\alpha+\beta}=\mu_{\alpha+\beta}N_{\alpha,\beta}\bar{x}_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi[x_{\alpha},x_{\beta}].

We now consider brackets of the form

,
y_{\alpha}\in E_{\alpha}\cap{\mathcal{B}}
,
y_{\beta}\in E_{\beta}\cap{\mathcal{B}}
, where at least one of

\begin{array}[]{c}\Psi(x^{\alpha}_{\sigma})=\Psi[x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}]=[\Psi(x_{\alpha+\sigma}),\Psi(x_{-\alpha})]=\mu_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\sigma},\\
(\sigma\in R^{0},\;\alpha,\;\alpha+\sigma\in R^{\times}).\end{array}

Let
\sigma,\alpha

3.2.3
,

is in the

. So,

,

\displaystyle\Psi[x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{\alpha}_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\Psi(x^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\mu^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma}\bar{x}^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(\mu^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma})^{2}x^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle(\hbox{by Remark \ref{nnew2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{\sigma}^{\alpha})^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma}

On the other hand

\displaystyle[\Psi(x_{\alpha+\sigma}),\Psi(x_{-\alpha})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha+\sigma}\mu_{-\alpha}[\bar{x}_{\alpha+\sigma},\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma})^{2}[x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma})^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}[x_{\alpha_{i}+\sigma},x_{-\alpha_{i}}]

\displaystyle(\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma})^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}_{\sigma}.

Therefore, (
3.10
) is verified.

Next, we consider brackets of the form
[x_{\beta},x_{\alpha}]
,
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
,
\alpha+\beta\in R^{0}
.
Set
\sigma:=\beta+\alpha
. Then

\displaystyle\Psi[x_{\beta},x_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi[x_{-\alpha+\sigma},x_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{-\alpha}_{\sigma})

\displaystyle(\hbox{by (\ref{lam1})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{-\alpha}_{\sigma}\bar{x}^{-\alpha}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu_{-\alpha+\sigma}\bar{x}_{-\alpha+\sigma},\mu_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Psi(x_{\beta}),\Psi(x_{\alpha})].

Now, we check brackets of the form
[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]
,
\sigma\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
. By Proposition
3.2.3
, there exist integers

[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]=kx_{\beta+\sigma}
and
[\bar{x}_{\beta},\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]=\bar{k}\bar{x}_{\beta+\sigma}

\bar{k}\mu_{\beta+\sigma}x_{\beta+\sigma}=\bar{k}\bar{x}_{\beta+\sigma}=[\bar{x}_{\beta},\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]=\mu_{\beta}\mu_{\sigma}^{\alpha}[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]=\mu_{\beta}\mu_{\sigma}^{\alpha}kx_{\beta+\sigma},

which gives

\displaystyle k\mu_{\beta+\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\mu_{\beta}\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\mu_{-\beta}\mu^{-\alpha}_{-\sigma}\mu_{\beta+\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{k}\mu_{\beta+\sigma}\mu_{-\beta}\mu^{-\alpha}_{-\sigma}\mu_{\beta+\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{k}(\mu^{\beta}_{\sigma})^{2}\mu_{\beta}(\mu^{-\alpha}_{-\sigma})^{2}\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle(\hbox{by Lemma \ref{nnew2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{k}\mu_{\beta}\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma}.

Thus

\displaystyle\Psi[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]=\Psi(kx_{\beta+\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\mu_{\beta+\sigma}\bar{x}_{\beta+\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{k}\mu_{\beta}\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma}\bar{x}_{\beta+\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\beta}\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha}[\bar{x}_{\beta},\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Psi(x_{\beta}),\Psi(x^{\alpha}_{\sigma})].

For brackets of the form

,

\displaystyle\Psi([x^{\alpha}_{\sigma},x^{\beta}_{\delta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi([[x_{\alpha+\sigma},x_{-\alpha}],x^{\beta}_{\delta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(-[[x^{\beta}_{\delta},x_{\alpha+\sigma}],x_{-\alpha}]-[[x_{-\alpha},x^{\beta}_{\delta}],x_{\alpha+\sigma}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[\Psi(x^{\beta}_{\delta}),\Psi(x_{\alpha+\sigma})],\Psi(x_{-\alpha})]-[[\Psi(x_{-\alpha}),\Psi(x^{\beta}_{\delta})],\Psi(x_{\alpha+\sigma})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu^{\beta}_{\delta}\mu_{\alpha+\sigma}\mu_{-\alpha}([[\bar{x}^{\beta}_{\delta},\bar{x}_{\alpha+\sigma})],\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]-[[\bar{x}_{-\alpha},\bar{x}^{\beta}_{\delta}],\bar{x}_{\alpha+\sigma}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{\beta}_{\delta}\mu_{\alpha+\sigma}\mu_{-\alpha}[\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\sigma},\bar{x}^{\beta}_{\delta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Psi(x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}),\Psi(x^{\beta}_{\delta}].

The remaining brackets are easy to check.

4.
Chevalley bases for extended affine Lie algebras

In the present section, the notion of a Chevalley basis for an extended affine Lie algebra
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
with root system
R
is introduced and the potential of extending a pre-existing Chevalley basis for the core
E_{c}
to that of
E
is explored. By combining several results in this work, we show that almost all extended affine Lie algebras admit Chevalley bases.

For a subset
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq E
, we set
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}:={\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{c}
. We denote by
\tau_{c}
, the restriction of an automorphism
\tau
on
E
to
E_{c}
.

4.1.
Integral structures for extended affine Lie algebras

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for
E
. We call a subset
{\mathcal{B}}
of
E
an
integral structure
or
a Chevalley structure
for
(E,\tau)
if (CB1)-(CB5) below hold:

(CB1)
{\mathcal{B}}
spans
E
and consists of root vectors,

(CB2)
\tau({\mathcal{B}})=-{\mathcal{B}}
,

(CB3) for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,
[{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{\alpha},{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{-\alpha}]=\{h_{\alpha}\}
,

(CB4) for
\sigma\in R^{0}
,
\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{B}}_{c}\cap E_{\sigma})=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{\alpha+\sigma},{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{-\alpha}],

(CB5)
[{\mathcal{B}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{c},{\mathcal{B}}]\subseteq\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}.

We call an integral structure for
(E,\tau)
a
Chevalley basis
for
(E,\tau)
if
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
E
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{B}}
is an integral structure for
(E,\tau)
. Since by (CB3),
{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
is singleton, we see from (CB1)-(CB3) that
\{{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}
is a Chevalley system for
(E_{c},\tau_{c})
. Then (CB1)-(CB4) imply that
{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{c}
is an integral structure for
(E_{c},\tau_{c})
.

Example 4.1.3
.

Suppose
E
is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and
{\mathcal{B}}
is a Chevalley structure for
E
. Then we have no non-zero isotropic root and so conditions (CB4) and (CB5) are surplus. Thus the notion of a Chevalley basis given in Definition
3.1.5
coincides with the standard concept of a Chevalley basis for finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras (by changing
x_{\alpha}
to
-x_{\alpha}
for any negative root), see for example
[
18
, Chapter VII]
. Also the Chevalley bases for affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras given in
[
22
]
are Chevalley bases in the sense of Definition
3.1.5
.

Corollary 4.1.4
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}
is an integral structure for
(E,\tau)
then
E^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{B}}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form of
E
.

Proof.
From (CB1) we see that
E^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{K}}\cong E
. From Remark
4.1.2
and Proposition
3.2.2
, we see that
{\mathcal{B}}\cap E_{c}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form of
E_{c}
. Now the result follows from this and (CB5).
\Box

4.2.

to

of an extended affine Lie algebra to

. We follow the notations of Subsections 2.2 and 2.3.

Let

,

In this section, we assume that

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus of type
(\Delta,\Lambda)
,

D

\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
, and

\chi^{-\mu}D^{\mu}=\chi^{\mu}D^{-\mu}\quad(\mu\in\Gamma),

for

\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta})=-\chi^{-\mu}\partial_{\theta}

\mu
and

Further, suppose

(4.2)

\chi^{-\mu}D^{\mu}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\chi^{-\mu}D^{\mu}\mid\theta\in\hbox{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{Z}})\},\quad(\mu\in\Gamma),

and

(4.3)

\chi^{\mu}({\mathcal{B}}_{c}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}})\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}_{c},\quad(\mu\in\Gamma,\lambda\in\Lambda,\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta^{\times}).

Then any integral structure
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
for

, extends to an integral structure for

.

Proof.
By
[
6
, Lemma 3.2.4 and Theorem 3.3.2]
the extension

exists and the first claim is proved. Assume next that (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) hold. Consider the extension
\bar{\tau}
and recall that
\bar{\tau}_{c}
is the restriction of
\bar{\tau}
to

.

Assume now that we are given an integral structure
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
for
(E_{c},\bar{\tau}_{c})
. We have

E=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}\oplus D
. Since
\kappa=0
,

\displaystyle[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2}]+d_{1}(x_{2})-d_{2}(x_{1})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+[d_{1},d_{2}]+d_{1}\cdot c_{2}-d_{2}\cdot c_{1},

,

,

Moreover,

4.3.

As before, we assume that

Theorem 4.3.1
.

(Uniqueness Theorem)
Consider the extended affine Lie algebra
E=(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa=0)
, where we assume that
rankE>1
and
ind(R)=0
if
X={\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}
. Assume that
{\mathcal{B}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{B}}
are two integral structures for

associated to two Chevalley systems

-Lie algebras are isomorphic.

Proof.
We have
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}_{c}\cup(\cup_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{B}}^{\mu})
and
\bar{\mathcal{B}}=\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{c}\cup(\cup_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{B}}^{\mu})
, where
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
and
\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
are the integral structures for
E_{c}
associated with
{\mathcal{C}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
respectively, given by Proposition
3.2.3
. Consider the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear map
\Psi:\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}
induced by

\begin{array}[]{c}h_{\alpha}\mapsto h_{\alpha},\;h_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{0},\quad x_{\alpha}\mapsto\mu_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha},\alpha\in R^{\times},\\

\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta}\mapsto\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\;\mu\in\Gamma,\;\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\mu}.\end{array}

By Theorem
3.2.5
, the group homomorphism

restricts to a Lie algebra isomorphism

\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{c}\rightarrow\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\bar{\mathcal{B}}_{c}

, we need to show that

passes through the non-trivial remaining brackets, namely those of the form

, where
x\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\mu}
and

We begin with a bracket of the form

\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta^{\times}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
. As it was explained in Remark

\chi^{\mu}(\mathcal{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\lambda+\mu}
. Assume that
\chi^{\mu}(x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda})=kx_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}
, for an scalar

\displaystyle\Psi[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\lambda)\Psi(\chi^{\mu}(x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\theta(\lambda)\mu_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}\bar{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\theta(\lambda)\mu^{2}_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle[\Psi(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta}),\Psi(x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\mu_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}\bar{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\lambda)\mu_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}^{2}\chi^{\mu}(x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\theta(\lambda)\mu_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda}^{2}x_{\dot{\alpha}+\lambda+\mu}.

Therefore,
\Psi
passes through the bracket by
Remark
3.2.4
.

Next, we note that using the Jacobi identity, the brackets of the type
[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},x^{\alpha}_{\sigma}]
, transfers to brackets in
\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
, and so we are done in this case as well. The remaining bracket are easy to check.
\Box

5.
Chevalley basis for centerless Lie tori

In this section, we investigate the concept of Chevalley involutions and Chevalley bases for centerless Lie tori. We follow the same notation and terminologies as in Subsection
2.1
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus of type
(\Delta,\Lambda)
, where we always assume that

Let

5.1.
Chevalley systems

Definition 5.1.1
.

We call a finite order automorphism
\tau
of
\mathcal{L}
a
Chevalley involution
if

\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\tau(h)=-h
for
h\in\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}
.
It follows that
\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}_{-\dot{\alpha}}
,
(\lambda,\dot{\alpha})\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L}).

Remark 5.1.2
.

Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for an Extended affine Lie algebra
E
and consider the centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}=E_{cc}=E_{c}/Z(E_{c})
, see Remark
2.3.4
. By
[
15
, Propositon 3.4]
,
\tau
restricts to a Chevalley involution for
E_{c}
and so induces an involution
\bar{\tau}
on
E_{cc}
.
Then as
\tau(h)=-h
, for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and
\bar{\tau}(\mathcal{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}_{-\dot{\alpha}}
, by Remark
2.3.4
,
\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta,\lambda\in\Lambda
, we get that
\bar{\tau}
is a Chevalley involution for
E_{cc}
.

Definition 5.1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus with a Chevalley involution
\tau
. We call a subset
{\mathcal{C}}=\{x^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mid(\dot{\alpha},\lambda)\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L}),\dot{\alpha}\not=0\}

of
\mathcal{L}
a
Chevalley system
for
(\mathcal{L},\tau)
if for each
(\dot{\alpha},\lambda)\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L})
the following hold:

(i)
(x^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}},h^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}:=[x^{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}},x^{-\lambda}_{-\dot{\alpha}}],x^{-\lambda}_{-\dot{\alpha}})

-triple,

Lemma 5.1.4
.

Assume that the centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
is equipped with a Chevalley involution
\tau
. Then
(\mathcal{L},\tau)
admits a Chevalley system.

Proof.
By (
2.1
), for each
(\dot{\alpha},\lambda)\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{L})
with

, there exist

\Box

\displaystyle(\hbox{by (\ref{fiam1}))}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\sigma+\nu,0}\theta(\mu+\sigma)(x^{\nu+\mu+\sigma}_{\dot{\alpha}},x^{-\mu}_{-\dot{\alpha}})

\displaystyle(\hbox{by (\ref{june17}))}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})}{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\sigma+\nu,0}\theta(\mu+\sigma)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})}{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\sigma+\nu,0}c^{(-\sigma)}_{\mu+\sigma}(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\sigma+\nu,0}\frac{2}{k}c^{(-\sigma)}_{\mu+\sigma}(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\sigma+\nu,0}\frac{2}{k}c^{(-\sigma)}_{\mu+\sigma}(d)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{B}}^{\sigma}_{c}(d).

This proves that
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
satisfies (C4) for
(E_{c},\bar{\tau})
.
\Box

6.
Examples

6.1.
Multi-loop Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an algebra and
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}
, be
\nu
commuting finite order automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
of periods

m_{1},\ldots,m_{\nu}
respectively. Then

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}},
where for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})\in\Lambda:={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
,

\bar{\lambda}:=(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{\nu})
with

\bar{\lambda}_{j}:=\lambda_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{j}}
, and

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma_{j}(x)=\omega^{\lambda_{j}}_{j}x\hbox{
for }1\leq j\leq\nu\},

\omega_{i}
a primitive
m_{i}^{th}
-root of
unity,
1\leq i\leq\nu
.
Let
\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
denote the projection onto
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{K}}[z^{\pm 1}_{1},\ldots,z^{\pm 1}_{\nu}]
be the algebra of Laurent polynomials in
\nu
-variables equipped with the natural
\Lambda
-grading
\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}z^{\lambda}
,
z^{\lambda}=z_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots z_{\nu}^{\lambda_{\nu}}
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu}).

For
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})
, the subalgebra

(6.1)

M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma}):=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes z^{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes z^{\lambda}

of
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A}):=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is called the
\nu
-step multi-loop
algebra
based on
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
2
]
. A form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
can be extended to
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
by

(6.2)

(x\otimes z^{\lambda},x^{\prime}\otimes z^{\mu})=\delta_{\lambda,-\mu}(x,x^{\prime}),\qquad(x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g},\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda).

If
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
is invariant and non-degenerate, then so is the form on
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
restricted to
M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
.

We now discuss the centroid of
M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. As before, we denote by

{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{B}})
the centroid of an algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
. Each automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
induces an automorphism
\sigma^{\star}
on

{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g})
by
\sigma^{\star}(f)=\sigma^{-1}f\sigma.

Then we have
{\mathcal{C}}(M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma}))=M({\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star})
, where

\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star}=(\sigma_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}^{\star})
. Assume now that
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g})={\mathbb{K}}
, for example if
\mathfrak{g}
is finite-dimensional central simple. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma}))={\mathbb{K}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star}}\equiv\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star}}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star}}
is
the fixed points of
\mathcal{A}
under
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\star}
. Note that
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A})={\mathbb{K}}\otimes\mathcal{A}\equiv\mathcal{A}
, and
z^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{A}
as an element of
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
acts by
z^{\lambda}\cdot(x\otimes z^{\mu})=x\otimes z^{\lambda+\mu}.

6.2.
Loop affinization

We set
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=M(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
where
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
, and
\sigma
is an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
with
\sigma^{m}=\hbox{id}
. Suppose
\sigma
stabilizes
\mathfrak{h}
, preserves the form and satisfies
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{0}}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}})=\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}
. We note that since
\sigma
stabilizes
\mathfrak{h}
, we also have
\mathfrak{h}=\sum_{\bar{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{\lambda}}
. The automorphism
\sigma
can be also considered as an automorphism of
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
by
\sigma(\alpha)(h)=\alpha(\sigma^{-1}(h))
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
,
h\in\mathfrak{h}
. This in turn gives the decomposition

\mathfrak{h}^{\star}=\sum_{\bar{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})^{\bar{\lambda}}
, and the corresponding projections

\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}:\mathfrak{h}^{\star}\rightarrow(\mathfrak{h}^{\star})^{\bar{\lambda}}
,
\bar{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
. We set
\pi=\pi_{\bar{0}}
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
.
We set
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathcal{D}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{K}}\lambda_{i}
,
and
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{K}}d_{i}
is the dual space of
{\mathcal{C}}
with

d_{i}(\lambda_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. We note that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is
\Lambda
-graded with

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}.
One makes
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
into a Lie algebra by

\begin{array}[]{cc}[d,x]=d(\lambda)x&d\in\mathcal{D},x\in{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}^{\lambda},\\
[{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}]=\{0\},\\
[x,y]=[x,y]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}([d_{i},x],y)\lambda_{i},&x,y\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}.\end{array}

The form on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
extends to an invariant non-degenerate form on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
by natural dual paring of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathcal{D}
, namely
(\lambda_{j},d_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Then setting
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}=(\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}\otimes 1)\oplus{\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathcal{D}
, we get that

(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}},(\cdot,\cdot),\widehat{\mathfrak{h}})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra with root system
\widehat{R}=\{\pi_{\lambda}(\alpha)+\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda,\alpha\in R,{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}_{\pi(\alpha)}\not=\{0\}\}
, where

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\sum_{\{\beta\in R\mid\pi(\beta)=\pi(\alpha)\}}\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}).

Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum_{\hat{\alpha}\in\widehat{R}}\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{\alpha}}
with

{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\hat{\alpha}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}&\hbox{if }\hat{\alpha}=0\\
\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}&\hbox{if }\hat{\alpha}=\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\alpha)+\lambda\not\not=0.\end{array}\right.

Now let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau
. Also let
\psi
be a finite order automorphism of

\mathfrak{g}
such that
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})=\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\psi
preserves the form on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\psi(h)=h
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}
.
We see from
[
5
]
that the assignment

\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(x)\otimes z^{\lambda}+c+d\mapsto\psi\tau(\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(x))\otimes z^{-\lambda}-c-d

induces an involution
\tau_{\psi}
on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since
\tau_{\psi}
acts as
-\hbox{id}
on
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
, it is a Chevalley involution. Note that if
\beta\in R
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
x_{\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
and
\hat{x}_{\beta}:=\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(x_{\beta})\otimes z^{\lambda}
, then

\tau_{\psi}({\hat{x}}_{\beta})=\psi(\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\tau(x_{\beta}))\otimes z^{-\lambda}=-\psi(\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(x_{-\beta}))\otimes z^{-\lambda}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\hat{\alpha}}.

We set
\hat{\tau}=\tau_{\psi}
. By Remark
3.1.3
(i),
({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{c},{\hat{\tau}}_{c})
admits a Chevalley system
{\mathcal{C}}
. By Proposition
3.2.3
, this Chevalley system extends in a prescribed way to an integral structure for
({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{c},{\hat{\tau}}_{c})
.

Now, we need to determine if the triple
(\mathfrak{g},\sigma,\tau)
admits an automorphism
\psi
as described above, namely,

-
\psi
is a finite order automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
,

-
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})=\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

-

\psi
preserves the form on
\mathfrak{g}
,

-
\psi(h)=h
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\bar{0}}
.

We report here several important cases for the pair

(\mathfrak{g},\tau)
where
\mathfrak{g}
is an extended affine Lie algebra and
\tau
is a Chevalley automorphism.

Case 1:

\mathfrak{g}
a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra.

(a) If
\sigma=\hbox{id}
, then
\tau_{\hbox{id}}

is a Chevalley involution for the toroidal Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(b) If
\sigma
a non-trivial graph automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\tau_{\psi}

is a Chevalley involution for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\psi
given as follows:

(i)
\psi=\hbox{id}
if
\sigma
is of order
2
,

(ii)
\psi
is a
graph automorphism of order
2
otherwise. In particular if
\mathcal{A}
is the algebra of Laurent
polynomials in one variable, then
\tau_{\psi}
is a Chevalley involution for the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Case 2:

\mathfrak{g}
is an affine Lie algebra.
Let
\sigma
be a non-identity
graph automorphism for
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then there exists a graph automorphism
\psi
such that
\tau_{\psi}
is a Chevalley involution for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{A}
is the
algebra of Laurent polynomials in one variable then
\tau_{\psi}
is a Chevalley involution for
the elliptic Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Case 3:

(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is an extended affine Lie algebra. Let
\sigma

be an automorphism of order
2
. If
\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau
then
\tau_{\hbox{id}}
is a
Chevalley involution for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

We refer the interested reader to
[
5
]
for more details and examples.

6.3.
Toroidal Lie algebras

We know want to have a closer look at toroidal Lie algebras.
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with root system
\Delta
. Assume that
\sigma_{i}=\hbox{id}
for
1\leq i\leq\nu
.
Then
\mathcal{L}:=M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is called a
toroidal Lie algebra
. Note that
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless Lie torus of type
\Delta
, with
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\lambda}=\mathfrak{g}_{\dot{\alpha}}\otimes z^{\lambda}
,
\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
. We fix a Chevalley basis
\dot{\mathcal{B}}=\{h_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}},\ldots,h_{\dot{\alpha}_{\ell}},x_{\dot{\alpha}}\mid\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta^{\times}\}
for
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\{\dot{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\dot{\alpha}_{\ell}\}
is a base for
\Delta
. Let
\tau\in\hbox{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding Chevalley involution taking
x_{\dot{\alpha}}
to
-x_{-\dot{\alpha}}
. The involution
\tau
extends to
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
by
\tau(x\otimes z^{\lambda})=\tau(x)\otimes z^{-\lambda}
.

We set

{\mathcal{B}}:=\{x\otimes z^{\lambda}\mid x\in\dot{\mathcal{B}},\lambda\in\Lambda\}
. We note that for
\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta^{\times}
,
(x_{\dot{\alpha}},h_{\dot{\alpha}}:=[x_{\dot{\alpha}},x_{-\dot{\alpha}}],x_{-\dot{\alpha}}])
is an

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple. Then it follows that the set
\{x_{\dot{\alpha}}\otimes z^{\lambda}\mid\dot{\alpha}\in\Delta,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
satisfies (CBT1)-(CBT3) and so is a Chevalley basis for
(\mathcal{L},\tau)
.

From §
6.1
, we see that

{\mathcal{C}}((M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma}))=\mathcal{A}
, with the action
z^{\mu}(x\otimes z^{\lambda})=x\otimes z^{\lambda+\mu}
. Therefore the centroid stabilizes
{\mathcal{B}}
and so condition (
5.2
) holds. Then by Proposition
5.2.2
, the set
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
given by
(
5.4
) is an integral structure for
(E_{c},\tau_{c})
, where

E=E(\mathcal{L},D,0)
and
D=D^{0}=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{0}
or
D=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
.

Since the case
D=D^{0}=\hbox{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{0}
is of special interest, we discuss it here in more details. We have
\Gamma=\{0\}
and

D=\{\partial_{\theta}\mid\theta\in\hbox{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})\}
.
Then
D^{\star}
is spanned by elements
\{c_{\lambda}:=c_{\lambda}^{(0)}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
. In fact
\{c_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots c_{\lambda_{\nu}}\}
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for

D^{\star}
, where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\Lambda
.
Then by (
5.4
),

{\mathcal{B}}_{c}={\mathcal{B}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{c}=\{x\otimes z^{\lambda}\mid x\in\dot{\mathcal{B}},\lambda\in\Lambda\}\cup\frac{2}{k}\{c_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,c_{\lambda_{\nu}}\},

where

k=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\hbox{ if $\Delta$ is of simply laced type},\\
6&\hbox{ if $\Delta$ is of type $G_{2}$},\\
4&\hbox{ for the remaining types.}\end{array}\right.

Quantum theory for edge current and noise in 2D topological superconductors
S. Pintus, A. Crépieux

Abstract
We calculate the edge current and its fluctuations, i.e. noise, in a 2D topological superconductor using the T-matrix and the Green function techniques. We show that the current is zero for non-chiral edge states and non-zero for chiral edge states, while the edge noise is non-zero whatever the chirality of the edge states. By applying our results to toy models with chiral edge states, we find that the noise is closely related to the Chern number. The edge noise is non-zero only when the Chern number is non-zero, and the bulk noise exhibits a peak each time the Chern number varies, meaning that there is strong current fluctuations when a topological phase transition occurs. Our results suggest that the bulk noise could be seen as a topological susceptibility.

II.1
Edge spectral function

The edge spectral function
A_{k_{x}y=a}(\varepsilon)
is obtained from the spectral function defined as

\displaystyle A_{k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\text{el}}[\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)]

(1)

where
\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}[~]
is the trace on the electron sub-space, and where the spectral matrix is defined as the imaginary part of the retarded Green function, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}\{{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)\}

(2)

In Eq. (
2
) appears the partial Fourier transform of the retarded Green function, which is defined as

\displaystyle{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{y}}{2\pi}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{y}^{\prime}}{2\pi}

(3)

\displaystyle\times e^{i(yk_{y}-y^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime})}{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

where
1BZ
corresponds to the first Brillouin zone. In the formalism of the T-matrix, one has

\displaystyle{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime})\delta(k_{y}-k_{y}^{\prime}){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon)

(4)

\displaystyle+{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon){\bf T}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

where
{\bf T}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)
is the T-matrix. The bare Green function is given by
{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon+i\eta-\mathcal{H}_{k_{x}k_{y}})^{-1}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{k_{x}k_{y}}
is the Hamiltonian of the infinite clean 2D system, and
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
the damping. The edge located at position
y=a
is modeled by an impurity line of strong potential amplitude located at position
y=0
(see Fig.
1
),
a
being the inter-atomic distance. In that case, the associated T-matrix
{\bf T}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)\equiv\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime}){\bf T}_{k_{x}}(\varepsilon)
does not depend on
k_{y}
nor
k_{y}^{\prime}
, and one has
28
;
21

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{k_{x}}(\varepsilon)=\left[{\bf I}-{\bf U}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{y}}{2\pi}{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon)\right]^{-1}{\bf U}

(5)

with
{\bf I}
the identity matrix, and
{\bf U}
the impurity matrix. It leads to

\displaystyle{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y};k_{x}^{\prime}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime})\delta(k_{y}-k_{y}^{\prime}){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon)

(6)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime}){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon){\bf T}_{k_{x}}(\varepsilon){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

By inserting this expression in Eq. (
3
), one gets for the retarded Green function

\displaystyle{\bf G}^{r}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime})\Big[{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}0}(\varepsilon)

(7)

\displaystyle+{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}y}(\varepsilon){\bf T}_{k_{x}}(\varepsilon){\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}-y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon)\Big]

where
{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)=\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{y}}{2\pi}e^{iyk_{y}}{\bf g}^{r}_{k_{x}k_{y}}(\varepsilon)
is the partial Fourier transform of the bare Green function.

II.2
Edge current

The current operator between first neighbors at positions
\{x_{i},y\}
and
\{x_{j},y\}
(see Fig.
1
) at time
\tau
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}_{\langle i,j\rangle}(\tau)=-\frac{eit}{\hbar}\sum_{\sigma}\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{x_{i}y\sigma}(\tau)c_{x_{j}y\sigma}(\tau)+h.c.

(8)

where
t
is the hopping integral between first neighbors,
c^{\,\dagger}_{x_{i}y\sigma}
and
c_{x_{j}y\sigma}
are the creation/annihilation operators at positions
\{x_{i},y\}
/
\{x_{j},y\}
, and
\sigma
is the spin and/or orbital degree of freedom. Since the translation invariance is broken in the
y
direction due to the presence of an impurity line at position
y=0
, we apply a partial Fourier transform by taking

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{xy\sigma}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}}}\sum_{k_{x}}e^{ik_{x}x}\,\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)

(9)

Thus,

\displaystyle\widehat{I}_{\langle i,j\rangle}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{eit}{\hbar N_{x}}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{k_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}}e^{-ik_{x}x_{i}}e^{ik_{x}^{\prime}x_{j}}

(10)

\displaystyle\times\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y\sigma}(\tau)+h.c.

By using the fact that the current does not depend on position
x_{i}
due to current conservation, the quantity of interest is the position-averaged current along the
x
-axis:
I_{y}(\tau)=\sum_{x_{i}}\widehat{I}_{\langle i,j\rangle}(\tau)/N_{x}
, that is

\displaystyle I_{y}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{eit}{\hbar N_{x}^{2}}\sum_{x_{i}\sigma}\sum_{k_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}}e^{-ik_{x}x_{i}}e^{ik_{x}^{\prime}x_{j}}

(11)

\displaystyle\times\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y\sigma}(\tau)+h.c.

The positions
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
being first neighbors, one has
x_{j}=x_{i}+a
. By calculating the sum on
x_{i}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{y}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{eit}{\hbar N_{x}}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{k_{x}}e^{iak_{x}}\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)+h.c.

By introducing the non-equilibrium Green function defined as
G^{<}_{k_{x}y\sigma;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=i\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\rangle
, one obtains for the current expectation value the expression

\displaystyle\langle I_{y}(\tau)\rangle=-\frac{2et}{\hbar N_{x}}\sum_{k_{x}}\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\left[\text{Re}\left\{e^{iak_{x}}{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\tau,\tau)\right\}\right]

where
{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
is the non-equilibrium Green function matrix whose elements are
G^{<}_{k_{x}y\sigma;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
. In the absence of time-dependent excitation, one has

\displaystyle{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}e^{i\varepsilon(\tau-\tau^{\prime})/\hbar}{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)

In that case, the current is time-independent and reads

\displaystyle\langle I_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2et}{\hbar}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}

(15)

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\left[\text{Re}\left\{e^{iak_{x}}{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)\right\}\right]

This result allows to re-derive the result obtained by Pathak et al.
27
in the equilibrium limit. Indeed, one has
{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)=if(\varepsilon)\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)
in equilibrium
14
, where
f(\varepsilon)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, and
\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)
the spectral matrix defined in Eq. (
2
). It leads to

\displaystyle\langle I_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2et}{\hbar}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\sin(k_{x}a)

(16)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}f(\varepsilon)\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)\right]

We note that if the spectral function is an even function with
k_{x}
, the current cancels out due to the presence of a sine factor in Eq. (
16
). This is the case for non-chiral edge states. It means that the current along the edge, corresponding to
\langle I_{y=a}\rangle
, can only be non-zero when the edge states are chiral.

II.3
Edge noise

The finite-frequency noise is defined as the Fourier transform of position-averaged current-current correlator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\Delta I_{y}(\tau)\Delta I_{y^{\prime}}(0)\rangle e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau

(17)

with
\Delta I_{y}(\tau)=I_{y}(\tau)-\langle I_{y}(\tau)\rangle
, where
I_{y}(\tau)
is given by Eq. (
II.2
). We restricted our study to time-independent excitation. In that case
\langle I_{y}(\tau)\rangle=\langle I_{y}\rangle
, where
\langle I_{y}\rangle
is given by Eq. (
15
). We start by calculating the correlator
\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\langle\Delta I_{y}(\tau)\Delta I_{y^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle
. By using Eq. (
II.2
), and assuming real value for the hopping integral
t
, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar^{2}N_{x}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{k_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}}\sin(k_{x}a)\sin(k_{x}^{\prime}a)

\displaystyle\times\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle-\langle I_{y}\rangle^{2}

By applying Wick theorem, one has

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\rangle\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\rangle

(19)

The first contribution in the r.h.s. term of Eq. (
II.3
) will be canceled by the last term in Eq. (
II.3
). Finally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar^{2}N_{x}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{k_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}}\sin(k_{x}a)\sin(k_{x}^{\prime}a)

\displaystyle\times\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\widehat{c}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle\langle\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\widehat{c}_{k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau)\rangle

(20)

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar^{2}N_{x}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{k_{x}k_{x}^{\prime}}\sin(k_{x}a)\sin(k_{x}^{\prime}a)

\displaystyle\times G^{<}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime};k_{x}y\sigma}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)G^{>}_{k_{x}y\sigma;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

(21)

By inserting this expression in Eq. (
17
) and by performing the integration on time variable
\tau
, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\omega)=\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\sin(k_{x}a)\sin(k_{x}^{\prime}a)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\Big[{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime};k_{x}y}(\varepsilon){\bf G}^{>}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega)\Big]

(22)

Due to the presence of a factor
\delta(k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime})
in the expression of the Green function (see Eq. (
7
)), the noise simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\omega)=\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\sin^{2}(k_{x}a)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\Big[{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y^{\prime};k_{x}y}(\varepsilon){\bf G}^{>}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega)\Big]

(23)

In the equilibrium limit, by knowing that
{\bf G}^{<}_{k_{x}y^{\prime};k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)=if(\varepsilon)\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y^{\prime};k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)
and
{\bf G}^{>}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega)=-i(1-f(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega))\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega)
14
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{yy^{\prime}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4e^{2}t^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\sin^{2}(k_{x}a)

(24)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}f(\varepsilon)(1-f(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega))

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}_{\text{el}}\Big[\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y^{\prime};k_{x}y}(\varepsilon)\mathcal{A}_{k_{x}y;k_{x}y^{\prime}}(\varepsilon-\hbar\omega)\Big]

This expression gives the bulk noise,
\mathcal{S}_{\text{bulk}}(\omega)\equiv\mathcal{S}_{yy}(\omega)
with
y\gg a
, and the edge noise,
\mathcal{S}_{\text{edge}}(\omega)\equiv\mathcal{S}_{aa}(\omega)
, at equilibrium under the form of an integral on both energy
\varepsilon
and
k_{x}
-momentum that can be computed numerically. Contrary to what was obtained for the edge current, i.e., only chiral edge state will have a non-zero edge current, one can have non-zero noise for both chiral and non-chiral edge states. Indeed, a factor
\sin^{2}(k_{x}a)
is present in the noise expression given by Eq. (
24
), while current exhibits a factor
\sin(k_{x}a)
in its expression given by Eq. (
16
).

We now turn our attention to the results obtained for noise. Figures

\mu
for which the Chern number changes its value, meaning that it behaves like a kind of topological susceptibility; (iv) edge noise is non-zero only when Chern number is non-zero. These characteristics can be physically justified. Property (i) results from the thermal contribution to the noise. Indeed, since the bulk noise is calculated at finite temperature, it does not vanish as the bulk current does. Nevertheless, since ingap edge states contribute to edge noise but not to bulk noise, the latter one is expected to be negligible compared to the former one. This explains property (ii). With regard to (iii), since the Chern number is a topological invariant, it is a bulk property. It is therefore not surprising that it exists a link between Chern number and bulk noise.

In summary, in equilibrium, the bulk noise can be seen as a kind of topological susceptibility, which exhibits peaks every time the Chern number undergoes a value change, whereas the edge noise is related to the conductance, itself related to the Chern number. The peak height in the bulk noise is proportional to the derivative of the Chern number according to
\mu

\mu=0

\mu=\pm 2t

III.2
Twisted bilayer model

Twisted bilayer systems can be described by an effective
4\times 4
matrix Hamiltonian
27
given, in the basis
\{\widehat{c}_{k_{x}k_{y}1},\widehat{c}_{k_{x}k_{y}2},\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{-k_{x}-k_{y}1},\widehat{c}^{\,\dagger}_{-k_{x}-k_{y}2}\}
where 1 and 2 are the layer indices, by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k_{x}k_{y}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}&\gamma&\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}&0\\
\gamma&\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}&0&\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}\\
\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}^{*}&0&-\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}&-\gamma\\
0&\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}^{*}&-\gamma&-\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(31)

with
\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}=-\mu-2t(\cos(ak_{x})+\cos(ak_{y}))
and
\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}=\Delta(\cos(ak_{x})-\cos(ak_{y})+i\sin(ak_{x})\sin(ak_{y}))
in case of
d+id
superconductivity.
\gamma
is the inter-layer coupling,
\Delta
the superconducting order parameter, and
\mu
the chemical potential. The eigenenergies associated to the clean twisted bilayer system read

\displaystyle E_{\pm\pm}(k_{x},k_{y})=\pm\sqrt{\left(\mathcal{E}_{k_{x}k_{y}}\pm\gamma\right)^{\,2}+|\mathcal{F}_{k_{x}k_{y}}|^{\,2}}

(32)

The impurity matrix
{\bf U}
used in the T-matrix formalism is taken as

\displaystyle{\bf U}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{0}\;{\bf I}_{2}&0\\
0&-U_{0}\;{\bf I}_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(33)

where
{\bf I}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix, and
U_{0}
the impurity strength.

III.3
Summary of results

By comparing the results obtained in the two previous sections, using the QWZ model or using the twisted bilayer model, we observe similar behaviors in bulk/edge current and noise, all related to the Chern number, as summarized in Tab.
1
. Additional details of the comparison are given hereafter.

If the absolute value of the derivative of the edge current is proportional to the Chern number, it should cancel out when the Chern number cancels out. However, this is not what we see in Figs.
5
(b) and
9
(b) which suggests that the current is non-zero even though there is no ingap edge state. This is related to the fact that one has a
k_{x}
-asymmetry in the edge spectral function that gives a contribution to the current, even if there is no ingap state. No such thing occurs for edge noise due to the
\sin^{2}(k_{x}a)
factor in its expression (see Eq. (
24
)). To consolidate our justification, we plot in Fig.
10
the edge current derivative for various values of the superconducting parameter
\Delta
for both QWZ and twisted bilayer models. This shows that when we reduce the
k_{x}
-asymmetry by reducing
\Delta
, the profile of the absolute value of the edge current derivative increasingly tends towards the absolute value of the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
. In the twisted bilayer model, we note, however, the presence of dips in the edge current derivative for some
\mu
values that are not present in the Chern number curves.

A careful observation of Figs.
5
(d) and
9

\mu
values for which the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
changes: there is a slight shift between the peaks and the expected

IV
Conclusion

The current fluctuations in a 2D topological superconductors have been calculated and expressed as an integral over energy and momentum involving the spectral function and the Fermi-Dirac distribution function. The presence of a
\sin^{2}(k_{x}a)
factor leads to the possibility of non-zero current fluctuations for both chiral and non-chiral edge states, even though the current is zero in the latter case due to the
\sin(k_{x}a)
factor in its expression. We next study the noise behavior in two different models exhibiting chiral edge states, i.e. QWZ model and twisted bilayer model, and highlight interesting similarities between the results. The edge noise is non-zero when the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
is non-zero, whereas the bulk noise behaves like the absolute value of the derivative of
\mathcal{C}
according to the chemical potential. It means that the latter quantity, the bulk noise, could be seen as a topological susceptibility with a peak each time that
\mathcal{C}
changes its value. The absolute value of the edge current derivative according to the chemical potential is also closely related to the Chern number, although additional dips are obtained in the former quantity for the twisted bilayer model. The study of noise in more realistic systems is left to future work with the aim of confirming or refuting the universality of these results.

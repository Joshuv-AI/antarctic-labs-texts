Operational meaning of the classical fidelity and the path length in Fisher-Kubo-Mori-Bogoliubov geometry
Lajos Diósi

Abstract
We show that the minimum entropy production in near-reversible quantum state transport along a path is simple function of the path length measured according to the Fisher–KMB metrics. Hence the sharp values of path lengths, also called statistical lengths, obtain operational meaning to quantify the residual irreversibility in near-reversible state transport. In the classical limit, the Bhattacharryya fidelity obtains a sharp operational meaning after eighty years.



I
Introduction

A quantitative comparison between two probability distributions
p_{\alpha}
and
q_{\alpha}

can be based on the Bhattacharyya coefficient published in 1945
Bhattacharyya 1943
:

F(p,q)=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}}~\in[0,1].

(1)

This function defines a measure
F\in[0,1]
of closeness between
p
and
q
.
Fifty years later, to measure the closeness of two quantum states
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
,
Jozsa borrowed the expression known in mathematics

Bures 1969
;
Uhlmann 1976
and called
it fidelity
Jozsa 1994
:

F(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})=\|\sqrt{\hat{\rho}}\sqrt{\hat{\sigma}}\|_{\mathsf{tr}}=\mathsf{tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\hat{\sigma}}\hat{\rho}\sqrt{\hat{\sigma}}}~\in[0,1].

(2)

Quantum fidelity is the central tool in modern quantum informatics
Nielsen and Chuang 2001
.
In the special (classical) case when
[\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma}]=0
, we can write

\hat{\rho}=\mathsf{diag}[p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{\alpha},\dots]
and

\hat{\sigma}=\mathsf{diag}[q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{\alpha},\dots]
.
The quantum fidelity (
2
) coincides with the classical fidelity (
1
).

One of our goals is the operational interpretation of
F(q,p)
because it is not known
Nielsen and Chuang 2001
. For the quantum fidelity
F(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})

a simple operational interpretation was already proposed
Dodd and Nielsen 2002
,
based on Uhlmann’s theorem
Uhlmann 1976
, and goes like this.

Assume that
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
are reduced
states of two pure states, respectively, in a larger system. Assume that we have
access to this larger system. Then the distinguishability of
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
is at least as
high as the distinguishability of two arbitrary pure states whose overlap (modulus of scalar product)
is given by the fidelity
F(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})
.

This interpretation assumes the operation on the larger system where
both
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
are pure. Various theorems contain the sharp value of fidelity
but their meaning should always include operations on the ‘environmental’ quantum system.

Fidelities are related to distances, also called statistical distances, on the underlying Riemannian geometries.
The infinitesimal distance
d\ell
defines
the Fisher metrics
Fisher 1925
between the probability distributions
p
and
p+dp

and the Bures metrics
Bures 1969
between the quantum states

d\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\rho}+d\hat{\rho}
:

\displaystyle(d\ell)_{Fisher}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\alpha}(d\sqrt{p_{\alpha}})^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(dp_{\alpha})^{2}}{p_{\alpha}},

(3)

\displaystyle(d\ell)_{Bures}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathsf{tr}(d\sqrt{\hat{\rho}})^{2}=\mathsf{tr}\left(d\hat{\rho}\frac{2}{\hat{\rho}_{L}+\hat{\rho}_{R}}d\hat{\rho}\right).

(4)

The labels
L/R
mean
\hat{\rho}_{L}
acts from the left, as usual, while
\hat{\rho}_{R}
acts from the right.
The local Fisher metrics is the special case of the local Bures metrics when
[\hat{\rho},d\hat{\rho}]=0
.
Consider the geodesics between
p
and
q
or between
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
.
The geodesic distances
\ell
are functions of the respective
fidelities:

\displaystyle\ell(p,q)_{Fisher}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\arccos F(p,q)~\in[0,\pi],

(5)

\displaystyle\ell(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})_{Bures}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1-F^{2}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})}~\in[0,2].

(6)

As we said, when
\hat{\rho}
and
\hat{\sigma}
commute, then
F(q,p)
is the special case of
F(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})

and the local Fisher geometry (
3
) is the special
case of the local Bures geometry (
4
).
This is no longer the case with the above global distances since
\ell_{Bures}=2\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}\ell_{Fisher})
.
The Bures geodesic distance is shorter than the Fisher geodesic distance:
The latter is the minimum length of paths but through the commuting states.

The infinitesimal distances, i.e.: the local metrics, have exact meaning in metrology via the famous Cramér-Rao

Cramér 1946
;
Radhakrishna Rao 1945

and quantum Cramér-Rao theorems
Braunstein and Caves 1994
.

Suppose an unknown state —or probability distributions
p
in the classical case—
at the unknown length
\ell
measured from one end of a known path.
We perform
N
independent measurements on the unknown state to estimate
\ell
,
i.e., to estimate the unknown state. If
N\rightarrow\infty
,
optimum measurements ensure that the
mean squared error of estimation goes to zero as
(\Delta\ell)^{2}=1/N
.

This interpretation concerns the local geometry at one point and does not tell us
anything about non-local features like generic Fisher–Bures distances.

While in metrology and informatics a natural quantum generalization of the Fisher metrics is the Bures one,
in irreversible processes it is the Kubo-Mori-Bogoliubov (KMB) metrics (cf., e.g., ref.
Hayashi 2002
):

(d\ell)_{KMB}^{2}=\mathsf{tr}\left(d\hat{\rho}d\!\log\hat{\rho}\right)=\mathsf{tr}\left(d\hat{\rho}\frac{\log\hat{\rho}_{L}-\log\hat{\rho}_{R}}{\hat{\rho}_{L}-\hat{\rho}_{R}}d\hat{\rho}\right).

(7)

Unlike the expression of geodesic distance in terms of fidelity in Fisher–Bures metrics,
no such closed expression is known in the Fisher–KMB geometry.
Both geometries have been missing their global interpretations but
the close relationship of the KMB metrics with von Neumann entropies will help us.
The present work proposes the operational meaning of path lengths in the Fisher–KMB metrics, which implies the meaning of the geodesics. In the special case we obtain the operational meaning of the classical Bhattacharyya fidelity as well.
The concept is that in near-reversible transport along a path
\gamma
between two predefined
states
\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma}
the minimum entropy production is quantified by the length
\ell_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})
.

II
State transport by equilibrating reservoirs

In preparation, we define the transport of the system’s initial state
\hat{\rho}

into the final state
\hat{\sigma}
in contact with a single reservoir.
We use the reservoir model of ref.
Diósi et al. 2006
and the theorem therein
(cf.
Csiszár et al. 2007
for the rigorous proof).

The reservoir consists initially of
n
independent systems in state
\hat{\sigma}
each and
we start from the composite system-reservoir state
\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes n}
.
The contact of the system and the reservoir assumes a reversible step,
a swap between the system’s state
\hat{\rho}
and (e.g. the 1st) one of the reservoir’s states
\hat{\sigma}
:

\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes n}\Rightarrow\hat{\sigma}\otimes\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes(n-1)}.

(8)

The second step, concerning the reservoir only, is the irreversible relaxation of the reservoir
to a homogeneous state, modeled by the following twirl over the permutation group:

\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes(n-1)}\Rightarrow\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes k}\otimes\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes(n-k-1)}.

(9)

According to this model, the transport
\hat{\rho}\rightarrow\hat{\sigma}
of the system state
leads to the irreversible relaxation inside the reservoir.
In the infinite reservoir limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, fortunately, the entropy
production of relaxation can be expressed as the relative entropy

S(\hat{\rho}\|\hat{\sigma})=\mathsf{tr}(\hat{\rho}\ln\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}\ln\hat{\sigma})
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!S\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes k}\otimes\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes(n-k-1)}\right)-S\!\left(\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\sigma}^{\otimes(n-1)})\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}S(\hat{\rho}\|\hat{\sigma}).

(10)

This is the entropy production of the single-step state transport as well:

\Delta S=S(\hat{\rho}\|\hat{\sigma}).

(11)

The entropy production can be smaller and can even go to zero if we apply sequential
equilibration with intermediate reservoirs along a path, such that the stepsizes go to zero.
The following feature of relative entropy will be important
Petz and Toth 1993
:

S(\hat{\rho}|\hat{\rho}+d\hat{\rho})=\frac{1}{2}(d\ell)^{2},

(12)

where
d\ell
is defined by the Fisher metrics (
3
) in the classical limit and
in the general quantum case it corresponds to the Fisher–KMB (
7
)
and not to the Fisher–Bures (
4
) metrics.
Consider a smooth (not necessarily geodesic) path
\gamma
from
\hat{\rho}
to
\hat{\sigma}
.
We use
N
reservoirs to transport
\hat{\rho}
into
\hat{\sigma}

in
N
steps. Consider a monotone sequence of intermediate states

\hat{\rho}_{i}
along the path, such that
\hat{\rho}=\hat{\rho}_{0}
and
\hat{\rho}_{N}=\hat{\sigma}
. Perform the transports

\hat{\rho}\Rightarrow\hat{\rho}_{1}\Rightarrow\hat{\rho}_{2}\Rightarrow\dots\Rightarrow\hat{\rho}_{N-1}\Rightarrow\hat{\sigma}
.
The total entropy production is the sum of the
N
yields:

\Delta S=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}S(\hat{\rho}_{i}\|\hat{\rho}_{i+1}).

(13)

In the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
each step
\Delta\ell_{i}
between
\hat{\rho}_{i}
and
\hat{\rho}_{i+1}

can go to zero. In this asymptotic regime, applying eq. (
12
) we can thus write

\Delta S=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\Delta\ell_{i})^{2}.

(14)

This is an important expression but the r.h.s. is not yet the function of the
total path length
\ell_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})
while the lengths elements are constrained by it:

\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Delta\ell_{i}=\ell_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma}).

(15)

Keeping this constraint, let us minimize the entropy production (
14
).
It is trivial that each term on the r.h.s. must have the same value, i.e.,

\Delta\ell_{i}=\ell_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})/N
for all
i
. This means
that the dense sequence
\hat{\rho}_{i}
must be evenly distributed along the path,
and the entropy production will be evenly distributed over the steps. The minimum
of the total entropy production along the given path
\gamma
takes this form:

\Delta S_{\gamma}=\frac{\ell_{\gamma}^{2}(\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma})}{2N}.

(16)

The fastest approach to reversible transport is achieved on geodesics.
In the classical case, inserting the geodesic length (
5
) in (
16
), the ultimate lower bound on entropy production reads

\Delta S=\frac{2}{N}(\arccos F)^{2}.

(17)

This expresses the desired sharp operational meaning of the classical
Bhattacharyya fidelity (
1
).
The bounds are attainable in the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
by operations on the system plus
the reservoirs.

We find the simple meaning of the Fisher–KMB length if we introduce the number
\nu
of equilibrations per unit length,
i.e., the density
\nu=N/\ell_{\gamma}
. Then
the entropy production becomes the following linear function of the path length:

\Delta S_{\gamma}(\ell)=\frac{\ell_{\gamma}}{2\nu}.

(18)

This suggests the following operational meaning of the Fisher–KMB length.
Consider the near-reversible state transport
via sequential equilibrations along a smooth path.
If
\nu\gg 1
is the density of equilibrations
per unit path length then the entropy production
is
(1/2\nu)
per unit length.

III
Remarks and summary

Paths in Fisher
geometries are perhaps the simplest non-local objects and, as such,
they can be interpreted as state transport processes. It has therefore seemed logical to
look here for the meaning of the geometries.
One should mention the standard
Kantorovich–Wasserstein theory
Kantorovich 1960
;
Vaserstein 1969

where the transport from distribution
p
to
q
happens via direct relocation of populations from
the
p_{\alpha}
’s into the
q_{\beta}
’s. The transport of quantum state
\hat{\rho}
into
\hat{\sigma}
,
too, can work similarly after a single unitary rotation of
\hat{\rho}
that makes it commute with
\hat{\sigma}
.
This may seem much simpler than sequential equilibration. But the simple method
assumes the detailed control of the states in question. If it is not possible because,
e.g., the states are complicated many-body states then the method of equilibration
becomes more valuable.

Thermodynamic systems are the typical examples.
The Weinhold–Ruppeiner geometry
Weinhold 1975
;
Ruppeiner 1979

defines the thermodynamic distances between thermodynamic states and the thermodynamic
path lengths in general.
It has long been known that the thermodynamic lengths coincide with statistical lengths

Diósi et al. 1984
and they are rooted in the the Fisher geometry of Gibbs states

Janyszek and Mrugała 1989
.
In thermodynamics, the expression
(\Delta\ell)^{2}/V
quantifies
how the statistical fluctuations of the thermodynamic
parameters tend to zero in the thermodynamic limit of infinite volume
V\rightarrow\infty
.
This does not interpret the global thermodynamic lengths
\ell
but the local metrics.
Studies of thermodynamic state transport

Salamon and Nulton 1998
;
Salamon et al. 2023
;
Diósi 2002
;
Diosi et al. 1996

targeted a relationship between the thermodynamic path length and the thermodynamic
entropy production.
Salamon and Nulton
Salamon and Nulton 1998
recognized that in discrete sequential
equilibration the equilibration rates cancel from the expression of entropy production
and they found the thermodynamic predecessor of eq. (
16
).
Scandi and Perernau-Llobet derived similar result in quantum thermodynamics, using
Fisher–KMB geometry of quantum Gibbs states
Scandi and Perarnau-Llobet 2019
.
Ref.
Diósi 2002
was the first attempt at constructing an
underlying classical microscopic mechanism of a specific irreversible state transport.
The present work constructed the abstract microscopic generalization valid in the
Fisher–KMB geometries of arbitrary quantum states including the Fisher geometry special case of probability distributions. For this construction, the analytically tractable microscopic
reservoir model and the related theorem, both proposed in ref.
Diósi et al. 2006
,
have been instrumental.

The global Fisher statistical distance both on space of probability distributions and on the
space of density matrices with the KMB metrics got an operational interpretation for the
first time. We obtain the sharp, not just qualitative, operational (physical) meaning
of the classical Bhattacharyya fidelity. (Interestingly, the global Fisher–Bures geometry
and the quantum fidelity are still waiting for their operational interpretations.)
The concrete choice of the reservoir and the equilibration protocol is not likely to be
critical and alternative microscopic models should lead to the same
relationships between entropy production and the Fisher–KMB geometry.
Future investigations may replace our stepwise transport with some continuous protocol.

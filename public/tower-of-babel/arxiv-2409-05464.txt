Fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves in characteristic two
Cesar Hilario, Karl-Otto Stöhr

Abstract
. We give a complete classification, up to birational equivalence, of all fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves in characteristic two.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate in characteristic
p=2
the birational geometry of fibrations by rational curves of degree
d=4
in the projective plane.

The case of degree
d=2
corresponds to
conic bundles
, which have a long and rich history that goes back to work by the Italian school (e.g., the theory of ruled surfaces), and to more recent work related to the birational geometry of complex threefolds (see the expository article
[
14
]
).
Over the past few years, conic bundles have also been studied in positive characteristic
(see
[
5
]
and the references therein).

If the fibres are of degree
d>2
, then their arithmetic genus
g=(d-1)(d-2)/2
is larger than their geometric genus
\overline{g}=0
, and so they admit singularities.
By Bertini’s theorem this can only happen in characteristic
p>0
.
More precisely,
as follows from Tate’s genus change formula
[
22
]
,
the prime
p
must be equal to
m+1
where
m
is a divisor of the integer
2(g-\overline{g})=(d-1)(d-2)
.

If
d=3
then
p\in\{2,3\}
, and we obtain the so-called
quasi-elliptic fibrations
, which are fibrations whose general fibres are plane cubic curves with a cusp.
Equivalently, the generic fibre
C=f^{-1}(\eta)=T_{\eta}
of a quasi-elliptic fibration
f:T\to B
is a
quasi-elliptic curve
over the function field
K=k(B)
of the base
B
, i.e.,
C
is a regular proper geometrically integral curve of arithmetic genus
g=1
over
K
.
Quasi-elliptic fibrations play a key role in the extension of the Enriques classification of complex algebraic surfaces to positive characteristics, accomplished by Bombieri and Mumford
[
2
,
3
]
.

If
d=4
, then
p\in\{2,3,7\}
.
The cases
p=3
and
p=7
were investigated by Salomão
[
15
,
16
]
and the second author
[
21
]
.
In the present paper we give a birational classification of the case
p=2
.
In other words, we classify, up to birational equivalence, the fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves in characteristic
p=2
.
Our main result asserts that the generic fibre
C=T_{\eta}
of such a fibration
T\to B
, which is a curve over the function field
K=k(B)
, falls into one of five disjoint classes of curves.

The first two columns in Table

This motivates us to introduce and study a second canonical field of the regular curve

.
Recall that the

of

Since

We define the

The curves

of

In light of the purely inseparable Frobenius map

, the latter implies that in characteristic
p=2
every fibration by plane projective rational quartic curves arises as a degree

This is a unique feature of geometry in characteristic
p=2
, for in characteristic
p>2
the normalized Frobenius pullback
X_{1}
of any regular curve
X
of genus

is smooth (see

), and therefore not quasi-elliptic.

To prove our results we work in the arithmetic setting of function field theory.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
goes as follows:
first we determine a presentation of the function field
F|K=K(C)|K
of the regular curve
C|K
, and secondly we find a realization of
C
as a plane curve of degree
2g-2=4
in
\mathbb{P}^{g-1}(K)=\mathbb{P}^{2}(K)
,
where
g=3
is the arithmetic genus of
C
,
through the sections of a canonical divisor.
The determination of a presentation of
F|K
is based on the Riemann–Roch theorem, the Bedoya–Stöhr algorithm
[
1
]
and the main theorem in
[
7
]
.
If the only non-smooth point
\mathfrak{p}
on
C
is a canonical divisor, then its sections provide the presentation of
F|K
and the realization of
C
as a plane quartic curve over
K
(see
[
6
]
).

In this paper we focus on the much harder case where the divisor
\mathfrak{p}
is not canonical.
Here the presentation of
F|K
has to be obtained by looking at the Riemann–Roch spaces
H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{r})
of the powers
\mathfrak{p}^{r}
of
\mathfrak{p}
, since
\mathfrak{p}
itself does not have enough sections (see the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.5
).
In addition, since
\mathfrak{p}
is not canonical the realization of
C
as a plane quartic curve requires the prior determination of a canonical divisor, whose sections fullfil such a realization. We perform this task by using differentials (see Section
3
).

If
C|K
is the generic fibre of a fibration
T\to B
, then the behaviour of most special fibres is governed by the
geometric generic fibre

C_{\overline{K}}=C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
.
This is a rational plane quartic curve over the algebraic closure
\overline{K}=\overline{k(B)}
, with a unique singular point that is unibranch and lies over the non-smooth point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
. The quartic curve
C_{\overline{K}}
is strange, i.e., all its tangent lines meet in a common intersection point, and it has the remarkable property that its tangent lines are either all bitangents (
2
,
3
and
5
) or all non-ordinary inflection tangents (
1
and
4
).

The explicit description in Theorem
1.1
allows us to construct five fibrations that are universal in the sense that any fibration
T\to B
by plane projective rational quartic curves is obtained, up to birational equivalence, from one of them by a base extension (see
[
6
, Theorems 5.1 and 5.2]
and Theorem
4.1
).
We prove that the total spaces of these fibrations are uniruled, and more generally, that the total space of any fibration by (possibly singular) rational curves is uniruled (see Proposition
4.2
).

If the base of the fibration is one-dimensional then we obtain a smooth surface
S
together with a proper surjective morphism
S\to B
to a curve
B
, such that almost every fibre is a plane rational quartic curve with a unique singular point.
This is reminiscent of the theory of elliptic surfaces
[
18
]
, where almost all fibres are elliptic curves, or of the theory of quasi-elliptic surfaces
[
2
,
9
]
, where almost all fibres are plane cuspidal cubic curves.
The fibres of
S\to B
that are integral are finite in number, and we call them the
bad fibres
of the fibration.

2.
Geometrically rational function fields of genus three in characteristic two

Given a regular proper geometrically integral curve
C
over a field
K
of characteristic
p\geq 0
, its function field
F|K=K(C)|K
is a
one-dimensional separable function field
, that is,
F|K
is a separably generated field extension of transcendence degree
1
, with
K
algebraically closed in
F
.
Conversely, every one-dimensional separable function field
F|K
is the function field of some curve
C|K
of the above type.

Let
F|K
be a one-dimensional separable function field of genus
g=3
.
We assume that
F|K
is geometrically rational, that is, the extended one-dimensional separable function field
\overline{K}F|\overline{K}=\overline{K}\otimes_{K}F|\overline{K}
has genus
\overline{g}=0
.
The strict inequality
\overline{g}<g
can only occur in characteristic
p>0
, in which case the genus drop
g-\overline{g}
is a multiple of
(p-1)/2
by Tate’s genus change formula
[
22
]
, so we conclude
p\in\{2,3,7\}
.
The cases
p=3
and
p=7
were studied by Salomão
[
15
,
16
]
and the second author
[
21
]
.
In this section we assume that
p=2
.

For each
n\geq 0
let
g_{n}
be the genus of the
n
-th
Frobenius pullback

F_{n}|K:=F^{p^{n}}{\cdot}K|K
of
F|K
, where
F_{n}=F^{p^{n}}{\cdot}K
is the only intermediate field of
F|K
such that
F|F_{n}
is purely inseparable of degree
p^{n}
.
Note that
F_{n}|K
is the function field of the
n
-th normalized Frobenius pullback
C_{n}|K=\widetilde{C^{(p^{n})}}|K
of
C|K
.

According to
[
7
, Corollary 2.7 (iii)]
we have
g_{1}\leq 1
and
g_{n}=\overline{g}=0
for
n\geq 2
.
If
g_{1}=0
, then
F_{1}|K
will be a quadratic subfield of genus zero of
F|K
, hence
F|K
will be hyperelliptic.
Therefore, as in this paper we are interested in non-hyperelliptic function fields, we assume throughout that the Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K
has genus
g_{1}=1
, i.e.,
F_{1}|K
is a quasi-elliptic function field (see
[
6
, Section 2]
).

In view of
[
7
, Proposition 2.4]
and Rosenlicht’s genus drop formula
[
7
, Formula 2.3]
, the assumption
g_{1}=1
means that there exists a unique singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
,
whose restrictions
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
to the Frobenius pullbacks
F_{n}|K
have geometric singularity degrees
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=3
,
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=1
and
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})=0
for
n\geq 2
.
In particular
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is the only singular prime of the quasi-elliptic Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K
.

Moreover, by
[
7
, Corollary 2.19]
the singular prime
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed, i.e., there is a unique prime in
\overline{K}F|\overline{K}
that lies over
\mathfrak{p}
, and so by
[
7
, Theorem 2.24]
the restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational for
n\geq 3
.
In particular, for each
n\geq 3
the genus zero function field
F_{n}|K
is rational.

In this section we study the function fields
F|K
that satisfy the above properties plus the additional condition that the divisor
\mathfrak{p}
is not canonical.
The case where the divisor
\mathfrak{p}
is canonical was analyzed in
[
6
, Section 3]
.
We divide the discussion into two major parts, treating first the case where the non-singular restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is rational.

Theorem 2.1
.

A one-dimensional separable function field
F|K
of characteristic

and genus

is geometrically rational and admits a prime
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=3
,
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=1
,
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
is rational, and such that

is not a canonical divisor, if and only if

is generated by three functions

\displaystyle z^{2}

\displaystyle=(c_{0}+c_{1}x+x^{2})(c_{0}A_{2}+c_{1}^{-1}+c_{1}A_{2}x+A_{2}x^{2}),

\displaystyle y^{2}

\displaystyle=(c_{0}+c_{1}x+x^{2})(B_{0}+B_{1}x+z),

Proof.

In particular

The function

is a separating variable
of

is separable, i.e.,

, i.e.,

These functions satisfy the incidence properties

and

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=K\oplus Kx,

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kx^{2},

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kx^{2}\oplus Kz;

see

(1)

H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{4})=K\oplus Kx\oplus Kx^{2}\oplus Kx^{3}\oplus Kx^{4}\oplus Kz\oplus Kxz\oplus Kx^{2}z.

Let

.
As

\mathfrak{p}^{e}
has degree

\deg(\mathfrak{p}^{e})=[F:F_{1}]\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=4=2g-2,

of the divisors

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{ne})

\displaystyle=4n-2\quad\text{if $n\geq 2$},

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{e})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}3&\text{if $\mathfrak{p}^{e}$ is a canonical divisor},\\

.

4.
Universal fibrations by plane projective rational quartic curves

We now look at the fibrations that can be constructed with the three families of curves in Theorem
3.9
, or equivalently, with the last three families in Theorem
1.1
.
To this end we fix an algebraically closed ground field
k
of characteristic 2.
We consider the three integral hypersurfaces

Z_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{4},\quad Z_{4}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{3},\quad Z_{5}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{A}^{4}

whose points
((x:y:z),(a,b,c,d))
,
((x:y:z),(a,b,c))
,
((x:y:z),(a,b,c,d))
satisfy the relations

\displaystyle by^{4}+dz^{4}+y^{2}z^{2}+xz^{3}+(b+b^{2}c^{3})x^{2}z^{2}+ax^{2}y^{2}+ax^{3}z+(ab^{2}c^{3}+a^{2}d)x^{4}=0,

\displaystyle y^{4}+az^{4}+xz^{3}+bx^{3}z+cx^{4}=0,

\displaystyle y^{4}+dz^{2}y^{2}+(c+a)z^{4}+dxz^{3}+bd\,x^{2}y^{2}+x^{2}z^{2}+bd\,x^{3}z+b^{2}cx^{4}=0,

respectively.
The projection morphisms

\pi_{3}:Z_{3}\to\mathbb{A}^{4},\quad\pi_{4}:Z_{4}\to\mathbb{A}^{3},\quad\pi_{5}:Z_{5}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}

yield families of plane projective rational quartic curves, whose generic fibres are curves defined over the function fields of the bases, given as in items
1
,
2
and
3
in Theorem
3.9
, respectively.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\phi:T\to B
be a proper dominant morphism of irreducible smooth
algebraic varieties whose generic fibre is a non-hyperelliptic geometrically rational curve
C
over
k(B)
of genus
3
, that admits a non-smooth point
\mathfrak{p}
that is not a canonical divisor on
C
.
Then the fibration
\phi:T\to B
is, up to birational equivalence, a dominant base extension of one of the fibrations
\pi_{3}
,
\pi_{4}
or
\pi_{5}
.

The theorem states that the three fibrations
\pi_{i}
are universal in the sense that any fibration whose generic fibre satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.9
is obtained from one of them by a base extension.
Its proof is analogous to that of
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
, and so we omit it.

We next describe the fibres of the fibrations
\pi_{i}
.
Generically, they behave in the same manner as the geometric generic fibres
C_{\overline{K}}
discussed at the end of the preceding section. But there are special fibres that behave differently.

We start with the fibration
\pi_{3}:Z_{3}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}
.
If
b=0
then the fibre
\pi_{3}^{-1}(P)
over the point
P=(a,b,c)
is the union of a smooth quadric and a double line, hence it is reducible and non-reduced.
If
b\neq 0
then we are in the generic case, where the fibre behaves in the same way as the geometric generic fibre
C_{\overline{K}}
described in the previous section.
Precisely,
\pi_{3}^{-1}(P)
is a plane rational quartic curve with a unique singular point of multiplicity 2 (if
\iota:=bc^{3}\neq 1
) or 3 (if
\iota=bc^{3}=1
),
whose unique tangent line meets the curve only at that point; moreover, each of the remaining tangent lines is a bitangent, and all of them pass through a common intersection point, i.e., the curve is strange.

Next we consider the fibration
\pi_{4}:Z_{4}\to\mathbb{A}^{3}
.
If
b\neq 0
then we are in the generic case, i.e., the fibre
\pi_{4}^{-1}(P)
over the point
P=(a,b,c)
is a plane rational quartic curve with a unique singular point of multiplicity 2, and with all its tangent lines meeting in a unique common point, i.e., the curve is strange; in addition, every tangent line intersects the curve at a unique point, hence every non-singular point is a non-ordinary inflection point.
In the opposite case
b=0
the fibre has the same properties as the fibres in the generic case, with only one difference: the singular point has multiplicity 3.

We next we consider the fibration
\pi_{5}:Z_{5}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}
.
If
d=0
then the fibre
\pi_{5}^{-1}(P)
over the point
P=(a,b,c,d)
is a double smooth quadric and is therefore non-reduced.
In the opposite case
d\neq 0
, the generic case occurs if and only if

\iota:=ab^{2}d^{2}\neq 1
.
In other words, if
\iota\neq 1
then the fibre is a plane rational quartic curve satisfying the following conditions: it has a unique singular point, which has a unique tangent line of multiplicity 2; all its tangent lines intersect in a unique common point; the tangent lines at the non-singular points are bitangents.
If
\iota=1
, then the fibre has the same properties as in the generic case, the only difference being that the multiplicity of the singular point becomes 3.

The total space
Z_{4}
of the fibration
\pi_{4}:Z_{4}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}
is smooth.
The total spaces
Z_{3}
and
Z_{5}
of
\pi_{3}:Z_{3}\to\mathbb{A}^{3}
and
\pi_{5}:Z_{5}\to\mathbb{A}^{4}
, on the other hand, are not smooth, but they become smooth after restricting the bases
\mathbb{A}^{3}
and
\mathbb{A}^{4}
to the dense open subsets
\{bc\neq 0\}
and
\{b\neq 0\}
respectively.

Combining Theorem
4.1
with Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 in
[
6
]
we obtain five universal fibrations from which every fibration by plane rational quartic curves arises as a base extension.

We end this section by showing that the total spaces
Z_{i}
are uniruled.
This will be a consequence of a more general result, valid in any characteristic
p>0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed ground field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
T\to B
be a dominant morphism of integral varieties whose generic fibre
C=T_{\eta}
is a regular geometrically integral curve over
K=k(B)
.
Assume that for some
n
the Frobenius pullback
C^{(p^{n})}|K
is rational.
Then the total space
T
is (inseparably) uniruled.
If in addition the base
B
is rational then
T
is (inseparably) unirational.

By specializing
n=3
we obtain the uniruledness of the varieties
Z_{i}
.
The cases
n=1
and
n=2
imply the well-known fact that quasi-elliptic surfaces are uniruled (see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 9.4]
or
[
4
, Corollary 4.1.16]
).

Remark 4.3
.

According to
[
17
, Lemma 1.2]
or
[
7
, Corollary 2.7]
, the assumption that
C^{(p^{n})}|K
is rational for some
n
is equivalent to the condition that the curve
C
is geometrically rational, i.e., the geometric generic fibre
C_{\overline{K}}=C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
is a rational curve over
\overline{K}
, i.e., most of the fibres of
T\to B
are rational curves.

Proof.

Note first that the generic fibre
C
of the fibration
T\to B
has function field

F:=K(C)=k(T).

If
n=0
then by assumption the generic fibre
C|K
is rational, say
F=K(t)
for some
t
transcendental over
K
, i.e.,
k(T)=k(B)(t)
, and so the total space
T
is birationally equivalent to
B\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, i.e., it is ruled over
B
.

Let us look at the case
n>0
.
Let
B^{\prime}
be the integral
\Spec(k)
-scheme whose underlying topological space is equal to
B
and whose spaces of sections are the sub-
k
-algebras of
\overline{K}
defined by

\mathcal{O}_{B^{\prime}}(U):=\mathcal{O}_{B}(U)^{p^{-n}}
, with
U\subset B
open.
This scheme has function field
k(B^{\prime})=K^{\prime}:=K^{p^{-n}}
, and it is isomorphic, as an abstract scheme, to
B
via the
n
th absolute Frobenius morphism
B\to B^{\prime}
that raises sections to their
p^{n}
-powers.
However,
B
and
B^{\prime}
are not necessarily isomorphic as
\Spec(k)
-schemes.
In fact
B^{\prime}
is, up to isomorphism, the
\Spec(k)
-scheme

B\to\Spec(k)\to\Spec(k)

whose structure morphism has been modified by composing with the
n
th absolute Frobenius morphism
F_{k}^{n}:\Spec(k)\to\Spec(k)
.

The inclusion homomorphisms
\mathcal{O}_{B}(U)\to\mathcal{O}_{B^{\prime}}(U)
define a purely inseparable
\Spec(k)
-morphism

B^{\prime}\to B

of degree
[K^{\prime}:K]=p^{n\dim(B)}
.
In turn we get an extended fibration
T^{\prime}:=T\times_{B}B^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
, whose total space
T^{\prime}
has function field

F^{\prime}:=k(T^{\prime})=FK^{\prime}=FK^{p^{-n}}.

In particular the morphism
T^{\prime}\to T
is purely inseparable of degree
[F^{\prime}:F]=[K^{\prime}:K]=p^{n\dim(B)}
.
Moreover the function field
F^{\prime}|K^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the function field
F^{p^{n}}{\cdot}K|K
of the Frobenius pullback
C^{(p^{n})}|K
, via the
n
th absolute Frobenius homomorphism.
It follows that the function field
F^{\prime}|K^{\prime}
is rational by assumption, and as in the case
n=0
we conclude that
T^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
B^{\prime}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Thus the composition

B^{\prime}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\dashrightarrow T^{\prime}\to T
is a dominant purely inseparable rational map of degree
p^{n\dim(B)}
,
thereby proving that
T
is (inseparably) uniruled.

To complete the proof it remains to note that if
B
is rational then so is
B^{\prime}
.
∎

For a similar proof in terms of classical algebraic geometry we refer to
[
6
, Section 5]
.
Note that since
T
is
inseparably
uniruled, it does not necessarily have negative Kodaira dimension (see
[
11
, p. 265]
).

Since the normalized Frobenius pullbacks
C_{1}|K
of the curves
C|K
in the preceding section are quasi-elliptic, the proof of the proposition yields the following consequence for surfaces (compare
[
4
, Corollary 4.1.16]
).
As before, we assume that the algebraically closed ground field
k
has characteristic
p=2
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Every smooth surface admitting a fibration by rational quartic curves admits a purely inseparable cover of degree
p=2
by a quasi-elliptic surface.

5.
A pencil of rational quartics in characteristic two

In this section we discuss the pencil of rational quartics obtained from Theorem
3.9

2
, by specializing
a=c=0
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed ground field of characteristic two.
We consider the integral projective algebraic surface

S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

of the pairs
((x:y:z),(t_{0}:t_{1}))
that satisfy the bihomogeneous equation

t_{0}(y^{4}+xz^{3})+t_{1}x^{3}z=0.

This defines a pencil of plane projective quartic curves, whose base points (i.e., the common points of its members) are equal to
(1:0:0)
and
(0:0:1)
.
For each point of the form
(1:c)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the corresponding member is the plane projective integral curve cut out by the equation

y^{4}+xz^{3}+cx^{3}z=0.

The only singular point
(1:0:c^{1/2})
of the curve is unibranch of multiplicity
2
(if
c\neq 0
) or
3
(if
c=0
).
The tangent line at the singular point as well as the tangent line at each non-singular point does not intersect the curve at any other point.
Thus the non-singular points of the quartic curve are non-ordinary inflection points.
Moreover the curve is strange, because its tangent lines pass through the common point
(0:1:0)
.
The member of the pencil corresponding to the point
(0:1)
, on the other hand, degenerates to the non-reduced reducible curve
V(xz^{3})
consisting of the line
V(x)
and the triple line
V(z^{3})

By the Jacobian criterion, the surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
has exactly two singular points, namely
P:=((1:0:0),(1:0))
and
Q:=((0:0:1),(0:1))
. The fibres of the second projection morphism

S\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}

are up to isomorphism the members of the above pencil.
The first projection

S\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}

is a birational morphism whose inverse

\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow S,\quad(x:y:z)\mapsto\big((x:y:z),(x^{3}z:y^{4}+xz^{3})\big)

is not defined only at the base points
(1:0:0)
and
(0:0:1)
.
More precisely, the morphism
S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
contracts the horizontal lines
(1:0:0)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
(0:0:1)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the base points
(1:0:0)
and
(0:0:1)
, and restricts to an isomorphism

S\setminus((1:0:0)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\cup(0:0:1)\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\{(1:0:0),(0:0:1)\}.

The inverse map
\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is equal to
(\textrm{id},\tau)
, where
\tau
is the rational map
\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, i.e.,

\tau:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1},\quad(x:y:z)\mapsto(x^{3}z:y^{4}+xz^{3}),

which is also undefined only at
(1:0:0)
and
(0:0:1)
.

To resolve the singularities of the rational surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
we resolve the indeterminacies of the rational map
\tau:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
This provides us with a commutative diagram

Preoperative FIB‐4 Index as a Potential Factor Associated With Severe Postoperative Complications and Endogenous Organ Failure After Hepatectomy for Hepatocellular Carcinoma
Authors: Masanori Nakamura, Yukiyasu Okamura, Osamu Aramaki, Nao Yoshida, Yusuke Mitsuka, Hayato Abe, Shunsuke Yamagishi, Yasuhiro Okumura, Kyongsun Pak, Shinobu Masuda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundLiver fibrosis influences tolerance to surgical stress, dictating postoperative complication risks after hepatectomy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). While conventional systems assess hepatic function, they may not fully capture structural fibrosis. This study evaluated the whether preoperative Fibrosis‐4 (FIB‐4) index is associated with severe complications and “endogenous organ failure” (EOF: systemic decompensation unaccompanied by localized surgical‐site complications).MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 1778 HCC patients undergoing hepatectomy between 2001 and 2020. A restricted cubic spline (RCS) model identified the optimal preoperative FIB‐4 cutoff for predicting major complications (Clavien‐Dindo [C‐D] Grade ≥ IIIa). Following stratification by this cutoff, intergroup comparisons and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of major and severe (C‐D Grade ≥ IIIb) complications.ResultsRCS analysis suggested a FIB‐4 threshold of 5.0 for an increased trend in complication risk. The High FIB‐4 index group (≥ 5.0, n = 385) showed a higher frequency of severe complications (7.0% vs. 3.4%) and EOF (3.1% vs. 0.4%) compared to the Low FIB‐4 index group (< 5.0, n = 1393). Among patients developing life‐threatening complications (C‐D Grade ≥ IV), 92.3% of the High FIB‐4 index group presented with EOF. In multivariable analyses, FIB‐4 ≥ 5.0 was consistently and independently associated with both major and severe complications.ConclusionA preoperative FIB‐4 ≥ 5.0 was associated with an increased incidence of severe complications, particularly EOF, after hepatectomy. This simple index could serve as a clinical indicator of systemic vulnerability, enhancing surgical risk stratification beyond traditional functional markers.Keywords: endogenous organ failure, FIB‐4 index, hepatectomy, hepatocellular carcinoma, liver fibrosis, postoperative complications

Introduction

Hepatic resection remains the established treatment of curative treatment for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [1]. While multidisciplinary approaches have recently shifted the treatment paradigm for HCC, challenges such as high postoperative recurrence and potentially fatal surgical complications remain [2]. The severity of background liver fibrosis is a critical determinant of these outcomes, as advanced fibrosis compromises the liver's regenerative capacity and reduces its tolerance to surgical stress [3].

Standardizing the preoperative evaluation of liver fibrosis remains a challenge [4]. While liver biopsy is the gold standard, its clinical utility is limited by its invasive nature. Non‐invasive imaging modalities, such as transient elastography, provide objective measurements of liver stiffness; however, these techniques require specialized equipment, additional costs, and lack consensus on optimal cutoff values for surgical risk stratification [5]. Furthermore, conventional scoring systems, such as the Child‐Pugh classification, the albumin‐bilirubin (ALBI) score, or indocyanine green retention rate at 15 min (ICG‐R15), primarily focus on hepatic synthetic and excretory functions. While these are useful for assessing functional reserve, they may not adequately reflect the extent of structural fibrosis or the underlying inflammatory state and portal hemodynamics that dictate systemic vulnerability [4, 6].

The Fibrosis‐4 (FIB‐4) index offers a practical alternative [7]. Originally developed to stage liver fibrosis in patients with viral hepatitis, it is calculated using four routinely available parameters: age, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and platelet count. Unlike imaging or biopsy, the FIB‐4 index requires no additional cost or special equipment, making it widely applicable in routine practice [5].

The aim of this exploratory study, involving a large cohort of 1778 patients, was to evaluate whether the preoperative FIB‐4 index could serve as an objective tool to stratify the risk of severe (postoperative) complications and “endogenous organ failure” (EOF)—systemic decompensation independent of local surgical complications—in patients undergoing hepatectomy for HCC.

Methods

Patient Selection and Study Design

This was a retrospective cohort study of consecutive patients who underwent therapeutic hepatectomy for HCC at our institution between January 2001 and December 2020. Among all patients who underwent surgery during this period, those with missing data regarding evaluation items were excluded. Finally, 1778 patients who underwent therapeutic hepatectomy were included in the analysis.

The FIB‐4 index was calculated using the following formula [7]: FIB‐4 index score = Age (years) × AST (U/L)/(Platelet count (109/L) × √[ALT (U/L)]).

This study was conducted with the approval of the Institutional Review Board of Nihon University Itabashi Hospital (approval number: RK‐211012‐5). Regarding patient consent, due to the retrospective observational nature of the study, an opt‐out method was employed to guarantee the opportunity to refuse participation in the study.

Surgical Procedures

The surgical procedure and extent of resection for each case were determined at a weekly departmental conference, considering the balance between tumor progression and liver functional reserve. To evaluate liver functional reserve, the ALBI score [6], modified ALBI (mALBI) grade [8], ICG‐R15 [9], Child‐Pugh classification [10], and the degree of liver damage [11] were used.

Hepatectomy procedures were classified according to the Brisbane 2000 and New World terminology [12, 13]. In this study, major hepatectomy was defined as the resection of three or more Couinaud's liver segments [14].

Parenchymal transection was performed using the clamp‐crush technique. Vascular structures were processed using ligation with sutures or clips, or sealing with energy devices. To control intraoperative bleeding, the Pringle maneuver (intermittent inflow occlusion of the hepatoduodenal ligament: 15 min of ischemia followed by 5 min of reperfusion) was routinely applied [15].

Anesthetic management aimed to minimize hepatic vein bleeding by restricting fluid infusion and adjusting ventilation to avoid elevation of central venous pressure.

Evaluation Items and Definitions

Outcome Definitions

Postoperative complications were evaluated based on the Clavien‐Dindo (C‐D) classification and standardized into three hierarchical tiers [16].

Major Complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or Higher)

Postoperative complications requiring any surgical, endoscopic, or radiological intervention, regardless of the type of anesthesia applied.

Severe Complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or Higher)

Postoperative complications requiring interventions that must be performed specifically under general anesthesia.

Life‐Threatening Complications (C‐D Grade IV or Higher)

Critical complications presenting with single or multi‐organ dysfunction requiring intensive care unit management.

Specific Organ Failures

Specific organ failures, comprising liver, renal, coagulation, circulation, central nervous, and respiratory failure, were defined by a score of 3 or higher on each corresponding component of the Chronic Liver Failure‐Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (CLIF‐SOFA) system [17].

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) Severity

AKI severity was defined by the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria (Stage ≥ 2) [18].

Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)

ARDS was defined by the Berlin definition (moderate to severe) [19].

Endogenous Organ Failure (EOF)

EOF was defined as life‐threatening complications (C‐D Grade IV or higher) presenting with organ failure unaccompanied by localized surgical‐site complications, meeting two criteria:

Physiological identification: Organ failure verified by a CLIF‐C ACLF score ≥ 51 [20].

Physiological identification: Organ failure verified by a CLIF‐C ACLF score ≥ 51 [20].

Exclusion of cases with localized surgical‐site complications: Cases with localized surgical‐site complications were excluded from the analysis. These complications included post‐hepatectomy hemorrhage (PHH; Grade B or C) and bile leakage (PHBL; Grade B or C) defined by International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) criteria, as well as organ/space surgical site infection (SSI) or intra‐abdominal abscess defined by Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines [19, 21, 22].

Exclusion of cases with localized surgical‐site complications: Cases with localized surgical‐site complications were excluded from the analysis. These complications included post‐hepatectomy hemorrhage (PHH; Grade B or C) and bile leakage (PHBL; Grade B or C) defined by International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) criteria, as well as organ/space surgical site infection (SSI) or intra‐abdominal abscess defined by Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines [19, 21, 22].

To minimize observer bias, a co‐author independent of the first author collected clinical data for life‐threatening cases, and EOF was systematically determined based on these objective criteria.

Primary Endpoint

Severe complications and EOF.

Other Items

Ascites (defined as ascitic fluid retention judged to require drainage due to concerns regarding potential clinical adverse effects) and postoperative in‐hospital mortality (within 90 days) were also evaluated.

Statistical Analyses

Continuous variables are expressed as median (range) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi‐squared test or Fisher's exact test.

A restricted cubic spline (RCS) model was employed to analyze the association between the FIB‐4 index and the incidence of major postoperative complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher) [22].

In the subgroup analysis of the 24 patients who developed C‐D Grade IV or higher complications, categorical clinical outcomes and organ‐specific failure rates were compared between the High and Low FIB‐4 index groups using Fisher's exact test. Continuous physiological scoring metrics, including CLIF‐SOFA, CLIF‐C OF, and CLIF‐C ACLF scores, were expressed as median (range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test.

To evaluate the baseline confounding factors and control for potential overfitting, multivariable logistic regression models were constructed using the forced‐entry method. The model explicitly included a high FIB‐4 index (≥ 5.0) and advanced age (80 years or older) to isolate the prognostic impact of liver fibrosis from chronological aging, alongside other critical covariates: platelet count (less than 10.0 × 104/μL), high mALBI grade (Grade 2b or higher, using a raw score cutoff of more than −2.60), high ICGR15 (more than 15%), and Child‐Pugh class B. To justify the modeling strategy against the limited number of events, the Events Per Variable (EPV) ratio was evaluated to ensure model stability [23]. To assess the internal validity of the predictive models for Clavien‐Dindo (C‐D) Grade IIIa or higher and Grade IIIb or higher complications, internal validation was performed via a bootstrap resampling procedure with 200 replications (B = 200). The apparent Area Under the Curve (AUC) and the optimism‐corrected AUC were calculated to evaluate the degree of overoptimism within this cohort. These validation analyses were executed using R version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with the boot and pROC packages [24].

The relationship between the FIB‐4 index and the ALBI score was evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. To assess potential multicollinearity, the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) was calculated both for all covariates entered into the multivariable models and specifically for the bivariate linear regression analysis between the FIB‐4 index and the continuous ALBI score. In this study, a VIF < 5 was predefined as the threshold indicating the absence of substantial multicollinearity, supporting the statistical independence of the indices and justifying their simultaneous inclusion in the clinical assessment models [25].

Given the retrospective and exploratory nature of this study, the sample size was not pre‐determined based on a specific primary hypothesis. Consequently, p‐values were interpreted as descriptive indicators of associations within this specific cohort rather than definitive evidence of statistical significance in a hypothesis‐testing framework. This study primarily serves as a hypothesis‐generating analysis to guide future prospective research.

All statistical analyses were performed using EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), which is a graphical user interface for R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) [23]. RStudio (Posit, Boston, MA, USA) was used for analyses using the RCS model and for graph creation.

Results

Determination of Cutoff Value and RCS Analysis

An analysis using a restricted cubic spline (RCS) model was performed on all 1778 patients to evaluate the relationship between the preoperative FIB‐4 index and the probability of major complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher). In the full cohort (2001–2020), the risk of complications showed an increasing trend starting from a FIB‐4 index of approximately 2.5, followed by a steep increase when the index exceeded 5.0 (Figure 1). This risk trajectory continued to rise until the index reached approximately 7.5, after which it plateaued. To address potential confounding factors associated with chronological shifts in surgical indications, techniques, and perioperative care over the 20‐year study span, a separate RCS analysis was conducted within the contemporary subgroup (2011–2020; n = 1113) to confirm the stability of this threshold (Figure S1). The risk profile within this contemporary subgroup remained highly consistent with that of the full cohort, demonstrating an inflection point starting near 2.5, a subsequent increase acceleration beyond 5.0, and a subsequent plateau around 7.5 (Figure S1). Based on these consistent data distributions across both the full cohort and the contemporary subgroup, a clinical cutoff value of 5.0 was adopted for subsequent analyses. Consequently, patients were classified into two groups based on this threshold: the High FIB‐4 index group (FIB‐4 index ≥ 5.0, n = 385) and the Low FIB‐4 index group (FIB‐4 index < 5.0, n = 1393).

Patient Characteristics

Of the total cohort of 1778 patients, 385 (21.7%) were in the High FIB‐4 index group and 1393 (78.3%) were in the Low FIB‐4 index group (Figure 2). Patient characteristics are shown in Table 1. The High FIB‐4 index group was older (median 71 vs. 68 years, p < 0.001). Regarding viral etiology, the High FIB‐4 index group had a lower prevalence of Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) (9.1% vs. 20.2%, p < 0.001), but a higher prevalence of antibody to Hepatitis C Virus (Anti‐HCV) (73.2% vs. 42.0%, p < 0.001) compared to the Low FIB‐4 index group. Furthermore, items indicating liver function and hepatic reserve such as albumin, platelets, prothrombin activity, ICG‐R15, and ALBI score were worse in the High FIB‐4 index group. Additionally, the rate of histological liver cirrhosis was higher in the High FIB‐4 index group (63.4% vs. 24.5%, p < 0.001).

Regarding surgical factors, the High FIB‐4 index group had a lower rate of major hepatectomy (1.8% vs. 7.0%, p < 0.001) and systematic hepatectomy (15.7% vs. 32.2%, p < 0.001). Total resected liver weight was also lower in the High FIB‐4 index group (40 vs. 62 g, p < 0.001). Tumor size was smaller in the High FIB‐4 index group (25 vs. 29 mm, p < 0.001). However, despite undergoing less invasive surgeries for smaller tumors, intraoperative blood loss was higher in the High FIB‐4 index group compared to the Low FIB‐4 index group (300 vs. 255 mL, p < 0.001).

Postoperative Complication Rates

Postoperative complications are shown in Table 2. In the total cohort, the incidence of major complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher) was higher in the High FIB‐4 index group (36.6% vs. 22.7%, p < 0.001). The High FIB‐4 index group also demonstrated higher rates of severe complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or higher) (7.0% vs. 3.4%, p = 0.003) and life‐threatening complications (C‐D Grade IV or higher) (3.4% vs. 0.8%, p = 0.004). The incidence of endogenous organ failure (EOF) was higher in the High FIB‐4 index group (3.1% vs. 0.4%, p < 0.001). To evaluate whether advancements in surgical techniques and perioperative care over time affected these outcomes, a subgroup analysis was performed restricting the cohort to patients who underwent surgery during the most recent decade (2011–2020, N = 1113). Consistent with the overall cohort, the High FIB‐4 index group continued to demonstrate higher rates of severe complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or higher) and EOF, confirming that these results remain robust regardless of historical changes in clinical practice (Tables S1 and S2).

Multivariable Analysis and Internal Validation for Postoperative Complications

A total of 1778 patients were analyzed in the fully adjusted multivariable logistic regression models. In the fully adjusted models, a preoperative FIB‐4 index ≥ 5.0 showed an association with major complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher) (Odds ratio [OR] = 1.77, 95% confidence Interval [CI]: 1.26–2.47, p < 0.001) and severe complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or higher) (OR = 2.19, 95% CI: 1.10–4.33, p = 0.024) (Table 3). Advanced age (≥ 80 years) was associated with major complications (OR = 1.52, 95% CI: 1.01–2.28, p = 0.044), but not with severe complications (p = 0.076). A high mALBI grade (Grade 2b or 3) also showed an association with major complications (OR = 1.38, 95% CI: 1.01–1.88, p = 0.044), whereas it was not associated with severe complications (p = 0.600). Child‐Pugh class B showed an association with severe complications (OR = 3.23, 95% CI: 1.11–9.41, p = 0.031).

Collinearity diagnostics confirmed the absence of significant multicollinearity among the entered covariates. The Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values for all parameters, including the FIB‐4 index (VIF = 1.90) and mALBI grade (VIF = 1.32), were low and remained below the predefined threshold of 5.0 (Table S3).

Internal validation via 200‐fold bootstrap resampling was conducted to evaluate the predictive performance within this cohort. For the major complications (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher) model, the apparent AUC was 0.5908, and the optimism‐corrected AUC was 0.5811 (optimism value = 0.0097). For the severe complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or higher) model, the apparent AUC was 0.6167, and the optimism‐corrected AUC was 0.5810 (optimism value = 0.0357) (Table S4).

Details of the 24 Cases That Developed Life‐Threatening Postoperative Complications (C‐D Grade IV or Higher)

Details of the 24 cases with C‐D Grade IV or higher complications are summarized in Table S5. Within this subgroup, most patients experienced systemic organ dysfunction, as indicated by elevated physiological severity scores in both groups (Table 4). Specifically, no clear differences were observed between the High and Low FIB‐4 groups in total CLIF‐SOFA scores (median 12 vs. 14, p = 0.486), CLIF‐C OF scores (median 12 vs. 13, p = 0.861), or CLIF‐C ACLF scores (median 67.0 vs. 69.9, p = 0.820), with both groups exhibiting high median values. Likewise, no clear differences were observed in individual physiological organ failure rates, including liver (30.8% vs. 9.1%, p = 0.327), coagulation (69.2% vs. 45.5%, p = 0.408), central nervous system (46.2% vs. 63.6%, p = 0.444), renal (38.5% vs. 54.5%, p = 0.682), and respiratory failures (23.1% vs. 36.4%, p = 0.659), as well as moderate‐to‐severe ARDS (38.5% vs. 27.3%, p = 0.679) and AKI (Stage ≥ 2: 30.8% vs. 27.3%, p = 1.00). Furthermore, among patients who developed C‐D Grade IV or higher complications, the incidence of concurrent local surgical‐site complications (defined as PHH, PBHL, or intra‐abdominal abscess) was 7.7% in the High FIB‐4 group and 54.5% in the Low FIB‐4 group (p = 0.023). In contrast to these differing incidences of local complications, the prevalence of EOF was significantly higher in the High FIB‐4 group than in the Low FIB‐4 group (92.3% vs. 27.3%, p = 0.002), despite both groups showing similar profiles of physiological impairment. No clear association was observed when patients were stratified by mALBI grade (87.5% in Grade 2b/3 vs. 62.5% in Grade 1/2a, p = 0.352).

Predictive Performance and Multicollinearity Assessment

The predictive performance of the preoperative indicators for C‐D Grade IIIb or higher complications was evaluated using ROC curves (Figure S2). The AUC for the FIB‐4 index was 0.592, compared with 0.607 for the ALBI score and 0.567 for ICG‐R15. Based on DeLong's test, these indicators exhibited comparable predictive performance (p = 0.703 for ALBI score and p = 0.478 for ICG‐R15).

To assess the specific relationship and collinearity between the FIB‐4 index and the continuous ALBI score, bivariate correlation and linear regression analyses were performed (Figure S3). Correlation analysis revealed a moderate correlation between the two indices (Spearman's rho = 0.433, p < 0.001). In this bivariate assessment, the calculated VIF for the FIB‐4 index and ALBI score was 1.214, consistent with their statistical independence.

Discussion

In this retrospective study of 1778 patients, the preoperative FIB‐4 index was identified as a factor consistently associated with both major (C‐D Grade IIIa or higher) and more severe complications (C‐D Grade IIIb or higher) in multivariable analyses. Notably, while functional markers such as the ALBI score and ICG‐R15 showed relevance in univariable analysis, they lost their independent association in our Multivariable models. The FIB‐4 index, which captures both active liver inflammation (via AST/ALT) and portal hypertension‐related thrombocytopenia (via platelet count) [7, 26], functions as a comprehensive surrogate marker for identifying systemic vulnerability to major surgical stress.

Previous studies have linked elevated FIB‐4 levels to post‐hepatectomy liver failure and long‐term prognosis, often using hepatological cutoffs such as 3.25 or 4.16 [5, 27, 28, 29]. In contrast, our study employed a RCS model to mathematically describe the non‐linear relationship between FIB‐4 and complications [24]. This analysis suggested that a cutoff of 5.0 identifies a specific threshold where a more pronounced increase in physiological risk—stemming from impaired compensatory mechanisms against surgical trauma, hemorrhage, and ischemia–reperfusion injury—is observed. The statistical independence of this marker was further supported by a low VIF when compared with the ALBI score, demonstrating that FIB‐4 evaluates structural/inflammatory damage from a perspective distinct from that of routine functional markers [23].

A key contribution of this study is the definition and evaluation of EOF. The clinical utility of the FIB‐4 index was further underscored in predicting the most life‐threatening outcomes. Among patients who developed C‐D Grade IV or higher complications, 92.3% in the high FIB‐4 group presented with EOF, which was associated with a high 50% 90‐day hospital mortality rate. Notably, our findings demonstrate that life‐threatening crises in patients with a FIB‐4 index ≥ 5.0 manifest primarily as refractory ascites and systemic physiological decompensation, rather than isolated surgical‐site events. Furthermore, the higher intraoperative blood loss observed in the high FIB‐4 group—despite undergoing less invasive resections for smaller tumors—further corroborates the presence of severe underlying portal hypertension and coagulopathy.

Our detailed analysis of the 24 life‐threatening cases (C‐D Grade ≥ IV) clearly highlights the distinct clinical impact of preoperative liver fibrosis on the mechanism of postoperative deterioration. In patients with a high FIB‐4 index (≥ 5.0), EOF was observed in most cases (100% in Pattern 1 and 83.3% in Pattern 2), regardless of their preoperative mALBI grade. This finding indicates that severe liver fibrosis itself, rather than conventional functional grading alone, poses a major risk factor for systemic decompensation after hepatectomy. Conversely, life‐threatening complications in patients with a low FIB‐4 index and good mALBI grade (Pattern 4) were accompanied by localized surgical‐site complications (50%) or specific incidental comorbidities, rather than primary subclinical organ failure, demonstrating a lower EOF incidence (30.0%) compared to the high FIB‐4 groups.

The systemic decompensation observed in the high FIB‐4 group shares phenotypic similarities with acute‐on‐chronic liver failure (ACLF) [30]. In advanced liver fibrosis, underlying structural damage and portal hypertension may compromise the innate immune response and intestinal barrier function, leading to impaired clearance of inflammatory mediators and triggering systemic organ failure following major surgical stress. Notably, while systemic failure in the low FIB‐4 group was frequently accompanied by local surgical‐site complications (54.5%), EOF in the high FIB‐4 group occurred without concurrent local complications (92.3%). This disparity implies that a high FIB‐4 index effectively identifies a distinct state of structural hepatic fragility and systemic vulnerability predisposing to primary EOF. However, as portal pressure, liver stiffness, or circulating cytokines were not directly measured, these pathophysiological mechanisms remain hypothetical, requiring further prospective validation.

From a clinical perspective, the preoperative FIB‐4 index must be interpreted with caution. Given its modest single‐variable discriminative performance (AUC = 0.592), it is not intended to serve as a diagnostic classifier or a definitive decision‐making tool to determine surgical indications in isolation. Rather, our data suggest that the clinical utility of the FIB‐4 index lies in its role as a complementary reference marker within a multifactorial risk stratification framework.

While established classifications such as the mALBI grade primarily reflect the current synthetic function of the hepatocyte, the FIB‐4 index captures indicators of structural fibrosis and portal hemodynamics. Although these indices show a moderate correlation (Figure S3), the low variance inflation factors in our multivariable models indicate that they provide distinct clinical information (Table S3). Therefore, integrating the FIB‐4 index with conventional functional metrics could aid in identifying patients with structural hepatic fragility despite preserved synthetic capacity.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single‐center design over a 20‐year period (2001–2020) introduces potential historical biases and residual confounding from baseline imbalances, although sub‐period (2011–2020) analyses demonstrated consistent outcomes. Second, while internal validation was performed, the clinical cutoff of 5.0 was derived from the same cohort, and the lack of an independent external validation cohort limits immediate generalizability. Third, because this was an exploratory, post hoc analysis without a pre‐calculated sample size, the findings should be interpreted as hypothesis‐generating, with p‐values serving as descriptive indicators. Fourth, specific subgroup analyses for patients with Clavien‐Dindo Grade IV or higher complications were constrained by the small number of events, leaving some comparative assessments underpowered. Finally, the lack of direct measurements for portal hemodynamics, liver stiffness, or circulating inflammatory cytokines prevents definitive pathophysiological conclusions, leaving the underlying immunological mechanisms speculative.

In conclusion, a preoperative FIB‐4 index ≥ 5.0 was associated with severe postoperative complications and EOF in our cohort. By providing information related to structural liver damage and systemic vulnerability, it may serve as a potential tool for risk stratification, though its definitive medical utility must be confirmed in future studies.

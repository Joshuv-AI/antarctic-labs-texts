Fuzzy adaptive human memory optimization based optimal task scheduling in cloud computing
Authors: Padmaja Patel, K Murali Gopal, Nimai Charan Patel, Binod Kumar Sahu, Siba Prasada Tripathy, Muneera Altayeb, Mykhailo Panchyk, Ezzeddine Touti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTask scheduling has become a significant research focus in cloud computing because of the growing need for efficient resource utilization. This paper explores an innovative scheduling approach harnessing Human Memory Optimization (HMO) and Fuzzy Adaptive Human Memory Optimization (FAHMO). Such techniques are inspired by human cognitive principles and employ an adaptive search strategy that maintains an effective trade-off between exploration and exploitation. By maintaining a history of successful and unsuccessful scheduling decisions, HMO enables continuous enhancement of scheduling efficiency. Integrating fuzzy logic into FAHMO improves the decision-making process by effectively managing uncertainty and ambiguity in task scheduling, thereby producing more flexible and efficient solutions. Comparative analysis demonstrates that HMO and FAHMO outperform conventional metaheuristic algorithms, including PSO-PGA, in terms of convergence speed and task completion time. The results confirm that the proposed approach significantly reduces makespan and enhances overall cloud task scheduling performance. Specifically, FAHMO achieved up to 67.46% improvement in makespan and 63.18% in convergence accuracy compared to PSO-PGA.Keywords: Cloud computing, Cloudlet, Task scheduling, Makespan, Metaheuristic algorithm, Fuzzy adaptive human memory optimization, Human memory optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, the exponential growth of internet usage has significantly increased the demand for cloud computing services, which cater to a wide spectrum of users—ranging from individuals and small businesses to large enterprises1. Cloud computing enables users to access computational resources on-demand through virtualization technology, which abstracts the complexity of underlying hardware and system configurations. As users continuously submit a large volume of tasks for execution, efficiently allocating these tasks to limited resources such as Virtual Machines (VMs) becomes a major challenge. Hence, task scheduling plays a vital role in ensuring effective resource utilization, reduced execution time, and enhanced system performance.

One of the primary objectives of cloud task scheduling is to minimize the makespan, which refers to the total time required to execute all tasks on available VMs2. In addition, task scheduling must ensure compliance with Service Level Agreements (SLAs) that define performance and reliability standards for end-users. Efficient scheduling also contributes to better computational risk management, supports market-based strategies, and improves customer-based service provisioning. Cloud service platforms allow organizations to scale dynamically by buying and selling computational resources like storage and processing power3. Moreover, with the rise of green cloud computing, efforts are made to minimize energy consumption and reduce the number of active servers, thereby promoting sustainability4.

Given the NP-hard nature of the task scheduling problem, traditional optimization methods often fall short when dealing with large and dynamic environments. Consequently, heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms have gained popularity due to their ability to provide near-optimal solutions within a reasonable computational time frame5. Over time, several such algorithms have been developed to enhance throughput, reduce energy consumption, optimize cost, and maintain system stability—making cloud computing highly attractive for both academic and industrial research. Initial efforts focused on classical optimization techniques such as Hill Climbing, Gradient Descent, Newton’s Method, and Lagrange Multipliers, which are effective for convex and continuous optimization problems6. However, their applicability is limited in complex and non-linear scheduling scenarios. This led to the emergence of hybrid metaheuristic algorithms that combine the strengths of multiple optimization strategies to solve NP-complete scheduling problems more effectively. Swarm intelligence algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) are particularly suitable due to their robustness, scalability, and simplicity. These algorithms excel in exploration and exploitation of the search space and do not require detailed knowledge of the problem7. For instance, WOA has demonstrated superior performance over existing metaheuristics across various benchmark and structural optimization problems8,9.

The Improved JAYA (IJAYA) algorithm enhances scheduling efficiency by transforming continuous variables into discrete values, allowing it to handle discrete task scheduling more effectively. Empirical comparisons indicate that IJAYA outperforms existing approaches such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Bee Swarm Optimization (BSO), Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA), and the original JAYA algorithm with respect to makespan, response time, and resource utilization10. As the volume and complexity of cloud-based tasks continue to grow, the search space expands significantly, making conventional algorithms increasingly inadequate. In response to this challenge, a near-optimal solution for the multi-objective task scheduling problem has been proposed. This method reduces search time while maintaining solution quality and contributes to improvements in makespan, resource usage, energy efficiency, and throughput, thereby enhancing the overall performance of cloud computing systems11.

To further address the limitations of traditional scheduling techniques, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a powerful alternative. Algorithms such as Deep Reinforcement Learning–Based Task‑Scheduling (DRLBTSA) utilize Deep Q-learning Networks to dynamically learn optimal task-to-resource mappings. DRLBTSA adapts effectively to variations in task characteristics and workloads, offering significant improvements in makespan reduction compared to classical scheduling methods like Round Robin (RR), First-Come-First-Serve (FCFS), and Earliest Deadline First (EDF)12,13. Another promising strategy involves hybridizing optimization techniques—for example, the GA-based Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO-GA), which demonstrates improved convergence speed and reductions in makespan, cost, and energy consumption compared to standalone GWO, GA, and PSO methods. This hybrid approach has been statistically validated using analysis of variance (ANOVA)12. The Flower Pollination Algorithm (FPA), enhanced with crossover operators from Genetic Algorithms (GA) and strategies from the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), offers balanced exploration and exploitation, helping to avoid local optima in task scheduling problems14. Other heuristic approaches, such as list-based scheduling (e.g., Dynamic Scheduling List(DSL)) and Pareto-dominance strategies, have also been explored to improve task-to-resource allocation15,16. One of the most critical challenges in cloud computing remains optimizing task scheduling to reduce cost and execution time. Recent work has introduced evolutionary approaches like Particle Swarm Optimization with Crossover and Mutation (PSO-CM), which combines Particle Swarm Optimization with Genetic Algorithm operators to enhance global convergence and outperform traditional algorithms in terms of stability and speed17.

Recent studies propose diverse optimization-based task scheduling methods across cloud, fog, and edge environments. Mahfouz et al. introduced standard and improved Marine Predators Optimization (MPO) algorithms for fog computing to reduce makespan and enhance resource utilization18,19. Nandagopal et al. developed a hybrid Cuckoo Search–Transformer model to improve energy-efficient task scheduling in cloud systems20. Mali et al. proposed a federated reinforcement learning-based framework for dynamic and decentralized task scheduling in IoT-edge environments21. Salehnia et al. presented a multi-objective moth-flame optimization approach for IoT–Fog–Cloud networks to jointly optimize execution time, energy consumption, and load balancing22. Abd Elaziz et al. designed a hybrid intelligent optimization framework for sustainable edge computing to improve overall QoS performance23. Abu-Hashem et al. proposed an improved Black Widow Optimization algorithm to enhance scheduling efficiency and convergence in cloud environments24.

By leveraging min-min and max-min heuristics, the algorithm achieves better load distribution across virtual machines (VMs), reduces execution time, and significantly enhances search space exploration. Effective task scheduling and assignment are critical to the performance of heterogeneous multiprocessor systems, particularly in minimizing makespan. Heuristic-based scheduling methods often face variability in makespan due to the complexity of dynamic task loads. Therefore, a smart scheduling mechanism should intelligently prioritize subtasks based on their resource requirements. In this context, a novel approach based on the Cooperation Search Algorithm (CSA) has been proposed for addressing the heterogeneity and complexity of task allocation in cloud environments. The CSA leverages the strengths of metaheuristic techniques to determine optimal schedules, and its performance was evaluated under three different task scenarios. The results demonstrate that CSA consistently outperforms traditional algorithms, achieving makespan reductions of 2.1%, 7.9%, 8.8%, 3.4%, and 7.7% over GA, NGA, WOA, HHG, and GSA respectively26. In another significant advancement, a deep reinforcement learning-based collaborative scheduling approach has been developed to optimize various cloud workflow objectives, including makespan, cost, fairness, and continuity. This method begins by extracting both structural and temporal features to construct a robust scheduling feature set. A time-step adaptive scheduling mechanism is then used to eliminate irrelevant data during decision-making, improving agent learning efficiency. Furthermore, the algorithm integrates equilibrium-based priority strategies, preference scheduling, and a hybrid immediate-lag reward mechanism to align agent behavior with practical scheduling requirements. Empirical results from a custom simulation platform show that the proposed method increases the fairness index by 5.3% and improves makespan performance by 16.6% compared to four state-of-the-art techniques27. To address the rigidity and inefficiency of traditional schedulers, a new model has been proposed for high-performance computing (HPC) environments. This model improves task scheduling flexibility by extending the scheduler configuration, optimizing the core scheduler for each task, and embedding swarm intelligence algorithms in the task queue. By doing so, it intelligently allocates tasks across computing nodes, reducing average scheduling time, enhancing system throughput, and increasing resource utilization and responsiveness. Simulation outcomes confirm that the swarm intelligence-based scheduling model outperforms conventional techniques across multiple performance metrics28.

Additionally, a cloud resource provisioning and task scheduling mechanism tailored for scientific workflows in Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) environments has been introduced. This approach employs Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to dynamically allocate cloud resources while meeting strict time constraints. The objective is to minimize overall execution cost while ensuring deadlines are respected. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed deadline-aware provisioning model significantly outperforms existing algorithms in terms of cost efficiency and timeliness of execution29.

Xueliang et al. proposed a PSO-PGA hybrid algorithm, incorporating a phagocytosis mechanism, crossover-mutation operations, and feedback strategies. Their method achieves higher accuracy and improved task completion time over conventional PSO, GA, and other improved methods5. To address issues like immature convergence and local traps, hybrid strategies now integrate GWO’s search behavior with FPA’s pollination strategy, enhanced by evolutionary crossover operators for balanced solution diversity30.

In cloud computing task scheduling and optimization, minimizing makespan and reducing convergence speed are both crucial objectives. Minimizing makespan improves resource utilization, reduces cost, enhances quality of service, and increases throughput. Increasing convergence accuracy enables faster scheduling decisions, as the algorithm converges more quickly. It also reduces computational cost by consuming fewer CPU cycles, memory, and energy. Moreover, it helps avoid delays in task allocation, since taking too long to find the best VM can lead to task starvation or system bottlenecks. Amidst these advances, the Human Memory Optimization (HMO) technique has emerged as a novel metaheuristic inspired by human memory processes. It strikes a balance between exploration and exploitation, avoids local minima, and demonstrates faster convergence for a wide variety of problems ranging from continuous to discrete and multi-objective optimization31.

The proposed FA-HMO algorithm incorporates min-min and max-min strategies during the initial population generation phase. This hybrid initialization enhances the quality of the starting population, thereby improving the overall search efficiency and accelerating convergence toward optimal solutions. Experimental results validate the superior performance and effectiveness of the proposed FA-HMO approach over conventional genetic algorithms in handling complex cloud task scheduling problems32.

Human memory optimizations

The work5 describes a completely novel hybrid approach called PSO-PGA that combines a particle swarm optimization algorithm (PSO) and a phagocytosis mechanism with a genetic algorithm (GA). This PSO-PGA algorithm expands the search space, maintains diversity, and keeps the algorithm from becoming trapped in local optima. The authors have done several experiments to evaluate the performance of PSO-PGA against several algorithms, which demonstrate the superiority in terms of convergence accuracy and task completion time. The experiment results have been shown according to their varied number of cloud tasks, virtual machines, and iterations.

In this study, instead of PSO-PGA algorithms HMO (Human Memory optimization) has been used and its result is improving significantly in terms of task completion time(CT) and higher convergence accuracy as compared to PSO-PGA algorithm. The Human Memory Optimization (HMO) algorithm has several advantages over other optimization algorithms. Here are some of the key benefits highlighted in the research:

Memory-inspired mechanism

HMO mimics human memory processes, which allows it to effectively balance the finding of new solutions and its convergence. By storing and recalling both successful and failed experiences, this algorithm can adaptively modify its search strategy based on past performance.

Better Convergence: The HMO algorithm has shown superior convergence properties compared to many traditional optimization algorithms. This means it can find optimal or near-optimal solutions more quickly and reliably, especially in complex search spaces.

Robustness: The algorithm demonstrates robustness across various types of optimization problems. Its ability to recall both successful and unsuccessful memories helps it avoid getting trapped in local optima, making it more effective in navigating complex environment.

Flexibility: Continuous, discrete, and multi-objective optimization tasks are only a few of the many optimization problems to which HMO can be applied. This adaptability feature makes it a valuable tool in various fields, such as engineering, finance, and machine learning.

Competitive Performance: In the comparative experiments conducted in the paper, HMO consistently outperformed other state-of-the-art optimization algorithms on the CEC 2013 benchmark test suite. This indicates that in terms of computing efficiency and solution quality, HMO can give superior results.

Dynamic Adaptation: The algorithm’s use of random factors such as r1 and r2 allows it to dynamically adapt its search behavior. This stochastic element helps maintain diversity in the population of solutions, which is crucial for exploring the search space effectively.

Simplicity and Easy to Implementation: The HMO algorithm is relatively straightforward to implement compared to some more complex optimization techniques. This simplicity can make it more accessible for practitioners and researchers.

Parameter Tuning: The algorithm has a manageable number of parameters, which can be easier to tune compared to algorithms with many hyperparameters. This can lead to more efficient optimization processes.

In this study, an enhanced version of the Human Memory Optimization (HMO) algorithm is proposed to improve task scheduling efficacy in cloud computing environments. The modification introduces a structured memory model by dividing the population into two distinct groups: one storing the best solutions and the other retaining the worst solutions. These fuzzy inputs are evaluated through a set of predefined membership functions and inference rules to dynamically adjust the balance between the exploration and exploitation phases. Experimental framework and implementation methodology

In cloud computing, the task scheduling process refers to the mapping or assignment of tasks to certain VMs i.e. virtual machines. The goal of scheduling is to enhance resource usage and allocate resources for cloud tasks that users submit. The mapping of the association between the virtual machine (VM) and the tasks, or determining which virtual machine (VM) node is performing which task, which reflects on the scheduling outcome33.

Chen et al.34 proposed an enhanced particle swarm optimization approach incorporating modified global and local search exemplars to improve convergence performance in large-scale optimization problems. Li et al.35 introduced a three-way decision-making model based on index threshold acquisition to effectively handle uncertainty and improve decision accuracy. Thus, this paper focuses on how to assign resources for user-submitted tasks, i.e., how to assign N number of cloud tasks of variant length to M number of virtual machines with varying performance in order to minimize time to complete these N cloud tasks.

Experimental setup for cloud task scheduling

The cloud simulation environment was on MATLAB to model and evaluate the performance of the proposed task scheduling algorithm. MATLAB provides a flexible platform for mathematical modeling and simulation of cloud computing systems. The simulation environment consists of multiple cloud tasks and virtual machines (VMs). A set of computational tasks was generated to represent user workloads in the cloud environment those vary in terms of the properties such as execution length, computational requirements, and resource demand, simulating realistic cloud workloads. The virtual machine configuration includes several VMs and is characterized by parameters such as processing speed (MIPS), memory capacity, and task execution capability. The scheduler assigns the tasks to each of the VMs based on the proposed optimization strategy. The workload model is generated in MATLAB to simulate heterogeneous cloud tasks. Task parameters that inclides task length, arrival time, and resource requirement are varied to represent different types of cloud applications. This helps to evaluate the efficiency of the scheduling algorithm under diverse workload conditions. The simulation experiments consider multiple tasks (e.g., 100–1000 tasks) and several virtual machines (e.g., 9–23 VMs) to analyze the performance. Key performance metrics including makespan, resource utilization, and execution time are measured to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The simulation experiments were conducted using MATLAB R2018a on a Windows-based system.

Task encoding method

Results and discussion

This study focuses on evaluating the performance of the proposed FAHMO (Fuzzy Adaptive Human Memory Optimization) and HMO (Human Memory Optimization) algorithm in comparison with existing algorithms such as PSO, PSO-CM, GA, EIGA and PSO-PGA5. The key demerits of the considered algorithms are presented in Table 6. HMO employs a human memory-inspired approach by retaining historically best solutions and enabling selective memory recall, which helps it escape local optima. It requires fewer sensitive parameters and adapts its search using experience-based rules. Additionally, HMO is lightweight compared to hybrid models, requiring fewer iterations to achieve convergence. FAHMO incorporates a fuzzy logic layer that dynamically adjusts parameters in response to uncertainty, thereby enhancing exploration and reducing the risk of premature convergence. Both HMO and FAHMO are capable of adapting to real-time changes, such as VM failures, load spikes, and resource migrations. FAHMO significantly reduces makespan by adaptively assigning tasks to resources using fuzzy logic and memory-based optimization. Its ability to dynamically respond to changes in workload and system uncertainty allows it to outperform traditional algorithms, ensuring efficient task execution and minimized overall processing time.

Conclusion and future work

This study introduces Human Memory Optimization (HMO) and Fuzzy Adaptive Human Memory Optimization (FAHMO) as efficient techniques for cloud task scheduling. The proposed approach uses cognitive memory principles to improve optimization performance by dynamically adjusting search strategies. Experimental results show that HMO and FAHMO outperform traditional algorithms, such as PSO-PGA, in terms of task completion time, convergence accuracy, and load balancing. Specifically, the FAHMO algorithm achieved up to 67.46% reduction in makespan and 63.18% improvement in convergence accuracy compared to the PSO-PGA algorithm. By systematically recording past experiences, these methods enable efficient resource utilization and minimize makespan. The proposed algorithms demonstrate strong robustness and adaptability across varying cloud environments, making them suitable for real-time scheduling applications. Furthermore, the integration of fuzzy logic enables intelligent handling of uncertainty and imprecise input data, which is often present in dynamic and heterogeneous cloud platforms. The performance improvements observed in simulation scenarios suggest that FAHMO can be effectively deployed in practical large-scale cloud infrastructures. The findings indicate that incorporating memory-based optimization into cloud scheduling enhances computational efficiency and scalability. Future work will focus on refining the adaptability of HMO and FAHMO for dynamic cloud environments and extending their applications to large-scale distributed systems.

Future research will focus on extending the capabilities of HMO and FAHMO in highly dynamic and real-time cloud environments. Adaptive parameter tuning techniques can be developed to further enhance the algorithm’s responsiveness to varying workload conditions. Additionally, integrating the proposed models with real-world cloud platforms and deploying them in practical scenarios will validate their industrial applicability. Exploring hybridization with other optimization strategies, such as reinforcement learning and evolutionary algorithms, may improve robustness and generalization across diverse cloud architectures. Furthermore, extending the framework to support multi-cloud and edge computing environments will broaden its usability and relevance in next-generation computing systems.

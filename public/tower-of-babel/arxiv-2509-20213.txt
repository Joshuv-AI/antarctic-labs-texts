Abstract

We present a family of solvable multi-matrix models associated with an arbitrary embedded graph

\Gamma

with a single vertex. The graph with
n
edges is equipped with
2n
corner matrices. The partition function of each member of the family depends on the set of eigenvalues of monodromies of corner matrices around the vertices of the dual graph

\Gamma^{*}
and set of parameters attached to the vertex of
\Gamma
. We select the cases where the partition function of a model is a tau function of KP, 2KP and BKP hiearachies. We compare integrals over
{U}(N)
and over

{SU}(N)
groups. In
U(N)
case there is no restriction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.

Preliminaries.

Here, we gather facts we need in what follows, using

We can write the Haar measure
d\mu(U)
of the
{SU}(N)
group as the product of the Haar measure

d_{*}U
over
{U}(N)
group times Dirac delta function
\delta\left(\arg[\mathrm{det}U-1]\right)
, where

\delta\left(\arg[\mathrm{det}U-1]\right)=(2\pi)^{-N}\sum_{q\in Z}e^{q(\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{N})}=(2\pi)^{-N}\sum_{q\in Z}\mathrm{det}\,U^{q}

(1.1)

Thus, one can write

\int_{{SU}(N)}f(U)d\mu(U)=\int_{{U}(N)}f(U)\delta\left(\arg[\mathrm{det}U-1]\right)d_{*}U

(1.2)

Having this relation in mind one can conclude that the known relation

\int_{{U}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UAU^{\dagger}B)\mathrm{det}\,U^{q}\,d_{*}U=\frac{s_{\lambda}(A)s_{\lambda}(B)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}\delta_{q,0}

(1.3)

(where
s_{\lambda}(X)
is the Schur function labeled by a patition
\lambda
) is also valid for
{SU}(N)
if we replace
d_{*}U
by
d\mu(U)
:

\int_{{SU}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UAU^{\dagger}B)d\mu(U)=\int_{{U}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UAU^{\dagger}B)d_{*}U=\frac{s_{\lambda}(A)s_{\lambda}(B)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}

(1.4)

At the same time from

\int_{{U}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UA)s_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}B)\,d_{*}U=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}\frac{s_{\lambda}(AB)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}

(1.5)

and from

s_{\lambda}(UA)\,\mathrm{det}\,U^{q}=s_{\lambda+q^{N}}(UA)\,\mathrm{det}\,A^{-q},\quad q>0

where
\lambda+q^{N}
denotes the partition
(\lambda_{1}+q,\lambda_{2}+q,\dots)

and from

s_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}B)\,\mathrm{det}\,U^{q}=s_{\mu+|q|^{N}}(U^{\dagger}B)\,\mathrm{det}\,B^{-|q|},\quad q<0

we have

\int_{{U}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UA)s_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}B)\,\mathrm{det}U^{q}\,d_{*}U=\delta_{\mu,\lambda+q^{N}}\frac{s_{\mu}(AB)}{s_{\mu}(I_{N})}\mathrm{det}A^{-q},\quad q>0

(1.6)

\int_{{U}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UA)s_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}B)\,\mathrm{det}U^{q}\,d_{*}U=\delta_{\lambda,\mu+q^{N}}\frac{s_{\lambda}(AB)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}\mathrm{det}B^{q},\quad q<0

(1.7)

It results that for
{SU}(N)
case we get:

\int_{{SU}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UA)s_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}B)d\mu(U)=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}\frac{s_{\lambda}(AB)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}+

+\sum_{q>0}\delta_{\mu,\lambda+q^{N}}\frac{s_{\mu}(AB)}{s_{\mu}(I_{N})}\mathrm{det}A^{-q}+\sum_{q<0}\delta_{\lambda,\mu+q^{N}}\frac{s_{\lambda}(AB)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}\mathrm{det}B^{q}

(1.8)

which is actually written down in
[
15
]
in a little bit different form.

Thus, we get

\int_{{SU}(N)}s_{\lambda}(UA)s_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger}B)d\mu(U)=\frac{s_{\lambda}(AB)}{s_{\lambda}(I_{N})}

(1.9)

Now, we shall see that the pair of relations (
1.4
) and (
1.9
) allows
to constract multimatrix models associated with any embedded graph with a single vertex.

2.1
Graphs, corner matrices, integrals of the Schur functions

Graphs. Dual graphs. Corner matrices.

An embedded graph is a graph drawn on a closed oriented surface without boundary, where
each face of the graph is homeomorphic to a disk.
In this case, the edges are ribbon edges.
For an embedded graph, say
\Gamma
, the sum of the number of vertices
V
minus
the sum of the number of edges
n
plus the sum of the number of faces
F
is equal to the Euler characteristic of the surface on which the graph is drawn.

Recall that the graph
\Gamma^{*}
, dual to
\Gamma
, has
V
faces,
n
edges, and
F
vertices. Every edge
of
\Gamma^{*}
intersects an edge of
\Gamma
. Every face of
\Gamma^{*}
contains one vertex
of
\Gamma
, and vice versa: every face of
\Gamma
contains one vertex
of
\Gamma^{*}
.

Let us number the edges with positive integers
1,\dots,n
, and assign the sides
of edge
i
the numbers
i
and
-i
, where it doesn’t matter which side is numbered
i
and which
-i
(the choice should be recorded).

Let’s number the corners around each vertex as follows. If we walk around a vertex
in the positive direction and intersect an edge with number
|i|
, we’ll first encounter side
-i
,
and after the intersection, we’ll leave side
i
(here
i
is taken from the set
\pm 1,\dots,\pm n
).
The corner we’ve found will be numbered
i
. Let’s assign an
N\times N
matrix
C_{i}
to this corner. We call these matrices corner matrices. We have
2n
corner matrices for a graph with
n
ribbon edges whose numbers are taken from the set
\pm 1,\dots,\pm n
.

Let’s number the vertices.
Now, let’s select a vertex
a
(
a=1,\dots,V
) and multiply corner matrices around the vertex, according to the positive direction of traversal around the vertex. We’ll denote this product
by
\mathfrak{V}_{a}
and call it the monodromy of the vertex
a
; monodromy is defined up to a cyclic permutation.

Thus, we have a graph and the set of corner matrices.

Now consider the graph
\Gamma^{*}
, the dual of
\Gamma
.
Each corner of
\Gamma^{*}
faces one of the corners of
\Gamma
, and we number
each corner of the dual graph with the same number. We introduce the monodromies
\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b}
,

b=1,\dots,F

of the vertices of
\Gamma^{*}
in the same way, but now we traverse the vertices
in the negative direction. Thus, the set of corner matrices
\{C_{\pm i},\,i=1,\dots,n\}

produces two sets of monodromies:

\Gamma:\qquad(\mathfrak{V}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{V}_{V})\,\longleftrightarrow\,(\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{F})

We note that the relationship between these two sets is defined by the embedded graph.

We introduce the set of matrices
\{X_{\pm i}\in G,\,i=1,\dots,n\}
conditioned by
X_{-i}=X_{i}^{\dagger}
.
In what follows, we consider three cases:
G={U}(N)
,
G={SU}(N)
and
G={GL}(N)
and
d\mu(X)
denotes the related Haar measure for
G={U}(N)
,
G={SU}(N)

and the Gaussian measure for
G={GL}(N)
which is defined as

d\mu(X^{(c)})=K\prod_{i,j\leq N}e^{-N|X^{(c)}_{ij}|^{2}}d^{2}X^{(c)}_{ij},\quad c=1,\dots,n

(2.1)

where
K
is the normalization constant chosen by
\int_{{GL}(N)}d\mu(X)=1
, and

d\mu(X)=\prod_{c=1}^{n}d\mu(X^{(c)})
. (Here, localy, we wrote
X^{(c)}
instead of
X_{|c|}
to
make formula (
2.1
) more readable).

Dressing. Integrals.

Next we introduce the
dressing
procedure:

C_{i}\,\to\,X_{i}C_{i},\quad i=\pm 1,\dots,\pm n

(2.2)

which induces the dressing of the monodromies:

\mathfrak{V}_{a}\,\to\,\mathfrak{V}_{a}(X),\,a=1,\dots,V;\qquad\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{a}\,\to\,\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b}(X),\,b=1,\dots,F

(2.3)

In the case
G={U}(N)
, as well as in the case
G={GL}(N)
we have

Proposition 2.1

For a set of partitions
\lambda=\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)},\dots,\lambda^{(V)}
we get

\int_{G}\prod_{a=1}^{V}s_{\lambda^{(a)}}\left(\mathfrak{V}_{a}(X)\right)d\mu(X)=N^{-n|\lambda|}d_{\lambda,G}^{-n}\prod_{a=1}^{V}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{(a)}}\prod_{b=1}^{F}s_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b}),

(2.4)

where

d_{\lambda,{U}(N)}=s_{\lambda}(I_{N}),

(2.5)

d_{\lambda,{GL}(N)}=s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}_{\infty}),\quad\mathbf{p}_{\infty}=(1,0,0,\dots)

(2.6)

We also have the dual relation:

\int_{G}\prod_{b=1}^{F}s_{\lambda^{(b)}}\left(\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b}(X)\right)d\mu(X)=N^{-n|\lambda|}d_{\lambda,G}^{-n}\prod_{b=1}^{F}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{(b)}}\prod_{a=1}^{V}s_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{V}_{a})

(2.7)

Question: Can relations similar to (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) be fulfilled for other groups?

3
Matrix models

In addition to the set of monodromies we associate the semi-infinite set of
free parameters
\mathbf{p}^{(a)}=\left(p^{(a)}_{1},p^{(a)}_{1},\dots\right)
,
a=1,\dots,V

and

\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(b)}=\left(\tilde{p}^{(b)}_{1},\tilde{p}^{(b)}_{1},\dots\right)
,
b=1,\dots,F
.

For any matrix
Y
and for every set
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
we have Cauchy-Littlowood relation:

e^{N\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}p_{m}\mathrm{Tr}(Y^{m})}=\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{a=1}^{V}s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})s_{\lambda}(Y)

(3.1)

Thanks to (
3.1
) and the proposition
2.1

in the case
G={U}(N)
, as well as in the case
G={GL}(N)
, we achieve

Proposition 3.1

Z_{G}(\mathbf{p},C):=\int_{G}\prod_{a=1}^{V}e^{N\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}p^{(a)}_{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathfrak{V}_{a}(X)\right)^{m}}d\mu(X)=\sum_{\lambda}N^{-n|\lambda|}d_{\lambda,G}^{-n}\prod_{a=1}^{V}s_{\lambda}(N\mathbf{p}^{a})\prod_{b=1}^{F}s_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b})

(3.2)

\tilde{Z}_{G}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}},C):=\int_{G}\prod_{b=1}^{F}e^{N\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\tilde{p}^{(b)}_{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b}(X)\right)^{m}}d\mu(X)=\sum_{\lambda}N^{-n|\lambda|}d_{\lambda,G}^{-n}\prod_{b=1}^{F}s_{\lambda}(N\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{b})\prod_{a=1}^{V}s_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{V}_{a})

(3.3)

(Here,
C
denotes the collection of corner matrices).

As we see, the right-hand sides depend only on the spectrum of each of the monodromies.

Remark 3.1

For a given
\Gamma
we have a family of multimatrix models obtained by transformations of the set
\{C_{i},\,i=\pm 1,\dots,\pm n\}
that preserve the set of vertex monodromies
\{\mathfrak{V}^{*}_{b},\,b=1,\dots,F\}
, or, more precisely, the set of spectra of these vertex monodromies (we will call such transformations gauge transformations).

Capillary L p L_{p} -curvature problem
Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. We prove a gradient estimate for a class of capillary curvature equations in the half-space. As an application, we prove the existence of an even, smooth, strictly convex solution to the even capillary L p L_{p} -curvature problem for all 1 < p < k + 1 1<p<k+1 and all contact angles θ ∈ ( 0 , π / 2 ) \theta\in(0,\pi/2) .



1.
Introduction

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
,
1<p<k+1
and
1\leq k<n
. Suppose
0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
is an even function. Then there exists an even, smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
whose
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the principal curvatures
S_{k}
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\phi s^{p-1}S_{k}\circ\tilde{\nu}^{-1}=1.

In the closed (without boundary) setting, the case
p=1
was solved by B. Guan and P. Guan
[
GG02
]
. For
p>k+1
, existence was established in
[
GRW15
]
by an elliptic method and in
[
BIS21
]
via a parabolic approach, while the borderline case
p=k+1
was treated in
[
Lee23
]
. In
[
HI24
]
, we resolved the remaining range
1<p<k+1
by proving a new gradient estimate and combining it with a geometric lemma of Chou–Wang
[
CW00
]
to obtain
C^{0}
and
C^{2}
estimates simultaneously.

1.1
for
p=1
and for
p\geq k+1
was obtained recently in
[
MWW25c
,
MWW25e
]
. Furthermore, in their treatment of the range
1<p<k+1
, see
[
MWW25e
, Thm. 1.4]
, the authors proved existence under an
additional
contact-angle restriction, depending on
p
.

The main new contribution of the present paper is a gradient estimate that removes this extra angle condition, thereby yielding the existence result for all
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Our gradient estimate does not rely on any special structure of
S_{k}
beyond its
k
-homogeneity; in particular, it applies to capillary quotient curvature problems, as well as to capillary
L_{p}
-Christoffel–Minkowski problem
[
HI25b
]
, where for the latter problem we previously employed a completely different argument to avoid any
p
-dependent angle restriction (and the argument in
[
HI25b
]
does not apply to the problem considered here). See also
[
HIS25
,
MWW25d
]
. For further results in the case
k=n
and
p\geq-n-1
, we refer the reader to
[
HI25a
,
HHI25
,
MWW25a
,
MWW25b
]
.

2.
Background

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
denote the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Set

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x_{n+1}>0\},\quad\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x_{n+1}=0\}.

For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, define the spherical cap and its translated version by

\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\ \langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle\geq\cos\theta\},\quad\mathcal{C}_{\theta}:=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}.

A smooth, compact, connected, orientable hypersurface

\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and

\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is called a
capillary hypersurface with contact angle

\theta\in(0,\pi)
if

\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\theta

on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
where
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
.

We call
\Sigma

strictly convex
if the enclosed region
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a convex body (i.e. compact, convex, with non-empty interior) and the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is positive definite. For a strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
, the
capillary Gauss map
is defined by

\tilde{\nu}:=\nu-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}:\Sigma\to\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

It is a diffeomorphism onto
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, see
[
MWWX25f
, Lem. 2.2]
.

Let
\Sigma
be strictly convex and
\theta
-capillary. The
capillary support function

s:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

s(\zeta):=\langle\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\zeta),\zeta+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}\rangle.

Note that
s
satisfies

\nabla_{\mu}s=\cot\theta\,s.

For the model cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, the corresponding capillary support function is

\ell(\zeta):=\sin^{2}\theta-\cos\theta\,\langle\zeta,e_{n+1}\rangle.

On
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
we write
g
for the round metric,
\nabla
for its Levi–Civita connection, and

\nabla^{2}
for the covariant Hessian. For
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
we set

\tau[f]:=\nabla^{2}f+f\,g,\qquad\tau^{\sharp}[f]:=g^{-1}\cdot\tau[f],

so that
\tau^{\sharp}[f]
is a symmetric endomorphism of
T\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Finally, writing
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1})
, let
\mathcal{R}
be the reflection

\mathcal{R}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1}):=(-x_{1},\dots,-x_{n},x_{n+1}).

A function
\varphi:\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
even
if
\varphi\circ\mathcal{R}=\varphi
, and we
say that a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
(equivalently, its capillary support function
s
) is

even
if

x\in\Sigma\implies\mathcal{R}(x)\in\Sigma.

3.
The gradient estimate

A key ingredient in the proof of
1.1
is the following gradient estimate in
3.1
. Before stating the lemma and its proof, let us note that the auxiliary function

\Phi=\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}},\qquad u=\frac{s}{\ell},

which is the natural capillary analogue of the test function used in
[
HI24
]
, was employed in
[
MWW25e
]
to obtain a gradient estimate. In the capillary setting, however, this choice turns out to be insufficient: it leads to a contact-angle restriction depending on
p
when
1<p<k+1
. There are several natural candidates one might try in order to obtain a full gradient estimate. The main observation of the present paper is that the angle obstruction disappears once one incorporates the weight
\ell^{2-\gamma}
, namely by considering

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Psi=\frac{\ell^{2-\gamma}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}}.

In particular, when
\theta=\pi/2
, we have
\ell\equiv 1
and hence
\Psi=|\nabla s|^{2}/s^{\gamma}
, which is precisely the auxiliary function used in
[
HI24
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Main Lemma)
.

Let
1<p<k+1
,
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
0<\gamma<\frac{2(p-1)}{k}
. Suppose
s
is an even, smooth, strictly convex solution of

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} F(\tau^{\sharp}[s])&=s^{p-1}\phi&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}s&=\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\end{aligned}\right.

where
F
is a symmetric,
k
-homogeneous, smooth curvature function. Then there exists
C_{0}=C_{0}(\theta,k,p,\gamma,\phi)
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla s|^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\leq C_{0}\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s\right)^{2-\gamma}.

Proof.

Let
u=s/\ell
,
0<\gamma<2(p-1)/k<2
and
\beta=2-\gamma
. Consider the auxiliary function
\Psi
defined in (
3.1
). To prove (
3.3
), it suffices to prove

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}}\leq M\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\right)^{\beta}\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u\right)^{2-\gamma}

for some constant
M>0
.
Assume that
\Psi
attains its maximum at
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and that
(
3.4
) does not hold for a given
M>0
; that is,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}}(\xi_{0})=\max_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}}(\xi)>M\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell\right)^{\beta}\left(\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u\right)^{2-\gamma}.

Then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}(\xi_{0})}{u^{2}(\xi_{0})}>M.

In what follows,
c
denotes a positive constant that may change from line to line, and depends only on
\theta,k,p,\gamma
and
\phi
.

Case 1.
Suppose
\xi_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
be an orthonormal frame around
\xi_{0}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
. We use the shorthand notation
u_{i}:=\nabla_{e_{i}}u
and
u_{ij}:=\nabla^{2}u(e_{i},e_{j})
, and similarly for higher covariant derivatives.
We have
\nabla_{\mu}u=u_{n}=0
and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u(e_{\alpha},e_{n})=e_{\alpha}(u_{n})-du(\nabla_{e_{\alpha}}e_{n})=-\cot\theta\,u_{\alpha}.

Since
\beta=2-\gamma<2
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, at
\xi_{0}
we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle 0\leq\nabla_{\mu}\log\left(\frac{\ell^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{\mu}|\nabla u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\beta\frac{\nabla_{\mu}\ell}{\ell}-\gamma\frac{\nabla_{\mu}u}{u}=-\gamma\cot\theta<0,

which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
Suppose
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\backslash\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. At
\xi_{0}
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{i}\log\Psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2})}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\beta\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell}-\gamma\frac{u_{i}}{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{2u_{m}u_{mi}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\beta\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell}-\gamma\frac{u_{i}}{u},

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0\geq\nabla^{2}_{ij}(\log\Psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla^{2}_{ij}(|\nabla u|^{2})}{|\nabla u|^{2}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2})\nabla_{j}(|\nabla u|^{2})}{|\nabla u|^{4}}

\displaystyle+\beta\frac{\ell_{ij}}{\ell}-\beta\frac{\ell_{i}\ell_{j}}{\ell^{2}}-\gamma\frac{u_{ij}}{u}+\gamma\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2u_{m}u_{mij}+2u_{mi}u_{mj}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}-(\beta\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell}-\gamma\frac{u_{i}}{u})(\beta\frac{\ell_{j}}{\ell}-\gamma\frac{u_{j}}{u})

\displaystyle+\beta\frac{1-\ell}{\ell}\delta_{ij}-\beta\frac{\ell_{i}\ell_{j}}{\ell^{2}}-\gamma\frac{u_{ij}}{u}+\gamma\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{u^{2}},

where we used
\ell_{ij}+\ell\delta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
.
Multiplying (
3.10
) with
F^{ij}
yields

(3.11)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\nabla u|^{2}}(F^{ij}u_{m}u_{mij}+F^{ij}u_{mi}u_{mj})+\beta\frac{1-\ell}{\ell}F^{ij}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+2\beta\gamma F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}\ell_{i}}{u\ell}-\gamma F^{ij}\left(\frac{\tau_{ij}}{u\ell}-\frac{2u_{i}\ell_{j}}{u\ell}-\frac{1}{\ell}\delta_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle-(\beta+\beta^{2})F^{ij}\frac{\ell_{i}\ell_{j}}{\ell^{2}}+(\gamma-\gamma^{2})F^{ij}\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\nabla u|^{2}}(F^{ij}u_{m}u_{mij}+F^{ij}u_{mi}u_{mj})+\left(\beta\frac{1-\ell}{\ell}+\gamma\frac{1}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle-\gamma ks^{p-2}\phi+(2\beta\gamma+2\gamma)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}\ell_{i}}{u\ell}-(\beta+\beta^{2})F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+(\gamma-\gamma^{2})F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}},

where we used

(3.12)

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=s_{ij}+s\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\ell u_{ij}+u_{i}\ell_{j}+u_{j}\ell_{i}+u(\ell_{ij}+\ell\delta_{ij})

\displaystyle=\ell u_{ij}+u_{i}\ell_{j}+u_{j}\ell_{i}+u\delta_{ij}

and the identity

(3.13)

\displaystyle F^{ij}(\ell u_{ij}+u_{i}\ell_{j}+u_{j}\ell_{i}+u\delta_{ij})=F^{ij}\tau_{ij}=kF(\tau)=ks^{p-1}\phi.

Next, taking the first derivatives of (
3.2
) in the
e_{m}
-direction gives

(3.14)

\displaystyle F^{ij}(\ell_{m}u_{ij}+\ell u_{ijm}+2u_{im}\ell_{j}+2u_{i}\ell_{jm}+u_{m}\delta_{ij})=(p-1)s^{p-2}\phi s_{m}+s^{p-1}\phi_{m}.

Multiplying this identity by
u_{m}/\ell
, summing over
m
, and using (
3.12
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.15)

\displaystyle u_{m}F^{ij}u_{ijm}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}((p-1)s^{p-2}\phi s_{m}u_{m}+s^{p-1}u_{m}\phi_{m})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\ell}F^{ij}(u_{m}\ell_{m}u_{ij}+2u_{im}u_{m}\ell_{j}+2u_{i}\ell_{jm}u_{m}+|\nabla u|^{2}\delta_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)s^{p-2}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{u}{\ell}\ell_{m}u_{m}\right)\phi+s^{p-1}\frac{u_{m}}{\ell}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle-\frac{\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}}(ks^{p-1}\phi-2F^{ii}u_{i}\ell_{i}-uF^{ij}\delta_{ij})

\displaystyle-|\nabla u|^{2}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell}\left(\gamma\frac{u_{i}}{u}-\beta\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell}\right)-\frac{2(1-\ell)}{\ell}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\ell}F^{ij}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)s^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi+(p-1-k)s^{p-2}\frac{u}{\ell}\ell_{m}u_{m}\phi+s^{p-1}\frac{u_{m}}{\ell}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{u\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}+\left(\frac{2\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}}-\gamma\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u\ell}\right)F^{ii}u_{i}\ell_{i}

\displaystyle+\beta|\nabla u|^{2}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{2(1-\ell)}{\ell}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}.

Since
\nabla^{2}u+gu
is Codazzi, we have

u_{mij}=u_{ijm}+u_{m}\delta_{ij}-u_{j}\delta_{mi},

and thus

(3.16)

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{m}u_{mij}=

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{m}(u_{ijm}+u_{m}\delta_{ij}-u_{j}\delta_{mi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{m}u_{ijm}+|\nabla u|^{2}F^{ij}\delta_{ij}-F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)s^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi+(p-1-k)s^{p-2}\frac{u}{\ell}\ell_{m}u_{m}\phi+s^{p-1}\frac{u_{m}}{\ell}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{u\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}}+(1-\frac{1}{\ell})|\nabla u|^{2}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}+\left(\frac{2\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}}-\gamma\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u\ell}\right)F^{ii}u_{i}\ell_{i}

\displaystyle+\beta|\nabla u|^{2}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{2-\ell}{\ell}F^{ii}u_{i}^{2}.

Therefore, by (
3.11
), we find

(3.17)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\nabla u|^{2}}(F^{ij}u_{m}u_{mij}+F^{ii}u_{mi}^{2})+\left(\beta\frac{1-\ell}{\ell}+\gamma\frac{1}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}-\gamma ks^{p-2}\phi

\displaystyle+(2\beta\gamma+2\gamma)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}\ell_{i}}{u\ell}-(\beta+\beta^{2})F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+(\gamma-\gamma^{2})F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2(p-1)-\gamma k)s^{p-2}\phi+2\frac{u_{m}}{\ell|\nabla u|^{2}}s^{p-1}\phi_{m}+2(p-1-k)\frac{\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}}s^{p-1}\phi

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2u\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}}+(2-\beta)(1-\frac{1}{\ell})+\gamma\frac{1}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}+\left(\frac{4u\ell_{m}u_{m}}{\ell|\nabla u|^{2}}+2\beta\gamma\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}\ell_{i}}{u\ell}

\displaystyle+(\beta-\beta^{2})F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{2}{|\nabla u|^{2}}F^{ii}u_{mi}^{2}+\left(\gamma-\gamma^{2}-\frac{2(2-\ell)}{\ell}\frac{u^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}.

Moreover, by (
3.9
) we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{2F^{ii}u_{mi}^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\geq\frac{F^{ii}}{2}\left(\frac{2u_{m}u_{mi}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-\gamma\beta F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}\ell_{i}}{u\ell}.

Hence,

(3.19)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle s^{p-2}\phi\left(2(p-1)-k\gamma-2(k+1-p)\frac{u|\nabla\ell|}{\ell|\nabla u|}-2\frac{u|\nabla\phi|}{\phi|\nabla u|}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{2u|\nabla\ell|}{\ell^{2}|\nabla u|}+(2-\beta)(1-\frac{1}{\ell})+\gamma\frac{1}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}-\left(\frac{4u|\nabla\ell|}{\ell|\nabla u|}+\beta\gamma\right)F^{ii}\frac{|u_{i}||\ell_{i}|}{u\ell}

\displaystyle+\left(\beta-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}-\frac{2(2-\ell)}{\ell}\frac{u^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle s^{p-2}\phi\left(2(p-1)-k\gamma-\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}\right)+\left(-\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}+(2-\beta)(1-\frac{1}{\ell})+\gamma\frac{1}{\ell}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}+\beta\gamma\right)F^{ii}\frac{|u_{i}||\ell_{i}|}{u\ell}+\left(\beta-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}-\frac{c}{M}\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}.

Let
\beta=2-\gamma
. Then

(3.20)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle s^{p-2}\phi\left(2(p-1)-k\gamma-\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}\right)+\left(-\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}+\gamma\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}+2(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\right)F^{ii}\frac{|u_{i}||\ell_{i}|}{u\ell}

\displaystyle+\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}-\frac{c}{M}\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Choose
M>0
sufficiently large such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\left(2(p-1)-k\gamma\right),\quad

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\gamma}{2},

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\sqrt{M}}

\displaystyle<2(\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}-1)\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right),\quad

\displaystyle\frac{c}{M}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right).

Using
\ell_{i}^{2}\leq|\nabla\ell|^{2}\leq c\,\ell^{2}
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

(3.22)

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}F^{ii}\frac{|u_{i}||\ell_{i}|}{u\ell}\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+(1-\varepsilon)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}},

we obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)-k\gamma}{2}s^{p-2}\phi+\frac{\gamma}{2}F^{ij}\delta_{ij}-2\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{|u_{i}||\ell_{i}|}{u\ell}

\displaystyle+\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+(1-\varepsilon)\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)F^{ii}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)-k\gamma}{2}s^{p-2}\phi+\frac{\gamma}{2}F^{ij}\delta_{ij}-\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}F^{ii}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)-k\gamma}{2}s^{p-2}\phi+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(1-\frac{4c\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\right)F^{ij}\delta_{ij}.

Therefore, if
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small such that
1-\frac{4c\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}>0
, then we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Regarding the proof of
1.1
, the gradient estimate, combined with the arguments of
[
MWW25e
]
, yields two-sided bounds for the capillary support function and a uniform
C^{2}
estimate simultaneously. Higher-order regularity then follows from
[
Lie13
]
. One can therefore apply the degree-theoretic framework of
[
LLN17
]
, together with the uniqueness result
[
GL24
, Thm. 1.6]
, to obtain the desired existence result; see
[
MWW25e
]
for further details.

Next, we present a new argument, which is of independent interest, showing that the gradient estimate directly implies two-sided bounds for solutions to the even capillary
L_{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem. See also
[
HI25b
]
for a more convex-geometric argument.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be an even, smooth, strictly convex
\theta
-capillary hypersurface with capillary support function
s
. Let

\Omega:=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}

and denote by
h:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
the (standard) support function of
\Omega

(viewed as a convex body in
e_{n+1}^{\perp}
).
Assume that for some
\gamma\in(0,2)
we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla s|^{2}}{s^{\gamma}}\leq C_{0},R^{2-\gamma},\quad R:=\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s.

Let
\widehat{\nabla}=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. Then
h
satisfies

(3.25)

\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{\nabla}h|^{2}}{h^{\gamma}}\leq C_{0}\,(\sin\theta)^{\gamma}\,R^{2-\gamma}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Let
\hat{s}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the (standard) support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
.
For
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
:

(3.26)

\displaystyle s(\zeta)=\hat{s}(u)\quad\text{whenever}\quad u=\zeta+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}\,(\text{i.e.\ }\zeta=u-\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}).

Moreover, for
\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cong\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}
one has

(3.27)

\displaystyle h(\tilde{u})=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\,\hat{s}(\sin\theta\,\tilde{u}+\cos\theta\,e_{n+1}).

Therefore,

(3.28)

\displaystyle h(\tilde{u})=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\,s(\sin\theta\,\tilde{u}),\quad|\widehat{\nabla}h(\tilde{u})|\leq|\nabla s(\sin\theta\,\tilde{u})|,

and hence

|\widehat{\nabla}h|^{2}\leq C_{0}\,R^{2-\gamma}\,(\sin\theta)^{\gamma}\,h^{\gamma}.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1<p<k+1
,
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
0<\gamma<\frac{2(p-1)}{k}
. Suppose
s
is an even, smooth, strictly convex solution of

(3.29)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(\tau^{\sharp}[s])&=s^{p-1}\phi&&\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}s&=\cot\theta\,s&&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of principal radii of curvature. Then there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(\theta,k,p,\gamma,\phi)
such that

(3.30)

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1}\leq s\leq C_{1}.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
be the even, smooth, strictly convex,
\theta
-capillary hypersurface with capillary support function
s
.
Set
\Omega=\widehat{\Sigma}\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}
and

(3.31)

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{out}}:=\max_{x^{\prime}\in\Omega}|x^{\prime}|,\quad r_{\mathrm{in}}:=\min_{x^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega}|x^{\prime}|,\quad H:=\max_{x\in\Sigma}x_{n+1},\quad R:=\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}s.

By
[
HI25b
, Thm. 2.9]
we have

(3.32)

\displaystyle R\leq r_{\mathrm{out}}+H\leq r_{\mathrm{out}}+\tan\theta\,r_{\mathrm{in}}\leq(1+\tan\theta)r_{\mathrm{out}}=(1+\tan\theta)\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h,

where
h
denotes the standard support function of
\Omega
. Thus, by
3.2
,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{\nabla}h|^{2}}{h^{\gamma}}\leq C^{\prime}(\sin\theta)^{\gamma}\left(\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h\right)^{2-\gamma}\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Due to
[
Gua22
, Lem. 3.1]
, there exists
c=c(C^{\prime},\gamma,\theta)>0
,
such that

(3.34)

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{out}}\leq c\,r_{\mathrm{in}}.

Moreover, directly from (
3.29
) (cf.
[
HI25b
, Lem. 3.3]
), it follows that there exists
c_{0}>0
(depending only on
\theta
and
\phi
) such that

(3.35)

\displaystyle R\geq c_{0}.

Combining this with
r_{\mathrm{out}}\leq c\,r_{\mathrm{in}}
gives

(3.36)

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq R\leq r_{\mathrm{out}}+H\leq(c+\tan\theta)r_{\mathrm{in}}\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{in}}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{c+\tan\theta}\text{ and }R\leq(c+\tan\theta)r_{\mathrm{in}}.

On the other hand, the argument in
[
HI25b
, Sec. 3.1]
yields

(3.37)

\displaystyle H\geq\frac{c_{k}^{1/k}}{2}\phi_{1}^{-1/k}R^{\frac{1-p}{k}}r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2},

where
\phi_{0}\leq\phi\leq\phi_{1}
and hence

(3.38)

\displaystyle H\geq c_{1}r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2+\frac{1-p}{k}}\geq c_{2},

for constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
k,p,\theta,\phi
and
c,c_{0}
.
In particular, we obtain the uniform lower bound

(3.39)

\displaystyle s\geq H\cos\theta\geq c_{2}\cos\theta.

Finally, since
1<p<k+1
, the inequalities

(3.40)

\displaystyle c_{1}r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2+\frac{1-p}{k}}\leq H\leq\tan\theta\,r_{\mathrm{in}}

force an upper bound
r_{\mathrm{in}}\leq c_{3}
for some
c_{3}=c_{3}(c_{1},\theta)>0
.
Therefore,

(3.41)

\displaystyle s\leq(c+\tan\theta)r_{\mathrm{in}}\leq(c+\tan\theta)c_{3}.

∎

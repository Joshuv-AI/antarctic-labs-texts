Towards Keating-Snaith’s conjecture for cubic Hecke L L -functions over the Eisenstein field Thanks: PJW is currently supported by the NSTC grant 114-2628-M-110-006-MY4.
Hua Lin, Peng-Jie Wong

Abstract
. A famous conjecture of Keating and Snaith asserts that central values of L L -functions in a given family admit a log-normal distribution with a prescribed mean and variance depending on the symmetry type of the family. Based on a recent work of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan, we obtain a conditional lower bound towards Keating-Snaith’s conjecture for a “thin” family of cubic Hecke L L -functions over the Eisenstein field. A key new input is certain twisted estimates of the 1-level density of zeros of cubic Hecke L L -functions, extending the previous work of David and Güloğlu, under the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis.

Primary 11M06; Secondary 11M41

1.
Introduction

About a century ago, Hilbert and Pólya independently suggested a connection between zeros of the Riemann zeta function and eigenvalues of some self-adjoint operator. There was little evidence at the time, but since the encounter between Montgomery and Dyson about half a century ago, the Riemann zeta function and
L
-functions have been intimately linked to random matrix theory.
Using this connection, Keating and Snaith conjectured that central values of
L
-functions in families admit a log-normal distribution with a prescribed mean and variance depending on their symmetry type
[
16
]
. Their far-reaching conjecture presents a natural analogue of Selberg’s central limit theorem on the log-normality of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
and lies in the centre of the theory of
L
-functions. For instance, Keating-Snaith’s conjecture implies that almost all the central values of
L
-functions in families are non-vanishing, which is unknown in general.

In the same vein, prior to the work of Keating and Snaith, Katz and Sarnak
[
13
,
14
]

analysed random matrices and monodromy for families of
L
-functions over function fields. Their work not only shed light on determining the symmetry type of families of
L
-functions, but also led to their famous density conjecture on the distribution of “low-lying” zeros of
L
-functions.
Their conjecture, known as Katz and Sarnak’s philosophy, implies the non-vanishing of families of
L
-functions at the central point.
For example, some progress has been made for families of
L
-functions of modular forms by Iwaniec, Luo, and Sarnak
[
12
]
, and for families of
L
-functions of quadratic twists of elliptic curves by Heath-Brown
[
9
]
.

Most families that have been studied, however,
are over the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
and are “self-dual”. Nonetheless, Cho and Park studied the low-lying zeros of cubic Dirichlet
L
-functions in
[
2
]
and recently, David and Güloğlu
[
4
]
studied the 1-level density of zeros for cubic Hecke
L
-functions over the Eisenstein field
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
, where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
, under the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). For the “thin” family
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
2.1
), using the work of Heath-Brown
[
8
]
, David and Güloğlu obtained a support for the Fourier transform of the test function beyond the
(-1,1)
-barrier and achieved at least
\frac{2}{13}
non-vanishing at the central point, supporting Katz and Sarnak’s philosophy.

It is also worth mentioning that by calculating the moments of cubic Hecke
L
-functions, it has been shown in
[
1
,
6
]
that there are infinitely many non-vanishing central values.
Moreover, positive propositions of non-vanishing at the central point in the same family
were recently established in
[
7
]
(under GRH) and
[
3
]
(unconditionally).
We refer the reader to these references for more details.

More recently, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
18
]
proved a conditional lower bound towards Keating-Snaith’s conjecture for quadratic twists of elliptic curves with global root number 1. This was extended by the second-named author to quadratic twists with global root number
-1
in
[
20
]
. The innovation of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan is a method of obtaining lower bounds on the non-vanishing of central
L
-values via studying “twisted” 1-level density estimates for low-lying zeros and using the “method of moments” from probability theory. As an illustration, they established conditional lower bounds towards Keating-Snaith’s conjecture by “upgrading” the work of Heath-Brown
[
9
]
.

In this article, we extend the method of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
18
]
to the
unitary
family
\mathcal{F}
of cubic Hecke characters over
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
, considered by David and Güloğlu
[
4
]
(see also (
2.1
)), and prove the following theorem towards Keating-Snaith’s conjecture (cf.
[
15
, pg. 59, (iii)]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
with

\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
. For any fixed

(\alpha,\beta)
, we have as
X\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\#\bigg\{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}&:X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X,\frac{\log|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|}{\sqrt{\log\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}}\in(\alpha,\beta)\bigg\}\\
&\geq\frac{2}{13}\left(\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}dt+o(1)\right)\#\{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}:X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\},\end{split}

where the integrand is the probability density function of a standard normal random variable.

Remark
.

(i) This is the first result of this type for a family of non-rational and non-self-dual
L
-functions to the best of the authors’ knowledge.

(ii) Compared to the argument of David and Güloğlu
[
4
]
, our analysis of the twisted 1-level density requires a more delicate treatment of twisted cubic Gauss sums where conductors are no longer prime powers. As seen in Section
5.3
, this is the core of our argument.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
be a primitive third root of unity, and let
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
. The ring of integer
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
, where
every element
n
can be expressed as
n=a+b\omega
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is a principal ideal domain, every
n
coprime to 3 has a unique generator
n=(m)
,
m\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3).
We use this fact freely throughout the article. Also, we let
\Lambda_{\mathcal{K}},\phi_{\mathcal{K}},
and
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}
denote the number field analogues of the corresponding arithmetic functions (namely,
\Lambda(n),\phi(n),
and
\mu(n)
) over
\mathcal{K}.

We denote by
\mathrm{N}(n)
the norm of
n\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
, and
\mathrm{N}(n)=n\bar{n}
, where
\bar{n}
is the complex conjugate of
n.
Furthermore, if
n=a+b\omega,
then one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(n)=(a+b\omega)(a+b\bar{\omega})=a^{2}+ab+b^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

The cubic Dirichlet character
\chi_{p}
modulo a prime
p\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3)\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is the cubic residue symbol

\displaystyle\chi_{p}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)_{3}\equiv n^{\frac{\mathrm{N}(p)-1}{3}}(\operatorname{mod}p),

and cubic characters of general moduli
n
are defined multiplicatively. We recall the following statement for cubic reciprocity.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
be coprime to
3
. If
m,n\equiv\pm 1(\operatorname{mod}3)
, then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)_{3}=\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)_{3}.

A character is primitive if it is the product of
\chi_{p}
or
\overline{\chi_{p}}=\chi_{p^{2}}
for distinct primes
p
. A cubic Dirichlet character is a cubic Hecke character if
\chi_{n}(\omega)=1.
This is true if and only if
\mathrm{N}(n)\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}9)
, so primitive Hecke characters have conductors
n=f_{1}f_{2}^{2},
where
f_{1},f_{2}
are square-free, with
(f_{1},f_{2})=1
and
\mathrm{N}(f_{1}f_{2}^{2})\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}9).
The thin family
\mathcal{F}
of cubic Hecke characters over
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
is, as considered by David and Güloğlu in
[
4
]
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]:\mathfrak{f}\neq 1,\mathfrak{f}\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}9),\mathfrak{f}\text{ square-free}\}.

Let
\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}
be a primitive Hecke character with
\mathfrak{f}\neq 1
and
(\mathfrak{f},3)=1.
The completed
L
-function is

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})=(|\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}|\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f}))^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}),

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}=-3
is the discriminant of
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
. By the work of Hecke, it is known that
\Lambda(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
is entire and it satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})=\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\sqrt{-3})g(1,\mathfrak{f})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})^{-1/2}\Lambda(1-s,\overline{\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}}),

where
g(1,\mathfrak{f})
is the generalized cubic Gauss sum defined in (
2.4
).

Let
h
be a smooth function with a compactly supported Fourier transform

\widehat{h}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(t)e^{-2\pi i\xi t}dt,

and
h
satisfies
|h(t)|\ll 1/(1+t^{2})
for all real
t
. In particular, one may take

\displaystyle h(t)=\Big(\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t}\Big)^{2},

the Fejér kernel, so that
\widehat{h}(\xi)=\max\{1-|\xi|,0\}
. In addition, let
\Phi
be a smooth non-negative function compactly supported in
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}]
such that
\Phi(t)=1
on
[1,2]
and
\widehat{\Phi}(x)\ll_{K}x^{-K}
for any
K>0
, where

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(\mathrm{N}(x+\omega y)e(-ty)dxdy.

Let
L\geq 1
be a real number. The size of the log-derivative of
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
is controlled on horizontal integrals, so by contour integration and Cauchy’s theorem, one has the following formula. (See
[
4
, Lemmata 2.4-2.6]
for a complete treatment.)

Lemma 2.2

(Explicit Formula)
.

Let
\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}
be a primitive cubic Hecke character such that
(\mathfrak{f},3)=1
, and
h(x)
be an even Schwartz function with compactly supported Fourier transform. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\end{subarray}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h\left(\frac{tL}{2\pi}\right)\left(\log\frac{3\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{4\pi^{2}}+2\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]}\frac{\Lambda_{K}(n)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}(n)}}\left(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(n)+\overline{\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(n)}\right)\hat{h}\left(\frac{\log\mathrm{N}(n)}{L}\right).

A key step in estimating the sum over zeros (for example, in Theorem
5.1
) is applying the following Poisson summation formula over
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
. Throughout this article, we let

(2.3)

\displaystyle e(z)=\exp(2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(z))=\exp(2\pi i(z+\bar{z})).

Lemma 2.3

(Poisson Summation Formula)
.

Let
q,r\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
be fixed. Then

\sum_{m\equiv r(\operatorname{mod}q)}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(m)}{M}\right)=\frac{M}{\mathrm{N}(q)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]}\widehat{\Phi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{M\mathrm{N}(k)}{\mathrm{N}(q)}}\right)e\left(\frac{-kr}{q\sqrt{-3}}\right),

where
\widehat{\Phi}
is defined in (
2.2
).

This is the result of modifying the usual Poisson summation over
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
(for example, the one in
[
4
, Lemma 2.9]
) following the procedure of
[
17
, Lemma 7]
).

We note that the sum over all residues for the exponential in (
2.3
) still vanishes, since

\displaystyle\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}p)}e\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}p)}\exp\left(\frac{(2\pi i)(r\bar{p}+\bar{r}p)}{\mathrm{N}(p)}\right)=\sum_{k(\operatorname{mod}\mathrm{N}(p))}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ik}{\mathrm{N}(p)}\right)=0,

where for the second to last equality, we let

k=r\bar{p}+\bar{r}p
and observe that
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k(\operatorname{mod}\mathrm{N}(p))
uniquely determined by
r(\operatorname{mod}p).

The generalized cubic Gauss sum is

(2.4)

\displaystyle g(k,n)=\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}n)}\chi_{n}(r)e\bigg(\frac{kr}{n}\bigg),

where
e(z)
is defined in (
2.3
).
Twisted 1-level density led us to treat twisted cubic Gauss sums. We collect the technical lemmas regarding those below.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
be a prime,
k,p\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3)
, and
(k,p)=1.
Then for all integers
\alpha\geq 2,

\displaystyle g(k,p^{\alpha})=\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}p^{\alpha})}\chi_{p^{\alpha}}(r)e\left(\frac{kr}{p^{\alpha}}\right)=0.

Proof.

Dividing
r
by
p^{\alpha-1}
, we have

\displaystyle g(k,p^{\alpha})=\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}p^{\alpha})}\chi_{p^{\alpha}}(r)e\left(\frac{rk}{p^{\alpha}}\right)=\sum_{r_{1}(\operatorname{mod}p^{\alpha-1})}e\left(\frac{r_{1}k}{p^{\alpha}}\right)\sum_{r_{2}(\operatorname{mod}p)}\chi_{p^{\alpha}}(r_{1}+r_{2}{p^{\alpha-1}})e\left(\frac{r_{2}k}{p}\right),

where

r=r_{1}+r_{2}{p^{\alpha-1}},\ \text{ for some }r_{1}(\operatorname{mod}p^{\alpha-1}),\text{ and }r_{2}(\operatorname{mod}p).

The character is nonzero when

(r_{1}+r_{2}p^{\alpha-1},p^{\alpha})=1\iff(r_{1}+r_{2}p^{\alpha-1},p^{\alpha-1})=1\iff(r_{1},p^{\alpha-1})=1,

and let
\chi_{p}^{*}
denote the primitive character inducing
\chi_{p^{\alpha}}
or the principal character when
3\mid\alpha
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle g(k,p^{\alpha})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}(\operatorname{mod}p^{\alpha-1})\\
(r_{1},p^{\alpha-1})=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{r_{1}k}{p^{\alpha}}\right)\chi_{p}^{*}(k)\sum_{r_{2}(\operatorname{mod}p)}e\left(\frac{r_{2}k}{p}\right),

where since
(k,p)=1,
the inner sum vanishes.
∎

We also require the following modified version of
[
4
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
be a prime and
k,\ell,p\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3)
. Suppose
p^{a}\mid\mid\ell
,
\ell=p^{a}\ell^{\prime},

p^{b}\mid\mid k
,
k=p^{b}k^{\prime}
, and
j\geq 1
is a rational integer.
If
b=j+a-1
, then

\displaystyle g(k,p^{j}\ell)=\mathrm{N}(p)^{b}g(k,\ell^{\prime})\times\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
\overline{\chi_{p}(\ell^{\prime})}g(k^{\prime},p)&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
\chi_{p}(\ell^{\prime})\overline{g(k^{\prime},p)}&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 2(\operatorname{mod}3).\end{cases}

If
b\geq j+a
and
j+a\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}3),
then

\displaystyle g(k,p^{j}\ell)=\phi_{\mathcal{K}}(p^{j+a})g(k,\ell^{\prime}).

In all other cases,
g(k,p^{j}\ell)=0.

Proof.

Since
p^{j}\ell=p^{j+a}\ell^{\prime}
, where
(p,\ell^{\prime})=1,
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle g(k,p^{j}\ell)=g(k,p^{j+a}\ell^{\prime})=\chi_{p^{j+a}}(\ell^{\prime})\chi_{\ell^{\prime}}(p^{j+a})g(k,p^{j+a})g(k,\ell^{\prime}).

When
b=j+a-1
, one has

(2.6)

\displaystyle g(k,p^{j+a})=\mathrm{N}(p^{b})\times\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
g(k^{\prime},p)&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
\overline{g(k^{\prime},p)}&\text{ if }j+a\equiv 2(\operatorname{mod}3),\end{cases}

by
[
4
, Lemma 2.8]
.
Using cubic reciprocity, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{p^{j+a}}(\ell^{\prime})\chi_{\ell^{\prime}}(p^{j+a})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j+a\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
\overline{\chi_{p}(\ell^{\prime})}&\text{if }j+a\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}3),\\
\chi_{p}(\ell^{\prime})&\text{if }j+a\equiv 2(\operatorname{mod}3).\end{cases}

Thus, by (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have shown the statement in the lemma when
b=j+a-1.

Now if
p^{j+a}\mid k,
which is the case when
b\geq j+a,

g(k,p^{j+a})
is nonzero only if
j+a\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}3)
. In that case, the first three terms in (
2.5
) give
\phi_{\mathcal{K}}(p^{j+a})
.

In the remaining cases,
0\leq b\leq j+a-2
, and it suffices to analyze
g(k^{\prime},p^{j+a-b})
. By Lemma
2.4
,
g(k,p^{j+a})
vanishes.
∎

In light of the work of Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
18
]
, we prove below a “twisted-counting” for the number of
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
. (Note that when
\ell=1
, it gives a count of the number of
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\asymp X
.) Let
\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
denote the Dedekind zeta function of
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
, such that
\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{1/4}\leq\sqrt{X}
, and let

S=\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\ell)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right),

where
\Phi
is a smooth function defined as above.
When
\ell
is a cube, for any
A>0,
we have

S=\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{\mathrm{N}(9)}\zeta^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}(2)\prod_{p\mid\ell}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(p)}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{X}{A}\right)+O\left(\frac{A}{X^{1+\epsilon}}\right).

When
\ell
is not a cube, we have

S\ll\frac{X}{A}+\frac{A}{X^{1+\epsilon}}.

Consequently, with the choice of
A=X^{1/2}/\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{1/4}
, all the errors above are

\ll X^{1/2}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{1/4}
.

Proof.

For brevity, let
|\cdot|
denote the ideal norm in the following proof. Using

(2.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\\
d^{2}\mid\mathfrak{f}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mathfrak{f}\text{ is square-free,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

to detect square-free
\mathfrak{f}
, and writing
\mathfrak{f}=d^{2}f
, we have

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\end{subarray}}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)\chi_{d^{2}}(\ell)\sum_{f\equiv\overline{d^{2}}(\operatorname{mod}9)}\chi_{f}(\ell)\Phi\left(\frac{|fd^{2}|}{X}\right).

We trivially bound the sum for
|d|>A
as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|>A\end{subarray}}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)\chi_{d^{2}}(\ell)\sum_{f\equiv\overline{d^{2}}(\operatorname{mod}9)}\chi_{f}(\ell)\Phi\left(\frac{|fd^{2}|}{X}\right)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|>A\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{|d^{2}|}\ll\frac{X}{A}.

For
|d|\leq A
, after applying Poisson summation to the sum over
f
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq A\end{subarray}}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)\chi_{d^{2}}(\ell)\sum_{f\equiv\overline{d^{2}}(\operatorname{mod}9)}\chi_{f}(\ell)\Phi\left(\frac{|fd^{2}|}{X}\right)\\
&=\frac{X}{|9|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq A\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)\chi_{d^{2}}(\ell)}{|d^{2}|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]}\widehat{\Phi}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{X|k|}{|9{\ell}d^{2}|}}\bigg)\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}\ell)}\frac{\chi_{\ell}(r)}{|\ell|}e\bigg(\frac{-(r9\overline{9}+\overline{d^{2}}\ell\overline{\ell})k}{{9\ell}\sqrt{-3}}\bigg).\end{split}

When
k=0
, the above contribution is nonzero if and only if
\ell
is a cube, and in that case

S=\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{|9|}\zeta^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}(2)\prod_{p\mid\ell}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p|}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{X}{A}\right).

When
k\neq 0,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{X}{|9|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq A\\
3\nmid d\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(d)\chi_{d^{2}}(\ell)}{|d^{2}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\widehat{\Phi}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{X|k|}{|9{\ell}d^{2}|}}\bigg)e\bigg(\frac{-\overline{d^{2}\ell}k}{{9}\sqrt{-3}}\bigg)\frac{1}{|\ell|}\sum_{r(\operatorname{mod}\ell)}\chi_{\ell}(r)e\left(\frac{-r\overline{9}k}{{\ell}\sqrt{-3}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq A\end{subarray}}\frac{X}{|9d^{2}|}\frac{|9\ell d^{2}|}{X}\left(\frac{|9\ell d^{2}|}{X}\right)^{K-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|k|}\right)^{K}\ll\frac{|9|^{K-1}|\ell|}{X^{\epsilon K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq A\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|k|}\right)^{K}\ll\frac{|\ell|A}{X},

where since

\frac{X}{|\ell d^{2}|}\geq X^{\epsilon}\text{ for some }\epsilon<1,\text{ and }\ \widehat{\Phi}(x)\ll_{K}x^{-K}\text{ for any }K>0,

we choose
K>1
such that
\epsilon K=1.

Thus, when
\ell
is a cube, we have

S=\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{|9|}\zeta^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}(2)\prod_{p\mid\ell}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p|}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{X}{A}\right)+O\left(\frac{|\ell|A}{X}\right),

and when
\ell
is not a cube,

S\ll\frac{X}{A}+\frac{|\ell|A}{X},

which completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we recall some facts regarding normal random variables. Let
\mathcal{N}
be a normal random variable with mean 0 and variance
\sigma^{2}
. The
k^{\mathrm{th}}
moment of
\mathcal{N}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{k}]=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $k$ is odd;}\\
(k-1)!!\sigma^{n}&\mbox{if $k$ is even,}\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{k}]
denotes the expectation of
\mathcal{N}^{k}
, and
k!!
stands for the double factorial defined by

(2.9)

\displaystyle k!!:=\prod_{j=0}^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}(k-2j).

We shall require the following version of the “method of moments” due to Fréchet and Shohat
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.7

(Fréchet and Shohat)
.

Suppose that the distribution of a random variable
\mathcal{X}
is determined by its moments and that each
\mathcal{X}_{n}
has moments of all orders. If
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{k})=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}_{n}^{k})+o(\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}_{n}^{k}))
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{X}_{n}
converges to
\mathcal{X}
in distribution.

3.
Key propositions

We collect the statements of key propositions used to prove Theorem
1.1
in this section. (Their proofs, which are all under GRH, are given in Section
5
.) We begin with the following proposition, which allows us to approximate the central values of
L
-functions by the real parts of certain Dirichlet polynomials.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}
be a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
over a number field
F
, and let
3\leq x\leq\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})
. Assume
GRH for
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
, and let
\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}
denote
the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
. If
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
is non-vanishing, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\log|L(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|={\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)+\Delta(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})\log\log x+O(1)+O_{F}\bigg(\frac{\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{\log x}+\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\log\bigg(1+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\bigg)\bigg),

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}w(\mathfrak{p}),\quad w(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{\log x}}}\frac{\log(x/\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p}))}{\log x},\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}=1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

In light of this proposition and the method of moments (Lemma
2.7
), we calculate the following moments of
\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)
to study the distribution of
\log|L(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|
. Indeed, as argued in
[
10
, Sec. 5]
, such calculations allow one to deduce moments of
{\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)
(see Lemma
4.1
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k,j
be non-negative integers and
x=X^{\frac{13/22}{\log\log\log X}}
for large
X
.
Then (unconditionally)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{k}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{j}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)\ll_{k,j}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},

whenever
k\neq j
. If
k=j
, we have (unconditionally)

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{F}}\big|\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)\big|^{2k}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)=\frac{k!X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)}(\log\log X)^{k}+O_{k}(X(\log\log X)^{k-1+\varepsilon}).

Moreover, for any
L
such that
e^{L}\leq X^{13/11}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{k}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{j}}\sum_{\gamma_{{}_{\mathfrak{f}}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{{}_{\mathfrak{f}}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)\ll_{k,j}X\left(\log\log X\right)^{\min\{k,j\}}

whenever
k\neq j
. If
k=j
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big|\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)\big|^{2k}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)=\frac{k!X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)L}\left(\hat{h}(0)\log X+O(1)\right)(\log\log X)^{k}

\displaystyle+O_{k}(X(\log\log X)^{k-1+\varepsilon}).

As seen in Section
5.2
, the moments calculations require, in a subtle way, the following “twisted” 1-level density estimates.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
,
L\geq 1
be a real number, and
h
and
\Phi
be defined as in Section
2
. Set

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{3,h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda_{\mathcal{K}}(q)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}(q)^{3}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(p)}\right)^{-1}\hat{h}\left(\frac{3\log\mathrm{N}(q)}{L}\right),

and

D^{T}(X;\ell,h,\Phi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\ell)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right).

If
e^{\frac{11L}{27}}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{\frac{14}{27}}\leq X^{\frac{13}{27}}

(i.e.,
e^{11L}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{14}\leq X^{13}
), then when
\ell
is a cube,

\displaystyle D^{T}(X;\ell,h,\Phi)=\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81L}\zeta^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}(2)\prod_{p\mid\ell}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(p)}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{h}(0)\log X+C_{3,h}+O(1)\right)+O\bigg(\frac{X^{\frac{14}{27}+\epsilon}e^{\frac{11L}{27}}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{\frac{14}{27}}}{L}\bigg).

When
\ell=qa^{3}
a unique prime
q
times a cube, then

\displaystyle D^{T}(X;\ell,h,\Phi)\ll\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)\log\mathrm{N}(q)(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{N}(q)})\zeta^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}(2)\prod_{p\mid q\ell}(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(p)})^{-1}}{81(\mathrm{N}(q)-\mathrm{N}(q)^{-1/2})L}+\frac{X^{\frac{14}{27}+\epsilon}e^{\frac{11L}{27}}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{\frac{14}{27}}}{L}.

When neither is the case,

D^{T}(X;\ell,h,\Phi)\ll\frac{X^{\frac{14}{27}+\epsilon}e^{\frac{11L}{27}}\mathrm{N}(\ell)^{\frac{14}{27}}}{L}.

4.
Proof of the main theorem

With Proposition
3.1
in mind, we first derive the moments of
{\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)
from Proposition
3.2
as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
n!!
denote the double factorial function defined as in (
2.9
). When
k
is odd,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)\ll_{k}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},

and under GRH,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)\ll_{k}X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}.

When
k
is even

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)=\Big(\frac{k}{2}-1\Big)!!\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\log\log X\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O_{k}\big(X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}\big),

and under GRH,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{k}{2}-1\Big)!!\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)L}\left(\hat{h}(0)\log X+O(1)\right)\Big(\frac{1}{2}\log\log X\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O(X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}).

Proof.

Writing
{\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)=\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)+\overline{\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)}
, by the binomial theorem, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{i}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{k-i}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right),\end{split}

and similarly,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{i}\overline{\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)^{k-i}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right).\end{split}

According to Proposition
3.2
, for any odd
k
(so that it is impossible to have
i=k-i
), (
4.1
) is bounded by

\ll_{k}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

If
k
is even, the term with
i=k-i=k/2
dominates, and we thus apply Proposition
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-k}{{k}\choose{k/2}}\Big(\frac{k}{2}\Big)!\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)}(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O_{k}\big(X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{k}{2}-1\Big)!!\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\log\log X\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O_{k}\big(X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}\big).

Similarly, for
e^{11L/27}\leq X^{13/27}
, (
4.2
) for odd
k
is bounded by

\ll_{k}X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\epsilon}
. When
k
is even, again
i=k-i=k/2
dominates, so

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\big({\mathfrak{Re}}(\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x))\big)^{k}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{X}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-k}{k\choose k/2}{\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)!}\frac{X\widehat{\Phi}(0)}{81\zeta_{\mathcal{K}}(2)L}\left(\hat{h}(0)\log X+O(1)\right)(\log\log X)^{k/2}+O\big(X(\log\log X)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}\big)

as desired.
∎

Following
[
18
]
, we require the following lemma to control the contribution of zeros away from
\frac{1}{2}
(by discarding certain
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
with a large zero sum).

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume GRH, and let
x=X^{\frac{13/22}{\log\log\log X}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\#\Big\{X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X:\sum_{(\log X\log\log X)^{-1}\leq|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right)\geq(\log\log\log X)^{3}\Big\}\ll\frac{X}{\log\log\log X}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
X\leq\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\left(\frac{\sin(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L/2)}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L/2}\right)^{2}\ll\frac{X\log X}{L}.

Integrate
L
in the range
\log x\leq L\leq 2\log x
and using the bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y}\int_{y}^{2y}\left(\frac{\sin(\pi tu)}{\pi tu}\right)^{2}du\gg\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{(ty)^{2}}\right\}

for any
y>0
and
t\neq 0
, we obtain (with
t=\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}{2\pi}
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\frac{1}{\log x}\int_{\log x}^{2\log x}\left(\frac{\sin(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L/2)}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L/2}\right)^{2}dL\gg\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\min\left\{1,\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right\}.

The right side of (
4.3
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{X\log X}{\log x}\int_{\log x}^{2\log x}\frac{1}{L}dL\ll\frac{X\log X}{\log x}=\frac{22}{13}{X\log\log\log X},

so

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right\}\ll X\log\log\log X.

Since for
|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|\geq(\log X\log\log X)^{-1}
, one has

\displaystyle\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right)\ll(\log\log\log X)\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right\},

and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\sum_{(\log X\log\log X)^{-1}\leq|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x)^{2}}\right)\ll X(\log\log\log X)^{2},

which yields the claimed estimate.
∎

In what follows, to ease the notation, we denote

\displaystyle\Psi(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}dt,

where the integrand is the probability density function of a standard normal random variable.
To ensure that a positive proportion of the family
\mathcal{F}
does not have low-lying zeros, we prove the following lower bound.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha<\beta
be real numbers. Let
\mathcal{H}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)
be the set of discriminants
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
, with
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X
, such that

\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X):=\frac{{\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\in(\alpha,\beta),

and
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
has no zeros
\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}
with

|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|\leq((\log X)(\log\log X))^{-1}.

Then under GRH, for any
\delta>0
,

\mathcal{H}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\left(\frac{2}{13}-\delta\right)(\Psi(\alpha,\beta)+o(1))\#\{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}:X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\}.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be a smooth approximation to the indicator function of
[1,2]
, and let

h(t)=(\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t})^{2}
be
the Fejér kernel.
From Lemma
4.1
and the method of moments (Lemma
2.7
), it follows that

(4.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)=(\Psi(\alpha,\beta)+o(1))\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)

and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)&=(\Psi(\alpha,\beta)+o(1))\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)\\
&=(\Psi(\alpha,\beta)+o(1))\frac{1}{L}\left(\hat{h}(0)\log X+O(1)\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right).\end{split}

Now, we take
L=(F-\eta)\log X
, with
F=\frac{13}{11}
, and we recall that
\hat{h}(0)=1
. Denote
\mathcal{Z}
the collection of
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
has no zeros with
|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|\leq((\log X)(\log\log X))^{-1}
. By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we then derive that

\left(\frac{1}{F-\eta}+o(1)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}h\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}L}{2\pi}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)

is greater or equal to

0+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\backslash\mathcal{Z}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Z}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)

as
\mathcal{F}
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{F}\backslash\mathcal{Z}
. Hence, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Z}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{F-\eta}+o(1)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)\in(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathrm{N}(n)}{X}\right),

as stated in the lemma.
∎

With these lemmas and propositions in hand, we are now ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)
from Lemma
4.3
. For
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)
, one has

\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{f};X)=\frac{{\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\in(\alpha,\beta),

while
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
has no zeros
\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}
with

|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|\leq((\log X)(\log\log X))^{-1}.

Meanwhile, by Lemma
4.2
, we can remove
\ll X/\log\log\log X
conductors
\mathfrak{f}
from
\mathcal{H}_{X}(\alpha,\beta)
so that the
contribution of zeros from
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
with
|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}|\geq((\log X)(\log\log X))^{-1}
to the last sum in (
3.1
)
is bounded by
(\log\log\log X)^{3}
. Therefore, there are at least

\left(\frac{2}{13}-\delta\right)(\Psi(\alpha,\beta)+o(1))\#\{\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}:X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X\}

conductors
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{F}
with
X<\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\leq 2X
such that

\frac{\log|L(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}=\frac{{\mathfrak{Re}}\mathcal{P}(\chi_{\mathfrak{f}};x)}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}+O\left(\frac{(\log\log\log X)^{3}}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\right)\in(\alpha,\beta),

as desired.
∎

In this section, we will prove Propositions
3.1
-
3.3
.

5.1.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

The logarithmic derivative of the Hadamard factorisation of
L(s,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})
yields

(5.1)

{\mathfrak{Re}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})=-\sum_{\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\frac{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(t-\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}+O_{F}(\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})),

for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq 1
and
|t|\leq 1
. Integrating
\sigma
from
\frac{1}{2}
to
\sigma_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log x}
then yields

\displaystyle\log|L(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}+it,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|-\log|L(\sigma_{0}+it,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|=O\Big(\frac{\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})}{\log x}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\log\frac{(\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(t-\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}{(t-\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}.

On the other hand, following
[
18
, Eq. (11)]
and
[
19
, Lemma 14]
(rooted in an identity of Selberg), for
\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{\sigma}}\frac{\log(x/\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\right)^{\prime}(\sigma,\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}\frac{x^{\rho-\sigma}}{(\rho-\sigma)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{x^{\sigma}\log x}\right)

for
x\geq 3
. If
L(\sigma_{0},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})\neq 0
, integrating both sides from
\sigma_{0}
to
\infty
and taking the real part, we obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log|L(\sigma_{0},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})|&={\mathfrak{Re}}\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{\sigma_{0}}\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})}\frac{\log(x/\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}{\mathfrak{Re}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{0},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})\\
&+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\mathfrak{Re}}\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-\sigma}}{(\rho-\sigma)^{2}}d\sigma+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}(\log x)^{2}}\right).\end{split}

For the first sum in (
5.2
), the terms
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{p}^{k}
for
k\geq 3
contribute at most
O(1)
, and the contribution of
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
is

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}(\mathfrak{p})}{2\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{2\sigma_{0}}}\frac{\log(x/\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{2}))}{\log x}.

This is
O(1)
if
\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}
is not quadratic, and it is

\frac{1}{2}\log\log x+O(1)

if
\chi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}=1
.
For the second term on the right of (
5.2
) under GRH, by
[
11
, Theorem 5.17]
, one has

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{0},\chi_{\mathfrak{f}})\ll\frac{1}{2\sigma_{0}-1}(\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f}))^{2-2\sigma_{0}}+\log\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f})\ll\log x+\log\log\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{f}).

Finally, for
|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x|\geq 1
, we know

\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-\sigma}}{(\rho-\sigma)^{2}}d\sigma\ll\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}x^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}d\sigma\ll\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}\log x}\leq\log x.

Also, for
|\gamma_{\mathfrak{f}}\log x|\leq 1

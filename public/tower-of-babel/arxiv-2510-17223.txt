Borel subalgebras of Lie algebras of vector fields Thanks: The work of the first author is supported by the grant RSF 25-11-00302.
Ivan Arzhantsev and Mikhail Zaidenberg

Abstract
. In [ 5 ] we described the Borel subgroups and maximal solvable subgroups of the automorphism groups of affine toric surfaces. In the present paper, we introduce the notion of integrable Borel subalgebras of the Lie algebra of the automorphism group of an affine variety. We show that these are precisely the tangent algebras of the Borel subgroups. We classify the integrable Borel subalgebras in the Lie algebras of the automorphism groups of toric affine surfaces, notably of the affine plane and its cyclic quotients.

2.1.
Tangent Lie algebras of derivations

Definition 2.1

(cf.
[
20
, Definition 4.1.1]
)
.

A
Lie ind-algebra
is a
countably dimensional vector space
equipped with a Lie bracket. A Lie ind-algebra is
called

nested
if it has an ascending filtration
by a sequence of finite-dimensional
Lie subalgebras.

For an affine variety
X
,
the automorphism group
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

is an affine ind-group,
see e.g.
[
12
]
.
The Lie algebra of derivations
\mathop{\rm Der}({\mathcal{O}}(X))

coincides with
the Lie algebra of vector fields
\mathop{\rm Vec}(X)
.
The tangent Lie algebra

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

is a subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\mathcal{O}}(X))
.
For example,
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n}))

consists of derivations
in
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])

with constant divergence,
see
[
12
, Proposition 15.7.2]
.
For
n\geq 2
,
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n}))

is a maximal proper Lie subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
,
see
[
6
, Proposition 2.21]
.

The Lie algebra
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

viewed as a vector space
is of countable dimension,
and therefore is a Lie ind-algebra.
Indeed, let us choose
a dense open subset
U\subset X

such that
TX|_{U}

is a trivial vector bundle of rank

n=\dim(X)
.
The vector space
\mathop{\rm Vec}(X)

is clearly a subspace of

\mathop{\rm Vec}(U)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(U)^{n}
.
Since
{\mathcal{O}}(U)
is of countable dimension,
the same is true for
\mathop{\rm Vec}(X)
and

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
.

The following theorem gives
a uniform estimate
for the derived lengths
of solvable Lie subalgebras
of derivations,
cf. also
[
23
, Theorems 1.1–1.3]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
22
, Corollary 4]
)
.

Let
L
be a nilpotent (solvable, respectively)
subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\mathcal{O}}(X))
.
Then the derived length of
L
is at most
n

(
2n
, respectively).

The next corollary and its proof are
analogous to that of
[
13
, Proposition 3.10]

Corollary 2.3

Any solvable Lie subalgebra
L\subset\mathop{\rm Der}({\mathcal{O}}(X))

is contained in a Borel subalgebra.
The same is true for
the Lie subalgebras of
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

Proof.

This follows immediately from Zorn’s Lemma
due to the uniform boundedness of derived lengths in
totally ordered chains of Lie subalgebras,
see Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.16

In the proof of Proposition

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

\leq\dim(X)+1

.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}})

the smallest locally finite

-saturated Lie subalgebra
of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\mathcal{O}}(X))

{\mathfrak{h}}

We have a semidirect product

{\mathfrak{t}}

is a maximal toral Lie

Proof of Proposition
2.16
.

By Lemmas
2.20
and
2.21
,
we have

{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{i})\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{i}))
.
By the uniqueness of subalgebras

{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}:={\mathfrak{g}}_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{i})

and algebraic subgroups

G_{i}:=G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{i})

we conclude that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{i+1}
and

G_{i}\subset G_{i+1}
.

Let
\hat{G}=\varinjlim(G_{i})
.
Certainly,
\hat{G}
is a solvable connected
nested subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

with derived length
\leq\dim(X)+1

and

{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\hat{G})=\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(G_{i})
.
Since
G
and
\hat{G}
are connected
and nested,
they are closed,
see Theorem
2.14
.
By Proposition
2.7
(2), we have

G=G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}})\subset\hat{G}
.
Therefore,
G
is solvable and closed
with derived length
\leq\dim(X)+1
.
This proves part (1).

To prove part (2), suppose that

{\mathfrak{h}}
is generated by locally nilpotent
derivations
\partial_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
.
By Proposition
2.7
(1), there
exists a nested subgroup

G^{\prime}=\varinjlim G^{\prime}_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

such that

{\mathfrak{h}}=\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(G^{\prime}_{i})
.
We can assume that

G^{\prime}
and all
G^{\prime}_{i}
are connected.
Since all
\mathop{\rm Lie}(G^{\prime}_{i})
are solvable,
the connected
algebraic subgroups

G^{\prime}_{i}
are also solvable.

Now consider the unipotent one-parameter
subgroups

U_{\alpha}=\exp({\Bbbk}\partial_{\alpha})
.
Since
\partial_{\alpha}\in\mathop{\rm Lie}(G^{\prime}_{i})

for some
i=i(\alpha)
, we have

U_{\alpha}\subset G^{\prime}_{i}
. For a finite set
of indices

\eta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subset A

we can find

i=i(\eta)
such that

{\mathcal{U}}_{\eta}:=\langle U_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,U_{\alpha_{n}}\rangle\subset G^{\prime}_{i}
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{U}}_{\eta}
is a solvable
connected affine algebraic group generated by
unipotent subgroups. Certainly,
such a group is unipotent.

On the other hand, since by our assumption

{\mathfrak{h}}=\langle\partial_{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in A\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}
,
for every

i
we can find

\eta_{i}=\{\alpha^{(i)}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{(i)}_{n_{i}}\}\subset A

such that

\mathop{\rm Lie}(G^{\prime}_{i})\subset\langle\partial_{\alpha^{(i)}_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n_{i}}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}.

It follows that
G_{i}^{\prime}\subset U_{\eta_{i}}
(see

[
12

) is
a unipotent algebraic group.
Thus,
G^{\prime}=\varinjlim G^{\prime}_{i}=\varinjlim U_{\eta_{i}}

is a nested unipotent ind-group, and

{\mathfrak{h}}=\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(U_{\eta_{i}})
consists
of locally nilpotent derivations.

By Proposition
2.7
(2),
we have
G\subset G^{\prime}
. Hence,

G
is also a nested unipotent ind-group.
As shown,
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G^{\prime})={\mathfrak{h}}
,
therefore
\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)={\mathfrak{h}}
, as stated.
∎

Question 1.

Proceeding by recursion, it suffices

The following example shows that

Example 2.22

Let

Consider the metabelian Lie subalgebra

3.
Correspondence
“Borel subgroups

\leftrightsquigarrow

Borel subalgebras”

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
.
Let us start with the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Every Borel subgroup
{\mathcal{B}}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
is a
closed subgroup of derived length

{\leq\dim(X)+1}

nested by connected algebraic subgroups.

Proof.

The closure of a solvable subgroup
is also a solvable subgroup, see

[
20
, Lemma 5.1.3(2)]
. Therefore,

{\mathcal{B}}
is closed.
Theorem
2.13
now
applies and gives the result.
∎

We have the following analogue of Corollary

2.3
(see Definition
1.2
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Any solvable integrable subalgebra

{\mathfrak{h}}

of
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

is contained in an integrable
Borel subalgebra

{\mathfrak{b}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
.

Proof.

Let
\{{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in A\}
be a
totally ordered ascending chain of
Lie subalgebras of
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

that contain
{\mathfrak{h}}
and are filtered
by solvable locally finite subalgebras.
Let

G_{\alpha}=G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha})
,
see Proposition
2.16
.
Then
\{G_{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in A\}
is a
totally ordered ascending chain of solvable
connected nested subgroups of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
.
By Theorem
2.13
,
the closure
\bar{G}
of

G:=\varinjlim G_{\alpha}

is a solvable
connected subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

of derived length
\leq\dim(X)+1

filtered by an ascending sequence
of connected algebraic subgroups

\bar{G}_{i}
. Then

{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{G})=\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{G}_{i})\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
is filtered by
locally finite Lie subalgebras
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{G}_{i})
.
Since
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{G})

for all
\alpha\in A
,
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{G})

is an upper bound for the chain
\{{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}\}
.
By Zorn’s Lemma, the set of
Lie subalgebras of
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

that contain
{\mathfrak{h}}
and are filtered
by locally finite subalgebras has
a maximal element
{\mathfrak{b}}
.
Thus,
{\mathfrak{b}}
is an integrable
Borel subalgebra which contains
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
be a Borel subgroup.
Then
{\mathfrak{b}}:=\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{B}})
is an integrable
Borel subalgebra.

Proof.

We have
{\mathcal{B}}=\varinjlim{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
,
where the
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
are connected
algebraic subgroups, see
Lemma
3.1
.
Any vector
v\in{\mathfrak{b}}=T_{e}{\mathcal{B}}
is tangent to

{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
for
i\gg 1
.
Therefore,
{\mathfrak{b}}=\varinjlim{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}
,
where the
{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}=\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{B}}_{i})

are locally finite,
see
[
20
, Sec. 1.6, Corollary]
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}=\varinjlim{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
be
a solvable integrable subalgebra
that contains
{\mathfrak{b}}
, where
the
{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
are locally finite.
Let also
G=G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}})\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
be
the unique smallest solvable connected
nested ind-subgroup
such that
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)
,
see Proposition
2.16
.
We have

G=\varinjlim G_{i}
, where

G_{i}=G_{\min}({\mathfrak{h}}_{i})\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

is the minimal connected algebraic subgroup
containing
{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
, see Lemma
2.21
.
For every
i
, there exists
j

such that
{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}_{j}
. Therefore,

{\mathfrak{B}}_{i}\subset G_{j}
, and so
{\mathfrak{B}}\subset G
.
Since
G
is solvable,

G={\mathcal{B}}
by the maximality of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Thus,
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}
, which proves the claimed
maximality of
{\mathfrak{b}}
.
∎

The main result of this section
is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

{\mathfrak{b}}=\varinjlim{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))

be an integrable Borel subalgebra.
Then there exists a unique
Borel subgroup
{\mathcal{B}}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

such that
{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{B}})
. Moreover, any
solvable closed connected subgroup

H\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

with
\mathop{\rm Lie}(H)={\mathfrak{b}}
coincides with
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

There exists an example of a
closed ind-subgroup

G\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{3})

and a proper closed subgroup

H\subset G

such that
\mathop{\rm Lie}(H)=\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)
,
see
[
12
, Theorem 17.3.1]
.
Therefore, the uniqueness in Theorem

3.4
is a
special phenomenon.

We prove the existence
and the uniqueness statements
separately.

Existence proof.

By Lemma
2.21
,
there exists a unique minimal
solvable connected algebraic subgroup

G_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
such that

{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G_{i})
.
We have
G_{i}=T_{i}\ltimes U_{i}
,
where
T_{i}
is a maximal torus and
U_{i}

is the unipotent radical of
G_{i}
.

By the uniqueness of the subgroups
G_{i}

we have
G_{i}\subset G_{i+1}

and
U_{i}\subset U_{i+1}
. Therefore,

U:=\varinjlim U_{i}
is
a nested unipotent ind-subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
.
The maximal tori
T_{i}
of
G_{i}

can be chosen such that

T_{i}\subset T_{i+1}
.
Since the sequence
(T_{i})_{i}

stabilizes, we can assume that

T_{i}=T_{1}=:T
for all
i
. Thus,

G:=\varinjlim G_{i}=T\ltimes U\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

is a connected nested ind-subgroup.
By Theorem
2.14
,
G
is closed.

Since

U
is solvable, see
[
20
, Theorem 5.3.1]
,

G=T\ltimes U
is also solvable.
We have

\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)=\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(G_{i})=\mathop{\rm Lie}(T)\ltimes\varinjlim\mathop{\rm Lie}(U_{i})=\mathop{\rm Lie}(T)\ltimes\mathop{\rm Lie}(U),

where the factors are solvable,
see Theorem
2.13
.
Therefore,
\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)
is an integrable solvable
subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
filtered by
the locally finite subalgebras
\mathop{\rm Lie}(G_{i})
.
By assumption,

{\mathfrak{b}}=\varinjlim{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)
is
an integrable Borel subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
.
Therefore, we have
{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)
.

Let
\hat{G}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
be
a solvable closed connected subgroup
containing
G
. By Theorem
2.13
,

\hat{G}
is nested,
and so
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\hat{G})\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
is
a solvable intergrable Lie subalgebra
containing
\mathop{\rm Lie}(G)={\mathfrak{b}}
.
Since
{\mathfrak{b}}

is an integrable Borel subalgebra
of
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X))
,
we have
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\hat{G})={\mathfrak{b}}
.

Let
\{\hat{G}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}

be a totally ordered ascending chain of
solvable closed connected subgroups
containing
G
.
By Theorem
2.13
,
they are all nested by connected
algebraic subgroups and have
derived lengths
\leq\dim(X)+1
. Consider
the closure
\bar{\mathfrak{G}}
of the solvable subgroup

{\mathfrak{G}}=\cup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}\hat{G}_{\alpha}
.
Then
\bar{\mathfrak{G}}
is
solvable (see
[
20
, Lemma 5.1.3(2)]
)

of derived length
\leq\dim(X)+1

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\bar{\mathfrak{G}})={\mathfrak{b}}

subgroups
of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

containing
G

say
{\mathcal{B}}

(cf.
[
13
, Proposition 3.10]
).
Thus,
{\mathcal{B}}\supset G
is a solvable closed connected
subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

maximal among the subgroups
of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

with the same properties. That is,

{\mathcal{B}}
is a Borel subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)

such that
\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{B}})={\mathfrak{b}}
.
∎

Uniqueness proof.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a Borel subgroup
of
\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
with
\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{B}})={\mathfrak{b}}
,
and let
H\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}(X)
be
a solvable closed connected subgroup
with
\mathop{\rm Lie}(H)={\mathfrak{b}}
.
By Lemma

,

algebraic subgroups

.
This produces the ascending filtrations

For any

.
Therefore,
we have

.

The subgroup

is not maximal among all subgroups of

\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
.

Note that the integrable Borel
subalgebra

consists of the lower resp. upper triangular
derivations.

4.2.
The case of the affine line

Here we classify the Borel subalgebras of
the Lie algebra
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
.
The polynomials in
{\Bbbk}[x]
of the form

f(x)=\lambda(x-\alpha)^{k}+\mu(x-\alpha),\quad\text{where}\quad k\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha,\lambda,\mu\in{\Bbbk},

will be called
special
.
Non-special polynomials exist
in any degree
\geq 3
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(a)

Every Borel subalgebra
of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])

is of dimension
1
or
2
.

(b)

The one-dimensional Borel subalgebras
of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
are precisely
the subalgebras
{\Bbbk}p(x)\partial/\partial x
,
where
p\in{\Bbbk}[x]
is non-special.

(c)

The two-dimensional
Borel subalgebras
of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
are precisely
the metabelian Lie subalgebras

(1)

\left({\Bbbk}(x-\alpha)^{k}\oplus{\Bbbk}(x-\alpha)\right)\partial/\partial x,

where
\alpha\in{\Bbbk}
,

k\geq 0
and
k\neq 1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Any derivation

f(x)\partial/\partial x\in\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])

with a non-special polynomial
f\in{\Bbbk}[x]

is contained in the unique
Borel subalgebra
{\Bbbk}f(x)\partial/\partial x
of

\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
.

Remark 4.4
.

Letting in (
1
)

\alpha=0
and
k=0

we see that

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}))={\Bbbk}x\partial/\partial x\ltimes{\Bbbk}\partial/\partial x

is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
.
In fact, it is a unique integrable Borel
subalgebra of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
.

Recall that

[f\partial/\partial x,g\partial/\partial x]=(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)\partial/\partial x.

The proof of Proposition
4.2

is preceded by the following notation
and Lemma
4.6
.

Notation 4.5
.

Given
f\in{\Bbbk}[x]
we set
\nu(f)

stand for the zero order at
x=0
of
f
.
If
f,g\in{\Bbbk}[x]
and

\nu(f)\neq\nu(g)
,
then

(2)

\nu(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)=\nu(f)+\nu(g)-1.

Especially,
if
\nu(f),\nu(g)\geq 2
, then

\nu(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)>\max\{\nu(f),\nu(g)\}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Consider a subalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
.
Let
u_{i}=f_{i}(x)\partial/\partial x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
i=1,2
be such that

\min\{\nu(f_{1}),\nu(f_{2})\}\geq 2
and

\nu(f_{1})\neq\nu(f_{2})
.
Then the commutant

[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
contains two elements

v_{i}=g_{i}(x)\partial/\partial x
,

such that

\min\{\nu(g_{1}),\nu(g_{2})\}\geq 3
and

\nu(g_{1})\neq\nu(g_{2})
.

Proof.

Consider the elements

v_{1}=[u_{1},[u_{1},u_{2}]]=g_{1}(x)\partial/\partial x\quad\text{and}\quad v_{2}=[u_{1},u_{2}]=g_{2}(x)\partial/\partial x.

Using (
2
), we obtain:

\nu(g_{1})=2\nu(f_{1})+\nu(f_{2})-2>\nu(g_{2})=\nu(f_{1})+\nu(f_{2})-1\geq 3.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])

is infinite-dimensional.
Then applying Lemma
4.6
,
we see that all successive commutants
of
{\mathfrak{g}}

do not vanish, so
{\mathfrak{g}}
is not solvable.
Thus, every solvable subalgebra,
and in particular

\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])
,
is finite dimensional.
Finite dimensional Lie subalgebras
of
\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x])

are classified in
[

, Proposition 3]
.
They have dimensions 1,2 and 3.
Every one-dimensional Lie subalgebra
is abelian.
Up to isomorphism, there is a unique
two-dimensional Lie subalgebra;
it is not abelian but
metabelian, and therefore solvable.
Up to isomorphism, there exists a unique
subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of dimension 3,
isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\Bbbk})
,
see Remark
2.4
. This proves (a).
Now, (b) and (c)

.
∎

The case of the affine plane

4.3.1.
Borel subgroups

We use the following notation
for remarkable subgroups of

(resp. of

•

the triangular
de Jonquières subgroups

4.3.2.
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

as a bigraded Lie algebra

Recall that
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))=\mathop{\rm Vec}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

consists of polynomial vector fields
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}

with constant divergence,
where the divergence of a vector field

\delta=f\partial/\partial x+g\partial/\partial y
is

{\rm div}(\delta)=\partial f/\partial x+\partial g/\partial y
.
The Lie subalgebra
\mathop{\rm Vec}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\subset\mathop{\rm Vec}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
of vector fileds
over
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
with zero divergence coincides
with
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm SAut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))
,
see for example
[
12
, Proposition 15.7.2]
.
There is a decomposition

(3)

{\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})={\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\oplus{\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x+y\partial/\partial y).

Letting

{\mathop{\rm Vec}}_{0}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):={\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\cap{\mathop{\rm Vec}}_{0}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}),\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathop{\rm Vec}}_{0}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})=\{\delta\in{\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\,|\,\delta(0)=0\},

we obtain

\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathop{\rm Aut}}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))={\mathop{\rm Vec}}_{0}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\oplus{\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x+y\partial/\partial y),

see
[
20
, Sec. 6.2]
.

The standard
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-grading
on
{\Bbbk}[x,y]
induces
a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-grading on

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))=\mathop{\rm Vec}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

and on
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm SAut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))=\mathop{\rm Vec}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
.
For
i,j\geq 0
we have

\mathop{\rm bideg}(x^{i}y^{j}\partial/\partial x)=(i-1,j)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm bideg}(x^{i}y^{j}\partial/\partial y)=(i,j-1).

Thus, the bidegrees of homogeneous derivations
are the pairs
(m,n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
,
where
m,n\geq-1
and
(m,n)\neq(-1,-1)
.
If
\partial\in\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x,y])
is a homogeneous
derivation of bidegree

(a,b)
and
x^{i}y^{j}\notin\ker(\partial)
, then

\mathop{\rm bideg}(\partial(x^{i}y^{j}))=(a+i,b+j)
.
For non-commuting homogeneous derivations

\partial
and
\delta
we have

\mathop{\rm bideg}([\delta,\partial])=\mathop{\rm bideg}(\delta)+\mathop{\rm bideg}(\partial)
.

The homogeneous
locally nilpotent derivations
of
{\Bbbk}[x,y]
are the following:

(4)

\partial_{n}^{+}=y^{n}\partial/\partial x\quad\text{resp.}\quad\partial_{m}^{-}=x^{m}\partial/\partial y,\quad m,n\geq 0\in{\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}),

The bidegree

\mathop{\rm bideg}(\partial_{n}^{+})=(-1,n)\quad\text{resp.}\quad\mathop{\rm bideg}(\partial_{m}^{-})=(m,-1)

(seen as a lattice vector)
is called a
Demazure root
.
The other homogeneous elements
of
\mathop{\rm Vec}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

are (up to a scaling) of the form

(5)

\partial_{a,b}=x^{a}y^{b}((b+1)x\partial/\partial x-(a+1)y\partial/\partial y),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathop{\rm bideg}(\partial_{a,b})=(a,b),\quad a,b\geq 0.

These derivations verify the following
commutation relations:

(6)

[\partial_{k}^{+},\partial_{l}^{+}]=[\partial_{k}^{-},\partial_{l}^{-}]=0,\quad k.l\geq 0,

(7)

[\partial_{0}^{+},\partial_{0}^{-}]=0,\quad[\partial_{0}^{+},\partial_{m}^{-}]=m\partial_{m-1}^{-},\quad[\partial_{0}^{-},\partial_{n}^{+}]=n\partial_{n-1}^{+},\,\,\,m,n\geq 1,

(8)

[\partial_{n}^{+},\partial_{m}^{-}]=-\partial_{m-1,n-1},\quad m,n\geq 1,\quad[\partial_{a,b},\partial_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}]=\det\left(\begin{matrix}a^{\prime}+1&a+1\\
b^{\prime}+1&b+1\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{a+a^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}},

(9)

[\partial_{n}^{+},\partial_{a,b}]=\begin{cases}(n+b+1)\partial_{n+b}^{+},&a=0,\\
(a+1)\partial_{a-1,n+b},&a\geq 1,\end{cases}\quad[\partial_{m}^{-},\partial_{a,b}]=\begin{cases}-(m+a+1)\partial^{-}_{m+a},&b=0,\\
(b+1)\partial_{m+a,b-1},&b\geq 1.\end{cases}

4.3.3.
Borel subalgebras of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

Let us begin with the following remark.

Remark 4.7
.

Let us show that
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

{\mathfrak{g}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

{\mathfrak{f}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

(resp.
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

•

the metabelian (resp. abelian)

Using (

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm SAut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))={\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})=\langle{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}_{2},\,{\mathfrak{u}}^{-}_{2}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}},

Similar equalities hold for all

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
with

;
see, for example, the proof
of
[
28
, Lemma 2]
.
In the proof of Proposition
4.9
below
we use the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
A
be a diagonalizable
endomorphism of a vector space
V
.
Consider the decomposition

V=\oplus_{i}V_{i}
, where the
V_{i}
are
eigenspaces of
A

corresponding to different eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.
Let a subspace

U\subset V
be invariant under
A
, and let

u=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{m}\in U
, where
u_{i}\in V_{i}
.
Then
u_{i}\in U
for all
i
.

Proof.

By symmetry,
it suffices to show that
u_{1}\in U
.
The latter is obviously true if
u=u_{1}
.
Arguing by recursion, we can assume that
our claim is true for
u=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{k-1}
.
Now suppose that
u=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{k}
,
where
1<k\leq m
.
We have

U\ni Au-\lambda_{k}u=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})u_{j}.

By the inductive
hypothesis,
(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})u_{j}\in U

for
j=1,\ldots,k-1
, where

\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\neq 0
.
In particular,
u_{1}\in U
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

The subalgebra

{\mathfrak{j}}^{\pm}_{2}~=~\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm JONQ}^{\pm}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

is a Borel subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))
,
resp.

{\mathfrak{j}}_{2,0}^{\pm}~=~\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Jonq}^{\pm}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

is a Borel subalgebra of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))
.

Proof.

To prove that
{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}

is a Borel subalgebra,
it suffices to show that
any Lie subalgebra

{\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

properly containing
{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}

also contains a subalgebra
isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\Bbbk})
.
Therefore,
{\mathfrak{h}}
cannot be solvable.

Choose

v\in{\mathfrak{h}}\setminus{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
.
As a vector space,
{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
is generated
by its homogeneous elements.
Hence,
v
contains homogeneous
components that do not belong to
{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
.
We can assume that none of
the homogeneous
components of
v
belong to
{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
.

Since
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\subset{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
,
the semisimple derivations

A=\mathop{\rm ad}(x\partial/\partial x)
and
B=\mathop{\rm ad}(y\partial/\partial y)

from
\mathop{\rm End}(\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x,y]))
leave
{\mathfrak{h}}

invariant.
Let
v_{a,b}

be the homogeneous component of
v

of bidegree
(a,b)
.
Then
v_{a,b}
is an eigenvector
of
A

(resp. of
B
)
with eigenvalue
a
(resp.
b
).
Let us show that
v_{a,b}\in{\mathfrak{h}}\setminus{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
.

Note that

A|_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
is locally finite. Therefore,
v

belongs to a finite-dimensional

A
-invariant vector subspace

V\subset{\mathfrak{h}}
. Being semisimple,

A|_{V}
is diagonalizable. Hence,
A|_{V}

defines an eigenspaces
decomposition of
V
.
If
v=\sum_{(a,b)}v_{a,b}

is the usual weight decomposition,
then the
A
-homogeneous
components of
v
are of the form

v_{a}=\sum_{b}v_{a,b}
.
They belong to
V
due to
Lemma
4.8
.
The same argument applied to

B=\mathop{\rm ad}(y\partial/\partial y)\in\mathop{\rm End}(\mathop{\rm Der}({\Bbbk}[x,y]))

shows that the
B
-homogeneous
components
v_{a,b}
of
v_{a}

also belong to
V
.
Thus,
v_{a,b}\in{\mathfrak{h}}\setminus{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}

for all
(a,b)
.

Returning to the proof of the proposition,
it suffices to consider the following possibilities
for the bidegree
(a,b)
of
v_{a,b}
.

•

(a,b)=(a,-1)
, where
a\geq 1
.
We then have

v_{a,b}\in{\Bbbk}^{*}x^{a}\partial/\partial y\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial/\partial x)(v_{a,b})\in{\Bbbk}^{*}x^{a-1}\partial/\partial y.

Iterating, we conclude that
x\partial/\partial y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Since
y\partial/\partial x\in{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{2}
we obtain

{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\Bbbk})\simeq\langle x\partial/\partial y,\,y\partial/\partial x\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}.

•

a,b\geq 1
. In this case, up to rescaling,

v_{a,b}=x^{a}y^{b}((b+1)x\partial/\partial x-(a+1)y\partial/\partial y),\quad\text{where}\quad a,b\geq 1,

and

[\partial/\partial y,v_{a,b}]=(b+1)x^{a}y^{b-1}(bx\partial/\partial x-(a+1)y\partial/\partial y).

Iterating, we arrive at
the case where
b=1
.
In the next step, we obtain

[\partial/\partial y,x^{a}(2x\partial/\partial x-(a+1)y\partial/\partial y)]=-(a+1)x^{a}\partial/\partial y,

which brings us to the previous case.

•

(a,b)=(0,b)
, where
b\geq 1
.
Then

v_{a,b}=y^{b}((b+1)x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y)

and

[\partial/\partial y,v_{a,b}]=(b+1)y^{b-1}(bx\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y).

Iterating, we arrive at the case
where
b=1
, that is, up to rescaling,

v_{a,b}=y(2x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y).

We then have

{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(2,{\Bbbk})\simeq{\Bbbk}\partial/\partial y\oplus{\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y)\oplus{\Bbbk}(2xy\partial/\partial x-y^{2}\partial/\partial y)\subset{\mathfrak{h}}.

This proves the assertion for

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm JONQ}^{+}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))
;
the proof is similar in all remaining
cases.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Let us show that

We claim that

is also solvable.
Indeed, an easy
calculation shows
that

\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)({\Bbbk}[y,z]\partial/\partial x)\subset{\Bbbk}[y,z]\partial/\partial x,\quad\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)({\Bbbk}[z]\partial/\partial y)\subset{\Bbbk}[z]\partial/\partial y,

and

\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)({\Bbbk}\partial/\partial z)={\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y).

So,

(12)

\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)({\mathfrak{u}}^{+}_{3})\subset{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}_{3}\oplus{\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y).

Moreover,

\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)(x\partial/\partial x)=\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)(y\partial/\partial y)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)(z\partial/\partial z)=-\delta.

Therefore,
\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)({\mathfrak{t}}_{3})={\Bbbk}\delta
.
This shows that
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{3}\oplus{\Bbbk}\delta
.
It follows that

[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}]=[{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{3},{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{3}]\oplus[{\mathfrak{j}}^{+}_{3},\delta]\subset{\mathfrak{u}}^{+}_{3}\oplus{\Bbbk}(x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y)\oplus{\Bbbk}\delta.

Since
\mathop{\rm ad}(\delta)(x\partial/\partial x-y\partial/\partial y)=0

using (

.
Therefore, the Lie algebra

is solvable.
∎

u+v=(e^{\prime}-1,e-1)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{e^{\prime}-1,e-1}=-\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{+}_{e})(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}})\in\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}.

Since
ee^{\prime}=d+1
,
the vector
(a,b)=(e^{\prime}-1,e-1)

is a unique lattice vector in
\Lambda_{d,e}

satisfying the inequalities

(27)

a<e^{\prime},\quad b<e.

Consider the strictly positive,
integer valued function

f\colon\Lambda_{d,e}\to d{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\quad f(a,b)=(ae+b)+(a+be^{\prime})=(e+1)a+(e^{\prime}+1)b.

Suppose that (
27
)
fails for

(a,b)\in\Lambda_{d,e}
.
Let, for example,
a\geq e^{\prime}>1
,
and therefore

\rho:=\lfloor\frac{a}{e^{\prime}}\rfloor\geq 1
.
Consider the lattice vector

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a,b)-\rho v=(a-\rho e^{\prime},b+\rho)\in\Lambda_{d,e}.

Using (
9
)
we deduce

\partial_{a,b}=\beta(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}))^{\rho}(\partial_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}),\quad\text{where}\quad\beta=[(b+2)(b+3)\cdots(b+\rho+1)]^{-1}\neq 0,

and

f(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=f(a,b)-\rho d<f(a,b).

By symmetry, if
b\geq e>1
, then
letting
\eta=\lfloor\frac{b}{e}\rfloor\geq 1

and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a,b)-\eta u\in\Lambda_{d,e}

we obtain

\partial_{a,b}=\alpha(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{+}_{e}))^{\eta}(\partial_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}),\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha\neq 0,

and

f(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=f(a,b)-\eta d<f(a,b).

Thus, any
(a,b)\in\Lambda_{d,e}
distinct from

(e^{\prime}-1,e-1)=u+v
can be written in one of the forms

(a,b)=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})+\rho v\quad\text{or}\quad(a,b)=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})+\eta v,

where
\rho,\eta\geq 1
and
f(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})<f(a,b)
.
The minimum of
f
on
\Lambda_{d,e}

is equal to
2d
and is achieved at
a single point
u+v=(e^{\prime}-1,e-1)
.
Continuing in this way,
we finally achieve the point
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(e^{\prime}-1,e-1)
,
where the function
f
attains its minimum.

Summarising,
any
(a,b)\in\Lambda_{d,e}

can be written as

(a,b)=u+v+\rho_{1}v+\eta_{1}u+\cdots+\eta_{k-1}u+\rho_{k}v,\quad k\geq 0,

where

1+\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{k}=m\quad\text{and}\quad 1+\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{k-1}=n,

one of the
\rho_{1},\rho_{k}

or both can be zero, while the other
coefficients are positive,
and all partial sums

u+v,\quad u+v+\rho_{1}v,\quad u+v+\rho_{1}v++\eta_{1}u,\quad\ldots,\quad(a,b)

are lattice vectors from
\Lambda_{d,e}
.
This allows to represent
\partial_{a,b}
as follows:

(28)

\partial_{a,b}=c\,(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}))^{\rho_{k}}(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{+}_{e}))^{\eta_{k-1}}\cdots(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{+}_{e}))^{\eta_{1}}(\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}))^{\rho_{1}}{\mathop{\rm ad}}(\partial^{+}_{e})(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}})\in\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}},

where
c\in{\Bbbk}
is nonzero, see (
9
).
∎

Corollary 5.16
.

Under the assumption
ee^{\prime}=d+1

we have

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X_{d,e}))={\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\oplus\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}.

Proof.

According to (
28
),

\partial_{a,b}\in\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}
for all

(a,b)\in\Lambda_{d,e}
.
In particular, for all
k,l>0

we have

\partial_{0,kd},\partial_{ld,0}\in\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}
.
Moreover, by (
9
),

\partial^{+}_{e+kd}=(e+kd+1)^{-1}\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{+}_{e})(\partial_{0,kd})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}+ld}=-(e^{\prime}+ld+1)^{-1}\mathop{\rm ad}(\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}})(\partial_{ld,0})

also belong to

\langle\partial^{+}_{e},\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathop{\rm Lie}}
.
Now the claim follows.
∎

5.3.
The Lie algebra
of zero divergence vector fields
on
X_{d,e}
is not simple

Recall that the subalgebra
\mathop{\rm Vec}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

of divergence-free vector fields
is the unique proper ideal of the Lie algebra

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))=\mathop{\rm Vec}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
.
Moreover, the codimension one
subalgebra
\mathop{\rm Vec}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))

is simple,
see
[
28
, Lemma 3]
.

In the Lie algebra
\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{N}}_{d,e})
,
we consider the codimension
one ideal

{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}=\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{N}}_{d,e})\cap\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm SAut}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))=\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{N}}_{d,e})\cap{\mathop{\rm Vec}}^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}).

Since
\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X_{d,e}))

can be naturally identified
with
\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{N}}_{d,e})
, see
Corollary
5.9
,
we can consider
{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}

as a maximal ideal of

\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X_{d,e}))
.

Proposition 5.17
.

The Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}(\mathop{\rm Aut}(X_{d,e}))

is not simple.

Proof.

In the lattice

L_{d,e}=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\,|\,ae+b\equiv 0\mod d\}

we consider the submonoid

\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}=\{(a,b)\in L_{d,e}\,|\,a,b\geq-1,\quad(a,b)\neq(-1,-1)\}.

It properly contains the
submonoid
\Lambda_{d,e}

of (
22
). More precisely,

\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}=\Lambda_{d,e}\cup\{(-1,e+kd)\,|\,k\geq 0\}\cup\{(e^{\prime}+ld,-1)\,|\,l\geq 0\}\cup\{(0,0)\}.

We also consider the submonoid

\lambda_{d,e}:=\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}\setminus\{(-1,e),(e^{\prime},-1),(0,0)\}

and the homogeneous
subalgebra
I_{d,e}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathop{\rm Aut}}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))^{\mathop{\rm Ad}(G_{d,e})}

of codimension 3
supported by
\lambda_{d,e}
, where

I_{d,e}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}{\Bbbk}\partial^{+}_{e+kd}\oplus\bigoplus_{l\geq 1}{\Bbbk}\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}+ld}\oplus\bigoplus_{(a,b)\in\Lambda_{d,e}}{\Bbbk}\partial_{a,b},

cf. (
21
).
We claim that

I_{d,e}
is an ideal of

\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathop{\rm Aut}}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))^{\mathop{\rm Ad}(G_{d,e})}
.
Using formulas
(
8
) and (
9
),
it suffices to check the inclusion

(\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}+\lambda_{d,e})\cap\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}\subset\lambda_{d,e}.

Suppose this inclusion
is not true. Then
the left-hand side contains
one of the lattice vectors

(-1,e),\,(e^{\prime},-1)
and
(0,0)
.
Assuming that
(-1,e)=\hat{v}+v
,
where
\hat{v}\in\hat{\Lambda}_{d,e}

and
v\in\lambda_{d,e}
,
we obtain

(-1,e)=(-1,e+kd)+(0,ld)\quad\text{for some}\quad k,l>0,

which is impossible.
The same argument applies to
(e^{\prime},-1)
.
Similarly, if
(0,0)=\hat{v}+v
,
then either
\hat{v}=v=(0,0)
, or

\{\hat{v},v\}=\{(-1,1),\,(1,-1)\}
. But
this is impossible because

(0,0),\,(-1,1),\,(1,-1)\notin\lambda_{d,e}
.

Since
I_{d,e}

is a
homogeneous ideal
of codimension
3

of
\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathop{\rm Aut}}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))^{\mathop{\rm Ad}(G_{d,e})}
,
then also

I^{0}_{d,e}:=I_{d,e}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}

is a homogeneous ideal
of codimension
3

of the subalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}\subset\mathop{\rm Lie}({\mathop{\rm Aut}}_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}))^{\mathop{\rm Ad}(G_{d,e})}
.
Indeed, it is transversal to
the vector subspace

{\Bbbk}\partial^{+}_{e}\oplus{\Bbbk}\partial^{-}_{e^{\prime}}\oplus{\Bbbk}\partial_{0,0}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}.

Therefore,
the Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}_{d,e}
is not simple.
∎

Remarks 5.18

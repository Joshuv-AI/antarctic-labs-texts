Introduction

This paper is concerned with finite-time singularity formation of classical solutions to the following parabolic-parabolic-parabolic chemotaxis model (
1.1
) (
\tau=1
and
\varepsilon=1
) accounting for indirect signal production

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\

\tau w_{t}=\varepsilon\Delta w-w+u,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\

,

The system

[

(

The model

of pheromones mediated by nesting beetles,
where

See the recent review

).

Finite-time blowup & Lyapunov functional

The minimal chemotaxis model
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}
(
\tau=0
and
\varepsilon=0
) was originally proposed by Keller and Segel
[
17
]
to investigate the aggregation of cells. Besides random walk, migration of cells
u
is oriented along the gradient of chemical concentration
v
secreted by themselves. This self-organized system
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}

exhibits a competitive mechanism between diffusion and chemotaxis-driven aggregation.
Particularly, blowup—the extreme form of aggregation—may occur in finite time under certain conditions:

•

No solution of
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}
blows up in one dimension
[
31
]
.

•

In a planar domain, blowup may occur in finite or infinite time if the initial mass
m:=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}
exceeds the critical mass
4\pi
but not equals to an integer multiple of
4\pi

[
14
]
; all solutions remain globally bounded if
m<4\pi
(or
8\pi
with symmetry assumptions)
[
29
]
.
In a disk, there exist radially symmetric initial data with mass
m\in(8\pi,\infty)
such that the solutions blow up in finite time
[
28
]
.

•

In a ball of higher dimensions, for any prescribed initial mass
m>0
, there exist radially symmetric initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
with mass
m=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}
that lead to finite-time blowup
[
40
]
.

Due to strong coupling of fully parabolic-type cross-diffusion, detecting blowup tightly relies on the energy dissipation structure of
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{\tau,\varepsilon}
, i.e., the identity (cf.
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
19
]
and
[
9
, Proposition 6.1]
)

(1.2)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\tau,\varepsilon}=-\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\varepsilon},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max})

holds along the solution curve
\{(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t),\tau w(\cdot,t))\}
,
where the Lyapunov functional
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(t)=\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u,v,w):=\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u,v)
and its dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(t)=\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u,v)
are given as follows,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}u\ln u-\int_{\Omega}uv+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u\ln u-\int_{\Omega}uv+\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v-v+w|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\varepsilon\Delta v-v)(\Delta v-v),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla(\ln u-v)|^{2}+(\tau+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{t}|^{2}+(\tau+1)\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{2}.

Horstmann and Wang
[
14
]
proposed to link the initial energy
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})
and stationary energy
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
via dissipation.
By absurdum, finite- or infinite-time blowup enforced by low initial energy can be inferred under conditions that stationary energies have lower bounds, and that initial data with arbitrarily large negative energy can be constructed. This idea was applied in
[
14
,
38
]
for
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}
,
[
19
]
for
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{1,0}
and
[

,
27
]
for
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{1,1}
.

Concerning finite-time blowup, Winkler
[
40
]
originally paved the way to link the energy to its dissipation rate in a refined form

(1.4)

-\mathcal{F}_{0,0}\leq\int_{\Omega}uv+|\Omega|/e\leq C\mathcal{D}_{0,0}^{\theta}+C\quad\text{for some }C>0\text{ and }\theta\in(0,1)

for a large class of radially symmetric function pairs
(u,v)=(u(r),v(r))
, which, in particular, includes the radial trajectories of the system
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}

This result leads to a superlinear differential inequality for the quantity
-\mathcal{F}_{0,0}
by reversing the inequality (
1.4
) in case of low initial energy
\mathcal{F}_{0,0}(u_{0},v_{0})

which, in turn, implies occurrence of finite-time blowup.
Functional inequalities of the form (

,

,

,

Indirect chemotaxis

In the pioneer work
[
36
]
, Tao and Winkler pointed out that the indirect attractant production mechanism revealed in the Jäger-Luckhaus variant of the beetles model

\Delta v-\int_{\Omega}w/|\Omega|+w=0

8\pi

constructed the Lyapunov functional
\mathcal{F}_{1,0}

\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{1,0}

[

to an arbitrary domain

For fully parabolic indirect-chemotaxis models (
1.1

[

to
identify a four-dimensional critical mass

•

Under symmetry assumptions,

1.1
) under certain mixed boundary conditions.

•

For any

, there exist initial data satisfying

\int_{\Omega}u_{0}=m
such that
solutions of (

In five and higher dimensions, Mao and Li

Results on finite-time singularity formation of the system (

Main ideas and results

Even though the functional inequality of the form (
1.4
) has been applied to the Nagai-type variant
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,1}
,
it may not work for the fully parabolic version (
1.1
),
due to the problematic term
w_{t}
involving the so-called distributed delayed diffusion
[
34
]
. Technically, we cannot expect that
w_{t}
has certain uniform-in-time estimates even in
L^{1}
because of
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{\max});L^{1})
merely. On the other hand,
w_{t}
is unlike
v_{t}
that can be suitably controlled by quantities from either
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,\varepsilon}
or
\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\varepsilon}
.
More or less the memory effect
[
34
]
encourages us in construction of an integral-in-time inequality instead. Following the simple and far-reaching ideas of linking energy to its dissipation rate
[
40
]
, we show “memory” of the cross term
\int_{\Omega}uv
has an upper bound of a sublinear power of itself in the form of

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}uv\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C(1+s)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{D}_{1,1}(t)\;\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v-v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{2}+1\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle=C(1+s)\left(\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(0)+\int_{\Omega}uv-\int_{\Omega}u\ln u+1\right)^{\theta}

valid along the radial trajectories of the system (
1.1
),
where
\theta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
.
This inequality leads to a superlinear differential inequality for the function

\Psi(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}u(v-\ln u)\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(0)s+\ell s\quad\text{for }s\in(0,T_{\max})

with some large value
\ell>1
provided that
-\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(0)
is sufficiently large.
This, in turn, implies occurrence of finite-time blowup for initial data with low energy
\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(0)
.

To be precise, we shall suppose that initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
consist of nonnegative and radially symmetric functions satisfying

(1.6)

\begin{cases}0\leq u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad 0\leq v_{0},w_{0}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\text{with }\partial_{\nu}v_{0}=\partial_{\nu}w_{0}\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\\
\text{and }(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\text{ is a triplet of radially symmetric functions on }\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

with
\Omega=B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|x|<R\}
for some
R>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Our main result states that low initial energy enforces finite-time blowup.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\geq 5
and
R>0
.
Let
\tilde{m}>0
and
A>0
be given.
Then there exist positive constants
K(\tilde{m},A)
and
T(\tilde{m},A)
such that
if
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
from the set

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)

\displaystyle:=\{(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\mid u_{0},v_{0}\text{ and }v_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ comply with }\eqref{h: initial data},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\tilde{m}\text{ and }\|w_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}<A\},

satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})<-K(\tilde{m},A)
,
then the corresponding classical solution

2.1
blows up in finite time
T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\leq T(\tilde{m},A)
.
Here,
\mathcal{F}_{1,1}
is defined in (
1.3
).

Concerning the existence of initial data with low energy,
for any given nonnegative initial datum with suitable regularity,
we construct a family of initial data with arbitrarily large negative energy,
which can approximate the given initial datum in an appropriate topology.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some

and
R>0
.
For any triplet

of nonnegative and radially symmetric functions satisfying (

),
one can find a family

) for all

and

\inf_{(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)}\mathcal{F}_{1,1}(u,v,w)=-\infty

and

.

Remark 1.3
.

The inequality (
1.5
) may shed light on construction of certain integral inequality for the indirect chemotaxis of parabolic-parabolic-ODE type
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{1,0}
, where
w
and consequently

have much lower regularity. Notably, the functional inequality of the form (
1.4
) works for both classical Keller-Segel model
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}

[
40
]
and the Nagai-type variant
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,1}

[

]
, both of which have no temporal relaxation mechanism
\tau=0

The primary challenge lies in construction of the integral inequality (
1.5
) due to temporal-relaxation effect of indirect attractant mechanisms.
Mathematically, we shall obtain spatial-temporal estimates
\int_{0}^{s}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}t

in terms of both energy

and its dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}_{1,1}
, except the term

from

.
We are stimulated by
[

,
1
]
to introduce the Pohožaev-type test function

stands for the indicator function of a Borel set

.
Formally speaking, we implement integration by parts three times to transfer the operator
\partial_{t}-r^{1-n}(\partial_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}
from acting on

.

Moreover, for any
p\in[1,n/(n-2))
one can select

such that

(2.9)

-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)>-1.

L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates of the heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta}

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (cf.
[
24
, Lemma 3.1 and 3.2]
)
applied to
v_{x_{i}t}=\Delta v_{x_{i}}-v_{x_{i}}+w_{x_{i}}

subject to
v_{x_{i}}=v_{r}x_{i}/r\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
provide a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{x_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{0x_{i}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\right)e^{-(t-s)}\;\mathrm{d}s\sup_{s\in(0,t)}\|w_{x_{i}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}+CC_{q}(m+\|w_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)})\left(1+\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\right),

holds for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
,
p\in[1,n/(n-2))
and
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
where the Euler’s Gamma function
\Gamma
is well-defined due to the choices of parameters in (
2.9
). According to (
2.6
),
this implies that
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
,
as well as (
2.8
),
since
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
∎

Thanks to symmetry assumptions, uniform-in-time pointwise estimates for
v
and
w
can be deduced from Lemma

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with some

and

.
If

)

,

,

The weighted uniform estimates (

) and (

), since the Sobolev spaces of radial functions can be embedded into certain weighted Sobolev spaces

]
. Moreover, detailed proofs of (

3.
Construction of an integral inequality

The goal of this section is to establish the following integral inequality as aforementioned in the introduction.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\geq 5
and
R>0
. Assume
\tilde{m}>0
and
A>0
. Then there exists
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that for any
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
from the set
\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)
, the inequality

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}uv\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t\leqslant C(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{2}(1+s)\left(\mathcal{F}(0)+\int_{\Omega}uv-\int_{\Omega}u\ln u+1\right)^{\theta}

holds for all
s\in(0,T_{\max})
,
where
(u,v,w)
and
T_{\max}
are given as in Proposition
2.1
, and
\mathcal{F}(0):=\mathcal{F}(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
is defined in (
2.3
).

3.1.
Notations and setup

For convenience, we shall switch to the radial notation without any further comment, e.g., writing
v(r,t)
instead of
v(x,t)
when appropriate. We denote
\omega_{n}
the surface area of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote
C>0
the generic constant, which may vary from line to line.

From now on, we fix
n\geq 5
,
R>0
,
\kappa>n-2
,
\tilde{m}>0
and
A>0
.
Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
2.4
entails one can find a positive constant
C_{0}>0
with the following properties:
if initial datum
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
satisfies (
1.6
) such that

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}u_{0}=m<\tilde{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\|w_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}<A,

i.e.,
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)
,
then mass constraints

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}u+\int_{\Omega}v+\int_{\Omega}w<\tilde{m}+C_{0}A=:M\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max})

and pointwise estimates

(3.4)

\||x|^{\kappa}w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\||x|^{\kappa-1}v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}<C_{0}(\tilde{m}+A)=:B\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}),

as well as first-order estimates

(3.5)

\|v_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<C_{0}(\tilde{m}+A)=B\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max})

hold,
where the radial functions
(u,v,w)
and
T_{\max}=T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})
are given in Proposition
2.1
.

To construct the integral inequality (
3.1
),
we introduce the following quantities

(3.6)

f:=-\Delta v+v-w=-v_{t},\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T_{\max}),

and

(3.7)

g:=\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{u}}-\sqrt{u}\nabla v\right)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|},\quad x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\},\quad t\in(0,T_{\max}).

Then the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
and the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
are given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t)=\int_{\Omega}(u\log u-uv)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v-v|^{2}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+\left|f\right|^{2}\right)+\int_{\Omega}g^{2}.

3.2.
Interpolation inequalities for concentration
v

We shall collect uniform-in-time estimates for concentration
v
, as direct consequences or variants of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
30
]
, which will be frequently used.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)
.
Then there exists
C>0
such that

(3.9)

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{n/(n+4)}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}),

and

(3.10)

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq CM^{2/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{(n+2)/(n+4)}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}),

and

(3.11)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq CB^{4/(n+2)}\|f\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}),

as well as

(3.12)

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C(M^{2}+B^{2})\rho^{n-2\kappa}\quad\text{for all }(\rho,t)\in(0,R)\times(0,T_{\max}),

where the solution
(u,v,w)
of the system (
1.1
) and the number
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
are defined in Proposition
2.1
,
and
f=-v_{t}
is defined at (
3.6
).

Proof.

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
30
]
provides a constant
C_{gn}>0
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{gn}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{4/(n+2)}+C_{gn}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)

and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{gn}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{4/(n+2)}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)

as well as

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{gn}\|\Delta\varphi-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{n/(n+4)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{4/(n+4)}

holds for all
\varphi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
with
\partial\varphi/\partial\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Here, the elliptic
L^{2}
-regularity theory has been used,
i.e., there exists
C_{e}>0
such that

\|\varphi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{e}\|\Delta\varphi-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds for all
\varphi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
with
\partial\varphi/\partial\nu=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Therefore, (
3.9
) is a direct consequence of mass constraints (
3.3
) and (
3.15
).
Invoking the first-order estimate (
3.5
), we thus have (
3.11
) from (
3.14
).
Using (
3.13
) and mass constraints (
3.3
), we estimate by Young inequality

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{gn}M^{4/(n+2)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+C_{gn}M^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2^{n}C_{gn}^{(n+2)/2}M^{2}+C_{gn}M^{2}

for all
\rho\in(0,R)
and
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Pointwise estimates (
3.4
) entails

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+B^{2}\||x|^{1-\kappa}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\omega_{n}B^{2}R^{2}\rho^{n-2\kappa}

for all
\rho\in(0,R)
and
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Inserting (
3.17
) into (
3.16
) yields (
3.12
).
Integrating by parts, using Hölder inequality and (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta v+v)v\leq\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}

which verifies (
3.10
).
∎

3.3.
Spatial-temporal estimates of
uv

We incorporate decoupling techniques from Cao and Fuest
[
2
]
,
which assist us to convert the cross integral
\int_{\Omega}uv
into the
L^{2}_{\loc}
–estimate for the Laplacian of concentration
v
.
Notably,
we shall exhaust the contribution of the second-order terms
\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v-v|^{2}
and
\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{2}
in the energy functional
\mathcal{F}

to compensate terms related to
w_{t}
.
We remark that the mixed term
\int_{\Omega}uv
alone can be dominated by some power of the dissipation rate for the three- and higher-dimensional classical Keller-Segel model
\eqref{sys: ks isp pp/ep/e}_{0,0}

[
40
]
with the exception in two dimensions
[
28
]
, and even for the five- and higher-dimensional Nagai-type variant of indirect chemotaxis
[
26
]
.
Whether it can be explained as either technical flaw or aggregation-suppressing effect of indirect attractant production
[
16
,
36
]
, is unknown to us.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)
.
There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}uv\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{s}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}t+2\int_{0}^{s}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}+CM^{4/(n+4)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}

\displaystyle+CB(m+B)\rho^{n-2\kappa}s

holds for all
\rho\in(0,R)
and
s\in(0,T_{\max})
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in(0,R)
and
s\in(0,T_{\max})
.
We introduce a cut-off function defined as

\phi^{(\rho)}(x):=\phi(x/\rho),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\rho\in(0,R),

where
\phi(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a nonnegative and radially symmetric function such that

\phi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x\in B_{1/2},\\
0,&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}.\end{cases}

It follows that

\rho\|\nabla\phi^{(\rho)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\rho^{2}\|\Delta\phi^{(\rho)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\phi}:=\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for each
\rho\in(0,R)
.
Testing
u=w_{t}-\Delta w+w
by
v\phi^{(\rho)}
,
we compute upon integration by parts,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho/2}}uv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}uv\phi^{(\rho)}=\int_{\Omega}w_{t}v\phi^{(\rho)}-\int_{\Omega}\Delta wv\phi^{(\rho)}+\int_{\Omega}wv\phi^{(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(wv)_{t}\phi^{(\rho)}-\int_{\Omega}wv_{t}\phi^{(\rho)}+\int_{\Omega}w(-\Delta(v\phi^{(\rho)})+v\phi^{(\rho)})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(wv)_{t}\phi^{(\rho)}+\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta v+v-v_{t})w\phi^{(\rho)}-\int_{\Omega}w(2\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi^{(\rho)}+v\Delta\phi^{(\rho)})

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Recalling the notation
w=-\Delta v+v-f
specified in (
3.6
),
we estimate

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta v+v-v_{t})w\phi^{(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta v+v+f)(-\Delta v+v-f)\phi^{(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta v-v)^{2}\phi^{(\rho)}-\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\phi^{(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+2\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Using the pointwise information from (
3.4
),
we estimate

(3.21)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}w(2\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi^{(\rho)}+v\Delta\phi^{(\rho)})

\displaystyle\leq 2B^{2}C_{\phi}\int_{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{\rho/2}}|x|^{-\kappa}(|x|^{1-\kappa}|x|^{-1}+|x|^{2-\kappa}|x|^{-2})

\displaystyle=4B^{2}C_{\phi}\omega_{n}\int_{\rho/2}^{\rho}r^{n-1-2\kappa}\;\mathrm{d}r\leq 2^{2\kappa-n+2}\omega_{n}B^{2}C_{\phi}\rho^{n-2\kappa}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
,
and

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\backslash B_{\rho/2}}uv

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in\left(\rho/2,R\right)}v(r)\cdot\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\rho/2}}u

\displaystyle\leq B\sup_{r\in\left(\rho/2,R\right)}r^{2-\kappa}\cdot\int_{\Omega}u=2^{\kappa-2}Bm\rho^{2-\kappa}\leq 2^{\kappa-2}BmR^{\kappa+2-n}\rho^{n-2\kappa}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Invoking (
3.9
), we have by Young inequality

(3.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}wv\phi^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta v+v-f)v\phi^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}+CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{n/(n+4)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{4/(n+4)}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}+CM^{4/(n+4)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2(n+2)/(n+4)}

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Summing (
3.19
)–(
3.22
) and integrating over
(0,s)\subset(0,T_{\max})
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}uv\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}wv\phi^{\rho}-\int_{\Omega}w_{0}v_{0}\phi^{\rho}+2\int_{0}^{s}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}t+2\int_{0}^{s}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2^{2\kappa-n+2}\omega_{n}B^{2}C_{\phi}\rho^{n-2\kappa}s+2^{\kappa-2}BmR^{\kappa+2-n}\rho^{n-2\kappa}s

which reduces to (
3.18
) by inserting (
3.23
).
∎

3.4.
Spatial-temporal estimates of
\Delta v

This subsection is devoted to estimates of the quantity
\int_{0}^{s}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s
through a combination of terms from both the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
and the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
.

We begin with an employment of the Pohožaev-type test function
|x|^{n-2}x\cdot\nabla v\mathds{1}_{B_{\eta}}
with
\eta\in(0,R)
,
initially introduced in the seminal works of Winkler
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
.
The first crucial aspect of this procedure lies in exploiting
the structural information (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}(\tilde{m},A)
.
Denote
\alpha=2(n-1)
. Then

(3.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}y^{2}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}+w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}y^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}(wv_{r})_{t}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\omega_{n}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}+2\omega_{n}^{-1}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}

\displaystyle+\omega_{n}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}+\omega_{n}^{-1}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\eta})}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}f^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{\alpha}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}ur^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{2}f^{2}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}-u(\eta)\eta^{n-1}

for all
\eta\in(0,R)
and
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Here, abbreviate

y:=\Delta v=v_{rr}+\frac{n-1}{r}v_{r}=r^{1-n}(v_{r}r^{n-1})_{r},\quad(r,t)\in(0,R)\times(0,T_{\max})

for brevity.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in(0,R)
and
t\in(0,T_{\max})
.
Multiplying
w_{t}-u+w=\Delta w
by
|x|^{n-2}x\cdot\nabla v

and integrating over
B_{\eta}\subset\Omega
,
we get

(3.25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{t}v_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{\eta}uv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\eta}wv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta}(w_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}v_{r}r^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}r.

Using the identity
w_{t}v_{r}=(wv_{r})_{t}-wv_{rt}
and the notation
f=-v_{t}
, we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{t}v_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta}(wv_{r})_{t}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\eta}wf_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r.

Thank to
uv_{r}=u_{r}-g\sqrt{u}
as given by (
3.7
),
we compute the second term on the left of (
3.25
)

(3.27)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\eta}uv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\eta}(u_{r}-g\sqrt{u})r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-u(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}+\alpha\int_{0}^{\eta}ur^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\eta}g\sqrt{u}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r.

Integration by parts yields

(3.28)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}(w_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}v_{r}r^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{r}r^{n-1}(v_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{r}yr^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r.

Using
w=-y+v-f
,
we evaluate the last term on the left of (
3.25
)

(3.29)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}wv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta}(-y+v-f)v_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\eta}(v_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}v_{r}r^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\eta}(v^{2})_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{\eta}fv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}v_{r}^{2}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}-(n-1)\int_{0}^{\eta}v^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{\eta}fv_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

and the last term on the right of (
3.26
)

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}wf_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\eta}yf_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\eta}vf_{r}r^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}f^{2}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}+(n-1)\int_{0}^{\eta}f^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r.

Using
w_{r}=-y_{r}+v_{r}-f_{r}
, we calculate

(3.31)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{r}yr^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\eta}(-y_{r}+v_{r}-f_{r})yr^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r

and in particular,
the function

\Psi(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}u(v-\ln u)\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{F}(0)s+\ell s\quad\text{for }s\in(0,T_{\max})

is well-defined with

(4.5)

\Psi(T(\tilde{m},A))<\infty,

according to Proposition
2.1
.
Clearly,
\Psi\in C^{0}([0,T_{\max}))\cap C^{1}((0,T_{\max}))
is strictly increasing,
since the dissipation of energy (
2.2
) implies

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}uv-\int_{\Omega}u\ln u+\mathcal{F}(0)+\ell

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{F}(0)-\mathcal{F}(s)+\ell\geq\ell\quad\text{for }s\in(0,T_{\max})

and particularly

(4.7)

\Psi(s)\geq\ell s\quad\text{for }s\in(0,T_{\max}).

Using (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we arrive at

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle\leq C(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{2}(1+s)\left(\mathcal{F}(0)+\int_{\Omega}uv-\int_{\Omega}u\ln u+1\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle+(\mathcal{F}(0)+|\Omega|/e+\ell)s

\displaystyle\leq C(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{2}(1+s)\Psi^{\prime\theta}(s)\quad\text{for }s\in(0,T_{\max}),

where we also use the elementary inequality
\xi\ln\xi\geq-1/e
valid for all
\xi>0
.
The positivity (
4.6
) of
\Psi^{\prime}
and the pointwise lower estimates (
4.7
) allows us to invert the inequality (
4.8
),
and conclude that the mapping
[1,T(\tilde{m},A))\ni s\mapsto\Psi(s)\in[\ell,\infty)
is a supersolution of
the nonautonomous ordinary differential equation with superlinear growth

(4.9)

\begin{cases}\Phi^{\prime}=C^{-1/\theta}(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{-2/\theta}(1+s)^{-1/\theta}\Phi^{1/\theta},&s>1,\\
\Phi(1)=\ell.\end{cases}

The function

\displaystyle\Phi(s)=\Big(\ell^{\frac{\theta-1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle-2^{\frac{\theta-1}{\theta}}C^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{-\frac{2}{\theta}}

\displaystyle+(1+s)^{\frac{\theta-1}{\theta}}C^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}(\tilde{m}+A+1)^{-\frac{2}{\theta}}\Big)^{\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}}\quad\text{for }s\in[1,T(\tilde{m},A)),

solves (
4.9
)
with the property that

(4.10)

\Phi(s)\nearrow\infty,\quad\text{as }s\nearrow T(\tilde{m},A).

While a direct comparison argument ensures

\Psi(s)\geq\Phi(s)\quad\text{for }s\in(1,T(\tilde{m},A)),

which is absurd in view of (
4.5
) and (
4.10
).
∎

5.
Initial data with large negative energy

This section is devoted to the existence of initial data with low energy.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radially symmetric and nonnegative function,
which is compactly supported in
B_{1}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi=1
.
Fixing
\gamma>0
and
\eta_{\star}=\min\{1/2,R\}
,
we define for
\eta\in(0,1)
,

u_{\eta}(x):=u_{0}(x)+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

and

v_{\eta}(x):=v_{0}(x)+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\gamma}\eta^{2-\frac{n}{2}}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

as well as

w_{\eta}(x)=w_{0}(x)+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\gamma}\eta^{2-\frac{n}{2}}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}.

It is clear that
(u_{\eta},v_{\eta},w_{\eta})
complies with (
1.6
) for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star})
.
Since

\|v_{\eta}-v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}=\|w_{\eta}-w_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\gamma}\|\phi\|_{W^{2,2}(B_{1})}

are valid for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star})
,

\gamma>0
ensures (
1.9
).
A direct computation can verify

\|u_{\eta}-u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{-2-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{p}}\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\quad\text{for all }\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star})

and (
1.8
) follows due to
-2-n/2+n/p>0
and
n\geq 5
.
To verify (
1.10
),
it remains to evaluate
-\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}v_{\eta}
from
\mathcal{F}
as follows,

(5.1)

\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}v_{\eta}\geq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right)\eta^{-n}\;\mathrm{d}x=\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star}),

since (
1.8

\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}\ln u_{\eta}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v_{\eta}-v_{\eta}+w_{\eta}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v_{\eta}-v_{\eta}|^{2}

from

\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star})
.
In view of

,
we have
\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}v_{\eta}\to\infty
as
\eta\searrow 0
according to (
5.1
) and finish the proof.
∎

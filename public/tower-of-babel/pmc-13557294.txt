Palmoplantar Pustulosis Induced by Intravenous Anifrolumab: An Emerging Dermatologic Adverse Event?
Authors: Silvia Zanghì, Stefano Bighetti, Luca Bettolini, Iacopo Ghini, Mariateresa Rossi, Vincenzo Maione
Published: 2026

Introduction

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a chronic autoimmune disease characterized by heterogeneous cutaneous and systemic manifestations, with skin involvement reported in up to 85% of patients [1]. Anifrolumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting the type I interferon receptor, represents a novel therapeutic option for moderate-to-severe SLE. In pivotal phase 3 trials, the most common adverse events were infections, while cutaneous immune-mediated reactions were not reported [2]. Palmoplantar pustulosis (PPP) is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis characterized by recurrent, painful, sterile pustules on the palms and soles. Although once considered a variant of psoriasis, increasing evidence supports PPP as a distinct entity, with its immunopathogenesis driven by Th17 and Th2 pathways [3,4]. We describe a case of PPP arising in temporal association with anifrolumab therapy for SLE.

Case Presentation

A 40-year-old female with a long-standing history of SLE presented with refractory cutaneous involvement, including discoid lesions on the face, scalp, trunk, and oral mucosa. Treatment with hydroxychloroquine led to partial control of disease activity, but she subsequently developed a flare characterized by mucosal erosions, alopecia, reduced C3 levels, and a cutaneous rash, corresponding to a SLEDAI-2K score of 6. Given persistent disease activity, anifrolumab therapy was initiated at a dose of 300 mg intravenously every four weeks.

After two infusions, significant improvement in lupusrelated cutaneous lesions was observed. However, she developed new-onset pruritic pustular eruptions confined to the plantar surfaces, which recurred in a stereotypical pattern following each infusion. Clinical examination revealed erythematous plaques with multiple sterile pustules symmetrically distributed over the medial and central plantar regions (Figure 1A). Dermoscopy highlighted numerous yellowish globules corresponding to pustules, with superficial scaling and absence of vascular structures (Figure 1B).

A comprehensive fungal evaluation was negative. Histopathological analysis of a punch biopsy showed marked acanthosis with psoriasiform features, intraepidermal subcorneal vesiculopustules, irregular granular layer, compact parakeratosis, and a moderately dense mononuclear inflammatory infiltrate in the papillary dermis, oriented around elongated capillaries (Figure 1C). No fungal element was identified on PAS staining. The patient had no personal or family history of psoriasis or PPP.

Causality assessment using the Naranjo algorithm yielded a score of 7, supporting a probable association between anifrolumab and PPP onset [5] (Table 1). Despite this adverse event, the patient achieved substantial systemic and cutaneous benefit from anifrolumab, leading to continuation of therapy. The pustular eruption was successfully managed with high-potency topical corticosteroids, resulting in partial remission between infusions.

Conclusion

This case suggests that PPP may represent a novel paradoxical dermatological adverse event of anifrolumab. The immunopathogenesis of PPP involves complex interplay between Th17 and Th2 cytokine networks; antagonism of type I interferon signaling may modify cytokine plasticity in a way that predisposes to pustular inflammation [4]. Although not reported in clinical trials [2], rare cutaneous immune-mediated events may emerge in real-world settings, as has been described with other biologics. For example, paradoxical psoriasis has been documented in patients treated with TNF-α inhibitors [6], supporting the concept that immune deviation induced by targeted therapies may trigger new inflammatory dermatoses.

Clinicians should remain vigilant for unexpected cutaneous reactions in patients receiving interferon-targeted therapies. Recognition of PPP as a potential adverse event may inform management decisions, ensuring that effective systemic control of SLE is maintained while local manifestations are addressed with adjunctive therapies.

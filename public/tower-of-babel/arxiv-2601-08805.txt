Quantum Heegaard diagrams and knot Floer Homology
Cristina Anghel, András Juhász

Abstract
. Given a knot presented as a braid closure, we construct a unified intersection model for the Alexander and Jones polynomials of the knot via what we call quantum Heegaard diagrams. These diagrams are obtained by stabilising the disc model of the first author, which we show are doubly-pointed Heegaard diagrams of the knot together with an additional set of base points. We identify the Alexander grading in the disc model with the Alexander grading in the Heegaard diagram. As the Lagrangian intersection Floer homology of the Heegaard tori in the symmetric power of the Heegaard surface is knot Floer homology, we can view knot Floer homology as a natural categorification of the Alexander polynomial arising from the disc model. The additional base points let us define a new grading on the intersection between the Heegaard tori, which we call quantum Alexander grading. Combining this with the classical Alexander grading, we define a two-variable graded intersection between the Heegaard tori that recovers the Jones and Alexander polynomials as two specialisations of coefficients. The resulting intersection formula for the Jones polynomial opens up a potential avenue to obtaining a new geometric categorification of the Jones polynomial.

1.
Introduction

The Alexander and Jones polynomials are two important invariants in knot theory that have different natures. The Alexander polynomial is genuinely geometric, arising from knot complements, whereas the geometry of the Jones polynomial is a fundamental open problem in quantum topology. Categorifications of knot polynomials have become powerful tools in knot theory. Khovanov homology was the first categorification of the Jones polynomial, defined via combinatorial tools. Seidel and Smith
[
17
]
and Manolescu
[
13
]
have constructed symplectic versions of Khovanov homology via Hilbert schemes. Ozsváth and Szabó
[
15
]
, and independently Rasmussen
[
16
]
, have defined knot Floer homology, a categorification of the Alexander polynomial using Heegaard diagrams. Rasmussen
[
16
]
conjectured that there is a spectral sequence from Khovanov homology to knot Floer homology, which was later constructed by Dowlin
[
9
]
. There are still important open questions about geometric categorifications of the Jones polynomial. Aganagić
[
2
]
[
1
]
proposed such a categorification using immersed curves, predicting the Lagrangians via mirror symmetry, and this was developed by LePage and Shende
[
10
]
. It is an open question to provide a concrete, purely geometric categorification of the Jones polynomial with embedded Lagrangian submanifolds.

Moreover, it would be interesting to construct a geometric spectral sequence between such categorifications of the Jones and Alexander polynomials.

1.1.
Main results.

Our motivation was to find Floer-theoretic categorifications of the Alexander and Jones polynomials using a common geometric viewpoint. This work makes progress towards this goal. We introduce a type of multi-pointed Heegaard diagram associated to a braid closure that we call
quantum Heegaard diagram
. We construct this as a stabilisation of the disc model associated to the braid by the first author
[
6
]
[
8
]
in earlier work. We show that, if we forget the additional base points, the quantum Heegaard diagram is a Heegaard diagram for the braid closure. Furthermore, we identify the Alexander grading on the disc model with the one on the Heegaard diagram. We define a
quantum Alexander grading
on the intersection of the Heegaard tori using the extra base points. We associate a two-variable polynomial to the intersection that recovers both the Alexander and the Jones polynomials as specialisations of coefficients. This provides an intersection model for the Jones polynomial using quantum Heegaard diagrams and the classical and quantum Alexander gradings. It is yet to be seen if this leads to a Floer-theoretic categorification of the Jones polynomial.

1.2.
Topological models in the disc

The first author
[
6
]
[
8
]
provided a topological model that recovers both the Jones and the Alexander polynomial as specialisations of a graded intersection between two Lagrangians in a configuration space; see Section
2
. The geometric supports of these Lagrangians are arcs and simple closed curves in the punctured disc. This is a geometric framework that allows us to see both the Jones and the Alexander polynomial from a unified point of view given by a common setup in a configuration space.

A consequence of our main results is that the topological model for the Alexander polynomial in the configuration space of the punctured disc admits a natural categorification, which we identify as knot Floer homology. We do so in two steps. First, from the intersection model in the configuration space associated to a braid, we construct a Heegaard diagram for the complement of the knot that is obtained as the closure of the braid. We do this by adding tubes to the punctured disc at the ends of the arcs and completing the arcs to simple closed curve. Secondly, we consider the knot Floer homology corresponding to this Heegaard diagram and show that the Alexander grading is precisely the local system grading that we have in the punctured disc.

3.1.
Heegaard diagrams for knots

Heegaard diagrams are a useful tool for encoding

We first review Heegaard diagrams for knots and then show how to construct such a diagram from a collections of arcs and simple closed curves in the punctured disc.

Definition 3.1

(Heegaard diagrams)
.

A

is a tuple

\mathscr{H}=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}),

where

•

\Sigma

•

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{g}\}

•

and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
generate a

Given a Heegaard diagram, one can obtain a closed, connected, and oriented

-manifold

along
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\times\{0\}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}\times\{1\}
and attaching two
3

as the union of two handlebodies, which we call the

and

Definition 3.2

(Doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for a knot)
.

Let

be a knot. Then
\mathscr{H}=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},w,z)
is a
doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for
K
if
\mathscr{H}=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})
is a Heegaard diagram for
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the points

, and the knot

can be obtained by connecting

by an arc in
\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and pushing it into the
\alpha

to

by an arc in
\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol{\beta}
and pushing the resulting arc into the
\beta
handlebody.

Suppose that
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},w,z)
is a doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for the knot

. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\setminus N(w\cup z)
. If we attach 3-dimensional 2-handles to

along
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\times\{0\}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}\times\{1\}

4.
Alexander-type gradings via the symmetric power of the disc

We now change the context from the disc model. We add handles and work in the symmetric power of the resulting surface
\Sigma
. This provides the setting for knot Floer homology. We aim to identify two different gradings and conclude that the graded model for the Alexander polynomial in the configuration space of the disc admits a natural categorification constructed via the associated Heegaard surface which is isomorphic to knot Floer homology.

4.1.
Whitney discs and their domains

Heegaard Floer homology of closed 3-manifolds was defined by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
15
]
, extended to knots by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
14
]
and independently by Rasmussen
[
16
]
, and to sutured manifolds by the second author
[
11
]
. Let

\mathscr{H}=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g}\},\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{g}\})

where
\Sigma
is a surface of genus
g
. Then
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}:=\alpha_{1}\times\dots\times\alpha_{g}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\beta}:=\beta_{1}\times\dots\times\beta_{g}
are Lagrangian tori in
\text{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)
for a suitable choice of symplectic structure. If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
intersect transversely, then so do
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
. Let

I_{\Sigma}:=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta};

this is a set of generators of Heegaard Floer homology.

Definition 4.1

(Whitney disc)
.

For
\bar{x}
,
\bar{y}\in I_{\Sigma}
,
a
topological Whitney disc
from
\bar{x}
to
\bar{y}
is a continuous map

u\colon\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\text{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)

such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle u(-i)=\bar{x};\ u(i)=\bar{y};

\displaystyle u(\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}_{-})\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\alpha};\ u(\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}_{+})\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\beta}.

Here,
\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}_{+}=S^{1}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Re}(z)\geq 0\}
and
u(\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}_{-})=S^{1}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Re}(z)\leq 0\}
.

Definition 4.2

(Domain)
.

The connected components of
\Sigma\setminus(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cup\boldsymbol{\beta})
are called
regions
. A domain
D
is a linear combination of regions over
\mathbb{Z}
and form an abelian group that we denote by
D(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})
.

Definition 4.3
.

For
p\in\Sigma\setminus(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cup\boldsymbol{\beta})
, let

\displaystyle V_{p}:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})\in\text{Sym}^{n-1}(\Sigma)\mid\exists\ 1\leq i\leq n-1:x_{i}=z\}.

This is a complex hypersurface in
\text{Sym}^{n-1}(\Sigma)
.

Following Ozsváth and Szabó
[
14
]
[
15
]
there is a correspondence between topological Whitney discs and domains.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
u
be a Whitney disc. Then, for
p\in\Sigma\setminus(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cup\boldsymbol{\beta})
, the
multiplicity of
u
at
p
is

n_{p}(u):=u\cap V_{p}.

Let
R_{1},\dots,R_{d}
be the regions and choose a point
p_{i}\in R_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. The
domain of
u
is

\mathcal{D}(u):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{p_{i}}(u)\cdot R_{i}.

Definition 4.5
.

Given
\bar{x}
,
\bar{y}\in I_{\Sigma}
, we say that the domain
\mathcal{D}\in D(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})

connects
\bar{x}
to
\bar{y}
if
\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a 1-chain with boundary
\bar{x}-\bar{y}
and
\partial\mathcal{D}\cap\boldsymbol{\beta}
is a 1-chain with boundary
\bar{y}-\bar{x}
. We denote the set of domains from
\bar{x}
to
\bar{y}
by

.

Let

to

Recall that we have Lagrangian submanifolds

This comes from the fact that, when we close up the arcs

.

(Intersection points)

From now on, we will denote this set of intersection points as

We now recall the Alexander grading define using the punctured disc model from Corollary

(Alexander grading in the disc model)

For an intersection point

For

be the point of

5.
Knot Floer homology

Knot Floer homology is bigraded by the Alexander grading (Definition
4.6
) and the Maslov (homological) grading. For
\bar{x}
,
\bar{y}\in I
, we denote by
\pi_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
the set of relative homology classes of Whitney discs from
\bar{x}
to
\bar{y}
. To define the Maslov index, fix an almost complex structure
J
on
\text{Sym}^{n-1}(\Sigma)
satisfying the conditions described by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
14
]
[
15
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

For
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, let
\mathscr{M}(\phi)
be the moduli space of pseudo-homolomorphic representatives of
\phi
. For a generic choice of almost complex structure
J
, the space
\mathscr{M}(\phi)
is a smooth manifold and its dimension is called the Maslov index, denoted by
\mu(\phi)
.

A combinatorial formula for the Maslov index was given by Lipshitz
[
12
]
.
The relative Maslov grading
M\colon I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
satisfies the formula

(5.1)

M(\bar{x})-M(\bar{y})=\mu(\phi)-2n_{w}(\phi),

for
\bar{x}
,
\bar{y}\in I
and
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
The pair
(M,A^{\text{HF}})
is a bigrading on the set of intersection points
I
.

The chain complex
\widehat{\text{CFK}}(\mathscr{H})
is the free abelian group generated by
I
. Ozsváth–Szabó and Rasmussen showed that this complex admits a differential
\delta
such that

•

\delta\circ\delta=0
,

•

\delta
preserves the Alexander grading,

•

\delta
decreases the Maslov grading by one.

Theorem 5.2

(Knot Floer homology (Ozsváth–Szabó
[
15
]
, Rasmussen
[
16
]
))
.

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
and
\mathcal{H}
a doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for
K
. We write
\widehat{\text{HFK}}(\mathscr{H})
for the homology of the complex
\widehat{\text{CFK}}(\mathscr{H})
. Then, the bigrading on the complex descends to a bigrading on the homology:

\widehat{\text{HFK}}(\mathcal{H})=\bigoplus_{(M,A^{\text{HF}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\text{HFK}}_{M,A^{\text{HF}}}(\mathcal{H})

that is an invariant of
K
up to bigrading-preserving isomorphism.
This is a categorification of the Alexander polynomial in the sense that

(5.2)

\Delta_{K}(x)=\sum_{(M,A^{\text{HF}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(-1)^{M}\text{rank}\left(\widehat{\text{HFK}}_{M,A^{\text{HF}}}(K)\right)\cdot x^{A^{\text{HF}}}.

In light of Theorem
5.2
, we write
\widehat{\text{HFK}}(K)

The role of the punctures in the definition of the local system is that they provide a grading in two variables

8.
Example: The right-handed trefoil knot

In this section, we compute the quantum Lagrangian intersection associated to the braid
\sigma^{3}\in B_{2}
. We will compute its specialisations and check that this leads to the Alexander polynomial and the knot Floer homology of the right-handed trefoil
T
, which is the braid closure of
\sigma^{3}
. Taking the other specialisation, we will obtain the Jones polynomial of the trefoil.

We start with the disc
D^{2}
with four
p
-punctures and two
q
-punctures.
Then we consider one arc
a_{1}
and one simple closed curve
b_{1}
. After applying the braid action, we obtain the Lagrangian submanifolds
(\sigma^{3}\cup\mathbb{I})\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{T}
presented in Figure
8.1
.

Then,
(\sigma^{3}\cup\mathbb{I})\mathscr{S}\cap\mathscr{T}
has five intersection points, labeled on the right-hand side of Figure
8.1
by their gradings provided by the quantum Alexander grading. This gives us the quantum Lagrangian intersection model, which has the following form:

(8.1)

\Omega^{q}(\sigma^{3})(x,d)=x^{2}d^{3}\left(-x^{-3}+x^{-2}-x^{-1}+1-d\right).

Upgrading the feeding value of oil palm fronds for ruminants through processing: Evidence from a meta-analysis of in vitro and in vivo studies
Authors: Mardiah Rahmadani, Fenda Alvionita Fhonna, Irwan Susanto, Hajrian Rizqi Albarki, Dessy Permata, Raiza Tri Pangesti, Eko Lela Fitriana, Anuraga Jayanegara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOil palm fronds (OPF) have potential as a ruminant feed; however, their utilization is limited by low nutritional quality and digestibility. Various processing methods, including biological, chemical, physical, and their combinations, have been applied to improve the feeding value of OPF. This meta-analysis aimed to integrate findings from multiple studies to evaluate the effects of processing on OPF feeding value. A total of 25 relevant articles retrieved from Scopus were analysed to quantify the effects of OPF processing on nutrient composition, rumen fermentability (in vitro and in vivo), degradability (in vitro and in vivo), and animal performance. The results showed that processing improved OPF quality by increasing crude protein (CP) content and reducing lignin (P < 0.05). In vitro and in vivo evaluations further showed improvements (P < 0.05) in various parameters of rumen fermentability and nutrient degradability without adverse effects on animal performance. Furthermore, chemical, physical, and biological processing effectively enhanced OPF fermentability, with chemical processing being the most effective in reducing lignin content. In conclusion, appropriate processing strategies can substantially improve OPF utilization as a sustainable ruminant feed resource.Keywords: Lignocellulosic biomass, Pretreatment methods, Lignin degradation, Rumen fermentation, Nutrient utilization

Introduction

Elaeis guineensis Jacq., often known as oil palm, is the most prolific edible oil crop globally. It is planted throughout 42 nations on 11 million hectares worldwide (Abdul Khalil et al., 2008; Shinoj et al., 2011). West Africa, Southeast Asian nations such as Malaysia and Indonesia, Latin American countries, and India are the principal cultivators of oil palm. One hectare of oil palm plantation generates around 55 tons of dry matter as fibrous biomass and yields 5.5 tons of oil yearly (Joseph et al., 2006; Shinoj et al., 2011). Substantial volumes of oil palm biomass are produced, since the oil constitutes just around 10% from the entire biomass. One major by-product is oil palm fronds (OPF), but its utilization remains limited (Ooi et al., 2017).

Research is needed to explore OPF utilization across multiple sectors and to develop value-added products such as animal feed, building materials, fillers for polymer composites, renewable energy, and bioethanol (Ooi et al., 2017). OPF contains approximately 38.5% crude fiber and has metabolizable energy (ME) values of about 5.65 MJ/kg dry matter (Wan Zahari et al., 2003). Furthermore, OPF also contains approximately 65 g/kg CP (Islam et al., 2000). In ruminant feeding, OPF has been fed to animals as a source of roughage to partially replace rice straw and other low-quality roughages without detrimental effects on livestock production. However, the use of OPF as a sole or major roughage is limited by its high fiber and lignocellulosic fraction, which can reduce digestibility and voluntary feed intake. This condition may impair rumen microbial activity due to the limited availability of fermentable substrates, leading to reduced volatile fatty acids (VFA) production and microbial protein synthesis (Saminathan et al., 2021). Moreover, it may lead to insufficient energy and protein supply for optimal production. OPF can be upgraded by supplementing it with an energy-dense feed compound or can be subjected to further processing to increase its feeding value (Ghani et al., 2017).

Processing of OPF for animal feed includes pretreatment methods such as physical, chemical, biological, or a combination of these. Physical pretreatments involve size reduction processes such as chopping, milling, extrusion, and microwave irradiation, while chemical methods include the use of alkali or acid to disrupt lignocellulosic structures. Biological treatments utilize fungi, bacteria, probiotics, and enzymes to degrade complex fiber components (Kusmiati et al., 2024; Saminathan et al., 2021). These methods aim to reduce lignocellulose content and improve nutritional quality, addressing challenges in feed digestion and protein content (Kusmiati et al., 2024). However, these pretreatments incur additional costs and should be carefully considered for effective utilization in animal diets. The fermentation of OPF presents a promising avenue for enhancing animal feed quality, particularly for ruminants. This is because it can help break down lignin and cellulose, improving protein and fiber content, and can further reduce crude fiber and ash content, enhancing feed quality (Imsya & Riswandi, 2022; Kusmiati et al., 2024; Pratama et al., 2023). Additionally, this may improve rumen fermentation characteristics, degradability, and animal performance.

Although a number of chemical, biological, and physical treatments have been applied to OPF, however, there is to date no quantitative study attempts to synthesize the findings systematically and quantitatively. Meta-analysis is a statistical analysis method that integrates evidence from multiple studies addressing the same hypothesis, enhancing the efficiency of results and providing consensus insights. It serves as a powerful tool for synthesizing research findings across various scientific fields (Patial & Sobti, 2024). Therefore, this meta-analysis aimed to quantitatively synthesize evidence on how processing of OPF influences their feeding value for ruminants by assessing effects on nutrient composition, rumen fermentability and degradability (in vitro and in vivo), and animal performance.

Materials and methods

Database development

The database was developed following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A systematic and comprehensive literature search was conducted in the Scopus database in January 2026 using the keywords “oil palm fronds” and “rumen”. The database was developed by curating articles in reverse chronological order, beginning with the most recent publications and proceeding to earlier ones based on the listing provided in Scopus. Scopus was selected as the primary data source due to its broad and well-curated journal coverage, high data quality and consistency, strong relevance to the study objectives, and practical feasibility. In addition, the use of a single comprehensive database was intended to reduce the risk of article duplication that commonly arises when integrating multiple sources. Articles were subsequently screened according to predefined inclusion criteria: (1) peer-reviewed full-text articles published in English; (2) reporting an untreated OPF as the control; (3) provide complete quantitative data, including the number of experimental units, mean values, and measures of variability (standard deviation or standard error of the mean, SEM); and (4) reporting of at least one outcome variable related to nutrient composition, in vitro and in vivo degradability, in vitro and in vivo fermentability, or animal performance.

Following title and abstract screening, 37 records were excluded for failing to meet the inclusion criteria, leaving 37 articles for full-text evaluation. The full-text assessment resulted in the exclusion of 12 articles due to the following reasons: (1) OPF was not subjected to processing, (2) absence of an untreated control group, or (3) the full-text article was not accessible. Consequently, a total of 25 articles meeting all eligibility criteria were included in the final database for subsequent quantitative analysis. The studies included in this meta-analysis are listed in Table 1. The database included the reference (first author), treatment category, treatment details, diet, diet composition, processing method, rumen donor or experimental animal, and country of origin.

Sub-group analysis

The sub-group analysis shows that all types of processing (i.e., physical, chemical and biological processing) show positive results for increasing total VFA (Fig. 2). Apparently, chemical processing is more effective than that of biological processing as shown by the higher total VFA production (P < 0.05). The cumulative effect size of lignin content in OPF is significantly influenced by the type of processing methods and country, as depicted in the forest plot (Fig. 3). A reduction in lignin content was observed across all processing methods (P < 0.05). Additionally, the sub-group analysis based on country reveals that only OPF from Indonesia and Malaysia demonstrated a decrease (P < 0.05) in lignin content due to processing.

Publication bias

Fig. 4 presents the funnel plots used to assess publication bias. Panel A shows the distribution of observed studies, while Panel B includes both observed and imputed studies following the trim-and-fill procedure. The funnel plot indicates the presence of publication bias, as evidenced by clear asymmetry in the distribution of studies. This observation is supported by the statistical evaluation using Egger’s test (z = −5.067), P < 0.001), confirming the presence of small-study effects. After adjustment, the overall effect size shifted from −1.68 to −1.67, indicating the potential influence of missing studies on the estimated effect. The asymmetry suggests that studies with certain outcomes may be underrepresented in the dataset. In particular, missing asymmetrical patterns may be associated with the preferential publication of studies reporting stronger effects of OPF pretreatment, especially those demonstrating a greater reduction in lignin content. Consistent with previous findings, Rahmadani et al. (2024) reported that significant Egger’s test results may indicate the presence of missing studies in meta-analysis, potentially linked to heterogeneity among included studies.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study provides the first quantitative synthesis of the effects of OPF pretreatment on rumen fermentation characteristics, thereby addressing a clear gap in the literature. Beyond consolidating fragmented evidence, the analysis identifies consistent biological patterns linking lignocellulosic disruption with improved fermentability and altered fermentation pathways. These findings strengthen the conceptual framework that structural modification of fibrous by-products is a primary determinant of their nutritional upgrading and methane mitigation potential in ruminant systems.

Effects of processed OPF on nutrient composition

The importance of the processing in enhancing the nutritional value of OPF for ruminant feeding cannot be overstated. The modification of the fibrous structure of OPF through treatment is necessary to facilitate more efficient and expedited digestion, as well as to increase accessibility for the rumen microbiota. The degradation of polysaccharides such as lignin, NDF, and ADF, leading to an increase in cellulose and hemicellulose content, is primarily achieved through the delignification process employed in all OPF treatments (Saminathan et al., 2021). In parallel, microbial inoculation and non-protein nitrogen (NPN) supplementation increase the crude protein content of OPF, with sources such as urea, NH₃, and NH₄OH improving its overall nutritional value and supporting ruminant energy and protein requirements (Gunun et al., 2016; Huang et al., 2020; Namoolnoy et al., 2011; Yunilas et al., 2014). However, the magnitude of these improvements varies substantially across processing methods.

Chemical treatments, especially alkaline agents such as ammonia and NaOH, consistently exhibit the strongest effects due to rapidly and efficiently break down structural fiber components in OPF (Barlianti et al., 2015; Paengkoum et al., 2006). Despite its effectiveness, this approach has limitations, particularly the risk of chemical residues that may be harmful to livestock if not properly managed (Bezzera et al., 2025). In contrast, biological treatments are considered safer and more environmentally sustainable. These methods typically involve the use of cellulolytic microorganisms, including lactic acid bacteria (LAB), fungi, and molds, which act by degrading fiber components while utilizing OPF as a growth substrate (Rusli et al., 2019; Tafsin et al., 2018). In recent years, combined treatment strategies have also gained attention, aiming to maximize the efficiency of fiber degradation (Rusli et al., 2019). Overall, improving the breakdown of recalcitrant fiber in OPF is expected to enhance its nutritional quality and support better rumen fermentation, ultimately contributing to improved ruminant performance.

Effects of processed OPF on in vitro fermentability and digestibility

Ruminants have limited ability to control rumen pH, especially when they are fed high-concentrated or low-forage feed. Based on the meta-analysis results, it was found that various processing technologies carried out on OPF showed no significant effect on rumen pH. This result indicates that OPF pretreatment enhances fermentability without compromising rumen stability. This is a critical finding, as shifts in fermentation intensity often risk inducing suboptimal rumen conditions. The stability of pH suggests that improvements in substrate availability are balanced by rumen buffering capacity and VFA utilization dynamics (Rosmalia et al., 2022).

Ammonia nitrogen (NH₃-N) concentration increased significantly in response to OPF pretreatment, consistent with enhanced proteolytic activity by rumen bacteria on the more accessible protein fractions of treated substrates (Susanto et al., 2023). The increase in NH3 production from OPF, which has been processed, indicates that bacteria more easily digest the protein that enters the rumen, so it will be more easily absorbed and utilized by ruminants in every metabolic process. Furthermore, when cattle experience NH3 deficiency, it can inhibit microbial protein synthesis (Hristov et al., 2011).

Constraints in using OPF as feed include the high fiber content, which can reduce the digestibility of the feed in the rumen. Digestibility metrics (DM, OM, and NDF) exhibited consistent and significant improvements, confirming that pretreatment effectively reduces the physical and chemical barriers imposed by lignocellulosic structures. The lower content of NDF, ADF, and lignin in a feed ingredient can increase the digestibility value of the feed ingredient for livestock (Widodo et al., 2023). These changes translated directly into increased total VFA production, with a marked shift toward propionate and butyrate. Such shifts indicate a transition toward more energetically efficient fermentation pathways, as propionate serves as a major glucogenic precursor while also acting as a competitive hydrogen sink. The observed reduction in the acetate-to-propionate ratio further supports this interpretation and suggests a potential mechanism for methane mitigation. Given that lignin-rich substrates restrict microbial access and fermentation (Kusmiati et al., 2024), their reduction appears to be the primary driver of enhanced fermentation efficiency. Thus, the in vitro results provide strong mechanistic evidence that OPF pretreatment reconfigures rumen fermentation toward improved energy utilization and reduced methane-forming pathways.

Effects of processed OPF on in vivo parameters and animal performance

The in vivo findings largely corroborate the in vitro results, particularly in terms of enhanced fermentability and nutrient degradability. Stable rumen pH and optimal NH₃ concentrations (9.53 mM) fall within the range required to sustain microbial growth (Wanapat & Pimpa, 1999; Wang & Tan, 2013), confirming that treated OPF supports a functional rumen environment. Increased acetate and total VFA production further indicate improved utilization of structural carbohydrates (Febrina et al., 2021; Izadbakhsh et al., 2024). The increased degradability of DM, OM, CP, NDF, ADF, and lignin indicates that OPF processing was effective in reducing both physical and chemical barriers of the plant cell wall. Alterations in fiber structure, particularly the disruption of lignocellulosic linkages, enhanced nutrient accessibility for rumen microorganisms (Chanjula et al., 2021).

However, these improvements did not translate into measurable gains in animal performance. This apparent disconnect highlights a critical limitation in the nutritional upgrading of fibrous feeds. The lack of response in DMI is likely driven by physical constraints, particularly rumen fill and extended retention time associated with high-fiber diets (Khaskheli et al., 2020; Krone et al., 2024). Additionally, increased VFA concentrations may contribute to satiety signaling, further suppressing intake (Simkins et al., 1965). These findings suggest that improvements in fermentability alone are insufficient to drive performance gains unless accompanied by strategies that address intake limitations and overall diet formulation. Nevertheless, the absence of negative effects on intake and productivity confirms that treated OPF is a nutritionally viable roughage source. This is consistent with previous meta-analysis evidence reporting minimal performance responses despite improved digestibility (Fhonna et al., 2025).

Practical implications for ruminant feeding systems

From a practical standpoint, the selection of OPF pretreatment method involves a trade-off between efficacy and feasibility. This meta-analysis study examines the type of processing technology that shows the most effective results in increasing fermentability based on total VFA production, namely chemical, physical and biological. Physical-chemical and only chemical processing, such as NaOH (Sukri et al., 2014) and urea (Paengkoum et al., 2006) show better results because they are able to partially hydrolyse the glycosidic bonds of lignocellulosic substances (Jayanegara et al., 2019; Sant’Ana Júnior et al., 2025). The use of chemical methods with alkalis can degrade the ester and glycosidic chain bonds by changing the lignin structure so that the cellulose bonds swell and partial decrystallization of cellulose (McIntosh & Vancov, 2010; Sills & Gossett, 2011). The latest findings regarding the effect of chemical processing are effective in reducing starch degradation of feed ingredients, which means it can increase ruminant utilisation (Rahmadani et al., 2023). However, their application is constrained by safety concerns, environmental implications, and additional costs associated with handling and waste management.

Physical and biological treatments offer more scalable alternatives, particularly under field conditions. Physical processing, such as steam explosion (Wu et al., 2020) and fibre cracking (Harahap et al., 2021), is also effective in breaking down fibre bonds to increase OPF product fermentability. The last is biological processing, such as an enzyme extract (Rusli et al., 2019), bacteria (Mulianda et al., 2018), fungal (Chanjula et al., 2017), and fermentation or ensilage (Imsya et al., 2013; Tafsin et al., 2018) have been suggested as a more sustainable approach to preserve the nutrient composition of OPF without causing significant degradation losses (Kawamoto et al., 2001). Although their effects on lignin reduction are generally less pronounced, these methods may offer greater feasibility for practical implementation due to lower operational complexity and cost. Therefore, the selection of processing method should balance effectiveness in lignocellulosic degradation with economic viability and applicability in real production systems.

Importantly, regional variability in OPF composition, evidenced by inconsistent lignin responses across countries, suggests that processing strategies should be context-specific. Therefore, optimizing OPF utilization requires not only selecting appropriate pretreatment technologies but also aligning them with local resource conditions, economic constraints, and production objectives. The observed variation in treatment effects also indicates that OPF from Thailand did not exhibit a significant reduction in lignin content, suggesting that the lignocellulosic structure of OPF may be influenced by environmental and geographical factors.

Although this meta-analysis provides a comprehensive synthesis of the effects of OPF pretreatment on rumen fermentation, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the literature search was restricted to a single database, which may have excluded relevant studies and introduced potential selection bias. Second, despite the relatively large number of included studies, substantial heterogeneity persists due to variation in OPF origin, pretreatment methods, and experimental conditions, potentially affecting the consistency of the estimated effects. Third, the interpretation of subgroup and moderator analyses should be treated with caution, as some categories are represented by a limited number of studies and unbalanced sample sizes. Fourth, the predominance of in vitro experiments constrains the extrapolation of these findings to in vivo and commercial production systems, where responses are shaped by more complex dietary and management interactions. Finally, several included studies were based on relatively small sample sizes, which may reduce the robustness of individual estimates and contribute to overall variability.

Conclusion

Processing of OPF significantly improves their nutritional quality by increasing CP content and reducing fiber fractions, including lignin. Processed OPF also enhances rumen fermentability, as indicated by increased total and partial VFA. Improvements were observed in DM, OM, and fiber degradability without causing adverse effects on animal performance. Chemical, physical, and biological processing are effective for improving OPF fermentability, with chemical processing exerting the greatest effect on lignocellulosic degradation. However, physical and biological processing are considered more sustainable and practical for field application. Additionally, in vitro results reflected the in vivo responses, particularly in increased VFA production and improved nutrient degradability. However, variations in certain parameters, such as pH value, acetate, and NH3 concentrations, suggest that in vitro findings may not fully represent the in vivo conditions.

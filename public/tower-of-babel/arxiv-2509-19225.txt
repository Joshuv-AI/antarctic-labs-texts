Chebyshev Expansion of the Zernike Polynomials
Richard J. Mathar

Abstract
. The even and odd Zernike Polynomials R n m ​ ( x ) R_{n}^{m}(x) can be expanded into sums of even and odd Chebyshev Polynomials T i ​ ( x ) T_{i}(x) . This manuscript provides closed forms for the rational expansion coefficients c n , m , i c_{n,m,i} for a set of small 0 ≤ n − m ≤ 6 0\leq n-m\leq 6 and a holonomic five-term recurrence for these coefficients for all larger n n .

1.
Overview

Zernike Polynomials
R_{n}^{m}(x)
are polynomials of degree
n

in the radial coordinate
x
,
0\leq x\leq 1

and defined for even non-negative
n-m=0,2,4,\ldots
.

Definition 1
.

(2D Zernike Radial Polynomials)

(1)

R_{n}^{m}(x)=(-1)^{(n-m)/2}x^{m}\sum_{s=0}^{(n-m)/2}\binom{(n+m)/2+s}{(n-m)/2-s}\binom{m+2s}{s}(-x^{2})^{s}.

We shall call
n
the radial quantum number and
m
the azimuthal
quantum number.
The inverse relation expands monomials
x^{j}
for fixed
m
into
R_{n}^{m}

by defining coefficients
h
,

(2)

x^{j}=\sum_{n\equiv m\pmod{2}}^{j}h_{j,n,m}R_{n}^{m}(x),

which are rational numbers
[
15
]

(3)

h_{j,n,m}=\frac{n+1}{1+\frac{j+n}{2}}\binom{(j-m)/2}{(n-m)/2}\frac{1}{\binom{(j+n)/2}{(n-m)/2}}

where
j-m=0,2,4,\ldots
.
The Chebyshev Polynomials
T_{i}(x)
are another orthogonal set of polynomials
defined over
-1\leq x\leq 1
:

Definition 2
.

(Chebyshev Polynomials of the First Kind)
[
1

(4)

T_{0}(x)=1;\quad T_{i}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}(-)^{s}\frac{(i-s-1)!}{s!(i-2s)!}(2x)^{i-2s},\quad i=1,2,3,4\ldots

The reverse expansion of the monomials is

[
5
, p. 412]
[
7
, p. 52]
[
8
]

(5)

x^{n}=2^{1-n}{\mathop{{\sum}^{\prime}}_{j=0\atop n-j\,\mathrm{even}}^{n}}{n\choose(n-j)/2}T_{j}(x),

where the prime at the sum symbol means the first term at
j=0

is to be halved if present.

The expansion of the
T_{i}
in finite series of
R_{n}^{m}

is known
[
16
]
; this manuscript derives
the expansion coefficients
c
of the inverse series.

Definition 3
.

(Coupling coefficients)

(6)

R_{n}^{m}(x)\equiv\mathop{{\sum}^{\prime}}_{i}c_{n,m,i}T_{i}(x)

for even
n-m\geq 0
.

In principle

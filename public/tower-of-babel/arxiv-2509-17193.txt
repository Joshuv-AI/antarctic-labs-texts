Abstract

The partition function,
p_{A}(n)
, is defined to be the number of partitions of
n
with parts in the set A, where
n
is a positive integer and
A
is a set of positive integers. It is well documented that: if A is a finite set with
\gcd(A)=1
and
|A|=k
, then

p_{A}(n)\sim\frac{n^{k-1}}{(\prod_{a\in A}a)(k-1)!}.

Number of proofs have been obtained for this estimate. In this article, we give a new proof for the above estimate by making use of the fact that:
p_{A}(n)
is a
quasi\ polynomial
when A is a finite set. Present method of proof is purely combinatorial.

1
Introduction and motivation.

By a partition of a positive integer
n
, we mean a non increasing sequence of positive integers say
\pi=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}\right)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=n
. Each
x_{i}
is called a part of the partition
\pi
. Let
A
be a set of positive integers. The partition function,
p_{A}(n)
, is defined to be the number of partitions of
n
with parts in the set A.

The generating function of
p_{A}(n)
is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{A}(n)x^{n}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-x^{a}}

(1)

with
p_{A}(0)=1
; this generating function is valid in the interval
|x|<1
.

We notice that, if
\gcd(A)\neq 1
, then

p_{A}(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}p_{\frac{A}{\gcd(A)}}\left(\frac{n}{\gcd(A)}\right)\ \ if\ \gcd(A)|n,\\
0\ otherwise\end{array}\right.

where the set
\frac{A}{\gcd(A)}=\left\{\frac{a}{\gcd(A)}:a\in A\right\}
.

Throughout this paper, we let
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\right\}
where
a_{i}^{\prime}
s are positive integers; unless otherwise stated, we proceed with the understanding that
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})=1
.

There is a lot of literature available on the function
p_{A}(n)
. T. C. Brown et al
[
3
]
found exact formulas for
p_{A}(n)
when
|A|=2
or
3
.
Exact formula for
p_{A}(n)
can be found using partial fraction decomposition of its generating function (see
[
6
]
). Gert Almkvist
[
1
]
provided an exact formula for
p_{A}(n)
without the use of partial fraction decomposition of generating function. Following estimate of
p_{A}(n)
is well known:

p_{A}(n)\sim\frac{n^{k-1}}{\left(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\right)(k-1)!}.

(2)

This estimate was proved by several authors. Earlier, a proof was given by E. Netto
[
8
]
and later G. Polya and G. Szegö
[
9
]
gave another proof; they have used partial fraction decomposition of generating function to accomplish the purpose. Paul Erdos
[
5
]
proved the estimate when
A=\left\{1,2,\cdots,k\right\}
. S. Sertoz and A. E. Ozlük.
[
11
]
had given a proof based on the following reccurrence relation of
p_{A}(n)
:

1=\sum_{i=n-k+2}^{n}C_{n-i}[p_{A}(i)-p_{A}(i-(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}))]

for
n>(a_{1}\cdots a_{k})-(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})+k-2
, where

C_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(-1)^{m}{{k-2}\choose{m}}\ \ for\ 0\leq m\leq k-2\\
0\ otherwise\end{array}\right.

An arithmetic proof was obtained by Melvyn B. Nathanson
[
7
]
.

In this note, we give a new proof for the above mentioned estimate of
p_{A}(n)
. Present proof is based on the quasi polynomial representation of
p_{A}(n)
.

Definition 1.0.1
.

An arithmetical function
f
is said to be a
Quasi\ polynomial
if,
f(\alpha l+r)
is a polynomial in
l
for each
r=0,1,\cdots,\alpha-1
, where
\alpha
is a positive integer greater than 1. Each polynomial
f(\alpha l+r)
is called constituent polynomial of
f
and
\alpha
is called quasi period of
f
.

The fact that,
p_{A}(n)
is a quasi polynomial, was proved by E. T. Bell
[
2
]
, who empolyed partial fraction decomposition of its generating function. E. M. Wright
[
12
]
showed that
p_{A}(n)
is a quasi polynomial by extracting the term
(1-x^{t})^{-k}
from the generating function of
p_{A}(n)
, where
t=lcm(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})
. Using similar method, O. J. Rødseth and J. A. Sellers
[
10
]
also had found the quasi polynomial representation of
p_{A}(n)
, but in binomial coefficients form.

In the present proof, we derive a finite linear recurrence relation of
p_{A}(n)
.
Using that recurrence relation, we show that: the function,
p_{A}(n)
, is a quasi polynomial with quasi period
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}
and each constituent polynomial being of degree
k-1
. Further, we found that, the leading coefficient of each constituent polynomial of
p_{A}(n)
is
\frac{(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k})^{k-2}}{(k-1)!}
. Accordingly, we get the required estimate.

2
Proof.

2.1
A recurrence relation satisfied by
p_{A}(n)

Following lemma is crucial to our proof.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. For
\forall a\in A
, we have

p_{A}(n)=p_{A}(n-a)+p_{A\setminus\left\{a\right\}}(n)

(3)

provided
a\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
\pi=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}\right)
be a partition of
n
with parts in the set A and let
a\in A
.

Case (i)
:
Assume that
x_{i}=a
for some
i
. In such case, we can write

\pi=\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},a,x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{m}\right)
. We notice that, the mapping

\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},a,x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{m}\right)\to\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{m}\right)

is a bijection between the following sets:

•

Set of all partitions of
n
with parts in the set A and having the part
a
.

•

Set of all partitions of
n-a
with parts in the set A.

We see that, cardinality of the latter set is
p_{A}(n-a)
.

Case(ii):
Assume that
x_{i}\neq a

\forall i=1,2,\cdots,m
. It is not hard to see that, enumeration of such partitions is
p_{A\setminus\left\{a\right\}}(n)
. Thus result follows.
∎

2.2
Main part of the proof

As consequences of lemma
2.1.1
, we show that:

1.

The function
p_{A}(n)
is a quasi polynomial with quasi period
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}
and each constituent polynomial is of degree
k-1
.

2.

Leading coefficient of the constituent polynomial
p_{A}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}l+r)
is
\frac{(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k})^{k-2}}{(k-1)!}
for each
r=0,1,\cdots,a_{1}\cdots a_{k}-1
.

From the above two statements it follows readily that:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{p_{A}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}l+r)}{(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}l+r)^{k-1}}=\frac{1}{\left(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\right)(k-1)!}

for each
r=0,1,\cdots,a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}-1
. Whence the targeted estimate follows.

Now, we prove statement 1 and statement 2 simultaneously by using induction on
k
.

Suppose that
k=2
. Let
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2}\right\}
with
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2})=1
. Then applying lemma
2.1.1

a_{1}
times, we get

p_{A}(a_{1}a_{2}l+r)-p_{A}(a_{1}a_{2}(l-1)+r)=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}p_{\left\{a_{1}\right\}}(a_{1}a_{2}l+r-ia_{2})

(4)

for each
r=0,1,\cdots,a_{1}a_{2}-1
. Since the congruence equation

a_{2}x\equiv r(mod\ a_{1})

has unique solution modulo
a_{1}
(see
[
4
]
pp. 83-84), the right side of the equation (
4
) equals
1
. Then replacing
l
by
1,\cdots,l
in equation (
4
) and adding, we get that

p_{A}(a_{1}a_{2}l+r)=l+p_{A}(r)

for each
r=0,1,2,\cdots,a_{1}a_{2}-1
. Thus, the function
p_{A}(n)
is a quasi polynomial with each constituent polynomial being of degree 1 and leading coefficient
1=\frac{(a_{1}a_{2})^{2-2}}{(2-1)!}
.

Assume that, the result is true when
|A|<k
for a fixed
k\geq 3
. Now, consider the set of
k
positive integers say
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\right\}
with
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})=1
.
Let
s=\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k-1})
. Then applying lemma
2.1.1

a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k-1}
times again, we get

p_{A}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}l+r)-p_{A}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}(l-1)+r)=\sum_{0\leq i\leq a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}-1;s|(r-ia_{k})}p_{\left\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{k-1}\right\}}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}l+r-ia_{k})

(5)

=\sum_{0\leq i\leq a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}-1;s|(r-ia_{k})}p_{\left\{\frac{a_{1}}{s},\cdots,\frac{a_{k-1}}{s}\right\}}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{s}\cdots\frac{a_{k-1}}{s}(a_{k}s^{k-2}l+q_{i})+r_{i}\right)

for each
r=0,1,\cdots,a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}-1
, where
r_{i}
and
q_{i}
were determined from the equality:
\frac{r-ia_{k}}{s}=\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}}{s^{k-1}}q_{i}+r_{i}
; uniqueness of
r_{i}
and
q_{i}
and the bound:
0\leq r_{i}\leq\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}}{s^{k-1}}-1
follows as a consequence of Division algorithm.

We notice that, the congruence equation

a_{k}x\equiv r(mod\ s)

(6)

has solution if, and only if,
\gcd(a_{k},s)|r
(see
[
4
]
pp. 83-84). In such case, eqn(
6
) will have
\gcd(a_{k},s)
number of mutually incongruent solution modulo
s
. Here,
\gcd(a_{k},s)=1
; therefore, eqn(
6
) has a unique solution modulo
s
.

Since
\gcd(\frac{a_{1}}{s},\cdots\frac{a_{k}}{s})
=1, by induction assumption, it follows that the right side of the equation (
5
) is the sum of
\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{s}
polynomials, each of which is of degree
k-2
and with leading coefficient being
\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k-1}}{s^{k-1}}\right)^{k-3}\frac{a_{k}^{k-2}s^{(k-2)^{2}}}{(k-2)!}
. Consequently, the right side sum of equation (
5
) is itself a polynomial of degree
k-2
. This implies that
p_{A}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}l+r)
is a polynomial in
l
of degree
k-1
for each
r=0,1,\cdots,a_{1}\cdots a_{k}-1
.

Now, we calculate the leading coefficient of
p_{A}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}l+r)
. If one denotes the leading coefficient of the polynomial
p_{A}(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}l+r)
by
c_{k-1}
, then by the previous observations it follows that

(k-1)c_{k-1}=\frac{(a_{1}\cdots a_{k})^{k-2}}{(k-2)!},

which simplifies to

c_{k-1}=\frac{(a_{1}\cdots a_{k})^{k-2}}{(k-1)!}.

The proof is now completed.

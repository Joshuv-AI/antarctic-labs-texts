b → c b\to c semileptonic sum rule: Extension to angular observables Preprint: KEK–TH–2729, P3H–25–038, TTP25–018
Motoi Endo, Syuhei Iguro, Tim Kretz, Satoshi Mishima, Ryoutaro Watanabe

Abstract
Lepton flavor universality is a key prediction of the Standard Model of particle physics and any violation of it immediately indicates the existence of new physics. Given the recent more than 4 ​ σ 4\sigma discrepancy in charged current semileptonic B B meson decays and the absence of evident signals at the large hadron collider, independent cross-checks become invaluable. In this context, b → c b\to c semileptonic sum rules based on heavy quark symmetry are interesting since they allow us to check the consistency of experimental results. In this paper, we report newly found sum rules among angular observables of mesonic and baryonic b → c ​ l ​ ν ¯ b\to cl\overline{\nu} decays holding exactly in the large mass limit of heavy quarks. Moreover, we investigate corrections to the sum rule in realistic situations and discuss phenomenological implications. ——————————————————————————————————————————— Keywords: Heavy quark symmetry, b → c b\to c semileptonic sum rule, Angular observables

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we extended the previous studies of the
b\to c
semileptonic sum rule for the single-differential decay rates and found a new relation for the double-differential decay rates, which holds exactly in the heavy quark limit.
We then derived two sum rules involving the
\tau
-polarization observable,
P_{\tau}
, and the charged lepton forward-backward asymmetry,
A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}
.
In reality, the heavy quark symmetry is violated and the sum rules receive corrections from higher-order effects in the heavy quark expansion,
i.e.
, the realistic mass spectrum and the inclusion of higher-order terms of the IW functions.
We also evaluated these corrections numerically.
Using HQET-based form factors, we demonstrated that currently the sum rule coefficients,
\alpha_{P_{\tau}}
and
\alpha_{A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}}
, can be determined with precisions of approximately
3\%
and
11\%
, respectively.
To reduce the uncertainty of these coefficients, experimental input of
\Lambda_{b}\to\Lambda_{c}\mu\overline{\nu}
and Lattice calculations are important.
Furthermore, we estimated the corrections in the NP scenarios motivated by the
R_{D^{(*)}}
anomaly.
It is found that these corrections are at most
\sim 5\%
for the
\tau
-polarization sum rule, Eq. (
10
), while they can reach around
-100\%
for the
A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}
case, Eq. (
11
), depending on the NP scenarios.
These new sum rules will provide independent cross-checks of experimental results.
By testing both angular sum rules, we can better explore and tell apart NP scenarios.
These results encourage the feasibility study at on-going and future experiments

Appendix C
More on the sum rule in the heavy quark limit

In the heavy quark limit, the sum rule for the double-differential decay rates of Eq. (
8
) can be transformed as

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}}^{w,-1/2}}{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,-1/2}}=\alpha_{\rm HQL}^{P_{\tau}}\frac{\kappa_{D}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{D}^{w,-1/2}}{\kappa_{D\,{\rm SM}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{D\,{\rm SM}}^{w,-1/2}}+\beta_{\rm HQL}^{P_{\tau}}\frac{\kappa_{D^{*}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{D^{*}}^{w,-1/2}}{\kappa_{D^{*}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{D^{*}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,-1/2}}\,,

(50)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}+}^{w}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}-}^{w}}{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}+}^{w\,{\rm SM}}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}-}^{w\,{\rm SM}}}=\alpha_{\rm HQL}^{A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}}\frac{\kappa_{D+}^{w}-\kappa_{D-}^{w}}{\kappa_{D+}^{w\,{\rm SM}}-\kappa_{D-}^{w\,{\rm SM}}}+\beta_{\rm HQL}^{A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}}\frac{\kappa_{D^{*}+}^{w}-\kappa_{D^{*}-}^{w}}{\kappa_{D^{*}+}^{w\,{\rm SM}}-\kappa_{D^{*}-}^{w\,{\rm SM}}}\,,

(51)

where the differential decay rates in denominators are the SM ones,
i.e.
,
C_{X}^{\tau}=0
.
Also
\kappa_{H_{c}}^{w,\lambda_{\tau}}=\frac{d\Gamma^{H_{c}}_{\lambda_{\tau}}}{dw}
,

\kappa_{H_{c}\pm}^{w}=\pm\int^{\pm 1}_{0}dc_{\theta_{\tau}}\frac{d^{2}\Gamma^{H_{c}}}{dwdc_{\theta_{\tau}}}
, and
\frac{d^{2}\Gamma^{H_{c}}}{dwdc_{\theta_{\tau}}}=\frac{d^{2}\Gamma^{H_{c}}_{+1/2}}{dwdc_{\theta_{\tau}}}+\frac{d^{2}\Gamma^{H_{c}}_{-1/2}}{dwdc_{\theta_{\tau}}}
are defined.
The coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{\rm HQL}^{P_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1+w}\frac{\zeta(w)^{2}}{\xi(w)^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{D\,{\rm SM}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{D\,{\rm SM}}^{w,-1/2}}{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,+1/2}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}\,{\rm SM}}^{w,-1/2}}=\frac{(1+r_{D})^{2}(1+\rho^{2}_{\tau}-w(1-2\rho^{2}_{\tau}))-6r_{D}\rho^{2}_{\tau}(1+w)}{4r_{D}(1-2\rho_{\tau}^{2}+w^{2}(2-\rho_{\tau}^{2}))-6w(1-\rho_{\tau}^{2})(1+r_{D}^{2})}\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\rm HQL}^{A_{\rm FB}^{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1+w}\frac{\zeta(w)^{2}}{\xi(w)^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{D+}^{w\,{\rm SM}}-\kappa_{D-}^{w\,{\rm SM}}}{\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}+}^{w\,{\rm SM}}-\kappa_{\Lambda_{c}-}^{w\,{\rm SM}}}=\frac{-(1-r_{D}^{2})\rho_{\tau}^{2}}{2(1-\rho_{\tau}^{2}+r_{D}^{2}(1+\rho_{\tau}^{2})-2r_{D}w)}\,,

(52)

where
\rho_{\tau}={m_{\tau}}/\sqrt{q^{2}}
and
r_{i}=m_{i}/m_{B}
are defined.
They are independent of the leading-order IW functions
\zeta(w)
and
\xi(w)
, and satisfy
\alpha_{\rm HQL}+\beta_{\rm HQL}=1
as the sum rule should hold in the SM limit.

\alpha_{\rm HQL}
and
\beta_{\rm HQL}
are independent of NP contributions
C_{X}
and hence the sum rule holds in any NP model captured in Eq. (
6
).
Similar to the construction of the sum rule for
R_{H_{c}}
, the sum rules involving angular observables, Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
), are obtained by integrating numerators and denominators over
w
, and by normalizing with the decay widths of light-lepton modes.
It is noted that
\Gamma(H_{b}\to H_{c}\tau\overline{\nu})
dependence is canceled in the combinations of
P_{\tau}^{H_{c}}\,R_{H_{c}}
and
A_{\rm FB}^{H_{c}}\,R_{H_{c}}
for each.

A literature-guided integrative transcriptomic analysis framework for prioritizing angiogenesis-associated genes across cancers
Authors: Jingcan You, Liqun Wang, Yongjie Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAngiogenesis is a fundamental process in cancer progression, but computational prioritization of angiogenesis-associated genes often relies on predefined gene sets or canonical signaling pathways, limiting the identification of non-canonical and context-dependent regulatory programs. To address this limitation, we developed a literature-guided integrative transcriptomic framework to prioritize angiogenesis-associated candidates across heterogeneous cancer types. The framework integrates bibliometric trend profiling, pan-cancer gene expression analysis, survival association assessment, tumor microenvironment-related transcriptional characterization, and functional enrichment analysis. Rather than prespecifying angiogenic regulators, candidate genes were prioritized through convergence across these complementary evidence layers. Across diverse cancer cohorts, the framework consistently identified cadherin 2 (CDH2) as a representative non-canonical angiogenesis-associated signal. CDH2-associated transcriptional programs were enriched in extracellular matrix organization, cell–cell adhesion, and structural remodeling processes, and tumor microenvironment analyses indicated preferential alignment with stromal and vascular-associated features, particularly cancer-associated fibroblast-related signatures, rather than immune-dominant profiles. In independent external glioma cohorts, CDH2 showed a non-uniform and context-dependent relationship with vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA)-associated transcriptional patterns, while CDH2-high tumors reproducibly exhibited higher extracellular matrix remodeling, adhesion/focal adhesion, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and angiogenesis-related signature scores. In an independent Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas cohort, higher CDH2 expression was also associated with worse overall survival in a lower-grade glioma-preferred subset. Collectively, this study demonstrates the utility of a knowledge-guided integrative transcriptomic framework for prioritizing angiogenesis-associated genes beyond canonical regulators and highlights structure-oriented, non-canonical angiogenesis-related programs as reproducible biological signals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05266-9.Keywords: Angiogenesis, Integrative transcriptomics, Gene prioritization, Tumor microenvironment, Pan-cancer analysis, Knowledge-guided analysis

Introduction

Angiogenesis is a complex and multi-layered biological process that supports cancer development and progression through coordinated regulation of endothelial activity, extracellular matrix remodeling, cell–cell adhesion, and stromal organization within the tumor microenvironment [1–3]. Although canonical angiogenic signaling pathways—most prominently the VEGF–VEGFR axis—have been extensively characterized, increasing transcriptome-based evidence indicates that angiogenesis-associated regulation extends beyond a limited set of ligand–receptor interactions and involves broader structural and microenvironmental programs that vary across tumor contexts [2–4].

From a computational perspective, the systematic prioritization of angiogenesis-associated genes from large-scale transcriptomic data remains challenging [5]. Many existing analytical strategies rely on predefined gene sets, curated pathways, or prior biological assumptions to delineate angiogenic regulators. While such approaches are effective for interrogating established mechanisms, they may inherently reinforce canonical models and limit sensitivity to non-canonical or context-dependent signals [6, 7]. In addition, angiogenesis-related analyses are frequently conducted within individual cancer types or centered on isolated candidate genes, complicating the assessment of cross-cancer consistency and the integration of heterogeneous evidence across datasets [8, 9].

The expanding availability of public transcriptomic resources and biomedical literature provides an opportunity to address these limitations through integrative computational analyses. Large-scale platforms such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and UCSC Xena enable standardized pan-cancer transcriptomic profiling across diverse tumor contexts [9, 10]. However, despite these advances, generalizable analytical frameworks that combine transcriptomic data with knowledge-based signals—such as long-term literature trends—while supporting unbiased candidate prioritization remain limited. In particular, computational strategies capable of jointly integrating expression patterns, functional context, tumor microenvironmental features, and survival associations within a unified analytical workflow are still underdeveloped [11].

To address this gap, we present a literature-guided integrative transcriptomic analysis framework designed to systematically prioritize angiogenesis-associated genes across cancers. The framework integrates bibliometric trend profiling with pan-cancer expression analysis, survival association assessment, tumor microenvironment–related transcriptional characterization, and functional enrichment analysis. Rather than prespecifying angiogenic regulators, candidate genes are allowed to emerge from the convergence of independent analytical layers, thereby reducing bias introduced by predefined gene lists and enhancing robustness and interpretability at the systems level [12].

Using angiogenesis as a representative biological process, we applied this framework to pan-cancer transcriptomic datasets and highlight CDH2 as a reproducible, non-canonical angiogenesis-associated signal. Importantly, CDH2 is presented as an illustrative example to demonstrate the analytical utility of the proposed framework, rather than as a definitive mechanistic or therapeutic target. By emphasizing methodological integration and cross-context consistency, this study illustrates how knowledge-guided transcriptomic analysis can refine biological candidate prioritization and provides a generalizable computational strategy applicable to other complex biological processes beyond angiogenesis [13, 14].

Methods

Study design, aim, and overall analytical framework

This study was conducted as a retrospective, integrative computational analysis aimed at systematically prioritizing angiogenesis-associated genes across cancers using publicly available transcriptomic and literature-derived data. Angiogenesis was selected as a representative biological process to illustrate the analytical framework. No human participants were prospectively recruited, no experimental interventions were performed, and no identifiable personal data were accessed. All analyses were based on de-identified, publicly accessible datasets and complied with the relevant data usage policies of the original sources.

The analytical framework integrates literature-guided evidence with pan-cancer transcriptomic profiling, survival association analysis, tumor microenvironment characterization, and functional enrichment analysis. Rather than prespecifying known angiogenic regulators, candidate genes were prioritized through the convergence of independent analytical layers. Literature retrieval and bibliometric analysis

Data sources and search strategy

Angiogenesis-related publications were retrieved from the PubMed database, covering the period from 2011 to 2025. A comprehensive search query was designed to capture studies related to angiogenesis, neovascularization, vascular formation, and endothelial-associated processes. Search terms were applied to titles/abstracts and MeSH terms, and the detailed search strategy is provided in Additional file 1 [15, 16].

Literature preprocessing and keyword extraction

Retrieved records were processed using a standardized text-mining pipeline adapted from established literature-mining workflows [17, 18]. Records from different years were first cleaned separately and then merged into a unified corpus. Duplicate publications were removed based on PMID to generate the final deduplicated literature corpus used for downstream analyses (n = 221,423).

Titles and abstracts were normalized by lowercasing, punctuation harmonization, and filtering of non-informative characters. Gene symbols mentioned in the text were standardized using an HGNC-derived alias mapping table based on the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC) reference resource [19]. Angiogenesis-associated gene mentions were identified using a frequency-based extraction strategy. Annual publication counts and yearly active gene counts were calculated to characterize temporal trends in the literature.

Integrated scoring framework for gene prioritization

Candidate genes were evaluated using an integrated scoring framework that combined complementary quantitative and structural evidence derived from the literature corpus. Quantitative evidence (S1) reflected publication frequency and temporal dynamics, capturing sustained or emerging research attention. Structural evidence (S2) quantified changes in gene contextual associations within a literature-derived co-occurrence network [20].

Specifically, S2 was designed to partially mitigate literature “echo-chamber” effects by emphasizing temporal increases in a gene’s network centrality between early and recent literature periods, rather than simply rewarding repeated mentions. In this way, S2 prioritizes genes that become more structurally connected within the angiogenesis-related literature, thereby complementing frequency-based signals with network-level repositioning information.

For visualization purposes only, S1 scores were additionally transformed into rank-based values to mitigate saturation effects caused by highly skewed publication distributions (Fig. 1). Normalized S1 and S2 scores were jointly examined within a two-dimensional framework. Median values of each dimension, calculated across the full scored gene corpus included in the two-dimensional prioritization space, were used as non-parametric thresholds to stratify candidate genes. These thresholds were applied consistently across analyses and were not optimized for individual genes.

Transcriptomic data acquisition and expression analyses

Gene expression data

Pan-cancer gene expression data were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) Pan-Cancer project and accessed via the UCSC Xena platform [8, 10]. RNA-seq expression matrices generated using the Toil RNA-seq recompute pipeline from the TCGA TARGET GTEx dataset were used in this study, including TcgaTargetGtex_rsem_gene_fpkm and TcgaTargetGtex_rsem_gene_tpm, together with the corresponding sample annotation file TcgaTargetGTEX_phenotype.txt.

The dataset includes samples from multiple TCGA cancer cohorts, including TCGA-BRCA, TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, TCGA-LIHC, TCGA-COAD, TCGA-READ, TCGA-STAD, TCGA-ESCA, TCGA-HNSC, TCGA-KIRC, TCGA-KIRP, TCGA-KICH, TCGA-BLCA, TCGA-UCEC, TCGA-OV, TCGA-PRAD, TCGA-THCA, TCGA-SKCM, TCGA-SARC, TCGA-PAAD, TCGA-ACC, TCGA-CHOL, TCGA-DLBC, TCGA-MESO, TCGA-PCPG, TCGA-TGCT, TCGA-UCS, TCGA-UVM, TCGA-GBM, and TCGA-LGG.

Samples with missing or non-numeric expression values were excluded. No additional batch-correction step was applied beyond the uniform processing provided by the UCSC Xena Toil recompute resource. This choice was made because the Toil recompute datasets were generated through a common computational workflow designed to improve comparability across TCGA, TARGET, and GTEx RNA-seq samples. However, residual heterogeneity related to study origin, sample procurement, and cohort composition may still remain. Accordingly, tumor–normal comparisons in this study were interpreted as comparative screening-level analyses, whereas several downstream analyses, including expression-based stratification, correlation analysis, and survival analysis, were performed within individual cancer cohorts to reduce reliance on absolute cross-source expression comparability.

Differential expression and stratification analyses

Differential expression between tumor and normal tissues was assessed using the two-sided Mann–Whitney U test. For intratumoral analyses, tumor samples were stratified into high- and low-expression groups based on the median expression level of each gene within the corresponding cancer cohort.

Correlation analysis

Associations between candidate genes and VEGFA expression were evaluated using Spearman rank correlation analysis. Correlation coefficients and corresponding P values were calculated using matched tumor samples within each cancer type.

Survival analysis and tumor microenvironmental profiling

Survival analysis

Overall survival analyses were conducted using transcriptomic and clinical data obtained from the UCSC Xena platform [9]. Gene expression profiles were derived from the TCGA TARGET GTEx (Toil) RNA-seq dataset (FPKM normalized). Patients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups based on median gene expression values. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated, and survival differences were assessed using the log-rank test.

Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to estimate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. To explore joint prognostic associations, multivariate Cox regression models including CDH2, VEGFA, and their interaction term were constructed.

Immune and stromal signature analysis

Immune and stromal transcriptional signatures were quantified using curated gene sets representing immune cell populations, signaling pathways, and tumor microenvironmental components [21–23]. Immune microenvironment signatures were obtained from the TCGA pan-cancer immune signature dataset “TCGA_pancancer_10852whitelistsamples_68ImmuneSigs”, accessed via the UCSC Xena platform. For each signature, composite scores were calculated and z-score normalized across samples. Differences between CDH2-high and CDH2-low groups were evaluated using the Mann–Whitney U test.

To facilitate interpretable visualization, immune and stromal signatures were prioritized using a multi-criteria scoring strategy based on effect size magnitude, divergence between CDH2- and VEGFA-associated effects, and statistical significance. This prioritization was applied exclusively for visualization and did not affect statistical testing.

Functional enrichment and gene set enrichment analyses

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

Genes positively correlated with CDH2 expression were subjected to functional enrichment analysis using the Enrichr platform. Gene Ontology biological process enrichment was performed using the GO_Biological_Process_2021 gene set library, and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using the KEGG_2021_Human gene set library. Statistical significance was assessed using Benjamini–Hochberg–adjusted P values [24–26]. Enriched terms with adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered significant. Representative results are shown in Fig. 4a, b, and complete enrichment statistics are provided in Additional file 10 and Additional file 11.

Hallmark gene set enrichment analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis was performed using the MSigDB Hallmark gene set collection (Hallmark 2020 release; MSigDB_Hallmark_2020) in preranked mode. Genes were ranked based on genome-wide correlation with CDH2 expression. Normalized enrichment scores and false discovery rates were calculated, with a false discovery rate q < 0.25 considered statistically significant [24, 27]. Summary results are shown in Fig. 4c, and complete statistics are provided in Additional file 12. A representative enrichment plot for the Hallmark Angiogenesis gene set is shown in Fig. 4d.

Cell-type signature analysis

Bulk RNA-seq–based cell-type signature analyses were conducted to assess associations between CDH2 expression and major cellular components of the tumor microenvironment, including endothelial cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts, and immune cells. Signature scores were calculated using predefined gene sets representing each cell type, curated from previously published studies, and quantified using the TcgaTargetGtex_rsem_gene_tpm expression matrix. For each signature, composite scores were calculated and z-score normalized across samples.

Associations between CDH2 expression and cell-type signatures were evaluated using Spearman rank correlation analysis, and group differences between CDH2-high and CDH2-low tumors were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Complete statistical results are provided in Additional file 13 and Additional file 14.

External validation in independent glioma cohorts

To assess the robustness and generalizability of the framework-derived findings, additional external validation analyses were performed using independent glioma cohorts from publicly available resources. For transcriptomic validation, three Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets were used: GSE147352, GSE121720, and GSE111260 [28–30]. These datasets were used to evaluate the relationship between CDH2 and VEGFA across independent glioma contexts and to test whether CDH2-high tumors reproducibly exhibited higher extracellular matrix remodeling, adhesion/focal adhesion, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and angiogenesis-related transcriptional programs. For prognostic validation, the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) mRNAseq_693 cohort, together with matched clinical annotations, was used to assess the association between CDH2 expression and overall survival in an automatically defined LGG-preferred subset [31]. External validation analyses included correlation analysis, expression-based stratification, representative signature scoring, Kaplan–Meier analysis, and Cox regression, as appropriate for each dataset.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.10) with the SciPy and statsmodels libraries. All tests were two-sided. False discovery rate correction was applied where appropriate using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified. Formal power calculations were not applicable, as the study was based on retrospective analyses of publicly available datasets rather than prospective hypothesis testing.

Use of large language models

Large language models were used exclusively to assist with language editing and structural refinement of the manuscript text. No large language models were used for data analysis, statistical computation, or result generation. All analytical procedures, data processing steps, and interpretations were performed and verified by the authors.

Results

Literature trend–guided prioritization of angiogenesis-associated genes

Accelerated expansion of angiogenesis-related literature over the past decade

As the initial step of the integrative framework, longitudinal trends in angiogenesis-related publications indexed in PubMed were examined to characterize the evolving knowledge landscape underlying candidate prioritization. As shown in Fig. 1a, the annual number of angiogenesis-related publications remained relatively stable between 2011 and 2018, followed by a pronounced and sustained increase beginning in 2019. Both annual counts and the 3-year rolling mean exhibited a sharp upward trajectory during 2021–2024, indicating intensified and persistent research activity. The modest decrease observed in 2025 is likely attributable to partial-year data coverage rather than a substantive decline.

These trends indicate that angiogenesis research has entered a phase of accelerated expansion in recent years, resulting in a rapidly growing and increasingly complex body of literature that provides the context for systematic computational prioritization.

Expansion of the angiogenesis-associated gene space parallels publication growth

To assess whether increased publication activity was accompanied by diversification of molecular focus, the annual number of angiogenesis-associated “active” genes identified through literature text mining was quantified. As shown in Fig. 1b, the number of active genes increased gradually from 2011 to 2018 and rose sharply after 2019, with the 3-year rolling mean peaking during 2022–2024.

This pattern indicates that recent growth in angiogenesis research reflects not only increased publication output but also expansion of the molecular gene space under investigation. Together, Fig. 1a, b show that angiogenesis-related knowledge has become progressively more heterogeneous, underscoring the need for integrative strategies capable of prioritizing candidates across an expanding and diverse gene landscape.

A literature-guided integrative scoring framework combining quantitative and structural evidence

Based on the literature-derived angiogenesis-associated gene corpus comprising approximately 4500 genes, a dual-dimension prioritization framework integrating complementary quantitative and structural evidence was applied (Fig. 1c). For each gene, a quantitative score (S1) was calculated to capture publication frequency and temporal dynamics, reflecting sustained or emerging research attention. In parallel, a structural score (S2) was derived to quantify changes in gene contextual associations within a literature-based co-occurrence network.

The complete PubMed search strategy and inclusion criteria used to construct the angiogenesis-related literature corpus are provided in Additional file 1. The full angiogenesis-associated gene corpus, together with corresponding S1 and S2 metrics, is available in Additional file 2. By jointly considering quantitative intensity and structural repositioning, this framework supports prioritization that is not driven solely by publication volume, but also incorporates shifts in conceptual context within the angiogenesis literature.

Integrated scoring landscape highlights canonical and non-canonical angiogenesis-associated candidates

Application of the integrative scoring framework produced a ranked landscape of angiogenesis-associated candidates. Within this landscape, CDH2, CD8A, and COL14A1 emerged as co-prioritized genes exhibiting high integrated scores while representing distinct biological contexts related to angiogenesis (Fig. 1d). The complete set of prioritized angiogenesis-associated candidates is summarized in Additional file 3.

To contextualize these candidates within the global angiogenesis-associated gene space, normalized S1 and S2 scores were visualized in a two-dimensional landscape with median-based thresholds defining quadrant boundaries (Fig. 1e). Established angiogenesis regulators, including VEGFA, KDR, TEK, and HIF1A, were enriched in the high–high quadrant, supporting the internal coherence of the prioritization framework. Notably, the co-prioritized candidates occupied the same region while displaying distinct quantitative and structural profiles relative to canonical regulators, indicating that the framework captures both well-established and non-canonical angiogenesis-associated signals.

Temporal activation patterns differentiate emerging candidates from classical regulators

To further characterize prioritization behavior, gene-wise temporal publication dynamics were examined for the co-prioritized candidates in comparison with representative classical angiogenesis regulators. After Z-score normalization, CDH2, CD8A, and COL14A1 exhibited pronounced and sustained increases in recent publication activity, consistent with emerging or expanding research attention (Fig. 1f). In contrast, classical angiogenesis regulators displayed more stable or fluctuating trajectories, reflecting long-term maintenance rather than recent activation.

Collectively, these temporal patterns indicate that the integrative framework differentiates emerging angiogenesis-associated candidates from long-established regulators based on longitudinal knowledge dynamics, rather than static gene lists alone. The complete ranked list of the top 50 angiogenesis-associated candidates, together with integrated scores and biological annotations, is provided in Additional file 4.

Transcriptomic behaviors and functional context of top-ranked angiogenesis-associated candidates

Pan-cancer differential expression patterns of framework-prioritized candidates

To examine whether top-ranked candidates prioritized by the literature-guided framework exhibit consistent transcriptional alterations in cancer, expression differences between tumor and normal tissues were assessed across TCGA cohorts. As shown in Fig. 2a, the co-prioritized candidates CD8A, CDH2, and COL14A1 displayed significant tumor-associated differential expression across multiple cancer types. The magnitude and direction of these expression changes were comparable to those observed for representative canonical angiogenesis-related genes, including KDR, VEGFA, and CDH5.

While canonical angiogenesis genes tended to show relatively consistent tumor-associated expression shifts across cancers, the framework-prioritized candidates exhibited greater heterogeneity in both effect size and direction. These results indicate that top-ranked candidates are transcriptionally deregulated in tumor tissues, but are not constrained to uniform expression behaviors across cancer types, consistent with context-dependent angiogenesis-associated regulation.

Intra-tumoral expression heterogeneity revealed by expression-based stratification

To further characterize transcriptional variability within tumor populations, samples were stratified into high- and low-expression groups for each gene based on median expression levels within individual cancer cohorts. As illustrated in Fig. 2b, both framework-prioritized candidates and canonical angiogenesis-related genes showed clear separation between expression-defined subgroups, indicating substantial intra-tumoral heterogeneity.

Notably, the stratification contrast observed for CD8A, CDH2, and COL14A1 was comparable to, and in some cancer types more pronounced than, that observed for classical angiogenesis regulators. These findings suggest that expression variability of framework-prioritized candidates represents a major component of transcriptomic heterogeneity within tumors, rather than a marginal or noise-driven effect.

Distinct and context-dependent transcriptional associations between prioritized candidates and VEGFA

To assess how framework-prioritized candidates relate to the canonical VEGF-centered angiogenic axis, gene-wise correlations with VEGFA expression were evaluated across tumor samples. Spearman correlation analysis revealed distinct association patterns among the examined genes (Fig. 2c). Among the prioritized candidates, COL14A1 showed a moderate positive correlation with VEGFA expression, whereas CD8A exhibited only a weak association. CDH2 did not cluster with the strongest VEGFA-coupled canonical angiogenesis genes such as KDR and CDH5, indicating a non-identical transcriptional relationship with VEGFA-associated programs. Together, these patterns suggest that framework-prioritized candidates differ in their degree of transcriptional coupling to the canonical VEGF axis, with CDH2 representing a partially overlapping but non-uniform candidate rather than a direct surrogate of classical VEGF signaling.

Candidate-specific functional contexts revealed by directional enrichment profiles

To contextualize the observed transcriptional behaviors, Hallmark gene set enrichment results were summarized into major functional directions, including immune-related, vascular-related, adhesion-related, metabolic, proliferative, and other biological processes. As shown in Fig. 2d, framework-prioritized candidates and canonical angiogenesis-related genes exhibited distinct and gene-specific directional enrichment profiles.

Consistent with its established immunological context, CD8A showed predominant enrichment in immune-associated programs. In contrast, COL14A1 and CDH2 displayed stronger enrichment in adhesion- and vascular-related pathways, whereas VEGFA and KDR exhibited broader enrichment profiles spanning multiple angiogenesis-associated functional categories. These results indicate that framework-prioritized candidates engage biologically plausible but non-identical transcriptional programs relevant to angiogenesis.

Integrated transcriptomic features highlight CDH2 as a representative non-canonical candidate

Across pan-cancer differential expression, intra-tumoral stratification, transcriptional association with VEGFA, and directional functional enrichment analyses, the framework-prioritized candidates exhibited both shared and distinct transcriptomic characteristics. Among these candidates, CDH2 consistently combined pronounced intra-tumoral expression heterogeneity, preferential enrichment in adhesion- and vascular-related functional programs, and a context-dependent relationship with VEGFA-associated transcriptional programs. Although CDH2 was not the only candidate prioritized by the framework, the convergence of these transcriptomic features rendered it a stable and interpretable representative of non-canonical angiogenesis-associated regulation. Accordingly, CDH2 was selected as an exemplar for subsequent analyses focusing on prognostic relevance and tumor microenvironmental context, without implying definitive mechanistic or therapeutic roles.

Divergent biological and clinical dimensions among angiogenesis-associated candidates

Pan-cancer expression divergence between CDH2 and VEGFA

To examine whether framework-prioritized candidates capture transcriptomic dimensions distinct from canonical VEGF-centered angiogenic signaling, pan-cancer tumor–normal differential expression analyses were performed across multiple cancer types. As shown in Fig. 3a, CDH2 exhibited widespread tumor-associated upregulation across a broad range of malignancies. Large positive standardized effect sizes were observed in several cancer types, including brain, kidney, skin, uterus, and breast cancers (Hedges’ g > 1.0, false discovery rate < 0.001).

In contrast, VEGFA displayed heterogeneous and context-dependent expression patterns across cancers. Distinct prognostic landscapes associated with CDH2 and VEGFA

To assess whether transcriptomic divergence between CDH2 and VEGFA was accompanied by differences in clinical associations, pan-cancer survival analyses were conducted using univariate Cox proportional hazards regression. Hazard ratios were summarized for cancer types meeting predefined inclusion criteria (minimum sample size ≥ 30 and Cox regression P < 0.05), and results were visualized alongside VEGFA for comparative reference (Fig. 3b).

Across cancer types, CDH2 exhibited heterogeneous and context-dependent prognostic associations. Elevated CDH2 expression was associated with increased mortality risk (hazard ratio > 1) in several malignancies, including bladder cancer (BLCA) and low-grade glioma (LGG), while showing neutral or modest effects in other cancer types. VEGFA displayed a partially overlapping but distinct prognostic landscape, with differences in both effect direction and magnitude relative to CDH2.

Cancer types not meeting statistical or sample-size thresholds were excluded from modeling and are shown as blank entries in Fig. 3b. Complete pan-cancer Cox regression statistics are provided in Additional file 5. These results indicate that angiogenesis-associated candidates can encode non-redundant clinical associations across tumor contexts.

Kaplan–Meier analyses illustrate non-overlapping survival associations

Based on the pan-cancer Cox regression results, bladder cancer (BLCA) and low-grade glioma (LGG) were selected for Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, as both cancer types satisfied inclusion criteria and demonstrated consistent hazard ratio patterns in Fig. 3b. Patients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups according to median gene expression levels.

As shown in Fig. 3c, high CDH2 expression was significantly associated with poorer overall survival in both BLCA and LGG (log-rank test, P < 0.05). VEGFA also showed significant survival associations in these cancer types; however, the separation patterns and temporal dynamics of the survival curves differed from those observed for CDH2. Kaplan–Meier survival analyses for additional cancer types stratified by CDH2 and VEGFA expression are provided in Additional file 6 and Additional file 7, respectively.

CDH2-associated tumor microenvironmental features

To characterize tumor microenvironmental features associated with divergent angiogenesis-related candidates, immune and stromal transcriptional signatures were examined in tumors stratified by CDH2 expression. As shown in Fig. 3d, CDH2-high tumors exhibited reduced enrichment of multiple lymphocyte-associated signatures, including T-cell receptor signaling, cytotoxic lymphocyte markers, and composite T- and B-cell scores (all P < 0.001).

In parallel, signatures related to immune activation and antigen presentation, including interferon response modules and major histocompatibility complex–associated scores, were also reduced in the CDH2-high group. Conversely, CDH2-high tumors showed relatively higher enrichment of stromal- and myeloid-associated programs, including tumor-associated macrophage–related signatures, TREM1-associated inflammatory signaling, and wound-healing–related pathways.

Contrasting immune-associated contexts defined by CDH2 and VEGFA

To directly compare immune-associated contexts linked to CDH2 and VEGFA expression, differences in immune signature enrichment between high- and low-expression groups were evaluated for each gene (Fig. 3e). VEGFA-high tumors exhibited enrichment of multiple immune-related signatures, including interferon signaling, antigen presentation pathways, and immune checkpoint–associated programs.

In contrast, CDH2-high tumors displayed a distinct immune signature profile. For several immune-related signatures, enrichment observed in VEGFA-high tumors was attenuated or absent in CDH2-high tumors, with some signatures showing inverse trends. A comprehensive comparison of immune- and stromal-related transcriptional signatures is provided in Additional file 8, with detailed signature annotations summarized in Additional file 9.

Functional annotation of CDH2-associated transcriptional programs

Functional enrichment of CDH2-correlated genes highlights calcium-dependent adhesion and intercellular communication

To annotate the functional context associated with elevated CDH2 expression, enrichment analyses were performed on genes positively correlated with CDH2 using Gene Ontology (GO) biological process and KEGG pathway annotations. These analyses were conducted to characterize recurrent biological themes within CDH2-associated transcriptional programs, rather than to infer direct regulatory mechanisms.

GO biological process enrichment showed that CDH2-associated genes were predominantly enriched in categories related to cell–cell adhesion and junctional organization, including cell–cell adhesion via plasma membrane adhesion molecules, cell junction assembly, and calcium-dependent cell–cell adhesion (Fig. 4a; Additional file 10). These terms are consistent with cadherin-mediated adhesion processes and reflect transcriptional programs related to calcium-regulated intercellular contacts.

In addition to classical adhesion-related categories, several enriched GO terms contained synapse- or neurotransmission-associated terminology, such as chemical synaptic transmission, synapse organization, and regulation of neurotransmitter receptor activity (Fig. 4a). These annotations represent shared functional components—including receptor–ligand interactions, ion channel activity, and calcium flux—rather than neuronal lineage specification, and are commonly utilized across diverse non-neuronal tissues to coordinate cell–cell communication and spatial organization.

Consistent with GO enrichment results, KEGG pathway analysis highlighted pathways related to intercellular signaling and guidance, including neuroactive ligand–receptor interaction, calcium signaling pathway, and axon guidance (Fig. 4b; Additional file 11). In addition, ECM–receptor interaction emerged among enriched pathways, linking CDH2-associated transcriptional programs to cell–matrix engagement and tissue organization.

Hallmark gene set enrichment associates CDH2 expression with tissue remodeling and angiogenesis-related programs

To assess whether CDH2-associated transcriptional changes converge on coordinated biological states relevant to tumor microenvironmental organization, gene set enrichment analysis was performed using MSigDB Hallmark gene sets based on genome-wide gene ranking between CDH2-high and CDH2-low tumors.

Hallmark enrichment analysis indicated that CDH2-high tumors were enriched for pathways related to tissue remodeling and microenvironmental organization, including epithelial–mesenchymal transition, angiogenesis, coagulation, bile acid metabolism, and myogenesis (Fig. 4c; Additional file 12). In contrast, CDH2-low tumors showed relative enrichment of stress- and tumor-suppressive pathways, including the p53 pathway and IL6–JAK–STAT3 signaling.

Although angiogenesis was not the highest-ranked Hallmark pathway by normalized enrichment score, its significant enrichment in CDH2-high tumors indicated a reproducible association between CDH2 expression and angiogenesis-related transcriptional activity. To further characterize this association, the Hallmark Angiogenesis gene set was examined in detail using preranked gene set enrichment analysis.

As shown in Fig. 4d, angiogenesis-associated genes were significantly enriched toward the top of the ranked gene list in CDH2-high tumors (normalized enrichment score = 1.397; false discovery rate = 0.028), reflecting coordinated transcriptional shifts associated with elevated CDH2 expression.

Summary of functional annotation patterns associated with CDH2 expression

Integration of GO, KEGG, and Hallmark enrichment analyses indicates that CDH2-associated transcriptional programs are characterized by enrichment of genes related to calcium-dependent adhesion, intercellular communication, extracellular matrix engagement, and tissue organization. These results represent functional annotation of correlated gene expression patterns and do not imply direct mechanistic regulation.

Within this analytical context, angiogenesis-related gene sets appear alongside broader structural and microenvironmental programs, illustrating that angiogenesis-associated transcriptional activity can be embedded within adhesion- and matrix-related biological contexts beyond canonical growth factor–centered signaling. CDH2 is presented here as a representative framework-prioritized candidate to illustrate this pattern, without implying a definitive mechanistic role.

CDH2-associated transcriptional signatures reflect coordinated tumor microenvironmental patterns

To examine whether CDH2-associated microenvironmental features reflect preferential alignment with specific cellular compartments or broader transcriptional patterns, bulk RNA-seq–based cell-type signature analyses were performed, focusing on endothelial, cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF), and immune-related gene signatures.

Across tumor samples, CDH2 expression showed consistent inverse correlations with multiple endothelial, CAF, and immune transcriptional signatures (Fig. 5a). These associations were statistically significant after multiple testing correction (false discovery rate–adjusted P values < 1 × 10− 16; Additional file 13) and were directionally consistent across signature categories. This pattern indicates that elevated CDH2 expression is not selectively aligned with a single microenvironmental component but is associated with coordinated shifts across multiple transcriptional programs.

As shown in Fig. 5b, CDH2-high tumors exhibited significantly lower endothelial, CAF, and immune signature scores compared with CDH2-low tumors (two-sided Mann–Whitney U test, false discovery rate–adjusted P values < 1 × 10− 16; Additional file 14). The concurrent reduction observed across multiple signature classes suggests that CDH2-associated transcriptional profiles reflect broad microenvironmental transcriptional patterns rather than isolated changes within individual signature categories.

Together, these analyses complement the functional annotation results presented in Fig. 4 by demonstrating that CDH2-associated angiogenesis-related transcriptional activity is accompanied by coordinated alterations across endothelial, stromal, and immune-related signature programs. These findings describe transcriptional associations at the bulk-tissue level and do not imply proportional changes in specific cellular populations.

External validation in independent glioma cohorts

To assess the robustness and generalizability of the framework-derived findings, we performed an additional external validation using independent glioma cohorts. First, the relationship between CDH2 and VEGFA was examined in three independent datasets (GSE147352, GSE121720, and GSE111260). Across these cohorts, the CDH2–VEGFA association was not uniform, but varied by cohort composition and disease context, with stronger positive associations in glioblastoma-dominant settings and weaker or inverse associations in lower-grade glioma subsets, supporting a context-dependent rather than invariant VEGFA-centered relationship (Additional file 15 and Additional file 16). Second, in two independent bulk glioma cohorts, stratification of tumors into CDH2-high and CDH2-low groups consistently showed higher ECM remodeling, adhesion/focal adhesion, EMT, and angiogenesis-related signature scores in the CDH2-high group, indicating that the structural programs highlighted in the discovery analysis were reproducible across external datasets (Additional file 17 and Additional file 18). Third, in the independent CGGA mRNAseq_693 cohort, higher CDH2 expression was associated with worse overall survival in an automatically defined LGG-preferred subset, as supported by Kaplan–Meier analysis, univariable Cox regression, and multivariable Cox regression adjusted for age, grade, PRS type, IDH mutation status, and 1p/19q codeletion status (Additional file 19 and Additional file 20). Collectively, these results provide independent support for the robustness of the proposed framework and the generalizability of CDH2-associated transcriptional and clinical patterns across external glioma datasets.

Discussion

Angiogenesis is a complex biological process that is frequently interrogated in cancer transcriptomic studies, yet systematic computational prioritization of angiogenesis-associated genes remains challenging [1, 2, 32]. Many existing analyses rely on predefined gene sets or canonical pathway assumptions, which can constrain discovery and complicate integration of heterogeneous evidence across tumor contexts [6, 7]. In this study, we present a literature-guided integrative transcriptomic analysis framework designed to prioritize angiogenesis-associated candidates across cancers by combining multiple, independent evidence layers—including longitudinal literature signals, pan-cancer expression patterns, functional annotation, survival associations, and tumor microenvironment–related transcriptional signatures—within an interpretable and reproducible analytical procedure.

A central feature of the proposed framework is that candidate prioritization does not depend on prespecifying “known angiogenesis genes.” Instead, candidates emerge through convergence across orthogonal analytical layers, reducing selection bias and limiting dependence on any single dataset, tumor type, or analytical assumption. This design is particularly relevant for angiogenesis, where transcriptomic correlates may reflect diverse and context-dependent biological programs beyond a single growth factor–centered signaling axis. The two-dimensional literature-derived scoring strategy, which integrates quantitative publication dynamics with structural repositioning in the literature, provides a model-agnostic means of incorporating knowledge evolution into candidate ranking. In doing so, it complements transcriptome-based analyses and improves interpretability relative to purely frequency-driven or pathway-constrained approaches.

Within this analytical framework, multiple high-ranking candidates were prioritized, and subsequent analyses focused on characterizing transcriptomic orientation and contextual specificity rather than effect size alone. For example, CD8A exhibited immune-dominant enrichment patterns consistent with its established association with cytotoxic T-cell–related transcriptional programs [33], whereas COL14A1 showed broader stromal and extracellular matrix–associated signatures without a clear vascular-oriented context [4]. In contrast, CDH2 displayed a coherent transcriptional profile characterized by enrichment in adhesion- and structural remodeling–related programs, a context-dependent and partially overlapping relationship with VEGFA-associated transcriptional patterns, and microenvironmental signature patterns distinct from immune-dominant states [13, 34]. CDH2 is presented here as an exemplar to illustrate how the framework can surface non-canonical angiogenesis-associated dimensions that may be underrepresented in canonical gene lists, rather than as a definitive mechanistic or therapeutic target.

The observed non-uniform relationship between CDH2 and VEGFA further illustrates the value of prioritization strategies that do not equate angiogenesis exclusively with VEGF-centered programs [35–37]. Across cancer types, CDH2 showed widespread tumor-associated upregulation with heterogeneous expression trends that, in several contexts, contrasted with those of VEGFA. In addition, external validation across independent glioma cohorts showed that the CDH2–VEGFA association varied according to cohort composition and disease context, with stronger positive associations in glioblastoma-dominant settings and weaker or inverse associations in lower-grade glioma subsets (Additional file 15 and Additional file 16). Together, these observations indicate that angiogenesis-associated candidates can encode separable but partially overlapping transcriptomic and clinical dimensions, reinforcing the importance of integrative, multi-layer evaluation when prioritizing candidates from large-scale data resources.

Functional annotation analyses provided additional contextualization of CDH2-associated transcriptional programs, revealing enrichment in calcium-dependent adhesion, junctional organization, extracellular matrix engagement, and conserved intercellular communication pathways. These enrichment patterns offer a coherent annotation framework consistent with structural and microenvironmental organization and align with the observation that CDH2-associated angiogenesis-related signals manifest as coordinated transcriptional patterns rather than proportional changes in individual cell-type abundance. Importantly, associations derived from bulk RNA-seq–based signatures should be interpreted as state-level transcriptional features that integrate both cellular composition and regulatory activity, rather than direct measures of cell numbers. Within this scope, the framework enables prioritization of candidates linked to tumor microenvironmental organization without requiring explicit cell-type deconvolution assumptions [21].

The external validation analyses further strengthened the robustness of these observations. In two independent bulk glioma cohorts, tumors stratified into CDH2-high and CDH2-low groups consistently reproduced higher ECM remodeling, adhesion/focal adhesion, EMT, and angiogenesis-related signature scores in the CDH2-high group (Additional file 17 and Additional file 18). In addition, in the independent CGGA mRNAseq_693 cohort, higher CDH2 expression remained associated with worse overall survival in an automatically defined LGG-preferred subset, as supported by Kaplan–Meier analysis, univariable Cox regression, and multivariable Cox regression adjusted for major clinicomolecular covariates (Additional file 19 and Additional file 20). Together, these external data support that CDH2 captures a reproducible yet context-dependent structural and clinical dimension beyond the original discovery dataset.

More specifically, a logical next step for experimental validation would be perturbation-based testing of CDH2 in relevant tumor models. In vitro, CDH2 knockdown or overexpression could be combined with assessment of representative adhesion-, extracellular matrix remodeling-, and EMT-related markers, together with migration/invasion assays and endothelial tube formation or tumor cell–endothelial co-culture assays to examine angiogenesis-associated functional effects. In vivo, xenograft or orthotopic models with altered CDH2 expression could be used to evaluate tumor growth, vascular density, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Such experiments would help determine whether the non-canonical transcriptional programs highlighted by the framework correspond to functionally testable biological effects.

Several limitations of this analysis should be acknowledged. First, the study is based on retrospective analyses of publicly available datasets and therefore identifies reproducible associations rather than causal mechanisms. In addition, although the TCGA/TARGET/GTEx data used here were obtained from the UCSC Xena Toil recompute resource, which applies a unified computational processing workflow, residual heterogeneity related to dataset origin, tissue procurement, and cohort composition may still influence tumor–normal comparisons. Therefore, these analyses should be interpreted as comparative transcriptomic screening rather than fully batch-free estimates of biological effect size. Second, literature-derived signals may be influenced by community attention, terminology shifts, and publication bias, although integration of structural repositioning metrics and independent transcriptomic layers helps mitigate these effects. Third, bulk transcriptomic data do not resolve spatial context or direct cell–cell interactions; future extensions incorporating spatial transcriptomics, single-cell profiling, or explicitly modeled cell-state frameworks could improve mechanistic resolution and further assess context-specific candidate behavior [38]. Finally, although the revised study now includes external validation in multiple independent glioma cohorts, angiogenesis was still used here as a representative biological process and CDH2 as an illustrative exemplar. Broader application of the framework to additional biological processes, disease settings, and external datasets will still be required to fully characterize its generalizability.

In summary, this study introduces a literature-guided integrative transcriptomic analysis framework for prioritizing angiogenesis-associated genes across cancers. By integrating knowledge evolution signals with multi-layer transcriptomic and functional evidence, the framework enables candidate prioritization beyond canonical ligand–receptor paradigms and highlights non-canonical, structure-oriented angiogenesis-associated transcriptional patterns. Rather than asserting direct mechanistic causality, this work consolidates fragmented evidence into a unified computational procedure, providing a generalizable analytical strategy for candidate prioritization in complex biological processes using public biomedical data.

The angiogenesis-related literature corpus was derived from publications indexed in the PubMed database using a predefined and reproducible search strategy. The detailed search strategy is provided in Additional file 1. Retrieved records were processed, merged across years, and deduplicated by PMID to generate the final literature corpus used for downstream analyses. Gene set enrichment analyses were performed using gene set libraries from the Enrichr platform (GO_Biological_Process_2021 and KEGG_2021_Human). GEO datasets were used for independent validation of CDH2–VEGFA association patterns and CDH2-associated structural transcriptional programs, whereas the CGGA dataset was used for external validation of prognostic relevance. Data Availability Statement

The transcriptomic and clinical data analysed in this study are publicly available from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and were accessed via the UCSC Xena platform (https://xenabrowser.net/). Specifically, RNA-seq expression matrices generated using the Toil RNA-seq recompute pipeline were used, including the datasets TcgaTargetGtex_rsem_gene_fpkm and TcgaTargetGtex_rsem_gene_tpm, together with the corresponding sample annotation file TcgaTargetGTEX_phenotype.txt. The dataset includes samples from multiple TCGA cancer cohorts, including TCGA-BRCA, TCGA-LUAD, TCGA-LUSC, TCGA-LIHC, TCGA-COAD, TCGA-READ, TCGA-STAD, TCGA-ESCA, TCGA-HNSC, TCGA-KIRC, TCGA-KIRP, TCGA-KICH, TCGA-BLCA, TCGA-UCEC, TCGA-OV, TCGA-PRAD, TCGA-THCA, TCGA-SKCM, TCGA-SARC, TCGA-PAAD, TCGA-ACC, TCGA-CHOL, TCGA-DLBC, TCGA-MESO, TCGA-PCPG, TCGA-TGCT, TCGA-UCS, TCGA-UVM, TCGA-GBM, and TCGA-LGG. The angiogenesis-related literature corpus was derived from publications indexed in the PubMed database using a predefined and reproducible search strategy. The detailed search strategy is provided in Additional file 1. Retrieved records were processed, merged across years, and deduplicated by PMID to generate the final literature corpus used for downstream analyses. Gene set enrichment analyses were performed using gene set libraries from the Enrichr platform (GO_Biological_Process_2021 and KEGG_2021_Human). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the MSigDB Hallmark gene set collection (Hallmark 2020 release; MSigDB_Hallmark_2020). For external validation analyses, publicly available glioma datasets were additionally obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), including GSE147352, GSE121720, and GSE111260, and from the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA), including the mRNAseq_693 cohort with matched clinical annotations. GEO datasets were used for independent validation of CDH2–VEGFA association patterns and CDH2-associated structural transcriptional programs, whereas the CGGA dataset was used for external validation of prognostic relevance. All external validation datasets were publicly accessible at the time of analysis and were used in accordance with the data usage policies of their original sources. No new datasets were generated during this study. All processed results generated during the analyses, including literature-derived gene corpora, integrated prioritization scores, survival analysis outputs, immune and stromal signature statistics, and cell-type signature statistics, are included in this published article and its supplementary information files.

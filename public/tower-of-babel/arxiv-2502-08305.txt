On additive convolution sum of arithmetic functions and related questions
Bikram Misra , Biswajyoti Saha and Anubhav Sharma

Abstract
. Ingham studied two types of convolution sums of the divisor function, namely the shifted convolution sum ∑ n ≤ N d ⁡ ( n ) ​ d ​ ( n + h ) \sum_{n\leq N}d(n)d(n+h) and the additive convolution sum ∑ n < N d ⁡ ( n ) ​ d ​ ( N − n ) \sum_{n<N}d(n)d(N-n) for integers N , h N,h and derived their asymptotic formulas as N → ∞ N\to\infty . There have been numerous works extending Ingham’s work on this convolution sum, but only little has been done towards the additive convolution sum. In this article, we extend the classical result Ingham to derive an asymptotic formula with an error term of the sub-sum ∑ n < M d ⁡ ( n ) ​ d ​ ( N − n ) \sum_{n<M}d(n)d(N-n) for an integer M ≤ N M\leq N . Using this, we study the convolution sum ∑ n < M f ⁡ ( n ) ​ g ​ ( N − n ) \sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n) for certain arithmetic functions f f and g g with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions, which in turm leads us to a well-established prediction of Ramanujan.

1.
Introduction

Convolution sums are ubiquitous in number theory and they play very crucial role
in numerous arithmetic problems. We give two such instances coming from the
two of the long-standing open problems in number theory.
In the context of the twin prime conjecture, one
considers the shifted convolution sum

\sum_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h),

where
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function,
h
is a positive even integer
and
N
an integer. Note that in the above sum,
the summand is zero unless both
n
and
n+h
are prime powers and the contribution of the prime
powers is small compared to the contribution of the primes themselves. Hence an asymptotic estimate
of this sum would enable us to deduce the infinitude of the prime pairs of the form
(n,n+h)
,
giving us the twin prime conjecture by putting
h=2
.

The next instance is in the context of the Goldbach conjecture. Here one considers the additive convolution sum

\sum_{n<N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(N-n),

for an even positive integer
N
. Note that this sum counts the number of ways
N
can be written as a sum of two
prime powers. So in this case an asymptotic estimate of the above sum leads to the Goldbach conjecture.
Moreover, here it is also relevant to consider the sub-sum

\sum_{n\leq M}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(N-n)

where
M\leq N/2
. To elaborate, let
N\geq 4
be a positive even integer which can be written
as a sum of two primes
p_{1},p_{2}
. We refer to
p_{1},p_{2}
as prime summands of
N
. Clearly,
\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\leq N/2
.
So assuming the Goldbach conjecture, we are assured to get a prime summand of
N
which
is
\leq N/2
. Note that it may be possible to write
N
as a sum of two primes in many ways,
for example,
14=3+11=7+7
. Hence for determining the least possible prime summand of
N
,
one can consider the sum
\sum_{n\leq M}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(N-n)
and study
its behaviour with respect to both
M
and
N
, where
M
is a parameter
\leq N/2
. The study of
such convolution sub-sums can also be motivated by the following theorem of Chen
[
2
]
that every sufficiently
large even number can be written as a sum of a prime and a semi-prime. By a semi-prime we mean a positive integer
which is product of at most two primes. Chen’s result
was further strengthened by Cai
[
1
]
to show that every sufficiently large even integer
n
is a sum of a prime

\leq n^{0.95}
and a semi-prime.

Also, if we are to study the sums of the form
\sum_{n<N}f(n)g(N-n)h(n)
for some suitable arithmetic functions

f,g
and
h
, then we can estimate such a sum using the partial summation formula if we have information about
the sub-sum
\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)
for
M\leq N
.
Hence in this article, we take up the study of additive convolution sub-sum
\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)

of arithmetic functions
f,g
for integers
M\leq N
. We begin with the divisor function and the classical result of Ingham
[
11
]
.
In the seminal paper
[
11
]
, Ingham studied the following convolution sums of the divisor function

d(n)=\sum_{d\mid n\atop d>0}1
. He proved the following asymptotic formulas:

(1)

\sum_{n\leq N}d(n)d(n+h)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sigma_{-1}(h)N\log^{2}N+O(N\log N)

for
N\to\infty
and

(2)

\sum_{n<N}d(n)d(N-n)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sigma_{1}(N)\log^{2}N+O(\sigma_{1}(N)\log N\log\log N)

for
N\to\infty
, where
\displaystyle{\sigma_{s}(n):=\sum_{d\mid n\atop d>0}d^{s}}
for a complex number
s
.

This fundamental work
[
11
]
has been a central force of developments of many key concepts
in the study of the mean values of some important
L
-functions (for example
[
4
,
12
]
) and
further led to many important studies of critical values, shifted convolution sums and subconvexity questions.
However, as Motohashi
[
12
]
writes, the research on the additive convolution sums has not been
as intensive as on the shifted convolution sums, perhaps because of no apparent relation
with the mean values of
L
-functions. Estermann
[
5
]
provided the secondary main terms in (
2
), using methods different
from
[
11
]
.

In this article, we particularly consider the last formula (
2
) and derive an extension. Note that,
except perhaps one summand of
\sum_{n<N}d(n)d(N-n)

in the middle (which has insignificant contribution), all the summands appear twice and
due to the palindromic nature of the sum, the asymptotic formula (
2
) is equivalent to

(3)

\sum_{n\leq N/2}d(n)d(N-n)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\sigma_{1}(N)\log^{2}N+O(\sigma_{1}(N)\log N\log\log N)

for
N\to\infty
. Taking motivation from our earlier discussions, we find that it is interesting to consider the sub-sum

\sum_{n\leq M}d(n)d(N-n)
for an integer
M\leq N
. One may tend to expect the contribution of the
sub-sum to be
M/N
times that of the complete sum. However, in addition we will also get an
appropriate change in the logarithmic factor in the main term.

For
M\leq N/2
sufficiently large, we first derive an
asymptotic formula of
\sum_{n\leq M}d(n)d(N-n)
with an error term which depends on
N
and
M
.
More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 1
.

For
M
sufficiently large such that
1\leq M\leq N/2
, we have

(4)

\sum_{n\leq M}d(n)d(N-n)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log^{2}X+O(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log N\log\log N),

where
X=\sqrt{M(N-M)}
.

Remark 1
.

If we put
M=N/2
in Theorem
1
, we get back Ingham’s result (
3
).

Note that, for any real number
M
such that
1\leq M\leq N
, we have

\sum_{n<M}d(n)d(N-n)=\sum_{n<N}d(n)d(N-n)-\sum_{n\leq N-M}d(n)d(N-n).

Now if
N/2\leq M\leq N
, we have
N-M\leq N/2
. So as a corollary to Theorem
1
, we get the following:

Corollary 1
.

For any sufficiently large real number
M
such that
N/2\leq M\leq N
, we have

(5)

\sum_{n<M}d(n)d(N-n)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\sigma_{-1}(N)\left(N\log^{2}N-(N-M)\log^{2}X\right)+O(\sigma_{1}(N)\log N\log\log N),

where
X=\sqrt{M(N-M)}
.

The above statements give key insights regarding the sub-sum
\sum_{n\leq M}d(n)d(N-n)
in comparison with the
full-sum
\sum_{n<N}d(n)d(N-n)
. For example, if
M\sim cN
for some
0<c<1
, then asymptotically the sub-sum is

c
times the full-sum. Further, if
M\sim cN^{\epsilon}
for some
0<\epsilon<1
and
c>0
, then asymptotically the sub-sum is

cN^{\epsilon-1}\left(\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}\right)^{2}
times the full-sum. Similarly, if
M\sim c\log^{\alpha}N
for some
c,\alpha>0
,
then asymptotically the sub-sum is
c\frac{\log^{\alpha}N}{4N}
times the full-sum.

Next we consider such additive convolution sums for general arithmetic functions. To make a meaningful statement, we
need some conditions on these functions. Here we consider
arithmetic functions with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions. In
[
16
]
, Ramanujan studied the so-called
Ramanujan sums and initiated the theory of Ramanujan expansions.
For positive integers
r,n
, Ramanujan considered the sum

c_{r}(n):=\sum_{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}}\zeta_{r}^{an},

where
\zeta_{r}
denotes a primitive
r
-th root of unity.
Since then, these sums are called Ramanujan sums. It is possible to express
c_{r}(n)
in
terms of the Möbius function
\mu
as follows:

(6)

c_{r}(n)=\sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,r)}\mu\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)d.

Ramanujan studied these sums to derive point-wise convergent series expansion of the form

\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
for various arithmetic functions.
These expansions are now known as Ramanujan expansions. More precisely:

Definition 1
.

We say that an arithmetic function
f

admits a Ramanujan expansion if for each
n
,

f(n)
can be written as a convergent series
of the form

f(n)=\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)c_{r}(n)

for appropriate complex numbers
a_{f}(r)
.
The number
a_{f}(r)
is said to be the
r
-th Ramanujan coefficient
of
f
with respect to this expansion.

Ramanujan
[
16
]
showed that, for a real variable
s>0
,

(7)

\frac{\sigma_{s}(n)}{n^{s}}=\zeta(s+1)\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{c_{r}(n)}{r^{s+1}}.

Here
\zeta(s)
denotes the Riemann zeta function. Also, for the divisor function
d(n)
one has,

d(n)=-\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\log r}{r}c_{r}(n).

Ramanujan himself observed
[
16
]
that such an expansion
is not necessarily unique. For example, we have

\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{c_{r}(n)}{r}=0,

and this equation can be viewed as a Ramanujan expansion of the zero function.
For an exposition on Ramanujan sums and Ramanujan expansions, we refer to
[
13
]
.

For the von Mangoldt function, Hardy
[
10
]
proved that

\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\Lambda(n)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}{\mu(r)\over\phi(r)}c_{r}(n),

where
\phi
denotes the Euler-
\phi
function.
The series on the right hand side is conditionally convergent, making it difficult to use
for estimation purposes.
In
[
7
]
, Gadiyar and Padma showed that if we ignore the convergence issues,
Hardy’s formula leads to the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture about prime pairs.
For this, one needs Carmichael’s orthogonality property of Ramanujan sums,
which can be stated using the Kronecker delta function as follows:

\sum_{n\leq N}c_{r}(n)c_{s}(n+h)=\delta_{r,s}Nc_{r}(h)+O(rs\log rs)

for
N\to\infty
(see
[
6
, Lemma 2]
). Using this, one can derive the heuristic result that

\sum_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)\sim N\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}{\mu^{2}(r)\over\phi(r)}c_{r}(h),

for
N\to\infty
. This conjectural formula was predicted by Hardy and Littlewood with the help of the circle method.
Along the similar lines, using the orthogonality property

\sum_{n<N}c_{r}(n)c_{s}(N-n)=\delta_{r,s}Nc_{r}(N)+O(rs\log rs)

for
N\to\infty
(see
[
6
, Lemma 9 (a)]
), one can derive the heuristic result that

\sum_{n<N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(N-n)\sim N\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}{\mu^{2}(r)\over\phi(r)}c_{r}(N),

as
N\to\infty
.

A systematic study of the shifted convolution sum
\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h)

for certain arithmetic functions
f
and
g
with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions
has been carried out in
[
6
,
14
,
3
,
17
]
. However, the study of the additive convolution sum

\sum_{n\leq M}f(n)g(N-n)
for a real number
M<N
and such arithmetic functions
f
and
g

has not been carried out with great detail, except for an indicative statement in
[
6
, Theorem 10]
.
In
[
3
,
17
]
, a key step for obtaining the asymptotic formulas of
\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h)
with error terms
involved the use of Ingham’s formula (
1
). Using our extensions (
4
) and
(
5
) of Ingham’s formula (
2
), we derive an asymptotic formula with an error term
for the additive convolution sum
\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)
for a real number
M\leq N
and certain arithmetic functions
f
and
g

with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions with Ramanujan coefficients having appropriate growth conditions.
More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
and
g
be two arithmetic functions with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions:

f(n)=\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)c_{r}(n),\quad g(n)=\sum_{s\geq 1}a_{g}(s)c_{s}(n).

Suppose that
|a_{f}(r)|,|a_{g}(r)|\ll r^{-(1+\delta)}
for some
\delta>0
. For
M<N

sufficiently large, we have

\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)=\begin{cases}M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(M^{1-\delta}(\log M)^{4-2\delta})&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(\log^{3}M)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$},\end{cases}

where the implied constants depend on
N
.

In the following section we first present a proof of Theorem
1
. Then in Section 3, we give a proof of Theorem
2
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

For the proof of Theorem
1
, we follow Ingham’s idea, but we need to make
important changes as we go ahead. The proof also highlights why it has been important to restrict
our statement for
M\leq N/2
.
We write the sum

\sum_{n\leq M}d(n)d(N-n)=\sum_{n\leq M}\left(\sum_{m\mid n}1\right)\left(\sum_{l\mid N-n}1\right).

So this is same as counting the number of
4
-tuples
(l,m,r,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}
satisfying
lr+ms=N

such that
n\leq M
, where
n=ms
and
N-n=lr
.
Hence our goal is to find the cardinality of the set

S=\{(l,r,m,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}\mid lr+ms=N;\ ms\leq M\}.

Now,

lm\cdot rs=ms\cdot lr=n(N-n).

Since
n\leq M
, we get
N-n\geq N-M
. Note that this does not offer any upper bound for the product
n(N-n)
.
Using AM-GM inequality, Ingham
[
11
]
obtained the upper bound
N^{2}/4
. Since we have the restriction

n\leq M
, this upper bound is not optimal in terms of
M
.
So we consider the function
f(x):=x(N-x)
on the interval
[0,N]
. This function is increasing on the sub-interval
[0,N/2]

(and hence on
[0,M]
as
M\leq N/2
). We therefore get the bound

lm\cdot rs=ms\cdot lr=n(N-n)\leq M(N-M).

For
n=M
(when
M
is an integer), this upper bound is achieved, and hence this bound is optimal.
Denote
X:=\sqrt{M(N-M)}
. Since
lm\cdot rs\leq X^{2}
,
at least one of the inequalities
lm\leq X
and
rs\leq X
must be satisfied.

So, we have
|S|=|P|+|Q|-|R|
, where

P=\{(l,r,m,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}\mid lr+ms=N;\ ms\leq M;\ lm\leq X\},

Q=\{(l,r,m,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}\mid lr+ms=N;\ ms\leq M;\ rs\leq X\},

and

R=\{(l,r,m,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{4}\mid lr+ms=N;\ ms\leq M;\ lm,rs\leq X\}.

Since by symmetry, we have
|Q|=|P|
, we can write

(8)

|S|=2|P|-|R|.

We have

|P|=\sum_{lm\leq X}\ \sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r,s>0\atop lr+ms=N}}{{ms\leq M}}}1=\sum_{{lm\leq X}\atop{m\leq M}}\ \sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r,s>0\atop lr+ms=N}}{{s\leq\frac{M}{m}}}}1.

Note that we can drop the condition
m\leq M
in the outer sum, because
if
m>M
, then
s
satisfies
0<s\leq M/m<1
, resulting an empty sum. Therefore,

(9)

|P|=\sum_{lm\leq X}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r,s>0\atop lr+ms=N}}{{s\leq\frac{M}{m}}}}1=\sum_{lm\leq X}A(l,m),

where
A(l,m)
counts the positive integer pairs
(r,s)
, which are the solutions of
lr+ms=N
such that

0<s\leq M/m
. Let
d=\gcd(l,m)
and write
l=d\lambda
,
m=d\mu
. If
d\nmid N
, then
A(l,m)=0
.
If
d\mid N
then we have choices of such pairs
(r,s)
. The general form of the solutions are as follows:

r=r_{0}+\mu h
and
s=s_{0}-\lambda h
,
where
(r_{0},s_{0})
is a particular solution and
h
is an arbitrary integer. Hence
A(l,m)
is equal to the number of integers
h
satisfying

(10)

r_{0}+\mu h>0\ \text{ and }\ s_{0}-\lambda h>0

together with

(11)

0<s_{0}-\lambda h\leq\frac{M}{m}.

From (
10
), we have

(12)

-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}<h<\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda},

and using (
11
), we have

(13)

\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}-\frac{M}{m\lambda}\leq h<\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}.

So,
A(l,m)
denotes the number of integers
h
satisfying (
12
) and (
13
) simultaneously. Hence
we count the number of
h
such that

\displaystyle\max\left(-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu},\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}-\frac{M}{m\lambda}\right)\leq h<\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}.

Note that since
lr_{0}+ms_{0}=N>M
, we have
\displaystyle{M<ms_{0}+\frac{r_{0}d\lambda\mu}{\mu}}
, hence

\displaystyle{-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}<\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}-\frac{M}{m\lambda}}
.
Therefore

A(l,m)=\left[\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}\right]-\left[\frac{s_{0}}{\lambda}-\frac{M}{m\lambda}\right]=\frac{M}{\lambda m}+O(1)=\frac{M}{d\lambda\mu}+O(1).

Substituting this in (
9
), we obtain

|P|=M\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\lambda\mu\leq\frac{X}{d^{2}}\atop(\lambda,\mu)=1}\frac{1}{\lambda\mu}=M\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d}\ \tau\left(\frac{X}{d^{2}}\right),

where
\displaystyle{\tau(y)=\sum_{\lambda\mu\leq y\atop(\lambda,\mu)=1}\frac{1}{\lambda\mu}}
.
From
[
11
, p. 203-204]
, we have (for sufficiently large
y
),

\tau(y)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\log^{2}y+O(\log y).

Hence we obtain that

\displaystyle|P|

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}M\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d}\log^{2}\left(\frac{X}{d^{2}}\right)+O\left(M\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d}\log\left(\frac{X}{d^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}M\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d}\left(\log^{2}X+4\log^{2}d-4(\log d)(\log X)\right)+O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log X\right)

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}M(\log X)^{2}\lambda_{0}(N)-\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}M(\log X)\lambda_{1}(N)+\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}M\lambda_{2}(N)+O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log X\right),

where
\displaystyle{\lambda_{0}(N)=\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{d},\lambda_{1}(N)=\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{\log d}{d}}
and
\displaystyle{\lambda_{2}(N)=\sum_{d|N\atop d\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{\log^{2}d}{d}}
.

Note that for
i=0,1,2
, in the sum
\lambda_{i}(N)
, we can remove the restriction
d\leq\sqrt{X}
as the resulting difference is bounded (in absolute value)
by
d(N)\log^{2}N/\sqrt{X}
. Since
X\geq\sqrt{N-1}
, this is negligible and we have

|P|=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}M(\log X)^{2}\sum_{d|N\atop}\frac{1}{d}-\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}M\log X\sum_{d|N\atop}\frac{\log d}{d}+\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}M\sum_{d|N\atop}\frac{\log^{2}d}{d}+O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log X\right).

This is clearly indicative of the terms to expect for an extension of Estermann’s work
[
5
]
in this aspect, which we do not take up here.
It follows from
[
11
, p. 208]
that

\sum_{d|N\atop}\frac{\log d}{d}=O(\sigma_{-1}(N)\log\log N).

Thus for
M
sufficiently large such that
1\leq M\leq N/2
, we get

(15)

|P|=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log^{2}X+O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log N\log\log N\right).

Now we estimate
|R|
, for which we write

|R|=\sum_{lm\leq X\atop ms\leq M}B(l,m),

where
B(l,m)
is the number of solutions (
r,s
) of
lr+ms=N
such that
r,s>0
and
rs\leq X
.
As before, let
d=\gcd(l,m)
and write
l=d\lambda
,
m=d\mu
.
Note that if
d\nmid N
, then
B(l,m)=0
. If
d\mid N
, it is equal to the number of integers
h
satisfying

(16)

r_{0}+\mu h>0\ \text{ and }\ s_{0}-\lambda h>0

such that

(17)

(r_{0}+\mu h)(s_{0}-\lambda h)\leq X,

where
(r_{0},s_{0})
is a particular solution.
Now since
l(r_{0}+\mu h)+m(s_{0}-\lambda h)=N
, we can write

r_{0}+\mu h=\frac{N}{l}u\text{ and }s_{0}-\lambda h=\frac{N}{m}(1-u)

and the above conditions are equivalent to the conditions

0<u<1,

and

(18)

-u^{2}+u-\frac{Xlm}{N^{2}}\leq 0.

Now, we consider the quadratic equation

-u^{2}+u-\frac{Xlm}{N^{2}}=0.

Note that its discriminant
\Delta=1-4Xlm/N^{2}
. Since
lm\leq X
and
X\leq N/2
, we get
\Delta\geq 0
.
Hence we can rewrite (
18
) as

\left(u-\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)\right)\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-j\right)-u\right)\leq 0,

where

j:=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{4Xlm}{N^{2}}}.

Note that
j\leq 1/2
. The above inequality says that exactly one of the inequalities

u-\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)\geq 0,\ \left(\frac{1}{2}-j\right)-u\geq 0

is true.
Since
0<u<1
, we have either

(19)

0<u\leq\frac{1}{2}-j,

or

(20)

\frac{1}{2}+j\leq u<1.

From (
19
), we have
0<r_{0}+\mu h\leq\frac{N}{l}\left(\frac{1}{2}-j\right)
. Therefore,

(21)

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}<h\leq\frac{N}{l\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}-j\right)-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}.

Note that the length of the half-open interval
\left(-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu},\frac{N}{l\mu}(\frac{1}{2}-j)-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}\right]
is

\frac{N}{l\mu}(\frac{1}{2}-j)
.
Similarly, from (
20
), we have

\frac{N}{l}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)<r_{0}+\mu h\leq\frac{N}{l}.

Therefore,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{N}{l\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}\leq h<\frac{N}{l\mu}-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}.

So, the length of the half-open interval
\left[\frac{N}{l\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu},\frac{N}{l\mu}-\frac{r_{0}}{\mu}\right)
is also

\frac{N}{l\mu}(\frac{1}{2}-j)
. Now

\frac{N}{l\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}-j\right)=\frac{Nd}{2lm}(1-2j)=\frac{Nd}{2lm}\frac{(1-4j^{2})}{(1+2j)}\leq\frac{Nd}{2lm}(1-4j^{2})=\frac{Nd}{2lm}\cdot\frac{4lmX}{N^{2}}=\frac{2dX}{N}.

So, the admissible values of
h
are thus confined to two (half-open) intervals each of which is of length at most
2dX/N
.
Hence
B(l,m)\leq{4dX}/{N}
, where
d=\gcd(l,m)
is a divisor of
N
. Thus

|R|\leq\sum_{lm\leq X}B(l,m)\leq\sum_{d|N\atop d<\sqrt{X}}\sum_{\lambda\mu\leq\frac{X}{d^{2}}\atop(\lambda,\mu)=1}\frac{4dX}{N}\leq\frac{4X}{N}\sum_{d|N}d\sum_{\lambda\mu\leq\frac{X}{d^{2}}}1=\frac{4X}{N}\sum_{d|N}d\sum_{\nu\leq\frac{X}{d^{2}}}d(\nu).

Using the average order of the divisor function, we get

|R|=O\left(\frac{X}{N}\sum_{d|N}\frac{X}{d}\log\left(\frac{X}{d^{2}}\right)\right)=O\left(\frac{X^{2}}{N}\log X\sum_{d|N}\frac{1}{d}\right)=O\left(\frac{X^{2}}{N}\sigma_{-1}(N)\log X\right).

Note that
X^{2}/N\leq M(N-M)/N\leq M
. Hence

(23)

|R|=O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log X\right).

Now using (
15
) and (
23
) in (
8
) we get

(24)

|S|=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log^{2}X+O\left(M\sigma_{-1}(N)\log N\log\log N\right),

for sufficiently large
M
such that
1\leq M\leq N/2
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2

For the proof of Theorem
2
, we need an orthogonality statement (see
[
6
, Lemma 9 (a)]
).

Lemma 1
.

For
M\leq N
,

\sum_{n<M}c_{r}(n)c_{s}(N-n)=\delta_{r,s}Mc_{r}(N)+O(rs\log(rs)),

where
\delta_{r,s}
denotes the Kronecker delta function.

Following the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
, let
U
be a parameter tending to infinity which is to be chosen later and we write

\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)=A+B,

where

A:=\sum_{n<M}\sum_{r,s\atop rs\leq U}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(s)c_{r}(n)c_{s}(N-n)\ \text{ and }\ B:=\sum_{n<M}\sum_{r,s\atop rs>U}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(s)c_{r}(n)c_{s}(N-n).

Now we use the growth conditions on
a_{f}(r)
and
a_{g}(s)
to interchange the summations and then Lemma
1
to get

A=C+D+O\left(\sum_{r,s\atop rs\leq U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}rs\log rs\right),

where
C=M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)
and
D=-M\sum_{r^{2}>U}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)
.
It has been deduced in
[
3
, Section 4.1]
that

O\left(\sum_{r,s\atop rs\leq U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}rs\log rs\right)=\begin{cases}O(U^{1-\delta}\log^{2}U)&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
O(\log^{3}U)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

The growth conditions on
a_{f}(r)
and
a_{g}(r)
also imply that
D=O\left(M\sum_{r>\sqrt{U}}{c_{r}(N)}/{r^{2+2\delta}}\right)
.
Following
[
3
, Section 4.1]
, we also get

\sum_{r>\sqrt{U}}{c_{r}(N)}/{r^{2+2\delta}}=O\left(\frac{e^{c\sqrt{\log N}}d(N)}{U^{1/2+\delta}}\right),

for some constant
c>0
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle D\ll_{N}\frac{M}{U^{1/2+\delta}},

and therefore

A=\begin{cases}M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O\left(\frac{M}{U^{1/2+\delta}}\right)+O(U^{1-\delta}\log^{2}U)&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O\left(\frac{M}{U^{3/2}}\right)+O(\log^{3}U)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O\left(\frac{M}{U^{1/2+\delta}}\right)+O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

The estimation for term
B
is slightly different, as we need to use
Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
here, in place of (
1
) as in
[
3
, Section 4.3]
.
Using hypotheses about
a_{f}(r)
and
a_{g}(s)
, we get

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{n<M}\sum_{r,s\atop rs>U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}\left|\sum_{r^{\prime}|r\atop r^{\prime}|n}r^{\prime}\mu(r/r^{\prime})\sum_{s^{\prime}|s\atop s^{\prime}|N-n}s^{\prime}\mu(s/s^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\atop rs>U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}\left|\sum_{r^{\prime}|r}r^{\prime}\mu(r/r^{\prime})\sum_{s^{\prime}|s\atop}s^{\prime}\mu(s/s^{\prime})\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n<M}}{{r^{\prime}|n,\ s^{\prime}|N-n}}}1\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\atop rs>U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}\left|\sum_{r^{\prime}|r}r^{\prime}\mu(r/r^{\prime})\sum_{s^{\prime}|s}s^{\prime}\mu(s/s^{\prime})\sum_{m<M/r^{\prime}\atop r^{\prime}m\equiv N\bmod{s^{\prime}}}1\right|.

Let
l=\gcd(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
. Hence if we write
r^{\prime}=lr^{\prime\prime}
and
s^{\prime}=ls^{\prime\prime}
, we get
\gcd(r^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime})=1
. Also note that
l\mid N
. Thus

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{r,s\atop rs>U}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime}|r}r^{\prime}\sum_{s^{\prime}|s}s^{\prime}\sum_{m<M/r^{\prime}\atop r^{\prime}m\equiv N\bmod{s^{\prime}}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\mid N}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r,s}}{{rs>U\atop l|r,l|s}}}\frac{1}{(rs)^{1+\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}|r/l}lr^{\prime\prime}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}|s/l}ls^{\prime\prime}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1\ .

Writing
r=lr_{0}
and
s=ls_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{2\delta}}\sum_{r_{0},s_{0}\atop r_{0}s_{0}>U/l^{2}}\frac{1}{(r_{0}s_{0})^{1+\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}|r_{0}}r^{\prime\prime}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}|s_{0}}s^{\prime\prime}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1

\displaystyle=E+F,

where

E:=\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{2\delta}}\sum_{r_{0},s_{0}\atop r_{0}s_{0}>U/l^{2}}\frac{1}{(r_{0}s_{0})^{1+\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}|r_{0}}r^{\prime\prime}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}|s_{0}\atop s^{\prime\prime}\leq M/lr^{\prime\prime}}s^{\prime\prime}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1

and

F:=\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{2\delta}}\sum_{r_{0},s_{0}\atop r_{0}s_{0}>U/l^{2}}\frac{1}{(r_{0}s_{0})^{1+\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}|r_{0}}r^{\prime\prime}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}|s_{0}\atop s^{\prime\prime}>M/lr^{\prime\prime}}s^{\prime\prime}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1\ .

Note that for
E
, we have
s^{\prime\prime}\leq M/lr^{\prime\prime}
. Since

\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1\leq\frac{M}{lr^{\prime\prime}s^{\prime\prime}}+1,

following
[
3
, Section 4.3]
we get

E\ll M\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{1+2\delta}}\sum_{r_{0},s_{0}\atop r_{0}s_{0}>U/l^{2}}\frac{d(r_{0})d(s_{0})}{(r_{0}s_{0})^{1+\delta}}\ll\frac{M\log^{4}U}{U^{\delta}}.

Following the treatment of the term
F
as in
[
3
, Section 4.3]
, here we get

\displaystyle F\ll\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{\delta}}\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1\ .

Now the sum

\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{m<M/lr^{\prime\prime}\atop r^{\prime\prime}m\equiv(N/l)\bmod{s^{\prime\prime}}}1=\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{mr^{\prime\prime}<M/l\atop s^{\prime\prime}|r^{\prime\prime}m-N/l}1=\sum_{r^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\geq 1}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n<M/l}}{{r^{\prime\prime}|n\atop s^{\prime\prime}|N/l-n}}}1=\sum_{n<M/l}d(n)d(N/l-n).

So, finally we have

(25)

F\ll\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{\delta}}\sum_{n<M/l}d(n)d(N/l-n).

Note that for the estimation of
F
in
[
3
, Section 4.3]
, Coppola, Murty and the second author used Ingham’s formula (
1
). But
in our case, we can’t readily apply Ingham’s formula (
2
). Instead we use Theorem
1
and
Corollary
1
.

If
M/l\leq N/2l
, i.e.
M\leq N/2
, using Theorem
1
, we get

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{\delta}}\ \sigma_{-1}\left(\frac{N}{l}\right)\frac{M}{l}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{M(N-M)}}{l}\right)

\displaystyle\ll M^{1-\delta}\left(\log^{2}\sqrt{M(N-M)}\right)\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{\sigma_{-1}(N/l)}{l^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle\ll M^{1-\delta}\left(\log^{2}\sqrt{M(N-M)}\right)\log N

\displaystyle\ll M^{1-\delta}\log^{3}N\ll_{N}M^{1-\delta},

as
N-1\leq M(N-M)\leq N^{2}/4
, in this case. If
M/l>N/2l
, i.e.
M>N/2
, using Corollary
1
, we get

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{\delta}}\ \sigma_{-1}\left(\frac{N}{l}\right)\frac{N}{l}\log^{2}\left(\frac{N}{l}\right)

\displaystyle=M^{1-\delta}(\log^{2}N)\sum_{l\mid N}\frac{1}{l^{1+\delta}}\sigma_{-1}\left(\frac{N}{l}\right)

\displaystyle\ll M^{1-\delta}\log^{3}N\ll_{N}M^{1-\delta}.

To optimise all the error terms, we choose

U=\begin{cases}M\log^{2}M&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
M\log M&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
M^{(1/\delta)}(\log M)^{4/\delta}&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

These choices yield

\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)=\begin{cases}M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(M^{1-\delta}(\log M)^{4-2\delta})&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(\log^{3}M)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

This concludes the proof of Theorem
2
.

Remark 2
.

The proof suggests that the implied constants in the error terms depend on
N
and have order
bounded by
e^{c\sqrt{\log N}}d(N)
for some constant
c>0
. If
N^{\alpha}\ll M\leq N
for some
\alpha>0
, the
implied constants in the error terms can be made independent of
N
by adjusting a
\log
factor
as follows:

\sum_{n<M}f(n)g(N-n)=\begin{cases}M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(M^{1-\delta}(\log M)^{4-\delta})&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(\log^{3}M)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
M\sum_{r\geq 1}a_{f}(r)a_{g}(r)c_{r}(N)+O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

3.1.
Applications

By the virtue of Theorem
2
, we can now naturally extend and improve the corollaries that were obtained in
[
6
]
.
Using the Ramanujan expansion (
7
), we have:

Corollary 2
.

For
\alpha,\beta>0
, let
\delta:=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
. Then for
M<N
sufficiently large,

{\sum}_{n<M}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha}(n)}{n^{\alpha}}\frac{\sigma_{\beta}(N-n)}{(N-n)^{\beta}}=\begin{cases}\frac{M\zeta(\alpha+1)\zeta(\beta+1)}{\zeta(\alpha+\beta+2)}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha+\beta+1}(N)}{N^{\alpha+\beta+1}}+O(M^{1-\delta}(\log M)^{4-2\delta})&\text{if $\delta<1$},\\
\frac{M\zeta(\alpha+1)\zeta(\beta+1)}{\zeta(\alpha+\beta+2)}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha+\beta+1}(N)}{N^{\alpha+\beta+1}}+O(\log^{3}M)&\text{if $\delta=1$},\\
\frac{M\zeta(\alpha+1)\zeta(\beta+1)}{\zeta(\alpha+\beta+2)}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha+\beta+1}(N)}{N^{\alpha+\beta+1}}+O(1)&\text{if $\delta>1$}.\end{cases}

where the implied constants may depend on
N
.

This result has been obtained in
[
6
, Theorem 12]
only in an asymptotic form and for
\alpha,\beta>1/2
.
Moreover, using partial summation techniques we can derive the following corollary of Corollary
2
, which was stated
by Ingham in
[
11
, eq. (17)]
without an error term (as previously predicted by Ramanujan
[
15
]
).

Corollary 3
.

For
\alpha,\beta>0
, let
\omega=\alpha+\beta+1-\min\{\alpha,\beta,1\}
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

{\sum}_{n<N}\sigma_{\alpha}(n)\sigma_{\beta}(N-n)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)}\frac{\zeta(\alpha+1)\zeta(\beta+1)}{\zeta(\alpha+\beta+2)}\sigma_{\alpha+\beta+1}(N)+O(N^{\omega}\epsilon(N)),

where
\epsilon(N)
is bounded above by
e^{c\sqrt{\log N}}d(N)
for some constant
c>0
.

A finer analysis using Remark
2
is likely to lead us to an error term of the form
N^{\omega}\log^{c}N
for some
0\leq c\leq 4
.
Obtaining secondary main terms and improving error term in the above sum is of classical interest. We refer the interested reader
to
[
8
,
9
]
.

Finite Bivariate Biorthogonal M - Konhauser Polynomials
Esra GÜLDOĞAN LEKESİZ, Bayram ÇEKİM, Mehmet Ali ÖZARSLAN

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct the pair of finite bivariate biorthogonal M - Konhauser polynomials, reduced to the finite orthogonal polynomials M k ( p , q ) ​ ( y ) M_{k}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(y\right) , by choosing appropriate parameters in order to obtain a relation between the Jacobi Konhauser polynomials and this new finite bivariate biorthogonal polynomials M ( p , q ) k ; υ K ​ ( y , z ) \ {}_{K}M_{k;\upsilon}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(y,z\right) similar to the relation between the classical Jacobi polynomials P k ( p , q ) ​ ( y ) P_{k}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(y\right) and the finite orthogonal polynomials M k ( p , q ) ​ ( y ) M_{k}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(y\right) . Several properties like generating function, operational/integral representation are derived and some applications like fractional calculus, Fourier transform and Laplace transform are studied thanks to that new transition relation and the definition of finite bivariate M - Konhauser polynomials.

The Fourier transform for a function
d(y,z)
in two variables is defined as

[

, p. 111, Equ. (7.1)]

\tciFourier\left(d\left(y,z\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}y+\xi_{2}z\right)}d\left(y,z\right)dzdy

and the corresponding Parseval identity is given by the statement

(27)

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\left(y,z\right)\overline{f\left(y,z\right)}dzdy=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tciFourier\left(d\left(y,z\right)\right)\overline{\tciFourier\left(f\left(y,z\right)\right)}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},\ d,f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right).

Now, we define the specific functions

\QATOPD\{.{d\left(y,z\right)=\left(1+e^{y}\right)^{-\left(p_{1}+q_{1}\right)}\exp\left(q_{1}y+\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}\right)z-\frac{e^{z}}{2}\right)\ _{K}M_{k;\upsilon}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(e^{y},e^{z}\right),}{f\left(y,z\right)=\left(1+e^{y}\right)^{-\left(p_{2}+q_{2}\right)}\exp\left(q_{2}y+\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}\right)z-\frac{e^{z}}{2}\right)\ _{K}\mathcal{M}_{r;\upsilon}^{\left(\gamma,\delta\right)}\left(e^{y},e^{z}\right),}

and using the transforms
\exp y=u
,
\frac{\exp z}{2}=x
, the definition (
9
), the Gamma integral (
18
) and the Beta integral given by

(28)

B(y,z)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}x^{y-1}\left(1-x\right)^{z-1}dx=\frac{\Gamma\left(y\right)\Gamma\left(z\right)}{\Gamma\left(y+z\right)}=B\left(z,y\right),\ \mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\left(y\right)>0,\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\left(z\right)>0,

the corresponding Fourier transform is obtained as follows

(29)

\displaystyle\tciFourier\left(d\left(y,z\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}y+\xi_{2}z\right)}d\left(y,z\right)dydz

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}y+\xi_{2}z\right)}e^{q_{1}y+\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}\right)z-\frac{e^{z}}{2}}\left(1+e^{y}\right)^{-\left(p_{1}+q_{1}\right)}\ _{K}M_{k;\upsilon}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(e^{y},e^{z}\right)dydz

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}}\left(-1\right)^{k}\Gamma\left(k+\beta+1\right)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k-l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(-k\right)_{l+s}}{l!s!\Gamma\left(l+\beta+1\right)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(k+1-\alpha\right)_{l}2^{\upsilon s}}{\Gamma\left(\upsilon s-\alpha-\beta+1\right)}B\left(l+q_{1}-i\xi_{1},\ p_{1}-l+i\xi_{1}\right)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{\upsilon s-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}}\left(-1\right)^{k}\Gamma\left(k+\beta+1\right)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k-l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(-k\right)_{l+s}\left(k+1-\alpha\right)_{l}2^{\upsilon s}}{l!s!\Gamma\left(l+\beta+1\right)\Gamma\left(\upsilon s-\alpha-\beta+1\right)}

\displaystyle\times B\left(l+q_{1}-i\xi_{1},\ p_{1}-l+i\xi_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\upsilon s-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

So, the Fourier transform (
29
) can be expressed as

(30)

\tciFourier\left(d\left(y,z\right)\right)=\left(-1\right)^{k}\Gamma\left(k+\beta+1\right)G_{1}\left(p_{1},q_{1};\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},\alpha,\beta,\upsilon;\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right),

where

G_{1}\left(p_{1},q_{1};\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}}B\left(p_{1}+i\xi_{1},q_{1}-i\xi_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}\right)

and

\Psi_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},\alpha,\beta,\upsilon;\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k-l}\frac{\left(-k\right)_{l+s}\left(k+1-\alpha\right)_{l}\left(q_{1}-i\xi_{1}\right)_{l}\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-i\xi_{2}\right)_{\upsilon s}2^{\upsilon s}}{l!s!\left(1-p_{1}-i\xi_{1}\right)_{l}\Gamma\left(l+\beta+1\right)\Gamma\left(\upsilon s-\alpha-\beta+1\right)}.

On the other hand, Fourier transform of
f\left(y,z\right)
is

(31)

\displaystyle\tciFourier\left(f\left(y,z\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}y+\xi_{2}z\right)}f\left(y,z\right)dydz

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}y+\xi_{2}z\right)}e^{q_{2}y+\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}\right)z-\frac{e^{z}}{2}}\left(1+e^{y}\right)^{-\left(p_{2}+q_{2}\right)}\ _{K}\mathcal{M}_{r;\upsilon}^{\left(\gamma,\delta\right)}\left(e^{y},e^{z}\right)dydz

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}u^{q_{2}-i\xi_{1}-1}\left(1+u\right)^{-p_{2}-q_{2}}M_{r}^{\left(\gamma,\delta\right)}\left(u\right)du\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{r}Y_{j}^{\left(-\gamma,-\delta\right)}\left(t;\upsilon\right)dt

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}}\left(-1\right)^{r}\Gamma\left(r+\delta+1\right)\sum_{s=0}^{r}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{s}\left(-r\right)_{s}\left(r+1-\gamma\right)_{s}}{s!\Gamma\left(s+\delta+1\right)}B\left(q_{2}+s-i\xi_{1},\ p_{2}-s+i\xi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}2^{l}}{j!l!}\binom{l}{m}\left(\frac{m-\gamma-\delta+1}{\upsilon}\right)_{j}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{l-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}}\left(-1\right)^{r}\Gamma\left(r+\delta+1\right)\sum_{s=0}^{r}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{s}\left(-r\right)_{s}\left(r+1-\gamma\right)_{s}}{s!\Gamma\left(s+\delta+1\right)}B\left(q_{2}+s-i\xi_{1},\ p_{2}-s+i\xi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\frac{\Gamma\left(l-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\ 2^{l}}{\left(-1\right)^{m}j!l!}\binom{l}{m}\left(\frac{m-\gamma-\delta+1}{\upsilon}\right)_{j}.

Then,
\tciFourier\left(f\left(y,z\right)\right)
can be written as

(32)

\tciFourier\left(f\left(y,z\right)\right)=\left(-1\right)^{r}\Gamma\left(r+\delta+1\right)G_{2}\left(p_{2},q_{2};\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\Psi_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},\gamma,\delta,\upsilon;\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right),

where

G_{2}\left(p_{2},q_{2};\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}}B\left(p_{2}+i\xi_{1},q_{2}-i\xi_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}\right)

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},\gamma,\delta,\upsilon;\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}\frac{\left(-r\right)_{s}\left(r+1-\gamma\right)_{s}\left(q_{2}-i\xi_{1}\right)_{s}}{s!\left(1-p_{2}-i\xi_{1}\right)_{s}\Gamma\left(s+\delta+1\right)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{r}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}2^{l}\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}-i\xi_{2}\right)_{l}}{j!m!\left(l-m\right)!}\left(\frac{m-\gamma-\delta+1}{\upsilon}\right)_{j}.

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}u^{q_{1}+q_{2}-1}\left(1+u\right)^{-\left(p_{1}+q_{1}+p_{2}+q_{2}\right)}e^{-t}t^{-\left(p_{1}+q_{1}+p_{2}+q_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\ _{K}M_{k;\upsilon}^{\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+1,q_{1}+q_{2}-1\right)}\left(u,t\right)\ _{K}\mathcal{M}_{r;\upsilon}^{\left(p_{2}+p_{1}+1,q_{2}+q_{1}-1\right)}\left(u,t\right)dudt

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{1}\left(p_{1},q_{1};\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},p_{1}+p_{2}+1,q_{1}+q_{2}-1,\upsilon;\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)

As a result, the following theorem can be given.

Theorem 22
.

For
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}>0,

p_{1}+p_{2}>2k
,
p_{1}+q_{1}<1/2
and
p_{2}+q_{2}<1/2
, the families of functions
\ \Upsilon_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},q_{2},p_{2},\upsilon;y,z\right)
and
\ \Upsilon_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},q_{1},p_{1},\upsilon;y,z\right)
are finite biorthogonal
functions with respect to the weight function

\displaystyle\rho\left(p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2};y,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(p_{1}+iy\right)\Gamma\left(p_{2}-iy\right)\Gamma\left(q_{1}-iy\right)\Gamma\left(q_{2}+iy\right)

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\left(1/2-p_{1}-q_{1}-iz\right)\Gamma\left(1/2-p_{2}-q_{2}+iz\right)

and the corresponding finite biorthogonality relation is

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Gamma\left(p_{1}+iy\right)\Gamma\left(p_{2}-iy\right)\Gamma\left(q_{1}-iy\right)\Gamma\left(q_{2}+iy\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}-iz\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}+iz\right)

\displaystyle\times\Upsilon_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},q_{2},p_{2},\upsilon;iz,it\right)\Upsilon_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},q_{1},p_{1},\upsilon;-iz,-it\right)dydz

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}k!\Gamma\left(p_{1}+q_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(p_{2}+q_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+1-k\right)}{\left(p_{1}+p_{2}-2k\right)\Gamma\left(k+q_{1}+q_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+q_{1}+q_{2}-k\right)}\delta_{k,r},

where

\Upsilon_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},q_{2},p_{2},\upsilon;y,z\right)=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{1}-q_{1}}\Psi_{1}\left(k,p_{1},q_{1},p_{1}+p_{2}+1,q_{1}+q_{2}-1,\upsilon;-iy,-iz\right)

and

\Upsilon_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},q_{1},p_{1},\upsilon;y,z\right)=2^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{2}-q_{2}}\Psi_{2}\left(r,p_{2},q_{2},p_{2}+p_{1}+1,q_{2}+q_{1}-1,\upsilon;-iy,-iz\right).

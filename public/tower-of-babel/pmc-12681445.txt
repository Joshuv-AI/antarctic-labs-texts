An efficient framework for scheduling security-critical tasks in resource-limited mobile edge computing using hybridized gold rush with golden jackal optimization strategy
Authors: Kapil Vhatkar, Shweta Koparde, Neeta Deshpande, Sonali Kothari, Sonali Patil, Ranjana Kale, Madhavi Nimkar (Darokar), Pooja Bagane
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMobile Edge Computing (MEC) is an advanced technology that has the ability to decentralize and transform the working functionality of phone networks. The MEC is implanted in the cell phone base stations. The available resources in the mobile applications are processed by the MEC. Yet, the user experience and Quality of Service (QoS) are affected by the problem of inevitable optimization. A practical and efficient option to transfer workloads is MEC servers that are equipped with tiny or large base stations. By shifting the tasks from mobile devices to edge servers, MEC can provide low-latency computing services and high throughput. This research work aims at scheduling security-critical workflow tasks in the MEC environment that significantly improves the computing power of the devices by scheduling the service workflows from computing mobile devices to the edges of the mobile network. The major objective to be considered during the scheduling of critical tasks is the minimization of workflow execution time and total energy consumption. The major contributions of the recommended security-critical tasks scheduling approach in resource-limited MEC are listed here.•To present a security-critical tasks scheduling method in resource-limited MEC to improve the offloading performance and energy efficiency to reduce the latency issues.•To secure the security-critical features of the tasks to schedule the tasks in resource-limited MEC for enhancing the user experience as well as quality of service.•To schedule the security-critical tasks in resource-limited MEC using the developed HGR-GJOS. It helps to minimize the workflow execution time and total energy consumption of the devices by optimizing which of the tasks are assigned to which machine.Keywords: Resource-limited mobile edge computing, Security-critical tasks scheduling, Hybridized gold rush, Golden jackal optimization strategy

Background

The tremendous growth of mobile applications is helpful in the fields of online gaming, image and signal processing, and augmented reality. The user devices can take advantage of cloud computing capabilities at the mobile network edge with MEC [1]. MEC is accomplished by deploying Edge Servers (ES) on the Base Station (BS) and cellular network edge, both have sufficient storage and computational capacity [2]. MEC's primary goals are to guarantee service deployment, facilitate efficient network operation, provide enhanced quality of experience, and reduce latency. The MEC is useful for a number of applications, such as traffic infraction tracking cameras, drone delivery services, and geological studies [3]. MEC servers have far more computer capability than wireless devices and are usually located closer to one another geographically. As a result, MEC can address both the issue of wireless device resource restrictions as well as the issue of transmission delay in cloud computing [4]. Under deadline constraints, the workloads from wireless devices can be transferred to the MEC server that is connected to the Macro BS (MBS). MEC is thought to be a workable substitute strategy to address any potential issues with cloud computing [5]. In order to place processing and storage resources closer to end devices, MEC shifts them from a centralized cloud to the network's edge. By reducing the volume of data sent through the network backbone, servicing tasks at edge devices helps to protect data privacy and lower the likelihood of network congestion [6].

The security-critical tasks need data encryption prior to offloading. Users prefer to trust MEC providers with a good reputation over those with none or even a negative one when selecting the MEC server to delegate these tasks [7]. Before offloading the jobs, users can also select from a variety of encryption schemes, each of which offers a different degree of data protection. The primary problem in this scenario is scheduling a user's independent security-critical actions in a resource-constrained MEC system that helps to minimize both the user's mobile device's energy utilization and the time it takes for all jobs to complete [8]. Quality of Service (QoS), cost, and energy usage are all associated with the massive processing workloads in the MEC [9]. Task scheduling is crucial to MEC because various task scheduling techniques might result in varying processing durations and transmission delays for the same computational operations. Thus, in the existing research works, a great deal of attention is provided to task scheduling issues in MEC [10]. Furthermore, shifting workloads to edge servers will expose security risks including spying, modification, and denial-of-service assaults. Consequently, in order to protect compute offloading from these risks, a security service mechanism is required [11].

Previous research concentrated on MEC task scheduling problems, like how to arrange jobs with varying priority within a queue buffer with limited resources. Greedy algorithms and other optimization techniques are used to provide solutions that can be applied to local optimal solutions. For such NP-hard issues, polynomial-time optimal solutions receive less attention [12]. Although the modified tabu search algorithm and greedy algorithm are suggested for locating the locally optimal solution, they are unable to locate the globally optimal solution for work scheduling in MEC [13]. An optimization algorithm and joint unloading are introduced to address the device's energy consumption issue. Furthermore, a method for merging MEC servers and cloud servers is introduced to lessen the burden on edge nodes [14]. Yet, when it comes to choosing scheduling and task classification systems, they are not sufficiently optimized for MEC. The issue of optimal allocation is crucial because excessive resource usage results in scarcity, while insufficient use significantly reduces the system's quality of service. Furthermore, by distributing the load among all available Virtual Machines (VMs), the optimal task allocation to VMs lowers latency and boosts system performance [15]. The need for mobile devices' computational capacity has increased due to the swift growth of mobile applications. Unfortunately, this need cannot be satisfied by resource-constrained mobile devices in general because they have limited computing power. In addition to lower the amount of data sent across the network backbone, serving jobs at edge devices also preserves data and lessens the likelihood of network congestion. When compared to cloud resources, the MEC has a very less amount of resources. Thus, affects the task allocation process in the MEC layer. Moreover, user mobility is a common problem in scheduling tasks in MEC [15]. All these obstacles are rectified by the developed algorithm for scheduling security-critical tasks in resource-limited MEC.

Literature review

In 2020, Zhang et al. [16] have developed a method to assign security-critical tasks in MEC by reducing the energy usage of devices and completion time of tasks. In order to rectify the NP-hard problem, they suggested two slow-movement Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithms. To obtain better scheduling solutions by mapping the particles, they proposed a position-based mapping scheme. With the help of this new approach, new high-quality solutions were attained by the position-based probability model from the current solution. In order to further improve the quality of the solutions attained by the movement of particles under the guidance of global and best particles, a novel particle updating strategy was proposed. This scheme helped the particles to slow down their movements and the particles do not need to change their positions. When comparing the traditional PSO algorithm, the proposed algorithm has better efficiency and effectiveness. In addition, the efficacy of the recommended particle updating strategy and mapping method was analyzed.

In 2023, Ma et al. [17] have aimed to minimize the average task response time within the resource budget limit by resolving the task scheduling issues in cloud-assisted MEC. The water-filling-based Dynamic Task Scheduling (WiDaS) algorithm was introduced to address the issues of communication-computation delay tradeoff, edge node heterogeneity, and task arrival dynamics. By utilizing the concept of water filling, WiDaS effectively arranged mobile tasks among edge nodes and dynamically adjusted the utilization of cloud resources using the Lyapunov optimization approach. To assess WiDaS under two mathematical traffic patterns and a trace-driven traffic pattern, extensive simulations were carried out. The findings revealed that WiDaS offered two advantages in terms of efficiency. When compared to the KKT-based algorithm, the suggested WiDaS has attained less computation complexity. Moreover, the effectiveness of the developed approach was high as it minimized the average response time of the tasks.

In 2021, Alfakih et al. [18] have minimized the processing time and service cost when offloading work to Edge Servers (ES) from mobile devices in MEC systems by optimizing resource allocation to reduce the service cost and computing time. The primary goal of the work was to deliver the offloaded tasks to the VMs in a scheduled way for increasing the associativity and fully utilize the capacity of ES by maintaining the mobility of the mobile devices. Using an integrated Accelerated PSO (APSO) algorithm with dynamic programming, they demonstrated a dynamic load-balancing and task-scheduling method. For the experimental evaluations, the suggested approach was contrasted with the PSO-GA, APSO, and PSO algorithms. The suggested technique outperformed the above algorithms with regards to effective resource utilization and less makespan of tasks. Furthermore, the proposed model achieved better fitness function value by reducing the waiting time and service cost.

In 2023, Mohan and Sharma [19] have suggested a work by utilized the benefits of blockchain technology to assign tasks with high security. In order to optimize schedules while utilizing the benefits of the fireworks algorithm, a BSFW approach was designed to schedule tasks with security. The goal of this study was to optimize the energy usage and makespan of schedules. Several experiments have been carried out to assess the efficacy of the recommended algorithm, and the findings showed that it outperformed other comparative strategies. The metrics like execution time, convergence, throughput, makespan, and energy consumption have all been evaluated. In comparison to alternative methods, the outcome has produced a greater performance improvement.

In 2021, Ma et al. [20] have proposed a new approach to assign the security-critical tasks with less makespan. They suggested three effective composite heuristics CH1–CH3 method to rectify the integer programming problem. Here, the solutions were considered as task permutations. The objective value was computed via the greedy heuristic algorithm. The recommended CH1–CH3 has two stages including solution improvement and solution initialization. A Biggest Data Task First (BTF) technique was employed in the first stage to generate the solutions. The obtained solution was enhanced in the second stage. The capability of the suggested CH1–CH3 was better than the Round Robin algorithm. Moreover, the employed BTF mechanism further increased the effectiveness of the proposed model.

In 2021, Ke et al. [21] have presented a model for wireless communication and computation that takes into account the stochastic arrival of workloads, bandwidth constraints, privacy preservation, and the time-varying channel state. This model also included resource allocation and partial computation offloading. They formulated the optimization problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to reduce the weighted sum cost of the system while simultaneously optimizing the execution and computation delays, bandwidth resources and power consumption. Due to the challenging issues of dimensionality and lack of prior information, they put forth a Decentralized Optimization Plan Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning (DOCRRL) for resource allocation and partial computation offloading. The DOCRRL method determined the best course of action based on the Signal-To-Interference Plus-Noise Ratio (SINR), the arrival rate of compute tasks, and the time-varying channel state. The curse of dimensionality issues with respect to state space and action space were solved by the DOCRRL algorithm. Even without prior knowledge, the proposed DOCRRL could have the ability to learn and explore optimal decision-making policies.

In 2023, Wang and Liu [22] have utilized the Bayesian Classifier for allocating tasks in MEC. The Naive Bayes classified the tasks based on priority. After the classification, the tasks were allocated using the proposed Priority-Based Classification Task Scheduling (PCTS) strategy. The empirical evaluation was taken based on performance measures like energy consumption, processing time, and latency. The suggested model obtained a better throughput rate and completion time when contrasted with existing mechanisms.

In 2022, Amer [23] have proposed a two-level cooperative scheduling approach. This model has two scheduling levels along with an orchestrator layer. The tasks were scheduled on the MEC servers in the first level of scheduling. The tasks were allocated to a cloud or base station in the second level scheduling. Based on the throughput and latency, the priority was estimated to deliver the assigned tasks. In order to provide an effective service with less amount of cost, better resource distribution was done by the designed resource optimization algorithm. In accordance with the traffic model, the resource optimization algorithm was utilized with two constraints. If there was an unbalanced traffic load, then one constraint was used and the other was used in the case of uniformly distributed traffic. The non-cooperative model performed better than the cooperative model was proved from experimental outcomes. When contrasted with existing scheduling algorithms, the recommended algorithm achieved high performance results.

This SDN-based cellular network is fully managed and controlled by the SDN controller, which installs routing tables. All MEC servers can receive computing tasks from mobile devices. A specialized VM and a single CPU core are presented on each resource-constrained MEC server to handle the offloaded activities based on the First Come First Serve (FCFS) policy.

To account for these scenarios, we incorporated a security validation model in our discussion. Specifically, we measured the additional latency and energy cost under adversarial traffic conditions, as reported in related studies [2,19]. These studies showed that blockchain-enabled MEC frameworks can effectively mitigate tampering and DoS attacks, though at the expense of higher computation overhead. Similarly, reinforcement-learning-based secure offloading [21] demonstrated resilience against dynamic attacks but required large training resources.

In comparison, our HGR-GJOS model can integrate such mechanisms by dynamically scheduling tasks away from overloaded or malicious nodes, while maintaining efficiency. For example, under simulated eavesdropping and replay attack scenarios, HGR-GJOS maintained a ∼20 % lower latency increase than PSO and GA-based methods, because its hybridized search strategy avoided assigning tasks repeatedly to vulnerable nodes.

Future work will extend this into active attack simulations using MEC security frameworks such as EdgeCloudSim with malicious node extensions, where denial-of-service and man-in-the-middle attacks can be explicitly modeled. This ensures that HGR-GJOS not only optimizes resource allocation but also provides resilience against practical MEC security threats.

Method details

The proposed Hybrid Heuristic Strategy-based Resource Limited Optimal Task Scheduling Process in Mobile Edge Computing is discussed in this section with results. This highlights how the issues identified earlier can be resolved.

Developed HGR-GJOS: The HGR-GJOS algorithm is developed to improve the task scheduling process in MEC by optimizing which of the tasks are assigned to which machine to minimize energy consumption and workflow execution time. The GJO algorithm has the ability to solve problems in unidentified search space. However, it easily falls into the local optimum because of the insufficient diversity of male jackals. The GRO algorithm could optimize continuous and skeletal structures for obtaining better solutions. But, the sense of hearing and sight affects the optimal solution. Therefore, a hybrid algorithm by integrating GRO and GJO is developed. The proposed HGR-GJOS algorithm works based on the iteration condition. If u<U2, then the algorithm is updated using the GRO algorithm or else, it is updated via the GJO algorithm. So, the limitations of those algorithms are rectified and the developed HGR-GJO is capable of solving all kinds of optimization problems and reaching global targets.

These outcomes confirm that HGR-GJOS enhances user experience and maintains balanced performance across heterogeneous workloads.

Results and discussion

Experimental Configuration: The designed security-critical task scheduling method in MEC was executed in Python. The entire count of population, iterations, and chromosome length utilized in this work was 10, 250, and 4, respectively. The potential of the recommended HGR-GJOS-based security-critical task scheduling method in MEC was justified by comparing the simulation outcomes with various optimization algorithms. The heuristic approaches including the Dove Swarm Optimization Algorithm (DSOA) [26], Water Strider Algorithm (WSA) [27], Gold Rush Optimization (GRO) [24], and Golden Jackal Optimization (GJO) [25] were considered for the evaluation process.

While the initial experiments were implemented in a Python simulation framework for proof-of-concept, Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) systems are inherently dynamic due to heterogeneous servers, user mobility, and time-varying workloads. To better reflect these conditions, we considered task arrivals following a Poisson distribution to simulate bursty traffic, and adopted a random waypoint mobility model to account for user handoff between MEC servers. Moreover, we discuss the integration of event-driven simulators such as EdgeCloudSim and iFogSim, which enable realistic modeling of SDN-based MEC, virtualization overhead, and server heterogeneity. These enhancements ensure that the proposed HGR-GJOS framework is not only validated in static conditions but also demonstrates robustness under dynamic network environments typical of MEC deployments.

To strengthen the validation of the proposed HGR-GJOS framework, we compared its performance with well-established baseline algorithms including Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and a recent Deep Reinforcement Learning (DOCRRL) approach. PSO-based schedulers such as the slow-movement PSO proposed by Zhang et al. [16] have shown promise in reducing energy consumption and task completion time. However, their scalability is limited, and they often fail to efficiently manage multiple simultaneous offloading tasks. In contrast, HGR-GJOS consistently achieved lower makespan and energy usage by avoiding premature convergence. Similarly, GA and hybrid PSO-GA approaches [18] were found effective in optimizing VM allocation and minimizing service costs, but they require complex constraint handling and converge more slowly than swarm-based methods. HGR-GJOS improved over GA by reducing execution delay and enhancing fairness across users. ACO has also been applied for load balancing and path selection in MEC scheduling [9], but its performance strongly depends on parameter tuning and tends to degrade under dynamic mobility conditions. Our experiments indicate that HGR-GJOS reduces energy consumption and improves throughput compared to ACO while maintaining robustness under varying workloads. Finally, reinforcement learning approaches such as DOCRRL [[14], [21]] demonstrate strong adaptability to dynamic wireless environments by modeling offloading as a Markov Decision Process. However, they involve high training overhead and require significant computational resources, which makes them less suitable for resource-constrained MEC systems. HGR-GJOS offered shorter convergence time and lower energy consumption while maintaining fairness, thus positioning itself as a practical and efficient alternative.

Conclusion

An optimization approach for scheduling security-critical workflow tasks in MEC was developed to improve the offloading performance. Encryption was performed to secure the task’s security-critical feature. Then, the proposed algorithm by integrating GRO and GJO called HGR-GJOS was utilized to assign the tasks. After receiving the tasks, the decryption was performed. The objective of the developed HGR-GJOS-based security-critical task scheduling in MEC was minimizing the workflow execution time and total energy consumption by optimizing which of the tasks are assigned to which machine. Therefore, the utilization of resources when allocating the tasks was highly minimized by the developed HGR-GJOS algorithm. The experimental analysis of the proposed security-critical task scheduling framework in resource-limited MEC was conducted with respect to different conventional algorithms. The workflow execution time of the recommended HGR-GJOS was reduced than DSOA, WSA, GRO, and GJO with 22.72 %, 11.81 %, 20.90 %, and 40 %, concurrently. Therefore, the suggested HGR-GJOS was capable of scheduling security-critical tasks in MEC with less amount of time.

The Berger-Wang formula for order-preserving homogeneous maps on cones
Brian Lins, Aljoša Peperko

Abstract
. We prove that the joint spectral radius and generalized spectral radius are equal for any bounded, equicontinuous family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a polyhedral cone. We also consider conditions which guarantee that the semigroup generated by a family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps is bounded when its generalized spectral radius r ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) = 1 r(\mathcal{A})=1 . Finally, we extend the notions of joint and generalized spectral subradii to the setting of homogeneous maps on wedges.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47H07, 47J10; Secondary 15A80, 15B48

Rota and Strang
[
23
]
first introduced the joint spectral radius for pairs of
n
-by-
n
matrices. Later Daubechies and Lagarias
[
6
]
introduced the generalized spectral radius and conjectured that the generalized spectral radius and the joint spectral radius are equal for any finite set of matrices. This was proved by Berger and Wang
[
2
]
not just for finite sets of matrices, but also bounded sets.

Recently there has been interest in nonlinear generalizations of these results. The joint and generalized spectral radius have been studied in the max algebra setting
[
20
,
21
,
15
,
16
,
10
]
. More recently
[
1
]
introduced the competitive spectral radius for pairs of families of nonexpansive mappings. The joint spectral radius we consider here can be seen as a one-player analogue of the competitive spectral radius. We are not the first to consider the joint and generalized spectral radii for families of order-preserving, homogeneous maps on cones. Motivated in part by neural networks,
[
7
]
have generalized the joint spectral radius to order-preserving subhomogeneous maps on cones.
They also consider both the joint and generalized spectral radii of order-preserving homogeneous maps. In
[
7
, Corollary 5.22]
they prove that the joint and generalized spectral radii are equal for families of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space if the maps all have eigenvectors in a compact subset of the interior of the cone.

The main result of this paper is to prove the equivalence of the joint and generalized spectral radii for any bounded, equicontinuous family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a polyhedral cone. As a corollary, we prove the continuity of the joint spectral radius for compact families with respect to the Hausdorff metric in Section
3
. In Section
4
we introduce irreducibility and primitivity conditions which can guarantee that the semigroup generated by a family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps is bounded. In Section
5
we prove the existence of extremal Barabanov norms for irreducible families of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on the standard cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, in Section
6
we extend the notions of joint and generalized spectral subradii to homogeneous maps on wedges.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a real normed space. For any set
S
, let
\operatorname{int}S
denote the interior and
\operatorname{cl}S
denote the closure of
S
. A convex set
W\subseteq X
is a
wedge
if
\lambda W\subseteq W
for all real
\lambda>0
. A wedge
K\subset X
is a
cone
if
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
. A cone is
solid
if it has nonempty interior and
polyhedral
if it has a finite number of extreme rays. Any closed cone
K
defines a partial order on
X
where
x\geq y
if and only if
x-y\in K
. We write
x\gg y
if
x-y\in\operatorname{int}K
. A closed cone
K
is
normal
if there is a constant
M>0
such that
\|x\|\leq M\|y\|
whenever
x,y\in K
and
x\leq y
. Note that any closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space is normal
[
14
, Lemma 1.2.5]
. A norm
\|\cdot\|
is
monotone
if
\|x\|\leq\|y\|
whenever
0\leq x\leq y
.

A closed cone
K
has
condition
\mathsf{G}
if for every
x\in K
and
c<1
, there is a
\delta>0
such that
y\geq cx
for all
y\in K
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
. A closed cone is polyhedral if and only if is has condition
\mathsf{G}

[
3
,
14
, Lemma 3.3, Lemma 5.1.4]
.

Given a domain
D\subseteq X
, we say that a map
f:D\rightarrow X
is
order-preserving
if
f(x)\geq f(y)
whenever
x\geq y
. We say that
f
is
(positively) homogeneous
if
f(cx)=cf(x)
for all
c>0
and
x\in D
.

Two elements
x,y
in a closed cone
K
are
comparable
if there are positive constants
\alpha,\beta
such that
\alpha x\leq y\leq\beta x
. Comparability is an equivalence relation on
K
, and the equivalence classes are called the
parts
of
K
. We consider the parts
\operatorname{int}K
and
\{0\}
to be trivial, all other parts are non-trivial. Note that a closed cone is polyhedral if and only if it has a finite number of parts. The closures of the parts are the faces of the polyhedral cone.

An important polyhedral cone is the
standard cone
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

The interior of the standard cone is

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}>0\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

Of particular interest are
multiplicatively topical
maps which are continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
such that
f(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
. When working with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the standard basis vectors and
\mathbf{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
is the vector with all entries equal to one.

Let
X
be real normed vector space and
W\subset X
a non-zero wedge.
The norm of a homogeneous map
f:W\rightarrow X
on a wedge
W\subseteq X
is

\|f\|_{W}:=\sup\{\|f(x)\|:x\in W,\|x\|=1\}.

When the domain of the function is understood, we omit the subscript and just write
\|f\|
. A homogeneous map
f
is
bounded
if
\|f\|<\infty
.

Let
f:W\to W
be homogeneous and bounded.
It is easy to see that

\|f^{m+n}\|\leq\|f^{m}\|\cdot\|f^{n}\|
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Fekete’s subadditivity lemma it follows that the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f^{n}\|^{1/n}
exists and is equal to
\inf_{n>0}\|f^{n}\|^{1/n}
. The
(Bonsall) cone spectral radius of
f
is the limit

r(f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f^{n}\|^{1/n}=\inf_{n>0}\|f^{n}\|^{1/n}.

When
K
is a normal closed cone with nonempty interior in a real Banach space and
f:K\rightarrow K
is continuous, homogeneous, and order-preserving, it is known that for any
x\in\operatorname{int}K
,
r(f)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f^{n}(x)\|^{1/n}

[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
. When
K
is a closed cone in a finite dimensional real normed space and
f:K\rightarrow K
is continuous, order-preserving, and homogeneous, there is always a nonzero eigenvector
x\in K
with eigenvalue
r(f)
, that is,
f(x)=r(f)x

[
14
, Corollary 5.4.2]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a set of homogeneous maps on a domain
D\subseteq X
. The family
\mathcal{A}
is
bounded
if
\{\|f\|:f\in\mathcal{A}\}
is bounded and
\mathcal{A}
is
equicontinuous
if it is pointwise equicontinuous on
D
.

If
D\subset X
is compact and
X
is a real Banach space, then any continuous, homogeneous map
f:D\rightarrow X
is also an element of the Banach space
C(D,X)
which consists of continuous functions from
D
into
X
. The norm on
C(D,X)
is

\|f\|:=\sup_{x\in D}\|f(x)\|.

If
K\subset X
is a closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space, then by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, a closed and bounded family
\mathcal{A}
of continuous, homogeneous functions
f:K\rightarrow K
is equicontinuous if and only if it is compact as a family of maps in
C(\Omega,X)
for every compact
\Omega\subset K
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of functions
f:Y\rightarrow Z
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of functions
f:X\rightarrow Y
for some sets
X,Y,Z
. Then the
composition
of the two families is

\mathcal{AB}:=\{f\circ g:f\in\mathcal{A},g\in\mathcal{B}\}.

When working with a family
\mathcal{A}
of maps
f:D\rightarrow D
, we adopt the following notational conventions. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

\mathcal{A}^{m}:=\{f_{m}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}:f_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\}.

We use
\mathcal{A}^{+}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}
to denote the semigroup and monoid generated by
\mathcal{A}
:

\mathcal{A}^{+}:=\bigcup_{m>0}\mathcal{A}^{m},\text{ and }\mathcal{A}^{*}:=\bigcup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{A}^{m}.

The
joint spectral radius
of
\mathcal{A}
is the quantity
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})
defined in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
W
be a wedge in a real normed space.
For any bounded family
\mathcal{A}
of homogeneous maps on
W
, the joint spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
is

\hat{r}(\mathcal{A}):=\inf_{m>0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{m}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|
.
For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, observe that

\displaystyle\alpha_{m+n}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m},g\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\sup_{x\in W,\|x\|=1}\|f(g(x))\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m},g\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\|f\|\|g\|=\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}.

Therefore, by Fekete’s lemma, the sequence
\alpha_{m}^{1/m}
converges, and
\inf_{m>0}\alpha_{m}^{1/m}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{m}^{1/m}
.
∎

We also define the
generalized spectral radius
of
\mathcal{A}
as follows.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded family of homogeneous maps on a wedge
W
in a real normed space. The generalized spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
is

r(\mathcal{A}):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}r(f)^{1/n}=\sup_{m>0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}.

Proof.

Clearly

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}r(f)^{1/n}\leq\sup_{m>0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}.

It is clear from the definition of the cone spectral radius that
r(f^{n})=r(f)^{n}
for any bounded, homogeneous map
f:W\rightarrow W
. For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{A}^{mn}}r(g)^{1/(mn)}\geq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f^{n})^{1/(mn)}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}.

Then for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}r(f)^{1/n}\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{A}^{mn}}r(g)^{1/(mn)}\geq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}.

This proves that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}r(f)^{1/n}=\sup_{m>0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}.

∎

In the sequel, we will also need to consider the
partial joint spectral radius
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a finite dimensional real normed space. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded family of continuous, homogeneous, order-preserving maps on
K
and let
P
be a part of
K
. The partial joint spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
on
P
is

\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P):=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}_{P}=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(x)\|^{1/m}

for every
x\in P
. In particular, if
P=\operatorname{int}K
, then

\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(x)\|^{1/m}

for every
x\in\operatorname{int}K
.

Proof.

Choose any
x\in P
. Since parts are relatively open convex sets
[
14
, 1.2.2]
, there is an
\epsilon>0
small enough so that
x-\epsilon y\in P
for every
y\in P
with
\|y\|=1
. Then
x\geq\epsilon y
for every
y\in P
with
\|y\|=1
. Since
K
is a finite dimensional cone, it must be normal which means that there is a constant
M>0
such that whenever
0\leq v\leq w
,
\|v\|\leq M\|w\|
. In particular,

\|f(\epsilon y)\|\leq M\|f(x)\|

for every
y\in P
with
\|y\|=1
and any order-preserving, homogeneous map
f
.
Therefore

\epsilon\|f\|_{P}\leq M\|f(x)\|\leq M\|f\|_{P}\|x\|

(1.1)

for every
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
. From this, we conclude that

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}_{P}=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(x)\|^{1/m}.

If
P=\operatorname{int}K
, then
\|f\|_{P}=\|f\|
for any continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous map on
K
, so
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)
and both sequences
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|_{P}^{1/m}
and
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(x)\|^{1/m}
converge by (
1.1
) and Lemma
1.1
.
∎

We will also need the following minor technical lemma.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space
X
. Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be bounded equicontinuous families of homogeneous maps
f:K\rightarrow K
. Then the family
\mathcal{AB}
is equicontinuous.

Proof.

For any
R\geq 0
, let
B_{R}:=\{x\in X:\|x\|\leq R\}
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is bounded, there is a constant
M>0
such that
g(B_{1}\cap K)\subseteq B_{M}\cap K
for all
g\in\mathcal{B}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
are equicontinuous, they are uniformly equicontinuous on the compact set
B_{M}\cap K
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\epsilon
for any
x,y\in B_{M}\cap K
with
\|x-y\|\leq\delta
.
At the same time, there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\|g(x)-g(y)\|\leq\delta
for any
x,y\in B_{1}\cap K
with
\|x-y\|\leq\gamma
.
Therefore,

\|f(g(x))-f(g(y))\|\leq\epsilon

for any
x,y\in B_{1}\cap K
with
\|x-y\|\leq\gamma
.
This proves that
\mathcal{AB}
is uniformly equicontinuous on
B_{1}\cap K
. It follows by homogeneity that
\mathcal{AB}
is equicontinuous on
K
.
∎

2.
A Berger-Wang Formula

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
K
is a closed polyhedral cone in a finite dimensional normed space
X
. If
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded, equicontinuous family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
K
, then

r(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A}).

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we collect some preliminary results below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
W
be a wedge in a real normed space. For any bounded family
\mathcal{A}
of homogeneous maps on
W
,
r(\mathcal{A})\leq\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

For any bounded homogeneous map
f:W\rightarrow W
,
r(f)\leq\|f\|
. Therefore

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

r(\mathcal{A})=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A}).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a real normed space, let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded family of homogeneous, order-preserving maps on
K
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
,
x\in K\backslash\{0\}
, and
\alpha>0
such that
f(x)\geq\alpha^{m}x
, then
r(\mathcal{A})\geq\alpha
.

Proof.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
and
f(x)\geq\alpha^{m}x
. Since
f
is order-preserving and homogeneous,
f^{n}(x)\geq\alpha^{mn}x
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This means that
\|f^{n}\|\geq\alpha^{mn}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore

r(f)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f^{n}\|^{1/n}\geq\alpha^{m}.

Then by Lemma
1.2
,
r(\mathcal{A})\geq r(f)^{1/m}\geq\alpha
.
∎

For a bounded family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps
\mathcal{A}
on a closed polyhedral cone
K
we can define a preorder on the parts of
K
as follows. We say that
P\geq_{\mathcal{A}}Q
if there exists
x\in P
,
y\in Q
, and
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
f(x)\geq y
. We will write
P>_{\mathcal{A}}Q
if
P\geq_{\mathcal{A}}Q
and
Q\ngeq_{\mathcal{A}}P
. Observe that the relation
\geq_{\mathcal{A}}
is transitive since if
P\geq_{\mathcal{A}}Q
and
Q\geq_{\mathcal{A}}R
, then there exist
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
x\in P
,
y\in Q
,
z\in R
such that
f(x)\geq y
and
g(y)\geq z
. Then
g(f(x))\geq g(y)\geq z
, so
P\geq_{\mathcal{A}}R
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K
be a closed polyhedral cone in a finite dimensional normed space and let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
K
. Assume that
r(\mathcal{A})<1
. Suppose that
x\in K
and there is an increasing sequence
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and maps
f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}^{m_{k}}
such that
\|f_{k}(x)\|\geq 1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

y=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{k}(x)}{\|f_{k}(x)\|}.

Let
P_{x},P_{y}
denote the parts of
K
containing
x
and
y
, respectively. Then
P_{x}>_{\mathcal{A}}P_{y}
.

Proof.

Since
K
is polyhedral, it has condition
\mathsf{G}
. So for any
c<1
, there is an
m
such that

f_{k}(x)\geq f_{k}(x)/\|f_{k}(x)\|\geq cy

for every
k\geq m
. Therefore
P_{x}\geq_{\mathcal{A}}P_{y}
.

Suppose that
P_{y}\geq_{\mathcal{A}}P_{x}
as well. Then there exist
y^{\prime}\in P_{y}
and
x^{\prime}\in P_{x}
and
g\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g(y^{\prime})\geq x^{\prime}
. Since
y^{\prime}
is comparable to
y
and
x^{\prime}
is comparable to
x
, there is a constant
\epsilon>0
such that

g(y)\geq\epsilon x
. Then

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}g\left(\frac{f_{k}(x)}{\|f_{k}(x)\|}\right)=g(y)\geq\epsilon x.

Since
K
is polyhedral, condition
\mathsf{G}
guarantees that for any
0<c<1
, there is an
m>0
such that

g(f_{k}(x))\geq g\left(\frac{f_{k}(x)}{\|f_{k}(x)\|}\right)\geq c\epsilon x

for all
k\geq m
.
Since
r(\mathcal{A})<1
we can choose a constant
\alpha
such that
r(\mathcal{A})<\alpha<1
. If
k
is large enough, then

g(f_{k}(x))\geq c\epsilon x\geq(\alpha^{n+m_{k}})x.

This implies that
r(\mathcal{A})\geq\alpha
by Lemma
2.3
, but that is a contradiction. So we conclude that
P_{x}>_{\mathcal{A}}P_{y}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
K
is a closed polyhedral cone in a finite dimensional normed space. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded, equicontinuous family of order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
K
. If
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},Q)\leq r(\mathcal{A})
for all parts
Q<_{\mathcal{A}}P
, then
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)\leq r(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)>r(\mathcal{A})
. We may scale the functions in
\mathcal{A}
to assume without loss of generality that
r(\mathcal{A})<1
and
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)>1
.
Fix a constant
r(\mathcal{A})<\beta<1
. By Lemma
1.3
, there is an
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
\|f\|_{Q}\leq\beta
for every part
Q<_{\mathcal{A}}P
and
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
.

Choose
x\in P
. Suppose that the set
\mathcal{O}_{m}:=\{f(x):f\in\mathcal{A}^{mk},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is unbounded. Then there is a sequence
y_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{m}
such that for each
y_{k}
there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
with

\|f(y_{k})\|\geq\|y_{k}\|\geq k.

We may assume, by passing to a subsequence that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}y_{k}/\|y_{k}\|=z
exists. By Lemma
2.4

z
is contained in a part
Q<_{\mathcal{A}}P
.

Choose
\epsilon>0
small enough so that
\beta+\epsilon<1
. Since the family
\mathcal{A}^{m}
is equicontinuous by Lemma
1.4
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|f(y)-f(z)\|<\epsilon
for any
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
and
y\in K
with
\|y-z\|<\delta
. In particular,

\|f(y)\|\leq\|f(z)\|+\epsilon\leq\beta+\epsilon.

Since
y_{k}/\|y_{k}\|
converges to
z
, there is a
k
large enough so that

\left\|f\left(\frac{y_{k}}{\|y_{k}\|}\right)\right\|\leq\beta+\epsilon.

Then

\|f(y_{k})\|\leq(\beta+\epsilon)\|y_{k}\|<\|y_{k}\|

for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
, which is a contradiction. So we conclude that
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is bounded. In that case, the set
\{f(x):f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\}
is also bounded, so
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)\leq 1
by Lemma
1.3
. But that contradicts our assumption that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)>1
, so we conclude that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)\leq r(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We have already shown in Lemma
2.2
that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})\geq r(\mathcal{A})
.
We can assume that
K
has nonempty interior. Otherwise we can replace
X
by the subspace
Y
spanned by
K
. Then
K
must have nonempty interior in
Y
since
K
is convex
[
22
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Suppose that there are parts
P
such that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)>r(\mathcal{A})
. Since there are only finitely many parts with this property, there must be at least one that is minimal with respect to the relation
<_{\mathcal{A}}
. But that contradicts Lemma
2.5
. So we conclude that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A},P)\leq r(\mathcal{A})
for all parts including
\operatorname{int}K
, and therefore
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=r(\mathcal{A})
by Lemma
1.3
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a closed cone
K
in a finite dimensional normed space. Unlike families of linear maps, it is possible for
\mathcal{A}
to be bounded without being equicontinuous. For example, consider
K=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{f_{k}:k>0\}
where

f_{k}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}\min\{x_{2},kx_{1}\}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Then
f_{k}(e_{2})=e_{2}
but
f_{k}(\delta e_{1}+e_{2})=\mathbf{1}

whenever
k>1/\delta
.

It turns out that even a simple bounded family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a polyhedral cone can fail the Berger-Wang condition in Theorem
2.1
if the family is not equicontinuous.

Example 2.7
.

Consider the family of multiplicatively topical maps
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
defined by

f_{\lambda}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{\lambda}x_{2}^{(1-\lambda)}\\
\tfrac{1}{2}x_{2}\end{bmatrix},~0<\lambda<1.

Observe that if
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
, then
f(x)_{2}=2^{-m}x_{2}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
. Therefore, the only possible eigenvalues of
f
are
0
(which corresponds to the eigenvector
e_{1}
) and
2^{-m}
(corresponding to
e_{2}
). Since
r(f)
must be an eigenvalue of
f
, it follows that
r(f)=2^{-m}
, and therefore
r(\mathcal{A})=\tfrac{1}{2}
by Lemma
1.2
.

For any
m
and
\epsilon>0
, it is possible to choose
\lambda
large enough so that
f_{\lambda}^{m}(\mathbf{1})_{1}>1-\epsilon
. But then
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}=1
which implies that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=1
by Lemma
1.1
. Therefore the generalized spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
is strictly smaller than the joint spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a real normed space. A map
f:K\rightarrow K
is
subadditive
if
f(x+y)\leq f(x)+f(y)
for all
x,y\in K
. The following lemma shows that we can remove the equicontinuity assumption from Theorem
2.1
if the maps are all subadditive.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
K
be a closed polyhedral cone in a finite dimensional normed space. If
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded family of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous maps
f:K\rightarrow K
, then
\mathcal{A}
is equicontinuous.

Proof.

We have to show for any
x\in K
and for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that
\|f(x)-f(y)\|<\epsilon
for any
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
y\in K
such that
\|x-y\|<\delta
. Since
K
is polyhedral, it satisfies condition
\mathsf{G}
. In particular, for any
\gamma>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
(1+\gamma)y\geq x
for any
y\in K
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
.

Let
z:=(1+\gamma)y-x
so that
z\in K
and

(1+\gamma)f(y)=f(x+z)\leq f(x)+f(z).

Therefore

0\leq(1+\gamma)f(y)-f(x)\leq f(z).

Since
K
is a closed cone in a finite dimensional space, it is normal and there is a constant
M>0
such that

\|(1+\gamma)f(y)-f(x)\|\leq M\|f(z)\|.

By the triangle inequality,

\|f(y)-f(x)\|\leq\|(1+\gamma)f(y)-f(x)\|+\gamma\|f(y)\|\leq M\|f(z)\|+\gamma\|f(y)\|.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is bounded, there is an
N>0
such that
\|f\|\leq N
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

\|f(y)-f(x)\|\leq MN\|z\|+\gamma N\|y\|.

By construction,

\|z\|\leq\|(1+\gamma)y-x\|\leq\|y-x\|+\gamma\|y\|\leq\delta+\gamma\|y\|

and

\|y\|\leq\|x\|+\delta.

Therefore

\|f(y)-f(x)\|\leq MN(\delta+\gamma(\|x\|+\delta))+\gamma N(\|x\|+\delta).

Since we can choose both
\gamma
and
\delta
arbitrarily small, we can guarantee that
\|f(y)-f(x)\|<\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Lemma
2.8
implies that if
K
is a closed polyhedral cone and
f:K\rightarrow K
is order-preserving, homogeneous, and subadditive, then
f
must be continuous. To see this, simply apply the theorem to a family which only contains the one function
f
.

Question 2.10
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
makes heavy use of the fact that
K
is polyhedral. Is the Berger-Wang formula true if
K
is not polyhedral? In Corollary
4.6
we show that the Berger-Wang formula holds on general closed cones in finite dimensional spaces if the family
\mathcal{A}
satisfies a primitivity condition. But it is an open question whether the Berger-Wang formula holds if we only assume the family is equicontinuous and bounded.

3.
Continuity Properties

For every
R\geq 0
, let
\Omega_{R}:=\{x\in K:\|x\|\leq R\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space
X
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{B}_{k}
are sequences of compact families of continuous, homogeneous maps
f:K\rightarrow K
such that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
converges to
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}_{k}
converges to
\mathcal{B}
in the Hausdorff metric on
C(\Omega_{1},X)
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{k}\mathcal{B}_{k}
converges to
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}
in the Hausdorff metric.

Proof.

There is an upper bound
M>0
such that
g(\Omega_{1})\subseteq\Omega_{M}
for all
g\in\mathcal{B}\cup\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{B}_{k}
.
Choose
\epsilon>0
. For all
f\in\mathcal{A}
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon

when
x,y\in\Omega_{M}
have
\|x-y\|\leq\delta
since
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly equicontinuous on the compact set
\Omega_{M}
.
Choose
k
large enough so that the Hausdorff distances
d(\mathcal{A}_{k},\mathcal{A})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon
and
d(\mathcal{B}_{k},\mathcal{B})\leq\delta
. Then, for any
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
g\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
g_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
such that the following inequalities hold for all
x\in\Omega_{M}
and
y\in\Omega_{1}
.

\|f(x)-f_{k}(x)\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon

and

\|g(y)-g_{k}(y)\|\leq\delta.

In addition, for any
f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
g_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
there exist
f\in\mathcal{A},g\in\mathcal{B}
such that the inequalities above also hold for all
x\in\Omega_{M}
and
y\in\Omega_{1}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|f(g(x))-f_{k}(g_{k}(x))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f(g(x))-f(g_{k}(x))\|+\|f(g_{k}(x))-f_{k}(g_{k}(x))\|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon=\epsilon

for all
x\in\Omega_{1}
. Therefore for any fixed
f\in\mathcal{A},g\in\mathcal{B}
,

\inf\{\|f\circ g-f_{k}\circ g_{k}\|:f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k},g_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}\}\leq\epsilon.

Likewise, for any fixed
f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k},g_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}

\inf\{\|f\circ g-f_{k}\circ g_{k}\|:f\in\mathcal{A},g\in\mathcal{B}\}\leq\epsilon.

So we conclude that the Hausdorff distance
d(\mathcal{A}_{k}\mathcal{B}_{k},\mathcal{AB})\leq\epsilon
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
3.1
is that if
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is a sequence of compact families of continuous, homogeneous maps
f
on a closed cone
K
that converges to a family
\mathcal{A}
in the Hausdorff metric on
C(\Omega_{1},X)
, then for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{m}
converges to
\mathcal{A}^{m}
. Before proving the main result of this section, we need one other minor technical lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a closed cone in a finite dimensional normed space
X
. If
h:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function on a closed subset
D\subseteq C(\Omega_{1},X)
, then the map
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}}h(f)
is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric on
D
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}
are compact subsets of
D
such that
\mathcal{A}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
in the Hausdorff metric. Let

\alpha_{k}:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}_{k}}h(f)\text{ and }\alpha:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}}h(f).

Since
\mathcal{A}
is compact, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
h(f)
is maximal. Consider a sequence
f_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
such that
f_{k}\rightarrow f
. Then
h(f_{k})\rightarrow h(f)=\alpha
. Since
\alpha_{k}\geq h(f_{k})
for every
k
, it follows that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}\geq\alpha
.

There is a subsequence
k_{n}
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k_{n}}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}
. For each
k_{n}
, choose
f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{k_{n}}
such that
\alpha_{k_{n}}=h(f_{n})
. Such an
f_{n}
exists since each
\mathcal{A}_{k_{n}}
is compact.
Since
\mathcal{A}_{k_{n}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we can choose
g_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|=0
. By passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
g_{n}
converges to
g\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h(f_{n})=h(g)
. Therefore

\alpha\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}=\alpha

which proves that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}=\alpha
.
∎

The main result of this section follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
K
be a closed polyhedral cone in a finite dimensional normed space
X
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is a sequence of compact families of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps
f:K\rightarrow K
such that
\mathcal{A}_{k}
converges to
\mathcal{A}
in the Hausdorff metric on
C(\Omega_{1},X)
. Then
r(\mathcal{A}_{k})
converges to
r(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

The spectral radius
r
for continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on polyhedral cones is continuous
[
13
, Corollary 4.5]
. Therefore the map
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}}r(f)
is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric by Lemma
3.2
. So is the map
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}}\|f\|
.

If follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\mathcal{A}^{m}
is continuous in the Hausdorff metric for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore both

\mathcal{A}\mapsto\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}\text{ and }\mathcal{A}\mapsto\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}

are continuous. Since

r(\mathcal{A})=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)^{1/m}

is a supremum of continuous functions, it is lower semicontinuous. At the same time,
r(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})
by Theorem
2.1
. Therefore

r(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|^{1/m}

is upper semicontinuous since it is the infimum of continuous function. Therefore
r(\mathcal{A})
is continuous in the Hausdorff metric.
∎

4.
Boundedness Conditions

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on a solid closed cone
K
in a real normed space. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
irreducible
if there is no non-trivial closed face of
K
that is an invariant set for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
primitive
if for every nonzero
x\in K
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
such that
f(x)\in\operatorname{int}K
.

Note that
[
7
]
proposed a different definition of irreducibility for families of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on polyhedral cones. A family is irreducible in their terminology if and only if it is primitive in ours.

The following result characterizes irreducibility for subadditive families on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is irreducible if and only if for every pair of distinct
i,j\in[n]
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
such that
f(e_{j})_{i}>0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is irreducible.
Let
G(\mathcal{A})
be the directed graph with vertices
[n]
, and an edge from
i
to
j
if there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
f(e_{j})_{i}>0
. Suppose that
G(\mathcal{A})
is not strongly connected. Then there is a nonempty, proper set of vertices
I\subset[n]
with no outbound edges in
G(\mathcal{A})
, that is,
f(e_{j})_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
and
j\in J:=[n]\backslash I
. Since the maps
f\in\mathcal{A}
are subadditive, the closed face

F_{J}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}:x_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\notin J\}

is invariant under
\mathcal{A}
. This contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{A}
is an irreducible family. So we conclude that the graph
G(\mathcal{A})
is strongly connected.

Since
G(\mathcal{A})
is strongly connected, there is a path of some length
m<n
that connects any
i
to any
j
in
[n]
. Hence there is an
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
such that
f(e_{j})_{i}>0
.

Conversely, it is clear that no proper closed face
F_{J}
can be invariant under
\mathcal{A}
if there is an
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
such that
f(e_{j})_{i}>0
for any
j\in J
and
i\notin J
.
∎

The following theorem for subadditive families is similar to Elsner’s theorem
[
8
, Lemma 4]
for families of matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded, irreducible family of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
r(\mathcal{A})=1
, then the semigroup
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is bounded.

Proof.

Suppose that
\{f(\mathbf{1}):f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\}
is unbounded. Then there exists
i\in[n]
such that
\{f(\mathbf{1})_{i}:f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\}
is unbounded. By subadditivitiy,

f(\mathbf{1})\leq\sum_{j\in[n]}f(e_{j})

for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
. Therefore there is a
j\in[n]
such that
\{f(e_{j})_{i}:f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\}
is unbounded. Lemma
4.2
implies that there is a
g\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
such that
g(e_{i})_{j}>0
. Therefore the set
\{f(g(e_{i}))_{i}:f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\}
is unbounded, which would imply that there is a map
f\circ g\in\mathcal{A}
such that
f(g(e_{i}))\geq ce_{i}
where
c>1
. That would contradict
r(\mathcal{A})=1
, by Lemma
2.3
. Therefore we conclude that
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is bounded.
∎

The conclusion of Theorem
4.3
can fail without the subadditivity assumption.

Example 4.4
.

Let
f,g,h:\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
be defined as follows.

f(x)=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}+\min(x_{1},x_{2})\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix},~g(x)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
x_{1}+x_{2}\end{bmatrix},~h(x)=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}+x_{2}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{f,g,h\}
. It’s clear that the iterates
f^{k}(\mathbf{1})
are unbounded, however no non-trivial face of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
is left invariant by all three maps. Observe the following composition relations:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}f\circ g=g,&g\circ g=g,&h\circ g=h,\\
f\circ h=h,&g\circ h=g,&h\circ h=h.\end{array}

Therefore
\mathcal{A}^{*}=\{f^{k},g\circ f^{k},h\circ f^{k}:k\geq 0\}
. By inspection, the only eigenvectors of
f^{k}
(up to scaling) are
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, both with eigenvalue 1. The only eigenvectors of
g\circ f^{k}
and
h\circ f^{k}
are
e_{2}
and
e_{1}
respectively, again both with eigenvalue 1. Therefore
r(f^{k})=r(g\circ f^{k})=r(h\circ f^{k})=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
r(\mathcal{A})=1
.

Without subadditivity, we need to assume a stronger condition like primitivity to guarantee that
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is bounded when
r(\mathcal{A})=1
. The following result holds for any closed cone with nonempty interior in a finite dimensional normed space.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
K
be a solid closed cone in a finite dimensional real normed space. If
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded, primitive family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
K
with
r(\mathcal{A})=1
, then the semigroup
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is bounded.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma:=\{x\in K:\|x\|=1\}
. Choose any
u\in\operatorname{int}K
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is unbounded. Then for every
M>0
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
such that
\|g(u)\|\geq M
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
and
\epsilon>0
, let
U_{f,\epsilon}:=\{x\in K:f(x)\gg\epsilon u\}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is a primitive family, every
x\in\Sigma
is contained in at least one
U_{f,\epsilon}
. Therefore the sets
U_{f,\epsilon}
are an open cover for
\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is compact, there is a finite subcover. Therefore there is one
\epsilon>0
such that for every
x\in\Sigma
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
with
f(x)\geq\epsilon u
. In particular, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
such that

f\left(\frac{g(u)}{\|g(u)\|}\right)\geq\epsilon u.

It follows that

f(g(u))\geq\epsilon u\|g(u)\|\geq\epsilon Mu.

Since
M
can be chosen arbitrarily large, we can guarantee that
\epsilon M>1
, but then
r(\mathcal{A})>1
by Lemma
2.3
, which is a contradiction.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we get the Berger-Wang formula for primitive families.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
K
be a solid closed cone in a finite dimensional real normed space. If
\mathcal{A}
is a bounded, primitive family of continuous, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
K
, then

r(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

We can assume that
r(\mathcal{A})=1
by scaling the maps in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is bounded by Theorem
4.5
. Therefore
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})\leq 1
by Lemma
1.1
. So by Lemma
2.2
we have

1=r(\mathcal{A})\leq\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})\leq 1.

Therefore
r(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

5.
Extremal and Barabanov Norms

An
extremal norm
for a family of functions
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
is a norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
such that

\|f(x)\|_{*}\leq\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})\|x\|_{*}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
. An extremal norm for
\mathcal{A}
is called a
Barabanov norm
if for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\|f(x)\|_{*}=\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})\|x\|_{*}.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
|x|\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be the vector obtained by applying the absolute value function to each entry of
x
.
If
\nu:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is any order-preserving, subadditive, homogeneous function such that
\nu(x)>0
for all non-zero
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, then observe that the function
x\mapsto\nu(|x|)
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a bounded irreducible family of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Let
\|\cdot\|
be a monotone norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the function

\|x\|_{*}:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\|f(|x|)\|

is a monotone extremal norm.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=1
by scaling the functions in
\mathcal{A}
if necessary. Note that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=r(\mathcal{A})
by Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.8
. Therefore
\|x\|_{*}
is finite for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Theorem
4.3
.

From the definition we have

\|g(x)\|_{*}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|f(g(x))\|\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|f(x)\|=\|x\|_{*}

for all
g\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. So we just need to verify that
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a norm. It is clear that
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is homogeneous, nonnegative, and monotone. Since
f
is order-preserving and subadditive,
f(|x+y|)\leq f(|x|)+f(|y|)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Then since
\|\cdot\|
is monotone, we have

\displaystyle\|x+y\|_{*}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|f(|x+y|)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|f(|x|)\|+\|f(|y|)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|f(|x|)\|+\sup_{g\in\mathcal{A}^{+}}\|g(|y|)\|

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{*}+\|y\|_{*}.

Finally, since the identity map is included in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, observe that
\|x\|_{*}\geq\|x\|>0
for all nonzero
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a compact irreducible family of subadditive, order-preserving, homogeneous maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. If
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a monotone extremal norm for
\mathcal{A}
, then the function

\|x\|_{**}:=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x|)\|_{*}

is a Barabanov norm for
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=1
, by scaling the functions in
\mathcal{A}
if necessary.
Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\alpha_{m}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x|)\|_{*}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is extremal, it follows that
\alpha_{m}
is a non-increasing sequence, and therefore the limit defining
\|x\|_{**}
exists for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It is easy to see that
\|\lambda x\|_{**}=|\lambda|\|x\|_{**}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and that
\|x\|_{**}
is always nonnegative. Since
\|\cdot\|_{*}
is monotone and any
f\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
is order-preserving and subadditive, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x+y|)\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x|+|y|)\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x|)\|_{*}+\|f(|y|)\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(|x|)\|+\sup_{g\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|g(|y|)\|

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows by taking the limit as
m
goes to infinity that
\|x+y\|_{**}\leq\|x\|_{**}+\|y\|_{**}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The function
\|\cdot\|_{**}
also has the extremal property. For every
g\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
,

\|g(x)\|_{**}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f(g(x))\|_{*}\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m+1}}\|f(x)\|_{*}=\|x\|_{**}.

We now show that
\|\cdot\|_{**}
has the Barabanov property.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
such that
\|f_{n}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\alpha_{n}-\tfrac{1}{n}
where
\alpha_{n}:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\|f(|x|)\|_{*}
. Each
f_{n}
is a composition of functions
f_{n,n}\circ\cdots\circ f_{n,2}\circ f_{n,1}
where each
f_{n,k}\in\mathcal{A}
. We can choose an increasing sequence
m_{n}
such that
g_{1}=\lim_{m_{n}\rightarrow\infty}f_{m_{n},1}
exists in
\mathcal{A}
. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can take a refinement of the sequence
m_{n}
and assume that
g_{k}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{m_{n},k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{m_{n},2}\circ f_{m_{n},1}
exists in
\mathcal{A}^{k}
. Observe that for every
k
and
m_{n}\geq k
,

\|f_{m_{n},1}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\|f_{m_{n},k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{m_{n},2}\circ f_{m_{n},1}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\|f_{m_{n}}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\alpha_{m_{n}}-\tfrac{1}{m_{n}}

since
\|\cdot\|_{*}
has the extremal property. Passing to the limit as
m_{n}
approaches infinity, we get

\|g_{1}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\|g_{k}(|x|)\|_{*}\geq\|x\|_{**}

for every
k
. If we let
k\rightarrow\infty
, then we see that

\|g_{1}(|x|)\|_{**}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{k-1}}\|f(g_{1}(|x|))\|_{*}\geq\|x\|_{**}.

We already know that
\|g_{1}(|x|)\|_{**}\leq\|x\|_{**}
since
\|\cdot\|_{**}
also has the extremal property, therefore
\|g_{1}(|x|)\|_{**}=\|x\|_{**}
and we conclude that
\|\cdot\|_{**}
has the Barabanov property.

It remains to show that
\|x\|_{**}=0
if and only if
x=0
. Note that the norm used to define the joint spectral radius
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})
is arbitrary. Therefore we could use the extremal norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and observe that

\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=1=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|_{*}^{1/m}.

Let
\beta_{m}:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|_{*}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We know that
\beta_{m}\leq 1
for all
m
since
\|f\|_{*}\leq\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})^{m}=1
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
. As noted in the proof of Lemma
1.1
,
\beta_{m+n}\leq\beta_{m}\beta_{n}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
\beta_{m}<1
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it follows that
\beta_{mk}\leq\beta_{m}^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and therefore

\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{mk}^{1/mk}\leq\beta_{m}^{1/m}<1

which is a contradiction since
\hat{r}(\mathcal{A})=1
. Therefore we conclude that
\beta_{m}=1
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is compact, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
such that
\|f\|_{*}=1
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\Sigma:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}:\|x\|_{*}=1\}
and for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\Sigma_{m}:=\{x\in\Sigma:\text{ there exists }f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}\text{ with }\|f(x)\|_{*}=1\}.

The sets
\Sigma_{m}
form a nested sequence of compact subsets of
\Sigma
, therefore, they have a nonempty intersection
\Sigma_{\infty}
. For any
x\in\Sigma_{\infty}
,
\|x\|_{**}=1
.

Since we have already shown that
\|\cdot\|_{**}
is a subadditive function, we have

\|x\|_{**}\leq\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\|e_{i}\|_{**}.

Therefore, at least one
e_{i}
has
\|e_{i}\|_{**}>0
.

Consider any nonzero
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then at least one entry
j
of
|x|
is strictly positive, so there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
|x|\geq\epsilon e_{j}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is an irreducible family, there is a
g\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
such that
g(e_{j})_{i}>\delta e_{i}
for some
\delta>0
by Lemma
4.2
. Therefore,

\|x\|_{**}\geq\epsilon\|e_{j}\|_{**}\geq\epsilon\|g(e_{j})\|_{**}\geq\epsilon\delta\|e_{i}\|_{**}>0.

∎

6.
Generalized and joint spectral subradii

The quantities generalized spectral subradius and joint spectral subradius for sets of matrices have been introduced in

[
12
]
to present conditions for Markov asymptotic stability of discrete linear inclusion and are also related to the so-called mortality problem
[
5
]
. It was shown in
[
12
]
that these quantities are actually the same for finite sets of matrices and this results was extended to nonempty sets of matrices in
[
5
]
. In this section we extend these results to wedges (and build on the setting from
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
).

Let
X
be real normed vector space and
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge. Assume

\mathcal{A}
is a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
and let

\mathcal{A}^{m}=\{f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{m}:f_{i}\in\mathcal{A},i=1,\ldots,m\}
.
Let
\beta_{m}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\|f\|
and
\gamma_{m}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}r(f)
.
The joint spectral subradius
\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
and the generalized spectral subradius
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
are defined as

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{m}^{1/m}

and

\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{m}^{1/m},

respectively. It obviously holds that
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
since
r(f)\leq\|f\|
holds for each homogeneous and bounded map
f
on
K
.

Remark 6.1
.

Observe that our results below apply also to the interesting special case when
K=X
is a Banach algebra.

The following four theorems generalize
[
5
, Theorem 1-4]
and are provable in very similar way as these results. The proofs are included for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be real normed vector space,
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge and let
\mathcal{A}
be a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
. Then
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

To prove the nontrivial inequality assume
\varepsilon>0
. By definition of
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
there exists
N
such that

(\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon)^{N}>\gamma_{N}.

Thus there exists
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon>r(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}.

(6.1)

It follows from definition of
\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\beta_{Nk}^{\frac{1}{Nk}}\leq\|(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{k}\|^{\frac{1}{Nk}}.

Letting
k
to infinity it follows that

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq r(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{\frac{1}{N}},

which in combination with (
6.1
) implies that

\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\geq\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Let us, under the assumptions of Theorem
6.2
, denote
r_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
be real normed vector space,
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge and let
\mathcal{A}
be a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
. Then

r_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\beta_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\gamma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}.

Proof.

Since
\beta_{m+n}\leq\beta_{m}\beta_{n}
the
\lim_{m\to\infty}\beta_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
exists by Fekete’s lemma. By definition it is clear that

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\beta_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
. To prove the reverse inequality we may by
[
5
, Lemma 1]
assume that there exists
N
such that

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\beta_{N}^{1/N}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist

f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon>\|f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N}\|^{\frac{1}{N}}.

It follows that for each
k
we have

\beta_{Nk}^{\frac{1}{Nk}}\leq\|(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{k}\|^{\frac{1}{Nk}}\leq\|f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N}\|^{\frac{1}{N}}<\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon

and so
\lim_{m\to\infty}\beta_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}\leq\hat{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})
.

It remains to prove that
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\gamma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
. Clearly
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\gamma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
, so we need to prove the reverse inequality. Again by
[
5
, Lemma 1]
we may assume that there exists
N
such that

\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\gamma_{N}^{1/N}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist

f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
such that for each
k
we have

\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon>r(f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}=r((f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{N})^{k})^{\frac{1}{Nk}}\geq\gamma_{Nk}^{\frac{1}{Nk}}.

This implies
\overline{r}_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\gamma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Let
X
be real normed vector space,
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge and let
\mathcal{A}
be a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be the set of sequences
d=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},\ldots)
of maps from
\mathcal{A}
. For a fixed
d\in\mathcal{D}
define a homogeneous and bounded map
F_{m}^{d}
on
K
by

F_{m}^{d}=f_{m}\circ f_{m-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}

and
r(d)=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\|F_{m}^{d}\|^{\frac{1}{m}}
. As pointed out in
[
5
]
the quantity
\log r(d)
is in control theory literature (at least in the linear case) known as the greatest Lyapunov exponent of the time varying system
x(m+1)=f_{m}(x(m))
where
f_{m}\in\mathcal{A}
. (Refer here to Mason, Gursoy for the tropical case and to the books Max plus at work and Bacelli et al)

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X
be real normed vector space,
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge and let
\mathcal{A}
be a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
. Then

r_{*}(\mathcal{A})=\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}r(d)
.

Proof.

Since
\|F_{m}^{d}\|^{\frac{1}{m}}\geq\beta_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}
for each
m
and each
d\in\mathcal{D}
, it follows that
r_{*}(\mathcal{A})\leq\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}r(d)
. To prove the reverse inequality fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{A}
. Define a sequence
d_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
by starting with
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{N}}
and then continuing periodically. Then

\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}r(d)\leq r(d_{0})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\|(f_{i_{N}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{1}})^{m}\|^{\frac{1}{Nm}}\leq\|f_{i_{N}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{1}}\|^{\frac{1}{N}}.

Since
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{A}
were arbitrary it follows that
r_{*}(\mathcal{A})\geq\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}r(d)
, which completes the proof.
∎

6.1.
Selectable stability of discrete inclusions

Let
DI(\mathcal{A})
be a set of all sequences
\{x(m):m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset K
such that

x(m+1)=f_{m}(x(m))
for some
f_{m}\in\mathcal{A}
. We say that
DI(\mathcal{A})
is
selectably stable

if and only if there exists a sequence
d\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\|F_{m}^{d}\|=0
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be real normed vector space,
K\subset X
a non-zero wedge and let
\mathcal{A}
be a nonempty set of homogeneous and bounded maps on
K
. Then
DI(\mathcal{A})
is selectably stable if and only if

r_{*}(\mathcal{A})<1
.

Proof.

If
DI(\mathcal{A})
is selectably stable then there exists
d=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},\ldots)\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\|F_{m}^{d}\|=0
. So there exists
N
such that

\|f_{N}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}\|<\frac{1}{2}
. Thus

r_{*}(\mathcal{A})<(\frac{1}{2})^{\frac{1}{N}}<1
.

Conversely, assume that
r_{*}(\mathcal{A})<1
. Then for a fixed
q\in(r_{*}(\mathcal{A}),1)
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{N}}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\|f_{i_{N}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{1}}\|<q
. Again let
d_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
be a sequence starting with
f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{N}}
and then continuing periodically. It follows that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\|F_{m}^{d_{0}}\|=0
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The last four theorems remain true (with the same proof) if
K\subset X
is a non-zero set such that
tK\subset K
for all
t\geq 0
.
Thus they can be applied to homogeneous and bounded maps defined on max cones studied in
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
and thus also to the tropical case studied in e.g.
[
17
,
15
,
20
,
14
,
11
,
9
,
4
]
.

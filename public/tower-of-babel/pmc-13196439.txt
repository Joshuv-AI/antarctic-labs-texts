Multilocular hepatic echinococcosis mimicking hepatic malignancy in a child: A diagnostic dilemma
Authors: Rupayan Kundu, Himanshi Batra, Anmol Kasera, Akhila Prasad, Akanksha Yadav, Ankit Batra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multilocular (alveolar) hepatic echinococcosis is an uncommon parasitic disease that may closely resemble malignant hepatic tumors because of its infiltrative growth pattern and complex internal architecture. The condition is rare in India and is therefore frequently misdiagnosed, particularly in pediatric patients. Early and accurate diagnosis is essential, as treatment strategies differ markedly from those of hepatic malignancies and pyogenic infections. We report the case of a 13-year-old boy who presented with a four-month history of intermittent fever and right upper abdominal pain. Initial ultrasonography revealed a complex multiloculated hepatic lesion with internal septations and mass effect at the hepatic hilum, raising concern for a mesenchymal tumor. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a large multiloculated cystic mass with enhancing septa and peripheral walls, without solid enhancing nodules. Magnetic resonance imaging further characterized the lesion, showing T2-hyperintense cystic cavities, T2-hypointense septa, and diffusion restriction confined to the septa and peripheral walls, a pattern favoring echinococcal disease over abscess or malignancy. Serological testing confirmed hepatic echinococcosis. The patient was treated conservatively with albendazole, resulting in clinical improvement and interval reduction in lesion size on follow-up imaging. This case highlights the importance of multimodality imaging, particularly MRI with diffusionweighted sequences, in differentiating multilocular echinococcosis from malignant hepatic masses and avoiding unnecessary invasive procedures.

A 13-year-old boy presented with intermittent low-grade fever and right upper abdominal pain for four months. There was no history of jaundice, weight loss, or gastrointestinal bleeding. Physical examination revealed mild hepatomegaly with right upper quadrant tenderness. Laboratory investigations showed normal liver function tests.

The patient was admitted previously twice for 1 week duration each for a presumed liver abscess; however, symptoms persisted, prompting further evaluation.

Based on imaging findings and positive echinococcal serology, a diagnosis of multilocular hepatic echinococcosis was established. The patient was managed conservatively with oral albendazole for six weeks. Clinical symptoms improved progressively during therapy. Follow-up ultrasonography demonstrated a reduction in lesion size and internal complexity. The patient remained asymptomatic on subsequent outpatient follow-up.

Multilocular hepatic echinococcosis represents an important diagnostic pitfall because of its infiltrative growth pattern and close resemblance to malignant hepatic tumors, particularly in pediatric patients. The disease often demonstrates irregular margins, internal septations, and progressive involvement of adjacent hepatic parenchyma, features that can mimic aggressive neoplasms on ultrasound and CT imaging. [1], [2] A key distinguishing feature of alveolar echinococcosis is diffusion restriction confined to the septa and peripheral walls, with absence of central diffusion restriction within cystic components. This pattern contrasts with pyogenic and amoebic abscesses, which typically demonstrate central restricted diffusion, and with tumors that show restriction within solid enhancing components. [3], [4], [5]

Early identification is essential to avoid unnecessary biopsy or surgical intervention and to facilitate timely initiation of antiparasitic therapy, which remains the cornerstone of management. [1], [2]

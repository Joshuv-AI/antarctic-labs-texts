Epidemiological analysis of Road Accident Data Management System (RADMS) data in Tamil Nadu, India from 2011 to 2016: Future directions for an integrated national database
Authors: Kowshick Raghavan, Sendhilkumar Muthappan, Karunya Ravi, Kathirvel Jothi, Devika Shanmugasundaram, Vettrichelvan Venkatasamy, Ganeshkumar Parasuraman, Manickam Ponnaiah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Globally, road traffic injuries (RTIs) are the eighth leading cause of death, with an estimated 1.35 million deaths yearly. In India, road traffic accidents (RTAs) are one of the major causes of mortality among the younger generation. We analyzed Tamil Nadu’s comprehensive Road Accident Data Management System (RADMS) data and described the epidemiological indicators of RTI in this setting.Methods:We obtained the data from the RADMS database for 2011–2016 and used 2011 population census data to project and standardize for different age groups and genders to calculate incidence. We calculated average annual percentage changes (AAPC) with a 95% confidence interval for the whole period. We computed Joinpoint regression analysis for trends and calculated the age-adjusted incidence rate with standard error (SE) using R statistical computing software.Results:We included 3,67,094 RTAs and 5,50,447 RTIs. We observed that the incidence of RTAs and RTIs declined between 2011 and 2016. Most injured were males (82%) and aged 20–39 years (49%). The highest number of accidents occurred on the state highways (65.2%) and on Sundays (17%). Age-adjusted incidence (per 1,00,000) declined from 121.87 (SE 0.4) in 2011 to 92.73 (SE 0.34) in 2016 (AAPC = -4.5% (95% CI = -7.8 to -1)). The age groups 30–39 and 20–29 were 9.82 (z = 8.98; P < 0.05) and 9.02 (z = 8.65; P < 0.05) times at a higher risk compared to 0–9 years old. The motorcyclists (14–27 times; P < 0.05) and pedestrians (12–23 times; P < 0.05) had the maximum risk of RTIs.Conclusion:Young adults, drivers, motorcyclists, and pedestrians remain vulnerable populations for RTIs. More accidents occurred in the state highways and on Sundays. The analysis provides insights on RTIs and RTAs, which will be used to reduce the burden of RTIs and save millions of lives.

Introduction

Road traffic accidents (RTAs) have become a global public health concern. Globally, road traffic injuries (RTIs) due to RTAs are the eighth leading cause of death across all ages, with an estimated 1.35 million deaths each year.[12] As road safety and RTAs are key global issues, it is included in the sustainable development goal as a target to reduce 50% of road traffic deaths by 2030.[3] More than 80% of all deaths are attributed to RTAs, and over 90% of the disability-adjusted life years due to RTA are contributed by low- and middle-income countries (LMIC).[45] A global modeling study estimated that road injuries will cost the world economy around $1.8 trillion in 2015–2030, which is equivalent to an annual tax of 0.12% on global gross domestic product.[67] In India, a total number of 4,12,432 road accidents have been reported in 2021, claiming 1,53,972 lives and causing injuries to 3,84,448 persons.[1]

Surveillance of RTIs is needed to generate evidence on the burden, implement targeted interventions, and control and prevent injuries, disabilities, and deaths due to RTIs.[89] World Health Organization’s (WHO) report highlights the importance of standard data systems on RTAs and RTIs to enhance road safety.[2] The WHO also recommended an efficient surveillance system to reduce RTAs.[101112] Traditionally, India depends upon indirect reporting systems to estimate the burden of RTAs and its outcome. Reports published by the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB)[13] and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) are an important source of such information. However, these reports vary considerably from each other and do not give enough evidence to identify risk factors and vulnerable populations. As per the NCRB report 2022, Tamil Nadu reported more RTA fatalities in 2021 than in the previous three years, while Chennai topped in RTAs among the metropolitan cities.[14]

Realizing the need for RTI surveillance, the Road Accident Data Management System (RADMS) was implemented in a few states in India in 2009, including Tamil Nadu. RADMS pioneered the integration of police, transport, and highways to collect data on RTAs and RTIs. Based on the learnings from RADMS, the MoRTH launched an “Integrated Road Accident Database” (iRAD),[15] which was developed by IIT-Madras later in 2021. Unlike the RADMS, the iRAD collects transport department key variables, including insurance validity, fitness certificate (FC) validity, pollution certificate, etc., which RADMS omitted. The iRAD brings the integration of various departments, including the health, unlike RADMS, which was dominated solely by the police department. iRAD has been launched as a pilot in 59 districts across 6 states of India, including Tamil Nadu.[15] However, there is no epidemiological analysis of implemented RADMS systems data in Tamil Nadu or elsewhere in India. Since iRAD is in its pilot phase, an epidemiological analysis of RADMS data will be highly helpful in understanding the dynamics of RTAs and RTIs. Analyzing RTA data could be highly valuable for both practitioners and patients. These data will be useful in identifying the high-risk areas, emergency care and response activities, and awareness creation. In this context, we analyzed and presented the epidemiological characteristics of RTAs in Tamil Nadu from 2011 to 2016 using the RADMS.

Methods

Study setting

We retrieved the RTA and RTI data from the RADMS database of Tamil Nadu, India. As of March 2018, 2,15,86,210 two-wheelers and 23,61,195 motor cars have been registered in Tamil Nadu. 65,562 RTAs and 16,157 deaths due to RTAs were reported in Tamil Nadu in 2017.

Data sources

The Government of Tamil Nadu developed the RADMS database to record information on RTAs and RTIs. The RADMS is a web-enabled geographical information system-based online system. The collected data were entered digitally into the RADMS platform. The collected data were synchronized in the cloud server across various sites in Tamil Nadu. The transport department coordinates with other departments and maintains the RADMS database.

Data compilation and analysis

We collected the RADMS data in PostgreSQL format. The socio-demographic factors, details related to different road users, vehicle and road infrastructure, and the age and sex of the injured were stored as individual datasets. Using the unique ID of each dataset, two datasets were created. We merged the data using PostgreSQL and converted it into MS Excel for further analysis.

Data cleaning and statistical analysis

We checked the completeness and internal logic after cleaned. We excluded incomplete records and non-qualified reports from the study. We presented the distributions of RTAs and RTIs across different variables as a proportion (%). We considered the demographic variables for analysis. We used the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) and classified the injured into five major groups and subgroups of road users for analysis. It has to be noted that fatal injury meant the severity of the injury in RADMS data. It does not represent the victim’s outcome (death or alive).

We used the following statistical methods to calculate the incidence

Population projection

The Government of India officially published the last census data in 2011. Since the study deals with data from 2011 to 2016, the population for 2012–2016 was projected based on the 2001 and 2011 census data. We calculated the population growth rate for each year, assuming that it follows an exponential rate of growth. The equation for the exponential rate of growth is Pt2 = Pt1ert where Pt2 is the size of the population at time t2, Pt1 is the size of the population at time t1, r is the exponential rate of growth at which the population is increasing between the periods t1 and t2 and t = t2-t1 which is the time interval between Pt2 and Pt1. The population was projected across different age groups for further analysis.

Incidence and age-adjusted incidence

We used the population denominators (projected based on the 2011 census population (16) for respective years) and calculated age-adjusted incidence along with a 95% confidence interval (CI). We used “The Joinpoint regression program version 4.7.0.0” for analyses.[16] We did a Joinpoint regression analysis to identify overall and subgroup morbidity trends from 2011 to 2016 and to identify significant changes in distinct periods. We considered the age groups, gender, road users, and occupants as a subgroup to examine trends. We calculated average annual percentage changes (AAPC) with a 95% CI for the whole period (2011–2016) and distinct periods to quantify changes and test significance. We used negative binomial regression to find the differences across the year, sex, age group, and different road users. Univariate and multivariate models were developed to find the differences across the year, sex, age group, and different road users. We considered 2011, male gender, 0–9 years, and other categories as the reference category for the year, sex, age group, and road users. The analysis was performed using R statistical computing software.[17]

Ethical considerations

We extracted the data from the RADMS database, which is a web-enabled geographical information system-based online system. This was secondary data analysis, so the ethics committee approval was not necessary. Hence, we did not obtain approval from the ethics committee.

Results

We collected data on 3,96,361 RTAs and 5,63,146 RTI cases between 2011 and 2016. We included 3,67,094 RTA data and 5,50,447 data after excluding 29,267 RTAs and 12,699 incomplete RTI records. Socio-demographic factors

The majority of the victims were males (n = 4,55,773: 82.8%) with a ratio of approximately 4:1. Among the injured, half of the cases were in the 20–39 years age group (20–29 years (n = 1,37,191; 24.9%) and 30–39 years (n = 1,33,786; 24.3%). Nearly half of the victims’ education was limited to school (n = 2,52,345; 45.8%) and graduate (n = 22,867; 4.1%) level. Nearly two-thirds of the (62.6%) victims were manual or unskilled labor. At the time of the accidents, more than half of the victims (n = 2,90,936; 53%) and one-tenth of the victims (n = 74,098; 13%) were traveling in a two-wheeler and four-wheeler, respectively. 68% (n = 1,82,957) of the injured drivers were riding two-wheelers [Table 2].

Injury type and severity

Most of the victims (n = 3,71,708; 67.5%) encountered a simple injury that required hospitalization. A total of 87,592 cases (15.9%) were recorded as fatal injury. Around 21% (n = 1,15,060) and 20.7% (n = 1,13,592) of the victims had a leg and head injury, respectively. Among the fatal injuries, 61.6% (N = 53,654) of the victims had suffered a head injury. Among the 3,67,094 RTAs, nearly one-third (n = 1,06,398; 29%) of the crash occurred between 16:00 hours - 20:00 hours). Weekends (Saturday and Sunday) contributed to nearly one-third (n = 1,14,534; 31.2%) of the RTAs [Table 2].

RTIs and the type of road users and type of vehicle

Out of the total 5,50,447 cases, nearly half of the cases (n = 2,68,526; 48.8%) were drivers and nearly one-third of cases (n = 1,70,033; 30.9%) were passengers. Among the drivers, 59% (n = 1,58,808) and 4% (n = 12,069) were in the age group of 20–39 years and <20 years, respectively [Table 3]. Around 43.4% (n = 32,149) and 33.5% (n = 57,047) of the victims were passengers/pillion-riders of cars and two-wheelers, respectively. Among the injured pedestrians, 46% (n = 50,932) and 16% (n = 18,121) were injured due to a crash with a two-wheeler and four-wheeler, respectively [Figures 2 and 3].

Crude incidence rate and age-adjusted incidence rate of RTIs

The crude incidence rate of RTIs in Tamil Nadu was 133.7 per 1,00,000 population in 2011 and it was 61.7 per 1,00,000 population in 2016. This represents -13.6% AAPC [95% CI: -16.7 to -10.4]. The crude incidence and age-adjusted incidence for RTIs in Tamil Nadu declined significantly across the study period [Table 3 and Figure 4]. Age-adjusted incidence declined from 121.87 (SE 0.4) in 2011 to 92.73 (SE 0.34) per 1,00,000 population in 2016 with -4.5% [95% CI: -7.8 to -1]) AAPC. Though crude incidence was higher than the age-adjusted incidence in 2011, it showed a declining trend from 2013 [Table 3].

The age-adjusted incidence declined significantly in males (-4.8% [-8.6 to -0.9]) compared to females (0.5%, 95% CI: -4.5%, 5.7)] [Figure 5]. There was a statistically significant decreasing trend in age-specific incidence across the study period in the following age groups: 20–29 (-6.3% [95% CI: -9.8 to -2.7]), 30–39 (-6.5% [95% CI: -10.9 to -2]), and 40–49 (-4.5% [95% CI: -8.3 to -0.6]). There was a significant decreasing trend in the age-adjusted incidence over the years among pedestrians, occupants, and cyclists. Trends among different road user groups

Of the different road user groups, motorcyclists and pedestrians were 14–27 times (z = 18.24; P < 0.05) and 12–23 times (z = 17.4; P < 0.05) at a higher risk, respectively. Among occupants, car occupants and truck/van occupants were 2.9 times (z = 29.812; P < 0.05) and 2.2 times (z = 16.667; P < 0.05) at a higher risk than the three-wheeler occupants. On performing negative binomial regression, the age group 30–39 and 20–29 were 9.82 (z = 8.98; P < 0.05) and 9.02 (z = 8.65; P < 0.05) times at a higher risk than 0–9 years old. Discussion

In our understanding, this may be the first epidemiological analysis done in India to provide the status of RTAs and RTIs. Based on the analysis, the majority of the injured were males and aged 20–39 years. The adult age group, the motorcyclists, and pedestrians had the maximum risk of RTIs. Leg and head injuries were highly reported in these years. The highest number of accidents occurred in the state highways. The accidents mostly took place on Sundays, and most of the victims were two-wheeler drivers. Based on the epidemiological analysis of RADMS data, we have identified that the incidence of RTAs and RTIs declined in the state between 2011 and 2016. The crude incidence of RTI also halved by 2016 when compared to 2011. The age-adjusted incidence decreased in 2016 compared to 2011

The results indicate that males, young adults (20–39 years), drivers, motorcyclists, pedestrians, and car occupants are at higher risk of RTIs.[1819] These findings are in line with the NCRB’s report, which indicated that two-wheeler users and males were at a higher risk of RTIs.[20] Among pedestrians, children and people aged >60 years are more vulnerable than other age groups. These findings are in line with several studies across India.[21222324] Hospital, population, and national data-based studies on RTIs in many LMICs have reported similar trends where motorcyclists and pedestrians were at a higher risk than other road users.[22242526] This could be due to a lack of footpaths and unsafe crossing and are more severe among the elderly people.[2728]

A large, prospective, hospital-based study[29] from Southern India reported that three-fourths of the injuries were dangerous, mostly took place on Sundays, and most of the victims were two-wheeler drivers. Similar findings were also reported from other parts of India.[212629] This is in line with the present study where the majority of the RTAs occurred during the weekend. This could be attributed to several factors like alcohol consumption, weekend parties, and speeding vehicles due to low traffic during weekends. NCRB’s reports indicate that the majority of the RTAs occur during 18–21 hours. A study reported similar to our findings in which the majority of the crashes occurred between 16:00-20:00 hours.[20] In the present study, leg and head injuries were highly reported. The majority of the victims with head injuries were recorded as fatal injuries. Many studies have reported that head injury is the predominant factor for deaths in RTIs.[2330]

As per NCRB’s yearly reports on RTAs, the number of RTAs and deaths due to RTAs in India has been increasing consistently between 2011 and 2016 except for 2013, which saw a decrease in the RTAs compared to the previous year.[20] Similar to the country’s scenario, the RTA cases increased consistently in Tamil Nadu between 2011 and 2016, except in 2013. However, our study results showed a significant decrease in the crude incidence and age-adjusted incidence of RTAs in the state between 2011 and 2016. While the numbers have increased during the study period, the significant decrease in crude incidence and age-adjusted incidence highlights the need for epidemiological analysis with denominators.

RADMS involved stakeholders from police, transport, and highways but not from emergency care services and health services. To bridge the above-mentioned gap in our data system, the Government of India has launched a nationwide central “Integrated Road Accident Database”(iRAD).[31] However, similar to RADMS, iRAD has not used any standardized tools like ICD-10 for the collection of injury-related details. The iRAD database similar to the RADMS is more concentrated on RTAs and not on RTIs. Also, iRAD has not included emergency transport services and insurance companies. A study by Jagnoor et al.[12] has reviewed and assessed the several reporting systems and data collection tools in place for injuries. It has been reported that the data on injuries lacked reliability and validity due to the use of non-standardized tools and collection methods. The use of standardized tools for data collection like ICD-10 by integrating departments at all levels, and developing standard operating procedures for data collection aided by capacity building of human resources will ensure robust and more reliable data on RTAs and RTIs. This is where we highlight the need for a more robust, standardized, and nationally coordinated approach toward injury surveillance.

Apart from RADMS and iRAD, Tamil Nadu has taken many initiatives to control and prevent RTAs and RTIs and to reduce the injury’s severity, such as TAEI. TAEI aims to reduce the fatalities due to RTIs by issuing treatment to the injured without delay. Under TAEI, injured patients are brought to the zero delay wards where they are assessed for the severity by trauma management experts. Based on their assessment, emergency treatment is provided.[32] Such initiatives in the state can be attributed to the decreasing trends of RTAs and RTIs observed in our study. “Innuyir KappomThittam: Nammai Kakkum 48”[33] was launched in December 2021 by the Tamil Nadu government. This scheme aimed to provide prompt emergency care to RTA victims, with the state government bearing the treatment expenses for the first 48 hours.

Limitations

The limitation of the study is that the system did not gather much information on vehicle, environment, road infrastructure, and traffic condition-related factors including the use of seat belts and helmets, alcohol consumption, and availability of emergency services. The RADMS did not collect hospital-related data in the system and ICD-10 was not followed in the system. We have used the RADMS data until 2016 only. However, this represents comprehensive analysis of one of India’s largest RADMS database based analysis and thus could pave the way for more such analysis during era of iRAD.

Conclusions

To conclude, RTAs and RTIs in Tamil Nadu have shown a decreasing trend in the study period. However, young adults, drivers, motorcyclists, pedestrians, and car occupants continue to be the vulnerable population for RTIs in the state and nation. The difference in trends between the numerator-based analysis and denominator analysis highlights the need for more epidemiological and statistical analysis in the state for better understanding. Further, more robust data on the risk factors, morbidity, mortality and post-discharge mortality could be collected or added for better insight.

Recommendations

As surveillance is key to collecting reliable data, we recommend the states use the lessons learned in RADMS while implementing the iRAD. We recommend the state perform a similar denominator-based analysis with the iRAD data to understand the trends and dynamics of RTAs and RTIs. Integrating all the relevant departments including health, emergency transport, and insurance departments will help in overcoming the underreporting and incomplete data issues. As such epidemiological analysis is currently unavailable, our work may be useful in analyzing and utilizing information systems for RTIs in India.

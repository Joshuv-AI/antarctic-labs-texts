On a Romanoff type problem of Erdős and Kalmár
Yuchen Ding

Abstract
. Let ℕ \mathbb{N} and 𝒫 \mathcal{P} be the sets of natural numbers and primes, respectively. Motived by an old problem of Erdős and Kalmár, we prove that for almost all y > 1 y>1 the lower asymptotic density of integers of the form p + ⌊ y k ⌋ ​ ( p ∈ 𝒫 , k ∈ ℕ ) p+\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor~(p\in\mathcal{P},k\in\mathbb{N}) is positive.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11P32, 11A41, 11B13

Inspired by old communications between Euler and Goldbach
[
3
]
as well as the de Polignac conjecture
[
7
,
8
]
, Romanoff
[
9
]
proved that for every integer
a>1
the lower asymptotic density of integers of the form
p+a^{k}~(p\in\mathcal{P},k\in\mathbb{N})
is positive, where
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{P}
are the sets of natural numbers and primes, respectively. Later, van der Corput
[
2
]
proved that the set of odd integers not with the form
p+2^{k}
also possesses lower asymptotic density. Shortly after, Erdős
[
4
]
constructed an odd arithmetic progression none of whose elements can be written as the form
p+2^{k}
.
Kalmár asked Erdős whether for any
y>1
the lower asymptotic density of integers of the form
p+\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor
is positive.
Erdős
[
5
, Page 230, (14)]
commented that
‘the answer no doubt is affirmative, but I have not been able to prove it.’
(See also Bloom’s problem list # 244
[
1
]
of Erdős).

For any
y>1
, let
\mathcal{S}_{y}=\big\{p+\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor:p\in\mathcal{P},k\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
and

\delta_{y}=\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{S}_{y}(N)/N,

where
\mathcal{S}_{y}(N)=\big|\mathcal{S}_{y}(N)\cap[1,N]\big|
. Although the resolution to the problem above seems distant, the following approach is probably of some interest.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}=\{y:\delta_{y}=0\}
and
\mu(\cdot)
be the Lebesgue measure. Then
\mu(\mathscr{Y})=0
.

Remark.
It is worth mentioning that the measurable fact of the set
\mathscr{Y}
here is not obvious and needs to be proved.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For given
y>1
, to prove
\delta_{y}>0
it suffices to show

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq\log N/\log y}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p|\lfloor y^{k_{2}}\rfloor-\lfloor y^{k_{1}}\rfloor\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\ll_{y}(\log N)^{2}

(1)

following the standard arguments of Romanoff’s theorem (see, e.g.
[
6
, Pages 200–204]
). Setting
a=\lfloor y^{k_{1}}\rfloor
and then expanding the product, (
1
) clearly follows from

\displaystyle f_{N}(y):=\sum_{d\leq N}\frac{1}{d}\max_{1\leq a\leq d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\log N/\log y\\
\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor\equiv a\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1\ll_{y}\log N

(2)

by rearranging the terms involving sums and products. Let

f(y)=\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}f_{N}(y)/\log N.

and

\mathscr{E}=\{y>1:f(y)=\infty\}.

We shall prove that
\mathscr{E}
is Lebesgue measurable and
\mu(\mathscr{E})=0
, from which we would deduce that
\mathscr{Y}
is measurable and
\mu(\mathscr{Y})=0
since
\mathscr{Y}\subset\mathscr{E}
.

Let
1<Y_{1}<Y_{2}
be two given numbers. Then

\displaystyle\int_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}f_{N}(y)d\mu=\sum_{d\leq N}\frac{1}{d}\int_{Y_{1}}^{Y_{2}}\max_{1\leq a\leq d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\log N/\log y\\
\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor\equiv a\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1~dy.

(3)

We will give a suitable estimate of the above integral. If
\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)
, then

a+dq\leq y^{k}<a+1+dq

for some nonnegative integer
q
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{Y_{1}}^{Y_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\log N/\log y\\
\lfloor y^{k}\rfloor\equiv a\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1~dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\leq\frac{\log N}{\log Y_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{Y_{1}^{k}-d-1}{d}<q\leq\frac{Y_{2}^{k}-1}{d}\\
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}\max_{1\leq a\leq d}\Big((dq+a+1)^{\frac{1}{k}}-(dq+a)^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle<\frac{2Y_{2}\log N}{d\log Y_{1}}.

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}f_{N}(y)d\mu<\frac{2Y_{2}D}{\log Y_{1}}\log N

(4)

by (
3
), where
D=\sum_{d}1/d^{2}
is an absolute constant (in fact it equals
\pi^{2}/6
).
By (
4
),

\displaystyle\int_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}f(y)d\mu\leq\frac{2Y_{2}D}{\log Y_{1}}.

(5)

Let
n
be an integer with
n\geq 3
. Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

\mathscr{N}_{n,N}=\!\left\{y\in\!\Big(1+\frac{1}{n},n\Big)\!:y^{k}\equiv a\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)~\text{for~some~}k\leq\frac{\log N}{\log y},d\leq N,~\text{and}~1\leq a\leq d\right\}

is finite (hence countable). So, we have
\mu\big(\mathscr{N}_{n,N}\big)=0
.
Let

\mathscr{L}_{n,N}=\Big(1+\frac{1}{n},n\Big)\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n,N}.

Then
\mathscr{L}_{n,N}
is a measurable set. We will make the following key observation. Suppose that
y_{0}\in\mathscr{L}_{n,N}
is a given number. Then there is some
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
f_{N}(y)=f_{N}(y_{0})
for any

y\in(y_{0}-\epsilon_{0},y_{0}+\epsilon_{0})\cap\mathscr{L}_{n,N}

from the definitions of
f_{N}(y)
and
\mathscr{N}_{n,N}
. Hence,

f_{N}|_{\mathscr{L}_{n,N}}(y):\mathscr{L}_{n,N}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty]

is a continuous function. Let

\mathscr{L}_{n}=\Big(1+\frac{1}{n},n\Big)\setminus\left(\cup_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{N}_{n,N}\right).

Then
\mathscr{L}_{n}
is measurable and
f_{N}|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}
is a continous function on
\mathscr{L}_{n}
since
\mathscr{L}_{n}\subset\mathscr{L}_{n,N}
, which means that
f_{N}|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}
is measurable on
\mathscr{L}_{n}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we know that

f|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}(y)=\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}f_{N}|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}(y)/\log N

is a measurable function on
\mathscr{L}_{n}
(see, e.g.
[
10
, 1.14 Theorem]
).
Let

\mathscr{D}_{n}=f|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}^{-1}(\{\infty\})=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}f|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}^{-1}\big((m,\infty)\big).

Then
\mathscr{D}_{n}\subset\mathscr{L}_{n}
is a measurable set. We claim
\mu(\mathscr{D}_{n})=0
for any integer
n\geq 3
. Actually, assume
\mu(\mathscr{D}_{n})=e_{n}>0
for some
n
. Then

\int_{(1+1/n,n)}f(y)d\mu\geq\int_{\mathscr{D}_{n}}f|_{\mathscr{L}_{n}}(y)d\mu=\infty\cdot\mu(\mathscr{D}_{n})=\infty\cdot e_{n}=\infty,

which is certainly a contradiction with (
5
) by choosing
Y_{1}=1+1/n
and
Y_{2}=n
.
Now, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 3
let

\mathscr{E}_{n}=\big\{y\in(1+1/n,n):f(y)=\infty\big\}.

Clearly,

\mathscr{E}_{n}\subset\Big(\mathscr{D}_{n}\cup\left(\cup_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{N}_{n,N}\right)\Big).

Denote by
\mu^{*}(\cdot)
the Lebesgue outer measure. Then we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\mathscr{E}_{n})\leq\mu^{*}(\mathscr{D}_{n})+\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mu^{*}(\mathscr{N}_{n,N})=0.

Recall that

\mathscr{E}=\cup_{n=3}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}_{n}.

Therefore, we have

\mu^{*}(\mathscr{E})\leq\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mu^{*}(\mathscr{E}_{n})=0

from which it follows that
\mathscr{E}
is a measurable set with
\mu(\mathscr{E})=0
, proving our theorem.
∎

On decycling and forest numbers of Cartesian products of trees
Ali Ghalavand alighalavand@nankai.edu.cn, Sandi Klavžar sandi.klavzar@fmf.uni-lj.si, Ning Yang yn@mail.nankai.edu.cn

Abstract
The decycling number ∇ ( G ) \nabla(G) of a graph G G is the minimum number of vertices that must be removed to eliminate all cycles in G G . The forest number f ⁡ ( G ) f(G) is the maximum number of vertices that induce a forest in G G . So ∇ ( G ) + f ⁡ ( G ) = | V ⁡ ( G ) | \nabla(G)+f(G)=|V(G)| . For the Cartesian product T ​ □ ⁡ T ′ T\cp T^{\prime} of trees T T and T ′ T^{\prime} it is proved that ∇ ( S n ​ □ ⁡ S n ′ ) ≤ ∇ ( T ​ □ ⁡ T ′ ) \nabla(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\leq\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime}) , thus resolving the conjecture of Wang and Wu asserting that f ⁡ ( T ​ □ ⁡ T ′ ) ≤ f ⁡ ( S n ​ □ ⁡ S n ′ ) f(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq f(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}}) . It is shown that ∇ ( T ​ □ ⁡ T ′ ) ≥ | V ⁡ ( T ) | − 1 \nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq|V(T)|-1 and the equality cases characterized. For prisms over trees, it is proved that ∇ ( T ​ □ ⁡ K 2 ) = α ′ ​ ( T ) \nabla(T\cp K_{2})=\alpha^{\prime}(T) , and for arbitrary graphs G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} , it is proved that ∇ ( G 1 ​ □ ⁡ G 2 ) ≥ α ′ ​ ( G 1 ) ​ α ′ ​ ( G 2 ) \nabla(G_{1}\cp G_{2})\geq\alpha^{\prime}(G_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(G_{2}) , where α ′ \alpha^{\prime} is the matching number.

Introduction

A
decycling set

S
of a graph
G
is a subset of its vertices such that
V(G)-S
is a forest. In other words, each cycle of the graph contains at least one vertex of a decycling set. From this reason, decycling sets are also known in the literature as feedback vertex sets, cf.
[
9
,
10
]
. The
decycling number

\nabla(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality of a decycling set. The
forest number

f(G)
of
G
is the maximum number of vertices that induce a forest in
G
. The following equation that goes back to Erdös et al.
[
14
]
is evident:

\nabla(G)+f(G)=|V(G)|\,.

(1)

The issue of eliminating all cycles in a graph by deleting a set of vertices was first introduced in 1974 in combinatorial circuit design
[
17
]
. Soon after it has been established that determining the decycling number is NP-complete for general graphs
[
18
]
.
Decycling sets have found applications across various fields, including deadlock prevention in operating systems
[
36
,
37
]
, constraint satisfaction problem and Bayesian inference in artificial intelligence
[
4
]
, monopolies in synchronous distributed systems
[
29
,
30
]
, the converter placement problem in optical networks
[
21
]
, and VLSI chip design by
[
15
]
.

In 1987, Akiyama and Watanabe
[
1
]
, along with Albertson and Haas in 1998
[
2
]
, independently conjectured that if
G
is a planar bipartite graph, then
f(G)\geq\frac{5}{8}|V(G)|
. Motivated by this conjecture, Alon
[
3
]
proved that every bipartite graph
G
with an average degree
d\geq 1
has
f(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+e^{-bd^{2}}\right)|V(G)|
for some positive constant
b
. On the other hand, there are bipartite graphs
G
with an average degree
d\geq 1
such that
f(G)<\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{e^{b^{\prime}\sqrt{d}}}\right)|V(G)|
, where
b^{\prime}
is an absolute constant. In 2014, Conlon et al.
[
8
]
improved Alon’s lower bound to
\left(\frac{1}{2}+d^{-db}\right)|V(G)|
for
d\geq 2
. In 2017, Wang et al.
[
39
]
proved that every simple bipartite planar graph
G
has
f(G)\geq\lceil\frac{4|V(G)|+3}{7}\rceil
. Upper and lower bounds for the forest number of a graph in relation to its order, size, and maximum degree are presented in
[
7
,
35
]
. The forest number has been further studied in the context of planar graphs
[
11
,
12
,
19
,
23
,
26
,
31
]
, regular graphs
[
33
,
34
]
, subcubic graphs
[
20
]
, and line graphs
[
27
]
.

The notations
P_{n}
,
S_{n}
, and
C_{n}
represent the path, the star, and the cycle graph on
n
vertices, respectively.

The study of decycling and forest numbers of Cartesian products of graphs has a long history. Beineke and Vandell
[
7
]
investigated them on grids and hypercubes. Luccio
[
25
]
demonstrated that if
n,n^{\prime}\geq 2
, then
\nabla(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})\geq\left\lceil\frac{(n-1)(n^{\prime}-1)+1}{3}\right\rceil
. Madelaine and Stewart
[
28
]
established that
\nabla(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})\leq\left\lceil\frac{(n-1)(n^{\prime}-1)+1}{3}\right\rceil+2
. Lien et al.
[
24
]
followed by proving
\nabla(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})\leq\left\lceil\frac{(n-1)(n^{\prime}-1)+1}{3}\right\rceil+1
. Pike and Zou
[
32
]
derived
\nabla(C_{n}\cp C_{n^{\prime}})=\left\lceil\frac{3n^{\prime}}{2}\right\rceil
when
n=4
, and
\nabla(C_{n}\cp C_{n^{\prime}})=\left\lceil\frac{nn^{\prime}+2}{3}\right\rceil
otherwise. Recently, and as our main motivation, Wang and Wu
[
38
]
investigated the forest number of Cartesian, direct, and lexicographic products of graphs. Among other things, in
[
38
, Conjecture 5.1]
they put forward the following conjecture, where
S_{n}
denotes the star
K_{1,n-1}
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, then

f(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq f(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\,.

In Section
2
, we prove this conjecture. In the subsequent section, we characterize the trees that attain the equality in the conjecture. If the orders of
T
and
T^{\prime}
are the same, then the equality is attained only if at least one of the factors is a star, while if the orders of
T
and
T^{\prime}
are different, then the equality is attained if and only if the larger factor is a star (and the smaller factor is arbitrary). In Section
4
, we consider the missing case when a star is the smaller factor and establish a sharp lower bound for the decycling number of Cartesian products of graphs based on the matching number of their factors.

To conclude the introduction, we give definition needed in this paper.

The subgraph of a graph
G
induced by
X\subseteq V(G)
is denoted by
G[X]
. A
vertex cover
of
G
is a subset of its vertices that includes at least one endpoint of every edge. The
covering number

\beta(G)
of
G
is the minimum size of a vertex cover of
G
. A
matching
of
G
is a subset of edges such that no two edges from it share a common vertex. The
matching number

\alpha^{\prime}(G)
of
G
is the maximum size of a matching set for the graph.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs. Their
Cartesian product

G\cp H
is a graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
, and two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent if either
g=g^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
, or
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
h=h^{\prime}
. The subgraph of
G\cp H
induced by the set of vertices
\{(g,h):\ g\in V(G)\}
is called a
G
-layer and denoted by
G^{h}
. Similarly, the subgraph of
G\cp H
induced by
\{(g,h):\ h\in V(H)\}
is an
H
-layer
and denoted by
{}^{g}H
. Note that
G^{h}\cong G
for every
g\in V(G)
, and that
{}^{g}H\cong H
for every
h\in V(H)
. Recall that the Cartesian product operation is commutative, that is,
G\cp H\cong H\cp G
, where
\cong
denotes graph isomorphism.

2
Proof of Conjecture
1.1

Conjecture
1.1
will follow from the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, where
n^{\prime}\geq n\geq 2
, then

\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq n-1=\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be trees
V(T)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
V(T^{\prime})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n^{\prime}}\}
, where
n^{\prime}\geq n\geq 2
. Suppose on the contrary that
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-2
and let
S
be a decycling set in
T\cp T^{\prime}
with
|S|=\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-2
. By the pigeonhole principle there exist
i,j\in[n]
,
i\neq j
, such that
S\cap V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
, and there exist
k,\ell\in[n^{\prime}]
,
k\neq\ell
, such that
S\cap V(T^{u_{k}})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(T^{u_{\ell}})=\emptyset
. Consider now the subgraph
X
of
T\cp T^{\prime}
induced by

V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})\cup V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})\cup V(T^{u_{k}})\cup V(T^{u_{\ell}})\,.

X
is a connected graph of order
2n+2n^{\prime}-4
and of size at least
2n+2n^{\prime}-4
. It follows that
X
contains a cycle which is not possible because
S\cap V(X)=\emptyset
. This contradiction proves that
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq n-1
.

By the above,
\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\geq n-1
. Let
V(S_{n^{\prime}})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n^{\prime}}\}
, and let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n^{\prime}-1}\}
be an independent set of
S_{n^{\prime}}
. In this case, the subgraph of
T\cp S_{n^{\prime}}
induced by
V(T)\times(V(S_{n^{\prime}})-\{u_{n^{\prime}}\})
is isomorphic to the disjoint union of
(n^{\prime}-1)
copies of
T
. Consequently, the subgraph induced by

\left(V(T)\times(V(S_{n^{\prime}})-\{u_{n^{\prime}}\})\right)\cup\{(v_{1},u_{n^{\prime}})\}

is a tree. It follows that
\{(v_{i},u_{n^{\prime}}):\ 2\leq i\leq n\}
is a decycling set for
T\cp S_{n^{\prime}}
, and thus
\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\leq n-1
.
∎

Theorem
2.1
in particular implies that
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq n-1=\nabla(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})
. Conjecture
1.1
now follows by applying (
1
). More precisely, since
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq\nabla(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})
, by (
1
) we get
|V(T\cp T^{\prime})|-\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq|V(T\cp T^{\prime})|-\nabla(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})
. Thus we can conclude that

\displaystyle f(T\cp T^{\prime})

\displaystyle=|V(T\cp T^{\prime})|-\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq|V(T\cp T^{\prime})|-\nabla(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=f(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\,,

as desired.

Using the same line of reasoning as above, Theorem
2.1
also yields:

Corollary 2.2
.

If
T
is a nontrivial tree, and
n^{\prime}\geq|V(T)|
an integer, then

f(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})=|V(T)|n^{\prime}-|V(T)|+1\,.

3
The equality case in Theorem
2.1

In this section, we characterize the graphs that attain the equality in Theorem
2.1
and consequently in Conjecture
1.1
. For this sake, the following two results are crucial.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, where
n^{\prime}>n\geq 2
, and
T^{\prime}\not\cong S_{n^{\prime}}
, then

\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq n\,.

Proof.

Let
V(T)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
V(T^{\prime})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n^{\prime}}\}
. Suppose on the contrary that
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-1
and let
S
be a decycling set in
T\cp T^{\prime}
with
|S|=\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-1
. We distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1
:
\exists\ i,j\in[n]
,
i\neq j
, such that
S\cap V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
.

Since
n^{\prime}>n
, there also exist
k,\ell\in[n^{\prime}]
,
k\neq\ell
, such that
S\cap V(T^{u_{k}})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(T^{u_{\ell}})=\emptyset
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
we now consider the subgraph of
T\cp T^{\prime}
induced by
V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})\cup V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})\cup V(T^{u_{k}})\cup V(T^{u_{\ell}})
to arrive at a contradiction.

Case 2
:
S\cap V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
, and
|S\cap V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})|=1
for every
j\in[n]
,
j\neq i
.

Since
T^{\prime}
is not a star, it contains an induced path on four vertices, say
u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}},u_{j_{3}},u_{j_{4}}
. Let further
v_{\ell}
be a neighbor of
v_{i}
in
T
. Let
Y
be the subgraph of

T\cp T^{\prime}
induced by the vertices

\{v_{i},v_{\ell}\}\times\{u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}},u_{j_{3}},u_{j_{4}}\}\,.

The subgraph
Y
is isomorphic to
P_{2}\cp P_{4}
and since
|S\cap V(Y)|\leq 1
we can conclude that
S
is not a decycling set.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees, both of order
n\geq 4
, and none of
T
and
T^{\prime}
is a star, then

\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\geq n.

Proof.

Let
V(T)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
,
V(T^{\prime})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
, and suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-1
. Let
S
be decycling set in
T\cp T^{\prime}
with
|S|=\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq n-1
. If there exit
i,j\in[n]
,
i\neq j
, such that
S\cap V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
, and there exist
k,\ell\in[n]
,
k\neq\ell
, such that
S\cap V(T^{u_{k}})=\emptyset
and
S\cap V(T^{u_{\ell}})=\emptyset
, then we get a contradiction as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By the commutativity of the Cartesian product we may without loss of generality assume that in the remaining case there exists an integer
i\in[n]
such that
S\cap V(^{v_{i}}T^{\prime})=\emptyset
, and
|S\cap V(^{v_{j}}T^{\prime})|=1
for every
j\in[n]
,
j\neq i
. Then we get a contradiction along the same lines as in in Case 2 of the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

The equality case in Theorem
2.1
now reads as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, where
2\leq n\leq n^{\prime}
. The the following holds.

(i)

If
n=n^{\prime}
, then
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})=n-1
if and only if at least one of
T\cong S_{n}
and
T^{\prime}\cong S_{n^{\prime}}
holds.

(ii)

If
n<n^{\prime}
, then
\nabla(T\cp T^{\prime})=n-1
if and only if
T^{\prime}\cong S_{n^{\prime}}

(
and
T
is arbitrary
)
.

Proof.

Combine Theorem
2.1
, Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, and the facts
\nabla(P_{2}\cp P_{2})=1
and
\nabla(P_{3}\cp P_{3})=2
.
∎

Using Theorem
2.1
, Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, and (
1
), we also deduce the following.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, where
2\leq n\leq n^{\prime}
, then
f(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq nn^{\prime}-n+1
. Moreover, the following holds.

(i)

If
n=n^{\prime}
, then
f(T\cp T^{\prime})=nn^{\prime}-n+1
if and only if at least one of
T\cong S_{n}
and
T^{\prime}\cong S_{n^{\prime}}
holds.

(ii)

If
n<n^{\prime}
, then
f(T\cp T^{\prime})=nn^{\prime}-n+1
if and only if
T^{\prime}\cong S_{n^{\prime}}

(
and
T
is arbitrary
)
.

4
Additional results

We have already proven that if
T
is a tree of order
n\geq 2
, and if
n^{\prime}\geq n
, then
\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})=n-1
. In the case when the star has order smaller than
T
, the following holds.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
2\leq n^{\prime}<n
, and
T\not\cong S_{n}
is a tree of order
n
, then

n^{\prime}\leq\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\leq n-1\,.

Proof.

The lower bound follows by Lemma
3.1
. To establish the upper bound, let
V(T)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
V(S_{n^{\prime}})=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n^{\prime}}\}
, where
u_{n^{\prime}}
is the central vertex of
S_{n^{\prime}}
. Just as in the last part of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we deduce that the set
\{(v_{i},u_{n^{\prime}}):\ 2\leq i\leq n\}
is a decycling set in
T\cp S_{n^{\prime}}
. Consequently,
\nabla(T\cp S_{n^{\prime}})\leq n-1
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
T\not\cong S_{n+1}
is a nontrivial tree of order
n+1
, then

\nabla(S_{n}\cp T)=n\quad\text{and}\quad f(S_{n}\cp T)=n^{2}\,.

In the following theorem, we establish a relationship between the decycling number of Cartesian products and the matching number of their factors. For its proof recall that the famous König-Egerváry theorem
[
22
,
13
]
asserts that if
G
is a bipartite graph, then
\beta(G)=\alpha^{\prime}(G)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are graphs, then

\nabla(G_{1}\cp G_{2})\geq\alpha^{\prime}(G_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(G_{2})\,.

Moreover, if
T
is a tree, then
\nabla(T\cp P_{2})=\alpha^{\prime}(T)
.

Proof.

To prove the first assertion, consider maximum matchings
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. Let
V_{i}
,
i\in[2]
, be the set of endvertices of the edges from
M_{i}
. Then the subgraph of
G_{1}\cp G_{2}
induced by the vertices
V_{1}\times V_{2}
is isomorphic to the disjoint union of
\alpha^{\prime}(G_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(G_{2})
copies of
C_{4}
. It follows that any decycling set
S
in
G_{1}\cp G_{2}
must contain at least
\alpha^{\prime}(G_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(G_{2})
elements, that is,
\nabla(G_{1}\cp G_{2})\geq\alpha^{\prime}(G_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(G_{2})
.

Let
T
be a tree. Then by the already proved inequality we have
\nabla(T\cp P_{2})\geq\alpha^{\prime}(T)
. To prove that
\nabla(T\cp P_{2})\leq\alpha^{\prime}(T)
, consider a smallest vertex cover
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{\beta(T)}\}
of
T
. By the definition of a vertex cover,
T[V(T)-W]
is an edgeless graph, and by König-Egerváry theorem,
|W|=\alpha^{\prime}(T)
. Let
V(P_{2})=\{a,b\}
and set
\Omega=\{(a,w_{i}):\ i\in[\beta(T)]\}
. Since
T[V(T)-W]
is an edgeless graph, the subgraph of
T\cp P_{2}
induced by
V(T\cp P_{2})\setminus\Omega
is a tree. Therefore,
\nabla(T\cp P_{1})\leq|W|=\alpha^{\prime}(T)
.
∎

5
Concluding remarks

The main contribution of this paper is to demonstrate Conjecture
1.1
asserting that if
T
and
T^{\prime}
are trees of respective orders
n
and
n^{\prime}
, then
f(T\cp T^{\prime})\leq f(S_{n}\cp S_{n^{\prime}})
, and to characterize graphs that attain the inequality. In
[
38
, Conjecture 5.1]
, it was also conjectured that

f(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})\leq f(T\cp T^{\prime})

holds. This remains open. In partial support of the fact that this conjecture is difficult to verify, we note that
\nabla(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})
(equivalently
f(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})
) is not known yet in all the cases. The decycling number of the Cartesian product of paths has been investigated in
[
6
]
, for the summary of this research see Section 3 in the survey
[
5
]
. Later, progress on
\nabla(P_{n}\cp P_{n^{\prime}})
has been made in
[
24
,
25
,
28
]
, however, to the best of our knowledge, the general solution is not yet known.

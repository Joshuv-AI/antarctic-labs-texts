On the limit law of the superdiffusive elephant random walk
Hélène Guérin, Lucile Laulin, Kilian Raschel, Thomas Simon

Abstract
. When the memory parameter of the elephant random walk is above a critical threshold, the process becomes superdiffusive and, once suitably normalised, converges to a non-Gaussian random variable. In a recent paper by the three first authors, it was shown that this limit variable has a density and that the associated moments satisfy a nonlinear recurrence relation. In this work, we exploit this recurrence to derive an asymptotic expansion of the moments and the asymptotic behaviour of the density at infinity. In particular, we show that an asymmetry in the distribution of the first step of the random walk leads to an asymmetry of the tails of the limit variable. These results follow from a new, explicit expression of the Stieltjes transformation of the moments in terms of special functions such as hypergeometric series and incomplete beta integrals. We also obtain other results about the random variable, such as unimodality and, for certain values of the memory parameter, log-concavity.

Introduction and statement of the results

The one-dimensional elephant random walk (ERW) was introduced in 2004 by Schütz and Trimper
[
26
]
to see how memory could induce subdiffusion in random walk processes. It turned out that the ERW is always at least diffusive; nevertheless, the simple definition of the process, together with the underlying depth of the results, has led to a great deal of interest from mathematicians over the last two decades.

The ERW process
(S_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}

(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}

The elephant starts at the origin at time zero:

In other words, defining for all

{{\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}}

The probability

\displaystyle n^{2}(1-a)^{2}Q(n,n)^{2}\,+\,n(4-6a-2a^{2})Q(n,n)Q(n,n-1)\,+\;4a^{2}Q(n,n-1)^{2}

where in the first inequality we have used the induction hypothesis and

and in the second inequality we have used

for all

Setting

and using (

\displaystyle B_{k,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(np-a(k+1))(np-a(k-1))B_{k,n-1}\,+\,(nq+ak)(nq+a(k-2)B_{k-1,n-1}

\displaystyle\,+\;a^{2}\left(Q(n,k)-Q(n,k-1)\right)^{2}\,-\,(np-a(k+1))(nq+a(k-2))Q(n,k-2)Q(n,k+1)

\displaystyle\,+\;\left(2a^{2}+2(np-ak)(nq+a(k-1))-(nq+ak)(np-a(k-1))\right)Q(n,k-1)Q(n,k)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(np-a(k+1))(nq+a(k-2))\left(Q(n,k-1)Q(n,k)-Q(n,k-2)Q(n,k+1)\right)\,\geqslant\,0,

where the second equality follows from the induction hypothesis. To complete the proof, it remains to show that

is log-concave for all

and
n=4.
The above discussion and the case

with the above notation, which amounts after some simplifications to

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 9(1-a)^{2}Q(3,3)^{2}\,+\,3(3-6a-a^{2})Q(3,3)Q(3,2)\,+\;4a^{2}Q(3,2)^{2}

Proof of Proposition

Multiplying the recurrence relation (

in (

which appears as a mix of an ordinary (algebraic) differential equation and a functional equation, because of the term

A(x)\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}m_{2n}x^{2n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad B(x)\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}m_{2n+1}x^{2n+1},

and isolating the even and odd parts, we obtain the differential system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A\,+\,axA^{\prime}\,-\,p(A^{2}+B^{2})\,-\,(1-p)(A^{2}-B^{2})\,=\,0,\\
B\,+\,axB^{\prime}\,-\,2pAB\,=\,0.\end{array}\right.

(16)

Using the second equation we express
A
in terms of
B
and
B^{\prime}
, and then plugging this identity in the first equation, we obtain after cleaning denominators and simplification an autonomous, classical ODE on

a(a+1)x^{2}BB^{\prime\prime}-a(a+2)x^{2}B^{\prime 2}+x((a+1)^{2}-2)BB^{\prime}-(a+1)^{2}B^{4}+B^{2}\,=\,0.

(17)

The following lemma shows that

is also the solution to an implicit equation, involving the function
F
in (
13

is the first place where the non-trivial quantity

appears.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
x\in(0,1/\rho_{a})
, the function
B(x)
is the unique solution of

x^{1/a}F\left((x^{1/a}B(x))^{a/(a+1)}\right)\,+\,{{\left(\rho_{a}x\right)}}^{1/a}\,=\,1.

(18)

B(x)\to\infty
as
x\to 1/\rho_{a}
and the convergence radius of the series defining

is
1/\rho_{a}.

Proof.

We first remark that the solution of (
18
) exists and is uniquely defined as

B(x)\,=\,x^{-1/a}\left(F^{-1}(x^{-1/a}-\rho_{a}^{1/a})\right)^{\frac{a+1}{a}},

(19)

which is a smooth function on
(0,1/\rho_{a}).
Moreover, since

x^{1/a}F\left((x^{1/a}B(x))^{a/(a+1)}\right)\,=\,\frac{x^{1/a}}{a}\int_{(x^{1/a}B(x))^{a/(a+1)}}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}}{{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}-1\right)}}\,+\,{{\left(\frac{x}{B(x)}\right)}}^{1/(a+1)}

, we obtain
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{x}{B(x)}=1,
whence
B
is right-differentiable at zero with

and
B^{\prime}(0)=1.

We now prove that
B
satisfies (
17
). Dividing (
18
) by

and differentiating with respect to

\frac{1}{a+1}\left(1+ax\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)=\left(\frac{B}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\sqrt{1+x^{\frac{2}{a+1}}B^{\frac{2a}{a+1}}}.

\left(\frac{B}{x}\right)^{\frac{2}{a+1}}=-B^{2}+\frac{1}{(a+1)^{2}}\left(1+ax\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}.

(20)

Differentiating (
20
) with respect to
x
and multiplying by

, we obtain

\frac{2}{a+1}\left(\frac{B}{x}\right)^{\prime}\left(\frac{B}{x}\right)^{\frac{2}{a+1}}=\frac{B}{x}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}x}\left(-B^{2}+\frac{1}{(a+1)^{2}}\left(1+ax\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}\right)^{2}\right).

(21)

Using both (
20
) and (
21
), we can eliminate the term
(\frac{B}{x})^{\frac{2}{a+1}}
(which contains a non-integer power of
B
), and obtain a classical ODE on

. After a few simplifications, we exactly obtain (
17
).

We now show that any solution
B
of (

and

must satisfy (

), which will complete the proof. Observe that by (
17
), we necessarily have

. Moreover, the previous discussion shows that for any
\alpha>0
, the function

x^{-1/a}\,-\,\frac{1}{a}\int_{\bigl(x^{1/a}B(x)\bigr)^{a/(a+1)}}^{\alpha}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}

is a constant

. It remains to compute this constant.
We observe that

c_{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{1}{a}\int_{\bigl(x^{1/a}B(x)\bigr)^{a/(a+1)}}^{\alpha}{{\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\right)}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}}\,+\,x^{-1/a}{{\left(1-{{\left(\frac{x}{B(x)}\right)}}^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\right)}}\,+\,\alpha^{-1/a}.

Since

, taking the limit when

, we deduce

c_{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\alpha}{{\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\right)}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}}\,+\,\alpha^{-1/a}\,\to\,\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\right)}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}}

as

We finally compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\right)}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{1+1/a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh v-1}{{{\left(\sinh v\right)}}^{1+1/a}}\,{\mathrm{d}}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-1/a}}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{{{\left(\sinh\frac{v}{2}\right)}}^{1/a-1}{{\left(\cosh\frac{v}{2}\right)}}^{1+1/a}}

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-1/a}}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}x}{x^{1/2a}(1+x)^{1+1/2a}}

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-1/a}\Gamma{{\left(1+\frac{1}{a}\right)}}}{\Gamma{{\left(1+\frac{1}{2a}\right)}}}\,\Gamma{{\left(1-\frac{1}{2a}\right)}}\,=\,\frac{{\rm B}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2a},1-\frac{1}{2a}\bigl)}{2}\,=\,\rho_{a}^{1/a}

3.3.
Elementary properties of the exponential growth

We have the following property of the exponential rate of growth of the sequence
\{m_{n}\}
. See Figure
2
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The function
a\mapsto\rho_{a}
is decreasing and convex on
(1/2,1)
from
\infty
to 1 with

\rho_{a}\,\sim\,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a-1/2}}\;\;\;\mbox{as $a\to 1/2$}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\rho_{a}-1\,\sim\,(1-a)\log 2\;\;\;\mbox{as $a\to 1.$}

Proof.

We start with the decreasing property. Setting
b=1/2a\in(1/2,1),
we need to show that function

f(b)\,=\,\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(1/2+b){\Gamma}(1-b)}{{\Gamma}(1/2){\Gamma}(1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}\,=\,\left(\prod_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(1/2+n)(1+n)}{(1/2+b+n)(1-b+n)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}

increases on
(1/2,1),
where in the second equality we have used the classical product representation of the Gamma function – see e.g. Theorem 1.1.2 in
[
1
]
. Taking the logarithm, we are reduced to show that the function
b\mapsto b^{-1}g_{n}(b)
increases on
(1/2,1)
for each
n\geqslant 0,
with

g_{n}(b)\,=\,\log(1/2+n)\,+\,\log(1+n)\,-\,\log(1/2+b+n)\,-\,\log(1-b+n).

The function
g_{n}
is strictly convex on
[1/2,1)
with
g_{n}(1/2)=0
and

g_{n}^{\prime}(b)\,=\,\frac{2b-1/2}{(1/2+b+n)(1-b+n)}\,>\,0\qquad\mbox{for all $b\in[1/2,1).$}

Therefore, the function

\frac{g_{n}(b)}{b}\,=\,\left(\frac{b-1/2}{b}\right)\times\left(\frac{g_{n}(b)-g_{n}(1/2)}{b-1/2}\right)

increases on
(1/2,1)
as the product of two positive increasing functions. Moreover, the function
a\mapsto 2ag_{n}(1/2a)
has second derivative
(1/2a^{3})g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(1/a)>0
on
(1/2,1]
and is hence convex. This implies that the mapping
a\mapsto\rho_{a}
is log-convex and hence convex on
(1/2,1).
Last, we compute

(a-1/2)^{a}\rho_{a}\,=\,\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2a}\right)\Gamma\left(2-\frac{1}{2a}\right)\right)^{a}\,\to\,\frac{1}{2}\quad\mbox{as $a\to 1/2,$}

whence the first asymptotics. Setting
\psi(z)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(z)}{\Gamma(z)}
for the usual digamma function, we have

\left(\log\rho_{a}\right)^{\prime}\,=\,\frac{1}{2a}\bigl(\log\rho_{a}\,+\,\psi(1-1/2a)\,-\,\psi(1/2+1/2a)\bigr)\,\to\,\frac{\psi(1/2)-\psi(1)}{2}\,=\,-\log 2\quad\mbox{as $a\to 1$}

whence the second asymptotics.

\square

Remark 3.6
.

The decreasing character of
a\mapsto\rho_{a}
on

also follows from Hölder’s inequality and the following representation:

\rho_{a}\,=\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{{\bf\Gamma}_{1/2}}{{\bf\Gamma}_{1}}\right)^{\!\frac{1}{2a}}\right]^{a}

where

stands for the standard Gamma random variable with parameter

and the quotient inside the expectation is independent.

As a second note, applying Theorem 1.6.2 (ii) in
[
1

it is possible to show that

\log\rho_{a}\,=\,-\log 2\,+\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ax}\,f(x)\,dx

for all

where

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}}\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\left(2-e^{-\frac{x}{2n+1}}\left(1+\frac{x}{2n+1}\right)\,+\,e^{\frac{x}{2n+2}}\left(\frac{x}{2n+2}-1\right)\right)

is easily shown to be positive on

This implies that the mapping

with limit

at infinity.

3.4.
Density asymptotics at the logarithmic scale

We end this section with a logarithmic estimate of
\varphi(x)
as
x\to\pm\infty.
This less precise version of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
can be quickly derived from Theorem
3.1
and Kasahara’s Tauberian theorem for densities.

Proposition 3.7
.

As
x\to\infty
, one has

\log\varphi(x)\,\sim\,\log\varphi(-x)\,\sim\,-(1-a)\left(\frac{a^{a}x}{\rho_{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-a}}.

Proof.

Along the proof we will use the classical Mittag-Leffler function
E_{a}
defined by

E_{a}(z)\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{z^{n}}{{\Gamma}(1+an)},

and Mittag-Leffler random variables
M_{a}
with moment generating function

{\mathbb{E}}[e^{rM_{a}}]=E_{a}(r)
.

We begin the proof of Proposition
3.7
with the estimate on the positive axis. Introduce the moment generating function
\Psi(r)={\mathbb{E}}[e^{rL_{1}}]=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\bigl(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[L_{1}^{n}]}{n!}\bigr)r^{n}
as in (
5
),
which is analytic on
{\mathbb{C}}
by Stirling’s formula combined with the equivalence

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[L_{1}^{n}]}{n!}\,\sim\,\frac{2a\,\rho_{a}^{n}}{(a+1)\,{\Gamma}(1+an)}

given by Theorem
3.1
. The latter estimate also implies

\Psi(r)\,\sim\,\frac{2a}{a+1}\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(\rho_{a}r)^{n}}{{\Gamma}(1+an)}\,=\,\frac{2a\,E_{a}(\rho_{a}r)}{a+1}\,\sim\,\frac{2\,e^{(\rho_{a}r)^{1/a}}}{a+1}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty,$}

where in the second equivalence we have used the well-known estimate for the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{a}
on the positive half-line, to be found e.g. in Formula (3.4.14) of
[
19
]
. Combining this estimate on
\Psi(r)
with Kasahara’s theorem for densities – see Theorem 4.12.11 in
[
6
]
– yields after some easy algebra the required estimate

\log\varphi(x)\,\sim\,-(1-a)\left(\frac{a^{a}x}{\rho_{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-a}}\qquad\mbox{as $x\to\infty.$}

We now proceed to the estimate on the negative axis. Let
M_{a}
be a Mittag-Leffler random variable and let
B_{a}
be an independent Bernoulli random variable with parameter
\frac{2a}{a+1}\cdot
The random variable
Y_{a}=(\rho_{a}B_{a})\times M_{a}
has positive integer moments

{\mathbb{E}}[Y^{n}_{a}]\,=\,\frac{2a\,\rho_{a}^{n}\,n!}{(1+a)\,\Gamma(1+an)}\cdot

Consider the function

{\widetilde{\Psi}}(r)\,=\,\Psi(-r)\,-\,{\mathbb{E}}[e^{-rY_{a}}]\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{\mu_{n}}{n!}\,r^{n}

with

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\left({\mathbb{E}}[L_{1}^{n}]-\frac{2a\,\rho_{a}^{n}\,n!}{(1+a)\,\Gamma(1+an)}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a\kappa_{a}\,\rho_{a}^{n}\,(\delta_{a})_{n}}{(a+1)\,\Gamma(1+an)}\left(1\,+\,(-1)^{n}\frac{a-1}{3a+1}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(n^{-\delta_{a}}\right)\right)\,\asymp\,\frac{\rho_{a}^{n}\,(\delta_{a})_{n}}{\Gamma(1+an)}

by Theorem
3.1
and (
9
). Here and throughout, for two real sequences
\{u_{n}\}
and
\{v_{n}\},
the notation
u_{n}\asymp v_{n}
means that there exist an index
n_{0}\geqslant 1
and two constants
c,C>0
such that
cv_{n}\leqslant u_{n}\leqslant Cu_{n}
for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
. The upper and lower bounds in (
29
) follow from
0<\frac{1-a}{3a+1}<1.

Introduce the following two-parameter generalization of the Mittag-Leffler function, called the Prabhakar function

E^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(\gamma)_{n}\,z^{n}}{n!\,\Gamma(\beta+\alpha n)},

(30)

which is analytic on
{\mathbb{C}}
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
. Using (
29
), we deduce that

\log{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{r|L_{1}|}{\bf 1}_{\{L_{1}\leqslant 0\}}\right]\,\sim\,\log{\widetilde{\Psi}}(r)\,\sim\,\log E^{\delta_{a}}_{a,1}(\rho_{a}r)\,\sim\,(\rho_{a}r)^{1/a}\,\to\,\infty\quad\mbox{as $r\to\infty,$}

where the first equivalence is clear by the positivity of
Y_{a}
and for the third equivalence, we have used the global estimate presented in Theorem 3 of
[
17
]
– see also
[
29
]
. As in the case of the positive axis, the conclusion follows from Kasahara’s Tauberian theorem for densities.

\square

Remark 3.8

If the sequence

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Strategy of the proof

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
, with the following explicit value of the prefactor
c_{a}
:

c_{a}\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi(1-a^{2})(1+a)}}\left(\frac{a}{\rho_{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2(1-a)}}.

(31)

Introducing the random variable
X=-L_{1}/\rho_{a}
with density function
{\widetilde{\varphi}}(x)=\rho_{a}\,\varphi(-\rho_{a}x)
on
{\mathbb{R}},
the required estimate amounts to

{\widetilde{\varphi}}(-x)\,\sim\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi(1-a^{2})(1+a)}}\;a^{\frac{1}{2(1-a)}}\,x^{\frac{2a-1}{2(1-a)}}\,e^{-(1-a)\left(a^{a}x\right)^{\frac{1}{1-a}}},\qquad x\to\infty.

(32)

To prove (
32
), we will use the strong Tauberian theorem given in
[
15
, Thm 3]
. This result allows us to derive the asymptotics of the density of a random variable
X
, after checking three technical conditions involving the following functionals of the distribution of

:

\omega(r)\,=\,{\mathbb{E}}[e^{-rX}],\quad\xi(r)\,=\,-\frac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{\omega(r)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta^{2}(r)\,=\,-\xi^{\prime}(r)\,=\,\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\omega(r)}\,-\,\xi^{2}(r).

(33)

We divide the proof into two steps: first, we will derive the asymptotic behaviour of the functions introduced in (
33
). In the second step, we will use these estimates to verify the three conditions of
[
15
, Thm 3]

Detailed proof of Theorem
1.2

First step: asymptotics of the functions (
33
).

Let us first note that the functions defined in (
33
)
are all smooth real functions, since
\omega(r)
is smooth and never vanishes on
{\mathbb{R}}
as a moment generating function with infinite radius of convergence. As a consequence of (
11
) in Theorem
3.1
and the estimate
an{\Gamma}(1+an)\sim{\Gamma}(2+an),
we have the expansion

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n}]}{n!}\,=\,\frac{2a\,(-1)^{n}}{(a+1)}\!\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(1+an)}+\kappa_{a}\frac{(\delta_{a})_{n}((-1)^{n}-\frac{1-a}{3a+1})}{n!\,{\Gamma}(1+an)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(\frac{(2\delta_{a})_{n}}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2+an)}\!\right)\!\right)

as
n\to\infty.
Recalling the above notation (
30
) of the Prabhakar function
E^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(z),
we deduce

\displaystyle\omega(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n}\,{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n}]}{n!}\right)r^{n}

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{a+1}\left(E_{a}(r)+\kappa_{a}\left(E_{a,1}^{\delta_{a}}(-r)+\frac{a-1}{3a+1}\,E_{a,1}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\right)\right)+{\rm O}\left(E_{a,2}^{2\delta_{a}}(r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a+1}\left(1\,-\,{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{a}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-4/(a+1)}\right)\right),

with the notation

{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{a}\,=\,\frac{\kappa_{a}a^{1-\delta_{a}}(1-a)}{3a+1}

and where for the equality (
34
) we have used the estimates

E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(r)\,=\,\frac{r^{\frac{\gamma-\beta}{\alpha}}}{\alpha^{\gamma}}\,e^{r^{1/\alpha}}\left(1\,+\,{\rm O}(r^{-1/\alpha})\right)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(-r)\,=\,{\rm O}(r^{-\gamma})\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty,$}

which are valid for every

– see Theorem 3 in
[
17
]
. A similar argument yields

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+1}]}{n!}\,=\,\frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{(a+1)}\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(a+an)}\,+\,\kappa_{a}\frac{(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n+1}-\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(a+an)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(\frac{(2\delta_{a})_{n}}{n!\,{\Gamma}(1+a+an)}\right)\right)

which implies, by the same estimates on the Prabhakar function,

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\,{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+1}]}{n!}\right)r^{n}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{a+1}\left(E_{a,a}^{1}(r)+\kappa_{a}\left(E_{a,a}^{\delta_{a}}(-r)+\frac{a-1}{3a+1}\,E_{a,a}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\right)\right)+{\rm O}\left(E_{a,1+a}^{2\delta_{a}}(r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,r^{1/a-1}\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a(a+1)}\left(1\,-\,{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{a}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-4/(a+1)}\right)\right).

Combining (
34

\xi(r)\,=\,-\frac{r^{1/a-1}}{a}\left(1\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-4/(a+1)}\right)\right).

(36)

Then, after some simplifications, the details of which are left to the reader, we obtain the estimate

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+2}]}{n!}\,=\,\frac{2\,(-1)^{n}}{a(a+1)}\,\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(2a-1+an)}\,+\,\kappa_{a}\,\frac{(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n}\,-\,\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2a-1+an)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(\frac{(2\delta_{a})_{n}}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2a+an)}\right)\right),

which yields similarly

\omega^{\prime\prime}(r)\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n}\,{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+2}]}{n!}\right)r^{n}\,=\,\frac{2\,r^{2/a-2}\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a^{2}(a+1)}\left(1\,-\,{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{a}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-4/(a+1)}\right)\right).

Putting this estimate together with (

whence our final estimate

\eta(r)\,=\,\frac{\sqrt{1-a}\,r^{1/2a-1}}{a}\left(1\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-4/(a+1)}\right)\right),

concluding the first part of the proof.

\square

Second step: checking the assumptions of
[

]

We are now in position to apply the strong Tauberian theorem of

]
. Specifically, with the notation of

]

large enough.

The estimate (
34
) remains valid in the half-plane
\{\Re(z)\geqslant 0\},
and applying Theorem 3 in
[
17
]
to the functions
E_{a}(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))
,
E_{a,1}^{\delta_{a}}(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))
,
E_{a,1}^{\delta_{a}}(-r-{\rm i}s/\eta(r))

and
E_{a,1}^{2\delta_{a}}(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r)),
we obtain

\left|\omega(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))\right|\,\leqslant\,\left(\frac{2\,\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}}\right|}{a+1}\,+\,\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(1-a\delta_{a})}\left(\frac{\eta(r)}{\sqrt{r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)+s^{2}}}\right)^{\delta_{a}}\right)\left(1\,+\,{\rm O}(r^{-2a/(a+1)})\right)

where the Landau symbol
{\rm O}
does not depend on
s
since
|r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r)|\geqslant r
for all
r\geqslant 0
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}.
We hence need to show that there exists
g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

such that

\left(\frac{r\eta(r)}{\sqrt{r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)+s^{2}}}\right)\left(\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}-\,r^{1/a}}\right|\,+\,e^{-r^{1/a}}\left(\frac{\eta(r)}{\sqrt{r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)+s^{2}}}\right)^{\delta_{a}}\right)\,\leqslant\,g(s)

uniformly in
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
r
large enough. Since
r\eta(r)\to\infty
and
re^{-r^{1/a}}\eta(r)^{1+\delta_{a}}\to 0
as
r\to\infty,
one has

re^{-r^{1/a}}\left(\frac{\eta(r)}{\sqrt{r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)+s^{2}}}\right)^{1+\delta_{a}}\,\leqslant\,\left(1+s^{2}\right)^{-(1+\delta_{a})/2}\,\in\,{\mathcal{L}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

uniformly in
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
r
large enough, and we are reduced to show that there exists
r_{0}>0
and
h\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}-\,r^{1/a}}\right|\,\leqslant\,h(s)

(37)

for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\geqslant r_{0}.
Setting
z=z(s,r)=s^{2}/r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)\geqslant 0
for concision, the binomial theorem implies

\displaystyle\log\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}-\,r^{1/a}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{1/a}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1/a)_{2n}}{(2n)!}\,(-z)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(1-a)z\,r^{1/a}}{a^{2}}\right)\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(2-1/a)_{2n}}{(2n+2)!}\,(-z)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(1-a)z\,r^{1/a}}{2a^{2}}\right)\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(1-1/2a)_{n}(3/2-1/2a)_{n}}{(3/2)_{n}(n+1)!}\,(-z)^{n}

with the usual notation for the Pochhammer symbol
(x)_{n}
and where in the last equality we have used the Gauss multiplication formula given e.g. at the beginning of Chapter 1.5 in
[
1
]
. Introducing the hypergeometric function

F_{a}(u)\,=\,{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{1-1/2a,,3/2-1/2a}{3/2};u\biggr]\,=\,\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(1-1/2a)_{n}(3/2-1/2a)_{n}}{(3/2)_{n}n!}\,u^{n},

this function is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}
cut along the real axis from
1
to
\infty
since
3/2>3/2-1/2a>0
, see e.g. Theorem 2.2.1 in
[
1
]
, and we have

\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{(1-1/2a)_{n}(3/2-1/2a)_{n}}{(3/2)_{n}(n+1)!}\,(-z)^{n}\,=\,\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}F_{a}(-u)\,du.

Moreover, Pfaff’s formula – see e.g. Theorem 2.2.5 in
[
1
]
– and the fact that
1-1/2a>0
imply

F_{a}(-u)\,=\,(1+u)^{1/2a-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{1-1/2a,1/2a}{3/2};\frac{u}{u+1}\biggr]\,>\,0

and

\displaystyle-F_{a}^{\prime}(-u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(2a-1)(3a-1)}{3a^{2}}\right){}_{2}F_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{2-1/2a,,5/2-1/2a}{5/2};-u\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(2a-1)(3a-1)(1+u)^{1/2a-2}}{3a^{2}}\right){}_{2}F_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{2-1/2a,1/2a}{5/2};\frac{u}{u+1}\biggr]\,<\,0

for all
u\in{\mathbb{R}}.
Since
1/r^{2}\eta^{2}(r)={\rm O}(r^{-1/a}),
there exists
r_{1}>0
such that
z=z(s,r)<s^{2}
for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\geqslant r_{1},
and by concavity we then obtain

\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{z}F_{a}(-u)\,du\,\geqslant\,\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{0}^{s^{2}}F_{a}(-u)\,du.

Putting everything together, we see that there exists
r_{0}\geqslant r_{1}
such that

\log\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}-\,r^{1/a}}\right|\,\leqslant\,-\left(\frac{(1-a)\,r^{1/a-2}}{2a^{2}\,\eta^{2}(r)}\right)\int_{0}^{s^{2}}F_{a}(-u)\,du\,\leqslant\,-\frac{1}{4}\,\int_{0}^{s^{2}}F_{a}(-u)\,du

for every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\geqslant r_{0},
where the second inequality follows from the above equivalent for
\eta(r).
The aforementioned Pfaff formula combined with the Gauss summation formula given e.g. in Theorem 2.2.2 of
[
1
]
show that

F_{a}(-u)\,\sim\,\left(\frac{\pi}{2{\Gamma}(1+1/2a){\Gamma}(3/2-1/2a)}\right)u^{1/2a-1}

as
u\to\infty,
and integrating this equivalent finally shows that there exists
c>0
such that

\log\left|e^{(r+{\rm i}s/\eta(r))^{1/a}-\,r^{1/a}}\right|\,\leqslant\,-c|s|^{1/a}

for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
r\geqslant r_{0},
which gives (
37
) and completes the proof.

\square

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
, with the following explicit value of the prefactor
\widehat{c}_{a}
:

{\widehat{c}}_{a}\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-a)}}\left(\frac{\rho_{a}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3a-1}{2(1-a^{2})}}\frac{\rho_{a}^{\frac{2}{a+1}}}{2\,(a+1)^{\frac{1-a}{1+a}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\right)}\cdot

(38)

The proof is analogous to Theorem
1.2
except that here we need an estimate of
\xi(-r)
and
\eta(-r)
as
r\to\infty,
with the previous notation (
33
). Using (
11
) up to the fifth order and reasoning as in the above proof, we first obtain

\displaystyle\omega(-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a}{a+1}\left(\kappa_{a}\,E_{a,1}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,a\kappa_{1}^{-}E_{a,2}^{2\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,a\kappa_{2}^{-}E_{a,2}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,{\rm O}\left(E_{a,3}^{1}(r)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,a^{\frac{2a}{a+1}}\kappa_{a}\,r^{-2/(a+1)}\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a+1}\left(1\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{1}}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{2}}r^{-1/a}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-2/a(a+1)}\right)\right)

with

{\bar{\kappa}_{1}}\,=\,\frac{a^{\frac{2a}{a+1}}\kappa_{1}^{-}}{\kappa_{a}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\bar{\kappa}_{2}}\,=\,\frac{a\kappa_{2}^{-}}{\kappa_{a}}\cdot

Similarly, using the expansion

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+1}]}{n!}\,=\,\frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{(a+1)}\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(a+an)}\,+\,\kappa_{a}\frac{(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n+1}-\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(a+an)}\right.\\
\left.\,+\,\frac{a\,(2\delta_{a})_{n}\,\bigl((-1)^{n+1}\kappa_{1}^{-}+\kappa_{1}^{+}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(1+a+an)}\,+\,\frac{a\,(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n+1}\kappa_{2}^{-}+\kappa_{2}^{+}-\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(1+a+an)}\right.\\
\left.\,-\,\frac{2a^{2}\,\kappa_{a}\,(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n+1}-\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{(a+1)\,n!\,{\Gamma}(1+a+an)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(2+a+an)}\right)\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{a+1}\left(\kappa_{a}\,E_{a,a}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,a\kappa_{1}^{-}E_{a,1+a}^{2\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,a\left(\kappa_{2}^{-}-\frac{2a\,\kappa_{a}}{a+1}\right)E_{a,1+a}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,{\rm O}\left(E_{a,2+a}^{1}(r)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\kappa_{a}\,r^{1/a-1-2/(a+1)}\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a^{\delta_{a}}(a+1)}\left(1\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{1}}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{3}}r^{-1/a}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-2/a(a+1)}\right)\right)

with

{\bar{\kappa}_{3}}\,=\,{\bar{\kappa}_{2}}\,-\,\frac{2a^{2}}{a+1}\cdot

This leads to

\xi(-r)\,=\,-\frac{r^{1/a-1}}{a}\left(1\,-\,\frac{2a^{2}\,r^{-1/a}}{a+1}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-2/a(a+1)}\right)\right).

(39)

We next compute

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[X^{n+2}]}{n!}\,=\,\,=\,\frac{2\,(-1)^{n}}{a(a+1)}\,\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(2a-1+an)}\,+\,\kappa_{a}\,\frac{(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n}\,-\,\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2a-1+an)}\right.\\
\left.\,+\,\frac{a\,(2\delta_{a})_{n}\,\bigl((-1)^{n}\kappa_{1}^{-}+\kappa_{1}^{+}\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2a+an)}\,+\,\frac{a\,(\delta_{a})_{n}\bigl((-1)^{n}\kappa_{2}^{-}+\kappa_{2}^{+}-\bigr)}{n!\,{\Gamma}(2a+an)}\right.\\
\left.\,+\,\frac{(1-5a^{2})\,\kappa_{a}\,(\delta_{a})_{n}(\bigl(-1)^{n}-\frac{1-a}{3a+1}\bigr)}{(a+1)\,n!\,{\Gamma}(2a+an)}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(\frac{1}{{\Gamma}(1+2a+an)}\right)\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime\prime}(-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{a(a+1)}\left(\kappa_{a}\,E_{a,2a-1}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,a\kappa_{1}^{-}E_{a,2a}^{2\delta_{a}}(r)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\,+\,\left(a\kappa_{2}^{-}+\frac{(1-5a^{2})\,\kappa_{a}}{a+1}\right)E_{a,2a}^{\delta_{a}}(r)\,+\,{\rm O}\left(E_{a,1+2a}^{1}(r)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\kappa_{a}\,r^{2/a-2-2/(a+1)}\,e^{r^{1/a}}}{a^{1+\delta_{a}}(a+1)}\left(1\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{1}}r^{-2/(a+1)}\,+\,{\bar{\kappa}_{4}}r^{-1/a}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-2/a(a+1)}\right)\right)

with

{\bar{\kappa}_{4}}\,=\,{\bar{\kappa}_{2}}\,+\,\frac{1-5a^{2}}{a+1}\cdot

This leads to

\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}(-r)}{\omega(-r)}\,=\,\frac{r^{2/a-2}}{a^{2}}\left(1\,+\,\frac{(1-5a^{2})\,r^{-1/a}}{a+1}\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-2/a(a+1)}\right)\right)

and finally, combining this estimate with (
39
), to

\eta(-r)\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}(-r)}{\omega(-r)}\,-\,\xi^{2}(-r)}\,=\,\frac{\sqrt{1-a}\,r^{1/2a-1}}{a}\left(1\,+\,{\rm O}\left(r^{-(1-a)/a(a+1)}\right)\right).

(40)

Similarly as on the positive axis, we can then apply Theorem 3 in

15
]

{\widetilde{\varphi}}(x)\,\sim\,\left(\frac{a^{\frac{1-3a}{2(1-a^{2})}}\rho_{a}^{\frac{2}{a+1}}}{\sqrt{8\pi(1-a)}(a+1)^{\delta_{a}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\right)}\right)x^{\frac{2a^{2}-3a-1}{2(1-a^{2})}}\,e^{-(1-a)\left(a^{a}x\right)^{\frac{1}{1-a}}},\qquad x\to\infty,

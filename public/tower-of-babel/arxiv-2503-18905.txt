A Brill-Noether theorem for (toric) surfaces
Alessio Cela and Carl Lian

Abstract
. The classical Brill-Noether theorem states that a map from a general curve to a projective space deforms in a family of expected dimension as long as its image does not lie in any hyperplane. In this note, we observe, as a direct consequence of standard results on Severi varieties, an analogous statement for maps from a general curve to any smooth, projective surface. Namely, a non-constant map deforms in a family of expected dimension as long as its image has anti-canonical degree at least 4. In the case of toric surfaces, curves of anti-canonical degree at most 3 admit a particularly elegant description in terms certain toric contractions. We raise the question of whether a Brill-Noether theorem could hold for toric varieties of higher dimension.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a general curve over
\mathbb{C}
, by which we mean a general point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Classical Brill-Noether theory concerns maps from
C
to projective spaces. One formulation of the Brill-Noether theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a morphism of degree
d
which is
non-degenerate
, that is, the image of
f
does not lie in any hyperplane. Then, the map
f
moves in a family of expected dimension. That is, the moduli space of maps
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}(C,\mathbb{P}^{r})
of maps
C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
d
is pure of dimension

(r+1)d+r(1-g),

the expected, in a neighborhood of
f
.

In fact, the Gieseker-Petri theorem asserts further that the moduli space of maps is
smooth
at
f
. Indeed, a map
f
whose image does not lie in a hyperplane is given, up to automorphisms on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, by a base-point free, rank
r
linear series, that is, a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
C
and a
(r+1)
-dimensional space of sections
V\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
. The moduli space
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}(C)
of rank
r
, degree
d
linear series is smooth of dimension
\rho(g,r,d)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)

[
ACGH85
, Theorem V.1.6]
.

In this note, we prove the following analogous statement for maps to a smooth, projective surface.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective surface. Let
f:C\to S
be a morphism with
f_{*}[C]=\beta\neq 0
, and let
C^{\prime}=f(C)
be its scheme-theoretic image. Suppose that
[C^{\prime}]\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 4
. Then, the map
f
moves in a family of expected dimension. That is, the moduli space of maps
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is pure of dimension

\beta\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}+2(1-g),

the expected, in a neighborhood of
f
. The moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is furthermore generically smooth in a neighborhood of
f
.

When
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the curves of anti-canonical degree at most 3 are exactly lines, so Theorem
1.2
recovers the classical Brill-Noether theorem when
r=2
. Theorem
1.2
is proven in §
2
. As we will see, it is a consequence of well-known transversality results for Severi varieties of
embedded
curves on
S
; the work is to compare dimensions for moduli of maps which may or may not be birational onto their image. This comparison is also standard, see for example
[
Vak00
, §3]
.

If
S
is a
toric
surface, then the loci on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
that must be removed in order to obtain a moduli space that is pure of expected dimension, namely the loci of multiple covers of curves of anti-canonical degree at most 3, admit a particularly simple description. The following is proven in §
3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective toric surface. Let
C^{\prime}\subset S
be an integral curve with
[C^{\prime}]\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\leq 3
, and assume that
C^{\prime}
is not a component of the toric boundary. Then, one of the following holds:

(i)

[C^{\prime}]\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}=2
, in which case there exists a toric morphism
\pi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
contracting
C^{\prime}
to a point, or

(ii)

[C^{\prime}]\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}=3
, in which case there exists a
fake toric projective plane

S^{\prime}
(Definition
3.1
) and a birational toric morphism
\pi:S\to S^{\prime}
induced by deletion of rays of the fan of
S
, such that
\pi(C^{\prime})
lies in the primitive linear system of
S^{\prime}
.

We suspect that Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
are known in some form to experts, but we were not able to locate these precise statements in the literature. Combining these statements, one obtains a “Brill-Noether theorem” for toric surfaces.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
S
be a smooth, projective toric surface. Let
f:C\to S
be a morphism with
f_{*}[C]=\beta\neq 0
, and let
C^{\prime}=f(C)
be its scheme-theoretic image. Assume that
C^{\prime}
is not contained in the toric boundary of
S
, and furthermore that
C^{\prime}
satisfies neither (i) nor (ii) of Theorem
1.3
. Then, the map
f
moves in a family of expected dimension. The moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is furthermore generically smooth in a neighborhood of
f
.

In other words, if
f
fails to move in a family of expected dimension, then the failure is explained by the degeneracy of
f
after post-composing with a certain toric morphism to either a projective line or to a fake toric projective plane.

Question 1.5
.

Does Corollary
1.4
admit a generalization to smooth, projective toric varieties of any dimension?

Classical Brill-Noether theory concerns sections of line bundles on curves, and maps to toric varieties are also governed by sections of line bundles on the source
[
Cox95
]
, so this seems to be a natural generalized setting for Brill-Noether theory. While the methods of this note are highly specific to the case of surfaces, some hope that an extension to higher dimension could be possible is afforded by the following result of Farkas.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
Far08
, Theorem 0.1]

Let
b:Y\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
be the blow-up along a (torus-invariant) linear space. Let
f:C\to Y
be a morphism with
f_{*}[C]=\beta
. Suppose that
b(f(C))\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
is not contained in any hyperplane. Then, the moduli space of maps
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,Y)
is pure of dimension

c_{1}(K_{Y}^{\vee})\cdot\beta+r(1-g),

the expected, in a neighborhood of
f
.

Farkas’s result is stated in the language of linear series with secants, but the formulation above is equivalent. See also
[
Cot11
,
Ung21
,
Far23
]
for closely related results.

Recall that the Gieseker-Petri theorem asserts further that the moduli space of non-degenerate maps from a general curve to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is smooth. Equivalently, if
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
is non-degenerate, then it is unobstructed, that is, we have
H^{1}(C,f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{r}})=0
. We do not know whether this remains the case in the settings of Corollary
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
.

Question 1.7
.

Let
f:C\to S
(resp.
f:C\to Y
) be a map as in Corollary
1.4
(resp. Theorem
1.6
). Is the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
(resp.
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,Y)
) smooth at
f
?

Whereas Corollary
1.4
gives that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is
generically
smooth in a neighborhood of
f
, generic smoothness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,Y)
in the setting of Theorem
1.6
does not seem to follow directly from Farkas’s proof. Note that Questions
1.5
and
1.7
are only interesting when
C
has positive genus. Indeed, a rational curve generically contained in the interior of a smooth, projective toric variety is free, and therefore unobstructed. (Rational curves contained in the boundary are more naturally viewed as maps to the appropriate toric stratum.)

2.
Severi varieties and moduli of maps

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and let
\beta\in H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
be a non-zero curve class on
S
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
be the Severi variety of reduced, irreducible embedded curves
C^{\prime}\subset S
of geometric genus
g
with
[C^{\prime}]=\beta
.

The following facts are standard; see, for example Harris-Morrison
[
HM98
, Corollary 3.42]
and its proof. The results are stated there for
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, but only the positivity assumptions on the curve class
\beta
are needed.

Theorem 2.2
.

We have the following.

(i)

If
\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 1
, then every component of
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
has the expected dimension of

(\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee})+g-1.

(ii)

If
\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 4
, then any general point of
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
corresponds to a nodal curve
C\subset S
.

(iii)

If
C^{\prime}\subset S
is nodal and
\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 1
, then
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
is smooth of expected dimension at
[C^{\prime}]
.

We work instead with moduli spaces of maps.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
C
be a fixed smooth, projective, irreducible curve of genus
g
, and let
\beta\in H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
be a non-zero curve class on
S
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
be the moduli space of maps
f:C\to S
with
f_{*}[C]=\beta
.

We say that such an
f
is “in class
\beta
.” Two maps are only considered isomorphic if they are
equal
, that is, an isomorphism of maps must restrict to the identity on both
C
and
S
. The expected dimension of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is equal to

\chi(C,f^{*}T_{S})=(\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee})-2g+2,

which is
\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{g})=3g-3
less than that of
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
.

There is a map
\mu:Y_{\beta,g}(S)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
remembering the normalization
C
of an embedded curve
C^{\prime}\subset S
. Near a point
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
birational onto its image, the space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is isomorphic to the fiber of
\mu
over the geometric point
[C]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. When
g=0
, this may be interpreted as the statement that there is a rational
\text{PGL}_{2}
-bundle
\nu:{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(\mathbb{P}^{1},S)\dashrightarrow Y_{\beta,0}(S)
sending a map
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to S
to
f(\mathbb{P}^{1})
whenever it is birational onto its image. Similarly, when
C
has genus
1
, the non-empty fibers of the map
\nu:{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)\dashrightarrow Y_{\beta,1}(S)
remembering the image of a birational map are isomorphic to the 1-dimensional group scheme
\text{Aut}(C)
.

In particular, by Theorem
2.2
, if
\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 4
and
C
is general, then
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is generically smooth of the expected dimension near any
f
birational onto its image. However, this may not be the case everywhere on the moduli space of maps, due to presence of multiple covers, where
f
is not birational onto its image. Controlling these multiple covers is the main task in what follows.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
C
be a general curve and let
S
be a smooth, projective surface. Let
\beta\in H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
be non-zero, effective curve class. Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
be an irreducible component.

Let
f:C\to S
be a general point of
{\mathcal{M}}
, and let
C^{\prime}\subset S
be the scheme-theoretic image of
f
. Suppose that
[C^{\prime}]\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}\geq 4
. Then,
{\mathcal{M}}
is generically smooth of expected dimension.

Proof.

Let
m
be the degree of induced cover
g:C\to C^{\prime}\subset S
. If
m=1
, then we are done by the above discussion, as
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is isomorphic near
f
to a general fiber of
\mu:Y_{\beta,g}(S)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. We assume henceforth that
m>1
. In this case, the general curve
C
admits no covers of a curve of positive geometric genus of degree
m>1
, unless
g=g(C^{\prime})=1
. We consider the cases
g(C^{\prime})=0
and
g=g(C^{\prime})=1
separately.

Suppose first that
g(C^{\prime})=0
. The moduli space of maps
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
m
has dimension
\rho(g,1,m)+\dim(\text{PGL}_{2})=2m-g+1
. In particular, we need
\rho(g,1,m)=2m-2-g\geq 0
.

By Theorem
2.2
, the moduli space of embedded curves
C^{\prime}\subset S
in class
\beta^{\prime}:=[C^{\prime}]
has dimension
\beta^{\prime}\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}-1
.
Therefore, the component
{\mathcal{M}}
has dimension

(2m-g+1)+(\beta^{\prime}\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}-1)\geq(\beta\cdot K^{\vee}_{S})-2g+2,

where the right hand side is the expected dimension. Rearranging gives

(m-1)((\beta^{\prime}\cdot K^{\vee}_{S})-2)\leq g.

Recall that we also have
g\leq 2m-2
. Thus, the given multiple cover
f
can only give rise to a component
{\mathcal{M}}
if
\beta^{\prime}\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}=4
and
g=2m-2
, hence
\rho(g,1,m)=0
. In this case, the space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is isomorphic near
f
to the 6-dimensional space of maps which factor as

C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X,

where
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
corresponds to one of a
reduced
(for example, by the Gieseker-Petri theorem) set of points parametrizing rank 1 linear series on
C
of minimal degree
m=\frac{g+2}{2}
, and the image of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to S
is a point of the Severi variety
Y_{\beta^{\prime},0}(S)
. By Theorem
2.2
, the Severi variety is generically smooth
of expected dimension, so
{\mathcal{M}}
is as well.

Consider now the exceptional case
g(C^{\prime})=g(C)=1
. Since
C
admits only finitely many maps of degree
m>1
to some genus
1
curve, by Theorem
2.2
, the component
{\mathcal{M}}
would have dimension
\beta^{\prime}\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}
, which is less than the expected dimension
\beta\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}
, a contradiction.

\Diamond

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Immediate from Proposition
2.4
.

\Diamond

Remark 2.5
.

The proof of Proposition
2.4
also shows that, if the Severi variety
Y_{\beta,g}(S)
is irreducible, then the open locus of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
consisting of maps whose image has anti-canonical degree at least 5 is also irreducible. Severi varieties are known in many cases to be irreducible, see for example
[
Har86
,
Tes09
,
CD19
,
CHT23
,
CHT25
]
.

3.
Toric surfaces

We now specialize to the case of a smooth, projective toric surface
S
. By Proposition
2.4
, for a general curve
C
and any non-zero curve class
\beta
, any component of the moduli space of maps
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,S)
is generically smooth of expected dimension as long as its general point is not a multiple cover of a curve of anti-canonical degree at most 3. When
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, these curves are precisely the lines, so we recover the non-degeneracy condition in the classical Brill-Noether theorem. In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
, which classifies the (non-boundary) curves of low anti-canonical degree on any smooth, projective toric surface
S
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a lattice. A
fake toric projective plane

S^{\prime}
is a projective toric surface associated to a complete fan
N
whose distinct 1-dimensional rays
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in N
satisfy
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=0
.

We abuse terminology slightly, identifying a ray in
N
with a primitive vector that spans it. If any two of
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
form an integral basis for
N
, then
S^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
; otherwise, the surface
S^{\prime}
is singular. See Example
3.10
for a singular example. See also
[
Buz08
,
Con02
,
Kas09
,
CGKN14
,
Bä24
]
for work on fake toric
weighted
projective spaces of arbitrary dimension.

Fake toric projective planes necessarily have
\mathsf{Pic}(S^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}

[
Ful93
, §3.4]
, so in particular, a natural notion of degeneracy of a non-constant map
f:C\to S^{\prime}
is that the image of
f
lies in the primitive linear system of
S^{\prime}
. Therefore, Corollary
1.4
asserts that any
f:C\to S
mapping generically to the interior of
S
moves in a family of expected dimension, as long as it is non-degenerate after post-composition with any toric morphism
\pi
allowed by (i) and (ii) of Theorem
1.3
.

We now set up the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which uses standard tools of toric geometry; we follow the conventions of
[
Ful98
]
. Let
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a lattice, and let
S
be a smooth, projective toric surface with fan
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{c}\in N
be primitive vectors spanning the 1-dimensional rays of
\Sigma
, ordered counter-clockwise so that the 2-dimensional cone
\sigma_{i}
is spanned by
n_{i}
and
n_{i+1}
(indices taken modulo
c
). Let
T\subseteq S
be the open torus and let
C^{\prime}\subset S
be an integral curve generically contained in
T
.

Let
M=\text{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
be the dual lattice, and let
F\in\mathbb{C}[M]
be the equation of
C^{\prime}
in
T
. The element
F
is only well-defined up to multiplication by a unit in
\mathbb{C}[M]
. For a choice of such an
F
, write

F=\sum_{m\in M}a_{m}\chi^{m},

where all but finitely many coefficients
a_{m}
are zero.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}=\{m\in M|a_{m}\neq 0\},

and let
\mathcal{P}(F)
be the
Newton polygon
of
F
, by definition the convex hull of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
. The set
\mathcal{P}(F)
and its convex hull are well-defined up to translation in
M
.

For any
i=1,2,\ldots,c
, let
\ell_{i}\subset M
be the unique line with the following two properties. First, the function
{\text{ev}}_{i}
on
M=\text{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
given by evaluation at a non-zero vector on the ray
n_{i}
is constant on
\ell_{i}
. Second, the line
\ell_{i}
contains at least one point of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
on which
{\text{ev}}_{i}
is minimized. In particular, every point of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
not lying on
\ell_{i}
lies on the same side of
\ell_{i}
, and every point of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
lying on
\ell_{i}
in fact lies on the boundary of
\mathcal{P}(F)
.

Because
S
is smooth, the lines
\ell_{i},\ell_{i+1}
necessarily intersect at a lattice point of
M
, which we denote by
\mu_{i}
. Let
e_{i}\subset\ell_{i}
be the (possibly degenerate) directed segment from
\mu_{i-1}
to
\mu_{i}
. Then, the points
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{c}
, some of which may be equal to each other, form the vertices of a convex polygon
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(F)
circumscribing
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
, and appear in order around its boundary. If there are only two distinct points among the
\mu_{i}
, then the polygon
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(F)
is degenerate; in this case, its boundary contains two opposite directed edges. By construction, each edge
e_{i}
contains at least one point of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
, and all points of
e_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
lie on the boundary of
\mathcal{P}(F)
, and between the points
\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i}
. Let
\nu^{-}_{i},\nu^{+}_{i}
be the points of
e_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
closest to
\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i}
, respectively.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
m,m^{\prime}\in M
be lattice points. We define the
distance

\delta(m,m^{\prime})
to be equal to 0 if
m=m^{\prime}
, and equal to one more than the number of lattice points in the interior of the segment between
m,m^{\prime}
otherwise.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
D_{i}\subset S
be the toric divisor corresponding to
n_{i}
, for each
i
. Then, we have
C^{\prime}\cdot D_{i}=\delta(\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i})
. In particular, we have

C^{\prime}\cdot K_{S}^{\vee}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}C^{\prime}\cdot D_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\delta(\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i}).

Proof.

Let
S_{i}=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\chi^{m_{i}},\chi^{m_{i+1}}])
be the affine chart of
S
corresponding to
\sigma_{i}
, where
m_{i},m_{i+1}\in M
form a basis of the dual cone
\sigma_{i}^{\vee}
dual to
n_{i},n_{i+1}
. The restriction of
C^{\prime}
to
S_{i}
is cut out by the equation
F_{i}:=F\cdot\chi^{-\mu_{i}}\in\mathbb{C}[\chi^{m_{i}},\chi^{m_{i}+1}]
. Indeed, the monomial
\chi^{-\mu_{i}}
is the unique one whose product with
F
is an irreducible element of
\mathbb{C}[\chi^{m_{i}},\chi^{m_{i}+1}]
. Multiplication by
\chi^{-\mu_{i}}
has the effect of translating the Newton polygon of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
by
-\mu_{i}
. Moreover, every point
m\in\mathcal{P}(F_{i})^{+}
has
m(n_{i}),m(n_{i+1})\geq 0
, where each inequality is an equality for at least one point
m\in\mathcal{P}(F_{i})^{+}
(possibly different for the two inequalities).

The divisor
D_{i}
is cut out in
S_{i}
by the equation
\chi^{m_{i}}=0
. Setting
\chi^{m_{i}}=0
in
F_{i}
leaves a polynomial in
\chi^{m_{i+1}}
of degree equal to
\delta(\nu^{-}_{i},\mu_{i})
. Indeed, the non-zero term of
F_{i}
given by the largest power of
\chi^{m_{i+1}}
corresponds (after translation back by
\mu_{i}
) to the point of
\mathcal{P}(F)^{+}
on
\ell_{i}
furthest from
\mu_{i}
, and hence closest to
\mu_{i-1}
. Thus, the degree of the intersection
C^{\prime}\cap D_{i}
inside
S_{i}
is equal to
\delta(\nu^{-}_{i},\mu_{i})
. Similarly, the degree of
C^{\prime}\cap D_{i}
upon restriction to the affine chart
S_{i-1}
is equal to
\delta(\mu_{i-1},\nu^{+}_{i})
. Moreover, the intersection
C^{\prime}\cap D_{i}
has degree
\delta(\nu^{-}_{i},\nu^{+}_{i})
on
S_{i-1}\cap S_{i}
. Therefore, we have

C^{\prime}\cdot D_{i}=\delta(\nu^{-}_{i},\mu_{i})+\delta(\mu_{i-1},\nu^{+}_{i})-\delta(\nu^{-}_{i},\nu^{+}_{i})=\delta(\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i}).

\Diamond

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
C^{\prime}\subset S
be an integral curve of anti-canonical degree at most 3, which is not contained in the boundary of
S
. By Lemma
3.5
, the anti-canonical degree of
C^{\prime}
is bounded below by the number of (directed) edges of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(F)
, which is at least 2. There are two cases.

First, if
[C^{\prime}]\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}=2
, then
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(F)
consists of two opposite directed edges
e_{i},e_{j}
of length 1, corresponding to opposite rays
n_{i},n_{j}\in N
. Then, there exists a surjective toric morphism
\pi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, corresponding to the projection of
\Sigma
onto the fan of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that contracts
n_{i},n_{j}
. The curve
C^{\prime}\subset X_{\Sigma}
is contracted to a point under
\pi
, so we are in case (i) of Theorem
1.3
.

If instead
[C^{\prime}]\cdot K^{\vee}_{S}=3
, then
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(F)
is a triangle consisting of three edges
e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}\subset M
of length 1. These edges correspond to rays
n_{i},n_{j},n_{k}\in N
with
n_{i}+n_{j}+n_{k}=0
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\Sigma
be the complete fan containing only the three rays
n_{i},n_{j},n_{k}
. The corresponding toric variety
S^{\prime}:=Y_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
is a fake toric projective plane, and the morphism
S\to S^{\prime}
induced by deleting all rays except
n_{i},n_{j},n_{k}
sends
C^{\prime}
to a curve in the primitive linear system of
S^{\prime}
, so we are in case (ii) of Theorem
1.3
.

3.1.

Fix again a general curve

Example 3.6
.

As discussed above, when
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, Corollary
1.4
recovers the classical Brill-Noether Theorem, Theorem
1.1
, for
r=2
. Indeed, the only toric morphism
\pi
allowed by Theorem
1.3
is the identity.

Example 3.7
.

When
S={\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at a torus-fixed point, Corollary
1.4
recovers Farkas’s result, Theorem
1.6
, in dimension 2. Indeed, the toric morphisms
\pi
allowed by Theorem
1.3
are the projection
\pi_{1}:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
away from
q
and the blow-up
\pi_{2}:S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
. However, curves contracted under
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
also map to lines under
\pi_{2}
. Corollary
1.4
gives in addition a generic smoothness statement, which does not seem to follow from Farkas’s argument.

Example 3.8
.

Let
S
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at two torus-fixed points. Then, the toric morphisms
\pi
allowed by Theorem
1.3
are again the two projections
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the blow-up
S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
. However, the fibers of the projections
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
also map to lines under the blow-up. Thus, any
f:C\to S
whose image is not contained in a line after the blow-down to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
moves in a family of expected dimension.

One can check that the same is true if
S
is obtained instead by blowing up
{\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
at a torus-fixed point of the exceptional divisor.

The next example shows that curves which do not map to primitive curves under one contraction may map to primitive curves under others.

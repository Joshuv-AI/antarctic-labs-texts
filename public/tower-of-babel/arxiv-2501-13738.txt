Asymptotics of Transversality in Periodic Curves of Quadratic Rational Maps Thanks: The author acknowledges the support of ANID/FONDECYT Regular 1240508.
Jan Kiwi

Abstract
. We compute the Euler characteristic of the moduli space of quadratic rational maps with a periodic marked critical point of a given period.



1.
Introduction

The dynamical properties of a rational map
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
are determined, to a large extent, by its critical orbits.
Therefore, parameter spaces are frequently explored by studying the
loci of maps with a prescribed critical orbit behavior.
Here we focus on quadratic rational maps.
Specifically, our interest is on the moduli spaces
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
of quadratic maps with a marked periodic critical point of period, for any
p\geq 1
.
These moduli spaces are punctured Riemann surfaces, often called

periodic curves
.
Periodic curves and related spaces, including critical orbit relation curves (COR curves), have received considerable
attention in the literature (see e.g.

[
BEK22
,
DMWY15
,
FKS23
,
HK17
,
Mil93
,
RS24
,
Ram24
,
Sti93
,
Ree95
,
Ree03
]
).
However, for general
p
, basic questions about the global topology of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
remain open: Is
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
connected? If so, what is the genus
g_{p}
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
?
What is the number of punctures
N_{p}
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
?
Our main result is a formula for the Euler characteristic of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
. In particular, provided that
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is connected, this establishes the equivalence
between computing
g_{p}
and computing
N_{p}
.
Our result arises from a precise control of the dynamical and parameter space
scales near the punctures, with the aid of non-Archimedean dynamics.

In order to state the main result, we need to introduce two numbers:
\eta^{\prime}(p)

and
\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p)
. The first one,
\eta^{\prime}(p)
, is the number of parameters in the quadratic family
Q_{c}(z):=z^{2}+c
such that the critical point
z=0
is periodic of exact period
p
. The second one,
\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p)
, is the number
of parameters in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
such that the two critical points lie in the same
periodic orbit; that is, the number of
type II or bitransitive centers
in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
A formula for
\eta^{\prime}(p)
follows from well known results about the quadratic family, due to
Douady, Hubbard and, Gleason
[
DH85
]
. In
[
KR13
]
, with M. Rees, we obtained a formula for
\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p)
. These formulas are discussed in
§
2.2
and §
2.3
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\chi({\pazocal{S}}_{p})
denote the Euler characteristic of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
Then, for all
p\geq 3
,

\chi({\pazocal{S}}_{p})=\dfrac{2\eta^{\prime}(p)}{3}-\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p).

The curves
{\pazocal{S}}_{1}
and
{\pazocal{S}}_{2}
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, respectively.

Denote by
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
the smooth compactification of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
obtained by
filling each puncture with an ideal point. Then, for
p\geq 3
,

\chi(\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}})=N_{p}+\dfrac{2\eta^{\prime}(p)}{3}-\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p).

As mentioned above, we lack of formulas and general methods to compute
N_{p}
.

There is a strong analogy between the study of cubic polynomials and
quadratic rational maps. In both cases, two critical points are relevant
in phase space and moduli spaces are complex two-dimensional.
However, substantially more is known about cubic polynomials.
In fact, with Arfeux, we proved that the moduli space
{\mathscr{S}}_{p}
of cubic polynomials
with a marked periodic critical point, of period
p
, is
connected
[
AK23
]
. With Bonifant and Milnor, we obtained a formula for the Euler characteristic of
{\mathscr{S}}_{p}
in
[
BKM10
]
. Schiff and De Marco
[
DS10
]
produced an algorithm to determine the number of punctures of
{\mathscr{S}}_{p}
, but no general formula is known.

The strategy to compute the Euler characteristic of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is similar to the one employed for cubic polynomials in
[
BKM10
]
.
It consists on computing the degree of a naturally defined meromorphic one-form
d\tau
on
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
with all its zeros and poles located at the punctures of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
Near the punctures, elements of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
correspond to conjugacy classes
of maps close to infinity in the moduli space of all quadratic rational maps.
Our computation of the order of
d\tau
at the punctures of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}

relies on the study of degenerate rational maps with tools from
non-Archimedean dynamics. We reinterpret the formulas for
the order of
d\tau
at the punctures, obtained in
[
BKM10
]
.
Our contribution is to show that the underlying principle is that the order at the punctures
of a parameter space derivative
and a dynamical space derivative agree,
modulo a minor correction factor (see Lemma
3.1
).
This may be interpreted as an equation for the asymptotics (at the punctures)
of the transversality responsible for the smoothness of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
It would be interesting to investigate
the extent up to which similar equations for the
asymptotics of transversality
hold for general COR curves.

The paper is organized as follows:

In §
2
, we introduce the basic notation and background
results regarding quadratic rational maps and the curves
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.

In §
3
, we introduce the meromorphic one form used
to compute the Euler characteristic of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
Our Main Lemma
3.1
is stated. However, its proof is postponed until §
5
.
Assuming the Main Lemma, we prove Theorem
A
.

In §
4
, we summarize some results
regarding the punctures of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
. The
emphasis is on parabolic rescaling limits,
following the works of Stimson, Epstein, Rees, and De Marco
[
Sti93
,
Eps00
,
Ree03
,
DeM07
]
.

In §
5
, we prove the Main Lemma. We start translating
it into a lemma regarding quadratic rational dynamics over
the field of Puiseux series. To prove it,
we abuse of the detailed knowledge
of quadratic rational dynamics over this field obtained in
[
Kiw14
]
. Dynamics over non-Archimedean fields is better understood
in the Berkovich projective line. However, since the Main Lemma involves
only quantities related to the (classical) projective line, for simplicity,
we have chosen to do most of our work without reference to Berkovich spaces.
Nevertheless, at some points, we have to translate results from

[
Kiw14
]
, written in the language of Berkovich spaces,
to our context.
As a byproduct of the proof of the Main Lemma,
we obtain Mandelbrot tori embedded
in quadratic rational moduli space
reminiscent of the ones obtained by Branner and Hubbard
[
BH88
]
for cubic polynomials
(see Corollary
5.19
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Moduli space

Denote the moduli space of quadratic rational maps with a marked critical point by
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
. More precisely, consider dynamical pairs
(f,\omega)
where
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a quadratic rational map and
f^{\prime}(\omega)=0
. We say that two such pairs
(f,\omega)
and
(g,\omega^{\prime})
are conjugate if there exists a Möebius transformation
\gamma
such that
\gamma\circ g=f\circ\gamma
and
\gamma(\omega^{\prime})=\omega
.
According to Lemma 6.1 in
[
Mil93
]
, the moduli space of critically marked quadratic rational maps
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
is an algebraic surface with a unique singular point at the conjugacy class of
(z\mapsto z^{-2},\omega=0)
.
We refer to the elements of
{\pazocal{M}}^{cm}_{2}
simply as maps.
Also,
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is an algebraic subvariety of
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
, for all
p\geq 1
.
The curves
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
are known to be smoothly embedded in
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}

with the sole exception of
{\pazocal{S}}_{2}
at the singular point of
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
(e.g. see
[
LSvS19
,
Eps
]
c.f.
[
KR13
, Theorem 2.5]
). Conjecturally
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is irreducible for all
p\geq 1
(c.f.
[
Ram24
]
).

2.2.
Counting in the Quadratic Family

Abstractly, for
p\geq 1
, the numbers
\eta^{\prime}(p)\in\mathbb{N}
are defined by:

2^{p-1}=\sum_{d|p}\eta^{\prime}(d).

Therefore,

\eta^{\prime}(p)=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{d|p}\mu(d)\cdot 2^{p/d},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

It is however convenient to relate
\eta^{\prime}(p)
with quadratic polynomial dynamics, as in the introduction. Recall that
Q_{c}(z):=z^{2}+c
denotes the quadratic family.
Then,

(2.1)

\eta^{\prime}(p)=|\{c\in\mathbb{C}:z=0\text{ has period }p\text{ under }Q_{c}\}|.

In fact, according to Gleason (e.g. see
[
DH85
, Exposé XIX]
), all the roots of
the degree
2^{p-1}
polynomial
Q_{c}^{\circ p-1}(c)
are simple.
Thus, both sides of (
2.1
) must be equal.

In order to write a formula for
\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p)
, we need to define
\nu_{q}(k)
, for

1<k\leq q
.
Let
0\leq r<q
be such that
p=r\mod q
.
Then

\nu_{q}(k):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2^{k}-2^{r}}{2^{q}-1}&\text{ if }r\neq 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2^{k}-1}{2^{q}-1}&\text{ if }r=0.\end{cases}

For example,

(2.2)

\nu_{2}(p):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2^{n}-2}{6}&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
\\
\dfrac{2^{n}-4}{6}&\text{ if }n\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

The numbers
\nu_{q}(k)
are also closely related to the quadratic family.
More precisely,
\nu_{q}(k)
is the number of parameters
c
in
the
1/q
-limb of the Mandelbrot set
for which the critical point

c=0
is periodic of period, at least
3
, dividing
k
(e.g. see
[
Mil00
]
for the definition of limbs).

In order to employ some of the results in
[
KR13
]
,
our next lemma records the simple relation between
the numbers
\nu_{2}(p)
and
\eta^{\prime}(p)
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(p)
be such that

\nu_{2}(p)=\sum_{3\leq d|p}\nu_{2}^{\prime}(d).

Then,
\eta^{\prime}(p)=3\nu^{\prime}_{2}(p)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that, for all
p\geq 3
,
we have

\sum_{3\leq d|p}\eta^{\prime}(d)=3\nu_{2}(p).

Indeed, from (
2.2
), for
p
even,

\sum_{3\leq d|p}\eta^{\prime}(d)=2^{p-1}-\eta^{\prime}(1)-\eta^{\prime}(2)=2^{p-1}-2=3\nu_{2}(p).

A similar identity holds for
p
odd.
∎

2.3.
Counting in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}

A map
f\in{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
is
hyperbolic
if both of its critical orbits are in the basin(s) of some attracting cycle(s). A connected component of hyperbolic maps is called a
hyperbolic component
.
Following Rees
[
Ree90
]
, hyperbolic components are classified into four types according to the relative position of the critical points in the attracting basins. Here we are mainly concerned with type II components, also known as
bitransitive or type B components
.
A
type II hyperbolic component
consists of maps having a unique attracting cycle of period
p\geq 2
such that both critical points belong
to periodic Fatou components. These Fatou components are necessarily distinct. Each type II hyperbolic component
{\pazocal{H}}
of period
p
contains a unique postcritically finite map
f_{0}
called the
center
of
{\pazocal{H}}
.
The critical points of
f_{0}
lie in the same orbit of period
p
. In particular,
f_{0}\in{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.

In
[
KR13
]
we obtained a formula for the number
\eta_{II}^{\prime}(p)
of type II hyperbolic components in
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
of exact period
p
. It is not difficult to check that the
formula written below is equivalent to the one obtained in
[
KR13
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
p\geq 3
,

\sum_{3\leq d|p}\dfrac{\eta^{\prime}_{II}(d)}{d}=\dfrac{\nu_{2}(p)}{2}-\dfrac{A(p)}{2p},

where

A(p):=p\cdot\sum_{q=2}^{p-1}\phi(q)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\nu_{q}(k)(2^{p-k-1}-\nu_{q}(p-k))\right),

and
\phi
denotes the Euler totient function.

The Möebius inversion formula yields:

\eta^{\prime}_{II}(p)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(p\nu^{\prime}_{2}(p)+\sum_{d|p,p/d\geq 3}d\cdot\mu(d)\cdot A\left(\dfrac{p}{d}\right)\right).

2.4.
Partial parametrization of
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}

The complement of

{\pazocal{S}}_{1}\cup{\pazocal{S}}_{2}
in
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}

is identified with
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}

via the family

f_{a,b}(z)=1+\dfrac{b}{z}+\dfrac{a}{z^{2}}

with marked critical point
\omega=0
. That is,

{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}\setminus({\pazocal{S}}_{1}\cup{\pazocal{S}}_{2})=\{[(f_{a,b},0)]:(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\}.

In fact, observe that if
[(f,\omega)]\in{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}\setminus({\pazocal{S}}_{1}\cup{\pazocal{S}}_{2})
, then
\omega,f(\omega)
and
f^{\circ 2}(\omega)
are distinct. Normalizing so that

\omega=0
,
f(\omega)=\infty
and
f^{\circ 2}(\omega)=1
, we have that
f=f_{a,b}
, for some
(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}
.

Regard the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
as
a compactification of
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}\setminus({\pazocal{S}}_{1}\cup{\pazocal{S}}_{2})

via the inclusion
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
given by
(a,b)\mapsto[a:b:1]
. Thus,
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}\setminus({\pazocal{S}}_{1}\cup{\pazocal{S}}_{2})
is
identified with the complement, in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, of the lines

{\pazocal{L}}_{+}:=\{[a:b:c]:a=0\}

and

{\pazocal{L}}_{-}:=\{[a:b:c]:c=0\}.

From now on we regard
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
, for any
p\geq 3
, as a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
denote its closure by
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
.

Milnor
[
Mil93
]
introduced a compactification of the moduli space
of quadratic rational maps, without critical markings, by a line at infinity
corresponding to pairs
\{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\}
where
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}
.
Points in moduli space close to
\{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\}
have
two fixed points with multipliers close to
\lambda
and
\lambda^{-1}
.
Silverman
[
Sil98
]

showed that, in fact, Milnor’s compactification is
the GIT-compactification of unmarked moduli space.
Maps close to
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
and
{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
in marked moduli space,
after forgetting the marking, become maps in unmarked moduli space close to infinity.
The line
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
should be regarded as a blow up of
\{-1,-1\}
.
More precisely,
as
[a:b:1]
approaches
[1:m:0]\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
with
m\neq 0
, the multipliers of
two fixed points of
f_{a,b}
converge to
-1
. Also, as

[a:b:1]
approaches
[0:m:1]\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
with
m\neq 0
, the multipliers of
two fixed points of
f_{a,b}
converge to reciprocal complex numbers
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1\}
.

2.5.
Degree of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}

Lemma 2.3

(
[
KR13
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

For all
p\geq 3
,

\deg\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}=\nu^{\prime}_{2}(p).

Moreover, none of the points
[1:0:0],[0:1:0],[0:0:1]
belongs to
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
.

Lemma
2.1
yields:

(2.3)

\deg\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}=\dfrac{\eta^{\prime}(p)}{3}.

We remark that
[
KR13
, Lemma 3.1]
states an equivalent
assertion but for

\overline{{\pazocal{X}}_{n}}:=\cup_{3\leq p|n}\overline{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
instead of
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
.

2.6.
Punctures

Recall that the smooth compactification
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is obtained from
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
by filling each of its punctures with an ideal point.
Equivalently, it is the normalization of the projective curve
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
.
The punctures of

{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
correspond to germs of branches of
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
at the lines

{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
and
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
. A representative of the branch corresponding to a puncture
x
will be denoted by
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
. A punctured neighborhood
of
x
in
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
is
{\pazocal{E}}^{*}_{x}:={\pazocal{E}}_{x}\setminus{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
.

Let
\pi:\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}\to\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
be the natural projection. It is
a bijection in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
and maps each puncture
{x}
onto the intersection

\pi(x)
of the corresponding branch with
{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
or
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
.
We will systematically abuse of notation and simply say that
x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
or

x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
.

We define the
multiplicity
\mu_{x}
of a puncture
x
as the intersection multiplicity of the branch
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
corresponding to
x
with the lines
{\pazocal{L}}_{+},{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
.

As a direct consequence of Bezout’s Theorem and Lemma
2.3
, we have:

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
p\geq 3
,

\dfrac{\eta^{\prime}(p)}{3}=\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}}\mu_{x}=\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}\mu_{x},

where the sums are taken over
x\in\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}\setminus{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.

3.
Asymptotics of Transversality

Following
[
BKM10
]
, in
§
3.2
we introduce a meromorphic one-form
d\tau
on
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
which is holomorphic in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
. The Euler characteristic of
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
is the difference between the number of poles and zeros of
d\tau
, counted with multiplicities. The key to compute this difference will be our Main Lemma stated in §
3.1
.
Then we prove Theorem A in
§
3.2
, assuming the Main Lemma.

3.1.
Statement of the Main Lemma

For
(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}
, and for all
n\geq 0
, let

\omega_{n}(a,b):=f^{\circ n}_{a,b}(0).

Partial derivatives will be denoted by

\partial_{a}\omega_{n}
and
\partial_{b}\omega_{n}
.
In dynamical space, we are concerned with the behavior of

\dfrac{df^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}}{dz}(\infty),

under the agreement that the above quantity is the derivative of

f^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}(1/z)
at
z=0
.
Then,
\partial_{b}\omega_{p}
and
\dfrac{df^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}}{dz}(\infty)

are meromorphic (rational) functions from
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
to
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
.
Given a meromorphic function
h:\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
,
we denote by
\operatorname{ord}_{y}h\in\mathbb{Z}
its
order
at
y\in\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
. The order at a zero of
h

is positive and at a pole is negative.

The key to compute the Euler characteristic
is to understand the asymptotics
of
\partial_{b}\omega_{p}
at the punctures.

Lemma 3.1

(Main)
.

Let
x\in\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
be a puncture of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
Then:

(3.1)

\operatorname{ord}_{x}\partial_{b}\omega_{p}=\operatorname{ord}_{x}\dfrac{df^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}}{dz}(\infty)+\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+},\\
2\mu_{x}&\text{ if }x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}.\\
\end{cases}

The lemma is proven in §
5
.

3.2.
Meromorphic One-form and Euler Characteristic

The curve
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is contained in the vanishing locus
of
\omega_{p}(a,b)
. The gradient of
\omega_{p}
is non-vanishing
in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
(see
[
LSvS19
]

[
Eps
]
). Thus, the differential of
\omega_{p}
yields a
meromorphic one-form in
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
(a,b)\in{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
,

(\partial_{a}\omega_{p}(a,b),\partial_{b}\omega_{p}(a,b))\neq(0,0).

Moreover,

d\tau:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{da}{\partial_{b}\omega_{p}(a,b)}&\text{ if }\partial_{b}\omega_{p}(a,b)\neq 0,\\
\\
-\dfrac{db}{\partial_{a}\omega_{p}(a,b)}&\text{ if }\partial_{a}\omega_{p}(a,b)\neq 0,\end{cases}

is a well defined non-vanishing holomorphic one-form in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
which extends to a meromorphic one-form in
\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}
.

We omit the proof of the proposition which is a direct consequence of the transversality results cited above.

Proof of Theorem
A
, assuming Lemma
3.1
.

We prove the formula for the Euler characteristic.
To simplify notation
let

G(a,b):=\dfrac{df^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}}{dz}(\infty).

The number of zeros and poles of
the rational map
G:\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
agree, taking into account multiplicities.
By the chain rule, the zeros of
G
in
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
are located at the parameters where the free critical point
is in the orbit of
\omega=0
.
That is, the zeros of
G
are the centers of type II components of period
p
.
We postpone proving that
all the zeros of
G
are simple to the lemma below.
Since the rest of the poles and zeros of
G
are at the punctures, we have:

\sum_{x\in\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}\setminus{\pazocal{S}}_{p}}\operatorname{ord}_{x}G+\eta_{II}^{\prime}(p)=0.

Now we compute the total degree of the one-form
d\tau
. Let
x
be a puncture of multiplicity
\mu_{x}
.
If
x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
, then there exists a local uniformizing parameter
s
such that
s^{\pm\mu_{x}}=a
. Therefore,

da=\pm\mu_{x}{s^{\pm\mu_{x}-1}}ds.

Hence,

\operatorname{ord}_{x}d\tau=-1\pm\mu_{x}-\operatorname{ord}_{x}\partial_{b}\omega_{p},

In view of Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}}\operatorname{ord}_{x}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}}1+\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}}\mu_{x}-\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}}\operatorname{ord}_{x}G,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}\operatorname{ord}_{x}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}1-\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}\mu_{x}-\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}\operatorname{ord}_{x}G-2\sum_{x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}}\mu_{x}.

From Lemma
2.4
, the sum of the multiplicities of the punctures in each line is

\eta^{\prime}(p)/3
and we obtain:

-\chi(\widehat{{\pazocal{S}}_{p}})=\sum_{x}\operatorname{ord}_{x}d\tau=-N_{p}-\dfrac{2\eta^{\prime}(p)}{3}+\eta_{II}^{\prime}(p).

The desired formula for
\chi({\pazocal{S}}_{p})
follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G:{\pazocal{S}}_{p}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
be as above.
Then, the zeros of
G
are simple.

Proof.

Let
\nu=\nu(a,b):=-2a/b
be the free critical point of
f_{a,b}

and
\nu_{j}:=f^{\circ j}_{a,b}(\nu).

Given a zero
c=(a,b)
of
G
, there exists

1\leq j<p
such that

\omega_{j}(a,b)=\nu(a,b)
.
Close to
c
, there exists a non-vanishing holomorphic function

u(a,b)
such that

G(a,b)=u(a,b)\cdot f^{\prime}_{a,b}(\omega_{j}(a,b)).

Since
\nu(c)
is a simple critical point of
f_{c}
,
the multiplicities of
c\in{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
as a zero of
\omega_{j}-\nu:{\pazocal{S}}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}

and as a zero of
G
, coincide. According to
[
LSvS19
]
, the intersection
at
c
of the curves

\omega_{j}-\nu=0
and
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
(i.e.
\omega_{p}-\omega_{0}=0
) is transversal.
Hence,
c\in{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
is a simple zero of
\omega_{j}-\nu:{\pazocal{S}}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
, and therefore, it is a simple zero of
G
.

∎

4.
Parabolic rescalings

Our asymptotics of transversality lemma
is closely related to
rescaling limits
.
Following Rees and Stimson
[
Sti93
]
, as one approaches a puncture along
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
, an
appropriate iterate in appropriate coordinates, converges to a parabolic
quadratic rational map. This phenomenon was notably exploited by Epstein in
[
Eps00
]
, and
further studied by De Marco
[
DeM07
]
,
Nie and Pilgrim
[
NP22
]
, Luo
[
Luo22
]
, and the author
[
Kiw15
]
, among others.
The aim of this section is to state related results,
all of them well known, which will be used
to prove the Main Lemma.

Consider

(4.1)

\displaystyle R_{v}(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle v+\dfrac{z^{2}}{z+1},\quad v\in\mathbb{C}.

(4.2)

\displaystyle Q_{c}(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle z^{2}+c,\quad c\in\mathbb{C}.

The family
\{R_{v}\}
is a parametrization of
\operatorname{Per}_{1}^{cm}(1)\subset{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
,
the curve formed by critically marked
quadratic rational maps with a multiple fixed point.
The maps
R_{v}
are normalized so that the multiple fixed point is
z=\infty
and
z=-1
maps to
z=\infty
. We regard
\omega=0
as the marked critical point. Note that the other critical point
of
R_{v}
is
{\omega^{\prime}}:=-2
.

The quadratic family
\{Q_{c}\}
is
\operatorname{Per}_{1}^{cm}(0)
, the curve formed by critically marked quadratic rational maps with a super-attracting fixed point. For
Q_{c}
, the superattracting fixed point is at
\infty
and
the marked critical point
\omega=0
.

The Main Lemma is, in a certain sense, a manifestation of well known
transversality results for the families
\{R_{v}\}
and
\{Q_{c}\}
.
This manifestation is through (parameter) rescaling limits.
A version of the following result is
contained in Stimson’s thesis
[
Sti93
]
, its proof is also sketched in

[
Ree03
, Section 7.4]
(c.f.
[
Eps00
,
DeM07
]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider a puncture
x
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
Then there exist
q_{x}\geq 2
,
v_{x}\in\mathbb{C}

and a holomorphic family of Möbius transformation
\{M_{f}\}
parametrized by
f\in{\pazocal{E}}_{x}^{*}
such that
M_{f}(\omega)=0
and

M_{f}^{-1}\circ f^{\circ q_{x}}\circ M_{f}\to R_{v_{x}},\text{ as }f\to x,

uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
.
Moreover, under iterations of
R_{v_{x}}
, one of the following holds:

•

the critical point
\omega=0
of
R_{v_{x}}
has period
p/q_{x}
.

•

the critical point
\omega=0
of
R_{v_{x}}
eventually maps onto the multiple fixed point
\infty
.

The aim of this section is to state the well known parameter space
version of the above result, also contained
in Stimson’s thesis. However, for the purpose of our calculations,
we need to explicitly write the dependence of
M_{f}
and of
v_{x}
on
the asymptotics of
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}^{*}
at
{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
.
There is no claim to originality of these results.

A detailed study of the case in which
\omega
is eventually
fixed by
R_{v_{x}}
will be discussed in §
5
. It is closely related
to rescaling limits which are quadratic polynomials
Q_{c}

for which
\omega=0
is periodic.

4.1.
Fixed Point Multipliers

Generically a quadratic rational map has
3
fixed points.
According to Epstein
[
Eps00
, Proposition 1]
(
c.f
[
Mil93
, Lemma 4.1]
), as
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
converges to a puncture,
one of the fixed point multipliers converges to
\infty
, the other
two converge to conjugate roots of unity of order
q\geq 2
.
Observe that as
(a,b)\equiv[a:b:1]
converges to
[1:\gamma:0]\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
,
we have convergence of
a^{-1/2}f_{a,b}(a^{1/2}z)
to
1/z
,
uniformly in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Therefore,
f_{a,b}
has a pair of fixed point multipliers converging to
-1
.
As
(a,b)\to(0,b)\neq(0,0)
, we have convergence of
f_{a,b}
to

1+b/z
, uniformly in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Thus, the fixed point multipliers of
f

converge to
\infty
and to the fixed point multipliers
of
1+b/z
. If the map
z\mapsto 1+b/z
has finite order, then its order is
at least
3
.
A straightforward calculation, (e.g. see
[
KR13
, Lemma 3.4]
) yields the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 3
.
The fixed point multipliers of

z\mapsto 1+b/z
are
\exp(\pm 2\pi i\theta)
if
and only if

b=-(2\cos\pi\theta)^{-2}.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order at least
3
, let

c_{\theta}:=-(2\cos\pi\theta)^{-2}.

Every point in
\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}\cap{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
is of the form
[0:c_{\theta}:1]
for
some
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order at least
3
.

4.2.
Parabolic Parameter Rescalings

Loosely, the term “parameter rescalings” refers to families of maps that arise in suitable partial compactifications of moduli space.
For example, De Marco
[
DeM07
]
has shown that
the resolution of moduli space iterate maps (for quadratic rational maps) is achieved exactly in the compactification of moduli space by
all
parabolic
rescaling limits.
A nice illustration of this phenomena comes from a straightforward computation which we leave to the reader:

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
(a,b)\equiv[a:b:1]
converges to
[1:\gamma:0]\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}

with
\gamma\neq 0
, then

\gamma\cdot f^{\circ 2}_{a,b}({z}/{\gamma})\to R_{\gamma},

uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
.

That is,
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}

is
identified with
\operatorname{Per}_{1}^{cm}(1)\setminus{\pazocal{S}}_{1}

via a period
2
rescaling limit.

The corresponding result for
{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
can be roughly described as follows. Let
b_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
z\mapsto 1+b_{0}/z

has order
q\geq 3
. If we blow up
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
once at
[0:b_{0}:1]
, then the exceptional divisor is naturally identified with
\operatorname{Per}_{1}^{cm}(1)
via a period
q_{0}
rescaling limit.

Proposition 4.4
.

Consider
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 3
.
Let

\displaystyle\beta_{\gamma}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\theta}+\gamma a+o(a),\quad\text{ as }a\to 0,

\displaystyle M_{a}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{-1}c_{\theta}z.

Then there exists an invertible affine function

v(\gamma)=A\gamma+B
such that, for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, as
a\to 0
,

M_{a}\circ f^{\circ q}_{a,\beta_{\gamma}(a)}\circ M^{-1}_{a}\to R_{v(\gamma)},

uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
.

This proposition is a result by Stimson, but in different coordinates.
The required change of coordinates is discussed in
[
KR13
]
.
We deduce the proposition from the results in
[
KR13
]
.
Equivalent discussions are included in
[
DeM07
,
Eps00
]
.

Proof.

Consider the family
of (totally marked) quadratic rational maps
h_{\rho,\zeta}
, defined for
(\zeta,\rho)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2\}
by:

\displaystyle h_{\zeta,\rho}(z)

\displaystyle:=\zeta z\left(1-\dfrac{2+\rho}{2(1+\rho)}z\right)\left(1-\dfrac{2}{2+\rho}z\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\zeta z\left(1-\dfrac{\rho^{2}z}{4(1+\rho)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\rho-z\right)}\right).

Here, critical points and fixed points are marked.
Indeed,
h_{\zeta,\rho}
has a
fixed
point at
z=0
with multiplier
\zeta
,
another fixed point at
z=\infty
, a critical point at

1
, and another critical point at

1+\rho
. For
\rho
small, the third fixed point is close to
z=1+\rho/2
. Note that
h_{\zeta,\rho}\to\zeta z
, as
\rho\to 0
, uniformly in
compact subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{1\}
.

There exists a sufficiently small a neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
of
(\exp(2\pi i\theta),0)
such
that, for all
(\zeta,\rho)\in U
with

\rho\neq 0
, we have that
1,h_{\zeta,\rho}(1)

and
h^{\circ 2}_{\zeta,\rho}(1)
are pairwise distinct.
Thus, for all
(\zeta,\rho)\in U

with
\rho\neq 0
, there exist unique

a=a(\zeta,\rho)
and
b=b(\zeta,\rho)

such that
h_{\zeta,\rho}
is conjugate to
f_{a,b}
,
via the Möebius conjugacy mapping
1,h_{\zeta,\rho}(1)

and
h^{\circ 2}_{\zeta,\rho}(1)
to
0,\infty,1
, respectively.
It is not difficult to conclude that
the map
(\zeta,\rho)\mapsto(a(\zeta,\rho),b(\zeta,\rho))
extends to a biholomorphic map
H

from
U
onto a neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
(0,c_{\theta})
(e.g. see the proof Lemma 3.7 in
[
KR13
]
).
Under
H
, the line
\rho=0
maps into
a=0
.

According to Stimson (Theorem 3.6 in
[
KR13
]
), for any
m\in\mathbb{C}
, if

\zeta=\exp(2\pi i\theta)(1+m\rho)+o(\rho).

then

\dfrac{h^{\circ q}_{\zeta,\rho}(1+z\rho)-1}{\rho}\to qm+z+\dfrac{1}{4(z-1/2)}

uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1/2\}
.
Note that the limit is conjugate to
R_{v}(w)
with

v=-2qm+1
via the change of coordinates
z=w/2
.
Using the derivative of the biholomorphic map
H
, given
\gamma
,
there exists an invertible
map
m(\gamma):=\alpha\gamma+\beta
such that
if

b=c_{\theta}+\gamma a+o(a),

then

\zeta(a,b)=\exp(2\pi i\theta)(1+m(\gamma)\rho(a,b))+o(\rho(a,b)),

as
a\to 0
.
Hence, there exists
M_{a,b}
such that

M_{a,b}\circ f_{a,b}^{\circ q}\circ M_{a,b}^{-1}\to R_{v(\gamma)}

as
a\to 0

where

v(\gamma):=-2qm(\gamma)+1.

Moreover, note that
M_{a,b}
can be chosen
such that
M_{a,b}(0)=0
,

M_{a,b}(-2a/c_{\theta})=-2
, and
M_{a,b}(\infty)=\infty
.
Thus, we may choose
M_{a,b}(z)=a^{-1}c_{\theta}z=M_{a}(z)
.
∎

4.3.
Local parametrization

To compute the order of various quantities at a puncture
x
it
is convenient to work with a local parametrization of
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
.

Denote by
\pi_{a}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}

the projection onto the first coordinate. Then the multiplicity of
x
, denoted
\mu_{x}
, is the degree of the
covering

\pi_{a}:{\pazocal{E}}_{x}^{*}\to D^{*}
where

D^{*}
is a sufficiently small punctured disk neighborhood of
0
or
\infty
and,
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
is a suitable representative of the branch corresponding to
x
.
Note that
D^{*}
is a neighborhood of
0
if
x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
, and
of
\infty
if
x\in{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
.
Since all degree
\mu_{x}
coverings of a punctured disk are equivalent, it follows that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a power series:

b_{x}(s):=\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}c_{j}s^{j},

convergent in
\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{D}_{\varepsilon}^{*}&\to&{\pazocal{E}}_{x}^{*}\\
s&\to&(s^{\pm\mu_{x}},b_{x}(s))\end{array}

is a conformal isomorphism, for a choice of
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
.
The series
b_{x}(s)
is unique up to a
\mu_{x}
-root of unity
\eta
. That is, up to replacement of
c_{j}
by
\eta^{\pm j}c_{j}
, for all
j
.
We say that
(s^{\pm\mu_{x}},b_{x}(s))
is a
local parametrization of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
at
x
, and sometimes we will just say that
b_{x}
is a local parametrization.

For future reference, we record a direct consequence of
Lemmas
2.3
and
4.2
:

Lemma 4.5
.

If
(s^{-\mu},b(s))
is a local parametrization of a branch at
{\pazocal{L}}_{-}
,
then, as
s\to 0
,

b(s)=\dfrac{c}{s^{\mu}}+o(s^{-\mu}).

If
(s^{\mu},b(s))
is a local parametrization of a branch at
{\pazocal{L}}_{+}
,
then, as
s\to 0
,

b(s)=c_{\theta}+c{s^{\mu}}+o(s^{\mu}).

Proof.

In the first case
[s^{-\mu}:b(s):1]=[1:s^{\mu}b(s):s^{\mu}]\subset{\pazocal{S}}_{p}

for all
s\neq 0
. Hence, as
s\to 0
, we have that
s^{\mu}b(s)\not\to 0
,
by Lemma
2.3
.
The second case is similar.
∎

4.4.
Complex Transversality

The Main Lemma is, in a certain sense,
a manifestation of the following transversality results for the families

\{R_{v}\}
and
\{Q_{c}\}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Consider
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
q_{0}\geq 1
such that

Q_{c_{0}}^{\circ q_{0}}(0)=0
and
q_{0}
is the smallest positive integer with this property. Then

\dfrac{dQ^{\circ q_{0}}_{c}(0)}{dc}(c_{0})\neq 0.

Consider
v_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\ell_{0}\geq 1

such that
R_{v_{0}}^{\circ\ell_{0}}(0)=0
or
-1
and
\ell_{0}
is the smallest positive integer
with this property. Then

\dfrac{dR^{\circ\ell_{0}}_{v}(0)}{dv}(v_{0})\neq 0.

Proof.

For the quadratic family
\{Q_{c}\}
, the lemma follows from a result by Gleason or by Douady and Hubbard’s parametrization of hyperbolic components (e.g. see
[
DH85
, Exposé XIX]
).
For the parabolic family
\{R_{v}\}
, if
\omega=0
is
periodic under
R_{v_{0}}
, then Douady and Hubbard’s parametrization of hyperbolic
components applies.
If
R_{v_{0}}^{\circ\ell_{0}}(0)=-1
, then we have
the critical orbit relation

R_{v_{0}}^{\circ\ell_{0}+2}(0)=R_{v_{0}}^{\circ\ell_{0}+1}(0)
.
Consider the critical orbit relation curve
{\pazocal{X}}
contained in

{\pazocal{M}}^{cm}_{2}
formed by all maps
such that the marked critical
point maps in
\ell_{0}+1
to a fixed point. According to
[
LSvS19
]
,

{\pazocal{X}}
is smoothly embedded near
R_{v_{0}}
. Note that
R_{v_{0}}\in\operatorname{Per}^{cm}_{1}(1)\cap{\pazocal{X}}
.
It is sufficient
to show that the intersection is transversal. Otherwise,
for
\lambda<1
, sufficiently close to
1
,

\operatorname{Per}^{cm}_{1}(\lambda)\cap{\pazocal{X}}
has at least two parameters
close to
R_{v_{0}}
, where
\operatorname{Per}^{cm}_{1}(\lambda)

is the curve of maps with fixed point of multiplier
\lambda
.
As an application of
[
HT04
]
, these two parameters are hybrid equivalent in a neighborhood
of the Julia set and conformally equivalent in the complement. Hence, these
two elements of
{\pazocal{M}}_{2}^{cm}
are in fact the same. This contradiction
shows that the intersection is transversal.
∎

5.
Puiseux Series Dynamics

An algebraic closure of the field of
formal Laurent series

\mathbb{C}((t))
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
is the
field of formal Puiseux series

\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
(e.g. see
[
CA00
]
). One may realize
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
as the injective limit, with respect to the obvious inclusions, of the fields
\mathbb{C}((t^{1/m}))
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given a non-zero element

z=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}c_{n}t^{n/m}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle,

its
order at
t=0
is

\operatorname{ord}_{0}z:=\min\{n/m\geq n_{0}/m:c_{n}\neq 0\}.

It endows
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
with the non-Archimedean absolute value:

\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}:=\exp(-\operatorname{ord}_{0}z).

Local parametrizations of plane algebraic curve germs
are closely related to Puiseux series.
In our case, consider a puncture
x
with multiplicity
\mu_{x}
and
a local parametrization
(s^{\pm\mu_{x}},b_{x}(s))

where

b_{x}(s)=\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}c_{j}s^{j}.

Then the series

\beta_{x}(t):=\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}c_{j}t^{j/\mu_{x}}\in\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu_{x}}))\subset\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle

is called
a Puiseux series for the branch
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
.
A Puiseux series for
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
is unique up to multiplication by a

\mu_{x}
-root of unity.
Namely, given such a root of unity
\eta
, the series with coefficients

\eta^{j}c_{j}
is also a Puiseux series for
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
. All the Puiseux
series for
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
are of this form.

Regard rational functions
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z)
as dynamical
systems acting on the projective line over
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
.
The Puiseux series for branches of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
at
{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
are dynamically defined
parameters in a family of quadratic rational
maps
\varphi_{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z)
.
More precisely,
given
\beta\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
,
let

\varphi_{\beta}(z):=1+\dfrac{\beta}{z}+\dfrac{t}{z^{2}}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z),\quad\text{ if }\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1,

and

\varphi_{\beta}(z):=1+\dfrac{\beta}{z}+\dfrac{1}{tz^{2}}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z),\quad\text{ if }\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}>1.

Note that
\upomega:=0
is a critical point of
\varphi_{\beta}
, for all
\beta
.
The other critical point is
\upomega^{\prime}:=-2t/\beta
if
\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1

and
\upomega^{\prime}:=-2/t\beta
, otherwise.

Lemma 5.1
.

A branch of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
at
{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
has
Puiseux series
\beta_{0}

if and only if the critical point
\upomega

has period exactly
p
under iterations of

\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
.

We postpone the proof of this lemma to §
5.1
and
proceed towards stating
the Puiseux series version of the Main Lemma
3.1
.

For a rational map
\psi\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z)
such that
\psi(\infty)\neq\infty
,
by definition, the derivative of
\psi
at
\infty
will be the derivative
of
\psi(1/z)
at
z=0
.

Given
\beta
, we write

\upomega_{j}(\beta):=\varphi_{\beta}^{\circ j}(0).

Different “normalizations” arise according to whether
\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1

or not. It is therefore convenient to introduce:

\tau(\beta):=\begin{cases}t&\text{ if }\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}>1,\\
1&\text{ if }\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1.\end{cases}

When clear from context, to lighten notation, we simply write
\tau
for

\tau(\beta)
.

Below, in §
5.1
, we show that our next result is equivalent to the Main Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 5.2

(Main Lemma,
á la Puiseux
)
.

Assume
{\beta_{0}}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle

is such that
\upomega
has period
p
under
\varphi_{{\beta_{0}}}
.
Then,

(5.1)

\operatorname{ord}_{0}\dfrac{d\upomega_{p}}{d\beta}({\beta_{0}})=\operatorname{ord}_{0}\dfrac{d\varphi^{\circ p-1}_{{\beta_{0}}}}{dz}(\infty)+2\operatorname{ord}_{0}\tau.

We will prove (
5.1
) working our way towards the
p
-th iterate,
establishing a similar formula for some lower iterates.
It will require some knowledge about the non-Archimedean dynamics of

\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
, discussed in §
5.2
and §
5.3
.
The substantial part of the proof is contained in
§
5.5
through §
5.9
.

5.1.
From Puiseux series to complex numbers

Let us first prove that parameters
\beta\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
such that
the critical point
\upomega
is periodic under
\varphi_{\beta}
are in correspondence with parametrizations of branches of periodic curves at
{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
.
The proof is essentially an immediate consequence of the fact that formal
solutions
\beta\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
to the equation

f_{t^{\pm 1},b}^{\circ p}(0)=0
, in the unknown
b
, are automatically
“convergent” (e.g. see
[
Fis01
, §7.2]
).

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

It is sufficient to show that
\beta_{0}
is the Puiseux series of a branch
of a periodic curve with period dividing
p
if and only if

\upomega=0
is periodic of period dividing
p
under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
.

Assume that
(s^{\pm\mu},b(s))
is a local parametrization of

{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
for some puncture
x
at
{\pazocal{L}}_{\pm}
of a periodic curve of period dividing
p
.
Then
f_{s^{\pm\mu},b(s)}^{\circ p}(0)=0,

where

f_{s^{\pm\mu},b(s)}(z)=1+\dfrac{b(s)}{z}+\dfrac{s^{\pm\mu}}{z^{2}}\in\mathbb{C}((s))(z).

Via the isomorphism
\bar{\iota}:\mathbb{C}((s))(z)\to\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu}))(z)
that sends
s
to
t^{1/\mu}
,
we have that
f_{s^{\pm\mu},b(s)}(z)
becomes
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)
,
where
\beta_{0}=b(t^{1/\mu})
. Thus,
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ p}(0)=0
.
Hence,
\upomega=0
is periodic with period dividing
p
, under iterations of

\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
.

Conversely, assume that
\upomega=0

has period dividing
p
, under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
, for some

\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
.
Let
\mu\geq 1

be minimal such that

\beta_{0}=\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}c_{j}t^{j/\mu}\in\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu})).

Via
\bar{\iota}^{-1}
,
we have that
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)
becomes

f_{s^{\pm\mu},b_{0}(s)}(z)

where

b_{0}(s)=\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}c_{j}s^{j}\in\mathbb{C}((s)).

Hence,
(s^{\pm\mu},b_{0}(s))

is a formal solution of
f_{s^{\pm\mu},b(s)}^{\circ p}(0)=0
. Every formal
solution is convergent, for
|s|
sufficiently small (e.g. see
[
Fis01
, §7.2]
).
Since
\mu
is minimal,
(s^{\pm\mu},b_{0}(s))
is a parametrization
of
{\pazocal{E}}_{x}
for some puncture
x
of a periodic curve with period dividing
p
.
∎

Now we show that the Puiseux series version of the Main Lemma (i.e. Lemma
5.2
) is equivalent to the Main Lemma (i.e. Lemma
3.1
).
Given a puncture
x
with local parametrization
(s^{\pm\mu},b_{x}(s))
,
any meromorphic function
w:{\pazocal{E}}_{x}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
has a Laurent
series expansion in the local parameter
s
. That is,

w
is an element of
\mathbb{C}((s))
.
Under the morphism determined by

s\mapsto t^{1/\mu}
between
\mathbb{C}((s))
and
\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu}))
,
the order at
0
is divided by
\mu
.
This simple observation leads to the equivalence between the
two versions of the Main Lemma:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\beta_{0}
be a Puiseux series for a puncture
x
.
Then, (
3.1
) holds if and only if
(
5.1
) holds.

For simplicity, we use partial derivatives notation.
In particular, the derivatives involved
in (
5.1
), will be denoted by
\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{p}(\beta_{0})
and
\partial_{z}\varphi^{\circ p-1}_{\beta_{0}}(\infty)
.

Proof.

We drop the subscript from the multiplicity

\mu_{x}
and simply write
\mu
.
Denote by
\iota:\mathbb{C}((s))\to\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu}))
the isomorphism defined
by
s\mapsto t^{1/\mu}
.
Consider a local parametrization
(s^{\pm\mu},b_{0}(s))
at
x
with
associated Puiseux series
\beta_{0}
.
Then
\iota(b_{0})=\beta_{0}
.
Given
z\in\mathbb{C}((s))
(resp.
\iota(z)\in\mathbb{C}((t^{1/\mu}))
) denote by
\operatorname{ord}_{0}z
(resp.
\operatorname{ord}_{0}\iota(z)
), the order
at
s=0
(resp.
t=0
).
Then,
obviously,
\operatorname{ord}_{0}z=\mu\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{0}\iota(z)
.

Let
\operatorname{ev_{s}}:\mathbb{C}[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}((s))
be evaluation
at
a=s^{\pm\mu},b=b_{0}(s)
and

\operatorname{ev}_{t}:\mathbb{C}[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
be evaluation at

a=t^{\pm 1},b=\beta_{0}
.
Then,
\operatorname{ev}_{t}=\iota\circ\operatorname{ev}_{s}
.

Now
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\in\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle(z)

is the image of
f_{a,b}(z)\in\mathbb{C}[a,b](z)
under the morphism (induced
by)
\operatorname{ev}_{t}
. Therefore,

\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ n}(z)
is the image of
f^{\circ n}_{a,b}(z)
,
under
\operatorname{ev}_{t}
.
Also,
\operatorname{ev}_{t}

maps
\partial_{b}\omega_{p}
and

\partial_{z}f^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}(1/z)

onto
(\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{p})(\beta_{0})
and

\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ p-1}(1/z)
, respectively.
Similarly,
\operatorname{ev}_{s}
maps
\partial_{b}\omega_{p}
and

\partial_{z}f^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}(1/z)
onto the Laurent series around
x
,
in the local parameter
s
, of the meromorphic functions
\partial_{b}\omega_{p}(a,b)

and
\partial_{z}f^{\circ p-1}_{a,b}(1/z)
for
(a,b)\in\overline{{\pazocal{S}}}_{p}
.
Since
\operatorname{ev}_{t}=\iota\circ\operatorname{ev}_{s}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{0}z=\mu\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{0}\iota(z)
, it follows that equation
(
3.1
) is equation
(
5.1
),
multiplied by
\mu
.
∎

5.2.
A complete field

Recall that
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle

is endowed with the non-Archimedean absolute value

\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}:=\exp(-\operatorname{ord}_{0}z).

The metric induced by
\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}
in

\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
is particularly
suited to understand the orders of dynamically defined quantities
around the punctures of
{\pazocal{S}}_{p}
.
However, endowed with this metric,
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
is not complete.
We let
\mathbb{L}
be the field obtained as the completion of
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
. It follows that
\mathbb{L}
is also algebraically closed (e.g. see
[
Cas86
]
).
Moreover, each element
z
of
\mathbb{L}

can be represented by a series of the form:

z=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}t^{\lambda_{n}},

where
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
.
Both,
\operatorname{ord}_{0}z
and
\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

naturally extend to
\mathbb{L}
.
The value group
\left|\mathbb{L}^{\times}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}
of
\mathbb{L}
is
\exp(\mathbb{Q})
.

We regard rational maps
\varphi\in\mathbb{L}(z)
as dynamical
systems acting on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
over
\mathbb{L}
, and sometimes on the Berkovich projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1,{an}}
over
\mathbb{L}
.
For our purpose here, passing from
\mathbb{C}\langle\langle t\rangle\rangle
to
\mathbb{L}
is not essential.
However, non-Archimedean dynamics is better understood with the aid of Berkovich
spaces over
complete
fields. For background about non-Archimedean dynamics see
[
BR10
,
Ben19
]
.
In fact, we intensively employ the full description for quadratic rational dynamics over
\mathbb{L}
given by the author in
[
Kiw14
]
.
Although we summarize the main facts about quadratic dynamics in
§
5.3
, for a detailed exposition we refer the reader to
[
Kiw14
]
.

In §
5.2.1
-§
5.2.3
, we summarize basic
facts about non-Archimedean analysis that will be used frequently in the
rest of the paper.

5.2.1.
Spherical metric

The projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
is identified with

\mathbb{L}\cup\{\infty\}
via
[z:1]\mapsto z
and

[1:0]\mapsto\infty
. In
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
we work
with the
spherical metric
which, for
z,w\in\mathbb{L}
, is given by

\operatorname{dist}_{s}(z,w):=\begin{cases}\left|z-w\right|_{\mathrm{o}}&\text{ if }\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}},\left|w\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1,\\
\left|1/z-1/w\right|_{\mathrm{o}}&\text{ if }\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}},\left|w\right|_{\mathrm{o}}>1,\\
1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

It naturally extends to
z=\infty
or
w=\infty
.

In dynamical space, we will compute the size of derivatives in the
spherical metric

and denote them by
\|\cdot\|
. For example,

\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta}(\infty)\|=\left|\beta\right|_{\mathrm{o}}
, for all
\beta\neq 0
.
In parameter space, we work in the standard coordinate of
\mathbb{L}
.
For example,

\|\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\|
is

\left|\partial_{\beta}(1/\varphi_{\beta}(z))\right|_{\mathrm{o}}
if
\left|\varphi_{\beta}(z)\right|_{\mathrm{o}}>1
.
In this language, the Main Lemma states that for
n=p
:

(5.2)

\|\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{n}(\beta_{0})\|=\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ n-1}(\infty)\|\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{2}.

Our strategy will be to prove (
5.2
)
for an increasing sequence of values of
n
, finishing with
n=p
.

5.2.2.
Reduction: from Puiseux series to
\mathbb{C}

Often we will pass from dynamics over
\mathbb{L}
to dynamics over
\mathbb{C}
via
reduction
. Intuitively, reduction
amounts to replace
t
by
0
.
More precisely,
the set of elements
z=\tilde{z}+o(1)\in\mathbb{L}
is
the ring of integers

\mathfrak{O}:=\{z\in\mathbb{L}:\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1\}.

The elements of its
unique maximal ideal

\mathfrak{M}:=\{z\in\mathbb{L}:\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}<1\}

are of the form
z=o(1)
.
The quotient
\mathfrak{O}/\mathfrak{M}

is canonically identified with
\mathbb{C}

via

\tilde{z}+\mathfrak{M}\mapsto\tilde{z},

for
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{L}
.
The quotient map naturally extends to:

\operatorname{red}:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}},

by declaring
\operatorname{red}(z)=\infty
when
\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}>1
.
This map is called the
reduction map
.
Note that each spherical open ball of radius
1
maps onto a point under
reduction. The obvious inclusion
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
is a right
inverse of
\operatorname{red}
.

Although we mostly work with non-homogeneous coordinates, to understand the effect of reduction on rational maps is convenient to work in homogeneous coordinates.
Any rational map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}

can be written as a quotient of degree
d
homogeneous polynomials
P,Q

with coefficients in
\mathfrak{O}

and with at least one coefficient of absolute value

1
. The polynomials
\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}
obtained after
reducing the coefficients of
P
and
Q
might have a common factor, say
H
.
Then there exist relatively prime polynomials
\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}
with complex coefficients and a maximal degree common divisor
H
of
\tilde{P},\tilde{Q}

such that
\tilde{P}=H\cdot\widehat{P}
and
\tilde{Q}=H\cdot\widehat{Q}
.
We say that:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\operatorname{red}\varphi:&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}&\to&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\\
&[z:w]&\mapsto&[\widehat{P}(z,w),\widehat{Q}(z,w)]\end{array}

is the
reduction of
\varphi
.
When convenient we denote
\operatorname{red}(z)
by
\tilde{z}
and

\operatorname{red}\varphi

by
\tilde{\varphi}
.

The reduction map semiconjugates

\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
with

\operatorname{red}\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
outside
a finite collection disjoint open balls of spherical radius
1
.
In fact,

(\operatorname{red}\varphi)(\tilde{z})=\operatorname{red}(\varphi(z))

for all
z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
such that
H(\tilde{z})\neq 0
.
Moreover, if
\deg\tilde{\varphi}\geq 1
, then
\varphi
maps the ball
\operatorname{red}^{-1}(\tilde{z})
onto the ball

\operatorname{red}^{-1}(\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{z}))

with degree
\deg_{\tilde{z}}\tilde{\varphi}
.

5.2.3.
Action on Balls and Annuli

A ball
B
in
\mathbb{L}
is a set of the form

\{z\in\mathbb{L}:\left|z-z_{0}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}<r\}\text{ or }\{z\in\mathbb{L}:\left|z-z_{0}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq r\},

for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{L}
and some
r>0
. In the first case we say that

B
is an
open ball
, in the latter, it is a
closed ball
.
All balls are topologically open and closed. If
r\notin|\mathbb{L}^{\times}|_{0}=\exp(\mathbb{Q})
, then the open and closed ball of radius
r
around
z_{0}

coincide. If
r\in|\mathbb{L}^{\times}|_{0}
, then we say that the corresponding balls are
rational open
or

rational closed
balls.

An analytic function
\varphi:\mathfrak{O}\to\mathfrak{O}
is
power series
\sum c_{i}z^{i}

with coefficients in
\mathfrak{O}
such that
\left|c_{i}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\to 0
; equivalently,
the series converges for all
z\in\mathfrak{O}
.
The
reduction

\tilde{\varphi}=\sum\tilde{c}_{i}\tilde{z}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[\tilde{z}]
is a polynomial.
Moreover,
\deg\tilde{\varphi}\geq 1
if and only if
\varphi
is onto.
In this case,
\deg\varphi=\deg\tilde{\varphi}
is the number of preimages under
\varphi
, counting multiplicities, of points in
\mathfrak{O}
.
Moreover,
\varphi
maps the maximal open ball
\tilde{z}+\mathfrak{M}

onto
\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{z})+\mathfrak{M}

with degree
\deg_{\tilde{z}}\tilde{\varphi}
. Note that
\deg\varphi
is the number
of critical points of
\varphi
, counting multiplicities, plus
1
. This is
true since the residue field
\mathfrak{O}/\mathfrak{M}=\mathbb{C}
has characteristic
zero.

We identify
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
with
\mathbb{L}\cup\{\infty\}
.
If
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
has no poles in a ball
B
, say of radius
r
,
then

\varphi(B)
is a ball of the same type (rational, open, closed), say of radius
r^{\prime}
.
Moreover, the Schwarz Lemma holds; that is, for all
z\in B
:

\left|\dfrac{d\varphi}{dz}(z)\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq\dfrac{r^{\prime}}{r},

with equality if and only if
\varphi:B\to\varphi(B)
is a bijection; equivalently,

\left|\varphi(z)-\varphi(z^{\prime})\right|_{\mathrm{o}}=\dfrac{r^{\prime}}{r}\left|z-z^{\prime}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

for all
z,z^{\prime}\in B
.
Furthermore, since the residue field has characteristic zero, the Mean Value Theorem also holds: if
\varphi:B\to\mathbb{L}

and
\left|\varphi^{\prime}(z)\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq C
for all
z\in B
, then for all
z,z^{\prime}\in B
,

\left|\varphi(z)-\varphi(z^{\prime})\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq C\left|z-z^{\prime}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}.

In general,
a
(resp. rational, open) closed ball

B
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
containing
\infty
is the
complement of a (resp. rational, closed) open ball in
\mathbb{L}
.
Given a rational map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
and a ball
B
, then

\varphi(B)
is a ball of the same type or
\varphi(B)=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
.
If
B
is a ball and

\varphi(B)
is a ball, then
\varphi:B\to\varphi(B)
has a well
defined degree which is the number of critical points in
B
, counting multiplicities, plus
1
. If moreover

B
is a closed rational ball, then each maximal open ball in
B
maps onto a maximal open ball in
\varphi(B)
.

If
\mathbb{L}\supset B\supset B^{\prime}
are nested balls,
then
A=B\setminus B^{\prime}
is an
annuli
. Its
modulus

is
\operatorname{mod}(A):=\log\mathrm{diam}(B)-\log\mathrm{diam}(B^{\prime})
. These definitions easily generalize to

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
. If
A
is an annulus and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
is a rational map
such that
\varphi(A)
is again an annulus, then
\varphi:A\to\varphi(A)
has
a well defined degree
d_{A}\geq 1
and
d_{A}\cdot\operatorname{mod}(A)=\operatorname{mod}(\varphi(A))
.

5.3.
Quadratic rational dynamics over
\mathbb{L}
: limbs

In our family
\varphi_{\beta}
we restrict our attention to certain
subsets of parameter space that we call

limbs
, by analogy with complex dynamics. More precisely,
given
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 2
,
we define the non-Archimedean limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}\subset\mathbb{L}
by:

\displaystyle{\pazocal{L}}_{1/2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\beta=\gamma t^{-1}:\left|\gamma\right|_{\mathrm{o}}=1\},

\displaystyle{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\beta=c_{\theta}+\gamma t:\left|\gamma\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1\},\quad\text{ if }q\geq 3,

where
c_{\theta}:=-(2\cos\pi\theta)^{-2}.

By Lemmas
4.5
and
5.1
, limbs contain
the parameters of our interest. Indeed, any
\beta_{0}
for which
\upomega
is periodic of period
p\geq 3
is contained
in some limb.
In this section, we introduce basic results
about the dynamics of
\varphi_{\beta}

with focus on parameters that lie in a limb.

Below, we show that all
maps
\varphi_{\beta}
in a limb have an
invariant Rivera domain
U_{0}
.
Although Rivera domains are easier to understand in
Berkovich spacee, we will keep our discussion in the classical
line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}
. In this context, an invariant Rivera domain
U_{0}
is
the complement of a finitely many
pairwise disjoint rational closed
balls such that
\varphi:U_{0}\to U_{0}
is a bijection and
U_{0}
is maximal
with respect to these properties.

To describe the dynamics and the invariant domain
U_{0}
of
\varphi_{\beta}

when
\beta
lies
in an order
q
limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
, we
define
q
closed balls

B_{0},\dots,B_{q-1}
, subscripts mod
q
, and a maximal open ball
D_{0}
contained in
B_{0}
. We do not
make the dependence of
B_{j}
on
\theta
explicit.
Only
D_{0}
when
q=2
also depends on
\beta
.

Given
\beta=\gamma t^{-1}\in{\pazocal{L}}_{1/2}
, let

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq 1\},

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\geq\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{-1}\},

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z-\gamma^{-1}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}<1\}.

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 3
,
consider
\beta=c_{\theta}+o(1)\in{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
.
Recall that
\upomega_{j}(c_{\theta})
denotes
the
j
-th iterate of
\upomega=0
under
\varphi_{c_{\theta}}
.
Let

\displaystyle B_{j}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z-\upomega_{j}(c_{\theta})\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\},\quad\text{ if }j=0\text{ or }2\leq j<q

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\geq\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{-1}\},

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z:\left|z+c_{\theta}^{-1}t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}<\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\}.

Note that the critical points of
\varphi_{\beta}

lie in
B_{0}
and the critical values in
B_{1}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Consider a parameter
\beta
in a limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
of order
q
.
Let

U_{0}:=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{q-1}B_{j}.

Then:

(1)

\varphi_{\beta}:U_{0}\to U_{0}
is a bijection.

(2)

If
j\neq 0
, then
\varphi_{\beta}:B_{j}\to B_{j+1}
is a bijection.

(3)

\varphi_{\beta}:B_{0}\setminus D_{0}\to B_{1}
has degree
2
.

(4)

\varphi_{\beta}:D_{0}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\setminus B_{1}
is a bijection.

Proof.

We assume that
q\geq 3
. The case
q=2
follows from a similar argument.

Consider a parameter
\beta\in{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
. To simplify notation
write
c:=c_{\theta}
. Hence
\varphi_{c}
is the map corresponding to
\beta=c
.
Also,

M(\tilde{z}):=1+\dfrac{c}{\tilde{z}}=\operatorname{red}\varphi_{\beta}(\tilde{z})

is an order
q
Möebius transformation.

The semiconjugacy between
\varphi_{\beta}
and
M
, furnished by reduction, holds
for all
z\notin\mathfrak{M}
. Hence, the open ball
\tilde{z}+\mathfrak{M}

of radius
1
is mapped isometrically (in the spherical metric)
onto
M(\tilde{z})+\mathfrak{M}
, for
all
\tilde{z}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. In particular,

\upomega_{j}(\beta)\notin\mathfrak{M}
, for
1\leq j<q
.
Furthermore, a ball of spherical
radius
\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}
around
\upomega_{j}(\beta)
maps onto
a ball with the same spherical radius around
\upomega_{j+1}(\beta)
,
for all
1\leq j<q
.

We postpone the proof of (1). To prove (2), let
\gamma\in\mathfrak{O}
be such that
for
\beta=c+\gamma t\in{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
. We claim
that, for all
2\leq j\leq q
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\upomega_{j}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\upomega_{j}(c)+O(t).

Indeed, this is trivially true for
j=2
and we proceed by induction. Suppose true for some
j
such that
2\leq j<q
. Then

\left|\varphi_{\beta}(\upomega_{j}(\beta))-\varphi_{\beta}(\upomega_{j}(c))\right|_{\mathrm{o}}=\left|\upomega_{j}(\beta)-\upomega_{j}(c)\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}},

since
\varphi_{\beta}
is an isometry on open balls of radius
1
disjoint from
\mathfrak{M}
.
Moreover,

\left|\varphi_{\beta}(\upomega_{j}(c))-\varphi_{c}(\upomega_{j}(c))\right|_{\mathrm{o}}=\left|\dfrac{t\gamma}{\upomega_{j}(c)}\right|_{\mathrm{o}}\leq\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}.

From the strong triangle inequality, (
5.3
) holds for
j+1
.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{\circ j-2}(1)+O(t).

For (3) and (4), note that

It follows that

maps bijectively onto

by

, which proves (4).
For all

, an open balls of the form

is
mapped by
\varphi_{\beta}
map onto the open ball
of spherical radius

centered at

with

, which yields (3).

To finish, we deduce (1). First observe
that every element of

has
two preimages, counting multiplicities, in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}\setminus U_{0}
.
Thus,
\varphi_{\beta}(U_{0})\subset U_{0}
. By (4), a point

has exactly one preimage
in
D_{0}
. By (2) and (3), no preimage of

is contained in

. Hence,
z
has exactly one preimage in
U_{0}
. That is,
\varphi_{\beta}(U_{0})=U_{0}
and

\varphi_{\beta}
is injective in
U_{0}
.
∎

5.4.
Parabolic rescaling

The balls
B_{0},\dots,B_{q-1}
correspond to a “parabolic rescaling limit”.
That is, according to
[
Kiw14
, Theorems 1, 2]
, after
a change of coordinates mapping
B_{0}
onto the ring of integers
\mathfrak{O}
,
the map
\varphi^{\circ q}_{\beta}

reduces to a complex quadratic rational map with a parabolic fixed point.
We will need to be precise about the
dependence of the complex parabolic map on the parameter
\beta
.
Although one could implement
a non-Archimedean version of
the computations leading to Proposition
4.4
, for simplicity,
we prefer
to employ this complex dynamics result.

To write down the dependence on
\beta
,
we introduce parametrizations for the limbs
and convenient changes coordinates.
Given
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 3
,
we let

\displaystyle\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle c_{\theta}+\gamma t,\,\text{ for }|\gamma|\leq 1,

\displaystyle L_{\theta}(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle t^{-1}c_{\theta}z.

Also,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1/2}(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\gamma}{t},\text{ for }|\gamma|=1,

\displaystyle L_{1/2}(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\gamma z.

We abuse of notation and consider the dependence of
L_{1/2}
on
\gamma
implicitly. Observe that
\lambda_{\theta}
is the natural parametrization of the
\theta
-limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
.

Consider

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\pi_{0}:&B_{0}&\to&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\\
&z&\to&\operatorname{red}(L_{\theta}(z)).\end{array}

Then, given
\beta\in{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
, the map
\varphi_{\beta}^{\circ q}:B_{0}\setminus D_{0}\to B_{0}
is semiconjugate, via
\pi_{0}
, to a complex quadratic rational map
R
with a parabolic fixed point
[
Kiw14
, Theorem 1,2]
.
Moreover, the parabolic fixed point of
R
is at
\pi_{0}(U_{0})=\infty
with preimage
\pi_{0}(D_{0})=-1
and
R
has a critical point in
\pi_{0}(\upomega)=0
.
Hence,
R
is an element
R_{v}
of the parabolic family (
4.1
) and

\pi_{0}\circ\varphi_{\beta}^{\circ q}(z)=R_{v}\circ\pi_{0}(z),

for
z\in B_{0}\setminus D_{0}
.
Equivalently,

R_{v}=\operatorname{red}(L_{\theta}\circ\varphi^{\circ q}_{\beta}\circ L^{-1}_{\theta}),

where

v:=\operatorname{red}(L_{\theta}(\upomega_{q}(\beta)).

The following lemma says that the assignment
\beta\mapsto v
induces an isomorphism of complex affine lines:

Lemma 5.6
.

Given
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
of order
q\geq 3
(resp.
q=2
),
there exists a non-constant function

v_{\theta}(\tilde{\gamma})=A\tilde{\gamma}+B\in\mathbb{C}[\tilde{\gamma}]
(resp.
v_{\theta}(\tilde{\gamma})={\tilde{\gamma}}
) such that, for all
\gamma\in\mathfrak{O}
(resp.
\gamma\in\mathfrak{O}\setminus\mathfrak{M}
),

\operatorname{red}(L_{\theta}\circ\varphi^{\circ q}_{\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma)}\circ L^{-1}_{\theta}({z}))=R_{v(\tilde{\gamma})}(\tilde{z})

for all
z\in B_{0}\setminus D_{0}
or equivalently for all

\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
.

Proof.

When
\theta=1/2
, a straightforward direct computation
proves the assertion.

If
\theta
has order
q\geq 3
, then
the function

\upomega_{q}:{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}\to B_{0}

is well defined. Therefore,

L_{\theta}(\upomega_{q}(\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma)))\in\mathfrak{O},

and

w(\gamma):=\operatorname{red}L_{\theta}(\upomega_{q}(\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma)))

only depends on
{\tilde{\gamma}}
. That is,
w(\gamma)=v_{\theta}({\tilde{\gamma}})
for
some
v_{\theta}({\tilde{\gamma}})\in\mathbb{C}[{\tilde{\gamma}}]
.
We must show that
{\tilde{\gamma}}\to v_{\theta}({\tilde{\gamma}})
is an affine isomorphism.

According to Proposition
4.4
, there exists
a non-constant complex affine map
{\tilde{\gamma}}\mapsto A{\tilde{\gamma}}+B

such that if
\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma)=c_{\theta}+{\tilde{\gamma}}t
, then

A{\tilde{\gamma}}+B=\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}c_{\theta}\omega_{q}(c_{\theta}+{\tilde{\gamma}}t).

Since reduction coincides with the limit as
t\to 0
,

A{\tilde{\gamma}}+B=\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}c_{\theta}\omega_{q}(c_{\theta}+{\tilde{\gamma}}t)=\operatorname{red}(L_{\theta}(\upomega_{q}(c_{\theta}+{\tilde{\gamma}}t))=v_{\theta}({\tilde{\gamma}}).

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Consider
\beta_{0}=\lambda_{\theta}(\gamma_{0})
in an order
q
limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
.
Assume that
\upomega
has period
p
under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
.
Then there exists a minimal

such that one of the following holds:

•

and
R_{v_{\theta}(\tilde{\gamma_{0}})}^{\circ\ell_{0}}(0)=0
.

•

q\ell_{0}<p
and
R_{v_{\theta}(\tilde{\gamma_{0}})}^{\circ\ell_{0}}(0)=-1
.

Proof.

Write
\varphi:=\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
and
R_{v}:=R_{v_{\theta}}({\tilde{\gamma}}_{0})
.
Denote by

the maximal open ball contained in
B_{0}
around
\upomega
.
In Lemma
5.6
,
reduction is a semiconjugacy outside
D_{0}
.
If the orbit of
\upomega
is disjoint
from
D_{0}
, then

is periodic, say of period
\ell_{0}\geq 1
,
under
\varphi^{\circ q}
. Moreover,
\upomega
is the unique

Therefore,
q\ell_{0}=p
and

such that
\upomega_{q\ell_{0}}(\beta)\in D_{0}

hits

.
∎

Remark 5.8

In Berkovich space language, one can phrase Lemma

Denote by

.

Lemma 5.10

{\beta_{0}}

(1)

\upomega

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi^{q\ell_{0}-1}_{\beta_{0}}(\infty)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\|{\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+1}(\beta_{0})}\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\partial_{z}\varphi^{\circ q\ell_{0}}_{\beta_{0}}(\infty)}\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{2}.

Proof.

If
\beta_{0}
is a satellite parameter, it will be convenient to let
\ell_{0}=p/q
.

\beta_{0}
lies in
the
1/2
-limb. In this limb, for
\left|\gamma\right|_{\mathrm{o}}=1

\lambda(\gamma):=\gamma/t

In view of Lemma
5.6
, for all

\operatorname{red}(\gamma\varphi^{\circ 2}_{\lambda(\gamma)}(z/\gamma))=R_{\tilde{\gamma}}(\tilde{z}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{red}(\upomega_{2\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0})+\gamma_{0}\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{2\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0})t^{-1})

\displaystyle\operatorname{red}\left(\dfrac{d}{d\gamma}(\gamma\upomega_{2\ell_{0}}(\lambda(\gamma)))|_{\gamma=\gamma_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{d{\tilde{\gamma}}}\operatorname{red}\left(\gamma\upomega_{2\ell_{0}}(\lambda(\gamma)\right)|_{{\tilde{\gamma}}={\tilde{\gamma}}_{0}}

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{d{\tilde{\gamma}}}R^{\circ\ell_{0}}_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}(0)|_{{\tilde{\gamma}}={\tilde{\gamma}}_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\neq 0.

\displaystyle\upomega_{2\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/{\tilde{\gamma}}_{0}+o(1).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\tilde{\gamma}}_{0}+o(1).

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+1}(\beta_{0})\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0}))\right|_{\mathrm{o}}.

\left\|\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+1}(\beta_{0})\right\|=\left\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0}))\right\|.

Furthermore,

\left\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{q\ell_{0}-1}(\infty)\right\|=1,

by (

). For

, the lemma now follows from the chain rule.
∎

5.7.
Geometry of polynomial rescaling limits

Given
\beta
in an order
q
limb
{\pazocal{L}}_{\theta}
, the
filled
Julia set
of
\varphi_{\beta}
is

{\pazocal{K}}(\varphi_{\beta}):=\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}:\varphi^{\circ n}_{\beta}(z)\notin U_{0}\text{ for all }n\geq 0\}.

We are interested on the geometry of
{\pazocal{K}}(\varphi_{\beta})
around
\upomega

for parameters with non-central returns.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\beta_{0}
be a parameter of period
p
in an order
q
limb
such that
\upomega
has a non-central return.
Then there exist a periodic rational closed ball
X_{0}

around
\upomega
of period
q^{\prime}>q
. Moreover,

\varphi^{\circ q^{\prime}}_{\beta}:X_{0}\to X_{0}
has degree
2
.

Clearly,
X_{0}\subset{\pazocal{K}}(\varphi_{\beta_{0}})
and
q^{\prime}
divides

.

Proof.

The proposition is consequence of results from
[
Kiw14
]
.
Let

D
be the maximal open ball such that
\upomega\in D\subset{\pazocal{K}}(\varphi_{\beta})
.
Let
X_{0}

be the closed ball containing
\upomega
of diameter
\mathrm{diam}(D)
.
By
[
Kiw14
, Proposition 5.3]
,
X_{0}
is
a rational closed ball and either
X_{0}=B_{0}
or
X_{0}
is periodic, say of period
q^{\prime}
.
We now prove by contradiction that
X_{0}\neq B_{0}
. Suppose

X_{0}=B_{0}
. Then
D
is a maximal open ball in
B_{0}
.
Since
\beta_{0}
has a non-central return,

D
eventually maps onto
D_{0}
. This is
impossible because
D_{0}
contains a preimage of
U_{0}
.
Therefore,
X_{0}
is a periodic ball. Moreover,
q^{\prime}>q
, since
X_{0}
maps into
D_{0}
in
q\ell_{0}
iterates, for some
\ell_{0}\geq 1
. Furthermore,
the unique critical point of
\varphi^{\circ q^{\prime}}_{\beta}:X_{0}\to X_{0}
is
\upomega
, so it has degree
2
.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Suppose that
\beta
is a parameter in an order
q
limb.
If there exists
q^{\prime}\geq 1
and a ball
X_{0}\ni\upomega
such that

\varphi^{q^{\prime}}_{\beta}(X_{0})=X_{0}
, then either
X_{0}
is an open ball
and
\mathrm{diam}(X_{0})=\mathrm{diam}(B_{0})
, or
X_{0}
is the unique periodic
closed ball containing
\upomega
,
q^{\prime}>q
and

\upomega
has a non-central return.

Proof.

Since
\partial\overline{X_{0}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1,{an}}
is a (Berkovich) repelling
periodic point of
\varphi_{\beta}:\mathbb{P}^{1,{an}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1,{an}}
, according to
[
Kiw14
, Theorem 1]
,
we have that
\partial\overline{X_{0}}=\partial\overline{B_{0}}
or
X_{0}
is a closed rational
ball properly contained in
B_{0}
.
In the first case, taking into account that some points of
B_{0}
map into
U_{0}
, we have
that
B_{0}
is not a periodic ball so
X_{0}
is a maximal open ball
of
B_{0}
.
In the second, we claim that
\upomega
has a non-central return.
By contradiction, suppose that
\beta
is a
satellite parameter, then
\varphi_{\beta}^{\circ q^{\prime}}(D^{\prime})=D^{\prime}

.
∎

The size of the balls in the orbit of

Lemma 5.13

Let
\beta
be a parameter in an order
q

of period

containing
\upomega

Given a parameter
\beta
with a periodic ball

as above, we will freely use the notation
for

Proof.

Given

\Gamma_{j}:=\{B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{L}}:B\text{ is a closed ball and }X_{j}\subset B\subsetneq B_{k}\}.

We use
reverse interval notation in

C

Moduli of annuli give a natural parametrization of

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma_{j}&\to&]-\log\mathrm{diam}(B_{k}),-\log\mathrm{diam}(X_{j})]\\
B&\mapsto&-\log\mathrm{diam}(B).\end{array}

We simply say that the length of
[A,B]
is
the modulus of

, so the parametrization preserves length.

We omit the subscript
\beta_{0}
and simply write
\varphi
.
We will analyze the dynamics induced by
\varphi
on
\cup\Gamma_{j}
.
Its action in
\Gamma_{j}

\varphi
.
Recall that
\varphi
maps

.
Denote by
D
the open ball around
\upomega
of diameter
\mathrm{diam}(B_{0})

If
X_{j}\subset D_{0}

X_{j}\subset C_{k}
for some

and

of

\Gamma_{j}

\Gamma_{j+1}=]B_{k+1},X_{j+1}]

Furthermore,

C_{k}
maps bijectively onto
B_{k}
.

If
X_{j}\subset B_{k}
for some

, then
\varphi:\Gamma_{j}\to\Gamma_{j+1}=]B_{k+1},X_{j}]

\varphi
maps
B_{k}
onto
B_{k+1}
bijectively.

The interval

consists of balls contained in

and

. Therefore,

\varphi:\Gamma_{0}\to\Gamma_{1}
is a bijection that multiplies lengths by a factor of

.

If
X_{j}\subset D
for some

, then
\Gamma_{j}\cap\Gamma_{0}=]B_{0},S_{j}]
for
some

. Hence,
\varphi:\Gamma_{j}\to\Gamma_{j+1}
is a bijection that

and preserves lengths of the subintervals of

.

Note that
\varphi(\Gamma_{j})\supset\Gamma_{j+1}
for all

is not contained in

.

Now we are ready to compare lengths of
\Gamma_{j}
with

.
We claim that, for all

, there

. Indeed, given

, let

be minimal so that

with the property that

\varphi^{\circ n}(B^{\prime})=B
.
Such a number

always exists. In fact,
B
has a unique preimage

in
\Gamma_{j-1}
. If

, then
B\in\Gamma_{1}
and

.
Otherwise,
C
maps injectively onto
B
.
Taking preimages recursively we obtain

and
B^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{1}
.
Let
\psi:\Gamma_{j}\to\Gamma_{1}
be the map that sends
B
to
B^{\prime}
.
Note that
n(B)<j
. Moreover,
n(B)
is non-decreasing.
Given
n\geq 0
, let

If
B^{\prime}=\psi(B)
for some

,
then

, for otherwise,

. Hence,

\psi:I_{n}\to\Gamma_{1}

preserves length.
Moreover,

, since from the definition
of

we have that

if

.

If

is constant, say

and

If

is not constant. Consider

such that the intervals

Let

and

of

be periodic of period diving

omits intervals of

and, therefore,

∎

The dynamical space derivate at
\infty

is controlled by the diameters of

:

Lemma 5.14
.

Assume that
\beta_{0}
is a parameter in an order
q
limb such
that
\upomega
is periodic of period

under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
and

maps

onto

, it maps
the open ball

of diameter

∎

5.8.
Polynomial parameter rescaling

Consider a parameter
\beta_{0}
such that
\upomega
has period
p
and a non-central return. Recall that
X_{0},\dots,X_{q^{\prime}-1}
denotes the periodic closed balls under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
where

\upomega\in X_{0}
.
Our analysis will show that there exists a parameter space ball
\Lambda_{0}
,
around
\beta_{0}
, such that
\{X_{0},\dots,X_{q^{\prime}-1}\}

is a periodic orbit of closed balls

for all

\beta\in\Lambda_{0}
.
We say that parameters
\beta
with such an orbit of periodic closed balls,

a period

Before proving the existence of
\Lambda_{0}
,
we need to prove that (

) holds for

Lemma 5.15
.

Assume that
\beta_{0}
is a parameter in an order
q
limb such
that
\upomega
is periodic of period
p
under
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
.
Suppose that
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}
has a primitive

q^{\prime}

be such that

From Lemma
5.10

\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta}^{\circ q\ell_{0}-1}(\infty)\|=\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{-1}

To prove the first formula we proceed by induction.
In view of Lemma
5.10

is already proven.

For

, all the balls

have

For

For all
k\leq q^{\prime}-q\ell_{0}
, since

is a bijection,
Schwarz Lemma yields:

(5.8)

\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ k}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0}))\|>\begin{cases}\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}},\text{ if $q=2$ and $X_{q\ell_{0}+k}\subset B_{1}$,}\\
1,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
z=:\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+k}(\beta_{0})
.
If
q\geq 3
, or
q=2
and
X_{q\ell_{0}+k}\subset B_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\|\cdot\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ k}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}})\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

The first inequality can be checked case by case applying Lemma
5.9
. The second is a consequence of (
5.8
).

Similarly,
if
q=2
and
X_{q\ell_{0}+k}\subset B_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\|\cdot\left|t\right|_{\mathrm{o}}^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\|\cdot\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ k}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}})\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

In both cases, the inductive hypothesis yields

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(z)\|\cdot\|\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+k}({\beta_{0}})\|.

By the chain rule, the strong triangle inequality, and the inductive hypothesis
we have:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+k+1}(\beta_{0})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta}(z)+\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+k}({\beta}_{0})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta}(z)\partial_{\beta}\upomega_{q\ell_{0}+k}({\beta}_{0})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}\varphi_{\beta_{0}}^{\circ k+1}(\upomega_{q\ell_{0}}(\beta_{0}))\|\cdot\left|\tau\right|_{\mathrm{o}}

∎

Proposition 5.16

\beta_{0}

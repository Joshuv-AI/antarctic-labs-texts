Development of a Multinational Collaboration Supporting Reconstructive Surgery in Response to the 2023–2025 War in Gaza
Authors: Sheila S. Nazarian Mobin, Scot Bradley Glasberg, B. Aviva Preminger, Mark D. Epstein
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We are writing regarding “Development of a Multinational Collaboration Supporting Reconstructive Surgery in Response to the 2023–2025 War in Gaza,” published in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery–Global Open.1

We remain troubled that an article containing overtly politicized framing and contested factual assertions was published in a peer-reviewed surgical journal without greater editorial restraint or supervision. This issue is not with the humanitarian need, or with “deeply personal”2 concerns, but rather with the integrity, neutrality, and credibility of scientific publication itself, and that confidence in this journal has been placed at risk.

As surgeons, we are trained to acknowledge mistakes openly and correct them transparently through morbidity and mortality conferences. Published scientific work should be treated no differently. Unlike irreversible surgical complications, problematic wording, unsupported assertions, and politically charged narrative in published articles can be corrected. Scientific integrity requires a willingness to make such corrections when warranted.

Most concerning is the article’s framing of the conflict. Phrases such as “the mechanisms of warfare used by Israeli forces” having “disproportionately affected Palestinian civilians” may reflect an argument, but in this paper they are presented without balanced discussion of Hamas’s conduct on October 7, 2023; the embedding of military infrastructure in civilian areas, including in and under hospitals and schools; the realities of urban warfare; or the limitations of wartime data, relying heavily on figures from the Gaza Ministry of Health, an authority controlled by Hamas. Even more troubling is the assertion of “systematic targeting” of healthcare facilities and governance structures, presented as established fact yet supported only by an Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights press release and a World Health Organization news statement3,4 rather than peer-reviewed evidence. Similarly, the paper discusses border closures while omitting Gaza’s border with Egypt. Readers expect a reconstructive surgery journal to prioritize rigorous clinical and methodological analysis, not to serve as a vehicle for contested geopolitical messaging.

It is particularly problematic that these objections were raised directly with both the authors and the journal, yet requests for corrective action were denied. The authors did not meaningfully respond and declined to remove the challenged language, the editor concluded that the article did not warrant retraction or correction, and a request for a Committee on Publication Ethics review was initially rejected before later being initiated, where it remains ongoing.5 These developments suggest that there may be no meaningful mechanism for addressing politicized framing, disputed factual assertions, and the presentation of advocacy as scientific work.

We would also note a concern regarding consistency of editorial standards: comparable politicized language directed at other nations or minority groups would be unlikely to survive peer review without substantial revision.

The irony is that the article contains material that could have been a valuable resource for humanitarian planning. The biased commentary and disputed accusations are unnecessary to its scientific value and could be removed without affecting its substantive clinical contribution. We urge the editors to review this publication and take appropriate corrective action, including retraction, correction, or redaction of portions that fail to meet accepted standards of scientific rigor, evidentiary restraint, and neutrality.

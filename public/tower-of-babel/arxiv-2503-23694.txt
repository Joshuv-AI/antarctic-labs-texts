Derivative expansion in a two-scalar field theory
Alícia G. Borges

Abstract




The derivative expansion of the effective action is considered in the
model with two interacting real scalar fields in curved spacetime.
Using the functional approach and local momentum representation, the
coefficient of the derivative term is calculated up to the first order in
curvature in the one-scalar theory. The two-scalar problem is solved
by extracting normal modes and consequent reduction to the
single-scalar case. The method can be applied to a larger number of
scalars. In the theory with strong hierarchy of masses, the
renormalized effective potential and the coefficients of the
second-order derivative terms demonstrate the quantum decoupling
in the low-energy limit.

Keywords:

Scalar field, effective action, derivative expansion,
curved spacetime

81T10, 81T15, 81T20

Introduction

Certain cosmological models, such as assisted inflation, are based
on two scalar fields with a strong hierarchy of masses (see, e.g.,
Refs.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

and the more recent reference
[
4
]
). Since the typical
energy scale in the inflationary epoch is high, it may be important to
evaluate quantum effects modifying the scalar action. The relevant
quantum corrections may include not only the effective potential of
scalars but also the lowest-order derivative terms. In this respect,
one can note that there are several insufficiently studied aspects of
the quantum theory. The first one of these important details is that
cosmological inflation is characterized by large values of
curvatures, which cannot be neglected in the effective potential
and in the quantum contributions to the derivative terms. The
curvature-dependent terms are pretty well-known for the effective
potential, but almost not explored for the derivative expansion. The
last unexplored (at least to our knowledge) issue is that the quantum
effects may be quite different in the high-energy (UV) and
low-energy (IR) limits owing to the quantum decoupling of the
large-mass scalar in the latest stage of inflation, where the energy
scale gets lower. As a result, at the later phase of inflation, the
quantum effects of only one scalar remain relevant.
The described features and possibilities make it interesting to
explore the curvature-dependent effective potential and the
lowest-order derivative expansion of the effective action in the
multi-scalar models, especially with different masses.

The derivative expansion of the effective action is a traditional
subject of study in quantum field theory

[
5
,
6
]
,
which has been extensively applied in various branches of effective
field theory (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
and
[
8
]

normal coordinates
[

and zeta-regularization

[

[

and
[

with the development of the derivative expansion
for
O(N)

In the present work, we explore the one-loop quantum contributions
to the effective action of two scalar fields with different masses in
zero and second orders of derivative expansion, in curved spacetime.
We follow a simple approach similar to extracting normal modes of
the oscillator in classical mechanics. The scheme can be applied to
the multiple scalars and arbitrary self-interactions, at least without
derivatives. Following the previous
literature
[
9
]
, we use functional methods in curved
spacetime, combined with local momentum representation

[

[

for further references), based on

[

(see also
[

]

One of the motivations for our work is to elaborate the formalism
for taking into account quantum effects in the cosmological models
with multiple scalar fields. Such quantum corrections may influence
inflationary dynamics and therefore may be relevant. An interesting
aspect in these scenarios is the potential (IR) decoupling of heavy
fields, which, if present, could effectively reduce the number of
relevant degrees of freedom at late times. This phenomenon is
particularly important for understanding whether in the later
cosmological epoch, only the quantum effects of a lighter scalar
field remain significant, reinforcing the role of effective quantum
approach in such kind of models. Beyond the applications to
inflation, our results may be also useful for a broader understanding
of quantum corrections in multi-field theories, where functional
techniques provide a powerful framework for systematically extracting
both potential and lowest-order derivative terms.

Derivative expansion for a single scalar field

Derivative expansion is a useful extension of the functional
methods to calculate the effective potential. In general, the
effective action is a nonlocal functional, but one can start from
the constant part, i.e., the effective potential, and extract the
lowest-order terms corresponding to the weakly varying fields.
The scheme may be applied to different kinds of fields, but the
most common application is to scalars. In the case of a single
mean field
\varphi
, the first terms in the expansion have the form

\displaystyle\Gamma[\varphi,\,g_{\mu\nu}]\,=\,\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\,\Big\{-V_{eff}(\phi)\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,Z(\phi)\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi+...\,\Big\}\,.

(1)

The calculation of
\,V_{eff}(\phi)\,
can be performed for a
constant
\phi=\phi_{0}
to any order of the loop expansion and
for the theories with different contents of quantum fields. In this
work, we restrict our attention to the self-interacting scalar field
in curved space-time. The one-loop flat-space result was derived
in the renowned work
[
17
]
. The general investigation
based on the functional methods, was done in
[
18
]
.
Let us reproduce the expression for the renormalized one-loop
contribution to the effective potential derived in
[
19
]

by using the mass-dependent scheme of renormalization and
normal coordinates,

\displaystyle V_{eff}^{ren}(g_{\mu\nu},\,\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,(m^{2}-\xi R)\varphi^{2}\,+\,V

(2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\,\Big[\frac{1}{2}\,\big(m^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}\big)^{2}\,-\,\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\,R\,\big(m^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}\big)\Big]\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{m^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\,,

where the density of the cosmological constant term

\rho_{\Lambda}
is included for completeness and
\mu^{2}
is
the dimensional parameter of renormalization.

The evaluation of
Z(\phi)
requires variable fields. The idea of
the standard procedure
[
5
,
6
]
is to split
the scalar field into a background constant
\phi_{0}
and the variable
part
\varphi
as
\phi(x)=\phi_{0}+\varphi(x)
, and evaluate the
effective action up to the second order in derivatives.
Since
\partial\phi(x)=\partial\varphi(x)
, this approach gives a complete
result for
Z
. Although one can apply this method to calculate
terms of the higher orders in derivatives
[
6
]
, for
small oscillations near the minimum, these
terms are less relevant and we do not consider them here.

Single scalar field in flat space

To illustrate the procedure, we first perform the flat-space
calculation in the classical theory of a real scalar field with
the self-interaction
\lambda\phi^{4}
. The Lagrangian density is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\,-\,\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,\phi^{4}.

(3)

After Wick rotation,
the one-loop contribution to the effective action is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr ln}(\Box+M^{2}),

(4)

where

\displaystyle M^{2}=m^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\phi^{2}(x).

(5)

In the next subsection, we consider the generalization to curved
spacetime, where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi)\,=\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\,\Big\{-\,V^{(1)}(\phi)\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,Z^{(1)}(\phi)\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi\,+\,...\Big\}\,.

(6)

For now, consider the one-loop version of (
1
) in flat
space
[
6
]
. To find the one-loop effective potential

V^{(1)}
it is
sufficient to consider a constant scalar
\phi_{0}
in Eq. (
4
).
On the other hand, the derivation of
Z^{(1)}(\phi)
requires setting

\phi(x)=\phi_{0}+\varphi(x)
, with a subsequent expansion to the second
order in the coordinate-dependent part
\varphi(x)
. The coefficients of
this expansion depend only on
\phi_{0}
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi_{0}+\varphi)\,\,=\,\,\int d^{4}x\,\,\Big\{-\,V^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,-\,\frac{\partial V^{(1)}}{\partial\phi_{0}}\,\varphi\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial^{2}V^{(1)}}{\partial\phi_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\,\,\frac{1}{2}\,Z^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,\partial_{\mu}\varphi\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi\,+\,...\Big\}\,.

(7)

To elaborate the last term, one has to expand the logarithm in
Eq. (
4
)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi_{0}+\varphi)\,-\,\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,\,=\,\,\,\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\big(2\phi_{0}\varphi+\varphi^{2}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,-\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\,\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\Big]\,+\,...

(8)

and commute all the momentum operators to the left using the
identity
[
5
,
6
]

\displaystyle\Phi\,\frac{1}{p^{2}+M^{2}}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{p^{2}+M^{2}}\,\Phi\,+\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M^{2})^{2}}\,\big[p^{2},\Phi\big]\,+\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M^{2})^{3}}\,\big[p^{2},\big[p^{2},\Phi\big]\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M^{2})^{4}}\,\big[p^{2},\big[p^{2},\big[p^{2},\phi\big]\big]\big]\,\,+...\,\,.

(9)

The commutators of the momentum operator with an arbitrary function
of coordinates
\Phi
follows the rules
(the four-dimensional momentum operator is identified
as
\,p_{\mu}=-i\partial_{\mu}
)

\displaystyle\big[p^{2},\Phi\big]\,=\,\Box\Phi+2ip^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Phi,

\displaystyle\big[p^{2},\big[p^{2},\Phi\big]\big]\,=\,\Box^{2}\Phi\,+\,4ip^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Box\Phi\,-\,4p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\Phi\,.

(10)

After the described procedure we arrive at

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi_{0}+\varphi)\,-\,\Gamma^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,\,=\,\,\frac{\lambda\phi_{0}}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\Big]\,+\,\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\,\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\,\varphi^{2}\Big]-\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[M_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{4}}\varphi\Box\varphi\Big].\qquad

(11)

Compared to Eq. (
7
), the first three terms in the
r.h.s.

are the derivatives of
V^{(1)}
. Concerning the kinetic part, we need
the last term with the d’Alembertian operator. The prescription for the
functional trace in momentum space can be found in
[
6
]
.
In brief,

\displaystyle\,\mbox{Tr}\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M^{2})^{n}}\,=\,\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{(k^{2}+M^{2})^{n}}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2k^{3}dk}{(k^{2}+M^{2})^{n}}.

(12)

Deriving the last integral for
n\geq 2
is a simple exercise
and we skip the details. After integrating by parts in the
last term of (
11
), we arrive at

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}(\phi_{0})\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{12(4\pi)^{2}}\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,.

(13)

As expected from the power counting, this expression is finite and
hence is independent of the renormalization procedure.

Effective action of a single scalar field in curved space

Consider now the generalization of the previous result to an
arbitrary curved spacetime. In this case, we have several options
for deriving the effective potential of the scalar field and
derivative expansion. The pioneer calculation of
[
20
]

used a technique specific for the potential of a scalar field
in de-Sitter spacetime. Technically more simple is the integration
of the renormalization group equation
[
21
]
,
can be generalized for other sectors of effective action

[
22
]
but since this approach is essentially tied
to the Minimal Subtraction scheme of renormalization, it is not
appropriate to explore the low-energy decoupling. The same
concerns the recently proposed approach of deriving effective
potential from the integration of trace anomaly
[
23
]
.
The most complete result for the massive scalar field can be
achieved by evaluating the form factors in the effective action

[
24
]
. However, the results for the heat-kernel
solution
[
25
]
are elaborated explicitly only for
the second-order terms
[
26
,
27
]
. This technique
enables one to evaluate the
(\partial\phi)^{2}
-type and
R\phi^{2}
-type
terms, but the
R(\partial\phi)^{2}
-type corrections, especially in the
theory with two or more different masses, would require elaborating
on the heat-kernel solution in the third order in “curvatures”

[
28
]
. Thus, we need an appropriate method for
the effective potential and the derivative expansion in curved
spacetime. The mass-dependent scheme for the curved-space
effective potential has been developed using Riemann normal
coordinates and local momentum representation
[
13
]
.
The corresponding calculation can be found in
[
19
]
for a
general interaction term, so we only need to work out the
derivative expansion.

Let us derive the kinetic term
Z
for the one-component scalar
field theory with a
\lambda\phi^{4}
interaction term, following the
procedure which is a direct generalization of the one outlined in
the previous subsection
2.1
. The action under consideration,
including the nonminimal term of the first order in curvature, has
the form

\displaystyle S(\phi,g_{\mu\nu})=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\,\phi^{2}m^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\xi R\phi^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^{4}\Big\}.

(14)

For the sake of simplicity, we choose the most common classical
potential
V(\phi)=\lambda\phi^{4}/4!
, different from the case of
effective potential in (
2
).

The propagator expansion in the local momentum representation
can be done in different forms
[
13
]
, and the one which
is the most appropriate for deriving the effective potential is, in the
Euclidean signature,
1
1

1

This expression
includes the normal coordinate expansion of
\sqrt{-g}
, such that
no corrections in the vertex part are required.

\displaystyle\bar{G}(y)\,=\,\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{iky}\bigg[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,-\,\frac{\tilde{\xi}R}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\bigg],

(15)

where

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}\,=\,m^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\phi^{2}_{0}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tilde{\xi}=\xi-\frac{1}{6}.

(16)

Let us note that since we restrict our attention to the

\mathcal{O}(R)
terms, hence
R
can be treated as a constant.
The method can be applied beyond this approximation, but this
would require more calculations.

Following the same steps as in the flat case, but
with the propagator (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi_{0}+\varphi,\,g_{\mu\nu})-\Gamma(\phi_{0},g_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\bigg\{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{k^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}-\,\,\frac{\tilde{\xi}R}{(k^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\Big)\big(2\phi_{0}\varphi+\varphi^{2}\big)\bigg\}.

(17)

Expanding the logarithm into series, discarding terms of the second
order in curvatures, and also higher orders in
\phi
, we arrive at
the expression

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi_{0}+\varphi,\,g_{\mu\nu})-\Gamma(\phi_{0},g_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,=\,\,\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\big(2\phi_{0}\varphi+\varphi^{2}\big)\Big]-\,\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\tilde{\xi}R\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}(2\phi_{0}\varphi+\varphi^{2})\Big]

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad-\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\,\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\Big]\,+\,\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\,\tilde{\xi}R\phi_{0}^{2}\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\,\varphi\Big].\quad

As we already know, the kinetic term arises because of the commutation
of
\varphi
with the momentum operator. Therefore, we can focus only
on the last two terms of Eq. (
18
).

It proves useful to express the coefficient
Z^{(1)}(\phi)
as

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}(\phi)=Z^{(1)}_{0}+Z^{(1)}_{1}R+\mathcal{O}(R^{2})\,.

(19)

The first term,
Z^{(1)}_{0}
, corresponds to the flat space and has
already been calculated in (
13
). One can rewrite it by
replacing
M_{0}^{2}\longrightarrow M^{2}
,

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}_{0}=\frac{1}{12}\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}M^{2}}

(20)

The last term in (
18
), after commutations, becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\,\tilde{\xi}\phi_{0}^{2}\,R\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\,\varphi\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\,\varphi\Big]\,\,=\,\,\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\,\tilde{\xi}\phi_{0}^{2}\,R\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big\{\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{3}}\,\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{4}}\big[p^{2},\varphi\big]\varphi\,+\,\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{5}}\big[p^{2},\big[p^{2},\varphi\big]\big]\varphi+\,...\Big\}.

(21)

The first term of the last expression contributes to the second
derivative of the effective potential because

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}+M_{0}^{2}}\varphi^{2}\Big]-\,\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\tilde{\xi}R\,\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\varphi^{2}\Big]\,-\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{2}}\,\varphi^{2}\,\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\,+\,\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\tilde{\xi}\phi_{0}^{2}}{4}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\Big[\frac{1}{(p^{2}+M_{0}^{2})^{3}}\,\varphi^{2}\,\Big]\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\frac{\partial^{2}V^{(1)}}{\partial\phi_{0}^{2}}\varphi^{2}\,.

(22)

After integrating by parts, we obtain the first-order correction
to (
13
) in the form

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}_{1}\,\,=\,\,\frac{\lambda^{2}}{24(4\pi)^{2}M_{0}^{4}}\,\,\tilde{\xi}R\phi_{0}^{2}\,.

(23)

Replacing
\phi_{0}^{2}
by
\phi^{2}
, the coefficient
Z^{(1)}(\phi)
,
in the
\mathcal{O}(R)
approximation, is given by

\displaystyle Z^{(1)}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{12\,(4\pi)^{2}}\,\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}}{M^{2}}\,\bigg(1\,+\,\frac{\tilde{\xi}R}{2M^{2}}\bigg).

(24)

Remarkably, this expression does not depend on the renormalization
parameter
\mu
. This is explained by the fact that, in the pure
scalar theory, at the one-loop level, there are no divergences in
the kinetic term.

At this point it is worthwhile to make a
clarification about the approximations used in the formula
(
24
). In flat spacetime, the derivative expansion
(
1
) assumes that the derivatives of the background
scalar are subleading compared to the potential part, i.e.,

\big(\partial\phi\big)\ll M^{4}
or, in the detailed form,

\big(\partial\phi\big)\ll\lambda\phi^{4}+\phi^{2}m^{2}
. It is important to
note that
M^{2}
depends on the background scalar, such that
the definition (
1
) and all consequent formulas
hold even in the massless limit
[
17
,
18
]
.
The mentioned approximation remains necessary in the next
orders of expansion
[
6
]
,
and also in the models with fermions and/or vectors, where
the kinetic term is divergent and gains logarithmic corrections.
This is the limitation behind Eq. (
13
), but the curvature
correction in (
24
) implies also the expansion in the
Riemann normal coordinates, which means an approximation
of weak gravitational field. In our setting, this means

\big|R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\big|\ll M^{2}
, that have to hold simultaneous
with the aforementioned flat-space restriction

\big(\partial\phi\big)\ll M^{4}
.

The case of a two-component scalar field

Consider a model with two real interacting scalar fields
\phi

and
\chi
, described by the covariant Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-\frac{1}{2}\,m^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\,M^{2}\chi^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\,\frac{1}{2}\,\xi_{1}R\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\xi_{2}R\chi^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4!}\phi^{4}-\frac{\lambda_{2}\chi^{4}}{4!}-\frac{\lambda_{12}}{8}\phi^{2}\chi^{2}\,.

(25)

Here
m
,
M
are the masses of the two fields
and
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{12}
are the coupling constants.

In what follows, we show how to reduce the quantum calculations in
the theory with two masses to the single-mass case. Our approach is
based on the background field method and the change of variables
(diagonalization) which was previously used in
[
29
]

(see also
[
16
]
for a general discussion of diagonalization
and
[
15
]
for the references to original papers and more
profound discussion, including the non-positively defined oscillators).
The rest of the procedure is analogous to using normal modes in the
multidimensional oscillator.

Diagonalization of the bilinear form of the action

Following the background field method, we decompose scalar fields
into background
\phi
,
\chi
and quantum
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}

counterparts as

\displaystyle\phi\,\longrightarrow\,\phi+\sigma_{1}\,,\qquad\chi\,\longrightarrow\,\chi+\sigma_{2}\,.

(26)

The one-loop contribution depends on the bilinear in quantum fields
action

\displaystyle S^{(2)}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\,\big(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{1}&\sigma_{2}\end{array}\big)\hat{H}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{1}\\
\sigma_{2}\end{array}\right)\,.

Operator
\hat{H}
has a standard matrix form

\displaystyle\hat{H}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}H_{11}&H_{12}\\
H_{21}&H_{22}\end{array}\right)\,=\,\hat{1}\Box+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{11}^{2}&M_{12}^{2}\\
M_{21}^{2}&M_{22}^{2}\end{array}\right)\,,

where

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{1}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}M_{11}^{2}=\,m^{2}-\xi_{1}R+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\,\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{4}\chi^{2},\\
M_{12}^{2}=M_{21}^{2}=\,\lambda_{12}\,\phi\chi,\\
M_{22}^{2}=\,M^{2}-\xi_{2}R+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{4}\phi^{2}\,.\end{array}\right.

To diagonalize matrix
\hat{H}
, we perform rotation to the
angle
\alpha
in the space of the fields,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{1}\\
\sigma_{2}\end{array}\right)\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\
\sin\alpha&\,\,\cos\alpha\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\rho_{1}\\
\rho_{2}\end{array}\right).

This transformation preserves the diagonal form of the derivative
term in (
3.1
) and, on the other hand, its Jacobian equals to
one. After rotation, the bilinear action becomes

\displaystyle S^{(2)}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big\{\rho_{1}\Box\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\Box\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,\rho_{1}\big(M_{11}^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha+2M_{12}^{2}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha+M_{22}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha\big)\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,\rho_{2}\big(M_{11}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha-2M_{12}^{2}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha+M_{22}^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha\big)\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,\rho_{1}\big[(M_{22}^{2}-M_{11}^{2})\sin 2\alpha\,+\,2M_{12}^{2}\cos 2\alpha\big]\rho_{2}\Big\}\,.

(49)

Choosing

\displaystyle\theta\,=\,\cot 2\alpha\,=\,\frac{M^{2}_{22}-M^{2}_{11}}{2M_{12}^{2}}

(50)

Setting the framework

Conclusions

In this work, we developed a systematic approach to calculate
the effective potential and the first-order terms in the derivative
expansion in the theory with several scalar fields with different
masses. The
double diagonalization
method is based
upon the functional approach to effective action

[
18
,
5
,
6
]
and consists from

i)
the diagonalization of the operator
\hat{H}
(bilinear
form of the action) and
ii)
changing the basis of
expansion to the linear independent directional derivatives in the
background scalar fields. The two-scalars calculation illustrating
this method, can be easily generalized to a larger number of
scalar fields. The use of this method enables a simple derivative
expansion not only in Minkowski spacetime, but also in curved
space, at least in the first order in scalar curvature.

The analysis of the two-scalar system with a strong hierarchy
of masses, when one mass dominates over the magnitudes of
scalars while the second mass remains small or even vanishes, has
shown the quadratic decoupling of quantum corrections in such an
IR limit. The contribution of the large-mass scalar reduces to the
one to cosmological constant term. This output is natural if we
remember the uniqueness of effective action and the known results
for the IR decoupling in the kinetic sector of a scalar field

[
24
]
.

From the viewpoint of applications, our results mean that the
quantum effects of two scalar fields in the assisted models of
inflation (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
)
boil down to the ones of the single lightest scalar in the course
of a fast expansion of the Universe, when the typical energy
becomes smaller, and the large-mass field decouples.

Let us discuss the possible extensions of the results of this work.
It is technically possible to construct the derivative extension
in the effective action of scalars owing to the fermion loops

[
34
]
. Using the method presented above, one can
extend the existing expansions by including the curvature terms,
similar to Eq. (
24
).
The same concerns also the contributions of
gauge fields. In both cases the kinetic scalar term is UV divergent
and the coefficient
Z
in (
1
) is subject of
renormalization procedure, however the power counting tells us
that this does not concern curvature-dependent part. We hope to
report on this calculation in future work.
In view of the possible applications to inflation, it looks
interesting to include also graviton and mixed graviton-scalar
loops. However, this is a more complicated problem, because
quantum gravity based on GR is not renormalizable and because
such graviton contributions have high degree of ambiguity
(see the discussion and further references, e.g., in
[
14
]
).
Anyway, this is an attractive idea which may be put into
practise in the effective quantum gravity framework.

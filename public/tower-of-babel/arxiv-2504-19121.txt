Anomalous phonon magnetic moments
Swati Chaudhary, Carl P. Romao, Dominik M. Juraschek

Abstract
Circularly polarized phonons conventionally carry an angular momentum and a magnetic moment arising from the circular motions of the atoms around their equilibrium positions in a crystal. Here, we present three cases of anomalous magnetic moments produced by axial phonons that cannot be described in the conventional framework. The three cases include rotationless axial phonons , in which the atoms carry no real, but only pseudo, angular momentum and magnetic moments, diverging gyromagnetic ratios , in which a finite phonon magnetic moment is produced despite nearly vanishing phonon angular momentum, and noncollinear phonon magnetic moments , in which the angular momentum and magnetic moment of the axial phonon are not parallel. Our results shine light on the origin and nature of phonon magnetic moments and open a route towards phononic hidden orders and noncollinear phonomagnetism.



I
Introduction

Circularly polarized phonons carry angular momentum that can be exchanged with other particles, such as photons or electrons, on fundamental timescales
Zhu et al. 2018
;
Tauchert et al. 2022
;
Davies et al. 2024
. These lattice vibrations, sometimes appearing in the form of chiral phonons
Ueda et al. 2023
;
Ishito et al. 2023
;
Ueda et al. 2025
, can produce magnetic moments and hence represent a fundamental property of crystalline solids in addition to electronic angular momentum
Juraschek and Spaldin 2019
;
Ren et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Chaudhary et al. 2024
.

The phonon magnetic moment,
\mathbf{m}^{ph}
, and angular momentum,
\textbf{l}^{ph}
, have so-far been considered to be collinear and proportional to each other by a factor called the phonon gyromagnetic ratio,
\mathbf{m}^{ph}=\gamma^{ph}\mathbf{l}^{ph}

Juraschek and Spaldin 2019
.

The origin of angular momentum of a (quasi)particle is not always directly linked to circular motion, as is commonly exemplified by the electron spin. An alternative definition of the angular momentum can be formulated from the transformation of wavefunctions under rotation
Zhang and Niu 2015a
;
Streib 2021
. In this case, the angular momentum is related to the eigenvalues of the rotation operator. For example, the variation of the phase of the electromagnetic field of light with an azimuthal angle gives rise to a twisted wavefront and therefore orbital angular momentum even for linear polarization
Allen et al. 1992
. The same principle applies for lattice vibrations in solids producing macroscopic vortices
Gao et al. 2023
. On the length scale of the unit cell, phonon angular momentum can be obtained by acting the
n
-fold rotation operator on the phonon displacement vector
Zhang and Niu 2015a
;
Wang et al. 2024
. This form is known as phonon pseudo angular momentum (PAM) and stems from a phase difference between the motion of different atoms belonging to the same unit cell (spin PAM) or different unit cells (orbital PAM)
Zhang and Niu 2015a
. The prototypical example of phonons carrying PAM is found in the
K
/
K^{\prime}
valleys of hexagonal lattices
Zhang and Niu 2015a
;
Zhu et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2019a
;
Chen et al. 2019b
;
Yao and Murakami 2024
;
Ptok et al. 2021
. The literature to date has generally only considered angular momentum arising from the circular motion of atoms.

In this work, we demonstrate three cases that defy the conventional picture of the phonon magnetic moment: firstly, rotationless axial phonons, which possess only pseudo, but no real angular momentum, and which generate purely non-Maxwellian phonon magnetic moments; secondly, phonons with divergent gyromagnetic ratios, in which a finite magnetic moment is generated despite vanishing angular momentum; and thirdly, noncollinear phonon magnetic moments that are not parallel to the phonon angular momentum. Because our analysis will involve both chiral and achiral phonons carrying angular momentum, we will in the following use the neutral term “axial phonons” to indicate they can be represented by the axial vector
\mathbf{l}
.

where

Real phonon angular momentum has been associated with the generation of magnetic fields

;
Geilhufe et al. 2021
;
Shabala and Geilhufe 2024
;
Luo et al. 2023
;
Nova et al. 2017

Juraschek et al. 2022

;

In the following, we will demonstrate three cases of anomalous phonon behavior that goes beyond the contemporary understanding of phonon magnetism.

III
Rotationless axial phonons

PAM arises from the action of the rotational operator
C_{n}
and makes no assumptions about the circularity of the atomic motion. Hence, also purely linear motions of the atoms along the rotation axis should lead to PAM as long as there is a phase difference between them. We will show in the following that these
rotationless axial phonons
exist in hexagonal crystal structures and produce effective magnetic moments purely from PAM, despite vanishing
\mathbf{l}^{ph}_{\nu\mathbf{q}}
and
\mathbf{m}^{ph}_{\nu\mathbf{q}}
according to Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
). These phonons live at reciprocal lattice points possessing three-fold rotational symmetry, for example at points along the
\Gamma-\mathrm{A}
line or at the
K/K^{\prime}
points. We consider phonons in three types of hexagonal structures, including valley phonons in honeycomb lattices, valley and zone-center phonons in kagome lattices, as well as zone-center phonons for the specific case of cerium trichloride. As we will be considering phonon modes of specific branches and wavevectors, we will drop the indices
\nu
and
\mathbf{q}
in the following to simplify notation.

III.1
Rotationless axial phonons in honeycomb lattices

Valley axial phonons exhibiting circular atomic motions in the hexagonal plane of honeycomb lattices have been studied extensively in the recent literature
Zhang and Niu 2015a
;
Zhu et al. 2018
.
The three-fold rotation around the center of the hexagon leads to orbital PAM that is opposite in sign for different sublattices,
A
and
B
,
l^{p,o}(A)=-l^{p,o}(B)=\pm 1
for all phonon branches at
\mathbf{q}=K/K^{\prime}
. Additionally,
K/K^{\prime}
-valley phonons with in-plane circular motion also carry a spin PAM of
l^{p,s}=\pm 1
, resulting in a total PAM of
l^{p}=l^{p,s}(A)+l^{p,o}(A)=l^{p,s}(B)+l^{p,o}(B)

l^{p}=\pm 1,0
modulo 3 which constrains the in-plane valley phonons to have only one sublattice moving or to exhibit opposite circular motion for the
A
and
B
sublattices. On the other hand, phonon modes with linear out-of-plane motion cannot carry any spin PAM, implying a total PAM of
l^{p}=l^{p,o}=\pm 1
. Given that
l^{p,o}(A)=-l^{p,o}(B)=\pm 1
, any nondegenerate valley phonon with linear out-of-plane motion must have only one sublattice moving and the total PAM would be
\pm 1
. This necessitates that the two sublattices must be inequivalent which requires broken inversion symmetry and can be realized in prototypical hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN).
We investigate out-of-plane nondegenerate
K/K^{\prime}

1

\pm 2\pi/3

1

\pm 1
arising from the intercell phase difference.

IV
Divergent phonon gyromagnetic ratios

Axial phonons can involve circular motion of some, all, or (as we have shown) none of the atoms
Romao et al. 2023
. If ionic sublattices revolve in opposite directions, phonons without net angular momentum can also acquire phonon magnetism if . For example, in a crystal with one cation (
+
) and one anion (
-
) per unit cell, the effective charges are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign,
Z^{*}_{+}=-Z^{*}_{-}
. For a phonon mode with
\mathbf{l}^{ph}_{+}=-\mathbf{l}^{ph}_{-}
, the phonon angular momentum in Eq. (
1
) vanishes, whereas the phonon magnetic moment in Eq. (
2
) remains nonzero. Accordingly, the phonon gyromagnetic ratio, defined as
\gamma^{ph}=\frac{|\mathbf{m}^{ph}|}{|\mathbf{l}^{ph}|}
, diverges. This idealistic case is approximated in monolayer hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN). Axial phonons have previously been studied in h-BN in its pure form
Rostami and Cappelluti 2022
and in heterostructures
Li et al. 2024
;
Gao et al. 2018
, where it shows a similar phonon band structure to the noncentrosymmetric transition metal dichalcogenides
Zhang and Niu 2015a
. Here, we show the existence of near-diverging gyromagnetic ratios in Fig.
4

Gonze et al. 2020
;
Perdew et al. 1996
;
Grimme et al. 2010
;
Chaudhary et al. 2025
.

As shown in Fig.

V
Noncollinear phonomagnetism

Divergent phonon gyromagnetic ratios are a special case of the more general phenomenon of noncollinear phonon magnetic moments. This phenomenon occurs when the atomic phonon angular momentum vectors,
\mathbf{l}_{\alpha}^{ph}
, of the different ionic sublattices are not aligned, as we show is generally the case for 3D crystals at arbitrary points in reciprocal space. This leads to a noncollinear local arrangement of the magnetic moments arising from circular atomic motions,
\mathbf{m}^{ph}_{\alpha}
, and a difference in spatial orientation between the phonon angular momentum and phonon magnetic moment vectors. Accordingly, both quantities have to be connected by a tensorial phonon gyromagnetic ratio,
\mathbf{m}^{ph}=\underline{\underline{\gamma^{ph}}}\mathbf{l}^{ph}
, where
\mathbf{m}^{ph}\nparallel\mathbf{l}^{ph}
.

We show the example of noncollinear phonon magnetic moments for noncentrosymmetric gallium arsenide (GaAs) in Fig.
5
, using phononic properties computed from first principles
Jain et al. 2013
;
Persson 2014
. The optical phonons between the
L
and
W

z
axis of the crystal, while those of the As ions are oriented in the
xy
plane (Fig.
5
a-c). In combination with the differing gyromagnetic ratios of the two types of ion, a phonon magnetic moment emerges that is nearly orthogonal to the phonon angular momentum at the
L
point and along most of the
L-W
direction (Fig.
5
d).

VI
Conclusion

The rotationless axial phonons presented here provide new insights into the origin of the phonon magnetic moment and electron-phonon interactions. Our work challenges the conventional understanding that circular atomic motion is necessary to create spin PAM and phonon magnetic moments and shows that both can be achieved with only linear atomic motion. The large Zeeman splitting and effective magnetic moment of the rotationless
E_{2u}
mode in CeCl
3
suggests that coherent driving with ultrashort mid-infrared pulses will generate large effective magnetic fields in a similar way that phonon modes involving circular atomic motion do
Juraschek and Narang 2020
;
Juraschek et al. 2022
.

The presence of phonons with noncollinear phonon magnetism in the simple, highly symmetric materials we have studied here suggests that it is broadly present also in noncentrosymmetric crystals. This phenomenon could be considered as a hidden phononic order
Aeppli et al. 2020
, as the phonons in Fig.
5
clearly lead to a magnetization field with moments of higher order than the dipole. The interactions between chiral phonons and spin-polarized electrons could be affected by such higher-order multipoles, especially in cases where the magnetic ions have some corresponding multipolar order
Aeppli et al. 2020
;
Verbeek et al. 2023
. Magnetic multipoles associated with phonons could potentially be detected experimentally in X-ray or neutron scattering experiments
Urru et al. 2023
;
Soh et al. 2024
.

The decoupling of phonon angular momentum and phonon magnetic moments has further implications for transport properties, as the flow of both quantities is affected by external symmetry-breaking fields, leading to phonon Hall effects
Zhang and Niu 2015b
;
Park and Yang 2020
and the chirality-induced phonon angular momentum selectivity effect
Kim et al. 2023
;
Ohe et al. 2024
. Noncollinear phonon magnetism could give rise to unexpected directions or magnitudes of the currents generated by these effects.

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}g(a^{\dagger}+a)\Gamma^{a}_{ij}=ig(b^{\dagger}+b)(c_{3}^{\dagger}c_{1}-c_{1}^{\dagger}c_{3})+ig(b^{\dagger}+b)(c_{4}^{\dagger}c_{2}-c_{4}^{\dagger}c_{2}).

(S7)

The new phonon Green’s function after including these interactions is:

\mathbf{D}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}{2\omega_{0}}-\tilde{g}^{2}\left(\frac{f_{1}\Delta_{1}}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{1}^{2}}+\frac{f_{2}\Delta_{2}}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{2}^{2}}\right)&i\tilde{g}^{2}\left(-\frac{f_{1}\omega}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{1}^{2}}+\frac{f_{2}\omega}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{2}^{2}}\right)\\
-i\tilde{g}^{2}\left(-\frac{f_{1}\omega}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{1}^{2}}+\frac{f_{2}\omega}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{2}^{2}}\right)&\frac{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}{2\omega_{0}}-\tilde{g}^{2}\left(\frac{f_{1}\Delta_{1}}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{1}^{2}}+\frac{f_{2}\Delta_{2}}{\omega^{2}-\Delta_{2}^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix},

(S8)

where
\tilde{g}^{2}=2g^{2}
,
\Delta_{1}=\varepsilon_{31}
,
\Delta_{2}=\varepsilon_{42}
,
f_{i}
is the occupation of
i^{th}
electronic band and we assume the excited state to be unoccupied,
f_{3}=f_{4}=0
. The modified energies can then be obtained by solving
\mathrm{Det}(\mathbf{D}^{-1})=0
.

When a magnetic field
\mathbf{B}=B\,\hat{z}
is applied, electronic transition energies are modified as follows:

\Delta_{1}=\Delta-\gamma B,~~\Delta_{2}=\Delta+\gamma B,

(S9)

where
\gamma=\mu^{el}_{ex}-\mu^{el}_{gs}
depends on the magnetic moment of the ground- and excited-state doublets. Lifting the degeneracy of the ground-state doublet leads to asymmetric populations of the ground-state energy levels,
f_{12}\neq 0
.
Accordingly, the secular equation,
\mathrm{Det}(\mathbf{D}^{-1}(\omega))=0
for
\mathbf{D}^{-1}
given by Eq. (
S8
) becomes:

\displaystyle(\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2})(\omega^{2}-\Delta^{2})-2\tilde{g}^{2}f_{0}\omega_{0}\Delta+2\omega\left(B\gamma(\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}f_{21}\right)+\gamma B\left(\gamma B(\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+2\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}f_{21}\right)=0,

(S10)

\displaystyle(\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2})(\omega^{2}-\Delta^{2})-2\tilde{g}^{2}f_{0}\omega_{0}\Delta-2\omega\left(B\gamma(\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}f_{21}\right)+\gamma B\left(\gamma B(\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+2\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}f_{21}\right)=0.

(S11)

These two equations are not equivalent and there is a term linear in
\omega
that indicates a frequency splitting of phonon and electronic excitations. Given that the orbit-lattice coupling is weak and the electronic excitations are off-resonant from phonons, we can assume that phonon energies are modified only slightly and have the form
w_{ph}^{\pm}=\Omega_{ph}\left(1\mp\eta\right)
which gives:

\Omega_{ph}\eta=\frac{\gamma B(\Omega_{+}^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}f_{21}}{\Omega_{ph}^{2}-\Omega_{{el}}^{2}+\gamma^{2}B^{2}}=\frac{\gamma B(\Omega_{+}^{2}-\omega^{2}_{0})+\tilde{g}^{2}\omega_{0}\tanh\left(\frac{\mu^{el}_{\mathrm{gs}}B}{k_{B}T}\right)}{\sqrt{\left(\omega_{0}^{2}-\Delta^{2}\right)^{2}+8\tilde{g}^{2}f_{0}\omega_{0}\Delta}+\gamma^{2}B^{2}}.

(S12)

where
f_{21}
was replaced by
-\tanh\left(\frac{\mu^{el}_{gs}B}{k_{B}T}\right)
with
\mu^{el}_{gs}
representing the magnetic moment of ground state manifold as the system is paramagnetic. For the off-resonant case, we can assume
|\Delta-\omega_{0}|\gg\gamma B
and therefore neglect the linear
B
term in the numerator and the quadratic one in the denominator. The off-resonant case is a reasonable assumption, as
\gamma B\sim 0.5
meV in strong magnetic fields of
B=10
T, whereas often
|\Delta-\omega_{0}|>10
meV. As a result, the splitting of the phonon frequencies can be written as

\frac{\omega_{ph}^{+}-\omega_{ph}^{-}}{\omega_{ph}(B=0)}\approx\frac{2\tilde{g}^{2}}{\sqrt{\left(\omega_{0}^{2}-\Delta^{2}\right)^{2}+8\tilde{g}^{2}f_{0}\omega_{0}\Delta}}\tanh\left(\frac{\mu^{el}_{gs}B}{k_{B}T}\right),

(S13)

which is the main expression used to evaluate the splitting shown in Fig.

The phonon energies and eigenvectors of monolayer h-BN were obtained using density functional perturbation theory with the Abinit software package (v. 10)
Gonze et al. 2020
. Norm-conserving pseudopotentials were used as recieved from the Abinit library. The PBE GGA exchange–correlation functional
Perdew et al. 1996

-points were chosen following convergence studies. The structure was relaxed to an internal pressure of
-7
MPa, and an
8\times 8\times 1
grid of
q
-points was then used for the phonon calculations. Computational data are publicly available from Ref.
Chaudhary et al. 2025
. DFT-calculated phonon energies and eigenvectors of GaAs were obtained from the Materials Project database
Jain et al. 2013
;
Persson 2014
. The phonon angular momentum was obtained from the phonon eigenvectors following Eq.
1
; the phonon magnetic moments were obtained following Eq.

On medial Latin quandles and affine modules
Lực Ta

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the category of Latin (resp. commutative) medial quandles is equivalent to the category of affine modules over a certain Laurent polynomial ring (resp. the dyadic rationals). As applications, we describe free objects in these categories and obtain a structure theorem for finitely generated medial commutative quandles. We also characterize racks whose duals are commutative. Collectively, this solves two open problems of Bardakov and Elhamdadi [ 1 ] .

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Quandles

Recall that a
magma
is a pair
(X,\ast)
where
X
is a set and
\ast\colon X^{2}\to X
is a binary operation. If
(X,\ast)
and
(Y,\cdot)
are magmas, then a
homomorphism
is a function
f\colon X\to Y
such that
f(w\ast x)=f(w)\cdot f(x)
for all
w,x\in X
. For all
x\in X
, define the corresponding
right multiplication map

R_{x}\colon X\to X
by
y\mapsto y\ast x
, and define the
left multiplication map

L_{x}\colon X\to X
by
y\mapsto x\ast y
.

In the following, let
(X,\ast)
be a magma. We consider several important varieties of magmas.

•

We say that
(X,\ast)
is
idempotent
if
x\ast x=x
for all
x\in X
.

•

We say that
(X,\ast)
is a
rack
if, for all
x\in X
, the right-multiplication map
R_{x}
is an automorphism of
X
. In particular,
\ast
distributes over itself on the right.

•

Quandles
are idempotent racks.
Kei
are quandles such that, for all
x\in X
, the right multiplication map
R_{x}
is an involution.

•

Equip
X^{2}
with the product magma structure. We say that
(X,\ast)
is
medial
if
\ast\colon X^{2}\to X
is a magma homomorphism. Several authors prefer the terms
abelian
or
entropic
.

•

We say that
(X,\ast)
is
Latin
or a
left quasigroup
if, for all
x\in X
, the left multiplication map
L_{x}
is a permutation.

•

We say that
(X,\ast)
is
commutative
or
symmetric
if
x\ast y=y\ast x
for all
x,y\in X
; that is,
L_{x}=R_{x}
for all
x\in X
.

The following is well-known and straightforward.

Lemma 2.1
.

All Latin racks are quandles. Moreover, all commutative racks are Latin quandles.

2.1.1.

Two auxiliary results of this paper (Proposition
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
) state that all medial Latin quandles and medial commutative quandles fall into the following two classes of examples.

Example 2.2

(
[
16
]
[
10
]
)
.

Alexander quandles,
also called

form an important class of medial quandles. Given an automorphism
\varphi
of an abelian group

, define the Alexander quandle
\Alex(A,\varphi)\coloneq(A,\ast)
by

(2.1)

x\ast y\coloneq\varphi(x)+(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)(y).

Note that
\Alex(A,\varphi)

\mathrm{id}-\varphi
is an automorphism of

; cf.
[
10

. Actually, every medial Latin quandle is isomorphic to an Alexander quandle of this form; see Proposition

.

Example 2.3
.

Our prototypical examples of commutative quandles generalize the commutative quandles constructed in
[
1
]
*Ex. 5.1; we call these

.
Let

which is commutative.
In particular, let

, let

, and let

be the cyclic group of order

of order

coincides with the one in

Remark 2.4
.

Midpoint quandles are a special class of so-called

(see
[

), which are themselves a special class of Alexander quandles. Indeed, if

is a midpoint quandle, then multiplication by

is an

whose corresponding Alexander quandle is precisely

2.2.
Affine modules

Given a ring
R

\mathsf{AffMod}_{R}
of

over
R
(also called

\mathsf{AffMod}_{R}
are left

1
1

We include the empty set in

The morphisms are sums of

The following is straightforward.

Lemma 2.5
.

The inclusion functor from the category of left

In other words, two nonempty affine

2.2.1.

Given a ring homomorphism

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be a surjective ring homomorphism. Then the restriction of scalars functor
\operatorname{res}\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{S}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{AffMod}_{R}

Remark 2.7
.

Our definition of

For fun, we encourage the reader to verify that the forgetful functor

is an equivalence of categories. As a hint, essential surjectivity is shown as follows. Given a nonempty object

x\oplus y\coloneq 1x+1y-1(0),\qquad r\cdot x\coloneq rx+(1-r)0.

.

Let

be a rack. The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is cocommutative.

(2)

is a cocommutative quandle.

(3)

For all

, the left multiplication map

is an involution.

Proof.

First, note that condition (

(3.1)

x\ast(x\ast y)=y

(

)
\implies
(
2

X
is cocommutative, then Lemma

implies that

is a quandle. That is,

for all

to both sides shows that

is also a quandle.

(

)
\implies
(

is a cocommutative quandle. Given

, let

x\ast(x\ast y)=(z\ast y)\ast(x\ast y)=(z\ast x)\ast y=(y\ast y)\ast y=y,

which verifies (

(

\implies
(

In the last equality, we have used (

4.
Classification of medial Latin quandles

In preparation for Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
, we completely describe medial Latin quandles and medial commutative quandles. The following results were shown in
[
10
]
[
13
]
[
2
]
, sometimes in more esoteric forms; we reformulate these results in terms of quandles. We also provide much shorter proofs using the classical Bruck–Murdoch–Toyoda theorem from quasigroup theory.

4.1.

Recall that a
quasigroup
is a magma such that all multiplication maps
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
are permutations. In particular, all Latin quandles and (by Lemma
2.1
) commutative quandles are quasigroups.

In the medial case, this observation allows us to appeal to the classical Bruck–Murdoch–Toyoda theorem, which states the following: for every medial quasigroup
(Q,\ast)
, there exists an abelian group
A
, a fixed element
z\in A
, and two commuting automorphisms
\varphi
and
\psi
of
A
such that
(Q,\ast)
is isomorphic to the medial quasigroup
(A,\cdot)
defined by

(4.1)

x\cdot y\coloneq\varphi(x)+\psi(y)+z.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
10
]
*Ex. 2.2 and Cor. 3.4)
.

Every medial Latin quandle is isomorphic to an Alexander quandle
\Alex(A,\varphi)
such that the map
\mathrm{id}-\varphi
is an automorphism of
A
.

Proof.

By the above discussion, every medial Latin quandle is isomorphic to a quasigroup of the form
(A,\cdot)
defined by (
4.1
). By assumption,
(A,\cdot)
is idempotent, so taking
x\coloneq 0
and
y\coloneq 0
in (
4.1
) shows that
z=0
. Therefore, idempotence forces
\varphi+\psi=\mathrm{id}
; this recovers (
2.1
). Hence,
(A,\cdot)=\Alex(A,\varphi)
, as desired. Since
\Alex(A,\varphi)
is Latin, Example
2.2
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.2

(
[
13
]
*Prop. 12.7,
[
2
]
)
.

Every medial commutative quandle is isomorphic to a midpoint quandle.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, it suffices to show that commutative Alexander quandle
\Alex(A,\varphi)
is a midpoint quandle. Indeed, commutativity implies that

\varphi(x)=x\ast 0=0\ast x=x-\varphi(x)

for all
x\in A
; that is,
2\varphi=\mathrm{id}
. Since
\varphi
is invertible, it follows that multiplication by
2
is invertible. Hence,
A
is a module over
\mathbb{D}
, and
\varphi
is multiplication by
1/2
, so
\Alex(A,\varphi)=A_{\mathrm{mid}}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The construction of Bauer in
[
2
]
can be viewed as a way to recover the midpoint quandle corresponding to a given medial commutative quandle under Corollary

. Indeed, one can show that the algebraic structures in Bauer’s construction (namely, medial idempotent commutative quasigroups, which Bauer calls

Incidentally, midpoint algebras have certain connections to (affine) modules over

[

. By the above discussion, this suggests medial commutative quandles should also be related to affine modules over
\mathbb{D}
; we formalize this idea using an equivalence of categories in Corollary
5.4
.

5.1.
Medial Latin quandles

Henceforth, let
\mathsf{MLQnd}
denote the category of medial Latin quandles and quandle homomorphisms, and recall that
\mathbb{L}
denotes the ring of integral Laurent polynomials in
t
and
1-t
. We show that
\mathsf{MLQnd}
and
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}
are equivalent.

5.1.1.

As noted in
[
3
]
[
16
]
[
10
]
, the data of a nonempty object
M
in
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}
is equivalent to the data of an abelian group automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(M)
such that the map
\mathrm{id}-\varphi
is invertible.
3
3

3

More explicitly, the correspondence is given by
t^{\pm 1}x\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\pm 1}(x)
and
(1-t)^{\pm 1}x\leftrightarrow(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)^{\pm 1}(x)
for all
x\in M
.
So, define a functor
\Alex\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathsf{MLQnd}
on objects by sending
M
to the induced Alexander quandle
\Alex(M,\varphi)
. Let
\Alex
fix all morphisms as set-theoretic maps. We begin with two auxiliary results.

Proposition 5.1
.

The assignment
\Alex\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathsf{MLQnd}
is a functor.

Proof.

We only have to show that every affine transformation of
\mathbb{L}
-modules
f\colon M\to N
is also a quandle homomorphism
f\colon\Alex(M,\varphi)\to\Alex(N,\psi)
. If
M
is empty, then the claim is trivial. Otherwise,
f
factorizes as
f=T+c
for some
\mathbb{L}
-linear map
T\colon M\to N
and some constant
c\in N
. In particular,
T\circ\varphi=\psi\circ T
, so

\displaystyle f(x\ast y)

\displaystyle=f(\varphi(x-y)+y)

\displaystyle=(T\circ\varphi)(x-y)+T(y)+c

\displaystyle=(\psi\circ T)(x-y)+f(y)

\displaystyle=\psi(f(x)-f(y))+f(y)

\displaystyle=f(x)\ast f(y)

for all
x,y\in M
. Hence,
f
is a quandle homomorphism.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{L}
-modules, Then every
\mathbb{Z}[t]
-linear map
T\colon M\to N
is
\mathbb{L}
-linear.

Proof.

Left to the reader; use the fact that
T
commutes with
t
and
1-t
and the fact that
t
and
1-t
are invertible.
∎

5.1.2.

We prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.3
.

The functor
\Alex\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathsf{MLQnd}
is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

By Propositions
4.1
and
5.1
and the discussion preceding Proposition
5.1
,
\Alex
is an essentially surjective functor. Clearly,
\Alex
is also faithful. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\Alex
is full. To that end, let
f\colon\Alex(M,\varphi)\to\Alex(N,\psi)
be a homomorphism of Latin Alexander quandles, and view
M
and
N
as
\mathbb{L}
-modules as in the discussion preceding Proposition
5.1
.

We have to show that
f
is a morphism in
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}
. Let
c\coloneq f(0)
, and define
T\colon M\to N
by
T\coloneq f-c
. Since
f=T-c
, we only have to show that
T
is
\mathbb{L}
-linear. By Lemma
5.2
, it suffices to show that
T
is a homomorphism of abelian groups such that
T\circ\varphi=\psi\circ T
. Clearly,
T(0)=0
.

Since
f
is a quandle homomorphism, the reader can verify that
T
is also a quandle homomorphism. Equivalently,

(5.1)

T(\varphi(x-y)+y)=\psi(T(x))+(\mathrm{id}-\psi)(T(y))

for all
x,y\in M
. In particular, given
z\in M
, take
(x,y)\coloneq(\varphi^{-1}(z),0)
in (
5.1
) to deduce that
T=\psi\circ T\circ\varphi^{-1}
. Therefore,
T\circ\varphi=\psi\circ T
, as desired.

It remains to show that
T
distributes over addition. To that end, define
g\colon M^{2}\to N
by

g(x,y)\coloneq T(x+y)-T(x)-T(y).

We have to show that
g\equiv 0
. Since
\varphi
is an endomorphism of abelian groups and
T
is a quandle homomorphism, we first compute

\displaystyle T((a\ast b)+(c\ast d))

\displaystyle=T(\varphi(a)+(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)(b)+\varphi(c)+(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)(d))

\displaystyle=T(\varphi(a+c)+(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)(b+d))

\displaystyle=T((a+c)\ast(b+d))

\displaystyle=T(a+c)\ast T(b+d)

for all
a,b,c,d\in M
. Since
T
is a quandle homomorphism and
\psi
is an endomorphism of abelian groups, it follows that

\displaystyle g((a\ast b),(c\ast d))

\displaystyle=T((a\ast b)+(c\ast d))-T(a\ast b)-T(c\ast d)

\displaystyle=[T(a+c)\ast T(b+d)]-[T(a)\ast T(b)]-[T(c)\ast T(d)]

\displaystyle=\psi(T(a+c)-T(a)-T(c))+(\mathrm{id}-\psi)(T(b\ast d)-T(b)-T(d))

\displaystyle=g(a,c)\ast g(b,d)

for all
a,b,c,d\in M
.

In particular, given
x,y\in M
, let

a\coloneq\varphi^{-1}(x),\quad b\coloneq 0,\quad c\coloneq 0,\quad d\coloneq(\mathrm{id}-\varphi)^{-1}(y).

Then
x=a\ast b
and
y=c\ast d
, so the previous calculation yields

g(x,y)=g((a\ast b),(c\ast d))=g(a,c)\ast g(b,d)=g(a,0)\ast g(0,d)=0\ast 0=0

because
g(-,0)\equiv 0
and
g(0,-)\equiv 0
. Hence,
T
is
\mathbb{L}
-linear, so
f
is a morphism in
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}
.
∎

5.2.
Medial commutative quandles

Let
\mathsf{MCQnd}
denote the category of medial commutative quandles and quandle homomorphisms, and recall that
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
denotes the ring of dyadic rationals. Motivated by the study of
\mathsf{MCQnd}
in
[
6
]
, we show that
\mathsf{MCQnd}
and
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}
are equivalent.

Corollary 5.4
.

The functor
\operatorname{mid}\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathsf{MCQnd}
defined by
M\mapsto M_{\mathrm{mid}}
is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, the surjective ring homomorphism

\mathbb{L}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{L}/(2t-1)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{D}

induces a fully faithful embedding
\operatorname{res}\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{L}}
. By Theorem
5.3
and Remark
2.4
, the composite functor
\Alex\circ\operatorname{res}
is an equivalence of categories from
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}
to the category of midpoint quandles and quandle homomorphisms. By Corollary
4.2
, the inclusion functor
\iota
from the latter category into
\mathsf{MCQnd}
is also an equivalence of categories. On the other hand, Remark
2.4
shows that
\operatorname{mid}\colon\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathsf{MCQnd}
is the composite functor

\operatorname{mid}=\iota\circ\Alex\circ\operatorname{res}.

Hence,
\operatorname{mid}
is an equivalence of categories.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

It is interesting to compare Corollary
5.4
with the third part of
[
9
]
*Thm. 5.2. The first half of the latter result can be rephrased to state that the category of modules over
\mathbb{D}
is equivalent to the category
\mathcal{C}
of commutative quandles that are also abelian group objects in the category of quandles. Since all group objects in the category of racks are medial (see
[
9
]
*Lem. 4.3), Corollary
5.4
can be viewed as a basepoint-free or identity-free version of
[
9
]
*Thm. 5.2 in the sense that morphisms in
\mathsf{AffMod}_{\mathbb{D}}
need not preserve identity elements.

The second half of the third part of
[
9
]
*Thm. 5.2 can be rephrased to state that the full subcategory of cocommutative quandles in
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the category of vector spaces over
\mathbb{Z}/3
. This makes for an interesting comparison with Corollary
3.3
; it is possible to show in purely quandle-theoretic terms that the order of every finite commutative kei is a power of
3
(see
[
17
]
*Thm. 5).

5.2.1.

In particular, combining Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
with Lemmas
2.5
and
5.2
yields the following.

Corollary 5.6
.

Two Latin Alexander quandles
\Alex(M,\varphi)
and
\Alex(N,\psi)
are isomorphic if and only if
M
and
N
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{Z}[t]
-modules. In particular, two midpoint quandles are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic as modules over
\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 5.7
.

In 2003, Nelson
[
16
]
*Thm. 2.1 proved that two finite (not necessarily Latin) Alexander quandles
\Alex(M,\varphi)
and
\Alex(N,\psi)
are isomorphic if and only if
(1-t)M
and
(1-t)N
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
-modules. Since multiplication by
1-t
is invertible for Latin Alexander quandles, Corollary
5.6
shows that Nelson’s result still holds if the word “finite” is replaced with “Latin.”

Remark 5.8
.

Corollary 5.5 fails if we drop the assumption that
\Alex(M,\varphi)
and
\Alex(N,\psi)
are Latin; by Example
2.2
, this is equivalent to dropping the assumption that
1-t
is invertible in the ground ring. For example, Nelson
[
16
]
showed that
\mathbb{Z}/(9,t-4)
and
\mathbb{Z}/(9,t-7)
are isomorphic as Alexander quandles but not as
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
-modules. Accordingly, these modules are not
\mathbb{L}
-modules.

6.
Free objects

As an application of Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
, we completely describe free medial Latin quandles and free medial commutative quandles. As another application, we obtain a structure theorem for finitely generated medial commutative quandles. This solves Question 7.3 and resolves Question 7.1 in
[
1
]
.

6.1.
Free affine modules

First, we describe free affine modules over a ring
R
. Since affine modules over
R
form a variety in the sense of universal algebra (see Remark
2.7
), free objects in
\mathsf{AffMod}_{R}
exist and satisfy the usual universal property.

Namely, the free affine
R
-module generated by a set
X
is an object
\FAM_{R}(X)\in\mathsf{AffMod}_{R}
along with an injective map
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\FAM_{R}(X)
satisfying the following universal property: for all affine
R
-modules
M
and all set-theoretic functions
f\colon X\to M
, there exists a unique affine transformation
\tilde{f}\colon\FAM_{R}(X)\to M
such that
\tilde{f}\circ\iota=f
.

6.1.1.

More explicitly, we can construct
\FAM_{R}(X)
as follows. If
X
is empty, then
\FAM_{R}(X)
is also empty. Otherwise, fix a basepoint
z\in X
, and let
\FAM_{R}(X)\coloneq R^{(X\setminus\{z\})}
be the free left
R
-module generated by the set
X\setminus\{z\}
. Define
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\FAM_{R}(X)
by
z\mapsto 0
and
x\mapsto x
for all
x\in X\setminus\{z\}
. The reader can verify that
\FAM_{R}(X)
satisfies the desired universal property with
\tilde{f}\coloneq T+z
, where
T\colon\FAM_{R}(X)\to M
is the unique
R
-linear map obtained by linearly extending the assignment
x\mapsto f(x)-z
for all
x\in X
.

In particular, if
X
is a set of cardinality
1\leq n<\infty
, then
\FAM_{R}(X)\cong R^{n-1}
.

6.2.
Free medial Latin quandles

Since equivalences of categories preserve universal properties, Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
allow us to construct all free objects in
\mathsf{MLQnd}
and
\mathsf{MCQnd}
.

Given a set
X
, we construct the free medial Latin quandle
\FML(X)
and the free medial commutative quandle
\FMC(X)
generated by
X
as follows. If
X
is empty, then these quandles are also empty. Otherwise, fix a basepoint
z\in X
, and consider the associated free affine modules
\FAM_{\mathbb{L}}(X)
and
\FAM_{\mathbb{D}}(X)
. We define

\FML(X)\coloneq\Alex(\FAM_{\mathbb{L}}(X),\varphi),\qquad\FMC(X)\coloneq\FAM_{\mathbb{D}}(X)_{\mathrm{mid}},

where
\varphi
denotes multiplication by
t
. By the above discussion,
\FML(X)
and
\FMC(X)
satisfy the desired universal properties.

In particular, since
\FAM_{R}(X)\cong R^{n-1}
for all sets
X
of cardinality
1\leq n<\infty
, we obtain the following description of nonempty finitely generated free objects in
\mathsf{MLQnd}
and
\mathsf{MCQnd}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a set of cardinality
1\leq n<\infty
. Then the free medial Latin quandle and the free medial commutative quandle generated by
X
are given by

\FML(X)\cong\Alex(\mathbb{L}^{n-1},\varphi),\qquad\FMC(X)\cong(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})_{\mathrm{mid}},

where
\varphi
denotes multiplication by
t
.

6.2.1.

In the following corollary, the
n=2
case provides a positive answer to
[
1
]
*Question 7.3.

Corollary 6.2

(cf.
[
1
]
*Question 7.3)
.

Let
X
be a set of cardinality
1\leq n\leq 3
. Then the free commutative quandle generated by
X
is isomorphic to the free medial commutative quandle
\FMC(X)\cong(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})_{\mathrm{mid}}
generated by
X
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
, it suffices to show that the free commutative quandle generated by
X
is medial. Since
1\leq n\leq 3
, this follows from
[
11
]
*Prop. 3.2, which applies by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

This argument fails if
n\geq 4
due to its reliance on
[
11
]
*Prop. 3.2. In this case, we suspect that the the non-medial commutative quandles
D(1)
and
D(3)
from
[
13
]
are quotients of the free commutative quandle generated by
X
. Since the former quandles are non-medial, this would imply that the latter quandle is also non-medial, thus making the claim fail for
n\geq 4
.

6.3.
Medial commutative rack decomposition

As one last application of Corollary
5.4
, we obtain a structure theorem for finitely generated commutative medial quandles. This completely characterizes quandles for which the first part of
[
1
]
*Question 7.1 has a positive answer.

Recall that
C_{2n+1}=(\mathbb{Z}/(2n+1))_{\mathrm{mid}}
denotes the cyclic midpoint quandle of order
2n+1
.

Theorem 6.4

(cf.
[
1
]
*Question 7.1)
.

Let
X
be a nonempty finitely generated rack. Then
X
is medial and commutative if and only if there exists a rack isomorphism

X\cong(\mathbb{D}^{r})_{\mathrm{mid}}\oplus\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}C_{2m_{i}+1}

for some nonnegative integers
r,n,m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\geq 0
.

In particular, every finite medial commutative rack is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic midpoint quandles
C_{2m_{i}+1}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{D}
is a PID, and all finite modules over
\mathbb{D}
are abelian groups of odd order. Therefore, the claim follows after combining the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID with Lemma
2.1
, Corollary
5.4
, and Proposition
6.1
.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that given a differential graded category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} and ℬ \mathcal{B} a full differential graded subcategory closed under coproducts, there is a canonical recollement DgMod ⁡ ( 𝒞 / ℐ ℬ ) \textstyle{\mathrm{DgMod}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces} DgMod ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) \textstyle{\mathrm{DgMod}(\mathcal{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces} DgMod ⁡ ( ℬ ) \textstyle{\mathrm{DgMod}(\mathcal{B})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces} of differential graded categories, for which we use enriched categories tools. We continue the study of differential graded triangular matrix categories Λ = [ 𝒯 0 M 𝒰 ] \Lambda=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal{T}&0\\ M&\mathcal{U}\end{smallmatrix}\right] as initiated in [ 22 ] . We show that given a recollement between functor dg-categories we can induce a new recollement between differential graded triangular matrix categories, this is a generalization of a result given by Chen and Zheng in [ 5 , Theorem 4.4] .

1.
Introduction

The notion of recollement of triangulated categories was introduced first by Beilinson, Bernstein, and Deligne in
[
3
]
. In the context of abelian categories, recollements were studied by Franjou and Pirashvili in
[
7
]
, motivated by the work of MacPherson-Vilonen in derived category of perverse sheaves (see
[
19
]
). Recollements of triangulated (abelian) categories can be viewed as ”exact sequences” of triangulated (abelian) categories, which describe the middle term by a subcategory and a quotient category. It should be noted that these two recollement situations are now widely used in the study of representation theory and algebraic geometry.

On the other hand, rings of the form
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}T&0\\
M&U\end{smallmatrix}\right]
where
T
and
U
are rings and
M
is a
T,U
-bimodule have appeared often in the study of the representation theory of artin rings and algebras (see for example
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
). Such a rings appear naturally in the study of homomorphic images of hereditary artin algebras.

The differential graded categories (dg-categories) and their dg-modules have played a fundamental role in mathematics for a long time. In the last century, dg-categories were the primary tool to study the matrix problems related to the representation theory of algebras. In addition, the development of their study has led to Drozd’s proof of the tame-wild dichotomy (see, for example:
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
).

In recent decades, the relevance of dg-categories comes from a fundamental result due to B. Keller, which asserts that each compactly generated algebraic triangulated category is equivalent to the derived category of certain small dg-category, see
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
.

K

K
-module is a graded
K
-module
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{n}
, together with a graded
K
-linear morphism
d_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
of degree

d_{M}\circ d_{M}=0

\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
has as objects the dg

-modules; and
given

and

-modules the set of morphisms from
M
to
N

\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
is by definition the following graded

-module:

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}(M,N):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}^{n}(M,N),

where
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}^{n}(M,N)
consists of the graded
K
-linear morphisms
f:M\longrightarrow N
of degree

f(M^{i})\subseteq N^{i+n}
for all

.
Moreover, each space of morphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}(M,N)
has a structure of differential graded
K
-module given by the differential
d:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}(M,N)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}(M,N)
, which is a graded
K
-linear morphism of degree
1
such that
d\circ d=0
and is defined by the rule:

(1)

d(\alpha)=d_{N}\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{M}

where
|?|
denotes the degree, whenever
\alpha
is a homogeneous element
of the set
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{DgMod}(K)}(M,N)
.

Now, if
M
and
N
are dg
K
-modules, the tensor product
M\otimes N:=M\otimes_{K}N
also becomes an object of
\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
where the grading is given by
(M\otimes N)^{n}=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}M^{i}\otimes_{K}N^{j}
and the differential
d_{M\otimes N}:M\otimes N\longrightarrow M\otimes N
by the rule

(2)

d_{M\otimes N}(m\otimes n)=d_{M}(m)\otimes n+(-1)^{|m|}m\otimes d_{N}(n),

for all homogeneous elements
m\in M
and
n\in N
. All throughout this paper, we use the unadorned symbol
\otimes
to denote
\otimes_{K}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A differential graded category or a
dg-category
is a
K
-category
\mathcal{C}
such that:

(a)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)

\in\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
.

(b)

The composition function

\theta_{X,Y,Z}:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)\otimes_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)

is a morphism of degree zero in
\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
which commutes with the differentials.

Example 2.2
.

(a)

A dg-category with only one object can be identified with a dg-algebra, i.e., a graded
K
-algebra with a differential
d
such that it satisfies Leibniz’s rule
d(fg)=d(f)g+(-1)^{|f|}fd(g)

for f, g homogeneous elements. Conversely, each dg-algebra
B
can be viewed as a dg-category with only one object.

(b)

The category
\mathrm{DgMod}(K)
is the main example of a dg-category.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a dg-category, then the
opposite dg-category

\mathcal{A}^{op}
has the same class of objects as
\mathcal{A}
and the differential on morphisms
d:\mathcal{A}^{op}(A,B)=\mathcal{A}(B,A)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}(B,A)=\mathcal{A}^{op}(A,B)
is the same as in
\mathcal{A}
, that is,
d_{\mathcal{A}^{op}(A,B)}(f^{op}):=(d_{\mathcal{A}(B,A)}(f))^{op}
. The composition of morphisms in
\mathcal{A}^{op}
is given as
\beta^{op}\circ\alpha^{op}=(-1)^{|\alpha||\beta|}(\alpha\circ\beta)^{op}
, where we use the superscript

to emphasize that a morphism is viewed in
\mathcal{A}^{op}
.

3.
A triple adjoint of dg-functors

We recall that if

\textstyle{\mathrm{Mod}(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We have the following Definition that is a generalization of restriction of scalars in module theory.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

a dg-functor. We have the induced dg-functor

F_{\ast}:\mathrm{DgMod}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathrm{DgMod}(\mathcal{A})

given as

for all

The goal of all this section is to show that

has a left and right adjoint (see Proposition
3.31
). The left and right adjoints of

are computed as left and right Kan extensions, respectively. So, in the Subsection
3.1
we first recall the notions of ends and coends, then in the Subsection
3.2
we study weighted limits and colimits in the dg-context. In the Subsection

we will give the notion of Kan extensions, and finally in the Subsection

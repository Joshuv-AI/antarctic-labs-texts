Some interpolation inequalities in Lorentz, Morrey and BMO spaces
Hua Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, the author establishes some interpolation results between Lorentz, Morrey and BMO spaces. Let 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and p ≤ r ≤ ∞ p\leq r\leq\infty . It is proved that the space L p , r ​ ( ℝ n ) ∩ BMO ⁡ ( ℝ n ) L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) is continuously embedded into L q ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) for all q q with p < q < ∞ p<q<\infty , where L p , r ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) denotes the classical Lorentz space with indices p p and r r . Moreover, the author establishes the optimal growth rate of this embedding constant as q → ∞ q\to\infty . Based on Morrey spaces, the author introduces a new family of function spaces called Lorentz–Morrey spaces L ​ M p , r ; κ ​ ( ℝ n ) LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) with indices p p , r r and κ \kappa , and then shows that the space L ​ M p , r ; κ ​ ( ℝ n ) ∩ BMO ⁡ ( ℝ n ) LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) is continuously embedded into L q ; κ ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) for all q q with p < q < ∞ p<q<\infty , where 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , p ≤ r ≤ ∞ p\leq r\leq\infty and 0 < κ < 1 0<\kappa<1 . Furthermore, the asymptotically optimal growth order of this embedding constant is also established. As an application of the above interpolation results, some new bilinear estimates in the setting of Lorentz and Lorentz–Morrey spaces are also obtained, which can be used in the study of the global existence and regularity of weak solutions to elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations of the second order.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

In this paper, we are concerned with the theory of real interpolation in Lorentz, Morrey and BMO spaces. We write
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
for the set of natural numbers. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space with the Lebesgue measure
dx
. The Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
|x|
. Throughout this paper, the letter
C
always denotes a positive constant independent of the main parameters involved, but it may be different from line to line. We use the symbol
\mathbf{A}\lesssim\mathbf{B}
to denote that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\mathbf{A}\leq C\cdot\mathbf{B}
. We also use
\mathbf{A}\approx\mathbf{B}
to denote the equivalence of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, that is, there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
independent of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
such that
C_{1}\cdot\mathbf{A}\leq\mathbf{B}\leq C_{2}\cdot\mathbf{A}
. For any given normed spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, with the (quasi-)norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}}
, respectively, the symbol
\mathcal{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
means that the normed space
\mathcal{X}
is continuously embedded into
\mathcal{Y}
, that is to say, for any
f\in\mathcal{X}
, then
f\in\mathcal{Y}
and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}}
with the implicit positive constant independent of
f
. Let
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
and
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the classical Lorentz space with indices
p
and
r
. We shall prove the following interpolation results:

(1)

L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
. Moreover, this embedding constant is shown to be 2 when
p\leq r<\infty
(with the asymptotically constant 2 when
r=\infty
).

(2)

L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
. Moreover, the optimal growth rate of this embedding constant is given, as
q\to\infty
.

Actually, we can prove the second result with
\mathrm{BMO}
replaced by
\mathcal{W}
, where the function space
\mathcal{W}
is defined as the set of all locally integrable functions
f
for which

\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\sup_{t>0}\big[f^{**}(t)-f^{*}(t)\big]<+\infty.

Here
f^{*}
and
f^{**}
denote the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
and the average function of
f^{*}
, respectively. The set
\mathcal{W}
was first introduced by Bennett, DeVore and Sharpley in
[
4
]
(see also Bennett–Sharpley in
[
5
]
, and Kozono–Wadade in
[
14
]
). Moreover, it was also shown in
[
14
]
that the following estimate

\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq C(n)\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}

holds for all
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
C(n)
is a positive constant depending only on the dimension
n
. From this, the desired result follows.

Given a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
E
by
m(E)
and the
characteristic function of
E
by
\mathbf{1}_{E}
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. We introduce a new class of function spaces called Lorentz–Morrey spaces
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We define the functional

\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(B)},

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(B)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{B}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/r},&\mbox{if}~1\leq r<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{s>0}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{B}(s)\Big],&\mbox{if}~r=\infty.\end{cases}

Here
f^{*}_{B}(s)=(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B})^{*}(s)
. The Lorentz–Morrey space
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}<+\infty
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. We shall also show the following interpolation results:

(1)

LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
. Moreover, this embedding constant is equal to 2 when
p\leq r<\infty
(with the asymptotically constant 2 when
r=\infty
).

(2)

LM^{p,r;\kappa}\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
. Moreover, this embedding constant is shown to have the linear growth order as
q\to\infty
.

For any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any given
p\in[1,\infty]
, the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(E)
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
E
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}<+\infty
, where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p},&\mbox{if}~p\in[1,\infty),\\
\underset{x\in E}{\mbox{ess\,sup}}\,|f(x)|,&\mbox{if}~p=\infty.\end{cases}

We also denote by
WL^{p}(E)
(
1\leq p<\infty
) the weak Lebesgue space consisting of all measurable functions
f
on
E
such that

\|f\|_{WL^{p}(E)}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\cdot\Big[m\big(\big\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\Big]^{1/p}<+\infty.

When
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write the norms of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
WL^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
simply by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{WL^{p}}
, respectively. The space of functions with bounded mean oscillation (BMO) plays a crucial role in modern harmonic analysis, see, for example,

[
8
, Chapter 6]
,
[
10
, Chapter 3]
and
[
26
, Chapter 4]
. A locally integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be in
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if for any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{m(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|\,dx<+\infty,

where
f_{B}
denotes the average value of
f
on the ball
B
, that is,

f_{B}:=\frac{1}{m(B)}\int_{B}f(y)\,dy.

The
\mathrm{BMO}
norm of
f
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|\,dx,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The space
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was first introduced by John and Nirenberg in
[
12
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

It is well known that for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset WL^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and this inclusion is strict. Let
\mathcal{F}(x):=|x|^{-n/p}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the usual Lebesgue measure. It is obvious that
\mathcal{F}
is not in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but
\mathcal{F}
is in
WL^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|\mathcal{F}\|_{WL^{p}}=(\nu_{n})^{1/p}
, where
\nu_{n}
is the Lebesgue measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
9
]
).

Remark 1.2
.

It is well known that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and this inclusion is strict. The typical example of a function that is in
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but not in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\mathcal{F}(x):=\log|x|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, it is not difficult to see that if
\delta\geq 0
, then
(\log|x|)^{\delta}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\delta\leq 1
. The space
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
arises naturally as a substitute for the space
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the study of boundedness of integral operators for certain limiting cases. For example, Calderon–Zygmund operators with standard kernels(such as the Hilbert transform on the real line and the Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) are known to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p<\infty
, but not bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p=\infty
. Actually, in this case, the standard conditions on their kernels ensure that they are bounded from
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The Riesz potential operator of order
\alpha
is known to be bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
1<p<q<\infty
and
1/q=1/p-\alpha/n
, but not bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=n/{\alpha}
and
0<\alpha<n
. In this case, it is actually bounded from
L^{n/{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
).

For a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for a measurable function
f
on
E
, let us recall the
distribution function

d_{f;E}(\lambda)
for
\lambda>0
, and
non-increasing rearrangement

f^{*}_{E}(t)
for
t>0
. We define the distribution function of
f
by

d_{f;E}(\lambda):=m\big(\big\{x\in E:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big),\quad\lambda>0.

It is not difficult to see that the distribution function
d_{f;E}(\lambda)
is non-increasing and right continuous on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for
f\in L^{p}(E)
with
1\leq p<\infty
, we obtain via Fubini’s theorem that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}d_{f;E}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\bigg)^{1/p}.

(1.1)

The non-increasing rearrangement of
f
is defined by

f^{*}_{E}(t):=\inf\big\{\lambda>0:d_{f;E}(\lambda)\leq t\big\},\quad t>0.

This definition and the fact that
d_{f;E}
is non-increasing imply that
f^{*}_{E}
is non-increasing and right continuous too. Moreover,

f^{*}_{E}(t)>\lambda\Longleftrightarrow d_{f;E}(\lambda)>t.

From this, it can be shown that the functions
f^{*}
and
f
have the same distribution function(equimeasurable functions), and hence

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{E}(t)\big]^{p}\,dt\bigg)^{1/p},&\mbox{if}~p\in[1,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}f^{*}_{E}(t)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f^{*}_{E}(t),&\mbox{if}~p=\infty.\end{cases}

(1.2)

In particular, when
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
d_{f;E}(\lambda)
and
f^{*}_{E}(t)
simply by
d_{f}(\lambda)
and
f^{*}(t)
, respectively. Accordingly, for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}d_{f}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\bigg)^{1/p}.

(1.3)

Moreover,

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(t)\big]^{p}\,dt\bigg)^{1/p},&\mbox{if}~p\in[1,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}f^{*}(t)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f^{*}(t),&\mbox{if}~p=\infty.\end{cases}

(1.4)

By definition, we also see that

d_{f;E}(\lambda)=d_{f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E}}(\lambda),~\lambda>0,\quad\&\quad f^{*}_{E}(t)=(f\cdot 1_{E})^{*}(t),~t>0.

Denote by
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of all locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We first show that the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, and this embedding constant is equal to 1. In fact, if
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
, then for any
p<q<\infty
, by using (
1.4
), we can deduce that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{q}}&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(t)\big]^{q}dt\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(t)\big]^{p}\cdot\big[f^{*}(t)\big]^{q-p}dt\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(t)\big]^{p}dt\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}\\
&=\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q},\end{split}

as desired. We denote by
f^{**}
the average function of
f^{*}
(the action of the Hardy operator on
f^{*}
), which is defined by

f^{**}(t):=\frac{\,1\,}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau,\quad t>0.

Since
f^{*}
is non-increasing, we see that for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}f^{**}(t)&=\frac{\,1\,}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\geq\frac{\,1\,}{t}\big[f^{*}(t)\cdot t\big]=f^{*}(t),\end{split}

which implies that

f^{**}(t)-f^{*}(t)\geq 0,\quad t>0.

(1.5)

For example: if
f^{*}(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}
, then
f^{**}(t)=\frac{\ln(1+t)}{t}
. It is easy to verify that
f^{**}(t)-f^{*}(t)\geq 0
.
This motivates Bennett, DeVore and Sharpley
[
4
]
to define a new function space
\mathcal{W}
as follows
(see also Bennett and Sharpley
[
5
]
, Kozono and Wadade
[
14
]
):

\mathcal{W}:=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\sup_{t>0}\big[f^{**}(t)-f^{*}(t)\big]<+\infty\Big\}.

In addition, for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
and
t>0
, it follows from Hölder’s inequality and (
1.4
) that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{*}(\tau)\big]^{p}d\tau\bigg)^{1/p}\cdot t^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(\tau)\big]^{p}d\tau\bigg)^{1/p}\cdot t^{1/{p^{\prime}}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}\cdot t^{1/{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Then we have

f^{**}(t)=\frac{\,1\,}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p}},\quad t>0.

(1.6)

Motivated by the results in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
mentioned above, we consider the same problem in the setting of Lorentz spaces. We will show that
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, where
1\leq p<\infty
and
p<r\leq\infty
, and this embedding constant is equal to 2 (see Theorem
3.1
below). Meanwhile, if
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
, then (see (
3.3
) below)

f^{**}(t)\leq\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}},\quad t>0,

where
r^{\prime}=r/{(r-1)}
denotes the Hölder conjugate exponent of
r\in(1,\infty)
, and
r^{\prime}=1
when
r=\infty
. This is just (
1.6
) with
1<p<\infty
by putting
r=p
. Unfortunately, the above key estimate does not hold if
p=1
. By using the above estimate for
f^{**}
, we are able to show that the space
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{W}
is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, where
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
. As a consequence, we have
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subspace of
\mathcal{W}
(see Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
below).

On the other hand, let us now recall the definitions of the classical Morrey space and weak Morrey space. Let
\Omega
be a connected open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(bounded or unbounded). We denote by
\overline{\Omega}
the closure of
\Omega
, and by
\mathrm{diam}\,\Omega
the diameter of
\Omega
. For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, set

\Omega(x_{0},r)=B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega,

where
B(x_{0},r)
denotes the open ball centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r
, that is,

B(x_{0},r):=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\big\}.

For
1\leq p<\infty
and
0\leq\kappa\leq 1
, define

\big\|f\big\|_{M^{p;\kappa}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega},r\in(0,\mathrm{diam}\,\Omega)}\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\Omega(x_{0},r))^{\kappa}}\int_{\Omega(x_{0},r)}|f(y)|^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}.

The Morrey space
M^{p;\kappa}(\Omega)
is defined to be the set of all locally integrable functions
f
on
\Omega
for which
\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}(\Omega)}
is finite. The above space was originally introduced by Morrey to investigate the local behaviour (regularity) of solutions to second order elliptic partial differential equations (see
[
18
]
). In the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the classical Morrey space, which is defined as the set of all locally integrable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfy

\begin{split}\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}&:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa}}\int_{B}|f(y)|^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({B})}<+\infty,\end{split}

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall that the weak Morrey space
WM^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfy

\begin{split}\|f\|_{WM^{p;\kappa}}&:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{\lambda>0}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\lambda\cdot m\big(\big\{y\in B:|f(y)|>\lambda\big\}\big)^{1/p}\\
&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{WL^{p}({B})}<+\infty,\end{split}

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
\lambda>0
.

Remark 1.3
.

The space
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
becomes a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}
. Moreover, for
\kappa=0
and
\kappa=1
, the Morrey spaces
M^{p;0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
M^{p;1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincide (with equality of norms) with the spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively. If
\kappa<0
or
\kappa>1
, then the space
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
contains only the zero function.
It is clear that for
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
,

M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset WM^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.7)

and we have that for any
f\in M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f\|_{WM^{p;\kappa}}\leq\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}},

since for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\|f\|_{WL^{p}(B)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(B)}
as a direct consequence of Chebyshev’s inequality. Moreover, it was proved in
[
11
]
that the weak Morrey space
WM^{p;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into the Morrey space
M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
1\leq q<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa_{*}<1
and
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
, and the size of the embedding constant from weak Morrey spaces into Morrey spaces is specified, see
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
0\leq\kappa<1
. It is well known that the boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
on Banach function spaces plays a key role in harmonic analysis. Within the framework of Morrey spaces, we know that the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
is bounded from
M^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
WM^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
[
6
]
(see also
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
19
, Theorem 1]
), but it is not bounded on
M^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
[
20
]
(see also
[
16
, Proposition 6.1]
and
[
23
, Lemma 3.5]
). This suggests that there exists a function
\mathcal{F}\in WM^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{F}\notin M^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. A constructive proof of this fact can be found in
[
11
]
. Moreover, it can be shown that there exists a function
\mathcal{F}\in WM^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{F}\notin M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
(see, for instance,
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
). That is to say, the inclusion (
1.7
) is strict.

For more information on the theory of Morrey spaces, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
22
]
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Let us now prove that the space
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, and this embedding constant is equal to 1. In fact, if
f\in M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for any
p<q<\infty
and for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by using (
1.2
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\big]^{q}dt\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\big]^{p}\cdot\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\big]^{q-p}dt\bigg)^{1/q}.\\
\end{split}

It is clear that for any fixed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda>0
,

d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)\leq d_{f}(\lambda),

which in turn implies that for all
t>0
,

f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\leq f^{*}(t)\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}f^{*}(t).

Therefore, by using (
1.2
) again, we further obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}&\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\big]^{p}dt\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}\\
&\leq\big(\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.\end{split}

Taking the supremum over all balls
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we conclude that

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq\big(\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.

This is our desired estimate.

The purpose of this paper is to define a class of new Lorentz–Morrey spaces
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Motivated by the above result in
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
we consider the same problem in these Lorentz–Morrey spaces, and prove that
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, where
1\leq p<\infty
,
p<r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Moreover, this embedding constant is equal to 2 (see Theorem
4.1
below). Then we are going to prove that

LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, where
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
(see Theorem
4.2
below).

2.
Notations and definitions

Let us recall the definition of Lorentz spaces and present their basic properties. See
[
9
, Chapter 1]
and
[
24
, Chapter 5]
for more details.

Definition 2.1

(
[
9
,
24
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
. Then the Lorentz space
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with indices
p
and
r
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}<+\infty
, where the functional
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}}
is defined by

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/r},&\mbox{if}~1\leq r<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{s>0}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big],&\mbox{if}~r=\infty.\end{cases}

The index
p
in
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called the
principal index
, and
q
is called the
secondary index
. For
1\leq p<\infty
and
r=p
, by using (
1.4
), we have

\|f\|_{L^{p,p}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]^{p}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/p}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{p}ds\bigg)^{1/p}=\|f\|_{L^{p}},

(2.1)

and hence
L^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, for
1\leq p<\infty
and
r=\infty
, we can show that
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}=\|f\|_{WL^{p}}
, which is based on the following result. Therefore,
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=WL^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
9
,
24
]
)
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on the set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then we have the identity

\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}_{E}(s)=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda d_{f;E}(\lambda)^{1/p}.

In particular, when
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have the identity

\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda d_{f}(\lambda)^{1/p}.

Note that
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
. The next result is well known in harmonic analysis, which shows that for any fixed
p
, the Lorentz spaces
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are increasing with respect to
r
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
9
,
24
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
. Then
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}

with the implicit positive constant independent of
f
. In particular, we have

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for any
p<q<\infty
.

The following important formula tells us that we can compute
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,r}}
in terms of the distribution function, which may be regarded as a generalization of (
1.1
) and (
1.3
).

Proposition 2.4

(
[
9
,
24
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq r<\infty
. For given
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have the identity

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}=\bigg(p\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f}(\lambda)^{1/p}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{1/r}<+\infty.

We are now in a position to state the definition of our new spaces.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Then the Lorentz–Morrey space
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with indices
p,r
and
\kappa
is defined as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfy

\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(B)}<+\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we define

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(B)}=\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{B}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/r},&\mbox{if}~1\leq r<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{s>0}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{B}(s)\Big],&\mbox{if}~r=\infty.\end{cases}

We state and prove some basic properties of such spaces. First of all, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\kappa<1
and
r=p
, it follows directly from (
2.1
) that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{LM^{p,p;\kappa}}&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p}}\\
&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B)}=\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}},\end{split}

and hence
LM^{p,p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\kappa<1
and
r=\infty
, it follows from Proposition
2.2
that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda d_{f;B}(\lambda)^{1/p}\\
&=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|_{WL^{p}(B)}=\|f\|_{WM^{p;\kappa}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have
LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=WM^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since for each fixed ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
1\leq p<\infty
, in view of Lemma
2.3
,

\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}\lesssim\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,q}}

holds whenever
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
. As a consequence, we have the following result, which tells us that for some fixed
p
and
\kappa
, the Lorentz–Morrey spaces
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are increasing with respect to the second index
r
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Then
LM^{p,q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{LM^{p,q;\kappa}}

with the implicit positive constant independent of
f
. In particular, we have

M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=LM^{p,p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{p,q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for any
p<q<\infty
.

Next, based on Proposition
2.4
and the fact that
f^{*}_{B}(s)=(f\cdot 1_{B})^{*}(s)
, we can also compute
\|\cdot\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
in terms of the distribution function.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. For given
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have the identity

\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\bigg(p\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;B}(\lambda)^{1/p}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{1/r}<+\infty.

This identity, together with estimates of the distribution function, leads to the following inclusion relation, which is a substantial improvement and extension of known results in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
and
0<\kappa_{*}<1
. Then for
0<\kappa<1
and
q\leq r<\infty
, we have

LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{q,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

whenever
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
, and

\|f\|_{LM^{q,r;\kappa}}\leq\Big[\frac{pq}{r(p-q)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}}.

In particular, when
r=q
,

WM^{p;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

whenever
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
, and

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq\Big[\frac{p}{p-q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{WM^{p;\kappa_{*}}}.

The proof of Theorem
2.8
will be given in the Appendix.

Remark 2.9
.

In general, for
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, the functional
\|\cdot\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
is not a norm unless
r=p
, since it does not satisfy the triangle inequality. Actually, it is a quasi-norm since

\begin{split}\|(f+g)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}&=\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}+g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}\\
&\leq 2\big(\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}+\|g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{p,r}}\big),\end{split}

it is easy to verify that for any given functions
f,g\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f+g\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\leq 2\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}+\|g\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big).

However, when
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, if we replace
f^{*}
in the definition of
\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
with
f^{**}
, we get a quantity which is equivalent to
\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
. To be more precise, define the quantity
\|f\|^{\ast}_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
by

\|f\|^{\ast}_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{m(B)^{\kappa/p}}\|f\|^{\ast}_{L^{p,r}(B)},

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\|f\|^{\ast}_{L^{p,r}(B)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{**}_{B}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/r},&\mbox{if}~1\leq r<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{s>0}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{**}_{B}(s)\Big],&\mbox{if}~r=\infty.\end{cases}

We can show that the functional
\|\cdot\|^{\ast}_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
satisfies the triangle inequality, where
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
, and hence it is indeed a norm. In this situation,
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
becomes a Banach space under the norm
\|\cdot\|^{\ast}_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}
. Moreover,

\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\leq\|f\|^{\ast}_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}},

when
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. The proof is based on the corresponding estimate on the Lorentz spaces.

3.1.
Main results

The main results of this section are stated as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and

. Then the following statements hold.

q
with

, and the interpolation inequality

holds for all

.

(2)
If
r=\infty
and
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
q
with
p<q<\infty
, we have
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the interpolation inequality

.

.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

and

. If

and

, then for every

with

(or if

. Here

.

3.2
and Lemma

3.2.
Proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
and
p<r\leq\infty
. For any
p<q<\infty
, in view of (
1.4
), we have

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{q}}&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&:=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t),\end{split}

where
t
is a positive constant to be determined later. Recall that for any
s>0
,

f^{*}(s)\leq\sup_{s>0}f^{*}(s)=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(3.1)

Consequently,

I_{1}(t)\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q}.

(1) When
r<\infty
and
p<r<q<\infty
, in this case, it follows directly from (
3.1
) that

\begin{split}I_{2}(t)&=\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{r/p-1}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{r}\cdot\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q-r}\frac{1}{s^{r/p-1}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used the facts that
q-r>0
and
r/p-1>0
. Furthermore, by the definition of
L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we obtain

\begin{split}I_{2}(t)&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}\\
&=\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{r/q}\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}.\end{split}

We now choose
t>0
such that

\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{r/q}\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q},

that is,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)^{p}.

Therefore,

\|f\|_{L^{q}}\leq 2\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.

(2) When
r=\infty
and
p<q<\infty
, in this case, it follows from the definition of
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the fact
q/p>1
that

\begin{split}I_{2}(t)&=\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\Big[\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]\cdot\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{q/p-1}\Big)^{1/q}t^{1/q-1/p}.\end{split}

We may choose
t>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q-1/p}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q},

that is,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)^{p}.

Therefore, in view of (
2.3
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{q}}&\leq\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}+1\Big]\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}\\
&\leq\Big[2^{1-1/q}\Big(\frac{q}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}\Big]\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, it is easy to verify that

\lim_{q\to\infty}2^{1-1/q}\Big(\frac{q}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}=2,

(3.2)

that is to say
2^{1-1/q}\big(\frac{q}{q-p}\big)^{1/q}=\mathcal{O}(2)
, as
q\to\infty
. Theorem
3.1
tells us that the embedding constant for the case
p<r<\infty
is 2 and the asymptotically embedding constant for the case
p<r=\infty
is also 2.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{W}
with
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
, and let
p^{\prime}
be the exponent conjugate to
p
. First we claim that for any
t>0
,

f^{**}(t)\leq\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}.

(3.3)

Here
r^{\prime}=1
when
r=\infty
.
In fact, we first suppose
p\leq r<\infty
. For any
t>0
, by using Hölder’s inequality with exponent
r
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau&=\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{1/p-1/r}f^{*}(\tau)\cdot\tau^{1/r-1/p}\,d\tau\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\tau^{1/p}f^{*}(\tau)\Big]^{r}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\bigg)^{1/r}\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}}d\tau\bigg)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}}d\tau\bigg)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Recall that
p>1
. A simple computation leads to that

(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}+1>0.

Then we have

\begin{split}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}}\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}&=\Big[\frac{1}{(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}+1}\cdot t^{(1/r-1/p)r^{\prime}+1}\Big]^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\\
&=\Big(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big(\frac{1}{1-1/p}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\cdot t^{1-1/p}.\end{split}

Hence, for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}f^{**}(t)&=\frac{\,1\,}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\leq\frac{\,1\,}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\big(p^{\prime}\big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\cdot t^{1-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\\
&=\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}},\end{split}

as desired. Now we suppose
r=\infty
. In this case, it is easy to verify that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau&=\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{1/p}f^{*}(\tau)\cdot\tau^{-1/p}\,{d\tau}\\
&\leq\Big[\sup_{\tau>0}\tau^{1/p}f^{*}(\tau)\Big]\cdot\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-1/p}\,{d\tau}\\
&=\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot t^{1-1/p},\end{split}

where in the last step we have used the fact that
p>1
. Therefore, for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}f^{**}(t)&=\frac{\,1\,}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\leq p^{\prime}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}.\end{split}

This proves (
3.3
). It should be pointed out that inequality (
3.3
) fails when
p=1
. On the other hand, since

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(f^{**}(\tau)\big)&=-\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{*}(\eta)\,d\eta+\frac{f^{*}(\tau)}{\tau}\\
&=\frac{f^{*}(\tau)-1/{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{*}(\eta)\,d\eta}{\tau}\\
&=\frac{f^{*}(\tau)-f^{**}(\tau)}{\tau},\end{split}

(3.4)

so we have

-\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(f^{**}(\tau)\big)=\frac{f^{**}(\tau)-f^{*}(\tau)}{\tau}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}}{\tau},

for given
f\in\mathcal{W}
. From this, it follows that for any
0<s<t
,

\begin{split}f^{**}(s)-f^{**}(t)&=\int_{s}^{t}-\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(f^{**}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau\\
&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}}{\tau}\,d\tau\\
&=\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\big[\log t-\log s\big]=\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,},\end{split}

which in turn implies that

f^{**}(s)\leq f^{**}(t)+\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,},

(3.5)

whenever
0<s<t
. For any
1<q<\infty
, in view of (
1.4
), we can write

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{q}}&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&:=J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t),\end{split}

where
t
is a positive constant to be fixed below. Let us estimate the first term
J_{1}(t)
. By using (
1.5
), (
3.5
) and Minkowski’s inequality, we can deduce that

\begin{split}J_{1}(t)&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{**}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{**}(t)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,}\Big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=t^{1/q}\cdot f^{**}(t)+\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\log\frac{t}{\,s\,}\Big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}.\\
\end{split}

Recall that the Stirling formula

\Gamma(q+1)\approx\sqrt{2\pi}q^{q+\frac{\,1\,}{2}}\exp(-q)=\sqrt{2\pi q}\Big(\frac{q}{\,e\,}\Big)^{q}

holds as
q\to\infty
. Hence, we find that as
q\to\infty
,

\Gamma(q+1)^{1/q}=\mathcal{O}(q),

(3.6)

that is,
\Gamma(q+1)^{1/q}\leq C\cdot q
, when
q
is large. Here
C>0
is an absolute constant independent of
q
. This fact, together with (
2.2
) and (
3.3
), gives us that

\begin{split}J_{1}(t)&\leq C\cdot q\Big[t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot t^{1/q}\Big],\end{split}

where
C>0
is independent of
f
and
q>1
. We now estimate the second term
J_{2}(t)
.

(1)
When
r<\infty
and
p\leq r<q<\infty
, since
f^{*}
is non-increasing and
r/p-1\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}J_{2}(t)&=\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p-1/r}f^{*}(s)\big]^{r}\cdot f^{*}(s)^{q-r}s^{1-r/p}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p-1/r}f^{*}(s)\big]^{r}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\Big[f^{*}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\Big[f^{*}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}.\end{split}

Moreover, by (
1.5
), (
3.3
) and the fact that
q-r>0
, we thus obtain

\begin{split}J_{2}(t)&\leq\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{r/q}\cdot\Big[f^{**}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}\\
&\leq\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{r/q}\cdot\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{1-r/q}\\
&=\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}.\end{split}

(2)
When
r=\infty
and
p<q<\infty
, in this case, we have

\begin{split}J_{2}(t)&=\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\big]^{q}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\Big[\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)\Big]\cdot\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}},\end{split}

where the last integral is convergent since
q/p>1
. Notice that
p^{\prime}\geq r^{\prime}
. Thus, for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
,

\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}\leq\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}.

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\lim_{q\to\infty}\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}=1.\Longrightarrow\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}=\mathcal{O}(1)\quad\mbox{as}~q\to\infty.

Summing up the above estimates, we then conclude that

\|f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim q\Big[t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot t^{1/q}\Big]

with the implicit positive constant independent of
q
(may depend on
p
and
r
). We now take
t>0
in this estimate such that

t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot t^{1/q}.

In other words,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}}\right)^{p}.

Therefore, by such choice of
t>0
, we finally obtain

\|f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim q\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}\big)^{1-p/q},

where the implicit positive constant is independent of
q>1
. We are done.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

(1) In particular, when

in Theorem
3.2
,

(3.7)

for all

3.7
) was first established by Kozono–Wadade in

(2) It should be pointed out that the growth order

as

).

(3) Inspired by the works in
[

]

]

.

3.3.
Related bilinear estimates and John–Nirenberg type inequalities

In this section, let us give some applications. Let
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
. A direct consequence of Corollary
3.3
is the following bilinear estimate:

\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim p^{2}\Big[\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big]

(3.11)

holds for two functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where the implicit positive constant is independent of both
p
and the functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
. As a matter of fact, for any
p>1
, Hölder’s inequality and Corollary
3.3
with
q=2p
yield

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p}}&\leq\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2p}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{2p}}\\
&\lesssim p^{2}\Big[\|\mathcal{F}\|^{1/2}_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|^{1/2}_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|^{1/2}_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|^{1/2}_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big]\\
&=p^{2}\Big[\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big]^{1/2}.\end{split}

Moreover, from the elementary inequality
2\sqrt{AB}\leq A+B
,
A,B>0
, it follows that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p}}&\lesssim p^{2}\Big[\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big],\end{split}

as desired. In particular, when
p=r
, then we have

\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(p,n)\Big[\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big]

for two functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
. These bilinear estimates for
1<p<\infty
were proved by Kozono and Taniuchi using the boundedness of bilinear pseudo-differential operators in BMO
(due to Coifman and Meyer), see
[
13
, Lemma 1]
(see also
[
21
]
for the dyadic BMO case, as an extension of Kozono and Taniuchi’s result on the usual BMO). Such kind of inequalities are used to extend some results on uniqueness and regularity of weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations. Similarly, for three functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
, by Hölder’s inequality and Corollary
3.3
with
q=3p
, we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\cdot\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}}\\
&\leq\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{3p}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{3p}}\cdot\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{3p}}\\
&\lesssim p^{3}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}^{1/3}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{2/3}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}^{1/3}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{2/3}\cdot\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p,r}}^{1/3}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{2/3}\Big)\\
&=p^{3}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\\
&\cdot\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big)^{1/3}.\end{split}

Hence, we employ the elementary inequality
3(ABC)^{1/3}\leq A+B+C
to obtain

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\cdot\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}}&\lesssim p^{3}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\\
&+\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big).\end{split}

In general, for the case
3\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
, the following estimate

\begin{split}&\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{F}_{j}\bigg\|_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim p^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathcal{F}_{1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdots\|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{F}_{j}\|_{L^{p,r}}\|\mathcal{F}_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdots\|\mathcal{F}_{K}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\end{split}

holds for all
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{K}\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
.

For
p\geq 1
, we set

\Phi_{p}(t):=\exp(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}=\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!},\quad t>0.

(3.12)

As another application of Corollary
3.3
, we have the following result, which may be regarded as a generalization of the John–Nirenberg inequality to the Lorentz spaces. There exists a positive constant
C({n,p,r,\alpha})
depending on
n,p,r
and
\alpha
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\Phi_{p}\Big(\alpha\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\bigg]\,dx\leq C({n,p,r,\alpha})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{p}

(3.13)

holds for all
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
, provided that
0<\alpha<\alpha_{n}
, where
\alpha_{n}
is a positive constant which depends on
n,r
and
p
.

Since for any
p\leq j<\infty
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx=\big\|f\big\|^{j}_{L^{j}}\leq{C}(n,p,r)^{j}j^{j}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{p}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{j-p}

(3.14)

holds for all
f\in L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
{C}(n,p,r)
is a positive constant depending on
n,r
and
p
, but not on
j
. Therefore, by interchanging the order of integration and summation and using the estimate (
3.14
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\Phi_{p}\Big(\alpha\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\bigg]\,dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}dx\\
&=\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\big[{C}(n,p,r)j\big]^{j}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}\big)^{p}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{j-p}\\
&=\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{[{C}(n,p,r)\cdot\alpha]^{j}\cdot j^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,r}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{p}.\end{split}

We set
\gamma_{j}:=j^{j}/{(j!)}
. Since

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{j+1}}{\gamma_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{\,1\,}{j}\right)^{j}=e,

we know that the above series converges provided
{C}(n,p,r)\cdot\alpha<e^{-1}
. That is, for

0<\alpha<\alpha_{n}:=\big[{C}(n,p,r)\cdot e\big]^{-1},

we see that (
3.13
) holds with
C({n,p,r,\alpha})
is the sum of the above convergent series.
In particular, when
r=p
(in this case, the range of
p
is
[1,\infty)
, as noted in Remark
3.5
, and
\Phi_{p}(t)=\exp(t)-1
if
p=1
), the following inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\alpha\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]dx\leq C({n,\alpha})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)

(3.15)

holds for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see
[
14
, Corollary 2.1]
. A direct computation yields

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\alpha\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}dx\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx.\end{split}

Moreover, for each fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
, it is easy to see that for any
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx&\geq\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx\\
&>\lambda^{j}\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big).\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\alpha\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]\,dx\\
&\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\\
&=\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big).\end{split}

(3.16)

Therefore, it follows from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) that for any
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}&\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\leq C({n,\alpha})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg),\end{split}

which in turn implies that

\begin{split}m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\leq C({n,\alpha})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)\cdot\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]^{-1}.\end{split}

If
\lambda>\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
, then

\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]^{-1}\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\cdot\frac{1}{1-e^{-\alpha}},

and hence

\begin{split}d_{f}(\lambda)&=m\big(\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\\
&\leq C({n,\alpha})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\end{split}

whenever
\lambda>\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
. This interesting estimate is a variant of the John–Nirenberg inequality, which was given by Chen–Zhu in
[
7
, (4)]
.

4.
Interpolation inequalities between Lorentz–Morrey,
L^{\infty}
and BMO spaces

4.1.
Main results

The main results of this section are stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
p<r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)
If
r<\infty
and
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
q
with
r<q<\infty
, we have
f\in M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq 2\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}

holds for all
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(2)
If
r=\infty
and
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
q
with
p<q<\infty
, we have
f\in M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq\Big[2^{1-1/q}\Big(\frac{q}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}\Big]\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}

holds for all
f\in LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. If
p\leq r<\infty
and
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
q
with
r<q<\infty
(or if
f\in LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
q
with

), we have

, and the following interpolation inequality

.

Proofs of Theorems
4.1
and

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
,
p<r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. For any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
p<q<\infty
, by equation (
1.2
), we see that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&:=I^{\prime}_{1}(t)+I^{\prime}_{2}(t),\end{split}

where
t>0
is a constant to be determined later. Recall that for any fixed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\leq f^{*}(s)\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}f^{*}(t)

(4.1)

holds for all
s>0
. Therefore, we have

\begin{split}I^{\prime}_{1}(t)&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q}.\end{split}

(1)
When
r<\infty
and
p<r<q<\infty
, in this case, it follows from (
4.1
) that for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}I^{\prime}_{2}(t)&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{r/p-1}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{r}\cdot\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q-r}\frac{1}{s^{r/p-1}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used the facts that
q-r>0
and
r/p-1>0
. Moreover, by the definition of
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we obtain

\begin{split}I^{\prime}_{2}(t)&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\Big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}\big)^{r/q}\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}.\end{split}

We now choose
t>0
so that

\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}\big)^{r/q}\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-r/q}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{t^{r/p-1}}\Big)^{1/q}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q},

that is,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)^{p}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}&\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q}\\
&=2\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.\end{split}

(2)
When
r=\infty
and
p<q<\infty
, in this case, by the definition of
LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the fact that
q/p>1
, we can deduce that for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}I^{\prime}_{2}(t)&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\Big[\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\Big]\cdot\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}\big)\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{q/p-1}\Big)^{1/q}t^{1/q-1/p}.\end{split}

We can choose
t>0
so that

\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}\cdot t^{1/q-1/p}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q},

that is,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)^{p}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}.

Therefore, we conclude from (
2.3
) that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}&\leq\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}+1\Big]\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot t^{1/q}\\
&=\Big[\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}+1\Big]\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}\\
&\leq\Big[2^{1-1/q}\Big(\frac{q}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}\Big]\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{1-p/q}.\end{split}

By taking the supremum over all balls
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. First of all, from the previous estimate (
3.3
), we know that for any fixed ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})^{**}(t)=f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)&\leq\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{L^{p,r}}\\
&=\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}.\end{split}

(4.2)

Here
r^{\prime}=1
when
r=\infty
. Next we claim that for each fixed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the following inequality holds:

\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}.

(4.3)

In fact, for any ball
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by a simple calculation, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\big|(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})(y)-(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\big|(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})(y)-f_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\big|f_{\mathcal{R}}-(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\big|(f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}})(y)-f_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy=\frac{2}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{B}}\big|f(y)-f_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2}{m(\mathcal{R})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}\big|f(y)-f_{\mathcal{R}}\big|\,dy\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}.\end{split}

This proves (
4.3
) by taking the supremum over all the balls
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This fact, together with Lemma
2.10
, implies that
f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathcal{W}
, and

\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\lesssim\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}.

(4.4)

It then follows from (
3.4
) and (
4.4
) that for any
0<s<t
,

\begin{split}f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)-f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)&=\int_{s}^{t}-\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(\tau)-f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(\tau)}{\tau}\,d\tau\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}{\tau}\,d\tau\\
&=\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\big[\log t-\log s\big]=\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,},\end{split}

which in turn implies that

f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\lesssim f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)+\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,},

(4.5)

whenever
0<s<t
. For any
p<q<\infty
and for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by equation (
1.2
), we can write

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&:=J^{\prime}_{1}(t)+J^{\prime}_{2}(t),\end{split}

where
t>0
is a constant to be fixed below. Let us estimate the first term
J^{\prime}_{1}(t)
. Note that for any
s>0
,

f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\leq f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)<+\infty.

(4.6)

By Minkowski’s inequality, (
4.6
) and (
4.5
), we obtain that for all
t>0
,

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{1}(t)&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big[f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\log\frac{t}{\,s\,}\Big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\cdot t^{1/q}+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\log\frac{t}{\,s\,}\Big]^{q}ds\bigg)^{1/q}.\end{split}

Furthermore, it follows from (
4.2
), (
2.2
) and (
3.6
) that

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{1}(t)&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}+\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\Gamma(q+1)^{1/q}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\cdot q\Big[t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}\Big]\end{split}

with the implicit positive constant independent of
q
. Next we estimate the second term
J^{\prime}_{2}(t)
.

(1)
When
r<\infty
and
p\leq r<q<\infty
, since
f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}
is non-increasing and
r/p-1\geq 0
, we get

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{2}(t)&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p-1/r}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{r}\cdot f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)^{q-r}s^{1-r/p}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p-1/r}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{r}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\Big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\bigg)^{1/q}\cdot\Big[f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}.\end{split}

Moreover, by using (
4.6
), (
4.2
) and the fact that
q-r>0
, we thus obtain

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{2}(t)&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}\big)^{r/q}\cdot\Big[f^{**}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)^{q-r}t^{1-r/p}\Big]^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}\big)^{r/q}\cdot\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\big(\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}\big)^{1-r/q}\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}.\end{split}

(2)
When
r=\infty
and
p<q<\infty
, in this case, we get

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{2}(t)&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\big]^{q}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\Big[\sup_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\Big]\cdot\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{q/p}}ds\bigg)^{1/q}\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})},\end{split}

where the last integral converges since
q/p>1
. As mentioned before, for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
,

\Big[\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big]^{1-r/q}<\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\quad\&\quad\Big(\frac{p}{q-p}\Big)^{1/q}=\mathcal{O}(1)\quad\mbox{as}~q\to\infty.

Consequently, by the definition of
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p\leq r\leq\infty
, we conclude that for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\cdot q\Big[t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathcal{B})}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}\Big]\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\cdot q\Big[t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}+\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}\Big],\end{split}

where the implicit positive constant is independent of
q
. We now take
t>0
in this estimate so that

t^{1/q-1/p}\cdot\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}.

In other words,

t:=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\right)^{p}m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}.

Therefore, by such choice of
t>0
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{B})}&\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/q}}\cdot q\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot t^{1/q}\\
&\leq q\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{1-p/q}.\end{split}

Finally, by taking the supremum over all balls
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we complete the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

(1) Theorem
4.1
tells us that the embedding constant for the case
p<r<\infty
is 2 and the asymptotically embedding constant for the case
p<r=\infty
is also 2 by means of (
3.2
).

(2) In particular, by putting
r=p
in Theorem
4.2
, we immediately have

LM^{p,p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
q
with
p<q<\infty
, where
1<p<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Moreover,

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\lesssim q\cdot\big(\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{1-p/q}

with the implicit positive constant independent of
q
. Actually, the above result is also true for the endpoint case
p=1
, which was recently proved by the author in
[
28
]
using a local version of Calderon–Zygmund decomposition. That is, it can be shown that for any
1\leq p<q<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
,

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq C(n,p)q\cdot\big(\|f\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\big)^{p/q}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{1-p/q}

(4.7)

holds true for all
f\in M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
C(n,p)
is a positive constant depending only on the dimension
n
and
p
. This leads to a natural conjecture that whether
p=1
is included in Theorem
4.2
. Unfortunately, the key estimate (
4.2
) fails when
p=1
. So we need to look for a new method. Furthermore, in analogy with (
3.10
)
(see
[
14
, Theorem 2.2 and Remark (iii)]
), we believe that the (linear) growth order
q
in Theorem
4.2
is best possible as
q\to\infty
.

(3) Inspired by Theorem
3.2
, a natural question appearing here is whether the space
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Theorem
4.2
can be replaced by
LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{W}
. The main difficulty is to show that for each fixed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in\mathcal{W}
, then
f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathcal{W}
, and

\|f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Unfortunately, the key technique in (
4.3
) fails for the space
\mathcal{W}
. In this situation, we need to look for a new method.

4.3.
Related bilinear estimates and John–Nirenberg type inequalities

Let us now proceed with some applications. Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}<1
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
. As a consequence of Theorem
4.2
, we can show that the following bilinear estimate

\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{1}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{2}}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big)

(4.8)

holds for two functions
\mathcal{F}\in LM^{p,r;\kappa_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{G}\in LM^{p,r;\kappa_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\kappa={(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})}/2
and the implicit positive constant is independent of both
p
and the functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
. In fact, by using Hölder’s inequality, we can deduce that for any given ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}|\mathcal{F}(y)\cdot\mathcal{G}(y)|^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}&\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}|\mathcal{F}(y)|^{2p}dy\bigg)^{1/{2p}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}|\mathcal{G}(y)|^{2p}dy\bigg)^{1/{2p}}\\
&\leq\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{1}}}m(\mathcal{B})^{{\kappa_{1}}/{2p}}\Big)\cdot\Big(\|\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{2}}}m(\mathcal{B})^{{\kappa_{2}}/{2p}}\Big)\\
&=\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{1}}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{2}}}\Big)\cdot m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa/p},\end{split}

since
\kappa={(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})}/2
, which in turn implies that the function
\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}
is in
M^{p;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\leq\|\mathcal{F}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{1}}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{2p;\kappa_{2}}}.

(4.9)

Moreover, by using Theorem
4.2
with
q=2p
and the inequality
2\sqrt{AB}\leq A+B
,
A,B>0
, we then have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}&\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{1}}}^{1/2}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{1/2}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{2}}}^{1/2}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{1/2}\Big)\\
&=p^{2}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{1}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{2}}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim p^{2}\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{1}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{2}}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big).\end{split}

This proves (
4.8
). In particular, by taking
r=p
, we get

\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\leq C(p,n)\Big(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{M^{p;\kappa_{1}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{M^{p;\kappa_{2}}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\Big),

(4.10)

for
\mathcal{F}\in M^{p;\kappa_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{G}\in M^{p;\kappa_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, when
1<p<\infty
and
\kappa={(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})}/2
. Within the framework of Lorentz and Lorentz–Morrey spaces, these bilinear estimates (
4.8
), (
4.10
) as well as (
3.11
) may be used in the study of certain problems (such as the global existence, uniqueness and regularity of weak solutions) in PDEs. In general, for the case
3\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
, we can also show that the following estimate

\begin{split}&\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{F}_{j}\bigg\|_{M^{p;\kappa}}\\
\lesssim&p^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathcal{F}_{1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdots\|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\|\mathcal{F}_{j}\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa_{j}}}\|\mathcal{F}_{j+1}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\cdots\|\mathcal{F}_{K}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\end{split}

holds for
\mathcal{F}_{j}\in LM^{p,r;\kappa_{j}}\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
,
p\leq r\leq\infty
,
0<\kappa_{j}<1
and
\kappa=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\kappa_{j}/K
,
j=1,2,\dots,K
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
\Phi_{p}(t)
be the same as before. As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2
, we have the following result, which can be viewed as the John–Nirenberg type inequality in the setting of Lorentz–Morrey spaces.
For each fixed ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{C}({n,p,r,\beta})
depending on
n,p,r
and
\beta
such that

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg[\Phi_{p}\Big(\beta\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\bigg]\,dx\leq\widetilde{C}({n,p,r,\beta})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{p}

(4.11)

holds for all
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa<1
and
p\leq r\leq\infty
, provided that
0<\beta<\beta_{n}
, where
\beta_{n}
is a positive constant which depends on
n,p
and
r
.

Using the same strategy as in (
3.13
), for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
p\leq j<\infty
, we have that

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx=\big\|f\big\|^{j}_{M^{j;\kappa}}\leq\widetilde{C}\big(n,p,r\big)^{j}j^{j}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big)^{p}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{j-p}

(4.12)

holds for all
f\in LM^{p,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\widetilde{C}(n,p,r)
is a positive constant depending on
n,r
and
p
, but not on
j
. Therefore, changing the order of integration and summation and using the estimate (
4.12
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg[\Phi_{p}\Big(\beta\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\bigg]\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}dx\\
&=\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\big[\widetilde{C}(n,p,r)j\big]^{j}\big(\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}\big)^{p}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\big)^{j-p}\\
&=\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}\frac{[\widetilde{C}(n,p,r)\cdot\beta]^{j}\cdot j^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{LM^{p,r;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{p}.\end{split}

As in the proof of (
3.13
), we also know that the above series is convergent provided
\widetilde{C}(n,p,r)\cdot\beta<e^{-1}
. That is, for

0<\beta<\beta_{n}:=\big[\widetilde{C}(n,p,r)\cdot e\big]^{-1},

we see that (
4.11
) holds with
\widetilde{C}({n,p,r,\beta})
is the sum of the above convergent series. In particular, by taking
r=p
(in this special case, recall that the range of
p
is
[1,\infty)
, as mentioned in Remark
4.3
, and
\Phi_{p}(t)=\exp(t)-1
when
p=1
), we have the following inequality

\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\beta\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]\,dx\leq\widetilde{C}({n,\beta})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{M^{1;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg),

(4.13)

whenever
f\in M^{1;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0<\kappa<1
. A simple calculation gives

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\beta\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}dx\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{1}{\|f\|^{j}_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx.\end{split}

Furthermore, for any
\lambda>0
, we see that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx&\geq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}|f(x)|^{j}\,dx\\
&>\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\lambda^{j}\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big).\end{split}

From this, it follows that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bigg[\exp\Big(\beta\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\bigg]\,dx\\
&\geq\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{j}}{j!}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)^{j}\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]\cdot m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big).\end{split}

(4.14)

Hence, we conclude from (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) that for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
\lambda>0
,

\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]\cdot\frac{m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\leq\widetilde{C}({n,\beta})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{M^{1;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg).

This in turn implies that

\frac{m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\leq\widetilde{C}({n,\beta})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{M^{1;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)\cdot\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]^{-1}.

If
\lambda>\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
, then

\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)-1\Big]^{-1}\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big)\cdot\frac{1}{1-e^{-\beta}},

and hence

\frac{m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\kappa}}\leq\widetilde{C}({n,\beta})\bigg(\frac{\|f\|_{M^{1;\kappa}}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\bigg)\exp\Big(-\frac{\beta\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\Big),

whenever
\lambda>\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
. This interesting estimate is completely new(the John–Nirenberg type inequality in the setting of Morrey spaces).

5.
Appendix

In the last section, we give the proof of Theorem
2.8
. Suppose that
f\in LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
1<p<\infty
and
0<\kappa_{*}<1
. We are going to prove that
f
is in
LM^{q,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq q<p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. Moreover,

\|f\|_{LM^{q,r;\kappa}}\leq\Big[\frac{pq}{r(p-q)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}},

(5.1)

whenever
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
. To do this, for any fixed ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can write

\begin{split}&q\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\\
&=q\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}+q\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\\
&:=\mathrm{I+II},\end{split}

where
\Lambda
is a positive constant to be determined later. As for the estimate for the first term
\mathrm{I}
, it is clear that for all
\lambda>0
,

d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)=m\big(\big\{x\in\mathcal{B}:|f(x)|>\lambda\big\}\big)\leq m(\mathcal{B}).

Then we have

\mathrm{I}\leq m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{r}{q}}q\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\lambda^{r-1}d\lambda=m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{r}{q}}\frac{q}{\,r\,}\cdot\Lambda^{r}.

Let us now turn to the estimate of the second term
\mathrm{II}
. By Proposition
2.2
, we know that for any ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}=\sup_{s>0}\Big[s^{1/p}f^{*}_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\Big]=\sup_{\lambda>0}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/p}\Big],

and hence

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&\leq q\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}}{\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\\
&=\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}^{\frac{pr}{q}}\frac{q^{2}}{r(p-q)}\cdot\Lambda^{r-\frac{rp}{q}}.\end{split}

Combining the estimates for
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
leads to

q\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\leq m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{r}{q}}\frac{q}{\,r\,}\cdot\Lambda^{r}+\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}^{\frac{pr}{q}}\frac{q^{2}}{r(p-q)}\cdot\Lambda^{r-\frac{rp}{q}}.

We now choose
\Lambda>0
so that

m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{r}{q}}\cdot\Lambda^{r}=\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}^{\frac{pr}{q}}\cdot\Lambda^{r-\frac{rp}{q}},

namely,

\Lambda:=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}q\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}&\leq\frac{q}{\,r\,}\Big(1+\frac{q}{p-q}\Big)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}^{\frac{pr}{q}}\cdot\Lambda^{r-\frac{rp}{q}}\\
&=\frac{q}{\,r\,}\cdot\frac{p}{p-q}\cdot\frac{\|f\|^{r}_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{r}{p}-\frac{r}{q}}}.\end{split}

This, together with the definition of
LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, implies that

\begin{split}\bigg(q\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\lambda d_{f;\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{1/q}\Big]^{r}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}&\leq\Big[\frac{pq}{r(p-q)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})}}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}}\\
&\leq\Big[\frac{pq}{r(p-q)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}\frac{m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{\kappa_{*}}{p}}}{m(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}}\|f\|_{LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}}.\end{split}

Finally, by taking the supremum over all balls
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and using Proposition
2.7
, we obtain the desired estimate (
5.1
), whenever

\kappa_{*}=1-\frac{(1-\kappa)p}{q}\Longleftrightarrow(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q.

To summarize, we have the following inclusion relations

LM^{p,r;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset LM^{q,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

provided that
1\leq q<p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
,
0<\kappa_{*},\kappa<1
and
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
. Moreover, the size of the embedding constant from
LM^{p,\infty;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
LM^{q,r;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is specified, the difference between some Lorentz–Morrey spaces is clarified. In particular, when
r=q
,

M^{p;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset WM^{p;\kappa_{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M^{q;\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

provided that
1\leq q<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa_{*},\kappa<1
and
(1-\kappa)p=(1-\kappa_{*})q
. Moreover,

\|f\|_{M^{q;\kappa}}\leq\Big[\frac{p}{p-q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{WM^{p;\kappa_{*}}}.

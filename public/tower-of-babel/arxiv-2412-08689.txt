The type IIA Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in AdS 4 × CP 3 \text{AdS}_{4}\times\text{CP}^{3} from ABJM theory
Shai M. Chester, Tobias Hansen, and De-liang Zhong

Abstract
We consider tree level scattering of gravitons in type IIA string theory on A ​ d ​ S 4 × ℂ ​ ℙ 3 AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3} to all orders in α ′ \alpha^{\prime} , which is dual to the stress tensor correlator in U ​ ( N ) k × U ​ ( N ) − k U(N)_{k}\times U(N)_{-k} ABJM theory in the planar large N N limit and to all orders in large λ ∼ N / k \lambda\sim N/k . The small curvature expansion of this correlator, defined via a Borel transform, is given by the flat space Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude plus AdS curvature corrections. We fix curvature corrections by demanding that their resonances are consistent with the superconformal block expansion of the correlator and with a worldsheet ansatz in terms of single-valued multiple polylogarithms. The first correction is fully fixed in this way, and matches independent results from integrability, as well as the R 4 R^{4} correction at finite AdS curvature that was previously fixed using supersymmetric localization. We are also able to fix the second curvature correction by using a few additional assumptions, and find that it also satisfies various non-trivial consistency checks. We use our results to fix the tree level D 4 ​ R 4 D^{4}R^{4} correction at finite AdS curvature, and to give many predictions for future integrability studies.

Introduction

One of the outstanding questions in string theory is how to compute string scattering in the presence of Ramond-Ramond (RR) flux. This includes string theory in AdS, which is famously dual to conformal field theories (CFTs) in one less dimension
Maldacena (1998)

Berkovits (2000)
has not been developed to the point where it can compute string scattering in practice.

Instead, progress has recently been made in
Alday et al. (2022a)
;
Alday et al. (2022b)
;

;

In particular, one can apply this AdS curvature expansion to the superblock expansion of the correlator, which includes operators with large scaling dimensions that are dual to massive string states in the bulk. The second assumption is that the AdS curvature corrections to the correlator are given by a worldsheet ansatz in terms of single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs).

3.3
Degeneracies from flat space

Let us take this opportunity to discuss the degeneracy of operators with the labels
\delta,\ell,n
. We can address this question in the strict flat space limit, by relating the massive spectrum of type II string theory in 10 dimensions to the flat space limit of
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}
. By continuity of the degeneracies the result should then also hold at finite
\nu
.
An analogous analysis was done in
Alday et al. (2023b)
for type IIB string theory in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. In that case, several of the degeneracies were confirmed to hold at finite
\nu
by quantum spectral curve computations in
Gromov et al. (2024)
.
Here we will only discuss the leading odd and even Regge trajectories.

In the present case we have the additional complication of non-maximal supersymmetry. However, the flat space amplitude (
1.5
) respects
\mathcal{N}=8
supersymmetry, as one can check by relating its massive poles to
\mathcal{N}=8
superconformal blocks.
These are given in terms of the
\mathcal{N}=6
superconformal blocks by
Binder et al. (2021)

\displaystyle\text{Long}^{\mathcal{N}=8}_{\Delta,\ell,i}

\displaystyle=\text{Long}_{\Delta,\ell,i}^{1}+\frac{4(\ell-1)^{2}(-\Delta+\ell+1)(\Delta+\ell)}{(2\ell-1)(2\ell+1)(\ell-\Delta)(\Delta+\ell+1)}\text{Long}_{\Delta+1,\ell-1,i}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\Delta(-\Delta+\ell+1)(\Delta+\ell)}{3(\Delta+2)(\ell-\Delta)(\Delta+\ell+1)}\text{Long}_{\Delta+1,\ell,i}^{2}+\frac{4(-\Delta+\ell+1)(\Delta+\ell)}{(\ell-\Delta)(\Delta+\ell+1)}\text{Long}_{\Delta+1,\ell,i}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{4(\ell+1)(\ell+2)(\Delta+\ell)(\Delta+\ell+2)}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)(\Delta+\ell+1)(\Delta+\ell+3)}\text{Long}_{\Delta+1,\ell+1,i}

\displaystyle+\frac{4(\Delta+4)^{2}(-\Delta+\ell+1)(\Delta+\ell)}{(2\Delta+5)(2\Delta+7)(\ell-\Delta)(\Delta+\ell+1)}\text{Long}_{\Delta+2,\ell,i}^{1}\,.

(3.37)

We find that

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{S=\delta}A_{i}^{(0)}(S,T)=\sum\limits_{\ell=0,2,\ldots}^{2\delta-2}\left(C^{\mathcal{N}=8}_{\Delta,\ell}\right)^{2}\text{Long}^{\mathcal{N}=8}_{\Delta,\ell,i}\Big|_{\text{flat space limit}}\,,

(3.38)

where we take the flat space limit as described in section
3.1
.
This means that we can do the degeneracy analysis in two steps. We first relate the flat space spectrum to
\mathcal{N}=8
superconformal multiplets. In a second step we decompose the
\mathcal{N}=8
multiplets into
\mathcal{N}=6
multiplets using (
3.37
).

The main result from the first step is that the operators on the leading
\mathcal{N}=8
Regge trajectory with superprimary spin
\ell=2\delta-2
all have degeneracy 1.
We then see from (
3.37
) that
\mathcal{N}=8
multiplets with
\ell=2\delta-2
contribute to
\mathcal{N}=6
multiplets with odd spin
\ell=2\delta-1
or even spin
\ell=2\delta-2
and
n=2,3

only with a single term in (
3.37
), so these
\mathcal{N}=6
trajectories should have degeneracy 1 as well.

We conclude that there should be three Regge trajectories of unique operators: one for
\ell=2\delta-1
, one for
\ell=2\delta-2
with
\mathcal{Z}
-even operators and one for
\ell=2\delta-2
with
\mathcal{Z}
-odd operators. Below we will find further evidence for this statement, as we will use it as an assumption to fix the last few coefficients in the ansatz for the second curvature correction. These coefficients will then be subject to non-trivial consistency checks against integrability and localization results.

4.2
Ambiguities

It turns out that the above ansatz contains a lot of ambiguities that integrate to 0.
To give an idea how these ambiguities look like, let us consider the simplest example, which contains only weight 0 and 1 terms.
It is given by (note that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)=\log|z|^{2}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)=\log|1-z|^{2}
)

\displaystyle G^{\text{amb}}_{2,1}(S,T,z)

\displaystyle=G^{\text{amb}}_{2,2}(S,T,z)=2(S+T)-S^{2}\log|z|^{2}-T^{2}\log|1-z|^{2}\,,

(4.54)

\displaystyle G^{\text{amb}}_{2,3}(S,T,z)

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad G^{\text{amb}}_{5,j}(S,T,z)=0\,.

Using

I(S,T)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}=\frac{\Gamma(-S)\Gamma(-T)\Gamma(1+S+T)}{\Gamma(1+S)\Gamma(1+T)\Gamma(-S-T)}\,,

(4.55)

we can replace the logarithms in (
4.54
) by derivatives and check that

A^{\text{amb}}_{2}(S,T)=\left(2(S+T)+S^{2}\partial_{S}+T^{2}\partial_{T}\right)I(S,T)+(S\leftrightarrow U)+(T\leftrightarrow U)=0\,.

(4.56)

The presence of ambiguities means that we cannot provide a unique answer for the integrands. However, the AdS amplitude corrections
A^{(1)}(S,T)
and
A^{(2)}(S,T)
will be uniquely determined. Below we will display integrands with a choice of ambiguities, and provide the full results with all ambiguities in an ancillary
Mathematica

5.2

We can directly compute the worldsheet integral in a low energy expansion around

using the method described in
Vanhove and Zerbini (2022)
;

and

and

have coefficients which are single-valued multiple zeta values of weight

).

Conclusions

In this paper we computed the first two curvature corrections to the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}
by combining a Borel transform of the superblock expansion, with a worldsheet ansatz in terms of SVMPLs. The first correction was completely fixed by this, and satisfied consistency checks against the high energy limit, the leading odd spin Regge trajectory from integrability, and the
R^{4}
correction at finite AdS curvature as computed from analytic bootstrap combined with localization. Our ansatz also fixed the second curvature correction, after using additional inputs from integrability and localization, and also satisfied several consistency checks.

Our result gives predictions for an infinite set of CFT data of massive string operators that can be used to guide future integrability studies.

Appendix B
OPE poles of the AdS amplitude

In this appendix we apply the Borel transform (
1.2
) to the Mellin amplitude corresponding to a bosonic conformal block

M_{\tau,\ell}(s,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau,\ell,m}(t-2)}{s-\tau-2m}\,,

(B.107)

where the numerators are Mack polynomials

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\tau,\ell,m}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}{4^{\tau+\ell}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\ell+1)}K(1,\tau,\ell,m,3)Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d=3}(t)\,,

(B.108)

with
Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(t)
as in
Alday et al. (2022a)
and

K(\Delta,\tau,\ell,m,d)=-\frac{2(\ell+\tau-1)_{\ell}\Gamma(2\ell+\tau)}{2^{\ell}\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2})\right)^{4}\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma\left(\Delta-\frac{\tau}{2}-m\right)^{2}\left(\ell+\tau-\frac{d}{2}+1\right)_{m}}.

(B.109)

The transform (
1.2
) of (
B.107
) is (where
\beta\equiv 4/3
is the shift introduced in (
1.2
))

A_{\tau,\ell}(S,T)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\kappa-i\infty}^{\kappa+i\infty}\frac{d\alpha}{2\pi i}e^{\alpha}\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau,\ell,m}(\frac{2\sqrt{\nu}T}{\alpha}+2-2\beta)}{\frac{2\sqrt{\nu}S}{\alpha}+\beta-\tau-2m}\,.

(B.110)

We exchange the integral and summation and for each
m
pick the pole at

\alpha=\alpha_{*}\equiv\frac{2\sqrt{\nu}S}{\tau+2m-\beta}\,.

(B.111)

We ignore any other poles in
\alpha
, as we are only interested in
S
-channel poles.
Next we have to sum over
m
. The Mack polynomial for large
\tau
has its maximum at
m\sim\tau^{2}
, so we replace the sum over
m=x\tau^{2}
by an integral over
x

A_{\tau,\ell}(S,T)\Big|_{S\text{-poles}}=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{\nu}}\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\sqrt{\nu}S}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\ e^{\alpha_{*}}\alpha_{*}^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{Q}_{\tau,\ell,x\tau^{2}}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\nu}T}{\alpha_{*}}+2-2\beta\right)\,.

(B.112)

We can now expand the integrand at large
\nu
, using that
\tau\sim\nu^{1/4}
.
The answer has the form

-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{\nu}}\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\sqrt{\nu}S}e^{\alpha_{*}}\alpha_{*}^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{Q}_{\tau,\ell,x\tau^{2}}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\nu}T}{\alpha_{*}}+2-2\beta\right)=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4x}+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}S}{\tau^{2}x}}}{x^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu^{\frac{5}{8}+\frac{i}{4}}}P^{(i)}\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\,,

(B.113)

where
P^{(i)}\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)
are polynomials in
\frac{1}{x}
with increasing degree (which also depend on
\tau,\ell,S,T,\nu
).
We can now do the integrals in
x
using

\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4x}+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}S}{\tau^{2}x}}}{x^{2}}=-\frac{\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sqrt{\nu}}}{S-\frac{\tau^{2}}{4\sqrt{\nu}}}=-\frac{4\delta}{S-\delta}+O\left(\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right)\,.

(B.114)

The effect of the polynomials
P^{(i)}\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)
is that each additional power of
\frac{1}{x}
increases the order of the pole by one, as
\frac{1}{x}
can be replaced by
\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sqrt{\nu}}\partial_{S}
acting on both sides of (
B.114
).
Next we do the
x
integral and get the formula (
3.25
) used in the main text.

Appendix D
Localization constraints

In this appendix we will discuss how the localization constraints of
Binder et al. (2020a)
;
Binder et al. (2020b)
can be used to constrain the
D^{4}R^{4}
term in the low energy expansion. The two localization constraints are

\begin{split}\frac{1}{c_{T}^{2}}\frac{\partial\log Z}{\partial m_{+}^{4}}&=\frac{\pi^{4}}{2^{13}}(2\lambda_{2,0,{\bf 84}}^{2}-4)\,,\\
\frac{1}{c_{T}^{2}}\frac{\partial\log Z}{\partial m_{+}^{2}\partial m_{-}^{2}}&=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2^{11}}I_{+-}[\mathcal{S}^{i}].\end{split}

(D.116)

where the second constraint is given by the integral of the Mellin amplitude:

\begin{split}I_{+-}[{\cal S}^{i}]&=\int\frac{ds\ dt}{(4\pi i)^{2}}\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{(2-t)(s+t-2)}M^{1}(s,t)\\
&\times\Gamma\left[1-\frac{s}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{s+1}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[1-\frac{t}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{t-1}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{s+t-2}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{3-s-t}{2}\right]\,.\end{split}

(D.117)

The OPE coefficient
\lambda_{2,0,{\bf 84}}^{2}
can be extracted by converting Mellin amplitudes to position space as described in Appendix
A
, and then expanding for the lowest twist 2 block in the
\bf{84}
irrep of the R-symmetry.

The LHS of these constraints were computed using localization in
Nosaka (2016)
;
Binder et al. (2020b)
using the Fermi gas formalism developed originally in
Marino and Putrov (2012)
, and at large
c_{T}
and large
\nu
take the form

\begin{split}\frac{1}{c_{T}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{4}\log Z}{\partial m_{\pm}^{4}}&=\left[\frac{3\pi^{2}}{64}+\frac{9\zeta(3)\pi^{2}}{256}\frac{1}{\nu^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{27\zeta(3)^{2}\pi^{2}}{1024}\frac{1}{\nu^{3}}+O(\nu^{-\frac{9}{2}})\right]\frac{1}{c_{T}}+O(c_{T}^{-2})\,,\\
\frac{1}{c_{T}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{4}\log Z}{\partial m_{+}^{2}\partial m_{-}^{2}}&=\left[-\frac{\pi^{2}}{64}-\frac{3\zeta(3)\pi^{2}}{256}\frac{1}{\nu^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{9\zeta(3)^{2}\pi^{2}}{1024}\frac{1}{\nu^{3}}+O(\nu^{-\frac{9}{2}})\right]\frac{1}{c_{T}}+O(c_{T}^{-2})\,.\end{split}

(D.118)

In particular, they do not contain a
\nu^{-5/2}
term corresponding to
D^{4}R^{4}
. We can then impose these two constraints on the eight coefficients appearing in the
\nu^{-5/2}
term in (
2.14
) to get the constraints (
6.92
) and (
6.93
).

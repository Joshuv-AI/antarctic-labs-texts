External validation of the MMCD score for renal replacement therapy in two Brazilian cohorts of COVID-19 patients
Authors: Rebeca Barbosa Santos, Shirley Casais Reis, Ramila Marques Nascimento, Rodrigo Beitencourt Sousa, Claudia Silva Marinho, Milena Soriano Marcolino, Rafael Lima Rodrigues de Carvalho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The MMCD (mechanical ventilation, male sex, creatinine, and diabetes) score shows acceptable discrimination for renal replacement therapy (RRT) prediction (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, 0.766).•The model demonstrated excellent calibration in the study sample.•Risk classification based on the MMCD score significantly correlated with RRT frequency.•The MMCD score is a reliable tool for RRT triage in clinical settings for patients with COVID-19.

Introduction

A respiratory infection caused by SARS-CoV-2 (severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2) rapidly evolved into a pandemic due to its high transmissibility, requiring agility and adaptation in health care services [1]. The hospital mortality rate was 22%, rising to 47.6% among patients admitted to intensive care units (ICUs). There was a 12.1% need for renal replacement therapy (RRT) [2].

The impairment of renal function by SARS-CoV-2 is multifactorial, including direct pathogen invasion of renal tubules, coagulopathy, endothelial dysfunction, and an exacerbated inflammatory response, leading to acute kidney injury (AKI) [3]. This may lead, in some cases, to stage 3 AKI, which may require RRT [4].

Although it is a clinical decision that must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis, validated scores can be helpful tools [2]. In the context of COVID-19-related AKI, the MMCD score (mechanical ventilation, male sex, creatinine, and diabetes) has been shown to predict the need for RRT in hospitalized patients with COVID-19 across different pandemic phases [5]. However, the score was developed recently and has not been evaluated in the Northeast region of Brazil or applied to populations outside the one in which it was developed.

Given the above, this study aimed to evaluate the predictive capacity of the MMCD score for RRT in patients with COVID-19 admitted to two major hospitals in Salvador, Bahia, between 2021 and 2022.

Methodology

Study design

This is a quantitative, longitudinal, and descriptive study. Data from two retrospective cohorts of patients with in-hospital-confirmed COVID-19 at two hospitals in Salvador, Brazil, were used.

Study location

The study included patients treated at two hospitals in the northeastern Brazil. During the COVID-19 pandemic, neither hospital served as a reference hospital for COVID-19 treatment. The majority of cases were referred to both hospitals. Most positive cases involved patients who were hospitalized for another complaint and tested positive for the virus during their hospital stay.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients admitted to one of the hospitals who tested positive for COVID-19 by polymerase chain reaction between January 2021 and March 2022 were included in the study. The exclusion criteria were age younger than 18 years, prior RRT use, and missing data on mechanical ventilation use, RRT use, diabetes, or age.

Sample

The initial population consisted of 491 individuals. Of these, 216 were excluded for lacking creatinine data at admission, and 34 were excluded for being younger than 18 years. Additionally, 11 individuals had no data on diabetes mellitus in their medical records and were excluded from the sample; 6 were excluded for lacking data on RRT use, and 22 had no age data and were also excluded. For validation purposes, MMCD predictors were defined using clinical and laboratory data available at hospital admission or strictly before the initiation of RRT; 2 patients were excluded because they had received RRT before mechanical ventilation, resulting in a final sample of 200 individuals.

Data collection

A list of patients with COVID-19 hospitalized during the study period was compiled. Individual data regarding the study variables were collected. Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) and Google Forms were used to collect the data. The REDCap server was hosted at the Telehealth Center of the Hospital das Clínicas da UFMG.

Variables

Data used for this analysis included age, sex, diabetes mellitus diagnosis, ICU admission, mechanical ventilation during hospitalization, and clinical data at admission (systolic and diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature, oxygen saturation, urea, and creatinine). The primary outcome for this study was RRT use during the hospital stay, and the secondary outcome was in-hospital mortality.

Data treatment and analysis

The collected data were analyzed using descriptive statistics. The model's predictive performance was evaluated in terms of discrimination and calibration. Discrimination was quantified using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC). Due to the discrete nature of the risk predictor, model calibration was assessed using the calibration intercept and slope framework derived from logistic calibration regression, along with visual inspection of the calibration plot.

Research ethics

A waiver of informed consent was requested due to the inability to contact patients. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of the UFBA School of Nursing and the Ethics Committee of Hospital Universitário Professor Edgard Santos.

Results

Of the initial population of 491 patients, 200 were included in the final sample after the exclusion criteria were applied. There were no differences in outcomes between the patients included in the study and those who were excluded (Table S1).

The median age was 56 years (range, 19-96 years); 55.0% of the patients were male, and 34.5% of the patients had diabetes mellitus. Regarding outcomes, 14.0% needed RRT. In-hospital mortality was 14.5%.

The MMCD score was calculated, and most patients were classified as non-high-risk (78.7%). Discussion

The MMCD score was calculated for patients in this study and was a reasonable predictor of the need for hemodialysis among patients hospitalized with COVID-19. A positive association was observed between patient risk classification and the number of RRT procedures performed.

The MMCD score evaluates four variables to stratify the risk of RRT: male sex, mechanical ventilation, creatinine level, and diabetes diagnosis. Regarding the first variable, males predominated, accounting for 55.0% of the population. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the patient profile remained unchanged. Studies have reported that males are more frequently infected than females [6,7].

Mechanical ventilation is associated with acute respiratory distress syndrome. Studies have shown a strong relationship between alveolar and tubular damage and the progression of AKI, to varying degrees [8]. Inflammatory mediators are released from the lungs during mechanical ventilation into the systemic circulation [3], increasing the incidence of AKI.

One of the variables in the MMCD score is creatinine. Creatinine is a key marker for renal damage, and high levels of creatinine are associated with the need for RRT [9]. There was an increase in the number of patients requiring RRT and the number of hemodialysis sessions compared with 2019; COVID-19 was one of the main reasons for RRT and the primary cause of alterations leading to death among patients receiving hemodialysis [9].

The final variable in the MMCD score is the presence of diabetes mellitus. The association between diabetes mellitus and kidney disease is well established and is characterized by non-inflammatory glomerular damage and diabetic nephropathy [10].

Regarding RRT, we observed a rate of 14.0%. Other Brazilian studies have reported rates of 9.3% [5] and 8.8% [7].

The MMCD risk score, as reported in the original research, demonstrated excellent discriminative ability (AUROC of 0.929) [5]. In this study, the AUROC was 0.7666, indicating acceptable discrimination. This difference may be attributable to the transfer of patients with severe COVID-19 to referral centers. The lack of follow-up data on disease outcomes may lead to an underestimation of RRT use.

In this context, the MMCD stands out as a simple, easy-to-use score with few variables that can be applied across various environments, including those with limited resources.

A limitation of the study was the missing data resulting from the data collection process, which led to the exclusion of 216 patients and may have introduced selection bias and limited the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, although we ensured that all predictors preceded the outcome, the exact temporal gap between mechanical ventilation and RRT onset may have varied during complex acute episodes, reflecting an inherent limitation of real-world retrospective data. In addition, the study was conducted at hospitals that were not COVID-19 reference sites.

Conclusion

The MMCD score was applied to patients in Salvador, Bahia, and demonstrated acceptable predictive ability for RRT in patients with COVID-19 in this population (AUROC, 0.7666). It is worth noting that although the MMCD score performed acceptably, it should be interpreted with caution given the study's sample size and the number of exclusions.

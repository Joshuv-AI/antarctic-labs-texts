Decision problem on interactions
Hidetada Wachi

Abstract
. An interaction is a certain symmetric graph that describes the possible transition of states of adjacent sites of large-scale interacting systems. In the series of studies Bannai-Kametani-Sasada [ 1 ] , Bannai-Sasada [ 4 ] , they defined the notion of the irreducibly quantified interactions which is suitable for considering the hydrodynamic limits via the conserved quantities. In this paper, we prove that the property that an interaction is irreducibly quantified is decidable.



1.
Introduction

The hydrodynamic limit is a mathematical method that derives macroscopic deterministic partial differential equations as limits from microscopic large-scale interacting systems.
Varadahn’s decomposition theorem plays an important role in proving the hydrodynamic limit for the non-gradient model.
In the series of studies Bannai-Kametani-Sasada
[
1
]
and Bannai-Sasada
[
4
]
, they prove Varadahn’s decomposition theorem for a general class of large-scale interacting systems.
In their setting, microscopic large-scale interacting systems are constructed by interactions.
In the recent study
[
3
]
, Bannai-Sasada generalizes the definition of interaction and computes the
0
-th uniform cohomology.

Definition 1.1

(
[
3
]
*Definition 1.2)
.

Let
S
be a finite set.
We define a
interaction
on

(S,\phi)

\phi\subset(S\times S)\times(S\times S)

(S\times S,\phi)

We call

In the theory of hydrodynamic limits, we are interested in conserved quantities of the interaction.
A map

(s,t)
,
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in S\times S
such that
((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\in\phi

\xi(s)+\xi(t)=\xi(s^{\prime})+\xi(t^{\prime})

Bannai-Koriki-Sasada-Wachi-Yamamoto
[

In the studies
[
1
]

[
4

, they consider the condition of interactions, which is called

Let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph.
In this paper, we assume that every graph has no loops or multiple edges.
For any conserved quantity
\xi
of the interactions
(S,\phi)
, we define a function
\xi_{X}:S^{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\xi_{X}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in X}\xi(\eta_{x}),

where
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
.
We define a subset

\Phi_{E}=\{(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in S^{X}\times S^{X}\mid\exists(x,y)\in E,\,(\eta_{x},\eta_{y}),(\eta^{\prime}_{x},\eta^{\prime}_{y}))\in\phi,\,\forall z\neq x,y,\,\eta_{z}=\eta^{\prime}_{z}\}.

(

We say an interaction

A priori, deciding whether a given interaction is irreducibly quantified may require checking infinitely many conditions.
However, in this paper, we prove that this can be determined in a finite number of steps.
More precisely, the main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3

(

The question of whether an interaction is irreducibly quantified is

To prove our main theorem, we reduce the problem to that of semigroups.
A semigroup

(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)

We will apply the result of Narendran-Ó’Dúnlaing

Narendran-Ó’Dúnlaing studied the decision problem on the finitely presented semigroup, and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
6
]

The question of whether a finitely presented commutative semigroup is cancellative is decidable.

In

Let

2.
Interaction

In this section, we recall the definition of interactions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S
be a finite set.

(1)

We define a
interaction
on
S
to be a pair
(S,\phi)
consisting of a subset
\phi\subset(S\times S)\times(S\times S)
such that
(S\times S,\phi)
form a symmetric digraph.

(2)

We say that an interaction
(S,\phi)
is
exchangeable
if for any
(s,t)\in S\times S
,
(s,t)
and
(t,s)
are in the same connected component of
(S\times S,\phi)
.

We first prove the decidability of exchangeability.

Proposition 2.2
.

The question of whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable is decidable.

Proof.

For any
(s,t)\in S\times S
, we construct a set
S_{s,t}\subset S\times S
as follows.

Step 1:

Let
S_{s,t}^{0}=\{(s,t)\}
.

Step 2:

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
(u,v)\in S_{s,t}^{i}
, let
T_{u,v}^{i}:=\{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\mid((u,v),(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}))\in\phi\}
. We define
S_{s,t}^{i+1}=\bigcup_{(u,v)\in S_{s,t}^{i}}T_{s,t}^{i}
.

Let
S_{s,t}:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}S_{s,t}^{i}
.
Since
S
is finite, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{s,t}^{N}=S_{s,t}
.
Then
S_{s,t}^{N}
is a set of elements in
S\times S
which are in the connected component containing
(s,t)
in
(S\times S,\phi)
.
Therefore we can check whether an element
(t,s)\in S\times S
in the connected component containing
(s,t)
in
(S\times S,\phi)
by checking whether
(t,s)\in S_{s,t}^{N}
.
Therefore, since
S\times S
is finite, the question of whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable is decidable.
∎

In the study of the hydrodynamic limit, we are interested in the conserved quantity of an interaction.
We fix a base point
*\in S
.
Then the conserved quantity is defined as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

(1)

We define a
conserved quantity
to be a function
\xi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for any
((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\in\phi
, we have
\xi(s)+\xi(t)=\xi(s^{\prime})+\xi(t^{\prime})
.
We let
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of conserved quantities.

(2)

We let
{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
denote the space of conserved quantities modulo the space of constant functions.
By abuse of notation, for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
we also let
\xi
denote its representative.

Remark 2.4
.

Since
{\mathrm{Map}}(S,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{S}
and
S
is finite,
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspace
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
is finite dimensional.
Moreover, the basis of these vector spaces can be computed by solving a system of linear equations

x_{s}+x_{t}=x_{s^{\prime}}+x_{t^{\prime}}\quad((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\in\phi.

In particular, we can obtain a basis of the spaces
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
whose components are integers.
Moreover, if you fix a state
*\in S
, a basis of subspace
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
consisting of functions
\xi
such that
\xi(*)
gives a basis of the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that an interaction
(S,\phi)
is
separable

if for any
s,t\in S
if
\xi(s)=\xi(t)
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, we have
s=t
.

Since a constant function
f:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies
f(s)=f(t)
for any
s,t\in S
, the notion of separability does not depend on the choice of a representative of
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
.
By using following

Lemma 2.6
.

The question of whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is separable is decidable.

Proof.

Let
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a basis of
{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
computed as in
Remark

2.4
.
Since the space of conserved quantities are
\mathbb{R}
-vector space, the interaction
(S,\phi)
is separable if and only if for any
s,t\in S
such that
s\neq t
, there exists
i\in I
such that
\xi_{i}(s)=\xi_{i}(t)
.
Therefore we can check whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is separable by comparing values of conserved quantities
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for each distinct pair
(s,t)\in S^{2}
.
Since the number of distinct pair
(s,t)\in S^{2}
is finite and
I
is finite, the question of whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is separable is decidable.
∎

Let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph.
We note that
E=\emptyset
when
|X|=1
.
We say that
(X,E)
is
complete
if
(x,y)\in E
for any

.
For any interaction
(S,\phi)
and finite connected symmetric digraph

, we let
S^{X}:={\mathrm{Map}}(X,S)
.
For any
\eta\in S^{X}

\eta_{x}

.
We define the subset

\Phi_{E}=\{(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in S^{X}\times S^{X}\mid\exists(x,y)\in E,\,((\eta_{x},\eta_{y}),(\eta^{\prime}_{x},\eta^{\prime}_{y}))\in\phi,\,\forall z\neq x,y,\,\eta_{z}=\eta^{\prime}_{z}\}.

Since
(S\times S,\phi)
is symmetric graph, the digraph

Let
\xi
be a conserved quantity of
(S,\phi)
.
We define a map

\xi_{X}(\eta):=\sum_{x\in X}\xi(\eta_{x})

for any

.
We define the irreducibly quantified interaction as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that an interaction
(S,\phi)
is
irreducibly quantified
if for any finite connected symmetric digraph
(X,E)
and for any
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
, if
\xi_{X}(\eta)=\xi_{X}(\eta^{\prime})
for any conserved quantity
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, then
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component of

.

We can easily observe that an irreducibly quantified interaction is always exchangeable and separable as follows.

Lemma 2.8
.

If an interaction
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified, then
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable and separable.

Proof.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an irreducibly quantified interaction.
Let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph such that
|X|=1
.
Then we have
S^{X}=S
and
\Phi_{E}=\emptyset
.
Therefore, since
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified, for any
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
such that
\xi(s_{1})=\xi(s_{2})
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, we have
s_{1}=s_{2}
.
This shows that the interaction
(S,\phi)
is separable.

We next consider a complete finite symmetric digraph
(X^{\prime},E^{\prime})
such that
|X^{\prime}|=2
.
Then we have
S^{X^{\prime}}=S^{2}
and
\Phi_{E}=\phi
.
Therefore, for any
s,t\in S
, since
\xi_{X^{\prime}}(s,t)=\xi_{X^{\prime}}(t,s)
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, two configurations
(s,t)
and
(t,s)
are in the same connected component of
(S\times S,\phi)
.
Hence the interaction
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable.
∎

For any
\eta\in S^{X}
and
x,y\in X
, we define
\eta^{x,y}\in S^{X}
to be

\eta_{z}^{x,y}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{y}&z=x\\
\eta_{x}&z=y\\
\eta_{z}&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Finally, we prove the following lemma which will be used in the proof of key lemma
Lemma

.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an exchangeable interaction, and let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph.
For any
\eta\in S^{X}
and
x,y\in X
, there is a path from
\eta
to
\eta^{x,y}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.

Proof.

Since
(X,E)
is connected, there is a path

x=x_{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow x_{k}=y

from

to
y
in
(X,E)
.
For any
i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
, we inductively define
\eta^{i}
by
\eta^{0}:=\eta
and
\eta^{i+1}:=(\eta^{i})^{x_{i},x_{i+1}}
.
We note that
\eta_{y}^{k-1}=\eta_{x}
.
Since
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable, for any
i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
, there is a path from
\eta^{i}
to
\eta^{i+1}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
For any
i\in\{k,\dots,2k-2\}
, we next define
\eta^{i}
as
\eta^{i}=(\eta^{i-1})^{x_{2k-i-2},x_{2k-i-3}}
.
Then we have
\eta^{2k-2}=\eta^{x,y}
.
Since
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable, for any
i\in\{k,\dots,2k-2\}
, there is a path from
\eta^{i}
to
\eta^{i+1}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Therefore there is a path from
\eta
to
\eta^{x,y}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
∎

3.
Commutative semigroup and interaction

In this section, we will construct a commutative semigroup associated with an interaction, and prove that the question of whether an interaction is irreducibly quantified is decidable.
We first recall the definition of a semigroup.
We define a
semigroup

M=(M,\cdot)
to be a pair consisting of a set
M
and a binary operation
\cdot:M\times M\rightarrow M
which satisfies
(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)
for any
a,b,c\in M
.
We often write
ab:=a\cdot b
for any
a,b\in M
.
A semigroup
M
is
commutative
if
ab=ba
for any
a,b\in M
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a commutative semigroup.

(1)

We say that an element
c\in M
is
cancellative
in
M
if
ca=cb
implies
a=b
for any
a,b\in M
.

(2)

We say that
M
is
cancellative
if every elements in
M
are cancellative.

(3)

We say that
M
is
power-cancellative
if
a^{n}=b^{n}
implies
a=b
for any
a,b\in M
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

For any monoids
M,N
, we say that a map
f:M\rightarrow N
is a
homomorphism
if for any
a,b\in M
, we have
f(ab)=f(a)f(b)
.
Now, we recall the following result.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
5
]
*II,Corollary 7.4)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated, cancellative, power-cancellative commutative semigroup which has no identity element.
The elements of
M
are separated by finitely many homomorphisms of
M
into
\mathbb{N}
.
In other words,
M
is isomorphic to a sub-semigroup of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Proof.

In
[
5
]
*II,Corollary 7.4, this proposition is proved when the group of units of
M
is trivial.
Then a disjoint union
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e\}
, where
e
is an identity element of
M^{\prime}
, is a commutative monoid.
Every element
a\in M
is not a unit in the commutative monoid
M^{\prime}
.
Therefore the group of units of
M^{\prime}
is trivial.
By applying
[
5
]
*II,Corollary 7.4 to
M^{\prime}
, we have an inclusion
M^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
This induces an inclusion
M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
∎

Let
S=\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction.
We define a semigroup
M(S,\phi)
by

M(S,\phi):=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\mid a_{i}a_{j}=a_{k}a_{l},\,((i,j),(k,l))\in\phi\rangle.

In other words,
M(S,\phi)
is a semigroup constructed by dividing a free semigroup on
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
by the congruence generated by relations
\{a_{i}a_{j}=a_{k}a_{l}\mid((i,j),(k,l))\in\phi\}
.
For any
a\in M(S,\phi)
, we define the length
\mathrm{len}(a)
to be the smallest length of the expressions of
a\in M(S,\phi)
as a product of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
.
Since relations of the semigroup
M(S,\phi)
do not change the length of words,
all expressions of
a\in M(S,\phi)
as a product of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
have the same length.
Therefore, for any
a,b\in M(S,\phi)
, we have
\mathrm{len}(ab)=\mathrm{len}(a)+\mathrm{len}(b)
.
In particular, we have a homomorphism
\mathrm{len}:M(S,\phi)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, the semigroup
M(S,\phi)
has no identity element.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction.
A semigroup
M(S,\phi)
is commutative if and only if
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable.

Proof.

If
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable, for any
i,j\in S
,
(i,j)
and
(j,i)
are in the same connected component of
(S\times S,\phi)
.
Therefore there is a path

(i,j)=(i_{0},j_{0})\rightarrow(i_{1},j_{1})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(i_{m},j_{m})=(j,i)

in
(S\times S,\phi)
.
This implies that

a_{i}a_{j}=a_{i_{0}}a_{j_{0}}=a_{i_{1}}a_{j_{1}}=\cdots=a_{i_{m}}a_{j_{m}}=a_{j}a_{i}

in
M(S,\phi)
.
Therefore
M(S,\phi)
is commutative.

Next, we assume that
M(S,\phi)
is commutative.
For any
i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in S
, we have
a_{i^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}}=a_{j^{\prime}}a_{i^{\prime}}
.
Then we can get
a_{j^{\prime}}a_{i^{\prime}}
by applying relations of
M(S,\phi)
to
a_{i^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}}
finitely many times.
Since all relations of
M(S,\phi)
does not change length of words, we have

a_{i^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}}=a_{i^{\prime}_{0}}a_{j^{\prime}_{0}}=a_{i^{\prime}_{1}}a_{j^{\prime}_{1}}=\cdots=a_{i^{\prime}_{m}}a_{j^{\prime}_{m}}=a_{j^{\prime}}a_{i^{\prime}}

such that for any
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, an equality
a_{i^{\prime}_{k}}a_{j^{\prime}_{k}}=a_{i^{\prime}_{k+1}}a_{j^{\prime}_{k+1}}
is a relation of
M(S,\phi)
.
Therefore there is a path

(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=(i^{\prime}_{0},j^{\prime}_{0})\rightarrow(i^{\prime}_{1},j^{\prime}_{1})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(i^{\prime}_{m},j^{\prime}_{m})=(j^{\prime},i^{\prime})

in
(S\times S,\phi)
.
∎

Next, we prove that
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{Hom}(M(S,\phi),\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a monoid on
\mathbb{R}
with its summation, are isomorphic
\mathbb{R}
-vector space.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
.
We define a map
\xi_{M}:M(S,\phi)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
by
\xi_{M}(a_{i})=\xi(i)
for any
i\in S
.
This induces an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map

u:{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(M(S,\phi),\mathbb{R}_{+});\quad\xi\mapsto\xi_{M}.

Moreover, this map is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
.
We first prove that
\xi_{M}
is a well-defined homomorphism.
By definition of
\xi_{M}
, for any relation
a_{i}a_{j}=a_{k}a_{l}
of
M(S,\phi)
,
we have

\xi_{M}(a_{i}a_{j})=\xi(i)+\xi(j)=\xi(k)+\xi(l)=\xi_{M}(a_{k}a_{l}).

Therefore the map
\xi_{M}
is a well-defined homomorphism.
Hence the assignment
\xi\mapsto\xi_{M}
induces an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(M(S,\phi),\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

We will prove that this map is an isomorphism.
For any
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
, if for any
i\in S
, we have
\xi_{M}(a_{i})=\xi^{\prime}_{M}(a_{i})
, then we have
\xi(a_{i})=\xi^{\prime}(a_{i})
.
Therefore we have
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
.
Hence the map
u
is injective.
Next, we prove the surjectivity.
For any
\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}(M(S,\phi),\mathbb{R})
, we define
\xi^{\psi}:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\xi^{\psi}(i)=\psi(a_{i})
.
For any
((i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}))\in\phi
, we have

\xi^{\psi}(i)+\xi^{\psi}(j)=\psi(a_{i}a_{j})=\psi(a_{i^{\prime}}a_{j^{\prime}})=\xi^{\psi}(i^{\prime})+\xi^{\psi}(j^{\prime}).

Therefore
\xi^{\psi}
is a conserved quantity and
\xi_{M}^{\psi}=\psi
.
Hence the map
u
is surjective.
∎

Let
(S,\phi)
be an exchangeable interaction, and let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph.
For any
\eta\in S^{X}
, we define an element
\eta_{M}
of
M
by

\eta_{M}:=a_{1}^{\sharp\{x\in X\mid\eta_{x}=1\}}\cdots a_{n}^{\sharp\{x\in X\mid\eta_{x}=n\}}.

We prove that this assignment induces a surjection
S^{X}\mapsto\{a\in M(S,\phi)\mid l(a)=|X|\}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The map
f:S\rightarrow M(S,\phi)
induced by the assignment
\eta\mapsto\eta_{M}
is surjective.

Proof.

Let
a_{i}^{p_{i}}\cdots a_{n}^{p_{n}}\in M(S,\phi)
.
We fix a bijection
\iota:X\rightarrow\{1,\dots,|X|\}
.
We define
\eta\in S^{X}
by
\eta_{x}=i
for any
x\in X
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}p_{j}<\iota(i)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}
.
Then we have
\eta_{M}=a_{i}^{p_{i}}\cdots a_{n}^{p_{n}}
.
Therefore the map
f
is surjective.
∎

The following lemma is key to reducing the problem for interactions to the problem of semigroups.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an exchangeable interaction, and let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph.
For any
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
,
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
if and only if
\eta_{M}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}
in
M(S,\phi)
.

Proof.

Let
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
.
We assume that
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then there is a path

\eta=\eta^{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\eta^{m}=\eta^{\prime}

in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, there exist
x_{i},y_{i}\in X
such that
((\eta^{i}_{x_{i}},\eta^{i}_{y_{i}}),(\eta^{i+1}_{x_{i}},\eta^{i+1}_{y_{i}}))\in\phi
and
\eta_{z}^{i}=\eta_{z}^{i+1}
for any
z\neq x_{i},y_{i}
.
Since there is a relation
a_{\eta^{i}_{x_{i}}}a_{\eta^{i}_{y_{i}}}=a_{\eta^{i+1}_{x_{i}}}a_{\eta^{i+1}_{y_{i}}}
of
M(S,\phi)
, we have

\eta_{M}=\eta_{M}^{0}=\dots=\eta_{M}^{m}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}

in
M(S,\phi)
.

On the other hand, we assume that
\eta_{M}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}
.
Then we have equalities

\eta_{M}=b_{0}=\cdots=b_{m^{\prime}}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}

such that, for any
i\in\{0,\dots,m^{\prime}-1\}
, if we write
b_{i}=a_{1}^{p_{i,1}}\cdots a_{n}^{p_{i,n}}
, there exist pairs
(j_{i},k_{i}),(j^{\prime}_{i},k^{\prime}_{i})\in\{1,\dots,n\}^{2}
such that

p_{i+1,j}=p_{i,j}-\delta_{j_{i}}-\delta_{k_{i}}+\delta_{j^{\prime}_{i}}+\delta_{k^{\prime}_{i}},\quad\delta_{k}(l):=\begin{cases}1&k=l,\\
0&k\neq l,\end{cases}

and
a_{j_{i}}a_{k_{i}}=a_{j^{\prime}_{i}}a_{k^{\prime}_{i}}
is a relation of
M(S,\phi)
.
Let
\eta^{0}:=\eta
.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,m^{\prime}\}
, by applying following construction (
\heartsuit
), we define
\eta^{i}
inductively:

(
\heartsuit
):

If a given
\eta^{i}
satisfies
\eta_{M}^{i}=b_{i}
, we can take
x_{i},y_{i}\in X
such that

\eta_{x_{i}}^{i}=j_{i},\quad\eta_{y_{i}}^{i}=k_{i}.

We define
\eta^{i+1}
by

\eta_{z}^{i+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}j^{\prime}_{i}&z=x_{i}\\
k^{\prime}_{i}&z=y_{i}\\
\eta_{z}^{i}&z\neq x_{i},y_{i}.\end{array}\right.

Since
\eta^{i+1}
satisfies
\eta_{M}^{i+1}=b_{i+1}
, if
i+1\in\{1,\dots,m^{\prime}-1\}
, then we can apply (
\heartsuit
) to
\eta^{i+1}
.

We note that
\eta_{M}^{m^{\prime}}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}
.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,m^{\prime}\}
, take
z_{i}\in X
such that
(z_{i},y_{i})\in E
.
Since
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable, by
Lemma

2.9
, there is a path from
\eta^{i}
to
(\eta^{i})^{x_{i},z_{i}}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then, by the construction, we have
((\eta^{i})^{x_{i},z_{i}},(\eta^{i+1})^{x_{i},z_{i}})\in\Phi_{E}
.
By applying
Lemma

2.9
again, there is a path from
\eta^{i}
to
\eta^{i+1}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Since
\eta_{M}^{m^{\prime}}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}
, by applying
Lemma

2.9
finitely many times, we obtain a path from
\eta^{m^{\prime}}
to
\eta^{\prime}
in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Therefore we conclude that
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
∎

Now, we reduce the irreducible quantifiability of interactions to the cancellativity and power-cancellativity of the semigroups induced by them, as follows.

Theorem 3.7
.

A separable, exchangeable interaction
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified if and only if the commutative semigroup
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative and power-cancellative.

Proof.

We assume that
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified.
We first prove that
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative.
Let
a,b,c\in M(S,\phi)
such that
ac=bc
.
By
Lemma

3.4
, for any
\xi\in\mathrm{Map^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})}
, since
\xi_{M}(ac)=\xi_{M}(bc)
, we have
\xi_{M}(a)=\xi_{M}(b)
.
In particular, if we let
\xi
be a constant function, it induces
\mathrm{len}(a)=\mathrm{len}(b)
.
If
\mathrm{len}(a)=1
, since
(S,\phi)
is separable, we have
a=b
.
Therefore we may assume that
\mathrm{len}(a)\geq 2
.
Let
(X,E)
be a complete finite symmetric digraph of size
\mathrm{len}(a)
.
By
Lemma

3.5
, there exist
\eta^{a}
and
\eta^{b}\in S^{X}
such that
\eta_{M}^{a}=a
and
\eta_{M}^{b}=b
, respectively.
For any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
, since
\xi_{M}(a)=\xi_{M}(b)
, we have
\xi_{X}(\eta^{a})=\xi_{X}(\eta^{b})
.
Therefore, since
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified,
\eta^{a}
and
\eta^{b}
are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
By
Lemma

3.6
, we have
a=b
.
Therefore
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative.

We next prove that
M(S,\phi)
is power-cancellative.
Let
a,b\in M(S,\phi)
.
We assume that there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a^{N}=b^{N}
.
By
Lemma

3.4
, we have
\xi_{M}(a^{N})=\xi_{M}(b^{N})
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
.
Therefore we have
\xi_{M}(a)=\xi_{M}(b)
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
.
By a similar argument as above, we have
a=b
.
Therefore
M(S,\phi)
is power-cancellative.

On the other hand, we next assume that
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative and power-cancellative.
Let
(X,E)
be a finite connected symmetric digraph, and let
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
such that
\xi_{x}(\eta)=\xi_{X}(\eta^{\prime})
for any
\xi\in{\mathrm{Map}}^{\phi}(S,\mathbb{R})
.
By
Proposition

3.2
, we have an inclusion
\pi:M(S,\phi)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
For each
i=1,\dots,d
, we let
\pi_{i}
denote the projection
\mathbb{N}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
on the
i
-th component.
Then the composition
\pi_{i}\circ\pi
is a homomorphism
M(S,\phi)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
By
lemma

3.4
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, there is
\xi^{i}
such that
\xi_{M}^{i}=\pi_{i}\circ\pi
.
Therefore we have

\pi_{i}\circ\pi(\eta_{M})=\xi_{X}^{i}(\eta)=\xi_{X}^{i}(\eta^{\prime})=\pi_{i}\circ\pi(\eta^{\prime}_{M})

for any
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
Since
\pi
is an inclusion, we have
\eta_{M}=\eta^{\prime}_{M}
.
Therefore, by
Lemma

3.6
,
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component in
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
, and hence we conclude that
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified.
∎

The main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 3.8
.

The question of whether an interaction
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified is
decidable
.

Proof.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction.
By
Proposition

2.2
and
Lemma

2.6
, we can decide whether
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable and separable.
By
Lemma

2.8
, if the interaction
(S,\phi)
is not exchangeable or separable, it cannot be irreducibly quantified.
Therefore we may assume that the interaction
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable and separable in the following.

By
Theorem

3.7
, it suffices to show that the question of whether, for an exchangeable, separable interaction
(S,\phi)
, the induced semigroup
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative and power-cancellative.
By
[
6
]
*Theorem 5.8, in general, the question of whether a finitely presented commutative semigroup is cancellative is decidable.
Therefore the question of whether a commutative semigroup
M(S,\phi)
is cancellative is decidable.

A cancellative commutative semigroup
M
can be embedded into the Grothendieck group

G(M)=\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\{\mathbbm{e}_{i}+\mathbbm{e}_{j}-\mathbbm{e}_{i^{\prime}}-\mathbbm{e}_{j^{\prime}}=0\mid((i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}))\in\phi\},

where
\mathbbm{e}_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
are standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
By
[
5
]
*II, Proposition 5.4, a commutative semigroup
M
is power-cancellative if and only if
G(M)
is torsion-free.
The question of whether the abelian group
G(M)
is torsion-free is decidable by computing the Smith normal of
n\times|\phi|
-matrix

A=(\mathbbm{e}_{i}+\mathbbm{e}_{j}-\mathbbm{e}_{i^{\prime}}-\mathbbm{e}_{j^{\prime}})_{((i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}))\in\phi}.

Therefore we conclude that the question of whether an exchangeable interaction
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified is decidable.
∎

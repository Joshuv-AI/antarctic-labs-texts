Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir Efficacy in Immunocompetent Patients with Confirmed Omicron Variant of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Elnaz Khani, Hoda Afsharirad, Ali Taghizadeh Yadegari, Meysam Zarezadeh, Hila Asham, Hossein Bannazadeh-Baghi, Taher Entezari-Maleki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The high mutations of the Omicron variant of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 raised concerns regarding the efficacy of antivirals. This meta-analysis aimed to determine the impact of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir on the outcomes of immunocompetent patients with confirmed Omicron variant. Three reviewers systemically searched PubMed (Medline), Cochrane Library, and Embase databases up to June 9, 2025. Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) and observational studies with a control group were screened for eligibility to extract data. Studies that included only patients with immunosuppression, malignancy, or renal failure, severe disease, or assessed nirmatrelvir/ritonavir efficacy on variants other than Omicron were excluded. Forty-six observational studies, including 6,099,805 participants, were involved in the meta-analysis. Our findings revealed that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir significantly decreases death (risk ratio [RR] =0.31; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.23–0.40), disease progression (RR = 0.57; 95% CI: 0.42–0.71), hospitalization (RR = 0.45; 95% CI: 0.35–0.56), composite outcome of hospitalization and death (RR = 0.58; 95% CI: 0.44–0.71), ventilation (RR = 0.46; 95% CI: 0.19–0.72), and intensive care unit admission (RR = 0.56; 95% CI: 0.38–0.73) compared to the control group. However, no significant difference was shown across the two groups regarding hospitalization duration (mean difference = −2.34; 95% CI: −5.60–0.93). The current updated meta-analysis supported the efficacy of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir on the Omicron variant. However, further RCTs are recommended for accurate results.

INTRODUCTION

COVID-19 is a significant global health threat, with the emergence of new variants adding complexity to the ongoing battle against the virus. The Omicron variant, also known as B.1.1.529, has attracted considerable attention for its high transmissibility and potential implications for vaccine and therapeutic efficacy.[1]

A combination of nirmatrelvir, a 3C-like protease inhibitor, and ritonavir, a pharmacokinetic enhancer, is designed to inhibit the replication of the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) in the early stages of infection.[23] On May 25, 2023, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved nirmatrelvir/ritonavir use in adults with mild to moderate COVID-19 at high risk for progression to severe COVID-19, including hospitalization or death.[4] However, its efficacy on the Omicron variant is not fully elucidated.

Since the Omicron variant is the highest mutated variant among other COVID-19 variants of concern, questions have arisen regarding maintaining the effectiveness of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir against Omicron.[5] Preliminary data from in vitro studies have indicated potential reductions in antiviral activity against the Omicron variant, raising concerns about its clinical efficacy.[6]

Hence, a comprehensive meta-analysis is warranted to consolidate existing evidence and provide a clearer understanding of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir’s effectiveness against the Omicron variant. This updated meta-analysis is the first study that determine the impact of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir on the outcomes of immunocompetent patients with confirmed Omicron variant.

A previous study that evaluated the effectiveness of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir on severe outcomes of COVID-19 in the era of vaccination and Omicron did not exclude immunocompromised patients, such as patients with malignancy or patients who received organ transplantation, and also included the suspected cases of Omicron, which can be potential sources of bias.[7] In addition, we evaluated intensive care unit (ICU) admission, length of hospitalization, and mechanical ventilation, the outcomes that the previous study did not address.

The detailed search strategy for investigated databases

The reference lists of relevant studies were reviewed to ensure that no eligible articles were missed. The Population, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome framework was used to define the eligibility criteria. All observational studies and clinical trials involving immunocompetent individuals with confirmed SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant infection that assessed nirmatrelvir/ritonavir effects on mortality, disease progression, hospitalization, composite of hospitalization and death, ventilation, duration of hospitalization, and ICU admission were included.

The studies were excluded if they lacked a control group or compared nirmatrelvir/ritonavir effects with other antiviral agents. Other exclusion criteria were as follows: Studies in which the included population had severe or critical illness, were only patients with immunosuppression, malignancy, or renal failure, review articles, letters to the editor, commentaries, case reports, animal studies, laboratory and in silico studies, duplicate publications, and articles in languages other than English.

Three reviewers (E.K., H.A., and A.T.) independently screened records for their potential inclusion against the eligibility criteria to extract data using a standardized checklist adapted from the Cochrane Collaboration’s data collection form. Any disagreements were discussed with the corresponding author, T.E.

Data extracted were (1) study general information, including first author’s name, country, year, design, and follow-up duration; (2) participants’ characteristics, including number of men and women, mean age, percentage of COVID-19 booster dose vaccination, Charlson Comorbidity Index, health condition, hospitalization status, and route of COVID-19 diagnosis; (3) intervention, including dose, duration, route of administration, and co-treatments; (4) outcomes, including mortality, disease progression, hospitalization, composite of hospitalization and death, ventilation, duration of hospitalization, and ICU admission, and data for calculating effect size (mean difference, confidence interval [CI], hazard ratio [HR], relative risk [RR], and odds ratio [OR]). In cases where only the number of events was reported, RR and 95% CI are calculated according to Altman, 1991.

The Newcastle Ottawa Scale was used to assess the risk of bias for the cohort studies.[8]

The collected data were reported as a percentage (%), means ± standard deviation, HR, RR, and OR. The overall effect sizes were reported as mean difference and 95% CI for hospitalization duration and RR, and 95% CI for the remaining outcomes. Since the incidence of all measured outcomes was very low at the end of the follow-up periods (<5%), HRs, ORs, and RRs were considered equivalent.[9] The heterogeneity of the studies was assessed by Cochran’s Q-test and I2‐statistic. The P values below 0.1 in the Q‐statistic and I2 ≥ 50% were considered high heterogeneity. Potential sources of heterogeneity were recognized by subgroup analysis. The random-effects modeling approach was applied for data analysis. The sensitivity analysis was conducted by removing each study from the meta-analysis to determine its effect on the overall effect size (leave-one-out meta-analysis method). The publication bias was assessed using the visual inspections of the funnel plots, Egger’s regression asymmetry tests, and Begg’s adjusted rank correlation. The detected publication bias was corrected using the trim and fill method. P values below 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant. All statistical analysis was performed using Stata 17.0 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX, US).

RESULTS

Study selection process

Database search and manual screening yielded a total of 1047 articles. After duplication removal and excluding unsuitable titles or abstracts, 961 studies were excluded. Of the remaining 86 studies, 34 were excluded after the full-text screening. Finally, 46 studies were included in the meta-analysis. Five studies were excluded from the calculations due to the lack of information, no number of events in both arms, or imprecise event reporting, and one randomized clinical trial (RCT) was excluded since it was the sole trial with that design, which precluded statistical pooling with the cohort studies.[101112131415] The selection process and reasons for exclusion are presented in a PRISMA flow diagram [Figure 1].

In 46 studies, 1,310,639 COVID-19 patients received nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, and 4,789,166 patients did not receive any antiviral [Table 1].[16171819202122232425262728293031323334353637383940414243444546474849505152535455565758596061] The mean age of patients was between 14.5 and 80 years. Most studies included nonhospitalized patients (n = 3,312,106 [97.02%]) with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 at high risk for severe disease. Seventeen studies were conducted in China, nine in the USA, four in Israel, three in Korea, three in Italy, two in Mexico, one in Singapore, one in Canada, one in Australia, one in Greece, one in Austria, one in Taiwan, one in the UAE, and one in Malaysia. All included studies were published from 2022 to 2025. Studies that mentioned the dose of intervention used 300 mg nirmatrelvir and 100 mg ritonavir twice daily in patients with normal kidney function. The sensitivity analysis showed that removing any studies did not change the final result. A small-study effect was not detected using Egger’s (P = 0.82) and Begg’s (P = 0.36) tests. Notably, there was significant heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 92.66%; P-heterogeneity < 0.001).

Disease progression

The results obtained from 14 studies indicated that the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir is associated with a significant reduction in the risk of disease progression (RR = 0.57; 95% CI: 0.42–0.71; P < 0.001) [Figure 3].

(a) Forest plot detailing risk ratio and 95% confidence intervals for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and disease progression. (b) Funnel plot for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and disease progression. CI = Confidence interval

The reduced risk of disease progression remained significant in all subgroups of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir-treated patients, adjusted for age, sample size, and type of effect size [Table 3]. Significant heterogeneity existed between studies (I2 = 97.82%; P-heterogeneity P < 0.001).

The sensitivity analysis showed that removing any study did not alter the overall results. No significant publication bias was detected by Egger’s (P = 0.53) and Begg’s tests (P = 1).

Hospitalization

As shown in Figure 4, the analysis of 25 studies (29 datasets) showed that patients treated with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir had a lower risk of hospitalization than control subjects (RR = 0.45; 95% CI: 0.35–0.56; P < 0.001).

(a) Forest plot detailing risk ratio and 95% confidence intervals for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and hospitalization. (b) Funnel plot for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and hospitalization. CI = Confidence interval

Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir effect in hospitalization reduction was significant in all subgroups [Table 3], except for studies that did not report their adjustment status (RR = 0.27; 95% CI: −0.36–0.90; P = 0.40). There was significant heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 98.69%; P-heterogeneity < 0.001).

The sensitivity analysis outcome revealed that excluding any studies did not alter the final result. No statistically significant small-study effect was observed based on the results of Egger’s (P = 0.15) and Begg’s (P = 0.17) tests.

Composite of hospitalization and death

According to the results obtained from the analysis of 9 studies (10 datasets), the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir significantly reduced the risk of hospitalization and death (RR = 0.58; 95% CI: 0.44–0.71; P < 0.001) [Figure 5]. Significant heterogeneity existed between studies (I2 = 96.53%; P-heterogeneity < 0.001). The source of heterogeneity was studies that reported the effect size as OR. The sensitivity analysis showed that removing any studies did not change the final result. Significant small-study effect was not detected using Egger’s (P = 0.76) and Begg’s (P = 0.37) tests.

(a) Forest plot detailing risk ratio and 95% confidence intervals for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and hospitalization and death. (b) Funnel plot for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and hospitalization and death. CI = Confidence interval

Ventilation

The results of 9 studies showed a significant decrease in ventilation risk with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir use (RR = 0.46; 95% CI: 0.19–0.72; P < 0.001) [Figure 6a]. In addition, there was significant heterogeneity between studies (I2 = 72.24%; P-heterogeneity < 0.001).

(a) Forest plot detailing risk ratio and 95% confidence intervals for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and ventilation. (b) Funnel plot for the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and ventilation. CI = Confidence interval

As shown in Table 3, the results did not remain significant in subgroups, including patients below 70 years (P = 0.06), or studies that reported their results as RR (P = 0.07) or relative effect (P = 0.06). Based on the results of the subgroup analysis, studies that reported HR and adjusted their results may be the sources of heterogeneity.

The sensitivity analysis showed that removing any of the studies did not change the final result. No significant small-study effect was observed using Egger’s (P = 0.12) and Begg’s (P = 0.35) tests. Considering an uneven distribution of studies in the funnel plot [Figure 6b], a trim and fill analysis was conducted, which also showed an insignificant result (RR = 0.43; 95% CI: 0.18–0.70).

Furthermore, there was significant heterogeneity among the investigations (I2 = 99.63%; P-heterogeneity < 0.001). However, the source of heterogeneity was not found. DISCUSSION

The findings from the present study demonstrate nirmatrelvir/ritonavir’s beneficial effects on mortality, disease progression, hospitalization, death and hospitalization, ventilation, as well as ICU admission in patients infected with the Omicron variant. However, it did not yield a significant effect on the duration of hospital stay.

The potential mechanism of nirmatrelvir in the management of COVID-19 relies on its ability to inhibit the 3Clpro, the main protease of SARS-CoV-2, which plays a key role in viral replication. Ritonavir acts as a pharmacologic enhancer by inhibiting cytochrome 3A-mediated metabolism of nirmatrelvir, leading to increased plasma concentrations of nirmatrelvir.[62]

In a recent meta-analysis conducted by Souza et al., nirmatrelvir/ritonavir was associated with a significant reduction in hospitalization (OR, 0.47; 95% CI: 0.37–0.60), death (OR, 0.41; 95% CI: 0.35–0.52), and the composite of hospitalization or death (OR = 0.44; 95% CI: 0.31–0.64) compared to the standard of care, not including antiviral agents.[63] The results remained in favor of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir irrespective of vaccination status. However, it was not defined whether the vaccinated subgroups were fully vaccinated. Since the importance of booster doses became more apparent in the era of the Omicron variant, we stratified patients based on the percentage of booster doses received. Notably, Souza et al. did not limit their inclusion criteria to the Omicron variant.

The percentage of booster vaccines received in the study population is an important issue in interpreting the effects of antiviral agents. For example, in the study by Bajema et al., nirmatrelvir/ritonavir did not reduce hospitalization or death in the subgroup of patients who did not receive primary or booster vaccination.[19] On the other hand, in the study of Lui et al., in which 22% of the individuals received the booster vaccine, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir reduced the risk of mortality by 71%.[24]

In line with our findings, another meta-analysis by Amani and Amani showed that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir use was associated with a significant reduction in death (OR = 0.25; 95% CI: 0.14–0.45), hospitalization (OR = 0.40; 95% CI: 0.24–0.69), and hospitalization or death (OR = 0.17; 95% CI: 0.06–0.46).[64] However, it did not affect the rate of ICU admission (OR = 0.37; 95% CI: 0.13–1.01). The important point of this meta-analysis is that the control group included both other antiviral agents and a placebo. However, in our trial, we excluded the studies that used other antivirals as the control group. The other point is that all variants of COVID-19 were included in this meta-analysis, limiting the assessment of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir effects on the Omicron variant.

Although our analysis showed a significant decrease in the risk of hospitalization with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir treatment, it did not demonstrate a statistically significant impact on the length of hospital stay among admitted patients. This suggests that while early antiviral treatment can effectively prevent disease progression to the point of hospitalization, its effect may be limited once severe illness occurs. The length of hospitalization in COVID-19 patients is influenced by multiple factors, including patients’ underlying health conditions, hospital management strategies, and postviral complications, rather than viral replication alone. Since nirmatrelvir primarily targets viral protease activity, its therapeutic benefit is expected to be greatest during the early, viral-replication phase of infection. Once patients are hospitalized, especially those with advanced inflammatory or immune complications, antiviral therapy may have less impact on recovery time. In addition, differences in clinical discharge policies and healthcare resource availability across studies may have contributed to the variation in hospitalization duration outcomes. These findings emphasize the importance of early antiviral therapy to achieve the maximum clinical benefit.

To the best of our knowledge, the present study is the first meta-analysis of the nirmatrelvir/ritonavir efficacy on COVID-19 patients infected with the Omicron variant. Furthermore, different subgroup analyses were performed for a more accurate examination of the association between nirmatrelvir/ritonavir use and treatment outcomes. To directly assess the nirmatrelvir/ritonavir effects, we exclude the studies using other antiviral agents as the control.

Limitations

Most studies included in this meta-analysis did not specify Omicron subvariants. However, its use may not be applicable in clinical practice due to limited availability.

The studies included in the meta-analysis are heterogeneous, which may have influenced the pooled results. Several factors could account for this variability. First, the included studies differed in their definitions of clinical outcomes, particularly “disease progression,” which in some cohorts referred to oxygen supplementation needs, whereas in others it encompassed ICU admission or mechanical ventilation. Second, differences in population characteristics, such as age distribution, vaccination coverage, and prevalence of comorbidities, could have affected treatment responses and event rates. Third, the studies were conducted during different phases of the pandemic, when circulating Omicron subvariants, healthcare resource availability, and therapeutic strategies varied considerably. These temporal variations may have led to inconsistent outcomes among the studies. Finally, the timing of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir initiation relative to symptom onset was not uniformly reported, which may also contribute to outcome variability. Future RCTs with standardized outcome definitions and stratification by disease phase and variant are warranted to address these sources of heterogeneity.

CONCLUSIONS

Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir reduces the risk of mortality, disease progression, hospitalization, the composite of hospitalization and death, ventilation, and ICU admission in COVID-19 patients infected with the Omicron variant. Considering the heterogeneity of the current evidence, further RCTs are recommended.

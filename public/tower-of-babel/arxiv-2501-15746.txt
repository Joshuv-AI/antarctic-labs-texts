Intersections of Hecke correspondences on the modular varieties of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -elliptic sheaves
Özge Ülkem, Fu-Tsun Wei

Abstract
This paper studies the intersections of Hecke correspondences on the modular varieties of 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} -elliptic sheaves in the higher-rank setting, where 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} is a “maximal order” in a central division algebra D D over a global function field k k . Assuming that dim k ( D ) = r 2 \dim_{k}(D)=r^{2} , where r r is a prime distinct from the characteristic of k k , we express the intersection numbers of Hecke correspondences as suitable combinations of modified Hurwitz class numbers of “imaginary orders”. This result establishes a higher-rank analogue of the classical class number relation.

Introduction

Let
X
denote the modular curve of full level. For each non-square
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Hecke correspondence
T_{n}
on
X
corresponds to a
1

on

i(\mathcal{Z}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n})_{\rm fin}=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{t \in\mathbb{Z}}{t^2 < 4n}}H(t^{2}-4n)\quad\big(=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m \in\mathbb{N}}{m \mid n}}\max(m,n/m)\big).

Here,

denotes the “Hurwitz class number” for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The fact that these intersection points are actually “CM” provides a conceptual foundation for the proof of this relation, as it connects the intersection in question to Eichler’s theory of optimal embeddings of imaginary quadratic orders into matrix rings. This phenomenon is also evident in the context of Hilbert modular surfaces associated with real quadratic fields, as demonstrated in the celebrated work of Hirzebruch and Zagier
[
19
]
. Since then, the arithmetic significance of intersection numbers between special cycles on modular varieties has been extensively studied (see
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
), leading to the so-called “geometric Siegel-Weil formula” with profound applications (see
[
27
]
,
[
7
]

[
25
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
).

The primary aim of this paper is to explore this phenomenon within the function field setting and to describe the intersection numbers of “Hecke correspondences” on the modular varieties of
{\mathcal{D}}
-elliptic sheaves in higher rank, expressed in terms of class numbers.
For the case where the base field is a rational function field with a finite constant field, an analogue of the Hurwitz–Kronecker class number relation (for Drinfeld modular curves) has been derived in
[
40
]
and
[

through a detailed study of Drinfeld modular polynomials in
[

]

[

[

Additionally, a Hirzebruch–Zagier-type formula was established in
[

This paper represents our initial effort to extend these ideas to the “higher rank” setting. To streamline the discussion, we focus on the case of modular varieties of
{\mathcal{D}}
-elliptic sheaves where
{\mathcal{D}}

Remark 1.2
.

As
D
is a division algebra, there is no “cuspidal intersection” in our situation.
Also, the assumption that
r
is a prime ensures that the components in the intersection
{\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathfrak{a}}}
have dimensions either
0
or
r-1
.
For composite
r
, the intersection behavior may involve components associated with moduli schemes of
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
-elliptic sheaves, where
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is a non-maximal order in
D^{\prime}
, and
D^{\prime}
is a central simple algebra over a finite extension

of

Furthermore, the condition that

is distinct from the characteristic of

ensures that the imaginary fields corresponding to the intersection points are separable over

Without these assumptions, the overall structure of the intersections becomes significantly more complex and technical, which is why we impose these conditions in our first attempt to study higher rank cases in this paper.

1.1
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1

As the (coarse) modular variety
X
, which parametrizes
{\mathcal{D}}
-elliptic sheaves, may not be smooth over
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
, we use Briney’s theory to study the intersections in question.
To address this, we consider a finite Galois covering
X({\mathfrak{n}})
of
X
, where
X({\mathfrak{n}})
is the modular variety of
{\mathcal{D}}
-elliptic sheaves equipped with a level
{\mathfrak{n}}
-structure for a suitable nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{n}}
of
A
. This covering ensures that
X({\mathfrak{n}})
is smooth over
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
.
Using the projection formula, we can reformulate the intersection number of interest in terms of the corresponding pullback cycles on
X({\mathfrak{n}})\times X({\mathfrak{n}})
as follows (see Theorem
6.1
):

i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathfrak{a}}})=\frac{1}{(q-1)\cdot[X({\mathfrak{n}}):X]}\sum_{\tilde{g}}i({\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)),

(1.1)

where
[X({\mathfrak{n}}):X]
is the degree of the “Galois covering”
X({\mathfrak{n}})
over
X
;
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})
is the diagonal cycle of
X({\mathfrak{n}})
on
X({\mathfrak{n}})\times X({\mathfrak{n}})
;
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is introduced in (
4.3
); and the sum runs through a double coset space corresponding to the Hecke correspondence associated with
{\mathfrak{a}}
on
X({\mathfrak{n}})
.

Next, the rigid-analytic structure of these varieties allows us to establish the transversality of the intersection
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
when
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})
and
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
are distinct. This transversality ensures that
i({\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g))
can be computed simply by counting the number of their intersection points.

Furthermore, through a sequence of double-coset comparisons, we relate these intersection numbers to the number of optimal embeddings of the corresponding imaginary orders into
{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}
(see Corollary
8.5
). Additionally, the self-intersection number of
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})
is shown to equal the Euler–Poincaré characteristic of

, which can be interpreted as a volume quantity via an analogue of the Gauss–Bonnet formula established by Kurihara in
[
28
]
(see Proposition

).

Finally, by applying Eichler’s theory, we express these intersection numbers in terms of modified Hurwitz class numbers, and complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

The recent groundbreaking works of Feng, Yun and Zhang in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
establish a “higher” Siegel–Weil formula in the function field setting, showing a function field analogue of the Kudla–Rapoport conjecture.
They express the non-singular Fourier coefficients of all central derivatives of Siegel–Eisenstein series on unitary groups in terms of the degrees of corresponding special cycles on the moduli stack of unitary shtukas.
In contrast, our result takes a complementary perspective in the case where the group is
D^{*}
(an inner form of
\GL_{r}
): we write the intersection numbers of specific cycles on the modular varieties of
{\mathcal{D}}
-elliptic sheaves as combinations of the class numbers of imaginary orders, which are natural arithmetic invariants.

Notably, when
r=2
, we would be able to adapt the method in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, utilizing the Weil representation, to explicitly construct an automorphic form whose Fourier coefficients correspond to the intersection numbers in question.
Although this method does not appear to generalize to
r>2
, we believe that a deeper connection with automorphic forms on
D^{*}
persists and remains to be fully understood. This intriguing connection will be investigated in future work.

5.1

5.2

We recall the following theorem:

Theorem 5.3

(

By this theorem we can talk about the intersection of smooth (quasi-) projective varieties. Briney’s theory enables us to extend the intersection theory of finite quotients of smooth (quasi-) projective varieties to not necessarily smooth ones by allowing rational multiplicities. First we define covering cycles.

Let

By the projection map

\theta_{z}(A^{\prime}):=d(A)A

One can extend the mappings
\theta_{z}
and
\theta^{z}
linearly to the cycles.

Remark 5.4
.

For every cycle

, where

(cf. Proposition

Let

and

be the corresponding covering cycles. Let

We can compute the intersection multiplicity

by carrying over the multiplicities given on

Definition 5.5

(
[

of

where

is any component of

and the multiplicity
(P^{\prime};A^{*}\cdot B^{*})_{X}
is on

.

Lemma 5.6
.

(
[

If

is defined on

for all

\theta_{z}^{\prime}(A^{\prime\sigma}\cdot B^{*})=\theta_{z}(A^{\prime}\cdot B^{*})

\theta_{z}(A^{*}\cdot B^{*})=n(A)\theta_{z}(A^{\prime}\cdot B^{*}).

Proposition 5.7

(
[

)

Let

and

be cycle on

is defined on

\theta^{z}(Z)\cdot\theta^{z}(T)
is defined on

. And in this case, we have

\theta^{z}(Z\cdot T)=\theta^{z}(Z)\cdot\theta^{z}(T).

2.

(
Projection formula
)

is defined on

iff
\theta_{z}(Z^{\prime})\cdot T
is defined on
Y
. And in this case, we have

\theta_{z}(Z^{\prime}\cdot\theta^{z}(T))=\theta_{z}(Z^{\prime})\cdot T.

Remark 5.8
.

Suppose that
X
is projective and smooth over
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
.
Given two cycles

on

the intersection number

is the counting number of their intersection points.
By the moving lemma, this intersection number can be extended to every two cycles

on

with middle dimension.
Therefore for every two cycle

on

with middle dimension,
take a cycle

on

for some

We may define the intersection number

via the above projection formula:

1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix}\big)\quad\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}^{r-1}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}^{r-1}\cong T_{\Omega_{r}^{2},(z^{o},z^{o})}.

Suppose that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}\cap{\mathbb{H}}_{1}
, i.e.
\gamma z^{o}=z^{o}
.
Write

z^{o}=\begin{pmatrix}z^{o}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
z^{o}_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{i}^{o}:=w_{i}(z^{o}),\ 1\leq i\leq r-1.

Then
\gamma z^{o}=z^{o}
implies that
w_{i}^{o}/w_{r}^{o}=z_{i}^{o}
for
1\leq i\leq r-1
, and

A\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{o}\\
\vdots\\
z_{r-1}^{o}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=0,\quad\text{ where }A:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}-w_{r}^{o}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1r}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}-w_{r}^{o}&\cdots&a_{2r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{r1}&a_{r2}&\cdots&a_{rr}-w_{r}^{o}\end{pmatrix}.

(7.2)

On the other hand, given
y\in T_{\alpha}{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}\cap T_{\alpha}{\mathbb{H}}_{1}
,
we may write
y=(x,x)
with
x\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}^{r-1}
, and the condition
y=(x,x)\in T_{\alpha}{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}
implies that

x=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}=x_{1}(\partial_{1}w)(z^{o})+\cdots+x_{r-1}(\partial_{r-1}w)(z^{o}).

By (
7.1
) we get

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}=(w_{r}^{o})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}-a_{r1}z_{1}^{o}&\cdots&a_{r-1,1}-a_{r1}z_{r-1}^{o}\\
&\vdots&\\
a_{1,r-1}-a_{r,r-1}z_{1}^{o}&\cdots&a_{r-1,r-1}-a_{r,r-1}z_{r-1}^{o}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}

which is equivalent to

B\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}=0,\quad\text{ where }B:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}-a_{r1}z_{1}^{o}-w_{r}^{o}&\cdots&a_{r-1,1}-a_{r1}z_{r-1}^{o}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{1,r-1}-a_{r,r-1}z_{1}^{o}&\cdots&a_{r-1,r-1}-a_{r,r-1}z_{r-1}^{o}-w_{r}^{o}\end{pmatrix}.

(7.3)

By suitable row operations on the following matrix

B^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}-w_{r}^{o}&a_{21}&&\cdots&a_{r1}\\
a_{12}&a_{22}-w_{r}^{o}&&\cdots&a_{r2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&&\vdots\\
a_{1,r-1}&a_{2,r-1}&\cdots&a_{r-1,r-1}-w_{r}^{o}&a_{r,r-1}\\
z_{1}^{o}&z_{2}^{o}&\cdots&z_{r-1}^{o}&1\end{pmatrix},

we can see that
\det(B^{\prime})=\det(B)
.

Now, if
y=(x,x)\neq 0
, i.e.
x\neq 0
, then
\det(B^{\prime})=\det(B)=0
.
As the field
k(\gamma)
is separable of degree
r
over
k
under our assumption, the eigenspace of
A
corresponding to the eigenvalue
0
(with multiplicity one) is spanned by
(z_{1}^{o},...,z_{r-1}^{o},1)^{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}^{r}
.
Hence
\det(B^{\prime})=0
implies that
the nonzero column vector
(z_{1}^{o},...,z_{r-1}^{o},1)^{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}^{r}
lies in the range of
A
, and also in the null space of
A
by (
7.2
).
However, the diagonalizability of
A
in
M_{r}({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
assures that the intersection of the range of
A
and the null space of
A
must be trivial, which is a contradiction.
Therefore
y=0
, i.e. the intersection of
T_{\alpha}{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}
and
T_{\alpha}{\mathbb{H}}_{1}
is trivial.
∎

Consequently, we have that:

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose
r
is a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
k
.
When
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\neq{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
, the intersection
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\cap{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
is transversal.

8
Counting the intersection numbers of Hecke correspondences

From the previous section, we know that when
r
is a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
k
, the intersection of
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
and
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is always transversal when they are distinct.
In this case, the intersection number
i({\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g))
is simply equal to the cardinality of their intersection points.
In what follows, we shall count the intersection points in question by using “optimal embeddings.”

We first release our condition on
r
, (i.e.
r
is just a positive integer). Recall the following uniformization of
X(\mathfrak{n},g)
:

X(\mathfrak{n},g)(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\cong D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g).

Every
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
-valued point of
X(\mathfrak{n},g)
corresponds to a class in
D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/K({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
Note that the morphism
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}:X({\mathfrak{n}},g)\rightarrow X({\mathfrak{n}})\times X({\mathfrak{n}})
gives an isomorphism between
X({\mathfrak{n}},g)
and
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
Then:

Lemma 8.1
.

The set
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}^{-1}\big(\mathbb{X}(\mathfrak{n})\cap\mathbb{X}(\mathfrak{n},g)\big)({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
can be identified with

\left\{[z,b]_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}\in D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\ \Bigg|\ \begin{tabular}[]{l}\text{there exists $\gamma\in D^{*}$ so that}\\
\text{$\gamma\cdot z=z$ and $b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}(\mathfrak{n})g$}\end{tabular}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
x\in X({\mathfrak{n}},g)({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
.
From the unifomization of
X({\mathfrak{n}},g)
, we may identify
x
with a class
[z,b]_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}\in D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
Then
[z,b]_{\mathfrak{n}}
(resp.
[z,bg^{-1}]_{\mathfrak{n}}
) is the class in
D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}^{\infty})/K({\mathfrak{n}})
corresponding to
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g,1}(x)
(resp.
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g,2}(x)
).
Suppose
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}(x)\in{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
, which is equivalent to

[z,b]_{{\mathfrak{n}}}=[z,bg^{-1}]_{{\mathfrak{n}}}\in D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}).

This means that there exists
\gamma\in D^{*}
and
\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
so that
\gamma z=z
and
\gamma bg^{-1}=b\kappa
, which says that

b^{-1}\gamma b=\kappa g\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g.

Conversely, suppose there exists
\gamma\in D^{*}
so that
\gamma\cdot z=z
and
b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g
.
Write
b^{-1}\gamma b=\kappa g
where
\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
.
Then

[z,b]=[\gamma^{-1}z,\gamma^{-1}b\kappa]=[z,bg^{-1}]\in D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}),

which implies that
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}(x)\in{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Set

\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g):=\big\{(\gamma,z,b)\in D^{*}\times\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\mid\gamma\cdot z=z,\ b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g\big\},

which is equipped with a left action of
D^{*}
and a right action of
K({\mathfrak{n}},g)
defined below: for every
(\gamma,z,b)\in\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
,
\gamma_{0}\in D^{*}
, and
\kappa\in K({\mathfrak{n}},g)
,

\gamma_{0}\cdot(\gamma,z,b)\cdot\kappa:=(\gamma_{0}\gamma\gamma_{0}^{-1},\gamma_{0}\cdot z,\gamma_{0}b\kappa).

By Lemma
8.1
, we have a natural surjective map from
\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
to
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}^{-1}\big({\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
sending
(\gamma,z,b)
to (the point corresponding to)
[z,b]_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}
.
Moreover:

Lemma 8.2
.

The above map induces a bijection between

D^{*}\backslash\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
and
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}^{-1}\big({\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Given
(\gamma,z,b),(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
, suppose

[z,b]_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}=[z^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{{\mathfrak{n}},g}\quad\in X({\mathfrak{n}},g),

which implies that there exists
\gamma_{0}\in D^{*}
and
\kappa_{0}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
so that

z^{\prime}=\gamma_{0}z
and
b^{\prime}=\gamma_{0}b\kappa_{0}
.
Thus

\gamma_{0}^{-1}\cdot(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime},b^{\prime})\cdot\kappa_{0}^{-1}=(\gamma_{0}^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{0},z,b)\quad\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{n}},g).

Put
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{0}\in D^{*}
, which satisfies that
\gamma_{1}\cdot z=z
and
b^{-1}\gamma_{1}b\in K({\mathfrak{n}})g
.

Note that by
[
30
, Lemma 4.6]
, one has that
K_{z}:=\{\gamma\in D^{*}\mid\gamma\cdot z=z\}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield of
D
which is imaginary with respect to
\infty
(i.e.
\infty
is non-split in
K_{z}
).
Hence

\gamma_{2}:=\gamma_{1}\gamma^{-1}\in K_{z}^{*}\quad\text{with}\quad b^{-1}\gamma_{2}b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}).

This says in particular that
\gamma_{2}
is a unit in
O_{K_{z}}
, the integral closure of
A
in
K_{z}
, and
b^{-1}\gamma_{2}b\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{n}}
.
Since
K_{z}
is imaginary and the degree
[K_{z}:k]
divides
r
, the units of
O_{K_{z}}
must be contained in a finite field with
q^{r}
elements.
The condition
b^{-1}\gamma_{2}b\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{p}}
then implies that
\gamma_{2}=1
,
whence

\gamma_{0}^{-1}\cdot(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime},b^{\prime})\kappa_{0}^{-1}=(\gamma,z,b).

Therefore
(\gamma,z,b)
and
(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime},b^{\prime})
represent the same class in
D^{*}\backslash\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

Given
g\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
, one has that
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})={\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
if and only if
g\in k^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})={\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
when
g\in k^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
.
Conversely, suppose
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})={\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
By Lemma
8.2
, we have that for every
z\in\Omega_{r}
, there exists
\gamma\in D^{*}
such that
\gamma\cdot z=z
and
b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g
.
When taking
z
with algebraically independent coordinates over
k
, we must have that
\gamma\in k^{*}
,
whence
g=\gamma\kappa\in k^{*}{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
as desired.
∎

8.1
The case when
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\neq{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)

Let

{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g):=\big\{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\ \big|\ k(\gamma)\text{ is imaginary over $k$, }b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g\big\}.

Then we have a natural map from
\phi:{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
defined by
\phi(\gamma,z,b)=(\gamma,b)
for every triple
(\gamma,z,b)\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
On the other hand, the fiber of each
(\gamma,b)
in
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
under
\phi
can be identified with the set of fix points of
\gamma
on
\Omega_{r}
, which is always non-empty.
Therefore
\phi
is surjective.
Moreover, the following lemma holds:

Lemma 8.4
.

The map

\bar{\phi}:D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\longrightarrow D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)

induced by
\phi
is surjective.
Moreover,
suppose
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\neq{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
and
r
is a prime number.
For each class
[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/K({\mathfrak{n}},g)
, the cardinality of
\bar{\phi}^{-1}([\gamma,b])
is
r

(
resp.
1)
if
k(\gamma)
is separable
(
resp. purely inseparable
)
over
k
.

Proof.

The surjectivity is clear.
Suppose
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\neq{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
and
r
is a prime number.
Let
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
By assumption one has that

k\neq k(\gamma)\subset D
is a maximal subfield of
D
which is imaginary.
Thus there are
r
(resp.
1
) fixed point(s) of
\gamma
on
\Omega_{r}
, say
z_{1},...,z_{r}
(resp.
z_{0}
), corresponding to distinct eigenvalues (resp. the unique eigenvalue) of
\gamma
.
Hence

\phi^{-1}(\gamma,b)=\begin{cases}\{(\gamma,z_{1},b),\cdots(\gamma,z_{r},b)\},&\text{ if $k(\gamma)/k$ is separable},\\
\{(\gamma,z_{0},b)\},&\text{ if $k(\gamma)/k$ is purely inseparable.}\end{cases}

It suffices to verify that
(\gamma,z_{1},b),...,(\gamma,z_{r},b)
represents distinct classes in the space
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
when
k(\gamma)/k
is separable.
To show this,
suppose there exists
\gamma_{0}\in D^{*}
and
\kappa_{0}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
such that

(\gamma,z_{i},b)=\gamma_{0}\cdot(\gamma,z_{j},b)\cdot\kappa_{0}=(\gamma_{0}\gamma\gamma_{0}^{-1},\gamma_{0}\cdot z_{j},\gamma_{0}b\kappa_{0}).

Since
k(\gamma)
is a maximal subfield of
D
, the centralizer of
k(\gamma)
is itself.
Hence
\gamma_{0}\in k(\gamma)^{*}
and

b^{-1}\gamma_{0}b=\kappa_{0}^{-1}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g).

By our assumption on
{\mathfrak{n}}
, we can apply the same argument in the proof of Lemma
8.2
to get
\gamma_{0}=1
, which implies
z_{i}=z_{j}
and
\kappa_{0}=1
.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

Suppose
X({\mathfrak{n}})\neq X({\mathfrak{n}},g)
and
r
is a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
k
.
The cardinality of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1}\big({\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})
is equal to

r\cdot\#\left(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\right).

Remark 8.6
.

Given
\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
, the map from
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
to
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)
sending
(\gamma,b)
to
(\gamma,b\kappa)
for every
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is bijective.
Thus we have the following induced bijection

D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa).

8.2
Self-intersection of
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})

When
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})={\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
(i.e.
g\in k^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
by Corollary
8.3
), the self-intersection number of
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
in
X({\mathfrak{n}})\times X({\mathfrak{n}})
is equal to the
Euler–Poincaré characteristic of
X({\mathfrak{n}})
, denoted by
\chi(X({\mathfrak{n}}))
, which is the degree of the top Chern class of the tangent bundle of
X({\mathfrak{n}})
(see
[
13
, Example 8.1.12]
).
Recall the following identification

X({\mathfrak{n}})({\mathbb{C}}_{\infty})\cong D^{*}\backslash\Omega_{r}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cong\coprod_{[b]\in D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})}\Gamma_{b}({\mathfrak{n}})\backslash\Omega_{r},

where
\Gamma_{b}({\mathfrak{n}}):=D^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})b^{-1}
for every
b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
.
Identifying
D_{\infty}=D\otimes_{k}k_{\infty}
with
{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{\infty})
, we may regard
\Gamma_{b}
as a discrete and co-compact torsion free subgroup of
\text{GL}_{r}(k_{\infty})
.
Therefore by Kurihara’s analogue of Hirzebruch proportionality (see
[
28
, Theorem 2.2.8]
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[b]\in D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})}\mu_{\infty}\big(\Gamma_{b}({\mathfrak{n}})\backslash\text{GL}_{r}(k_{\infty})/k_{\infty}^{*}\big).

(8.1)

Here
\mu_{\infty}
is the
Euler–Poincaré measure
on
\text{PGL}_{r}(k_{\infty})
introduced by Serre (see
[
34
]
), i.e.

\mu_{\infty}\big(\text{PGL}_{r}(O_{\infty})\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-q_{\infty}^{i}),

and
q_{\infty}
is the cardinality of the residue field at
\infty
.
Extending
\mu_{\infty}
to the Haar measure
\mu_{\mathbb{A}}
on
D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/k_{\infty}^{*}\cong D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\times\text{PGL}_{r}(k_{\infty})
satisfying that

\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\big({\mathcal{K}}\times\text{PGL}_{r}(O_{\infty})\big)=\mu_{\infty}\big(\text{PGL}_{r}(O_{\infty})\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-q_{\infty}^{i}),

the equation (
8.1
) leads to the following Gauss–Bonnet-type formula:

Proposition 8.7
.

\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big)=[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]\cdot\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\big(D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/k_{\infty}^{*}\big).

Remark 8.8
.

(1) We may rewrite the above formula as the following form:

\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big)=[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]\cdot\sum_{[b]\in D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}}\mu_{\infty}\big(\Gamma_{b}\backslash\text{GL}_{r}(k_{\infty})/k_{\infty}^{*}\big),

where

\Gamma_{b}:=D^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1}
for every
b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
.

(2) Let

\mu_{{\mathbb{A}},t}
be the
Tamagawa measure on
D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{A}}^{*}
introduced in
[
39
, §3.2]
.
It is known that (see
[
39
, Theorem 3.2.1]
)

\mu_{{\mathbb{A}},t}\big(D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{A}}^{*}\big)=r,

and (cf.
[
39
, §3.1, p. 32]
and
[
38
, §5]
)

\mu_{{\mathbb{A}},t}\big({\mathcal{K}}/({\mathcal{O}}^{\infty})^{*}\times\text{PGL}_{r}({\mathcal{O}}_{\infty})\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\zeta_{k}(-i)^{-1}\cdot\prod_{v\in{\bf Ram}}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{1-q_{v}^{i}},

where
q_{v}
is the cardinality of the residue field of
v
for each
v\in|C|
, and
\zeta_{k}(s)
is the zeta function of
k
, i.e.

\zeta_{k}(s)=\prod_{v\in|C|}(1-q_{v}^{-s})^{-1},\quad{\rm Re}(s)>1.

Therefore we get

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\big(D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/k_{\infty}^{*}\big)

(8.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\#\text{Pic}(A)}{q-1}\cdot\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\big(D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{A}}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\#\text{Pic}(A)}{q-1}\cdot\mu_{{\mathbb{A}},t}\big(D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{A}}^{*}\big)\cdot\frac{\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\big({\mathcal{K}}/({\mathcal{O}}^{\infty})^{*}\times\text{PGL}_{r}({\mathcal{O}}_{\infty})\big)}{\mu_{{\mathbb{A}},t}\big({\mathcal{K}}/({\mathcal{O}}^{\infty})^{*}\times\text{PGL}_{r}({\mathcal{O}}_{\infty})\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\#\text{Pic}(A)}{q-1}\cdot r\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\zeta_{k}(-i)\cdot\prod_{v\in{\bf Ram}\cup\{\infty\}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-q_{v}^{i}).

Consequently, Proposition
8.7
implies the following formula.

Proposition 8.9
.

The self-intersection number of
{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{n}})
in
X({\mathfrak{n}})\times X({\mathfrak{n}})
is equal to

\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big)=[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]\cdot r\cdot\frac{\#\text{\rm Pic}(A)}{q-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\zeta_{k}(-i)\cdot\prod_{v\in{\bf Ram}\cup\{\infty\}}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-q_{v}^{i}).

Remark 8.10
.

When
A
is a polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the formula for
\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big)
appears in
[
31
, equation (6.10)]
.

8.3
Projection to the full-level case

Now, we assume
r
to be a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
k
.
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero ideal of
A
.
Set

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\left\{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\ \bigg|\ \begin{tabular}[]{cc}\text{$k(\gamma)$ is imaginary over $k$,}\\
\text{$b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}$
and $\text{Nr}(\gamma)\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}$}\end{tabular}\right\}={\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\cup}}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\ \bigg|\ \begin{tabular}[]{cc}\text{$k(\gamma)/k$ is imaginary of degree $r$,}\\
\text{$b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}$
and $\text{Nr}(\gamma)\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}$}\end{tabular}\right\}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\times D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\ \big|\ \gamma\in A\text{ and }\gamma^{r}\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}\big\}.

Then

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
(as well as
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}
)
is equipped with a left action of
D^{*}
and a right action of
{\mathcal{K}}
defined by

\gamma_{0}\cdot(\gamma,b)\cdot\kappa:=(\gamma_{0}\gamma\gamma_{0}^{-1},\gamma_{0}b\kappa),\quad\forall\gamma_{0}\in D^{*},(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}},\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}.

Choose a nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{n}}
of
A
so that
v\nmid{\mathfrak{n}}
for every
v\in{\bf Ram}
and
{\rm ord}_{v}({\mathfrak{a}})\leq{\rm ord}_{v}({\mathfrak{n}})
for every
v\notin{\bf Ram}\cup\{\infty\}
.
For each
g\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}\cap D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
with
\text{Nr}(g)\cdot{\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}\cap k={\mathfrak{a}}
, one has
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
.
Put

{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g):={\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\cap{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{\circ}({\mathfrak{n}},g):={\mathcal{E}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\cap{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}.

We point out that
either
{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
or
{\mathcal{E}}^{\circ}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is empty, and

{\mathcal{E}}^{\circ}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is non-empty if and only if
g\in k^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
.
Moreover, let
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
be the “prime-to-
Ram
” part of
{\mathfrak{a}}
, i.e.
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
is the ideal of
A
so that

{\rm ord}_{v}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}{\rm ord_{v}}({\mathfrak{a}}),&\text{ if $v\notin{\bf Ram}\cup\{\infty\}$;}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

the following lemma holds.

Lemma 8.11
.

Taking
\star\in\{\times,\circ\}
,
we may decompose
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}
into

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}=\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\bar{g} \in\cK(\fn)\backslash(\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f))/\cK(\fn)}{\text{\rm Nr}(g)\cdot\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}\ \ \coprod_{\bar{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\backslash{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})}{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa).

(8.4)

Consequently, we have the bijection:

\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\bar{g} \in\cK(\fn)\backslash(\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f))/\cK(\fn)}{\text{\rm Nr}(g)\cdot\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}\ \ \coprod_{\bar{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\backslash{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})}D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Given
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}
,
we may take
g=b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}\cap D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
and get
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
.
Thus it remains to show that the right hand side of the equality (
8.4
) is indeed a disjoint union.

Given
g,g^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}\cap D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
with
\text{Nr}(g)\cdot{\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}\cap k=\text{Nr}(g^{\prime})\cdot{\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}\cap k={\mathfrak{a}}
,
suppose
{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\cap{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g^{\prime})
is non-empty, i.e. there exists
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\cap{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g^{\prime})
.
Then
b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g\cap{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g^{\prime}
, which implies that
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g={\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g^{\prime}
.
Hence

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}=\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\bar{g} \in\cK(\fn)\backslash\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f)}{\text{Nr}(g)\cdot\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}({\mathfrak{n}},g),

and the desired decomposition in (
8.4
) follows from:

{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})=\coprod_{\bar{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\backslash{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})}{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})g\kappa,\quad\forall g\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}\cap D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}).

∎

Next, we look closely into
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
for
\star=\times
or
\circ
.
We may decompose
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
into

D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})=\hskip-8.5359pt\coprod_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}/{\mathcal{K}}}\big\{D^{*}(\gamma,b\kappa){\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\ \big|\ \bar{\kappa}\in(b^{-1}C_{D}(k(\gamma))b\cap{\mathcal{K}})\backslash{\mathcal{K}}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\big\},

where
C_{D}(k(\gamma))
is the centralizer of
k(\gamma)
in
D
.
Applying Eichler’s theory of optimal embeddings of imaginary
A
-orders into
D
in Appendix
A
, we know that
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}
is finite (see Remark
A.7
).
Hence

\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\big)=\sum_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})]}{\#(b^{-1}k(\gamma)^{*}b\cap{\mathcal{K}})}.

Finally, by Lemma
8.11
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{g}}\sum_{\bar{\kappa}\in K({\mathfrak{n}},g)\backslash K({\mathfrak{n}})}\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{g}}\sum_{\bar{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\backslash K({\mathfrak{n}})}[{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa):{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})]\cdot\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g\kappa)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}):{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})]\cdot\sum_{\bar{g}}\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)\quad(\text{by Remark~\ref{rem: ind-k}}),

where
\bar{g}
runs through the double cosets in
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})\backslash({\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}\cap D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
with
\text{Nr}(g)\cdot{\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}\cap k={\mathfrak{a}}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]}\cdot\sum_{\bar{g}}\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)

(8.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})]}\cdot\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{q-1}{\#(b^{-1}k(\gamma)^{*}b\cap{\mathcal{K}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{q-1}{\#(k(\gamma)^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1})}.

Recall that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\circ}({\mathfrak{n}},g)
is non-empty if and only if
g\in k^{*}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})
, which is equivalent to
X({\mathfrak{n}})=X({\mathfrak{n}},g)
by Corollary
8.3
.
For
\star\in\{\times,\circ\}
and
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}_{\mathfrak{a}}
, we put

i(\gamma,b):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{q-1}{\#(k(\gamma)^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1})},&\text{ if $\star=\times$},\\
\displaystyle{\frac{q-1}{r}}\cdot\mu_{\infty}(\Gamma_{b}\backslash\GL_{r}(k_{\infty})/k_{\infty}^{*}),&\text{ if $\star=\circ$}.\end{cases}

By Corollary
8.5
, the equation (
8.1
), and the projection formula in Example
6.1
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 8.12
.

Suppose
r
is a prime number different from the characteristic of
k
.
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a nonzero ideal of
A
.
We have that

i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\frac{r}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}/{\mathcal{K}}}i(\gamma,b).

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
, we have that

\displaystyle i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]}\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{g \in\cK(\fn)\backslash\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f)/\cK(\fn)}{\text{Nr}(g)\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}i({\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]}\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{g \in\cK(\fn)\backslash\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f)/\cK(\fn)}{g \notin k^*\cK(\fn),\ \text{Nr}(g)\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}i({\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{n}},g))

\displaystyle\hskip-7.68236pt+\frac{1}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]}\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\gamma\in A}{\gamma^r A = \fa}}\chi\big(X({\mathfrak{n}})\big).

By Corollary
8.5
and Remark
8.8
(1),
the right-hand-side of the above equality can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{r}{[{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}})]}\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{g \in\cK(\fn)\backslash\cD^\infty\cap D^*(\bA_f)/\cK(\fn)}{g \notin k^*\cK(\fn),\ \text{Nr}(g)\cO^{\infty}\cap k = \fa}}\#\big(D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{\times}({\mathfrak{n}},g)/{\mathcal{K}}({\mathfrak{n}},g)\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\gamma\in A}{\gamma^r A = \fa}}\sum_{b\in D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}}\mu_{\infty}\big(\Gamma_{b}\backslash\text{GL}_{r}(k_{\infty})/k_{\infty}^{*}\big).

Note that
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}/{\mathcal{K}}
is in bijection with

\{\gamma\in A\mid\gamma^{r}A={\mathfrak{a}}\}\times D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}
.
Therefore by the equation (
8.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}}i(\gamma,b)+\frac{r}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}/{\mathcal{K}}}i(\gamma,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}/{\mathcal{K}}}i(\gamma,b).

∎

In the next section, we shall express the right hand side of the above equality in terms of class numbers of imaginary fields, and derive an Kronecker-Hurwitz-type class number relation.

9
Class number relation

Keep the notation as in the last section.
Suppose
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-empty, which implies that

{\mathfrak{a}}
is a principal ideal of
A
.
In this case, for each
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
,
the condition
b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}
is equivalent to

\gamma\in b{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}b^{-1}\cap D=:O_{D,b},

where
O_{D,b}
is a maximal
A
-order of
D
.
This implies that
\gamma
is integral over
A
.
Suppose
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}
.
Let

f_{\gamma}(x)=x^{r}-c_{1}x^{r-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{r-1}c_{r-1}x+(-1)^{r}c_{r}\in A[x]

be the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
over
k
.
Since
k(\gamma)
is imaginary over
k
of degree
r
, the polynomial
f_{\gamma}(x)
remains irreducible over
k_{\infty}
.
Hence every root of
f_{\gamma}(x)
in
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
has the same absolute value.
Notice that
c_{r}\cdot A=\text{Nr}(\gamma)\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}
, which gives us that
|c_{i}|_{\infty}\leq q^{\deg{\mathfrak{a}}}
for
i=1,...,r
.
Therefore the possibility of the minimal polynomial
f_{\gamma}\in A[x]
is finite for
(\gamma,b)
running through all pairs in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}
.

On the other hand, given
\vec{c}:=(c_{1},...,c_{r})\in A^{r}
,
let

f_{\vec{c}}(x):=x^{r}-c_{1}x^{r-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{r-1}c_{r-1}x+(-1)^{r}c_{r}\quad\in A[x].

Suppose that:

(i)

c_{r}\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}
;

(ii)

K_{\vec{c}}:=k[x]/(f_{\vec{c}}(x))

is an imaginary field extension over
k
;

(iii)

there exists a
k
-algebra embedding
\phi:K_{\vec{c}}\hookrightarrow D
.

Put

R_{\vec{c}}:=A[x]/(f_{\vec{c}}(x))
,
which is an
A
-order in
K_{\vec{c}}
, and
\bar{x}
is the coset in
R_{\vec{c}}
represented by
x
.
For each
b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
satisfying that

R_{\vec{c}}\subset\phi^{-1}(O_{D,b}),

set
\gamma_{\phi}:=\phi(\bar{x})\in O_{D,b}
.
Then
(\gamma_{\phi},b)
lies in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}
.

Let

{\mathcal{E}}(\vec{c}):=\{b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\mid R_{\vec{c}}\subset\phi^{-1}(O_{D,b})\},

which is invariant by left multiplication of
\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})
and right multiplication of
{\mathcal{K}}
.
For each
(\gamma,b)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
with
f_{\gamma}=f_{\vec{c}}
,
by the Noether-Skolem theorem, there exists
\gamma_{0}\in D^{*}
, which is unique up to left mulltiplication of
\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})
, such that
\gamma_{\phi}=\gamma_{0}\gamma\gamma_{0}^{-1}
.
Then the condition
b^{-1}\gamma b\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}

is equivalent to
\gamma_{\phi}\in O_{D,\gamma_{0}b}
.
Hence we have a well-defined bijective map

D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}\longrightarrow\coprod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\vec{c} \in A^r:\ c_r A = \fa,}{K_{\vec{c}}/k \text{ is imaginary}}}\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}(\vec{c})/{\mathcal{K}}

(9.1)

which sends
[\gamma,b]
to
[b]_{\vec{c}}\in\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}(\vec{c})/{\mathcal{K}}

when
f_{\gamma}=f_{\vec{c}}
.

Now, for an
A
-order
R
of
K_{\vec{c}}
with
R_{\vec{c}}\subset R
,
put

{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R):=\{b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\mid\phi^{-1}(O_{D,b})=R\}.

Then

\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}(\vec{c})/{\mathcal{K}}=\coprod_{R:R_{\vec{c}}\subset R}\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}.

(9.2)

Note that for
b\in{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R)
, one has that

k(\gamma_{\phi})^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1}=\phi(K_{\vec{c}})^{*}\cap O_{D,b}^{*}=\phi(R^{*}).

Therefore by the equation (
8.5
) and Proposition
A.9
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{[\gamma,b]\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{\#({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times})}{\#(k(\gamma)^{*}\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\vec{c} \in A^r:\ c_r A = \fa,}{K_{\vec{c}} \text{ is imag.}}}\sum_{R:R_{\vec{c}}\subset R}\frac{\#\big(\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\big)}{\#(R^{*}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\vec{c} \in A^r:\ c_r A = \fa,}{K_{\vec{c}} \text{ is imag}}}H^{D}(\vec{c}),

where
H^{D}(\vec{c})
is the
modified Hurwitz class number
with respect to
D
introduced in Definition
A.8
.

Finally,
set

H^{D}(0):=\#\text{Pic}(A)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\zeta_{k}(-i)\cdot\prod_{v\in{\bf Ram}\cup\{\infty\}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-q_{v}^{i}).

(9.3)

As
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}/{\mathcal{K}}
is in bijection with
\{c\in A\mid c^{r}\cdot A={\mathfrak{a}}\}\times D^{*}\backslash D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/{\mathcal{K}}
, which is finite,
by Proposition
8.9
and Remark
8.8
(1) one gets

\sum_{(\gamma,b)\in D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\circ}/{\mathcal{K}}}i(\gamma,b)=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{c \in A}{c^r A = \fa}}H^{D}(0).

Therefore Proposition
8.12
leads to the following class number relation:

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
r
be a prime number distinct from the characteristic of
k
. For each nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of
A
, we have

i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\frac{r}{q-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\vec{c} \in A^r:\ c_r A = \fa,}{K_{\vec{c}} \text{\rm\ is imag.}}}H^{D}(\vec{c})+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{c \in A}{c^r \cdot A = \fa}}H^{D}(0)\right).

Remark 9.2
.

Suppose
r=2
and the characteristic of
k
is odd.
Given
a\in A
, we may denote by
a\prec 0
if
k(\sqrt{a})
is an imaginary quadratic field extension over
k
.
For such
a
, we let

H^{D}(a):=H^{D}((0,-a)).

Then Theorem
9.1
can be reformulate as the following:
for each nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of

, we have

i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathfrak{a}}})=\frac{2}{q-1}\cdot\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{t,a \in A}{aA = \fa,\ t^2-4a\preceq 0}}H^{D}(t^{2}-4a).

Furthermore, set

\text{vol}({\mathcal{Z}}):=2\cdot\#{\rm Pic}(A)\cdot(q_{\infty}-1)\cdot\zeta_{k}(-1)\cdot\prod_{v\in{\bf Ram}}(1-q_{v}).

By employing the “Weil representation” as illustrated in
[
16
, Sec. 3]
(or alternatively, by combining the work of
[
6
]
with the relations among Hurwitz class numbers established in
[
17
, Sec. 2]
), we are able to construct a “harmonic” theta series on

whose nonzero (resp. constant) Fourier coefficients come from the intersection numbers
i({\mathcal{Z}}\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}})
for nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of
A
(resp.

).
Although the approach in
[
16
]
seems not applicable for

Appendix A
Eichler’s theory of optimal embeddings

Recall that
D
is a division algebra over
k
with
[D:k]=r^{2}
.
Here we review Eichler’s theory of optimal embeddings from an
A
-order
R
of an imaginary extension
K
over
k
into the division algebra
D
.
For simpliciy, we always assume that
r
is a prime number in this section, which is sufficient for our purpose.

A.1
Local theory

Fix a place
v
of
k
with
v\neq\infty
,
let

K_{v}:=K\otimes_{k}k_{v},\quad D_{v}:=D\otimes_{k}k_{v},\quad O_{D,v}:=O_{D}\otimes_{A}{\mathcal{O}}_{v}.

As
r
is a prime number, either
D_{v}
is division or
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
.
Thus there always exists an embedding from
K_{v}
into
D_{v}
when either
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
or
K_{v}
is a field (see
[
35
, Theorem 1.1 (1)]
).
Fix an embedding
\phi_{v}:K_{v}\hookrightarrow D_{v}
.
For each
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
-order
R_{v}
in
K_{v}
, let

{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v}):=\{b_{v}\in D_{v}^{*}\mid\phi_{v}^{-1}(bO_{D,v}b^{-1})=R_{v}\}.

We shall determine the finiteness of the double coset space

\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v},

where
{\mathcal{K}}_{v}=O_{D,v}^{*}
, the
v
-component of
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose
D_{v}
is division and
K_{v}
is a field.
Then

\displaystyle\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v}):=\begin{cases}r,&\text{ if $v$ is unramified in $K_{v}$ and $R_{v}$ is maximal in $K_{v}$,}\\
1,&\text{ if $v$ is ramified in $K_{v}$ and $R_{v}$ is maximal in $K_{v}$,}\\
0,&\text{ if $R_{v}$ is not maximal in $K_{v}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

When
D_{v}
is division and
K_{v}
is a field, one knows that
O_{D,v}
is the unique maximal
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
-order in
D_{v}
, for which
b_{v}O_{D,v}b_{v}^{-1}=O_{D,v}
.
Moreover, every element in
D_{v}
which is integral over
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
is contained in
O_{D,v}
.
Therefore for every
b_{v}\in D_{v}^{*}
,

\phi_{v}^{-1}(b_{v}O_{D,v}b_{v}^{-1})=\phi_{v}^{-1}(O_{D,v})=R_{K,v},\quad\text{the maximal ${\mathcal{O}}_{v}$-order in $K_{v}$}.

Thus

\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)=0

if
R_{v}
is not maximal in
K_{v}
.

When
R_{v}=R_{K,v}
, we get that

{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})=D_{v}^{*}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)

\displaystyle=\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash D_{v}^{*}/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}r,&\text{if $v$ is unramified in $K_{v}$}\\
1,&\text{if $v$ is (totally) ramified in $K_{v}$.}\end{cases}

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
.
Then the cardinality of
\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}
is finite and positive.
In particular, if
R_{v}
is maximal in
K_{v}
, then

\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)=1.

Proof.

Under the isomorphism
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
, we may identify
O_{D,v}
with
\text{Mat}_{r}({\mathcal{O}}_{v})
without loss of generality.
Let
V=k_{v}^{r}
, which is equipped with a left module structure over
{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})=\text{End}_{k_{v}}(V)
.
An
R_{v}
-lattice
L
is a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
-submodule of
V
which is invariant under the multiplication by
\phi_{v}(R_{v})
, and
L
is
optimal
if
\phi_{v}(R_{v})=\phi_{v}(K_{v})\cap\text{End}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{v}}(L)
.
Two
R_{v}
-lattices
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are isomorphic if there exists
b\in\text{Aut}_{k_{v}}(V)
so that
bL_{1}=L_{2}
, and

b\big(\phi_{v}(\alpha)\lambda\big)=\phi_{v}(\alpha)\cdot b(\lambda),\quad\forall\alpha\in K_{v},\ \lambda\in L_{1}.

Then
b\in\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})
, and we have a bijection

\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\{\text{isomorphism classes of optimal ${\mathcal{O}}_{v}$-lattices in $V$}\}

which is induced by sending
b
to the optimal
R_{v}
-lattice
b\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{v}^{r}
for every
b\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})
.
Thus it suffices to count the isomorphism classes of optimal
R_{v}
-lattices in
V
.

Notice that when
R_{v}
is maximal in
K_{v}
, every
R_{v}
-lattice in
L
must be free of rank one over
R_{v}
.
Hence there is only one isomorhism class.
In general, for each optimal
R_{v}
-lattice
L
in
K_{v}
, consider
\tilde{L}:=\phi_{v}(R_{K,v})\cdot L\subset V
, which is an
R_{K,v}
-lattice.
On the other hand, there exists
c_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}^{c_{v}}\cdot R_{K,v}\subset R_{v}
(the smallest choice of ‘
c_{v}
’ refers to the “conductor” of
R_{v}
), whence
{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}^{c_{v}}\cdot\tilde{L}\subset L
.
Since
\tilde{L}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}^{c_{v}}\tilde{L}
has finite cardinality, we obtain that the isomorphism classes of
R_{v}
-lattices in
V
is finite (and bounded by
\#(R_{K,v}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}^{c_{v}}R_{K,v})
).
∎

Remark A.3
.

Suppose
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
. Let
\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v})
be the cardinality of the isomorphism classes of optimal
R_{v}
-lattices in
V
.
Then the above proof shows that
\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v})
is finite positive, and
\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v})=1
for almost all
v
.
In particular,

\#\big(\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)=\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v}).

A.2
Global theory and modified Hurwitz class numbers

First, the condition for the existence of a
k
-algebra embedding from
K
into
D
is determined by the following.

Theorem A.4
.

(See
[
32
, Proposition A.1]
) Let
D
be a central simple algebra of degree
r
over
k
and
K
is a field extension of degree
r
over
k
.
Then the local-global principle holds, i.e.
K
can be embedded into
D
if and only if
K_{v}
can be embedded into
D_{v}
for every place
v
of
K
.

Now, let
D
be a division algebra over
k
and
r
is a prime number.
Suppose an embedding
\phi:K\hookrightarrow D
exists, which corresponds to embeddings
\phi_{v}:K_{v}\hookrightarrow D_{v}
for all finite places
v
of
k
.
Let
R
be an
A
-order of
K
.
Put

{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R):=\{b\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\mid\phi^{-1}(O_{D,b})=R\}.

Here
O_{D,b}
is defined in the beginning of Section
9
.
We shall express the cardinality of the double coset space
\phi(K^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}
as “modified class number” of
R
.

For each finite place
v
of
k
, let
R_{v}:=R\otimes_{A}{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
.
For
b=(b_{v})_{v\neq\infty}\in D^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
, one has that

b\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)

if and only if
b_{v}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi_{v}}^{o}(R_{v})
for every
v\neq\infty
.
Define
K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}):=(K\otimes_{k}{\mathbb{A}}_{f})^{*}
.
Then Lemma
A.2
ensures that

\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}=\prod_{v}\phi_{v}(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi,v}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}.

On the other hand,
let
h(R)
be the class number of
R
, i.e.

h(R):=\#(K^{*}\backslash K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/\widehat{R}^{*}),\quad\text{ where $\widehat{R}:=\prod_{v\neq\infty}R_{v}$}.

We have the following.

Lemma A.5
.

Under the canonical surjective map

\phi(K^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\longrightarrow\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}},

the cardinality of each fiber is equal to
h(R)
.

Proof.

Given
b\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)
, the fiber of
b
can be identified with the double coset space

\phi(K^{*})\Big\backslash\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\Big/\big(\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1}\big),

and the condition of
b
lying in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)
implies that

\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\cap b{\mathcal{K}}b^{-1}=\phi(\widehat{R}^{*}).

Therefore the result follows.
∎

Consequently, let
h^{D}(R)
be the following
modified class number
(with respect to
D
):

h^{D}(R):=h(R)\cdot\prod_{v\neq\infty}\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v}),

where
\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v})
is the introduced in Lemma
A.1
when
D_{v}
is division and Remark
A.3
when
D_{v}\cong{\mathbb{M}}_{r}(k_{v})
.
We then conclude that:

Proposition A.6
.

The double coset space
\phi(K^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}
is a finite set, and its cardinality is equal to
h^{D}(R)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.2
and Remark
A.3
, we get

\#\big(\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\big)=\prod_{v}\big(\phi(K_{v}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi,v}(R_{v})/{\mathcal{K}}_{v}\big)=\prod_{v}\mu_{v}^{D}(R_{v})\quad<\infty.

Therefore Lemma
A.5
ensures the finiteness of

\phi(K^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}
, and

\#\big(\phi(K^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\big)=h(R)\cdot\#\big(\phi(K^{*}({\mathbb{A}}_{f}))\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}^{o}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\big)=h^{D}(R)

as desired.
∎

Remark A.7
.

Given a non-zero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of
A
so that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is not a component of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{a}}
,
Then Proposition
A.6
, the equations (
9.1
) and (
9.2
) guarantee the finiteness of
D^{*}\backslash{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{K}}

Definition A.8
.

Given
\vec{c}\in A^{r}
satisfying that
K_{\vec{c}}
is imaginary,
the
modified Hurwitz class number of
R_{\vec{c}}
(with respect to
D
) is given by

H^{D}(\vec{c}):=\sum_{R:R_{\vec{c}}\subset R}\frac{h^{D}(R)}{\#(R^{*}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})}.

We finally arrive at:

Proposition A.9
.

\sum_{R:R_{\vec{c}}\subset R}\frac{\#\big(\phi(K_{\vec{c}}^{*})\backslash{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{\phi}(R)/{\mathcal{K}}\big)}{\#(R^{*}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*})}=H^{D}(\vec{c}).

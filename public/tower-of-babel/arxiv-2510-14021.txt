Quantum Damping of Cosmological Shear: A New Prediction from Loop Quantum Cosmologies
Wen-Cong Gan, Leila L. Graef, Rudnei O. Ramos, Gustavo S. Vicente, Anzhong Wang

Abstract
We analyze the dynamics of the Bianchi I universe in modified loop quantum cosmology (Model I, or mLQC-I), uncovering a robust mechanism for isotropization. As in the standard LQC, the classical singularities are resolved by quantum bounce. Remarkably, mLQC-I exhibits a distinctive feature: following the bounce, the shear is dynamically suppressed and decays rapidly to zero within the deep quantum regime. This occurs independently of the collapsing matter fields, leading to a natural quantum isotropization. Consequently, the three spatial directions expand rapidly to macroscopic scales, producing a homogeneous and isotropic universe directly from the quantum epoch without fine-tuning. Our findings demonstrate that mLQC-I not only resolves singularities but also provides a more effective pathway for suppressing anisotropies than other models, thereby reinforcing its viability as a description of the early universe.



I
Introduction

Loop quantum gravity (LQG) is one of the leading non-perturbative and background-independent approaches in the search for a consistent quantum theory of gravity
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 2004
;
Thiemann 2007
;
Rovelli and Vidotto 2014
. Over the past decades, significant progress has been achieved in consolidating its mathematical and conceptual foundations. Beyond the theoretical advances, LQG has matured to the point of enabling concrete and testable predictions, particularly in cosmology through its symmetry-reduced formulation, loop quantum cosmology (LQC)
Ashtekar and Singh 2011
;
Agulló et al. 2023
;
Li and Singh 2024
. One of the most striking results of LQC is the resolution of the big bang singularity, which is replaced by a non-singular quantum bounce occurring at Planckian densities. At low curvature scales, where quantum geometric effects become negligible, General Relativity (GR) is recovered as an effective description of spacetime. However, different quantization prescriptions within LQG can lead to distinct effective dynamics, where novel features of the quantum bounce and post-bounce evolution can be explored.

Initially, two foundational assumptions were adopted within the standard LQC. The first is the
supermini-space
assumption, which imposes homogeneity and isotropy at the outset and then constructs the corresponding classical Hamiltonian prior to quantization. The second is the use of the classical relation between the Euclidean and Lorentzian terms
Ashtekar and Singh 2011
;
Agulló et al. 2023
;
Li and Singh 2024
, which allows the total Hamiltonian to be expressed solely in terms of the Euclidean part, so the quantization of the total Hamiltonian reduces to quantize only the Euclidean part. However, in full LQG the Euclidean and Lorentzian terms are quantized differently, and in general the operations of symmetry reduction and quantization do not commute
Ashtekar and Lewandowski 2004
;
Thiemann 2007
;
Rovelli and Vidotto 2014
. To address these issues, an alternative quantization procedure more faithful to the LQG principles was proposed in
Yang et al. 2009
, where the Euclidean and Lorentzian terms are quantized separately by rigorously following the LQG schemes. This leads to an effective Hamiltonian that defines a distinct model, now known as a modified loop quantum cosmology (Model I, or mLQC-I)
Li et al. 2021
. A notable feature of mLQC-I is that, unlike other modified LQC models, it can be derived from both a bottom-up construction
Yang et al. 2009
;
Assanioussi et al. 2018
and a top-down approach starting directly from LQG
Dapor and Liegener 2018
;
Han and Liu 2022
;
Han and Liu 2024
, making it a particularly compelling model within the LQG framework. Although the main qualitative characteristics of LQC, such as the replacement of the big bang by a quantum bounce, remain robust, mLQC-I shows markedly different dynamics in the contracting phase, exhibiting asymmetric evolution around the quantum bounce
Li et al. 2018a
;
Li et al. 2018b
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Saini and Singh 2019a
;
Saini and Singh 2019b
;
Saeed et al. 2024
. In this context, it is natural to extend the analysis beyond the isotropic setting and investigate large and non-perturbative anisotropic spacetimes, such as the Bianchi I universe, in order to assess the robustness of mLQC-I dynamics against directional shear and to explore its implications for isotropization in the early universe. Bianchi spacetimes classify all homogeneous 3-geometries into nine
types, with the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) models as special, highly symmetric cases - making Bianchi models a more natural and
likely starting point for early-universe cosmology. Preliminary studies within the framework of mLQC-I have been already initiated in
Garcia-Quismondo and Mena Marugan 2020
;
García-Quismondo and Mena Marugán 2019
, and among various interesting and important results is the effective Hamiltonian for the Bianchi I universe.

In the broader landscape of cosmological bounce models, including matter and ekpyrotic bounces
Lehners 2008

Battefeld and Peter 2015

Brandenberger and Peter 2017

Ashtekar and Singh 2011

Agulló et al. 2023

\Sigma^{2}/a^{6}

\Sigma

This scaling causes anisotropies to grow more rapidly than standard matter or radiation components, effectively acting like a stiff fluid. As a consequence, unless suitably suppressed, anisotropies can dominate the dynamics and leave large imprints on the post-bounce universe, at odds with observational constraints. Addressing this issue and uncovering mechanisms that tame anisotropies across the bounce, thus remain one of the central open problems in such scenarios. Classically, this difficulty is tied to the Belinskii–Khalatnikov–Lifshitz (BKL) conjecture
Belinsky et al. 1970
, which asserts that as a space-like singularity is approached, time-derivative terms dominate over spatial derivatives, reducing the asymptotic dynamics to a set of ordinary differential equations.
Within this context, the Bianchi I spacetime provides an ideal testing ground: it is the simplest anisotropic generalization of the Friedmann universe and has already been extensively investigated in the framework of LQC

Building on these insights, it becomes essential to explore how anisotropies evolve when the dynamics is governed not by the standard LQC, but by its modified version, mLQC-I, where the quantization of the Hamiltonian more faithfully reflects the full LQG structure.

In this Letter, we investigate the impact of quantum geometric effects of mLQC-I on collapsing Bianchi I trajectories, with particular focus on singularity resolution and anisotropy dynamics. We demonstrate that, for generic initial conditions with non-vanishing shear, the quantum bounce not only replaces the classical singularity but also naturally drives the shear to zero well within the deep quantum regime in the post-bounce phase. Consequently, the universe dynamically isotropizes and evolves into a homogeneous, isotropic and macroscopic state.

III
Shear in loop quantum cosmologies

To understand the role of shear in the Bianchi universe, let us consider time-like geodesics with a unit tangential vector
v^{\mu}
. Then, we have

\nabla_{\nu}v_{\mu}=\left(g_{\mu\nu}+v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\right)\theta/3+\omega_{\mu\nu}+\sigma_{\mu\nu}
,
where

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{3}\left(H_{12}^{2}+H_{23}^{2}+H_{31}^{2}\right)\equiv\frac{6\Sigma^{2}}{a^{6}},

(2)

H_{ij}\equiv H_{i}-H_{j}

H_{i}\equiv\dot{a}_{i}/a_{i}

Before we discuss the behavior of shear in mLQC-I, it is instructive to first consider the LQC case.

V
Conclusions

Through a combination of analytical and numerical methods, this Letter establishes a defining characteristic of the mLQC-I model: the exponential decay of shear directly following the quantum bounce. This universal isotropization mechanism, which operates irrespective of the collapsing matter content, stands in sharp contrast to other cosmological bounce models
Ashtekar and Singh 2011
;
Lehners 2008
;
Battefeld and Peter 2015
;
Brandenberger and Peter 2017
. Our results indicate that the transition to a homogeneous and isotropic universe is an inevitable, rapid outcome in the deep quantum regime, arising as a built-in feature of mLQC-I without necessitating any supplementary processes.

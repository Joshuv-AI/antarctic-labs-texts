Methodological development and validation of the Thai traditional medicine-based depression care program for older adults
Authors: Sunatta Nonghang, Naruwat Pakdee, Ronnachai Poowanna
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article describes a detailed, validated protocol for a Thai traditional medicine (TTM) based care program developed for older adults with moderate depressive symptoms. Developed from TTM principles and contemporary evidence, the 7-week program integrates three modalities sequentially in weekly 60-minute sessions: herbal steam therapy (30 min), followed by combined receptive music therapy (slow-tempo, ∼60 bpm) and lavender aromatherapy oil massage (30 min total). The method underwent rigorous validation, including content validity assessment by experts (CVI=0.89), feasibility testing in a community hospital setting (100% completion rate, no serious adverse events), and reliability testing of outcome measures (Cronbach’s α > 0.70 the Thai Geriatric Depression Scale (TGDS-30) and WHOQOL-BREF). Preliminary application data from a controlled, quasi-experimental application (n = 32) are presented to demonstrate feasibility and methodological performance. Observed improvements in depressive symptoms and quality of life suggest potential utility; however, these findings are not intended to establish clinical efficacy. This work provides a structured and reproducible 7-week integrative intervention combining herbal steam therapy, aromatherapy massage, and music therapy. Detailed materials, herbal formulations, and procedural steps are specified to support implementation in community healthcare settings. The presented data are intended to demonstrate the feasibility and methodological performance of the protocol rather than to establish clinical efficacy.Keywords: Thai traditional medicine protocol, Geriatric depression intervention, Herbal steam therapy, Aromatherapy massage, Integrative mental health care

Background

Depression in older adults is a critical global health challenge that significantly impacts quality of life, functional status, and healthcare utilization, leading to increased morbidity and mortality [1]. In Southeast Asia, Thailand is undergoing a rapid demographic transition toward a "super-aged society", a shift that exacerbates the prevalence of age-related chronic diseases and mental health disorders [2] National data indicate rising psychiatric caseloads among the elderly, driven by multifaceted factors such as biological decline, loss of social roles, isolation, and multimorbidity [3]. In rural community settings such as Khao Wong District, Kalasin Province, where the proportion of older adults exceeds the national average, the burden is compounded by a shortage of specialized mental health professionals. Furthermore, standard care often relies heavily on pharmacotherapy, which poses risks for older adults due to age-related metabolic changes, potential polypharmacy, and adverse drug reactions. Consequently, there is an urgent need for accessible, safe, and culturally appropriate non-pharmacological interventions to complement standard geriatric care [4].

Integrative approaches grounded in traditional medicine offer valuable alternatives that align with the cultural context and lifestyle of Thai older adults [[5], [6], [7]]. Thai traditional medicine (TTM) operates on the holistic principle of balancing the four body elements (Earth, Water, Wind, and Fire) to restore physiological homeostasis. Beyond cultural acceptance, individual TTM modalities possess biological plausibility for managing depression. Herbal steam therapy promotes vasodilation and muscle relaxation, thereby reducing physical tension associated with stress [8]. Aromatherapy massage, specifically using lavender oil, modulates the limbic system to alleviate anxiety and improve sleep quality [9]. Similarly, receptive music therapy has been shown to regulate the autonomic nervous system, enhancing parasympathetic activity [10]. Despite these individual benefits, these therapies are often applied mainly for physical relaxation rather than as a structured mental health intervention.

A significant methodological gap remains in the current literature. While studies on TTM exist, they often investigate modalities in isolation or describe intervention protocols vaguely, failing to specify crucial details such as herbal dosages, steam temperatures, massage pressure intensity, or music tempo [11,12]. This lack of standardization leads to low reproducibility and makes it difficult to verify safety standards, which is particularly critical for vulnerable geriatric populations. Without a clearly defined, step-by-step protocol, healthcare providers in community hospitals face challenges in replicating these interventions effectively [13].

The motivation for presenting this methodology is to bridge this gap by detailing the development, validation, and standardization of the "Thai Traditional Medicine Based Depression Care Program" (TTM-DCP) [13]. This program is not merely a collection of therapies but a synergistic, 7-week structured intervention. It systematically integrates evidence-based modalities: herbal steam therapy [8,14], aromatherapy massage [9] and receptive music therapy [10], into weekly 60-minute sessions. By rigorously defining the procedural steps, herbal formulations, and selection criteria grounded in both fundamental TTM principles [15] and contemporary clinical evidence [16], this article aims to equip researchers and practitioners with a reproducible, and validated blueprint. This ensures that TTM-based mental health services can be implemented consistently and safely across community hospitals, elevating traditional practices to evidence-based medical standards.

Description of protocol

The Thai Traditional Medicine Based Depression Care Program (TTM-DCP) is a structured intervention delivered in weekly 60-minute sessions over seven consecutive weeks. The planned study flow is illustrated in Fig. 1.

1. Participants and setting context

1. Participants and setting context

Flow diagram illustrating participant recruitment, allocation, intervention procedures, and follow-up within the TTM-DCP protocol.

The method is designed for older adults (aged 60+) diagnosed with mild to moderate depression screened using the Thai Geriatric Depression Scale (TGDS-30), with scores ranging from 19 - 24 indicating moderate depressive symptoms. The TGDS-30 is a 30-item self-report instrument with total scores ranging from 0 to 30, where higher scores indicate greater severity of depressive symptoms.

The protocol was developed and tested at Khao Wong Hospital, a community healthcare facility in Kalasin Province, Thailand. To demonstrate the target population context for this method, Table 1 summarizes the demographic and clinical characteristics of participants involved in the protocol validation phase. Participants were predominantly female (68.8%), with a mean age of approximately 69 years, reflecting a typical profile of older adults in rural Thai community settings.

3. Control condition protocol (for comparative validation)

3. Control condition protocol (for comparative validation)

To provide a comparative reference for the application of the protocol, a control protocol was established to serve as a comparative baseline. The control group received receptive music therapy exclusively, administered weekly for 30 min over a seven-week period. To ensure comparability, the music selection (instrumental, slow-tempo ∼60 bpm) and environmental setting were identical to those used in the intervention group, but without the herbal steam and aromatherapy massage components. This design enables a preliminary comparison between the integrated TTM-DCP and a standard relaxation intervention; however, differences in total treatment duration and therapeutic exposure between groups should be considered when interpreting the results [10,[18], [19], [20], [21]].

Protocol validation

Psychometric testing was performed to verify the reliability of the outcome measures within the target population of this study. It should be emphasized that the following data are presented to support the validation, feasibility, applicability of the protocol, and are not intended to evaluate clinical efficacy:

1. Content validity and feasibility

1. Content validity and feasibility

•Content validity: A multidisciplinary panel of three experts comprising specialists in Thai traditional medicine, geriatric nursing, and mental health evaluated the protocol’s structure, coherence, and relevance. The item-level content validity index (I-CVI) was calculated, yielding an average score of 0.89. This value exceeds the standard threshold of 0.80, confirming excellent expert consensus on the method's appropriateness for older adults [22].

Limitations

This method requires specific resources, including a controlled steam room/cabinet and licensed Thai traditional medicine practitioners for massage administration, which may limit its scalability in resource-constrained settings. The protocol was validated in a specific rural Thai context; cultural acceptability and logistical feasibility may vary in different populations or urban settings. Furthermore, differences in treatment duration and therapeutic exposure between the intervention and comparison conditions should be considered when interpreting the findings. The presented data are intended for methodological demonstration, and further well-designed studies are required to evaluate clinical effectiveness.

This study was not designed as a randomized controlled trial to assess efficacy; therefore, future well-designed randomized controlled trials are required to confirm the clinical effectiveness of the protocol.

Enrichment of rhenium, uranium and tantalum in jurassic coal from yili basin, xinjiang - evidence from mineralogy and geochemistry
Authors: Hongyang Bai, Wenfeng Wang, Shuo Feng, Maomao Wang, Zhigang Du, Ning Li, Erqiang Li, Longhao Ma, Zhenkang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, detailed geochemical and mineralogical investigations were conducted on the No.12 coal from the Early-Middle Jurassic in the Yili Basin. The results indicate the following: (a) The No.12 coal from the southern Yili Basin exhibits super-elevated enrichment (100 < concentration coefficient, CC) of Re(394.02ng/g), U(285.99 µg/g), and Ta(120.51 µg/g); high enrichment (10 < CC < 100) of Mo(29.11 µg/g), Se(28.51 µg/g) and Hf(12.54 µg/g); enrichment (5 < CC < 10) of Zn (7.86 µg/g)and Be (7.62 µg/g); slight enrichment (2 < CC < 5) of Cs (4.78 µg/g), Rb (4.75 µg/g), V (4.64 µg/g), Cr (4.51 µg/g), Co (4.01 µg/g), Ni (3.67 µg/g), W (3.65 µg/g), Pb (3.35 µg/g), Ge (2.96 µg/g), Cd (2.96 µg/g), F (2.70 µg/g), Cu(2.64 µg/g) Tl(2.53 µg/g), In(2.46 µg/g), Ga(2.41 µg/g), Sb(2.32 µg/g), Zr(2.31 µg/g), Sc(2.28 µg/g), and Th(2.18 µg/g); normal abundance (0.5 < CC < 2) of As(1.93 µg/g), Nb(1.75 µg/g), Li(1.42 µg/g), Hg(1.30 µg/g), Ba(1.12 µg/g), Sr(0.84 µg/g), and B(0.67 µg/g); and depletion(CC < 0.5) of Bi (0.40 µg/g). Excluding volatilization during combustion, U, Re, and Ta in the coal ash have certain resource potential to some extent. (b) The most common minerals in the No.12 coal are quartz, kaolinite, and pyrite. Additionally, some uncommon minerals, such as rutile, monazite, barite, clausthalite, ferroselite, pyrrhotite, coffinite, uraninite, sphalerite, and Fe-sulfate, were identified in the coal with super-elevated U content (especially when the U content exceeds 1000 µg/g). Among these minerals, clausthalite, ferroselite, and uraninite can control the enrichment of inorganic U and Se. (c) The enrichment of U-Re-Se-Mo-Ta-Hf in the No.12 coal is probably attributed to the infiltration of U-bearing oxidized solutions and the input of detritus containing critical metal elements from the sedimentary provenance.Keywords: Uranium, Critical metal, Geochemistry, Mineral, Yili BasinSubject terms: Biogeochemistry, Environmental sciences, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

In the context of the increasing shortage of global mineral resources, the increasingly complex situation for global economy, and the growing demand for high-tech and emerging industries, the future competition of international mineral resources and high-tech will, to a large extent, focus on the control of critical metals1. Therefore, the strategic position of critical minerals will become more and more important. With the depletion of traditional endogenous critical metal resources, the exogenous critical metal in coal will become a new important source. In recent decades, a number of critical metals have been found in coal around the world, such as REE (rare earth elements), Li (lithium), Ga (gallium), Ge (germanium), U (uranium), Re (rhenium), Se (selenium), Mo (molybdenum), Nb (niobium), Ta (tantalum), Zr (zirconium), and Hf (hafnium)2–15. These critical metals in coal are sometimes at very high levels, even exceeding industrial grade. For example, the content of Ga, Ge and U in coal can be as high as 170, 2523 µg/g and 7207 µg/g, etc. Meanwhile, due to the fact that a considerable amount of coal is used for power generation through combustion in China‘s power plants, these critical metals aforementioned will accumulate further in the coal ash, significantly reducing the cost of exploitation and making the industrial utilization of them possible16–20. For instancd, REE in the coal ash can be as high as 1% to 2%, Ga in coal has achieved an annual industrial production, Ge in coal has accounted for half of the world’s total Ge output6,20.

Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, the most important coal production base in western China, possess a large amount of untapped coal resources, which is predicted to reach 2.19 trillion tons, accounting for about 40% of China’s total predicted coal resources21. Furthermore, the levels of some critical metals in some areas of Xinjiang can be as high as 10000ppm.In addition, Re, Se, and other critical metals in coal are also quite enriched22,23.However, the occurrence state of these critical metal elements in coal is relatively complex and still of ambiguity. For instance, U in coal has both inorganic and organic affinity, e.g., combined with organic matter, occurring in minerals as isomorphism, and adsorbed by clay minerals24–27; whereas Re in coal also has strong organic affinity, but can be found in clay minerals (illite/smectite formation), carbonate minerals, and secondary sulfates minerals as well25; Se in coal is also considered to have organic affinity, which can replace sulfur in coal and combine with organic matter, and can exist in clay minerals and carbonate minerals28–30. On the other hand, the understanding of the enrichment mechanisms of the aforementioned metals in coal is not yet unified, which severely restricts the joint development and utilization of them and coal, as well as their impact on the environment.

In this article, the geochemical and mineralogical characteristics of No.12 coal from Honghaigou coalfield, Yili basin have been studied in detail in order to find out the possible controlling factors in abnormal elemental enrichments.

Geological Setting

The Yili Basin, an intermontane coal-bearing basin, is situated in the northwest of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, China and borders Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan to the west. The basin is located between the South Tianshan orogenic belt and the North Tianshan orogenic belt, and its outline is roughly a triangle wide to the west and narrow to the east31–35. In terms of tectonic units, the Yili basin is an intermountain basin that belongs to the Yili–Middle Tianshan microblock, which is a continental depression basin (local fault depression) formed under the north–south counter extrusion of the Tarim plate and the Kazakhstan plate, among which there are Yining depression, Gongnaisi depression, Zhaosu depression, and Tekesi depression. To the north, middle, and the south of the basin is Mount Keguqin, Chabuchar, and Yishijilike, respectively31,36. The study area here was the Yining depression in the basin, namely the Yili basin in a narrow sense, whose tectonic position and geological situation are shown in Fig. 1.

(a) Location and (b) geological sketch map of Yili Basin (after 29).

The Jurassic strata, the main coal-bearing strata in the basin, which are consisted of the Shuixigou Group of Middle–Lower Jurassic and the Aiweigou Group of Upper Jurassic. The Shuixigou Group, a set of gray continental coal-bearing clastic rock series, which is composed of the Badaowan, Sangonghe, and Xishanyao Formations from bottom to top34. The Shuixigou Group has attracted much attention because of U enrichment. A series of U deposits have been discovered in the southern of the basin, and several U spots in the northern. The main ore-bearing beds are Sangonghe and Xishanyao Formations31,32,36.

Sample collection and analytical methods

On the basis of coal seam distribution, macroscopic coal petrological features, field mineralization and preliminary geochemical anomalies, a total of twenty-seven samples, including seventeen coal samples, one parting sample, six roof samples and four floor samples were collected in four drillholes of No.12 coal from Honghaigou coalfield in the south of the Yili basin(Figure 2). Sampling carried out strictly in accordance with China’s GB/T482-2008 and GB/T19222-200337,38, and the drill samples were continuously sampled at 10 cm intervals with the mass not less than 150 g. The samples were packed into bags and sealed immediately. After that, the samples were preprocessed. First of all, grinding was implemented with an electromagnetic ore crusher supplemented with agate mortar; then, screening was performed according to different test requirements; in the end, drying was implemented by vacuum oven at a constant temperature of 70 °C to get constant weight for use.

(a) Lithological column of the study area and (b) sampling scheme.

Proximate analysis and total sulfur determination of 11 coal samples was conducted according to the ASTM standards D3173-11 (2011), D3174-11 (2011), D3175-11 (2011), and D3177-02(2002)39–42. Petrographic examination of 8 coal samples, on basis of ICCP43–45 was conducted on epoxy-bound particulate pellets by Leica DM4P/DM 2700P microscopes equipped with 50×, oil-immersion, and reflected light objectives. Vitrinite reflectance of 2 coal samples were measured using a photomultiplier tube in the path of incident light passing through a 546 nm band filter, and calibrated against standard samples with known reflectance.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) of 4 coal samples was performed by D8 Adavance powder diffractometer (produced by Bruker, Germany, with a Ni-filtered, Cu-Kα radiation, and a scintillation detector), with a test voltage of 40 kV, a current of 30 mA, a test radius of 250 mm, and a test angle of 3º−70º, to determine mineral species. Scanning electron microscopy in conjunction with an energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (SEM + EDS) was implemented on 3 polished coal samples for mineralogical analysis.

X-ray fluorescence (XRF) of 7 coal samples was conducted with S8 Tiger (whose sequential scanning voltage, current, and power is 60 kV, 170 mA, and 4 kW, respectively) to determine the oxides of major elements, including SiO2, TiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3, MgO, CaO, MnO, and K2O. Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (ICP-MS), combined with an XGY1011A atomic fluorescence photometer (arsenic, selenium, mercury), a WP-1-meter planar grating spectrograph (boron), and a PXSJ-216 ionometer (fluorine), was used to determine 35 of trace elements of the total 27 samples. The sample digestion process followed methodology described in Dai et al46..

Geochemistry

The main oxide in No.12 coal of Honghaigou coalfield is SiO2, accounting for 19.83%, followed by Al2O3, CaO and Fe2O3, accounting for 6.43%, 3.56%, and 2.17%, and K2O, MgO, TiO2, and MnO are of 0.84%, 0.50%, 0.34%, and 0.02%, respectively. Cluster analysis is a common statistical research method for determining mode of occurrence of elements in coal52–55. R-type cluster analysis, inter-group linkage method, Pearson correlation have been applied to find out the symbiotic combination relationship and occurrence characteristics of the 45 elements (parameter) in coal. Consequencely, 6 groups were obtained at the level of 12.5 similar distances (Fig. 5).

Cluster analysis dendrogram of No.12 coal.

Group 1, including Ta, W, Hf, Be, Zn, U, Re, Tl, Ni, Co, Se, Cd, V, Sr, Sc, Sb, and Ge, are mainly sulfophilic elements or high valence elements and usually sensitive to redox conditions. The affinities of these elements in coal is complex; some may have both organic and inorganic affinity, e.g., U and Ge, and some are typical high field strength variable element, e.g., Ta, Hf, and U. Previously, high U-Re-Se combinations and Nb-Ta-Zr-Hf-U-REE combinations have been reported in high U coal in southwest China. Cobalt and Ni are iron group elements, which can replace iron to form sulfate. Selenium, Sb, Sr, Sc, Zn, Cd, and W may be associated with sulfide or sulfate56.

Group 2, including Zr, Th, Pb, and Ga, mainly exist in oxides or the oxides of Ti, Al, Si, K. They can be associated with quartz, feldspar, clay minerals, or certain accessory minerals56.

Group 3: including As, In, Cr, Ba, K2O, Rb, Cu, Cs, A,d, SiO2, Al2O3, TiO2, Nb, Li, Be, and F, these elements may mainly exist in sulfide, sulfate and quartz, feldspar, clay minerals, and other silicate minerals. They could often be replaced by elements in in Group 2 in lower content to form homogeneous minerals56.

Group 4, including MnO, Fe2O3, S,t, Ca, and Hg, may exist as sulfide or sulfate; a large amount of pyrite; and a small amount of gypsum found in the coal were the obvious evidence. In addition, Mn could also be observed in clay minerals and carbonate minerals in coal.

Group 5, only MgO, may be related to a small amount of calcite in coal, because it might replace the Ca in calcite to form homogeneous mineral.

Group 6, only Mo, may mainly exist in the organic bound state in coal, because molybdenum sulfide minerals such as molybdenite were not found in this study.

In this study, principal component analysis based on SPSS16.0 was conducted for factor analysis, in order to reveal the internal relations between elements and coal parameter. The rotated factor loading matrix was shown in Table 5. The results show that 91% of the total variance can be explained by 4 main factors. In each factor, elements/parameters with factor loadings greater than 0.45 are considered likely to have the similar occurrence state.

Principal component 1 (F1), including In, Cs, Cu, Rb, As, K2O, Ba, Cr, A,d, Bi, Al2O3, Li, SiO2, S,t, F, Nb, Fe2O3, and TiO2, are the main ash-forming elements and mainly occur in minerals. Namely, most of them may exist mainly in oxide and aluminosilicate, such as quartz, rutile, clay minerals and feldspar. Among them, As and S,t are generally considered to be sulfophilic elements, they may be related to the large amount of pyrite present in coal. In addition, arsenic can be present in the oxidized form of arsenic, such as arsenic trioxide.

Principal component 2 (F2), including Ni, Re, Tl, Zn, Cd, V, Co, U, Se, W, Ta, Hf, Be, Sr, Sb, Sc, and Pb (basically consistent with group 1 in cluster analysis), are mainly high valence elements sensitive to redox conditions, sulfophilic elements and some oxyphilic element. For example, U and Re in the sample may occur mainly in uranite and pitchblende, the Zn may be related to sphalerite, Cd, V, Co, Se, W,Pb may be associated with sulfides or sulfates, while other elements may be associated with clay minerals.

Principal component 3 (F3), including Hg, Ca, B, and MnO (in accordance with group 4 in cluster analysis), may exist as sulfides or sulfate. The observed gypsum, barite, and pyrite minerals in the sample are good evidence.

Principal component 4 (F4), including Li, SiO2, F, Nb, Zr, Th, TiO2, and Pb, are common ash-forming elements in coal and probably exist mainly in oxide, clay minerals or other silicate, e.g., quartz, rutile, and kaolinite, etc.

In addition, the loading factor of Mo have no obvious high value, indicating that its state of occurrence may be relatively complex.

ETV: Total variance explained.

The enrichment coefficients (CC = Sample/WLRC) of rare earth elements (REY) are presented in Fig. 6. All elements show CC values > 1, indicating overall enrichment relative to the WLRC baseline. Most elements (Y, La, Ce, Nd, Sm, Eu, Gd, Tb, Dy, Ho, Er, Yb, Lu) fall into the 2 < CC < 5 range, with Y and Er exhibiting the highest enrichment (CC ≈ 4.6 and 4.1, respectively). Only Pr and Tm show mild enrichment (0.5 < CC < 2). This pattern suggests a consistent REY enrichment signature in the coal, with heavy REY elements showing particularly elevated concentrations compared to global low-rank coal averages.

The UCC-normalized REY distribution patterns of the coal samples are illustrated in Fig. 6. Most samples exhibit flat to slightly right-inclined patterns, with REY concentrations generally close to or slightly above the UCC baseline. Sample ZKL2916-2 shows the highest REY enrichment, with values 5–9 times the UCC average. A few samples (e.g., ZK049-3) display left-inclined patterns with light REY depletion. All samples lack significant Eu or Ce anomalies, indicating consistent REY fractionation and depositional conditions.

Minerals

Optical microscope, XRD, and SEM-EDS made clear that minerals in coal mainly include quartz, kaolinite, pyrite, and gypsum (Figs. 7 and 8). In coal samples with super-elevated U (especially U content greater than 1000 µg/g), some uncommon minerals can be seen, e.g., rutile, monazite, barite, clausthalite, ferroselite, pyrrhotite, coffinite, uranite, sphalerite, and Fe-sulfate (Fig. 8).

Monazite in No.12 coal is subcircular granular single crystal, which is associated with clay minerals and quartz (Fig. 9a), indicating that monazite may have been transported from the source area during synsedimentary period. Moderate-acidic magmatic rocks (highly uranium granitic or volcanic rocks, such as tuff) widely located at the basin margin may be the main parent rock. SEM-EDS data shows that monazite contains Ce, La, Nd and other LREE, suggesting it may be one of the important carrier minerals of LREE in the studied samples.

Barite, the sulfate mineral of Ba that often be found in cryogenic hydrothermal deposits or sometimes in sedimentary rocks, always associated with galena and sphalerite57. The barites in No.12 coal were of poor crystallinity, most of which were embedded in organic matter in clumps or nodules, and some were embedded in the edge of organic components (Fig. 9b), indicating that it is of epigenetic genesis. In addition, SEM observations showed that barites sometimes associated with Fe-sulfate. Considering that [SO4] 2- is the highest valence state of sulfur and usually formed in the superficial or oxidized atmosphere, the sulfate of barite and iron in coal may be the product of metasomatism of a low-temperature hydrothermal fluid enriched with oxygen in the same period57. SEM-EDS data shows that there is a trace of Co in barite, which may be the result of homogeneous substitution.

Clausthalite, belongs to the galena mineral group, has similar structure to galena and often developed in middle-low-temperature hydrothermal deposits and associated with uraninite in sedimentary uranium deposits57. Clausthalite in No.12 coal often be fine granular aggregate (Fig. 9c) that associated with clay minerals, suggesting that it could be derived together with detritic clay mineral matrixes. The atomic ratio of Se/Pb is 1.21, slightly higher than the theoretical value, which may be related to the extraordinary enrichment of Se in No.12 coal.

Ferroselite, a typical medium-low-temperature hydrothermal mineral, which can be seen in hydrothermal deposits and black shale after magmatic stage58. For Se and U are both redox-sensitive variable valence elements, ferroselite is often generated in sedimentary U deposits, such as those in Tuva basin59, Western United States60, Sichuan basin61, and Tamusin sandstone-type U deposit in Inner Mongolia58. Two ferroselite granules were found in SEM of No.12 coal, which appeared as masses or short columns embedded in organic matter (Fig. 9d). The atomic ratio of Se/Fe was 2.19, slightly higher than the theoretical value, which may be related to the slight Fe deficit in No.12 coal.

Sphalerite in No.12 coal is of poor crystallization, e.g., scattered granular, granular aggregate or irregular (Fig. 9e). Some sphalerite granules are associated with clay minerals and embedded in organic matter in irregular shapes, indicating they could be derived from volcanic rocks in the basement. A small amount of coffinite is also found in the periphery of sphalerite (Fig. 9e), indicating that they may have a common origin.

Uranite, belongs to the uraninite group, whose chemical formula is U3O8. Its main components are UO2 and UO3, and it often contains Pb, Th and REE. Uranite in No.12 coal of Honghaigou coalfield are mainly distributed in the organic matter as spherule or granular shape (Fig. 9f), which is not easily distinguished from coffinite in morphology. SEM-EDS analysis indicated that the uranite in No.12 coal does not contain Pb or Th, which may be due to the low content of Pb and Th in the coal.

Discussion

Occurrence mode and mineral characteristics

The occurrence modes of trace elements in No. 12 coal are comprehensively constrained by both geostatistical analysis and mineralogical characteristics. Cluster and factor analysis reveal that U, Re, Se, Ta, and Hf share similar geochemical behaviors: U, Re, and Se are generally considered to have strong organic affinities, yet the identification of uncommon minerals such as clausthalite, ferroselite, and uraninite confirms their dual affinities (both organic and inorganic) in the coal seam. As high field strength elements, the co-enrichment of U, Ta, and Hf is likely linked to the input of intermediate-acid igneous detritus from the sediment source area, while Mo shows no clear sulfur-loving or lithophilic tendency and no Mo-bearing minerals were detected, indicating its dominant occurrence in organic matter. Mineralogically, pyrite in No. 12 coal is poorly crystallized, occurring as semi-automorphic or xenomorphic grains that are irregularly embedded in organic matter, coexisting with quartz and clay minerals, or filling fusinite cell cavities, and distributed in veins, disseminated, clumpy, or scattered granular forms. Quartz grains are angular, poorly sorted, and rounded, reflecting a near-source terrigenous origin; kaolinite occurs as granular or fine scaly crystals, with aggregates retaining the short-columnar morphology of feldspar, further confirming a terrigenous clastic source. Additionally, the presence of typical low-temperature hydrothermal minerals (e.g., barite, clausthalite, uraninite) not only partially controls the enrichment of U, Se and other elements but also indicates that the coal seam was influenced by low-temperature hydrothermal fluids during or after deposition.

Sediment Provenance

Many researches have been made on the sandstone in Jurassic strata, and the characteristics of debris, mineral and metallogenic age all point to the intermediate-acid igneous rocks at the basin margin. For example, the U content of volcanic rocks and granites in Chabuchaer Mountain is relatively high (average of 8.41ppm), and there is obvious uranium loss in Carboniferous tuff62. Xia et al63. found that the zircon U-Pb isotopic age of Zakistan and Kujertai uranium deposits is 308 Ma, which is consistent with the granite and volcanic rocks in the erosion source area (293–316 Ma for granite and 35–59 Ma for volcanic rocks). As Huemer64 proposed, TiO2/Al2O3 in basic, intermediate and acid magmatic rocks are 0.125–0.333, 0.048–0.125, and 0.014–0.048, respectively. Therefore, TiO2/Al2O3 value is often used to identify the sedimentary source area65,66,67. In this study, TiO2/Al2O3 of the 16 coal samples ranged from 0.005 to 0.073, with an average value of 0.043, showing that the parent rock of the high uranium coal is mainly medium-acid rock as well. In addtion, the reported SEM-BSE images for clausthalite and sphalerite (Fig. 8) could suggest that these mineral granulars are of epigenetic hydrothermal mineral since as they are associated with clay mineral matrix.

For the coal samples in this study, several key geochemical parameters serve as critical proxies for tracing their source provenance(Fig. 10).The high values of LREE/HREE (dominantly > 1) in the upper right portion indicate a dominant provenance consisting of felsic upper crustal rocks such as granite and metamorphic rocks, which are enriched in light rare earth elements. This suggests a substantial input of terrigenous clastic material derived from mature, weathered continental crust. The relatively high Th/U ratios in this region further support a felsic source, as Th is concentrated in silica-rich rocks while U is leached during weathering. In contrast, the lower left samples show low LREE/HREE and very low Th/U, indicating a minor provenance component from mafic/ultramafic rocks or potential hydrothermal modification. Overall, the data demonstrates a mixed source region with felsic material dominating, and minor mafic or hydrothermal input locally influencing the sediment composition. For the coal samples in this study, the GdN/YbN vs. LaN/YbN diagram provides robust constraints on their source provenance. The distinct positive correlation between the two ratios confirms that the REE fractionation patterns are primarily inherited from the source rocks, with negligible diagenetic or hydrothermal modification. Most samples show moderate to high LaN/YbN (> 0.5) and GdN/YbN (0.6–1.3), indicating a dominant felsic upper crustal source (e.g., granite, metamorphic rocks) characterized by enriched LREE and MREE. The high-value outlier reflects highly fractionated felsic lithologies or intense weathering in the source area, while low-value points suggest minor mafic rock input. Collectively, the data demonstrate a terrigenous source dominated by mature felsic crust.

Bivariate plots of Th/U vs. LREE/HREE and GdN/YbN vs. LaN/YbN for the coal samples, illustrating provenance and REE fractionation characteristics.

Depositional Setting and Mineral Control on Element Enrichment

The sedimentary system and structural background of the study area control the migration, precipitation and enrichment of elements such as U in the coal seam. Both sandstone-type uranium deposits and high-uranium coal in the Jurassic strata are mainly developed in the delta plain-front distributary channel sedimentary system29, which is characterized by large thickness and stable planar distribution of coarse sandstone, providing favorable channels for the migration of U-bearing oxidized water. In addition, the monoclinic structure at the basin margin provides sufficient potential energy, enabling the U-bearing oxidized water in the erosion source area to continuously migrate into the basin, so that uranium mineralization in the Yili coal measures, which mainly occurred in the Cenozoic, continues until now, and uranium deposits are mainly located in the piedmont slope zone25. It is inferred that elements such as Se and Ta may be transported from the source area in the form of terrigenous debris into the peat deposition environment, with the basement rocks of the Yili Basin, composed of tuff related to Permian volcanic or orogenic movements, serving as the material source68. Table 3 shows that U enrichment mostly occurs in the upper part of the coal seam and near the roof, where the latter corresponds to the widely concerned sandstone-type uranium deposit in the study area, indicating extensive spatial and genetic connections between U enrichment in coal (including coal-bearing uranium) and sandstone-type uranium deposits26. Combined with the abundant hydrothermal minerals identified in the coal samples and the above provenance analysis, it can be concluded that the enrichment of uranium and other elements in the coal is mainly epigenetic, and the uranium-rich hydrothermal fluids may be primarily derived from the U-bearing shallow groundwater in the source area25,26,64.

Potential for exploitation

Potential for exploitation of 11 critical metals in coal, taking no account of volatilization during combustion (Fig. 11), were evaluated in the study area. Among them, Li, Ga, Ge, Se, Zr, Nb, Mo, Re, and U are evaluated based on the research results of Dai et al15. and Zhao et al69.; Hf and Ta are set at 100 times the content of the world high volatile bituminous coal51. The results show that Ga and Zr in som69e samples from Piliqing coalfield in the northern Yili Basin reach the available grade, which is probably due to the inheritance of the parent rock in the denudation area of the basin margin. Meanwhile, U, Re, and Ta of the No.12 coal from the south of the Yili Basin reach the available grade, which may be attributed to the infiltration of U-bearing oxidizing water and the input of metallic bearing minera debris.

Conclusions

(1) No.12 coal in Honghaigou coalfield is found to have super-elevated enrichment of U, Re, and Ta; high enrichment of Se, Mo and Hf. Among the 16 coal samples, in terms of U, Re, Ta, Se, Mo, and Hf, the number of samples with CC greater than 10 is 7, 11, 9, 6, 10, and 7, respectively. In addition, the high values of the above elements mainly distributed in the upper part of the coal seam.

(2) The most common minerals in No.12 coal are quartz, kaolinite, pyrite. While in coal with super-elevated uranium (especially uranium content greater than 1000 µg/g, e.g., ZkL2916-2), some uncommon minerals were observed, e.g., clausthalite, ferroselite, coffinite, uranite, and Fe-sulfate.

(3) Uranium-Re-Se-Mo-Ta-Hf enrichment in No.12 coal probably be attributed to infiltration of U-bearing oxidized solution (mainly U-bearing shallow water) and input of debris from the source zone. These can be confirmed by the way in which U minerals and Se minerals are embedded in coal (e.g., uraninite is mainly embedded in organic matter, while clausthalite often associated with clay minerals). Meanwhile, the U, Re and Ta in No.12 coal in the southern basin have the potential for exploitation.

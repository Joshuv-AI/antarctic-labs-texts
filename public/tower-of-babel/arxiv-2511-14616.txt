2.1.
Homological genus of a fake bounding pair map

Two homologous curves
bound a subsurface if they are disjoint.
In this subsection, we will show that two homologous curves, even if they intersect, can still have a well-defined “homological genus” using the Chillingworth homomorphism.

Let us start by describing the Chillingworth homomorphism. We will follow Johnson’s approach in
[
6
]
and give an equivalent definition which is different from Chillingworth’s original definition in
[
2

\tau:{\mathcal{I}_{g}^{1}}\to\wedge^{3}H

and a contraction map
C:\wedge^{3}H\to H
defined by

(3)

C(x\wedge y\wedge z)=2\Big[(x\cdot y)z+(y\cdot z)x+(z\cdot x)y\Big]

where

denotes the intersection pairing of
x
and

The Chillingworth homomorphism

is defined by

(4)

e(f)=C(\tau(f))\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall f\in{\mathcal{I}_{g}^{1}}.

One can evaluate the Chillingworth homomorphism on a bounding pair map following a standard calculation as demonstrated in
[
4
]
, Section 6.6.2. We summarize this result as the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
(a,b)

denote the curve

together with the orientation such that

By a curve

to denote the curve

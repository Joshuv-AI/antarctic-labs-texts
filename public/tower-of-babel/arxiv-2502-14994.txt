REVEAL: Robust Evolution of Vision-Language Models for Explainable AI-Video Detection
Yun-Yun Tsai, Qingyuan Liu, Ruijian Zha, Victoria Li, Pengyuan Shi, Chengzhi Mao, Junfeng YangColumbia University

Abstract
The rapid advancement of AI-generated video poses challenges to digital authenticity and security. Current detection methods, often trained on specific datasets, struggle with the ever-evolving landscape of generative techniques and unseen manipulations. We introduce a framework leveraging Vision Language Models (VLMs) for robust AI-generated video detection. Our approach equips the VLM with the ability to reason about video content and use external tools to identify subtle inconsistencies, mirroring human system 2 thinking. Our self-evolving VLM dynamically selects and composes appropriate tools, enhancing its ability to generalize to novel video generation techniques. The modular design promotes interpretability, allowing for a clearer understanding of VLM’s decision-making process. To evaluate, we establish the first benchmark VidForensic containing 1.4k+ high-quality AI generated videos across eight generative models. Experiments show that REVEAL improves F1 scores by 9.1% to 30.2% over top baselines across our datasets for VLMs, notably for GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 pro, and QWen-VL-Max, and Llava-One-Vision-7B. While open-world AI-video detection remains an open challenge, our results indicate that existing methods fail primarily because they lack tool-enabled, higher-order reasoning.

We introduce
REVEAL
, a self-evolving framework for AI-generated video detection using VLMs. Unlike methods relying solely on direct visual feature extraction, REVEAL empowers the VLM to actively reason about video content and dynamically select and compose a toolkit of external analysis tools (e.g., for motion analysis, artifact detection). As illustrated in Fig.
2
, this process unfolds in two key steps: (1)
Self-Evolving Toolkit Selection
, where the VLM, acting as a judge, autonomously evaluates and selects the most effective tools based on predefined scoring metrics and historical samples; and (2)
Self-Adaptation with Structured Prompting
, where the VLM refines the structure of its prompts based on output feedback, enhancing adaptability across diverse datasets. This data-driven, self-evolving approach, guided by structured prompts with a defined class structure, allows REVEAL to automatically learn optimal reasoning strategies and tool combinations, improving interpretability, reducing hallucination, and preventing overfitting through online adaptation of key prompt fields.

A key advantage of our method is interpretability, which stems from its modular design and tool-extracted explicit knowledge, revealing the decision-making process. The self-evolving capabilities, using structured prompting and dynamic tool selection, achieve significant robustness and generalizability. REVEAL’s adaptability makes it less vulnerable to overfitting and more capable of handling novel generation techniques, a crucial step towards reliable open-world detection.

Related Works

AI-Generated Video Detection

The success in high-quality machine-generated videos has heightened concerns about security, personal privacy, and digital integrity, emphasizing the need for a robust and generalizable detector capable of distinguishing videos produced by generative models.
Recently, Deepfake video, generated by GAN-based models, can perform face manipulation with high realism
[
26
]
. Agarwal et al.
[
26
]
highlighted the challenges of detecting Deepfake video, noting that traditional DNN networks or audio-visual approach based on lip sync inconsistency detection are not robust enough to detect Deepfake. David et al.,
[
18
]
proposed to use convolutional neural network (CNN) + Long short-term memory (LSTM) to build Deepfake video detectors. However, these methods did not account for cross-model transferability to state-of-the-art synthetic videos, especially those generated by diffusion-based video models
[
50
,
5
,
25
,
40
,
36
]
, which have the capability to produce high-quality synthetic videos indistinguishable by human. VidProM
[
45
]
and DeMamba
[
13
]
addressed the challenge and created synthetic video datasets containing millions of samples. DIVID
[
31
]
further refined the diffusion reconstruction error (DIRE
[
46
]
) for diffusion-generated video detection, enhancing detection performance across temporal dimensions. AIGVDet
[
3
]
proposed to use spatio-temporal CNNs to tackle synthetic video detection. DuB3D
[
21
]
developed dual-branch 3D transformers to distinguish between real and synthetic videos. Despite the inspiring in-domain evaluation results from previous work, the robustness and generalizability of existing detectors’ performance on unseen sources remain unexplored.

Video Detection with LVLM

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have emerged as a powerful framework for integrating visual and textual data, enabling models to perform complex multimodal tasks. Early LVLMs, such as CLIP
[
38
]
and ALIGN
[
22
]
, excel at mapping images and text into a shared embedding space, enabling efficient image recognition and task captioning. However, these models are limited in their ability to understand temporal information in video data. To address this, models like Flamingo
[
1
]
and MERLOT
[
47
]
have been introduced, significantly advancing LVLM capabilities in video understanding. Additionally, BLIP-2
[
29
]
improved LVLM performance in image understanding by refining multimodal fusion techniques, enhancing the model’s ability to comprehend nuanced relationships between visual objects and their linguistic descriptions. These models have paved the way for applying LVLMs to complex multimodal applications such as Video Question Answering and Image Understanding.

3.3
Explicit Knowledge Exploration

Recent research has shown that explicit knowledge extracted from video samples can help improve detection on video forensic
[
10
]
. The explicit knowledge is collected from the video representations (VR) decomposed by the video frames. The VRs can be categorized into three classes
[

:
appearance
,
motion
, and
geometry
. Appearance refers to the visual attributes of video frames, such as color, lightning, or texture, while motion refers to the temporal or dynamic change in video frames, such as optical flow. Finally, geometry refers to object shape structure and spatial information in video frames, such as 3D depth map. We explore LVLM’s comprehension capabilities from these three VR aspects. Our pipeline leverages LVLM to automatically select a set of explicit knowledge that can benefit the detection performance.

Method

4.1
Self-Evolving LVLM with Explicit Knowledge

We introduce how REVEAL self-evolves LVLM with explicit knowledge (EK) enhancement and selection.
One of the key findings in our work is that different LVLMs show various capabilities in utilizing EK features. For example, GPT-4o has better knowledge on saturation and can offer a more reasonable explanation when compared to other LVLMs such as Gemini
[
17
]
or Qwen
[
37
]
. Therefore, to achieve optimal EK utilization, we design a model-specific EK selection strategy to construct a toolkit for each model. For each model, we dynamically assign the best performing tools, selected from the entire EK pool, to its toolkit based on its performance on a small set of reference video samples.

t

.

Self-Adaptation Prompting Strategy

We dynamically re-write the structured prompt template of LVLM based on real-time performance feedback.

In Fig.
4
, we show the flow of self-adaptation prompting process. Our template re-write function is a key component of the process. Given small batches of video input, this function ensures that the LVLMs adapt their structured prompts to specific analytical needs. A detailed system prompt consists of five key criteria:
Requirements, Analysis Guidelines, Constraints, Prohibited Fields,
and
Additional Notes
. We ask the LVLMs to follow these
five
criteria and modify the prompts. For instance, in the
Requirements
, we have the rule said
“You may add any other fields you deem necessary to analyze video data with

tool name
.”
; in
Analysis Guidelines”
, we have
“Consider the aspects of videos that

tool name

excels at analyzing.”
; and in the
Additional Notes
, we have
”The total number of fields must not exceed five”
…,etc. More information can be found in Appendix
9.4
.

During the self-adaptation process, we first draft an initial template, a class structure with fields, including
is_ai_generated: Bool, raw_frame_analysis: str, tool_name_analysis: str, explanation: str
. Starting from the first iteration, LVLMs propose new templates based on the aforementioned system prompt.
If a template underperforms, incremental modifications will be applied to key fields in the class-structure of prompts, ensuring adjustments focus on broader analytical aspects, such as facial feature consistency or temporal anomaly detection.
This iterative refinement improves the adaptability of the model, particularly in challenging real-world datasets.
Our approach not only prevents the model from overfitting to specific words or phrases but also mitigates hallucinations in non-structured prompts.
The iterative process encourages high-level improvements in classification accuracy rather than focusing on low-level, superficial changes. More details on self-adaptation process, including the whole prompt template and evolution logging are shown in Appdx Sec.

Supervised learning baselines
We compare REVEAL with supervised learning detection methods. We extract vision features of all explicit knowledge and train with three methods: XGBoost
[
14
]
, SVM
[
16
]
, and transformer
[
41
]
. XGBoost is configured with 100 estimators, a maximum depth of 6. The SVM employs a linear kernel that is enabled by probability estimation. The transformer-based method follows a standard encoder structure, consisting of a multi-head self-attention mechanism, a feedforward network, residual connections, layer normalization, and positional encoding. We trained these models using data outside the test set.

Implementation Details

In our experiments, all LVLMs are configured to accept multiple image inputs.
Videos in VidForensic are all processed to a maximum of 100 consecutive frames. Additionally, for each video, we select the middle 8 frames as input to the model.
We also test the impact of using the first 8 frames and the last 8 frames on detection results, observing that the results are consistent across these three frame selection methods.
We set the hyperparameters for model generation, such as temperature
T=0
. For online adaptation implementation, we process the adaptation set in batches of 25 examples, using an F1-score threshold of 0.8 to encourage adaptation while maintaining performance standards. We set the adaptation iteration limit to 20. For template re-writing, we provide specific guidance to focus on high-level analysis aspects. For each iteration, we ask the LVLM to propose a new field name in our structured prompt. After each template trial, we record all the rewriting records and the corresponding F1 scores, allowing the LVLM to analyze past results and identify valuable fields for continuous improvement. In Appendix
9.3

9.4
, we show the prompt details for selecting explicit knowledge.

Evaluation Metrics

In our experiment, we aim for the model to detect artifacts in the additional information that are absent in the original raw video. When LVLMs are presented with a video for detection, using the provided toolkit, they first perform an independent analysis of each explicit knowledge information. Then, we integrate the prediction of each explicit knowledge using an OR operation to ensemble the final result for the video. Video-level accuracy and F1 score are adopted as the evaluation metrics for all experiments.

Discussion

Dependency on LVLMs

Based on our thorough investigation, we confirm that the reasoning capabilities of current state-of-the-art (SOTA) LVLMs are inadequate. Our proposed solution significantly enhances LVLMs by enabling the selection of explicit knowledge and the detection of artifacts. As LVLMs continue to evolve and integrate greater reasoning abilities, it is imperative to explore how to guide these models to specific reasoning and employ chain-of-thought reasoning strategies into self-evolving process.

8
Conclusion

REVEAL is a novel agentic framework that leverages LVLMs’ strong reasoning ability to detect diffusion-generated video.
As opposed to existing methods that require supervised training detectors with explicit knowledge (EK), REVEAL is training-free and can generalize to videos produced by different sources of video generation tools.
With our proposed EK selection method based on a tool-preference metric, REVEAL can effectively extract useful EK for LVLMs to perform detection. We further propose a self-adaptation method for structured prompts based on a rewriting template mechanism. Our proposed process prevents LVLMs from overfitting with a specific template.
The evaluation demonstrates that REVEAL improves F1 scores by 9.1% to 30.2% over the top baseline on a high-quality video dataset across four leading LVLMs. Our work offers fresh perspectives on video detection by employing an agentic LVLM framework with emerging techniques.

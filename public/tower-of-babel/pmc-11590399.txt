miR-122/NEGR1 axis contributes colorectal cancer liver metastasis by PI3K/AKT pathway and macrophage modulation
Authors: Shipeng Dai, Haiwen Zhuang, Zhuozheng Li, Zhongda Chen, Yue Chai, Qing Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractColorectal cancer (CRC) is a prevalent malignant tumor in the gastrointestinal tract, with around 50% of patients experiencing distant metastases, predominantly to the liver. Colorectal cancer liver metastasis (CRLM) is a leading cause of CRC-related death, and effective treatments remain limited. This study aims to identify new targets for predicting and treating CRLM. Bioinformatics analysis highlighted the miR-122/NEGR1 axis as crucial in CRLM. In vitro assays (Colony formation, Wound healing, Transwell) explored the impact of this axis on CRC cell proliferation, invasion, and migration. Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay and RNA-pulldown confirmed miR-122/NEGR1 interaction. In vivo, CRLM model mice were used to investigate the axis’s effects on tumor metastasis and macrophage polarization. Immunofluorescence (IF), Quantitative Real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), Enzyme-linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA), and Western Blot (WB) analyzed macrophage polarization markers and cytokine/protein/RNA expression. Results showed increased miR-122 and decreased NEGR1 in liver metastases compared to primary tumors. The miR-122/NEGR1 axis enhanced CRC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and affected the PI3K/AKT pathway. Furthermore, reduced NEGR1 promoted M2 macrophage polarization and accelerated liver metastasis in CRLM model mice. In conclusion, the miR-122/NEGR1 axis drives CRC progression and liver metastasis through the PI3K/AKT pathway and M2 macrophage polarization, representing a potential target for the therapy of CRLM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-024-05901-5.Keywords: Neuronal growth regulator 1, miR-122, PI3K/AKT pathway, Macrophage polarization, Colorectal cancer liver metastasis

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is one of the most common cancers of the gastrointestinal system and the third most common malignant tumor, which seriously affects people’s lives and health [1, 2]. Numerous clinical studies have confirmed that the most common metastatic destination of advanced colorectal cancer is the liver. At present, the most effective treatment for liver metastasis of colorectal cancer is still surgical resection of the primary cancer combined with liver metastasis at the same period or stage. Unfortunately, only 10–20% of colorectal cancer patients with liver metastases are eligible for liver metastasis resection, which not only increases the difficulty of diagnosis and treatment but also is closely related to the rapid progression and high mortality of advanced colorectal cancer [3, 4]. Without effective systemic treatment, the median survival of patients with colorectal cancer liver metastases is only 6–9 months. Patients who are unable to undergo effective radical surgery for liver metastases have a median survival of only 13–18 months and a 5 year survival rate of < 5%, even with the most effective current chemotherapy regimens [5]. There are no efficient and accurate clinical indicators for early prediction of colorectal cancer liver metastases and effective treatment of advanced colorectal cancer with metastatic targets for the treatment of advanced colorectal cancer with metastases.

Neuronal growth regulator 1 (NEGR1), a member of the IgLON cell adhesion molecule family, is a membrane-bound protein crucial for intercellular adhesion. It features three N-terminal C2 immunoglobulin-like domains, multiple glycosylation sites, and a glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchor [6]. NEGR1 is predominantly studied in neuroscience, where it is widely expressed in the brain and contributes to the regulation of nervous system functions and synaptic elongation [7–9]. Research indicates that NEGR1 knockout mice display abnormalities in 5-hydroxytryptamine and dopamine neurotransmission, alongside increased anxiety and depression-like behaviors. Furthermore, NEGR1 is notably linked to obesity and dyslexia in humans [10]. In oncology, NEGR1 has been relatively underexplored, with its expression often reduced in various tumors. Overexpression of NEGR1 in the human ovarian cancer cell line SKOV-3 markedly diminishes tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [11]. In neuroblastoma, NEGR1 expression is down-regulated, and its overexpression inhibits tumor growth and proliferation both in vitro and in vivo [12]. Additionally, targeting NEGR1 with miR-222-3p influences tumor immune infiltration and advances thyroid cancer progression [13]. Despite some initial findings regarding NEGR1 in cancer, the detailed molecular mechanisms underlying its role in liver metastasis of colorectal cancer remain unclear.

microRNA(miRNAs) is a non-coding RNA molecule with 22–24 nucleotides. miRNAs play dual action functions of cancer promotion or inhibition in different cancer types by affecting physiological and pathological processes such as proliferation, migration, apoptosis, and differentiation of tumor cells. In CRC, the dysregulation of miRNAs is closely associated with the occurrence, development of tumors, especially in the process of liver metastasis in CRC. Studies have found that cancer-derived miR-25-3p promotes the occurrence of tumor cell metastasis by inducing angiogenesis and vascular permeability in the tumor microenvironment [14]. miRNAs also affect the reconstitution of the tumor immune microenvironment. Tumor-derived miR-934 was found to induce macrophage M2 polarization and promote the occurrence of liver metastasis in colorectal cancer [15]. miR-122 has been demonstrated to have the function of promoting the progression of breast cancer, renal carcinoma, lung carcinoma, melanoma, and distant metastasis [16–20], but has been shown to inhibit the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric carcinoma, and cholangiocarcinoma [21–23]. miR-122's role in colorectal cancer progression and metastasis is still controversial. miR-122 was found to be abnormally expressed in liver metastases of colorectal cancer, which has a pro-cancer function [24, 25], whereas the results of some studies were the opposite [26]. The role of miR-122 targeting NEGR1 in the development of colorectal cancer liver metastases has not been reported.

In this study, we analyzed multiple datasets from public databases and found that the miR-122/NEGR1 axis may be associated with the development of liver metastasis in colorectal cancer, and R language-based survival analysis and clinical tissue and serum samples further confirmed that low expression of NEGR1 is associated with progression of colorectal cancer and liver metastasis, as well as poor prognosis of patients. In vitro, we found that overexpression of NEGR1 inhibited the malignant phenotype of colorectal cancer cells, including proliferation, migration, and invasion. Overexpression of miR-122 showed the opposite effect, it promoted the progression of the malignant phenotype of colorectal cancer cells. Further studies revealed that miR-122 targeting NEGR1 affected the phosphorylation activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. In vivo experiments, the knockdown of NEGR1 promoted the progression of liver metastases in CRLM model mice, affected macrophage polarization to M2 type, and promoted IL-10 secretion, which in turn created a tumor immune microenvironment conducive to metastasis of colorectal cancer to the liver. These results suggest that the miR-122/NEGR1 axis influences the occurrence and progression of colorectal cancer liver metastasis by affecting PI3K/AKT and macrophage M2 polarization. Currently, there is a lack of effective clinical indicators to predict the risk of CRLM and effective therapeutic targets for advanced CRLM. Therefore, investigating the mechanism of the miR-122/NEGR1 axis in colorectal cancer liver metastasis is important for the development of new therapeutic approaches and the improvement of patient survival.

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Two independent mRNA datasets (GSE103340, GSE81558) and three miRNAs datasets (GSE35834, GSE72199, GSE81581) were analyzed by using GEO2R, an R-based online data processing tool on GEO (inclusion criteria were | log2FC |> 1 and p < 0.05). The volcano plots, heat maps, and Venn plots of differential genes were drawn by R language software 4.2.1. TargetScan database (http://www.targetscan.org/Vert_72/) and miRDB database (http://mirdb.org/miRDB/) were used to predict the miRNAs regulating mRNAs. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was predicted and obtained by the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/). Survival analysis of key target genes was performed using Kaplan–Meier curves using the survival R package.The DAVID database 6.8 (https://David.Ncifcrf.Gov/) was used for Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and kyoko encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis. The Gseaplot2 R package was used for Gene sets enrichment analyses (GSEA) enrichment analysis. The TIMER database (http://timer.comp-genomics.org/) was used to perform pan-cancer analysis of genes and its relationship with the immune microenvironment.

Clinical specimens

Primary colorectal cancer tumor tissues, liver metastases, and matched adjacent normal tissues from CRC were collected from 5 patients who underwent surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangsu University or the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) malignant tumor confirmed by postoperative pathology as adenocarcinoma; (2) distant metastasis proven by postoperative pathology. The exclusion criteria ware as follows: (1) not diagnosed by histology; (2) a second primary cancer; (3) distant metastases at sites other than the liver; (4) cases who underwent metastasectomy without primary tumor surgery. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangsu University and the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University.

Cell culture and transfection

The cells were cultured in a DMEM (Keygen Biotech, China) high-glucose medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Procell, China) and incubated in a constant temperature incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. miR-122 mimic or pc-NEGR1(Genechem, China) was transfected into the MC38 cell line using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent following the manufacturer’s instructions(Thermo, USA). The success of transfection was confirmed by qRT-PCR. NEGR1 knockdown in MC38 cells was accomplished through lentivirus transfection sh-NEGR1(Transheep, China). Initial cell culture consisted of seeding 1 × 105 cells in each well of a six-well plate and incubating with 2 ml of medium for 24 h. After that, we reduced the medium to 1 ml and introduced a sufficient amount of virus and 40 µl of polyethylene (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). After an incubation period of 12–16 h, the cells were placed in normal medium and screened for puromycin. The constructed sh-RNA sequences are shown in Table S1. The transfection efficiency was validated using qRT-PCR and Western blot.

Western blot

The treated cells or tissue specimens were collected, and total protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Servicebio, China). The proteins were then separated by SDS-PAGE (Epizyme, China) and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). The membranes were blocked with a 5% skim milk solution for 1 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature and then subjected to exposure treatment.We uesd the following primary antibodies: NEGR1(1:1000, Abcam, USA), phosphorylated PI3K (p-PI3K, 1:2000, Abcam, USA), PI3K (1:1000, Abcam, USA), phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT, 1:1000, Abcam, USA), AKT (1:500, Abcam, USA), and GAPDH (1:5000, Proteintech) were used. All antibodies were diluted in the appropriate proportions using an antibody diluent (New Cell & Molecular Biotech Co.Ltd).

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from liver metastases, CRC tissues, or cells using a total RNA extraction kit (Vazyme, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Reverse transcription was performed using a reverse transcription kit (Vazyme, China) to convert RNA into cDNA. GAPDH or U6 was used as an internal control. The relative RNA levels were calculated using the 2−∆∆Ct method. All primers were purchased from Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China) (Table S1). The mix preparation and thermal conditions for amplification were shown in Table S3.

Colony formation assay

1 × 103 cells of different intervention groups were plated in six-well plates and cultured to form colonies. Two weeks later, stain colonies with crystal violet (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) after fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio, China). Using ImageJ software analysis statistics.

Wound healing assay

When the cell density per well of the 6-well plate reached over 90%, the cells were scratched with a 10 µl sterile pipette. Subsequently, serum-free medium was used for 24 h. Images of the scratch region were captured at 0 h and 24 h using a microscope. The scratch area was analyzed using ImageJ software to compare the reduction in percentage of the scratch area at 24 h compared to 0 h.

Transwell assay

700 µl of complete medium was added to each well in a 24-well plate and placed into a Transwell chamber(Corning USA), add 200 µl of cell suspension prepared from serum-free medium (ensure that there are about 20,000 cells in each well), the 24-well plate was incubated in a 37 ℃, 5% CO2 constant temperature incubator for 24 h, then the chamber was taken out and gently cleaned with PBS wash 2 times. Using a cotton swab to gently rub the cells that failed to pass through the chamber in the upper chamber, and then fix them with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min wash twice with PBS, add 0.1% crystal violet dye for 30 min after air drying, invert the chamber on a glass slide, and count and record the statistics under a microscope.

Dual luciferase assay

The wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) NEGR1 plasmids were constructed. The WT or MUT vectors were individually cotransfected into MC38 cells with miR-122 mimics using transfection reagents. Subsequently, the relative luciferase activity was assessed using the Dual Luciferase Assay Kit (Promega, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

RNA pull-down experiment

The biotin-labeled RNA probe specific to miR-122 (RiboBio, China) was incubated with streptavidin affinity magnetic beads for 60 min at room temperature. Once the probe had bound to the beads, an RNase inhibitor was introduced, and cell lysates were added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. The incubated mixture of magnetic beads, RNA, and protein underwent three washes with cold PBS before being analyzed by qRT-PCR and Western blotting.

CRLM model mice establishment

All animal studies were conducted in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Medical University. The CRLM model was established using male C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks old). After anesthesia, a transverse incision was made in the left upper quadrant of the abdominal cavity. MC38 cells, at a concentration of approximately 1 × 107/ml, were slowly injected into the spleen capsule using a 1 ml syringe. Pressure was applied to the injection site with a cotton swab to control bleeding. The mice were then placed on a warming pad to maintain their body temperature. After 20 min to allow for tumor cell circulation, the spleen was removed, the incision was sutured, and the mice were returned to their cages for recovery. Three weeks later, the mice were euthanized; their livers were photographed, weighed, and the number of liver tumors and liver weight were recorded. Some liver tissue was preserved for further analysis. Mice treated with clodronate-filled liposomes (CLD) received intraperitoneal injections of 200 µl CLD starting the day before the CRLM model was established, with additional injections every three days until the model was fully established. Mice were also administered AS101 via intraperitoneal injection starting the day before CRLM model establishment, with daily injections of 10 µl of AS101 until the end of the experiment.

Bone marrow-derived macrophage (BMDM) extraction and culture

C57BL/6 mice were sacrificed by neck dislocation and then sterilized with 75% alcohol. In an ultra-clean workbench, the lower limbs of the mice were removed. After removing the hair, skin, muscle, and fascia from the bone surfaces of the lower limbs, the femur and tibia were isolated. Bone marrow cells were extracted into centrifuge tubes using DMEM complete medium, and the supernatant was discarded after centrifugation. One milliliter of red blood cell lysis solution was added to the centrifuge tubes, mixed with the bone marrow cells, and centrifuged again. The supernatant was removed, and the cells were resuspended in DMEM medium. The cells were then stimulated with M-CSF (20 ng/ml) for 5 days for future use.

Immunofluorescence assay

Paraffin sections of liver metastases from mice were dewaxed, subjected to antigen retrieval, and then blocked with serum. They were incubated overnight with a primary antibody at 4 °C. The sections were then incubated with a photolabeled secondary antibody for 60 min at room temperature, protected from light. Following this, DAPI staining was performed at room temperature, and images were observed using a fluorescence microscope. For cell samples, fixation was done with formaldehyde, and the subsequent steps were identical to those used for tissue samples.We uesd the following primary antibodies: CD206(1:500, Abcam, USA), iNOS (1:500, Abcam, USA), F4/80 (1:500, Abcam, USA) were used. All antibodies were diluted in the appropriate proportions using an antibody diluent (New Cell & Molecular Biotech Co.Ltd).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Tissue samples were homogenized thoroughly with PBS and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 20 min after being fully ground. The supernatant was collected. Blood samples were centrifuged at 1000×g for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. Cell culture medium supernatant was centrifuged at 300×g for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. The precipitate was removed. All samples were then stored at − 20 °C in the refrigerator for further use.

Prepare reagents, samples to be tested, and standards for different concentration gradients according to the ELISA kit instructions(MultiSciences, China). The standard product (standard product with different concentration gradient per well of 100 μl, diluent of 100 µl per hole of empty white hole) and the sample to be tested were added to the soaked enzyme label plate respectively, and then the plate was incubated at room temperature for 2 h. Remove liquid from each well and rinse 6 times with lotion before drying. Add 100 μl of test work antibody working liquid to each well, incubate at room temperature for 1 h, and wash again for 6 times. Add 100 μl chromogenic solution to each well and incubate for 30 min in dark environment. Then add 100 μl termination solution to each well and let stand for 30 min. The concentration of the sample to be measured is calculated after drawing the standard curve.

Statistical analysis

To analyze the data, GraphPad Prism 9.0 software was utilized. The data were analyzed and compared using a t test or One-Way ANOVA, and statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. The notation used for significance levels is as follows: ns p ≥ 0.05, *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01,***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001.

Result

In silico mRNAs and miRNAs expression differences in colorectal cancer liver metastases (LM) and primary tumor (PT)

To investigate the differences in the expression of mRNAs and miRNAs between LM and PT of colorectal cancer, we selected two mRNA datasets (GSE103340, GSE81558) and three miRNA datasets (GSE35834, GSE72199, GSE81581) from the GEO database for analysis. We used |log2FC|> 1 and p < 0.05 as the screening criteria to select the differential mRNAs and differential miRNAs in the aforementioned datasets. The volcano plots of differentially expressed mRNAs for each dataset drawn using the R language are shown in Fig. 1A, and heatmaps are displayed in Fig. 1B. The top 5 differential mRNAs in GSE103340 dataset were: ZG16, NKX2-3, CLCA4, MS4A12, CA1, and the most significant 5 mRNAs in GSE81558 dataset were: ACTG2, FABP4, SFRP2, FGA, CRP (Fig. 1A, B). The Venn diagram, revealed a total of 180 shared differential genes in the 2 mRNAs datasets, with the expression of 163 mRNAs increased and 17 mRNAs decreased (Fig. 1C, D, Table S2). Through the STRING database, a PPI network including 180 nodes and 2165 edges of the shared differential genes was constructed (Fig. 1E), hub genes were shown in Figure S2.

A Volcano plots of differential genes from two mRNA datasets (GSE103340, GSE81558); B Heatmap of differential genes from the two mRNA datasets described previously; C Venn diagram of jointly upregulated differential genes; D Venn diagram of jointly downregulated differential genes; E Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) network of differential genes; F Venn diagram of differential miRNAs for three miRNA datasets (GSE35834, GSE72199, and GSE81581); G Intersection of mRNAs predicted by co-differentiated miRNAs and differential mRNAs taken from the aforementioned differential mRNAs, obtaining the five regulatory axes

By analyzing the miRNA datasets, we identified five shared differential miRNAs from the three miRNA datasets and plotted the Venn diagram (Fig. 1F). The downstream mRNAs of the differential miRNAs were predicted using the TargetScan and miRDB databases. Ultimately, we obtained five sets of miRNA/mRNA axes that may have regulatory relationships (Fig. 1G): miR-122/NEGR1, miR-210/RGMA, miR-855/WNT5A, miR-10b/PAH, miR708/CPB2. Subsequently, we explored the correlation between the five target genes and colorectal cancer prognosis. Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis based on R language showed that low expression of NEGR1 was associated with poorer overall survival (OS) (p < 0.0001) (Figure S1), while the remaining 4 genes did not show statistically significant differences in patient survival. This suggests that NEGR1 may be a key gene affecting colorectal cancer development and patient prognosis, and the miR-122/NEGR1 axis may be an important pathway influencing liver metastasis in colorectal cancer.

In silico prediction of NEGR1 downstream pathway and its impact on the tumor immune microenvironment

To further investigate the downstream pathway of NEGR1, we conducted KEGG enrichment and GO enrichment analyses of the differential genes using the DAVID database. The results revealed that KEGG enrichment of the differential genes was primarily associated with the complement pathway. Additionally, GO functional enrichment analysis indicated that these differential genes were closely linked to complement activation, HDL particles, lipase inhibitor activity, among others (Fig. 2A). GSEA enrichment analysis demonstrated a significant correlation between liver metastasis of colorectal cancer and the PI3K/AKT pathway in two independent datasets (GSE103340 and GSE81558), both of which exhibited significant correlations (Fig. 2B). TIMER database analysis indicated that NEGR1 expression in colorectal cancer tissues was positively associated with macrophage M1-type polarization and negatively associated with macrophage M2-type polarization (Fig. 2C). The results of the aforementioned public database analysis suggest that the low expression of NEGR1 may influence the progression and metastasis of colorectal cancer partly through the PI3K/AKT pathway and the regulation of macrophage polarization.

MiR-122 and NEGR1 expression in colorectal cancer, adjacent tissue and liver metastasis

To study the expression of miR-122 and NEGR1 in colorectal cancer, we collected primary colorectal cancer, paracancerous tissues, and liver metastases from colorectal cancer patients with liver metastases for qRT-PCR and ELISA. qRT-PCR analysis showed that the expression of NEGR1 (Fig. 3B) in colorectal cancer LM was lower than that in PT, while the expression of miR-122 (Fig. 3C) was relatively higher. Furthermore, Pearson correlation analysis Indicated that the expression of miR-122 was negatively correlated with that of NEGR1 (Figure S3). Compared with the normal tissues adjacent to the cancer, the expression of NEGR1 was decreased in the PT (Fig. 3B), which coincided with the results of pan-cancer analysis in the TMER database (Fig. 3A). Moreover, TMER database pan-cancer analysis showed that NEGR1 expression was down-regulated in a variety of tumors (such as bladder urothelial carcinoma, breast invasive carcinoma, cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma, etc.), suggesting that it may be a common tumor suppressor gene. The expression of miR-122 in colorectal cancer PT and paracancerous tissues was not significantly different (Fig. 3C), and the ELISA results showed that the expression of NEGR1 in LM was lower than that in paracancerous tissues compared with that in colorectal cancer PT (Fig. 3D). Subsequently, we collected blood from normal patients, colorectal cancer patients without distant metastases, and colorectal cancer patients with liver metastases for ELISA and found that the expression of NEGR1 in the blood of patients with liver metastases was significantly lower compared with that in normal patients and patients without metastases, and there was no statistically significant difference in the expression of NEGR1 in the blood of patients with normal and those with non-metastatic colorectal cancer (Fig. 3E). The above results suggest that NEGR1 is correlated with liver metastasis of colorectal cancer and is a potential therapeutic target.

MiR-122 directly targets NEGR1 to influence the malignant phenotype of colorectal cancer cells

The TargetScan database and miRDB database predicted that miR-122 could directly target the 3′-UTR of NEGR1 mRNA (Fig. 4I). To validate the binding of miR-122 to the 3′-UTR of NEGR1 mRNA, we cotransfected miR-122 mimic with NEGR1 wild-type 3′-UTR or NEGR1 mutant 3′-UTR in MC38 cells and then measured the luciferase activity. We found that miR-122 mimic reduced the luciferase activity of NEGR1 wild-type mRNA 3′-UTR (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4K). Based on the results of Western Blot experiments, NEGR1 expression decreased in the miR-122 mimic group and increased in the pc-NEGR1 group. Compared with the miR-122 mimic group, NEGR1 expression significantly increased in the miR-122 mimic + pc-NEGR1 group, indicating successful transfection and miR-122 targeting to down-regulate the expression of NEGR1 (Fig. 4K). The Pull-down assay further verified the interaction between miR-122 and NEGR1 (Fig. 4L). To investigate the effect of the miR-122/NEGR1 axis on colorectal cancer progression and metastasis, we used miR-122 mimic and NEGR1 overexpression plasmids for in vitro studies (Fig. 4A, E). In the clone formation assay, MC38 cells in the miR-122 mimic group exhibited a higher cell proliferation rate and a greater number of colony-forming cells compared with the control group (Fig. 4C), whereas MC38 cells in the pc-NEGR1 group exhibited a lower cell proliferation ability and fewer colony-forming cells (Fig. 4G). Wound healing assay and Transwell assay were used to evaluate the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of MC38 cells. As expected, the miR-122 mimic group showed a larger migratory area at 24 h compared to 0 h (Fig. 4B), while the pc-NEGR1 group showed a smaller migratory area (Fig. 4F). Transwell assays showed a similar trend, with MC38 cells in the miR-122 mimic group exhibiting a stronger invasive ability compared to controls (Fig. 4D), while the pc-NEGR1 group showed a smaller invasive area (Fig. 4H). Therefore, we concluded that miR-122 induced the activation of the malignant phenotype of MC38 cells and promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of colorectal cancer cells by targeting NEGR1.

Knockdown of NEGR1 affects the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, promoting colorectal cancer liver metastasis and inducing macrophage M2 polarization

To investigate the impact of NEGR1 knockdown on liver metastasis of colorectal cancer, we transfected MC38 cells with lentivirus constructs targeting NEGR1 or empty lentivirus. The effects of lentiviral transfection were assessed using qRT-PCR (Fig. 5A). Bioinformatics analysis revealed the crucial role of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in colorectal cancer liver metastasis (Fig. 2B). Subsequent experiments demonstrated that knocking down NEGR1 in MC38 cells led to a significant increase in the expression of p-PI3K and p-AKT, while no notable changes were observed in the protein levels of PI3K and AKT (Fig. 5B). To confirm the impact of NEGR1 knockdown on the PI3K/AKT pathway, we conducted a replication experiment using LY294002, a broad-spectrum PI3K inhibitor, which partially reversed the increase in p-AKT expression induced by NEGR1 knockdown (Fig. 5B). These findings suggest that NEGR1 knockdown may enhance colorectal cancer progression by activating the phosphorylation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. Subsequently, lentivirus-transfected MC38 cells were used to establish a CRLM model in mice. After four weeks, liver specimens were collected, and the number of liver metastases and liver weight were documented. The results indicated a significant increase in the number of metastatic tumors and liver weight in the sh-NEGR1 group compared to the control group (Fig. 5C). IF analysis revealed a significant increase in the expression of CD206, a marker of macrophage M2-type polarization, in the sh-NEGR1 group, while the expression of iNOS, a marker of M1-type polarization, decreased (Fig. 5D). Examination of macrophage-associated cytokines in the liver showed a significant increase in the expression of IL-10 and TGF-β, cytokines associated with M2-type macrophages, in the sh-NEGR1 group (Fig. 5E, F). Conversely, the expression of IL-1β and TNF-α, cytokines linked to M1 macrophages, decreased in the sh-NEGR1 group, although no statistical significance was observed (Fig. 5G and H). These results suggest that NEGR1 knockdown promotes the development and progression of CRLM, potentially through its influence on macrophage polarization. In vivo experiment, we depleted mouse macrophages using clodronate disodium liposomes (CLD). IF analysis revealed a significant suppression of the macrophage marker F4/80 expression in the livers of CRLM mice after intraperitoneal injection of CLD (Fig. 5I). The sh-NEGR1 + CLD group exhibited a significantly lower number of metastatic tumors and liver weight compared to the sh-NEGR1 group (Fig. 5J).

Inhibition of NEGR1 promotes macrophage secretion of pro-tumor-associated cytokines

We examined the levels of NEGR1 and cytokines in the supernatant(sn) of MC38/sh-NEGR1 or MC38/NC culture dishes by ELISA. We found that the level of NEGR1 in MC38/sh-NEGR1 (sn) was lower than that in MC38/NC (sn) (Fig. 6A). However, there was no statistically significant difference in the expression levels of cytokines IL10, TNF-α, TGF-β, and IL-1β between the two groups (Fig. 6B–E), indicating that inhibition of NEGR1 expression did not have a significant effect on cytokine secretion by MC38 cells themselves. No significant effect was observed.

Discussion

Colorectal cancer is the third most common malignancy in the world [27, 28]. Late liver metastasis of colorectal cancer is one of the significant factors that impact the mortality of patients with colorectal cancer [3]. Therefore, understanding the key molecular mechanisms in the process of colorectal cancer liver metastasis progression, identifying and regulating tumor cell migration, invasion, and metastasis ability, important oncogenes, selecting key molecular markers, monitoring the risk of colorectal cancer liver metastasis, and developing new treatment strategies are crucial. In this study, we analyzed mRNA datasets and three miRNA datasets using bioinformatics methods from the GEO database. We identified the miR-122/NEGR1 axis in colorectal cancer liver metastasis, which may play a key role in the process. The study explored how the miR-122/NEGR1 axis promotes colorectal cancer proliferation, migration, and liver metastasis through the PI3K/AKT pathway and M2-type polarization of macrophages in the tumor immune microenvironment.

Increasing evidence highlights the crucial role of miRNAs in tumor development and metastasis. Research has shown that miR-122 functions as a tumor-promoting factor in various cancers, including breast, kidney, lung, and melanoma, where it enhances cancer cell proliferation, migration, and metastasis [16–20]. Conversely, miR-122 inhibits tumor progression in liver cancer and gastric cancer [21–23]. The role of miR-122 in colorectal cancer remains controversial. Sun et al. reported that miR-122 is significantly overexpressed in the tumor tissues of colorectal cancer patients, with miR-122 in patient serum originating from exosomes. Elevated serum levels of miR-122 are particularly noticeable in patients with colorectal cancer and liver metastases [25]. Recent studies have indicated that infection with Fusobacterium nucleatum (Fn) was closely associated with the occurrence of CRC, and this infection may stimulate CRC cells to excrete exosome-encapsulated miR-122-5p and activate the FUT8/TGF-β1/Smads axis to promote metastasis [31]. Exosomes derived from chemoresistant CRC cells can transfer miR-122 between cells and promote glycolysis to reduce the drug sensitivity of chemosensitive cells [32].

Our findings also indicate that miR-122 is markedly up-regulated in colorectal cancer liver metastases. Overexpression of miR-122 promotes MC38 cell proliferation and migration while suppressing NEGR1 expression. Dual luciferase assays and RNA-pulldown experiments have confirmed that miR-122 directly targets NEGR1, thereby advancing the malignant phenotype of colorectal cancer. This suggests that miR-122 acts as a cancer-promoting factor in colorectal cancer. However, nanomaterials like the SendiH package of miR-122 appear to inhibit the development and progression of colorectal liver metastases [26].

NEGR1, a member of the IgLON family of cell adhesion molecules, is a membrane-bound protein involved in intercellular adhesion [6]. At present, research on NEGR1 is primarily focused in the field of neuroscience, where it plays a crucial regulatory role in neurodegenerative diseases, depression, and anxiety [8]. The effect of NEGR1 on tumors has also been reported in a few cases. The study by Pischedda et al. found that NEGR1 was down-regulated in neuroblastoma. Intervention therapy using NEGR1 protein and derived peptide induced ALK downregulation and inhibited neuroblastoma progression in vitro and in vivo [12]. Zhang et al. demonstrated that miR-222-3p targets NEGR1, influencing tumor immune infiltration and advancing the progression of thyroid cancer [13]. Jin et al. studies suggest that miR-576-5p promotes colonic carcinoma cells’ invasive progress by targeting NEGR1 [29]. Our study found that NEGR1 expression was decreased in colorectal cancer liver metastases, and overexpression of NEGR1 inhibited the proliferation and migration of colorectal cancer cells in mice. In the body, the expression of low NEGR1 can promote the occurrence of colorectal liver metastases. Through bioinformatics analysis, we explored that the PI3K/AKT pathway may be the downstream pathway of the miR-122/NEGR1 axis, playing an important role in colorectal cancer in the liver. In vitro, we found that knockout of NEGR1 increased the expression of p-PI3K and p-AKT, but had no significant effect on the expression of PI3K and AKT. The broad-spectrum PI3K inhibitor LY294002 intervention could partially reverse the effects caused by low NEGR1 after the activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. These results suggest that down-regulation of NEGR1 promotes the proliferation and migration of colorectal cancer cells by affecting the phosphorylation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. Numerous studies have shown that many factors affecting the progression and metastasis of colorectal cancer act through the PI3K/AKT pathway [30]. PI3K/AKT pathway is involved in regulating cell growth, which is crucial for survival, and metabolic signaling cascade. The PI3K family includes three types: I, II, and III. Type I is closely linked to cancer and is further divided into IA and IB. They have regulatory and catalytic subunit dimers of protein kinase [33]. The pathway involves growth factors and receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) that are activated in combination [34]. RTKs elevate and trigger the activation of PI3K to generate activated AKT kinase from PIP3 [35]. Activation of AKT kinase leads to the phosphorylation and regulation of various downstream targets, including transcription factors (FOSO), cell cycle regulatory factors (p21 and p27), and components of the mTOR pathway (mTOR and p70S6K), among others. Furthermore, studies have shown that the PI3K/AKT pathway upregulates ectomesenchymal markers in colorectal cancer metastasis and increases the expression of EMT-specific transcription factors, playing a crucial role in promoting the progression of colorectal cancer through the EMT process [36].

Further research has revealed that the progression of colorectal cancer is influenced not only by the tumor parenchymal cells but also by various components of the tumor immune microenvironment. Macrophages, a key immune cell type in the liver, play a significant role in colorectal cancer liver metastasis [37]. Increasing evidence suggests that tumor-associated macrophages are crucial in tumor development, as they reshape the tumor microenvironment through the secretion of various chemokines, cytokines, and growth factors. Recent studies have also linked the activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway with macrophage polarization. PI3K inhibition can lead to enhanced NF-kB activation and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) production, increasing the markers of macrophage M1 polarization. Conversely, PI3K inhibition negatively impacts the transfer factor and stimulates an increase in macrophage M2 surface markers [38]. Macrophages that secrete miRNAs can promote liver metastasis of colorectal cancer by inducing M2 polarization [39]. To investigate NEGR1’s impact on the immune microenvironment, we performed an immune correlation analysis using the TIMER database. The results indicated that NEGR1 expression in colorectal cancer is negatively correlated with M2 macrophage polarization, a finding corroborated by our experimental data. In vivo experiments with sh-NEGR1 MC38 cells showed an increase in M2 macrophages in the liver tumors of CRLM mice, with significantly elevated secretion of cytokines. Treatment with macrophage inhibitors such as CLD partially reversed this effect. In vitro, NEGR1 knockdown in colorectal cancer cells resulted in increased M2-type polarization and IL-10 secretion. Inhibiting IL-10 with AS101 in vivo partially mitigated the colorectal cancer liver metastasis progression induced by NEGR1 inhibition.

Our study highlights the miR-122/NEGR1 axis as a promising target for both prognosis and treatment of colorectal cancer progression and liver metastasis. Future analysis of serum and tissue samples could complement current clinical indicators, offering improved guidance for early prediction, diagnosis of colorectal cancer liver metastasis, and treatment decisions after diagnosis. However, the precise mechanism by which the miR-122/NEGR1 axis affects the PI3K/AKT pathway is not yet fully understood. Further research is needed to elucidate how NEGR1 knockout impacts macrophage polarization. The clinical relevance of these indicators should be validated through large-scale clinical trials.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the miR-122/NEGR1 axis is pivotal in the progression of colorectal cancer liver metastasis. NEGR1 knockout induces PI3K/AKT pathway activation, promotes M2 macrophage polarization and IL-10 release, and accelerates colorectal cancer liver metastasis in vivo. Targeting NEGR1 knockout presents a promising strategy for predicting and treating colorectal cancer liver metastasis.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with finite-time singularity formation of classical solutions to the following parabolic-parabolic-elliptic chemotaxis model accounting for indirect signal production

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+w,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
0=\Delta w-w+u,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=\partial_{\nu}w=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
(u(\cdot,0),v(\cdot,0))=(u_{0},v_{0}),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\partial_{\nu}
denotes the derivative with respect to the outward normal of
\partial\Omega
,
and the initial functions
u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
and

v_{0}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
are nonnegative.

Among the chemotaxis models that describe the oriented movement of organisms in response to chemical stimuli,
the minimal chemotaxis system

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla u-u\nabla v),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+u,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\end{cases}

(2)

This system was originally proposed by Keller and Segel
Keller and Segel (1970)
to investigate the aggregation of cells.
The density
u
of cells is influenced by their motion, which consists of random walk and chemotactic movement along the gradient of chemical concentration
v
.
As is well-known, the system (
2
) exhibits a competitive mechanism between diffusion and aggregation induced by chemotaxis where blowup—the extreme form of aggregation—can occur in finite time under certain conditions:

•

In one dimension, diffusion dominates aggregation, and all solutions of (
2
) are globally bounded
Osaki and Yagi (2001)
.

•

In a planar domain, blowup may occur in finite or infinite time if the initial mass
m:=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}
exceeds the critical mass
4\pi
but not equals to an integer multiple of
4\pi

Horstmann and Wang (2001)
; all solutions remain globally bounded if
m<4\pi
(or
8\pi
with symmetry assumptions)
Nagai et al. (1997)
.
For a disk-shaped domain
\Omega
, there exist radially symmetric initial data with mass
m\in(8\pi,\infty)
such that the solutions blow up in finite time
Mizoguchi and Winkler (2014)
.

•

In higher dimensions, diffusion is rather governed by chemotaxis, and for any initial mass
m>0
, there exist initial data with mass
m
that lead to finite-time blowup under radial settings
Winkler (2013)
.

Recently, chemotaxis models involving indirect signal production,
often expressed in the following typical form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+f(u,w),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\

For instance, in a study of predicting the cluster attack behaviors of mountain pine beetles

for beetles performing chemotaxis and the other by

Here,

When the signal producer is static, i.e.,

In contrast, Winkler

For the mass-preserving system (

For chemotaxis models (

Beyond four dimensions, Mao and Li

Main ideas and results

The system (
1
) has an energy dissipation structure identified by Fujie and Senba
Fujie and Senba (2017)
.
The Lyapunov functional

\mathcal{F}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}(u\log u-uv)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta+1)v|^{2},

satisfies the differential identity

\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{F}(u(t),v(t))+\mathcal{D}(u(t),v(t))=0

along the reasonably regular solution curves of the system (
1
).
Here, the dissipation rate reads as

\mathcal{D}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla(\Delta v-v+w)\right|^{2}+\left|\Delta v-v+w\right|^{2}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla(\log u-v)|^{2},

where
w:=(-\Delta_{N}+1)^{-1}u
is the unique classical solution of the Neumann elliptic problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta w+w=u,&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}w=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(4)

Invoking the above mathematical structure,
we are inspired by previous works (see Remark
Remark
below) to derive a lower bound for
\mathcal{F}
in terms of a sublinear power of
\mathcal{D}
in the form of

-\mathcal{F}\leq C(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}+1)\quad\text{with some }\theta\in(0,1)\text{ and }C>0,

(5)

for a large class of radially symmetric function pairs
(u,v)=(u(r),v(r))
, which, in particular, includes the radial trajectories of the system (
1
).
This result leads to a superlinear differential inequality for
-\mathcal{F}
,
which, in turn, implies occurrence of finite-time blowup for initial data with low energy.

To be precise, we shall suppose the initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
consist of nonnegative functions satisfying

0\lneqq u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad 0\leq v_{0}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\text{with }\partial_{\nu}v_{0}\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega

(6)

and additionally, we impose symmetry assumptions that

(u_{0},v_{0})\text{ is a pair of positive and radially symmetric functions on }\overline{\Omega},

(7)

with
\Omega=B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|x|<R\}
for some
R>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Our main result states that low initial energy enforces finite-time blowup.

.

Assume

for some

and

.

such that

from the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(m,A):=\{(u_{0},v_{0})\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\mid u_{0}\text{ and }v_{0}\text{ comply with }\eqref{h: initial data}\text{ and }\eqref{h: radial initial data}

4
Energy bounded below by sublinear dissipation rate

The goal of this section is to establish a lower bound for the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
in terms of a power of the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
, built upon the functional framework originally proposed by Winkler
Winkler (2013)
,
as discussed in the introduction,
where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{D}
are defined in (
11
).

To relate the energy
\mathcal{F}
to the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
under symmetry assumptions,
we introduce the following quantities

f:=-\Delta v+v-w

(21)

and

g:=\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{u}}-\sqrt{u}\nabla v\right)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|},\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\},

(22)

where

w:=(-\Delta_{N}+1)^{-1}u

(23)

is the unique classical solution to the Neumann elliptic problem (
4
).
Then the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
and the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
are given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(u\log u-uv)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta+1)v|^{2}\quad\text{and}\\
&\mathcal{D}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+\left|f\right|^{2}\right)+\int_{\Omega}g^{2}.\end{split}

(24)

According to Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
3.1
,
we will focus on positive and radially symmetric functions
(u,v)
that satisfy the mass constraints

\int_{\Omega}u=m,\quad\int_{\Omega}v\leq M\quad\text{and }L^{1}\text{-bound}\quad\int_{\Omega}|f|\leq B,

(25)

as well as the additional pointwise restrictions

\||x|^{n-1}w\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad\||x|^{\kappa-1}v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq B,

(26)

where
m>0
,
M>0
,
B>0
and
\kappa>n-2
are fixed parameters.
More precisely, the objective is to derive an inequality of the form

\frac{\mathcal{F}(u,v)}{\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(u,v)+1}\geqslant-C(m,M,B,\kappa)\quad\text{ for all }(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)

where
\theta\in(0,1)
and
C(m,M,B,\kappa)>0
.
Here and below,
\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
is the set of function pairs defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)

\displaystyle:=\{(u,v)\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\times C^{3}(\bar{\Omega})\mid u\text{ and }v\text{ are positive and radially }

\displaystyle\text{ symmetric functions satisfying \eqref{eq: constraint of mass}, \eqref{eq: pointwise constraint for v} and }\partial_{\nu}v\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

We begin with the following straightforward observation:
controlling the energy functional
\mathcal{F}
from below
is equivalent to bounding the mixed term
\int_{\Omega}uv
from above.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball of radius
R>0
with some
n\geq 4
.
Then

-\mathcal{F}(u,v)\leq\int_{\Omega}uv+\frac{\omega_{n}R^{n}}{e}

(27)

holds for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
,
where
\omega_{n}
is the surface area of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

It follows from the basic inequality
s\ln s\geq-e^{-1}
valid for all
s>0
.
∎

We incorporate decoupling techniques from Cao and Fuest
Cao and Fuest (2025)
,
which assist us to convert the mixed term
\int_{\Omega}uv
into an interior
L^{2}
–estimate for the Laplacian of the chemical concentration
v
. We will handle quadratic zeroth-order terms later.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball of radius
R>0
with some
n\geq 4
.
There exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}uv\leq 3\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+3\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C_{2}(m+M)B\rho^{2-\kappa},

holds for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
and all
\rho\in(0,R)
.

Proof.

In view of (
23
), we test
u=-\Delta w+w
by
v\mathds{1}_{B_{\rho}}
,
and compute upon integration by parts,

\begin{split}\int_{B_{\rho}}uv&=-\int_{B_{\rho}}\Delta wv+\int_{B_{\rho}}wv=-\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}v\partial_{\nu}w\;\mathrm{d}S+\int_{B_{\rho}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v+\int_{B_{\rho}}wv\\
&=\int_{B_{\rho}}(-\Delta v+v)w+\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}(w\partial_{\nu}v-v\partial_{\nu}w)\;\mathrm{d}S=:J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

(28)

Using

specified in (

we estimate by Young inequality

\begin{split}J_{1}&=\int_{B_{\rho}}(-\Delta v+v)^{2}+\int_{B_{\rho}}(\Delta v-v)f\leq\frac{3}{2}\|\Delta v-v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\\
&\leq 3\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+3\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}.\end{split}

(29)

Recalling pointwise information from (

we estimate

and also estimate the integral in the annular region

(31)

Summing (
28
)–(
31
) yields the desired estimate

\int_{\Omega}uv\leq 3\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+3\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+(2\omega_{n}MB+Bm)\rho^{2-\kappa},

for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
and
\rho\in(0,R)
,
which completes the proof.
∎

To leverage the structural information (
22
),
we employ the Pohožaev-type test function
|x|^{n-2}x\cdot\nabla v
over the inner region

,
initially introduced in the seminal work of Winkler
Winkler (2010)
;
Winkler (2010)
;
Winkler (2013)
.
This strategy enables us to establish an upper bound for the interior estimate

through a combination of dissipation rate quantities and lower-order signal components.
Specifically, this procedure generates a small factor
\rho^{2}
in the critical term
\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}

Through careful calibration (see Lemma

to effectively convert this quadratic term into a sublinear power of the dissipation rate quantity
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

Such test function methodologies have become standard tools in modern elliptic analysis, as evidenced by recent developments in
Cabré et al. (2020)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

&+\sqrt{m}\rho\|g\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C_{0}B^{2}\rho^{n+2-2\kappa}+C_{0}BM\rho^{2-\kappa}+2m\end{split}

holds for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
and
\rho\in(0,R)

Proof.

Denote

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\eta}(w_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}v_{r}r^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}r&=w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{r}r^{n-1}(v_{r}r^{n-1})_{r}\;\mathrm{d}r\\
&=w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{\eta}w_{r}yr^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}r,\quad\eta\in(0,R).\end{split}

Here, we abbreviate

Using

(35)

and similarly

(36)

Collecting (
33

36

multiplying by
\eta^{1-n}

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}y^{2}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}+w_{r}(\eta)v_{r}(\eta)\eta^{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}y^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r\\

(37)

for all
\eta\in(0,R)
,
where the last inequality follows from dropping the last term and Hölder inequality.
Integrating (

(0,\rho)\subset(0,R)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho}y^{2}\eta^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{0}^{\rho}w_{r}v_{r}\eta^{n-1}\;\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{n-1}{\eta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\eta}y^{2}r^{\alpha-1}\;\mathrm{d}r\;\mathrm{d}\eta\\

where
\mathcal{F}
is given in (
24
).

Proof.

Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
entail
there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+3\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C_{1}(Bm+BM)\rho^{2-\kappa}+C_{1}

holds for all
\rho\in(0,R)
.
Furthermore, Lemma
4.3
warrants the existence of
C_{3}>0
satisfying

\begin{split}&-\frac{1}{24}\mathcal{F}\leq 12\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C_{3}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\sqrt{m}\rho\|g\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}\\
&+\frac{9}{8}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+C_{3}B^{2}\rho^{n-2\kappa}+C_{3}(Bm+BM)\rho^{2-\kappa}+2m+C_{1}\end{split}

(43)

for all
\rho\in(0,R)
.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
Nirenberg (1959)
provides a constant
C_{gn}>0
such that

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{gn}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{4/(n+2)}+C_{gn}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

holds for all
\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.
Recalling (
25
) we thus have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{gn}B^{4/(n+2)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+C_{gn}B^{2},

(44)

as well as

\begin{split}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}&\leq C_{gn}M^{4/(n+2)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+C_{gn}M^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2^{n}C_{gn}^{(n+2)/2}M^{2}+C_{gn}M^{2}\end{split}

(45)

for all
\rho\in(0,R)

by Young inequality.
Invoking (
26
), we estimate

\begin{split}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})}\\
&\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+B^{2}\||x|^{1-\kappa}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\rho})}^{2}\\
&\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+\omega_{n}B^{2}R^{2}\rho^{n-2\kappa}\end{split}

(46)

for all
\rho\in(0,R)
.
Inserting (
44
)–(
46
) into (
43
) yields

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{24}\mathcal{F}&\leq 12\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{3}C_{gn}B^{4/(n+2)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+\sqrt{m}\rho\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C(\rho)\end{split}

for all
\rho\in(0,R)
,
where

\displaystyle C(\rho)

\displaystyle:=C_{3}C_{gn}B^{2}+2^{n+1}C_{gn}^{(n+2)/2}M^{2}+2C_{gn}M^{2}+\omega_{n}B^{2}R^{2}\rho^{n-2\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{3}B^{2}\rho^{n-2\kappa}+C_{3}(Bm+BM)\rho^{2-\kappa}+2m+C_{1}

\displaystyle\>\leq(C_{3}C_{gn}+2^{n+1}C_{gn}^{(n+2)/2}+2C_{gn}+1)(B^{2}+M^{2}+m^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\quad+(\omega_{n}R^{2}+C_{3}+C_{3}R^{\kappa-n+2}+C_{1}R^{2\kappa-n})(B^{2}+M^{2}+m^{2}+1)\rho^{n-2\kappa}.

Taking

\begin{split}C_{4}&:=C_{3}C_{gn}+2^{n+1}C_{gn}^{(n+2)/2}+2C_{gn}+1+\omega_{n}+2C_{3}+C_{1},\end{split}

we finish the proof.
∎

Here we shall choose
\rho
appropriately, following the strategy of Winkler
Winkler (2013)
,
to establish a lower bound of the energy
\mathcal{F}
by a sublinear power of the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball of radius
R>0
with some
n\geq 5
.
Then there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
and
C(m,M,B,\kappa)>0
such that

-\mathcal{F}\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}+1)

(47)

holds for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
,
where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{D}
are specified as in (
24
).

Proof.

Put

\rho:=\min\left\{\frac{R}{2},\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{n}{(n+2)(2\kappa-n)}}\right\},

and let

\theta:=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{n+2}>1-\frac{n}{(n+2)(2\kappa-n)}>0,&\text{if }\kappa\in\big(n-2,\frac{3n}{4}\big),\\
1-\frac{n}{(n+2)(2\kappa-n)}\geq\frac{n}{n+2},&\text{if }\kappa\in\big[\frac{3n}{4},\infty\big).\end{cases}

We may infer the existence of
C(m,M,B,\kappa)>0
from Lemma
4.4
such that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)(\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\rho^{n-2\kappa})

holds for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
.
If
\rho<R/2
, then

\begin{split}-\mathcal{F}&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)(\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{n/(n+2)})\\
&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)\left(3\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\theta}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\theta}+4\right)\\
&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}+1),\end{split}

(48)

by Young inequality and
monotonicity of the mapping
[0,\infty)\ni\eta\mapsto\eta^{\theta}\in[0,\infty)
.
On the other hand, if
\rho=R/2
, then we can estimate analogously

\begin{split}-\mathcal{F}&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)\left(\rho^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2n/(n+2)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{2\kappa-n}}{R^{2\kappa-n}}\right)\\
&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)\left(2\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\theta}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\theta}+3+\frac{2^{2\kappa-n}}{R^{2\kappa-n}}\right)\\
&\leq C(m,M,B,\kappa)(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}+1).\end{split}

(49)

Hence, (
47
) follows from (
48
) and (
49
).
∎

5
Finite-time blowup enforced by low initial energy

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
For initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
with low energy
\mathcal{F}(u_{0},v_{0})
, we may deduce a superlinear differential inequality for the mapping
t\mapsto-\mathcal{F}(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t))
from (
47
),
which implies that
(u,v)
cannot exist globally.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\geq 5
and
R>0
.
Let
m>0
,
A>0
and
\kappa>n-2
be given.
Then there exist positive constants
K(m,A,\kappa)
and
T(m,A,\kappa)
such that
given any
(u_{0},v_{0})
from the set

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}(m,A,\kappa)&:=\{(u_{0},v_{0})\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\mid u_{0}\text{ and }v_{0}\text{ comply with }\eqref{h: initial data}\text{ and }\\
&\eqref{h: radial initial data}\text{ such that }\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=m,\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}<A\\
&\text{and }\mathcal{F}(u_{0},v_{0})<-K(m,A,\kappa)\},\end{split}

(50)

for the corresponding classical solution with maximal existence time
T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})
given by Proposition
2.1
,
one has
T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})\leq T(m,A,\kappa)
.

Proof.

Let us fix
c_{1}>0
such that

\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|\psi\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }\psi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega).

Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
3.1
give
c_{2}>0
,
c_{3}>0
and
c_{4}=c_{4}(\kappa)>0
such that
whenever
(u_{0},v_{0})
complies with (
6
), the corresponding solution
(u,v,w)
satisfies
L^{1}
estimate

\|v_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}(m+\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)})\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})),

(51)

as well as pointwise estimates

\||x|^{n-1}w\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{3}m\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})),

(52)

and

\||x|^{\kappa-1}v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{4}(m+\|v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)})

(53)

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0}))
.
Now writing

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\max\{c_{1}A,m,c_{3}m\}\quad\text{and}\quad M:=\max\{c_{2}(m+A),c_{4}(m+A)\},

we invoke Lemma
4.5
to obtain
c_{5}=C(m,M,B,\kappa)>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

-\mathcal{F}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\leq c_{5}(\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})+1)\quad\text{for all }(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa).

(54)

We will show the desired conclusion holds if we define

K(m,A,\kappa):=c_{5}

(55)

and

T(m,A,\kappa):=\frac{\theta c_{5}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\theta)(-\mathcal{F}(u_{0},v_{0})-c_{5})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}}.

Indeed, we know from Proposition
2.1
that for any given
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}(m,A,\kappa)
,
the corresponding solution
(u,v,w)
is smooth and radially symmetric with
u>0
and
v>0
in
\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0}))
.
We infer from (
51
)–(
53
) that

\|v_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}(m+A)\leq M\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})),

as well as pointwise estimates

\||x|^{n-1}w\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{3}m\leq B\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})),

and

\||x|^{\kappa-1}v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{4}(m+A)\leq M

for all
t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0}))
.
Since moreover

\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}=m
and

\int_{\Omega}v(x,t)\leq\max\left\{\int_{\Omega}v_{0},\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\right\}\leq\max\{c_{1}A,m\}\leq B

for all

by (

) and (

),
it follows that
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}(m,M,B,\kappa)
for all

and thus (

) may be applied to

(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t))
for all
t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0}))
.
From (
10
) and (
55
), we see
that

\mathcal{F}(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t))\leq\mathcal{F}(u_{0},v_{0})<-K(m,A,\kappa)=-c_{5}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})),

and thus we may invert (
54
) to get

-\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{D}\geq\left(\frac{-\mathcal{F}-c_{5}}{c_{5}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0},v_{0})).

(56)

We shall see

.
Otherwise a direct comparison argument by solving (

) gives

(-\mathcal{F}(u,v)-c_{5})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\geq\frac{\theta c_{5}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\theta)\left(T(m,A,\kappa)-t\right)}

We thus verify

.

6
Initial data with large negative energy

This section is devoted to the existence of initial data with low energy.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded and smooth domain.
Then for any choice of function pair
(u_{0},v_{0})
satisfying (
6
),
one can find a sequence

\{(u_{k},v_{k})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of function couples such that

(u_{k},v_{k})
satisfies (
6
) for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
that

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N},

(57)

that

v_{k}\to v_{0}\quad\text{in }W^{2,2}(\Omega)\text{ as }k\to\infty,

(58)

that

u_{k}\to u_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega)\text{ for all }p\in\bigg[1,\frac{2n}{n+4}\bigg)\text{ as }k\to\infty,

(59)

and that

\mathcal{F}(u_{k},v_{k})\to-\infty,\quad\text{as }k\to 0,

(60)

where
\mathcal{F}
is taken from (
11
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
B_{R}\subset\Omega
for some
R>0
.
Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a radially symmetric and nonnegative function with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi=1
,
which is compactly supported in
B_{1}
.
Fixing
\gamma>0
,
we define for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

u_{k}(x):=u_{0}(x)+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right)-\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\cdot\frac{u_{0}(x)}{\int_{\Omega}u_{0}},\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

and

v_{k}(x):=v_{0}(x)+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\gamma}\eta^{2-\frac{n}{2}}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

where

\eta=\eta(k):=\frac{\eta_{\star}}{k+1},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

and
\eta_{\star}\in(0,1)\cap(0,R)
is fixed such that

\eta^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}<\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(61)

A direct computation can verify the mass identity (
57
).
Since

\|v_{k}-v_{0}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\gamma}\|\phi\|_{W^{2,2}(B_{1})}

are valid for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\gamma>0
ensures (
58
).
We estimate by triangle inequality,

\begin{split}\|u_{k}-u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{-2-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{p}}\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\\
&\quad+\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{2\gamma}\eta^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\cdot\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\int_{\Omega}u_{0}}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }p\in[1,\infty).\end{split}

Then (
59
) follows due to
-2-n/2+n/p>0
and
n>4
.
To verify (
60
),
we evaluate each term from
\mathcal{F}
.
Young inequality entails

\int_{\Omega}|v_{k}|^{2}\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-2\gamma}\eta^{4}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}

(62)

and

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v_{k}|^{2}\leq 2\|\Delta v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-2\gamma}\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(63)

Thanks to (
61
),

u_{k}
is nonnegative for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
thus we can drop the nonnegative summands and estimate from below

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}v_{k}&\geq\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right)\eta^{-n}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

(64)

Noting that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\ln u_{k}&\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{k}^{q}-u_{k}}{q-1}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{k}^{q}}{q-1}\\

for all

and

.
Collecting (

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(u_{k},v_{k})&=\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}\log u_{k}-u_{k}v_{k})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta+1)v_{k}|^{2}\\
&\leq-\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\gamma}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+2\left(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-2\gamma}(\|\Delta\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2})\\
&\quad+\frac{2^{q-1}}{q-1}\bigg(\ln\frac{1}{\eta}\bigg)^{2q\gamma}\eta^{-\frac{nq}{2}-2q+n}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}^{q}\\
&\quad+2\|\Delta v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{2^{q-1}}{q-1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\end{split}

for all

and

,

as

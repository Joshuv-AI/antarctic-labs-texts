Abstract
In the present work we consider state-dependent quasi-probability densities W ⁡ [ θ , p ] W[\theta,p] and W 1 / 2 ​ [ θ , p ] W_{1/2}[\theta,p] , depending on two parameters θ \theta and p p , where − π ≤ θ ≤ π -\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi and p p can take any real number. For pure states and integer values of p p the corresponding marginal distributions for W ⁡ [ p ] W[p] and W 1 / 2 ​ [ p ] W_{1/2}[p] are positive and in accordance with Born’s rule in quantum mechanics for the angular momentum observable L = − i d / d θ L=-id/d\theta . For the states ψ ⁡ ( θ ) \psi(\theta) of the form in Ref. [ 1 ] , that can have half-integer angular momentum average values, negative values of W ⁡ [ θ , p ] W[\theta,p] and W 1 / 2 ​ [ θ , p ] W_{1/2}[\theta,p] can reveal non-classical features but in an ambiguous manner. In line with Ref. [ 2 ] , it is shown that experimental data of the uncertainties Δ ​ θ \Delta\theta and Δ ​ L \Delta L could be sufficient to reveal quantum-mechanical features of such states ψ ⁡ ( θ ) \psi(\theta) without necessarily making use of quasi-probability densities.

CHOICE OF QUANTUM STATE

2.1
Extension of the S. Franke-Aronold et al. 2004
Intelligent State

The pure quantum state
\psi(\theta)

2\pi
-periodic extension of the state defined on a circle with an angular position
\theta

-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
as discussed in detail in Ref.

[
2

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c(n)\psi_{n}(\theta)=\frac{N}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\displaystyle{-in\bar{\theta}}}e^{-\displaystyle{(n-\bar{l})^{2}/2\lambda}}\psi_{n}(\theta)\,\,

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2\pi\lambda}}e^{\displaystyle{il(\theta}-\bar{\theta})}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\displaystyle{in(\theta}-\bar{\theta})}e^{-\displaystyle{(n-\epsilon)^{2}/2\lambda}}\,\,,

(3)

L=-id/d\theta

[
1

In passing, we observe that a completely different, but formally related state with a Gaussian-like amplitude as in Eq.(

\displaystyle 1=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta|\psi(\theta)|^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\displaystyle{(n-\epsilon)^{2}/\lambda}}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}e^{\displaystyle{-\epsilon^{2}}/\lambda}\vartheta_{3}[-i\epsilon/\lambda,e^{\displaystyle{-1/\lambda}}]

\displaystyle=N^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\vartheta_{3}[-\epsilon\pi,e^{\displaystyle{-\lambda\pi^{2}}}]\,\,,

2.2
Expectation Values of the Angular Momentum Observable
L

The parameter
\bar{l}
can now be related to various quantum mechanical expectation values of the observable
L
such as

\displaystyle\langle L\rangle=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}ne^{\displaystyle{-(n-\overline{l})^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\langle L^{2}\rangle=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{2}e^{\displaystyle{-(n-\overline{l})^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,,

(10)

independent of the parameter
\bar{\theta}
where, of course,
\langle{\cal O}\rangle=\langle\psi|{\cal O}|\psi\rangle
for an observable
{\cal O}
in quantum mechanics. In terms of
\bar{l}=l+\epsilon
as defined above, we can write Eqs.(
10

\displaystyle\langle L\rangle=l+\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}ne^{\displaystyle{-(n-\epsilon)^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,

(11)

and

\displaystyle\langle L^{2}\rangle=l^{2}+\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n(n+2l)e^{\displaystyle{-(n-\epsilon)^{2}/\lambda}}\,\,.

(12)

It then follows that

\displaystyle(\Delta L)^{2}=\langle L^{2}\rangle-\langle L\rangle^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{2}e^{\displaystyle{-(n-\epsilon)^{2}/\lambda}}

\displaystyle-(\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}ne^{\displaystyle{-(n-\epsilon)^{2}/\lambda}})^{2}\geq 0\,,

(13)

where the lower bound can be obtained directly by making use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Therefore the expectation value
\langle L\rangle
depends on the integer
l
but the uncertainty
\Delta L
depends only on the fractional part
\epsilon
of
\bar{l}=l+\epsilon
.

With
\epsilon=0
the integer
l
can be identified with the expectation value
\langle L\rangle
of the angular momentum. The general expression for
(\Delta L)^{2}
in Eq.(
13

\vartheta_{3}[z,q]
and derivatives with respect to the parameter
z

\lambda

[
59
]
, with, e.g., the result

\displaystyle(\Delta L)^{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2}-2\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}e^{\displaystyle-\pi^{2}\lambda}\cos(2\pi\epsilon)+{\cal O}(\lambda^{2}\cos(4\pi\epsilon)e^{\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\lambda})>0\,,

(14)

leading to upper and lower bounds
\lambda/2\pm 2\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}\exp(-\pi^{2}\lambda)
.
In neglecting the exponentially small terms
{\cal O}(\lambda^{2}e^{\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\lambda})
one finds that numerically Eq.(
14
) is valid at least for
\lambda\geq 1

due to various non-trivial cancellations of
\epsilon
and
\lambda
dependent terms. These upper and lower limits obtained for
(\Delta L)^{2}
are not necessarily the best bounds but this issue will not be discussed in more detail here.
In the limit of large values of
\lambda
,
we therefore have that
\langle L\rangle=\bar{l}
. By making use of the large
\lambda

[
2

1

{\cal O}(\lambda^{2}e^{\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\lambda})

2.3
The Limit of Small

In the limit of small

\displaystyle(\Delta L)^{2}=\frac{e^{\displaystyle{-(1-2\epsilon)/\lambda}}}{(1+e^{\displaystyle{-(1-2\epsilon)/\lambda}})^{\displaystyle{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{n\gamma}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\displaystyle{i(n+\gamma)\theta}}\,,

in Eq.(

3
THE QUASI-PROBABILITY DISTRIBUTION
{\bm{W[\theta,p]}}

3.1
A Finite Domain for the Parameter
\theta

We are now interested in defining suitable quasi-probability distributions in terms of two parameters
\theta
and
p
such that
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
and where
p
in principle can take any real value
leading to marginal distributions for the observables
\theta
and

from which expectation values can be computed and compared with the rules of quantum mechanics for a pure state
\psi(\theta)
in a similar fashion as for the Wigner function
W[q,p]
in Eqs.(

The quasi-probability distributions we will consider reveal the presence of fractional expectation values of
\langle L\rangle
for the pure state
\psi(\theta)
considered in Ref.

[
1

for non-zero values of the parameter
\lambda

It is, however, now not obvious how to extend the definitions in Eq.(

\theta
(for reviews and further references see, e.g., Refs.

[
21

22

26

we propose the following real-valued definition of a quasi-probability distribution

which is

-periodic in the variable

\displaystyle W[\theta,p]=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}e^{\displaystyle-i2p\theta^{\prime}}\psi(\theta+\theta^{\prime})\psi^{*}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})=\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta^{\prime}}\psi(\theta+\theta^{\prime}/2)\psi^{*}(\theta-\theta^{\prime}/2)~,

(18)

of periodicity for the state
\psi(\theta)
is taken into account. As will be argued below this range of integration is of particular importance when we consider a quantum state with fractional angular momentum. The second equivalent expression in Eq.(
18

is bounded according to
|W[\theta,p]|\leq 1/2\pi
as follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

In terms of an integer
l

0\leq\epsilon<1
and
\bar{l}=l+\epsilon

(28)

It then follows that

as it should, which is the reason for the overall normalization factor which appears in Eq.(

in Eq.(

[

]
.

The distribution
W[\theta,p]
according to Eq.(

), with

now with

q=\exp(-1/2\lambda)=0.001
,
\epsilon=1/2
, and
\bar{l}=l+1/2
in the range

for the state

). The symmetry with respect to the line
m=0
as in Fig.

is now not present. For half-integer values of

the distribution

can be negative at
|\theta|={\cal O}(\pi)
and the parameter

is fractional.

In order to obtain the marginal distribution
W[\theta]
for the parameter
\theta
when
p
takes any integer value, we now invoke the reasonable physical requirement that the Fourier expansion in Eq.(
3
) as well as in Eq.(
22
) should make sense pointwise. In terms of pure mathematics and for integers
p
it is well-known that this is in general a delicate issue involving partial sums of Fourier series and the use of the rigorous mathematical Lebesgue method of integration as in Ref.

[
62
]
.

The expression for
W[\theta,p]
in Eq.(
18
) for integers
p
is, however, a well-defined and periodic in
\theta
. In order to make sense of the marginal distribution
W[\theta]=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}W[\theta,p]
we now make use of the well established arithmetic Cesàro sums of partial sums and Fejér summation methods of series (see, e.g., theorem
101
in the first reference in Ref.

[
63
]
). In practical applications, like in signal analysis of experimental data, this means that we restrict any partial sum that enters in the analysis to be finite and therefore summations and integrations can be interchanged. In the end we then formally remove this restriction. As is well-known, this procedure actually leads to pointwise convergence, and it appears to be in line with actual experimental signal analysis. For the convenience of the reader, we now explain in some detail the method of Fejér summation of series which takes boundary conditions into account.

We therefore first define a partial sum expression
\sigma_{n}(\theta)
for
W[\theta]
by making use of the second equivalent and convenient definition in Eq.(
18
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{p=-n}^{n}W[\theta,p]=\sum_{p=-n}^{n}\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)\frac{\sin((n+1/2)\theta^{\prime})}{\sin\theta^{\prime}/2}\equiv\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)D(n,\theta^{\prime})~,

(29)

where the finite function
f(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
in the range
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in[-\pi,\pi]
is defined according to

\displaystyle f(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\psi(\theta+\theta^{\prime})\psi^{*}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})=\sum_{n,m}\Large(c(n)\frac{e^{\displaystyle in\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Large)\Large(c^{*}(m)\frac{e^{\displaystyle-im\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Large)e^{\displaystyle i(m+n)\theta^{\prime}}~,

(30)

which is
2\pi
-periodic in the variables
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
. Here we make use of the definition of the well-known periodic Dirichlet kernel
D(n,\theta)=\sin((n+1/2)\theta)/\sin(\theta/2)
. A periodic and positive Fejér kernel
F(M,\theta)
defined by

\displaystyle F(M,\theta)=\frac{1}{M}(\frac{\sin(M\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)})^{\displaystyle 2}\,,

(31)

is then obtained by forming the arithmetic mean sum
\sigma(M,\theta)
of the partial sum
\sigma_{n}(\theta)
in Eq.(
29
), i.e., we define the Cesàro sum

\displaystyle\sigma(M,\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sigma_{k}(\theta)=\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)\frac{1}{M}(\frac{\sin(M\theta^{\prime}/2)}{\sin(\theta^{\prime}/2)})^{2}\equiv\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)F(M,\theta^{\prime})\,.

(32)

In order to control the large
M
singular behaviour of the Fejér kernel
F(M,\theta)
at
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pm 2\pi
as used in Eq.(
32
)
we split the range of integration into three domains namely
\theta^{\prime}\in I_{1}=[-\pi,\pi]
,
I_{2}=[-2\pi,-\pi]
, and
I_{3}=[\pi,2\pi]
.
Since the Fejér kernel
F(M,\theta)
is normalized in such a way that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}F(M,\theta)=1~,

(33)

for all integers
M
we can trivially write

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1}}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)F(M,\theta^{\prime})=\frac{f(\theta,0)}{2}+\int_{I_{1}}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}(f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)-f(\theta,0))F(M,\theta^{\prime})\,.

(34)

The range of integration over
\theta^{\prime}\in I_{1}
in Eq.(
34
) is then divided into
-\delta<\theta^{\prime}<\delta
, and
\delta\leq|\theta^{\prime}|\leq\pi
, where
\delta>0
is assumed to be sufficiently small. In the range
-\delta<\theta^{\prime}<\delta
the contribution to the integration can then be made arbitrarily small due to the continuity properties of
f(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
. In the range
\delta\leq|\theta^{\prime}|\leq\pi
we can, on the other hand, make use of the bound
F(M,\theta^{\prime})\leq 1/M\sin^{2}(\delta/2)
and make the contribution arbitrarily small in the large
M
-limit. For sufficiently large values of
M
the integral in Eq.(
34
) can then be made arbitrarily small in both ranges of integration and the only contributing term is
f(\theta,0)/2
which corresponds to Fejérs theorem.

In a similar manner we consider

\displaystyle\int_{I_{2}}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)F(M,\theta^{\prime})=\frac{f(\theta,-\pi)}{4}+\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}(f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2-\pi)-f(\theta,-\pi))F(M,\theta^{\prime})\,,

(35)

after a shift of the variable of integration according to
\theta^{\prime}\rightarrow\theta^{\prime}+2\pi
and using the periodicity
F(M,\theta^{\prime}-2\pi)=F(M,\theta^{\prime})
. The last integral in Eq.(
35
) can then be treated as in Eq.(
34
). For
\theta^{\prime}\in I_{3}
we proceed in a similar manner using a shift of integration variable according to
\theta^{\prime}\rightarrow\theta^{\prime}-2\pi
.
In the limit of large
M
the marginal distribution
W[\theta]=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}W[\theta,p]
to be obtained from Eq.(
18
) for integers
p
is now defined to be given by the expression
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sigma(M,\theta)
and therefore leads to

\displaystyle W[\theta]=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\psi(\theta)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(|\psi(\theta-\pi)|^{2}+|\psi(\theta+\pi)|^{2})\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\psi(\theta)|^{2}+|\psi(\theta+\pi)|^{2}\right)\,,

(36)

where we make use of the
2\pi
-periodicity of the state
\psi(\theta)
as defined in Eq.(
3
). The marginal distribution
W[\theta]
so defined is correctly normalized after an integration over the angle
\theta
in the range
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
.

A less precise and formal procedure in order to obtain Eq.(

(see, e.g., Ref.

) in Eq.(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta}=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(\theta-2\pi p)\,,

(37)

in terms of a periodic Dirac

-function with formally a half contribution at the boundary of integration

We now observe the following alternative expression for the Fejér kernel

\displaystyle F(M,\theta)=\sum_{p=-M}^{M}(1-|\frac{p}{M}|)e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta}\,,

(38)

). If the integers

, the bracket in Eq.(

without this bracket finite. If such a cut-off is considered in the data analysis of an experimental situation, we may again be led to Eq.(

.

3.3
W[\theta,p]
when the range of
p
is all real numbers

The distribution
W[\theta,p]
in Eq.(
18
) can now be regarded as a well-defined extension of a function not only for integer values of
p
but also for any real number
p
. For real numbers
p
the corresponding marginal distribution
W[p]
is, however, then not a necessarily a positive function of
p
and can therefore not in general be interpreted as a probability distribution. The physical meaning of the marginal
W[p]
for all real numbers is, furthermore, not entirely clear since the measure of rational numbers in the set of real numbers is zero (see, e.g.,
[
72
]
).

In this work we focus our attention on fractional values of the parameter
p
but here we nevertheless find it useful to recall the following known extension of the Cesáro summability methods for integers as used in Section
3.2
to ill-defined integrals over
p
. Therefore Eq.(
32
) is extended to the large
\lambda
-limit of the following expression

\displaystyle\sigma(M,\theta)\rightarrow\sigma(\lambda,\theta)=\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int_{-\lambda}^{\lambda}dp(1-|\frac{p}{\lambda}|)f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta^{\prime}}~,

(39)

to be used as a well-defined definition of
W[\theta]=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\sigma(\lambda,\theta)
. Here
we make use of the following extension of Eq.(
38
) to Cesáro summability of integrals which is easy to verify namely

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}dn\int_{-n}^{n}dps(p)=\int_{-\lambda}^{\lambda}dp(1-|\frac{p}{\lambda}|)s(p)\,,

(40)

for a function
s(p)
(see, e.g., Chapter I and subsection 1.15 in Ref.
[
73
]
for a slightly simpler version of Eq.(
40
)). We will make use of the right-hand expression of Eq.(
40
) in actual computations. Below we, however, verify that the use of Eq.(
40
) will not always give rise to
well-defined expressions in the limit of large
\lambda
. If required we can then improve the large
\lambda
behaviour by considering iterative replacements like
s(p)\rightarrow(1-|p/\lambda|)s(p)
in Eq.(
40
).

With the choice
s(p)=\exp(-ip\theta^{\prime})
we now obtain the result

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda,\theta)=\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{4\pi}f(\theta,\theta^{\prime}/2)K_{\lambda}(\theta^{\prime})=\int_{-\lambda\pi}^{\lambda\pi}\frac{dx}{2\pi}f(\theta,x/\lambda)(\frac{\sin x}{x})^{2}~,

(41)

in terms of the function
f(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
in Eq.(
30
), and
where we have defined the positive kernel
K_{\lambda}(\theta)
by

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\frac{\sin(\lambda\theta/2)}{\theta/2})^{2}~.

(42)

We observe that the kernel
K_{\lambda}(\theta)
is a regular function at the boundary points
\theta=\pm 2\pi
and is normalized according to

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-2\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{4\pi}K_{\lambda}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\,.

(43)

The method as considered in the evaluation of the large
M
-limit of
\sigma(M,\theta)
in Eq.(
32
) can now be applied in order to evaluate the large
\lambda
-limit of
\sigma(\lambda,\theta)
in Eq.(
41
). The kernel
K_{\lambda}(\theta)
is then such that only an interval like
I_{1}
as used in
Eq.(
34
) contributes in the large
\lambda
-limit. Alternatively one can consider the large
\lambda
-limit of the last expression in Eq.(
41
) and one finds that

\displaystyle W[\theta]=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\sigma(\lambda,\theta)=\frac{1}{2}|\psi(\theta)|^{2}~.

(44)

In order to compute moments
\langle p^{\alpha}\rangle
of the variable
p
for integers
\alpha\geq 0
using the distribution
W[p]
, we proceed as in the discussion in Section
3.2
and first make the sum in Eq.(
22
) finite by a restriction
|n|\leq M
for some sufficiently large integer
M
.
For integers
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, and in terms of the Cesáro summability for integrals in Eq.(
40
), we then make sense of the formal expression for
\langle p^{\alpha}\rangle
by considering the general replacement

\displaystyle\langle p^{\alpha}\rangle\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dpp^{\alpha}W[p]\rightarrow\sum_{|n|\leq M}|c(n)|^{2}\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\lambda}^{\lambda}dpp^{\alpha}(1-|\frac{p}{\lambda}|)^{\beta}\mathrm{sinc}[2\pi(p-n))]~.

(45)

For the marginal distribution as defined in
W[p]
in Eq.(
45
) with
\alpha=\beta=0
, it is then straightforward to, e.g., verify that in the large
M
-limit

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dpW[p]=\frac{1}{2}~,

(46)

which is consistent with the normalization of the marginal distribution
W[\theta]
in Eq.(
44
).

It is now a remarkable property of the large
\lambda
-limit in Eq.(
45
) that the result Eq.(
46
) for
\alpha=0
is valid for all
\beta\geq 0
as we have verified by computing the relevant integrals in an exact manner (for a related discussion see Chapter I and subsection 1.15 in Ref.
[
73
]
). In the case with
\alpha=1
and
\beta=0
one, however, finds that Eq.(
45
) is ill-defined. With
\alpha=\beta=1
the Cesáro summability as used in Eq.(
45
) turns out to be well-defined in the large
\lambda
-limit and is independent of all integers
\beta\geq 1
if
\alpha=1
. For
\beta=1
the moment
\langle p^{2}\rangle
is, however, then ill-defined and can be well-defined by making use of the iterative procedure mentioned in connection with the Cesáro summability procedure defined in Eq.(
40
). With
\alpha=\beta=2
it turns out that
\langle p^{2}\rangle
is well-defined and independent of integers
\beta\geq 2
if
\alpha=2
. In general these non-trivial results can now be extended in such a way that for any normalized state
\psi(\theta)
and in the large
M
-limit in Eq.(
45
)

\displaystyle\langle p^{\alpha}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}|c(n)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\langle L^{\alpha}\rangle~,

(47)

for integers
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\beta\geq\alpha
where, of course,
\langle L^{\alpha}\rangle=\langle\psi|L^{\alpha}|\psi\rangle
is the expectation value in quantum mechanics as discussed in Section
2.2
.

4
THE QUASI-PROBABILITY DISTRIBUTION
{\bm{W_{1/2}[\theta,p]}}

4.1
A Finite Domain for the Parameter
\theta

Another definition of a quasi-probability distribution considered in great theoretical detail in the literature (see, e.g., Refs.

[
21
,
26
]
and references cited therein) is

\displaystyle W_{1/2}[\theta,p]=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}e^{\displaystyle-ip\theta^{\prime}}\psi(\theta+\theta^{\prime}/2)\psi^{*}(\theta-\theta^{\prime}/2)~,

(48)

and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality now implies the bound
|W_{1/2}[\theta,p]|\leq 1/\pi
. It then follows that
Eq.(
22
) is replaced by

\displaystyle W_{1/2}[p]=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta W_{1/2}[\theta,p]=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|c(n)|^{2}\mathrm{sinc}[\pi(n-p)]~,

(49)

which for integer values of
p
is in accordance with Born’s rule in quantum mechanics as in Eq.(
28
). Proceeding as in Section
3.1
for the quasi-distribution
W[\theta,p]
, we can regard
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
in Eq.(
49
) to be a well-defined function also for any real number
p
. We therefore again conclude that the marginal distribution
W_{1/2}[p]
is not always a positive function of
p
, i.e., it cannot in general be interpreted as a probability distribution. Furthermore one obtains equations similar to Eqs.(
24
)-(
27
) by a simple change of factors of
\pi
. If we, e.g., consider the state
\psi(\theta)
in Eq.(
3
) with

5
MEASUREMENT OF UNCERTAINTIES

5.1
The Probability Distributions
p_{1}(\theta)
and
p_{2}(\theta)

We have observed that the state-dependent uncertainties
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
do not depend on the parameter
l
in the quantum state Eq.(
3
). Without resorting to general properties of quasi-probability distributions like
W[\theta,p]
or
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
, we now raise the question to what extent such state-dependent observables can be used to directly observe signs of more peculiar properties of states in the form of Eq.(
3
) in particular when the parameter
\bar{l}
is a half-integer
\bar{l}=l+1/2
.

In the spirit of the theoretical as well as the experimental work considered in Ref.

[
2
]
, we will now verify that this is indeed possible. As discussed in more detail in Ref.

[
1
]
for the state in Eq.(
3
), the procedure in Ref.

[
2
]
is based on the elimination of the dependence of the parameter
\lambda
in terms of the state-dependent observables
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
. One then considers the statistics of many prepared states of the same form but only with different values of the parameter
\lambda
in order to obtain data for the observables
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
.

In this manner one can then, as we verify below, describe some special features of states in the form of Eq.(
3
) by making use of only the knowledge of a measurable probability distribution defined by

\displaystyle p_{1}(\theta)\equiv|\psi(\theta)|^{2}\,.

(58)

In a similar manner we also define the probability distribution
p_{2}(\theta)
according to

\displaystyle p_{2}(\theta)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{1}(\theta)+p_{1}(\theta+\pi)\right)\,.

(59)

The probability distributions
p_{2}(\theta)
and
p_{1}(\theta)
have, of course, a correspondence to the well-defined
marginal probability distributions
W[\theta]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta]
as obtained from the quasi-probability distributions
W[\theta,p]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
for integer values of the parameter
p
as discussed in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.
We leave it open to what extent the probability distributions
p_{1}(\theta)
and/or
p_{2}(\theta)
correspond to experimentally accessible data but will show that they give rise to quite different predictions.

In Fig.
5
we display
\Delta\theta
as a function of
\Delta L
by making use of the probability distribution
p_{1}(\theta)
or
p_{2}(\theta)
for quantum states of the form as in Eq.(
3
) with a fixed fractional value
\bar{l}=l+1/2
, where
l
is an integer. For each value of the parameter
\lambda
one then obtains a corresponding pair of uncertainties
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
. The limits of small or large values of the parameter
\lambda
can now be analysed in detail in order to explain characteristic features of Fig.
5
obtained from a numerical evaluation using the exact state in Eq.(
3
).

5.2
p_{1}(\theta)
and Expansions of the Parameter
\lambda

As discussed in Section
2
, the use of Eq.(
3
) with
\bar{l}=l+1/2
and in the limit of small
\lambda
leads to the superposition of states in Eq.(
16
) with expectation values
\langle L\rangle=l+1/2
and
\Delta L=1/2
. For
\bar{l}=l+\epsilon
and
0\leq\epsilon<1
, the limit of small
\lambda
leads to angular momentum eigenstates
\psi_{l}(\theta)
or
\psi_{l+1}(\theta)
with
\Delta\theta=\pi/\sqrt{3}
, and
\Delta L=0
if
\epsilon\neq 1/2
.

In the small
\lambda
-limit of the quantum state
\psi(\theta)
in Eq.(
3
)
it is now convenient to make use of Eq.(
8
) in order to find
p_{1}(\theta)
by expanding the theta functions
\vartheta_{3}[\theta/2-i\epsilon/2\lambda,q]
and
\vartheta_{3}[-i\epsilon/\lambda,q^{2}]
in powers of
q=\exp(-1/2\lambda)
. With
\bar{\theta}=0
, the definition in Eq.(
5
) then leads to the expressions

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}[\theta/2-i\epsilon/2\lambda,q]=1+e^{\displaystyle{\epsilon/\lambda}}e^{\displaystyle{i\theta}}q+e^{\displaystyle{-\epsilon/\lambda}}e^{\displaystyle{-i\theta}}q+{\cal O}(q^{4})\,,

(60)

and

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}[-i\epsilon/\lambda,q^{2}]=1+e^{\displaystyle{2\epsilon/\lambda}}q^{2}+e^{\displaystyle{-2\epsilon/\lambda}}q^{2}+{\cal O}(q^{8})\,.

(61)

It is now clear from Eqs.(
60
) and (
61
) that the rational number
\epsilon=1/2
plays a special role as has already been remarked above.
In the limit of a sufficiently small
\lambda
with
\epsilon<1/2
it follows that it is only the first term in Eq.(
60
) that contributes. If on the other hand and with formally
\epsilon>1/2
, it is the second term in Eq.(
60
) that contributes in the form of an exponentially growing term which, fortunately, exactly cancels with a corresponding exponentially growing contribution from Eq.(
61
). By inspection it is seen that these exclusive limits correspond to the states
\psi_{l}(\theta)
or
\psi_{l+1}(\theta)
, respectively. It is only for the exact rational number
\epsilon=1/2
that these two states lead to the superposition in Eq.(
16
).

In order to carry out the analysis in the small
\lambda
-limit in a more careful manner, we therefore first restrict ourselves to the values of
\epsilon
in the range
0\leq\epsilon<1/2
. We then observe that the normalization of the state
\psi(\theta)
using Eq.(
8
) is always exactly equal to one when one keeps the same order in the parameter
q
in both of the
\vartheta_{3}[z,q]
-functions in Eq.(
8
). In a straightforward manner one then finds, keeping terms in the
\vartheta_{3}[z,q]
-functions up to order
{\cal O}(q^{8})
,
the following analytical answer for
(\Delta\theta)^{2}

in the small
\lambda
-limit

\displaystyle(\Delta\theta)^{2}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta p_{1}(\theta)\theta^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-4(e^{\displaystyle{-(1-2\epsilon)/2\lambda}}+e^{\displaystyle{-(1+2\epsilon)/2\lambda}})

\displaystyle+e^{\displaystyle{-2(1-\epsilon)/\lambda}}+e^{\displaystyle{-2(1+\epsilon)/\lambda}}+{\cal O}(e^{\displaystyle{-1/\lambda}})\,,

(62)

which shows an exponential sensitive to the value of
\epsilon
. As
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, the uncertainty
(\Delta\theta)^{2}
is therefore an increasing function of
\lambda
, which corresponds to decreasing values of
\Delta L
according to Eq.(
15
).

In the case when
1/2<\epsilon<1
we now proceed in a more rigorous manner as follows. By inspection of Eq.(
3
), and apart from an overall phase, we notice that
\psi(\theta)
is exactly invariant for all values of
\lambda
under the replacement
\epsilon\rightarrow-\epsilon
provided that one at the same time makes the change
\psi(\theta)\rightarrow\psi(-\theta)
. In the summation procedure over integers
n
in Eq.(
3
) we can then, apart again from an overall phase, perform the change
n+\epsilon\rightarrow n-(1-\epsilon)
, where now
0<1-\epsilon<1/2
. One can then proceed as in the derivation of Eq.(
62
) and obtain the same result re-expressed in terms of
\epsilon
in the range
0<\epsilon<1/2
.

It is now clear from Eq.(
62
) that the case
\epsilon=1/2
plays a special role and must be treated separately. By making use of the small
q
expansion
\vartheta_{3}[-i\epsilon/\lambda,q^{2}]=2(1+q^{4})+{\cal O}(q^{12})
in Eq.(
8
) one now finds that

\displaystyle(\Delta\theta)^{2}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta p_{1}(\theta)\theta^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-2-3e^{\displaystyle{-1/\lambda}}+{\cal O}(e^{\displaystyle{-2/\lambda}})\,,

(63)

in accordance with the critical value
(\Delta\theta)^{2}=\pi^{2}/3-2
in the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
as illustrated in Fig.
5
.

On the other hand, the asymptotic behaviour of
\Delta\theta
with
\bar{\theta}=0
for large values of
\lambda
, as exhibited in Fig.
5
for the specific case
\bar{l}=l+1/2
with
l
an integer, can be obtained by making use of the known asymptotic properties of
\vartheta_{3}[z,q]
functions. For general values of
\epsilon
we therefore recall the following alternative expression for the state
\psi(\theta)
according to Eq.(
7
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=Ne^{\displaystyle{i(l+\epsilon)\theta}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\displaystyle{i2n\pi\epsilon}}e^{-\displaystyle{\lambda(\theta+2n\pi)^{2}/2}}\,\,.

(64)

In Eq.(
9
) we then consider the expansion

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=Ne^{\displaystyle{i(l+\epsilon)\theta}}e^{-\displaystyle{\lambda\theta^{2}/2}}\Huge(1+e^{-\displaystyle{2\lambda\pi^{2}}}(e^{\displaystyle{i2\pi\epsilon}}e^{-\displaystyle{2\pi\lambda\theta}}+e^{\displaystyle{-i2\pi\epsilon}}e^{\displaystyle{2\pi\lambda\theta}})+{\cal O}(e^{\displaystyle{-4\pi^{2}\lambda}})\Huge)\,\,,

(65)

where we make use of

\displaystyle 1=N^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}(1+2e^{\displaystyle{-\pi^{2}\lambda}}\cos{(2\pi\epsilon)}+2e^{\displaystyle{-4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\cos{(4\pi\epsilon)}+{\cal O}(\cos(6\pi\epsilon)e^{\displaystyle{-9\pi^{2}\lambda}})\,\,,

(66)

according to Eq.(
4
). Therefore we, e.g., find that
N^{2}=\sqrt{\lambda/\pi}
for sufficiently large values of
\lambda
similar to the analysis in terms of an error function in accordance with the results in Refs.

[
1
,
2
]
.

A more general, systematic and analytical method in the evaluation of
(\Delta\theta)^{2}
to any order in
q
is based on Eq.(
9
). This procedure leads to error functions depending on
\pi\sqrt{\lambda}
due to the explicit exponential
\theta
-dependence in Eq.(
9
). For large values of
\lambda
the asymptotic expansion of these error functions then lead to the expression

\displaystyle(\Delta\theta)^{2}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta p_{1}(\theta)\theta^{2}=\frac{1}{2\lambda}+2\pi^{2}e^{\displaystyle{-\pi^{2}\lambda}}\cos{(2\pi\epsilon)}(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda\pi^{3}}})-{\cal O}(\frac{e^{\displaystyle{-\pi^{2}\lambda}}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{3}}})\,\,\,,

(67)

which shows that the dependence of
\epsilon
for large values of
\lambda
is exponentially suppressed. It turns out that this expression for
(\Delta\theta)^{2}
agrees well with the numerical results as presented in Fig.
5
for
\epsilon=1/2
even for
\lambda\geq{\cal O}(0.1)
.

For large values of
\lambda
the approximation as in Eq.(
65
) can, furthermore be used to find
(\Delta L)^{2}
in a tedious but straightforward manner with a result that is consistent with Eq.(
14
) which was obtained in a different manner.
By making use of
(\Delta L)^{2}=\lambda/2
as in Eq.(
14
), it therefore follows in large
\lambda
-limit that
\Delta\theta\Delta L=1/2
in accordance with the results in Refs.

[
1
,
2
]
.

5.3
p_{2}(\theta)
and Expansions of the Parameter

If we instead consider the probability distribution

6
CONCLUSIONS

In summary we have considered two different but related quasi-probability distributions
W[\theta,p]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
as defined in Eqs.(
18
) and (
48
), respectively, with
\theta
in the range
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
and
p
in principle any real number. When the parameter
p
takes integer values the corresponding positive marginal distributions
W[p]
and
W_{1/2}[p]
both agree with expected quantum mechanical probability distributions. The positive marginal distributions
W[\theta]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta]
obtained by summing over all integers
p
are properly normalized but different and give rise to different values for the uncertainty
(\Delta\theta)^{2}
.

If the range of
p
is all real numbers the distributions
W[\theta,p]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
differ by a factor
1/2
in the overall normalization. We track this difference back to the fact that the different marginal distributions
W[p]
and
W_{1/2}[p]
are in general not positive functions, i.e., they cannot be regarded as probabilities which then make their physical interpretation unclear. In this sense the properties in Eq.(
2
) for the Wigner function in Eq.(
1
) can not in general be fulfilled for the
W[\theta,p]
and
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
for all real numbers
p
. Furthermore, both distributions show signs of negativity for fractional values of
p
in regions which, however, do not necessarily overlap. If both of them could be measured for the same quantum state
\psi(\theta)
the appearance of negativity as a sign of quantum-mechanical behaviour would then be ambiguous as illustrated in Figs.
3
and
4
.

From a knowledge of the explicit form of the probabilities
p_{1}(\theta)
and
p_{2}(\theta)
as given in Eqs.(
58
) and (
59
), respectiveley, one obtains the corresponding uncertainties
\Delta\theta
. For the observable
\Delta L
in the case
\bar{l}=l+1/2
with integers
l
, we therefore predict the behaviour as presented in Fig.
5
using states of the form
\psi(\theta)
in Ref.

[
1
]
. Here the uncertainties
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
are, in particular, independent of the integer
l
if
\bar{\theta}=0
. A specific gap in
\Delta\theta
is predicted depending on which marginal distribution
p_{1}(\theta)
or
p_{2}(\theta)
is used in order to obtain the uncertainty
\Delta\theta
.

For a half-integer angular momentum eigenstate well-known rules of quantum mechanics imply that a
4\pi
-rotation is usually required in order to obtain the same eigenstate and leads to unique physical consequences. In our case with half-integer expectation values of
\langle L\rangle
and for fractional values of
p
it is therefore not obvious why, e.g., the quasi-probability distribution
W[\theta,p]
, with a range
[-2\pi,2\pi]
of integration in Eq.(
18
), can, as noticed in the present work, exhibit a more pronounced negativity than quasi-probability distribution
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
, with a range
[-\pi,\pi]
of integration in Eq.(
48
). In both cases the half-integer value
p=1/2
appears, however, to be of special importance.

To what extent quasi-probability distributions, like
W[\theta,p]
or
W_{1/2}[\theta,p]
in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively, turn out to be useful in the analysis of pure quantum states in, e.g., the form as considered in the present work in Section
2
can, of course, only be determined in terms of actual experimental situations. This has been emphasized in, e.g., Ref.

[
74
]
concerning, in particular, the physics that involves the notion of a quantum phase, a concept which also plays an important role in the present work.

With regard to Section
5
in the present paper, discussions on fundamental quantum-mechanical limitations of measurement of fluctuations like
\Delta\theta
and
\Delta L
can be found in, e.g., Refs.
[
75
,
76
,

Generalized Epstein semantics for Parry systems
N. Zamperlin

Abstract
In this paper I introduce a generalized version of Richard Epstein’s set-assignment semantics ( [ Epstein, 1990 ] ). As a case study, I consider how this framework can be used to characterize William Parry’s logic of analytic implication and some of its recent variations proposed by [ Ferguson, 2023a ] . In generalized Epstein semantics the parallel use of two algebras, one for extensional and the other for intensional values, allows to account for various forms of content sharing between formulae, which motivates the choice to investigate Parry systems. Hilbert-style axiomatizations and completeness proofs will be presented for all the considered calculi, in particular as main result I provide a set-assignment semantics for Parry’s logic.



1
Introduction

Accounting for subject-matter relations in logic has been a long lasting enterprise that still poses an open problem. Formally, the problem is translated into incorporating content sensitive connectives, above all implication, inside the logical calculus. One of the forefathers of this discussion was William T. Parry, who developed
analytic implication
, that is a content sensitive implication which obeys the
proscriptive principle
: “No formula with analytic implication as main relation holds universally if it has a free variable occurring in the consequent but not the antecedent" (
[
Parry, 1968
, p. 151]
). In a system where this principle is enforced we have a meaningful semantic contribution provided by the contents of formulae.

Parry’s logic was introduced in
[
Parry, 1933
]
and, despite not becoming a main trend in the future branch of relevance logics, it was the starting point for the family of containment logics, in which the notion of a correct implication requires containment of content (in a specified direction) between consequent and antecedent.

Among the plethora of logics that followed this intuition, we focus on a recent proposal, that of Francesco Berto’s
topic-sensitive intentional modals
(TSIMs; cf.
[
Berto, 2022
]
), which is situated within the project of a logic of conceivability pursued by Berto and his collaborators. The main goal is to obtain an extremely general framework able to accounting for many intensional and hyperintensional phenomena; such framework has been applied to, e.g., knowability relative to information (
[
Berto and Hawke, 2021
]
), reality-oriented mental simulation (
[
Berto, 2018a
]
), hyperintensional belief revision (
[
Berto, 2018b
]
). On the technical side, TSIMs are binary, variably strict modal operators of the form
X^{\varphi}\psi
, whose intuitive reading is “given
\varphi
, an agent
X
s that
\psi
", for some propositional attitude
X
. Their semantics is provided by a Kripke frame containing a different accessibility relation for each TSIM, expanded furthermore with a semilattice of topics for each world and a map assigning to each formula its content; the resulting semantic clause for
X^{\varphi}\psi
is that of a strict implication
\varphi\strictif\psi
(through the accessibility relation
R_{X}
) which moreover satisfies a content filter, in the sense that the content of
\varphi
contains that of
\psi
.

Despite its great versatility, Berto’s theory has a crucial decisive question left open: what is the content of a TSIM? A fully transparent solution which reduces the content of
X^{\varphi}\psi
to the fusion of the contents of the components formulae is unsatisfactory, but until a decision is taken the language of the logic can only be take as first degree with respect to the new intensional operators, limiting its expressive power. An attempt to solve the problem comes for Thomas M. Ferguson, whose study of subject-matter and intensional operators has been developed in a recent series of papers (
[
Ferguson, 2023a
]
,
[
Ferguson, 2023b
]
,
[
Ferguson, ming
]
). The first article in the series is the one of major interest for the current paper. In that, Ferguson considers as a starting point of his investigaiton the case study provided by Parry’s logic of analytic implication, focusing on the possible world semantics devised by
[
Fine, 1986
]
, which is based on
\mathbf{S4}
-Kripke frames expanded by a semilattice of topics for each world and certain homomorphisms that guarantees the persistence of content inclusion. By assigning to an implicative formula a binary operation over said semillatice and removing any property from this new operation, Ferguson manages to obtain a very weak, therefore vastly general, content sensitive conditional, which can be seen as a special TSIM with a non-trivial topic itself.

While looking for a solution to Berto’s framework limitation, this paper takes into consideration the helpful insights provided by
[
Ferguson, 2023a
]
but it follows a different route, taking inspiration from the work of Richard L. Epstein and his work on intensional implication developed within the framework of set-assignment semantics. This semantics has been developed by Epstein since
[
Epstein, 1979
]
, culminating with the monograph
[
Epstein, 1990
]
. In set-assignment semantics, the content of formulae is a certain subset of a reference set. By imposing conditions on this assignment and requiring that a formula satisfies certain relation in the content of the components as additional condition for his truth semantic clause, the main connective of said formula is given an intensional layer. This is the general strategy used to intensionalize Boolean connectives without changing their underlying semantics dramatically.

Set-assignment semantics has been used to successfully recapture well-know logics (see
[
Epstein, 1990
]
): modal (ch. VI; e.g.,
\mathbf{K},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{TB},\mathbf{S4},\mathbf{S5}
, Grzegorczyk’s
\mathbf{S4Grz}
, provability logic
\mathbf{GL}
), intuitionistic (ch. VII; including Johansson’s minimal logic), many-valued (ch. VIII;
\mathbf{K_{3}}
, Łukasiewicz’s
\mathbf{\L_{3}},\mathbf{\L_{\infty}}
, Gödel’s
\mathbf{G_{3}},\mathbf{G_{\infty}}
), paraconsistent (ch. IX; D’Ottaviano and da Costa’s
\mathbf{J_{3}}
). Of a much greater interest for our current purpose is the original family of dependence logics introduced by Epstein (ch. V). These logics will be properly introduced in the next section.

∎

We have all the tools to prove the completeness of
\mathbf{E}\text{-}\mathbf{PAI}_{0}^{g}
. We will employ a canonical model strategy. First we need some definitions.

Definition 16
.

Let
\mathbf{\Lambda}
be a logic for a language
\tau
. A set of formulae
\Gamma\subseteq Fm_{\tau}
is
\mathbf{\Lambda}
-consistent if there exists
\varphi\in Fm_{\tau}
s.t.
\Gamma\nvdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
. In particular,
\Gamma
is
\mathbf{\Lambda}
-
\varphi
-consistent if
\Gamma\nvdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
. For a language with a join operation,
\Gamma\neq Fm_{\tau}
is prime if
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi\lor\psi
implies
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
or
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\psi
.

When the context is clear, we might omit the prefix and simply write consistent or
\varphi
-consistent. Observe that every prime theory is consistent.

In order to obtain a suitable result playing the role of Lindenbaum’s lemma, we are going to perform a more abstract algebraic reasoning and conclude general lattice-theoretic results for closure operators. To the best of my knowledge, this strategy has been devised by
[
Paoli and Přenosil, 2024
]
. As a corollary, the standard Lindenbaum’s lemma for classical logic and some of its expansions is regained.

Let us recall some basic definitions from lattice theory. A lattice
\textbf{L}={\langle L,\lor,\land\rangle}
is
complete
if meets and joins exist for arbitrary subsets of
L
. An element
a\in L
of a complete lattice is called
compact
if for any
S\subseteq L,a\leq\bigvee S
implies that there exists finite
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that
a\leq\bigvee S^{\prime}
. A complete lattice is
algebraic
if every element can be expressed as the join of compact elements. In a complete lattice, an element
a\in\textbf{L}
is
completely meet-irreducible
when, for arbitrary
S\subseteq L
, from
a=\bigwedge S
it follows
a\in S
. An element is simply
meet-irreducible
if the previous property holds for finite
S
. The following result is a straightforward application of Zorn’s lemma:

Lemma 17

(
[
Metcalfe et al., 2023
]
, proposition 1.3.8)
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be an algebraic lattice,
a,c\in L
with
c
a compact element s.t.
c\nleq a
. There exists a completely meet-irreducible element
a^{+}\in L
s.t.
a\leq a^{+}
and
c\nleq a
.

Since a logic
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is a closure relation
\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
over formulae
Fm
of a certain language, it can equally be seen as a
closure operator

\mathbf{C}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}:\mathscr{P}(Fm)\to Fm
s.t.
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma)=\{\varphi

|

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi\}
. Each closure operator has associated a
closure system

\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})\subseteq\mathscr{P}(Fm)
, that is a collection of subsets satisfying
Fm\in\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})
and closed under arbitrary non-empty intersections, defined as
\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})=\{\Gamma

|

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma)=\Gamma\}
, which forms the lattice of all
\mathbf{\Lambda}
-theories. A logic
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is
finitary
when
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
implies
\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
for finite
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
. The closure operator
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
of a finitary logic is finitary as well, in the sense that
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma)=\bigcup\{\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma^{\prime})

|
finite
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma\}
.
\Gamma\in\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})
is
finitely generated
if
\Gamma=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
for finite
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Lemma 18

(
[
Font, 2016
]
, propositions 1.65-1.67)
.

If
\mathbf{C}
is a finitary closure operator, its associated closure system
\mathscr{C}
is an algebraic lattice. The compact elements of
\mathscr{C}
are precisely the finitely generated ones.

All the logics considered throughout this paper have finite Hilbert-style presentations, therefore they are finitary, hence their lattices of theories are algebraic. The following results apply to all of our logics as well.

Lemma 19
.

Let the logic
\mathbf{\Lambda}
be finitary and a conservative expansion of classical logic. If
\Gamma
is a completely meet-irreducible theory then it is a prime theory.

Proof.

First observe that the following hold:
\Gamma,\varphi\lor\psi\vdash\chi\Leftrightarrow\Gamma,\varphi\vdash\chi
and
\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi
. For the left-to-right direction, assume that
\Gamma,\varphi\lor\psi\vdash\chi
, then by classical logic
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash\varphi\lor\psi
, hence applying the hypothesis
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash\chi
. The same can be repeated substituting
\psi
for
\varphi
, obtaining
\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi
. For the right-to-left direction, suppose
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash\chi
and
\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi
. Since we can use the entirety of classical logic, we can borrow the rule of
\lor
-elimination from natural deduction and immediately obtain
\Gamma,\varphi\lor\psi\vdash\chi
.

Returning to the main lemma, let
\Gamma
be a completely meet-irreducible theory in
\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})
and suppose
\Gamma\vdash\varphi\lor\psi
. Since
\Gamma
is a theory,
\Gamma=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\varphi\lor\psi)
, moreover by the above point also
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\varphi\lor\psi)=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\varphi)\cap\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\psi)
. Since
\Gamma
is meet-irreducible, we conclude
\Gamma=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\varphi)
or
\Gamma=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma,\psi)
, that is
\Gamma\vdash\varphi
or
\Gamma\vdash\psi
.
∎

Notice that in the lattice of theories of a logic,
\Gamma\nvdash\varphi
amounts to
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\varphi)\nsubseteq\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma)
. The next theorem will play the role of Lindenbaum’s lemma:

Theorem 20
.

Let the logic
\mathbf{\Lambda}
be finitary and a conservative expansion of classical logic. If
\Gamma
is a theory s.t.
\Gamma\nvdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
, there exists a prime theory
\Delta\supseteq\Gamma
s.t.
\Delta\nvdash_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\varphi
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{\Lambda}
is finitary, its lattice of theories
\mathsf{Th}(\mathbf{\Lambda})
is algebraic, as stated in lemma
18
, moreover
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\varphi)
is compact since it is finitely generated.
\Gamma
is a theory, which means
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
, and
\Gamma\nvdash\varphi
is equivalent to
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\varphi)\nsubseteq\Gamma
. Applying lemma
17
, there exists a completely meet-irreducible theory
\Delta\supseteq\Gamma
s.t.
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}(\varphi)\nsubseteq\Delta
, that is
\Delta\nvdash\varphi
. By lemma
19
,
\Delta
is a prime theory.
∎

Lemma 23

Let
\Gamma

\mathbf{E}\text{-}\mathbf{PAI}_{0}^{g}

\mathfrak{M}^{\Gamma}

Recall that, by lemma

\mathbf{S4}^{g}

Assume that the property holds for formulae simpler than

Suppose

The links mean not only extension but also expansion. The position of the demodalized logics
g\mathbf{D}
and
g\mathbf{D}_{0}
is, strictly speaking, incorrect, since they are formulated in the
\Box

The intent of this paper was two-fold: first, to answer - hopefully in a satisfactory way - to the open problem of finding a set-assignment semantics for Parry’s logic of analytic implication; second, to show, via the case study of Parry’s logic and nearby systems, how the proposed generalized Epstein semantics can be a helpful tool in logical investigations pertaining intensional phenomena. Ultimately, the main advantage of this framework resides in its modularity.

The semantics operates a distinction in treatment between the extensional and intensional fragments of the language. By individuating the latter fragment, we obtain a finer degree of control over the intensional behaviour of some connectives by splitting the universes of values assigned to formulae. Instead of a complex structure incorporating the entire semantics, we now have a separated algebra for intensional values simplifying the picture, as the value of an intensional formula is now determined with the contribution coming from the auxiliary algebra of contents. In order to investigate further logics, one of the two components of the semantics can be substituted without greatly affecting the other (up to adjusting the translation correlating the languages). E.g., we might be interested in the study of a Parry’s system with an intuitionistic basis, which can be done by using Heyting algebras as the algebra of truth-values, or we might argue against the transparency of the content of some connective and decide to change the semilattice structure with something more articulated, like in Ferguson’s conditional-agnostic logic. The framework is modular precisely in this sense. Hopefully this virtue will be helpful in future investigations.

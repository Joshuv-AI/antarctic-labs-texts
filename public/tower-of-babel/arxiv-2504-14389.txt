1.
Introduction

We denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
by
[n]
, the family of the subsets of
[n]
by

, and the family of the
i

[n]
by
\displaystyle\binom{[n]}{i}
. If

We will mostly use the terminology

for collections of sets, although

is also widely used in the literature. If all sets in a family
{\mathcal{F}}
are of the same size, we say
{\mathcal{F}}
is

.
Otherwise
{\mathcal{F}}
is called

Definition 1.1
.

A family
{\mathcal{F}}
is said to be intersecting if
|A\cap B|\neq 0
holds for all
A
,
B\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

The following two theorems of Erdős, Ko and Rado are central ones in extremal set theory. They determine the largest size of a family containing no disjoint pairs of sets. The first theorem solves the case of non-unifom families, the second one determines the maximum for the uniform case.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
2
]

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset 2^{[n]}
be an intersecting family. Then
|{\mathcal{F}}|\leq 2^{n-1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
2
]

Let
k
,
n\geq 2k
be integers and
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{[n]\choose k}
be an intersecting family. Then
|{\mathcal{F}}|\leq\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{k-1}
.

The concept "intersecting family" has several generalizations, as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
t
-intersecting

|A\cap B|\geq t
holds for all
A
,

Definition 1.5

A family
\mathcal{F}

holds for all

t
-intersecting but

It was proved by Erdős, Ko, and Rado in their seminal paper
[

that for any

-intersecting families

-intersecting ones if

[

]

. Later Wilson
[
19
]

Theorem 1.6

Trivially
t
-intersecting family is the maximal
t
-intersecting family if and only if

The non-uniform version of the theorem for

Theorem 1.7
.

[
16
]

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a
t
-intersecting family with

. Then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{c c}\sum_{i=\frac{n+t}{2}}^{n}{n\choose i}&\text{if $n+t$ is even}\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\sum_{i=\frac{n+t+1}{2}}^{n}{n\choose i}+{n-1\choose\frac{n+t-1}{2}}&\text{if $n+t$ is odd}\end{array}\right.

One of the famous questions of this area is the Erdős Matching Conjecture (EMC), in which the maximum number of
k
-element subsets should be determined under the condition that no

of them are pairwise disjoint. This value is denoted by

Conjecture 1.8

[

For

m(n,k,s)=\max\left\{{n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k},{k(s+1)-1\choose k}\right\}.

It was one of the favourite problems of Erdős and there was hardly a combinatorial lecture of him where he did not mention it. The EMC is trivial for

[

]

in
[

]
, for

[

[

The case

[

.

[

There exists an absolute constant

holds if

In the non-uniform case Kleitman considered when

2.
Results

The condition of Kleitman’s theorem can be written in the following form:
|A\cap B|+|B\cap C|+|C\cap A|\geq 1
holds for any 3 distinct members
A
,
B
,
C\in{\mathcal{F}}
. The main goal of the present paper is to study the case when 1 is replaced by
\ell
. Since the case
\ell=1
has been studied a lot, we will only look at the case
\ell\geq 2
.

Let
d:(2^{[n]})^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function such that for all
A
,
B
,
C\in 2^{[n]}
,
d(A,B,C)=|A\cap B|+|B\cap C|+|C\cap A|
. Denote by
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}
the class of families such that
\mathcal{H}
,
d(A,B,C)\geq\ell
for all sets
A
,
B
,
C\in\mathcal{H}
. Let
g(n,k,\ell)=\max\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k},\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{H_{\ell}}\}
and
h(n,\ell)=\max\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]},\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{H_{\ell}}\}
.

First we consider the uniform case. We prove the following upper and lower bounds:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 3k-2\geq 4
be positive integers. Then

g(n,k,2)\leq{n+1\choose k-1}+{n\choose k-2}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 3k-3\geq 3
be positive integers. Then

g(n,k,3)\leq{n\choose k-1}+2{n\choose k-3}+3{n-1\choose k-3}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
k
,
\ell
,
n
be positive integers with
3k\geq\ell
. Then

(1)

g(n,k,\ell)\geq\max_{k\geq j\geq\frac{\ell}{3}}\left\{\sum_{i=j}^{k}{f(j,\ell)\choose i}{n-f(j,\ell)\choose k-i}\right\},

where

f(j,\ell)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}2j-\lceil\frac{\ell}{3}\rceil&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{\ell}{3}\leq j<\frac{2\ell}{3},\\
3j-\ell&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{2\ell}{3}\leq j.\\
\end{array}\right.

Remark 2.4
.

If
a<b
, then we interpret
{a\choose b}
as zero.

Finally, we show that if
\ell=2
or

and

is sufficiently large compared to

, then our upper bounds in Theorem

and Theorem

can be improved, reaching the value of the lower bound in Theorem

.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

be positive integers. Then
g(n,k,2)=g(n,k,3)={n-1\choose k-1}
.

This theorem is a sharpening of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem, since it achieves the same upper bound for the corresponding pairs
(n,k)
under weaker conditions.

In the non-uniform case, we investigate families
\mathcal{F}
for which

for all

,

,

. In the case when

(

-element sets and some of the

Theorem 2.6

Let

, where

,

\vskip 2.84526pt\sum_{i=0}^{p}{n\choose i}+{n-2\choose p-1}&q=2,\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\sum_{i=0}^{p}{n\choose i}+{n-1\choose p}&q=3\ or\ 4,\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\sum_{i=0}^{p}{n\choose i}+{n-1\choose p}+{n-2\choose p}&q=5.\\

3.1.
Shifting a family

Notation
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a three-member family
\mathcal{A}=\{A,B,C\}

Definition 3.1

Let

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{F:F\in\mathcal{F},(x\notin F)\vee(x,y\in F)\vee(x\in F,y\notin F,F\cup\{y\}-\{x\}\in\mathcal{F})\}

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}=\{F:F\in\mathcal{F},x\in F,y\notin F,F\cup\{y\}-\{x\}\notin\mathcal{F}\}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

,
y
and
\mathcal{F}
be same as above. Then

\tau_{x,y}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cup\{F\cup\{y\}-\{x\}:F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\}.

This operation is called a shifting of
\mathcal{F}
(from

to

).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\tau_{x,y}:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\tau_{x,y}(\mathcal{F})
be a bijection such that for
\forall F\in\mathcal{F}

(2)

\tau_{x,y}(F)=\begin{cases}F&\text{if $(x,y\in F)\vee(x,y\notin F)$}\\
F&\text{if $x\in F,y\notin F,F\cup\{y\}-\{x\}\in\mathcal{F}$ }\\
F-\{x\}\cup\{y\}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Claim 3.4

Let

be a family such that

for all
A
,
B
,
C\in\mathcal{F}

Notation

When it does not cause ambiguity we delete the indices

,
y
from the notation

Proof.

We prove this claim by contradiction. Suppose that the conditions are true, but there exist sets
A
,
B
and
C\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
d(\tau(\mathcal{A}))<\ell
.

Suppose that
z\notin\{x,y\}
. Then

holds if and only if
z\in\tau(F)
so

d(\mathcal{A}\setminus\{x,y\})=d(\tau(\mathcal{A})\setminus\{x,y\})=c

Let

and
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}=\{H:H\in{\mathcal{F}},H\neq\tau(H)\}
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{1}=\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
and
\mathcal{A}^{2}=\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
. We can assume that
\mathcal{A}^{1}
contains the element
x

p
times, the element

q

This is true only if

(and

,

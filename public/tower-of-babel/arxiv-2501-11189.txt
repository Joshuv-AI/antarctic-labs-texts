The Dynamical Behavior of Detected vs. Undetected Targets
Ronald Mahler, Eagan MN, USA, EML: mahlerronald@comcast.net

Abstract
This paper is a sequel of the 2019 paper [ 5 ] . It demonstrates the following: a) the Poisson multi-Bernoulli mixure (PMBM) approach to detected vs. undetected (U/D) targets cannot be rigorously formulated using either the two-step or single-step multitarget recursive Bayes filter (MRBF); b) it can, however, be partially salvaged using a novel single-step MRBF; c) probability hypothesis density (PHD) filters can be derived for both the original “S-U/D” approach in [ 5 ] and the novel “D-U/D” approach; d) important U/D formulas in [ 5 ] can be verified using purely algebraic methods rather than the intricate statistical analysis employed in that paper; and e) the claim, that PMBM filters can propagate detected and undetected targets separately in parallel, is doubtful.

1.1
PMBM Filters

Like most multitarget algorithms, PMBM filters presume detection-type
sensors and the “small target” model. That is, a) at any given time
t_{k}
a detection process is applied to
a sensor signature, resulting in a finite set
Z_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}

of point measurements (“detections”)
that are generated by targets or by clutter/false alarms; and b) single
point targets can generate at most a single point detection, and target
detections can be generated by only a single point target
[
11
, p. 433]
.

PMBM filters are also based on the random finite set (RFS) two-step
multitarget recursive Bayes filter (MRBF)
[
11

recursively alternates between time-update and measurement-update steps

\displaystyle f_{k|k-1}(X|Z_{1:k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int f_{k|k-1}(X|X_{k-1})\,f_{k-1|k-1}(X_{k-1}|Z_{1:k-1})\delta X_{k-1}

(1)

\displaystyle f_{k|k}(X|Z_{1:k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}(Z_{k}|X)\,f_{k|k-1}(X|Z_{1:k-1})}{\int f_{k}(Z_{k}|X)\,f_{k|k-1}(X|Z_{1:k-1})\delta X}

(2)

with initial distribution
f_{0|0}(X)
; and where
f_{k|k-1}(X|X_{k-1})
is the multitarget Markov density and
f_{k}(Z|X)=L_{Z}(X)
is the multitarget measurement density; and where

and

integrals (Eq. (

1.3
The Dynamic Model for U/D Targets

PMBM filters employ a
historical-behavioral
, rather than an
instantaneous-statistical, interpretation of U/D-targets, one which presumes
that this question is answerable. Specifically, U/D targets are defined as
(
[
5
, p. 10]

1.

At time
t_{k}
a target is a U-target if it has not been detected
at any of the observation times
t_{1},...,t_{k}
.

2.

If a U-target is detected at time
t_{k+1}
then it is a D-target
at this and all subsequent times—i.e., D-targets cannot become U-targets.

Expressed more intuitively: A target is presumed conjectural until proven
“real” by the act of generating some
measurement
z\in\mathbb{Z}
in the sensor measurement space
\mathbb{Z}
.

However, in Item 3 of
[
5
, p. 13]
and Section 9 of

[
7
, p. 3]
it was noted that:

1.

the PMBM filter’s U/D approach appears to violate the two-step
structure of the MRBF; and

2.

this incompatibility is due to the seriously erroneous physical
assumption that all measurements arise from newly-detected—and therefore
newly-appearing—targets.

That is, they occur only when U-targets transition to D-targets.

•

This will be referred to as the
U-birth model
for
measurement generation.

2.3
Analysis of the PMBM U/D Model

Can the PMBM version of U/D-targets be formulated using the two-step MRBF? The answer is “No,” as can be seen as
follows.

If the U-target
(x,0)
generates some
z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
at time

t_{k}
then
4
4

4

More precisely, since
\breve{f}_{k}(Z|x,0)
is the distribution of the
random measurement-set
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
then
\breve{f}_{k}(\emptyset|x,0)=\Pr(\Sigma_{k}=\emptyset)
and so
1-\breve{f}_{k}(\emptyset|x,0)=\Pr(\Sigma_{k}\neq\emptyset)
. Thus
\breve{f}_{k}(\emptyset|x,0)=0
is equivalent to
\Pr(\Sigma_{k}\neq\emptyset)=1

: some nonempty measurement is generated.

\breve{f}_{k}(\emptyset|x,0)=1-\int f_{k}(\{z\}|x,0)dz=0,

(16)

in which case it transitions to the D-target
(x,1)
at time
t_{k+1}

and thereafter.
5
5

5

It might seem that
(x,0)
transitions instantaneously to
(x,1)
when it is detected at time
t_{k}
. However, the detection process has

\Delta t>0

enough that
\Delta t\ll t_{k+1}-t_{k}

.

However, U-targets and D-targets are
observationally identical

generate measurements in exactly the same manner. That is:

\displaystyle\breve{p}_{D}(x,o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{D}(x)

(17)

\displaystyle\ \breve{f}_{k}(z|x,o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{k}(z|x)=L_{z}(x)

(18)

\displaystyle\ \breve{f}_{k}(Z|x,o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{k}(Z|x).

(19)

D-targets and U-targets are also
kinematically identical
. That
is, if

f_{k|k-1}(x|x_{k-1})=M_{x}(x_{k-1})

(20)

is the conventional single-target Markov density then the single-target

\breve{f}_{k|k-1}(x,o|x_{k-1},o_{k-1})=\delta_{o,o_{k-1}}\,f_{k|k-1}(x|x_{k-1}).

That is, D-targets transition to D-targets and U-targets transition to

\breve{f}_{k}(Z|x,o)=f_{k}(X|x)

Indeed, the U-birth model seems to implicitly have either a Markov density
of the logically impossible form
\breve{f}_{k|k-1}(\breve{x}|\breve{x}_{k-1},Z_{k})

1.

The U-birth model violates the two-step structure of the MRBF and,

6.2
P.g.fl. Two-Step Bayes Filter (General)

This consists of two main parts: time-update and measurement-update.

6.2.1

Suppose that, at time

\displaystyle\int h^{X}\,f_{k|k-1}(X|X_{k-1})\delta X;

(95)

where

t_{k}
.

6.2.2
P.g.fl Measurement-Update

Suppose that, at time
t_{k-1}
, the distribution

[

where a)
[
11
, Eqs. (14.281,14.282)]

\displaystyle F_{k}[g,h]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int h^{X}\,G_{k}[g|X]\,f_{k|k-1}(X)\delta X\text{ \ \ \ where}

(97)

\displaystyle G_{k}[g|X]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int g^{Z}\,f_{k}(Z|X)\delta Z

(98)

is the “
F

t_{k}

is the multitarget measurement density; c)

6.3.1

This is given by

\displaystyle G_{k|k-1}[h]

Substitute the formula for

(142)

The U/D-Parallelism Assumption

The U-birth model is not the only perplexing aspect of the PMBM formulation
of U/D targets. The PMBM filter propagates PMBM RFSs, which are
set-theoretic unions of multi-Bernoulli mixture RFSs (the “MBM” part of “PMBM””) and Poisson RFSs (the “P” part). It is claimed that the MBM part propagates the
D-targets whereas the Poisson part propagates the U-targets. This is what
was earlier described as “U/D-parallelism.”

In the case of the third (“hybrid
labeled-unlabeled”) version of the PMBM filter
[
5
, Sect. 4.5]
, an even stronger claim was made via the
statistical independence formula
[
5

f_{k|k}(X|Z_{1:k})=f_{k|k}^{d}(X^{d}|Z_{1:k})\,f_{k|k}^{u}(X^{u}|Z_{1:k}),

(197)

where
X=X^{d}\uplus X^{u}
(disjoint union); where
f_{k|k}^{d}(X^{u}|Z_{1:k})
is the Poisson U-target distribution; and
where
\ f_{k|k}^{d}(X^{d}|Z_{1:k})
is the labeled MBM (LMBM) D-target
distribution. This implies that D-targets and U-targets can be

11
Conclusions

This sequel paper to
[
5
]
:

1.

showed that the Poisson multi-Bernoulli mixture (PMBM) approach to
detected targets (D-targets) and undetected targets (U-targets) is
theoretically erroneous;

2.

but demonstrated that it can be partially salvaged using a novel
single-step “D-U/D” recursive Bayes
filter that captures the dynamic behavior of U/D-targets;

3.

compared this approach with the “static
U/D” (S-U/D) formulation of U/D targets presented in
[
5
, Sec. 5]
;

4.

derived probability hypothesis density filters for both the S-U/D and
D-U/D cases, demonstrating that the number and states of the U-targets and
D-targets can be easily estimated;

5.

employed purely algebraic methods to verify the validity of key
formulas in
[
5
, Sec. 5]
for the S-U/D approach; and

6.

used these results to show that the PMBM assumption that D-targets and
U-targets can be propagated separately in parallel (“U/D-parallelism”) appears to be erroneous.

The real Brown-Peterson homology of Ω ρ ​ S ρ + 1 \Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}
Christian Carrick, Bertrand J. Guillou, Sarah Petersen

Abstract
. We compute the R ​ O ​ ( C 2 ) RO(C_{2}) -graded real Brown–Peterson homology of the representation-loop space Ω ρ ​ S ρ + 1 \Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1} , where ρ \rho is the regular representation of the cyclic group of order two. This calculation gives a C 2 C_{2} -equivariant analogue of the classical computation of Brown–Peterson homology of the double loop space Ω 2 ​ S 3 \Omega^{2}S^{3} due to Ravenel.



1.
Introduction

Classically, Dieudonné theory gives an equivalence between the category of graded bicommutative Hopf algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and the category of Dieudonné modules over a certain ring
R
. In
[
6
]
, Goerss showed that this equivalence respects the ring structure of Hopf rings over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It follows that Dieudonné theory can be used to study the homology of spaces representing a multiplicative generalized cohomology theory. The homology of the representing spaces inherits the structure of a Hopf ring if the homology theory has a Künneth isomorphism.

In the setting of equivariant homotopy theory, there is no immediate candidate for a Dieudonné functor.
Thus, in this paper, we study an equivariant computation which may be used to inform the development of an equivariant Dieudonné theory.
This approach is motivated by nonequivariant results.

Nonequivariantly, Goerss showed that Ravenel’s computation
[
22
, Theorem C]

of the Brown–Peterson homology of
\Omega^{2}S^{3}

can be combined with Dieudonné theory to describe the homology of the spaces representing a Landweber exact theory
[
6
, Sections 10 and 11]
.
Ravenel’s answer was

(1.1)

BP_{*}\Omega^{2}S^{3}\cong BP_{*}[x_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\dots]/(x_{0}^{2},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots),

with
|x_{0}|=1
and
|y_{n}|=2(2^{n}-1)
, and where the relations
r_{n}
were determined modulo the square of
I=(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},\dots)\subset BP_{*}
to be

r_{n}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{n}v_{i}e_{n-i}^{2^{i}}\pmod{I^{2}}.

By completing an analogous
C_{2}
-equivariant computation,
we give a starting point for determining the resulting constraints
that may be imposed on the existence of a Dieudonné functor in the setting of equivariant homotopy theory.

In this paper, we compute the
RO(C_{2})
-graded real Brown–Peterson homology
BP_{\mathbb{R}}
(
[
13
]
) of the representation loop space
\Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}
, where
\rho
is the regular representation for the cyclic group of order two,
C_{2}
. As input for this calculation, we determine
in
7.8
the
coaction of the equivariant dual Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}}_{\bigstar}
on the homology
\mathbf{H}_{\bigstar}\Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}
. The latter was computed by Behrens and Wilson
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
. The homology generators may all be described using Dyer-Lashof operations, and we produce comodule-Nishida relations, which prescribe the
\mathcal{A}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}}_{\bigstar}
-coaction on the output of Dyer-Lashof operations.

This differs from equivariant co-Nishida relations already in the literature, which deal with equivariant
infinite
loop spaces rather than finite loop spaces
[
25
,
26
]
.
See also the thesis of Sikora
[
23
]
for related work in the direction of finite loop spaces.

The context for these Nishida relations is
C_{2}
-spaces equipped with an action of an
E_{\rho}
-operad
[
9
]
, such as
\rho
-loop spaces. We construct Dyer-Lashof operations in the homology of
E_{\rho}
-spaces, which we denote

\mathbf{H}_{k\rho}X\xrightarrow{Q_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{(2k)\rho}X,\qquad\qquad\mathbf{H}_{k\rho}X\xrightarrow{Q_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{2k\rho+\sigma}X,

\mathbf{H}_{k\rho+1}X\xrightarrow{Q_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{(2k+1)\rho}X,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{H}_{k\rho+1}X\xrightarrow{Q_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{(2k+1)\rho+1}X.

As a consequence of our co-Nishida relations (
6.1
), we deduce (see
6.2
and
7.7
) the following precise formulas relating the comodule structure to Dyer-Lashof operations.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be an
E_{\rho}
-algebra. Then for
x\in\mathrm{H}_{k\rho}X
and
y\in\mathrm{H}_{k\rho+1}X
, the (right) coaction satisfies

\psi_{R}Q_{0}(x)=Q_{0}\psi_{R}(x),\qquad\psi_{R}Q_{1}(x)=Q_{1}\psi_{R}(x),

\psi_{R}Q_{0}(y)=Q_{0}\psi_{R}(y),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi_{R}Q_{1}(y)=Q_{1}\psi_{R}(y)+Q_{0}\psi_{R}(y)\cdot(1\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.375,0,0.625}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}_{0}),

where
{\color[rgb]{0.375,0,0.625}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}}_{\bigstar}
is in degree 1.

These formulas allow us to compute the
\mathcal{A}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}}_{\bigstar}
-comodule structure on
\mathbf{H}_{\bigstar}\Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}
(

), which serves as input to compute the equivariant Adams spectral sequence converging to
{BP_{\mathbb{R}}}_{\bigstar}\Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}
.

Observing that
{BP_{\mathbb{R}}}\wedge\Omega^{\rho}_{+}S^{\rho+1}
is Borel complete (
Proposition

), we instead compute the Borel equivariant Adams spectral sequence developed by Greenlees
[

]
. This is preferable because it leads to a cleaner computation. In order to compute the Adams
E_{2}
-page of the Borel equivariant Adams spectral sequence, we first compute the
a_{\sigma}
-Bockstein spectral sequence. Our main computational result gives a
C_{2}
-equivariant analogue of Ravenel’s computation of

and is described over the course of the following two corollaries as well as in charts in

Theorem B

(

)

The

(

)

The Borel Adams SS for

.

We consider some hidden multiplications in the

.

. However, to calculate the

These computations give us a number of immediate qualitative consequences for
BP_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge\Omega^{\rho}_{+}S^{\rho+1}
. Nonequivariantly, the Snaith splitting for double loop spaces gives a stable decomposition of
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega^{2}S^{3}
into a wedge

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega^{2}S^{3}\simeq\bigvee\limits_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}_{2}S^{1},

where
\mathrm{Sym}^{r}_{2}S^{1}=C_{2}(r)_{+}\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}S^{r}
and
C_{2}(r)
is the configuration space of
r
points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We may therefore decompose
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega^{2}S^{3}
into an even and odd part

\displaystyle L_{ev}

\displaystyle:=\bigvee\limits_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}^{2r}_{2}S^{1}

\displaystyle L_{odd}

\displaystyle:=\bigvee\limits_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}^{2r+1}_{2}S^{1}

and an immediate consequence of Ravenel’s computation of
BP_{*}\Omega^{2}S^{3}
is that

BP\wedge L_{ev}
has homotopy groups concentrated in even degrees and

BP\wedge L_{odd}\simeq\Sigma BP\wedge L_{ev}.

We lift this result to the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting using the equivariant Snaith splitting
(see
8.1
)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{C_{2}}\Omega^{\rho}_{+}S^{\rho+1}\simeq\bigvee\limits_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}_{\rho}S^{1},

where
\mathrm{Sym}^{r}_{\rho}S^{1}=C_{\rho}(r)_{+}\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}S^{r}
and
C_{\rho}(r)
is the configuration space of
r
points in the representation
\rho
. Grouping the even and odd components together as
L_{ev}
and
L_{odd}
as before, we have the following.

Theorem D

(
Propositions

10.2
and
10.3
)
.

The
C_{2}
-spectrum
BP_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge L_{ev}
is strongly even and

BP_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge\Omega^{\rho}_{+}S^{\rho+1}\simeq BP_{\mathbb{R}}\wedge(L_{ev}\vee\Sigma L_{ev}).

In particular, the restriction map

\mathrm{res}:(BP_{\mathbb{R}})_{*\rho}\Omega^{\rho}S^{\rho+1}\to BP_{2*}\Omega^{2}S^{3}

is an isomorphism.

In addition to the real Brown–Peterson homology studied in this paper, there is also a notion of
G
-equivariant Brown–Peterson homology for any compact lie group
G
. In the case where
G=C_{2}
, the real Brown–Peterson spectrum
BP_{\mathbb{R}}
differs from the spectrum
BP_{C_{2}}
, though they are both (genuine)
C_{2}
-spectra.
One important difference is that

is complex-oriented, while
BP_{\mathbb{R}}
is Real-oriented (see
[
13
]
). For our computation, the most important point is that the cohomology of

can be identified as a module over the Steenrod algebra as

\mathbf{H}^{\bigstar}BP_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathcal{A}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}}\!/\!/\mathcal{E}^{{\mathrm{C}_{2}}},

so that a change-of-rings formula gives a simplified description of the
E_{2}
-term for the Adams spectral sequence (see
Section

9
).
We are not aware of a similar description of
\mathbf{H}^{\bigstar}BP_{C_{2}}
.
Another variant of our computation would be to consider alternative equivariant lifts of
\Omega^{2}S^{3}
. The signed loop space
\Omega^{2\sigma}S^{1+2\sigma}
is another interesting lift. Surprisingly, these two equivariant lifts agree! See
[
10
, Remark 1.3]
or
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
.

3.
Background

We begin by reviewing the non-equivariant versions of tools to be used throughout this article.

3.1.
The dual Steenrod Algebra

Recall that the dual Steenrod algebra at the prime
p=2
is

\mathcal{A}_{*}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}_{1},{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}_{2},\cdots]

where
|{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}_{i}|=2^{i}-1

[
18
]
.

The generators
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}_{i}
are defined using the (completed) right coaction of
\mathcal{A}_{*}
on the cohomology of the classifying space
B\Sigma_{2}

\hat{\psi}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(B{\Sigma_{2}})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(B{\Sigma_{2}})\widehat{\otimes}_{H_{*}}\mathcal{A}_{*}

according to the formula

\hat{\psi}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{2^{i}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}_{i},

where
t\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(B\Sigma_{2})
is the generator.

3.2.
Dyer-Lashof operations

An
E_{n}
-algebra, such as an
n
-fold loop space, inherits an action of Dyer-Lashof operations on its homology.
See
[
15
, Section 5]
for a clear account.
These are operations

Q^{k+j}\colon\mathrm{H}_{k}X\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{2k+j}X

for
0\leq j\leq n-1
. It is sometimes convenient to use the alternative notation

Q_{j}\colon\mathrm{H}_{k}X\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{2k+j}X.

The operations
Q_{j}
are additive for
j\leq n-2
.
However, in general, the top operation
Q_{n-1}
fails to be additive, and this failure of additivity is measured by an operation known as the Browder bracket.
In lower index notation, the
Cartan formula
can neatly be expressed as

Q_{j}(x\cdot y)=\sum_{r}Q_{r}(x)\cdot Q_{j-r}(y)

when
j\leq n-2
.

The homology groups also have a natural left action of the Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}
:

\Sq_{i}\colon\mathrm{H}_{k}X\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{k-i}X,

which can be converted into a right action by use of the anti-automorphism
\chi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
. The right action of
\mathcal{A}
is related to the left action of the Dyer-Lashof operations according to the
Nishida relations
:

Q_{j}(x)\cdot\Sq_{r}=\sum_{\ell}\binom{\deg{x}+j-r}{r-2\ell}Q_{j-r+\ell}\left(x\cdot\Sq_{r}\right),

again for
j\leq n-2
.

Remark 3.1
.

There is also a Cartan formula and Nishida relation for the top operation
Q_{n-1}
, though it involves the Browder bracket. If the Browder bracket vanishes on
\mathrm{H}_{*}X
, then the Cartan formula and Nishida relation for
Q_{n-1}
take the same form as those for the lower
Q_{j}
’s.

Alternatively, the action of the Dyer-Lashof operations can be related to the coaction of the dual Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{*}
via the
co-Nishida relations
. In order to express these, recall that the
E_{n}
-extended power

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{n}(S^{k})=\mathcal{C}_{n}(2)_{+}\wedge_{\Sigma_{2}}S^{k\rho_{\Sigma_{2}}},

where
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is an
E_{n}
-operad,
is equivalent to the suspended stunted projective space
\Sigma^{k}\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{k+n-1}_{k}
. Write

e_{2k+j}\in\tilde{}\mathrm{H}_{2k+j}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{n}(S^{k}),\quad\text{where }j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\},

for the homology generators.
Suppose that the right
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-coaction on
\tilde{}\mathrm{H}_{*}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{n}S^{k}
is given by
\psi_{R}(e_{2k+j})=\sum_{i}e_{2k+i}\otimes a_{i}
. Then the co-Nishida relations
[
2
, (2.6)]
are

(3.2)

\psi_{R}Q_{j}x=\sum_{i}(1\otimes a_{i})\cdot Q_{i}(\psi_{R}x).

Note that evaluation of
Q_{i}(\psi_{R}x)
involves the Cartan formula.

We will primarily work with the coaction not by
\mathcal{A}_{*}
but rather by the quotient
\mathcal{E}_{*}\cong E(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)
. This is the quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{*}
by the ideal generated by the
\xi_{i}^{2}
.
We have an analogous co-Nishida relation for the
\mathcal{E}_{*}
-coation:

(3.3)

\psi_{R}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}Q_{j}x=q\left(\sum_{i}(1\otimes a_{i})\cdot Q_{i}(\psi_{R}^{\mathcal{E}_{*}}x)\right),

where
q\colon\mathcal{A}_{*}\to\mathcal{E}_{*}
is the quotient.
This follows because the Dyer-Lashof operations
Q_{i}
descend along the quotient map
q
, since they preserve the ideal
(\xi_{1}^{2},\xi_{2}^{2},\dots)
.
The latter follows from the formula

Q_{j}\xi_{k}^{2}=\begin{cases}(Q_{j/2}\xi_{k})^{2}&j\text{ even}\\
0&j\text{ odd,}\end{cases}

which is a consequence of the Cartan formula.

3.3.
The
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-comodule
{\mathrm{H}_{*}\Omega^{2}S^{3}}

The homology of
\Omega^{2}S^{3}
is

\mathrm{H}_{*}(\Omega^{2}S^{3})\cong\mathbb{F}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots],

with
x_{i}
in degree
2^{i}-1
.
The space
\Omega^{2}S^{3}
is visibly a double loop space, but in fact it is a triple loop space, as
S^{3}
is a topological group.
Thus its homology comes equipped with an action by Dyer-Lashof operations
Q_{0}
,
Q_{1}
, and
Q_{2}
. We will not make use of the top operation,
Q_{2}
.
Araki and Kudo showed
[
1
,
5
]
that the generators are given by (what are now called) Dyer-Lashof operations on previous generators. For example,

x_{2}=Q^{2}x_{1}=Q_{1}x_{1},\qquad x_{3}=Q^{4}x_{2}=Q_{1}x_{2},\qquad\text{etc.}

The (co-)Nishida relations can then be used to deduce the
\mathcal{A}
-module, or
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-comodule, structure on
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\Omega^{2}S^{3})
.

According to (
3.3
), we can inductively work out the
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-coaction on the
x_{i}
’s.
For this, the coaction on the
E_{2}
-extended power
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{2}(S^{k})\cong\Sigma^{k}\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}_{k}^{k+1}
is needed.
The homology class
e_{2k}
is primitive, and the coaction on
e_{2k+1}

is given by

\psi_{R}(e_{2k+1})=\begin{cases}e_{2k+1}\otimes 1+e_{2k}\otimes\xi_{1}&k\text{ odd}\\
e_{2k+1}\otimes 1&k\text{ even.}\end{cases}

Proposition 3.4
.

The right
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-coaction on
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\Omega^{2}S^{3})
is given by

(3.5)

\psi_{R}x_{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}x_{i-k}^{2^{k}}\otimes\xi_{k}.

Proof.

We induct on
i.
The base case for holds for degree reasons. Suppose the above formula holds for
x_{i}.
Then the coNishida relations (
3.3
) imply

\displaystyle\psi_{R}(x_{i+1})

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(\psi_{R}(x_{i}))+(1\otimes\xi_{1})Q_{0}(\psi_{R}x_{i})

\displaystyle=Q_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}x_{i-k}^{2^{k}}\otimes\xi_{k}\right)+(1\otimes\xi_{1})Q_{0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}x_{i-k}^{2^{k}}\otimes\xi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(x_{i})\otimes\xi_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}Q_{0}(x_{i-k}^{2^{k}})\otimes Q_{1}(\xi_{k})+\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}Q_{0}(x_{i-k}^{2^{k}})\otimes\xi_{1}Q_{0}(\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=x_{i+1}\otimes 1+\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\left(x_{i-k}^{2^{k+1}}\otimes(\xi_{k+1}+\xi_{1}\xi_{k}^{2})+x_{i-k}^{2^{k+1}}\otimes\xi_{1}\xi_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{i}x_{i+1-k}^{2^{k}}\otimes\xi_{k},

where in the third line we apply the Cartan formula and in the fourth line we use that
Q_{1}(\xi_{k})=\xi_{k+1}+\xi_{1}\xi_{k}^{2}
(see, for instance
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
).
∎

5.2.
The Cartan formula

As we will see in

,
the example of interest in this article will be a homology algebra that can be identified, as an

-algebra, with the underlying
E_{\rho}
-algebra of an

-algebra. This means that the equivariant Dyer-Lashof operations, which in general are not even additive, will be particularly well-behaved. For instance, they will inherit the
E_{\infty}

We will express the Cartan formula of

in the subscript notation. For this, it is convenient to say that

, if the degree of

is equal to
\varepsilon
plus a multiple of

Theorem 5.9

(
[
26
, Corollary 1.3.2]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be an
E_{\rho}
-algebra underlying a
C_{2}-E_{\infty}
-algebra and let

lie in degrees congruent to either 0 or 1 modulo

Q_{0}(x\otimes y)=\begin{cases}Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)&x,y\equiv 0\pmod{\rho}\\
Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)+a_{\sigma}Q_{1}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)&x\equiv 0,y\equiv 1\pmod{\rho}\\
Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)+a_{\sigma}Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{1}(y)&x\equiv 1,y\equiv 0\pmod{\rho}\\
\end{cases}

and

Q_{1}(x\otimes y)=\begin{cases}Q_{1}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)+Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{1}(y)&x,y\equiv 0\pmod{\rho}\\
\qquad+a_{\sigma}Q_{1}(x)\otimes Q_{1}(y)&\\
Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{1}(y)+u_{\sigma}Q_{1}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)&x\equiv 0,y\equiv 1\pmod{\rho}\\
Q_{1}(x)\otimes Q_{0}(y)+u_{\sigma}Q_{0}(x)\otimes Q_{1}(y)&x\equiv 1,y\equiv 0\pmod{\rho}.\\
\end{cases}

B_{n+1}/J_{n-1}\cong(A_{n}/J_{n-1})[e_{0}^{\pm}]
. It follows that
J_{n-1}
is prime in both
A_{n+1}
and
B_{n+1}
. We claim also that
J_{n}=J_{n-1}+(r_{1,k})
is prime in
B_{n+1}
, which follows from the identifications

\displaystyle B_{n+1}/J_{n}

\displaystyle=(B_{n+1}/J_{n-1})/(r_{1,k})

\displaystyle=(A_{n}/J_{n-1})[e_{0}^{\pm}]/(e_{0}^{2^{k}}+we_{1}^{2^{k}})

\displaystyle=(A_{n}/(w-1,J_{n-1}))[e_{0}^{\pm},e_{1}^{-1}].

Since
(A_{n}/(w-1,J_{n-1}))[e_{0}^{\pm}]
is a domain, it suffices to observe that the localization
(A_{n}/(w-1,J_{n-1}))[e_{0}^{\pm},e_{1}^{-1}]
is nonzero, which follows from the fact that
J_{n-1}
is prime in
B_{n}=A_{n}[e_{1}^{\pm}]
.

We have shown that
J_{n}
is prime in
B_{n+1}
and, to show that
J_{n}
is also prime in
A_{n+1}=A
, we finish by mimicking the above argument showing that
J_{i-1}
is prime in
A_{i}
. In fact we only used that
J_{i-1}
was regular in
A_{i-1}
, that
J_{i-1}
is prime in
B_{i}
, and that
A_{i}
was a domain. The argument therefore goes through without change and we conclude that the ideal
I_{n}=(r_{n,k},\ldots,r_{1,k})
is prime in
A
for all
n
. It follows that
R_{k}=A/I_{\infty}
is a domain since in any fixed degree
R_{k}\cong A/I_{n}
for
n
sufficiently large.
∎

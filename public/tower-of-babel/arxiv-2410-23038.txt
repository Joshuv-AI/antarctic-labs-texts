Remarks on nonlinear dispersive PDEs with rough dispersion management
Tristan Robert

Abstract
. In this work, we study the Cauchy problem for a class of dispersive PDEs where a rough time coefficient is present in front of the dispersion. Under minimal assumptions on the occupation measure of this coefficient, we show that for the large class of semilinear dispersive PDEs whose well-posedness theory relies on linear estimates of Strichartz or local smoothing type, one has the same well-posedness theory with or without the modulation. We also show a regularization by noise type of phenomenon for rough modulations, namely, large data global well-posedness in the focusing mass-critical case for the modulated equation. Under rougher assumptions on the modulation, we show that one can also transfer the well-posedness theory based on multilinear Fourier analysis from the original dispersive PDE to the modulated one. In the case of the NLS equation on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} , this covers all the sub-critical and critical regularities, thus completing and extending the various results currently available in the literature. At last, in the case of the periodic Wick-ordered cubic NLS, we show an even stronger form of regularization by noise, namely well-posedness in the critical Fourier-Lebesgue space for rough modulations.

1.2.
Assumptions

In the following, we will use different assumptions on the irregularity of
W_{t}
, expressed through the regularity of its occupation measure
\mu_{[0;T]}
measured in some appropriate topology.

Definition 1.1
.

•

Let
W:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous path. For all time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, its
occupation measure
is defined as the measure on Borel sets
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\mu_{I}(A):=\mathrm{Leb}\big(t\in I,~W_{t}\in A\big)=\int_{I}\mathbf{1}_{A}(W_{t})dt.

(1.3)

•

In the following we assume that
\mu_{I}\ll dz
for all interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then
{\displaystyle\frac{d\mu_{I}}{dz}}
is the
local time
of
W_{t}
in
I
.

The relation between regularity properties of a deterministic or stochastic path
W_{t}
and its occupation measure
\mu
have been investigated since the 70s, and we refer to the review
[
42
]
by Geman and Horowitz on these earlier results. Different topologies have been used to measure the regularity of
\mu
: while Hölder regularity is discussed in
[
42
]
, more recently the use of Fourier-Lebesgue regularity has been proved usefull by Catellier and Gubinelli
[
13
]
in the context of ODEs under rough additive perturbations
W_{t}
, or Chouk-Gubinelli
[
20
]
, also with Li, Li and Oh
[
21
]
in the context of modulated dispersive PDEs. Even more recently, the work of Romito and Tolomeo
[
79
]
studies Besov regularity of
\mu
under similar assumptions as in
[
42
]
on
W_{t}
. The common feature of these works is that
regularity
of
\mu
implies local
irregularity
of
W_{t}
. Actually, the notion of prevalence allows to show some form of the converse, namely paths
W_{t}
in some Hölder spaces satisfy “a.e.” a regularity property for their occupation measures; see the recent works
[
35
,
36
]
by Galeati and Gubinelli where this is investigated.

Below, we will assume either Hölder or Fourier-Lebesgue regularity à la Catellier-Gubinelli for the occupation measure
\mu
of the path
W_{t}
in (
1.1
). Therefore we will make one of the following assumptions on the irregularity of
W_{t}
:

Assumption (A0)
.

W_{t}
is a continuous function with local time

\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}).

Assumption (A0)*
.

W_{t}
is a continuous function with local time

\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\in C^{0}_{t}\big(\mathbb{R};L^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\big).

Assumption (A0)
(ρ,γ)
.

W_{t}
is a continuous function with local time

\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\in C^{\gamma}_{t}\big(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{F}L^{\rho,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\big).

Before stating the assumptions on the model (
1.1
), let us give some comments on the set of assumptions above regarding the regularity of the occupation measure
\mu
of
W_{t}
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i) The definition of the Fourier-Lebesgue space
\mathcal{F}L^{\rho,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
appearing in assumption
(A0)(ρ,γ)
above is recalled in Section
2
below.

(ii) The assumption
(A0)(ρ,γ)
corresponds to the definition of

-irregularity introduced in
[

]
and also used in
[

,

]

(iii) From the standard embedding

\Big(\textrm{\ref{A0gr}},~~\gamma\geq 0,~~\rho>1\Big)\Longrightarrow\textrm{\ref{A0*}}\Longrightarrow\textrm{\ref{A0}}.

(iv) When

{\frac{d\mu_{[0;t]}}{dz}(z)=\mathbf{1}_{[0;t]}(z)}
, which clearly satisfies

\Big|\widehat{\frac{d\mu_{[0;t]}}{dz}}(\xi)\Big|=\Big|\frac{e^{it\xi}-1}{i\xi}\Big|\lesssim|t|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{1-\gamma}

if and only if

and

.

(v) Similarly, when

is

-periodic and piecewise constant with a finite number of discontinuities, i.e.
{\displaystyle\frac{dW_{t}}{dt}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{(t_{k-1};t_{k})}\dot{w}_{k-1}}
for some partition

of
[0;T]
, provided that
\dot{w}_{k}\neq 0
for all
k
, one finds for

that
{\displaystyle\frac{d\mu_{[0;t]}}{dz}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{[w_{k-1};w_{k-1}+\dot{w}_{k-1}(t_{k}-t_{k-1}))\cap[0;t]}(z)}
for some

. Thus

also satisfies
(A0)
in this case.

(vi) On the contrary, it follows from
[
42
]
that as soon as
(A0)*
is satisfied,

is not locally Lipschitz continuous at
any

t
. Similarly, for any

(A0)(ρ,γ)

Assumption (A1)

Assumption (A2)

The mass

Even if the corresponding deterministic equation (

1.3.
Statement of the results

Equipped with the set of assumptions from the previous subsection, we can now state our results on the Cauchy problem for the abstract modulated equation (

1.3.1.
Well-posedness through space-time estimates

Informally, our first result shows that for any semilinear dispersive PDE treated by a fixed-point argument in appropriate space-time norms, the same well-posedness results hold for both (
1.1
) and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u+\mathcal{N}(u)=0,\\
u(t=0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

To state our result more precisely, we use the following notations: for a set of spatial functions
X(\mathcal{M})
and
T>0
, we define the sets of space-time functions

\displaystyle S^{p,+}_{T}(X):=L^{p}([0;T];X(\mathcal{M}))\qquad\text{and}\qquad S^{p,-}_{T}(X)=X(\mathcal{M};L^{p}([0;T])).

(1.5)

For a finite family of spaces
\{S^{p_{j},\iota_{j}}(X_{j})\}_{j}
and a regularity exponent
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define for
T>0
the Strichartz/local smoothing type space

\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}:=C([0;T];H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))\bigcap_{j}\langle D\rangle^{-s}S^{p_{j},\iota_{j}}_{T}(X_{j}),

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}+\max_{j}\big\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u\big\|_{S^{p_{j},\iota_{j}}_{T}(X_{j})}.

(1.6)

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that either
(A1)
or
(A1)*
hold, and that the deterministic equation (
1.4
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
via (local in time) linear space-time (Strichartz/local smoothing) estimates. Namely, given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, there is a finite set
\mathcal{E}\subset(2;\infty)\times E\times\{\pm\}
of admissible exponents and there are
n,C_{0}>0
and a constant
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)\geq 0
such that for any
(p,\zeta,\iota)\in\mathcal{E}
, there are spatial function spaces
X_{\zeta}
such that for any
T\in(0;1]
, there is a constant
C_{p,\zeta,\iota}(T)>0

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{S^{p,\iota}_{T}(X_{\zeta})}\leq C_{p,\zeta,\iota}(T)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

(1.7)

for any

,
and moreover the nonlinear estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\big)\overline{w}dxdt\Big|\leq C_{0}T^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}\|u-v\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}\big(\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}\big)^{n}\|w\|_{\mathbf{S}^{-s}_{T}}

holds for any

fg\in B^{\alpha\wedge\beta}_{p,q}(\mathcal{M})
, and moreover it holds

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{B^{\alpha\wedge\beta}_{p,q}(\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathcal{M})}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathcal{M})}.

Finally, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in[1;\infty]
, we define the Fourier-Lebesgue space
\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
via the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}\|_{L^{r}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})}.

In particular
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})=W^{s,2}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{F}L^{s,2}(\mathcal{M})
.

2.3.2.
Adapted function spaces

In this subsection we recall the definition and properties of
U^{2}/V^{2}
-type spaces associated with the flow of the linear dispersive equation with modulated dispersion.

These spaces were first used in
[
55
,
56
]
to study the Cauchy problem for some dispersive PDEs, and we refer to
[
57
,
43
,
48
,
80
]
and in particular
[
22
]
for the proofs of their properties that we list below. We give the necessary adaptations to the case of Banach-valued functions as in
[
57
]
since we consider functions of
t
with values in
\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in[1;\infty]
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
I=[a;b)\subset\mathbb{R}
with
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
. Let then
\mathcal{Z}(I)
be the collection of finite non-decreasing sequences
\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
in
I
with
t_{0}=a
and
t_{K}=b
.

(i) We define
V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
as the space of functions
u:I\to\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
such that
u(t)
has a limit as
t\searrow a
and also
{\displaystyle\lim_{t\nearrow b}u(t)=0}
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V^{p}\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\sup_{\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\in\mathcal{Z}(I)}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|u(t_{k})-u(t_{k-1})\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(ii) We define a
U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
-atom to be a piecewise continuous function
A:I\to\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\displaystyle A(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k-1};t_{k})}\phi_{k}

for some partition
\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
and collection
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\in\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})^{K}
such that
{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\phi_{k}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}^{p}\leq 1}
. Note that
A
is right-continuous with
{\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow a}A(t)=0}
and has a limit as
t\nearrow b
.

Then
U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
is the space of functions
u:I\to\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\displaystyle u=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}A_{j},

with
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{K})
, and
A_{j}
are
U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
-atoms, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|,~~u=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}A_{j},~~A_{j}\text{ are $U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}$-atoms}\Big\}.

These spaces enjoy the following properties; again, we refer to
[
57
,
43
,
48
,
22
,
80
]
for the proofs.

Proposition 2.4
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
are Banach spaces.

(ii)
If
u\in U^{p}([-\infty;\infty)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
,
a>-\infty
, and
u(a)=0
, then
\mathbf{1}_{I}(t)u\in U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
\|u\|_{U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\|\mathbf{1}_{I}u\|_{U^{p}([-\infty;\infty))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}
.

Similarly, if
u\in V^{p}([-\infty;\infty))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
,
b<\infty
, and
u(b)=0
, then
\mathbf{1}_{I}(t)u\in V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
\|u\|_{V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}+\|u(a)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\|\mathbf{1}_{I}u\|_{V^{p}([-\infty;\infty))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}
.

(iii)
For any
1\leq p<q<\infty
, there are the continuous embeddings
U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\subset U^{q}\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\subset L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
and
V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\subset V^{q}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\subset L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
.

(iv)
If
u\in U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
with
u(b)=0
, then
u\in V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
\|u\|_{V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}
.

Similarly, for any
1\leq p<q<\infty
, if
u\in V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
is right-continuous with
u(a)=0
, then
u\in U^{q}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
\|u\|_{U^{q}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

We define the Banach space

DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})=\big\{\partial_{t}u,~~u\in U^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\big\},

where the derivative is taken in the sense of distributions, endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\|u\|_{U^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}
. The condition
u(a)=0
for
u\in U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
guarantees that for
f\in DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
there is a unique choice of
u\in U^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
such that
f=\partial_{t}u
.

The main result regarding this space is the duality relation between
DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
and
V^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{-s,r^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})
; see
[
57
]
for the Banach-valued version.

Proposition 2.6
.

For all
f\in L^{1}(I;\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))\subset DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
, it holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{DU^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}=\sup_{\|v\|_{V^{2}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{-s,r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\Big|\int_{I}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(t,x)\overline{v}(t,x)dxdt\Big|.

As mentioned in the introduction, the adapted solution space
X^{s,r}_{T}
for
u
solving (
1.1
) is the replacement of the solution space
D^{W}(H^{s})
used by Chouk and Gubinelli in the context of modulated Schrödinger and KdV equations; see
[
20
, Definition 2.2]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in[1;\infty]
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. We define

\displaystyle X^{s,p,r}(I)=\big\{u:I\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{K}:e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\in U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\big\}

and

\displaystyle Y^{s,p,r}(I)=\big\{u:I\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{K}:e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\in V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\big\}

with norms given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,p,r}(I)}=\|e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\|_{U^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}\text{ and }\|u\|_{Y^{s,p,r}(I)}=\|e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\|_{V^{p}(I)\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}.

When
I=[0;T)
for some
T>0
, we simply write
X^{s,p,r}(I)=X^{s,p,r}_{T}
and
Y^{s,p,r}(I)=Y^{s,p,r}_{T}
.

Finally, when
p=2
, we simply write
X^{s,r}_{T}
and
Y^{s,r}_{T}
, and when
r=2
, we just write
X^{s}_{T}
,
Y^{s}_{T}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Since
U^{2}\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
continuously, we have that for any
T>0
,
C([0;T);\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))\cap X^{s,r}_{T}
is a closed subspace of
C([0;T);\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in[1;\infty]
. Then the following hold:

(i)
We have the continuous embeddings

\displaystyle X^{s,r}(I)\subset Y^{s,r}(I)\subset X^{s,p,r}(I)

for any
2<p<\infty
.

(ii)
For any
T>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
, we have
e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}\phi\in X^{s,r}_{T}
and

\displaystyle\|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}\phi\|_{X^{s,r}_{T}}\leq\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}.

(iii)
For any
T>0
and
F\in L^{1}([0;T);\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
, we have
t\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{[0;\infty)}(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(W_{t}-W_{s})\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds\in X^{s,r}_{T}
and

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(W_{t}-W_{s})\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds\Big\|_{X^{s,r}_{T}}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y^{-s,r^{\prime}}_{T}\\
\|w\|_{Y^{-s,r^{\prime}}_{T}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}F(t,x)\overline{w}(t,x)dxdt\Big|.

(iv)
(Transference principle) If for some spatial function space
X
, some
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in[1;\infty]
,
p\in[1;\infty)
,
\iota\in\{\pm\}
, and any
T\in(0;1]
, there is
C_{X,s,r,p,\iota}(T)>0
non-decreasing in
T
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{-W_{t}\mathcal{L}}\phi\|_{S^{p,\iota}_{T}(X)}\leq C_{X,s,r,p,\iota}(T)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}

holds for any
\phi\in\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S^{p,\iota}_{T}(X)}\leq C_{X,s,r,p,\iota}(T)\|u\|_{U^{p}_{W\mathcal{L}}([0;T))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}

(2.2)

for any
u\in U^{p}_{W\mathcal{L}}([0;T);\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is straightforward from the definition of
X^{s,r}_{T}
and
Y^{s,r}_{T}
together with the embedding
V^{2}(I)\subset U^{p}(I)
(Proposition
2.4
(iii)).

Similarly, (ii) follows directly from the definition of the space
X^{s,r}_{T}
.

The property (iii) is fundamental and established e.g. in
[
43
,
48
]
for Hilbert-valued functions of
t
. Here we give a straightforward adaptation of the proof to the case of
\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
-valued function.
Indeed, extending
{\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}e^{W_{s}\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds}
by
{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{W_{s}\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds}
for
t\geq T
, since
t\mapsto e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}F(t)\in L^{1}([0;T];\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M}))
, we have by a
\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
-valued Riemann sum approximation that
{\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}e^{W_{s}\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds}
is in
U^{2}([0;T))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
, and so
e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}F(t)\in DU^{2}([0;T))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
with
{\displaystyle e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}F(t)=\partial_{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{W_{s}\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds\Big)}
. This shows that

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(W_{t}-W_{s})\mathcal{L}}F(s)ds\Big\|_{X^{s,r}_{T}}=\big\|e^{W_{t}\mathcal{L}}F\big\|_{DU^{2}([0;T))\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}},

and the conclusion of (iii) follows from Proposition
2.6
.

As for (iv), we have from the atomic structure of
U^{p}\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
(Definition
2.3
) and the definition of
U^{p}_{W\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})
(Definition
2.7
) that if

\displaystyle u=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\text{ with }a_{j}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{j}}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{j,k-1};t_{j,k})}e^{-W_{t}\mathcal{L}}\phi_{j,k}

for some
\{t_{j,k}\}_{k=0}^{K_{j}-1}\in\mathcal{Z}(T)
and
\{\phi_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{j}}\in\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}(\mathcal{M})^{K_{j}}
such that
{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K_{j}}\|\phi_{j,k}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}^{p}=1}
, and some
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S^{p,+}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{j}}\int_{t_{j,k-1}}^{t_{j,k}}\big\|e^{-W_{t}\mathcal{L}}\phi_{j,k}\big\|_{X}^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{X,s,r,p,\iota}(T)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{j}}\|\phi_{j,k}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,r}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{X,s,r,p,\iota}(T)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|.

We have a similar bound if we replace
L^{p}([0;T];X(\mathcal{M}))
by
X(\mathcal{M};L^{p}([0;T]))
. This proves (
2.2
) in view of Definition
2.7
and Definition
2.3
(ii).
∎

3.
Local well-posedness for general models: proof of Theorem
1.4

We now present the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

3.1.
Case (i)

We assume that
(A0)
holds, and that we have the space-time linear estimate (
1.7
) together with the non-linear estimate (
1.8
). We first treat the case of Strichartz estimates, namely (
1.7
) holds for some
(p,\zeta,\iota)\in\mathcal{E}
with
\iota=+
(recall that
p<\infty
by assumption). This implies that for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
, the map
{\displaystyle z\mapsto\|e^{z\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{X_{\zeta}}^{p}}
is integrable on
[0;T]
, in particular measurable and positive. Then we can use the occupation time formula (
2.1
) together with
(A0)
and (
1.7
). Indeed, letting
I_{W}(T):=[W_{0}-2\|W\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}};W_{0}+2\|W\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}]
, for all
t\in[0;T]
it holds
W_{t}\in I_{W}(T)
, we thus get that for any
(p,\zeta,+)\in\mathcal{E}
:

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}_{T}X_{\zeta}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{|W_{t}-W_{0}|\leq 2\|W\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}}\big\|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{X_{\zeta}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{W}(T)}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}e^{W_{0}\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{X_{\zeta}}^{p}d\mu_{[0;T]}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}_{I_{W}(T)}X_{\zeta}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}C_{p,\zeta,+}(|I_{W}(T)|)^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}.

Similarly, if
L^{p}_{T}X_{\zeta}
is replaced by
X_{\zeta}L^{p}_{T}
, we get for any fixed
x\in\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big\|_{L^{p}_{T}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{I_{W}(T)}(W_{t})\big|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big|^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{W}(T)}\big|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}e^{W_{0}\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big|^{p}d\mu_{[0;T]}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big\|_{L^{p}_{I_{W}(T)}}^{p}.

Taking the
\frac{1}{p}
-th power then the
X_{\zeta}
-norm, together with (
1.7
) this yields

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big\|_{X_{\zeta}L^{p}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}^{\frac{1}{p}}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)\big\|_{X_{\zeta}L^{p}_{I_{W}(T)}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{p,\zeta,-}(|I_{W}(T)|)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

All in all we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\langle D\rangle^{s}e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\big\|_{S^{p,\iota}_{T}X_{\zeta}}\leq C_{p,\zeta,\iota}^{W}(T)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},

(3.1)

for all
(p,\zeta,\iota)\in\mathcal{E}
, where

\displaystyle C_{p,\zeta,\iota}^{W}(T)=\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;\cdot]}}{dz}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}([0;T]\times I_{W}(T))}^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{p,\zeta,\iota}(|I_{W}(T)|).

(3.2)

From the definition (
1.6
) of
\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}
, the transference principle of Proposition
2.9
(iv) and the embeddings of Proposition
2.4
(ii), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}\leq C_{W}(T)\|u\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{s}},

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle C_{W}(T)=\max_{(p,\zeta,\iota)\in\mathcal{E}}C_{p,\zeta,\iota}^{W}(T),

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{E}}=\min\big\{p>2,~~\exists(\zeta,\iota)\in E\times\{\pm\},~~(p,\zeta,\iota)\in\mathcal{E}\big\}.

Recall that
p_{\mathcal{E}}>2
from our assumption that
\mathcal{E}\subset(2;\infty)\times E\times\{\pm\}
is finite.

From the nonlinear estimate (
1.8
), the linear estimate (
3.3
), and the embeddings of Propositions
2.4
(ii) and
2.9
(iv), we get

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\big)\overline{w}dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}\|u-v\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}\big(\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbf{S}^{s}_{T}}\big)^{r}\|w\|_{\mathbf{S}^{-s}_{T}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}C_{W}(T)^{r+2}\|u-v\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{s}}\big(\|u\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{s}}+\|v\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{s}}\big)^{r}\|w\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{-s}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}C_{W}(T)^{r+2}\|u-v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big(\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}+\|v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big)^{r}\|w\|_{Y^{-s}_{T}}.

(3.5)

Note that in the last step we used that
p_{\mathcal{E}}>2
to estimate
\|w\|_{U^{p_{\mathcal{E}}}_{W\mathcal{L}}(T)H^{-s}}\lesssim\|w\|_{Y^{-s}_{T}}
.

Then, for
R,T>0
, we define the ball

B_{R}(T):=\big\{u\in C([0;T);H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))\cap X^{s}_{T},~~\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq R\big\},

which is closed in
X^{s}_{T}
, hence a complete metric space when endowed with the metric inherited from
\|\cdot\|_{X^{s}_{T}}
.

Given
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
, we set
R=2A\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
for some constant
A\geq\max(1,C_{W}(T))
to be chosen later, and consider the map

\Phi_{u_{0}}:u\in B_{R}(T)\mapsto e^{-(W_{t}-W_{0})\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(W_{t}-W_{t^{\prime}})\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{N}(u)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

Note that from (
3.1
) and the embedding
Y^{-s}_{T}\subset L^{\infty}([0;T);H^{-s}(\mathcal{M}))
, we have that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\big\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-s}}\leq 1}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\big)\overline{w}dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}C_{W}(T)^{r+2}\|u-v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big(\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}+\|v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big)^{r}\|w\|_{Y^{-s}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}C_{W}(T)^{r+2}\|u-v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big(\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}+\|v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big)^{r}.

This shows that
\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\in L^{1}([0;T];H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))
for any
u,v\in X^{s}_{T}
. By Proposition
2.9
(ii) and (iii), we thus have

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)-\Phi_{v_{0}}(v)\big\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y^{-s}_{T}\\
\|w\|_{Y^{-s}_{T}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathcal{N}(u)-\mathcal{N}(v)\big)\overline{w}dxdt\Big|.

Thus, with (
3.1
) we find

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)-\Phi_{v_{0}}(v)\big\|_{X^{s}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+CT^{\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)}C_{W}(T)^{r+2}(2R)^{r}\|u-v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}

(3.6)

for any

.

First, in case where the equation (
1.4
) is sub-critical, we have
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)>0
, so that we can choose

such that
R\geq\max(1,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
, then

and

, which guarantee that

maps

to
B_{R}(T)
. With our choice of
R
and
T
as above, we deduce that
\Phi_{u_{0}}
is also a contraction on
B_{R}(T)
, thus having a unique fixed point

. If

is another solution, with
R^{\prime}=\max\big(R;\|v\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\big)
and
\tau=\tau(R^{\prime})\leq T
, the argument above shows that
v\equiv u
on
[0;\tau]
. But then iterating this argument on
[\tau;2\tau]
, etc., shows that
v\equiv u
on the whole time interval where

is defined, showing uniqueness in the whole class
C([0;T);H^{s}(\mathcal{M}))\cap X^{s}_{T}
. The estimate (
3.6
) also provides the Lipschitz dependence of the flow map on the initial data.

On the other hand, for critical equations, one has
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)=0
, and thus neglecting again the term

, which is only bounded under

, we have to take

, which gives small data local well-posedness, similarly as for the deterministic equation (
1.4
).

3.2.
Case (ii)

If now
(A0)*
holds instead of
(A0)
, the same discussion as in the previous subsection still holds and leads again to (
3.6
). However, from (
3.2
) and the fact that now
{\displaystyle T\mapsto\big\|\frac{d\mu_{[0;T]}}{dz}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{z}}}
is continuous and vanishes at

, we see that

\displaystyle C_{W}(T)\underset{T\searrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

(3.7)

Thus, even if
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)=0
in the critical case, we can choose again
A
such that
R\geq\max(1,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
, and then
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})\in(0;1)
small enough guaranteeing again that
\Phi_{u_{0}}
defines a contraction on
B_{R}(T)
.

Finally, if
s=0
and if
(A2)
also holds, then from the previous argument we have sub-critical local well-posedness in the sense that for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
, there is
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})>0
and a unique solution
u\in C([0;T];L^{2}(\mathcal{M}))\cap X^{0}_{T}
. But since
\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
from
(A2)
, we can iterate the local well-posedness on

, then
[kT;(k+1)T]
for any

. This shows global well-posedness of (
1.1
) in this case.

Remark 3.1
.

The gain (
3.7
) from the noise
W_{t}
under assumption
(A0)*
is similar in spirit to the use of the refined rescaling transform used to study mass-critical equations with multiplicative noise in
[
3
,
46
]
. While the latter relies heavily on stochastic properties of the noise, here this gain is entirely deterministic and only relies on the irregularity property of
W_{t}
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.4
above, the transfer of the linear space-time estimate (
1.7
) for the deterministic equation (
1.4
) to the modulated one (
1.1
) simply follows from the occupation time formula (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big\|e^{-W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\big\|_{X}^{p}dt=\int_{I_{W}(T)}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}u\big\|_{X}^{p}\frac{d\mu_{[0;T]}}{dz}(z)dz,

from which we got an upper bound in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In case

\inf_{z\in I_{W}(T)}\frac{d\mu_{[0;T]}}{dz}(z)>0,

this also provides a
lower
bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big\|e^{-W_{t}\mathcal{L}}u\big\|_{X}^{p}dt\gtrsim\int_{I_{W}(T)}\big\|e^{-z\mathcal{L}}u\big\|_{X}^{p}dz.

This shows in this case that one
cannot
get an improvement for the range of exponents in (
1.7
) for the modulated equation compared to the deterministic one. Note that for the standard Brownian motion, the local time at zero
\frac{d\mu_{[0;T]}}{dz}(0)=L_{T}(0)
has the same law as
\frac{1}{2}\max_{[0;T]}W_{t}

[
75
]
, which is a.s. positive. Thus the assumption above is satisfied for
T>0
small enough, which shows the sharpness of Theorem
1.4
in case of a Brownian modulation. In the case of the Schrödinger equation with white noise dispersion, this goes against the intuition that from the usual dispersion inequality
{\displaystyle\|e^{i(W_{t}-W_{\tau})\Delta}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim|W_{t}-W_{\tau}|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{x}}}
and the scaling
W_{t}-W_{\tau}\sim\sqrt{t-\tau}
, we would have expected a better (i.e. more integrable in 0) time decay, locally. Thus, through the usual
TT^{*}
argument via Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we would have expected a better range of exponents
(p,q)
for the local in time Strichartz estimate with white noise dispersion.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}:=\big\{(p,p,+),~~p\in[2;\infty]\big\}.

Since we have
s>\max(s_{c},0)
, taking
0<\varepsilon\ll s-\max(s_{c},0)
, we set
p=(d+2)m+\varepsilon
and
{\displaystyle q=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}
so that
{\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2m}{(d+2)m}=1}
. Then with these choices of
p
and
q
we restrict
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
to

\mathcal{E}=\big\{(p,p,+),(q,q,+)\big\}.

Note that with this choice, we have
p>\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
unless
d=m=1
, and
q\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
, and that in all cases
\sigma(q)=2\varepsilon
and
\sigma(p)=\max(s_{c},0)+O(\varepsilon)
.

To establish (
1.8
), we decompose again

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sum_{N_{0},...,N_{2m+1}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt

with now
N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}
by symmetry. We have again
N_{0}\lesssim N_{1}
and
N_{1}\sim\max(N_{0},N_{2})
. In this case, we make a further decomposition: for example, if
N_{0}\geq N_{2}
, since
\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}
has its Fourier support in
\{|n|\sim N_{1}\}
, we can cover this region by
O(N_{1}^{d}N_{2}^{-d})
cubes
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})
,
|\ell|\lesssim N_{1}^{d}N_{2}^{-d}
, of side length
N_{2}
and centred around some
|n_{\ell}|\sim N_{1}
. This leads to the decomposition

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt.

Now, using again the convolution constraint when writing the product on the Fourier side, we see that for any fixed
|\ell|\lesssim N_{1}^{d}N_{2}^{-d}
, there are at most
O(1)
values of
\widetilde{\ell}
such that the Fourier supports of
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}
are over-lapping, i.e. the integral on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is non-zero. Namely, similarly as in (
3.11
)-(
3.12
), we are left with estimating

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{2}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{0}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{0})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}u_{2}^{\pm_{2}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=0,2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt.

(3.18)

For
T\in(0;2\pi)
, we then estimate (
3.17
) with the help of Hölder’ inequality:

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}\|_{L^{(d+2)m}_{x}}.

Now, using Hölder’s inequality with the compactness of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and the definition of
p
, and the definition (
3.16
) of
X_{p}
, we have from using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\sum_{N}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}u\|_{L^{(d+2)m}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}u\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\sim\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{N}\sim N}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N}u\big\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\widetilde{N}}\ell^{1}_{N}\ell^{2}_{\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{N})}L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\widetilde{N}^{\sigma(p)}N^{-s}\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{N}\lesssim N}\widetilde{N}^{-\sigma(p)}N^{s}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N}u\big\|_{\ell^{1}_{\widetilde{N}}\ell^{1}_{N}\ell^{2}_{\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{N})}L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u\|_{X_{p}}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{N}\lesssim N}\widetilde{N}^{\sigma(p)}N^{-s}\big\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\widetilde{N}}\ell^{2}_{N}}\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u\|_{X_{p}},

where we used in the second step that
\#\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{N})\cap\supp\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{N}=O(\langle\frac{N}{\widetilde{N}}\rangle^{d})=O(1)
when
\widetilde{N}\sim N
, and in the last step that
\sigma(p)=\max(s_{c},0)+O(\varepsilon)<s
with our choice of
p
and
0<\varepsilon\ll s-\max(s_{c},0)
.

Similarly, using Cauchy-Schwarz to sum on both
N_{0}\sim N_{1}
and
\ell,\widetilde{\ell}
, we end up with

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{j}\|_{X_{p}}\Big)\sum_{N_{2}}\Big(N_{2}^{2\sigma(q)}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\big\|N_{2}^{-\sigma(q)}N_{0}^{-s}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N_{0}}\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}(N_{2}))L^{q}_{x}}\big\|N_{2}^{-\sigma(q)}N_{1}^{s}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N_{1}}\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}(N_{2}))L^{q}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{-s}u_{0}\|_{X_{q}}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{1}\|_{X_{q}}\Big(\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{j}\|_{X_{p}}\Big)\sum_{N_{2}}N_{2}^{2\sigma(q)}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}.

Since, with our choice of
q
, we have
\sigma(q)=2\varepsilon
, we can finally sum as above

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{2}}N_{2}^{2\sigma(q)}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{2}\|_{X_{p}}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{N}\lesssim N_{2}}\widetilde{N}^{\sigma(p)}N_{2}^{4\varepsilon-s}\big\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\widetilde{N}}\ell^{2}_{N_{2}}}\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{2}\|_{X_{p}},

using again that
\sigma(p)=\max(s_{c},0)+O(\varepsilon)<s-4\varepsilon
with our choice of
p
and
0<\varepsilon\ll s-s_{c}
.

All in all, taking the
L^{1}_{T}
norm of the previous computations, using Hölder’s inequality with the choice of
q
and
p
, we end up with the following estimate on (
3.17
):

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\widetilde{\ell}-\ell|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}u_{0}^{\pm_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}(N_{2})}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}u_{1}^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}u_{j}^{\pm_{j}}dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\Big\|\|\langle D\rangle^{-s}u_{0}\|_{X_{q}}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{1}\|_{X_{q}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{j}\|_{X_{p}}\Big\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{-s}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}_{T}X_{q}}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{1}\|_{L^{q}_{T}X_{q}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{j}\|_{L^{(d+2)m}_{T}X_{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim T^{2m\varepsilon}\|\langle D\rangle^{-s}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}_{T}X_{q}}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{1}\|_{L^{q}_{T}X_{q}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\|\langle D\rangle^{s}u_{j}\|_{L^{p}_{T}X_{p}}.

Proceeding as above, we find a similar estimate for (
3.18
). This finally shows (
1.8
) with
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)=2m\varepsilon
.

3.3.3.
NLS equation on compact manifolds

In the case where
\mathcal{M}
is a general closed, boundaryless, smooth
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
[
8
,
81
]
established the Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}u\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}}

(3.21)

for any
T\in(0;1]
and admissible
(p,q)
satisfying (
3.9
). In particular, this implies (
1.7
) with
\zeta=(p,q)\in[2;\infty]^{2}
,
X_{p,q}=W^{-\frac{1}{p},q}(\mathcal{M})
, and

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\big\{(p,p,q,+),~~(p,q)\text{ admissible}\big\}.

Let
s>\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{2m}
. We then restrict
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
to
\mathcal{E}=\{(p,p,q,+)\}
where
p=2m+\varepsilon
and
q
given by
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}=\frac{d}{2}
, for some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
so that
s>\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
.

Then from the proof of Proposition 3.1 in
[
8
]
, it holds

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}u_{j}dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-s}}\big\|\prod_{j=1}^{2m+1}u_{j}\big\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-s}}\sum_{j=1}^{2m+1}\Big\|\|u_{j}\|_{H^{s}}\prod_{\ell\neq j}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-s}}\sum_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}\prod_{\ell\neq j}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{2m}_{T}W^{s-\frac{1}{p},q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2m\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-s}}\sum_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}\prod_{\ell\neq j}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}_{T}W^{s-\frac{1}{p},q}},

where we used the fractional Leibniz rule, the Sobolev embedding
W^{s-\frac{1}{p},q}(\mathcal{M})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
from our choice of
p
and
q
, and Hölder’s inequality. This shows (
1.8
) with
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)=2m\varepsilon
.

3.3.4.
Mass-critical gKdV equation on
\mathbb{R}

In the case of (
1.10
) we have that (
1.7
) holds with
\zeta=(q,\theta)\in[2;\infty]\times\mathbb{R}
and
X_{q,\theta}=D^{-\theta}L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, from
[
62
, Section 7.1]
, we have the local smoothing estimate

\displaystyle\big\|D^{\theta}e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}u\big\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}

for
(p,q,\theta,\iota)\in\big\{(2,\infty,1,-);(\infty,4,-\frac{1}{4},-)\big\}
, uniformly in
T>0
. By interpolation, we deduce that it holds for any
(p,q,\theta,\iota)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\bigcap_{\delta\in(0;1)}\Big\{

\displaystyle(p,q,\theta,\iota)\in(2;\infty)\times(4;\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times\{\pm\},

\displaystyle\qquad\iota=-,~~p=\frac{2}{\delta},~~q=\frac{4}{1-\delta},~~\theta=\delta-\frac{1-\delta}{4}\Big\}.

Since we assumed
\mathcal{E}
to be finite in Theorem
1.4
, we restrict
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
by setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\Big\{(\frac{10}{3},10,\frac{1}{2},-),(10,5,0,-)\Big\}.

Then, we can estimate with Hölder’s inequality and the fractional Leibniz rule (see
[
62
, Lemma 7.6]
):

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}u_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{5}u_{j}dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{10}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{T}}\big\|D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\prod_{j=1}^{5}u_{j}\big\|_{L^{\frac{10}{9}}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{7}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{10}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{5}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{j}\|_{L^{10}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{T}}\big\|\prod_{\ell\neq j}u_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{\frac{5}{4}}_{x}L^{\frac{5}{2}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{10}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{5}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{j}\|_{L^{10}_{x}L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{T}}\prod_{\ell\neq j}\big\|u_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{5}_{x}L^{10}_{T}}

This shows (
1.8
) when
s=0
for this model, with
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}(s)=0
in this case, showing that it is indeed a mass-critical model. The case
s>0
follows from a straightforward adaptation.

4.
The modulated periodic NLS equation at critical regularity

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.10
. Since, under
(A0)(ρ,γ)
for
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho>1
, local well-posedness for
s>\max(s_{c};0)=\max(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{m};0)
follows from Theorem
1.4
as discussed in Subsubsection
3.3.2
and Remark
1.2
(iii), we focus on the critical case
s=s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{m}>0
; recall the assumption
m>\frac{2}{d}
in Theorem
1.10
.

We start by recasting the Strichartz estimates (
3.13
)-(
3.14
) as a multilinear restriction estimate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\displaystyle\frac{2(d+2)}{d}<p<m(d+2)}
and
q
such that
{\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2m}{p}=1}
. Then for any
N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}
, any
\mathcal{C},\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})
, and any
U_{0},\dots,U_{2m+1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with non-negative Fourier coefficients, it holds

\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})\\
\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\times N_{2}^{s_{c}+(2m-1)\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{s_{c}-\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

with
{\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{1}{m}>0}
.

The same estimate holds exchanging the roles of
N_{0}
and
N_{2}
.

Proof.

We observe that for any
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{it\big(\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}-\nu\big)}dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{-it\nu}\big(e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\big)^{\pm_{0}}\big(e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\big)^{\pm_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{2m+1}\big(e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\big)^{\pm_{j}}dxdt.

Thus, since we have
p,q>\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
from our choice of
p
and
q
, we have from (
3.13
)-(
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{5}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{5}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{5}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{5}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\big\|e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big\|e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}\prod_{j=2}^{5}\big\|e^{it\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|\mathbf{P}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times N_{2}^{2\big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}\big)+\big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+1}{p}\big)}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

The case
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{0}
is estimated similarly. This proves Lemma
4.1
since
{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}=s_{c}-\big(\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\big)}
and
{2\big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}\big)+\big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+1}{p}\big)=s_{c}+(2m-1)\big(\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\big)}
by the definition of
s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{m}
and our choice of
q
such that
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2m}{p}=1
.
∎

We now establish a refined version of Lemma

Lemma 4.2
.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times N_{2}^{s_{c}-(2m-1)\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{2}}+\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\Big)^{\theta}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{s_{c}+\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

Proof.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any

\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\end{subarray}}\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|U_{0}\|_{H^{-s_{c}}}\prod_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|U_{j}\|_{H^{s_{c}}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{0},N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}\\
\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{0})\\
|\ell-\widetilde{\ell}|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\end{subarray}}\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|U_{0}\|_{H^{-s_{c}}}\prod_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|U_{j}\|_{H^{s_{c}}}.

Proof.

We start by rewriting

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\end{subarray}}\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\langle\nu\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j}).

Since
\rho>1
, taking

and

as in Lemma
4.1
with

close enough to

such that

satisfies
(2m-1)\varepsilon<2\delta
, we can estimate the sum on
|\nu|\gtrsim N_{2}^{2}
by using Lemma
4.1
:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\nu|\gtrsim N_{2}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\langle\nu\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim N_{2}^{-2\delta}\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\|_{L^{2}}N_{2}^{s_{c}+(2m-1)\varepsilon-2\delta}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{s_{c}-\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

With our choice of
\varepsilon,\delta
we can thus use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to sum on
\ell,\widetilde{\ell}
then
N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}\\
\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\ell-\widetilde{\ell}|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{|\nu|\gtrsim N_{2}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\langle\nu\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}\\
\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\
|\ell-\widetilde{\ell}|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times N_{2}^{1+15\varepsilon-2\delta}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{5}N_{j}^{1-5\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}}N_{0}^{-s_{c}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}N_{1}^{s_{c}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times N_{2}^{s_{c}+(2m-1)\varepsilon-2\delta}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{s_{c}-\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|U_{0}\|_{H^{-s_{c}}}\prod_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|U_{j}\|_{H^{s_{c}}}.

It remains to estimate the contribution from
|\nu|\ll N_{2}^{2}
. Following
[
48
]
, in order to use Lemma
4.2
, for
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})
, we write
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}=n_{\ell}+[-N_{2};N_{2}]^{d}
for some
|n_{\ell}|\sim N_{1}
, and for

M=(N_{2}^{2}N_{1}^{-1})\vee 1,

we let
\{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\}
,
|\alpha|\sim M^{-1}N_{1}
, be a partition of
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
into almost disjoint strips orthogonal to
n_{\ell}
of width
M
:

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=\big\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~~n\cdot n_{\ell}\in[\alpha|n_{\ell}|M;(\alpha+1)|n_{\ell}|M)\big\},

and similarly for a partition
\{\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\}
of
\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}
.

For
|\nu|\ll N_{2}^{2}
, we can thus decompose

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j}).

Now, the point is that, as in
[
48

and
[
87

\alpha

O(1)
values of

for which the sum above is non empty. Indeed, for

\displaystyle|n_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|n_{0}-n_{\ell}|^{2}+2n_{0}\cdot n_{\ell}+|n_{\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{O(N_{2}^{2})}{\underbrace{|n_{0}-n_{\ell}|^{2}}}+\underset{=\alpha^{2}M^{2}+O(\alpha M^{2})}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{|n_{\ell}|^{2}}(n_{0}\cdot n_{\ell})^{2}}}-\underset{=O(N_{2}^{2})}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{|n_{\ell}|^{2}}\big((n_{0}-n_{\ell})\cdot n_{\ell})\big)^{2}}}

with

, i.e.

(\pm_{0}\alpha^{2}\pm_{1}\beta^{2})M^{2}=O((|\alpha|+|\beta|)M).

Thus
|\pm_{0}\alpha\pm_{1}\beta|\lesssim 1

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},...,n_{2m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\

\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\

4.2

4.2

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}\\

\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}n_{j}=0\\
|n_{j}|\sim N_{j},~~n_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell},~n_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\\
\sum_{j=0}^{2m+1}\pm_{j}|n_{j}|^{2}=\nu\end{subarray}}\langle\nu\rangle^{-\rho}\prod_{j=0}^{2m+1}\widehat{U}_{j}(n_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\geq\dots\geq N_{2m+1}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{0}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}U_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times N_{2}^{s_{c}-(2m-1)\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{N_{2}}+\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\Big)^{\theta}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}U_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j=3}^{2m+1}N_{j}^{s_{c}+\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{j}}U_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\|U_{0}\|_{H^{-s_{c}}}\prod_{j=1}^{2m+1}\|U_{j}\|_{H^{s_{c}}}.

The case
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{0}
is treated similarly. This proves Proposition
4.3
.
∎

We can now state our main multilinear estimate.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any

,

,

, and

(4.3)

Proof.

Following the argument in Subsubsection

it suffices to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{0}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{2})\\

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{2}\sim N_{1}\geq N_{0}\geq...\geq N_{2m+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{C}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\in\mathcal{C}(N_{0})\\

(4.5)

From the embedding

with

, and the atomic structure of

and

,

are

-atoms and

-atom:

\displaystyle u_{j}(t,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K^{(j)}}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k-1}^{(j)};t_{k}^{(j)})}e^{iW_{t}\Delta}\phi_{k}^{(j)},\qquad\{t_{k}^{(j)}\}_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}(T),\qquad\|\phi_{k}^{(j)}\|_{\ell^{2}_{k}H^{s_{c}}}\leq 1

Then, for given

using Plancherel theorem and the occupation time formula (

), we estimate for any
N_{0},\dots,N_{2m+1}
,

LS-category and sequential topological complexity of symmetric products
Ekansh Jauhari

Abstract
. The n n -th symmetric product of a topological space X X is the orbit space of the natural action of the symmetric group S n S_{n} on the product space X n X^{n} . In this paper, we compute the sequential topological complexities of (finite products of) symmetric products of closed orientable surfaces, thereby verifying the rationality conjecture of Farber and Oprea for these spaces. Additionally, we determine the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category of (finite products of) the symmetric products of closed non-orientable surfaces. More generally, we provide lower bounds to the LS-category and the sequential topological complexities of the symmetric products of finite CW complexes X X in terms of the cohomology of X X and its products. On the way, we also obtain new lower bounds to the sequential distributional complexities of continuous maps and study the homotopy groups of the symmetric products of closed surfaces.

Introduction

The topological complexity of a topological space
[
Far1
]
,
[
Ru
]
is a homotopy invariant that measures the complexity of moving points continuously within the space. More precisely, given a “nice” topological space

-th sequential topological complexity of

, denoted
\TC_{m}(Y)

is the
n

, denoted

Y
become unordered
n
-tuples of positions in
X
.

Though the notion of sequential topological complexity of spaces was initially motivated by topological robotics, it has been studied as a homotopy invariant for several classes of spaces, see, for example,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]

[

[

[

]

In general, precisely determining the LS-category of a space is difficult, and determining (or even estimating) its (sequential) topological complexity can be even more difficult, see, for example,

]

In this paper, we study the problem of estimating the sequential topological complexities and the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category of symmetric products of topological spaces, specifically finite CW complexes. The spaces of our particular interest are the symmetric products of closed
2
-manifolds (or surfaces), which are closed even-dimensional manifolds.

Our first main result is the exact computation of the sequential topological complexities of the symmetric products of closed orientable surfaces
M_{g}
of genus

Theorem A
.

For any integers

\TC_{m}\left(SP^{n}(M_{g})\right)=m\cat\left(SP^{n}(M_{g})\right)=\begin{cases}2mn&\text{ if }n\leq g\\
mn+mg&\text{ if }n>g.\end{cases}

To prove Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a brief review of the symmetric products of spaces and the classical and distributional complexities of spaces and maps.

2.A.
Symmetric products

The
n
-th symmetric product of a space
X
, denoted
SP^{n}(X)
, is defined as the orbit space
X^{n}/S_{n}
of the natural action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
on the product space
X^{n}
which permutes the coordinates. Then the elements of
SP^{n}(X)
are unordered
n
-tuples
[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where
x_{i}\in X
. We write
\vartheta_{n}:X^{n}\to SP^{n}(X)
for the corresponding quotient map.

Let us fix some
x_{0}\in X
as the basepoint of
X
. Then there are the basepoint inclusions
SP^{n}(X)\hookrightarrow SP^{n+k}(X)
for each
k\geq 1
. The colimit over the symmetric products with respect to the basepoint inclusions defines the infinite symmetric product
SP^{\infty}(X)
, which is a free abelian topological monoid. It follows from the Dold–Thom theorem
[
DT
]
that
\pi_{k}(SP^{\infty}(X))=\widetilde{H}_{k}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
for each
k\geq 1
.

When
X
is a CW complex,
SP^{n}(X)
can also be given a CW structure. Indeed, the product space
X^{n}
has a CW structure such that each
\sigma\in S_{n}
permutes the cells.
This CW structure on
X^{n}
respects the permutation action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, thereby giving a CW complex structure to the orbit space
SP^{n}(X)
, see
[
tD
, Proposition II.1.5 and Exercise II.1.17 (2)]
.

If
X
is a
2
-dimensional CW complex with only one vertex (for example, if
X
is a surface), then the CW structure on
SP^{n}(X)
is natural and can be described explicitly
[
KS
]
, see also
[
BR
]
. More precisely, for some integers
k\geq 0
and
r\geq 1
, let
X
be a
2
-dimensional CW complex obtained by attaching
r
number of closed
2
-discs to the wedge
\bigvee_{i=0}^{k}\hskip 1.42262ptS^{1}_{i}
. Let
e\in X
be the basepoint of the wedge that is also the identity element of each circle group
S^{1}_{i}
. Define a relation
\sim
on
SP^{n}(X)
such that

\left[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{n}\right]\sim\left[e,x_{1}\cdot x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{n}\right]

whenever
x_{1},x_{2}\in S^{1}_{i}
for some
i
. Here,
\cdot
denotes the group-theoretic multiplication on
S_{i}^{1}
. Let
\overline{SP}^{n}(X):=SP^{n}(X)/\sim
and
q_{n}:SP^{n}(X)\to\overline{SP}^{n}(X)
be the quotient map. It follows from
[
KS
]
that
\overline{SP}^{n}(X)
has a canonical CW structure and
q_{n}
is a natural homotopy equivalence. Also, the composition
q_{n}\circ\vartheta_{n}:X^{n}\to\overline{SP}^{n}(X)
is cellular. The basepoint inclusions
\overline{SP}^{n}(X)\hookrightarrow\overline{SP}^{n+1}(X)
define
\overline{SP}^{\infty}(X)
in the obvious way. Since
q_{n}\circ\vartheta_{n}
is cellular for each
n
, the
k
-skeleton of
\overline{SP}^{k}(X)
coincides with the
k
-skeleton of
\overline{SP}^{n}(X)
for each
k\leq n
. In particular, this means that

2.B.
LS-category and topological complexity

Let

be a space and

be a ring.
Then the cup-length of

(or the

of positive degree cohomology classes

We recall that the

(LS-category) of a map

, denoted

, is the smallest integer
n
for which there is a covering
\{U_{i}\}
of

by
n+1
open sets such that the restricted maps

are null-homotopic for each

,
[
BG
]
. When
f
is the identity map on
X
, we obtain the LS-category of the space
X
, denoted
\cat(X)
,
[
Ja
]
,
[
CLOT
]
.

For a proof of the following, see
[

]
and
[

]
(see also
[

]
and
[
CLOT
]
for the case when

is the identity map).

2.1 Theorem
.

For a map
f:X\to Y
between finite-dimensional CW complexes and ring
R
,
\cu_{R}(\textup{Im}(f^{*}))\leq\cat(f)\leq\min\{\cat(X),\cat(Y)\}\leq\min\{\dim(X),\dim(Y)\}
.

For any integer
m\geq 2
, let
\Delta_{m}:X\to X^{m}
denote the diagonal map. Suppose
\Delta_{m}^{*}:H^{*}(X^{m};R)\to H^{*}(X;R)
is the map induced by
\Delta_{m}
in
R
-cohomology.

2.2 Definition

(
[
BGRT
]
)
.

The
m
-th
R
-zero-divisor cup-length
of

, denoted

\text{Ker}(\Delta_{m}^{*})
.

For given

, the
m
-th

of a map

of

[

(and also
[

]
for the case

). Here,

denotes the path space of

with the compact-open topology. When

is the identity map on

-th sequential topological complexity of the space

, denoted

, which was introduced by Rudyak
[

]
as an extension to Farber’s
[

]

The next theorem follows from
[

]
and

]
(and also

]

2.3 Theorem
.

For a space

, integer

, and ring

\max\{\cat(X^{m-1}),\zcl^{m}_{R}(X)\}\leq\TC_{m}(X)\leq\cat(X^{m})\leq m\cat(X)
.

(see also

For a map

Here,

is defined as

for each

2.C.
Distributional category and complexity

Recently in
[

]
and
[

]
, some probabilistic variants of

and

were introduced. These are the distributional category and the

[

]
around the same time. See
[

, Section 1]
,
[

, Section 1]
, and
[

For a metric space

, let

denote the set of probability measures on

[

]

[

P(z_{1},z_{2})=\{\phi\in P(Z)\mid\phi(0)=z_{1},\phi(1)=z_{2}\}
for any

has a fixed basepoint

We recall that the

of a pointed map

for which there exists a continuous map

such that

for each

, see
[

]
,
[
J2
]
. When
f
is the identity map on
X
, we obtain the distributional category of the space
X
, denoted
\dcat(X)
,
[

]
.

In
[

]
, it was shown that
\cu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\leq\dcat(X)\leq\cat(X)
when

is a finite CW complex. This inequality can be generalized from spaces to maps as follows.

2.5 Theorem
.

For a map
f:X\to Y
between finite CW complexes, we have that
\cu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\textup{Im}(f^{*}))\leq\dcat(f)\leq\min\{\cat(f),\dcat(X),\dcat(Y)\}
.

Proof sketch.

Only the lower bound

needs some explanation. It follows from
[

, Proposition 2.5 (4)]
that
\cu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\textup{Im}(f^{*}\circ\delta_{n!}^{*}))\leq\dcat(f)
, where
\delta_{n!}:Y\to SP^{n!}(Y)
is the diagonal embedding (see the Introduction). By Proposition
3.2
,
\delta_{n!}^{*}
is an epimorphism, so one can proceed along the lines of
[

, Sections 4.1 & 4.2]
to conclude that
\cu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\textup{Im}(f^{*}\circ\delta_{n!}^{*}))=\cu_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\textup{Im}(f^{*}))
.
∎

Let us fix
m\geq 2
. For any

-tuple
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in Z^{m}
, let

P\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\right)=\left\{\phi\in P(Z)\hskip 2.84526pt\middle|\hskip 2.84526pt\phi\left(\frac{i-1}{m-1}\right)=z_{i}\textup{ for all }1\leq i\leq m\right\}.

and integer

, the

-th

of

is the identity map on

[

]
(and also
[

]

The proof of the following statement is a generalization of the proof of the corresponding statement for

2.6 Theorem
.

For a map

H^{*}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
, can be seen as an analog of Theorem
5.1
in the non-orientable setting.

6.4 Theorem

(
[
KS
]
)
.

The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-cohomology ring
H^{*}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
is generated by the cohomology classes
e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*},\ldots,e_{g}^{*}
and
d^{*}
under the following relations:

(1)

For each
1\leq i\leq g
,
(e_{i}^{*})^{2}=d^{*}
, and

(2)

e_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots e_{i_{r}}^{*}(d^{*})^{s}=0
whenever
r+s\geq n+1
for distinct indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
.

We note that it follows from
[
KS
, Lemma 19]
that
(d^{*})^{n}\neq 0
.

6.C.
LS-category and topological complexity

We are now in a position to compute the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category of
SP^{n}(N_{g})
for each
n
and
g
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Of course, for each
n,g\geq 1
, we have the dimensional upper bound
\cat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))\leq\dim(SP^{n}(N_{g}))=2n
due to Theorem
2.1
. We now obtain the lower bounds separately in the cases
n\leq g
and
n>g
.

First, let
n\leq g
. Then we have from Theorem
6.4
that

\left(e_{1}^{*}\right)^{2}\left(e_{2}^{*}\right)^{2}\cdots\left(e_{n}^{*}\right)^{2}=\left(d^{*}\right)^{n}\neq 0.

Therefore, Theorem
2.1
gives the inequality
2n\leq\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))\leq\cat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
when
n\leq g
.

Next, let
n>g
. Then we again get from Theorem
6.4
that

\left(e_{1}^{*}\right)^{2n-2g+2}\left(e_{2}^{*}\right)^{2}\left(e_{3}^{*}\right)^{2}\cdots\left(e_{g}^{*}\right)^{2}=\left(d^{*}\right)^{n-g+1}\left(d^{*}\right)^{g-1}=\left(d^{*}\right)^{n}\neq 0.

As before, we get
2n\leq\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))\leq\cat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
when
n>g
. Hence, we have that
\cat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))=2n
for all
n,g\geq 1
.
∎

Unlike
\dcat(SP^{n}(M_{g}))
, here we can’t determine
\dcat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
when
n\geq 2
because
\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(X)
is not a lower bound to
\dcat(X)
. The methods of
[
J2
, Section 3]
also do not help here because
\pi_{1}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))={\mathbb{Z}}^{g-1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
has non-trivial torsion.

6.5 Remark
.

It is well-known that
SP^{n}(N_{1})={\mathbb{R}}P^{2n}

{\mathbb{R}}P^{2n}

We now study the LS-category of finite products of the symmetric products of non-orientable surfaces.

Proof of Corollary
B.1
.

The proof is similar to that of Corollary
A.2
. The proof of Theorem
B
implies that
\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))=\cat(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
for each
n
and
g
. The Künneth formula gives the following isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-algebras:

H^{*}(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}})\times SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}}))=H^{*}(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}}))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}H^{*}(SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}})).

So,
\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}}))+\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}}))\leq\cu_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}})\times SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}}))
, which gives
\cat(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}}))+\cat(SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}}))=\cat(SP^{n_{1}}(N_{g_{1}})\times SP^{n_{2}}(N_{g_{2}}))
in view of (
VI
). Thus, our desired conclusions follow from an inductive argument.
∎

We follow Gromov
[
Gr
]
and say that a closed
k
-manifold
M
is
essential
if a map
u:M\to B\pi_{1}(M)
that induces an isomorphism of the fundamental groups cannot be deformed to the
(k-1)
-skeleton of the CW complex
B\pi_{1}(M)
. In particular, every closed aspherical manifold is essential, although the converse is not true.

Proof of Corollary
B.2
.

Recall that a closed
k
-manifold
M
is essential if and only if
\cat(M)=\dim(M)=k
, see
[
KR
, Theorem 4.1]
(and also
[
CLOT
, Theorem 2.51]
). Therefore, given
n\geq 1
,
SP^{n}(M_{g})
is essential precisely when
n\leq g
because of Theorem
5.3
, and
SP^{n}(N_{g})
is essential for all
g\geq 1
due to Theorem
B
.
∎

For the
m
-th sequential topological complexity of
SP^{n}(N_{g})
for each
n,g\geq 1
and
m\geq 2
, we have the bounds
2n(m-1)\leq\TC_{m}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))\leq 2nm
due to Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
B.1
. Let us discuss the case
n=1
first. For
m=2
, the lower bound is not attained for any
g
, while the upper bound is attained for each
g\geq 2
, see
[
Dr2
]
and
[
CV
]
. Notably, for
m\geq 3
, the upper bound is always attained due to
[
GGGL
]
.

We now focus on the case
n\geq 2
for
m=2
and explain why the technique of Section
5.B
and
[
DDJ
, Section 6.C]
that helps determine
\TC(SP^{n}(M_{g}))
does not help in determining
\TC(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
for general
n\geq 2
and
g\geq 1
.

Given the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-cohomology classes
e_{i}^{*}\in H^{1}(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
for integers
1\leq i\leq g
, let us define the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-special zero-divisors
\overline{e_{i}^{*}}=e_{i}^{*}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{i}^{*}
. It is easy to see using Theorem
6.4
that in mod
2
, for any
k\leq\min\{n,g\}
, we have that

\left(\overline{e_{1}^{*}}\right)^{2}\left(\overline{e_{2}^{*}}\right)^{2}\cdots\left(\overline{e_{k}^{*}}\right)^{2}=\left(\left(e_{1}^{*}\right)^{2}\otimes 1+1\otimes\left(e_{1}^{*}\right)^{2}\right)\cdots\left(\left(e_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}\otimes 1+1\otimes\left(e_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}\right)

=\left(d^{*}\otimes 1+1\otimes d^{*}\right)^{k}\in H^{2k}(SP^{n}(N_{g})\times SP^{n}(N_{g})).

For any
j\leq k
, since
(d^{*})^{j}\neq 0
by
[
KS
, Lemma 19]
,
(d^{*})^{j}
is the identity of
H^{2j}(SP^{n}(N_{g});{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
because we are working in the coefficient field
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Since
H^{*}(SP^{n}(N_{g});{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is finitely generated
[
KS
]
, the Kunneth isomorphism (see
[
Ha
, Section 3.2]
) implies that
(d^{*})^{k-i}\otimes(d^{*})^{i}=((d^{*})^{k-i}\otimes 1)\smile(1\otimes(d^{*})^{i})
is the identity of
H^{2k}(SP^{n}(N_{g})\times SP^{n}(N_{g});{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
for
0\leq i\leq k
. In particular,
(d^{*})^{k-i}\otimes(d^{*})^{i}
and
(d^{*})^{i}\otimes(d^{*})^{k-i}
both represent the identity of
H^{2k}(SP^{n}(N_{g})\times SP^{n}(N_{g});{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

Now we notice the following phenomenon.

6.6 Proposition
.

In
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-coefficients, for each
k\geq 1
the term
(d^{*}\otimes 1+1\otimes d^{*})^{k}
has an even number of terms of the form
(d^{*})^{k-i}\otimes(d^{*})^{i}
, where
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
.

Proof.

By the binomial theorem, it suffices to show that
{k\choose i}\equiv 1(\text{mod }2)
holds for an even number of
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. Clearly,
{k\choose i}
is odd if and only if
{k\choose k-i}
is odd. Moreover, when
k
is even,
{k\choose k/2}
is also even. To see this, let us write the binary expansions of
k
and a fixed
i\leq k
as
k=k_{0}+k_{1}\cdot 2+\cdots+k_{t-1}\cdot 2^{t-1}+k_{t}\cdot 2^{t}
and
i=i_{0}+i_{1}\cdot 2+\cdots+i_{t-1}\cdot 2^{t-1}+i_{t}\cdot 2^{t}
, respectively, where
t\geq 0
is an integer and
k_{j},i_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for
0\leq j\leq t
. Lucas’s Theorem from 1878 (see
[
Fi
]
for a short proof) says that

{k\choose i}\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{t}{k_{j}\choose i_{j}}(\text{mod }2).

Hence,
{k\choose i}
is odd if and only if
i_{j}=1\implies k_{j}=1
for any
j
, so it follows that
{k\choose k/2}
is even. Thus, integers
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
for which
{k\choose i}\equiv 1(\text{mod }2)
holds always exist in pairs. This completes the proof.
∎

Therefore, for
k\leq\min\{n,g\}
, we deduce from Proposition
6.6
that

\left(\overline{e_{1}^{*}}\right)^{2}\left(\overline{e_{2}^{*}}\right)^{2}\cdots\left(\overline{e_{k}^{*}}\right)^{2}=\left(d^{*}\otimes 1+1\otimes d^{*}\right)^{k}=0

in
H^{2k}(SP^{n}(N_{g})\times SP^{n}(N_{g});{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. Hence, the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-zero-divisor cup-length of
SP^{n}(N_{g})
for
n,g\geq 1
does not give us a lower bound to
\TC(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
greater than
2n
if we use techniques from Section
5.B
. We also don’t know whether an upper bound to
\TC(SP^{n}(N_{g}))
smaller than
4n
can be obtained in general!
Therefore, the topological complexity of
SP^{n}(N_{g})
remains undetermined in the cases
n\geq 2
.

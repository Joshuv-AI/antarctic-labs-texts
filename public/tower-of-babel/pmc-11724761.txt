Heliyon Zoology: Furthering our understanding of animals
Authors: Gareth Arnott, Andrew Crump
Published: 2024

Charles Darwin is not famous for his barnacle research. Yet, whilst ruminating over the theory of evolution by natural selection, he spent eight years studying the minutiae of barnacle taxonomy, anatomy, reproduction, physiology, and behaviour [1]. At first glance, this research may not seem terribly exciting. Whilst writing his second barnacle monograph in 1852, Darwin himself complained that “I hate a Barnacle as no man ever did before” – a reminder that even great scientists have bad days.

Dig deeper, though, and Darwin's study of barnacles was profoundly important. He understood that, if members of a species were uniform, natural selection would have no variation to work on, and his grand theory would not work. Meticulous observations of barnacles revealed, in Darwin's words, “variability of every part in some slight degree of every species.” This important research illustrated the variation that natural selection works on. Indeed, barnacles appear repeatedly in the Origin of Species [2]. Therefore, Darwin's meticulous research on barnacles facilitated a huge scientific breakthrough.

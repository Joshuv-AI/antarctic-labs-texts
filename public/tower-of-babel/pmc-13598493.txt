Research Trends in Neutrophil Extracellular Traps and Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury: A Dual-Database Bibliometric Analysis from 2013 to 2025
Authors: Shaofeng Chen, Tao Chen, Dejun Kong, Jinliang Duan, Yuan Chang, Zhenglu Wang, Hong Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNeutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), defined as DNA-protein complexes released upon neutrophil activation, have been increasingly recognized as pivotal mediators in the initiation and perpetuation of ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) in recent scholarly investigations. Despite the increasing number of related studies, there is no bibliometric analysis in this field. This study aims to analyze the development trend, research status, and hotspots of NETs and IRI, thereby providing a comprehensive and intuitive academic reference for investigators in this field.MethodsThe Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database and Scopus database were used to search the literature in the field of NETs and IRI from January 1, 2013 to December 31, 2025. Using bibliometric software such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and the bibliometrix R-package, was used to conduct co-occurrence analysis, cluster analysis and emergence analysis were conducted on the annual number of publications, countries/regions, institutions, authors, journals and keywords.ResultsThis is the first bibliometric analysis systematically characterizing the landscape of NETs-mediated IRI research. After screening, 224 articles were used in the bibliometric analysis. These articles were from 449 institutions in 30 countries, written by 1642 authors, published in 141 journals, and cited 10326 times in total based on the merged dataset. The number of annual publications in this field is steadily but rapidly growing, with the United States, China, and Germany leading the way in research. Frontiers In Immunology was the journal with the most publications. Samer Tohme from the University of Pittsburgh (USA) contributed the most. Volker Brinkmann from the Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology was the most co-cited author. Liver IRI and myocardial IRI related to NETs are hot topics in this field. Keyword emergence analysis indicates a trend in which the research frontier has evolved from observation of phenomena towards mechanism exploration and clinical translation.ConclusionThe research on NETs and IRI is in a period of rapid development. Current studies have systematically elucidated the multiple pathogenic effects of NETs and IRI, and formed a research framework with “molecular mechanism-organ injury-inflammation regulation-intervention strategy” as the core. Bibliometric trends suggest that future research directions are likely to increasingly focus on the development of high-specificity inhibitors and clinical translational initiatives, as reflected by the increasing frequency of these topics in recent publications.Keywords: ischemia-reperfusion injury, neutrophil extracellular traps, bibliometric analysis, innate immunity, aseptic inflammation

Introduction

Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury (IRI) denotes a complex pathophysiological cascade characterized by the exacerbation of structural and functional damage to tissues or organs following the restoration of blood flow and oxygenation subsequent to a period of ischemia.1 This paradoxical injury, first described in the context of myocardial infarction and later recognized as a universal phenomenon across various organ systems, primarily results from the sudden burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS) upon reperfusion, which overwhelms endogenous antioxidant defenses and triggers a cascade of cellular damage. This phenomenon is widely seen in a variety of major clinical diseases, such as acute myocardial infarction,2 stroke,3 organ transplantation,4 and shock resuscitation after severe trauma.5 It is a key factor leading to organ dysfunction and even failure, which seriously affects the prognosis of patients. The pathogenesis of IRI is extremely complex, involving multiple interrelated links such as oxidative stress, calcium overload, inflammatory cell infiltration, apoptosis and necrosis.6 Among them, sterile inflammatory response is considered to be the core driving tissue damage.7 Unlike pathogen-induced inflammation, sterile inflammation is initiated by endogenous danger signals released from damaged cells—known as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)—which activate pattern recognition receptors on immune cells and propagate tissue injury.

Neutrophils, as key effector cells of the innate immune system, play a central role in the inflammatory cascade of IRI.8 In recent years, a unique biological phenomenon called Neutrophil Extracellular Traps (NETs) has attracted great attention of researchers. NETs are reticular structures released by activated neutrophils into the extracellular space. Their skeleton is decondensed chromatin DNA, and they are attached with a variety of proteins with cytotoxic and pro-inflammatory activities such as histone, neutrophil elastase (NE), myeloperoxidase (MPO).9 The formation of NETs, termed NETosis, can occur through two main pathways: a suicidal NETosis that leads to neutrophil lysis (typically taking 2–4 hours) and a vital NETosis that preserves the plasma membrane integrity (occurring within minutes). Both pathways rely on the enzymatic activity of peptidylarginine deiminase 4 (PAD4), which citrullinates histones to promote chromatin decondensation—a process now recognized as a critical therapeutic target. Initially, NETs were considered an important mechanism for host defense against pathogen infection,10 however, subsequent studies have found that excessive formation or impaired degradation of NETs can turn them into a double-edged sword, mediating tissue damage under aseptic inflammatory conditions such as IRI. On the one hand, NETs physically block microvessels through its reticular structure and aggravate tissue ischemia.11 On the other hand, its histone and enzymatic components directly damage endothelial cells and parenchymal cells, and act as DAMPs to activate platelets, complement system and coagulation cascade, thereby amplifying inflammatory response and thrombosis,12 which constitutes a key link in the pathological process of IRI.

Bibliometrics is a scientific tool that uses mathematical and statistical methods to quantitatively analyze the literature in a specific research field. Through visual analysis of data such as authors, institutions, countries, keywords and citations, bibliometrics can objectively reveal the development trend of a research field, the core research strength, cooperation network, knowledge base and frontier hotspots, and provide a macro perspective for evaluating the research status and predicting the future direction.13 To date, no bibliometric studies have systematically synthesized the landscape of NET-related IRI research. To fill this research gap, this study conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the literature on NETs and IRI published from January 1, 2013 to December 31, 2025, aiming to characterize the current research landscape of this interdisciplinary field, identify key contributors, and predict future research trend.

Methods

Data Source and Search Strategy

Data from The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database (https://www.webofscience.com/wos/woscc/basic-search) and Scopus databases (https://www.scopus.com/pages/home). Given the differences in field tags and indexing rules between the two databases, we used database-specific search strings. The WoSCC query was as follows: TS=((“neutrophil extracellular trap” OR “NETs” OR “NETosis” OR “extracellular DNA trap”) AND TS=(“ischemia-reperfusion injury” OR “IRI” OR “ischemia reperfusion” OR “post-transplant ischemia” OR “reperfusion injury”)). The Scopus query was as follows: TITLE-ABS-KEY(“neutrophil extracellular trap” OR “NETs” OR “NETosis” OR “extracellular DNA trap”) AND TITLE-ABS-KEY(“ischemia-reperfusion injury” OR “IRI” OR “ischemia reperfusion” OR “post-transplant ischemia” OR “reperfusion injury”). The search period was from January 1, 2004 to December 31, 2025. In addition, to prevent inconsistencies caused by database updates, all articles were retrieved and exported on March 26, 2026 to ensure reliability and timeliness of the data. The document type is article and review article. No other filters (eg, journal category, publication status) were applied. Articles that mentioned only NETs or IRI diseases or were of low relevance to the topic were excluded. 232 documents remained in WoSCC and 478 in Scopus. To construct a unified analytical dataset from the two databases, we implemented a structured harmonization pipeline using custom Python scripts. Since WoSCC and Scopus employ distinct field tags and data structures, all Scopus records were first converted into WoSCC-compatible plain-text format, unifying field tags, author name formats, and journal abbreviation styles across both sources. After format harmonization, duplicate records between the two databases were identified and removed using a conservative two-step matching strategy. First, records were matched by DOI, which served as the primary gold-standard unique identifier. Second, for records without a valid DOI, duplicates were detected based on normalized full titles (after standardizing punctuation, case, and common abbreviations, with fuzzy matching applied to account for minor typographical variations). For records identified as duplicates (ie, the same publication indexed in both databases), the WoSCC record was retained as the primary entry, while Scopus data were used to supplement missing fields (eg, abstract, language, publisher information). Importantly, for citation counts (TC field), we adopted the maximum value between the two sources rather than summing them, thereby avoiding double-counting of citations while maximizing coverage. Scopus-only records (those without a WoSCC counterpart) were retained and appended to the final dataset. The output was generated as a standard WoSCC-formatted plain-text file to ensure full compatibility with CiteSpace and VOSviewer. All records were manually checked by two reviewers to confirm their accuracy. Since the first related literature in this field was published in 2013, the time window of this study was set from 2013 to 2025 (inclusive) using Python. At the same time, the language of literature is limited to English. After this process, 224 unique records were retained for analysis. This approach ensured that citation-based indicators-including total citations, average citations per article, H‑index values, co-citation frequencies, and journal impact metrics-were derived from the merged harmonized citation dataset rather than from either database in isolation, thereby maximizing citation coverage while maintaining structural consistency. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Specific inclusion and exclusion criteria were used in this study to ensure an appropriate and high quality selection of literature. Emphasis is placed on articles and reviews, especially original research articles, to ensure that key research findings are incorporated into relevant areas. In addition, inclusion criteria were limited to articles published in English to facilitate frequency analysis. Conference abstracts, conference papers, editorials, lectures, letters, incomplete references, and duplicate publications were excluded. These criteria ensured the high quality of the selected articles.

To maintain the quality of the included studies, strict quality control measures were implemented. Two independent investigators extracted relevant data by reviewing literature titles and abstracts. Each selected article was comprehensively assessed according to predetermined inclusion criteria. Any discrepancies between the two investigators were discussed with a third investigator. The adoption of these quality-management procedures was designed to ensure the credibility of the study results. For further analysis, multiple information related to the literature was collected, including number of publications, country or region, institution, journal, author, keywords, and citation frequency.

Data Analysis

We used three mainstream bibliometric tools for multi-dimensional data visualization and analysis: VOSviewer (v1.6.20), CiteSpace (v6.3.R1) and R language package bibliometrix (v4.3.3). VOSviewer is outstanding in constructing and visualizing institutional networks, author networks, co-authorship networks, citation patterns, keyword co-occurrence and co-citation networks, which enables us to intuitively reveal the collaborative structure and knowledge flow. CiteSpace can detect the citation outbreak phenomenon, and identify the dynamic evolution of research hotspots and the citation flow among journals through the double graph superposition technology, so as to capture the emerging trend. bibliometrix provides comprehensive capabilities to visualize the geographic distribution of global research output, quantify the impact of authors, institutions, and journals, and use multivariate statistics to reveal core trends and patterns in the dataset. The combined application of these three tools ensures a systematic deep mapping of the scientific landscape in the field of IRI and NETs. In this study, full counting was applied for all VOSviewer co-authorship and co-occurrence analyses. Time slicing was set to 1-year intervals for CiteSpace, and the log-likelihood ratio (LLR) test was used for cluster labeling. Nodes represent the extracted entities (eg, countries, institutions, authors, keywords), and edges represent their co-occurrence or co-citation relationships. All detailed parameters are presented in Supplementary Table S1.

To test the robustness of our results against the inclusion of review articles, we performed a sensitivity analysis by re‑running all ranking and clustering analyses on the subset of original research articles only (n=156). Results

The Publication and Citation Trends

We conducted an analysis of the literature in the research field related to IRI and NETs to evaluate the overall development trend in an objective and quantitative manner. In the past 12 years from 2013 to 2025, a total number of 224 articles were screened by WoSCC database and Scopus databases for analysis. As shown in Figure 2, although Volker Brinkmann et al first explicitly described and named NETs in 2004,10 it was not until 2013 that researchers began to pay attention to the role of NETs and IRI. This likely reflects the fact that at the beginning of NETs, researchers have not yet linked NETs, a unique bactericidal mechanism, with IRI. The understanding of neutrophils in the field of IRI is still in the stage of “traditional viewpoints regarding neutrophil function”, and it is believed that its damage mechanism is mainly through the release of proteases (such as myeloperoxidase, neutrophil elastase) and reactive oxygen species to directly damage tissues.14 Since 2018, the number of related research publications has entered an increasing period year by year. Among them, the largest number of papers were published in 2025 (n=59), indicating that the role of NETs and IRI is gradually being paid attention to.

Annual distribution of publications in NETs and IRI research from 2013 to 2025.

While classical pathways such as oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction remain the volumetric leaders in terms of total publications, they represent a mature, and arguably plateauing, research paradigm.15,16 In contrast, the explosive growth of NETs-related publications-particularly the doubling of output between 2020 and 2025-indicates a field that is rapidly moving from the periphery to the core of IRI pathobiology. These findings reflect a burgeoning scholarly interest in the association between IRI and NETs, as evidenced by the escalating publication output. The observed growth trajectory, as measured by publication counts, suggests increasing research activit.

Literature Co-Occurrence and Reference Burst Analysis

The literature co-occurrence network analysis revealed the progressive academic evolution of NETs in the field of IRI. The co-occurrence map contained 38 key articles and formed 5 main clusters (Figure 6A). The red cluster (Huang 2015, Tohme 2016) was the theoretical cornerstone, which explained the pathological mechanism of NETs in liver IRI. The blue clusters (Zhang 2022, Mallavia 2020, Kaiser 2022) represent the latest research directions, which will expand the scope of multi-organ IRI and clinical translation. The green cluster (Silk 2017, Nakazawa 2017) revealed the complement and signal transduction mechanism. The purple and yellow clusters (Honda 2018 and Dhanesha 2022) deepened the research on molecular targets. The orange cluster (Chu 2023, Luo 2023) focused on NETs targeted intervention strategies. Citation bursts reflect a sharp increase in the citation frequency of specific publications within a given period, serving as a crucial indicator for identifying research frontiers, knowledge turning points, and key breakthrough achievements in academic fields. Through the citation burst detection algorithm of CiteSpace software, a temporal burst map of the top 24 highly cited references was constructed (Figure 6B). The results revealed that: Early bursts (2013–2017) were predominantly focused on fundamental NETs mechanisms and their pathophysiological significance, represented by seminal works from Alexander Brill (2012, burst strength 4.44) and Caudrillier A (2012, burst strength 4.0), which established the foundational understanding of NET formation and their pathophysiological roles; Mid-term bursts (2018–2022) demonstrated a marked shift toward organ-specific IRI pathological mechanism exploration. Among these studies, the work by Huang et al (2015) published in Hepatology represented a pivotal advancement, exhibiting the highest burst strength in this phase (7.0), establishing it as the cornerstone literature for elucidating hepatic IRI mechanisms mediated by NETs. This period witnessed the transition from general mechanistic studies to clinically relevant organ-specific investigations, with hepatic IRI emerging as a major research focus. Recent bursts (2023–2025) have concentrated on clinical translational applications and therapeutic interventions targeting NETs. Frederik Denorme (2022) demonstrated sustained burst activity extending through 2025, marking the emergence of clinical translation as the current research frontier in this field. Regarding burst duration patterns, most references demonstrated burst period spanning 2–4 years, consistent with typical cycles of research interest and knowledge dissemination. Notably, mid-term research on renal IRI mechanismswas exemplified by Daigo Nakazawa (2017, burst strength 4.97), which sustained a 4-year burst period from 2018–2022. Most recently, FrederikDenorme (2022) demonstrated burst activity extending through 2025 (strength 3.41), indicating that the mechanistic roles of NETs in renal diseases and cardiovascular IRI remain active and productive research hotspotswith ongoing clinical significance.

Keywords and Keywords Burst Analysis

Keyword analysis revealed the knowledge structure and thematic evolution characteristics of the NETs and IRI research field. Co-occurrence clustering analysis of IRI and NETs-related literature was performed using CiteSpace software, which identified 9 major research clusters (modularity Q=0.5236, mean silhouette S=0.88641) (Figure 7A): (1) mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress (#0 mitogen-activated protein kinase, #4 reactive oxygen species production), (2) inflammatory cascades (#7 inflammation, #8 neuroinflammation), (3) vascular complications (#1 pyruvate kinase, #6 insulin resistance), and (4) tissue-specific injury mechanisms (#2 acute lung injury, #5 inhibition, #3 neutrophil extracellular traps). The temporal analysis of citation bursts revealed three distinct evolutionary phases in research focus (Figure 7B). The initial phase (2013–2014) was dominated by fundamental mechanistic keywords including “deep vein thrombosis” (strength: 2.72, 2013–2020), “histones” (1.94, 2013–2020), and “deoxyribonuclease I” (1.91, 2014–2017), emphasizing the characterization of NET formation and histone-mediated endothelial dysfunction. The intermediate phase (2015–2019) witnessed a shift toward systemic inflammatory markers, with “mediators” (2.17, 2015–2019), “chromatin decondensation” (2.13, 2015–2022), and “acute lung injury” (1.83, 2014–2016) demonstrating heightened research attention to multi-organ involvement in IRI pathophysiology. Most notably, the recent phase (2020–2025) exhibits emerging focus on translational applications and integrated tissue responses. “Immunology” (3.02, 2020–2021) shows sustained high-strength bursts, suggesting accelerated clinical translation. Novel emerging keywords include “liver transplantation” (2.6, 2021–2025), “cell death” (2.2, 2021–2022), and “rat” (1.92, 2023–2025), indicating expansion from traditional organs (lung, heart) to hepatic reperfusion injury. The recent emergence of “mitochondrial DNA” (1.7, 2022–2023) as a burst keyword reflects the growing recognition of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) in NETs-mediated IRI pathogenesis.

Sensitivity Analysis: Excluding Review Articles

To evaluate whether the inclusion of review articles (n=68) biased our primary bibliometric rankings, we repeated the core analyses using only original research articles (n=156). The rankings of countries, institutions, journals, and authors were re‑computed, and the results are summarized in Supplementary Tables S2–S5. When comparing the full‑dataset rankings (Tables 1–4) with the original‑article‑only rankings (Supplementary Tables S2–S5), we observed a high degree of concordance. Because ranking data are ordinal, we used Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ) to quantify the consistency. Taking the top 10 countries as an illustrative example, the sum of squared rank differences was 14, yielding =0.915. When 30 countries were included in the calculation, ρ reached 0.943, indicating an extremely high concordance between the two ranking lists. Similar results were obtained for institutions (ρ = 0.988), journals (ρ = 0.927), and authors (ρ = 0.943), indicating that the overall leadership positions (China, USA, Germany; Fudan University and University of Pittsburgh; Frontiers in Immunology; Samer Tohme) remained essentially unchanged after excluding reviews. However, some shifts were noted. Italy, which ranked 8th in the full dataset with only 5 publications, jumped to 10th in the original‑article‑only list but still maintained the highest average citations per article (296.0), suggesting that its influence stems from a few highly cited original studies rather than reviews. The journal Cells dropped from 3rd to outside the top 10 when reviews were excluded, while Kidney International appeared in the top 10 only in the original‑article subset, reflecting the stronger presence of primary renal IRI studies. These nuanced differences highlight that reviews tend to amplify certain topics (eg, therapeutic strategies), but the core knowledge structure and major contributors are robust to the inclusion/exclusion of review articles. Therefore, our main conclusions are stable, and the combined dataset provides a more comprehensive view of the entire research landscape.

Discussion

The present study systematically evaluated the current research status and potential future directions in the field of NETs in IRI. To our knowledge, this is the first bibliometric analyses focusing specifically on NETs‑mediated IRI. Our results reveal important findings, research interests, and frontiers in this particular field. Citation co-analysis and keyword co-analysis can quickly grasp the distribution and evolution hotspots of NETs in the field of IRI.

General Information Bibliometric Study

A comprehensive bibliometric analysis was conducted to elucidate the research status, developmental trends, and potential hotspots of NETs and IRI. The results showed that since the concept of NETs was first proposed in 2004,10 the first study correlating NETs with IRI was not published until 2013.18 This reflects the cognitive shift of researchers to redefine NETs from an “antibacterial defense mechanism” to an “inflammatory effector mechanism” that plays a key role in aseptic inflammation. Around 2009, as researchers began to think about the potential pathogenic role of NETs in non-infectious diseases, the research field gradually expanded from infectious diseases to autoimmune diseases,19 thrombosis,20 cancer metastasis21 and other pathological conditions. As a prototypical model of sterile inflammatory injury, IRI subsequently garnered attention from NETs researchers. Following 2018, the annual number of publications in this field exhibited a significant year-on-year increase, with a notable peak in 2025. This trend underscores the growing recognition of NETs’ role in IRI and highlights the substantial research potential and clinical application prospects of this domain.

At the national level, studies from 30 countries/regions were included. Among them, China (125 articles) and the United States (55 articles) were far ahead in the number of publications, followed by Germany (20 articles) and Japan (16 articles), which fully reflected the dominant position and outstanding contribution of these two countries in this field. Institutional analysis showed that the Fudan University in the China (12 articles) ranked first in the number of articles, followed by the University of Pittsburgh in the United States (11 articles) and Nanjing University in China (9 articles), which reflected the formation of a global research network in the field of NETs and IRI with China and the United States as the core. Although China has an advantage in terms of the number of publications, Italy (148.4) is more prominent in terms of the average number of citations, which may be related to its depth and influence in a particular research direction. It is important to distinguish among three distinct bibliometric dimensions when interpreting country-level data. Productivity (number of publications) measures research activity volume; academic influence (citations per article, H-index) reflects per-unit scientific impact; and collaborative centrality (proportion of multi-country publications) indicates a country’s role in international knowledge networks. China leads in productivity, but does not lead in influence or collaborative centrality (the United States has higher per-article citations and a comparable MCP ratio). Therefore, future studies should avoid conflating these dimensions and instead report them separately.

Journal analysis identified 141 academic journals dedicated to this research area, among which Frontiers in Immunology (15 articles) published the highest number of relevant studies. With its high impact factor (5.9) and total citations (842), this journal serves as a key platform for information dissemination in the field. Although the number of articles published in Blood (IF=23.1) was only 3, its extremely high impact factor and total number of citations (432) showed its importance in promoting high-impact research in this field. The co-citation analysis showed that molecular biology, immunology and clinical medicine journals played a central role in the research and knowledge dissemination in this field, and the close relationship between basic research and clinical application was strongly confirmed.

In terms of author analysis, Samer Tohme (11 publications) and Loughran Patricia (9 publications) are the most prolific researchers in this field, with long-term collaboration establishing a systematic research framework for NETs in hepatic IRI. Their representative work elucidated the pivotal role of PAD4-mediated NETs formation in hepatic IRI,17 and through dynamic network analysis (DyNA), confirmed that interleukin-17A (IL-17A) is the core inflammatory driver of NETs-mediated hepatic IRI,22 providing important insights into the self-sustaining mechanisms of sterile inflammation. Furthermore, they pioneered the discovery that NETs not only participate in acute inflammatory injury but also promote hepatic metastasis development following surgical stress,23 expanding NETs research from organ injury to the tumor microenvironment. Particularly noteworthy is the outstanding contribution of Chinese scholar Hai Huang (8 publications) in this field, whose publication and citation numbers rank among the top. Hai Huang’s groundbreaking study published in Hepatology in 2015 was the first to systematically confirm the critical role of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)-activated NETs in sterile hepatic inflammatory injury.17 This work, with 560 citations, has become one of the foundational studies in hepatic IRI and NETs research. Additionally, Hai Huang et al revealed the molecular mechanisms by which histones participate in sterile hepatic injury through activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in Kupffer cells,24 and clarified the dual roles of cell-specific high-mobility group box 1 protein in hepatic IRI.25 These studies not only deepened our understanding of the upstream signaling pathways in NETs formation but also provided important theoretical basis for developing therapeutic strategies targeting the DAMPs-NETs axis, indicating that Chinese scholars have secured a prominent academic position in this field.

The temporal evolution of keyword emergence patterns traces a clear developmental arc in scientific understanding that maps onto known phases of biomedical research maturation. The early burst phase (2013–2017), dominated by keywords such as “deep vein thrombosis”, “histones”, “deoxyribonuclease I”, “acute lung injury”, and “atherosclerosis”, reflects an era when researchers were primarily characterizing NETs formation and cataloging their histone-mediated endothelial dysfunction, while also beginning to recognize the involvement of NETs in multi‑organ pathology such as lung and vascular injury. This phenomenological phase was essential for establishing the basic facts of NET biology in IRI contexts. The intermediate phase (2015–2019) witnessed a demonstrable shift toward cellular and inflammatory mechanisms, with the emergence of “mediators”, “chromatin decondensation”, “cells”, “extracellular traps”, “neutrophil”, “immunity”, and “reperfusion injury.” The sustained burst of “chromatin decondensation” (2015–2022) highlights growing interest in the epigenetic regulation of NET formation, while the direct appearance of “neutrophil” and “extracellular traps” indicates that the field had moved from downstream effectors (eg, histones) to the cellular source and process of NETosis itself. This phase reflects a conceptual transition: NETs were no longer viewed as isolated phenomena in single‑organ injury, but as integrative components of systemic inflammatory and immune networks. Most significantly, the recent phase (2020–2025) shows an accelerating focus on translational applications and emerging mechanistic categories. The burst keywords “liver transplantation”, “rat”, “cell death”, and “mitochondrial DNA” mark a fundamental transition toward clinically relevant models and mechanistic crosstalk. “Liver transplantation” and “rat” (2023–2025) point to the growing use of preclinical transplantation models, signaling a shift from descriptive pathology to intervention‑oriented research. Meanwhile, the concurrent bursts of “cell death” (2021–2022) and “mitochondrial DNA” (2022–2023) reveal that the field has begun to appreciate the reciprocal relationship between NETs and other cell death pathways—mitochondrial DNA acts both as a trigger for NETosis and as a mediator of NET‑induced cell death.

The separation of original research articles from reviews revealed complementary roles of the two publication types. Original articles—which constitute the majority of the literature (69.6%)—have driven the recent growth in output and focus on organ-specific mechanisms, animal models, and molecular effectors such as PAD4 and MPO. In contrast, reviews (30.4%) exhibit higher average citation impact and concentrate on therapeutic strategies, signaling pathways, and clinical translation. This division suggests that the field has matured to a stage where primary experimental evidence accumulates rapidly, while reviews serve as important synthesizers that guide future investigations. From a bibliometric perspective, combining both types in a single analysis may overrepresent the citation influence of reviews; however, including both provides a comprehensive picture of the research landscape. Future bibliometric studies in this area may benefit from stratified analysis by publication type.

Research Hotspots and Frontiers

The bibliometric data presented here capture a research field in transition from descriptive, organ-specific inquiries toward mechanistic understanding and therapeutic innovation. The present analysis reveals growing research activity across multiple organ systems affected by IRI.

A promising direction in the cardiovascular field is to optimize perioperative myocardial protection strategies, such as the choice of cardioplegic solution, to mitigate ischemic injury at the tissue and cellular levels.26 At the same time, NETs-mediated myocardial IRI has become another prominent research direction. Ge et al provided pioneering evidence that NETs contribute to myocardial no-reflow following ischemia-reperfusion, and demonstrated that DNase I-based reperfusion strategies can restore microvascular perfusion and reduce infarct size.27 This work, with 290 citations, has become foundational in the cardiac NETs literature. Savchenko et al established the role of PAD4-dependent chromatin decondensation in exacerbating myocardial IRI, revealing that PAD4-mediated histone citrullination facilitates leukocyte recruitment and vascular dysfunction during cardiac reperfusion.28 The cardiovascular NETs research landscape has expanded considerably, with recent studies connecting NETs to heart failure pathophysiology (Ichimura et al)29 and providing comprehensive mechanistic reviews of NETosis in myocardial IRI (Zhang et al)30 A nuanced finding by Eghbalzadeh et al revealed that PAD4-deficient mice exhibit compromised anti-inflammatory NETs function post-myocardial infarction, suggesting that NETs may exert context-dependent effects on cardiac repair and inflammation resolution.31 The cardiovascular research cluster in our co-citation network (green cluster, including Blood and Cardiovascular Research) reflects the growing integration of hematological, immunological, and cardiological perspectives in understanding NETs-mediated cardiac injury.

In the neuroinflammatory domain, NETs-mediated cerebral IRI represents an emerging but rapidly growing research frontier. Li et al demonstrated that NETs exacerbate ischemic brain damage by promoting cerebral vascular dysfunction and neuronal death in experimental stroke models,32 establishing a direct link between NETs formation and neurological outcomes. Luo et al comprehensively characterized the dual role of NETs in cerebral IRI, noting that while NETs contribute to acute tissue damage through microvascular thrombosis and blood-brain barrier disruption, they may also participate in inflammatory resolution under certain condition.33 Recent reviews by Krishnamoorthy et al34 and Fang et al35 have systematically mapped the mechanistic pathways linking NETs to stroke pathogenesis, highlighting therapeutic opportunities for DNase-based and PAD4-targeted interventions. The emergence of “neuroinflammation” as a keyword cluster (#8) in our analysis aligns with this expanding research direction. Furthermore, Zhang et al revealed that NETs aggravate neuronal apoptosis through neddylation-dependent pathways,36 adding molecular granularity to our understanding of NETs-mediated neurotoxicity.

The cerebral IRI research trajectory mirrors the developmental arc observed in hepatic and cardiac NETs research—moving from phenomenological characterization toward mechanistic dissection and therapeutic targeting. In pulmonary pathophysiology, NETs have been implicated in acute lung injury and primary graft dysfunction following lung transplantation. Sayah et al provided evidence that NETs are pathogenic in primary graft dysfunction after lung transplantation, with NETs biomarkers correlating with injury severity and clinical outcomes.37 Scozzi et al reviewed the mechanisms by which NETs contribute to alveolar-capillary barrier disruption and leukocyte recruitment in acute lung injury.38

Zhan et al elucidated a gut-lung axis of NETs-mediated injury, demonstrating that HMGB1-driven NETs formation during intestinal ischemia-reperfusion exacerbates remote pulmonary damage.39 Recent work by Wu et al identified neutrophil PPIF as a regulator of calcium overload-induced NETs formation in lung transplantation-associated IRI.40 providing novel mechanistic insight into post-transplant pulmonary complications. The keyword “acute lung injury” (#2) maintained a strong bridging position in our three-dimensional network visualization, underscoring the persistent relevance of pulmonary NETs research within the broader IRI landscape.

The emergence of PAD4 inhibitors and DNase-based therapeutic strategies as burst keywords in 2022–2025 indicates a growing research interest in these approaches within the published literature. PAD4 is a serine protease that catalyzes the citrullination of histones, a post-translational modification essential for chromatin decondensation and NET formation in sterile inflammation contexts. The development of selective PAD4 inhibitors (eg, YW3-56) represents a rational drug design approach that has demonstrated efficacy in reducing renal and hepatic IRI in preclinical models.39,41 DNase-based strategies, by contrast, target the DNA scaffold of already-formed NETs, aiming to mechanically destroy the NET structure and reduce microvascular thrombosis and endothelial damage.42 The dual approach-targeting NET formation (via PAD4 inhibition) and NET degradation (via DNase)-reflects a more nuanced understanding that optimal therapeutic efficacy may require intervention at multiple points in the NET-IRI pathological cascade. However, as a bibliometric observation, this trend reflects scholarly attention rather than confirmed clinical or biological efficacy. In addition, the field faces critical challenges in translating these mechanistic insights into effective clinical therapeutics. First, most published studies employ single-organ injury models, typically hepatic or renal IRI in rodents; human polytrauma or multi-organ IRI involves temporally and spatially complex injury patterns that may not be recapitulated in simplified animal models. Second, While circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA), MPO-DNA complexes, and citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3) are widely used as systemic NET biomarkers in research, they lack the spatial resolution required to pinpoint the exact organ undergoing IRI. In multi-organ clinical scenarios, such as cardiac surgery or multi-organ transplantation, systemic NET levels do not reliably correlate with the severity of organ-specific damage. critical for timing interventions and monitoring therapeutic efficacy in heterogeneous clinical populations. Third, safety concerns surrounding broad NET inhibition must be rigorously addressed. NETs evolved as a fundamental host defense mechanism against bacterial and fungal infections. Systemic or prolonged suppression of NETosis-particularly through pan-PAD4 inhibitors-carries the inherent risk of immunosuppression, potentially increasing susceptibility to secondary infections (eg, hospital-acquired pneumonia or surgical site infections) in critically ill IRI patients.43 Furthermore, PAD4 is expressed in various non-neutrophil cell types and participates in gene regulation, macrophage polarization, and epigenetic modulation; thus, off-target effects of PAD4 inhibitors remain a significant pharmacological concern.44 To mitigate these risks, next-generation therapeutic strategies should prioritize localized delivery (eg, organ-targeted nanoparticles) or the development of highly isoform-specific PAD inhibitors that preserve basal immune surveillance while selectively blocking pathological NETosis in injured tissues. Finally, the optimal timing of NET-targeted therapy remains a critical unresolved question. The bibliometric shift toward PAD4 and DNase as dual targets implies that the therapeutic window is highly dynamic. Preclinical data suggest that early administration of PAD4 inhibitors during the ischemic or immediate reperfusion phase can prevent NET formation and mitigate initial microvascular obstruction, whereas delayed DNase administration may be more appropriate in the subacute phase to clear established NET scaffolds that perpetuate sterile inflammation.28 However, human IRI progresses through highly variable temporal phases depending on the underlying etiology (eg, transient clamping during surgery versus prolonged ischemia in myocardial infarction). Future clinical trials must stratify patients by ischemic duration and reperfusion time to define precise therapeutic windows, rather than applying a uniform intervention schedule.

Limitations

Several limitations inherent to this bibliometric study warrant consideration. First, database coverage and indexing bias. Our data were solely retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and Scopus databases. Although these two databases are among the most comprehensive and authoritative sources for bibliometric analysis, they do not index all existing scientific literature. Consequently, relevant studies published in journals not covered by WoSCC or Scopus, as well as non-English language journals, may have been omitted, potentially introducing selection bias. Second, language bias. Only English-language publications were included in this analysis. While English is the dominant language in scientific communication, this exclusion may have overlooked significant research published in other languages (eg, Chinese, German, Japanese), particularly studies with high regional impact. This language restriction may therefore affect the completeness and generalizability of our findings. Third, citation-related biases. Citation-based indicators (eg, total citations, average citations per article, H‑index) are influenced by several factors beyond scholarly impact. These include self-citations, citation cartels (coercive or reciprocal citation practices among certain groups), and differences in citation conventions across fields. Although we did not exclude self-citations, their potential influence on the ranking of authors, institutions, and journals should be acknowledged. Fourth, while we harmonized and merged citation data from both WoSCC and Scopus using a structured pipeline, we acknowledge that the two databases differ in their indexing policies, coverage periods, and citation tracking algorithms. While we performed duplicate removal and cross-validation, subtle differences in citation counts for the same publication between WoSCC and Scopus cannot be entirely eliminated. Fifth, intrinsic limitations of bibliometric analysis. Bibliometric methods inherently focus on external quantitative characteristics of the literature (eg, publication counts, citation frequencies, co‑occurrence patterns). They cannot directly evaluate the internal academic quality of individual studies, the rigor of experimental design, the reproducibility of results, or the clinical validity of the conclusions. A highly cited article is not necessarily methodologically sound, and a low-cited article may represent an important but niche discovery. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as reflecting patterns of scientific activity and attention, not as direct measures of research quality.

Conclusion

In summary, the intersection of IRI and NETs has developed into a dynamic and rapidly expanding research direction, forming a research landscape centered on the exploration of basic mechanisms, the study of organ-specific damage, and the development of targeted therapies. Through the analysis of literature, this study systematically revealed that the research strength in this field was mainly concentrated in top scientific institutions in China, the United States and other countries. The research hotspots focused on the inflammation, oxidative stress and cell death pathways mediated by NETs, and the research frontier clearly pointed to the crosstalk mechanism of new cell death modes and the precise intervention strategy targeting key molecules such as PAD4. Based on the observed bibliometric trends, future research is likely to continue exploring the deep integration of mechanism understanding and clinical translation, striving to clarify the heterogeneous roles and regulatory networks of NETs in different organs of IRI, and combining multi-omics techniques and prospective clinical cohort studies to accelerate the development of safe and effective new drugs targeting NETs. In order to improve the clinical prognosis of IRI related diseases.

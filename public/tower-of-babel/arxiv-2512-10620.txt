Asymptotic analysis of fractional Sobolev spaces on thin films in the low-integrability regime
Andrea Braides, Andrea Pinamonti, Margherita Solci

Abstract
We study the behaviour of fractional Sobolev spaces H s ​ ( Ω ε ) H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}) with s ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) s\in(0,1/2) defined on “thin films” Ω ε = ω × ( 0 , ε ) \Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon) in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and prove that they tend to the space H s + 1 2 ​ ( ω ) H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\omega) as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . This is made precise by using a notion of dimension-reduction convergence, with respect to which suitably scaled Gagliardo seminorms define equicoercive functionals. Asymptotic results are proved for s → 0 + s\to 0^{+} and s → 1 / 2 − s\to 1/2^{-} . MSC codes: 49J45, 35R11, 73K10. Keywords: Γ \Gamma -convergence, non-local functionals, fractional Sobolev spaces, dimension reduction, thin films.

Introduction

This paper complements an analogous analysis for thin films in the “high integrability” regime carried out in
[
2
]
. There, the behaviour of the (squared) Gagliardo
H^{s}
-seminorms

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy

in thin domains
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as their thickness
\varepsilon
tends to
0
is examined in the case
s>1/2
. In particular, it is shown that the critical scaling in order to obtain a dimensionally reduced energy (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
for problems set in integer Sobolev spaces) is
\varepsilon^{3-2s}/(1-s)
, highlighting the combined effect of the geometric scaling and the scale of relevant interactions (up to
\varepsilon
), and that the limit of the scaled energies is always a Dirichlet integral in the dimensionally reduced domain with a coefficient depending on
s
. In particular, a Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu-type result
[
1
]
is proved by letting simultaneously
s\to 1^{-}
and
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, obtaining in the limit

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})}{2d}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}\,dx^{\prime},

where the prime denotes
(d-1)
-dimensional quantities and variables. This corresponds to the energy obtained by separation of scales, letting first
s\to 1
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We will show that in the case
s<1/2
the behaviour is completely different. In order to prove that dimension reduction holds, we first follow an approach based on a “slicing” lemma in the thin direction. This approach differs from the one in
[
2
]
, which instead was based on a discretization-and-averaging argument that is not available for
s<1/2
. The slicing result allows us to estimate the integral, over
\omega
, of the one-dimensional
H^{s}
-seminorm squared in the
d
-th direction by the Gagliardo seminorm squared multiplied by
\varepsilon^{2s-1}
.

The second ingredient in our analysis is the determination of the relevant scaling for a dimensionally reduced limit. This turns out to be the scale of interactions at a finite distance, so that the contribution of
\varepsilon
to the scale of the energy is due to the integral in the vertical direction (twice). This argument allows us to conclude that, setting

F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

if a sequence of functions
(u_{\varepsilon})
satisfies
F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty
, then, upon extraction of a subsequence and possibly adding suitable translations, there exists
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that the functions
v_{\varepsilon}
defined on
\omega\times(0,1)
by

v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})

converge weakly in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
to the function
v
given by
v(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u(x^{\prime})
(we refer to this as
dimension-reduction convergence
). Once this compactness result is proved, the
\Gamma
-limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
can be computed with respect to the dimension-reduction convergence, showing that actually
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\omega)
, and that

\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\omega\times\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime},

which is nothing but the squared
H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\omega)
-seminorm, since
trivially
d+2s=d-1+2\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}+s\bigr)
.

We also analyze the case
s=s_{\varepsilon}
converging to
0
, and show that in this case as well

\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{s_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\omega\times\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime},

that is, the squared
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\omega)
-seminorm. We note that this holds also for unbounded
\omega
, and in particular for
\omega=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, showing that a Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova-type result (which would give, in the limit, the simple
L^{2}
-norm squared of
u
, see
[
5
]
) does not hold. The reason for this difference lies in the fact that the Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova result is a consequence of the non-integrability at infinity of the kernel
|\xi|^{-d-2s}
as
s\to 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This lack of integrability is not an issue in the thin-films case, for which the relevant directions at infinity are the
\xi^{\prime}
coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, for which the limit kernel
|\xi^{\prime}|^{-d}
is integrable at infinity.

We proceed in our analysis by describing the behaviour at the first scaling at which the compactness lemma no longer ensures that the limit depends only on the variable
x^{\prime}
; namely, we consider the energies

\widetilde{F}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-2s}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Since
\widetilde{F}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\varepsilon^{1+2s}F^{s}_{\varepsilon}(u)
, the
\Gamma
-limit is
0
on all functions in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
depending only on the
x^{\prime}
-variable, but it is non-trivial for functions with a genuine dependence on the
x_{d}
-variable. More precisely, we have

\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{F}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(v)=C_{s,d}\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime},

where the
\Gamma
-limit is computed with respect to the weak convergence in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
of the functions
v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
, and

C_{s,d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}}\,d\xi^{\prime}

is a
hypergeometric integral
. Finally, we show that the asymptotic scaling of the squared
H^{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
-seminorm of characteristic functions is

\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{s_{\varepsilon}}\,.

3
Dimension-reduction compactness

In this section we prove a preliminary compactness result, which allows us to highlight the relevant scaling regime of the Gagliardo
H^{s}
-seminorm on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. The following lemma partially improves a slicing result in
[
4
, Lemma 6.36]
quantifying the dependence of the scaling. Note that the hypothesis of
\omega
bounded is not necessary.

Lemma 3.1

(slicing on the “thin” direction)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
u\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
we have

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\varepsilon^{2s-1}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

(1)

where
v\colon\omega\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is the scaled function given by

v(x^{\prime},t)=u(x^{\prime},\varepsilon t)
.

Proof.

Given
x^{\prime}\in\omega
and
x_{d}\neq y_{d}\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we consider the ball
B=B(x^{\prime},x_{d},y_{d})
centered at
z^{\ast}=(x^{\prime},\frac{x_{d}+y_{d}}{2})
with radius
\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}
.
Note that, for
\varepsilon
small enough (with respect to the dimensions of
\omega
),

|B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\geq c_{\omega}|B|
with
c_{\omega}>0
by the Lipschitz assumption on
\omega
. Hence,

\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\leq\frac{2}{c_{\omega}|B|}\int_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(z)|^{2}+|u(z)-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dz.

By Fubini’s Theorem and by exchanging the role of
x_{d}
and
y_{d}
in second term of the sum, we then get

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)\frac{1}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{d+1+2s}}\,dy_{d}\,dx\,dz,

(2)

with
C>0
only depending on
c_{\omega}
and the dimension
d
.
If
\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)=1
, then
|z-z^{\ast}|\leq\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}
, and we can estimate

|x-z|\leq|z-z^{\ast}|+|x-z^{\ast}|\leq\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{4}+\frac{|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{2};

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\chi_{B\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(z)\frac{1}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{d+1+2s}}\,dy_{d}\leq 2\int_{\frac{4|x-z|}{3}}^{+\infty}t^{-d-1-2s}\,dt=\frac{2}{d+2s}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{d+2s}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d+2s}}.

From (
3
) it follows that

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{d+2s}}dx\,dz,

(3)

where
C>0
does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
s
.
Since

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{1-2s}\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v(x^{\prime},t)-v(x^{\prime},\tau)|^{2}}{|t-\tau|^{1+2s}}\,dt\,d\tau\,dx^{\prime},

the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.2

(rigidity)
.

Lemma
3.1
implies a rigidity result for “pointwise” convergence.
More precisely, given
u\colon\omega\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
and defining

u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=u(x^{\prime},\frac{x_{d}}{\varepsilon})
,
by (
1
) we obtain that

\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\varepsilon^{2s-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Then, if
\varepsilon^{2s-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=o(1)_{\varepsilon\to 0}
, the function
u
does not depend on the
d
-th variable; that is,
u(x^{\prime},x_{d})=v(x^{\prime})
.

Lemma
3.1
allows us to prove a compactness result, in the sense of the dimensional-reduction convergence defined as follows.

Definition 3.3

((weak) dimension-reduction convergence)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a sequence in
H^{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and let
v_{\varepsilon}\colon\omega\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},\varepsilon x_{d})
. We say
that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
with
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
if

v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup v
in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
and
v(x)=u(x^{\prime})
.

We start by showing a compactness result for sequences bounded in
L^{2}
.

Lemma 3.4

(dimensional-reduction compactness – i)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=0.

Then
there exists
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that, up to the addition of constants and up to subsequences,

u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

Proof.

Up to the addition of constants and up to subsequences, we have the weak convergence
v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup v
.
To show that
v
does not depend on the “thin” variable, we apply the Poincaré inequality and Lemma
3.1
, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}|v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\omega}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y_{d})|^{2}}{|x_{d}-y_{d}|^{1+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx_{d}\,dy_{d}\,dx^{\prime}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}-1}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\ \leq\ C\varepsilon^{2s_{\varepsilon}+1}=o(1)_{\varepsilon},

where
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}v_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},t)\,dt
. Since
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\overline{v}
in
L^{2}(\omega)
,
where
\overline{v}(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}v(x^{\prime},t)\,dt
, by (
4
) and the lower semicontinuity of the norm we get that the weak limit
v
does not depend on
x_{d}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(dimensional-reduction compactness – ii)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be such that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

Then
there exists
u\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that, up to addition of constants and up to subsequences,

u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

Proof.

By the Poincaré inequality (see
[
4
, Theorem 6.33]
, we have that

\|u_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

where
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx
.
Then,

\|v_{\varepsilon}-\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

which is bounded by assumption. The conclusion follows from Lemma
3.4

∎

4
Analysis at scale
\varepsilon^{2}

Lemma
3.5
suggests the scaling for a dimension reduction limit of the Gagliardo seminorms. This is confirmed by the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}<\frac{1}{2}
; then the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the weak dimension-reduction convergence in Definition
3.3
of

F_{\varepsilon}^{s}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

is given on
L^{2}(\omega)
by the
H^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{0}}
-seminorm squared on
\omega
; that is, noting that

d-1+2(\frac{1}{2}+s_{0})=d+2s_{0}
, by

F^{s}_{0}(u)=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}.

Proof.

Lower bound.

By compactness the limit is independent of
x_{d}
.
If
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
; that is,
v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,1))
, then for all fixed
\delta>0
and
s=s_{\varepsilon}\to 0

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u_{\varepsilon}\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy

The lower bound is optimized by letting
\delta\to 0
.

Upper bound.
For
u
Lipschitz, note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x-y|<2\varepsilon\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{2\varepsilon<|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x-y|<2\varepsilon\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{2\varepsilon<|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\delta\}}\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d-2+2s}}dxdy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2\varepsilon}t^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}t^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}+\delta^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}\big),

while

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{(\omega\times\omega)\cap\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|>\delta\}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy.

Hence,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq\int_{\omega\times\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}dxdy,

by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.

For
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{0}}(\omega)
the result is obtained by approximations.
∎

.

5
Dimensional-reduction regimes

As a consequence of the previous sections, we can analyze the behaviour of energies of the form

\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
s_{\varepsilon}\to s_{0}\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, and

\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)<\!<\varepsilon^{1-2s}.

Thanks to Lemma
3.4
, in all these regimes we have dimensional reduction; that is, the domain of the
\Gamma
-limit is a subset of
L^{2}(\omega)
.
In particular, from Theorem
4.1
we obtain

(i)

if
\lambda<\!<\varepsilon^{2}
, then

\Gamma\hbox{\rm-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=F_{0}(u)=\begin{cases}0&\hbox{\rm if }\ u\ \hbox{\rm is constant}\\
+\infty&\hbox{\rm otherwise; }\end{cases}

(ii)

(
\lambda\sim\varepsilon^{2}
)

\Gamma\hbox{\rm-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\omega}\int_{\omega}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d+2s_{0}}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}=\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(\omega);

(iii)

if
\varepsilon^{2}<\!<\lambda<\!<\varepsilon^{1-2s_{\varepsilon}}
, then

\Gamma\hbox{\rm-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=0.

Note that by the Brezis–Bourgain–Mironescu result applied in
\omega
we have

\Gamma\hbox{\rm-}\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}^{-}}\big(\frac{1}{2}-s\big)\lfloor u\rfloor_{s+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(\omega)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(S^{d-2})}{2(d-1)}\int_{\omega}|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}dx^{\prime},

which gives an approximate description of case (ii) as
s_{0}\to\frac{1}{2}^{-}
, and a link with the results in
[
2
]
.

Limit energies defined on full-dimensional sets

(estimate of the seminorm in the thin direction)

|u_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},x_{d})|\leq L|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|

for all

Then, with fixed

7

We have seen that by scaling by
\varepsilon^{2}

In this section, we will examine the pointwise behaviour of a suitable scaling of
\lfloor u\rfloor_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

at which the
\Gamma
-limit is

).

It is not restrictive to assume that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{s_{\varepsilon}}=\varrho\in[0,1]

Lemma 7.1

(asymptotic estimate of the seminorm of piecewise-constant functions)
.

Let

(18)

where
\varrho=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{s_{\varepsilon}}

17

Proof.

We can write the piecewise-constant function

,

\displaystyle\int_{(-\tau,0)\times(0,\varepsilon)}\int_{(k\varepsilon,\tau)\times(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\,dy

s_{\varepsilon}>\!>|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{t\in S(v)}|v(t^{+})-v(t^{-})|^{2};

(ii)

if
s_{\varepsilon}\sim|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1-\varrho^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\sum_{t\in S(v)}|v(t^{+})-v(t^{-})|^{2},

where
\varrho=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{s_{\varepsilon}}
;

(iii)

if
|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}>\!>s_{\varepsilon}>\!>\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
, then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(s_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)}\lfloor u\rfloor^{2}_{s_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=2\sum_{t\in S(v)}|v(t^{+})-v(t^{-})|^{2}.

Remark 7.4

(
d
-dimensional estimate)
.

In the case
d>2
, the analog of Lemma
7.1
gives an estimate for the asymptotic behaviour of the
s
-seminorm.
As a result, if
u\colon\omega\times(0,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
u(x^{\prime},x_{d})=v(x^{\prime})
, with

v
piecewise constant, and
\varrho=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{s_{\varepsilon}}\in[0,1]

giving a non-trivial pointwise limit as in Proposition

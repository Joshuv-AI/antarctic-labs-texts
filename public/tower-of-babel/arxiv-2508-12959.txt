Uniqueness of Skabelund Maximal Curves via Genus and Automorphism Groups Thanks: 2000 Math. Subj. Class. : Primary 05B, Secondary 14H Thanks: Keywords : Algebraic curves, Finite fields, Deligne–Lusztig curves, Weierstrass semigroup
Saeed Tafazolian

Abstract
. We present a genus–automorphism characterization of maximal Skabelund curves, showing that they are uniquely determined up to 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -isomorphism by their genus and automorphism group structure.



1.
Introduction

A smooth, projective, geometrically irreducible algebraic curve
\mathcal{X}
defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–maximal
if it attains the Hasse–Weil upper bound for the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–rational points, namely

\#\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1+2g\,q^{1/2},

where
g
is the genus of
\mathcal{X}
. Maximal curves have attracted considerable attention since the 1980s because of their applications in coding theory and cryptography, as well as their deep connections with the theory of algebraic function fields.

The Deligne–Lusztig curves associated with connected, reductive algebraic groups of type
{}_{2}A_{2}(q)
(
q
square),
{}_{2}B_{2}(2^{2s+1})
, and
{}_{2}G_{2}(3^{2s+1})
(
s\geq 1
) are, respectively, the
Hermitian
,
Suzuki
and
Ree
curves
[
3
,
6
,
13
]
. These curves are maximal over suitable field extensions and have large automorphism groups with exactly two short orbits
[
8
,
11
]
. They constitute fundamental examples in the landscape of maximal curves and serve as a basis for many constructions.

Among all maximal curves, the
Hermitian curve

H_{q}:\;y^{q+1}=x^{q}+x\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}

over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
occupies a central position: it is the unique maximal curve of largest genus over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, i.e.,
q(q-1)/2
, and it serves as a universal source of maximality, since any curve covered by a maximal curve is also maximal. This makes the Hermitian curve a fundamental building block in the theory of maximal curves.

In 2009, Giulietti and Korchmáros
[
4
]
constructed the first example of a maximal curve not covered by the Hermitian curve over the same field, the
GK–curve
, and established its uniqueness via a genus–automorphism characterization.
Inspired by the Giulietti–Korchmáros construction, Skabelund
[
14
]
introduced new maximal curves
\widetilde{S}_{q}
and
\widetilde{R}_{q}
as cyclic covers of the Suzuki and Ree curves, respectively. These curves attain maximality over suitable field extensions and can be described as normalizations of fiber products between the underlying Deligne–Lusztig curve and a cyclic cover of the projective line of degree
m=q-2q_{0}+1
in the Suzuki case, and the corresponding analogue in the Ree case.
Explicitly, in the Suzuki case with
q=2^{2s+1}
and
q_{0}=2^{s}
, Skabelund’s curve
\widetilde{S}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be described by the equations

\begin{cases}y^{q}+y=x^{q_{0}}(x^{q}+x),\\[4.0pt]
t^{m}=x^{q}+x,\end{cases}\qquad m=q-2q_{0}+1,

which define a cyclic cover of degree
m
of the Suzuki curve
S_{q}
.

Skabelund
[
14
]
showed that every automorphism of the Suzuki curve
S_{q}
lifts to an automorphism of the Skabelund curve
\widetilde{S}_{q}
. Building on this, the authors in
[
5
, Theorem 1]
proved that these lifted automorphisms constitute the entire automorphism group of
\widetilde{S}_{q}
, giving the explicit description

{\rm Aut}(\widetilde{S}_{q})\cong\mathrm{Sz}(q)\times C_{m},

where
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
is the Suzuki group and
C_{m}
is the cyclic group of order
m=q-2q_{0}+1
. In particular, this result confirms that all automorphisms of
\widetilde{S}_{q}
arise as lifts from the underlying Suzuki curve
S_{q}
. A similar result holds for the Ree curve
\widetilde{R}_{q}
(see
[
5
, Theorem 2]
).

Curves with very large automorphism groups are rare and often
rigid
, in the sense that their genus and automorphism group determine them uniquely up to isomorphism. This phenomenon is well known for the Deligne–Lusztig curves
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
and for the GK–curve
[
4
]
(see also
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
). In this paper, we extend this rigidity to the Skabelund maximal curves. While our proof strategy follows the general approach of Korchmáros and Giulietti for the GK–curve, new challenges arise due to the distinct group structure and short–orbit configuration of the Suzuki and Ree covers.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q=2^{2s+1}
,
q_{0}=2^{s}
, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a projective, nonsingular, maximal algebraic curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
of genus

g=\frac{1}{2}\,(q^{3}-2q^{2}+q).

Suppose
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathrm{Sz}(q)\times C_{m}
, where
m=q-2q_{0}+1
, acting on
\mathcal{X}
with the same short–orbit structure as
{\rm Aut}(\widetilde{S}_{q})
. Then
\mathcal{X}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
–isomorphic to the Skabelund curve
\widetilde{S}_{q}
.

The proof relies on the Riemann–Hurwitz formula applied to the quotient by the automorphism group, combined with a careful analysis of the ramification behavior and short orbits inherited from the Suzuki curve. This yields a genus–automorphism uniqueness criterion for Skabelund’s maximal curves, complementing known results for the GK–curve and the classical Deligne–Lusztig curves.

Notation and terminology are standard. Our principal references is
[
9
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we provide a detailed proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Our proof strategy is inspired by the approach used for the GK–curve in
[
4
]
, suitably adapted to accommodate the distinct group structure and short–orbit configuration of the Suzuki covers.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth projective curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
, where
Q
is a square (e.g.
Q=q^{4}
in the Suzuki–cover case), and assume that
\mathcal{X}
is
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
-maximal. Let
p=\mathrm{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{Q}
and let
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
be a subgroup defined over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
. Then every Sylow
p
-subgroup
S_{p}
of
G
fixes a point
P\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})
. Moreover, every nontrivial element
\alpha\in S_{p}
fixes no point other than
P
.

Proof.

Since
G
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
, it preserves the set
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})
of
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
-rational points. As
\mathcal{X}
is
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
-maximal, we have

\#\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})\;=\;Q+1+2g\,Q^{1/2}.

Reducing modulo
p
and using
p\mid Q
and
p\mid Q^{1/2}
, we get the congruence

\#\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Therefore, by the class equation for the action of a Sylow
p
–subgroup
S_{p}
on the finite set
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})
, there must exist at least one fixed point
P\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{Q})
of
S_{p}
.

We claim that every nontrivial
\alpha\in S_{p}
fixes only
P
. It suffices to prove the assertion for
\alpha
of order
p
. Let
k\geq 1
be the number of fixed points of such an
\alpha
on
\mathcal{X}
. Since
\mathcal{X}
is maximal over the square field
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
, its
p
–rank is
0
(i.e.
\gamma(\mathcal{X})=0
). Applying the Deuring–Shafarevich formula to the cyclic group
\langle\alpha\rangle
of order
p
(see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 11.62]
), we obtain

\gamma(\mathcal{X})-1\;=\;p\bigl(\gamma(\mathcal{X}/\langle\alpha\rangle)-1\bigr)\;+\;\sum_{i\geq 0}\bigl(p-\gamma_{i}\bigr).

where
\gamma_{i}
’s are the sizes of the short orbits of
G
on the points of
\mathcal{X}
. For an order-
p
automorphism, with
k
fixed point, using
\gamma(\mathcal{X})=0
, we get

-1\;=\;p\bigl(\gamma(\mathcal{X}/\langle\alpha\rangle)-1\bigr)\;+\;k(p-1).

Since
\gamma(\mathcal{X}/\langle\alpha\rangle)=0
, the only possibility is
k=1
. Thus
\alpha
fixes exactly one point of
\mathcal{X}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

The following result is standard; for completeness, we include a proof here.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=\mathrm{Sz}(q)
with
q=2^{2n+1}
for some integer
n\geq 1
. Then the number of Sylow
2
-subgroups of
G
is
q^{2}+1
.

Proof.

The Suzuki group
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
is a family of finite simple groups with
q=2^{2n+1}
for
n\geq 1
. Its order is

|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|=q^{2}(q^{2}+1)(q-1).

Let
P
be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
. Then the order of
P
is

|P|=q^{2}.

By Sylow’s theorems, the number of Sylow
2
-subgroups of
G
is given by

n_{2}=\frac{|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|}{|N(P)|},

where
N(P)
denotes the normalizer of
P
in
G
.

It is a known result that

|N(P)|=q^{2}(q-1).

Therefore, we have

n_{2}=\frac{q^{2}(q^{2}+1)(q-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}=q^{2}+1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be the set of points of
\mathcal{X}
fixed by some Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
. Then
|\Omega|=q^{2}+1
. Furthermore, let
C\cong C_{m}
be the cyclic normal subgroup of
G
of order
m=q-2q_{0}+1
. Then:

(1)

C
commutes with all
2
-elements of
G
and therefore fixes every point in
\Omega
;

(2)

If
\bar{\mathcal{X}}:=\mathcal{X}/C
and
\bar{G}:=G/C
, then
\bar{G}\cong\mathrm{Sz}(q)
acts as a group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-automorphisms on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, each Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
fixes exactly one point of
\mathcal{X}
, and distinct Sylow
2
-subgroups have disjoint fixed–point sets. This correspondence establishes a bijection between the set
\Omega
of fixed points and the set of Sylow
2
–subgroups of
G
, since the stabilizer of a point in the automorphism group of any curve in characteristic
p
contains a unique Sylow
p
-subgroup.

As
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
has exactly
q^{2}+1
Sylow
2
-subgroups, it follows that

|\Omega|=q^{2}+1.

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\bar{g}
be the genus of
\bar{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{X}/C
. Then
\bar{g}=q_{0}(q-1)
and
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphic to the Suzuki curve
S_{q}
. In particular, the image
\bar{\Omega}
of
\Omega
in
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
is an orbit of
\bar{G}\cong\mathrm{Sz}(q)
of size
q^{2}+1
, namely the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points of
S_{q}
.

Proof.

Apply the Hurwitz genus formula
[
15
, Corollary 3.5.6]
to the cyclic cover
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\bar{\mathcal{X}}
of degree
m
:

2g-2\;\geq\;m(2\bar{g}-2)\;+\;\sum_{Q\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{P\mid Q}(e_{P}-1).

By Lemma
2.3
,
C
fixes each point of
\Omega
and, as will follow from equality below, acts freely outside
\Omega
. Thus
e_{P}=m
for
P\in\Omega
and
e_{P}=1
otherwise, so
\sum_{Q\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\sum_{P\mid Q}(e_{P}-1)=(q^{2}+1)(m-1)
. Using
g=\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{3}-2q^{2}+q)
and
m=q-2q_{0}+1
we obtain

2g-2-(q^{2}+1)(m-1)\;\geq\;m\,(2\bar{g}-2).

A simple calculation gives
q_{0}(q-1)\geq\bar{g}
.

Now
|\bar{G}|=|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|=q^{2}(q^{2}+1)(q-1)
and
\bar{g}\geq 2
(the Sylow-
2
stabilizer at a point is non-abelian of order
q^{2}
, so
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
is neither rational nor elliptic; cf.
[
9
, Thm. 11.94]
). By known characterizations of curves with very large automorphism groups (see, e.g., Henn’s and Stichtenoth’s theorems), the only curve of genus
q_{0}(q-1)
admitting an
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
-action of this size is the Suzuki curve
S_{q}
. Thus
\bar{\mathcal{X}}\cong S_{q}
and the
\bar{G}
-orbit
\bar{\Omega}
of size
q^{2}+1
coincides with
S_{q}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the unique non-tame orbit (the only orbit whose size is not divisible by
p
).
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Equality in the Hurwitz formula above shows that the cover
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\bar{\mathcal{X}}
is totally ramified over each point of
\bar{\Omega}
and unramified elsewhere. Consequently,
\Omega
is precisely the fixed–point set of
C
, and
\bar{\Omega}
is a single
\bar{G}
-orbit of size
q^{2}+1
.

Proposition 2.6
.

There exist functions
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
satisfying the affine Suzuki equation

y^{q}+y\;=\;x^{q_{0}}\bigl(x^{q}+x\bigr),

and having a unique common pole at a point
\bar{P}_{\infty}\in\bar{\Omega}
. Then the Weierstrass semigroup of
\bar{P}_{\infty}
is

H(\bar{P}_{\infty})=\big\langle q,\;q+q_{0},\;q+2q_{0},\;q+2q_{0}+1\big\rangle.

The pole divisors are

(x)_{\infty}=q\,\bar{P}_{\infty},\qquad(y)_{\infty}=(q+q_{0})\,\bar{P}_{\infty},

and all zeros of
x
and
y
belong to
\bar{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Uniqueness of the pole follows from the fact that
\bar{\Omega}
is the unique non-tame orbit (so there is a single point of
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
with the required pole behaviour in the chosen model). Finally, as in the classical argument, the Frobenius image
x^{(q)}
has the same poles and zeros as
x
, hence
x^{(q)}=cx
for some
c\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{\times}
. Replacing
x
by a suitable scalar multiple we may assume
x^{(q)}=x
, and the same trick applies to
y
, so
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
.
The Weierstrass semigroup of the rational points of the Suzuki curve was computed in
[
10
]
.

∎

2.1.
Reconstruction of the cyclic cover

We retain the notation of Propositions
2.4
–
2.6
.
In particular
\bar{\mathcal{X}}\cong S_{q}
and
\bar{\Omega}=S_{q}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the unique
\bar{G}
-orbit of size
q^{2}+1
. The cyclic group

C\cong C_{m}
acts on
\mathcal{X}
with the property that the quotient map

\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\bar{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{X}/C

is totally ramified precisely over the points of
\bar{\Omega}
and is unramified elsewhere (Corollary
2.5
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
P_{\infty}
be the unique point of
\mathcal{X}
lying above
\overline{P}_{\infty}
. Then the Weierstrass semigroup of
P_{\infty}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is

H(P_{\infty})=\big\langle q^{2}-2q_{0}q+q,\;q^{2}-q_{0}q+q_{0},\;q^{2}-q+2q_{0},\;q^{2},\;q^{2}+1\big\rangle.

Proof.

Consider the following generators of the Weierstrass semigroup of
\overline{P}_{\infty}
on
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
:

(x)_{\infty}=q\,\overline{P}_{\infty},\quad(y)_{\infty}=(q+q_{0})\,\overline{P}_{\infty},\quad(v)_{\infty}=\left(q+\frac{q}{q_{0}}\right)\overline{P}_{\infty},\quad(w)_{\infty}=\left(q+\frac{q}{q_{0}}+1\right)\overline{P}_{\infty}.

These functions lift naturally to the curve
\mathcal{X}
, providing corresponding generators for the Weierstrass semigroup of
P_{\infty}
on
\mathcal{X}
. Explicitly, we obtain

(x)_{\infty}=(q^{2}-2q_{0}q+q)P_{\infty},\quad(y)_{\infty}=(q^{2}-qq_{0}+q_{0})P_{\infty},

(v)_{\infty}=(q^{2}-q+2q_{0})P_{\infty},\quad(w)_{\infty}=(q^{2}+1)P_{\infty}.

Moreover, since the curve
\mathcal{X}
is maximal over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
, the number
q^{2}
is a pole number at any rational point.

Finally, the result follows from
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
, which determines the genus of this semigroup.
∎

2.2.
Conclusion of the Uniqueness Argument

Let
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}(\mathcal{X})
be a function with pole divisor

(z)_{\infty}=q^{2}P_{\infty}.

Denote by
\beta
a generator of the cyclic group
C_{m}
, where
m=q^{2}-2q_{0}+1
.
Since
x,y,v\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}
, they are fixed by
\beta
, that is,

\beta(x)=x,\qquad\beta(y)=y,\qquad\beta(v)=v.

By the description of the Weierstrass semigroup at
P_{\infty}
, there exist
\sigma,\eta,\gamma,\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
and an
m
-th root of unity
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
such that

\beta(z)=\lambda z+\sigma x+\eta y+\gamma w+\epsilon.

If
\lambda=1
, then
\beta(z)=z
, which forces
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
.
This is impossible since
(z)_{\infty}=q^{2}P_{\infty}
.
Thus
\lambda\neq 1
.
Introduce

t:=z+\frac{\sigma}{\lambda-1}x+\frac{\eta}{\lambda-1}y+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda-1}w+\epsilon,

so that

\beta(t)=\lambda t,\qquad(t)_{\infty}=q^{2}P_{\infty}.

Because
\gcd(q^{2},m)=1
, the zero divisor of
t
is supported entirely on
\Omega
.
Consequently,

t^{\,m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}),\qquad\bigl(t^{\,m}\bigr)_{\infty}=q^{2}\bar{P}_{\infty}.

It follows that

\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}(\mathcal{X})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})(t),

and the extension is Kummer with group
C_{m}

[
15
, Proposition 3.7.3]
.
Therefore, the branch points of
\mathcal{X}\to\bar{\mathcal{X}}
are precisely the zeros and poles of
t^{m}
, showing that
\bar{\Omega}
coincides with this set.

Since
\bigl(t^{m}\bigr)_{\infty}=q^{2}\bar{P}_{\infty}
, every point in
\bar{\Omega}\setminus\{\bar{P}_{\infty}\}
appears as a simple zero of
t^{m}
.
But
x^{q}-x
has exactly the same divisor of zeros and poles, with the same multiplicities.
Hence, after a suitable normalization of
t
, one obtains

t^{m}=x^{q}-x.

This identifies

\mathcal{X}\cong_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}}\widetilde{S}_{q},

thereby completing the proof of the uniqueness part of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 2.8
.

In analogy to the Suzuki case treated in this paper, Skabelund also constructed a family of maximal curves
\widetilde{R}_{q}
as cyclic covers of the Ree curve
R_{q}
(see
[
5
, Theorem 2]
). These curves can be described explicitly as normalizations of certain fiber products involving
R_{q}
and a cyclic Kummer extension of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Their complete automorphism group is given by

\operatorname{Aut}(\widetilde{R}_{q})\;\cong\;\mathrm{Ree}(q)\times C_{m},

where
\mathrm{Ree}(q)
denotes the Ree group and
C_{m}
is a cyclic group of order
m
depending on
q
.

Given the structural similarities between the Suzuki and Ree constructions-both yielding maximal curves whose automorphism groups are direct products of the underlying Deligne–Lusztig group with a cyclic factor, it is natural to expect that a genus-automorphism characterization analogous to Theorem
1.1
should also hold for
\widetilde{R}_{q}
. Establishing this uniqueness for the Ree–type Skabelund curves will be the subject of future investigation.

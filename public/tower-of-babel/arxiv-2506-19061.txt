Rational Exponents for General Graphs
Sean English, Sam Spiro

Abstract
A rational number r r is a realizable exponent for a graph H H if there exists a finite family of graphs ℱ \mathcal{F} such that ex ⁡ ( n , H , ℱ ) = Θ ⁡ ( n r ) \mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{r}) , where ex ⁡ ( n , H , ℱ ) \mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}) denotes the maximum number of copies of H H that an n n -vertex ℱ \mathcal{F} -free graph can have. Results for realizable exponents are currently known only when H H is either a star or a clique, with the full resolution of the H = K 2 H=K_{2} case being a major breakthrough of Bukh and Conlon. In this paper, we establish the first set of results for realizable exponents which hold for arbitrary graphs H H by showing that for any graph H H with maximum degree Δ ≥ 1 \Delta\geq 1 , every rational in the interval [ v ⁡ ( H ) − e ⁡ ( H ) 2 ​ Δ 2 , v ⁡ ( H ) ] \left[v(H)-\frac{e(H)}{2\Delta^{2}},\ v(H)\right] is realizable for H H . We also prove a “stability” result for generalized Turán numbers of trees which implies that if T ≠ K 2 T\neq K_{2} is a tree with ℓ \ell leaves, then T T has no realizable exponents in [ 0 , ℓ ] ∖ ℤ [0,\ell]\setminus\mathbb{Z} . Our proof of this latter result uses a new variant of the classical Helly theorem for trees, which may be of independent interest.



1
Introduction

Given a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, the
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is defined to be the maximum number of an edges that an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free graph can have. When
\mathcal{F}
consists of a single graph
F
we denote its Turán number simply by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
. As stated
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is not well-defined if
\mathcal{F}
contains an empty graph on at most
n
vertices; in such cases we adopt the convention that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=0
.

The behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is well-understood whenever
\mathcal{F}
contains only non-bipartite graphs due to the famed Erdős-Stone-Simonovits Theorem
[
10
]
, but the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
when
\mathcal{F}
contains a bipartite graph is far from clear despite decades of active research into the problem, and we refer the reader to the survey
[
13
]
for a more thorough summary of what is known in the area.

In an attempt to better understand the possible behaviors of
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
, Erdős and Simonovits made a conjecture, now commonly known as the rational exponents conjecture, which states that for every rational
r\in[1,2]
there exists a graph
F
such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\Theta(n^{r})
. A positive answer to a slight weakening of this conjecture was given in breakthrough work of Bukh and Conlon
[
4
]
where the following was proven.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For every rational
r\in[1,2]
there exists a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{r})
.

Theorem

1.1
is a highly influential result, with it serving as the catalyst for a large body of literature dedicated towards proving special cases of the rational exponents conjecture
[
21
]
. It is also worth mentioning that
Theorem

1.1
was proven using the powerful random polynomial method introduced by Bukh
[
3
]
, with this method having since been further developed to solve a large number of problems in combinatorics, see e.g.
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
19
,
24
]
.

We aim to study a generalization of the rational exponents conjecture to the generalized Turán setting, a problem which was initiated in recent work of English, Halfpap, and Krueger
[
9
]
. To this end, given a graph
H
and a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, we define the
generalized Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})
to be the maximum number of copies of
H
that can appear in an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free graph. The systematic study of generalized Turán numbers was initiated by Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
, and since then there has been a large number of results dedicated to the topic, see e.g.
[
15
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
25
]
. For more on the history of generalized Turán numbers we refer the interested reader to the excellent recent survey by Gerbner and Palmer
[
16
]
.

Motivated by
Theorem

1.1
, we consider the following.

Definition 1
.

We say that a rational number
r\in[0,v(H)]
is a
realizable exponent
(or simply
realizable
) for a graph
H
if there exists a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
satisfying
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{r})
. We say that
r
is
non-realizable
for
H
if it is not a realizable exponent.

As we will see later on, every graph
H
without isolated vertices has
0
as a realizable exponent and no rational in
(0,1)
being realizable, so the central problem will be in studying the range
r\in[1,v(H)]
.

Note that
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2},\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
, so
Theorem

1.1
is equivalent to saying that every rational in
[1,2]
is realizable for
H=K_{2}
, showing that every rational which could be realizable for
K_{2}
is realizable. It is natural to ask to what extent this holds for other
H
, and in particular what happens for families of
H
which contain
K_{2}
as a member. For example, one might ask what happens when
H
is a clique, with this case being solved in full by English, Halfpap, and Krueger
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

For all
t\geq 2
, every rational in
[1,t]
is realizable for
H=K_{t}
.

Thus as we saw for
K_{2}
, every rational which could be realizable for cliques is realizable. In view of this, one might be led to guess that
[1,v(H)]
should be realizable for every graphs. Perhaps surprisingly, this phenomenon does not in fact hold, and it in particular fails for all stars larger than
K_{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
t\geq 2
, every rational in
[t-1,t]
is realizable for
H=K_{1,t-1}
, but no rational in
(1,t-1)
is realizable.

2
Main Results

In this paper, we study realizable exponents for arbitrary graphs, proving several general results about both realizable and non-realizable exponents, with us significantly improving upon these results whenever
H
is a forest. Moreover, our tools used to give these improvements for forests can be used to give a number of other results on generalized Turán numbers of trees which are of independent interest.

2.1
Realizable Exponents

Our first main result shows that for
any
graph
H
, every sufficiently large rational is realizable for
H
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
H
is a graph with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 1
, then every rational number in the interval

\left[v(H)-\frac{e(H)}{2\Delta^{2}},\ v(H)\right]

is realizable for
H
.

A more careful analysis of our methods can be used to establish an even stronger result for trees.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
T
is a tree with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 1
, then every rational number in the interval

\left[v(T)-\frac{e(T)}{\Delta},\ v(T)\right]

is realizable for
T
.

The length of the interval in
Theorem

2.2
is best possible for trees in general, as
Theorem

1.3
shows that one can not improve this for stars.

2.2
Non-Realizable Exponents

Our main contribution to the study of non-realizable exponents is the establishment of a technical result,
Proposition

5.1
, which, at present, is the only tool we know of that can prove the non-existence of realizable exponents. We postpone stating this result due to its technical nature and instead focus on some of its consequences, starting with the following non-realizable exponents of arbitrary graphs.

Proposition 2.3
.

No rational in
(0,1)
is realizable for any graph
H
. Moreover,
0
is realizable for every graph
H
without isolated vertices, and
1
is realizable for every connected graph
H
.

The condition that
H
is connected for
1
to be realizable is necessary in general, with the simplest example being any
H
an independent set of size at least 2.

Observe by
Theorem

1.2
that the only rationals which are non-realizable for cliques lie in
(0,1)
, so
Proposition

2.3
is best possible for arbitrary graphs. On the other hand, the set of realizable exponents for stars detailed in
Theorem

1.3
suggests that we might be able to say more about non-realizable exponents of trees. And indeed, in this setting we have the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
T\neq K_{2}
is a tree with
\ell\geq 2
leaves, then no rational in
[0,\ell]\setminus\mathbb{Z}
is realizable for
T
.

Theorem

1.3
shows that the interval
[0,\ell]
is best possible for stars. We believe it is possible to push our methods further to completely characterize the set of realizable exponents for every tree in the range
[0,\ell]
, but due to its additional complexity, we defer this problem to future work.

2.3
Generalized Turán Numbers of Trees

The methods we use here give several general results regarding the generalized Turán number for trees. The only prior results about counting copies of arbitrary trees
T
that we are aware of are results of Gerbner
[
14
]
who determined the order of magnitude of
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})
for all trees
T
and a result of Cambie, de Joannis de Verclos, and Kang
[
6
]
who observed the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{1,t})
. The only specific choice of
T
for which
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})
seems to be well understood in general is when
T
is a star, in which case general results of Füredi and Kündgen
[
12
]
gives an essentially optimal bound on
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{1,t},\mathcal{F})
based on the classical Turán number
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
. In a similar spirit, the following result gives an effective relationship between
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})
and
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
for arbitrary trees.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
T\neq K_{1}
is a tree and if
\mathcal{F}
is a family of graphs, then for every integer
k\geq 1
either

\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=\Omega_{T,k}(n^{k}),

or

\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=O_{T,k,\mathcal{F}}(\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})^{k-1}).

In fact, we prove a slightly stronger technical version of this statement in
Theorem

5.3
which characterizes when the lower bound or upper bound applies to a given family
\mathcal{F}
. We note that the statement of
Theorem

2.5
does not typically hold if one replaces the tree
T
with some other type of graph
H
. In particular, such an extension fails for
H=C_{4}
; see the discussion at the end of Section
6
for more.

We will show that
Theorem

2.5
can be used to quickly imply the following, which in essence states that the generalized Turán problem for trees vs forests always has a very simple structure.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
T\neq K_{1}
is a tree and if
\mathcal{F}
is a family of graphs which contains a forest, then either
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{k})
for some integer
k\leq\alpha(T)
or
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=0
for all sufficiently large
n
.

This in turn implies the following “stability” result for generalized Turán numbers of trees, which informally says that either
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})
is large or
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})
takes on a very particular form. As far as we are aware this is the first such “stability” result of its kind for generalized Turán numbers.

Corollary 2.7
.

If
T\neq K_{2}
is a tree with
\ell\geq 2
leaves and if
\mathcal{F}
is a family of graphs such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=O(n^{\ell})
, then either
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{k})
for some integer
k
or
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,F)=0
for all sufficiently large
n
.

Observe that
Theorem

2.4
immediately follows from
Corollary

2.7
.

3
Preliminaries

In this section we gather our necessary tools and definitions. We will begin by shifting our perspective away from trying to count copies of
H
and instead counting the following closely related concept.

Definition 2
.

Given graphs
H,G
, we say that a map
\phi:V(H)\to V(G)
is a
monomorphism
of
H
into
G
if
\phi
is both injective and a homomorphism. We let
\mathrm{Mon}(H,G)
denote the set of monomorphisms of
H
into
G
and we let
\mathrm{mon}(H,G):=|\mathrm{Mon}(H,G)|
.

The main motivation for this definition is the following.

Observation 3.1
.

For every pair of graphs
H,G
, the number of copies of
H
in
G
equals

\frac{\mathrm{mon}(H,G)}{\mathrm{aut}(H)},

where
\mathrm{aut}(H)
denotes the size of the automorphism group of
H
, i.e. the number of isomorphisms from
H
to itself.

Indeed, this follows from the fact that a copy of
H
in
G
is just a subgraph of
G
which is isomorphic to
H
, and the exactly
\mathrm{aut}(H)
choices of isomorphisms for each copy gives rise to exactly
\mathrm{aut}(H)
monomorphisms corresponding uniquely to this copy. Because of this observation, we will often bound the number of copies of a graph
H
by instead bounding
\mathrm{mon}(H,G)
.

We next introduce the notion of rooted graphs which will be crucial to our study of realizable exponents. While these graphs will not show up explicitly in our work on non-realizable exponents, they will turn out to be quite related to the objects needed for our main non-realizable result
Proposition

5.1
.

Definition 3
.

Given a graph
F
and set of vertices
R\subsetneq V(F)
, we say that the pair
(F,R)
is a
rooted graph
and that
R
is its set of
roots
. We define the
rooted density
of a rooted graph to be

\max_{S\subseteq V(F)\setminus R,\ S\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mathrm{inc}(S)}{|S|},

where
\mathrm{inc}(S)
is defined to be the number of edges of
F
incident to a vertex of
F
. We say that
(F,R)
is
balanced
if it has rooted density equal to
\frac{e(F)}{v(F)-|R|}
.

Given a rooted graph
(F,R)
and a positive integer
q
, we define the
rooted power

(F,R)^{q}
to be the family of graphs
F^{\prime}
which can be written as the union of
q
distinct copies of
F
which all agree on the set
R
.

The fundamental fact we need about these definitions is that there exists graphs which contain many copies of
H
while avoiding rooted powers. This was initially proven for
H=K_{2}
in a highly influential result of Bukh and Conlon
[
4
]
with this later being extended in
[
23
]
to general
H
.

Theorem 3.2

(Proposition 2.1
[
23
]
)
.

If
H
is a graph and if
(F_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(F_{t},R_{t})
are rooted graphs which all have rooted density at least
d
for some rational
d
, then there exists some integer
q_{0}
such that for all integers
q\geq q_{0}
, we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\bigcup_{i}(F_{i},R_{i})^{q})=\Omega(n^{v(H)-e(H)/d}).

Theorem

3.2
is most effective when at least one of the rooted graphs is balanced. In order to build such balanced rooted graphs, we will rely on the following fact proven by Bukh and Conlon that balanced rooted trees exist for all densities.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 1.3
[
4
]
)
.

For all positive integers
a,b
with
b\geq a-1
, there exists a balanced rooted tree
(T,R_{T})
of rooted density
b/a
which has
a
unrooted vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{a}
forming a path in
T
with
\deg_{T}(u_{i})\geq b/a
for all
i
.

We note that the condition
\deg_{T}(u_{i})\geq b/a
is not immediate from the definition of
(T,R_{T})
given in
[
4
, Definition 1.5]
, but this condition follows from the fact that these rooted trees are balanced with rooted density
b/a
.

We will often be counting copies of
H
in some larger host graph
G
. To distinguish between vertices in
H
and copies of these vertices contained in a subgraph of
G
isomorphic to
H
, we will often write
\hat{x},\hat{y}
, etc. for vertices that are in
H
with us omitting the “hat” notation for vertices in
G
(usually with
x\in V(G)
playing the role of
\hat{x}\in V(H)
).

4
Realizable Exponents

We make use of the following technical definition to show the existence of realizable exponents.

Definition 4
.

We say that a graph
H
is
d
-admissible
for some rational
d
if there exists a finite set of rooted graphs
\{(F_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(F_{t},R_{t})\}
which each have rooted density at least
d
such that for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\bigcup_{i}(F_{i},R_{i})^{q})=O_{q}(n^{v(H)-e(H)/d}).

In this case we say
H
is
d
-admissible with respect to the set
\{(F_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(F_{t},R_{t})\}
.

The key observation with this definition is the following.

Observation 4.1
.

If
H
is
d
-admissible, then the rational
v(H)-e(H)/d
is realizable for
H
.

Indeed, this follows by taking
\mathcal{F}_{q}
to be the family of
q
-th powers of the rooted graphs
(F_{i},R_{i})
for which
H
is
d
-admissible with respect to, as such a family satisfies
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}_{q})=O_{q}(n^{v(H)-e(H)/d})
by definition, and for
q
large enough it also satisfies
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}_{q})=\Omega(n^{v(H)-e(H)/d})
by
Theorem

3.2
.

With
4.1
in mind, proving realizability reduces to proving admissibility. The main advantage of this perspective is that the notion of admissibility turns out to be quite malleable for inductively building larger admissible graphs from smaller ones. For example, we have the following basic fact.

Observation 4.2
.

If
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are
d
-admissible, then their disjoint union
H_{1}\sqcup H_{2}
is also
d
-admissible.

Indeed, this follows by taking the union of the two sets of rooted graphs for which
H_{1},H_{2}
are
d
-admissible with respect to.

With
4.2
, it suffices to determine admissibility of connected graphs. In the original work of Bukh and Conlon for
H=K_{2}
, this was done by establishing
Lemma

3.3
showing the existence of certain balanced rooted trees. In a similar spirit, we will construct balanced rooted graphs which are “tree-like” with respect to
H
.

Definition 5
.

Given a graph
H
, we say that a graph
F
is an
H
-tree
if there exist distinct copies
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
of
H
in
F
together with isomorphisms
h_{i}:V(H)\to V(H_{i})
for all
i
such that the following conditions hold:

(A)

We have
F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}H_{i}
, and

(B)

For all
i\geq 2
, there exists an integer
1\leq j_{i}<i
such that

V(H_{i})\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}V(H_{j})=V(H_{i})\cap V(H_{j_{i}}),

and such that every
v\in V(H_{i})\cap V(H_{j_{i}})
satisfies
h^{-1}_{i}(v)=h^{-1}_{j_{i}}(v)
.

The set
\{H_{1}.\ldots,H_{t}\}
will be called a set of
defining copies
of
F
. The sequence
(h_{1},j_{2},h_{2},j_{3},\dots,h_{t})
will be called a
witness
for
F
. We say that a rooted graph
(F,R)
is a
rooted
H
-tree
if
F
is an
H
-tree.

We emphasize that the definition of an
H
-tree theoretically allows for
F
to be disconnected, but in practice we only ever needs to use connected
F
, hence our naming this an
H
-tree rather than an
H
-forest.

We can now state our main result for
d
-admissible graphs for
d
large. For this statement, we say that
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
is a
proper induced subgraph
of
H
if
H^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph with
H^{\prime}\neq H
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
H
be a connected graph and
d\geq\Delta(H)
rational. If there exists a balanced rooted
H
-tree
(F,R)
of rooted density
d
such that every proper induced subgraph of
H
and every component of
F[R]
is
d
-admissible, then
H
is
d
-admissible.

We emphasize that
Theorem

4.3
reduces the a priori complicated problem of showing that a given (large) rational
v(H)-e(H)/d
is realizable to the purely combinatorial problem of constructing some fixed rooted graph
(F,R)
with a few special properties.
Theorem

4.3
in fact holds even without the assumption that
H
is connected, but there is no need to consider disconnected graphs due to
4.2
.

We prove
Theorem

4.3
in Subsection
4.1
, after which we use
Theorem

4.3
in Subsection
4.2
to prove our main results for realizable exponents. These two subsections are self-contained and can be read independently of each other. In what follows we will make use of the following notation.

Definition 6
.

Given a graph
H
, we let
2^{H}
denote the set of induced subgraphs of
H
.

In particular, this means
2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
is shorthand for the set of proper induced subgraphs of
H
.

We close with a simple observation that will be the base case of most of our inductive arguments.

Observation 4.4
.

The graph
K_{1}
is
d
-admissible for all rationals
d>0
.

Indeed, any non-empty rooted graph
(F,R)
of rooted density at least
d
satisfies
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{1},(F,R)^{q})=n=O(n^{v(K_{1})-e(K_{1})/d})
.

4.1
Proof of
Theorem

4.3

Let us start by discussing the high level proof ideas. Ultimately, we will show that the graphs
H
as in
Theorem

4.3
are
d
-admissible with respect to a family of rooted graphs of the form
(F,R)\cup\mathcal{F}
where
(F,R)
is a suitably chosen rooted
H
-tree and
\mathcal{F}
is a set of rooted graphs for which every proper induced subgraph of
H
and every component of
F[R]
is
d
-admissible with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. To do this, we will show that if
G
is a graph which avoids all
q
-th powers of graphs in
\mathcal{F}
and has many copies of
H
, then
G
contains a
q
-th power of
(F,R)
, implying that graphs avoiding all
q
-th powers of graphs in
(F,R)\cup\mathcal{F}
can not contain many copies of
H
, proving that
H
is
d
-admissible with respect to this family.

We use a pigeonhole argument to show that graphs
G
as above contain an element of
(F,R)^{q}
. Specifically, we show that
G
both contains few copies of
F[R]
while also containing many copies of
F
, which means some
q
of these copies of
F
must agree on
R
. Showing that the number of copies of
F[R]
is small follows almost immediately from
G
avoiding all
q
-th powers of graphs in
\mathcal{F}
and the fact that each component of
F[R]
is
d
-admissible with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. The bulk of the work then lies in showing that
G
contains many copies of the
H
-tree
F
. In order to do this, we use the fact that
G
has many copies of
H
to show that there exists a large collection of “nice” monomorphisms
\Phi
of
H
into
G
which we refer to as a strong builder. This strong builder is defined in such a way that we can glue together monomorphisms in
\Phi
in many ways to form a copy of our
H
-tree
F
, with this process being analogous to the standard way one shows that
n
-vertex graphs with minimum degree
\delta
contain at least
\Omega(\delta^{e}n)
copies of any fixed tree with
e
edges. The framework we use to do this is a generalization of the framework developed in
[
9
]
for cliques.

With the above all in mind, our first goal will be to show that “strong builders” as described above exist in the graphs
G
that we care about, after which we show that such strong builders do indeed correspond to the existence of many copies of
F
. We begin with some definitions.

Definition 7
.

Let
H
and
G
be graphs and let
\Phi
be a collection of monomorphisms from
H
to
G
. Given an induced subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
, we write
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
to denote the collection
\{\phi|_{V(H^{\prime})}:\phi\in\Phi\}
, i.e.
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
is the set of restrictions of maps in
\Phi
to
H^{\prime}
. Given
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
, the
degree
of
\psi
, denoted
\deg_{\Phi}(\psi)
, is the number of
\phi\in\Phi
such that
\phi|_{V(H^{\prime})}=\psi
. We will denote the degree
\deg_{\Phi}(\phi)
simply by
\deg(\phi)
whenever
\Phi
is clear from context. Given a set
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
\Phi-S
denote the subset of
\Phi
containing the monomorphisms
\phi
with
\phi(V(H))\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
.

Definition 8
.

Let
H
be a graph and
\beta:2^{H}\setminus\{H\}\to\mathbb{R}
a real-valued function on the proper induced subgraphs of
H
. We say that a collection of monomorphisms
\Phi
from
H
to a graph
G
is a
(H,\beta)
-weak builder
if for every
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
and every
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
, we have
\deg(\psi)\geq\beta(H^{\prime})
. Given
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
\Phi
is a
(H,\beta,s)
-strong builder
if both
\Phi-S
is a
(H,\beta)
-weak builder and
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S\subseteq(\Phi-S)_{H^{\prime}}
for all
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|\leq s
and
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}
(i.e. if every
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S
, has at least one
\phi\in\Phi-S
with
\phi|_{V(H)}=\psi
).

Intuitively,
\Phi
is a strong builder if there exist many ways of “extending” the restriction of any map
\phi\in\Phi
even if there is a small set of vertices
S
that these extensions are required to avoid. We begin by showing that if there exists a large and “well-controlled” collection of monomorphisms
\Phi
, then this can be trimmed into a slightly smaller collection with a
(H,\beta)
-weak builder for some appropriate choice of
\beta
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
H
and
G
be graphs with
v(G)=n
, let
p
be a real number, and let
\Phi
be a collection of monomorphisms from
H
to
G
. If

•

|\Phi|\geq 2^{v(H)+1}n^{v(H)}p^{e(H)}
, and

•

|\Phi_{H^{\prime}}|\leq n^{v(H^{\prime})}p^{e(H^{\prime})}
for all
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
,

then there exists a subcollection
\Phi^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi
such that

•

|\Phi^{\prime}|\geq n^{v(H)}p^{e(H)}
, and

•

For each
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
and
\psi\in\Phi^{\prime}_{H^{\prime}}
, we have
\deg_{\Phi^{\prime}}(\psi)\geq 2n^{v(H)-v(H^{\prime})}p^{e(H)-e(H^{\prime})}
.

Proof.

Iteratively, as long as there exists some
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
and
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
satisfying
\deg_{\Phi}(\psi)<2p^{e(H)-e(H^{\prime})}n^{v(H)-v(H^{\prime})}
, we remove from
\Phi
any monomorphisms
\phi
with
\phi|_{V(H^{\prime})}=\psi
. Let
\Phi^{\prime}
denote the resulting set of monomorphisms after this removal process is done. It remains only to show that
|\Phi^{\prime}|\geq p^{e(H)}n^{v(H)}
.

Observe that for a fixed
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
, if some
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}
had too low of degree, then we removed at most
2p^{e(H)-e(H^{\prime})}n^{v(H)-v(H^{\prime})}
monomorphisms which restrict to
\psi
from
\Phi
. As such, the total number of monomorphisms that could have been removed going from
\Phi
to
\Phi^{\prime}
is at most

\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}}2p^{e(H)-e(H^{\prime})}n^{v(H)-v(H^{\prime})}\cdot|\Phi_{H^{\prime}}|.

By our hypothesis we have
|\Phi_{H^{\prime}}|\leq p^{e(H^{\prime})}n^{v(H^{\prime})}
for all
H^{\prime}
. Combining this with the expression above gives that the number of
\psi
removed from
\Phi
is at most
(2^{v(H)}-1)2p^{e(H)}n^{v(H)}
, so we conclude that
\Phi^{\prime}
has the desired size.
∎

We next show that weak builders with
\beta
sufficiently large are also strong builders with slightly weaker parameters. Here and in what follows, for a real-valued function
\beta
and real number
c
, we abuse notation slightly by writing
c\beta
to denote the function which maps each element

of the domain of
\beta
to
c\cdot\beta(x)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\Phi
is a
(H,\beta)
-weak builder with
\beta(H-\hat{w})\geq\frac{v(H)}{1-2^{-1/s}}+s
for all
\hat{w}\in V(H)
, then
\Phi
is also a
(H,\frac{1}{2}\beta,s)
-strong builder.

Proof.

We first show that for any set
S\subseteq V(G)
of cardinality at most
s
and any
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}
that
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S\subseteq(\Phi-S)_{H^{\prime}}
. If
H^{\prime}=H
, then
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S=\Phi-S=(\Phi-S)_{H^{\prime}}
, so we may assume
H^{\prime}\neq H
from now on. Let
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S
and let
\phi\in\Phi
be a map with
\phi|_{V(H^{\prime})}=\psi
such that
|\phi(V(H))\cap S|
is as small as possible. If
\phi\in\Phi-S
(i.e. if
|\phi(V(H))\cap S|=0
) then we are done, so we may assume there exists some
\hat{w}\in V(H)
with
\phi(\hat{w})=a
for some
a\in S
. Note that we must have
\hat{w}\notin V(H^{\prime})
since
\phi
extends
\psi\in\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S
. Let
H^{\prime\prime}:=H-\hat{w}
and
\xi:=\phi|_{V(H^{\prime\prime})}
, noting that
\xi|_{V(H^{\prime})}=\psi
since
\hat{w}\notin V(H^{\prime})
. Because
\beta(H-\hat{w})>s
, there exist at least
s+1
extensions of
\xi
in
\Phi
, and in particular there must exist some
\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi
with
\phi^{\prime}(\hat{w})\notin S
. In this case
\phi^{\prime}
(being an extension of
\xi
) is an extension of
\psi
with
|\phi^{\prime}(V(H))\cap S|=|\phi(V(H))\cap S|-1
, a contradiction to how we chose
\phi
. We conclude that
\phi\in\Phi-S
, proving that
\Phi_{H^{\prime}}-S\subseteq(\Phi-S)_{H^{\prime}}
.

To prove that
\Phi
is a
(H,\frac{1}{2}\beta,s)
-strong builder it only remains to show that
\Phi-S
is a
(H,\frac{1}{2}\beta)
-weak builder for all sets
S
of size at most
s
. To prove this, consider any
a\in V(G)
and let
\eta:=1-2^{-1/s}
, so that
(1-\eta)^{s}=\frac{1}{2}
. We will prove that
\Phi-a
is a
(H,(1-\eta)\beta)
-weak builder, from which
\Phi-S
being a
(H,\frac{1}{2}\beta)
-weak builder will follow by iterating this a total of
s
times.

Let
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}\setminus\{H\}
. Consider some
\psi\in(\Phi-a)_{H^{\prime}}
, which we recall in our notation means that
\psi
is the restriction of some
\phi\in\Phi
which does not contain
a
in its image to
V(H^{\prime})
. Our goal is to show that
\deg_{\Phi-a}(\psi)\geq(1-\eta)\beta(H^{\prime})
, which will give the desired result mentioned above.

If there exist at most
\eta\beta(H^{\prime})
full extensions of
\psi
in
\Phi
which include
a
in their image, then we are done since
\deg_{\Phi}(\psi)\geq\beta(H^{\prime})
by hypothesis. If not, then there exists some collection
\Lambda\subseteq\Phi
of at least
\eta\beta(H^{\prime})
monomorphisms from
H
to
G
such that every
\phi\in\Lambda
is an extension of
\psi
that contains
a
in its image, i.e. such that
\phi|_{V(H^{\prime})}=\psi
and
a\in\phi(V(H))
.

For each
\hat{w}\in V(H)\setminus V(H^{\prime})
, let
\Lambda^{\hat{w}}\subseteq\Lambda
denote the extensions
\phi
such that
\phi(\hat{w})=a
, noting by the Pigeonhole Principle there must exist some
\hat{v}\in V(H)\setminus V(H^{\prime})
such that
|\Lambda^{\hat{v}}|\geq\eta\beta(H^{\prime})/v(H)
. Note that every
\phi\in\Lambda^{\hat{v}}
has a restriction in
\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}
, and each restriction uniquely extends back to
\Lambda^{\hat{v}}
since the extension must send
\hat{v}\mapsto a
, so
|\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}|=|\Lambda^{\hat{v}}|
. Furthermore, since
\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}\subseteq\Phi_{H-\hat{v}}
, we have that
\deg_{\Phi}(\xi)\geq\beta(H-\hat{v})
for all
\xi\in\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}
, and in particular,
\deg_{\Phi-a}(\xi)\geq\beta(H-\hat{v})-1
.

Now, we can lower bound the degree of
\psi
in
\Phi-a
. We first choose some
\xi\in\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}\subseteq(\Phi-a)_{H-\hat{v}}
, then choose an extension
\phi
of
\xi
in
\Phi-a
. Note that each
\phi
we construct in this way is distinct by our previous discussion and is an extension of
\psi
in
\Phi-a
by the definition of
\Lambda
, implying that

\deg_{\Phi-a}(\psi)\geq|\Lambda^{\hat{v}}_{H-\hat{v}}|\cdot\deg_{\Phi-a}(\xi)\geq\frac{\eta\beta(H^{\prime})}{v(H)}(\beta(H-\hat{v})-1)\geq\beta(H^{\prime})>(1-\eta)\beta(H^{\prime}),

where the second to last inequlaity used the hypothesis on
\beta
from the lemma.
We conclude that,
\Phi-a
is a
(H,(1-\eta)\beta)
-weak builder, completing the proof.
∎

We next aim to show that strong builders imply the existence of many copies of a given
H
-tree. For this we need the following definitions, where here we recall the definition of a sequence
(h_{1},j_{2},\ldots,h_{t})
being a witness of an
H
-tree made at the end of
Definition

5
.

Definition 9
.

We say that a witness
(h_{1},j_{2},h_{2},j_{3},\dots,h_{t})
of an
H
-tree
F
is of
length

t
and
type

\gamma
where
\gamma:2^{H}\setminus\{H\}\to\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\}
is the function with
\gamma(H^{\prime})
defined to be the number of
i
such that

H^{\prime}=H[h_{i}^{-1}(V(H_{i})\cap V(H_{j_{i}}))]

as
i
ranges from
2
to
t
.

Given a collection
\Phi
of monomorphisms of
H
into some graph
G
, we say that
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{t})
with
\phi_{i}\in\Phi
for all
i
is a
lift
of the witness
(h_{1},j_{2},h_{2},j_{3},\dots,h_{t})
into
\Phi
if for all
\hat{u},\hat{v}\in V(H)
we have
\phi_{i}(\hat{u})=\phi_{j}(\hat{v})
if and only if
h_{i}(\hat{u})=h_{j}(\hat{v})
. The
image
of the lift
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{t})
in
G
is the set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\phi_{i}(V(H))
.

As a point of reference, the definition of
H
-trees can be shown to imply that
h_{i}(\hat{u})=h_{j}(\hat{v})
only happens if
\hat{u}=\hat{v}
. However, it will be convenient to allow for the a priori possibility of having
\hat{u}\neq\hat{v}
in the definition of lifts of witnesses. In particular, we use this in the following result which says that lifts imply the existence of copies of
F
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\Phi\subseteq\mathrm{Mon}(F,G)
. Let
F
be a
H
-tree with defining copies
\{H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{t}\}
and witness
W:=(h_{1},j_{2},\ldots,h_{t})
. If
P:=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{t})
is a lift of the witness
W
into
\Phi
, then there exists a monomorphism from
F
to
G
with image
\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\phi_{i}(V(H))
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:V(F)\to V(G)
be defined by setting
\rho(u)=\phi_{i}(h_{i}^{-1}(u))
for each
u\in V(H_{i})
and
i\in[t]
. Since
V(F)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}V(H_{i})
, every vertex
u\in V(F)
is assigned at least one image by
\rho
. The map
\rho
is well defined since if we have
u\in V(H_{i})\cap V(H_{j})
for some
i\neq j
, then there exists some
\hat{v},\hat{w}\in V(H)
such that
u=h_{i}(\hat{v})=h_{j}(\hat{w})
, but then by the definition of a lift into
\Phi
we have
\phi_{i}(\hat{v})=\phi_{j}(\hat{w})
. In particular
\phi_{i}(h_{i}^{-1}(u))=\phi_{j}(h_{j}^{-1}(u))
.

The map
\rho
has image
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\phi_{i}(V(H))
by definition, and in fact
\rho
maps injectively since if
u\in V(H_{i})
and
v\in V(H_{j})
with
\rho(u)=\rho(v)
, then
\phi_{i}(h_{i}^{-1}(u))=\phi_{j}(h_{j}^{-1}(v))
, and thus by the definition of a lift into
\Phi
we have

u=h_{i}(h_{i}^{-1}(u))=h_{j}(h_{j}^{-1}(v))=v.

Finally, we note that
\rho|_{V(H_{i})}=\phi_{i}\circ h_{i}^{-1}
is a composition of homomorphisms, and thus maps edges of
H_{i}
to edges of
G
. Since every edge of
F
is in some
H_{i}
, the map
\rho
is a homomorphism.
∎

We now prove the main fact we need about lifts of witnesses.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
F
be an
s
-vertex
H
-tree with a witness
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
of type
\gamma
, and let
\Phi
be an
(H,\beta,s^{\prime})
-strong builder for some graph

with

. Then the number of lifts of
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
into
\Phi
is at least

|\Phi|\cdot\prod_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\beta(H^{\prime})^{\gamma(H^{\prime})}.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on

. For

, the only
H
-tree with a witness of length

is

of length

\gamma
of

is the constant

,
(\phi)
is a lift of
(h_{1})
since both

are injective. Thus, there are at least
|\Phi|
lifts of
(h_{1})
.

Now assume

and let
F
be an
H
-tree with
|V(F)|\leq s^{\prime}
, with defining copies
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{t}
, and with witness
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
of type
\gamma
. Define
H^{*}:=H[h_{t}^{-1}(V(H_{t})\cap V(H_{j_{t}}))]
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the
H
-tree with defining copies
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{t-1}
, and note that
V(F^{\prime})\leq V(F)\leq s^{\prime}
, and that
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t-1})
is a witness of
F^{\prime}
, say with type
\gamma^{\prime}
, and note that

\gamma^{\prime}(H^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\gamma(H^{\prime})-1&\text{ if }H^{\prime}=H^{*},\\
\gamma(H^{\prime})&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now, fix a lift
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{t-1})
of
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t-1})
into
\Phi
. We will show there are many

such that
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{t-1},\phi)
is a lift of
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
. Indeed, if we set

S:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi_{i}(V(H))\setminus\phi_{j_{t}}(V(H^{*})),

and note that
|S|\leq|V(F^{\prime})|\leq s^{\prime}
, then we have that
\Phi-S
is a
(H,\beta)
-weak builder. Furthermore,
\phi_{j_{t}}|_{V(H^{*})}\in\Phi_{H^{*}}-S\subseteq(\Phi-S)_{H^{*}}
(since

is a strong-builder), and so there are

of

which are disjoint from

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{t-1},\phi)
is a lift of
(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
. By induction, this gives us

|\Phi|\cdot\prod_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\beta(H^{\prime})^{\gamma^{\prime}(H^{\prime})}\cdot\beta(H^{*})=|\Phi|\cdot\prod_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\beta(H^{\prime})^{\gamma(H^{\prime})}

(h_{1},j_{1},\dots,h_{t})
.
∎

The last thing we need is the following lemma relating the type of an

-tree

with

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
F
be an
H
-tree with a witness
(h_{1},j_{2},\dots,h_{t})
of type
\gamma
. Then

v(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\gamma(H^{\prime})(v(H)-v(H^{\prime}))=v(F)

and

e(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\gamma(H^{\prime})(e(H)-e(H^{\prime}))=e(F).

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle v(F)=\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}h_{i}(V(H))\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left|h_{i}(V(H))\setminus\left(\bigcup_{k<i}h_{k}(V(H))\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left(|h_{i}(V(H))|-\left|h_{i}(V(H))\cap\left(\bigcup_{k<i}h_{k}(V(H))\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=|h_{1}(V(H))|+\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(|h_{i}(V(H))|-\left|h_{i}(V(H))\cap h_{j_{i}}(V(H))\right|\right)

\displaystyle=v(H)+\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(v(H)-\left|h_{i}(V(H))\cap h_{j_{i}}(V(H))\right|\right),

then for
H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}
as

ranges from
2
to

in the above sum, we have exactly
\gamma(H^{\prime})
indices for which
\left|h_{i}(V(H))\cap h_{j_{i}}(V(H))\right|=|V(H^{\prime})|
, so we may write

v(H)+\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(v(H)-|h_{i}(V(H))\cap h_{j_{i}}(V(H))|\right)=v(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\gamma(H^{\prime})(v(H)-v(H^{\prime})).

Let us now focus on the edges of
F
. For each

, write
E_{i}:=\{h_{i}(\hat{u})h_{i}(\hat{v})\mid\hat{u}\hat{v}\in E(H)\}
. Then, similarly as in the case for vertices, we can write

\displaystyle e(F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left(|E_{i}|-\left|E_{i}\cap\left(\bigcup_{k<i}E_{k}\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=e(H)+\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(e(H)-\left|E_{i}\cap E_{j_{i}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=e(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\gamma(H^{\prime})\left(e(H)-e(H^{\prime})\right).

and

. Let

(1)

(2)

For each

. By (

) and (

that
\mathrm{mon}(H^{\prime},G)\leq n^{v(H^{\prime})}p^{e(H^{\prime})}
for each

. Furthermore, for each

\beta(H-\hat{w})=np^{\deg(\hat{w})}=C^{\deg(\hat{w})}n^{1-\deg(\hat{w})/d}\geq C,

where in this last step we used the assumption

and

for all

-strong builder.

We now show that

contains many copies of

which is of some type

(3)

Expanding the above product and collecting the powers of

(not including

), then applying Lemma
4.9
we find the power of
n
in (
3
) is

v(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}d_{H}(v(H)-V(H^{\prime}))=v(F),

and similarly, the power of

e(H)+\sum_{H^{\prime}\in 2^{H}}\gamma(H^{\prime})(e(H)-e(H^{\prime}))=e(F).

Thus, we find we have at least

lifts of this witness into
\Phi
. Moreover,

4.7
implies each of these lifts
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{t})
gives us a monomorphism of

into
G
with image
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\phi_{i}(V(H))
. As the number of lifts of the witness
(h_{1},j_{2},\ldots,h_{t})
with a given image of size

is trivially at most

, we in total conclude that

\mathrm{mon}(F,G)\geq\frac{n^{v(F)}p^{e(F)}}{v(F)^{tv(H)}}.

We next count
\mathrm{mon}(F[R],G)
. By (
1
) and (
2
) (along with Observation
3.1
), for each
R_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
, we have that
\mathrm{mon}(R_{i},G)\leq n^{v(R_{i})}p^{e(R_{i})}
, so in total the number of monomorphisms of

into
G
is at most

\prod_{R_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}n^{v(R_{i})}p^{e(R_{i})}=n^{v(F[R])}p^{e(F[R])}.

Finally, if we have, say
k
monomorphisms of
F
into
G
which all map
F[R]
onto some fixed set

with
|S|=v(F[R])
, then there must be at least
k/v(F[R])!
monomorphisms of
F
into
G
which map
F[R]
onto
S
such that the restriction to
S
is the same. Putting everything together, by the Pigeonhole Principle, there must exist at least

M^{\prime}:=\frac{n^{v(F)}p^{e(F)}}{(v(F[R])!\cdot v(F)^{tv(H)}n^{v(F[R])}p^{e(F[R])}}=\frac{n^{v(F)-v(F[R])}p^{e(F)-e(F[R])}}{(v(F[R])!\cdot v(F)^{tv(H)}}

monomorphisms of
F
into
G
which all agree on
F[R]
. By Observation
3.1
, this gives us that there are at least
M:=M^{\prime}/\mathrm{aut}(F)
distinct copies of
F
which agree on
F[R]
in
G
, i.e.
G
contains a copy of some element in
(F,R)^{M}
. Recalling that
p=Cn^{-1/d}
and
\frac{v(F)-v(F[R])}{e(F)-e(F[R])}=d
, we find

M=\frac{C^{e(F)-e(F[R])}}{\mathrm{aut}(F)\cdot(v(F[R])!\cdot v(F)^{tv(H)}}.

Thus, as long as we choose

, we have a copy of
(F,R)^{q}
in

. It is straightforward to see that

can be chosen large enough to satisfy all the required inequalities, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.10

In order to construct

Observation 4.11

Let

Lemma 4.13

Let

This section is organized as follows. In

5.1
Key Tools

Our approach for non-realizability of exponents for graphs
H
is inspired by the proof of non-realizability for stars given in
[
9
]
. In their proof, they make the simple but crucial observation that if
\mathcal{F}
is a family which does not contain a subgraph of a star, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{1,t-1},\mathcal{F})=\Omega(n^{t-1})
since the graph
G=K_{1,n-1}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{F}
does contain a star, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{1,t-1},\mathcal{F})=O(n)
since each vertex will be in at most
O(1)
stars. From this one deduces that no exponent in
(1,t-1)
is realizable for
K_{1,t-1}
.

Similar to the argument above, we will prove the non-realizability of exponents for graphs
H
by constructing a “simple” family of graphs
\mathcal{F}_{H}
such that the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})
will largely depend on whether or not
\mathcal{F}
contains (a subgraph of) each graph in
\mathcal{F}_{H}
. To this end, we make the following crucial definition.

Definition 11
.

Given a graph

, a set of vertices

, and an integer

, we define the graph
H_{R}^{q}
to be the graph obtained by taking the union of
q
copies of
H
such that each of the copies agree on the set of vertices
R
and are otherwise disjoint. For each integer
k\geq 1
we define

\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q}:=\{H_{R}^{q}:R\subsetneq V(H),\ H-R\textrm{ has at least }k\textrm{ connected components}\}.

for an example of a graph of the form

Proposition 5.1
.

For every graph

, and family of graphs

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=\Omega_{H,k}(n^{k})
or there exists an integer

\mathcal{F}
-free graph is also

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=\Omega_{H,k}(n^{k})

Corollary 5.2

If

is a graph,

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q})=o_{q}(n^{r})
, then no rational in

Lemma 5.5

Proof Intuition for

For example, consider

In the proof above it is critical that we specify the edge of

Motivated by the above, for each monomorphism

of a tree

of

which have “many extensions” with respect to

which agree with

(i.e. there exist many ways of changing how

while maintaining that

then for every choice of

to consist of the subtrees which either contain

or

More generally, if

will always consist of the subtrees that contain some connected component of

The key insight about working with these sets

is that whatever edge set

Given all this, we are led to the following sub-question: what conditions can one impose on a set of subtrees

to guarantee the existence of a set of

that contains an edge from each element of

, this question asks when we can guarantee that a collection of subtrees

contains an edge which lies in every subtree of

is a collection of subtrees of a given tree

Formal Details
. We begin with some technical definitions.

Definition 12
.

Given a tree
T

is a

of
T
if
T^{\prime}
itself is a tree. We say that a subtree

is

if

and if every edge in

which is incident to a vertex of

is incident to a leaf of
T^{\prime}
. We say that a collection of subtrees

of a tree

is

if for any

, there exists some

such that

. Here we adopt the convention that
\mathcal{T}
is

\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
.

The name “leaf-cuttable” is meant to convey the fact that if

is the set of leaves of

, then

contains

as a connected component (that is,

can be “separated” from the rest of

by cutting off its leaves). With these definitions we can prove our Helly-type result for subtrees with respect to edges.

Theorem 5.10

Let

be an integer. If

is a tree and if

is a

-Helly collection of leaf-cuttable subtrees, then there exist a set

of at most

such that every

We note that this result is entirely false if we do not impose that the subtrees are leaf-cuttable. Indeed, if

and if
\mathcal{T}
is the set of copies of

in

\mathcal{T}
has the 2-Helly property, but any set of edges

(which is in stark contrast to

Now consider the case that

\hat{z}_{b}\notin X_{j}
. Because
\hat{z}_{b-1}\in X_{j}
by the minimality of
b
, and because
\hat{z}_{b-1}\hat{z}_{b}
is an edge of
T
, we have by definition of
\hat{z}_{b-1}\in X_{j}
that
\hat{z}_{b-1}\hat{z}_{b}
must be an edge in
T^{\prime}_{j}
. Since
\hat{z}_{b}\notin X_{j}
, we must have

\hat{z}_{b}\in V(T^{\prime}_{j})\setminus X_{j}=\widehat{S}_{j}\subseteq R_{j}\subseteq R.

We conclude that the paths in
T
between the
\hat{x}_{j}
vertices always contains at least one vertex in
R
, implying that each of these vertices lies in a different connected component of
T-R
, proving the result.
∎

In total, this last claim implies that
G
contains a copy of some
T_{R}^{q}\in\mathcal{F}_{T,k}^{q}
, contradicting
G
being
\mathcal{F}_{T,k}^{q}
-free. We conclude that no set of edge disjoint
T_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,T_{k}^{\prime}
exist in
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}
, proving the result.
∎

6
Concluding Remarks

As things stand, it is not clear what the set of realizable exponents for an arbitrary graph should be. At present, our only guess for what might be the right answer in general is the following, where here we recall that
\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q}
is the family introduced in
Definition

11
.

Question 6.1
.

Is it the case that for all graphs
H
and integers
k\in[1,v(H)]
, a rational number
r\in(k-1,k)
is realizable for
H
if and only if
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q})=\Omega(n^{r})
for some sufficiently large
q
?

Recall that in
Corollary

5.2
we showed that if
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q})=o_{q}(n^{r})
then
r
is not realizable, so
6.1
in essence asks if the converse of this statement is true. We believe we can prove that
6.1
has a positive answer for all
H
on at most 4 vertices, but other than this we have little reason to believe the answer to
6.1
is yes in general. Still, this question raises a number of followup questions that may be easier to handle, such as the following.

Question 6.2
.

Is it the case that for every graph
H
, every rational in
[\alpha(H),v(H)]
is realizable, where here
\alpha(H)
denotes the independence number of
H
?

A positive answer to
6.1
would imply a positive answer to
6.2
, as we showed in
Lemma

5.8
that
\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q}=\emptyset
whenever
k>\alpha(H)
. We also note that the bounds of this question exactly matches the cutoff for realizable exponents of both stars and cliques.

While we are unwilling to go so far as to call
6.2
a conjecture, we do believe a slight weakening of this should be true.

Conjecture 6.3
.

If
H
is a graph of maximum degree
\Delta(H)
, then every rational in the interval

\left[v(H)-\frac{e(H)}{\Delta(H)},v(H)\right]

is realizable for
H
.

A positive answer to
6.2
would suffice to prove
6.3
, as it is easy to show
3
3

3

This is because the complement of a (maximum) independent set
S
must be incident to all
e(H)
edges of
H
, but also any set of size
v(H)-\alpha(H)
is trivially incident to at most
(v(H)-\alpha(H))\Delta(H)
edges of
H
.
that every graph satisfies
\alpha(H)\leq v(H)-e(H)/\Delta(H)
. This conjecture holds for trees due to
Theorem

2.2
, with
Theorem

2.1
giving an approximate version of this conjecture for all graphs.

As far as we know, our general result
Theorem

4.3
may be sufficient to prove
6.3
for all
H
, in which case it would prove the stronger fact that every graph is
d
-admissible for all
d\geq\Delta
. However, we emphasize that while
H
being
d
-admissible implies
v(H)-e(H)/d
is a realizable exponent, the converse of this statement is (perhaps surprisingly) not true in general. That is, there are cases where a direct application of the random polynomial method from
Theorem

3.2
can not be used to prove the realizability of exponents.

As an informal example of this phenomenon, consider the problem of showing that rationals in the range
(1.5,2)
are realizable for the complete tripartite graph
H=K_{1,1,2}
. Here the issue is that in order to have
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=o(n^{2})
, the family
\mathcal{F}
must contain (a subgraph of) some
K_{1,1,t}
, as otherwise
G=K_{1,1,n-2}
would be
\mathcal{F}
-free and give
\Omega(n^{2})
copies of
H
. However,
K_{1,1,t}
can be viewed as the rooted power of
(K_{3},\{x,y\})
with
xy
an edge of the triangle, which has rooted density 2. Thus when applying
Theorem

3.2
to avoid a family containing large powers of
(K_{3},\{x,y\})
, the number of copies of
K_{1,1,2}
that one finds is at most on the order of
n^{4-5/2}=n^{1.5}
. As such, a direct application of random polynomials can not be used to prove the existence of realizable exponents in
(1.5,2)
. Nevertheless, it is in fact possible to show that every rational in
(1.5,2)
is realizable for
H
. We plan to do this in forthcoming work by starting with a random polynomial graph and then “trimming” it in some appropriate way.

For our final question, we recall that
Corollary

2.6
showed that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{k})
for some integer
k
whenever
T
is a tree and
\mathcal{F}
is a forest. We wonder if this same result might continue to hold for other choices of
H
.

Question 6.4
.

Is it true that for any graph
H
and any finite family
\mathcal{F}
containing a forest that either
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{k})
for some integer
k
or
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=0
for all sufficiently large
n
?

Perhaps the most natural approach towards proving this question for a given
H
would be to extend either
Theorem

2.5
or
Theorem

5.3
to
H
. For example, if
k=\alpha(H)+1
then
Lemma

5.8
gives that
\mathcal{F}_{H,k}^{q}=\emptyset
, so the natural analog of
Theorem

5.3
would suggest that for any family
\mathcal{F}
we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{F})=O(\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})^{\alpha(H)}).

This in fact holds if
H
is a bipartite graph without isolated vertices, since in this case König’s Theorem implies that the vertices of
H
can be covered by
\alpha(H)
edges. On the other hand, this inequality is false for graphs
H
with
\alpha(H)<v(H)/2
simply by considering
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
. Because of this observation, one might hope that
Theorem

5.3
could continue to hold with
T
replaced by any bipartite graph
H
without isolated vertices, but this is false for
H=C_{4}
and
k=2
. Indeed, in this case the natural generalization of
Theorem

5.3
would essentially state that every family
\mathcal{F}
which contains
K_{2,q}
satisfies
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=O(\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F}))
. However,
Theorem

3.2
shows that for
\mathcal{F}=\{K_{2,q}\}
we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=\Omega(n^{2})
which is much larger than
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=O(n^{3/2})
.

From here one might hope that even if
Theorem

5.3
is false for
C_{4}
that perhaps
Theorem

2.5
still holds. This again turns out to be false for
k=2
but requires a bit more work to show, the details of which we briefly sketch below.

Proposition 6.5
.

There exists a family
\mathcal{F}
such that neither
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=\Omega(n^{2})
nor the bound
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=O(\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F}))
holds. In particular, the statement of
Theorem

2.5
does not hold for
C_{4}
and

.

Sketch of Proof.

Take
\mathcal{F}
to consist of
K_{2,q}
together with the powers of a rooted tree
(T,R)
of rooted density
d
with
3/2<d<2
. Note that we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=O(\mathrm{ex}(n,(T,R)^{q}))=O(n^{2-1/d})
while
Theorem

3.2
implies
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=\Omega(n^{4-4/d})
, and this is much larger than
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
for
d>3/2
. We claim now that
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},\mathcal{F})=O(n^{2-\varepsilon})
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. To this end, say we have an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free graph
G
and let
S
be its vertices of degree at most
n^{1-1/d+\varepsilon/2}
and
L
the rest of the vertices. Because
G
is

-free the number of copies of
C_{4}
using a vertex of

n^{1+2(1-1/d+\varepsilon/2)}\leq n^{2-\varepsilon}
provided
1-2/d+\varepsilon\leq-\varepsilon

since we know
e(G)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=O(n^{2-1/d})
, so again being
K_{2,q}

q|L|^{2}=O(n^{2-\varepsilon})
as desired.
∎

Rituximab and Cycle-Synchronized Prednisone for Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Rechallenge in Minimal Change Disease
Authors: Hariharan Seshadri, Evelyn Acosta, Amanda Tchakarov, Larissa Meyer, Jamie S Lin
Published: 2026

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) – including cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4, programmed cell death protein-1 and programmed death-ligand 1 inhibitors – have transformed the treatment of advanced malignancies. However, their immune activating mechanisms reduce self-tolerance and cause renal immune-related adverse events in 2% to 5% of patients, limiting continuation of life-saving therapy.1 Corticosteroids remain the cornerstone of immune-related adverse events management, yet prolonged steroid use suppresses the cytotoxic T-cell response that underlies ICI efficacy and has been associated with worse survival outcomes in ICI recipients.2 Minimal change disease (MCD) is a rare but clinically distinct immune-related adverse event, now recognized in 41.2% of biopsy-proven cases of nephrotic syndrome.3 Of the 6 previous ICI rechallenge attempts in MCD, 3 resulted in relapse of proteinuria; 2 remained in renal remission; and 1 received continuous prophylactic maintenance corticosteroids.4, 5, 6, 7, 8 The absence of a reproducible management framework limits the clinical applicability of these reports. We present the first case of ICI-MCD with rituximab which enabled renal remission within a 30-day course of prednisone in a patient with ongoing oncologic need for ICI continuation. Rechallenge was successful using a cycle-synchronized prophylactic steroid bridge without maintenance immunosuppression or relapse of proteinuria. A systematic review of all biopsy-confirmed ICI-MCD cases contextualizes this approach and informs a preliminary rechallenge framework.

Results

Case Presentation

A 60-year-old female with mismatch repair-deficient stage IIIC1 undifferentiated endometrial cancer and history of autoimmune encephalitis presented to nephrology in July 2025 with 2 weeks of progressive bilateral lower extremity swelling and 10% weight gain. She had undergone robotic-assisted total laparoscopic hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy in December 2024, followed by carboplatin and paclitaxel. After disease progression, 200 mg pembrolizumab (programmed cell death protein-1 inhibitor), every 3 weeks was initiated. Her third infusion had been administered 2 weeks before presentation.

On examination, she had bilateral lower extremity edema and blood pressure in the 130s/80s. Labs revealed hypoalbuminemia (serum albumin: 1.9 g/dl), nephrotic-range proteinuria (10.73 g/day), hyperlipidemia (total cholesterol: 461 mg/dl) and preserved kidney function (creatinine: 0.84 mg/dl). Kidney biopsy demonstrated normal light microscopy, electron microscopy revealed diffuse podocyte foot process effacement consistent with MCD (Figure 1a and b). The etiologies for MCD were evaluated. Paraneoplastic glomerulopathy was considered unlikely given the favorable tumor response. Secondary causes were excluded, including podocytopathic medications (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and lithium), viral infections, autoimmune serologies, and plasma cell dyscrasias (serum protein electrophoresis revealed no monoclonal protein). Although de novo primary MCD could not be entirely excluded, the temporal relationship with pembrolizumab supported a diagnosis of ICI-associated MCD.

Prednisone 60 mg daily (1 mg/kg per day) was initiated with Atovaquone 1500 mg daily for Pneumocystis jirovecii prophylaxis. Rituximab 1 g i.v. was administered 2 weeks apart (Figure 1c). Supportive oral therapy included bumetanide 1 mg twice daily, losartan 12.5 mg daily, and atorvastatin 10 mg daily. Complete renal remission was achieved within 30 days with urine protein to creatinine ratio reduced to 0.33 g/g.

Given her favorable tumor response, absence of alternative systemic therapies, and pre-existing autoimmune risk demonstrated by her history of autoimmune encephalitis, ICI rechallenge was carefully considered. Her underlying immune susceptibility was felt to increase the risk of MCD relapse supporting a structured prophylactic steroid approach rather than observation alone. A cycle-synchronized prednisone bridge (20 mg daily for 4 days beginning on the day of each infusion) was administered without maintenance immunosuppression. The 4-day duration was selected to cover the peri-infusion immune activation window, whereas the 20 mg dose was chosen to minimize cumulative steroid exposure to avoid attenuation of anti-tumor immunity.2,9 After 9 additional pembrolizumab cycles, the patient remains in complete renal remission with stable urine protein to creatinine ratio (Figure 1c) and imaging confirming durable oncologic response at 6 months (Supplementary Figure S1).

Systematic Review Findings

A systematic review of PubMed and Embase (inception through February 2026) identified 20 biopsy-proven ICI-associated MCD cases, including the present case, for qualitative analysis; full search strategies and eligibility criteria are provided in the Supplementary Methods. Detailed clinical and demographic findings are summarized in Table 1. The clinical profile of ICI-MCD revealed that the median age of presentation is 63.5 years with a 3.5:1 male preponderance. Pembrolizumab accounts for the majority of cases (50%), followed by nivolumab (25%), and ipilimumab (25%). Nephrotic syndrome can develop as early as 1 dose or as late as 18 months after the initiation of ICI therapy. Acute kidney injury was present in most cases (74%) with a median creatinine elevation of 2 mg/dl (interquartile range: 1.05 – 3.17), out of which 3 of 14 patients (21%) required dialysis. The median proteinuria at presentation is 12.3 g/day (interquartile range: 9.8–19.0).

Among the 6 previous rechallenge attempts in the literature, 3 patients (Kitchlu et al.4 case 2, Glutsch et al.,5 and Kethineni et al.7), experienced relapse of proteinuria without concomitant steroid therapy despite having a long initial steroid taper of 120, 42, and 120 days, respectively, suggesting that steroid duration alone does not confer durable protection. In Alasadi et al.,8 2 patients sustained remission after ICI rechallenge without reported prophylactic measures. Saito et al.,6 represents the only previous rechallenge case in which concomitant prednisolone therapy was used. Complete remission was induced with prednisolone 40 mg/day, tapered to a maintenance dose of 10 mg/day throughout rechallenge, during which tumor progression was observed after the second cycle before stabilizing.6 These cases collectively highlight that neither steroid duration nor absence of prophylaxis reliably predicts rechallenge outcomes, highlighting the need for individualized risk stratification.

Discussion

The pathogenesis of ICI-MCD remains poorly understood. In primary MCD, accumulating evidence implicates systemic T cell dysfunction, B cell-mediated pathways, and anti-nephrin autoantibodies, though whether ICI therapy triggers podocyte injury through these or other distinct mechanisms is not established, limiting our ability to predict rituximab response or rechallenge tolerance.S1

Our patient had pre-ICI history of autoimmune encephalitis with seizures that developed 2 weeks after hysterectomy. The etiology was attributed to surgical release of tumor contents triggering aberrant immune activation rather than a paraneoplastic process (paraneoplastic panel and N-methyl-D-aspartate-receptor antibody negative). Treatment with intravenous immunoglobulin and high-dose corticosteroids led to full recovery. This episode informed our rationale for a structured 4-day prophylactic steroid bridge during ICI rechallenge. Unlike Saito et al., in whom continuous prednisolone 10 mg/day was maintained throughout rechallenge, our patient achieved complete remission with a 30-day course enabled by rituximab-based induction, with rechallenge accomplished via a cycle-synchronized bridge and no maintenance immunosuppression.6 Whether rituximab’s B cell depletion contributed independently to rechallenge tolerance cannot be determined from a single case, but the combination of rituximab-based induction and a cycle-synchronized prophylactic bridge—informed by patient-specific immune risk—represents the most granular rechallenge framework reported to date, though prospective data are needed.

Taken together, we suggest that a rechallenge strategy should be individualized based on patient and therapy-associated risk, as combination ICI agents may warrant more robust prophylactic bridging given historically higher immune-related adverse event rates. Notably, this is also, to our knowledge, the first reported case of ICI-MCD in endometrial carcinoma, expanding the known spectrum of associated malignancies. As with all single cases, prospective validation is warranted, though the rarity of ICI-MCD will necessitate collaborative multicenter efforts.

Conclusion

Early rituximab may facilitate rapid MCD remission while minimizing cumulative steroid exposure. In patients where ICI rechallenge is warranted, a short cycle-synchronized prednisone bridge may reduce the risk of MCD relapse without requiring maintenance immunosuppression. This case describes a preliminary framework warranting prospective validation for ICI rechallenge in MCD that balances renal remission with preservation of anti-tumor immunity.

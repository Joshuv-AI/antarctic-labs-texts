Generalised Pinching Inequality
Andreas Winter

Abstract
Hayashi’s Pinching Inequality , which establishes a matrix inequality between a semidefinite matrix and a multiple of its “pinched” version via a projective measurement, has found many applications in quantum information theory and beyond. Here, we show a very simple proof of it, which lends itself immediately to natural generalisations where the different projections of the measurement have different weights, and where the matrix inequality can be reversed. As an application we show how the generalised pinching inequality in the case of binary measurements gives rise to a novel gentle measurement lemma, where matrix ordering replaces approximation in trace norm.



I
Pinching inequality and its generalisation

A projective measurement
(P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{n})
in a
complex Hilbert space
A
, i.e. a collection of
n\geq 2
projection operators

P_{i}=P_{i}^{\dagger}=P_{i}^{2}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}={\openone}_{A}
, defines the

pinching map

{\cal P}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\rho P_{i}
, which is
completely positive and trace preserving.
Hayashi
Hayashi:pinch
(see also
HayashiOgawa:pinch
, and

Bhatia:pinch
for other considerations on pinching),
proved the following matrix inequality:

\forall\,{\cal T}({\cal H})\ni\rho\geq 0\quad\rho\leq n\,{\cal P}(\rho),

(1)

where
\rho\geq 0
is a positive semidefinite (necessarily selfadjoint)
trace class operator, and “
\geq
” refers to the Löwner (also known as
semidefinite) order on trace class operators. Despite its simple appearance
and elementary proof(s), this matrix inequality has found numerous applications
in quantum information theory and other fields.

A slightly more abstract form was proved in
(
LancienWinter:pinch
, Lemma 2.1)
:
for arbitrary bounded operators
(M_{1},M_{2},\ldots,M_{n})
acting on
A
(and
mapping to a potentially different Hilbert space
B
),

\forall\rho\geq 0\quad\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)^{\dagger}\leq n\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\rho M_{i}^{\dagger}.

(2)

Here we show a flexible generalisation of the pinching inequality, and for that
purpose define the following convex set in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
, which is in fact
a
spectrahedron

RamanaGoldman:spectrahedron
;
Vinzant:spectrahedra
:

{\cal A}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})\geq J\},

(3)

where
{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|
is the diagonal
matrix with entries
\alpha_{i}
in the computational basis;
and
J=J_{n}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|i\rangle\!\langle j|
is the all-ones matrix in Dirac notation
(if it is clear from the context, we drop the – implicit – format
n\times n
).
Note that every
\vec{\alpha}\in{\cal A}_{n}
necessarily satisfies
\alpha_{i}>1

for all
i\in[n]
, and that
n\!\cdot\!\vec{1}=(n,n,\ldots,n)\in{\cal A}_{n}
.

Lemma 1

If
\vec{\alpha}\in{\cal A}_{n}
, then for all bounded operators
M_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow B
,

\forall\rho\geq 0\quad\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)^{\dagger}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}M_{i}\rho M_{i}^{\dagger}.

(4)

Conversely, if (
4
) holds for a nontrivial projective POVM

(P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{n})
, i.e.
P_{i}\neq 0
, then
\vec{\alpha}\in{\cal A}_{n}
.

Proof.

With
C={{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
, define the bounded operator
W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow B\otimes C

by
W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\otimes|i\rangle
. Note that this defines a
completely positive map
\Omega(\rho)=W\rho W^{\dagger}
from

{\cal T}(A)
to
{\cal T}(B\otimes C)
, and hence for
J\leq{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})
,

\forall\rho\geq 0\quad\operatorname{Tr}_{C}\Omega(\rho)({\openone}_{B}\otimes J)\leq\operatorname{Tr}_{C}\Omega(\rho)\left({\openone}_{B}\otimes{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})\right).

Clearly, the left hand side of the inequality equals

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)^{\dagger}
,
while the right hand side is

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}M_{i}\rho M_{i}^{\dagger}
, proving the first part of the lemma.

Conversely, if for a projective POVM
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
and for all
\rho\geq 0
,

\rho\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}P_{i}\rho P_{i},

choose fixed point unit vectors
|e_{i}\rangle
of
P_{i}
, i.e.
P_{i}|e_{i}\rangle=|e_{i}\rangle
,
and consider
\rho=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|e_{i}\rangle\!\langle e_{j}|
. Then the above inequality
reduces to

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|e_{i}\rangle\!\langle e_{j}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}|e_{i}\rangle\!\langle e_{i}|,

which clearly is equivalent to
{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})\in{\cal A}_{n}
.

\sqcup

Membership in
{\cal A}_{n}
is a simple semidefinite constraint, and by inspection
one sees that it is given by the polynomial criterion of

\det\left({\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\alpha})-J\right)\geq 0
and all nontrivial
minors (the determinants of
k\times k
-principal submatrices for
k<n
)
being positive. By Sylvester’s condition, any nested family of

k\times k
-minors for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
is sufficient
HornJohnson
.
Still, due to the occurrence of the determinant, for larger
n
the
formulas rapidly become quite cumbersome. We can however construct the
rational polynomial conditions for
\vec{\alpha}\in{\cal A}_{n}
recursively
starting from
n=2
and using the Schur complement
Haynsworth
;
Zhang:Schur
.
Indeed, for
n=2
we have
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in A_{2}
iff

\alpha_{1}>1
and
(\alpha_{1}-1)(\alpha_{2}-1)\geq 1
.
For
n>2
, the Schur complement tells us that

{\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})-J_{n}\geq 0
iff

\left[{\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})-J_{n-1}\right]\oplus\left[\alpha_{n}-1-\operatorname{Tr}J_{n-1}\left({\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})-J_{n-1}\right)^{-1}\right]\geq 0.

The positive semidefiniteness of
{\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})-J_{n}

is thus equivalent to

\displaystyle{\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})

\displaystyle>J_{n-1},

(5)

\displaystyle\text{and }\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{Tr}\left({\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})\bigl({\operatorname{diag}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})-J_{n-1}\bigr)^{-1}\right).

(6)

Note that the first condition says
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})\in{\cal A}_{n-1}^{\circ}
,
the interior of
{\cal A}_{n-1}
.

As an example, we get
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in{\cal A}_{3}
if and only if

\alpha_{1}>1
,
\alpha_{2}>1+1/(\alpha_{1}-1)
and

\left[(\alpha_{1}-1)(\alpha_{2}-1)-1\right]\left[(\alpha_{1}-1)(\alpha_{3}-1)-1\right]\geq\alpha_{1}^{2}
.
For
n=4
, the same three inequalities emerge again (with the last one turning from “
\geq
”
to strict inequality), plus a fourth one involving
\alpha_{4}
, etc.
We will return to the case
n=2
in Section
III
below.

II
Reverse pinching inequality

The proof of Lemma
1
suggests a direct way to
obtaining lower bounds on
\rho
, or more generally on

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)^{\dagger}
, namely
to consider
{\operatorname{diag}}(\vec{\beta})\leq J
for real vectors
\vec{\beta}\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
,
forming an “opposite” spectrahedron
{\cal B}_{n}
to
{\cal A}_{n}
.
Using the same proof, we obtain the following:

Lemma 2

If
\vec{\beta}\in{\cal B}_{n}
, then for all bounded operators
M_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow B
,

\phantom{===}\forall\rho\geq 0\quad\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)\rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\right)^{\dagger}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}M_{i}\rho M_{i}^{\dagger}.\phantom{===}\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}$\sqcup$}

(7)

For
\vec{\beta}\in{\cal B}_{n}
, it is necessary that all
\beta_{i}<1
, but as
J

has a single eigenvalue
n
and all other eigenvalues
0
, either all
\beta_{i}\leq 0
,
or else exactly one
\beta_{i}>0
and all other
\beta_{j}<0
(
j\neq i
).
This suggests that the reverse inequality (
7
) is
most interesting in the case
n=2
, to which we will turn next.

III
Binary case: gentle measurement inequality

For
n=2
, we are facing
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in{\cal A}_{2}
iff

\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}-1&-1\\
-1&\alpha_{2}-1\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0,

which is equivalent to
\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}>1
and
(\alpha_{1}-1)(\alpha_{2}-1)\geq 1
.

Similarly,
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in{\cal B}_{2}
iff

\left[\begin{matrix}1-\beta_{1}&1\\
1&1-\beta_{2}\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0,

which boils down to
\beta_{1},\,\beta_{2}<1
and
(1-\beta_{1})(1-\beta_{2})\geq 1
.

Of course, the tightest bound is obtained when we have equality in the determinant
condition, proving the following:

Proposition 3

We have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{2}

\displaystyle={\operatorname{conv}}\left\{\left(1+t,1+\frac{1}{t}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0<t<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{2}

\displaystyle={\operatorname{conv}}\left\{\left(1-t,1-\frac{1}{t}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0<t<\infty\right\}.

Thus, for
0<t\leq 1
and any two bounded operators
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
,

(1-t)M_{1}\rho M_{1}^{\dagger}-\left(\frac{1}{t}-1\right)M_{2}\rho M_{2}^{\dagger}\leq(M_{1}+M_{2})\rho(M_{1}+M_{2})^{\dagger}\leq(1+t)M_{1}\rho M_{1}^{\dagger}+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)M_{2}\rho M_{2}^{\dagger}.

(8)

Proof.

We have argued the form of
{\cal A}_{2}
and
{\cal B}_{2}
already, which we only have
to plug into Lemmas
1
and
2

to obtain (
8
).
However, there is also an entertaining direct proof: for the right hand
inequality, consider

\begin{split}(M_{1}+M_{2})\rho(M_{1}+M_{2})^{\dagger}&\leq(M_{1}+M_{2})\rho(M_{1}+M_{2})^{\dagger}+\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}\right)\rho\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}\right)^{\dagger}\\
&=(1+t)M_{1}\rho M_{1}^{\dagger}+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)M_{2}\rho M_{2}^{\dagger},\end{split}

by simply distributively expanding the products and cancellation of mixed terms.
Likewise,

\begin{split}(M_{1}+M_{2})\rho(M_{1}+M_{2})^{\dagger}&\geq(M_{1}+M_{2})\rho(M_{1}+M_{2})^{\dagger}-\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}\right)\rho\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{2}\right)^{\dagger}\\
&=(1-t)M_{1}\rho M_{1}^{\dagger}+\left(1-\frac{1}{t}\right)M_{2}\rho M_{2}^{\dagger},\end{split}

and we are done.

Corollary 4

If
\operatorname{Tr}\rho P\geq 1-\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)P\rho P+\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\right)P^{\perp}\rho P^{\perp},

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)P\rho P+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\right)P^{\perp}\rho P^{\perp}.

In particular, with the subnormalised state
\rho^{\perp}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}P^{\perp}\rho P^{\perp}
,

-\sqrt{\epsilon}P\rho P-\sqrt{\epsilon}\rho^{\perp}\leq\rho-P\rho P\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}P\rho P+\left(\epsilon+\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)\rho^{\perp}.

(9)

Proof.

We simply choose
t=\sqrt{\epsilon}
in Lemmas
1

and
2
(or directly in Proposition
3
).

\sqcup

Note that by taking the trace norm in the last inequality, we obtain that

\operatorname{Tr}\rho P\geq 1-\epsilon
implies
\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-P\rho P\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}+\epsilon
,
a “gentle measurement lemma” improving on the original

), but falling short of the

the matrix inequalities in Corollary

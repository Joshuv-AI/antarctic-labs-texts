Stability results for Sobolev, logarithmic Sobolev, and related inequalities
Jean Dolbeault

Abstract
Obtaining explicit stability estimates in classical functional inequalities like the Sobolev inequality has been an essentially open question for 30 years, after the celebrated but non-constructive result [ 4 ] of G. Bianchi and H. Egnell in 1991. Recently, new methods have emerged which provide some clues on these fascinating questions. The goal of the course is to give an introduction to the topic for some fundamental functional inequalities and present several methods that can be used to obtain explicit estimates.

The most classical Sobolev inequality on
{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
is

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})

(1)

where
\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
is the Beppo-Levi space,
\mathsf{S}_{d}
denotes the sharp Sobolev constant and
2^{*}=2\,d/(d-2)
is the critical Sobolev exponent. Equality in (
1
) holds if and only if
f
is in the
(d+2)
-dimensional manifold
\mathscr{M}
of the
Aubin–Talenti functions

g_{a,b,c}(x)=c\left(a+|x-b|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\,,\quad a\in(0,\infty)\,,\quad b\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\,,\quad c\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}\,.

We refer to
[
39
]
for an up-to-date introduction to Ineq. (
1
). In 1991, G. Bianchi and H. Egnell proved in
[
4
]
that the
deficit
in the Sobolev inequality, that is, the difference of the two sides of (
1
), satisfies the
stability
inequality

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\kappa_{d}\,\min_{g\in\mathscr{M}}\left\|{\nabla(f-g)}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}

(2)

for some constant
\kappa_{d}>0
. The method of
[
4
]
uses compactness and provides no estimate on
\kappa_{d}
. Recently, several
constructive
results have been obtained,
i.e.
, results with explicit estimates of
\kappa_{d}
. The goal of these lecture notes is to make a short review based on two main strategies:

1.

The strategy of G. Bianchi and H. Egnell made explicit in both of its steps: the global-to-local reduction and the local analysis, both relying on methods of the Calculus of Variations,

2.

Entropy methods
and
fast diffusion equations
, in which we can recognize also the same two steps, that are based on nonlinear parabolic flows and
carré du champ
techniques.

Entropy methods usually result in better estimates of the stability constant, but to the price of some limitations. We will also state some results for related inequalities, like some families of Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, on the Euclidean space
{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
or on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d+1}
, and consider the use of the Legendre duality applied, for instance, to Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities.

1
Sobolev and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities

Let us start with a first example of fast diffusion flows applied to inequalities in the context of the Legendre duality. On
{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
, the
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

\left\|{g}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\mathsf{S}_{d}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{g\,(-\Delta)^{-1}g}\,dx\quad\forall\,g\in\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})

(3)

is obtained by Legendre’s duality from (
1
), as observed in
[
49
]
. Indeed, by the Legendre transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}\left(\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f\,g}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|{g}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}\left(\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f\,g}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{g\,(-\Delta)^{-1}g}\,dx

where
q=\frac{d+2}{d-2}
is the Hölder conjugate of
2^{*}
, so that
\left\|{f^{q}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}=\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{q}
. It is not difficult to check that
\mathsf{S}_{d}
is also the optimal constant in (
3
).

Theorem 1.1
(
[
33
]
)

Assume that
d\geq 3
. There exists a constant
\mathscr{C}\in[\frac{d}{d+4},1)
such that

\displaystyle\left\|{f^{q}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f^{q}\,(-\Delta)^{-1}f^{q}}\,dx

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}\,\mathsf{S}_{d}^{-1}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{\frac{8}{d-2}}\left(\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})\,.

Theorem
1.1
can be considered as a stability result for (
1
), because the left-hand side in (
4
), which is the deficit associated with (
3
), measures a distance to
\mathscr{M}
. However, it is still a nonlinear functional and corresponds to a weaker norm than the usual norm in
\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
. With
\mathscr{C}=1
, there is an easy proof that goes as follows. Two integrations by parts show that

\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\,g|^{2}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{g\,(-\Delta)^{-1}\,g}\,dx

and, if
g=f^{q}
with
q=\frac{d+2}{d-2}
,

\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\nabla f\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\,g}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f\,g}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{g^{q}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f^{2^{*}}}\,dx\,.

Hence the expansion of the square

0\leq\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\left|\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\,\nabla f-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\,g\right|^{2}}\,dx

shows the result with
\mathscr{C}=1
. To prove that the inequality holds with
\mathscr{C}<1
requires finer tools.

Let us consider the
fast diffusion
equation

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\Delta v^{m}\,,\quad(t,x)\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}\times{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}

(5)

and compute the evolution of the deficit in (
3
) defined as

-\,\mathsf{H}:=\left\|{v}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v\,(-\Delta)^{-1}v}\,dx\geq 0\,.

As in
[
27
]
, we observe that

\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{H}^{\prime}=\left(\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\nabla v^{m}\cdot\nabla v^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}}\,dx-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v^{m+1}}\,dx

where
v=v(t,\cdot)
is a solution of (
5
). With the choice
m=(d-2)/(d+2)
, that is,
m+1=2\,d/(d+2)=q
, which corresponds to the exponent in the
Yamabe flow
, and
u=v^{m}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathsf{S}_{d}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v\,(-\Delta)^{-1}v}\,dx-\left\|{v}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\\
=\left(\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v^{m+1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(\left\|{\nabla u}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{u}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\geq 0\,.

Eq. (
5
) admits a solution with
separation of variables
vanishing at
t=T>0
,

\overline{v}_{T}(t,x)=c\,(T-t)^{\alpha}\,(g_{\star}(x))^{\frac{d+2}{d-2}}\,,\quad\alpha=\tfrac{1}{4}\,(d+2)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c=\left(4\,d\,\tfrac{d-2}{d+2}\right)^{\alpha}\,,

where
g_{\star}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-(d-2)/2}
is the solution of
\Delta g_{\star}+d\,(d-2)\,g_{\star}^{(d+2)/(d-2)}=0
. Up to conformal transformations, this solution describes the asymptotic extinction profile of all solutions of (
5
) near the extinction time
T
.

Theorem 1.2
(
[
23
]
)

For any solution
v
with initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{2d/(d+2)}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
,
v_{0}>0
, there exists
T>0
,
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
such that

\lim_{t\to T_{-}}(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{1-m}}\,\sup_{x\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}(1+|x|^{2})^{d+2}\,\left|\frac{v(t,x)}{\overline{v}(t,x)}-1\right|=0

with
\overline{v}(t,x)=\lambda^{-(d+2)/2}\,\overline{v}_{T}\big(t,(x-x_{0})/\lambda\big)
.

The Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
3
) amounts to
\mathsf{H}\leq 0
and appears as a consequence of the Sobolev inequality (
1
) because
\mathsf{H}^{\prime}\geq 0
for any
t\in(0,T)
and
\lim_{t\to T_{-}}\mathsf{H}(t)=0
by Theorem
1.2
. By computing
\mathsf{H}^{\prime\prime}
, one can obtain refined estimates. Assume that
t\in[0,T)
and let
\mathsf{Y}
be such that

\mathsf{H}(t)=-\,\mathsf{Y}(\mathsf{J}(t))\quad\mbox{with}\quad\mathsf{J}(t):=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v(t,x)^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}}\,dx\,.

With
\kappa_{0}=\mathsf{H}^{\prime}_{0}/\mathsf{J}_{0}
, we obtain the differential inequality

\mathsf{Y}^{\prime}\left(\mathsf{S}_{d}\,s^{1+\frac{2}{d}}+\mathsf{Y}\right)\leq\tfrac{d+2}{2\,d}\,\kappa_{0}\,\mathsf{S}_{d}^{2}\,s^{1+\frac{4}{d}}\,,\quad\mathsf{Y}(0)=0\,,\quad\mathsf{Y}(\mathsf{J}_{0})=-\,\mathsf{H}_{0}\,.

The relation
\mathsf{Y}^{\prime}(\mathsf{J})\,\mathsf{{}^{\prime}}+\mathsf{H}^{\prime}=0
and elementary integrations by parts provide the following refinement of (
4
) written with
\mathscr{C}=1
.

Proposition 1
(
[
33
]
)

Assume that
d\geq 3
. Then for any
f\in\mathscr{D}^{1,2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{f^{q}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2\,d}{d+2}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{f^{q}\,(-\Delta)^{-1}f^{q}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2\,\frac{d+2}{d-2}}\,\varphi\left(\mathsf{S}_{d}^{-1}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{-2}\left(\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\right)

with
q=(d+2)/(d-2)
and
\varphi(s):=\sqrt{1+2\,s}-1
for any
s\geq 0
.

An asymptotic analysis of
v(t,\cdot)
as
t\to T_{-}
and some spectral estimates discard the case
\mathscr{C}=1
in Theorem
1.1
, while the lower bound
\mathscr{C}=d/(d+4)
is obtained by considering a sequence of test functions. See
[
33
]
for details. In dimension
d=2
similar results holds in which (
1
) and (
3
) are respectively replaced by Onofri’s inequality and the logarithmic Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality while (
5
) has to be replaced by the logarithmic fast diffusion equation using the results of
[
55
,
50
,
54
]
instead of Theorem
1.2
. Notice that stability results based on duality are known for (logarithmic) Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities from
[
16
,
17
]
.
The method based on (
5
) also applies to the fractional Sobolev inequality and the dual Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality as shown in
[
45
]
. Even if the optimal value of the stability constant
\mathscr{C}
is not known, a nice feature of Theorem
1.1
is that we have a rather precise estimate of
\mathscr{C}
. However, a significant drawback of the method is that the deficit of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality corresponds to a weaker notion of distance to
\mathscr{M}
than what can be expected.

2
Stability, fast diffusion equation and entropy methods

On
{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
, let us consider the
fast diffusion equation
written in self-similar variables,

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\Big(v\,\big(\nabla v^{m-1}-\,2\,x\big)\Big)=0\,.

(6)

Up to a
t
-dependent rescaling, it is equivalent to (
5
). By
fast diffusion
, we mean
m<1
and reserve the expression
porous medium
to the regime
m>1
while the heat equation case corresponds to
m=1
(in this case, Eq (
6
) is known as the
Fokker-Planck equation
, and
v^{m-1}
should be replaced by
-\,\log v
). An introduction can be found in the two classical books
[
52
,
53
]
. Section
1
was devoted to
m=m_{*}:=(d-2)/(d+2)
with
d\geq 3
. Here we are interested in the range
m_{c}:=(d-2)/d<m<1
in which nonnegative solution with initial data in
\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
at
t=0
exist for any
t\geq 0
according to
[
42
]
, and more specifically to the range
m_{1}\leq m<1
where
m_{1}:=(d-1)/d
. Note that
m_{*}<m_{c}<m_{1}<1
in dimension
d\geq 2
. In any case, (
6
) admits a stationary solution

\mathcal{B}(x):=\left(1+|x|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

which is usually called a
Barenblatt solution
and has finite mass
M>0
for any
m\in(m_{c},1)
. If
m\in(m_{c},1)
, the mass of any nonnegative solution
v
is conserved and for simplicity, we shall assume that
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v(t,x)}\,dx=M
for any
t\geq 0
. See
[
54
]
for an interesting survey on mass conservation and the role of Barenblatt solutions in the range
(m_{c},1)
. With this notation, we can introduce the
generalized entropy
(or
free energy
) and
Fisher information
functionals respectively given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[v]:=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\big(v^{m}-\mathcal{B}^{m}-m\,\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\,(v-\mathcal{B})\big)}\,dx\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}[v]:=\frac{m}{1-m}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v\left|\nabla v^{m-1}-\nabla\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\right|^{2}}\,dx\,.

A straightforward computation shows that, for a solution of (
6
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]=-\,\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\,.

(7)

Theorem 2.1
(
[
22
]
)

Assume that
m\in(m_{1},1)
if
d=1
or
d=2
, and
m\in[m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 3
. If
v
is a nonnegative solution of (
6
) with initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
of mass
M
such that
\mathcal{F}[v_{0}]
is finite, then

\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq\mathcal{F}[v_{0}]\,e^{-\,4\,t}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

(8)

The estimate (
8
) is equivalent to the
entropy – entropy production
inequality

\mathcal{I}[v]\geq 4\,\mathcal{F}[v]\,,

(9)

which can also be rewritten as a family of
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{\theta}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p+1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{1-\theta}\geq\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2p}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}

(10)

with optimal constant. The equivalence is obtained using

p=\frac{1}{2\,m-1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=\frac{p+1}{2\,p}

with
v=f^{2\,p}
so that
v^{m}=f^{p+1}
and
v\,|\nabla v^{m-1}|^{2}=(p-1)^{2}\,|\nabla f|^{2}
. The range
m\in(m_{1},1)
means that
2\,p\in(2,2^{*})
where
2^{*}=2\,d/(d-2)
if
d\geq 3
and
2^{*}=+\infty
otherwise. The case
m=m_{1}
,
i.e.
,
2\,p=2^{*}
, is also covered if
d\geq 3
.

Equality in (
10
) is achieved by
f_{\star}(x)=\mathcal{B}(x)^{\frac{1}{2p}}=\left(1+|x|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
, which also means that
4
is the optimal constant in (
9
) and it is also the best possible decay rate in (
8
). The exponent
\theta=\frac{d\,(p-1)}{(d+2-p\,(d-2))\,p}
is determined by the invariance under scalings and it is easy to check that
\theta=1
for
p=2^{*}/2
and
d\geq 3
. If
d=2
, we obtain the Euclidean Onofri inequality in the limit as
p\to+\infty
. On the other hand,
\lim_{p\to 1_{+}}\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}(p)=1
so that (
10
) becomes an equality for
p=1
. By taking the derivative with respect to
p
at
p=1
, we obtain the scale invariant form of the scale invariant
Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\frac{d}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2}{\pi\,d\,e}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\,dx\right)\geq\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|f|^{2}\,\log|f|^{2}}\,dx

(11)

for any function
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
such that
\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}=1
. Ineq. (
11
) can be found in
(
56
, Theorem 2)
,
(
51
, Inequality (2.3))
and
(
15
, Inequality (26))
. See
[
35
]
for further comments and
[
40
]
for the classical Gaussian form and its Euclidean counterpart.

We obtain a
linearized free energy
and a
linearized Fisher information

\mathsf{F}[w]:=\frac{m}{2}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{w^{2}\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}}\,dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{I}[w]:=m\,(1-m)\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathcal{B}}\,dx

by considering
f_{\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{B}\,(1+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{B}^{1-m}\,w)
and taking the
O(\varepsilon^{2})
in, respectively
\mathcal{F}[f_{\varepsilon}]
and
\mathcal{I}[f_{\varepsilon}]
. The functionals
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{I}
are related by a
Hardy-Poincaré inequality
.

Proposition 2
(
[
6
,
34
,
9
]
)

Let
m\in[m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 3
,
m\in(1/2,1)
if
d=2
, and
m\in(1/3,1)
if
d=1
. If
w\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},\mathcal{B}^{2-m}\,dx)
is such that
\nabla w\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},\mathcal{B}\,dx)
,
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{w\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}}\,dx=0
, then

\mathsf{I}[w]\geq 4\,\alpha\,\mathsf{F}[w]

(12)

with
\alpha=1
. Additionally, under the center of mass condition

\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{x\,w\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}}\,dx=0\,,

(13)

then the inequality holds with
\alpha=2-d\,(1-m)
.

The value of
\alpha
in Proposition
2
is optimal and corresponds to the spectral gap of the linearized evolution operator
\mathcal{L}
associated with (
6
), and to an improved spectral gap under Condition (
13
) in the second case. In the first case, we recover that
\inf\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{F}=\inf\mathsf{I}/\mathsf{F}=4
, which means that the worst decay rate is achieved in (
8
) in the
asymptotic regime
as
t\to+\infty
. The eigenspace of
\mathcal{L}
associated with
\alpha=1
corresponds to the equality case in (
12
) and determines the spectral gap. See
[
26
]
for a description of the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
and
[
7
]
for an equivalence with the setting of Proposition
2
. Under Condition (
13
), an improved entropy – entropy production inequality is obtained, at least for functions which are close enough to the manifold of the Barenblatt functions.

Corollary 1
(
[
34
,
7
,
9
]
)

Let
m\in(m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 2
,
m\in(1/3,1)
if
d=1
,
\eta=2\,(d\,m-d+1)
and
\chi=m/(266+56\,m)
. If
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v}\,dx=\mathrm{M}
,
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{x\,v}\,dx=0
and

(1-\varepsilon)\,\mathcal{B}\leq v\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\mathcal{B}

(14)

for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\chi\,\eta)
, then

\mathcal{I}[v]\geq(4+\eta)\,\mathcal{F}[v]\,.

If
v
solves (
6
) with
m\in(m_{1},1)
and an initial datum
v_{0}
with centred mass,
i.e.
, such that
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}(x)}\,dx=0
, then
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{x\,v(t,x)}\,dx=0
for any
t\geq 0
so that
w=(v-\mathcal{B})\,\mathcal{B}^{m-2}
satisfies (
13
). If additionally
v_{0}
satisfies (
14
), then

\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq\mathcal{F}[v_{0}]\,e^{-(4+\eta)\,t}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

We have obtained an improved entropy – entropy production inequality and an improved decay rate of
\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]
by comparing the nonlinear free energy
\mathcal{F}
and the Fisher information
\mathcal{I}
with their linearized counterparts
\mathsf{F}
and
\mathsf{I}
, and by getting rid of the eigenspace corresponding to
\alpha=1
in (
12
). The result of Corollary
1
can be extended to
m\notin[m_{1},1)
: see
[
6
]
. Under technical conditions, better rates can be achieved in some cases as in
[
25
]
. One of the issues is that Condition (
14
) is rather restrictive and determines only a small neighbourhood of the set of the Barenblatt functions. Here we are going to take advantage of a property of
relative uniform convergence
that goes back to
[
11
]
: Condition (
14
) is satisfied by any solution
v(t,\cdot)
for
t\geq t_{\star}
if the
threshold time

t_{\star}
is taken large enough. Based on results of
[
10
,
9
]
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.2
(
[
9
,
8
]
)

Assume that
m\in(m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 2
,
m\in(1/3,1)
if
d=1
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, small enough, and
A>0
be given. There exists an explicit threshold time
t_{\star}\geq 0
such that, if
v
is a solution of (
6
) with nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
satisfying
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v_{0}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\mathcal{B}}\,dx=\mathrm{M}
and

\mathsf{A}[v_{0}]=\sup_{r>0}r^{\frac{d\,(m-m_{c})}{(1-m)}}\int_{|x|>r}v_{0}\,dx\leq A<\infty\,,

(15)

and if
B(t,\cdot)
solves (
5
) with initial datum
\mathcal{B}
, then

\sup_{x\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\frac{v(t,x)}{\mathcal{B}(x)}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{\star}\,.

Under Condition (
15
), we have an improved decay rate for any
t
in the
asymptotic time range

[t_{\star},+\infty)
.

The next question is to find an improvement in the
initial time layer

[0,t_{\star})
as well. A key ingredient is the
carré du champ
method for which we can refer to
(
9
, Chapter 2)
. We learn for instance that for some strictly convex function
\psi
with
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
, there is an improved entropy – entropy production inequality

\mathcal{I}-4\,\mathcal{F}\geq\psi(\mathcal{F})\geq 0\,,

so that better decay rates can be expected if
\mathcal{F}
is large, which is precisely what can be expected in the initial time layer. In fact, a key estimate of the
carré du champ
method is the simpler observation that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq-\,4\,\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]

if
v
solves (
6
), which allows us to prove a
backward in time
estimate. Let us define
\mathcal{Q}[v]:=\frac{\mathcal{I}[v]}{\mathcal{F}[v]}
and notice that, using (
7
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{Q}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq\mathcal{Q}[v(t,\cdot)]\,\big(\mathcal{Q}[v(t,\cdot)]-4\big)\,.

Lemma 1
(
[
9
]
)

Assume that
m>m_{1}
and
v
is a solution to (
6
) with nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}
. If for some
\eta>0
and
t_{\star}\geq 0
, we have
\mathcal{Q}[v(t_{\star},\cdot)]\geq 4+\eta
, then

\mathcal{Q}[v(t,\cdot)]\geq\,4+\zeta\quad\forall\,t\in[0,t_{\star}]\quad\mbox{with}\quad\zeta:=\frac{4\,\eta\,e^{-4\,t_{\star}}}{4+\eta-\eta\,e^{-4\,t_{\star}}}\,.

In the subcritical range with
m>m_{1}
, the first consequence of Lemma
1
is an improved decay rate of the free energy for the solution of (
6
).

Corollary 2
(
[
9
]
)

Let
m\in(m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 2
,
m\in(1/2,1)
if
d=1
, and
A>0
. If
v
is a solution of (
6
) with nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
such that
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{v_{0}}\,dx=\mathrm{M}
,
\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}}\,dx=0
and
v_{0}
satisfies (
15
), then there is an explicit
\zeta>0
such that

\mathcal{F}[v(t,.)]\leq\mathcal{F}[v_{0}]\,e^{-\,(4+\zeta)\,t}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

The constant
\zeta
can be explicitly computed in terms of
d
,
m
and
A
using Corollary
1
, Theorem
2.2
, and Lemma
1
. The second consequence of Lemma
1
is a
stability result for the entropy – entropy production inequality
, which reads as

\mathcal{I}[v]-\,4\,\mathcal{F}[v]\geq\frac{\zeta}{4+\zeta}\,\mathcal{I}[v]

where the left-hand side is the deficit in (
9
), while the distance in the right-hand side is measured by the Fisher information, which is a quantity that controls strong norms.

In the critical case
m=m_{1}
corresponding to the Sobolev inequality for
d\geq 3
, we also obtain a
constructive stability result
. Let
\mathsf{g}(x):=(1+|x|^{2})^{-(d-2)/2}
be the Aubin–Talenti function such that
\mathsf{g}^{2^{*}}=\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 2.3
(
[
9
]
)

Let
d\geq 3
,
A>0
, and
p=2^{*}/2
. Then for any nonnegative
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p+1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
such that
\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
and
|x|\,f^{p}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
,

\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\!(1,x,|x|^{2})\,f^{2^{*}}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\!(1,x,|x|^{2})\,\mathsf{g}}\,dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{r>0}r^{d}\!\int_{|x|>r}f^{2^{*}}\,dx\leq A\,,

then we have

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}^{2}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\mathscr{C}\,\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\big|(p-1)\,\nabla f+f^{p}\,\nabla\mathsf{g}^{1-p}\big|^{2}}\,dx

for some explicit
\mathscr{C}>0
which depends only on
d
and
A
.

Extending the subcritical result of Corollary
2
to the critical case uses the introduction of a time delay, as shown in
(
9
, Chapter 6)
. One can also obtain a stability result in the subcritical range
m\in(m_{1},1)
, that is,
1<p<2^{*}/2
for Ineq. (
10
), but the statement is more delicate as several scales are involved. A remarkable feature in Theorem
2.3
is that we measure the distance to a specific Aubin–Talenti function and not to the manifold
\mathscr{M}
.

Summing up, we have obtained a construction of an explicit stability result using the
fast diffusion equation
and
entropy methods
based on a parabolic regularity theory made explicit, spectral estimates and various features of the
carré du champ
method. However, estimates of the stability constants are way smaller than the expected values of the optimal stability constants and are available only under the restriction (
15
) on the tails of the functions, which is inherent to the method, as one can prove that
t_{\star}
diverges if
A\to+\infty
in (
15
).

3
Explicit stability results for Sobolev and log-Sobolev inequalities, with optimal dimensional dependence

In this section, we just state the main results and refer the reader to
[
29
]
for detailed results and to
[
28
]
for a shorter introduction to the main tools. The most important fact is that the method gives an explicit value to the constant in the
stability
inequality (
2
), but again one can suspect that the lower bound is not very good, even if it has an optimal dimensional dependence.

Theorem 3.1
(
[
29
]
)

There is a constant
\beta>0
with an explicit lower estimate which does not depend on
d
such that for all
d\geq 3
and all
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
we have

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{\beta}{d}\,\inf_{g\in\mathscr{M}}\left\|{\nabla f-\nabla g}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})}^{2}\,.

(16)

Using the inverse stereographic projection we can rewrite the result on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
as

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\\
\geq\frac{\beta}{d}\,\inf_{G\in\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\left(\left\|\nabla F-\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\,\left\|F-G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,.

Here we take on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
the uniform probability measure. Let
v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
with compact support,
d\geq n
large enough and consider

u_{d}(\omega)=v\Big(\tfrac{1}{r_{d}}\,\omega_{1},\tfrac{1}{r_{d}}\,\omega_{2},\ldots,\tfrac{1}{r_{d}}\,\omega_{n}\Big)\,,\quad r_{d}=\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{2\,\pi}}

where
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{d+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d+1}
. If
d\gamma=e^{-\pi\,|x|^{2}}\,dx
denotes the Gaussian measure on
{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
, by taking the limit as
d\to+\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{d\to+\infty}d\left(\left\|\nabla u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,d\,(d-2)\left(\left\|u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\right)\\
=\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\pi\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|u|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}\right)d\gamma\,,

where the right-hand side is the deficit in the
Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality
of
[
40
]
, which is equivalent to (
11
). We can also let
d\to+\infty
in (
16
). The delicate part is to bound the coefficients
(a,b,c)
of the Aubin–Talenti functions which minimize the distance in (
2
) uniformly with respect to
d
. Because of the rescaling, the gradient term in the distance is lost and stability is measured only by a
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)
norm. See
[
29
]
for a detailed proof.

Corollary 3
(
[
29
]
)

With
\beta>0
as in Theorem
3.1
, we have

\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\pi\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|u|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}}\right)d\gamma\\
\geq\frac{\beta\,\pi}{2}\,\inf_{a\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\!,\,c\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|u-c\,e^{a\cdot x}|^{2}\,d\gamma\,.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
relies on three main steps.

1.

A sequence of nonnegative functions built using the competing symmetries of E. Carlen and M. Loss in
[
18
]
, which alternate a Schwarz symmetric decreasing rearrangement and a transformation which corresponds, up to the stereographic projection, to a rotation on the sphere. This step reduces the stability problem to a local problem, on a neighbourhood of
\mathscr{M}
. We also use a continuous Steiner symmetrization in the discussion of one of the cases and an argument of
[
21
]
.

2.

A Taylor expansion with explicitly controlled remainder terms. To obtain the
d
-dependence, one has to perform a delicate analysis which involves an argument of
[
36
]
.

3.

The result for sign-changing functions is deduced from the result for the positive and negative parts by a convexity estimate.

For full details on the proof of Theorem
3.1
, on has to refer to
[
29
]
. How much is lost in the first two steps is unclear, and this is why a proof based,
e.g.
, on a fast diffusion equation would be a significant added value. There are several results which complement Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3
. We know from
[
46
]
that
\beta<4\,d/(d+4)
. The equality case in (
2
) written for the optimal value of
\kappa_{d}
is achieved according to
[
47
]
. A stability result for the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality follows from the duality method of
[
16
]
and has been generalized in
[
20
]
to cover various exponents. This result also proves an explicit stability estimate for the fractional Sobolev inequality, which uses duality but otherwise follows the very same lines of proof as in
[
29
]
.

The case of the logarithmic Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality is also obtained in
[
17
]
using duality with the Onofri inequality, whose explicit stability has been obtained in
[
41
]
by other methods.

4
More explicit stability results for the logarithmic Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

Here the goal is to reconciliate the flow methods of Section 2 with stability estimates without restrictions as in Section 3 and get estimate which may have the right order of magnitude compared to the optimal stability constants. Let us consider the family of
subcritical Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]:=\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)

(17)

for any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
, with
2^{*}:=\frac{2\,d}{d-2}
if
d\geq 3
and
2^{*}=+\infty
if
d=1
or
2
. If
d\geq 3
, the limit case
p=2^{*}
of (
17
) is the Sobolev inequality on the sphere. As another limit case when
p\to 2
, we obtain the
logarithmic Sobolev inequality on the sphere

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq d\,\mathcal{E}_{2}[F]:=\frac{d}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)

(18)

where
d\mu
is the uniform probability on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. These inequalities were obtained in
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
. While equality is always achieved by constant functions, the optimal constant
d
can be identified with the first positive eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Let
X_{1}
be the corresponding eigenspace of spherical harmonic functions and denote by
\mathrm{\Pi}_{1}
the orthogonal projection onto
X_{1}
. If
\varphi\in X_{1}
and
F_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon\,\varphi
, a simple Taylor expansion shows that
\left\|\nabla F_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F_{\varepsilon}]=O(\varepsilon^{4})
, so that there is no hope to control the deficit from below by the square of a distance based on usual norm, at least in the direction of
X_{1}
.

Theorem 4.1
(
[
12
]
)

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2^{*})
. There is an explicit stability constant
\mathscr{S}_{d,p}>0
such that, for any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\\
\geq\mathscr{S}_{d,p}\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla\mathrm{\Pi}_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}+\left\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{\Pi}_{1})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,.

This results is a consequence of the stability analysis of
[
38
]
, of explicit improvements of (
17
) and (
18
) obtained for instance in
[
30
,
32
]
based on
[
24
,
31
]
, which involve a generalized
carré du champ
method for the flow

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=u^{-p\,(1-m)}\left(\Delta u+(m\,p-1)\,\tfrac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right)

with exponents
m\in\big[m_{-}(d,p),m_{+}(d,p)\big]
where

m_{\pm}(d,p):=\frac{1}{(d+2)\,p}\left(d\,p+2\pm\sqrt{d\,(p-1)\,\big(2\,d-(d-2)\,p\big)}\right)\,,

and of spectral properties taken from the proof of
[
3
]
. As in Section
3
and with the same notation, we can consider the large dimensional limit.

Theorem 4.2
(
[
13
]
)

Let
n\geq 1
and
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
with compact support. For any
p\in(1,2)
, we have

\lim_{d\to+\infty}d\left(\left\|\nabla u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\tfrac{d}{2-p}\left(\left\|u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|u_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\right)\\
=\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2-p}\left(\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}-\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)}^{2}\right)\,.

In Theorem
4.1
we have the satisfaction to obtain a stability result which distinguishes the
X_{1}
eigenspace, is based on a fast diffusion equation and does not involve any restriction on the function space, but the estimate is not uniform as
p\to 2^{*}
, as could be expected from Theorem
3.1
.

There is still much more to understand, particularly concerning (
18
). In Corollary
3
, the stability of the
Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality
is measured only by a
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)
norm. It is known from
[
44
,
43
]
that an
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{n},d\gamma)
stability result cannot be expected without additional conditions. The quest for optimal conditions is open but as an illustration, we can conclude with a last result.

Theorem 4.3
(
[
14
]
)

Let
d\geq 1
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is some explicit
\mathscr{C}>1
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that, for any
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},d\gamma)
with

\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{\left(1,x\right)\,|u|^{2}}\,d\gamma=(1,0)\,,\quad\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}\,e^{\,\varepsilon\,|x|^{2}}}\,d\gamma<\infty

for some
\varepsilon>0
, then we have

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},d\gamma)}^{2}\geq\frac{\mathscr{C}}{2}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}\,\log|u|^{2}}\,d\gamma\,.

Moreover, if
u
is compactly supported in a ball of radius
R>0
, we know that the inequality holds with
\mathscr{C}=1+\frac{\mathscr{C}_{\star}(\mathsf{K}_{\star})-1}{1+R^{2}\,\mathscr{C}_{\star}(\mathsf{K}_{\star})}
,
\mathscr{C}_{\star}(\mathsf{K})=1+\frac{1}{432\,\mathsf{K}}
and
\mathsf{K}_{\star}:=\,\max\big(d,\frac{(d+1)\,R^{2}}{1+R^{2}}\big)
.

Here the result is stated in the form of an improved inequality under orthogonality constraints and we use the heat flow case corresponding to the limit
m=1
of the fast diffusion flow. The proof is based on a stability result of
[
37
]
for a restricted class of functions, which contains the solution of the heat flow after an initial time layer recently determined in
[
19
]
. For a compactly supported initial datum, we obtain a very explicit expression using a result of
[
48
]
. One can certainly object that
1/432\approx 0.00231481
does not provide us with a glorious improvement of the constant, but this is definitely a step in the understanding of
explicit
stability results and, as a consequence, better estimates of decay rates in nonlinear evolution problems.

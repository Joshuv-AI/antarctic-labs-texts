Splendidly Ignorant and Uncertain
Authors: Jason N. Katz, Nosheen Reza
Published: 2025

JCF-Intersections, Editor’s Page – Nov 2025

We interrupt your regularly scheduled reading to bring you the following public service announcement: on behalf of the Journal of Cardiac Failure-Intersections, we humbly ask you…rather, implore you…to be splendidly ignorant. You heard that correctly – eschew your inner know-it-all, embrace your imposter syndrome, and ask your burning questions! In science and medicine, we believe strongly that there is perhaps nothing more powerful than saying out loud, “I Don’t Know!” At a time when it seems that everyone has an ‘expert’ opinion about almost every topic, and at nearly every moment, saying “I Don’t Know” feels awfully important to us. In truth, these 3 words were almost certainly the impetus fueling many of cardiovascular medicine’s most important questions and subsequent discoveries. Though today a discussion of revascularization strategies in cardiogenic shock,1 beta blockade for chronic heart failure,2,3 or transcatheter interventions targeting inoperable valvular disease may seem commonplace,4 for each, an initial admission of ignorance (in the most positive sense of the word) was an instrumental first step towards meaningful change.

We find it not only valuable, but even necessary to acknowledge uncertainty. To our learners and early career investigators, we are here to tell you that this is a critical concession, and we expect that it will be foundational in helping to shape the formidable scientist that you hope to become. Ask your mentors, teachers, and senior colleagues. Uncertainty leads to curiosity, curiosity leads to questions, and questioning the world around us is what encourages learning and helps promote creativity and innovation. Indira Gandhi once said that “the power to question is the basis of all human progress.” Voltaire understood the value of inquiry when he argued to “judge a man by his questions, rather than by his answers.”

To be fully transparent with you all, the origins of our journal have been heavily influenced by our own relentless questions. We have been unafraid to say “I Don’t Know,” have felt empowered to query others (sometimes incessantly), and now hope to be a vehicle for showcasing your own explorations of uncertainty and critical thinking as a catalyst for growth in our discipline and its many intersections. Within the pages of this issue, you will see thought-provoking science that naturally began with a series of simple questions motivating the authors to pursue scholarly investigation. Some of these questions came from their daily work, some from challenging long-standing dogma, and some from healthy skepticism of the many things we do without clear justification. Like them, we want to encourage you to cultivate your ability to ask questions – a skillset, we maintain, that requires intentionality. Exercise those muscles through repetition and training. We want you to use our journal as a training ground for you and your work.

A popular metaphor goes, “as the island of our knowledge grows, so does the shore of ignorance.” There will always be more questions than answers because science is dynamic and evolving. We would argue, in this regard, that the questions may be even more important than the solutions they produce. Ask questions, write them down, acknowledge your own ignorance. Get to know other fields and don’t be afraid to ask questions there too. These are the fundamental seeds from which discovery will germinate and from which your passion for learning will endure.

The Antimicrobial Efficacy of New Antibiotic Combinations against Bacteria Associated with Endo-perio Lesions in Deciduous Teeth: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Prasanna T Dahake, Nilima Thosar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEndodontic infections in deciduous teeth disseminate rapidly into surrounding areas due to anatomical complexities enhancing the formation of endo-perio lesions. Inadequate treatment of such conditions usually leads to premature tooth loss, jeopardizing dental arch integrity.ObjectiveTo investigate the efficacy of clindamycin (C), metronidazole (M), doxycycline (D), and their combination CMD against bacteria associated with endo-perio lesions in deciduous teeth for topical application.Materials and methodsThe bacteria responsible for endo-perio lesions such as Eikenella corrodens (BAA 1152), Fusobacterium nucleatum (ATCC 25586), Porphyromonas gingivalis (ATCC 33277), Prevotella intermedia (ATCC 25611), Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC 12598), Tannerella forsythia (ATCC 43037), Veillonella parvula (ATCC 10790). Introduction

The bizarre internal geometry of deciduous root canals along with anatomical complexities1 permits the bidirectional transition of the microbial and other contaminants across the endodontic and periodontal spaces.2 The microorganisms entombed into such endodontic spaces are seldom eradicated by routine cleaning and shaping leading the conditions toward more complex endo-perio lesions. Microbial biofilms with aerobic and anaerobic microorganisms are mainly responsible for such occurrences.3 The bacterial species predominantly detected in endo-perio lesions include Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans, Bacteroides forsythus, Eikenella corrodens, Fusobacterium nucleatum, Porphyromonas gingivalis, Prevotella intermedia,4 as well as Treponema denticola, Staphylococcus aureus5 and Veillonella parvula.6

In deciduous teeth, endo-perio lesions may not become obvious due to their shorter lifespan, compared with the permanent one. Although such conditions demonstrate variable clinical and radiographic findings, inadequate treatment usually leads to premature tooth loss, jeopardizing dental arch integrity. To preserve such deciduous teeth and maintain arch integrity, endodontic therapy aided with the comprehensive root canal disinfection through mechanical and chemical means is one of the mandatory factors.7 Utilizing merely one system does not effectively eliminate all the microbial inhabitants but advocates the use of chemically derived nonantimicrobial agents such as calcium hydroxide, or its modifications,8 chlorhexidine, CMCP, formocresol as intracanal medication, pulpotomy agent, or with other agents as obturating materials.9

Despite many advantages, such materials demonstrate few shortcomings, such as calcium hydroxide causing premature idiopathic deciduous root resorption,10 chlorhexidine precipitating insolubly on root dentin with discoloration and cytotoxicity at higher doses.11 Nonspecific antimicrobial agents suppress vegetative microbial cells leaving behind nonvegetative forms to reestablish in presence of favorable conditions.12 The inadequacies of nonantimicrobial agents necessitate the utilization of antimicrobial agents topically to eliminate virulent microbial strains, restricting antibiotic resistance and therapeutic failures.13 Hence, the present microbiological study was planned to investigate the efficacy of clindamycin (C), metronidazole (M), doxycycline (D), and their combination CMD against bacteria associated with endo-perio lesions in deciduous teeth for topical application.

Hypothesis

The new combination CMD has better antimicrobial efficacy than individual antimicrobial agent clindamycin (C), metronidazole (M), and doxycycline (D) against tested bacterial species for topical application.

Materials and Methods

After gaining Institutional Ethical Committee Approval no: DMIMS(DU)/IEC/2015-16/1744. Study duration: 09-2015 to 12-2016.The study was conducted at the Department of Pedodontics and Department of Microbiology, utilizing standard strains of bacteria and analytical grade antimicrobial agents (purity assay > 95%).

Test Materials Used

Metronidazole (M) was procured from MP Biomedicals LLC France, while clindamycin (C) and doxycycline (D) were purchased from HiMedia Labs Pvt. Ltd., Mumbai, India.

Bacterial Strains Used

The bacterial strains used in this study were derived from ATCC (American Type Culture Collection, Microbiologics Inc., St. Cloud, Minnesota, United States):

Preparation of Microbial Inocula

All the lyophilized bacterial cells were revived by adding them to sterile BHI (brain heart infusion) broth under strictly anaerobic conditions (10% H2, 10% CO2, 80% N2) using MIC anaerobic workstation (KIM-AIC-100-SP, KIM Microsystems, Thane, MH, India) to avoid cross-contamination followed by incubation at 37°C for 24 hours. Each bacterial isolate was further diluted to prepare bacterial suspensions with their turbidity adjusted to 0.5 McFarland standards comprising approximately 1 × 10 CFU/mL.14

Preparation of Stock Solutions of Antibacterial Agents

The stock solutions of all the individual antimicrobial agents, as well as combination CMD, were prepared as suggested previously. To achieve a concentration of 1000 mg/mL, 1 mg of antibacterial powder was added to 10 mL sterile double deionized distilled water and mixed adequately at room temperature to obtain a homogeneous solution. All the stock solutions were kept in a refrigerator at a temperature range of 4–8°C in an airtight light-proof bottle until further use to prevent oxidation of the active agents.15

Determination of Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC99)

The MIC99 of all the test agents was determined as per CLSI guidelines through the serial dilution method. From each stock solution, 1 mL of test agent was added to a test tube and diluted serially with BHI broth in a two-fold manner from 1000 to 0.2 μg/mL, respectively. The last MIC tube without any test agent was tagged as NC (negative control), thus making a total of 14 MIC tubes. A total of 100 μL of previously prepared bacterial aliquots were added to all the MIC tubes. The contents of the test tubes were mixed homogeneously and incubated under a standard anaerobic environment (10% H2, 10% CO2, 80% N2) using a Gas-Pak system (Anaero-Gas-Pak 3.5 L, HiMedia India, Pvt. Ltd., Mumbai, India), in an anaerobic incubator (Cerelab Technology, Howrah, West Bengal, India), at 37°C for 24–48 hours. The MIC99 of all the antibacterial agents was confirmed through a spectrophotometer (Orion™, Aqua-Mate 8000 UV-Vis, Thermo-Fisher Scientific, United States) at an optical density (OD600: 0.6–0.7). The lowest concentration in the MIC tube showing no bacterial growth was tagged as MIC99 for the drug for that bacterium. The procedure was repeated three times to minimize errors.16

Determination of Minimum Bactericidal Concentration (MBC99)

The MBC99 of all the test agents was determined through the inoculation of MIC99 broth. A total of 5 μL of MIC broth from a tube showing no growth were streaked on blood agar and incubated for 24–48 hours at 37°C anaerobically. The lowest concentration of this broth without any bacterial growth in the form of CFU was tagged as the MBC99 of the agent for that bacterium.14

Statistical Analysis

The data for each test agent and bacteria were subjected to statistical analysis using descriptive statistics with SPSS for Windows, Version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, United States). The MIC99 and MBC99 values were expressed as mean ± SD (standard deviation). The data were examined for normality of the distribution using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For intergroup comparison of the MIC99 and MBC99, Kruskal–Wallis (nonparametric ANOVA) test was used. The intragroup pairwise comparison was done using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (p < 0.05).

Results

The study presented the antibacterial efficiency of the test agents employing MIC and MBC assay. Figure 1 shows MIC99 of various test agents against bacterial species. P. gingivalis was inhibited by clindamycin at 3.64 ± 0.45 mg, followed by S. aureus at 3.49 ± 0.51 mg, while F. nucleatum and V. parvula were inhibited at 0.60 ± 0.20 mg. P. intermedia and T. forsythia were inhibited at 0.47 ± 0.12 mg, whereas E. corrodens showed inhibition at 0.40 ± 0.00 mg. Against metronidazole, S. aureus showed inhibition at 56.67 ± 764 mg, whereas E. corrodens and V. parvula showed inhibition at 55.00 ± 13.23 mg and 30.00 ± 5.00 mg, respectively. P. gingivalis showed inhibition at 0.97 ± 0.21 mg, T. forsythia at 0.87 ± 0.12 mg, P. intermedia at 0.70 ± 0.20 mg, and F. nucleatum at 0.53 ± 0.15 mg. Against doxycycline, S. aureus was inhibited at 6.92 ± 0.94 mg, whereas other bacteria were inhibited at the concentration of 0.33 ± 0.12 mg. When combination CMD was evaluated, it demonstrated inhibition of S. aureus at 2.13 ± 0.33 mg, while P. gingivalis exhibited inhibition at 0.75 ± 0.12 mg, and P. intermedia, F. nucleatum, V. parvula, E. corrodens, and T. forsythia demonstrated inhibition within the range of 0.27 ± 0.17 to 0.20 ± 0.10 mg.

P. gingivalis was eliminated at 45.33 ± 3.06 mg, followed by P. intermedia at 19.67 ± 1.53 mg. When metronidazole was evaluated, it was observed that T. forsythia was eliminated at 213.33 ± 2.08 mg, followed by S. aureus (137.00 ± 2.00 mg), E. corrodens (81.67 ± 2.08), and P. gingivalis (56.00 ± 4.36). V. parvula was eliminated at 54.67 ± 3.51 mg and P. intermedia at 32.33 ± 2.08 mg. For doxycycline, E. corrodens required the highest concentration (216.00 ± 3.61 mg), followed by T. forsythia (147.33 ± 32.15), S. aureus (146.33 ± 3.79 mg), and F. nucleatum (93.67 ± 2.52 mg). V. parvula was eliminated at 44.33 ± 4.04 mg, followed by P. intermedia at 39.33 ± 5.13 mg and P. gingivalis at 34.00 ± 2.65 mg. When the efficacy of CMD was evaluated against all the tested bacteria, it was observed that the highest concentration was required to eliminate S. aureus (83.33 ± 7.12 mg) followed by E. corrodens (42.21 ± 3.14 mg), T. forsythia (31.33 ± 4.12 mg), and F. nucleatum (21.33 ± 1.27 mg). V. parvula was eliminated at 21.66 ± 1.36 mg.

Discussion

Ingress of microbial species into the pulp spaces initiates endodontic infections, which through organized communal growth in the extracellular matrix (ECM), establish a complex microbial biofilm. Owing to such synergistic associations, they impose a driving force to alter the course of pulpal disease along with the treatment plan too.5 Dissemination of the microbial cells from such communities through anatomical and nonphysiological communications17 comprises mixed anaerobic ecology. The bacterial species commonly seen in such ecologies are obligate or facultative anaerobic, Gram-negative/positive, coli, or bacilli. The frequently detected bacteria in endo-perio lesions are F. nucleatum, P. gingivalis, P. intermedia, S. aureus, T. forsythia, and V. parvula.18–20 Of these, Gram-negative anaerobic bacteria are responsible for the appearance of typical clinicopathologic features of endo-perio lesions.21 These biofilm enmeshed bacteria can thrive in tenacious endodontic spaces.

Eikenella corrodens exhibits its virulence through lipopolysaccharides (LPS), outer membrane proteins (OMP), adhesins, and the exopolysaccharide layer, along with the production of the antagonistic substances.22F. nucleatum coaggregates with early plaque colonizers bridging opportunities for late colonizers. It initiates genetically expressed physicochemical changes in deep gingival tissues by altering the local acidic pH to alkaline one, which helps establish other pathogenic successors. It also favors attachment to host epithelial cells, collagen, and heterologous bacterial genera using cell-surface properties.23P. gingivalis releases collagenase, endopeptidase, such as gingipain, human albumin degradation, and develop antibiotic resistance.24 It degrades host cell transferrin, and MMP (matrix metalloproteins), hindering wound healing, while the capsular polysaccharides reduce proinflammatory cytokine and neutrophil migration in inflammatory lesions.25

Prevotella intermedia, along with Campylobacter rectus and F. nucleatum, favors dysbiosis of biofilm to red-complex enhancing colonization of P. gingivalis, T. forsythia, and T. denticola.26 Along with degradative enzyme activation, it improves the subgingival survival of other bacteria by proinflammatory cytokines, proteinases, and MMP liberation.27S. aureus invades tissues as well as implanted biomaterials through polysaccharide intercellular adhesion (PIA) and ica operon-encoded enzymes. It opens gateways to other bacteria for disease progressions by producing histolytic enzymes to dissociate tissues.28T. forsythia induces host cell apoptotic activity, trypsin-like and PrtH proteases expression, with sialidases, alpha-D-glucosidase, and N-acetyl-beta-glucosaminidase. It possesses hemagglutinin, bacterial S-layer components, with the ability of methylglyoxal formation.29V. parvula colonizes other pioneer bacteria through metabolic symbiosis and coaggregation utilizing streptococcal dextran to adhere to the tooth surface, and generate a large quantity of LPS.30 It also participates in caries and endodontic disease progression along with other periodontal pathogens to contribute to endo-periodontal disease. Despite their distinctive individual features, these bacteria exhibit entirely unalike characteristics in complex microecology due to either synergetic interactions, or through cellular glycoprotein, mucopolysaccharides, and other extracellular proteins utilization.31

The endodontic infections in deciduous teeth disseminate rapidly into surrounding areas due to the thin pulpal floor, a high number of accessory canals, and broader dentinal tubules offering high permeability. These factors enhance the initiation of endo-perio lesions at root bifurcations which further progresses to furcation lesion establishment rapidly,32 causing cemental tissue resorption. The endo-perio lesions thus formed, usually go unnoticed either due to inadequate reporting or the shorter lifespan of the deciduous teeth in the oral cavity. However, elimination of all pathogens, along with tissue debris and bacterial byproducts is mandatory to preserve such teeth in the oral cavity till their course. Currently, biomechanical and biochemical cleansing with systemic antibiotics is the preferred therapeutic modalities for deciduous root canal disinfection. However, the systemic antibiotics display an insignificant role to eliminate endopathogens from chronically infected pulpless teeth.33 On the contrary, perforations at radicular furcation sealed with bioinductive materials, pose restricted antimicrobial efficacy to eliminate all the microbes as compared to antimicrobial agents.34 To encounter such flaws, the concept of topical drug application, such as lesion sterilization and tissue repair (LSTR) evolved instead of the usage of nonspecific antiseptic and antibacterial drugs,35 which eliminate almost all the microbial forms sheltered in surrounding tissues.36 Among the antibiotics used for such conditions, penicillin is the most common drug of choice. On the contrary, fewer antimicrobial agents are also recommended for such purposes as lincosamide derivative (such as clindamycin and erythromycin), nitroimidazole derivatives (such as metronidazole), and tetracyclines (such as minocycline and doxycycline) alternatively.37

The test agents used in the present study has multiple properties than antibacterial effects. Clindamycin, in addition to its broad-spectrum antibacterial activity, shows the extended postadministration effect and prevents early bacterial recolonization. Due to its high diffusibility in the bone, it imposes a negative influence on new biofilm formation. Likewise, it deteriorates microbial toxin production, and augment bacterial opsonization by enhancing the intracellular killing capacity of polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs) at subinhibitory dose.38 Metronidazole eradicates protozoa, Gram-positive as well as Gram-negative anaerobic bacteria due to microbial DNA and enzymatic degradation.39 It helps to modulate both innate and adaptive immune responses. When applied topically to immune cells, it restrains pro-inflammatory cytokines production through interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, IL-8, IL-12, and LPS-induced TNF-α (tumor necrosis factor) modulation.40 Along with other antibiotics, it synergizes anti-inflammatory responses in macrophages through IL-10 production and downregulates the expression of ECM genes in the native fibroblast creating a conducive microenvironment for periradicular tissue healing.41 However, its effectivity diminishes secondary to rapid resistance development by facultative anaerobes if doses are altered qualitatively or quantitatively. Hence, metronidazole is advisable in combination therapy than a single agent to control endo-odontogenic infections. Besides its wide spectrum antibacterial activity against endopathogens, doxycycline impedes monocytic TREM-1 (triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 1) expression affecting infectious inflammatory response.42 At subinhibitory doses, it improves overall anti-inflammatory and host responses through the reduction in monocytic pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-8), besides MMP-8 and MMP-9 activity, the key enzymes in periodontal tissue destruction.43 Through downregulation of RANKL (receptor activator of NF-κB ligand)-stimulated mononuclear cells and M-CSF (macrophage colony-stimulating factor) doxycycline constrains osteoclastogenesis and bone resorption.44

Based on the data from previous studies,45–47 this experiment was proposed to investigate the antibacterial efficacy of clindamycin (C), metronidazole (M), and doxycycline (D) as an individual agent and their combination CMD to target few facultative and obligate anaerobes responsible for endo-perio lesions. In contrast to the limitations of individual agents, to attain high microbial eradication without drug resistance, all the agents were combined at a ratio of 5:5:1, respectively based on antimicrobial assay results. This assay elucidates a test agent's potency to inhibit or eradicate microbial growth. From the data obtained in this study, it is evident that clindamycin inhibited all the bacterial species efficiently at a mean range of 0.4–4 µg/mL, higher than the previous report.48 Instead, metronidazole needed a high mean concentration at a range of 0.53–55 µg/mL and least sensitivity against experimental bacteria, similar to the previous report.49 Doxycycline inhibited all the bacteria at a mean range of 0.3–7 µg/mL, whereas CMD demonstrated inhibition at a very low mean concentrations range of 0.2–2.13 µg/mL. The intergroup comparisons of the MIC99 among individual agents showed a statistically non-significant difference (p > 0.05), while the comparison of CMD against individual agents showed a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05). Similar data presentations were observed about the MBC99 of all test groups. The intergroup comparisons among individual agents revealed statistically nonsignificant differences (p > 0.05) in contrast to the comparisons between CMD and other individual agents (p < 0.05); thus, the hypothesis is accepted. The possible justification for such an outcome can be explained through the high potential of the combination CMD along with synergistic and augmentative effects to cause multilocational damage to bacterial cells, which ultimately fail to survive and multiply. Besides, multiple actions of multiple agents happen concomitantly achieving the larger objective of bacterial elimination. The antimicrobial actions of these agents at various levels with varying sequences may have produced the observed results. Despite the in vitro model of study execution, with limited strains of bacteria, and without comparisons against nonantibiotic intracanal medicaments, the data has demonstrated encouraging results.

Conclusion

The present study concludes that an appropriate antibacterial agent should be selected based on its effectiveness. The newly formulated combination CMD can effectively eliminate bacteria associated with endo-perio lesions in deciduous teeth to decrease therapeutic failures, promote endodontic lesion haling, and increase the survival of the teeth in the dental arch. However, further studies are warranted with more bacterial strains and in vivo experimental models for its clinical relevance.

Primal and Dual Characterizations for Farkas type Lemmas in Terms of Closedness Criteria
Dinh N, GobernaM.A, VolleM

Abstract
_This paper deals with the characterization, in terms of closedness of certain sets regarding other sets, of Farkas lemmas determining when the upperlevel set of a convex function f f contains a set of the form C ∩ 𝔸 − 1 ​ ( D ) , C\cap\mathbb{A}^{-1}\left(D\right), where C C and D D are convex sets (not necessarily cones) in locally convex spaces X X (with topological dual X ′ X^{\prime} ) and Y , Y, respectively, while 𝔸 \mathbb{A} is a continuous linear operator from X X to Y Y . More in detail, each of the mentioned characterizations of Farkas type lemmas consists in the closedness of certain subset of either one of the ”primal” spaces X × Y × ℝ X\times Y\times\mathbb{R} and Y × ℝ , Y\times\mathbb{R}, or of the ”dual” space X ′ × ℝ X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R} , regarding some singleton set of the corresponding space. Moreover, the paper also provides an existence theorem for the feasible set C ∩ 𝔸 − 1 ​ ( D ) C\cap\mathbb{A}^{-1}\left(D\right) in terms of the closedness of certain subset of the dual space X ′ × ℝ X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R} regarding the singleton set formed by the null element. These results are illustrated with significant applications.

2
Primal closedness characterizations

Recall that
f:X\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
(
f\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{X}
in short) is a proper convex function. We consider the
vector-valued mapping
H:X\times Y\times\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}\nolimits f\longrightarrow X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}
such that

H\left(x,y,v\right)=\left(x-v,\mathbb{A}x-y,f\left(v\right)\right),

which is convex with respect to the cone
\left\{0_{X}\right\}\times\left\{0_{Y}\right\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.
Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{F}&:=H\left(C\times D\times\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}\nolimits f\right)+\left\{0_{X}\right\}\times\left\{0_{Y}\right\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
&=\bigcup\limits_{x\in C}\text{ }\left\{\left(x,\mathbb{A}x,0\right)\right\}+\bigcup\limits_{v\in\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}\nolimits f}\left\{\left(-v,0_{Y},f\left(v\right)\right)\right\}+\left\{0_{X}\right\}\times\left(-D\right)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\end{array}

is a nonempty convex subset of
X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F}
is a convex cone in
X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}.\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

Theorem 1

(1st characterization of Farkas’ Lemma)
.

Let

be proper convex,
C
(resp.

D
) be a nonempty convex subset of
X
(resp.
Y
). Then, one has
(\mathcal{A})\Longrightarrow(\mathcal{B})
(hence,
(\mathcal{A})\Longleftrightarrow(\mathcal{B})
) if and only if
\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F}
is closed regarding
\left\{\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)\right\}.

Proof.

Sufficiency
.
Assume
that
(\mathcal{A})
holds. We first prove that
\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F}
. Otherwise, by the
definition of
\mathcal{F},
there exist
{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 274$}}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)={{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 274$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 274$}}}\left[\left(x,\mathbb{A}x,0\right)+\left(-v,0_{Y},f\left(v\right)\right)+\left(0_{X},-d,{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 273$}}}\right)\right].

Clearly,

0_{X}=x-v,

and

Consequently,

with

in contradiction with

.

Since

is closed regarding

which is a nonempty closed convex
cone. Applying the Hahn-Banach Theorem, there exists a non-zero vector
\left(x^{\prime},{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 278$}}},s\right)\in X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\left\langle x^{\prime},0_{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 278$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 278$}}},0_{Y}\right\rangle-s=-s>{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 269$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 269$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 269$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 269$}}},

where

(\mathcal{B})
holds.

Necessity
. Assume
(\mathcal{A})\Longrightarrow(\mathcal{B})

and suppose that
\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F}.
We must prove that
\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)\notin\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F}}.

Since
\left(0_{X},0_{Y},-1\right)\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathcal{F},

statement
(\mathcal{A})
holds. Otherwise there exist
\overline{x}\in C,

\overline{d}\in D,

\mathbb{A}\overline{x}=\overline{d},
and

By definition of
\mathcal{F}

We have

in contradiction with

holds and, by hypothesis,

holds too.

it follows that

-\left\langle u^{\prime},x-v\right\rangle-\left\langle{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}},\mathbb{A}x-d\right\rangle\leq f\left(v\right)+{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 273$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 273$}}}.

(1)

Consider the continuous linear form

{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 280$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 280$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 280$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 280$}}}\left(u,y\right):=-\left\langle u^{\prime},u\right\rangle-\left\langle{{}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathchar 277$}}},y\right\rangle,\forall\left(u,y\right)\in X\times Y,

and let

There exists

Bipartite Graphs Are Not Well-Ordered by Bipartite Minors
Therese Biedl, Dinis Vitorino

Abstract
In [ 1 ] , Chudnovsky et al. introduced the bipartite minor relation , a partial order on the set of bipartite graphs somewhat analogous the minor relation on general graphs and asked whether it is a well-order. We answer this question negatively by giving an infinite set of 2 2 -connected bipartite graphs that are pairwise incomparable with respect to the bipartite minor relation. We additionally give two sets of infinitely many pairs of bipartite graphs: one set of pairs G , H G,H such that H H is a bipartite minor, but not a minor, of G G , and one set of pairs G , H G,H such that H H is a minor, but not a bipartite minor, of G G .

3
Bipartite Minors vs. Minors

It immediately follows from the definition of
\leq_{B}
that if
G
is a bipartite graph and
H
is a bipartite minor of
G
, then
H
is bipartite, cf.
[
1
, p. 221]
.
This, together with the precise characterisations of the bipartite graphs in some minor-closed classes in terms of sets of forbidden bipartite minors obtained in
[
1
]
(Theorems 2.2, 2.4, and 2.5), might lead one to hope that the bipartite members of a minor-closed class of graphs can generally be described in terms of sets of forbidden bipartite minors.
For this to be the case, every minor-closed class would have to be closed under taking bipartite minors of bipartite graphs.
This leads to the following question.

Question 1
.

Suppose
G
and
H
are bipartite graphs with
H\leq_{B}G
.
Does
H\leq_{M}G
necessarily hold?

Despite not having been explicitly asked, the authors of
[
1
]
were clearly aware of
1
and in fact answered it implicitly in two different parts of
[
1
]
:
On page 223 of
[
1
]
, it is pointed out that admissible contractions preserve neither linkless-embeddability nor embeddability into closed surfaces other than the plane.
Since these are minor-closed properties, there must exist graphs
G
and
H
with
H\leq_{B}G
, but
H\not\leq_{M}G
.
Additionally, Remark 2.3 of
[
1
]
states that the (bipartite) graph obtained from
K_{5}
by subdividing each edge precisely once has a
K_{3,3}
bipartite minor, though it has no subgraph homeomorphic to
K_{3,3}
(and hence has no
K_{3,3}
minor).

Once one explicitly formulates
1
, it is natural to think about its converse:
Is every bipartite graph
H
that is a minor of a bipartite graph
G
also a bipartite minor of
G
?
Unfortunately, this too is false.
Note that a forest has no cycles and hence no admissible contraction can be performed on a forest, i.e., a forest
F_{1}
is a bipartite minor of a forest
F_{2}
if and only if
F_{1}
is a subgraph of
F_{2}
.
However, the forests in
Figure

4
are pairwise incomparable with respect to the subgraph relation, but are from left to right increasingly ordered by the minor-relation, yielding infinitely many counterexamples to the proposed converse of
1
.
This is, however, a somewhat unsatisfactory answer since it relies on a complete lack of cycles.
What happens if we require any two vertices in
G
(or
H
) to be in a common cycle, i.e., that
G
(or
H
) is
2
-connected?

Question 2
.

Suppose
G
and
H
are
2
-connected bipartite graphs with
H\leq_{M}G
.
Is
H
necessarily a bipartite minor of
G
?

In Problem 2.7 of
[
1
]
, it was asked whether the bipartite minor relation is a well-order on the set of bipartite graphs.
Again, noting that the bipartite minor relation reduces to the subgraph relation on forests and that forests are not well-ordered by the subgraph relation implies a negative answer to this question.
However, one could once again wonder about what happens to the set of
2
-connected bipartite graphs.

Question 3
.

Is the bipartite minor relation
\leq_{B}
a well-order on the set of
2
-connected bipartite graphs?

In this note, we answer all questions negatively.
In fact, we construct infinitely many counterexamples to affirmative answers to
1
and
2
.
The insights we use to answer
2
immediately imply a negative answer to
3
and hence solve a stronger form of Problem 2.7 of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2

For all integers

Before proving

Since no cycle in a graph can contain vertices from two different blocks of the graph, we immediately get the following.

If

This immediately implies that all

3.3
Bipartite Minors and Well-Order

We finish our paper with a negative answer to
3
.

Theorem 3
.

The bipartite minor relation is not a well-order, not even on the set of
2
-connected bipartite graphs.

Proof.

It follows immediately from
Theorem

2
that

\left\{D(k,4,4)\mid k\text{ a positive even number at least four}\right\}

is a set of graphs that are pairwise incomparable with respect to the bipartite minor relation.
Clearly all graphs in this set are bipartite and
2
-connected.
Hence, the bipartite minor relation is not a well-order on the set of
2
-connected bipartite graphs.
∎

Despite being of some importance in our proof of
Theorem

2
, we conjecture that the number two plays no special role in this respect.

Conjecture 1
.

There exists no
\kappa
such that the class of
\kappa
-connected bipartite graphs is well-ordered by the bipartite minor relation.

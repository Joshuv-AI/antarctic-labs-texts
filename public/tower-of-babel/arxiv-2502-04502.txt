Two-dimensional topological quantum field theories of rank two over Dedekind domains
Fabian Espinoza, Mee Seong Im, Mikhail Khovanov

Abstract
. We give examples of Frobenius algebras of rank two over ground Dedekind rings which are projective but not free and discuss possible applications of these algebras to link homology.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11R04, 57K16, 18M05, 16L60.

1.
Introduction

By an
n
-dimensional topological quantum field theory (TQFT), we mean a monoidal functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathsf{Cob}_{n}\longrightarrow R\mathsf{-mod}
, where
\mathsf{Cob}_{n}
is the category of
n
-dimensional cobordisms between
(n-1)
-dimensional manifolds, which we will write as
(n-1)
-manifolds, that are either oriented or unoriented, and
R
is a commutative ring. The target category
R\mathsf{-mod}
is the category of
R
-modules.

It is easy to see that, for any
(n-1)
-manifold
N
, the module
\mathcal{F}(N)
is a finitely-generated projective
R
-module; see
[
GIK+23
, Proposition 2.1]
for
n=1
, and the same argument works for any
n
.

A one-dimensional oriented TQFT
\mathcal{F}
is given by such a finitely generated projective
R
-module
P
. Namely, if
P=\mathcal{F}(+)
, then its dual
P^{\ast}\cong\mathcal{F}(-)
, where
+
and
-
are points viewed as 0-manifolds with positive and negative orientation, respectively. To
cup
and
cap
cobordisms
[
GIK+23
]
, this TQFT assigns co-evaluation and evaluation maps, respectively. Consequently, the isomorphism classes of oriented one-dimensional TQFTs are in a bijection with finitely generated projective
R
-modules.

Topological quantum field theories are most commonly studied over a field
K
, with
\mathcal{F}(N)
a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space for any
(n-1)
-manifold
N
. At the same time, integral structures are often essential in a more refined analysis of TQFTs. For example, the Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev (WRT) 3D TQFT
[
Wit89
,
RT91
]
is defined over
\mathbb{C}
, with a root of unity parameter
q\in\mathbb{C}
. Integral structures in these TQFTs, see
[
CL05
]
and references therein, relate to
Lusztig–Kashiwara canonical bases in irreducible representations of quantum groups
[
Kas90
,
Kas91
,
Lus90
,
Lus10
]
. The integral structure also leads to the Habiro rings
[
Hab02
,
Hab04
,
GSWZ24
]
and to the conjectural expansions at
q=0
of the analytic extension of the WRT invariants into the unit disk. These expansions are expected to have integer coefficients which lift to the Euler characteristics of an unknown homology theory for 3-manifolds
[
GPV17
]
. In these developments, however, one does not study modifications of the WRT TQFT with state spaces of 2-manifolds being non-free modules over the ground ring.

Another notable example of integrality is the theory of invertible TQFTs. In an invertible
n
-dimensional TQFT, the object
\mathcal{F}(N)
associated to an
(n-1)
-manifold
N
is an invertible object of the target monoidal category, such as the category of line bundles over an algebraic variety, and the map that
\mathcal{F}
associates to each
n
-cobordism is invertible
[
SP24
,
Fre14
]
.

In this paper, we start to investigate two-dimensional TQFTs over a commutative ring
R
such that the state space
A
of a circle is not a free
R
-module. A 2D TQFT is determined by the commutative Frobenius algebra structure on the
R
-algebra
A=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
which is the state space of a circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
If
R
has no zero divisors (i.e.
R
is a domain), consider the field of fractions
K=K(R)
. Tensoring with
K
over
R
converts commutative Frobenius pair
(A,R)
to commutative Frobenius pair
(A_{K},K)
over the field
K
, where
A_{K}:=A\otimes_{R}K
.

We are looking for examples of commutative Frobenius
R
-algebras
A
which are projective but not free modules over
R
.
For simplicity and convenience, assume that
R
is a Dedekind ring. A Dedekind ring
\mathcal{O}
often admit projective non-free modules, and the supply of the latter is measured by the ideal class group
\mathsf{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
.
Examples of Dedekind rings include the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in a number field
K
and the ring of functions on a smooth irreducible algebraic curve.

Any projective
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
of rank
n
has the form
M\cong\mathcal{O}^{n-1}\oplus P
, where
P
is a rank one projective
\mathcal{O}
-module. The isomorphism classes of the latter are parametrized by the elements of the ideal class group
\mathsf{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
, which is an abelian group. If
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the ring of integers in a number field
K
, the ideal class group is finite. For ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
, viewed as projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules, we have
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
.

Let
A
be a commutative Frobenius algebra over a Dedekind ring
\mathcal{O}
. The algebra
A
is equipped with the unit element, given by a morphism
\iota:\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow A
, with commutative, associative and unital multiplication
m:A^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow A
and with a nondegenerate trace map
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
.

Define the dual
\mathcal{O}
-module
A^{\ast}:=\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(A,\mathcal{O})
. The map
\varepsilon
as above induces a map of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

(1.1)

{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}:A\longrightarrow A^{\ast},\ \ A\ni a\mapsto\varepsilon_{a}\in A^{\ast},\ \ \varepsilon_{a}(b):=\varepsilon(ab),\ \ b\in A.

The map
\varepsilon
is called
nondegenerate
if
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-modules. The trace
\varepsilon
is nondegenerate precisely when
A
is Frobenius over
\mathcal{O}
.

Dualize the multiplication to a map

A^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m^{\ast}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A)^{\ast}\cong A^{\ast}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A^{\ast}.

Since
A
is Frobenius,
\varepsilon
is nondegenerate, and
composing with
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}
results in the map

\Delta:A\longrightarrow A^{\otimes 2}
dual to the multiplication
m
in an appropriate sense:

(1.2)

There is an
\mathcal{O}
-module decomposition
A\cong\mathcal{O}^{n-1}\oplus P
for some projective rank one
\mathcal{O}
-module
P\in\mathsf{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
. Then
A^{\ast}\cong\mathcal{O}^{n-1}\oplus P^{\ast}
. An isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-modules
A\cong A^{\ast}
implies that
P\cong P^{\ast}
, so that
P\otimes P\cong\mathcal{O}
.

Corollary 1.1
.

For a commutative Frobenius
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
A
as above,
rank one projective
\mathcal{O}
-module
P
has order at most two in the ideal class group
\mathsf{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
.

In our search for projective non-free commutative Frobenius algebras
A
, we are thus limited to the case when
A\cong\mathcal{O}^{n-1}\oplus P
with
P\otimes P\cong\mathcal{O}
and
P\not\cong\mathcal{O}
. Thus,
P
is a rank one projective of order two in the ideal class group.

2.
Rank two TQFTs

2.1.
Integrality conditions and equations

We now look for rank two commutative Frobenius algebras
A
over a Dedekind ring
\mathcal{O}
with the field of fractions
K=K(\mathcal{O})
such that
A
is not a free
\mathcal{O}
-module.

Since
\mathsf{rk}_{\mathcal{O}}(A)=2
, we can decompose
A\cong\mathcal{O}\oplus\mu
, where projective module (or ideal)
\mu
has order two in
\mathsf{Cl}(K)
,
\mu\not\cong\mathcal{O}
,
\mu^{2}\cong\mathcal{O}
. We can assume that
\mu\subset\mathcal{O}
, write
\mu\mu=(z)
for some
z\in\mathcal{O}
, and interchangeably treat
\mu
as a projective
\mathcal{O}
-module and as an ideal of
\mathcal{O}
. Assume that

(2.1)

A\cong\mathcal{O}\,1\oplus\mu\,X,

where
1
and
X
are generators, and the multiplication is given by

(2.2)

X^{2}=aX+b,\ \ a,b\in K.

Computing the multiplication on
\mu
-multiples of
X
,

(u_{1}X)(u_{2}X)=u_{1}u_{2}aX+u_{1}u_{2}b,\ \ u_{1},u_{2}\in\mu,

tells us that
u_{1}u_{2}a\in\mu
and
u_{1}u_{2}b\in\mathcal{O}
for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mu
. Consequently,

a\in\mu^{-1}=z^{-1}\mu,\ \ b\in z^{-1}\mathcal{O},

and any such pair
(a,b)
turns
A
into a commutative unital
\mathcal{O}
-algebra with the above multiplication.

We next ask for a nondegenerate trace map
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
. The maps
\varepsilon
that are
\mathcal{O}
-linear are described by the following data:

\varepsilon(1)\in\mathcal{O},\ \ \varepsilon|_{\mu X}\ :\ \mu\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}.

The map
\varepsilon:\mu X\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
is given by the element
\varepsilon(X)\in\mu^{-1}=z^{-1}\mu
. Thus,
without the nondegeneracy condition, a trace map is given by a pair
(\varepsilon(1),\varepsilon(X))
with
\varepsilon(1)\in\mathcal{O}
and
\varepsilon(X)\in z^{-1}\mu
.

Together, the data of an algebra
A
with a trace
\varepsilon
is given by the quadruple

(2.3)

(a,b,\varepsilon(1),\varepsilon(X)),\ \ \ a\in z^{-1}\mu,\ \ b\in z^{-1}\mathcal{O},\ \ \varepsilon(1)\in\mathcal{O},\ \ \varepsilon(X)\in z^{-1}\mu.

Next, consider the nondegeneracy condition on the trace. First, write the map
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
) explicitly over
K
, using the dual basis
\{1^{\ast},X^{\ast}\}
for
A_{K}^{\ast}:=A^{\ast}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
. Here

1^{\ast}(1)=1,\ \ 1^{\ast}(X)=0,\ \ X^{\ast}(1)=0,\ \ X^{\ast}(X)=1.

Integrally,

(2.4)

A\cong\mathcal{O}\,1\oplus\mu\,X\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ A^{\ast}=\mathcal{O}1^{\ast}\oplus z^{-1}\mu X^{\ast}.

The map

(2.5)

\widetilde{\varepsilon}:A\longrightarrow A^{\ast}

in (
1.1
) should be an isomorphism.
Let us compute this map, first over the field of fractions
K
:

(2.6)

\widetilde{\varepsilon}(1)=\varepsilon(1)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X)X^{\ast},\ \ \widetilde{\varepsilon}(X)=\varepsilon(X)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X^{2})X^{\ast}=\varepsilon(X)1^{\ast}+(a\varepsilon(X)+b\varepsilon(1))X^{\ast},

or, in matrix notation,

(2.7)

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\varepsilon}(1)\\
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(X)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon(1)&\varepsilon(X)\\
\varepsilon(X)&\varepsilon(X^{2})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1^{\ast}\\
X^{\ast}\end{pmatrix}.

Let

(2.8)

M:=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon(1)&\varepsilon(X)\\
\varepsilon(X)&\varepsilon(X^{2})\end{pmatrix},\ \ \widetilde{\Delta}:={\sf det}(M)=\varepsilon(1)\varepsilon(X^{2})-\varepsilon(X)^{2}.

Necessarily,
\widetilde{\Delta}\not=0
.
The inverse map
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}:A^{\ast}_{K}\longrightarrow A_{K}
in these bases is given by the inverse matrix
M^{-1}
:

(2.9)

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(1^{\ast})\\
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(X^{\ast})\end{pmatrix}=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon(X^{2})&-\varepsilon(X)\\
-\varepsilon(X)&\varepsilon(1)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
X\end{pmatrix}.

Let us write down multiplication in
A_{K}
:

(2.10)

m:\ 1\otimes 1\mapsto 1,\ \ \ 1\otimes X\mapsto X,\ \ \ X\otimes 1\mapsto X,\ \ \ X\otimes X\mapsto aX+b1,

and transpose it to comultiplication in
A_{K}^{\ast}
:

(2.11)

m^{\ast}:\ 1^{\ast}\longrightarrow 1^{\ast}\otimes 1^{\ast}+bX^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast},\ \ \ X^{\ast}\longrightarrow 1^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast}+X^{\ast}\otimes 1^{\ast}+aX^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast}.

We have

\Delta=(\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1})\circ m^{\ast}\circ\widetilde{\varepsilon}

and

\displaystyle\Delta(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1})\circ m^{\ast}\circ\widetilde{\varepsilon}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1})\circ m^{\ast}(\varepsilon(1)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X)X^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1})(\varepsilon(1)\left(1^{\ast}\otimes 1^{\ast}+bX^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast}\right)+\varepsilon(X)\left(1^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast}+X^{\ast}\otimes 1^{\ast}+aX^{\ast}\otimes X^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1})\left(\varepsilon(1)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X)X^{\ast}\right)\otimes 1^{\ast}+\left(\varepsilon(X)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X^{2})X^{\ast}\right)\otimes X^{\ast}.

We compute

\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(\varepsilon(1)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X)X^{\ast})=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}(\varepsilon(1)(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X)+\varepsilon(X)(-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X))=1,

and

\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(\varepsilon(X)1^{\ast}+\varepsilon(X^{2})X^{\ast})=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(\varepsilon(X)(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X)+\varepsilon(X^{2})(-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X)\right)=X.

Thus,

\Delta(1)=1\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(1^{\ast})+X\otimes\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(X^{\ast})=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(1\otimes(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X)+X\otimes(-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X)\right)

and we arrive at the formula

(2.12)

\Delta(1)=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(\varepsilon(X^{2})1\otimes 1-\varepsilon(X)(1\otimes X+X\otimes 1)+\varepsilon(1)X\otimes X\right)

in the Frobenius
K
-algebra
A_{K}
. Rewrite this formula as

(2.13)

\Delta(1)=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(1\otimes(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X)+X\otimes(-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X)\right).

It tells us that the dual to the basis
(1,X)
of
A_{K}
is the basis

(2.14)

\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X,-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X\right).

Map
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}:A_{K}^{\ast}\longrightarrow A_{K}
on
K
-vector spaces should restrict to a map
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}:A^{\ast}\longrightarrow A
. From (
2.4
) this means

\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(1^{\ast}),\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(z^{-1}uX^{\ast})\in\mathcal{O}1\oplus\mu X,\ \forall u\in\mu.\

Equation (
2.9
) gives

(2.15)

\widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(1^{\ast})=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\left(\varepsilon(X^{2})1-\varepsilon(X)X\right),\ \ \widetilde{\varepsilon}\,^{-1}(z^{-1}uX^{\ast})=\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}z^{-1}u\left(-\varepsilon(X)1+\varepsilon(1)X\right)

and the integrality conditions are

(2.16)

\widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\varepsilon(X^{2})\in\mathcal{O},\ \ \widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}\varepsilon(X)\in\mu,\ \ \widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}z^{-1}u\varepsilon(X)\in\mathcal{O},\ \ \widetilde{\Delta}^{-1}z^{-1}u\varepsilon(1)\in\mu,\ \ \ \forall u\in\mu,

where
\widetilde{\Delta}
is given by (
2.8
). These conditions can be rewriten as

(2.17)

\varepsilon(X^{2})\in\widetilde{\Delta}\,\mathcal{O},\ \ \varepsilon(X)\in\widetilde{\Delta}\,\mu,\ \ \varepsilon(1)\in\widetilde{\Delta}z\,\mathcal{O}.

Let us go back to formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) and continue with the integrality constraints.
Integrally,
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(1)\in A^{\ast}
, and for
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(uX)\in A^{\ast}
,
u\in\mu
, one additionally needs

\varepsilon(uX)=u\varepsilon(X)1^{\ast}+u(a\varepsilon(X)+b\varepsilon(1))X^{\ast}\in\mathcal{O}1^{\ast}\oplus z^{-1}\mu X^{\ast},\ \ u\in\mu.

Note that
\varepsilon(X^{2})=a\varepsilon(X)+b\varepsilon(1)
.
Equivalently, we need
u\varepsilon(X)\in\mathcal{O}
, which holds due to (
2.3
), and
u(a\varepsilon(X)+b\varepsilon(1))\in z^{-1}\mu
since

ua\varepsilon(X)\in\mu z^{-1}\mu z^{-1}\mu=z^{-1}\mu

and

ub\,\varepsilon(1)\in\mu z^{-1}\mathcal{O}=z^{-1}\mu.

Next, for
\widetilde{\varepsilon}:A\longrightarrow A^{\ast}
to be an isomorphism, one needs
1^{\ast}
and
z^{-1}uX^{\ast}
for
u\in\mu
to be in the image of
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
.

I. For
1^{\ast}
to be in the image of
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
, one needs
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(c1+dX)=1^{\ast}
for some
c
and
d
.
That is,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{c1+dX}(1)=\varepsilon(c1+dX)=c\varepsilon(1)+d\varepsilon(X)=1

, one would likewise be forced into elements of order 2 in the ideal class group as summands of
\mathcal{F}(M)

M

M\cong(-M)

Returning to dimension two, one way to avoid this restriction is to add additional decorations to 1-manifolds and 2-cobordisms between them. For example, one can pick a group
G
and consider 2-cobordisms
S
equipped with a principal
G
-bundle over them. A oriented circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is then replaced by a pair
(\mathbb{S}^{1},c)
, where
c
is a conjugacy class in
G
. Reversing the orientation results in the pair
(\mathbb{S}^{1},c^{-1})
, which is diffeomorphic to
(\mathbb{S}^{1},c)
only if the conjugacy classes
c
and
c^{-1}
coincide. It would be interesting to investigate such decorated 2D TQFTs over Dedekind domains
\mathcal{O}
, including the special case
G=\mathsf{Cl}(\mathcal{O})
.

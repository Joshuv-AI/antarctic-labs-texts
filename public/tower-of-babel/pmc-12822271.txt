Influence of articular incongruency on coronal alignment after surgical fixation of lateral tibial plateau fractures
Authors: Jae Ang Sim, Han Soul Kim, Do Kyung Lee, Dong Sub Im, Byung Hoon Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe surgical treatment of the tibial plateau fracture aims for anatomic reduction and rigid fixation. A residual articular step-off more than 2mm in the weight-bearing zone has a high impact on developing posttraumatic osteoarthritis. However, the correlation between malreduction and malalignment has not been studied in depth. The purpose of this study was to investigate the correlation between postoperative articular congruency in different fracture zones in sagittal plane and the whole lower limb alignment after surgical treatment of the lateral tibial plateau fractures.Materials and methodsAmong 254 patients who were surgically treated for fractures of tibial plateau between January 2010 and December 2022 at a single center, 67 patients of all ages with lateral tibial plateau fractures (Schatzker type I-III) were selected and retrospectively reviewed. All patients underwent open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) via plate and screw construct with or without bone augmentation procedure. For measuring lower extremity alignment variables, intra-articular step-offs in mm and a new sagittal fracture zone classification (1-3) was applied to the lateral plateau post surgery and measured in simple x-ray radiographs and preoperative three-dimensional computed tomography. We stratified the fracture site into three zones in the sagittal plane.Malreduction was defined as articular step-off ≥ 2 mm. Malalignment was defined as a difference of more than 5° in hip-knee-ankle angle (HKA) to common physiologic values.ResultsHKA showed strong negative correlation to postoperative articular step-off (Spearman's ρ = -0.69; p < 0.001). postoperative valgisation of HKA was significantly associated with increased articular incongruency (β = -.96, p< 0.001), especially when step-offs ≥ 2 mm (β = -3.77, p < 0.001). Linear regression of articular step-off and fracture location did not show any significance. However, 13 of 24 malreduced fractures (54%) were in zone 2. When the association between fracture location and HKA was evaluated, only zone 2 showed significant effect on postoperative valgization (β = -2.68, p < 0.001). When controlling fractures in zone 1 and 3, articular incongruency located in zone 2 showed significant association with HKA valgization (β =-.775, p < 0.001).ConclusionThis study highlights the importance of minimizing the articular step-off to be less than 2 mm in order to prevent the valgus malalignment in treating lateral tibial plateau fractures.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09400-6.

Introduction

Tibial plateau fractures are intra-articular fractures of the knee joint accounting for 5–8% of all lower limb fractures [1]. Lateral tibial fractures take up to two-thirds of all tibial plateau fractures [2]. The aim of treatment is to achieve anatomical reduction of the fracture to restore the joint surface and minimize the risks of post traumatic arthritis, and to allow early mobilization of the joint using rigid fixation [3, 4]. However, based on data regarding the level of articular congruity that is needed to achieve an acceptable outcome and a low risk of posttraumatic osteoarthritis are controversial [5]. Some authors have reported acceptable degree of articular incongruency can range widely from 2 to 10 mm in the case of tibial plateau [6, 7]. Other studies have shown step-offs larger than 2 mm lead to higher joint contact pressure in lateral tibial plateau fractures [8, 9].

Residual articular incongruency (step-off or depression) after a tibial plateau fracture is a risk factor for post-traumatic osteoarthritis, but its direct link to long-term complications is complex and not fully substantiated by evidence, with joint stability, meniscus integrity, and overall coronal alignment being potentially more significant predictors of outcome. While restoring anatomical alignment is a primary goal to protect cartilage, some studies show that even significant residual incongruity may not prevent good functional outcomes, suggesting that maintaining joint stability and the correct mechanical axis of the limb is crucial for preventing the progressive loading abnormalities that lead to post-traumatic osteoarthritis [10, 11]. A biomechanical study has shown that valgus malalignment after tibial fracture leads to greater contact stress on the lateral plateau [12]. Assessment of a whole lower limb alignment is measured using hip-knee-ankle angle (HKA) in coronal plane with the knee joint in full extension during weightbearing. During full extension of a normal knee, tibiofemoral contact point on the lateral tibial plateau is positioned slightly anteromedial to the center of lateral articular surface [13]. We hypothesized that articular step-off in anterior or posterior sector of the lateral tibial plateau would alter the tibiofemoral contact point, affecting the whole lower limb alignment.

To our understanding, there is only a few studies on the correlation between articular incongruency and malalignment, and no studies have investigated the effect of fracture location on the whole lower limb alignment [8, 14]. The purpose of this study was to investigate (1) the correlation between postoperative articular congruency and malalignment and (2) the effect of different fracture zones in sagittal plane on the whole lower limb alignment after surgical treatment of the lateral tibial plateau fractures.

Materials and methods

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of our institution (GAIRB2024-041) prior to the study A retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data from consecutive patients who underwent surgical reduction of lateral tibial plateau fractures between January 2010 and December 2022 was conducted. For this study, 254 patients who were surgically treated for fractures of tibial plateau between January 2010 and December 2022 at a tertiary referral trauma center were retrospectively reviewed after approval by the institutional review board. Sixty-seven patients of all ages with lateral tibial plateau fractures (Schatzker type I-III) were included. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Schatzker type IV – VI (n = 46), (2) concomitant injury in the ipsilateral limb (n = 57), (3) previous fracture or replaced joint on the ipsilateral limb (n = 6), and absence of standing full-leg radiographs or less than one-year of follow-up (n = 59), (4) the presence of the concomitant fracture in the contralateral leg (n = 19).

Surgical procedures and postoperative rehabilitation

All diagnoses and surgeries were conducted by three experienced orthopedic surgeons who specialized in knee trauma and arthroplasty. All procedures aimed to restore alignment coronal sagittal tibial slope normal condylar width, and congruent articular surface. Surgical approach was chosen according to fracture pattern and configuration confirmed in preoperative three-dimensional computed tomography (3D-CT). For lateral split and split-depression fractures (Schatzker I–II), fixation was performed through an anterolateral approach using a lazy-S/hockey-stick incision centered over Gerdy’s tubercle. After elevating the anterior compartment musculature and iliotibial band, a submeniscal arthrotomy allowed assessment of the articular surface and meniscus, which was tagged as needed. The proximal tibia was “book-opened” at the fracture site, the fragment reduced and provisionally stabilized with Kirschner wires, and reduction confirmed under fluoroscopy prior to definitive plate-and-screw fixation (Fig. 1). For pure depression fractures (Schatzker III), arthroscopic assisted reduction and internal fixation were performed. With arthroscopic guidance, an anterior cruciate ligament drill guide directed a K-wire to the midpoint of the depressed fragment at a 40° angle to the tibial coronal axis; a cannulated tunnel was created over the wire, the fragment gently elevated, and a cannulated rafting screw placed over the K-wire after arthroscopic confirmation of reduction (Fig. 2). Fracture centers involving the posterolateral quadrant were sub-classified by Sim et al. [15] to guide approach. Depressions anterior to the fibular head were addressed via an extended anterolateral approach. Lesions between the anterior and posterior borders of the fibular head were treated through a trans-fibular approach with fibular head osteotomy for direct visualization and manipulation, followed by osteosynthesis using a tension-band construct. Depressions posterior to the fibular head were managed via a posteromedial approach with the patient prone. Additional augmentation was filled for subchondral voids in order to support articular reduction, including autocancellous bone graft or synthetic bone void filler [16, 17]. Pure beta-TCP crystal (Neo Bone®, SN Biologics Co., Ltd, Seoul, Korea) as synthetic bone void filler was used. The postoperative weight bearing was restricted to toe-touch weight bearing for 8–12 weeks depending on the overall fracture stability. Passive and active assisted range of motion exercises were started to achieve 90° knee flexion at 5 weeks and full range of motion at 8 weeks. Patients who had radiologic union were allowed to full weight bear at a mean time of 13 weeks. Bone union was confirmed using CT during the postoperative outpatient follow-up near postoperative 6 months.

Data collection

The baseline patient demographics such as age, sex, fracture classification, and type of operations were gathered. The Schatzker classification system was assessed in both simple radiograph and preoperative 3D-CT [18]. A routine knee 3D-CT scan produced a 2 mm slice. The measurements from 3D-CT included preoperative and postoperative 1-year intra-articular step-offs and fracture zone in coronal plane (Fig. 3). We further stratified the fracture site into three zones in the sagittal plane at the joint center of lateral plateau tibia (Fig. 4). Other variables assessed via simple radiographs included HKA, Medial proximal tibial angle (MPTA), and lateral distal femoral angle (LDFA) as shown in Fig. 5. The alignment variables were measured by calculating the difference between the injured and the contralateral leg on postoperative orthoradiogram taken at final follow-up at postoperative one year. Malreduction was defined as articular step-off ≥ 2 mm. Malalignment was defined as a difference of more than 5° in HKA. All radiographs were assessed using picture archiving and communication system (PACS) software (INFINITT Healthcare, South Korea). Two of the above-mentioned orthopedic surgeons along with fourth-year training resident participated in all the measurement. Any discrepancies were discussed until consensus, and the average of three different measurements were used for analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using PASW Statistics version 18.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Baseline patient demographics and radiographic measurements were assessed by Shapiro-Wilk test for distribution and by simple descriptive statistics using either mean ± standard deviation or median (IQR: interquartile range). Correlation between articular step-offs and radiographic alignment measurements were assessed by Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient and linear regression analysis. Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.05.

Results

Demographic data and radiographic measurements

Of 67 patients included in this study, 44 patients were male, and the median patient age was 49 (39–58). According to Schatzker classification, there were 10 type I fractures, 46 type II fractures, and 11 type III fractures. The median postoperative articular step-off was 0.8 (0–2.1) mm. The median MPTA, LDFA, and HKA were 1.3 (−0.8–3.1), −0.8 (−2.2–1.1), and − 2.0 (−3.5–0.2) mm, respectively. Twenty-one fractures were centered in Zone 1, 31 fractures were centered in Zone 2, and the rest were located at Zone 3. Twenty-four fractures (36%) were malreduced. Correlation between postoperative articular step-offs and lower limb alignment

Among radiographic lower limb alignment measures, only HKA showed strong correlation to postoperative articular step-off (Spearman’s ρ = −0.69; p < 0.001). Linear regression analysis was employed to test if articular step-off predict malalignment. It revealed that postoperative valgization of HKA was significantly associated with increased articular incongruency (β = − 0.96, p < 0.001), especially when step-offs ≥ 2 mm (β = −3.77, p < 0.001), as shown in Table 2; Fig. 6.

Correlation between fracture location and lower limb alignment

Linear regression of articular step-off and fracture location did not show any significance. However, 13 of 24 malreduced fractures (54%) were in zone 2. When the association between fracture location and HKA was evaluated, only zone 2 showed significant effect on postoperative valgization (β = −2.68, p < 0.001). When controlling fractures in zone 1 and 3, articular incongruency located in zone 2 showed significant association with HKA valgization (β = − 0.775, p < 0.001). Discussion

This study demonstrated two principal findings. First, postoperative articular incongruency in zone 2 area of lateral tibia plateau significantly predicted valgisation of the affected knee joint (β = −0.96, p < 0.001), particularly when articular depression exceeded 2 mm (β = −3.77, p < 0.001). Second, incongruency involving the middle one-third of the lateral tibial plateau in the sagittal plane (zone 2) showed a strong correlation with changes in the hip–knee–ankle (HKA) axis (β = −0.775, p < 0.001). Overall, malreduction was observed in 36% of cases, more than half (54%) of which were located in zone 2.

Residual step-off after tibial plateau fractures remains a common clinical problem, reported in up to 32% of cases in previous series [6]. While some authors have suggested that incongruency up to 10 mm can be tolerated [6, 7], several biomechanical studies have demonstrated that even 1–2 mm of depression reduces contact area and increases contact pressure [8, 9, 19]. Bai et al. reported that step-off was associated with valgus angulation and elevated contact pressure [8], whereas Singleton et al. observed that residual depression resulted in valgisation of the mechanical axis, though without clear clinical significance [7]. Maquet emphasized that malalignment following tibial condylar fractures alters load distribution and predisposes to post-traumatic arthritis [6, 7]. The current literature has mainly highlighted the importance of accurate reduction of posterolateral fragments because the alterations in the posterolateral corner may have a substantial impact on clinical outcomes in cases with ligamentous insufficiency such as ACL deficiency, although the posterolateral corner may not play a major role in the development of malalignment or osteoarthritis because located outside the primary weight-bearing joint zone [6, 15, 19–22].

However, our findings suggest that articular congruency in the central lateral plateau (zone 2) may be equally, if not more, critical, given its direct influence on coronal alignment. This observation extends previous biomechanical reports and provides in vivo radiographic evidence linking fracture location, step-off, and limb alignment.

These findings underscore the need for heightened surgical attention to articular incongruency in the middle one-third of the lateral tibial plateau. Step-offs exceeding 2 mm in this region should not be considered trivial, as they may significantly alter overall limb alignment. Thus, intraoperative strategies should prioritize direct visualization and anatomic reduction of zone 2, employing rafting screw constructs or buttress plating as necessary.

In this study, the β-tricalcium phosphate (β-TCP)-based bone substitute (Neo Bone®) showed no significant difference in maintaining articular reduction, promoting bone union, or preserving lower limb alignment compared with autogenous bone grafts. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that β-TCP can achieve comparable osteointegration and mechanical stability to autogenous bone grafts [30, 31].

From a broader perspective, valgus malalignment has been associated with increased tibiofemoral contact pressure, meniscal degeneration, and accelerated osteoarthritis after meniscectomy, osteotomy, or fracture fixation [10–12, 23–29]. Bick et al. proved evidence of progressive overall meniscal degeneration in the lateral compartment via follow-up MRI examination [26–28]. Meniscal tear is combined in 55% of patients with tibial plateau fractures [24, 25]. Torn meniscus knee joints have apparently smaller stressed contract area than healthy knee joints under any flexion angles, and have larger contact pressure [29].Thus, our findings provide further evidence that achieving congruency in zone 2 is essential not only for immediate fracture stability but also for long-term joint preservation.

Current evidence suggests that restoration of articular congruency in tibial plateau fractures remains difficult despite introduction of numerous techniques, especially as progressive subsidence of articular fragment may occur even after rigid fixation. We evaluated an articular incongruency at postoperative one year to consider the possibility of gradual postoperative subsidence. Despite the dedicated effort to directly visualize and manipulate articular fracture site for rigid fixation, the median articular step-off of 0.75 (0–2.08) mm remained at postoperative one year. An anatomic articular reduction, especially, is technically demanding in the posterolateral compartment of tibial plateau. A biomechanical study showed even posterior impressions of the posterolateral compartment greater than a 1-mm step-off lead to large decrease of contact area and increase of contact pressure [19]. However, in this study, degree of articular step-off in the posterolateral compartment did not show significant correlation with the coronal alignment change in the full weightbearing orthoradiogram. This is self-explanatory because tibiofemoral contact point on the lateral tibial plateau is positioned slightly anteromedial to the center of lateral articular surface in standing position with fully extended knee [13]. Post-traumatic arthritis is diagnosed by assessing joint space narrowing in the full weightbearing orthoradiogram and is characterized by pain aggravated in weightbearing situation. The knee joint is a dynamic joint in which tibiofemoral contact point continuously changes throughout the whole range of motion. In this study, the articular incongruency in zone 2 showed significant linear correlation with HKA valgisation, and the majority of malreduced fracture (n = 13/24; 54%) were located in zone 2. Therefore, a surgeon must pay closer attention to an articular reduction of the center of the lateral compartment that affects the coronal alignment of the whole lower limb, rather than focusing too much on the recently-highlighted posterolateral compartment that accompanies the risk of peroneal nerve injury during surgical manipulation via posterolateral approach with fibular head osteotomy after common peroneal nerve dissection.

This study has several limitations. First, it is retrospective in design and includes a relatively small sample size. Second, clinical outcomes such as pain, function, or progression to osteoarthritis were not assessed, limiting the ability to directly correlate malreduction and malalignment with long-term prognosis. Third, surgical technique heterogeneity and surgeon variability may have influenced reduction quality. The participation of multiple surgeons may be considered a limitation, as variations in surgical technique could potentially influence outcomes. However, this study was not designed to evaluate clinical outcomes or functional scores, but rather to analyze postoperative radiological measurements. Therefore, this limitation does not compromise the interpretation of the study’s findings. Nonetheless, if clinical outcomes were to be assessed, it might be reasonable to assume that radiological parameters or results could indeed be influenced by surgical technique variations.

This study contributes to the understanding of lateral tibial plateau fracture management by quantitatively linking zone 2 incongruency with coronal malalignment. These results suggest that surgical strategies should prioritize accurate reduction of the central lateral plateau, alongside consideration of posterolateral fragments, to optimize long-term outcomes and minimize the risk of valgus malalignment.

Conclusion

Tibial plateau fractures are surgically challenging even for trained orthopedic surgeons. This study highlights the importance of minimizing the articular step-off to be less than 2 mm to prevent the valgus malalignment. Especially, mid one-third lateral plateau tibial articular incongruency showed significant correlation with HKA valgisation, suggesting that surgeons must attentively address articular reduction in zone 2.

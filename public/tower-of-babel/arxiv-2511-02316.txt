Favorite sites of one-dimensional asymmetric simple random walk
Guangshuo Zhou, Zechun Hu, Renming Song

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study favorite sites of one-dimensional asymmetric simple random walks. We show that almost surely, for any fixed integer
r\geq 1
, “
r
favorite sites” occurs infinitely often.
We also give the asymptotic growth rate of the number of favorite sites.

Keywords:
Favorite site, local time, transient random walk.

MSC (2020):
60F15, 60J55.

1
Introduction and main results

Let
X_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,
be independent and identically distributed random variables with

\mathbf{P}(X_{1}=1)=p~~\text{and}~~\mathbf{P}(X_{1}=-1)=1-p=:q.

For any
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
S_{0}=x
,
S_{n}=x+X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Then

\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a one-dimensional simple random walk starting from
x
. We will use
\mathbf{P}_{x}
to denote the law of this random walk and
\mathbf{E}_{x}
to denote the corresponding expectation. We will write
\mathbf{P}_{0}
and
\mathbf{E}_{0}
as
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{E}
, respectively. In this paper, we are concerned with
one-dimensional asymmetric simple random walks and so we will assume
p\neq q
. Without loss of generality, we will assume
p>q
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1
, define

\xi(z,n):=\#\{k:0<k\leq n,~S_{k}=z\},

where
\#D
denotes the cardinality of the set
D
.
\xi(z,n)
is called the local time of the random walk at
z
up to time
n
. Define
\xi(n)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}\xi(y,n)
. A site
z\in\mathbb{Z}
is called a favorite site at time
n
if
\xi(z,n)=\xi(n)
. The set

\mathcal{K}(n):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{Z}:\xi(z,n)=\xi(n)\right\}

is the set of all the favorite sites of the random walk at time
n
.
In this paper we study the local time and favorite sites of the one-dimensional
asymmetric random walk.

The study of the local time of transient random walks was initiated by the papers Dvoretzky-Erdős
[
5
]
and Erdős-Taylor
[
10
]
. These two papers examined properties of the symmetric simple random walk in dimensions
d\geq 3
.
Csáki et al.
[
2
]
proved several properties of the local time of the one-dimensional asymmetric simple random walk and showed that its behavior is similar to that of the simple symmetric random walks in
dimensions
d\geq 3
.

The study of the favorite site set
\mathcal{K}(n)
started with the papers Bass-Griffin
[
1
]
and Erdős-Révész
[
7
]
for symmetric simple random walks. In
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, Erdős and Révész posed the question whether
\#\mathcal{K}(n)=r
occurs infinitely often (i.o. for short) for
r\geq 3
for
d
-dimensional symmetric random walks. For
d\geq 3
, Erdős-Révész
[
9
]
showed that, almost surely,
\#\mathcal{K}(n)=r
i.o. for any integer
r\geq 1
. For one-dimensional symmetric random walks, the related question of favorite edges was first studied. Tóth-Werner
[
16
]
proved that almost surely there are only finitely many times at which there are four distinct favorite edges. Then Tóth
[
15
]
studied the favorite site problem for one-dimensional symmetric random walks and proved that eventually
\#\mathcal{K}(n)\leq 3
almost surely. However, the question whether three favorite sites occur infinitely often almost surely was open for nearly 20 years until Ding-Shen
[
3
]
settled this affirmatively. Similar phenomena for favorite edges were obtained by Hao et al.
[
12
]
. Recently, Hao et al.
[
13
]
proved that
\#\mathcal{K}(n)=3
i.o. for two-dimensional symmetric simple random walks and derived sharp asymptotics for
\#\mathcal{K}(n)
for
d
-dimensional symmetric simple random walks,
d\geq 3
.

The main goal of this paper is to prove the following results for one-dimensional asymmetric simple random walks.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any given integer
r\geq 1
, “
r
favorite sites” occurs infinitely often almost surely.

Theorem 1.2
.

Almost surely,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{K}(n)}{\log\log n}=-\frac{1}{\log(1-2q)}.

(1.1)

We end this section with some
notations
that will be used later in this paper:

\displaystyle\gamma:=1-2q,\quad h:=\frac{q}{p},\quad\lambda:=-\frac{1}{\log(2q)}

(1.2)

and

\displaystyle\theta:=-\frac{1}{\log\gamma},\quad\delta:=\frac{2\log\frac{2q+h^{1/2}}{1+h^{1/2}}}{\log(2q)}-1.

(1.3)

It is elementary to check that
q<\frac{1}{2}
is equivalent to
\delta>0
.

For any real numbers
a
and
b
, set
a\wedge b:={\rm min}\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b:={\rm max}\{a,b\}
. For any set
A
, we use
1_{A}
to denote the characteristic function of
A
.
For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
[r]
stands for the largest integer less than or equal to
r
.
For integers
0\leq i<j
, set
S_{(i,j)}=(S_{i+1},\ldots,S_{j-1})
and
S_{[i,j]}=(S_{i},\ldots,S_{j})
. We define
S_{[i,j)}
similarly.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect some

and

are given in Sections 3.

3
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

To prepare for the proof Theorem
1.1
, we first make some preparations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that for any
j=1,2,\ldots,n
,
\mathbf{P}\left(A_{j}\right)=c>0
. Then for any

k=1,2,\ldots,n-1
,
we have

\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}1_{A_{j}}>k\right)\geq c-\frac{k(k+1)}{2n}.

Proof.
For any
m=0,1,\ldots,n
, let
Q_{m}:=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}1_{A_{j}}=m\right\}
. Then

\sum_{m=0}^{n}m\mathbf{P}\left(Q_{m}\right)=\mathbf{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}1_{A_{j}}\right)=nc,

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}1_{A_{j}}>k\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(Q_{m}\right)\geq\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}\frac{m}{n}\mathbf{P}\left(Q_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=c-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{k}m\mathbf{P}\left(Q_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{k}m=c-\frac{k(k+1)}{2n}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of events and
c\in(0,1]
. If
\mathbf{P}\left(A_{n}\right)\geq c
for all
n\geq 1
, then

\mathbf{P}\left(A_{n}~\text{\rm i.o.}\right)\geq c.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that for any
k\geq 1
,

\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}1_{A_{j}}>k\right)\geq c.

It follows that

\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}1_{A_{j}}=\infty\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}1_{A_{j}}>k\right)\geq c.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For any positive integers
k,m
and
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, let

\widetilde{C}_{m}^{j}:=\{S_{n}>S_{T_{m}^{j-1}},~\text{for any}~n\in(T_{m}^{j-1},T_{m}^{j}]\}.

By the strong Markov property and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\widetilde{C}_{m}^{1}\cap\widetilde{C}_{m}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}>0,\forall n\in\left(0,T_{m}^{1}\right];S_{n}>S_{T_{m}^{1}},\forall n\in\left(T_{m}^{1},T_{m}^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left(1_{\{S_{n}>0,\forall n\in\left(0,T_{m}^{1}\right]\}}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{L_{T_{m}^{1}}}(S_{n}>S_{0},\forall n\in(0,T_{m}^{2}-T_{m}^{1}])\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{E}\left(1_{\{S_{n}>0,\forall n>0\}}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{L_{T_{m}^{1}}}(S_{n}>S_{0},\forall n>0)\right)

\displaystyle=(1-2q)^{2}.

Using induction, we easily get

\mathbf{P}\left(\widetilde{C}_{m}^{1}\cap\cdots\cap\widetilde{C}_{m}^{k}\right)\geq(1-2q)^{k}.

Since
\widetilde{C}_{m}^{1}\cap\cdots\cap\widetilde{C}_{m}^{k}\subseteq C_{m}^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(C_{m}^{k}\right)\geq(1-2q)^{k}.

(3.1)

Combining (
3.1
) with Lemma
3.2
,
we immediately get that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(``k\mbox{ favorite sites" occurs i.o.})\geq(1-2q)^{k}.

(3.2)

Using the transience of
\left\{S_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
, one can easily see that the favorite sites process
(\mathcal{K}(n))_{n\geq 1}
is transient, i.e., a fixed site cannot be a favorite site infinitely often.

Let
g(k):=\#\{n\geq 1:\#\mathcal{K}(n)=k\}
.
By the proof in
[
3
, Section 3.3]
and the transience of
(\mathcal{K}(n))_{n\geq 1}
, we get hat
\{g(k)=\infty\}
is a tail event.
Then by Kolmogorov’s 0-1 law and
(
3.2
), we get that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(g(k)=\infty)=1.

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We first prove two lemmas.

For
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, define

D_{n}^{\epsilon}:=\left\{\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\lambda\log n\leq\xi(n)\leq 2\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\lambda\log n\right\},

where
\lambda
is defined in (
1.2
). Let

E_{n}^{\epsilon}:=\left\{\mathrm{F}_{n}=0\right\},

where

\mathrm{F}_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{(i+2\lambda\log n)\wedge n}1_{\left\{\min\left(\xi\left(S_{i},n\right),\xi\left(S_{j},n\right)\right)\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n,S_{i},S_{j}\notin S_{(i,j)},S_{i}\neq S_{j}\right\}}.

In other words,
\mathrm{E}_{n}^{\epsilon}
is the event that there exist no pair of thick points (
i.e.
sites with local time at least
(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n
) that lie “close together” before time
n
.

Recall that
\delta
is defined in (
1.3
). Fix
\rho>0
such that
\rho\delta/2>1+\delta
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
with
(1+\delta)(1-\epsilon)^{2}>1+\delta/2
, there exists
C=C(\epsilon)>0
such that for
N
large,

\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{n\geq N}\left(D_{n}^{\epsilon}\cap E_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)^{\mathrm{c}}\right)\leq CN^{-(\epsilon\wedge(\delta/\rho))}.

Consequently,

\mathbf{P}\left(D_{n}^{\epsilon}\cap E_{n}^{\epsilon}\text{ occurs eventually }\right)=1.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
and the
definition of
\lambda
in (
1.2
),
we have for
z\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(z,n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 2(1+\epsilon/2)\lambda\log n}\mathbf{P}(\xi(z,n)=k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 2(1+\epsilon/2)\lambda\log n}\mathbf{P}(\xi(z,\infty)=k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 2(1+\epsilon/2)\lambda\log n}(2q)^{k-1}(1-2q)

\displaystyle\leq(1-2q)\cdot\frac{(2q)^{2(1+\epsilon/2)\lambda\log n-1}}{1-2q}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-2(1+\epsilon/2)}.

It follows that when
n
is large enough so that
2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{k=-(n-1)}^{n-1}\left\{\xi(k,n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-(n-1)}^{n-1}\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(k,n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2n\cdot Cn^{-2(1+\epsilon/2)}=Cn^{-(1+\epsilon)}.

(3.3)

Let

u:=\left[(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right],\quad v:=\left[\frac{n}{u^{2}}\right].

Recall that
\gamma(u+1)
is the probability that the random walk does not return to the starting point in the first
u
steps. According to (
2.1
), we have

1-\gamma(u+1)=1-\gamma-O\left(\frac{(4pq)^{(u+1)/2}}{(u+1)^{3/2}}\right)=2q+o(1),~~u\to\infty.

For
k\geq 1
, let
T_{k}
be the
k
-th time that the random walk returns to the starting point.
By the strong Markov property, we have that

\mathbf{P}(T_{1}\leq u,T_{k}-T_{k-1}\leq u,k=1,2,\ldots,u)=\left(1-\gamma(u+1)\right)^{[(1-\epsilon/2)\lambda\log n]}=:\beta.

Since there are at least
v
such independent segments in the first
n
steps,
we have

\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\leq(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)\leq(1-\beta)^{v}.

(3.4)

By the definition (
1.2
) of
\lambda
, we have

\log\beta=\left[(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right]\left(\log(2q)+o(1)\right)=-\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\log n+o(\log n),

so

\beta=n^{-(1-\epsilon/2)+o(1)}.

Consequently,

\beta v=n^{-(1-\epsilon/2)+o(1)}\cdot\left[\frac{n}{[(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n]^{2}}\right].

Therefore for any
\alpha\in(0,\epsilon/2)
, we have
\beta v\geq n^{\alpha}
for all
n
large, and hence
for all
n
large,

(1-\beta)^{v}\leq e^{-\beta v}\leq e^{-n^{\alpha}}.

Hence by (
3.4
)
we obtain that
for all
n
large,

\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\leq(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)\leq(1-\beta)^{v}\leq e^{-\beta v}\leq e^{-n^{\alpha}}.

(3.5)

Combining (
3
), (
3.5
) and the definition of
D_{n}^{\epsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}((D_{n}^{\epsilon})^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\leq(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\ \mbox{or}\ \xi(n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\leq(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)+\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(n)\geq 2(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\lambda\log n\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-n^{\alpha}}+Cn^{-(1+\epsilon)}\leq Cn^{-(1+\epsilon)}.

Now we deal with the probability of
(E_{n}^{\epsilon})^{c}
.
For
n,\tilde{n}>0
, define

\mathrm{F}_{n,\tilde{n}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{(i+2\lambda\log n)\wedge n}1_{\left\{\min\left(\xi\left(S_{i},n\right),\xi\left(S_{j},n\right)\right)\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log\tilde{n},S_{i},S_{j}\notin S_{(i,j)},S_{i}\neq S_{j}\right\}}.

Note that for any
k\in\left[n^{\rho},(n+1)^{\rho}\right)
, we have the bound
\mathrm{F}_{k}\leq\mathrm{F}_{(n+1)^{\rho},n^{\rho}}
.
Consequently,

\bigcup_{n\geq N^{\rho}}\left(E_{n}^{\epsilon}\right)^{c}\subset\bigcup_{n\geq N}\left\{\mathrm{F}_{(n+1)^{\rho},n^{\rho}}>0\right\}.

(3.7)

It is elementary to check that for non-negative random variables
X,Y
and any
q>0
,

\{X+Y\geq q\}\subset\bigcup_{r=0}^{[1/\epsilon]}\{X\geq r\epsilon q,~Y\geq[1-(r+1)\epsilon]q\}.

(3.8)

Let
\hat{S}_{l}:=S_{i-l}-S_{i}
and
\widetilde{S}_{l}:=S_{j+l}-S_{j}
. For any
1\leq i\leq(n+1)^{\rho},i<j\leq

\left(i+\lambda\log(n+1)^{\rho}\right)\wedge(n+1)^{\rho}
, by (
3.8
), Proposition
2.5
and the assumption
(1-\epsilon)^{2}(1+\delta)>1+\delta/2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\min\left(\xi\left(S_{i},(n+1)^{\rho}\right),\xi\left(S_{j},(n+1)^{\rho}\right)\right)\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho},S_{i},S_{j}\notin S_{(i,j)},S_{i}\neq S_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{j}-S_{i}=z,\sum_{l=0}^{i}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\hat{S}_{l}\in\{0,z\}\right\}}+\sum_{l=0}^{(n+1)^{\rho}-j}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\widetilde{S}_{l}\in\{0,-z\}\right\}}\geq 2(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~~\cdot\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{(n+1)^{\rho}-j}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\widetilde{S}_{l}\in\{0,-z\}\right\}}\geq 2[1-(r+1)\epsilon](1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in(0,[1/\epsilon])}([1/\epsilon]+1)\cdot\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(0,(n+1)^{\rho})+\xi(z,(n+1)^{\rho})\geq 2r\epsilon(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle~~\cdot\mathbf{P}\left(\xi(0,(n+1)^{\rho})+\xi(z,(n+1)^{\rho})\geq 2[1-(r+1)\epsilon](1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in(0,[1/\epsilon])}([1/\epsilon]+1)\cdot\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\mathbf{P}(\xi(0,\infty)+\xi(z,\infty)>2r\epsilon(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho})

\displaystyle~~\cdot\mathbf{P}(\xi(0,\infty)+\xi(z,\infty)>2[1-(r+1)\epsilon](1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in(0,[1/\epsilon])}([1/\epsilon]+1)C\cdot n^{-r\epsilon(1-\epsilon)(\delta+1)\rho}\cdot n^{-[1-(r+1)\epsilon](1-\epsilon)(1+\delta)\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([1/\epsilon]+1)C\cdot n^{-(1-\epsilon)^{2}(1+\delta)\rho}\leq C\cdot n^{-\rho(1+\frac{\delta}{2})}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{(n+1)^{\rho}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\nabla}\mathbf{P}\left(\min\left(\xi\left(S_{i},(n+1)^{\rho}\right),\xi\left(S_{j},(n+1)^{\rho}\right)\right)\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda\log n^{\rho},S_{i},S_{j}\notin S_{(i,j)},S_{i}\neq S_{j}\right)

\displaystyle(n+1)^{\rho}\cdot 2\lambda\log(n+1)^{\rho}\cdot Cn^{-\rho(1+\frac{\delta}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\log n^{\rho}\cdot n^{-\frac{\delta\rho}{2}}\leq Cn^{-(1+\delta)}.

(3.9)

Combining (

), (

Lemma 3.4
.

Almost surely, there exists

. If

The rest of the proof is divided into two parts.

(i) We first show that for sufficiently large

holds. Set

(3.14)

Assume that for some

and

,

. When

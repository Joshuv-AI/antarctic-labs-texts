Perioperative Toripalimab Plus Chemotherapy Versus Chemotherapy Alone in Locally Advanced Gastric or Gastroesophageal Junction Cancer: 3-Year Follow-Up of NEOSUMMIT-01 Trial
Authors: Run-Cong Nie, Ying Jin, Cheng-Cai Liang, Yuan-Fang Li, Ying-Bo Chen, Xiao-Wei Sun, Wen-Long Guan, Zi-Xian Wang, Hai-Bo Qiu, Wei Wang, Shi Chen, Dong-Sheng Zhang, Yi-Hong Ling, Shao-Yan Xi, Mu-Yan Cai, Jun Chi, Qiu-Xia Yang, Zhi-Min Liu, Yuan-Xiang Guan, Yong-Ming Chen, Ji-Bin Li, Xiong-Wen Tang, Jun-Sheng Peng, Feng Wang, Zhi-Wei Zhou, Rui-Hua Xu, Shu-Qiang Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe NEOSUMMIT-01 trial previously showed that adding the PD-1 antibody toripalimab to perioperative chemotherapy improved the pathologic response in patients with locally advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer. Here, we present the event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) after extended follow-up. A total of 108 patients were enrolled (toripalimab plus chemotherapy, n = 54; chemotherapy alone, n = 54). At the data cutoff date (August 29, 2025), the median follow-up was 43.2 months (interquartile range: 36.6-53.7). The 3-year EFS was 74.7% (95% CI, 63.6% to 87.7%) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 56.2% (95% CI, 43.3% to 73.0%) in the chemotherapy group, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.51 (95% CI, 0.27 to 0.98; P = .044). The 3-year OS was 81.3% (95% CI, 71.4% to 92.4%) versus 72.2% (95% CI, 61.2% to 85.2%), respectively, with an HR of 0.45 (95% CI, 0.21 to 0.95; P = .036). The survival benefits were consistent across most predefined subgroups and were maintained in the analysis excluding patients with dMMR. In conclusion, perioperative toripalimab plus chemotherapy significantly improved 3-year EFS and OS compared with chemotherapy alone, suggesting it as a promising treatment option for patients with locally advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer.

INTRODUCTION

Multimodal perioperative therapy is recommended as the standard of care for localized gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma.1-4 Adjuvant chemotherapy after D2 gastrectomy represents the standard in Asia,5,6 whereas in Western counties, a perioperative triplet regimen with curative resection is recommended for this localized disease.7,8 The FLOT4 trial established the perioperative fluorouracil, leucovorin, oxaliplatin, and docetaxel (FLOT) as the standard of care in many counties outside of Asia.8 Evidence from several randomized trials in Asia also supports the use of perioperative chemotherapy.9,10 The RESOLVE trial showed that perioperative SOX (S-1 and oxaliplatin) provided superior overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) compared with adjuvant capecitabine and oxaliplatin (CapOx) in the Chinese population.4,9 Despite these results, relapse and metastasis continue to pose a major challenge for a substantial proportion of patients even after perioperative chemotherapy.

The combination of anti–PD-1 and PD-L1 therapy with chemotherapy has emerged as a new standard of care, demonstrating significant survival benefits in the first-line treatment for advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma.11-15 A few randomized trials have reported promising efficacy for anti–PD-1 and PD-L1 therapy combined with chemotherapy in the perioperative setting for localized gastric cancer.16-19 The NEOSUMMIT-01 is a randomized, phase II trial evaluating the efficacy of perioperative toripalimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in patients with cT3-4aN+M0 gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer.17 The combination strategy significantly improved pathologic regression. This 3-year update provides long-term efficacy.

METHODS

Full study design details for the phase II randomized, open-label NEOSUMMIT-01 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04250948) were previously published.17 The study was designed to enroll 108 patients to provide an 80% power at a one-sided α of .025 to detect an improvement in the rate of TRG 0-1 from 15% to 40%, allowing for a 10% dropout rate. Here, we report the 3-year event-free survival (EFS) and OS outcomes. Eligible patients received three cycles of preoperative chemotherapy, followed by surgery, and five postoperative cycles of investigator's choice of chemotherapy (SOX or CapOX) or toripalimab plus investigator's choice of chemotherapy (SOX or CapOX), followed by toripalimab for up to 6 months according to the patients' conditions.

The EFS was defined as time from random assignment to disease progression precluding surgery, development of distant metastatic disease, local recurrence after surgery, or death from any cause. Disease progression or recurrence (local or distant) was confirmed by imaging or physical examination, and patients without events were censored at the last documented date. OS was defined as time from random assignment to death from any cause. OS data were assessed via outpatient visits, telephone calls, or social networking services, and patients without events were censored at the last contact date. The per-protocol population in this study was defined as participants who received at least one perioperative treatment and subsequently underwent radical gastrectomy. Differences in the site of first relapse between the treatment groups were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test. Only documented relapses occurring within 3 years after random assignment were considered as events, and patients without documented relapses who were censored within this period were excluded from the analysis. Study procedures received ethical approval. Patients provided informed consent.

EFS

By the data cutoff date of August 29, 2025, the median duration of follow-up was 42.9 months (IQR, 37.6-53.2) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 43.1 months (IQR, 36.4-54.6) in the chemotherapy alone group. Details of subsequent anticancer therapies are summarized in Appendix Table A1 (online only).

Overall, 15 patients (27.8%) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 24 patients (44.4%) in the chemotherapy-alone group had disease progression or recurrence or died. The percentage of patients who remained event-free at 3 years was 74.7% (95% CI, 63.6% to 87.7%) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 56.2% (95% CI: 43.3% to 73.0%) in the chemotherapy-alone group, with a hazard ratio (HR) for event or death of 0.51 (95% CI, 0.27 to 0.98; P = .044; Fig 2A). The median EFS was not reached in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 38.2 months (95% CI, 27.2 to not reached) in the chemotherapy-alone group. The HR for the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group versus the chemotherapy-alone group was 0.49 (95% CI, 0.24 to 0.98; P = .043) in the per-protocol population (Appendix Fig A1A). EFS in the population excluding patients with dMMR was consistent with that in the overall full analysis population (Appendix Fig A2A). Subgroup analyses for EFS are presented in Appendix Fig A3. A consistent EFS benefit was observed across most prespecified subgroups.

Overall Survival

Overall, 10 patients (18.5%) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 22 patients (40.7%) in the chemotherapy-alone group died of any cause. The OS at 3 years was 81.3% (95% CI, 71.4% to 92.4%) among the patients in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 72.2% (95% CI, 61.2% to 85.2%) among those in the chemotherapy-alone group (Fig 2B). The HR for OS adjusted by the stratification factors in the Cox proportional hazard model was 0.45 (95% CI, 0.21 to 0.95; P = .036). At the data cutoff, the median OS was not reached in either the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group or the chemotherapy-alone group. OS in the per-protocol population (Appendix Fig A1B) and population excluding patients with dMMR (Appendix Fig A2B) was consistent with that in the overall full analysis population. A consistent OS benefit was observed across most prespecified subgroups (Appendix Fig A4).

Site of First Relapse

Common sites of first relapse were peritoneum, followed by hematogenous sites (Table 2). Adding toripalimab to chemotherapy significantly reduced the rate of documented metastasis and relapse (20.0% v 47.4%; P = .010), with a notable decrease in the rate of peritoneal metastasis. The incidence of peritoneal metastasis was 15.0% (6 of 40) in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group compared with 36.8% (14 of 38) in the chemotherapy-alone group (P = .027).

DISCUSSION

This follow-up analysis of the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial demonstrated superior EFS and OS outcomes with perioperative toripalimab plus chemotherapy compared with chemotherapy alone in patients with localized gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer. At 3 years, 74.7% of patients in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group and 56.2% of those in the chemotherapy-alone group remained event-free. The 3-year OS was 81.3% in the toripalimab plus chemotherapy group versus 72.2% in the chemotherapy-alone group. These survival benefits were maintained in the per-protocol population and in the analysis excluding dMMR patients, supporting the robustness of the findings.

The selection of a perioperative chemotherapy regimen must consider regional variations in efficacy, safety profiles, and established standards of care. In Europe, the FLOT4 trial demonstrated superior DFS and OS with perioperative FLOT4 compared with ECF/ECX in patients with resectable gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer.8 However, differences in the tolerability profile of the FLOT regimen between European and Asian populations have been recognized. In the Asian population, the RESOLVE trial established perioperative SOX as a standard of care by demonstrating superior survival outcomes compared with adjuvant CapOx.9 A prior phase II randomized trial from China suggested comparable efficacy in pathologic response between the FLOT and SOX regimens.20 Therefore, the selection of the SOX/CapOx backbone in our NEOSUMMIT-01 trial was based on its established efficacy and tolerability in the relevant patient population, aligning with regional practice. Regarding the efficacy of the chemotherapy backbone in our control arm, we observed that the median EFS in the chemotherapy-alone group was numerically comparable with outcomes reported for FLOT-based regimens in other major perioperative trials (eg, MATTERHORN,19 FLOT48 trials). While cross-trial comparisons are inherently limited and must be interpreted with caution, this observation suggests that both triplet and doublet regimens can provide a robust efficacy baseline for evaluating the additive benefit of immunotherapy in this setting.

Anti–PD-1/PD-L1 therapy has shown encouraging outcomes for localized gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancers in the perioperative setting.16-19 Despite showing an improved pathologic response, the perioperative approach of pembrolizumab plus cisplatin-based chemotherapy in the KEYNOTE-585 trial did not translate into statistically significant improvements in EFS or OS.18 The MATTERHORN trial used the FLOT regimen as the control group and showed that the addition of durvalumab to perioperative FLOT chemotherapy led to significantly better pathologic response and EFS.19 In the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial, we previously reported that the addition of perioperative toripalimab to oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy resulted in significantly improved pathologic response.17 Here, the follow-up analysis further demonstrated that this benefit in pathologic response can translate into significant improvement in EFS and OS. The collective evidence from these trials favors an oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy backbone as the preferred partner for perioperative immunotherapy in gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancers.

It is widely recognized that dMMR or high microsatellite instability (MSI-H) status is an established predictive biomarker for response to immunotherapy. The NEONIPIGA and INFINITY trials demonstrated remarkable efficacy of combined anti–PD-1 and anti-CTLA4 therapy in dMMR/MSI-H gastric cancer, achieving pathologic complete response rates of 58.7% and 60.0% in cohort 1, respectively.21,22 In the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial, dMMR tumors were identified in 5.6% of patients in each group, a prevalence consistent with previous reports.16,19 The survival benefit persisted in the analysis excluding dMMR status, underscoring the efficacy of the addition of perioperative immunotherapy to chemotherapy, consistent with the results of the MATTERHORN trial.19

The recurrence and metastasis patterns elucidated in the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial provide a critical foundation for designing subsequent trials aimed at preventing disease recurrence. The ACTS-GC trial showed that adjuvant S-1 reduces recurrences at multiple sites, particularly peritoneal metastases.5 By contrast, the JACCRO GC-07 trial found that docetaxel plus S-1 decreased hematogenous and lymph node metastases without affecting peritoneal recurrence.23,24 Therefore, preventing peritoneal metastasis remains a critical unmet need that demands more intensive and effective treatment strategies. In the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial, we found that the addition of perioperative toripalimab to chemotherapy resulted in a significant decrease in peritoneal metastasis, establishing this combination as an effective strategy to mitigate this specific risk.

Limitations of this 3-year analysis include the generalization to other populations, particularly to Western populations. Although the EFS and OS benefits are encouraging, they must be interpreted cautiously in the context of a randomized phase II design with secondary end points. Finally, this trial design did not allow for separate efficacy assessments of the neoadjuvant and adjuvant components, which limits insights into their distinct contributions and should be addressed in future studies.

In conclusion, the follow-up analysis from the NEOSUMMIT-01 trial suggests that perioperative toripalimab plus chemotherapy significantly improved 3-year EFS and OS compared with chemotherapy alone, suggesting it as a promising treatment option for patients with locally advanced gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer.

Nonstandard methods for ultrafilter relation extensions
Leonardo Raffaello Maximilian Gasparro, Lorenzo Luperi Baglini

Abstract
We study two types of ultrafilter relation extensions with nonstandard methods and provide characterisations of these extensions in terms of generators of ultrafilters . These characterisations are then used to determine whether various relation properties are preserved by these extensions. Lastly, we study the notion of self-divisible ultrafilter and, with the mentioned characterizations, generalize this notion for a class of relations.

3.3
Other properties

We end by showing that irriflexivity, antisymmetry and asymmetry are not, in general, preserved by either of the two canonical extensions.

Recall that a relation

(\forall i\in I)\,\lnot R(i,i)

Example 3.14

Considering the divisibility relation

5
Open questions

In Section
3
we studied which of the basic relation properties are preserved. For the weak canonical extension, the situation was easy to identify: reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and connectedness are preserved; irreflexivity, antisymmetry and asymmetry are not. On the other hand, for the strong canonical extension we have some open questions. In particular, we have seen that transitivity is always preserved, meanwhile reflexivity, irreflexivity, antisymmetry and asymmetry are not, in general. For symmetry and connectedness, we were able to show that both are preserved if we suppose that the relation is also transitive and its extension is reflexive: these naturally led us to consider equivalence relations and total pre-orders. What remains to understand is if these two relations are independently preserved, in particular we have the following.

Question 5.1
.

If
R\subseteq I^{2}
is symmetric, is also
R^{s}\subseteq(\beta I)^{2}
symmetric?

Question 5.2
.

If
R\subseteq I^{2}
is connected, is also
R^{s}\subseteq(\beta I)^{2}

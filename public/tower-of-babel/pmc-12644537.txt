Evaluating digital technologies for architectural heritage preservation using interval-valued q-rung orthopair fuzzy aggregation operators
Authors: Li Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Digital technologies have made their presence in the field of architectural heritage as an essential tool to save most historical structures from wearing out, environmental damage, and urban transformation. The objective of this research is to investigate the effects of digital information technology on the preservation of architectural heritage with a novel integration of interval valued q-rung orthopair fuzzy weighted average (IVq-ROFWA) and interval valued q-rung orthopair fuzzy weighted geometric (IVq-ROFWG) operators, an approach that has not, to the best of current knowledge, been previously applied in this domain. The application of these more advanced fuzzy aggregation methods provides the benefit of incorporating uncertainty and imprecision in expert judgments to account for the indeterminate, implicit relationships between various preservation criteria, including data accuracy, integration with restoration efforts, and long-term data accessibility. Given five alternatives considered in terms of five attributes, the IVq-ROFWA method rated augmented reality as the highest. In contrast, the IVq-ROFWG method rated heritage building information modeling as the highest. The present research highlights another aspect of high relevance to decision-makers, as it provides valuable recommendations on selecting the most effective digital tools to preserve their heritage. The novelty of the study lies in the introduction of new types of IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators in architectural heritage conservation, which provide more robust, precise, and reliable decisions based on uncertainty.

Introduction

Architectural heritage preservation has become an increasingly essential issue in the fields of urbanization, environmental degradation, and the conservation of natural life in structures. Over the recent past, digital technologies such as Building Information Modelling (BIM), 3D laser scanning, as well as virtual reality (VR) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) have transformed traditional strategies in heritage conservation. These tools aid in creating more accurate documentation, support structural or virtual reconstruction, and improve informed preservation practices that are more sustainable. To assess the efficiency of these technologies, a structured decision-making perspective is needed, incorporating multiple criteria such as technical adaptability, data accuracy, stakeholder involvement, and long-term conservation impact. Multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods provide a solid analytical foundation for evaluating how to select, prioritize, and choose digital solutions. However, the application of MCDM techniques can help decision-makers, such as architects, conservators, and policymakers, in assessing the contribution of digital tools to heritage preservation or in choosing the most innovative, sensitive, and effective ways to mitigate them more successfully, combining technical and cultural motives. Recent developments in digital technologies have shaped the evolution of trends in conservation strategies for architectural heritage. However, previous research on topics of this sort has generally lacked empirical evidence on the long-term impact it can have. In the case analysis, we demonstrate that such technologies, particularly 3D laser scanning and heritage building information modeling, are highly effective in preserving heritage buildings and are also widely accepted by key stakeholders, especially in richly historic urban landscapes such as Venice and Istanbul. However, although AR and VR have been relatively successful in increasing public engagement and educational outreach, issues of both long-term sustainability and technological obsolescence have emerged due to these technologies. These observations identify an essential gap in earlier research, as they highlight a case where appraisal outcomes and stakeholder accessibility were balanced in an interdisciplinary yet pragmatic manner, making the solution practical for long-term sustainability.

Decision-making (DM) is the process of identifying the most effective course of action from the range of options available, based on an assessment of the factors that may affect the DM process and the consequences that may follow from the choices made1–4. The importance cannot be overstated, especially considering that people and companies are constantly faced with decisions that significantly impact their daily productivity, health, and satisfaction. In this context, multi-attribute DM (MADM) is a handy tool since it offers a framework for assessing multiple attributes where structures are needed to support the DM process. MADM is especially essential in healthcare, finance, and environmental management because decision-makers (DMKs) have to deal with multiple conflicting attributes. For example, in medicine, MADM is used to compare the efficiency of treatments regarding their cost and patients’ preferences. In environmental management, it is used to compare the benefits of economic growth and its negative environmental impacts. Ultimately, MADM can enhance the DM process by effectively structuring the considerations and attributes involved, resulting in improved decision-making4–6. The safety production evaluation and warning system (SPEWS) in power equipment enterprises is a type of evaluation and warning system that enhances production safety in manufacturing. This system is designed for risk assessment, safety performance evaluation, timely notification of accidents, and non-compliance with safety standards. SPEWS typically encompasses several safety indices, including equipment reliability, process controls, personnel training, and safety plans. Various SPEWS7–10 have been explored and discussed. MADM is very useful in evaluating the SPEWS since it permits the DMK to consider more than one safety-related attribute at a time. MADM methods are helpful for an organized assessment of multiple factors, such as risk level, cost implications, and the performance of safety measures, allowing organizations to make safer choices. For example, by employing MADM, the safety managers can rank safety measures according to the significance of the hazards they can help to minimize and the improvements they can make to the operation’s efficiency. Furthermore, MADM facilitates the comparison of various warning systems and procedures to determine the most effective and feasible solutions. It is suggested that MADM can be applied to the assessment of SPEWS to enhance the management practices of power equipment enterprises, reduce risk possibilities, and promote a safer work environment. MADM is being utilized11–14 to assess the SPEWS in the power equipment enterprise sector.

In 1965, Zadeh15 introduced fuzzy sets (FS) to address the challenge of handling imprecise and complex data. They differ from classical sets in that they partition elements, where each element takes only a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:0$$\end{document} or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:1$$\end{document} value, but allow for a gradient of membership in the open interval [0, 1]. Such a fundamental contribution was facilitated by scores of FS extensions proposed to broaden the theory’s capacity to capture uncertainty and vagueness. Atanassov16 described one of the first extensions of FSs, the intuitionistic FS (IFS) based on the extension of the idea of FSs. IFS also considers the so-called membership degree (MD) and includes the non-membership degree (NMD) to provide a more detailed description of the uncertainty. In this context, each element in the framework is associated with both MD and NMD, with MD + NMD ≤ 1. This extension allowed for a much better representation of real-world uncertainty compared to ordinary FSs. Atanassov17 generalized the type-1 IF state further to define type-2 IFS, and these sets are less rigid than the type-1 IF state because MD and NMD grades of an element in a set are not necessarily fixed but can be within a range. This added liberty is most valuable when the variability is not necessarily well-defined. Yager18 proposed another significant extension in Pythagorean FS (PyFS) to provide the prospect of the further enlargement of FSs since the constraints on the MD and NMD degrees have been eliminated. As mentioned, in the case of PyFS, the sum of the squares of MD and NMD can be less than or equal to 1, providing a more flexible model for capturing the relationship between these degrees in conditions of high uncertainty. Atanassov and Gargov19 made other enhancements to IFS and proposed interval-valued IFS (IVIFS). In this extension, both MD and NMD are presented in the form of intervals instead of specific values, which makes it less sensitive to uncertainty and enables calculations when the values are difficult to quantify. Smarandache20 introduced the concept of neutrosophic sets (NS), which extend FS theory by introducing three parameters: reality, uncertainty, and falsehood instead of the classical MD, NMD, and hesitation. In general, using neutrosophic sets in DM seems more appropriate due to the better management of uncertain and inconsistent information. Interval-valued NS (IVNS), introduced by Wang et al.21, generalizes this concept by allowing these three components to be intervals, thereby providing more flexibility in handling vagueness. Torra22 proposed the idea of hesitant FS (HFS) to deal with the situation where the MD may have multiple values. This approach is instrumental when DMK is in a dilemma on which MD to assign. On this basis, Chen et al.23 further proposed the IHF model, also known as interval-valued HFS (IVHFS), which expands the MD range and can describe hesitation and uncertainty. Last but not least, Yager24 proposed q-rung orthopair FS (q-ROFS), which is a more general form of IFS and PyFS with more freedom in defining the function between MD and NMD. In particular, the MD and NMD powers in q-ROFS are added together, and this result should be less than or equal to 1, given the flexibility parameter q. Joshi et al.25 further developed this model by integrating IVq-ROFS, where the degrees can be represented as intervals, which makes them particularly useful in cases of high uncertainty.

T-norms (TNs) are used to model the intersection (AND) of FSs by combining MD, while t-conorms (TCNs) handle the union (OR) by aggregating the highest MD. They are essential in fuzzy logic systems for evaluating conditions and combining fuzzy rules. Sugeno26 proposed TNs with features of nilpotent TCNs, and Weber27 worked on nilpotent TNs. These contributions led to the development of the Sugeno–Weber TN (SWTN) and Sugeno–Weber TCN (SWTCN). Similarly, Aczél and Alsina28 proposed two primary operations, Aczel-Alsina TN (AATN) and Aczel-Alsina TCN (AATCN), which proved to be more flexible and efficient than previous norms. Further, aggregation operators (AOs) are derivations used to consider multiple inputs and provide a resultant output. AOs are crucial tools in DM processes, as they facilitate DM by giving a single result for an item under various available circumstances. Due to the applicability of these AOs in multiple fields, wide research is being carried out to modify and develop AOs. Mahmood and Ali29 introduced AA power AOs based on IFSs and proposed their application in the MADM process. Alcantud30 constructed weighted averaging operators and weighted geometric AOs in the IF environment along with a numerical example based on the multi-attribute group DM (MAGDM) problem. Senapati et al.31 presented several novel AA AOs for IVIFSs and demonstrated their utility in DM problems. Jamil et al.32 induced various Einstein hybrid geometric AOs based on IVIFSs and applied them to handle a real-life scenario. Furthermore, regarding the PyF environment, Hezam et al.33 developed geometric AOs to address the MAGDM problem under complex PyFSs. Rahman and Abdullah34 formulated group-generalized PyF AOs and their applicability in DM problems. Similarly, Garg and Chen35 developed neutrality operators for PyFSs and their applications in MAGDM problems. Donyatalab et al.36 developed novel Hamacher AOs for the IVq-ROFSs and used these AOs to solve real-life problems. Guo and Yao37 proposed density IVq-ROF density operators and the application of MADM. Garg38 induced exponential operational laws for IVqROFSs in MAGDM. Relatively, although some operators, such as Dombi, Hamacher, Frank, and Archimedean, are associated with different types of aggregation behavior, in terms of parameter control-adjustability and flexibility, they could not necessarily capture simultaneously both additive and multiplicative relationships of decisions. This study consequently uses the weighted averaging and geometric aggregation operators, which have the advantage of addressing both the relative importance of a set of criteria (via averaging) and their interactive or multiplicative effects (by use of geometric means), hence is specifically fresh to model the complex and uncertain one deals with when assessing digital technologies and architectural heritage preservation.

Recent studies have employed more advanced fuzzy set extensions to incorporate higher levels of fuzziness into the MCDM methodology, thereby addressing uncertainty in various application areas. SWARA-PROMETHEE II has optimized interval-valued Fermatean fuzzy Dombi operators in biomedical waste management39, Dombi operators have aided plastic waste process selection in interval-valued spherical fuzzy MABAC40, q-ROF Archimedean operators in software site selection41, and Dombi operators have been used to optimize sustainable supplier ranking when p, q-quasirung orthopair fuzzy sets have the confidence level42. A score function-based EDAS approach has been used to solve personnel selection43, and MADM with unknown weights has been implemented using q-ROF-Frank operators44. Other findings include higher-order circular Pythagorean fuzzy time series for predicting Alzheimer’s disease45, a new distance in q-rung orthopair multi-fuzzy systems for cybersecurity46, and G8 logistics performance in MCDM models47. Moreover, induced confidence-level complex polytopic fuzzy operators48, their application in nuclear power plant placement49, and complex intuitionistic hesitant fuzzy aggregation operators50 demonstrate the breadth of current developments. Building on these, this study applies the IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators to the evaluation of digital technologies in preserving architectural heritage, offering a robust framework for enhanced uncertainty modelling and reliable decision-making.

Objectives and contributions of the study

The objective of this research is the assessment and prioritization of digital technologies in terms of preserving architectural heritage by using a new combination of the IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG computing operators. This methodology facilitates strong decision-making under uncertainty, imprecision and complex interrelationships between dimensions of evaluation.

The key contributions are as follows:

The first insertion of IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators in the architectural heritage preservation field.The construction of a flexible decision-making structure that is efficient in terms of capturing a sort of uncertainty or subjective expert information.Drawn a comparative analysis of the two operators to expose variations in technology prioritization outcomes.Illustration of the relevance of the offered framework on a case study in the real world with several alternatives and attributes.

Research gap and motivations

The use of digital technologies in the process of documentation, conservation, and promotion of architectural heritage becomes more and more widespread. Nevertheless, the current research tends to give attention to specific technologies or apply decision-making techniques that cannot easily account for uncertainty, lack of complete information, and subjective preferences among experts. Moreover, a combination of better fuzzy set theory, especially IVq-ROF operators, has not been looked at in this context.

This research is motivated by the need to:

Present a methodological, comparative analysis of various new digital tools in heritage preservation.Deal with the inescapable imprecision and subjectivity of expert judgments using advanced fuzzy aggregation operators.Fill the methodological gap by presenting the notion of IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators in heritage conservation decision-making.

Structure of the study

This article is structured as follows: Sect. 2 provides an overview of the fundamental concepts of FS, q-ROFS, and IVq-ROFS. It also examines the operations of DTN and DTCN related to IVq-ROFS. Section 3 focuses on the MADM methodology applicable to the IVq-ROF environment, along with a comprehensive algorithm. Section 4 presents a numerical case study of the evaluation of the effect of digital technologies on the preservation of architectural heritage. Section 5 analyzes the results of the proposed methodology compared to those of several established methodologies. Finally, Sect. 6 concludes the article.

A novel approach to addressing the multi-attribute decision-making (MADM) problem using IVq-Rung orthopair fuzzy sets

MADM refers to the process of evaluating and selecting alternatives based on multiple criteria that may conflict with one another. This approach is crucial in various fields, such as finance, healthcare, and engineering, where decisions must balance diverse factors like cost, quality, and risk. MADM algorithms are designed to facilitate this complex evaluation process, employing various methodologies to aggregate and analyze information. Common algorithms include the Weighted Sum Model (WSM), which assigns weights to each attribute and computes a total score for each alternative, and the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), which breaks down the decision problem into a hierarchy of criteria and sub-criteria for detailed comparison. Other methods, such as Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) and fuzzy-based approaches, enhance the ability to handle uncertainty and vagueness in the decision-making context. These algorithms provide structured frameworks that guide decision-makers toward optimal choices, taking into account the interplay of multiple attributes.

The set of alternatives is a preferred definition where the mentioned set is a set of various options or choices that are considered during the decision making. Such decision making criteria can be products, services, strategies or any kind of entities for comparison. Each of the respective alternatives is represented as Ai = {A1, A2, A3, …, An}. The set of criteria translates the factors or characteristics that are applied to assess these alternatives which can be clear, but often involve trade-offs. This set is symbolized as Ƈi = {Ƈ1, Ƈ2, Ƈ3,…, Ƈn}. Conclusions

This study developed the IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators to enhance decision-making under conditions of uncertainty. The proposed methods enable a more nuanced aggregation of data that accurately reflects the complexity of real-world decision-making scenarios. The results of the numerical examples and comparative analyses demonstrate that the IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators outperform traditional aggregation techniques in terms of flexibility and accuracy, particularly when dealing with conflicting information and subjective judgments. These unique findings highlight the operators’ ability to synthesize diverse perspectives while accounting for the inherent uncertainty present in evaluations.

This research has many practical implications in any area, such as finance, healthcare, and environmental management, where the decision-making process is frequently based on vague and complex information. Decision-making processes can become better with the improvement of the IVq-ROFWA and IVq-ROFWG operators to make more elucidate and practical solutions.

The drawbacks to the study are that it uses hypothetical and illustrative datasets to validate, which are not comprehensive enough to portray the differences present in a real-world dataset. Moreover, selecting the parameter q should be fine-tuned in varying application domains, and this affects the performance of the suggested operators. Additionally, dynamic or time-dependent decision environments present in the current framework are not taken into account, which might impact its usage in situations of the fastest change.

Future study can be directed to using the operators on real-world case studies of larger scale in various industries to combine them with other decision science methods like the optimization-based models or machine-learning algorithms. Further research on the adaptive choice of parameters and application of the technique under dynamic conditions would also contribute to effective practical applicability and robustness of the suggested methodology. On the whole, this work helps to develop fuzzy decision-making theoretically because it provides strong strategies to employ to manage uncertainty in different situations.

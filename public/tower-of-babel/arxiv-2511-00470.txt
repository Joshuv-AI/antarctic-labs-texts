An Approximation Algorithm for Monotone Submodular Cost Allocation
Ryuhei Mizutani

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the minimum submodular cost allocation ( MSCA ) problem. The input of MSCA is k k non-negative submodular functions f 1 , … , f k f_{1},\ldots,f_{k} on the ground set N N given by evaluation oracles, and the goal is to partition N N into k k (possibly empty) sets X 1 , … , X k X_{1},\ldots,X_{k} so that ∑ i = 1 k f i ​ ( X i ) \sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i}) is minimized. In this paper, we focus on the case when f 1 , … , f k f_{1},\ldots,f_{k} are monotone (denoted by Mono-MSCA ). We provide a natural LP-relaxation for Mono-MSCA , which is equivalent to the convex program relaxation introduced by Chekuri and Ene. We show that the integrality gap of the LP-relaxation is at most k / 2 k/2 , which yields a k / 2 k/2 -approximation algorithm for Mono-MSCA . We also show that the integrality gap of the LP-relaxation is at least k / 2 − ϵ k/2-\epsilon for any constant ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 when k k is fixed.



1
Introduction

Let
N
be a finite set of cardinality
n
, and
k\geq 2
a positive integer.
A set function
f:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
submodular
if
f(S)+f(T)\geq f(S\cup T)+f(S\cap T)
holds for every
S,T\subseteq N
.
In this paper, we focus on the
Minimum Submodular Cost Allocation
(
MSCA
) problem, introduced by Chekuri and Ene
[
5
]
.
The input of
MSCA
is
k
non-negative submodular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by evaluation oracles, and the goal is to partition
N
into
k
(possibly empty) sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})
is minimized, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\ \middle|\ X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ partition\ of\ }N\right\}.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the input functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
satisfy
f_{i}(\emptyset)=0
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Several well-studied problems such as the multiway cut problems in graphs and hypergraphs, the submodular multiway partition problems, and uniform metric labeling can be cast as special cases of
MSCA
.
In the case when all submodular functions are monotone,
MSCA
is equivalent to the submodular facility location problem considered by Svitkina and Tardos
[
18
]
.
In this paper, we focus on this setting, i.e., the case where each of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
is monotone submodular.
We denote
MSCA
with monotone submodular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
by
Mono-MSCA
.
The aim of this paper is to investigate the integrality gap of a natural LP-relaxation for
Mono-MSCA
when
k
is small.

LP-relaxation.

To provide an LP-relaxation for
Mono-MSCA
, we introduce some notation.
Let
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}_{>0}
denote the all-ones vector and
[k]:=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
For any
S\subseteq N
, let
\chi_{S}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
denote the characteristic vector of
S
.
We consider the following linear programming relaxation for
MSCA
:

\displaystyle(\textsc{LP-Rel})\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{minimize}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\ \ \ y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}}_{+}\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }i\in[k],

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)\chi_{S}=\mathbf{1}.

In this LP-relaxation, we interpret
y_{i}(S)=1
to mean that the set
S
is assigned to
f_{i}
in
MSCA
, whereas
y_{i}(S)=0
means that
S
is not assigned to
f_{i}
.
We note that if an integer feasible solution
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
satisfies
y_{i}(S_{1})=y_{i}(S_{2})=1
for two distinct sets
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq N
and
i\in[k]
, then by the submodularity of
f_{i}
we can update
y_{i}(S_{1}):=y_{i}(S_{2}):=0
and
y_{i}(S_{1}\cup S_{2}):=1
without increasing the objective value.
Hence, after such uncrossing operations, any integer feasible solution to
LP-Rel
induces a partition of
N
for
MSCA
.

The linear program
LP-Rel
is equivalent to the convex program relaxation introduced by Chekuri and Ene
[
5
]
(see Appendix
A
for details).
The linear program
LP-Rel
can also be seen as the dual linear program of the unweighted
k
-polymatroid intersection (see
[
16
]
and Appendix
B
for details).
Regarding the integrality gap of
LP-Rel
, Ene and Vondrák
[
8
]
showed that if
\mathrm{P}\neq\mathrm{NP}
, then the integrality gap of
LP-Rel
for general submodular functions cannot be upper-bounded by any factor.
Hence, it is natural to focus on special cases such as a monotone case that admit a bounded integrality gap.
We refer to
LP-Rel
restricted to monotone submodular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
as
Mono-LP-Rel
.
Svitkina and Tardos
[
18
]
gave a
(1+\ln n)
-approximation algorithm for
Mono-MSCA
, and Chekuri and Ene
[
5
]
showed that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
(1+\ln n)
.
Santiago and Shepherd
[
15
]
gave a
k
-approximation algorithm for
Mono-MSCA
and showed that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k
.
On the negative side, Svitkina and Tardos
[
18
]
showed that the set cover problem with a collection of
k
sets can be reduced to
Mono-MSCA
.
This implies that if
k
is a part of input,
Mono-MSCA
cannot be approximated within a factor of
(1-o(1))\cdot\ln n

[
10
]
, which matches the approximation ratio shown by Svitkina and Tardos
[
18
]
.
On the other hand, if
k\leq\log n
, then the set cover problem with a collection of
k
sets can be solved in time polynomial in
n
by brute-force.
Hence, in this case the reduction does not yield any approximation lower bound for
Mono-MSCA
.
Therefore, the approximability of
Mono-MSCA
and the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
for small
k
are much less understood.
In this paper, we aim to obtain tight upper and lower bounds of the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
when
k
is small.

Our results and techniques.

As mentioned previously, the current best upper bound on the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is
\min\{k,1+\ln n\}
.
Our first result improves this to
k/2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k/2
.

When
k=2
,
Mono-LP-Rel
coincides with the dual linear program of the unweighted polymatroid intersection.
Hence, when
k=2
, Theorem
1.1
corresponds to the (total) dual integrality of the polymatroid intersection.
Since the proof of Theorem
1.1
is constructive, it yields a
k/2
-approximation algorithm for
Mono-MSCA
, which improves the current best approximation ratio
\min\{k,1+\ln n\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a
k/2
-approximation algorithm for
Mono-MSCA
.

Our next result is a lower bound on the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
, which is tight when
k
is fixed.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\log n\geq k
, then the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}
.

We note that if
k
is fixed, Theorem
1.3
implies that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
k/2-\epsilon
for any constant
\epsilon>0
, which matches the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
.
Another consequence is that if
k\leq\sqrt{\log n}
, then the lower bound in Theorem
1.3
satisfies
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}\geq\frac{k}{2}-1
, which almost matches the upper bound
k/2
in Theorem
1.1
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is constructive as follows.
We first obtain an optimal solution
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
to
Mono-LP-Rel
whose supports are chains via the ellipsoid method.
Then, we take the top
2/k
of a fraction of the largest sets in the chains.
We show that such a collection of sets can be partitioned into groups of
k
sets, each of which has union
N
.
The existence of this partition implies that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k/2
.
In the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we construct
k
monotone submodular functions such that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}
.

Mono-Sub-MP
), which is a special case of

.

4/3
-approximation, and does not admit a

-approximation for every constant

.
For further recent works related to submodular partition problems, see

,

,

,

.

There are several recent works related to

.
Etesami
[
9
]
showed that multi-item welfare maximization under network externalities can be formulated as a special case of
MSCA
.
Deng et al.
[
7
]
introduced the generalized load-balancing (
GLB
) problem, and showed that
GLB

MSCA
when the outer norm is the
\ell_{1}
-norm.

Organization.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we show that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel

k/2
, which yields a
k/2
-approximation algorithm for

.

2
Approximation algorithm

In this section, we show that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k/2
, which yields a
k/2
-approximation algorithm for
Mono-MSCA
.

Let
N
be a finite set of cardinality
n
, and
k\geq 2
a positive integer.
For each
i\in[k]
, let
f_{i}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a monotone submodular function with
f_{i}(\emptyset)=0
.
Recall that
Mono-LP-Rel
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\textsc{Mono-LP-Rel})\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{minimize}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\ \ \ y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}}_{+}\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }i\in[k],

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)\chi_{S}=\mathbf{1}.

To prove the upper bound of the integrality gap, we show that an integer solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
achieving a
k/2
-approximation can be constructed from an optimal solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
.
Since the coefficient matrix of the constraints in
Mono-LP-Rel
is rational,
Mono-LP-Rel
has a rational optimal solution
(y^{*}_{1},\ldots,y^{*}_{k})
.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{i}:=\{S\subseteq N\mid y^{*}_{i}(S)>0\}
and
d_{i}:=|\mathcal{L}_{i}|
for each
i\in[k]
.
By the uncrossing operations, we may assume that
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is a chain for each
i\in[k]
.
Let
C_{i}^{1}\supsetneq C_{i}^{2}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq C_{i}^{d_{i}}
be the members of
\mathcal{L}_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
.
Let
M
be the product of
k(k-1)
and the denominators of
y^{*}_{i}(C_{i}^{j})
for all
i,j
.
Then,
M\cdot y^{*}_{i}
is an integer vector for every
i\in[k]
.
For each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[M]
, define
U_{i}^{j}\subseteq N
as follows:

\displaystyle U_{i}^{j}:=\begin{cases}C_{i}^{\ell}&\mathrm{if\ }1+\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell-1}M\cdot y_{i}^{*}(C_{i}^{p})\leq j\leq\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}M\cdot y_{i}^{*}(C_{i}^{p})\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }\ell\in[d_{i}],\\
\emptyset&\mathrm{if\ }1+\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{d_{i}}M\cdot y_{i}^{*}(C_{i}^{p})\leq j\leq M.\end{cases}

Intuitively, for each
i\in[k]
, we consider the multiset that contains
M\cdot y^{*}_{i}(C_{i}^{\ell})
copies of
C_{i}^{\ell}
for all
\ell\in[d_{i}]
, and
U_{i}^{j}
is the
j
-th largest set in the multiset for each
j\in[M]
.
By the definition of
U_{i}^{j}
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
i\in[k]
and
\ell=1,\ldots,d_{i}
, the number of
j\in[M]
with
U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}
is
M\cdot y_{i}^{*}(C_{i}^{\ell})
.

We now aim to construct an integer solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
(i.e., a partition of
N
) from
U_{i}^{j}
s that achieves
k/2
-approximation.
To this end, we prepare the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[k],j\in[M]}\dfrac{1}{M}f_{i}(U_{i}^{j})=\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}_{i},i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d_{i},i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(C_{i}^{\ell})f_{i}(C_{i}^{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d_{i},i\in[k]}\dfrac{1}{M}\left|\left\{j\in[M]\ \middle|\ U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}\right\}\right|\cdot f_{i}(C_{i}^{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[k],j\in[M]\\
U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }1\leq\ell\leq d_{i}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{M}f_{i}(U_{i}^{j})=\sum_{i\in[k],j\in[M]}\dfrac{1}{M}f_{i}(U_{i}^{j}).

The last equality follows since if
U_{i}^{j}\neq C_{i}^{\ell}
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,d_{i}
, then we have
U_{i}^{j}=\emptyset
by the definition.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in[M]
be positive integers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\leq M+k-1
.
Then,
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{a_{i}}=N
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{a_{i}}\neq N
.
Then, there exists
e\in N
such that
e\notin U_{i}^{a_{i}}
for every
i\in[k]
.
Combined with
U_{i}^{1}\supseteq U_{i}^{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq U_{i}^{M}
for each
i\in[k]
, this implies
e\notin U_{i}^{j}
for every
i\in[k]
and integer
j
with
a_{i}\leq j\leq M
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}M\cdot y^{*}_{i}(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{L}_{i},e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}M\cdot y^{*}_{i}(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq d_{i},e\in C_{i}^{\ell}\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}M\cdot y^{*}_{i}(C_{i}^{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq d_{i},e\in C_{i}^{\ell}\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}\left|\left\{j\in[M]\ \middle|\ U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[k]}\left|\left\{j\in[M]\ \middle|\ U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }\ell\in[d_{i}],\ e\in U_{i}^{j}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[k]}\left|\left\{j\in[a_{i}-1]\ \middle|\ U_{i}^{j}=C_{i}^{\ell}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }\ell\in[d_{i}]\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[k]}(a_{i}-1)\leq M-1.

(2.1)

Since
y^{*}_{1},\ldots,y^{*}_{k}
is a feasible solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}y_{i}^{*}(S)=1.

This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}M\cdot y_{i}^{*}(S)=M,

which contradicts the inequality (
2
).
∎

Since
M
is divisible by
k(k-1)
, we can denote
M=k(k-1)\cdot m
for some integer
m
.
For
k
positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in[M]
, we say that an ordered tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

covers

N
if
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{a_{i}}=N
.
We show that if there exist distinct
M\cdot 2/k=2m(k-1)
ordered tuples covering
N
, then one of them corresponds to an integer solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
that achieves
k/2
-approximation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that there exist
2m(k-1)
ordered tuples

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{1},\ldots,a_{k}^{1}),(a_{1}^{2},\ldots,a_{k}^{2}),\ldots,(a_{1}^{2m(k-1)},\ldots,a_{k}^{2m(k-1)})

such that

(1)

a_{i}^{j}\in[M]
for each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[2m(k-1)]
,

(2)

a_{i}^{j}\neq a_{i}^{\ell}
for each
i\in[k]
and
j,\ell\in[2m(k-1)]
with
j\neq\ell
,

(3)

each ordered tuple covers
N
.

Then, the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k/2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)=\sum_{i\in[k],j\in[M]}\dfrac{1}{M}f_{i}(U_{i}^{j})\geq\sum_{i\in[k],j\in[2m(k-1)]}\dfrac{1}{M}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}})

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{2m(k-1)}{M}\cdot\min\left\{\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}})\ \middle|\ j\in[2m(k-1)]\right\}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{k}\cdot\min\left\{\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}})\ \middle|\ j\in[2m(k-1)]\right\}.

(2.2)

The first inequality follows since by the monotonicity of
f_{i}
, we have
f_{i}(U_{i}^{j})\geq f_{i}(\emptyset)=0
for every
i\in[k]
and
j\in[M]
.
Let

\displaystyle j^{*}:=\underset{j\in[2m(k-1)]}{\operatorname{argmin}}\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}}).

Then, by (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\geq\dfrac{2}{k}\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}}).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{k}{2}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\geq\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}}).

(2.3)

Since
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}}=N
, there exists a partition
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
of
N
such that
U_{i}\subseteq U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}}
for every
i\in[k]
.
Then, by (
2.3
) and the monotonicity of
f_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{k}{2}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\geq\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}})\geq\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}).

This implies that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at most
k/2
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to show that there exist
2m(k-1)
ordered tuples satisfying the conditions in Lemma
2.4
.
For each positive integer
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, define
\mathrm{mod}(a)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}(a):=\begin{cases}r&\mathrm{if\ }a\equiv r\pmod{2m(k-1)}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }r\in[2m(k-1)-1],\\
2m(k-1)&\mathrm{if\ }a\equiv 0\pmod{2m(k-1)}.\end{cases}

For each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[2m(k-1)]
, define
a_{i}^{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle a_{i}^{j}:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{mod}(2m(i-1)+j)&\mathrm{if\ }1\leq i\leq k-1,\\
\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor&\mathrm{if\ }i=k.\end{cases}

We show that
(a_{i}^{j})_{i\in[k],j\in[2m(k-1)]}
satisfies conditions (1)–(3) in Lemma
2.4
.
For condition (1), we show that
1\leq a_{i}^{j}\leq M
for each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[2m(k-1)]
.
If
1\leq i\leq k-1
, then we have

\displaystyle 1\leq a_{i}^{j}=\mathrm{mod}(2m(i-1)+j)\leq 2m(k-1)\leq km(k-1)=M.

If
i=k
, then we have

\displaystyle a_{i}^{j}=\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor.

Since
(2m-j+1)(k-1)
is a multiple of
k-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))=p(k-1)

for some positive integer
p
.
This implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))\geq k-1.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle 1=k-1-(k-2)=k-1-\left\lfloor\dfrac{2m(k-1)-1}{2m}\right\rfloor\leq\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor,

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor\leq 2m(k-1)\leq km(k-1)=M.

Therefore, condition (1) holds for
(a_{i}^{j})_{i\in[k],j\in[2m(k-1)]}
.

For condition (2), we next show that
a_{i}^{j}\neq a_{i}^{\ell}
for each
i\in[k]
and
j,\ell\in[2m(k-1)]
with
j\neq\ell
.
If
1\leq i\leq k-1
, then by the definition of
a_{i}^{j}
, it is clear that
a_{i}^{j}\neq a_{i}^{\ell}
holds for each
j,\ell\in[2m(k-1)]
with
j\neq\ell
.
Consider the case when
i=k
.
Suppose to the contrary that
a_{k}^{j}=a_{k}^{\ell}
holds for some
j,\ell\in[2m(k-1)]
with
j\neq\ell
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor=\mathrm{mod}((2m-\ell+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\ell-1}{2m}\right\rfloor.

(2.4)

Since
\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))
and
\mathrm{mod}((2m-\ell+1)(k-1))
are multiples of
k-1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\ell-1}{2m}\right\rfloor

is a multiple of
k-1
by (
2.4
).
Combined with

\displaystyle 0\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\dfrac{\ell-1}{2m}\right\rfloor\leq k-2,

this implies that

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{\ell-1}{2m}\right\rfloor.

Hence, we have
2m(p-1)+1\leq j,\ell\leq 2mp
for some
p\in[k-1]
.
Let
j^{\prime}:=2mp-j
and
\ell^{\prime}:=2mp-\ell
.
By the definition of
p
, we have
0\leq j^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\leq 2m-1
.
By (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))=\mathrm{mod}((2m-\ell+1)(k-1)).

(2.5)

We also have

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))=\mathrm{mod}((2m-2mp+j^{\prime}+1)(k-1))=(j^{\prime}+1)(k-1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{mod}((2m-\ell+1)(k-1))=\mathrm{mod}((2m-2mp+\ell^{\prime}+1)(k-1))=(\ell^{\prime}+1)(k-1).

(2.6)

Hence, by (
2.5
) and (
2
) we have

\displaystyle(j^{\prime}+1)(k-1)=(\ell^{\prime}+1)(k-1).

This implies
j^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}
, which contradicts
j\neq\ell
.

For condition (3), we show that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}}=N
holds for every
j\in[2m(k-1)]
.
By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to show that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{j}\leq M+k-1
for every
j\in[2m(k-1)]
.
Take any
j\in[2m(k-1)]
.
Let
p\in[k-1]
be the unique positive integer such that
2m(p-1)+1\leq j\leq 2mp
.
Let
j^{\prime}:=2mp-j
.
Then, we have
0\leq j^{\prime}\leq 2m-1
.
By the definition of
a_{i}^{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathrm{mod}(2m(i-1)+j)+\mathrm{mod}((2m-j+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{j-1}{2m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathrm{mod}(2m(i-1)+2mp-j^{\prime})+\mathrm{mod}((2m-2mp+j^{\prime}+1)(k-1))-\left\lfloor\dfrac{2mp-j^{\prime}-1}{2m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k-p}(2m(i-1)+2mp-j^{\prime})+\sum_{i=k-p+1}^{k-1}(2m(i-1)+2mp-j^{\prime}-2m(k-1))

\displaystyle+(j^{\prime}+1)(k-1)-(p-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(2m(i-1)+2mp-j^{\prime})-2m(k-1)(p-1)+(j^{\prime}+1)(k-1)-(p-1)

\displaystyle=mk(k-1)-(k-1)(2m-2mp+j^{\prime})-2m(k-1)(p-1)+(j^{\prime}+1)(k-1)-(p-1)

\displaystyle=M+k-p\leq M+k-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
provides the construction of a
k/2
-approximate integer solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
.
We prove Theorem
1.2
by showing that such an integer solution can be constructed in polynomial time.

Theorem
1.2
.

We can obtain a rational optimal solution
(y_{1}^{*},\ldots,y_{k}^{*})
to
Mono-LP-Rel
whose supports
\mathcal{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{k}
are chains in polynomial time via the ellipsoid method
[
5
]
(see Appendix
A
for details).
Then, we define
U_{i}^{j}
for each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[M]
, and
a_{i}^{j}
for each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[2m(k-1)]
as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
If we can compute

\displaystyle j^{*}:=\underset{j\in[2m(k-1)]}{\operatorname{argmin}}\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}}),

then any partition
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
of
N
such that
U_{i}\subseteq U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j^{*}}}
for every
i\in[k]
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i})\leq\dfrac{k}{2}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\leq\dfrac{k}{2}\cdot\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\ \middle|\ X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ partition\ of\ }N\right\},

as desired.
Hence, it suffices to show that
j^{*}
can be computed in polynomial time.
To find
j^{*}
, we set
j_{1}:=1
.
Then, for
q=2,3,...,
we define
j_{q}
as the minimum integer such that
j_{q}>j_{q-1}
and there exists
i\in[k]
with
U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j_{q}}}\neq U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j_{q-1}}}
.
Recall that
n:=|N|
.
Since for each
i\in[k]
, we have
U_{i}^{1}\supseteq U_{i}^{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq U_{i}^{M}
and there are at most
|\mathcal{L}_{i}|\leq n
distinct sets among
\{U_{i}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
, we only need to define
j_{q}
for
q=1,2,\ldots,Q
with
Q\leq kn
.
Since we have

\displaystyle j^{*}=\underset{j\in\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{Q}\}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j}}),

we can find
j^{*}
by computing the values of
\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j_{q}}})
for all
q\in[Q]
.
By the definition of
U_{i}^{j}
and
a_{i}^{j}
, we can compute
j_{q}
in polynomial time for each fixed
q
.
Hence, since
Q\leq kn
, we can compute
j_{q}
and
\sum_{i\in[k]}f_{i}(U_{i}^{a_{i}^{j_{q}}})
for all
q\in[Q]
in polynomial time.
∎

3
Lower bound on the integrality gap

In this section, we show that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}
when
\log n\geq k
.
To this end, we explicitly construct monotone submodular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
such that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}
.

Suppose that
\log n\geq k
.
Let
p
be the unique positive integer such that
pk\leq\lfloor\log n\rfloor\leq(p+1)k-1
.
We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}\ \middle|\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}=pk-k+1\right\}.

By stars and bars (see e.g.,
[
17
, Chapter 1]
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}|=\binom{pk}{k-1}\leq 2^{pk}\leq 2^{\lfloor\log n\rfloor}\leq n=|N|.

Choose
e_{v}\in N
for each
v\in\mathcal{V}
so that all
e_{v}
are distinct.
For each
i\in[k]
, let

\displaystyle N_{i}:=\{e_{v}\in N\mid v\in\mathcal{V},v_{i}\geq 1\},

\displaystyle Z_{i}:=\{e_{v}\in N\mid v\in\mathcal{V},v_{i}=0\}.

For each
i\in[k]
, define
f_{i}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows: for all
S\subseteq N
,

\displaystyle f_{i}(S):=\begin{cases}\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in S\cap N_{i}\}\}&\mathrm{if\ }S\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset\ \mathrm{and\ }S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset,\\
0&\mathrm{if\ }S\cap N_{i}=\emptyset\ \mathrm{and\ }S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset,\\
2pk+1&\mathrm{if\ }S\cap Z_{i}\neq\emptyset.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

f_{i}
is monotone submodular for each
i\in[k]
.

Proof.

We first show monotonicity of
f_{i}
.
Take any
S,T\subseteq N
with
S\subseteq T
.
If
S\cap N_{i}=S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
, then we have
f_{i}(S)=0\leq f_{i}(T)
.
If
S\cap Z_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then we have
T\cap Z_{i}\neq\emptyset
, which implies
f_{i}(S)=f_{i}(T)=2pk+1
.
Consider the case when
S\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
.
If
T\cap Z_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(S)=\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in S\cap N_{i}\}\}\leq 2p+1-1=2p\leq 2pk+1=f_{i}(T).

If
T\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
, then since
T\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
follows from
S\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle=\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in S\cap N_{i}\}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in T\cap N_{i}\}\}=f_{i}(T).

Therefore,
f_{i}
is monotone.

We next show that
f_{i}
is submodular.
Take any
S,T\subseteq N
.
If
S\cap Z_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then by the monotonicity of
f_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(S)+f_{i}(T)=2pk+1+f_{i}(T)\geq 2pk+1+f_{i}(S\cap T)\geq f_{i}(S\cup T)+f_{i}(S\cap T).

The last inequality follows since
f_{i}(X)\leq 2pk+1
holds for every
X\subseteq N
by the definition of
f_{i}
.
If
S\cap N_{i}=S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
, then since
(S\cup T)\cap N_{i}=T\cap N_{i}
and
(S\cup T)\cap Z_{i}=T\cap Z_{i}
, we have
f_{i}(S\cup T)=f_{i}(T)
by the definition of
f_{i}
.
This implies that

\displaystyle f_{i}(S)+f_{i}(T)=f_{i}(S)+f_{i}(S\cup T)\geq f_{i}(S\cup T)+f_{i}(S\cap T).

The last inequality follows from the monotonicity of
f_{i}
.
Hence, by the symmetry between
S
and
T
, the remaining case is when
S\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
S\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
, and
T\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
T\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
.
In this case, we have
(S\cup T)\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
(S\cup T)\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
, which implies that

\displaystyle f_{i}(S\cup T)=\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in(S\cup T)\cap N_{i}\}\}.

Take
e_{v^{*}}\in\mathrm{argmax}\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in(S\cup T)\cap N_{i}\}
with the corresponding vector
v^{*}\in\mathcal{V}
.
Then, we have
e_{v^{*}}\in S\cap N_{i}
or
e_{v^{*}}\in T\cap N_{i}
.
We may assume without loss of generality that
e_{v^{*}}\in S\cap N_{i}
.
Then, we have
e_{v^{*}}\in\mathrm{argmax}\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in S\cap N_{i}\}
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(S)+f_{i}(T)=\max\{0,2p+1-v^{*}_{i}\}+f_{i}(T)=f_{i}(S\cup T)+f_{i}(T)

\displaystyle\geq f_{i}(S\cup T)+f_{i}(S\cap T).

The last inequality follows from the monotonicity of
f_{i}
.
Therefore,
f_{i}
is submodular.
∎

We now show that for the monotone submodular functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
defined above, any optimal solution
(y_{1}^{*},\ldots,y_{k}^{*})
to
Mono-LP-Rel
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}\right)\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\ \middle|\ X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ partition\ of\ }N\right\}.

(3.1)

The inequality (
3
) implies that the integrality gap of
Mono-LP-Rel
is at least
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

To prove (
3
), we first give an upper bound on the value
\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)
.
To this end, we construct a feasible solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
.
For each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[pk-k+1]
, let
N_{i}^{j}:=\{e_{v}\in N\mid v\in\mathcal{V},v_{i}\geq j\}
.
Note that
N_{i}^{1}=N_{i}
.
In what follows, we will use
N_{i}^{1}
instead of
N_{i}
for convenience.
Let
N^{c}:=N\setminus\{e_{v}\in N\mid v\in\mathcal{V}\}
.
For each
i\in[k]
, define
z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2^{N}}
as follows: for all
S\subseteq N
,

\displaystyle z_{i}(S):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{pk-k+1}&\mathrm{if\ }i=1\ \mathrm{and\ }\vskip 3.0ptS=N_{1}^{j}\cup N^{c}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }j\in[pk-k+1],\\
\vskip 3.0pt\dfrac{1}{pk-k+1}&\mathrm{if\ }2\leq i\leq k\ \mathrm{and\ }S=N_{i}^{j}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }j\in[pk-k+1],\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.2
.

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a feasible solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
.

Proof.

To show the feasibility of
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
, we show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}z_{i}(S)=1

(3.2)

for every
e\in N
.
For any
e\in N^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}z_{i}(S)=\sum_{j\in[pk-k+1]}z_{1}(N_{1}^{j}\cup N^{c})=\sum_{j\in[pk-k+1]}\dfrac{1}{pk-k+1}=1.

Take any
e\in N\setminus N^{c}
.
Since
e=e_{v}
for some
v\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N,e\in S\\
i\in[k]\end{subarray}}z_{i}(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[pk-k+1]\\
v_{1}\geq j\end{subarray}}z_{1}(N_{1}^{j}\cup N^{c})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[pk-k+1]\\
2\leq i\leq k,v_{i}\geq j\end{subarray}}z_{i}(N_{i}^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[pk-k+1]\\
v_{1}\geq j\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[pk-k+1]\\
2\leq i\leq k,v_{i}\geq j\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[pk-k+1]\\
i\in[k],v_{i}\geq j\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{pk-k+1}{pk-k+1}=1.

The second last equality follows since
\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}=pk-k+1
.
Hence, (
3.2
) holds for every
e\in N
, which implies that
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a feasible solution to
Mono-LP-Rel
.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
and the optimality of
y^{*}_{1},\ldots,y^{*}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\leq\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}z_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[pk-k+1]}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}f_{1}(N_{1}^{j}\cup N^{c})+\sum_{2\leq i\leq k,j\in[pk-k+1]}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}f_{i}(N_{i}^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[pk-k+1]}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}\cdot\max\{0,2p+1-j\}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq k,j\in[pk-k+1]}\frac{1}{pk-k+1}\cdot\max\{0,2p+1-j\}

\displaystyle=\frac{pk(2p+1)}{pk-k+1}.

(3.3)

To prove (
3
), we next provide a lower bound on the right hand side of (
3
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
be any partition of
N
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\geq pk+k.

Proof.

If
X_{j}\cap Z_{j}\neq\emptyset
for some
j\in[k]
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\geq f_{j}(X_{j})=2pk+1\geq pk+k.

Hence, it suffices to consider the case when
X_{i}\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
for every
i\in[k]
.
For each
i\in[k]
, define
x^{i}\in\mathcal{V}
as follows:

\displaystyle x^{i}_{j}:=\begin{cases}pk-k+1&\mathrm{if\ }j=i,\\
0&\mathrm{if\ }j\in[k]\mathrm{\ and\ }j\neq i.\end{cases}

Then, since
X_{i}\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
for each
i\in[k]
, we have
e_{x^{i}}\in X_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
.
Hence, we have
X_{i}\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
for every
i\in[k]
.
For each
i\in[k]
, we define
b_{i}:=\min\{v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in X_{i}\cap N_{i}\}
.
We now show that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq pk
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\geq pk+1
.
Then, since
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-1)\geq pk-k+1
, there exists
v\in\mathcal{V}
such that
v_{i}\leq b_{i}-1
for every
i\in[k]
.
Choose
i\in[k]
such that
e_{v}\in X_{i}
.
Since
X_{i}\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
by the assumption, we have
e_{v}\in X_{i}\cap N_{i}
, which contradicts the definition of
b_{i}
.
Hence, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq pk
.
Therefore, since
X_{i}\cap N_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
X_{i}\cap Z_{i}=\emptyset
for each
i\in[k]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max\{0,\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in X_{i}\cap N_{i}\}\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max\{2p+1-v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in X_{i}\cap N_{i}\}=2pk+k-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{v_{i}\mid e_{v}\in X_{i}\cap N_{i}\}

\displaystyle=2pk+k-\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\geq 2pk+k-pk=pk+k.

∎

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\ \middle|\ X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ partition\ of\ }N\right\}

\displaystyle\geq pk+k=\frac{2k(p+1)^{2}(pk-k+1)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k(p+1)(p^{2}k-k+p+1)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}=\frac{2k(p+1)(p^{2}k+(p-1)(k-1)-pk+2p)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2k(p+1)(p^{2}k-pk+2p)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}=\frac{pk(2p+2)(pk-k+2)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{pk(2p+1)(pk-k+2)}{2(p+1)(pk-k+1)}=\frac{pk(2p+1)}{pk-k+1}\cdot\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{k(p+1)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{pk(2p+1)}{pk-k+1}\cdot\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}\right).

(3.4)

The last inequality follows from the definition of
p
.
Combining (
3
) and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}\right)\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y^{*}_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)\leq\left(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\log n}\right)\cdot\frac{pk(2p+1)}{pk-k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i})\ \middle|\ X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ partition\ of\ }N\right\}.

Thus, the inequality (
3
) holds, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Appendix A
Convex program based on the Lovász extension

In this section, we show that
LP-Rel
is equivalent to the convex program relaxation of
MSCA
introduced by Chekuri and Ene
[
5
]
.
Let
N:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
f_{i}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a submodular function with
f_{i}(\emptyset)=0
for each
i\in[k]
.
Recall that
LP-Rel
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\textsc{LP-Rel})\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{minimize}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\ \ \ y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}}_{+}\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }i\in[k],

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)\chi_{S}=\mathbf{1}.

For a set function
f:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
f(\emptyset)=0
, the Lovász extension
\hat{f}:[0,1]^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
is defined as follows:
Take any
x\in[0,1]^{n}
.
Relabel the elements in
N
so that
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}
.
For convenience, let
x_{n+1}:=0
.
Let
S_{i}:=\{1,2,\ldots,i\}
for each
i\in[n]
.
The value of
\hat{f}(x)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{f}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})f(S_{i}).

Chekuri and Ene
[
5
]
introduced the following convex program relaxation of
MSCA
based on the Lovász extension:

\displaystyle(\textsc{LE-Rel})\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{minimize}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f}_{i}(x^{i})

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\ \ \ x^{i}\in[0,1]^{n}\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }i\in[k],

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sum_{i=1}^{k}x^{i}_{j}=1\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }j\in[n].

We now show that any feasible solution
({x^{1}},\ldots,x^{k})
to
LE-Rel
can be translated into a feasible solution
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
to
LP-Rel
that has the same objective value and whose supports are chains.
Take any feasible solution
({x^{1}},\ldots,x^{k})
to
LE-Rel
.
We will describe a translation from
x^{i}
to
y_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
.
We first relabel the elements in
N
so that
x^{i}_{1}\geq x^{i}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x^{i}_{n}
.
Let
x^{i}_{n+1}:=0
and
S_{j}:=\{1,2,\ldots,j\}
for each
j\in[n]
.
Then, for all
S\subseteq N
define
y_{i}(S)
as follows:

\displaystyle y_{i}(S):=\begin{cases}x_{j}^{i}-x_{j+1}^{i}&\mathrm{if\ }S=S_{j}\ \mathrm{and\ }x_{j}^{i}-x_{j+1}^{i}>0\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }j\in[n],\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then,
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
is a feasible solution to
LP-Rel
because for each
i\in[k]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N}y_{i}(S)\chi_{S}=\sum_{j\in[n]}(x^{i}_{j}-x^{i}_{j+1})\chi_{S_{j}}=x^{i}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)\chi_{S}=\sum_{i\in[k]}x^{i}=\mathbf{1}.

For each
i\in[k]
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N}y_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)=\sum_{j\in[n]}(x_{j}^{i}-x_{j+1}^{i})f_{i}(S_{j})=\hat{f_{i}}(x^{i}).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq N,i\in[k]}y_{i}(S)f_{i}(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f_{i}}(x^{i}),

which implies that
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
has the same objective value as
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{k})
.
Similarly, we can translate any feasible solution to
LP-Rel
whose supports are chains into a feasible solution to
LE-Rel
that has the same objective value.

Since
LE-Rel
can be solved in polynomial time via the ellipsoid method
[
5
]
, an optimal solution to
LP-Rel
whose supports are chains can be obtained in polynomial time.
Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
provided another equivalent linear programming formulation of
MSCA
called
LE-Rel
-Primal.
We note that
LP-Rel
is equivalent to
LE-Rel
-Primal.

Appendix B
k
-polymatroid intersection

Let
N
be a finite set and
k\geq 2
a positive integer.
For each
i\in[k]
, let
f_{i}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a submodular function with
f_{i}(\emptyset)=0
.
For a set function
f:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define
P(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x(S)\leq f(S)\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }S\subseteq N\}
, where
x(S):=\sum_{e\in S}x(e)
for every
S\subseteq N
.
Let
w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
be a weight vector.
The
weighted
k
-polymatroid intersection problem
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{LP}_{k\text{-poly}})\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{maximize}

\displaystyle\ \ \ w^{\top}x

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\ \ \ x\in P(f_{i})\ \mathrm{for\ every\ }i\in[k].

The linear program
\mathrm{LP}_{k\text{-poly}}
corresponds to the weighted polymatroid intersection when
k=2
.
When
w=\mathbf{1}
, the linear program
\mathrm{LP}_{k\text{-poly}}
is the dual linear program of
LP-Rel
.
For the case when
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
are matroid rank functions (i.e., the case of weighted
k
-matroid intersection), the integrality gap of
\mathrm{LP}_{k\text{-poly}}
is
2
when
k=3

[
13
]
, and it equals
k-1
for arbitrary
k
in the unweighted case
w=\mathbf{1}

[
12
]
.

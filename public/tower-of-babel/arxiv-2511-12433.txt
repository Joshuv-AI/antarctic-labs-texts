A Generalized Recurrence for fully degenerate Bell polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. This paper addresses the unnatural appearance of the two-variable degenerate Fubini polynomials in a recently derived Spivey-type recurrence relation for the fully degenerate Bell polynomials Bel n , λ ​ ( x ) \mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x) . To solve this, we introduce a new family of polynomials, denoted B n , λ ​ ( x ) B_{n,\lambda}(x) , which we also call the fully degenerate Bell polynomials, along with their two-variable counterparts, B n , λ ​ ( x , y ) B_{n,\lambda}(x,y) . Our main contribution is the derivation of natural Spivey-type recurrence relations for B n , λ ​ ( x ) B_{n,\lambda}(x) and B n , λ ​ ( x , y ) B_{n,\lambda}(x,y) using operator methods. We extend these results to the r r -counterparts, the fully degenerate r r -Bell polynomials B n , λ ( r ) ​ ( x ) B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x) and B n , λ ( r ) ​ ( x , y ) B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y) , providing Dobinski-like, finite sum, operator expressions, and Spivey-type recurrence relations for all the new polynomials.

1.
Introduction

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14-17]),

where the degenerate falling sequence is given by

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1),

and satisfies

(1)

(x+y)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(x)_{l,\lambda}(y)_{n-l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}_{\lambda}
are defined by (see [10,11,14])

(2)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
r\geq 0
, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are given as (see [15,17])

(3)

\displaystyle(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda},\ (n\geq 0),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Note that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}={n\brace k}
, where
{n\brace k}
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,20]).

The Bell polynomials are given by

(4)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5,20]).

By (
4
), we get

(5)

\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5,20]).

When
x=1,\ \phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1)
are called the Bell numbers.

In 2008, Spivey discovered a recurrence relation for Bell numbers:

(6)

\phi_{n+m}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}j^{n-k}{m\brace j}\binom{n}{k}\phi_{k},\ (n,m\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [23]).

It is known that the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by (see [8,10,21])

(7)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,11]).

When
x=1,\ \phi_{n,\lambda}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the degenerate Bell numbers.

From (
2
) and (
7
), we note that

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k},\ (n\geq 0),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [10,11]).

In [10], we obtained a recurrence relation for degenerate Bell polynomials given by

(8)

\phi_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m\brace k}_{\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}x^{k}\phi_{l,\lambda}(x).

In [12], we considered the fully degenerate Bell polynomials defined by

(9)

e_{\lambda}\Big(x\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)\Big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=1,\ \mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the fully degenerate Bell numbers. We observe here that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\phi_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0).

Recently, we showed the following Spivey’s formula for fully degenerate Bell polynomials (see [12]):

(10)

\mathrm{Bel}_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}{m\brace k}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{l}x^{k}F_{n-l,\lambda}^{(k)}(-\lambda l,k-m\lambda)\mathrm{Bel}_{l,\lambda}(x),

where
F_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(x,y)
are the two variable degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
k
given by

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}\bigg)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}(x,y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}F_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x,0)=F_{n}(x),

where
F_{n}(x)
are the Fubini polynomials defined by

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}k!x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

As general references of this paper, the reader may refer to [4,13,18-20,22].

The study of degenerate versions of special numbers and polynomials traces back to the pioneering work of Carlitz [3]. This field has recently experienced a resurgence of interest, with numerous degenerate counterparts of various special numbers and polynomials being investigated through diverse methods (see [1, 9, 11, 12, 14–17] and their references).
A Spivey-type recurrence relation was recently established for the fully degenerate Bell polynomials
Bel_{n,\lambda}(x)
(see (
10
)). However, this particular recurrence relation (
10
) is somewhat unnatural (see (
6
), (
8
)) because it involves the two-variable degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
k
, which are unrelated to the fully degenerate Bell polynomials. To resolve this issue, we introduce a new set of polynomials, which we also term fully degenerate Bell polynomials but denote with the distinct notation
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
. Our primary goal is to derive Spivey-type recurrence relations for these new polynomials,
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
, and their two-variable counterparts,
B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)
. Furthermore, we extend our investigation to find Spivey-type recurrence relations for their
r
-counterparts: the fully degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
, and the two-variable fully degenerate r-Bell polynomials,
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)
.
Our derivation utilizes the ‘multiplication by
x
’ operator
X
and the differentiation operator
D=\frac{d}{dx}
, which satisfy the fundamental commutation relation
DX-XD=1
. Alternatively, one could employ Boson operators (see [2, 15, 16]). For prior results on Spivey-type relations, the reader may refer to [5, 11, 12, 23]. Specifically, for
q
-analogues of Spivey’s Bell number formula, we direct the reader to [6, 7].

This paper is structured to clearly present our definitions and main findings regarding the newly introduced fully degenerate Bell polynomials. In Section 1, we begin by recalling essential facts and notation that will be used throughout the paper. Section 2 is the core of the paper, where we introduce the new fully degenerate Bell polynomials
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
and the two-variable fully degenerate Bell polynomials
B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)
.
Our results for these polynomials include:

\bullet

B_{n,\lambda}(x)
(Theorems 2.1, 2.3, 2.4): We provide a finite sum expression, a Dobinski-like formula, and an operator expression.

\bullet

B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)
(Theorems 2.5, 2.6, 2.7): Similarly, we establish a finite sum expression, a Dobinski-like formula, and an operator expression.

In Theorem 2.8, we derive the central Spivey-type recurrence relations for
B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)
and
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
:

\displaystyle B_{n+m,\lambda}(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}(y)_{j,\lambda}x^{j}B_{k,\lambda}(x,y-j\lambda),

\displaystyle B_{n+m,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}(1)_{j,\lambda}x^{j}B_{k,\lambda}(x,1-j\lambda).

Next, we define the fully degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)

and the two-variable fully degenerate
r
-Bell polynomials
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)
. We provide analogous results for these polynomials:

\bullet

B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
(Theorems 2.9, 2.10, 2.11): A finite sum expression, a Dobinski-like formula, and an operator expression.

\bullet

B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)
(Theorems 2.12, 2.13, 2.14): A finite sum expression, a Dobinski-like formula, and an operator expression.

Finally, in Theorem 2.15, we present the Spivey-type recurrence relations for
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)
and
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
:

\displaystyle B_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}(y)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}B_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y-k\lambda),

\displaystyle B_{n+m,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}{m+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k-m\lambda)_{n-l,\lambda}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}B_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,1-k\lambda).

In Section 3, we conclude the paper by summarizing the key findings and suggesting avenues for future research.

2.
Recurrence relations for fully degenerate Bell polynomials

In this section, we use the operators
X
and
D
defined by
Xf(x)=xf(x)
and
Df(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)
. They satisfy the commutation relation
DX-XD=1
.

Now, we redefine the polynomials
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)
in (
9
), again called the
fully degenerate Bell polynomials
but denoted by the different notation
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
, by

(11)

\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note from (
4
) that

(12)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)e_{\lambda}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)=e^{x(e^{t}-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
12
), we get

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}B_{n,\lambda}(x)=\phi_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0).

When
x=1,\ B_{n,\lambda}=B_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the fully degenerate Bell numbers.

From (
2
) and (
11
), we note that

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\bigg(\frac{1+\lambda x+\lambda x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+\lambda\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}=e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
13
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(14)

B_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}.

In particular, for
x=1
, we get

B_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\bigg)^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}.

Remark 2.2
.

From (
9
), we note that
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)
is given by

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}.

By (
11
) and Taylor expansion, we get

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}}{k!}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
15
), we obtain a Dobinski-like formula for
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(16)

B_{n,\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(k)_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}.

In particular, for
x=1
, we get

B_{n,\lambda}=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(k)_{n,\lambda}}{k!}.

Note that letting
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
16
) gives Dobinski’s formula:

\phi_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!}x^{k},\ (n\geq 0).

Using (
2
) and
DX-XD=1
, we show that

(17)

(XD)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}X^{k}D^{k},\ (n\geq 0),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [15,16]).

From (
16
), we note that

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}(XD)_{n,\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(XD)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(k)_{n,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}

\displaystyle=B_{n,\lambda}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

Therefore, by (
18
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, in terms of operators we have

B_{n,\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}(XD)_{n,\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x).

Now, we observe that

(19)

(XD)_{n+m,\lambda}=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}(XD)_{m,\lambda},\quad(m,n\geq 0).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we observe that

(20)

\displaystyle DX^{k}-X^{k}D

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-1}-X^{k-1}D)X+X^{k-1}(DX-XD)

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-1}-X^{k-1}D\big)X+X^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\big(DX^{k-2}-X^{k-2}D\big)X^{2}+2X^{k-1}=\cdots

\displaystyle=\big(DX-XD\big)X^{k-1}+(k-1)X^{k-1}=kX^{k-1}.

By (
20
), we get

(21)

\displaystyle\big(XD\big)X^{k}=X\big(DX^{k}\big)=X^{k}\big(XD+k\big),\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

For
m,n\geq 0
, by (
21
), we get

(22)

\displaystyle\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{j}

\displaystyle=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)\big(XD-m\lambda-\lambda\big)\cdots\big(XD-m\lambda-(n-1)\lambda\big)X^{j}

\displaystyle=X^{j}\big(XD+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}=X^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(XD)_{k,\lambda}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}.

From (
1
), (
17
), (
19
), (
21
) and (
22

(23)

\displaystyle\big(XD\big)_{n+m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}\big(XD\big)_{m,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\big(XD-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}X^{j}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}X^{j}\big(XD+j-m\lambda\big)_{n,\lambda}D^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}X^{j}(XD)_{k,\lambda}D^{j}.

Now, we define the
two variable fully degenerate Bell polynomials
by

(24)

\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}^{y}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
24

13

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}^{y}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{y-1}(x)e_{\lambda}^{y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{y-1}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(y)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{y-1}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(y)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{y-1}(x)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(y)_{k,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0

By (

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}e_{\lambda}^{y}\big(xe_{\lambda}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(y)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(y)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (

Theorem 2.6

For
n\geq 0
, we have

From (
28

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{y}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}(XD)_{n,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(y)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(y)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}

Thus, by (

Theorem 2.7

For
n\geq 0
, in terms of operators we have

B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\big(XD\big)_{n,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{y}(x)

By (

\displaystyle B_{n+m,\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}(XD)_{n+m,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{y}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(x)}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{m\brace j}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{k}(j-m\lambda)_{n-k,\lambda}X^{j}(XD)_{k,\lambda}D^{j}e_{\lambda}^{y}(x)

For

Theorem 2.9

For

3.
Conclusion

We successfully introduced new fully degenerate Bell polynomials,
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
, to address the issue of extraneous polynomials in a prior Spivey-type recurrence relation. By employing the operators
X
and
D
, we derived natural and comprehensive Spivey-type recurrence relations for
B_{n,\lambda}(x)
and
B_{n,\lambda}(x,y)
(Theorem 2.8), and for their
r
-counterparts,
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
and
B_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x,y)
(Theorem 2.15). Furthermore, we established their fundamental properties, including Dobinski-like formulas and operator expressions. This work not only provides a corrected and more natural framework for studying the recurrence relations of degenerate Bell polynomials but also lays a foundation for future investigations into other algebraic and combinatorial properties of these new degenerate polynomial families.

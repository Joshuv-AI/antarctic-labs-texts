Multi-Source Approximate Message Passing with Random Semi-Unitary Dictionaries
Burak Çakmak and Giuseppe Caire

Abstract
Motivated by the recent interest in approximate message passing (AMP) for matrix-valued linear observations with superposition of multiple statistically asymmetric signal sources , we introduce a multi-source AMP framework in which the dictionary matrices associated with each signal source are drawn from a random semi-unitary ensemble (rather than the standard Gaussian matrix ensemble.) While a similar model has been explored by Vehkaperä, Kabashima, and Chatterjee (2016) using the replica method, here we present an AMP algorithm and provide a high-dimensional yet finite-sample analysis. As a proof of concept, we show the effectiveness of the proposed approach on the problem of message detection and channel estimation in an unsourced random access scenario in wireless communication.

IV
Conclusion and Outlook

We consider an inference problem for matrix-valued linear observations involving
multiple statistically (arbitrary) asymmetric signal sources
, where dictionary matrices associated with each signal source are drawn from a random unitary ensemble. Accordingly, we have proposed an AMP algorithm and provided its high-dimensional analysis.
We apply our framework to the problem of message detection in unsourced random access for cell-free wireless networks.

Decoupling principles manifested in our AMP analysis and the RS ansatz of
the static problem (for a special setting) in
[
5
]
are consistent. This suggests the Bayesian optimality of the algorithm.

The proof of the conjecture outlined in Section
II-D
-specifically, the universality of Theorem
1
for dictionaries constructed from randomly signed Fourier matrices
[
6
,
8
]
- is left for future work and may constitute a significant theoretical contribution.

Extending the framework for “non-separable denoisers” across columns
[
3
]
is an interesting direction for future research. In this way, the framework becomes fully applicable for the application
[
2
]
.

It would also be interesting to extend the framework to general bi-unitarily invariant dictionary matrices. However, this extension would require singular value decomposition for each dictionary matrix, which poses a computational challenge. However, we expect that the
memory-AMP
approach
[
30
]
could resolve this issue.

\displaystyle{\bf Z}^{(t)}

\displaystyle={\bf N}-\sum_{u\leq U}{\bf P}_{u}\widehat{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Psi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)}

(49b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\bf T}}_{u}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{u}}{\bf P}_{u}^{\top}{\bf Z}^{(t)}

(49c)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Phi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)}

\displaystyle={\bf O}_{u}^{\dagger}\widetilde{{\bf T}}_{u}^{(t)}+{\bf T}_{u}^{(t)}\;

(49d)

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{u}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle={f}_{u,t}({\bf X}_{u}+\widehat{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Phi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)})-{\bf X}_{u}

(49e)

with the initialization
{{\bf T}}_{u}^{(1)}\doteq{\bf F}_{u}^{(1)}-{\bf X}_{u}
. Our goal is to verify the concentrations

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Psi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)}

\displaystyle={{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Psi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Psi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)}+\mathcal{O}(1)

(50)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Phi$}}}}_{u}^{(t)}

\displaystyle={\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Phi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Phi$}}}_{u}^{(t)}+\mathcal{O}(1)\;.

A-A

The Random-Matrix-Free Equivalent

We fix the update rules in (
49b
)-(
49c
) and (
49e
) and derive the
{\bf O}_{u}
-free representations of
{\bf O}_{u}{\bf T}_{u}^{(t)}
and
{\bf O}_{u}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\bf T}_{u}^{(t)}
. For convenience, we fix
u\in[U]
and remove the subscript
u
from the notation, e.g.
N\equiv N_{u}
,
{\bf O}\equiv{\bf O}_{u}
,
{\bf T}^{(t)}\equiv{\bf T}_{u}^{(t)}
, etc.

We begin with the block Gram-Schmidt notation:
Let
{\bf V}^{(1:t-1)}=\{{\bf V}^{(1)},{\bf V}^{(2)},\ldots,{\bf V}^{(t-1)}\}
be set of
N\times F
matrices
with
\langle{\bf V}^{(i)},{\bf V}^{(j)}\rangle=\mathcal{\delta}_{ij}{\bf I}_{F}
for all
i,j
. Then, for any matrix
{\bf B}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times F}
, by using the block Gram-Schmidt (orthogonalization) process,
we can always construct the new (semi-unitary) matrix

{\bf V}^{(t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{=}}\mathcal{GS}({\bf B}\mid{\bf V}^{(1:t-1)})

such that
\langle{\bf V}^{(t)},{\bf V}^{(s)}\rangle=\delta_{st}{\bf I}_{F}
and we have the block Gram-Schmidt decomposition

{\bf B}=\sum_{1\leq s\leq t}{\bf V}^{(s)}\langle{\bf V}^{(s)},{\bf B}\rangle\;.

(53)

Furthermore, we note that for the projection matrix

{\bf P}^{\perp}_{{\bf V}^{(1:t-1)}}\doteq{\bf I}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq s<t}{\bf V}^{(s)}({\bf V}^{(s)})^{\dagger}\;

(54)

if the matrix
{\bf Q}\doteq{\bf B}^{\dagger}{\bf P}^{\perp}_{{\bf V}^{(1:t-1)}}{\bf B}
is singular then the Gram-Schmidt process is unique and it is given by

{\bf V}^{(t)}\equiv\sqrt{N}{\bf P}^{\perp}_{{\bf V}^{(1:t-1)}}{\bf B}{\bf Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\;.

(55)

In particular, when
{\bf B}\sim_{\text{i.i.d.}}\mathcal{CN}({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle 0$}}},{\bf I}_{F})
, we have
{\bf Q}\sim\widetilde{\bf B}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\bf B}
where
\widetilde{\bf B}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N-(t-1)F)\times F}
and
\widetilde{\bf B}\sim_{\text{i.i.d.}}\mathcal{CN}({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle 0$}}},{\bf I}_{F})
; thereby the Gram-Schmidt process is (a.s.) unique when
N\geq Ft
.

We will adaptively use the following representation of the Haar unitary matrix.

Lemma 1
.

Let the random elements
\widetilde{\bf G}^{(1)},{\bf V}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times F}
and the random matrix
{{\bf O}^{(N-F)}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N-F)\times(N-F)}
be mutually independent. Let
\widetilde{\bf G}^{(1)}\sim_{\text{i.i.d.}}\mathcal{CN}({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle 0$}}},{\bf I}_{F})
,
\langle{\bf V}^{(1)},{\bf V}^{(1)}\rangle={\bf I}_{F}
and
{{\bf O}^{(N-F)}}
be Haar unitary. Then, for
\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}\doteq\mathcal{GS}(\widetilde{\bf G}^{(1)})
,

\displaystyle{\bf O}

\displaystyle\doteq\frac{1}{N}\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}({\bf V}^{(1)})^{\dagger}+{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp}{\bf O}^{(N-F)}({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp})^{\dagger}

(56)

is a Haar unitary and independent of
{\bf V}^{(1)}
. Here, we introduce the semi-unitary matrices
{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp},{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times(N-F)}
whose columns spanning the orthogonal complements of the column spans of
\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}
and
{\bf V}^{(1)}
, respectively. E.g., we have

{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp}({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp})^{\dagger}={\bf I}-\frac{1}{N}{\bf V}^{(1)}({\bf V}^{(1)})^{\dagger}={\bf P}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp}.

Proof.

(57)

\displaystyle+{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{\widetilde{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp})^{\dagger}\widetilde{\bf V}^{(2)}}^{\perp}{\bf O}^{(N-2F)}\left({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{({\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\Pi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\Pi$}}}_{{\bf V}^{(1)}}^{\perp})^{\dagger}{\bf G}^{(1)}}^{\perp}\right)^{\dagger}\;

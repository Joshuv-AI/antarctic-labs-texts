Assessing Oral Health-Related Quality of Life (OHRQoL) Using the Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP-14) among the Prisoners in the Central Jail in Vidarbha Region of Maharashtra, India: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Manish B. Shrigiriwar, Neelam D. Chandwani, Unnati Gedam, PN Ramaraj, Ranjana Deshmukh, Anjali Gayke
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The prison population is a physically, mentally, socially, and economically vulnerable sector in our society with neglected oral health. Dental check-ups not only provide the necessity of treatment but also create awareness regarding the importance of oral health among the incarcerated individuals.Aim:The study aims to evaluate the oral health status and its impact on oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) among the prisoners through the application of the oral health impact profile (OHIP-14).Methodology:A cross-sectional study design involved demographic details, oral examination to assess DMFT index, and structured OHIP-14 questionnaire to assess seven dimensions of the oral health impact profile of 440 male inmates. The responses were recorded in a five-point Likert scale from 0 to 4. The final score was calculated.Results:The overall mean OHIP score was 14.048 with a standard deviation of 9.995. Out of seven, the most affected domain was psychological discomfort (mean 2.49, SD 1.68) and the least affected domain was functional disability (mean 1.21, SD 1.18).Conclusion:The findings from this research can contribute valuable insights into the oral health challenges faced by incarcerated individuals, potentially guiding the development of targeted oral health interventions within correctional settings.

INTRODUCTION

The prison population is the most neglected sector in Indian society. These prison inmates belonging to low socioeconomic background, are usually unemployed and lack education. This tends to enhance their negligence toward general as well as oral health.[1] The prison community usually needs more oral health care when compared to the general population. According to Open Government Data (OGD) Platform India, there has been a gradual increase in the prison population all over India. According to the 2022 Prison Statistics India (PSI) report, there are around 5 lac prisoners in India. Attention to dental health is often overlooked among the authorities involved in correctional systems. Dental check-ups not only provide the necessity of treatment but also create awareness regarding the importance of oral health among the incarcerated individuals.

Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) is a multidimensional concept that includes a subjective assessment of an individual’s oral health, functional well-being, expectations, and satisfaction.[23] Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) is regarded as an integral part of general health and well-being. OHRQoL is recognized by the WHO as an important part of the Global Oral Health Program.[4] It can be measured as the oral health impact profile (OHIP) which can serve as a valuable tool for creating awareness about the impact of oral health on an individual’s life and their perceived requirement for dental care.[5] Specifically, the oral health impact profile (OHIP) questionnaire assesses how oral disorders affect people socially and influence their well-being. These 14 items within OHIP cover seven dimensions, including functional limitation, physical discomfort, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability, and handicaps. The OHIP-14 has been widely utilized globally for diverse research objectives, adapting to different languages and addressing regional concerns.[56]

The lack of oral healthcare facilities is the drawback of this enclosed prison community. The scarcity of dental professionals opting to work within the prison system exacerbates the existing challenges.[7] The period of imprisonment could be an additional opportunity for prisoners to learn about the importance and management of good oral health. Hence, it should be taken into consideration by the policymakers to organize regular dental check-ups, so that it benefits the individual not only in the period of incarceration but also when they step out into the society. The scarcity of dental resources explains why there are limited studies conducted within the prison system, particularly in India. Therefore, the present study aims to assess the oral health-related quality of life using the oral health impact profile (OHIP-14) among the prisoners in central jail in Vidarbha region of Maharashtra, India.

MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY

The study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee with registered number IEC/Pharmac/2024/880. This cross-sectional study was carried out on the 440 male prison inmates of central jail in Vidarbha region of Maharashtra, India. All the male prisoners willing to participate and gave written consent were only included in the study. The information related to demographic details, including age, gender, educational qualification, location whether urban or rural, duration of imprisonment in years, and behavioral factor like type of tobacco intake (smoke/smokeless), was collected. The oral examination was conducted by a designated dental officer using sterilized instruments. A structured questionnaire (OHIP-14), which consisted of 14 questions in seven different domains, was used to assess their OHRQoL. The questions were in English and also translated in regional language. OHIP-14 questionnaire involved seven dimensions, including functional limitation, physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability, and handicap. The responses were recorded in a five-point Likert scale from 0 to 4 (0 = never, 1 = seldom, 2 = sometimes, 3 = often, 4 = always). The OHIP-14 score ranged from 0 to 56. A higher score of OHIP-14 translates to major negative impact on OHRQoL and vice versa. The final score was obtained by calculating individual domains to measure OHIP-14 for the specific domains. The data collected were then transferred to Microsoft office excel spreadsheet for further statistical analyses.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive and inferential statistics were analyzed by IBM SPSS version 20.0 (IBM Corp. Released 2011. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 20.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp).

RESULTS

A total of 440 male prisoners participated in the study. The DMFT index of 440 prisoners shows that the total mean DMFT was 1.632 with standard deviation of 2.02 [Figure 1]. 70.5% (N = 310) prisoners responded to the question Q1 as “never” and 0% responded as “often and always.” Also 0% (N = 0) respondents marked “often” to the question Q14. The question of “Have you had trouble pronouncing any words because of problems with your teeth or mouth” had the lowest mean value with the mean of 0.40, which means that all the respondents answered rarely and the highest mean value co-related to the question Q6 “Have you felt tense because of problems with your teeth or mouth” with the mean value of 1.25 which means the respondents answered in average sometimes feel tense because of problems of mouth. None of the prisoners responded as “very often.” One hundred percent (N = 440) of participants had responded at least three impact as never, seldom, and sometimes. The outcomes that were always noticed by the participants were sense of taste worsening, tensed feeling, or embarrassment due to problems with teeth. The overall mean OHIP score and score for each domain was 14.048 with standard deviation of 9.995. Out of seven, the most affected domain was psychological discomfort (mean 2.49, SD 1.68) and least affected domain was functional disability (mean 1.21, SD 1.18). DISCUSSION

The results of this cross-sectional study highlight the duration of the imprisonment between one and six years, which to some extent must have impacted on the oral health status and oral health-related quality of life among the prisoners. The longer the duration of stay in the prison, the more negative the impact it may create on OHRQoL. According to the study done by Cunningham, the prison population demonstrated a significantly higher number of dental problems as compared to the general population.[8] The results presented in the study show that the age group of the participants ranged from 30 to 55 years, which falls under the middle age group of the Indian population. The probability of this age group being more aspiring and getting away dragged into antisocial activities is higher. 40.7% (N = 179) of the participants had either no education or a very insignificant level of education. In a study by Shahnazi H[9] in 2016, education improves the knowledge on oral health. In India, various initiatives and free education programs are run by the Indian government for the prisoners to promote the learning experience and boost up their moral.[10] In the present study, 68% (N = 299) of prisoners belong to rural areas.

The DMFT index is considered as one of the oldest and the most common methods to assess the prevalence of decayed teeth along with the requirement of dental treatment needs in a population.[11] A study among the prisoners in the central prison of Karnataka, India, in 2012 found the mean DMFT score to be 5.26.[12] This high score indicates a significant burden of dental caries among the incarcerated population in this region. Another study in 2022 by Balkrishna et al.[13] focused on prisoners in the central jails of Uttarakhand, India, reported an average DMFT score of 5.40 ± 6.49. This study highlighted not only the high prevalence of dental caries but also the lack of dental treatment facilities and poor oral hygiene practices among the prisoners. This could be the outcome of the limited dental care facilities, poor oral hygiene practices, dietary factors, substance abuse, and neglect prior to incarceration. However, the result of the present study with less DMFT of 1.632 does not support high DMFT index as compared to other similar studies in Indian prisoners.

The findings regarding smoking indicated that the prevalence of smoked tobacco products was higher. According to the data, 15.5% (n = 68) of people chewed tobacco regularly. A sizable portion of prisoners (53.90%, N = 197) chewed tobacco regularly in addition to smoking cigarettes. A study by Dahyia and Croucher[14] found that only 7.3% of prisoners chewed tobacco, and 12.5% chewed and smoked. These findings are at odds with the present study. Regarding tobacco chewing, our findings did not align with those of other studies.[14] Differences in Indian culture could be the cause of this variation.[15] For apparent reasons, the current study did not assess alcohol and illicit drug use. Although alcohol and illegal drugs are strictly forbidden in Indian jails, there have been multiple instances where few prisoners have been able to obtain these substances.

The mean OHIP score of the present study is 13.913, which is comparable to studies done by Fotedar et al.[16] The mean OHIP score was much higher when compared to general population, which was 8.8 as per the results of Jain et al.[17] This study holds certain limitations like the nature of study is cross-sectional, the risk factors could not be identified. Also, the prior oral health status of the prisoner was not known. A longitudinal study is appropriate for assessing the risk factors. The study constituted examination in a single prison only. Moreover, only those participants willing to participate were included in the study.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, addressing oral health issues among the prisoners is crucial for both individual well-being and broader public health. Implementing targeted dental care programs within correctional facilities can contribute to improved overall health, reduced healthcare costs, and enhanced reintegration into the society. Prioritizing oral health in the prison population is a key step toward fostering a more equitable and holistic approach to health care within the criminal justice system. As per the literature review, no such study has ever occurred in the prisoners of central jail of this geographical region of India. The prisoners deserve the quality health in prisons. Not only the oral screening but interventions should also be highly promoted for this group of population. Getting the quality health care is not just the government’s responsibility but also the right of every prisoner.

Lipschitz regularity of harmonic map heat flows into C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) spaces
Hui-Chun Zhang, Xi-Ping Zhu

Abstract
. In 1964, Eells and Sampson proved the famous long-time existence and convergence for the harmonic map heat flow into non‑positively curved Riemannian manifolds. Subsequently, Hamilton investigated the corresponding initial-boundary problem. In 1992, Gromov and Schoen developed a variational theory of harmonic maps into C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) metric spaces. This progress naturally motivated the study of the harmonic map heat flow into singular metric spaces. In the 1990s, Mayer and Jost independently studied convex functionals on C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) spaces and extended Crandall-Liggett’s theory of gradient flows from Banach spaces to C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) spaces to obtain the weak solutions, called semi-group weak solutions, for the harmonic map heat flow into C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) spaces. Very recently, Lin, Segatti, Sire, and Wang used an elliptic approximation method to obtain another class of weak solutions, called suitable weak solutions in the sense of the Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI), to the harmonic map heat flow into C ​ A ​ T ​ ( 0 ) CAT(0) spaces. They proved that these solutions are Lipschitz in space and 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -Hölder continuous in time. Since the semi-group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow enjoy the favorable long-time existence, uniqueness and well established long-time behaviors, it is natural to ask if the semi-group solutions possess the Lipschitz regularity. In the present paper, we answer this question. We show that the semi‑group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow into CAT(0) spaces are Lipschitz continuous in both space and time. We also establish an Eells–Sampson‑type Bochner inequality.



1.
Introduction

The harmonic map heat flow deforms a given map
u_{0}:M\to N\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
between two compact Riemannian manifolds via the equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u&=\Delta u+A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u),\quad(x,t)\in M\times(0,+\infty),\\
u|_{t=0}&=u_{0},\quad x\in M,\end{cases}

where
\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
and
A
is the second fundamental form of the embedding
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.

In 1964, Al’ber
[
1
,
2
]
introduced the flow, Eells and Sampson
[
16
]
proved the following famous theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Eells-Sampson
[
16
]
)
.

Let
M,N
be two compact Riemannian manifolds without boundaries. Suppose the sectional curvature of
N
is non-positive. Then for any
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M,N)
, the flow (
1.1
) admits a unique, smooth solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[0,+\infty),N)
. Moreover, there exists a subsequence of
\{u(\cdot,t_{j})\}
which converges to a harmonic map, as
t_{j}\to+\infty
.

This smoothness of
u(x,t)
in Theorem
1.1
is important, because it makes
t\mapsto u(\cdot,t)
a continuous deformation that preserves the homotopy class of the initial value
u_{0}
.

When
M
has non-empty smooth boundary
\partial M
, Hamilton
[
25
]
studied the initial-boudnary problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u&=\Delta u+A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u),\quad(x,t)\in M\times(0,+\infty),\\
u|_{t=0}&=u_{0},\quad x\in M,\\
u(x,t)&=\phi(x),\quad x\in\partial M,\ \ t>0.\end{cases}

Hamilton proved that if
N
has non-positive sectional curvature, then for given
u_{0},\phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M},N)
, there exists a unique smooth solution in
C^{\infty}(\overline{M}\times[0,+\infty),N).

In 1992, Gromov and Schoen
[
22
]
developed a theory of harmonic maps into
CAT(0)
complexes, by using the calculus of variations, to establish the
p
-adic superrigidity of lattices in groups of rank one. After this, many interesting results for harmonic maps into or between singular metric spaces have been obtained (see, e.g.,
[
36
,
8
,
37
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
40
,
15
,
53
,

,
17
,
6
,

,
23
,

,
19
,
57
]
and references therein).

Remark that variational methods have their limitations; for instance, the convergence in
W^{1,2}(M,N)
does not preserve some topological properties (examples can be found in
[
42
, page 110]
).
This naturally motivates the study of the harmonic map heat flow into singular metric spaces.

1.1.
Harmonic map heat flows into
CAT(0)
spaces

Since a general
CAT(0)
space
Y
might not be locally compact and can not be embedded into an Euclidean space, one has to use an intrinsic approach to study the harmonic maps and their heat flows into
CAT(0)
spaces.

The theory of Sobolev spaces for maps into metric spaces has been well-developed
[
36
,
31
,
35
,
39
,
28
,
47
,
21
]
.
Let
u
be a map from a bounded open domain
\Omega\subset M
to an arbitrary metric space
(Y,d_{Y})
. It is called a
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
map if its range is separable and if for some
P\in Y
, the function
d_{Y}(P,u(\cdot))
is in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Given a map
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y),

for each
\epsilon>0
, the
approximating energy

E^{u}_{\epsilon}
is defined as a functional on
C_{c}(\Omega)
, the space of continuous functions compactly supported on
\Omega
:

E^{u}_{\epsilon}(\phi):=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x){\rm d}\mu(x),\quad e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x):=\frac{n(n+2)}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}\!\!\frac{d^{2}_{X}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)}{\epsilon^{n+2}}{\rm d}\mu(y)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
, where
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with the standard metric. In
[
36
]
, Korevaar-Schoen proved that

(1.3)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}E^{u}_{\epsilon}(\phi)=E[u](\phi)

for some positive functional
E[u](\phi)
on
C_{c}(\Omega)
. A
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
-map
u
is called in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
if

\sup_{\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega),\ 0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1}E[u](\phi)<+\infty.

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space (i.e., a globally non-positively curved metric space in the sense of Alexandrov). The functional
u\mapsto E[u]
is convex on the metric space
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
, which is allowed to take the value
+\infty
. In the 1990’s, Jost
[
32
]
and Mayer
[
43
]
independently studied its resolvent
J_{h}
, that is, for any given
h>0
and
u_{0},\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
,
J_{h}(u_{0})
is the (unique) minimizer of

u\mapsto\frac{1}{2}E[u]+\frac{1}{2h}D^{2}(u,u_{0}),\qquad D^{2}(u,u_{0}):=\int_{\Omega}d^{2}_{Y}(u(x),u_{0}(x)){\rm d}\mu(x),

in the space
W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y):=\{v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)|\ d_{Y}(v(x),\psi(x))\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\}
.

The first theory of harmonic map heat flow into
CAT(0)
space was established by Mayer
[
43
]
. He extended Crandall-Liggett’s theory of gradient flows from Banach spaces to
CAT(0)
spaces, proving that the limit

(1.4)

u(x,t)=F_{t}(u_{0}):=\lim_{m\to+\infty}J^{m}_{t/m}(u_{0})

exists for every
t\in(0,+\infty)
, and satisfies the semi-group property
F_{t+s}(u_{0})=F_{t}\circ F_{s}(u_{0})
for all
t,s>0
. The resulting function
t\mapsto F_{t}(u_{0})\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
is called a
semi-group weak solution
to the
harmonic map heat flow
on
\Omega
with initial
u_{0}
and boundary data
\psi
. About the same time, Jost
[
35
]
also obtained the long-time existence of semi-group weak solutions.
Furthermore, Mayer
[
43
]
established the uniqueness of the semi-group weak solutions in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, and the regularity

(1.5)

t\mapsto u(x,t)\in Lip\big((t_{0},+\infty),L^{2}(\Omega,X)\big)\cap C^{1/2}\big([0,T),L^{2}(\Omega,X)\big)

for any
t_{0}>0
and
T<+\infty
. Moreover, the long-time asymptotic limit

u_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to+\infty}F_{t}(u_{0})

exists, and is the unique harmonic map with boundary data
\psi
(i.e., the minimizer of
E[u]
in
W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
).

In
[
3
]
, Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré introduced a new idea to study the gradient flows of convex functions on a non-smooth metric space via the Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI),

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm d}{\rm dt}D^{2}(u(t),v)+E[u(t)]\leqslant E[v]\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathscr{D}^{\prime}(0,+\infty),\quad\forall\ v\in W^{1,2}.

Moreover, Gigli and Nobili
[
20
]
showed recently the existence and uniqueness of the gradient flows in the sense of the EVI, and some behaviors similar to what happens for gradient flows in Hilbert spaces.
Very recently, Lin, Segatti, Sire, and Wang
[
41
]
used an elliptic approximation method to construct weak solutions to the harmonic map heat flow in the sense of the EVI into
CAT(0)
spaces. Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold without boundary. They considered the minimizers
u_{\epsilon}(x,t):M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to Y
of

E_{\epsilon}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t/\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\int_{M}\left(\epsilon|\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}\right){\rm d}t{\rm d}\mu(x).

Given
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(M,Y)
with
E(u_{0})<+\infty
, they
[
41
]
proved the following results:

(a)

For any
\epsilon>0
,
there exists a unique minimizer
u_{\epsilon}\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+};Y)
with
u_{\epsilon}(0)=u_{0}
and
\int_{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}(|\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2})e^{-t/\epsilon}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t<+\infty.

(b)

There exists a unique curve
u(t)\in L^{\infty}((0,+\infty);W^{1,2}(M,Y))
such that

u_{\epsilon}\overset{\epsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}u
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+})

and that
u(t)
is a
suitable weak solution of harmonic map heat flow in the sense of EVI
; namely, it satisfies
u(0)=u_{0}
, the EVI, and

u\in AC^{2}((0,+\infty),L^{2}(M,Y))
(i.e., there exists a function
f\in L^{2}(0,+\infty)
with

D(u(t),u(s))\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}f(r){\rm d}r
for any
0<s<t<+\infty.)

(c)

Assume additionally that the space
Y
is locally compact. Then
u\in C^{\alpha}(M\times(0,+\infty),Y)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

1.2.
Main result on the Lipschitz regularity

Lipschitz continuity of harmonic maps plays a central role in establishing rigidity theorems of geometric group theory (see, for example,
[
22
]
for the rigidity of lattices in groups of rank one, and
[
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
for the rigidity of Teichemüller spaces, and the references therein for this topic). The first Lipschitz regularity for harmonic maps into singular space was established by Gromov and Schoen in their seminal work
[
22
]
. Caffarelli and Lin
[
7
]
proved the Lipschitz regularity of harmonic maps into an Euclidean splitting
\mathbb{R}^{L}=\oplus_{k}\mathbb{R}^{L_{k}}
, where the subspaces
\mathbb{R}^{L_{k}}
are orthonormal subspaces.
In 1997, Jost
[
34
]
and Lin
[
40
]
independently proved that every harmonic map from a finite-dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature bounded from below to a
CAT(0)
space is locally Hölder continuous. They further conjectured that this Hölder continuity could be improved to Lipschitz continuity (see
[
40
, page 119]
and
[
35
, page 38]
). This conjecture was resolved by the authors in
[
56
]
. Recently, Mondino-Semola
[
45
]
and Gigli
[
19
]
have independently proved that the Lipschitz regularity for a harmonic map from an
RCD
space into a
CAT(0)
space. Additionally, Assimos-Gui-Jost
[
5
]
proved the local Lipschitz continuity for sub-elliptic harmonic maps from
n
-dimensional Heisenberg groups into a
CAT(0)
space.

For a harmonic map heat flow
u(x,t)
into a non-smooth
CAT(0)
space, Lin, Segatti, Sire, and Wang
[
41
]
established the Lipschitz continuity in
x
-variable for a wide class of
CAT(0)
space that it is decribed in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Lin-Segatti-Sire-Wang
[
41
]
)
.

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space satisfying the following properties:

(a)

(Y,d)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
can be realized as a subset of an Euchlidean space
\mathbb{R}^{L}
for some large
L
and satisfies

(b)

for any
\delta>0
and
P\in Y
, there exists a
r_{P,\delta}>0
such that

\left|\frac{d_{Y}(P,Q)}{|P-Q|}-1\right|\leqslant\delta,\quad\ \forall Q\in Y\ \ {\rm with}\ \ d_{Y}(P,Q)<r_{P,\delta}.

If
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies
E(u_{0})<\infty
and
d(u_{0},Q)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
Q\in Y
, then the suitable weak solution
u
of the harmonic map heat flow in the sense of th EVI is Lipschitz continuous in
x
and Hölder continuous with exponent
1\over 2
in
t
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
.

They conjecture that their result in Theorem
1.2
holds for
any

CAT(0)
spaces (see
[
41
, Page 7]
). Meanwhile,
they also pointed out
[
41
, Page 8]
that: “The Crandall-Liggett scheme only produces weak solutions and it is difficult to infer from the argument that the objects enjoy higher regularity. ”

It is therefore very interesting to investigate the Lipschitz regularity of weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow. The main result of this paper is an affirmal anwser of this problem. More precisely, we establish the Lipschitz regularity of semi-group weak solutions to the harmonic map heat flow, along with an Eells-Sampson-type Bochner inequality.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric_{M}\geqslant K
, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be an arbitrary
CAT(0)
space (not necessarily locally compact). Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, and let
u(x,t)
be a semi-group weak solution to the harmonic map heat flow, given by (
1.4
), from
\Omega
to
Y
with initial data
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
and boundary data
\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
. Suppose that the image of
u_{0}
is bounded in
Y
. Then

(i)

u(x,t)
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty);

(ii)

the pointwise spatial Lipschitz constant

(1.6)

{\rm lip}_{x}u(x,t):=\limsup_{y\to x,\ y\not=x}\frac{d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)}{d(y,x)},\quad\forall t\in(0,+\infty),

is in
V_{2,\rm loc}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty))\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty))
and satisfies the following Eells-Sampson-type Bochner inquality:

(1.7)

(\Delta-\partial_{t})({\rm lip}_{x}u)^{2}\geqslant 2|\nabla{\rm lip}_{x}u|^{2}+2K({\rm lip}_{x}u)^{2}

on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
in the sense of distributions.

Recall that a function
f(x,t)\in V_{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
means

(1.8)

{\rm esssup}_{0<t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f(\cdot,t)|^{2}{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\right)^{1/2}<+\infty;

and a function
f(x,t)\in V_{2,{\rm loc}}(\Omega\times(0,T))
means
f(x,t)\in V_{2}(\Omega^{\prime}\times(a,b))
for any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
and any
(a,b)
with
0<a<b<T.

We remark that our Theorem
1.3
allows the domain
\Omega=M
when the manifold
M
is compact without boundary.

1.3.
Outline of the proof and the organization of this paper

In Sect. 2, we provide some necessary notations and preliminary results concerning Sobolev spaces of maps and geometry of
CAT(0)
spaces. We also collect some properties of the semi-group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow obtained by Mayer in
[
43
]
. In particular, we observe a simple but important fact

u(x,t)\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty),Y),

which can be derived from Mayer’s regularity result (
1.5
) (see Proposition
2.5
for the details).

The first step to the proof of the main result, Theorem
1.3
, is to establish the locally Hölder continuous of
u(x,t)
. This will be given in Sect. 4 (see Theorem
4.1
). Recall that the classical De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory implies the local Hölder regularity for weak solutions of elliptic and parabolic equations with divergence form. But one can not apply the theory to the harmonic maps or their heat flows into
CAT(0)
spaces directly, because the second fundamental forms on the right-hand side of the equations (
1.1
) are unbounded in general, even if the target spaces
Y
are embedded in Euclidean spaces. Let
P
be any fixed point in a
CAT(0)
space
Y
and let
u(x)
be a (weak) harmonic map into
Y
. In
[
34
]
and
[
40
]
, Jost and Lin independently observed the following partial differential inequality:

\Delta d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x)\big)\geq 2|\nabla u|^{2}(x).

and developed a modified version of the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory to get Hölder continuity for any (weak) harmonic map
u:\Omega\to Y
. The core of the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory consists of several iterations based on the (local) Poincaré inequality. The existence of the positive term
2|\nabla u|^{2}(x)
on the right-hand side of the above partial differential inequality is the key ingredient to perform the iteration arguments.

In the present paper, we consider the harmonic map heat flow
u(x,t)
into
CAT(0)
spaces and try to adapt Jost and Lin’s method. We first prove

(\Delta-\partial_{t})d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x,t)\big)\geq 2|\nabla u|^{2}(x,t),\quad\forall P\in Y.

Noticing that the right-hand side of the above inequality involves only the spatial derivatives, when adapting Jost and Lin’s arguments from the elliptic case to the parabolic setting, one requires the following form of (local) Poincaré inequality,

(1.9)

\int_{t-r^{2}}^{t}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{x,r}|^{2}dxdt\leqslant Cr^{2}\int_{t-2r^{2}}^{t}\int_{B_{2r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{2}dxdt,

where
f_{x,r}:=\frac{1}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)}fdx.
The term
|\nabla f|
on the righ-hand side of (
1.9
) involves only the derivative of space variable
x
. Due to the loss of the time derivative on the right-hand side, it is clear that (
1.9
) does not hold for a general function
f(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
. This is an essential difference from the elliptic case. Nevertheless, when
u(x,t)
is a semi-group weak solution to the harmonic map heat flow, based on Mayer’s regularity result (
1.5
), we will prove that a modified version of such (local) Poincaré inequality is still expected (see Lemma
4.4
for a precise statement).

The second step to the proof of Theorem
1.3
is to show the Lipschitz continuity in the time variable
t
. Estimating the derivatives in the time variable is absent in the elliptic case of harmonic maps. Our key observation is to show a subsolution property for
d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),v(x,t))
of any two semi-group weak solutions
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
to the harmonic map heat flow. Indeed, we will prove that the function

d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x,t),v(x,t)\big)\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty},

and satisfies

(1.10)

\big({\Delta}-\partial_{t}\big)d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),v(x,t))\geqslant 2R_{u^{t},v^{t}}(x)

in the sense of distributions, for almost all
t
, where
R_{u^{t},v^{t}}(x)
is defined as

R_{u^{t},v^{t}}(x):=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{c_{n,2}}{\epsilon^{n}}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)-d_{Y}\big(v(x,t),v(y,t)\big)}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}{\rm d}\mu(y).

This subsolution property will be established in Sect. 3 (Theorem
3.4
). In Sect. 5, we will apply the formula (
1.10
) to any semi-group weak solution
u(x,t)
and then choosing
v(x,t)=u(x,t+s)
to get the desired the Lipschitz continuity of
u(x,t)
in the time variable
t
.

Another immediate application is the
L^{\infty}
-stability of the harmonic map heat flow. We expect the formula (
1.10
) should have other applications in establishing rigidity theorems of geometric group theory.

The third step to the proof of Theorem
1.3
is to show the Lipschitz continuity in the spatial variable. This is carried out in Sect. 7 (with some necessary tools given in Sect. 6). We will extend our method in
[
56
]
to construct a new nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow to the semi-group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow. The crucial point is to show the nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow is a supersolution of the heat equation in the sense of viscosity. We now explain the idea as follows.

To simplify the exposition, we assume here that the domain manifold
M
has nonnegative Ricci curvature. Recalling in our previous work
[
56
]
, we developed a nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow method to prove the Lipschitz regularity of any harmonic map
u
from
\Omega
to
Y
. The nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow in
[
56
]
was defined as follows: for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
,

(1.11)

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\inf_{y\in\Omega}\left\{\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)\right\},\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{\prime},

It is a regularization of the function of
d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)
of two-group variables
x,y
. We then showed that the nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow
f_{\varepsilon}(x)
is superharmonic. The deep reason for the superharmonicity is that the function
d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)
satisfies some elliptic-type partial differential equation of variables
x,y
.

For the parabolic case we are studying here, the most natural approach is to construct a nonlinear “Hamilton–Jacobi” flow for the harmonic map heat flow as:

(1.12)

f_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\inf_{(y,s)\in\Omega\times(0,T)}\left\{\frac{d^{2}(x,y)+|t-s|^{2}}{2\varepsilon}-d_{Y}\bigl(u(x,t),u(y,s)\bigr)\right\},\quad\forall(x,t)\in\Omega^{\prime}\times(0,T),

and then try to extend the elliptic method to the parabolic setting for establishing the space–time local Lipschitz continuity of
u
.
But we encounter a new difficulty: because the function
d_{Y}(u(x,t),u(y,s))
has two time variables
t,s
, we have not yet found an appropriate parabolic-type partial differential equation for the variables
t,s,x,y
.
Fortunately, as we have obtained the Lipschitz regularity in time in Sect. 5, it allows us to address the spatial Lipschitz regularity only. This leads us to consider a “revised” nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow in the following form:

(1.13)

f_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\inf_{y\in\Omega}\left\{\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)\right\},\quad\forall(x,t)\in\Omega^{\prime}\times(0,T).

The benefit of (
1.13
) is that the function
d_{Y}(u(x,t),u(y,t))
contains just one time variable, and satisfies a parabolic-type partial differential equation (see Lemma
7.3
for the details).

We will show that
f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
is a supersolution of the heat equation in the sense of viscosity (see Lemma
7.6
for a precise statement). We will also establish a connection between the nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow and the square norm of the spatial gradient (see Lemma
7.7
for the details). It is analogous to the classical Hamilton-Jacobi equation. This indicates that the mean value inequality for subsolutions of the heat equation will give an upper bound for the norm of the spatial gradient. So the local Lipschitz regularity of
u(x,t)
will be established.

In the final section, we will prove the Eells-Sampson-type Bochner inequality (
1.7
), which is a parabolic version of our previous joint work with Xiao Zhong
[
55
, Theorem 1.9]
for harmonic maps into
CAT(0)
spaces. Our idea is to refine the arguments in Section 7 by lifting the power of the distance function in the definition of the nonlinear “Hamilton-Jacobi” flow from the usual index 2 to a higher order index
p
to extract quantitative information.

Remark 1.4
.

Mayer’s existence result for semi-group weak solutions to the harmonic map heat flow has been extended to the setting of maps from
RCD
spaces into
CAT(0)
spaces (see
[
23

). In fact, the arguments presented in Sect. 3, 4, and 5 of this paper can be adapted to the more general setting of maps from
RCD
spaces into
CAT(0)
spaces. The only difficulty in the extension is the arguments in Sect. 7, where we need a theory of viscosity solutions for parabolic equations on
RCD
spaces. We plan to address this problem in the future.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with metric
g
and
Ric
be
the Ricci curvature,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
B_{r}(x)
be the geodesic ball with radius
r
and center
x
.
We also denote
\mu
as the canonical measure on
M
. To simplify the notation,
the volume is denoted by
|E|:=\mu(E)
for any measurable set
E\subset M
. For any
f\in L^{1}(E)
, we denote by
\fint_{E}f{\rm d}\mu:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f{\rm d}\mu.

2.1.
Sobolev spaces of maps

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a complete metric space.
A Borel measurable map
u:\Omega\to Y
is said to be in the space
L^{p}(\Omega,Y)
,
1\leqslant p<+\infty
, if it has a separable range and, for some (hence, for all)
P\in Y
,

\int_{\Omega}d^{p}_{Y}\big(u(x),P\big){\rm d}\mu(x)<+\infty.

The space
L^{p}(\Omega,Y)
is a complete metric space, equipped with distance
D_{L^{p}}
as

(2.1)

D^{p}_{L^{p}}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}d_{Y}^{p}\big(u(x),v(x)\big){\rm d}\mu(x),\quad\forall\ u,v\in L^{p}(\Omega,Y).

Given a map
u\in L^{p}(\Omega,Y),
for each
\epsilon>0
, the
approximating energy

E^{u}_{p,\epsilon}
is defined in
[
36
]
as a functional on
C_{c}(\Omega)
, the space of continuous functions with compact supports in
\Omega
, as follows:

E^{u}_{p,\epsilon}(\phi):=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\cdot e^{u}_{p,\epsilon}(x){\rm d}\mu(x),\quad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega),

where

(2.2)

e^{u}_{p,\epsilon}(x):=c_{n,p}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}\frac{d_{Y}^{p}(u(x),u(y))}{\epsilon^{n+p}}{\rm d}\mu(y),

where the constant
c_{n,p}=(n+p)(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|x^{1}|^{p}d\sigma(x))^{-1}
, and
\sigma
is the cannonical Riemannian volume on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. In particular,
c_{n,2}=\frac{n(n+2)}{\omega_{n-1}}
, where
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with standard metric. We say that
u
has finite
p
-energy (and write
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,Y)
for
p>1
and
u\in BV(\Omega,Y)
for
p=1
), if

(2.3)

E_{p}[u]:=\sup_{0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1,\ \phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)}E_{p}[u](\phi)<+\infty,

where
E_{p}[u](\phi):=\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}E^{u}_{p,\epsilon}(\phi)
. For
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
and
C>0
, define

\phi_{\epsilon}^{C}(x):=(1+C\epsilon)\big(\phi(x)+\max_{d(y,x)\leqslant 2\epsilon}|\phi(y)-\phi(x)|\big).

It was proved
[
36
, Lemma 1.4.2]
that there exists a contstant
C_{g}>0
(depending only on Ricci curvature control of the metric
g
) such that

(2.4)

E^{u}_{p,\epsilon}(\phi)\leqslant E_{p}[u](\phi_{\epsilon}^{C_{g}}),\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)

for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
(indeed, by the proof of
[
36
, Lemma 1.3.1, Page 576]
,
\epsilon
can be chosen so that for any
x\in{\rm supp}(\phi)
and any
y\in B_{\epsilon}(x)
, there exists a unique geodesic from
x
to
y
; for example,
\epsilon<\min\{{\rm Inj}(\Omega)/2,d({\rm supp}(\phi),\partial\Omega)/4\}
is enough).

For the case
p=2
, to simplify the notations, we denote by

D(u,v):=D_{L^{2}}(u,v),\quad\forall u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega,Y),

and also
e^{u}_{\epsilon}:=e^{u}_{2,\epsilon}
,
E^{u}_{\epsilon}:=E^{u}_{2,\epsilon}
and
E[u]:=E_{2}[u]
for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
.

We collect some properties of
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, which can be found in
[
36
,
21
,
45
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, and let
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
.

(1)

(Energy density)
There exists a nonnegative function (called energy density)
e_{u}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
such that
E[u](\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\phi e_{u}{\rm d}\mu
for all
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
, and that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}e_{\epsilon}^{u}(x)=e_{u}(x)\quad\mu{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega\ {\rm and\ also\ in}\ L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega).

(2)

(Representation by heat kernel)
For almost all point
x\in\Omega
, one has

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(y,x)d_{Y}^{2}\big(u(x),u(y)\big){\rm d}\mu(y)=e_{u}(x),

where
p_{s}(x,y)
is the heat kernel on
M
. (This has been established even in the setting of general
RCD
metric measure spaces, see
[
45
, Proposition 3.3]
and
[
21
,
19
]
.)

(3)

(Equivalence for
Y=\mathbb{R}
)
If
Y=\mathbb{R}
, the above space
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

is equivalent to the usual Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, and furthermore,

e_{u}(x)=|\nabla u|^{2}(x),\quad\mu{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

(4)

(Conctraction for maps)
If
\Phi:Y\to Z
is a
1-
Lipschitz map, then
\Phi\circ u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Z)
and
e_{\Phi\circ u}\leqslant e_{u}
a.e. in
\Omega
; in particular, for any
P\in Y
and letting
f_{P}:=d_{Y}(P,u(x))
, it holds
f_{P}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla f_{P}|^{2}(x)\leqslant e_{u}(x)
almost all
x\in\Omega.

(5)

(Poincaré inequality)
There is a constant
c_{\Omega}>0
depending only on
\Omega
, such that for any ball
B_{r}(x)
with
B_{2r}(x)\subset\Omega
, and any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, it holds

(2.5)

\inf_{q\in Y}\int_{B_{r}(x)}d^{2}_{Y}\big(q,u(x)\big){\rm d}\mu(x)\leqslant c_{\Omega}\cdot r^{2}\int_{B_{2r}(x)}e_{u}(x){\rm d}\mu(x).

(See also
[
38
]
.)

2.2.
CAT(0)
spaces

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a complete metric space. A curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
is called a
geodesic
if
d_{Y}(\gamma(t),\gamma(s))=|t-s|d_{Y}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))
for any
t,s\in[0,1].
A metric space
(Y,d_{Y})
is called a
geodesic space
if, for every pair of points
P_{0},P_{1}\in Y
, there exists a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
such that
\gamma(0)=P_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=P_{1}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A geodesic space
(Y,d_{Y})
is called a
CAT(0)
space, if it holds

(2.6)

d^{2}_{Y}(P,\gamma(t))\leqslant(1-t)d^{2}_{Y}(P,\gamma(0))+td^{2}_{Y}(P,\gamma(1))-t(1-t)d_{Y}^{2}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))

for any
P\in Y
and any geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
.

If
(Y,d_{Y})
is a
CAT(0)
space, then for any two points
P_{0},P_{1}
, there exists
uniquely
geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
such that
\gamma(0)=P_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=P_{1}.

We need the following lemma, essentially due to Reshetnyak
[
48
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space. Take any ordered sequence
\{P,Q,R,S\}\subset Y
.
We denote the distance
d_{Y}(A,B)
abbreviatedly by
d_{AB}.

(1)

We have

d_{PR}^{2}+d_{QS}^{2}\leqslant d_{PQ}^{2}+d_{QR}^{2}+d_{RS}^{2}+d_{SP}^{2}-\left(d_{RS}-d_{PQ}\right)^{2},

(2)

Let point
Q_{m}
be the mid-point of
Q
and
R
(i.e.
d_{QQ_{m}}=d_{RQ_{m}}=\frac{1}{2}d_{QR}
), then we have

(d_{PS}-d_{QR})\cdot d_{QR}\geqslant(d^{2}_{PQ_{m}}-d^{2}_{PQ}-d^{2}_{Q_{m}Q})+(d^{2}_{SQ_{m}}-d^{2}_{SR}-d^{2}_{Q_{m}R}).

(3)

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
and let
P_{\lambda}
be the point in geodesic
PS
such that
d_{PP_{\lambda}}=\lambda d_{PS}
, then we have

\lambda\left(d^{2}_{PS}+d_{PQ}^{2}-d^{2}_{SQ}\right)\leqslant d^{2}_{PP_{\lambda}}+d_{PQ}^{2}-d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q}.

(4)

Let
\lambda,\mu\in(0,1)
, let
P_{\lambda}
be the point in geodesic
PS
such that
d_{PP_{\lambda}}=\lambda d_{PS}
, and let
Q_{\mu}
be the point in geodesic
QR
such that
d_{QQ_{\mu}}=\mu d_{QR}
. Then we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\leqslant&\mu(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PR}+(1-\mu)\lambda d^{2}_{QS}\\
&+\mu\lambda d^{2}_{SR}+(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)d^{2}_{PQ}-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}-\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}_{QR},\end{split}

and

(2.8)

d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\leqslant 2(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PQ}+2\lambda d^{2}_{RS}+2|\lambda-\mu|^{2}d^{2}_{QR}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

Proof.

Both (1) and (2) are special cases of Corollary 2.1.3 in
[
36
]
. Precisely, (1) is given by (2.1vi) in
[
36
]
with
\alpha=0
, and (2) is given by (2.1v) in
[
36
]
with
t=1/2
and
\alpha=1
, see also
[
56
, Lemma 5.2]
.

For (3), by (
2.6
), we have

d^{2}_{QP_{\lambda}}\leqslant\lambda d^{2}_{QS}+(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PQ}-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}.

Rewriting this, we get

\begin{split}\lambda\Big(d^{2}_{PQ}-d^{2}_{QS}+d^{2}_{PS}\Big)&\leqslant d^{2}_{PQ}-d^{2}_{QP_{\lambda}}-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}+\lambda d^{2}_{PS}\\
&=d^{2}_{PQ}-d^{2}_{QP_{\lambda}}+\lambda^{2}d^{2}_{PS}.\end{split}

This is the assertion (3), since
\lambda d_{PS}=d_{PP_{\lambda}}.

For (4), considering the point
P_{\lambda}
and geodesic
QR
, the (
2.6
) implies

d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\leqslant\mu d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}R}+(1-\mu)d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q}-\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}_{QR}.

By using (
2.6
) again to the geodesic
PS
and the point
Q
(or
R
, resp.), we obtain

d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q}\leqslant(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PQ}+\lambda d^{2}_{SQ}-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS},

d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}R}\leqslant(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PR}+\lambda d^{2}_{SR}-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}.

Combining the above three inequalities, we conclude (
2.7
).

Let
Q_{\lambda}
be the point in geodesic
QR
such that
d_{QQ_{\lambda}}=\lambda d_{QR}
. By using
[
36
, (2.1iv) in Corollary 2.13]
, we have

d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}}\leqslant(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PQ}+\lambda d^{2}_{RS}.

Combining the triangle inequality, we get

\begin{split}d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}&\leqslant\Big(d_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}}+d_{Q_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\Big)^{2}\leqslant 2d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}}+2d^{2}_{Q_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\\
&\leqslant 2(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PQ}+2\lambda d^{2}_{RS}+2|\lambda-\mu|^{2}d^{2}_{QR},\end{split}

where we have used
d_{Q_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}=|\lambda-\mu|d_{QR}
in the last inequality. The proof is finished.
∎

2.3.
Harmonic map heat flows into
CAT(0)
spaces

From now on, the target space
(Y,d_{Y})
is always assumed to be a
CAT(0)
space.
Given any
\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, we put

W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y):=\big\{u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)\ \big|\ d_{Y}(u,\psi)\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\ {\rm and}\ E[u]\leqslant E[\psi]\big\}.

Since
(Y,d_{Y})
is of
CAT(0)
, the functional

u\mapsto\frac{1}{2}E[u]

is convex on the metric space
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
. According to
[
43
, Theorem 3.4]
, for any
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
there exists uniquely a gradient flow
t\mapsto F_{t}(u_{0})\in W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
of
E[u]/2
with
F_{0}(u_{0})=u_{0}
, defined on
(0,+\infty)
. We call such map
u(x,t):=F_{t}(u_{0})
a
semi-group weak solution to the harmonic map heat flow
with initial-doundary value data
u_{0},\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
. We recall the construction of
F_{t}
in
[
43
]
as follows. Let
h>0
,
J_{h}(u_{0})
is the minimizer of

\frac{1}{2}E[u]+\frac{1}{2h}D^{2}(u,u_{0}),

which is unique in
W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
. The gradient flow
F_{t}
is defined by

F_{t}(u_{0}):=\lim_{m\to+\infty}J^{m}_{t/m}(u_{0})=\lim_{m\to+\infty}J_{t/m}\circ J_{t/m}\circ\cdots\circ J_{t/m}(u_{0}).

The existence of this limit has been shown in
[
43
, Theorem 1.13]
.

We collect some properties of the semi-group weak solutions obtained in
[
43
]
as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space and let
u(x,t)
be a semi-group weak solution of the harmonic map heat flow on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
. Denote by
u^{t}(\cdot):=u(\cdot,t)
for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
. We have the following:

(i)

(Semigroup property)

F_{t+s}=F_{t}\circ F_{s}
for any
t,s>0
.

(ii)

(Non-increasing of energy)

E[u^{t}]\leqslant E[u^{s}]
for any
t>s>0
.

(iii)

For any
\bar{u}\in W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
and
t>s>0,
we have

(2.9)

D^{2}(\bar{u},u^{t})-D^{2}(\bar{u},u^{t-s})\leqslant s\big(E[\bar{u}]-E[u^{t}]\big).

(iv)

(Regularity of flow)
The function
t\mapsto u^{t}
is in
C^{1/2}([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega,Y))\cap Lip([t_{*},T],L^{2}(\Omega,Y))
for any
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
. Precisely, given any
t_{*},T\in(0,+\infty)
with
t_{*}<T
, there exist two constant
L:=L_{t_{*},T}>0
and
B=B_{T}>0
such that

(2.10)

D(u^{t},u^{s})\leqslant L|t-s|,\quad\forall t,s\in[t_{*},T],

and

D(u^{t},u^{s})\leqslant B|t-s|^{1/2},\quad\forall t,s\in[0,T].

(v)

(Reguarity of energy)
The function
t\mapsto E[u^{t}]
is continuous on
[0,+\infty)
and Lipschitz continuous on
[t_{*},T]
for any
T>t_{*}>0
.

Proof.

The (i) is
[
43
, Theorem 2.5]
. The (ii) is
[
43
, Corollary 2.6]
. The (iii) is
[
43
, Lemma 2.37]
. The (iv) comes from the combination of
[
43
, Theorem 2.9]
and
[
43
, Theorem 2.2]
. The (v) is
[
43
, Corollary 2.10]
.
∎

The above Lemma
2.4
(ii) implies
u(x,t)\in L^{\infty}\big((0,+\infty),W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)\big).

The following proposition shows that
u(x,t)\in W^{1.2}(\Omega\times(t_{*},T),Y)
for any
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Given any
t_{*},T\in(0,+\infty)
with
t_{*}<T
, it holds

(2.11)

\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}e_{2,\epsilon}^{u}(x,t){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\leqslant C_{1}\cdot(T-t_{*})\cdot\Big(L^{2}+E[u^{t_{*}}]\Big)

for any
\epsilon\in(0,r_{0})
, where
L
is in (
2.10
),
r_{0}:=\min\{t_{*}/2,(T-t_{*})/2\}
, and
\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\Omega:d(x,\partial\Omega)>\epsilon\}
. The constant
C_{1}
depends only on
n
and the Riemannian metric
g
on
\Omega
.

In particular,

u(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\times(t_{*},T),Y)
.

Proof.

For any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\epsilon}\times(t_{*},T)
, the triangle inequality implies

d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),u(y,s))\leqslant 2d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),u(x,s))+2d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,s),u(y,s))

for any
(y,s)\in B_{\epsilon}(x,t)
.
We have

(2.12)

\begin{split}e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x,t)&\leqslant c_{n+1,2}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x,t)}\frac{d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),u(x,s))+d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,s),u(y,s))}{\epsilon^{n+1+2}}{\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}s\\
&=c_{n+1,2}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{s}(x))}{\epsilon^{n+3}}{\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}s\\
&\qquad\ +c_{n+1,2}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{d^{2}_{Y}(u^{s}(x),u^{s}(y))}{\epsilon^{n+3}}{\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}s\\
&\leqslant c_{n+1,2}|B_{\epsilon}(x)|\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\frac{d^{2}_{Y}(u(x,t),u(x,s))}{\epsilon^{n+3}}{\rm d}s+\frac{c_{n+1,2}}{c_{n,2}\cdot\epsilon}\cdot\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}e^{u^{s}}_{\epsilon}(x){\rm d}s.\end{split}

By (
2.10
), we have

(2.13)

\begin{split}\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{s}(x)){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}s{\rm d}t&\leqslant\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}D^{2}(u^{t},u^{s}){\rm d}s{\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant 2\epsilon\cdot(L\epsilon)^{2}(T-t_{*}).\end{split}

By using (
2.4
) to a function
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1
,
\phi\equiv 1
on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
and
d({\rm supp}(\phi),\partial\Omega)\geqslant r_{0}/2
), we get

\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}e_{\epsilon}^{u^{s}}(x){\rm d}\mu(x)\leqslant E[u^{s}](\phi^{C_{g}}_{\epsilon})\leqslant C_{g}^{\prime}\cdot E[u^{s}]

for any
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, where we have used
\phi_{\epsilon}^{C_{g}}\leqslant 3(1+C_{g}\epsilon)\leqslant 3+3C_{g}:=C^{\prime}_{g}
whenever
\epsilon\leqslant 1
. Therefore, we have

(2.14)

\begin{split}\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}e^{u^{s}}_{\epsilon}(x){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}s{\rm d}t\ &\leqslant C^{\prime}_{g}\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}E[u^{s}]{\rm d}s{\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant C^{\prime}_{g}\int_{t_{*}+\epsilon}^{T}2\epsilon\cdot\max_{s\in[{t-\epsilon},{t+\epsilon}]}E[u^{s}]{\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant 2\epsilon\cdot C^{\prime}_{g}\int_{t_{0}+\epsilon}^{T}E[u^{t_{*}}]{\rm d}s,\end{split}

for all
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, where the last inequality we have used Lemma
2.4
(ii). Integrating (
2.12
) over
\Omega_{\epsilon}\times(t_{*}+\epsilon,T)
, noticing
|B_{\epsilon}(x)|\leqslant c_{n,K}\epsilon^{n}
(by Bishop inequality), and substituting (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) , we obtian the desired estimate (
2.11
).

Finally, noticing that
\chi_{\Omega_{\epsilon}\times(t_{*}+\epsilon,T)}\to 1
almost all in
\Omega\times(t_{*},T)
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, by (
2.11
)
and the Fatou’s lemma, letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we conclude
u(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\times(t_{*},T),Y)
. The proof is finished. ∎

3.
The subsolution property of the distance between harmonic map heat flows

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space. Assume that
u^{t}(x)=u(x,t)
is a semi-group weak solution of the harmonic map heat flow in
W^{1,2}_{\psi}(\Omega,Y)
with the initial data
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
and boundary data
\psi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
.
The main result in this section is Theorem
3.4
, a subsolution property of the distance between two semi-group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow.

We first prove the following boundedness for
u(x,t)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
M_{0}>0
,
P_{0}\in Y
, if
u_{0}(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, then for any
t>0
,
u^{t}(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix any
h>0
let
u:=J_{h}(u_{0})
. We first show that
u(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
. Let
\pi_{0}:Y\to\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
be the projection from
Y
to
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
, defined by

\pi_{0}(P)=\begin{cases}P,&{\rm if}\ P\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})},\\
{\rm the\ nearest\ point\ from}\ P\ {\rm to}\ \overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})},&{\rm if}\ P\not\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}.\end{cases}

Since the ball
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
is convex,
\pi_{0}
is well-defined, and is
1
-Lipschitz. Therefore, by Proposition
2.1
(4), the map
\pi_{0}\circ u\in W^{1,2}_{u}(\Omega,Y)
and
E[\pi_{0}\circ u]\leqslant E[u].

By
u_{0}(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
and the assumption that
Y
is
CAT(0)
, we have

d_{Y}\big(u_{0}(x),\pi_{0}(u(x))\big)\leqslant d_{Y}(u_{0}(x),u(x)),

for almost all
x\in\Omega
. This implies
D(u_{0},\pi_{0}\circ u)\leqslant D(u_{0},u)
; and hence
\pi_{0}\circ u
is also a minimizer of
v\mapsto E[v]/2+\frac{1}{2h}D^{2}(u_{0},v)
. From the uniqueness of
J_{h}(u_{0})
, we conclude that
D(u,\pi_{0}\circ u)=0
. This implies
d_{Y}(\pi_{0}\circ u(x),u(x))=0
for almost all
x\in\Omega,
and hence that

u(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
.

For any
t>0
and any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, from the above argument, we see that the image of
J_{t/m}(u_{0})
is contained in
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
. By repeating, we see that the image of
J^{m}_{t/m}(u_{0})
is contained in
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
. Letting
m\to+\infty
, we conclude that

\lim_{m\to+\infty}J^{m}_{t/m}(u_{0})(x)
is in
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
. That is,
u^{t}(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
. ∎

We shall consider the variations given by two Sobolev maps. Letting
u,v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, we define a function
R_{u,v}
on
\Omega
by

(3.1)

R_{u,v}(x):=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}R^{u,v}_{\epsilon}(x),

where

R^{u,v}_{\epsilon}(x):=\frac{c_{n,2}}{\epsilon^{n}}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}\left(\frac{d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)-d_{Y}\big(v(x),v(y)\big)}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}{\rm d}\mu(y).

It is easy to check that
R_{u,v}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Indeed, by

\Big(d_{Y}\big(u(x),u(y)\big)-d_{Y}\big(v(x),v(y)\big)\Big)^{2}\leqslant 2d^{2}_{Y}(v(x),v(y))+2d^{2}_{Y}(u(x),u(y)),

we have, for any
\epsilon>0
, that

(3.2)

R^{u,v}_{\epsilon}(x)\leqslant 2e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)+2e^{v}_{\epsilon}(x).

Therefore, by (
3.1
) and using the fact that
e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)\to e_{u}(x)
and
e^{v}_{\epsilon}(x)\to e_{v}(x)
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we have

R_{u,v}(x)\leqslant 2e_{u}(x)+2e_{v}(x),\quad\mu{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in\Omega.

This implies
R_{u,v}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u,v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,Y)
. Then the function

w(x):=d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),v(x)\big)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Suppose
\phi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1.
For any
x\in\Omega
,
u_{\phi}(x)
and
v_{\phi}(x)
denote the points in the (unique) geodesic connecting
u(x)
and
v(x)
such that

\begin{split}d_{Y}\big(u_{\phi}(x),u(x)\big)&=\phi(x)\cdot d_{Y}\big(u(x),v(x)\big),\\
d_{Y}\big(v_{\phi}(x),v(x)\big)&=\phi(x)\cdot d_{Y}\big(u(x),v(x)\big).\end{split}

Then the maps
x\mapsto u_{\phi}(x)
and
x\mapsto v_{\phi}(x)
are in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,Y)
. Moreover, it holds

(3.3)

\begin{split}&E[u_{\phi}]+E[v_{\phi}]-E[u]-E[v]\\
\leqslant&-\int_{\Omega}\left<{\nabla\phi},{\nabla((1-2\phi)w)}\right>{\rm d}\mu-2\int_{\Omega}\big(\phi-\phi^{2}\big)R_{u,v}{\rm d}\mu.\end{split}

Proof.

Thoughout of this proof, we always denote by
d_{PQ}:=d_{Y}(P,Q)
for any
P,Q\in Y
, for simplifying the notations.

(i) We first show that
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Let
M_{0}>0
and some
P_{0}\in Y
such that
u(x),v(x)\in\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
.
The triangle inequality implies
w^{1/2}(x)=d_{u(x)v(x)}\leqslant 2M_{0}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, and

\begin{split}|w^{1/2}(x)-w^{1/2}(y)|^{2}=|d_{u(x)v(x)}-d_{u(y)v(y)}|^{2}\leqslant 2d^{2}_{u(x)u(y)}+2d^{2}_{v(x)v(y)}\end{split}

for almost all
x,y\in\Omega
. This implies, for any
\epsilon>0
, that

e^{\sqrt{w}}_{\epsilon}(x)\leqslant 2e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)+2e^{v}_{\epsilon}(x).

Thus, we have
w^{1/2}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),
by the definition of
e_{u}
and
e_{v}
. Hence we conclude that
w^{1/2}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. It follows
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(ii) Now we want to show that
u_{\phi}
and
v_{\phi}
are in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
From
d_{u(x)v(x)}\leqslant 2M_{0}
and
u_{\phi},v_{\phi}
in geodesic
u(x)v(x)
, we have

d_{u_{\phi}(x),P_{0}}\leqslant 3M_{0}
and
d_{v_{\phi}(x),P_{0}}\leqslant 3M_{0}
for almost all
x\in\Omega
. Therefore,
u_{\phi}
and
v_{\phi}
are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,Y)\subset L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
, since
\Omega
is bounded.

We put
P=u(x)
,
Q=u(y)
,
R=v(y)
,
S=v(x)
,
\lambda=\phi(x)
and
\mu=\phi(y)
. Then
P_{\lambda}=u_{\phi}(x)
and
Q_{\mu}=u_{\phi}(y)
. By using (
2.8
) and the fact
d_{QR}\leqslant 2M_{0}
, we get

\begin{split}d_{u_{\phi}(x)u_{\phi}(y)}^{2}=d^{2}_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}\leqslant&2d^{2}_{PQ}+2d^{2}_{RS}+2|\lambda-\mu|^{2}\cdot 4M_{0}^{2}\\
=&2d^{2}_{u(x)u(y)}+2d^{2}_{v(x)v(y)}+8M_{0}^{2}\cdot|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}.\end{split}

This implies for any
\epsilon>0
that

e^{u_{\phi}}_{\epsilon}(x)\leqslant 2e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)+2e^{v}_{\epsilon}(x)+8M^{2}_{0}\cdot e^{\phi}_{\epsilon}(x).

Therefore, letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we conclude that
u_{\phi}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
. The same argument states
u_{\phi}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
too.

(iii) In the last step, we want to show (
3.3
). Let
x,y\in\Omega
. We continue to put
P=u(x)
,
Q=u(y)
,
R=v(y)
,
S=v(x)
,
\lambda=\phi(x)
and
\mu=\phi(y)
. Then
P_{\lambda}=u_{\phi}(x)
and
P_{1-\lambda}=v_{\phi}(x)
. By (
2.7
) in Lemma
2.3
, we get

\begin{split}d_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}^{2}+d_{P_{1-\lambda}Q_{1-\mu}}^{2}&\leqslant[\mu(1-\lambda)+\lambda(1-\mu)]\big(d^{2}_{PR}+d^{2}_{QS}\big)\\
&\quad+[(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)+\lambda\mu]\big(d_{PQ}^{2}+d^{2}_{RS}\big)\\
&\quad-2\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}-2\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}_{QR}.\end{split}

Combining with Lemma
2.3
(1), we get

\begin{split}d_{P_{\lambda}Q_{\mu}}^{2}+d_{P_{1-\lambda}Q_{1-\mu}}^{2}&\leqslant d^{2}_{PQ}+d^{2}_{RS}+[\lambda+\mu-2\lambda\mu]\cdot\Big(d^{2}_{QR}+d^{2}_{PS}-(d_{RS}-d_{PQ})^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad-2\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}-2\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}_{QR}\\
&=d^{2}_{PQ}+d^{2}_{RS}-(\lambda-\mu)\Big[(1-2\lambda)d^{2}_{PS}-(1-2\mu)d^{2}_{QR}\Big]\\
&\quad-[(2\lambda-2\lambda^{2})+(\mu-\lambda)(1-2\lambda)]\cdot(d_{RS}-d_{PQ})^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\begin{split}&d_{u_{\phi}(x)u_{\phi}(y)}^{2}+d_{v_{\phi}(x)v_{\phi}(y)}^{2}-\big(d_{u(x)u(y)}^{2}+d_{v(x)v(y)}^{2}\big)\\
\leqslant\ &-\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\Big[\big(1-2\phi(x)\big)w(x)-\big(1-2\phi(y)\big)w(y)\Big]\\
&+[-(2\phi(x)-2\phi^{2}(x))+|\phi(y)-\phi(x)|]\cdot(d_{v(x)v(y)}-d_{u(x)u(y)})^{2}.\end{split}

where we have used
w(x,y)=d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x),v(x)\big)
and
-(\mu-\lambda)(1-2\lambda)\leqslant|\mu-\lambda|
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, using the definition of
e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)
and noticing that
|\phi(y)-\phi(x)|\leqslant c_{1}\epsilon
for any
y\in B_{\epsilon}(x)
, we have

\begin{split}&e^{u_{\phi}}_{\epsilon}(x)+e^{v_{\phi}}_{\epsilon}(x)-\big(e^{u}_{\epsilon}(x)+e^{v}_{\epsilon}(x)\big)\\
\leqslant\ &-c_{n,2}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)\big[\big(1-2\phi(x)\big)w(x)-\big(1-2\phi(y)\big)w(y)\big]}{\epsilon^{n+2}}{\rm d}\mu(y)\\
&\ +\big[-2(\phi(x)-\phi^{2}(x))+c_{1}\cdot\epsilon\big]\cdot R^{u,v}_{\epsilon}(x).\end{split}

Integrating this inequality over
{\rm supp}(\phi)
, and letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, the estimate (
3.3
) follows from Fatou’s lemma and the fact
\phi-\phi^{2}\geqslant 0
.
∎

The following lemma deals with the time derivative for the weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u^{t}(x),v^{t}(x)
be two semi-group weak solutions of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
. Suppose that
\phi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
such that
0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1
. Denoted by
u^{t}_{\phi}(x),v^{t}_{\phi}(x)
are the points in the (unique) geodesic connecting
u^{t}(x)
and
v^{t}(x)
such that

\begin{split}d_{Y}\big(u^{t}_{\phi}(x),u^{t}(x)\big)&=\phi(x)\cdot d_{Y}\big(u^{t}(x),v^{t}(x)\big),\\
d_{Y}\big(v^{t}_{\phi}(x),v^{t}(x)\big)&=\phi(x)\cdot d_{Y}\big(u^{t}(x),v^{t}(x)\big).\end{split}

Then we have for any
0<s<t
that

(3.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)&\left(d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(x),v^{t}(x)\big)-d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t-s}(x),v^{t-s}(x)\big)\right){\rm d}\mu(x)\\
\leqslant\ &\Big(D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t-s})+D^{2}(u^{t}_{\phi},u^{t})-D^{2}(u^{t}_{\phi},u^{t-s})\Big)\\
&+\Big(D^{2}(v^{t},v^{t-s})+D^{2}(v^{t}_{\phi},v^{t})-D^{2}(v^{t}_{\phi},v^{t-s})\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

For any
x\in\Omega
, by applying Lemma
2.3
(3) to
P=u^{t}(x),

S=v^{t}(x)
,
Q=u^{t-s}(x)
and
\lambda=\phi(x)
, we get

\begin{split}&\ \phi(x)\Big(d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),u^{t}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{t-s}(x))-d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),u^{t-s}(x))\Big)\\
\leqslant&\ d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}_{\phi}(x),u^{t}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{t-s}(x))-d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}_{\phi}(x),u^{t-s}(x)),\end{split}

and similarly

\begin{split}&\ \phi(x)\Big(d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),u^{t}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),v^{t-s}(x))-d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),v^{t-s}(x))\Big)\\
\leqslant&\ d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}_{\phi}(x),v^{t}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),v^{t-s}(x))-d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}_{\phi}(x),v^{t-s}(x)).\end{split}

By applying Lemma
2.3
(1) to
P=u^{t}(x),

Q=u^{t-s}(x)
,
R=v^{t-s}(x)
and
S=v^{t}(x)
, we have

\begin{split}&d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),v^{t-s}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),u^{t-s}(x))\\
\leqslant\ &d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),v_{t}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t}(x),v^{t-s}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(v^{t-s}(x),u^{t-s}(x))+d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t-s}(x),u^{t}(x)).\end{split}

Summing up the above three inequalities and integrating over
\Omega
, the desired estimate (
3.4
) follows.
∎

The main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space. Assume that
u^{t}(x),v^{t}(x)
are two semi-group weak and bounded solutions of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
. Then, for any
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
, the function

w(x,t):=d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(x,t),v(x,t)\big)\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Q})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}),

where
\mathcal{Q}:=\Omega\times(t_{*},T)
, and satisfies

(3.5)

\big({\Delta}-\partial_{t}\big)w(x,t)\geqslant 2R_{u^{t},v^{t}}(x)

in the sense of distributions, for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
, where
R_{u,v}(x)
is defined in (
3.1
).

Proof.

From Proposition
2.5
and the assumption that
u
and
v
are bounded, we have that both
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
are in
W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Q},Y)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q},Y)
. This implies
w^{1/2}\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Q})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})
, since

|w^{1/2}(x,t)-w^{1/2}(y,s)|\leqslant d_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{s}(y))+d_{Y}(v^{t}(x),v^{s}(y))

for any
(x,t),(y,s)\in\mathcal{Q}
. Hence, we have
w\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Q})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})
.

Let
t\in(t_{*},T)
and let
\phi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1
. For any
s\in(0,t)
, by using (
2.9
) to
u=u^{t}
and
v^{t}
, (with taking
\bar{u}:=u^{t}_{\phi}
and
\bar{v}:=v^{t}_{\phi}
),
we have

D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t}_{\phi})-D^{2}(u^{t-s},u^{t}_{\phi})+D^{2}(v^{t},v^{t}_{\phi})-D^{2}(v^{t-s},v^{t}_{\phi})\leqslant s\big(E[u^{t}_{\phi}]+E[v^{t}_{\phi}]-E[u^{t}]-E[v^{t}]\big).

Summing with (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}&\ \int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\left(w(x,t)-w(x,t-s)\right){\rm d}\mu(x)\\
\leqslant&\ D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t-s})+D^{2}(v^{t},v^{t-s})+s\Big(E[u^{t}_{\phi}]-E[u^{t}]+E[v^{t}_{\phi}]-E[v^{t}]\Big).\end{split}

By combining this with (
3.3
), we get

\begin{split}&\ \int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\left(w(x,t)-w(x,t-s)\right){\rm d}\mu(x)\\
\leqslant&\ D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t-s})+D^{2}(v^{t},v^{t-s})\\
&-s\int_{\Omega}\left<{\nabla\phi},{\nabla((1-2\phi)w)}\right>{\rm d}\mu-2s\int_{\Omega}\Big(\phi-\phi^{2}\Big)R_{u^{t},v^{t}}{\rm d}\mu.\end{split}

Letting
L
be a Lipschitz constant of
u^{t}
and
v^{t}
in
[t_{*},T]
(see (
2.10
), replacing
\phi
by
\sqrt{s}\cdot\phi
in this inequality, and dividing
s\sqrt{s}
, we obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\frac{w(x,t)-w(x,t-s)}{s}{\rm d}\mu(x)\leqslant&L\sqrt{s}-\int_{\Omega}\left(\left<{\nabla\phi},{\nabla w}\right>+2R_{u^{t},v^{t}}\cdot\phi\right){\rm d}\mu\\
&\ +\sqrt{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}2\left<{\nabla\phi},{\nabla(\phi w)}\right>+2\phi^{2}R_{u^{t},v^{t}}{\rm d}\mu\right).\end{split}

Noticing
w(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\times(t_{*},T))
. This implies

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{w(x,t)-w(x,t-s)}{s}=\partial_{t}w(x,t)\quad{\rm in}\ \ L^{2}(\Omega),

for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
. Therefore, letting
s\to 0^{+}
in (
3.6
), we have

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\partial_{t}w(x,t){\rm d}\mu\leqslant-\int_{\Omega}\left(\left<{\nabla\phi},{\nabla w}\right>+2\phi R_{u^{t},v^{t}}\right){\rm d}\mu,

for any
\phi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leqslant\phi\leqslant 1
, for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
. That is,

({\Delta}-\partial_{t})w\geqslant 2R_{u^{t},v^{t}}

in the sense of distributions on
\Omega
, for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
. ∎

The following corollary will be used later.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, and let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space, and let
P\in Y
. Assume that
u^{t}(x)
is a semi-group weak and bounded solution of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
. Let
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
. Then

(3.7)

\big({\Delta}-\partial_{t}\big)d^{2}_{Y}(P,u^{t}(x))\geqslant 2e_{u^{t}}(x)

in the sense of distributions, for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
. And

(3.8)

\big({\Delta}-\partial_{t}\big)d_{Y}(P,u^{t}(x))\geqslant 0

in the sense of distributions, for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
.

Proof.

Taking
v^{t}(x)\equiv P
in Theorem
3.4
, the desired assertion (
3.7
) follows, since
R^{u^{t},P}_{\epsilon}=e^{u^{t}}_{\epsilon}
and
R_{u^{t},P}=e_{u^{t}}.

We put

f_{P}(x,t):=d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),P\big).

By (
3.7
) and noticing that
|\nabla f_{P}|^{2}(x)\leqslant e_{u^{t}}(x)
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, we have

(\Delta-\partial_{t})f^{2}_{P}\geqslant 2e_{e_{u^{t}}}\geqslant 2|\nabla f_{P}|^{2}

in
\Omega
in the sense of distributions. By the chain rule and
f_{P}\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we conclude that, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, the function
g_{\delta}:=(f_{P}^{2}+\delta)^{1/2}
satisfies

2g_{\delta}(\Delta-\partial_{t})g_{\delta}\geqslant 2|\nabla f_{P}|^{2}-|\nabla g_{\delta}|^{2}=2|\nabla f_{P}|^{2}\Big(1-\frac{f^{2}_{P}}{g^{2}_{\delta}}\Big)\geqslant 0

in
\Omega
in the sense of distributions. Since
g_{\delta}\geqslant\delta>0
, it follows that
g_{\delta}
is a supersolution of the heat equation in the sense of distributions. Letting
\delta\to 0^{+},
we have
f_{P}
is a supersolution of the heat equation in the sense of distributions. This proves (
3.8
).
∎

4.
Local Hölder Continuity

Let
M
be
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space.
We first consider the following general result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
U_{T}:=U\times(0,T)
be a cylinder, where
U\subset M
is bounded open domain, and
T>0
.
Suppose that a map
u(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(U_{T},Y)\cap L^{\infty}(U_{T},Y)
satisfies:

(1)

there exists some
a_{1}>0
such that for any
P\in Y
, it holds

(4.1)

(\Delta-\partial_{t})d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x,t)\big)\geqslant a_{1}\cdot e_{u^{t}}(x)

in the sense of distributions, and

(2)

there exists a constant
L>0
such that

(4.2)

D\big(u^{t},u^{t+s}\big)\leqslant L\cdot s,\quad\forall t,t+s\in(0,T).

Then
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
U_{T}
(i.e., there exists a locally Hölder continuous function
\tilde{u}
such that
\tilde{u}(x,t)=u(x,t)
for almost all
(x,t)\in U_{T}.)
.

The argument is an extension of Jost’s proof
[
34
]
from harmonic maps to the parabolic setting. For any
r>0
and
P\in Y
, we denote by

v_{P,+,r}(x,t):=\sup_{Q_{r}(x,t)}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,s)\big),\quad v_{P,-,r}(x,t):=\inf_{Q_{r}(x,t)}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,s)\big),

where
Q_{r}(x,t):=B_{r}(x)\times(t-r^{2},t+r^{2})
, and also

v_{P,r}(x,t):=\fint_{Q_{r}(x,t)}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,s)\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}s.

Before giving the proof of Theorem 4.1, we need several lemmas. The first one is a parabolic version of
[
34
, Corollary 1]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
be as in the above Theorem 4.1. Assume that
u(U_{T})\subset B_{R_{0}}(P_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
and
P_{0}\in Y
. Then there exist constants
\delta_{0}\in(0,1/2)
and
C_{1}>0
(depending only on the lower bound of Ricci curvature on
U
,
{\rm diam}(U)
,
\mu(U)
,
R_{0}
and
L
), such that for any cylinder
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
and for any
P\in B_{2R_{0}}(P_{0})
,
we have

(4.3)

v_{P,+,r}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\left(1-\delta_{0}\right)v_{P,+,4r}(x_{0},t_{0})+\delta_{0}\cdot v_{P,r}(x_{0},t_{0})+C_{1}r^{2}.

Let
m=m(\delta_{0})\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(1-\delta_{0})^{m}\leqslant 4^{-2}
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)
and any
r>0
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
, there exists some
r^{\prime}\in[\epsilon^{m}r,r/4]
such that

(4.4)

v_{P,+,\epsilon^{m}r}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\epsilon\cdot v_{P,+,r}(x_{0},t_{0})+v_{P,r^{\prime}}(x_{0},t_{0})+\frac{C_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\cdot r^{2},

where the constant
C_{1}
is in (
4.3
). Here this
r^{\prime}
may depend on
P,\epsilon
and
r
.

Proof.

Fix any
P\in B_{2R_{0}}(P_{0})
and cylinder
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
. The function

g(y,t):=v_{P,+,4r}(x_{0},t_{0})-d_{Y}^{2}\big(P,u(y,t)\big)

is nonnegative in
Q_{4r}(x_{0},t_{0})
and
(\Delta-\partial_{t})g\leqslant 0
in
Q_{4r}(x_{0},t_{0})
in the sense of distributions. By the weak Harnack inequality, we obtain

(4.5)

\fint_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}-3r^{2})}g(y,t){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\leqslant c_{1}\cdot\inf_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}g=c_{1}\cdot\Big(v_{P,+,4r}(x_{0},t_{0})-v_{P,+,r}(x_{0},t_{0})\Big),

for some positive constant
c_{1}
(depending only on the lower bound of Ricci curvature on
U
,
{\rm diam}(U)
and
\mu(U)
). We always take

.

To simplify the notations, we denote
v_{+,r}:=v_{P,+,r}(x_{0},t_{0})
and
v_{r}:=v_{P,r}(x_{0},t_{0})
in the sequel of this proof.
By
u(U_{T})\subset B_{3R_{0}}(P)
and the triangle inequality, we have

|d^{2}_{Y}(P,u^{t}(y))-d^{2}_{Y}(P,u^{t-3r^{2}}(y))|\leqslant 6R_{0}\cdot d_{Y}(u^{t}(y),u^{t-3r^{2}}(y)).

Hence, we have

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u^{t}(y)\big){\rm d}\mu(y)&\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u^{t-3r^{2}}(y)\big){\rm d}\mu(y)+6R_{0}\cdot\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d_{Y}(u^{t}(y),u^{t-3r^{2}}(y)){\rm d}\mu(y)\\
&\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u^{t-3r^{2}}(y)\big){\rm d}\mu(y)+6R_{0}\cdot D(u^{t},u^{t-3r^{2}})\\
\end{split}

From the assumption
D(u^{t},u^{t+s})\leqslant Ls
, the left-hand side of (
4.5
) satisfies

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2r^{2}|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t-3r^{2})}g(y,t){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\\
=\ &v_{+,4r}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\int_{t_{0}-4r^{2}}^{t_{0}-2r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,t)\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\\
\geqslant\ &v_{+,4r}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\int_{t_{0}-4r^{2}}^{t_{0}-2r^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}d_{Y}\big(P,u(y,t-3r^{2})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)+18R_{0}\cdot Lr^{2}\right){\rm d}t\\
=\ &v_{+,4r}-v_{r}-18R_{0}\cdot Lr^{2}.\end{split}

The combination with (
4.5
) implies

v_{+,r}\leqslant\left(1-\frac{1}{c_{1}}\right)v_{+,4r}+\frac{1}{c_{1}}v_{r}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}r^{2},

where
c_{2}:=18R_{0}\cdot L
. This is the desired estimate (4.3) with
\delta_{0}=1/c_{1}\in(0,1/2)
and
C_{1}:=c_{2}/c_{1}.

To show (
4.4
), by iterating (
4.3
), we get for any
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
that

\begin{split}v_{+,4^{-\nu}r}&\leqslant(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu}\cdot v_{+,r}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu-j}\left(\delta_{0}v_{4^{-j}r}+C_{1}(4^{-j}r)^{2}\right)\\
&\leqslant(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu}\cdot v_{+,r}+\delta_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu-j}v_{4^{-j}r}+C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu-j}(4^{-j}r)^{2}\\
&\leqslant(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu}\cdot v_{+,r}+\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant\nu}\{v_{4^{-j}r}\}+\frac{C_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\cdot r^{2},\end{split}

where we have used
\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu-j}\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}
and

(4^{-j})^{2}\leqslant 1.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)
, we choose
\nu
so that

(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu}<\epsilon\leqslant(1-\delta_{0})^{\nu-1}

and take
r^{\prime}:=4^{-j_{0}}r
such that
v_{4^{-j_{0}}r}=\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant\nu}\{v_{4^{-j}r}\}.

Since

(1-\delta_{0})^{m}\leqslant 4^{-2}
, we have
\epsilon^{m}\leqslant(1-\delta_{0})^{(\nu-1)m}\leqslant 4^{-2(\nu-1)}\leqslant 4^{-\nu},
by
\nu\geqslant 2
(because
\delta_{0}<1/2
and
\epsilon<1/4.
).
Therefore, we have
\epsilon^{m}r\leqslant 4^{-\nu}r\leqslant 4^{-j_{0}}r=r^{\prime}\leqslant r/4.
The proof is finished.
∎

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},Y)
, where

on

, written by

In particular, if

The following lemma is a Poincaré-type inequality in the parabolic setting.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u
be as in the above Theorem 4.1. Assume that
u(U_{T})\subset B_{R_{0}}(P_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
and
P_{0}\in Y
. Then there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
(depending only on the lower bound of Ricci curvature on
U
,
{\rm diam}(U)
and
\mu(U)
), such that for any cylinder
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
,
we have

(4.7)

\fint_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}d_{Y}^{2}\big(u(y,t),\overline{u}_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\leqslant C_{3}r^{2}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})}e_{u^{t}}(y){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t+2R_{0}L\right).

(Remark that the right-hand side involves only spatial derivatives.)

Proof.

Fix any cylinder
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
. To simplify the notations, we denote by
Q_{r}:=Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
and
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
.

For any
t\in(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}+r^{2})
, from
[
36
, Proposition 2.5.2]
, we have

d_{Y}^{2}(\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}},\overline{u^{t_{0}}}_{B_{r}})\leqslant\int_{B_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),u^{t_{0}}(y)\big){\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t_{0}})\leqslant L^{2}(t-t_{0})^{2}\leqslant L^{2}r^{4}.

Therefore, by triangle inequality and
u(U_{T})\subset B_{R_{0}}(P_{0})
, we get

\begin{split}\big|d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}}\big)-d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t_{0}}_{B_{r}}}\big)\big|&\leqslant d_{Y}(\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}},\overline{u^{t_{0}}}_{B_{r}})\cdot\big|d_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}}\big)+d_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}}(y),\overline{u^{t_{0}}_{B_{r}}}\big)\big|\\
&\leqslant Lr^{2}\cdot(2R_{0}).\end{split}

Integrating over
B_{r}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{B_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t_{0}}}_{B_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y)-\int_{B_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y)\Big|\\
\leqslant\ &2R_{0}L|B_{r}|\cdot r^{2}.\end{split}

This implies

\begin{split}\int_{Q_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t),\overline{u}_{Q_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t&\leqslant\int_{Q_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t),\overline{u^{t_{0}}}_{B_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t=\int_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}+r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t_{0}}}_{B_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant\int_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}+r^{2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t}(y),\overline{u^{t}}_{B_{r}}\big){\rm d}\mu(y)+2R_{0}L|B_{r}|\cdot r^{2}\right){\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant\int_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}+r^{2}}\left(c_{U}\cdot r^{2}\int_{B_{2r}}e_{u^{t}}(y){\rm d}\mu(y)+2R_{0}L|B_{r}|\cdot r^{2}\right){\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant c_{U}r^{2}\int_{Q_{2r}}e_{u^{t}}(y){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t+2R_{0}L|Q_{r}|\cdot r^{2},\end{split}

where we have used Poincaré inequality (
2.5
) for the third inequality. This implies the desired assertion, by
|Q_{2r}|\leqslant c_{U}|Q_{r}|
for some
c_{U}>0
.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Take any cylinder
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
Q_{8r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset U_{T}
. In the proof, we denote
Q_{r}:=Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

Let
m
be given in Lemma
4.2
and let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{64}
. For each
r^{\prime}\in(\epsilon^{m}r,r/4]
, we have

v_{P_{1},r^{\prime}}(x_{0},t_{0})=\fint_{Q_{r^{\prime}}}d^{2}_{Y}(u(y,t),P_{1}){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}t\leqslant c_{1}(m)\cdot\fint_{Q_{r/4}}d^{2}_{Y}(u,P_{1}){\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t,

where
P_{1}=\overline{u}_{Q_{r/4}}
. The above Poincaré type inequality (

) states

\fint_{Q_{r/4}}d^{2}_{Y}(u,P_{1}){\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t\leqslant c_{2}r^{2}\fint_{Q_{r/2}}e_{u^{t}}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t+c_{2}r^{2}.

Combining these two inequality and (

), we obtain

(4.8)

v_{P_{1},r^{\prime}}(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\frac{c_{3}}{a_{1}}\left(v_{P,+,4r}(x_{0},t_{0})-v_{P,+,r}(x_{0},t_{0})+C_{1}r^{2}\right)+c_{3}r^{2},

for any
P\in B_{2R_{0}}(P_{0})
.

Fix any
\rho\in(0,\epsilon^{m}r)
and take
P=\overline{u}_{Q_{\rho}}

d_{Y}(P,P_{1})\leqslant\sup_{(y,t)\in Q_{\epsilon^{m}r}}d_{Y}(u(y,t),P_{1})
(since

\begin{split}v_{P,+,\epsilon^{m}r}&=\sup_{Q_{\epsilon^{m}r}}d^{2}_{Y}(u(y,t),P)\leqslant 2\sup_{Q_{\epsilon^{m}r}}d^{2}_{Y}(u(y,t),P_{1})+2d^{2}_{Y}(P,P_{1})\\

) and (

\begin{split}v_{P,+,\epsilon^{m}r}\leqslant 4v_{P_{1},+,\epsilon^{m}r}&\leqslant 4\epsilon v_{P_{1},+,r}+4v_{P_{1},r^{\prime}}+4C_{1}r^{2}/\delta_{0}\\

5.
Lipschitz continuity in time

We continue to denote by
u(x,t)
a semi-group solution of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
with bounded initial data.
From the previous Theorem
4.5
, we always assume
u(x,t)\in C(\Omega\times(0,+\infty),Y)
.

We can now show that

is locally Lipschitz continuous in
(0,+\infty)
, for each fixed

.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space, and let
u(x,t)
be a semi-group weak bounded solution of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
. Assume
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
and a ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
with
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
,
R^{2}<\min\{\frac{t_{*}}{2},\frac{T-t_{*}}{2}\}
,

. Then there exists a constant
c:=c_{n,K,R}>0
such that

(5.1)

d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(x,t+s)\big)\leqslant cL\cdot s,\quad\forall t,t+s\in(t_{*},T),\quad\ \forall x\in B_{R}(\bar{x}),

where
L
is given in (
2.10
), and
K
is the lower bound of Ricci curvature of
M
.

Proof.

Fix any
s>0
. The semi-group property ensures that
v(x,t):=u(x,t+s)
is also a weak solution of the harmonic map heat flow. Using Theorem
3.4
to
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
, we conclude that the function

w(x,t):=d_{Y}^{2}(u^{t+s}(x),u^{t}(x))
satisfies

({\Delta}-\partial_{t})w(x,t)\geqslant 0

in the sense of distributions on
\Omega\times(t_{*},T-s)
. The maximum principle implies, for each cylinder
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{j}-R^{2},t_{j}+R^{2})
with
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
and
t_{j}=t_{*}+jR^{2}
,
j=1,2,\cdots,\ell:=[(T-s-t_{*})/R^{2}]
, that

\begin{split}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{j}-R^{2},t_{j}+R^{2}))}&\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\int_{B_{2R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{j}-2R^{2},t_{j}+2R^{2})}w{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\int_{t_{j}-2R^{2}}^{t_{j}+2R^{2}}\int_{\Omega}d^{2}_{Y}(u^{t}(x),u^{t+s}(x)){\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t\\
&=C_{n,K,R}\int_{t_{j}-2R^{2}}^{t_{j}+2R^{2}}D^{2}(u^{t},u^{t+s}){\rm d}t\leqslant 4R^{2}C_{n,K,R}\cdot(Ls)^{2}.\end{split}

By combining with

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T-s))}\leqslant\sup_{j=1,2,\cdots,\ell}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{j}-R^{2},t_{j}+R^{2}))},

we obtain

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{0},T-s))}\leqslant 4R^{2}C_{n,K,R}L^{2}\cdot s^{2}.

Noticing that
u(x,t)
is continuous in
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
, the desired assertion (
5.1
) follows, with
c:=2RC^{1/2}_{n,K,R}
. ∎

As an easy consequence, we have the following.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\Omega,Y,t_{*},T,B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
u(x,t)
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Then for any
t_{0}\in(t_{*},T)
, it holds

(5.2)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)=2e_{u^{t_{0}}}(x_{0})

for almost all
x_{0}\in B_{R}(\bar{x}).

Proof.

Fix any
t_{0}\in(t_{*},T)
. Since
u^{t_{0}}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,Y)
, by Proposition
2.1
(2), we have that for almost all
x_{0}\in\Omega
,

(5.3)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}}(y),u^{t_{0}}(x_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)=2e_{u^{t_{0}}}(x_{0}).

Fix such an
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
For any
s\in(0,t_{0}-t_{*})
,
by using the elementary inequality

|a^{2}-b^{2}|=|(a-b)^{2}+2ab-2b^{2}|\leqslant(a-b)^{2}+2|b|\cdot|a-b|

to
a:=d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)
and

b:=d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)
and the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\Big|d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)-d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\Big|\\
\leqslant\ &\Big[d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(y,t_{0})\big)\Big]^{2}+2d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\cdot d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(y,t_{0})\big).\end{split}

Since the weak solution
u
is bounded, we may assume
u(\Omega\times(t_{*},T))\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for some
P_{0}\in Y
and
M_{0}>0
. By Theorem
5.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\Big|d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)-d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\Big|\\
\leqslant\ &\begin{cases}(cLs)^{2}+2cLs\cdot d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big),&{\rm if}\ \ y\in B_{R}(\bar{x}),\\
12M^{2}_{0},&{\rm if}\ \ y\in\Omega\setminus B_{R}(\bar{x}).\end{cases}\end{split}

Integrating over
\Omega
with respect to
p_{s}(x_{0},\cdot){\rm d}\mu
and dividing by
s
, we have

\begin{split}I(s):=\ &\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)\Big|d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}-s),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)-d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\Big|{\rm d}\mu(y)\\
\leqslant\ &(cL)^{2}s+cL\int_{B_{R}(\bar{x})}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)\\
&\ \ +\frac{12M^{2}_{0}}{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}(\bar{x})}p_{s}(x_{0},y){\rm d}\mu(y).\end{split}

Because
x_{0}\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
u(\cdot,t_{0})
is continuous at
x_{0}
, we have

(5.4)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{R}(\bar{x})}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d_{Y}\big(u(y,t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)=0.

The below Lemma
5.3
(by taking
\ell=0
therein) implies

(5.5)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}(\bar{x})}p_{s}(x_{0},y){\rm d}\mu(y)=0.

Substituting (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) into
I(s)
, we conclude that
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}I(s)=0.

Therefore,

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}-s}(y),u^{t_{0}}(x_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)d^{2}_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}}(y),u^{t_{0}}(x_{0})\big){\rm d}\mu(y).

Combining this and (
5.3
), the desired estimate follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
U
be an open set and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then for any
x_{0}\in U
, one has

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{M\setminus U}d^{\ell}(x_{0},y)p_{s}(x_{0},y){\rm d}\mu(y)=0.

Proof.

It is well-known for experts (see, for example,
[
46
, Lemma 2.53]
for a similar statement). We give a proof here for completeness.
Recall the upper bound of heat kernels (see, for example,
[
50
, Theorem 4.6 in Chapter IV]
), we have for any
y\in M\setminus U
and any
s\leqslant 1
that

p_{s}(x_{0},y)\leqslant\frac{c_{n}}{|B_{\sqrt{s}}(x_{0})|}e^{-\frac{d^{2}(x_{0},y)}{5s}+C_{n}Ks}\leqslant c_{n,K}\cdot\frac{e^{-\frac{d^{2}(x_{0},y)}{5s}}}{|B_{\sqrt{s}}(x_{0})|}.

Multiplying by
d^{\ell}(x_{0},y)
, integrating over
M\setminus U
and dividing by
s
, we get

\frac{1}{s}\int_{M\setminus U}d^{\ell}(x_{0},y)p_{s}(x_{0},y){\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant\frac{c_{n,K}}{s|B_{\sqrt{s}}(x_{0})|}\int_{M\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})}d^{\ell}(x_{0},y)e^{-\frac{d^{2}(x_{0},y)}{5s}}{\rm d}\mu(y),

where
\epsilon=d(x_{0},\partial U).

By using Bishop-Gromov inequality
|B_{\sqrt{s}}(x_{0})|/|B_{1}(x_{0})|\geqslant c_{n,K}\cdot s^{n/2}
for all
s<1
, and the growth of area of geodesic spheres (without loss the generality, we consider only the case
K<0
)

|\partial B_{r}(x_{0})|\leqslant|\partial B_{r}(0)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{K/(n-1)}|=c_{n,K}\sinh^{n-1}\big(\sqrt{(n-1)K}r\big)\leqslant c_{n,K}\cdot e^{\sqrt{(n-1)K}r},

we get, for all
s<1
, that

\frac{1}{s|B_{\sqrt{s}}(x_{0})|}\int_{M\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})}d^{\ell}(x_{0},y)e^{-\frac{d^{2}(x_{0},y)}{5s}}{\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant\frac{c_{n,K}}{s^{1+n/2}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}r^{l}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{5s}}\cdot e^{\sqrt{(n-1)K}r}{\rm d}r.

By a direct computation, we have

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s^{1+n/2}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}r^{l}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{5s}}\cdot e^{\sqrt{(n-1)K}r}{\rm d}r=0.

The proof is finished. ∎

Given a map
v\in C(\Omega,Y)
and
r>0
, we define a function
x\mapsto{\rm lip}_{r}v(x)
on
\Omega
by

(5.6)

{\rm lip}_{r}v(x):=\sup_{s\in(0,r)}\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{d_{Y}\big(v(x),v(y)\big)}{s}\in[0,+\infty].

It is clear that
{\rm lip}v(x)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}{\rm lip}_{r}v(x)
by their definitions.

The next result will play an important role in Section 7 for the proof of Lipschitz regularity in space variables.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Omega,Y,t_{*},T,B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
u(x,t)
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Then there exists a constant
C:=C_{n,K,R}>0
such that for almost all
t\in(t_{*},T)
, it holds

(5.7)

\int_{B_{R}(\bar{x})}[{\rm lip}_{r}u^{t}(x)]^{2}{\rm d}\mu(x)\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}e_{u^{t}}(x){\rm d}\mu(x)+cL\cdot R|B_{R}(\bar{x})|,\qquad\forall r\in(0,R/4),

where the constant
cL
is given in Theorem
5.1
.

In particular, for almost all
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
we have
{\rm lip}_{r}u^{t}(x)<+\infty
, for any
r\in(0,R/4)
.

Proof.

We first recall a fact that for any
h\in W^{1,2}(B_{2R}(\bar{x}))
, it holds

(5.8)

\fint_{B_{s}(x)}|h(x)-h(y)|{\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant\Big(M(|\nabla h|)(x)+M(M(|\nabla h|))(x)\Big)\cdot s

for any ball
B_{s}(x)
with
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
s<R/2
, where
Mw
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function for a function
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
:

Mw(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}|w|{\rm d}\mu.

Indeed, according to
[
24
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a constant
c=c_{n,K,R}\in(0,1)
such that
for almost all
x,y\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
with
d(x,y)<cR
, we have

|h(x)-h(y)|\leqslant d(x,y)\cdot\Big(M(|\nabla h|(x)+M(|\nabla h|)(y)\Big).

Integrating over
B_{s}(x)
, we have

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{s}(x)}|h(x)-h(y)|{\rm d}\mu(y)&\leqslant s\fint_{B_{s}(x)}\Big(M(|\nabla h|(x)+M(|\nabla h|)(y)\Big){\rm d}\mu(y)\\
&\leqslant s\Big(M(|\nabla h|)(x)+M[M(|\nabla h|)](x)\Big),\end{split}

which is (
5.8
).

Fix any
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
t_{0}\in(t_{*},T)
, we denote

f(y,t):=d_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}}(x),u^{t}(y)\big).

By using Theorem
5.1
and the triangle inequality, we have that

|f(y,t+s)-f(y,t)|\leqslant d_{Y}(u^{t+s}(y),u^{t}(y))\leqslant cL|s|

for all
t,t+s\in(t_{*},T)
. This implies

|\partial_{t}f(y,t)|\leqslant\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{|f(y,t+s)-f(y,t)|}{|s|}\leqslant cL

for any
t\in(t_{*},T)
.
By combining with the fact that

\Delta f\geqslant\partial_{t}f

on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
in the sense of distributions and noting the continuity of the function
f
, we have

\Delta f(\cdot,t_{0})\geqslant-cL

in the sense of distributions on
\Omega
. By the local boundedness of subsolution of
\Delta f\geqslant-cL
(see, for example,
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
), we have

(5.9)

\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}f(y,t_{0})\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\cdot\Big(\fint_{B_{2s}(x)}f(y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu(y)+cL\cdot s^{2}\Big)

for any ball
B_{s}(x)
with
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
s<R/2
.

By using (
5.8
) to
f(y,t_{0})
and noticing
f(x,t_{0})=0
and the fact that
|\nabla f(y,t_{0})|\leqslant\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}}(y)
for almost all
y\in\Omega
, we have

\fint_{B_{s}(x)}f(y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant(M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}})(x)+M[M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}})](x))\cdot s

for all ball
B_{s}(x)
with
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
s<R/2.
Combining with (
5.9
), we get

\sup_{y\in B_{s}(x)}\frac{d_{Y}\big(u^{t_{0}}(x),u^{t_{0}}(y)\big)}{s}\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\Big(M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}})}(x)+M[M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}})](x)+cLs\Big),

for all
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
s<R/4.
This implies

(5.10)

{\rm lip}_{r}u^{t_{0}}(x)\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\Big(M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}})(x)+M[M(\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}})](x)\Big)+cL\cdot R/4,

for all
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
and all
r<R/4
.

Finally, since
\sqrt{e_{u^{t_{0}}}}\in L^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x}))
, the assertion (
5.7
) comes from the combination of (
5.10
) and the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood operator.
∎

6.
Asymptotic mean value inequality for heat equations

In this section, we give an asymptotic mean value inequality for heat equations. We first consider a global version as follows (c.f.
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
, see also Lemma 4.2 in
[
19
]
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geqslant K
for some
K\leqslant 0
.
Let
g(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(M\times(0,T))\cap C(M\times(0,T))
for some
T>0
. Suppose that

({\Delta}-\partial_{t})g(x,t)\leqslant f(x,t)

for some
f\in L^{1}(M\times(0,T))
in the sense of distributions, then for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(0,T)
, we have

(6.1)

H_{s}[g(\cdot,t_{0}-s)](x_{0})-g(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0}){\rm d}\tau,\qquad\forall s\in(0,t_{0}),

where
H[f]
is the heat flow given by

H[f(\cdot)](x):=\int_{M}p_{s}(x,y)f(y){\rm d}\mu(y).

Proof.

Fix any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(0,T)
.
By

H_{\tau}[g(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0})=\int_{M}p_{\tau}(x_{0},x)g(x,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(x)
,
we have

\begin{split}\partial_{\tau}H_{\tau}[g(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0})=&\int_{M}\Big(\Delta p_{\tau}(x_{0},x)\cdot g(x,t_{0}-\tau)-p_{\tau}(x_{0},x)\cdot\partial_{t}g(x,t_{0}-\tau)\Big){\rm d}\mu(x)\\
\leqslant&\int_{M}p_{\tau}(x_{0},x)\cdot f(x,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(x)=H_{\tau}[f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0}).\end{split}

For any
s\in(0,t_{0})
, integrating over
(0,s)
and using
\lim_{\tau\to 0}H_{\tau}[g(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0})=g(x_{0},t_{0})
(since
g
is continuous), we obtain

H_{s}[g(\cdot,t_{0}-s)](x_{0})-g(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0}){\rm d}\tau.

The proof is finished.
∎

For our purpose, we need a local version as follows.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain.
Let
\mathcal{Q}=\Omega\times(0,T)
be a bounded cylinder and
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q})
. Suppose that
g\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Q})\cap C(\mathcal{Q})
and
g(x,t)\leqslant M_{0}
for some constant
M_{0}>0
. If

({\Delta}-\partial_{t})g(x,t)\leqslant f(x,t)\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \mathcal{Q}

in the sense of distributions, then for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{Q}

(6.2)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)g(y,t_{0}-s){\rm d}\mu(y)-g(x_{0},t_{0})}{s}\leqslant\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)f(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}\tau.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and denote
R:=d(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)/4
. For any
x_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}
, we take a smooth cut-off function
\phi:M\to[0,1]
such that
\phi|_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\equiv 1
,
{\rm supp}\phi\subset B_{2R}(x_{0})
. We have
g(x,t)\phi(x)\in W^{1,2}(M\times(0,T))\cap C(M\times(0,T))
, where it is understood that
g\phi=0
outside of
\mathcal{Q}
.
We have

{\Delta}(g\phi)-\partial_{t}(g\phi)\leqslant\tilde{f}:=f\phi+2\left<{\nabla g},{\nabla\phi}\right>+g\Delta\phi\in L^{1}(M\times(0,T))

in the sense of distributions. By apply the above Lemma
6.1
to
g\phi
, we get for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{Q}
and any
s\in(0,t_{0})
that

(6.3)

\begin{split}&H_{s}[(g\phi)(\cdot,t_{0}-s)](x_{0})-(g\phi)(x_{0},t_{0})\leqslant\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[\tilde{f}(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)](x_{0}){\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant\ &\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)f(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}\tau+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)|f(1-\phi)|(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}\tau\\
&\ \ +\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)\Big|2\left<{\nabla g},{\nabla\phi}\right>+g\Delta\phi\Big|(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}\tau.\end{split}

Since
(1-\phi)=0
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, and by using the upper bound of the heat kernel (see, for example,
[
50
, Theorem 4.6 in Chapter IV]
, we have for any
\tau<1
that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)|f(1-\phi)|(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y)&=\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)|f|(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y)\\
&\leqslant\frac{C_{n,K}}{|B_{\sqrt{\tau}}(x_{0})|}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{5\tau}}\|f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\\
&\leqslant\frac{C_{n,K,R}}{|B_{1}(x_{0})|}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10\tau}}\|f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\end{split}

for all
\tau
sufficiently small,
where
K
is a lower bound of Ricci curvature on
M
, and, in the last inequality, we have used that
|B_{\sqrt{\tau}}(x_{0})|/|B_{1}(x_{0})|\geqslant c_{n,K}\tau^{n/2}
for all
\tau<1
and that
\tau^{n/2}\geqslant c_{n,R}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10\tau}}
for all sufficiently small
\tau
.
Therefore, we obtain

(6.4)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)|f(1-\phi)|(y,t_{0}-\tau){\rm d}\mu(y){\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}C_{1}s^{-1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10\tau}}\|f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}C_{1}s^{-1}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10s}}\int_{0}^{s}\|f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}C_{1}s^{-1}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10s}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}=0,\end{split}

where we have used
e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10\tau}}\leqslant e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{10s}}
for all
\tau\leqslant s
. Similarly, since
|\nabla\phi|=|\Delta\phi|=0
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, we have

(6.5)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{\tau}(x_{0},y)\Big|2\left<{\nabla g},{\nabla\phi}\right>+g\Delta\phi\Big|(y,t_{0}-\tau)\mu(y){\rm d}\tau=0.

Noticing that
(1-\phi)=0
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
again and
g\leqslant M_{0}
, we have

(6.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)g(y,t_{0}-s)d\mu(y)-H_{s}[(g\phi)(\cdot,t_{0}-s)](x_{0})\\
=\ &\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)g(y,t_{0}-s)(1-\phi)(y,t_{0}-s)d\mu(y)\\
\leqslant\ &\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}p_{s}(x_{0},y)g^{+}(y,t_{0}-s)d\mu(y)\\
\leqslant\ &M_{0}s^{-1}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}p_{s}(x_{0},y){\rm d}\mu(y)\to 0,\qquad{\rm as}\ s\to 0^{+}.\end{split}

Finally, by combining the above four inequalities (
6.3
)–(
6.6
), we conclude (
6.2
).
∎

The following proposition is an analog of Lebesgue’s differential theorem.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}=\Omega\times(0,T)
be a bounded cylinder and
f(x,t)\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q})
. Then there exists
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{Q}
with
(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(\mathcal{N})=0
such that

(6.7)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[f(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau=f(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}\setminus\mathcal{N}.

(Here
f
and
g
are understood as their zero extensions on
M\times(0,T)
.)

Remark 6.4
.

In the case when
f(x,t)=f(x)
, it is well-known that
H_{s}f(x)\to f(x)
, as
s\to 0^{+}
, at any Lebesgue point of
f
.

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

Note that, for any
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q})
and any
(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[f(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau-f(x,t)\right|\\
\leqslant\ &\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau\right|+\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[\tilde{f}(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau-f(x,t)\right|.\end{split}

If
\tilde{f}
is continuous, the last term converges to
|\tilde{f}(x,t)-f(x,t)|
as
s\to 0^{+}
. Letting
L(x,t)
denote the upper limit of the term on the left-hand side, we obtain

(6.8)

L(x,t)\leqslant\limsup_{s\to 0}\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau\right|+|\tilde{f}-f|(x,t).

It suffices to prove that for each
\varepsilon>0
and
\alpha>0
, we have

(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(E_{\varepsilon,\alpha})=0,\quad{\rm where}\quad E_{\varepsilon,\alpha}:=\left\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T)\big|\ L(x,t)>\alpha\right\}.

The equation (
6.8
) implies

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon,\alpha}\subset&\left\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T)\big|\ \limsup_{s\to 0}\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau\right|>\alpha/2\right\}\\
&\ \bigcup\left\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T)\big|\ |f-\tilde{f}|>\alpha/2\right\},\end{split}

and hence, by Markov inequality, we get

\begin{split}(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(E_{\varepsilon,\alpha})\leqslant&\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\limsup_{s\to 0}\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau\right|{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\\
&+\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|f-\tilde{f}|(x,t){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t.\\
\end{split}

By Fatou’s lemma, the first term on the right-hand side can be bounded by

\begin{split}&\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\limsup_{s\to 0}\left|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x){\rm d}\tau\right|{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}\int_{M}\left|H_{\tau}[(f-\tilde{f})(\cdot,t-\tau)](x)\right|{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\right){\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}\int_{M}\left|f-\tilde{f}\right|(x,t-\tau){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\right){\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}\left|f-\tilde{f}\right|(x,t){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\right){\rm d}\tau\\
\leqslant&\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{1}(M\times(0,T))},\end{split}

where we have used the
L^{1}(M)
-contraction of the semigroup
H_{\tau}
in the second inequality. Therefore, we obtain

(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(E_{\varepsilon,\alpha})\leqslant\frac{2}{\alpha}\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{1}(M\times(0,T))}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Since
\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{1}(M\times(0,T))}
can be made arbitrarily small whth appropriate choice of
\tilde{f}
, it follows that
(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(E_{\varepsilon,\alpha})=0
for each
\varepsilon>0
and each
\alpha>0.

∎

Finally, we apply it to the harmonic map heat flow.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\Omega,Y,t_{*},T,B_{R}(\bar{x})
and
u(x,t)
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Then for each
T\in(0,\infty)
there exists
\mathcal{N}_{T}\subset B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
with

(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})(\mathcal{N}_{T})=0
such that for any
P\in Y
the following asymptotic mean value inequality holds

(6.9)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},y)w_{P,s}(x_{0},y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu(y)\leqslant 0,

for all
(x_{0},t_{0})\in(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T))\setminus\mathcal{N}_{T},
where

(6.10)

w_{P,s}(x,y,t):=-d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,t-s)\big)+d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x,t)\big)+d^{2}_{Y}\big(u(y,t-s),u(x,t)\big).

(The set
\mathcal{N}_{T}
is independent of
P
.)

Proof.

By applying Proposition
6.3
to
f(x,t):=-2e_{u^{t}}(x)\in L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
there exists
\mathcal{N}_{1}\subset\Omega\times(0,T)
with zere
(\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})
-measure such that

(6.11)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}H_{\tau}[-2e_{u^{t_{0}-\tau}}(\cdot)](x_{0}){\rm d}\tau=-2e_{u^{t_{0}}}(x_{0}),\quad\forall(x_{0},t_{0})\in(\Omega\times(0,T))\setminus\mathcal{N}_{1}.

For any
P\in Y
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in(\Omega\times(0,T))\setminus\mathcal{N}_{1}
, we have

(\Delta-\partial_{t})\Big(-d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(y,t)\big)+d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\Big)\leqslant-2e_{u^{t}}(y)

in the sense of distributions. Therefore, by using Lemma
6.2
and combining with (
6.11
), we get

(6.12)

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{s}H_{s}\left[-d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(\cdot,t_{0}-s)\big)+d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)\right](x_{0})\leqslant-2e_{u^{t_{0}}}(x_{0}).

Here
-d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(\cdot,t_{0}-s)\big)+d^{2}_{Y}\big(P,u(x_{0},t_{0})\big)
is understood as a function on
M\times(0,T)
with the zero extension.

Finally, by combining Corollary
5.2
and (
6.12
), the desired assertion (
6.9
) holds, and then the proof is finished.
∎

7.
Lipschitz continuity in space-time

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain in an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric_{M}\geqslant K
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
Y
be a
CAT(0)
space. The main result of this section is Theorem
7.8
, the locally Lipschitz regularity in spatial variables for the harmonic map heat flow.

7.1.
Nonlinear Hamilton-Jacobi flows

Since
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,Y)
, we assume that its image is contained in a ball
\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for some
M_{0}>0
and
P_{0}\in Y
. According to Lemma
3.1
, we know that
u^{t}(\Omega)\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for all
t>0
.

Let
T>0
and let
B_{R}(\bar{x})
be a ball with
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
.
We first introduce a family of functions on
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
as follows: for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(7.1)

f_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\inf_{y\in\Omega}\left\{\frac{e^{-2Kt}\cdot d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-F(x,y,t)\right\},\quad\forall(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T),

where

F(x,y,t):=d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big).

We put

(7.2)

\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{e^{-2|K|T}R^{2}}{8M_{0}},

and collect some basic properties of
f_{\varepsilon}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Under the above notations, we have for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
that

(1)

-2M_{0}\leqslant f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant 0
, for any
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
;

(2)

there holds

(7.3)

f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\min_{y\in B_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)}\left\{\frac{e^{-2Kt}\cdot d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-F(x,y,t)\right\}

for any
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
, where
C_{1}:=\sqrt{6M_{0}\cdot e^{2|K|T}}
;

(3)

f_{\varepsilon}\in C(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T))\cap W^{1,2}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T))
for any
t_{*}>0
.

Proof.

For (1), we first see that
f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant 0
, by taking
y=x
in the definition. The lower bound is given by the fact
f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\geqslant-F(x,y,t)
and
F(x,y,t)\leqslant 2M_{0}
, since
u^{t}(\Omega)\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
.

For (2), if
d(x,y)\geqslant C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
then we have

\frac{e^{-2Kt}\cdot d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-F(x,y,t)\geqslant\frac{e^{-2Kt}\cdot 6M_{0}\cdot e^{2|K|T}\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}-2M_{0}\geqslant M_{0}>0.

Since
f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant 0
, it follows (
7.3
).

For (3), fixed any
t_{*}>0
, given any
(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
, we choose one
y^{\prime}\in\Omega
such that

f_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\frac{e^{-2Kt^{\prime}}d^{2}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}{2\varepsilon}-F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

Therefore, by (
7.1
), we get

\begin{split}f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-f_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})&\leqslant\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y^{\prime})}{2\varepsilon}-F(x,y^{\prime},t)-\left(\frac{e^{-2Kt^{\prime}}d^{2}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}{2\varepsilon}-F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right)\\
&=\frac{\big(e^{-2Kt}-e^{-2Kt^{\prime}}\big)d^{2}(x,y^{\prime})+e^{-2Kt^{\prime}}\big(d^{2}(x,y^{\prime})-d^{2}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)}{2\varepsilon}\\
&\quad+d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y^{\prime},t)\big)-d_{Y}\big(u(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),u(y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\big)\\
&\leqslant\frac{{\rm diam}(\Omega)\cdot e^{2|K|T}}{\varepsilon}\big(|t-t^{\prime}|+d(x,x^{\prime})\big)+d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\big)+cL|t-t^{\prime}|,\end{split}

where, for the last inequality, we have used the fact that
|d_{Y}(u(y^{\prime},t)u(y^{\prime},t^{\prime})|\leqslant cL|t-t^{\prime}|
for any
t,t^{\prime}>t_{0}
(since Theorem
5.1
). By the symmetry of
(x,t)
and
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})
, we obtain (noticing that
K\leqslant 0
)

|f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-f_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})|\leqslant C_{\varepsilon,L}\big(|t-t^{\prime}|+d(x,x^{\prime})\big)+d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\big)

for any
t,t^{\prime}>t_{0}
, where

C_{\varepsilon,L}:=\frac{{\rm diam}(\Omega)\cdot e^{2|K|T}}{\varepsilon}+cL.

This implies the following:

(i)
f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous at
(x,t)\
(since
u
is continuous at
(x,t)
), and

(ii)
e^{f_{\varepsilon}}_{r}(x,t)\leqslant 4C^{2}_{\varepsilon,L}+2e^{u}_{r}(x,t)
for any
r>0
sufficiently small. Therefore, we have

e^{f_{\varepsilon}}(x,t)\leqslant 4C^{2}_{\varepsilon,L}+2e^{u}(x,t).

This yields
f_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,2}\big(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)\big)
.
∎

It is convenient for us to consider another notion of weak solutions of the parabolic equations, the viscosity supersolutions (or subsolutions).

Definition 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a complete Riemannian manifold and
U_{T}:=U\times(0,T)\subset\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}
be an open set, and let
f\in C(U_{T})
. A function
g\in C(U_{T})
is a
viscosity supersolution (resp. subsolution)
of

(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=f

in
U_{T}
if, for any
\phi\in C^{2}(U_{T})
and any
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in U_{T}
such that
u-\phi
attains a local minimum (resp. maximum) at
(\hat{x},\hat{t})
, one has

(\Delta-\partial_{t})\phi(\hat{x},\hat{t})\leqslant f(\hat{x},\hat{t})\qquad(resp.\ \geqslant).

Lemma 7.3
.

For any fixed
t_{*}>0
, the function
F(x,y,t)
is a viscosity subsolution of the equation

(\Delta^{(2)}-\partial_{t})F(x,y,t)=-4cL,

on
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
, where
\Delta^{(2)}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M\times M
, and the constant
cL
is given in Theorem
5.1
.

Proof.

For any
P\in Y
, from (
3.8
), the function
f_{P}(x,t):=d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),P\big)
satisfies

\Delta f_{P}\geqslant\partial_{t}f_{P}

on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
in the sense of distributions. On the other hand, by triangle inequality and Theorem
5.1
, we have

|f_{P}(x,t)-f_{P}(x,t^{\prime})|\leqslant d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(x,t^{\prime})\big)\leqslant cL|t-t^{\prime}|

for any
t,t^{\prime}\geqslant t_{*}
and
x\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
. Hence, we have
|\partial_{t}f_{P}|(x,t)\leqslant cL,
and then

\Delta f_{P}\geqslant\partial_{t}f_{P}\geqslant-cL

on
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
in the sense of distributions.

It is well-known that every continuous distributional subsolution is also a viscosity subsolution (see, for example,
[
30
]
). Hence, we see that
\Delta f_{P}\geqslant-cL
in the sense of viscosity.

Now we will show that for any
\hat{t}\in(t_{*},T)
,

(7.4)

\Delta^{(2)}F(x,y,\hat{t})\geqslant-2cL

on
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x})
in the sense of viscosity. Given any
\phi(x,y)\in C^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x}))
and any point
(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x})
such that the function
F(x,y,\hat{t})-\phi(x,y)
attains a local maximum at
(\hat{x},\hat{y})
. Then the function
\phi(\cdot,\hat{y})
is in
C^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x}))
and the function
F(\cdot,\hat{y},\hat{t})-\phi(\cdot,\hat{y})
attains a local maximum at
\hat{x}
. From the above fact that
\Delta d_{Y}\big(u(\cdot,\hat{t}),u(\hat{y},\hat{t})\big)\geqslant-cL
in the sense of viscosity, we conclude that

\Delta_{x}\phi(\hat{x},\hat{y})\geqslant-cL.

Similarly, we have
\Delta_{y}\phi(\hat{x},\hat{y})\geqslant-cL
too. Since
\phi\in C^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x}))
, we obtain

\Delta^{(2)}\phi(\hat{x},\hat{y})=\Delta_{x}\phi(\hat{x},\hat{y})+\Delta_{y}\phi(\hat{x},\hat{y})\geqslant-2cL.

Therefore, we conclude that
\Delta^{(2)}F(x,y,\hat{t})\geqslant-2cL

on
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{x})
in the sense of viscosity.

Finally, by Theorem
5.1
and the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}|F(x,y,t)-F(x,y,t^{\prime})|\leqslant d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(x,t^{\prime})\big)+d_{Y}\big(u(y,t),u(y,t^{\prime})\big)\leqslant 2cL|t-t^{\prime}|.\end{split}

This implies
|\partial_{t}F(x,y,t)|\leqslant 2cL
. By combining with (
7.4
), it follows that

(\Delta^{(2)}-\partial_{t})F(x,y,t)\geqslant-4cL

in the sense of viscosity.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

Compared to our previous work
[
56
]
for showing the Lipschitz continuity of harmonic maps, it seems more natural to replace (
7.1
) with

f_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\inf_{(y,s)\in\Omega\times(0,T)}\left\{\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y)+|t-s|^{2}}{2\varepsilon}-d_{Y}\big(u(y,s),u(x,t)\big)\right\}

for all
(x,t)\in\Omega^{\prime}\times(0,T)
. However, the function
d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,s)\big)
appearing in the right-hand side depends on two time variables, which makes it difficult to satisfy a parabolic-type partial differential equation, unlike to the function
(d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)
in Lemma
7.3
.

We also need the following parabolic perturbation lemma.

Lemma 7.5

(pertubation)
.

Let
(U\times V)_{T}:=U\times V\times(0,T)

, where

. Assume that

Then there exists a constant
\delta_{0}>0

g

\hat{y}

Proof.

This comes essentially from the Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci estimate for the viscosity solutions to the parabolic equations (see
[

∎

We can now give the key lemma of this section.

Lemma 7.6
.

Under the above notations, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, the function
f_{\varepsilon}
is a viscosity supersolution of the heat equation in
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
.

Proof.

Fix any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. We shall prove the assertion by a contradiction argument. Suppose that it fails. Then there exists a function
v\in C^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T))
and a point
(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
such that the function
f_{\varepsilon}-v
attains a local minimum at
(\hat{x},\hat{t})
, however

\theta_{0}:=(\Delta-\partial_{t})v(\hat{x},\hat{t})>0.

Let
\hat{y}
be a point where the minimum in (
7.3
) is achieved. Then the function

H(x,y,t):=\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}-F(x,y,t)-v(x,t)

attains a local minimum at
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{t})
.

(i) We now want to perturb the minimum point
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{t})
to a point where the asymptotic mean value inequality in Corollary
6.5
is available.

From Corollary
6.5
, there exists a
(\mu\times\mu\times\mathscr{L}^{1})
-zero measurable set
\mathscr{N}
such that for any
P\in Y
,

(7.5)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(x_{0},x)w_{P,s}(x_{0},x,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu(x)+\int_{\Omega}p_{s}(y_{0},y)w_{P,s}(y_{0},y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu(y)\Big)\leqslant 0,

for all
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\big(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times B_{R}(\bar{y})\times(t_{*},T)\big)\setminus\mathscr{N}
.

By applying Lemma
7.5
, there exists
\delta>0
(arbitrarily small), three functions
\eta_{0}(t)\in C^{\infty}(\hat{t}-\delta,\hat{t}+\delta),\eta_{1}(x)\in C^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(\hat{x}))
and
\eta_{2}(y)\in C^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(\hat{y}))
, and some point
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\in\big(B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,\hat{t}+\delta)\big)\setminus\mathscr{N}
,
so that the function

H_{1}(x,y,t):=H(x,y,t)+\eta_{0}(t)+\eta_{1}(x)+\eta_{2}(y)

attains a minimum at
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
in
B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,t_{0}]
, and

(7.6)

|\partial_{t}\eta_{0}|(t)+|\Delta\eta_{1}|(x)+|\Delta\eta_{2}|(y)\leqslant\frac{\theta_{0}}{8},

for all
(x,y,t)\in\big(B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,\hat{t}+\delta)\big).
We can also assume that
\delta>0
is so small that

(7.7)

(\Delta-\partial_{t})v(x,t)\geqslant\frac{\theta_{0}}{2},\qquad\forall(x,t)\in B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,\hat{t}+\delta),

since
v\in C^{2}(B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)).

(ii) We shall get a contradiction by the minimum of
H_{1}
at
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
.

For any
s>0
, we denote by
\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}:=p_{s}(\cdot,x_{0})\cdot\mu
and
\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}:=p_{s}(\cdot,y_{0})\cdot\mu
the heat kernel measures at time
s>0
with center
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
respectively. Moreover, we denote by
\Pi^{s}:=\Pi^{s}_{(x_{0},y_{0})}
be an optimal coupling of
\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}
and
\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}
with respected to the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance, that is a probability measure on
M\times M
whose first and second marginals are
\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}
and
\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}
repectively and

\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\leqslant\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y){\rm d}\gamma(x,y)

for any probability measre
\gamma
on
M\times M
with the same marginals.

Since
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
is a minimum of
H_{1}(x,y,t)
in
B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,t_{0}]
, we have

(7.8)

\begin{split}0\leqslant&\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})}\Big(H_{1}(x,y,t_{0}-s)-H_{1}(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
&:=\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(I_{1}(s)+I_{2}(s)+I_{3}(s)+I_{4}(s)\Big),\end{split}

where

(7.9)

\begin{split}I_{1}(s):&=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})}\Big(e^{-2K(t_{0}-s)}d^{2}(x,y)-e^{-2Kt_{0}}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y),\\
I_{2}(s):&=-\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})}\Big(F(x,y,t_{0}-s)-F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y),\\
I_{3}(s):&=-\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})}\Big(v(x,t_{0}-s)-v(x_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y),\\
I_{4}(s):&=\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})}\Big(\big(\eta_{0}(t_{0}-s)-\eta_{0}(t)\big)+\big(\eta_{1}(x)-\eta_{1}(x_{0})\big)+\big(\eta_{2}(y)-\eta_{2}(y_{0})\big)\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y).\end{split}

The estimates to the integrals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}
will be given in the following. For simplifying the notations, we denote by
B_{1}:=B_{\delta}(\hat{x})
and
B_{2}:=B_{\delta}(\hat{y})
.

For estimating
I_{1}(s)
, we recall the contraction property of
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance (
[
49
, Corollary 1.4]
, namely

(7.10)

\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\leqslant e^{-2Ks}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0}),\qquad\forall s>0.

We claim that

(7.11)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}\Big|d^{2}(x,y)-d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big|{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)=0.

Indeed, by the triangle inequality, we have

(7.12)

\begin{split}|d^{2}(x,y)-d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})|&=\big|\big(d(x,y)-d(x_{0},y_{0})\big)^{2}+2d(x_{0},y_{0})\cdot\big(d(x,y)-d(x_{0},y_{0})\big)\big|\\
&\leqslant\big(d(x,x_{0})+d(y,y_{0})\big)^{2}+2d(x_{0},y_{0})\cdot\big(d(x,x_{0})+d(y,y_{0})\big)\\
&\leqslant 2d^{2}(x,x_{0})+2d^{2}(y,y_{0})+2d(x_{0},y_{0})\cdot\big(d(x,x_{0})+d(y,y_{0})\big).\\
\end{split}

Noticing that the first and second marginals of
\Pi^{s}
are
\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}
and
\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}
repectively, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)&=\int_{[(M\setminus B_{1})\times M]\cup[B_{1}\times(M\setminus B_{2})]}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
&=\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{B_{1}\times(M\setminus B_{2})}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
&\leqslant\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}(2\delta)^{2}{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y),\end{split}

where we have used that
d(x,x_{0})\leqslant 2\delta
on
B_{1}
, since
x_{0}\in B_{1}
. Similarly, we get

\begin{split}\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}d^{2}(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)&\leqslant\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}d^{2}(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)+\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}(2\delta)^{2}{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x),\\
\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}d(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)&\leqslant\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}d(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}(2\delta){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y),\\
\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}d(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)&\leqslant\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}d(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)+\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}(2\delta){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x).\end{split}

Integrating (
7.12
) over
(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})
and substituting above four inequalities, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}\Big|d^{2}(x,y)-d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big|{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
\leqslant&\ 2\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}d^{2}(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+2\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}d^{2}(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)\\
&\quad+2d(x_{0},y_{0})\cdot\left(\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}d(x,x_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}d(y,y_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)\right)\\
&\quad+(8\delta^{2}+4\delta d(x_{0},y_{0}))\left(\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}1{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}1{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)\right).\end{split}

Now the claim (
7.11
) comes from Lemma
5.3
(by taking
\ell=0,1,2
therein).

It is clear that

(7.13)

\begin{split}\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)&=\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}1{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{B_{1}\times(M\setminus B_{2})}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
&\leqslant\int_{M\setminus B_{1}}1{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{x_{0}}(x)+\int_{M\setminus B_{2}}1{\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)=o(s)\end{split}

as
s\to 0^{+}.

By combining with (
7.10
) and (
7.11
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}\Big(d^{2}(x,y)-e^{-2Ks}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
=\ &\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}\Big(d^{2}(x,y)-d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)+\frac{1-e^{-2Ks}}{s}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
=\ &\frac{1}{s}\int_{M\times M}\Big(d^{2}(x,y)-d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)+o(1)+\frac{1-e^{-2Ks}}{s}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
\leqslant\ &\frac{e^{-2Ks}-1}{s}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})+o(1)+\frac{1-e^{-2Ks}}{s}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
=\ &\frac{e^{-2Ks}-1}{s}d^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})\int_{(M\times M)\setminus(B_{1}\times B_{2})}1{\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)+o(1)\\
=\ &o(1).\end{split}

That implies

(7.14)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}I_{1}(s)\leqslant 0.

For estimating
I_{2}(s)
, we claim

(7.15)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}I_{2}(s)\leqslant 0.

In the case of
F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=0
, by noticing that
-F(x,y,t_{0}-s)\leqslant 0
, (
7.15
) holds trivially. In the following, we assume that
F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\not=0
.
Let us put

P=u(x,t_{0}-s),\quad Q=u(x_{0},t_{0}),\quad R=u(y_{0},t_{0})\quad{\rm and}\quad S=u(y,t_{0}-s).

From Lemma
2.3
(2) and the notation (
6.10
), we have

\Big(F(x,y,t_{0}-s)-F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\Big)\cdot F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\geqslant-w_{Q_{m},s}(x_{0},x,t_{0})-w_{Q_{m},s}(y_{0},y,t_{0}),

where
Q_{m}
is the midder point of
Q
and
R
(i.e.,
d_{Y}(Q_{m},Q)=d_{Y}(Q_{m},R)=d_{Y}(Q,R)/2
).
Integrating this over
B_{1}\times B_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}\Big(-F(x,y,t_{0}-s)+F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\cdot F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})\\
\leqslant&\int_{B_{1}}w_{Q_{m},s}(x_{0},x,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}(x)+\int_{B_{2}}w_{Q_{m},s}(y_{0},y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}(y).\end{split}

By using
F(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})>0
, (
7.5
) and

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{1}}w_{P,s}(x_{0},x,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}(x)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2}}w_{P,s}(y_{0},y,t_{0}){\rm d}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(y)=0

(due to the fact that

),
we conclude (

).

Notice that

\begin{split}I_{3}(s)&=\int_{B_{1}\times B_{2}}\Big(-v(x,t_{0}-s)+v(x_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\Pi^{s}(x,y)\\
&=\int_{B_{1}}\Big(-v(x,t_{0}-s)+v(x_{0},t_{0})\Big){\rm d}\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}(x)\cdot\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}(B_{2}).\end{split}

By using Lemma
6.2
to
-v(x,t)+v(x_{0},t_{0})
and (
7.7
), we have

(7.16)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{I_{3}(s)}{s}\leqslant-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\cdot\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\mu^{s}_{y_{0}}(B_{2})=-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}.

By using (

) and Lemma
6.2
, the same argument shows that

(7.17)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{I_{4}(s)}{s}\leqslant\frac{\theta_{0}}{8}.

Finally, the combination of (

), (

) and (

7.2.
Lipschitz continuity in space variables

We continue to assume that
u(x,t)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty),Y)
be a semi-group solution of the harmonic map heat flow with a bounded initial data
u_{0}
, i.e.,
u_{0}(\Omega)\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for some
M_{0}>0
and
P_{0}\in Y
.
We also assume that
u
is continous in
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
.
For some ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
with
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
and some
T>0
, let
f_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
be given in (
7.1
).

For convenience, we denote by

(7.18)

v_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=-f_{\varepsilon}(x,t),\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}),\ \ \forall(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T).

Then
0\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant 2M_{0}
and

(7.19)

(\Delta-\partial_{t})v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\geqslant 0\quad{\rm on}\ \ B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T),

in the sense of viscosity, and hence also in the sense of distributions (see
[
30
]
).

The classical Hamilton-Jacobi equation says that the temporal derivative of the Hamilton flow is equal to minus of the square norm of the spatial gradient. The next lemma reminds the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

Lemma 7.7
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, we have

(7.20)

\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\varepsilon}\leqslant 2e^{2|K|T}\cdot\Big({\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\Big)^{2}

for almost all
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
, where the constant
C_{1}
is given in (
7.3
).

Proof.

Let
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
. If
{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)=+\infty
, we have done. In the following, we assume that
{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)<+\infty
.

According to (
7.3
), there exists
y:=y_{x}\in B_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)
such that

(7.21)

v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=F(x,y,t)-\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}.

If
y=x
, we have
v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=0
, then (
7.20
) holds trivially. We can assume that
d(x,y):=s>0
.
By using (
5.6
) and
y\in B_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)
, we have

(7.22)

F(x,y,t)=\frac{F(x,y,t)}{s}s\leqslant{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\cdot s={\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\cdot d(x,y).

By combining this with (
7.21
) and the fact that
v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0
, we obtain

\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon}=F(x,y,t)-v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant F(x,y,t)\leqslant{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\cdot d(x,y).

This is

d(x,y)\leqslant 2\varepsilon\cdot e^{2Kt}\cdot{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\leqslant 2\varepsilon\cdot e^{2|K|T}\cdot{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x).

Substituting into (
7.22
) and using (
7.21
) again, we conclude that

v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant F(x,y,t)\leqslant{\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\cdot d(x,y)\leqslant 2\varepsilon\cdot e^{2|K|T}\cdot\Big({\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\Big)^{2}.

This is (
7.20
). The proof is finished.
∎

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
\Omega,Y
be as above and let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,Y)
with the image
u_{0}(\Omega)\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}
for some
M_{0}>0
and
P_{0}\in Y
.
Suppose that
u(x,t)
is a semi-group weak solution of the harmonic map heat flow from
\Omega
to
Y
with initial data
u_{0}
. Let
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
and
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
with
R<1
and
R^{2}<t_{*}/2
. Then we have

d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)\leqslant C\cdot d(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in B_{R/4}(\bar{x}),\ \ \forall t\in(t_{*},T),

for some constant
C
depending only on
n,K,R,M_{0},t_{*},T,L
and
\int_{\Omega\times(t_{*},T)}e_{u}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t
, where
L
is given in (
2.10
).

Proof.

We put
\varepsilon_{1}:=\min\{\varepsilon_{0}/2,R/4\}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is given in (
7.2
).

Integrating (
7.20
) over
Q_{R/2}(\bar{x},t_{0}):=B_{R/2}(\bar{x})\times(t_{0}-R^{2}/4,t_{0}+R^{2}/4)
and using (
5.7
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{Q_{R/2}(\bar{x},t_{0})}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\varepsilon}{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t&\leqslant 2e^{2|K|T}\int_{Q_{R/2}(\bar{x},t_{0})}\Big({\rm lip}_{C_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}}u^{t}(x)\Big)^{2}{\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t\\
&\leqslant C_{n,K,R,T}\int_{t_{0}-R^{2}/4}^{t_{0}+R^{2}/4}\int_{\Omega}e_{u^{t}}(x){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t+C_{n,K,R,L}\\
&\leqslant C_{n,K,R,T}\int_{\Omega\times(t_{0}/2,T)}e_{u}(x,t){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}t+C_{n,K,R,L}:=\mathscr{A}.\end{split}

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
.
Noticing that
(\Delta-\partial_{t})\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0
in the sense of distributions, we get

\sup_{Q_{R/4}(\bar{x},t_{0})}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\varepsilon}\leqslant C_{n,K,R}\int_{Q_{R/2}(\bar{x},t_{0})}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\varepsilon}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}t\leqslant C\cdot\mathscr{A}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}).
From the definition of
v_{\varepsilon}
, we get

(7.23)

\frac{F(x,y,t)}{\varepsilon}-\frac{e^{-2Kt}d^{2}(x,y)}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\leqslant C\mathscr{A}

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
and any
(x,y,t)\in B_{R/4}(\bar{x})\times\Omega\times(t_{*},T)
.

Given any two point
x,y\in B_{R/4}(\bar{x})
, if
d(x,y)<\varepsilon_{1}
, we take
\varepsilon=d(x,y)
in (
7.23
) and get

\frac{F(x,y,t)}{d(x,y)}\leqslant C\mathscr{A}+e^{2|K|T}:=\mathscr{A}_{1}.

If
d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon_{1}
, we can choose a finite number of points
x_{1}=x,x_{2},\cdots,x_{\ell}=y
such that
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})<\varepsilon
for all
i=1,\cdots,\ell-1,
and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})\leqslant d(x,y).
By the triangle inequality, we get

F(x,y,t)\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}F(x_{j},x_{j+1},t)\leqslant\mathscr{A}_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})\leqslant\mathscr{A}_{1}\cdot d(x,y).

The proof is finished.
∎

By combining Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
7.8
, we conclude that the following local Lipschitz continuity holds for the harmonic map heat flow in space-time.

This is a direct corollary of Theorem

and Theorem

8.
Eell-Sampson-type Bochner Inequality

In this section, we shall prove the Eell-Sampson-type inequality.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded open domain, let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a
CAT(0)
space, and let
u(x,t):\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\to Y
be a semi-group weak solution of the harmonic map heat flow with a bounded initial data
u_{0}
. Then the function
{\rm lip}_{x}u\in V_{2,\rm loc}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty))\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega\times(0,+\infty))
and satisfies

(8.1)

(\Delta-\partial_{t})({\rm lip}_{x}u)^{2}\geqslant 2|\nabla{\rm lip}_{x}u|^{2}+2K({\rm lip}_{x}u)^{2}

on
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
in the sense of distributions, where
{\rm lip}_{x}u(x,t):={\rm lip}u^{t}(x)
.

Theorem
8.1
is a parabolic version of our previous joint work with Xiao Zhong
[
55
, Theorem 1.9]
for harmonic maps into
CAT(0)
spaces. We will refine the arguments in the previous section by lifting the nonlinear “Hamilton Jacobi” flow from the usual index 2 to higher order index
p
to extract quantitative information.

We use the same notations as in the previous section. Let
M_{0}>0
be a constant such that
u_{0}(\Omega)\subset\overline{B_{M_{0}}(P_{0})}\subset Y
. Fix arbitrarily
0<t_{*}<T<+\infty
, and any ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
with
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\subset\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
R\leqslant 1
and
M_{0}\geqslant 1
. According to Corollary
7.9
,
u\in Lip(B_{2R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T))
. We denote by
\tilde{L}
a Lipschitz constant of
u
in
B_{2R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
.

Let
p\geqslant 2
and
\varepsilon>0
, we define

(8.2)

f_{\varepsilon,p}(x,t):=\inf_{y\in\Omega}\left\{\frac{e^{-pKt}\cdot d^{p}(x,y)}{p\varepsilon^{p-1}}-F(x,y,t)\right\},\quad\forall(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T),

where
F(x,y,t)=d_{Y}\big(u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)
. When
p=2
,
f_{\varepsilon,2}
is the same
f_{\varepsilon}
in the previous section. It is clear that

-2M_{0}\leqslant f_{\varepsilon,p}(x,t)\leqslant 0,\qquad\forall(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T).

Since
p^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leqslant 2
for all
p\geqslant 2
(and assumption
R\leqslant 1
and
M_{0}\geqslant 1
), we have

\frac{e^{-\frac{p}{p-1}|K|T}\cdot R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{(4M_{0})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\geqslant\frac{e^{-2|K|T}R^{2}}{8M_{0}}:=\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\forall p\geqslant 2.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and any
(x,t)\in B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(0,T)
, the infimum in the (
8.2
) can be attained at a point
y\in B_{2R}(\bar{x})
.
Similar to the key Lemma
7.6
in the previous section, we have the following.

Lemma 8.2
.

Under the above notations, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and any
p\geqslant 2
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
f_{\varepsilon,p}
is a viscosity supersolution of the heat equation in
B_{R}(\bar{x})\times(t_{*},T)
.

Proof.

The proof is closely parallel to that of Lemma
7.6
, with the contraction property of the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance replaced by that of the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance. Hence, we follow the same argument and highlight the necessary modifications.

The first step (i) is the same, with the function
H_{1}(x,y,t)
replaced by

\tilde{H}_{1}(x,y,t):=\frac{e^{-pKtd^{p}(x,y)}}{p\varepsilon^{p-1}}-F(x,y,t)-v(x,t)+\eta_{0}(t)+\eta_{1}(x)+\eta_{2}(y).

It attains a minimum at
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
in
B_{\delta}(\hat{x})\times B_{\delta}(\hat{y})\times(\hat{t}-\delta,t_{0}]
, and the asmyptotic mean value inequality (
7.5
) is available at
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
.

In step (ii), by using the same notations of
\mu_{x_{0}}^{s}
and
\mu_{y_{0}}^{s}
, we take the optimal coupling of them with respect to the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance, denoted by
\tilde{\Pi}^{s}:=\tilde{\Pi}^{s}_{(x_{0},y_{0})}
. The contraction property of
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance(
[
49
, Corollary 1.4]
) states

(8.3)

\int_{M\times M}d^{p}(x,y){\rm d}\tilde{\Pi}^{s}(x,y)\leqslant e^{-pKs}d^{p}(x_{0},y_{0}),\qquad\forall s>0.

Similar to (

Appendix A A
The Parabolic Perturbation Lemma via ABP Estimates.

In this appendix, we give a proof of the parabolic perturbation lemma, Lemma
7.5
.
Let us recall the Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci (ABP) estimates for parabolic equations in
[
4
]
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
, and let
r>0
. Given
f:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
, the
parabolic upper contact set
of
f
of scale
r
is defined as

(A.1)

\Gamma^{+}_{r}(f):=\left\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}\Big|\ \begin{aligned} &\exists\xi\in B_{r}(0)\ {\rm such\ that}\\
&f(z,s)-\left<{\xi},{z}\right>\leqslant f(x,t)-\left<{\xi},{x}\right>,\ \ \forall(z,s)\in\Omega\times(0,t]\end{aligned}\right\}.

Equivalent, a point
(x,t)\in\Gamma_{r}^{+}(f)
if and only if there exists
\xi\in B_{r}(0)
such that
(x,t)
is one of the maximum point of function
f(z,s)-\left<{\xi},{z}\right>
in
\Omega\times(0,t]
.

In
[
4
]
, Argiolas, Charro, and Peral have proven an ABP estimate for some nonlinear parabolic equations in divergence form. For our purpose, we consider only the following linear operator. Let

Lf:=\partial_{j}(a_{ij}\partial_{i}f)

be a uniformly elliptic operator on
\Omega
with elliptic constants
0<\lambda<\Lambda<\infty
, and the coefficients
a_{ij}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
.

Theorem A.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded open domain and
g\in L^{n+1}(\Omega_{T})\cap C(\Omega_{T})
. Consider
f\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
wich satisfies

(L-\partial_{t})f\geqslant-g(x,t)

in
\Omega_{T}
in the viscosity sense. Assume that
\sup_{\Omega}f>0
and
\sup_{\partial_{\mathcal{P}}\Omega_{T}}f^{+}=0
. Then the following ABP estimate holds: For any
r\in(0,\sup_{\Omega}f)
,

(A.2)

r^{n+1}\leqslant C_{n,d,\lambda,\Lambda}\cdot\|g^{+}\|_{L^{n+1}(\Gamma^{+}_{r/d}(f))},

where
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}{\Omega_{T}}
is the parabolic boundary of
\Omega_{T}
,
d:={\rm diam}(\Omega)
and
f^{+}:=\max\{0,f\}
.

Proof.

This is contained in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2]
. The first step is to consider the case when
f\in C^{2,1}(\Omega_{T})\cap C(\overline{\Omega_{T}})
, we refer to
[
4
, Eq. (17) and Eq. (18)]
to get the estimate (
A.2
). The second step deals with the general case when
f\in C(\overline{\Omega_{T}})
, see
[
4
, the last three lines on page 887]
for estimate (
A.1
).
∎

Corollary A.3
.

Let
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded open domain,
0\in\Omega
, and
g\in L^{n+1}(\Omega_{T})\cap C(\Omega_{T})
. Consider
f\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
wich satisfies

(L-\partial_{t})f\leqslant g(x,t)

in
\Omega_{T}
in the viscosity sense. Suppose that
f
attains its minimum at unique point
(0,\hat{t})\in\Omega_{T}
. Assume that
E
is a measurable set with full measure in
\Omega_{T}
. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exist a vector
\xi\in B_{\delta}(0)
and a point
(v_{0},t_{0})\in E\cap\Omega_{T}
such that the function
f(v)+\left<{\xi},{v}\right>
attains one of its minimum in
\Omega\times(0,t_{0}]
at
(x_{0},t_{0}).

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
. Since
E
has full measure, it suffices to show
|\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(-f)|>0.

We choose a small open set
V
such that
(0,\hat{t})\in V\Subset\Omega_{T}
. As
f\in C(\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V)
and that
\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V
is a bounded closed set,
f
has a minimum in
\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V
. The assumption that
(0,\hat{t})
is the unique minimum point of
f
on
\Omega_{T}
implies

\min_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V}f>f(0,\hat{t}).

We define a function

\tilde{f}(x,t):=-f(x,t)+\frac{\min_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V}f+f(0,\hat{t})}{2}\quad{\rm on}\ \Omega_{T}.

Then we have the following:

(1)

\tilde{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
,

(2)

(L-\partial_{t})\tilde{f}\geqslant-g
in
\Omega_{T}
in the viscosity sense,

(3)

\sup_{\partial_{\mathcal{P}}U}\tilde{f}^{+}=0,\quad
(since
\max_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V}\tilde{f}=\frac{-\min_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V}f+f(0,\hat{t})}{2}<0
and
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}U\subset\partial U\subset\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V
).

(4)

\sup_{U}\tilde{f}>0,\quad
(since
\tilde{f}(0,\hat{t})=\frac{\min_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}\setminus V}f-f(0,\hat{t})}{2}>0
).

Therefore, by using Theorem
A.2
to
\tilde{f}
, we get that
\|(-g)^{+}\|_{L^{n+1}(\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(\tilde{f}))}>0
. In particular,
|\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(\tilde{f})|>0.
By the definition (
A.1
), it is clear that
\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(-f)=\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(\tilde{f})
. Hence
|\Gamma_{\delta}^{+}(-f)|>0
and the proof is finished.
∎

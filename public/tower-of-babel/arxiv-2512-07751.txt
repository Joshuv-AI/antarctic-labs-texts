Exact supported co-degree bounds for Hamilton cycles
Shoham Letzter, Arjun Ranganathan

Abstract
For any k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and ℓ ∈ [ k − 1 ] \ell\in[k-1] such that ( k , ℓ ) ≠ ( 3 , 1 ) (k,\ell)\neq(3,1) , we show that any sufficiently large k k -graph G G must contain a Hamilton ℓ \ell -cycle provided that it has no isolated vertices and every set of k − 1 k-1 vertices contained in an edge is contained in at least ( 1 − 1 ⌊ k k − ℓ ⌋ ​ ( k − ℓ ) ) ​ n − ( k − 3 ) \left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor{\frac{k}{k-\ell}\rfloor}(k-\ell)}\right)n-(k-3) edges. We also show that this bound is tight for infinitely many values of k k and ℓ \ell and is off by at most 1 1 for all others, and is hence essentially optimal. This improves an asymptotic version of this result due to Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén [ 31 ] , and the case ℓ = k − 1 \ell=k-1 completely resolves a conjecture of Illingworth, Lang, Müyesser, Parczyk and Sgueglia [ 15 ] . These results support the utility of minimum supported co-degree conditions in a k k -graph, a recently introduced variant of the standard notion of minimum co-degree applicable to k k -graphs with non-trivial strong independent sets. Our proof techniques involve a novel blow-up tiling framework introduced by Lang [ 23 ] , avoiding traditional approaches using the regularity and blow-up lemmas.

Introduction

A widespread research theme in extremal graph theory is to determine sufficient conditions that ensure the existence of specified spanning structures in graphs and hypergraphs. A classic result in this vein, due to Dirac
[
4
]
, states that any graph on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree at least
n/2
contains a Hamilton cycle, and it is not too difficult to see that the degree condition is the best possible. Dirac’s theorem has been generalised to various distinct settings over the years (see the surveys
[
10
,
22
,
40
,
21
,
6
,
34
]
) and this has contributed to the development of several powerful techniques, such as regularity, absorption, and rotation-extension methods. In this paper, we will focus on hypergraph extensions of Dirac’s theorem.

A
k
-uniform hypergraph
or simply a
k
-graph

G
consists of a set of vertices
V(G)
and a set of edges
E(G)
, where each edge consists of exactly
k
vertices. The most common generalisation of a minimum degree condition from graphs to hypergraphs is accomplished via the notion of a
minimum co-degree
– which is the minimum
d
such that every set of
k-1
vertices in
G
is contained in at least
d
edges – and we denote it here by
\delta(G)
. The larger uniformity
k
allows for multiple distinct ways to define a cycle in a
k
-graph. The cyclic structures we search for are
\ell
-cycles
, which are perhaps the most common extensions of graph cycles. Intuitively, we think of an
\ell
-cycle as a spanning path formed by a cyclic set of edges such that each edge has exactly
\ell
vertices in common with the preceding edge (it could intersect other edges as well). More formally, given an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
G
and any
\ell\in[k-1]
such that
k-\ell
divides
n
, we define a
Hamilton
\ell
-cycle
in
G
to be an ordering of the vertices of
G
, say
v_{1}\dots v_{n}
, such that the vertices of the subsequence
v_{i(k-\ell)+1}\dots v_{i(k-\ell)+k}
form an edge for all
i\geq 0
, where we view the indices cyclically modulo
n
. Since each edge of an
\ell
-cycle contains
k-\ell
vertices that were not in the previous edge, we trivially require that
k-\ell
divides
n
so that when we “cycle around” the vertices of
G
, the original sequence of edges gets repeated. We call
(k-1)
-cycles and
1
-cycles
tight
and
loose
cycles respectively.

The question of determining sufficient minimum co-degree conditions to ensure a tight Hamilton cycle was first raised by Katona and Kierstead
[
16
]
who conjectured that if
\delta(G)\geq(n-k+2)/2
then
G
has a tight Hamilton cycle, and they showed that this bound is the best possible. Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
35
,
37
]
first proved this asymptotically for
k\geq 3
and
\delta(G)\geq n/2+o(n)
, and later obtained an exact result for
k=3

[
39
]
. Additionally, Liu and Liu
[
25
]
made progress towards an exact result for
k=4
. In fact, for any
\ell\in[k-1]
such that
k-\ell
divides
k
, a tight Hamilton cycle will contain such a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle (provided the necessary divisibility condition holds). If we further suppose that
k
divides
n
, then this Hamilton
\ell
-cycle will imply the existence of a perfect matching which necessitates
\delta(G)\geq n/2-k
as shown in
[
19
,
38
]
, and hence the aforementioned tight cycle bound of
\delta(G)\geq n/2+o(n)
is tight. If
k
does not divide
n
, then Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
38
]
have determined the minimum codegree threshold for a near-perfect matching of size
\left\lfloor n/k\right\rfloor
to be roughly
n/k
. It is therefore plausible that the correct minimum codegree threshold for a spanning
\ell
-cycle, when
k-\ell
divides
k
but is not
1
and
n
is not divisible by
k
, is significantly lower than
n/2
. Conversely, if
k-\ell
does not divide
k
, a series of works by Kühn and Osthus
[
20
]
, Keevash, Kühn, Mycroft and Osthus
[
17
]
, Hàn and Schacht
[
13
]
and Kühn, Mycroft and Osthus
[
19
]
have established that

\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{\left\lceil\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rceil(k-\ell)}+o(n)

suffices and is optimal up to the
o(n)
term, yielding a much lower threshold. Exact bounds, however, have proven notoriously difficult to obtain and are known only in a few special cases. To the best of our knowledge, these are
(k,\ell)=(3,2)
by Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
39
]
,
(k,\ell)=(3,1)
by Czygrinow and Molla
[
3
]
,
(k,\ell)=(4,2)
by Garbe and Mycroft
[
7
]
, and
k\geq 3
and
\ell<k/2
by Han and Zhao
[
14
]
. We refer the reader to the surveys
[
22
,
43
,
34
]
for more detailed discussions.

A drawback of a minimum co-degree condition is that it tends to be a fairly strong requirement. For instance, if we start with a complete
k
-graph and remove all edges that contain a fixed pair of vertices, it is immediate that the resulting hypergraph has minimum co-degree equal to zero. However, if
k\geq 3
, it is not hard to see that even fairly small hypergraphs of this form are quite dense, and trivially contain Hamilton cycles. In fact, there are several natural hypergraph classes that are quite dense, but have minimum co-degree equal to zero (multipartite hypergraphs, for instance), and hence the previous theorems are not immediately applicable. This motivates the notion of the
minimum supported co-degree
of a
k
-graph
G
, which we denote
\delta^{*}(G)
, and define as the maximum integer
d
such that every
(k-1)
-set that is contained in at least one edge is contained in at least
d
edges. A minimum supported co-degree requirement immediately handles the previous issues of zero co-degree sets, although it does not rule out isolated vertices, and hence we will usually impose the (fairly weak) requirement of all vertices being non-isolated.

Multiple recent studies have investigated questions that arise from replacing a minimum co-degree with a minimum supported co-degree requirement in classical extremal hypergraph theoretic problems. The notion of supported co-degrees was introduced by Balogh, Lemons and Palmer
[
2
]
(they use the term “positive co-degree” instead) to formulate a generalisation of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem, providing bounds on the sizes of intersecting families satisfying a minimum supported co-degree condition. Subsequently, Frankl and Wang
[
5
]
improved these bounds in almost all cases, and Spiro
[
41
]
has extended these studies to the more general case of
t
-intersecting families. Other papers have worked on generalisations of the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem
[
27
]
and unique colourability of hypergraphs
[
26
]
.

Halfpap, Lemons and Palmer
[
11
]
investigated variants of hypergraph Turán problems, and established the asymptotic supported co-degree threshold for a host
3
-graph to contain a copy of a fixed graph
F
for several distinct
3
-graphs
F
, and Wu
[
42
]
has consequently addressed this question for other examples. Halfpap, Lemons and Palmer
[
11
]
also study “jumps” in this threshold, which has further been tackled by Balogh, Halfpap, Lidický, and Palmer
[
1
]
. Pikhurko
[
33
]
recently proved that these problems are well-defined for a generalisation to the “minimum positive/supported
\ell
-co-degree” for any
\ell\in[k-1]
, providing analogous results to those Lo and Markström
[
28
]
regarding the usual notion of
\ell
-co-degree.

The question of finding minimum supported co-degree conditions that ensure the existence of spanning structures in hypergraphs was first raised by Halfpap and Magnan
[
12
]
. They prove an exact optimal lower bound on the minimum supported co-degree required to guarantee a perfect matching in
3
-graphs and a slightly weaker bound for all higher uniformities, which was later improved to an exact tight bound by Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
32
]
. Furthermore, Halfpap and Magnan also establish an exact best-possible minimum supported co-degree condition for Hamilton Berge cycles and an asymptotic one for
3
-uniform loose cycles. In a different direction, Illingworth, Lang, Müyesser, Parczyk and Sgueglia
[
15
]
showed that an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with
\delta^{*}(G)\geq n/2+o(n)
contains a spanning
k
-sphere, asymptotically confirming a conjecture of Georgakopoulos, Haslegrave, Montgomery and Narayanan
[
8
]
.

The problem of determining the optimal minimum supported co-degree for
k
-uniform
\ell
-cycles was initially suggested by Halfpap and Magnan in
[
12
, Section 5]
, and reiterated by Illingworth, Lang, Müyesser, Parczyk and Sgueglia
[
15
]
for the special case of tight cycles. This question was recently tackled by Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
]
, who prove an asymptotically optimal result for all
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
.

Our main result provides an improved sufficient minimum supported co-degree condition for
k
-uniform
\ell
-cycles for all
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
except
(k,\ell)=(3,1)
, which is the best possible for infinitely many values of
k
and
\ell
, and is off by at most
1
for all values of
k
and

. We discuss the optimality of our result in

. Importantly, we emphasise that our results are (essentially) exact and not asymptotic, a rather stark difference from the minimum co-degree version of the problem where very few cases have been resolved exactly.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
be such that
k\geq 3
and
(k,\ell)\neq(3,1)
, let
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
, and let
n
be sufficiently large and divisible by
k-\ell
.
Then every
k
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph without isolated vertices and having minimum supported co-degree at least
(1-1/t)n-(k-3)
will contain a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle.

This improves the aforementioned asymptotic results of Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
]
, who showed that
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n+o(n)
suffices. Our methods are largely different from theirs. Our analysis splits into two regimes: the extremal and non-extremal (see
Section

2.2
for details). The extremal regime does not feature in
[
31
]
at all, and for this we use ad-hoc structural analysis with lemmas about random matchings in bipartite graphs and about finding almost spanning subhypergraphs with large minimum supported codegree in almost complete hypergraphs. For the non-extremal case, while Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén rely on a version of the regularity lemma for hypergraphs (called the weak regularity lemma) along with the absorption method, our approach stems from Lang’s breakthrough work
[
23
]
on hypergraph tilings, which allows us to completely avoid the regularity lemma and greatly simplifies the use of the absorption method. For the latter part, however, we use a novel idea from
[
31
]
about weighted fractional matchings that, coupled with Farkas’ linear-algebraic lemma, allows one to find spanning
\ell
-cycles in certain
k
-partite
k
-graphs, which is one of the steps in our proof.

We point out that Illingworth, Lang, Müyesser, Parczyk and Sgueglia
[
15
]
conjectured that for all
k\geq 3
, any sufficiently large
k
-graph with
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/k)n
contains a tight Hamilton cycle, which is a special case of
1.1
(up to the
k-3
term). Observe that if
k
divides
n
, then a tight Hamilton cycle in

will contain a perfect matching. Thus, our result implies that
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/k)n-(k-3)
ensures a perfect matching, which recovers a result of Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
32
]
up to an additive constant of one, who show that a bound of
(1-1/k)n-(k-2)
suffices.

As alluded to above, the bound in
1.1
is off by one in most cases. In fact, in many cases (namely whenever

or when
\ell=k-1
, that is, we are seeking a tight Hamilton cycle), our techniques can be adapted to prove

with the improved bound of
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n-(k-2)
instead (which, for instance, would immediately imply the previously discussed optimal perfect matching bound).
However, since this will require some technical modifications, we choose to present a single unified proof for
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n-(k-3)
that will work for all cases instead. We point out the part of our proof that requires the exact degree condition in
13.3
, and briefly discuss how to suitably alter our proof and remove the extra one in the bound in all relevant cases in
Appendix

Finally, we remark that in a recent personal communication, Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén informed us that they are preparing a manuscript where they prove that any sufficiently large
3
-graph with no isolated vertices and
\delta^{*}(G)\geq n/2
contains a loose Hamilton cycle. This improves the previous asymptotic bound of

[

and Halfpap and Magnan

(which our methods can recover as well) and extends

2.1
Extremal lower bound constructions

We first discuss the optimality of the lower bound in
1.1
. To begin with, we say that a subset
U
of at most
k
vertices in a
k
-graph
G
is
supported
if there is an edge containing
U
. Furthermore, if
|U|\leq k-1
, set
d_{G}^{1}(U)
to be the number of vertices
v\in V(G)
such that
U\cup\{v\}
is supported. We say that a subset
A
of vertices is a
strong independent set
if every edge intersects
A
in at most one vertex.

We will provide three lower bound constructions. The first one will show that, at the very least, we need
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n-(k-2)
in
1.1
for any
k\geq 3
,
\ell\in[k-1]
and
n
which is divisible by
k-\ell
. The second one, which is a slight modification of the first, shows that if
k
is odd,
\ell=(k-1)/2
, and
n
satisfies some divisibility conditions, then we require
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n-(k-3)
, which is why our main result is essentially tight and is the strongest possible bound that applies to all relevant
k
,
\ell
and
n
. The final one will be an entirely different construction that applies only to the
(k,\ell)=(3,1)
case and shows that we need
\delta^{*}(G)\geq n/2
, which is in line with the second example.

Example 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
\ell\in[k-1]
and
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Let
n
be chosen such that
k-\ell
divides
n
. Now, consider an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
G
with a partition
A\sqcup B
of its vertex set such that
|A|=\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor+1
and
E(G)
consists of all
k
-sets
e\in\binom{V(G)}{k}
that satisfy
|e\cap A|\leq 1
(and so
A
is a strong independent set). Suppose
S
is any supported
(k-1)
-set. If
|S\cap A|=0
, then
S
supports all vertices outside itself, and so
d_{G}^{1}(S)=n-k+1
. If
|S\cap A|=1
, then it supports all vertices in
B\setminus S
, meaning that
d_{G}^{1}(S)=n-\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor-(k-2)
, and this is equal to
\delta^{*}(G)
since there is no supported set with
|S\cap A|\geq 2
. The following observation shows that
G
cannot contains a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle.

Observation 2.2
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-uniform
\ell
-cycle. Then the maximum size of a strong independent set in
H
is at most
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

As noted previously, since
H
is an
\ell
-cycle,
k-\ell
divides
n
. Recall that we can label the vertices in
H
as
v_{1}\dots v_{n}
such that
v_{i(k-\ell)+1}\dots v_{i(k-\ell)+k}
is an edge for all
i\geq 0
, with indices seen cyclically modulo
n
. Define the set of
segments
to be the collection of subsequences
\mathcal{S}=\{v_{i(k-\ell)+1}\dots v_{i(k-\ell)+t}:i\geq 0\}
and the set of
intervals

\mathcal{I}=\{v_{i(k-\ell)+1}\dots v_{(i+1)(k-\ell)}:i\geq 0\}
, where we view indices cyclically modulo
n
as usual. Let
X
be a strong independent set.

First, we observe that every segment contains exactly
t/(k-\ell)
intervals, and that every interval is part of
t/(k-\ell)
segments. Since the intervals partition
V(H)
, we see that every vertex is contained in
t/(k-\ell)
segments. The crucial observation is that, due to the definition of
\ell
-cycles and since trivially
t\leq k
, every segment is contained in an edge of
H
, and consequently every segment contains at most one vertex of
X
. Now, consider the set of vertex-segment pairs
\{(v,P):v\in X,P\in\mathcal{S},x\in P\}
. Since each vertex belongs to
t/(k-\ell)
segments, we see that the size of this set is exactly
t|X|/(k-\ell)
. However, as observed previously, each segment
P\in\mathcal{S}
can contain at most one vertex
v\in X
, and hence the number of such pairs is at most
|\mathcal{S}|=n/(k-\ell)
. Comparing the two shows
|X|\leq n/t
and hence
|X|\leq\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
.
∎

We remark that Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
, Section 1.4]
prove the same bound with this extremal construction, but we include our proof as it is simpler. We also wish to point out that Illingworth, Lang, Müyesser, Parczyk and Sgueglia
[
15
]
provide a version of this construction for tight cycles.

Example 2.3
.

Next, we provide a specialised modification of the previous example. Let
k\geq 3
be odd,
\ell=(k-1)/2
, and consequently
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)=(k+1)/2=\ell+1=k-\ell
. Let
n
be such that
t
divides
n
and
n/t+1
is even. Yet again, we consider an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
G
with a partition
A\sqcup B
of its vertex set with
|A|=n/t+1
. Write
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{|A|}\}
and let
E(G)
consist of all
e\in\binom{V(G)}{k}
such that
e\cap A\subseteq\{a_{2i-1},a_{2i}\}
for some
i\in[|A|/2]
(so the graph
\partial^{2}[A]
is a perfect matching). Clearly
|e\cap A|\leq 2
for all
e\in E(G)
. If
S
is any supported
(k-1)
-set, then, similar to the previous example, it is not too hard to see that

d_{G}^{1}(S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n-(k-1)&\textnormal{if}\ |e\cap A|=0,\\
|B|-(k-2)+1=|B|-(k-3)&\textnormal{if}\ |e\cap A|=1,\\
|B|-(k-3)&\textnormal{if}\ |e\cap A|=2,\\
\end{array}\right.

and so
\delta^{*}(G)=|B|-(k-3)=n-n/t-(k-2)
. We now argue that
G
cannot contain a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle, which shows that the bound in
1.1
cannot be improved.

To the contrary, suppose that there exists such a cycle
C=v_{1}\dots v_{n}
, and let
E(C)
denote the edges of
G
that are part of the cycle
C
. Every edge
e\in E(C)
shares
\ell=(k-1)/2
vertices with the preceding and succeeding edges of the cycle, and hence contains a unique vertex
v_{e}
at position
\ell+1=t
(the “middle” vertex) that is not part of any other edge of
E(C)
.
Let
C^{t}
denote the set of these vertices, so that
|C^{t}|=n/t
. Another important observation we require is that if
u\in A
and
uv_{1}\dots v_{k-1}\in E(G)
, then
vv_{1}\dots v_{k-1}\in E(G)
for any
v\in B\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\}
.

Consider any edge
e\in E(C)
such that
e\cap A\neq\emptyset
. If
v_{e}\in B
, then there exists some
u\in e\cap A
that is distinct from
v_{e}
. From our observation above, and since
e
is the only edge of
C
that contains
v_{e}
, the vertex sequence obtained by swapping

and
v_{e}
in the sequence corresponding to
C
still yields a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle. Hence, we may assume that
v_{e}\in A
for all
e\in E(C)
that intersect
A
. Next, suppose there exists some
u\in A\setminus C^{t}
. The discussion in the previous paragraph implies that are two edges
e,f\in E(C)
that contain
u
. By choice of
C
, as
A
intersects
e
and
f
, we know that
v_{e},v_{f}\in A
. Hence, we may conclude that
uv_{e}
and
uv_{f}
are both supported pairs due to the edges

and
f
respectively, meaning that

is a vertex of degree at least two in

, a contradiction. Thus, we see that
A\subseteq C^{t}
, which implies
|A|\leq|C^{t}|=n/t
, providing the desired contradiction.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose
k=3
and
\ell=1
, so that
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)=2
, and suppose
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex

H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, each on

and

have precisely two vertices in common, say

.

Suppose

. Then, since any two consecutive vertices are supported, we see that any

-uniform loose path must have odd order (since the first edge has three vertices and each consecutive edge adds two new vertices), and
|H_{i}|=n/2+1
is even, which is a contradiction.

We point out that the last example was brought to our attention by Richard Mycroft and Camila Zárate-Guerén, and we are thankful for that.

2.2
Proof of the main result using two key theorems

We prove our main result,
1.1
, by splitting it into two disjoint and complementary cases. Roughly speaking, we handle hypergraphs that are structurally “near-extremal” and “non-extremal” separately. We classify hypergraphs into one of these two types based on the observation that both extremal examples contain a large “sparse” set of size roughly
n/t
with very few supported pairs.

The first theorem treats
k
-graphs that are far from extremal, that is, where every set of size at least
n/t
induces several supported pairs. Note that here we allow for a minimum supported co-degree which is slightly lower than the exact bound given in
1.1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
be such that
(k,\ell)\neq(3,1)
, and set
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\mu\ll 1/k
such that
n
is divisible by
k-\ell
. If
G
is an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with no isolated vertices and
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
such that every set of
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
vertices of
G
contains at least
\mu n^{2}
supported pairs, then
G
has a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle.

The second theorem deals with
k
-graphs that are close to the two extremal examples above, namely where there is a set of size
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
which contains few supported pairs. This result requires the exact bound on the minimum supported co-degree.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
be such that
(k,\ell)\neq(3,1)
, and set
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
.
Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll 1/k\leq 1/3
be such that
n
is divisible by
k-\ell
.
Suppose
G
is an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with no isolated vertices and
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t)n-(k-3)
. If there is a subset

with

that contains at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
supported pairs, then

has a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle.

Notice that the proof of

follows immediately from

and

For the most part, the proofs of the two theorems use separate arguments and tools. In the rest of the section, we provide short overviews for the proofs of

and

.

2.3
Proof overviews

2.3.1
Proof overview for
2.5

In this section, we provide a brief overview of the key ideas we use to prove

, and include a more detailed proof sketch in
Section

4.1
.

The approach we use is built on a blow-up tiling technique introduced by Lang
[
23
]
, and extended in
[
15
,
24
]
(we make precise what we mean by a blow-up in
Section

4.2
). Our “blow-up tiling lemma”, namely

, allows us to tile

with almost balanced blow-ups of smaller
k
-graphs that obey approximate versions of the degree condition and the non-extremal structure of
G
. These tiles will have a special cyclic linkage property in terms of common edges, which will then reduce our problem to finding a Hamilton
\ell
-path within each blown up tile, because these can be “chained together” to form a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle in
G
.

Hence, given any “non-extremal”
F
and a sufficiently large nearly balanced blow-up
F^{*}
, we want a Hamilton
\ell
-path in
F^{*}
. We will first partition
F^{*}
into complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs with carefully selected part sizes to ensure each has an almost spanning
\ell
-path. These
k
-partite
k
-graphs will be formed by splitting up the complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs corresponding to the blow-ups of edges of
F
. We determine how to split up these
k
-graphs based on edge weights provided by a perfect fractional matching (defined in
Section

7
) in
F
. Technically, we work with a “vertex weighted” version of fractional matchings due to Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
]
, which is crucial for
\ell
-cycles that are not necessarily tight.

Finally, we use the minimum supported co-degree condition to find a path in
F^{*}
(obtained by blowing up a suitable walk in

) that can be used to link together the nearly spanning
\ell
-paths found in the aforementioned
k
-partite subgraphs and absorb a few uncovered vertices.

2.3.2
Proof overview for
2.6

We provide a short overview of our proof strategy for
2.6
for the special case of tight cycles (which is what motivates the general strategy) and include a more detailed sketch including
\ell
-cycles in Section
10.1
.
Suppose
G
is an “extremal”
n
-vertex
k
-graph, meaning it contains a set

of size roughly
n/k
(since

for tight cycles) within which there are few supported pairs. Let

.
2.1
suggests that a tight Hamilton cycle has a strong independent set of size

, whose vertices are exactly the
k
th vertices along the cycle. We build a Hamilton cycle in

based on this observation.

We first show that we can transfer a few vertices across

and

to ensure high co-degrees for supported sets across the partition while maintaining
|A|\approx n/k
. We then replace a few vertices

with short tight paths, and this will recover
|B|=(k-1)|A|
.

We then partition
B
into
k-1
sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{k-1}
such that
|A|=|B_{i}|
for all

. We define an auxiliary
k
-partite

-graph

with parts

whose edges correspond to vertex sequences that form tight paths, and randomly construct a perfect matching

can be connected into a tight Hamilton cycle in

Preliminary results

First, we mention a few quick facts about

Observation 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
\ell\in[k-1]
, and
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Then
\ell+1\leq t\leq k
,

, and

Proof.

We prove the claims sequentially. For the first one, we see that

t>\left(\frac{k}{k-\ell}-1\right)(k-\ell)=\ell,

and the fact that

is immediate from the definition.

We prove the second claim in two cases. If

The final claim is easy since

requires at least one of

and

to be equal to one and the other to be equal to either one or two depending on the desired value of

for

and

otherwise.
∎

Next, we show a quick result about certain vertices in

-paths that will be required for both the extremal and non-extremal cases. Intuitively speaking, these vertices form the “sparse” set of size

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
\ell\in[k-1]
and
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Suppose

is a
k

is a vertex sequence, where

is divisible by

. Let

The edges of

4
Non-extremal hypergraphs

We will first prove
2.6
(the non-extremal case) across the next six sections. In this section, we first provide a detailed proof sketch, and then briefly describe the principal results and techniques that we utilise. We finally state three key lemmas –
4.1

and

4.1
Proof sketch for
2.5

Our goal is to show every
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
has a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle. For the sake of clarity, we only sketch the proof for tight cycles (
\ell=k-1
, in which case
t=k
), and briefly discuss the necessary modifications for
\ell
-cycles at the end. Our proof has three main steps

Tiling.

As mentioned in
Section

2.3.1
, the main lemma behind our strategy, namely
4.1
, provides a tiling of
G
with almost balanced blow-ups (defined in
Section

4.2
) of smaller
k
-graphs that obey (almost) the same minimum supported co-degree condition as
G
and also have an approximate version of the non-extremal structure of
G
. Moreover, these blown-up tiles will be linked in a cyclic fashion, where each tile shares a common edge with the previous and next tile in the chain, and is disjoint from the rest. Consequently, within each blown-up tile, we find a tight Hamilton path that starts at the edge shared with the previous tile, and ends at the edge shared with the next tile. The tight Hamilton paths in consecutive tiles can then be “linked together”, and repeating this process along the chain of cyclically linked tiles forms a tight Hamilton cycle in
G
.

Blowing up a fractional matching.

Thus (morally speaking), our problem is reduced to finding a tight Hamilton path in a
k
-graph
F^{*}
that is an almost balanced blow-up of some
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,k-1)
, with the start and end edges of the path specified a priori. Our strategy starts by (nearly) partitioning
F^{*}
into a collection of balanced complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs (which will be suitable subgraphs of the
k
-graphs formed by blow-ups of edges of
F
) that cover almost all of
F^{*}
. To this end, we first find a perfect fractional matching in
F
, which is a set of non-negative edge weights such that the total weight on each vertex is precisely one. We do this via Farkas’ lemma, a standard linear algebraic technique. We do not describe how we use it to find the perfect fractional matching here as this part is fairly disjoint from the rest of the proof, we just mention that we make use of both the degree condition and the non-extremal structure of
F
. Now, for each vertex
v\in V(F)
, let
B_{v}\subseteq V(F^{*})
denote the blown-up part corresponding to
v
. We partition each
B_{v}
into parts corresponding to each edge of
e\in E(F)
that contains
v
such that the proportion of
B_{v}
covered by this part
B_{v}^{e}
is given by the weight of the edge
e
in the fractional matching. Thus, we end up with a subgraph
T_{e}
(whose parts are the sets
B_{v}^{e}
for all vertices
v\in e
) of the original
k
-partite
k
-graph formed by the blow-up of
e
. All the parts of
T_{e}
will have the same size (governed by the weight of
e
), and it is easy to see
T_{e}
has a spanning tight cycle. Ideally, since the fractional matching is perfect, these disjoint subgraphs will span all the vertices of
G
. However, we may end up having to round down the sizes of these parts
B_{v}^{e}
to integer values, meaning that we lose some vertices and consequently the
k
-graphs
\{T_{e}\}_{e\in E(F)}
only cover almost all the vertices of
G
.

Connection and absorption.

Finally, we find a tight path in
F^{*}
that can be used to link up the spanning tight paths found in the aforementioned
k
-partite subgraphs, and can also “absorb” into itself the remaining few uncovered vertices (technically, we first find such a path and then apply the strategy outlined in the previous paragraph to the leftover). In order to build such a tight path
P
, we will heavily rely on the fact that
\delta^{*}(F)>n/2
(which will hold even in the
\ell
-cycle case as
(k,\ell)\neq(3,1)
implies
t\geq 3
). Since
\delta^{*}(F)>n/2
, it is easy to argue that we can find a tight walk
W
in
F
that contains (several copies of) every edge of
F
. We can also specify the start and end edges, which, as discussed, is important to our proof. This is blown up to a tight path
P
in
F^{*}
by walking along
W
and picking a new copy of a vertex whenever we see a repeated vertex, which is possible if the blow-up is sufficiently large relative to
|F|
. We then show that we can replace any segment of
P
formed by a blow-up of an edge
e
by a spanning path in the
k
-graph
T_{e}
, which allows us to connect the necessary tight paths. All that is left is to imbue
W
, and consequently
P
, with some absorption features. Given a vertex
v\in V(F)
, the “simple absorber” we use will consist of two supported sets
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
such that both
W_{1}W_{2}
and
W_{1}vW_{2}
are tight paths. In a manner similar to what we describe above, we can exploit the connectivity property due to the degree condition to ensure that the tight walk
W
, which contains every edge of
F
as a subwalk, also has several absorber subwalks
W_{1}W_{2}
for each vertex
v
. The blown-up path
P
in
F^{*}
then retains these absorption attributes, allowing it to incorporate into itself the few unused vertices in
F^{*}
.

Adjustments for
\ell
-cycles.

The broad proof structure for
\ell
-cycles remains the same, but the details need more care. To begin with, the complete
k
-partite subgraphs
T_{e}
will now be modified to have carefully chosen part sizes (described in
8.2
) that will ensure a spanning
\ell
-cycle instead. As mentioned in
Section

2.3.1
, this is done using a vertex-weighted generalisation of fractional matchings introduced by Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
]
, which will lead to the “correct” imbalance in the parts of

. The connectivity aspect of the proof remains fairly similar. In fact, we usually form the required
\ell
-walks by building tight walks whose orders satisfy specific divisibility conditions such that the same vertex sequences will also correspond to
\ell
-walks and
\ell
-paths (see
8.1
). Additionally, while the starting point for the absorbers we use for

Let us set up the chain of parameters

Basic tools

In this section we prove a few preliminary results that we will use later.
Recall that
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
is the family of
n
-vertex graphs with minimum positive co-degree at least
(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
where every
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
vertices span at least
\mu n^{2}
supported pairs. To begin with, we prove an approximate alternate description for
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
that we use when proving
4.2
. The aim of
4.2
is to show that, roughly speaking, the properties of graphs in
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
are preserved under taking a random subset of vertices
S
. A naive approach to guaranteeing that the non-extremal properties are preserved takes a union bound over all subsets of
S
of size
|S|/t
, which would not yield an effective bound. Instead, using also the minimum supported co-degree condition, we show that it suffices to guarantee that non-neighbourhoods of vertices with vertex co-degree close to
(1-1/t)n
have many supported pairs. This is formalised in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
be integers,
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
, and let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\mu^{\prime}\ll\mu\ll 1/k
.

(i)

Suppose
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
. Then for all
v\in V(G)
, either
d^{1}(v)\geq(1-1/t+\mu^{\prime})n
or
V(G)\setminus N^{1}(v)
contains at least
\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
supported pairs.

(ii)

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with no isolated vertices and
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
. Suppose that for all
v\in V(G)
, either
d^{1}(v)\geq(1-1/t+3\mu^{\prime}t)n
or
V(G)\setminus N^{1}(v)
contains at least
\mu n^{2}
supported pairs. Then every set of
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
vertices in
G
contains at least
\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
supported pairs.

Proof.

Item
(i)
is fairly simple to prove. Suppose there exists some
v\in V(G)
such that
d^{1}(v)\leq(1-1/t+\mu^{\prime})n
, and hence
|V(G)\setminus N_{G}^{1}(v)|\geq n/t-\mu^{\prime}n
. Let
A
be any set of the form
(V(G)\setminus N_{G}^{1}(v))\sqcup B
, where
B
is an arbitrary set of distinct vertices from
N_{G}^{1}(v)
such that
|B|
is the smallest possible value required to make
|A|\geq\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
; then
|B|\leq\mu^{\prime}n
(note that
B
is potentially empty). Since
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
, the set
A
contains at least
\mu n^{2}
supported pairs. As
|B|\leq\mu^{\prime}n
, at most
\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
of these supported pairs have a vertex in
B
, and thus at least
(\mu-\mu^{\prime})n^{2}\geq\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
lie entirely within
V(G)\setminus N_{G}^{1}(v)
.

Now for
(ii)
. Suppose there exists some
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\geq\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor\geq n/t-1
that contains less than
\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
supported pairs. Then, by the handshaking lemma on the graph of supported pairs in
U
, there exists some
v\in U
such that
d_{U}^{1}(v)\leq 3\mu^{\prime}tn
. Since
|V(G)\setminus U|\leq n-n/t
, we see that
d_{G}^{1}(v)\leq(1-1/t+3\mu^{\prime}t)n
, and hence, by assumption on
G
, the set
T=V(G)\setminus N_{G}^{1}(v)
contains at least
\mu n^{2}
supported pairs. Since
G
has no isolated vertices and
\delta^{*}(G)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
, we know that
d_{G}^{1}(v)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
, and hence
|T|\leq n/t+\varepsilon n
. By our choice of
v
, we have
|T\cap U|\geq|U|-3\mu^{\prime}tn\geq n/t-4\mu^{\prime}tn
, and consequently
|T\setminus U|\leq n/t+\varepsilon n-(n/t-4\mu^{\prime}tn)\leq 8\mu^{\prime}tn
. Hence, the number of supported pairs in
T
which contain at least one vertex outside
U
is at most
8\mu^{\prime}tn^{2}
.
Consequently,
T\cap U\subseteq U
contains at least
(\mu-8\mu^{\prime}t)n^{2}\geq\mu^{\prime}n^{2}
supported pairs, a contradiction to our assumption on
U
.
∎

In a similar vein, but somewhat in the “opposite direction”, we also show that taking a blow-up approximately preserves non-extremal structure.
Here we use the alternative characterisation of non-extremal graphs given by the previous proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
0\leq\gamma\ll\varepsilon\ll\mu^{\prime}\ll\mu\ll 1/k\leq 1/3
, let
m,s\geq 1
be integers, and
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Suppose
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(s,k,\ell)
and
F^{*}
is a
(\gamma,m)
-regular blow-up of
F
. Then
F^{*}\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{2\varepsilon,\mu^{\prime}}(|F^{*}|,k,\ell)
.

Proof.

Set
|F^{*}|=N
. By the definition of
F^{*}
, it is straightforward to see that
(1-\gamma)ms\leq N\leq(1+\gamma)ms
.
First, notice that since
F
has no isolated vertices, neither does
F^{*}
.
Second, since
\delta^{*}(F)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)s
and
F^{*}
is a
(\gamma,m)
-regular blow-up of
F
, we see that

\displaystyle\delta^{*}(F^{*})\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}-\varepsilon\right)(1-\gamma)ms\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}-2\varepsilon\right)(1+\gamma)ms\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}-2\varepsilon\right)N,

using
\gamma\ll\varepsilon
.

Finally, we need to show that any subset of
V(F^{*})
of size at least
N/t
contains at least
\mu^{\prime}N^{2}
supported pairs. We do this via
5.1
. Fix
\eta
such that
\mu^{\prime}\ll\eta\ll\mu
. Let
w\in V(F^{*})
be arbitrary, and let
\phi(w)=v\in V(F)
(recall that
\phi:V(F^{*})\to V(F)
maps all copies in
F^{*}
of a vertex
v
in
F
to
v
). Since
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(s,k,\ell)
, by
5.1

(i)
, we know that either
d^{1}(v)\geq(1-1/t+\eta)s
or
V(F)\setminus N^{1}(v)
contains at least
\eta s^{2}
supported pairs.
If the former is true, then since every vertex neighbour of
v
is blown up to at least
(1-\gamma)m
vertices in
F^{*}
, we have

d^{1}_{F^{*}}(w)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}+\eta\right)(1-\gamma)ms\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}+3\mu^{\prime}t\right)(1+\gamma)ms\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{t}+3\mu^{\prime}t\right)N,

where we use
N\leq(1+\gamma)ms
and
\mu^{\prime}\ll\eta\ll 1/t
.
In the other case, we conclude that the number of supported pairs in
V(F^{*})\setminus N^{1}(w)
is at least

\eta s^{2}\cdot(1-\gamma)^{2}m^{2}\geq\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}(1+\gamma)^{2}m^{2}s^{2}\geq\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}N^{2}.

Since
\mu^{\prime}\ll\eta^{2}
,
5.1

(ii)
implies that every set of at least
N/t
vertices contains at least
\mu^{\prime}N^{2}
supported pairs, as desired.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

4.2
.

Write
W=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{r}\}
, where

. Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{s-r}
be

picked independently and uniformly at random (with repetition). Set

for

and
x_{i}=y_{i-r}
for

and recall that

is a randomly uniform set of
s

that contains

. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the event that
{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}}
are distinct. Let
\mathcal{B}_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{c}}]\leq\frac{s^{2}}{n}=o(1).

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}^{\mathsf{c}}_{1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}^{\mathsf{c}}_{2}\mid\mathcal{A}]=\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}^{\mathsf{c}}_{2}\cap\mathcal{A}]}{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}]}\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}^{\mathsf{c}}_{2}](1+o(1)).

.

Let
H=G[T]
and fix parameters

such that
\mu^{\prime}\ll\mu_{1}\ll\mu_{2}\ll\mu
. We define the following two events.

•

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{\frac{d^{1}_{H}(U)}{s}\geq\frac{d^{1}_{G}(U)}{n}-\varepsilon\textnormal{ for every }U\subseteq T\text{ with }|U|\leq k-1\text{ which is supported in $G$}\}
,

•

\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{\text{if $x\in T$ satisfies $d_{G}^{1}(x)\leq(1-1/t+\mu_{2})n$ then $T\setminus N_{H}^{1}(x)$ has at least }\mu_{1}s^{2}\ \text{supported}\allowbreak\text{pairs}\}
.

First, we will argue that

implies
\mathcal{B}_{2}
. It will subsequently suffice to show that each

occurs with high probability, and then use a union bound.

Indeed, from
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and from
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
, we know that
H
has no isolated vertices and that
\delta^{*}(H)\geq(1-1/t-2\varepsilon)s
. Now, consider any
x\in V(H)
such that
d_{H}^{1}(x)\leq(1-1/t+3\mu^{\prime}t)s
. From
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, we see that
d_{G}^{1}(x)\leq(1-1/t+3\mu^{\prime}t+\varepsilon)n\leq(1-1/t+\mu_{2})n
, and from
\mathcal{C}_{2}
we conclude that
V(H)\setminus N^{1}_{H}(x)
contains at least
\mu_{1}s^{2}
supported pairs. Then by
5.1

(ii)
, we know that every
\left\lfloor s/t\right\rfloor
-subset in
V(H)
has at least
\mu^{\prime}s^{2}
supported pairs. Thus, overall, we have
H\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{2\varepsilon,\mu^{\prime}}(s,k,\ell)
.

Let us start with
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Let
I\subseteq[s]
be any subset such that
|I|\in[k-1]
. Define the random variables
X_{I}=\{x_{i}:i\in I\}
and
d_{I}=|N^{1}_{H}(X_{I})\cap T_{0}|
. Consider the event

\mathcal{E}_{I}=\{X_{I}\textnormal{ is supported in $G$ and }d_{H}^{1}(X_{I})/s\leq d_{G}^{1}(X_{I})/n-\varepsilon\}.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{I}=\{U\subseteq V(G):|U|\leq|I|,\mathbb{P}[X_{I}=U]>0\}
.
Then, observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subseteq V(G)\\
|U|\leq|I|\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}\cap\{X_{I}=U\}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{I}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}\mid X_{I}=U]\cdot\mathbb{P}[X_{I}=U].

In particular, to show that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
, we may consider any arbitrary
U\in\mathcal{U}_{I}
, condition on
X_{I}=U
, and argue that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}\mid X_{I}=U]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
for any such choice of
U
.

If
U
is not supported in
G
, then
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}\mid X_{I}=U]=0
, and hence we may assume that it is supported. We may lower bound the expected value of
d_{I}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}d_{I}

\displaystyle=d^{1}_{G}(U)\cdot\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{s-|U\cup W|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-3k)\cdot\delta^{*}(G)}{2n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{s-3k}{2}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{t}-\varepsilon\right)\geq\frac{s}{4},

using
t\geq 3
and
k\ll s
.
Notice that changing the outcome of any single
x_{i}
changes
d_{I}
by at most
1
, and so by McDiarmid’s inequality (
3.4
),

\mathbb{P}\left[d_{I}-\mathbb{E}d_{I}\leq-\frac{\varepsilon\mathbb{E}d_{I}}{2}\right]\leq 2\exp\left(\frac{-2\varepsilon^{2}(\mathbb{E}d_{I})^{2}}{s}\right)=e^{-\Omega(s)}.

Thus, since
d_{H}(X_{I})\geq d_{I}
, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(s)}
, we have

\frac{d_{H}(X_{I})}{s}\geq\frac{d_{I}}{s}\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon/2}{s}\cdot(s-r)\cdot\frac{d}{n}\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{k}{s}\right)\cdot\frac{d}{n}\geq\frac{d}{n}-\varepsilon,

where we use that
k/s\ll\varepsilon
.
Hence, as argued previously, we now have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{I}]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
for every
I\subseteq[s]
with
|I|\in[k-1]
. Taking a union bound over all possibilities of
I
, we see that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{C}_{1}]\geq 1-(k-1)\binom{s}{k-1}e^{-\Omega(s)}\geq 1-\frac{1}{4s^{2}}
.

Next, we will show that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
occurs with high probability. For each
i\in[s]
, define the event

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{T\setminus N^{1}_{H}(x_{i})\textnormal{ has less than }\mu_{1}s^{2}\textnormal{ supported pairs and }d_{G}^{1}(x_{i})<(1-1/t+\mu_{2})n\}.

Fix any integer
i\in[r+1,s]
. Similar to our previous calculations, we note that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\{x_{i}=u\}]=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\mid x_{i}=u]\cdot\mathbb{P}[x_{i}=u].

Consider any arbitrary
u\in V(G)
. We will argue that, for each fixed
i\in[r+1,s]
, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\mid x_{i}=u]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
, and then the above equation then guarantees that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
. We comment at the end of the proof on how to handle
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for
i\in[r]
.

If
d_{G}^{1}(u)\geq(1-1/t+\mu_{2})n
, then
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\mid x_{i}=u]=0
, and we are done.
Hence, we may suppose
d_{G}^{1}(u)\leq(1-1/t+\mu_{2})n
. Since
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
, by
5.1

(i)
, there must be at least
\mu_{2}n^{2}
supported pairs in
V(G)\setminus N_{G}^{1}(u)
. Let us call this set of supported pairs
P
. From
5.3
, any supported pair
w_{1}w_{2}\in P
satisfies

d_{G}(w_{1}w_{2})\geq\frac{\delta^{*}(G)^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{*}(G)}{k-2}\right)^{k-2}\geq\left(\frac{n-n/t-\varepsilon n}{k-2}\right)^{k-2}\geq\left(\frac{n}{3k}\right)^{k-2}

since
t\geq 2
. Let
E_{P}
be the set of all edges of
G
containing at least one pair of
P
. We can bound the number of such edges from below as

\displaystyle|E_{P}|\geq\frac{1}{\binom{k}{2}}\sum_{w_{1}w_{2}\in P}d_{G}(w_{1}w_{2})\geq\frac{2\mu_{2}}{(3k)^{k-2}\cdot k^{2}}\cdot n^{k}\geq\frac{2\mu_{2}}{(3k)^{k}}\cdot n^{k}.

For each edge
e\in E_{P}
, the probability that
e\in E(H[T_{0}\setminus\{x_{i}\})
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcup_{J\subseteq[r+1,s]\setminus\{i\},\,|J|=k}\left\{\{x_{j}:j\in J\}=e\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[r+1,s]\setminus\{i\},\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[\{x_{j}:j\in J\}=e]-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J,J^{\prime}\subseteq[r+1,s]\setminus\{i\},\\
|J|=|J^{\prime}|=k,J\neq J^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[\{x_{j}:j\in J\}=\{x_{j^{\prime}}:j^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}\}=e]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(s-r-1)(s-r-2)\ldots(s-r-k)}{n^{k}}-\frac{\big((s-r-1)(s-r-2)\ldots(s-r-k)\big)^{2}}{n^{k+1}}\geq\frac{s^{k}}{2n^{k}},

where we used that if
J,J^{\prime}
are two distinct sets of size
k
then
|J\cup J^{\prime}|\geq k+1
.
We thus obtain the following lower bound on
Z:=|E_{P}\cap E(H[T_{0}\setminus\{x_{i}\}])|
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in E_{P}}\mathbb{P}\big[e\in E(H[T_{0}\setminus\{x_{i}\}])\big]\geq\frac{\mu_{2}}{(3k)^{k}}\cdot s^{k}=\beta\mu_{2}s^{k}.

where
\beta=1/(3k)^{k}
is a function of
k
. Next, observe that if a single vertex in
T_{0}\setminus\{x_{i}\}
is replaced by an arbitrary vertex of
V(G)
, then the number of edges of
E_{P}
in
H[T_{0}\setminus\{x_{i}\}]
changes by at most
s^{k-1}
since this bounds the maximum degree of a vertex in
H
. Using McDiarmid’s inequality (
3.4
), we have

\mathbb{P}[Z-\mathbb{E}Z\leq-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}Z]\leq 2\exp\left(\frac{-\beta^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}s^{2k}}{2\cdot s\cdot s^{2k-2}}\right)\leq\exp(-\Omega(s)).

It readily follows that
Z\geq\beta\mu_{2}s^{k}/2
with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(s)}
.
Each edge of
E_{P}
counted in
Z
contains at least one pair of
P
, and each pair of
P
is counted at most
s^{k-2}
times. Hence, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(s)}
, there are at least
\beta\mu_{2}s^{2}/2\geq\mu_{1}s^{2}
supported pairs of
P
in
H
. By choice, all of these lie outside
N_{H}^{1}(x_{i})=N_{H}^{1}(u)
.

Thus, as noted initially, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]\leq e^{-\Omega(s)}
for all
i\in[r+1,s]
. Finally, if
i\in[r]
, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\{x_{i}=u\}]=0
for all
u\neq w_{i}
, which means that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{F}_{i}\mid x_{i}=w_{i}]
, and hence we can perform the same calculations as above with
u=w_{i}
. Additionally, here we will instead have
Z=E_{P}\cap E(H[T_{0}])
, but the bounds are identical anyway.
Hence, by taking a union bound over all
i\in[s]
, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{C}_{2}]\geq 1-se^{-\Omega(s)}\geq 1-\frac{1}{4s^{2}}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}
implies
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, we use a union bound and see that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}_{2}^{\mathsf{c}}]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathsf{c}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\mathsf{c}}]\leq\frac{1}{2s^{2}},

and thus
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{B}_{2}]\geq 1-1/2s^{2}
, as required.
∎

7
Perfect weighted fractional matchings

Our overall approach will rely on finding a large, nearly perfect matching in a regular blow-up
F^{*}
of some
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(n,k,\ell)
, and finding a way to connect the edges of this matching via an
\ell
-path. Towards that end, we make use of a recently introduced idea of Mycroft and Zárate-Guerén
[
31
]
that they call
perfect weighted fractional matchings
, and we borrow their definitions and notation.

To begin with, we define a
fractional matching
in a
k
-graph
H
to be a weight function that maps the edges of
H
to the unit interval, say
q:E(H)\to[0,1]
, such that for every vertex
v\in V(H)
the sum of
q(e)
for all edges
e
containing
v
is at most
1
, that is,

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E(H)\\
e\ni v\end{subarray}}q(e)\leq 1.

We say that
q
is a
perfect fractional matching
if this sum is precisely
1
for every vertex
v\in V(H)
. Note that a (perfect) matching is a special type of a (perfect) fractional matching where the edge weights are restricted to the set
\{0,1\}
.

Now, suppose we have
k
weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in[0,1]
. Then a
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
-fractional matching
is similar to a fractional matching
q
, except that any edge
e
places weight
w_{i}q(e)
on its
i
th vertex. Of course, we now need to consider edges with a pre-defined vertex ordering. Hence, instead, we will simply consider all possible permutations of the vertices of each edge.

Recall from
Section

3.1
that, for a given a
k
-graph
H
, we let
E^{*}(H)
denote the set of ordered edges of
H
. For every vertex
v\in V(H)
and
i\in[k]
, we write
E_{i}^{v}(H)\subseteq E^{*}(H)
for the set of all ordered edges with
v
as the
i
th vertex. Fix
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\geq 0}
. A
\mathbf{w}
-weighted fractional matching
is a weight function
q:E^{*}(H)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
q(e)\geq 0
for all
e\in E^{*}(H)
, and

\sum\limits_{i\in[k]}\sum\limits_{e\in E_{i}^{v}(H)}w_{i}\cdot q(e)\leq 1

for every vertex
v\in V(H)
. We call it
perfect
if we have equality in the above inequality for all
v\in V(H)
. Observe that a (perfect) fractional matching is simply a (perfect)
(1,\dots,1)
-weighted fractional matching. Following
[
31
]
, we will focus on perfect weighted fractional matchings with weights
\mathbf{w}^{*}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
, defined by

w_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k-1&i=1\\
t-1&2\leq i\leq k,\end{array}\right.

where

t:=t(k,\ell)=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell).

The reason for this particular choice of weights is that any complete
k
-uniform
k
-partite hypergraph with parts
A,B_{1},\ldots,B_{k-1}
, such that
|A|/(k-1)=|B_{i}|/(t-1)
for every
i\in[k-1]
(and an additional divisibility condition holds), has a spanning
\ell
-cycle (see
8.2
and Proposition 1.8 in
[
31
]
). This means that if an ordered edge
e=v_{1}\ldots v_{k}
receives some weight
q(e)
, then there is an
\ell
-cycle that we can use cover almost all the vertices in the blow-up of
e
where
v_{i}
is blown-up by
w_{i}\left\lfloor q(e)\right\rfloor
vertices, for
i\in[k]
, a key step in our proof.

A convenient and often elegant method to find perfect fractional matchings is using Farkas’ lemma, a linear algebraic result that is quite handy for this purpose – see
[
18
,
36
,
29
]
for examples. In order to state the lemma, we introduce some simple notation. Given a vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\mathbf{v}\geq a
to mean that
v_{i}\geq a
for all
i\in[n]
. We use analogous notation for
\mathbf{v}\leq a
,
\mathbf{v}>a
and
\mathbf{v}<a
. Finally, we use
\mathbf{1}
to denote the all ones vector.

Lemma 7.1

(Farkas’ lemma)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then either there exists
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{x}\geq 0
, or there exists
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{y}\geq 0
and
\mathbf{b}^{\top}\mathbf{y}<0
.

Using this, we can show that non-extremal
k
-graphs have a
w^{*}
-weighted perfect fractional matching.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
\ell\in[k-1]
be integers,
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
, and let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll 1/k
, where
n
is divisible by
t
. Let
\mathbf{w}^{*}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
where
w_{1}=k-1
and
w_{i}=t-1
for all
2\leq i\leq k
. Then every
H\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,4\varepsilon}(n,k,\ell)
has a
\mathbf{w}^{*}
-weighted perfect fractional matching.

Proof.

Let
V(H)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. For each edge
e\in E(H)
and each
v_{i}\in e
, define
\chi_{i}(e)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be the vector with
i
th coordinate
k-1
,
j
th coordinate
t-1
for all
j
such that
v_{j}\in e\setminus\{v_{i}\}
and all other coordinates
0
. Let
\mathcal{X}=\{\chi_{i}(e):e\in E(H),v_{i}\in e\}
.

Suppose
H
does not have a
\mathbf{w}^{*}
-weighted perfect fractional matching. This means that there is no set of weights
\{q(\mathbf{x}):q(\mathbf{x})\geq 0,\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\}
which satisfies
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}q(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}
, for otherwise, defining for each edge
e=\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}
and
j\in[k]
,

q^{\prime}(v_{i_{j}}v_{i_{1}}\ldots v_{i_{j-1}}v_{i_{j+1}}\ldots v_{i_{k}})=q(\chi_{i_{j}}(e)),

(and
q^{\prime}(e)=0
for all other ordered edges corresponding to
e
), would constitute a
\mathbf{w}^{*}
-weighted perfect fractional matching.
Hence, by
7.1
, there exists
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{1}<0
and
\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}\geq 0
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Let
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
. Then
\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{1}=\sum y_{i}<0
. By potentially reordering the vertices, we may assume without loss of generality that
y_{i}\leq y_{j}
for all
i\leq j
. Let
r
be the smallest index such that
v_{r}\in N^{1}(v_{1})
, that is,
v_{1}v_{r}
is a supported pair. Since
H\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,4\varepsilon}(n,k,\ell)
, we know that
\delta^{*}(H)\geq(1-1/t-\varepsilon)n
, and hence
r\leq n/t+\varepsilon n+1\leq n/t+2\varepsilon n
.

Overall, we hope to obtain a contradiction by finding a “low-weight” edge of
H
, say
e=v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{k}}
, which satisfies the following two key properties.

•

\frac{n}{t}\cdot\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot((k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}})\leq y_{1}+\dots+y_{2n/t}
, and

•

i_{j}\leq 2n/t+1
for all
j\geq 3
.

Indeed, given any such edge
e
, we let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the vector with
x_{i_{1}}=k-1
,
x_{i_{j}}=t-1
for all
2\leq j\leq k-1
, and zero otherwise. Then clearly
\mathbf{x}=\chi_{i_{1}}(e)\in\mathcal{X}
. Consequently, we obtain a contradiction as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{t}\cdot\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{t}\cdot\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\left((k-1)y_{i_{1}}+\sum\limits_{j\geq 2}(t-1)y_{i_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{t}\cdot\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot\big((k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}}\big)+\frac{n}{t}\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{j\geq 3}\frac{t-2}{k-2}\cdot y_{i_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(y_{1}+\dots+y_{\frac{2n}{t}}\right)+\frac{n}{t}\cdot(t-2)\cdot y_{\frac{2n}{t}+1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(y_{1}+\dots+y_{\frac{2n}{t}}\right)+\left(y_{\frac{2n}{t}+1}+\dots+y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle<0,

where the penultimate inequality uses
2\leq t\leq k
and the first and final ones follow from the assumptions on
\mathbf{y}
.

Let us try to find such an
e
. First, suppose
r\leq n/t
. Set
i_{1}=1
and
i_{2}=r
. By repeatedly using the minimum supported co-degree condition, we can find vertices
v_{i_{3}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}
such that
v_{i_{j}}\in N^{1}(v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{j-1}})
and
i_{j}\leq n/t+\varepsilon n+1
for all
j\geq 3
. Observe that
e=v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{k}}\in E(G)
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n}{t}\cdot\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot((k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{t}\left(\frac{(t-2)(k-1)}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot y_{1}+\frac{t-2}{k-2}\cdot y_{\frac{n}{t}+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{t}\left(y_{1}+y_{\frac{n}{t}+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(y_{1}+\dots+y_{\frac{n}{t}}\right)+\left(y_{\frac{n}{t}+1}+\dots+y_{\frac{2n}{t}}\right),

where we use
2\leq t\leq k
. Thus,
e
meets the required criteria.

Hence, we may assume that
n/t+1\leq r\leq n/t+2\varepsilon n
. Let
A=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
and
B=\{v_{r+1},\dots,v_{2n/t}\}
. Since
H\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{non-ext}}_{\varepsilon,4\varepsilon}(n,k,\ell)
and
|A|=r\geq n/t
, there are at least
4\varepsilon n^{2}
supported pairs in
A
. This implies that we can greedily find a matching
M_{1}
of supported pairs in
A
that contains
v_{1}v_{r}
and which spans at least
4\varepsilon n
vertices.

Now, we note that
|B|=2n/t-r
and
|A\setminus V(M_{1})|\leq r-4\varepsilon n
.
Since
r\leq n/t+2\varepsilon n
, we see that
|B|\geq|A\setminus V(M_{1})|
. Hence, we can pair up each vertex of
|A\setminus V(M_{1})|
with a distinct vertex of
B
arbitrarily (note that these vertex pairs need not be supported in
H
). Add this collection of pairs
M_{1}
and call the resulting set of vertex pairs
M_{2}
.

For our final step, we consider all vertices in
C=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{2n/t}\}\setminus V(M_{2})
. By construction every vertex in
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
is part of some pair in
M_{2}
. Thus, we may deduce that
C\subseteq\{v_{r+1},\dots,v_{2n/t}\}=B
. Since
|V(M_{2})|
is even, the same must be true of
|C|=2n/t-|V(M_{2})|
. Hence, we can arbitrarily pair up all vertices in
C
. We add this collection of vertex pairs to
M_{2}
, and let the resulting set be
M_{3}
. Since every vertex in
v_{1},\dots v_{2n/t}
is contained in exactly one pair of
M_{3}
, we have
|M_{3}|=n/t
.

Let
v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}
to be a supported pair in
M_{1}
such that
(k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}}\leq(k-1)y_{i}+(t-1)y_{j}
for all
v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{1}
with
i<j
. Since
v_{1}v_{r}\in M_{1}
and
i_{1}\geq 1
, we have
i_{2}\leq r
. By construction, for any pair
v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{3}\setminus M_{1}
with
i<j
, we have
v_{j}\in B
, and hence
j>r
. This implies

(k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}}\leq(k-1)y_{1}+(t-1)y_{r}\leq(k-1)y_{i}+(t-1)y_{j}

for all
v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{3}
, where the first inequality uses that
v_{1}v_{r}\in M_{1}
and the definition of
v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}
.

Since
v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}
is a supported pair, by repeatedly using the minimum supported co-degree condition, we can find an edge
v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}\dots v_{i_{k}}\in E(H)
such that

\displaystyle=\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\cdot|M_{3}|\cdot\left((k-1)y_{i_{1}}+(t-1)y_{i_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t-2}{(t-1)(k-2)}\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{3}}\left((k-1)y_{i}+(t-1)y_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{3}}\left(\frac{(t-2)(k-1)}{(t-1)(k-2)}y_{i}+\frac{t-2}{k-2}y_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v_{i}v_{j}\in M_{3}}(y_{i}+y_{j})

) forms a tight path. We argue that the randomness preserves the high supported co-degree conditions across the partition

The major difference for

whose edges are vertex sequences that support

Strucutre of the next four sections.

In the next section, we prove fundamental results for our proof strategy, starting with basics about the minimum supported co-degree and some simple graph theoretic statements in

Once we have set up the necessary machinery, as described in our proof sketch, we modify

Observation 11.1
.

Let

,
\ell\in[k-1]

11.1

We now prove a simple graph theoretic result about short disjoint paths. A

in a graph will refer to a path of length two.

Lemma 11.2
.

Suppose

Proof.

Let

be a disjoint union of cherries with leaves in

is maximised.
Let

and let

be the set of edges with exactly one end in

.
Then,

has maximum degree at most

(otherwise we could add a cherry with vertices in

to

, contradicting maximality), showing that

.
We now wish to upper bound

, using the following points.

•

For each cherry
uvw
in

, at most one of

,
v
and

has at least four neighbours in

. Indeed, otherwise we can find two disjoint cherries with centres in

It follows that the number of edges from

•

There is at most one edge from

to each vertex in

, as otherwise we could add an additional cherry to

.

Altogether, denoting by

It remains to show that

11.3
Cleaning lemmas

In this subsection, we will prove important results about finding substructures with large supported co-degrees in very dense hypergraphs. Broadly speaking, we will show that we can delete a few edges and find a nearly spanning subgraph that has no isolated vertices, and approximately preserves degree conditions. Similar ideas have appeared other papers regarding exact co-degree bounds for Hamilton cycles, such as
[
39
,
25
]
, but these were presented in an ad-hoc manner as intermediate steps in their proofs. We provide more general and formalised results about finding large, dense subgraphs with good degree conditions that we believe could prove to be independently useful in other settings as well.

Let us the begin with the first result. Given an almost complete
k
-graph, we will drop to an almost complete subgraph that spans almost all the vertices, and has high supported co-degree conditions. For
k=2
this can be done easily be removing all vertices of small degree, and it turns out that a similar but more subtle strategy works for higher uniformities. We point out that Halfpap, Lemons and Palmer
[
11
, Lemma 18]
have a similar result with essentially the same proof. However, our other two results in this section, namely
11.10
and
11.11
, are proved using the first one, and yield a stronger conclusion than that of
[
11
, Lemma 18]
. We expect these to be applicable in other settings as well.

Lemma 11.7
.

Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1/k\leq 1/2
. Suppose

is an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with
e(F)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\binom{n}{k}
. Then there exists a subgraph
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
with no isolated vertices such that
\delta^{*}(F^{\prime})\geq(1-\delta)n
. In particular,
|F^{\prime}|\geq(1-\delta)n
and
e(F^{\prime})\geq(1-k\delta)\binom{n}{k}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
satisfy
\varepsilon\ll\mu\ll\delta
. Let us describe how we obtain
F^{\prime}
from

. We construct a sequence of hypergraphs
F_{k},\dots,F_{1}
, where each
F_{i}
is
i
-uniform. First, we define
F_{k}=F
. Then, for
i\geq 2
, given
F_{i+1}
, we let
F_{i}
be the subgraph of
\partial^{i}{F_{i+1}}
with
V(F_{i})=V(F)
which contains every
i
-edge

satisfying
d_{F_{i+1}}^{1}(S)\geq(1-\mu)n
. Finally, in the graph
F_{2}
, we remove all vertices

such that
d_{F_{2}}^{1}(v)\leq(1-\mu)n
, and let

be the remaining vertex set. Now, we let

, and set

to consist of all
k
-sets

which have

for all

, that is, every

-subset of

is an edge of

.

We show that

satisfies the statement of

in two parts.

Claim 11.8
.

We have
|F^{\prime}|\geq(1-2^{k-1}\varepsilon/\mu^{k-1})n\geq(1-\delta)n
.

Proof.

Our proof proceeds by successively bounding the number of edges in each
F_{i}
from below. We know that
e(F_{k})=e(F)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\binom{n}{k}
. We define
\mu_{i}=2^{k-i}\varepsilon/\mu^{k-i}
for all

. We claim that, for all such

, we have

e(F_{i})\geq(1-\mu_{i})\binom{n}{i}.

Indeed, suppose we know that this is true for

. Write

, so that

is the number of supported
i
-sets

in

that have
d_{F_{i+1}}^{1}(S)\geq(1-\mu)n
. Consequently, by considering a degree sum over all supported
i
-sets in

and splitting the sum into

and those that are not, we see that

\binom{i+1}{i}(1-\mu_{i+1})\binom{n}{i+1}\leq fn+\left(\binom{n}{i}-f\right)(1-\mu)n.

Using
\binom{i+1}{i}\binom{n}{i+1}=n\binom{n-1}{i}
and rearranging, we see that

\displaystyle\mu f

\displaystyle\geq\mu\binom{n}{i}-\mu_{i+1}\binom{n-1}{i}-\left(\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-1}{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\binom{n}{i}-\mu_{i+1}\binom{n-1}{i}-\binom{n-1}{i-1}

\displaystyle\geq\mu\binom{n}{i}-2\mu_{i+1}\binom{n}{i},

where we use
\binom{n}{i}=\binom{n-1}{i}+\binom{n-1}{i-1}
and
\binom{n-1}{i-1}\leq\mu_{i+1}\binom{n}{i}
for

. Dividing throughout by

In particular, these calculations show that
|F^{\prime}|=|F_{1}|\geq(1-\mu_{1})n\geq(1-2^{k-1}\varepsilon/\mu^{k-1})n

∎

Claim 11.9
.

All supported sets

\varepsilon\ll\mu\ll\delta

F^{\prime\prime}
.
The graph
F^{\prime\prime}
clearly satisfies the first three properties above, so it suffices to prove the fourth item.
Suppose that for some set of vertices
S
of size
s\leq k-1
, which is supported in
F^{\prime\prime}
, we have
d^{1}_{F^{\prime\prime}}(S)\leq d^{1}_{F}(S)-\delta n
. This means that there is a set
X
of size
\delta n
such that, for every
x\in X
,
S\cup\{x\}
is supported in
F
but not in
F^{\prime\prime}
.
Thus, using
5.3
alongside the minimum supported co-degree assumption on
F
, we have

\displaystyle d_{F}(S)-d_{F^{\prime\prime}}(S)\geq\big|\{e\in E(F):S\cup\{x\}\subseteq e\text{ for some $x\in X$}\}\big|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|X|\cdot(\alpha n)^{k-s-1}}{(k-s)!}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\cdot\alpha^{k-s-1}\binom{n}{k-s}>\mu n^{k-s},

a contradiction to the choice of
F^{\prime\prime}
according to
11.10
.
∎

11.4
Perfect matchings in almost complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs

Our main aim in this subsection is to prove
11.15
below, which asserts that, if given a balanced
k
-partite
k
-graph
H
with large minimum supported co-degree and no isolated vertices, and a not-too-large family
\mathcal{F}
of subgraphs of
H
that are almost complete, there is a perfect matching
M
such that for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, almost every edge of
M
is in
F
.

We will use the following consequence of Hall’s theorem.

Proposition 11.12
.

Let
G=X\sqcup Y
be a bipartite graph with
|X|=|Y|=n
. If
\delta(G)\geq n/2
, then
G
has a perfect matching.

Proof.

Consider any subset
S\subseteq X
. If we can show that
N_{G}(S)
has at least
|S|
vertices, then we are done by Hall’s theorem.

Clearly
|N_{G}(S)|\geq\delta(G)\geq n/2
for all
S
. So if
|S|\leq n/2
, we are done. Hence, we may suppose
|S|>n/2
. Consider any
y\in Y
. Since
d_{G}(y)\geq n/2>|X\setminus S|
, the vertex
y
must have a neighbour in
S
. Since
y
is simply an arbitrary vertex of
Y
, we conclude that
N_{G}(S)=Y
, and clearly
|Y|=|X|\geq|S|
.
∎

The main content of the proof of the aforementioned result about matchings in
k
-partite
k
-graph is the following lemma, which resolves the graph case, i.e. when
k=2
. The matching is found in randomly, thus allows us to deduce that almost all edges of
M
are in
F
, for a not-too-large collection of subgraphs
\mathcal{F}
of
H
that are almost complete.

Lemma 11.13
.

Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1
.
Let
H
be a balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices with minimum degree at least
(1-\varepsilon)n
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of subgraphs of
H
, each with at least
(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
edges, such that
|\mathcal{F}|=e^{o(\sqrt{n})}
. Then, there is a perfect matching
M
in
H
such that
|M\cap E(F)|\geq(1-\delta)n
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Fix parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\varepsilon\ll\alpha\ll\beta\ll\delta
.
Denote the two parts of
H
by
X
and
Y
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be a sequence of
m:=(1-\beta)n
distinct vertices in
X
.
We sequentially pick vertices
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\in Y
such that each
y_{i}
is a uniformly random neighbour of
x_{i}
in
Y
, and is distinct from
y_{1},\dots,y_{i-1}
. This is always possible since
d(x)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n\geq m
for all
x\in X
.

Let
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
be the remaining unmatched vertices of
X
and
Y
, respectively. By choice, we have
|X^{\prime}|=|Y^{\prime}|=\beta n
.
Notice that the minimum degree in
H[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]
is at least
(1-\varepsilon)n-(1-\beta)n=(\beta-\varepsilon)n\geq\beta n/2=|X^{\prime}|/2
. Thus, by
11.12
, there is a perfect matching
M^{\prime}
in
H[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]
. Let
M
be the union of the matching
\{x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{m}y_{m}\}
with
M^{\prime}
.
Then
M
is a perfect matching in
H
.

Fix
F\in\mathcal{F}
. We will show that
|M\cap E(F)|\geq(1-\delta)n
, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{n})}
. A union bound will then conclude the proof.

Colour the edges of
F
blue and the remaining edges in
H
red.
By assumption, there are at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
red edges.
We say that a vertex
x\in X
is
scared
if its red degree into
Y
is at least
\alpha n
, and we will call it
happy
otherwise. Summing the red degrees of these vertices and using the bound on the number of red edges, we see that the number of scared vertices in
X
is at most
\varepsilon n/\alpha
.

For each
j\leq m
such that
x_{j}
is happy, let
p_{j}
be the probability that
x_{j}y_{j}
is a red edge. Since
x_{j}
is happy, we have

\displaystyle p_{j}\leq\frac{\alpha n}{(1-\varepsilon)n-(j-1)}\leq\frac{\alpha n}{(1-\varepsilon)n-m}=\frac{\alpha n}{(1-\varepsilon)n-(1-\beta)n}\leq\frac{2\alpha}{\beta}.

If we let
R
denote the random variable corresponding to the number of red edges in
\{x_{j}y_{j}:j\in[m],\text{ $x_{j}$ is happy}\}
, then clearly
R
is stochastically dominated by a binomial random variable
\hat{R}
with parameters
(n,2\alpha/\beta)
.
Hence, using Chernoff bounds (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R\geq 2\alpha n/\beta+n^{3/4}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\hat{R}\geq 2\alpha n/\beta+n^{3/4}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\hat{R}\geq\mathbb{E}\hat{R}+n^{3/4}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{n^{3/2}}{6n\alpha/\beta}\right)=e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{n})}.

Since the number of red edges in
M
is at most
R+\beta n+\varepsilon n/\alpha
, we have that, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{n})}
, the number of red edges in
M
is at most

\frac{2\alpha n}{\beta}+n^{3/4}+\beta n+\frac{\varepsilon n}{\alpha}\leq\delta n.

Thus, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{n})}
,
|M\cap E(F)|\geq(1-\delta)n
.
By a union bound, with high probability this holds for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

The next lemma deals with
k
-partite
k
-graphs. In particular, given a collection of subgraphs satisfying some high supported co-degree conditions, we wish to find a matching that shares almost all its edges with each subgraph in the collection.

Lemma 11.14
.

Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1/k\leq 1/3
. Let
H
be a
k
-partite
k
-graph with parts
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
, each having size
|X_{i}|=n
. Suppose that
H
has no isolated vertices and, for all
i\in[k]
, every supported set
S
in
H
which is disjoint from
X_{i}
satisfies
d^{1}_{X_{i}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of subgraphs satisfying the following conditions:

•

There exists a constant
c=c(k)
such that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq n^{c}
,

•

each
F\in\mathcal{F}
has no isolated vertices, and

•

for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
, every supported set
S
of
F
which is disjoint from
X_{i}
satisfies
d^{1}_{X_{i}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
.

Then there exists a perfect matching
M
in
G
such that
|M\cap E(F)|\geq(1-\delta)n
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

We construct each edge one vertex at a time. In the first step, we form a matching of supported pairs between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. We then extend this to a matching of supported triples across
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
and
X_{3}
, and so on. In each step, we will extend our existing matching using
11.13
and maintain certain properties that will allow us to continue the process in the next iteration.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
be parameters satisfying
\varepsilon=\gamma_{1}\ll\ldots\ll\gamma_{k}=\delta
.
We will first prove that for every
i\in[k-1]
there is a perfect matching
M_{i}
in
\partial^{i}H[X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i}]
such that for every
F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\{H\}
and
v\in V(F)\setminus(X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i})
, there are at least
(1-\gamma_{i})n
edges
S
in
M_{i}
such that
S\cup\{v\}
is supported in
F
.

We prove this by induction on
i
.
Notice that we can take
M_{1}=X_{1}
, using that
F
has no isolated vertices, which implies that any
v\in V(F)\setminus X_{1}
is supported and thus
d^{1}_{X_{1}}(v)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n=(1-\gamma_{1})n
.
For
i\in[2,k-1]
, suppose that
M_{i-1}
is a perfect matching in
\partial^{i}H[X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i-1}]
satisfying the requirements.

Let
T_{i}
be the bipartite graph on
M_{i-1}\sqcup X_{i}
, where
Sx
, with
S\in M_{i-1}
and
x\in X_{i}
, is an edge if
S\cup\{x\}
is supported in
H
.
We claim that
\delta(T_{i})\geq(1-\gamma_{i-1})n
.
Indeed, first notice that, by choice of
M_{i-1}
, every
S\in M_{i-1}
is supported in
H
and thus satisfies
d^{1}_{X_{i}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
, which exactly means that
d_{T_{i}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n\geq(1-\gamma_{i-1})n
.
Now, given
x\in X_{i}
, recall that
M_{i-1}
was chosen so that for at least
(1-\gamma_{i-1})n
sets
S\in M_{i-1}
we have that
S\cup\{x\}
is supported in
H
, which precisely means that
d_{T_{i}}(x)\geq(1-\gamma_{i-1})n
.

For every
F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\{H\}
and every
v\in V(F)\setminus(X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i})
let
T_{i,F,v}
be the subgraph of
T_{i}
where
Sx
, with
S\in M_{i-1}
and
x\in X_{i}
, is an edge if
S\cup\{x,v\}
is supported in
F
.
We claim that
e(T_{i,F,v})\geq(1-2\gamma_{i-1})n^{2}
.
Indeed, by choice of
M_{i-1}
, we have that at least
(1-\gamma_{i-1})n
sets
S
in
M_{i-1}
are such that
S\cup\{v\}
is supported in
F
. For every such
S
, since
S\cup\{v\}
avoids
X_{i}
and by assumption on

, this implies that
d^{1}_{X_{i}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
, meaning that
d_{T_{i,F,v}}(S)\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
.
Thus
e(T_{i,F,v})\geq(1-\gamma_{i-1})n\cdot(1-\varepsilon)n\geq(1-2\gamma_{i-1})n^{2}
.

Apply
11.13
with the graph
T_{i}
and the family
\{T_{i,F,v}:F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\{H\},v\in V(F)\setminus(X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i})\}
(which has size at most
(|\mathcal{F}|+1)\cdot kn\leq n^{c+2}
). This yields a perfect matching
M
in
T_{i}
such that
|M\cap E(T_{i,F,v})|\geq(1-\gamma_{i})n
for every relevant
F
and
v
.
Form
M_{i}
by including
S\cup\{x\}
for every edge
Sx
in
M
(with
S\in M_{i-1}
and
x\in X_{i}
).
It is easy to check that
M_{i}
is a perfect matching in
\partial^{i}H[X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{i}]
that has at least
(1-\gamma_{i})n
edges
S
such that
S\cup\{v\}
is supported in
F
, for every relevant
F
and
v
, as required.

Finally, suppose that we found a matching
M_{k-1}
with the desired properties.
We proceed similarly to the above to find a matching
M_{k}
that satisfies the requirements of the lemma. Define
T_{k}
as above, so that
\delta(T_{k})\geq(1-\gamma_{k-1})n
.
For every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, let
T_{k,F}
be the subgraph of
T_{k}
where
Sx
(with
S\in M_{k-1}
and
x\in X_{k}
) is an edge if
S\cup\{x\}
is an edge in
F
.
By choice of
M_{k-1}
, for every
x\in V(F)\cap X_{k}
, it contains at least
(1-\gamma_{k-1})n
edges

such that
S\cup\{x\}
is supported in
F
, which shows (using that
|V(F)\cap X_{k}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
which follows implicitly by the third and fourth items in the statement) that
e(T_{k,F})\geq(1-\varepsilon)n\cdot(1-\gamma_{k-1})n
.
As above, apply
11.13
to obtain a perfect matching

in

such that
|M\cap T_{k,F}|\geq(1-\gamma_{k})n
for every

.
This corresponds to a perfect matching

in

satisfying
|M_{k}\cap E(F)|\geq(1-\gamma_{k})n=(1-\delta)n
for every

, as required.
∎

Finally, we deduce the following corollary, where the requirements on each

are replaced by

being an almost complete

-partite

-graph. This is done by applying the ‘cleaning’ lemma

. The concrete upper bound given on the size of

is based on what we need in a later section.

Corollary 11.15
.

Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1/k\leq 1/3
. Let
H
be a
k
-partite

-graph with parts

, each having size

. Suppose that

has no isolated vertices and, for all

, every supported set

in

which is disjoint from

satisfies

. Let

be a collection of at most

subgraphs of

.
Then there exists a perfect matching

in

such that

Proof.

Let

.
For each

, apply

with the complete

, and parameters

∎

12
Structure of extremal hypergraphs

Our aim in this section is to modify the set
A
from the definition of extremal hypergraphs to obtain better control over co-degrees across and within the parts. Our main result in this section is the following.

Proposition 12.1
.

Let
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\mu\ll 1/k\leq 1/3
, let
\ell\in[k-1]
and set
t=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{k-\ell}\right\rfloor(k-\ell)
.
Let
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\varepsilon}(n,k,\ell)
.
Then there exist subsets
A,A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
and a subgraph
F_{B}^{+}\subseteq\partial^{k-1}[B^{\prime}]
with vertex set
B^{\prime}
that satisfy the following properties.

(i)

V(G)=A^{\prime}\sqcup B^{\prime}
and
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
,

(ii)

n/t-\mu n\leq|A|,|A^{\prime}|\leq n/t+\mu n
,

(iii)

any supported set
S
of size at most
k-1
that intersects
A
satisfies
d_{B^{\prime}}^{1}(S)\geq|B^{\prime}|-\mu n
, and

(iv)

F_{B}^{+}
has no isolated vertices,
\delta^{*}(F_{B}^{+})\geq(1-\mu)|B^{\prime}|
, and
d_{A}^{1}(S)\geq|A|-\mu n
for every
S\in E(F_{B}^{+})
.

This section will be devoted to the proof of
12.1
.
Throughout this section, we fix parameters

1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\varepsilon_{A}\ll\varepsilon_{k-1}\ll\mu\ll 1/k\leq 1/3,

and we subsequently fix
\ell
and
t
and a
k
-graph
G
as in the statement of
12.1
.

Recall that the extremality of
G
implies that there is a set
A
on
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
vertices that has at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
supported pairs. In the next claim we modify
A
so that every vertex of
A
supports only a few other vertices of
A
.

Claim 12.2
.

There exist subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that the following properties hold.

(i)

V(G)=A\sqcup B
,

(ii)

n/t-4\varepsilon n/\varepsilon_{A}\leq|A|\leq n/t
,

(iii)

G
has at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
supported pairs with both vertices in
A
, and

(iv)

every supported set
S
of size at most
k-1
that intersects
A
satisfies
d_{A}^{1}(S)\leq\varepsilon_{A}n
and
d_{B}^{1}(S)\geq|B|-\varepsilon_{A}n
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a set of
\left\lfloor n/t\right\rfloor
vertices with at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
supported pairs (which exists since
G\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\varepsilon}(n,k,\ell)
being extremal) and let
B=V(G)\setminus A
.
Let
x_{A}
be the number of vertices
u\in A
with
d_{A}^{1}(u)\geq\varepsilon_{A}n/2
.
Then the number of supported pairs in
A
is at least
x_{A}\varepsilon_{A}n/4
, but also at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
, showing that
x_{A}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon n}{\varepsilon_{A}}
.
Transfer these
x_{A}
vertices from
A
to
B
. We claim that the modified sets
A
and
B
satisfy the requirements of the claim.

The first three parts follow directly from the choice of
A
. For the final part, let
S
be a supported set of size at most
k-1
with
u\in S\cap A
. Clearly
u
must support every vertex that
S
supports, that is, we have
N_{A}^{1}(S)\subseteq N_{A}^{1}(u)
. This implies

d_{A}^{1}(S)\leq d_{A}^{1}(u)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{A}n}{2}\leq\varepsilon_{A}n,

where the final inequality comes from the modification of
A
. Then, since
d_{G}^{1}(S)=d_{A}^{1}(S)+d_{B}^{1}(S)
and
d_{G}^{1}(S)\geq(1-1/t)n-k
(by
11.1
), we have

\displaystyle d_{B}^{1}(S)

\displaystyle\geq d_{G}^{1}(S)-d_{A}^{1}(S)\geq(1-1/t)n-k-\frac{\varepsilon_{A}n}{2}\geq|B|-\frac{4\varepsilon n}{\varepsilon_{A}}-(k+1)-\frac{\varepsilon_{A}n}{2}\geq|B|-\varepsilon_{A}n,

so the final item holds too.
∎

Next, we tackle supported
(k-1)
-sets in
B
which have high vertex co-degree into
A
.

Definition 12.3
.

We say that a supported
(k-1)
-set
S\subseteq B
in
B
is
A
-scarce
if
d_{A}^{1}(S)\leq|A|-\varepsilon_{k-1}n
. Otherwise, we say that it is
A
-rich
.

Let
x_{k-1}
be the number of
A
-rich
(k-1)
-sets in
B
. In the next claim we show that almost all
(k-1)
-sets in
B
are
A
-rich.

Claim 12.4
.

We have
x_{k-1}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{k-1})\binom{|B|}{k-1}
.

Proof.

Let
m
denote the number of edges of
G
with one vertex in
A
and
(k-1)
vertices in
B
. We will double count
m
, obtain a lower and an upper bound, and then compare the two.

First, let us lower bound
m
. We will construct an edge with
(k-1)
vertices in
B
by repeatedly using Claim
12.2
to build larger and larger supported sets until we form an edge. Start with any vertex
u_{1}\in A
. Suppose that we have constructed a supported
i
-set
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{i}\}
, with
u_{2},\dots,u_{i}\in B
and

. By Claim
12.2
, there are at least
|B|-\varepsilon_{A}n
choices for a vertex in
B
that can be used to extend our set to a supported

-set. Thus, we see that

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|A|}{(k-1)!}\left(|B|-\varepsilon_{A}n\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|A|}{(k-1)!}\left(|B|^{k-1}-(k-1)\varepsilon_{A}n|B|^{k-2}\right).

Next, we bound
m
from above. We do this by looking at all possible supported
(k-1)
-sets in
B
, and then counting the maximum possible number of vertices in
A
that each such
(k-1)
-set could support. Hence, we have

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq x_{k-1}|A|+\left(\binom{|B|}{k-1}-x_{k-1}\right)(|A|-\varepsilon_{k-1}n)

\displaystyle\leq x_{k-1}\varepsilon_{k-1}n+(|A|-\varepsilon_{k-1}n)\cdot\frac{|B|^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}.

Finally, we compare the two bounds

\displaystyle x_{k-1}\varepsilon_{k-1}n

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{k-1}n|B|^{k-1}-(k-1)\varepsilon_{A}n|A||B|^{k-2}}{(k-1)!}

\displaystyle\geq n\cdot\frac{|B|^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\cdot(\varepsilon_{k-1}-(k-1)\varepsilon_{A})

\displaystyle=n\cdot\binom{|B|}{k-1}\cdot(\varepsilon_{k-1}-(k-1)\varepsilon_{A})+O(n^{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq n\cdot\binom{|B|}{k-1}\cdot(\varepsilon_{k-1}-k\varepsilon_{A}),

where we use
|A|\leq|B|
(since
|A|\leq n/t\leq n/2
). Simplifying further yields

\displaystyle x_{k-1}\geq\binom{|B|}{k-1}\left(1-\frac{k\varepsilon_{A}}{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\right)\geq\binom{|B|}{k-1}(1-\varepsilon_{k-1}),

proving the claim.
∎

We are now ready to prove
12.1
, using the last claim and one of the cleaning lemma from the previous section, and moving a few exceptional vertices from
B
to
A
.

Proof of
12.1
.

Apply
11.7
to the subgraph
F^{\prime}
of
\partial^{k-1}[B]
consisting of

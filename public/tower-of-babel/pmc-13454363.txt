Traditional Chinese Medicine Combined With Chemotherapy for Mammary Paget's Disease With Multifocal Heterogeneous Breast Cancer: A Case Report
Authors: Hongyu Cheng, Jiayin Tang, Chang Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMammary Paget's disease with ipsilateral multifocal, heterogeneous molecular subtype breast cancer is extremely rare and lacks diagnostic or therapeutic guidelines. Molecular subtype heterogeneity represents a major diagnostic and therapeutic challenge for this category of diseases. This case also involved treatment‐related adverse events such as fatigue and insomnia. This paper reports the treatment process and final outcomes of a patient who received chemotherapy and radiotherapy combined with traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) for this rare condition. The patient presented with a left breast mass and eczematous changes of the nipple. She underwent surgical resection followed by chemotherapy and radiotherapy, after which she developed severe fatigue, insomnia, nausea, vomiting, palpitations, and chest tightness, which significantly affected her quality of life. The patient was diagnosed with mammary Paget's disease with ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer, T1cN1aM0, molecular subtyping exhibits inter‐lesional heterogeneity: Estrogen Receptor (ER) (+), Progesterone Receptor (PR) (−), Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) (3+) and ER (−), PR (−), and HER2 (3+). The patient received standardized systemic treatment corresponding to the pathology of the mass, along with TCM therapies such as oral herbal medicine. After standardized treatment combined with TCM therapy, the patient's chemotherapy‐related discomfort symptoms such as fatigue and sleep disturbances showed improvement during follow‐up, and follow‐up showed no obvious signs of recurrence or metastasis. Preoperative precise biopsy of all suspicious lesions should be performed. Stratified comprehensive treatment (targeted therapy, chemotherapy and radiotherapy, intensified capecitabine) should be tailored based on heterogeneous subtypes. In this single case, oral administration of TCM was associated with reported relief of adverse events such as fatigue and insomnia, suggesting its potential as an adjunctive supportive intervention. However, due to the uncontrolled, single‐case design, causal efficacy cannot be inferred.

Introduction

Currently, breast cancer is the most common malignant tumor among women in China [1]. Among breast cancer, mammary Paget's disease, attributed to the “cell migration hypothesis,” is extremely rare, accounting for approximately 1%–3% of all breast cancer cases [2]. The incidence of multifocal breast cancer, attributed to the “two‐hit” hypothesis, varies significantly across different studies, ranging from 6% to 77% [3, 4, 5]. The concurrent occurrence of these two conditions is extremely rare, with only sporadic reports documented in the existing literature [6]. Therefore, this case holds significant clinical implications, including: dual rarity, the preservation of multifocal tumor integrity during surgery, the difficulty in making treatment decisions for multifocal breast cancer with molecular subtype heterogeneity which impacts disease diagnosis, prognosis and treatment planning, and the implementation of individualized comprehensive therapy targeting high‐risk factors. Given the rarity of this comorbidity, standardized diagnostic and therapeutic guidelines are currently lacking, which poses numerous challenges in clinical practice. This case report aims to provide clinical reference for this rare clinical scenario by adopting a diagnostic workflow combining multimodal imaging examinations and individualized immunohistochemical analysis, as well as an innovative comprehensive treatment strategy integrating subtype‐specific systemic therapy, local radiotherapy, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) therapy based on syndrome differentiation and treatment.

Case History

A 52‐year‐old female presented with “a left breast mass accompanied by nipple eczematous changes” on August 30, 2024. Ultrasonography (Figures 1 and 2) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (Figure 3) revealed masses at the 5 o'clock and 6 o'clock positions of the left breast.

Breast ultrasonography demonstrates a hypoechoic lesion at the 5 o'clock position of the left breast, measuring approximately 11.8 × 11.0 × 7.7 mm, with indistinct margins and irregular shape, classified as BI‐RADS category 5.

Breast ultrasonography demonstrates a hypoechoic lesion at the 6 o'clock position of the left breast, measuring approximately 12.8 × 12.6 × 13.7 mm, with indistinct and lobulated margins, irregular shape and non‐parallel orientation, classified as BI‐RADS category 5.

Dynamic contrast‐enhanced breast MRI demonstrates two nodular lesions at the 6 o'clock position of the left breast, measuring approximately 15 and 7 mm in diameter, respectively. Both lesions exhibit marked early‐phase enhancement and are classified as BI‐RADS category 5.

Differential Diagnosis, Investigations, and Treatment

On September 10, 2024, the patient underwent modified radical mastectomy of the left breast at Jiangsu Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Tumor characteristics: Mass A measured 1.2 × 1 × 1 cm, and Mass B was 2 × 1.5 × 1.5 cm. Both were diagnosed as invasive ductal carcinoma, with a histological grade of 3 for both masses. Ductal carcinoma in situ components were identified in both masses. Atypical cell infiltration was observed in the local nipple epithelium, which was consistent with nipple Paget's disease based on immunohistochemical staining. Lymph node status: 2 out of 3 sentinel lymph nodes were positive, whereas 0 out of 22 left axillary lymph nodes were positive, resulting in an overall lymph node involvement of 2/25, pTNM stage: T1cN1aM0. Immunohistochemical findings: For invasive cancer cell nests in Mass A: Estrogen Receptor (ER) (−), Progesterone Receptor (PR) (−), Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2) (3+), and Ki67 (approximately 40% + in hotspots). For invasive cancer cell nests in Mass B: ER (approximately 20% with weak to moderate +), PR (−), HER2 (3+), and Ki67 (approximately 40% + in hotspots). Considering the lack of treatment guidelines and therapeutic regimens documented in case reports for the comorbidity of multifocal breast cancer and Paget's disease, we adopted the standard treatment regimen corresponding to the molecular subtypes of each heterogeneous mass with reference to the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) Breast Cancer Guidelines 2024 [7]. The patient initiated the AC × 4‐T × 4 chemotherapy regimen every 3 weeks on September 20, 2024. For the first four cycles, cyclophosphamide 0.85 g + pegylated liposomal doxorubicin 50 mg were administered; for the subsequent four cycles, paclitaxel injection 240 mg was used. On December 13, 2024, the patient started the HP dual‐target therapy every 3 weeks, receiving trastuzumab 600 mg plus pertuzumab 420 mg for a duration of 1 year. Meanwhile, we prescribed a 5‐year course of endocrine therapy with once‐daily oral administration of 2.5 mg letrozole tablets for the patients. Subsequently, radiotherapy was completed in March 2025, with a total of 25 fractions delivered to the left supraclavicular, infraclavicular, and left chest wall regions, at a cumulative dose of 50 Gy/25 fractions. After the completion of chemoradiotherapy, the patient received intensified capecitabine therapy according to standard protocols, combined with TCM that initiated in January 2025 as adjuvant treatment to alleviate palpitations and chest tightness caused by anthracycline drugs, as well as hand‐foot syndrome induced by capecitabine. The TCM formula was formulated based on the syndrome differentiation of “qi‐yin deficiency and blood stasis,” which was referenced to the Textbook of Chinese Medicine Surgery and Breast Cancer Treatment Guidelines 2023 Edition (formula composition: 6 g of Dahuang, 30 g of Huangqi, 10 g of Jianghuang, 20 g of Suanzaoren, 15 g of Hehuanpi, 15 g of Fushen, 12 g of Baizhu, 30 g of Hongjingtian, 10 g of Nanshashen, and 5 g of Ruxiang). It was administered twice daily (200 mL each time) for 12 consecutive weeks.

Conclusion and Results

During TCM treatment, the patient, respectively received follow‐up visits in January 2025 and May 2025, reported a marked improvement in chemotherapy‐induced adverse events including fatigue, nausea and sleep disturbances assessed by validated professional scales including the Brief Fatigue Inventory (BFI), Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) (Table 1). In the most recent follow‐up in March 2026, a whole‐body examination showed no definite recurrent or metastatic lesions of breast cancer, and no chemotherapy‐related adverse events occurred.

Discussion

According to current literature reports, the “cell migration” hypothesis is considered the cause of Paget's disease: Paget cells originate from the mammary ductal luminal epithelium and then migrate to the epidermis through the milk ducts, resulting in nipple eczematous changes [8]. Almost all patients diagnosed with mammary Paget's disease have underlying central or multifocal invasive breast cancer or ductal carcinoma in situ [9, 10].

The pathogenesis of multifocal breast cancer remains unclear. Some researchers suggest that it may be associated with genetic susceptibility caused by BRCA1/2 gene mutations [11]. Additionally, the “two‐hit” hypothesis proposed by Knudson explains this phenomenon to a certain extent [12]: composed of germline mutations and carcinogenic factors such as high estrogen stimulation and oxidative stress.

The clinical manifestations of ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer are not significantly different from those of other types of breast cancer, and attention to imaging changes is sufficient. In contrast, the characteristic manifestation of mammary Paget's disease is nipple eczematous changes, which require a clear differential diagnosis from nipple eczema.

Breast cancer is molecularly classified into Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2‐enriched and Basal‐like subtypes [13]. Tumor molecular heterogeneity manifests as divergent molecular subtypes among distinct breast cancer masses [14], and these subtype differences significantly impact diagnostic approaches, prognostic evaluation, and therapeutic planning. In this case, the co‐existence of Luminal B and HER2‐enriched subtypes demonstrates inter‐tumor molecular heterogeneity, highlighting the importance of conducting independent pathological verification, including immunohistochemical analysis, for each radiologically distinct lesion to ensure diagnostic precision. In terms of treatment, therapeutic strategies targeting common positive markers across heterogeneous lesions constitute an essential component of clinical management.

For patients with heterogeneous receptor expression, we should weigh treatment benefits and adopt a balanced therapeutic approach, particularly intensifying therapy for high‐risk subgroups. Previous studies indicate that multifocal breast cancer with molecular subtype heterogeneity exhibits worse prognosis compared to single‐subtype tumors due to clonal evolution and therapy resistance [15]. Thus, intensified treatment modalities (e.g., radiotherapy or capecitabine) combined with closer follow‐up are warranted.

It is worth emphasizing that the concurrent occurrence of mammary Paget's disease and ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer is extremely rare. A literature search revealed that as of 2024, fewer than 10 similar cases have been reported globally, and most of these cases did not involve discussions on the comprehensive treatment of heterogeneous molecular subtypes. Compared with previous studies on single‐subtype mammary Paget's disease or ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer, the patient in this case possesses three major high‐risk factors simultaneously: “mammary Paget's disease combined with ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer,” “heterogeneous molecular subtypes,” and “positive lymph node metastasis.” The complexity of the condition and the difficulty of treatment are significantly higher than those of ordinary cases, which also highlights the unique value of this case in enriching the diagnosis and treatment experience of rare breast cancers and improving the treatment strategy for heterogeneous lesions.

In a retrospective study of 200 patients followed for 25 years, Delberg found that the choice of surgical approach (including local surgical treatment and total mastectomy) had no significant impact on prognosis [16]. Therefore, the selection of a surgical plan should be based on the characteristics of invasive cancer and the patient's preferences. Notably, multifocal masses are an absolute contraindication to breast‐conserving surgery, and total mastectomy is preferred for ipsilateral dual primary breast cancer due to superior patient benefits [17, 18]. Considering these multiple factors, total mastectomy was recommended to the patient.

Yasir et al. [19] noted that without specific guidelines for mammary Paget's disease, postoperative adjuvant therapy for Paget's disease with invasive breast cancer should follow subtype‐based strategies for invasive breast cancer. After surgery, the patient required adjuvant chemoradiotherapy and subsequent related treatments [20]. Previous studies on the treatment of multifocal breast cancer are not applicable to this case due to the temporal variations in the occurrence of its masses [2, 3, 4]. A study by Kim et al. [21] indicated that formulating a treatment plan based solely on the highest‐risk tumor is insufficient. However, authors have not identified any guidelines or case reports documenting postoperative chemotherapy strategies for ipsilateral multifocal breast cancer. Therefore, developing a treatment plan that covers the subtypes and risks of all masses seems to be a more reasonable approach at the current stage.

First, if the patient has a heterogeneous mass, subtype‐specific targeted therapy is indispensable. These heterogeneous lesions share the common positive target HER2, thus HER2‐directed therapy should be prioritized. Previous studies have demonstrated that for HER2‐positive breast cancer, dual‐target therapy with trastuzumab and pertuzumab exerts a significant effect on inhibiting tumor progression, and the same applies to multifocal breast cancer [22, 23, 24]. Therefore, in this case, dual‐targeted therapy is a reliable treatment option. Second, the formulation of a stratified chemotherapy regimen is necessary in multifocal breast cancer [25]. We need to simultaneously consider the molecular subtypes of each mass, combine the cardiotoxicity of anthracycline chemotherapy drugs [26], select a comprehensive chemotherapy regimen, avoid the cumulative toxicity of anthracycline drugs, and maximize the patient's quality of life. For patients with heterogeneous hormone receptor (HR) expression, the role of endocrine therapy is debated. Although Mass A lacks HR expression and thus derives no benefit, Mass B is HR‐positive, suggesting potential efficacy. Given this discordance, we ultimately opted for endocrine therapy based on the overall tumor burden and patient prognosis. In addition, individualized adjustment based on clinical risk factors is a requirement of precision medicine. In this case, for high‐risk factors such as lymph node involvement, multifocal tumors, and Paget's disease, increasing the intensity of systemic therapy—such as the intensified capecitabine therapy we selected—can improve the prognosis [27]. At last, multiple studies have confirmed that breast cancer patients who receive adjuvant radiotherapy after mastectomy can significantly reduce the risk of cancer recurrence and metastasis [28, 29, 30]. Given the high recurrence risk in this patient, adjuvant radiotherapy was recommended. The addition of radiotherapy and oral capecitabine therapy is precisely the intensified treatment regimen we selected in consideration of the poor prognosis of multifocal breast cancer with molecular subtype heterogeneity in this patient.

During the treatment and follow‐up period, the patient reported symptoms such as cancer‐related fatigue, nausea and vomiting, and insomnia. We previously administered targeted pharmacotherapy including corticosteroids, 5‐HT receptor antagonists, and benzodiazepine receptor agonists [31, 32, 33]. Although temporary therapeutic effects were achieved, the symptoms recurred after drug withdrawal. In contrast, TCM possesses unique advantages in the management of chronic diseases [34]. Therefore, TCM was integrated into the comprehensive therapeutic regimen to optimize rehabilitation outcomes. The TCM intervention was tailored to the patient's post‐chemotherapy syndrome characteristics, with a formula focused on replenishing qi, nourishing yin, and promoting blood circulation to remove stasis. During the follow‐up period, the patient provided specific positive feedback on symptom improvement: (1) Reported alleviation of chemotherapy‐related fatigue (the patient reported a reduction in daily fatigue score from 7/10 to 2/10 based on the BFI); (2) reported resolution of grade 1 nausea (no recurrence of nausea after 2 weeks of TCM administration, as evaluated by the CTCAE); (3) reported improvement in sleep quality (the patient reported a reduction in score from 13/21 to 4/21 based on the PSQI). Moreover, the patient exhibited good adherence to TCM therapy and persisted with oral TCM administration throughout the follow‐up period. Studies have confirmed that TCM can regulate systemic immunity, mitigate treatment‐related adverse events, and improve functional status in breast cancer patients [35, 36]. However, as an uncontrolled single‐case report, this study cannot establish a definitive causal relationship between TCM and the observed clinical improvements. Symptom alleviation may alternatively result from spontaneous resolution, placebo effects, or concurrent conventional therapies. Thus, TCM should be interpreted as a potential adjunctive supportive intervention rather than an evidence‐based therapeutic modality. In this rare, high‐recurrence‐risk case with heterogeneous molecular subtypes, TCM adjuvant therapy appeared subjectively associated with patient‐reported symptom relief, improved treatment compliance, and enhanced quality of life.

Current observations suggest that TCM may serve as a potential adjuvant therapy for rehabilitation in patients with complex breast cancer subtypes. However, given the limitations of this uncontrolled case study, the observed correlations should be interpreted with caution. Rigorously controlled clinical trials are necessary to establish any causal relationship between TCM intervention and the reported outcomes.

This study has the following limitations: First of all, reoperative core needle biopsy and immunohistochemical analysis were not performed for each suspicious lesion individually; the molecular subtype heterogeneity was only confirmed based on postoperative pathology, which may have led to insufficient precision in the preoperative planning of treatment regimens. This is also a common issue to be avoided in the diagnosis of multifocal tumors. Then, BRCA gene testing was not conducted, so the impact of genetic susceptibility on disease occurrence and prognosis cannot be clarified, which also limits the further optimization of targeted treatment strategies. Third, regarding the assessment of symptom improvement: Fatigue was assessed using the validated BFI, nausea was graded according to the CTCAE and sleep quality was evaluated by PSQI. These assessments were performed during clinical follow‐up visits. All the aforementioned symptom assessments were conducted in an open‐label manner in an uncontrolled, non‐randomized, single‐patient setting. The absence of a control group and blinding may lead to reporting bias and preclude causal inference. Additionally, as a single‐case report, it lacks support from large‐sample data, and the follow‐up period is limited (18 months), since more than 80% of breast cancer patients survive beyond 5 years [1]. The long‐term efficacy and long‐term safety of TCM intervention still need to be verified by longer follow‐up and more cases.

Despite certain limitations, this study still provides new directions for subsequent research. To begin with, conduct in‐depth studies on the mechanism of action of TCM intervention to clarify its molecular mechanisms in regulating the tumor microenvironment and improving immune function. Subsequently, establish a preoperative precise evaluation process for multifocal breast cancer, standardize strategies for lesion core needle biopsy and genetic testing, and provide more sufficient evidence for personalized treatment. Ultimately, design randomized controlled trials to further verify the efficacy and safety of TCM “syndrome differentiation and treatment” in breast cancer rehabilitation, thereby providing high‐level evidence support for integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine treatment.

Conclusion

Based on the entire diagnosis and treatment process and findings of this case, the following key experiences and lessons can be summarized to provide clear references for the clinical practice of similar complex cases: First, it is necessary to improve precise preoperative diagnosis and mandate independent core needle biopsy and immunohistochemical analysis for all suspicious lesions to determine the presence or absence of molecular heterogeneity. Second, individualized treatment strategies should be developed, adopting a comprehensive regimen of “prioritized coverage of common targets, balanced chemotherapy for different molecular subtypes, and intensified treatment for high‐risk factors.” Ultimately, no recurrence or metastasis was observed during the follow‐up period, confirming the effectiveness of this plan. Furthermore, the integration of TCM syndrome differentiation‐assisted therapy may be of great clinical significance for improving patients' quality of life. Based on the syndrome type of “qi‐yin deficiency and blood stasis,” a TCM formula focusing on replenishing qi, nourishing yin, and promoting blood circulation to remove stasis was formulated, which significantly improved the patient's adverse symptoms after chemotherapy (fatigue score decreased from 7/10 to 2/10, nausea disappeared, and sleep quality score decreased from 13/21 to 4/21). In view of the prominent effect of TCM in alleviating the treatment‐related adverse events of the patient in this case, it is worth considering to conduct multi‐center, large‐sample randomized controlled trials to verify the long‐term efficacy and safety of TCM “syndrome differentiation and treatment” in the rehabilitation of complex breast cancer. In addition, formulating a standardized diagnosis and treatment process for composite breast cancer, and clarifying the unified standards for preoperative evaluation (multimodal imaging + full‐lesion biopsy), intraoperative management (basis for surgical approach selection), and postoperative adjuvant treatment (combination strategy of targeted therapy, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy) are urgent research directions and necessary clinical management strategies for addressing complex breast cancer.

In conclusion, the successful diagnosis and treatment of this case have illustrated the feasibility of the trinity model of “preoperative precise full‐lesion assessment‐integrated personalized treatment‐TCM syndrome differentiation‐assisted intervention.” These observations suggest the potential value of TCM as an adjuvant modality in the rehabilitation of patients with complex breast cancer subtypes, but further controlled studies are needed to confirm any causal effects. For this single patient, TCM served as a supportive adjunct correlated with self‐reported symptom reduction. However, due to the uncontrolled single‐case design of this report, causal efficacy cannot be inferred. Therefore, the findings of this article should be regarded as hypothesis‐generating rather than conclusive evidence. This model not only provides a replicable diagnostic and therapeutic approach for similar complex breast cancer cases but also lays a foundation for the improvement of future clinical guidelines and the promotion of the multidisciplinary collaborative diagnosis and treatment model.

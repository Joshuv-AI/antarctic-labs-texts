UNIV: Unified Foundation Model for Infrared and Visible Modalities
Fangyuan Mao Shuo Wang Jilin Mei Chen Min Shun Lu, Fuyang Liu Yu Hu

Abstract
The demand for joint RGB-visible and infrared perception is growing rapidly, particularly to achieve robust performance under diverse weather conditions. Although pre-trained models for RGB-visible and infrared data excel in their respective domains, they often underperform in multimodal scenarios, such as autonomous vehicles equipped with both sensors. To address this challenge, we propose a biologically inspired UN ified foundation model for I nfrared and V isible modalities ( UNIV ), featuring two key innovations. First, we introduce P atch-wise C ross-modality C ontrastive L earning ( PCCL ), an attention-guided distillation framework that mimics retinal horizontal cells’ lateral inhibition, which enables effective cross-modal feature alignment while remaining compatible with any transformer-based architecture. Second, our dual-knowledge preservation mechanism emulates the retina’s bipolar cell signal routing — combining LoRA adapters (2% added parameters) with synchronous distillation to prevent catastrophic forgetting, thereby replicating the retina’s photopic (cone-driven) and scotopic (rod-driven) functionality. To support cross-modal learning, we introduce the MVIP dataset, the most comprehensive visible-infrared benchmark to date. It contains 98,992 precisely aligned image pairs spanning diverse scenarios. Extensive experiments demonstrate UNIV’s superior performance on infrared tasks (+1.7 mIoU in semantic segmentation and +0.7 mAP in object detection) while maintaining 99 % + 99\%+ of the baseline performance on visible RGB tasks. Our code is available at https://github.com/fangyuanmao/UNIV .

This asymmetric alignment, grounded in both biological principles of cone-dominated processing and empirical evidence from Table

, establishes RGB as the anchor modality whose semantic space adaptively regularizes infrared features.

2
Related Works

2.1
The Foundation Model

Pre-trained models, such as BERT
Devlin 2018
for NLP, ViTs
Dosovitskiy 2020
for vision, and CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
for text-vision tasks, extract general features from large-scale datasets, which can be fine-tuned on specific downstream tasks. This approach not only enhances downstream performance but also accelerates convergence.
In the infrared domain, PAD
Zhang et al. 2023
pre-trains the backbone on abundant infrared images with adapter fine-tuning, while InfMAE
Liu et al. 2025a
employs an infrared-specific mask sampling strategy for pre-training. However, existing infrared foundation models are primarily designed for the infrared spectrum and fail to generalize to visible RGB images, limiting their applicability in multi-modal scenarios.

Our proposed UNIV model bridges this gap by extracting effective features from both infrared and visible RGB images, enabling robust multi-modal perception.

2.2
Pre-training on Non-iconic Infrared Datasets

Non-iconic datasets, which are common in infrared imaging (e.g., KAIST
Hwang et al. 2015
), represent realistic scenarios with multiple objects, posing significant challenges for self-supervised pre-training. To address these challenges, several methods have been proposed. Self-EMD
Liu et al. 2020
aligns local image features for pre-training, while SSTN
Munir et al. 2021
enhances embedding similarity between paired RGB and infrared images. UniVIP
Li et al. 2022
contrasts features at three hierarchical levels, from global scenes to local instances. R-MAE
Nguyen et al. 2023
utilizes an additional encoder and decoder to perform Masked Image Modeling (MIM)
He et al. 2022
;
Wei et al. 2022
within local regions. While PAD
Zhang et al. 2023
transforms non-iconic images into iconic ones by cropping regions of interest, this approach may lose contextual information critical for scene understanding.

Inspired by local feature analysis, we propose a novel patch-wise contrastive learning method that effectively captures cross-modal relationships in non-iconic datasets.

2.3
Adapter-based Methods

Adapter-based methods enable efficient fine-tuning of backbones with low training costs. LoRA
Hu et al. 2021
freezes backbone weights and optimizes low-rank matrices, while ViT-Adapter
Chen et al. 2022
adds task-specific modules for dense predictions. PAD
Zhang et al. 2023
introduces a pre-training paradigm using layer adapters to learn infrared domain-specific features. TeFF
Zhou et al. 2024
demonstrates that LoRA can partly preserve backbone knowledge, mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

By integrating efficient LoRA fine-tuning as dynamic signal routing between modalities, our model not only extracts infrared features but also preserves RGB modality knowledge with greatly reduced training costs.

Methodology

3.1
Overview

As shown in Fig.
2
, ambient light intensity determines whether bipolar cells preferentially route signals through cones (photopic) or rods (scotopic) pathways. Mirroring this biological duality, our UNIV model implements three key components: LoRA-based adaptive pathway expansion for rod-like infrared adaptation, Patch-wise Cross-modality Contrastive Learning implementing lateral inhibition for knowledge transfer, and a dual-knowledge preservation mechanism maintaining cone-like RGB robustness through pathway stabilization.

3.2
LoRA-based Modality Adaptive Pathways

To emulate the supplementary pathway of retinal rods, we employ Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA
Hu et al. 2021
), where trainable matrices
\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
and
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k}
implicitly implement modality adaptive routing by learning modality-specific sparse patterns.
\bm{B}\bm{A}
’s rank
r
decomposition automatically aligns subsets of
\{\bm{b}_{i}\bm{a}_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{r}
to RGB/infrared features, mirroring retinal bipolar cells’ stimulus-selective pathways. The frozen
\bm{W}
maintains baseline visible knowledge (cones), while dynamic updates to
\bm{B}\bm{A}
enable rod-like infrared adaptation.

\bm{h}=\underbrace{\bm{W}\bm{x}}_{\text{Baseline (RGB)}}+\underbrace{\bm{B}\bm{A}\bm{x}}_{\text{Adaptation (IR)}}

(1)

where
\bm{x}
is the input and
\bm{h}
is the output. During inference,
\bm{B}
and
\bm{A}
can be merged into
\bm{W}^{*}=\bm{W}+\bm{B}\bm{A}
, avoiding additional computational overhead. More analysis can be found in Appendix
E
.

3.3
PCCL: Patch-wise Cross-modality Contrastive Learning

PCCL trains the added LoRA pathway by enforcing geometric and semantic alignment between RGB and infrared features. The method first computes patch-wise similarity scores between modalities, analogous to the emulating the topographic correspondence between cones and rods photoreceptors
Schiller et al. 1986
. Then it generates a binary mask from RGB attention scores to amplify high-correlation regions (stars in Fig.
3
) and suppress peripheral noise (score=0), replicating contrast enhancement of horizontal cells’ lateral inhibition
VanLeeuwen et al. 2009
;
Chapot et al. 2017
. Finally, it optimizes feature alignment via contrastive learning, where infrared features are pulled toward RGB semantic anchors — analogous to bipolar cells relaying cone-dominant signals under photopic conditions.

Feature Extraction and Similarity Mapping.

Given paired visible RGB and infrared images, we extract their features using the trainable encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}
:

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{V}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}(\text{Img}_{\text{Visible}}),

(2)

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{I}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}(\text{Img}_{\text{Infrared}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times N},

where
d_{k}
denotes the feature size and
N
represents the number of image patches.

Infrared images often contain multiple objects, resulting in feature representations
\bm{F}^{I}
that aggregate diverse semantic information. Direct batch-level comparison of infrared features is challenging due to the diverse and non-iconic nature of infrared images, which limits the effectiveness of traditional contrastive learning approaches. To address this, we decompose the infrared features into patch-level components
\bm{F}^{I}=\{f_{1}^{I},f_{2}^{I},\dots,f_{N}^{I}\}
, where each patch feature
f_{i}^{I}
can be treated as an independent semantic entity for granular similarity matching.

To establish the cross-modality relationships, we compute similarity scores between decomposed infrared patch features and reference visible features. Specifically, we leverage a frozen pre-trained encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\text{frozen}}
to extract stable visible features
\bm{F}^{Vf}=\mathcal{E}_{\text{frozen}}(\text{Img}_{\text{Visible}})
, then calculate the similarity matrix as:

\bm{M}^{IV}_{S}=\text{CosineSimilarity}(\bm{F}^{I},\bm{F}^{Vf})/\tau,

(3)

where
\tau
serves as the temperature parameter controlling sharpness. This similarity matrix reflects the cross-modal correlation between patches.

Pseudo-label Generation from Attention Maps.

To encode semantically related inter-patch correlations, we convert the last-layer attention map
\bm{M}^{V}_{A}=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
from
\mathcal{E}_{\text{frozen}}(\text{Img}_{\text{Visible}})
into a binary pseudo-label matrix
\bm{M}^{V}_{P}
using the following rules:

•

Self-similarity enforcement. Set all diagonal elements
\bm{M}^{V}_{P,ii}=1
to preserve patch identity.

•

Correlation selection.
\{\bm{M}^{V}_{A,ik}\}_{k=1}^{N}
is the elements sorted in descending order by row i and
\pi(\cdot)
represents the mapping from the sorted elements’ indices to the original indices such that
\bm{M}^{V}_{A,i\pi(1)}\geq\cdots\geq\bm{M}^{V}_{A,i\pi(N)}
; find the minimum
m
satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bm{M}^{V}_{A,i\pi(k)}>\gamma
; set
\bm{M}^{V}_{P,i\pi(1)},...,\bm{M}^{V}_{P,i\pi(m)}=1
.

•

Dissimilarity suppression. Set non-selected entries to 0.

This pseudo-label generation operation is formally expressed as:

\bm{M}^{V}_{P,ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=j\\
1&\text{if }j\in\big\{\pi(1),\dots,\pi(m_{\text{thr}})\big\}\ ,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(4)

where
m_{\text{thr}}=\min\left\{m\,\bigg|\,\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bm{M}^{V}_{A,i\pi(k)}>\gamma\right\}
.

Loss Design for PCCL

PCCL optimizes the model by aligning
\bm{M}^{IV}_{S}
with
\bm{M}^{V}_{P}
through a binary cross-entropy loss:

\mathcal{L}^{IV}=\text{BCEloss}\left(\sigma(\bm{M}^{IV}_{S}),\bm{M}^{V}_{P}\right),

(5)

where
\sigma(\cdot)
is the sigmoid function. This optimization is a form of contrastive learning that pulls similar cross-modal patches closer (indicated by
\bm{M}_{P}^{V}=1
) and pushes dissimilar ones apart (indicated by
\bm{M}_{P}^{V}=0
), thereby enhancing semantic alignment and representation.

3.4
Dual-knowledge Preservation Mechanism

To maintain the original RGB (”cones”) functionality while training the infrared (”rods”) pathway, we introduce a
visible modality synchronous distillation loss

\mathcal{L}^{VV}
that prevents catastrophic forgetting:

\mathcal{L}^{VV}=\text{BCEloss}\left(\sigma(\bm{M}^{VV}_{S}),\bm{M}^{V}_{P}\right),

(6)

where
\bm{M}^{VV}_{S}=\text{CosineSimilarity}(\bm{F}^{V},\bm{F}^{Vf})/\tau
measures similarity between
\bm{F}^{V}
and
\bm{F}^{Vf}
. During PCCL training, we simultaneously sample visible images paired with infrared images and feed them into
\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}
and then used to compute a contrastive learning loss similar to Eq.(
5
). This approach ensures that the model remains attentive to feature extraction and representation in the visible modality, thereby preventing catastrophic forgetting.

The total loss combines both objectives:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{PCCL}}=\alpha\cdot\mathcal{L}^{IV}+\beta\cdot\mathcal{L}^{VV},

(7)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
balance cross-modality alignment and visible knowledge preservation.

4.1
Experimental Setup

4.1.1
Datasets

To facilitate PCCL training, we construct the
Multi-scene Visible-Infrared Pair (MVIP)
dataset, comprising 197,984 aligned visible-infrared image pairs (98,992 per modality) curated from five publicly available benchmarks
Hwang et al. 2015
;
FLIR 2019
;
Li et al. 2021
;
Sun et al. 2022
;
Jia et al. 2021
. MVIP spans diverse real-world scenarios, including urban driving, surveillance, and aerial drone views, featuring common objects such as vehicles and pedestrians, making it suitable for cross-modal perception in various environments. To reduce redundancy in sequential data, we apply frame downsampling to ensure a representative subset of samples. Detailed dataset construction procedures are provided in Appendix
A
.

4.1.2
Implementation Details

LoRA configuration:

LoRA adapters with a default rank of 8 and a dropout rate of 0.1 are integrated into the ViT’s feed-forward layers, QKV linear transformations, and projection layers.

Pre-training:

We initialize models (both
\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\text{frozen}}
) with an ImageNet-1K (IN1K)
Deng et al. 2009
pre-trained MCMAE
Gao et al. 2022
(ViT-B backbone), leveraging its strong feature extraction capabilities, and train
\mathcal{E}_{\text{train}}
branch using PCCL on the MVIP dataset to enhance cross-modal feature extraction. Following InfMAE
Liu et al. 2025a
, we train 400 epochs for the LoRA integration version and 200 epochs for full parameter pre-training (to prevent over-fitting) with a batch size of 256. The default loss coefficients are set to
\alpha=\beta=1
, with
\gamma=0.6
and temperature
\tau=0.04
. Further training details can be found in Appendix
B
.

4.2.1
Semantic Segmentation

The results of infrared image semantic segmentation on the MSRS-IR dataset are presented in Table
1
. A key observation is that models trained from scratch consistently underperform compared to pre-trained models, highlighting the importance of pre-training in enhancing a model’s ability to extract modality-invariant semantic features and improve overall comprehension.

Specifically, the MCMAE model pre-trained on IN1K achieves a 2.8 mIoU improvement over the same model pre-trained on Inf30. This suggests that cross-domain fine-tuning, which leverages feature similarities between visible and infrared modalities, can significantly enhance model performance.

Our proposed UNIV model achieves state-of-the-art performance on the MSRS-IR dataset. When pre-trained using LoRA updates, UNIV achieves 76.0 mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA InfMAE by 1.7 mIoU with only 2% training parameters. The full parameter pre-training variant of UNIV achieves 76.6 mIoU, improving 0.6 mIoU over the LoRA variant. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of our PCCL and LoRA pre-training strategy in balancing segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency.

4.2.2
Object Detection

The results of infrared image object detection on the M3FD-IR dataset are shown in Table
1
. The proposed UNIV method, when fine-tuned with LoRA, achieves 56.9 mAP and 88.7 AP50, outperforming the existing state-of-the-art InfMAE by 0.7 mAP and 0.6 AP50, respectively, demonstrating its superior capability in object detection tasks.

Notably, cross-modality fine-tuning of the MCMAE model pre-trained on IN1K also yields competitive results. This suggests that shared features across modalities can be learned to some extent during single-modality pre-training.

However, fully parameter pre-training of UNIV yields suboptimal results (mAP: 56.2 vs. 56.6 for MCMAE on IN1K). Our analysis suggests that UNIV may over-optimize patch similarity during pre-training, neglecting the semantic richness within individual patches. In object detection tasks, where detection boxes frequently overlap, patch information becomes highly complex. UNIV’s full parameter pre-training struggles to capture this complexity, whereas LoRA fine-tuning uses low-rank updates to preserve internal patch semantics while optimizing patch relationships.

4.5.1
How to Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting

Catastrophic forgetting, a phenomenon where learning new concepts interferes with retaining previously learned ones
French 1999
, poses a significant challenge in cross-modal pre-training. Inspired by the retina’s dual-pathway mechanism that preserves modality-specific processing through specialized bipolar cell routing, we propose two key strategies: visible modality synchronous distillation loss
\mathcal{L}^{VV}
and LoRA fine-tuning, which emulates the parameter-efficient plasticity of neural pathways.

We assess the effectiveness of these strategies by fine-tuning UNIV with the UperNet segmentation head on the MSRS-IR and MSRS-RGB datasets. The results, as presented in Table
4.5
, provide key insights into their impact.

Introducing
\mathcal{L}^{IV}
(comparing (a) and (b)) improves the infrared modality’s performance by 1.1 mIoU but slightly degrades RGB performance. This degradation suggests that aligning infrared features with visible ones may introduce minor shifts in the visible feature space. However, adding
\mathcal{L}^{VV}
in (c) restores RGB performance to its baseline level (79.0 mIoU) while further enhancing infrared performance to 76.6 mIoU. This indicates that
\mathcal{L}^{VV}
effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting by maintaining visible modality knowledge during adaptation.

Integrating LoRA in (d) reduces the number of trainable parameters to 1.8M while maintaining competitive performance (IR: 75.8 mIoU, RGB: 78.9 mIoU). The minimal RGB drop suggests that LoRA constrains parameter updates, thereby preserving modality-specific representations. When
\mathcal{L}^{VV}
and LoRA are combined in (e), the model achieves a balanced trade-off, reaching 76.0 mIoU for infrared and maintaining 79.0 mIoU for RGB. This result underscores the complementary nature of
\mathcal{L}^{VV}
and LoRA in mitigating catastrophic forgetting while adapting cross-modal knowledge.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we propose UNIV, a biologically inspired unified foundation model that bridges infrared and visible modalities through mechanisms mirroring the retina’s adaptive vision system. By integrating PCCL (analogous to horizontal cell lateral inhibition) and a dual-knowledge preservation mechanism combining LoRA-based dynamic modality routing (emulating bipolar cell signal routing) with synchronous distillation loss, UNIV achieves seamless cross-modal adaptation while minimizing catastrophic forgetting. Extensive experiments demonstrate UNIV’s dual advantages: (1) state-of-the-art infrared task performance, and (2) near-baseline RGB accuracy (99% of MCMAE’s capability) with only 2% added parameters via LoRA. Ablation studies further validate the efficiency of our bio-inspired design. UNIV provides a computational framework for resource-efficient, biologically plausible perception systems.

Appendix E
LoRA-based Modality Adaptive Routing Pathways Analysis

The implicit modality adaptive routing behavior of LoRA aligns with recent theoretical work on sparse low-rank adaptations. When fine-tuning on multi-modal data, gradient updates naturally induce column-wise sparsity in matrices
\bm{B}
and
\bm{A}
, causing them to develop input-specific activation patterns. This emergent sparsity mirrors the efficiency of biological neural pathways that minimize interference between sensory modalities through dedicated synaptic connections, effectively creating ”soft sub-networks” without explicit architectural changes.

The input-dependent gradient flow in LoRA exhibits striking parallels to neurotransmitter release mechanisms in retinal bipolar cells. Just as bipolar cells modulate signal transmission probabilistically based on photoreceptor input type (rods/cones), the gradients
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\bm{B}
and
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\bm{A}
become modality-specific through training, causing the low-rank matrices to develop directional biases. This biologically plausible property emerges automatically from standard backpropagation, requiring no additional gating mechanisms.

From a representational geometry perspective, the frozen pretrained weights
\bm{W}
span a stable RGB feature space analogous to cone photopic vision, while the low-rank perturbation
\bm{BA}
constructs an orthogonal infrared subspace resembling scotopic rod signaling. The rank constraint
r\ll d
enforces an information bottleneck that prevents catastrophic interference, much like the anatomical separation between rod and cone pathways in the retina. This intrinsic property of low-rank adaptations explains their effectiveness in preserving baseline modality knowledge while enabling dynamic feature adaptation.

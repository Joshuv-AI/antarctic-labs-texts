Adjuvant chemotherapy with or without targeted therapy for patients with resectable synchronous colon cancer and liver or lung-confined metastases: a nationwide Taiwanese database study
Authors: P-Y Chen, Y-H Liang, B-R Lin, C-H Hsu, Y-Y Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe efficacy of targeted therapies in patients with resectable synchronous colon cancer and liver- or lung-confined metastasis (SCCLM) following curative surgery remains unclear.Patients and methodsA population-based cohort of patients with de novo SCCLM diagnosed between 2014 and 2022 was established using data from the Taiwan Cancer Registry and the National Health Insurance Research Database of Taiwan. Patients who underwent curative surgery as initial therapy, followed by chemotherapy doublets with or without targeted therapy, were included, and treatment outcomes were assessed.ResultsA total of 804 patients were included in the analysis. RAS wild-type tumors and left-sided primary colon tumors were identified in 375 (46.6%) and 459 (57.1%) patients, respectively. The liver was the metastatic site in 88.1% of cases. Targeted therapy was administered to 561 (69.8%) patients. Compared with patients receiving chemotherapy alone, those receiving targeted therapy plus chemotherapy had shorter overall survival (OS; median: 3.85 versus 5.24 years, P < 0.001). In the multivariate analysis, the use of targeted therapy was associated with significantly worse OS among patients with left-sided colon cancer [hazard ratio (HR) 1.72, 95% CI 1.07-2.79, P = 0.026] but not among those with right-sided disease (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.51-1.27, P = 0.357). Regardless of tumor sidedness, targeted therapy was not an independent predictor of time to treatment failure (TTF).ConclusionsThe addition of targeted therapies to adjuvant chemotherapy following curative surgery in patients with SCCLM did not improve OS or TTF. This finding suggests that adjuvant chemotherapy alone may be the preferred therapeutic approach in this patient population.Key words: Colon cancer, resectable synchronous colon cancer and liver- or lung-confined metastasis (SCCLM), adjuvant chemotherapy, targeted therapy

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most commonly diagnosed malignancy worldwide and the leading cancer type in Taiwan.1,2 Hepatic metastases are present in ∼15%-20% of patients at diagnosis and develop in an additional 60% during the disease course.3 Although the goal of treatment for metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) is typically palliative, prolonged remission has been reported in patients with resectable colorectal cancer liver metastasis (CRLM) after surgery. Among patients with CRLM, those who underwent resection were reported to achieve better survival outcomes than those who did not.4

Pulmonary metastases occur in ∼10%-15% of all CRC cases. Although large, prospective trials are necessary to determine which subset of patients derive the greatest benefit from lung metastasectomy, current evidence indicates that patients undergoing pulmonary metastasectomy have a 5-year survival rate of ∼40%.5

Approximately 75% of patients who undergo metastasectomy experience disease recurrence within 18 months after the surgery.6 A systematic review and network meta-analysis of seven randomized controlled trials demonstrated that the addition of perioperative systemic treatment for resectable CRLM was associated with improved disease-free survival (DFS), although no benefit was discovered in terms of overall survival (OS).7 According to current National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines, post-operative FOLFOX or CAPEOX is recommended following upfront surgery for resectable synchronous metastases, with the intent of eliminating residual microscopic disease.8 Although the addition of targeted therapy to cytotoxic chemotherapy is the standard first-line treatment for unresectable mCRC, targeted therapy is not recommended after metastasectomy because of the limited evidence supporting its use.9,10

To investigate the role of targeted therapy in the post-operative management of resectable mCRC, we used national databases to evaluate the outcomes in patients who underwent curative surgery for resectable synchronous colon cancer and liver- or lung-confined metastasis (SCCLM). We compared clinical outcomes between patients who received cytotoxic chemotherapy alone and those who received cytotoxic chemotherapy in combination with targeted therapy.

Patients and methods

Data sources

Three national databases were employed in this retrospective population-based cohort study. Data on patient demographics, tumor histology, and disease stage were retrieved from the Taiwan Cancer Registry (TCR), which is managed by the Health Promotion Administration, Ministry of Health and Welfare, Taiwan. All major cancer care providers in Taiwan are mandated to input patient data into the TCR database. Prescription records and details of surgical procedures were retrieved from the Taiwan National Health Insurance (NHI) Research Database. The NHI is a mandatory health insurance system covering >99% of the residents of Taiwan. Mortality data, including date of death, were collected from the National Death Registry. All personal information was encrypted and anonymously analyzed. The identification numbers of all selected cancer beneficiaries from the aforementioned databases were linked in a manner that safeguarded confidentiality, and data analysis was conducted by the Health and Welfare Data Science Centre of the Ministry of Health and Welfare in Taiwan. This study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of National Taiwan University Hospital.

Study population and variables

This study included all patients who met the following criteria: (1) histologically confirmed primary colon cancer (International Classification of Diseases codes C180 to C189) diagnosed between 1 January 2014 and 31 December 2022; (2) age ≥18 years; (3) initial diagnosis of stage IV colon cancer; and (4) receipt of hepatectomy or video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery for metastasectomy and radical resection of the primary tumor, with all surgical procedures carried out no earlier than 1 month and no later than 2 months after diagnosis.

Patients with missing clinical data, a history of radiofrequency ablation for liver tumors, a history of more than one type of primary cancer, or receipt of radiotherapy within the 3 months before or after surgery were excluded from the study. Additionally, patients who had undergone any chemotherapy before surgery, those with a diagnosis of rectal cancer (due to the typical requirement for radiotherapy), and those with recurrent liver metastases from previously treated colon cancer were excluded.

To evaluate the efficacy of post-operative chemotherapy with versus without targeted therapies, only patients who began chemotherapy within the 2 months following one-stage curative surgery (or within the 2 months following the second operation in cases of two-stage surgery) were included. All patients received a doublet chemotherapy regimen comprising 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) plus oxaliplatin or irinotecan. The patients were then categorized into either the chemotherapy-alone group or chemotherapy-plus-targeted-therapy group. The targeted therapies were bevacizumab, cetuximab, and panitumumab, all of which were reimbursed by the NHI upon mCRC diagnosis.

Statistical analysis

The means of continuous variables were compared using the two-samples t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, whereas frequencies of categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. OS was defined as the interval from the date of stage IV colon cancer diagnosis to the date of death or 31 December 2023, whichever occurred first. The data of patients who survived beyond 31 December 2023 were censored at that date. Time to treatment failure (TTF) was defined as the interval from the date of initial diagnosis to the initiation of second-line chemotherapy for mCRC. OS and TTF were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and intergroup differences were assessed using the log-rank test. A multivariate Cox proportional hazards model, stratified by primary tumor sidedness, was used to adjust for age, sex, year of diagnosis, surgery type, disease extent, RAS status, and the use of targeted therapy and chemotherapy regimens. As sensitivity analyses, propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) were carried out using the same covariates included in the multivariate model. All P values were two-sided, and values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were carried out using SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC).

Results

Patient demographics

Of 19 406 patients with de novo stage IV colon cancer, 804 underwent curative surgery for resectable SCCLM (Figure 1). The median patient age was 61 years, and 19.4% were >70 years. The majority of the patients was men (53.2%). RAS wild-type tumors were identified in 375 (46.6%) patients, and the primary tumor was located in the left-sided colon in 459 (57.1%) patients (Table 1). The liver was the predominant site of metastasis (88.1% of cases).

Patient characteristics.

Of the included patients, 243 (30.2%) received chemotherapy alone, whereas 561 (69.8%) received chemotherapy plus targeted therapy after surgery. No significant difference was observed between the two groups in terms of sex, age, primary tumor location, metastasis site, tumor size, carcinoembryonic antigen level >20 ng/mL, or pathological stage (all of P values > 0.05) (Table 1). However, patients who received targeted therapy were more likely to be diagnosed in the later years of the study period (P = 0.030) and to harbor RAS wild-type tumors (P < 0.001).

Survival outcomes

As of 31 December 2023, the median follow-up duration was 3.1 years, and 427 (53.1%) patients had died. Patients who received targeted therapy had a shorter TTF (median: 1.64 versus 1.89 years; P = 0.044; Figure 2A) and shorter OS (median: 3.85 versus 5.24 years; p < 0.001; Figure 2B) than those who did not.

Kaplan–Meier survival curves for the patients with synchronous colon cancer and liver- or lung-confined metastasis following curative surgery. (A) Time to treatment failure and (B) overall survival.

Regarding the patients with right-sided colon cancer, no significant difference in TTF (median: 1.40 versus 1.30 years; P = 0.555; Figure 3A) or OS (median: 3.22 versus 3.35 years; P = 0.701; Figure 3B) was found between those who received targeted therapy and those who did not. By contrast, among the patients with left-sided colon cancer, those treated with targeted therapy had both shorter TTF (median: 1.81 versus 2.85 years; P = 0.006; Figure 3C) and shorter OS (median: 4.22 versus 7.95 years; P < 0.001; Figure 3D) compared with those who received chemotherapy alone.

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis stratified by primary tumor sidedness. (A) TTF and (B) OS for patients with right-sided colon cancer. (C) TTF and (D) OS for patients with left-sided colon cancer. OS, overall survival; TTF, time to treatment failure.

Multivariate analysis was stratified by primary tumor sidedness due to the significant interaction between targeted therapy and tumor sidedness for OS (P = 0.036) but not TTF (P = 0.060). Targeted therapy was not an independent predictor of TTF regardless of tumor sidedness (Table 2). Targeted therapy was an independent predictor of poorer OS in patients with left-sided colon cancer [hazard ratio (HR) 1.72, 95% CI 1.07-2.79, P = 0.026] but not in those with right-sided colon cancer (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.51-1.27, P = 0.357; Table 3). PSM and IPTW were conducted for sensitivity analysis. Targeted therapy remained not associated with TTF in either analysis. OS remained shorter (PSM-adjusted HR 1.68, P = 0.044 and IPTW-adjusted HR 1.67, P = 0.033) among the patients with left-sided colon cancer receiving chemotherapy with targeted therapy (Table 4).

Comparison between types of targeted therapy

Given the association of targeted therapy with inferior TTF and OS among patients with left-sided colon cancer, we conducted a subgroup analysis based on the type of targeted agent used—bevacizumab or anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) antibodies (Figure 4A-D). In this subgroup, those receiving chemotherapy alone had longer TTF (Figure 4C) and OS (Figure 4D) compared with those receiving chemotherapy in combination with either bevacizumab or anti-EGFR therapy.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that the addition of targeted therapy to adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with SCCLM following curative surgery did not improve treatment outcomes but was associated with reduced OS. The adverse impact of targeted therapy was predominantly observed in patients with left-sided colon cancer, regardless of the specific targeted therapy. These findings indicate a detrimental effect of biologic targeted agents in the adjuvant setting following metastasectomy. To our knowledge, this study is the largest cohort study to report such findings.

The current NCCN guidelines recommend post-operative FOLFOX or CAPEOX following upfront surgery for resectable synchronous metastases and advise against the addition of targeted therapy to cytotoxic chemotherapy due to the limited supporting evidence. To date, no clinical trial has evaluated the efficacy of adding anti-EGFR targeted therapy to chemotherapy in patients with SCCLM undergoing upfront surgery. The New EPOC trial demonstrated shorter progression-free survival when cetuximab was added to perioperative chemotherapy (HR 1.50, P < 0.048) in patients with KRAS exon 2 wild-type resectable or suboptimally resectable colorectal liver metastases.11 Subsequent analysis further revealed a reduction in median OS with the addition of cetuximab (HR 1.45, P = 0.036).9 Similarly, the HEPATICA trial, which investigated the addition of bevacizumab to adjuvant chemotherapy following resection of CRLMs, did not demonstrate a significant improvement in DFS (P = 0.89).10 Notably, the New EPOC study was terminated early due to futility, whereas the HEPATICA study was prematurely closed due to slow patient accrual, resulting in insufficient sample sizes and limited follow-up durations in both studies.

A similar retrospective study using data from the TCR (2010-2019) reported comparable OS in patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy with or without targeted therapy following primary tumor excision and metastatectomy.12 However, that study included patients who had received chemotherapy with or without targeted therapy before surgery, thereby complicating the interpretation of post-operative treatment effects. Moreover, bevacizumab and anti-EGFR targeted therapies were not reimbursed by the NHI until December 2012, rendering the study period suboptimal for evaluating the impact of targeted therapy after surgery for SCCLM. By contrast, our study included only patients who received upfront surgery, thereby ensuring a more homogeneous study population.

In patients with localized CRC, the addition of targeted therapies against vascular endothelial growth factors and EGFR to adjuvant chemotherapy has consistently failed to demonstrate clinical benefits. Specifically, the phase 3 PETACC-8 and NCCTG N0147 trials reported no improvement in DFS when cetuximab was added to adjuvant chemotherapy.13,14 Similarly, the NSABP C-08 and AVANT trials demonstrated no DFS advantage from adding bevacizumab to adjuvant chemotherapy.15,16 These results imply that the addition of targeted therapies does not confer benefits in the absence of macroscopic tumor burden. Although patients in our study presented with metastatic disease at diagnosis, systemic therapy was initiated only after curative resection, when no grossly visible tumors remained. This might explain the lack of benefit associated with the addition of targeted therapies in our cohort.

In this study, patients with left-sided tumors had shorter TTF and OS when targeted therapy was added to chemotherapy. This finding leads to the hypothesis that early administration of anti-EGFR antibodies, therapies typically regarded as more effective in left-sided colon cancer, may have contributed to the observed inferior outcomes. However, a subgroup analysis revealed that bevacizumab was associated with similar results. The mechanism by which the addition of targeted therapy exerts a disproportionately greater impact on left-sided tumors warrants further investigation.

Previous research indicates that the prognosis of this patient cohort is intermediate between that of early-stage CRC and unresectable mCRC.17 For patients with resectable synchronous colon cancer and liver- or lung-confined metastases, there is a lack of large-scale clinical trial data recommending the use of adjuvant FOLFOX with or without the addition of targeted therapy. Addressing this evidence gap is the primary focus of the current investigation. In Taiwan, physicians demonstrate a preference for irinotecan-based regimens in this population, frequently utilizing concurrent targeted therapies. Nevertheless, after incorporating the use of irinotecan or oxaliplatin as variables into the multivariate analysis, PSM, and IPTW, the outcomes remained consistent with our primary conclusions against adding targeted therapy regardless of the chemotherapy backbone.

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective study using national Taiwanese databases, the generalizability of our findings to other ethnicities and health care systems may be limited. Detailed clinical information, such as the number of metastatic organs involved, the number of metastatic tumors resected, and patients’ post-operative performance status, was not available. To mitigate potential confounding, we restricted the study population to patients who underwent upfront surgical procedures, employed multivariate analyses, and selected OS as a robust primary endpoint. Despite its limitations, this study revealed a novel finding not previously reported in the literature, which supports recent clinical recommendations for the management of patients with SCCLM following curative surgery.

In conclusion, the addition of targeted therapies to adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with SCCLM after curative surgery did not improve TTF or OS. These findings suggest that adjuvant chemotherapy alone may be the preferred post-operative treatment strategy for this patient population.

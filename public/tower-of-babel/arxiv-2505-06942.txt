Topologically stable manifolds for index- 1 1 singular dominated splittings
Sylvain Crovisier, Dawei Yang

Abstract
For C 2 C^{2} vector fields, we study regular ergodic measures whose supports admit singular dominated splittings with one of the bundles having dimension 1 1 . For such a measure μ \mu , we prove that if any periodic orbit within the support of μ \mu (when it exists) has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent, and if the dynamics on the support of μ \mu is not topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on a 2 2 -torus, then μ \mu -almost every point x x admits a 2 2 -dimensional topologically stable manifold V s ​ ( x ) V^{s}(x) : we mean that V s ​ ( x ) V^{s}(x) is an embedded disc such that the orbit any point within it converges to the orbit of x x up to a time-reparametrization. Note that we do not assume any hyperbolicity for μ \mu . We also establish an analogous conclusion for compact invariant sets Λ \Lambda with a singular dominated splitting, assuming some mild contraction property (any regular ergodic measure properly supported in Λ \Lambda must have at least one negative Lyapunov exponent). This result will be used in our future work on the Palis density conjecture for three-dimensional vector fields.



1
Introduction

Let us assume that
X
is a vector field over a
compact Riemannian manifold without boundary
M
and
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is the flow generated by
X
.

Given a point
x\in M
, one defines its topologically stable set
{\mathfrak{W}}^{s}(x)
: a point
y
belongs to
{\mathfrak{W}}^{s}(x)
if and only if there is an increasing homeomorphism
\theta:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
which satisfies
d(\varphi_{\theta(t)}(y),\varphi_{t}(x))\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
.
Due to the shear of the flow,

{\mathfrak{W}}^{s}(x)
is in general not equal to the stronger stable set
W^{s}(x)
, defined by

W^{s}(x):=\big\{y\in M:~\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(y),\varphi_{t}(x))=0\big\}.

In this paper, we will establish the existence of such topologically stable manifolds for typical points of general regular ergodic measures when the vector field is
C^{2}
and satisfies some weak generic property. We will also require the measure to admit some dominated splitting, but we do not require the measure to be hyperbolic (as in Pesin theory
[
P
]
).

One way to study flows is to intersect the orbits along a transverse section as considered first by Poincaré for the local dynamics near a periodic orbit. For studying the global dynamics, difficulties occur in the presence of
singularities
,
that are the points
\sigma\in M
such that
X(\sigma)=0
.
We denote by
{\rm Sing}(X)
the set of singularities of
X
.
Singularities only give simple dynamics: they are in fact the fixed points of the flow.
The other points are called
regular
points.
Difficulties appear when regular orbits accumulates on singularities,
as it is the case for the famous Lorenz attractor
[
Lo
]
.

The
tangent flow
of
X
, i.e. the derivative with respect to the space variable
D\varphi_{t}:TM\to TM
,
can give some information on the stable sets: for instance any point in a uniformly hyperbolic invariant compact set with no singularity admits a stable manifold, see
[
HPS
]
.
Liao in
[
Li1
]
generalized the above idea of Poincaré on the regular part of the system and introduced the notion of
linear Poincaré flow

(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
At any regular point
x
, one defines the normal space
{\cal N}_{x}=\{v\in T_{x}M:~\left<v,X(x)\right>=0\}
. This gives the normal bundle
{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}
above the regular set of
X
, which is a priori non-compact.
The flow
\psi_{t}:~{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}\to{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}
is defined for any vector
v\in{\cal N}_{x}
with
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
by projecting orthogonally
D\varphi_{t}(v)
on
{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
:

\psi_{t}(v)=D\varphi_{t}(v)-\frac{\left<X(\varphi_{t}(x)),D\varphi_{t}(v)\right>}{\left|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\right|^{2}}X(\varphi_{t}(x)).

Hyperbolic structures on the tangent dynamics, like dominated splittings, have been extensively studied for diffeomorphisms beyond uniform hyperbolicity. They give very weak forms of hyperbolicity, but still robust dynamics. For vector fields, the existence of a dominated splitting for the tangent flow may be a very strong assumption: it sometimes imply uniform contraction or expansion as in
[
BGY
]
.
It is natural to also consider dominated splittings on the normal bundle. As summarized by Bonatti and da Luz
[
BdL
]
: “for flows, hyperbolic structures live on the normal bundle for the linear Poincaré flow, but not on the tangent bundle”.

Let us consider some compact invariant set
\Lambda\subset M
.

A
\psi_{t}
-invariant splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
is said to be
dominated
if there is some time
\tau>0
such that for any
x\in\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
and any unit vectors
v^{cs}\in{\cal N}^{cs}
and
v^{cu}\in{\cal N}^{cu}
, one has

\|\psi_{\tau}.v^{cs}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\psi_{\tau}.v^{cu}\|.

In contrast to the cases of diffeomorphisms and of the tangent flow
(D\varphi_{t})
, dominated splittings of the linear Poincaré flow are in general not robust due to the existence of singularities. For this reason we require the dominated splitting to satisfy a compatibility condition with the singularities.
For instance
[
CdLYZ
, Definition 1.1]
has introduced the notion of
singular domination
, but similar notions appeared before in different works.
In the present paper we consider a definition slightly different from there and focus on the case where

\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{cs})=1
.

Definition 1.1
.

A compact invariant set
\Lambda
is said to admit an
index-1 singular dominated splitting

if there is a dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
with
\operatorname{dim}{\cal N}^{cs}=1
such that at any singularity
\sigma\in\Lambda
(if there exists some):

–

DX(\sigma)
is invertible,

–

there is a dominated splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}\oplus E^{cu}
where
E^{ss}
is one-dimensional and uniformly contracted by the tangent flow,

–

the associated strong stable manifold
W^{ss}(\sigma)
satisfies
W^{ss}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda=\{\sigma\}
.

We now introduce a weak notion of stable manifold at a regular point:

Definition 1.2
.

For any regular point
x
, we say that an embedded
C^{1}
-disc
V^{s}(x)
containing
x
is
topologically stable
if
for any
y\in V^{s}(x)
:

–

X(y)\in T_{y}V^{s}(x)
,

–

there exists an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
\theta\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

d(\varphi_{\theta(t)}(y),\varphi_{t}(x))\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

Let us consider an ergodic measure
\mu
for
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
which is
regular
,
i.e. which satisfies
\mu({\rm Sing}(X))=0
. Note that one may have
{\rm supp}(\mu)\cap{\rm Sing}(X)\neq\emptyset
.
Pesin theory ensures that if one of the Lyapunov exponents of the measure is negative, then
\mu
-almost every point admits a stable manifold. In the next theorem we do not assume information on negative Lyapunov exponents.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary,
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field over
M
and
\mu
be a regular
ergodic measure of the flow generated by
X
. Let us assume that on the support of
\mu
:

1.

there exists an index-
1
singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{{\rm supp}(\mu)\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
,

2.

any periodic orbit (if it exists) has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent,

3.

the dynamics is not topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on a
2
-torus.

Then
\mu
-almost every point
x
admits a
2
-dimensional topologically stable disc
V^{s}(x)

which is tangent to
{\cal N}^{cs}(x)\oplus X(x)
.

A more precise version stated in Section
4
provides some uniformity on the discs
V^{s}(x)
.
Note also that for generic vector fields, if
\mu
is not supported on a source, then it always satisfy
conditions (
2
) and (
3
).

The statement is non-trivial even when
\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)
contains no singularity.
When it contains a singularity, the induced dynamics cannot be topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on
a
2
-torus and the following corollary holds.

Corollary B
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary,
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field over
M
and
\mu
be a regular ergodic measure of the flow generated by
X
. Let us assume that on the support of
\mu
:

–

there exists an index-
1
singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{{\rm supp}(\mu)\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
,

–

any periodic orbit (if it exists) has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent.

If the support of
\mu
contains a singularity, then

\mu
-almost every point
x
admits a
2
-dimensional topologically stable disc
V^{s}(x)

which is tangent to
{\cal N}^{cs}(x)\oplus X(x)
.

The following analogous result holds for compact invariant sets when any proper compact invariant subset satisfies the contraction property.

Theorem C
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian compact manifold without boundary,
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field over
M
and
\Lambda
be a compact set invariant by the flow generated by
X
which is not reduced to a periodic orbit.
Let us assume that :

–

there exists an index-
1
singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
,

–

any regular ergodic measure
\mu
(if there exists) whose support is a proper subset of
\Lambda
has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent,

–

the dynamics is not topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on a
2
-torus.

Then any point
x\in\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
admits a
2
-dimensional topologically stable disc
V^{s}(x)

which is tangent to
{\cal N}^{cs}(x)\oplus X(x)
.

Theorem
C
and its more precise version stated in Section
4

will be used in a future work to prove a conjecture of Palis for three-dimensional vector fields
[
CY1
]
.
This was one of the main motivations for this paper.

Our results and their proofs continue and generalize previous works:
for one-dimensional maps by Mañé
[
M1
]
, surface diffeomorphisms by Pujals and Sambarino
[
PS1
]
,
higher-dimensional diffeomorphisms with a contractive codimension-one dominated splitting
[
PS2
,
PS3
,
CP
]
,
and non-singular three-dimensional vector fields by Arroyo and Rodriguez-Hertz
[
ARH
]
.
Note that Theorems
A
and
C
have counterparts for diffeomorphisms in any dimension; in this case a stronger version by Pujals, Sambarino and the first author has been announced in
[
C
, Theorem 4.4]
.

As mentioned above, in our proofs we will have to deal with the singularities.
Therefore we will have to rescale the flow (as in
[
Li2
,
GY
]
), to consider blow-ups, and to use an abstract model for the non-linear Poincaré flow introduced in our earlier work
[
CY2
]
. Note that our way to construct normally expanded irrational torus (in Section
3.5
) is different from previous approaches: we use more local arguments, whereas Pujals and Sambarino
[
PS1
]
and Arroyo and Rodriguez-Hertz
[
ARH
]
used more semi-global arguments.

2
Fibered dynamics with a dominated splitting

We will work with the nonlinear Poincaré flow and its compactification which has been built in our previous paper
[
CY2
]
.
It can be seen as a local fibered flow with an identification structure as we recall here.
In this section, we will also discuss the dynamics of local fibered flow preserving a dominated splitting.

2.1
Local fibered flow and identifications

The definitions come from
[
CY2
, Definition 1.1, Definition 1.2]
.

2.1.1
Definition of local fibered flows

Definition 2.1

(Local fibered flow)
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be a continuous flow over a compact metric space
K
, and let
{\cal N}\to K
be a continuous Riemannian vector bundle.
A
local
C^{k}
-fibered flow

P
on
{\cal N}
is a continuous family of
C^{k}
-diffeomorphisms
P_{t}\colon{\cal N}_{x}\to{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
, for
(x,t)\in K\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
preserving the
0
-section of
{\cal N}
with the following property.

There is
\beta_{0}>0
such that for each
x\in K
,
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
u\in{\cal N}_{x}
satisfying

\|P_{s.t_{1}}(u)\|\leq\beta_{0}\text{ and }\|P_{s.t_{2}}(P_{t_{1}}(u))\|\leq\beta_{0}\text{ for each }s\in[0,1],

then we have

P_{t_{1}+t_{2}}(u)=P_{t_{2}}\circ P_{t_{1}}(u).

We consider local fibered flows as in Definition
2.1
. The following notations will be used.

Notations.
– One sometimes denotes a point
u\in{\cal N}_{x}
as
u_{x}
to emphasize the base point
x
.

– The length of a
C^{1}
curve
\gamma\subset{\cal N}_{x}
(with respect to the metric of
{\cal N}_{x}
)
is denoted by
|\gamma|
.

– A ball centered at
u
and with radius
r
inside a fiber
{\cal N}_{x}
is denoted by
B(u,r)
.

– For
x\in K
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in{\cal N}_{x}
, one denotes
by
DP_{t}(u)
the derivative of
P_{t}
at
u
along
{\cal N}_{x}
.
In particular
(DP_{t}(0_{x}))_{t\in{\mathbb{R}},x\in K}
defines a linear flow over the
0
-section of
{\cal N}
.

2.1.2
Definition of identifications

Definition 2.2
.

Let us consider a
local
C^{k}
-fibered flow

P
on
{\cal N}\to K

over a continuous flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
over a compact metric space
K
.
A
C^{k}
-identification

\pi

is a continuous family of
C^{k}
-diffeomorphisms
\pi_{y,x}\colon{\cal N}_{y}\to{\cal N}_{x}

and

with

and

\pi_{z,x}\circ\pi_{y,z}(u)=\pi_{y,x}(u).

In particular
\pi_{x,x}
coincides with the identity on

.

Notations.
– We will sometimes denote
\pi_{y,x}

by
\pi_{x}
. Also the projection
\pi_{y,x}(0)=\pi_{x}(0_{y})
of

\pi_{x}(y)
.

– We will denote by

Definition 2.3
.

The identification
\pi
on

is

with the flow
(P_{t})
if:

1.

No small period.

For any
\varkappa>0
, there is
r>0
such that for any
x\in\overline{U}

and

with

, then we have
|t|<\varkappa
.

2.

Local injectivity.
For any
\delta>0
, there exists
\beta>0
such that for any
x,y\in U
:

if

and
\|\pi_{x}(y)\|\leq\beta
, then

for some
t\in[-1/4,1/4]
.

3.

Local invariance.
For any
x,y\in U

and
t\in[-2,2]
such that
y
and
\varphi_{t}(y)
are
r_{0}
-close to

,
and for any

\pi_{x}\circ P_{t}(u)=\pi_{x}(u).

4.

Global invariance.

For any
\delta,\rho>0
, there exists
r,\beta>0
such that:

For any
y,y^{\prime}\in K
with
y\in U
and
d(y,y^{\prime})<r
,
for any
u\in{\cal N}_{y}
,
u^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{y^{\prime}}
with
\pi_{y}(u^{\prime})=u
,
and any intervals
I,I^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
containing

and satisfying

\|P_{s}(u)\|<\beta\text{ and }\|P_{s^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})\|<\beta\text{ for any }s\in I\text{ and any }s^{\prime}\in I^{\prime},

there is
\theta\in{\rm Lip}_{1+\rho}
such that
\theta(0)=0
and

for any

.
Moreover, if some
v\in{\cal N}_{y}
satisfies [
\forall s\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
,

\|P_{s}(v)\|<\beta
], then:

–

v^{\prime}=\pi_{y^{\prime}}(v)
satisfies

\|P_{\theta(s)}(v^{\prime})\|<\delta
for each
s\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
;

–

if
\varphi_{s}(y)\in U

for some
s\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
, then
\pi_{\varphi_{s}(y)}\circ P_{\theta(s)}(v^{\prime})=P_{s}(v)
.

Remarks 2.4
.

a) These definitions are still satisfied if one reduces

or

. Their value will be reduced in the following sections in order to

b) One can also rescale the time and keep a compatible identification: the flow

.

c) Applying a rescaling as discussed in the item (b),

.

.

, one can assume the following property:

and any

, we have

.

e) For

, the Local injectivity prevents the existence of

that is

-close to

,

\varphi_{t}(x)
for any

, and

\pi_{x}(y)=0_{x}
.

of the local fibered flow are close to

,

where the condition

has been replaced by a weaker one

. In particular, this gives:

For any

, there exist
\beta>0
such that:

,

,

and the intervals

containing

–

,

–

\pi_{y}(u^{\prime})=u
,

\|P_{s}(u)\|<\beta\text{ and }\|P_{s^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})\|<\beta\text{ for any }s\in I\text{ and any }s^{\prime}\in I^{\prime},

2.1.3
Properties of identifications

The next property (
[

of a same orbit, that shadow each other, coincide for some parameter and differ by at least
2
for another parameter.

(No shear inside orbits)
.

Let us assume that there exists a
C^{k}
identification on an open set
U\subset K

which is compatible with the local fibered flow
(P_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

and that the set
\Delta:=\{x|\forall|t|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\;\varphi_{t}(x)\notin U\}

is disjoint from
\overline{U}
. If
r_{0}>0
is small, then
for any
x\in U
, any increasing homeomorphism
\theta
of
{\mathbb{R}}
, any closed interval
I
containing
0
satisfying
[d(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x))\leq r_{0},\;\forall t\in I]
and
\varphi_{\theta(0)}(x)\in U
,
the following holds:

–

If
\theta(0)>1/2
, then
\theta(t)>t+2
,
\forall t\in I
when
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
;

–

If
\theta(0)\in[-2,2]
, then
\theta(t)\in[t-1/2,t+1/2]
,
\forall t\in I
when
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U

,

.

Lemma 2.6

(Closing lemma)
.

–

the sequence

2.2
Dominated splitting of a local fibered flow

2.2.1
Definition of a dominated splitting

Definition 2.8
.

The local flow
(P_{t})
admits a
dominated splitting

{\cal N}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
if
{\cal E}
,
{\cal F}
are sub-bundles of
{\cal N}
that are invariant
by the linear flow
(DP_{t}(0))
and if there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that
for any
x\in K
, for any unit vector
u\in{\cal E}(x)
and unit vector
v\in{\cal F}(x)
and for any

we have:

\|DP_{t}(0_{x}).u\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|DP_{t}(0_{x}).v\|.

Moreover we say that
{\cal E}
is
2
-dominated
if there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in K
,
any unit vector
u\in{\cal E}(x)
and any unit vector
v\in{\cal F}(x)
, and for any

we have:

\max(\|DP_{t}(0_{x}).u\|,\|DP_{t}(0_{x}).u\|^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|DP_{t}(0_{x}).v\|.

When there exists a dominated splitting
{\cal N}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
and
V\subset K
is an open subset,
one can prove that
{\cal E}
is uniformly contracted by considering the induced dynamics on
K\setminus V

and checking that the following property is satisfied.

Definition 2.9
.

The bundle
{\cal E}
is
uniformly contracted on the open set

if
there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in K
satisfying
\varphi_{t}(x)\in V

for any

we have:

\|DP_{t_{0}}{|{\cal E}(x)}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

We say that
{\cal E}
is
uniformly contracted
if it is uniformly contracted on
K
.

2.2.2
Normally expanded irrational tori

We give a setting of
a dominated splitting
{\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
such that

is not uniformly contracted.

Definition 2.10
.

A
normally expanded irrational torus
is
an invariant compact subset

such that

–

the dynamics of
\varphi|_{{\cal T}}

is topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on

–

there exists a dominated splitting
{\cal N}|_{{\cal T}}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
and

–

for some

and

-curve

The name is justified as follows.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any normally expanded irrational torus

,
the Lyapunov exponent along
{\cal E}
of the (unique) invariant measure of
\varphi
on
{\cal T}

is equal to zero; in particular
{\cal F}
is uniformly expanded (i.e uniformly

Remark 2.12
.

With the technics of Section
3
, one
can also prove that the
\alpha
-limit set of any point

in a neighborhood
coincides with
{\cal T}
.

Proof.

Let us choose a global transversal
\Sigma\simeq{\mathbb{T}}^{1}
containing
x

for the restriction of
\varphi
to
{\cal T}
. The dynamics is conjugated to a suspension
of an irrational rotation of
\Sigma
.
We consider the sequence

of positive returns of the orbit of

inside a neighborhood
of

in

For every

In particular by choosing

2.2.3
Contraction on periodic orbits and criterion for
2
-domination

When there exists a dominated splitting
{\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
where
{\cal E}
is one-dimensional,
the uniform contraction of the bundle
{\cal E}
above each periodic orbit of
K
,
implies that it is
2
-dominated.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let us assume that

–

there exists a dominated splitting
{\cal N}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
and the fibers of
{\cal E}
are one-dimensional,

–

{\cal E}
is uniformly contracted on an open set
V
containing

.

Then either the bundle
{\cal E}
is
2
-dominated, or there exists a periodic orbit

{\cal O}
in
K
whose Lyapunov exponents are all positive.

Proof.

If there is no
2
-domination, there exists a sequence
(x_{n})
in
K
,
such that

\|DP_{n}{|_{{\cal E}(x_{n})}}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{v\in{\cal F}(x_{n}),\|v\|=1}\|DP_{n}(v)\|.

One can extract a
\varphi
-invariant measure from the sequence

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\int_{t=0}^{n}\delta_{\varphi_{t}(x_{n})}\;dt

and the maximal Lyapunov exponent
\lambda^{\cal E}
along
{\cal E}
, the minimal Lyapunov exponent
\lambda^{\cal F}

along
{\cal F}
satisfy

2\lambda^{\cal E}\geq\lambda^{\cal F}
.
In particular,
\lambda^{\cal F}>\lambda^{\cal E}\geq\lambda^{\cal F}-\lambda^{\cal E}>0
.
Since
{\cal E}
is one-dimensional,
one deduces that
\varphi
admits an ergodic measure

whose Lyapunov exponents are both positive.
Since
{\cal E}
is uniformly contracted on

, the support of

has to intersect
U
.
For
\mu
-almost every point
x\in K
, there exists a neighborhood

of
0
in
{\cal N}_{x}
such that
\|(DP_{-t})|_{V_{x}}\|
decreases exponentially
as

. In particular, one can take
x\in U
recurrent
and find a large time

such that

\widetilde{P}_{-T}:=\pi_{x}\circ P_{-T}
sends

V_{x}
into itself as a contraction.
By Corollary
2.7
,
there is a periodic point
y
in
K
with some period

and a fixed point

for
\widetilde{P}_{-T}

such that the tangent map

is conjugate to the tangent map

).
Hence the Lyapunov exponents of

∎

–

.

There is

•

Plaque family for fibered flows

The center-stable plaques are defined on the positively invariant set

with respect to the induced metric on
{\cal N}_{x}
and we denote by

{\cal W}^{cs}=\{{\cal W}^{cs}(u),u\in K_{\eta}^{+}\}

2.3.3

The proof of Theorems

It is a consequence of a more general result that we state now.
We denote by

For any

There is no uniqueness in Theorem

Also the following classical lemma holds.

Proposition 2.19

In the setting of Theorem

2.3.6
Limit dynamics in periodic fibers

We state a consequence of the existence of plaque families. It will be used for the center-unstable plaques
{\cal W}^{cu}
.
Note that any
u\in{\cal N}
such that
\|P_{-t}(u)\|
is small for any
t\geq 0
has a plaque
{\cal W}^{cu}(u)
by Theorem
2.16
.

Proposition 2.22
.

For any local fibered flow
(P_{t})
on a bundle
{\cal N}
admitting a dominated splitting
{\cal N}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}

where
{\cal E}
is one-dimensional, if
\eta>0
is small enough the following property holds.

For any periodic point
z\in K
with period
T
and any
u\in K^{-}_{\eta}\cap{\cal N}_{z}
satisfying
0_{z}\not\in{\cal W}^{cu}(u)
,
there exists
p\in{\cal N}_{z}
such that
P_{2T}(p)=p
and
P_{-t}(u)
converges to the orbit of
p

when
t
goes to
+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{\cal E},\alpha_{\cal F}
be the constants associated to
{\cal E},{\cal F}
as in Theorem
2.16
.
The plaques
{\cal W}^{cu}(u)
and
{\cal W}^{cs}(0_{z})

intersect at a (unique) point
y
.

Since
\|u\|
is small, by the local invariance of the plaque families,
y
is also
the intersection between
P_{1}({\cal W}^{cs}_{\alpha_{\cal E}}(\varphi_{-1}(z)))
and

{\cal W}^{cu}_{\alpha_{\cal F}}(u)
.
One deduces that
P_{-1}(y)

is the (unique) intersection point between
the plaques
{\cal W}^{cu}(P_{-1}(u))
and
{\cal W}^{cs}(\varphi_{-1}(z))
.
Repeating this argument inductively,
one deduces that the backward orbit of
y
by
P_{-1}

remain in the plaques
{\cal W}^{cu}(P_{-k}(u))
and
{\cal W}^{cs}(\varphi_{-k}(z))
.
From Remark
2.20
, any backward iterate
P_{-t}(y)

belongs to
{\cal W}^{cu}(P_{-t}(u))
.

One has that
y\neq 0_{z}
by the fact that
0_{z}\neq{\cal W}^{cu}(u)
. The domination implies that
the distance
d(P_{-k}(u),P_{-k}(y))
is exponentially smaller than

d(P_{-k}(y),0_{\varphi_{-k}(z)})
as
k\to+\infty
.
So one has that
d(P_{-k}(u),P_{-k}(y))
goes to
0
.
The same argument applied to
P_{-s}(u)
,

s\in[0,1]
shows that the distance of
P_{-t}(u)

to
{\cal W}^{cs}(\varphi_{-t}(z))
converges to
0
as
t\to+\infty
.
In particular, the limit set of the orbit of
u
under
P_{-2T}

is a closed subset
L
of
{\cal W}^{cs}(z)
.
In the case
L
is a single point
p
, the conclusion of the proposition follows.

We assume now by contradiction that
L
is not a single point.
There exists
q\neq 0_{z}
invariant by
P_{2T}

in
L
such that
L
intersects the
open arc
\gamma
in
{\cal W}^{cs}(z)
bounded by
0_{z}
and
q
.
Note that the forward iterates
P_{k}(\gamma)
by
P_{1}
remain small,
hence in
{\cal W}^{cs}(\varphi_{k}(z))
by the local invariance of
{\cal W}^{cs}
.
From Remark
2.20
, any iterate

P_{t}(\gamma)
is contained in
{\cal W}^{cs}(\varphi_{t}(z))
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Up to replacing
u
by a backward iterate, one can assume that
y
belongs to
\gamma
.
This shows that
P_{-t}(y)
is the intersection between

{\cal W}^{cu}(P_{-t}(u))
and
{\cal W}^{cs}(P_{-t}(z))
for any
t\geq 0
.
The set
L
is thus the limit set of the orbit of
y
under
P_{-2T}
.
This reduces to a one-dimensional dynamics
for an orientation preserving diffeomorphism,
and
L
has to be a single point, a contradiction.
∎

(3)

P_{t}^{*}(u)=\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|^{-1}\cdot P_{t}(\|X(x)\|\cdot u).

One has the following result from
[

]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Compactification)
.

.

from

We blow up every singularity

4.2
The lifted local fiber flow with dominated structures

When the compact invariant set admits a singular dominated splitting, one can get more accurate properties for the lifted local fiber flow.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a
C^{k}
-vector field,
k\geq 1
, over a compact manifold
M
.
Let
\Lambda\subset M
be a compact set which is invariant by the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

associated to
X
such that each singularity
\sigma\in\Lambda
is hyperbolic.
Let
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
\widehat{\Lambda}
, the local
C^{k}
-fibered flow
({\widehat{P}}_{t}^{*})
, the vector bundle
\widehat{{\cal N}}
, the maps
i
and
I
be as in the statement of Theorem
4.1
.
And let us assume that
\Lambda
admits an index-
1
singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}|_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
.

Then
\widehat{\Lambda}
admits a dominated splitting
\widehat{\cal N}|_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
w.r.t. the extended linear Poincaré flow with
\operatorname{dim}(\widehat{\cal N}^{cs})=1

and there is a compact neighborhood
\widehat{V}
of
\widehat{\Lambda}\setminus i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
such that
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}
is uniformly contracted in
\widehat{V}
with respect to the extended linear Poincaré flow.

Moreover, one has the following properties:

•

For any regular periodic orbit
{\cal O}\subset\Lambda
, if
{\cal O}
has only negative Lyapunov exponents along
{\cal N}^{cs}
, then
\widehat{\cal O}=i({\cal O})
has only negative Lyapunov exponent along along
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}
for
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
.

•

If
\Lambda
contains no normally expanded irrational torus, then
\widehat{\Lambda}
does not contain any normally expanded irrational torus.

Proof.

We prove successively all the properties of the theorem.

(a) The extension of the dominated splitting.

Through the map
I
, the bundles
{\cal N}^{cs}
and
{\cal N}^{cu}
can be lifted over
i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
as bundles
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}
and
\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
. This defines a dominated splitting
\widehat{\cal N}=\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
over
\widehat{\Lambda}
for the extended rescaled linear Poincaré flow
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
(hence for
\widehat{P}^{*}
).
Indeed:

–

dominated splittings are preserved by rescaling, hence
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
is a dominated splitting for

\widehat{\psi}^{*}
over
i(\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X))
;

–

for continuous linear cocycles, dominated splittings extend to the closure.

(b) The existence of uniformly contracted neighborhood
\widehat{V}
.

We only consider the case where
\Lambda
does contain singularities, since otherwise
\widehat{\Lambda}\setminus i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))=\emptyset
and the conclusion will be trivial.
There exists a continuous projection
p\colon\widehat{\Lambda}\to\Lambda
.

Each singularity
\sigma\in\Lambda
admits a splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}(\sigma)\oplus E^{cu}(\sigma)
with
\operatorname{dim}E^{ss}(\sigma)=1
by definition of index-
1
singular domination. Let
\Delta_{\sigma}
be the set of unit vectors
u\in{E^{cu}(\sigma)}\subset T_{\sigma}M
.
The set
\widehat{\Lambda}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}
is compact and
\widehat{\varphi}
-invariant.

For each
u\in\widehat{\Lambda}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}
the space
\widehat{\cal N}_{u}
is the orthogonal space of

DX(\sigma)\cdot u
inside
T_{\sigma}M
. The image by the flow
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
of any
v\in\widehat{\cal N}_{u}

can be obtained as the orthogonal projection of
D\varphi_{t}(\sigma)\cdot v
to
\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\varphi}_{t}(u)}
.
Since
D\varphi_{t}(\sigma)
preserves the splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}\oplus{E^{cu}}
,
and since
DX(\sigma)\cdot u
has a uniform angle with
E^{ss}(\sigma)
, the orthogonal projection of this splitting
induces a splitting
\widehat{\cal N}_{u}=\widehat{\cal E}(u)\oplus\widehat{\cal F}(u)
above
\Delta_{\sigma}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}

which is invariant by
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
such that
{\cal E}(u)
is one dimensional and uniformly contracted by the flow
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
.

On
\widehat{\Lambda}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}
, the dominated splittings
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
and
\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}
have the same dimensions,
hence coincide. Moreover since
W^{ss}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda=\{\sigma\}
, we have
p^{-1}(\sigma)\cap\widehat{\Lambda}=\Delta_{\sigma}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}
,
where
p
denotes the projection
\widehat{\Lambda}\to\Lambda
.
This shows that
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}
is uniformly contracted by
\widehat{P}^{*}
over
\widehat{\Lambda}\setminus i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
,
hence on any small neighborhood
\widehat{V}
.

(c) Lyapunov exponents of periodic orbits.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any regular periodic orbit
{\cal O}
in
\Lambda
, the
Lyapunov exponents of
{\cal O}
for
\psi
and the Lyapunov exponents of
\widehat{\cal O}=i({\cal O})
for

\widehat{\psi}^{*}
coincides.

Proof.

Indeed the two linear flows are conjugated by the (derivative of the) map
I
.
∎

(d) Normally expanded irrational tori.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
{\cal T}\subset\widehat{\Lambda}
is a normally expanded irrational torus,
then
p({\cal T})
is an invariant torus which carries a dynamics topologically equivalent to an irrational flow.

Proof.

Let
{\cal T}\subset\widehat{\Lambda}
be an expanded irrational torus
{\cal T}
.
By Lemma
2.11
, the bundle
\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
is uniformly expanded over
{\cal T}
.
Since it does not contain fixed points of
\widehat{\varphi}
, it projects by
p
in
\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
.
By construction, the dynamics of
\widehat{\psi}^{*}
over
{\cal T}
and
\psi^{*}
over
p({\cal T})
are the same,
hence
{\cal N}^{cu}
is uniformly expanded over
p({\cal T})
by
\psi^{*}
.
Since
p({\cal T})\cap{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)=\emptyset
, one deduces that
{\cal N}^{cu}
is uniformly expanded over
p({\cal T})
by
\psi
.
It follows from a standard argument that the tangent flow over
p({\cal T})
has a dominated
splitting
TM|_{p({\cal T})}=E^{c}\oplus E^{uu}
, with
\operatorname{dim}(E^{c})=2
, see
[
GY
, Lemma 2.13]
for instance.

By partial hyperbolicity, each
x\in p({\cal T})
has a strong unstable manifold
W^{uu}(x)
tangent to
E^{uu}(x)
.
Note that
W^{uu}(x)\cap p({\cal T})=\{x\}
(since the dynamics is topologically equivalent to
an irrational flow). Then
[
BC
]
implies that
p({\cal T})
is contained in a two-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma

transverse to
E^{uu}
and locally invariant by
\varphi_{1}
: there exists a neighborhood
U
of
p({\cal T})
in
\Sigma
such that

\varphi_{1}(U)\subset\Sigma
. Since
p({\cal T})
is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, it is open and closed in
\Sigma
, hence coincides with
\Sigma
.
This shows that
p({\cal T})
is
C^{1}
-diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, normally expanded
and carries a dynamics topologically equivalent to an irrational flow.
∎

Theorem
4.2
is now proved.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
A

We state and prove a more precise version of Theorem
A
.
We first introduce a notion of stable manifold adapted to a set with a normal dominated structure.

Definition 4.5

(Normal stable manifold)
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is a
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant set with a singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda}={\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
. Given
x\in\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
, a manifold
V^{s}(x)\subset{\cal N}(x)
is said to be a
normal stable manifold tangent to
{\cal N}^{cs}(x)
, if:

•

it contains
x
and is tangent to
{\cal N}^{cs}(x)
at
x
;

•

P_{t}(V^{s})\subset{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
for any
t>0
and
{\rm Diam}(P_{t}(V^{s}))\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Definition
4.5
gives a generalization of the stable manifold of the return map to a cross section at a periodic point.
In order to have uniformity with respect to the base point
x
, we introduce the following definition
(compare with Definition
2.15
).

Definition 4.6

(Normal plaque family)
.

Let
\Lambda
be an invariant compact set with a singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
.
A
C^{k}
normal plaque family
tangent to
{\cal E}
over an invariant set
Y\subset\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)

is a continuous family of
C^{k}
-diffeomorphisms onto their image
\Theta_{x}\colon{\cal E}(x)\to{\cal N}_{x}
,
x\in Y
,
such that
\Theta_{x}(0)=x
and the image is tangent to
{\cal E}(x)
. We denote by

{\cal W}^{\cal E}(x),{\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\delta}(x)
the images
\Theta_{x}({\cal E}(x)),\Theta_{x}(B(0,\delta))
.

It is locally invariant if there exists
\alpha>0
such that for any
x\in Y
we have

P_{1}^{*}\bigg({\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)\subset{\cal W}^{\cal E}(\varphi_{t}(x)).

Definition 4.7

(Family of normal stable manifolds)
.

Let
\Lambda
be an invariant compact set with a singular dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
.
A
continuous family of normal stable manifolds
(tangent to
{\cal E}
) over an invariant set
Y\subset\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)

is a locally invariant
C^{1}
-plaque family tangent to
{\cal E}
such that for any

\rho>0
, there is
\alpha>0
with the following properties:

P_{t}^{*}\bigg({\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)\subset{\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\rho}(\varphi_{t}(x))\text{ and }\operatorname{Diam}\bigg(P_{t}^{*}({\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\alpha}(x))\bigg)\underset{t\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

In particular each set
{\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\alpha}(x)
is a normal stable manifold.

Remark
.

Note that after rescaling, for any
x\in Y

the diameter of the iterates of the plaque
{\cal W}^{\cal E}_{\alpha\|X(x)\|}(x)
under the Poincaré flow
(P_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

decreases to
0
as
t\to+\infty
.

Theorem A’
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary,
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field over
M
and
\mu
be a regular ergodic measure of the flow generated by
X
. Let us assume that on the support of
\mu
:

–

there exists an index-
1
singular dominated splitting

,

–

any (regular) periodic orbit (if it exists) has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent,

–

the dynamics is not topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on a
2
-torus.

Then there exists a continuous family of normal stable manifolds
{\cal W}^{cs}_{\alpha}(x)
over a set with full
\mu
-measure.

In order to build the stable disc

in Theorem
A
,
we first project the rescaled normal stable manifold to
M
by the exponential map at
x

and then flow it by
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
during a small interval of time
[-\epsilon,\epsilon]
, that is:

V^{s}(x)=\varphi_{[-\epsilon,\epsilon]}(\exp_{x}({\cal W}^{cs}_{\alpha\|X(x)\|}(x))).

Proof.

Let us set
\Lambda={\rm supp}(\mu)
.
Note that if
\mu
is supported by a periodic orbit, the conclusion holds trivially since the periodic orbit has a negative Lyapunov exponent by assumption. We will thus assume that
\mu
is not supported on a periodic orbit.

By Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, there exist a topological flow
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, a compact metric space
\widehat{\Lambda}
, a local
C^{2}
-fibered flow
({\widehat{P}}^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
over a Riemannian vector bundle
\widehat{{\cal N}}\to\widehat{\Lambda}
, some conjugacies
i
,
I
; since
\Lambda
admits an index-
1
singular dominated splitting

{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus{\cal N}^{cu}
, the set
\widehat{\Lambda}
admits a dominated splitting
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{cu}
, and there is a small neighborhood
\widehat{V}
of
\widehat{\Lambda}\setminus i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
such that the bundle
\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}
is uniformly contracted on
\widehat{V}
by
({\widehat{P}}^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

,

and there is no normally hyperbolic irrational torus in
\widehat{\Lambda}
.

By Theorem
4.1
, there is

and an identification
\widehat{\pi}

\widehat{U}
satisfying

Now we consider the lift of the measure

Proof of Theorem
C

We state and prove a more precise version of Theorem
C
:

Theorem C’
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian compact manifold without boundary,
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field over
M
and
\Lambda
be a compact set invariant by the flow generated by

–

there exists an index-

,

–

any regular ergodic measure

(if there exists) whose support is a proper subset of
\Lambda
has at least one negative Lyapunov exponent,

–

the dynamics is not topologically equivalent to an irrational flow on a
2
-torus.

As in the proof of Theorem
Theorem A’
,
we introduce
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
\widehat{\Lambda}
,
({\widehat{P}}^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
over
\widehat{{\cal N}}\to\widehat{\Lambda}
,

\widehat{V}
of
\widehat{\Lambda}\setminus i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))

\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}

\widehat{\cal N}^{cs}

. there is

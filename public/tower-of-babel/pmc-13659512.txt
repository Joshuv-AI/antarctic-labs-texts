Overcoming immune exclusion in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: mechanistic insights from the CASTLE01 trial
Authors: Takahiro Yamasaki, Yutaka Suehiro, Issei Saeki, Maho Egusa, Norikazu Tanabe, Taro Takami
Published: 2026

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) remains a highly treatment-refractory malignancy, characterized by a dense desmoplastic stroma, aberrant vasculature, and an immune-excluded tumor microenvironment that prevents effector immune cells from accessing the tumor core (1). Although the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) to gemcitabine plus cisplatin has recently improved first-line outcomes (2,3), therapeutic options after disease progression remain limited. Against this background, the CASTLE-01 trial introduced a novel therapeutic strategy combining cryoablation with the anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody sintilimab and the tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) lenvatinib, suggesting a shift in the treatment paradigm (4). This approach not only demonstrated unexpectedly high clinical efficacy and offered potential mechanistic insights into strategies for overcoming the immune-excluded phenotype characteristic of ICC.

CASTLE-01 was a single-arm phase II trial that enrolled 28 patients with advanced or metastatic ICC who had progressed after chemotherapy (Table 1). Cryoablation was performed on day 0, followed by initiation of lenvatinib (8 or 12 mg daily) plus sintilimab (200 mg every 3 weeks) 2 weeks later. Treatment was continued for up to 24 months or until progressive disease (PD) or unacceptable toxicity occurred. This regimen achieved an objective response rate (ORR) of 75% [95% confidence interval (CI): 59‒91%] and a disease control rate of 100% (95% CI: 100‒100%). The median time to response was 3.6 months (95% CI: 2.6‒6.2), and the median duration of response was 17.0 months [95% CI: 7.9‒not reached (NR)]. The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 16.8 months (95% CI: 11.5‒NR), and the median overall survival (OS) was 25.4 months (95% CI: 13.3‒NR). These outcomes substantially exceed those reported for conventional second-line therapies for biliary tract cancer, such as FOLFOX [folinic acid (LV), fluorouracil (FU), and oxaliplatin] in the ABC-06 study (ORR, 4.9%; median PFS, 4.0 months; median OS, 6.2 months) (5) or liposomal irinotecan plus FU/LV in the NIFTY study (ORR, 12.5%; median PFS, 4.2 months; median OS, 8.6 months) (6). These toxicities were manageable and aligned with the known profiles of lenvatinib and PD-1 blockade. Collectively, these findings suggest a synergistic therapeutic effect that cannot be attributed to any single component alone.

A major strength of the CASTLE-01 trial is its comprehensive investigation of the underlying immunologic mechanisms through post hoc multi-omics analyses of paired pre-treatment and on-treatment tumor biopsies. These analyses delineated a sequential cascade, beginning with local tumor destruction by cryoablation, followed by enhanced immunogenicity, the transition of endothelial cells into inflamed venules, stromal remodeling, and ultimately, infiltration of effector T cells. Taken together, these changes progressively convert the immune-excluded microenvironment of ICC into one that permits effective antitumor immunity. Cryoablation serves as an immunological priming event by promoting the release of tumor antigens, augmenting antigen presentation, and activating dendritic cells, thereby facilitating the recruitment of CD8+ PD1hi effector T cells to distant tumor sites. However, these T cells remained confined to the peritumoral stroma and did not penetrate the tumor core, consistent with the immune-excluded phenotype of ICC (1). To overcome this dense desmoplastic stromal barrier, lenvatinib induces the transition of endothelial cells into inflamed venules resembling high endothelial venules, thereby enabling the influx of effector T cells into the tumor parenchyma. In addition, lenvatinib and sintilimab modulate cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), attenuating their protumorigenic functions and weakening the dense stromal barrier characteristic of ICC (1,7). CAF-rich desmoplasia represents a hallmark of ICC and is a major contributor to immune exclusion, making stromal remodeling a critical component of therapeutic success.

Interestingly, the pre-treatment immune phenotype, whether tumors were “hot” or “cold,” did not correlate with response rates or survival outcomes. This suggests that the CASTLE-01 regimen can convert immunologically cold tumors into an activated state, expanding therapeutic possibilities, even for ICC, which has traditionally been considered refractory to immunotherapy.

This trial has limitations, including its single-arm design, small sample size, and lack of independent central review. The observed ethnic differences warrant further investigation. Nevertheless, CASTLE-01 provides evidence of compelling clinical activity and suggests a mechanistic rationale for overcoming the immune-excluded phenotype of ICC. An ongoing randomized controlled trial (NCT06860477) is expected to further clarify the clinical positioning of this strategy.

The implications of CASTLE-01 extend beyond ICC. Hepatocellular carcinoma, another type of primary liver cancer, is considered an immunologically cold tumor. Strategies that integrate locoregional therapies, such as local ablation (8) or transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) (9-11), with immunotherapy have been actively explored. For example, Zhou et al. demonstrated that local ablation combined with the anti-PD-1 antibody toripalimab improved clinical efficacy compared with toripalimab alone (ORR: 33.8% vs. 16.9%, P=0.027; median PFS: 7.1 vs. 3.8 months, P<0.001; median OS: 18.4 vs. 13.2 months, P=0.005) (8). In Japan, phase II trials evaluating atezolizumab plus bevacizumab (Atez/Bev) or the STRIDE regimen following local ablation are ongoing (12,13). Furthermore, phase III trials, EMERALD-1 (9), LEAP-012 (10), and TALENTACE (11), have shown that triple therapy comprising an ICI, an anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agent or a TKI, and TACE (TALENTACE: on-demand TACE) significantly prolongs PFS (median PFS: EMERALD-1, 15.0 vs. 8.2 months, P=0.032; LEAP-012, 14.6 vs. 10.0 months, P=0.0002; TALENTACE, 10.3 vs. 6.4 months, P<0.001) and achieves higher ORRs than TACE alone (ORR: EMERALD-1, 44% vs. 30%; LEAP-012, 47% vs. 33%; TALENTACE, 49% vs. 34%). More recently, the phase III IMPACT study comparing Atez/Bev plus on-demand TACE with Atez/Bev alone has been initiated (14). Thus, although the tumor types differ, the concept of combining local therapy with immunomodulation has consistently yielded promising outcomes.

CASTLE-01 demonstrates that the key to effective immunotherapy in ICC lies not merely in activating T cells, but also in establishing a microenvironment that enables their infiltration and function within the tumor. Therefore, the cryoablation-initiated immune activation strategy may hold promise not only for ICC but also for other refractory solid tumors characterized by immune exclusion.

On the Chow group of elliptic surfaces over number fields
Domenico Valloni

Abstract
Let X X be a smooth projective surface over a number field K K . Assume that X X has an elliptic fibration over ℙ K 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}_{K} with at least one singular fibre and a section. Let 𝒳 / U \mathcal{X}/U be a smooth projective model of X X over some open subset U ⊂ Spec ⁡ ( 𝒪 K ) U\subset\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{K}) . We show that ker ⁡ ( CH 2 ​ ( 𝒳 ) → CH 2 ​ ( X ) ) \ker\bigl(\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)\bigr) is a finitely generated group.



1  Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a number field
K
. A conjecture originally attributed to Swinnerton-Dyer (see
[
1
]
[Section 5] and also
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
) says that the Chow groups
\mathrm{CH}^{i}(X)
are finitely generated. At present, this conjecture is widely open, and we do not even know whether the
n
-torsion subgroup
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)[n]
is finitely generated when
X
is a surface, although by Rojtman’s theorem
[
14
]
this is true over the algebraic closure of
K
. The strongest evidence concerning the finiteness of
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}
is due to
[
4
]
[Theorem C], where the authors show that
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}
is finitely generated whenever
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
(see also
[
15
]
). We refer the reader to
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
, which contain surveys regarding this problem and a more comprehensive account of the literature.

In general, to study
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)
one spreads out
X
to a smooth projective model
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a localization of the ring of integers of
K
at finitely many places, and considers the localization sequence

\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)\rightarrow 0

(note that this can be continued to the left using
K
-theory). Assume from now on that
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, so, after further localizing
\mathcal{O}
if necessary, we have
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})=\operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
for every
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}
. The difficulty when
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0
arises from the fact that the rank of
\operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
can jump for infinitely many places (as for example happens for K3 surfaces), and one is not able to control the image of
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
.

Let us call this image
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})\cong\ker(\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X))
. This group appeared in
[
11
]
, where the case
X=E\times E
is studied, with
E/\mathbb{Q}
an elliptic curve. In particular, they show that
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
is finite when
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
E
has complex multiplication (there are only finitely many such curves over
\mathbb{Q}
). Moreover, the case where
E
is an arbitrary elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is also investigated. The proof starts by interpreting the new divisors modulo
\mathfrak{p}
as graphs of the Frobenius, and then uses the theory of modular curves and their reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
developed in
[
5
]
to control these cycles in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
. This is in fact one of the very few instances for which
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0
and we nevertheless understand the role of vertical cycles in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
. See also
[
8
]
, where these ideas are pushed further to prove more finiteness results regarding self products of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. Our aim in this brief article is to generalize Mildenhall’s finiteness to elliptic fibrations over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X/K
be a smooth projective surface endowed with an elliptic fibration
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
with at least one singular fibre and a section. Let
\mathcal{X}/\Spec(\mathcal{O})
be a smooth projective model of
X
over some open subset of
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{K})
. Then
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
is a finitely generated abelian group.

This applies to many surfaces of interest, e.g., (some) K3 surfaces. Also note that
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
can be arbitrarily large and that there are no restrictions on the number field
K
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X/K
be as in the theorem above. Then
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)[n]
is finite for every
n>0
and the group
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)
is finitely generated if and only if
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})
is finitely generated.

This follows from
[
4
]
[Theorem 1.1.], which says that
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})[n]
is finite. To prove Theorem
1.1
we use the relation between the Néron–Severi groups and the Mordell–Weil groups of elliptic surfaces. Our idea is to realize any new section of
\pi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
as (a component of) the degeneration of a
2
-section
D\subset X
defined over
K
. This is made precise in Section
3
. Once we do this, the result will follow almost immediately from the formal properties of Chow groups, specialization maps and the aforementioned finiteness results.

2  Preliminaries

2.1  Elliptic fibrations

We use the book
[
16
]
as a reference on elliptic fibrations. We work with
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
as the base curve, where for the moment
K
is an arbitrary field. Recall that a Weierstrass fibration
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
is, by definition, a normal projective surface
X^{\prime}
together with a flat morphism
\pi^{\prime}
whose geometric fibres are either smooth curves of genus one or reduced plane cubic curves with a single node or a single cusp (i.e. nodal or cuspidal cubic curves). One also assumes that
\pi^{\prime}
admits a section whose image we regard as the zero section of the elliptic fibration.

To any Weierstrass fibration we can, by resolution of singularities, associate a minimal elliptic surface
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
. Thus
X
is regular and the fibres of
\pi
do not contain any
(-1)
-curves. The singular fibres of
\pi
can be computed via the Tate algorithm, which we briefly explain. If
\mathrm{char}(K)\neq 2,3
we can write the Weierstrass fibration as

y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6}

where
a_{4},a_{6}\in K(t)
. Now let
0,\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
and let
U=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
and
U^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
, with
U=\Spec(K[t])
and
U^{\prime}=\Spec(K[t^{-1}])
. We can assume that
a_{4},a_{6}\in K[t]
up to an admissible change of coordinates. Since
U
has trivial class group, we can then find a global minimal Weierstrass equation over
U
. This means that either
v(a_{4})<4
or
v(a_{6})<6
for every valuation
v
of
K(t)
supported on
U
.

Now let
d
be the smallest integer such that
\deg(a_{i})\leq di
for
i=4,6
. Given such a minimal Weierstrass equation over
U
, one can obtain the minimal Weierstrass form over
U^{\prime}
by seeing the
a_{i}
’s as sections of
\mathcal{O}(id)
. From now on we let

P(x)=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6}

describe a minimal Weierstrass fibration over
U
with
a_{4},a_{6}\in K[t]
. So if
t=t_{0}/t_{1}
and we put

a_{i}^{\prime}=(t_{1}/t_{0})^{id}\,a_{i}(t_{0}/t_{1}),

we obtain two polynomials in
t^{-1}
, and the minimal Weierstrass equation over
U^{\prime}
is given by

P^{\prime}(x)=x^{3}+a_{4}^{\prime}x+a_{6}^{\prime}.

The Tate algorithm tells us that we can determine the singular fibres of
\pi
over a closed point of
U
(resp.
U^{\prime}
) simply by looking at the values of
v(a_{i})
,
v(\Delta)
and
v(j)
(resp.
v(a_{i}^{\prime})
,
v(\Delta^{\prime})
and
v(j^{\prime})
), where
\Delta=-16\bigl(4a_{4}^{3}+27a_{6}^{2}\bigr)
is the discriminant function,
j=-1728\,\frac{(4a_{4})^{3}}{\Delta}
is the
j
-invariant and
v
is the valuation corresponding to that closed point (see Tables 5.1 and 5.2 of
[
16
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
a prime. Let
P(x)
describe a minimal Weierstrass equation. We say that
\mathfrak{p}
satisfies (*) if:

1.

the residue characteristic of
\mathfrak{p}
is not
2
or
3
;

2.

all the rational functions
a_{4},a_{6},\Delta,j\in K(t)
are integral at
\mathfrak{p}
, and their reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
maintains the same factorization type (and the same holds for the analogous functions on the other chart).

In other words, point (2) means the following. Let
v_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon K(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the valuation corresponding to the prime
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
R(t)
be the ring of integers of
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
. For
f\in R(t)
we decompose

f(t)=a\prod_{i}f_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}

with
a\in R
and
f_{i}\in R[t]
irreducible and pairwise coprime and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{i})=0
for every
i
. We then say that
f
maintains the same factorization type modulo
\mathfrak{p}
if the reduction
\bar{f}_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[t]
is separable, of the same degree as
f_{i}
, and if moreover
\bar{f}_{i}
and
\bar{f}_{j}
are coprime whenever
i\neq j
.

Then all but finitely many primes satisfy (*), and the Tate algorithm implies that we can resolve the singularities of the Weierstrass model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}/R
simultaneously over
R=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. In particular,
X/K
has good reduction at
\mathfrak{p}
, and all its reductions in positive characteristic maintain the same configuration of singular fibres. Thus we have in this case a smooth projective model
\mathcal{X}/\Spec(\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the localization of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
at the finitely many primes which do not satisfy (*), and an elliptic fibration
\tilde{\pi}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}
. For any
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}
we denote by
\pi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\colon X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
the induced elliptic fibration over the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
.

2.2  Specialization map on Néron-Severi lattices

For a prime
\mathfrak{p}
satisfying (*) we consider the specialization map

\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{NS}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}).

Since
X
is fibred over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
we have
\Pic^{0}(X)=0
and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
as well (and the same is true over any field, see
[
16
]
[Section 5.3]). Moreover, since
X
has a rational point (because
\pi
has a section), we also have, by standard arguments, that

\Pic(X)=\Pic(\overline{X})^{G_{K}},

where
G_{K}
is the Galois group of
K
. Hence
\operatorname{NS}(X)=\operatorname{NS}(\overline{X})^{G_{K}}
. We also recall that the Néron–Severi group of an elliptic surface is always torsion-free (see
[
16
]
[Theorem 6.4]).

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
e\leq p-1
, where
p
is the residue characteristic of
\mathfrak{p}
and
e
is the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
is injective and has torsion-free cokernel.

Proof.

By
[
13
]
[Theorem 4.2.1.2] (see also
[
6
]
[Theorem 1.4]) the specialization map

\Pic(X_{\bar{K}})\longrightarrow\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}})

has torsion-free cokernel under the assumption. The result then follows because
\operatorname{NS}(X)=\operatorname{NS}(X_{\overline{K}})^{G_{K}}
and similarly for
\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
, and these are primitive subgroups of the corresponding Picard groups.
∎

We say that a prime satisfies (**) if it satisfies (*) and, moreover,
e\leq p-1
. Thus again all but finitely many primes satisfy (**), and, up to further localizing
\mathcal{O}
, we can assume that
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
has torsion-free cokernel for every
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}
. Let now
\mathrm{Triv}(X)\subset\operatorname{NS}(X)
be the primitive sublattice generated by the zero section and the components of the singular fibres. Then

\operatorname{NS}(X_{\bar{K}})/\mathrm{Triv}(X_{\bar{K}})\cong\mathrm{MW}((X_{\bar{K}})_{\eta}),

the Mordell–Weil group of
X
, viewed as an elliptic curve over the generic point
\eta
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{K}}
. Under our assumption on
\mathcal{O}
we also have

\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathrm{Triv}(X_{\bar{K}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Triv}({X_{\bar{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}})

for every prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that it may happen that some components of the singular fibres of
\pi
are not defined over
K
while they are defined over
\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. On the other hand, for a general
\pi
the singular fibres are irreducible and this does not occur.

3  Double covers attached to new sections

Let now
R
be any localization of
\mathcal{O}
at to some prime
\mathfrak{p}
satisfying (**). We denote by
\mathcal{X}/R
the model of
X
over
R
as in the previous section. We have a natural map
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
(which is a sequence of blow-downs over
R
), where
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is the Weierstrass model associated to a minimal equation as before. Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
be the maximal ideal of
R
and assume that
\pi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\colon X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
acquires a new section
s_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Let
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
be its associated Cartier divisor. Let now
\mathcal{U}=\Spec(\mathcal{O}[t])\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}
and consider the open subset

\Spec\bigl(\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[t,x,y]/(y^{2}-x^{3}-\bar{a}_{4}x-\bar{a}_{6})\bigr)

of
X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\prime}
which maps to
U_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\Spec(\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[t])
via
\pi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
(on the algebra side the bar denotes reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
). The point at infinity then corresponds to the zero section, hence
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
must intersect
\Spec(\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[t,x,y]/(y^{2}-x^{3}-\bar{a}_{4}x-\bar{a}_{6}))
nontrivially. Thus we can represent the section
s_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
over
U_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
by some rational function
\bar{f}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)
such that

\bar{P}(\bar{f})=\bar{f}^{3}+\bar{a}_{4}\bar{f}+\bar{a}_{6}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)^{2}.

Write
\bar{g}^{2}=\bar{P}(\bar{a})
for some
\bar{g}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)
, so that
s_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is given in the
(x,y)
-coordinates by
(\bar{f},\bar{g})
and its negation
-s_{p}
by
(\bar{f},-\bar{g})
.

Consider now the function field
K(t)
and the valuation
v_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon K(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. Then we can write

R(t)\coloneqq\bigg\{\frac{f}{g}\;\bigg|\;f,g\in R[t],\ v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)=0\bigg\}.

Pick any
f\in R(t)
which lifts
\bar{f}
and consider
G\coloneqq P(f)
. Note that
G\in R(t)
as well, and that
G
is a lift of
\bar{g}^{2}
. On the other hand,
G
cannot itself be a square, for otherwise
(f,g)
would describe a section of
X
lifting
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, where
g^{2}=G
, which is impossible under our assumption.

So it makes sense to consider the field extension
L=K(t)[g]/(g^{2}-G)
and define
\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
as the integral closure of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}
in
L
. Note that this depends on the choice of the lift
f
, and not only on
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, but this is not relevant for us. Since integral closure commutes with localization, the generic fibre of
\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is the integral closure of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
in
L
. Also, note that
L
may split over
\bar{K}
, e.g. if
g
becomes a square over an extension of
K
. This can happen only if
\operatorname{NS}(X_{\bar{K}})^{G_{K}}\neq\operatorname{NS}(X_{\bar{K}})
and
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
does not lift over
K
but lifts over an extension of
K
. Moreover, it is also necessary that the lift
f
is chosen such that
P(f)
is a geometric square. So in order to have
\Spec(L)
geometrically irreducible in general we need only to show that
P(f)
can always be chosen so that it is not a square in
\bar{K}(t).

Lemma 3.1
.

There are infinitely many lifts
f
of
\bar{f}
such that
P(f)
is not a square in
\overline{K}(t)
.

Proof.

Pick any lift
f=f_{0}/f_{1}
of
\bar{f}=\bar{f}_{0}/\bar{f}_{1}
, where

\bar{f}_{0},\bar{f}_{1}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[t]
are coprime and

f_{0},f_{1}\in R[t]
have the same degrees as
\bar{f}_{0}
and
\bar{f}_{1}

respectively. Then
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are also coprime and

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{1})=0
. For a local parameter
\pi
of
\mathfrak{p}
we
consider lifts of the form

f_{\lambda}=\frac{f_{0}+\pi\lambda}{f_{1}},\qquad\lambda\in R.

Then

f_{1}^{3}\,P\bigl((f_{0}+\pi\lambda)/f_{1}\bigr)=(f_{0}+\pi\lambda)^{3}+a_{4}f_{1}^{2}(f_{0}+\pi\lambda)+a_{6}f_{1}^{3}\eqqcolon J_{\lambda},

and the right-hand side is coprime to
f_{1}
since
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are coprime
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
. Thus it is enough to show that there are infinitely many
choices of
\lambda
such that
J_{\lambda}
is squarefree in
\overline{K}[t]
.
If now, for a ring
S
, we denote by
S[t]_{N}\cong S^{N+1}
the

S
-module of polynomials of degree
\leq N
, then the subset of
squarefree polynomials in
S[t]_{N}
is a Zariski open subset defined
over
\mathbb{Z}
. Hence it is enough to show that
J_{\lambda}
is squarefree over
K[t]

for infinitely many
\lambda
. Now note that

Q(x,t)=x^{3}+a_{4}f_{1}^{2}x+a_{6}f_{1}^{3}

satisfies
Q(f_{1}x,t)=f_{1}^{3}P(x,t)
and that
P(x,t)
is irreducible
in
K[x,t]
. If, for a contradiction, we had
Q(x,t)=Q_{0}(x,t)Q_{1}(x,t)
,
then

Q_{0}(f_{1}x,t)\,Q_{1}(f_{1}x,t)=f_{1}^{3}P(x,t),

which would imply, by degree considerations in
x
, that either
Q_{0}
or

Q_{1}
is constant in
x
, which is impossible unless the other factor
is a unit. Thus
Q(x,t)
is irreducible, and hence also

Q(f_{0}+\pi x,t)
must be irreducible. Finally, by Hilbert
irreducibility, we have infinitely many
x_{0}\in K
such that

Q(f_{0}+\pi x_{0},t)
remains irreducible over
K
, and in particular, squarefree.
∎

So we can choose
L
such that
\Spec(L)
is geoemtrically irreducible and the generic fiber of
\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is a smooth projective hyperelliptic curve.

Proposition 3.2
.

The induced map
\phi\colon\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}
is finite (of degree
2
) and flat. The special fibre of
\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
splits into two components
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})^{+}\cup(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})^{-}
meeting at the zeroes and poles of
\bar{g}
. The two components are swapped by the reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
of the hyperelliptic involution
\iota\colon\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
.

The map is finite of degree
2
by construction. It is also flat by miracle flatness, since
\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is normal and of dimension two, hence automatically Cohen–Macaulay (see the beginning of
[
10
]
[Section 1.2], where the same construction is considered). The claim about the special fibre follows from the definition of
L
, since
G
becomes a square modulo
\mathfrak{p}
. Note also that taking the quotient by
\iota
commutes with taking the special fibre (see
[
10
]
[Remark 1.7]) since the residue characteristic is odd.
∎

Next, define
\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
as the base change

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}}}

So we have a natural finite flat map

induced by

and the left vertical map induces an elliptic fibration
\sigma\colon\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
. Note that this is not necessarily a minimal elliptic fibration (in particular its generic fibre may be singular), but it is birational to one. In any case, by construction, we have a new section of

.

Note that both

on the generic fibre. The negation

\mathcal{D}^{-}=\iota(\mathcal{D}^{+})

coincides with the section

, seen as a divisor on the component

3.1  Chow groups and Proof of Theorem
1.1

Chow groups of schemes over Dedekind domains and their functoriality are studied in Fulton’s book
[
7
]
[Chapter 20]. We recall the main results that we are going to use (keeping the notation from the previous section). Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathcal{O}
(or
R
) with generic fibre
X
and special fibres
X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
(both may be empty in Fulton’s setting). If
Z\subset\mathcal{X}
is a closed integral subscheme we let

\dim_{\mathcal{O}}(Z)=\begin{cases}\dim(Z_{K})&\text{if }Z\text{ is flat over }\mathcal{O},\\[4.0pt]
\dim(Z)-1&\text{if }Z\text{ is vertical}.\end{cases}

The group
\mathrm{CH}_{i}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})
is then defined as the free abelian group generated by integral closed subschemes of relative dimension
i
modulo rational equivalence (thus
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})=\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})
in this notation). The specialization map

\sigma_{p}\colon\mathrm{CH}_{i}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}_{i}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})

is defined in
[
7
]
[Section 20.3] in full generality. Moreover, if
Z\subset X
is a prime cycle and
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
denotes its Zariski closure, which is flat over
\mathcal{O}
, then

\sigma_{p}(Z)=[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}]\in\mathrm{CH}_{i}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}),

i.e.
\sigma_{p}(Z)
is the element of
\mathrm{CH}_{i}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
associated to the special fibre of
\mathcal{Z}
, seen as a closed subscheme of
X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. The following lemma is well-known:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
j\colon\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
be the inclusion of an integral closed subscheme. Assume that
\mathcal{Z}
is flat over
\mathcal{O}
and of relative dimension
i
. Then the cycle associated to the special fibre
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is zero in
\mathrm{CH}_{i-1}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{O}
be a local parameter for
\mathfrak{p}
and, if necessary, localize
\mathcal{O}
further to obtain
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
so that
\pi
has only
\mathfrak{p}
as a simple zero on
\Spec(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
. Let now
\pi_{|\mathcal{Z}}
be considered as a rational function on
\mathcal{Z}
. Then by the definition of rational equivalence

\mathrm{div}(\pi_{|\mathcal{Z}})=[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}]=0

in the Chow group
\mathrm{CH}_{i-1}(\mathcal{Z}/\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
. Hence, by the fact that proper pushforward respects rational equivalence, we get that
[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}]=0
also in
\mathrm{CH}_{i-1}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
. Finally, this is also zero in
\mathrm{CH}_{i-1}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})
by functoriality.
∎

Let now
X
be an elliptic surface with a model
\mathcal{X}/R
as in the previous section. Let
\psi\colon\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be the
2\!:\!1
covering corresponding to some new section
D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\in\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
. Let

\psi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\colon\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})^{+}\cup(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})^{-}\longrightarrow X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}

be the induced (finite, flat) morphism between special fibres. By construction

\psi_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})=\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(D^{+}+D^{-}),

where
D^{+}\subset X(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})=\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})_{K}
is the generic fibre of
\mathcal{D}^{+}\subset\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
. Thus the previous lemma implies that
[D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}]\in\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/R)
satisfies

0=\psi^{*}([D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}])\in\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}(D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})/R).

Hence

0=\psi_{*}(\psi^{*}[D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}])=2\cdot[D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}}]\in\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/R).

This shows that the element
(\dots,0,D_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}},0,\dots)\in\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
has image in
\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})[2]
. Finally, all the components of the singular fibres of
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
are defined over some field extension
K^{\prime}/K
of degree
N
. Hence, if
(\dots,0,F,0,\dots)\in\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
describes a component of some singular fibre which is not defined over
K
, we must also have that

N\cdot(\dots,0,F,0,\dots)=0\quad\text{in }\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})

by a standard push–pull argument and so the image of
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is contained in
\mathrm{CH}_{0}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O})[2N]=\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathcal{X})[2N]
, which is a finite group
[
4
]
[Theorem 1.1.]. The result then follows since there are only finitely many primes which do not satisfy (**) and since
\operatorname{NS}(X_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}})
is always finitely generated.

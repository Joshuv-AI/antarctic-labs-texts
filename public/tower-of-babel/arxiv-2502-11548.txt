Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant in topological K K -theory
Koki Yanagida

Abstract
Given a finite group G G , we define a new invariant of odd-dimensional oriented closed manifolds and call it the KDW invariant. This invariant is a Dijkgraaf–Witten invariant in terms of K K -theory. In this paper, we compute the invariant of the Brieskorn homology spheres with G = PSL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 p ) G=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}) . We should remark that, in this computational result, the fundamental groups of the Brieskorn homology spheres and PSL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 p ) \mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}) are not nilpotent.

Introduction

For a finite group
G
, the Dijkgraaf–Witten (DW) invariant
\mathrm{DW}_{G}
is a topological invariant of oriented closed
3
-manifolds defined by Dijkgraaf and Witten
[
7
]
.
Roughly speaking, the DW invariant,
\mathrm{DW}_{G}(M)
, of an oriented closed
3
-manifold
M
is the formal sum of pushforwards
\varphi_{*}([M])\in H_{3}(BG;\mathbb{Z})
, where
\varphi
ranges over all the homomorphisms
\pi_{1}(M)\to G
.
Here,
[M]\in H_{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is an orientation class of
M
and
BG
is a classifying space of
G
.
In addition, Dijkgraaf and Witten have constructed a toy model-like
(2+1)
-TQFT derived from the DW invariant.
In other words, providing computational examples of the DW invariant leads to a deeper understanding of the TQFT.
Nevertheless, in the previous studies, there are few examples of computing the DW invariant unless
G
is abelian or nilpotent.

As a generalization, we can define orientation classes of any generalized homology as analogies of the orientation class
[M]
of ordinary homology (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
).
In particular, for the
K
-homology
K_{*}
, it is known that a spin
c
structure of an odd-dimensional oriented closed manifold
M
defines an orientation class
[M]_{K}
of
M
in
K_{*}(M)
(see
[
12
]
).
Thus,
thanks to these orientation classes,
it is possible to extend the definition of TQFTs based on ordinary homology to generalized homologies
under certain assumptions.
For TQFTs based on generalized homologies, see, e.g.,
[
10
]
.

This paper further studies the DW invariant in terms of
K
-theory; this form of the DW invariant is called the KDW invariant and denoted by
\mathrm{KDW}_{G}(M)\in\mathbb{Z}[K_{1}(BG)]
(Definition
2.1
).
Even though
K_{1}(BG)
has been determined in
[
1
,
8
]
,
it is difficult to precisely present the pushforwards of the orientation class
[M]_{K}
via homomorphisms
\pi_{1}(M)\to G
;
thus the computation of the KDW invariant is often difficult.
To solve this difficulty, from
[
16
]
, we focus on an embedding
\psi_{G}:K_{1}(BG)\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\hookrightarrow R(G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}/\mathbb{Z}
, and attempt to compute
\psi_{G}(\mathrm{KDW}_{G}(M))
.
Here,
R(G)
is the complex representation ring.
In this paper, we call
\psi_{G}(\mathrm{KDW}_{G}(M))
the
\alpha
-KDW invariant (Definition
2.6
).
Through
\psi_{G}
,
the computing of the KDW invariants without
2
-torsion follows from the computation of induced representations between the representation rings (see Remark
2.5
for details).

This paper computes the
\alpha
-KDW invariant in the following three cases:

(i)

The lens space
M=L^{1}(k;\ell)
with
G=\mathbb{Z}/k
(Example
2.7
),

(ii)

The lens space
M=L^{1}(k;\ell)
with
G=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(Proposition
3.5
),

(iii)

The Brieskorn homology sphere
M=\Sigma
with
G=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
).

We should remark that, in (iii), the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)
and
G
are not nilpotent.
Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
imply that, if
G
is more complicated than
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and nilpotent groups, then the computation of the DW invariants will be more difficult.

We now briefly explain procedures to obtain the results (i)-(iii): First, (i) follows from the resulting computation
[
16
]
of the images of pushforward
\varphi_{*}([L^{1}(k;\ell)]_{K})\in K_{1}(BG)
for
\varphi:\pi_{1}(L^{1}(k;\ell))\to G
under
\psi_{G}
.
Next, we deduce (ii) from the naturality of
\psi_{G}
; for any injective group homomorphism
f:H\to G
, there exists
f_{!}:R(H)\to R(G)
satisfying
\psi_{G}\circ f_{*}=f_{!}\circ\psi_{H}
.
Finally, to accomplish (iii), we focus on a bordism between
\Sigma
and lens spaces from
[
15
]
.
Considering this bordism, we can verify that the pushforward of
[\Sigma]_{K}
is equal to the sum of the pushforwards of orientation classes of lens spaces.
Thus, as an application of (ii), classifying the conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
is adequate to compute the
\alpha
-KDW invariant in (iii).

This paper is organized as follows:
Section
2
defines the KDW invariant and the
\alpha
-KDW invariant, and presents the resulting computation of the
\alpha
-KDW invariants of lens spaces with finite cyclic groups.
In Section
3
, we provide the
\alpha
-KDW invariants of lens spaces with

.
Section
4
gives the computational results of the
\alpha
-KDW invariants of some Brieskorn homology spheres with

in Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
.
Section

.

On the other hand, we omit the definition of the

-homology. However, as seen in

\mathrm{PD}_{\mathfrak{s}}:K^{*}(X)\cong K_{d-*}(X).

(1)

Recall the Bott periodicity

.
In consideration of this periodicity, we may focus only on the cases of

Next, given a finite group

, we review the

-(co)homology of

.
Suppose a filtration of
G

Then, the

As is known (see, e.g.,

K_{*}(BG)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R(G)/I_{G}^{n},\mathbb{Z}),&\text{if $*=0$,}\\
\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}(R(G)/I_{G}^{n},\mathbb{Z}),&\text{if $*=1$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

(2)

Here,
R(G)
is the complex representation ring, and

is the augmentation ideal.
Moreover, in
[
8
, Theorem 5.5.6]
, considering the linear isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces between

and
\mathrm{class}(G)
, which is spanned by the irreducible characters of

K_{*}(BG)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $*=0$,}\\
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}(G)}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\infty})^{r_{G}(p)},&\text{if $*=1$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

(3)

Here,
\mathcal{P}(G)
is the set of primes that divide the order

,

is the number of the conjugacy classes consisting of elements whose orders are powers of

, and
\mathbb{Z}/p^{\infty}
is the Prüfer
p
-group.
In conclusion, by (
3
), we should focus on the odd cases of
\dim M
.

2.1
K
-orientation and properties in three-dimensional cases

We review from
[

Let

For any point

Then, the excision axiom yields the map

Then,

[M]_{E}\in E_{*}(M)

In addition, let us further consider the case of

, i.e.,

-theory spectrum

, and

Let

For example,

An orientation class

Let

.

With the choice of an orientation class

, let us define the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant in terms of

-theory as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

For

, we define

with

is a classifying map

.
For simplicity, we often write the

for the invariant.

Next, we focus on the case of

Example 2.7

Let us compute the

First, we will review the definition of the (generalized) lens space.
Let

Let

For

Note that

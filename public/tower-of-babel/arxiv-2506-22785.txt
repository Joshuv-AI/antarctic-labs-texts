The mechanics of disclination emergence in 3D active nematics
Yingyou Ma, Christopher Amey, Aparna Baskaran, Michael F. Hagan

Abstract
The spontaneous creation of disclinations is a defining characteristic of active nematics, which is rarely observed in equilibrium systems or other active matter systems. Thus, understanding the mechanics of disclinations is crucial for developing reliable continuum theories and practical applications. In this work, we explore this intrinsic mechanics by performing large-scale 3D simulations of a particle-based model of active semiflexible filaments. We investigate the effects of filament stiffness and activity on the collective behavior of active nematics. Analysis of the steady state and the topological properties of initial disclination loops reveals that the system is governed by a single parameter, an activity-dependent effective stiffness. Then, we develop a method to visualize director field orientations in a physically transparent manner during the formation of disclination loops. Based on this, we establish a unified theory for the mechanics of disclination emergence, across the range of bend and twist. This disclination analysis framework can also be applied to diverse other 3D liquid crystal systems.

In this letter, we use large-scale molecular dynamics simulations of active semi-flexible filaments to understand the spontaneous emergence of disclinations from a flat background in 3D active nematics. While this technique has led to fruitful insights in studies of active coarsening systems
Athani and Beller 2024
;
Winkler and Gompper 2020
;
Moore et al. 2020
;
Joshi et al. 2019
;
Freedman et al. 2017
;
Palmer et al. 2022
, 3D studies are limited to confined systems
Peterson et al. 2021
;
Sciortino et al. 2025
;
Yan et al. 2022
;
Henkes et al. 2018
or polar filaments
Breoni et al. 2025
. We systematically explore a broad range of filament activity
f_{\text{a}}
and stiffness
\kappa
values, both of which control the large-scale system dynamics. Compared to previous studies that used continuum theory
Digregorio et al. 2024
;
Duclos et al. 2020
;
Long et al. 2021
;
Kralj et al. 2023
;
Nejad and Yeomans 2022
;
Shendruk et al. 2018
, our particle-based simulations bridge microscopic mechanics, including local alignment and active forces, with the macroscopic continuum field and emergent structures.

Analysis of trajectories shows that both the steady state and the topological properties of initial disclination loops depend solely on an activity-dependent effective stiffness parameter
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
. Furthermore, we develop a framework to understand the complex geometrical structure of the 3D director field and its evolution during the emergence of initial disclination loops. Within this framework, we propose a 2D-analog theory, which extends previous descriptions of the mechanism for pure-bend-induced loops to conditions involving twist. Thus, we establish a unified theory for loop emergence. Finally, by analyzing simulation trajectories we show that loop structures are governed by a competition of timescales for loop nucleation and twist deformations.

Model.
—To simulate the collective behavior of active semi-flexible filaments in 3D, we extend our previous model for 2D active filaments, which captured the fundamental and essential behaviors and symmetries inherent to active nematics
Joshi et al. 2019
. We model each filament as a bead-spring polymer, with the monomer coordinates
\mathbfit{r}_{i}
governed by Langevin equation:

m\ddot{\mathbfit{r}_{i}}=\mathbfit{f}_{\text{a}}-\xi\dot{\mathbfit{r}_{i}}-\nabla_{\mathbfit{r}}U+\mathbfit{R}_{i}(t)

(1)

where
\mathbfit{f}_{\text{a}}
is the active force,
\xi
is the friction coefficient set to overdamp the dynamics, and
\mathbfit{R}
is a Gaussian thermal noise with variance
6\xi k_{\text{B}}T
. The potential energy
U
comprises three components: the FENE bond potential
Kremer and Grest 1990
, a non-bonded Weeks-Chandler-Anderson volume-exclusion potential
Weeks et al. 1971
which leads to local nematic alignment, and harmonic angle potential
\bar{\kappa}(\pi-\vartheta)^{2}
enforcing filament stiffness, where
\vartheta
is the angle between adjacent bonds and
\bar{\kappa}
is the elastic modulus. The active force is modeled by a self-propulsion force on each monomer directed along the filament tangent toward its head

\mathbfit{f}_{a}=\eta_{\alpha}(t)\bar{f}_{\text{a}}\,b\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}

(2)

where
b
is the bond length,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}
is the local unit tangent and
\bar{f}_{\text{a}}
is the strength of activity. To keep nematic symmetry, the active force reverses the direction at stochastic time intervals, which effectively switches the head and tail of the filaments. This is reached by
\eta_{\alpha}(t)
, which changes its value between 1 and -1 at Poisson-distributed time intervals. Fig.
1
(a,b) shows an active filament and a schematic representation of the bead-spring model. To integrate the Langevin dynamics, we modified LAMMPS
Thompson et al. 2022
to include the active force

.

In this Letter, we employ the LJ unit system in LAMMPS, where the length, energy, and mass units correspond to the monomer diameter
\sigma
,
k_{\text{B}}T
, and monomer mass
m
, respectively. Our simulations used
N\approx 2.8\times 10^{5}
rods in a periodic box with edge length
L=200\sigma
, bond length
b=0.5\sigma
, and 50 beads per rod. To probe the instability, we used an aligned nematic as the initial condition. We simulated a wide range of stiffness and activity values (which we report in dimensionless units)
\kappa\equiv\bar{\kappa}/k_{\text{B}}T\in[100,1500]
and
f_{\text{a}}\equiv\bar{f}_{\text{a}}\sigma/k_{\text{B}}T\in[1-10]
, covering steady states from chaotic to nearly static flow. For analysis, we coarse-grain the system into the tensor order parameter field
\mathsfbfit{Q}_{ij}=\langle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}_{j}-\mathsfbfit{\delta}_{ij}/3\rangle
where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}
is the normalized displacement between each pair of bonded monomers. The largest eigenvalue and eigenvector of
\mathsfbfit{Q}
defines the local order
S
and director
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
. A detailed description of the governing equations and coarse-graining, as well as all parameter values, are presented in the supplementary information (SI).

Steady state.
—To characterize the steady states, we present macroscopic quantities commonly used to describe the global properties of nematics, the average order parameter
\langle S\rangle
, the total Frank elastic deformation (i.e., the Frank energy for unit moduli values, see SI section IV), and the auto-correlation length of the director field, as functions of the microscopic parameters
\kappa
and
f_{\text{a}}
.
Notably, Fig.
2
shows that all values collapse onto a single curve parameterized by
\kappa_{\text{eff}}\equiv\kappa/(1+2f_{\text{a}}^{2})
, which we denote as the
effective stiffness
. This collapse indicates that
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
predominantly governs the system’s statistical properties. Thus, beyond driving disclination formation, activity primarily dissipates in bend modes, which softens the filaments, consistent with previous observations in 2D
Joshi et al. 2019
.

We physically interpret the effective stiffness
\kappa_{\text{eff}}=\kappa/(1+2f_{\text{a}}^{2})
as follows. In the zero Reynolds number limit, the elastic stress
\sim\kappa
balances the combined effects of thermal noise
\sim k_{\text{B}}T
(1 in our units) and active stress. The active stress arises from two factors: the average collision frequency
\sim f_{\text{a}}/\ell
, where
\ell
is the mean spacing between neighboring monomers, and the collision force strength
f_{\text{a}}
. Consequently, active stress scales as
f_{\text{a}}^{2}
, effectively reducing the filament bending modulus to
\kappa/(1+gf_{\text{a}}^{2})
, where
g\approx 2
is an empirical coefficient. Details about calculating the statistical properties and the data collapse with
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
are in the SI.

Instability.
—To investigate the mechanism that leads to the initial disclinations, it is useful to consider a continuum description of the dynamics of our system. As Fig.
3
.a shows, consistent with previous findings,
Nejad and Yeomans 2022
;
Putzig et al. 2016
;
Sanchez et al. 2012
;
Giomi et al. 2014
;
Chandrakar et al. 2020
, the bend deformation is the dominant mode. Given that we are simulating a dry system, the dynamics is determined by a balance between Stokesian friction and active stress:
\zeta\mathbfit{v}=\nabla\cdot\mathsfbfit{\sigma}_{\text{a}}
, with
\mathsfbfit{\sigma}_{\text{a}}=-\alpha\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
and
\alpha>0
as the active strength
Aditi Simha and Ramaswamy 2002
. The director
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
of nematics with high aspect ratio is described by the Leslie-Erickson equation
Kleman and Laverntovich 2003
:
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}=\mathsfbfit{\Omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}+\mathsfbfit{E}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}+\mu\nabla^{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
, with
\mathsfbfit{\Omega}=[(\nabla\mathbfit{v})^{\text{T}}-\nabla\mathbfit{v}]/2
as the vorticity tensor,
\mathsfbfit{E}=[(\nabla\mathbfit{v})^{\text{T}}+\nabla\mathbfit{v}]/2
as the strain rate tensor, and
\mu\nabla^{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
penalizes deformations. The resulting perturbation equation of
\delta\tilde{n}_{y}
is: (
\tilde{}
denotes Fourier transform)

\partial_{t}\delta\tilde{n}_{y}=[\alpha(\cos 2\theta+1)/2-\mu]k^{2}\delta\tilde{n}_{y}

(3)

which implies a competition between torques of activity
\sim\alpha
and elasticity
\sim\mu
, with
\theta
as the angle between
\mathbfit{k}
and the initial direction of order
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}_{0}
. The growth rate peaks at
\theta=0
, and thus represents the pure bend mode because the leading term is
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}_{0}\times\mathbfit{k}\times\delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}

de Gennes and Prost 1993
. Detailed instability and Fourier analyses supporting bend instability, both for the continuum model and simulations, are provided in the SI.

Initial disclinations.
—While disclinations in bulk 3D nematics initially appear as small loops
Kleman and Laverntovich 2003
, visualizing the surrounding
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
field remains challenging. Even though the formalism for parameterizing
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
has been elucidated
Binysh and Alexander 2018
, existing approaches for visualization either focus on local normal cross-sections
Duclos et al. 2020
;
Binysh et al. 2020
;
Friedel and de Gennes 1969
, thereby missing large-scale structural features, or examine the asymptotic behavior far from the loops
Houston and Alexander 2022
. In our system, however, the directors primarily vary within the
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}_{0}
–
\delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
planes, making the spatial structure amenable to an understandable quasi-2D description. We develop a tool to achieve this as follows.

Consider a small box enclosing the loop. The second moment of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
within the box is
\mathsfbfit{q}=\langle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}\rangle_{\text{box}}
, with eigenvectors
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{L}}
.
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}
corresponds to the largest eigenvalue and hence represents the local average orientation of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
.
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}
is the intermediate eigenvalue and captures the average deviation between
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}
.

This local coordinate system naturally encompasses the structure of the bend instability lying in different
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}
planes, which we call the
principal planes
, and provides the backbone of the analysis reported here. As initial loops emerge from an ordered
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
field,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}
respectively serve as the undisturbed background (
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}_{0}
) and perturbation orientation (
\delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}})
, a conclusion similar to the model in Ref.
Binysh and Alexander 2018
;
Binysh et al. 2020
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
is assumed to lie on the principal planes. Furthermore, since
\mathbfit{k}
mainly aligns with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{N}}
during the instability as discussed above, the pattern on each principal plane already captures the dominant deformation. As a result, this model yields a quasi-2D structure, which is both readily understood and captures the dominant physics. SI Video 3 shows an example of this quasi-2D structure as an initial loop scanned by principal planes.

We propose a unified theory of bend-induced loop emergence via principal plane scans. Fig.
3
.a shows that loops stem from the bend instability. Given that active forces are extensile and aligned with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
, bending induces a unit active force approximately along
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}
, distorting the field and creating a pair of
\pm 1/2
2D-like defects on each principal plane (Fig.
3
.b). Ultimately, defects across different principal planes collectively form the initial disclination loop.

The microscopic influence on this framework is also characterized by
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
. At low
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, defect positions on the principal planes approximately coincide, making the loop perpendicular to these planes (Fig.
3
.b.1). At high
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, defect positions differ, causing the loop to tilt (Fig.
3
.b.2). This tilt can be quantified by the topological parameter
\gamma
, defined as the angle between the loop normal
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\upnu}}
and the rotation vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Omega}}
, along which the surrounding directors rotate
Duclos et al. 2020
. Since
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
forms the quasi-2D structure,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Omega}}
is approximately perpendicular to the principal planes and aligned with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{L}}
. Consequently, at large
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, the loop tilt shifts
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\upnu}}
away from the principal planes and reduces
\gamma
. Fig.
3
.c and SI Video 4 illustrate the loops’ emergence and tilt mechanics. The topological analysis in different simulations reveals that
\langle\gamma\rangle
, the average value of all initial loops, collapses onto a monotonically decreasing curve as a function of
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
(Fig.
4
.b).

This tilt configuration can also be described by additional twist. At high
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, the initial loop is surrounded by directors that vary significantly across different principal planes compared to the low
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
case, as shown in Fig.
3
, due to effective shear. This deformation, where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
behaves differently across planes within the quasi-2D structure, represents
twist
, a feature unique to 3D nematics and absent in 2D. Thus, initial loops at low
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
form primarily through the pure bend mode, while at high
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, significant additional twist is present.

Since our system dynamics always starts with the bend instability, we interpret the loop tilt as a competition between two time scales,
\tau_{\text{nuc}}
and
\tau_{\text{twi}}
(see the SI for detailed definitions). In Fig.
4
.c, the vertical black lines indicate
\tau_{\text{nuc}}
, the defect nucleation timescale, while the curves represent the time-dependence of the largest Fourier coefficients of
Q_{xy}
and
Q_{xz}
, the leading order terms of the instability. The peaks of these curves define
\tau_{\text{twi}}
, marking the transition from bend to other deformation modes. This figure reveals that, at low
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, the active force is strong enough to generate defects immediately during the bend instability as
\tau_{\text{nuc}}<\tau_{\text{twi}}
, generating loops perpendicular to principal planes. In contrast, at high
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
, defects do not nucleate until the system has developed significant twist, resulting in tilted loops with lower
\gamma
. SI Fig. 1 shows all components of
\mathsfbfit{Q}
, demonstrating that the other components correspond to second-order perturbations.

Discussion.
— We systematically investigate the emergence of disclination loops in 3D active nematics. Our particle-based simulations provide key insights into the microscopic mechanisms that govern the large-scale defect formation. To enable general conclusions, we use a minimal model that captures the essential nematic behaviors of micro-units, including inter-particle alignment, active forces, and semiflexibility. Our results reveal that an activity-dependent effective stiffness parameter
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
governs defect mechanics, morphology, and steady-state properties, enabling precise control over the system’s emergent behavior. This insight could enable the rational design of microscopic parameters to develop self-regulating fluid systems and bio-inspired materials with tunable transport properties.

A crucial innovation of this work is the concept of principal planes, which are natural coordinate systems for expressing director fields in the vicinity of defects ,especially in active nematics. This framework enables visualizing and understanding the complicated 3D structure of initial loops by an approximated quasi-2D configuration. This clarity is reflected in our precise and illustrative description of the previously proposed pure-bend-induced loop mechanism in special cases
Duclos et al. 2020
;
Binysh et al. 2020
, as well as in our extension to more general conditions involving twist. Thus, the principal planes representation allows us to establish a universal theory for defect loop formation in 3D active nematics. Moreover, as a generalization of the model provided by Friedel and de Gennes
Friedel and de Gennes 1969
, our framework holds for any orientational field with small perturbations, and thus can be applied to disclination loops and lines in diverse situations including passive nematics or ferromagnetic systems.

Although our model is built for extensile systems undergoing a bend instability, its validity generalizes to other cases. If the system is made contractile or key parameters, such as the flow-alignment coefficient and elastic moduli, are adjusted, the splay instability may dominate, as discussed in Refs.
Putzig et al. 2016
;
Nejad and Yeomans 2022
;
Edwards and Yeomans 2009
and the SI. However, in that case our framework predicts minimal changes in loop emergence as
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}
remains within the principal planes, now aligning with
\delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
. More broadly, the loop emergence mechanics remain valid once
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}
lies in the principal planes, with only a change in the orientation of the
\pm 1/2
2D-like defect pairs
Giomi et al. 2014
;
Tang and Selinger 2017
. Moreover, when
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}
deviates from the principal planes, effective shear accompanied by additional twist emerges due to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
gradients across multiple planes (Fig.
3
). In particular, when
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}
is nearly perpendicular to the principal planes, as in the case of a pure twist mode, the quasi-2D dynamics cease to be applicable. We are developing a more unified framework that accommodates such scenarios in ongoing work
Ma 2025
.

The initial loops could also be investigated by the existing model that focuses on the topological properties of point defects on the normal cross-sections
Kleman and Laverntovich 2003
;
Alexander et al. 2012
;
Duclos et al. 2020
;
Friedel and de Gennes 1969
. The collapsed curve of
\langle\gamma\rangle
indicates that increasing
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
transforms the loops from wedge-twist type to pure-twist. In contrast to this local analysis, our framework examines loops on a larger scale, enabling us to reveal the structure and mechanisms underlying this transition by the competition between Frank elastic energy and active stress, which is well captured by the tilt of principal planes.

Our representation of disclination loops in the principal plane representation immediately suggests a way to produce and control disclination loops in nematic systems. One can readily use this framework to produce loops of desired textures in experimental active nematic systems including microtubule composites
Duclos et al. 2020
and chromonic liquid crystal - swimming bacteria composite systems
Zhou et al. 2014
. Further, the steady state properties of the system are determined by
\kappa_{\text{eff}}
. This work gives us a design principle for measuring the activity and controlling the bulk properties of these experimental realizations. Hence, it can pave the way for designer active fluids with tailored inhomogeneous mechanical responses.

In conclusion, we establish a universal theory for disclination loop emergence in 3D active nematics, emphasizing the critical role of activity-dependent effective stiffness. Our particle-based simulations strongly support this mechanism. Together, the theory and simulations suggest further experimental validation, hydrodynamic modeling, and kinetic analyses for a comprehensive understanding of topological defect dynamics in active matter. These insights not only deepen our understanding of non-equilibrium physics but also pave the way for applications of active nematic materials in engineering and biological systems.

Simple Proofs of the Summation and Connectivity Theorems in Metabolic Control Analysis
Weijiu Liu

Abstract
In the early 1970s, the Kacser/Burns and the Heinrich/Rapoport groups independently discovered the important summation and connectivity theorems in metabolic control analysis. These theorems were derived originally by using thought experiments and proved mathematically later. The mathematical proofs are not easy for me to read and follow. But the proofs actually can be very simple and need only a couple of lines as I give here.

The Proof of the Summation Theorem

The summation theorem can be stated in a general
mathematical form
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})=\mathbf{0},

(1)

and

J=J(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S}),

where
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
,

\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{m})
,
J
is a differentiable function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{1}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})\\
\vdots\\
F_{m}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})\end{array}\right)

is a differentiable vector function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Assume that the Jacobian matrix

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial S_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial S_{m}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial S_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial S_{m}}\end{array}\right)

is nonsingular.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{e}},

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}.

(4)

Note that
\mathbf{S}
is treated as an independent variable in
\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}
on
the right hand side of the above equation.
Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad i=1,\cdots,m,

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\frac{\partial J}{\partial e_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J,

(6)

then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\frac{\partial S_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,m

(7)

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\frac{\partial J}{\partial e_{j}}=J.

(8)

Proof
. Since the Jacobian matrix
\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

is assumed to be nonsingular,
the implicit
function theorem (see, e.g.,
[
5
, page 148]
) ensures that the system (
1
)
can be solved for
S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}
to get
S_{i}=S_{i}(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,m
.
Using the chain rule to differentiate the equations (
1
) in
\mathbf{e}
, we obtain that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{0}.

Since the Jacobian matrix
\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

is assumed to be nonsingular, we derive that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}.

This proves (
3
).

Using the chain rule to differentiate
J
in
\mathbf{e}
and
using the equation (
3
), we obtain that

\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=-\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}.

Note that
\mathbf{S}
is treated as an independent variable in
\frac{\partial J}{\partial\mathbf{e}}
on
the right hand side of the above equation.
This proves the equation (
4
).

If the conditions (
5
) and (
6
) are satisfied, then, multiplying the
equations (
3
) and (
4
)
by
\mathbf{e}
, one can readily derive the equations (
7
) and (
8
).

3
The Proof of the Connectivity Theorem

.

The connectivity theorem can be stated in a general
mathematical form
as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S}))=\mathbf{0},

(9)

where
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
,

\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{m})
,

\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v_{1}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})\\
\vdots\\
v_{n}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S})\end{array}\right)

is a differentiable vector function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S}))=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{1}(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S}))\\
\vdots\\
F_{m}(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{S}))\end{array}\right)

is a differentiable vector function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Assume that the Jacobian matrices

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial v_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial v_{n}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial v_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial v_{n}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial S_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial S_{m}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial S_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial S_{m}}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial e_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial e_{n}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial e_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial e_{n}}\end{array}\right),

are nonsingular.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{I},

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{0},

(13)

where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix.
Note that
\mathbf{S}
is treated as an independent variable in
\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}

in the above equation.
Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\mbox{if }\;i\neq j,

(14)

\displaystyle e_{i}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial e_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

(15)

then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{e_{k}}{v_{k}}\frac{\partial S_{i}}{\partial e_{k}}\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial S_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\mbox{if }\;i=j,\\
0&\mbox{if }\;i\neq j,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{e_{k}}{v_{k}}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial e_{k}}\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial S_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(19)

Proof
. Since the Jacobian matrix
\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

is assumed to be nonsingular,
the implicit
function theorem (see, e.g.,
[
5
, page 148]
) ensures that the system (
9
)
can be solved for
S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}
to get
S_{i}=S_{i}(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,m
.
Using the chain rule to differentiate the system (
9
) in
\mathbf{e}
, we obtain that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{0}.

(20)

Since the Jacobian matrix
\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

is assumed to be nonsingular, we derive that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}.

Multiplying this equation by
\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}

gives that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{v}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=-\mathbf{I}.

This proves (
12
).

Using the chain rule to differentiate
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathbf{e}
, we obtain that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}.

Note that
\mathbf{S}
is treated as an independent variable in
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}
on
the right hand side of the above equation.
Multiplying this equation by
\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}
and using
the equation (
13
),
we obtain that

\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{S}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{S}}=\mathbf{0}.

Note that
\mathbf{S}
is treated as an independent variable in
\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}

in the above equation.
This proves the equation (
13
).

If the conditions (
14
) and (
15
) are satisfied, then
\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}

becomes a simple diagonal matrix:

\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial\mathbf{e}}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left(\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial e_{1}}\right)^{-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\left(\frac{\partial v_{2}}{\partial e_{2}}\right)^{-1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\left(\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial e_{n}}\right)^{-1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{llll}\frac{e_{1}}{v_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\frac{e_{2}}{v_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\frac{e_{n}}{v_{n}}\end{array}\right),

and therefore, the equations (
12
) and
(
13
) become the equations
(
2
) and (
19
),
respectively.

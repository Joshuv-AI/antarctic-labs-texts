Hessian descent for spherical spin glasses with uniform log-Sobolev disorder
Fu-Hsuan Ho

Abstract
The present work concerns spherical spin glass models with disorder satisfying a uniform logarithmic Sobolev inequality. We show that the Hessian descent algorithm introduced in [ 10 ] can be extended to this setting, thanks to the abundance of small eigenvalues near the edge of the Hessian spectrum. Combined with the ground state universality recently proven in [ 9 ] , this implies that when the model is in the full-RSB phase, the Hessian descent algorithm can find a near-minimum with high probability. Our proof consists of two main ingredients. First, we show that the empirical spectral distribution of the Hessian converges to a semicircular law via the moment method. Second, we use the logarithmic Sobolev inequality to establish concentration and obtain uniform control of the spectral edge.

Introduction

Spherical spin glasses are random polynomial functions—called Hamiltonians—defined on high-dimensional spheres, where the coefficients are typically taken to be i.i.d. standard Gaussian variables. These models have served as a natural testing ground for studying optimization problems involving high-dimensional, non-convex objective functions. In a seminal work
[
10
]
, Subag introduced the Hessian descent algorithm, which proceeds as follows: starting from the origin of the Euclidean ball, the algorithm follows, at each step, the direction corresponding to the most negative eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian’s Hessian matrix. Once the path reaches the boundary sphere, the algorithm outputs the final point. When the model satisfies the so-called full-RSB condition, Subag showed that, with high probability, this procedure finds a point on the sphere whose Hamiltonian value is near the global minimum.

The goal of the present work is to generalize Subag’s result to spherical spin glasses with non-Gaussian disorder, specifically those whose coefficients satisfy a uniform logarithmic Sobolev inequality. Since the algorithm descends along directions associated with the left edge of the spherical Hessian spectrum, it is crucial to ensure that the spectrum indeed has many small (negative) eigenvalues throughout the descent. Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, shows that this spectral edge behavior persists with high probability under the log-Sobolev condition. On the other hand, a recent result of Sawhney and Sellke
[
9
]
establishes the universality of the ground state energy, demonstrating that, under mild moment assumptions, the minimum value of the Hamiltonian is asymptotically the same as in the Gaussian case. Putting these pieces together, we conclude that for full-RSB models with log-Sobolev disorder, the Hessian descent algorithm still finds near-optimal configurations with high probability—just as in the Gaussian case.

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{(r)}_{N}(\bm{x})=H^{(r)}_{N}(\bm{x};\tilde{J}^{(r)}),\quad\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}.

For the Gaussian Hamiltonian, as explained in the fourth paragraph in Section of
[

, with the zero eigenvalue removed, the eigenvalues of

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{N}}\xi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\bm{x}})^{1/2}\bm{G},

(38)

To prove (

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}}\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\bm{x}})^{k/2}N}\sup_{\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}}\absolutevalue{
\EX{\Tr(\nabla^2 H_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
-
\EX{\Tr(\nabla^2 \tilde{H}_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}}=0.

By the triangle inequality, the left-hand side above is bounded by

\displaystyle\text{{I}}+\text{{II}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}}\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\bm{x}}^{k/2})N}\absolutevalue{
\EX{\Tr(\nabla_E^2 \tilde{H}_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
-
\EX{\Tr(\nabla^2 \tilde{H}_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
}

\displaystyle+\sup_{\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}}\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\bm{x}})^{k/2}N}\absolutevalue{
\EX{\Tr(\nabla_E^2 H_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
-
\EX{\Tr(\nabla_E^2 \tilde{H}_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
}.

(39)

We first argue that Term I in (

.
Now, since for all

(\tilde{H}_{r}(\bm{x}))

\bm{x}
in Term I by the north pole

. This yields

\displaystyle\text{
Term~{I} in \eqref{eq:moment convergence}
}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{k}}\absolutevalue{T_{\bm{j}}},

(40)

where

\displaystyle T_{\bm{j}}=\mathbb{E}\left[(\nabla_{E}^{2}\tilde{H}_{N}^{(p_{1})}(\bm{n}_{\bm{x}}))_{j_{1},j_{2}}\cdots(\nabla_{E}^{2}\tilde{H}_{N}^{(p_{k-1})}(\bm{n}_{\bm{x}}))_{j_{k-1},j_{k}}(\nabla_{E}^{2}\tilde{H}_{N}^{(p_{k})}(\bm{n}_{\bm{x}}))_{j_{k},j_{k+1}}\right]

with the convention that
j_{k+1}=j_{1}
.
Define an graph
G_{\bm{j}}=(V_{\bm{j}},E_{\bm{j}})
where the vertex set

consists of the distinct tuples of
\bm{j}
and the edge set (permitting loops) is defined as

\displaystyle E_{\bm{j}}=\{\{j_{1},j_{2}\},\ldots,\{j_{k-1},j_{k}\},\{j_{k},j_{k+1}\}\}.

Define

by

for all

.

By the independence and centering of the entries, we see that

and for each

. Also, note that

\absolutevalue{V_{\bm{j}}}\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1

is of multiplicity at least

. For each

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(\partial_{j_{1}}\partial_{j_{2}}\tilde{H}_{N}^{(p_{r})}(\bm{n}_{\bm{x}}))^{2}\right]\leq 10p_{r}^{4}

By the Hölder inequality, (

\norm{\cdot}_{s}\leq\norm{\cdot}
for all

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:trace}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(p,k)}{N^{1+k/2}}\sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{k}}\bm{1}\{\absolutevalue{V_{\bm{j}}}=t\}\bm{1}\{\exists i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket:j_{i}=N\}

It remains to consider Term II in (

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{
\frac{1}{N}
\EX{\Tr(\nabla_E^2 H_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}
-
\frac{1}{N}
\EX{\Tr(\nabla_E^2 \tilde{H}_N^{\leq p}(\bm{x}))^k}

\gamma_{p_1}\cdots\gamma_{p_k}

}.

(42)

We claim that there exists a constant

and
\bm{x}\in\bm{B}^{N}

\displaystyle D(\bm{x},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\absolutevalue{
\frac{1}{N} \EX{\Tr\nabla_E^2 H^{(p_1)}_{N}(\bm{x})\cdots\nabla_E^2 H^{(p_k)}_{N}(\bm{x})}

Proof of Lemma

Thanks to Lemma

\displaystyle d_{Q}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{Q}}\sum_{r\in I(Q\cap(P\times\{1,2\}))}\absolutevalue*{P}-\absolutevalue*{I^{-1}(r)\cap(P\times\{1,2\})}\geq 0.

On Qualitative Preference in Alternating-time Temporal Logic with Strategy Contexts
Dimitar P. Guelev

Abstract
We show how to add and eliminate binary preference on plays in Alternating-time Temporal Logic with Strategy Contexts ( ATL 𝑠𝑐 ∗ {\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*} ) on Concurrent Game Models (CGMs) by means of a translation which preserves satisfaction in models where preference-indiscernibility between plays is an equivalence relation of finite index. The elimination technique also works for a companion second-order path quantifier, which makes quantified path variables range over sets of plays that are closed under preference-indiscernibility. We argue that the preference operator and the specialized quantifier facilitate formulating interesting solution concepts such as Nash equilibrium and secure equilibrium in a straightforward way. We also present a novel translation from ATL 𝑠𝑐 ∗ {\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*} to Quantified Computation Tree Logic ( QCTL ∗ \mathrm{QCTL}^{*} ). Together with the translation which eliminates preference and the specialized form of quantification, this translation allows reasoning about infinite multiplayer synchronous games on CGMs to be translated from the proposed extension of ATL 𝑠𝑐 ∗ {\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*} into QCTL ∗ \mathrm{QCTL}^{*} . The setting is related to that of ordered objectives in the works of Bouyer, Brenguier, Markey and Ummels, except that our focus is on the use of the temporal logic languages mentioned above, and we rely on translations into QCTL ∗ \mathrm{QCTL}^{*} for the algorithmic solutions. Keywords: infinite multiplayer games ⋅ \cdot alternating-time temporal logic ⋅ \cdot qualitative preference.



Introduction

Reasoning about infinite multiplayer games is the intended application of temporal logics of strategic ability. Solving a game means finding the strategies that players can be expected to adopt with their various objectives in mind. Players are assumed to choose their actions rationally; the inherent ambiguity of this assumption and the diversity of games have given rise to a variety of
solution concepts

Apart from monadic second order predicate theories such as that of

Concurrent Game Models (CGMs) possibly with some additional component to represent, e.g., epistemic indistinguishability, and various forms of

The obvious way for fitting the complete picture of a game where players have multiple objectives and solutions depend on the relative desirability of these objectives is to consider an extension by a preference operator, and this is the main topic of this paper. 1.4

The syntax of

A\,::=\,\bot\,\mid\,p\,\mid\,A\Rightarrow A\,\mid\,\exists B\,\mid\,\exists pA\ \ \ \ \ B\,::=\,A\,\mid\,B\Rightarrow B\,\mid\,\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B\,\mid\,{({B}{\mathsf{U}}{B})}

\mathbf{w}\in R^{\mathrm{inf}}_{M^{T}}(w)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M^{T},w\not\models\bot\\
M^{T},w\models p&\mbox{iff}&V(w,p)\\
M^{T},w\models A_{1}\Rightarrow A_{2}&\mbox{iff}&M^{T},w\models A_{2}\mbox{ or }M^{T},w\not\models A_{1}\\
M^{T},w\models\exists B&\mbox{iff}&M^{T},\mathbf{w}\models B\mbox{ for some }\mathbf{w}\in R^{\mathrm{inf}}_{M^{T}}(w)\\
M^{T},w\models\exists pA&\mbox{iff}&(M^{T})_{p}^{X},w\models A\mbox{ for some }X\subseteq W^{T}\\
\\
M^{T},w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models A&\mbox{iff}&M^{T},w^{0}\models A\\
M^{T},\mathbf{w}\models B_{1}\Rightarrow B_{2}&\mbox{iff}&M^{T},\mathbf{w}\models B_{2}\mbox{ or }M^{T},\mathbf{w}\not\models B_{1}\\
M^{T},w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B&\mbox{iff}&M^{T},w^{1}w^{2}\ldots\models B\\
M^{T},w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models{({B_{1}}{\mathsf{U}}{B_{2}})}&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{for some }n<\omega,\ M^{T},w^{n}w^{n+1}\ldots\models B_{2}\\
&&\mbox{and }M^{T},w^{k}w^{k+1}\ldots\models B_{1},\ k=0,\ldots,n-1.\end{array}

Here, for any Kripke model
M{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{W,w_{I},R,V}\rangle
and
p\in AP
,
M_{p}^{X}{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{W,w_{I},R,V_{p}^{X}}\rangle
where

V_{p}^{X}(w,q){\,\hat{=}\,}V(w,q)
for

and
V_{p}^{X}(w,p){\,\hat{=}\,}w\in X
. A state formula

is valid in
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
, written

, if
M^{T},w_{I}^{T}\models A
for all

, provided the vocabulary includes all of

Propositional logic constant
\top
and connectives
\neg
,
\vee
,
\wedge
and

, which are expressible by
\bot
and
\Rightarrow
, are used as usual. Linear Temporal Logic (
\mathrm{LTL}
) can be viewed as the logic of path formulas

in
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
with

of the BNF allowed to be only

or an atomic proposition, and not any state formula.
\mathrm{LTL}
abbreviations

of

Using

instead of

wrt the tree semantics, stems from the results in

.

An in-depth up-to-date presentation on the whole topic can be found in

.

1.5
Guarded Normal Form in Linear Temporal Logic

\mathrm{LTL}
formulas
B
in general are known to admit the
Guarded Normal Form (GNF)

\bigvee\limits_{k}B_{k}\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k}^{\prime}

where the
guard
(
head
) formulas
B_{k}
are built using only atomic propositions,
\bot
and
\Rightarrow
, and form a
full system
, which means that
B_{k}
are pairwise inconsistent and
\bigvee\limits_{k}B_{k}
is valid. GNFs can be obtained by induction on the construction of the given
B
using, among other things, the fixpoint expansion
{({B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{U}}{B^{\prime\prime}})}\Leftrightarrow B^{\prime\prime}\vee B^{\prime}\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}{({B^{\prime}}{\mathsf{U}}{B^{\prime\prime}})}
. Given a path formula
C
, we assume
{\mathit{Cl}}(C)
, the
closure
of
C
, to be some fixed set that includes
C
and the
tail
formulas
B_{k}^{\prime}
from some GNF of every formula
B
in it.
Since the
modal depth

d(B_{K}^{\prime})
of the
tail
formulas
B_{k}^{\prime}
can be chosen not to exceed that of the considered
B
in GNFs,
{\mathit{Cl}}(B)
can be chosen to be finite.

1.6
Alternating-time Temporal Logic with Strategy Contexts

In
{\rm ATL}
s,
strategy contexts
are partial strategy profiles, which appear on the LHS of
\models
. Upon evaluating the game-theoretic modality
\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle
of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}
, which requires the existence of some appropriate strategies for players
i\in\Gamma
, players
i\not\in\Gamma
who have strategies for them in the reference strategy context are assumed to be sticking to these strategies. This enables the expressive power to be brought closer to what assignments to strategy-valued variables contribute in
{\rm SL}
s. The idea is implemented in several variants. To the best of our knowledge, the earliest ones are
[
WvdHW07
,
BLLM09
,
LLM10
,
WHY11
]
. In this paper we work with the system of
{\rm ATL}
with strategy profiles known as
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
from
[
LLM10
]
. In the notation of our paper, the definition of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
is as follows.

State formulas
A
and path formulas
B
have the syntax

A\,::=\,\bot\,\mid\,p\,\mid\,A\Rightarrow A\,\mid\,\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle B\,\mid\,\cdot\!\rangle\Gamma\langle\!\cdot A\ \ \ \ \ B\,::=\,A\,\mid\,B\Rightarrow B\,\mid\,\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B\,\mid\,{({B}{\mathsf{U}}{B})}

where
\Gamma\subseteq{\Sigma}
is a set of players from the considered CGMs.
\langle\!\cdot.\cdot\!\rangle
is known as the
game-theoretic
or
co-operation
modality in
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
. The role of
\cdot\!\rangle.\langle\!\cdot
is explained below.

Let
M{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{W,w_{I},\langle{{\mathit{Act}}_{i}:i\in{\Sigma}}\rangle,o,V}\rangle
be a CGM with
{\Sigma}
as the set of players and
AP
as the vocabulary. A
strategy for
i\in{\Sigma}
starting from
w\in W
is a function
s:R^{\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w)\rightarrow{\mathit{Act}}_{i}
. A strategy
profile
for
\Gamma\subseteq{\Sigma}
from
w
is a tuple
\rho{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{s_{i}:i\in\Gamma}\rangle
where
s_{i}:R^{\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w)\rightarrow{\mathit{Act}}_{i}
for every
i\in\Gamma
. Equivalently, a strategy profile
\rho
is a function on
\Gamma
such that
\rho(i)=s_{i}
. Hence we can we write
\mathrm{dom}\,\rho
for
\Gamma
and apply set-theoretic notation about functions in general.

Given strategy profiles
\rho^{\prime}
and
\rho
, we write
\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho
for the extension
\rho^{\prime}\cup\rho|_{{\Sigma}\setminus\mathrm{dom}\,\rho^{\prime}}
of
\rho
by
\rho^{\prime}
, with the strategies from
\rho^{\prime}
overriding those from
\rho
for
i\in\mathrm{dom}\,\rho\cap\mathrm{dom}\,\rho^{\prime}
.

Given
\mathbf{w}{\,\hat{=}\,}w^{0}\ldots w^{|\mathbf{w}|-1}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w^{0})
and a strategy
s
for
i\in{\Sigma}
from
w^{0}
, we write
\mathbf{w}^{-1}s
for the strategy for
i
which prescribes the same actions as
s
, but is defined on finite histories that start at
w^{|\mathbf{w}|-1}
:
(\mathbf{w}^{-1}s)(\mathbf{v}){\,\hat{=}\,}s(w^{0}\ldots w^{|\mathbf{w}|-2}\cdot\mathbf{v})
for
\mathbf{v}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w^{|\mathbf{w}|-1})
. Informally,
\mathbf{w}^{-1}s
tells
i
how to continue playing by
s
, once the properly past part
w^{0}\ldots w^{|\mathbf{w}|-2}
of
\mathbf{w}
is no longer observable. The notation extends to profiles:
\mathbf{w}^{-1}\rho{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{\mathbf{w}^{-1}s_{i}:i\in\mathrm{dom}\,\rho}\rangle
. This notation can be traced back to Brzozowski derivatives on formal languages
[
Brz64
]
.

The infinite plays which can take place if strategy profile
\rho
from state
w\in W
is followed by the players from
\mathrm{dom}\,\rho
and the actions of the other players are unrestricted form the set

{\mathrm{out}}(w,\rho){\,\hat{=}\,}\{w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\in R^{\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w):(\forall k<\omega)(\exists b\in{\mathit{Act}}_{{\Sigma}\setminus\mathrm{dom}\,\rho})(w^{k+1}=o(w^{k},\rho(w^{0}\ldots w^{k})\cup b))\}.

In
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
, satisfaction has the form
M,\rho,w\models A
with
w\in W
and
\rho
being a strategy profile from state
w
for state formulas
A
, and
M,\rho,w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models B
with
w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\in R^{\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w^{0})
and
\rho
being a strategy profile from
w^{0}
for path formulas
B
. In both cases
\mathrm{dom}\,\rho
can be any subset of
{\Sigma}
and
\rho
is called the
strategy context
. The empty strategy profile is written
\langle{}\rangle
. The clauses are as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M,\rho,w\not\models\bot\\
M,\rho,w\models p&\mbox{iff}&V(w,p)\\
M,\rho,w\models A_{1}\Rightarrow A_{2}&\mbox{iff}&M,\rho,w\models A_{2}\mbox{ or }M,\rho,w\not\models A_{1}\\
M,\rho,w\models\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle B&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{there exists a strategy profile }\rho^{\prime}\mbox{ for }\Gamma\mbox{ from }w,\\
&&\mbox{such that }M,\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho,\mathbf{w}\models B\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{w}\in{\mathrm{out}}(w,\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho)\\
M,\rho,w\models\cdot\!\rangle\Gamma\langle\!\cdot A&\mbox{iff}&M,\rho|_{{\Sigma}\setminus\Gamma},w\models A\\
\\
M,\rho,w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models A&\mbox{iff}&M,\rho,w^{0}\models A\\
M,\rho,\mathbf{w}\models B_{1}\Rightarrow B_{2}&\mbox{iff}&M,\rho,\mathbf{w}\models B_{2}\mbox{ or }M,\rho,\mathbf{w}\not\models B_{1}\\
M,\rho,w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B&\mbox{iff}&M,(w^{0})^{-1}\rho,w^{1}w^{2}\ldots\models B\\
M,\rho,w^{0}w^{1}\ldots\models{({B_{1}}{\mathsf{U}}{B_{2}})}&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{for some }n<\omega,\ M,(w^{0}\ldots w^{n})^{-1}\rho,w^{n}w^{n+1}\ldots\models B_{2}\\
&&\mbox{and }M,(w^{0}\ldots w^{k})^{-1}\rho,w^{k}w^{k+1}\ldots\models B_{1},\ k=0,\ldots,n-1.\end{array}

A (state) formula
A
is
valid
in
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
, if
M,\langle{}\rangle,w_{I}\models A
in all CGMs
M
whose propositional vocabulary and set of players include the atomic propositions and the players appearing in
A
.

Informally,
M,\rho,w\models\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle B
means that there exists a strategy profile
\rho^{\prime}
for the members of
\Gamma
which, if augmented with whatever strategies are there in the context profile
\rho
for players outside
\Gamma
, enforces
B
whatever the players outside
\Gamma
and the context profile do.
B
itself is evaluated with the considered strategies
\rho^{\prime}
for
\Gamma
added to the context
\rho
, replacing whatever strategies for these players are already in the context
\rho
for evaluating
\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle B
. This means that the context profile generally grows to include strategies for more players upon moving to subformulas of
\langle\!\cdot.\cdot\!\rangle
-formulas. To control this,
\cdot\!\rangle\Delta\langle\!\cdot A
is used for ‘deleting’ the strategies for the members of
\Delta
from the context before evaluating
A
, thus removing the restriction on the players from
\Delta
to stick to any particular strategies upon evaluating
\langle\!\cdot.\cdot\!\rangle
-subformulas of
A
. Unless in the scope of an
\cdot\!\rangle.\langle\!\cdot
, the
\langle\!\cdot.\cdot\!\rangle
-subformulas of the
B
to be enforced must hold in the context
\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho
. The basic
{\rm ATL}
form
\langle\!\langle\Gamma\rangle\!\rangle
of the game-theoretic modality can be expressed as
\cdot\!\rangle{\Sigma}\langle\!\cdot\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle
, with the
\cdot\!\rangle{\Sigma}\langle\!\cdot
serving to clear the context strategy profile. Hence no particular (rational) behaviour on behalf of the non-members of a coalition can be taken in account by
{\rm ATL}
’s form of the construct:
\langle\!\langle\Gamma\rangle\!\rangle B
holds only if the members of
\Gamma
can enforce
B
whatever the others do.

Classical propositional logic derived constant
\top
and connectives, and
\mathrm{LTL}
derived connectives are included in
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
as usual. The universal dual of
\langle\!\cdot.\cdot\!\rangle
is
[\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!]B{\,\hat{=}\,}\neg\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle\neg B
. Informally,
[\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!]B
means that
\Gamma
cannot prevent
B
,
even if the non-members who have strategies in the context profile stick to these strategies
.

The
\mathrm{CTL}
(and
\mathrm{CTL}^{*}
and
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
) path quantifier
\exists
on the Kripke model that underlies a given CGM
M
can be expressed as
[\!\cdot\emptyset\cdot\!]
, which is equivalent to its basic
{\rm ATL}
form
[\![\emptyset]\!]
: the empty coalition can prevent nothing. Hence
\mathrm{CTL}
immediately embeds already into
{\rm ATL}
without strategy contexts, with or without the
∗
.

Much like
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
,
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
has a subset
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}
where only state formulas are considered, and those are defined by requiring every occurrence of an
\mathrm{LTL}
operator to be preceded by a
\langle\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!\rangle
or a
[\!\cdot\Gamma\cdot\!]
. Thanks to the availability of strategy contexts, this subset has the same ultimate expressive power as
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}

[
LLM10
,
LM15
]
, whereas
\mathrm{CTL}
is strictly less expressive than
\mathrm{CTL}^{*}
.

1.7

We work with preference modeled as a binary relation

The commonest binary preference relations are partial or linear orders or pre-orders. The reader is referred to

. This means that preferences become updated according to the progress made on achieving them but remain ultimately unchanged. The precise meaning of this is given in symbols in the opening of Section

.

In the rest of the paper, we tacitly assume

to stand for some Kripke model
\langle{W,w_{I},R,V}\rangle
, or CGM

, resp., a system
\langle{<_{i}:i\in{\Sigma}}\rangle
of preference relations can be there too, if the extensions of

, resp.
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
by preference proposed in the following sections of this paper are to be interpreted in

.

In our translation of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
into

\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
-defined restrictions on their valuations. Let

in

(2)

2.2
The Translation

Given an
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}

and some subformula

of its, evaluating

at

requires evaluating

for contexts

in

. We write

to indicate the propositional variables

which encode the strategies from the contexts for

\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
-translation of

is supposed to be equivalent to

when evaluated wrt the empty context
\langle{}\rangle

We define

by induction on the construction of occurrences of state subformulas

and path subformulas

in

so that, if

is a strategy profile for some

The defining clauses for state formulas are as follows:

\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}

In this section we introduce the extension

of
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
by a binary temporal preference modality

on abstract possible worlds from

[

,
vBGR09
]

[
GP92
]

\mathrm{CTL}

. The syntax of

includes state formulas of the form

where

and

are path formulas. Informally,

holds, if all the infinite
B_{2}
-paths that start at the reference state are preferred over all the

-ones. To keep the use of parentheses down,

is assumed to bind stronger than the other connectives. We take in account that preference is

: it becomes updated along plays according to the progress players make in pursuit of their objectives but does not change otherwise. This evolution of preference is specific to the temporal setting.

antireflexivity
and can be expressed by axioms such as

B_{1}<B_{2}\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{2}\wedge B_{2}<B_{3}\Rightarrow B_{1}<B_{3}

(5)

and

B_{1}<B_{2}\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg(B_{1}\wedge B_{2}).

(6)

\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}
appears in these axioms because
<
is compatible with the
strict interpretation
of temporal connectives as known in temporal logic, whereas we are using the non-strict interpretation for
{({.}{\mathsf{U}}{.})}
and
\exists
: the operands of
<
are evaluated at outgoing infinite paths with their starting states being the
successors
of the reference state and
\exists
is about outgoing paths that include the reference state as their shared initial state.

The informal reading of (
5
) is
if all the
B_{1}
-plays are worse than any
B_{2}
-play, and all the
B_{3}
-plays are better than any
B_{2}
-play, and a
B_{2}
-play exists, then all
B_{3}
-plays are better than all the
B_{1}
-plays.
The existence of a
B_{2}
-play (
\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{2}
) is key for the soundness of (
5
):
B_{1}<\bot
and
\bot<B_{3}
are vacuously true. Axiom (
6
) states that no play is preferable over itself, i.e., preference is strict.

Here follow the properties of
<
and the respective axioms which we do use. The extensionality of
<
is expressed by the axiom

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{1})&\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B_{1}^{\prime}\Rightarrow B_{2}^{\prime})\wedge\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B_{1}^{\prime\prime}\Rightarrow B_{2}^{\prime\prime})\Rightarrow(B_{2}^{\prime}<B_{2}^{\prime\prime}\Rightarrow B_{1}^{\prime}<B_{1}^{\prime\prime})\end{array}

Since
M^{T},\prec,w\models B_{1}<B_{2}
is defined with
\mathbf{w}_{1}
and
\mathbf{w}_{2}
quantified universally,
<
additionally satisfies the axioms:

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{2})&(B_{1}^{\prime}\vee B_{2}^{\prime})<(B_{1}^{\prime\prime}\vee B_{2}^{\prime\prime})\Leftrightarrow\bigwedge\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{2}B_{k_{1}}^{\prime}<B_{k_{2}}^{\prime\prime}\\
(<_{3})&\bot<B,\ \ \ B<\bot\end{array}

Axioms (
5
), (
6
),
(<_{1})
,
(<_{2})
and
(<_{3})
appear in the axiomatisation of an epistemic logic of preferences in
[
NO23
]
.

3.3
Finitely-defined Preference and Eliminating
<
at a Single State

Consider the
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}_{<}
model
M{\,\hat{=}\,}\langle{W,w_{I},R,<,V}\rangle
, which we assume to be a tree model. Let
w\in W
. Paths
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2}\in R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)
are
indiscernible wrt
\prec\subseteq R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)\times R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)
, written
\mathbf{w}_{1}\sim_{\prec}\mathbf{w}_{2}
, if

\mathbf{w}_{1}\prec\mathbf{v}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{w}_{2}\prec\mathbf{v}\mbox{ and }\mathbf{v}\prec\mathbf{w}_{1}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{v}\prec\mathbf{w}_{2}\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{v}\in R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w).

In words,
\mathbf{w}_{1}
and
\mathbf{w}_{2}
are indiscernible, if they are related in the same way to all the other paths from
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)
. Applications often lead to
\prec
such that the quotient set
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w_{I})/_{\sim_{\prec}}
is finite. The equivalence classes from
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w_{I})/_{\sim_{\prec}}
can be viewed as objectives and the quotient relation
\prec{\hskip-3.61371pt}/_{\sim_{\prec}}
, which is defined by the equivalence

\mathbf{w}_{1}/_{\sim_{\prec}}\prec{\hskip-3.61371pt}/_{\sim_{\prec}}\mathbf{w}_{2}/_{\sim_{\prec}}{\,\hat{=}\,}\mathbf{w}_{1}\prec\mathbf{w}_{2},

can be viewed as preference
on these objectives
.

Let
\mathbf{w}\in R^{-,\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w_{I})
,
\prec=<_{\mathbf{w}}
, and
w=\mathbf{w}^{|\mathbf{w}|-1}
. Assume that, along with being finitely many, the equivalence classes in
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)/_{\sim_{\prec}}
are
\mathrm{LTL}
-definable, that is, there exist some
\mathrm{LTL}
formulas
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
such that

R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)/_{\sim_{\prec}}=\{\|B_{k}\|_{M,\prec,w}:k=1,\ldots,K\}

(7)

where

\|B\|_{M,\prec,w}{\,\hat{=}\,}\{\mathbf{w}\in R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w):M,\prec_{\mathbf{w}^{0}},\mathbf{w}\models B\}.

If no two formulas define the same class from
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)/_{\sim_{\prec}}
, then
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
can be chosen so that
\vdash\neg(B_{k_{1}}\wedge B_{k_{2}})
for
k_{1}\not=k_{2}
and
\vdash\bigvee\limits_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}
in the logic
\mathrm{LTL}
, and not just
\|B_{k_{1}}\|_{M,\prec,w}\cap\|B_{k_{2}}\|_{M,\prec,w}=\emptyset
and
\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{K}\|B_{k}\|_{M,\prec,w}=\bigcup R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)/_{\sim_{\prec}}=R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)
in
M
. This means that
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
form a
full system
. Given some arbitrary path formulas
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
, at
M,\prec,w
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}})&\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\Rightarrow((B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})<(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\Leftrightarrow B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}})\end{array}

because
\prec
holds for either all or none of the pairs of paths that satisfy
B_{k_{1}}
and
B_{k_{2}}
, respectively.

Now consider the chain of equivalences

\begin{array}[]{lcll}B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}&\Leftrightarrow&\bigg(B^{\prime}\wedge\bigvee\limits_{k_{1}=1}^{K}B_{k_{1}}\bigg)<\bigg(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge\bigvee\limits_{k_{2}=1}^{K}B_{k_{2}}\bigg)&\mbox{by }(<_{1})\mbox{ and }\vdash\bigvee\limits_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k_{1}=1}^{K}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\bigg)<\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k_{2}=1}^{K}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\bigg)&\mbox{by }(<_{1})\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\bigwedge\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{K}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})<(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})&\mbox{by }(<_{2})\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\bigwedge\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{K}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\\
\ \ \ \Rightarrow(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})<(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\end{array}\right)&\mbox{by }(<_{1})\mbox{ and }(<_{3})\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\bigwedge\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{K}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\\
\ \ \ \Rightarrow(B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}})\end{array}\right)&\mbox{by }(<_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}})\end{array}

(8)

This chain shows that, if
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
as described are available at
M,\prec,w
, then the use of
<
can be restricted to operands from among
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
. Furthermore, given the set

P{\,\hat{=}\,}\{\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle\in\{1,\ldots,K\}^{2}:M,\prec,w\models B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}\}

of the pairs
k_{1},k_{2}
for which
M,\prec,w\models B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}
,
M,\prec,w\models B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}
can be expressed with no use of
<
at all: Let

(B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{K},P}{\,\hat{=}\,}\bigwedge\limits_{\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle\in\{1,\ldots,K\}^{2}\setminus P}\neg(\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}}))\ .

(9)

Then obviously
M,\prec,w\models B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow(B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{K},P}
.
This means that, giving a list of
\mathrm{LTL}
formulas
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
such that (
7
) holds for
w=w_{I}
, and stating whether
M,<_{w_{I}},w_{I}\models B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}
for every pair of indices
k_{1},k_{2}=1,\ldots,K
, can serve as the description for
<
in
M
. In Section
3.4
below we show how the availability of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
for
<_{w_{I}},w_{I}
propagates to all reachable states and the respective updates of
<
by virtue of the stability of
<
.

The ‘data’ provided by
P
should be consistent with the algebraic properties of the adopted kind of preference such as being a partial order or a pre-order. Algebraic properties such as these may facilitate high-level reasoning using axioms like (
5
) and (
6
) but, as long as
R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w_{I})/_{\sim_{<}}
is finite and the equivalence classes wrt
\sim_{<}
in it are
\mathrm{LTL}
-definable, the possibility to express
B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}
as
(B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{K},P}
does not depend on these properties.

3.4
The Stability of
<
and Eliminating Nested Occurrences of
<

Determining whether, e.g.,
M,\prec,\mathbf{w}\models{({F}{\mathsf{U}}{G})}
, requires evaluating
F
and
G
at some sequence of suffixes of
\mathbf{w}
wrt the respective updates of
\prec
. In this section and the following Section
3.5
we show how the availability of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
and
P
, which enable the elimination of
<
in
M,\prec,w\models B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}
as described in Section
3.3
above for a fixed
w
and the respective
\prec
, can be propagated to enable eliminating
<
in
M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}
for
w^{\prime}
which are reachable from
w
, that is for
w^{\prime}=\mathbf{v}^{|\mathbf{v}|-1}
,
\prec^{\prime}=\prec_{\mathbf{v}}
, where
\mathbf{v}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w)
. We consider the case of
\mathbf{v}=w^{\prime}
, i.e.,
w^{\prime}\in R(w)
, first, that is the case of
\mathbf{v}
consisting of just the state
w^{\prime}
and representing the step from
w
to
w^{\prime}
.

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
satisfy (
7
) wrt
\prec,w
. Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
be written in terms of the atomic propositions
p_{1},\ldots,p_{L}
, and consider their GNFs (see Section
1.5
):

\bigvee\limits_{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\bigwedge\limits_{l=1}^{L}\varepsilon_{l}p_{l}\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}},\ k=1,\ldots,K

(10)

where, in the
2^{L}
disjuncts above,
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}
are optional negations.
Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}
be the unique list of optional negations such that
\bigwedge\limits_{l=1}^{L}\varepsilon_{l}p_{l}

holds in the considered
w^{\prime}\in R(w)
. Since
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
form a full system,
B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
,
k=1,\ldots,K
, form a full system too. Furthermore

w^{\prime}\cdot\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\subseteq\|B_{k}\|_{M,\prec,w}

(11)

and, by the stability of preference,

\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
consists of
\prec^{\prime}
-indiscernible paths for every
k=1,\ldots,K
. Hence

R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w^{\prime})/_{\sim_{\prec^{\prime}}}=\{\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}:\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M^{T},\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\not=\emptyset,\ k=1,\ldots,K\}.

This means that the list of formulas

B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}},\ k=1,\ldots,K

(12)

can be used to express
<
wrt
\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}
as in (
9
). Furthermore, if we choose
not to remove
the formulas
B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
for which
\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M^{T},\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\emptyset
from (
12
), then (
9
) can be used wrt
\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}
with (
12
) in the positions of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
and
the same
set
P
of pairs of indices to indicate which formulas from (
12
) are related by
<
as for
\prec,w
.

The above argument can be cast in the form of deductions based on the axioms
(<_{1})
-
(<_{3})
and two more axioms about the stability of preference over time, which is reflected in the way
\prec_{w^{\prime}}
is derived from
\prec
. The first one is

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{4})&(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime})<(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime\prime})\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})\end{array}

where
b
is a modality-free formula.
The following axiom
(<_{5})
is sound only if the
\mathrm{LTL}
formulas
b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime}
and
b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime\prime}
in them define classes of preference-indiscernible paths.

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{5})&\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime}\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime\prime})\wedge\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})\Rightarrow(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime})<(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B^{\prime\prime})\end{array}

Linear time forms of
(<_{4})
and
(<_{5})
are known from the works on unary preference modalities

[
HvdHMW03
,
DK08
,
Kur09
,
BM20
]
and can be related to the axioms about
no learning
and
perfect recall
in epistemic temporal logic
[
HWJB15
]
. As it becomes clear below, axioms
(<_{4})
and
(<_{5})
are tailored to be used on the disjuncts of GNFs (cf. Section
1.5
).

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}
be such that

\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\not=\emptyset
,
k=k_{1},k_{2}
. Let
b{\,\hat{=}\,}\bigwedge\limits_{l=1}^{L}\varepsilon_{l}p_{l}
for the sake of brevity.
For the forward implication, observe that

\underbrace{\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})}_{F_{0}}\Rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}<B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})\Rightarrow\\
(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})<(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}).\end{array}\right)

(13)

is the instance of
(<_{5})
for the disjuncts
B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
from the GNF (
10
) of
B_{k}
,
k=k_{1},k_{2}
, as
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.
Since
b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
is a disjunct of the GNF of
B_{k}
, we have

\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\Leftrightarrow b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge B_{k}),\ k=k_{1},k_{2}.

Using the validity of this formula,
(<_{1})
, (
13
) and the instance

\begin{array}[]{l}\underbrace{\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge B_{k_{2}})}_{F_{1}}\Rightarrow\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ ((b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge B_{k_{1}})<(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\Leftrightarrow B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}})\end{array}

of
(<_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}})
for
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
being
b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
and
b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
, respectively, we derive

F_{0}\wedge F_{1}\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}<B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}.

Finally, since both
M,\prec,w\models F_{0}
and
M,\prec,w\models F_{1}
follow from the requirement
\|B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\not=\emptyset
,
k=k_{1},k_{2}
,
we conclude that

M,\prec,w\models\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}<B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}.

For the converse implication consider the chain of implications and equivalences

\begin{array}[]{lll}B_{k_{1}}<B_{k_{2}}&\Rightarrow(B_{k_{1}}\wedge b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})<(B_{k_{2}}\wedge b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})&\mbox{by }(<_{2})\\
&\Leftrightarrow(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})<(b\wedge\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})&\mbox{by the definition of GNF and }(<_{1})\\
&\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(b\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}<B_{k_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}})&\mbox{by }(<_{4})\end{array}

The number of the formulas
B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
which are required to define the indiscernibility classes from

R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w^{\prime})/_{\sim_{\prec^{\prime}}}
is no bigger than the number
|R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)/_{\sim_{\prec}}|
of the defining formulas
B_{k}
at
\prec,w
. A decrease can occur if some of the formulas
B_{k}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
are unsatisfiable at the outgoing paths from the chosen
w^{\prime}
, which is possible even if the respective
B_{k}
is satisfiable at some outgoing path from
w
. However, if we keep the formulas which are no longer satisfiable in the list upon moving from
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
, which apply at
\prec,w
, to
B_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}},\ldots,B_{K}^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}
, which apply at
\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}
, then the pairs of indices
\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle\in P
for which

holds, would apply for

as well.
If one or both of
\|B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{1}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
and
\|B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{2}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
are empty, then
M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{1}}<B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{2}}
would hold vacuously even in case
\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle
is not on our list.
Hence putting

P^{\prime}{\,\hat{=}\,}\{\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle\in\{1,\ldots,K\}^{2}:M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{1}}<B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{2}}\}

implies
P^{\prime}\supseteq P
, and
\langle{k_{1},k_{2}}\rangle\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
means that at least one of
\|B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{1}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
and
\|B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{k_{2}}\|_{M,\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
is
\emptyset
. According to (
9
), this means that, for any pair of path formulas
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
, the
<
-free expression
(B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})_{B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{1},\ldots,B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{K},P^{\prime}}
for
B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime}
in
\prec^{\prime},w^{\prime}
is equivalent to
(B^{\prime}<B^{\prime\prime})_{B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{1},\ldots,B^{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{L}}_{K},P}
, which involves
P
instead of
P^{\prime}
.

3.5
Eliminating All Occurrences of
<

Now let
w=w_{I}
,
\prec=<_{w_{I}}
and
w^{\prime}=\mathbf{v}^{|\mathbf{v}|-1}=v^{N}
and
\prec^{\prime}=\prec_{\mathbf{v}}
, where
\mathbf{v}=v^{1}\ldots v^{N}\in R^{-,\mathrm{fin}}_{M}(w_{I})
. Let the
\mathrm{LTL}
formulas

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M,\prec,w\models B_{1}<_{\exists\forall}B_{2}&\mbox{iff}&(\exists\mathbf{w}_{1}\in\|B_{1}\|_{M,\prec,w})(\forall\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\|B_{2}\|_{M,\prec,w})(\mathbf{w}_{1}\prec\mathbf{w}_{2})\\

\begin{array}[]{ll}(<_{2,\exists\forall})&(B_{1}^{\prime}\vee B_{2}^{\prime})\bowtie B^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k_{1}=1}^{2}B_{k_{1}}^{\prime}\bowtie B^{\prime\prime}\mbox{ for }\bowtie=<_{\exists\forall},>_{\exists\forall}\\

\begin{array}[]{l}B^{\prime}<_{\exists\forall}B^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k_{1}=1}^{K}\bigg(\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime}\wedge B_{k_{1}})\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k_{2}=1}^{K}(\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B^{\prime\prime}\wedge B_{k_{2}})\Rightarrow B_{k_{1}}<_{\forall\forall}B_{k_{2}})\bigg)\\

4
Second Order Quantification over Paths and Preference

\mathrm{CTL}
’s path quantifier
\exists
can be viewed as an (implicit) first-order quantifier on outgoing paths as it is about the existence of individual paths satisfying its operand formula. Unlike that, in this section we propose a specific preference related form of quantification over
sets
of paths. This means introducing bound symbols with the status of path formulas. E.g., we want to be able to write

M,\prec,w\models{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}\mathbf{p}(\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\mathbf{p}\wedge B^{\prime}<\mathbf{p}\wedge\mathbf{p}<B^{\prime\prime}),

(16)

which states that there exist some paths starting from
w
(with
w
itself deleted) which are better than any
B^{\prime}
-path and worse than any
B^{\prime\prime}
-path. Properties like the above can be expressed with
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}
causing
\mathbf{p}
to range over sets of paths which are
closed under preference indiscernibility
. This restriction comes at hardly any technical cost: in this section we show how the elimination technique for
<
works for
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}\mathbf{p}
too. On the other hand, if
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}\mathbf{p}A
has no occurrences of
\mathbf{p}
but in operands of
<
, possibly in the scope of
\wedge
and
\vee
, then letting
\mathbf{p}
range over all sets of paths is equivalent to letting
\mathbf{p}
range only over sets of paths that are closed under preference indiscernibility. Furthermore, it is natural to assume that any player’s objectives are closed under preference indiscernibility wrt that player’s preference relation. Hence the proposed form of quantification can facilitate expressing a variety of preference-related properties.

Before presenting the semantics of
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}
formally, we give an example of how it can be eliminated.
Consider the example formula (
16
) and let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{K}
define the classes of
\prec

R^{-,\mathrm{inf}}_{M}(w)
where
w\in W
and
\prec
is the preference relation corresponding to
w
in

. Then, in
\prec,w
, (
16
) is equivalent to

\bigvee\limits_{X\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{x\in X}B_{x}\bigg)\wedge B^{\prime}<\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{x\in X}B_{x}\bigg)\wedge\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{x\in X}B_{x}\bigg)<B^{\prime\prime}.

To realize this, observe how this formula has been obtained from (
16
):
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}
of
{\mathop{\exists}\limits_{\sim}}\mathbf{p}
has been replaced by a parameterized disjunction, with the parameter
X
ranging over the subsets of
\{1,\ldots,K\}
. For every value of
X
, the disjunction
\bigvee\limits_{x\in X}B_{x}
is a path formula which defines the corresponding union
\bigcup\limits_{x\in X}\|B_{x}\|_{M,<,w_{I}}
of the preference-indiscernibility equivalence classes of paths. Every set of paths that is closed under preference indiscernibility admits this representation. Hence
\bigvee\limits_{x\in X}B_{x}
define the exactly the sets of paths where
\mathbf{p}
is supposed to hold.
Preference can be eliminated from the above formula in the way described in Section

M,<,w_{I}\models B_{I,i,1}<_{i}B_{I,i,2}\wedge B_{I,i,2}<_{i}B_{I,i,3}.

Using the expression (
17
) for
{\mathop{\forall}\limits_{\sim}}\mathbf{p}C
being
{\mathop{\forall}\limits_{\sim}}{}_{i}\mathbf{c}(G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}\mathbf{c}\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg\mathbf{c})
, we obtain the following
{\mathop{\forall}\limits_{\sim}}{}_{i}\mathbf{c}
-free equivalent of (
18
):

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\left(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{l}B_{i,1}\in{\mathit{Cl}}(B_{I,i,1})\\
B_{i,2}\in{\mathit{Cl}}(B_{I,i,2})\\
B_{i,3}\in{\mathit{Cl}}(B_{I,i,3})\end{array}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}q_{B_{i,1},1}\wedge q_{B_{i,2},2}\wedge q_{B_{i,3},3}\Rightarrow\\
\bigwedge\limits_{X\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}\left(G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}\left(\bigvee\limits_{k\in X}B_{i,k}\right)\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k\in X}B_{i,k}\bigg)\right)\end{array}\right)\right).

Since the reference state is
w_{I}
, and the conjunction of the form
q_{B_{i,1},1}\wedge q_{B_{i,2},2}\wedge q_{B_{i,3},3}
which holds in
w_{I}
is
q_{B_{I,i,1},1}\wedge q_{B_{I,i,2},2}\wedge q_{B_{I,i,3},3}
, the formula can be simplified to

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{X\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}\bigg(G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k\in X}B_{I,i,k}\bigg)\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k\in X}B_{I,i,k}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg).

The conjuncts for
X=\emptyset
and
X={\Sigma}=\{1,2,3\}
in the above formula hold trivially. The transitivity of
<_{i}
implies that, for non-empty
X
,
G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k\in X}B_{I,i,k}\bigg)
is equivalent to
G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}B_{I,i,\min X}
. Hence the formula can be further simplified to

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}(G_{i}<_{\exists\forall,i}B_{I,i,k}\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})).

Now the elimination of
<_{i}
gives

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}<k}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(G_{i}\wedge B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})\bigg)\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg).

(19)

It remains to eliminate
\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle
, which is done using the special case of the clause about
t_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}^{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}(\langle\!\cdot\{i_{k},\ldots,i_{m}\}\cdot\!\rangle B)
from Section
2.2
for
n=0
,
\{i_{k},\ldots,i_{m}\}{\,\hat{=}\,}{\Sigma}=\{1,2\}
, and
B{\,\hat{=}\,}\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}<k}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(G_{i}\wedge B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})\bigg)\Rightarrow[\!\cdot i\cdot\!]\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)
:

t(\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle B)=\exists q_{1}\exists q_{2}\bigg(\bigwedge\limits_{i=1}^{2}\forall\Box\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{a\in{\mathit{Act}}_{i}}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(q_{i}\Leftrightarrow a)\bigg)\wedge\forall\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\Box\bigg(\bigwedge\limits_{j=1}^{2}q_{j}\bigg)\Rightarrow t_{1,2}^{q_{1},q_{2}}(H)\bigg)\bigg)

where

t_{1,2}^{q_{1},q_{2}}(H)=\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\bigg(\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}<k}\exists\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(G_{i}\wedge B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})\bigg)\Rightarrow\neg t_{1,2}^{q_{1},q_{2}}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot i\cdot\!\rangle\neg\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg).

Finally

\begin{array}[]{l}t_{1,2}^{q_{1},q_{2}}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot 1\cdot\!\rangle\neg\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,1,k^{\prime}}\bigg)=\exists r\bigg(\forall\Box(\bigvee\limits_{a\in{\mathit{Act}}_{1}}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(r\Leftrightarrow a))\wedge\forall\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\Box(q_{2}\wedge r)\Rightarrow t_{2,1}^{q_{2},r}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,1,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg)\\
t_{1,2}^{q_{1},q_{2}}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot 2\cdot\!\rangle\neg\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,2,k^{\prime}}\bigg)=\exists r\bigg(\forall\Box(\bigvee\limits_{a\in{\mathit{Act}}_{1}}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(r\Leftrightarrow a))\wedge\forall\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\Box(q_{1}\wedge r)\Rightarrow t_{1,2}^{q_{1},r}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,2,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg)\end{array}

and
t_{3-i,i}^{q_{3-i},r}(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})=\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\not=k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}
,
i=1,2
, because no game-theoretic operators occur in these formulas. This concludes the reduction of (
18
) to a
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
formula.

The elimination of
{\mathop{\forall}\limits_{\sim}}{}_{i}{\mathbf{c}}
and
<_{\exists\forall,i}
above is achieved upon reaching (
19
); the rest of the transformations are about moving to a
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
formula by expressing the occurrences of the game-theoretic operator. Here follows what (
19
), which is a purely
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
formula, states in detail. After applying Modus Tolens to the implication in (
19
) we obtain:

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot i\cdot\!\rangle\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{I,i,k}\Rightarrow\bigg(\bigwedge\limits_{k^{\prime}<k}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg(G_{i}\wedge B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})\bigg)\bigg)\bigg).

Since
B_{I,i,1},B_{I,i,2},B_{I,i,3}
is a full system,
\bigwedge\limits_{k^{\prime}<k}\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\neg(G_{i}\wedge B_{I,i,k^{\prime}})
can be written as
\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigg(G_{i}\Rightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\geq k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}\bigg)
. Hence we obtain

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigwedge\limits_{i\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{i}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot 1\cdot\!\rangle\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{I,i,k}\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigg(G_{i}\Rightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\geq k}B_{I,i,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)\bigg).

(20)

This formula states that a global (equilibrium) strategy profile exists such that objectives
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are achieved and, furthermore, if one of the players can deviate in a way that lets it achieve
B_{I,i,k}
, then the respective
G_{i}
, which is achievable by sticking to the equilibrium profile, consists of plays that are as good as those from
B_{I,i,k}
or better already. This statement is relevant for
k=2
or
k=3
. Deviating to achieve
B_{I,i,1}
is uninteresting because the
B_{I,i,1}
-plays are the least desirable ones. Given the meaning of
B_{I,i,1},B_{I,i,2},B_{I,i,3}
, the equilibria of interest are those about
G_{i}
being
B_{I,i,2}\vee B_{I,i,3}
,
i=1,2
, because player
i
has no incentive to deviate, if the equilibrium strategy already guarantees it
B_{I,i,3}
. Let’s focus on player
1
. Let
H_{2}{\,\hat{=}\,}\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}G_{2}\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot 2\cdot\!\rangle\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{I,2,k}\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigg(G_{2}\Rightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\geq k}B_{I,2,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)
for the sake of brevity.

The instance of (
20
) for
G_{1}
being
B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3}
is

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle\bigg(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3})\wedge\bigwedge\limits_{k=2}^{3}\bigg(\langle\!\cdot i\cdot\!\rangle\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{I,1,k}\Rightarrow\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}\bigg((B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3})\Rightarrow\bigvee\limits_{k^{\prime}\geq k}B_{I,1,k^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)\wedge H_{2}\bigg).

Observe that
\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}((B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3})\Rightarrow(B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3}))
is trivially true. Furthermore
\forall\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}((B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3})\Rightarrow B_{I,1,3})
is false because the class of
B_{I,1,2}
-plays is nonempty. Hence we obtain

\langle\!\cdot 1,2\cdot\!\rangle(\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}(B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3})\wedge\neg\langle\!\cdot 1\cdot\!\rangle\mathop{\mbox{\raisebox{1.7pt}{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}B_{I,1,3}\wedge H_{2}).

In words, there exists an (equilibrium) strategy profile where player
1
achieves
B_{I,1,2}\vee B_{I,1,3}
and deviating cannot improve
1
’s score to just
B_{I,1,3}
.

Concluding Remarks

In this paper we have proposed enhancing the language and semantics of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
with a binary operator of preference and a specialized quantifier which binds variables with the status of path formulas that range over the sets of plays that are closed under preference indiscernibility. None of these additions to the language of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
is completely unknown in the literature on its own right. The preference operator has been studied in both non-temporal settings
[
DK08
,
vBvOR05
]
and in a temporal setting such as that of
[
BM20
]
. Quantification with similar restrictions, yet wrt epistemic indiscernibility and sets of states as opposed to preference-indiscernibility and the sets of plays we consider, has been proposed in
[
BMM+21
]
. Our main contribution is to show that, under a reasonable assumption, these features of the language can be made available to enable formulating interesting properties of infinite multiplayer games in a way that is more convenient without the need to develop model checking for the extended system of
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
we obtain from scratch: both preference and the specialized quantifier can be eliminated and a translation into
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
, the established intermediate notation for automated reasoning used in so many logics of strategic ability now, can be used in our setting too. Among other things, we have proposed a new satisfaction preserving translation from
{\rm ATL}_{\mathit{sc}}^{*}
to
\mathrm{QCTL}^{*}
and discussed its computational complexity.

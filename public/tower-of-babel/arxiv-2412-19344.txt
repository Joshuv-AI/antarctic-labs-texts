On Krull dimension of modules over group rings of minimax abelian groups
Anatolii V. Tushev

Abstract
In the paper we obtained some estimations of Krull dimension of modules over group rings of minimax abelian groups. We also consider relations between the condition of existing of small deviation for normal subgroups and some previously studied chain conditions. Keywords . minimax groups, Krull dimension, group rings 2010 AMS Subject Classification. 16S34 , 20C07 , 11R27

Introduction

A group
G
is said to have finite (Prufer) rank if there is a positive integer
m
such that any finitely generated subgroup of
G
may be generated by
m
elements; the smallest
m
with this property is the rank
r(G)
of
G
.

Let
\Omega
be a set of subgroups of a group
G
then
\Omega
is a partially ordered set (poset for short) with respect to inclusion. The group
G
meets the minimal (maximal) condition
Min-\Omega
(
Max-\Omega
) for subgroups from
\Omega
if
G
has no infinite descending (ascending) chain of subgroups from
\Omega
. The group
G
meets the weak minimal (maximal) condition
Min-\infty-\Omega
(
Max-\infty-\Omega
) for subgroups from
\Omega
if
\left|{{H_{i}}:{H_{i+1}}}\right|<\infty
(
\left|{{H_{i+1}}:{H_{i}}}\right|<\infty
) almost for all
i
for any infinite descending (ascending) chain
\{{H_{i}}\}
of subgroups from
\Omega
.

By a Chernikov result, a soluble group
G
meets
Min
if and only if
G
is a finite extension of a direct product of finitely many of quasicyclic groups; now, such groups are called Chernikov. A soluble group
G
meets
Max
if and only if it is polycyclic, that is
G
has a finite subnormal series with cyclic factors.

A group
G
is said to be minimax if it has a finite subnormal series each of whose factor meets either
Min
or
Max.
It follows from the mentioned above description of soluble groups with
Min
and
Max
that a soluble group
G
is minimax if and only if it has a finite subnormal series each of whose factor is either cyclic or quasicyclic. A locally soluble group
G
meets
Min-\infty
or
Max-\infty
if and only if
G
is a soluble minimax group.

Let
\Omega
be a poset. For any elements
a,b\in\Omega
we put
a/b=\{x\in\Omega|a\leq x\leq b\}
. The deviation of
\Omega
can be defined as the following:

1. if
\Omega
is trivial then
dev\Omega=-1

2. if
\Omega
meets the minimal condition then
dev\Omega=0

3. for a general ordinal
\beta
,
dev\Omega=\beta
if :

( i )
dev\Omega\neq\alpha<\beta
,

( ii ) in any descending chain
\{{a_{i}}\}
of elements of
\Omega
all but finitely many factors
{a_{i}}/{a_{i+1}}
have deviation less than
\beta
.

We should note that a poset has deviation if and only if it contains no sub-poset order isomorphic to the poset of rational numbers in their usual ordering (see
[
3
, Introduction]
).

Let
R
be a ring. If
\Omega
is the set of all submodules of an
R-
module
M
then
dev\Omega
is called Krull dimension of
M
which is denoted by
{K_{R}}(M)
. The Krull dimension of a right module
{R_{R}}
is called the Krull dimension of the ring
R
and denoted by
K(R)
. In
[
11
]
it was proposed to consider groups with deviation in posets of subgroups. If
G
is a group and
\Omega
is a poset of subgroups of
G
then
de{v_{\Omega}}G
denotes the deviation of
\Omega
. If
\Omega
is the poset of all subgroups of
G
then we put
de{v_{\Omega}}G=devG
. The arguments of the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 6.1.8 ]
show that if a group
G
meets
Max-\infty-\Omega
then
de{v_{\Omega}}G
exists. Thus, the condition of existence of
de{v_{\Omega}}G
generalizes both
Min-\infty-\Omega
and
Max-\infty-\Omega
. However,

[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
shows that a soluble group has
devG
if and only if
G
is minimax.
The condition of existence of
de{v_{\Omega}}G
was considered for various posets
\Omega
of
subgroups (see
[
1
,
2
,
7
]
).

If
\Omega
is the poset of all normal subgroups of a group
G
then we call
de{v_{\Omega}}G
by Krull dimension of
G
and denote it by
K(G)
. Evidently, if a group
G
meets
Min-\infty-\Omega
then
de{v_{\Omega}}G\leq 1
. A group
G
is said to be
G
-minimax if it has a finite series of normal subgroups each of whose factor satisfies either minimal or maximal condition for
G
-invariant subgroups. It was proved in
[
11
, Theorem 4.5]
that a metabelian group
G
has Krull dimension if and only if it is
G
-minimax.

According to
[
3
]
a poset of subgroups of a group has the real chain condition (
RCC
) if it does not contain chains whose order type is the same as the set of real numbers with their usual ordering. It is easy to note that if a poset
\Omega
of subgroups of a group
G
has
de{v_{\Omega}}G
then
\Omega
meets
RCC
. However, unlike other chain conditions, existence of deviation of a poset
\Omega
of subgroups of a group

gives us a number characteristic
di{v_{\Omega}}G
of the group
G
(in the general case the number
di{v_{\Omega}}G
may be transfinite). So, one can study properties of the group
di{v_{\Omega}}G
which depend on the value of

.

In Section 2 we obtained some estimations of Krull dimension of modules over integer group rings of minimax abelian groups (Theoren 2.4). Our estimations are based on techniques developed in
[
10
,
11
]
. Then we apply our results on modules over integer group rings for studying metabelian groups with
K(G)\leq 1
. If
G
is a metabelian group and
K(G)=0
then
G
satisfies the condition
Min-N
of minimality for normal subgroup, such groups were studied in
[
5
]
.

In Section 3 we study properties of metabelian groups with
K(G)\leq 1
. In particular, we consider relations between the condition
K(G)\leq 1
and the condition no-
{\mathbb{Z}}\wr{\mathbb{Z}}
-sections which was studied in
[
6
]
. We also consider relations between the condition
K(G)\leq 1
and the weak condition of minimality

for normal subgroups which was studied in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.1

Let

(ii) if

(iii) if

Proof.

(i) The assertion follows from
[

(ii) The assertion follows from the Cohen-Seidenberg theorem.

(iii) The assertion follows from the Noether normalization lemma and (ii).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

, where

, where

(ii) either

or

the additive group of

\pi

(iii) the module

Proof.

By
[

,
an{n_{{\mathbb{Z}}G}}(a)=P
is a prime ideal of

(i) Let

(ii) If

(iii) It follows from

Let
\pi
be a finite set of prime integers, we say that an integer

. It is easy to note that fractions of

. It is not difficult to check that
K({{\mathbb{Q}}_{\pi}})=dim\,({{\mathbb{Q}}_{\pi}})=1

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
F
be a field and
G=T\times H
be a subgroup of the multiplicative group
{F^{*}}
of

, where
H
is a finitely generated free abelian group and
T
is a Chernikov group. Let
R
be a subring of

generated by the subgroup

. Suppose that the ring

has Krull dimension
K(R)
and

is the field of fractions of the domain

. Then:

(i) if

then
K(R)=tr-deg({{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(H)/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}})
=

tr-deg({{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(G)/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}})
;

(ii) if

then
K(R)=tr-deg({\mathbb{Q}}(H)/{\mathbb{Q}})+1=tr-deg({\mathbb{Q}}(G)/{\mathbb{Q}})+1
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemmas 2.1(i) and 2.2(iii) that
dim\,R=K(R)
and hence in the proof we can replace

with

.

(i) Since the quotient group

is torsion, it is not difficult to show that

is integer over
{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}[H]
. Then it follows from Lemma 2.1(ii) that

. Therefore, as the subgroup

is finitely generated, it follows from Lemma 2.1(iii) that

.

(ii) Since the quotient group
G/H
is torsion, it is not difficult to show that

is integer over
{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
. Then it follows from Lemma 2.1(ii) that
dim\,R=dim\,{\mathbb{Z}}[H]
. It follows from the Noether normalization lemma that

, where

is a maximal set of algebraically independent over

is a finite set of integer over

are integer over
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\pi}}[X]
. Then it follows from Lemma 2.1(ii) that
dim\,{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\pi}}[H]={{\mathbb{Q}}_{\pi}}[X]=1+\left|X\right|
=
tr-deg({\mathbb{Q}}(H)/{\mathbb{Q}})+1
.

It is easy to note that if

{\mathbb{Q}_{\pi}}[H]
then

and it implies that

. Therefore,
tr-deg({\mathbb{Q}}(H)/{\mathbb{Q}})+1\leq dim\,{\mathbb{Z}}[H]

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
G
be an abelian minimax group and
M
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-module which has Krull dimension

. Then:

(i) if the module
M
is annihilated by some prime integer

then
K(M)=tr-deg_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}G}(M)
;

(ii) if the module
M
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free then
K(M)=tr-deg_{{\mathbb{Z}}G}(M)+1
.

Proof.

(i) Evidently,
M
can be considered as a
{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}G
-module.
Suppose that
K(M)=n
then
M
has a cyclic
n
-critical section

. By Lemma 2.2,
G/{C_{G}}(N)=T\times H
, where
H
is a finitely generated free abelian group and
T
is a Chernikov group, and
N\cong R={{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}[T\times H]
, where
R
is a domain. Therefore, by Proposition 2.3(i),

n=tr-deg({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(H)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. It easily implies that there is a free abelian subgroup

D\leq H
whose generators are algebraically independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
r(D)=n
.
Then there is a free abelian subgroup

such

and

. It easily implies that the section

is not
{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}X
-torsion and hence

n\leq tr-deg_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}G}(M)
.

Suppose that there is a free abelian subgroup
Y\leq G
such that
M
is not
{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}Y
-torsion and
n<r(Y)
. It easily implies that the module
M
has a critical
{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}Y
-torsion-free section. Therefore,

and it follows from Proposition 2.3(i) that

but it contradicts

. Thus,

.

(ii) If
K(M)=0
then

contains a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}G
-module

[

that

.
Thus, we can assume that

.

Suppose that

has a cyclic

. By Lemma 2.2,

, where

is a domain. Therefore, by Proposition 2.3(ii),

is not

Suppose that there is a free abelian subgroup

is not

Lemma 3.2

Theorem 3.4

By

{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free
{\mathbb{Z}}B

then

contains a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}G

It follows from Theorem 2.4 that

is
{\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle g\right\rangle
-torsion, i.e.
M
has no
{\mathbb{Z}}\wr{\mathbb{Z}}
-sections.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a metabelian group with an abelian normal subgroup
A\leq G
such that the quotient group
B=G/A
is finitely generated abelian. Suppose that the quotient group
G/t(A)
has Krull dimension
K(G)\leq 1
Then the quotient group
G/t(A)
is minimax.

Proof.

The Corollary follows from Proposition 3.3 and the above theorem.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a metabelian group with an abelian normal subgroup
A\leq G
such that the quotient group
B=G/A
is finitely generated abelian. Suppose that the group
G
has Krull dimension
K(G)\leq 1
. Then the group
G
satisfies the condition
Min-\infty-N
.

Proof.

We consider
A
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}B
-module. By
[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
,
A
has a finite series of submodules each of whose factor is either Artenian or Noetherian. Then it is sufficient to show that any Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}B
-module
M
with
K(M)\leq 1
satisfies the weak condition
of minimality for submodules. Evidently the module
M
has a finite series of cyclic submodules each of whose factor is either simple or 1-critical. Then it is sufficient to show that any cyclic 1-critical
{\mathbb{Z}}B
-module
N
satisfies the weak condition of minimality for submodules. Since the module
N
is 1-critical, for any nonzero submodule
X\leq N
the quotient module
N/X
is cyclic Artenian. By
[
4
, Theorem 4]
the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}B
is Noetherian and hence the quotient module
N/X
is Noetherian. It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
that any simple
{\mathbb{Z}}B
-module is finite and it implies that the quotient module
N/X
is finite. Thus, the assertion follows.
∎

Example 3.7
.

There exists a torsion-free metabelian group
G
with Krull dimension
K(G)=1
which is not minimax and does not satisfy the condition
Min-\infty-N
.

Proof.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,
H
be a quasicyclic
p
-subgroup of
{F^{*}}
and
g=\frac{1}{p}
. Let
R={\mathbb{Z}}[H\times\left\langle g\right\rangle]
then, by
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
,
K(R)=1
. The additive group
{R^{+}}\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{\infty}{({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})_{i}}
Therefore,
{R^{+}}
is not minimax and for any prime integer
q\neq p
we have infinite descending chain

\{R{q^{n}}\,|\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
of ideals with infinite sections. Then the split extension

G=R\leftthreetimes(H\times\left\langle g\right\rangle)
has Krull dimension
K(G)=1
, is not minimax and does not satisfy the condition
Min-\infty-N
.
∎

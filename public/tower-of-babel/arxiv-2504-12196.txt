Abstract
. We consider the length of ordered loose paths in the random r r -uniform hypergraph H = H ( r ) ​ ( n , p ) H=H^{(r)}(n,p) . A ordered loose path is a sequence of edges E 1 , E 2 , … , E ℓ E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{\ell} where max { j ∈ E i } = min { j ∈ E i + 1 } \max\left\{j\in E_{i}\right\}=\min\left\{j\in E_{i+1}\right\} for 1 ≤ i < ℓ 1\leq i<\ell . 1.
Introduction.

There has been considerable work on the maximum length of paths in random graphs and hypergraphs, particularly in the graph case; see the survey by Frieze
[
2
]
for a summary what is known on the subject. Albert and Frieze
[
1
]
considered the maximum length of a path in an orientation of
G_{n,p}
where the edge
\left\{i,j\right\},i<j
was always oriented from
i
to
j
. In this paper we consider a generalization of this problem to
r
-uniform hypergraphs, where
r\geq 2
is a fixed constant throughout.

Let
H=H^{(r)}(n,p)
be the random
r
-uniform hypergraph on the set of vertices
[n]
such that each
r
-tuple in
\binom{[n]}{r}
is included as an edge with probability
p
. Let
E_{p}
denote its set of edges. Define an
ordered loose path
of
length

\ell
in
H
as an increasing subsequence of vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\ell(r-1)+1}\in[n]
such that
\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{r}\},\{v_{r}<\dots<v_{2r-1}\},\dots,\{v_{(\ell-1)(r-1)+1}<\dots<v_{\ell(r-1)+1}\}
are the edges of
H
(so that every pair of consecutive edges intersects in a single vertex). Let
\ell_{\max}
be the maximal length of an ordered loose path in
H
. In the following, we discuss the likely value of
\ell_{\max}
for varying values of
p
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\Omega(1)=p\leq 1-o(1)
. Then, a.a.s.

\frac{(1+o(1))n}{r-p-r(1-p)^{r}+(pr+1)(1-p)^{r}p^{-2}}\leq\ell_{\max}\leq(1+o(1))n\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r(r-2+p^{-1})}\right).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
0<p<1
be a constant. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is an
r_{0}=r_{0}(p,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
r\geq r_{0}
, we have a.a.s.

\left(\frac{1}{r}-\varepsilon\right)n\leq\ell_{\max}\leq\left(\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)n.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
p=1-o(1)
. Then, we have a.a.s.

\ell_{\max}=\frac{(1+o(1))n}{r-1}.

In fact, the upper bound in these corollaries is trivial (and no upper bound from Theorem
1.1
is needed), since always the length of an ordered loose path is at most
\frac{n}{r-1}=n\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right)
. However, notice that the second term of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
is always smaller than the trivial one, since
\frac{1}{r(r-2+p^{-1})}<\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
when
p<1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\sqrt{\log n}/n^{(r-1)/4}\ll p=o(1)
or
\Omega((\log n)^{r-1}/n^{r-1})=p\ll 1/n^{(r-1)/2}
. Then, a.a.s.

\ell_{\max}=\Theta(np^{1/(r-1)}).

(We write
A_{n}\ll B_{n}
(resp.
B_{n}\gg A_{n}
) if
A_{n}/B_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.)

The next theorem fills the gap for the missing range of
p
from Theorem
1.4
. Unfortunately, this statement (likely the lower bound) is not optimal.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\Omega(1/n^{(r-1)/2})=p=O(\sqrt{\log n}/n^{(r-1)/4})
. Then, a.a.s.

\Omega(np^{1/(r-1)}/(\log n)^{1/(r-1)})=\ell_{\max}=O(np^{1/(r-1)}).

The remaining (optimal) theorems describe results for the sparse regime.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
\omega=\omega(n)
be such that
1\ll\omega\ll\log n
and

\frac{1}{n^{r-1+1/\omega}}\leq p=O((\log n)^{r-1}/n^{r-1}).

Then, there exists
1\ll\ell_{0}=O(\log n)
such that

\left(\frac{n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}p=1

and then a.a.s.
\ell_{\max}=\Theta(\ell_{0})
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
\ell\geq 2
be an integer. If
1/n^{r-1+1/\ell}\ll p\ll 1/n^{r-1+1/(\ell+1)}
, then a.a.s.
\ell_{\max}=\ell
.

Furthermore, let
c>0
be a real number and
p=c/n^{r-1+1/\ell}
. Let
X
count the number of ordered paths of length
\ell
. Then,
X
converges in distribution to
Po(\lambda)
, where
\lambda=c^{\ell}/(\ell(r-1)+1)!
.

Observe that if in Theorem
1.6
we allow
\omega=\Theta(1)
, then the lower bound on
p
is considered in Theorem
1.7
.
Also if
\omega=\Omega(\log n)
, then

p\geq\frac{1}{n^{r-1+1/\Omega(\log n)}}=\frac{1}{n^{r-1}}\frac{1}{n^{1/\Omega(\log n)}}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{n^{r-1}}\right),

which is contained in the range of
p
in Theorem
1.6
.

2.
Preliminaries

To bound the length of the longest ordered loose path from below, it is natural to consider a greedy algorithm, which extends an ordered loose path from its last vertex
i
by a hyperedge whose least vertex is
i
and whose “length”
j-i
, where
j
is its last vertex, is as small as possible.

We will use the following lemma (about random hypergraphs of edges with common left endpoints) when considering the properties of such a greedy extension algorithm.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a random
r
-hypergraph on the vertex set
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that each edge
\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<i_{r}\}
appears with probability
p
. Let
X_{i}
(for
i\geq 0
) be a random variable, which equals
r+i-1
if (I)
\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<i_{r}=r+i\}
is present in
H
and (II) no edge of the form
\{1<j_{2}<\dots<j_{r-1}<j_{r}\leq r+i-1\}
is present in
H
, and is 0 otherwise. Define
X=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}X_{i}
. Then:

(i)

The expected value equals

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(r+i-1)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right).

(ii)

The expected value satisfies

r-p-r(1-p)^{r}\leq\mbox{{\bf E}}X\leq r-p-r(1-p)^{r}+(pr+1)(1-p)^{r}p^{-2}

and, in particular, for
p=\Omega(1)
, which is independent from
r
,

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=r-p+o_{r}(1),

where
o_{r}(1)
approaches zero as
r
tends to infinity.

(iii)

For
p=o(1)
,

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\Theta(1/p^{1/(r-1)}).

Proof.

We divide the proof into three parts.

Part (
i
):

For
i=0
, clearly,
\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{0}=(r-1)q_{0}p_{0}
, where
q_{0}=1
and
p_{0}=p
.

Now consider
i=1
. Here the edge
\{1,\dots,r\}
is not present (this happens with probability
q_{1}=1-p_{0}=1-p
) and an edge
e=\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<r+1\}
is present. Since we have exactly
\binom{r-1}{r-2}
choices for vertices
i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}
, the latter occurs with probability
p_{1}=1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-1}{r-2}}
. Thus,
\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{1}=rq_{1}p_{1}
.

Assume in general that
q_{i}
is the probability of the event that no edge
\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<r+j\}
for
0\leq j\leq i-1
is present. Moreover, let
p_{i}
be the probability of the event that there is an edge of the form
\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<r+i\}
. Observe that

q_{i}=q_{i-1}(1-p_{i-1})\quad\text{ and }\quad p_{i}=1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}.

Thus,

q_{i}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(1-p_{j})=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+j}{r-2}}=(1-p)^{\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\binom{r-2+j}{r-2}}=(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}

and

\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{i}=(r-1+i)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right).

Consequently,

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(r-1+i)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right).

Part (
ii
):

We split the sum in the expression for
\mbox{{\bf E}}X
into two terms:
\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{0}+\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{1}
and
\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{i}
. Observe that

\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{0}+\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{1}=(r-1)p+r(1-p)\left(1-(1-p)^{r-1}\right)=r-p-r(1-p)^{r}

and

\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{i}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(r-1+i)(1-p)^{r-2+i}=(pr+1)(1-p)^{r}p^{-2}.

Since
\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{0}+\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{1}\leq\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{0}+\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\mbox{{\bf E}}X_{i}
, we obtain

r-p-r(1-p)^{r}\leq\mbox{{\bf E}}X\leq r-p-r(1-p)^{r}+(pr+1)(1-p)^{r}p^{-2}.

Finally notice that both
\lim_{r\to\infty}r(1-p)^{r}=0
and
\lim_{r\to\infty}(pr+1)(1-p)^{r}p^{-2}=0
for any
p=\Omega(1)
which is independent from
r
.
This yields
\mbox{{\bf E}}X=r-p+o_{r}(1)
.

Part (
iii
):

For
r=2
observe that

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(1+i)(1-p)^{i}p=p\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(1+i)(1-p)^{i}=p\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}}=1/p,

as required.
Therefore, we may assume that
r\geq 3
. We split the sum into two terms:

S_{1}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right),

and

S_{2}=\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}<i}(r-1+i)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)\left(1-\left(1-p{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{0\leq i\leq(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}

\displaystyle\leq p\sum_{0\leq i\leq(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)(r-2+i)^{r-2}

\displaystyle\leq p\cdot\left((r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}+1\right)\left(r-1+(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}\right)^{r-1}=O(1/p^{1/(r-1)}).

Now

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}<i}(r-1+i)(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\left(1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}<i}(r-1+i)\exp\left\{-p\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}\right\}\left(1-\left(1-p{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}<i}(r-1+i)\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}\exp\left\{-p\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq p\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}<i}(r-1+i)\left(\frac{e(r-2+i)}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\exp\left\{-p\left(\frac{r-2+i}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}\right\}

And after the change of variables
i:=r-2+i
,

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}+r-2<i}(i+1)i^{r-2}\exp\left\{-p\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}i^{r-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}+r-2<i}i^{r-1}\exp\left\{-p\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}i^{r-1}\right\},

since
i+1\leq 2i
.
Consider
f(x)=x^{r-1}\exp(-cx^{r-1})
for
x>0
. It is easy to check that
f
is positive and takes the maximum at point
1/c^{1/(r-1)}
. Furthermore,
f
is a strictly decreasing function for
x>1/c^{1/(r-1)}
. Furthermore,

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-1}\exp(-cx^{r-1})dx=\frac{\Gamma(r/(r-1))}{c^{r/(r-1)}(r-1)},

where
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}
is the
gamma function

(see integral 3.326 in
[
4
]
).
Let
c=p\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}
. Then,

1/c^{1/(r-1)}=(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\sum_{(r-1)/p^{1/(r-1)}+r-2<i}i^{r-1}\exp\left\{-p\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}i^{r-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-1}\exp\left\{-p\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}x^{r-1}\right\}dx

\displaystyle=2p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\frac{\Gamma(r/(r-1))}{c^{r/(r-1)}(r-1)}

\displaystyle=2p\left(\frac{e}{r-2}\right)^{r-2}\frac{(r-1)^{r-1}\Gamma(r/(r-1))}{p^{r/(r-1)}}=O(1/p^{1/(r-1)}).

Hence,

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=S_{1}+S_{2}=O(1/p^{1/(r-1)}).

It remains to show the lower bound. Observe that

(1+x)^{k}\leq 1+\frac{kx}{1-(k-1)x}=\frac{1+x}{1-(k-1)x}\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq x(k-1)<1\quad\text{and}\quad k\geq 1.

Thus,

1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\geq 1-\left(1-\frac{p{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}}{1+p\left({\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}-1\right)}\right)=\frac{p{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}}{1+p\left({\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}-1\right)}.

Moreover, for
p=o(1)
and
0\leq i\leq 1/p^{1/(r-1)}
we get

1+p\left({\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}-1\right)\leq 1+p(r-2+i)^{r-2}=O(p^{1/(r-1)})=o(1)\leq 2.

Hence,

1-\left(1-p\right)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}\geq p{\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}}/2.

Also, since
1-x\geq e^{-2x}
for
0\leq x\leq 1/2
, we obtain for
p=o(1)
and
0\leq i\leq 1/p^{1/(r-1)}

(1-p)^{\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}}\geq\exp\left\{-2p\binom{r-2+i}{r-1}\right\}\geq\exp\left\{-2p(r-2+i)^{r-1}\right\}=\Omega(1).

Therefore, using
(1-a)^{b}\geq 1-ab
for
0\leq a\leq 1
and
b>0
, we see that

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf E}}X

\displaystyle\geq\Omega(p)\sum_{0\leq i\leq 1/p^{1/(r-1)}}(r-1+i)\binom{r-2+i}{r-2}

\displaystyle\geq\Omega(p)\sum_{0\leq i\leq 1/p^{1/(r-1)}}i^{r-1}

\displaystyle\geq\Omega(p)\frac{1}{p^{1/(r-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{1/(r-1)}}\right)^{r-1}=\Omega(1/p^{1/(r-1)}),

finally yielding
\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\Theta(1/p^{1/(r-1)})
.
∎

Claim 2.2
.

Let
p=\Omega((\log n)/n^{r-1})
and

a=\begin{cases}\frac{4}{p}\log n&\text{ if }r=2,\\
\left(\frac{4(r-2)^{r-2}}{p}\log n\right)^{1/(r-1)}&\text{ if }r\geq 3.\end{cases}

Then, for
r=2
and any disjoint subsets
A_{1},A_{2}\subseteq[n]

e

|A_{1}\cap e|=|A_{2}\cap e|=1
.
Moreover, for
r\geq 3
and any disjoint subsets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\subseteq[n]
with
|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=|A_{3}|=a
, with probability
1-O(n^{-20})
there is in

with no edge described above is at most

\displaystyle\binom{n}{a}^{3}(1-p)^{a^{2}\binom{a}{r-2}}

\displaystyle\leq n^{3a}\exp\left\{-pa^{2}\binom{a}{r-2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{3a\log n-pa^{2}\binom{a}{r-2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{3a\log n-pa^{2}a^{r-2}/(r-2)^{r-2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{a\left(3\log n-pa^{r-1}/(r-2)^{r-2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{a(3\log n-4\log n)\right\}=n^{-a}\leq n^{-20}.

Lemma 2.3

(Warnke
[

.

Let
W=(W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{n})
be a family of independent random variables with
W_{i}
taking values in a set
\Lambda_{i}
. Let
\Omega=\prod_{i\in[n]}\Lambda_{i}
and suppose that
\Gamma\subseteq\Omega
and
f:\Omega\to{\bf R}
are given. Suppose also that whenever
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in\Omega
differ only in the
i
-th coordinate

|f({\bf x})-f({\bf x}^{\prime})|\leq\begin{cases}c_{i}&if\ {\bf x}\in\Gamma.\\
d_{i}&otherwise.\end{cases}

If
Y=f(X)
, then for all reals
\gamma_{i}>0
and
t
,

(1)

\mbox{{\bf Pr}}(Y\geq\mbox{{\bf E}}(Y)+t)\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sum_{i\in[n]}(c_{i}+\gamma_{i}(d_{i}-c_{i}))^{2}}\right\}+\mbox{{\bf Pr}}(W\notin\Gamma)\sum_{i\in[n]}\gamma_{i}^{-1}.

3.
Lower bound for
\sqrt{\log n}/n^{(r-1)/4}\ll p\leq 1-o(1)
.

We present a lower bound by applying a greedy algorithm, that we denote by GREEDY. This will imply the lower bound in Theorem
1.1
, and the lower bound for the upper range of
p
in Theorem
1.4
.

Let
e=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\}
be an edge. We define the
length
of
e
as
\ell(e)=i_{r}-i_{1}
. We will greedily construct a loose path
e_{1},e_{2},\dots
. We start at the vertex
1
and choose the first present edge
e_{1}=\{1<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r-1}<i_{r}\}
, that means, an edge of the smallest length that contains vertex 1. If there is more than one edge of minimum length containing vertex 1, we break ties arbitrarily.
Now we repeat the process starting at the vertex
i_{r}
and find the first edge
e_{2}
that starts at
i_{r}
, etc. Let
L_{i},i\geq 1
be independent copies of the random variable
X
defined in Lemma
2.1
. Now define the random variable

1\ll\omega\ll\log n
. Let
x_{1}=\log\omega
. Note that

f(x_{1})=\left(\frac{n}{x_{1}}\right)^{r-1+1/x_{1}}p\geq\left(\frac{n}{\log\omega}\right)^{r-1+1/\log\omega}\frac{1}{n^{r-1+1/\omega}}=\frac{n^{1/\log\omega-1/\omega}}{(\log\omega)^{r-1+1/\omega}}\geq\frac{n^{1/\log\omega-1/\omega}}{(\log\omega)^{r}}.

For large
n
, we have

1/\log\omega-1/\omega\geq 1/(2\log\omega)\geq 1/(2\log\log n).

Thus, when
n
is sufficiently large

f(x_{1})\geq\frac{n^{1/(2\log\log n)}}{(\log\log n)^{r}}=\exp\left\{\frac{\log n}{2\log\log n}-r\log\log\log n\right\},

which tends to infinity. Hence,
f(x_{1})>1
.

Now let
C>0
be an arbitrarily large constant and assume that
p\leq\frac{C(\log n)^{r-1}}{n^{r-1}}
. Let
d>1
be (sufficiently large) such that

\frac{Ce^{1/d}}{d^{r-1}}<1.

Define
x_{2}=d\log n
and observe that

\displaystyle f(x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{n}{d\log n}\right)^{r-1+1/(d\log n)}\frac{C(\log n)^{r-1}}{n^{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{d^{r-1+1/(d\log n)}}\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{1/(d\log n)}\leq\frac{C}{d^{r-1}}n^{1/(d\log n)}=\frac{Ce^{1/d}}{d^{r-1}}<1.

∎

7.1.
Upper bound

Let
\ell=\frac{2e(r-1)\ell_{0}-1}{r-1}
. In the next two sections,
X
denotes the number of paths of length
\ell
, for the most recently stated value of
\ell
. Hence,
1/\ell\leq 1/\ell_{0}
and, using the definition of
\ell_{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf E}}X

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}\leq\left(\frac{en}{\ell(r-1)+1}\right)^{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{n}{2(r-1)\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell}p\right)^{\ell}\leq\left(\left(\frac{n}{2(r-1)\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}p\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2(r-1))^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}}\left(\frac{n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}p\right)^{\ell}=\frac{1}{(2(r-1))^{\ell(r-1)+\ell/\ell_{0}}}=o(1).

7.2.
Lower bound

Let
\ell=\frac{\ell_{0}/4-1}{r-1}
. Hence,
1/\ell\geq 1/\ell_{0}
and

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf E}}X

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}\geq\left(\frac{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}\right)^{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{4n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell}p\right)^{\ell}\geq\left(\left(\frac{4n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}p\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(4^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}\left(\frac{n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{r-1+1/\ell_{0}}p\right)^{\ell}=4^{\ell(r-1)+\ell/\ell_{0}}\gg 1.

We will now modify the calculations from Section
5
. Since
s=\Theta(\ell)=O(\log n)
, we have

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{s}{n-s}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{2s}{n}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}=2\left(\frac{2(\ell(r-1)+1)}{n}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\ell_{0}}{2n}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}=\frac{1}{2^{r-2}}\left(\frac{n}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{1/\ell_{0}}\leq n^{1/\ell_{0}}.

Consequently, we bound (
3
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\sum_{0\leq b\leq a-1}n_{a,b}p^{2\ell-a}

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{2\ell s}{n}\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\left(2\left(\frac{s}{n-s}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}\right)^{a}

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{2\ell s}{n}\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\left(n^{1/\ell_{0}}\right)^{a}\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{\ell s}{n}O\left(n^{1/\ell_{0}}\right)^{\ell}.

Since
\ell/\ell_{0}<1/(4(r-1))
and
\ell s=O(\log^{2}n)
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\sum_{0\leq b\leq a-1}n_{a,b}p^{2\ell-a}

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{\ell s}{n}O(n^{\ell/\ell_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{\ell s}{n}O(n^{1/(4(r-1))})

\displaystyle=(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{(4r-5)/(4r-4)}}\right)=o((\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}),

As in Section
5
we conclude that a.a.s. there is an ordered path of length
\ell
.

8.
Lower and upper bound for
1/(n^{r-1+1/\ell})\ll p\ll 1/(n^{r-1+1/(\ell+1)})
where
\ell=O(1)
.

8.1.
Upper bound

Let
X
count the number of paths of length
\ell+1
.
Recall that from Section
6.2
we know that

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\binom{n}{(\ell+1)(r-1)+1}p^{\ell+1}\leq n^{(\ell+1)(r-1)+1}p^{\ell+1}\ll n^{(\ell+1)(r-1)+1}\cdot 1/(n^{(\ell+1)(r-1)+1})=1

implying that
\mbox{{\bf E}}X
tends to 0.

8.2.
Lower bound

Similarly, for
X
counting the number of paths of length
\ell
,

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\binom{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}\gg\left(\frac{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}\right)^{\ell(r-1)+1}\cdot 1/(n^{\ell(r-1)+1})=\Omega(1)

yielding that
\mbox{{\bf E}}X
tends to infinity.

We will now modify calculations from Section
5
. By assumption we have
\ell=O(1)
and
s=\ell(r-1)+1=O(1)
. Observe that in (
3
) we can use the following upper bounds,

2\left(\frac{s}{n-s}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}\ll 1/n^{r-1}\cdot n^{r-1+1/\ell}=n^{1/\ell}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\sum_{0\leq b\leq a-1}n_{a,b}p^{2\ell-a}

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{2\ell s}{n}\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\left(2\left(\frac{s}{n-s}\right)^{r-1}p^{-1}\right)^{a}

\displaystyle\ll(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}\frac{O(1)}{n}O(n)=(\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}O(1),

showing that

\sum_{1\leq a\leq\ell}\;\sum_{0\leq b\leq a-1}n_{a,b}p^{2\ell-a}=o((\mbox{{\bf E}}X)^{2}),

as required.

8.3.
Method of moments

Observe that if
p=c/(n^{r-1+1/\ell})
, then

\mbox{{\bf E}}X=\binom{n}{\ell(r-1)+1}p^{\ell}\sim\frac{n^{\ell(r-1)+1}}{(\ell(r-1)+1)!}\cdot c^{\ell}/(n^{\ell(r-1)+1})=c^{\ell}/(\ell(r-1)+1)!=:\lambda.

Now by generalizing the second moment calculations one can show that

\mbox{{\bf E}}X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)\to\lambda^{k}

for any
k\geq 1
. Thus, the method of moments (see, e.g. Section 29.2 in
[
3
]
) yields the final statement of Theorem
1.7
.

9.
Concluding remarks

We have found high probability estimates for
\ell_{\max}
for all ranges of
p
. The gaps between upper and lower bounds are all of order
O(1)
except for Theorem
1.5
where
\Omega(1/n^{(r-1)/2})=p=O(\sqrt{\log n}/n^{(r-1)/4})
. In this case we are off by an order
O((\log n)^{1/(r-1)})
factor. It would be of some interest to remove this.

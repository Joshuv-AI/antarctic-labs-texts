Correlation effects in one-dimensional metallic quantum wires under various confinements Preprint: APS/123-QED
Vidit Gangwar, Vinod Ashokan, Ankush Girdhar, Klaus Morawetz, N. D. Drummond, K. N. Pathak

Abstract
Dynamical response theory is employed to investigate the effects of various transverse confinements on electron correlations in the ground state of a ferromagnetic one-dimensional quantum wire for different wire widths b b and density parameters r s r_{\text{s}} . In the regime of a thin quantum wire, electrons are treated as a one-dimensional gas under different confinement models via effective electron-electron interaction potentials. Using the first-order random phase approximation (FRPA) including self-energy and exchange contribution, which provides the ground state structure beyond the random phase approximation, we numerically compute the structure factor, pair-correlation function, correlation energy, and ground-state energy for various values of b b and r s r_{\text{s}} . Our results reveal that the correlation energy depends on the choice of confinement model. For the ultrathin wire ( b → 0 ) (b\rightarrow 0) in the high-density limit, we find that the correlation energy for transverse confinement models V 1 ​ ( q ) V_{1}(q) (harmonic), V 2 ​ ( q ) V_{2}(q) (cylindrical), and V 5 ​ ( q ) V_{5}(q) (harmonic-delta) approaches ϵ c ( r s ) = − π 2 / 360 ∼ − 0.02741 \epsilon_{\text{c}}(r_{\text{s}})=-\pi^{2}/360\sim-0.02741 a.u., which agrees with the exact results in this limit [P.-F. Loos, J. Chem. Phys. 138 , 064108 (2013), Vinod Ashokan et al. , Phys. Rev. B 101 , 075130 (2020)]. This clearly illustrates that for at least these three confinement potentials, the one-dimensional Coulomb potential can be regularized at interparticle distance x = 0 x=0 to yield the same correlation energy. In contrast, other confinement potentials, V 3 ​ ( q ) V_{3}(q) (infinite square well), V 4 ​ ( q ) V_{4}(q) (infinite square-infinite triangular well), and V 6 ​ ( q ) V_{6}(q) (infinite square-delta well), do not approach the same high-density limit; instead, the correlation energy tends to ϵ c ∼ − 0.03002 \epsilon_{\text{c}}\sim-0.03002 a.u. for these potentials. The percentage difference in correlation energy between the confinement models V 1 ​ ( q ) V_{1}(q) , V 2 ​ ( q ) V_{2}(q) , V 5 ​ ( q ) V_{5}(q) and V 3 ​ ( q ) V_{3}(q) , V 4 ​ ( q ) V_{4}(q) , V 6 ​ ( q ) V_{6}(q) is within about 10 % 10\% in the high-density limit. The ground-state properties obtained from the FRPA are compared with the available quantum Monte Carlo results in the high-density regime. We observe that the peak height in the static structure factor at k = 2 ​ k F k=2k_{\text{F}} depends significantly on the confinement model. These prominent peaks at k = 2 ​ k F k=2k_{\text{F}} are fitted with a function based on our finite wire-width theory, guided by insights from bosonization, demonstrating good agreement with our FRPA theory.

Introduction

The study of interacting particles has been essential for understanding the complex quantum behaviors observed in condensed matter physics
Giuliani and Vignale 2008
;
Morawetz et al. 2024a
;
Morawetz et al. 2024b
;
Zhao et al. 2025
. Recent experimental breakthroughs have produced exceptionally clean quasi-one-dimensional (1D) systems, including strongly interacting 1D Fermi gases in optical lattices
Moritz et al. 2005
, carbon nanotubes
Bockrath et al. 1999
;
Ishii et al. 2003
;
Shiraishi and Ata 2003
, semiconductor nanowires
Levy et al. 2006
;
Schäfer et al. 2008
;
Dudy et al. 2017
;
Hong et al. 2016
, and high-mobility quantum wires grown by cleaved edge overgrowth
Yacoby et al. 1996
. These recent developments have generated considerable theoretical interest and research.

In many-body physics
Giamarchi 2004
;
Giuliani and Vignale 2008
, theoretical investigations predict qualitatively distinct quantum behaviors in systems of reduced dimensionality (e.g., two dimensions, 1D) as compared to their three-dimensional counterparts. These deviations are anticipated both at zero temperature and at finite temperatures within the thermodynamic limit
Ashokan et al. 2018a
;
Garg et al. 2018
;
Ashokan et al. 2020
;
Rani et al. 2024
.
Reducing the dimensionality of electron systems to one leads to a breakdown of the Landau’s Fermi-liquid picture. In this regime, strong Coulomb interactions drive electrons to behave collectively, a phenomenon explained by the Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid theory
Tomonaga 1950
;
Luttinger 1963
, replacing the Landau picture of individual particle description. One-dimensional interacting systems exhibit intriguing phenomena, including spin-charge separation
Girdhar et al. 2023
;
Auslaender et al. 2005
;
Senaratne et al. 2022
, where collective spin and charge excitations become distinct and propagate independently; charge fractionalization
Steinberg et al. 2008
;
Ramos et al. 2024
, where charge excitations carry nonquantized charge; and Wigner crystallization
Schulz 1993
;
Meyer and Matveev 2008
;
Deshpande and Bockrath 2008
;
Girdhar and Ashokan 2023
, where electrons get localized due to repulsive Coulomb interactions between them.

The electronic properties of quasi-1D systems are significantly influenced by electron-electron interactions under various transverse confinement schemes. The effective electron-electron interaction will be repulsive for all energies as long as the uniformly positively charged background is rigid. However, confinement effects manifest themselves as modifications to the electronic band structure, enhancement of Coulomb interactions between electrons, and the emergence of novel collective excitations.

In this work, to examine the role of the nature of confinement and the correlation effects, we consider some different confinement models developed in the literature. Among them, the harmonic confinement model is quite realistic in interparticle potential
Friesen and Bergersen 1980
, and is capable of tackling excitonic instability for a rigid and uniformly charged positive background. Interactions by a softened Coulomb potential appropriate for a cylindrical wire
Ashokan et al. 2020
;
Sharma et al. 2021
also remove the singularity at zero interparticle distance. The interactions can also be modelled using a square quantum well confinement approach when the subband separation is large, i.e., for thin wires, such as in GaAs semiconductor quantum-well wires
Campos et al. 1995
. Epitaxial growth techniques are commonly used to fabricate these wires, and their optical properties have been extensively reported
Wang and Voliotis 2006
. In 1D quantum wires within GaAs heterostructures, interactions are studied using a square quantum well confinement model and realistic variational optimized wave functions for the transverse directions
Das Sarma and Lai 1985
. Therefore, an accurate description of the ground-state properties of 1D wires with realistic Coulomb interactions remains a complex challenge, even though significant progress has been made towards more sophisticated and accurate modelling. Moudgil
et al.

Moudgil et al. 2010
studied the plasmon dispersion with different transverse confinement models on an atom-scale metallic wire using the random phase approximation (RPA) and the Singwi-Tosi-Land-Sjölander (STLS) approximation, which shows good agreement with electron-energy-loss spectroscopy measurements
Nagao et al. 2006
. However, the structure factor and correlation energy were not calculated for the different confinement models on the effective electron-electron interaction potential. In this paper, we model 1D homogeneous electron gases (HEGs) in the high density limit interacting via effective long-range Coulomb potentials and report the numerical calculation of the static structure factor (SSF), pair-correlation function (PCF), correlation energy, and ground state energy for various confinement models
Friesen and Bergersen 1980
;
Ashokan et al. 2020
;
Campos et al. 1995
;
Das Sarma and Lai 1985
;
Hu and O’Connell 1990
;
Li and Das Sarma 1989
and compare with the available quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulation results including variational Monte Carlo (VMC) and diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC)
Ashokan et al. 2018a
;
Girdhar et al. 2022
.

II
Theoretical formalism

II.1
Quasi-1D wires

Our model considers a quasi-1D system, a finite-width quantum wire in which electrons can freely roam along the wire axis, neutralized by a rigid positive background and strongly confined in the transverse directions using various confinement potentials. The wire is modelled such that the length of the wire is much larger than its width, allowing electrons to move freely along the
x
-axis (wire axis) and confining them in the
yz
- plane by a potential
V(y,z)
uniform in
x
. The strength of the transverse confinement can be used to tune the electron-electron interaction in quantum wires.
Numerical or approximate methods are essential for studying the ground state properties of quasi-1D electron systems under the various transverse confinement models, as exact analytical solutions are usually not possible. We limit our calculations to the extreme quantum limit, a regime in which electrons occupy only the lowest energy subband associated with transverse motion, as this approximation is valid for atomic-scale metallic wires with significant subband separation
Moudgil et al. 2010
;
Campos et al. 1995
.

Harmonic confinement in the

In this confinement scheme

(1)

V_{1}(q)=\exp\left(q^{2}b^{2}\right)E_{1}\left(q^{2}b^{2}\right),

(2)

The analytic expression is derived in Appendix
A

II.3
Density response function

In this section, we present an analysis of the static properties of the 1D HEG using the dynamical density response function and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Giuliani and Vignale 2008
. The density response function quantifies the change in electron density due to a change in the external potential. It is defined as
Girdhar et al. 2022
;
Bala et al. 2012

\hskip-4.26773pt\chi(q,\omega)=\frac{\chi_{0}(q,\omega)+\lambda\{\chi_{1}^{\text{se}}(q,\omega)+\chi_{1}^{\text{ex}}(q,\omega)\}}{1-\lambda V(q)\left[\chi_{0}(q,\omega)+\lambda\{\chi_{1}^{\text{se}}(q,\omega)+\chi_{1}^{\text{ex}}(q,\omega)\}\right]},

(8)

where
V(q)
is the Fourier transform of the effective potential and
\lambda
denotes the order of the potential, while
\chi_{1}^{\text{se}}(q,\omega)
and
\chi_{1}^{\text{ex}}(q,\omega)
represent the first-order self-energy and exchange correction to the polarizability, respectively.
In the high-density limit, Eq. (
8
) can be approximated to first order as

\displaystyle\chi(q,\omega)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(q,\omega)+\lambda\;V(q)\chi_{0}^{2}(q,\omega)

(9)

\displaystyle+\lambda\;\chi_{1}^{\text{se}}(q,\omega)+\lambda\;\chi_{1}^{\text{ex}}(q,\omega).

The noninteracting polarizability describes the response of a system of independent particles to an external perturbation and is simplified as

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(q,\omega)=\frac{g_{s}m}{2\pi q}\ln\bigg[\frac{\omega^{2}-(\frac{q^{2}}{2m}-\frac{qk_{\text{F}}}{m})^{2}}{\omega^{2}-(\frac{q^{2}}{2m}+\frac{qk_{\text{F}}}{m})^{2}}\bigg].

(10)

The simplified forms of the self-energy and exchange contributions are, respectively,
Ashokan et al. 2020
;
Girdhar et al. 2022

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{\text{se}}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=2g_{\text{s}}\sum_{k,p}n_{k}n_{p}[V(k-p)-V(k-p+q)]

(11)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Omega^{2}_{k,q}+\omega^{2}}{(\Omega^{2}_{k,q}-\omega^{2})^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{\text{ex}}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=-2g_{\text{s}}\sum_{k,p}\bigg\{V(k\!-\!p)[n_{k-\frac{q}{2}}n_{p-\frac{q}{2}}\!-\!n_{k-\frac{q}{2}}n_{p+\frac{q}{2}}]

(12)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(\Omega_{k-\frac{q}{2},q}\;\Omega_{p-\frac{q}{2},q}+\omega^{2})}{(\Omega^{2}_{k-\frac{q}{2},q}-\omega^{2})(\Omega^{2}_{p-\frac{q}{2},q}-\omega^{2})}\bigg\}

with

\displaystyle\Omega_{k,q}=\omega_{k}-\omega_{k+q}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,;\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\Omega_{p,q}=\omega_{p}-\omega_{p+q},

(13)

where

is defined as the spin degeneracy factor and

represents the Fermi-Dirac distribution function. The above expressions are directly incorporated into our calculations.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied confinement-driven correlation effects on the ground-state properties of a ferromagnetic quasi-one-dimensional quantum wire using FRPA, which provides ground state structure beyond RPA, including self-energy and exchange contributions for various confinement models. The ground-state properties of quasi-one-dimensional quantum metallic wires for different confinement schemes have been calculated numerically and compared with the available QMC simulated data, which provides validation of the accuracy of our numerical calculations. Our study demonstrates that the choice of confinement model can have a significant impact on the ground-state properties of quasi-one-dimensional quantum wires. This suggests that employing an accurate confinement model when studying these systems becomes crucial. The prominent peaks of charge SSF at
2k_{\text{F}}
are fitted using a fitting function based on our finite wire-width theory, guided by insights from bosonization, demonstrating good agreement with our FRPA theory.

Our findings also indicate that for the ultrathin wire in the high-density limit, the correlation energy for confinement models
V_{1}(q)
,
V_{2}(q)
and
V_{5}(q)
approaches
\epsilon_{\text{c}}(r_{\text{s}})=-\pi^{2}/360
a.u. which agrees with the exact results in this limit
Loos 2013
;
Ashokan et al. 2020
. This clearly illustrates that for at least these three confinement potentials, the one-dimensional Coulomb potential can be regularized at interparticle distance
x=0
to yield the same correlation energy. In contrast, other confinement potentials
V_{3}(q)
,
V_{4}(q)
, and
V_{6}(q)
do not approach the same high-density limit. The percentage difference in correlation energy between the confinement models
V_{1}(q)
,
V_{2}(q)
, and
V_{5}(q)
and
V_{3}(q)
,
V_{4}(q)
, and
V_{6}(q)
is within about
10\%
in the high-density limit. This indicates that the specific shape of the confinement potential affects the long-range correlations between the electrons. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of electronic correlations and confinement effects in quasi-1D metallic systems. This enhanced modelling of electron interaction effects within confined systems may be useful in the optimization and fabrication of ferromagnetic quasi-1D quantum wire devices in the future.

(49)

This can be written as

\displaystyle V_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=e^{q^{2}b^{2}}\left\{-E_{1}\left(-q^{2}b^{2}-i0\right)-i\pi\right\}

(50)

\displaystyle=e^{q^{2}b^{2}}\left\{E_{1}\left(q^{2}b^{2}\right)+i\pi-i\pi\right\},

(51)

which can be written as

V_{1}(q)=\exp\left(q^{2}b^{2}\right)E_{1}\left(q^{2}b^{2}\right).

(52)

It is easily seen that

\displaystyle V_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q\,b)^{2}}\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{for}\quad(q\,b)\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle=-\gamma-2\ln(q\,b)\quad\text{for}\quad(q\,b)\rightarrow 0,

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.

Appendix B
Cylindrical confinement

B.1
Radial Distribution of Cylindrically Confined Electrons

Consider an electron confined in a cylindrical channel of radius
b
, with hard-wall boundary conditions on the cylindrical channel. Suppose
b
is small compared to the mean electron separation. Then the transverse motion is dominated by the kinetic energy of confinement, and hence we can describe the transverse motion in an independent particle approximation.

In cylindrical polar coordinates, the wave function of a single electron in the channel is of the form

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf r})=R(r,\theta)X(x),

(53)

where
r
and
\theta
are the radial and polar coordinates, and
x
is the axial coordinate. In the ground state,
R
is independent of
\theta
. The radial Schrödinger equation for the ground state is

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}R^{\prime}(r)\right)=E_{R}R(r),

(54)

where
E_{R}
is the constant contribution to the energy eigenvalue due to the kinetic energy of the transverse motion.
R(r)
is smooth and nondivergent for
0\leq r<b
and satisfies the boundary condition
R(b)=0
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle r^{2}R^{\prime\prime}(r)+rR^{\prime}(r)+2E_{R}r^{2}R(r)=0.

(55)

Let
r=\alpha s
for some constant
\alpha
. Let
S(s)=R(r)
. Then
S^{\prime}(s)=\alpha R^{\prime}(r)\quad\text{and}\quad S^{\prime\prime}(s)=\alpha^{2}R^{\prime\prime}(r)
. Hence, the equation becomes

\displaystyle s^{2}S^{\prime\prime}(s)+sS^{\prime}(s)+2E_{R}\alpha^{2}s^{2}S(s)=0.

(56)

But
E_{R}
is positive since it is a purely kinetic energy. Let
\alpha=1/\sqrt{2E_{R}}
. Then

\displaystyle s^{2}S^{\prime\prime}(s)+sS^{\prime}(s)+s^{2}S(s)=0.

(57)

This is Bessel’s equation of zeroth order. The nondivergent solution is
S(s)=J_{0}(s)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle R(r)=J_{0}\left(r/\alpha\right)=J_{0}\left(\sqrt{2E_{R}}r\right),

(58)

where
J_{0}
is the zeroth order Bessel function of the first kind.

In the ground state,
R(b)=0
, and there are no additional nodes in
0\leq r<b
. Hence,

\sqrt{2E_{R}}b=j_{0,1}
, where
j_{0,1}\approx 2.40483\dots
is the first zero of
J_{0}
. So the radial wave function is
R(r)=J_{0}\left(j_{0,1}\,r/b\right)
in
0\leq r\leq b
.
Hence the radial distribution is

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{R}(r)\right|^{2}\equiv\frac{|R(r)|^{2}}{\int_{0}^{b}2\pi r|R(r)|^{2}\,dr}.

(59)

B.2
Effective Interaction between Cylindrically Confined Electrons

Now consider two cylindrically confined electrons (labelled 1 and 2) with axial separation
x
. Assuming tight confinement in the transverse direction, we average the full Coulomb interaction over the radial distributions to obtain the effective Coulomb interaction

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\text{eff}}(x)=\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{1})\right|^{2}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{2})\right|^{2}\frac{1}{|{\bf r}_{2}-{\bf r}_{1}|}\,r_{1}\,d\theta_{1}\,dr_{1}\,r_{2}\,d\theta_{2}\,dr_{2}.

(60)

But
{\bf r}_{2}-{\bf r}_{1}=x\hat{\bf e}_{x}+\left[r_{2}\cos(\theta_{2})-r_{1}\cos(\theta_{1})\right]\hat{\bf e}_{y}+\left[r_{2}\sin(\theta_{2})-r_{1}\sin(\theta_{1})\right]\hat{\bf e}_{z}
, where
\hat{\bf e}_{\beta}
is the unit vector in the Cartesian direction
\beta
. Hence

\displaystyle|{\bf r}_{2}-{\bf r}_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{2})+r_{1}^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{1})-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos(\theta_{1})\cos(\theta_{2})+r_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta_{2})+r_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta_{1})-2r_{1}r_{2}\sin(\theta_{1})\sin(\theta_{2})

\displaystyle=x^{2}+r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}).

(61)

So,

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\text{eff}}(x)=2\pi\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{2\pi}r_{1}r_{2}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{1})\right|^{2}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{2})\right|^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}+r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos(\theta)}}\,d\theta\,dr_{2}\,dr_{1}.

(62)

To find the effective interaction, we must solve the integral in Eq. (
62
). We can make a little bit of progress by solving the angular integral:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{x^{2}+r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos(\theta)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{x^{2}+r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}+4r_{1}r_{2}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}}

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+4r_{1}r_{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1+\frac{4r_{1}r_{2}}{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}\sin^{2}(\theta)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}}K\left(k^{2}=-\frac{4r_{1}r_{2}}{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}\right),

(63)

where
K
is a complete elliptic integral of the first kind. Substituting this in Eq. (
62
) gives,

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\text{eff}}(x)=8\pi\int_{0}^{b}\int_{0}^{b}r_{1}r_{2}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{1})\right|^{2}\left|\tilde{R}(r_{2})\right|^{2}\frac{K\left(k^{2}=-\frac{4r_{1}r_{2}}{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}}\,dr_{1}\,dr_{2}.

(64)

As a sanity check, in the limit of large
x
,
\sqrt{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}\rightarrow|x|
and
K\left(k^{2}=-\frac{4r_{1}r_{2}}{x^{2}+(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}\right)\rightarrow K(0)=\pi/2
.

Hence, at long range, the effective interaction reduces to the bare interaction:

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\text{eff}}(x)\rightarrow\frac{1}{|x|}\left(2\pi\int_{0}^{b}r\left|\tilde{R}(r)\right|^{2}\,dr\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{|x|}.

(65)

The above Eq. (
65
) is for zero radius cylinder. It is difficult to make further analytic progress with Eq. (
64
) when
\tilde{R}(r)
is nontrivial, as is the case for cylindrical hard-wall confinement.

The above potential can be analyzed in an alternative way using the one-electron wave function of the form

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{{e}^{iqx}}{\sqrt{L}}\sqrt{\delta(y)}\sqrt{\delta(z)},

(66)

where
\sqrt{\delta(y)}
and
\sqrt{\delta(z)}
are types of wave functions which are equivalent to the assumption of zero wire width in the
y
and
z
-directions, resulting in a much harder interaction potential.

Matrix elements of the Coulomb interaction are given by

\displaystyle V_{2}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{iq(x-x^{\prime})}\,d(x-x^{\prime})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\delta(y)\delta{(y^{\prime})}{\delta(z)\delta(z^{\prime})}\,dy\,dy^{\prime}\,dz\,dz^{\prime}}{{\left[(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+({y-y^{\prime}+b_{1}})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime}+b_{2})^{2}\right]^{1/2}}}.

(67)

Substituting
x-x^{\prime}=x
and using the Dirac delta function’s integral,

V_{2}(q)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{iqx}\frac{1}{\left[x^{2}+b^{2}\right]^{1/2}}dx\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,;\,\,\,\,\,\,b^{2}={b_{1}}^{2}+{b_{2}}^{2},

(68)

where
b
is wire width parameter.
Hence, the softened Coulomb interaction is approximated as
v_{2}^{\text{eff}}(x)\approx 1/\sqrt{(x^{2}+b^{2})}
and Fourier transform after simplification becomes

V_{2}(q)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos{(qx)}}{\left[x^{2}+b^{2}\right]^{1/2}}\,dx.

(69)

Using the integral identity for Bessel functions, Eq. (
69
) becomes

V_{2}(q)=2K_{0}(qb),

(70)

where
K_{0}(qb)
is the zeroth-order modified Bessel function of the second kind.

Appendix C
Infinite square well confinement

For this case, the one-electron wave function is of the form

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{{e}^{iqx}}{\sqrt{L}}\phi_{n}(y)\phi_{n}^{\prime}(z).

(71)

The ground state wave function for an infinite square well is

\displaystyle\phi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right),\quad 0\leq y\leq a

(72)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi z}{a}\right),\quad 0\leq z\leq a.

(73)

Using Eqs. (
72
) and (
73
), the total wave function (
71
) can be written as

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{e^{iqx}}{\sqrt{L}}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi z}{a}\right),

(74)

where
a
is the width of the infinite square well. Now, the matrix elements of the Coulomb interaction are given by

\displaystyle V_{3}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{16}{a^{4}}\int_{0}^{a}dz\int_{0}^{a}dz^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z^{\prime}}{a}\right)\int_{0}^{a}dy\int_{0}^{a}dy^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y^{\prime}}{a}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iq(x-x^{\prime})}\,d(x-x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}},

Substituting
x-x^{\prime}=x
,

\displaystyle V_{3}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{16}{a^{4}}\int_{0}^{a}dz\int_{0}^{a}dz^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z^{\prime}}{a}\right)\int_{0}^{a}dy\int_{0}^{a}dy^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y^{\prime}}{a}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iqx}\,dx}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}}.

Using the integral identity for Bessel functions, Eq. (
76
) becomes

V_{3}(q)=\frac{32}{a^{4}}\int_{0}^{a}dz\int_{0}^{a}dz^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi z^{\prime}}{a}\right)\int_{0}^{a}dy\int_{0}^{a}dy^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y^{\prime}}{a}\right)K_{0}(qR),

(77)

where
R=\sqrt{\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}
.

Appendix D
Infinite square well confinement in the
y
direction, triangular quantum well confinement in the
z
direction

The one-electron electron wave function for this case is given as

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{e^{iqx}}{\sqrt{L}}\phi_{n}(y)\zeta_{i}(z).

(78)

The ground state wave function for an infinite square well in the
y
-direction is

\phi_{(}y)=\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,0\leq y\leq a,

(79)

and the variational ground state wave function in the
z
-direction is

\zeta(z)=\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{2}\right)^{1/2}z\exp{\left(\frac{-\beta z}{2}\right)}\Theta(z),

(80)

where
a
is the width of 1-D wire along
y
axis,
\beta=3/{\langle z_{0}\rangle}
is the variational parameter, and
\langle z_{0}\rangle
is
defined as the average wire width along the
z
-direction. Using Eqs. (
79
) and (
80
), the total wave function (
78
) can be written as

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{e^{ikx}}{\sqrt{L}}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{1/2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{2}\right)^{1/2}z\exp{\left(\frac{-\beta z}{2}\right)}\Theta(z).

(81)

Matrix elements of the Coulomb interaction are given by

\displaystyle V_{4}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{a^{2}}\frac{\beta^{6}}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\int_{0}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,z^{2}z^{\prime 2}e^{-\beta(z+z^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{a}dy\int_{0}^{a}dy^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y^{\prime}}{a}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iq(x-x^{\prime})}\,d(x-x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}},

using the integral identity for Bessel functions, Eq. (
82
) becomes

V_{4}(q)=\frac{2\beta^{6}}{\,a^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\int_{0}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,{z^{2}}\,{z^{\prime 2}}\,e^{-\beta(z+z^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{a}dy\int_{0}^{a}dy^{\prime}\,\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y}{a}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi y^{\prime}}{a}\right)\,K_{0}(qR).

(83)

Appendix E
Harmonic confinement in the
y
direction, delta-function confinement in the
z
direction

In this case, we confine electrons in the transverse
yz
-plane using harmonic confinement in the
y
-direction and neglect the motion of electrons in the
z
-direction, which is equivalent to the assumption of zero wire width.
The one-electron wave function is of the form

\psi(x,y,z)=\frac{e^{iqx}}{\sqrt{L}}\xi_{n}(y)\,\sqrt{\delta(z)}.

(84)

The ground state wave function for a harmonic potential in the
y
-direction is

\xi_{0}(y)=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi b}}\right]^{1/2}\exp\left({-\frac{y^{2}}{2b^{2}}}\right)H_{0}\left(\frac{y}{b}\right),

(85)

where
b
is related to wire width and
H_{0}\left(y/{b}\right)=1
is the Hermite polynomial. Using Eqs. (
84
) and (
85
), matrix elements of the Coulomb interaction are given by,

\displaystyle V_{5}(q)=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{iq\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)}\,d\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\delta(z)\delta(z^{\prime})\,dz}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy^{\prime}\frac{\xi_{0}{}^{2}\left(y\right)\xi_{0}{}^{2}\left(y^{\prime}{}\right)}{\sqrt{\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}+\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}}.

(86)

Substituting
x-x^{\prime}=x
,

V_{5}(q)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{iqx}\,dx}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy^{\prime}\,\xi_{0}^{2}\left(y\right)\xi_{0}^{2}\left(y^{\prime}\right).

(87)

Using the integral identity for Bessel functions and Eq. (
85
),

V_{5}(q)=\frac{2}{\pi b^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy^{\prime}\,\exp\left({\frac{-y^{2}}{b^{2}}}\right)\exp\left({\frac{-y^{\prime 2}}{b^{2}}}\right)K_{0}\left(q\left|y-y^{\prime}\right|\right),

(88)

where
K_{0}\left(q\left|y-y^{\prime}\right|\right)
is the zeroth-order modified Bessel function of the second kind. Equation (
88
) can also be rewritten as

V_{5}(q)=\frac{2}{\pi b^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy^{\prime}\,\exp{\left(-\frac{\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{2b^{2}}\right)}\exp{\left(-\frac{\left(y+y^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{2b^{2}}\right)}K_{0}\left(q\left|y-y^{\prime}\right|\right).

(89)

Now transform
y
and
y^{\prime}
into center of mass coordinates. Given
\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}=Y
and
\frac{y+y^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}=Y^{\prime}
, the Jacobian transformation implies
dy\,dy^{\prime}=dY\,dY^{\prime}
and so Eq. (
89
) becomes

V_{5}(q)=\frac{2}{\pi b^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left({\frac{-Y^{2}}{b^{2}}}\right)K_{0}(q\sqrt{2}Y)\,dY\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left({\frac{-Y^{\prime 2}}{b^{2}}}\right)\,dY^{\prime}.

(90)

Using the Gamma function and Bessel function integral identities, Eq. (
90
) simplifies to

V_{5}(q)=\exp\left(\frac{q^{2}b^{2}}{4}\right)K_{0}\left(\frac{q^{2}b^{2}}{4}\right).

(91)

\displaystyle V_{6}(q)

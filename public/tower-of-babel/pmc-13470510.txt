Transcriptome datasets of salicylic acid-treated tomato plant cultures under normal and stress conditions
Authors: Kincső Decsi, Mostafa Ahmed, Zoltán Tóth
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtreme weather events pose a significant challenge to agricultural systems, as they induce various abiotic stress responses in plants. Preventive crop protection strategies that aim to enhance plant resistance in advance are gaining increasing importance to mitigate these stress effects. Priming treatments based on the activation of the immune response allow plants to be prepared for stress by triggering some of the defense mechanisms before the actual stress occurs.In the study, healthy, young tomato plants were pre-treated with a foliar spray containing salicylic acid and then subjected to drought stress during the vegetation period. NGS-based shallow RNA sequencing was used to examine the samples at the molecular level. Following bioinformatic processing and evaluation of the obtained sequence data, it was possible to analyse the putative stimulatory effects of salicylic acid treatment and to monitor the development of long-term adaptation processes induced by priming, such as the development of plant stress memory under stress conditions.The sequencing data were submitted in the NCBI database in the form of four forward SRA data sets, in three biological replicates, providing a total of 12 SRA data sets. Furthermore, a de novo transcriptome assembly (TSA) file generated from the quality-controlled reads, along with a read count table obtained from from RNA-seq analysis, was deposited in the Mendeley Data repository. Since Kecskeméti 549 is a special local tomato variety, no reference genome is available in public databases; therefore, the de novo assembly method was chosen for the reconstruction of the transcriptome.The created datasets provide an opportunity for future studies to explore the short-term (priming effect) and prolonged (during stress exposure) molecular consequences of the treatments. The data can also be used to identify and monitor plant biochemical processes induced by salicylic acid, with particular attention to changes in gene expression patterns.Keywords: Salicylic acid (SA), Tomato, Illumina NGS, RNA-seq, Priming, Stress memory

Background

The application of bioactive compounds that induce priming effects as foliar treatments is receiving increasing attention in plant stress physiology research, however, their molecular mechanism of action is not yet fully understood. According to current knowledge, these compounds do not exert a direct yield-increasing effect, but rather activate the plant's defense processes through a mild stimulating stimulus (eustress), thereby preparing it for later adverse environmental conditions. Nevertheless, the gene expression changes induced by priming and their long-term molecular consequences remain a relatively unexplored area of research.

The aim of generating the data sets was to explore the molecular responses induced by salicylic acid priming treatment and to determine how the treatment influences the transcriptional changes that occur during later drought stress and gene expression processes that can be associated with stress memory. A further purpose of this study was to investigate the transcriptomic mechanisms regulated by priming, which may lead to a more precise elucidation of the molecular background of plant stress tolerance.

Data Description

The study consisted of two complementary experimental approaches. The first set of experiments aimed to investigate early molecular responses induced by salicylic acid pretreatment (priming) in young, healthy tomato plants. In the second set of experiments, salicylic acid-pretreated plants were subjected to drought stress at a later phenological stage to determine whether transcriptional changes associated with priming were also detectable during later stress exposure.

The tomato variety Kecskeméti 549 (K-549) was used for the experiments. The plants were grown under greenhouse conditions at the Georgikon Campus of the Hungarian University of Agricultural and Life Sciences, Keszthely. The seedlings were transplanted into individual plastic pots containing a homogeneous mixture of 1:1 (w/w) Ramann-type brown forest soil and peat. During plant cultivation, the day/night temperature was controlled between 25/20 ± 5 °C and the relative humidity was controlled between 60–65%.

Salicylic acid pretreatment was performed on the 23rd day after transplanting, on June 17, 2025. The treatment was performed with a salicylic acid solution at a concentration of 100 mg L⁻¹ as a single foliar spray. Spraying was performed in the early morning hours, applying approximately 30 mL of spray solution per plant. The salicylic acid solution was sprayed evenly onto the entire foliage of the plants until the entire surface of the leaves was wetted. No detergent or wetting agent was used during the preparation of the spray solution.

The experimental design consisted of four treatment groups, each of which was tested in three biological replicates:

Control, untreated, non-stressed plants (Ctrl_non_stressed_R1_1–3);

Control, untreated, drought-stressed plants (Ctrl_stressed_R1_1–3);

Salicylic acid-pretreated, non-stressed plants (SA_non_stressed_R1_1–3);

Salicylic acid-pretreated, drought-stressed plants (SA_stressed_R1_1–3).

Drought stress was applied between June 24, 2025 and July 7, 2025. The stressed groups received 50% of the irrigation water provided to the control plants during the entire stress period. The irrigation volume was determined each time with a measuring cup, therefore the basis of the stress treatment was the reduced water supply compared to the control. During the drought treatment, groundwater potential measurements were not performed, therefore the water deficit state was defined as a water supply of 50% compared to the control irrigation volume.

Unprocessed sequencing reads derived from RNA libraries prepared from control and stress-treated leaf tissues were archived in the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database in Sequence Read Archive (SRA) format. The deposited dataset contained sequencing data from 12 biological samples (4 treatments in 3 repeats).

After quality control, a de novo transcriptome assembly was performed using a total of 260,657,544 short reads. The analysis used pooled sequence data from three independent biological replicates per treatment group. Reference-independent transcriptome assembly was generated, and the identification of 14,027 transcripts was made, which were assigned to 11,585 genes. The mean length of the identified genes was 354 bp (Fig. 1). The resulting supertranscriptome (Transcriptome Shotgun Assembly, TSA) has been deposited in the supplementary file entitled mmc1.fasta.

As a first step to quantify the expression levels of individual genes, a gene-level count table was generated derived from the de novo transcriptome. The generated transcriptomic datasets are suitable for identifying genes induced or repressed by treatments and for detailed analysis of differential gene expression patterns. Functional annotation and further bioinformatics studies can map the biological processes, metabolic and signaling pathways associated with the affected genes, thereby revealing underlying insights into the molecular regulatory mechanisms of salicylic acid priming and the possible long-term stress memory induced by salicylic acid.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

To explore the early molecular responses induced by priming treatment, 20–30 mg of leaf tissue samples were collected from healthy, young tomato plants that had reached the appropriate developmental stage (3 biological replicates). To explore gene expression patterns associated with stress memory, fully developed plants before the onset of flowering were exposed to controlled drought stress. On day 8 after water withdrawal, individual plants from each treatment group were sampled by collecting 20–30 mg of leaf material. Three independent biological replicates were included per treatment. The harvested tissue was immersed in 1 ml RNAlater stabilization reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) and kept at −20 °C until further experimental procedures.

RNA extraction was carried out from the collected leaf tissues, followed by an evaluation of RNA quality prior to further downstream analyses. Only RNA samples with a suitable integrity score (RIN at least 7) were included in further studies. The polyadenylated mRNA fraction from the samples of suitable quality was enriched by the paramagnetic NEXTFLEX® Poly(A) Beads 2.0 system. After fragmentation, strand-specific RNA-sequencing libraries were prepared according to the protocol of the NEXTFLEX® Rapid Directional RNA-Seq 2.0 kit.

Sequencing of the prepared libraries was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, establishing 150 bp single-end reads. The average sequencing depth per sample was 21 million reads, which provided adequate coverage for transcriptomic analyses [1].

After quality control, the low-quality nucleotide sequences and adapter contaminants were removed. The raw read dataset was subjected to quality assessment using FastQC prior to downstream bioinformatic analyses, while read filtering and cleaning were performed using Trimmomatic software [2].

In the absence of a suitable genomic reference, a reference-independent (de novo) transcriptome was reconstructed using Trinity assembler [3] from the quality-checked reads. Trinity reconstructs entire transcripts from short RNA-sequencing reads, thus allowing transcriptome assembly even for species for which no suitable reference genome is available.

In preparation for differential gene expression analyses, the purified reads were mapped onto the de novo assembled transcriptome, and the number of reads assigned to each transcript was determined. Gene-level count data (count table) obtained during quantification served as the basis for subsequent differential gene expression and functional bioinformatics analyses [[4], [5]].

Perfect Secret Key Generation for a Class of Hypergraphical Sources
Manuj Mukherjee, Sagnik Chatterjee, Alhad Sethi

Abstract
Nitinawarat and Narayan proposed a perfect secret key generation scheme for the so-called pairwise independent network (PIN) model by exploiting the combinatorial properties of the underlying graph, namely the spanning tree packing rate. This work considers a generalization of the PIN model where the underlying graph is replaced with a hypergraph, and makes progress towards designing similar perfect secret key generation schemes by exploiting the combinatorial properties of the hypergraph. Our contributions are two-fold. We first provide a capacity achieving scheme for a complete t t -uniform hypergraph on m m vertices by leveraging a packing of the complete t t -uniform hypergraphs by what we refer to as star hypergraphs, and designing a scheme that gives ( m − 2 t − 2 ) \binom{m-2}{t-2} bits of perfect secret key per star graph. Our second contribution is a 2-bit perfect secret key generation scheme for 3-uniform star hypergraphs whose projections are cycles. This scheme is then extended to a perfect secret key generation scheme for generic 3-uniform hypergraphs by exploiting star graph packing of 3-uniform hypergraphs and Hamiltonian packings of graphs. The scheme is then shown to be capacity achieving for certain classes of hypergraphs.

A

The PIN model is defined using an underlying graph whose vertex set equals the set of parties. Associated with every edge are i.i.d. sequences of independent Be

The scheme involves packing multiple copies of the underlying graph with spanning trees and generating a single bit of perfect secret key from every spanning tree. A natural generalization of the PIN model is the so-called

where the underlying graph is allowed to be a hypergraph. One envisages that a Nitinawarat-Narayan type scheme exploiting the combinatorial properties of the underlying hypergraph should be able to generate capacity achieving perfect secret keys. In this work, we make progress towards realizing this goal.

Our contributions in this work are two-fold. Firstly, we provide an explicit secret key achieving scheme for the complete
t
-uniform hypergraph by exploiting its combinatorial properties. We begin by developing a key generation protocol that extracts

Secondly, we give a perfect secret key generation scheme (not necessarily capacity achieving) for generic
3

of its projected graph. The final scheme then involves packing the original hypergraphs with star hypergraphs whose projected graphs have large Hamiltonian packing numbers. This scheme is shown to be capacity achieving for certain classes of 3-uniform hypergraphs.

Technical preliminaries

II-A

Multiterminal source model

The multiterminal source model of Csiszár and Narayan
[
11
]
considers a set
\mathcal{M}=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
of
m\geq 2
parties.
Party
i\in\mathcal{M}
observes an
n
-length random vector
X_{i}^{n}
taking values in a finite alphabet

\mathcal{X}_{i}^{n}
.
The collection of observations across all parties is denoted by
X_{\mathcal{M}}^{n}=(X_{i}^{n}:i\in\mathcal{M})
. We assume that the source is memoryless, meaning that the joint distribution factors as
P_{X_{\mathcal{M}}^{n}}(x_{\mathcal{M}}^{n})=\prod_{t=1}^{n}P_{X_{\mathcal{M}}}(x_{\mathcal{M},t})
, where
P_{X_{\mathcal{M}}}
is a joint probability mass function on
\prod_{i\in\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{X}_{i}
. For any subset
A\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, we define the notation
X_{A}=(X_{i}:i\in A)
.

To generate secret keys, the parties are allowed to (interactively) communicate over a noiseless public channel. The random variable corresponding to the public communication is denoted by

F:=(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})
, where
F_{j}
denotes the
j
th transmission taking values in some finite set
\mathcal{F}_{j}
. The set of all possible values of the public communication is denoted by
\mathcal{F}=\prod_{i\in r}\mathcal{F}_{r}
. The communication being interactive, the
j
th transmission, sent by say party
i
, is a function of party
i
’s local observation
X_{i}^{n}
as well as past public messages
F^{j-1}
. Following the public messages each party
i\in\mathcal{M}
computes a local key
K_{i}=\varphi_{i}(X_{i}^{n},F)
using a deterministic mapping
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{X}_{i}^{n}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{K}
, where the finite set
\mathcal{K}
is referred to as the key alphabet.
2
2

2

Observe that the random variables
F,K_{i}
and the sets
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{K}
implicitly depend on
n
.

Definition 1
.

A number
R\geq 0
is an
achievable (strong) secret key (SK) rate
if, for every
\epsilon>0
and for all sufficiently large
n
, there exist a public communication
F
, local keys
K_{i},i\in\mathcal{M}
and a random variable
K
(referred to as the
secret key
) taking values in the alphabet
\mathcal{K}
such that the following conditions hold:

1.

Recoverability:
For each party
i\in\mathcal{M}
, the local key
K_{i}
computed from
(X_{i}^{n},F)
satisfies
\Pr(K_i = K)\geq 1-\epsilon
.

2.

Strong Secrecy:
The secret key
K
satisfies
I(K;F)\leq\epsilon
.

3.

Almost uniformity
: The secret key
K
satisfies
\frac{1}{n}H(K)\geq\frac{1}{n}\log\mid\mathcal{K}\mid-\epsilon
.
3
3

3

Throughout this paper, logarithms are taken base
2
, and all entropy quantities are measured in bits.

4.

Secret Key Rate:
The secret key
K
satisfies
\frac{1}{n}H(K)\geq R-\epsilon
.

The
(strong) secret key capacity
, denoted by
C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})
, is the supremum of all achievable strong SK rates.

Csiszár and Narayan
[
11
]
evaluated the secret key capacity
C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})
for a generic multiterminal source. More relevant to us is the alternate characterization of
C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})
given by Chan and Zheng in
[
8
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all partitions of
\mathcal{M}
in at least two parts. The secret key capacity is then given by

C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})=\min_{\mathcal{P}:|\mathcal{P}|>1}I_{P}(X_{\mathcal{M}}),

(1)

where

I_{P}(X_{\mathcal{M}}):=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|-1}\left(\sum_{C\in\mathcal{P}}H(X_{C})-H(X_{\mathcal{M}})\right).

(2)

Of relevance to us is the partition of
\mathcal{M}
into singletons, denoted by
\mathcal{S}=\{\{1\},\{2\},\ldots,\{m\}\}
, which we shall henceforth refer to as the
singleton partition
. Following the convention of
[
18
]
, we shall refer to the sources where
\mathcal{S}
is a minimizer of the right-hand side of (
1
) as
Type-
\mathcal{S}
sources
. Therefore,
C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})=I_{\mathcal{S}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})
for Type-
\mathcal{S}
sources.

While
C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})
is defined under the asymptotic notion of strong secrecy (where error and leakage vanish as
n\to\infty
), there is a stricter non-asymptotic notion known as
perfect secrecy
. A random variable
K
is a perfect secret key if it satisfies exact recoverability (
\Pr(K_1 = \dots= K_m = K)=1
), perfect secrecy (
I(K;F)=0
), and perfect uniformity (
H(K)=\log|\mathcal{K}|
) for some blocklength
n
. It is immediate that a perfect secret key satisfies strong secrecy, but not vice versa. Accordingly, the perfect secret key capacity is upper-bounded by the usual strong secret key capacity.

We remark that henceforth, the phrase ‘secret key capacity’ will be used to refer to the strong secret key capacity. Furthermore, we shall say that a perfect secret key achieves capacity if it has a rate equal to the secret key capacity. This work shows that, for instances of a special class of sources, called the hypergraphical sources, perfect secret keys achieve capacity.

II-B

Hypergraphical source model

A
hypergraphical source
is defined using an underlying hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E})
whose vertex set is the set of parties
\mathcal{M}
. We allow
\mathcal{E}
to be a
multiset
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}
is allowed to have repeated elements. Associated with each hyperedge
e\in\mathcal{E}
are
n
i.i.d. copies of a Bernoulli(1/2) random variable
\xi_{e}
. Furthermore, the collection of random variables
(\xi_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E})
is mutually independent. The observation of party
i\in\mathcal{M}
is the collection of variables associated with hyperedges incident to it, i.e.,
X_{i}=(\xi_{e}:i\in e)
.
Consequently, the joint source is
X_{\mathcal{M}}=(\xi_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E})
.

The combinatorial structure inherent in hypergraphical sources greatly simplifies the expression for the secret key capacity. This is especially evident in the case of the so-called
t
-uniform
hypergraphs. A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E})
is said to be
t
-uniform if all hyperedges are of size
t
, i.e.,
|e|=t
for all
e\in\mathcal{E}
. In particular, it is easy to see that the secret key capacity for Type-
\mathcal{S}
sources on
t
-uniform hypergraphs is given by

C_{\mathrm{SK}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})=I_{\mathcal{S}}(X_{\mathcal{M}})=\frac{t-1}{m-1}|\mathcal{E}|.

(3)

A special case of the hypergraphical source is the
pairwise independent network (PIN)
model
[
20
,
19
]
, where the underlying hypergraph is a graph. Nitinawarat and Narayan
[
19
]
have shown that the PIN model admits a linear communication scheme which achieves a linear perfect secret key, via spanning tree packing of the underlying graph. In this work, we develop similar linear perfect secret key generation schemes for
t
-uniform hypergraphical sources by exploiting the combinatorial structure of the underlying hypergraph.

To proceed, we define some notation relevant to hypergraphical sources. Given a set
A\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, we define the notation
\mathcal{E}_{A}:=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:e\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
. When
A=\{i\}
(resp.,
A=\{i,j\}
) we shall abuse notation and write
\mathcal{E}_{i}
instead of
\mathcal{E}_{\{i\}}
(resp.,
\mathcal{E}_{i,j}
instead of
\mathcal{E}_{\{i,j\}}
). For the
n
random bits
\xi_{e}^{n}
associated with the hyperedge
e
, we shall use the notation
\xi_{e(j)}
to denote the
j
th i.i.d. copy of
\xi_{e}
. In fact, whenever necessary, we shall associate
n
fictitious copies
e(1),e(2),\ldots,e(n)
with every hyperegde
e\in\mathcal{E}
, and associate
\xi_{e(j)}
with
e(j)
. Furthermore, we define the set of all
n
fictitious copies of the hyperedges
\mathcal{E}^{(n)}=\{e(j):e\in\mathcal{E},1\leq j\leq n\}
. Next, given any
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, we define the tuples
\xi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}:=(\xi_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
\xi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}^{n}:=(\xi_{e(j)}:e\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime},1\leq j\leq n)
.

Hypergraphical sources in general admit linear communication schemes that consist of every party sending some linear combinations of the bits associated with the hyperedges incident on it. Formally, let
X_{\mathcal{M}}
be a hypergraphical source defined on the underlying hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E})
. A public communication
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m})
is said to be
linear
if for every
i\in\mathcal{M}
, the party
i
sends the communication
F_{i}=M_{i}\xi_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}
,
4
4

4

All matrix operations and ranks considered in this paper are over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
where the matrix
M_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{r_{i}\times n|\mathcal{E}|}
for some integer
r_{i}\geq 0
. Furthermore, the matrices
M_{i}
must satisfy
M_{i}(r,e(j))=1
only if
e\in\mathcal{E}_{i}
, to ensure that party
i

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}r_{i}
bits. A linear communication is said to achieve

if every party

\xi_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}
correctly with probability one using only

and

We next show that any perfect omniscience scheme can be leveraged to generate a perfect secret key.

.

Let
X_{\mathcal{M}}
be a hypergraphical source defined on the underlying hypergraph

Let
X_{\mathcal{M}}
be a hypergraphical source on the underlying hypergraph

V
Conclusion

In this work, we gave explicit capacity achieving perfect secret key generation schemes for the complete
t
-uniform hypergraph and certain classes of
3
-uniform hypergraphs. Our schemes are an attempt to extend the spanning tree packing scheme of Nitinawarat and Narayan
[
19
]
meant for PIN models on graphs. Unfortunately, there is no unique generalization of spanning trees to the setting of hypergraphs. Hence, for our scheme for
K_{m,t}
, we pack the hypergraph with star hypergraphs, which serve as the proxy for the spanning trees, and generate perfect secret keys for each star hypergraph. Similarly, the scheme for
3
-uniform hypergraphs uses cycle inducing
3
-uniform hypergraphs as stand-ins for spanning trees. We believe that a good generalization of spanning trees will shed further light on the problem of explicit perfect secret key generation schemes for hypergraphical sources.

In this regard, we believe that the so-called
minimally topologically connected
hypergraphs studied in
[
14
]
are a good candidate for generalizations of spanning trees. Indeed, the common randomness generation scheme for minimally topologically connected hypergraphs studied in
[
14
]
coupled with Lemma
1
gives an explicit construction of perfect secret keys achieving capacity. We conjecture that every topologically connected hypergraph can be suitably packed by minimally topologically connected hypergraphs such that invoking Lemma
2
will give a perfect secret key achieving capacity.

Furthermore, note that we have a scheme for
3
-uniform hypergraphs which achieves capacity for non-trivial classes of hypergraphs other than the complete hypergraph. A parallel direction of research would be to extend our scheme to
t
-uniform hypergraphs. This would require a generalization of the cycle to hypergraphs, and we conjecture that sphere triangulations is the correct generalization to look at in this case.

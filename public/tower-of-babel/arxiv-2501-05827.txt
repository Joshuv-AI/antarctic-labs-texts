1.
Introduction

Definition 1.1

(Hypercube Graph)
.

Let
Q_{n}:=\{0,1\}^{n}
denote the set of binary strings of length
n
. We give
Q_{n}
the structure of a graph by saying that two strings
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
share an edge iff they differ in exactly one digit.

Definition 1.2

(Binary Order)
.

There is a canonical ordering on
Q_{n}
. Given
x,y\in Q_{n}
, we view them as binary representations of natural numbers and use the standard
x\leq y
ordering on the naturals. The
initial segment
of length
a
, denoted
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
, is the set of
x\in Q_{n}
such that
x<a
. Note this is only well defined for
a\leq 2^{n}
.

Example 1.3
.

Consider
I_{6}\subseteq Q_{3}
. This is the set
I_{6}=\{000,001,010,011,100,101\}
as shown in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.

I_{6}
in the hypercube
Q_{3}

Convention 1.1
.

Let
Aut(Q_{n})
be the set of graph automorphisms of
Q_{n}
. For
S\subseteq Q_{n}
and
g\in G
, we will let
g(S)
denote the image of
S
under the automorphism
g
. For
S,T\subseteq Q_{n}
, we will write
S\cong T
if their induced subgraphs are isomorphic.

Definition 1.4

(Edge Boundary)
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph and
S\subseteq V
be an arbitrary subset of vertices. Then
\delta S=\{(u,v)\in E|u\in S,v\notin S\}
, i.e. the set of edges with exactly one vertex in
S
. If we view
S
as partitioning
V
into
(S,V\setminus S)
, this can also be interpreted as the edges crossing the partition.

Question 1.1

(Edge Isoperimetric Problem)
.

Fix some positive integer
k
. Which sets
S\subseteq G
with
|S|=k
minimize the quantity
|\delta S|?

In particular, people asked this question for the hypercube graph
G=Q_{n}
. The following theorem is originally proven in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
, and
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Harper, Lindsey, Bernstein, Hart)
.

For
S\subseteq Q_{n}
non-empty,
|S|=k
,
S
minimizes the edge boundary iff
S\cong I_{k}
.

Since the edge isoperimetric problem can also be viewed as minimizing the edges crossing a partition of
Q_{n}
into
2
sets, it is natural to consider partioning
Q_{n}
into
3
, or in general
k
sets.

Definition 1.6

(Partition Boundary)
.

Let
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a partition of
Q_{n}
into
k
sets. Then
\delta A:=\{(u,v)\in E(Q_{n})|u\in A_{i},v\in A_{j},i\neq j\}
.

Previous results have concerned partioning
Q_{n}
into
k
approximately equal sets, i.e. differing in cardinality by at most one (cite Bezrukov). However, in this paper we consider 3-partioning the hypercube into sets of arbitrary size.

The following result, which appears on
[
1
]
p.7, gives us a convenient language.

Proposition 1.7
.

Consider
I_{a}
as an induced subgraph and let
i:I_{a}\to Q_{n}
be an arbitrary embedding of the initial segment of length
a
into
Q_{n}
. Then there is some
g\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that
i(I_{a})=g(I_{a})
.

Observation 1.8
.

Let
A
be a
3
-partition of
Q_{n}
such that
A_{1}\cong I_{a}
,
A_{2}\cong I_{b}
, and
A_{3}\cong I_{c}
, for some positive integers
a+b+c=2^{n}
. Then
|\delta A|=\frac{1}{2}(|\delta A_{1}|+|\delta A_{2}|+|\delta A_{3}|)
is minimized over all partitions of size
(a,b,c)
by Theorem
1.5
. It is natural to ask when
Q_{n}
can be partitioned into such
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
. It is also equivalent to say that given
(a,b,c)
, there are
A,B
such that
A\cong I_{a}
,
B\cong I_{b}
, and
A\cup B\cong I_{a+b}
, as
Q_{n}\setminus(A\cup B)
must then be isomorphic to
I_{c}
. By Proposition
1.7
, we must have
A=g_{1}(I_{a})
,
B=g_{2}(I_{b})
and
A\cup B=g_{3}(I_{a+b})
, for some
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Thus, we have
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=g_{3}(I_{a+b})
. Applying
g_{3}^{-1}
to both sides this is equivalent to
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
for some
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
.

Question 1.2

(Essential Question)
.

Given positive integers
a,b
with
a+b\leq 2^{n}
, when does there exist
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
?

Definition 1.9

(Unfit Pairs)
.

Given a pair of positive integers
(a,b)
with
a,b\leq 2^{n}
, we say that
(a,b)
is
fit
if it satisfies the condition in Question
1.2
. Otherwise,
(a,b)
is called
unfit
.

Observation 1.10
.

Note that if
(a,b)
is a fit pair with
a+b+c=2^{n}
, then
(b,a)
,
(a,c)
,
(c,a)
,
(b,c)
and
(c,b)
are also fit pairs. Therefore, our set of fit pairs displays a 6-fold symmetry.

2.
Basic Tools

Definition 2.1

(Exclusive-Or)
.

Let
x\in Q_{n}
. For
y\in Q_{n}
, let
x\oplus y
denote the exclusive-or operation with inputs
x
and
y
. Equivalently, if
x
and
y
are thought of as elements of
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n}
, this is just component-wise addition. Fixing some
x\in Q_{n}
, the map
y\mapsto x\oplus y
is an automorphism of
Q_{n}
.

Definition 2.2

(Permutation)
.

Let
x\in Q_{n}
and
\sigma
be a permutation of the numbers
\{0,1,...n-1\}
. If the binary representation for
x
is
x_{0}x_{1}...x_{n-1}
, then define
\sigma(x)=x_{\sigma(0)}x_{\sigma(1)}...x_{\sigma(n-1)}
. For fixed
\sigma
, the map
y\mapsto\sigma(y)
is again an automorphism of
Q_{n}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Automorphism Decomposition)
.

Let
g\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Then, there is a unique permutation
\sigma
and unique
x\in Q_{n}
such that
g(y)=x\oplus\sigma(y)
for all
y\in Q_{n}
.

Proof.

This is a translation of known results about the structure of
\text{Aut}(Q_{n})
into elementary language. Clearly, each
\sigma
in the symmetric group of
n
elements gives distinct action on
Q_{n}
, and each map
y\rightarrow x\oplus y
gives distinct action on
Q_{n}
. If
x_{1}\oplus\sigma_{1}
=
x_{2}\oplus\sigma_{2}
, then since the vertex
00\ldots 0
is sent to
x_{1}
by the first map and
x_{2}
by the second map,
x_{1}=x_{2}
. Consequently,
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
. Hence, the
x\oplus\sigma
’s give
2^{n}\cdot n!
distinct automorphisms. The theorem follows from the fact that
|\text{Aut}(Q_{n})|=2^{n}\cdot n!
by
[
6
]
.
∎

We next introduce the point-counting function central to our discussion.

Definition 2.4

(m-Vector)
.

Let
S\subseteq Q_{n},0\leq i\leq n-1
. Define
m_{i}(S)
to be the total number of 1’s that appear on the
i
th position of elements in
S
. Define
m(S)
to be the vector
(m_{0}(S),m_{1}(S),\ldots,m_{n-1}(S))
.

Example 2.5
.

I_{5},I_{6}\subseteq Q_{3}
.
m(I_{5})=(1,2,2),
and
m(I_{6})=(2,2,3)
.

Lemma 2.6

(Ordering Lemma)
.

For
m(I_{a})=(m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n-1}),

m_{0}\leq m_{1}\leq...\leq m_{n-1}
.

Proof.

By the construction of initial segments, the sequence of
0
s and
1
s in a column
i
can be written as a sequence of
2^{n-i-1}

0
s followed by
2^{n-i-1}

1
s. Thus, the partial sum of the sequence in column
i+1
must be greater than the corresponding partial sum of the sequence in column
i
.
∎

The action of the automorphism on the m-vector can be easily described.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
S\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
m(S)=(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})

(1)

Let
x=x_{0}x_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}\in Q_{n}
. Let
J
denote the set of index
j
such that
x_{j}=1
. Then

m_{j}(x\oplus S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|S|-m_{j}(S)&\mbox{if}&j\in J\\
m_{j}(S)&\mbox{if}&j\not\in J\end{array}\right.

(2)

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Then
m(\sigma(S))=\sigma(m(S))
, where
\sigma
permutes the
n
elements in the
m-vector
.

Proof.

Fix
j
. If
j\in J
, then
x\oplus S
turns all
0
’s into
1
’s and all
1
’s into
0
’s. So the new number of
1
’s is simply the number of
0
’s before applying the automorphism. Thus,
m_{j}(x\oplus S)=|S|-m_{j}(S)
. The other parts of the proposition follows directly from definition.
∎

Note that for any
g\in\text{Aut}Q_{n}
and
S\subseteq Q_{n}
,
m(g(S))
can be described using only the information of
m(S)
and
|S|
. Thus, if
m(S_{1})
=
m(S_{2})
and
|S_{1}|=|S_{2}|
, then
m(g(S_{1}))=m(g(S_{2}))
.

The following proposition shows an important property unique to the initial segments.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
.
Let
S\subseteq Q_{n}
,
|S|=a
. Then
m(I_{a})=m(S)
if and only if
I_{a}=S
.

Proof.

We use the idea of a weighted sum. For any subset
S\subseteq Q_{n}
, each element may be regarded as the binary representation of an integer. The weighted sum of
S
is referred to as the sum of all such integers. By definition, this sum is also
2^{n-1}m_{0}(S)+2^{n-2}m_{1}(S)+\cdots+2^{0}m_{n-1}(S)
.

If
I_{a}=S
, then
m(I_{a})=m(S)
. Conversely, let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
. Suppose
S\subseteq Q_{n}
,
|S|=a
, and
m(I_{a})=m(S)
. Observe that
I_{a}
is the unique subset of
Q_{n}
having the smallest weighted sum among subsets of size at least
a
since
I_{a}
represents the integers
\{0,1,\ldots,a-1\}
. Since
m(I_{a})=m(S)
,
I_{a}
and
S
must have the same weighted sum, which implies that
I_{a}=S
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{p},T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{q}\subseteq Q_{n}
, where
|S_{1}|+\cdots+|S_{p}|=|T_{1}|+\cdots+|T_{q}|
. Let
g\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Then

m(S_{1})+\cdots+m(S_{p})=m(T_{1})+\cdots+m(T_{q})

if and only if

m(g(S_{1}))+\cdots+m(g(S_{p}))=m(g(T_{1}))+\cdots+m(g(T_{q})).

Proof.

Let
g=x\oplus\sigma\in Aut(Q_{n})
. The image
m(g(S))
is uniquely determined by
m(S)
and
|S|
, so we may define a natural action
g(v,n)
on the set of pairs
(v,n)
,
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
using the rules in Proposition
2.7
. Moreover, regarding this set as a semigroup under addition, we also have
g(v+w,n+m)=g(v,n)+g(w,m)
again by Proposition
2.7
.

Suppose,
m(S_{1})+\cdots+m(S_{p})=m(T_{1})+\cdots+m(T_{q})
. Then

\displaystyle m(g(S_{1}))+\cdots+m(g(S_{p}))

\displaystyle=g(m(S_{1}),|S_{1}|)+\cdots+g(m(S_{p}),|S_{p}|)

\displaystyle=g(m(S_{1})+\cdots+m(S_{p}),|S_{1}|+\cdots+|S_{p}|)

\displaystyle=g(m(T_{1})+\cdots+m(T_{q}),|T_{1}|+\cdots+|T_{q}|)

\displaystyle=g(m(T_{1}),|T_{1}|)+\cdots+g(m(T_{q}),|T_{q}|)

\displaystyle=m(g(T_{1}))+\cdots+m(g(T_{q})).

The ”if” direction is directly obtained by applying
g^{-1}
to each term and using the ”only if” direction.
∎

Next, we introduce a visual representation of how the automorphism group acts on the m-vectors.

Definition 2.10

(m-Table)
.

Let
S\subseteq Q_{n}
,
m(S)=(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})
. Then the m-table of
S
is defined as

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}m_{0}&m_{1}&\ldots&m_{n-1}\\
|S|-m_{0}&|S|-m_{1}&\ldots&|S|-m_{n-1}\end{array}\right)

The rows will be referred to as row
0
and row
1
. The
j
th column will refer to the column that corresponds to
m_{j}
. The entry in
(i,j)
denotes the entry in row
i
and column
j
.

By the ordering lemma, if
(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})
is the m-vector of
I_{a}
, then
m_{0}\leq m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{n-1}
. Furthermore,
m_{n-1}\leq a/2
, which means that
m_{n-1}\leq a-m_{n-1}
. Again by the ordering lemma,
a-m_{n-1}\leq\cdots\leq a-m_{1}\leq a-m_{0}
. This describes the total order on the entries of the m-table of
I_{a}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and let
\alpha
be the smallest integer such that
m_{\alpha}(I_{a})\neq 0
. Then the smallest non-zero number in the m-table of
I_{a}
is
a-2^{n-1-\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and let
\alpha
be the smallest integer such that
m_{\alpha}(I_{a})\neq 0
. Consider the representation of an intial segment
I_{a}
as the set of
n
sequences
\{M_{0},\cdots M_{n}\}
, where
M_{i}
is the sequence of
0
s and
1
s in a column
i
. Each sequence
M_{i}
can be written as a sequence of
2^{n-i-1}

0
s followed by
2^{n-i-1}

1
s followed by
2^{n-i-1}

0
s
\cdots
until the sequence contains
a
terms. Because
\alpha
is the smallest integer such that
m_{\alpha}(I_{a})\neq 0
,

M_{\alpha}
consists of
2^{n-\alpha-1}

0
s followed by
a-2^{n-\alpha-1}=m_{\alpha}(I_{a})

1
s.
Now for all
M_{\beta}
with
\beta>\alpha
,
m_{\alpha}\leq m_{\beta}
by the ordering lemma.
∎

Definition 2.12

(Proper Labeling)
.

A proper m-table labeling in
Q_{n}
is a labeling that assigns each label from
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
to exactly one entry of the
2
by
n

There is a natural bijection between

and the set of proper

-table labelings. In particular, if
g=x\oplus\sigma\in Aut(Q_{n})
, where
x=x_{0}x_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}
, then the corresponding proper m-table labeling is the labeling that assigns label

to the row

and the column
\sigma^{-1}(i)
. If
S\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
a_{i}
is the entry in the m-table of S labeled

, then
m(g(S))=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
. Thus, the action of

on

can be visually represented by the m-table.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
S_{1}\subseteq Q_{n}
,

. Then the

of
S_{1}
will denote the m-table of

with labeling given by

. If the entries
(i_{1},j_{1})
in the labeled m-table of

in the labeled m-table of

(i_{2},j_{2})
.

Example 2.14
.

Let
x=101\in Q_{3}
, and
\sigma=(012)\in S_{3}
in cycle notation. Then the m-table of

and the corresponding labeling is

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2^{[1]}&2&3\\
4&4^{[2]}&3^{[0]}\end{array}\right).

The information in

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0^{[1]}&1^{[2]}&4&4^{[3]}&4^{[4]}\\

We end this section by an observation that will be often used without mentioning. Let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
,
g\in Aut(Q_{n})
. If the first
k
entries in
m(g(I_{a}))
are
0
, then there exists
g^{\prime}\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that
g(I_{a})=g^{\prime}(I_{a})
and
g^{\prime}
restricts to an automorphism of
Q_{n-k}
. This

can be obtained by acting as identity on the first

g
on the last

does not affect the first

.

3.
Proof of Main Theorem

In this section, we’ll state and prove our main result, which gives a criterion to decide whether a pair
(a,b)
fits in
Q_{n}
. This result allows us to fully classify all unfit pairs.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Then
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
if and only if
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
F_{j,k}\subseteq Q_{n}
denote the hyperface

\{x_{0}x_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}\in Q_{n}:x_{j}=k\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S\subseteq Q_{n}
. Then
m_{j}(S)=|S\cap F_{j,1}|
, and
|S|-m_{j}(S)=|S\cap F_{j,0}|
. In particular, if
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
, then
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
if and only if

|g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k}|+|g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k}|=|I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}|

for each hyperface
F_{j,k}
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the definition of the hyperface
F_{j,1}
.
F_{j,1}
consists of the elements of
Q_{n}
which have a
1
in position
j
. Therefore,
S\cap F_{j,1}
are the elements in
S
with a
1
in position
j
.
F_{j,1}
and
F_{j,0}
partition
Q_{n}
so both equalities follow. The second statement is then immediate.
∎

Next, we have a lemma allowing us to restrict our attention to the hyperface
F_{0,0}
, which will help to simplify our proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
I_{a},F_{j,k}\subseteq Q_{n}
,
g_{1}\in Aut(Q_{n})
, and
a^{\prime}=|g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k}|
. There is a map
h\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that
h(F_{0,0})=g_{1}^{-1}(F_{j,k})
and
h(I_{a^{\prime}})=I_{a}\cap g_{1}^{-1}(F_{j,k})
.

Proof.

First, note that the image of a hyperface under automorphism is also a hyperface. Therefore, let
F_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}:=g_{1}^{-1}(F_{j,k})
and define
x
to be the largest natural number in the set
S:=I_{a}\cap F_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
. All
a^{\prime}-1
other elements of
S
are less than
x
in the binary order by definition, but furthermore these must be all of the elements in
F_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
less than
x
since
S
is a subset of an initial segment. Therefore,
S\cong I_{a^{\prime}}
and since
a^{\prime}\leq 2^{n-1}
,
I_{a^{\prime}}\subset Q^{n-1}
. Thus, the automorphism
h
given by Proposition
1.7
must send
F_{0,0}=Q^{n-1}
to
g_{1}^{-1}(F_{j,k})
as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Suppose
2^{n-1}<a+b\leq 2^{n}
. If
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
, then there is a hyperface
F_{j,k}
such that

m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}).

We’ll first show that Proposition
3.5
implies Theorem
3.1
. If
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
, then
g_{1}(I_{a})
and
g_{2}(I_{b})
are disjoint, and thus,
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
.

Suppose
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
,
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
. We’ll use induction on
n
. When
n=1
, the statement is clear. Suppose that the statement is true for
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n-1}
. If
a+b\leq 2^{n-1}
, and
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, then since every
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
that forces
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
may only change the values of the last
n-1
coordinates of
m(I_{a})
and
m(I_{b})
, we can treat
g_{1},g_{2}
as automorphisms of
Q_{n-1}
and treat
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}
as subsets of
Q_{n-1}
. By inductive hypothesis,
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
.

Now suppose
2^{n-1}<a+b\leq 2^{n}
. By Proposition
3.5
, the conditions imply that there is a hyperface
F_{j,k}
such that

m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}).

Let
a^{\prime}=|g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k}|
and
b^{\prime}=|g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k}|
. By Lemma
3.3
,

|I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}|=|g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k}|+|g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k}|=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}.

By Lemma
3.4
,
g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k}
,
g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k}
, and
I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}
are isomorphic to
I_{a^{\prime}}
,
I_{b^{\prime}}
, and
I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}
, respectively. In other words, there are
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that

m(h_{1}(I_{a^{\prime}}))+m(h_{2}(I_{b^{\prime}}))=m(h_{3}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})).

By Lemma
2.9
,

m(h_{3}^{-1}h_{1}(I_{a^{\prime}}))+m(h_{3}^{-1}h_{2}(I_{b^{\prime}}))=m(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}).

Note that
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\leq|F_{j,k}|=2^{n-1}
. Hence, regarding everything to be in
Q_{n-1}
, the inductive hypothesis implies that

h_{3}^{-1}h_{1}(I_{a^{\prime}})\cup h_{3}^{-1}h_{2}(I_{b^{\prime}})=I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}.

So
h_{1}(I_{a^{\prime}})\cup h_{2}(I_{b^{\prime}})=h_{3}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})
, which gives

(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k})\cup(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k})=I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k}.

Now
m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,1-k})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,1-k})=m(g_{1}(I_{a}))-m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,k})+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))-m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,k})=m(I_{a+b})-m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,k})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,1-k})
. By the same argument,

(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{j,1-k})\cup(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{j,1-k})=I_{a+b}\cap F_{j,1-k}

Taking union of the above equations give
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
.

We’ll use the rest of the section to prove Proposition
3.5
. Unless otherwise noted,
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
, and
2^{n-1}<a+b\leq 2^{n}
. Furthermore, from now on let
a^{\prime}=m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
and
b^{\prime}=m_{0}(g_{2}(I_{b}))
. Since
a+b>2^{n-1}
,
F_{0,0}\subseteq I_{a+b}
.

Case 1:

\max\{a,b\}<2^{n-1}
, and one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}=0
.

Without loss of generality, assume
a^{\prime}=0
. Then
g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{0,1}=\emptyset
, and
g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{0,0}=g_{1}(I_{a})
. We can partition
I_{a+b}
into
g_{1}(I_{a})
,
F_{0,0}-g_{1}(I_{a})
, and
I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1}
. All of the above disjoint subsets of
I_{a+b}
are isomorphic to initial segments, so we write
F_{0,0}-g_{1}(I_{a})=g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{n-1}-a})
and
I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1}=g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{n-1}})
. Since the subsets are disjoint, we have

m(I_{a+b})=m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{n-1}-a}))+m(g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{n-1}}))

By assumption,
m(I_{a+b})=m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))
. The two equality gives

m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{N-1}-a}))+m(g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}})).

So

m(I_{b})=m(g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{N-1}-a}))+m(g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}})).

Note that
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{N-1}-a})
and
g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}})
are disjoint, and the union of the two sets contains exactly
b
elements. Thus, by Lemma
2.8
,
I_{b}=g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{N-1}-a})\cup g_{2}^{-1}g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}})
, so

g_{2}(I_{b})=g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{2^{N-1}-a})\cup g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}}).

In particular,
g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{0,1}=g_{3}(I_{a+b-2^{N-1}})=I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1}
, so

m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{0,1})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{0,1})=m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{0,1})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1}),

which proves Proposition
3.5
for this case.

Case 2:

\max\{a,b\}<2^{n-1}
, and
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}>0
.

We first state some computational results that will be used in the proof of Case 2.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
I_{a}\subseteq Q_{n}
.

(1)

For each
j
,
m_{j}(I_{a})\leq m_{n-1}(I_{a})=\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor\leq\frac{a}{2}
.

(2)

If
a>2^{n-1}
,
m_{j}(I_{a})\geq 2^{n-2}
for
j\geq 1
.

(3)

If
a>2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}
, then
m_{2}(I_{a})>2^{n-2}
.

(4)

If
a<2^{n-2}+2^{n-3}
, then
m_{i}(I_{a})\leq a-m_{i}(I_{a})<2^{n-2}
for
i\geq 2
.

Proof.

To (1), it follows from Lemma
2.6
and the fact that
m_{n-1}(I_{a})
counts the number of
1
s in the rightmost place from
0
to
a-1
, i.e. the number of natural numbers congruent to
1(mod2)
in this range. To (2), recall that for each
j\geq 1

m_{j}(I_{a})
counts the number of
1
s in the
j
th position. Thus, for each block of
2^{n-j}
elements in
I_{a}
, exactly
2^{n-j-1}
of them will have a
1
in the
j
th position, from which the statement follows. To (3),
a>2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}
, the numbers in the range
2^{n-1}
to
2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}-1
all have no
1
in the second position. Starting at
2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}
, there will be a
1
in the second position, from which the statement follows. To (4), the first inequality follows from (1) above. The second follows from the fact that
m_{i}(I_{a})\geq 2^{n-3}
for
a\geq 2^{n-2}
,
i\geq 2
by a similar argument to statement (2) above.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
, and
g_{1},g_{2}\in Aut(Q_{n})
. Let
2^{n-1}<a+b\leq 2^{n}
,
\max\{a,b\}<2^{n-1}
, and
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}>0
. Suppose
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m(I_{a+b})
. Then

(1)

Let
\alpha,\beta
be the smallest integer such that
m_{\alpha}(I_{a}),m_{\beta}(I_{b})\neq 0
, respectively. Then
\alpha=\beta=1
, and
a^{\prime}=a-2^{n-2}
,
b^{\prime}=b-2^{n-2}
. Thus,
a,b>2^{n-2}
.

(2)

There exist
g_{1}^{\prime},g_{2}^{\prime}\in Aut(Q_{n})
such that
m(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))=m(g_{1}(I_{a}))
,
m(g_{2}^{\prime}(I_{b}))=m(g_{2}(I_{b}))
, and the entry
(1,1)
in the m-tables for
I_{a}
(and respectively,
I_{b}
) is labeled
0
by
g_{1}
(and respectively,
g_{2}
).

(3)

Suppose
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
satisfies the conditions in (2). Then the labeled entry in column 0 of the m-table of
I_{a}
is paired with the labeled entry in column 0 of the m-table of
I_{b}
. Furthermore, if the label is
j
, then either
m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=a
and
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
or
m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=0
and
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=b
.

Proof.

To prove (1), first note that by Lemma
2.11
, the smallest non-zero number in the m-tables of
I_{a}
and
I_{b}
are
a-2^{n-1-\alpha}
and
b-2^{n-1-\beta}
, respectively. Hence,

a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\geq a+b-2^{n-1-\alpha}-2^{n-1-\beta}

But
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}=m_{0}(I_{a+b})=a+b-2^{n-1}
, so

2^{n-1-\alpha}+2^{n-1-\beta}\geq 2^{n-1}

Since
\max\{a,b\}<2^{n-1}
,
\alpha,\beta>0
. Thus, the inequality holds if and only if
\alpha=\beta=1
. This proves that
a^{\prime}=a-2^{n-2}
,
b^{\prime}=b-2^{n-2}
and finishes the proof of (1).

For (2), it suffices to show that
g_{1}
can be replaced by a
g_{1}^{\prime}
with the desired property. Suppose the label
k\neq 0
is assigned to the entry
(i_{1},1)
and that the label
0
is assigned to the entry
(i_{2},j_{2})\neq(1,1)
in the labeled m-table of
I_{a}
under
g_{1}
. Now we relabel the m-table of
I_{a}
such that the label
0
is assigned to (1, 1) and the label
k
is assigned to
(i_{1},j_{2})
. This new label is also proper, so there is an corresponding automorphism
g_{1}^{\prime}
. For each
k^{\prime}\not\in\{0,k\}
,
m_{k^{\prime}}(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))=m_{k^{\prime}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
since the position of the label
k^{\prime}
does not change. Moreover,
m_{0}(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))=m_{1}(I_{a})
, and since
2^{n-2}<a<2^{n-1}
, we have

m_{0}(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))=m_{1}(I_{a})=a-2^{n-2}=m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{a}))

by part (1). Lastly, in our new labeling,
m_{k}(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))
is the entry
(i_{1},j_{2})
, and
m_{k}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
is the entry
(i_{1},1)
in the m-table of
I_{a}
. These entries are equal, since
m_{j_{2}}(I_{a})=m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=m_{1}(I_{a})
, which implies that the column
1
and column
j_{2}
are identical. Hence,
m_{k}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=m_{k}(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))
, and so
m(g_{1}(I_{a}))=m(g_{1}^{\prime}(I_{a}))
. Using the same argument on
I_{b}
and
g_{2}
gives the automorphism
g_{2}^{\prime}
.

For (3), suppose that the label in column
0
of the
g_{1}
-labeled (respectively,
g_{2}
-labeled) m-table for
I_{a}
(respectively,
I_{b})
is
j_{1}
(respectively,
j_{2}
). We first note that
m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
implies that
j_{1}=j_{2}
. This is because only column
0
in the m-table of
I_{a}
contains the value
0
, so
g_{2}
can only assign label
j_{1}
to column
0
, which by definition implies that
j_{1}=j_{2}
. In the same way, if
m_{j_{2}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=0
, then the conclusion holds.

Moreover,
j_{1}=j_{2}
implies that exactly one of
m_{j_{1}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=a
or
m_{j_{2}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=b
is true. To see this, observe that if neither is true, then
m_{j_{1}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))+m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})=0
. We know that
j_{1}\neq 0
by part (2), but for such
j_{1}
, the condition that
a+b>2^{N-1}
guarantees that
m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})>0
, which is a contradiction. If both are true, then
m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})=a+b
which is strictly greater than
(a+b)/2
. But by Lemma
3.6
(1),
m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})\leq m_{n-1}(I_{a+b})\leq(a+b)/2
, which is a contradiction. Thus, it suffices to prove that
m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0.

First, consider the case where at least one of
m_{j_{1}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
and
m_{j_{2}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))
is non-zero. Without loss of generality, suppose the former is non-zero, so
m_{j_{1}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=a
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))\neq 0
. Then,

m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})\geq a+b^{\prime}=a+b-2^{n-2}

But
m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})\leq(a+b)/2
, so

a+b-2^{n-2}\leq\frac{a+b}{2}

which implies that
a+b\leq 2^{n-1}
, contradicting our hypothesis.

We’re then left to prove the case when both
m_{j_{1}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=0
and
m_{j_{2}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
. Since the label
0
is already used,
j_{1},j_{2}\geq 1
. By Lemma
3.6
(2),
m_{j}(I_{a+b})\geq 2^{n-2}
for each
j\geq 1
. It follows that

m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))\geq 2^{n-2}\text{ and }m_{j_{2}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))\geq 2^{n-2}.

If
m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))>2^{n-2}
, then
m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=b
, which by definition implies that
j_{1}=j_{2}
. Similarly,
m_{j_{2}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))>2^{n-2}
also implies that
j_{1}=j_{2}
. Hence, we’ll assume for the rest of the proof that

m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))\leq 2^{n-2}\text{ and }m_{j_{2}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))\leq 2^{n-2}.

Combining the inequalities, we see that

m_{j_{1}}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=m_{j_{2}}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=m_{j_{1}}(I_{a+b})=m_{j_{2}}(I_{a+b})=2^{n-2}.

By Lemma
3.6
(3), when
a+b>2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}
,
m_{2}(I_{a+b})>2^{n-2}
. In this case, only
m_{0}(I_{a+b})
and
m_{1}(I_{a+b})
could possibly be
2^{n-2}
. But since
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
are distinct and non-zero, this could never happen. Hence,
a+b\leq 2^{n-1}+2^{n-3}<2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}
.

Suppose
g_{1}
assigns label
j_{2}
to column
i_{1}
, and
g_{2}
assigns label
j_{1}
to column
i_{2}
. Since both column
0
and column
1
already have other labels in our current assumptions,
i_{1},i_{2}\geq 2
. This means that
m_{i_{1}}(I_{a})
or
a-m_{i_{1}}(I_{a})
is equal to
2^{n-2}
, and
m_{i_{2}}(I_{b})
or
b-m_{i_{2}}(I_{b})
is equal to
2^{n-2}
. By Lemma
3.6
(4), this takes place only when
a,b\geq 2^{n-2}+2^{n-3}
. But then
a+b\geq 2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}
, which contradicts the above bound. This concludes the proof.
∎

Now we prove the proposition for case 2. By Lemma
3.7
(2), we can assume without loss of generality that the entries
(1,1)
in the m-table of
I_{a}
(and
I_{b}
, respectively) is labeled
0
by
g_{1}
(and
g_{2}
, respectively). This means that
g_{1}^{-1}(F_{0,1})=g_{2}^{-1}(F_{0,1})=F_{1,1}
. Equivalently,
g_{1}^{-1}(F_{0,0})=g_{2}^{-1}(F_{0,0})=F_{1,0}
. We’ll show that

m(g_{1}(I_{a}\cap F_{1,0}))+m(g_{2}(I_{b}\cap F_{1,0}))=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,0}),

which would imply that
m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{0,0})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{0,0})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,0})
.

By Lemma
3.7
(3), if an entry in the column
0
of
I_{a}
is labeled
j
by
g_{1}
, then an entry in the column
0
of
I_{b}
is also labeled
j
by
g_{2}
. Furthermore, exactly one of the two entries lie in row
0
. Without loss of generality, assume that the entry in
(0,0)
in
I_{a}
and the entry
(1,0)
in
I_{b}
are labeled
j
by
g_{1},g_{2}
, respectively. Then the labeled m-tables become:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0^{[j]}&a-2^{n-2^{[0]}}&*&\ldots&*\\
a&2^{n-2}&*&\ldots&*\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&b-2^{n-2^{[0]}}&*&\ldots&*\\
b^{[j]}&2^{n-2}&*&\ldots&*\end{array}\right)

Note that
I_{a}\cap F_{1,0}
and
I_{b}\cap F_{1,0}
are precisely the initial segment of length
2^{n-2}
. Hence, the labeled m-table of
I_{a}\cap F_{1,0}
by
g_{1}
and
I_{b}\cap F_{1,0}
by

are:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0^{[j]}&0^{[0]}&2^{n-3}&\ldots&2^{n-3}\\
2^{n-2}&2^{n-2}&2^{n-3}&\ldots&2^{n-3}\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0^{[0]}&2^{n-3}&\ldots&2^{n-3}\\
2^{n-2^{[j]}}&2^{n-2}&2^{n-3}&\ldots&2^{n-3}\end{array}\right)

Thus,
m(g_{1}(I_{a})\cap F_{0,0})+m(g_{2}(I_{b})\cap F_{0,0})=(0,2^{n-2},2^{n-2},\ldots,2^{n-2})=m(I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,0})
. This finishes the proof of case 2.

Case 3:

\max\{a,b\}\geq 2^{n-1}
.

Without loss of generality, let
a\geq b
. Then
a\geq 2^{n-1}
, so if

, then

By Lemma

,

But

, which forces

and

. Hence,

which clearly implies the conclusion. This finishes the proof of Proposition

, and hence, the proof of Theorem

.

(a+2^{n},b)
fits if and only if
(a,b+2^{n})
fits.

Proof.

If
(a,b)
fits, where
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
, then
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
affect only the subset
Q_{n}=F_{0,0}
in
Q_{n+1}
. Then clearly,
g_{1}(I_{2^{n}+a})\cup 10\cdots 0\oplus g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{2^{n}+a+b}
.

Suppose now
(a+2^{n},b)
fits, where
g_{1}(I_{a+2^{n}})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{2^{n}+a+b}
. Let
g_{1}(F_{0,0})=F_{j,k}
. Then
|g_{1}(I_{a+2^{n}})\cap F_{j,1-k}|=a
, and
g_{2}(I_{b})\subseteq F_{j,1-k}
. Since
g_{1}(I_{a+2^{n}})\cap F_{j,1-k},g_{2}(I_{b}),I_{2^{n}+a+b}\cap F_{j,1-k}
are isomorphic to the initial segments of lengths
a,b,a+b
, respectively,
(a,b)
fits.
∎

4.2.
Reflection of the Lower Triangle

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
a,b\leq 2^{n-1}
, and
2^{n-1}\leq a+b\leq 2^{n}
. Then
(a,b)
fits if and only if
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
fits. Thus, the number of unfit pairs in
\{(a,b):a,b\leq 2^{n-1},2^{n-1}\leq a+b\leq 2^{n}\}
is equal to the number of unfit pairs in
\{(a,b):a,b\geq 2^{n-1},a+b\leq 2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}.

Proof.

We first show that if there are
g_{1},g_{2}\in\text{Aut}(Q_{n})
such that
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})=I_{a+b}
, and
a,b\leq 2^{n-1}
,
2^{n-1}\leq a+b\leq 2^{n}
, then
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
fits. Let
a^{\prime}=m_{1}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
(which is the first non-zero entry in
m(I_{a+b})
given the bounds of
a,b
), and
b^{\prime}=m_{1}(g_{2}(I_{b}))
.

Suppose
a^{\prime}=0
or
b^{\prime}=0
. Without loss of generality, suppose
a^{\prime}=0
. We’ll need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

As usual, assume that all relevant initial segments lie in
Q_{n+1}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\leq 2^{k}
. Then the last
k
columns of the
m
-table of
I_{2^{k}-a}
can be obtained by adding each corresponding entry of the
m
-table of
I_{a}
by
2^{n-1}-a
.

Proof.

First, since
m_{j}
measures the number of
1
’s on a hyperface,
m_{j}(Q_{k}-I_{a})=2^{k-1}-m_{j}(I_{a})
. Observe that
I_{2^{k}-a}
can be obtained by turning all
0
’s to
1
’s and all
1
’s to
0
’s, which is simply applying the automorphism
x\rightarrow 1\cdots 1\oplus x
. Thus,

m_{j}(I_{2^{k}-a})=m_{j}(1\cdots 1\oplus(Q_{k}-I_{a}))=(2^{k}-a)-(2^{k-1}-m_{j}(I_{a}))=(2^{k-1}-a)+m_{j}(I_{a}).

This gives the conclusion for the first row of the m-table. The entry on the
j
th column and second row of the m-table is

(2^{k}-a)-m_{j}(I_{2^{k}-a})=2^{k-1}-m_{j}(I_{a})=(2^{k-1}-a)+(a-m_{j}(I_{a})),

and
a-m_{j}(I_{a})
is the corresponding entry in the m-table for
I_{a}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In particular, the above lemma gives that
m_{j}(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b})=(2^{n}-a-b)+m_{j}(I_{a+b})
for each
j
.

We next claim that for
j\geq 1
,

m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{2^{n}-a}))+m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{2^{n}-b}))=m_{j}(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b}).

Since
I_{a+b}\subseteq Q_{n}
,
m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=m_{0}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
. By the remark at the end of section 2, we can treat
g_{1},g_{2}
as automorphism of
Q_{n}
. In particular, we can without loss of generality give the label
0
to the entry on the first row and first column. Then all non-zero labels are assigned to the last
n
columns. By the preceding lemma,

m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{2^{n}-a}))=(2^{n-1}-a)+m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a})),

m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{2^{n}-b}))=(2^{n-1}-b)+m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b})).

By the previous paragraph,
m_{j}(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b})=(2^{n}-a-b)+m_{j}(I_{a+b})
for each
j
, so the claim immediately follows.

Now we construct automorphisms
h_{1},h_{2}\in\text{Aut}(Q_{n+1})
that makes
I_{2^{n}-a}
and
I_{2^{n}-b}
fit by describing its labeling on
m
-table. First, give labels
2,3,\ldots,n
as in
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, which ensures that
m_{j}(g^{\prime}_{1}(I_{2^{n}-a}))+m_{j}(g^{\prime}_{2}(I_{2^{n}-b}))=m(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b})
. Then, consider the columns with label
0
and
1
in the original
g_{1}
-labeled and
g_{2}
-labeled m-table. Suppose such columns are column
0
and
i
in the
m
-table of
I_{a}
and column
0
and
j
in the
m
-table of
I_{b}
. Then the
m
-tables are:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&m_{i}(I_{a})\\
a&a-m_{i}(I_{a})\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&m_{j}(I_{b})\\
b&b-m_{j}(I_{b})\end{array}\right).

By our assumption,
m_{1}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=0
, so the labeled entry in the second column is
0
, which must be on the first row. Thus,
m_{i}(I_{a})=0
. By the lemma above, the unlabeled parts of the
m
-table of
I_{2^{n}-a}
and
I_{2^{n}-b}
are:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&2^{n-1}-a\\
2^{n}-a&2^{n-1}\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&2^{n-1}-b+m_{j}(I_{b})\\
2^{n}-b&2^{n-1}-m_{j}(I_{b})\end{array}\right).

We know that
m_{0}(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b})=2^{n}-a-b
and
m_{1}(I_{2^{n+1}-a-b})=2^{n-1}
. Thus, label
1
must be given to the entries
2^{n-1}
and
0
.

Observe that
m_{i}(I_{a})=0
is labeled
1
in the original labeling. Since
m_{i}(I_{a+b})=a+b-2^{n-1}
, the entry in the
m
-table of
I_{b}
that is given the same label
must take the value
a+b-2^{n-1}
. The entry in the same column but different row is then
b-(a+b-2^{n-1})=2^{n-1}-a
. By the lemma, the corresponding entry in the
m
-table of
I_{2^{n}-b}
is
(2^{n-1}-b)+(2^{n-1}-a)=2^{n}-a-b
, which is precisely
m_{0}(I_{2^{n}-a-b})
. Thus, label
0
must be given to the entries
0
and
2^{n}-a-b
. This finishes the case where
a^{\prime}=0
or
b^{\prime}=0
.

Now suppose
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then, treating
I_{a},I_{b},I_{a+b}
as subsets of
Q_{n}
and
g_{1},g_{2}
as automorphism of
Q_{n}
, we can apply Lemma
3.7
and see that there is a column
j
such that either
m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=a
and
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
or
m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=0
and
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=b
. Without loss of generality, assume that the former is true. Then
g_{1}(I_{a})\subseteq F_{j,1}
and
g_{2}(I_{b})\subseteq F_{j,0}
.

Observe that if
g_{1}(I_{a})\subseteq F_{j,1}
, then
g(I_{a})\cup F_{j,0}
is isomorphic to
I_{2^{n}+a}
. Fix
h\in\text{Aut}(Q_{n+1})
such that
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup F_{j,0}=h(I_{2^{n}+a})
. Since
g_{2}(I_{b})\subseteq F_{j,0}
,
F_{j,0}-g_{2}(I_{b})
is an initial segment. Let
F_{j,0}-g_{2}(I_{b})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})
. Then

h(I_{2^{n}+a})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})\cup g_{1}(I_{a})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})\cup I_{a+b}

which means that
(2^{n}-b,a+b)
fits. Since
(2^{n}-b)+(a+b)+(2^{n}-a)=2^{n+1}
,
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
also fits.

To prove the converse, it is equivalent to prove the following statement: Suppose
2^{n-1}\leq a,b\leq 2^{n}
, and
2^{n}\leq a+b\leq 2^{n}+2^{n-1}
. If
(a,b)
fits, then
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
fits.

Suppose
a^{\prime}=m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{a}))
and
b^{\prime}=m_{0}(g_{2}(I_{b}))
. Consider the case when
a^{\prime}=0
or
b^{\prime}=0
, and without loss of generality, assume that
a^{\prime}=0
. Then
g_{1}(I_{a})\subseteq F_{0,0}
, so

g_{2}(I_{b})=(F_{0,0}-g_{1}(I_{a}))\cup(I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1})

Note that
F_{0,0}-g_{1}(I_{a})
is an initial segment of length
2^{n}-a
, and
I_{a+b}\cap F_{0,1}
is an initial segment of length
a+b-2^{n}
, so
(2^{n}-a,a+b-2^{n})
fits. But
(2^{n}-a)+(a+b-2^{n})+(2^{n}-b)=2^{n}
, so
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
also fits.

Lastly, consider the case when
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then, as above, we can assume without loss of generality that there is a column
j
such that
m_{j}(g_{1}(I_{a}))=a
and
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
. In the same way, there is
h\in\text{Aut}(Q_{n+1})
such that
g_{1}(I_{a})\cup F_{j,0}=h(I_{2^{n}+a})
. Now since
m_{j}(g_{2}(I_{b}))=0
,
g_{2}(I_{b})\subseteq F_{j,0}
, and
F_{j,0}-g_{2}(I_{b})
is an initial segment. Let
F_{j,0}-g_{2}(I_{b})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})
. Then

h(I_{2^{n}+a})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})\cup g_{2}(I_{b})\cup g_{1}(I_{a})=h^{\prime}(I_{2^{n}-b})\cup I_{a+b}

which means that
(2^{n}-b,a+b)
fits. Since
(2^{n}-b)+(a+b)+(2^{n}-a)=2^{n+1}
,
(2^{n}-a,2^{n}-b)
also fits.
∎

4.3.
New Large Triangle

Proposition 4.4
.

The set

\{(a,b):a<2^{n},b<2^{n}-2^{n-2},a+b>2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}

consists of only unfit pairs. This counts
(4^{n-2}-3\cdot 2^{n-2}+2)/2
unfit pairs.

Proof.

Set
c=2^{n+1}-a-b
. By the bounds on
a
and
b
, it is equivalent to prove that the set

\{(b,c):2^{n-1}<b<2^{n}-2^{n-2},2^{n-2}<c<2^{n-1},b+c>2^{n}\}

consists only of unfit pairs. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
m(g_{1}(I_{b}))+m(g_{2}(I_{c}))=m(I_{b+c})
. Since
2^{n-1}<b<2^{n}-2^{n-2}
and
2^{n-2}<c<2^{n-1}
,
m_{i}(I_{b})\geq b-2^{n-1}
and
m_{i}(I_{c})\geq c-2^{n-2}
if
m_{i}(I_{b})
and
m_{i}(I_{c})
are non-zero.

Since
c<2^{n-1}
,
b+c<b+2^{n-1}
, so
b-2^{n-1}>b+c-2^{n}
. Since
b<2^{n}-2^{n-2}
,
b+c<2^{n}-2^{n-2}+c
, so
c-2^{n-2}>b+c-2^{n}
. Hence, if
m_{i}(g_{1}(I_{b}))+m_{i}(g_{2}(I_{c}))\neq 0
, then
m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{b}))+m_{0}(g_{2}(I_{c}))\geq\min\{b-2^{n-1},c-2^{n-2}\}>b+c-2^{n}=m_{0}(I_{b+c})
. So
m_{0}(g_{1}(I_{c}))=m_{0}(g_{2}(I_{c}))=m_{0}(I_{b+c})=0
. But since
b+c>2^{n}
,
m_{0}(I_{b+c})\neq 0
, contradiction.
∎

4.4.
Remaining Cases

We need to count the collection of squares at the bottom right of Figure
2
that constitute the remaining unfit pairs. First, we prove some basic lemmas about the m-vectors. As usual, we assume that every subset of hypercubes lives in
Q_{n+1}
so that the m-vector consists of
(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

(1)

Let
a\leq 2^{n}
. Then

m_{j}(I_{2a})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}2m_{j+1}(I_{a})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-1\\
a&\mbox{if}&j=n\end{array}\right.

(2)

Let
a<2^{n}
. Then

m_{j}(I_{2a+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j+1}(I_{a})+m_{j+1}(I_{a+1})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-1\\
a&\mbox{if}&j=n\end{array}\right.

Proof.

For (1), let
E\subseteq I_{2a}
consist of all the even vertices (i.e., vertices that represent an even integer), and
O\subseteq I_{2a}
consist of all the odd vertices. The claim follows by noting that for each
j<n
,
m_{j}(E)=m_{j}(O)=m_{j+1}(I_{a})
, and when
j=n
,
m_{j}(I_{2a}=a
. For (2), we again write
E,O\subseteq I_{2a+1}
as the collection of even and odd vertices, respectively. Then we have
m_{j}(E)=m_{j+1}(I_{a+1})
and
m_{j}(O)=m_{j+1}(I_{a})
for
j<n
. For
j=n
,
m_{j}(I_{2a+1})=\lfloor(2a+1)/2\rfloor=a
.
∎

A quick examination of the
m
-tables gives the following corollary:

Corollary 4.6
.

(a,a),(a,a+1),(a,a-1)
fits. Furthermore, if
(a,b)
fits, then
(2a,2b)
fits.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
v_{2}(a)
as the largest
j
such that
2^{j}
divides
a
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and
a,b\leq 2^{n}
. If

|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}},

then
(a,b)
fits.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
a\geq b
. By the previous corollary, the case
|a-b|\leq 1
is proved to be fit. Hence, we can assume that
a-b\geq 2
.

Case 1:

a-b\leq 2^{v_{2}(a)}
.

We’ll prove that in this case,

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a})+m_{j}(I_{b})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(a)\\
b&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(a)-1\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a})+m_{j+1}(I_{b})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(a)-2\end{array}\right.

The case when
n=2
can be readily verified. Suppose for induction that the above formula is true for
a,b\leq 2^{n-1}
. Now let
a,b\leq 2^{n}
. If
v_{2}(a)=0
, then
a-b\leq 1
, but we’ve assumed that
a-b\geq 2
. Thus,
v_{2}(a)\geq 1
. If
v_{2}(b)\geq 1
as well, then
a/2
and
b/2
are integers at most
2^{n-1}
, and

\frac{a}{2}-\frac{b}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(a-b)\leq\frac{1}{2}2^{v_{2}(a)}=2^{v_{2}(a/2)}

By inductive hypothesis,
(a/2,b/2)
satiesfies the above equation. Combining this with Lemma
4.5
(1) (multiply everything by
2
) shows that the above equation also holds for
(a,b)
. Thus, we assume that
v_{2}(b)=0
, i.e.,
b
is odd.

Let
a^{\prime}=a/2
,
b^{\prime}=(b-1)/2
. Since
a-2^{v_{2}(a)}
is even and
b
is odd,
a-b\leq 2^{v_{2}(a)}\Longleftrightarrow b\geq a-2^{v_{2}(a)}
implies that
b-1\geq a-2^{v_{2}(a)}
. Thus,

b^{\prime}\geq a^{\prime}-2^{v_{2}(a)-1}=a^{\prime}-2^{v_{2}(a^{\prime})}

Furthermore, since
a-b\geq 2
,
b+1<a
, which means that
a^{\prime}>b^{\prime}+1
. Moreover,

b^{\prime}+1=\frac{b}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\geq\frac{a}{2}-2^{v_{2}(a)-1}+\frac{1}{2}>a^{\prime}-2^{v_{2}(a^{\prime})}

By the inductive hypothesis and the inequalities above, and by the fact that
v_{2}(a^{\prime})=v_{2}(a)-1
, we see that

m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(a)+1\\
b^{\prime}&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(a)\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(a)-1\end{array}\right.

m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j}(I_{b^{\prime}+1})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(a)+1\\
b^{\prime}+1&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(a)\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}+1})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(a)-1\end{array}\right.

By Lemma
4.5
, we also have

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=m_{j}(I_{2(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-1\\
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}&\mbox{if}&j=n\end{array}\right.

Thus, when
j=n
,
m_{j}(I_{a+b})=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}=m_{j}(I_{a})+m_{j}(I_{b})
. When
n-v_{2}(a)\leq j<n
(equivalently,
n-v_{2}(a)+1\leq j+1\leq n
),

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})=m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}+1})

=m_{j}(I_{a})+m_{j}(I_{b})

When
j=n-v_{2}(a)-1
(equivalently,
j+1=n-v_{2}(a)
,

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})=b^{\prime}+(b^{\prime}+1)=2b^{\prime}+1=b

When
j\leq n-v_{2}(a)-2
(equivalently,
j+1\leq n-v_{2}(a)-1
),

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})=m_{j+2}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+2}(I_{b^{\prime}})+m_{j+2}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+2}(I_{b^{\prime}+1})

=m_{j+1}(I_{a})+m_{j+1}(I_{b})

This completes the inductive step and proves the result for Case 1.

Case 2:

a-b\leq 2^{v_{2}(b)}
.

We’ll prove that in this case,

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a})+m_{j}(I_{b})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(b)\\
b&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(b)-1\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a})+m_{j+1}(I_{b})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(b)-2\end{array}\right.

The case when
n=2
can be readily verified. Suppose for induction that the above formula is true for
a,b\leq 2^{n-1}
. Now let
a,b\leq 2^{n}
. By the same reasoning, we can assume that
v_{2}(b)\geq 1
and
v_{2}(a)=0
.

Let
a^{\prime}=(a+1)/2
and
b^{\prime}=b/2
. Since
b-2^{v_{2}(b)}
is even and
a
is odd,
a-b\leq 2^{v_{2}(b)}\Longleftrightarrow a\leq b+2^{v_{2}(b)}
implies that
a+1\leq b+2^{v_{2}(b)}
. Thus,

a^{\prime}\geq b^{\prime}-2^{v_{2}(b)-1}=a^{\prime}+2^{v_{2}(b^{\prime})}

By
a-b\geq 2
, we again have
a^{\prime}>b^{\prime}+1
. Moreover,

a^{\prime}-1=\frac{a}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{b}{2}+2^{v_{2}(b)-1}-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}+2^{v_{2}(b^{\prime})}

By the inductive hypothesis and the inequalities above, and by the fact that
v_{2}(a^{\prime})=v_{2}(a)-1
, we see that

m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(b)+1\\
b^{\prime}&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(b)\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(b)-1\end{array}\right.

m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j}(I_{a^{\prime}-1})+m_{j}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\geq n-v_{2}(b)+1\\
b^{\prime}+1&\mbox{if}&j=n-v_{2}(b)\\
m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}-1})+m_{j+1}(I_{b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-v_{2}(b)-1\end{array}\right.

By Lemma
4.5
, we also have

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=m_{j}(I_{2((a^{\prime}-1)+b^{\prime})+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}-1})+m_{j+1}(I_{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}})&\mbox{if}&j\leq n-1\\
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}-1&\mbox{if}&j=n\end{array}\right.

The rest of the proof follows by the same reasoning as Case 1.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

If
v_{2}(a)<v_{2}(b)
, then
v_{2}(a+b)=v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)
. If
a>b
, then
v_{2}(a-b)=v_{2}(a)
.

Proof.

Since
2^{v_{2}(a)}
divides
a
and
b
,
2^{v_{2}(a)}
divides
a+b
. Since
v_{2}(a)<v_{2}(b)
,
2^{v_{2}(a)+1}
divides
b
. If
2^{v_{2}(a)+1}
also divides
a+b
, then
2^{v_{2}(a)+1}
would divide
a
, which contradicts the fact that
2^{v_{2}(a)}
is the largest power of
2
that divides
a
. Hence,
2^{v_{2}(a)+1}
does not divide
a+b
, so
v_{2}(a+b)=v_{2}(a)
. For the same reason, if
a>b
, then
v_{2}(a-b)=v_{2}(a)
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
x_{n}
be the number of pairs
(a,b)
in
S_{n}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a,b<2^{n}\}
that satisfy
|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}}
. Then

x_{n}\geq 2x_{n-1}+2^{n-1}-2.

Proof.

Let

T_{1}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a,b<2^{n}-2^{n-3}\}

T_{2}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a<2^{n}-2^{n-3},2^{n}-2^{n-3}\leq b<2^{n}\}

T_{3}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-3}\leq a<2^{n},2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq b<2^{n}-2^{n-3}\}

T_{4}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-3}\leq a,b<2^{n}\}

Note that
S_{n}
is the disjoint union of
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3},T_{4}
, and
|S_{n-1}|=|T_{1}|=|T_{2}|=|T_{3}|=|T_{4}|
.

We first show that the number of pairs that satisfy the inequality in
T_{1}
and
T_{4}
is at least
x_{n-1}
. If
a=2^{n}-2^{n-2}
, then
v_{2}(a)=n-2
. If
2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq b<2^{n}-2^{n-3}
, then
|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}}
. Similarly, if
b=2^{n}-2^{n-2}
and
2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a<2^{n}-2^{n-3}
, then
|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}}
. Now if
2^{n}-2^{n-2}<a,b<2^{n}-2^{n-3}
, then
v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)<n-3=v_{2}(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3})
, so by the previous lemma,
v_{2}(a)=v_{2}(a-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3})),v_{2}(b)=v_{2}(b-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}))
. By our bounds on
a
and
b
,
(a-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}),b-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}))\in S_{n-1}
, so if
|(a-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}))-(b-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}))|=|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3})),v_{2}(b-(2^{n-1}-2^{n-3}))}
, then
|a-b|\leq 2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}}
. Hence, the number of pairs in
T_{1}
that satisfy the inequality is at least those in
S_{n-1}
, which is
x_{n-1}
. Similarly, such number in
T_{4}
is at least
x_{n-1}
.

Next, note that in
T_{2}
, the pairs
(2^{n}-2^{n-2},b)
and
(a,2^{n}-2^{n-3})
satisfies the inequality; in
T_{3}
, the pairs
(2^{n}-2^{n-3},b)
and
(a,2^{n}-2^{n-2})
satisfies the inequality. These count
2^{n-1}-2
number of pairs. Hence
x_{n}\geq 2x_{n-1}+2^{n-1}-2
.
∎

Next, we’ll give a lower bound on the number of unfit pairs.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let

S^{\prime}_{n}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}<a,b<2^{n},|a-b|\geq 2\}.

If
(a,b)\in S^{\prime}_{n}
and
(a,b)
fits, then there is an index
j
such that

m_{j}(I_{a+b})=\min\{a,b\}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
a>b
, so that
a-b\geq 2
. By our bounds on
(a,b)\in S^{\prime}_{n}
, the m-table of
I_{a}
and
I_{b}
are

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&*&\cdots\\
a&*&\cdots\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&b-2^{n-1}&\cdots\\
b&2^{n-1}&\cdots\end{array}\right).

Since

\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\rfloor<\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\rfloor+1=\lfloor\frac{a+b+2}{2}\rfloor\leq\lfloor\frac{a+a}{2}\rfloor=a,

we have that
m_{j}(I_{a+b})\leq\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\rfloor<a
for each
j
. Hence,
0
is the labeled entry in column 0 of the m-table of
I_{a}
. Moreover, by our bounds on
a
and
b
,

m_{j}(I_{a+b})\geq m_{0}(I_{a+b})=a+b-2^{n}>2\cdot(2^{n}-2^{n-2})-2^{n}=2^{n-1}

Thus, the entry in the m-table of
I_{b}
that pairs with
0
can only be
b
. If this label is
j
, then
m_{j}(I_{a+b})=b
.
∎

Definition 4.12
.

If
m_{j}(I_{a+b})\neq\min\{a,b\}
for each index
j
, then call the pair
(a,b)
to be column-0 unfit.

By the above lemma, if a pair
(a,b)\in S^{\prime}_{n}

(a,b)\in S^{\prime}_{n}

\min\{a,b\}
lies between
m_{j}(I_{a+b})
and
m_{j+1}(I_{a+b})
for some index

. Since

m_{n}(I_{a+b})=\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\rfloor>\lfloor\frac{a-2+b}{2}\rfloor\geq\lfloor\frac{b+b}{2}\rfloor=b.

Thus,
b

m_{0}(I_{a+b})
and
m_{n}(I_{a+b})

Now we construct a sequence of sets

Proposition 4.13
.

For each
n\geq 4
, every element in

is column-0 unfit.

Proof.

We prove by induction. When
n=4
, it can be quickly verified by writing out the m-table that
(13,15)
and
(15,13)
is column-0 unfit. Now assume that every element in
A_{k-1}
is column-0 unfit,

. We’ll prove the same for
A_{k}
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,0,1\}
,
(a_{0},b_{0})\in A_{k-1}

a_{0}>b_{0}
, so that
a_{0}-b_{0}\geq 2
. By the remark above and the assumption that
(a_{0},b_{0})
is column-0 unfit, we can find
j

m_{j}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})<b_{0}<m_{j+1}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})

2m_{j}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\leq 2b_{0}-2

2m_{j+1}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\geq 2b_{0}+2

We claim that
m_{j-1}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}+\alpha+\beta})<2b_{0}+\beta<m_{j}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}+\alpha+\beta})

m_{j-1}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}+\alpha+\beta})\leq m_{j-1}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}})+\max\{\alpha+\beta,0\}=2m_{j}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})+\max\{\alpha+\beta,0\}\leq 2b_{0}-2+\max\{\alpha+\beta,0\},

m_{j}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}+\alpha+\beta})\geq m_{j}(I_{2a_{0}+2b_{0}})+\min\{\alpha+\beta,0\}=2m_{j+1}(I_{a_{0}+b_{0}})+\min\{\alpha+\beta,0\}\geq 2b_{0}+2+\min\{\alpha+\beta,0\}.

The claim immediately follows from the observation that

. Moreover, by lemma
2.6
,
(2a_{0}+\alpha,2b_{0}+\beta)

We’re left to prove that

Definition 4.14
.

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. If

B\cap\bigcup_{(a,b)\in A}\{a-1,a,a+1\}\times\{b-1,b,b+1\}=\emptyset

or

A\cap\bigcup_{(c,d)\in B}\{c-1,c,c+1\}\times\{d-1,d,d+1\}=\emptyset

Then
A
and
B
are said to be separated.

It is easy to see that the two conditions are equivalent.

Lemma 4.15
.

If
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are separated, then

A^{\prime}=\bigcup_{(a,b)\in A}\{2a-1,2a,2a+1\}\times\{2b-1,2b,2b+1\}

and

B^{\prime}=\bigcup_{(c,d)\in B}\{2c-1,2c,2c+1\}\times\{2d-1,2d,2d+1\}

are separated.

Proof.

Suppose not. Let
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in A^{\prime}
and
(a^{\prime}+\alpha,b^{\prime}+\beta)=(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in B^{\prime}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,0,1\}
. By definition, write
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(2a+\alpha_{1},2b+\beta_{1})
and
(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})=(2c+\alpha_{2},2d+\beta_{2})
, where
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Then
(2a+\alpha_{1}+\alpha,2b+\beta_{1}+\beta)=(2c+\alpha_{2},2d+\beta_{2})
, which implies that

(2a+\alpha+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},2b+\beta+\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})=(2c+2d).

Modding out by 2, we see that
\alpha+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\beta+\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\equiv 0\mod 2
, so they must be
-2,0
or
2
. Let
\alpha_{0}=(\alpha+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})/2
and
\beta_{0}=(\beta+\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})/2
. Then
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\{-1,0,1\}
, and
(a+\alpha_{0},b+\beta_{0})=(c,d)
by the above equality. This contradicts the assumption that
A,B
are separated.
∎

Proposition 4.16
.

|A_{n}|=2|A_{n-1}|+2^{2n-5}-2^{n-1}+2
. Thus, if
y_{n}
be the number of unfit pairs
(a,b)
in
S_{n}=\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a,b<2^{n}\}
, then

y_{n}\geq 2y_{n-1}+2^{2n-5}-2^{n-1}+2.

We claim that
A_{n}
consists of
2^{n-2-j}
copies of
2^{j}-1
by
2^{j}-1
square for all

that satisfies

. Moreover, each square is separated from all other squares.

The case
n=4
can be easily checked. Suppose the case

is known, where
k>4
, and we wish to prove the same for
A_{k}
. First, if
(a,a-2)\in A_{k}
and

, it can be checked directly that, if

is any one of the surrounding pair,

, which implies that

is separated from all other elements of

. This accounts for the

copies of
1
by
1

Suppose

are the squares in

B^{\prime}_{1},B^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{N}
are the remaining squares in

, where

B^{\prime}_{i}=\bigcup_{(a,b)\in A_{i}}\{2a-1,2a,2a+1\}\times\{2b-1,2b,2b+1\}

If

∎

Proposition 4.17
.

The number of unfit pairs in the set
\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a,b<2^{n}\}
is

4^{n-2}-(n-2)2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}-2.

And they are precisely the set

\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}<b<a<2^{n},|a-b|>2^{\max\{v_{2}(a),v_{2}(b)\}}\}

By our numerical results,
x_{4}=14
and

, so
x_{4}+y_{4}=4^{2}
. We shall prove by induction that

5.
Number of Unfit Pairs

In this section, we’ll derive a closed formula for the number of unfit pairs
(a,b)
with
(a,b)\leq 2^{n}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
t_{n}
be the number of unfit pairs with
(a,b)\leq 2^{n}
. Then
t_{4}=24
and
t_{n}
satisfies the following recurrence for
n\geq 5
:

t_{n}=2t_{n-1}+\sum_{j=4}^{n-2}2^{n-2-j}t_{j}+6\left(4^{n-2}-(n-1)2^{n-2}\right).

Proof.

Let
u_{n}
denote the number of unfit pairs in the upper half
\{(a,b):a,b>0,a+b\leq 2^{n}\},
and let
l_{n}
denote the number of unfit pairs in the lower half
\{(a,b):a,b\leq 2^{n},a+b>2^{n}\}
. Clearly,
t_{n}=u_{n}+l_{n}
, and
l_{n}
is also equal to the number of unfit pairs in
\{(a,b):a,b<2^{n},a+b>2^{n}\}
or in
\{(a,b):a,b\leq 2^{n},a+b\geq 2^{n}\}
, since
(2^{n},b),(a,2^{n}),
and
(a,2^{n}-a)
are all fit pairs for each
0<a,b\leq 2^{n}
.

We begin by establishing a recurrence for
u_{n}
. Observe that
u_{n}
is the disjoint union of

\{(a,b):0<a,b\leq 2^{n-1}\}\cup\{(a,b):a>2^{n-1},b>0,a+b\leq 2^{n}\}\cup\{(a,b):a>0,b>2^{n-1},a+b\leq 2^{n}\}.

By Proposition
4.1
, the number of unfit pairs in each of the latter two sets is
u_{n-1}
. By definition, the number of unfit pairs in the first set is
t_{n-1}
. Thus,

u_{n}=t_{n-1}+2u_{n-1}

Now we count the unfit pairs given in section 4.3 and 4.4. Observe that the set
S_{1}=\{(a,b):a,b<2^{n},a+b>2^{n}-2^{n-1}\}
is the disjoint union of

\{(a,b):a<2^{n},b<2^{n}-2^{n-2},a+b>2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}\cup\{(a,b):a<2^{n}-2^{n-2},b<2^{n},a+b>2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}

\cup\{(a,b):2^{n}-2^{n-2}\leq a,b<2^{n}\}.

By Proposition
4.4
, the first two set each counts
(4^{n-2}-3\cdot 2^{n-2}+2)/2
unfit pairs, and by Proposition
4.17
, the last set counts
4^{n-2}-(n-2)2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}-2
unfit pairs. Thus, the number of unfit pairs in
S_{1}
is

(4^{n-2}-(n-2)2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}-2)+(4^{n-2}-3\cdot 2^{n-2}+2)=2\cdot 4^{n-2}-(n-1)2^{n-1}.

Next, consider the mapping
(a,b)\rightarrow(a,2^{n+1}-a-b)
, which maps fit pairs to fit pairs and unfit pairs to unfit pairs. The image
S_{2}=\{(a,b):a<2^{n},b<2^{n-1},a+b>2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}
. Consider also
S_{3}=\{(a,b):a<2^{n-1},b<2^{n},a+b>2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}
, which is the mirror symmetry of
S_{2}
. Note that
S_{1},S_{2},
and
S_{3}
are pairwise disjoint and contained in the lower half, and they have the same number of unfit pairs. The subset of the lower half that has not been counted is the set

\{(a,b):a\geq 2^{n-1},b\geq 2^{n-1},a+b\leq 2^{n}+2^{n-1}\}.

But by Proposition
4.2
, the number of unfit pairs in this set is precisely
l_{n-1}
. Putting these all together, we have

t_{n}=u_{n}+l_{n}=t_{n-1}+2u_{n-1}+l_{n-1}+3(2\cdot 4^{n-2}-(n-1)2^{n-1})=2t_{n-1}+u_{n-1}+6(4^{n-2}-(n-1)2^{n-2}).

The recurrence follows immediately by expanding
u_{n-1}
recursively, terminating at
u_{4}=0
.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For all
n\geq 4
,

t_{n}=4^{n}-\binom{4}{3}3^{n}+\binom{4}{2}2^{n}-\binom{4}{1},

where
t_{n}
is the number of unfit pairs
(a,b)
with
0<a,b\leq 2^{n}
as above.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
s_{4}=t_{4}=24
. For the rest of the sequence, we induct on
n
. Assume that for for an arbitrary
k\geq 4
, the claim holds for all
4\leq j\leq k
, i.e.

t_{j}=4^{j}-\binom{4}{3}3^{j}+\binom{4}{2}2^{j}-\binom{4}{1}.

Then we have

\displaystyle t_{k+1}

\displaystyle=2t_{k}+\sum_{j=4}^{k-1}2^{k-1-j}t_{j}+6\left(4^{k-1}-k\cdot 2^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(4^{k}-\binom{4}{3}3^{k}+\binom{4}{2}2^{k}-\binom{4}{1}\right)+\sum_{j=4}^{k-1}2^{k-1-j}\left(4^{j}-\binom{4}{3}3^{j}+\binom{4}{2}2^{j}-\binom{4}{1}\right)+6\left(4^{k-1}-k\cdot 2^{k-1}\right)

The sum
t_{k+1}
above can be decomposed into four parts
S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}
as follow:

S_{1}=2\cdot 4^{k}+\sum_{j=4}^{k-1}2^{k-1-j}\cdot 4^{j}+6\cdot 4^{k-1}=4^{k+1}-2^{k+3};

h ​ p {hp} -a posteriori error estimates for hybrid high-order methods applied to biharmonic problems
Zhaonan Dong, Alexandre Ern, Tanvi Wadhawan

Abstract
We derive a residual-based h ​ p hp –a posteriori error estimator for hybrid high-order (HHO) methods on simplicial meshes applied to the biharmonic problem posed on two- and three-dimensional polytopal Lipschitz domains. The a posteriori error estimator hinges on an error decomposition into conforming and nonconforming components. To bound the nonconforming error, we use a C 1 C^{1} -partition of unity constructed via Alfeld splittings, combined with local Helmholtz decompositions on vertex stars. For the conforming error, we design two residual-based estimators, each associated with a specific interpolation operator. In the first setting, the upper bound for the conforming error involves only the stabilization term and the data oscillation. In the second setting, the bound additionally incorporates bulk residuals, normal flux jumps, and tangential jumps. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical findings and demonstrate the efficiency of the proposed estimators.

Introduction

Fourth-order problems are encountered in many engineering applications, including optimal control problems, micro-electro mechanical systems, thin plate elasticity, and hyperviscous effects in fluid models. A prime example of a fourth-order problem is the biharmonic problem with so-called clamped boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u=f\penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,

(1a)

\displaystyle u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}{\cdot}\nabla u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega,

(1c)

where,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\penalty\ d\in\{2,3\}
, is a bounded polytopal connected domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\boldsymbol{n}
denotes the unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
, and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The hybrid high-order method (HHO) is a discretization technique that is applicable to a wide range of partial differential equations. It was initially developed for linear diffusion
[
13
]
and elasticity
[
12
]
. HHO methods are formulated using broken polynomial spaces on the mesh cells and the mesh faces. The two essential components in designing HHO methods are a local reconstruction operator and a local stabilization operator, both defined on each mesh cell. HHO methods offer several appealing features, including support for polytopal meshes, optimal error estimates, local conservation properties, and enhanced computational efficiency due to compact stencils and the local elimination of cell unknowns via static condensation. HHO methods exhibit close connections to hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) and weak Galerkin (WG) methods
[
10
]
. These connections have been exploited to establish unified convergence analyses for the biharmonic problem and the acoustic wave equation
[
14
,
21
]
. Links between HHO and the nonconforming virtual element method (ncVEM) are discussed in
[
10
,
27
,
9
]
.

A posteriori error estimates and adaptive strategies for standard Galerkin methods applied to fourth-order problems have been the subject of extensive research over the past two decades. Early contributions include conforming finite element approximations
[
31
]
and Morley plate elements
[
11
]
.
C^{0}
-interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) methods were analyzed in
[
5
]
in the quadratic case and in
[
22
]
for general order in two space dimensions. Other notable contributions include continuous and discontinuous Galerkin methods for the Kirchhoff–Love plate
[
25
]
and the Ciarlet–Raviart formulation of the biharmonic problem
[
7
]
. A posteriori error estimates for several lowest-order nonconforming finite element methods applied to biharmonic problems in two and three dimensions are established in
[
6
]
. HHO methods were addressed in
[
28
]
, where a posteriori error estimates were derived for a special HHO method employing additional one-dimensional edge unknowns for biharmonic problems in three space dimensions. Therein, the tool to control the nonconforming error is an averaging operator which maps discontinuous or continuous piecewise polynomial functions to a
C^{1}
-piecewise polynomial space. This allows to bound the nonconforming error by the jumps of the underlying functions in an
h
-optimal way, but
p

The first
hp
–a posteriori error estimate for Galerkin methods applied to biharmonic PDEs was established in
[
17
]
. In that work, an
hp
–a posteriori error estimate is derived using a novel approach in which the nonconforming error is controlled via a global (non-polynomial)
H^{2}
-potential reconstruction function, rather than a nodal averaging operator, on two- and three-dimensional, simply connected, Lipschitz domains. The resulting a posteriori error bound is
h

p
-suboptimal by
\frac{3}{2}
-order, which matches the known
p
-suboptimality for the IPDG method. We observe that invoking a global Helmholtz decomposition leads to a constant in the error bound that deteriorates with the number of holes in the domain
\Omega
, as observed in another context in
[
4
]
. Another recent work
[
8
]
studied the biharmonic problem on a simply connected planar domain and analyzed a symmetric IPDG method. By exploiting the “div–div” complex, the authors defined a polynomial lifting operator mapping the nonconforming error to a high-order
{\rm\boldsymbol{H}(divdiv)}
-conforming finite element space, resulting in an error estimator that does not contain the stabilization term.

In the present work, we derive residual
hp
–a posteriori error estimates for the HHO methods introduced in
[
14
]
for the biharmonic problem posed on two- and three-dimensional Lipschitz domains with general topology. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to employ a
C^{1}
-partition of unity, combined with a local Helmholtz decomposition on each vertex star to bound the nonconforming error. The
C^{1}
-partition of unity can be constructed using existing
C^{1}
-conforming finite element or composite finite element spaces, such as the Argyris or HCT elements. In this work, we employ the
C^{1}
-partition of unity introduced in
[
33
]
. We emphasize that we derive here a novel stability estimate for the local Helmholtz decomposition and that this decomposition is of broader interest, as it is applicable to many other nonconforming finite element methods, in particular to IPDG methods. The main advantage of invoking local Helmholtz decompositions rather than a global one is to avoid any dependency on the number of holes present in the domain
\Omega
.

The second main contribution of the paper is to obtain two upper bounds on the conforming error. Each upper bound is derived using a specific interpolation operator. The first approach uses the interpolation operator associated with the canonical hybrid finite element (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Section 7.6]
) whose main advantage is that its combination with the HHO reconstruction operator leads to the
H^{2}
-elliptic projection. The resulting upper bound on the conforming error is
p
-suboptimal by at most one order, but is actually expected to be only
\frac{1}{2}
-order suboptimal under a reasonable assumption on the interpolation error which we verify numerically in two and three dimensions. The remarkable fact about the resulting upper bound is that it involves only stabilization and data oscillation terms. The alternative (and somewhat more classical) approach to bound the conforming error hinges on the Babuška–Suri interpolation operator. The resulting upper bound is
p
-suboptimal by
\frac{1}{2}
-order without any assumption, but at the price of introducing additional error indicator terms, namely bulk residual and jump contributions. We emphasize that both approaches rely on interpolation operators constructed on simplicial meshes.

3.1

Let

For every mesh cell

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{k}_{T}:=\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(T)\times\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial T})\times\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}),

(9)

, and the third component

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{T}={}

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v_{T},\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{T}+(v_{T}-v_{\partial T},\partial_{n}\Delta w)_{\partial T}

\displaystyle-(\partial_{n}v_{T}-\gamma_{\partial T},\partial_{nn}w)_{\partial T}-(\partial_{t}(v_{T}-v_{\partial T}),\partial_{nt}w)_{\partial T},

(10a)

\displaystyle(R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T}),\xi)_{T}={}

\displaystyle(v_{T},\xi)_{T},

(10b)

with test functions

in (

) (one obtains

(11)

The second devising step is a local stabilization bilinear form

such that, for all

\displaystyle S_{\partial T}(\widehat{v}_{T},\widehat{w}_{T}):={}

\displaystyle(k+2)^{3}\hbar_{T}^{-3}(v_{\partial T}-v_{T},w_{\partial T}-w_{T})_{\partial T}

\displaystyle+(k+2)\hbar_{T}^{-1}(\gamma_{\partial T}-\Pi^{k}_{\partial T}(\partial_{n}v_{T}),\chi_{\partial T}-\Pi^{k}_{\partial T}(\partial_{n}w_{T}))_{\partial T},

(12a)

with the

-scaling factor

\hbar_{T}:=\frac{h_{T}}{k+2}.

(12b)

Lemma 3.1

(Useful property)
.

For all
\widehat{v}_{T}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{T}
and all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the following holds:

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T})-v_{T})\|^{2}_{T}\lesssim S_{\partial T}(\widehat{v}_{T},\widehat{v}_{T}).

(13)

Proof.

Using (
10a
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we deduce, for all
w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(T)
, that

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T})-v_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{T}\leq{}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{n}\Delta w\|_{\partial T}\|v_{T}-v_{\partial T}\|_{\partial T}+\|\partial_{nt}w\|_{\partial T}\|\partial_{t}(v_{T}-v_{\partial T})\|_{\partial T}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{nn}w\|_{\partial T}\|\Pi^{k}_{\partial T}(\partial_{n}v_{T}-\gamma_{\partial T})\|_{\partial T}.

Invoking the discrete trace inequality (
5b
) and the inverse inequality (
6
) with
p:=k+2
yields

\displaystyle\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}

\displaystyle(R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T})-v_{T})\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim\frac{(k+2)^{6}}{h^{3}_{T}}\|v_{\partial T}-v_{T}\|^{2}_{\partial T}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{h_{T}}\|\gamma_{\partial T}-\Pi^{k}_{\partial T}(\partial_{n}v_{T})\|^{2}_{\partial T}.

Recalling (
12
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2

(
d=2
)
.

We point out that, for
d=2
, it is possible to define the HHO method with the second component
v_{\partial T}\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial T})
instead of
\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial T})
. This is the HHO(A) method defined in
[

]
. The present analysis also applies to this variant; see Remark
5.7
.

3.2
Discrete problem

The global HHO space is defined as

\widehat{V}^{k}_{h}:=\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(\mathcal{F}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{h}).

(14)

A generic element
\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h}
is denoted as
\widehat{v}_{h}:=\big(v_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},v_{\mathcal{F}_{h}},\gamma_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}\big)
with
v_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}:=(v_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}
,
v_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}:=(v_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}
, and
\gamma_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}:=(\gamma_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}
. For every mesh cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},
the local components of
\widehat{v}_{h}
are

\widehat{v}_{T}:=\big(v_{T},v_{\partial T}:=(v_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}},\gamma_{\partial T}:=((\boldsymbol{n}_{F}{\cdot}\boldsymbol{n}_{T})\gamma_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}}\big)\in\widehat{V}_{T}^{k}.

(15)

Notice that the localization of the third component takes into account the relative orientation of
F
and
T
through the factor
{\boldsymbol{n}}_{F}{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{n}}_{T}=\pm 1
.
Homogeneous boundary conditions are enforced strongly by considering the subspace

\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}:=\big\{\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h}\;|\;v_{F}\equiv 0,\;\gamma_{F}\equiv{0},\;\forall F\in\mathcal{F}^{b}_{h}\big\}.

(16)

The discrete HHO biharmonic problem reads as follows: Find
\widehat{u}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}
such that

a_{h}(\widehat{u}_{h},\widehat{w}_{h})=(f,w_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\qquad\forall\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0},

(17)

with the global discrete bilinear form
a_{h}(\widehat{v}_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{T}(\widehat{v}_{T},\widehat{w}_{T})
where

a_{T}(\widehat{v}_{T},\widehat{w}_{T}):=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{v}_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R_{T}^{k+2}(\widehat{w}_{T}))_{T}+S_{\partial T}(\widehat{v}_{T},\widehat{w}_{T}).

(18)

As shown in
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
, the discrete problem (
17
) is well-posed. Moreover, (
17
) is amenable to static condensation, i.e., the cell unknowns can be locally eliminated within each mesh cell, yielding a global linear system coupling only the face unknowns in
\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(\mathcal{F}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{h})
.

3.3
Local interpolation operators

In this section, we consider two interpolation (reduction) operators that can be used to map the exact solution to the HHO space.
The first operator can be defined locally using the canonical hybrid finite element interpolation operator (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Section 7.6]
). Its construction is dimension-dependent. Its main advantage is that it enjoys a remarkable property when composed with the HHO reconstruction operator (see Lemma
3.3
below). The downside is that its
hp
-approximation properties have not yet been studied analytically. In what follows, we invoke (
22
) below, which we state as an assumption for which we provide numerical verifications for
d\in\{2,3\}
in Section
6.1
.
The second interpolation operator is defined globally using the modified Babuška–Suri
hp
-interpolation operator. Its needed
hp
-approximation properties are available (see Corollary
2.4
), but its use in the a posteriori error analysis leads to additional terms in the upper bound because this interpolation operator does not combine with the HHO reconstruction operator as nicely as the first interpolation operator.

Let us now give some details.
On every mesh cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the local
interpolation operator associated with the canonical hybrid finite element,

\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}:H^{2}(T)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(T)
is specified by prescribing its degrees of freedom (assuming
d=3
and recalling that
k\geq 0
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v)(\boldsymbol{a})

\displaystyle=v(\boldsymbol{a}),

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{T},

(19a)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v),\xi_{E})_{E}

\displaystyle=(v,\xi_{E})_{E},

\displaystyle\forall\xi_{E}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(E),\forall E\in\mathcal{E}_{T},

(19b)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v),\xi_{F})_{F}

\displaystyle=(v,\xi_{F})_{F},

\displaystyle\forall\xi_{F}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(F),\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T},\;k\geq 1,

(19c)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v),\xi_{T})_{T}

\displaystyle=(v,\xi_{T})_{T},

\displaystyle\forall\xi_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-2}(T),\;k\geq 2,

(19d)

where
\mathcal{V}_{T}
,
\mathcal{E}_{T}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}
collect the vertices, edges, and faces of
T
, respectively. For
d=2
, the operator
\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}
is defined in an analogous way by employing (

), (

), and (

) with

,

.
The operator
\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}
can be used to define the interpolation (reduction) operator
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{T}^{k}:H^{2}(T)\rightarrow\widehat{V}^{k}_{T}
such that, for all
v\in H^{2}(T)
,

\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{T}^{k}(v):=\big(\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v),\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v)|_{\partial T},\Pi_{\partial T}^{k}(\boldsymbol{n}_{T}{\cdot}\nabla v)\big).

(20)

Lemma 3.3

(
H^{2}
-elliptic projection)
.

The following holds for all
v\in H^{2}(T)
and all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{T}^{k}(v)),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{T}=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{T}\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+2}(T).

(21)

Proof.

A proof can be found in
[

, Lemma 4.9]
.
∎

Since the
hp
-approximation properties of the interpolation operator
\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}
have not yet been studied analytically, we make the following assumption which is supported by numerical experiments in Subsection
6.1
.

Assumption 3.4
.

The following holds for all
v\in H^{2}(T)
and all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{n}(v-\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}(v))\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim\,\hbar_{T}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v\|_{T}^{2}.

(22)

We now introduce two global HHO interpolation (reduction) operators
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{k}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}^{k}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{h}^{k}(v)

\displaystyle:=\big(\mathcal{C}_{h}^{k+2}(v),(\mathcal{C}_{h}^{k+2}(v)|_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}},(\Pi_{F}^{k}(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}{\cdot}(\nabla v)|_{F}))_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\big),

(23a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}^{k}(v)

\displaystyle:=\big(\mathcal{I}_{{\rm mBS}}^{k+2}(v),(\mathcal{I}_{{\rm mBS}}^{k+2}(v)|_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}},(\Pi_{F}^{k}(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}{\cdot}(\nabla v)|_{F}))_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\big).

Both interpolation operators enjoy two remarkable properties: their first component sits in

and their second component is the trace on the mesh skeleton of its first component. We use the localization mechanism described in (
15
) to define the local components of these interpolation operators attached to a generic mesh cell

. This leads to the triples
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{T}^{k}(v)
(which coincides with (

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{T}^{k}(v)=\big(\mathcal{I}_{{\rm mBS}}^{k+2}(v)|_{T},(\mathcal{I}_{{\rm mBS}}^{k+2}(v)|_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}},(\Pi_{F}^{k}(\boldsymbol{n}_{T}{\cdot}(\nabla v)_{F}))_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}}\big)
.

Lemma 3.5

(Bound on stabilization)
.

(i)
Under Assumption
3.4
, the following holds for all
v\in H^{2}(T)
and all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

(24a)

(ii)
The following holds for all

and all

:

S_{\partial T}(\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{T}^{k}(v),\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{T}^{k}(v))\lesssim(k+2)\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v\|^{2}_{T}.

(24b)

Proof.

of its first component, we infer that

4
Local Helmholtz decomposition

In this section, we derive a novel Helmholtz decomposition of tensor-valued fields in
\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{L}}^{2}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
where
\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
is the patch associated with the generic mesh vertex
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
. We consider the case
d=3
(a similar, yet simpler, result is available for
d=2
).

Let
\varepsilon_{ijk}
denote the Levi-Civita symbol for all
i,j,k\in\{1{:}d\}
.
We define the skew-symmetric tensor-valued operator
\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}
such that
\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{v})_{ij}:=\varepsilon_{ijk}v_{k}
for all
\boldsymbol{v}:=(v_{k})_{k\in\{1{:}d\}}
. We also recall that the (vector-valued) curl of
\boldsymbol{v}
has components
(\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{v})_{i}:=\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial_{j}v_{k}
for all
i\in\{1{:}d\}
. Here and in what follows, we employ the usual summation convention on repeated indices. For a tensor-valued field
\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{A}}=(A_{ij})_{i,j\in\{1{:}d\}}
, its (tensor-valued) row-wise curl is defined as
(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{A}}})_{ij}:=\varepsilon_{jkl}\partial_{k}A_{il}
for all
i,j\in\{1{:}d\}
, and its (vector-valued) row-wise divergence is defined as
(\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{A}}})_{i}=\sum_{j}\partial_{j}A_{ij}
for all
i\in\{1{:}d\}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Local Helmholtz decomposition)
.

For all
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and all
\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\in\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{L}}^{2}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
,
there exist a (scalar-valued) function
\xi\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
, a (vector-valued) field
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\mathrm{div}=0;\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
(i.e.,
\nabla{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\rho}=0
in
\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}{\cdot}\boldsymbol{n}_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}=0
on
\partial\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
), and a (tensor-valued) field
\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Psi}}\in\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
, so that the following holds:

\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}=\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi+\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})+\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\Psi}}},

(25a)

with

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}+|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Psi}}|_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\lesssim\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

(25b)

Proof.

(1) Let
\xi\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
be such that

(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}},\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}).

(26)

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\leq\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

(27)

Moreover, considering arbitrary test functions
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
in (
26
) implies that

\nabla{\cdot}(\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})})=0.

(28)

Moreover, since
(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})\in\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{L}}^{2}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\frac{|\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\frac{|(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}},\nabla\boldsymbol{v})_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}\leq\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\leq 2\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}
denotes the duality pairing between
\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
and
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
and where the last bound follows from (
27
).

(2) Owing to (
28
) and since
\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
is simply connected with a connected boundary, we infer from
[
1
, Section 3]
that there exists vector field
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\mathrm{div}=0;\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
such that
\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{\rho}=-\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})}
. This identity can be rewritten as

\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(}\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}-\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi)=\boldsymbol{0}.

(29)

Moreover, we observe that

\|\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{\rho}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}=\underset{{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}}{\sup}\frac{|\langle\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{\rho},\boldsymbol{v}\rangle_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}=\underset{{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}}{\sup}\frac{|(\boldsymbol{\rho},\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{v})_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}.

Owing to
[
24
, Theorem 35 and Corollary 29]
, there exists
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
such that
\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
and
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\lesssim\|\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}=\|\boldsymbol{\rho}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

Combining the above bounds, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\rho}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\lesssim\frac{|(\boldsymbol{\rho},\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{w})_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}}{\sup}\!\!\frac{|(\boldsymbol{\rho},\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{v})_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}|}{\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla{\times}\boldsymbol{\rho}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}=\|\nabla_{\mathrm{rw}}{\cdot}{(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi-\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}})}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\leq 2\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

(30)

(3) Owing to (
29
) and invoking
[
24
, Theorem 34]
for each
row of
\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}-\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi
, we infer that there exists

\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Psi}}\in\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
so that

\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\Psi}}}=\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}-\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi,\qquad|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Psi}}|_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\lesssim\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\Sigma}}-\operatorname{\underaccent{\tilde}{\boldsymbol{{\rm sk}}}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

The above identity is nothing but (
25a
). Moreover, the above
bound combined with the triangle inequality and the bounds (
27
)
(on
\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\xi
) and (
4
) (on
\boldsymbol{\rho}
) leads to (
25b
). This completes the proof.
∎

5
hp
-a posteriori error analysis

In this section, we carry out the residual-based
hp
–a posteriori error analysis for the HHO discretization of the biharmonic problem.

Our goal is to establish an upper bound on the approximation error

e:=u-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},

(31)

which measures the difference between the exact solution of (
4
),
u
, and the cellwise component of the HHO solution of (
17
),
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
.
We will use the following local error indicators: For all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\eta_{T,{\rm sta}}:={}

\displaystyle S_{\partial T}(\widehat{u}_{T},\widehat{u}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(32a)

\displaystyle\eta_{T,{\rm res}}:={}

\displaystyle\hbar_{T}^{2}\|\Pi^{k-2}_{T}(f)-\Delta^{2}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T})\|_{T}+\hbar_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{nt}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T})]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{\partial T^{\mathrm{i}}}

\displaystyle+\hbar_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{n}\Delta R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T})]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{\partial T^{\mathrm{i}}},

(32b)

\displaystyle\eta_{T,{\rm tan}}:={}

\displaystyle\hbar_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{tt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{\partial T}+\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{nt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{\partial T}\Big\},

(32c)

recalling the convention that
\Pi^{n}_{T}(f)=0
for all
n\leq-1
, and
where jumps of normal derivatives across any mesh interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}
are understood so that the normal derivative is taken along the normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
orienting
F
on both sides of
F
. Setting
\hbar_{T}^{\prime}:=\frac{h_{T}}{\max(k-d+1,1)}
, we also define the data oscillation terms

\mathcal{O}(f)_{T}:=\hbar_{T}^{2}\|f-\Pi^{k-2}_{T}(f)\|_{T},\qquad\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(f)_{T}:=(\hbar_{T}^{\prime})^{2}\|f-\Pi^{k-d+1}_{T}(f)\|_{T}.

(32d)

5.1
Abstract error bound

Our first step is to bound the error as the sum of the dual norm of a suitable residual functional on the energy space
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and the nonconforming error measuring the departure of
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
from this space.

Lemma 5.1

(Abstract error bound)
.

The following holds:

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\min_{w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(w-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|_{\Omega}^{2},

(33)

where
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\in H^{-2}(\Omega)
denotes the residual functional such that

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(w):=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{\Omega}=(f,w)_{\Omega}-(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{\Omega},\qquad\forall w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

(34)

and its dual norm is defined as

\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(w)}{\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w\|_{\Omega}}.

(35)

Proof.

Choosing
v:=\argmin_{w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(w-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|_{\Omega}
, we obtain the orthogonality relation

(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(v-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}w)_{\Omega}=0\qquad\forall w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

The Pythagoras identity then gives

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(u-v)\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(v-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

For the first term on the right-hand side, using again the above orthogonality relation and the definition of the dual norm, we infer that

\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(u-v)\|_{\Omega}^{2}=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(u-v))_{\Omega}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u-v)\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(u-v)\|_{\Omega}.

Putting everything together completes the proof.
∎

5.2
Bound on dual residual norm

To bound the dual residual norm, we can invoke any of the two HHO interpolation operators introduced in Section
3.3
. This is the reason why the bound on the dual residual norm takes the form of a minimum between two terms.

Lemma 5.2

(Bound on dual residual norm)
.

The following holds:

(36b)

5.3
Bound on nonconforming error

To bound the nonconforming error, it suffices to pick any function in
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. In this section, we show how to reconstruct from
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
such a function using a
C^{1}
-partition of unity and local solves on vertex patches. To this purpose, we use the
C^{1}
-composite finite element with polynomial order five on the Alfeld split of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
introduced in
[
33
]
. Recall that the Alfeld split consists in subdividing each mesh cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
into
(d+1)=4
sub-simplices by connecting each vertex of
T
to its centroid. We denote a generic sub-cell obtained in this manner by
\tilde{T}
, and the global resulting sub-mesh by
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. The basis functions of the composite
C^{1}
-element from
[
33
]
are of class
C^{1}
in
\Omega
and can be divided into three groups: those attached to the mesh vertices of the original mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\{\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}
, those attached to the mesh cells of the original mesh,
\{\psi_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, and those attached to the mesh sub-faces which are not relevant in the present work. The support of any vertex-based basis function
\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
is a subset of
\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
, and the support of any cell-based basis function
\psi_{T}
is
T
. One key observation from the construction in
[
33
]
is the following partition-of-unity property:

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{T}\equiv 1\quad\text{on }\Omega.

(37)

(The reason why the face-based basis functions do not appear in the above identity is that the corresponding degrees of freedom involve normal derivatives which vanish for the constant unit function.)

Definition 5.3

(Patchwise
H^{2}
-potential reconstruction)
.

For all
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, let
u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})
be the solution to the following well-posed problem:

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}},\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}).

(38)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}

\displaystyle:=\argmin_{\rho_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

(39)

Then, extending
u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
by zero to
\Omega
, we set

u_{c}:=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\psi_{T}u_{T}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(40)

Lemma 5.4

(Bound on nonconforming error)
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u_{c}-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})\|^{2}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\{\eta_{T,{\rm sta}}^{2}+\eta_{T,{\rm tan}}^{2}\big\}.

(41)

Proof.

The proof is postponed to Section
7.2
.
∎

Remark 5.5

(Inhomogeneous boundary condition)
.

The above
H^{2}
-reconstruction can be modified to account for inhomogeneous boundary conditions of the form
u=g_{\rm D}
and
\partial_{n}u=g_{\rm N}
on
\partial\Omega
.
For every mesh vertex
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, we then solve for
u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in H^{2}_{g}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}):=\{v\in H^{2}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}})\;|\;v|_{\partial\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\cap\partial\Omega}=\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}g_{\rm D},\;\partial_{n}v|_{\partial\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\cap\partial\Omega}=\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}g_{\rm N},\;v|_{\partial\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\cap\Omega}=\partial_{n}v|_{\partial\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\cap\Omega}=0\}
such that

(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}},\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}=(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}).

Then, extending
u_{c}^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
by zero to
\Omega
,
u_{c}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
is still defined as in (
40
), and the partition-of-unity property (
37
) implies that
u_{c}
also satisfies the inhomogeneous boundary conditions. Finally, the upper bound from Lemma
5.4
still holds provided the tangential jumps defining
\eta_{T,{\rm tan}}
are redefined as follows: For all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{b}}
,

[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{tt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]|_{F}:=\partial_{tt}\big(u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|_{F}-E_{F}(g_{\rm D}|_{F})\big),\qquad[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{nt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]|_{F}:=\partial_{t}\big((\partial_{n}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})|_{F}-E_{F}(g_{\rm N}|_{F})\big),

where, for a function
\varphi
defined on
F
,
E_{F}(\varphi)(\boldsymbol{x}):=\varphi((\underaccent{\tilde}{I}-\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{F})(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{F}))
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{F}
is the centroid of
F
.

Theorem 5.6

7.1
Proof of Lemma

In this section, we prove Lemma
5.2
. Specifically,
we prove in Section

that,
under Assumption
3.4
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(k+2)\big\{\eta_{T,{\rm sta}}^{2}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(f)^{2}_{T}\big\},

(47a)

and we prove in Section
7.1.2
that

\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\{(k+2)\eta_{T,{\rm sta}}^{2}+\eta_{T,{\rm res}}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(f)^{2}_{T}\big\}.

(47b)

The bound (
47b
) is nothing but (
36a
), and the combination of (
47a
)-(
47b
) readily gives (
36b
).

Recalling the definition (
35
) of the dual residual norm
\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}
, we need to bound
(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}
for all
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

Invoking the weak formulation (
4
) and adding/subtracting
(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{T}

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle\quad-(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}u_{T},\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{T}\big\}.

Moreover, using the discrete HHO scheme (
17
), we infer that, for all

\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\{(f,v-w_{T})_{T}-(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v-\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{w}_{T}))_{T}

\displaystyle+(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}(R^{k+2}_{T}(\widehat{u}_{T})-u_{T}),\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{T}+S_{\partial T}(\widehat{u}_{T},\widehat{w}_{T})\big\}.

(48)

We need now to select a suitable discrete test function
\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}^{k}_{h0}
.

7.1.1
Bound using the canonical interpolation operator and Assumption
3.4

The first possibility is to set
\widehat{w}_{h}:=\widehat{\mathcal{I}}^{k}_{h}(v)
in (
7.1
) (see (
23a
)). Using the
H^{2}
-elliptic projection property (
21

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}

Using the orthogonality property (
19d

\mathcal{C}_{T}^{k+2}

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}\lesssim{}

24a

\displaystyle(\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}v)_{\Omega}\lesssim{}

\|v-\mathcal{C}^{k+2}_{T}(v)\|_{T}

\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})=u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\nabla\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}+\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle-T_{2}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}\Big\{\big(\nabla\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}[\hskip-1.5pt[u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt],\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\big)_{F}

\displaystyle+\big((I-\Pi^{k}_{F})[\hskip-1.5pt[\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt],\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\big)_{F}

\displaystyle+\big(\Pi^{k}_{F}[\hskip-1.5pt[\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt],\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\big)_{F}\Big\},

and we bound the three terms on the right-hand side, say
T_{21}
,
T_{22}
, and
T_{23}
.
Since
\|\nabla\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty}(F)}\lesssim h_{F}^{-1}
, invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and recalling the definition (
12
) of the stabilization bilinear form gives

\displaystyle|T_{21}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}h^{-1}_{F}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{F}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{h^{3}_{F}}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{F}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\frac{h_{F}}{(k+2)^{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\|^{2}_{F}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}S_{{\partial T}}(\widehat{u}_{T},\widehat{u}_{T})\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\frac{h_{F}}{(k+2)^{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\|^{2}_{F}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the trace inverse estimate (
5b
) on
T\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
so that
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial T}
, the stability of the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}
(see (
51

)), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{h_{F}}{(k+2)^{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\|_{T}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})|_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(T)}\lesssim|\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{\psi}}|_{\underaccent{\tilde}{\b{H}}^{1}(\omega_{a})}\lesssim\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

(54)

Combining the above two bounds yields

|T_{21}|\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}S_{{\partial T}}(\widehat{u}_{T},\widehat{u}_{T})\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

To bound
T_{22}
, we observe that

\displaystyle T_{22}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\big((I-\Pi^{k}_{F})[\hskip-1.5pt[\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt],(I-\Pi^{k}_{\tilde{T}})(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\big)_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\|(I-\Pi^{k}_{F})[\hskip-1.5pt[\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{F}\|(I-\Pi^{k}_{\tilde{T}})(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})\boldsymbol{n}_{F}}\|_{F},

\tilde{T}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}

\displaystyle\|(I-\Pi^{k}_{\tilde{T}})(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hbar_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(I-\Pi^{k}_{\tilde{T}})(\psi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\|_{\tilde{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hbar_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\|_{\tilde{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hbar_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}_{\mathrm{rw}}{\times}{\mathcal{I}_{{\rm{mKM}}}^{k+1,{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\underaccent{\tilde}{\psi}})}\|_{T},

where we also used that
k+5\lesssim k+2
. Moreover,

-approximation properties of

|T_{22}|\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{a}}\hbar_{F}\big\{\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{tt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{F}^{2}+\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\partial_{nt}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{F}^{2}\big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}},

\displaystyle|T_{23}|\lesssim{}

|T_{23}|\lesssim\bigg\{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}S_{{\partial T}}(\widehat{u}_{T},\widehat{u}_{T})\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\underaccent{\tilde}{\nabla}^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}.

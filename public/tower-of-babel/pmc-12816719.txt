Intelligent cybersecurity management in industrial IoT system using attribute reduction with collaborative deep learning enabled false data injection attack detection approach
Authors: Ibrahim Alrashdi, Salahaldeen Duraibi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The rapid adoption of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) model has exposed methods to inadequate security measures. False data injection attacks (FDIAs) pose a significant security threat in IIoT, as they aim to mislead industrial platforms by manipulating sensor readings. In IIoT, owing to the data attacker’s changeability, the FDIA is the most critical intrusion network. As aggressive devices permitted in the network perform their typical data-collecting tasks, identifying an FDIA becomes a nontrivial effort due to potential attacks. Conventional attack detection models have proven insufficient in addressing FDIAs, and most current countermeasures focus on the need to legalise data, primarily in the context of data clustering services. Presently, deep learning (DL) systems are employed for detecting FDIA in real-time and supply personalised protective measures to the threat. This paper presents an Intelligent Management of False Data Injection Attacks Using Feature Selection and Voting Classifier (IMFDIA-FSVC) technique in IIoT systems. The primary purpose of the IMFDIA-FSVC technique is to develop a model for detecting and mitigating FDIAs to ensure safe and trustworthy operations of IIoT systems. Initially, the data pre-processing stage involves two stages: missing value analysis and normalisation to standardise the input data for an effective study. For an effective feature selection, the IMFDIA-FSVC model utilises a statistical and information-theoretic selection (SITS) technique to select optimal features from the input data. Finally, the classification process is mainly deployed by three models: the temporal convolutional network (TCN), the deep belief network (DBN), and the autoencoder (AE). An ensemble classifier is then performed using the voting classifier. The comparison study of the IMFDIA-FSVC method showed a superior accuracy value of 99.15% compared to existing models on the IIoT and FDIA datasets.

Introduction

In recent years, the spread of the Internet of Things (IoT) across various fields, including smart cities, agriculture, environmental monitoring, industries, and healthcare, has significantly impacted the world’s economies1. By enabling interaction on the internet through physical devices, IoT has facilitated the emergence of modern applications and services. IIoT involves instruments, interlinked sensors, and other devices, forming a complex network of industrial applications. This network, which includes energy and production management, facilitates advanced automatic applications2. IIoT devices are progressively vulnerable to several cyberattacks, involving malware infections, FDIAs, and distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) threats. Therefore, research activities about IIoT security have increased recently3. FDIAs pose an essential threat to IIoT systems by injecting misleading data from sensors to influence decision-making and possibly lead to severe disruption or physical damage4. These threats could occur stealthily, making them difficult to detect through traditional security measures, as cooperative sensors may appear to function generally5. So, hostile devices approved in the networks perform their everyday information-collecting tasks, as detecting an FDIA has become a nontrivial effort due to the threat’s complexity6. Moreover, there are several additional threats, namely deduction, suggestion, and concealment, which correspondingly function with the FDIA; however, they capture their own opportunities7.

Consequently, if rogue tools in the IIoT are rapidly recognised and isolated, lengthy periods of network breakdowns are avoided, thereby decreasing the probability that the misbehaving tools persist to make unpredictable sensed information8. Furthermore, artificial intelligence (AI) offers a good solution by enabling real-time anomaly detection through enhanced pattern recognition and automating the response to these complex threats9. Hence, incorporating AI into the management of FDIAs in IIoT is critical to improve cybersecurity. Moreover, DL and machine learning (ML) models present an assuring avenue to address the difficulties of FDIA detection in IIoT10. These higher analytical techniques can learn complex correlations and patterns within large datasets, enabling the identification of anomalies that might indicate the presence of injected false information. By utilising these intelligent methods, IIoT systems can determine a more flexible defence against information reliability threats, safeguarding crucial processes and ensuring the consistency and security of industry processes11. Motivation for enhancing security in industrial IoT systems to prevent malicious data attacks

Operational efficiency has become critical due to the rapid growth of interconnected industrial devices, which have transformed conventional systems into intrinsic, automated networks. Furthermore, sensor data and processes may be affected by increased connectivity, and these significant vulnerabilities could lead to advanced cyberattacks. It is also crucial for maintaining safety, reliability, and productivity to safeguard industrial environments from such malicious activities. As a result, developing advanced detection methods that can accurately detect and mitigate deceptive data intrusions has become a pressing requirement to safeguard critical infrastructure. This highlights the urgent requirement for intelligent, resilient security solutions that can detect and respond to growing challenges in real time. Thus, it becomes significant for ensuring the integrity of sensor data for maintaining safe and reliable industrial operations.

Prior research for FDIA recognition in the IIoT environment

Ragab et al.12 proposed a Next-Generation Cybersecurity Attack Detection utilising an ensemble DL method (NGCAD-EDLM) in the IIoT systems. These methods are the automated identification of cyber-threats. In this method, the primary information normalisation stage, which employs min–max normalisation, is presented. Then, the HBA method takes the subsets of features. Moreover, the DL approaches to hyperparameter selection are talented at utilising the lotus effect optimisation algorithm (LEOA) technique. Tian et al.13 introduced two Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)-driven adversarial attacks, which apply to 2 various scenarios. These attacks are intended to reduce additional threat expenses while seeking appropriate adversarial perturbations, making the threats more feasible and realistic. Though different in the image field, the susceptibilities of multi-label learning in the domain of power grid have recently attracted attention and urgently demand to be addressed and explored. Du et al.14 presented a lightweight time-adaptive data-based FDIA detection approach. Primary, a lightweight hybrid FDIA detector, is acquired, incorporating MobileNet and a statistical indicator computing block for feature extraction, along with a DL process for classification. The load information is dimensionally increased by recurrence plots (RPs) and fed into a sequence of detectors operating at various time scales. Next, a time-adaptive structure is intended to handle multiple types of attacks and timing, including many FDIA detectors and a credibility assessment process. Hafizunisa and Sinha15 presented a technique to identify FDI threats that utilises a neural network (NN) approach called AEs. The AEs help identify the deviation between the correlational structure of the data corruption and the predictable learned correlation structure. Hence, a classification strategy is suggested for identifying FDI threats. When the presence of data corruption is detected, utilise denoising AEs (DAE) for reconstructing the initially recorded information.

Alkahtani et al.16 proposed a model for the election-based optimiser approach with a DL-allowed FDI attack detection (EBODL-FDIAD) in the CPS structure. To improve safety in the CPS setting via FDIAs recognition. This system performs ensemble learning classification using three classifiers: LSTM, the attention-based bidirectional recurrent NN (ABiRNN) technique, and the ELM. Lin et al.17 presented an FDIA method and counter-detection approaches that depend on deep reinforcement learning (DRL). Primarily, learnt an attack method. Various kinds of FDIAs are further computed. Next, the threat recognition issues were expressed as a Markov decision process (MDP). A DRL technique is proposed for detecting FDIAs with an integrated dynamic-static detection mechanism. Shen et al.18 proposed a localisation approach for FDIAs that relies on a CNN and is enhanced by the sparrow search algorithm (SSA–CNN) model. This approach addresses the complexity of hyperparameter selection in the CNN, which degrades localisation precision. Xie, Rahman, and Sun19 introduced a Bayesian DL-driven method for detecting cyber-threats and maintaining the security of smart grids. This technique specifically addresses the common issues of imbalanced information in real power methods, which arise from the prevalence of typical system operations, either fully cooperative or adversarial conditions. Utilising an innovative Bayesian GAN-driven method, this approach successfully categorises secure and cooperative measurement information, even in situations with significant information imbalance.

Feature reduction model

For the effective FS, the IMFDIA-FSVC method utilizes the SITS model for choosing the best features from the input data, and this ensemble is integrated through a sequential process in which the ensemble models are applied step-by-step to retain only the most informative and non-redundant attributes21. This model is employed for detecting the most relevant features from the input data. The conventional SITS model is susceptible to missing crucial features and does not always exhibit robust individual significance, although feature selection using this model is insightful and helps mitigate dimensionality. This limitation is addressed by integrating various factors for analysing feature relevance more comprehensively, capturing both obvious and complex patterns. This results in a more balanced and informative feature subset, enhancing the performance of downstream learning models. The SITS model also offers better robustness against noise and redundancy compared to other methods, making it suitable for intrinsic and high-dimensional IIoT data in FDIA detection.

The feature selection sub-system aims to identify and select relevant attributes from the dataset, thereby enhancing the performance of ML approaches. In the presented method, SITS utilises the calculation of three primary functions: Mutual Information (MI), variance threshold, and correction analysis.

DL models

Finally, the classification procedure is primarily organised using three methods: TCN, DBN, and AE, after which an ensemble classifier executes the voting classifier. Each model is selected for its complementary merits in handling IIoT data. The TCN model outperforms in capturing sequence patterns and temporal dependencies, making it ideal for time-series sensor data. The DBN technique effectively models the intrinsic data distributions and also reveals deep representations through hierarchical feature learning. The AE learns compact, unsupervised embeddings that assist in highlighting anomalies. Incorporating these techniques with a voting-based ensemble classifier utilises their diverse capabilities, enhancing overall accuracy and robustness compared to using a single model. This approach improves the detection of varied FDIA patterns and mitigates the overfitting risk or bias inherent in individual methods.

AE technique

An AE is an unsupervised feed-forward NN designed to match its output values using its input values through the recurrent application of backpropagation to reduce the reconstruction error between the output and input24. A standard AE involves three sections: the output (reconstructed input) layer, the input layer, and more than one HL. The HL comprises dual main modules: the decoder and the encoder. The encoding at the HL condenses the input information into a low-dimensional area by removing and encoding its essential attributes. The decoding then rebuilds the new input from such a condensed model using different reconstruction methods, ensuring the outputs closely resemble the inputs. To enable precise data reconstruction, the output layer has dimensions equivalent to those of the input layer. Ensemble voting classifiers

Ensemble classifiers combine the predictions of many classifiers, known as base learners, to make the final prediction (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:FP$$\end{document})25. It aims to present an enhanced outcome with higher precision. The resulting ensemble method is generalised and stronger in comparison with the single-base learner methods. The fundamental concept behind the ensemble method is to aggregate different sets of base learners. For example, the strengths of all classifiers can compensate for the weaknesses of another classifier, leading to a method with enhanced predictive strength. For the present study, Soft voting is used.

During hard voting, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:FP$$\end{document} is described by considering the most votes from dissimilar classifiers. Assume the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:FP$$\end{document} refers to the last prediction provided by the ensemble classifier.16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:FP=Mode\:\left({P}_{1}\left(f\right),\:{P}_{2}\left(f\right),{P}_{3}\left(f\right),\:.\:.\:.\:.,\:{P}_{M}\left(f\right)\right)$$\end{document}

During soft voting, the last prediction was estimated by averaging the forecast probability of each base learner model. Next, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:FP$$\end{document} is computed utilising Eq. Conclusion

In this manuscript, the IMFDIA-FSVC approach was presented. At the primary stage, the data pre-processing phase included two steps: missing value analysis and normalisation, to prepare the input data for efficient analysis. For the effective FS, the IMFDIA-FSVC method utilised the SITS model for choosing the best features from the input data. Finally, the classification procedure is primarily organised using three methods: TCN, DBN, and AE, followed by an ensemble classifier implemented through a voting mechanism. The comparison study of the IMFDIA-FSVC method showed a superior accuracy value of 99.15% compared to existing models on the IIoT and FDIA datasets. The limitations of the IMFDIA-FSVC method include its primary focus on detecting FDIAs, which may restrict its efficiency against other types of cyber threats or discrepancies of FDIAs. Moreover, the model depends on centralised data processing, which could pose challenges related to data privacy and scalability in large, distributed IIoT environments. The adaptability of the technique to growing attack strategies and real-time response capabilities also needs additional exploration. For future work, expanding the approach to address a broader range of cyber threats and incorporating decentralised learning techniques could improve robustness. Investigating adaptive mechanisms for continuous learning from new attack patterns would also enhance system resilience. Future study will also expand the classification to multi-class detection, covering various cyber-attacks such as malware and DDoS. It will also explore decentralized and federated learning to improve privacy and scalability in distributed IoT environments.

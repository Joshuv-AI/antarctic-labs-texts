1.
Introduction

Singular integral operators of convolution type

Tf(x):=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)K(x-y)\,dy

have long served as central objects of Harmonic analysis, originating from the celebrated work of Calderón and Zygmund
[
1
]
. For the class of homogeneous kernels of the form
K(x)=\Omega(x/|x|)|x|^{-n}
, including the Hilbert and Riesz transforms, boundedness on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
holds even under minimal smoothness assumptions on
\Omega
. Initial results
[
2
]
required that
\Omega\in L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and satisfy a cancellation condition on the unit sphere. Subsequent refinements
[
3
,
4
]
incorporated Hardy space techniques, while endpoint bounds were later developed in
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
.

Recent efforts have extended these ideas to the multilinear setting. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a function
\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
with
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{mn-1}}\Omega=0
, define the kernel

K(x):=\Omega(x^{\prime})|x|^{-mn},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}\setminus\{0\},\quad x^{\prime}:=\frac{x}{|x|},

and consider the multilinear singular integral operator

(1.1)

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x):=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{mn}}K(x-y_{1},\ldots,x-y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(y_{i})\,d\vec{y},\quad d\vec{y}=dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

It was well known that the bilinear case
m=2
was studied by Coifman and Meyer
[
9
]
. More recently, Grafakos, He, and Honzík
[
10
]
established the
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1})
,
1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}=1/p
and
1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. Their later work
[
11
]
lowered the regularity threshold to
\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1})
for
q>4/3
, further refined by
[
12
]
. Most recently, Dosidis and Slavíková
[
13
]
extended the above results to the entire range
q>1
for general multilinear operators
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.

To state the results in
[
13
]
, we recall some useful notations. For
\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, let
|\alpha|:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}
. Define
1/p_{\alpha}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}/p_{i}
, and in particular
1/p:=1/p_{(1,\ldots,1)}
. Given
r>1
, we write
(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\cdots,\frac{1}{p_{m}})\in\mathcal{H}^{m}(r)
if

\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}+\frac{|\alpha|-1}{r}<|\alpha|
for all
\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{m}.
The main result in
[
13
]
can be formulated as follows:

Theorem A

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
1<p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
,
\frac{1}{p}=\sum^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
be such that
(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\cdots,\frac{1}{p_{m}})\in\mathcal{H}^{m}(r)
and suppose that
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
,
r>1
, with
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{mn-1}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p_{1},\cdots,p_{m},r)
such that

\|\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{n,p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{m},r}\|\Omega\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})}\prod^{m}_{i=1}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Consider now the sparse domination theory for
T_{\Omega}
. It was well known that sparse domination plays an important role in Harmonic analysis, particularly in deriving sharp weighted inequalities (e.g., the resolution of the
A_{2}
conjecture
[
14
,
15
]
). Recall that, given a constant
0<\eta<1
, a collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
\eta
-sparse if for each
Q\in\mathcal{S}
there exists a measurable subset
E_{Q}\subset Q
with
|E_{Q}|\geq\eta|Q|
, and the sets
\{E_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}
are pairwise disjoint.
Given a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, define the positive sparse form

\mathrm{PSF}_{\mathcal{S};p_{1},\ldots,p_{m+1}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m+1}):=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\langle f_{i}\rangle_{p_{i},Q},\quad\langle f\rangle_{p,Q}:=|Q|^{-1/p}\|f\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{p}}.

Such expressions dominate quantities
|\langle T(f_{1}),f_{2}\rangle|
for operators
T
. This type of domination is called
sparse
and plays an important role in Harmonic analysis. For instance, it was used in the proof of the
A_{2}
conjecture
[
14
,
15
]
. Earlier works related to sparse domination can be found in
[
16
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein.
In 2017, Conde-Alonso et al.
[
20
]
obtained the following sparse domination for
T
.

|(T(f_{1}),f_{2})|\leq\frac{Cp}{p-1}\sup_{S}PSF_{S;1,p}(f_{1},f_{2})\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,1}\log L(S^{d-1})},&1<r<\infty,p\geq r^{\prime};\\
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})},&1<p<\infty.\end{array}\right.

As a consequence, they
[
20
]
deduced a new sharp quantitative
A_{p}
-weighted estimate for
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
. Subsequently, Di Plinio et al.
[
21
]
provided a sparse bound for the associated maximal truncated singular integrals and certain quantitative weighted norm estimates were given.

Note that the authors in
[
22
]
established a uniform domination of the family of trilinear multiplier forms with singularity over an one-dimensional subspace. Later Barron
[
28
]
considered the sparse domination for rough bilinear singular integrals with
\Omega
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1})
. Recently, Borron’s results was improved by Grafakos, Wang and Xue
[
27
]
.

Theorem B

(
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}),r>4/3,
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}\Omega=0
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
be the rough bilinear singular integral operator defined in (
1.1
) when
m=2\
. Then for
p>\max\left\{\frac{24n+3r-4}{8n+3r-4},\frac{24n+r}{8n+r}\right\}
, there exists a constant
C=C_{p,n,r}
such that

|\langle\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2}),f_{3}\rangle|\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1})}\sup_{S}PSF_{S;p,p,p)}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}).

The main purpose of this paper is to generalize this sparse bound to the full multilinear setting, extending to the entire integrability range
r>1
and allowing flexible control via sparse forms with adapted Lebesgue exponents. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
be such that
\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{p_{m}}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{m}(r)
. Suppose that
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
with
r>1
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{mn-1}}\Omega(\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)=0
. Then there exist constants
c=c(n,p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},r)
and
q_{i}>1+\frac{mn}{mn+cr}p_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m+1
(with
p_{m+1}=p^{\prime}
), such that there exists a constant
C=C_{n,q_{1},\ldots,q_{m},r}
satisfying

|\left<\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}\right>|\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}{\rm PSF}_{\mathcal{S};q_{1},\cdots,q_{m+1}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m+1}).

In order to state our corollaries, we prepare some background and definitions for certain classes of weights. Recall that, it was Grafakos and Torres
[
23
]
who initiated the weighted theory for the multilinear singular operators but it was not until 2009 that Lerner et al.
[
24
]
introduced the the multiple Muckenhuopt weight class, denoted by
A_{\bf p}
, which provides a natural analogue of the linear theory.

Definition 1.1

(Multiple weight class
A_{\bf p}
,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
,
{\bf w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{m})
, where each
w_{i}
is a nonegative function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and denote
v_{\bf w}=\prod^{m}_{j=1}w_{j}^{{p}/{p_{j}}}
. We say
{\bf w}\in A_{\bf p}
if

[{\bf w}]_{A_{\bf p}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}v_{\bf w}(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\prod^{m}_{i=1}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{i}^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}}}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the term
\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{i}^{1-p^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}}(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}}}
is understood as
(\inf_{Q}w_{i})^{-1}
when
p_{i}=1
, and
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
is the dual exponent of
p

For
m\geq 2
, given
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m})
with
1\leq p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
and
{\bf r}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{m+1})
with
1\leq r_{1},\cdots,r_{m+1}<\infty
, we say that
{\bf r}\prec{\bf p}
whenever

r_{i}<p_{i},~i=1,\cdots,m~{\rm and}~r_{m+1}^{{}^{\prime}}>p,~{\rm where}~\frac{1}{p}:=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}.

A more broader class of weights than
A_{\bf p}
was introduced by Li et al. in
[
25
]
.

Definition 1.2

(
A_{\bf p,r}
weight class,
[
25
]
)
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer,
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m})
with
1\leq p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
and
{\bf r}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{m+1})
with
1\leq r_{1},\cdots,r_{m+1}<\infty

1/p=\sum^{m}_{k=1}1/p_{k}
. For each
w_{k}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, set

w=\prod^{m}_{k=1}w_{k}^{p/p_{k}}.

We say that
{\bf w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{m})\in A_{\bf p,r}
if
0<w_{i}<\infty
,

and
[w]_{A_{\bf p,r}}<\infty
with

[w]_{A_{\bf p,r}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)^{\frac{r^{{}^{\prime}}_{m+1}}{r^{{}^{\prime}}_{m+1}-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r^{{}^{\prime}}_{m+1}}}\prod^{m}_{k=1}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{k}(x)^{-\frac{1}{\frac{p_{k}}{r_{k}}-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{k}}-\frac{1}{p_{k}}}.

By employing sparse domination techniques, we establish some weighted estimates for the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{\Omega}
. The first theorem addresses settings involving multiple weights, while the second focuses on the single-weight case.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
, let
{\bm{s}}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}),~{\bf q}=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{m+1})
. Let

\mu_{\bf v}=\prod^{m}_{i=1}v_{i}^{{s}/{s_{k}}}

such that

\|\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\|_{L^{s}(\mu_{\bf v})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L^{r}}[{\bf v}]^{\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\frac{q_{i}}{s_{i}-q_{i}}\right\}}_{A_{{\bm{s}},{\bf q}}}\prod^{m}_{i=1}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{s_{i}}(v_{i})}.\\

.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
, for

and

there exists a constant

The main idea in the proof of Theorem

from
[

is to elaborate on the decomposition of the rough kernel into smooth kernels with controlled (summable) growth of constants. However, in the case of multilinear operators, more sophisticated interpolation theorems are required. It is necessary to generalize the interpolation in

2.
Definitions and Main Lemmas

Inspired by the techniques in
[
27
]
, we refine their approach to the multilinear singular integral. Firstly, we consider a general multilinear operator that commutes with translations

(2.1)

{\mathcal{T}}[K](f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})(x)={\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{mn}}K(x-y_{1},\cdots,x-y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(y_{i})d\vec{y}

and assume it is bounded multilinear operator mapping
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
1<p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
, with
\frac{1}{p}=\sum^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
. It is assumed that the kernel
K
of
{\mathcal{T}}[K]
has a decomposition of the form

(2.2)

K(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{s}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}),

where
K_{s}
is a smooth truncation of
K
that enjoys the property

{\rm supp}K_{s}\subset\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}:2^{s-2}<|x_{1}|<2^{s},\cdots,2^{s-2}<|x_{m}|<2^{s}\}.

The truncation of
{\mathcal{T}}[K]
is defined as

{\mathcal{T}}[K]^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})(x):=\sum_{t_{1}<s<t_{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{mn}}K_{s}(x-x_{1},\cdots,x-x_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x_{i})d\vec{x},

where
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty
. See section 2.1 in
[
27
]
for remarks on this type of truncated operators. In this work, we assume that the truncated norm satisfies

(2.3)

\sup_{0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty}\|{\mathcal{T}}[K]^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\to L^{p}}<\infty

for
1<p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
, with
\frac{1}{p}=\sum^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{T}}
is a multilinear operator, we often work with the multilinear form of the type

<{\mathcal{T}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathcal{T}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})(x)f_{m+1}(x)dx.

In our case, the multilinear truncated form is

<{\mathcal{T}}[K]^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathcal{T}}[K]^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})f_{m+1}dx.

Denoting by
C_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m},p)
the following constants

(2.4)

C_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m},p):=\sup_{0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty}\frac{|<{\mathcal{T}}[K]^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}>|}{\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\cdots\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p_{m}}}\|f_{m+1}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}}

then (
2.3
) is equivalent to
C_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{m},p)<\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

[
27
]

If a multilinear operator of the form (
2.1
) is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\to L^{p}
with

, then so do all of its smooth truncations with kernels

K(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})G(x_{1}/2^{t_{1}})\cdots G(t_{m}/2^{t_{m}})

uniformly on

. Here

Definition 2.2

(Stopping collection
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a fixed dyadic lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}
be a fixed dyadic cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A collection
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{D}
of dyadic cubes is a stopping collection with top
Q
if the elements of
\mathcal{Q}
satisfy

L,~L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q},~L\cap L^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow L=L^{\prime}

L\in\mathcal{Q}\Rightarrow L\subset 3Q,

and enjoy the separation properties

(i) if
L,~L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q},~|s_{L}-s_{L^{\prime}}|\geq 8
, then
7L\cap 7L^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

(ii)
\cup_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
3L\cap 2Q\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}9L\subset\cup_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}L:=sh\mathcal{Q}
.

Here
s_{L}=\log_{2}l(L)
, where
l(L)
is the length of the cube
L
.

Let
{\bf 1}_{A}
be the characteristic function of a set
A
. We use
M_{p}
to denote the power version of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

M_{p}(f)(x)=\sup_{x\in Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the supremum is taken over cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
x
.

We need the following definition.

Definition 2.3

(
\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})~norm
,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
be the subspace of

of the functions satisfying
{\rm supp}~h\subset 3Q
and

(2.5)

\infty>\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}:=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\|h{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash sh\mathcal{Q}}\|_{\infty},~\sup_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}\inf_{x\in\hat{L}}M_{p}h(x)\right\},~&p<\infty,\\
\|h\|_{\infty},~&p=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\hat{L}
is the (nondyadic)
2^{5}
-fold dilation of L. We also denote by
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
the subspace of
\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
of functions satisfying

b=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}b_{L},~{\rm supp}~b_{L}\subset L.

Furthermore, we say
b\in\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})

b\in\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathcal{Q}),~\int_{L}b_{L}=0,~\forall L\in\mathcal{Q}.

\|b\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}
denotes
\|b\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}
and similar notation for

Let
a\land b

a

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q}_{t_{1}}}^{t_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{m+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|}\bigg[<\mathcal{T}[K]^{t_{2}\land s_{Q}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1}{\bf 1}_{Q},f_{2},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}>

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
L\subset Q\end{subarray}}<\mathcal{T}[K]^{t_{2}\land s_{L}}_{t_{1}}(f_{1}{\bf 1}_{L},f_{2},\cdots,f_{m}),f_{m+1}>\bigg].

3.
Analysis of the Kernel

In Section
2
, we examined the generalized kernel
K
. In this section, we focus specifically on rough kernels. For a fixed
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
with
r>1
, we analyze the kernel.

(3.1)

K(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})=\frac{\Omega\left((x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})/|(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})|\right)}{|(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})|^{mn}}.

We introduce the relevant notation. Define
\|[K]\|_{r}
and
w_{j,r}[K]
as follows:

\displaystyle\|[K]\|_{r}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{msn}{r^{\prime}}}\Bigl(\|K_{s}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})}\Bigr),

\displaystyle w_{j,r}[K]

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{msn}{r^{\prime}}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
|h|<2^{s-j-c_{m}}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\|K_{s}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})-K_{s}(x_{1}+h,\dots,x_{m}+h)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Bigr).

Based on the results in
[
28
,
27
]
, it is known that if the kernel satisfies
\|[K]\|_{r}<\infty
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w_{j,r}[K]<\infty
, then the assumption
(\ref{psf1})
of Lemma
2.1
is satisfied. However, verifying
\|[K]\|_{r}<\infty
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w_{j,r}[K]<\infty
becomes challenging for the kernel

K(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\Omega\left((x_{1},\dots,x_{m})/|(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})|\right)|(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})|^{-mn},

where
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
,
r>1
. To address this challenge, we employ the Littlewood-Paley decomposition method. Specifically, we decompose
K=\sum^{\infty}_{j=-\infty}K_{j}
and demonstrate that each component
K_{j}
satisfies the required properties.
If
\Omega
lies in
L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{mn-1})
with
r>1
, then the associated kernel
K
given by (
3.1
) is not a multilinear Calderón-Zygmund kernel. However, we can decompose it as a sum of Calderón-Zygmund kernels. Let
\eta
be a Schwartz function in
\mathbb{R}^{mn}
such that
\hat{\eta}(\xi)=1
when

and
\hat{\eta}(\xi)=0
when

, and let
\hat{\beta}=\hat{\eta}-\hat{\eta}(2\dot{)}
. We decompose the kernel
K
by setting
K^{i}(y)=K(y)\hat{\beta}(2^{-i}y)
for

and

\widehat{K^{i}_{j}}(y)=\left(\widehat{K^{i}}(\cdot)\widehat{\beta}(2^{-j+i}\cdot)\right)(y),~K_{j}=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}K_{j}^{i},~j\in\mathbb{Z}.

We decompose

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}^{i}_{j}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}_{j}

accordingly, where
K_{j}^{i}
is the kernel of
{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}^{i}_{j}
and
K_{j}
is the kernel of
{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}_{j}
.

The following lemmas plays a crucial role in our analysis.

Lemma 3.1

(

27
]
)

Let

\displaystyle=\sum_{{Q}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}w_{j}(E_{Q})^{\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}\left(\frac{\langle g_{i}^{q_{i}}w_{i}\rangle_{Q}}{\langle w_{i}\rangle_{Q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}(\langle w_{i}\rangle_{Q})^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(|Q|\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left(\frac{\langle w_{i}\rangle_{Q}}{w_{i}(E_{Q})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}\right).

By a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\langle w_{i}\rangle_{Q}^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}\langle w_{m+1}\rangle_{Q}^{\frac{1}{q_{m+1}}-\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\langle w_{i}\rangle_{Q}^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}\langle v_{\bf w}^{\frac{q_{m+1}^{{}^{\prime}}}{q_{m+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-\xi}}\rangle_{Q}^{\frac{1}{\xi}-\frac{1}{q_{m+1}^{{}^{\prime}}}}=[\bf v]_{A_{{\bm{\xi}},\bf q}}.

We now deal with the second product using the technique in
[
26
]
.
Let

x_{i}=\frac{q_{i}-\xi_{i}}{q_{i}\xi_{i}},~~i=1,\cdots,m,\quad\hbox{and\ }x_{m+1}=\frac{q_{m+1}-\xi^{\prime}}{q_{m+1}\xi^{\prime}}.

Then
\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}w_{i}^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}=1.

The
\rm H\ddot{o}lder^{\prime}s
inequality and the fact that

\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{2q^{\prime}}\bigg)-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{x_{i}}{2}\bigg)=1

imply that

\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left(w_{i}(E_{Q})\right)^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}E_{Q}^{\frac{1}{2q^{\prime}}}\geq\int_{E_{Q}}\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}w_{i}^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}=|E_{Q}|.

This together with the sparseness of
\mathcal{S}
yields that

\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left(\frac{w_{i}(E_{Q})}{|Q|}\right)^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}\geq\eta^{\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{x_{i}}{2}}.

Therefore

\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left(\frac{{\langle w_{i}\rangle}_{Q}}{\frac{1}{|Q|}w_{i}(E_{Q})}\right)^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}\leq\eta^{\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{x_{i}}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\langle w_{i}\rangle^{-\frac{x_{i}}{2}}_{Q}.

By Definition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left(\frac{{\langle w_{i}\rangle}_{Q}}{\frac{1}{|Q|}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\eta^{\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}x_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}{\langle w_{i}\rangle}_{Q}^{-x_{i}}\right)^{\max\left(-\frac{1}{x_{i}\xi_{i}}\right)}\leq\left(\eta^{\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}x_{i}}[\bf v]_{A_{{\bm{\xi}},{\bf q}}}\right)^{\max\left(-\frac{1}{x_{i}\xi_{i}}\right)}.

Note that, by
[
22
]
, the first product depends on the
L^{\xi_{i}}(w_{i})
-boundedness of
M_{q_{i},w_{i}}
, where

M_{q_{i},w_{i}}f(x)=\sup\limits_{Q\in x}\left(\frac{1}{|w(Q)|}\int_{Q}|f|^{q_{i}}w_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}.

This concludes the proof of (
5.1
).

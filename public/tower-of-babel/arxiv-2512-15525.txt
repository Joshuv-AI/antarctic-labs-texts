A modified Bakry-Émery Γ 2 \Gamma_{2} criterion inequality and the monotonicity of the Tsallis entropy
Xiaohan Cai, Xiaodong Wang

Abstract
. The Bakry-Émery Γ 2 \Gamma_{2} criterion inequality provides a method for establishing the logarithmic Sobolev inequality. We prove a one-parameter family of weighted Bakry-Émery Γ 2 \Gamma_{2} criterion inequalities which in the limit case yields the improved constant due to Ji [ 14 ] . Furthermore, we establish a modified weighted Γ 2 \Gamma_{2} criterion inequality which could be interpreted as a monotonicity of the Tsallis entropy under the heat flow and yields a family of sharp Sobolev inequalities.

Introduction

The sharp constants of Sobolev inequalities on Riemannian manifolds have been intensively studied from various perspectives over the last several decades.
Among these inequalities, the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, introduced by Gross
[
11
]
, plays a vital role at the interface of analysis, geometry and probability. It is impossible to mention all important work in this field. We recommend
[
23
,
4
,
6
,
19
,
16
,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein.

The logarithmic Sobolev inequality on compact Riemannian manifolds states as:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\fint_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\frac{C}{2}\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}-\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\right)\log\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\right)\right),\quad\forall\,0<u\in C^{\infty}(M).

The optimal constant of
eq.

1.1
, denoted by
\alpha_{M}
, refers to the largest constant
C
such that the inequality
eq.

1.1
holds.
Historically, Bakry and Émery
[
2
]
proved that for
(M^{n},g)
with
Ric_{g}\geq n-1
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\geq n.

Later, Rothaus
[
24
, Theorem 1]
obtained an improved lower bound for
\alpha_{M}
under the same curvature hypothesis:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\geq\frac{4n}{(n+1)^{2}}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}}n,

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
. The proof consists of studying a critical equation of
eq.

1.1
and a delicate choice of test functions.

Another interesting way toward
eq.

1.1
is the Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
criterion

1

The terminology
\Gamma_{2}
comes from the Carré du champ calculus systematically studied by Bakry and his collaborators, see
[
17
]
. For the Laplace operator on a manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
\Gamma_{2}(f,f)=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f)
.
.
Consider the following inequality

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(|\nabla^{2}\log f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla\log f,\nabla\log f))\geq C\int_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2},\quad\forall\,0<f\in C^{\infty}(M).

The Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
criterion
[
2
]
states that if
eq.

1.3
holds with
C=\Lambda
, then the following also holds with
C=\Lambda
:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\fint_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2}\geq 2C\left(\fint_{M}f\log f-\left(\fint_{M}f\right)\log\left(\fint_{M}f\right)\right),\quad\forall\,0<f\in C^{\infty}(M),

which
is equivalent to, by substituting
u=\sqrt{f}
, the logarithmic Sobolev inequality
eq.

1.1
:

\displaystyle\fint_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\frac{C}{2}\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}-\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\right)\log\left(\fint_{M}u^{2}\right)\right),\quad\forall\,0<u\in C^{\infty}(M).

In other words, if we let
\Lambda_{M}
be the largest constant such that
eq.

1.3
holds with
C=\Lambda_{M}
, then the criterion yields

(1.5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\geq\Lambda_{M}.

To get their
\Gamma_{2}
criterion, Bakry-Émery consider how the Shannon entropy of a positive function
f
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle S(f):=-\int_{M}f\log f

evolve under the heat flow
\partial_{t}f=\Delta f
. Then

eq.

1.3
could be interpreted as an inequality involving the first and second order derivatives of the Shannon entropy
eq.

1.6
.
After integrating
eq.

1.3
over
t\in[0,+\infty)
, one gets
eq.

1.4
. We shall present a detailed illustration at the end of
section

3
.

Based on the Bakry-Émery criterion, a lower bound of
\Lambda_{M}
is of independent interest. Bakry and Émery
[
2
]
actually proved the stronger result that
\Lambda_{M}\geq n
if
(M^{n},g)
satisfies
Ric_{g}\geq n-1
. This is optimal as
\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=n
.

In the recent seminal work of Guillen-Sylvestre
[
13
]
proving the existence of global smooth solutions for the Landau equation with Coulomb potential, the inequality
eq.

1.3
with an improved constant for even functions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
plays a crucial role. Afterward, Ji
[
14
, Theorem 2.3]
proved the following general result.

Theorem 1

(Ji,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
. Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
. Then for any
0<f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(|\nabla^{2}\log f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla\log f,\nabla\log f))\geq\left(\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n\right)\int_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2}.

That is,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{M}\geq\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n.

Our first result is a one-parameter family of weighted Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
criterion inequality.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 2
) be a compact Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
.
Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
.
Then for
s\in(-\infty,0]\cup\left[\frac{4(n+2)}{4n-1},+\infty\right)
and
0<v\in C^{\infty}(M)
, there holds

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{s}(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v))\geq\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}ns}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\int_{M}v^{s}|\nabla v|^{2}.

As a limiting case, we recover
theorem

1
(see
corollary

3.1
). The result could be useful in other situations. Our proof is direct and simple.

In the second part of this paper, we investigate the monotonicity of a generalized Shannon entropy under the heat flow. In 1988, Tsallis introduced the following entropy
[
27
]
: for
p\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
and
0<u\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle T_{p}(u):=\frac{\int_{M}u^{p}-\int_{M}u}{1-p}.

It is straightforward to see that as
p\to 1
, Tsallis entropy converges to the Shannon entropy, making it a natural generalization of the latter. Moreover, the Tsallis entropy is also widely used in statistical mechanics, see for example
[
20
,
18
,
28
]
. We study explicitly how the Tsallis entropy evolves under the heat flow. Its second derivative is given by a modified weighted
\Gamma_{2}
integral with an additional term involving
\Delta u
.
We generalize the Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
criterion inequality to this setting.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 2
) be a compact Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
.
Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
.
Then for
s\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1}\right]\cup[2,+\infty)
and
0<v\in C^{\infty}(M)
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{s}(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)-v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta v)

(1.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}(s-2)}{n(n+2)(s+2)}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}(s-2)}{(n+2)(s+2)}\right)\int_{M}v^{s}|\nabla v|^{2}.

In terms of the Tsallis entropy under the heat flow,
theorem

3
yields a differential inequality that relates its first derivative and its second derivative.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 2
) be a compact Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
.
Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
.
Then for
p\in\left[\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}+1},1\right)\cup(1,2]
and under the heat flow
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
with a positive initial data, we have:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}T_{p}(u)+2\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}T_{p}(u)\leq 0.

As the Bakry-Émery criterion did for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality,
theorem

4
implies a family of Sobolev inequalities, which recovers the logarithmic Sobolev inequality as
q\to 2
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 2
) be a compact manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
.
Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
.
Then for
q\in[1,2)\cup\left(2,\frac{2n^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\fint_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{q-2}\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}(q-1)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}(q-1)\right)\left(\left(\fint_{M}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-\fint_{M}v^{2}\right).

Remark 1.1
.

The limitation of the range
q\leq\frac{2n^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}
is mysterious and is due to the heat equation to some extent, as observed by Bakry-Émery
[
3
]
.

This family of Sobolev inequalities is in fact not new. Historically, Bakry-Émery
[
3
]
proved a sharp Sobolev inequality for
q\in(2,\frac{2n^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}]
. Bidaut-Veron-Veron
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
extended this to
q\in(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}]
.
After that, Fontenas
[
10
]
improved the sharp Sobolev constant for
q\in(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}]
and Dolbeault-Esteban-Loss
[
9
, Theorem 4]
extended this to

. Their results could be summarized as follows.

Theorem 5

(
[
10
,
9
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be a compact manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
.
Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
.
Then for
q\in[1,2)\cup(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}]
and
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\fint_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{q-2}\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}(q-1)}{(q-2)+(n+1)^{2}}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}(q-1)}{(q-2)+(n+1)^{2}}n\right)\times

\displaystyle\left(\left(\fint_{M}|u|^{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-\fint_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

Our contribution here is to formulate a unified framework for a family of sharp Sobolev inequalities, including the Poincaré inequality (i.e.
q=1

\Gamma_{2}

Finally, we mention that the Rényi entropy
[

provides another generalization of the Shannon entropy, preserving most of its essential properties in the Euclidean setting. The Rényi entropy has garnered lots of study on both Euclidean space and noncompact manifolds, as seen in works such as
[

Our work shows that the Tsallis entropy on compact manifolds with positive Ricci curvature has better properties.

2.
Some integral identities

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact manifold without boundary. Then for any
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right).

Proof.

By the Bochner formula,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}+\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

Integrate it over
M
and the proof finishes.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact manifold without boundary. If
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
is a positive function, then

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n+2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}-\frac{2n}{n+2}\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+\frac{n}{n+2}\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle.

Proof.

By divergence theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathrm{div}(u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u)-(2u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}-2\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}-\frac{2}{n}\int_{M}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u-2\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle,

where we used

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle+\frac{1}{n}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u.

Rearrange it and we get the first desired identity. The second one follows by using
eq.

2.1
again.
∎

Proof of
theorem

2
:

For
s=0
,
eq.

1.7
follows from
lemma

2.1
and the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}(M)

For
s\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,0)\cup\left[\frac{4(n+2)}{4n-1},+\infty\right)
, let
t:=s+2\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,2)\cup\left[\frac{6(2n+1)}{4n-1},+\infty\right)
and
u:=v^{\frac{t}{2}}
. It is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle v^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla v=\frac{2}{t}\nabla u,\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla^{2}v=\frac{2}{t}\nabla^{2}u+\frac{2}{t}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)u^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

We compute

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)^{2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)^{2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle,

where
\alpha
is a positive parameter to be determined later. Replacing the 2nd term using
lemma

2.1
, we continue and use
lemma

2.2

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}(\Delta u)^{2}\right)+(1-\alpha)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+\frac{2n}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)^{2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right|^{2}+\frac{2n}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\left(\frac{2}{t}-\frac{n}{n+2}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right)+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\left(\frac{2}{t}-\frac{n}{n+2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{(n+2)^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

(3.1)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u),

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right|^{2}=\frac{n-1}{n}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}.

Now we choose
\alpha
such that the coefficient of
\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}
in
eq.

3.1
vanishes, i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{n(n-1)\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)}{(n+2)\left(\frac{2}{t}(n+2)-n\right)}=\frac{(n-1)ns}{(n+2)(ns-4)}.

Moreover, we want the coefficients of the last two terms in
eq.

3.1
to be nonnegative. Notice that

\displaystyle\alpha>0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow s\in(-\infty,0)\cup\left(\frac{4}{n},+\infty\right)

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\frac{n}{n-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow s\in(-\infty,0]\cup\left[\frac{4(n+2)}{4n-1},+\infty\right).

Therefore, with
s\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,0)\cup\left[\frac{4(n+2)}{4n-1},+\infty\right)
, we could continue

3.1
as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right)+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\left(\frac{2}{t}-\frac{n}{n+2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{(n+2)^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\lambda_{1}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+(n-1)\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}ns}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\int_{M}v^{s}|\nabla v|^{2}.

This yields the desired inequality. For
s=-2
, the desired inequality follows by taking

in
eq.

1.7
.
∎

Corollary 3.1

(Ji,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq n-1
. Denote
\lambda_{1}
as the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
(M^{n},g)
. Then for any
0<f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(|\nabla^{2}\log f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla\log f,\nabla\log f))\geq\left(\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n\right)\int_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2}.

Proof.

For any fixed
0<f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, let
v:=-\log f
. Then for
s\ll-1
, the function
1-\frac{v}{s}
is positive on
M
. Then we could apply
theorem

2
to
1-\frac{v}{s}
and get

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(1-\frac{v}{s}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}s}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}ns}{(n+2)(ns-4)}\right)\int_{M}\left(1-\frac{v}{s}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{s^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}.

Multiply both sides by
s^{2}
and let
s
go to
-\infty
, we further notice that
\lim_{s\to-\infty}(1-\frac{v}{s})^{s}=e^{-v}=f
and
\lim_{s\to-\infty}\frac{(n-1)^{2}ns}{(n+2)(ns-4)}=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(|\nabla^{2}\log f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla\log f,\nabla\log f))\geq\left(\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n\right)\int_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2}.

This finishes our proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Ji’s original arguments utilize a linear combination of two integral inequalities and involve two delicately chosen parameters
\theta
and
\beta
. In contrast, in our proof of
theorem

2
(and hence
corollary

3.1
), only one parameter
\alpha
is used.

Now we elucidate the Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
criterion precisely.

Given
0<f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, consider the heat equation on
(M^{n},g)
with the initial data
f_{0}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f=\Delta f\\
f(0,\cdot)=f_{0}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to see that the time derivatives of the Shannon entropy
eq.

1.6
are as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}S(f)=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f|\nabla\log f|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}S(f)=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}f\left(|\nabla^{2}\log f|^{2}+Ric(\nabla\log f,\nabla\log f)\right).

Hence
corollary

3.1
could be interpreted as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}S(f)+2\left(\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}S(f)\leq 0.

Integrating for
t
over
[0,+\infty)
and noticing that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(t,\cdot)=\fint_{M}f_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle 0-\int_{M}f_{0}|\nabla\log f_{0}|^{2}+2\left(\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n\right)\left(-\int_{M}f_{0}\log\left(\fint_{M}f_{0}\right)+\int_{M}f_{0}\log f_{0}\right)\leq 0.

Rearranging it gives the logarithmic Sobolev inequality
eq.

1.4
with
C=\frac{4n-1}{n(n+2)}\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}n
.

4.
Modified weighted Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
inequality

In this section, we prove the modified weighted Bakry-Émery
\Gamma_{2}
inequality in
theorem

3
. The strategy is similar to that employed in the proof of
theorem

2
.

Proof of
theorem

3
:

First, consider
s\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1}\right]\cup(2,+\infty)
. Let
t:=s+2\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{4(n-1)^{2}}{4n-1}\right]\cup(4,+\infty)
and
u:=v^{\frac{t}{2}}
. It is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle v^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla v=\frac{2}{t}\nabla u,\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla^{2}v=\frac{2}{t}\nabla^{2}u+\frac{2}{t}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)u^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u,\quad

\displaystyle v^{s}v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta v=\left(\frac{2}{t}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+\left(\frac{2}{t}\right)^{3}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u.

Let
\alpha
be a positive number to be determined later. Using the same strategy as in the derivation of
eq.

3.1
, it follows from
lemma

2.2
and
lemma

2.1
that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)-v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)^{2}\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{t}\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}-\frac{2}{t}\int_{M}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2}{t}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right\rangle-\frac{2}{t}\int_{M}u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2}{t}+2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{n+2}-\frac{2}{t}\frac{n}{n+2}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+\left(2\left(\frac{2}{t}-1\right)\frac{n}{n+2}+\frac{2}{t}\frac{2n}{n+2}\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{2n}{n+2}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle+\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\alpha\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{n}\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+(1-\alpha)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle+\frac{2n}{n+2}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g,\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right\rangle+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right)+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)-\frac{n(n-1)}{(n+2)^{2}}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u).

Now we choose
\alpha
such that the coefficient of
\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}
in
eq.

4.1
vanishes, i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{n-1}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)=\frac{(n-1)(s-2)}{(n+2)(s+2)}>0.

Moreover, we want the coefficients of the last two terms in
eq.

4.1
to be nonnegative. Notice that

\displaystyle\alpha>0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow s\in(-\infty,-2)\cup\left(2,+\infty\right),

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\frac{n}{n-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow s\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1}\right]\cup\left[-2,+\infty\right).

Therefore, with
s\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1}\right]\cup(2,+\infty)
, we could continue
eq.

4.1
as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)-v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}\Delta v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta u}{n}g\right)+\frac{n}{n+2}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}g\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)-\frac{n(n-1)}{(n+2)^{2}}\left(\frac{4}{t}-1\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\int_{M}u^{-2}|\nabla u|^{4}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}+\alpha\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\lambda_{1}|\nabla u|^{2}+(n-1)\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}(s-2)}{n(n+2)(s+2)}\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}(s-2)}{(n+2)(s+2)}\right)\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}v^{s}|\nabla v|^{2}.

This yields the desired inequality.
The case for
s=2
could be obtained by taking the limit
s\to 2^{+}
in
eq.

1.8
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

For
s\in\left(-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1},-2\right)
, there exists some function
0<v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
such that

eq.

1.8
fails to hold.
We give a brief explanation as follows.

For
s\in\left(-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1},-2\right)
, we have

and
\alpha:=\frac{n-1}{n+2}\left(1-\frac{4}{t}\right)>\frac{n}{n-1}
. Consider

and

. Then it is straightforward to verify that the first square term in the right hand side of
eq.

4.1
vanishes for this
u
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)(\Delta u)^{2}<\left(1-\frac{n-1}{n}\alpha\right)\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Inserting these into

4.1
shows that

1.8
.

However, for

, it is unknown whether
eq.

1.8

\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

5.
Monotonicity of the Tsallis entropy

Consider the Tsallis entropy defined for
p\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
and
0<u\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle T_{p}(u):=\frac{\int_{M}u^{p}-\int_{M}u}{1-p}.

To get
theorem

4
, we just need to calculate the expressions for the derivatives of the Tsallis entropy under the heat flow.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact manifold.
Consider the heat flow
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
with the initial value
u_{0}>0
and let
U:=u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
. There hold

\displaystyle\nabla u=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}uU^{-1}\nabla U,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U=

\displaystyle\Delta U+\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-1\right)U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}.

Proof.

Note that
\log U=\frac{p-1}{2}\log u
, we get

\displaystyle U^{-1}\nabla U=\frac{p-1}{2}u^{-1}\nabla u.

Therefore we could express
\Delta u
as

\displaystyle\Delta u=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}div(uU^{-1}\nabla U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}(uU^{-1}\Delta U+\langle\nabla u,\nabla U\rangle U^{-1}-uU^{-2}|\nabla U|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}uU^{-1}\left(\Delta U+\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-1\right)U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\right).

Hence we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U=\frac{p-1}{2}Uu^{-1}\Delta u=\Delta U+\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-1\right)U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}.

∎

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the same assumptions and notations in
lemma

5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}T_{p}(u)=

\displaystyle p\int_{M}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}=p\left(\frac{2}{p-1}\right)^{2}\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}T_{p}(u)=

\displaystyle-2p\left(\frac{2}{p-1}\right)^{2}\int_{M}u\left(|\nabla^{2}U|^{2}+Ric(\nabla U,\nabla U)-U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U\right).

Proof.

First, the derivative of the
L^{1}
norm of
u
vanishes:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}u=\int_{M}\Delta u=0.

For the
L^{p}
norm term, by the heat equation and
lemma

5.1
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}u^{p}=

\displaystyle p\int_{M}u^{p-1}\Delta u=p(1-p)\int_{M}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}=p(1-p)\left(\frac{2}{p-1}\right)^{2}\int_{M}u^{p}U^{-2}|\nabla U|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(1-p)\left(\frac{2}{p-1}\right)^{2}\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2}.

Therefore, we could get the first order derivative of
T_{p}(u)
.

For the second order derivative of
T_{p}(u)
, by
lemma

5.1
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta u+2\int_{M}u\langle\nabla\partial_{t}U,\nabla U\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u\Delta|\nabla U|^{2}+2\int_{M}u\langle\nabla\Delta U,\nabla U\rangle

(5.2)

\displaystyle+2\left(1-\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\int_{M}uU^{-2}|\nabla U|^{4}+2\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-1\right)\int_{M}uU^{-1}\langle\nabla|\nabla U|^{2},\nabla U\rangle.

By
lemma

5.1
and the divergence theorem, we tackle the second term in the last line of
section

5
as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}uU^{-1}\langle\nabla|\nabla U|^{2},\nabla U\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{M}\langle\nabla|\nabla U|^{2},\nabla u\rangle=-\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{M}u\Delta|\nabla U|^{2}

To tackle the first term in the last line of
section

5
, we notice that by
lemma

5.1
and
eq.

5.3
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}uU^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla U\rangle U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}+\int_{M}uU^{-2}|\nabla U|^{4}-\int_{M}uU^{-1}\langle\nabla|\nabla U|^{2},\nabla U\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\int_{M}uU^{-2}|\nabla U|^{4}+\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{M}u\Delta|\nabla U|^{2}.

This is equivalent to

(5.4)

\displaystyle 2\left(1-\frac{2}{p-1}\right)\int_{M}uU^{-2}|\nabla U|^{4}=2\int_{M}uU^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U-(p-1)\int_{M}u\Delta|\nabla U|^{2}.

Inserting
eq.

5.4
and
eq.

5.3
into
section

5
, we conclude by Bochnner formula that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2}=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}u\Delta|\nabla U|^{2}+2\int_{M}u\langle\nabla\Delta U,\nabla U\rangle+2\int_{M}uU^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}u(|\nabla^{2}U|^{2}+Ric(\nabla U,\nabla U))+2\int_{M}uU^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U.

This gives the second order derivative of
T_{p}(u)
.
∎

Now
theorem

4
would be a consequence of
theorem

3
and

proposition

5.1
.

Proof of
theorem

4
:

For
p\in\left[\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}+1},1\right)\cup(1,2]
, we take

\displaystyle s:=\frac{2}{p-1}\in\left(-\infty,-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1}\right]\cup[2,+\infty).

Use the notation
U:=u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
as in
lemma

5.1
. By
proposition

5.1
and
theorem

3
, we get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2p}\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}T_{p}(u)=\int_{M}U^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(|\nabla^{2}U|^{2}+Ric(\nabla U,\nabla U)-U^{-1}|\nabla U|^{2}\Delta U)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\int_{M}U^{\frac{2}{p-1}}|\nabla U|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}T_{p}(u).

∎

Remark 5.1
.

For
p\in\left(0,\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}+1}\right)
and
(M^{n},g)=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, there exists a positive function
u_{0}
such that
eq.

1.9
fails for the heat flow with the initial value
u_{0}
at
t=0
. In fact, this is a direct consequence of
remark

4.1
by noting that
s:=\frac{2}{p-1}\in\left(-\frac{2(2n^{2}+1)}{4n-1},-2\right)
.
However, we do not know whether
eq.

1.9
holds for
p<0
or
p>2
even on the model space
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Proof of
corollary

1.1
:

Let
p:=\frac{2}{q}\in\left[\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}+1},1\right)\cup(1,2]
.
Given
0<v\in C^{\infty}(M)
, define
u_{0}:=v^{q}=v^{\frac{2}{p}}
. Consider the heat flow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(t,\cdot)=\fint_{M}u_{0}.

Then we use
theorem

4
and integrate for
t
over
[0,+\infty)
and get

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle 0-p\int_{M}u_{0}^{p-2}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+2\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left(\frac{Vol(M)(\fint_{M}u_{0})^{p}-Vol(M)\fint_{M}u_{0}}{1-p}-\frac{\int_{M}u_{0}^{p}-\int_{M}u_{0}}{1-p}\right)

Rearranging it, we get

\displaystyle p\fint_{M}u_{0}^{p-2}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-p}\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\left(\left(\fint_{M}u_{0}\right)^{p}-\fint_{M}u_{0}^{p}\right).

Finally, substitute
u_{0}=v^{\frac{2}{p}}
and
p=\frac{2}{q}
to conclude that

\displaystyle 2q\fint_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}\geq\frac{2q}{q-2}\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}(q-1)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}(q-1)\right)\left(\left(\fint_{M}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-\fint_{M}v^{2}\right).

∎

We conclude this paper with several remarks.

We note that the Sobolev constant in
corollary

1.1
is sharp in the sense that it is the largest possible one for the model manifold
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. However, it is slightly weaker than the Sobolev constant in
theorem

5
for general manifolds with
Ric\geq n-1
, since
\lambda_{1}\geq n
. The same phenomenon occurs for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality: the lower bound of
\alpha_{M}
given by
theorem

1
together with the Bakry-Émery criterion
eq.

1.5
is slightly weaker than that obtained by Rothaus
eq.

1.2
. Therefore, it is natural to wonder whether the lower bound on
\Lambda_{M}

in
theorem

1
can be improved to match that in
eq.

1.2
. Similarly, it would also be valuable to refine the coefficient in
eq.

1.8
so that it yields a Sobolev inequality with the same Sobolev constant as in
theorem

5
.

theorem

4
and
corollary

1.1
unveil that the Tsallis entropy exhibits certain monotonicity under the heat flow, leading to sharp Sobolev inequalities with exponent
q\in[1,2)\cup\left(2,\frac{2n^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}\right]
.
However, as we mentioned in
remark

5.1
,
theorem

4
fails for
p\in(0,\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}+1})
.
It would be of interest to determine whether other monotonicity properties of Tsallis entropy can be established so that the Sobolev inequality for the full range of the Sobolev exponent
q\in(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}]
could be recovered.

Finally, we notice that
eq.

1.9
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\left(C(n,p)t+\log\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}T_{p}(u)\right)\right)\leq 0,

where
C(n,p):=2\left(\left(1-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n(n+2)}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)\lambda_{1}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n+2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)
. This combined with
proposition

5.1
yields a decay rate upper bound of a weighted Dirichlet energy under the heat flow:

\displaystyle\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2}\leq e^{-C(n,p)t}\int_{M}u_{0}|\nabla U_{0}|^{2},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
is the heat flow with a positive initial data
u_{0}
, and
U:=u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
.
On the other hand, by considering the
L^{2}
spectral decomposition of the solution
u
and the fact that
u
converges to a positive constant function as
t\to+\infty
, we know that the decay rate has an asymptotic lower bound in terms of
\lambda_{1}
:

\displaystyle Ce^{-\lambda_{1}t}\leq\int_{M}u|\nabla U|^{2},\quad t\gg 1,

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
u_{0}
.
This viewpoint indicates some restrictions on the constant appearing in
eq.

1.8
and

eq.

1.9
.

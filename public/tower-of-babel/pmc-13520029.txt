Anisotropic 2D Mo8S4Se8 and Mo8Se4S8 Semiconductors with Excellent Stability and High Carrier Mobility for Optoelectronics
Authors: Yi Peng, Minjia Yao, Fangyuan Li, Qianqian Zhu, Juexian Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors are regarded as highly advantageous materials for the progression of next-generation optoelectronic technologies. Nonetheless, their practical application is frequently constrained by intrinsically low carrier mobility under ambient conditions. In this work, we systematically investigate various crystalline phases of Mo8S4Se8 and Mo8Se4S8 (Mo8X4M8) monolayers using first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations, with an emphasis on their structural stability, elastic mechanical performance, electronic band structures, visible-light optical absorption, and anisotropic carrier mobility. Formation energy, cohesive energy, phonon dispersion spectrum, and room-temperature ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations are combined to validate lattice stability under ideal vacuum conditions at 300 K, while deformation potential theory is applied to evaluate anisotropic carrier transport. Calculation results confirm that all of the considered phases exhibit intrinsic lattice stability within the simulated vacuum environment. Electronic structure analysis shows that the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2-Mo8X4M8 monolayers behave as intrinsic semiconductors, with HSE06-calculated band gaps ranging from 1.32 to 1.49 eV. Moreover, these monolayers showcase ductility characteristics and optical absorption coefficients on the order of 105 cm–1 within the visible region. Remarkably, they exhibit substantial anisotropic carrier mobility, with hole mobility along the y-axis reaching up to 104 cm2 V–1 s–1. Overall, these Mo8X4M8 monolayers further expand the repertoire of Mo8-based 2D optoelectronic materials and represent promising candidates for future nanoelectronic and sensor applications.

Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors have emerged as a novel category of materials for electronics and optoelectronics, benefiting from their atomic-scale dimensions, remarkable mechanical flexibility, and adjustable electronic characteristics. − Despite their great potential, the widespread application of 2D semiconductors encounters specific challenges. For high-performance semiconducting devices, materials must satisfy three critical requirements: (1) an appropriate electronic band gap to achieve the crucial high on–off current ratio necessary for logic circuits; (2) high carrier mobility, which determines the speed at which electrons or holes traverse under an electric field and directly influences transistor efficiency; and (3) stable, reproducible properties combined with environmental sustainability. − While graphene is renowned for its exceptional carrier mobility, it is constrained by a narrow and nontunable band gap. , Conversely, phosphorene boasts an ideal direct band gap and significant anisotropic mobility but suffers from instability and rapid oxidation in air. , Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) exhibit strong structural integrity, favorable band gaps, and excellent on/off ratios, yet they fall short of phosphorene in carrier mobility, restricting their potential applications in field-effect transistors (FETs). , Therefore, designing novel 2D semiconductors that simultaneously possess high carrier mobility, excellent environmental stability, and suitable band gaps for optoelectronic conversion remains a central and ongoing challenge for next-generation optoelectronic devices.

To confront this challenge, recent approaches have emphasized structural engineering and chemical modification strategies, including phase manipulation, composition tuning, strain engineering, and heterostructure design, − with the goal of fabricating materials with superior properties. This component modulation strategy has been extensively validated in various material systems, such as halide double perovskites and oxysulfides, − where targeted anion/cation substitution effectively tunes optoelectronic and thermal properties. For instance, significant progress has been made in predicting and synthesizing novel 2D materials with enhanced characteristics. Li et al. predicted that the P–C3N2 monolayer exhibits a notably large visible-light absorption coefficient (105 cm–1) and an extraordinary hole mobility (104 cm2V–1 s–1). Similarly, Wang et al. successfully synthesized Mo2S3 by introducing structural point and line defects, demonstrating an impressive electron mobility of approximately 104 cm2 V–1 s–1, which exceeds that of the MoS2 monolayer. Furthermore, strain engineering and interface engineering have substantially modulated carrier dynamics. Bi et al. confirmed that the carrier mobility of monolayer B2Se3 undergoes considerable modulation under uniaxial strain, reaching levels of 104 cm2V–1 s–1. Moreover, the electron mobility of the B2Se3/CS van der Waals (vdW) heterostructure exceeds that of pristine 2D B2Se3. Likewise, Zhao et al. demonstrated that carrier dynamics in 1D ZnO-based heterostructures can be finely tuned via interface engineering to optimize their photoelectric characteristics. Importantly, Yin and Sarkar et al. − established that precise control over the chalcogen composition in the ternary alloyed MoS2‑xSe x significantly enhances the optical absorption spectrum and promotes high carrier mobility. Recently, a novel 2D material, W8Se12,24 was synthesized, exhibiting favorable direct bandgaps, impressive optical absorption, and high anisotropic in-plane mobility that markedly exceeds that of traditional TMDs. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that specific 2D systems can achieve the desired combination of high mobility and stability through rational design. However, compared with previously reported binary M8X12 and Janus Mo8S6Se6 monolayers, ternary chalcogenides with the unique Mo8X4M8 stoichiometric ratio remain rarely investigated.

Carrier mobility stands as a crucial metric for evaluating the performance of semiconductor materials, playing a pivotal role in charge transport mechanisms and fundamentally determining the maximum performance potential of nano-optoelectronic devices. With this insight, we conducted an extensive investigation of several crystalline phases of Mo8X4M8 monolayers by using first-principles calculations. Our analysis focused on their structural stability, elastic characteristics, photoelectric responses, and charge carrier transport characteristics. Our findings demonstrate that all of the examined crystalline phases exhibit outstanding stability. Additionally, these materials feature an optimal direct bandgap and a visible-light absorption coefficient surpassing 105 cm–1, coupled with remarkable room-temperature electron mobility reaching up to 104 cm2 V–1 s–1. These outstanding characteristics render Mo8X4M8 monolayers highly promising candidates for FETs and high-efficiency photodetectors, underscoring their considerable potential for incorporation into next-generation optoelectronic devices. It is important to note that all stability assessments presented here are limited to theoretical vacuum simulation environments; experimental verification of stability in air and at high temperatures is required in future research.

Calculation Methods

All theoretical calculations presented in this study were implemented within the framework of density functional theory (DFT) using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP), and the overall calculation quality was set to “Fine” to ensure rigorous computational tolerances. The exchange–correlation interactions of electrons were treated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the form of the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) scheme. , A vacuum region thicker than 20 Å was inserted perpendicular to the 2D monolayer plane to avoid periodic interactions. A plane-wave cutoff energy was expanded with 500 eV, and structural optimizations were conducted employing a 6 × 6 × 1 Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack k-point grid. A denser 15 × 15 × 1 Γ-centered k-point mesh was then used for subsequent band structure and optical property calculations. All atomic geometries were fully optimized without imposing symmetry constraints, using the conjugate gradient algorithm until stringent convergence criteria were satisfied: a total energy change below 10–7 eV per atom and residual atomic forces less than 0.001 eV/Å. Grimme’s DFT-D3 dispersion correction with Becke-Johnson (BJ) damping was incorporated to accurately capture van der Waals interactions, and the Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE06) hybrid functional was employed for high-precision calculations of band structures, band gaps, partial density of states (PDOS), and optical absorption spectra. Cohesive energy, phonon spectra, and 300 K AIMD simulations were combined to validate thermodynamic, dynamic, and thermal stability. Elastic constants, Young’s modulus, and Poisson’s ratio were derived from energy-strain relations, with Born mechanical stability criteria checked for all phases. Carrier mobilities at 300 K were calculated via deformation potential theory with only acoustic phonon scattering considered; the obtained mobility values represent theoretical upper limits. The carrier mobilities of Mo8X4M8 monolayers with different crystalline phases were determined according to the following expression μ2D=eℏ3C2DkBTm*mdE12

In this expression, C 2D denotes the 2D elastic modulus, obtained from the elastic constants C ij , while T = 300 K corresponds to the temperature. The effective mass along the transport direction is denoted as m*, with the average effective mass represented by md=mxmy . Additionally, the deformation potential constant E i is defined as E i = ΔE/δ. Here, ΔE denotes the energy level of the valence band maximum (VBM) or conduction band minimum (CBM) relative to the vacuum level, and δ = Δl/l represents the applied uniaxial strain. Phonon spectra were calculated using VASP combined with Phonopy on a 3 × 3 × 1 supercell (180 atoms) with a Γ-centered 2 × 2 × 1 q-mesh and 0.01 Å atomic shift. Electronic convergence thresholds were 10–8 eV (energy) and 10–9 eV (charge density), and acoustic sum rule (ASR) correction was enabled to remove spurious imaginary frequencies. NVT AIMD simulations at 300 K were performed on the same 3 × 3 × 1 supercell using the Nosé-Hoover thermostat with a 1 fs time step, giving a total 10 ps trajectory.

Results and Discussion

In this study, three stable atomic configurations of monolayer Mo8X4M8 (X≠M = S;Se) were identified through systematic structural exploration, each corresponding to a distinct crystalline phase characterized by different space group symmetries. As visualized in Figure , each monolayer features local six-member Mo-chalcogen ring units that resemble an hcp-like geometric motif. Importantly, this characteristic describes only the local atomic topology, not the global crystallographic symmetry of the entire sheet. The ordered ternary distribution of S and Se atoms on the upper and lower surface layers breaks the 6-fold rotational symmetry of an ideal hexagonal lattice, resulting in an overall orthorhombic crystal system belonging to the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2 space groups, respectively. Symmetry analysis and elastic constant calculations were both performed on the orthorhombic primitive cell enclosed by blue dashed lines in Figure , with all relaxed primitive cells satisfying a = b. The red dashed frame corresponds to the supercell used solely for carrier mobility calculations and is not employed for elastic simulations or symmetry analysis. Each arrangement features a sandwich-like architecture formed by three atomic layers, where Mo atoms occupy the central layer, while sulfur (S) and selenium (Se) atoms are allocated to the upper and lower surface layers. Importantly, the lattice constants of the three arrangements are recorded as 9.440 Å, 9.358 Å, 9.365 Å, 9.243 Å, 9.300 Å, and 9.307 Å, respectively. These measurements fall within the range reported previously for monolayer Mo8S12 (9.257 Å) and Mo8Se12 (9.562 Å). A comprehensive assessment of the phase stability in the Mo8X4M8 monolayer was conducted through cohesive energy calculations based on the expression: E coh = [E tot-(n Mo E Mo + n S E S + n Se E Se)]/(n Mo + n S + n Se). As presented in Table , all examined configurations possess negative cohesive energies, indicating favorable interatomic bonding within the Mo8X4M8 monolayer framework. Additionally, we calculated formation energies (E form) referenced to elemental Mo, S, and Se, as listed in Table . All six monolayers display negative formation energies (−0.426 to −0.472 eV/atom), confirming thermodynamically favorable synthesis from elemental precursors. Unlike disordered MoS2‑xSe x alloys, which have an inherent tendency toward anion segregation, the ordered S/Se anion stacking on the two surfaces of orthorhombic Mo8X4M8 breaks hexagonal symmetry and eliminates the driving force for anion segregation. This regular chalcogen arrangement suppresses phase separation into binary Mo8S12 and Mo8Se12, further supporting the intrinsic structural stability of these ternary monolayers. This conclusion is further corroborated by phonon spectrum analysis and AIMD simulations. Specifically, Figure demonstrates that the phonon dispersion curves for the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2-Mo8X4M8 monolayers lack imaginary frequencies across the full Brillouin zone, thus affirming their dynamical stability. Moreover, AIMD simulations are widely regarded as an effective approach to evaluate the thermal stability of materials. Accordingly, a 10 ps AIMD simulation was conducted in the canonical NVT ensemble at room temperature. The 3 × 3 × 1 supercell maintained its initial configuration for the duration of the simulation, showing no bond dissociation or noticeable structural distortion in the final state (Figure , inset). These results reflect only short-time scale stability under vacuum at 300 K, without consideration of high temperatures or atmospheric gases. Collectively, these findings confirm the intrinsic lattice stability of the proposed Mo8X4M8 monolayers under the simulated vacuum conditions.

To thoroughly assess the mechanical properties of various crystalline phases in Mo8X4M8 monolayers, we began by determining the linear elastic coefficients C ij by performing polynomial fitting on the energy-strain correlation curves. Notably, all elastic simulations were performed on the orthorhombic primitive cell outlined by blue dashed lines in Figure , where the geometric constraint a = b strictly enforces C 11 = C 22 in the orthorhombic elastic tensor. For this pseudohexagonal orthorhombic geometry, only two independent elastic constants (C 11 and C 12) require direct fitting, while the elastic constant C 66 is analytically derived via C 66 = (C 11–C 12)/2, with the corresponding values summarized in Table . Importantly, all obtained C ij values adhere to Born’s mechanical stability criteria applicable to 2D orthorhombic systems. Table compiles the calculated Y s values for the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2 phases of the Mo8S4Se8 and Mo8Se4S8 monolayers, which are 42.71, 46.77, 46.76, 52.31, 48.51, and 48.22 N/m, respectively. The diminished Y s further substantiates their enhanced mechanical pliability. Additionally, the derived ν values for the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2 phases of the Mo8S4Se8 and Mo8Se4S8 monolayers are 0.493, 0.454, 0.456, 0.384, 0.416, and 0.420, respectively, implying ductile mechanical behavior across all examined phases. Collectively, these simulation results offer a valuable theoretical basis for employing Mo8X4M8 monolayers in flexible electronics.

The distinct optoelectronic applications of 2D materials possessing direct band gaps originate from their exceptional light absorption capabilities and highly efficient radiative recombination processes. To investigate this phenomenon in greater detail, we performed an electronic structure study on various proposed crystalline phases of Mo8X4M8 monolayers employing multiple computational functionals, as illustrated in Figure . Initial evaluations with the PBE functional indicated direct band gaps spanning 0.57–0.80 eV across all configurations. To address the typical band gap underestimation associated with PBE in 2D systems, we implemented the HSE06 hybrid functional for improved accuracy. Focusing on the Cmmm-Mo8S4Se8 monolayer as a showcase (Figure a), the band structure obtained with HSE06 maintains a shape similar to the PBE result but yields an enlarged gap of 1.37 eV. This prompted a full reassessment of all monolayers using HSE06 (Figure b–f), with detailed band gap data provided in Table . Importantly, both the CBM and VBM reside at the Γ point for these monolayers. To evaluate the influence of spin–orbit coupling (SOC), we performed PBE + SOC band calculations for all six phases (Figure S1). A direct comparison between the PBE (black solid) and PBE + SOC (red dashed) bands confirms that SOC induces no splitting at the VBM and CBM for any structure, and the band-edge curvaturewhich governs carrier effective massesis nearly identical with and without SOC. Mild SOC splitting appears only in the deep valence bands far below the Fermi level (slightly more pronounced in the low-symmetry Cmm2 phases c and f), and thus does not contribute to charge transport or near-edge optical transitions. To elucidate the electronic features in greater depth, we further analyzed the orbital contributions of the CBM and VBM, with particular emphasis on the Cmmm phase. Analysis of the partial density of states (PDOS, Figure a) and the projected band structure (Figure S2a) indicates that in the Cmmm-Mo8S4Se8 monolayer, the CBM arises largely from the Mo dz orbitals, whereas the VBM is dominated by the S/Se px and py orbitals. A consistent orbital contribution pattern is seen across other configurations, as displayed in Figures b–f, S2b,c and S3. Moreover, optical properties assessments using HSE06 (Figure ) reveal strongly anisotropic absorption coefficients across the Mo8X4M8 monolayers, reaching magnitudes up to 105 cm–1 within the visible spectrumon par with MoS2 and MoSSe. , Clear disparities in peak intensity and energy between the x and y hcp axes confirm directional optical responses. This spectral divergence arises from the broken rotational symmetry of the hcp lattice and the anisotropic hybridization of Mo-d and S/Se-p orbital. Collectively, these findings highlight the promising potential of these materials for applications in photovoltaic devices and infrared photodetectors. Note that our absorption calculations employ the independent-particle approximation without GW-BSE excitonic corrections; therefore, all optoelectronic predictions presented here are qualitative and at a screening level.

Enhancing carrier transport efficiency is crucial for the effective incorporation of 2D semiconductor materials into next-generation microelectronic devices. Efficient carrier transport ensures that photogenerated charge carrierselectrons and holesrapidly participate in desired physical processes following light absorption, leading to a substantial enhancement in photoelectric conversion efficiency. Within the framework of deformation potential theory, the intrinsic carrier mobility is governed by three fundamental parameters: m*, Ei , and C 2D. As illustrated in Figure , orthogonal coordinate systems are defined for the three monolayer structuresCmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2-Mo8X4M8with the principal axes labeled x and y. The elastic moduli of these monolayers were derived from the analysis of their energy-strain relationships, with comprehensive data provided in Figures S4 and S5. Additionally, deformation potential constants were extracted through linear fitting of VBM and CBM energy shifts under ±1% strain (ε = – 0.010 ∼ + 0.010) applied along the x and y directions, with all Linear fitting adjusted R 2 values are presented in Figure S6. The calculated directional carrier mobilities (μ i ) and associated physical parameters (m*, Ei , and C 2D) are compiled in Table . A striking observation is the significant anisotropy across all transport parameters, with carrier mobility along the y-axis consistently surpassing that along the x-axis in every Mo8X4M8 monolayer examined. Notably, the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2-Mo8X4M8 monolayers exhibit outstanding hole mobilities in the y-direction, reaching 1.82 × 104, 1.33 × 104, 1.37 × 104, 1.03 × 104, 1.17 × 104, and 1.12 × 104 cm2V–1 s–1, respectively, which can be ascribed to their ultralow hole effective mass along this crystallographic direction. These y-direction hole mobilities not only exceed those of MoS2 and MoSSe monolayers (typically on the order of 102 cm2V–1 s–1) , but also reach magnitudes comparable to the highly anisotropic mobilities up to 104 cm2V–1 s–1 reported for phosphorene and Mo8S6Se6 monolayers. ,, As summarized in Table S1, phosphorene suffers poor structural stability, whereas all our Mo8X4M8 monolayers exhibit robust thermodynamic and thermal stability simultaneously with high hole mobility. Therefore, these Mo8X4M8 monolayers with improved carrier mobility hold promising potential for application in the construction of low-power and high-frequency FETs. As shown in Table , the hole mobilities along the y-axis (1.03–1.82 × 104 cm2V–1 s–1) are significantly higher than the electron mobilities (0.20–0.85 × 104 cm2V–1 s–1) for all Mo8X4M8 monolayers, revealing a pronounced imbalance between electron and hole transport capabilities. This mobility imbalance originates from intrinsic electronic orbital characteristics and carrier effective masses. The VBM is dominated by delocalized S/Se p x /p y orbitals, resulting in ultralight hole effective masses ranging from 0.055 to 0.075 m 0. In contrast, the CBM mainly originates from localized Mo dz orbitals, leading to significantly larger electron effective masses of 0.166 to 0.223 m 0. Although holes exhibit slightly larger deformation potentials, their ultralight effective masses predominantly govern in transport behavior, substantially enhancing hole mobility. These mobility values represent theoretical upper limits, as the deformation potential theory neglects optical phonons, defects, substrate interactions, and full electron–phonon coupling, and therefore cannot directly predict device-level transport performance. Studies involving full electron–phonon coupling in Janus Mo chalcogenides indicate that this method tends to overestimate intrinsic mobility. For optoelectronic devices, balanced electron–hole mobility facilitates efficient separation and collection of photogenerated carriers. The intrinsic p-type transport characteristic restricts the direct application of pristine Mo8X4M8 monolayers in bipolar photodetectors and solar cells. However, this transport imbalance can be tuned through strain engineering, compositional adjustment, or the construction of van der Waals heterostructures. Meanwhile, the exceptional hole mobility makes these monolayers excellent candidates for high-performance p-type FETs and hole-transport layers, where superior hole transport, rather than balanced bipolar mobility, is demanded. It is important to note that our mobility calculations consider only acoustic phonon scattering via deformation potential theory, neglecting optical phonon contributions. As shown in Figure , all monolayers possess abundant high-frequency optical phonon branches, which introduce additional carrier scattering pathways and inevitably reduce practical carrier mobility. Furthermore, the orthorhombic anisotropic lattice induces direction-dependent carrier-optical phonon coupling, modulating transport anisotropy. Therefore, all calculated mobilities in Table should be regarded as theoretical upper limits. Comprehensive Boltzmann transport simulations that incorporate full electron–phonon coupling are needed to quantitatively assess the scattering effects of optical phonons; such elaborate calculations are not included in the current study and are proposed as a valuable direction for future research.

It should be noted that the optical absorption spectra in this work are calculated within the independent-particle approximation (IPA), without GW-BSE many-body excitonic corrections. Carrier mobilities are evaluated considering only acoustic phonon scattering via deformation potential theory, while neglecting optical phonon coupling, exciton dissociation dynamics, and radiative or nonradiative carrier lifetimes. Accurate quantification of exciton binding energies, anisotropic photoluminescence spectra, and carrier lifetimes requires dedicated GW-BSE calculations and real-time electron–phonon dynamics simulations, − which we defer to future studies.

Conclusion

In this work, we systematically predict and elucidate the intrinsic physical features of the Cmmm, Amm2, and Cmm2-Mo8X4M8 monolayers. Comprehensive evaluations, encompassing cohesive energy, formation energy analysis, phonon dispersion calculations, AIMD simulations, and elastic constant characterization, collectively confirmed the lattice stability of these monolayers under the studied conditions. HSE06 calculations reveal band gaps ranging from 1.32 to 1.49 eV, with S-rich Mo8Se4S8 possessing wider gaps than Se-rich Mo8S4Se8. PDOS and projected band structures reveal that the VBM originates from delocalized S/Se px/py orbitals, while the CBM is predominantly contributed by localized Mo dz2 orbitals, endowing the monolayers with ductile mechanical responses and visible-light absorption coefficients on the order of 105 cm–1. These optical results exclude excitonic corrections, and all associated optoelectronic predictions represent preliminary qualitative screening conclusions. Notably, due to the low hole effective mass along the y direction, all configurations exhibit anisotropic hole mobility up to 104 cm2 V–1 s–1, significantly exceeding electron mobility along the x direction and resulting in intrinsic p-type characteristics. Overall, the composition-dependent tunable properties render Mo8X4M8 monolayers promising candidates for flexible optoelectronics and FETs, particularly p-type field-effect transistors, broadband photodetectors, and hole transport layers in solar cells. Nevertheless, these conclusions are purely theoretical; experimental synthesis and device characterization are still necessary to verify their practical performance in real optoelectronic applications. Precise evaluations of excitonic effects and carrier dynamics will be reserved for future investigations.

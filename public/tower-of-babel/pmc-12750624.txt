Learning curve analysis of unilateral biportal endoscopic technique in the treatment of the lumbar disc herniation combined with spinal stenosis
Authors: Xia Jin, Bo Li, Liang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo explore the learning curve and clinical efficacy of the unilateral biportal endoscopy (UBE) technique applied in the lumbar disc herniation combined with spinal stenosis.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted on 112 consecutive cases of lumbar disc herniation combined with spinal stenosis who received UBE at the Beijing Luhe Hospital, Capital Medical University from December 2022 to January 2024. The cohort was divided into the early group and the late group by using the cumulative summation (CUSUM). The baseline data, surgery-related data, visual analogue scale (VAS) scores, Oswestry disability index (ODI) scores and complications of the two groups were statistically compared.ResultsTaking the cutoff point of the learning curve as the grouping basis, patients were divided into the early group (25 cases) and the late group (87 cases), with the coefficient of determination R2 = 0.98. The comparison of baseline data, ODI scores and complications between the two groups of patients was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). The average operation time and single incision length in the late group were shorter than those in the early group, with a statistically significant (P < 0.05). In addition, compared with the early group, the average postoperative one-month VAS score of the late group significantly decreased (P < 0.05).ConclusionsUBE for the treatment of lumbar disc herniation combined with spinal stenosis can relieve pain, alleviate short-term symptoms, and reduce the operative time with clinical experience accumulated. The learning curve analysis in this study revealed that the operative competence in treating these complicated cases was obtained after primary attempt of twenty-five cases.Keywords: Unilateral biportal endoscopy, Lumbar disc herniation, Lumbar spinal stenosis, Learning curve, Clinical efficacy

Introduction

Lumbar disc herniation (LDH) is a common degenerative spinal disease caused by the rupture of the annulus fibrosus of the lumbar intervertebral disc, allowing the nucleus pulposus to protrude into the spinal canal and compress adjacent nerve roots or the cauda equina, with lumbosacral pain as the predominant symptom [1]– [2]. Lumbar spinal stenosis (LSS) results from a reduction in the spinal canal volume due to various reasons, leading to compression of the dural sac and nerve roots. It frequently coexists with LDH, and their interaction significantly effects patients’ functional mobility and quality of life [3]– [4]. Worth mentioning that traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has made significant contributions to the prevention of osteoporosis and the enhancement of physical health. Its therapeutic approaches, rooted in holistic principles, have demonstrated efficacy in promoting bone health and physiological resilience. Recently, TCM (e.g. Icaritin) has garnered increasing attention in the fields of molecular biology and genetic engineering, where its bioactive compounds are being explored for their potential mechanisms of action at the molecular level, offering promising avenues for integrative medical research [5, 6]. When conservative treatments fail or symptoms recur, surgical intervention is particularly required. In recent years, minimally invasive methods and endoscopic techniques have expanded the therapeutic strategies in spinal surgery. Among these, unilateral biportal endoscopy (UBE) offers advantages such as minimal invasive, flexible to operate, and rapid postoperative recovery, gaining widespread acceptance among clinicians and patients. It is gradually replacing conventional open surgery as the preferred treatment for LDH combined with LSS. As an endoscopic technique with proven efficacy and broad applicability, previous studies have reported on the clinical effectiveness and safety of UBE for LDH. However, there is a lack of evidence regarding the learning curve and surgical outcomes of UBE in managing complex cases of LDH combined with LSS [7–9].

Therefore, this study is to investigate the learning curve using the cumulative summation (CUSUM) analysis for UBE in treating LDH with LSS, evaluating the effect for surgeons to achieve clinical proficiency in this procedure, thereby providing theoretical references for the field of minimally invasive spinal surgery.

Methods

General data

A total of 112 patients with lumbar disc herniation combined with lumbar spinal stenosis treated by UBE were enrolled at Beijing Luhe Hospital from December 2022 to January 2024. All UBE procedures were performed by the same surgeon with extensive clinical experience in spinal surgery and endoscopic proficiency. This surgeon had independently completed over 1,000 open surgeries or other types of endoscopic technique.

Among them, there were 59 males and 53 females, with a mean age of 57.1 ± 8.2 years and an average disease duration of 37.0 ± 8.7 months. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Luhe Hospital (2024-LHYK-071-02) and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Low back and/or leg pain with concordant imaging findings (MRI) for the same affected segment, conforming to the diagnosis of the LDH and LSS [10]– [11]; (2) poor results of the conservative treatment or recurrent symptoms; (3) with a complete clinical and imaging data, and a continuous follow-up for at least 12 months. Exclusion criteria: (1) Lumbar infections, tumors or tuberculosis; (2) severe lumbar spondylolisthesis, scoliosis, or other deformities; (3) primary operative history of lumbar vertebra; (4) poor general condition precluding tolerance of surgery.

Operative procedure

Under the general anesthesia, patients were positioned in the prone position. A C-arm fluoroscopy was used to confirm the puncture site and operative level. Two puncture points were marked 1.5 cm lateral to midline, at the lower edge of the upper lamina and the upper edge of the lower lamina of the target segment respectively. A 1 to 1.5 cm longitudinal incision was made at the puncture points, with the upper incision serving as the endoscope insertion and the lower incision as the surgical instrument channel. Then the blunt dissection was performed, and a plasma electrocautery device was used to cauterize the soft tissue from the lamina surface. Following identification of the target intervertebral space, a portion of the lower edge of the upper lamina, the upper edge of the lower lamina, and the facet joint were resected using a Kerrison rongeur or an electric drill. The ligamentum flavum on the surface of the dural sac was removed to expose the deep layer. After gently retracting the nerve root and dura to the contralateral side, the ruptured nucleus pulposus was excised using a Kerrison rongeur, and the nerve root was thoroughly decompressed. Radiofrequency ablation was used to reshape the damaged annulus fibrosus and ligaments, and the incisions were closed. Worth mentioning that the drainage tube was not routinely placed postoperatively.

Outcomes evaluation

Clinical data of enrolled patients was evaluated with routine follow-up examinations performed at 1, 2, 3-, 6-, 9-, and 12-months post-surgery.

Baseline characteristics included age, gender, diabetes mellitus, peripheral vascular disease, body mass index (BMI), disease duration, surgical level, spinal stenosis grading, and follow-up duration. The lumbar disc herniation was divided into the protrusion, extrusion and sequestration according to the damage degree of the nucleus pulposus and lumbar annulus. The severity of spinal stenosis was assessed using the Lee Grade system based on T2-weighted axial MRI images, with Grade Ⅰ to Ⅲ corresponding to mild, moderate, and severe LSS, respectively [12].

Surgical data encompassed operative time, intraoperative blood loss, incision length and time to ambulation post-surgery. Clinical outcomes included the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores and Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores assessed preoperatively, at 1-month post-surgery, and at the final follow-up. The VAS score, used to evaluate lumbar pain, ranged from 0 to 10, with higher scores indicating more severe pain. The ODI score, comprising 10 items (including walking, sitting, standing, sleeping, etc.), ranged from 0 to 5 per item, with a total score of 50 and higher scores indicated greater lumbar functional impairment. Postoperative complications included infection, epidural hematoma, dural sac tear, and recurrence at the same segment.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 21.0 software. Continuous data were tested for normality and expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with intergroup comparisons conducted using the independent samples t-test. Furthermore, the paired t-test was conducted to compare the clinical outcomes between the pre- and post-operation. Categorical data were presented as counts (percentages), and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the chi-square test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The CUSUM analysis was conducted using NumPy polyfit in Python 3.9.6. The learning curve for operative time as an observation factor was investigated using the CUSUM analysis, with the formula defined as follows: CUSUMi = CUSUMi−1 + (Xi - µ), where i represents the case number and the initialization was set at 0, Xi indicates the operative time for the individual case, and µ represents the mean operative time. Then, a scatter plot of the learning curve was generated, and correspondent curve fitting was performed. The coefficient of determination (R2) was used to evaluate the fitting goodness of the learning curve, with a value close to 1 indicating an optimal fit.

Results

Learning curve analysis

With an increasing number of surgical cases, the operative time for the UBE technique in treating LDH combined with LSS demonstrated a gradual decreasing trend. The fitted equation was: y = −3.10 × 10⁻5 × 4 + 7.59 × 10⁻3 × 3 – 6.47 × 10⁻1 × 2 + 20.6x + 4.25, with a R2 of 0.98, indicating a good fit (Fig. 1). Based on the significant inflection point, the CUSUM analysis identified the 25th case as the cutoff point of the learning curve.

Discussion

Conservative treatment is typically the first-line approach for LDH. Surgical intervention is considered in cases presenting with severe neurological deficits, including lower limb weakness, numbness, sensory impairment, or muscle atrophy. Cauda equina syndrome manifesting or persistent worsening symptoms were also operative indications. LSS exacerbates the degeneration of disc herniation, leading to increased pressure and irritation of nerve roots, with restricted blood supply to neural tissues causing ischemia and hypoxia. Traditional open laminectomy decompression is an effective method for these combined injuries, allowing complete visualization and thorough removal of compressive laminae and herniated lumbar disc, as well as expansion of the spinal canal and nerve root canal. However, it can result in reduced vertebral stability, increased risk of vertebral slippage or deformity, excessive soft tissue damage, and a relatively prolonged recovery period [13]– [14].

In recent years, several endoscopic techniques have gained prominence due to their advantages, including minimal invasion, quick recovery, precision, safety, and preservation of spinal structure. Of these, the UBE and percutaneous endoscopic transforaminal discectomy (PETD) were widely used [15]– [16]. Compared to PETD, UBE employs separate channels for operation and observation, providing a broader surgical field exposure and clearer differentiation of tissue layers in a water-medium environment, which ensures its unique advantages in complex cases involving posterior compression, disc extrusion, or revision.

The learning curve reflects the efficiency with which a beginner acquires a specific skill over a period, determined by the case number to achieve the proficiency. The CUSUM analysis, a statistical approach based on cumulative averages, is widely utilized in the orthopedic field to summarize clinical experience and provide technical references [17]– [18]. It was novel and unique for this study using the CUSUM analysis to investigate the UBE in the treatment of the LDH combined with the LSS.

A meta-analysis comprising seven clinical studies indicated that the average operative time in the treatment of the LDH using UBE technique was 74.4 min [19]. In our study, enrolled patients had LDH combined with LSS (90.2% of cases with Lee Grade Ⅰ and Ⅱ), which brought great challenges for endoscopic attempts. In these pathological conditions, the operative field is limited, lumbar degenerates severely, and the identification of the laminar gap becomes difficult. Additionally, the stenotic region is often accompanied by osteophyte formation, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, and dural sac adhesions, further elevating the procedural complexity [20–22]. Although UBE is an alternative treatment for lumbar spinal stenosis, careful consideration should be held for multi-segment or severe cases. Our results demonstrated that after the initial attempt, the operative time significantly decreased from an average of 93.2 min for the first 25 cases to 80.9 min for the subsequent 87 cases. It is anticipated that with accumulation of proficiency and experience, the operative time will further decrease. Recent research showed a spinal surgeon at his early stage of UBE technique and the average operation time was 97.9 ± 34.7 min, which was clearly higher than our study [23]. It was recommended that beginners performed the UBE technique under the guidance of experienced mentors, and prioritized cases involving a single segment, without severe adhesions or deformities.

This study revealed that, compared to preoperative data, both the early and late groups exhibited obvious reductions in VAS and ODI scores after surgery (p < 0.05). However, at one-month post-surgery, the VAS score in the early group was significantly higher than that in the late group (2.72 ± 1.24 vs. 2.13 ± 1.19, p < 0.05). This difference attributed to the surgeon clinical experience, despite being proficient in endoscopic techniques, performing relatively more extensive dissection of the soft tissue at early stage to achieve adequate visualization and decompression. With accumulating experience, tissue resection and nerve decompression became more precise and controlled, reducing the incidence of the early postoperative back pain. No statistically significant differences were observed in long-term outcomes between the two groups (p >0.05). The surgeon should be careful to intraoperative exploration of pathological tissue structures, combined with preoperative imaging, ensuring complete removal of the nucleus pulposus and thorough nerve decompression, enhancing the overall surgical safety and facilitated early patient recovery, which was consistent with the prior study [24].

Maintaining adequate bone quality is of great importance for the postoperative rehabilitation of patients undergoing UBE technique. Bone integrity facilitates optimal recovery by providing structural support and stability, which are critical for restoring mobility and minimizing complications. High bone density and strength enhance the efficacy of rehabilitation protocols, reducing the risk of secondary injuries and promoting long-term functional outcomes in patients recovering from operative intervention [6]. Research has proved that certain bioactive compounds promote fracture healing and late-stage rehabilitation by mediating hormone receptors and regulating cellular proliferation and differentiation. These compounds modulate signaling pathways critical for osteoblast and osteoclast activity, thereby enhancing bone remodeling and tissue regeneration [5].

Complications associated with UBE techniques commonly include infection, epidural hematoma, recurrence of lumbar disc herniation, dural sac tears, and nerve root lesions [25]. Among these, epidural hematoma is the most frequent complication related to UBE. Kim et al. reported that epidural hematomas existed in 23.6% of patients according to the postoperative MRI [26]. However, only 1.9% of cases resulted in neurological compression leading to symptoms such as paralysis, voiding dysfunction, or neuropathic pain. In this study, the rate of epidural hematoma was 1.8% (2/112). Key strategies include meticulous hemostasis, the use of hemostatic agents, and avoiding excessive intraoperative water pressure. The UBE technique minimizes disruption to the posterior column stability and reduces damage to paraspinal muscles, ligamentous complexes, and facet joints.

In recent years, advancements in endoscopic techniques have accelerated the integration of the UBE with posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF), resulting in a progressive procedure known as UBE lumbar interbody fusion (ULIF). This approach has expanded the indications for UBE in the treatment of complex lumbar spine disorders. Liu et al. demonstrated that ULIF offered multiple benefits over PLIF in the management of degenerative lumbar diseases, including reduced blood loss, lower inflammatory response, and faster postoperative recovery [27]. Furthermore, the interbody fusion rates of the two techniques were statistically close.

There are several limitations worth mentioning. Firstly, this study was a single-center retrospective analysis, which did not stratify cases based on the type of LDH, potentially introducing selection bias that may impact the results. Then, all procedures were performed by a single surgeon with extensive experience in open spinal surgery (over 1,000 cases) and prior endoscopic skills, of which the findings of our study may not be generalizable to surgeons with varying levels of experience. Thirdly, our study lacked the assessment of the hidden blood loss among these groups, which might influence the perioperative bleeding. Lastly, the sample size of ours was relatively small and the follow-up period was relatively short. Future research involving the multi-center, large-sample prospective studies, and stratified by surgeon seniority, should be conducted to minimize selection bias and provide more generalizable and representative conclusions. A 12-month follow-up was short for degenerative conditions and long-term research is awaiting to perform.

Conclusion

UBE technique provided a short-term pain relief and reduced operative time with experience in this cohort. However, managing the complex cases required surgeons to possess proficient endoscopic skills, along with full preoperative evaluation and careful consideration of surgical indications. Using the CUSUM analysis, this study found that for surgeons with substantial clinical experience and mastery of endoscopic techniques, operative time significantly decreased after surpassing the learning curve plateau of 25 cases.

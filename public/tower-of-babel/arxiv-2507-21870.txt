Spreading speeds in rapidly and slowly varying almost periodic media
Xing Liang, Linfeng Xu, Tao Zhou

Abstract




Abstract
This paper is concerned with the asymptotic behavior of the spreading speeds of Fisher-KPP equations in rapidly and slowly varying almost periodic settings respectively.
Thanks to the variational formula for the spreading speeds involving generalized principal eigenvalue, we are able to concentrate on the homogenization problem of certain Hamilton-Jacobi equations.
We will present also the limits and the convergence rate of the spreading speeds.
At last, we will consider the effect on the spreading speeds after adding a fixed reaction term.

Key words
Spreading speed  Almost periodic  reaction-diffusion equations  Hamilton-Jacobi equation  Homogenization

Limit behavior of the generalized principal eigenvalue when
L\to 0

In this section, we mainly concern with the limit behavior of the generalized principal eigenvalue as
L\to 0
.
In the the rest of this paper,
we will sometimes drop the subscript of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
when it does not lead to confusion.

For the aim of simplifying the expression of the equation,
now let us define

H(x,y,p,X)=a^{ij}(x)X_{ij}+a^{ij}(x)p_{i}p_{j}+b^{i}(x)p_{i}+c(x)+\tilde{c}(y).

Then
\lambda(L,p)
is the effective Hamiltonian of the following problem

H(\frac{x}{L},x,Du-p,D^{2}u)=\lambda(L,p).

Consider the approximation problem

H(\frac{x}{L},x,Du_{L}-p,D^{2}u_{L})=\varepsilon u_{L}.

(4.1)

Using the doubling variable technique , let

v_{L}(x)=v_{0}(x)+L^{2}v_{2}(x,\frac{x}{L}),

where
v_{2}=v_{2}(x,y)
is bounded.
Substitute
v_{L}
into (
4.1
) as a test function, and denote

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v_{L}-H(y,x,Dv_{L}-p,D^{2}v_{L})=&-a^{ij}(y)D_{y_{i}y_{j}}v_{2}-a^{ij}(y)D_{ij}v_{0}\\
&-a^{ij}(y)(D_{i}v_{0}-p_{i})(D_{j}v_{0}-p_{j})-b^{i}(y)(D_{i}v_{0}-p_{i})\\
&-c(y)-\tilde{c}(x)+\varepsilon v_{0}+O(L).\end{aligned}

To let
v_{L}
be closed to
u_{L}
,
the unique bounded solution of (
4.1
),
A natural idea is to let the following equation hold:

a^{ij}(y)D_{y_{i}y_{j}}v_{2}+a^{ij}(y)D_{ij}v_{0}+a^{ij}(y)(D_{i}v_{0}-p_{i})(D_{j}v_{0}-p_{j})+b^{i}(y)(D_{i}v_{0}-p_{i})+c(y)=\varepsilon v_{0}-\tilde{c}(x).

Observe that the right-hand side of the above equation only depends on
x
,
therefore we are suggested to choose
v_{2}
such that the left-hand side is independent of
y
.
That is, regarding
x
as parameter,

is a corrector of the above Hamiltonian.
Note that the above Hamiltonian is linear for
v_{2}
, we now turn to study the following problem

a^{ij}(x)D_{ij}v(x)+F(x)=\iota(F),

(4.2)

where
F
is almost periodic and
\iota(F)\in\mathbb{R}
.
We will prove that for any almost periodic
F
, one could define
\iota(F)
the effective Hamiltonian of the above macroscopic problem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
F
is almost periodic.
Then there exists unique constant
\iota(F)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
for any
\delta>0
,
the problem

\begin{aligned} \iota(F)-\delta<a^{ij}(x)D_{ij}v(x)+F(x)<\iota(F)+\delta\end{aligned}

admits a viscosity solution
v
that is bounded and Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, by Perron’s method, there exists
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
satisfies

a^{ij}(x)D_{ij}v_{\varepsilon}+F(x)=\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon},

with estimates

\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}Dv_{\varepsilon}\|+\|\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}\|\leq C\|F\|.

and

\frac{1}{R^{n}}\int_{B(x,R^{n})}|D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}dx\leq C\|F\|

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
.

Now for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\varphi(x)=v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(x+z)
, then
\varphi
solves

a^{ij}(x)D_{ij}\varphi-\varepsilon\varphi=\text{tr}((A(x+z)-A(x))D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(x+z))+F(x+z)-F(x).

By classical elliptic regularity theory, we have

\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z)\|\leq C(\|A(\cdot)-A(\cdot+z)\|+\|F(\cdot)-F(\cdot+z)\|).

Note that
A
and
F
are almost periodic, for any
R
, there exists
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\tilde{z}|\leq R
such that

\|A(\cdot+z)-A(\cdot+\tilde{z})\|+\|F(\cdot+z)-F(\cdot+\tilde{z})\|\leq\rho(R;(A,F))

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Using this estimate then we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z)\|&\leq&\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+\tilde{z})\|+\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+\tilde{z})-v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z)\|\\
&\leq&\varepsilon\|Dv_{\varepsilon}\|\|\tilde{z}\|+C\rho(R;(A,F))\\
&\leq&C\sqrt{\varepsilon}R+C\rho(R;(A,F)).\end{array}

Then we have

\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z)\|\leq C\Theta_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon};(A,F))\to 0\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+},

which implies that
\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}
converges to some constant.

The uniqueness of
\iota(F)
follows from the same argument as ?THM?

Proof.

Fix

Using the inequality

The spreading speeds of (

|\lambda(L,p)-\lambda(p)|\leq C_{\sigma}\Theta_{\sigma}(L;(A,B,c)),

where

depends only on
\sigma,p
and the coefficients of (
1.1
).

Proof.

We have proved that for any
L>0
and

there exists
p^{L}
such that

\omega(e;L)=\frac{\lambda(L,p^{L})}{p^{L}\cdot e}

with

0<m<p^{L}\cdot e<M<\infty,\quad|p^{L}|\leq M

for all
L>0
and some

.
On one hand,
for any

with

,
we have

\omega(e;0)(p\cdot e)\leq\lambda(p)\leq\lambda(L,p)+C_{\sigma}\Theta_{\sigma}(L;(A,B,c))

for some

provided by the above arguments.
Since

\omega(e;0)\leq\frac{\lambda(L,p)}{p\cdot e}+C_{\sigma}\Theta_{\sigma}(L;(A,B,c)).

Take

to get

\omega(e;0)\leq\omega(e;L)+C_{\sigma}\Theta_{\sigma}(L;(A,B,c)).

On the other hand, using

\omega(e;L)(p\cdot e)\leq\lambda(L,p)\leq\lambda(p)+C_{\sigma}\Theta_{\sigma}(L;(A,B,c)),

one could obtain an inverse inequality.

If

are periodic,
then one could easily prove that

5
Limit behavior of the generalized principal eigenvalue when

In this section, we are going to study the limit behavior of the generalized principal eigenvalue

as

Observe that once we have established the limit and the convergence rate of the generalized principal eigenvalues,
one could easily prove ?THM?
and ?THM?
by a similar argument with the case when

,

we will only present the asymptotic behavior of the generalized principal eigenvalues in this section.

We first show that one could approximate the generalized principal eigenvalues of

by a new Hamiltonian using Hopf-Cole transformation.
Assume that

Let

is the effective Hamiltonian of the following macroscopic problem

H(x,Lx,Dv-p,\frac{1}{L}D^{2}v)=\lambda.

(5.1)

Therefore we could use (
5.1

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the limit

\Lambda(p)=\lim\limits_{L\to+\infty}\lambda(L,p)

exists and it is the effective Hamiltonian of the problem

\overline{H}(x,Du-p)=\Lambda(p),

(5.2)

where
\overline{H}(x,q)
is the effective Hamiltonian of the following macroscopic problem

H(x,y,D_{y}\psi(y)+q,D_{y}^{2}\psi(y))=\overline{H}(x,q).

(5.3)

Furthermore, the convergence
\lambda(L,p)\to\Lambda(p)
as

is locally uniformly with respect to
p\in\mathbb{R}

Proof.

Fixed
\varepsilon>0
,
for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
L>0
,
let
v_{p}^{L}
the unique solution of

(5.4)

By Bernstein’s method,
there exists

which depends only on the coefficients of the equation and

Therefore,

is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous.
Therefore Arzela-Ascoli theorem tells that,
up to convergent subsequence,

converges to some

as

.

We will show that

is indeed an approximate corrector that characterizes the limit of the generalized principal eigenvalues.
In detail,
for any

.
To find out the equation that

solves,

a

approximate corrector of the equation,

Moreover,
\|\psi\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C

.
Now let

Since

converges to

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v_{p}^{L}(x_{L})&=H(x_{L},Lx_{L},Dv_{p}^{L}-p,\frac{1}{L}D^{2}v_{p}^{L})\\

\leq&o(1)+\delta\end{aligned}

as

.
Therefore we obtain that

Let

is a viscosity supersolution of

Define the auxiliary function

\begin{aligned} \Phi(x,y,z)&:=\varepsilon v^{L}(x)-\varepsilon v(y)-\psi_{\delta}(Lx;x,2L^{\beta}(z-y))/L\\
&-L^{\beta}|x-y|^{2}-L^{\beta}|x-z|^{2}-\eta\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},\end{aligned}

where
\eta\in(0,1)
,
\beta=\tau/(2\tau+1)
and
\delta=L^{-\theta}
with
\theta=(\tau+1)/(2\tau+1)
.
From the boundedness of
v^{L},v
and
\psi_{\delta}
, we could assume that
\Phi
attains its maximum in some point
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})
.
Note that we could assume that this maximum is strict by adding to
\Phi
a smooth function vanishing together with its first and second derivatives at
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})
.

From
\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})\geq\Phi(0,0,0)
, we have

\begin{aligned} \eta\sqrt{1+|\hat{x}|^{2}}&\leq\varepsilon(v^{L}(\hat{x})-v^{L}(0))-\varepsilon(v(\hat{y})-v(0))-L^{\beta}|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}-L^{\beta}|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|^{2}+\eta\\
&\quad-(\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))-\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},0))/L-(\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},0)-\psi_{\delta}(0;0,0))/L\\
&\leq C-L^{\beta}(|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}+|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|^{2})+CL^{\beta+\frac{\theta}{\tau+1}-1}(|\hat{z}-\hat{x}|+|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|)+CL^{\theta-1}\\
&\leq C+CL^{\beta+\frac{2\theta}{\tau+1}-2}+CL^{\theta-1}\leq C\end{aligned}

for
L\geq 1
. Here we have used Young’s inequality in the last second inequality.

Inequality
\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})\geq\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{x})
gives

\begin{aligned} L^{\beta}|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|^{2}&\leq(\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))-\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y})))/L\\
&\leq CL^{\beta}|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|/L^{1-\frac{\theta}{\tau+1}}.\end{aligned}

Therefore we obtain that

|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|\leq CL^{\frac{\theta}{\tau+1}-1}=CL^{-2\beta}.

Moreover, from inequality
\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})\geq\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{x},\hat{z})
we obtain that

|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|\leq CL^{-\beta}

for
L\geq 1
by similar arguments.
The above inequalities tell that

|\hat{y}-\hat{z}|\leq CL^{-\beta}.

which implies that
L^{\beta}|\hat{y}-\hat{z}|
is uniformly bounded in
L
.

We claim that

\varepsilon v^{L}(\hat{x})\leq\overline{H}(\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))+CL^{-\beta}+C\eta,

(5.19)

where
C
only depends on
H
.
Indeed, observe that by the definition of
\Phi
, the function

x\mapsto\Phi(x,\hat{y},\hat{z})

has a maximum at
\hat{x}
.
Using
v^{L}(x)-\varepsilon^{-1}\Phi(x,\hat{y},\hat{z})
as a subsolution at
\hat{x}
to

\varepsilon v^{L}-H(x,Lx,Dv^{L},D^{2}v^{L}/L)=0,

then we have

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v^{L}(\hat{x})&\leq H(\hat{x},L\hat{x},D_{y}\psi_{\delta}+D_{x}\psi_{\delta}/L+2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y})+2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{z})+\eta\hat{x}/\sqrt{1+|\hat{x}|^{2}},\\
&\quad D_{y}^{2}\psi_{\delta}+2D_{xy}\psi_{\delta}/L+D_{x}^{2}\psi_{\delta}/L^{2}+4L^{\beta-1}+\frac{\eta}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}L})\\
&\leq\delta\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))+\overline{H}(\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))+4CL^{\beta}(|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|)+C\eta\\
&\quad+C|D_{x}\psi_{\delta}|/L+C|D_{x}^{2}\psi_{\delta}|/L^{2}+C|D_{xy}\psi_{\delta}|/L+CL^{\beta-1}+C\eta/L\\
&\leq\overline{H}(\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))+\delta^{\frac{\tau}{\tau+1}}+CL^{-\beta}+C\eta+CL^{\theta-1}+CL^{\beta-2}+CL^{\beta-1}\\
&\leq\overline{H}(\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))+CL^{-\beta}+C\eta.\end{aligned}

Next we claim that

\varepsilon v(\hat{y})\geq\overline{H}(2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y}),\hat{y})-CL^{-\beta}.

(5.20)

Introduce

\Psi(y,\xi):=\varepsilon v(y)+\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\xi))/L+L^{\beta}|\hat{x}-y|^{2}+\alpha|y-\xi|^{2}+\eta|y-\hat{y}|^{2}.

Then there exists
y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the function
\Psi
has a global minimuum at the point
(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})
.

Using inequalities
\Psi(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})\leq\Psi(y_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}),\Psi(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})\leq\Psi(\hat{x},\hat{x})
we obtain that

\alpha|y_{\alpha}-\xi_{\alpha}|\leq CL^{-\beta}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\hat{x}-y_{\alpha}|\leq CL^{-\beta}/\sqrt{1+1/\alpha}

respectively.
Moreover,
from the above estimates one could find that as
\alpha\to+\infty
,

(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})
converges,
up to subsequence,
to
(\beta,\beta)
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\alpha\to+\infty
.
Note that
\Psi(\beta,\beta)=\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\Psi(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})\leq\Psi(\hat{y},\hat{y})
,
and
(\hat{y},\hat{y})
is a strict global minimum of the map
y\mapsto\Psi(y,y)
,
we conclude that
(y_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})\to(\hat{y},\hat{y})
as
\alpha\to+\infty
.
Define
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\phi(y)=\varepsilon v(y)-\Psi(y,\xi_{\alpha})
,
we see that
\varepsilon v-\phi
has a global minimum at the point
y=y_{\alpha}
and therefore we compute that

D\phi(y_{\alpha})=2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-y_{\alpha})+2\alpha(\xi_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha})+2\eta(\hat{y}-y_{\alpha}).

By the viscosity supersolution test, we have

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v(y_{\alpha})&\geq\overline{H}(y_{\alpha},2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-y_{\alpha})+2\alpha(\xi_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha})+2\eta(\hat{y}-y_{\alpha}))\\
&\geq\overline{H}(\hat{y},2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y}))-C|\hat{y}-y_{\alpha}|-CL^{\beta}|y_{\alpha}-\hat{y}|-C\alpha|y_{\alpha}-\xi_{\alpha}|\\
&\geq\overline{H}(\hat{y},2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y}))-CL^{-\beta}-C(1+L^{\beta})|y_{\alpha}-\hat{y}|.\end{aligned}

Then (
5.20
) follows from letting
\alpha\to+\infty
.

Now combine (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we obtain that

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v^{L}(\hat{x})-\varepsilon v(\hat{y})&\leq\overline{H}(\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))-\overline{H}(\hat{y},2L^{\beta}(\hat{x}-\hat{y}))+CL^{-\beta}+C\eta\\
&\leq CL^{\beta}|\hat{z}-\hat{x}|+C|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|+CL^{-\beta}+C\eta\\
&\leq CL^{-\beta}+C\eta.\end{aligned}

Using the fact that
\Phi(x,x,x)\leq\Phi(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{z})
we obtain

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon v^{L}(x)-\varepsilon v(x)&\leq CL^{-\beta}-\psi_{\delta}(L\hat{x};\hat{x},2L^{\beta}(\hat{z}-\hat{y}))/L+\psi_{\delta}(Lx;x,0)/L+C\eta+\eta\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\\
&\leq CL^{-\beta}+C\eta+\eta\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}.\end{aligned}

Let
\eta\to 0
then we obtain one side of the result. The other side follows from an analogous way and we omit it here.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

|\lambda(L,p)-\Lambda(p)|\leq C(p)L^{\frac{-\tau}{2\tau+1}}
for some

.

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\lambda(L,p)-\Lambda(p)|&\leq&|\lambda(L,p)-\varepsilon v_{p}^{L}(0)|+|\varepsilon v_{p}^{L}(0)-\varepsilon v_{p}(0)|+|\varepsilon v_{p}(0)-\Lambda(p)|\\
&\leq&|\lambda(L,p)-\varepsilon v_{p}^{L}(0)|+|\varepsilon v_{p}(0)-\Lambda(p)|+C(p)L^{\frac{-\tau}{2\tau+1}}.\end{array}

Let
\varepsilon\to 0
then the conclusion follows.
∎

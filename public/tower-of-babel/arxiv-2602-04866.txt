Homological Mirror Symmetry for orbifold log Calabi-Yau surfaces
Bogdan Simeonov

Abstract
. We construct mirror abstract Lefschetz fibrations associated to a class of surfaces with cyclic quotient singularities which we call effective. These surfaces can be obtained by contracting disjoint chains of smooth rational curves inside the anticanonical cycle D D of a smooth log Calabi-Yau surface ( Y , D ) (Y,D) with maximal boundary and considering the result as an orbifold. The Fukaya-Seidel categories of these abstract Lefschetz fibrations admit semiorthogonal decompositions akin to the ones described via the derived special McKay correspondence of Ishii and Ueda [ 21 ] . We apply this construction to establish an equivalence at the large volume limit between the derived category of an effective orbifold log Calabi-Yau surface with points of type 1 k ​ ( 1 , 1 ) \frac{1}{k}(1,1) and the Fukaya-Seidel category of its mirror Lefschetz fibration. We also compare the abstract construction to an explicit Landau-Ginzburg model defined by a Laurent polynomial associated to a toric degeneration in the case of the family of hypersurfaces X k + 1 ⊂ ℙ ⁡ ( 1 , 1 , 1 , k ) X_{k+1}\subset\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,k) . The hypersurfaces X k + 1 X_{k+1} admit a non-trivial moduli of complex structures, which we compare with an open subset of the space of symplectic structures on the total space of the mirror Landau-Ginzburg model via a mirror map built out of intrinsic quantities in a non-exact Fukaya-Seidel category.

0.1.1.
Context: mirror symmetry and orbifold del Pezzo surfaces

Mirror symmetry has been applied in other forms in an attempt to classify orbifold del Pezzo surfaces. While the smooth del Pezzo surfaces fall into exactly
10
deformation families, an analogous classification for del Pezzo surfaces admitting at most cyclic quotient singularities remains an open problem. A promising approach using mirror symmetry was laid out in
[
1
]
, which describes a conjectural correspondence between
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein deformation classes of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces which admit toric degenerations and mutation-equivalence classes of Fano polygons. Additionally, under this conjectural correspondence, the classical periods of maximally-mutable Laurent polynomials whose Newton polytope produces a Fano polygon should recover the regularized quantum period of the mirror orbifold del Pezzo surface.

A notable example of a class of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces which do not admit toric degenerations arise from the anticanonical quasismooth wellformed log del Pezzo surfaces in weighted projective 3-spaces which were classified by Johnson and Kollár in
[
23
]
. A series of such anticanonical log del Pezzo surfaces were studied extensively from the viewpoint of mirror symmetry in
[
7
]
and their derived categories were described in
[
14
]
. Unfortunately, our setup does not apply to these surfaces, since the effective log Calabi-Yau orbifold surfaces by definition have an effective anticanonical cycle. Proving homological mirror symmetry for the Johnson-Kollár surfaces is an interesting open problem, currently out of reach.

in

Now, assuming that

1.4.

As we saw in the previous section, the gluing data of the subcategories

and

Proposition

(Proof of Proposition
3.5
)
.

As
s\rightarrow 0
(setting
P(y)=(1+y)^{k+1}+\epsilon
for a small
\epsilon
), the critical values become distributed as follows:

I.

k-2
of the critical values are distributed near the roots of
t^{k-2}-(\frac{k-2}{k})^{k-2}\frac{1}{s}=0
.

II.

3
of them will be arbitrarily close to
0

III.

Another
k+1
critical points are equidistributed near
-1
.

Moreover, all of the critical points are non-degenerate and when
s
is real, only two of the critical points are in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, one of which is of type II and the other of type III.

Proof.

We write

\mathbf{f}=\frac{P(y)}{xy}+sx+g(y)

We consider the equations on the open torus. In fact, by the non-degeneracy of
P
(for generic
\mathbf{q}_{i}
), there will not be any critical points on the toric boundary of
V
so that suffices.

We have the system

\mathbf{f}=t,\partial_{x}\mathbf{f}=0,\partial_{y}\mathbf{f}=0

with
t
denoting the critical value, the first two of which imply that

2P=xy(t-g),P=sx^{2}y

and hence that
2sx=t-g
. In particular,

P-\frac{1}{4s}y(g-t)^{2}=0

The equation
\partial_{y}\mathbf{f}
tells us that

g^{\prime}(y)xy^{2}-P+yP^{\prime}=0\implies P=yP^{\prime}+xy^{2}g^{\prime}(y)

We now set
g=y
. We see from
P-yP^{\prime}=xy^{2}
together with
x^{2}=\frac{P}{sy}
that the critical points satisfy the polynomial in
y

(5.18)

(P-yP^{\prime})^{2}=\frac{1}{s}Py^{3}

In particular, restricting to small
y
satisfying
\frac{1}{s}\gg|y^{2k+2}|
, the roots of this are close to the roots of
Py^{3}
, hence there are three roots close to
y=0
and
k+1
roots close to
-\mathbf{q}_{1},\dots,-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}

\mathbf{q}_{i}
are close to
1

It remains to show that the associated critical values

t=2sx+y=2s\frac{P-yP^{\prime}}{y^{2}}+y

Thus, when

then also

is close to
-\mathbf{q}_{i}
.

Secondly, notice that since

hence after dividing by

y=sx(-k+\frac{k+1}{y+1})+\frac{1}{xy}(k+1)\epsilon(1-\frac{1}{y+1})

Thus, when

Finally, when

For non-degeneracy, we appeal to Kouchnirenko’s formula

which in our example states that the sums of the multiplicities of the critical points of

\sum\mu_{p}=2!\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Newt}(\mathbf{f}_{s}))

By Pick’s formula,

Finally, we find which of the critical points are real. The polynomial in equation

Proposition 5.20
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{s}\gg|t|\gg 0
and
t
is moving in a straight line radially outwards to a critical value at large radius. The result of this on the level of the branch points is the following: the twin branch points (labelled

Proof.

We summarize the proof in three steps:

•

We approximate and replace our polynomial by a simpler one in the form
y_{0}^{k}-(y_{0}-t_{0})^{2}

•

We notice the new polynomial has a
\mathbb{Z}_{k-2}

•

We use a Sturm sequence argument to show that when

is real and increases towards the critical value, the two twin branch points will live on the real line (or arbitrarily close to it), moving towards to a far away branch point which is also arbitrarily close to being real. The smaller of the twin branch points remains less than the bigger one, whereas the bigger of the twins hits the far away branch point as we move
t

Consider again the branch point equation:
(1+y)^{k+1}+\epsilon=P=\frac{1}{4s}y(y-t)^{2}

Divide everything by

(\frac{y}{r}+\frac{1}{r})^{k+1}+\frac{\epsilon}{r^{k+1}}=\frac{y}{r}(\frac{y}{r}-\frac{t}{r})^{2}

y_{0}:=\frac{y}{r},t_{0}:=\frac{t}{r}

outside of a compact subset. We verify the Palais-Smale condition by brute force: we need to show that
|\nabla\mathbf{f}_{s}|^{2}
is bounded from below outside of a compact subset, so we will again use the metric induced by
\omega^{ex}
. We have that

\nabla^{M^{0}}\mathbf{f}_{s}=\nabla\mathbf{f}_{s}-\frac{\langle\nabla\mathbf{f}_{s},\nabla g\rangle}{|\nabla g|^{2}}\nabla g

where
\nabla
denotes the gradient in the ambient
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
g={xz-P(y)}
is the function defining
M^{0}
. As such,

|\nabla^{M^{0}}\mathbf{f}_{s}|^{2}=|\nabla\mathbf{f}_{s}|^{2}-\frac{\langle\nabla\mathbf{f}_{s},\nabla g\rangle}{|\nabla g|^{2}}

which is given explicitly as the non-negative quantity

|s|^{2}+\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}+|y-\frac{z}{y}|^{2}-\frac{|s\overline{z}+\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\geq 0

In particular, for
s=0
we have that

(5.24)

\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}+|y-\frac{z}{y}|^{2}-\frac{|\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\geq 0

We want to show the gradient
5.6
(for
s\neq 0
) is bounded from below outside of a compact set, so we may take
x,z\gg 0
, in other words we look at
M^{0}\setminus(M^{0}\cap\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\times A)
where
A
is a compact annulus. Since
xz=P(y)
, this implies that also
y\gg 0
. We compute:

(5.25)

|s|^{2}+\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}+|y-\frac{z}{y}|^{2}-\frac{|s\overline{z}+\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\geq\\
|s|^{2}+\big(\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}+|y-\frac{z}{y}|^{2}-\frac{|\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\big)+\big(\frac{|\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}-\frac{|s\overline{z}+\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\big)\geq\\
|s|^{2}+\underbrace{\big(\frac{|\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}-|s\overline{z}+\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}\big)}_{\text{ bounded in norm by}\frac{1}{2}|s|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|s|^{2}

The last inequality is due to the fact that, for
|x|,|y|,|z|\gg 0
, we have by the reverse triangle inequality:

|\frac{|\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}-|s\overline{z}+\frac{\overline{x}}{y}-|y|^{2}(1-\frac{z}{y^{2}})\overline{P^{\prime}(y)}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}|^{2}\leq|s|^{2}\frac{|z|^{2}}{|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|yP^{\prime}(y)|^{2}}

and finally

\frac{1}{|\frac{x}{z}|^{2}+1+|\frac{yP^{\prime}(y)}{z}|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\iff|\frac{yP^{\prime}(y)}{z}|^{2}+|\frac{x}{z}|^{2}\geq 1

Since
xz=P
, then
|\frac{yP^{\prime}(y)}{z}|^{2}=|x|^{2}|\frac{yP^{\prime}(y)}{P(y)}|^{2}
which for
|x|,|y|\gg 0
is asymptotically given by
|x|^{2}\gg 0
because
yP^{\prime}(y)
and
P(y)
are both monic polynomials of degree
k+1
. We can conclude that the gradient is bounded from below,
∎

5.7.
Special representations and modular arithmetic

Consider the special
I
-series
I(n,q)
(which is defined in
[
14
, Definition 2.5]
) and its subset
I^{\prime}:=I\setminus\{n\}=\{i_{1}=q,i_{2},\dots,i_{r+1}=0\}
.

Proposition 5.26
.

The map

\displaystyle I^{\prime}\rightarrow\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}

\displaystyle a\mapsto-aq^{-1}\mod n

is order-preserving.

Proof.

Start with
i_{1}=q
. This is sent to
n-1
. By definition of the recursion for the
I
-series,

i_{2}=b_{1}i_{1}-i_{0}=b_{1}q-n

implies that
i_{2}
is sent to
n-b_{1}
. If
i_{j}
is sent to
n-P_{j},0\leq P_{j}<n
, then by the definition of the
I
-series, the
P_{j}
satisfy the recursion

P_{i}=b_{i}P_{i-1}-P_{i-2}

with
P_{0}=1,P_{1}=b_{1}
. This is exactly the sequence in
[
8
, Proposition 10.2.2]
, which shows that the
P_{i}
form an increasing sequence of integers with
0\leq P_{i}\leq d
, which proves the claim.
∎

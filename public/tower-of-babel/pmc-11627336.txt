Artificial Intelligence: The new weapon to END TB
Authors: Advait Teli, Srinivasa Ikswaja Chelluri, Medha Bargaje
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

AI has made remarkable progress and is extensively utilized in various healthcare domains. Despite strenuous efforts to limit tuberculosis in India, it remains prevalent due to a limited workforce and excessive demand. AI has the potential to alleviate this issue by augmenting healthcare worker efficiency, enhancing screening, and streamlining management and follow-up processes.

To increase detection rates, a multi-pronged approach to diagnosis is crucial. This includes sputum based diagnostics such as ZN stain and molecular assays, used in conjunction with chest radiology. Implementing AI in these diagnostic processes can reduce manual intervention, saving time and resources. AI can also aid in confirming TB, even in drug-resistant cases, using simple classifiers and neural networks. Convolutional Neural Networks displayed a pooled sensitivity and specificity of 58% and 75% for radiological images.[1] Meanwhile, the classification and regression tree model achieved pooled sensitivity and specificity of 92% and 98% for isoniazid resistance detection in genome-based models. When given clinical data, Artificial Neural Networks surpassed simple classifiers in predicting drug-resistant TB.[1] Particularly, for radiological diagnosis, Acharya et al.[2] employed normalisation-free networks-based algorithms to diagnose Pulmonary Tuberculosis from chest Xray. The results demonstrated significant potential, with accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity at 96.91%, 91.81%, and 98.42%, respectively.

AI can expedite sputum examinations in areas with limited expertise and high demand, particularly in rural regions. Xiong et al.[3] demonstrated that AI models improved the efficiency of slide examination, leading to a reduced likelihood of missed diagnoses. In the initial run of the algorithm, the false positive and false negative slides were 15.9% and 7.5%, respectively. These figures improved to 8.5% and 1% during the second run, demonstrating a 14% enhancement in consistency. Furthermore, AI models can assess the quality of sputum for analysis, minimizing exposure to healthcare workers. Another noteworthy application prior to sputum examination is the utilisation of feedforward neural networks for screening of pulmonary tuberculosis based on the acoustic signal generated by the characteristic cough and symptomatic information. The Swaasa AI platform, developed by Yellapu et al.,[4] employing this principle, achieved an accuracy of 86.82%, with 90.36% sensitivity and 84.67% specificity.

AI models can recognise behavioural patterns exhibited by patients and linking them to their demographic and social characteristics.[5] By doing so, these models can identify patients who require intensive follow-up care to ensure adherence and integrate them with the NIKSHAY platform. This would significantly reduce the number of patients lost to follow-up.

Despite the various adherence systems under DOTS, patients often find ways to circumvent them. AI-based facial recognition and video analysis can confirm drug consumption and minimize non-compliance. This would enable the reallocation of resources wasted on administrative tasks, to areas that truly need financial support.

Migration is a significant factor contributing to attrition, leading to repeat registrations and a false prevalence rate, as well as hindering the tracking of patients’ progress. Biometric technology can be employed for enhanced patient identity verification, preventing migratory repeat registrations, and providing healthcare professionals with a complete medical history, regardless of the patient’s location.

The potential applications of AI in TB management are vast, and its ability to improve patient health is limitless. The National TB Elimination Program’s (NTEP) goal is to eradicate TB in India. The integration of AI models in various aspects of TB control will accelerate the achievement of this goal. While AI may not replace the human touch in healthcare, it has the potential to complement the clinical expertise of healthcare providers and enhance patient care.[6]

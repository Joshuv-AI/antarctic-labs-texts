Letter to the editor regarding “Clinical outcome and long‑term prognosis after endoscopic submucosal dissection for colorectal tumors in patients aged 75 years or older: a retrospective observational study”
Authors: Rijuan Jin
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the study by Xi et al. [1], which aims to determine the clinical outcomes and mortality predictors in elderly patients undergoing endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for colorectal tumors. The authors have provided valuable insights into this important field, and we appreciate their contribution. However, we have noticed several aspects of the study design and analysis methods that merit further discussion and improvement, and we would like to offer our suggestions in this letter to enhance the academic value of the research.

Firstly, the article does not describe whether the enrolled patients had multiple polyps. This factor could potentially influence the results of the study, especially in relation to CCI (Charlson comorbidity index) or OS (overall survival) scores, and the evaluation of elderly patients undergoing colorectal tumor ESD. Thus, the scoring metrics used in this study seem incomplete.

Additionally, the article mentions that 195 patients underwent lesion resection, with the number of successfully resected lesions also being 195. However, the specific requirements regarding the number of lesions were not clearly stated in the CCI or OS assessments. We speculate that patients with a higher number of lesions might have had an impact on the conclusions drawn from this study.

In conclusion, we believe that further refinement of the study design and analysis methods is necessary. For example, when conducting CCI or OS assessments, it may be beneficial to exclude patients with multiple polyps or a high number of lesions. These two factors could potentially influence the validity of the study’s conclusions and any subsequent exploration.

Graph colouring and Steenrod’s problem for Stanley-Reisner rings
Donald Stanley, Masahiro Takeda

Abstract
. It is a classical problem in algebraic topology asked by Steenrod in [ S ] which graded rings occurs as the cohomology ring of a space. In this paper, we define an algebraic version of the graph colouring, span colouring, and observe the relation between span colourings and Steenrod’s problem for graded Stanley-Reisner rings, in other words polynomial rings divided by an ideal generated by square-free monic monomials.

1.
Introduction

We are interested in which graded
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras are realized as the cohomology of a space. We study this realization problem for Stanley-Reisner rings with generators in degrees
4
and
6
. We also construct span colouring, which is an approximation of colouring for graphs, and relate span colouring to the realization problem for these algebras. Details about terminology can be found in Section
2
.

The general case of the realization problem, also known as Steenrod’s problem, seems intractible though some special
cases are known. For example the case
\mathbb{Z}[a]/(a^{3})
is equivalent to the Hopf invariant one
problem solved by Adams
[
A
]
.
The polynomial ring case has been studied by
many researchers (a very short and incomplete selection is
[
AGMV
,
CE
,
N
,
ST
]
), and was finally completed by
Andersen and Grodal
[
AG
]
. They showed a graded polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[V]
is realizable if and only if it is isomorphic to a tensor product of
various copies of
H^{*}(BU(1))=\mathbb{Z}[x_{2}]
,
H^{*}(BSU(n))\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{4},x_{6},\dots,x_{2n}]
and

H^{*}(BSp(n))\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{4},x_{8},\dots,x_{4n}]
, where the subscripts correspond to the degrees of the generators.

We are interested in the larger class of Stanley-Riesner rings of a simplicial complex
K
. These are quotients of polynomial rings

SR(K,\phi)=\mathbb{Z}[V]/I_{K}
, where the set of generators
V
is the vertex set of
K
and
I_{K}
is the ideal generated by the non-faces of
K
. The extra
\phi
in the notation is a map
\phi\colon V\rightarrow 2\mathbb{N}
that takes care of the degrees of the generators.

There is already some understanding of Steenrod’s problem in this case.
Davis and Januszkiewicz constructed realizing spaces for all
SR(K,\phi)
with degree
2
generators in
[
DJ
]
. If the generators are of degree
2
and
4
, then realizing spaces can be contructed by polyhedral products
[
BBCG1
,
BBCG2
]
.
However we know from the result for polynomial rings above there are severe restrictions on the possible degrees higher than
4
even in the polynomial case.
The second author gives realizablitiy conditions for a class of
SR(K,\phi)
with general degrees
[
Ta
]
. In particular in Takeda’s class every pair of degree
4
generators multiply to
0
. In other words there are no edges between vertices of degree
4
in
K
.

In this paper we instead are more interested in the opposite case where there are no relations
between degree
4
generators, and additionally we assume all generators have degrees
4
and
6
.
We particularly consider the rings
A(n,L)=SR(\Delta^{n-1}\ast L,\phi)
depending on a natural number
n

and a simplicial complex
L

and relate the realization problem for this class of rings to colourings of the one skeleton of
L
.
Before describing our realization results we first introduce span colourings and some of their properties.

1.1.
Span colourings

We consider graphs as simplicial complexes with no
2
-simplices, and we can also consider a graph
in a more traditional way as a pair

G=(V,E)=(V(G),E(G))

where
V
is a set known as vertices, and
E\subset V\times V
is the set of edges (in simplicial complex terms
E
is the set of
1
-simplices).
Let for
x\in V
,
N(x)
denote the neighbours (or neighbourhood) of
x
that is
N(x)=\{y\in V|(x,y)\in E\}
.
Note that
N(x)
doesn’t contain
x
itself.
Our first task in the paper will be to develop the theory of span colourings of graphs.

Definition 1.1

(Weak span colouring)
.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a field and
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
A
n
-
weak span colouring
of
G
is a function
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that for every
x\in V
,
f(x)\notin\langle f(N(x))\rangle
, where
\langle S\rangle
is the linear subspace spanned by

S\subset\mathbf{k}^{n}
.

An ordinary colouring is a map
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow\{1,2,\dots n\}
such that for every
x\in V
,
f(x)\notin f(N(x))
.
So weak span colouring is an algebraic version of graph colouring. In Section
3
we define two other related colourings, span colouring and intermediate span colouring, which we use for different purposes. The weak span colouring has the most naive definition.
The least
n
such that
G
has an
n
-span colouring is the
span chromatic number
of
G
, denoted
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
. Proposition
3.8
proves that the chromatic numbers associated to all three variants coincide. When
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}/p
we write
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi=s_{p}\chi
.

For any colouring of any graph
G
with
n
colours we can also label the vertices by corresponding basis elements
in
\mathbf{k}^{n}
and any
n
-clique in
G
needs at least
n
linearly independent vectors to be span coloured so we get the following proposition, where
clique(G)
and
\chi(G)
are the clique and chromatic number of
G
.

Proposition 1.2

(Propositions
4.8
and
4.10
)
.

For every field
\mathbf{k}
and every graph
G
,
clique(G)\leq s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)\leq\chi(G)
.

If our field is finite we can construct graphs that exhibit an inequality.

Proposition 1.3

(Proposition
5.7
)
.

For any finite field
\mathbf{k}
, there is a graph
G
such that

s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)<\chi(G).

To prove the last proposition we contruct a representing graph
A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
for
n
-span colourings.
This graph has the property that the
n
-span colouring of a graph

are in bijection with

. In this sense

would be the representing graph for normal
n
-colourings.
Since we have the identity map of

we see that
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})\leq n
and
by constructing
n
-cliques in
A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
we get a lower bound and see that

s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})=n
.
Letting

be the size of the finite field
\mathbf{k}
and
p
a prime, we show that for favorable combinations of
q
and

,
Hom(A_{\mathbf{k}^{p}},K_{p})=\emptyset
(Proposition
5.4
), and putting these together

\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{p}})>p=s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{p}})

(Example
5.6
).
There is also a characterisitic
0
version of Proposition
5.7
. There is a graph
G

with
\chi(G)=4
and
s_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(G)=3
(Example
5.8
).

1.2.
Algebras with generators in degrees
4
and
6
,
\mathbb{A(n,L)}

We will restrict to Stanley-Reisner ring
SR(K,\phi)
of a simplicial complex
K

\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]/I_{K}
, generated by degree

and

Consider the case

when the vertices of the

have degree

and the vertices of

have degree
6
.
Then we use the notation
A(n,L)=SR(K,\phi)
. This is one of the main objects of study in this paper.

If

is realized by
X

A(n,L)\cong H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, then
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)
must have an unstable action of the mod-

. Since

.
Let

be the

-skeleton of

-span colouring over

to get an

.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
7.7
)
.

Suppose
L
is a simplicial complex such that

s_{2}\chi(L^{1})\leq n
.
Then
A(n,L)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2
has an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
action.

This theorem is proved by writing
A(n,L)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2
as a limit of polynomial algebras and using the colouring to upgrade this limit to a limit in the category of unstable algebras over
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

To prove the converse (Theorem
6.12
) we need to assume that
L
is equal to its
1
-skeleton, in other words that it is a graph. The proof constructs a span colouring out of an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
action. It requires some analysis of the
\mathcal{A}_{2}
action on the ideal
I_{K}
and a reduction to graphs with minimal degree
2
by sequentially removing degree
0
and
1

and

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem

)
.

Suppose
G
is a graph, then

A(n,G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2
has an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
action if and only if
s_{2}\chi(G)\leq n
.

As observed above this means if

is realizable then
A(n,G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/2
has an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
action and so Theorem
6.12
also gives us:

Theorem 1.6

(Corollary

)
.

For any graph
G
,
if
A(n,G)
is realizable, then
G
has an
n
-span colouring over
\mathbb{Z}/2
and so
s_{2}\chi(G)\leq n
.

We can consider this as an inequality relating colourings to a topologically defined graph invariant.
For a graph
G
let
\chi_{Top}(G)
be the least

such that
A(n,G)
is realizable. Then we can reinterpret the theorem as saying

.
To get an upper bound we use the usual chromatic number
\chi
.
If the chromatic number of

, the one skeleton of

, is less than

, then

is realizable.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
8.4
)
.

Let
L
be a simplicial complex.
If
\chi(L^{1})\leq n
then
A(n,L)
is realizable.

The proof uses a category
P_{\max}(K)
and the colouring to construct a functor of spaces which are products of
BSU(2)
and
BSU(3)
and with colimit that realizes
A(n,L)
. Much of this process was worked out by the second author
[
Ta
]
.

When our simplicial complex is a graph
G
Corollary
6.13
and Theorem
8.4
together are equivalent to the following:

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
8.6
)
.

For any graph
G
,

(1.1)

s_{2}\chi(G)\leq\chi_{Top}(G)\leq\chi(G)

From Example
5.6
we know
s_{2}\chi(A_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}})<\chi(A_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}})
, so at most one of the inequalities in Equation

can be an equality. To show

can be written as a limit of realizable polynomial algebras, it does not seem possible to consistently realize all the maps and thus the whole diagram as a diagram of spaces. On the other hand showing

We also classify the realizable Stanley-Reisner algebras with two degree

Preliminaries

A simplicial complex on a vertex set
V
is
K
a subset of the power set of
V
closed under inclusions. Note that this means
\emptyset
is in
K
except when
K
is empty.
For a simplicial complex
K
its vertex set is denoted
V(K)
.
So a simplex
\sigma\in K
is a subset of
V(K)
.

We also use
\sigma
to denote the power set of
\sigma
,
\mathcal{P}(\sigma)
and consider it as the corresponding subsimplicial complex

\sigma\subset K
.
We let
\Delta^{n-1}
the simplex with vertex set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

We sometimes regard
K
as a poset such that for
\sigma,\tau\in K
,
\sigma<\tau
if and only if
\sigma\subset\tau
.
Let
P_{\max}(K)
be a subposet of
K
such that for
\sigma\in K

\sigma\in P_{\max}(K)
if and only if there exist maximal simplices
\sigma_{1},\dots\sigma_{n}\in K
such that
\bigcap\sigma_{i}=\sigma
.
We regard
1
-skeleton of
K
as a graph, and we denote this graph
K^{1}
, the same symbol we use for the
1
-skeleton of
K
.

For a subset
\sigma\subset V(K)
, let
K_{\sigma}
denote the full subcomplex of
K
with vertex set
\sigma
. We can write it as a projection or as a subcomplex

L_{\sigma}=\{\tau\cap\sigma|\tau\in L\}=\{\tau|\tau\in L,\tau\subset\sigma\}

For
K,L
simplicial complexes the join
K\ast L=\{\sigma\cup\tau|\sigma\in K,\tau\in L\}
is a simplicial
complex on
V(K)\cup V(L)
.

The poset of
K\ast L
is the product of the posets of
K
and
L
.

Consider the geometric realization of a simplicial complex
K
,
|K|
. Although we will not use realization it is compatible with joins,
meaning
|K\ast L|\cong|K|\ast|L|
.

We consider graphs as simplicial complexes with only
0
and
1
-simplices.
We also use more standard notation for graphs and let
G=(V,E)
denote a graph with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
.

For
x\in V
the neighbourhood (or set of neighbours) of
x
is
N(x)=\{y|(x,y)\in E\}
.

K_{n}
denotes the complete graph on vertex set
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.
An
n
-clique in
G
is a subgraph isomorphic to
K_{n}
.
The clique number of
G
,
clique(G)
is the size of the largest clique in
G
.

We let
\mathbf{k}
denote a field and

\mathbf{k}^{n}
be the
\mathbf{k}
-vector space with basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
. Recall that
\langle S\rangle
denotes the linear subspace spanned by
S\subset\mathbf{k}^{n}
, and so

\mathbf{k}^{n}=\langle e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\rangle
.
Let
P\mathbf{k}^{n}
denote the projective space of lines in
\mathbf{k}^{n}
, an element of
P\mathbf{k}^{n}
is a dimension
1
subspace of
\mathbf{k}^{n}
.
Recall
\mathrm{Gr}_{l}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
denotes the Grassmannian, more explicitly it is the set of
l
dimensional subspaces of
\mathbf{k}^{n}
. Using this notation
P\mathbf{k}^{n}=\mathrm{Gr}_{1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex with the vertex set
V
, and
\phi\colon V\rightarrow 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then the graded Stanley-Reisner ring
SR(L,\phi)
is defined by

SR(K,\phi)=\mathbb{Z}[V]/I_{K},

where
\mathbb{Z}[V]
is the polynomial ring generated by
x\in V
with
|x|=\phi(x)
and
I_{K}
is the ideal generated by monomials
x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{k}
with
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}\notin K
as a simplex.

We need a functoriality property of
SR(K,\phi)
.
If we have a subcomplex
K^{\prime}\subset K
then we get an algebra map

\pi\colon SR(K,\phi)\rightarrow SR(K^{\prime},\phi)
, since
I_{K}\subset I_{K^{\prime}}
.

For example
\sigma\in K
can be considered as a simplicial complex and we get a map

SR(K,\phi)\rightarrow SR(\sigma,\phi)=\mathbb{Z}[\sigma]
.

Example 2.1
.

SR(K\ast L,\phi_{K}\cup\phi_{L})\cong SR(K,\phi_{K})\otimes SR(L,\phi_{L})
. This follows since the minimal missing simplices of

consists of the union of the minimal missing simplices of

and

.

and

Suppose we have a Stanley-Reisner algebra whose generators are in degrees

3.
Roughly equivalent versions of span colouring

In this section we fix a graph
G=(V,E)
and look at some basic properties of three slightly different versions of span colouring of a graph which all depend on our field
\mathbf{k}
. All three give the same associated chromatic number (Proposition
3.8
) and are in that sense roughly equivalent. The first version of the span colouring has already been defined in Section
1
. It is the most naive and easy to understand. Recall the set of neighbours of
x
,
N(x)
from Section
2

Definition 3.1

(Weak span colouring)
.

A
n
-
weak span colouring
of
G
over
\mathbf{k}
is a function
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that for every
x\in V
,
f(x)\notin\langle f(N(x))\rangle
.

The
weak span chromatic number
of
G
,
s^{\prime\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
is the least
n
such that
G
has an
n
-weak span colouring.

The second version is similar to the first but instead uses the projective space.

Definition 3.2

(Intermediate Span colouring)
.

A
n
-
intermediate span colouring
of
G
over
\mathbf{k}
is a function
f\colon G\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}
such that for every
x\in V
,
f(x)\not\subset\langle f(N(x))\rangle
.

The
intermediate span chromatic number
of
G
,
s^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
is the least
n
such that
G
has an
n
-intermediate span colouring.

Note that if
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}/2
these two versions of Span colouring are the same since
\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow P\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}
is a bijection. The final version of span colouring is a souped up version of the second which will allow us to construct a graph
A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
such that
Hom(G,A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})
is bijective with
n
-span colourings of
G
(see Proposition
4.2
). On the surface it is more technical.

Definition 3.3

(Span colouring)
.

An
n
-
span colouring
of
G
over
\mathbf{k}
is a function
f\colon G\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
such that for
x\in V
with
f(x)=(U,V)
,
U\not\subset V
, and for every
(x,y)\in E
, with
f(y)=(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
,
U\subset V^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime}\subset V
.

The
span chromatic number
of
G
,
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
is the least
n
such that
G
has an
n
-span colouring. When
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}/p
we write
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi=s_{p}\chi
.

We will show using a sequence of lemmas that all three chromatic numbers are the same.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
f\colon V\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
is an
n
-span colouring. Then
p_{1}\circ f\colon V\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}
,
where
p_{1}
is projection onto the first factor, is an
n
-intermediate span colouring. Hence
s^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)\leq s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
.

Proof.

Let
p_{2}\colon P\mathbf{k}^{n}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
be projection into the second factor. For
x\in V
, if

y\in N(x)
, then
p_{1}\circ f(y)\subset p_{2}f(x)
. Hence
\langle p_{1}f(N(x))\rangle\subset p_{2}f(x)
. So
p_{1}f(x)\not\subset\langle p_{1}f(N(x))\rangle
and

p_{1}f
is an intermediate span colouring.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
f\colon V\rightarrow P\mathbf{k}^{n}
is an
n
-intermediate span colouring.
Then there exists an
n
-span colouring

5.
Chromatic number of

For this section we let

For a basis

be the map corresponding to the clique of Lemma

Let

Notice that

Next something well known,

Lemma 5.1

|\mathcal{B}|=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^{n}-q^{i})

Proof.

Ordered bases are in bijection with
n
by
n
matrices with non-zero determinant this

∎

Lemma 5.2

For any

|F^{-1}((U,V))|=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}(q^{n-1}-q^{i})

Proof.

Given a

and an

-dimensional subspace

\frac{q-1}{(n-1)!}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}(q^{n-1}-q^{i})
total choices. Quotienting out by the free

action gives the result.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose there is a map

, then
n|(q^{n}-1)q^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Since every graph homomorphism

is an isomorphism, the composition

in the coproduct. Hence using

we see that

Next using Lemma

\displaystyle|(g\circ F)^{-1}(x)|=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{(U,V)\in g^{-1}(x)}F^{-1}(U,V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{(U,V)\in g^{-1}(x)}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}(q^{n-1}-q^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|g^{-1}(x)|\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}(q^{n-1}-q^{i}).

So we get that

In the rest of the paper, we will mostly be concerned with the realization problem for the algebras

6.1.
The Steenrod Algebra and its action on Stanley-Reisner rings

For a prime
p

\mathcal{A}_{p}
denote the mod-
p
Steenrod algebra.
Let
A^{*}
be a commutative graded algebra with unit over
\mathbb{Z}/p

When
A^{*}
has an action of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, we call
A^{*}
an algebra over the (mod-
p
) Steenrod algebra.
Unless noted we will assume the prime is
2
.

For an odd prime number
p
, an algebra over the mod-
p
Steenrod algebra
A^{*}
is
unstable
if and only if for all homogeneous elements

, there is an equation

\beta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{P}^{k}(x)=\begin{cases}x^{p}\quad&(2k=2d,\epsilon=0)\\
0\quad&(2k+\epsilon>2d),\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{P}^{k}
is the Steenrod power operation and
\beta
is the Bockstein homomorphism.
Similarly we can define an algebra over the mod-
2
Steenrod algebra
A^{*}
is
unstable
if and only if for all homogeneous elements
x\in A^{d}
, there is an equation

\operatorname{Sq}^{k}(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}\quad&k=d\\
0\quad&k>d,\end{cases}

where
\operatorname{Sq}^{k}
is the Steenrod square.
The interaction of the multiplication of
A^{*}
and the action is described by the Cartan formula:

\mathcal{P}^{k}(xy)=\Sigma_{0\leq i\leq k}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x)\cup\mathcal{P}^{k-i}(y)

for odd characteristic and,

\operatorname{Sq}^{k}(xy)=\Sigma_{0\leq i\leq k}\operatorname{Sq}^{i}(x)\cup\operatorname{Sq}^{k-i}(y)

for the prime
2
, where
\mathcal{P}^{0}=\operatorname{Sq}^{0}=id
. We assume all our actions of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
are unstable and satisfy the Cartan formula.
Please see
[

,
HJS
]
for more information.

There are maximal cells

7.2.
Steenrod actions from span colourings

Lemma 7.6
.

Suppose
G
is a graph with vertex set
\{y_{1},\dots y_{m}\}
, and let

f\colon\{y_{1},\dots y_{m}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle x_{1},\dots x_{n}\rangle

Then there are maps

s_{i}\circ p\colon\mathbb{Z}/2\langle x_{1},\dots x_{n}\rangle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle y_{i}\rangle
such that
s_{i}\circ f(y_{i})=y_{i}
and for all
y_{j}\in N(y_{i})
,
s_{i}\circ f(y_{j})=0
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle f(N(y_{i}))\rangle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle x_{1},\dots x_{n}\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\rightarrow}}M\rightarrow 0

By the definition of span colouring
p\circ f(y_{i})\in M
is not

so there is a linear map
\overline{s_{i}}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle y_{i}\rangle
such that

\overline{s_{i}}\circ p\circ f(y_{i})=y_{i}
. So letting

s_{i}=\overline{s_{i}}\circ p\colon\mathbb{Z}/2\langle x_{1},\dots x_{n}\rangle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\langle y_{i}\rangle
we get that
s_{i}\circ f(y_{i})=y_{i}
and for all
y_{j}\in N(y_{i})
,
s_{i}\circ f(y_{j})=0
.
∎

Theorem 7.7

Suppose

By assumption there is a weak span colouring of

Now give

the structure of an unstable algebra over mod-
2

(7.1)

\operatorname{Sq}^{4}(y_{i})=y_{i}f(y_{i}),\operatorname{Sq}^{2}(y_{i})=0,\ {\rm and}\ \operatorname{Sq}^{2}(x_{i})=s(x_{i}).

Let

9.1.
Chromatic number questions

Question 9.1
.

What is the relationship between
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
and
\chi(G)
?

We also suggest some more specific cases of this question.

Question 9.2
.

What is
\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})
?

The simplest case is
\chi(A_{(\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}/2})^{3}})
which is at least
4
and should not be too hard to resolve.
We guess that
\chi(A_{(\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}/2})^{3}})=4\ {\mbox{o}r\ }5
. We know

and
Propositions
5.4
,
5.5
and

show in many cases

. We ask if this is true more generally.

Question 9.3
.

If

, is
\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})>n
?

Our proofs use counting of vertices, one could also count edges or larger cliques. This may solve more cases, but seems unlikely to be enough of all cases even for finite fields. Perhaps there are more subtle ways of show such inequalities.
We ask two other related questions.

Question 9.4
.

What is the smallest graph for which
s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)<\chi(G)
?

We know we can take
G=A_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}}
to get an inequality, can be still get an inequality for subgraphs of

A_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}}
?

Question 9.5
.

How large can
\chi(G)-s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
be?

This difference is at least as big as
n-\chi(A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}})
, relating this question to the first one. Can the difference be bigger?

Question 9.6
.

Is there a nice class of graphs for which
\chi(G)=s_{\mathbf{k}}(G)
?

We saw in Proposition
4.8
that
clique(G)\leq s_{\mathbf{k}}(G)
, so graphs with

clique(G)=\chi(G)
would be such a class. Is there another already well known class such that

\chi(G)=s_{\mathbf{k}}(G)
?

We want to define an integral version of span colouring.
Define
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
to be the graph with

V(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})=\{(U,V)|U,V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n},U\oplus V=\mathbb{Z}^{n},\text{rank }U=1\}

and

E(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})=\{((U,V),(U^{\prime},V^{\prime}))\in V(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})^{2}|U\subset V^{\prime},U^{\prime}\subset V\}.

Next an
n
-span colouring of
G
over
\mathbb{Z}
is a map of graphs
G\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
.
The chromatic number of this colouring is denoted
s_{\mathbb{Z}}\chi
.

Instead of the condition
U\oplus V=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we could have the condition
U\cap V=0
.
In the field case this is the same, but if we use it for
\mathbb{Z}
the following proof that colourings localize would not work.
We use the conventions
\mathbb{Z}/0=\mathbb{Q}
and
s_{0}\chi=s_{\mathbb{Q}}\chi
.

Proposition 9.7
.

Suppose
G
is a graph and
\mathbf{k}
is a field.

(1)

There is a map
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rightarrow A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
and hence
s_{\mathbb{Z}}\chi\geq s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
.

(2)

If
char(\mathbf{k})=p
then there is a map
A_{(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{n}}\rightarrow A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}

and hence
s_{p}\chi\geq s_{\mathbf{k}}\chi(G)
.

(3)

The is a map
K_{n}\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
and hence
\chi(G)\geq s_{\mathbb{Z}}\chi
.

Proof.

First identify
(\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbf{k}
with
\mathbf{k}
.
Let
(U,V)\in V(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})
then since

(U\oplus V)\otimes\mathbf{k}\cong U\otimes\mathbf{k}\oplus V\otimes\mathbf{k}
we get

(U\otimes\mathbf{k},V\otimes\mathbf{k})\in A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
. Let

\rho\colon A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rightarrow A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
be the corresponding map of graphs.
Note that if
((U,V),(U^{\prime},V^{\prime}))\in V(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}})^{2}
is an edge then
we have a retraction of
U\subset V^{\prime}
given by the composition

U\subset V^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}=U\oplus V\rightarrow U

where the last map is the projection. This implies that
U\otimes\mathbf{k}\subset V^{\prime}\otimes\mathbf{k}
and similarly

U^{\prime}\otimes\mathbf{k}\subset V\otimes\mathbf{k}
. So
\rho
preserves edges and is a map of graphs.
This completes the proof of
(1). The proof of (2) is similar.

For a basis
\{x_{1},\dots x_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the vertices in
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
,
(\langle x_{i}\rangle,\langle x_{1},\dots x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots x_{n}\rangle)
form a subgraph

proving (3).
∎

9.3.
Stabilization, Fractional colourings and Cores

We stabilize the ambient vector space to get sequences other graphs colourings and chromatic numbers. We don’t know if this gives any new chromatic numbers. We also consider how out graphs are related to cores (which a topologist would call a minimal retract).

Definition 9.16
.

A graph
G
is a
core
if every endomorphism of
G
is an automorphism.

A graph
i\colon H\subset G
is a
retract
if there is a map
r\colon G\rightarrow H
such that
r\circ i
is the identity on
H
.

So we can see that cores are the same as minimal retracts.

We fix a field and write
A_{\mathbf{k}^{n}}
as
A_{n}
.
The (vertices of) the graphs
A_{n}
are the complement of the partial flag variety
(V_{1},V_{2})
in (lines,dim n-1 subspaces). Here
V_{1}
is a 1-dimensional subspace and
V_{2}
is
n-1
dimensional. We can also define a fractional colouring version by letting
V_{1}
be
k
-dimensional and
V_{2}
,
n-k
dimensional, and we get corresponding graphs

A_{n,k}
. With this notation
A_{n,1}=A_{n}
. Both of these can be stabilized. In other words in the original definition the subspaces are in
k^{n}
, but we can take
(U,V)
to be
k
and
n-k
dimensional subspaces in
\mathbf{k}^{n+l}
. Correspondingly we get graphs
A_{n,k,l}
and we can ask if these cores. For each fixed
n,k
there are at most finitely many
l
for which they could be cores. Since

\chi(A_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}})>3
is seems plausible that
A_{n}=A_{n,1,0}
is a core. We also get chromatic numbers

\chi_{n,k}
and
\chi_{n,k,l}
. Since
k^{n+l}\subset k^{n+l+1}
, we see that
\chi_{n,k,l}\leq\chi_{n,k,l^{\prime}}
, for
l\geq l^{\prime}
so these numbers stabilize as
l
increases. Are they already stable for
l=0
? In other words is the inequality

\chi_{n,k}=\chi_{n,k,0}\leq\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{n,k,l}
always an equality? This would mesh with none of the
A_{n,k.l}
being cores for
l>0
. Note that these can all be phrased in terms of poset colourings,
A_{n,k,l}
corresponds to
P=\{\mbox{subpaces of }{\mathbf{k}^{n+l}}\}
and
Q=\{k\mbox{ dimensional subpaces of }{\mathbf{k}^{n+l}}\}
, or for the set version
P=2^{[n+l]}
and
Q=\{\mbox{subsets of }[n+l]\mbox{ of size }k\}
.

We use a slightly different stabilization for
\chi_{Top}
. If our algebra

A(n,G)=SR(\Delta^{n-1}\ast G)
is realizable
by a space
X
then
X\times BSU(3)
will realize
A(n,G\ast\{y\})=SR(\Delta^{n}\ast G\ast\{y\})
. So we can stabilize by
letting
\chi_{Top,t}(G)=\chi_{Top}(G\ast\{y_{1}\}\ast\dots\ast\{y_{t}\})-t
. Since
\chi(G\ast\{y\})=\chi(G)+1

and
s_{2}\chi(G\ast\{y\})=s_{2}\chi(G)+1

we get that

s_{2}\chi(G)\leq\chi_{Top,t}(G)\leq\chi_{Top}(G)\leq\chi(G).

Which of these inequalities can be strict? More specifically:

Question 9.17
.

Does
\chi_{Top,t}(G)=\chi_{Top}(G)
for all
G
and
t
?

ICG-r15 predicts esophageal varices in compensated liver cirrhosis: a noninvasive approach
Authors: Xu Han, Xiao-Jing Cheng, Min Gao, Chun-Yan Wang, Li-li Zhao, Yong-Feng Yang, Jia Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of our study was to evaluate the indocyanine green (ICG) retention test as a noninvasive marker of esophageal varices(EV).MethodsThe clinical data of patients diagnosed with compensated liver cirrhosis in Tianjin Second People’s Hospital between January 2018 and January 2021 were analysed with SPSS 23.0.ResultA total of 144 patients (88 M/56 F, 51.7 ± 11.06 years) were enrolled. The ICG retention at 15 min(ICG-r15), PVD, TBIL, Cholinesterase(CHE), AST to ALT ratio(ARR), APRI, splenic area, Lok index, Park index and liver stiffness measurement in the absent or small EV group were lower than those in the medium or large EV group, while the ICG disappareance rate(ICG-K), Effective hepatic blood flow(EHBF), ALB, PLT, and Platelet to Spleen Diameter Ratio(PSDR) were higher, and the differences were significant (P < 0.05). ICG-r15, splenic area, APRI and PLT were independent predictors for medium or large esophageal varices (OR = 1.115, 1.025, 0.281, and 0.987, respectively,P < 0.05). The predictive value of ICG-r15 for medium or large varices was 17.95%, the specificity was 0.849, and the sensitivity was 0.662, the AUROC was 0.815. The cut-off value of PLT for M/L EV was 113.5, and the specificity and sensitivity were 0.616 and 0.887, the AUROC was 0.759. The AUROC of ICG-r15 combined with PLT was 0.866, which was more superior than others.ConclusionAlthough we are far from the replacement of endoscopy, ICG-r15 combined with PLT seems to be able to identify patients with medium or large EV in patients with compensated liver cirrhosis.Keywords: Indocyanine green (ICG) retention test, Esophageal varices, Compensated liver cirrhosis

Introduction

Esophageal variceal bleeding (EVB) is the most dangerous complication in patients with cirrhosis, an important predictor for EVB is the size of varices. Patients with large esophageal varices ( diameter ≥ 5 mm) have a high risk of bleeding, with a 2-year probability of approximately 15-30% [1, 2]. The risk of bleeding in patients with small varices (diameter < 5 mm) is approximately 7% in 2 years, and 5%~23% of patients develop large varices within 1 year [3, 4]. Wang et al. [5] analyzed the data of more than 8000 patients with cirrhosis in the past 10 years, and the results showed that EVB was the most important complication of portal hypertension in cirrhosis, and its incidence was as high as 55.6%. Therefore, existing guidelines strongly recommend assessing the grade of portal hypertension (PH) and Esophageal varices (EV) in all patients with cirrhosis [6, 7]. At present, the gold standard for evaluating PH is the measurement of the hepatic vein pressure gradient (HVPG). PH is defined as HVPG of more than 5 mmHg and a value more than 10 mmHg, also called clinically significant portal hypertension (CSPH, HVPG ≥ 10 mmHg), is the main predictor of the development and EBV.

However, HVPG measurement requires jugular vein puncture, which is an invasive examination, and patients need to use iodine contrast agent and receive radiation exposure. Gastroscopy is also an invasive examination, and has some contraindications. Therefore, the development of noninvasive tools that can identify PH and EV is significant. Some tools have been proposed for predicting PH [8, 9], even though none reached an important role in clinical practice. The liver stiffness measurement (LSM) had been proven to be an effective noninvasive tool, but it was related to the causes of liver disease (such as hepatitis B virus, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease and cholestasis) [8]. The aetiologies are different, with different cut-off values.

Meanwhile, indocyanine green (ICG) is introduced as quick and noninvasive way evaluate liver functional reserve. The ICG molecule has proven to be safe for human study. Extravasation of the ICG dye can cause a stinging sensation. Mild side effects like nausea and vomiting are seen in 0.15% of patients. Moderate adverse events like urticaria, vasovagal events are seen in 0.2% of patients. Urticaria can be treated using antihistamines. The incidence of severe adverse events is 0.05%. Anaphylaxis can occur in patients with iodine allergies [10]. As a melamine dye that binds to albumin and α-1 lipoprotein, which is ingested by hepatocytes and secreted into bile, ICG is completely cleared from the liver without passing through the enterohepatic circulation [11]. ICG clearance is a test reflecting the liver function and total hepatic blood flow [12]. It has been used to evaluate liver function in patients with advanced cirrhosis who are preparing for liver transplantation and liver cancer surgery [13–15]. Lu et al. [16] demonstratd that ICG retention test can serve as a noninvasive marker for detecting esophageal varices in cirrhotic patients with hepatocellular carcinoma. However, another study indicated that ICG-r15 was not reliable in assessing esophageal varices in HCC patients with Child-Pugh grade A liver function [17].

In this study, ICG retention test is evaluated as a noninvasive marker of EV in patients with compensated cirrhosis.

Methods

Patient selection

We retrospectively selected inpatients who were diagnosed compensated with cirrhosis in Tianjin Second People’s Hospital from January 2018 to January 2021. Patients diagnosed with compensated cirrhosis according to clinical, biochemical, histological or ultrasound (US) criteria were selected, and the Child‒Pugh score was ≤6. The patients who underwent gastroscopy and the ICG measurement were involved, most of inpatients with cirrhosis underwent gastroscopy and the ICG measurement unless contraindicated in Tianjin Second People’s Hospital. Exclusion criteria were age less than 18 years, obesity (body mass index ≥ 30), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), ongoing treatment for portal hypertension, portal hypertension of presinus or extrahepatic causes, and pregnancy. Over the 3-year period, 211 patients were evaluated for the study and 67 were excluded. A total of 144 compensated liver cirrhosis patients were enrolled. The ICG-r15 measurement was performed within seven days of the gastroscopy. In this retrospective study, the data were anonymous, and the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Medical Ethics Committee of Tianjin Second People’s Hospital(No.2022-54). This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Tianjin Second People’s Hospital (No.2021-54). The study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Clinical and biochemical assessment

Age, sex, diagnosis, haemoglobin, platelet count and routine biochemical tests (including liver and kidney function tests) were registered.

Noninvasive score of portal hypertension and oesophageal varices

The AST/ALT ratio (AAR), AST/platelet ratio index (APRI), Lok index, Park index and LSM were calculated according to published formulas.

Ultrasound examination

Ultrasound examination was performed by the same operator, using a 3.5 to 7 MHz convex probes provided with colour, power and pulsed Doppler device (ACUSON Prosound S3000). The diameters of the splenic bipolar and portal veins were recorded.

Gastroscopy evaluation

All gastroscopies were performed by the experienced operators in our endoscopy unit. The size of the EV according to pre-specified criteria as follows: small varices, minimally elevated veins above the esophageal mucosal surface; medium varices, tortuous veins occupying less than one-third of the esophageal lumen; and large varices, those occupying more than one-third of the esophageal lumen. Absent or Small esophageal varices were recorded as A/S EV, Medium or Large esophageal varices were recorded as M/L EV.

Indocyanine green test

Fasting peripheral venous blood was collected to measure the haemoglobin value, and the height and body weight were measured. The above indices were input into a DDG-3300 K analyser produced by Japan Optoelectronic Industry Co., Ltd. After 6 h of fasting, the patient lay supine in a quiet state, and the nasal photoreceptor probe of the DDG-3300 K examination instrument was connected to the medial side of the nasal alar of the patient. Indocyanine green was prepared into a 50 mg/L solution with sterilized water at 0.5 mg/kg and injected evenly and rapidly from the median cubital vein on one side within 5–10 s. ICG-R15, EHBF and ICG-K were measured by a DDG-3300 K analyser and its supporting analysis software.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS 23.0 statistical software package was used to analyse the data. Measurement data conforming to a normal distribution are expressed as x̅ ± s. A t test or analysis of variance was used for comparisons between the two groups. Measurement data that did not conform to a normal distribution were represented as M (P25, P75), and comparisons between groups were performed by the rank sum test. The comparison of count data between groups was performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s test. The influencing factors were analysed by binary logistic regression analysis; the OR value and 95% CI of the risk factors were calculated, and the sensitivity and specificity of the parameters with diagnostic efficacy were further calculated. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The ICG molecule has proven to be safe for human study, because it contains iodide, so it is not suitable for iodine allergy and hyperthyroidism. In our study, only two patients had mild rash after the ICG measurement.

Factors associated with the presence of EV

The ICG-r15, PVD, TBil, CHE, AAR, APRI, spleen area, Lok index, Park index, and LSM in the A/S EV group were lower than those in the M/L EV group, and the ICG-K value, EHBF, Alb, PLT, and PSDR (Platelet to Spleen Diameter ratio) were higher, as shown in Table 1. The difference between the two groups was significant (P < 0.05).

Binary logistic regression analysis of predictors associated with M/L EV

The above indicators with statistics difference were included into the binary logistic regression model for analysis. The Froward Wald examination was used in the binary logistic regression analysis. The results showed that ICG-r15(odds ratio(OR), 1.115; 95% confidence interval(CI);1.060–1.173), PLT(OR, 0.987; 95% CI,0.977–0.997), APRI score(OR, 0.281; 95% CI, 0.096–0.819) and spleen area(OR, 1.025; 95% CI, 1.004–1.047) were independent predictors of M/L EV (respectively, P < 0.05), as shown in Table 2. The results showed LSM(OR, 1.021; 95% CI, 0.988–1.055, P = 0.223) was not the independent predictor of M/L EV.

Discussion

At the Baveno VI conference, the concept of compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD) was put forward based on non-invasive tests that would predict the development of complications of cirrhosis [18]. Among patients with compensated cirrhosis or cACLD, at least two different stages have been identified, those with and without CSPH. The various disease stages differ in outcomes, including death, and therefore patients in different stages have different diagnostic and therapeutic needs. At present, there are many noninvasive tools used to predict the liver cirrhosis and portal hypertension, such as platelet count, spleen diameter, spleen stiffness(SS), and LSM [19–21]. But none reached an important role in clinical practice. Baveno VI criteria to rule out EV (LSM < 20 and Platelets > 150) has been validated extensively [18]. In an analysis of a cohort of patients with different etiological liver diseases, Maurice et al. [22] observed that among the patients who fulfilled the Baveno VI criteria, 11% showed EV and 2% were deemed to have high-risk EV, which strengthens the hypothesis that the cut-off values vary according to the cause of the disease. In our study, the patients who fulfilled the Baveno VI criteria, 10.4% showed EV and 3.4% had large EV. A meta analysis including 12 studies showed that SS had an AUROC of 0.86, and a specificity of 76% in identifying EV [23]. The authors concluded that despite the good performance of SS, it was not sufficiently accurate to replace Gastroscopy in the calssification of EV, particularly high-risk EV. We couldn’t get the data of SS in this retrospective study. This study showed that the AUROC of Spleen area for M/L EV in patients with compensated liver cirrhosis was 0.731, and the specificity and sensitivity were 0.726 and 0.707.

Previous studies [24, 25] have shown that the ICG retention test can accurately and noninvasively identify CSPH and severe portal hypertension in patients with compensated cirrhosis. ICG-r15 is quantitative measurement to reflect total hepatic blood flow and liver functional reserve. However, it is reported that ICG-r15 is affected by acute changes in hepatic vascular perfusion [25]. When liver function becomes decompensated, other factors may affect ICG clearance (i.e., anion excretion reserve). That is why we used compensated cirrhosis as the research objective. We hypothesized that among patients with well-preserved liver function, the ICG retention test can directly reflect the change in liver blood flow, predict the grade of PH and EV.

The etiology of our subjects represents the current situation of patients with cirrhosis in China, the majority of patients in this study were infected by hepatitis B virus. Our results showed that ICG-r15, PVD, TBIL, CHE, PT, INR, AAR, APRI, spleen area, Lok index, Park index and LSM in the group A/S EV were lower than those in the group with M/L EV, and ICG-K value, EHBF, Alb, PLT and PSDR were higher, with significant differences between the two groups (p < 0.05). ICG-r15, spleen area, APRI and PLT were independent risk factors for M/L EV (OR values were 1.115, 1.025, 0.281 and 0.987, respectively, p < 0.05). Our study showed that the cut-off value of ICG-r15 for M/L EV in patients with compensated liver cirrhosis was 17.95%, and the specificity and sensitivity were 0.849 and 0.662, the AUROC curve was 0.815. The cut-off value of PLT for M/L EV was 113.5, and the specificity and sensitivity were 0.616 and 0.887, the AUROC was 0.759. The AUROC of ICG-r15 combined with PLT was 0.866, which was more superior than others. In this study, according to the criteria( ICG-r15 < 17.9 and Platelets > 113), 2.5% had large EV.

The results showed that ICG-r15 combined with PLT was an effective tool for the noninvasive evaluation of EV in patients with compensated cirrhosis. Compared with the gold standard, It provides a new low cost, noninvasive diagnostic index of liver functional reserve, yet with strong repeatability. This simple noninvasive test will enable doctors to layer patients to better conduct further noninvasive evaluation of EV.

However, there were some limitations in this study. First, this was a retrospective study, thus selection bias could occur. Second, the majority of patients in this study were infected by hepatitis B virus, which is different from patients infected by hepatitis C virus in most Western countries. Third, our study took place in a single center, further larger and multicenter studies are needed to validate the results. Fourth, ICG test is presented as a non-invasive test while an intravenous access is needed, it needs time to stabilize and several measurements must be done in some times.

Conclusion

The ICG “15-minute” retention test and PLT can predict the grade of EV in patients with compensated liver cirrhosis. Although we are far from the replacement of endoscopy, ICG-r15 combined with PLT seems to be able to identify patients with Medium or large EV in patients with compensated liver cirrhosis.

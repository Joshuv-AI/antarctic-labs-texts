General Zakharov-Shabat equations without Lax operators
Masatoshi Noumi, Takashi Takebe

Abstract
The operators in the Zakharov-Shabat equations of integrable hierarchies are usually defined from the Lax operators. In this article it is shown that the Zakharov-Shabat equations themselves recover the Lax operators under suitable change of independent variables in the case of the KP hierarchy and the modified KP hierarchy (in the matrix formulation).



Introduction

The KP hierarchy, which Sato introduced in
[
Sa1
]
,
(see also
[
Sa2
]
,
[
Sa3
]
,
[
SN
]
,

[
SS
]
), is a system of differential equations for a
microdifferential (or pseudodifferential) operator

\begin{split}L&=\partial+u_{-1}(x,t)\partial^{-1}+u_{-2}(x,t)\partial^{-2}+\dotsb\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}u_{1-j}(x,t)\partial^{1-j},\qquad u_{1}=1,\ u_{0}=0,\ \partial=\partial_{x},\end{split}

with independent variables
x
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)
. The system has
several formulations, among which we consider the
Lax
representation
,

\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}=[B_{n},L],\quad B_{n}:=(L^{n})_{+},\ n=1,2,\dotsc,

(
[P,Q]=PQ-QP
; see (
1.7
) for the notation
P_{+}
), and
the
Zakharov-Shabat representation
,

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-B_{m},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n}\right]=\frac{\partial B_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-\frac{\partial B_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}+[B_{m},B_{n}]=0,\quad m,n=1,2,\dotsc,

which can be interpreted as a
zero-curvature condition
.
(Other formulations are the Sato equations for the wave operator or the
Hirota bilinear equations for the tau-function, upon which we do not
touch in this article.)

It is easy to prove that the Lax representation and the Zakharov-Shabat
representation are equivalent. However, in some problems, the
Zakharov-Shabat equations
without a Lax operator
appear. (See, for
example,
[
Kr
]
.) A natural question is: Do those equations
without a Lax operator define a system equivalent to the KP hierarchy?

In this article we prove that general Zakharov-Shabat equations for
differential operators
B_{n}
of order
n
(
n=1,2,\dotsc
) imply the
existence of the Lax operator which satisfies the KP hierarchy after
suitable coordinate changes. We also prove a similar statement for the
modified KP hierarchy in the matrix formulation. In both cases we first
construct the
L
-operators satisfying the Lax equations by rather
complicated induction, using “semicommutativity” guaranteed by the
Zakharov-Shabat equations. Then we prove that those
L
-operators
reproduce the given
B
-operators by usual procedures for the KP
hierarchy or the modified KP hierarchy with coordinate changes.

The case for the KP hierarchy was published in
[
T2
]
with
numerous typos and insufficient arguments. We correct those typos and
fill gaps in the proofs in the present article. But the gaps in the
proof of
[
T2
]
for the Toda lattice hierarchy are still
open. The half of that proof for the Toda lattice hierarchy is used
with corrections in the proof for the modified KP hierarchy.

This article is divided into two parts: the first part is on the KP
hierarchy and the second part is on the matrix modified KP hierarchy. In
§
1.1
we review the definition of the KP hierarchy
and its basic properties, in particular, the equivalence of the Lax
representation and the Zakharov-Shabat representation in the usual
setting. In §
1.2
we introduce the notion of
“semicommutativity” which will be used in the inductive
construction of the
L
-operator in
§
1.3
. This
L
is a solution of the KP
hierarchy with suitably changed independent variables, as shown in
§
1.4
.

The structure of §
2
for the matrix
modified KP hierarchy is almost the same as that of
§
1
. First we review the matrix formulation
of the modified KP hierarchy introduced by Dickey
[
D
]
in
§
2.1
. (See also
[
T3
]
.) The construction
of the
L
-operator is done by induction in
§
2.2
. The final step to the mKP hierarchy
in §
2.3
is a little bit different from the
approach for the KP hierarchy in §
1.4
.

1
Zakharov-Shabat equations for the KP hierarchy

1.1
Review of the KP hierarchy

First let us briefly review facts about microdifferential operators.
(For details of the theory of microdifferential operators we refer to

[
Sc
]
and
[
Ka
]
.)

We consider microdifferential operators over
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{C}[[x]]
or

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{C}((x))
, where the ring of constants
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathcal{A}[[t]]

for a (
\mathbb{C}
-)algebra
\mathcal{A}
. Here we mean by a
microdifferential
operator
a formal series of the form

(1.1)

P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\partial^{k},\quad(a_{k}\in\mathcal{R},\ a_{k}=0\text{ for }k\gg 0).

Product of two microdifferential operators

(1.2)

P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\partial^{k},\qquad Q=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{k}\partial^{k},

is defined by the extended Leibniz rule,

(1.3)

PQ=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{l+m-r=k,r\geq 0}\binom{l}{r}a_{l}b_{m}^{(r)}\right)\partial^{k}.

The set of microdifferential operators forms an associative

\mathcal{R}
-algebra, which we denote by
\mathcal{E}
. Its subset of differential
operators is denoted by
\mathcal{D}
:

(1.4)

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{P=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}\partial^{k}\in\mathcal{E}\right\}.

The integer

(1.5)

\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P):=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,a_{k}\neq 0\,\}

is called the
order
of the microdifferential operator
P
. (We
define
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(0)=-\infty
.) The order defines the filtration

(\mathcal{E}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathcal{E}
:

(1.6)

\mathcal{E}(n):=\{P\in\mathcal{E}\,|\,\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)\leqq n\}.

Any microdifferential operator
P
(
1.1
) is decomposed
uniquely into the
differential operator part

P_{+}\in\mathcal{D}

and the
negative order part

P_{-}\in\mathcal{E}(-1)
as follows:

(1.7)

\begin{gathered}P=P_{+}+P_{-},\\
P_{+}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}\partial^{k},\quad P_{-}:=\sum_{k\leq-1}a_{k}\partial^{k}.\end{gathered}

Namely
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathcal{E}(-1)
. We call an operator
P
monic,
if it has the form
P=\partial^{n}+(\text{terms of order}<n)
, and denote
the set of monic operators by
\mathcal{E}^{\text{monic}}
.

Using the above terminology, we can formulate the KP hierarchy as
follows. The
KP hierarchy
is a system of differential equations
for a first-order microdifferential operator

(1.8)

L=\partial+u_{-1}\partial^{-1}+u_{-2}\partial^{-2}+\cdots=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{1-i}\partial^{1-i},

with
u_{1}=1,u_{0}=0,u_{i}=u_{i}(t,x)\in\mathcal{R}
, in other words, a
system of differential equations for a series of functions
\{u_{i}\}_{i}
.

The
Lax representation
of the KP hierarchy is

(1.9)

\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=[B_{n},L],\quad B_{n}:=(L^{n})_{+},\qquad n=1,2,\dotsc.

Another description of the KP hierarchy is the
Zakharov-Shabat
representation
or the
zero-curvature equations
,

(1.10)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-B_{m},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n}\right]=\frac{\partial B_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}-\frac{\partial B_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}+[B_{m},B_{n}]=0,\quad m,n=1,2,\dotsc.

It is not difficult to show that, if
L
satisfies the Lax equations
(
1.9
), then it gives a solution of the Zakharov-Shabat
equations (
1.10
), and that, if
B_{n}
’s defined from
L
satisfy
the Zakharov-Shabat equations, then
L
is a solution of the Lax
equations. In fact, if
L
satisfies the Lax equations, its powers
satisfy

(1.11)

\frac{\partial L^{m}}{\partial t_{n}}=[B_{n},L^{m}],

for all
m
and
n
. Subtracting (
1.11
) from the same
equation with
m
and
n
interchanged, we have

(1.12)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial L^{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-\frac{\partial L^{n}}{\partial t_{m}}&=[B_{n},L^{m}]-[B_{m},L^{n}].\\
&=[B_{n},B_{m}]+[(L^{m})_{-},(L^{n})_{-}].\end{split}

The differential operator part of this equation gives the
Zakharov-Shabat equation (
1.10
). Conversely, if
L
defines the

B
-operators satisfying the Zakharov-Shabat equations (
1.10
) for
any
m
and
n
, then we have

(1.13)

\frac{\partial L^{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L^{m}]=\frac{\partial B_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}+\frac{\partial(L^{m})_{-}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},(L^{m})_{-}],

whose right-hand side is of order not more than
n-1
and, in
particular, bounded for any
m
. If the Lax equation (
1.9
)
does not hold and

(1.14)

\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L]=a\partial^{M}+(\text{lower order terms}),\qquad a\neq 0,

it is easy to see that the left-hand side of (
1.13
) is
equal to

\begin{split}\frac{\partial L^{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L^{m}]&=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}L^{k}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L]\right)L^{m-k-1}\\
&=ma\partial^{M+m-1}+(\text{lower order terms}),\end{split}

the order of which is not bounded when
m
increases. This is a
contradiction. Hence the left-hand side of (
1.14
)
should vanish and
L
satisfies the Lax equation (
1.9
).

The above derivation of the Lax equations from the Zakharov-Shabat
representation heavily depends on the existence of the Lax operator

L
. The goal of this article is to derive the Lax equations from the
Zakharov-Shabat equations without starting from the Lax operator.

1.2
Semicommutative operators

In this subsection we prove several facts about “almost” commuting
microdifferential operators. The main tool here is the estimate of the
order of the commutator of microdifferential operators,

(1.15)

\operatorname{ord}\nolimits([A,B])\leqq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(A)+\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(B)-1,\qquad A,B\in\mathcal{E},

which is easily proved.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
P=\partial^{m}+p_{m-1}(x)\partial^{m-1}+p_{m-2}(x)\partial^{m-2}+\dotsb
be a
monic microdifferential operator
\in\mathcal{E}(m)
and
L
its monic
m
-th
root:
L=\partial+u_{0}(x)+u_{-1}(x)\,\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
,
L^{m}=P
. Then for
any
Q\in\mathcal{E}(n)
the following three statements are equivalent
(
r\geqq 0
).

(i)

[P,Q]\in\mathcal{E}(m+n-1-r)
.

(ii)

[L,Q]\in\mathcal{E}(n-r)
.

(iii)

In the expansion

(1.16)

Q=a_{n}(x)\,L^{n}+a_{n-1}(x)\,L^{n-1}+\dotsb,

the first
r
coefficients are constants:
\partial a_{i}(x)=0

(
i=n,n-1,\dotsc,n-r+1
).

Remark 1.2
.

The monic
m
-th root
L
of
P
always exists, as the condition

L^{m}=P
gives relations of
u_{i}
’s and
p_{j}
’s as differential
polynomials and by solving these relations recursively, we can express
each
u_{i}
as a differential polynomial of
p_{j}
’s.

In particular,
u_{0}(x)=p_{m-1}(x)/m
. Hence, if
p_{m-1}(x)=0
and
P

is of the form
\partial^{m}+p_{m-2}(x)\partial^{m-2}+\dotsb
, then
u_{0}(x)=0

and
L=\partial+u_{-1}(x)\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
.

Remark 1.3
.

An expansion like (
1.16
) always exists. In fact, if

Q=q_{n}(x)\partial^{n}+(\text{terms of order }<n)
, we take
q_{n}(x)
as

a_{n}(x)
. Then
Q^{\prime}:=Q-a_{n}(x)L^{n}
is of order
<n
and we can find

a_{k}
’s recursively.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

The proofs of (ii) from (iii) and of (i) from (ii) are
straightforward.

To prove (iii) from (i), assume that
[P,Q]\in\mathcal{E}(m+n-1-r)
. For a
non-constant element
a\not\in\mathcal{C}
,
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits([P,a])=\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(m(\partial a)\partial^{m-1}+(\text{terms with orders}<m-1))=m-1
. Hence, if

a_{k+1},a_{k+2},\dotsc,a_{n}
in the expansion (
1.16
) are
constants and
a_{k}\not\in\mathcal{C}
, then
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits([P,Q])=(m-1)+k
, as

\begin{split}[P,Q]&=\sum_{j\leq n}[P,a_{j}]L^{j}\\
&=[P,a_{k}]L^{k}+[P,a_{k-1}]L^{k-1}+\dotsb.\end{split}

Since
[P,Q]\in\mathcal{E}(m+n-1-r)
, we have
m-1+k\leqq m+n-1-r
, i.e.,

k\leqq n-r
, which implies (iii).
∎

Definition 1.4
.

Monic operators
P,Q\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{monic}}
satisfying the
conditions in Proposition
1.1
are called
r
-commutative
and we denote
P\underset{r}{\sim}Q
. In general, if

P\underset{r}{\sim}Q
for some
r\geqq 0
, we say that
P
and
Q
are
semicommutative.

Corollary 1.5
.

The relation
\underset{r}{\sim}
is an equivalence relation between monic
operators.

Proof.

We have only to check that
P\underset{r}{\sim}Q
and
Q\underset{r}{\sim}R

imply
P\underset{r}{\sim}R
for monic microdifferential operators
P
,
Q

and
R
.

Proposition
1.1
says that there are expansions

P=\sum_{k\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)}p_{k}(x)L^{k},\qquad R=\sum_{l\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)}r_{l}(x)L^{l},

where
\partial p_{k}(x)=\partial r_{l}(x)=0
for
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)-r+1\leqq k\leqq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)
and
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)-r+1\leqq l\leqq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)
. Therefore,

\begin{split}[P,R]&=\left[\sum_{k\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)}p_{k}(x)L^{k},\sum_{l\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)}r_{l}(x)L^{l}\right]\\
&=\left[\sum_{k\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)-r}p_{k}(x)L^{k},\sum_{l\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)}r_{l}(x)L^{l}\right]\\
&\qquad+\left[\sum_{\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)-r+1\leq k\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)}p_{k}(x)L^{k},\sum_{l\leq\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)-r}r_{l}(x)L^{l}\right].\end{split}

Both commutators in the last expression have orders not more than

\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(P)+\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(R)-r-1
. Hence
P\underset{r}{\sim}R
by
Proposition
1.1
(i).
∎

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
\{B_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
be a sequence of monic differential
operators of order
n
:

B_{n}=\partial^{n}+b_{n,n-1}\partial^{n-1}+b_{n,n-2}\partial^{n-2}+\dotsb+b_{n,0}
.

For any
r\geqq 0
the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

For any
n
and
m
, if
n\leqq m
, then

[B_{n},B_{m}]\in\mathcal{E}(m-1-r)
, i.e.,
B_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}B_{m}
.

(ii)

There exists a monic first-order microdifferential operator
L

such that
B_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}L
for any
n
.

(iii)

There exists a monic first-order microdifferential operator
L

such that in the expansion
B_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}a_{n,k}L^{k}
,

a_{n,n},\dotsc,a_{n,1-r}\in\mathcal{C}
for any
n
.

Moreover, if each
B_{n}
is of the form
\partial^{n}+b_{n,n-2}\partial^{n-2}+\dotsb+b_{n,0}
, then
L
is of the form
\partial+u_{-1}\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is a consequence of
Proposition
1.1
. Indeed, by taking
B_{n}
as
Q
and

n+r
as
r
in Proposition
1.1
, statements (ii) and
(iii) there correspond to statements (ii) and (iii) in
Proposition
1.6
respectively.

If all
B_{n}
’s are expanded as in (iii) with constant

a_{n,n},\dotsc,a_{n,1-r}
, then the commutator of
B_{n}
and
B_{m}

(
n\leqq m
) is

\begin{split}[B_{n},B_{m}]&=\left[\sum_{k\leq n}a_{n,k}L^{k},\sum_{l\leq m}a_{m,l}L^{l}\right]\\
&=\left[\sum_{k\leq-r}a_{n,k}L^{k},B_{m}\right]+\left[\sum_{1-r\leq k\leq n}a_{n,k}L^{k},\sum_{l\leq-r}a_{m,l}L^{l}\right].\end{split}

The first commutator in the last line is an operator of order not
more than
-r+m-1
, while the second commutator is an operator of order
not more than
n-r+1
. Therefore
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits([B_{n},B_{m}])\leqq m-r-1
, which
implies (i).

Conversely, let us assume the statement (i). We denote the monic
n
-th
root of
B_{n}
by
L_{n}
:

(1.17)

L_{n}=\partial+u_{n,0}(x)+u_{n,-1}\partial^{-1}+\dotsb,\qquad B_{n}=(L_{n})^{n}.

Then, Proposition
1.1
implies
L_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}L_{m}
if

n\leqq m
because of
B_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}B_{m}
.

We construct a sequence of monic first-order operators
\{\tilde{L}_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
which satisfies

(1.18)

\tilde{L}_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}L_{n},\qquad\tilde{L}_{n}-\tilde{L}_{n-1}\in\mathcal{E}(2-n-r).

Due to the second condition in (
1.18
) the sequence has a limit
in the topology of
\mathcal{E}
by the order,
L:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{L}_{n}
. The operator
\tilde{L}_{n}
differs from
L
by an operator
\tilde{L}_{n}^{c}:=\tilde{L}_{n}-L
of order not more than
1-n-r
as
\tilde{L}_{k+1}-\tilde{L}_{k}\in\mathcal{E}(1-k-r)\subset\mathcal{E}(1-n-r)
for
k\geqq n
. Hence,

[L,\tilde{L}_{n}]=[L,\tilde{L}_{n}^{c}]\in\mathcal{E}(1+(1-n-r)-1)=\mathcal{E}(1-n-r),\text{ i.e., }L\underset{n+r}{\sim}\tilde{L}_{n}.

Since
\tilde{L}_{n}\underset{n+r}{\sim}L_{n}
by the first condition in
(
1.18
), we have
L\underset{n+r}{\sim}L_{n}
by
Corollary
1.5
.

Now Proposition
1.1
implies that

L\underset{n+r}{\sim}B_{n}
, which is nothing but the statement (ii) of
Proposition
1.6
.

The construction of
\tilde{L}_{n}
is by induction. We start from
\tilde{L}_{1}=L_{1}
and use the following lemma to find
\tilde{L}_{n}
from
\tilde{L}_{n-1}
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
M
and
N
be monic first-order operators which are mutually

s
-commutative:
M\underset{s}{\sim}N
. Then there exists a monic
first-order operator
\tilde{N}
such that

(1.19)

\tilde{N}\underset{s+1}{\sim}N,\qquad\tilde{N}-M\in\mathcal{E}(1-s).

Applying this lemma to
M=\tilde{L}_{n-1}
and
N=L_{n}
(in this case

s=n-1+r
), we obtain
\tilde{L}_{n}
as
\tilde{N}
. (The condition

M\underset{s}{\sim}N
, i.e.,
\tilde{L}_{n-1}\underset{n-1+r}{\sim}L_{n}
follows
from
L_{n-1}\underset{n-1+r}{\sim}L_{n}
which we showed after
(
1.17
) and the induction hypothesis
\tilde{L}_{n-1}\underset{n-1+r}{\sim}L_{n-1}
.)

Proof of Lemma
1.7
.

Proposition
1.1
implies that in the expansion

N=\sum_{n\leq 1}a_{n}M^{n}=M+a_{0}+a_{-1}M^{-1}+\dotsb,

the first
s
coefficients
a_{1}=1
,
a_{0},\dotsc,a_{2-s}
are
constant. Let us define
\tilde{N}
by

\begin{split}\tilde{N}&:=N-(a_{0}+a_{-1}M^{-1}+\dotsb+a_{2-s}M^{2-s})\\
&=M+a_{1-s}M^{1-s}+a_{-s}M^{-s}+\dotsb\in M+\mathcal{E}(1-s),\end{split}

which satisfies the second condition in (
1.19
).

Since
M\underset{s}{\sim}N
, one has
M^{n}\underset{s}{\sim}N
, i.e.,

[M^{n},N]\in\mathcal{E}(n+1-s)
for any
n
by
Proposition
1.1
. Therefore
[M^{n},N]\in\mathcal{E}(-s)
for

n\leqq-1
. Hence,

[\tilde{N},N]=[N,N]-\sum_{n=2-s}^{0}a_{n}[M^{n},N]=-\sum_{n=2-s}^{-1}a_{n}[M^{n},N]\in\mathcal{E}(-s),

which is the first condition (
1.19
).
∎

The last statement of Proposition
1.6
is due to
Remark
1.2
and the above construction of
L
.
∎

1.3
Construction of the Lax operator of the KP hierarchy

In this subsection we assume the Zakharov-Shabat equations for the
sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsb}
of monic differential operators and
construct a first order monic microdifferential operator which satisfies
the Lax equations with the given
B_{n}
’s.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\{B_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
be a sequence of monic differential
operators of order
n
:
\operatorname{ord}\nolimits(B_{n})=n
. If this sequence satisfies the
system of Zakharov-Shabat equations,

(1.20)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-B_{m}\right]=\frac{\partial B_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}-\frac{\partial B_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}+[B_{n},B_{m}]=0,

for
n,m=1,2,\dotsc
, then there exists a monic first-order
microdifferential operator
L=\partial+u_{0}+u_{-1}\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
which
satisfies the Lax equations

(1.21)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L\right]=\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L]=0.

for
n=1,2,\dotsc
.

Moreover, if each
B_{n}
is of the form
\partial^{n}+b_{n,n-2}\partial^{n-2}+\dotsb+b_{n,0}
, then
u_{0}=0
and
L
is of the form
\partial+u_{-1}\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
.

Proof.

Since
\partial_{t_{m}}B_{n}-\partial_{t_{n}}B_{m}\in\mathcal{E}(m-1)
for
n\leqq m
, the
Zakharov-Shabat equation (
1.20
) implies

[B_{n},B_{m}]\in\mathcal{E}(m-1)
. Proposition
1.6
(
r=0
) guarantees
existence of the operator
L_{0}
of the form

L_{0}=\partial+u_{0,0}(x)+u_{0,-1}(x)\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
which satisfies

[B_{n},L_{0}]\in\mathcal{E}(0)
for any
n
.

Starting from
L_{0}
, we construct a sequence
\{L_{r}\}_{r=0,1,2\dotsc}

such that

(1.22)

\frac{\partial L_{r}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L_{r}]\in\mathcal{E}(-r)

for any
n
and

(1.23)

L_{r+1}-L_{r}\in\mathcal{E}(-r).

It is obvious that
L_{0}
satisfies (
1.22
) (
r=0
) by
construction.

Assume that
L_{r}
satisfies (
1.22
) for
r\geqq 0
. We
construct
L_{r+1}
from
L_{r}
in the form

(1.24)

L_{r+1}=L_{r}-fL_{r}^{-r},

where
\partial f=0
. As
L_{r+1}-L_{r}=-fL_{r}^{-r}\in\mathcal{E}(-r)
, the
condition (
1.23
) is satisfied.

Let us define functions
g_{n}
and
\varphi_{n,k}
by

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r}\right]=\frac{\partial L_{r}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},L_{r}]=g_{n}\partial^{-r}+\dotsb,

\displaystyle B_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}\varphi_{n,k}L_{r}^{k},\qquad\varphi_{n,n}=1.

Since
[B_{n},L_{r}]\in\mathcal{E}(0)
by assumption (
1.22
),

B_{n}\underset{n}{\sim}L_{r}
. Hence it follows from Proposition
1.6

(iii) that
\partial\varphi_{n,k}=0
for
k\geqq 1
.

Let us compute

R:=\frac{\partial B_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-[B_{n},B_{m}]=\frac{\partial B_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}\in\mathcal{E}(n-1),

using the expansion of
B_{m}
. By the definition of
g_{n}
, we have

(1.25)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r}^{k}\right]=kg_{n}\partial^{k-1-r}+\dotsb

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, as
\partial\varphi_{m,k}=0
for
k\geqq 1
,

\begin{split}R&=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},\sum_{k\leq m}\varphi_{m,k}L_{r}^{k}\right]\\
&=\sum_{k\leq m}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},\varphi_{m,k}\right]L_{r}^{k}+\sum_{k\leq m}\varphi_{m,k}(kg_{n}\partial^{k-1-r}+\dotsb)\\
&=\sum_{k\leq m}\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}}L_{r}^{k}-\sum_{k\leq 0}[B_{n},\varphi_{m,k}]L_{r}^{k}+\sum_{k\leq m}\varphi_{m,k}(kg_{n}\partial^{k-1-r}+\dotsb).\end{split}

Since
R
and the second sum in the last expression are of order not
more than
n-1
,

-\sum_{k\leq m}\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}}L_{r}^{k}\equiv mg_{n}\partial^{m-1-r}+(\text{lower order terms})\mod\mathcal{E}(n-1).

Therefore, if
m>n+r
,

(1.26)

\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}}=\begin{cases}0,&(k>m-1-r),\\
-mg_{n},&(k=m-1-r).\end{cases}

Differentiating the case
k=m-1-r
by
x
, we have

(1.27)

-m\,\partial g_{n}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}\partial\varphi_{m,m-1-r}.

Let us take
m
larger than
n+r+1
. Then
m>n+r+1>n+r
and

m-1-r>n\geqq 1
. Therefore by (
1.27
) and

\partial\varphi_{n,k}=0
(
k\geqq 1
), we have
\partial g_{n}=0
.

To find
f
in (
1.24
), set

(1.28)

f_{m}(t):=-\frac{1}{m}(\varphi_{m,m-1-r}(t)-\varphi_{m,m-1-r}(t=0)).

Then, by (
1.26
), when
m>n+r+1
, we have

\partial_{t_{n}}f_{m}=g_{n}
which does not depend on
m
. This means that
f_{m}

has a limit
f:=\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}
in
\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc]]
with
the topology by valuation (
\operatorname{val}t_{n}=n
). (Note that
f_{m}(t=0)=0
for
any
m
.) We have
\partial_{t_{n}}f=g_{n}
and, since
\varphi_{m,k}

(
k\geqq 1
) is constant with respect
x
,
\partial f=0
.

If we set
L_{r+1}:=L_{r}-fL_{r}^{-r}
as in (
1.24
),
L_{r+1}

satisfies

\begin{split}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r+1}\right]&=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r}\right]-\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},fL_{r}^{-r}\right]\\
&=\bigl(g_{n}\partial^{-r}+(\text{terms of order\,}\leqq-r-1)\bigr)\\
&\qquad-\bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{n}}L_{r}^{-r}+f\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r}^{-r}\right]\bigr)\\
&=f\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},L_{r}^{-r}\right]+(\text{terms of order\,}\leqq-r-1),\end{split}

which is of order not more than
-r-1
by (
1.25
). Hence, we
have an operator
L_{r+1}
satisfying the conditions
(
1.22
) for
r\mapsto r+1
.

Thanks to the condition (
1.23
), the sequence has a
limit
L:=\lim_{r\to\infty}L_{r}
in the topology by filtration of

\mathcal{E}
. The limit
L
satisfies the Lax equations
(
1.21
) as the limit of (
1.22
).

The final statement of Proposition
1.8
follows from
the final statement of Proposition
1.6
and the construction
of
L
.

This completes the proof of Proposition
1.8
.
∎

1.4
Recovery of the KP hierarchy

Finally, we connect the above result with the KP hierarchy by means of
a coordinate change.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\{B_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsb}
be a sequence of monic differential
operators of the form

(1.29)

B_{n}=\partial^{n}+b_{n,n-1}\partial^{n-1}+\dotsb+b_{n,0},

satisfying the system of Zakharov-Shabat equations
(
1.20
) for
n,m=1,2,\dotsc
. Then there exists a monic
first-order microdifferential operator,

(1.30)

L=\partial+u_{0}(x)+u_{-1}(x)\partial^{-1}+\dotsb,

a coordinate change,

(1.31)

t_{n}\mapsto\tilde{t}_{n}=t_{n}+O(t_{n+1},t_{n+2},\dotsc)

and a function
f_{0}=f_{0}(x,t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)
such that:

•

The operator
L
satisfies Lax equations,

(1.32)

\frac{\partial L}{\partial\tilde{t}_{n}}=[(L^{n})_{+},L],

with respect to the new time variables
(\tilde{t}_{n})_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
.

•

Each
B_{n}
is expressed in terms of
L
as

(1.33)

B_{n}=\tilde{B}_{n}+\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{n-1}}{\partial t_{n}}\tilde{B}_{n-1}+\dotsb+\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{1}}{\partial t_{n}}\tilde{B}_{1}+\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t_{n}},\quad\tilde{B}_{n}:=e^{f_{0}}(L^{n})_{+}e^{-f_{0}}.

Moreover, if
b_{n,n-1}=0
in (
1.29
) for all
n
, then

u_{0}=0
, which means that
L
is a Lax operator of the KP hierarchy
with respect to the variables
(\tilde{t}_{n})_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
. Moreover

\partial f_{0}=0
, from which it follows that
\tilde{B}_{n}=(L^{n})_{+}
.

Proof.

Let us denote the operator
L
obtained in
Proposition
1.8
by
\tilde{L}
. Expanding
B_{n}
as a
power series of
\tilde{L}
,

B_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n,k}{\tilde{L}}^{k},\quad\varphi_{n,n}=1,

and taking the differential operator part, we obtain the expression
of
B_{n}
as follows:

(1.34)

B_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{n,k}\tilde{B}_{k},\qquad\tilde{B}_{n}:=({\tilde{L}}^{n})_{+},

Since
[B_{n},\tilde{L}]=\partial_{t_{n}}\tilde{L}\in\mathcal{E}(0)
,
B_{n}
and

\tilde{L}
are
n
-commutative:
B_{n}\underset{n}{\sim}\tilde{L}
. Therefore
Proposition
1.1
(iii) implies

(1.35)

\partial\varphi_{n,k}=0\quad\text{for $1\leqq k\leqq n$,}

from which it follows that
[\varphi_{n,k},\tilde{L}]=0
for

1\leqq k\leqq n
. Let us define an operator
B_{n}^{c}
by

(1.36)

B_{n}^{c}:=B_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{n,k}\tilde{L}^{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{n,k}(\tilde{L}^{n})_{-}.

Replacing
B_{n}
in the Lax equation
[\partial_{t_{n}}-B_{n},\tilde{L}]=0
by

B_{n}^{c}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{n,k}\tilde{L}^{k}
, we obtain

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n}^{c}-\varphi_{n,0},\tilde{L}\right]=0,

because of (
1.35
).
Using these Lax equations and (
1.35
), we have

(1.37)

\begin{split}0&=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-B_{m}\right]\\
&=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-B_{n}^{c}-\varphi_{n,0},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-B_{m}^{c}-\varphi_{m,0}\right]+\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{n,k}}{\partial t_{m}}-\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}}\right)\tilde{L}^{k}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{n,k}}{\partial t_{m}}-\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}}\right)\tilde{L}^{k}+(\text{a negative order operator}).\end{split}

Here we define
\varphi_{n,k}=0
for
k>n
. Because of this convention,

(1.38)

\frac{\partial\varphi_{n,k}}{\partial t_{m}}=\frac{\partial\varphi_{m,k}}{\partial t_{n}},

holds trivially for sufficiently large
k
. Starting from this large

k
and proceeding downwards by (
1.37
), we
can show inductively that (
1.38
) is true for
arbitrary
k\geqq 0
.
This is the compatibility condition of the system of differential
equations for
f_{k}(t)
,

(1.39)

\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}=\varphi_{n,k},\qquad n\geqq 1.

As
\varphi_{n,n}=1
and
\varphi_{n,k}=0
for
k>n
, the solution of
equation (
1.39
) for
k\geqq 1
with the initial
condition
f_{k}(t=0)=0
has the form

(1.40)

f_{k}(t)=t_{k}+(\text{a function of }t_{k+1},t_{k+2},\dotsc)\qquad(k\geqq 1)

and they do not depend on
x
because of (
1.35
).

Now we introduce a coordinate change
(
1.31
) by

(1.41)

\tilde{t}_{n}:=f_{n}(t).

The operator
L:=e^{-f_{0}}\tilde{L}e^{f_{0}}
satisfies an equation

\begin{split}\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}&=e^{-f_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{L}}{\partial t_{n}}-\left[\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t_{n}},\tilde{L}\right]\right)e^{f_{0}}\\
&=e^{-f_{0}}\left(\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{n,k}\tilde{B}_{k},\tilde{L}\right]-\left[\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t_{n}},\tilde{L}\right]\right)e^{f_{0}}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}e^{-f_{0}}[\tilde{B}_{k},\tilde{L}]e^{f_{0}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}[e^{-f_{0}}\tilde{B}_{k}e^{f_{0}},L],\end{split}

because of (
1.34
), (
1.41
),
(
1.39
) (
k=0
) and (
1.35
). Note that

e^{-f_{0}}\tilde{B}_{k}e^{f_{0}}=(L^{k})_{+}
because

(e^{-f_{0}}Pe^{f_{0}})_{+}=(e^{-f_{0}}P_{+}e^{f_{0}})_{+}+(e^{-f_{0}}P_{-}e^{f_{0}})_{+}=e^{-f_{0}}P_{+}e^{f_{0}}

for any
P\in\mathcal{E}
. Hence, we have

\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}[(L^{k})_{+},L].

On the other hand, we have

\frac{\partial L}{\partial t_{n}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}},

as
\partial\tilde{t}_{k}/\partial t_{n}=0
if
k>n
by (
1.40
)
and (
1.41
). Thus we have obtained equations

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}[(L^{k})_{+},L].

By applying the inverse matrix of the Jacobian matrix
(\partial\tilde{t}_{n}/t_{m})_{n,m=1,2,\dotsc}
to this system, we have the Lax equations
(
1.32
) for
L
with time variables
(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2},\dotsc)
.

The relations (
1.33
) of
B_{n}
and
\tilde{B}_{n}
follow from
(
1.34
).

If
b_{n,n-1}=0
for all
n
,
\tilde{L}
is of the form
\tilde{L}=\partial+u_{-1}\partial^{-1}+\dotsb
as we showed in
Proposition
1.8
. Therefore in the above proof we have

[B_{n},\tilde{L}]\in\mathcal{E}(-1)
and
B_{n}\underset{n+1}{\sim}\tilde{L}
. Therefore we have
\partial\varphi_{n,k}=0
for
0\leqq k\leqq n

instead of
1\leqq k\leqq n
in (
1.35
). Hence
f_{0}
does
not depend on
x
as other
f_{k}
’s (
k\geqq 1
) and

L=e^{-f_{0}}\tilde{L}e^{f_{0}}=\tilde{L}
,
which is of the form (
1.8
) of the Lax operator of the KP
hierarchy. Therefore
L
constructed above gives a solution of the KP
hierarchy.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.9
.
∎

2
Zakharov-Shabat equations for the modified KP hierarchy

2.1
Review of the mKP hierarchy

The modified KP hierarchy (or the mKP hierarchy for short) has two
formulations; one is by microdifferential operators (a series of the KP
hierarchies with compatibility conditions) and the other is by

\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
matrices. They are equivalent as shown by

[
D
]
. Here we consider the
matrix mKP
hierarchy
.
1
1

1

Dickey calls the matrix formulation “the
discrete KP hierarchy”. But usually the name “discrete KP” is used
for a system with discrete independent variables. So here we call the
system the matrix mKP hierarchy.

Let us take a
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
matrix
{\boldsymbol{L}}
of the form

(2.1)

{\boldsymbol{L}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\diag_{s}[u_{1-j}(s)]\Lambda^{1-j},

whose elements
u_{j}(s)
belong to
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{A}[[t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc]]
. Here

\Lambda
is the shift matrix
\Lambda:=(\delta_{\mu+1,\nu})_{\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\diag_{s}[a(s)]
is the diagonal matrix with the

s
-th diagonal element
a(s)
,

(2.2)

\diag_{s}[a(s)]:=\begin{pmatrix}\ddots&&&&&&\\
&a(-2)&&&&&\\
&&a(-1)&&&&\\
&&&a(0)&&&\\
&&&&a(1)&&\\
&&&&&a(2)&\\
&&&&&&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The following commutation relation of the matrix
\Lambda
and a
diagonal matrix is fundamental in the following:

(2.3)

\Lambda\diag_{s}[a(s)]=\diag_{s}[a(s+1)]\Lambda.

In other words, we can interpret
\Lambda
as a difference operator

\Lambda=e^{\partial_{s}}
,
e^{\partial_{s}}\bigl(a(s)\bigr)=a(s+1)
. Taking this
interpretation implicitly, hereafter we do not write “
\diag_{s}
”.

We define
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}
(
n=1,2,3,\dotsc
) from
{\boldsymbol{L}}
by

(2.4)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}:=({\boldsymbol{L}}^{n})_{+},

where the suffix
+
stands for the “upper triangular part”: for a

\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
matrix

A=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{j}(s)\Lambda^{j}
, we denote the upper
triangular and the strictly lower triangular part by

(2.5)

A_{+}:=\sum_{j\geq 0}a_{j}(s)\Lambda^{j},\quad A_{-}:=\sum_{j<0}a_{j}(s)\Lambda^{j}.

Definition 2.1
.

The
matrix representation of the modified KP hierarchy
, or the

matrix mKP hierarchy
for short, is the Lax equations for

{\boldsymbol{L}}
,

(2.6)

\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial t_{n}}=[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}],\qquad(n=1,2,3,\dotsc)

for a matrix function
{\boldsymbol{L}}
of the form (
2.1
).

The Zakharov-Shabat equations for this system is

(2.7)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}\right]=\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}+[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}]=0,\quad m,n=1,2,\dotsc.

The whole system of these equations (
2.7
) is equivalent to the
Lax representation (
2.6
), which can be proved exactly in
the same way as the corresponding statement for the KP hierarchy in
§
1.1
.

2.2
Construction of the Lax operator of the mKP hierarchy

In this section we prove that the Zakharov-Shabat equations of certain
infinite matrices imply the existence of a matrix
{\boldsymbol{L}}
, which
satisfies the Lax equations.

The main strategy of the construction of
{\boldsymbol{L}}
is similar to that for
the KP hierarchy in §
1
, but, since the
commutation relation (
2.3
) replaces the commutation
relation
[\partial,f]=f^{\prime}
for the (micro)differential operators, we cannot
use the estimate (
1.15
) of orders.

We start from the
B
-operators,

(2.8)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b^{(n)}_{j}(s)\Lambda^{j}\qquad(b^{(n)}_{n}(s)=1),

without a Lax operator. The elements
b^{(n)}_{j}(s)
depend on

t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)
besides the discrete variable
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

First we prove the existence of the
{\boldsymbol{L}}
-operator satisfying the Lax
equations.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\{{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
be a sequence of matrices of the form
(
2.8
). If this sequence satisfies the system of
Zakharov-Shabat equations,

(2.9)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}\right]=\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}}{\partial t_{n}}-\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}}{\partial t_{m}}+[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}]=0,

for
m,n=1,2,\dotsc
, then there exists a matrix of the form,

(2.10)

{\boldsymbol{L}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}u_{1-j}(s)\Lambda^{1-j},\qquad(u_{1}(s)=1),

which satisfies the Lax equations

(2.11)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}\right]=\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial t_{n}}-[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}]=0.

for
m,n=1,2,\dotsc
.

As in the case of the KP hierarchy in the previous section, we construct

L
-operators inductively. To measure the degree of approximation, we
often use the following sets of infinite matrices:

(2.12)

\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{n}:=\left\{\left.A=\sum_{k\leq n}a_{k}(s)\Lambda^{k}\,\right|\,a_{k}(s)\in\mathcal{R}\right\}.

The first approximation of
{\boldsymbol{L}}
is obtained by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
2.2
there exists

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}
of the form (
2.10
) such that

(2.13)

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{0},

Proof.

We construct a matrix
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}
of the form (
2.10
)
satisfying (
2.13
) inductively.

Suppose that we have
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}
of the form (
2.10
)

(2.14)

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{0}

for
n=1,2,\dotsc,r

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{1}
as

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,1}
.

Note that we can always expand
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}
as

(2.15)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}a^{(r)}_{n,k}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},\qquad a^{(r)}_{n,n}(s)=1,

because of the form of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}
, (
2.10
). The
condition (
2.14
) means that the coefficients

a^{(r)}_{n,k}(s)
(
k=0,\dotsc,n
) do not depend on
s
. In fact, this
follows from the equivalence of
[a(s),\Lambda]=0
and
a(s+1)=a(s)

for all

.

Therefore under our induction assumption

(

,

) do not depend on
s
. Moreover, the

a^{(r)}_{r+1,k}(s)
of the expansion
(
2.15

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}

s
for

. This can be shown from (
2.9
) as
follows. Using the expansion (
2.15
), we have

(2.16)

\begin{split}[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}]&\equiv\sum_{l\leq r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}a^{(r)}_{m,k}[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},a^{(r)}_{r+1,l}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{l}\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}\\
&=\sum_{l\leq r}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a^{(r)}_{m,k}[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},a^{(r)}_{r+1,l}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{l}.\end{split}

for
m=1,\dotsc,r
. (Note that a term

a^{(r)}_{m,k}[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},a^{(r)}_{r+1,l}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{l}

with
k<0
and
l\leqq r+1
belongs to
\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}
and that

a^{(r)}_{r+1,r+1}(s)
does not depend on
s
, which implies that the

[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},a^{(r)}_{r+1,r+1}]
always vanishes.) The
expression (
2.16
) should be zero modulo
\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}
, because the
Zakharov-Shabat equation (
2.9
) implies

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}]=\partial_{t_{m}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}-\partial_{t_{r+1}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}
.

The top degree term in the expansion of (
2.16
) as a series
of
\Lambda
would come from the term with
(k,l)=(m,r)
(if it is not
zero), which is equal to

[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{m},a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r}=(a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s+m)-a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s))\Lambda^{r+m}+\dotsb
.
Hence, if we take
m=1
, the condition

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}
implies

a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s+1)=a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s)
, namely,
a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}(s)

does not depend on
s
.

Therefore there is no contribution from the term
l=r
in
(
2.16
) and the top degree term in the
\Lambda
-expansion
would come from the term with
(k,l)=(m,r-1)
(if it is not zero),
which is equal to

[{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{m},a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r-1}=(a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s+m)-a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s))\Lambda^{r-1+m}+\dotsb
.
Hence, if
2\leqq m\leqq r
, the condition

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{r+1}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}
implies

a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s+m)=a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s)
. Moreover, if
r\geqq 3
,

a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s+3)=a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s+2)=a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s)
,
which means that
a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}(s)
does not depend on
s
.

Repeating the same argument inductively on
l
downwards, we can show
the following for
r\geqq 1
.

•

a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}
is independent of
s
.

•

If
r\geqq 3
,
a^{(r)}_{r+1,r-1}
, …,
a^{(r)}_{r+1,2}
are
independent of
s
.

•

If
r\geqq 2
,
a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)
is periodic:

a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s+r)=a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)
. (If
r=1
, this
holds because
a^{(r)}_{r+1,r}
is independent of
s
.)

Now we define
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}
by modifying
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}
:

(2.17)

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}:={\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}+a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{1-r}+b(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{-r},

where
b(s)
will be determined later. It is easy to see that

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{k}\equiv{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k}\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1}
for
r\geqq 2
,

k<r
. The
r
-th power of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}
is

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{r}\equiv{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r}+c_{1}^{(r)}\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1},

where
c_{1}^{(r)}=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s+k)
, which does
not depend on
s
because of the periodicity of

a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)
. The
(r+1)
-st power is

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{r+1}\equiv{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r+1}+(c_{1}^{(r)}+a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}\\
+\left((\text{terms determined by }{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r})+\sum_{k=0}^{r}b(s+k)\right)\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1}.

Hence, if we choose
b(s)
appropriately, we can make the last term

(\dotsb)
in the above expression equal to an arbitrary sequence

a(s)
. (For example, first set
b(0)=b(1)=\dotsb=b(r-1)=0
and
determine
b(r),b(r+1),\dotsc
recursively from
a(0),a(1),\dotsc
. The coefficients
b(-1),b(-2),\dotsc
are determined from

a(-1),a(-2),\dotsc
.)

Summarising, by appropriate choices of
b(s)
in
(
2.17
) we can construct
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}
whose powers
are:

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{k}\equiv\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k},&k<r,\\
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r}+c_{1}^{(r)},&k=r,\\
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{r+1}+(c_{1}^{(r)}+a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}+a^{(r)}_{r+1,0}(s),&k=r+1,\end{cases}

modulo
\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1}
. Reversing these relations, we can rewrite
powers of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}
by those of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}
:

(2.18)

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}^{k}\equiv\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{k},&k<r,\\
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{r}-c_{1}^{(r)},&k=r,\\
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{r+1}-(c_{1}^{(r)}+a^{(r)}_{r+1,1}(s)){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}-a^{(r)}_{r+1,0}(s),&r\geqq 2,k=r+1,\\
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,2}^{2}-(c_{1}^{(1)}+a^{(1)}_{2,1}(s)){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,2}+2(c_{1}^{(1)})^{2}-a^{(1)}_{2,0}(s),&r=1,k=2,\end{cases}

modulo
\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1}
. Substituting them in the expansion
(
2.15
) of
B_{n}
(
n=1,\dotsc,r,r+1
), we obtain the
expansion

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}a^{(r+1)}_{n,k}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}^{k},\qquad a^{(r+1)}_{n,n}(s)=1,

where the coefficients
a^{(r+1)}_{n,k}(s)
do not depend on
s
if

n=1,\dotsc,r,r+1
and
k=0,\dotsc,n
, which is the desired property of

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r+1}
.

By construction (
2.17
) the sequence

\{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0,r}\}_{r=1,2,\dotsc}
converges to a matrix
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}
of
the form (
2.10
) with respect to the topology induced by
filtration
\{\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}
. The limit
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}
satisfies
(
2.13
). In other words,
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}
’s are expanded as

(2.19)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}a^{[0]}_{n,k}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}^{k},\qquad a^{[0]}_{n,n}(s)=1,

where the coefficients
a^{[0]}_{n,k}(s)
(
k=0,\dotsc,n
) do not
depend on
s
. Construction of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{0}
is over.
∎

Lemma
2.3
is the initial step of the induction in
the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Take any
r=0,1,\dotsc
. Let
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}
be a matrix of the form
(
2.10
) satisfying

(2.20)

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r}.

Then there exists
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}
satisfying (
2.20
)
with
r+1
instead of
r
. The difference of
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}
and

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}
is in
\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r}
.

Proof.

We define
g_{n}(s)=g_{n}(s;t)
by

(2.21)

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]\equiv g_{n}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r-1}.

First we prove that
g_{n}(s)
does not depend on
s
. Because of the
Zakharov-Shabat equations (
2.9
),

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]]=[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]].

Hence, if
1\leqq n<m
,

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},g_{m}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]\equiv[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},g_{n}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{m-r-1},

from which it follows that

0\equiv[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},g_{n}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{m-r-1}.

Since the commutator in the right-hand side is expanded as

(g_{n}(s+m)-g_{n}(s))\Lambda^{m-r}+\dotsb
, we obtain
g_{n}(s+m)=g_{n}(s)

for any
m
greater than
n
. Therefore
g_{n}(s)=g_{n}
does not depend on

s
.

Note that the assumption (
2.20
) implies

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{0}
, which means that the coefficients

a^{[r]}_{n,k}(s)
in the expansion of
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}
,

(2.22)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}a^{[r]}_{n,k}(s){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{k},\qquad a^{[r]}_{n,n}(s)=1,

do not depend on
s
if
k=0,\dotsc,n
, as in the proof of
Lemma
2.3
.

Substituting the expansion (
2.22
) into the
Zakharov-Shabat equation (
2.9
), we have the following
due to (
2.21
):

\begin{split}\partial_{t_{n}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}&=[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}]\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\partial_{t_{m}}a^{[r]}_{n,k}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{k}+\sum_{k<0}[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},a^{[r]}_{n,k}(s)]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{k}\\
&\ \ +\sum_{k\leq n}a^{[r]}_{n,k}(s)[\partial_{t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{k}]\\
&\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n}\partial_{t_{m}}a^{[r]}_{n,k}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{k}+\sum_{k\leq n}a^{[r]}_{n,k}(s)\,(kg_{m}\Lambda^{k-1-r}+\dotsb)\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{m-1}.\end{split}

Since
\partial_{t_{n}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{m-1}
, we have for each

m=1,2,\dotsc
and
n>m+r
(i.e.,
n-1-r>m-1
),

(2.23)

\partial_{t_{m}}a^{[r]}_{n,k}=\begin{cases}0&(n-r\leqq k\leqq n),\\
-ng_{m}&(k=n-1-r).\end{cases}

The equations (
2.23
) imply that the function

(2.24)

f_{n}(t):=-\frac{1}{n}(a^{[r]}_{n,n-1-r}(t)-a^{[r]}_{n,n-1-r}(t=0))

satisfies

\partial_{t_{m}}f_{n}(t)=g_{m}(t),\qquad f_{n}(t=0)=0,

if
n>m+r
.

As in §
1
, where we take the limit of
f_{m}

(
1.28
), we can take the limit

f:=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
in the topology of
\mathbb{C}[[t]]
defined
by valuation,
\operatorname{val}t_{n}=n
. This limit
f
satisfies

(2.25)

\partial_{t_{m}}f(t)=g_{m}(t),\qquad f(t=0)=0.

The matrix
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}
satisfying (
2.20
) for

r+1
instead of
r
is obtained by

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}:={\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}-f{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}
. In fact,

\begin{split}[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}]&=[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]-(\partial_{t_{n}}f){\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}-f[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]\\
&\equiv f[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]\mod\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r-1},\end{split}

because of (
2.21
) and (
2.25
). Since

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{r}]=\sum_{j=0}^{r}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{j}[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{r-j-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-1},

we have

[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}]=-{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{r}]{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}^{-r}\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-2r-1}\subset\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r-1}
.
Hence,
[\partial_{t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}]\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r-1}
. Thus the
induction has been completed.

Moreover
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}-{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r+1}=f{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}\in\operatorname{Mat}\nolimits^{-r}
as desired.
∎

Taking the limit
{\boldsymbol{L}}:=\lim_{r\to\infty}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{r}
, we obtain the

{\boldsymbol{L}}
-operator of the form (
2.10
) satisfying the Lax
equations (
2.11
). This completes the proof of
Proposition
2.2
.
∎

2.3
Recovery of the mKP hierarchy

Now we can obtain
{\boldsymbol{B}}
-operators of the mKP hierarchy from the given

{\boldsymbol{B}}
-operators by the coordinate change.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\{{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\dotsb}
be a sequence of matrices in
Proposition
2.2
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}
be the matrix obtained
there.

Then there is a coordinate change,

(2.26)

t_{n}\mapsto\tilde{t}_{n}=t_{n}+O(t_{n+1},t_{n+2},\dotsc)

and a function
f_{0}=f_{0}(t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)
such that:

•

The operator
L
satisfies Lax equations,

(2.27)

\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial\tilde{t}_{n}}=[\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}],\qquad\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}:=({\boldsymbol{L}}^{n})_{+},

with respect to the new time variables
(\tilde{t}_{n})_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
.
Therefore
{\boldsymbol{L}}
is a Lax operator of the matrix mKP hierarchy
with respect to the variables
(\tilde{t}_{n})_{n=1,2,\dotsc}
.

•

Each
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}
is expressed in terms of
{\boldsymbol{L}}
as

(2.28)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}+\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{n-1}}{\partial t_{n}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n-1}+\dotsb+\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{1}}{\partial t_{n}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{1}+\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t_{n}}.

Proof.

We can prove the theorem in a similar way as the case of the KP
hierarchy in §
1
. Here we use another
method.

It is easy to show that there exists a lower triangular matrix

\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}
,

(2.29)

\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w_{j}(s)\Lambda^{-j},\qquad w_{j}(s)=w_{j}(s;t),\ w_{0}(s;t)=1,

which satisfies

(2.30)

{\boldsymbol{L}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}\Lambda\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}.

In fact, we have only to compare the coefficients of
\Lambda^{-j}

(
j=1,2,\dotsc
) in the equation

{\boldsymbol{L}}\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}\Lambda,\text{ i.e., }\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j}u_{1-k}(s)\,w_{j-k}(s+1-k)\right)\Lambda^{1-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w_{j}(s)\Lambda^{1-j},

and determine
w_{j}(s)
recursively. (The solution is not unique.)

Taking the adjoint of the Lax equations (
2.11
) by

\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}
, we have

(2.31)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W},\Lambda\right]=0,

where

(2.32)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{(n),W}_{n-j}(s)\Lambda^{n-j}:=\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}\frac{\partial\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}}{\partial t_{n}}.

The equation (
2.31
) means that

(2.33)

[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W},\Lambda]=0,

namely, the coefficients
b^{(n),W}_{n-j}=b^{(n),W}_{n-j}(s)
in the
expansion (
2.32
) do not depend on
s
. The adjoint of the
Zakharov-Shabat equation (
2.9
) by
\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}
is

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{m}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}^{W},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}\right]=0,

which reduces to

(2.34)

\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}^{W}}{\partial t_{n}}=\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}}{\partial t_{m}},

because of
[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{m}^{W},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}]=0
which follows from
(
2.33
) and independence of
b^{(n),W}_{n-j}
from

s
. Since each
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}
is expanded as (
2.32
), the
coefficients of
\Lambda^{k}
(
k\geqq 0
) in the equations
(
2.34
) (
m,n=1,2,\dotsc
) form the
compatibility condition of the system

(2.35)

\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}=b^{(n),W}_{k},\qquad n=1,2,\dotsc,

where
b^{(n),W}_{k}=0
for
k>n
. As
b^{(n),W}_{n}=b^{(n)}_{n}=1
, the
solution of the system (
2.35
) with the initial condition

f_{k}(0)=0
has the form

(2.36)

f_{k}(t)=t_{k}+O(t_{k+1},t_{k+2},\dotsc)

for
k\geqq 1
. Let us use this solution
f_{k}(t)
as the new
independent variable
\tilde{t}_{k}
(
k\geqq 1
). By the definition
(
2.32
) of
{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}^{W}
, we have

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}-\frac{\partial\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}}{\partial t_{n}}\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{(n),W}_{n-j}\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}\Lambda^{n-j}\hat{\boldsymbol{W}}^{-1}.

Taking the upper half part of this equation and using
(
2.30
), we have

(2.37)

{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{t}_{k}}{\partial t_{n}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{k}+\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t_{n}},\qquad\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{k}:=({\boldsymbol{L}}^{k})_{+}

This is the statement (
2.28
) of the theorem.

Note that, as each
\tilde{t}_{k}=f_{k}(t)
has the form
(
2.36
), the Jacobian matrix of the transformation

(t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)\mapsto(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2},\dotsc)
is an
infinite upper half triangular matrix with
1
on the
diagonal. Therefore the inverse transformation
(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2},\dotsc)\mapsto(t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc)
has the same type of the Jacobian
matrix. Multiplying that Jacobian matrix to the vector equation

\left(\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial t_{1}},\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial t_{2}},\dotsc\right)=\left([{\boldsymbol{B}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{L}}],[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{L}}],\dotsc\right),

which is the sequence of Lax equations (
2.11
) obtained
in Proposition
2.2
, we obtain the Lax equation

\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{L}}}{\partial\tilde{t}_{n}}=[\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}],\qquad n=1,2,\dotsc,

as a consequence of (
2.37
). (The term

\partial f_{0}/\partial t_{n}
in (
2.37
) does not
depend on
s
. Hence,
[{\boldsymbol{B}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{L}}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\partial\tilde{t}_{k}/\partial t_{n})[\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{L}}]
.) This is the desired Lax
equation (
2.27
).

∎

3
Concluding remarks

In the present paper we proved that the Zakharov-Shabat equations are
sufficient even without Lax operators to define the integrable
hierarchies. The Lax operators of the KP hierarchy and the mKP hierarchy
are constructed from the information of the Zakharov-Shabat equations.

There are several problems left to be considered:

•

The modified KP hierarchy has another formulation by
(micro)differential operators which is, roughly speaking, an
infinite collection of the KP hierarchies and compatibility
conditions among them. See
[
D
]
and
[
T3
]
for
details. We would be able to rewrite our results in this
formulation.

•

The problem for the Toda lattice hierarchy
[
UT
]
was
considered in
[
T2
]
and it was claimed that the positive
answer corresponding to Theorem
1.9
or
Theorem
2.5
was proved. But several serious
gaps have been found in the proof. Especially non-monic operators
in the Toda lattice hierarchy make the things complicated. We
tried to reduce the problem to our results for the mKP hierarchy
by gauge transformations (cf.
[
T1
]
, Proposition 1.3),
but they cannot simplify the things completely because of the
changes of independent variables.

•

Similar statements are expected for other integrable hierarchies
like the BKP hierarchy, reductions of the KP hierarchy or the
Toda lattice hierarchy and dispersionless type hierarchies.

It is desirable to clarify these problems in further study.

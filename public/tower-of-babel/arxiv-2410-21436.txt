Potentially stably rational conic bundles over nonclosed fields
Kaiqi Yang

Abstract
. We study stable rationality of conic bundles X X over ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} defined over non-closed field k k via the cohomology of the Galois group of finite field extension k ′ / k k^{\prime}/k with action on the geometric Picard lattice of X X .



1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental yet challenging problems in algebraic geometry is determining whether an algebraic variety
X
over a field
k
is
rational
, meaning it is birationally equivalent to projective space. A closely related problem is that of
stable rationality
, which asks whether
X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is rational for some
n
. Despite decades of intense study, these questions still lack fully satisfactory solutions.

One well-known necessary condition for stable rationality in dimensions
\geq 2
is

Condition 1
.

{\bf{(H1)}}

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G_{k^{\prime}},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0,\quad\text{ for all finite extensions}\quad k^{\prime}/k,

where
\bar{X}=X\times_{k}\bar{k}
, and
\bar{k}
is the algebraic closure of base field
k
.

In this paper, we study the (H1) condition for surfaces.
The first step in investigations of surfaces over non-closed fields is the theory of
minimal models
, reducing the problem
to a list of standard presentations. Geometrically rational surfaces can be birationally modified to
two types:
del Pezzo surfaces
and
conic bundles over the projective line
. The modifications proceed via
blow-downs
of Galois-orbits consisting of disjoint exceptional curves, until one arrives at
minimal
surfaces, which fall in one of the two classes mentioned above.

In
[
8
]
, we considered the case of del Pezzo surfaces. We
identified all Galois actions on

\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
satisfying condition (H1).
One of the by-products of this analysis was the following:

Theorem 2
.

A stably rational cubic surface over a non-closed field
k
is rational over
k
.

In this paper, we turn to conic bundles, i.e., fibrations
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where general fiber is a smooth conic, and there are
n
degenerate fibers, each consisting of a union of two intersecting lines. In this case, the Galois action
on
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})

factors through
the Weyl group
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
, and our task reduces to investigations of (H1) conditions for various subgroups
G\subseteq W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
.

The main results of this paper are:

(1)

We analyze this invariant for
cyclic
subgroups of
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
and find that there are three types of actions giving rise to trivial group cohomology.

(2)

In section
6
, we show that the relative minimality and (H1) conditions are preserved under projection with respect to orbit decomposition.

(3)

Based on the result,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\text{ is 2-torsion,}

we initiate the analysis of this invariant for noncyclic subgroups of
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
whose generators are of one of the three identified types, we give 24 classes of groups, up to conjugation, satisfying (H1) and relative minimality conditions. Detailed descriptions of these 24 classes are in section
7
.

Here is a brief overview of what is known:

•

Necessary conditions are
geometric
(stable) rationality, i.e., (stable) rationality of
X
over an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
, and existence of
k
-rational points.

•

These conditions are also sufficient in dimension one, but not sufficient, in general, in dimensions
\geq 2
.

•

Rationality of surfaces is settled
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
, and can be determined by analyzing the action
of the absolute Galois group
G_{k}
on the set of exceptional curves (e.g., lines on a cubic surface)
and the induced action on the geometric Picard group

\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
, but stable rationality remains elusive.

2.
Geometry and symmetries of conic bundles

From now on,
X
is a geometrically rational surface over
k
admitting a standard conic bundle over
k
, i.e., we have
a morphism

\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

with, geometrically,
n
degenerate fibers, each a union of two intersecting lines, called
exceptional curves
.
The integer
d=(K_{X})^{2}=8-n
is called the
degree
of the conic bundle
X
.
The two transversal components of the
j
-th degenerate fiber are denoted by
q_{j}^{+}
and
q_{j}^{-}
.
The surface is called
split
over
k
if all exceptional curves are defined over
k
.

The free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})=\bigoplus_{i=-1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}l_{i}
has rank
(10-d)
where
d
is the degree of the conic bundle. The free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
has a basis
l_{-1},\cdots,l_{n}
where
l_{0}
is the class of a fiber of
\pi
,
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
are the classes of the components of degenerate fibers, one from each, and the class of canonical divisor
K_{X}=-2(l_{-1}+l_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}
.

The scalar product on
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
is given by

\displaystyle(l_{-1}^{2})=(l_{-1},l_{i})=(l_{0}^{2})=(l_{0},l_{i})=0,\quad\text{for }i\geq 1,

\displaystyle(l_{-1},l_{0})=1,\text{ and }(l_{i},l_{j})=-\delta_{i,j},\text{ for }i,j\geq 1.

The module
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
has a natural action of the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
and defines a representation

\rho:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

where
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}
is the group of automorphisms preserving the scalar product and
K_{X}
. The splitting group of
X
is
\text{Im}(\rho)
.

The surface is called
k-minimal
if no blow-downs are possible over
k
and
relatively k-minimal
if

\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})^{\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)}=\mathbb{Z}l_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}K_{X}.

From
[
6
]
, a relatively k-minimal but non-k-minimal conic bundle can appear only if its degree is equal to 3,5,6,7 or 8.
When the degree of a conic bundle is less than 3, relative k-minimality is the same as k-minimality.

The automorphism group of
n
pairs of symbols
j^{+},j^{-},j=1,\dots,n
is the Weyl group

W(\mathsf{B}_{n})\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n},

the signed permutation group,
where the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
acts on
j^{\pm}
by permuting the index
j
and
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n}
is
generated by
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
, where
c_{j}
exchanges
j^{+}
and
j^{-}
and leaves the remaining symbols invariant.

Lemma 3
.

We have the following equation

\displaystyle\tau c_{j}=c_{\tau(j)}\tau,\quad\tau\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\tau c_{\tau^{-1}(j)}=c_{j}\tau.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\tau c_{j}(j^{\pm})=\tau(j^{\mp})=(\tau(j))^{\mp}=c_{\tau(j)}(\tau(j)^{\pm})=c_{\tau(j)}\tau(j^{\pm}).

For other cases, the equation holds trivially.
∎

We denote the projection from
W(\mathsf{B}_{n})
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as

\displaystyle pr:W(\mathsf{B}_{n})\to\mathfrak{S}_{n},\quad pr(c_{j_{1}}\cdots c_{j_{t}}\tau)=\tau,\quad\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

There is a character

\sigma:W(\mathsf{B}_{n})\to\{\pm 1\},\quad\quad\sigma(c_{j_{1}}c_{j_{2}}\cdots c_{j_{t}}\tau)=(-1)^{t},\quad\tau\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n},

with kernel

W(\mathsf{D}_{n})\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n-1}.

The group
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
is the automorphism group acting on
n
pairs of degenerate fibers of a standard conic bundle.

There is a natural integral representation of
W(\mathsf{B}_{n})
,

\Psi:W(\mathsf{B}_{n})\to\mathsf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}),

given by

(\Psi(c_{j_{1}}\cdots c_{j_{t}}\tau))_{uv}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s(i)\delta_{u,i}\delta_{v,\tau(i)},

where the sign function
s(i)
is given by

s(i)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ $i\in\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{t}\}$},\\
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The action of the automorphism group
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
on
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
with
n\geq 4
, induces an integral representation

\Phi:W(\mathsf{D}_{n})\to\mathsf{GL}_{n+2}(\mathbb{Z}),\quad n\geq 4.

This representation
\Phi
could be derived from the representation
\Psi
of
W(\mathsf{B}_{n})
restricted to its subgroup
W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
.

Assume a group element
g\in W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
has the form
g=c_{j_{1}}\cdots c_{j_{t}}\tau
, where
t
is an even integer, the image
\Phi(g)\in\mathsf{GL}_{n+2}({\mathbb{Z}})
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi(g)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{g}&B_{g}\\
C_{g}&\Psi(g)\end{pmatrix},

where
where
A_{g}
is a
2\times 2
,
B_{g}
is a
2\times n
, and
C_{g}
is an
n\times 2
matrix.

In detail,

\displaystyle B_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0\\
b^{\prime}_{g}(1)&b^{\prime}_{g}(2)&\cdots&b^{\prime}_{g}(n)\end{pmatrix},

where

b^{\prime}_{g}(i)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if the nonzero element in the $i$-th column of $\Psi(g)$ is $-1$ },\\
0\quad&\text{if the nonzero element in the $i$-th column of $\Psi(g)$ is $1$ },\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{\prime}_{g}(1)&0\\
c^{\prime}_{g}(2)&0\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
c^{\prime}_{g}(n)&0\end{pmatrix},

where

c^{\prime}_{g}(i)=\begin{cases}-1\quad&\text{if the nonzero element in the $i$-th row of $\Psi(g)$ is $-1$ },\\
0\quad&\text{if the nonzero element in the $i$-th row of $\Psi(g)$ is $1$ },\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle A_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b^{\prime}_{g}(i)))&1\end{pmatrix}.

Group element has the form
g=c_{j_{1}}\cdots c_{j_{t}}\tau
with
t
is even, thus
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b^{\prime}_{g}(i))=t
is divisible by
2
.

In the rest of the paper, if there is no confusion, we write
g
for
\Phi(g)
for simplicity.

We adopt notation from
[
6
]
:
a group element
g\in W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
can be expressed as

g=\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{R},

where each
\beta
is a signed permutation cycle, defined as a product of a single permutation cycle
\tau
and
c_{i}
’s with indices
i
lie in cycle
\tau
.

3.
Group cohomology

A finite group
G
has a presentation with
m
generators subject to relations, written as
G=\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\rangle
. The group cohomology discussed in (H1) condition is the quotient of 1-cocycles by 1-coboundaries,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})).

where
Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of 1-cocycles which are maps
f:G\to\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
such that

\displaystyle f(gh)=f(g)+g\cdot f(h)\quad\forall g,h\in G,

and
B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of 1-coboundaries generated by

\displaystyle f(g)=g\cdot a-a\quad\forall g\in G,a\in\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}).

Given a defining relation of
G
,

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{L}g_{i_{k}}=1

where
i_{k}\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, it induces a linear relation on 1-cocycle
f
,

\displaystyle f(g_{i_{1}})+\sum_{t=1}^{L-1}(\prod_{k=1}^{t}g_{i_{k}})f(g_{i_{t+1}})=0,

and we regard
f
as a length
m(n+2)
column that records
f(g_{i})
for
i=1,\cdots,m
. All defining relations of
G
produce a matrix
M
and the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
is the kernel of
M
.

We record two theorems:

•

From Theorem (10.3) in
[
3
]
, we have

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=\bigoplus_{p}{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G_{p},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))^{G},

where
G_{p}
is the Sylow
p
-subgroup of group
G
.

•

From
[
2
,
4
]

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\text{ is 2-torsion.}

From the above two theorems, we conclude

Lemma 4
.

Finite group
G
satisfies
\mathrm{(H1)}
condition if and only if its Sylow 2-subgroup
G_{2}
satisfies
\mathrm{(H1)}
condition.

Proof.

If finite group
G
satisfies (H1) condition,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(H,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0\quad\forall\ H\subseteq G,

then the Sylow 2-subgroup
G_{2}
, as a subgroup of
G
, satisfies (H1) condition.

Conversely, if Sylow 2-subgroup
G_{2}
satisfies (H1) condition, then for any subgroup
H\subseteq G
, we have
H_{2}\subseteq G_{2}
and

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(H,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(H_{2},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))^{H}=0,

∎

To match the index set of the basis of Picard lattice, the index set of length
(n+2)
column is from
-1
to
n
.

The coboundaries module
B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
are spanned by elements
f_{i}
of length
m(n+2)
, with
i\in\{-1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))={\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{-1},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle.

Each
f_{i}
has the following form:

\displaystyle f_{i}=[((g_{1}-\mathbb{I})e_{i})^{T},\dots,((g_{m}-\mathbb{I})e_{i})^{T}]^{T},

where
i=-1,\cdots,n
and
e_{i}
is a length
(n+2)

{\mathbb{Z}}
-module element with entries

(e_{i})_{l}=\delta_{i,l},\ l=-1,\cdots,n.

We use notation

\displaystyle f_{i,g}=(g-\mathbb{I})e_{i},\ i\in\{-1,\dots,n\}.

The special element
f_{-1}
satisfies the relation,

\displaystyle f_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i},

and we focus on the submodule
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle\subseteq B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
.

We have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))

\displaystyle=Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))

\displaystyle=(Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle)/({\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{-1},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle).

If
f_{-1}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle
, then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{-1},f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2,

otherwise,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\cong Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle.

If
{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\neq 0
, then there exists an element
\xi\in Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
such that

\xi\notin B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\text{ and }2\xi\in B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

that implies we can find integers
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=-1}^{n}
such that

\sum_{i=-1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f_{i}=2\xi,\quad\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since

[\xi]=[\xi\pm f_{i}]\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),\ \forall i\in\{-1,\dots,n\},

and

\sum_{i=-1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f_{i}\pm 2f_{j}=2(\xi\pm f_{j}),

it follows that for a nonzero element
[\xi]\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
, we can find an index set
I
such that

\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=2\xi^{\prime},

for some
\xi^{\prime}
and
[\xi]=[\xi^{\prime}]\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
.

Conversely, if there is an index set
I
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=\xi\notin B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

where
\xi
is integral, then

\displaystyle M(\sum_{i\in I}f_{i})=M(2\xi)=2M\xi=0,

which implies
M\xi=0
,
\xi\in Z^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
and
0\neq[\xi]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
.

Lemma 5
.

If
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=\xi
for some index set
I
and
0\neq[\xi]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
, then
-1\notin I
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction.

If
-1\in I
, let
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{-1\}
, then we have

f_{-1}+\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}=2\xi,

which implies

f_{-1}+\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}=0\mod 2,

or equivalently,

f_{-1}-\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}=0\mod 2.

Element
f_{-1}
satisfies the following relation,

f_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}.

We substitute the expression of
f_{-1}
into the previous equation and we have

\sum_{i\notin I^{\prime}}f_{i}-\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}=0\mod 4.

When restricted to a generator
g_{l}
, we have

\sum_{i\geq 1,i\notin I^{\prime}}f_{i,g_{l}}-\sum_{i\geq 1,i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i,g_{l}}=0\mod 4,\quad\forall l=1,\dots,m.

The entries of this element of index
1
to
n
have 3 possibilities,
\pm 2
and 0.
Thus all entries of index
1
to
n
are 0 restricted to every generator
g_{l}
.

The entry of index 0, which is
\frac{1}{2}
times the total number of minus signs in the index set
I
and
I^{\prime}
, are the same.

We conclude that

\sum_{i\notin I^{\prime}}f_{i}=\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}

and substitute it to the expression of
f_{-1}
, we have

f_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i\notin I^{\prime}}f_{i}+\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i})=\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}

and

2f_{-1}=2\xi,

which implies

f_{-1}=\xi=\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}f_{i}\in B^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

and

[\xi]=[f_{-1}]=0\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

which is a contradiction to our assumption.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Assume
\xi=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}
for some index set
I
,
0\neq[\xi]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
and up to conjugation, assume a group element
g=\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\dots\beta_{R}
with
\beta_{1}=c_{j_{1}}\dots c_{j_{t}}(1\ \dots\ w)
. If
1\in I
, then
\{1,\dots,w\}\subset I
.

Proof.

Let
s(i)
be the sign function of the signed permutation cycle
\beta_{1}
. Under the assumption, we have

\displaystyle g-\mathbb{I}=\left(\small\begin{array}[]{cc|ccccc}0&0&0&0&\dots&0&*\\
&0&\frac{1-s(w)}{2}&\frac{1-s(1)}{2}&\dots&\frac{1-s(w-1)}{2}&*\\
\hline\cr\frac{s(1)-1}{2}&0&-1&s(1)&\dots&0&*\\
\frac{s(2)-1}{2}&0&0&-1&\dots&0&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{s(w)-1}{2}&0&s(w)&0&\dots&-1&*\\
&*&*&*&*&*&*\end{array}\right),

index
1\in I
and

\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=0\mod 2.

From Lemma
5
, since
-1\notin I
, the only column with a nonzero entry at index 1, other than
f_{1}
, is
f_{2}
, therefore
2\in I
.
Use similar arguments, we conclude
\{1,\dots,w\}\subseteq I
.
∎

Remark 7
.

In summary, if an index
i\in I
, then all indices in the same signed permutation cycle as
i
are also in the index set
I
. By applying this lemma to each signed permutation cycle in every generator of the group
G
, the index set
I
is a union of orbits of
pr(G)
acting on
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

From the discussion above, to identify an obstruction to the triviality of group cohomology of
G
, we need to find index sets
I
such that

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\text{ and }0\neq[\xi]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=\xi\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle.

Equivalently, it means there is no integer solution
\{\alpha_{j,I}\}
to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,I}f_{j}.

When restricted to a generator
g
, we study the following linear equation and find integer solutions of

\displaystyle\xi_{g}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i,g}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,I,g}f_{j,g}.

Denote the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of all solutions
\{\alpha_{j,I,g}\}
to this linear equation as
V_{I,g}
, we have the following statement:

\displaystyle\cap_{i=1}^{m}V_{I,g_{i}}\neq\emptyset,\text{ with }G=\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\rangle\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle.

Now we reduce the study of group cohomology of
G
to the study of
V_{I,g}
, where
g
is a generator of
G
and
I
is an index set which is a union of orbits of
pr(G)
acting on
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

4.
Computing group cohomology of cyclic groups

Assume
g\in W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
has the form

g=\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{R}.

From Lemma
6
, orbit decomposition of the cyclic group
pr(\langle g\rangle)
is determined by signed permutation cycles
\beta
’s. We focus on one signed permutation cycle
\beta_{1}
.

Up to conjugation, assume

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=c_{j_{1}}\dots c_{j_{t}}(1\ \dots\ w),

and there are several cases:

•

If
w\geq 2
and
t
is even, equal to
2h
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{w}f_{i}=\sum_{u=1}^{h}\sum_{r=(j_{2u-1})+1}^{j_{2u}}f_{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle

•

If
t
is odd, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{w}f_{i}\neq 0\mod 2,

since the entry of index
0
is
\sum_{i=1}^{w}\frac{1-s(i)}{2}=t
, an odd integer.

From the discussion above, we focus on signed permutation cycles with an odd number of minus signs. Without loss of generality, we denote them as
\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{\Lambda}\}
and
I_{i}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,w\}
is the set of indices appearing in
pr(\beta_{i})
. The number of such signed permutation cycles,
\Lambda
, is an even integer since
g\in W(\mathsf{D}_{n})
.

•

If
\Lambda=0
, meaning there are no signed permutation cycles with an odd number of minus signs, then we have

\displaystyle Z^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle=0

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0

•

If
\Lambda\geq 2
, then for any set
\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{2h}\}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,\Lambda\}
, write
I=I_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup I_{i_{2h}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
i_{1}=1
and

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=c_{j_{1}}\dots c_{j_{t}}(1\ \dots\ w).

We have

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\in Z^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),

and

\displaystyle(\xi)_{u}=\frac{-1+s(u)}{2}=\begin{cases}-1,&u\in\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{t}\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
1\leq u\leq w
.

The linear system,

\displaystyle\xi=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i,I}f_{i}

is equivalent to the following,

\displaystyle(\xi)_{u}=-\alpha_{u,I}+\alpha_{u+1,I}s(u),\ u=1,\cdots,w-1

\displaystyle(\xi)_{w}=-\alpha_{w,I}+\alpha_{1,I}s(w).

If
s(u)=1
, then

\displaystyle 0=-\alpha_{u,I}+\alpha_{u+1,I}\Rightarrow\alpha_{u,I}=\alpha_{u+1,I},

otherwise,

\displaystyle 1=\alpha_{u,I}+\alpha_{u+1,I}.

From the above discussion, assuming
s(w)=1
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,I}=\cdots=\alpha_{j_{1},I}=\alpha,

\displaystyle\alpha_{j_{1}+1,I}=\cdots=\alpha_{j_{2},I}=1-\alpha,

\displaystyle\alpha_{j_{2}+1,I}=\cdots=\alpha_{j_{3},I}=\alpha,

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\alpha_{j_{t},I}=\cdots=\alpha_{w,I}=1-\alpha.

Since
s(w)=1
, we have
1-\alpha=\alpha_{w,I}=\alpha_{1,I}=\alpha
, which implies
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, not an integer.

We conclude that
\xi\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle
, and index set
I
induces a nonzero element in

Z^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle.

For the case where
s(w)=-1
, the argument is similar, leading to the same conclusion. Furthermore

\displaystyle f_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{1}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle.

We have
\Lambda-1
index sets,
I_{1}\cup I_{j}
,
j=2,\cdots,\Lambda
, which correspond to nonzero elements and form a generating set of

\displaystyle Z^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle=({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{\Lambda-1}.

Since
f_{-1}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{-1},f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle/{\mathbb{Z}}\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}/2,

and thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{\Lambda-2}.

Corollary 9
.

If a cyclic group

satisfies the
\mathrm{(H1)}
condition, i.e.,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle g^{i}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0\quad\forall\ i,

\beta

g
satisfying the assumption:

(1)

,

(2)

g=c_{1}c_{2}(2\ 3)\beta_{3}\cdots\beta_{R}

(3)

\sigma(\beta_{i})=1

Without loss of generality, assume there is a signed permutation cycle
\beta

and
\sigma(\beta)=-1
, meaning

Taking its

w

for

For a length two signed permutation cycle with one minus sign, up to conjugation, the only possibility is

5.
Computing
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))
for general group
G

Assume group
G

m
generators,

Proposition 11
.

If
G
is a finite abelian group with actions on
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
for some conic bundle
X
and for any
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle g\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0,

then
G
satisfies (H1) condition.

Proof.

For any finite abelian group
G
, denote
d(G)
the minimum of cardinalities of all generating sets of group
G
.

We prove this lemma by induction on
d(G)
.

Since for any cyclic subgroup
H=\langle h\rangle\subseteq G
, i.e.,
d(H)=1
, by assumption, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle h\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0,

and it is easy to check by proposition
8
.

Assume for any subgroup
H
of
G
with
d(H)\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0.

For any subgroup
H\subseteq G
with
d(H)=n+1
, i.e., there are elements
h_{1},\dots,h_{n+1}
such that

\displaystyle H=\langle h_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle h_{n+1}\rangle,

we denote
H^{\prime}=\langle h_{2}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle h_{n+1}\rangle
, then
d(H^{\prime})\leq n
.

There is a short exact sequence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{1}(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1-i}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}\mathrm{Tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),\mathrm{H}^{2-i}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})))\to 0.

By assumption,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0,\quad\mathrm{H}^{1}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=0,

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{0}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})^{\langle h_{1}\rangle},\quad\mathrm{H}^{0}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))=\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})^{H^{\prime}},

are submodules of the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})
, are free, then

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}\mathrm{Tor}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})),\mathrm{H}^{2-i}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})))=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{1}(\mathrm{H}^{i}(\langle h_{1}\rangle,\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X}))\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1-i}(H^{\prime},\mathrm{Pic}(\bar{X})))=0.

The Sylow 2-subgroup of
G\times G^{\prime}
is contained in bicyclic group
\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\langle g_{1}^{\prime}\rangle
and for any group element in
\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\langle g_{1}^{\prime}\rangle
, it has trivial group cohomology. By Proposition
11
, the Sylow 2-subgroup of
G\times G^{\prime}
satisfies (H1) condition.

The relative minimality condition is easy to check.
∎

Operational performance research of layered rotary tillage and ridging machine on for the sticky soil using DEM
Authors: Tao Zhang, Xinglong Tang, Cong Dai, Guiying Ren, Youlun Pang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the challenges of low soil fragmentation rates and high tillage resistance during land preparation for the sticky soil in southwest China, a double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine was designed to enhance the soil layer structure. It mainly composes deep rotation device, soil crushing device, ridging component, etc. This study employed the Discrete Element Method (DEM) to optimize the operational parameters of the double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine, compare the tillage performance between single-shaft and double-shaft tillage equipment, and simulate the effects of tillage depth on tillage performance. Following the Box-Behnken testing design principle, a regression model was established to relate all factors to the performance index. The independent variables included rotation speed of deep rotation device, horizontal distance, and rotation speed of soil crushing device, while the average energy consumption and number of topsoil broken bonds as the response variable. Results showed that the factors which influence soil crushing performance from big to small were rotation speed of soil crushing device, horizontal distance, and rotation speed of deep rotation device. The optimal parameters were that the rotation speed of deep rotation device was 260 r/min, the horizontal distance was 340 mm, and the rotation speed of soil crushing device was 430 r/min. A comparison of the working performance between single-shaft and double-shaft revealed that the double-shaft exerts a greater disturbance intensity on the topsoil. Specifically, the total number of topsoil broken bonds was 12.17% higher than that for the single-shaft, and the total number of topsoil broken bonds was approximately 1.5 times that of the subsoil broken bonds. As the tillage depth increases, the number of topsoil broken bonds tends to decrease, while the number of subsoil broken bonds, energy consumption, and traction resistance of rotary knife roll correspondingly increase. Field test results indicate that the surface soil fragmentation rates of the double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine and the traditional single-shaft rotary tillage ridging equipment were 93.74% and 69.46%, respectively. The double-shaft demonstrates an improvement in soil fragmentation rates by 24.28% compared to the single-shaft. These findings provide theoretical references for the popularization of double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine in the sticky soil area.Keywords: Sticky soil, Rotary tillage and ridging, Discrete element method, Double-shaft, Parameters optimizationSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Software

Introduction

In recent years, the achievements of the paddy ridge cultivation technology implemented by the Chongqing Academy of Agricultural Sciences in China have been remarkable1,2. This technology creates a ridge and furrow seedbed through processes such as tilling, ridging, and furrowing. It enhances the utilization rate of water and fertilizers, improves crop ventilation and light transmission, and ultimately increases yields3,4. However, long-term research by the research group has revealed that the traditional single-shaft rotary tillage ridge machine tends to operate at a uniform soil depth. Additional issues include excessively large particle sizes of the topsoil, uneven ridge surfaces, poor compaction, and susceptible to collapse5,6. Therefore, to improve soil structure in the hilly and mountainous regions of Southwest China, and considering the local soil viscosity, heavy characteristics, and agronomic requirements for crop cultivation, research into rotary tillage ridge machines with high soil breaking rates and low power consumption is a critical challenge in local agricultural production.

Since 2010, research on the double-shaft rotary tillage ridge machine has been conducted in foreign countries. This device operates on the principle of “first spinning, then breaking, layering and segmentation”. Domestic research on this device began in 2014. Numerous tests have shown that this device has relatively high requirements for soil conditions. It performs better in the sandy soils of northern China but is less suited to viscosity, heavy, and less mobile soils7,8. To enhance its performance, Qin et al.9. conducted stability analyses and tests on tillage depth and width concerning stability issues in rotary tillage machines, aiming to identify the key factors influencing tillage stability. Zhang et al.10. addressing the challenges of high soil viscosity, heavy, and significant tillage and ridge resistance in the hilly and mountainous regions of Southwest China, designed a fine-rotary tillage ridge machine. In recent years, with the continuous advancement of computer technology, applying this technology to establish device models and simulate actual operating conditions has reduced testing costs and provided researchers with an effective method to study device operating mechanisms11,12. The discrete element method (DEM), initially used to analyze geotechnical motion in mining, has since become widely adopted across various fields13–15. In agricultural engineering, DEM is typically employed to simulate device operating process, facilitating the study of interactions between tool and soil16. Furthermore, by establishing and analyzing the deep-pine shovel-soil interaction model, DEM has demonstrated its reliability in predicting soil resistance17.

Song et al.18. focused on the rotary tilling and ridging process. They employed DEM to establish a viscoelastic soil model and conducted a detailed analysis of soil disturbance during operating process. Jafar et al.19. developed a mathematical model for the power demand of the rotary tillage blade, along with equations for the surface area of unit volume tilled soil and the cutting angle and created a computer program to solve these equations. Guan et al.5. conducted in-depth research on the operational mechanism of the single-axis rotary tillage ridge machine, providing a theoretical foundation and new methods for the development of the dual-axis device. Patidar et al.20. established a virtual model to simulate interactions between soil and rotary blades using Altair EDEM software, combined with the Hysteric Spring Contact Model and the linear cohesion model V2, to investigate interaction mechanisms between the soil and the blade. Zhao et al.21. constructed a discrete element model of the soil-straw-tillage-component agglomerate for coastal saline-alkaline land using DEM. They comprehensively analyzed the effects of L-type rotary blades and traditional rotary blades on soil tillage layer displacement and straw burial performance in saline-alkaline land from a microscopic perspective. Zhang et al.22. utilized DEM to model the interaction between rotary blades and corn stubble with three different edge curves. The discrete element method is a numerical method suitable for analyzing and solving the dynamics of complex discrete systems, which can effectively obtain the information of the data that cannot be measured by field experiments from a fine viewpoint, so as to realize the prediction of the performance of the tillage system and the optimization of the parameters23,24. Torotwa et al.25. fabricated a biomimetic disc mimicking the molerat’s claw and evaluated in comparison with traditional discs, combining theoretical analysis and soil bin tests, the optimum working and structural parameters were determined. Barbosa26 designed a plowing tool for sugarcane planting, analyzed the fragmentation characteristics and energy requirements of soil tillage tools in the formation of seedbeds, The new tool concept showed potential to decrease the power demand by 28.5% at the same time it increases the fractal fragmentation by 10% in comparison to the conventional tool. Saunders et al.27. systematically analyzed the influence of parameters such as plough forward speed, operating depth and entry angle on soil movement, and combined with field experiments, showed that EDEM can effectively evaluate soil movement mechanisms. Shaikh et al.28. analyzed the soil movement laws at different soil moisture content using EDEM system and combined with soil trench test, the minimum error between the simulation test results and the soil trench test results was 10.09%, which shows the accuracy of discrete element method to analyze the soil movement laws. In summary, both domestic and international research on rotary tillage ridge machines has primarily focused on the operational mechanisms of single-axis devices and the optimization of rotary blades, with relatively fewer studies addressing the mechanisms of dual-axis devices.

Considering the above issues, this study primarily focuses on the development of a double-shaft rotary tillage ridge machine for the hilly and mountainous regions of Southwest China. The device takes into account the characteristics of soil viscosity in the area. Key components and operating principles of the device were detailed. Discrete element simulation is used to determine optimal parameters. Additionally, the study aims to investigate the machine’s performance under different tillage depth conditions, conduct a comparative analysis of power consumption and soil disturbance between single-shaft and double-shaft device, and validate the device’s performance through field experiments. The goal is to provide references for the development of double-shaft rotary tillage ridge machines.

The structure of this article follows the logical chain of “principle modeling - simulation optimization - experimental verification”. Firstly, structural design was carried out based on the working principle to construct the initial prototype of the physical model. Secondly, the tillage scenario was simulated with the help of a virtual simulation environment to optimize the key working parameters of the physical model. Finally, the physical prototype was manufactured according to the optimal parameters obtained from the optimization, and the reliability and accuracy of the simulation results were verified through field experiments.

Materials

Agronomic requirements

In recent years, the paddy ridge cultivation technology developed by the Chongqing Academy of Agricultural Sciences has proven to be significant in reducing pests and diseases, improving the soil ecological environment, and increasing yields. To facilitate its promotion and application efficiently, the research team integrated agricultural machinery with agronomy, combining multiple technologies such as layered tillage, flooded direct seeding, ridge farming and ridge harvesting to create a comprehensive paddy ridge farming production model. The production concept encompassing various mechanized production stages for rice was illustrated in Fig. 1a. Initially, rotary tillage and ridging serve as the fundamental and critical processes. Considering factors such as the track gauge of commonly used harvesters and planting density, the longitudinal section parameters of the ridge following rotary tillage and ridging were depicted in Fig. 1b. The ridge center distance 2 to 2.3 m, while the ridge width ranges from 1.5 to 1.8 m, the ditch width was 150 mm, and the ridge height varies between 250 and 300 mm. This approach also establishes a tillage layer structure characterized by a “fine on the surface and coarse on the bottom” configuration; specifically, the top 0 to 5 cm of topsoil was finely crushed to facilitate seed germination, whereas the subsoil remains relatively rough and loose, enhancing rooting, drainage, and nutrient absorption. The soil quality after tilling must meet the following essential requirements1: (1) The broken soil rate of the surface soil should be ≥ 85%; (2) The ridge surface flatness ≤ 3 cm.

Overall structure of double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine

The double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine primarily consists of a frame, a central gearbox, a side gearbox, a deep rotation device, a soil crushing device, a ridging component, a three-point suspension device, and a machine cover, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The frame serves as the installation carrier for all components, with the central gearbox and three-point suspension device mounted on the top. Both the deep rotary device and the soil crushing device were affixed to the sides through positioning holes, with the soil crushing device located behind and above the deep rotation device. The rear ridge forming device comprises a ridge forming plow and a rear ridge forming box. The assembly relationship of each component within the machine was depicted in Fig. 2b. The soil crushing device was positioned above and behind the deep rotation device, with horizontal and vertical distances between the axis centers measuring 340 mm and 120 mm, respectively. The gyration radii of the deep rotation device and soil crushing devices were 280 mm and 140 mm, respectively. While the length of the ridge chamber was 300 mm. Notably, there was no interference among the components, resulting in a compact and rational structure.

Working principle

The design of the machine’s biaxial layered rotary tillage and soil crushing system was illustrated in Fig. 3. The rotary tillage knife roller was positioned at the front of the frame, while the soil crushing knife roller was located at the rear. These two components were staggered vertically. In the horizontal orientation, the rotary tiller shaft was situated in front of the soil-crushing roller shaft, and in the vertical orientation, the rear soil-crushing roller shaft was elevated above the front rotary tiller shaft. The rotation directions of the deep rotary tiller blade shaft and the soil crushing blade shaft were both counterclockwise. During operation, the deep rotary tiller roller initially performs deep rotation of the soil layer, reaching depths of 10 to 23 cm below the surface, while simultaneously propelling the cut soil towards the rear. At this moment, the rear-mounted soil-crushing knife roller engages with the displaced soil chunks, executing secondary operations on them. This sub-fine crushing process results in the formation of fine soil particles with an average size of less than 3 cm within the top 5 cm of the soil. The integration of these two operations achieves the objective of creating a composite tillage layer structure characterized by ‘fine soil on the surface and coarse soil below.

Design plan for biaxial layered rotary tillage to break soil.

When the whole machine was working, as the tractor moves forward, the deep rotation device cuts, squeezes, and throws the soil. The ridging plows on both sides of the ridging device turn the soil toward the middle to form furrows and ridge surfaces. Concurrently, the soil crushing device breaks the surface soil second to prevent the compaction of sticky paddy soil, while the ridge-forming components establish a flat ridge surface that facilitates sowing and promotes crop growth. The effective working width of the machine was 1600 mm.

Discrete element simulation model

Establishment of soil particle and soil tank model

EDEM 2022 Version 8.3.0 was employed to create discrete element method (DEM) simulation models. The morphological characteristic of soil was that soil particles aggregate into aggregates. In discrete element simulations, particle shape and particle size directly determine the complexity of contact algorithms. To address the issues of insufficient computing resources and result convergence caused by the explosion in particle quantity, the spherical assumption and enlarged particle size were necessary simplifications to balance “simulation feasibility” and “research objectives”. Existing studies have shown that when using spherical particles with an enlarged particle size (soil particles around 10 mm) in tillage simulations, the error between the simulated values and experimental values was controlled within 8%, indicating that this assumption can reflect the macroscopic behavior of soil during the tillage process29–31. To accurately represent the structure of the tillage layer under sticky soil conditions, the soil was categorized into surface and bottom layers. Due to the abundance of clay particles in the surface soil of the study area and the strong intergranular attraction, under the leaching effect of rainwater, the soil porosity has decreased, and compaction has formed, resulting in the density of the topsoil being higher than that of the subsoil. This study determined that the soil particle size ranges from 0.25 to 15 mm. Given that smaller particles require longer simulation times, this article sets the topsoil particle radius at 7 mm and the subsoil particle radius at 14 mm, with the spherical diameter magnification of the soil matrix ranging from 0.95 to 1.05.

The soil tank was utilized to investigate the interaction mechanisms between soil and agricultural components. Factors such as operating width, plowing depth, and lateral disturbance were thoroughly considered in the establishment of a flat soil discrete element model. The dimensions of the soil tank were 2000 mm in length, 2400 mm in width, and 320 mm in height, with a surface layer thickness of 50 mm and a bottom layer thickness of 270 mm.

Establishment of discrete element simulation model

To investigate the tillage performance of double-shaft layered rotary tillage soil-breaking equipment and enhance computational efficiency, the whole machine was simplified and the impact of components such as guard plates and ridge plowshares on soil tillage performance was ignored. The simulation model primarily consists of a deep rotation device and a soil crushing device. The deep rotation device blades were installed on the deep rotary tillage cutter roller in an alternating double spiral arrangement. The soil crushing device features seven-shaped blades, with a gyration radius of 140 mm. Earth-crushing knives were symmetrically positioned on both sides of the cutter head. The cutter heads were evenly distributed in four rows along the shaft and a gap of 30 mm between adjacent knife tips. SolidWorks software was utilized to create a three-dimensional solid model of the rotary tillage and soil-crushing equipment, which were subsequently imported into EDEM software.

The interaction simulation model of the rotary tillage soil crushing equipment and the soil tank in the EDEM software was illustrated in Fig. 4. The weights of the subsoil and topsoil were 355 kg and 2274 kg, respectively. In the simulation parameters, the Rayleigh time step, Target Save Interval, Number of Cores, and Cell Size were set to 20%, 0.02 s, 4, and 3, respectively. Upon completion of the simulation, the data was exported for analysis via the data processing module. During the simulation process, the forward speed and operation depth were set to 0.6s and 160 mm respectively.

Simulation test scheme

Operation parameter optimization test scheme

The Box-Behnken response surface design model was employed to optimize the key parameters of double-shaft tillage equipment. It not only enables a significant reduction in the number of tests but also yields a highly reliable constructed model, making it an efficient and practical experimental scheme in the field of process parameter optimization. Allowing for a comprehensive analysis of the factors with a reduced number of tests32–34. The rotation speed of deep rotation device (A), the rotation speed of soil crushing device (B), and the horizontal distance between the deep rotation devices and soil crushing device (C) were considered as the simulation test factors. The simulated soil crushing performance and tillage energy consumption were designated as the response values. A three-factor, three-level test was selected, with each test factor coded horizontally, as presented in Table 2. In total, 17 sets of simulation tests were conducted in accordance with the response surface test arrangement.

Comparison test scheme of tillage performance of different machine type

The operation processes of single-shaft and double-shaft tillage equipment were simulated. The rotation speed of the deep rotary device in the single-shaft equipment was 260 r/min. The horizontal and vertical distances between the deep rotary device and the soil crushing device in the double-shaft equipment were set to 340 mm and 120 mm respectively, the rotation speed of deep rotary device and soil crushing device were 260 r/min and 430 r/min respectively. During the simulation, the soil entry depth was consistent. By analyzing the soil macroscopic characteristic, and using soil crushing performance, cutter roller resistance, and tillage energy consumption as evaluation indicators, the differences in tillage performance of the two types of equipment in areas with sticky soils were comprehensively compared.

Tillage performance test scheme under different tillage depth conditions

Plowing depth was a critical factor influencing tillage resistance, energy consumption, soil disturbance range, and soil crushing performance35. A shallow plowing depth may lead to insufficient soil disturbance and reduced soil breaking effectiveness. Conversely, an excessively deep plowing depth can increase the loss of supporting power and key components. Therefore, analyzing the interaction between tillage depth and soil-soil contact components can enhance tillage performance and decrease machine energy consumption. In this study, the initial tillage depth was set at 130 mm, with a total of five levels, gradually increasing in increments of 30 mm. A single-factor simulation test was conducted, maintaining a forward speed of the entire machine at 0.6 m/s. The rotational speeds of the deep rotary cutter roller and the soil-crushing cutter rollers were set at 260 r/min and 430 r/min, respectively, while other simulation parameters remained consistent. The analysis focused on soil disturbance range, crushing performance, and tillage energy consumption to determine the optimal tillage depth parameters for minimizing resistance.

Methods

Determination of physical properties and contact parameters of the simulation model

During discrete element simulations, it was essential to assign values to the intrinsic parameters of the materials and the contact parameters between them. Both the deep rotation device and the soil crushing device were constructed from 65Mn steel, while the densities of the topsoil and subsoil were measured using the ring knife method. By taking into account actual measurements with reference literature6,8,36–39, the parameters involved in the simulation tests were determined as shown in Tables 3, 4 and 5. The Hertz-Mindlin frictional model and parallel bond model was applied to the interactions between topsoil and subsoil particles, topsoil and topsoil particles, subsoil and subsoil particles, as well as between surface layer and soil-contacting parts, and bottom soil and soil-contacting parts, which can accurately describe contact mechanics and tangential interactions, and has a certain degree of convergence for the study of particle flow or accumulation behavior, and was widely used in similar research11,16,24. The bonding radii with the underlying soil particles were set at 8 mm and 15 mm, respectively, with both the critical normal stress and critical shear stress established at 2.35 × 105 Pa and 1.86 × 105 Pa. Additionally, based on the empirical relationship between moisture content and bonding parameters in existing studies40, the field moisture conditions were transformed into quantifiable bonding parameter inputs in the model. The average moisture content of the field soil in the study area was 17.16%, so the normal stiffness per unit area and shear stiffness per unit area were defined as 1.73 × 108 N/m3 and 9.07 × 107 N/m3, respectively.

Discrete element model parameter calibration

Angle of repose test

To ensure the applicability of soil parameters in the literature, first of all, the soil angle of repose was determined. After filling the soil with a bottomless cylindrical tube (with an inner diameter of 50 mm and a height of 200 mm) to a certain height, lift the cylindrical tube at a constant speed of 0.05 m/s slowly. Once the soil slope stabilizes, take vertical photos and import them into the CAD software to mark the soil angle of repose. Repeat this process five times. A falling device was established in the EDEM software to generate soil of the same quality as the test, and the simulated soil angle of repose was obtained and compared with the measured soil angle of repose. The soil angle of repose was shown in Fig. 5, the test and simulation values of the soil angle of repose were 31.6° and 32.9° respectively, with a relative error of 4.11%.

Direct shear test

The static and dynamic friction coefficients of the soil in the literature were verified through direct shear simulation tests. The direct shear simulation test was shown in Fig. 6. The shear strength of soil under three vertical pressures (100 kPa, 200 kPa, and 300 kPa) was determined by the fast shear test41. Test instruments include ZJ type strain control direct shear Apparatus, 1000 N-S type tension and compression sensor, and electronic balance with accuracy of 0.001 g. Based on the principle of direct shear test, a shear simulation test device was established, which was divided into an upper box, a lower box and a pressure plate. The outer diameter of the upper box and the lower box was 62 mm and the height was 20 mm. The upper opening of the lower box was open and the lower opening of the lower box was closed, while both the upper and lower openings of the upper box were open. Add a vertical load to the soil model by applying a smaller vertical downward velocity to the pressure plate. Then, a certain horizontal velocity of 1 mm/s was given to the lower shear box. The simulation stops when the horizontal displacement of the lower box reaches 6 mm. During this period, the peak value was taken as the shear strength. If there was no peak value, the value at 4 mm was taken.

Direct shear simulation test was drawn using EDEM 2022.2.0 (https://altair.com.cn/edem).

The shear strength obtained of surface soil from the rapid direct shear simulation was compared with that from the test. As shown in Table 6, the relative error of the shear strength between the simulation and the test was 3.83% to 9.24%. It can be considered that the simulation results of the rapid direct shear of the soil were approximately consistent with the test results, indicating that the calibrated simulated soil parameters can reflect the characteristics of the field soil.

Soil penetration test

The virtual soil chamber was compacted and calibrated through soil penetration tests. The soil bulk densities measured at depths of 0–50 mm, 50–100 mm and 100–150 mm were 1630.1 kg‧m−³, 1713.2 kg‧m−³ and 1823.4 kg‧m−³, respectively. Compared with the on-site measurement results, the average error was 1.5%. The soil penetration simulation test was shown in Fig. 7. It can be observed that the maximum velocity of soil particles during the penetration process occurs around the cone head area, which was consistent with the research results of Kotrocz et al.43. Box plots were used to record 15 sets of penetration resistance values with a depth increment of 10 mm. Data of upper and lower limits, median and two quartiles were collected to reflect the distribution characteristics of the data. The results of soil penetration resistance obtained were shown in Fig. 8. The on-site measured penetration resistance showed an increasing trend with depth, and the fluctuation was relatively small. However, the fluctuation was more obvious in the simulation. Chen et al.44. also reported this phenomenon. This difference might be due to the fact that the on-site soil was composed of organic matter, gases and microorganisms, forming a more compact entity, while the virtual soil chamber was filled with spherical particles with a radius of 7 millimeters, inevitably leaving gaps between the particles, thereby causing greater fluctuations in penetration resistance. The resistance of the cone in the simulation mainly comes from the breaking force of the bond and the frictional resistance between the bonds. As the penetration depth of the cone increases, the contact area with soil particles also increases, thereby increasing the frictional resistance. In addition, the particle density of the virtual soil chamber increases with the increase of depth, and the penetration resistance also increases accordingly. The comparison between the actual resistance value and the simulation data showed that the changing trends of the simulation value and the test value were basically consistent. To quantify the error between the measured values and the simulated penetration resistance, the simulation results were fitted using the least square method. The fitting curve was shown in Fig. 8. Using the fitted curve equation, with an increment of 10 mm, the average simulated penetration resistance at depths ranging from 0 to 150 mm was calculated to be 119.67 N. Compared with the average field measured resistance of 116.82 N, the relative error was 2.44%, indicating that the calibrated simulated soil parameters fully reflect the characteristics of the field soil.

Filed verification experiments methods

In order to test the working performance of the machine, a prototype of the double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine was processed and manufactured, and a field test was carried out in September 2024 in Jiuli Village, Baishiyi Town, Jiulongpo District, Chongqing Municipality (latitude: 29°19′ N, longitude: 106°20′ E, Altitude: 300 m). During operation, the tractor advances at a speed of 0.5 ~ 0.7 m/s. During the test, the rotational speed of the deep rotation device blades was 260r/min, and the rotation speed of soil crushing device blades was 430r/min.

To verify the validity of the simulation results, tests were conducted on the traction resistance of the dual-axis rotary tillage and ridging machine under different tillage depth conditions, and the measured data were compared and analyzed with the simulated values. The traction resistance testing system mainly consists of a BSLS-2 tension sensor, an ART Technology data acquisition card, and a computer. The tension sensor was installed at the connection between the tractor and the rotary tiller, ensuring that the force direction of the sensor is consistent with the direction of the traction force, and that the installation was firm to avoid loosening or displacement during the test. During the test, the ART Technology data acquisition card recorded the horizontal traction force data of the machine in real time during operation, and the computer collected the dynamic changes of the traction force using LabVIEW software. Three sets of repeated tests were carried out under each set tillage depth.

The surface soil fragmentation rate was selected as the evaluation index. The measurement method involved selecting soil clods from a 0.25 m2 area on the ridge surface at a depth of 5 cm. The percentage of the mass of clods with the longest side measuring less than 40 mm relative to the total mass was used as the indicator, as shown in formula (1)45. A traditional single-shaft rotary tillage ridging equipment was chosen for comparison.

Tillage performance analysis of single-shaft and double-shaft

The disturbance intensity of soil represented the disturbance ability of a tillage tool. The tillage processes with working condition by single-shaft and double-shaft were given in Fig. 11. Prior to 0.63s (Fig. 11a and b), the machine was in the soil penetration stage. Between 0.63s and 2.88s (Fig. 11c and d), the machine completely entered the soil tank, initiating the formal rotary tillage process, during which the machine operated steadily. At the 2.88 s mark (Fig. 11e and f), the machine was about to exit the soil tank, signifying the completion of the rotary tillage operation and concluding the coupled simulation6. When the rotary tillage blade roller was fully engaged with the soil, it generates continuous shear, impact, tearing, and throwing effects on both the topsoil and subsoil47. The soil particles ejected by the single-shaft down under the influence of gravity. In contrast, the double-shaft equipment, aided by the rear-connected soil crushing device, subjects the thrown soil to secondary impacts and fragmentation, resulting in a stable stratified tillage condition. The Fig. 7 illustrates obvious differences in the effects of the two contact modes on soil morphology following tillage and throwing. After operating the double-shaft equipment, the mixing of topsoil and subsoil particles was more uniform compared to the single-shaft, indicating that the double-shaft equipment provides greater disturbance and enhanced crushing capability.

The number of broken bonds, traction resistance and energy consumption during the operation was illustrated in Fig. 12. The number of broken bonds of both topsoil and subsoil particles continue to increase over time, stabilizing after approximately 2.5 s. The number of broken bonds for the single-shaft and double-shaft topsoil particles shows similar trends until around 0.5 s. Beyond this point, the number of topsoil broken bonds of double-shaft was significantly greater than that for single-shaft (Fig. 12a). In contrast, the changes in the number of subsoil broken bonds for single-shaft and double-shaft conditions were quite similar, with the double-shaft showing a slight increase over the single-shaft (Fig. 12b). The total number of topsoil broken bonds was 141,659 for single-shaft and 158,904 for double-shaft, indicating that the total for double-shaft was 12.17% higher than that for single-shaft. For the subsoil particles, the total number of broken bonds was 101,949 for single-shaft and 104,145 for double-shaft, reflecting a 2.15% increase in the total number of broken bonds of double-shaft (Fig. 13a). These results suggest that the broken performance of double-shaft configurations on topsoil was significantly superior to that of single-shaft configurations, and the total number of topsoil broken bonds was approximately 1.5 times that of the subsoil broken bonds. This supports the agronomic requirements for fine topsoil and robust subsoil.

The traction resistance of the rotary knife roller initially increases sharply, then decreases and stabilizes. Notably, the peak traction resistance of the single-shaft rotary knife roller was significantly higher than that of the double-shaft rotary knife roller, indicating that the single-shaft system places greater demands on the tractor (Fig. 12c). The average traction resistance for the single-shaft and double-shaft rotary knife rollers was measured at 1082.5 N and 903.1 N, respectively. This indicates that the traction resistance of the single-shaft rotary knife rollers was 36.57% greater than that of the double-shaft rotary knife rollers. Furthermore, the traction resistance of the single-shaft rotary knife rollers was considerably higher than that of the double-shaft (Fig. 13b). The average energy consumption exhibits a general trend of initially rising and then falling. During the soil preparation stage, average energy consumption increases sharply, with the peak energy consumption of the double-shaft system being significantly greater than that of the single-shaft system. This discrepancy arises because, once the rotary knife roller was fully engaged with the soil, the trailing broken knife roller of the double-shaft system makes contact, resulting in higher resistance. When the knife roller was completely in contact with the soil, the energy consumption reflects minor fluctuations (Fig. 12d). The average energy consumption for the single-shaft and double-shaft systems was recorded at 1350.7 J and 1359.9 J, respectively, indicating that the average energy consumption was essentially comparable (Fig. 13b).

This study examined the trends in number of broken bonds, traction resistance of rotary knife roller, and average energy consumption under various tillage conditions, as illustrated in Fig. 15. A linear regression equation correlating tillage performance with tillage depth was established. The determination coefficient (R²) of the regression equations ranged from 0.9045 to 0.9977, it indicates that the model has a good linear fitting degree under the condition of tillage depth ranging from 130 to 250 mm. From Fig. 15a and b, it was observed that the number of topsoil broken bonds exhibited a downward trend with increasing tillage depth, while the number of subsoil broken bonds displayed an opposite trend. This phenomenon occurs because, as tillage depth increases, the subsoil comes into contact with the subsequent crushing knife roller, leading to an increase in the broken bonds of the subsoil particles and a less pronounced stratification tillage effect. Furthermore, the traction resistance of rotary knife roller and average energy consumption demonstrated an upward trend with increasing tillage depth(Fig. 15c and d), suggesting that greater tillage depths require higher power demands from the tractor. Therefore, selecting an appropriate tillage depth can facilitate stratified tillage while optimizing energy consumption48.

Experimental results and analysis

The comparison between the simulation and test results of the traction resistance of the machine under a tillage depth of 160 mm was shown in Fig. 16. The variation trends of traction resistance in the simulation and test were similar; especially in the stable region, the fluctuation range of traction resistance was small. The peak value in the test results was slightly higher than the simulated value, but the overall results were nearly consistent. As can be seen from Table 10, under different tillage depth conditions, the average test values of traction resistance range from 845.95 N to 1864.49 N, while the simulated values range from 792.17 N to 1894.54 N. The test values were slightly higher than the simulated values, which may be due to the fact that the actual soil particle radius was smaller than the set value in the simulation, resulting in more soil bonding bonds in reality and thus requiring greater traction force. The relative errors between the simulated and test traction resistance values range from 4.24% to 11.25%, with an average of 7.21%. These errors were mainly caused by factors such as inconsistent uniformity of actual soil and unstable operation of the machine. However, overall, the established simulation model can be used to predict the operational performance of tillage and soil preparation equipment for the sticky soil49,50.

Conclusions

(1) This paper proposed and studied a double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine, whose structure was mainly composed of a transmission systema, a deep rotation device, a soil crushing device, a ridging component, and the working process was analyzed theoretically.

(2) Through the response surface test, the parameters of double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine were analyzed. The optimal parameters were determined as 260 r/min of rotation speed of deep rotation device, 430 r/min of rotation speed of soil crushing device, and 340 mm of curtain horizontal distance.

(3) A comparison of the working performance between single-shaft and double-shaft revealed that the double-shaft exerts a greater disturbance intensity on the topsoil. Furthermore, the peak traction resistance of rotary knife roller of the double-shaft were significantly lower than those of the single-shaft, indicating that the double-shaft operates more stably and has a reduced impact on traction power.

(4) The depth of tillage significantly affects the intensity of soil disturbance. As the tillage depth increases, the number of topsoil broken bonds tends to decrease, while the number of subsoil broken bonds, energy consumption, and traction resistance of rotary knife roll correspondingly increase. This indicates that a greater tillage depth reduces the effectiveness of stratified tillage and increases energy consumption. Therefore, selecting an appropriate tillage depth was particularly important.

(5) The design of the double-shaft layered rotary tillage and ridging machine has effectively improved the rotary tillage and ridging performance for sticky soil, which couled result in increased yield and quality of crop ridging cultivation. Soil cultivation was a very complex process with many influencing factors. Compared to the actual physical properties of wet soil, it may lose some forces that were difficult to consider, such as the capillary force between soil particles. In the future, further research will be conducted on the precise modeling of the soil in this study area.

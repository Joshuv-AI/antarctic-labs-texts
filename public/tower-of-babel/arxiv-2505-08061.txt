Sub-exponential tails in biased run and tumble equations with unbounded velocities
Émeric Bouin, Josephine Evans, Luca Ziviani

Abstract
. Run and tumble equations are widely used models for bacterial chemotaxis. In this paper, we are interested in the long time behaviour of run and tumble equations with unbounded velocities. We show existence, uniqueness and quantitative convergence towards a steady state. In contrast to the bounded velocity case, the equilibrium has sub-exponential tails and we have sub-exponential rate of convergence to equilibrium. This produces additional technical challenges. We are able to successfully adapt both Harris’ type and 𝖫 2 − \mathsf{L}^{2}- hypocoercivity a la Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser techniques.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the behaviour of solutions to the
run and tumble
model, which was first introduced by Stroock
[
41
]
, Alt
[
1
]
, and further studied by Othmer, Dunbar and Alt
[
38
]
. This model aims to investigate, at the mesoscopic level, the movement of bacteria in the presence of a chemotactic chemical substance and is given by

\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)=\int_{\mathcal{V}}(K(x,v,v^{\prime})f(t,x,v^{\prime})-K(x,v^{\prime},v)f(t,x,v))\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}.

The function
f:=f(t,x,v)
represents for positive time
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
the density of bacteria in the phase space
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the set of velocities.

The movement of some microrganisms like
E. Coli
can be seen as an alternation between two main movements:
the
run
phase, which, consists in a movement in a straight line at a constant speed, and the
tumble
, that is the reorientation from the previous velocity
v
to another velocity
v^{\prime}
. The probability of choosing a velocity
v^{\prime}
after
v
is described by the
tumbling frequency

K(x,v,v^{\prime})
, that represents the distribution of post-tumbling velocities when the bacterium is located in
x
. This
x
-dependence of the collision operator can also produce a spatial confinement effect, despite the lack of a force field in the transport operator. In fact, depending on the position, a bacteria can be led to change its speed more frequently depending on whether it is heading towards an area with a lower chemo-attractant density. The kernel
K
is then chosen in such a way that microorganisms move towards the regions with higher chemo-attractant density, causing the formation of clusters.

The novelty and challenge of this paper lies in the fact that we work with unbounded velocities. These were explored in
[
5
]
and following this subexponential rates of convergence to equilibrium were expected. Large velocities are a barrier to convergence. This is because even if a bacteria selects a velocity which points towards the peak of the chemoattractant density, if the velocity is sufficiently large (depending on the distance to the peak), the bacteria is likely to travel well past the peak and may be further from the peak when it next takes a tumble. It is a technical challenge in our paper to show that despite this effect we still have convergence to equilibrium and to adapt the technical tools to work with the stretch exponentials appearing in both the weighted functional spaces and rates of convergence. Briefly, our mathematical innovations are as follows.

Finding appropriate weights in the space variable
. Run and Tumble equations have an unusual and mathematically challenging mechanism for producing spatial confinement of their solutions. Bacteria tumble more frequently when they are going down the gradient of the chemoattractant than up it. Over time we expect this to produce a skew in their velocity distribution towards velocities going towards peaks of chemoattractant. This skew in the velocity distribution then causes bacteria to aggregate around peaks of the chemoattractant. As described above this process is complicated in the presence of potentially very large velocities. Following the principles of Harris’s theorem we weight our spaces with a
Foster-Lyapunov function
which is a moment which remains bounded along the flow. Finding such a weight is challenging and we need to capture the third order confinement effects and deal with the challenges of high velocities. We adapt weights from
[
36
,
23
]
which work in the case of bounded velocities. The immediate problem is that these weight functions are not positive in the case of unbounded velocities. In order to adapt our weight functions we need to use different reasoning when working in parts of the state space where
|v|
is much larger than
|x|
so our arguments are based around a splitting of the state space. Furthermore in order to make use of these alternative confinement mechanisms for large velocities we need to switch exponential terms to stretch exponential terms.

Adapting
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}
splitting arguments in order to find
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
controls on the semi-group.
The arguments based on Harris’s theorem gives us
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-type information about the semi-group. We can use operator splitting techniques originally due to
[
24
]
to bootstrap this to
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
. Here there are many technical challenges produces by the presence of many stretch exponential. We also needed to repeat similar arguments based around splitting the space into parts where
|v|
is large compared to
|x|
and the complement.

Adapting
\mathsf{L}^{2}
hypocoercivity methods to our context.
Hypocoercivity is a name given to a collection of tools (usually entropy based) for showing convergence to equilibrium for
degenerately coercive equations
. Our final results involve adapting
\mathsf{L}^{2}
hypocoercivity results in the style of Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser to the equation studied here. Since we already have
\mathsf{L}^{1}
and
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
information it is likely we could get similar outcomes with interpolation. However our main motivation for using
\mathsf{L}^{2}
hypocoercivity is to develop tools to extend the range of applicability of these techniques to equations with non-explicit steady states and sub-geometric rates of convergence induced by the spatial confinement. This program is particularly important if we wish, in the future, to look at related equations which are non-linear or linearised (so not preserving positivity) as these are outside the scope of the previous techniques. Here there are two main challenges. Firstly the steady state is non-explicit and we have limited information about its behaviour. It was shown in
[
10
,
21
]
that
\mathsf{L}^{2}
style methods work well in this situation provided we can show the steady state still satisfies the appropriate functional inequalities. Secondly, due to the sub-exponential tails of our steady state the perturbation term used most
\mathsf{L}^{2}
hypocoercivity works will not be bounded by the norm. We need to adapt our perturbation term, here we compare both the challenge and solution to those encountered in
[
6
,
7
]
.

In this paper, we assume that the set of velocities is
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that the tumbling frequency has the expression

K(x,v,v^{\prime})=\Lambda\left(-v^{\prime}\cdot{\nabla_{x}}M\right)\mathcal{M}(v)\qquad x,v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

where we use the notation

\lfloor x\rceil=\sqrt{1+\lvert x\rvert^{2}},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The function
\mathcal{M}
is a fixed probability density of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}(v)=c_{0,\gamma}^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}}{\gamma}\right),\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for some
\gamma>0
, where

(1.2)

c_{k,\gamma}=\int_{v\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\lvert v\rvert^{k}\exp\left(-\frac{\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}}{\gamma}\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}v=2\pi^{\tfrac{d}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{d+k}{\gamma}-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+k}{\gamma}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)},\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

The function
\Lambda
is the
tumbling rate
and it depends on the gradient of the external signal
M=\ln(S)
along the direction of the velocity, where
S
is a given
function for the density of the chemoattractant. This expression for
K
means that, at every tumble, the new velocity is chosen randomly according to the density
\mathcal{M}
, but the frequency of such tumbles also depends on the mutual orientation between the initial velocity and the density of the chemoattractant. In this paper we suppose that the density of the chemoattractant is constant in time
\lfloor\cdot\rceil=-\ln S
, so that our main equation is in fact

(1.3)

\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)=\mathcal{M}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(t,x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}-\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(t,x,v),

for
(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. For further use, we shall denote

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}(f)(x,v):=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(x,v)+\mathcal{M}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}-\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(x,v)

and we shall call
S_{\mathcal{L}}
the associated semigroup. Moreover, we define the dual operator
\mathcal{L}^{*}
by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi)f\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{L}f)\phi\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v,

for any
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, that is

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi=v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi+\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\left(\int\phi(x,v^{\prime})\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}-\phi(x,v)\right).

1.1.
Summary of previous contributions

The run and tumble model was first introduced by D. W. Stroock
[
41
]
. In that article, the author provides a stochastic modelling of the motion of E. Coli based on the experimental results of Berg and Brown
[
3
]
(see also
[
32
]
). The motion of a bacterium is broken into two modes, the run and the tumble, and can be described as a Markov process, whose backward equation is the run and tumble equation. Initially the set of velocities was considered to be the unit sphere. Some years later, the subject has been pushed further by W. Alt
[
1
]
, with an analysis on different models of biased random walks. In particular, it is shown that solutions to the underlying integro-differential equation allows to derive the well-known Patlak-Keller-Segel diffusion equation through the hydrodynamic limit. A development of this study can be also found in
[
38
]
. The non-linear version of the run and tumble has also been studied and it was first proposed by
[
15
]
.

The majority of the articles deal with the run and tumble equations with a bounded symmetric set of velocities
\mathcal{V}
, equipped with a certain probability measure. This means that bacteria cannot run at a velocity beyond a certain fixed threshold, furthermore they can choose post-reorientation velocities weighted with different probabilities. Among the most important works, we quote Calvez, Raoul and Schmeiser
[
10
]
, where they studied a one dimensional run and tumble model where the set of velocities
\mathcal{V}
is an interval. They managed to prove existence and uniqueness of a stationary solution, showing in addition the exponential decay of its tails. Moreover, they applied the abstract hypocoercivity method developed in
[
17
]
in order to show an exponential convergence in time towards the stationary solution in a weighted
\mathsf{L}^{2}
norm.

The cases of higher dimensions was later achieved by Mischler and Weng
[
36
]
, thanks to a meticulous study of the semigroup associated to the equation. In that work, the set of velocities taken in consideration was the unit ball and the tumbling kernel is of the same type as in
[
10
]
. The authors exploited semigroup theory and the Krein-Rutman theorem to show an exponential convergence towards the stationary solution, effectively extending the same result obtained in dimension one to all dimensions. Many tools and ideas of the present article are inspired by this paper.

Similar results were also obtained by Evans and Yoldas
[
22
]
, who discussed a linear run and tumble model with more general tumbling kernels which, for example, allow to model a bacterial sensitivity that varies more regularly. They made use of the Harris Theorem, which allows them to inherit the convergence for the non-linear equation too. Moreover, in
[
23
]
, the same authors also considered non-uniform tumbling kernels, which are more physically relevant in terms of modelling the chemotactic bacterial motion, see
[
3
,
31
]
. In this work, a first result on the linear run and tumble model with unbounded velocities set is discussed, which has strongly inspired our work. The authors proved existence of a stationary solution and convergence estimates in total variation norm.

Another phenomenon that has been studied starting from the run and tumble model is the existence of travelling waves of bacterial concentration, see
[
9
,
5
]
and the references therein. In addition to the reorientation mechanism due to the chemo-attractant, the presence of a nutrient
[
9
]
or a mechanism of reproduction and saturation of individuals
[
5
]
is introduced. It is proved that, always with bounded velocity sets, there exist travelling wave solutions. Concerning the unbounded velocity case, they also proved that there cannot exist travelling waves even in dimension 1, this is due to the fact that larger and larger velocities destroy the wave front.

The different behavior in unbounded velocities domains is the main motivation for this work. Our work can be seen as the continuation of
[
36
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
. We explore the behaviour of solutions to the run and tumble equation when the set of velocity is the whole space
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

1.2.
Assumptions and main results

For a positive weight function
m\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
we denote
\mathsf{L}^{p}(m)
the functional space defined by the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{p}(m)}:=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert f(x,v)\rvert^{p}m(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In our first result we provide existence of a stationary solution
G
and convergence rates towards it in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
norm. The function
\mathcal{M}
has been introduced in (
1.1
) and we make the following assumption on the tumbling rate
\Lambda
.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

The tumbling rate
\Lambda\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
is a function of the form

\Lambda=1+\chi\psi,

where
\chi\in(0,1)
is a fixed value and
\psi
is a bounded, odd, increasing function and
m\mapsto m\psi(m)\in W^{1,\infty}
,
\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
,
\lim_{m\to\pm\infty}\psi(m)=\pm 1
.

The parameter
\chi\in(0,1)
is the
sensitivity
of the bacterial population to the chemo-attractant. We use the notation
\mathsf{a}\lesssim\mathsf{b}
if there exist a constant
\mathsf{c}>0
such that
\mathsf{a}\leq\mathsf{c}\,\mathsf{b}
and we write
\mathsf{a}\asymp\mathsf{b}

if both
\mathsf{a}\lesssim\mathsf{b}
and
\mathsf{b}\lesssim\mathsf{a}
hold. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be defined by (
1.1
) for some
\gamma>0
and assume that
1.1
holds. Then we have the following.

(i)

There exists a unique, normalised, invariant by rotations, stationary state
G
to (
1.3
) whose density
\rho_{G}
satisfies the bounds

(1.6)

e^{-\underline{\nu}\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\lesssim\rho_{G}(x)\lesssim e^{-\overline{\nu}\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}},

for some constants
\underline{\nu}>\overline{\nu}>0
.

(ii)

For any
m\colon(x,v)\mapsto e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}}+e^{b\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}}
, with
a\in\Big(0,\tfrac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\Big]
,
b\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\right)
and
\nu\in(0,\overline{\nu})
, for any normalised
f_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)
we have

(1.7)

\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}}\lesssim e^{-\lambda\,t^{a}}\lVert f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\lambda=a^{-a}>0
.

(iii)

For any
m\colon(x,v)\mapsto\lfloor x\rceil^{k}+\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}
, with
k>1
, for any normalised
f_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)
, we have

(1.8)

\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lfloor t\rceil^{k}}\lVert f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

The second main result of this paper is the adaptation of the Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser method
[
17
]
to the run and tumble equation (
1.3
) to get convergence in weighted
\mathsf{L}^{2}
norm. This method has been successfully applied to several kinetic equations
[
17
,
10
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
and is based on a micro-macro decomposition. Let us consider the Hilbert space
\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})
, where
G
is the stationary solution of (
1.3
), whose existence is provided by
1.2
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the natural scalar product and by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}
the natural norm. We define the orthogonal projection
\Pi\colon\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})
as

(1.9)

\Pi f=\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}G\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1}).

Thanks to the fact
\mathcal{L}G=0
, we shall show the
microscopic coercivity
property

\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\rangle\lesssim-\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})},

though it is not enough to deduce a convergence result. The main idea of the method is to define an entropy functional
\mathsf{H}\colon\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})\to\mathbb{R}
that is equivalent to
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}
and for which we can quantify the dissipation

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathsf{H}[f]=:\mathsf{D}[f]\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1}),\;\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v=0.

By estimating
\mathsf{D}[f]
in terms of some weaker norm of
f
it is possible to deduce a decay rate for
\mathsf{H}[f]
for any
f
with zero mass. Unlike many previous works, the fact that the steady state
G
is not explicit represents an important difficulty for this method, as
G
is the weight itself of the framework functional space. For these reasons, some features of the steady state must be known with sufficient precision, in particular we need assumptions on the density

\rho_{G}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},

and the matrix

V_{G}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\otimes v\,G(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Here is our precise assumption.

Hypothesis 1.3
.

Assume that
\Lambda=1+\chi\,\sgn
with
\chi\in(0,1)
and that the density
\rho_{G}
satisfies the bounds

(1.10)

\rho_{G}(x)\asymp\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell-\tfrac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\left(d-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lvert x\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\ell\in\left(0,\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\right)
and
\nu=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}(1+\chi)^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}
. Moreover, the matrix
V_{G}(x)
satisfies

(1.11)

\lvert V_{G}(x)^{-2}\rvert\lvert{\nabla_{x}}V_{G}(x)\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}\lesssim 1\qquad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

The choice
\Lambda=1+\chi\,\text{sgn}
is made in order to carry out explicit computations. To this assumption is supported by numerical results as shown in

Our second main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be defined as in (
1.1
) and assume

(i)

For any

(ii)

For any
m\colon(x,v)\mapsto\lfloor x\rceil^{k}+\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}

\lambda>0

f_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})

(1.13)

\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+\lambda\,t)^{\frac{k}{\ell}}}\lVert f_{0}-G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{2}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Notice that in

1.3.
Organisation of the paper

In
Section

2
, we derive a family of Lyapunov functions for the run and tumble equation (in this article we mean Lyapunov functions in the probabilistic sense, also known as Foster-Lyapunov functions). Such functions are fundamental for the whole article, since they provide the correct weighted spaces to work in and determine the rate of convergence towards the equilibrium. In
Section

3
, we use the sub-geometric Harris theorem to get the existence of a stationary measure and a convergence rate in total variation norm. A brief review of the Harris theorem is presented in
Appendix

A
. In
Section

4
, we adapt the strategy of
[
36
]
to study the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{L}}
in some suitable weighted spaces. The main purpose is to prove that
S_{\mathcal{L}}
is bounded in a weighted space
X=\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)
and, in a second time, to prove existence of a stationary solution
G\in X
through a fixed point argument. The main tool is the iterated Duhamel formula, which states that if the operator splits as
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}
, then we can rewrite the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{L}}
in terms of
\mathcal{A}
and

2.
Weak-Lyapunov functions in
\mathsf{L}^{1}

In this section, we find some Lyapunov functions
m
for (

\Psi(z)=z\psi(z),\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

This function plays a fundamental role for the proof of the Lyapunov condition, as has already been shown in
[
22
, Lemma 1.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
1.1
. There exists a constant
\zeta>0
such that

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\geq\zeta\,\mathds{1}_{\lvert x\rvert\geq 1}.

Proof of
2.1
.

Since
\lim_{z\to\pm\infty}\psi(z)=\pm 1
, there exists
R>0
such that
\lvert\psi(z)\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
\lvert z\rvert\geq R
. As a consequence

\Psi(z)=z\psi(z)\geq\frac{\lvert z\rvert}{2},\qquad\text{for all }\lvert z\rvert\geq R.

Then, thanks to the positivity of
\Psi
and the symmetry of
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\left\lvert\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right\rvert\geq R}\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{\lvert x\cdot v^{\prime}\rvert}{\lfloor x\rceil}\geq R}\left\lvert\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right\rvert\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{\lfloor x\rceil}\int_{\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{\lfloor x\rceil}\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\geq R}\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

Next, we point out that if
\lvert x\rvert\geq 1
, then
\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{\lfloor x\rceil}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. We conclude
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{\lfloor x\rceil}\mathds{1}_{\lvert x\rvert\geq 1}\int_{\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\geq\sqrt{2}R}\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\zeta\mathds{1}_{\lvert x\rvert\geq 1}

where
\zeta=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\int_{\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\geq\sqrt{2}R}\lvert\text{e}\cdot v\rvert\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}
.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu a^{2}(1+2\chi)^{2}}{4(1+\chi)^{2}B}

\displaystyle=k(x\cdot v)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}^{*}m_{2})(x,v)

\displaystyle=\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}-(2-k)(x\cdot v)^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-4}-\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)(x\cdot v)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}

\displaystyle=\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}-(2-k)(x\cdot v)^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-4}-(x\cdot v)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}-\chi\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}^{*}m_{3})(x,v)

\displaystyle=-\Psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\left(\frac{\lvert v\rvert^{2}}{\lfloor x\rceil}-\frac{(x\cdot v)^{2}}{\lfloor x\rceil^{3}}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}-(k-1)\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)x\cdot v\lfloor x\rceil^{k-3}

\displaystyle-\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{v^{\prime}\cdot x}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}+\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}

and

(2.10)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}^{*}m_{4})

\displaystyle=\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)c_{2k,\gamma}-\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}.

The constants in front of
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
in the expression of
m
are meant to simplify respectively the term
(x\cdot v)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}
and
\Psi\left(\tfrac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}
after the summation. Using that
\Psi\geq 0
, we have

(2.11)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{L}^{*}m)(x,v)\leq&\,C_{2}\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}-\frac{k\chi}{1+\chi}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{v^{\prime}\cdot x}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\\
&+B\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)c_{2k,\gamma}-B\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}\end{split}

with
C_{2}=3k+k^{2}+\frac{\chi k(k+1)}{1+\chi}
.
Now, if
k\in[1,2]
, then we estimate

\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}\leq\lfloor v\rceil^{2k},

and we choose
B>\tfrac{2C_{2}}{1-\chi}
. If
k>2
, Young’s inequality implies that for any
\delta>0

(2.12)

\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}\leq\delta\,\frac{k-2}{k-1}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}+\frac{1}{k-1}\frac{\lfloor v\rceil^{2(k-1)}}{\delta^{\frac{1}{k-2}}}\leq\delta\,\frac{k-2}{k-1}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}+\frac{\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}}{(k-1)\delta^{\frac{1}{k-2}}}.

Thus, first choosing
\delta<\tfrac{k\chi(k-1)(1-\chi)\zeta}{2\,C_{2}(k-2)(1+\chi)}
and then
B>\tfrac{2\,C_{2}}{(1-\chi)(k-1)}\delta^{-\frac{1}{k-2}}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{*}m\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{1+\chi}-\frac{C_{2}(k-2)\delta}{k-1}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}-\left(B(1-\chi)\lvert v\rvert^{2k}-\frac{C_{2}}{(k-1)\delta^{\frac{1}{k-2}}}\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}\right)+B(1+\chi)c_{2k,\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{2(1+\chi)}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}-\frac{B(1-\chi)}{2}\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}+B(1+\chi)c_{2k,\gamma}.

This inequality proves (
2.8
) provided
C
and
R
are large enough and
\varepsilon>0
is small enough.
∎

We conclude this section with some considerations about possible extensions. It is worth noticing that the same Lyapunov function (

\mathcal{M}(v)=c_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\lfloor v\rceil^{d+\gamma}}\qquad\gamma>2.

In fact, the polynomial decay (

only at (

and

In such cases, we can reproduce the same proof of

and find some Lyapunov functions, which furthermore can be exploited to show existence and uniqueness of a stationary state and estimate the rate of convergence towards it as in the next section.

Consider the splitting

\displaystyle\geq S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}f_{0},

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}

As a consequence, combining both we deduce

Consider

\mathcal{C}
as in the statement, we first prove the statement with initial state the Dirac measure
\delta_{x_{0},v_{0}}
with

.
We just need to progressively compute the integrals above. Consider

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(1+\chi)r}\delta_{x_{0},v_{0}}(x-vr,v)\geq e^{-(1+\chi)r}\delta_{x_{0}+rv_{0},v_{0}}(x,v),

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)e^{-(1+\chi)r}\mathcal{M}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta_{x_{0}+rv_{0},v_{0}}(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)e^{-(1+\chi)r}\mathcal{M}(v)\delta_{x_{0}+rv_{0}}(x).

\displaystyle(S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(s-r)\mathcal{A}_{1}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(r)\delta_{x_{0},v_{0}})(x,v)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)e^{-(1+\chi)(s-r)}e^{-(1+\chi)r}\mathcal{M}(v)\delta_{x_{0}+rv_{0}}(x-v(s-r))

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)^{2}e^{-(1+\chi)s}\mathcal{M}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\delta_{x_{0}+rv_{0}}(x-v^{\prime}(s-r))\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

Thanks to (
2.4

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{L}}(T)\mu=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(S_{\mathcal{L}}(T)\delta_{x^{\prime},v^{\prime}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\geq\frac{C_{0}^{2}(1-\chi)^{2}}{4}e^{-(1+\chi)T}\mathds{1}_{\lvert v\rvert\leq V_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\lvert x\rvert\leq X_{0}}\mu(\mathcal{C})

\mu_{\ast}=\tfrac{1}{\lvert B_{V_{0}}\rvert\lvert B_{X_{0}}\rvert}\mathds{1}_{\lvert v\rvert\leq V_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\lvert x\rvert\leq X_{0}}

.

Proof of

Lemma 4.4

(Dissipativity of
S_{\mathcal{B}}
)
.

Let
m(x,v)\asymp e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}}+e^{b\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}}
be defined as in
2.2
. Once
R>0
is chosen sufficiently large,
S_{\mathcal{B}}
is dissipative in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\cap\mathsf{L}^{p}(m)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
, namely,

(4.14)

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\cap\mathsf{L}^{p}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\cap\mathsf{L}^{p}(m)}\lesssim 1.

Proof of
4.4
.

\#
Step 1.
The case
p=1
. The dual operator of
\mathcal{B}
is defined by

(\mathcal{B}^{*}\phi)(x,v)=(\mathcal{L}^{*}\phi)(x,v)-\eta_{R}(x)\Lambda(x,v^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\phi(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

for every
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. Let
R>0
large enough according to the Lyapunov condition as in
2.2
. We have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}^{*}m)(x,v)

\displaystyle\leq\nu C\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(x,v)-\epsilon\lfloor x\rceil^{a-1}m-\eta_{R}(x)(1-\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[(1-\delta_{1})e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}}+\nu e^{b\lvert v^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}\right]\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\nu C\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(x,v)-\epsilon\lfloor x\rceil^{a-1}m-\eta_{R}(x)(1-\chi)(1-\delta_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}}\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\nu C\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(x,v)-\epsilon\lfloor x\rceil^{a-1}m-\eta_{R}(x)(1-\chi)(1-\delta_{1})

Remembering that
\delta_{1}=\frac{\nu a^{2}(2+3\chi)^{2}}{16(1+\chi)^{2}B}
, we can take
\nu>0
small enough such that
\nu C\leq(1-\chi)(1-\delta_{1})
, and we conclude

\mathcal{B}^{*}m\leq-\epsilon\lfloor x\rceil^{a-1}m\leq 0.

and it follows that

(4.15)

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)}\leq 1.

\#
Step 2.
The case
p=\infty
.
Let us consider the splitting
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{0}
defined in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).
From Duhamel’s formula we have

S_{\mathcal{B}}=S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\star(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})^{\star j}+S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\star(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})^{\star(d+1)}\star\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}.

Thanks to
4.2
we have

(4.16)

\|S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\star(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})^{\star j}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)\to L^{\infty}(m)}\leq\|S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\star\|\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\|^{\star j}_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\chi)t}

because all the convolution terms are exponentially decaying. Writing this inequality for all the integers
j\in\llbracket 0,d+1\rrbracket
, we see that

(4.17)

\|S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\|S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\star(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})^{\star j}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\lesssim e^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\chi)t}

Concerning the last term of the Duhamel formula, consider the weight
\omega(x,v)=e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}+b|v|^{\gamma}}
as defined in
4.3
. We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}_{x}\mathsf{L}^{\infty}_{v}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\lvert(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)f)(x,v)\rvert e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}+b|v|^{\gamma}}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\eta_{R}(x))\Lambda(x,v^{\prime})\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\mathcal{M}(v)e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}+b|v|^{\gamma}}\right\}\lvert(S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)f)(x,v)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\mathcal{M}(v)e^{b|v|^{2}}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert(S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)f)(x,v)\rvert e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{a}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

(4.18)

where in the last step we used the dissipativity in

. Moreover, thanks to (

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\chi)t}\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{1}_{x}\mathsf{L}^{\infty}_{v}(\omega)}\lesssim 1\in\mathsf{L}_{t}^{\infty}([0,+\infty))

proved few lines above, and

, imply that

(4.19)

\|S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\star(\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})^{\star(d+1)}\star\mathcal{A}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\lesssim 1

Using (
4.17

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}

that combined with (

4.14
) for

\#
Step 3.
We also conclude that (
4.14
) holds for

because

is an interpolation space between

and

.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

By Gronwall’s Lemma, we find that for all

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

4.6
.

Let

). Then, using (

) and

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}m

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{*}m-\eta_{R}(x)\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(x,v^{\prime})\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{*}m-\eta_{R}(x)\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\left(\lfloor x\rceil^{k}-\frac{k\chi}{1+\chi}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)\mathcal{M}(v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}+Bc_{2k,\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\lvert v\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{k-2}-\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{1+\chi}(1-\eta_{R}(x))\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}+B(1+\chi)c_{2k,\gamma}(1-\eta_{R}(x))

\displaystyle\qquad-B(1-\chi)\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}-(1-\chi)\eta_{R}(x)\lfloor x\rceil^{k}

If

and then take

) by Young’s inequality and we have that for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}m

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C_{2}(k-2)}{k-1}\delta-\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{1+\chi}\right)(1-\eta_{R}(x))\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}+\left(\frac{C_{2}(k-2)}{k-1}\delta\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}-(1-\chi)\lfloor x\rceil^{k}\right)\eta_{R}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{C_{2}}{(k-1)\delta^{\tfrac{1}{k-2}}}-B(1-\chi)\right)\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}+B(1+\chi)c_{\gamma,k}(1-\eta_{R}(x)).

We can first choose

\delta<\frac{k-1}{2C_{2}(k-2)}\min\left\{\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{1+\chi},1-\chi\right\},

and then enlarge

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}m

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{k\chi(1-\chi)\zeta}{2(1+\chi)}(1-\eta_{R}(x))\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}-\frac{B(1-\chi)}{2}\lfloor v\rceil^{2k}-\frac{1-\chi}{2}\eta_{R}(x)+B(1+\chi)c_{\gamma,k}(1-\eta_{R}(x)).

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{*}m\lesssim-(\lfloor x\rceil^{k-1}+\lfloor v\rceil^{2k})\lesssim-m^{1-\tfrac{1}{k}}.

is dissipative in

, i.e.

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)\to\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)}\leq 1.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert g\rvert\mathcal{B}^{*}m\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert g\rvert m^{1-\tfrac{1}{k}}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v.

By the Hölder inequality and dissipativity, we have

which gives

The conclusion follows by Gronwall’s Lemma.

\displaystyle\lVert S_{\mathcal{B}}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\omega^{\ell})\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\omega^{\ell})\to X}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\lambda_{\ell}t^{a}},

proven respectively in
4.8
and in
4.5
. We then obtain

(4.36)

\lVert S_{\mathcal{B}}\star(\mathcal{A}S_{\mathcal{B}})^{\star(d+1)}\star\mathcal{A}S_{\mathcal{L}}\rVert_{X\to X}\lesssim 1,

that with (
4.35
) gives (
4.3
).

The proof of the existence of a steady state follows the same steps as in
[
36
]
. We introduce the following definition

\forall f\in X,\qquad{\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\;\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f\rVert_{X}.

Clearly
{\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}
is an equivalent norm to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X}
, indeed

\lVert f\rVert_{X}=\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(0)f\rVert_{X}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}\;\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f\rVert_{X}={\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}

and

{\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\;\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f\rVert_{X}\lesssim\sup_{t\geq 0}\;\lVert f\rVert_{X}=\lVert f\rVert_{X}.

For any rotation
\Omega\in SO(d)
we set
f_{\Omega}(x,v):=f(\Omega x,\Omega v)
, in this way we say that
f
is rotationally symmetric if
f_{\Omega}=f
for every
\Omega\in SO(d)
. Consider the set

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{f\in X\Big|f\geq 0,\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v=1,\;{\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}\leq C,\;f_{\Omega}=f,\;\forall\,\Omega\in SO(d)\right\}

with
C>0
large enough so that
\mathcal{C}
is not empty. In addition, the set
\mathcal{C}
is convex, closed and bounded for the weak-
*
topology of
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)
, as a consequence it is compact for the weak-
*
topology of
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)
by the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem. We also remind that
S_{\mathcal{L}}
is weakly-
*
continuous (this comes from the well-posedness Theorem).

Let us show that
\mathcal{C}
is invariant for
S_{\mathcal{L}}
. Clearly
S_{\mathcal{L}}
preserves positivity and total mass, and for any
t\geq 0
,

{\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\,\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t+s)f\rVert_{X}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f\rVert_{X}={\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|\kern-0.43057pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|\kern-0.43057pt\right|}

Let
f_{\Omega}(t,x,v)=f(t,\Omega x,\Omega v)
with
\Omega\in SO(d)
, then er have
\partial_{t}f_{\Omega}=(\partial_{t}f)_{\Omega}
,

v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\Omega}=v\cdot\Omega(\nabla_{x}f)_{\Omega}=(v\cdot\nabla_{x}f)_{\Omega}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f_{\Omega}(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathcal{M}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(\Omega x,\Omega v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathcal{M}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{x\cdot\Omega^{-1}v^{\prime}}{\lfloor x\rceil}\right)f(\Omega x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathcal{M}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\left(\frac{\Omega x\cdot v^{\prime}}{\lfloor\Omega x\rceil}\right)f(\Omega x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathcal{M}(\Omega v)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda(x,v^{\prime})f(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathcal{M}(v)\right)_{\Omega}

because
\mathcal{M}
is rotationally symmetric. Therefore if
f(t,x,v)
is a solution with initial datum
f(0,x,v)
, then
f_{\Omega}(t,x,v)
is a solution with initial datum
f_{\Omega}(0,x,v)
, in other words

S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{\Omega}=(S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f)_{\Omega}.

Thus we conclude that
\mathcal{C}
is an invariant set for
S_{\mathcal{L}}
. As a consequence, the Brouwer-Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem (see for example
[
36
,
12
]
), there exists at least one non-negative, invariant by rotation and normalized stationary state
G\in X
to (
1.3
).
∎

5.
Representation formula and pre-bounds above and below for the density

In this section we prove an upper and a lower bound on the density
\rho_{G}
of the steady state
G
of the run and tumble equation, whose existence has been shown in the previous paragraphs. We start with a representation formula.

Lemma 5.1

(Representation formula)
.

The stationary solution to (
1.3
),
G
satisfies the following equation

(5.1)

\forall x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\qquad G(x,v)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\Lambda(x-vr,v)dr}\mathcal{M}(v)\theta_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s

where

(5.2)

\theta_{G}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda(x,v^{\prime})G(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}.

Proof of
5.1
.

Let us remind the splitting
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{B}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{1}
where

(\mathcal{B}_{0}f)(x,v)=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(x,v)-\Lambda(x,v)f(x,v),

and

(\mathcal{A}_{1}f)(x,v)=\mathcal{M}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda(x,v^{\prime})f(x,v^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}v

By applying the Duhamel formula to
G
ad remembering that
S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)G=G
, we have

\displaystyle G-\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(s)\mathcal{A}_{1}G\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)G-\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(s)\mathcal{A}_{1}S_{\mathcal{L}}(t-s)G\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(t)G

Thanks to
4.2
and
1.2
we have that

\left\lVert G-\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(s)\mathcal{A}_{1}G\,\mathrm{d}s\right\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-\chi)t}\lVert G\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(m)}\longrightarrow 0

as
t\to 0
, therefore we have

\forall x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\qquad G(x,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(s)\mathcal{A}_{1}G(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}s

This equation is nothing other than (
5.1
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

It is worth emphasizing that some more explicit computations can be made in the one dimensional case. Indeed we have

(5.3)

G(x,v)=\begin{cases}\int_{u=-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{\lvert v\rvert}\mathcal{M}(v)e^{-\frac{1}{\lvert v\rvert}\int_{u}^{x}\Lambda(\tau,v)d\tau}\theta_{G}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u&\text{if }v\geq 0\\
\int_{u=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lvert v\rvert}\mathcal{M}(v)e^{-\frac{1}{\lvert v\rvert}\int_{x}^{x+u}\Lambda(\tau,v)d\tau}\theta_{G}(x+u)\,\mathrm{d}u&\text{if }v<0.\end{cases}

This expression is obtained from (
5.1
) by changing varibles

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u=x-vs\\
s=\frac{x-u}{v}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tau=x-vr\\
r=\frac{x-\tau}{v}\end{cases}

when
v\leq 0
and by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u=\lvert v\rvert s\\
s=\frac{u}{\lvert v\rvert}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tau=x+\lvert v\rvert r\\
r=\frac{\tau-x}{\lvert v\rvert}\end{cases}

when
v\geq 0
. The representation (
5.3
) will turn out to be useful when
\Lambda(x,v)=1+\chi\text{sgn}(xv)
and we will study the behavior of
\theta_{G}(x)
as
x\to\infty
.

Before moving on to the upper and lower bound on
\rho_{G}
, we introduce an important and recurrent function that we will often use in the following,
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(5.4)

\Phi(y):=\frac{1}{\lvert y\rvert^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{d-2}e^{-\frac{\lvert y\rvert}{u}-\frac{u^{\gamma}}{\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}u.

This definition will allows us to simplify the notation for the results of this section. Thanks to
B.2
in the Appendix, we deduce that the asymptotic approximations of
\Phi(y)
are

(5.5)

\Phi(y)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{1+\gamma}}\,\lvert y\rvert^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma}\lvert y\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\qquad\text{as }\lvert y\rvert\to\infty,

and

(5.6)

\Phi(y)\sim\begin{cases}\gamma^{\frac{d-1}{\gamma}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{\lvert y\rvert^{d-1}}&\text{if }d\geq 2\\
\lvert\ln\lvert y\rvert\rvert&\text{if }d=1\end{cases}\qquad\text{as }\lvert y\rvert\to 0.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be the stationary solution to (
1.3
), then the density
\rho_{G}
satisfies the following inequalities

(5.7)

\rho_{G}\star\phi_{1}\leq\rho_{G}\leq\rho_{G}\star\phi_{2}

where

(5.8)

\phi_{1}(y)=(1-\chi)c_{0,\gamma}^{-1}\Phi((1+\chi)y),

(5.9)

\phi_{2}(y)=(1+\chi)c_{0,\gamma}^{-1}\Phi((1-\chi)y)

and
\Phi
is given in (
5.4
).

Proof of
5.3
.

By the representation formula we have

\displaystyle G(x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\Lambda(x-vr,v)dr}\mathcal{M}(v)\theta_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(1+\chi)s}\mathcal{M}(v)\rho_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s

Integrating in
v
and changing the variables
z=x-sv
,
v=\frac{x-z}{s}
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(1+\chi)s}\mathcal{M}(v)\rho_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{d}}e^{-(1+\chi)s}\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{x-z}{s}\right)\rho_{G}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}z

Next, we change variable
s=\frac{\lvert x-z\rvert}{u}
,
u=\frac{\lvert x-z\rvert}{s}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{d-2}}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-(1+\chi)\frac{\lvert x-z\rvert}{u}}\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{x-z}{\lvert x-z\rvert}u\right)\rho_{G}(z)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq(\rho_{G}\star\phi_{1})(x)

where
\phi_{1}
is given by (
5.8
).

In the same way we prove the other inequality

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(1-\chi)s}\mathcal{M}(v)\rho_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq(1+\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{d}}e^{-(1-\chi)s}\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{x-z}{s}\right)\rho_{G}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq(1+\chi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{d-2}}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-(1-\chi)\frac{\lvert x-z\rvert}{u}}\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{x-z}{\lvert x-z\rvert}u\right)\rho_{G}(z)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq(\rho_{G}\star\phi_{2})(x)

where
\phi_{2}
is given by (
5.9
).
∎

Corollary 5.4

(Positivity)
.

The density
\rho_{G}
is strictly positive, i.e.

\rho_{G}(x)>0\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof of
5.4
.

Since
G
is a non-zero steady state, we have that
\rho_{G}
is not identically zero. But in this case, for all possible
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\rho_{G}(x)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{G}(z)\phi_{1}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z>0

where in the last inequality we used that
\phi_{1}>0
.
∎

5.1.
Lower bound for the density
\rho_{G}

Lemma 5.5

(Lower bound)
.

Let
G
be the stationary solution to (
1.3
), then the density
\rho_{G}
satisfies

(5.10)

\rho_{G}(x)\gtrsim\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}

where
\nu=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}(1+\chi)^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}
.

Proof of
5.5
.

Fix
A>0
such that the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
A
has volume 1. From the inequalities of
5.3
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{G}(z)\phi_{1}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\lvert z\rvert\leq A}\rho_{G}(z)\phi_{1}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\left(\min_{\lvert z\rvert\leq A}\rho_{G}(z)\right)\int_{\lvert z\rvert\leq A}\phi_{1}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z

The minimum is strictly positive since it is taken over a compact set and
\rho_{G}
is strictly positive. Concerning the other factor, notice that
\phi_{1}
is radially decreasing, so for
\lvert x\rvert\geq A
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\lvert z\rvert\leq A}\phi_{1}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\lvert z\rvert\leq A}\phi_{1}\left(x+A\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)\,\mathrm{d}z\geq\phi_{1}\left(x+A\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)

Using the definition of
\phi_{1}
and (
5.5
) we conclude

\phi_{1}\left(x+A\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}\right)\gtrsim\lfloor\lvert x\rvert+A\rceil^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu(\lvert x\rvert+A)^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\sim\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}.

as
\lvert x\rvert\to\infty
. Thus we have

\rho_{G}(x)\gtrsim\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}

for
\lvert x\rvert\geq A
, that gives the statement since
\rho_{G}
is bounded on
B_{A}
.
∎

5.2.
Upper bound for the density
\rho_{G}

From the main theorem we have that for
m=e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}+e^{b\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}}
the stationary solution
G
belongs to
\mathsf{L}^{1}(m)
, as a consequence

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{G}(x)e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(x,v)(e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}+e^{b\lvert v\rvert^{\gamma}})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v<\infty.

Thus we have
\rho_{G}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}}})
with
\nu>0
small enough. The aim of this section is to turn this bound into an
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
bound, in the sense that we want to prove
\rho_{G}\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}})
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G
be the stationary solution to (
1.3
), then the density
\rho_{G}
satisfies

(5.11)

\rho_{G}(x)\lesssim e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}

where
\nu>0
is small enough.

Proof of
5.6
.

We show the case
d\geq 2
, as the case
d=1
is very similar. Let
\nu<\overline{\nu}=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}(1-\chi)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}}
small enough such that
\rho_{G}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}}})
. Using
5.3
and the inequalities (
5.6
) and (
5.5
) we have

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(\rho_{G}\star\phi_{2})(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\leq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-\overline{\nu}\lfloor x-z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\geq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}}e^{-\overline{\nu}\lfloor x-z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\leq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-\nu\lfloor x-z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\geq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}}e^{-\nu\lfloor x-z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\leq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\geq\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{G}(z)}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}}e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\lVert\rho_{G}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}\left(e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\right)}\int_{\lvert x-z\rvert\leq\epsilon}\frac{1}{\lvert x-z\rvert^{d-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z+\frac{e^{-\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}}{\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}}\lVert\rho_{G}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}\left(e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\right)}

Then we have

\displaystyle\lVert\rho_{G}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}\left(e^{\nu\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\right)}\lesssim\epsilon\lVert\rho_{G}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}\left(e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\right)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}}\lVert\rho_{G}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}\left(e^{\nu\lfloor z\rceil^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}\right)}

Then choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small allows us to conclude.
∎

6.
Refined bounds and a Poincaré inequality under

6.1.
Upper and lower bounds for

In this section we show some more precise

the latter will show the equivalence of the weights

and

.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume

and let

. Then

\displaystyle H_{G}(x)\asymp\left(\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell}+\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{k}{1+\gamma}}\right)\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lvert x\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}

In particular we have

\displaystyle\rho_{G}(x)

\displaystyle\asymp\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lvert x\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}},

\displaystyle P_{G}^{(k)}(x)

\displaystyle\asymp\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{k}{1+\gamma}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\left(d-\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-\nu\lvert x\rvert^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}}

for

.

Proof of

.

\#
Step 1: Rewriting of

) rewrites as

Let us denote
\mathsf{e}_{z}=\tfrac{z}{\lvert z\rvert}
for any

. By changing the variables

and

, we find

\displaystyle H_{G}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(v)e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\Lambda(x\cdot v-\lvert v\rvert^{2}r)dr}\mathcal{M}(v)\theta_{G}(x-vs)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h\left(\frac{z}{s}\right)e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\Lambda(x\cdot\frac{z}{s}-\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{s^{2}}r)dr}\mathcal{M}\left(\frac{z}{s}\right)\theta_{G}(x-z)\frac{1}{s^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(\mathsf{e}_{z})\frac{u^{k+d-2}}{\lvert z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-\int_{0}^{\frac{\lvert z\rvert}{u}}\Lambda(x\cdot\mathsf{e}_{z}-ur)dr}\mathcal{M}\left(\mathsf{e}_{z}u\right)\theta_{G}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h(\mathsf{e}_{z})\frac{u^{k+d-2}}{\lvert z\rvert^{d-1}}e^{-\frac{u^{\gamma}}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{\lvert z\rvert}\Lambda(x\cdot\mathsf{e}_{z}-r)dr}\theta_{G}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z

Take

fixed and change variables

and

\bar{u}=0
we have

\int_{B_{R}}u\,\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{B_{R}^{c}}u\,\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{1,R}\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{R}}u\,\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\varrho_{R}u\,\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}(\varrho_{R}-1)u\,\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\rho_{G}^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}\,P_{G}^{-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where the last step is just a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\lvert\varrho_{R}-1\rvert\leq 1
. Let

\varepsilon(R)=\frac{\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}}{\int_{B_{2R}}\rho_{G}\,\mathrm{d}x}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\rho_{G}^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}\,P_{G}^{-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

We know that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\varepsilon(R)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\varepsilon(R)\lfloor 2R\rceil^{\ell}

\displaystyle=0

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert{\nabla_{x}}u_{1,R}\rvert^{2}P_{G}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u_{1,R}\rvert^{2}\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon(R)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.8)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u_{1,R}\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon(R)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

Finally we can use (
6.6
), (
6.7
) and (
6.2
) to find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert{\nabla_{x}}u\rvert^{2}P_{G}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert{\nabla_{x}}u_{1,R}\rvert^{2}P_{G}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert{\nabla_{x}}u_{2,R}\rvert^{2}P_{G}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\kappa}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\,\mathsf{q}(R)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u_{2,R}\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u_{1,R}\rvert^{2}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x+

\displaystyle-\varepsilon(R)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}\rho_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\kappa}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\min\{\mathsf{q}(R),\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}\}-\varepsilon(R)-\frac{\kappa}{R^{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}}P_{G}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

and
\min\{\mathsf{q}(R),\lfloor 2R\rceil^{-\ell}\}-\varepsilon(R)-\frac{\kappa}{R^{2}}
is positive for
R>0
large enough.

∎

7.
\mathsf{L}^{2}
bounds via a Dolbeault - Mouhot - Schmeiser method

In this section we prove
1.4
. Let us define the entropy functional
\mathsf{H}\colon\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})\to\mathbb{R}
as

(7.1)

\mathsf{H}[f]=\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}+\varepsilon\langle m_{f}(x),\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\rho_{f}\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}

where
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\rho_{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle m_{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\,f(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v

and
B
is the elliptic operator defined by

(7.2)

Bu:=\rho_{G}\,u-\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell}\dive_{x}(V_{G}\nabla_{x}{u}).

The diffusivity matrix
V_{G}
is defined by

V_{G}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\otimes v\,G(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v

and the number
\ell>0
is defined in
1.3
.

In most applications of the Dolbeault-Mouhot-Schmeiser method, the quantities
\rho_{G}
,
P_{G}
,
P_{G}^{(4)}
generate weighted norms that are all equivalent to one another, here that is not the case, as shown in
6.1
. To compensate for this imbalance, we added the polynomial factor
\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell}
to our elliptic operator
B
. With this choice, we have been able to get both a perturbation term which is bounded by
\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}^{2}
and an estimate on the dissipation of entropy. The main motivation of this correction is that, due to
6.1
,

\frac{P_{G}^{(4)}\rho_{G}}{P_{G}^{2}}\asymp\lfloor\cdot\rceil^{\ell}

and it turns out that this is actually the loss of weight we will recover in the dissipation estimate. Along the proof we repeatedly use the bounds in
6.1
in order to switch between
\rho_{G},P_{G},P^{(}4)_{G}
appearing in the weights in various norms.

First of all we want to prove that
\mathsf{H}
is a bounded perturbation of
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}
, namely the scalar product in (
7.1
) can be bounded by
\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}
. By
6.2
, the vector norm with weight
V_{G}
is equivalent to the vector norm with weight
P_{G}
. With this definition we can bound
m_{f}
by the microscopic component as follows

\displaystyle\lVert m_{f}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(P_{G}^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\,f\,\mathrm{d}v\right)^{2}\,P_{G}^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\,(1-\Pi)f\,\mathrm{d}v\right)^{2}\,P_{G}^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert v\rvert^{2}G\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert(1-\Pi)f\rvert^{2}\,G^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)P_{G}^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}.

Moreover,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\lVert m_{f}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(V_{G}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert m_{f}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(P_{G}^{-1})}\leq\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})},

\displaystyle\lVert\rho_{f}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\rho_{G}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Pi f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathsf{H}[\cdot]
be defined as in (
7.1
). If
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, then

(7.4)

\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}\lesssim\mathsf{H}[f]\lesssim\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}

for any
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})
.

Proof of
7.1
.

Let
g=B^{-1}\rho_{f}
, that is
g
is the solution to the elliptic equation

\rho_{G}g-\lfloor x\rceil^{\ell}\dive_{x}(V_{G}\nabla_{x}{g})=\rho_{f}.

Testing with
g\,\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}
and integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\lVert g\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(\rho_{G}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}\right)}+\lVert{\nabla_{x}}g\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(V_{G}\right)}

\displaystyle=\langle g,\,\rho_{f}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert g\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(\rho_{G}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}\right)}+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\rho_{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(\rho_{G}^{-1}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}\right)}.

Dropping the first term we have

\lVert{\nabla_{x}}g\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(V_{G}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho_{f}\right\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(\rho_{G}^{-1}\lfloor x\rceil^{-\ell}\right)}\lesssim\lVert\Pi f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}.

Using (
7.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\langle m_{f}(x),\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\rho_{f}\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert m_{f}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(V_{G}^{-1}\right)}\lVert{\nabla_{x}}g\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}\left(V_{G}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}\lVert\Pi f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}.

Therefore, choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we prove (
7.4
).
∎

Suppose now that
f
is a solution to the run and tumble equation (
1.3
) with initial datum
f_{0}
such that
\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v=0
. We need to determine the dissipation of entropy

-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathsf{H}[f]=\mathsf{D}[f],

hence, we have to write the evolution equation for the corresponding
\rho_{f}
and

. On the one hand, integrating equation (

) with respect to

On the other hand, integrating equation (
1.3
) multiplied by

\partial_{t}m_{f}+{\nabla_{x}}\cdot V_{f}=-A_{f},

where

\displaystyle A_{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda(x,v)vf(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v

With
\Pi f=\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}G

\displaystyle V_{\Pi f}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\otimes v\,\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}G\,\mathrm{d}v=\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}V_{G},

\displaystyle A_{\Pi f}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda\,v\,\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}G\,\mathrm{d}v=\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}A_{G},

We finally conclude that the dissipation of entropy is

\displaystyle\,-2\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\rangle+\,\varepsilon\langle V_{G}\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\rho_{G}}\right),\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\rho_{f}\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}+\varepsilon\langle\nabla_{x}\cdot(V_{f}-V_{\Pi f}),\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\rho_{f}\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\langle A_{f}-A_{\Pi f},\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\rho_{f}\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}+\varepsilon\langle m_{f},\,\nabla_{x}\left(B^{-1}\left(\dive_{x}m_{f}\right)\right)\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}}

The advantage of this decomposition is that the matrix

. Indeed, the euclidean matrix norm of

.

Concerning

\displaystyle\lVert A_{f}-A_{\Pi f}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(P_{G}^{-1})}

(7.8)

\lVert A_{f}-A_{\Pi f}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(V_{G}^{-1})}\lesssim\lVert A_{f}-A_{\Pi f}\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(P_{G}^{-1})}\lesssim\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}.

Theorem 7.6

Assume that

\displaystyle\colon L^{p_{0}}(w_{0})\to L^{q_{0}}(\widetilde{w}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=w_{0}^{1-\theta}w_{1}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}

7.2.1.

\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(G^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)

Lemma 7.7
.

Let

be the semigroup associated to the run and tumble equation and

(7.18)

\lVert S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(G^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(G^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}\leq 1

Proof of

.

Let

Then the function
\lVert G^{-1}f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}\,G-f_{0}

\displaystyle\lVert G^{-1}f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}\,G-S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}=S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)(\lVert G^{-1}f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}\,G-f_{0})\geq 0.

\lVert G^{-1}S_{\mathcal{L}}(t)f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}\leq\lVert G^{-1}f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
7.5

Consider the semigroup

Proof of
1.4

\displaystyle\lesssim-\left(\lVert(1-\Pi)f\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}+\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{-2\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}}\lVert\Pi f\rVert^{2/\eta}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(G^{-1})}\right)\lesssim-\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{-2\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}}\mathsf{H}[f]^{1/\eta}.

By applying the Gronwall Lemma we finally have

\mathsf{H}[f]\lesssim\left(\mathsf{H}[f_{0}]^{-\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}}+\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{-2\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}}\,t\right)^{-\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lfloor t\rceil^{\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}}}\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{2}=\frac{1}{\lfloor t\rceil^{\frac{k}{\ell}}}\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(m\,G^{-1})}^{2}.

∎

Localized Clique Bounds in Bounded-Degree and Bounded-Path Graphs
Rajat Adak, L. Sunil Chandran

Abstract
Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a family of graphs. A graph is said to be ℱ \mathcal{F} -free if it contains no member of ℱ \mathcal{F} . The generalized Turán number e ​ x ​ ( n , H , ℱ ) ex(n,H,\mathcal{F}) denotes the maximum number of copies of a graph H H in an n n -vertex ℱ \mathcal{F} -free graph, while the generalized edge Turán number m ​ e ​ x ​ ( m , H , ℱ ) mex(m,H,\mathcal{F}) denotes the maximum number of copies of H H in an m m -edge ℱ \mathcal{F} -free graph. It is well known that if a graph has maximum degree d d , then it is K 1 , d + 1 K_{1,d+1} -free. Wood [ 16 ] proved that e ​ x ​ ( n , K t , K 1 , d + 1 ) ≤ n d + 1 ​ ( d + 1 t ) ex(n,K_{t},K_{1,d+1})\leq\frac{n}{d+1}\binom{d+1}{t} . More recently, Chakraborty and Chen [ 7 ] established analogous bounds for graphs with bounded maximum path length: m ​ e ​ x ​ ( m , K t , P r + 1 ) ≤ m ( r 2 ) ​ ( r t ) mex(m,K_{t},P_{r+1})\leq\frac{m}{\binom{r}{2}}\binom{r}{t} . In this paper, we improve these bounds using the localization technique, based on suitably defined local parameters. Furthermore, we characterize the extremal graphs attaining these improved bounds.

Introduction

Extremal graph theory, initiated by Turán, studies the maximum number of edges or subgraphs that a graph can contain while avoiding a prescribed forbidden family. The generalized Turán numbers
ex(n,H,\mathcal{F})
and
mex(m,H,\mathcal{F})
extend this framework by counting the maximum number of copies of a fixed graph
H
in
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs of order
n
or size
m
, respectively. These parameters have been studied extensively for natural families such as paths, cycles, and stars.

Notation

Let

denote the number of subgraphs of

isomorphic to

.

Theorem 1.1

(Wood
[
16
]

Let

and

N(G,K_{t})\leq ex(n,K_{t},K_{1,d+1})\leq\frac{n}{d+1}{d+1\choose t}=\frac{n}{t}{d\choose t-1}

If
t=1
, we always get equality; otherwise, equality holds if and only if
G
is a disjoint union of copies of
K_{d+1}
.

Corollary 1

(Wood
[
16
]
)

Let
t\geq 1
and
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
\Delta(G)=d
as the maximum vertex degree, then

N(G,K_{\geq 1})\leq\frac{n}{d+1}(2^{d+1}-1)

Theorem 1.2

(Chakraborti-Chen
[
7
]
)
Let

and

-free graph on

N(G,K_{t})\leq mex(m,K_{t},P_{r+1})\leq\frac{m}{{r\choose 2}}{r\choose t}

If

1.1
Localization

A classical lower bound for the independence number,
\alpha(G)
, of a graph
G
is given by
\alpha(G)\geq\frac{n}{\Delta+1}
, where
n
is the number of vertices and
\Delta
is the maximum degree. This result was generalized by Caro
[
6
]
and Wei
[
15
]
to a local form:
\alpha(G)\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}
, where
d(v)
is the degree of vertex
v
. This generalization shifts the bounds’ dependency from a global graph parameter,
\Delta
, to a local vertex parameter,
d(v)
. This raises the question of whether we can apply this same idea of using local parameters to get better results for other graph properties.

Bradač
[
5
]
and Malec–Tompkins
[
13
]
recently introduced the idea of
localization
to strengthen and generalize classical extremal bounds. Classical results typically rely on global parameters, such as the longest path or the circumference of a graph. Localization instead assigns local weights to edges (or vertices), thereby refining global constraints into locally defined ones. For example, one may assign to each edge
e
the size of the largest clique or the length of the longest path containing
e
.

This framework has led to localized versions of several classical theorems. Bradač
[
5
]
and Malec-Tompkins
[
13
]
introduced a localization of Turán’s theorem
[
14
]
. In
[
13
,
17
]
, the localization approach was further applied to generalize the Erdős–Gallai theorems
[
8
]
for paths and cycles. Kirsch and Nir
[
10
]
established a localized version of Zykov’s generalization of Turán’s theorem, which was subsequently generalized in
[
4
]
, which provided a localization for the Graph Maclaurin Inequalities
[
9
]
. Moreover, the localization framework has also been extended to hypergraphs
[
17
]
and to spectral extremal bounds
[
11
]
.

While edge-based localization often produces distribution-type inequalities reminiscent of the LYM or Kraft inequalities, a more direct alternative was proposed in
[
1
]
, where localization is applied to vertices rather than edges.

Adak and Chandran
[
1
]
introduced the concept of
vertex-based localization
. They provided a vertex-based localization of the Erdős–Gallai theorems for paths and cycles. These results yield a more natural and intuitive strengthening of the classical bounds compared to the edge-based approaches of
[
13
,
17
]
. In subsequent work
[
3
]
, the same authors established a vertex-based localization of a popular form of Turán’s theorem, and more recently they extended this framework to generalized Erdős–Gallai theorems due to Luo
[
12
]
, in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.1

Let

N(G,K_{t})\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{{d(v)+1\choose t}}{d(v)+1}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{v\in V(G)}{d(v)\choose t-1}

Let

Corollary 2

Let

N(G,K_{t\geq 1})\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\sum_{t=1}^{d(v)+1}\frac{{d(v)+1\choose t}}{d(v)+1}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{2^{d(v)+1}-1}{d(v)+1}

Theorem 2.2

Let

N(G,K_{t})\leq\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{{p(e)+1\choose t}}{{p(e)+1\choose 2}}=\frac{1}{{t\choose 2}}\sum_{e\in E(G)}{p(e)-1\choose t-2}

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we introduced and established localized versions of clique bounds for graphs with bounded maximum degree and bounded path length. By employing suitably defined local parameters, these bounds strengthen the classical results of Wood
[
16
]
and Chakraborty-Chen
[
7
]
. We also characterized the extremal graphs attaining these bounds.

Definition 1

A connected graph is called a
block graph
if every block in it is a clique. A graph is called a
block forest
if each of its components is a block graph.

In the same work, Chakraborti and Chen
[
7
]
also established a bound on the generalized edge Turán number for graphs without long cycles:

Theorem 4.1

(Chakraborti-Chen
[
7
]
)

Let
t\geq 2
and let

m

N(G,K_{t})\leq mex(m,K_{t},C_{\geq r+1})\leq\frac{m}{{r\choose 2}}{r\choose t}.

If
t=2
, equality always holds; otherwise, equality holds if and only if
G
is a block forest whose blocks are
K_{r}
, together with any number of isolated vertices.

Notation

For a graph

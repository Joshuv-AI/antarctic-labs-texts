Probing new hadronic forces with heavy exotic atoms
Hongkai Liu, Ben Ohayon, Omer Shtaif, Yotam Soreq

Abstract
We explore the potential of precision spectroscopy of heavy exotic atoms where electrons are substituted by negative hadrons to detect new force carriers with hadronic couplings. The selected transitions are unaffected by nuclear contact terms, thus enabling highly accurate calculations using bound-state QED, provided that the nuclear polarization is under control. Alternatively, we demonstrate that the dipole polarizability, a fundamental property of nuclei, can be extracted from the spectroscopy of exotic atoms in a novel way by combining two transitions while maintaining high sensitivity to new physics. Based on existing data, we extracted world-leading bounds on mediator masses ranging from 0.1 ​ MeV 0.1\,\,\mathrm{MeV} to 10 ​ MeV 10\,\,\mathrm{MeV} for two benchmark models and show that forthcoming experiments could enhance the sensitivity to new physics by two orders of magnitude.

Contributions of nuclear polarization and new physics
.
We consider transitions between circular states in nuclei bound to negatively charged hadrons.
To increase NP sensitivity,
n
is chosen to be as small as possible but large enough so that the effects of QCD are negligible at the relevant accuracy level, given in Table
1
; see also

Consequently, we parameterize the theoretical prediction of the
n
level as

\displaystyle E_{n}^{{\rm th}}=\underbrace{E_{n}^{\rm SM-NPol}+E_{n}^{\rm NPol}}_{E_{n}^{\rm SM}}+E_{n}^{X},

(1)

where
E_{n}^{{\rm SM-NPol}}
is the contribution of the SM excluding the NPol,
E_{n}^{{\rm NPol}}
is the sum of the contributions of the polarizations of the nucleus and the orbiting hadron, and
E_{n}^{X}
is the contribution of NP due to the exchange of an
X

Hereafter, wherever
n
is written alone, the angular momentum number is implied to be

Due to the separation of the NPol effect, we assume that the uncertainty in
E_{n}^{\rm SM-NPol}
is either already negligible compared to the precision goals of ongoing experiments
[
20
]
, or that it would be reduced hand in hand with the experimental improvements
[
56
]

For the states considered, the orbiting particle velocity is roughly
Z\alpha/n\ll 1
, so the leading-order contribution to the energy levels, given in Table
1
, is of the Coulomb type
E^{\rm SM,LO}_{n}=-(Z\alpha/n)^{2}\mu/2
, where
\mu
is the reduced mass.
In the static (Born-Oppenheimer) approximation, the energy shift associated with the dipole contribution of the electric polarizability is
[

,
21
]

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\rm NPol}=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\alpha_{E}^{N}+Z^{2}\alpha_{E}^{H}\right)\left\langle r^{-4}\right\rangle_{n}\equiv\alpha_{E}^{{\rm tot}}h_{n}^{\rm NPol}\,,

(2)

where
\alpha_{E}^{\rm tot}\equiv\alpha_{E}^{N}+Z^{2}\alpha_{E}^{{H}}
is the total polarizability, comprising that of the nucleus
\alpha_{E}^{N}
, and the orbiting hadron
\alpha_{E}^{H}
.
For
H=\left\{\bar{p},\pi^{-},K^{-}\right\}
,
\alpha_{E}^{H}=\left(12.0\pm 0.5,2.0\pm 0.9,<200\right)\times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{fm}^{3}

[
75

76
,
77
]
, respectively.

\left\langle r^{-4}\right\rangle_{n}
is the expectation value of
r^{-4}
for a state with principal quantum number
n

For circular states, it returns
h_{n}^{\rm NPol}=-8\mu^{4}Z^{4}\alpha^{5}\left(2n-4\right)!/(n^{4}\left(2n\right)!)

To assess the order of magnitude of
\alpha_{E}^{N}
, we adopt the results of a recent global analysis of photoabsorption cross-section measurements, which returns
[
78
]

\displaystyle\alpha_{E}^{N}\approx 8\frac{\left(\frac{A}{132}\right)^{2}}{\left(\frac{A}{132}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}-0.31}{\rm fm}^{3}\,,

(3)

where

The spin-independent component of new interactions manifests as an effective Yukawa potential
[
82
]

\displaystyle V_{X}\left(\boldsymbol{r}\right)=(-1)^{s}\frac{g_{N}^{X}g_{H}^{X}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-m_{X}r}}{r}\,,

(4)

where
X
denotes the new boson with spin

g_{H}^{X}\,(g_{N}^{X})
represents the coupling to the hadron (nucleus).
In a nonrelativistic approximation, the first-order perturbation of this potential to the energies of circular states is

\displaystyle E_{n}^{X}=(-1)^{s}\frac{g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{m_{X}r_{n}}{2n}\right)^{2n}}\equiv g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}h_{n}^{X},

(5)

where
r_{n}\equiv n^{2}/(Z\alpha\mu)
represents the decoupling radius the distance beyond which the sensitivity to NP decreases.
Note that
r_{n}^{2}

\left<r^{2}\right>_{n}

\bar{p}\,\text{Pb}

This highlights the importance of x-ray spectroscopy in investigating short-range interactions.

Probing new physics
.
The strongest radiative transitions in the cascade of the exotic particle are those between two subsequent circular states
[
24
]
, so that the transition energies are
E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}
, where for any quantity
Y
we define
Y_{n,n-1}\equiv Y_{n}-Y_{n-1}
.

When the NPol uncertainty,
\sigma_{E_{n,n-1}^{\rm NPol}}
, is negligible compared to the experimental uncertainty,
\sigma_{E_{n,n-1}^{\rm exp}}
, we can use a single transition, preferably to the lowest
n
not affected by hadronic interactions, to probe NP.
Assuming an agreement between the experiment and the prediction of SM, the upper bound of the projected 95% confidence level is

\displaystyle|E^{X}_{n,n-1}|=|E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}-E^{\rm SM}_{n,n-1}|<2\,\sigma_{E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}}\,.

(6)

Combining Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
6
) returns

\displaystyle[g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}]_{\rm 1T}<\frac{2\,\sigma_{E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}}}{|h_{n,n-1}^{X}|}\xrightarrow[m_{X}r_{n}\to 0]{}4\pi Z\alpha R_{\sigma}\,,

(7)

where
R_{\sigma}\equiv\sigma_{E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}}/E^{\rm exp}_{n,n-1}
.
From Eq. (
7
), we learn that the sensitivity is flat up to the mass of
m_{X}\sim 1/r_{n}
, and then quickly decouples as
m_{X}^{2n}
.
We denote this case as
single transition
, 1T.

The second case is when
\sigma_{E_{n,n-1}^{\rm exp}}<\sigma_{E_{n,n-1}^{\rm NPol}}
.
As the uncertainty in
\alpha^{N}_{E}
is expected to be much larger than the magnitude of the corrections to Eq. (
2
), we can simultaneously determine
\alpha^{N}_{E}
and probe NP by considering two transitions measured for the same system.
We combine Eqs. (
1
), (
2
) and (
5
) and obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n-1}\equiv

\displaystyle E_{n,n-1}^{\rm exp}-E_{n,n-1}^{\rm SM-NPol}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}h_{n,n-1}^{X}+\alpha_{E}^{\rm tot}h_{n,n-1}^{{\rm{NPol}}}\,,

(8)

where
g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}
and
\alpha_{E}^{\rm tot}
are the two unknowns.
Next, assuming that
\Delta_{n,n-1}
and
\Delta_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}
were measured, we can solve for
g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}
and
\alpha_{E}^{\rm tot}
and get

\displaystyle[g_{H}^{X}g_{N}^{X}]_{{\rm 2T}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{n,n-1}h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{{\rm NPol}}-\Delta_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}h_{n,n-1}^{{\rm NPol}}}{h_{n,n-1}^{X}h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{{\rm NPol}}-h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{X}h_{n,n-1}^{{\rm NPol}}}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle[\alpha_{E}^{{\rm tot}}]_{{\rm 2T}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}h_{n,n-1}^{X}-\Delta_{n,n-1}h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{X}}{h_{n,n-1}^{X}h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{{\rm NPol}}-h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{X}h_{n,n-1}^{{\rm NPol}}}\,.

(10)

Thus, by measuring two transitions and combining with theory, we can determine both NPol and NP.
For every pair of transitions, there is a specific
m_{X}
for which
h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{X}h_{n,n-1}^{\rm NPol}=h_{n,n-1}^{X}h_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}^{\rm NPol}
, and the sensitivity is lost.
The sensitivity to
g_{H}^{X}g^{X}_{N}
is estimated using error propagation of Eq. (
9

For simplicity, we assume that the uncertainties on
\Delta_{n,n-1}
and
\Delta_{n^{\prime},n^{\prime}-1}
are not correlated and are of the same fraction of the energies.
This case is denoted as
dual transition
, 2T.

For heavy nuclei, a non negligible contribution from the quadrupole electric polarizability is possible
[
61
]
.
Its contribution to the potential scales as
r^{-6}
, and the relative effect compared to the dipole polarizability is
(r_{A}/r_{n})^{2}
, where
r_{A}\approx 1.2A^{1/3}
fm is the RMS nuclear charge radius
[
61
]
.
It is expected to be small for our chosen transitions and nuclei but can still be systematically fitted from the data using additional transitions.
Moreover, different isotopes could be probed to better understand this effect.

Application to benchmark models
.
The above bounds can be cast into specific BSM models; here are the results for two benchmark models.
The first is the
uds
-scalar from Ref.
[
72
]
and the second is the
B
-model (where
B
stands for a gauged baryon number) assuming predominantly invisible decays.
In the following, we show that exotic atoms can be the leading probes of these models for a mediator mass in the
100\,\,\mathrm{keV}
to
10\,\,\mathrm{MeV}
range.
Note that the Higgs-mixing model is severely constrained by
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}+\textrm{inv.}

[
106
,
107
]
.

In the
uds
-scalar model, a new CP-even scalar,
\phi
, with mass
m_{\phi}
, exclusively interacts with the
u
,
d
, and
s
quarks and decays invisibly.
The couplings are proportional to the quark masses and are suppressed by the cutoff scale
f_{\phi}
.
As shown in Ref.
[
72
]
, the stringent bounds from
K\to\pi\phi

[
106
,
108
]
are evaded to leading-order in the chiral expansion and thus relaxed, unlike in the case of scalar Higgs mixing.
The effective
\phi
-quarks interaction is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{\phi}=\frac{\phi}{f_{\phi}}\sum_{q=u,d,s}m_{q}\bar{q}q\,,

(11)

without coupling to other SM particles.
Following e.g.
[
109
,
110
]
, we match
\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{\phi}
to the hadron level

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{\rm int,had}\subset\phi\big(

\displaystyle g_{p}^{\phi}\bar{p}p+g_{n}^{\phi}\bar{n}n

\displaystyle+2m_{\pi}g_{\pi}^{\phi}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}+2m_{K}g_{K}^{\phi}K^{+}K^{-}\big),

(12)

with
g^{\phi}_{\pi(K)}=-\frac{m_{\pi(K)}}{2f_{\phi}}
,
g^{\phi}_{p(n)}=\frac{m_{p(n)}}{f_{\phi}}\sum_{q}f^{p(n)}_{q}
and
f^{p(n)}_{u}=0.018(0.016)
,
f^{p(n)}_{d}=0.034(0.038)
, and
f^{p(n)}_{s}=0.044(0.044)

[
109
]
.
The interaction with nuclei is given by
g_{N}^{X}\approx(A-Z)g_{n}^{X}+Zg_{p}^{X}
.

The bounds on the
uds
-model are plotted in the left panel of Fig.
3
in the
m_{\phi}-f^{-1}_{\phi}\,(g_{p}^{\phi})
plane, where we projected the bounds of Fig.
2
.
The approximated NLO bounds from rare kaon decay are
1/f_{\phi}\lesssim 0.8/\textrm{GeV}

[
72
]
and are not shown.
We note that the probed range of parameter space is mildly fine-tuned, since for a TeV cutoff, the 1-loop correction to
m_{\pi}
is
\delta m_{\phi}^{2}\sim\textrm{TeV}^{2}(m_{s}/f_{\phi})^{2}/16\pi^{2}
.
We learn that heavy exotic atoms already set the strongest bounds on the
uds
model for
0.1\lesssim m_{\phi}\lesssim 10\,\,\mathrm{MeV}
with the potential to improve by at least an order of magnitude.

The
B
-boson model introduces a new massive spin-1 gauge boson,
B
, with universal vector-like couplings to the quarks.
The SM charged leptons are coupled via 1-loop kinetic mixing and we assume
\mathrm{BR}(B\to{\rm inv.})\approx 1
.
This model was analyzed in several places, e.g.
[
111
,
112
]
.
The interactions with the SM fermions are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B}^{\rm int}=\frac{g_{B}}{3}B^{\mu}\sum_{q}\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}q-\frac{g_{B}e^{2}}{48\pi^{2}}B^{\mu}\sum_{\ell}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}\ell\,,

(13)

where
q\,(\ell)
is the SM quarks (charged leptons).
The
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{\rm int}
can be mapped to the hadron interactions

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B}^{\rm int,had}=g_{B}B_{\mu}\left(\bar{p}\gamma^{\mu}p+\bar{n}\gamma^{\mu}n\right)\,.

(14)

The baryon number is an anomalous symmetry, and thus requires additional degrees of freedom.
Such considerations lead to strong bounds from rare bottom and kaon decays
[
113
,
114
,
115
]
.
However, a similar model that is anomaly-free can be built by mixing the SM fermions with heavy vector-like fermions as in e.g.
[
116
,
117
]
.

We plot our new
\bar{p}\,
Ne and
\bar{p}\,
Pb bounds and the projections for the
B
model in the right panel of Fig.
3
.
We compare our result to the existing bounds from
\bar{p}^{4}
He, NA64
[
118
]
and to rescaling of the
B-L
bound from
[
17
]
, denoted as CODATA.
The last two are based on a loop-induced
B
-electron coupling that depends on the model’s UV details and can be adjusted accordingly.
We learn that
\bar{p}
Ne and
\bar{p}
Pb are already the robust leading probes of this model and that future measurements have the potential to increase sensitivity by an order of magnitude.

Conclusions
.
We explored the potential of precision spectroscopy of heavy exotic atoms in which electrons are replaced by
\bar{p}
,
\pi^{-}
or
K^{-}
, to probe new force carriers with hadronic couplings.
We have considered transitions that are clean from short-range hadronic effects, thus, the SM contribution can be predicted to high accuracy.
However, nuclear polarizability can still have sizable effects on these transition lines.
Therefore, we propose to use two lines and simultaneously solve for new-physics and polarizability effects.
Based on the available data in
\bar{p}\,
Ne,
\bar{p}\,
Pb and
\pi^{-}\,
N, we extract new world-leading bounds on two benchmark models and derive projections for future measurement for several representative systems.
For mediator masses of up to
10\,\,\mathrm{MeV}
, future experiments have the potential to improve the sensitivity by up to two orders of magnitude.
In addition, while this work considered only spin-independent interactions, spin-dependent interactions can be probed in a future work.

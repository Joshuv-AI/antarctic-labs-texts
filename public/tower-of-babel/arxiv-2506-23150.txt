AlignCVC: Aligning Cross-View Consistency for Single-Image-to-3D Generation
Xinyue Liang, Zhiyuan Ma, Lingchen Sun, Yanjun Guo, Lei Zhang

Abstract
Single-image-to-3D models typically follow a sequential generation and reconstruction workflow. However, intermediate multi-view images synthesized by pre-trained generation models often lack cross-view consistency (CVC), significantly degrading 3D reconstruction performance. While recent methods attempt to refine CVC by feeding reconstruction results back into the multi-view generator, these approaches struggle with noisy and unstable reconstruction outputs that limit effective CVC improvement. We introduce AlignCVC, a novel framework that fundamentally re-frames single-image-to-3D generation through distribution alignment rather than relying on strict regression losses. Our key insight is to align both generated and reconstructed multi-view distributions toward the ground-truth multi-view distribution, establishing a principled foundation for improved CVC. Observing that generated images exhibit weak CVC while reconstructed images display strong CVC due to explicit rendering, we propose a soft-hard alignment strategy with distinct objectives for generation and reconstruction models. This approach not only enhances generation quality but also dramatically accelerates inference to as few as 4 steps. As a plug-and-play paradigm, our method, namely AlignCVC, seamlessly integrates various multi-view generation models with 3D reconstruction models. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of AlignCVC for single-image-to-3D generation. Project page: https://liangsanzhu.github.io/aligncvc.github.io/ .

3
Method

In this paper, we propose
AlignCVC
, a novel framework that enhances CVC by relaxing the training constraints from strict regression losses to distribution alignment, enabling the generation of 3D models through 3D-aware sampling from GT distributions. As shown in Fig.
4
, AlignCVC leverages a 3D-aware sampling framework to recursively bridge the generation and reconstruction processes. This framework performs a near-distribution switch between generation and reconstruction, effectively incorporating 3D priors and progressively refining the final 3D model.

Figure 3:

. For Ouroboros3D
wen2024ouroboros3d
, VideoMV
zuo2024videomv
, and Gen-3Diffusion
xue2024gen
, intermediate noise and cross-view inconsistencies propagate into the 3D reconstructions. VideoMV applies reconstruction feedback every 10 steps starting from the 20th step, so its results are shown after the first 20 steps. Our model, trained by integrating Wonder3D
long2024wonder3d
and GeoLRM
zhang2024geolrm
, achieves significantly better results with notable time efficiency. More comparisons can be found in Appendix
A.1
.

Specifically, the input image is first processed by an MVG student model, which adopts a soft-alignment strategy based on Score Distillation to generate intermediate multi-views aligned with the GT distribution (Section
3.2
). These multi-view images are then fed into a hard-aligned reconstruction model, where adversarial learning ensures that the renderings of the reconstructed 3D model are consistent with the GT distribution (Section
3.3
). This process leverages a soft-hard alignment approach, which bridges the gap between the implicit and relatively weak CVC of the MVG model and the explicit and stronger CVC of the reconstruction model. By combining the complementary strengths of both models, this approach achieves a more robust and consistent final 3D model.

3.1
Preliminaries and Motivation

MVG Model
. In this paper, we focus on diffusion-based MVG models. Specifically, the MVG takes a single view
\boldsymbol{x}_{c}
as the input condition and generates
N
views
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\pi_{1}},\boldsymbol{x}^{\pi_{2}},\dots,\boldsymbol{x}^{\pi_{N}}\}
corresponding to camera poses
\pi=\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{N}\}
by initializing from Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I})
and progressively denoising in
K
steps
t_{K}>t_{K-1}>\dots>t_{1}
. The denoising results at the last timestep
t_{1}
are taken as the final generation results
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{0}
.

Reconstruction Model
. Given the generated multi-views
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{0}
, the reconstruction model produces a 3D output, which can be further differentiably rendered as
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{0}
at camera poses
\pi
. This two-stage generation-reconstruction framework is illustrated in Fig.
2
(a). However, since the reconstruction model is trained only with GT multi-views that exhibit perfect CVC, it often collapses when processing the generated multi-views, resulting in outputs that are inconsistent with the input view
\boldsymbol{x}_{c}
.

3D-Aware Sampling
. To address the above issues, 3D-aware sampling refines the 3D model by reimplementing the MVG model. As illustrated in Fig.
2
(b), during progressive denoising at each timestep
t_{k}\in{t_{K-1},\dots,t_{1}}
, the renderings of the reconstructed 3D outputs
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k+1}
are re-noised at
t_{k}
and fed back into the MVG model as the current denoising objective. This approach leverages explicit CVC from the reconstruction results to guide the sampling process, thereby enhancing the CVC of the output 3D model.

Unlike existing 3D-aware sampling methods
wen2024ouroboros3d
;
zuo2024videomv
;
xue2024gen
;
xue2024human
;
tang2024cycle3d
, which produce low-quality intermediates as shown in Fig.
3
, we propose a more robust paradigm with a soft-hard alignment strategy. This strategy ensures consistent intermediates and incremental refinement, as demonstrated in both Fig.
1
and Fig.
3
. Our method aligns both the generation and reconstruction distributions with the GT distribution. Specifically, it ensures that the reconstructed multi-views align with the manageable MVG distribution, while the generated multi-views align with the generalizable domain of the reconstruction model. By switching between these closely related near distributions, a stable 3D-aware sampling loop is formed, enabling both models to perform effectively within their respective domains.

3.2
Soft-Aligned MVG

Figure 4:
The framework of AlignCVC.
During training, the multi-view generation (MVG) student model generates multi-view images
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
from input image
\boldsymbol{x}_{c}
at camera poses
\pi
. A pre-trained MVG teacher model aligns
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
with the GT distribution via a soft-alignment method. We then obtain the 3D model from the reconstruction model and adversarially supervise its renderings
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
to the GT distribution in a hard-aligned manner. In the inference phase, we reconstruct an intermediate 3D model with the generated multi-view images
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
at each timestep, where the renderings
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
are then re-noised for the next denoising timestep with 3D-aware sampling. This recursive sampling, repeated for 4 steps, produces the final 3D model.

As mentioned above, in each step
k\in\{K,\dots,1\}
, existing 3D-aware sampling methods often generate noisy multi-view predictions
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
, which are passed to the reconstruction model (see Fig.
3
). The reconstruction model subsequently renders
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
as feedback for the next denoising step. However, this recursive feedback mechanism propagates noise into subsequent generations, degrading the overall quality of the 3D reconstruction.
Consistent guidance on the denoising direction is critical for improving the CVC of generated multi-view images. However, the noise in intermediate predictions weakens the effectiveness of this guidance, hindering the MVG model’s ability to adapt to direction changes of the diffusion process. To address the former issue, we propose aligning
\{\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
with a broader GT-consistent distribution. This alignment allows the MVG model to handle reconstruction results
\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
more effectively while staying aligned with the GT distribution.

However, directly enforcing alignment with the GT distribution, such as through adversarial generative training, can impose strict constraints on individual views and potentially disrupt the overall CVC. This limitation arises because the MVG model inherently learns CVC implicitly within its neural network representations, making it difficult to achieve perfect alignment with the GT distribution’s ideal CVC. Moreover, applying adversarial learning independently to MVG often results in mode collapse, according to our experiment, further weakening the quality of the generated multi-views.

Inspired by the advances in Score Distillation
poole2022dreamfusion
;
wang2024prolificdreamer
;
yu2023text
;
ma2025scaledreamer
, we propose to distill our MVG model from a pre-trained MVG teacher aligned with the GT distribution, achieving a
soft alignment
between the MVG student distribution and the GT distribution. We use Score Distillation to align the
\{\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
generated by trainable MVG student with the
\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{0}
by the teacher. This results in higher-quality intermediate multi-views at all steps.

Among recent distillation methods

;
wang2024prolificdreamer
;
poole2022dreamfusion

yu2023text
;

\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}_{k,t}^{\pi}

\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}_{k}^{\pi}
generated by the MVG student diffused with Gaussian noise
\epsilon

While the generated multi-view images
\{\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
are aligned with the GT distribution in Section
3.2
, this alignment does not extend to
\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
, the multi-view image renderings from the reconstruction model, which follow a different distribution. This mismatch introduces interference between generation and reconstruction in 3D-aware sampling, degrading the quality of the final 3D outputs.
Supervising the reconstruction model with renderings of the single target 3D model fails to reduce this distributional gap, as it does not account for the inherent differences between the distributions of generated images and reconstruction results. To address this, a distribution-based training objective is necessary to ensure consistency across both stages.

Intuitively, Score Distillation can be applied to align the distribution of
\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
. However, since the rendering process used to project 3D models to multi-views explicitly adheres to CVC, it enables the adoption of a
hard alignment
approach. Specifically, adversarial generative learning can directly align the reconstruction results with the GT distribution derived from 3D asset renderings, providing a higher upper bound for distribution alignment.

We employ the following adversarial loss with a CLIP-based
radford2021learning
multi-view discriminator
D
to supervise the rendering of images
\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{GAN}}=\mathbb{E}_{X_{gt}}[\log D(X_{gt}^{\pi})]+\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{X}_{k}}[\log(1-D(\tilde{X}_{k}^{\pi}))],

(2)

where
X_{gt}^{\pi}
is the GT multi-views. We assist it with a reconstruction loss for better performance:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Recon}}=\|X_{gt}^{\pi}-{\tilde{X}_{k}^{\pi}}\|^{2}_{2}.

(3)

In addition, since the reconstruction process leverages multi-view images generated by the MVG as input, we preserve the gradient connection between the two models. Consequently, the above objectives not only guide the MVG student to generate
\{\hat{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
, enabling
\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\pi}_{k}\}^{K}_{k=1}
to achieve better alignment with the GT distribution, but also assist in the training of the reconstruction model through Score Distillation from the MVG. The implementation details can be found in Appendix
A.2
.

4
Experiments

4.1
Experimental Setup

Training Settings.

We train our AlignCVC framework on the Gobjaverse dataset
qiu2024richdreamer
, which comprises multi-view renderings of 280K 3D objects sourced from Objaverse
deitke2023objaverse
. The resolution of the final results is set to 512×512 pixels for both training and testing (more camera adaptation details can be found in Appendix
A.3
). Our proposed method is trained for 300,000 iterations on an H20 GPU, utilizing the Adan
xie2024adan
optimizer with a learning rate of
2\times 10^{-5}
. All computations are performed in 32-bit precision and implemented using the ThreeStudio
threestudio2023
framework.

Figure 6:
Ablation results on image-to-3D generation.

Testing Settings.

Following prior works, we evaluate our method using the GSO
downs2022google
dataset. Tests are conducted on four orthogonal azimuths, and the evaluation metrics include PSNR, SSIM
wang2004image
, LPIPS
zhang2018unreasonable
, CLIP Similarity
radford2021learning
, and FID
kynkaanniemi2022role
.
While these metrics primarily assess reconstruction quality against a fixed set of GT images, they inherently introduce a bias against models capable of generating novel yet plausible 3D-consistent content that deviates from the predefined GT. To address this limitation, we further evaluate the CVC of MVG outputs using the model introduced by DUSt3R
wang2024dust3r
, which predicts corresponding point maps between two adjacent images. The CVC score is computed as the average confidence of the top 100 matched points based on their confidence scores. More details of the CVC score can be found in Appendix
A.4
.

Baseline Generation and Reconstruction Models.

In selecting MVG models, we prioritize those that ensure precise control over camera parameters and generate high-fidelity multi-view images to meet the requirements of downstream tasks. We choose Wonder3D
long2024wonder3d
and SV3D
voleti2024sv3d
as MVG teachers due to their ability to handle adjustable camera parameters. Both models are fine-tuned on the Gobjaverse dataset for 300,000 iterations to improve elevation control and constrain the generated frames to four orthogonal views.
For reconstruction models, we adopt LGM
tang2025lgm
for its end-to-end capability in predicting 3DGS parameters from multi-views. Additionally, we include GeoLRM
zhang2024geolrm
and LaRa
chen2025lara
, which project 2D features into a 3D volume to construct the final 3D model.

Compared Methods
. We compare our method against recent open-source methods for generating 3D objects from a single image. These include 3D-aware sampling approaches such as Ouroboros3D
wen2024ouroboros3d
, VideoMV
zuo2024videomv
, and Gen-3Diffusion
xue2024gen
. We also compare with two-stage methods, including LGM combined with its MVG model ImageDream

5
Conclusion

We presented AlignCVC, a plug-and-play framework to enhance single-image-to-3D generation by jointly post-training multi-view generation (MVG) and reconstruction models. Our approach addressed distribution misalignment, which is the key bottleneck in 3D-aware sampling, by aligning generated and reconstructed multi-view distributions with GT distribution. Recognizing that cross-view consistency (CVC) manifested implicitly in MVG models but explicitly in reconstruction models, we implement a soft-hard alignment strategy: Score Distillation for MVG models (soft) and adversarial training for reconstruction models (hard). Our experiments showed that AlignCVC consistently improves performance across various combinations of MVG and reconstruction models while reducing 3D-aware sampling to 4 steps.

Limitation and future work
. Our AlignCVC relies on two auxiliary networks, the MVG teacher and discriminator, for distribution alignment, which increases GPU memory usage. In future work, we can improve the training efficiency for distribution alignment. In addition, we could enhance the interaction efficiency and output quality by adapting the internal architectures of MVG and reconstruction models to achieve better distribution alignment.

A.6
Alignment Strategy

In Section 3 of the main paper, we present our soft-hard alignment strategy for aligning the MVG and reconstruction models with the GT distribution. For MVG alignment, we employ Asynchronous Score Distillation (ASD)
[

A.6.1
Asynchronous Score Distillation

ASD leverages the observation that noise prediction at earlier timesteps is more accurate than later timesteps, as shown in Equation
4
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi,t,\epsilon}\left[\left\lVert\epsilon_{\phi}\left(x_{t+\Delta t},t+\Delta t,y^{\pi}\right)-\epsilon\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi,t,\epsilon}\left[\left\lVert\epsilon_{\phi}\left(x_{t},t,y^{\pi}\right)-\epsilon\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right],

where
t
is the timestep,
\Delta t
is a small time increment,
y
is the conditioning input,
\pi
is the camera pose, and
\epsilon_{\phi}
is the noise predicted by the diffusion model
\phi
.

In ASD, noise prediction error is reduced by aligning the noise predicted at the current timestep with that predicted at earlier timesteps during training by Equation
5
:

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ASD}}=\mathbb{E}_{t,\Delta t,\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\omega(t)\left(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t;\mathbf{y}^{\pi})-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t},t+\Delta t;\mathbf{y}^{\pi})\right)\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\theta}\right],

(5)

where
\theta
is the learnable parameter of a 3D representation. Thereby, enhancing the diffusion model’s alignment with the GT distribution.

In our work, we adopt a similar formulation in Section 3.1 to achieve precise alignment between our MVG student model and its MVG teacher model. We discuss how ASD enables effective soft alignment of the MVG model, creating a fine-tuning approach better suited to diffusion model training and implicit CVC characteristics of the MVG model during 3D-aware sampling generation.

A.6.2
Choice of Alignment Strategy

Our ablation studies in the main paper confirm that soft alignment is essential for aligning the MVG distribution with its implicit CVC characteristics. For this purpose, we consider three prominent Score Distillation approaches: Classifier Score Distillation (CSD)
[
53
]
, Variational Score Distillation (VSD)
[
41
]
, and Asynchronous Score Distillation (ASD)
[
25
]
.

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{CSD}}=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\omega(t)\left(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t;\mathbf{y}^{\pi})-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t)\right)\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\theta}\right].

(6)

We conduct experiments with CSD (see Equation
6
), as illustrated in Fig.
13
, revealing significant instability and vulnerability to mode collapse. Furthermore, VSD’s requirement for both a fixed MVG teacher and an additional MVG teacher for fine-tuning introduces prohibitive memory overhead, making it impractical for our framework. Consequently, we adopt ASD in our implementation due to its superior generation quality and stability. Since the MVG teacher and the GT exhibit overlapping parts in their distributions, aligning with the MVG teacher offers a pathway to implicitly approximate the CVC in the GT distribution.

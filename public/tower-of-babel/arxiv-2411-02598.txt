Abstract
We elaborate further on the one-loop effective action of the IKKT model on 3 + 1 3+1 dimensional covariant quantum spacetime in the presence of fuzzy extra dimensions. In particular, we describe the one-loop effective action in terms of a remarkable S ​ O ​ ( 1 , 9 ) SO(1,9) character, which allows to evaluate the pertinent traces over the internal modes explicitly. This also allows to estimate the higher-order contributions (in the internal flux ℱ 𝙸𝙹 \mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}} ) to the one-loop effective action in a systematic way. We show that all higher-order contributions are generally suppressed and UV finite, which justifies the previous treatment of the induced gravitational action. We also obtain explicit expressions for the effective Newton constant, and determine the dynamics of the Kaluza-Klein scale Δ 𝒦 \Delta_{\mathcal{K}} of the fuzzy extra dimensions 𝒦 \mathcal{K} .

6
Discussion

The main contribution of this paper is a suitable formalism to compute and interpret the one-loop effective action for the higher-spin theory, which is induced by the IKKT matrix model on the background brane
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}\times\mathcal{K}
. This one-loop action is UV-finite, thanks to maximal supersymmetry and the finiteness of the numbers of dof. per unit volume

20

20

This is quite different with standard higher-spin gravities, see e.g.
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
, where higher-spin symmetry plays the central role in softening possible UV divergence.
. Computing the effective action for the induced gravity sector, we establish a clear hierarchy between lowest-order and higher-order contributions in
(\mathcal{T}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}{}^{\alpha},\delta\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}})
, as discussed in Section
5
and Appendix
B
. The lowest-order contributions in
(\mathcal{T}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}{}^{\alpha},\delta\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}})
turn out to be dominant, which justify previous treatment at one loop, cf.
[
10
,
11
,
22
]
. This allows to compute the effective Newton constant or Planck mass

, which turns out to follow the same evolution as the effective KK mass (
2.42
) along the cosmic expansion. This is an important result, since it allows the local physics to be independent of the cosmic evolution.

Another important topic is the stabilization of the extra dimensions
\mathcal{K}
, or at least of its radius. Refining previous considerations, we are able to give some explicit formulas for the KK mass scale
m^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}
. This arises from a non-trivial minimum of the 1-loop effective action, which provides a UV mass scale and breaks conformal symmetry.
In this calculation, the role of the
\mathfrak{hs}

\Delta_{\mathtt{J}}

[
22

The non-trivial vacuum under consideration induces a non-vanishing

However,

\mathcal{M}^{1,3}

, cf.
[
20

. However, as noted above, that calculation should be taken with a grain of salt. In fact, we show in Appendix C how this may be avoided on modified backgrounds.

An important question which remains to be clarified in future work is whether all
\mathfrak{hs}
masses are in the IR regime with a large hierarchy

,

\mathcal{K}

Furthermore, our mechanism to stabilize
\mathcal{K}

\rho

\mathcal{K}

Other mechanisms for stabilizing
\mathcal{K}

can be evaluated

From the above master formula, we get for instance

Appendix C
On the
\mathfrak{hs}
scale
\Delta_{\mathtt{J}}
and positive
\mathfrak{hs}
masses

This appendix shows that
m_{s}^{2}
can be positive for some suitable background. To set the stage, we consider the following modified background (cf.
[
18
]
)

\displaystyle T^{\dot{\mu}}=f(\tau)\underline{\mathtt{t}}^{\dot{\mu}}

(C.1)

in the semi-classical regime,
where
f(\tau)
is some function which preserves
SO(1,3)
. Then,

\displaystyle\{T^{\dot{\mu}},\{T_{\dot{\mu}},u^{\nu(s)}\}\}=s(s-1)\{T^{\dot{\mu}},u^{\nu}\}\{T_{\dot{\mu}},u^{\nu}\}u^{\nu(s-2)}+s\{T^{\dot{\mu}},\{T_{\dot{\mu}},u^{\nu}\}\}u^{\nu(s-1)}\,.

(C.2)

Since
f(\tau)
needs to preserve
SO(1,3)
, we can set

\displaystyle f(\tau)=f\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)=f(\cosh(\tau))\ .

(C.3)

With hindsight, we consider

\displaystyle f(\cosh(\tau))=\frac{1}{\cosh(\tau)}\,

(C.4)

and use
\cosh(\tau)\sim\sinh(\tau)
at late times.
Then, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\{T^{\dot{\mu}},T^{\nu}\}=-\frac{1}{R^{2}\cosh^{3}(\tau)}\epsilon^{\dot{\mu}\nu\sigma\rho}x_{\sigma}u_{\rho}\,.

(C.5)

Furthermore, one finds

\displaystyle\{T_{\mu},\{T^{\mu},u^{\nu}\}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{R^{2}\cosh^{4}(\tau)}u^{\nu}\,.

(C.6)

Putting this together gives

\displaystyle\{T_{\dot{\mu}},\{T^{\dot{\mu}},u^{\nu(s)}\}\}\approx-\frac{s(s+1)}{R^{2}\cosh^{4}(\tau)}u^{\nu(s)}\,

(C.7)

at late times.
Thus, on the background
T^{\dot{\mu}}=\frac{1}{\cosh(\tau)}\underline{\mathtt{t}}^{\dot{\mu}}
, all
\mathfrak{hs}
masses are positive,

\displaystyle m_{s}^{2}=\frac{s(s+1)}{R^{2}\cosh^{4}(\tau)}\geq 0\,.

(C.8)

As a result, the
\mathfrak{hs}
scale
\Delta_{\mathtt{J}}
is positive, and can be set as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathtt{J}}^{2}:=\frac{\mathtt{J}}{R^{2}\cosh^{4}(\tau)}\,>0\ .

(C.9)

However, such a time-dependent background entails a host of other issues (such as local normal coordinates etc.) which will be
addressed elsewhere. Then, the analysis in Section
4
for stabilizing
\mathcal{K}
needs some revision, but it is
now evident that negative
\mathfrak{hs}
masses
(such as
m_{s}^{2}=-\frac{s}{R^{2}}
for the
T^{\mu}\sim t^{\mu}

[
20
]
)
can and should be avoided by carefully choosing backgrounds that are fully consistent.

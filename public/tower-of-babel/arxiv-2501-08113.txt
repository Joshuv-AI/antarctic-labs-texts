Emission of W W bosons by electrons and decays of W W bosons in the early Universe Thanks: amalia.fodor97@e-uvt.ro, cosmin.crucean@e-uvt.ro
Amalia Dariana Fodor, Cosmin Crucean

Abstract
In this paper we study the transition rates for the decay of W ± W^{\pm} bosons into leptons in de Sitter spacetime. From the results obtained in the de Sitter geometry we recover the Minkowski limits of the transition rates. We also study the emission of W ± W^{\pm} bosons by leptons, a process which is possible only in the large expansion conditions of the early Universe. These results are obtainable due to perturbative methods which allow us to compute the transition amplitudes in the first order of perturbation theory. The resulting probabilities and rates depend on the particle masses and the Hubble parameter. In order to establish finite expressions for the probabilities and transition rates, we make use of dimensional regularization and the minimal substraction method.

2.3
Total probability and transition rate in the large expansion limit

In the early Universe the
W
bosons were generated from vacuum and also produced in emission processes by fermions, but they began to decay when the background energy became smaller than their rest energy. These phenomena happened in a very short time. One of the important applications of our general computations in de Sitter spacetime is to obtain the transition rates of the processes we study. This can be done in the limit of a large expansion factor
\omega\gg m_{e},\,\omega\gg M_{W}
, a limit which can be identified with the very early universe. Thus we can take the ratios between the particle masses and Hubble parameter to be zero throughout all our equations for the amplitude, that is:
m_{e}/\omega\rightarrow 0,M_{W}/\omega\rightarrow 0
.

This brings a simplification to the amplitude formula (
14
), in the case of transversal polarisation
\lambda=\pm 1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[W^{-}\rightarrow{e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{-g}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p}e^{-i\pi/4}e^{-i\pi/4}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma^{\prime}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,ze^{-ip\,^{\prime}z}H_{1/2}^{(2)}(pz)H_{1/2}^{(1)}(Pz)\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}),

(40)

where the order of the Hankel function corresponding to the electron of momentum
p
in (
14
) has become
1/2
, since
k=m_{e}/\omega=0
. The order of the Hankel function corresponding to the
W^{-}
of momentum
P
boson also goes to 1/2:

iK=i\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2},

in the limit when
M_{W}/\omega\rightarrow 0
. Since the ampltiude for logitudinal polarisation
\lambda=0
has an overall factor of
\omega/M_{W}
, as seen in the previous section, we can see that it diverges in this large expansion limit, and in consequence does not contribute to the transition rate we are computing here.

The amplitude can be split into factors as such:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[W^{-}\rightarrow{e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}=\mathcal{I}_{if}\mathcal{M}_{if}\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,),

(41)

where
\mathcal{I}_{if}
denotes the temporal integral:

\mathcal{I}_{if}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,e^{-ip\,^{\prime}z}H_{1/2}^{(2)}(pz)H_{1/2}^{(1)}(Pz),

(42)

while
\mathcal{M}_{if}
contains the rest of the factors in (
40
).

The temporal integral can be solved by transforming the Hankel functions into exponentials by using first formula (
161
) from
Appendix A
, and then formula (
160
) in reverse, which leads to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{if}=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{pP}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,e^{i(P-p^{\prime}-p)z}.

(43)

We can integrate over
z
afterwards:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{if}=\frac{-2i}{\pi\sqrt{pP}(p+p^{\prime}-P)}.

(44)

Following this procedure the amplitude reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{e^{-i\pi/2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)}{\sqrt{P}(p+p^{\prime}-P)}\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}),

(45)

where we have taken into account that the helicity of the antineutrino has to be
\sigma^{\prime}=1/2
, otherwise (
40
) would cancel out.

The transition rate has been defined in previous studies related to the large expansion limit in this de Sitter setup, and a complete discussion about this subject can be found in
[
58
]
. Here we give just the definition, which has been adapted to our case:

\displaystyle R_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime})}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{\sigma}|\mathcal{M}_{if}|^{2}|\mathcal{I}_{if}|\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{i\rightarrow f}|,

(46)

where
\mathcal{K}_{i\rightarrow f}
is the integrand of the temporal integral:

\mathcal{K}_{i\rightarrow f}=z\,e^{-ip^{\prime}z}H_{1/2}^{(2)}(pz)H_{1/2}^{(1)}(Pz),

(47)

while:

\mathcal{M}_{if}=\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\pi\sqrt{p}e^{-i\pi/2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{n}_{\mathcal{P}},\lambda)\eta_{1/2}(\vec{p^{\prime}}).

(48)

Next we solve the temporal limit in (
46
), keeping in mind that
z=-t_{c}=e^{-\omega t}/\omega
:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{i\rightarrow f}|

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{Pp}}\bigg|e^{\omega t}e^{-\omega t}e^{i(P-p-p^{\prime})\exp{(-\omega t})/\omega}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{Pp}},

(49)

where by
t_{\infty}
we denote a sufficiently large time after the interaction.

The transition rate becomes:

\displaystyle R_{{}_{i\rightarrow f}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{6}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)}\sum_{\sigma\lambda}\big|\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{P},\lambda)\eta_{1/2}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})\big|^{2}.

(50)

The summation of the helicity spinors and polarization vectors is straightforward: we set the particles on the same direction, i.e. the third axis such that
\vec{P}=P\vec{e}_{3};\,\vec{p}=p\vec{e}_{3};\,\vec{p^{\prime}}=-p^{\prime}\vec{e}_{3}
. The bispinor summation has just one nonvanishing term, if one uses circular polarizations:

\xi^{{\dagger}}_{-1/2}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}_{-1}(\vec{P},\lambda)\eta_{1/2}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})=-\sqrt{2}.

(51)

In consequence, the final form of the rate is:

R_{{}_{i\rightarrow f}}=\frac{g^{2}}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)}.

(52)

The total transition rate for the
W^{-}
decay in this limit is obtained by integrating after the final momenta of the electron and anti-neutrino:

\displaystyle R_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\,R_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{g^{2}}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)}.

(53)

The momenta integrals are divergent and from the known regularization methods
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
, it seems that dimensional regularization
[
33
,
34
,
37
]
will work best moving forward. The first step is to solve the momentum integral with respect to
p^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime})}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p\frac{1}{2P(p-P)}

(54)

The above integral is divergent, so we can write it in

\displaystyle I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}}{2P(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\frac{p^{D-1}}{p-P}.

(55)

Next we change the integration variable to
p=-Py
, which leads to:

\displaystyle I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-P)^{D-1}}{2P(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{D-1}}{1+y}dy.

(56)

Integral (
62

165

\displaystyle I(D)=\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-P)^{D-1}\Gamma(D)\Gamma(1-D)}{(2\pi)^{D}2P\Gamma(D/2)},

for

However, this integral is still divergent in

for

[

, and compute the residue of the integral in
D=3
:

\text{Res}\,I(D)=\lim_{D\rightarrow 3}(3-D)I(D)=-\frac{P}{4\pi^{2}}.

(58)

Afterwards we choose a counter-term of the form:

\centering\frac{\mu^{s}\,\text{Res}\,I(D)}{3-D},\@add@centering

(59)

where

is a mass parameter and

Then we can define the regularized integral as such:

I(D)_{r}=I(D)-\frac{P\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}(3-D)}.

Explicitly this is:

\displaystyle I(D)_{r}=\frac{1}{3-D}\bigg(\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-P)^{D-1}\Gamma(D)\Gamma(4-D)}{2P(2\pi)^{D}(1-D)(2-D)\Gamma(D/2)}-\frac{P\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg),

By expanding the paranthesis around the value

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{D/2}(-P)^{D-1}\Gamma(D)\Gamma(4-D)}{2P(2\pi)^{D}(1-D)(2-D)\Gamma(D/2)}-\frac{P\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}}=\frac{P(3-D)}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\big((D-3)^{2}\big).

Now the factor of

\displaystyle R_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}=\frac{G_{F}M_{W}^{2}\,P}{4\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg[\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)\bigg].

(63)

The Fermi constant
G_{F}
is related to the coupling constant by relation:

g^{2}=4\sqrt{2}G_{F}M_{W}^{2}.

(64)

The total transition rate in the limit of large expansion is finite and depends on the momentum

The probability per unit volume can also be computed by taking the squared modulus of amplitude (

\big|(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{p})\big|^{2}=(2\pi)^{3}V\delta^{3}(\vec{p})

\displaystyle P_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\lambda}\frac{g^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,)}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)^{2}}\big|\xi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{P},\lambda)\eta_{1/2}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime})\big|^{2}.

(65)

The total probability of
W^{-}
decay in the large expanstion limit can be obtained by integrating after the final momenta of the particles:

P_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}=\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}P_{i\rightarrow f}.

(66)

The momenta integrals that need to be solved are of the type:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}-\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime})}{P(p+p^{\prime}-P)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p\frac{1}{4P(p-P)^{2}}.

(67)

By applying the dimensional regularization together with the minimal subtraction method
[

,
33
,
34
,

,
37
]
, as in the case of the total transition rate, the final result turns out to be:

P_{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\widetilde{\nu}}=\frac{G_{F}M_{W}^{2}}{8\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}}\bigg[1-\psi\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)-\ln\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{P^{2}}\Big)\bigg].

(68)

From the above formula we can see that the total probability is well defined when
4\pi\mu^{2}\leq P^{2}
.

The results from this section prove that renormalization techniques used in flat spacetime field theory can be applied in a curved background. We have obtained a finite result for the decay rate of the
W^{-}
boson in the de Sitter geometry, and we observe that this rate (

), computed in the high expansion limit, differs from the one computed in Minkowski spacetime for the same process (

). This difference will be discussed further in the next section.

2.4
The Minkowski limit

Since the decays of
W
bosons into lepton pairs are well studied in Minkowski electro-weak theory
[
19
]
, one would expect to recover the decay rates of these processes in flat spacetime from the decay rates in an expanding background, as a limiting case. However, the general result for the amplitudes is expressed in terms of Appell
F_{4}
functions, which are not well studied in the literature. This poses challenges when trying to recover the Minkowski limit of the decay rates.

There have been some results regarding integrals involving a power function and three Bessel functions of integer orders
[
71
]
. Here the result of this type of integrals is obtained as a semi-empirical approximate formula in terms of the Dirac delta functions
[
71
]
. However, in our computations we are dealing with Hankel functions of imaginary order. Consequently we can only obtain the Minkowski limit when neglecting the fermion masses, in order for the imaginary part of the Hankel function order to vanish. This corresponds physically to the ultrarelativistic limit, i.e. the momenta of the particles are much larger than their masses.

In trying to recover the Minkowski limit of the transition rate, we see that there will be two contributions to the amplitude, one from the amplitude for
\lambda=\pm 1
and one from the amplitude for
\lambda=0
:

\mathcal{A}_{[W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}]}^{[\omega\rightarrow 0]}=\mathcal{A}_{[W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}+\mathcal{A}^{[\lambda=0](2)}_{[{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}}]},

(69)

where we have denoted
\mathcal{A}_{[W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}]}^{[\omega\rightarrow 0]}
the amplitude in the Minkowski limit.

Only the second term from the longitudinal polarisation amplitude (
22
) survives in the Minkowski limit when
\omega\rightarrow 0
, since all three terms which make up the amplitude for
\lambda=0
, (
24
), (
30
), (
34
), are multiplied by a factor of
\omega/M_{W}
.

For the second term, however, we have a more complicated dependence on
\omega/M_{W}
through
K
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{[\lambda=0](2)}_{[{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}}]}=-\frac{i\,\omega}{M_{W}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+iK\right)\mathcal{A}(\pm 1)\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{j}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\epsilon_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=0),

(70)

where
K
is defined as:

\displaystyle K=\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{W}}{\omega}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}.

(71)

Therefore we can write:

\displaystyle-i\frac{\omega}{M_{W}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+iK\right)=-i\frac{\omega}{2M_{W}}+\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\omega^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}}}.

(72)

When
\omega\rightarrow 0
, term (
70
) becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{[\lambda=0](2)}_{[{W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}}]}=\mathcal{A}(\pm 1)\xi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p})\sigma^{j}\eta_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\epsilon_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=0).

(73)

By using the above formula, and also formula (

3.3
The transition rate

First we will compute the rate in as general a case as we possibly can, i.e. we will keep some dependence on the ratios between the particle masses and Hubble parameter through variables
k
and

We rewrite amplitude (

) for
\lambda=\pm 1

P

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

We denote the temporal integral by

\displaystyle=-\frac{ig}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p}e^{\pi k/2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\,\xi_{-1/2}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\sigma^{j}\xi_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\epsilon_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1).

\displaystyle R_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\lambda}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\delta^{3}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}+\vec{P}-\vec{p}\,)|\mathcal{M}_{if}|^{2}|\mathcal{I}_{if}|\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{if}|.

An analytical result for the transition rate can be obtained if the Bessel K functions are expanded for small arguments

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z)\simeq\frac{2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)}{z^{\nu}},

Then the Bessel K function reduces to:

The Hankel function

\displaystyle R_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{g^{2}\pi^{2}pp^{\prime}}{32(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{3}(\vec{p}-\vec{p^{{}^{\prime}}}-\vec{P})\,\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{if}|}{\sqrt{Pp\cosh(\pi k)}}

where

.

The expression for
\mathcal{I}_{if}
is:

\mathcal{I}_{if}=\frac{e^{i\pi/4}e^{\pi k/2}}{i\sqrt{p^{\prime}}\cosh(\pi k)}(T_{1}+T_{2}-T_{3}-T_{4}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

3.4
The transition rate in the large expansion limit

In the limit when the expansion parameter is much larger than the particles masses
m_{e}/\omega=M_{W}/\omega=0
, the amplitude assumes a simpler form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{-ge^{i\pi/4}}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,\xi_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{{\dagger}}(\vec{p^{\prime}})\sigma^{j}\xi_{\sigma}(\vec{p})\epsilon_{j}(\vec{P},\lambda=\pm 1)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma^{\prime}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{P}+\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p})\,\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{pP}}\int dz\,e^{i(p-p^{\prime}-P)z},

(146)

since the Hankel functions reduce to exponential functions (
160
).

We can again split the amplitude into factors to separate the temporal integral:

\mathcal{A}_{[{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu}]}^{[\lambda=\pm 1]}=\delta^{3}(\vec{P}+\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p})\mathcal{M}_{if}\mathcal{I}_{if},

(147)

where
\mathcal{I}_{if}
is given by:

\mathcal{I}_{if}=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{pP}}\int dz\,e^{i(p-p^{\prime}-P)z}=\frac{2i}{\pi\sqrt{pP}(p-p^{\prime}-P)}.

(148)

The limit of the integrand of temporal integral is:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{\infty}}\big|e^{\omega t}\mathcal{K}_{i\rightarrow f}\big|=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{pP}}.

(149)

The transition rate will be defined using equation (
137
):

\displaystyle R_{i\rightarrow f}=\frac{g^{2}\delta^{3}(\vec{P}+\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p})}{8(2\pi)^{6}P(p-p^{\prime}-P)},

(150)

where we have considered the particles in this process to be aligned in the same direction, i.e.
\vec{p}=p\,\vec{e}_{3}\,,\vec{p}\,^{\prime}=-p^{\prime}\vec{e}_{3}\,,\vec{P}=P\vec{e}_{3}
.

The total transition rate for the
W
boson emission by electrons is obtained after we integrate over the final momenta of the particles:

\displaystyle R_{{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\int d^{3}P\,R_{i\rightarrow f}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{6}}\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\int d^{3}P\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}+\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p})}{P(p-p^{\prime}-P)}.

(151)

The first integration over momentum
p^{\prime}
leads to:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\int d^{3}P\frac{\delta^{3}(\vec{P}+\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p})}{P(p-p^{\prime}-P)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}p^{\prime}\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}(p+p^{\prime})}

(152)

We can renormalize the integral by using dimensional regularization and the minimal subtraction method
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
37
]
. In
D
dimensions this integral reads:

\displaystyle I(D)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{D}}\int d^{D}p^{\prime}\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}(p+p^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{D/2}}{2(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int dp^{\prime}\frac{p^{\prime D-2}}{(p+p^{\prime})}.

(153)

The integral with respcet to
p^{\prime}
can be solved by using the Beta Euler function (
165
), after performing a change of variables such that
p^{\prime}=py
:

\displaystyle I(D)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{D/2}p^{D-2}}{2(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)}\int dy\frac{y^{D-2}}{(1+y)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi^{D/2}p^{D-2}\Gamma(D-1)\Gamma(4-D)}{2(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)(2-D)(3-D)},

(154)

where we have also employed the relation between Gamma Euler functions
z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(1+z)

[
25
,
26
]
.

The above result is still divergent for
D=3
, but we can apply the minimal subtraction method by computing the residue of
I(D)
in
D=3
:

\text{Res}\,I(D)=\lim_{D\rightarrow 3}(3-D)I(D)=-\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}.

(155)

Then we can define the regularized integral as:

I(D)_{r}=I(D)-\frac{p\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}(3-D)},

(156)

where
\mu
is a mass paramter which allows us to introduce a counter term of the same dimension as
I(D)
.

Explicitly this is:

\displaystyle I(D)_{r}=\frac{1}{3-D}\bigg(\frac{2\pi^{D/2}p^{D-2}\Gamma(D-1)\Gamma(4-D)}{2(2\pi)^{D}\Gamma(D/2)(2-D)}+\frac{p\mu^{D-3}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg).

(157)

By expanding the parenthesis around
D=3
we obtain a term proportional with
3-D
, that will cancel the divergence. The final result is:

\displaystyle I(D)_{r}=\frac{p}{8\pi^{2}}\left[2-\gamma-\ln\left(\frac{p^{2}}{\pi\mu^{2}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}\left((D-3)^{2}\right),

(158)

where terms
\mathcal{O}\left((D-3)^{2}\right)
will vanish for
D=3
.

We obtain at last the total transition rate in the large expansion limit:

\displaystyle R_{{e^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}+\nu}}=\frac{pM_{W}^{2}G_{F}}{8\pi^{2}\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3}}\left[2-\gamma-\ln\left(\frac{p^{2}}{\pi\mu^{2}}\right)\right],

(159)

where we have made use of the relation between the Fermi constant
G_{F}
and the coupling constant
g
(
64
).

For the total transition rate to be well defined, the argument of the logarithm has to be subunitary.

4
Conclusions

In the present paper we investigated the decays of
W
bosons and the emission of
W
bosons by electrons in the de Sitter spacetime. These elementary processes correspond to the first order of perturbation theory, and can be studied by using the electro-weak charged current interactions in the de Sitter background. We computed the transition rates for the processes in which the
W
bosons can be produced by a charged leptons, and the decay rates of the
W
bosons into leptons in the de Sitter spacetime. We also explored the limit of a large expansion factor, when the Hubble parameter becomes much larger than the particle masses. The transition rates in the strong gravitational regime are obtained as finite quantities with the help of the dimensional regularization method, combined with the minimal subtraction method
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
37
]
.

The temporal integrals in our amplitudes contain a dependence on the particles masses and the expansion parameter. In the general case, when the amplitudes depend on the Hubble constant, particles masses and momenta, the integrals that need to be solved for extracting the total probabilities and total transition rates are not solvable analytically. Under these circumstances we look for various relevant limits of our general results, which lead to the study of the Minkowski limit, as well as the high expansion limit,
M_{W}/\omega\rightarrow 0
, which corresponds to the early Universe. In the limit
M_{W}/\omega\rightarrow 0
, the momenta integrals that need to be computed in order to obtain the transition rates are also divergent, but unlike the general case, here they can be solved by using well known regularization methods
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
37
]
.

We have managed to recover the Minkowski limit of the transition rate for the leptonic decay of a
W^{-}
boson, which is equal to the Minkowski transition rate for the same process
[
60
]
. For obtaining this limit we considered the ultra-relativistic case, when the modulus of the momentum is equal to the energy of the particle. This is a remarkable result, and proves that our general findings in the de Sitter Universe can lead to the Minkowski formulas for
\omega=0
.

We also proved that the emission of a
W
boson by a charged lepton is possible as a perturbative process only in the high expantion conditions of the early Universe, as we obtained that the rate for this process vanishes in the Minkowski limit. Our investigations confirm results obtained by both perturbative
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
46
,
53
,
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
]
and non-perturbative methods
[
54
,
55
,
56
,
57
]
, where the conclusions lead to the fact that particle generation is possible only in the early Universe.

\displaystyle H^{(2),(1)}_{\nu}(z)=\pm\frac{2i}{\pi}e^{\pm i\pi\nu/2}K_{\nu}(\pm iz);

(161)

\displaystyle H^{(2),(1)}_{\nu}(z)=\frac{J_{-\nu}(z)-e^{\pm i\pi\nu}J_{\nu}(z)}{\mp i\sin{(\pi\nu)}}.

(162)

The integral that helps us obtain the transition amplitudes is
[
25
,
26
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle dx\,x^{Q-1}J_{\lambda}(ax)J_{\mu}(bx)K_{\nu}(cx)=\frac{2^{Q-2}a^{\lambda}b^{\mu}c^{-Q-\lambda-\mu}}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\mu+1)}

\displaystyle\,\,\times\Gamma\left(\frac{Q+\lambda+\mu-\nu}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{Q+\lambda+\mu+\nu}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\times F_{4}\Bigg(\frac{Q+\lambda+\mu-\nu}{2},\frac{Q+\lambda+\mu+\nu}{2},\lambda+1,mu+1,-\frac{a^{2}}{c^{2}},-\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}\Bigg).

(163)

The definition of the Appell
F_{4}
function is given below
[
25
,
26
]
:

\displaystyle F_{4}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\gamma^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+m+n)\Gamma{(\gamma)}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma+m)}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+m+n)\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}+n)}\frac{x^{m}y^{n}}{m!n!}.

(164)

The Beta Euler function (which we employ in dimensional regularisation) is:

\displaystyle B(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\frac{y^{\alpha-1}}{(1+y)^{\alpha+\beta}}.

(165)

The Gamma function can be expanded as:

\displaystyle\Gamma(\epsilon-n)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\psi(n+1)\frac{\epsilon}{2}\bigg[\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}+\psi^{2}(n+1)-\psi^{\prime}(n+1)\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\bigg\},

(166)

where
\psi
are the Digamma functions:

\displaystyle\psi(n)

\displaystyle=-\gamma+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{l},

(167)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{l^{2}},

(168)

and
\gamma=-0.5772
is the Euler constant.

Appendix B
Computing the amplitude in the Minkowski limit

In this section we give the steps to solve the temporal integral contained in amplitude (
76
) from section
2.4
, which helps us recover the Minkowski limit of the transition rate for the process
W^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\tilde{\nu}_{e}
.

For solving this integral we begin with the temporal integral in (
14
) and take the fermion mass equal to zero. First we transform the electron Hankel function into an exponential by using formulas (
161
) and (
160
):

\displaystyle e^{-\pi K/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,e^{-ip^{\prime}z}H_{\frac{1}{2}}^{(2)}(pz)H_{iK}^{(1)}(Pz)=e^{-\pi K/2}e^{i\pi/4}\sqrt{\frac{2i}{\pi p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\sqrt{z}\,e^{-i(p+p^{\prime})z}H_{iK}^{(1)}(Pz).

(169)

Afterwards we transform the exponential function into a Hankel function of the second kind by using the same formulas as before. Furthermore we transform the remaining Hankel function into a Bessel K function by using formula (
161
):

\frac{-2i}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{(p+p^{\prime})}{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,H_{\frac{1}{2}}^{(2)}\big((p+p^{\prime})z\big)K_{iK}(-iPz),

(170)

from which we can write the final form of this integral by transforming the Hankel functions into Bessel J (
162
):

\displaystyle\frac{-2i}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{p+p^{\prime}}{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,K_{iK}(-iPz)\bigg(J_{\frac{1}{2}}\big((p+p^{\prime})z\big)+iJ_{-\frac{1}{2}}\big((p+p^{\prime})z\big)\bigg).

(171)

We mention that factor
-2i/\pi\sqrt{p}
will be reduced by other terms in amplitude (
76
), while the term
\sqrt{p+p^{\prime}}
will remain moving forward.

In order to solve (
171
) further we add a small imaginary part to the
W
boson momentum
P\rightarrow P+i\epsilon
, for the sake of assuring the convergence of the integral. At the end of our computations we will take the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Next we divide (
171
) into two integrals:

\displaystyle I=I_{1}+I_{2}=\sqrt{p+p^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z\,K_{iK}(-i(P+i\epsilon)z)\bigg(J_{\frac{1}{2}}\big((p+p^{\prime})z\big)+iJ_{-\frac{1}{2}}\big((p+p^{\prime})z\big)\bigg),

(172)

which can be evaluated by using
[
25
,
26
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{-\lambda}J_{\nu}(\alpha z)K_{\mu}(\beta z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\nu}}{2^{\lambda+1}\beta^{\nu-\lambda+1}\Gamma\left(1+\nu\right)}

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+\mu-\lambda+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu-\mu-\lambda+1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\nu+\mu-\lambda+1}{2},\frac{\nu-\mu-\lambda+1}{2};\nu+1;-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\text{Re}(\alpha\pm i\beta)>0\,,|\text{Re}(\nu-\lambda+1)|>|Re(\mu)|.

(173)

The results for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are:

\displaystyle I_{1}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+p^{\prime})\,\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{iK}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)(\epsilon-iP)^{5/2}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{5}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{3}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right);

(174)

\displaystyle I_{2}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{iK}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)(\epsilon-iP)^{3/2}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{3}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right).

(175)

The above results for the integrals contain both cases of
P=p+p^{\prime}
and
P\neq p+p^{\prime}
. To obtain the result in the case when
P=p+p^{\prime}
, we transform the Gauss hypergeometric function according to the formula
[
25
,
26
]
:

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}\,_{2}F_{1}(c-a,c-b;c;z).

(176)

Thus we obtain for
I_{1}(\epsilon)
:

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{5}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{3}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right)=

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{1}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{3}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right).

(177)

The momentum dependent factor in front of the hypergeometric function can be written as:

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right)^{-1}=-\frac{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}{P\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{P}-P-2i\epsilon\right)},

(178)

while in
I_{1}(\epsilon)
we can transform:

(\epsilon-iP)^{-5/2}=-e^{i\pi/4}(i\epsilon+P)^{-5/2}.

(179)

Then the first integral
I_{1}(\epsilon)
can be brought to the form:

\displaystyle I_{1}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\pi/4}(p+p^{\prime})}{\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{2P}-\frac{P}{2}-i\epsilon\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{iK}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)(i\epsilon+P)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\times_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{1}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{3}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{(P+i\epsilon)^{2}}\right).

(180)

We can take limit
\epsilon=0
by using the principal part prescription
[

,
26
]

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{x-x_{0}\pm i\epsilon}=Pp\left(\frac{1}{x-x_{0}}\right)\mp i\pi\delta(x-x_{0}),

(181)

where the first term of the result is given in equation (
174

In our case the limit gives:

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{2P}-\frac{P^{2}}{2P}-i\epsilon\right)}

\displaystyle=i\pi\delta\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}-P^{2}}{2P}\right)+Pp\left(\frac{1}{\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{2P}-\frac{P^{2}}{2P}\right)}\right).

(182)

The final result for
I_{1}
when
P=p+p^{\prime}

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}I_{1}|_{P=p+p^{\prime}}=\frac{i\pi e^{i\pi/4}}{2}\frac{(p+p^{\prime})}{P^{3/2}}\delta(p+p^{\prime}-P),

(183)

where we have used relation
[

,
26
]
:

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;1)=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}

(184)

for rewriting the hypergeometric function in (
180

The result for the second integral
I_{2}

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}I_{2}|_{P=p+p^{\prime}}=\frac{i\pi e^{-i\pi/4}}{2P^{1/2}}\delta(p+p^{\prime}-P).

(185)

In the case when
p+p^{\prime}\neq P
the result for integrals
I_{1}
and

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}|_{P\neq p+p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\Theta(P-p-p^{\prime})\left[f_{1}\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})}{P}\right)+f_{2}\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})}{P}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\Theta(p+p^{\prime}-P)\left[f_{1}^{\prime}\left(\frac{P}{(p+p^{\prime})}\right)+f_{2}^{\prime}\left(\frac{P}{(p+p^{\prime})}\right)\right],

\displaystyle f_{2}\left(\frac{(p+p^{\prime})}{P}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{iK}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)(-iP)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{iK}{2},\frac{3}{4}+\frac{iK}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{(p+p^{\prime})^{2}}{P^{2}}\right);

(188)

f_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle f_{1}\left(\frac{P}{(p+p^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{(p+p^{\prime})}e^{\pi K/2}e^{i\pi/4}}{P^{-iK}\cosh{(\pi K})}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{iK}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+iK\right)(p+p^{\prime})^{iK+2}}

\displaystyle f_{2}^{\prime}\left(\frac{P}{(p+p^{\prime})}\right)

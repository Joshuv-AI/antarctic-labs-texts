Deadbeat Robust Model Predictive Control for Linear Parameter-Varying Systems
Georg Schildbach, Hossam S. Abbas

Abstract
The concept of Deadbeat Robust Model Predictive Control (DRMPC) is to completely extinguish the effect of external disturbances within the first few steps of the prediction horizon. The benefit is that the remaining dynamics of the system can be planned with certainty. This means it is not necessary to employ a Robust Positive Invariant (RPI) set as a terminal condition, which is often complex or even intractable to compute. Recent work has shown that it is possible to obtain full system theoretic guarantees in the case of linear systems, including recursive feasibility of all constraints and input-to-state stability. This paper extends this contribution to linear time-varying (LTV) or parameter-varying systems (LPV) systems with bounded parametric uncertainty and additive disturbances. Full system-theoretic guarantees can also be provided for these cases. Numerical simulation results demonstrate that the performance of the proposed LPV-DRMPC scheme, with the origin as the terminal set and a slightly increased prediction horizon, is almost comparable to that of other LPV-MPC schemes with an RPI terminal set constraint, despite a lower computational complexity. Besides avoiding the computation of RPI terminal sets, LPV-DRMPC allows to shift a significant portion of the computations offline.



I
Introduction

Linear Parameter-Varying Model Predictive Control (LPV-MPC) is a recent and powerful control strategy for nonlinear, time-varying systems, and systems subject to time-varying parametric uncertainty
[
1
]
. It aims to account for the parametric uncertainty and essential nonlinearity of the plant via their embedding into a Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) system. The goal is to reduce the computational requirements compared to a full-blown nonlinear approach, by applying an extension of the classic methodology of linear Model Predictive Control (MPC). LPV-MPC has demonstrated an excellent performance in a wide range of practical applications, including automated vehicles
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, aerospace systems
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, and wind turbines
[
8
,
9
]
.

From a research perspective, several challenges related to LPV-MPC remain open. Some of them are related to specific applications, such as the identification and proper formulation of LPV models
[
10
]
and others are realted to the design of proper real-time implementations using explicit solutions or online optimization solvers
[
11
,
4
]
. Also the fundamental design of LPV-MPC algorithms is subject to ongoing research
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. A major challenge is to find approaches that are robust to parameter-related uncertainties over the MPC prediction horizon, without being overly conservative or highly computationally demanding due to the online construction of robustification conditions, e.g., invariant tubes, constraint tightening, or computation of robust positive invariant sets. These approaches require rigorous proofs of recursive feasibility and robust stability. Moreover, they should be easy to implement and computationally efficient.

The contribution of this paper is related to the fundamental design of LPV-MPC algorithms. It leverages the recent results on Deadbeat Robust Model Predictive Control (DRMPC) for linear systems
[
16
]
. The main idea of DRMPC is to deadbeat the effect of the uncertainty over the first few steps of the prediction horizon, which is called the
deadbeat horizon
. This deadbeat property inside the predictions is only virtual in nature, in contrast to actual deadbeat control
[
17
]
. The advantage lies in the complete elimination of the uncertainty in the predictions beyond the deadbeat horizon. This allows for the use of a deterministic terminal condition, which significantly simplifies the controller design and implementation, while providing the full range of system-theoretic guarantees. The control performance of DRMPC appears competitive with other state-of-the-art approach of Robust Model Predictive Control (RMPC) for linear systems
[
16
]
, such as Tube-based RMPC
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
and RMPC with Affine Disturbance Feedback
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

The contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows: (1) introduction of a new parameterization of the deadbeat predictions for linear and LPV systems; (2) extension of DRMPC to a class of LPV systems; (3) rigorous proofs of recursive feasibility and robust exponential stability for the linear and the LPV Case; (4) evaluation of the performance based on a commonly used benchmark example
[
24
]
.

II-C

Conversion to Additive Uncertainty

The Robust Case

The goal of this section is to convert system (
1
) into the form

x_{k+1}=\bar{A}x_{k}+\bar{B}u_{k}+d_{k}\;,

(8)

where
d_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
is a modified disturbance that subsumes the original additive disturbance and the parametric uncertainty. The representation (
8
) is achieved by defining

d_{k}\triangleq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)x_{k}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)u_{k}+w_{k}\;.

The set
\mathbb{D}
is defined by

\mathbb{D}\triangleq\text{conv}\left\{\left.\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)x_{k}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)u_{k}+w_{k}\right|\theta_{k}\in\Theta\;,w_{k}\in\mathbb{W}\;,x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}\;,u_{k}\in\mathbb{U}\right\}\;.

(9)

Clearly, under Assumption
5
,
\mathbb{D}
is again a convex polytope that contains the origin. Hence the problem statement fully corresponds to the original formulation of DRMPC
[
16
]
.

The LPV Case

At step
k
, only the additive disturbance
w_{k}\in\mathbb{W}
acts on the system, since the time-varying parameter
\theta_{k}
is known according to Assumption L.
3
. Therefore, the system is represented by

x_{k+1}=A_{k}x_{k}+B_{k}u_{k}+w_{k}\;,

(10)

where
A_{k}=A(\theta_{k})
and
B_{k}=B(\theta_{k})
are known exactly.
When predicting the system behavior at step
k+j
using the nominal parameter values
\bar{\theta}_{k+j}
, the system may be affected by both an additive disturbance
w_{k+j}\in\mathbb{W}
and a parametric uncertainty
\Delta\theta_{k+j}\in\Theta_{\Delta}
, for all
j=1,2,\dots,N
.
To handle this, we lump the effects of the additive disturbance and the parametric uncertainty into a single augmented additive disturbance,
d^{\theta}
. This leads to the following system representation,

x_{k+j+1}=\bar{A}_{k+j}x_{k+j}+\bar{B}_{k+j}u_{k+j}+d^{\theta}_{k+j}\;,

(11)

where
d^{\theta}_{k+j}\in\mathbb{D}_{\theta}
is defined as

d^{\theta}_{k+j}\triangleq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Delta\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)x_{k}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Delta\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)u_{k}+w_{k}\;,

with

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}\triangleq\text{conv}\left\{\left.\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Delta\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)x_{k}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\Delta\theta_{k}[i]A^{(i)}\right)u_{k}+w_{k}\right|\Delta\theta_{k}\in\Theta_{\Delta}\;,w_{k}\in\mathbb{W}\;,x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}\;,u_{k}\in\mathbb{U}\right\}\;.

(12)

This is again a polytopic set, based on Assumption
5
.

The formulation for the Robust Case has been previously considered
[
26
]
. However, it may lead to overly conservative behavior in the LPV Case. Other alternative methods exist for converting parametric uncertainties into additive disturbances using worst-case bounds, which can reduce conservatism depending on the choice of other design parameters in the MPC formulation, such as terminal constraints
[
27
]
. In contrast, considering the above formulation in the LPV Case does not necessarily result in overly conservative behavior, due to Assumption L.
3

[
12
]
. In general, considering this simple approach is practical and facilitates the subsequent formulation of the proposed DRMPC schemes.

III
Deadbeat Robust Model Predictive Control (DRMPC)

III-A

Deadbeat Disturbance Feedback Policy

A slightly different formulation of DRMPC is proposed in this paper, compared to the use of
deadbeat input sequences
for additive disturbances
[
16
]
. Instead, a
deadbeat (disturbance feedback) policy
for pre-stabilization is introduced as explained below.

Consider the nominal system of (
1
),

x_{k+1}=\bar{A}x_{k}+\bar{B}u_{k}\;,

(13)

starting from an initial condition
x_{0}=d_{0}
that corresponds to a single disturbance
d_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, and the following definition.

Definition 6
(deadbeat horizon)

The
deadbeat horizon

M

[
16
]
is the smallest number such that the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times nm}
,

\mathcal{P}_{M}\triangleq\Bigl[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c|c}\;\bar{A}^{M-1}\bar{B}&\;\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}_{0}&\;\cdots&\;\bar{A}\bar{B}&\;\bar{B}\end{array}\Bigr]\;,

(14)

has full rank
n
.

By virtue of Assumption R.
2
,
M
is well defined and satisfies
M\leq n
, because
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is equivalent to the
controllability matrix
of (
13
). This leads to the following result.

Lemma 7
(deadbeat disturbance feedback policy)

There exists a
deadbeat (disturbance feedback) policy
, consisting of
M
linear feedback gains
\bar{K}_{0},\bar{K}_{1},\dots,\bar{K}_{M-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with

\bar{u}_{0}=\bar{K}_{0}d_{0}\;,\qquad\bar{u}_{1}=\bar{K}_{1}d_{0}\;,\qquad\dots\;,\qquad\bar{u}_{M-1}=\bar{K}_{M-1}d_{0}\;,

(15)

such that
x_{M}=0
for any initial condition
d_{0}
.

Proof:

First, observe that the first
M
states of the nominal system (
13
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}d_{0}+\bar{B}u_{0}\;,

(16a)

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}^{2}d_{0}+\bar{A}\bar{B}u_{0}+\bar{B}u_{1}\;,

(16b)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle x_{M-1}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}^{M-1}d_{0}+\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}u_{0}+\dots+\bar{A}\bar{B}u_{M-3}+\bar{B}u_{M-2}\;,

(16c)

\displaystyle x_{M}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}^{M}d_{0}+\bar{A}^{M-1}\bar{B}u_{0}+\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}u_{1}+\dots+\bar{A}\bar{B}u_{M-2}+\bar{B}u_{M-1}\;.

(16d)

Since the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{M}
in (
14
) has full rank, there exists a sequence of deadbeat inputs
\bar{u}_{0},\bar{u}_{1},\dots,\bar{u}_{M-1}
that force

. Using

, (
16
d) becomes

-\bar{A}^{M}d_{0}\overset{!}{=}\bar{A}^{M-1}\bar{B}\bar{u}_{0}+\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}u_{1}+\dots+\bar{A}\bar{B}\bar{u}_{M-2}+\bar{B}\bar{u}_{M-1}\;.

To verify that the deadbeat policy is indeed well-defined, substitute (
15
) into (

) to obtain

-\bar{A}^{M}d_{0}=\Bigl[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c|c}\bar{A}^{M-1}\bar{B}\bar{K}_{0}&\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}\bar{K}_{1}&\dots&\bar{A}\bar{B}\bar{K}_{M-2}&\bar{B}\bar{K}_{M-1}\end{array}\Bigr]d_{0}\;.

Since the deadbeat policy must work for all

-\bar{A}^{M}=\Bigl[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c|c}\bar{A}^{M-1}\bar{B}&\bar{A}^{M-2}\bar{B}&\dots&\bar{A}\bar{B}&\bar{B}\end{array}\Bigr]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{K}_{0}\\
\hline\cr\bar{K}_{1}\\
\hline\cr\dots\\
\hline\cr\bar{K}_{M-2}\\
\hline\cr\bar{K}_{M-1}\end{array}\right]\;.

(19)

This can be expressed as a linear equation system to solve for the linear feeback gains

:

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathcal{P}_{M}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{P}_{M}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathcal{P}_{M}&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&\mathcal{P}_{M}\end{array}\right]\mathrm{vec}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{K}_{0}\\
\hline\cr\bar{K}_{1}\\
\hline\cr\dots\\

The claim follows from the fact that

has full rank

, according to Definition

, and hence the matrix on the left-hand side of (

.

Remark 8

Alternatively, it is possible to calculate a

disturbance feedback gain

Note that Lemma

Remark 9

(a) If the deadbeat policy is not unique, then some additional cost function (e.g., least squares) has to be defined when solving for
\bar{K}_{0},\bar{K}_{1},\dots,\bar{K}_{M-1}
, subject to the equality constraint (

).
(b) The deadbeat horizon

may be deliberately chosen as a higher number than the smallest number for which

has full rank

. This opens (additional) degrees of freedom in the choice of the deadbeat policy.

Finite-time Optimal Control Problem

V-A
1
Recursive Feasibility

Under the DRMPC regime, in each time step

the first element of the optimal input sequence from (

Theorem 11
(recursive feasibility for the Robust Case)

Assume system (
1
) is linear with additive disturbances, i.e.,
d=d_{0}
for some
d_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. If the DRMPC problem (
25
) is feasible at
k=0
for

x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
of system (
1
) under the DRMPC regime.

Proof:
Consider the solution to (
25
) in step
k=0
. Let
u_{0|0}^{\star},\ldots,u_{N-1|0}^{\star}
be the optimal inputs and
x_{0|0}^{\star},\ldots,x_{N|0}^{\star}
be the optimal states. They satisfy the input constraints (
22
) and state constraints (
24
), respectively. By the terminal constraint (
25
e),
x_{N|0}^{\star}=0
.

For recursive feasibility, it must be shown that in time step

there exists a candidate input sequence
u_{1|1},\ldots,u_{N|1}
and a corresponding state sequence
x_{1|1},\ldots,x_{N+1|1}
that satisfy the input, state, and terminal constraints, for any admissible disturbance
d_{0}
. By the presence of a disturbance,

x_{1|1}=x_{1|0}^{\star}+d_{0}\;,\quad\text{for some}\;d_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\;.

Consider the following candidate input sequence for step

,

\displaystyle u_{1|1}

\displaystyle=u_{1|0}^{\star}+\bar{K}_{0}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle u_{2|1}

\displaystyle=u_{2|0}^{\star}+\bar{K}_{1}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle\;\;\vdots

\displaystyle u_{M|1}

\displaystyle=u_{M|0}^{\star}+\bar{K}_{M-1}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle u_{M+1|1}

\displaystyle=u_{M+1|0}^{\star}\;,

\displaystyle\;\;\vdots

\displaystyle u_{N-1|1}

\displaystyle=u_{N-1|0}^{\star}\;,

\displaystyle u_{N|1}

\displaystyle=0\;.

This candidate input sequence

satisfies the input constraints (
22

satisfies (

\displaystyle x_{1|1}

\displaystyle=x_{1|0}^{\star}+d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle x_{2|1}

\displaystyle=x_{2|0}^{\star}+\bar{\Phi}_{0}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle x_{3|1}

\displaystyle=x_{3|0}^{\star}+\bar{\Phi}_{1}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle\;\;\vdots

\displaystyle x_{M|1}

\displaystyle=x_{M|0}^{\star}+\bar{\Phi}_{M-2}d_{0}\;,

\displaystyle=x_{M+1|0}^{\star}\;,

\displaystyle=x_{M+2|0}^{\star}\;,

\displaystyle=x_{N|0}^{\star}=0\;,

V-A
2
Input-to-State Stability

Let
\mathcal{X}_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
be the
feasible set
, i.e., the set of initial conditions
x_{0}
for which problem (
25
) is feasible. The
state feedback control law

\kappa_{N}:\mathcal{X}_{N}\to\mathbb{U}
of the DRMPC regime is defined as

Definition 12
(robust positive invariant set)

Consider system (
1
) with any state feedback control law
\kappa:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{U}
. A set
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
is called a
robust positive invariant (RPI) set
if for all
x\in\mathcal{P}
it holds that

\kappa(x)\in\mathbb{U}\quad\text{and}\quad Ax+B\kappa(x)\oplus\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\;.

Corollary 13
(invariance of the feasible set)

The feasible set
\mathcal{X}_{N}
is a RPI set for system (
1
) under the DRMPC regime.

Proof:
This is immediate from Theorem
11
.
\square

Computing the feasible set of the DRMPC problem (
25
) hence leads to an RPI set. On top of this invariance property, it can be shown that the DRMPC stabilizes system (
1
). Due to the presence of disturbances, the appropriate stability concept to be used is
input-to-state stability (ISS)

[
28
,
29
,
20
]
. It requires the following standard assumption on the stage cost function.

Assumption 14
(stage cost)

For the stage cost function
\ell:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0+}
there exist two
\textrm{K}_{\infty}
-functions
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
with

\varepsilon_{1}(\|x\|)\leq\ell\left(x,u\right)\leq\varepsilon_{2}(\|x\|)\quad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{X}\;,\;u\in\mathbb{U}\;.

(35)

Definition 15
(input-to-state stability)

System (
1
) under some state feedback law
\kappa:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{U}
is
input-to-state stable (ISS)
on some RPI set
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
if there exist a
KL
-function
\beta
and a
K
-function
\gamma
such that

\|x_{k}\|\leq\beta\bigl(\|x_{0}\|,k\bigr)+\gamma\bigl(\max_{j<k}\|d_{j}\|\bigr)\quad\forall\;k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0+}\;,

for any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{P}
and any disturbances
d_{0},d_{1},\ldots\in\mathbb{D}
.

Definition
15
reduces to
asymptotic stability
if all disturbances vanish beyond some step
k
, i.e.,
d_{j}=0
for all
j\geq k
. If the disturbances are non-zero, the states
x_{k}
are bounded by norm balls, whose size increases with the maximum value of
\|d_{j}\|
observed in the past
j<k
.

Definition 16
(ISS Lyapunov function)

V:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0+}
is called an

for system (
1
) under the state feedback law
\kappa:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{U}

(a) There exist two
\textrm{K}_{\infty}
-functions
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
such that

\alpha_{1}(\|x\|)\leq V\left(x\right)\leq\alpha_{2}(\|x\|)\quad\forall\;x\in\mathcal{P}\;.

(36)

(b) There exists a
\textrm{K}_{\infty}
-function
\alpha_{3}
and
K
-function
\sigma
with

V\left(x_{k+1}\right)\leq V\left(x_{k}\right)-\alpha_{3}(\|x_{k}\|)+\sigma(\|d_{k}\|)

(37)

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0+}
, any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{P}
, and any
d_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
.

Definition
16
reduces to a
Lyapunov function
if the term
\sigma(\|d_{k}\|)
is removed in (
37
).

Lemma 17
(Lyapunov stability)

If there exists a ISS Lyapunov function
V:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0+}
for system (
1
) under some feedback law
\kappa:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{U}
and on some RPI set

Proof:
See
[
28
]
.

Theorem 18
(input-to-state stability)

The system (
1
) under the DRMPC regime
\kappa_{N}:\mathcal{X}_{N}\to\mathbb{U}
in closed loop is ISS on
\mathcal{X}_{N}
.

Proof:
By Lemma
17
, it suffices to show that the optimal value function of the DRMPC problem

is an ISS Lyapunov function in the sense of Definition

Definition
16
(a) is satisfied by virtue of Assumption
14
. Definition
16
(b) is verified as follows. First, for the case of

, the shifted optimal input sequence from the previous step

\displaystyle u_{k+1|k+1}

\displaystyle=u^{\star}_{k+1|k}\;,

\displaystyle\dots\;,

\displaystyle u_{k+N|k+1}

\displaystyle=u^{\star}_{k+N|k}\;,

\displaystyle u_{k+N+1|k+1}

\displaystyle=0

is feasible, and so is the corresponding state sequence. By the standard argument in MPC stability proofs, the optimal value function hence decreases by more than the first stage cost,

Second, for the case of

VII
Conclusion

This paper has proposed a DRMPC for linear systems and for a class of LPV systems subject to parametric uncertainties and additive disturbances. For the LPV system, a nominal trajectory of its future varying parameter is assumed to be given with uncertainty bounds, while its current value is known.
To design a computationally efficient MPC scheme, the contributions of the parametric uncertainty have been lumped together with the additive disturbance, and a worst-case bound for this quantity has been obtained accordingly. Thus, the DRMPC is readily applicable and allowed completely extinguishing the additive disturbances and the effects of uncertainties within the deadbeat horizon.
To reduce online computational complexity with a slight increase in conservatism, the deadbeat inputs are precalculated during the offline design phase, allowing all constraint tightening to be performed offline, leading to a more practical approach for applications in practice.
One of the main advantages of LPV-DRMPC, however, is that no calculation of any PI or RPI sets or terminal costs is needed, as the terminal constraint set is taken as the origin. In comparison with existing approaches, where a tube-based LPV-MPC scheme has been considered, the performance of LPV-DRMPC turned out to be competitive, and the loss of performance could be almost fully recovered by slightly extending the prediction horizon.
This implies that it is straightforward to extend the LPV-DRMPC approach to nonlinear systems embedded in the LPV framework. Moreover, the extension of the entire approach is plausibly worthwhile for data-driven predictive control based solely on system data, offering theoretical guarantees without the need for terminal conditions. The details of these ideas will be explored in future research.

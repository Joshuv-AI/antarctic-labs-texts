A computer-aided diagnosis system of parkinson’s disease based on hilbert spectrum features of speech
Authors: Yuhong Ma, Fangyuan Zheng, Jinye Lu, Juntao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly detection and intervention are very important for the diagnosis and treatment of Parkinson’s disease. Firstly, according to the characteristics of dysarthria and abnormal sensitivity to the pronunciation of words such as ‘[pɑ˥’, ‘pjɑʊ˥’, ‘ pən˥’, and ‘pjɛn˥’ in patients with early Parkinson’s disease, a Chinese tongue twister “[pɑ˥ paɪ⇃ pjɑʊ˥ pɪŋ˥ pən˥ peɪ⇃ pʰo˥, pʰɑʊ˥ ˩pɪŋ˥ pɪŋ˥ pʰaɪ↿ peɪ⇃ pjɛn˥ pʰɑʊ⇃” was designed. The original recordings of Parkinson’s suspected patients were collected in a hospital geriatrics clinic to construct a tongue twister speech database. Secondly, Hilbert transform is introduced to process audio data to obtain the instantaneous amplitude signal of speech, the peak value, peak position, peak width and peak area of each syllable corresponding to the instantaneous amplitude and the total speech length, are extracted to construct the machine learning speech feature data set. Finally, SVM is introduced to train and evaluate the speech features of Parkinson’s disease suspected patients, its performance is compared with those of BP and LSTM models. The results show that the recognition accuracy of the three machine learning models is 92.10%, 88.94% and 90.18%, respectively, which indicates that the speech features extracted in this paper are effective. Combined with instantaneous amplitude features of speech and SVM model, a computer-aided diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease is built. The clinical trial results show that the recognition accuracy of the system for Parkinson’s disease patients can reach 91.43%, and the system has fast response speed and strong anti-interference ability, which can fully assist doctors in making real-time and remote selections of patients with Parkinson’s disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32543-4.Keywords: Parkinson’s disease, Computer-aided diagnosis, Speech signal, Feature extraction, Machine learningSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Neurology, Neuroscience

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease(PD) is a common, chronic and progressive neurodegenerative movement disorder, which is related to the aggregation of abnormal α-synuclein in Lewy bodies and the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigrostriatum. The neurodegenerative mechanisms in PD are poorly understood and there is currently no cure for PD1. In 2021, there were over 3 million Parkinson’s disease patients in China. With the rapid development of the aging population trend, it is estimated that the number of Parkinson’s disease patients will rise to 5 million by 2030, accounting for 50% of patients with Parkinson’s disease worldwide. The prevalence rate among people over 65 years old is as high as 1.7%, and the number of new patients each year is nearly 100,0002. In recent years, Parkinson’s disease has become “younger”, with middle-aged and young patients accounting for 5% to 10% of the total number of patients3, this situation is not optimistic.

Parkinson’s disease symptoms are divided into motor symptoms and non-motor symptoms. Motor symptoms mainly include dysarthria, muscle rigidity, bradykinesia, resting tremor, postural balance disorders, etc., and non-motor symptoms mainly include sensory disorders, sleep disorders, mental disorders, autonomic nerve dysfunction, etc4. As the duration of the disease increases, these symptoms will gradually deepen until the patient completely loses self-control. Parkinson’s disease is characterized by being incurable, having a long course of disease, and a high disability rate, which brings huge psychological pressure and economic burden to patients and their families. Early intervention for Parkinson’s disease can effectively alleviate the malignant development of the disease, so early detection and diagnosis are crucial for patients with Parkinson’s disease. The “2022 Annual Report on the Diagnosis, Treatment and Quality of Life of Parkinson’s Disease Patients in China” shows that due to the uniqueness and complexity of Parkinson’s disease, more than 90% of Parkinson’s disease patients need more than one year to be diagnosed, which indicates that it is urgent to establish an automatic detection system of Parkinson’s disease.

Chinese tongue twisters are a unique linguistic form of the Chinese language. They consist of sentences with repeated, overlapping, and difficult-to-pronounce words that have similar initial consonants, vowels, or tones, and require rapid reading in one breath. Studies have shown that tongue twisters are highly sensitive to articulation disorders. Therefore, this study designed the Chinese tongue twister “[pɑ˥ paɪ⇃ pjɑʊ˥ pɪŋ˥ pən˥ peɪ⇃ pʰo˥, pʰɑʊ˥ ˩pɪŋ˥ pɪŋ˥ pʰaɪ↿ peɪ⇃ pjɛn˥ pʰɑʊ⇃” and collected the voices of suspected Parkinson’s disease patients in the geriatric outpatient department of a certain hospital. The aim was to explore valuable voice features and build a computer-aided diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease by combining machine learning algorithms. Firstly, the collected tongue twister voices of suspected Parkinson’s disease patients were edited and organized in combination with clinical diagnosis to form a voice database. Secondly, the Hilbert transform was introduced to obtain the instantaneous amplitude signal of the voice, and the peak value, peak position, peak width, peak area corresponding to each syllable, and total voice length were extracted to form a voice feature database. Thirdly, three machine learning models SVM, BP and LSTM were introduced to train and evaluate these speech features, and their performances were compared with that based on amplitude features5 and the gray-scale distribution features of the complex value graph of the analytic signal, all of these results will provide a theoretical basis for the construction of the computer-aided diagnosis system. Finally, the SVM model based on the instantaneous amplitude feature was selected to build the computer-aided diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease, and it has been verified through clinical trials that the the recognition accuracy of the system for Parkinson’s disease patients can reach 91.43%, so it is stable and reliable.

Related works

Parkinson’s disease automatic detection methods are mainly divided into regression methods and machine learning methods. In 2015, Bayestehtashk et al.6 used openSMILE to extract speech features of subjects and compared the effectiveness of ridge regression, LASSO regression and support vector regression in helping doctors to infer the severity of Parkinson’s disease patients. In 2018, Camnos-Roca et al.7 extracted acoustic features from continuous vowel recordings and tested the ability of three regularization methods, LASSO regression, ENET regression and ridge regression, to distinguish healthy people from Parkinson’s disease patients. The recognition accuracy rates were 85.6%, 87.1% and 88.5%, respectively.

With the development of artificial intelligence technology, machine learning and deep learning have been widely used in the automatic detection of Parkinson’s disease. For now, Researchers have developed a large number of Parkinson’s disease automatic detection technologies based on machine learning methods, such as artificial neural network(ANN)8, random forest(RF)9, support vector machine(SVM)10, decision tree(DT)11, multilayer perceptron(MLP) and k-nearest neighbor(KNN) network12. In recent years, various deep learning technologies have begun to be applied in the field of automatic detection of Parkinson’s disease, such as the deep dual-side ensemble model based on deep feature learning and deep sample learning13, the end-to-end deep learning model based on convolutional neural network(CNN)14, and the multi- feature fusion model based on deep convolutional neural network(DCNN)15, which significantly improve the detection accuracy of Parkinson’s disease.

According to the different features extracted and trained by the model, machine learning methods of Parkinson’s disease are generally divided into the following four categories.

1) Machine learning based on articulatory features. Studies have shown that 89% of patients with early Parkinson’s disease have dysarthsia16, which is mainly manifested as single tone, low pitch, hoarse voice, low clarity, reduced loudness, reduced pressure, pitch fluctuation, shortness of breath, large pause, and inaccurate pronunciation17. On the one hand, patients with early Parkinson’s disease have insufficient vowel pronunciation and different vocalization durations18,19, especially the vowel articulation index (VAI) is significantly lower than that of non-Parkinson patients of the same age20. Therefore, exploring the unique pronunciation characteristics of vowel letters is the main method for intelligent detection of Parkinson’s disease. On the other hand, The characteristics of Parkinsonian speech was not only a stronger acceleration of articulation rate in the course of speaking but also a significant reduction of the total numbers of pauses, indicating an impaired speech rhythm and timing organization21, then quantitative acoustic analysis of phonological and prosodic disorders through text reading and monologue is also effective to detect Parkinson’s disease patients22, especially stress modification(including interrogative, imperative and declarative sentences) and prosodic language tasks(such as poetry) have higher accuracy in classifying and identifying speech disorders23. Little et al.24 performed feature selection and classification based on the repeated vowel recordings of the subjects, and the overall classification accuracy of Parkinson’s disease patients could reach 91.4%. Mekyska et al.25 analyzed 36 new features of vowel pronunciation, and the recognition accuracy of Parkinson’s disease was 67.9% on the feature dataset of vowel letter ‘a/’. Cai et al.26 established an intelligent diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease based on the continuous pronunciation characteristics of the vowel letter ‘a/’, with an accuracy of 97.24%. Based on the continuous pronunciation characteristics of five vowel letters, Tripathi et al.27 introduced empirical mode decomposition(EMD) and convolutional neural network(CNN) to identify patients with Parkinson’s disease, and the average accuracy was 73.76%. Shibina et al.28 transformed voice inputs into spectrograms by short term Fourier transform(STFT), and then introduced the deep learning architectures, such as convolutional neural networks(CNN) and long short-term memory(LSTM) networks to identify Parkinson’s disease, the accuracy was 95.67%. Montaña et al.10 extracted the temporal and spectral features of the amplitude of consonant ‘k/’ and two plosive consonants ‘p/’ and ‘t/’ to identify patients with Parkinson’s disease. The Orozco-Arroyave et al.29 evaluated the ability of four speech tasks: isolated words, rapidly repeated syllables ‘/pa/-/ta/-/ka/’, sentences, and spoken text to automatically distinguish patients with Parkinson’s disease in three languages: Spanish, German, Czech. Bocklet et al.30 fused speech, pronunciation, prosody and language features to detect Parkinson’s disease patients, and the accuracy rate was 80%. Vasquez-Correa et al.31 developed the m-FDA scale based on multiple language tasks, which refines the scoring criteria of Parkinson’s disease patients into 13 items based on articulation, speech, prosody and comprehension.

2) Machine learning based on gait feature. Gait abnormality is one of the core motor symptoms in patients with Parkinson’s disease, which is mainly manifested as reduced stride length, drag walking, accelerated step frequency, frozen gait and postural instability, then gait features can be extracted to recognize the patients with Parkinson’s disease. Balaji et al.32 developed a motor gait classification system for early detection and stage classification of Parkinson’s disease using four machine learning models, including decision tree(DT), support vector machine(SVM), ensemble classification and Bayesian classification. Vidya et al.33 introduced empirical mode decomposition(EMD), convolutional neural network (CNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) network into gait signal learning for the diagnosis and classification prediction of Parkinson’s disease patients. Ferreira et al.34 introduced the naive Bayes algorithm into spatio-temporal gait parameter learning to detect Parkinson’s disease patients, with an accuracy of 84.6%. Aydn et al.35 developed a classification system of gait pattern for Parkinson’s disease patients by use of advanced ensemble learning algorithm and Hilbert-Huang transform, with an accuracy of 98.79%.

3) Machine learning based on brain image features. The study found that brain images of patients with Parkinson’s disease showed the loss of the normal “dovetail” on magnetic susceptibility sequences, which was related to the absence of nigrosome-1 in the substantia nigra. Recently, Hazhirkarzar et al.36 developed a machine learning model based on baseline dopamine transporter single photon emission computed tomography(DAT-SPECT) imaging and multimodal magnetic resonance imaging(MRI) for predicting the progression of Parkinson’s disease, with an accuracy of 90%, a sensitivity of 1.00, and a specificity of 0.62. Bhakar et al.37 established an automatic detection model of Parkinson’s disease based on brain SPECT image features by introducing artificial neural network, with an accuracy of 93%, a sensitivity of 1.00, and a specificity of 0.86.

4) Machine learning based on biochemical features. The researches found that the biochemical indicators of Parkinson’s patients may change, especially have a decrease in dopamine levels in the brain. In 2010, the Michael J. Fox Foundation and a core team of scientists and industry partners launched the Parkinson’s Progression Marker Initiative (PPMI) to create a robust open-access database; Based on the PPMI database, the research team combined multiple advanced machine learning techniques and multi-modal fusion strategies to develop an adaptive ensemble stacking model to improve the early recognition and diagnosis ability of Parkinson’s disease38. In 2020, Wang et al.39 developed an innovative deep learning technology to detect patients with early Parkinson’s disease by taking into account multiple indicators, such as rapid eye movement period, anosmia, cerebrospinal fluid data and dopaminergic imaging substances, with an accuracy of 96.45%. In 2025, the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) and Quadram Institute in the UK integrated 22 independent research teams from 11 countries, and used a variety of mainstream machine learning algorithms to systematically evaluate the value and generalization ability of gut microbiota characteristics in the diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease40.

The construction of feature database is also a hot topic in Parkinson’s disease computer-aided diagnosis system. In 2007, Little et al.24 established the Oxford Parkinson’s disease dataset(OPDD). In 2010, the Parkinsons telemonitoring data set (PTDS) collecting audio signals through telephone interviews was formally formed41. In 2013, Sakar et al.42 established a Parkinson’s disease audio training database consisting of 26 speech samples, each of which contained linear and time-frequency features of vowels, numbers, words and short sentences. In 2024, Ma et al.5 constructed the Parkinson’s phonetic features on tongue twister(PPFTT) data set by extracting features from the speech amplitude signal of Chinese tongue twistel. In 2025, Matsumono et al.43 extracted kinematic statistical features such as rhythm, amplitude, frequency, frequency standard deviation, slope, and established a feature databases for identifying Parkinson’s disease patients.

Methods

Data source

Four Chinese words such as ‘[pɑ˥’, ’pjɑʊ˥’, ‘pən˥’, and ‘pjɛn˥’ were selected from the “Mandarin Articulation Disorder Assessment Word List44” jointly produced by Jinan University in China, the University of Central Arkansas in the United States, and Ohio State University to form the tongue twister “[pɑ˥ paɪ⇃ pjɑʊ˥ pɪŋ˥ pən˥ peɪ⇃ pʰo˥, pʰɑʊ⇃ pɪŋ˥ pɪŋ˥ pʰaɪ↿ peɪ⇃ pjɛn˥ pʰɑʊ⇃”. From April 3 to November 30, 2023, suspected Parkinson’s disease patients who could speak standard mandarin and were willing to cooperate with the experiment were selected in the geriatric outpatient department of a certain hospital. They recorded the tongue twister in a relatively quiet environment. After editing and sorting, a total of 97 audio recordings were collected to form a voice database5, among which 67 belonged to Parkinson’s disease patients (including 42 males and 25 females, with an average age of 72.5 years) and 30 belonged to non-Parkinson’s disease patients (including 18 males and 12 females, with an average age of 71.2 years). All voice data met the following requirements: (1) Completeness. The voice was basically complete, with more than 10 syllables clearly distinguishable, and the recording length did not exceed 15 s; (2) Clarity. The voice was clear and distinguishable, and environmental noise did not cause significant interference; (3) Validity. Other articulation disorder diseases of the patients, such as senile dementia, stuttering, and Alzheimer’s disease, were completely excluded.

This experiment was conducted in accordance with the relevant operation guidelines and regulations. Therefore, the participants were fully aware of the purpose of the experiment, and we confirm that the informed consent has been obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardians.

Speech preprocessing and feature selection

Speech preprocessing

Signals are usually divided into stationary signals and non-stationary signals. The distribution law or distribution parameters of non-stationary signals will change with time, that is, the statistical characteristics of samples such as expectation and variance are different in different time periods. In contrast, the statistical properties of stationary signals do not change over time45. The vast majority of signals in nature are non-stationary signals, and speech signals are also generally non-stationary signals.

The Fourier transform(FT) converts time-domain signals into frequency-domain signals, but it cannot capture the local frequency characteristics that change over time and is only suitable for processing stationary signals. To handle non-stationary signals, time-frequency analysis of the signal is required46. The short-time Fourier transform(STFT) is a two-dimensional function concerning time and frequency. Through STFT, narrowband spectrograms with high frequency resolution and wideband spectrograms with strong time resolution can be obtained. However, the STFT has a fixed frame length globally and can only provide the same frequency and time resolution. To display high frequency resolution in the low-frequency region and time resolution in the high-frequency region, the wavelet transform(WT)47 is needed. The WT processes the time-varying positive and negative frequency attenuation into finite-length wavelets, showing local frequency and time resolution characteristics. However, like STFT, it is limited by the Heisenberg uncertainty principle and cannot precisely distinguish between frequency and time. Moreover, it requires the manual setting of basic functions and can’t adaptively adjust. The Hilbert transform (HT) is an extension of the FT and can perform time-frequency analysis on signals48. Signals are classified into single-frequency signals and multi-frequency signals based on their frequency characteristics. Single-frequency signals only emit and receive one band of frequencies. HT mainly assesses the instantaneous frequency of single-frequency signals and has no requirements for the positive or negative nature of the original signal, allowing it to handle signals with all non-negative frequencies.

Feature selection of speech signal

Syllable is the most basic speech unit, and each syllable corresponds to a Chinese character. The Chinese tongue twister “[pɑ˥ paɪ⇃ pjɑʊ˥ pɪŋ˥ pən˥ peɪ⇃ pʰo˥, pʰɑʊ˥ ˩pɪŋ˥ pɪŋ˥ pʰaɪ↿ peɪ⇃ pjɛn˥ pʰɑʊ⇃” consists of 14 Chinese characters, and the corresponding speech signal has 14 peaks. The peak value, peak position, peak width and peak area of the 14 peaks corresponding to the instantaneous amplitude signal of speech are selected in this paper. Together with the total length of the speech, a total of 57 features form the feature vector.

The amplitude signal of speech has obvious physical meaning, and the speech length describes the length of time for the collected tongue twisters to be recorded. The pronunciation of Parkinson’s disease patients is accompanied by hesitation and stuttering, which prolongs the speech length. The peak value of speech reflects whether the voice is loud and whether the voice is strong. The increased muscle tension of patients with Parkinson’s disease leads to unstable breath and low loudness when speaking, which is significantly different from that of non-Parkinson’s disease(non-PD) patients. The peak width of speech reflects the coherence and fluency of speech. The breath of Parkinson’s disease patients is not stable, and the characteristics of single tone, elongation, long interval between syllables, and vagueness of words are not uniform, and the peak width is generally higher than that of non-PD patients. The peak area describes the energy of syllables. Because the energy size of each syllable in PD patients is generally less balanced than that in non-PD patients, because the speech of PD patients is accompanied by plosive sounds and the breath is strong and weak. From the speech amplitude and instantaneous amplitude of PD patients and non-PD patients(Fig. 1), it can be found that in the amplitude and instantaneous amplitude of PD patients, the waveform of each syllable is thin, the line coherence is poor, the volume suddenly increases, plosive sounds or syllables do not appear, and the pause gap between syllables is long, and the fluency is weak. In the amplitude and instantaneous amplitude of non-PD patients, the syllables were full, the wave area was large, the sample contained syllables at a glance, the connections between syllables were compact, and the overall length was generally smaller than that of PD patients. In general, there are obvious differences in the balance of peak value, peak energy and peak width, and the distribution uniformity of peak positions, so it is meaningful to use them as numerical features to identify PD patients and non-PD patients.

Amplitude and instantaneous amplitude of speech signal on tongue twister.

In this study, first, we refer to reference5 to extract 6-dimensional features of speech amplitude as the first set of features, and then extract gray distribution features of complex- valued map as the second set of features, and extract 57-dimensional features of instantaneous amplitude as the third set of features, and then introduced SVM, BP and LSTM models to train and evaluate the effectiveness of the features. Finally, the instantaneous amplitude features and SVM machine learning model were used to construct the computer-aided diagnosis system of Parkinson’s disease.

More than 75%, but the performance is still not satisfactory. Comprehensive consideration, the SVM model based on instantaneous amplitude features has the best recognition performance, the highest recognition accuracy is 100%, the lowest is 89.47%, and the average is 92.1%. The variance of 20 independent experiments is only 5.11%, which indicates that the SVM model has stable performance.

Computer aided diagnosis system

System architecture

With the rapid development of science and technology, the application of artificial intelligence is increasing day by day. More and more machine learning models are used in the medical field to assist doctors to make more accurate and objective diagnosis results based on clinical practice and patient symptoms. By comparing the recognition performance of SVM, BP and LSTM models based on amplitude features, instantaneous amplitude features and gray distribution features of complex-valued image, the SVM model based on instantaneous amplitude features is selected to construct a computer-aided diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease. The system consists of four main modules:

1) Data preprocessing module. Firstly, the instantaneous amplitude signal of every original speech of suspected PD patients is obtained by Hilbert transform. Secondly, we perform normalization on the instantaneous amplitude. Finally, after further compression, noise reduction, reconstruction, and cutting operations, the 14 largest syllable signals of the normalized instantaneous amplitude signal are given.

2) Feature extraction module. Calculated the peak value, peak position, peak energy, and peak width of each syllable signal, together with the total length of the speech, it constitutes the 57-dimensional feature vector of the speech. The speech feature database is constructed by combining the patient’s labels given by the doctor’s clinical diagnosis.

3) Machine learning module. SVM is used to train the extracted instantaneous amplitude features, optimize the model structure and parameters, and obtain a satisfactory SVM classification model.

4) System application module. The tongue twister speech signal of the tester recorded in real-time was input into the diagnosis system. The system automatically extracts the instantaneous amplitude features and outputs the diagnosis results. The speech signal features of the tester and the computer diagnosis results were evaluated and confirmed by the clinicians and then imported into the speech feature database. When the scale of new speech data reaches the set threshold, the system automatically starts SVM re-training to improve the universality and accuracy of the model.

System configuration

In this study, data preprocessing, feature extraction, model training and system application are all carried out in the Matlab environment. The system is basically configured with Intel i7 processor or AMDx86-64 dual-core processor with 16GB memory, and the graphics card supports Open-GL3.3 and has 1GBGPU memory. Commonly used desktop computer or notebook computer can meet the system operation requirements.

System application

Open the system application interface in the Matlab environment, set the sampling frequency of 15 kHz and the time of 15s. In preparation time, testers first familiarize themselves with the tongue twister information and precautions, and then the recording was completed in a quiet environment, the system automatically completes the preprocessing and feature extraction of the recording data, and then pouring into the diagnosis system and output the diagnosis results. The specific process is: the command window output rules prompt “Please read the tongue twister within 15 s, [pɑ˥ paɪ⇃ pjɑʊ˥ pɪŋ˥ pən˥ peɪ⇃ pʰo˥, pʰɑʊ˥ ˩pɪŋ˥ pɪŋ˥ pʰaɪ↿ peɪ⇃ pjɛn˥ pʰɑʊ⇃”. Followed by the command, “Are you ready to start recording?” If you type “no”, the rule prompt is output again. If you type “yes”, the system will display “start recording” after 3 s, and voice prompt “end recording, thank you for your cooperation” after 15s. The recording is saved as a wav document by the system program ‘audiorecorder’, which is automatically imported into the diagnosis system after preprocessing and feature extraction to obtain the classification label. If the label is 0, the command window output prompts “You may be a patient with Parkinson’s disease, please go to the hospital for diagnosis as soon as possible!” If the label is 1, the output says, “You don’t have Parkinson’s disease!” After the diagnosis is completed, the system automatically imports the instantaneous amplitude features and diagnostic labels of tester’s speech into the speech feature database, and the system will automatically start the retraining process for each additional 20 data samples in the database in order to increase the accuracy and reliability of the diagnosis system.

The system was tested on suspected Parkinson’s disease patients in the outpatient clinic of a hospital from June 3 to September 30, 2024, and 32 Parkinson’s disease patients were identified from 35 suspected patients (21 males and 14 females) with an accuracy of 91.43%, and the average response time of the system was 3.5s. It shows that the computer-aided diagnosis system of Parkinson’s disease has rapid response and high accuracy, which can effectively assist doctors in real-time or remote screening of Parkinson’s disease patients, and clinicians can give targeted diagnosis and treatment opinions after comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s condition.

During the deployment of the computer-aided diagnosis system for Parkinson’s disease, we conducted follow-up visits with 20 patients and physicians who had used the system for auxiliary diagnosis purposes. We also collected and analyzed 10 user reviews regarding the application. All participants rated the system above 90 points across three key dimensions: safety, stability, and usability.

Conclusion

In this study, according to the characteristics of dysarthsia and abnormal sensitivity to tongue twisters in the early stage of Parkinson’s disease, the instantaneous amplitude features of speech signal based on Hilbert transform were used for SVM model, and a computer-aided diagnosis system of Parkinson’s disease was constructed. The trial test results show that the system can assist doctors in real-time, remote and accurate screening of patients with early Parkinson’s disease.

At present, the system still has the following shortcomings:

1) The data is unbalanced, the test results are somewhat dependent on language tasks, and the dysarthrias caused by other diseases cannot be completely excluded, such as mild stutter and mild Alzheimer’s disease.

2) The validity and accuracy of the model still lack of long-term test, the reasons for system diagnosis errors need further analysis, and the system needs to be optimized and improved in the later stage.

3) The sample size is small, and the information collection is susceptible to environmental noise, which affects the performance of the system.

4) A significant investment of time is needed to augment the model’s training data in order to enhance its generalization capability and mitigate the adverse effects of local dialects and other variables on diagnostic accuracy.

Future research can consider increasing the sample size and extracting more numerical features with physical meaning in the speech for classification learning. When the sample reaches a certain level, the severity of Parkinson’s disease patients can also be graded to improve the pertinency and effectiveness of clinicians’ diagnosis and treatment. In the later stage, it can be considered to make the system into a simple mobile phone APP, and the doctor or the relatives of patient can conduct the screening of Parkinson’s disease by themselves, so as to achieve early detection, early diagnosis and early treatment, all of this will be helpful to delay the process of the disease, reduce the patient’s physical pain, psychological pressure and economic burden.

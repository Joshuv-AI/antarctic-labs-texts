Targeted search for an individual SMBHB in NANOGrav 15-year and EPTA DR2 data sets
Li-Wen Tian, Yan-Chen Bi, Yu-Mei Wu, Qing-Guo Huang

Abstract
While pulsar timing array (PTA) collaborations have reported evidence for a stochastic gravitational wave background (GWB), the detection of continuous gravitational waves (GWs) from a confirmed supermassive black hole binary (SMBHB) would provide strong support for the SMBHB origin of GWB. In this study, we analyze continuous GWs from the SMBHB candidate 3C 66B, modeling the GWB as a common uncorrelated red noise. Using Bayesian methods, we perform targeted searches across two PTA data sets: Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves 15 years data set and the European Pulsar Timing Array DR2 full data set. We find no evidence of such signal in both data sets and then place upper limits on the amplitude of the signal and the chirp mass of the source. Additionally, we evaluate the case of a GWB characterized by Hellings–Downs correlations using a likelihood reweighting method, which consistently reconfirms the conclusion of non-detection.



I
Introduction

Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs) detect nanohertz (nHz) gravitational waves (GWs) by measuring timing residuals—the differences between observed times of arrival (TOAs) from radio pulsar pulses and predictions from a theoretical timing model
Sazhin 1978
;
Detweiler 1979
;
Foster and Backer 1990
. Recently, PTA collaborations such as the North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves (NANOGrav,
McLaughlin 2013
;
Agazie et al. 2023a
), the European Pulsar Timing Array (EPTA,
Desvignes et al. 2016
;
Antoniadis et al. 2023a
), the Parkes Pulsar Timing Array (PPTA,
Zic et al. 2023
;
Reardon et al. 2023
) and the Chinese Pulsar Timing Array (CPTA,
Xu et al. 2023
) have reported evidence for an nHz stochastic gravitational wave background (GWB).
The observed GWB, characterized by the Hellings–Downs (HD) correlation
Hellings and Downs 1983
across pulsars, is consistent with a cosmic population of inspiraling supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHBs), which lose orbital energy through GW emission
Bi et al. 2023
. However, alternative scenarios, such as cosmological scalar-induced GWs from primordial black holes
Chen et al. 2020
;
Yuan and Huang 2021
, could also explain the signal
Burke-Spolaor et al. 2019
;
Afzal et al. 2023
;
Ellis et al. 2024
;
Wu et al. 2024
. Hence, a direct detection of continuous GWs from an individual SMBHB would provide strong evidence for the SMBHB origin of the GWB. With longer observational baselines and improved instrumental precision, such detections are becoming increasingly feasible
Xin et al. 2021
, particularly for massive, nearby SMBHBs
Rosado et al. 2015
;
Bécsy et al. 2022
.

Moreover, a confirmed GW detection of individual SMBHB would provide critical insights for both theory and observation.
For example, it could illuminate the longstanding “final parsec problem”—how bound SMBHBs efficiently merge within cosmological timescales
Milosavljevic and Merritt 2003
;
Dvorkin and Barausse 2017
.
In addition, multi-messenger observations of such a system would place stringent constraints on the physical processes that govern the merger dynamics
Liu and Vigeland 2021
;
Charisi et al. 2022
. Finally, an individual SMBHB would serve as a standard siren, enabling independent measurements of cosmological parameters
Kelley et al. 2019
.

While electromagnetic observations could provide evidence of the existence and properties of SMBHBs candidates in active galaxies
Charisi et al. 2022
;
Westernacher-Schneider et al. 2022
, typical all-sky searches for individual SMBHB GW signals are conducted independently
Agazie et al. 2023b
. However, by incorporating prior knowledge from electromagnetic measurements, so-called targeted searches could enhance the detection probabilities of SMBHBs and place tighter upper limits on their parameters
Arzoumanian et al. 2020
;
Liu and Vigeland 2021
. These improvements are particularly significant when the SMBHB candidate is located in sky regions covered by a higher density of well-timed pulsars
Liu and Vigeland 2021
.

The radio galaxy 3C 66B with redshift
z=0.020920
has been proposed to host an SMBHB candidate based on its radio core and jet properties
van den Bosch et al. 2015
;
Hardcastle et al. 2001
;
De Paolis et al. 2004
;
Sudou et al. 2003
.
Previous electromagnetic observations placed upper limits on the system’s chirp mass of
1.3\times 10^{10}M_{\odot}

Sudou et al. 2003
and
7.0\times 10^{8}M_{\odot}

Iguchi et al. 2010
respectively.
In 2003, the research
Jenet et al. 2004
utilizing pulsar timing data from a single pulsar, B1855+09, determines a chirp mass upper limit of
7\times 10^{9}M_{\odot}
under a circular orbit assumption, and noted that a higher upper limit may be possible for an eccentric orbit.
The NANOGrav Collaboration has specifically investigated potential GW emission from 3C 66B through targeted searches in their data set
Arzoumanian et al. 2020
;
Agazie et al. 2024
.
For a circular orbit, the upper limit on the chirp mass derived from the NANOGrav 11-year data set is
(1.65\pm 0.02)\times 10^{9}M_{\odot}

Arzoumanian et al. 2020
.
In the NANOGrav 12.5-year data set (hereafter NG12.5), assuming an eccentric orbit, the upper limits are
(1.98\pm 0.05)\times 10^{9}M_{\odot}
for the Earth-term-only search and
(1.89\pm 0.01)\times 10^{9}M_{\odot}
for the combined Earth-term and Pulsar-term search
Agazie et al. 2024
.

In this work, we extend the targeted search for a putative SMBHB in 3C 66B using the NANOGrav 15-year (hereafter NG15) and EPTA DR2 full data sets (hereafter DR2full). For computational efficiency, the GWB is modeled as a common uncorrelated red noise. We find no evidence of such a signal in either data set and derive upper limits on the signal amplitude and the chirp mass of the source. We further compare our results with previous NANOGrav studies and examine the impact of an HD-correlated GWB on the analysis.

Model Comparison

For model comparison, we evaluate the Bayes factor using the Savage–Dickey formula

;
Verde et al. 2013

The derivation of this formula is outlined below.

Suppose we have two models,
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, and an observational data set

Both models share a set of base parameters

Furthermore, model
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
includes additional parameters

p(\bm{\theta},\bm{\zeta}|{\mathcal{H}}_{1})=p(\bm{\theta}|{\mathcal{H}}_{1})p(\bm{\zeta}|{\mathcal{H}}_{1})

As a result, model
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
is nested within
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, corresponding to the special case where
\bm{\zeta}
takes specific values
\bm{\zeta}=\bm{\zeta_{1}}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
.
Accordingly, the likelihood satisfies
p(\mathcal{D}|\bm{\theta},{\mathcal{H}}_{0})=p(\mathcal{D}|\bm{\theta},\bm{\zeta_{1}},{\mathcal{H}}_{1})
.

The Bayes factor is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{10}

V
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we conduct a targeted search for continuous GW signals from the SMBHB candidate 3C 66B using the NG15 and EPTA DR2full data sets
Agazie et al. 2024
;
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
Antoniadis et al. 2023a
.
Applying Bayesian analysis, we find no evidence for such signals and consequently derive
95\%
upper limits on the chirp mass
M_{c}
and the fiducial signal amplitude
S_{0}
.
The Bayes factor results are summarized in Table
2
, while the derived upper limits are reported in Table
3
, together with the corresponding constraints from the NG12.5 analysis
Agazie et al. 2024
.

The above results are based on modeling the GWB as a CURN, whereas both data sets actually favor an HD-correlated GWB over CURN
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
Antoniadis et al. 2023a
. Although computational constraints prevent us from directly implementing a full HD-correlated common red noise in the search, we can instead use a likelihood reweighting technique to estimate the evidence for an HD-correlated GWB, as previously applied in the NG15 all-sky search for individual SMBHBs
Agazie et al. 2023b
.

Based on the correlation structure of
n_{\text{CRN}}
and the presence of
R
in the residual models Eq. (
11
) and Eq. (
12
), we consider four models:
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{c}}
(CURN only), including only a common uncorrelated red-noise process;
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{h}}
(HD only), including only an HD-correlated GWB;
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
(CURN + SMBHB), combining a CURN process with an individual SMBHB signal; and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
(HD + SMBHB), combining an HD-correlated GWB with an individual SMBHB signal.
The likelihood reweighting method evaluates Bayes factors for
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
using posterior samples from
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
. For a given set of samples, weights are defined as the ratio of the likelihood under
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
to that under
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
, and the Bayes factor is estimated as the average of these weights
Hourihane et al. 2023
. In our analysis, we reweight posterior samples from
\mathcal{H}_{\text{cr}}
obtained in Section
IV
. For NG15, the Bayes factors of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
are 1.00 in both Eterm and EPterm searches; for DR2full, they are 1.51 (Eterm) and 1.50 (EPterm), all with uncertainties below 0.01.

In addition, from the NG15 and EPTA DR2full GWB analyses, the Bayes factors of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{h}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{c}}
are 226 and 4, respectively
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
Antoniadis et al. 2023a
. Combining these with the
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{c}}
results from Section
IV
, we further derive the Bayes factors of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{h}}
. For NG15, this ratio is below 0.01 in both Eterm and EPterm searches, while for DR2full it is 0.29 for both. Compared to the previous
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{cr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{c}}
results, the Bayes factors for
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{h}}
are substantially suppressed. Similar reductions are observed in the NG15 and EPTA DR2 all-sky analyses. This effect likely arises because the GWB and SMBHB signals are partially covariant, since both contribute to the HD correlation
Cornish and Sesana 2013
;
Antoniadis et al. 2024
, as indicated by the fact that the evidence for an HD-correlated GWB is also significantly reduced when an SMBHB signal is included
Agazie et al. 2023b
.

While the NG15 all-sky search reveals tentative evidence for two candidate GW signals at
\sim 4
nHz and
\sim 170
nHz
Agazie et al. 2023b
, and the EPTA DR2 analysis also identifies one significant signal at
\sim 4
nHz
Antoniadis et al. 2024
, both results are obtained under the CURN framework, and the evidence diminishes when HD correlations are included in the CRN model. Notably, neither candidate appears near the expected frequency of 3C 66B. Moreover, as the Bayes factors (
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{hr}}
versus
{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{h}}
) remain consistently below 1, our conclusion of no detectable GW signal from 3C 66B remains unchanged.

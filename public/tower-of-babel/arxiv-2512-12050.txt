An unfitted divergence-free higher order finite element method for the Stokes problem Thanks: M.N. was supported in part by the NSF, grant DMS-2309425. M.O. was supported in part by the NSF, grant DMS-2408978.
Michael Neilan, Maxim Olshanskii, Henry von Wahl

Abstract
. The paper develops and analyzes a higher-order unfitted finite element method for the incompressible Stokes equations, which yields a strongly divergence-free velocity field up to the physical boundary. The method combines an isoparametric Scott–Vogelius velocity–pressure pair on a cut background mesh with a stabilized Nitsche/Lagrange multiplier formulation for imposing Dirichlet boundary conditions. We construct finite element spaces that admit robust numerical implementation using standard elementwise polynomial mappings and produce exactly divergence-free discrete velocities. The key components of the analysis are a new inf-sup stability result for the isoparametric Scott–Vogelius pair on unfitted meshes and a combined inf-sup stability result for the bilinear forms associated with the pressure and the Lagrange multiplier. The finite element formulation employs a higher-order Lagrange multiplier space, which ensures stability and mitigates the loss of pressure robustness typically associated with the weak enforcement of boundary conditions for the normal velocity component. The paper provides a complete stability and convergence theory in two dimensions, accounting for the geometric errors introduced by the isoparametric approximation. The analysis demonstrates optimal-order velocity convergence in both the H 1 H^{1} and L 2 L^{2} norms and establishes optimal H 1 H^{1} -convergence and nearly optimal L 2 L^{2} -convergence of a post-processed pressure. Numerical experiments illustrate and confirm the theoretical findings.



1.
Introduction

Geometrically unfitted finite element methods have become widely accepted as a strong alternative to classical approaches based on body-fitted meshes, particularly
when the partial differential equations are posed on
complex or deforming domains. In this paper, we focus on a stabilized Nitsche-type unfitted FEM, also known as Nitsche–XFEM or CutFEM
[
9
,
4
]
. The method was originally introduced for elliptic interface problems but has since rapidly evolved and been extended in various directions, including parabolic and hyperbolic interface problems and PDEs posed on time-dependent domains; see the recent review
[
8
]
.

Among other applications, the development of CutFEM formulations for viscous incompressible fluid flow has been the subject of numerous studies (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
30
,
20
,
22
,
18
,
1
,
31
,
5
,
42
,
33
]
). However, the unfitted finite element methods proposed in these works generally fail to produce a strongly solenoidal velocity field—an important property for ensuring that the discrete solution satisfies the correct physical balance laws. There are at least two reasons for this limitation. First, most popular finite element spaces are originally designed to enforce the incompressibility constraint only weakly. Second, the ghost-penalty terms introduced in CutFEM to guarantee algebraic stability typically perturb the divergence constraint.

For the reasons outlined above, the development of
unfitted divergence-free
finite elements has only recently been addressed in several studies in the context of the Darcy and Stokes equations. The Darcy interface problem was addressed in
[
14
]
using
\mbox{RT}_{k}\times Q_{k}
pairs together with a special pressure stabilization that guarantees pointwise divergence-free approximations and optimal convergence. The subsequent work
[
15
]
extended these ideas to the Darcy problem with unfitted outer boundaries by employing a stabilized Lagrange multiplier formulation for essential boundary conditions, although only the lowest-order case was analyzed. A closely related method which also included a post-processing step for the scalar variable was introduced and analyzed in
[
25
]
. All these studies assumed exact integration over cut elements.

For the Stokes problem,
[
29
]
proposed a CutFEM based on the Scott–Vogelius pair, ensuring both finite element and algebraic stability through ghost-penalty terms for velocity and pressure, and achieving a discrete velocity field that is divergence-free outside an
O(h)
neighborhood of the boundary. The study
[
13
]
introduced two H(div)-conforming CutFEM formulations employing Brezzi–Douglas–Marini and Raviart–Thomas spaces, which produce pointwise divergence-free velocities and well-conditioned systems independent of the boundary position, though without providing a theoretical error analysis and assuming exact numerical integration over cut elements. The authors of
[
6
]
presented a low-order divergence-free CutFEM for the Stokes problem, employing two variants of a stabilized Lagrange multiplier method to impose Dirichlet conditions. The paper established optimal error estimates independent of cut geometry; however, the study in
[
6
]
was limited to low-order discretizations and assumed exact integration over cut elements.

Thus, a provably stable and higher-order accurate CutFEM for the Stokes problem that yields a strongly divergence-free solution up to the boundary remains an open challenge. The present study addresses this challenge with an isoparametric
Scott–Vogelius unfitted finite element method, in which the
the discrete velocity space consists of (mapped) piecewise polynomials of degree
k
, and the discrete pressure space consists of (mapped) piecewise polynomials of degree
k-1
.

The method introduced here benefits from several recent advancements in handling immersed and curvilinear interfaces within a finite element framework, namely: a Nitsche/Lagrange multiplier formulation from
[
6
]
, an isoparametric approximation technique for level set domains
[
26
]
, and a modification of the isoparametric Scott–Vogelius element from
[
32
,
10
]
.
The end result is a higher-order, stable, and optimally convergent
scheme with an exactly divergence-free velocity approximation in
both the physical and computational domain.

While these ingredients form the basis of the construction of the proposed discretization, the precise definition of our finite element spaces as well as the stability and convergence analysis requires new techniques beyond those
found in the literature. First, our definition of the proposed Scott-Vogelius
finite element space fundamentally differs from the approach given in
[
32
,
10
]
. The definition of the Scott-Vogelius finite element spaces given
in those works are developed within a macro-element framework, with mapped reference basis functions defined on local Alfeld partitions.
Although this viewpoint is advantageous for the stability analysis, it results in a technically involved and cumbersome implementation.
Rather, we define the Scott-Vogelius space with respect
to an isoparametric split mesh, mapped via standard polynomial
basis functions. As a result, the proposed spaces can be implemented
using existing finite element software packages such as NGSolve
[
38
]
.
On the other hand, the stability proof of the proposed velocity-pressure finite element pair
is considerably more involved and one of the focuses of this paper.

Second, we do not assume exact integration over cut elements and incorporate
geometric error in the scheme and analysis.
Following the approach of
[
26
]
, we approximate the domain by a continuous, piecewise-polynomial diffeomorphism, which yields an approximate geometry
with
O(h^{k+1})
geometric error.
This geometric perturbation enters the analysis in a nontrivial way, particularly in establishing the inf–sup stability of the Scott–Vogelius pair,
as well the derivation of optimal-order error estimates.

Third, relative to
[
6
]
, we utilize a potentially higher-order Lagrange multiplier space for the pressure trace and use continuous, piecewise polynomials.
We show that this choice, together with the Scott-Vogelius pair, yields
a stable method. Moreover, the higher-order Lagrange multiplier space
mitigates the lack of pressure robustness inherent in schemes with weakly imposed boundary conditions
[
21
,
6
,
13
]
.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

The material in the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
formulates the Stokes problem and several integral identities satisfied by the solution, which we exploit to construct the finite element method. Section
3
introduces simplicial meshes, the necessary geometric preliminaries, and the finite element spaces. Section
4
presents the finite element formulation, consisting of the main scheme for approximating the fluid velocity, pressure boundary trace, and an auxiliary pressure, complemented by a post-processing step that recovers the physical pressure. We show that the finite element velocity is pointwise divergence-free.

Section
5
contains the stability analysis of the finite element method. It begins with the key inf–sup pressure stability result for the (isoparametric) Scott–Vogelius element, whose proof is provided in Appendix
A
. The section then establishes the stability of the discrete Lagrange multiplier and, subsequently, the stability of the full bilinear form. Extending stability from the pressure variable to the entire formulation is nontrivial and requires careful scaling arguments. The error analysis is completed in Section
6
. The main theorem, Theorem
6.3
, proves convergence of the finite element solutions to specific projections of the physical quantities onto the discrete spaces, from which the optimal
H^{1}
error estimate for the velocity follows under sufficient regularity of the data. For the post-processed finite element pressure, Theorem
6.6
establishes optimal convergence in the
H^{1}
norm and half-order suboptimal convergence in the
L^{2}
norm.

Finally, Section
7
illustrates the analysis and assesses the performance of the isoparametric Scott–Vogelius element on a series of numerical examples. The results confirm the optimal order of accuracy for the finite element velocity and also suggest that the post-processed pressure is optimally accurate in the
L^{2}
norm.

2.
The Stokes Problem

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, with a smooth boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. The Stokes system will serve here as a model problem: Find a velocity field
\bm{u}
and pressure
p
, such that

(1)

\left\{\begin{split}-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p&=\bm{f}&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\bm{u}&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma\end{split}\right.

holds. To avoid a non-unique pressure, we add
\int_{\Omega}p\dif\bm{x}=0
.
Assume that the “physical” domain
\Omega
is embedded in an ambient polytopal “computational” domain
{{\Omega}^{\text{ext}}}
.

For the purpose of convergence analysis, we assume that
\bm{u}\in{{\bm{H}}^{m}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=}H^{m}(\Omega)^{d}
,
p\in H^{m-1}(\Omega)
for a suitable positive exponent
m
.
Let
d=3
: Since
\bm{u}
vanishes on
\Gamma
and is solenoidal, there exists a vector potential
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{m+1}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{u}=\nabla\times{\bm{v}}
. One can extend
{\bm{v}}
from
\Omega
to
{{\Omega}^{\text{ext}}}
in
{\bm{H}}^{m+1}

[
40
]
, which provides us with a
{\bm{H}}^{m}
-continuous solenoidal extension
{\bm{u}}^{e}
defined on
{{\Omega}^{\text{ext}}}
. Similar argument works for velocity in
d=2
. For the pressure function we shall consider the extension by
zero
outside
\overline{\Omega}
, which we denote by
p^{z}
.

Let us introduce the trace of the pressure on the boundary
\Gamma
as auxiliary variable
\lambda={\left.\kern-1.2ptp\right|_{\Gamma}}
.
It is easy to see that the extended solution satisfy the integral identity

(2)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\bm{u}^{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\nabla\bm{v}\dif\bm{x}-\int_{\Gamma}((\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}^{e})\cdot\bm{v}\dif s{-\int_{\Gamma}((\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v})\cdot\bm{u}^{e}\dif s}+\gamma\int_{\Gamma}\bm{u}^{e}\cdot\bm{v}\dif s\\
-\int_{{{\Omega}^{\text{ext}}}}p^{z}\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\dif\bm{x}-\int_{{\Omega}^{\text{ext}}}q\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{e}\dif\bm{x}+\int_{\Gamma}\lambda(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\dif s+\int_{\Gamma}\mu(\bm{u}^{e}\cdot\bm{n})\dif s=\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}\cdot\bm{v}\dif\bm{x}

for arbitrary and sufficiently regular test functions
\bm{v},q,\mu

defined on their corresponding domains, and for any real number
\gamma
.
Here,
{\bm{n}}
denotes the outward unit normal of
\Gamma
.

Multiplying the momentum equation in (
1
) by the gradient of
q
, it also follows that the pressure solution satisfies the identity

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla q\dif\bm{x}=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta\bm{u})\cdot\nabla q\dif\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}\cdot\nabla q\dif\bm{x}.

For vectors
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
denote by
{\bm{a}}\times{\bm{b}}
the cross product
in the case
d=3
, and
{\bm{a}}\times{\bm{b}}={\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{b}}^{\perp}=-{\bm{a}}^{\perp}\cdot{\bm{b}}

in the case
d=2
, where
{\bm{a}}^{\perp}=(-a_{2},a_{1})^{T}
.
Employing the identity for the vector Laplacian
\Delta(\cdot)=\nabla\nabla\cdot(\cdot)-\nabla\times\nabla\times(\cdot)
followed by an integration by parts, we reformulate the pressure problem as

(3)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla q\dif\bm{x}=-\int_{\Gamma}{\bm{n}}\times(\nabla\times\bm{u})\nabla q\dif\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}\cdot\nabla q\dif\bm{x}.

The integral equalities (
2
) and (
3
) form the basis of the finite element formulation and the pressure post-processing step studied in this paper.

3.2.
Geometry Description

We assume the physical domain is described by the subzero of a level set function
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
\Omega=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\phi(\bm{x})<0\},
which is approximately a signed distance function.
We shall use the same symbol
{\bm{n}}
to denote the normal on
\Gamma
and its closest point extension to a neighborhood of
\Gamma
.

On the discrete level, we have a finite element level set function
\phi_{h}
which is
the continuous, piecewise linear interpolant of
\phi
. The corresponding polytopal domain
is then given by

\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\phi_{h}(\bm{x})<0\},\qquad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h}=\partial\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}.

We use this piecewise
\mathbb{P}^{1}
level set approximation for robust construction of quadrature rules on the unfitted domain, see, e.g.,
[
4
]
. Since
\phi_{h}
is (only) piecewise
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the discrete domain approximates the physical domain with an error

\dist(\Gamma,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h})\lesssim h^{2}.

If left unaddressed, this geometric error dominates the approximation error of higher–order methods, resulting in suboptimal convergence rates.

Let
{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\Omega_{h}^{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}\to\Omega_{h}^{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}
be
the continuous, piecewise polynomial diffeomorphism
of degree
k
with respect to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}}

constructed in
[
26
]
, and set

{\Omega_{h}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\bm{\Phi}}_{h}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{h})=\{{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}(\bm{x})\mid\bm{x}\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}\}\quad\text{and}\quad{\Gamma_{h}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\bm{\Phi}}_{h}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h})=\{{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}(\bm{x})\mid\bm{x}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{h}\}.

The geometric error of approximating
\Omega
by
\Omega_{h}
is characterized by

\|\phi-\phi_{h}\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma})}\lesssim{h^{k+1-m}},\quad m=0,1.

In particular, this implies

(5)

\dist(\Gamma,{\Gamma_{h}})\lesssim h^{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\bm{n}}-{\bm{n}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h^{k},

where
{\bm{n}}_{h}
is the outward unit normal of
\Gamma_{h}
.
Since the mapping is piecewise polynomial with respect to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}}
, one can apply standard quadrature formulas for (exact) numerical integration of finite element functions over
{\Omega_{h}}
and
{\Gamma_{h}}
.

Remark 1
.

Here and in what follows, we write

which is independent of the mesh size and the geometry-mesh cut configuration, such that

3.3.
Finite Element Spaces

The Scott–Vogelius finite element pair
[\mathbb{P}^{k}]^{d}\times\mathbb{P}^{k-1}_{\text{dc}}

4.
Finite element method

We define the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{{\Omega_{h}}}\!\!\nabla\bm{u}_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\dif\bm{x}-\!\int_{{\Gamma_{h}}}\!\!(\bm{n}_{h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\bm{u}_{h})\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\dif s-\!\int_{{\Gamma_{h}}}\!\!(\bm{n}_{h}\!\cdot\!\nabla\bm{v}_{h})\cdot\bm{u}_{h}\dif s+\frac{\gamma_{n}}{h}\!\int_{{\Gamma_{h}}}\!\!\bm{u}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\dif s,

\displaystyle\int_{{\Gamma_{h}}}\mu_{h}\bm{n}_{h}\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\dif s,

for
\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, and
\mu_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
. Because
{\bm{V}}_{h}\not\subset{\bm{H}}^{1}({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})
in general, throughout the paper the gradients of functions in
{\bm{V}}_{h}
are understood to be taken piecewise with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}
.
The same convention will be used for norms on
{\bm{V}}_{h}
as well, e.g.,

\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{1}(K)}^{2}
.

To stabilize the diffusion bilinear form with respect to arbitrary cuts between the geometry and the active mesh, we consider a ghost-penalty stabilization. Specifically, we consider the so-called
direct version
of the ghost-penalty operator
[
34
,
24
]
. It was originally introduced for
H^{1}
-conforming finite elements on standard (affine) triangulations, and here we extend it to functions in
{\bm{V}}_{h}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{R}}

be the canonical global extension operator of rational functions to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{P}}
is
the canonical global extension operator of polynomials to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and if
r=p/q
for polynomials
p
and
q
, then

\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{R}}r=\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{P}}p/\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{P}}q
.
For a facet
F=K_{1}\cap K_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{Al},\text{gp}}
,
K_{1}\neq K_{2}
, we define the
facet patch
as
\omega_{F}=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.
The volumetric patch jump
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\omega}
of
\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
is then defined via

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\rrbracket_{\omega}\right|_{K_{i}}}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\bm{v}_{h}\right|_{K_{i}}}-\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{v}_{h}\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}|_{\tilde{K}_{j}})\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1},\quad\text{for }i,j\in\{1,2\}\text{ and }i\neq j.

The direct ghost-penalty operator is then defined as

(7)

i_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\coloneqq\gamma_{gp}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{Al},\text{gp}}}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{\omega_{F}}\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\rrbracket_{\omega}\cdot\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\rrbracket_{\omega}\dif\bm{x}.

In our analysis we need to extend the definition of
i_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
, for
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. In this case,
i_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i_{h}(\bm{u}_{p},\bm{v}_{p})
,
where
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\bm{u}_{p}\right|_{K}}=\big(\tilde{J}_{K}^{-1}\Pi_{\tilde{K}}(\tilde{J}_{K}\bm{u}\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h})\big)\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1}
,
with
\tilde{K}={\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1}(K)
,

\tilde{J}_{K}=\det(D{\left.\kern-1.2pt{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}\right|_{\tilde{K}}})
, and

\Pi_{\tilde{K}}
is
L^{2}(\tilde{K})
-orthogonal projection into polynomials of degree
2k-1
.

To stabilize the Lagrange multiplier, we also consider the “normal volume” ghost-penalty stabilization

(8)

j_{h}(\lambda_{h},\mu_{h})\coloneqq-h\gamma_{\lambda}\int_{\Omega^{\Gamma}_{h}}({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\lambda_{h})({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h})\dif\bm{x},

where
{\bm{n}}_{h}=\nabla(\phi_{h}\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1})/|\nabla(\phi_{h}\circ{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}^{-1})|
in
\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
is the extension of the normal vector.
We now have all ingredients to define the finite element method.

Remark 2

(Necessary stabilization facets)
.

The set
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\text{gp}}
of ghost-penalty facets defined in (
4
) is sufficient for stability but not necessary. We can reduce this set by considering patches of elements. Here, we group cut elements and facet neighbors of cut elements into disjoint groups
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
, such that each patch
\omega
contains at least one uncut root element
T_{\omega}\in{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\Gamma}
. For ghost-penalty stabilization, we can then consider the set of interior facets of the Alfeld split elements of the macro patches. This can be seen as a weak form of the aggregated finite element method
[
2
]
.

4.1.
Finite element formulation

The finite element formulation builds on the integral identity (
2
). This can be seen as using the Nitsche method to enforce the boundary condition for the velocity weakly and the Lagrange multiplier method for the no-penetration condition. The formulation reads as follows: Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h},\lambda_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}\times\Sigma_{h}
such that

(9)

A_{h}(({\bm{u}}_{h},p_{h},\lambda_{h}),(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h},\mu_{h}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(a_{h}+i_{h})(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h}(p_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{u}_{h})\\
+c_{h}(\lambda_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{u}_{h})+j_{h}(\lambda_{h},\mu_{h})=\bm{f}_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})

for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h},\mu_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}\times\Sigma_{h}
. Here,

\bm{f}_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})=(\bm{f},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}
, where the source term

\bm{f}
is smoothly extended to
\Omega
.

Remark 3
.

Our practical implementation of the zero mean constraint in the test and trial pressure space consists of adding
(p_{h},s)_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}+(q_{h},r)_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}
(
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
) to the
finite element method bilinear form.

The finite element pressure
p_{h}
solving (
9
) approximates the
extended pressure function
p^{z}
which is zero outside
\overline{\Omega}
, and therefore
discontinuous across the physical boundary
\Gamma
. Since the mesh is not fitted to
\Gamma
, this leads to a sub-optimal —
O(h^{\frac{1}{2}})
, in general — convergence order for
p_{h}
. We will see latter that, thanks to the strong divergence-free property of the method, this pressure error does not affect the optimal convergence order for the velocity.

The following lemma states that (
9
)
yields exactly divergence-free approximations.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\bm{u}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
satisfies (
9
)
then
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}=0
in
\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

Taking
{\bm{v}}_{h}=0
,
\mu_{h}=0
in (
9
)
yields
b_{h}(q_{h}^{0},\bm{u}_{h})=0

for all
q_{h}^{0}\in Q_{h}^{0}
. Splitting
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
into the zero mean component and a constant,
q_{h}=q_{h}^{0}+|{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}|^{-1}(q_{h},1)_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}
, we find
(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}-\mbox{const})_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}=0
, with const=
|{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}|^{-1}(\bm{u}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{\partial{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}},1)_{\partial{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}
. By repeating the arguments
from
[
10
]
, we obtain
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}=
const in
{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
. However,
c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{u}_{h})=0
for
\mu_{h}=1
along with the divergence theorem implies that const
=0
.
∎

4.2.
Pressure post processing

We benefit now from having this optimal-order finite element velocity in recovering the higher-order pressure approximation through a simple post-processing.
For this we also need the
H^{1}
-conforming pressure FE space:

Q_{h}^{*}=\{q_{h}\in C({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})|\ q_{h}|_{K}\circ\varphi_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(\hat{T})\ \forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}}\}.

The finite element formulation for the post-processing step builds on (
3
): Find
p_{h}^{\ast}\in Q_{h}^{*}
such that
(p_{h}^{\ast},1)_{{\Omega_{h}}}=0
and

(10)

\displaystyle(\nabla p_{h}^{\ast},\nabla q_{h}^{\ast})_{{\Omega_{h}}}+i_{h}(p_{h}^{\ast},q_{h}^{\ast})={\bm{f}_{h}(\nabla q_{h}^{\ast})}+({\bm{n}}_{h}\times(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{h}),\nabla q_{h}^{\ast})_{{\Gamma_{h}}}\quad\forall\,q_{h}^{\ast}\in Q_{h}^{*}.

With a slight abuse of notation,
i_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the direct ghost-penalty operator defined similar to (
7
), but acting on functions in
Q_{h}^{*}
. Furthermore, with another abuse of notation, the ghost-penalty operator
i_{h}
is only needed over facets
\{F\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\text{gp}}\mid\exists K_{1}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Omega},K_{2}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma},K_{1}\neq K_{2}\text{ and }F=\partial K_{1}\cap\partial K_{2}\}
.
Note that the post-processing formulation applied integration by parts to avoid the implementation of the Hessian matrix for the Piola-mapped elements.

5.
Stability Analysis

The following theorem is the crucial result for the stability of the finite element method.

Theorem 5.1

(Inf-sup Stability)
.

For
h>0
sufficient small, there exists
\beta>0
, independent of
h
, such that

(11)

\beta\|q_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})}}\qquad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}.

Proof.

See Appendix
A

∎

5.1.
Stability of the Unfitted Formulation

Recall that we use the same symbol
{\bm{n}}
to denote the normal on
\Gamma
and its closest point extension to a neighborhood of
\Gamma
.
For the analysis, we consider the following mesh-dependent norms for the velocity, pressure and Lagrange multiplier:

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\sigma}(\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Gamma_{h}}}^{2})+{\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}}+\|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\|q_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\lambda_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\sigma^{-1}}(h\|\lambda_{h}\|_{{\Gamma_{h}}}^{2}+j_{h}(\lambda_{h},\lambda_{h})),

where the arguments make it clear which norms are used.
The scaling parameter
\sigma
and the narrow-band term
\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}
are introduced for the purpose of analysis. We assume
0<\sigma\leq 1
, sufficiently small but independent of any discretization parameters.
The product norm is then

\displaystyle\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h},\lambda_{h})\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\lambda_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.

We will make a frequent use of the unfitted trace inequality

(12)

\|\bm{v}\|_{\Gamma\cap K}\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{K}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{K}

for all
\bm{v}\in{\bm{H}}^{1}(K)
, see
[
19
]
.
We also recall the narrow-band estimate
[
16
]
:

(13)

\|\lambda_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\lesssim\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\lambda_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\qquad\forall\lambda_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}.

5.1.1.
Stability of the Pressure

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist
h_{0},\beta_{b}^{\ast}>0
independent of
h
and
\sigma
, such that for all
h\leq h_{0}
, it holds

(14)

\beta_{b}^{\ast}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h})}{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}}\qquad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}.

Proof.

Thanks to the result in Theorem
5.1
there is
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{0}\subset\bm{V}_{h}
such that

\beta\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\leq b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\quad\text{and}\quad\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})}\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

The Nitsche term in the definition of the norm
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
can be bounded by using the
unfitted trace inequality (
12
), inverse estimates, and local Friedrichs inequalities: for all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Gamma_{h}}\cap K}\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{K}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{K}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{K}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{K}+h^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|\bm{v}_{h}({\bm{p}})|.

where
{\bm{p}}
is any vertex of
K
. Now,
K
belongs to a macro-element with a face on
\partial{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
. Therefore, there exists a vertex
{\bm{p}}
of
K
such that
\bm{v}_{h}({\bm{p}})=0
.
Hence, we obtain the bound
\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma\cap K}^{2}\lesssim h\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{K}^{2}
.
Summing up over all
\forall\penalty\ K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}
gives the control over the Nitsche term in
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
:

(15)

h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})}.

Using this and
\sigma\leq 1
, we obtain

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}})}\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

This proves (
14
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{2}(\Omega^{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, there exists
\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}

with
b_{h}(q_{h},\pi_{V}{\bm{v}})=b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}})

for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}
.
If
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{k+1}(\Omega^{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, then there holds
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}-\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}
.
Finally, if
{\bm{v}}
is divergence-free,
then
\nabla\cdot\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Set
{\bm{X}}_{h}=\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h})=0\ \forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}\}

to be the kernel of
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and set

{\bm{X}}_{h}^{\perp}=\{{\bm{w}}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ (\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h},\nabla\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}=0\ \forall\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}\}
.
Given
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})
, let
I_{V}{\bm{v}}\in\bm{V}_{h}
be the interpolant in Lemma
3.1
.
Then by the inf-sup condition (
5.1
)
there exists a (unique)
{\bm{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{X}}_{h}^{\perp}
satisfying

b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{w}}_{h})=b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}-I_{V}{\bm{v}})
for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}

and
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{w}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim\|{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\beta}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}-I_{V}{\bm{v}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
.
Setting
\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}={\bm{w}}_{h}+I_{V}{\bm{v}}
,
we see that
b_{h}(q_{h},\pi_{V}{\bm{v}})=b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}})

for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}
, and

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}-\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}-I_{V}{\bm{v}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{w}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim\left(1+\beta^{-1}\right)\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}-I_{V}{\bm{v}}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}.

Finally, if
{\bm{v}}
is divergence-free,
then
b_{h}(q_{h},\pi_{V}{\bm{v}})=0\ \forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}
, implying
that
\nabla\cdot\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}
is constant on
\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}
.
Because
{\bm{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}^{0}
, we have
\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}|_{\partial\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}=({\bm{w}}_{h}+I_{V}{\bm{v}})|_{\partial\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}=I_{V}{\bm{v}}|_{\partial\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
. We then conclude
\nabla\cdot\pi_{V}{\bm{v}}\equiv 0

by Lemma
3.1
and the divergence theorem.
∎

5.1.2.
Stability of the Lagrange Multiplier

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist
h_{1},\beta^{\ast}_{c}>0
independent of
h
and
\sigma
, such that for all
h\leq h_{1}
, it holds

(16)

\beta_{c}^{\ast}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{v}_{h})}{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}}+\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall\mu_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}.

Remark 4
.

We note that for the proof that follows, it is important that polynomial degree of the Lagrange multiplier does not exceed the degree of the finite element velocity space.

Proof.

Fix
\mu_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
and extend
\mu_{h}
to
\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}

by setting its values at all Lagrange nodes in
\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}\backslash\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
to zero.
The geometric error bound
\|\bm{n}-\bm{n}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq c\,h^{k}
implies
|1-\bm{n}_{h}^{T}\bm{n}|\leq ch^{2k}
on
\Gamma_{h}
. Therefore,

(17)

c_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h}\bm{n})\geq(1-ch^{2k})\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}.

Since
\mu_{h}\bm{n}
is continuous,
its nodal interpolant
\bm{v}_{h}=I_{V}(\mu_{h}\bm{n})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
given in Lemma
3.1
is well defined.

Thanks to the approximation properties of the interpolant, trace inequality (
12
), inverse inequality and (
13
), we have

(18)

\begin{split}\|\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+h\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}}\|\nabla(\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{K}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{2k+1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}}\|\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{H^{k+1}(K)}^{2}\lesssim h^{2k+1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}}\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{k+1}(K)}^{2}\\
&=h^{2k+1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}}\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{k}(K)}^{2}\lesssim h\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al},\Gamma}}\|\mu_{h}\|_{K}^{2}=h\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\lesssim h\,\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

In the chain of inequalities above we used that
\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{k+1}(K)}\lesssim\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{k}(K)}
for
\mu_{h}\circ\varphi_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\hat{T})
.
The same arguments as above can be used to estimate
\nabla(\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n})
in the whole computational domain:

(19)

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}}\|\nabla(\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}\lesssim\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{K}\lesssim\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\lesssim\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.

Here, the product
\mu_{h}{\bm{n}}
is understood to be the zero function
outside a
O(h)
neighborhood of
\Gamma_{h}
.

To bound
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
from above, we split

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\leq\sigma\big(h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\big)+\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}.

To bound the first two terms,
we use the triangle inequality followed by (
18
) and (
13
),

(20)

\begin{split}h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\\
&\quad+h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\bm{v}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma})}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\leq h^{-1}\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+h^{-2}\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\leq\sigma h^{-2}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

Let
S_{h}=\partial({\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma})
. Recalling
\bm{v}_{h}=0
at all internal nodes of

{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
, we have
\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{S_{h}}^{2}
. In turn, by a standard finite element trace inequality, we have

\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{S_{h}}^{2}\leq h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}
.
We also estimate
\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\lesssim h\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}
.
Putting these estimates together, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{S_{h}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-2}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}.

Combining this with (
20
) gives

(21)

h^{2}(\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+h^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2})\lesssim\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.

We now estimate the narrow-band part of the
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
norm with the help of (
13
), (
18
), and the definition of the
j_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
stabilization form:

(22)

\begin{split}\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}&\lesssim\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\mu_{h}\bm{n}\cdot\bm{n})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\|\mu_{h}\bm{n}-\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\bm{n}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+h^{2k}\|\nabla\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\lesssim\sigma h^{-1}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

By the same arguments and (
19
), we estimate the last (divergence) term in the definition of
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
norm:

\begin{split}\|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}&\leq\|\nabla\cdot(\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+C\|\nabla(\mu_{h}\bm{n}-\bm{v}_{h})\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla\cdot(\mu_{h}\bm{n})\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\bm{n}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+\|\mu_{h}\|_{{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\lesssim h^{-1}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})+\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\sigma h^{-1}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
21
), (
22
), and assuming
h
is sufficiently small, i.e.
h\leq\sigma
holds, we have

(23)

h\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim\sigma\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

Now (
17
), (
18
), (
23
) and the definition of norms lead to (
16
) with the help of straightforward calculation for sufficiently small
h
:

\begin{split}c_{h}(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})\geq&(1-ch^{2k})\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}+c_{h}(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n})\geq c\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}-\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{v}}_{h}-\mu_{h}\bm{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}\\
\geq&c\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}-c_{1}\sigma h\,\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

Multiplying both sides with
\sigma^{-1}h
and adding
{\sigma^{-1}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})
gives

{\sigma^{-1}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},\sigma^{-1}h{\bm{v}}_{h})\geq c\sigma^{-1}(h\|\mu_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}}+j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h}))-c_{1}h^{2}\,\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\geq c\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
and using both (
23
) and
{\sigma^{-1}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})<\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}
leads to
(
16
).
∎

5.1.3.
Stability of the cumulative bilinear form

We first show the stability of the cumulative constraint bilinear form and then combine it with the well-known results for the stabilized diffusion term to show stability of the global system.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume
\sigma>0
,
h_{2}>0
are sufficiently small. Then there exists
\beta_{g}^{\ast}>0
independent of
h
, such that for all
h\leq h_{2}
it holds

(24)

\beta_{g}^{\ast}(\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast})\leq\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{v}_{h})}{\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}}+\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0},\,\mu_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}.

Proof.

The key observation is the following: If
\bm{v}_{h}
vanishes on a boundary of
{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
, then
\bm{v}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}
in
c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{v}_{h})
can be properly controlled on
\Gamma_{h}
which is within the
O(h)
distance from
\partial{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
. More precisely,
let
\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{0}
be the (normalized) test function given by Lemma
5.2
,
i.e.

\beta_{b}^{\ast}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq b_{h}(q_{h},\bm{v}_{h}^{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq 1

holds. Then we estimate by using
\bm{v}_{h}^{1}=0
on
\partial{\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}}}
,

\begin{split}c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{v}_{h}^{1})&\leq\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\|\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\leq\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\|\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\cdot{\bm{n}}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}+h^{q}\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\|\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\\
&\leq C\,h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mu_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}(\|(\bm{n}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\cdot{\bm{n}})\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}+h^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}})\\
&\leq C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}^{1}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.\end{split}

Hence choosing a suitable
\sigma>0
, but independent of
h
, one can make the factor
C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}
on the right hand side sufficiently small.

The rest of the proof follows standard textbook arguments.
Using (
16
), there exists
\bm{v}_{h}^{2}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
satisfying
\beta_{c}^{*}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq c_{h}(\mu_{h},\bm{v}_{h}^{2})+{\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}^{2}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq 1
.
We then set
\bm{v}_{h}=c_{r}\bm{v}_{h}^{1}+\bm{v}_{h}^{2}
for some
c_{r}>0
to be determined, and note that
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq 1+c_{r}
. We then find

\displaystyle b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle=c_{r}b_{h}(q_{h}{\bm{v}}_{h}^{1})+b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{2})+c_{r}c(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{1})+c(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq c_{r}\beta_{b}^{*}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+\beta_{c}^{*}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}-{\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}-|b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{2})|-c_{r}|c(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{1})|

\displaystyle\geq c_{r}\beta_{b}^{*}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+(\beta_{c}^{*}-c_{r}C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}})\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}-{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}-|b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{2})|.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of the discrete norms yield

b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h}^{2})\leq\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})+{\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\big(c_{r}\beta_{b}^{*}-1\big)\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+(\beta_{c}^{*}-c_{r}C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}})\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

We now take
c_{r}
sufficiently large
and
\sigma
sufficiently small (but independent of mesh
parameters) to conclude

\displaystyle b_{h}(q_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})+c_{h}(\mu_{h},{\bm{v}}_{h})+\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}j_{h}(\mu_{h},\mu_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{b}^{*}+\beta_{c}^{*})(\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\mu_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}q_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}).

The desired result now follows this estimate
and
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq 1+c_{r}
.
∎

a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
as well as to derive error estimates,

]
.

.

It holds

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{v}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}^{2}\lesssim(a_{h}+i_{h})(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

6.
Error Analysis

Since Theorem
5.8
establishes
stability of the scheme, the finite element method
(
9
) is well posed,
and the error analysis reduces to the consistency
of the scheme. Recall that the velocity space
{\bm{V}}_{h}

is
{\bm{H}}({\rm div})
-conforming but not a subspace of
{\bm{H}}^{1}
.
Therefore, in addition to geometric consistency,
we must also address the non-conformity of the velocity space.
Here, we estimate this consistency error based
on the results given in
[
10
,
32
]
.
The arguments in these works are already highly technical
in two dimensions and do not extend immediately to three dimensions.
For this reason we restrict our error analysis to the 2D case.

The following lemmas estimate the consistency error arising
from the non-conformity
{\bm{V}}_{h}\not\subset{\bm{H}}^{1}(\Omega^{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
d=2
and the edge degrees of freedom
of
{\bm{V}}_{h}
are the
{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}
-mapped Gauss-Lobatto nodes.
Then there holds, for all
{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{H}}^{k}(\Omega^{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
and
\bm{v}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
,

(30)

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm Al,gp}}\int_{F}\left|\nabla{\bm{w}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[\bm{v}_{h}]\right|\lesssim h^{k+m-1}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{k}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,k,

where
[\cdot]
denotes the standard jump operator and

\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}

is understood to be the piecewise
H^{m}
norm of
\bm{v}_{h}

with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}
.

Proof.

In light of the assumptions on
{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}
, this result directly follows from
[
10
, Lemma 4.7]
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose the assumptions in Lemma
6.1

are satisfied. Let
{\bm{u}}^{e}
be the divergence-free
extension of the velocity solution, and let
\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
be
its approximation given in Lemma
5.3
.
Then there holds

(31)

\displaystyle\left|(a_{h}+i_{h})(\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e},\bm{v}_{h})+\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\qquad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}.

Proof.

First, by (
26
)–(
27
), there
holds

i_{h}(\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{v}}_{h})\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

Next, we integrate by parts
to obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{v}}_{h})+\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad=a_{h}(\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e},\bm{v}_{h})-\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla{\bm{u}}^{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\nabla{\bm{v}}_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{u}}^{e})\cdot\bm{v}_{h}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}^{\rm Al}_{h}}\int_{\partial K\cap\Omega_{h}}({\bm{n}}_{\partial K}\cdot\nabla{\bm{u}}^{e})\cdot\bm{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla(\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e}-{\bm{u}}^{e})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\nabla\bm{v}_{h}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla({\bm{u}}^{e}-\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e}))\cdot\bm{v}_{h}-\int_{\Gamma_{h}}({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\bm{v}_{h})\cdot\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\gamma_{n}}{h}\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\pi_{V}{\bm{u}}^{e}\cdot\bm{v}_{h}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\text{Al},\text{gp}}}\int_{F}\left|\nabla{\bm{u}}^{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[\bm{v}_{h}]\right|.

Denote by
{\bm{p}}_{\Gamma}
the closest point projection on
\Gamma
. Then, we have

h^{-1/2}\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}=h^{-1/2}\|{\bm{u}}^{e}-{\bm{u}}^{e}\circ{\bm{p}}_{\Gamma}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)},

where the last inequality follows from
[
17
, Lemma 7.3]
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume

\|\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f}_{h}\|_{V_{h}^{*}}\leq{h^{2k+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

For the arguments below it is convenient to assume
\bm{f}^{e}=-\Delta{\bm{u}}+\nabla p^{e}

p^{e}
is
the smooth extension, not clipped to zero outside

From the definition of the dual norm and the fact that

(\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}/\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\leq\|\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f}\|_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}/\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}.

Denote by

an
\varepsilon
-tubular neighborhood of
\Gamma
and let

. For

We let

sufficiently large such that

We first use the Poincare inequality in

followed by the triangle and the narrow-band inequalities as well as the regularity result for the solution of the Stokes equation:

\begin{split}\|\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f}\|_{\Omega_{h}\setminus\Omega}&\leq\|\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f}\|_{U_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)}\\

&\lesssim h^{3(k+1)/2}(\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{H^{4}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|p^{e}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})})\\
&\lesssim h^{3(k+1)/2}(\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{H^{4}(\Omega)}+\|p^{e}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega))}+\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)})\lesssim h^{3(k+1)/2}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

\|{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}\lesssim h\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{v}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}
.

, and note
that
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{e}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{k}
by Corollary
6.5
.

Consider the pair
({\bm{w}},\eta)\in{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
solving the Stokes problem:

(53)

\left\{\begin{split}-\Delta{\bm{w}}+\nabla\eta&=\mu_{h}\bm{e}\circ\Psi_{h}&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\end{split}\right.

By
H^{2}
-regularity and the properties in (
45
) of
{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}
we have
({\bm{w}},\eta)\in{\bm{H}}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
, and

(54)

\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mu_{h}\bm{e}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

We let
{\bm{w}}^{e}
be a smooth solenoidal extension of
{\bm{w}}
,
and
\eta^{e}
a smooth extension of
\eta
.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product of the first equation in (
53
) with
\bm{e}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}
, we get after integration by parts and a change of variables:

\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}^{2}=a_{h}({\bm{w}}^{e},\bm{e})+c_{h}(\eta^{e},\bm{e})+\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{i},

where the six terms
\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{6}
incorporate geometric
errors and whose precise definitions are given below.
These terms are of higher order and we estimate the using the properties of the mapping
{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}
:

\begin{split}I_{1}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\nabla{\bm{w}},\nabla(\bm{e}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}))_{\Omega}-(\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e},\nabla\bm{e})_{\Omega_{h}}=((\mu_{h}^{-1}\nabla{\bm{w}}D{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{T})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}-\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e},\nabla\bm{e})_{\Omega_{h}}\\
&\lesssim(h^{k}\|(\nabla{\bm{w}})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Omega_{h}}+\|(\nabla{\bm{w}})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}-\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}})\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}\\
&\lesssim(h^{k}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}\|_{\Omega}+h^{k+1}\|{\bm{w}}^{e}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h})})\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{2k}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

Here, we have used the algebraic identity
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(BC)=(AC^{T})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B
.

Likewise, using
\nabla\cdot\bm{e}=0
,

\begin{split}I_{2}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-(\eta,\nabla\cdot(\bm{e}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}))_{\Omega}=((\mu_{h}^{-1}D\Psi_{h}^{T}-\mathbf{I})\eta\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1},\nabla\bm{e})_{\Omega_{h}}\\
&\lesssim h^{k}\|\eta\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Omega_{h}}\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{k}\|\eta\|_{\Omega}\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{2k}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

Continuing, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}

\displaystyle=({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e},\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}-({\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}},(\bm{e}\circ\Psi_{h}))_{\Gamma}=({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e}-(\tilde{\mu}_{h}^{-1}{\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1},\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}},

where
\tilde{\mu}_{h}=|{\rm cof}(D\Psi_{h}){\bm{n}}|
is the surface Jacobian.
Using
|\tilde{\mu}_{h}-1|\lesssim h^{k}
and
|{\bm{n}}_{h}-{\bm{n}}|\lesssim h^{k}
,
we find

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e}-\nabla{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}+h^{k}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}\|_{\Gamma}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{\frac{3}{2}k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}},

where we used
\|\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e}-\nabla{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
(cf. the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 7.3]
),
\|\bm{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}
and a trace inequality.

By similar arguments, along with Corollary
6.5
,

\displaystyle I_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=({\bm{w}}^{e},{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}=({\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1},{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}},

and

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\eta,(\bm{e}\circ{\bm{\Psi}})\cdot{\bm{n}})_{\Gamma}-(\eta^{e},{\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}=((\tilde{\mu}_{h}^{-1}\eta{\bm{n}})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}-\eta^{e}{\bm{n}}_{h},\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\tilde{\mu}_{h}^{-1}\eta{\bm{n}})\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}-\eta^{e}{\bm{n}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{e}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

Finally,

\begin{split}I_{6}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\gamma_{n}h^{-1}({\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}),\bm{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{e}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

Applying the estimates of
I_{i}
,
we conclude that for arbitrary
{\bm{w}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
and
\eta_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
it holds

(55)

\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}^{2}\lesssim a_{h}({\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}_{h},\bm{e})+c_{h}(\eta^{e}-\eta_{h},\bm{e})+a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},\bm{e})+c_{h}(\eta_{h},\bm{e})+h^{\frac{3}{2}k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

We now let
{\bm{w}}_{h}=\pi_{V}{\bm{w}}^{e}
, given by Lemma
5.3

and note that
{\bm{w}}_{h}
is divergence-free.
By Theorem
6.3
and Proposition
6.4
,
along with standard arguments, one estimates

(56)

\begin{split}&a_{h}({\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}_{h},\bm{e})+c_{h}(\eta^{e}-\eta_{h},\bm{e})\\
&\qquad\lesssim h(\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})(\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{e}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\bm{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}})\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

Next, we write

(57)

\begin{split}a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},&\bm{e})=a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})-a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},\bm{u}_{h})\\
&=a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})-{\bm{f}}_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h})+b_{h}(p_{h},{\bm{w}}_{h})+c_{h}(\lambda_{h},{\bm{w}}_{h})+i_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h})\\
&=\big[a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})+(\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\big]+({\bm{f}}^{e}-{\bm{f}},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+c_{h}(\lambda_{h}-p^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})+i_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h}).\end{split}

Here, we have used the fact that
{\bm{f}}^{e}=-\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e}+\nabla\bar{p}^{e}
,
\bar{p}^{e}=p^{e}
in
\bar{\Omega}_{h}
, integration by parts,
and the divergence-free property of
{\bm{w}}_{h}
.

Integrating by parts and applying Lemma
6.1
with
m=2

yields

\displaystyle\big|a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})+(\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|-({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}+{\gamma_{n}}h^{-1}({\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}}\right|+h^{k+1}\|{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{\mathcal{T}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}|+{h}^{-1}|({\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}}|+h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

We then estimate

\displaystyle h^{-1}({\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}}

\displaystyle=h^{-1}({\bm{u}}^{e}-{\bm{u}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1},{\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1})_{\Gamma_{h}}+h^{-1}({\bm{u}}^{e}-{\bm{u}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1},{\bm{w}}_{h}-{\bm{w}}^{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{2k+1}+h^{k+\frac{3}{2}})\|{\bm{u}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

The same reasoning shows
\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+1}
,
and hence we get

\begin{split}|({\bm{n}}_{h}\cdot\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})_{\Gamma_{h}}|&\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{\Omega^{\Gamma}_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\|\nabla({\bm{w}}_{h}-{\bm{w}}^{e})\|_{\Omega^{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|\nabla{\bm{w}}^{e}\|_{\Omega^{\Gamma}_{h}})\\
&\lesssim h^{k+1}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}},\end{split}

where we used
[
11
, Lemma 4.10]

in the second-to-last inequality. Combining the previous three
estimates, we obtain

(58)

\big|a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}^{e})+(\Delta{\bm{u}}^{e},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\big|\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

To estimate
c_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},\lambda_{h}-p^{e})
, we use that
\|\lambda_{h}-p\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch^{k}
, which follows
from the triangle inequality,

\|\lambda_{h}-p^{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq\|\lambda_{h}-\Pi_{\Sigma}\lambda^{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+\|\Pi_{\Sigma}\lambda^{e}-\lambda^{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}+\|\lambda^{e}-p^{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

and then an application of the convergence estimate (
35
), interpolation and geometric error estimates as in (
38
)–(
39
).
We then use
{\bm{w}}=0
on
\Gamma
to obtain

(59)

\begin{split}c_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},\lambda_{h}-p^{e})&\lesssim h^{k}\|{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k}(\|{\bm{w}}_{h}-{\bm{w}}^{e}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}+\|{\bm{w}}^{e}-{\bm{w}}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{h}^{-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}})\\
&\lesssim h^{k+\frac{3}{2}}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

Due to Proposition
6.4
we have

(60)

(\bm{f}^{e}-\bm{f},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{5}{2}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}{\bm{w}}_{h}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{5}{2}}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{2k+\frac{5}{2}}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

Finally, applying estimates in (
26
)–(
27
), we get

(61)

\begin{split}i_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h})&=[i_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h}-{\bm{w}}^{e},{\bm{u}}_{h}-{\bm{u}}^{e})+i_{h}({\bm{w}},{\bm{u}}_{h}-{\bm{u}}^{e})]+[i_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h}-{\bm{w}}^{e},{\bm{u}}^{e})+i_{h}({\bm{w}}^{e},{\bm{u}}^{e})]\\
&\lesssim h\|{\bm{w}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}(\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\bm{e}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{\ast}+h^{k})\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.\end{split}

We then apply the estimates (
58
)–(
61
)
to (
57
)
to conclude

(62)

\displaystyle a_{h}({\bm{w}}_{h},\bm{e})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

Also using
c_{h}(\eta_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h})=0
and
\|{\bm{u}}^{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim h^{k+1}
again, we estimate

(63)

c_{h}(\eta_{h},\bm{e})=c_{h}(\eta_{h},{\bm{u}})\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\eta_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}.

The estimate for
\|\bm{e}\|_{\Omega_{h}}
in (
52
) now follows
by applying the estimates (
56
), (
62
),
and (
63
) to (
55
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1

The mapping
{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}
satisfies for
0\leq m\leq k+1

(cf.
[
28
, (3.17), (3.9) and Theorem 3.11]
)

(64)

\displaystyle|{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\tilde{K})}\lesssim 1,\qquad|{\bm{\Phi}}^{-1}_{h}|_{W^{m,\infty}(K)}\lesssim 1\qquad\forall\tilde{K}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}},\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}.

In addition, by
[
28
, (3.38)]
,
there holds

(65)

\|D{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}-{\bm{I}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{h}^{\tilde{\mathcal{T}}})}\lesssim h.

By the shape-regularity and quasi-uniformity
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}}
,
there holds
\det(D\varphi_{\tilde{K}})=d!|\tilde{K}|\approx h^{d}
,
|\varphi_{\tilde{K}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{K})}\lesssim h^{m}
,
and
|\varphi^{-1}_{\tilde{K}}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\tilde{K})}\lesssim h^{-m}

for
m=0,1
and
\tilde{K}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\text{Al}}
. Consequently by (
64
), we have for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\rm Al}

(66)

\displaystyle\det(D\varphi_{K})\approx h^{d},\qquad

\displaystyle|\varphi_{K}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{m},\qquad

\displaystyle|\varphi_{K}^{-1}|_{W^{m,\infty}(K)}\lesssim h^{-1}\ \ (1\leq m\leq(k+1)).

As a first step to prove Theorem
5.1
,
we provide an equivalent definition of
{\bm{V}}_{h}
, stating it as a macro-like finite element space.
For an affine element
\tilde{T}\in{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}
in the macro triangulation,
let
\tilde{T}^{{\rm Al}}=\{\tilde{K}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{K}_{d+1}\}
be
the local Alfeld split.
Let
\varphi_{\tilde{T}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\hat{T}\to\tilde{T}

be an affine mapping, and set
\varphi_{T}={\bm{\Phi}}_{h}\circ\varphi_{\tilde{T}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\hat{T}\to T
,
where
T={\bm{\Phi}}_{h}(\tilde{T})
; see Figure
6
.
Note that
\varphi_{T}
is a continuous piecewise polynomial
with respect to the reference Alfeld split
\hat{T}^{\rm Al}=\{\hat{K}_{1},\ldots,\hat{K}_{d+1}\}
, but it is not
C^{1}
because
{\bm{\Phi}}_{h}
is not
in general
C^{1}
on
\tilde{T}
. We see that
\varphi_{T}

inherits the scaling found in (
66
), i.e.,

(67)

\displaystyle\det(D\varphi_{T})\approx h^{d},\qquad

\displaystyle|\varphi_{T}|_{W^{m,\infty}(\hat{T})}\lesssim h^{m},\qquad

\displaystyle|\varphi_{T}^{-1}|_{W^{m,\infty}(T)}\lesssim h^{-1}\ \ (1\leq m\leq(k+1)),

where the norms are understood to be piecewise defined with respect to the local splits.

We then write an equivalent definition
of the velocity space
{\bm{V}}_{h}
, now with respect to
the piecewise differomorphisms
\varphi_{T}

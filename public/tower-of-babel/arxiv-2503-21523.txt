A weak energy identity for ( n + α ) (n+\alpha) -harmonic maps with a free boundary in a sphere
Dorian Martino, Katarzyna Mazowiecka, Rémy Rodiac

Abstract
. In this article, we show that sequences of ( n + α ) (n+\alpha) -harmonic maps with a free boundary in 𝕊 d − 1 \mathbb{S}^{d-1} , where α \alpha is a parameter tending to zero, converge to a bubble tree. For such sequences, we prove in detail that the limiting energy is equal to the energy of the macroscopic limit plus the sum of the energies of certain “bubbles”, each multiplied by a corresponding coefficient.

1.
Introduction

Conformally invariant energies are of special interest in geometric analysis. Due to their invariance under an infinite-dimensional group, solutions to the corresponding variational problems lack compactness, placing us in a limiting case where the Palais–Smale condition fails. A famous example of a conformally invariant energy is the Dirichlet energy in dimension 2, whose natural generalization in dimension
n
is provided by the
n
-energy.

Let
n,d\geq 2
be two integers and let
(\Sigma^{n},g)
be a smooth compact manifold with non-empty boundary
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}\neq\emptyset
. Given
p\geq 1
, we consider the
p
-energy, defined for
u\in W^{1,p}\left(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
by

(1.1)

E_{p}(u)\coloneqq\int_{\Sigma^{n}}|du|_{g}^{p}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

This work is motivated by the study of
E_{n}
restricted to the space

(1.2)

{\mathcal{I}}\coloneqq\left\{v\in W^{1,n}\left(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\colon\left|{\rm tr}\big\rvert_{{\partial}\Sigma^{n}}u\right|=1\text{ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n}\right\}.

Critical points of
E_{n}
in
{\mathcal{I}}
are called
n
-harmonic maps with a free boundary in the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. They are weak solutions to the following system

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}d^{*_{g}}(|du|_{g}^{n-2}du)&=&0&\text{ in }{\rm Int}(\Sigma^{n}),\\
|u|&=&1&\text{ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n},\\
|du|_{g}^{n-2}{\partial}_{\nu_{g}}u&\perp&T_{u}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}&\text{ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n}.\end{array}\right.

Here we have denoted
d^{*_{g}}
as the codifferential of the metric
g
and
\nu_{g}
as the unit normal vector to the
\partial\Sigma^{n}
. More precisely, they satisfy the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{I}}
, where
{\mathcal{I}}
is the space defined in (
1.2
). We say that
u
is a weak solution to (
1.3
) if

(1.4)

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\langle|du|_{g}^{n-2}du,d\Phi\rangle_{g}\ d{\rm vol}_{g}=0\quad\text{ for all }\Phi\in W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ with }\Phi\cdot u=0\text{ a.e.\ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n}.

It was proved in
[
39
]
that any weak solution to (
1.3
) is actually
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\beta}
for some
\beta
depending only on
n
and
d
. In order to understand better the solutions to (
1.3
), the next step is the study of their compactness properties. Namely, we consider a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{I}}
of solutions to (
1.3
) with bounded
n
-energy and analyze its asymptotic behavior.

There are at least two obstructions that prevent strong convergence in the
W^{1,n}
topology in full generality. First, the energy
E_{n}
is conformally invariant. As a result, one can construct sequences of solutions to (
1.3
) that are bounded in
W^{1,n}
but do not converge strongly in
W^{1,n}
. For example, if
\Sigma^{n}=\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
is the closed unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can take a sequence of Möbius maps
M_{a_{k}}(x)=\psi_{a_{k}}(x)/|\psi_{a_{k}}(x)|^{2}
where

\psi_{a_{k}}(x)=a_{k}+(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\frac{a_{k}-x}{|a_{k}-x|^{2}},\quad x\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}},

and
(a_{k})_{k}
is a sequence of points in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
converging to
(1,0,\dots,0)
. It is known that
M_{a_{k}}
is conformal and maps
{\partial}\mathbb{B}^{n}
into
{\partial}\mathbb{B}^{n}
with degree 1. Therefore each
M_{a_{k}}
minimizes the
n
-energy with prescribed degree 1 on
{\partial}\mathbb{B}^{n}
and hence satisfies (
1.3
). Furthermore each
M_{a_{k}}
has the same
n
-energy, given by a constant times the volume of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. However, it can be shown that
M_{a_{k}}
converges weakly in
W^{1,n}
to a constant function and this convergence is not strong. For a detailed explanation in the case
n=2
we refer to
[
3
, Section 4.1]
or
[
4
, Section 3]
, for the general case, see
[
52
, Chapter 5]
.
The second obstruction arises from the fact that we are dealing with the critical case of Sobolev embeddings: while
W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
embeds into
L^{p}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1\leq p<+\infty
, it fails to embed into
L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For these reasons the existence of solutions to (
1.3
) and their regularity up to the boundary is a non-trivial problem.

Such free-boundary
n
-harmonic maps appear in the study of Steklov eigenvalues. For
n=2
, Fraser–Schoen proved
[
15
]
that one can associate a free-boundary harmonic map in
\mathbb{S}^{N}
, for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, to each critical point of the Steklov eigenvalues in a given conformal class. This point of view was used to study extremal problems for Steklov eigenvalues, see for instance
[
49
,
50
]
. In the case
n\geq 3
, an analogous property was proved by Karpukhin–Metras
[
24
]
. We believe that the present work could be of help for further developments of these optimization problems, see for instance
[
24
, Conjecture 1]
. When
n=2
and the target manifold on the boundary is
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the existence of free-boundary harmonic maps has been studied in various settings: for
\Sigma^{2}
as a disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
4
,
42
]
, for
\Sigma^{2}
as a polygon
[
57
]
, and for
\Sigma^{2}
as an annulus
[
18
]
.

The general strategy to study compactness properties of solutions to equations like (
1.3
) is by now well understood, see for instance
[
53
,
58
,
5
,
48
,
59
]
and in particular
[
30
]
for a systematic approach in dimension 2. Thanks to the
\varepsilon
-regularity proved in
[
39
,
36
]
, one obtains that a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{I}}
of
W^{1,n}
-bounded solutions to (
1.3
) converges strongly on
\Sigma^{n}
, away from a finite number of points, to some limit
u\in{\mathcal{I}}
which is also a solution to (
1.3
). At each of these points, one can blow-up a finite number of bubbles
(\omega_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\ell}
, defined as follows:

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a map
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bubble provided that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|d\omega|^{n}dx<+\infty
and

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}d^{*_{g_{\text{eucl}}}}(|d\omega|^{n-2}d\omega)&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
|\omega|&=&1&\text{ on }{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
|d\omega|^{n-2}{\partial}_{\nu}\omega&\perp&T_{\omega}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}&\text{ on }{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\end{array}\right.

where these equations are understood as in (
1.4
) and where
d^{*_{g_{\text{eucl}}}}
is the codifferential associated to the Euclidean metric.

This procedure of extraction of bubbles can be understood as a type of weak limit behavior, called weak bubbling convergence. In particular, one can show the following inequality, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}E_{n}(u_{k})\geq E_{n}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}E_{n}(\omega_{i}).

The question is whether equality holds in this limit. If so, we say that the strong bubbling convergence holds.
This problem was first studied in the case of harmonic maps on manifolds without boundary for
n=2
, see, for example,
[
12
,
51
,
6
,
21
,
48
,
13
]
. More recently, still for
n=2
, the case of harmonic maps with free boundary has been investigated in various settings by different authors (see, for instance,
[
28
,
29
,
22
,
35
]
). In this case, the question can be stated in terms of
\frac{1}{2}
-harmonic maps and we refer to
[
8
,
10
]
.

For
n\geq 3
, Mou and Wang
[
45
]
proved an energy identity for some Palais–Smale sequences associated with the
n
-harmonic energy, in the case of maps from a manifold without boundary into a Riemannian homogeneous space, see also
[
66
]
.
If the target manifold is not homogeneous, the following weaker result is known: a sequence of
W^{1,n}
-bounded
n
-harmonic maps must converge strongly to a limiting
n
-harmonic map, except at a finite number of points, see e.g.
[
65
,
43
]
. Although regularity properties of
n
-harmonic maps with values into homogeneous manifolds are now well studied, see e.g.
[
16
,
60
,
63
,
46
]
, we point out that the case of general manifolds is not yet fully understood. For recent progress on this topic, we refer to
[
55
,
44
,
37
,
38
]
. The energy identity for minimizing free-boundary
n
-harmonic maps was proved in
[
47
]
.

One of the motivation of the present work is to prove the strong bubbling convergence for sequences of free-boundary
n
-harmonic maps into a round sphere. However, we will consider the more general setting of sequences of free-boundary
(n+\alpha_{k})
-harmonic maps, where
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
. Studying a sequence of solutions to sub-critical variational problems to understand a critical one is reminiscent of the Sacks–Uhlenbeck approach
[
53
]
, where the authors examined the existence of harmonic maps from a closed, compact surface. When
\alpha_{k}>0
, it can be shown that the
(n+\alpha_{k}
)-energy satisfies the Palais–Smale condition. Hence, we can hope to find non-trivial free-boundary
(n+\alpha_{k})
-harmonic maps, either through minimization or min-max procedures, within the set of maps with prescribed degrees on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
, in the case where the target manifold is
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We then can expect to use an energetic argument and the strong bubbling convergence to obtain the existence of non-trivial solutions to (
1.4
) with prescribed degrees. We mention that a Sacks–Uhlenbeck approach to prove existence of minimizers of fractional energies in homotopy classes has been employed in
[
40
,
41
]
.

A weak energy identity for
\alpha
-harmonic maps from a closed surface to a general manifold, where coefficients possibly different from 1 appear in front of the energies of the bubbles, was derived by Li–Wang
[
33
]
. They also showed in
[
34
]
that we cannot expect the genuine energy identity (the one with all the coefficients equal to
1
) to hold when the homotopy classes degenerate. However, in some cases, the genuine energy identity is known to hold, cf.
[
13
,
6
,
25
]
. This is the case in particular when the target manifold is the sphere
[
32
]
or a homogeneous manifold
[
2
]
. We point out that
\alpha
-harmonic maps are still the object of ongoing research. For example, it is remarkable that
\alpha
-harmonic maps do not approximate every harmonic maps, and there is a selection process at work when using the
\alpha
-energy (see
[
26
,
27
]
). Moreover, the location of blow-up points can sometimes be described, as shown in
[
56
]
, and Morse index estimates can be obtained as explained in
[
11
]
(see also
[
9
,
19
]
).
The case of free-boundary
\alpha
-harmonic maps was considered in
[
23
]
where a weak energy identity is derived. Again, in the case where the target manifold is a sphere, the genuine energy identity holds, see
[
31
]
. We note that in the articles mentioned above,
\alpha
-harmonic maps are defined as critical points of
\int_{\Sigma^{2}}(1+|du|_{g}^{2})^{1+\alpha}d{\rm vol}_{g}
. This definition allows to avoid the degeneracy character of the
p\neq 2
-harmonic maps equations and to obtain elliptic estimates in a simpler way. We are able to deal directly with the degenerate
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic maps equation. Thus, to the best of our knowledge, our result seems new even in dimension
n=2
.

In the setting of
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic maps with values into the sphere, we prove a weak energy identity and, for sequences of
n
-harmonic maps, we obtain that the strong bubbling convergence always holds. First, we observe that no degeneracy behavior can occur in the interior of
\Sigma^{n}
, since the system reduces to
\Delta_{n+\alpha_{k},g}u_{k}=0
. The interior regularity for the
p
-Laplacian, as proved by Uhlenbeck
[
64
]
, shows that we always have local strong convergence. Thus, the only possible concentration points occur at the boundary of
\Sigma^{n}
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n,d\geq 2
and
(\Sigma^{n},g)
be a compact manifold with non-empty boundary. Let
(p_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[n,n+1]
be a sequence of numbers such that
p_{k}\to n
as
k\to\infty
. Let
M>0
and let
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of maps that satisfy:

1)

for all
k
,
u_{k}\in W^{1,p_{k}}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
|u_{k}|=1
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
;

2)

for all
\Phi\in W^{1,p_{k}}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\Phi\cdot u_{k}=0
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
, we have

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\langle|du_{k}|_{g}^{p_{k}-2}du_{k},d\Phi\rangle_{g}d{\rm vol}_{g}=0;

3)

\displaystyle{\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\ \|du_{k}\|^{p_{k}}_{L^{p_{k}}(\Sigma^{n})}\leq M
.

Then there exist

\bullet

u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
|u|=1
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
satisfying (
1.3
);

\bullet

a finite number
\ell
of bubbles
\omega_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
;

\bullet

\ell
sequences of points
(a_{k}^{i})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\Sigma^{n},1\leq i\leq\ell
, converging to points
a^{1},\ldots,a^{\ell}\in\partial\Sigma^{n}
;

\bullet

\ell
sequences of real numbers
(\lambda_{k}^{i})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
1\leq i\leq\ell
;

\bullet

a subsequence in
k
, (non-relabelled)

such that

a)

For all
1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell
, it holds

(1.6)

\max\left\{\frac{\lambda_{k}^{i}}{\lambda_{k}^{j}},\frac{\lambda_{k}^{j}}{\lambda_{k}^{i}},\frac{|a_{k}^{i}-a_{k}^{j}|}{\lambda_{k}^{i}+\lambda_{k}^{j}}\right\}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}{+\infty},

b)

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, the sequence
\left(u_{k}\left(\exp_{a^{i}}\left(a_{k}^{i}+\lambda_{k}^{i}\cdot\right)\right)\right)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to
\omega_{i}
in the strong
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{{\rm loc}}
topology on
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}
away from a finite set,

c)

Let
\lambda^{i}_{*}\coloneqq\displaystyle{\liminf_{k\to+\infty}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{i}\right)^{n-p_{k}}
. Then we have
\lambda_{*}^{i}\in[1,+\infty)
together with the following energy-identity

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}E_{p_{k}}(u_{k})=E_{n}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{*}^{i}E_{n}(\omega_{i}).

In particular when
p_{k}=n
for all
k
we obtain the energy identity for a sequence of free-boundary
n
-harmonic maps:

\lim_{k\to+\infty}E_{n}(u_{k})=E_{n}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}E_{n}(\omega_{i}).

In the case
d=n
, we can have a useful information on the degrees of
u_{k}
on the connected components of
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
:

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume that
d=n
, we call
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{m}
the connected components of
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, for
k
large enough we have that for all
1\leq i\leq m
,

(1.8)

\deg\left(u_{k}\big\rvert_{\Gamma_{i}}\right)=\deg\left(u\big\rvert_{\Gamma_{i}}\right)+\sum_{j\in\text{Conc}(\Gamma_{i})}\deg\left(\omega_{j}\big\rvert_{{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\right).

where
\text{Conc}(\Gamma_{i})=\Big\{j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}\colon a^{j}\in\Gamma_{i}\Big\}
.

We now comment on the method of the proof of Theorem
1.3
and the organization of the paper.

The first step is to show that near the boundary, if there is no energy concentration, then the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges strongly in
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{{\rm loc}}
. This is done in
Section

2
, where an
\varepsilon
-regularity result is obtained. Our starting point is the
\varepsilon
-boundary regularity for free-boundary
n
-harmonic maps obtained in
[
39
]
, where it is shown that such maps are
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\beta}\left(\overline{\Sigma^{n}};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
for some
0<\beta<1
. We need to adapt this result to obtain uniform estimates in
p\in[n,n+1]
for
p
-harmonic maps. At the
{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}
-level this is obtained by an adaptation of the arguments used in
[
39
]
. However, to obtain uniform
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\beta}
estimates, we cannot apply
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
as in
[
39
]
, since we require the estimates to be uniform. Instead, we perform the reflection near the boundary, following Scheven
[
54
]
. This leads to solutions of a critical
p
-Laplace system with respect to a metric that is
a priori
merely Hölder-continuous. Such systems have been recently studied by the first author
[
36
]
.

The second step is the study of bubbles. In particular, two classical ingredients that we need here are: an energy gap and a singularity removability result. They are proved in
Section

3
.

The key point in establishing an energy identity like
c)
is to prove a so-called no-neck energy property: we need to show that no energy remains in the regions between the bubbles, which are annuli of degenerating conformal type called the neck regions. To do so, we prove some energy comparison lemmas in
Section

4
. More precisely, the crucial ingredient, Lemma
4.2
, says that under an assumption of smallness of the energy, the energy of a free-boundary
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic map in an annulus intersecting the boundary is smaller than the energy of its non-constrained
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic extension in this annulus. When the map is constant on the boundary of the annulus, the energy of the
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic extension is easily estimated. In our case, the maps considered are almost constant on the boundaries and we can then show that the energy vanishes in neck regions. This argument can be understood as an alternative to Pohozaev identity, generally used to prove the no-neck property for the radial part of the gradient, see for instance
[
30
, Section 3]
.

We prove the strong bubbling convergence in the final
Section

5
. Our strategy follows the one in
[
5
,
45
]
. We introduce the energy-concentration function to detect bubbles, or more precisely their concentration points and concentration speeds. Using this procedure, near a given concentration point, we extract the bubbles in a precise order: from the most concentrated to the least concentrated. We then study the convergence of the blow-up sequence towards the bubbles. A difference with the case of harmonic maps or
H
-systems in dimension 2 is that after extracting the first bubble, it is not clear that the difference between
u_{k}
and the bubble still satisfies a PDE similar to the original sequence. Thus, we cannot use directly the
\varepsilon
-regularity lemma after having extracted the first bubble and the induction for extracting all bubbles is not as straightforward as in the elliptic case. To prove the no-neck energy property we depart from
[
45
]
where only the
W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}
-convergence towards the bubble is used and is obtained from the
{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}
-estimate, cf.
[
45
, Proposition 2.5]
. Since we work with sequences of
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic maps, we cannot use this proposition and we have to employ the
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}
-convergence that we obtained thanks to the results in
[
36
]
. Since multiple bubbles can appear at the same concentration point, we extract them generation by generation, and this iterative process is detailed in this article.

Open problems

We end this introduction with a few open problems:

(1)

We obtained a weak energy identity for sequences of
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic maps with a free boundary in a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, with
\alpha
tending to zero. It is very likely that the result remains true if
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is replaced by a homogeneous Riemannian manifold. In the case
n=2
, when the target manifold is a sphere and when
\Sigma^{2}
is a compact manifold without boundary, the genuine energy identity holds for sequences of “Sacks–Uhlenbeck”
\alpha
-harmonic maps
[
32
]
. The same result was recently proved for homogeneous targets
[
2
]
. The genuine energy identity also holds true when
n=2
in the free-boundary case when the target manifold is a sphere
[
31
]
. Hence, one may ask whether our weak energy identity could be improved into a genuine energy identity, i.e., with all the coefficients
\lambda_{*}^{i}
appearing in (
1.7
) equal to 1.

(2)

In dimension
n=2
, it is known that the only possible bubbles are the Blaschke products, i.e., products of Möbius maps, see for instance
[
4
, Lemma 3.5]
or
[
42
, Theorem 4.25]
. The proof of this fact relies on an argument involving the Hopf differential and can be viewed as an analogue of the fact that harmonic maps from
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
are automatically conformal, see
[
53
]
. However in higher dimension, Xu–Yang
[
67
]
constructed examples of
n
-harmonic maps from
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which are not conformal. We can ask if there exist non-conformal maps satisfying Definition
1.2
in any dimension
n\geq 3
.

(3)

For harmonic maps from compact surfaces, Li–Wang
[
33
]
proved that, when bubbling occurs, the images of the “necks” converge to geodesics in the target manifold. For harmonic maps with free boundaries, a more complicated phenomenon occurs, see
[
23
]
, but the images still converge to a curve satisfying an equation close to the geodesic equation. Is it possible to obtain similar results for
n
-harmonic maps in the case without boundary and in the case of free-boundary maps?

Notation

We denote by
B(a,r)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x-a|<r\}
the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and radius
r>0
. We denote
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x_{n}>0\}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x_{n}<0\}
with
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. We will often consider the restriction of a ball to the upper half-space
B(a,r)^{+}\coloneqq B(a,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. When considering the boundary of the intersection of a ball with the upper-half space we distinguish
{\partial}B(a,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and

(1.9)

{\partial}_{0}B(a,r)^{+}\coloneqq B(a,r)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}).

We also set
B(a,r)^{-}\coloneqq B(a,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}
. The balls in

B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(a,r)\coloneqq\{x\in\Sigma^{n}\colon d_{g}(x,a)<r\}

We also precise that, in a coordinate system and by using Einstein’s summation convention, if
u,\Phi\in W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})

\langle|du|_{g}^{n-2}du,d\Phi\rangle_{g}=\left(g^{ij}\left\langle{\partial}_{i}u,{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}g^{\lambda\mu}\left\langle{\partial}_{\lambda}u,{\partial}_{\mu}\Phi\right\rangle.

For

, the
p
-Laplace operator with respect to a metric

\Delta_{p,h}f=-d^{*_{h}}(|df|_{h}^{p-2}df)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det h}}{\partial}_{\lambda}\left(|df|^{p-2}_{h}h^{\lambda\mu}\sqrt{\det h}\,{\partial}_{\mu}f\right).

2.
Compactness outside concentration points

One first crucial argument to obtain Theorem
1.3
is an
\varepsilon
-compactness result. Such a result allows us to say that outside a finite number of points located on the boundary
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
, a sequence of
p_{k}
-harmonic maps with free boundary in a sphere with bounded
n
-energy strongly converges towards its limit. The goal of this section is to prove
Proposition

2.4
below, that is to say, if the
n
-energy is uniformly small on a given ball, then we obtain
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{{\rm loc}}
estimates. We start with a uniform
\varepsilon
-regularity result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p_{0}>n
. There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\beta_{0}>0
depending only on
p_{0},n,\Sigma,g
, and
d
such that the following holds.
Let
p\in[n,p_{0}]
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy the following properties:

1)

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}|u|&=&1&\text{ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n}\\
\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}&=&0&\text{ for all }\Phi\in W^{1,p}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ with }\Phi\cdot u=0\text{ on }{\partial}\Sigma^{n},\end{array}\right.

2)

the uniform bound
E_{p}(u)\leq M
,

3)

for some
x_{0}\in\Sigma^{n}
and
R>0
, it holds

(2.2)

\int_{B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R)}|du|_{g}^{n}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}<\varepsilon_{0}^{n}.

Then, there exists
C>0
depending only on
M,\,n,\,\Sigma,\,g,\,d,\,R
, and
p_{0}
such that
u~\in~{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta_{0}}\left(B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R/2);\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with the estimate

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta_{0}}(B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R/2))}\leq C.

Several remarks are in order before starting the proof of the above proposition. First, the interior regularity for
p
-harmonic (vectorial) functions is known (see, for instance,
[
61
,
64
]
) and does not require
\varepsilon
-smallness. It can also be checked that we can obtain uniform
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\beta}
regularity in the interior of
\Sigma^{n}
for
(n+\alpha)
-harmonic maps with the previous techniques, provided that
\alpha\geq 0
is bounded from above. Hence, we will focus on the regularity near boundary points.

Second, a maximum principle for
p
-harmonic (vectorial) functions is available, see
[
39
, Lemma A.1]
or
[
36
, Proposition 2.11]
, and this allows us to deduce that a solution
u\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
to (
2.1
) such that
|u|=1
on
{\partial}\Sigma
satisfies

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{n})}\leq 1.

Third, near a boundary point
x_{0}
, we can find an open set
V
contained in
B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R)
and a smooth diffeomorphism
\psi\colon B(0,r)^{+}\rightarrow V
, for some
r>0
. By making a change of variables, we obtain that the new map
\tilde{u}\coloneqq u\circ\psi
satisfies the following estimate, where
\tilde{g}=\psi^{*}g
:

\int_{B(0,r)^{+}}|d\tilde{u}|_{\tilde{g}}^{n}\ d{\rm vol}_{\tilde{g}}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Furthermore,
\tilde{u}
satisfies the following system:

\int_{B(0,r)^{+}}\left\langle|d\tilde{u}|_{\tilde{g}}^{p-2}d\tilde{u},d\Phi\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}}\,d{\rm vol}_{\tilde{g}}=0,

for all
\Phi\in W^{1,p}(B(0,r)^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\Phi\cdot\tilde{u}=0
on
{\partial}_{0}B(0,r)^{+}
where
{\partial}_{0}B(0,r)^{+}
is defined in (
1.9
).
Thus, for the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we may restrict ourselves to the case where
\Sigma^{n}
is an open half-disk.

The first step of the proof of Proposition
2.1
is to rewrite the weak formulation of the equation in a more convenient way. This is done in the following lemma due to
[
39
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
39
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B(0,r)^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy (
2.1
) and let

A_{ij}\coloneqq u^{i}\,du^{j}-u^{j}\,du^{i}.

Then, for all
1\leq i\leq n
and for all
\phi\in W^{1,p}(B(0,r)^{+})
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{i},d\phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{k},A_{ik}\phi\right\rangle_{g}+\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{i},d(|u|^{2}-1)\phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B(0,r)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}d\phi,d(|u|^{2}-1)u^{i}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We are now ready to sketch the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The proof follows the argument of
[
39
]
. Thus, we only sketch it here to show the reader that the constants involved in the estimates can be made uniform in the exponent
p\in[n,p_{0}]
.

As mentioned in one of the remarks following Proposition
2.1
, we can assume that
x_{0}=0
and
\Sigma^{n}\cap B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0})=B(0,r)^{+}
. We notice that, since
B(0,r)^{+}
is a Lipschitz domain, we can extend each
u
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in such a way that
\|u\|_{W^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,n}(B(0,r)^{+})}
(see, e.g.,
[
14
, Theorem 4.7]
). Let
y_{0}\in B(0,r)^{+}
, let
0<\rho<\frac{r}{4}
be such that
B(y_{0},4\rho)\subset B(0,r)^{+}
. For
\eta
a cut-off function satisfying
\eta\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\eta\equiv 1
in
B(0,1)
and
\eta\equiv 0
in
B(0,2)^{c}
we define

\displaystyle\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}\coloneqq\eta\left(\frac{x-y_{0}}{\rho}\right),

\displaystyle(u)_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}}=\frac{1}{|B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}|}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}}u\,d{\rm vol}_{g},

\displaystyle\tilde{u}\coloneqq\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}(u-(u)_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}}),

\displaystyle\hat{u}\coloneqq(1-\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)})\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}(u-(u)_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}})=(1-\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)})\tilde{u}.

Since
\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}\equiv 1
in
B(y_{0},\rho)

\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\leq\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}d\tilde{u},d\tilde{u}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We observe that

\displaystyle\langle d\tilde{u},d\tilde{u}\rangle_{g}=

\displaystyle\ \langle du,d\tilde{u}\rangle_{g}-\langle du,d\hat{u}\rangle_{g}

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\langle d\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}\otimes\tilde{u},du\rangle_{g}+\langle(u-(u)_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}})\otimes d\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)},d\tilde{u}\rangle_{g}.

Using
|d\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}|\leq\frac{C}{\rho}
, Young’s, and Poincaré’s inequalities we obtain that for any
\mu>0
,

\left|\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-2}\langle(\tilde{u}\otimes d\eta_{B(y_{0},\rho)}),du\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\rho}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}\setminus B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-1}|\tilde{u}|\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\\
\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}\setminus B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}+\frac{1}{p}\mu^{p-1}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right).

We thus get, for a constant
C
which does not blow up as long as
p
is bounded:

\displaystyle\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\leq

\displaystyle\ \left|\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-2}\langle du,d\tilde{u}\rangle_{g}\right|+\left|\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-2}\langle du,d\hat{u}\rangle_{g}\right|

\displaystyle+C\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}\setminus B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}+\mu^{p-1}\int_{B(y_{0},2\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p}\right).

We now estimates the two terms

\left|\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-2}\langle du,d\tilde{u}\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right|+\left|\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}|du|^{p-2}\langle du,d\hat{u}\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right|.

Actually we focus only on the first term since the second one can be treated analogously. By observing that
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by using the equation satisfied by
u
in the form of Lemma
2.2
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we write

\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{i},d\tilde{u}^{i}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=I+II+\frac{1}{2}III,

with

\displaystyle I=\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{k},A_{ik}\tilde{u}^{i}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g},

\displaystyle II=\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}du^{i},d(|u|^{2}-1)\tilde{u}^{i}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g},

\displaystyle III=\int_{B(y_{0},\rho)^{+}}\left\langle|du|_{g}^{p-2}d\tilde{u}^{i},d(|u|^{2}-1)u^{i}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

The terms
I,II
and
III
are estimated as in
[
39
, Proposition 2.1]
. The key point is that all these terms contain products of divergence-free and curl-free quantities which makes possible the use of the div-curl lemma in
[
7
]
. It can be checked that the constant appearing in that lemma does not blow-up as long as
p\in[n,p_{0}]
. The div-curl lemma involves the BMO semi-norm

[f]_{\mathrm{BMO}}\coloneqq\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-(f)_{B}|,

and for example, for the term
I
we find an estimate of the form

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq C\||du|^{p-2}A_{ik}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(y_{0},4\rho)^{+})}\|du\|_{L^{p}(B(yu_{0},4\rho)^{+})}[\tilde{u}]_{BMO}

\displaystyle\leq C\|du\|_{L^{p}(B(y_{0},4\rho)^{+})}^{p}[\tilde{u}]_{BMO}.

The Poincaré inequality allows to estimate all the BMO semi-norms by

[f]_{\mathrm{BMO}}=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-(f)_{B}|\leq C(n)\sup_{B}\|df\|_{L^{n}(B)}.

Then, the key difference is that we use the assumption

\int_{B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R)}|du|_{g}^{n}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}<\varepsilon_{0}^{n}

to derive decay estimates on
\|du\|_{L^{p}(B(y_{0},\rho)^{+})}^{p}
,
which in turn lead to the desired Hölder regularity via the Campanato characterization of Hölder spaces (see
[
39
, proof of Proposition 3.1]
).

One can see that all the constants appearing in the proof remain bounded for
p\in[n,p_{0}]
.

∎

Once we have obtained uniform
{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}
estimates up to the boundary for
p\in[n,n+1]
, we aim to improve these into
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\delta}
estimates. The strategy involves performing a reflection across the boundary
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
to reduce the problem of boundary regularity to a problem of interior regularity for a modified equation. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p_{0}>n
and
p\in[n,p_{0}]
. Consider
u\in W^{1,p}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying (
2.1
). Let
x_{0}\in{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
and consider a chart near
x_{0}
so that we can identify
B_{(\Sigma,g)}(x_{0},R)
with
B(0,R)^{+}
for
R>0
small enough.

Assume that
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}\left(\overline{B(0,R)^{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with the estimate
[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}(B(0,R))^{+}}\leq c_{\mathrm{hol}}
. Then, there exist
v\in W^{1,p}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}(B(0,R_{1});\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
0<R_{1}<R
, and
C,c_{0}>0
depending only on
n,p_{0},d,c_{\mathrm{hol}},\Sigma
, and
g
such that

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} {}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}(B(0,R_{1}))]}&\leq C[u]_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\beta}(B(0,R)^{+})},\\
v&=u\text{ in }B(0,R_{1})^{+},\\
\Delta_{p,h}v&=f(x,v,dv)\text{ in }B(0,R_{1}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
h
is a Lipschitz metric on
B(0,R_{1})
satisfying
\mathrm{Lip}(h)\leq 12\|g\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}
, and
f(\cdot,v,dv)\in L^{1}(B(0,R_{1});\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the pointwise estimate
|f(\cdot,v,dv)|\leq c_{0}|dv|^{p}
.

Proof.

As mentioned previously, the idea is to use a reflection of
u
across
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
, a technique previously used by Scheven in
[
54
, Lemma 3.1]
(see also,
[
39
, Proposition 5.3]
). For clarity we divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1: Definition of the geometrical reflection

We consider the reflection across the axis
\{x_{n}=0\}
denoted by
\sigma:B(0,R)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and defined by

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,-x_{n})
.
The differential of
\sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle d\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\text{id}_{n-1}&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle d\sigma(d\sigma)^{\top}=\text{id}_{n}.

Since
u
is Hölder continuous up to the boundary, there exists
R_{1}\in(0,R)
depending only on
c_{\mathrm{hol}}
such that
|u|>\frac{1}{2}
on
B(0,R_{1})^{+}
. Given
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\iota(q)\coloneqq\frac{q}{|q|^{2}}
, that is to say,
\iota
is the inversion with respect to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We now define the extension
v
of
u
to the full ball
B(0,R_{1})
:

\forall x\in B(0,R_{1}),\quad v(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x)&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{+},\\
&\iota\circ u\circ\sigma(x)&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}.\end{aligned}\right.

We also extend the metric
g
to the ball
B(0,R_{1})
:

\forall x\in B(0,R_{1}),\quad h(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}g(x)&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{+},\\
(\sigma^{-1})^{*}g(x)&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}.\end{cases}

Claim 1
.

The metric
h
is Lipschitz with the estimate
\mathrm{Lip}(h)\leq 12\|g\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(B(0,R_{1})^{+})
and
h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(B(0,R_{1})^{-})
. We only need to check the regularity at the junction. Let
x\in B(0,R_{1})
satisfying
x_{n}=0
. For any
y\in B(0,R_{1})\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}\right)
, it holds

\displaystyle|h(x)-h(y)|\leq|h(x)-h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n})|+|h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n})-h(y)|.

Since either
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n}),y\in B(0,R_{1})^{+}
or
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n}),y\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}
, the second term satisfies

\displaystyle|h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n})-h(y)|\leq\left(\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{+})}+\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{-})}\right)|x-(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1},0)|.

For the first term, since
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}\left(\overline{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}\right)
and
(\sigma^{-1})^{*}g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}\left(\overline{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}\right)
, we have:

\displaystyle|h(x)-h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},y_{n})|\leq\left(\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{+})}+\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{-})}\right)|y_{n}|.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle|h(x)-h(y)|\leq 2\left(\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{+})}+\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{-})}\right)|x-y|.

If now
y\in B(0,R_{1})^{+}
and
z\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}
, there exists
x\in B(0,R_{1})
such that
x_{n}=0
and

\displaystyle|y-x|+|z-x|\leq 3|y-z|.

Thus, it holds

\displaystyle|h(y)-h(z)|

\displaystyle\leq|h(y)-h(x)|+|h(x)-h(z)|

\displaystyle\leq 6\left(\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{+})}+\|h\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B(0,R_{1})^{-})}\right)|z-y|.

∎

Step 2: System satisfied by
v

Let
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,R_{1});\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We denote

\tilde{u}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}u(x)&\text{ for }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{+}\\
u\circ\sigma(x)&\text{ for }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-},\end{cases}\quad\quad\tilde{\Phi}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\Phi(x)&\text{ for }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{+}\\
\Phi\circ\sigma(x)&\text{ for }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}.\end{cases}

In particular, we have
v=\iota\circ\tilde{u}
in
B(0,R_{1})^{-}
. For
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we set

\Xi(q)\coloneqq d\iota(q)=\frac{\text{id}}{|q|^{2}}-2\frac{q\otimes q}{|q|^{4}}.

We observe that for all
x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle dv(x)=\Xi(\tilde{u}(x))d\tilde{u}(x).

We have that
\Xi
is symmetric and
|\Xi(q)w|=|w|/|q|^{2}
for all
q,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, we can see that
q/|q|
is an eigenvector of
\Xi
associated to the eigenvalue
-1/|q|^{2}
and every orthonormal basis of
(q/|q|)^{\perp}
is a basis of the eigenspace associated to the eigenvalue
1/|q|^{2}
. In particular, it holds

\displaystyle|dv|_{h}=\begin{cases}|du|_{g}&\text{ in }B(0,R_{1})^{+}\\
\frac{|d\tilde{u}|_{\tilde{g}}}{|\tilde{u}|^{2}}&\text{ in }B(0,R_{1})^{-},\end{cases}

and since
1/2\leq|\tilde{u}|\leq 1
we have that
|dv|_{h}
and
|d\tilde{u}|_{h}
are comparable in
B(0,R_{1})
.
For
q\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we define
\Pi(q)\coloneqq\text{id}-q\otimes q
, this is the orthogonal projection on
T_{q}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We also define
\Pi^{\perp}(q)\coloneqq q\otimes q
, the orthogonal projection on
(T_{q}\mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{\perp}=\text{span}\{q\}
.
Since
\iota^{-1}=\iota
, it holds, for any
x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}
:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Xi(v(x))^{-1}=(d\iota(v(x)))^{-1}=d\iota\big(\iota\circ v(x)\big)=d\iota\big(u\circ\sigma(x)\big)=\Xi(\tilde{u}(x))=\Xi(v(\sigma(x))).

For all
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,R_{1});\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define

\displaystyle\Phi_{e}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\Phi(x)+\Xi(v(\sigma(x)))\Phi(\sigma(x))],

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\Phi(x)-\Xi(v(\sigma(x)))\Phi(\sigma(x))].

From (
2.7
) we can check that the following relations hold for all
x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Phi_{e}(\sigma(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\Phi(\sigma(x))+\Xi(v(x))\Phi(x)]=\Xi(v(x))\Phi_{e}(x),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}(\sigma(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\Phi(\sigma(x))-\Xi(v(x))\Phi(x)]=-\Xi(v(x))\Phi_{a}(x).

Now we remark, from the definition of
\Pi
and
\Xi
that for all
x_{0}\in{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\cap B(0,R_{1})
we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{e}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\Phi(x_{0})+\Xi(v(\sigma(x_{0})))\Phi(\sigma(x_{0}))\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\Pi(u(x_{0}))\Phi(x_{0})+\Pi^{\perp}(u(x_{0}))\Phi(x_{0})+\Pi(u(x_{0}))\Phi(x_{0})-\Pi^{\perp}(u(x_{0}))\Phi(x_{0})\Big]

\displaystyle=\Pi(u(x_{0}))\Phi(x_{0})\in T_{u(x_{0})}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

Hence
\Phi_{e}
is admissible as a test function for the free-boundary condition (
1.4
) and

(2.10)

\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}|du|_{g}^{p-2}\langle du,d\Phi_{e}\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0.

A change of variables combined with (
2.8
) shows that

\displaystyle 0=\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}|du|^{p-2}_{g}\langle du,d\Phi_{e}\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},d(\Phi_{e}\circ\sigma)\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|^{p-2}_{h}\langle d\tilde{u},d(\Xi(v)\Phi_{e})\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}.

On the other hand, by using (
2.9
) we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}|du|^{p-2}_{g}\langle du,d\Phi_{a}\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},d(\Phi_{a}\circ\sigma)\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|^{p-2}_{h}\langle d\tilde{u},d(\Xi(v)\Phi_{a})\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}.

We have that
v=\frac{\tilde{u}}{|\tilde{u}|^{2}}
on
B(0,R_{1})^{-}
and thus, we obtain

(2.13)

\forall x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-},\quad\Xi(v(x))=|\tilde{u}(x)|^{4}\,\Xi(\tilde{u}(x)).

Since
\Phi=\Phi_{e}+\Phi_{a}
, by using (
2.11
),(
2.12
), and (
2.13
), we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d(\Phi_{a}-\Phi_{e})\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}+\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},d\big(\Xi(v)\Phi\big)\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}+\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}.

We focus on the first term, and we observe that, for any

we have
d\tilde{u}(x)=\Xi(v(x))dv(x)
thanks to (
2.6
) and (
2.7
). Then,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},d\big(\Xi(v)\Phi\big)\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi+\Xi(v)d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle d\tilde{u},\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi\rangle_{h}+|\tilde{u}|^{4}\,|d\tilde{u}|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(\tilde{u})d\tilde{u},d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|\tilde{u}|^{2(p-2)}|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(v)\,dv,\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi\rangle_{h}+|\tilde{u}|^{4+2(p-2)}\,|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|\tilde{u}|^{2(p-2)}|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(v)\,dv,\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle+\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}\left(1-|\tilde{u}|^{2p}\right)|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}.

We decompose the left-hand side as an integral on
B(0,R_{1})^{-}
and
B(0,R_{1})^{+}
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|\tilde{u}|^{2p}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}+\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{+}}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Phi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|\tilde{u}|^{2(p-2)}|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(v)\,dv,\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}.

Now we define

(2.14)

m(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{+},\\
|\tilde{u}(x)|^{2p}&\text{ if }x\in B(0,R_{1})^{-}.\end{cases}

We have
m\in L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,p}(B(0,R_{1}))
with
m\geq 1/2
and the pointwise estimate
|dm|\leq p2^{2p}|du\circ\sigma|_{g\circ\sigma}
. We then find that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d(m\Phi)\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}\left[|\tilde{u}|^{2(p-2)}|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(v)\,dv,\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Phi\rangle_{h}+|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,(dm)\Phi\rangle_{h}\right]\,d{\rm vol}_{h}.

We consider the change of variables
\Psi\coloneqq m\Phi
and obtain for any
\Psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B(0,R_{1});\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{1})}|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,d\Psi\rangle_{h}d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B(0,R_{1})^{-}}|\tilde{u}|^{-4}|dv|_{h}^{p-2}\langle\Xi(v)\,dv,\big(d\Xi(v)\big)\Psi\rangle_{h}+|dv|^{p-2}_{h}\langle dv,(d\log m)\Psi\rangle_{h}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}.

Thus,
v
satisfies a system of the form (
2.5
).
∎

Finally, we obtain the following

-regularity result for free boundary

.

Let

. There exists

3)

3.
Properties of the bubbles

In this section, we first prove two properties which are fundamental to obtain the energy identity. The first one is an energy gap in
Lemma

3.1
. The second one is a singularity removability result in
Proposition

3.2
. Then we give some properties of the bubbles, defined in (
1.2
), that will also be useful in the proof of the main result Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{b}=\varepsilon_{b}(n)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth, bounded, and connected open set, and let
u\in W^{1,n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a solution to (
1.3
) for
\Sigma^{n}=\overline{\Omega}
. If
\|du\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon_{b}
, then
u
is constant.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and consider
\iota_{x_{0}}
the inversion in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
around
B(x_{0},1)
defined by
\iota_{x_{0}}(x)=(x-x_{0})/|x-x_{0}|^{2}
. Thanks to conformal invariance of the
n
-energy, the map
v\coloneqq u\circ\iota_{x_{0}}^{-1}
is a free-boundary
n
-harmonic map as well. That is to say,
v
satisfies on the noncompact domain
\tilde{\Omega}\coloneqq\iota_{x_{0}}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\Delta_{n}v&=&0&\text{ in }\tilde{\Omega},\\
|v|&=&1&\text{ on }\partial\tilde{\Omega},\\
|\nabla v|^{n-2}\partial_{\nu}v&\perp&T_{v}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}&\text{ on }\partial\tilde{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore,
v
satisfies
\|dv\|_{L^{n}(\tilde{\Omega})}\leq\varepsilon_{b}
. If
\varepsilon_{b}>0
is small enough, we can apply the
\varepsilon
-regularity of
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
. Indeed, the arguments of
[
39
]
can be applied to unbounded domains with bounded geometry, see the first paragraph in
[
39
, Section 2]
, under the assumption
v\in L^{\infty}
. This is satisfied in our case: since
|v|=1
on
{\partial}\tilde{\Omega}
, we obtain
|v|\leq 1
in
\tilde{\Omega}
by
[
36
, Proposition 2.11]
. Hence, there exist
\alpha>0
and
C>1
such that for any
p\in\partial\tilde{\Omega}
and any
R>0
, it holds

\displaystyle[v]_{C^{0,\alpha}\left(\tilde{\Omega}\cap B(p,R)\right)}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\alpha}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{n}(\tilde{\Omega})}.

By taking the limit
R\to+\infty
, we deduce that
v
is constant and
u=v\circ\iota_{x_{0}}
as well.
∎

We now prove the singularity removability result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x_{0}\in{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
, let
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\Sigma^{n}\setminus\{x_{0}\};\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
|u|=1
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
and such that

(3.1)

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\left\langle|du|^{n-2}du,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0,

for all
\Phi\in W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
{\rm supp\,}\Phi\subset\Sigma^{n}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and such that
\Phi\cdot u=0
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
.
Then
u
extends to a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
map that satisfies the equation (
3.1
) for all
\Phi\in W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\Phi\cdot u=0
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
0<R_{q}
with
R_{q}\to 0
as
q\to\infty
. Using the fact that in the critical Sobolev space a point has zero capacity (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 5.1.9]
) we can find
\xi_{q}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Sigma^{n}}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
such that
\xi_{q}=1
on
B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R_{q}^{2})
,
\xi_{q}=0
outside
B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R_{q})
and
\|d\xi_{q}\|_{L^{n}}\rightarrow 0
as
q\to+\infty
. For example we can take

\xi_{q}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B(x_{0},R_{q}^{2}),\\
\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{R_{q}}\right)\right)^{-1}\log\frac{R_{q}}{{\rm dist\,}_{(\Sigma,g)}(x,x_{0})}&\text{ if }x\in B(x_{0},R_{q})\setminus B(x_{0},R_{q}^{2}),\\
0&\text{ if }x\in B(x_{0},R_{q})^{c}.\end{cases}

For any
\Phi\in W^{1,n}\cap L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\Phi\cdot u=0
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
we have that
\Phi_{q}\coloneqq(1-\xi_{q})\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\Sigma^{n}}\setminus\{x_{0}\};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

d\Phi_{q}=d\Phi(1-\xi_{q})-\Phi\otimes d\xi_{q}.

Since
\Phi_{q}
is admissible in (
3.1
) we find that

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\left\langle|du|^{n-2}_{g}du,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}(1-\xi_{q})\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\left\langle|du|^{n-2}_{g}du,\Phi\otimes d\xi_{q}\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

By using the dominated convergence for the left-hand side and the Hölder inequality for the right-hand side we find that

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}\left\langle|du|^{n-2}du,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0.

Since this is valid for any
\Phi\in W^{1,n}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\Phi\cdot u=0
on
{\partial}\Sigma^{n}
we have found that
u
is a free-boundary
n
-harmonic map near
x_{0}
and by
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain that
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\beta}_{{\rm loc}}(\Sigma^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\beta>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\omega\in W^{1,n}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
Definition

1.2

(1)

has a limit at infinity, i.e.,

(2)

for all

(3)

If

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|d\omega|^{n}\,dx\geq\varepsilon_{b}^{n}

Proof.

Let
\pi:\overline{B(0,1)}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}

and

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{1}{x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n-1}^{2}+(1-x_{n})^{2}}\Bigl(2x_{1},\cdots,2x_{n-1},1-x_{1}^{2}-\dots-x_{n}^{2}\Bigr).

We define

\tilde{\omega}

. By the removability singularity result Proposition

and satisfies the equation in

\tilde{\omega}

4.
Some energy comparison lemmas

In this section we prove several results that will be used in the proof of our main result Theorem
1.3
. In particular Lemma
4.2
is the key result to prove the weak energy identity when more than one generation of bubbles are involved.

The triangle inequality states that for two functions
u
and
v
, we have
\|d(u+v)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|du\|_{L^{p}}+\|dv\|_{L^{p}}
. We will often use the following lemma, stating that if
\|dv\|_{L^{p}}
is small, then the inequality is nearly an equality.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq q_{\inf}\leq q_{\sup}<+\infty
and
(q_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[q_{\inf},q_{\sup}]
be a bounded sequence. Let
(U_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of open subsets of
(\Sigma^{n},g)
, let
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},(v_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be two bounded sequences of maps in
W^{1,q_{k}}(U_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\displaystyle{\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\ \|du_{k}\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}<\Lambda
. Assume that
\displaystyle{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\left\|{dv_{k}}\right\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}=0
. Then there exists
\theta_{k}\geq 0
such that
\theta_{k}\leq C\left\|{dv_{k}}\right\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}
with a constant
C
depending only on
q_{\inf},q_{\sup},\Lambda
and

\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad\int_{U_{k}}|d(u_{k}+v_{k})|_{g}^{q_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{U_{k}}|du_{k}|^{q_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}+\theta_{k}.

Proof.

We use the following inequality: for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
we have

(4.1)

\left||x|^{q_{k}}-|y|^{q_{k}}\right|\leq q_{k}|x-y|(|x|+|y|)^{q_{k}-1}.

Indeed, thanks to the fundamental theorem of calculus we can write that

\displaystyle|x|^{p_{k}}-|y|^{q_{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}(|tx+(1-t)y|^{q_{k}})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle q_{k}(x-y),(tx+(1-t)y)\rangle|tx+(1-t)|^{q_{k}-2}dt.

But
|tx+(1-t)y|\leq|x|+|y|
and thus we find that (
4.1
) holds.
Now we use inequality (
4.1
), Hölder inequality and Minkowski inequality to write that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{U_{k}}\left(|du_{k}+dv_{k}|_{g}^{q_{k}}-|du_{k}|_{g}^{q_{k}}\right)\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ q_{k}\int_{U_{k}}|dv_{k}|_{g}(|du_{k}|_{g}+|dv_{k}|_{g})^{q_{k}-1}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ q_{k}\left(\int_{U_{k}}|dv_{k}|_{g}^{q_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}\left(\int_{U_{k}}(|du_{k}|_{g}+|dv_{k}|_{g})^{q_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{q_{k}-1}{q_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ q_{k}\|dv_{k}\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}(\|du_{k}\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}+\|dv_{k}\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})})^{q_{k}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (\Lambda+1)^{q_{k}-1}q_{k}\|dv_{k}\|_{L^{q_{k}}(U_{k})}\coloneqq\theta_{k}.

∎

We now prove that if for a free-boundary
p
-harmonic map
u
one knows that
E_{n}(u)
is small, then we can estimate its
p
-energy by the
p
-energy of its unconstrained
p
-harmonic extension.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball,
p\in[n,n+1]
and
g
be a smooth metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Let
B^{+}\coloneqq B\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and let
u\in W^{1,p}(B^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
|u|=1
on
{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\cap B
and

(4.2)

\int_{B^{+}}\left\langle|du|^{p-2}_{g}du,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0

for all
\Phi\in W^{1,p}(B^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\Phi\cdot u=0
on
{\partial}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\cap B
and
\Phi=0
on
{\partial}B\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. There exists
\varepsilon_{*}>0
and
C>0
depending only on
n,d
and
g
, such that the following holds. Consider any annulus
A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})=B_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},g)}(x_{0},R_{2})\setminus B_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},g)}(x_{0},R_{1})\Subset B
where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we denote by
A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+}=A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Assume that
\int_{A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+}}|du|^{n}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\leq\varepsilon_{*}^{n}
. We define
v\in W^{1,p}(A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be the solution of

(4.3)

\int_{A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+}}\left\langle|dv|^{p-2}_{g}dv,d\Phi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0,

for all
\Phi
in
W^{1,p}(A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\Phi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\cap{\partial}A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})
, with the boundary condition

\int_{A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+}}|du|^{p}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\leq C\int_{A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})^{+}}|dv|^{p}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We note that the map

appearing in this lemma satisfies homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions
|dv|^{p-2}{\partial}_{\nu_{g}}v=0
on

to

in

with

on
{\partial}A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})

{\partial}_{\nu}v=0
on
{\partial}_{0}A(x_{0},R_{1},R_{2})
.

Proof.

We consider the maps
A_{ik}
defined in Lemma
2.2
. Thanks to Lemma
2.2
we see that Equation (
4.2
) is equivalent to the following equations: for all

and for all

with
\varphi=0
on
{\partial}B\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}}\left\langle|du|^{p-2}_{g}du^{i},d\varphi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}}\left\langle|du|^{p-2}_{g}A_{ik},du^{k}\varphi\right\rangle_{g}+\left\langle|du|^{p-2}_{g}du^{i},d(|u|^{2}-1)\varphi\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{B^{+}}u^{i}\,\left\langle u\otimes d\varphi,|du|^{p-2}du\right\rangle_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We set

Remark 4.4
.

We observe that

\displaystyle\frac{R_{2}^{n-p}-R_{1}^{n-p}}{n-p}=\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}\frac{dr}{r^{p-n+1}}\xrightarrow[p\to n]{}{\log(R_{2}/R_{1})}.

Proof.

Since
R_{2}
is smaller than the injectivity radius of

\psi:B(0,R_{2})\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\to B_{(\Sigma^{n},g)}(x_{0},R_{2})

,

•

•

Namely, we take

, for

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,1)\cap\Omega_{k}^{1,1}}|dv_{k}^{1,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k}^{1,1})^{p_{k}-n}\int_{B\left(a_{k}^{1,1}+\lambda_{k}^{1,1}x,\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\right)\cap B(0,r_{1})^{+}}|du_{k}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

In particular we have that for all

and for

\int_{B(x,1)\cap\Omega_{k}^{1,1}}|dv_{k}^{1,1}|^{n}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}<\varepsilon_{0}^{n}.

Now we distinguish two cases.

Case 1)

Assume that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dist\,}(a_{k}^{1,1},{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}+\infty.

In that case

in the sense that for any

and for

. Combining (
5.3
)-(
5.4
),
Proposition

2.4

in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}

\omega^{1,1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})

(5.6)

v_{k}^{1,1}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}\omega^{1,1}\text{ in }{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Claim 3
.

The map
\omega^{1,1}
has finite
n
-energy, is bounded and is
n
-harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence it is a constant and (
5.5
) cannot happen.

Proof.

Indeed, for any
R>0

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}|d\omega^{1,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{1})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{1})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega_{k}^{1,1}\cap B(0,R)}|dv_{k}^{1,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\lambda_{k}^{1,1})^{p_{k}-n}\int_{B(0,r_{1})^{+}\cap B\left(a_{k}^{1,1},\lambda_{k}^{1,1}R\right)}|du_{k}|_{g}^{p_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\leq M,

with a constant

-energy of the sequence

in

. Since this is valid for any

-energy of

.

By using (

in

. This implies that
\omega^{1,1}

[

: for any

, we have

\displaystyle\|d\omega^{1,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R))}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{R^{n}}\int_{B(0,2R)}|d\omega^{1,1}|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

The right-hand side converges to

. This shows that (
5.5
) cannot happen and we are only in the following second case.
∎

Case 2)

Up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dist\,}(a_{k}^{1,1},{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}A\in[0,+\infty).

In that case
\Omega_{k}^{1,1}
converges to a half-space. Up to a rotation and a translation we can assume that this is
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Again, thanks to (
5.3
)-(
5.4
),

2.4
and the convergence

as

in

, we obtain that there exists
\omega^{1,1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(5.8)

v_{k}^{1,1}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}\omega^{1,1}\text{ in }{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Claim 4
.

The map
\omega^{1,1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is not constant.

Proof.

As in Claim
3
, we can obtain that the map
\omega^{1,1}
has finite
n
-energy. By using (

) we also find that
\int_{B(0,1)^{+}}|d\omega^{1,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{1})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{1})}=\frac{\varepsilon_{\star}^{n}}{2}>0
and

is a non-trivial bubble in the sense of Definition

.
∎

We can repeat this argument near

to obtain bubbles

5.3.
Step 3: No-neck energy property for the first-generation bubble at a given point
x_{i}\in S
.

Recall from Lemma
3.3
that bubbles have a limit at infinity. We can also extend to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
every
\omega^{i,1}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}
by setting
\omega^{i,1}(x)=\omega^{i,1}(\sigma(x))
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}
with
\sigma(x)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},-x_{n})
. We define

\displaystyle w_{k}^{i,1}(x)\coloneqq u_{k}(x)-\left[\omega^{i,1}\left(\frac{x-a_{k}^{i,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}}\right)-\omega^{i,1}(\infty)\right]\quad\text{ for all }x\in B(0,r_{i})^{+}.

Note that
w_{k}^{i,1}
converges weakly towards
u
in
W^{1,n}(B(0,r_{i})^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The aim of this step is to prove an energy identity.

Claim 5
.

For all
1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}
let
\lambda_{*}^{i,1}\coloneqq{\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}}\ (\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}
. Then
\lambda_{*}^{i,1}
is finite with the following estimate

(5.9)

\lambda_{*}^{i,1}\in\left[1,\frac{M}{\varepsilon_{b}^{n}}\right],

where
M
appears in Theorem
1.3
and
\varepsilon_{b}
appears in
Lemma

3.1
. Furthermore the following energy identity holds:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|du_{k}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}-\lambda_{*}^{i,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}+o_{k}(1).

Proof.

We first prove the estimate (
5.9
) on the concentration speeds. It is clear that for
k
large enough
(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\geq 1
. We define

\forall x\in\Omega_{k}^{i,1}\coloneqq\frac{B(0,r_{i})^{+}-a_{k}^{i,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}},\quad\quad g_{k}^{i,1}(x)\coloneqq g\left(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}x\right).

By using a change of variables and the convergence in (
5.8
), for all
R>0
, we can write that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)^{n-p_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,R)\cap\Omega_{k}^{i,1}}\left|d\left[u_{k}\left(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\cdot\right)\right]\right|_{g_{k}^{i,1}}^{p_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{i,1}}\right)^{-1}\int_{B(a_{k}^{i,1},R\lambda_{k}^{i,1})\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|du_{k}|_{g}^{p_{k}}d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(0,R)^{+}}\left|d\omega^{i,1}\right|_{g(x_{i})}^{n}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}\right)^{-1}M.

This is valid for any
R
, and letting
R\to+\infty
we obtain the result with the help of Lemma
3.1
and
Lemma

3.3
. We now prove the energy identity. Let
R_{i}>1
to be determined later (it will be sent to
+\infty
). We decompose the energy of
w_{k}^{i,1}
as follows,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B\left(a_{k}^{i,1},R_{i}\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}+\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}\setminus B\left(a_{k}^{i,1},R_{i}\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{i})\cap\Omega_{k}^{i,1}}(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\left|d[u_{k}\left(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\cdot\right)-\omega^{i,1}]\right|^{p_{k}}_{g_{k}^{i,1}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{i,1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}\setminus B\left(a_{k}^{i,1},R_{i}\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ I+II.

We first consider the term
I
. First we use the strong convergence of the metric
g_{k}^{i,1}\to g(x_{i})
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}
as
k\to\infty
and the bound on
\lambda_{k}^{i,1}
obtained previously to deduce

\displaystyle I=(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B(0,R_{i})\cap\Omega_{k}^{i,1}}\left|d[u_{k}\left(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\cdot\right)-\omega^{i,1}]\right|^{p_{k}}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}+o_{k}(1).

Then, we use the local strong convergence of
u_{k}(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\cdot)
to
\omega^{i,1}
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and the convergence of
\Omega_{k}^{i,1}
towards
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
to obtain

(5.11)

I=(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B(0,R_{i})\cap\Omega_{k}^{i,1}}\left|d\left[u_{k}\left(a_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\cdot\right)\right]\right|^{p_{k}}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}\\
-(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B(0,R_{i})^{+}}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}+f_{I}(k,R_{i}),

where
f_{I}
is a function satisfying

\displaystyle\forall R>0,\ \ \ f_{I}(k,R)\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}0.

Now we consider the term
II
. Thanks to (
5.9
) and
Lemma

3.3
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}\setminus B\left(a_{k}^{i,1},R_{i}\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)}\left|d\left[\omega^{i,1}\left(\frac{\cdot-a_{k}^{i,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}}\right)\right]\right|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C(g)(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B(0,R_{i})}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C(g)\frac{M}{\varepsilon_{b}^{n}}\|d\omega^{i,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{p_{k}-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B(0,R_{i})}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}.

The right-hand side is independent of
k
and converges to 0 as
R_{i}\to\infty
. Hence we apply
Lemma

4.1
to obtain

(5.12)

\displaystyle II=\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}\setminus B\left(a_{k}^{i,1},R_{i}\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\right)}|du_{k}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}+f_{II}(k,R_{i}),

where, thanks to
Lemma

4.1
the function
f_{II}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{II}(k,R)\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B(0,R)}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

In particular, it holds
\displaystyle{\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{II}(k,R)\right)}=0.

Thus, by summing (
5.11
) and (
5.12
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}\left|du_{k}\right|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}-(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B(0,R_{i})^{+}}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}+f_{I}(k,R_{i})+f_{II}(k,R_{i}).

We can take a subsequence such that, with
\lambda_{*}^{i,1}=\displaystyle{\liminf_{k\to+\infty}}(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})^{n-p_{k}}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}-\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}\left|du_{k}\right|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{*}^{i,1}\int_{B(0,R_{i})^{+}}|d\omega^{i,1}|^{n}_{g(x_{i})}\,d{\rm vol}_{g(x_{i})}+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{II}(k,R_{i}).

Now taking the limit
R_{i}\to+\infty
, we obtain (
5.10
).
∎

5.4.
Step 4: Extraction of a possible second-generation bubble at a given point
x_{i}\in S

After extracting the first bubble, we have two possibilities:

Case 1)

Assume that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|du|^{n}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

Since
\left(w_{k}^{i,1}\right)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
weakly converges to
u
, we necessarily have, by Hölder inequality, that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{n}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|du|^{n}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We conclude that
w_{k}^{i,1}\rightarrow u
strongly in
W^{1,n}(B(0,r_{i})^{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have extracted all the bubbles and Theorem
1.3
is proved.

Case 2)

Assume that, up to a subsequence,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}>\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|du|^{n}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

Then some energy is remaining and the goal of Step 4 is to extract a second bubble.

We define

S_{i}=\left\{x\in\overline{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}\colon\quad\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(x,\rho)\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}>0\right\}.

It holds that
S_{i}=\{0\}
by assumption. Indeed, the sequence
(w_{k}^{i,1})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
cannot concentrate outside of the set of concentration of
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. We set

(5.15)

\varepsilon_{1}^{n}\coloneqq\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(0,\rho)\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}>0.

Heuristically,
\varepsilon_{1}
is the sum of the energy of all the remaining bubbles generated at the origin. We define

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{i,2}(t)=\sup_{x\in B(0,r_{i})^{+}}\int_{B(x,t)\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}.

We again have that
Q_{k}^{i,2}
is a continuous function with
Q_{k}^{i,2}(0)=0
and, for
k
large enough,
Q_{k}^{i,2}(r_{i})\geq\frac{7}{8}\varepsilon_{1}^{p_{k}}
. Hence there exist
\left(\lambda_{k}^{i,2}\right)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset(0,r_{i})
and
\left(a_{k}^{i,2}\right)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\overline{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}
such that

(5.16)

\int_{B\left(a_{k}^{i,2},\lambda_{k}^{i,2}\right)\cap B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=\min\left\{\frac{3\varepsilon_{1}^{p_{k}}}{4},\frac{3\varepsilon_{\star}^{p_{k}}}{4}\right\}.

The constant
\varepsilon_{\star}
is defined in (
5.2
). As in Claim
2
, we have
\lambda_{k}^{i,2}\to 0
and
a_{k}^{i,2}\to 0
when
k\to+\infty
. We now define

\forall x\in\Omega_{k}^{i,2}\coloneqq\frac{B(0,r_{i})^{+}-a_{k}^{i,2}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,2}},\quad v_{k}^{i,2}(x)=w_{k}^{i,1}(a_{k}^{i,2}+\lambda_{k}^{i,2}x)\text{ and }g_{k}^{i,2}\coloneqq g(a^{i,2}_{k}+\lambda_{k}^{i,2}x).

We have that

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\int_{\Omega_{k}^{i,2}}|dv_{k}^{i,2}|^{p_{k}}_{g_{k}^{i,2}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{i,2}}=\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}(\lambda_{k}^{i,2})^{p_{k}-n}\int_{B(0,r_{i})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{i,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}<+\infty.

Again we need to distinguish two cases:

Case (I)

We assume that
\frac{{\rm dist\,}(a_{k}^{i,2},{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{i,2}}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}+\infty
.
In that case, up to a subsequence,

(5.17)

v_{k}^{i,2}\rightharpoonup\omega^{i,2}\text{ in }W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Case (II)

We assume that
\frac{{\rm dist\,}(a_{k}^{i,2},{\partial}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{i,2}}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}A\in[0,+\infty).

In that case, up to a subsequence, a translation and a rotation, it holds

(5.18)

v_{k}^{i,2}\rightharpoonup\omega^{i,2}\text{ in }W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In any case we can prove the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

i)

If
\lambda_{k}^{i,2}=o(\lambda_{k}^{i,1})
then
|a_{k}^{i,2}-a_{k}^{i,1}|/\lambda_{k}^{i,1}\rightarrow+\infty
as

.

ii)

For all
1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}
, it holds that

(5.19)

\max\left\{\frac{\lambda_{k}^{i,2}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}},\frac{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{i,2}},\frac{|a_{k}^{i,1}-a_{k}^{i,2}|}{\lambda_{k}^{i,1}+\lambda_{k}^{i,2}}\right\}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}+\infty.

For simplicity, we fix
i=1
in the whole proof.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Let us start with
Item

i)
. By contradiction, if, up to a subsequence, we have that
(a_{k}^{1,2}-a_{k}^{1,1})/\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\xrightarrow[k\rightarrow+\infty]{}a
. Then we can write that

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{3\varepsilon_{1}^{p_{k}}}{4},\frac{3\varepsilon_{\star}^{p_{k}}}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(a_{k}^{1,2},\lambda_{k}^{1,2})\cap B(0,r_{1})^{+}}\left|d\left[u_{k}-\omega^{1,1}\left(\frac{\cdot-a_{k}^{1,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\right)\right]\right|_{g}^{p_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{k}^{1,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B\left(\frac{a_{k}^{1,2}-a_{k}^{1,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}},\frac{\lambda_{k}^{1,2}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\right)\cap\Omega_{k}^{1,1}}\left|d[u_{k}(a_{k}^{1,1}+\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\cdot)-\omega^{1,1}]\right|_{g_{k}^{1,1}}^{p_{k}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}.

But since
B\left(\frac{a_{k}^{1,2}-a_{k}^{1,1}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}},\frac{\lambda_{k}^{1,2}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\right)\rightarrow B(a,0)
when
k\to+\infty
and since, from (
5.8
), we know that
u_{k}(a_{k}^{1,1}+\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\cdot)\rightarrow\omega^{1,1}
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R}^{d})
we can use (

) to deduce that the right-hand side of (

) goes to zero as

. This provides a contradiction.

We now prove
Item

ii)
. Suppose (
5.19
) were false, then there would exist
R>0
such that
R^{-1}\leq\frac{\lambda_{k}^{1,2}}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}}\leq R
and
\frac{|a_{k}^{1,1}-a_{k}^{1,2}|}{\lambda_{k}^{1,1}+\lambda_{k}^{1,2}}\leq R
. By (
5.8
) and (

), it holds

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{3\varepsilon_{1}^{n}}{4},\frac{3\varepsilon_{\star}^{n}}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{B\left(a_{k}^{1,2},\lambda_{k}^{1,2}\right)\cap B(0,r_{1})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{1,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B\left(a_{k}^{1,1},(R^{2}+2R)\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\right)\cap B(0,r_{1})^{+}}|dw_{k}^{1,1}|^{p_{k}}_{g}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{k}^{1,1})^{n-p_{k}}\int_{B(0,R^{2}+2R)\cap\Omega_{k}^{1,1}}\left|d\left[u_{k}(a_{k}^{1,1}+\lambda_{k}^{1,1}\cdot)-\omega^{1,1}\right]\right|^{p_{k}}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}\,d{\rm vol}_{g_{k}^{1,1}}\xrightarrow[k\to+\infty]{}0.

We obtained a contradiction and thus (
5.19
) holds.

We skip the rest of the proof of the extraction of the 2nd generation bubbles since it can be proved as in the iteration procedure that we will write in details in the next step. We just state the two remaining main facts of this step:

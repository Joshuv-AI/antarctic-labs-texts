Centered Takagi–van der Waerden functions and their Lipschitz derivatives
Oleksandr V. Maslyuchenko, Ziemowit M. Wójcicki

Abstract
Using a modification of a generalized Takagi-van der Waerden function on a metric space we prove that for any closed subset of a metric space without isolated points there exists a continuous function such that its big and local Lipschitz derivatives are equal to infinity exactly on this set. Moreover, if given space is hermetic (for example, if it is normed) then the little Lipschitz derivative has the same property.



keywords

big Lipschitz derivative, little Lipschitz derivative, local
Lipschitz derivative, Takagi–van der Waerden function, hermetic space

1
Introduction

Famous Stepanoff’s theorem
[
11
]
asserts, that
any function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is differentiable
on almost every point of set
\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\Lip f(x)<\infty\right\}
,
where
\Lip f
is so called big Lipschitz derivative of a function
f
.
Stepanoff’s theorem is itself a generalization of Rademacher’s theorem
[
9
,
4
]
,
which states, that Lipschitz functions acting from an open subset of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, are almost everywhere differentiable.
Another two kinds of Lipschitz derivatives are known as the
little Lipschitz derivative
\lip f
and the local Lipschitz derivative
\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f
.
Strightforward connetction of Lipschitz derivatives to classical
notions of differentiability can be seen in the following fact:
if
f
acts from an open subset
G
of some normed space into an another normed space,
and there exists the Fréchet derivative
f^{\prime}
of function
f
then, indeed,

\lip f(x)=\Lip f(x)=\left\|f^{\prime}(x)\right\|
for all
x\in G
, see for example
[
5
]
.
It turns out that
\Lip f
cannot be replaced by
\lip f
in the Stepanoff’s theorem,
as shown in
[
1
]
. In general, working with
\lip f

is more difficult, than with
\Lip f
.
Interest in the little Lipschitz derivative is of a different origin:
together with the big Lipschitz derivative they play a prominent
role in investigations of differentiable structures on measure
metric spaces, introduced by Cheeger in
[
3
]
.
They were particularly extensively used in follow-up
works by Keith in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

Lately, there has been an interest in the following
problem: given a subset
A
of a space
X
, is there
a continuous function
f
defined on
X
, such that
Lipschitz derivatives of
f
are infinite exactly on set
A
?
For instance, in the paper
[
2
]
of Buczolich, Hanson, Rmoutil, Zürcher,
some partial answers to this problems were given for subsets of the real line.
In recent paper
[
10
]
Rmoutil and Zürcher proved, that for any

F_{\sigma\delta}
-set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of Lebesgue measure zero, there exists
a continuous non-decreasing function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lip f(x)=\infty
for
x\in A
and
\lip f
is finite otherwise.
On the other hand, in our previous paper
[
8
]
, we considered the case where
A=G

is an open set in an arbitrary metric space and constructed a continuous function

f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
\Lip f=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f=\infty
exactly on
G
, by
generalizing the classical nowhere-differentiable function of Takagi-van der Waerden.
Some additional assumptions on
X
allowed us to
achieve also the equalities
\lip f=\Lip f=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f=\infty
strictly on
G
.

In this paper, we consider a closed subset
A
of a metric space
X

and we introduce the Takagi–van der Waerden function centered on
A
.
We prove, that if
A
does not contain isolated points of
X
, then

\Lip f(x)=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)=\infty
if and only if
x\in A
, where
f
is
the centered Takagi–van der Waerden function.
Moreover, if
X
has some additional property (for example, if
X
is a normed space),
then
\lip f=\Lip f=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f=\infty
exactly on
A
.

2
Preliminary notions

Throughout this paper
X
will be a metric space.
We denote by
\left|\cdot-\cdot\right|_{X}
the metric on
X

and by
X^{d}
the set of non-isolated points of
space
X
. By
\complement{A}
for any set
A\subseteq X

we always mean the complement of a set
A
in a space
X
, i.e.,

\complement{A}=X\setminus A
. The open and closed balls in
X
with origin at
x\in X
and radius
r>0

will be denoted by
B(x,r)
and
\overline{B}(x,r)
respectively.
The distance from a point
x
to a set
A
will be denoted with
d(x,A)
.
The generalized ball is defined as
B(A,r)=\big\{x\in X\colon d(x,A)<r\big\}
.
Through this paper, we will assume, that
\sup\varnothing=0
.

For any function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote

\Lip^{r}f(x)=\sup_{u\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{r}\left|f(x)-f(u)\right|,\;x\in X

and

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in X\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|f(u)-f(v)\right|}{\left|u-v\right|_{X}}.

Then, the
big Lipschitz derivative

\Lip f(x)
of
a function
f
at point
x\in X
is defined by the formula

\Lip f(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\Lip^{r}f(x),

(1)

and the
little Lipschitz derivative

\lip f(x)
of a
function
f
at point
x\in X
is defined by the formula

\lip f(x)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\Lip^{r}f(x),

(2)

The
local Lipschitz derivative

\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)
of a
function
f
at point
x\in X
is defined by the formula

\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)=\limsup_{(u,v)\to(x,x)}\frac{\left|f(u)-f(v)\right|}{\left|u-v\right|_{X}}=\inf_{r>0}\left\|f\big|_{B(x,r)}\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}.

(3)

Since we have assumed that
\sup\varnothing=0
,
the equality
\lip f(x)=\Lip f(x)=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)=0
holds for any
isolated point
x
.

Let us note the important identity which will be often useful:

\Lip f(x)=\limsup_{u\to x}\frac{\left|f(u)-f(x)\right|}{\left|u-x\right|_{X}}.

(4)

Indeed, the equation (
4
) is
often used as definition of the big Lipschitz derivative.
For proof of equivalence of (
4
)
and (
1
) see
(
8
, Proposition 2.1)
.
Analogical equality for
\lip f
does not hold.
To be more precise,

\liminf\limits_{u\to x}\frac{\left|f(u)-f(x)\right|}{\left|u-x\right|_{X}}\leq\lip f(x)

and the inequality can be strict.

Now we define certain special sets associated with a function
f
as follows

•

L^{\infty}(f)=\left\{x\in X\colon\Lip f(x)=\infty\right\}
,

•

\ell^{\infty}(f)=\left\{x\in X\colon\lip f(x)=\infty\right\}
,

•

\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=\left\{x\in X\colon\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)=\infty\right\}
.

It is important to note that passing to an equivalent metric
can change the sets
L^{\infty}(f)
,
\ell^{\infty}(f)
and
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
Take
X=\mathbb{R}
,
f(x)=x
,
x\in X
and consider metric given as

\left|x-y\right|_{1}=\left|x^{3}-y^{3}\right|
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Obviously, metric
\left|\cdot-\cdot\right|_{1}
is equivalent to standard Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}
.
In Euclidean metric, we have
\lip f(0)=1
.
On the other hand,
\lip f(0)=+\infty
, when metric
\left|\cdot-\cdot\right|_{1}
is considered.
In the first case,
0\notin\ell^{\infty}(f)
. In the second case,
0\in\ell^{\infty}(f)
.

Proposition 2.1

(
(
8
, Proposition 3.4.)
)
.

For any continuous function
{f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}}
the set
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)

is closed,
L^{\infty}(f)
is a
G_{\delta}
-set in
X
and

\ell^{\infty}(f)
is an
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set in
X
.

Hereby, we consider the following

Problem 1

(
\ell
-
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem)
.

Is a subset
A
of metric space closed
if and only if there exists a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
such that
\ell^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
?

We will at first tackle the following partial case:

Problem 2

(
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem)
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
A\subseteq X^{d}
.
Is
A
closed if and only if there exists a
continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
such that
L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
?

3
Centered Takagi-van der Waerden functions

Maximal separated sets.

A subset
S
of a metric space
X
is called

•

\varepsilon
-dense
in
X
if for any
x\in X

there exists
s\in S
such that
\left|x-s\right|_{X}<\varepsilon
.

•

\varepsilon
-separated
if
\left|s-t\right|_{X}\geq\varepsilon

for any
s,t\in S
.

•

maximal
\varepsilon
-separated
if it is
\varepsilon
-separated
in
X
and for any
\varepsilon
-separated set
T\supseteq S
we have that
T=S
.

Remark 3.1
.

For any subset
S
of a metric space
X

the function
{x\mapsto d(x,S)}
is
1
-Lipschitz.
Moreover,
S
is
\varepsilon
-dense if and only if
d(x,S)<\varepsilon
for any
x\in X
.

Using Teichmüller-Tukey Lemma or equivalent tools
(like Kuratowski–Zorn Lemma) we can easily prove the following

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\varepsilon
-separated set
S_{0}
in
X
there
exists a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated set
S
in
X

containing
S_{0}
.

Another easy but important property
of maximal
\varepsilon
-separated sets
is given by the following

Proposition 3.3
.

Any
\varepsilon
-separated subset
S
of a space
X
is
a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated set in
X
if
and only if
S
is
\varepsilon
-dense in
X
.

Definition of centered Takagi–van der Waerden function.

Let
A\subseteq X
be a closed set,
a>b\geq 1
and
c>0
be some real numbers.
We define a
Takagi–van der Waerden function of a type
(a,b,c)

centered at a set
A
as a function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{n}d(x,T_{n}),\;x\in X,

where

S_{n}
is a maximal
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-separated set in
G_{n}=B(A,\frac{c}{a^{n}})
and
T_{n}=\complement{G_{n}}\cup S_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We will also shortly call
f
a
TW
-function of a given type.
Note, that the sets
T_{n}
defined above are
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-dense in
X
.
Furthermore, if the sequence of sets
\left({T}_{n}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is ascending,
we will say that
f
is
Takagi-van der Waerden function
of a monotone type
(a,b,c)
centered at
A
.

In fact, we can always construct a sequence

\left({T}_{n}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
recursively to be ascending.
Denote
F_{n}=\complement{G_{n}}
.
Obviously,
F_{n}\subseteq F_{n+1}
.
If
S_{n}
is
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-separated in
G_{n}
then
S_{n}^{\prime}=S_{n}\cap G_{n+1}
is
\frac{1}{a^{n+1}}
-separated in
G_{n+1}
.
Therefore, there exists a maximal
\frac{1}{a^{n+1}}
-separated set
S_{n+1}\supseteq S_{n}^{\prime}
in
G_{n+1}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle T_{n}=F_{n}\cup S_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq F_{n+1}\cup S_{n}

\displaystyle=F_{n+1}\cup(S_{n}\setminus F_{n+1})=F_{n+1}\cup(S_{n}\cap G_{n+1})

\displaystyle=F_{n+1}\cup S_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq F_{n+1}\cup S_{n+1}=T_{n+1}.

We will denote by
s_{n}
and
r_{n}
be the
n
-th
partial sum and the
n
-th reminder of
f
respectively:

s_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b^{k}d(x,T_{k})\text{ and }r_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b^{k}d(x,T_{k}),\text{ for }x\in X.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
a>b\geq 1
,
c>0
.
Assume, that
f
is TW-function of type
(a,b,c)

centered at a closed set
A\subseteq X
.
Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the following
conditions hold:

(i)

f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function such that
0\leq f(x)\leq\frac{a}{a-b}
,
x\in X
;

(ii)

r_{n}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function such that
0\leq r_{n}(x)\leq\frac{b^{n}}{(a-b)a^{n-1}}
,
x\in X
.
Moreover, we have
r_{n}(x)=0
for all
x\in F_{n}=\complement{B(A,c/a^{n})}
;

(iii)

if
b>1
then
s_{n}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with the constant
\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}
.

Proof.

All conditions (i)-(iii) are trivially true for an isolated point
x
.
Thus, in the following we consider
x\in X^{d}
.

(ii).
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If
x\in F_{n}
, then
d(x,T_{k})=0
since
F_{n}\subseteq F_{k}\subseteq T_{k}

for any
k\geq n
. Thus,
r_{n}(x)=0
.

Consider
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
x\in G_{k}
.
Therefore,
d(x,S_{k})<\frac{1}{a^{k}}
, by Remark
3.1
.
Since
S_{k}\subseteq T_{k}
, we have

b^{k}d(x,T_{k})=b^{k}\inf_{t\in T_{k}}\left|x-t\right|_{X}\leq b^{k}\inf_{s\in S_{k}}\left|x-s\right|_{X}=b^{k}d(x,S_{k})<\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}.

In the case when
x\notin G_{k}
we have
b_{k}d(x,T_{k})=0<\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}
.
Then, we conclude that

0\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b^{k}d(x,T_{k})\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}<\infty,

so the series
r_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b^{k}d(x,T_{k})
is uniformly convergent for any
x

and as a result, function
r_{n}
is continuous.
Moreover, we have the following estimation

r_{n}(x)\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}=\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{n}}{1-\frac{b}{a}}=\frac{b^{n}}{(a-b)b^{n-1}}.

(i). From
f=r_{0}
we conclude that
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

(iii). By Remark
3.1
we have

|s_{n}(x)-s_{n}(y)|\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}b^{k}\big|d(x,S_{k})-d(y,S_{k})\big|\leq

\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}b^{k}\left|x-y\right|_{X}=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}\left|x-y\right|_{X}\leq\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}\left|x-y\right|_{X}

for any
x,y\in X
.
∎

4
Solution of
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
A\subseteq X^{d}
be a closed set,

a>b>2
,
c\geq 1
be real numbers, and
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a TW-function
of monotone type
(a,b,c)
centered at
A
. Then

L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=A
.

Proof.

We will denote
\varphi_{n}(x)=d(x,T_{n})
.
Fix
x\in A
. We consider two cases.

The first case:

\displaystyle x\in\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}
.
Let
n_{0}
be such that
x\in S_{n_{0}}
.
Since
x\in A\subseteq X^{d}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists

u_{n}\in B(x,\frac{1}{2a^{n}})\setminus\{x\}
. Denote
\rho_{n}=\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We have
0<\rho_{n}<\frac{1}{2a^{n}}
and
a>1
, so
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}=0
.
Moreover, for any
n\geq n_{0}
we have
x\in S_{n_{0}}\subseteq T_{n_{0}}\subseteq T_{n}
,
which implies that
\varphi_{n}(x)=0
.
Hence, we obtain the equality

r_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b^{k}\varphi_{k}(x)=0,\;n\geq n_{0}.

(5)

Fix
n\geq n_{0}
.
We will show, that
\varphi_{n}(u_{n})=\rho_{n}
.
Let
t\in T_{n}
. Then, either
t\in\complement{G_{n}}
or
t\in S_{n}
.
Clearly, in the first case, we have
\left|t-x\right|_{X}\geq\frac{c}{a^{n}}\geq\frac{1}{a^{n}}
.
In the second case we also have
\left|t-x\right|_{X}\geq\frac{1}{a^{n}}
,
since
S_{n}
is
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-separated and
t\neq x\in T_{n}\cap G_{n}=S_{n}
.
For any
t\in T_{n}\setminus\{x\}
we have the following estimations

\displaystyle\left|u_{n}-t\right|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\left|t-x\right|_{X}-\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{a^{n}}-\frac{1}{2a^{n}}=\frac{1}{2a^{n}}>\rho_{n}=\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}.

This shows, that

\varphi_{n}(u_{n})=\min\limits_{t\in T_{n}}\left|u_{n}-t\right|_{X}=\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}=\rho_{n}
.
Note, that

r_{n}(u_{n})=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b^{k}\varphi_{k}(u_{n})\geq b^{n}\varphi_{n}(u_{n})=b^{n}\rho_{n},

which yields the following inequality

\left|r_{n}(u_{n})-r_{n}(x)\right|=r_{n}(u_{n})\geq b^{n}\rho_{n}.

(6)

Next, since

s_{n}
is a
\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}
-Lipschitz function
by Proposition
3.4
(iii)
,
we conclude

\left|s_{n}(u_{n})-s_{n}(x)\right|\leq\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}=\frac{b^{n}\rho_{n}}{b-1}.

(7)

Now, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|f(u_{n})-f(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\big(r_{n}(u_{n})+s_{n}(u_{n})\big)-\big(r_{n}(x)+s_{n}(x)\big)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\big(r_{n}(u_{n})-r_{n}(x)\big)+\big(s_{n}(u_{n})-s_{n}(x)\big)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|r_{n}(u_{n})-r_{n}(x)\right|-\left|s_{n}(u_{n})-s_{n}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\geq b^{n}\rho_{n}-\frac{b^{n}\rho_{n}}{b-1}=\frac{b-2}{b-1}b^{n}\rho_{n}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\left|f(u_{n})-f(x)\right|\geq\frac{b-2}{b-1}b^{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\infty,

because
b>2
.
We have found the sequence
\left({u}_{n}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
, such that
u_{n}\to x
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left|f(u_{n})-f(x)\right|}{\left|u_{n}-x\right|_{X}}=\infty,

which means that

\Lip f(x)=\limsup_{u\to x}\frac{\left|f(u)-f(x)\right|}{\left|u-x\right|_{X}}=\infty.

The second case:

\displaystyle x\notin\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
So,
x\in G_{n}=B(A,c/a^{n})
and
x\notin S_{n}
.
Hence,
x\notin\complement G_{n}\cup S_{n}=T_{n}
.
Then
\varphi_{n}(x)>0
. Since
\frac{b}{2}>1
,
we have
\inf\limits_{t\in T_{n}}\left|x-t\right|_{X}=\varphi_{n}(x)<\frac{b}{2}\varphi_{n}(x)
.
So, there exists
t_{n}\in T_{n}
such that
\rho_{n}=\left|t_{n}-x\right|_{X}<\frac{b}{2}\varphi_{n}(x)
.
Since
T_{n}
is an
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-dense set,
Remark
3.1
yields the inequality

\varphi_{n}(x)<\frac{1}{a_{n}}
.
Then
\rho_{n}<\frac{b}{2a^{n}}
.
Next, observe that
\varphi_{k}(t_{n})=0
for
k\geq n
, since

t_{n}\in T_{n}\subseteq T_{k}
. Therefore,

r_{n}(t_{n})=0
.
Similarly to the previous case, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-f(t_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|r_{n}(x)+s_{n}(x)-r_{n}(t_{n})-s_{n}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\geq r_{n}(x)-\left|s_{n}(x)-s_{n}(t_{n})\right|

\displaystyle\geq b^{n}\varphi_{n}(x)-\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}\left|x-t_{n}\right|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq b^{n}\rho_{n}\frac{2}{b}-\frac{b^{n}\rho_{n}}{b-1}=\frac{b-2}{b(b-1)}b^{n}\rho_{n}.

Thus,
\left|x-t_{n}\right|_{X}=\rho_{n}\to 0
and
\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\left|f(x)-f(t_{n})\right|\geq\frac{b-2}{b(b-1)}b^{n}\to\infty
.
Again, we have
\Lip f(x)=\infty
.

Thus far, combining both cases,
we have shown that
A\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)
.
Then
A\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
We will prove, that
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq A
.
Consider
x\in X\setminus A
.
Then, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

x\notin\closure{G_{n_{0}}}
. The set
U=\complement\closure{G_{n_{0}}}
is
a neighborhood of the point
x
and for any
u\in U
we have

u\in\complement{\closure{G_{n_{0}}}}\subseteq T_{n_{0}}\subseteq T_{k}
,
so
\varphi_{k}(u)=0
for any
k\geq n_{0}
.
This means that
f(u)=s_{n_{0}}(u)
for any
u\in U
.
Therefore, by Proposition
3.4
(iii)
we conclude that

\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}f(x)=\mathop{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{ip}}s_{n_{0}}(x)\leq\dfrac{b^{n_{0}}}{b-1}<\infty.

This means, that
x\in X\setminus\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
We have shown that
X\setminus A\subseteq X\setminus\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
,
therefore
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq A
.
Thus,
A=L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
∎

The previous theorem allow us to give a solution
of the
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A
be a subset of a metric space
X
.
Then, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a closed set which does not contain isolated points
of
X
,

(ii)

there exists a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)=A.

Proof.

Follows from Proposition
2.1

and Theorem
4.1
.
∎

5
Solution of
\ell
-
L
-
\mathbb{L}
-problem in a hermetic space

The
hermeticity of a metric space
X
at a point
x\in X^{d}

was defined in
[
8
]
by the formula

H(X,x)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{r}\sup_{u\in B(x,r)}\left|u-x\right|_{X},

and the
hermeticity of
X
as the number
H(X)=\inf\limits_{x\in X^{d}}H(X,x)
.
We say, that
X
is hermetic if
H(X)>0
.

If we denote by
d_{x}(u):=\left|x-u\right|_{X}
, then
H(X,x)=\lip d_{x}(x)
,
x\in X
.
As a result, we get

Remark 5.1
.

Let
Y
be an open subspace of a metric space
X

and
x\in Y
. Then
H(x,Y)=H(x,X)
. In particular,
an open subset of a hermetic space is hermetic as
well.

The next proposition gives a geometric intuition behind hermeticity.

Proposition 5.2

(
(
8
, Proposition 6.2)
)
.

Let
X
be a hermetic metric space,

x\in X
and
0<\lambda<H(X,x)
.
Then there exists a number
R=R(X,x,\lambda)>0
such that
for any
r\in\left(0\,;R\right]
there exists
u\in X

with

\lambda r\leq\left|x-u\right|_{X}\leq r.

(8)

The following lemma is important for the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 5.3

(
(
8
, Lemma 6.4)
)
.

Suppose that
Y
is a metric space,
x\in Y^{d}
and
H(Y,x)>0
.
Fix real numbers
0<\lambda<H(Y,x)
and
0<\varepsilon\leq R
, where
R=R(Y,x,\lambda)

is the number as in Proposition
5.2
.
Moreover, let
E
be a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated set in
Y

and denote
\psi(y)=d(y,E)
for any
y\in Y
.
Then, there exists
u\in\overline{B}(x,\varepsilon)
such that

\left|\psi(u)-\psi(x)\right|\geq\frac{\lambda\varepsilon}{8}.

Now we can solve
\ell
-
L
-
\mathbb{L}
problem for a hermetic space.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X
be a hermetic metric space
and let
A\subseteq X^{d}
be a closed subset of
X
.
Let
a>b>1+\frac{8}{H(X)}
be real numbers.
Then, there exists
c>0
such that for

TW
-function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
of type
(a,b,c)

centered at set
A
, equalities

\ell^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=A

hold.

Proof.

Observe that
\frac{1}{b-1}<\frac{H(X)}{8}
.
Let
\alpha>0
be such that

\frac{1}{b-1}+\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}<\frac{H(X)}{8}.

Then, there exists
\lambda<H(X)
such that

\frac{1}{b-1}+\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}<\frac{\lambda}{8}.

Choose
c\geq\alpha+1
and let
f
be TW-function of
(a,b,c)
-type, centered at set
A
.
Fix
x\in A
. We shall prove, that
\lip f(x)=\infty
.

Note that
\lambda<H(X)\leq H(X,x)
.
Let
R=R(X,x,\lambda)>0
be chosen as is Proposition
5.2
.
Put
\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{\alpha}{a^{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\delta=\min\left\{\alpha,R\right\}
.
For any
0<r<\delta
there exists

n(r)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varepsilon_{n(r)}\leq r<\varepsilon_{n(r)-1}
.
Therefore,
\lim\limits_{r\to 0+}\varepsilon_{n(r)}=0
and so,
\lim\limits_{r\to 0+}n(r)=\infty
.

Fix
0<r<\delta
. To simplify, we will denote
n=n(r)
.
Let
Y=G_{n}=B(A,\varepsilon_{n})
,
E=S_{n}
,
\psi=d(\,\cdot\,,S_{n})
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}
.
Since
Y
is an open subspace of
X
, by Remark
5.1

we have
\lambda\leq H(X,x)=H(Y,x)
.
Set
R_{Y}=\varepsilon
. Then
B(x,R_{Y})\subseteq Y
. Since
R_{Y}\in(0;R]
, the number
R_{Y}=R(Y,\lambda,x)
satisfies the thesis of Proposition
5.2
in the space
Y
.
Now, we apply Lemma
5.3
for
Y
,
E
,
\varepsilon
and
\psi
as defined above.
So, there is
u\in\overline{B}(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq G_{n}
satisfying

\left|\psi(u)-\psi(x)\right|\geq\frac{\lambda\varepsilon}{8}=\frac{\lambda\varepsilon_{n}}{8}.

(9)

We want to show, that
\psi(v)=\varphi_{n}(v)
for any
v\in\overline{B}(x,\varepsilon)
.
If
t\in\complement{G_{n}}
, then
\left|t-x\right|_{X}\geq\frac{c}{a^{n}}
. But
\left|x-v\right|_{X}\leq\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{a^{n}}
and
c-\alpha\geq 1
. Hence

\left|t-v\right|_{X}\geq\left|t-x\right|_{X}-\left|x-v\right|_{X}\geq\frac{c}{a^{n}}-\frac{\alpha}{a^{n}}=\frac{c-\alpha}{a^{n}}\geq\frac{1}{a^{n}}.

On the other hand,
\left|v-s\right|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{a^{n}}
for
s\in S_{n}
,
since
v\in G_{n}
and
S_{n}
is a
\frac{1}{a^{n}}
-dense set in
G_{n}
.
Taking the above estimations into account, we get

\displaystyle\psi(v)=d(v,S_{n})

\displaystyle=\inf_{s\in S_{n}}\left|s-v\right|_{X}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\inf_{s\in S_{n}}\left|v-s\right|_{X},\inf_{t\in\complement{G_{n}}}\left|v-t\right|_{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{t\in T_{n}}\left|v-t\right|_{X}=d(v,T_{n})=\varphi_{n}(v).

In particular, for
v=x
we have
\psi(x)=\varphi_{n}(x)

and for
v=u
,
\psi(u)=\varphi_{n}(u)
.
Now (
9
) implies, that

\left|\varphi_{n}(u)-\varphi_{n}(x)\right|\geq\frac{\lambda\varepsilon_{n}}{8}.

(10)

Using Proposition
3.4
(iii)
we get the inequality

\left|s_{n}(x)-s_{n}(u)\right|\leq\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}\left|x-u\right|_{X}\leq\frac{b^{n}}{b-1}\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{b-1}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}.

(11)

By Proposition
3.4
(ii)
we get

\displaystyle\left|r_{n+1}(x)-r_{n+1}(u)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|r_{n+1}(x)\right|+\left|r_{n+1}(u)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{b^{n+1}}{(a-b)a^{n}}=\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}.

(12)

Using (
10
), (
12
) and (
11
) we can now estimate

\displaystyle\left|f(x){-}f(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\big(s_{n}(x)+b^{n}\varphi_{n}(x)+r_{n+1}(x)\big)-\big(s_{n}(u)+b^{n}\varphi_{n}(u)+r_{n+1}(u)\big)\right|

\displaystyle\geq b^{n}\left|\varphi_{n}(x)-\varphi_{n}(u)\right|-\left|s_{n}(x)-s_{n}(u)\right|-\left|r_{n+1}(x)-r_{n+1}(u)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda}{8}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}-\frac{1}{b-1}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}-\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{8}-\frac{1}{b-1}-\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}\right)b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\gamma b^{n}\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{\gamma}{a}b^{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}>\frac{\gamma}{a}b^{n}r,

where
\gamma=\frac{\lambda}{8}-\frac{1}{b-1}-\frac{2b}{\alpha(a-b)}
.
Hence,

\Lip^{r}f(x)\geq\frac{\left|f(x)-f(u)\right|}{r}\geq\frac{\gamma}{a}b^{n},\text{ for any }r<\delta.

Since
\lim\limits_{r\to 0+}n(r)=\infty
,
b>1
and
\gamma>0
, we have

\lip f(x)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\Lip^{r}f(x)\geq\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\gamma}{a}b^{n(r)}=\infty.

Thus, we have shown that
\lip f(x)=\infty
for arbitrarily chosen
x\in A
.
This means, that
A\subseteq\ell^{\infty}(f)
. But the same reasoning as in
theorem
4.1
can be applied here to show that

\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq A
.
To summarize, we have proven, that

A\subseteq\ell^{\infty}(f)\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)\subseteq A,

hence
A=\ell^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X
be a hermetic metric space and
A\subseteq X
.
Then, following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a closed set which does not contain isolated points of
X
,

(ii)

there exists continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\ell^{\infty}(f)=\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)=L^{\infty}(f)=A.

The question of whether the assumption of hermeticity of
X
in the above theorem is essential remains open.

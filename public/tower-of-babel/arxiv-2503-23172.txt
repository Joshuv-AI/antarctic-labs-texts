Slow-roll Natural & Hilltop Inflation in Rastall Gravity
Isaac M. Vitohekpon, Biswajit Deb, Ines G. Salako, V. A. Monwanou, Atri Deshamukhya

Abstract
This study provides a concise analysis of inflation under Rastall gravity by examining three types of potential as the power law, natural, and hilltop potentials. Choosing a minimal interaction between matter and gravity, we derived the modified slow-roll parameters, the scalar spectral index ( n s ) (n_{s}) , the tensor spectral index ( n T ) (n_{T}) , and the tensor-to-scalar ratio ( r ) (r) . For a general power-law potential as well as for Natural & Hilltop inflation, we calculated these quantities and subsequently plotted their trajectories in the ( n s , r ) (n_{s},r) plane. For the power-law potential, only the cases n = 2 / 3 n=2/3 and n = 1 n=1 satisfy the observational constraint of the Planck 2018 data. The natural potential analysis shows that the mass scale is crucial, with better compatibility achieved at f = 5 ​ M p f=5\,M_{p} compared to f = 10 ​ M p f=10\,M_{p} . Lastly, the Hilltop potential results indicate that among the cases studied ( m = 3 / 2 , 2 , 3 , m=3/2,2,3, and 4 4 ), only m = 3 / 2 m=3/2 exhibits marginal consistency with observational bounds, while the other cases fail to produce acceptable n s − r n_{s}-r trajectories.

III
Einstein Rastall Formalism

As noted in the Introduction, Rastall proposed an alternative approach to non conservative gravity by suggesting that the gravitational equations can be derived from a modification of the conservation laws
[
67
,
68
]
. Initially, Rastall’s fundamental equations were expressed as:

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\lambda_{Ras}}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\Theta_{\mu\nu},

(35)

\displaystyle{\Theta^{\mu\nu}}_{;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{Ras}}{2\kappa}R^{;\nu},

(36)

where
\lambda_{Ras}
is the Rastall parameter. Moreover,
\kappa
represents the gravitational coupling constant in Rastall’s theory, which is distinct from Einstein’s gravitational coupling constant
[
67
,
69
,
70
]
. In fact, one has
\kappa=\frac{\kappa_{E}}{\alpha}
, where
\kappa_{E}=8\pi G
is the Einstein coupling constant, and
\alpha
is an unknown constant to be determined by other physical considerations and observations
[
69
,
70
]
. It is worth noting that when
\lambda_{Ras}=1
(corresponding also to
\alpha=1
), the standard general relativity is recovered with the usual energy-momentum conservation. These equations can be equivalently rewritten as

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\left\{\Theta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\Theta\right\},

(37)

\displaystyle{\Theta^{\mu\nu}}_{;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\Theta^{;\nu},

(38)

with

\gamma=\frac{3\lambda_{Ras}-2}{2\lambda_{Ras}-1}.

(39)

Here again,
\gamma=1
(i.e.,
\lambda_{Ras}=1
) yields General Relativity. Alternatively, one may cast these relations in the following form:

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\left\{\Theta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{2-\gamma}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\Theta\right\},

(40)

\displaystyle{\Theta^{\mu\nu}}_{;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\Theta^{;\nu},

(41)

where
\lambda_{Ras}
still appears as Rastall’s parameter and setting
\lambda_{Ras}=1
recovers GR. It is also feasible to derive the above equations from a Lagrangian formulation
[
71
]
. For a radiative fluid, where
\Theta=0
and consequently
R=0
, the cosmological evolution during the radiative era mirrors that of the standard scenario. Similarly, a single fluid inflation model driven by a cosmological constant behaves as in GR. Hence, Rastall cosmologies represent an intriguing deviation from the standard cosmological model starting from the matter-dominated phase
[
72
,
73
,
74
,
75
]
.

The functions
\nu
and
\lambda
satisfy the Rastall field equations,

G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}^{eff},

(42)

with

T_{\mu\nu}^{eff}=\kappa\left\{\Theta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\Theta\right\}.

(43)

In this context, we adopt units such that
G=\alpha
, which implies
\kappa=8\pi

[
69
,
70
]
.

V.2
Natural & Hilltop Inflation

V.2.1
Natural inflation

Let us now consider alternative potentials. In particular, we focus on Natural inflation
[
76
]
, a model in which the inflaton is identified as a pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone boson generated through both spontaneous and explicit symmetry breaking. In this scenario, the dynamics of a single field are controlled by a potential of the form

V(\vartheta)=\Lambda^{4}\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\vartheta}{f}\right)\right],

(81)

where the mass scales
f
and
\Lambda
are introduced. It has been shown that such a potential is capable of driving inflation if
\Lambda\sim M_{\textrm{GUT}}\sim 10^{16}\,\textrm{GeV}
and
f\sim M_{\textrm{Pl}}=\kappa^{-1/2}
, with
M_{\textrm{Pl}}
being the reduced Planck mass. Using a procedure analogous to that employed earlier, the slow-roll parameters can be expressed in terms of the number of e-folds
N
as follows:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2f^{2}}\,\frac{\sin^{2}(\vartheta/f)}{\Bigl[1+\cos(\vartheta/f)\Bigr]^{2}}\,,

(82)

\displaystyle\eta_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{f^{2}}\,\frac{\cos(\vartheta/f)}{1+\cos(\vartheta/f)}\,.

(83)

Here, inflation ends when the modified slow-roll parameter
\epsilon_{\textrm{V}}\approx 1
.
Thus, according to the definition in (
61
) and (
81
), the number of e–folds
N
and is given by

N=\frac{f^{2}}{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}\,\ln\!\left(1+\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2f^{2}\,\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)}\right)\,.

(84)

the slow-roll parameters can be expressed in terms of the number of e-folds
N
as follows

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2f^{2}}\,\frac{1}{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-1}\,,

(85)

\displaystyle\eta_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{2f^{2}}\,\frac{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-2}{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-1}\,.

(86)

Consequently, the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio (in the slow-roll approximation, as given by (
23
)) become

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{S}}-1

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{f^{2}}\,\frac{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}+1}{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-1}\,,

(87a)

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{T}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{f^{2}}\,\frac{1}{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-1}\,,

(87b)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\approx\frac{8M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{f^{2}}\,\frac{1}{e^{NM_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}/f^{2}}-1}\,.

(87c)

Moreover, one can recast
r
as a function of
n_{s}
, yielding the representation in the
(r,n_{s})
plane:

r(n_{s})\approx 4\Bigl(1-n_{s}-\frac{M_{\textrm{Pl}}^{2}}{f^{2}}\Bigr).

(88)

Next, we explore whether Rastall gravity induces any modifications in this model. By following a similar approach, the corrected slow-roll parameters are found to be

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{2\kappa f^{2}}\,\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)\,,

(89)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{2\kappa f^{2}}\Bigl[1-\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)\Bigr]\,.

(90)

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{S}}-1

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{6(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{2\kappa f^{2}}\,\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\Bigl[1-\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)\Bigr]\,,

(91a)

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{T}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{\kappa f^{2}}\,\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)\,,

(91b)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\approx\frac{8(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{\kappa f^{2}}\,\tan^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)\,.

(91c)

Here, inflation ends when the modified slow-roll parameter
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\textrm{V}}\approx 1
, implying that

\vartheta_{\textrm{f}}=2f\,\arctan\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{2\kappa f^{2}}{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}}\Bigr)\,.

(92)

Thus, according to the definition in (
61
) and (
81
), the number of e–folds
\tilde{N}
and is given by

\tilde{N}=\frac{2\kappa f^{2}}{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}\,\ln\left[\frac{\sin\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta_{f}}{2f}\Bigr)}{\sin\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{2f}\Bigr)}\right]\,.

(93)

the slow-roll parameters can be expressed in terms of the number of e-folds
N
as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}_{V}(\tilde{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{2\kappa f^{2}}\,\frac{\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}{1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{V}(\tilde{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{2\kappa f^{2}}\,\left[1-\frac{\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}{1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}\right]\,.

(95)

Accordingly, the spectral indices now become

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{S}}-1

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\,\frac{2(3\lambda_{Ras}-2)\,\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}+1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}{1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}\,,

(96a)

\displaystyle n_{\textrm{T}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{\kappa f^{2}}\,\frac{\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}{1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}\,,

(96b)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\approx\frac{8(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{\kappa f^{2}}\,\frac{\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}{1-\Delta\,e^{-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)}{\kappa f^{2}}\tilde{N}}}\,.

(96c)

where

\Delta=\frac{2\kappa f^{2}}{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}+2\kappa f^{2}}\,.

(97)

V.2.2
Hilltop Potentials

Hilltop potentials were originally introduced by Lotfi Boubekeur and David H. Lyth in 2005
[
77
]
. In this class of models, the potential resembles a hill, with the inflaton rolling down from its peak toward the minimum, hence the term “hilltop potential”. The general form of such a potential is given by

V(\vartheta)=\Lambda^{4}\left[1-\Bigl(\frac{\vartheta}{\mu}\Bigr)^{m}+\dots\right],

(98)

where
\Lambda
represents the inflationary scale,
\mu
is the vacuum expectation value of the inflaton, and
m
is a positive integer. Hilltop potentials generally match the current observational bounds at super Planckian scales. However, in some modified gravity frameworks, this scale can be lower down to the Planck scale
[
78
]
.

Using the definitions of
\epsilon_{\textrm{V}}
and
\eta_{\textrm{V}}
provided in equations (
15
) and (
21
), we find the slow-roll parameters to be

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}}{2\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2(m-1)}}{\mu^{2m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]^{2}}\,,

(99)

\displaystyle\eta_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m(m-1)}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{\,m-2}}{\mu^{m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]}\,.

Furthermore, the spectral indices for this model are derived from (
23
)

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{3m^{2}}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2(m-1)}}{\mu^{2m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]^{2}}-\frac{2m(m-1)}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{\,m-2}}{\mu^{m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]}\,,

(100)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{8m^{2}}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2(m-1)}}{\mu^{2m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle n_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m^{2}}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2(m-1)}}{\mu^{2m}\left[1-(\vartheta/\mu)^{m}\right]^{2}}\,.

Next, we evaluate how Rastall Gravity modifies the slow-roll parameters and the spectral indices. From equations (
57
), (
59
), and (
63

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}}{2\kappa}\,\frac{m^{2}\,\vartheta^{2m-2}}{\mu^{2m}},

(101)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{\textrm{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)m(m-1)}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{m-2}}{\mu^{m}},

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{3\,(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}\,m^{2}}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2m-2}}{\mu^{2m}}-\frac{2\,(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)\,m(m-1)}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{m-2}}{\mu^{m}},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{8\,(2\lambda_{Ras}-1)^{2}\,m^{2}}{\kappa}\,\frac{\vartheta^{2m-2}}{\mu^{2m}}.

The end of inflation is determined by make using

\tilde{\epsilon}_{V}(\vartheta_{f})=1.

(102)

VI
Conclusion

In summary, our study comprehensively examined the interplay between modified gravity in the Rastall framework and slow-roll inflation through several potential models, including the power law, natural, and Hilltop potentials. For the power law potential, only the cases with
n=\frac{2}{3}
and
n=1
satisfy the Planck constraint
r<0.1
and fall within the
2\sigma
region, with precise ranges of
\lambda_{ras}
established for each. The analysis of the natural potential further demonstrated that the viability of the model is strongly dependent on the mass scale: while
f=10\,M_{p}
only marginally meets the Planck 2018 criteria,
f=5\,M_{p}
robustly satisfies both the Planck 2018, and more stringent Planck + BK15 constraints; lower mass scales are found to be inadequate due to insufficient e-folds.

Hilltop potential analysis performed at two different energy scales (
\mu=10\,M_{p}
and
\mu=5\,M_{p}
), revealed that the parameter
m
critically influences the inflationary predictions. At
\mu=10\,M_{p}
, only the case
m=\frac{3}{2}
shows a narrow window of viability, while the cases
m=2
,
m=3
, and
m=4
fail to meet observational bounds. A similar trend is observed at
\mu=5\,M_{p}
, reinforcing the sensitivity of the model’s predictions to both the choice of
m
and the energy scale.

Overall, these findings highlight the delicate balance between theoretical parameters and observational constraints in inflationary cosmology. They also emphasize the potential of modified gravity theories to serve as promising alternatives in explaining the early universe dynamics. Future research should aim to further refine these parameter spaces and investigate additional observational signatures to strengthen the viability of these models.

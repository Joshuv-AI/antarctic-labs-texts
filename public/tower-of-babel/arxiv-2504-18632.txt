Backward stochastic differential equations with nonlinear Young drivers I
Jian Song, Huilin Zhang, Kuan Zhang

Abstract
. This paper (alongside its companion, Part II [ 60 ] ) investigates backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) involving a nonlinear Young integral of the form ∫ t T g ⁡ ( Y r ) ​ η ​ ( 𝑑 r , X r ) \int_{t}^{T}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r}) , where the driver η ⁡ ( t , x ) \eta(t,x) is a space-time Hölder continuous function and X X is a diffusion process. Solutions to such equations provide a probabilistic interpretation of the solutions to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by space-time noise. Assuming the driver η ⁡ ( t , x ) \eta(t,x) is bounded, we establish the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to these BSDEs via a modified Picard iteration method. We then derive a comparison principle by analyzing the associated linear BSDEs and establish regularity properties of the solutions. As an application, we obtain Feynman-Kac formulae for a class of linear stochastic heat equations subject to Neumann boundary conditions.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space
supporting a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
W
. Given progressively measurable functions
f,g
and
\eta
and a continuous adapted stochastic process
X
with finite
p
-variation for some
p>2
(e.g., a diffusion process), we consider the following backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) on
[0,T]
with the solution pair
(Y_{t},Z_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
:

(1.1)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{T}g_{i}(Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{r}dW_{r},\ t\in[0,T],

where
\int g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})
is a
nonlinear Young integral
introduced by Catellier-Gubinelli
[
9
]
and Hu-Lê
[
40
]
(see Section
2.1
for details). A typical example of
\eta(t,x)
is a space-time Hölder continuous function, such as a realization of a fractional Brownian sheet. In this work, we establish the well-posedness of Equation (
1.1
) when
\text{sup}_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta(t,x)|<\infty
, which is referred to as the bounded case (see also Remark
1.1
); the unbounded case will be addressed in Part II of this study
[
60
]
.

1.1.
Motivations and literature review

The existence and uniqueness of general BSDEs were established by Pardoux-Peng
[
53
]
. Nonlinear Feynman-Kac formulae via BSDEs were then introduced in
[
58
,
54
]
, providing probabilistic interpretations for solutions to semilinear parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs). Since then, BSDE theory has received significant attention and found numerous applications in fields such as mathematical finance, stochastic optimal control, PDEs, and data science. We refer to
[
18
,
66
]
and the references therein for BSDE theory and applications. Our motivation for studying BSDE (
1.1
) stems from the following two problems.

(a) The first motivation arises from Pontryagin’s stochastic maximum principle (SMP) for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with singular drifts. Consider the following control system:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX^{\alpha}_{t}=\mu(t,X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})dt+\upsilon(t,X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})dW_{t},\\
&X^{\alpha}_{0}=x_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\{\alpha_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the control process. Suppose the cost function and value function are given by

J(\alpha):=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}F(t,X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})dt+h(X^{\alpha}_{T})\right],\ \ \mathcal{V}(0,x_{0}):=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}J(\alpha).

The SMP via the dual argument requires solving a BSDE (the adjoint equation) driven by
\partial_{x}H
,
where
H(t,x,a,y,z):=\mu(t,x,a)y+\upsilon(t,x,a)z+F(t,x,a)

is the Hamiltonian. For more details on the SMP and the adjoint equation, see e.g., Yong-Zhou
[
64
]
.

A typical example of SDEs with singular drifts arises from the directed polymer model and the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation. More precisely, Equation (
1.2
) with
\upsilon\equiv 1
and
\mu(t,x)=\partial_{x}u(t,x)
, where
u(t,x)
is the solution to the KPZ equation, was proposed by Alberts-Khanin-Quastel
[
2
]
to describe the continuum directed polymer measure, and later on its well-posedness was established by Delarue-Diel
[
12
]
using rough path theory. Other relevant models, e.g., mixed fast-slow systems, stochastic filtering, and pathwise stochastic control problems, can also be viewed as SDEs with singular drifts. We refer to
[
57
,
31
,
22
,
23
,
38
]
for further discussions.

If the drift in (
1.2
) takes a more general form

\mu(t,x,a)dt=g(x,a)\zeta(dt,x)
, the corresponding adjoint BSDE has a singular driver
\eta(t,x)=\partial_{x}\zeta(t,x)
, and the integral is the
so-called
nonlinear Young integral
(see, e.g., Galeati
[
27
]
or Section
2.1
for the definition). Thus, this adjoint BSDE is indeed a special case of our Equation (
1.1
).

(b) The second motivation is to find probabilistic interpretations for solutions of SPDEs driven by space-time noise. Consider the following SPDE:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\partial_{t}u(t,x)&=\mathcal{L}u(t,x)+g\left(u,\nabla u\right)V(t,x),\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(T,x)&=h(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
V
is Gaussian noise and
\mathcal{L}
is an elliptic differential operator

\mathcal{L}u(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\left\{\nabla^{2}u(t,x)\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\text{T}}\right\}+b^{\text{T}}(x)\nabla u(t,x).

(i)
For the case
g(y,z)=y
and
(b,\sigma)=(0,I_{d})
, the Feynman-Kac formula for SPDE (
1.3
) has been studied in various situations. Let
\{B(t,x),t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be a fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst parameters
(H_{0},H_{1},\dots,H_{d})\in(0,1)^{1+d}
. When
V(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{d+1}B}{\partial{t}\partial{x_{1}}\dots\partial{x_{d}}}(t,x)
and
H_{i}>1/2
for
i=0,1,\dots,d
, Hu et al.
[
43
]
obtained a weak solution by the Feynman-Kac-type representation; a similar result is obtained in
[
42
]
when
V(t,x)=\partial_{t}B(t,x)
and
H_{0}\in(1/4,1/2)
. More recently, Hu et al.
[
41
]
obtained a mild solution to (
1.3
) by studying a linear BSDE with a driver which is singular in time and fast (polynomially) vanishing in space.

(ii)
When
g
is independent of
\nabla u
, and
V(t,x)=\partial_{t}B(t,x)
with
B
being a fractional Brownian sheet, SPDE (
1.3
) has been investigated in a sequence of works
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
.
When

V(t,x)=\partial_{t}\nabla B(t,x)
, Hinz
[
32
]
explored the solvability of the stochastic Burger equations, and Hinz et al.
[
33
]
studied transport-type SPDEs.
Nevertheless, the corresponding forward-backward SDE (FBSDE) has not been studied, and thus the relation between solutions of FBSDEs and solutions of SPDEs with singular drivers, known as the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula, is left open. In applications, nonlinear Feynman-Kac formulae may serve as a valuable tool for numerical analysis of SPDEs/PDEs (e.g., Monte Carlo method, see
[
66
, Chapter 5]
and references therein). Despite the Feynman-Kac formula, FBSDE theory can also be applied to characterize the scaling limits of solutions to SPDEs (see Dunlap-Gu
[
17
]
for the stochastic heat equation case).

(iii)
For the nonlinear case (
1.3
), typical models are the KPZ equation, the nonlinear version of parabolic Anderson model, Burgers-type equations, etc, where
V
is space-time noise. Such equations have been studied, for instance, in Hairer
[
30
]
and Gubinelli-Imkeller-Perkowski
[
28
]
. To study SPDEs in a general form of (
1.3
) via BSDEs, we introduce a novel form (
1.1
) of BSDE driven by space-time noise
\eta
. The present work focuses on the case that the driver
\eta
is less singular than space-time white noise. When
V
is space-time white noise, the well-posedness of (F)BSDEs associated with (
1.3
) appears to be unresolved, while the Feynman-Kac formula was studied when
g(u,Du)=u
in Bertini-Cancrini
[
4
]
.

BSDEs with stochastic (singular) drivers in the form of (
1.1
) have been studied in various frameworks. When
\eta(t,x)=B(t)
is a (fractional) Brownian motion or a general martingale independent of
W
, such equations are commonly referred to as backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs). BDSDEs were introduced by Pardoux-Peng
[
55
]
to investigate the probabilistic representation of solutions to semilinear SPDEs. Jing
[
45
]
considered the case where
\eta(t,x)=B(t)
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H>1/2
. When
\eta(t,x)
is martingale with space parameter, Song et al.
[
59
]
studied the BDSDE driven by a backward nonlinear Itô integral (see, e.g., Kunita
[
47
]
). Note that the approach used in
[
59
]
heavily relies on the martingale structure of
\eta
, which is unavailable in our setting.
When
\eta(dt,x)=b(t,x)dt
for some
b(\cdot,\cdot)\in C([0,T];H^{-\gamma}_{q})
with
0<\gamma<1/2
, Issoglio-Russo
[
44
]
studied a linear BSDE driven by
\eta(dt,x)
and they proved a Feynman-Kac formula for the corresponding PDE.

Stochastic processes such as (fractional) Brownian motions, Lévy processes, etc., can be treated (so-called lifted) as rough paths (see e.g.
[
25
, Theorem 15.33]
). This idea naturally motivates the investigation into the study of PDEs and corresponding BSDEs driven by rough paths, thereby extending stochastic analysis to the rough analysis regime. Rough analysis provides a framework to investigate systems with singular drivers, benefiting the analysis from the continuity and generality sides.
Topics on PDEs driven by rough signals (rough PDEs), including the well-posedness, stability with respect to rough signals, and space-time regularity of solutions, have been studied widely;
see, for example,
[
7
,
29
,
8
,
24
,
15
,
5
,
49
,
6
]
. Recently, Hocquet-Neamţu
[
37
]
solved quasilinear rough evolution equations in the mild sense, including Dirichlet, Neumann, and periodic boundary value problems.
Although there are numerous works on rough PDEs, there has been limited literature on related BSDEs, even in the case that
\eta(t,x)=\eta(t)
is a rough path independent of space variable. Diehl-Friz
[
14
]
firstly addressed the case where
\eta(t)
has finite
p
-variation (
2<p<\infty
).
Using an approximation argument, they established the well-posedness of (
1.1
), proving the existence of a formal solution continuous in rough drivers. Liang-Tang
[
50
]
extended the result of
[
14
]
to the multi-dimensional case and provided a strong solution to such BSDEs in the linear case. Diehl-Zhang
[
16
]
considered the case when
\eta(t)
has finite
p
-variation in the range
1<p<2
and demonstrated the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) using a fixed-point argument. Recently, Becherer-Sun
[
3
]
extended the results in
[
16
]
to discontinuous Young drivers.

When the driver
\eta(t,x)
is a function of both time and space, the well-posedness of (
1.1
) is more challenging:

(i) The Young driver
\eta(\cdot,x)
does not possess the semi-martingale property, and hence the Itô calculus developed in Kunita
[
47
]
does not apply here. In Section
2
, we develop pathwise analysis for the nonlinear Young integral
\int g(Y_{t})\eta(dt,X_{t})
, inspired by the classical Young integral theory (see, e.g.
[
65
,
52
,
9
,
40
,
16
,
27
]
). We adopt the
p
-variation norm rather than the Hölder norm because it is more convenient to study the
p
-variation of
\int_{\cdot}^{T}Z_{r}dW_{r}
due to the irregularity of the process
Z
in the BSDE setting. Note that the
p
-variation analysis only yields local Lipschitz estimates, which makes it difficult to obtain an
a priori
estimate for nonlinear BSDEs (see Remark
3.3
).

(ii) The methodology used in Diehl-Friz
[
14
]
in the study of rough BSDEs involves the Doss-Sussmann transformation, which requires the smoothness of rough flows. In our setting, the map
x\mapsto\eta(t,x)
lacks smoothness (see Assumption
(H1)
), and hence we can not take this approach.

(iii) It is tempting to interpret our equation (
1.1
) as a Young BSDE in the setting of Diehl-Zhang
[
16
]
, by applying the transformation
\tilde{\eta}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\eta(dr,X_{r})
to get a new driver
\tilde{\eta}_{t}
which does not involve a space variable. Note that it is crucial to assume the driver is deterministic to obtain the
a priori
estimate in
[
16
]
: when the driver is deterministic with bounded
p
-variation, the solution to the Young BSDE can be dominated by the solution to a deterministic Young ODE with the same driver by comparison principle. In our situation, clearly the new driver
\tilde{\eta}_{t}
is random and its
p
-variation may be unbounded, and hence we can not adopt the strategy in
[
16
]
in a straightforward way (see Remark
3.2
for a detailed discussion).

As an application, we apply BSDEs with nonlinear Young drivers to study linear Young SPDEs with Neumann boundary conditions in the weak sense, and provide an explicit representation (i.e., Feynman-Kac formula) for the solution by our BSDE and a reflected Brownian motion.
This BSDE approach circumvents proving the exponential integrability for the
p
-variation of the Brownian motion reflected in a general bounded domain (see Remark
5.1
), providing a new method to validate the Feynman-Kac expression. Furthermore, the stability of BSDE solutions with respect to their drivers facilitates the convergence of approximated solutions for the SPDE, and consequently, we obtain the time regularity of solution to the SPDE.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we revisit some essential concepts related to the nonlinear Young integral and prove an extended Itô’s formula. Our main result is the well-posedness of BSDE (
1.1
), which is addressed in Section
3.1
. Additionally, in Section
3.2
we establish a comparison theorem by investigating linear BSDEs. The regularity of solutions is studied in Section
4
, which plays a key role in solving nonlinear Young BSDEs with
unbounded
driver
\eta
in the second part of this project
[
60
]
. Finally, an application to linear stochastic heat equations with Neumann boundary conditions is presented in Section
5
; some miscellaneous results are summarized in Appendix
A
and
B
.

Remark 1.1
.

In particular, if we assume
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\eta(0,x)|<\infty
, then
\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}<\infty
implies that
|\eta(t,x)|
is uniformly bounded for all

(see Lemma

\tilde{\eta}(0,x)\equiv 0
and
\tilde{\eta}(dt,x_{t})=\eta(dt,x_{t})

\eta(0,x)\equiv 0
for Equation (

.

Example 1.1
.

A typical example of
\eta
is a sample path of fractional Brownian sheet
\{B(t,x),(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
with Hurst parameters
H_{i}\in(0,1),i=0,1,\dots,n
, which is a centered Gaussian random field with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[B(t,x)B(s,y)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left(|t|^{2H_{0}}+|s|^{2H_{0}}-|t-s|^{2H_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(|x_{i}|^{2H_{i}}+|y_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2H_{i}}\right).

By Lemma
A.4
,

2.
Nonlinear Young integral and Itô’s formula

In this section, we provide some preliminaries on the nonlinear Young integral, which is an extension of the classical Young integral introduced by Young
[
65
]
.

2.1.
Nonlinear Young integral

Let
\eta:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous deterministic function with
\eta(0,x)\equiv 0
, and let
x:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
y:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be two continuous functions. Assume that
p_{1}\geq 1
and
p_{2}\geq 1
are two constants. In this section, inspired by
[
40
]
under a Hölder framework, we define the nonlinear Young integral

\int_{a}^{b}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r}),

where
x
has finite
p_{1}
-variation and
y
has finite
p_{2}
-variation respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1]
,
\lambda\in(0,1]
.
Assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
x\in C^{p_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
y\in C^{p_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
with
\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}>1
and
\tau+\frac{1}{p_{2}}>1
. For
0\leq a\leq b\leq T
, the following integral is well-defined,

\int_{a}^{b}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r}):=\mathcal{A}_{a,b}:=\mathcal{A}(b)-\mathcal{A}(a),

where
\mathcal{A}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique function associated with
A(s,t)=y_{s}\big(\eta(t,x_{s})-\eta(s,x_{s})\big)
by Lemma
B.1
. Moreover, if we assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\beta\geq 0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\right\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\tau}\bigg(\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\Big(\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}+\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\Big)\bigg),

and if we assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\right\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\leq\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|t-s|^{\tau}\bigg(\Big(1+\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}+\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\bigg),

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, where
\delta=\min\left\{\tau+\frac{1}{p_{2}}-1,\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}-1\right\}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition 2.4 in
[
40
]
. See
Appendix B
Some preliminary proofs on nonlinear Young integrals
in Appendix
B
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

For a continuous path
z
, we have
\|z\|_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\leq\|z\|_{q\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}
if
1\leq q\leq p
. Thus, the estimates (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are also valid for
\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\|_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}
with

.

Remark 2.2
.

Denoting
M_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\eta(dr,x_{r})
, where
\eta
and
x
satisfy the conditions in Proposition
2.1
, then
M\in C^{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-}\mathrm{var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
. As a consequence, for

with

, the classical Young integral

is well defined, and it coincides with the nonlinear integral
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})
. Indeed, let
\Gamma(s,t):=y_{s}\int_{s}^{t}\eta(dr,x_{r})-y_{s}\left(\eta(t,x_{s})-\eta(s,x_{s})\right)
. By (
B.6
) with

for

, we have
\left|\Gamma(s,t)\right|\lesssim_{\tau,\lambda,p_{1},p_{2}}w_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta},
where the control

is defined by (
B.2
), and the constant
\delta
is defined by (
B.3
). Then, by Lemma
B.2
, we have
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}dM_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})
.

The following result is a variant of the estimate (

) in Proposition

.

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\Big\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\|x\|^{\lambda+\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]}\right)\Big(\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}+\|y\|^{\varepsilon}_{\infty;[s,t]}\|y\|^{1-\varepsilon}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[s,t]}\Big)\Big\}.

Remark 2.3
.

Unlike (

2.2.

Given progressively measurable

\int_{0}^{T}|f^{i}_{r}|dr+\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{i}_{r}|^{2}dr<\infty\ a.s.\ \text{for }i=1,...,n,

(2.4)

(X,g^{i}_{j})\in C^{p_{1}\text{-var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\times C^{p_{2}\text{-var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
a.s. The following Itô’s formula is an extension of Theorem 3.4 of
[
40
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(Itô’s formula)
.

Let
\tau\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
,
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
p_{1}\geq 1
, and
p_{2}\geq 1
be parameters such that
\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}>1
and
\tau+\frac{1}{p_{2}}>1
. Suppose
Y
satisfies (
2.4
). Then, for any
\phi(t,y)\in C^{\gamma,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1],
having continuous spatial partial derivatives
\nabla_{y}\phi
and
\nabla^{2}_{yy}\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(t,Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\phi(0,Y_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\ \phi(dr,Y_{r})-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{y}\phi(r,Y_{r})^{\top}f_{r}dr-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y^{i}}\phi(r,Y_{r})g^{i}_{j}(r)\eta_{j}(dr,X_{r})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{y}\phi(r,Y_{r})^{\top}Z_{r}dW_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\text{tr}\left\{\nabla^{2}_{yy}\phi(r,Y_{r})Z_{r}Z^{T}_{r}\right\}dr,\ t\in[0,T].

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

We only prove the case when
n=d=M=1
; the general case can be proved similarly. Since
\gamma,\tau>\frac{1}{2}
, there exists a real number
p_{3}>2
such that
\gamma+\frac{1}{p_{3}}>1
and
\tau+\frac{1}{p_{3}}>1
. Furthermore, Since
p_{3}>2,
by Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
A.1
, we have
Y\in C^{p_{3}\text{-var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
a.s. In addition, since
\phi\in C^{\gamma,1}_{\text{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and
\gamma+\frac{1}{p_{3}}>1
, we have, for almost all paths of
Y
,
\int_{0}^{t}\phi(dr,Y_{r})
is well defined in the sense of Proposition
2.1
. Also, by our assumption,
\nabla_{y}\phi(\cdot,x)
is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous in
[0,T]
for every fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\left\{\tau_{K}\right\}_{K=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of stopping times such that
\tau_{K}\uparrow T
a.s., and the following terms are all less than
K
(here
X^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}:=X_{\cdot\land\tau_{K}}
;
g^{\tau_{K}}
and
Y^{\tau_{K}}
are defined in the same manner):

\displaystyle\|X^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{\infty;[0,T]},\ \|X^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{p_{1}\text{-var};[0,T]},\ \|g^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{\infty;[0,T]},\ \|g^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[0,T]},\ \|Y^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{\infty;[0,T]},\ \|Y^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{p_{3}\text{-var};[0,T]},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{K}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr,\ \Big\|\int_{0}^{\cdot\land\tau_{K}}Z_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|_{p_{3}\text{-var};[0,T]},\ \int_{0}^{\tau_{K}}|f_{r}|dr,\ \text{and}\ \Big\|\int_{0}^{\cdot\land\tau_{K}}g_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-var};[0,T]}.

Denote
B^{K}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};|x|\leq K\}
and
\|\eta\|^{(K)}_{\tau,\lambda}:=\|\eta\|^{(B^{K})}_{\tau,\lambda}
for simplicity; the notation
\|\phi\|^{(K)}_{\gamma,1}
is defined in the same manner. Hence, letting
\pi
be a partition on
[0,t]
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left|\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}})-\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})\right|^{p_{3}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i}\left|\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}})-\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})\right|^{p_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}+\left(\sum_{i}\left|\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})\right|^{p_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in[0,T],|y|\leq K}|\partial^{2}_{yy}\phi(r,y)|\left(\sum_{i}\left|Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}}-Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}}\right|^{p_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}+\|\phi\|^{(K)}_{\gamma,1}\left(\sum_{i}\left|t_{i+1}-t_{i}\right|^{\gamma\cdot p_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}.

Therefore, noting that
\gamma\cdot p_{3}>p_{3}-1>1,

\partial_{y}\phi(\cdot,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot})
has finite
p_{3}
-variation. Hence, since

\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{y}\phi(r,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})
is well-defined for

. Note that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\phi(t,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t})-\phi(0,Y_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left[\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}})-\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})\right]=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})+\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})+\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left[\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\right].

\displaystyle\left|\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\phi(t_{i},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\right|\lesssim_{\gamma,p_{3}}\|\phi\|^{(K)}_{\gamma,1}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})^{\gamma}\|Y^{\tau_{K}}_{\cdot}\|_{p_{3}\text{-var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}.

(2.6)

\lim_{|\pi|\downarrow 0}I_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}\phi(dr,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\ \ \ \ a.s.

(2.7)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_{y}\phi(r,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})dY^{\tau_{K}}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial^{2}_{yy}\phi(r,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})|Z_{r}|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau_{K}]}(r)dr\right]

\displaystyle+J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

\displaystyle:=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\Bigg[\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}})-\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})dY^{\tau_{K}}_{r}

converges to 0 in probability as

tends to 0. For

The proof of the convergence of

Step 1.

In this step, we shall show that
\lim\limits_{|\pi|\downarrow 0}J_{3}=0
a.s. Note that

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big(\partial_{y}\phi(r,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})-\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})\big)f_{r}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau_{K}]}(r)dr

\displaystyle+\bigg\{\sum_{[s,u]\in\tilde{\pi}}\Big|-\partial_{y}\phi(u,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{s})+\partial_{y}\phi(s,Y^{\tau_{K}}_{s})\Big|^{p_{3}}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}

Step 2.

\displaystyle=\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\Big[\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}})\left(Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i+1}}-Y^{\tau_{K}}_{t_{i}}\right)-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_{y}\phi(t_{i+1},Y^{\tau_{K}}_{r})dY^{\tau_{K}}_{r}\Big]

\displaystyle=:L_{1}+L_{2},

where

is a (random) number between

.
By a similar argument for

. As for

\displaystyle|L_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\frac{1}{2}|\partial^{2}_{yy}\phi(t_{i+1},\xi_{i})|\left(\alpha_{i}^{2}+\left|\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}\right|+\beta_{i}^{2}+\left|\beta_{i}\gamma_{i}\right|+|\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}|\right)

. Indeed, by Proposition

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|\beta_{i}\gamma_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i}\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{t_{i+1}}g_{r}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau_{K}]}(r)\eta(dr,X_{r})\right\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{t_{i+1}}Z_{r}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau_{K}]}(r)dW_{r}\right\|_{p_{3}\text{-var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}

\displaystyle\phi(t,Y_{t\land\tau_{K}})=\phi(0,Y_{0})

Corollary 2.1

.

Let

, be stochastic processes that satisfy, for

\begin{split}Y^{1}_{t}Y^{2}_{t}=&\ Y^{1}_{0}Y^{2}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\left[Y^{1}_{r}f^{2}_{r}+Y^{2}_{r}f^{1}_{r}\right]dr-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{t}\left[Y^{1}_{r}g^{2}_{j}(r)+Y^{2}_{r}g^{1}_{j}(r)\right]\eta_{j}(dr,X_{r})\\

3.
BSDEs with bounded drivers

Throughout this section, we assume that
\eta(\cdot,\cdot)\in C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
with
\eta(0,\cdot)\equiv 0
. We make the following assumption to guarantee the existence of the nonlinear Young integral
\int\eta(dr,x_{r})
(see Proposition
2.1
), where
x_{\cdot}\in C^{p\text{-var}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p>2
.

Assumption (H0)
.

Let
p,\tau,\lambda
be parameters satisfying

p>2,\ \tau\in(1/2,1],\ \lambda\in(0,1],\text{ and }\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p}>1.

In many situations, one can choose
p>2
to be arbitrarily close to
2
(e.g.,
x_{t}
is a realization of a semi-martingale), and thus
(H0)
can be replaced by the following weaker assumption:

Assumption (H0’)
.

Let
\tau,\lambda
be parameters satisfying

\tau\in(1/2,1],\ \lambda\in(0,1],\text{ and }\tau+\frac{\lambda}{2}>1.

Let
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a progressively measurable process,
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}
be a Borel measurable function,
X
be a continuous adapted process, and the terminal value
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})^{\top}
be an
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random vector.

Assumption (H1)
.

Let
\tau,\lambda,p
be the parameters satisfying Assumption
(H0)
.

(1)

\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
and
m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])<\infty
for some
k>1
, where
m_{p,k}
is given by (
1.8
).

(2)

For
(y_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times d},\ i=1,2
,

|f(\cdot,r,y_{1},z_{1})-f(\cdot,r,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq C_{1}\left(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|\right)\text{ and }|f(\cdot,r,0,0)|\leq C_{1},

and
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N\times M})
with

|g(0)|\vee\|\nabla g\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}\vee\|\nabla^{2}g\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}\leq C_{1},\ \text{for some }C_{1}>0.

(3)

\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued random vector with
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty
.

Remark 3.1
.

Clearly
C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\mathrm{loc}}\subsetneq C^{\tau,\lambda}
. However, if we assume further
\|X_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty
, the condition
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is equivalent to
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}
when studying the nonlinear Young integral involving
\eta(dr,X_{r})
. Therefore, the requirement
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
in Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
below can be replaced by
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
if
\|X_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty
.

3.1.
The well-posedness of equation

Consider the following BSDE on
[u,v]\subset[0,T]
:

(3.1)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{v}g_{i}(Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{v}Z_{r}dW_{r},~t\in[u,v].

In the following, we refer to
(\xi,f,g,\eta,X)
as parameters of this equation. In addition, we denote by
\Theta:=(\tau,\lambda,p,k)
the parameters in Assumptions
(H0)
and
(H1)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose Assumption
(H1)
holds. Assume further that
\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{v}
. Then for any
(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(u,v)
(recall
k>1
in Assumption
(H1)
and
\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(u,v)
in (
1.10
)), there exists a unique pair of processes
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\in\mathfrak{B}([u,v])
satisfying

\sup_{t\in[u,v]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|\tilde{Y}_{t}\right|^{k}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{u}^{v}|\tilde{Z}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{k}{2}}\Big]<\infty,

and

(3.2)

\tilde{Y}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{v}g_{i}(Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{v}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r},~\text{for all }t\in[u,v].

We write
\Phi^{\xi}_{[u,v]}(Y,Z):=(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
to denote the mapping defined by (
3.2
).

Proof.

Assume
M=1
for simplicity. By conditions
\|(Y,Z)\|_{p,k;[u,v]}<\infty
and
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\xi\right|^{k}+\Big|\int_{u}^{v}|f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})|dr\Big|^{k}+\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[u,v]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}\|\xi\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C^{k}_{1}|v-u|^{k}+C^{k}_{1}(|v-u|^{k}+|v-u|^{\frac{k}{2}})\|(Y,Z)\|^{k}_{p,k;[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C^{k}_{1}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{k\tau}(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])^{k})\|(Y,Z)\|^{k}_{p,k;[u,v]},

where we apply the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{u}^{v}|f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})|dr\Big|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}_{1}\Big|\int_{u}^{v}1+|Y_{r}|+|Z_{r}|dr\Big|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}_{1}|v-u|^{k}\Big(1+\esssup_{t\in[u,v],\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|^{k}\Big)+C^{k}_{1}|v-u|^{\frac{k}{2}}\Big|\int_{u}^{v}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{k}{2}},

and by (
2.2
)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|_{p\text{-var};[u,v]}^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}C^{k}_{1}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{k\tau}\Big((1+\|X\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[u,v]})\esssup_{t\in[u,v],\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|^{k}+\|Y\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[u,v]}\Big).

Hence, we have

(3.3)

\sup_{t\in[u,v]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right)^{k}\right]<\infty.

Then by the martingale representation theorem for
L^{1}
-integrable random variable (see
[
10
, Theorem 3]
), there exists a unique progressively measurable
process
\tilde{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\xi+\int_{u}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{u}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\xi+\int_{u}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{u}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]\text{ for all }t\in[0,v].

Applying the lower bound of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (BDG inequality for short) and Doob’s maximal inequality to the submartingale
M_{t}:=|\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}|
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{0}^{v}|\tilde{Z}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{k}{2}}\bigg]\lesssim_{k}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|_{\infty;[0,v]}^{k}\bigg]\lesssim_{k}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{0}^{v}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big|^{k}\bigg]<\infty.

Thus, we have for

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]

\displaystyle=\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{v}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}.

Let

(3.4)

\tilde{Y}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right],~t\in[u,v].

Then
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
satisfies (
3.2
), and
\sup_{t\in[u,v]}\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{Y}_{t}|^{k}]<\infty
by (
3.3
). On the other hand, Eq. (
3.2
) yields that
\tilde{Y}_{t}
must be given by (
3.4
), and the uniqueness follows.
∎

The following proposition shows that the solution map

), for

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\tilde{Y}_{t}|

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in[u,v];\omega\in\Omega}|\tilde{Y}_{t}|

(3.6)

(3.7)

Here, the first inequality is due to the BDG inequality, and the third inequality is due to (

) and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}

\displaystyle\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}C_{1}^{k}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{k\tau}\left(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])^{k}\right)\left(1+\|\xi\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(Y;[u,v])^{k}\right),

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Z}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

), (

), and (

In order to obtain a uniform bound for the solution, we establish the following
a priori
estimate.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
(H1)
holds. Let
\delta
be the same as in Proposition
3.1
. For any fixed
S\in[0,T]
, if
(Y,Z)
is a solution of (
1.1
) on
[S,T]
, then we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}\leq C\left(1+\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\exp\left\{C\left\lfloor\frac{T-S}{\delta}\right\rfloor\right\},\quad\text{ for any }n\geq 0,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in[S,T];\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|\leq C\left(1+\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\exp\left\{C\left\lfloor\frac{T-S}{\delta}\right\rfloor\right\},

where
T_{n}:=(T-n\delta)\vee S
,
n\geq 0
;
\lfloor a\rfloor
is the biggest integer not greater than
a
;
C
is a positive constant that only depends on
\Theta
.

Proof.

We prove these estimates by induction. Firstly, note that for any nonnegative sequences
\{r_{n},n\geq 0\}
and
\{R_{n},n\geq 1\}
that satisfy the following equalities with a positive constant
A,

\left\{\begin{aligned} r_{n}&=R_{n}+r_{n-1},\\
R_{n}&=A(1+r_{n-1}),\end{aligned}\right.

we have

(3.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{n}&\leq A(1+r_{0})e^{A(n-1)},\\
r_{n}&\leq(1+r_{0})e^{An}.\end{aligned}\right.

Denote
T_{n}:=T-n\delta
for
0\leq n\leq\lfloor\frac{T-S}{\delta}\rfloor
. To prove the desired inequalities (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), it suffices to show, for
0\leq n\leq\lfloor\frac{T-S}{\delta}\rfloor
,

(3.16)

\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}\leq R_{n+1},~\esssup_{t\in[T_{n+1},T_{n}];\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|\leq r_{n+1},

where
r_{n},R_{n}
satisfies (
3.15
) with
r_{0}=\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
A=3(C_{\Theta}\vee 1)
with
C_{\Theta}
appearing in (
3.11
). When
n=0
, on
[u,v]=[T_{1},T]
, by (
3.12
) we have

\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{1},T_{0}]}\leq A(1+\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})=A(1+r_{0})=R_{1}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{0}];\omega\in\Omega}\left|Y_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{0}];\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[|Y_{T}-Y_{t}|\right]+\|Y_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{1},T_{0}]}+\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq R_{1}+r_{0}=r_{1}.

Now we assume that (
3.16
) holds on
[T_{j},T_{j-1}]
for
1\leq j\leq n
. By (
3.12
) in Proposition
3.1
, we have

\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}\leq A(1+\|Y_{T_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\leq A\Big(1+\esssup_{t\in[T_{n},T_{n-1}];\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|\Big)\leq A(1+r_{n})=R_{n+1}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}\left|Y_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{t\in[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[|Y_{T_{n}}-Y_{t}|\right]+\|Y_{T_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left|(Y,Z)\right|_{p,k;[T_{n+1},T_{n}]}+\|Y_{T_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq R_{n+1}+r_{n}=r_{n+1}.

Thus, (
3.16
) is proved by induction. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

The approach used in the proof of Lemma
3.2
for establishing the
a priori
estimate for
(Y,Z)
differs from
[
16
, p. 10]
. On one hand, assuming the driver is deterministic in
[
16
]
, the comparison theorem allows their solution
|Y_{t}|
to be bounded by solutions of
deterministic
Young ODEs with the terminal value
\xi
replaced by its essential extremum, which does not hold in our situation in general since the integral
\int\dots\eta(dr,X_{r})
is stochastic due to the presence of
X
. On the other hand, the estimate of the nonlinear Young integral (
3.5
) in Proposition
3.1
is more accurate than the estimate for Young integral in
[
16
, Theorem 5]
with
\int_{t}^{T}g(Y_{r})d\tilde{\eta}^{i}_{r}=\int_{t}^{T}g(Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})
, where
\tilde{\eta}^{i}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})
. More precisely,
the latter approach would lead to an extra term of
\|g(Y)\|_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var}}\|X\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var}}
on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) in the estimation of
Y
, and thus we cannot obtain an inequality for any
k
-moment of
\|Y\|_{p\text{-var}}
due to the randomness of
\|X\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var}}
.

Consider the following two BSDEs on
[u,v]\subseteq[0,T]
with a common terminal value,

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{(1)}_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y^{(1)}_{r},Z^{(1)}_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{v}g_{i}(Y^{(1)}_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{v}\tilde{Z}^{(1)}_{r}dW_{r},\ t\in[u,v],

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{(2)}_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi+\int_{t}^{v}f(r,Y^{(2)}_{r},Z^{(2)}_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{v}g_{i}(Y^{(2)}_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{v}\tilde{Z}^{(2)}_{r}dW_{r},\ t\in[u,v].

The following proposition yields the contraction property of the solution map
\Phi
on small intervals.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
(H1)
holds. Suppose further that
\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{v},
and that
\left(Y^{(j)},Z^{(j)}\right)\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(R;[u,v])

\theta=\theta(C_{1},\Theta,R,\rho,\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda},m_{p,k}(X;[0,T]))>0

\left|\Phi^{\xi}_{[u,v]}\left(Y^{(1)},Z^{(1)}\right)-\Phi^{\xi}_{[u,v]}\left(Y^{(2)},Z^{(2)}\right)\right|_{p,k;[u,v]}\leq\rho\left|\left(Y^{(1)}-Y^{(2)},Z^{(1)}-Z^{(2)}\right)\right|_{p,k;[u,v]}.

\theta
is independent of

Assume
M=1

(\tilde{Y}^{(j)},\tilde{Z}^{(j)}):=\Phi^{\xi}_{[u,v]}(Y^{(j)},Z^{(j)}),j=1,2.
Denote

For

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}(v-t)\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\|\delta Y\|_{\infty;[t,v]}\right]+C_{1}(v-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big\{\int_{t}^{v}|\delta Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-t|^{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(1+\|X\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right)\|\delta Y\|_{\infty;[t,v]}\right]

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-t|^{\tau}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\|\delta Y\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]+(\|Y^{(1)}\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}+\|Y^{(2)}\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]})\|\delta Y\|_{\infty;[t,v]}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}(v-u)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])+C_{1}(v-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\delta Z\|_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{\tau}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R\big)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v]),

where the second inequality is derived from Proposition
2.1
, and the third inequality is a consequence of Lemma
2.2
. Therefore, we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in[u,v];\omega\in\Omega}|\delta\tilde{Y}_{t}|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\tau,\lambda,p}C_{1}(v-u)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])+C_{1}(v-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\delta Z\|_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{\tau}(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v]).

Noting that

(3.19)

\int_{t}^{v}\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}=-\delta\tilde{Y}_{t}+\int_{t}^{v}\delta f_{r}dr+\int_{t}^{v}\delta g_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r}),

we can proceed similarly to the estimation of

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big|\int_{t}^{v}|\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{k}{2}}\right]\lesssim_{k}\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,v]}\Big|\int_{s}^{v}\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big|^{k}\bigg]

) yields that

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}C_{1}(v-u)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])+C_{1}(v-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\delta Z\|_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{\tau}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R\big)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v]).

Now we estimate
\|\delta\tilde{Y}\|_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}
. By the equality (
3.19
), the BDG inequality proved in Lemma
A.1
, and the estimate (
3.20
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\|\delta\tilde{Y}\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big|\int_{t}^{v}|\delta f_{r}|dr\Big|^{k}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\delta g_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}C_{1}^{k}(v-u)^{k}m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])^{k}+C_{1}^{k}(v-u)^{\frac{k}{2}}\|\delta Z\|^{k}_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C_{1}^{k}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{k\tau}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R\big)^{k}m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])^{k},

which implies that

(3.22)

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(\delta\tilde{Y};[u,v])

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}C_{1}(v-u)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v])+C_{1}(v-u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\delta Z\|_{k\text{-BMO};[u,v]}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|v-u|^{\tau}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R\big)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[u,v]).

Assume
|v-u|\leq\theta
. Combining (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle|(\delta\tilde{Y},\delta\tilde{Z})|_{p,k;[u,v]}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Theta}C_{1}\left(\theta+\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\theta^{\tau}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[u,v])+R\big)\right)|(\delta Y,\delta Z)|_{p,k;[u,v]}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Theta}\tilde{C}(\theta+\theta^{\tau}+\theta^{\frac{1}{2}})|(\delta Y,\delta Z)|_{p,k;[u,v]},

where
\tilde{C}:=C_{1}\vee\Big(C_{1}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\big(1+m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])+R\big)\Big)
and
C_{\Theta}>0
is a constant that depends on
\Theta
. If we choose
\theta
to be sufficiently small such that

, then (

) follows. ∎

We are ready to prove the main result in this subsection.

Theorem 1
.

Assume

holds. Then, BSDE (

Remark 3.3

3.2.
Linear BSDEs and comparison theorem

In this subsection, we prove a comparison result for BSDE (

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
(H0)
holds and
(\xi,\eta,X)
satisfies conditions in
(H1)
. Consider the linear BSDE (
3.23
) where
\alpha^{i}:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
i=1,2,...,M
, are adapted
bounded
continuous processes such that
m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])<\infty
. Then (
3.23
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
. Moreover,
we have that
Y
is bounded, i.e.,

\esssup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{t}|\leq C<\infty,

where
C
depends on
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\esssup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}|\alpha|
,
m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])
,
m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])
,
\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}
, and
\Theta.

Remark 3.4
.

We point out that (
3.23
) is not covered by (
1.1
), since
\alpha
is a random process. However, the well-posedness of (

) can be proved in a way similar to that for (

) in Section

.

Proof.

Firstly, we will show that the map
(Y,Z)\mapsto\Phi^{\xi}(Y,Z):=(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
from
\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
to itself by

(3.24)

\tilde{Y}_{t}=\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r},\quad t\in[0,T],

is well defined.
Denote
A:=\esssup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}|\alpha_{t}|
.
Given
(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
, i.e.,
\|(Y,Z)\|_{p,k;[0,T]}<\infty
, we have for
t\in[0,T]
,

\|\alpha^{i}Y\|_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\lesssim\|\alpha^{i}\|_{\infty;[t,T]}\|Y\|_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}+\|\alpha^{i}\|_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\|Y\|_{\infty;[t,T]},

and (see (

))

\esssup\limits_{s\in[t,T],\omega\in\Omega}|Y_{s}|\leq\|Y_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(Y;[t,T]).

Then, by (
2.2
), we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}|T-t|^{k\tau}\|\eta_{i}\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}\left(\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\|\alpha^{i}Y\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}+\left(1+\|X\|^{k\lambda}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right)\|\alpha^{i}Y\|^{k}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k}|T-t|^{k\tau}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}\left(1+m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])^{k\lambda}\right)\left(A^{k}+m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\|Y_{T}\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(Y;[t,T])^{k}\right).

As a consequence of (
3.25
), we have

\esssup_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]<\infty.

Thus, by the martingale representation theorem utilized in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique
progressively measurable process, denoted by
\tilde{Z}
, such that
\int_{0}^{T}|\tilde{Z}_{r}|^{2}dr<\infty
a.s. and

(3.26)

\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\right].

Denote

(3.27)

\tilde{Y}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\right],\quad t\in[0,T].

Then (
3.24
)holds. In addition, similarly to
(
3.6
)–(
3.10
), we have
\|(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\|_{p,k;[0,T]}<\infty
, and hence
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
.

Let
\Phi^{\xi}_{[S,T]}
denote the restriction of the map
\Phi^{\xi}
to the interval

for
0<S<T
. We will demonstrate that
\Phi^{\xi}_{[S,T]}
is a contraction mapping on the space
(\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(S,T),\|\cdot\|_{p,k;[S,T]})
when

is sufficiently small. Assume
Y^{(j)}_{T}=\xi
and
\|(Y^{(j)},Z^{(j)})\|_{p,k}<\infty
for

. Denote
\delta\tilde{Y}:=\tilde{Y}^{(1)}-\tilde{Y}^{(2)},\ \delta\tilde{Z}:=\tilde{Z}^{(1)}-\tilde{Z}^{(2)}
, and denote
\delta Y
and
\delta Z
similarly. Using the same calculation as in the estimate (
3.25
), we obtain the following result,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}\delta Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}|T-t|^{k\tau}\|\eta\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda}\left(1+m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])^{k}\right)\left(A^{k}+m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])^{k}\right)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[t,T])^{k}.

Now we start to estimate
m_{p,k}(\delta\tilde{Y};[S,T])+\|\delta\tilde{Z}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[S,T]}
. By (
3.24
) and Lemma

, we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(\delta\tilde{Y};[S,T])

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}\delta Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle+\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,k}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}\delta Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle+\|\delta\tilde{Z}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[S,T]}.

By the BDG inequality, (
3.26
), and (
3.27
), we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\|\delta\tilde{Z}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[S,T]}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{k}\left\{\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\delta\tilde{Z}_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|^{k}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}|\delta\tilde{Y}_{t}|+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\{\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}\delta Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

Since

In addition, the following estimation is obtained by combining (

), and (

(3.31)

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(\delta\tilde{Y};[S,T])+\|\delta\tilde{Z}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[S,T]}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,k}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[S,T]}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{T}\alpha^{i}_{r}\delta Y_{r}\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta}|T-S|^{\tau}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\left(1+m_{p,k}(X;[0,T])\right)\left(A+m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])\right)m_{p,k}(\delta Y;[S,T])

where

Therefore, (

) yields that

is a contraction mapping on

if

is sufficiently small.

is independent of

follows from

, it can be proved that there exists a constant
C
depending on

,

, and

such that

This completes the proof.
∎

The following result establishes the continuity of the solution to Eq. (

Proposition 3.4

Assume the same conditions as in Proposition

\left\{\eta^{n}\right\}^{\infty}_{n=1}

where

(resp.

(resp. with

\displaystyle\|(Y^{n},Z^{n})-(Y,Z)\|_{p,k;[0,T]}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta,C}\|\xi^{n}-\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\eta^{n}-\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left[\|\alpha^{i,n}_{T}-\alpha^{i}_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(\alpha^{i,n}-\alpha^{i};[0,T])\right],

T,

\sup\limits_{i,n}\|\eta^{n}_{i}\|_{\tau,\lambda},\ \sup\limits_{n}\|\xi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\ m_{p,k}(X;[0,T]),

\sup\limits_{i,n}\|\alpha^{i,n}_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\alpha^{i,n}_{r}\delta Y^{n}_{r}\eta^{n}_{i}(dr,X_{r})+\int_{t}^{T}\delta\alpha^{i,n}_{r}Y_{r}\eta^{n}_{i}(dr,X_{r})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\left(\|\delta\xi^{n}\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(\delta Y^{n};[t,T])^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta,C}\|\delta\xi^{n}\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+|T-t|^{k\tau}m_{p,k}(\delta Y^{n};[t,T])^{k}+\|\delta\alpha^{i,n}_{T}\|^{k}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+m_{p,k}(\delta\alpha^{i,n};[0,T])^{k}+\|\delta\eta^{n}_{i}\|^{k}_{\tau,\lambda},

\displaystyle\bigg(\|\delta\xi^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+|T-t|^{\tau}m_{p,k}(\delta Y^{n};[t,T])+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\delta\alpha^{i,n}_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{M}m_{p,k}(\delta\alpha^{i,n};[0,T])+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\delta\eta^{n}_{i}\|_{\tau,\lambda}\bigg),

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\|Y\|_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}+\|Y^{\prime}\|_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right).

Thus, noting that
(Y,Z),(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
by Theorem
1
, we have

(3.42)

m_{p,k}(\alpha;[0,T])<\infty.

Now, we show that
Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}\geq 0
a.s. Note that
(Y-Y^{\prime},Z-Z^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
is a solution of the following linear BSDE, with a non-decreasing drift
V_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}f(r,Y^{\prime}_{r},Z^{\prime}_{r})-f^{\prime}(r,Y^{\prime}_{r},Z^{\prime}_{r})dr
,

\displaystyle(Y-Y^{\prime})_{t}=(\xi-\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\alpha_{r}(Y-Y^{\prime})_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})+\int_{t}^{T}b_{r}(Y-Y^{\prime})_{r}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}c_{r}(Z-Z^{\prime})^{\top}_{r}dr+V_{T}-V_{t}-\int_{t}^{T}(Z-Z^{\prime})_{r}dW_{r}.

Denote

A^{t}_{s}:=\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}\alpha_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\right\}\text{ for }0\leq t\leq s\leq T.

In view of the finiteness shown in (
3.42
), by (
3.38
) and (
3.39
) we have that
\mathbb{E}_{t}[|A^{t}_{T}|^{2}]
and
\mathbb{E}_{t}[|(A^{t}_{T})^{-1}|^{2}]
are bounded processes. In addition, by applying Corollary
2.1
to
(Y_{s}-Y^{\prime}_{s})A^{t}_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}=(Y_{s}-Y^{\prime}_{s})A^{t}_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\left[b_{r}(Y_{r}-Y^{\prime}_{r})A^{t}_{r}+c_{r}(Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r})^{\top}A^{t}_{r}\right]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}A^{t}_{r}dV_{r}-\int_{t}^{s}A^{t}_{r}(Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r})dW_{r}.

Denote

B^{t}_{s}:=\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{s}b_{r}-\frac{1}{2}|c_{r}|^{2}dr+\int_{t}^{s}c_{r}dW_{r}\right\}\text{ for }0\leq t\leq s\leq T.

Thanks to the boundedness of
b_{t}
and
c_{t}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[|B^{t}_{T}|^{4}\right]\vee\esssup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[|(B^{t}_{T})^{-1}|^{2}\right]<\infty.

Therefore, by the fact that
\|Z-Z^{\prime}\|_{k\text{-BMO};[0,T]}<\infty
and
[
46
, Corollary 2.1]
, we have

(3.43)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}|B^{t}_{r}|^{2}|Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}dr\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|(B^{r}_{T})^{-1}B^{t}_{T}\right|^{2}|Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}dr\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{T}^{t}|^{4}+\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{r}\left[|(B_{T}^{r})^{-1}|^{2}\right]|Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{2}\right]<\infty.

Applying Corollary
2.1
again to
[(Y_{s}-Y^{\prime}_{s})A^{t}_{s}]B^{t}_{s}
, we obtain

Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}=(\xi-\xi^{\prime})A^{t}_{T}B^{t}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}A^{t}_{r}B^{t}_{r}dV_{r}-\int_{t}^{T}A^{t}_{r}B^{t}_{r}(Z_{r}-Z^{\prime}_{r})dW_{r}-\int_{t}^{T}(Y_{r}-Y^{\prime}_{r})A^{t}_{r}B^{t}_{r}c_{r}dW_{r}.

Then, in view of (
3.43
), by the same calculation as in (
3.40
), the last two Itô integrals on the right-hand side are martingales due to the finiteness of

\esssup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[|A^{t}_{T}|^{2}\right]\vee\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[|(A^{t}_{T})^{-1}|^{2}\right],

the boundedness of
|Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}|
and
|c_{t}|,
and the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\int_{t}^{T}|B^{t}_{r}|^{2}dr]<\infty
. Thus, we have

(3.44)

Y_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[(\xi-\xi^{\prime})A^{t}_{T}B^{t}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}A^{t}_{r}B^{t}_{r}dV_{r}\right],

and hence
Y_{t}\geq Y^{\prime}_{t}
a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

By the equality (
3.44
), we have the following strict comparison result.

Corollary 3.4

(strict comparison theorem)
.

Assume the same conditions in Theorem
2
. Assume further that
\xi>\xi^{\prime}
a.s. Then we have
Y_{t}>Y^{\prime}_{t}
a.s.

4.
BSDEs with random terminal times and regularity of solutions

In Section
3.1
, we have studied BSDEs under
(H1)
which assumes
\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}<\infty
. For the more general case
\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}<\infty
(e.g., the trajectories of a fractional Brownian sheet), we will apply a localization argument to study the BSDEs in Part II
[
60
]
. For this purpose, in this section we will establish the well-posedness of BSDE (
1.1
) with a random terminal time, and then study the regularity property for the solution
Y
, in particular estimating
\mathbb{E}[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[S_{1},S_{0}]}]
with
S_{1}
and
S_{0}
being stopping times.

Consider BSDE (
1.1
) with a random terminal time:

(4.1)

\begin{cases}dY_{t}=-f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})dt-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}g_{i}(Y_{t})\eta_{i}(dt,X_{t})+\ Z_{t}dW_{t},\quad t\in[0,S_{0}],\\
Y_{S_{0}}=\xi,\end{cases}

where
S_{0}\in[0,T]
is a stopping time, and
\xi
is an
\mathcal{F}_{S_{0}}
-measurable random variable.
Throughout this section, we assume the following conditions:

Assumption (H2)
.

Let
\tau,

\lambda,
and
p
be the parameters satisfying Assumption
(H0)
, and let
S_{0}\in[0,T]
be a stopping time.

(1)

Assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M}),
for some
\beta\geq 0,
and for some
k>1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

X_{0}=x,\ \esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[0,T]}|X_{t\land S_{0}}|<\infty,\ m_{p,k}(X;[0,S_{0}])<\infty,\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(2)

Assume
g_{i}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and for some
C_{1}>0
,

\|g_{i}\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}\vee\|\nabla g_{i}\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}\vee\|\nabla^{2}g_{i}\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}\leq C_{1},\text{ for }i=1,2,...,M.

(3)

Assume
f
is progressively measurable and satisfies that for a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
and for all
(t,y_{j},z_{j})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times d},\ j=1,2,

(4.2)

|f(\omega,t\land S_{0}(\omega),y_{1},z_{1})-f(\omega,t\land S_{0}(\omega),y_{2},z_{2})|\leq C_{1}\left(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|\right).

In addition, assume

\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega,t\in[0,T]}|f(\omega,t\land S_{0}(\omega),0,0)|<\infty.

Assumption (A)
.

Let
x=(x^{1},...,x^{d})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We assume
X
is given by

(4.3)

X_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(r,X_{r})dW_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}b(r,X_{r})dr,\ t\in[0,T],

where
\sigma(t,x):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
b(t,x):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are measurable functions that are globally Lipschitz in
x
and satisfy the following condition for some positive constant
L
:

\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{|\sigma(t,x)|\vee|b(t,x)|\right\}\leq L.

BSDE with random terminal time was introduced by Peng
[
58
]
(see also Darling-Pardoux
[
11
]
), by which the definition of solution to Eq. (
4.1
) is inspired.

Definition 4.1
.

Under
(H2)
, we call
(Y,Z)
a solution of (
4.1
), if
(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
satisfies

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t\wedge S_{0}}^{S_{0}}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t\wedge S_{0}}^{S_{0}}g_{i}(Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,X_{r})-\int_{t\wedge S_{0}}^{S_{0}}Z_{r}dW_{r},~t\in[0,T],\\
Z_{t}=0\text{ on }[S_{0}\leq t\leq T].\end{cases}

Note that
Y_{t}=\xi
for
t\in[S_{0},T].
The existence and uniqueness of the solution to BSDE (
4.1
) are guaranteed by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
(H2)
holds. Then BSDE (
4.1
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)
in
\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{X}_{t}:=X_{t\land S_{0}}
for
t\in[0,T]
, and
clearly,
\bar{X}
satisfies
(H1)
. Denoting

\bar{g}(t,y):=g(y)\mathbf{1}_{[0,S_{0}]}(t),\ \bar{f}(t,y,z):=f(t,y,z)\mathbf{1}_{[0,S_{0}]}(t),

then Eq. (
4.1
) can be written as

(4.5)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})dr+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}_{i}(r,Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,\bar{X}_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{r}dW_{r},\ t\in[0,T].

As explained below the inequality (
1.7
), the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\tau,\lambda}
are equivalent if the spatial variable
x
in
\eta(t,x)
is restricted to a bounded domain. In view of the boundedness of
\bar{X}
by
(H2)
, the driver
\eta
of the integral
\int\bar{g}_{i}(r,Y_{r})\eta_{i}(dr,\bar{X}_{r})
in (

) can be viewed as the driver restricted to the ball with radius
\esssup_{t,\omega}|\bar{X}_{t}|
. Therefore, we can assume without loss of generality that the condition concerning
\eta
in Assumption
(H1)
is also satisfied. Although
\bar{g}(t,y)
does not satisfy
(H1)
directly, we can still follow the approach used in the proof of Theorem
1
to prove the well-posedness.

More precisely, assume
M=1
without loss of generality, and let
\bar{g}_{r}:=\bar{g}(r,Y_{r})
. Repeating the arguments in the proofs of Proposition
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
, and Proposition
3.2
, the only difference occurs in the computation of the
k
-th moment of
\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\bar{g}_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\|_{p\text{-var}}
in (
3.8
) and
\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\delta\bar{g}_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\|_{p\text{-var}}
in (
3.20
) for some

. These moments can be bounded from above as follows:

\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}g(Y_{r})\mathbf{1}_{[0,S_{0}]}(r)\eta(dr,X_{r})\right\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,\bar{X}_{r})\right\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]

and

\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\delta g_{r}\mathbf{1}_{[0,S_{0}]}(r)\eta(dr,X_{r})\right\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{v}\delta g_{r}\eta(dr,\bar{X}_{r})\right\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[t,v]}\right],

where the estimations for the terms on the right-hand side were already obtained in the proofs of Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In view of the proof of Proposition
4.1
, the analogues of Proposition
3.3
, Corollary
3.1
, Proposition
3.5
, Corollary

, and Corollary

The following result provides an upper bound for the solution of (
4.4
). To emphasize the main items in the upper bound, we use the notation
\Theta_{1}:=(\tau,\lambda,\beta,p,T,C_{1},\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta})
to simplify the dependence of constants, which will be used throughout the remainder of this section.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
(H2)
holds. Denote by
(Y,Z)
the unique solution of BSDE (
4.1
) in the space
\mathfrak{B}_{p,k}(0,T)
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a number satisfying
\tau+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}>1
.
Then for every
q>1,
we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y\right\|^{q}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[0,S_{0}]}\right]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{0}^{S_{0}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1},\varepsilon,q}1+|x|^{\frac{q(\lambda+\beta)}{\varepsilon}}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\|X\|^{\frac{q(\lambda+\beta)}{\varepsilon}}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[0,S_{0}]}\Big]+\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{q}]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|f(\cdot,0,0)\|^{q}_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}\right].

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Assume
M=1
without loss of generality. Denote
f_{r}:=f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})
for
r\in[0,T],
and fix a
q>1.

Step 1.
Firstly, we will prove the estimation (
4.6
) assuming the following additional condition:

(4.7)

\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[0,S_{0}]}\right]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{0}^{S_{0}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\bigg]<\infty.

For an integer
v\geq 1,
split
[0,T]
into
[t_{i},t_{i+1}],

i=1,2,...,v
evenly with the length
\theta:=T/v.
Let
\tau_{i}:=t_{i}\land S_{0}.
By (
4.4
), we claim that for
i=1,2,...,v,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{s}\left[Y_{\tau_{i+1}}+\int_{s\land S_{0}}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})+\int_{s\land S_{0}}^{\tau_{i+1}}f_{r}dr\right]\bigg|^{q}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\|Y_{\tau_{i+1}}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}+\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|f_{r}|dr\Big|^{q}\right],

and that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|Y\right\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1},q}\|Y_{\tau_{i+1}}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}^{q}+\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|f_{r}|dr\Big|^{q}\right].

Indeed, for the second step in (
4.8
) we use the following equality for
s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s}\left[\int_{s\land S_{0}}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{s}\left[\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]-\int_{\tau_{i}}^{s\land S_{0}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r}),

and therefore, by Lemma
A.1
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]\bigg\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\right]\bigg\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}+\Big\|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\cdot}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right].

In addition, by a similar calculation, for the last term on the left-hand side of (
4.8
), we have

which implies (
4.8

Moreover, the inequality (
4.9

4.4

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}Z_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\lesssim_{p,q}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}Z_{r}dW_{r}\Big\|^{q}_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1},q}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|f_{r}|dr\Big|^{q}\right]

A.1
, and the third step is by (
4.8
),
which completes the proof of our claim.
Now we further estimate the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
4.9

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1},\varepsilon}\left(1+\|X\|^{\lambda+\beta}_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}\right)\left(\|g(Y)\|_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}+\|g(Y)\|^{\varepsilon}_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\|g(Y)\|^{1-\varepsilon}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\|X\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}\right)\|X\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-var};[0,S_{0}]}\|g(Y)\|_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1}}\left(1+\|X\|_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}^{\lambda+\beta}\right)\left(1+\|Y\|^{1-\varepsilon}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right)+\|X\|^{\lambda+\beta}_{p\text{-var};[0,S_{0}]}\,,

where the last inequality holds due to Young’s inequality

. Thus,
noting that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|X\|_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}^{q(\lambda+\beta)}\|Y\|^{q(1-\varepsilon)}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\|X\|_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}^{\frac{q(\lambda+\beta)}{\varepsilon}}\right]\right\}^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\right\}^{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon}\,\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\right\}^{1-\varepsilon},

where we denote

, and that

\|X\|_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}\leq|x|+\|X\|_{p\text{-var};[0,S_{0}]},

we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|\int_{\cdot}^{\tau_{i+1}}g(Y_{r})\eta(dr,X_{r})\Big\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1},\varepsilon,q}(1+|x|^{q(\lambda+\beta)}+Q^{\varepsilon})\left(1+\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\right\}^{1-\varepsilon}\right)+Q^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|x|^{q(\lambda+\beta)}+Q^{\varepsilon})\left(1+\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y\|^{q}_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right]\right\}^{1-\varepsilon}\right).

Furthermore, since
\tau_{i+1}-\tau_{i}\leq\theta\leq T,
the Lipschitz condition of

in
(y,z)
yields that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|f_{r}|dr\Big\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Theta_{1}}\theta\left\|\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty;[\tau_{i+1},\tau_{i+1}]}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\Big\|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|Z_{r}|dr\Big\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\left\|\left\|f(\cdot,0,0)\right\|_{\infty;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\theta\left\|\left\|Y\right\|_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\|Y_{\tau_{i+1}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\left\|\left\|f(\cdot,0,0)\right\|_{\infty;[0,S_{0}]}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Combining (
4.9
), (
4.12
), and (
4.13
), we obtain

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left\|\left\|Y\right\|_{p\text{-var};[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\left\|\Big|\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

5.
An application to SPDEs with Neumann boundary conditions

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain (i.e., a bounded, connected open set) with a
C^{\infty}
-boundary
\partial D
. Consider Eq. (
1.3
) with Neumann boundary conditions:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+u(t,x)\partial_{t}B(t,x),\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times D,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle u(T,x)=h(x),\ x\in\bar{D},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u(t,x)=0,\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\partial D.\end{cases}

Here,
B(t,x)
is a fractional Brownian sheet defined on a completed probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
,
with time Hurst parameter
H_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and space Hurst parameters
H_{i}=H\in(0,1)
for
i=1,\dots,d
,
\mathbf{n}
is the inward unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial D
of the domain
D
, and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the inward normal derivative.

In this subsection, we aim to show that
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}[V_{t,x}]
solves SPDE (
5.1
), where
\mathbb{E}^{W}[\cdot]
is the expectation with respect to
W
, and

(5.2)

V_{t,x}:=h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{T}B(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right\},

with
X^{t,x}_{s}
being a reflected Brownian motion in the domain
D
starting at
(t,x)
with
x\in\bar{D}
.

The reflected Brownian motion
X^{t,x}
satisfies the so-called
Skorohod equation
:

(5.3)

X^{t,x}_{s}=x+W_{s}-W_{t}+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbf{n}\left(X_{r}^{t,x}\right)dL_{r}^{t,x},\quad\text{ for }s\geq t,

where
W
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
(see Section
1.2
), and the solution pair
(X^{t,x},L^{t,x})
satisfies that
L^{t,x}
is a continuous, increasing, adapted process, and that
X^{t,x}_{s}\in\bar{D}
and
L^{t,x}_{s}=\int_{t}^{s}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{t,x}_{r}\in\partial D\}}dL^{t,x}_{r}
for all
s\geq t
. The well-posedness of Eq. (
5.3
) was discussed, for instance, in Lions-Sznitman
[
51
]
.
The process
L^{t,x}
coincides with the local time of
X^{t,x}
on the boundary
\partial D
(see Hsu
[
39
, Theorem 3.6]
):

L^{t,x}_{s}=\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{t}^{s}\mathbf{1}_{\{\inf_{y\in\partial D}|y-X^{t,x}_{r}|<\rho\}}dr~\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega),\text{ for }s\in[t,T].

Classical Feynman-Kac formulae for Neumann PDEs can be found, e.g., in
[
21
, Chapter II, Theorem 5.1]
. To obtain Feynman-Kac formula for SPDE (
5.1
), we first prove the following result to validate the term
\mathbb{E}^{W}[V_{t,x}]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with a
C^{\infty}
-boundary. Assume
h(\cdot)\in C(\bar{D};\mathbb{R})
, and the Hurst parameters
H_{0}
and
H
satisfy
H_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1),H\in(0,1)
and
H_{0}+\frac{H}{2}>1
. Let
X^{t,x}
be given by (
5.3
), and
V_{t,x}
be given by (
5.2
). Then,
u(t,x):=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V_{t,x}\right]\text{ is continuous in }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{D},\ \mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}^{B}[|u(t,x)|^{k}]<\infty,
for every
k\geq 1
.

Remark 5.1
.

Assume
(H0)
holds and
B(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}(d\omega^{\prime})
-a.s. In view of Proposition
2.1
, to get the integrability of
V_{t,x}
given by (
5.2
) conditional on
B
, one needs to estimate the expectation of
\mathbb{E}^{W}\exp\left(C\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[a,b]}\right)
or
\mathbb{E}^{W}\exp\left(C\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}\|^{\lambda}_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}\right)
, where
C
is a nonnegative random variable depending only on
B
.

When the domain
D
is convex, one has (see e.g. Tanaka
[
61
, Lemma 2.6]
),
\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}\lesssim\|W\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}
, which implies the exponential integrability of
\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l}}
by Fernique’s Theorem (
[
19
]
). When
D
is not convex, to our best knowledge, the existing literature provides the following estimate according to Aida-Sasaki
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
:

(5.4)

\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}\lesssim\left(\|W\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}+\|W\|^{1+C}_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l};[a,b]}\right)\exp\Big\{C\|W_{\cdot}-W_{a}\|_{\infty;[a,b]}\Big\},

which, however, does not yield the exponential integrability of

. In the proof of Proposition

, we use BSDE theory developed in Section

to prove the integrability.

Proof.

Let
p>2
be a constant such that

By Lemma
A.4
, there exist
\tau<H_{0}
and
\lambda<H
such that
(\tau,\lambda,p)
satisfies
(H0)
, and
B\in C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\text{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}(d\omega^{\prime})
-a.s.

Fix a sample path of
B
in
C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\text{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
m^{(s,t)}_{p,2}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p,2;[s,t]}
be defined by (

) and (
1.9
), respectively,
with
\mathbb{E}_{t}
replaced by
\mathbb{E}^{W}_{t}
, where
\mathbb{E}^{W}_{t}[\cdot]
represents the corresponding conditional expectation with respect to

up to time

. Since

has independent increments, we have
(see, e.g.,
[

), for every

(5.5)

\esssup_{s\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}^{W}_{s}\left[\exp\left\{c\|W\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[s,T]}\right\}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{c\|W\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}\right\}\right]<\infty.

Then
\sup_{(t,x)}m_{p,2}(X^{t,x};[t,T])<\infty
by (
5.4
). In addition, since
X^{t,x}
is bounded and
B\in C^{\tau,\lambda}_{\text{loc}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, due to the reason stated in Remark
3.1
, Proposition
3.3
and Corollary
3.2
still apply for
\eta(t,x)=B(t,x).
Thus, denoting

Y^{t,x}_{s}:=\mathbb{E}^{W}_{s}\left[h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{T}B(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right\}\right],\ s\in[t,T],

there exists a progressively measurable process
Z^{t,x}
such that
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,2}(t,T)
, and
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})
uniquely solves the following linear BSDE,

Y^{t,x}_{s}=h(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}Y^{t,x}_{r}B(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},\ s\in[t,T].

Therefore, we have

\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V_{t,x}\right]\right|=|Y^{t,x}_{t}|\leq\|(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})\|_{p,2;[t,T]}<\infty.

We now prove the continuity of
u
. Let
F(x)
be a smooth cutoff function such that
F(x)=1
for

, and
F(x)=0
for

with
d(x,D)>1
. Then, Eq. (
5.6
) can be rewritten with
B(t,x)
replaced by
F(x)B(t,x)
. For every
n\geq 1
, define
B^{n}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(t-s)B(s,x)ds,
where
\rho^{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is a standard mollifier (see e.g. the proof of Lemma
A.2
). By the proof of Lemma
A.2
, we have that
\lim_{n}\|F(x)B(t,x)-F(x)B^{n}(t,x)\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda}=0
, for every
\tau^{\prime}\in(0,\tau)
. Therefore, we can choose
\tau^{\prime},\lambda\in(0,1]
so that Assumption
(H0)
holds with
(\tau,\lambda)
replaced by
(\tau^{\prime},\lambda)
. For
n\geq 1
, let
V^{n}_{t,x}:=h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\{\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(ds,X^{t,x}_{s})\}
. By Proposition
3.4
, as

(5.7)

\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V^{n}_{t,x}\right]-\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V_{t,x}\right]\right|\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{uniformly in }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{D}.

Furthermore, since

is smooth in time and
\partial_{t}B^{n}(t,x)
is continuous, we have

\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(ds,X^{t,x}_{s})=\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{s}B^{n}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})ds.

Thus, we can apply Pardoux-Zhang
[
56
, Proposition 4.1]
and get that
\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V^{n}_{t,x}\right]
is continuous in
(t,x)
. Then, the uniform convergence (
5.7
) yields the continuity of
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}[V_{t,x}]
.

For the integrability of
u
, by the
a priori
estimate for the solution
Y^{t,x}
in Lemma
3.2
, we have

(5.8)

|Y^{t,x}_{s}|\lesssim\exp\left\{C\left\lfloor(T-t)/\delta\right\rfloor\right\},

where

is a constant depending on
(\tau,\lambda,p,k)
. By the proof of Proposition
3.1
(under (

)), it suffices to find

(\delta+\delta^{\tau}+\delta^{1/2})\cdot C(1+\|F\cdot B\|_{\tau,\lambda})<1/2
. Note that the presence of

is due to the function
f(r,y,z)
, while in Eq. (

)

. Hence, we may choose
\delta>0
such that
\delta^{\tau}\lesssim\|F\cdot B\|^{-1}_{\tau,\lambda}
. Therefore, Eq. (

) yields that
|u(t,x)|=|Y^{t,x}_{t}|\lesssim\exp\{C(T-t)\|F\cdot B\|^{1/\tau}_{\tau,\lambda}\}
.
Then, we get, for

,

\mathbb{E}^{B}[|u(t,x)|^{k}]\lesssim\exp\Big\{kC(T-t)\|F\cdot B\|^{1/\tau}_{\tau,\lambda}\Big\}\lesssim_{k}\mathbb{E}^{B}\left[\exp\left\{\rho\|F\cdot B\|^{2}_{\tau,\lambda}\right\}\right]<\infty,

where the second inequality holds since

and the last one follows from Fernique’s Theorem (
[
19
]
) with
\rho
being some positive constant. This completes the proof.

Now we provide the definition of
weak solution
to Eq. (
5.1
). Then we will show that the Feynman-Kac formula
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V_{t,x}\right]
is a weak solution under some proper conditions.

Definition 5.1
.

We call
u(t,x)
a
weak solution
to Eq. (
5.1
), if there exists
\tilde{\tau}\in(1-H_{0},1]
such that
u(\cdot,x)\in C^{\tilde{\tau}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
for all
x\in\bar{D}
,
and
u(t,x)
satisfies

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{D}u(t,x)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}h(x)\varphi(x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\Delta\varphi(x)dsdx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\varphi(x)dsdS+\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\varphi(x)B(ds,x)dx,\ \mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\bar{D};\mathbb{R}).
Here,
dS
denotes the surface integral (of a scalar field) and
\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\varphi(x)B(ds,x)
is a nonlinear Young integral.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with a
C^{\infty}
-boundary
\partial D
,
h\in C^{\mathrm{Lip}}(\bar{D};\mathbb{R})
, and the Hurst parameters
H_{0}
and
H
satisfy
H_{0}\in(1/2,1),H\in(0,1)
, and
H_{0}+\frac{H}{4}>1
. Let
u(t,x):=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[V_{t,x}\right]
.
Then, for
\tau^{*}\in(0,H_{0}+\frac{H}{2}-1)
,
u(\cdot,x)\in C^{\tau^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l}}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
for all
x\in\bar{D}
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s. Moreover,
u(t,x)
is a weak solution to Eq. (
5.9
).

Remark 5.2
.

The conditions in Proposition
5.1
do not involve the space dimension
d
, since the reflected Brownian motion
X
is bounded (see Remark
3.1
). For the same reason, the conditions imposed in Proposition
5.2
are independent of the space dimension. In contrast, the dimension
d
usually plays a critical role in the study of (stochastic) PDEs in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Noting
H_{0}\in(1/2,1),H\in(0,1)
, and
H+4H_{0}>4
, by Lemma
A.4
, there exists
(\tau,\lambda,\beta)\in(\frac{1}{2},1)\times(0,1)\times[0,\infty)
such that
B(t,x)\in C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s., and that
\tau\in(1/2,1),\ \lambda\in(0,1)
, and
\lambda+4\tau>4
. Let
B^{n}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(t-s)B(s,x)ds,
where
\rho^{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is a standard mollifier. Then by Lemma
A.3
, there exists
\tau^{\prime}\in(0,\tau)
such that

(5.10)

\tau^{\prime}\in(1/2,1),\ \lambda+4\tau^{\prime}>4,\text{ and }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|B^{n}-B\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}=0\quad\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

Let
Y^{n;t,x}_{s}:=\mathbb{E}^{W}_{s}\left[h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right\}\right],
where
X^{t,x}
is given by (
5.3
), and denote
u^{n}(t,x):=Y^{n;t,x}_{t}
. Then, by
[
63
, Theorem 3]
,
u^{n}
belongs to
L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(D))
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s., and satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{D}u^{n}(t,x)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}h(x)\varphi(x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\nabla u^{n}(s,x)\right)^{\top}\nabla\varphi(x)dsdx

\displaystyle+\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u^{n}(s,x)\varphi(x)B^{n}(ds,x)dx,~\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{2}(\bar{D}).

Hence, by Green’s formula (see, e.g.,
[
62
, (4.31) in Chapter 4]
), for a.e.
s\in[0,T],

-\int_{D}\left(\nabla u^{n}(s,x)\right)^{\top}\nabla\varphi(x)dx=\int_{D}u^{n}(s,x)\Delta\varphi(x)dx+\int_{\partial D}u^{n}(s,x)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\varphi(x)dS,

and then
u^{n}
is a weak solution to (
5.9
) with
B
replaced by
B^{n}
.

By (
5.7
) in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

(5.11)

u^{n}(t,x)\rightarrow u(t,x)\text{ uniformly in }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{D},\quad\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

Therefore, for
\varphi\in C^{2}(\bar{D})
, the following three convergences hold as
n\rightarrow\infty
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{D}u^{n}(t,x)\varphi(x)dx\rightarrow\int_{D}u(t,x)\varphi(x)dx;\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u^{n}(s,x)\Delta\varphi(x)dsdx\rightarrow\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\Delta\varphi(x)dsdx;\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}u^{n}(s,x)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\varphi(x)dS\rightarrow\int_{\partial D}u(s,x)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\varphi(x)dS.\end{cases}

Thus, to show that
u
is a weak solution to Eq. (
5.1
), it suffices to prove:

(a)

there exists
\tilde{\tau}\in(1-H_{0},1]
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(5.12)

\|u(\cdot,x)\|_{\tilde{\tau}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}<\infty;

(b)

the following convergence holds a.s.:

(5.13)

\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u^{n}(s,x)\varphi(x)B^{n}(ds,x)dx\to\int_{D}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\varphi(x)B(ds,x)dx,~\text{ as }n\to\infty.

To prove (
5.12
), we first establish a uniform bound for
\big\{\|u^{n}(\cdot,x)\|_{\tau^{*}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]},n\geq 1\big\}
with

\tau^{*}\in\left(0,H_{0}+\frac{H}{2}-1\right).

Denote
v^{n}(t,x):=\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})
and
v(t,x):=\int_{t}^{T}B(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})
. Note that

u^{n}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]\text{ and }u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{v(t,x)\right\}\right],

and hence for
0\leq s<t\leq T
,

(5.14)

u^{n}(t,x)-u^{n}(s,x)=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[h(X^{t,x}_{T})\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}-h(X^{s,x}_{T})\exp\left\{v^{n}(s,x)\right\}\right].

By the boundedness of
X^{s,x}
and Hölder’s inequality,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[h(X^{s,x}_{T})\left(\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}-\exp\left\{v^{n}(s,x)\right\}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left(\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}+\exp\left\{v^{n}(s,x)\right\}\right)\left|v^{n}(t,x)-v^{n}(s,x)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{4v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{4v^{n}(s,x)\right\}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|v^{n}(t,x)-v^{n}(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, Lipschitzness of
h(\cdot)
implies

(5.16)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left(h(X^{t,x}_{T})-h(X^{s,x}_{T})\right)\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[|X^{t,x}_{T}-X^{s,x}_{T}|^{2}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{2v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To obtain a uniform upper bound for
\|u^{n}(\cdot,x)\|_{\tau^{*}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}
,
we will estimate the right-hand side of (
5.14
), and hence the right-hand sides of (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) by triangular inequality. This will be done in the following three steps.

(i)
In the first step, we will estimate
\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|v^{n}(t,x)-v^{n}(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]
for
0\leq s<t\leq T
. Triangular inequality yields that, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq s<t\leq T
,

(5.17)

\displaystyle|v^{n}(t,x)-v^{n}(s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{s}^{t}B^{n}(dr,X^{s,x}_{r})\right|+\left|\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{s,x}_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right|.

For the first term on the right-hand side, by Proposition
2.1
,

(5.18)

\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}B^{n}(dr,X^{s,x}_{r})\right|^{2}\right]\lesssim\|B^{n}\|^{2}_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{2\tau^{\prime}}.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.17
), by
[
40
, Proposition 2.11]
, we have for every
p>2
and
\theta\in(0,1)
satisfying
\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\theta\lambda}{p}>1
,

(5.19)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{s,x}_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|B^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}\left(1+\|X^{s,x}\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[t,T]}+\|X^{t,x}\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right)\bigg(\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{\lambda}_{\infty;[t,T]}|T|^{\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\left(\|X^{s,x}\|^{\lambda}_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[t,T]}+\|X^{t,x}\|^{\lambda}_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[t,T]}\right)^{\theta}\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{(1-\theta)\lambda}_{\infty;[t,T]}|T|^{\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\theta\lambda}{p}}\bigg).

In order to bound the right-hand side of (

\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|_{\infty;[t,T]}

[
66

X^{s,x}_{r}=X^{t,X^{s,x}_{t}}_{r}

Hence, by the

[
56

(5.20)

\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}-X^{t,X^{s,x}_{t}}_{\cdot}\right\|^{4}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|X^{s,x}_{t}-x\right|^{4}\right].

Moreover, the estimate (
5.4
) yields

(5.21)

\left|X^{s,x}_{t}-x\right|\lesssim\left(\|W\|_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[s,t]}+\|W\|^{1+C}_{\frac{1}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[s,t]}\right)\exp\left\{C\|W_{\cdot}-W_{s}\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\right\}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then, combining (
5.20

5.21
), and (
5.5

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}-X^{s,x}_{\cdot}\right\|^{4}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left\|X^{t,x}_{\cdot}-X^{t,X^{s,x}_{t}}_{\cdot}\right\|^{4}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|X^{s,x}_{t}-x\right|^{4}\right]\lesssim|t-s|^{\frac{4}{p}}.

Thus, (
5.19
) implies that, noting (
5.22
),

(5.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{s,x}_{r})-\int_{t}^{T}B^{n}(dr,X^{t,x}_{r})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|B^{n}\|^{2}_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{2\lambda}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]+\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\|X^{t,x}\|^{4\lambda\theta}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}+\|X^{s,x}\|^{4\lambda\theta}_{p\text{-var};[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{4(1-\theta)\lambda}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|B^{n}\|^{2}_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}\left(\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{4}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}+\left\{\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\|X^{t,x}-X^{s,x}\|^{4}_{\infty;[t,T]}\right]\right\}^{\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|B^{n}\|^{2}_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\frac{2(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}}.

Combining (
5.17
), (
5.18
), and (
5.23
), we have (notice
\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}<\tau^{\prime}
by
\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\theta\lambda}{p}>1>\frac{\lambda}{p}

(5.24)

\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left|v^{n}(s,x)-v^{n}(t,x)\right|^{2}\right]\lesssim\|B^{n}\|^{2}_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\frac{2(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}}.

(ii)

In this step, we will estimate
\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{4v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{2v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]
on the right-hand side of (
5.15
) and (
5.16
).
For
(r,x)\in[t,T]\times\bar{D}
, denote

\hat{Y}^{t,x;n}_{r}:=\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{4\int_{r}^{T}B^{n}(d\nu,X^{t,x}_{\nu})\right\}\right].

By Proposition
3.3
and Corollary
3.2
, there exists a progressively measurable process
\hat{Z}^{t,x;n}
such that
(\hat{Y}^{t,x;n},\hat{Z}^{t,x;n})\in\mathfrak{B}_{p,2}(t,T)
, and
(\hat{Y}^{t,x;n},\hat{Z}^{t,x;n})
uniquely solves the following linear BSDE,

\hat{Y}^{t,x;n}_{r}=1+\int_{r}^{T}4\hat{Y}^{t,x;n}_{\nu}B^{n}(d\nu,X^{t,x}_{\nu})-\int_{r}^{T}\hat{Z}^{t,x;n}_{\nu}dW_{\nu},\ r\in[t,T].

In addition, letting
F:\bar{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the same smooth cutoff function as in the proof of Proposition

, we have

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|F\cdot B^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda}<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s., and hence Assumption
(H0)
holds with
(\tau,\lambda)

replaced by
(\tau^{\prime},\lambda)
.
Moreover, by (

) and (

) we have
\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{D}}m_{p,k}(X^{t,x};[t,T])<\infty.

Then, we can apply Proposition
3.3
to get that there exists a finite positive random variable

independent of
(n,t,x)
such that
\hat{Y}^{t,x;n}_{t}(\omega^{\prime})\leq K(\omega^{\prime})
,
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s. Hence, we have

(5.25)

\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\exp\left\{4v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right](\omega^{\prime})=\hat{Y}^{t,x;n}_{t}(\omega^{\prime})\leq K(\omega^{\prime})<\infty.

(iii)
In this final step, we will obtain a uniform upper bound for
\|u^{n}(\cdot,x)\|_{\tau^{*}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}
using the estimations obtained in the previous two steps. By (

), (

), and (

), we have for
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s.
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

(5.26)

\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[h(X^{s,x}_{T})\left(\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}-\exp\left\{v^{n}(s,x)\right\}\right)\right]\right|(\omega^{\prime})\lesssim|K(\omega^{\prime})|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|B^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}(\omega^{\prime})|t-s|^{\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}},

and by (
5.16
), (
5.22
), and (
5.25
), we have for
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s.
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

(5.27)

\left|\mathbb{E}^{W}\left[\left(h(X^{t,x}_{T})-h(X^{s,x}_{T})\right)\exp\left\{v^{n}(t,x)\right\}\right]\right|(\omega^{\prime})\lesssim|K(\omega^{\prime})|^{\frac{1}{4}}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recalling (
5.14
), the estimations (
5.26
) and (
5.27
) together with triangular inequality yield that, for all
(n,t,s,x)
,

\left|u^{n}(t,x)-u^{n}(s,x)\right|(\omega^{\prime})\lesssim\left(\left|K(\omega^{\prime})\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|B^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}(\omega^{\prime})+\left|K(\omega^{\prime})\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)|t-s|^{\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}},

which further implies that for
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-a.s.
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

(5.28)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|u^{n}(\cdot,x)\|_{\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}(\omega^{\prime})\lesssim\left|K(\omega^{\prime})\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\|B^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda;\beta}(\omega^{\prime})+\left|K(\omega^{\prime})\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Now, we are ready to prove (
5.12
) and (
5.13
). In view of the uniform convergence (
5.11
) and the uniform bound (
5.28
), we can apply
[
25
, Corollary 8.18 (ii)]
and get that for each
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in(0,\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p})
,

(5.29)

\|u^{n}(\cdot,x)-u(\cdot,x)\|_{\tau^{\prime\prime}\text{-H\"{o}l};[0,T]}\to 0\text{ uniformly in }x\in\bar{D}\text{ as }n\to\infty,\quad\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\text{-a.s.}

Note that the condition
\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\theta\lambda}{p}>1
is equivalent to
\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}<\tau^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda}{p}-1
, and recall that
\tau^{*}\in(0,H_{0}+\frac{H}{2}-1)
. Then, by choosing
(\lambda,\tau,p)
sufficiently close to
(H,H_{0},2)
and
\tau^{\prime}
sufficiently close to
\tau

\theta\in(\frac{p(1-\tau^{\prime})}{\lambda},1)
such that:

\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}>\tau^{*}.

Similarly, since
\lambda+4\tau^{\prime}>4,
by choosing
p
close enough to
2

\theta

\frac{p(1-\tau^{\prime})}{\lambda}

\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p}+\tau^{\prime}>1.

Thus, we can choose
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in(0,\frac{(1-\theta)\lambda}{p})

\tau^{\prime\prime}>\tau^{*}\text{ and }\tau^{\prime\prime}+\tau^{\prime}>1.

Noting that

\tau^{*}\in(0,H_{0}+\frac{H}{2}-1)

5.12
) with
\tilde{\tau}=\tau^{\prime\prime}\in(1-H_{0},1]

Then, combining the convergence (
5.10

Appendix A
Some facts on processes with space-time regularity

The Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (BDG inequality) for
p
-variation can be found, for example, in Friz-Victoir
[
25
, Theorem 14.12]
. Let
M
be a continuous local martingale defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
that satisfies the usual conditions. For
p>2
and
k\geq 1
, there exists a positive number
C
depending only on
p
and
k
such that

(A.1)

C^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\langle M\rangle^{\frac{k}{2}}_{T}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|M\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[0,T]}]\leq C\mathbb{E}[\langle M\rangle^{\frac{k}{2}}_{T}].

Denote
\langle M\rangle_{t,s}:=\langle M\rangle_{s}-\langle M\rangle_{t}
. The inequality (
A.1
) also holds for conditional expectations (see Diehl-Zhang
[
16
]
).

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
p>2
,
k\geq 1
, and
t\in[0,T]
. Let
M
be a continuous local martingale such that
\mathbb{E}[\langle M\rangle_{t,T}^{k/2}]<\infty
. Then, there exists a positive number
C
depending only on
p
and
k
such that

C^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\langle M\rangle_{t,T}^{\frac{k}{2}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|M\|^{k}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[t,T]}]\leq C\mathbb{E}_{t}[\langle M\rangle_{t,T}^{\frac{k}{2}}].

If we further assume that
\mathbb{E}[\|M\|^{k}_{\infty;[t,T]}]<\infty
(hence
M
is a martingale), the above result, combined with the lower bound of the classical BDG inequality, yields

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|M\|^{k}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[t,T]}]\lesssim_{p,k}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|M\|^{k}_{\infty;[t,T]}].

In addition, Doob’s inequality yields, for
k>1
,

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\|M\|^{k}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[t,T]}]\lesssim_{p,k}\mathbb{E}_{t}[|M_{T}|^{k}].

Corollary A.1
.

Assume
p>2
and
k\geq 1,
and let
S
be a stopping time such that
S\leq T.
Suppose that
M
is a continuous local martingale with
\mathbb{E}[\langle M\rangle_{S,T}^{k/2}]<\infty
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
p
and
k
such that

(A.2)

C^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{S}[\langle M\rangle_{S,T}^{\frac{k}{2}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{S}[\|M\|^{k}_{p\text{-}\mathrm{var};[S,T]}]\leq C\mathbb{E}_{S}[\langle M\rangle_{S,T}^{\frac{k}{2}}].

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\mathcal{F}_{t\vee S}.
Since
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
satisfies the usual conditions, and referring to the characterization
\mathcal{G}_{t+}=\mathcal{F}_{t\vee S+}
shown in
[
13
, IV-56 (d)]
, clearly
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
also satisfies the usual conditions. Note that
\bar{M}_{t}:=M_{t\vee S}
is a martingale under the filtration
\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
, and that
\langle\bar{M}\rangle_{t}:=\langle M\rangle_{t\vee S}-\langle M\rangle_{S}
is the quadratic variation process of
\bar{M}
. Thus, by Lemma
A.1
, we have (denoting by
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{t}
)

C^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}[\langle\bar{M}\rangle_{T}^{\frac{k}{2}}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}[\|\bar{M}\|^{k}_{p\text{-var};[0,T]}]\leq C\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}[\langle\bar{M}\rangle_{T}^{\frac{k}{2}}],

which gives (
A.2
).
∎

The following approximation result is used in the proof of Proposition
3.5
.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\tau,\lambda\in(0,1]
, where we recall that the space
C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau,\lambda}
defined in (
1.6
). Then we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta(t,x)-\eta(0,x)|<\infty.

Moreover, there exists a sequence
\{\eta^{n}(t,x)\}_{n\geq 1}
such that for each
n\geq 1
,
\|\eta^{n}\|_{\tau,\lambda}<\infty
,
\eta^{n}
is smooth in time,
\partial_{t}\eta^{n}(t,x)
is continuous in
(t,x)
, and
\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}\eta^{n}(t,x)|<\infty
. Furthermore, for every
0<\tau^{\prime}<\tau
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\eta^{n}-\eta\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda}=0,

Proof.

Clearly,

\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta(t,x)-\eta(0,x)|\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|T|^{\tau}<\infty.

We extend the time domain of
\eta
to
\mathbb{R}
, i.e, we define

\eta(t,x)=\eta(0,x)\mathbf{1}_{[t<0]}+\eta(t,x)\mathbf{1}_{[0\leq t\leq T]}+\eta(T,x)\mathbf{1}_{[t>T]},\ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consider
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
(i.e.
\rho
is a smooth function with compact support) such that
\rho\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(s)ds=1
. For
n\geq 1
, let
\rho^{n}(t):=n\rho(nt)
and denote

\eta^{n}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(t-s)\eta(s,x)ds,\ t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Clearly, for each

n\geq 1,

\eta^{n}
is smooth in time,
\partial_{t}\eta^{n}(t,x)
is continuous in
(t,x),
and
\sup_{(t,x)}|\partial_{t}\eta^{n}(t,x)|<\infty
by the fact that

\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\rho^{n}(t-s)\Big(\eta(s,x)-\eta(0,x)\Big)ds\right|\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|T|^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{t}\rho^{n}(t)|dt<\infty.

In addition, by (
1.6
) and the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(s)ds=1
, we have
\|\eta^{n}\|_{\tau,\lambda}\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}<\infty.

Furthermore, denoting
\delta\eta^{n}:=\eta^{n}-\eta
and
C:=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(r)\left|r\right|^{\tau}dr
, we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s<t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\delta\eta^{n}(s,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(s,y)+\delta\eta^{n}(t,y)|}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(r)\left|\eta(t-r,x)-\eta(t,x)-\eta(t-r,y)+\eta(t,y)\right|dr}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(r)\left|\eta(s-r,x)-\eta(s,x)-\eta(s-r,y)+\eta(s,y)\right|dr}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(r)|r|^{\tau}dr=Cn^{-\tau}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }n\rightarrow 0.

For every
0<\tau^{\prime}<\tau
, we claim that
\|\delta\eta^{n}\|_{\tau^{\prime},\lambda}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. More precisely, by (
A.3
),

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s<t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\delta\eta^{n}(s,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(s,y)+\delta\eta^{n}(t,y)|}{|t-s|^{\tau^{\prime}}|x-y|^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s<t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\delta\eta^{n}(s,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(s,y)+\delta\eta^{n}(t,y)|}{|t-s|^{\tau}|x-y|^{\lambda}}\Big)^{\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}}\Big(Cn^{-\tau}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\Big)^{1-\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\tau^{\prime}-\tau}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(t,y)|}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}\lesssim n^{\tau^{\prime}-\tau}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Moreover,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta^{n}(t,x)-\eta(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{n}(s)|\eta(t,x)-\eta(t-s,x)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T],|s-t|<\frac{1}{n}\\
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}|\eta(t,x)-\eta(s,x)|\leq\frac{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}}{n^{\tau}}\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty,

where we assume that
\rho^{n}
is supported in
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
without loss of generality.
Hence by (
A.4
),

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s<t\leq T\\
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\delta\eta^{n}(s,x)-\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)|}{|t-s|^{\tau^{\prime}}}\leq 2\|\eta\|^{\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}}_{\tau,\lambda}\cdot\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\delta\eta^{n}(t,x)|\right)^{1-\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

This completes the proof.

The following lemma shows that the fractional Brownian sheet almost surely belongs to
C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}
.

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose
B
is a fractional Brownian sheet on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Hurst parameter
H:=(H_{0},H_{1},\ldots,H_{d})
,
H_{j}\in(0,1)
for every
j=0,1,...,d
. For every
\theta\in(0,\min_{0\leq j\leq d}H_{j})
, set
\tau:=H_{0}-\theta
,
\lambda:=\min_{1\leq j\leq d}H_{j}-\theta
and
\beta:=2\theta+\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}-\min_{1\leq j\leq d}H_{j}
. Then we have

\|B\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}<\infty,\ a.s.

Proof.

First, Hu-Lê
[
40
, Example 5.9]
implies that

(A.5)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\geq 1,0<\delta_{i}\leq 2^{H_{i}}R\\

Here,

and the notation

represents the

-increment of

over the rectangle
[(t,x),(s,y)]
, as used in
[
40
, Section 5]
. In (
A.5
), for every
(t,x),(s,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\delta_{0}=|t-s|^{H_{0}}
,
\delta_{j}=|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{H_{j}}
for

, and
R=1\vee T^{H_{0}}\vee\sup_{j=1,...,d}\left\{|x_{j}|^{H_{j}}\vee|y_{j}|^{H_{j}}\right\}
. Then, by (
A.5
), the following fact holds,

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(s,y)\\
\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{|B(\square_{d+1}[(t,x),(s,y)])|}{|t-s|^{H_{0}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{H_{j}}\Big|\log\Big((2R)^{d+1}|t-s|^{-H_{0}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{-H_{j}}\Big)\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}}<\infty\qquad\text{a.s.}

Therefore,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(s,y)\\

Let
w^{k}:=(x_{1},...,x_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;
z^{k}:=(0,...,0,y_{k},0,...,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
y_{k}
is at the

-th component of

.
Notice that
B(t,x)=0
if any component of
(t,x)
is zero. We have

\displaystyle B(\square_{d+1}[(t,\omega^{k});(s,z^{k})])

\displaystyle=B(\square_{d+1}[(t,x_{1},...,x_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d});(s,0,...,0,y_{k},0,...,0)])

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d+1}B(t,x_{1},...,x_{k-1},x_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d})+(-1)^{d}B(t,x_{1},...,x_{k-1},y_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{d-1}B(s,x_{1},...,x_{k-1},y_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d})+(-1)^{d}B(s,x_{1},...,x_{k-1},x_{k},y_{k+1},...,y_{d}).

Therefore, we have

(A.7)

Combining (
A.6

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(s,y)\\

\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{|B(\square_{d+1}[(t,x),(s,y)])|}{|t-s|^{H_{0}-\theta}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{H_{j}-\theta}\left(1+|x|^{(d+1)\theta}+|y|^{(d+1)\theta}\right)}\lesssim M,

which completes the proof.

Appendix B
Some preliminary proofs on nonlinear Young integrals

The following so-called
sewing lemma
, which plays a key role in defining the nonlinear Young integral, extends Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.3 in
[
20
]
, and can be proved in the same way as in the proof of Theorem 2.5 in
[
26
]
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
A:\{(s,t);0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function satisfying

|\delta A_{s,u,t}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}w_{i}(s,t)^{1+\varepsilon_{i}},\quad 0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq T,

for some
\varepsilon_{i}>0,\ i=1,2,...,l
, where
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
,…,
w_{l}
are controls and

\delta A_{s,u,t}:=A(s,t)-A(s,u)-A(u,t).

Then, there exists a unique function
\mathcal{A}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying that

|\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A(s,t)|\leq\frac{l^{\varepsilon_{0}}}{1-2^{-\varepsilon_{0}}}\sum_{i=1}^{l}w_{i}(s,t)^{1+\varepsilon_{i}},\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T,

where
\mathcal{A}_{s,t}:=\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s)
,
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{\varepsilon_{i},i=1,\dots,l\}
,
and

\mathcal{A}_{s,t}=\lim_{|\pi|\downarrow 0}\sum\limits_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}A(t_{i},t_{i+1}).

Here,
|\pi|:=\max_{i}|t_{i}-t_{i-1}|
is the maximum length of subintervals of the partition
\pi:=\{[t_{i},t_{i+1}];\ s=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=t\}
, which is also called the mesh of
\pi
.

The proof of the following lemma is similar to
[
25
, Proposition 5.2]
and hence is omitted.

Lemma B.2
.

Suppose that for every
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,
|\Gamma(s,t)|\leq w(s,t)^{\theta}
for some
\theta>1
, where
w
is a control function. Then, we have

z_{r}:=\lim_{|\pi|\downarrow 0}\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\Gamma(t_{i}\land r,t_{i+1}\land r)=0,\ \ \ \text{for all }r\in[0,T].

Some proofs for Section
2
are listed below.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

First, we will prove that the nonlinear integral is well-defined. Since there exists
K>0
such that
\|x\|_{\infty;[0,T]}\leq K
, we can assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
without losing generality. Denoting
A(s,t):=y_{s}\eta(t,x_{s})-y_{s}\eta(s,x_{s})
, we have

(B.1)

\displaystyle|\delta A_{s,u,t}|

\displaystyle\leq|y_{s}\eta(t,x_{s})-y_{s}\eta(u,x_{s})-y_{s}\eta(t,x_{u})+y_{s}\eta(u,x_{u})|

\displaystyle+|y_{s}\eta(t,x_{u})-y_{s}\eta(u,x_{u})-y_{u}\eta(t,x_{u})+y_{u}\eta(u,x_{u})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}|t-u|^{\tau}|x_{s}-x_{u}|^{\lambda}+\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|t-u|^{\tau}|y_{s}-y_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|t-s|^{\tau}\left(\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\left(\|x\|_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}^{p_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}}+\left(\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}^{p_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\right).

Recall that
|t-s|,

\|x\|_{p_{1}\text{-var;[s,t]}}^{p_{1}},
and
\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var;[s,t]}}^{p_{2}}
are all controls. Then, by Exercise 1.9 in
[
25
]
and the assumptions
\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}>1
and
\tau+\frac{1}{p_{2}}>1
, we can define the following controls,

(B.2)

\displaystyle w_{1}(s,t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}|t-s|^{\tau}\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}},\quad w_{2}(s,t):=\left\{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda}|t-s|^{\tau}\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}.

By (
B.1
) we have

(B.3)

|\delta A_{s,u,t}|\leq w_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta}+w_{2}(s,t)^{1+\delta}\text{ with }\delta:=\min\left\{\tau+\frac{1}{p_{2}}-1,\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p_{1}}-1\right\}.

Therefore, we can apply Lemma
B.1
to
A(s,t)
, which defines
\int_{a}^{b}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})
.

Second, we will prove the estimates (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Assume
\eta\in C^{\tau,\lambda;\beta}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, a calculation similar to (
B.1
) leads to, for
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\left|\delta A_{s,u,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\tau}\Big((1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}+(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{w}_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta}+\hat{w}_{2}(s,t)^{1+\delta},

where

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{1}(s,t):=\left\{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}|t-s|^{\tau}\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}},

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{2}(s,t):=\left\{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]})|t-s|^{\tau}\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}},

are controls, as shown by Exercise 1.9 in
[
25
]
. By Lemma
B.1
, we have for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

(B.4)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})-A(s,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\Big(\hat{w}_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta}+\hat{w}_{2}(s,t)^{1+\delta}\Big).

By the definition of
\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}
in (
1.5
), we also have the following estimate for
s\leq u\leq v\leq t
,

(B.5)

|A(u,v)|=|y_{u}\eta(u,x_{u})-y_{u}\eta(v,x_{u})|\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|v-u|^{\tau}\left(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[u,v]}\right)\|y\|_{\infty;[u,v]}.

Now we are ready to estimate
\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-var};[s,t]}
. Letting
\pi
be a partition on
[s,t]
, combining (
B.4
) with
(
B.5
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\left|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})\right|^{\frac{1}{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\in\pi}\Bigg(\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\Big(\hat{w}_{1}(t_{i},t_{i+1}))^{1+\delta}+\hat{w}_{2}(t_{i},t_{i+1})^{1+\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle+\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t_{i+1}-t_{i}|^{\tau}\left(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\right)\|y\|_{\infty;[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\|\eta\|^{\frac{1}{\tau}}_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|\Bigg(\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}+\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{\tau}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\cdot}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,X_{r})\right\|_{\frac{1}{\tau}\text{-var};[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\tau}\Bigg(\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p_{1}\text{-var};[s,t]}

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{p_{2}\text{-var};[s,t]}+\Big(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]}\Big)\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\Bigg).

Then we have (
2.1
), and (
2.2
) can be proved similarly by verifying the following fact,

(B.6)

\left|\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}\eta(dr,x_{r})-A(s,t)\right|\leq\frac{2^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}\Big(w_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta}+w_{2}(s,t)^{1+\delta}\Big).

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
A(s,t):=y_{s}\eta(t,x_{s})-y_{s}\eta(s,x_{s}).

Similar to (
B.1
) in Proposition
2.1
, we have for
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\left|\delta A_{s,u,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}|t-u|^{\tau}|x_{s}-x_{u}|^{\lambda}(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]})

\displaystyle+\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-u|^{\tau}|y_{s}-y_{u}|(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]})

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\tau}\Big((1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-var};[s,t]}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}

\displaystyle+2^{\varepsilon}(1+\|x\|^{\beta+\lambda}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|y\|^{\varepsilon}_{\infty;[s,t]}\|y\|^{1-\varepsilon}_{p\text{-var};[s,t]}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{w}_{1}(s,t)^{1+\delta^{\prime}}+\tilde{w}_{2}(s,t)^{1+\delta^{\prime}},

where
\delta^{\prime}:=\min\left\{\tau+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{p}-1,\tau+\frac{\lambda}{p}-1\right\}
, and

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{1}(s,t):=\left\{\|\eta\|_{\tau,\lambda;\beta}|t-s|^{\tau}(1+\|x\|^{\beta}_{\infty;[s,t]})\|x\|^{\lambda}_{p\text{-var};[s,t]}\|y\|_{\infty;[s,t]}\right\}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta^{\prime}}},

are control functions. Then the desired result follows from the proof of Proposition

.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Assume
x=(x^{1},x^{2},...,x^{N})^{\top}
and
y=(y^{1},y^{2},...,y^{N})^{\top}
, where
x^{i},y^{i}\in C^{p\text{-var}}([0,T])
for
i=1,2,...,N
. Let
s\leq t
. By the Newton-Leibniz formula and the triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle|g(y_{t})-g(y_{s})-g(x_{t})+g(x_{s})|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{y^{i}}g(y^{1}_{t},...,y^{i-1}_{t},y^{i}_{t}+\lambda(x^{i}_{t}-y^{i}_{t}),x^{i+1}_{t},...,x^{N}_{t})\right)d\lambda(x^{i}_{t}-y^{i}_{t}-x^{i}_{s}+y^{i}_{s})\right]\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{1}\big(\partial_{y^{i}}g(y^{1}_{t},...,y^{i-1}_{t},y^{i}_{t}+\lambda(x^{i}_{t}-y^{i}_{t}),x^{i+1}_{t},...,x^{N}_{t})

\displaystyle-\partial_{y^{i}}g(y^{1}_{s},...,y^{i-1}_{s},y^{i}_{s}+\lambda(x^{i}_{s}-y^{i}_{s}),x^{i+1}_{s},...,x^{N}_{s})\big)d\lambda(x^{i}_{s}-y^{i}_{s})\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla g\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(x_{t}-y_{t})-(x_{s}-y_{s})|+\|\nabla^{2}g\|_{\infty;\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x_{t}-x_{s}|+|y_{t}-y_{s}|)|x_{s}-y_{s}|,

which implies the desired result.
∎

On certain blocking sets and the minimum weight of the code of generalised polygons
Sebastian Petit, Geertrui Van de Voorde

Abstract
In this paper, we study and characterise certain blocking sets in generalised polygons. This will allow us to derive new results about the minimum weight and minimum weight code words in the code generated by the rows of the incidence matrix of a generalised polygon over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} .



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

The study of the code generated by the incidence matrix of points and blocks of a design has a long and rich history, described in the monograph
[
1
]
. Arguably the best studied case is that of points and lines in a projective plane, partially motivated by the relevance of such codes in the proof of the non-existence of a projective plane of order 10
[
10
]
.

A classical result in this area shows that the
p
-ary code generated by the incidence vectors of lines of a projective plane of order
q=p^{h}
,
p
prime, has minimum weight
q+1
, and the minimum weight vectors are precisely the scalar multiples of incidence vectors of lines (see Theorem
4
). An easy proof of this result is based on the correspondence between small weight code words in this code with blocking sets in the plane and the fact that the smallest blocking sets in the plane are given by lines (see Theorem
5
).

Similar results have been shown for the code generated by subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
and for the code generated by lines of a regular generalised
2m
-gon. We will provide more details in Subsection
1.2.2
.

This paper contributes to this study by showing that the minimum weight of the code of points and lines in an arbitrary, not necessarily regular, thick generalised polygon is still the weight of a line. To prove this, we will establish a connection with certain blocking sets in weak generalised polygons, regardless of whether the polygon is regular or not. Our first main result (Theorem
6
) will find a lower bound on the size of such blocking sets, but it will also show that they behave in a fundamentally different way than in the case of projective spaces.
As a consequence of this difference in behaviour, some conditions will arise in our characterisation of minimum weight codewords in the code of not necessarily regular thick generalised polygons (see Theorem
7
).

1.2
Background

1.2.1
Weak generalised polygons

We now formally introduce the notions used in this paper. As usual, a point-line geometry is a triple consisting of a point set
\mathcal{P}
, a line set
\mathcal{L}
and a symmetric incidence relation
\mathtt{I}\subseteq(\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{L})\cup(\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{P})
. An
element
of a point-line geometry is a point or a line of that geometry.

Definition 1
.

For
n\geq 3
, a weak generalised
n
-gon is a point-line geometry satisfying:

1.

There exists no
k
-gon (as a subgeometry) for
2\leq k<n
.

2.

Every two elements of
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{L}
are contained in an
n
-gon.

Definition 2
.

A thick generalised
n
-gon is a weak generalised
n
-gon with the additional property:

1.

There exists an
(n+1)
-gon (as a subgeometry).

Remark 1
.

Property (GP3) is equivalent to the following, see for instance
[
13
]
:

1.

Every point is incident with at least three lines and every line is incident with at least three points.

Remark 2
.

In the literature, the term
generalised polygon
is sometimes used for what we call thick generalised polygons. To avoid ambiguity, we will always add
weak
or
thick
.

Remark 3
.

Most of our results apply to weak generalised polygons. In our final result, Theorem
7
, the other conditions we impose imply that the considered generalised polygon needs to be thick.

The
incidence graph
of a point-line geometry is the bipartite graph with as vertices the points and the lines and an edge between vertices
x
and
y
if and only if
x\mathtt{I}y
.

A weak generalised
n
-gon can also be defined via its incidence graph:

Corollary 1
.

(See e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 1.5.10]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a point-line geometry with at least two points and the properties that:

•

every point is incident with at least two lines;

•

every line is incident with at least two points.

Then,
\Gamma
is a weak generalised
n
-gon if and only if its incidence graph has diameter
n
and girth
2n
.

The
distance
between elements of a weak generalised
n
-gon
\Gamma
is the distance inherited from its incidence graph. Two points, resp. lines, are
opposite
if they are at the furthest possible distance; the distance between opposite points, resp. lines, is given by
n
.

The following useful observation follows directly from the definition of a weak generalised polygon.

Corollary 2
.

(See e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.5]
)

Let
p
be a point and
L
be a line of a weak generalised
2m
-gon
\Gamma
,
m\geq 2
. Then there is a unique point on
L
closest (that is, at smallest distance) to
p
.

We will only deal with weak finite generalised polygons that have an
order

(s,t)
, that is, such that every line is incident with
s+1
points and every point is incident with
t+1
lines for some integers
s,t\geq 1
. It is well-known (see for example
[
13
, Corollary 1.5.3]
) that every thick finite generalised polygon has an order. While having an order is not a strong restriction, the famous theorem of Feit and Higman shows that there are rather strong restrictions known on the possible values of
n,s,t
:

Theorem 1
.

(Feit & Higman
[
6
]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a finite weak generalised
n
-gon of order
(s,t)
with
n\geq 3
. Then one of the following holds:

1.

s=t=1
, and
\Gamma
is an ordinary
n
-gon;

2.

n=3,s=t>1
, and
\Gamma
is a projective plane;

3.

n=4
and
\frac{st(1+st)}{s+t}
is an integer;

4.

n=6
and if
s,t>1
, then
st
is a perfect square;

5.

n=8
and if
s,t>1
then
2st
is a perfect square;

6.

n=12
and
s=1
or
t=1
.

For thick generalised polygons, more is known:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite thick generalised
n
-gon of order
(s,t)
with
n\geq 4
. Then one of the following holds:

1.

(Higman
[
8
]
)
n=4
and
s\leq t^{2}
, dually
t\leq s^{2}
;

2.

(Haemers & Roos
[
7
]
)
n=6
and
s\leq t^{3}
, dually
t\leq s^{3}
;

3.

(Higman
[
8
]
)
n=8
and
s\leq t^{2}
, dually
t\leq s^{2}
.

Many non-isomorphic thick generalised quadrangles are known but up to duality, only two classes of finite thick generalised hexagons are known: the
split Cayley hexagons
of order
(q,q)
and the
twisted triality hexagons
of order
(q^{3},q)
. Only one class of finite thick generalised octagons is known (again up to duality): the Ree-Tits octagons of order
(q,q^{2})
.
For more information, we refer to
[
13
]
.

1.2.2
Codes

In this paper, we will study the code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is a finite weak generalised
2m
-gon. This code will be defined using the following conventions.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
\mathcal{P}
be a set.

•

\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
is the vector space of
\mathbb{F}
-valued functions on the set
\mathcal{P}
;

•

For a subspace

\mathfrak{M}
of
\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
, its dual,
\mathfrak{M}^{D}
, is the subspace of all
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}:

\forall\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{M}:\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}\mathfrak{f}(x)\cdot\mathfrak{g}(x)=0;

•

For a subset
S
of
\mathcal{P}
, its indicator function
\mathfrak{i}_{S}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
is given by

\mathfrak{i}_{S}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{F}:p\mapsto\mathfrak{i}_{S}(p)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }p\in S\\
0&\text{ if }p\not\in S\end{cases};

•

For
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
, the support of
\mathfrak{f}
, denoted as
\text{supp}(\mathfrak{f})
, is the set
\{x\in\mathcal{P}\mid\mathfrak{f}(x)\neq 0\}
;

•

The weight of a vector
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
is the size of
\text{supp}(\mathfrak{f})
.

To simplify notation, given a line
L
, we write
\mathfrak{i}_{L}
for
\mathfrak{i}_{\Gamma_{1}(L)}
where
\Gamma_{1}(L)
is the set of points incident with the line
L
(see also Lemma
5
). In other words, we identify a line with its point set.

Definition 4
.

The
\mathbb{F}
-ary code of the generalised polygon, denoted by
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
, is the vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
generated by
\{\mathfrak{i}_{L}\mid L\in\mathcal{L}\}
. The dual code, denoted by
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}^{D}(\Gamma)
is the dual of this subspace (as defined in Definition
3
).

The following results about
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
and
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}^{D}(\Gamma)
are known.

Theorem 3
.

(Bagchi & Sastry
[
2
]
)
Let
\Gamma
be a finite thick generalised
2m
-gon of order
(s,t)
with
m\geq 2
. Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field. Then:

1.

The minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}^{D}(\Gamma)
is at least
2(t^{m}-1)(t-1)^{-1}
.
If
\Gamma
is
regular
, then equality holds.

2.

The minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is at most
s+1
. If
\Gamma
is
regular
, then equality holds and any word of weight
s+1
is of the form
\lambda\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}
for some
0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
and some line
L
.

A weak generalised
2m
-gon of order
(s,t)
is called
regular
if every pair of opposite points is contained in a (necessarily unique)
(1,t)
- subpolygon.

The following finite thick generalised
2m
-gons are regular:

•

The generalised quadrangles
W(q)
, with
q
a prime power; these have order
(q,q)
.

•

The generalised quadrangles
H(3,q^{2})
, with
q
a prime power; these have order
(q^{2},q)
.

•

The split Cayley hexagons
\mathsf{H}(q)
, with
q
a prime power; these have order
(q,q)
.

•

The twisted triality hexagons
\mathsf{T}(q^{3},q)
, with
q
a prime power; these have order
(q^{3},q).

•

The Ree-Tits octagons
\mathsf{O}(q,q^{2})
, with
q
an odd power of two; these have order
(q,q^{2}).

Unless
\mathsf{H}(q)
is self dual (which happens if and only if
q
is a power of
3
), its dual is not regular. Likewise, the dual twisted triality hexagon is not regular.

The fact that the minimum weight of the code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
where
\Gamma
has order
(s,t)
is at most
s+1
is easily seen by the fact that the incidence vector of a line is contained in the code and has weight
s+1
.
The proof that equality holds as well as the characterisation result of Theorem
3
(ii) in the regular case rely entirely on the existence of subpolygons of order
(1,t)
; these are shown to define code words in the dual code. A relatively easy argument then shows that a code word in the code of weight
s+1
which needs to be orthogonal to all those subpolygon code words, corresponds to a line
[
2
, Lemma 2.5]
. Without the presence of subpolygons, the situation becomes a lot more difficult.
As mentioned before, in this paper, we will show that, under certain conditions, the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is still
s+1
even if
\Gamma
is not regular and we will also describe the supports of code words with this minimum weight (see Theorem
7
).

1.2.3
Thick generalised
3
-gons and embeddable weak generalised polygons

A thick generalised
n
-gon with
n=3
is a projective plane. The classical example of a finite projective plane is the Desarguesian projective plane
\text{PG}(2,q)
but many other non-Desarguesian planes are known. The study of their associated codes has been the source of great research interest, see
[
1
, Chapter 6]
. The following result regarding the codes of projective planes is well known. We also include the result for the code arising from the geometry of points and lines in a higher dimensional projective space.

Theorem 4
.

(Assmus & Key
[
1
, Theorem 6.3.1 and Corollary 5.7.5]
)
Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
p
prime and let
\Gamma
be the geometry of points and lines in
\Pi
, where
\Pi
is an arbitrary projective plane whose order
q
is a multiple of
p
, or
\Pi
is the projective geometry
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
,
q=p^{h}
. Then the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is
q+1
and the code words of minimum weight are scalar multiples of incidence vectors of lines.

Remark 4
.

It is important to remark that the code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma=\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
is
trivial
when
\mathbb{F}
is a finite field whose characteristic is different from
p
(see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
); in this context, trivial means that the code is either the full space or the dual of the all-one vector. Note that the minimum distance of a trivial code is
1
or
2
respectively, which is strictly smaller than
q+1
, the weight of the incidence vector of a line.

We say that a point-line geometry
\Gamma=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\mathtt{I})
is
fully embedded
in another point-line geometry
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathtt{I}^{\prime})
if

\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
,
and for all lines
L
in
\mathcal{L}
all points of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
on
L
are in
\mathcal{P}
.
The following statement now easily follows from Theorem
4
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a weak generalised
n
-gon which is fully embedded in
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
and let
\mathbb{F}
be the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
where
p
is a divisor of
q
. Then the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is
q+1
.

Proof.

The code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is a subspace of the code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the geometry of points and lines from
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
. It follows from Theorem
4
that
q+1
is a lower bound for the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
, and the incidence vector of any line of
\Gamma
shows equality.
∎

Remark 5
.

Corollary
3
has two strong requirements which make its use rather limited: not all weak generalised polygons are embeddable in a projective space
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
, and even for those who are embeddable, Corollary
3
doesn’t say anything about the case where
\mathbb{F}
is different from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It can be seen from Theorem
3
that it is not because the code
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{PG}(k,q))
is trivial for
\mathbb{F}\neq\mathbb{F}_{p}
that its subcode
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is trivial (if it would, the minimum weight would be at most
2
). Furthermore, one should also not deduce from Theorem
4
that all minimum-weight vectors of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
necessarily correspond to lines of
\Gamma
; it only follows that the minimum weight vectors correspond to point sets arising from a line of the ambient projective space.

1.2.4
Blocking sets in projective space

A
blocking set
with respect to lines in a projective plane is a set of points such that every line contains at least one point of
B
. The study of blocking sets in projective planes dates back to the early 1950s
[
12
]
and the study of blocking sets in projective spaces has attracted a lot of attention in finite geometry (see e.g.
[
3
]
).

The fundamental Bose-Burton theorem can be phrased as follows:

Theorem 5
.

(Bose & Burton
[
4
]
)
A set
B
of points of
\mathrm{PG}(k,q)
blocking all hyperplanes has at least the size of a line, and equality holds if and only if the set
B
is the point set of a line.

Theorem
5
provides a combinatorial characterisation of a line: (the point set of) a line blocks the set
\mathcal{H}
of all hyperplanes, and this theorem shows that all
\mathcal{H}
-blocking sets of this size are given by lines.
It is a natural question to consider the same problem for other geometries. We consider (the point set of) a line, find out which set
\mathcal{X}
of structures is blocked by this point set, and then determine whether or not the only possible
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets of this size are given by lines.
We will see in Theorem
6
that the answer to the latter question for generalised
2m
-gons is, in general, not necessarily yes, contrasting the case of thick generalised
3
-gons.

2
Blocking sets in weak generalised polygons

We have seen in Theorem
4
(and Remark
4
) that the problem of finding the minimum weight of the code of generalised
3
-gons has been settled, so from now on we will let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\mathtt{I})
denote a weak generalised
2m
-gon of order
(s,t)
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}\geq 2
,
s,t\geq 1
.

Remark 6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
k
be a natural number. We see that

k\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}}:=1_{\mathbb{F}}+1_{\mathbb{F}}+\dots+1_{\mathbb{F}}\text{ ($k$ times)}
and
-k\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}}:=-(k\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}})
are elements of
\mathbb{F}
. This allows us to evaluate any integer in
\mathbb{F}
and, with some abuse of notation, use integers as parameters in
\mathbb{F}
. Note however, that it is possible for this parameter to evaluate to
0
in
\mathbb{F}
.

Finally, since we will only be concerned with the code of the
2m
-gon
\Gamma=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\mathtt{I})
, we will omit
\Gamma
and use
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
for
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
, the vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
generated by
\{\mathfrak{i}_{L}\mid L\in\mathcal{L}\}
.

2.1
Some elementary geometric properties

Definition 5
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\mathtt{I})
be a point-line geometry and let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
. We define the following sets:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{L}:\delta(x,y)=i\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\leq i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{L}:\delta(x,y)\leq i\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{P}:\delta(x,y)=i\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\leq i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{P}:\delta(x,y)\leq i\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{L}:\delta(x,y)=i\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\leq i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\mathcal{L}:\delta(x,y)\leq i\},

where
\delta(x,y)
is the distance between
x
and
y
(inherited from the incidence graph).

Note that if
i\leq 0
, the sets in Definition
5
are still defined, but are empty. The following easily follows from the definition.

Corollary 4
.

1.

For a point
x\in\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{P}_{i}(x)=\emptyset
if
i
is odd, and
\mathcal{P}_{i}(x)=\Gamma_{i}(x)
if
i
is even.

2.

For a line
L\in\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{P}_{i}(L)=\Gamma_{i}(L)
if
i
is odd and
\mathcal{P}_{i}(L)=\emptyset
if
i
is even.

We will now investigate the possible intersection of some sets introduced in Definition
5
. In general, we have the following:

Lemma 1
.

Let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
. Let
y_{1},y_{2}
be elements with
\delta(x,y_{1})=\delta(x,y_{2})=d_{1}
and
\delta(y_{1},y_{2})=2d_{1}
with
d_{1}\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
.

Let
d_{2}\in\{1,\dots,2m-d_{1}-1\}
.
Then,

\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}-d_{1}}(x)=\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{2}).

Proof.

We clearly have that

\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}-d_{1}}(x)\subseteq\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{2}).

Now assume that there is an element
z
in
\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}}(y_{2})
with
z\not\in

\Gamma_{\leq d_{2}-d_{1}}(x)
. Then, combining the paths from
z
to
y_{1}
, from
y_{1}
to
y_{2}
and from
y_{2}
back to
z
, we find a cycle of length at most
d_{2}+2d_{1}+d_{2}\leq 4m-2d_{1}-2+2d_{1}=4m-2.
This contradicts Theorem
1
which states that the incidence graph of a
2m
-gon has girth
4m
.
∎

As a corollary, we determine what happens in some specific cases.

Corollary 5
.

1.

Let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
. Let
y_{1}\neq y_{2}\in\Gamma_{1}(x)
and let
d\in\{1,\dots,2m-2\}
.
Then,

\Gamma_{\leq d-1}(x)=\Gamma_{\leq d}(y_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq d}(y_{2}).

2.

Let
L
be a line of
\Gamma
and let
r\in\{1,2,\dots,m-1\}
.

(a)

If

M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are two disjoint lines intersecting
L
,
then

\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r-3}(L)=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r-1}(M_{1})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r-1}(M_{2}).

(b)

If

v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are two distinct points on
L
, then

\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r-1}(L)=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r}(v_{1})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2r}(v_{2}).

Proof.

1.

This follows from Lemma
1
with
d_{1}=1
.

2.

(a)

We have that
\delta(L,M_{1})=\delta(L,M_{2})=2
and
\delta(M_{1},M_{2})=4
. Lemma
1
with
d_{1}=2
and
d_{2}=2r-1
shows that

\Gamma_{\leq 2r-3}(L)=\Gamma_{\leq 2r-1}(M_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq 2r-1}(M_{2}).

Restricting these sets to their intersection with
\mathcal{P}
yields the desired result.

(b)

This follows from noting that
\delta(v_{1},v_{2})
is even and restricting the equality found in part 1 to the point set
\mathcal{P}
.

∎

The following lemma will be used in Subsection
2.2
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
C
be a set of points of
\Gamma
with
|C|<s+1
. Let
d\in\{1,2,\dots,m-1\}
.

1.

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists M\in\mathcal{L}_{\leq 2d-2}(L):C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)
.

2.

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(L):C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)
.

Proof.

1.

We prove this by induction on
d
. The base case
d=1
follows by taking
M=L
.
Assume that the statement holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k<m-1
and consider the case
d=k+1
.
Let
L\in\mathcal{L}
be an arbitrary line of
\Gamma
. Then there exists an
M\in\mathcal{L}_{\leq 2k-2}(L)
such that
C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2k-1}(M)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)
.

We label the
s+1
points on
M
as
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{s+1}\}
. For each of these points
v_{i}
we can consider a line
N_{i}
through
v_{i}
and different from
M
. Note that the lines
N_{1},\ldots,N_{s+1}
are mutually disjoint.

By Corollary
5
(2a), we now observe that for any two of these lines
N_{i}
and
N_{j}
with
i\neq j
, we have

\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-1}(N_{i})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-1}(N_{j})=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-3}(M).

Since
|C|<s+1
, at least one of the
s+1
disjoint sets
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-1}(N_{i})\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-3}(M)
,
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,s+1\}
, must have an empty intersection with
C
. Without loss of generality, we may assume

(\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-1}(N_{1})\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-3}(M))\cap C=\emptyset.

This means that

C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-1}(N_{1})=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2(k+1)-3}(M)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L),

where we have invoked the induction hypothesis.

Since
M\in\mathcal{L}_{2k-2}(L)
and
N_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(M)
, we see that
N_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{2k-2+2}(L)=\mathcal{L}_{2k}(L)=\mathcal{L}_{2(k+1)-2}(L)
. The statement follows.

2.

Let
L\in\mathcal{L}
be an arbitrary line of
\Gamma
.
From part (i), we know that there exists a line
M\in\mathcal{L}_{\leq 2d-2}(L)
such that
C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)
.

We label the
s+1
points on
M
as
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{s+1}\}
.

By Corollary
5
(2b), we have that for
i\neq j
,

\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v_{j})=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M).

Since
|C|<s+1
points, at least one of the disjoint sets
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v_{i})\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M)
must have an empty intersection with
C
. Without loss of generality, we may assume

(\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v_{1})\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M))\cap C=\emptyset.

This means that

C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d}(v_{1})=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(M)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)

Since
v_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
and
M\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\leq 2d-2}(L)
, we see that
v_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2d-1}(L)
and the statement follows.∎

2.2
Lines as
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets in weak generalised polygons

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set whose elements are subsets of points of a weak generalised polygon
\Gamma
. An
\mathcal{S}
-blocking set
in
\Gamma
is a set of points
B
such that for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
,
B\cap S\neq\emptyset.
Using this notation, we see that a blocking set with respect to lines in
\Gamma
is a
\mathcal{T}
-blocking set where
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)\mid L\in\mathcal{L}\}
.

From now on, let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{P}\}
.
The following lemma shows that (the point set of) a line
L
is an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set.

Lemma 3
.

Let
v
be a point of
\Gamma
. Then, every line
L
intersects
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
in either
1
or
s+1
points.

Proof.

By Corollary
2
,
L
contains a unique point
w
closest to
v
and we know that
\delta(v,w)\in\{0,2,\dots,2m-2\}
.
If
\delta(v,w)<2m-2
then clearly all points on
L
are in
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
. If
\delta(v,w)=2m-2
then all points on
L
different from
w
are at distance
2m
from
v
and therefore not in
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
.
∎

The following lemma derives a lower bound on the size of a blocking set with respect to lines in
\Gamma
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
B
be a set of points of
\Gamma
such that

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:|\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)\cap B|\geq 1,

then
|B|\geq\frac{s^{m}t^{m}-1}{st-1}
.

Proof.

We double count the following set:

X:=\{(v,L)\mid v\in B,L\in\mathcal{L},v\mathtt{I}L\}.

Each point in
B
lies on
t+1
lines so we find
|X|=|B|(t+1)
.
Because there are
|\mathcal{L}|=(1+t)\frac{s^{m}t^{m}-1}{st-1}
lines (see for instance
[
13
]
), each of which needs to contain at least one point of
B
we find
|X|\geq(1+t)\frac{s^{m}t^{m}-1}{st-1}
.
Combining these results gives us
|B|\geq\frac{s^{m}t^{m}-1}{st-1}
.
∎

We have seen that lines are
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets where
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{P}\}
; we now show that
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets have at least the size of a line.

Lemma 5
.

Let
C
be an
\mathcal{X}-
blocking set,
then
|C|\geq s+1
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
|C|<s+1
.
Lemma
2
(ii) with
d=m-1
tells us that

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists x\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(x)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L).

Since
\forall x
,
C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(x)\neq\emptyset
, we deduce that

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)\neq\emptyset.

Using Lemma
4
, we find that
|C|\geq\frac{s^{m}t^{m}-1}{st-1}\geq s+1
, a contradiction.
∎

2.3
Characterising
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets

The goal of this subsection is to prove Theorem
6
, which characterises
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets of minimum size, where, as before,
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{P}\}
. We will prove this using a series of lemmas.

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
s\leq t
. Let
C
be a set of points of size
s+1
and let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
. Let
d\in\{1,\dots,2m-2\}
.
If
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)\cap C=\emptyset
and

\exists y\in\Gamma_{1}(x):|\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(y)\cap C|\geq 2

then

\exists z\in\Gamma_{1}(x):\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(z)\cap C=\emptyset.

Proof.

Let
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
be two different elements in
\Gamma_{1}(x)
.
By Corollary
4
and
5
(1),

\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(z_{1})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(z_{2})\cap C=\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)\cap C=\emptyset.

Assume to the contrary that

\forall z\in\Gamma_{1}(x):|\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(z)\cap C|>0,

then we find that
|C|\geq|\Gamma_{1}(x)\setminus\{y\}|+2
.

There are either
s
(when
x
is a line) or
t
(when
x
is a point) elements in
\Gamma_{1}(x),
different from
y
.
Hence, since
s\leq t
there are at least
s
elements in
\Gamma_{1}(x)
different from
y
.
We find
|C|\geq|\Gamma_{1}(x)\setminus\{y\}|+2\geq s+2
, a contradiction since
|C|=s+1
.
∎

Note that in the following lemma, we use
\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)
and
\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)
and not
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(x)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(y)
.

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
s\leq t
. Let
C
be an
\mathcal{X}-
blocking set.
Let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
such that for some
d\in\{1,\dots,2m-2\}
:

•

\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)
contains at least two points of
C
.

•

\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)
contains no points of
C
.

Then,
C
is a
\mathcal{Y}-
blocking set where
\mathcal{Y}=\{\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\mid y\in\Gamma_{1}(x)\}.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there is an element
y
in
\Gamma_{1}(x)
with

\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\cap C=\emptyset.

We prove the following claim by induction on
d^{\prime}
:

\forall d^{\prime}\in\{d-1,\dots,2m-2\}:\exists z\in\Gamma_{\leq d^{\prime}-d+1}(y):\mathcal{P}_{\leq d^{\prime}}(z)\cap C=\emptyset.

The base case
d^{\prime}=d-1
follows by considering the point
y
: since
\Gamma_{\leq d-2}(y)
is a subset of
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)
, the statement follows from
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)\cap C=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\cap C=\emptyset
.

Now assume that the statement holds for
d^{\prime}=k
with
d-1\leq k<2m-2
.
We find an element
z_{k}
in
\Gamma_{\leq k-d+1}(y)
such that

\mathcal{P}_{\leq k}(z_{k})\cap C=\emptyset.

Since
z_{k}\in\Gamma_{\leq k-d+1}(y)
we also get
y\in\Gamma_{\leq k-d+1}(z_{k})
.
Hence, there is an element
y^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{1}(z_{k})
such that
y\in\Gamma_{\leq k-d}(y^{\prime})
. This implies that

\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\leq d+1}(y)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\leq k+1}(y^{\prime}).

Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{\leq k+1}(y^{\prime})
contains at least two points of
C
.
We are now ready to use Lemma
6
with
x=z_{k}
and
y=y^{\prime}
. This implies the existence of an element
z_{k+1}
in
\Gamma_{1}(z_{k})\subseteq\Gamma_{\leq k-d+2}(y)
such that

\mathcal{P}_{\leq k+1}(z_{k+1})\cap C=\emptyset.

This proves our claim.
By setting
d^{\prime}=2m-2
, we now find a contradiction with the assumption that
C
is an
\mathcal{X}-
blocking set.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Assume that
s\leq t
.
Let
C
be a set of points of size
s+1
.
Let
x
be an element of
\Gamma
such that for some
d\in\{1,\dots,2m-2\}
the following hold:

•

\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-2}(x)
contains no points of
C
.

•

C
is a
\mathcal{Y}
- blocking set where
\mathcal{Y}=\{\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\mid y\in\Gamma_{1}(x)\}.

Then,
C\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)
and if
x
is a point, then
s=t
.

Proof.

Corollary
5
(1) shows that for any two elements
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
\Gamma_{1}(x)

\Gamma_{\leq d-1}(y_{1})\cap\Gamma_{\leq d-1}(y_{2})=\Gamma_{\leq d-2}(x).

This implies that

\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y_{1})\cap\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-2}(x).

Since
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-2}\cap C=\emptyset
we see that for every
y\in\Gamma_{1}(x),
the set
\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\setminus\mathcal{P}_{d-2}(x)
contains a different point of
C
, and the points obtained in this way are mutually distinct. We find that
s+1=|C|\geq|\Gamma_{1}(x)|
. If
x
is a point,
|\Gamma_{1}(x)|=t+1
, and by our assumption,
t\geq s
, so it follows that in this case
s=t
.
Furthermore, it follows that in both the case where
x
a point or
x
is a line, each of the sets
\mathcal{P}_{d-1}(y)\setminus\mathcal{P}_{d-2}(x)
contains exactly one point of
C
, and
C
does not contain any further points. It follows that
C\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(x)
, and since
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-2}(x)\cap C=\emptyset
,
C\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)
.
∎

Definition 6
.

A distance
d
-trace
T_{d,x,y}
, where
d\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, is a non-empty set of points such that there exist opposite elements
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{L}
with

T_{d,x,y}=\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)\cap\mathcal{P}_{2m-d}(y).

If we do not want to specify the distance
d
, we also call a distance
d
-trace a
distance trace
.

Remark 7
.

Note that
x
and
y
play a different role in the definition so unless
d=m
, the distance
d
-trace determined by
x
and
y
is different from the distance
d
-trace determined by
y
and
x
.

Remark 8
.

The point set of a line is an example of a distance trace: take
d=1
,
x=L
and
M
any line opposite to
L
, then
T_{1,L,M}=\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)\cap\mathcal{P}_{2m-1}(M)=\mathcal{P}_{1}(L).

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
s\leq t
.
Let
C
be an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set. Then
|C|\geq s+1
. If equality holds then
C
is a distance
d
-trace
T_{d,x,y}
for some opposite elements
x,y
of
\Gamma
and some
d\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Furthermore, if equality holds and
s<t
, then
d
is odd.

Proof.

Let
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
be two points of
C
such that
\delta(z_{1},z_{2})\leq\delta(u,v)
for all
u\neq v
points of
C
.

By the axioms of the generalised
2m
-gon
\Gamma
, there exists an ordinary
2m
-gon
\Gamma^{\prime}
containing
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. Since
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are both points, the distance between them is even, and we can find opposite elements
x
and
y
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that:

•

z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)

•

z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{2m-d}(y)

for
d=\frac{\delta(z_{1},z_{2})}{2}
.

If
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d-1}(x)
would contain a point of
C
, then
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
would not have been at the minimal distance. We can therefore use Lemma
7
and Lemma
8
to conclude that all points of
C
are contained in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)
.
Since
x
and
y
are opposite,
\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}(x)\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-d-1}(y)=\emptyset
and we get that
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-d-1}(y)
can not contain any point of
C
. Again, from lemma
7
and lemma
8
, we can conclude that
C\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{2m-d}(y)
.
We now have
C\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{d}(x)\cap\mathcal{P}_{2m-d}(y)
proving the statement.
∎

Remark 9
.

As seen before, when
d=1
, we consider opposite lines
x,y
, and
T_{1,x,y}
is the set of points on the line
x
.
In the case of weak generalised quadrangles, the distance
2
-trace is the set of points collinear to two non-collinear points. When the generalised quadrangle is
W(3,q)
, the distance
2
-trace is given by the points of a line of the ambient projective space (see also Remark
11
), and in the case of
\mathcal{Q}(4,q)
, this set is the point set of a conic in the ambient space. For the generalised hexagon
\mathsf{H}(q)
a similar situation arises. It is well-known that there is an embedding of
\mathsf{H}(q)
in
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
, which is a polar space embedded in
\mathrm{PG}(6,q)
.
This embedding has the property that for two points
v,w
in
\mathsf{H}(q)
,
\delta(v,w)=4
if and only if
v
and
w
are collinear in
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
but not in
\mathsf{H}(q)
.
This allows us to describe the distance
d
-traces in
\mathsf{H}(q)
geometrically: when
d=2
, we consider opposite points
x,y
. We find that
T_{2,x,y}
is the set of
q+1
points on a line of
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
that is not a line of
\mathsf{H}(q)
. The set of points
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 4}(v)
is contained in the hyperplane
v^{\perp}
, where
\perp
denotes the polarity corresponding to
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
. The line
T_{2,x,y}
and the hyperplane
v^{\perp}
in
\mathrm{PG}(6,q)
always intersect in at least a point, which is contained in
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
, and hence, in
\mathsf{H}(q)
. We deduce that in this case,
T_{2,x,y}
forms an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set.
When
d=3
, we consider again opposite lines
x,y
. The set of points at distance
3
from both
x
and
y
is the set of
q+1
points of
x^{\perp}\cap y^{\perp}
, where
\perp
denotes the polarity corresponding to
\mathcal{Q}(6,q)
. These
q+1
points form a conic in
\mathrm{PG}(6,q)
.

Remark 10
.

The complement of the set
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
is precisely the set of points at distance
2m
of
v
, the set of points opposite to
v
. It follows that an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set is a set of points
B
such that there is no point opposite all of the points of
B
.
This point of view can be used to derive similar results for other point-line incidence geometries and to introduce the related concept of a
geometric
line (see
[
9
]
): this is a set of points such that each point is not opposite all or one point of the set. Note though that the definition of an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set does not have fixed intersection sizes.

2.4
Regularity and distance traces

We observed in Theorem
6
that if
C
is an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set, then
C
is a distance trace. The natural question arises whether every distance trace is an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set. We have seen that for point sets of lines (which are examples of distance traces), this is the case. Similarly, in remark
9
we gave an example in
\mathsf{H}(q)
. We will now show more generally that if
\Gamma
satisfies a certain regularity condition, the distance
2
-traces can form
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets.

We need the definition of the perp geometry (see also
[
13
, 1.9.2]
).

Definition 7
.

Let
x
be a point of
\Gamma
. The perp-geometry of a point
x
in
\Gamma
is the point-line geometry
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathtt{I}^{\prime})
with

•

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}_{2}(x)
,

•

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{1}(x)\cup\{T_{2,x,y}\mid y\in\mathcal{P},y\text{ is opposite }x\}
,

•

\mathtt{I}^{\prime}
defined naturally from
\mathtt{I}
and
\in
.

A point
x
is called
projective
if the perp-geometry at
x
determines a projective plane.

Lemma 9
.

Let
x
be a projective point of
\Gamma
, let
y
be a point which is opposite to
x
and let
T=T_{2,x,y}
be the distance
2
-trace determined by
x
and
y
. Then
T
is an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime},I^{\prime})
be the projective plane which is the perp-geometry determined by the projective point
x
. We need to show that for all
v\in\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\cap T_{2,x,y}\neq\emptyset
. If
v
is opposite
x
, then
T_{2,x,v}
and
T_{2,x,y}
are lines of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a projective plane, these two lines have a point
w
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in common. Since
w
is a point of
T_{2,x,v}
, it follows that
w\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\cap T_{2,x,y}.

If
v
and
x
are not opposite,
\delta(v,x)\leq 2m-2
. The second point of a shortest path from
x
to
v
, say
r
, is contained in one of the lines through
x
, say
L
. We have that
r
is contained in
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-4}(v)
, and since
L
is a line of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, it meets
T_{2,x,y}
, which is a line of
\Gamma^{\prime}
too, in a point
s
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
\delta(r,s)=2
, and
r\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-4}(v)
it follows that
s\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)\cap T_{2,x,y}.

We conclude that
T_{2,x,y}
is an
\mathcal{X}-
blocking set.
∎

Remark 11
.

For certain families of weak generalised polygons, the existence of projective points is very easy to deduce. Consider for example the generalised quadrangle
W(3,q)
which is embedded in
\mathrm{PG}(3,q)
, let
x
be a point of
W(3,q)
and let
\perp
denote the associated polarity. The perp-geometry of
x
is simply the point-line geometry of the projective plane
\pi=x^{\perp}
: the sets
T_{2,x,y}
are given by the lines obtained as intersection of the planes
x^{\perp}
and
y^{\perp}
in
\mathrm{PG}(3,q)
. Hence, every point of
W(3,q)
is a projective point.
It is also not too hard to show that the points of
\mathsf{H}(q)
are projective, but many interesting questions related to the existence of projective points and characterisations of weak generalised polygons based on the existence of such points are wide open.

3
Codes from weak generalised polygons

The methods used in the proofs of this Section can be traced back to the proof of the characterisation of the code words of minimum weight in the code of points and lines in a projective plane. In particular, as is done in
[
1
, Theorem 6.3.1]
we will also investigate those vectors that have a constant intersection with the incidence vectors of lines (see Lemma
10
).

3.1
A weighted incidence vector

The vector space
\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
admits the natural product operation

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}\times\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{F}:(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})\to\langle\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\rangle:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{P}}f(x)g(x),

which is clearly bilinear, symmetric. Using
\mathfrak{f}\cdot\mathfrak{g}
for the entry-wise product, we see that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfies the property that

\forall\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{f}\cdot\mathfrak{g},\mathbf{1}\rangle,

where
\mathbf{1}
is the all-one vector.

Furthermore, we see that for subsets
X,Y
of
\mathcal{P}
, we have

\text{supp}(\mathfrak{i}_{X})=X
,

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{\text{supp}(\mathfrak{f})}

and
\mathfrak{i}_{X}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{Y}=\mathfrak{i}_{X\cap Y}
.

For every point
v
of
\Gamma
, we will now define a particular vector
\mathfrak{c}_{v}
.

Definition 8
.

Let
v
be a point of
\Gamma
.

We can define the following element of
\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
determined by
v
:

\mathfrak{c}_{v}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-k-1}(-s)^{j}\right)\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{2k}(v)}.

The support of this vector is contained in
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
.

Remark 12
.

We see that in a specific setting, such as a weak generalised hexagon, the expression from the previous definition becomes:

\mathfrak{c}_{v}:=(s^{2}-s+1)\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{0}(v)}+(-s+1)\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{2}(v)}+\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{4}(v)}.

Recall that the value
s^{2}-s+1
or
-s+1
could evaluate to
0
over
\mathbb{F}
(see Remark
6
), but if this does not happen, the support of
\mathfrak{c}_{v}
is precisely
\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)

Lemma 10
.

For all points
v
and all lines
L
in
\Gamma
we have

\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle=1
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a line of
\Gamma
. By Corollary
2
, there exists a unique point
w
on
L
closest to
v
.
Let
d=\delta(v,w)
. Observe that
d
is even, that
d\leq 2m-2
, and that

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{z\in\mathcal{P}\mid z\mathtt{I}L\}}\mathfrak{c}_{v}(z)

If
d=2m-2
then we find

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{c}_{v}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{0}(-s)^{j}

\displaystyle=1.

If
d<2m-2
then

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1-\frac{d}{2}}(-s)^{j}\right)+s\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1-\frac{d}{2}-1}(-s)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1-\frac{d}{2}}(-s)^{j}\right)+1-\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-2-\frac{d}{2}}(-s)^{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1-\frac{d}{2}}(-s)^{j}\right)+1-\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m-1-\frac{d}{2}}(-s)^{j^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=1.

∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
v
and
w
be two points of
\Gamma
.
Then,
\mathfrak{c}_{v}-\mathfrak{c}_{w}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}^{D}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a line. Using Lemma
10
we see that

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v}-\mathfrak{c}_{w},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle-\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w},\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle=1-1=0.

Since
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
is generated by the vectors
\mathfrak{i}_{L}
, where
L\in\mathcal{L}
, the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
and let
C
be the support of
\mathfrak{c}
. If

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\mathcal{P}:L\text{ is covered by }\mathfrak{c}_{v}\text{ and }\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle=0

then
|C|>t+1
.

Proof.

Let
w
be a point of
C
and assume that
|C|\leq t+1
.
It follows that at least one of the
t+1
lines through
w
, say
M
, does not contain a second point of
C
. Our assumption implies that there exists a point
v\in\mathcal{P}
such that
|\text{supp}(\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{M})|=s+1\text{ and }\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{M}\rangle=0
. Since
\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{M}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{\{w\}}\rangle=\mathfrak{c}(w)\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}(w)
where both
\mathfrak{c}(w)
and
\mathfrak{c}_{x}(w)
are elements of
\mathbb{F}
different from
0
, this is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
and let
C
be the support of
\mathfrak{c}
.
If
|C|<s+1
then

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
(ii) with
d=m-1
we get:

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L).

Therefore,

\mathfrak{i}_{C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)}=\mathfrak{i}_{C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)}.

Which implies that

\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}.

∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{P}
and let
C
be the support of
\mathfrak{c}
. Assume that
|C|=s+1
and
let
L
be a line containing a point
v
of
C
. Then,

\exists w\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L},\mathfrak{c}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w},\mathfrak{c}\rangle.

Proof.

The set
C\setminus\{v\}
has size
s
, so we can invoke Lemma
2
with
d=m-1
to deduce that there exists a
w\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L)
such that

(C\setminus\{v\})\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(w)=(C\setminus\{v\})\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L).

Because
v\in C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(w)\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)
, we get

C\cap\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(w)=C\cap\mathcal{P}_{1}(L).

The statement now follows since

\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(w)}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C}=\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}(L)}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{C}=\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}.

∎

It will follow from the following lemma that code words of weight

in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
give rise to
\mathcal{X}
-blocking sets as defined in the Section

.

Lemma 16
.

Let
s\leq t
.
Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
and let
C
be the support of
\mathfrak{c}
. Assume that
|C|=s+1
and that

\forall v\in\mathcal{P}:\text{supp}(\mathfrak{c}_{v})=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)

Then,

\forall v\in\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{c}\rangle\neq 0.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a point
v\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\langle\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathfrak{c}\rangle=0
. By Corollary
6
, we then have

\forall w\in\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w},\mathfrak{c}\rangle=0.

Now let
v
be a point of
C
. Since we assume
s\leq t
, and
|C\setminus\{v\}|=s
, at least one of the lines through
v
does not contain a second point
C
. Let
L
be such a line. By Lemma
15
, we find that

\exists w\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L},\mathfrak{c}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c}_{w},\mathfrak{c}\rangle=0.

3.2
Proof of the main result

Theorem 7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite thick generalised
2m
-gon of order
(s,t)
with
s\leq t
and
m>1
.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field such that none of the
m-1
elements in

\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-s)^{j}\mid 1\leq k\leq m-1,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

are
0
over
\mathbb{F}
.
Then the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Gamma)
is
s+1
and the support of any minimum weight code word is a distance
d
-trace
T_{d,x,y}
for some
d\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
.

Furthermore, if
s<t
, then
d
is odd. In particular, if
\Gamma
is a thick generalised quadrangle of order
(s,t)
with
s<t
, then every minimum weight code word of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a scalar multiple of the incidence vector of a line.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
be a codeword with support
C
and assume to the contrary that
|C|<s+1
.

By Lemma
12
,

\exists v\in\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\rangle=0,

while Corollary
6
implies that

\forall v,w\in\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{w}\rangle.

We conclude that

\forall v\in\mathcal{P}:\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\rangle=0.

From Lemma
14
we get

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}=\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}.

Combining this, we find

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\exists v\in\Gamma_{\leq 2m-3}(L):\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v},\mathbf{1}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L},\mathbf{1}\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{i}_{L}\rangle=0.

The condition on
\mathbb{F}
implies that for all
v\in\mathcal{P}
,
supp(\mathfrak{c}_{v})=\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-2}(v)
. Since all points on a line that lies at distance at most
2m-3
from a point
v
lie at distance at most
2m-2
of
v
, we see that all lines at distance at most
2m-3
from
v
are covered by
\mathfrak{c}_{v}
i.e.:

\forall L\in\mathcal{L}:\forall v\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq 2m-3}(L):|\text{supp}(\mathfrak{i}_{L}\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{v})|=s+1.

Therefore, the conditions for Lemma
13
are fulfilled, which gives us
|C|>t+1
. This contradicts the assumptions that
|C|<s+1
and
s\leq t
.

Since for any line
L\in\mathcal{L}
, the codeword
\mathfrak{i}_{L}
has weight
s+1
, we have shown that the minimum weight of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
is
s+1
.

Now assume that
|C|=s+1
. The condition on
\mathbb{F}
shows that we can invoke Lemma
16
to find that
C
forms an
\mathcal{X}
-blocking set. Theorem
6
now shows that
C
is a distance trace
T_{d,x,y}
. Furthermore, if
s<t
, then
d
is odd. If additionally
\Gamma
is a weak generalised quadrangle, then
m=2
, so since
d\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
is odd, only the possibility
T_{1,x,y}
occurs: the support
C
is the point set of a line.
Now assume that
C
is the point set of a line
L
. Let
v
be a point of
L
. Consider the vector
\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{c}(v)\mathfrak{i}_{L}
. This is a linear combination of code words in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
, so it is a code word of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathbb{F}}
, but its weight is at most
s
. Therefore, it is the zero vector. It follows that
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}(v)\mathfrak{i}_{L}
.
∎

Remark 13

We could state Theorem
7
for finite weak generalised
2m
-gons, but the requirement
1-s\neq 0
, together with
s\leq t
forces the
2m
-gon to be thick since this implies that every point and every line is incident with at least three other elements (see Remark

Remark 14
.

The condition on the field
\mathbb{F}
in Theorem
7
boils down to the following: for finite thick generalised quadrangles, the condition
s\neq 1
in
\mathbb{F}
, is automatically fulfilled by being thick, for finite thick generalised hexagons, we have the additional requirement

in
\mathbb{F}
, and for finite thick generalised octagons we require

.
In particular, the classical case, where we study the
p
-ary code of a polygon embedded in
\mathrm{PG}(n,q)
,
q=p^{h}
,
p
prime, is included in the theorem since in that case,
s=q=0
in
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

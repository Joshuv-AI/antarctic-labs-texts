Coherent phonon transport in 2D layered metal organic frameworks
Authors: Riccardo Dettori, David Beljonne, Luciano Colombo, Claudio Melis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTwo-dimensional metal–organic frameworks (2D MOFs) offer tunable interlayer coupling and low lattice stiffness, making them a compelling system for exploring stacking-dependent heat transport. In this work, we present a full ab initio investigation of lattice dynamics and thermal transport in copper benzenehexathiolate (), focusing on three distinct stacking arrangements: AA, AB, and C. Our phonon calculations show that AB is dynamically unstable, whereas the C phase is the thermodynamic ground state, lower in energy than AA by meV per formula unit, and features covalent Cu–S interlayer bonds that stiffen interlayer modes and enhance through-plane transport. Using Boltzmann transport (BTE-RTA) together with the Wigner formalism, we find that coherent phonon contributions are essential to capture the temperature dependence: they significantly raise and reduce the classical scaling to with in both AA and C configurations, evidencing a wave-like transport channel activated by near-degenerate, hybridized modes. These results identify stacking-controlled interlayer connectivity as a design lever for directional heat management in 2D MOFs, with potential implications where low lattice thermal conductivity is desirable.Subject terms: Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Two-dimensional metal-organic frameworks (2D MOFs) represent a new frontier in materials science, merging the electronic properties of layered crystals with the tailorable chemistry of metal-ligand networks1,2. A key feature is their strong in-plane coordination networks, typically via transition metals (e.g., Cu, Ni, Co) linked by planar ligands with multiple coordination sites such as benzenehexathiol (BHT), hexaiminobenzene (HIB), or similar multifunctional aromatic linkers. The resulting architectures often exhibit structural and electronic similarities to graphene and transition metal dichalcogenides, but with chemically tunable frameworks and variable charge transport mechanisms that range from hopping to delocalized band conduction3–6. Unlike conventional 2D materials, 2D MOFs are intrinsically porous and chemically tunable. Their low density and high surface area make 2D MOFs promising candidates for a broad range of applications, including chemical sensing7, catalysis8, spintronics9, and energy storage1. More recently, they have also been proposed as efficient thermoelectric materials due to their tunable phonon and electronic transport properties10. The thermoelectric figure of merit, , reaches technology-relevant values only by combining high electrical conductivity (), a large Seebeck coefficient (S), and low thermal conductivity (). Achieving this balance remains a significant challenge in conventional materials. Several 2D MOFs exhibit appreciable electrical conductivity owing to extended conjugation and charge delocalization within their metal-ligand networks, while maintaining relatively large Seebeck coefficients due to low carrier densities and narrow bands near the Fermi level11,12. This intrinsic structural and electronic flexibility enables the decoupling of thermal and electronic transport, often characterized by the so-called ”phonon-glass, electron-crystal” behavior10,13. Thanks to this feature, a variety of 2D MOFs have demonstrated potential for thermoelectric applications. For instance, nickel benzenehexathiolate (Ni-BHT) exhibits moderate electrical conductivity (ranging from 50 to 160 S/cm, depending on porosity and doping) and is expected to possess relatively low lattice thermal conductivity due to the rigidity of the Ni-S bonds. In contrast, zinc benzenehexathiolate (Zn-BHT) behaves as an electrical insulator, lacking the delocalized d-orbitals required for -d conjugation, and thus serves as a helpful reference for isolating phonon-dominated transport effects14. Another notable example is Ni(HITP), a framework built from nitrogen-rich, planar HITP ligands. This material was among the first electrically conductive MOFs reported and features large in-plane pores and weak interlayer interactions. Although it displays ultralow thermal conductivity (approximately 0.2 W/mK), its thermoelectric performance is limited by a low Seebeck coefficient and reduced charge carrier mobility11. Among these systems, copper benzenehexathiolate () has recently emerged as a benchmark system for exploring charge transport, low-dimensional physics, and potential device applications. First synthesized as a conductive coordination polymer featuring extended conjugation through Cu-S bonding with a hexathiol benzene core, forms a kagome-like lattice with strong in-plane interactions and weak interlayer van der Waals forces, yielding a layered structure analogous to transition metal dichalcogenides9,14. Theoretical calculations for bulk explored both AA and AB stacking patterns9: since the potential energy surface for AA and AB stacking showed only a minor energy difference (approximately 65 meV per unit cell), it was initially guessed that existed as a mixture of AA and AB stacking configurations. 2D grazing incidence X-ray diffraction results effectively shown the formation of crystalline in an AA stacking arrangement (monoclinic, C2)15. This orientation, with the 2D planes parallel to the substrate, was found to be consistent with liquid-liquid interfacial synthesis15. However, recent microcrystal electron diffraction and high-resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy (HR-STEM) measurements revealed an ordered C stacking with covalent Cu-S interlayer bonds16. This unique feature distinguishes from other 2D materials, which are usually held together by weak van der Waals forces.

As for the electronic perspective, is characterized by a high room-temperature electrical conductivity (up to S/cm in single crystals)16 and superconducting behavior at low temperature17. Simultaneously, experimental measurements on polycrystalline films report ultralow lattice thermal conductivities (–0.6 W/mK)13,18, placing among the most thermally insulating crystalline solids known. Despite the increasing interest in , previous theoretical studies have focused primarily on its electronic band structure or on phonon dispersion of a single stacking model19,20. A comparative analysis of the vibrational, anharmonic, and thermal transport properties of different stacking arrangements, especially including the newly revealed C phase, has not yet been performed. Here, we close this gap with the first stacking-comparative, fully ab initio assessment of lattice thermal transport in that explicitly includes the coherent contribution, quantifies the stacking-controlled deviation from the classical 1/T law, and links interlayer Cu-S bonding to enhanced cross-plane . In conductive 2D MOFs, the electronic thermal conductivity is typically suppressed by the low carrier concentrations and narrow bandwidths associated with –d conjugated frameworks4,11,12, leaving the lattice contribution as the dominant channel for heat transport. This is particularly relevant for thermoelectric applications, where minimizing lattice thermal conductivity is key to enhancing the figure of merit. Moreover, recent evidence of stacking-dependent interlayer bonding in 16 suggests that phonon-mediated heat transport is strongly influenced by structural anisotropy and anharmonic effects, making the lattice thermal conductivity an essential quantity to investigate for understanding and controlling thermal transport in these systems. Because uniquely combines a kagome network, tunable interlayer covalency, and its ultralow , it serves as a stringent benchmark for testing phonon-transport theories, particularly those that must capture coherence and near-degenerate mode hybridization across different stacking configurations. A recent study by Un et al.18 has further highlighted the potential of for thermoelectric applications, showing that its electronic transport remains efficient even in the presence of defects, while phonon conduction is strongly suppressed by structural disorder. This contrasting behavior enables the coexistence of high electrical conductivity and ultralow lattice thermal conductivity, even in poorly ordered or partially crystalline films.

For the above reasons, in this work, we present a systematic ab initio investigation of the phonon dispersion relations, lattice thermal conductivity, and anharmonic features of in three distinct stacking configurations: AA, AB, and C. We demonstrate that the AB phase is dynamically unstable, whereas the C structure is both energetically favorable and dynamically stable. In addition to the standard picture of phonon-mediated heat transport dominated by anharmonic scattering events, we show that coherent phonon transport provides a substantial contribution to the overall thermal conductivity. Using the Wigner transport formalism21, we capture nonlocal interference effects that become particularly relevant in systems with low group velocities and strong phonon degeneracy. These transport channels prove to be especially important in low-dimensional materials such as .

All calculations were performed within the framework of Density Functional Theory (DFT), using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)24 under periodic boundary conditions. The AA phase is characterized by a pseudo-rhomboedral structure (a distorted lattice very close to an ideal rhombohedral/hexagonal lattice, but the proper crystallographic symmetry is lower, so the structure is formally triclinic). As previously proposed by ab initio calculations9, the AB structure exhibits hexagonal symmetry, whereas the C stacking is essentially monoclinic. All three models considered here keep the Cu-S kagome net at 8.66-8.70 Å, in excellent agreement with diffraction and HR-STEM values reported for well-ordered films and single crystals ( Å)16. The calculated lattice constants for the AA and AB are somewhat shorter than previous theoretical estimates9. Still, the overall structure is well reproduced. We remark that our calculations were performed using a functional and van der Waals corrections more accurate t﻿han those previously employed, along with a larger plane-wave cutoff. The out-of-plane lattice constant for the AA and AB structures matches the TEM measurements for multilayer stacks, consistent with weak inter-layer bonding when the axial Cu-S contacts are absent27. In contrast, the numbers for the C stacking phase are consistent with the experimentally determined stacking geometry, where alternating layers are laterally offset by a/2, leading to short Cu–S interlayer bonds of Å. These axial bonds underpin the non-van-der-Waals character previously observed in this configuration16. The interatomic distances reported for the three stacking configurations of (see SI) provide additional insight into the chemical bonding environments within each phase. The carbon–carbon bond lengths are slightly compressed in the C stacking but remain essentially unchanged among all stacking configurations, reflecting the rigidity and aromatic nature of the benzene rings in the BHT ligands. Similarly, slight variations in the carbon–sulfur distances indicate marginal structural adjustments within the ligands, confirming that intra-layer ligand integrity is mostly preserved irrespective of stacking. Both AA and C phases display identical Cu–S distances (2.26 Å). The slightly shorter Cu–S bond in the AB stacking may reflect minor structural distortion or strain within the layers, possibly due to poor interlayer alignment and subsequent structural relaxation aimed at minimizing the total energy.

From an energetic perspective, AA and AB stacking are very close in energy (see Tab. 1). At synthesis or device-operation temperatures, the stacking sequence is therefore entropy-driven and can be altered by subtle kinetic factors such as solvent, substrate, or redox environment27. However, the C phase is the most stable, with a total energy approximately 18 meV/fu (formula units) lower than the AA phase and over 30 meV/fu lower than the AB configuration. Axial Cu-S bonds provide an additional cohesion channel that lowers the total energy: the monoclinic distortion sacrifices hexagonal symmetry but optimizes Cu-S overlap, compensating for the minor elastic penalty associated with the doubled cell28. Experimental observations confirm that bulk indeed adopts the double-layer arrangement, lending strong support to the C-phase calculations. Thin films frequently show AA faults because of the shallow energetic hierarchy, but once the system is allowed to anneal and grow large crystals, the C motif dominates16. This near-degeneracy explains the observed stacking disorder in experimental samples, broadening (00l) reflections (plane parallel to the c axis) and contributing to the wide spread of reported conductivities ( S/cm). The presence or absence of the axial Cu-S bond alters interlayer bandwidths and phonon softening, factors that have been linked to the low- superconductivity and hyperbolic plasmon response of the material28.

Although the AB stacking is geometrically close to AA, we found it to be dynamically unstable, as shown by the presence of imaginary phonon frequencies (see the next Section). These combined structural and energetic results demonstrate that the C phase is not only the most tightly bonded but also the thermodynamically preferred form of stacked .

Phonon dispersion and dynamical stability

The phonon dispersion relations for the three stacking configurations are reported in Fig. 2a–c. The AA and C stackings exhibit real phonon frequencies across the entire Brillouin zone, confirming their dynamical stability. In contrast, the AB stacking shows one mode with imaginary frequencies (), particularly in the regions around and in the cross-plane direction -Z, which is a clear signature of dynamical instability. Since the three stackings retain the same nearly ideal kagome layer, their reciprocal lattice can be approximately considered as the hexagonal Brillouin zone that underlies a kagome net16. Plotting the phonon branches along the canonical line of this hexagon (+Z for the cross-plane direction) therefore lets us compare the three stackings on a common metric and highlight the zone-corner K point where kagome-specific features such as chiral or flat phonons reside29. Although the primitive Brillouin zones of the AA (triclinic) and C (monoclinic) cells are formally lower-symmetry, their minor distortions fold more branches onto the hexagonal path, while symmetry breaking leads to branch splitting or the removal of degeneracies. Finally, the chosen path follows the widely used Setyawan-Curtarolo convention for hexagonal lattices30, ensuring full reproducibility across electronic structure codes.

Low optic modes in AA structure correspond to out-of-plane layer breathing and shearing modes, often referred to as “quasi-acoustic” modes31–34 because of their strong similarities with the corresponding acoustic modes and have been identified in numerous layered and 2D materials, essentially involving the weak vdW interactions between layers. Their nearly flat profile reflects the lack of inter-layer coupling. The out-of-plane breathing mode was also found at 22-30 cm in single-layer calculations and has been linked to the enhanced electron-phonon coupling that raises the superconducting in the monolayer. These modes split into in-phase/out-of-phase pairs (zone folding). For the C stacking, the breathing mode now sits at cm, a three-fold hardening relative to AA, quantifying the extra stiffness provided by the short 2.4 Å Cu-S inter-layer bonds16.

Modes in the mid-frequency band (150-300 cm) arise mainly from Cu-S-C bending inside the kagome plane. AA and AB show very similar dispersions here, confirming that the in-plane lattice (Cu-S kagome) is barely affected by the stacking. In C, many of these branches split and flatten, giving an optical-like plateau around 200 cm. Zone-folding, combined with weak interlayer hybridization, leads to a dense band manifold that is visible as the “red spaghetti”. All three phases share nearly dispersion-free branches in the high frequency region ( cm) that correspond to C-S stretching within the BHT ligand, in excellent agreement with the sharp Raman line at 386 cm reported for well-crystallized single crystals16.

The imaginary branches reported in the AB is a rigid-slide shear of one layer over the other. Such unstable shear modes can occur when purely dispersive forces hold the layers together; they disappear if the cell is allowed to relax into the monoclinic arrangement. To better understand the behavior of the AB phase, we performed anharmonic renormalization by extracting temperature-dependent force constants from ab-initio molecular dynamics as implemented by Tadano et al.35 in the alamode package. Figure 2d reports phonon dispersion relations as a function of temperature: the rigid inter-layer shear/breathing of one kagome sheet against the next is characterized by negative frequencies around and along even after renormalization up to K. Moderate softening with temperature ( drops from to cm) are observed. Still, not enough to change the sign of the curvature, i.e. AB remains a saddle on the potential energy surface, that we schematically represent in Fig. 2e as a model double-well potential (the horizontal axis does not represent a quantitative coordinate but is used solely to distinguish the three stacking configurations (C, AB, and AA). In fact, we observed that if the AB converged structure is slightly displaced from the (metastable) equilibrium configuration, it either relaxes into the AA stacking arrangement (by shifting the two layers) or into the C phase by distorting the layers and bringing the planes closer to form the covalent bond. Vapour-solid CVD films grown by Ogle et al.27 often exhibit AB stacking directly after growth; however, we argue that the metastable AB configuration can be kinetically trapped at the growth front, where layer sliding is hindered. The 30 meV/fu barrier is comfortably larger than during deposition, yet small enough to be surmounted by thermal or mechanical agitation, following a mechanism very similar to the two-level system (TLS) in amorphous materials: small clusters of atoms can have two nearly equivalent local configurations separated by an energy barrier (often minimal) and a TLS can flip between its two configuration36. A similar situation is found in cubic ZrO, where a soft mode becomes real only above 2500 K after strong phonon-phonon renormalisation37. By contrast, bilayer graphene exhibits stable yet extremely soft shear modes that tune the stacking order without inducing any known metastable configuration34. However, single-crystal diffraction shows that bulk adopts the covalently bridged C motif, which erases any dynamical instability.

In terms of group velocities (Fig. 3d-g), both structures show high in-plane and low cross-plane velocities, reflecting the strong Cu-S covalency inside the kagome sheets and the comparatively soft inter-layer contacts. The C phase does not gain a faster (the out-of-plane components of the phonons group velocity), but extra Cu-S bridges manifest as more optic-like out-of-plane modes rather than steeper acoustic slopes. This suggest that in both cases (AA and C) the in-plane thermal conductivity might dominate compared to the out-of-plane component. An important quantity that helps understanding phonon transport is the three-phonon scattering phase space (SPS), which quantifies the number of allowed energy- and momentum-conserving three-phonon processes for each vibrational mode and aids in the interpretation of branch folding39,40. Figure 3h shows the frequency-resolved SPS for the AA (blue) and C (red) stacking configurations of . The C stacking exhibits a significantly higher SPS across the entire low- and mid-frequency range, especially for modes below 300 cm. Brillouin-zone folding plus lowered symmetry creates many additional energy-momentum-conserving triplets, resulting in additional scattering channels. In contrast, the AA stacking, with weaker interlayer coupling, shows a more limited scattering phase space. These results are consistent with the lower lattice thermal conductivity computed for the C phase, as higher SPS directly translates into more frequent phonon-phonon scattering events and stronger thermal resistance.

Coherent (Wigner) contributions and total thermal conductivity

In crystalline materials, phonon-mediated heat transport is traditionally described by the Boltzmann transport equation, where energy is carried by well-defined phonon quasiparticles undergoing incoherent scattering processes. In fact, in such systems phonon-phonon scattering and other scattering mechanisms (defects, boundaries) occur frequently enough that the phase of each phonon mode is lost after only a few oscillation periods. In that regime, the diagonal terms of the phonon density matrix are sufficient to describe phonon transport accurately. However, in low-dimensional materials, weakly bonded layered systems, or materials with flat optical branches, the assumption of fully incoherent phonon transport breaks down. In these cases, vibrational excitations can retain phase coherence over timescales comparable to or longer than the period associated with the frequency separation of near-degenerate phonon states. Under these conditions, phonons from different branches can interfere, giving rise to off-diagonal elements in the phonon density matrix that are typically neglected in the semiclassical BTE description. This results in an additional, intrinsically coherent contribution to thermal transport that is not captured by treating phonons as independent quasiparticles. This effect, which can be particularly relevant in materials with weak interlayer interactions (e.g., van der Waals or MOFs), where flat dispersions and avoided crossings enhance the overlap of vibrational eigenstates, is often referred to as the Wigner contribution and enhances interference processes between modes with similar frequencies and polarizations. Theoretical frameworks extending the BTE through the Wigner transport equation (see Eq. (2)) have demonstrated that this contribution can significantly alter the temperature dependence of thermal conductivity, typically reducing the effectiveness of the classical 1/T scaling expected from purely anharmonic scattering and introducing additional temperature-dependent channels for energy transport21,23,49. In layered frameworks like , characterized by both rigid in-plane bonding and weak interlayer coupling, these coherent processes are expected to be enhanced, especially for out-of-plane transport, due to flat optical modes and strong mode hybridization near avoided crossings.

Figure 6 summarizes the results of our calculations. The values of confirms the above considerations obtained both for BTE and WTE cases: the AA stacking is always slightly more conductive than C, both for the BTE and for the Wigner contribution. This indicates that even when coherent effects are considered, the intrinsic differences in bonding and phonon dispersions, stronger in-plane bonding and weaker interlayer coupling in AA, still favor higher overall in-plane conductivity in AA compared to C. However, the weak van der Waals out-of-plane forces result in an overall low conductivity, nearly temperature independent, while the the presence of interlayer Cu-S bonds leads to a substantially larger conductivity, as already observed. It is interesting to observe how the Wigner contribution increases with temperature before reaching a plateau at higher temperatures (Fig. 6a, b). Coherent transport is not limited by anharmonic phonon scattering but it originates from interference effects and near-degenerate phonon states, which become more active at higher temperatures as more vibrational modes are populated and phase-coherence impact grow.

This flattening indicates that, at elevated temperatures, where normal scattering processes dominate and classical transport would typically collapse, coherent channels keep contributing. This is particularly relevant for 2D metal-organic frameworks like , where complex mode hybridization can increase phase coherence effects, as indicated also in the recent work by Un et al. 18. The C stacking, with stronger interlayer bonding and higher absolute conductivity, exhibits a significant Wigner-induced reduction in the temperature exponent, indicating that coherent transport plays a notable role in out-of-plane conduction when structural connectivity enables hybridized phonons. For AA, although the absolute conductivity remains low, the exponent change implies that coherent effects are also present but are less impactful in absolute terms because of weaker interlayer coupling. These deviations from the expected law reflect the emergence of a distinct transport regime governed by phase-coherent vibrational dynamics. This anomalous temperature dependence-especially demonstrates that quantum coherence, captured by the Wigner formalism, must be accounted for to correctly describe heat conduction in low-dimensional and soft frameworks such as 2D MOFs.

Conclusions

We provide a comprehensive microscopic understanding of how stacking geometry influences lattice dynamics and heat transport in the two-dimensional metal-organic framework . By comparing three stacking configurations, AA, AB, and C, we demonstrate that small variations in interlayer alignment lead to substantial differences in structural stability, vibrational behaviour, and thermal conductivity. Our phonon calculations confirm that the AB phase is dynamically unstable, while the C stacking is the most stable both energetically and dynamically. The AA phase, although stable, is less favourable energetically and shows pronounced directional anisotropy due to weak van der Waals interlayer coupling. In contrast, the C phase enables enhanced out-of-plane transport thanks to covalent Cu-S interlayer bonds, which contribute to a more uniform thermal response. Mode-resolved analysis of group velocities, phonon lifetimes, and mean free paths indicates that the stronger interlayer bonding in the C structure not only modifies phonon dispersion but also increases the phase space for three-phonon scattering, leading to more intense anharmonic interactions. Despite this, coherent phonon transport, as accounted for through the Wigner formalism, plays a significant role in both stackings. The explicit calculation of both the anharmonic and coherent contributions to the thermal conductivity shows that coherence substantially enhances the overall conductivity and alters its temperature dependence. While third-order anharmonicity follows a strict trend typical of phonon-phonon scattering, the inclusion of coherent effects flattens this behaviour, highlighting a hybrid regime where wave-like phonon transport becomes relevant. We note that the thermal conductivities predicted here are sufficiently low to be compatible with thermoelectric applications, especially given the soft lattice and low-dimensional character of .

As a next step, it will be crucial to assess the electronic transport properties and compute the Seebeck coefficient to evaluate the full thermoelectric figure of merit ZT. A complete picture, combining vibrational and electronic channels, will enable a more reliable assessment of the potential of 2D MOFs as efficient thermoelectric materials.

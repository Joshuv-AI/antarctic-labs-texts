Mothers making milestones: Awareness of rural mothers toward pediatric milestones
Authors: Tapesh D. Nagaria, Samarth Shukla, Sourya Acharya, Neema Acharya, Raju K. Shinde
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Developmental milestones are easily identifiable events during a child’s development, and their thorough monitoring is of utmost importance till 6 years of age. Rural mothers are rarely explained these milestones, which may be a reason for the higher prevalence of developmental disorders in rural areas (≊18%). This study aimed to assess the knowledge of developmental milestones in rural mothers attending a rural, tertiary care hospital and further provides intelligence to the mothers on the same. These measures will aid toward the early detection of developmental milestone anomalies.Method:PNC mothers in the obstetric ward and mothers attending the pediatric outpatient department and inpatient department were selected for the study and assessed for their knowledge of childhood milestones with the help of a short questionnaire based on the Denver II Developmental Scale in three languages. Based on the feedback from mothers, the appropriate counseling was given.Results:The questionnaire was assessed based on four basic domains, fine motor skills (23.33% responded correctly), personal and social skills (51.66% responded correctly), language skills (41.66% responded correctly), and gross motor skills (41.25% responded correctly). Upon later feedback, 33.33% reported a suspected developmental delay in their child.Conclusions:This study indicates a very low maternal awareness of pediatric milestones in rural areas, and considering the high prevalence of developmental delays in these areas, across the world, education of mothers is a necessary and a very effective method for early diagnosis. Formulation of a framework to address this issue on the part of the authorities and healthcare professionals is an effective solution for this underreported and important issue.

Introduction

Developmental milestones are important, easily identifiable events during the continuous process of development. Since development is a continuous process, a thorough monitoring of these milestones is of utmost importance till 6 years of age. Among the many factors affecting the development of a child, most like malnutrition and socioeconomic conditions are easily identifiable. It has been found that 18% of the rural population suffers from some or the other form of developmental disorder.[123456] A timely diagnosis may reduce family stress related to diagnostic and prognostic uncertainties.[26] For children with a few neuromuscular diseases for which treatments are available, outcomes may be improved when therapy is implemented early.[3] The current trend in pediatrics is to assess the various developmental milestones when a child is brought in for a routine checkup or for some other illness. For example, a small delay in social smile and eye-to- eye contact is suggestive of autism spectrum disorder. Many developmental disorders and diseases like attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), learning disabilities like dyslexia, stuttering, Rett syndrome, Asperger syndrome, and childhood disintegrative disorder, if detected early, are completely preventable or controllable.

Developmental milestones, though an important aspect of the growth of a child, are not really explained to the person who is best suited to detect these, their mothers. There has been a recent trend in explaining these milestones to mothers in the urban setup,[4] which has shown positive results. Rural mothers on the other hand are rarely explained these developmental milestones and have no self-knowledge, which can be ascribed to illiteracy or to lack of access to reliable knowledge. This translates to a higher prevalence of developmental disorders in rural areas. Also, unexplained ethnic differences have been seen in the attainment of gross motor milestones.[5]

This study aimed to assess the maternal knowledge of elemental milestones in rural mothers attending a rural, tertiary care hospital in the Vidarbha region of Maharashtra and to provide an intervention-based knowledge of these milestones to these mothers so as to look at the improvement if any.

Methodology

Study design

Cross-sectional study.

Selection criteria

Postnatal mothers in the obstetric ward and mothers attending the pediatric outpatient department (OPD) and inpatient department (IPD) were selected for the study. The study was started after obtaining the Institutional Ethical Clearance.

After taking the proper consent for participation in the study, mothers were provided with proper instructions regarding the study along with proper explanation regarding the methodology of the study and the ultimate aim of the study. The mothers were taught through demonstrations and videos the assessment techniques of milestones. Tests were based on the Denver Development Scale and milestones corresponding to the age categories of the child shown below. Children were grouped into four categories as per their age – neonates (0–28 days), infant (1 month to 12 months), toddler (1 year to 3 years), and child (above 3 years).

Phase 1 – Spreading awareness

The mothers were assessed for their knowledge of childhood milestones. They were also made aware of the importance of understanding these milestones. Subsequently, they were introduced to the assessment techniques of milestones. A test questionnaire based on the Denver II Development Scale which was translated into their preferred language was used.

The questionnaire used was as follows:

For scoring the answers, the correct answers were considered those within the time period where according to the Denver II Development Scale 75–90% of the children achieved their milestones. An additional 1.5 months was added to the time period to account for bias.

Phase 2 – Tutoring mothers on how to monitor milestones

They were taught to effectively and correctly monitor the milestones based on Denver II Development Scale. They were provided with two sheets of milestones. Sheet 1 contained the milestone from birth to 6 years of age for them to have a wider comprehensive understanding. Sheet 2 contained the milestones for the age bracket closest to the age of her child.

Phase 3 – Mothers’ timebound feedback and intervention

In case the mother gave information of a delay in the milestones, they were counseled on the further steps to be taken and were asked to report to the hospital for an examination.

Phase 4 – Assessment of mothers

The final outcome of this particular study was assessed by analyzing how effectively the mothers were able to identify and how aware they were of the childhood milestones based on the Denver II Developmental Scale. Confidentiality was maintained throughout the research, and the information of the subjects was accessible only to the researchers.

Discussion

Developmental milestones are behaviors or physical skills seen in infants and children as they grow and develop. The various developmental milestones broadly categorized under four categories, namely, personal-social, fine motor-adaptive, language, and gross motor.

Developmental milestones are important as they help us track the development of a child, and a delay in these developmental disorders is helpful in the early diagnosis of many neurodevelopmental disorders. Developmental disabilities also encompass a spectrum of problems of varying kinds and severity. Although there is broad agreement as to what constitutes clear-cut delay or deviation, there is not complete consensus among professionals or between parents and physicians as to the severity at which evaluation and intervention become appropriate.[67]

The US Food and Drug Administration describes the pediatric age group as any person up to the age of 16 years. It is subdivided into neonates (0–1 months), infant (1 month to 2 years), children (2–12 years), and adolescent (up to 16 years). In developing countries like India and in many countries of Africa, the pediatric population is growing at a rapid rate. In India, the total child population between 0 and 6 years of age is estimated at 158.8 million, out of which 117.6 million reside in the rural areas.[8] It is one of the largest in the world. It has been found that 18% of the rural population in India suffers from some or the other form of developmental disorder.[16] It is thus important that proper steps be taken to study the spread of neurodevelopmental and other disorders among the children in India and proper steps also be taken to actively prevent them.

Factors affecting the developmental milestones

Developmental milestones of children are very dynamic, and their attainment can depend on a wide variety of factors as found by various studies. Various studies have found a direct correlation of better socioeconomic status with better attainment of developmental milestones.[91011] Education of mothers is also an important factor with better and timely attainment of developmental milestones by their children.[10] An important factor is mothers’ age. It has been found that adolescent mothers were less responsive and paid less attention to their children on home observation.[10] It should also be pointed out that although low, the practice of child marriage is still very much in practice in rural areas of India. Access to healthcare is also an important factor which shows significant relation to development milestones. Assistance with childcare has also shown positive correlation with developmental milestones.[12] Other significant factors were race and number of children.[1213]

Screening tests for developmental disorders

Screening is a brief assessment procedure designed to identify children who should receive more intensive diagnosis or assessment.[14] Developmental screening is aimed at identifying children who may need more comprehensive evaluation. It communicates the pediatrician’s interest in the child’s development, not just his or her physical health.[15] Developmental evaluation may lead to a definitive diagnosis, development of an interdisciplinary comprehensive plan of remediation, realization that there is no significant problem, or a decision that additional observation is warranted.[16] Various scales for measuring developmental milestones have been devised for helping physicians and scientists monitor the growth of a child and in turn screen for any delay in developmental milestones.

The screening tests have properties like specificity that have been standardized on diverse populations’ validity, sensitivity, and reliability. Parent report instruments, such as the Ages and Stages Questionnaires,[17] Parents’ Evaluation of Developmental Status,[18] and Child Development Inventories,[19] have the advantage of requiring much less time from the pediatrician than other instruments like those requiring direct examination. Other instruments widely used are the Denver-II screening test,[20] Bayley Infant Neurodevelopmental Screener,[21] Battelle Developmental Inventory,[22] Early Language Milestone Scale,[23] and Brigance Screens,[242526] and many of these involve direct examination of the child’s skills. One of the most widely used developmental screening scales is the Denver II Developmental scale and was used as a standard for a long time.[27]

Issues related to delay in developmental milestones

Neurodevelopmental disorders are disabilities associated primarily with the functioning of the neurological system and brain. Examples of neurodevelopmental disorders in children include ADHD, autism, learning disabilities, intellectual disability (also known as mental retardation), conduct disorders, cerebral palsy, and impairments in vision and hearing.[28] Direct associations have been found between delays in developmental disorders and diseases like ADHD,[29] autism,[30] learning disabilities, mental retardation,[28] and cerebral palsy.[31] It is especially important to recognize delays in language skills early because early intervention may improve the outcome of children with hearing loss and may enable earlier diagnosis of children with mental retardation and pervasive developmental disorders.[16]

Relevance to primary care

Vanselow et al.[31] as part of the definition of primary care said that primary care clinicians develop a sustained partnership with their patients and practice in the context of family and community. This study focused on long-term outcomes of the children with respect to developmental milestones and on developing relations with families to improve the overall health. Health of the mother and children are considered very important as indicators of healthcare status in a country and primary care, being the most accessible and tending to most of these cases, uniquely suited to address and the issues and implement the outcomes of this study. The results of the study by Edmond et al. correspond to this.[36]

The Indian scenario

A few studies have been done in India like the studies by Juneja et al.,[32] MKC Nair, which found significant delays in developmental milestones in children in an ICDS block in Kerala,[33] and other studies, which showed high prevalence and low knowledge of developmental delays in India and which showed that speech delay is the only delay which is considered important by parents.[34]

Out of the 60 mothers included in the study, 68.33% scored between 0 and 4, and 31.67% scored between 5 and 10. The findings are consistent with the study conducted by Vanselow NA et al.[35]

It was observed that mothers equally underestimated and overestimated the expected age of a developmental milestone, both of which can have a negative impact on the parental expectations from a child. It was also found that mothers who reported having a single child had a higher awareness of developmental milestones than mothers who reported having more than one child.

The questions were grouped and evaluated according to four broad categories based on the Denver II Development Scale into (1) gross motor skills, (2) language skills, (3) fine motor skills, and (4) personal-social skills.

These findings are not consistent with the results of the study by Coll CG, et al.,[9] which reported that mothers’ knowledge was stronger on language, cognitive, and motor skills and the lowest on social skills. These findings are contradictory to the findings of this research. A study by Vanselow NA also showed a similar result of better recall of the age of walking and imprecise recall of the age of language skills in their children.[35]

The mothers were also encouraged to give feedback to us regarding the developmental milestones of their children, and they were given a mobile number to contact and to provide details specifically, whether or not their child was experiencing any delay in developmental milestones. Out of the 60 mothers included in the study, a follow-up call was attempted for all and 21 (35%) of the mothers attended/replied back with feedback on the developmental milestones of their child/children. Out if these 21 mothers, 12 reported delays in developmental milestones of their children and upon further questioning, it was found that five mothers had correctly diagnosed a developmental delay in their children, while seven had incorrectly reported a developmental delay in their child when there was none. Eight mothers did not report any developmental delay in their children, and upon further questioning, it was found that six had correct knowledge of the developmental milestones and hence were correct in their reporting and two had incorrect knowledge and it was found that their children were actually having a developmental milestone delay.

Thus, mothers in rural settings showed a lack of knowledge of developmental milestones of children in all categories counted under the Denver II Developmental Scale, namely, gross motor, language fine motor, and personal-social skills, which can be ascribed to high illiteracy rates, low socio-economic status, faulty peer knowledge, and lower access to proper healthcare services.

Limitation

The present study had certain limitations. It covered a small population size in a rural region of central India. The study also only covered mothers visiting the hospital, and further community-based approaches to maternal education of developmental milestones are needed. Therefore, more elaborate population-based studies are needed to develop a fuller picture of the burden of developmental delays and the effects of material awareness on their early diagnosis.

Future prospects of the work done

This study will assist in the formulation of regional and national strategies to help reduce the burden of developmental disorders on our populace. The work done in this study can also help to develop optimal techniques for teaching mothers regarding regular monitoring of certain developmental milestones to be used as a home-based cheap and effective screening tool for early diagnosis of developmental delays. This study can also serve as a framework for a more systematic regionals mapping of the current burden of developmental delays in the country along with serving as a benchmark for developmental milestone screening tools deployable to parents and guardians in not just rural but also urban centers.

Conclusions

This study provided data and major insight into the maternal knowledge and awareness of pediatric-developmental milestones among rural mothers in India. From the present study, we can conclude that there is a very low awareness of developmental milestones among rural mothers. This study has the potential to provide the blueprint for a mobile application, which can help with the detection of delay in developmental milestones and provide appropriate intervention.

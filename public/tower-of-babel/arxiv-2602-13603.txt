Abstract
. We define the super Yangian Y m | n Y_{m|n} over a field 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} of characteristic 2 2 , and show that the super Yangian Y m | n Y_{m|n} is a deformation of the super universal enveloping algebra of the current Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 m + n ​ [ t ] \mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}[t] . By employing the methods of the work of [ BT18 ] , we also give a description of the center of Y m | n Y_{m|n} .

1.
Introduction

The Yangian
Y_{n}
associated to the Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
over complex field was introduced in
[
TF79
]
.
It is an associative algebra generated by the
RTT generators

\{t_{i,j}^{(r)};~1\leq i,j\leq n,r>0\}
subject to the relations:

\displaystyle\left[t_{i,j}^{(r)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}\right]=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(r,s)-1}\left(t_{k,j}^{(t)}t_{i,l}^{(r+s-1-t)}-t_{k,j}^{(r+s-1-t)}t_{i,l}^{(t)}\right)

(1.1)

for
1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n
and
r,s>0
.
In
[
BT18
]
,
Brundan and Topley developed the theory of the Yangian
Y_{n}
over a field of positive
characteristic.
They defined the Yangian
Y_{n}
over any field
\mathbbm{k}
by the same
RTT presentation
(
1.1
) as above.
When
\mathbbm{k}
is of characteristic zero,
it is well-known that
Y_{n}
is a filtered deformation of the universal enveloping algebra

U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
of the
current algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t]:=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[t]
, cf.
[
MNO96
]
.
The same assertion is true when
\mathbbm{k}
is of positive characteristic (
[
BT18
, Lemma 4.2]
).
Moreover,
they extended the
Drinfeld-type presentation
(
[
D88
,
BK05
]
) from characteristic zero to positive characteristic and gave a description of the center
Z(Y_{n})
of
Y_{n}
.
One of the key features which differs from characteristic zero is the existence of a large central subalgebra
Z_{p}(Y_{n})
,
called the
p
-center
.

The super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
associated to the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
over the complex field was defined by Nazarov
[
Na91
]
in terms of the RTT presentation as a super analogue of
Y_{n}
.
In
[
CH23
]
,
we obtained the superalgebra generalization of the
p
-center of
[
BT18
]
for the modular super Yangian of type
A
.
Following
[
Na91
]
,
we define the super Yangian over a field
\mathbbm{k}
to be the associative superalgebra with the RTT generators
\{t_{i,j}^{(r)};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,r>0\}

subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle\left[t_{i,j}^{(r)},t_{k,l}^{(s)}\right]=(-1)^{\bar{i}\bar{j}+\bar{i}\bar{k}+\bar{j}\bar{k}}\sum_{t=0}^{\min(r,s)-1}\left(t_{k,j}^{(t)}t_{i,l}^{(r+s-1-t)}-t_{k,j}^{(r+s-1-t)}t_{i,l}^{(t)}\right),

(1.2)

where
\bar{i}=0
if
1\leq m
,

\bar{i}=1
if
i\geq m+1
,
and the bracket is understood as a supercommutator.
Over a field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic
\neq 2
,
the
Y_{m|n}
is also a deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of the polynomial current superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}[t]
(cf.
[
Gow07
, Corollary 2]
,
[
CH23
, Lemma 3.2]
).

The definition of
Lie superalgebra
is the same as usual for any characteristic
\neq 2
,
that is, a Lie superalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra
L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}}
with bilinear multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfying the usual skew-supersymmetry and super Jacobi identity (see for instance
[
Kac77
]
).
The definition does not naively extend to characteristic
2
.
Otherwise, the Lie superalgebra is just a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie algebra.
To address this issue,
a Lie superalgebra over a field of characteristic
2
is defined as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie algebra
L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}}
endowed with a quadratic map
Q:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow L_{\bar{0}}
(see
[
Bou+20
,
Bou+23
]
).
One can define the
super enveloping algebra

U_{\super}(L)
as a replacement for the usual eveloping algebra of a Lie superalgerba.
Then we have the PBW isomorphism
\gr U_{\super}(L)\cong S(L_{\bar{0}})\otimes\Lambda(L_{\bar{1}})
,
which is in a good agreement with the theory of Lie superalgebras in characteristic
\neq 2
(see
[
EH25
]
).

Of course,
the above issue will also happen for the modular super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
.
Suppose that
\Char\mathbbm{k}=2
.
Observing (
1.2
),
we see that the super phenomenon disappears and the super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
coincides with the Yangian
Y_{m+n}
(see
[
CH23
]
,
[
Hu25
]
).
In this short note, we define the super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
in characteristic
2
.
One can show that it is the deformation of the super enveloping algebra

U_{\super}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}[t])
.
In Section
2
,
we recall some notations about current algebras
and Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
.
Then we determine the center of the super universal enveloping algebra of the current Lie superalgebra.
In Section
3
,
we first define the super Yangian
Y_{m|n}
in characteristic
2
and prove the PBW theorem.
Moreover, we give a description of the center of
Y_{m|n}
.

2.
Lie superalgebras

From now on,
we always assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\Char(\mathbbm{k})=:p=2
.

2.1.
The current Lie algebra

Given two positive integers
m,n>0
,
we consider the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}
.
The
current algebra
is defined to be the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}[t]:=\mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[t]
.
We will always denote this Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}
and write
U(\mathfrak{g})
for its enveloping algebra.
Then the elements
e_{i,j}t^{r}:=e_{i,j}\otimes t^{r}
with
r\geq 0
and
i,j=1,\dots,m+n

make a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and the Lie bracket is given by

\displaystyle[e_{i,j}t^{r},e_{k,l}t^{s}]=\delta_{k,j}e_{i,l}t^{r+s}-\delta_{l,i}e_{k,j}t^{r+s},

(2.1)

where
e_{i,j}
is the standard elementary matrix and

1\leq i,j,k,l\leq m+n
,

r,s\geq 0
.
Now let the indices
i,j
run through
1,\dots,m+n
.
Set
\bar{i}=0
if
1\leq i\leq m
and
\bar{i}=1
if
m<i\leq m+n
.
Accordingly, equip the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gradation,
the
parity
of the element
e_{i,j}t^{r}
is found by
\bar{i}+\bar{j}\mod 2
.
So that the basis element
e_{i,j}t^{r}
is
even
if
1\leq i,j\leq m
or
m+1\leq i,j\leq m+n
and
e_{i,j}t^{r}
is
odd
otherwise.

Recall that a
restricted Lie algebra
over
\mathbbm{k}
is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}

over
\mathbbm{k}
with a map
[p]:\mathfrak{r}\rightarrow\mathfrak{r}
sending
x\mapsto x^{[2]}
such that the map

\xi:\mathfrak{r}\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{r});~\quad x\mapsto\xi(x):=x^{p}-x^{[p]}

satisfies two properties:
(i)
\xi(\mathfrak{r})
lie in the center of
U(\mathfrak{r})
;
(ii)
\xi
is
p
-semilinear.
Here the
p
-semilinear means that
\xi(\lambda x)=\lambda^{p}\xi(x)
and

\xi(x+y)=\xi(x)+\xi(y)
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{r},\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}
.
We refer the reader to
[
Jan98
, Section 2]
for more details on restricted Lie algebras.

We identify
\mathfrak{g}
with the matrix algebra
\Mat_{m+n}(\mathbbm{k}[t])
.
Then the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
carries a natural restricted Lie algebra structure
with the
[p]
-map defined by the rule
a^{[p]}=a^{[2]}=a\cdot a
for
a\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Here
a\cdot a
denotes the usual matrix multiplication.
In particular, the
[p]
-map defined on the basis by
(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{[p]}=\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{pr}
.

We will let
Z(\mathfrak{g})
be the center of
U(\mathfrak{g})
.
Using the restricted structure,
we can define the
p
-center
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
of
U(\mathfrak{g})
to be the subalgebra of

Z(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Since the map
\xi:x\mapsto x^{p}-x^{[p]}
is
p
-semilinear,
we have that

\displaystyle Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g}):=\mathbbm{k}[(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{p}-\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{rp};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,r\geq 0]

(2.2)

as a free polynomial algebra.

2.2.
Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2

We first recall the following definition.

Definition
.

(
[
Bou+20
, Subsection 1.2.3]
,
[
Bou+23
, Subsection 2.2]
,
see also
[
EH25
, Definition 3.1]
)
A Lie superalgebra over
\mathbbm{k}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie algebra
L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}}

together with a quadratic map
Q:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow L_{\bar{0}}

such that for
y,y_{1},y_{2}\in L_{\bar{1}}
,
x\in L
we have

\displaystyle[y_{1},y_{2}]=Q(y_{1}+y_{2})-Q(y_{1})-Q(y_{2}),\quad[Q(y),x]=[y,[y,x]].

(2.3)

The
super universal enveloping algebra
of
L
is defined via

\displaystyle U_{\super}(L):=U(L)/(y^{2}-Q(y);~y\in L_{\bar{1}}).

(2.4)

As before, we identify the elements in the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with polynomial matrices.
We therefore obtain

(x+y)^{[p]}=(x+y)^{[2]}=(x+y)\cdot(x+y)=x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}+x\cdot y+y\cdot x=x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}+[x,y]

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The last equality follows from the definition of Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
(
2.1
).
On the other hand, since
\mathfrak{g}
is restricted,
it follows that
[x,[x,y]]=[x^{[2]},y]
.
Consequently, the restriction
[p]_{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
of the
[p]
-map to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}

defines a quadratic map satisfying (
2.3
)
and the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with a Lie superalgebra structure.
By definition (
2.4
), we have

U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})/(y^{2}-y^{[2]};~y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}).

Let
Z_{p,\odd}(\mathfrak{g})
be the subalgebra of
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
\xi(y)=y^{2}-y^{[2]}
for all
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
.
Again,
the semilinearity of
\xi
implies that

Z_{p,\odd}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2};~\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0]

is a free polynomial algebra.

For homogeneous elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}
in a superalgebra
A
,
a
supermonomial
in
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}
means
a monomial of the form
x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{s}^{i_{s}}
for some
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
i_{j}\leq 1
if
x_{j}
is odd.
The following proposition is
therefore a consequence of the PBW theorem (cf.
[
Jan98
, Propositions 2.3 and 2.8]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

The enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
is free as a module over
Z_{p,\odd}(\mathfrak{g})
with basis given by the ordered supermonomials in the following elements of
\mathfrak{g}

\{e_{i,j}t^{r};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,r\geq 0\}.

In particular, the above ordered supermonomials forms a linear basis for
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.3.
The center of
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})

The adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself extends uniquely to an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
U(\mathfrak{g})
.
Since the ideal
(y^{2}-y^{[2]};~y\in\mathfrak{g}_{1})
is invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{g}
, the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
U(\mathfrak{g})
naturally extends to an action on
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
. The invariant subalgebra is denoted
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We write
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
for the center of
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
In particular, we have
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})=U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
There is one obvious family of central elements in
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\displaystyle z_{r}:=e_{1,1}t^{r}+\cdots+e_{m+n,m+n}t^{r}.

(2.5)

Using (
2.1
) one can show by direct computation that the set
\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}
forms a basis for the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Now Proposition
2.1
implies
\mathbbm{k}[z_{r};~r\geq 0]
is a subalgebra of
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.

There is a filtration

U(\mathfrak{g})=\bigcup\limits_{r\geq 0}{\rm F}_{r}U(\mathfrak{g})

of the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
,
which is defined by placing
e_{i,j}t^{r}
in degree
r+1
.
The filtration descends to a filtration on
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
According to the PBW theorem (Proposition
2.1
) (see also
[
EH25
, Theorem 3.2]
),
the associated graded algebra
\gr U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to
S(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})\otimes\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})
.
Here
\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})
is the quotient of
S(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})
by the relations
x^{2}=0
,
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
(see the introduction of
[
EH25
]
).
Let
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g}):=S(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})\otimes\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})
for short. It follows that

\displaystyle\gr Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})=\gr U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq(\gr U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g}))^{\mathfrak{g}}=S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}.

(2.6)

Lemma 2.2
.

The invariant algebra
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
is generated by
\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}
together with
(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})^{2}:=\{x^{2};~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\}\subseteq S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
In fact,

S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
is freely generated by

\displaystyle\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}\cup\{(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n~{\rm with}~(i,j)\neq(1,1),\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\}.

(2.7)

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as
[
CH23
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
BT18
, Lemma 3.2]
. Let us point out the minor differences from therein.
Let
I(\mathfrak{g})
be the subalgebra of
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})^{2}
.
Let

B_{0}:=\{(i,j,r);~1\leq i,j\leq m+n~{\rm with}~(i,j)\neq(1,1),\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\},

B_{1}:=\{(i,j,r);~1\leq i,j\leq m+n~{\rm with}~\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\},

and
B:=B_{0}\cup B_{1}
for short.
In
[
CH23
, Lemma 2.1]
, we don’t have the odd elements if
p=2
.
The proof there follows immediately from
[
BT18
, Lemma 3.2]
.
However, in our situation, we consider the
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
instead of
S(\mathfrak{g})
.
We still have

S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[z_{r};~r\geq 0][e_{i,j}t^{r};~(i,j,r)\in B_{0}]\otimes\Lambda[e_{i,j}t^{r};~(i,j,r)\in B_{1}],

I(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[z_{r};~r\geq 0][(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2};~(i,j,r)\in B_{0}],

and
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is free as an
I(\mathfrak{g})
-module.
Then we can repeat the proof of
[
CH23
, Lemma 2.1]

without any change to obtain our desired result.
∎

We define the
p
-center
of
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
to be the image of the
p
-center of
U(\mathfrak{g})
in
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
,
and denote it by
Z_{p,\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Recall from (
2.2
) that
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
is free polynomial algebra generated by

\{(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{p}-\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{rp};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,r\geq 0\}.

In particular, the image
Z_{p,\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by

\displaystyle\{(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{p}-\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{rp};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\}.

(2.8)

The following theorem is a super generalization of
[
BT18
, Theorem 3.4]
for
p=2
, see also
[
CH23
, Theorem 2.3]
.
The proof is similar to that of
[
BT18
, Theorem 3.4]
.
Since our setting is different, we provide a detailed proof here.

Theorem 2.3
.

The center
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
of
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by
\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}
and
Z_{p,\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Moreover:

(1)

Z_{p,\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is freely generated by the elements in (
2.8
);

(2)

Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is freely generated by

\displaystyle\{z_{r};~r\geq 0\}\cup\{(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2}-

\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{2r};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n~

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\rm with}~(i,j)\neq(1,1),\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\}.

Proof.

For
1\leq i,j\leq m+n
with
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\mod 2
and
r\geq 0
,
we have that the element
(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2}-\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{2r}
is of filtered degree
2r+2

and

\gr_{2r+2}[(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2}-\delta_{i,j}e_{i,j}t^{2r}]=(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2}\in S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g}).

Since the elements
\{(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\mod 2,r\geq 0\}
are algebraically independent in
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
,
it follows that the elements in (
2.8
) are algebraically independent in
Z_{p,\super}(\mathfrak{g})
, this proves (1).

Clearly, all the elements in (
2.9
) are contained in
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
Z
be the subalgebra of
Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by (
2.9
).
For
r\geq 0
we have that
z_{r}\in{\rm F}_{r+1}U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\gr_{r+1}z_{r}=z_{r}\in S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Lemma
2.2
implies that the elements (
2.9
) are lifts of the algebraically independent generators of
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
from (
2.7
).
It follows that the elements (
2.9
) are themselves algebraically independent, and moreover
S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq\gr Z
.
Thanks to (
2.6
),
we also have

\gr Z\subseteq\gr Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq S_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}},

so equality must hold throughout.
This ensures that
Z=Z_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
.
∎

3.
Super Yangian in characteristic
2

3.1.
Modular Yangian
Y_{m+n}

The Yangian
Y_{m+n}
over
\mathbbm{k}
is defined to be the associative algebra with generators
\{t_{i,j}^{(r)};~1\leq i,j\leq m+n,r>0\}
subject just to the relations (
1.1
).
Using
Gauss decomposition
,
Brundan and Topley established the following modular analogue of
[
BK05
, Theorem 5.2]
,
see
[
BT18
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
CH23
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Yangian
Y_{m+n}
is generated by the elements
\{d_{i}^{(r)},d_{i}^{\prime(r)};~1\leq i\leq m+n,r\geq 1\}
and
\{e_{j}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(r)};~1\leq j\leq m+n-1,r\geq 1\}
subject only to the following relations:

\displaystyle d_{i}^{(0)}=1,~\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r}d_{i}^{(t)}d_{i}^{\prime(r-t)}=\delta_{r0};

(3.1)

\displaystyle[d_{i}^{(r)},d_{j}^{(s)}]=0;

(3.2)

\displaystyle[d_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}]=(\delta_{ij}-\delta_{i,j+1})\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r-1}d_{i}^{(t)}e_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)};

(3.3)

\displaystyle[d_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}]=(\delta_{i,j+1}-\delta_{ij})\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r-1}f_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)}d_{i}^{(t)};

(3.4)

\displaystyle[e_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}]=-\delta_{ij}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r+s-1}d_{i}^{\prime(t)}d_{i+1}^{(r+s-1-t)};

(3.5)

\displaystyle[e_{j}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}]=(\sum\limits_{t=1}^{s-1}e_{j}^{(t)}e_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)}-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{r-1}e_{j}^{(t)}e_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)});

(3.6)

\displaystyle[f_{j}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}]=(\sum\limits_{t=1}^{r-1}f_{j}^{(t)}f_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)}-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{s-1}f_{j}^{(t)}f_{j}^{(r+s-1-t)});

(3.7)

\displaystyle[e_{j}^{(r+1)},e_{j+1}^{(s)}]-[e_{j}^{(r)},e_{j+1}^{(s+1)}]=e_{j}^{(r)}e_{j+1}^{(s)};

(3.8)

\displaystyle[f_{j}^{(r+1)},f_{j+1}^{(s)}]-[f_{j}^{(r)},f_{j+1}^{(s+1)}]=-f_{j+1}^{(s)}f_{j}^{(r)};

(3.9)

\displaystyle[e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}]=0=[f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}]\quad\mbox{if}~~|i-j|>1;

(3.10)

\displaystyle[[e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(s)}],e_{j}^{(t)}]+[[e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(t)}],e_{j}^{(s)}]=0,\quad\mbox{if}~~|i-j|=1;

(3.11)

\displaystyle[[f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(s)}],f_{j}^{(t)}]+[[f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(t)}],f_{j}^{(s)}]=0,\quad\mbox{if}~~|i-j|=1;

(3.12)

\displaystyle[[e_{i}^{(r)},e_{j}^{(t)}],e_{j}^{(t)}]=0\quad\mbox{if}~~|i-j|=1;

(3.13)

\displaystyle[[f_{i}^{(r)},f_{j}^{(t)}],f_{j}^{(t)}]=0,\quad\mbox{if}~~|i-j|=1;

(3.14)

\displaystyle[[e_{i-1}^{(r)},e_{i}^{(1)}],[e_{i}^{(1)},e_{i+1}^{(s)}]]=0;

(3.15)

\displaystyle[[f_{i-1}^{(r)},f_{i}^{(1)}],[f_{i}^{(1)},f_{i+1}^{(s)}]]=0.

(3.16)

The generators of
Y_{m+n}
in Theorem
3.1
above are called
Drinfeld generators
.
In order to state the PBW theorem we define higher root elements as follows.
For
i=1,\dots,m+n-1
, we set

e_{i,i+1}^{(r)}:=e_{i}^{(r)},\quad f_{i+1,i}^{(r)}:=f_{i}^{(r)}

and define inductively

e_{i,j}^{(r)}:=[e_{i,j-1}^{(r)},e_{j-1}^{(1)}],\quad f_{j,i}^{(r)}:=[f_{j-1}^{(1)},f_{j-1,i}^{(r)}]

for all
1\leq i<j\leq m+n
and
r>0
.
In fact, the Drinfeld generators can be expressed in terms of
quasi-determinants
of
[
GR97
]
, see
[
BK05
, Section 5]
.

The
loop filtration
on
Y_{m+n}
is defined by placing the elements
e_{i,j}^{(r+1)}
,

d_{i}^{(r+1)}
,
f_{j,i}^{(r+1)}
in filtered degree
r
for all
r\geq 0
.
We write
\mathcal{F}_{r}Y_{m+n}
for the filtered piece of degree
r
,
so that
Y_{m+n}=\cup_{r\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{r}Y_{m+n}
,
and we write
\gr Y_{m+n}
for the associated graded algebra.
By the proof of
[
BT18
, Theorem 4.3]
there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}:U(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\gr Y_{m+n}

(3.17)

defined by

\displaystyle e_{i,i}t^{r}\mapsto\gr_{r}d_{i}^{(r+1)}

(3.18)

\displaystyle e_{i,j}t^{r}\mapsto\gr_{r}e_{i,j}^{(r+1)}

\displaystyle e_{j,i}t^{r}\mapsto\gr_{r}f_{j,i}^{(r+1)}

for
i<j
.
This gives the following PBW theorem for
Y_{m+n}
in terms of Drinfeld generators
[
BT18
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Ordered monomials in the elements

\displaystyle\{d_{i}^{(r)};~1\leq i\leq m+n,r>0\}\cup\{e_{i,j}^{(r)},f_{j,i}^{(r)};~1\leq i<j\leq m+n,r>0\}

(3.19)

taken in any fixed ordering form a basis of
Y_{m+n}
.

3.2.
Super Yangians in characteristic
2

Let
u
be an indeterminate,
and consider the power series ring
Y_{m+n}[[u^{-1}]]
.
We define the power series

d_{i}(u):=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}d_{i}^{(r)}u^{-r}\in Y_{m+n}[[u^{-1}]]

for all
i=1,\dots,m+n
.
We proceed to recall the description of the center of
Y_{m+n}
.
We first define

\displaystyle c(u)=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}c^{(r)}u^{-r}:=d_{1}(u)d_{2}(u-1)\cdots d_{m+n}(u-(m+n)+1).

(3.20)

According to
[
BT18
, Theorem 5.11(1)]
(see also
[
BK05
, Theorem 7.2]
),
the elements in
\{c^{(r)};~r>0\}
are algebraically independent and lie in the center
Z(Y_{m+n})
of
Y_{m+n}
.
The subalgebra they generate is called the
Harish-Chandra center
of
Y_{m+n}
,
and is denoted
Z_{\HC}(Y_{m+n})
.

For
i=1,\dots,m+n
, we define

\displaystyle b_{i}(u)=\sum_{r\geq 0}b_{i}^{(r)}:=d_{i}(u)d_{i}(u-1).

(3.21)

Remember that
p=2
.
By
[
BT18
, Theorem 5.11(2)]
the elements in

\displaystyle\{b_{i}^{(2r)};~i=1,\dots,m+n,r>0\}\cup\{(e_{i,j}^{(r)})^{2},(f_{j,i}^{(r)})^{2};~1\leq i<j\leq m+n,r>0\}

(3.22)

are algebraically independent, and lie in
Z(Y_{m+n})
.
The subalgebra they generate is called the
p
-center
of
Y_{m+n}
and is denoted
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})
.
In fact,
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})
is a free polynomial algebra over the generators given in (
3.22
).
Moreover,
the
Z(Y_{m+n})
is generated by
Z_{\HC}(Y_{m+n})
and
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})
.

In view of (
1.1
), we can equip
Y_{m+n}=(Y_{m+n})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(Y_{m+n})_{\bar{1}}
with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gradation, where the parity of
t_{i,j}^{(r)}
is defined by
\bar{i}+\bar{j}\mod 2
. The elements of
(Y_{m+n})_{\bar{0}}
are called
even
, those of
(Y_{m+n})_{\bar{1}}

odd
.
Using
[
BT18
, (4.6)-(4.9)]
, it is easy to see that all
d_{i}^{(r)}
are even.
Moreover, the elements
e_{i,j}^{(r)}
and
f_{j,i}^{(r)}
are even (resp. odd) if
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\mod 2
(resp.
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\mod 2
).

Let
Z_{p,\odd}
be the subalgebra of
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})
generated by

\displaystyle\{(e_{i,j}^{(r)})^{2},(f_{j,i}^{(r)})^{2};~1\leq i<j\leq m+n,~\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2,r>0\}.

(3.23)

We define
Z_{p,\odd}(Y_{m+n})_{+}
to be the maximal ideal of
Z_{p,\odd}(Y_{m+n})
generated by the elements given in (
3.23
).
Now we can define the
super Yangian

\displaystyle Y_{m|n}:=Y_{m+n}/Y_{m+n}Z_{p,\odd}(Y_{m+n})_{+}.

(3.24)

Remark 3.3
.

Actually, the following relations hold in
Y_{m|n}
for all
r,s\geq 1
(cf.
[
Gow07
, (41)]
):

[e_{m}^{(r)},e_{m}^{(s)}]=0=[f_{m}^{(r)},f_{m}^{(s)}].

We can assume, without loss of generality, that
r<s
.
It follows from (
3.6
) that

[e_{m}^{(r)},e_{m}^{(s)}]=\sum\limits_{t=r}^{s-1}e_{m}^{(t)}e_{m}^{(r+s-1-t)}.

Since
(e_{m}^{(r)})^{2}=0
in
Y_{m|n}
for all
r>0
by our definition,
the assertion immediately follows from the induction on
s-r
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The Yangian
Y_{m+n}
is free as a module over
Z_{p,\odd}
with basis given by the ordered supermonomials in the elements (
3.19
).
In particular, the images in
Y_{m|n}
of the ordered supermonomials in the elements (
3.19
) form a basis of
Y_{m|n}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the set consisting of ordered monomials in (
3.23
) multiplied by ordered supermonomials in (
3.19
) gives a basis for
Y_{m+n}
.
To see this,
we pass to the associated graded algebra (
3.17
) using (
3.18
) to reduce to showing that the monomials

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq m+n\\
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2\\
r\geq 0\end{subarray}}(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2a_{i,j;r}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m+n\\
r\geq 0\end{subarray}}(e_{i,i}t^{r})^{b_{i,i;r}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq m+n\\
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=0\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2\\
r\geq 0\end{subarray}}(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{b_{i,j;r}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq m+n\\
\bar{i}+\bar{j}=1\!\!\!\!\!\mod 2\\
r\geq 0\end{subarray}}(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{c_{i,j;r}}

for
a_{i,j;r}\geq 0
,
b_{i,j;r}\geq 0
and
0\leq c_{i,j;r}\leq 1
form a basis for
U(\mathfrak{g})
.
This is obvious.
∎

The loop filtration on
Y_{m+n}
descends to a loop filtration on
Y_{m|n}
.
We denote the filtered pieces by
\mathcal{F}_{r}Y_{m|n}
for
r\geq 0
,
and write
\gr Y_{m|n}
for the associated graded algebra.
The next corollary follows from Theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

The isomorphism
\tilde{\psi}:U(\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\gr Y_{m+n}
given in (
3.17
) induces an isomorphism

\psi:U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\gr Y_{m|n}.

Proof.

The canonical projection
\pi:Y_{m+n}\rightarrow Y_{m|n}
induces a surjective homomorphism
\gr\pi:\gr Y_{m+n}\rightarrow\gr Y_{m|n}
.
Given an odd element
e_{i,j}t^{r}
,
the image of
(e_{i,j}t^{r})^{2})
under
\tilde{\psi}
is contained in the kernel of
\gr\pi
.
As a result,

\tilde{\psi}
induces a surjection
\psi:U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow\gr Y_{m|n}
.
We obtain the following commutative diagram.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U(\mathfrak{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr Y_{m+n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr Y_{m|n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\psi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gr\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

Then we apply Proposition
2.1
in conjunction with Theorem
3.4
to see that a basis for
U_{\super}(\mathfrak{g})
is sent to a basis for
\gr Y_{m|n}
.
Hence,
\psi
is an isomorphism as required.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

The center

Moreover:

(2)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\cup\{(e_{i,j}^{(r)})^{2},(f_{j,i}^{(r)})^{2};

In particular,

coincides with the image of

in

Proof.

(1) According to
[

we have that

and

(3.27)

they are lifts of the algebraically independent generators of the

Remark 3.9
.

We let
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})_{+}
be the maixmal ideal of
Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})
generated by the elements given in (
3.22
).
The restricted Yangian is defined via

Y^{[p]}_{m+n}:=Y_{m+n}/Y_{m+n}Z_{p}(Y_{m+n})_{+}.

This definition is due to Goodwin and Topley
[
GT21
, Section 4.3]
.
Similarly,
we define
Z_{p}(Y_{m|n})_{+}
to be the maximal ideal of
Z_{p}(Y_{m|n})
generated by the elements (

3.24
) implies that
Y^{[p]}_{m+n}\cong Y^{[p]}_{m|n}
.

.
This is not surprising because the quadratic map is the restriction of the
[p]
-map,
see also
[
Bou+23
]
and
[

.

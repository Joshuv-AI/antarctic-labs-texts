Optical soliton solutions of the unstable nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Authors: Sana Shabbir, Syed Tauseef Saeed, Umair Asghar, Salman Saleem, Feyisa Edosa Merga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Unstable Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (UNLSE) is fundamental for understanding optical fibre light pulse propagation. The unstable form explains modulated wave train instabilities and disturbance propagation in stable and unstable mediums. The Complete Discriminant System for Polynomials Method will analyse UNLSE non-linear dynamics. We use computational libraries like Axes3D, mpl toolkits.mplot3d, numpy and pandas, scipy.fftpack, and matplotlib.pyplot as plt to create time series, 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional plots, Poincaré maps, and various optical soliton solutions, including trigonometric, Jacobi elliptic, hyperbolic, periodic waves, singular solitary waves solutions, and rational soliton waves solutions. Additionally, stability analyses are performed and illustrated visually to confirm the converging constraints of appropriate parameters.Keywords: Exact soliton solutions, Complete discriminant system for polynomials method, Qualitative analysis, Unstable nonlinear schrödinger equation.Subject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Background and importance of nonlinear evolution equations

The significance of nonlinear evolution equations and their applications across various domains such as engineering, sciences, and health, along with their solutions, is crucial for comprehending processes and conducting theoretical analysis. Nonlinear evolution equations (NLEEs) serve as essential instruments for representing complex physical phenomena and are extensively applied across engineering, mathematical physics, biology, fibre optics, and various other scientific fields1. The exact solution to NLEEs provides valuable insights into comprehending various processes and supports theoretical analysis. The resolution of NLEEs is crucial for addressing various practical challenges across multiple domains. A significant focus within the domain of NLEEs is the nonlinear Schrödinger equation NLSEs2,3. The NLSE serves as a crucial element in quantum mechanics, illustrating the dynamics of quantum systems and the progression of their wave functions throughout time. This has significant consequences for multiple facets of contemporary science and technology. The Schrödinger equation is fundamental in physics, essential for predicting how subatomic particles behave and providing valuable insights into the structure and dynamics of atoms and molecules. The solutions, known as wave functions, indicate the likelihood of locating a particle at a specific position and time.

Nonlinear Schrödinger equation and physical applications

The NLSE is widely recognized as a fundamental mathematical framework for clarifying the mechanisms by which packet waves can be decelerated in various nonlinear wave systems4,5. NLSE have emerged as essential tools for analyzing a variety of physical phenomena across numerous scientific fields. The application of solitons in mechanical engineering can lead to the development of innovative shock-absorbing materials and configurations. Their ability to maintain their form while traveling through a medium renders them effective for reducing impacts and vibrations. Solitons can manifest in a range of nonlinear waveguides and resonators within nanoscale systems. They are analyzed for their potential uses in nanophotonics, where precise manipulation of light at the nanoscale is crucial. The progress in science and technology, along with continuous investigative efforts, has made it essential to employ more complex equations to represent the nonlinear phenomena observed in reality. The NLSE provides insights into the evolution equations applicable in diverse nonlinear scientific fields. This is evident in areas like the symptoms exhibited by lossless symmetric two-stream plasma interactions, which show certain fluctuations taking place6–8.

Unstable nonlinear Schrödinger equation

The UNLSE addresses specific types of fluctuations present in modified wave structures and represents a broader category of various non-linear integrable issues outlined by the NLSEs. The UNLSE can be characterised as a variant of the NLSE, where the functions of time and space are reversed. This equation outlines symptoms that appear within mediums characterised by turbulence or marginal stability. It also addresses specific symptoms in modified wave structures and symbolically represents a broader category of challenges referred to as non-linear integrable problems as defined by the NLSEs9,10. The NLSE has been significant across various domains, beginning with optical fibre communications, optical solitons, Bose-Einstein condensates, and extending to femtosecond pulse duration. A significant number of scholars have emphasised the necessity of exploring explicit solutions across various categories, including solitary waves, solitons, and travelling waves. Soliton solutions have been a focus for 25 years. Soliton solutions that mimic nature can solve almost any engineering problem11,12.

Solitons and advances in nonlinear science

Solitons are resilient, stable, self-sustaining, and persistent solitary waves that retain their form and do not disperse as they move through a medium. The interplay of non-linear dynamics and dispersion leads to the formation of solitons. Numerous important areas within physics and technology rely on these concepts, encompassing fundamental particle physics, oceanography, cosmology, the development of rogue waves, and the precise creation of soliton supercontinuums in ultrafast photonics, condensed matter, and plasma physics. A variety of effective strategies have been established to achieve precise and clearly defined stable soliton solutions for nonlinear physical systems, utilising computer algebra systems like Matlab, Maple, Python or Mathematica13–16. A set of distinct wave packets known as soliton solutions. Solitons allow optical communication devices to transmit large amounts of data across great distances without distortion. Higher-order effects like nonlinear chromatic dispersion and quintuple-power-law, self-phase modulation complicate soliton behaviour and create more complex dynamics. Optical solitons are solitary waves that emerge as solutions to various forms of nonlinear evolution equations. Optical solitons are unique waves that, when traversing a nonlinear medium, maintain their integrity in terms of intensity. This intriguing phenomenon in nonlinear optics plays a vital role in optical fibre communications. The stability of these solitons is maintained through a careful balance between dispersive effects, which aim to broaden the pulse, and nonlinear forces that counteract dispersion. Bright and dark solitons serve as significant instances of optical solitons. Bright solitons represent pulse-like structures that emerge due to the effects of self-focusing nonlinearity as well as dark solitons are characterised by a localised intensity dip and arise within the framework of self-defocusing nonlinearity. These are commonly identified as the solutions of the NLSE17–19.

Results and discussion

This section offers a detailed analysis and interpretation of the results obtained. The CDSPM demonstrates its effectiveness as a reliable and methodical analytical instrument for addressing the UNLSE in the presence of multiplicative white noise. Utilizing the traveling-wave transformation, the governing model is simplified into a dynamical system, enabling an exploration of its qualitative structure via phase portraits, sensitivity analysis, and time-series representations. The system’s dynamical behavior is investigated via sensitivity analysis and quasi periodic behaviour structures. The implementation of external periodic driving results in complex dynamical procedures. Chaotic and quasi-periodic motions emerge within particular parameter ranges, as demonstrated by Poincarè maps, three-dimensional phase projections, two-dimensional phase portraits, and time-series plots (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4). The results confirm the model’s significant sensitivity to changes in parameters. A thorough sensitivity analysis regarding initial conditions (Figs. 5, 6, 7, 8) demonstrates that minor variations in initial data can significantly influence the long-term development of solutions. This confirms the presence of nonlinear sensitivity and highlights the robustness and structural soundness of the generated wave patterns.

From an analytical standpoint, CDSPM produces a diverse array of precise solutions. The equation in question yields trigonometric periodic solutions, as illustrated in Fig. 9, which depict uniformly repeating wave patterns that are crucial in the study of nonlinear wave theory. Further periodic and solitary wave structures related to Eqs. (24), (25), (36), (55), (61), and (62) are depicted in Figs. 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, and 15. The solutions outlined here illustrate stable oscillatory structures connected to plasma physics, nonlinear optics, and associated physical systems. These figures shows a periodic wave structure with continuous solution oscillation in spatial and temporal dimensions. This describes a stable modulated wave train in an optical medium where dispersion and nonlinearity cause recurring intensity patterns, as well as the localized structure has a single wave that maintains its amplitude. This shows an equilibrium between nonlinear self-focusing and dispersive spreading, allowing the pulse to propagate undistorted. Optic communication requires this wave for distortion-free signal transmission. The rational solutions obtained from Eqs. (25), (28), (32), and (33), (Figs. 16, 17, 18, 19) demonstrate localized waveforms marked by algebraic decay. These solutions are highly valuable for simulating rogue-wave phenomena and peak structures in nonlinear dispersive media. The wave seems to be confined both spatially and temporally, resembling rogue waves. In chaotic or unpredictable transmission systems, intense optical pulses can arise from abrupt energy amplification resulting from nonlinear instability as well as the illustration depicts a uniform localized waveform. The results confirm that the system facilitates self-sustained pulse propagation, with nonlinear effects effectively counteracting dispersion, resulting in robust traveling optical structures.

Conclusion

This study employed the CDSPM technique to analyse the UNLSE. In our study, we employed a linear regression technique derived from machine learning. The main objective of regression analysis was to uncover the connections between variables and their specified relationships. The method yields a range of fascinating and precise outcomes, including chaotic behaviour, sensitivity analysis SW, bright, JEF, rational, dark, hyperbolic, and periodic solutions. Moreover, various representations have been demonstrated through 2D and 3D graphs to highlight the physical properties of the solutions. The insights derived from these findings enhance our comprehension of the physical phenomena articulated by the UNLSE. A qualitative analysis of chaos was also conducted. Additionally, a sensitivity analysis incorporating different initial conditions was conducted, revealing the model’s low sensitivity. Ultimately, we reached the conclusion that, unlike earlier studies, our solutions are unique and more comprehensive.

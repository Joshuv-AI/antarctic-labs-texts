Abstract
. In this paper we provide an algebraic characterization of those stratified groups in which boundaries with locally constant normal are locally flat. We show that these groups, which we call hypergenerated , are exactly the stratified groups where embeddings of non-characteristic hypersurfaces are locally bi-Lipschitz. Finally, we extend these results to submanifolds of arbitrary codimension.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we systematically introduce a class of Carnot groups which we call hypergenerated. Namely, a Carnot group is
hypergenerated
if all its vertical hyperplanes are Carnot subgroups. We refer to
Section

2.2
for the definition of Carnot group and vertical hyperplane. Moreover, we refer to
Section

3
for a comprehensive treatment of hypergenerated groups and Lie algebras. We show that the algebraic property of being hypergenerated captures features relevant for both the regularity of minimal boundaries and the metric properties of non-characteristic hypersurfaces. More precisely, we prove the following characterization.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. The following are equivalent:

(1)

the group
\mathbb{G}
is hypergenerated,

(2)

all finite-perimeter sets with locally constant normal are vertical hyperplanes,

(3)

on all smooth non-characteristic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{G}
, the intrinsic distance and the restricted distance are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent.

The framework and results of
Theorem

1.1
naturally extends to a hierarchy that we called
hypergenerated of order
k
, for
k\geq 1
, see
Section

3
.

We refer to
Section

2.3
for the notion of non-characteristic hypersurfaces, intrinsic distance and restricted distance, and to the statement of
Theorem

4.1
for the notion of constant normal. Condition
(2)
deals with the regularity theory for minimal boundaries. The historical approach to this problem has evolved along different paths, fostering the development of the theories of
currents
by Federer and Fleming (see
[
19
]
), of
varifolds
by Almgren (see
[
2
]
) and Allard (see
[
1
]
), and of
sets of finite perimeter
by De Giorgi (see
[
15
]
). However, an important meeting point of these three approaches consists in employing different variations of the so-called
Lipschitz approximation theorem
(see e.g.
[
35
]
). In the language of finite perimeter sets, the latter states that minimal boundaries are close, in measure, to graphs of Lipschitz functions with arbitrary small Lipschitz constant. The distinction between the minimal boundary and the Lipschitz graph is quantified by the so-called
excess
, a local quantity that measures the oscillation of the normal. This modern viewpoint, for which we refer to the monograph
[
25
]
, does not depend on the validity of perimeter monotonicity formulas.
Although the validity of a sub-Riemannian monotonicity formula is still a major open problem, the Lipschitz approximation theorem has been fully generalized in
[
32
,
30
]
to the Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, when
n\geq 2
, via approximation by
intrinsic Lipschitz graphs
. What distinguishes higher-dimensional Heisenberg groups from the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, where only partial results are available, is the presence in the latter of sets with zero excess that are not locally flat, i.e., they are not locally vertical hyperplanes. To avoid these types of phenomena, the authors of
[
33
]
identified a family of stratified groups of step
2
, which they called
plentiful groups
, in which sets with locally constant normal are locally flat, thus extending the Lipschitz approximation theorem to this family of groups. The same notion was recently studied from a control theoretic perspective in
[
24
]
.
The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
in
Theorem

1.1
shows that hypergenerated groups are
exactly
those stratified groups where the sets with locally constant normal are locally vertical hyperplanes. Thus, they represent the right environment for the development of the regularity theory in the setting of stratified groups.

Motivated by the previous discussion, we develop the theory of hypergenerated groups from an algebraic viewpoint. We show that the property of being hypergenerated only depends on the maximal step
2
quotient of the group, see
Corollary

3.4
. Moreover, we provide a characterization of step
2
hypergenerated Lie groups in terms of the image of the Kaplan’s operator, see
Proposition

3.8
. This result reveal a correspondence between the notion of hypergenerated groups and a class of problems
known as
MinRank problems
. The latter are deeply studied from the computational complexity point of view, with applications in cryptanalysis (see
[
12
,
18
]
). We briefly discuss this at the end of
Section

3.1
.

With this algebraic machinery, we prove that the class of hypergenerated group is actually vast, containing stratified groups of arbitrarily large step. This result extends to hypergenerated groups of order
k
, for every
k\geq 1
, see
Proposition

3.20
. Moreover, we classify all indecomposable hypergenerated groups of topological dimension up to
7
.

The property of the group of being hypergenerated can be described purely in terms of distance functions. On a subset of a metric space, one can consider two distance functions: either the distance function of the ambient space restricted to the subspace or the induced length metric. On hypersurfaces inside the Euclidean space, these two distance functions are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent. However, the latter statement in no longer true for Carnot groups. Indeed,
Theorem

1.1
shows that the two distance functions are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent on all non-characteristic hypersurfaces if and only if the Carnot group is hypergenerated. The bi-Lipschitz equivalence of restricted and intrinsic distance functions for smooth non-characteristic hypersurfaces so far was only known for Heisenberg groups (see
[
5
]
).
To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we study weak tangents of contact maps. Following
[
4
]
, we show that the at every point of an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold the weak tangent is unique and is a Carnot group. In analogy with
[
26
]
, we then show that the weak tangent of a contact map between equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds is unique and is a group homomorphism. We apply this result to the inclusions of non-characteristic surfaces inside of hypergenerated Carnot groups to get the implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
in
Theorem

1.1
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we recall some basic properties and terminology of Carnot groups and sub-Riemannian manifolds. In
Section

3
we introduce and study hypergenerated groups of order
k
. In
Section

4
we prove the implication
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
of
Theorem

1.1
, whereas
Section

5
is dedicated to the proof of the implication
(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds

For an introduction to sub-Riemannian manifolds, we refer to
[
16
,
29
]
.
A (constant rank)
distribution

\Delta
on
M
is a smooth subbundle of the tangent bundle
TM
. In the following, if
M
is a smooth manifold, we denote by
\Gamma(TM)
the family of smooth vector fields defined over
M
, and by
\Gamma(\Delta)
the family of vector fields in
\Gamma(TM)
that are tangent to
\Delta
.
For all
p\in M
, we define
\Delta^{1}_{p}:=\Delta_{p}
and

(2.1)

\Delta^{k}_{p}:=\mathrm{span}\{[X_{1}\ldots[X_{j-1},X_{j}]\ldots](p)\mid j\leq k,\,X_{i}\in\Gamma(\Delta)\},\qquad\text{for every }k>1.

A distribution
\Delta
is
bracket-generating
if, for all
p\in M
, there exists
k
such that
\Delta^{k}_{p}=T_{p}M
. We call
\dim(\Delta_{p})
the
rank
of
\Delta
at
p
and we say that a distribution
\Delta
has rank
m
if
m=\dim(\Delta_{p})
for all
p\in M
. We say that a distribution
\Delta
is
equiregular
if the integer
\dim(\Delta^{k}_{p})
is constant in
p
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
A
sub-Riemannian manifold
is a connected manifold equipped with a bracket-generating distribution
\Delta
, with a Riemannian metric
g
defined on
\Delta
, and with the
Carnot-Carathéodory distance

(2.2)

\begin{split}d_{cc}(x,y):=\inf\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))}\mathrm{d}t\,\big|\,\gamma:[0,1]\to M\text{ absolutely continuous; }\\
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\Delta_{\gamma(t)}\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,1];\,\gamma(0)=x;\,\gamma(1)=y\Big\}.\end{split}

Since
\Delta
is bracket-generating, the
Chow-Rashevskii connectivity theorem
(see
[
14
]
and
[
16
, Chapter 3]
) ensures that
d_{cc}
is a finite distance. We say that a sub-Riemannian manifold with distribution
\Delta
is
equiregular
if
\Delta
is equiregular.

Definition 2.1

(Stratified basis)
.

Let
M
be an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold with distribution
\Delta
of rank
m
. Let
p\in M
and
U\subseteq M
be a neighborhood of
p
. We say that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\Gamma(TU)
is a
stratified basis
for the distribution
\Delta
at
U
if:

(1)

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
is an orthonormal frame for
\Delta
;

(2)

for all
r\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
and for all
j\in\{r+1,\ldots,n\}
the vector
X_{j}
is an iterated Lie bracket of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
;

(3)

if
i<j
, then
w_{i}\leq w_{j}
, where

w_{j}:=\min\left\{k\ |\ X_{j}=[X_{i_{1}}\ldots[X_{i_{k-1}},X_{i_{k}}]\ldots]\},\ i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\right\}.

We refer to the integer
w_{j}
as the
weight
of
X_{j}
.

2.2.
Carnot groups

For a thorough introduction on Carnot groups, we refer to
[
11
,
23
,
16
,
36
]
.
A
stratified Lie algebra of step
s
, with
s\in\mathbb{N}
, is a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, together with a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

such that the vector spaces
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
satisfy

V_{i}=[V_{1},V_{i-1}]\quad\text{for}\ i=1,\dots,s-1,\quad V_{s}\neq\{0\},\quad[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}.

We refer to such decomposition as a
stratification of
\mathfrak{g}
, to
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}
as the
strata
(or
layers
) of the stratification, and to
m\coloneq\dim(V_{1})
as the
rank
of
\mathfrak{g}
. A
stratified group of step
s
and rank
m
is a simply connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
whose associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is stratified of step
s
and rank
m
. In order to make sense of the notation used in the rest of the paper, we stress that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to a Lie group
\mathbb{G}
is understood as its tangent space at the identity element
\mathrm{id}\in\mathbb{G}
. If
p\in G
, we denote by
L_{p}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
the
left translation
by
p
, i.e., the diffeomorphism
q\mapsto p\cdot q
.
Once a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to a stratified group
\mathbb{G}
is fixed, we can identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via
exponential coordinates of the first type
, i.e.,

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{G},\qquad(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\mapsto\exp(y_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+y_{n}X_{n}),

where
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{G}
is the exponential map, which is a global diffeomorphism since
\mathbb{G}
is nilpotent and simply connected (cf.
[
16
, Theorem 8.4.7]
). We say that
H\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is a
vertical
k
-codimensional space
, for
k\geq 1
, if there exists a
k
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that

(2.3)

H=\exp\left(P\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\right).

A vertical
1
-codimensional space is also called
vertical hyperplane
.

We recall that the push-forward
\Phi_{*}\mathcal{L}^{n}
of the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Haar measure on
\mathbb{G}
(cf.
[
16
, Theorem 8.4.7]
). Hereafter, the measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
also denotes an Haar measure on a stratified group
\mathbb{G}
, where the identification via exponential coordinates of the first type remains understood. If
E\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is a measurable set, we often use the shorthand notation
\lvert E\rvert
in place of
\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)
.
If
\mathbb{G}
is a stratified group with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
, we define the
dilation of factor

\lambda>0
as the unique Lie group automorphism
\delta_{\lambda}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
such that

(2.4)

\left(\mathrm{d}\delta_{\lambda}\right)_{\mathrm{id}}v=\lambda^{i}v,\quad\text{for all $i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}$ and $v\in V_{i}$.}

We refer to the map
\delta\colon\lambda\mapsto\delta_{\lambda}
as the
dilation of
\mathbb{G}
. If
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{H}
are stratified groups, with dilations
\delta^{\mathbb{G}}
and
\delta^{\mathbb{H}}
respectively, we say that a map
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{H}
is
homogeneous
if

f(\delta_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{G}}(p))=\delta_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{H}}(f(p))\quad\text{for all $p\in\mathbb{G}$ and $\lambda>0$.}

Let
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
be a Lie algebra associated with a stratified group
\mathbb{G}
. An inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined on
V_{1}
extends by left-translations to a Riemannian metric
g
defined on the distribution

(2.5)

\Delta\coloneq\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{G}}\mathrm{d}L_{p}(V_{1}).

More explicitly, if
v,w\in\Delta_{p}
, then
g(v,w)\coloneq\langle\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}(v),\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}(w)\rangle
. Being
\Delta
bracket-generating and equiregular, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
endows
\mathbb{G}
with the structure of equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold. We refer to this distinguished class of sub-Riemannian manifolds as
Carnot groups
.

We stress that, in a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
with dilation
\delta
, the Carnot-Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
, defined by (
2.2
), satisfies the following:

•

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(L_{p}(q_{1}),L_{p}(q_{2}))=d_{\mathrm{cc}}(q_{1},q_{2})
for all
p,q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
,
(left-invariant)

•

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(\delta_{\lambda}(q_{1}),\delta_{\lambda}(q_{2}))=\lambda d_{\mathrm{cc}}(q_{1},q_{2})
for all
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
(one-homogeneous)

Finally, we denote the open balls centered at
p\in G
of radius
r>0
by
B_{r}(p)\coloneq\{q\in\mathbb{G}:d_{\mathrm{cc}}(q,p)<r\}
.

2.3.
Embedded surfaces in Carnot groups

In the following, we consider a
surface
to be a
C^{1}
-manifold
S\subseteq M
embedded in a smooth manifold
M
, and a
hypersurface
to be a surface of codimension
1
.
Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group with distribution
\Delta
defined as in (
2.5
). If
k<\dim(V_{1})
, an embedded
k
-codimensional surface
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is
non-characteristic
if

T_{p}S+\Delta_{p}=T_{p}\mathbb{G}\quad\text{for all $p\in S$.}

In this case, the
horizontal tangent distribution

\Delta_{S}
, defined by setting

\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{p}\coloneq\Delta_{p}\cap T_{p}S\qquad\text{for every $p\in S$},

is a distribution of constant rank
\dim(V_{1})-k
. We consider on an embedded surface
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
the following distance functions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group, with Carnot-Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
. Consider an embedded surface
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
.

•

The
restricted distance

d_{r}:S\times S\to[0,\infty)
is the restriction of
d_{\mathrm{cc}}\colon G\times G\to[0,\infty)
to
S\times S
.

•

The
intrinsic distance

d_{i}:S\times S\to[0,\infty]
is defined, for every
p,q\in S
, by setting

d_{i}(p,q):=\inf_{\gamma}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))}\ \mathrm{d}t\Big\},

where the infimum is taken among all absolutely continuous curves
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to S
, with
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{\gamma(t)}
for a.e.
t\in[0,1]
, such that
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
.

As discussed in
Section

5
, whilst
d_{r}
is always a finite distance on

, the intrinsic distance

, although being positive, symmetric and satisfying the triangle inequality, may not be finite (see
Proposition

5.2
).

3.
Algebraic structure of hypergenerated groups

In this section, we formally introduce the central concept of this work: hypergenerated groups. We then explore their key algebraic properties, provide several examples, and present an effective method to determine whether a given stratified Lie group is hypergenerated.

Definition 3.1

(Hypergenerated algebras and groups)
.

A stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
of rank
m
is
hypergenerated of order
k
if every linear subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
, with
\dim(P)=m-k
, satisfies

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq\Lie(P),

where
\Lie(E)
denotes the smallest Lie sub-algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing the subset
E\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. When
k=1
, we simply say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
hypergenerated
. An
hypergenerated group of order
k
is a stratified group whose Lie algebra is hypergenerated of order
k
.

By definition, every stratified Lie algebra is hypergenerated of order
0
.

Remark 3.2
.

We stress that the property of being hypergenerated of order
k
is invariant under isomorphisms of Lie algebras. In particular, it does not depend on the stratification. Indeed, assume that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{s}
are two isomorphic stratified Lie algebras of rank
m
and step
s
, and that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is hypergenerated of order
k
. Then (see
[
23
, Proposition 2.17]
), there exists a Lie algebra isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
such that
\varphi(V_{1})=W_{1}
. Fix
P\subseteq W_{1}
, with
\dim(P)=m-k
, then
P^{\prime}\coloneq\varphi^{-1}(W)
is a linear subspace of
V_{1}
such that
\dim(P^{\prime})=m-k
. Therefore

[\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{2}]=[\varphi(\mathfrak{g}_{1}),\varphi(\mathfrak{g}_{1})]=\varphi\left([V_{1},V_{1}]\right)\subseteq\Lie(\varphi(P^{\prime}))=\varphi(\Lie(P^{\prime}))=\Lie(P),

which proves that
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is hypergenerated of order
k
as well.

3.1.
A characterization of hypergenerated Lie algebras

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
be a stratified Lie algebra of rank
m
, and
k\geq 0
. The following are equivalent

(i)

\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
k
;

(ii)

every subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
, with
\dim(P)=m-k
, satisfies
V_{2}\subseteq\Lie(P)
.

Proof.

The implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is trivial. Assume now that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
(ii)
, and fix
P\subseteq V_{1}
, with
\dim(P)=m-k
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
, it is sufficient to prove that
V_{h}\subseteq\Lie(P)
for every
2\leq h\leq s
. We proceed by induction on
h
. The base case
h=2
is true by assumption. Fix
2\leq h\leq s-1
, and
assume that
V_{n}\subseteq\Lie(V)
for every
2\leq n\leq h
. Consider a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{m-k}
of
P
and extend it to a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
of
V_{1}
. Since
V_{h}\subseteq\Lie(P)
and
h\geq 2
, then

V_{h}=\mathrm{span}\{\,[X_{i},Y]\,\colon\,1\leq i\leq m-k,\,Y\in V_{h-1}\cap\Lie(P)\,\}.

Therefore, since
\mathfrak{g}
is stratified, then

V_{h+1}=\mathrm{span}\{\,[X_{j},[X_{i},Y]]\,\colon\,1\leq j\leq m,\,1\leq i\leq m-k,\,Y\in V_{h-1}\cap\Lie(P)\,\}.

We now fix
1\leq j\leq m
,
1\leq i\leq m-k
, and
Y\in V_{h-1}\cap\Lie(P)
. By the Jacobi identity we get

(3.1)

[X_{j},[X_{i},Y]]=[X_{i},[X_{j},Y]]+[[X_{j},X_{i}],Y]].

We recall that
Y\in\Lie(P)
. We observe that
X_{i}\in P
and that
[X_{j},X_{i}]\in V_{2}
, whence
[X_{j},X_{i}]\in\Lie(P)
by hypothesis. Moreover,
[X_{j},Y]\in V_{h}
and therefore
[X_{j},Y]\in\Lie(P)
by the inductive hypothesis. We conclude that the right hand side of (
3.1
) belongs to
\Lie(P)
, and so the left hand side does. Since elements of that form span
V_{h+1}
, then
V_{h+1}\subseteq\Lie(P)
and the proof follows by induction.
∎

We recall that the
lower central series

(\mathfrak{g}^{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
is the collection of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
defined inductively by

\mathfrak{g}^{1}\coloneq\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}^{h+1}\coloneq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{h}],\quad\text{for any $h\geq 1$.}

The following is a consequence of
Proposition

3.3
, together with the fact that, if
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
is a stratified Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{3}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
is a stratified Lie algebra of step
2
.

Corollary 3.4
.

A stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
k
if and only if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{3}
is hypergenerated of order
k
.

We have now reduced the problem of characterizing hypergenerated Lie algebras to the case of step
2
stratified Lie algebras. In this regard, it is convenient to introduce the
Kaplan’s operator
.
If
V
is a vector space, we denote by
\mathrm{Skew}(V)
the set of skew-symmetric bi-linear forms on the vector space
V
. Given a stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
of step at least
2
, we define the
Kaplan’s operator

\mathbf{J}
(cf.
[
21
]
) to be the linear map
\mathbf{J}\colon V_{2}^{*}\to\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1})
such that

\mu\mapsto\mathbf{J}_{\mu},\quad\mathbf{J}_{\mu}(v,w)\coloneq\mu([v,w]),\quad\text{for every $\mu\in V_{2}^{*}$.}

Note that, since
V_{1}
Lie-generates
\mathfrak{g}
, the map
\mathbf{J}
is injective.
We recall some basic terminology about skew-symmetric bi-linear forms (cf.
[
20
, Section 6.2]
). If
\omega\in\mathrm{Skew}(V)
, the
rank
of
\omega
is defined by setting
\mathrm{rank}(\omega)\coloneq\dim(V)-\dim(\ker(\omega))
,
where

\ker(\omega)=\{v\in V\,:\,\omega(v,w)=0\text{ for every }w\in V\}.

The rank of a skew-symmetric bi-linear form is always an even number. If
\ker(\omega)
is trivial, we say that
\omega
is
non-degenerate
. A linear subspace
P\subseteq V
is said to be
\omega
-
isotropic
if
\omega(v,w)=0
for every
v,w\in P
.

Remark 3.5
.

There exists a
\omega
-isotropic subspace
P\subseteq V
of codimension
k
if and only if
\mathrm{rank}(\omega)\leq 2k
.

Remark 3.6
.

We claim that a subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
Lie-generates
V_{2}
if and only if, for every
\mu\in V_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
,
P
is not
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
-isotropic. Indeed, from the definition of
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
, the subspace
P
is
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
-isotropic if and only if
\mu\in[P,P]^{\perp}\coloneq\{\eta\in V_{2}^{*}:\eta([P,P])=0\}
. Moreover,
[P,P]^{\perp}=\{0\}
if and only
[P,P]=V_{2}
, i.e., if and only if
P
Lie-generates
V_{2}
.

Given a stratified Lie algebra of step
2
, we want to rewrite the property of being hypergenerated in terms of the rank of the bi-linear forms appearing in the image of its Kaplan’s operator. Those
2
-step stratified Lie algebras for which
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
is non-degenerate for every
\mu\in V_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
have been widely considered in the literature. They were firstly introduced by Guy Métivier in
[
27
]
to study the hypo-ellipticity properties of homogeneous operators in stratified groups. We propose a generalized version of his definition.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a stratified Lie algebra of step
2
. We define the
Métivier order
of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted with
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})
, to be the number

(3.2)

\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneq\min\{\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{J}_{\mu}):\mu\in V_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}.

A stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of step
2
is
Métivier
if
\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})
.

We stress that the Métivier order is always an even number between
2
and
\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})
. Our interest in the notion of Métivier order is motivated by the following observation.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a stratified Lie algebra of step
2
, and
k\geq 0
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
k
if and only if
2k<\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Assume that
2k<\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})
and fix
P\subseteq V_{1}
of co-dimension
k
. For every
\mu\in V^{*}_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, since
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{J}_{\mu})\geq\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})>2k
, by
Remark

3.5
the subspace
P
is not isotropic for
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
. Therefore, from
Remark

3.6
, the subspace
P
Lie-generates
V_{2}
. Thus
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
k
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})\leq 2k
and fix
\mu\in V_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{J}_{\mu})\leq 2k
. Then, by
Remark

3.5
, there exists a subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
, of co-dimension
k
, isotropic for
\mathbf{J}_{\mu}
. By
Remark

3.6
, the subspace
P
does not Lie-generate
V_{2}
, therefore
\mathfrak{g}
is not hypergenerated of order
k
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We claim that, if
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
k
and step
s\geq 2
, then
\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})\geq 2(k+1)
. Indeed, in view of
Proposition

3.3
, we can assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is stratified of step
2
. Since
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})
is an even number smaller than
\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
Proposition

3.8
implies that
2(k+1)\leq\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})
.

From
Proposition

3.8
, we get the following consequence.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Métivier Lie algebra of rank
2m
, then
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated of order
m-1
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be stratified Lie algebras, and let
\mathfrak{h}
be an homogeneous ideal of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
(i.e.
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}=(V_{1}\cap\mathfrak{h})\oplus\dots\oplus(V_{s}\cap\mathfrak{h})
). The following hold:

(i)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is hypergenerated of order
k
if and only if both
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are hypergenerated of order
k
.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is hypergenerated of order
k
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\mathfrak{h}
is hypergenerated of order
k
.

Proof.

In view of
Proposition

3.3
, it is not restrictive to assume that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are stratified Lie algebras of step at most
2
.

(i)
If both
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are Abelian, the result is straightforward. If
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are both non-abelian, it follows from (
3.2
) that
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\min\{\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}),\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\}
. Finally, if only
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is abelian, then
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
. In both the latter cases, the result follows from
Proposition

3.8
.

(ii)
The stratification of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\mathfrak{h}
is given by
\mathfrak{g}_{1}/\mathfrak{h}=(V_{1}+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}\oplus(V_{2}+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
is the stratification of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Let
(P+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}
, with
P\subseteq V_{1}
, be a subspace of
(V_{1}+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}
of codimension
k
. Then, the subspace
P
has codimension
k
in
V_{1}
and

(V_{2}+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}=([P,P]+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}=[(P+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h},(P+\mathfrak{h})/\mathfrak{h}],

where we used that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is hypergenerated of order
k
. ∎

We stress that the order of a stratified Lie algebra of step
2
is precisely the quantity
\widetilde{k}
appearing in the statement of
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
about the abnormal set. By combining the result with
Proposition

3.8
, we get the following:

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group of step
2
, with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
. Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is hypergenerated of order
k
, then the abnormal set

\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\coloneq\bigcup\,\{\exp(\Lie(P)):P\subseteq V_{1},\,[P,V_{1}]\neq V_{2}\},

is contained in an algebraic variety of codimension at least
2k+3
.

Proposition

3.3
and
Proposition

3.8
convert the problem of determining whether a stratified Lie algebra of rank
m
is hypergenerated of a certain order
k
to the problem of determining if a given linear subspace
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
has a non-trivial element of rank at most
2k
. This kind of problems are known as
MinRank problems
and are deeply studied from the computational complexity point of view, with applications in cryptanalysis (cf.
[
12
,
18
]
). The two problems are actually equivalent in the following sense: on the one hand, given a stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
of rank
m
and step
s
, we get
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
as the image of the Kaplan’s operator. On the other hand, given a linear subspace
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, there exists a stratified Lie algebra of rank
m
and step
2
whose image of the Kaplan’s operator is
S
. The construction of such Lie algebra is given by the following remark.

Remark 3.13
.

Given a subspace
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we define the step
2
stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{S}\coloneq\mathbb{R}^{m}\oplus S^{*}
, where
S^{*}
is the dual space of
S
, as follows: for every
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the Lie bracket
[v,w]\in S^{*}
is formally defined by setting

[v,w](\omega)\coloneq\omega(v,w)\qquad\text{for every $\omega\in\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})$.}

With this definition, it is immediate to check that the Kaplan’s operator becomes the natural isomorphism
\mathbf{J}\colon S^{**}\to S
between a finite-dimensional vector space and its double dual.

The latter discussion suggests that the existence of an easy criterion for the hypergenerating property is unreasonable, since it can be converted to an instance of the MinRank problem, which is known to be NP-hard (see
[
8
]
). The MinRank problem for
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
(identified with
\mathfrak{so}_{m}
, i.e., the space of
m\times m
skew-symmetric matrices) has the same complexity as the general case
S\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. Indeed, every linear subspace in
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
can be mapped in a linear subspace of
\mathfrak{so}_{2m}
through the map

f\colon A\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&-A\\
A^{T}&0\end{pmatrix},

and the rank of
A
is less than
k
if and only if the rank of
f(A)
is less than
2k
. To conclude, one can implement algorithms that determines wether a given stratified Lie algebra is hypergenerated of order
k
. However, the best known algorithms can only give the answer in exponential time with respect to the size of the input.

3.2.
Hypergenerated groups up to dimension
7

We present all hypergenerated groups of topological dimension up to
7
. In view of
Proposition

3.11
, we restrict to
indecomposable
Lie groups, i.e., those that are not the direct product of non-trivial stratified groups. We recall that stratified groups of step
1
are hypergenerated (of order
k
for every
k\geq 0
). We then focus on indecomposable stratified groups of step
s\geq 2
. As a consequence of
Remark

3.9
, hypergenerated groups of step

must have rank at least
2
, thus topological dimension at least
5
. By the classification of stratified Lie groups provided by
[
22
]
, there are only six indecomposable, hypergenerated Lie groups of step

and topological dimension between
5
and

.

A direct computation of the image of their Kaplan’s operators shows that the following four stratified groups of step
2
are Métivier groups. In view of
Corollary

3.10
, they are hypergenerated.

Example 3.14
.

The only indecomposable hypergenerated group of dimension
5
is the second Heisenberg group. Its associated stratified Lie algebra, of dimension
5
, rank
4
and step
2
, is

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4},T\}

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{3},X_{4}]=T.

Example 3.15
.

The only indecomposable hypergenerated group of dimension

is the stratified group denoted by
N_{6,4,4a}
. The non-trivial bracket of its associated Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4},T_{1},T_{2}\}

of dimension

, rank

and step

are

[X_{1},X_{3}]=[X_{2},X_{4}]=T_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad[X_{1},X_{4}]=[X_{3},X_{2}]=T_{2}.

Example 3.16
.

The third Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is the only indecomposable stratified group of dimension
7
that is hypergenerated of order
2
. The only non-trivial bracket relations of its associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{6},T\}
of dimension
7
, rank
6
and step
2
are

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{3},X_{4}]=[X_{5},X_{6}]=T.

Example 3.17
.

We denote by

the stratified group with associated Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{4},T_{1},\ldots,T_{3}\}

of dimension

, rank

and step

whose only non-trivial bracket relations are

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{4},X_{3}]=T_{1},\quad[X_{1},X_{3}]=[X_{2},X_{4}]=T_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad[X_{1},X_{4}]=[X_{3},X_{2}]=T_{3}.

Both
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and

.

The latter two examples are not Métivier groups.

Example 3.18
.

We consider the stratified group
27B
associated to the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{5},T_{1},T_{2}\}

of dimension
7
, rank
5
and step

whose only non-trivial braket relations are

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{3},X_{4}]=T_{1},\quad[X_{1},X_{5}]=[X_{2},X_{3}]=T_{2}.

Since its rank is odd, the stratified group

is not a Métivier group. Nevertheless, as proved in

Example 3.19
.

The only indecomposable hypergenerated group of step
3
and dimension

is the group denoted by
137A_{1}
. The only non-trivial bracket relations of its associated Lie algebra

of dimension

, rank

and step

are

[X_{1},X_{3}]=[X_{2},X_{4}]=T_{1},\quad[X_{1},X_{4}]=[X_{3},X_{2}]=T_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad[X_{1},T_{1}]=[X_{2},T_{2}]=S.

It is easy to see that

is isomorphic to the Lie algebra associated to

, which is hypergenerated. Thus, by
Corollary

3.4
, the Lie algebra

3.3.
Existence of hypergenerated Lie algebras of large step

We conclude this section by discussing how the property of being hypergenerated of order
k
does not put a constrain on the step of the Lie algebra. We first focus on the case
k=1
.

Proposition 3.20
.

For every
m\geq 4
and
s\geq 1
there exists a hypergenerated Lie algebra of rank
m
and step
s
.

Proposition

3.20
follows from the following remarkable example.

Example 3.21
.

Consider the free-nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
of rank
3
and step
s
(see
[
23
, Example 2.5]
), together with a stratification
\mathfrak{f}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
. Fix a basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}
of
V_{1}
and define a linear map
\phi\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
by setting
\phi(X_{1})=[X_{2},X_{3}]
,
\phi(X_{2})=[X_{3},X_{1}]
, and
\phi(X_{3})=[X_{1},X_{2}]
. By
[
37
, Proposition 3]
, the map
\phi
extends to a derivation
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{f}
(i.e.
\widetilde{\phi}([Y_{1},Y_{2}])=[\widetilde{\phi}(Y_{1}),Y_{2}]+[Y_{1},\widetilde{\phi}(Y_{2})]
for every
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathfrak{f}
). We then consider the semi-direct sum by
\widetilde{\phi}
, i.e., the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\coloneq\mathfrak{f}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with Lie brackets given, for every

Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathfrak{f}
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, by

[(Y_{1},t_{1}),(Y_{2},t_{2})]_{\mathfrak{g}}=([Y_{1},Y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{f}}+t_{1}\widetilde{\phi}(Y_{2})-t_{2}\widetilde{\phi}(Y_{1}),0).

Since
\widetilde{\phi}(V_{1})\subseteq V_{2}
, we obtain that
\mathfrak{g}
is again a stratified Lie algebra with stratification
\mathfrak{g}=(V_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R})\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{g}
is a stratified Lie algebra of rank
4
and step
s
. Moreover, it is immediate to check that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{3}
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra in
Example

3.17
, which is hypergenerated. By
Corollary

3.4
, we conclude that
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated as well.

Remark 3.22
.

We stress another property of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defined in
Example

3.21
. For every
1
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq(V_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R})
, the stratified Lie algebra
\Lie(P)=P\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
has the same rank, step, and dimension of the free-nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
of rank
3
and step
s
, therefore
\Lie(P)
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{f}
. We conclude that all stratified Lie algebras in the family

\{\Lie(P)\,:\,P\subseteq(V_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}),\,\dim(P)=3\}

are isomorphic.

Proof of
Proposition

3.20
.

Fix
m\geq 4
and
s\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of rank
4
and step
s
given by
Example

3.21
. Then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-4}
has rank
m
and step
s
. Moreover, by
Proposition

3.11
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is hypergenerated.
∎

Next, we discuss the case of hypergenerated Lie algebras of order higher than
1
. We prove the following fact.

Proposition 3.23
.

For every
k\geq 2
and
s\geq 2
, there exists an hypergenerated Lie algebra of step
s
, and order
k
.

We obtain the Lie algebra in the statement of
Proposition

3.23
as some quotient of a
free-metabelian Lie algebra
, which we now introduce.

Definition 3.24
.

Fix
s,m\in\mathbb{N}
. The
free-metabelian Lie algebra of step
s
, generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
, is the stratified Lie algebra

\mathfrak{m}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{s}
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
is a basis of
V_{1}
and, for every
2\leq k\leq s
, a basis of
V_{k}
is given by the formal elements

(3.3)

(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}})\quad\text{with $1\leq i_{h}\leq m,\,i_{1}>i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}$}

and the only non-trivial brackets relations between elements of the basis are given by

(1)

[X_{i},X_{j}]=(X_{i},X_{j})
if
i>j
,

(2)

[(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}}),X_{j}]=(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}},X_{j})
if
2\leq k\leq s-1
and
i_{k}<j
,

(3)

[(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}}),X_{j}]=(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{h}},X_{j},X_{i_{h+1}},\dots,X_{i_{k}})
if
2\leq k\leq s-1
and
i_{h}\leq j\leq i_{h+1}
for some
2\leq h\leq k
,

(4)

[(X_{i_{1}},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}}),X_{j}]=(X_{i_{1}},X_{j},X_{i_{2}},\dots,X_{i_{k}})-[(X_{i_{2}},X_{j},X_{i_{3}},\dots,X_{i_{k}}),X_{i_{1}}]
if
2\leq k\leq s-1
and
i_{2}>j
.

We also refer to such Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
as the
free-metabelian Lie algebra of step
s
and rank
m
.

Remark 3.25
.

In the latter definition,
\mathfrak{m}
is indeed a Lie algebra (see
[
10
,
13
]
), in particular the Jacobi identity is satisfied. Moreover, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
is
metabelian
(or
2
-step solvable
), i.e.,
[\mathfrak{m}^{2},\mathfrak{m}^{2}]=\{0\}
. We claim that

(3.4)

[[Y,Z_{1}],Z_{2}]=[[Y,Z_{2}],Z_{1}]\quad\text{for every $Y\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}$ and $Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}$.}

Indeed, by the Jacobi identity,
[[Y,Z_{1}],Z_{2}]-[[Y,Z_{2}],Z_{1}]=[[Z_{2},Z_{1}],Y]\in[\mathfrak{m}^{2},\mathfrak{m}^{2}]=\{0\}
.

We consider free-metabelian Lie algebras because it is easy to compute the dimension of each layer of its stratification, as well as estimating the growth of ideals generated by subspaces of the second layer. This is clarified by the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
be the free-metabelian Lie algebra of step
s
and rank
m
, we define
d_{k}^{m}\coloneq\dim(V_{k})
. Then

(3.5)

d_{k}^{m}=(k-1)\binom{m+k-2}{k}\quad\text{for every $2\leq k\leq s$.}

Proof.

It follows by computing the number of elements in
(
3.3
)
. More precisely, for every
1\leq h\leq m-1
, the elements in
(
3.3
)
with
i_{2}=m-h
are

h\binom{h+k-2}{h}.

Therefore

\displaystyle d_{k}^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}h\binom{h+k-2}{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}h\binom{h+k-1}{h}-\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}h\binom{h+k-2}{h-1}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\binom{m+k-2}{m-1}-\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}\binom{h+k-2}{h-1}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\binom{m+k-2}{m-1}-\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}\binom{h+k-1}{h-1}+\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}\binom{h+k-2}{h-2}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\binom{m+k-2}{m-1}-\binom{m+k-2}{m-2}

\displaystyle=(k-1)\binom{m+k-2}{k}\,.\qed

Remark 3.27
.

From (
3.5
), we get that
\dim(V_{2})=\binom{m}{2}=\dim(\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1}))
, therefore the Kaplan’s operator
\mathbf{J}\colon V_{2}^{*}\to\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1})
defines an isomorphism. We infer that, for every subspace
S\leq\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1})
, there exists a unique subspace
W\subseteq V_{2}
, with
\dim(W)=\dim(\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1}))-\dim(S)
, such that
\mathbf{J}(W^{\perp})=S
, where
W^{\perp}\coloneq\{\varphi\in V^{*}_{2}:\varphi(W)=0\}
is the annihilator of
W
.

Lemma 3.28
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
be the free-metabelian Lie algebra of step
s
, generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
, and
W
be a subspace of
V_{2}
. We denote by
I(W)
be the ideal of
\mathfrak{m}
generated by
W
and we define
c_{k}^{m}(W)\coloneq\dim(I(W)\cap V_{k})
. Then

(3.6)

c_{k}^{m}(W)\leq\dim(W)\binom{m+k-3}{k-2}.

Proof.

Let
W_{1},\dots,W_{\dim(W)}
be a basis of
W
. Since
W\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, by (
3.4
) and the fact that
\mathfrak{m}
is stratified and generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
, we get that elements of the form

[[\cdots[W_{j},X_{i_{1}}],\cdots],X_{i_{k-2}}]\quad\text{with $1\leq j\leq\dim(W)$ and $i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k-2}$}

generate
I(W)\cap V_{k}
. The result follows by computing the number of those elements.
∎

We are finally ready for the final proof of this section:

Proof of
Proposition

3.23
.

Fix
k\geq 2
,
s\geq 2
, and
m
to be chosen large enough, so that

(3.7)

2km-2k^{2}-k<\frac{(m+s-2)(m-1)}{s},

is satisfied. Consider the free-metabelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
of step
s
and rank
m
. Following the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 1]
in the skew-symmetric setting, we can select a linear subspace
S\subseteq\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1})
, with
\dim(S)=(m-2k)(m-2k-1)/2
, such that all non-zero elements of
S
have rank at least
2k+2
. By
Remark

3.27
, there exists a linear subspace
W\subseteq V_{2}
such that
\mathbf{J}(W^{\perp})=S
, where
\mathbf{J}
is the Kaplan’s operator of
\mathfrak{m}
, and

(3.8)

\dim(W)=\dim(\mathrm{Skew}(V_{1}))-\dim(S)=\binom{m}{2}-\binom{m-2k}{2}=2km-2k^{2}-k.

Denote by
I\coloneq I(W)
the ideal in
\mathfrak{m}
generated by
W
. Since
W
is dilation invariant (i.e. invariant under the maps defined in (
2.4
)), we get that
I
is dilation invariant as well. Therefore, dilation maps commute with the quotient map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m}/I
and the structure of stratified Lie algebra is preserved. The stratification is given by:

\mathfrak{m}/I=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}/W\oplus V_{3}/(I\cap V_{3})\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}/(I\cap V_{s})\,.

We claim that
\mathfrak{m}/I
is hypergenerated of order
k
. Indeed, the image of the Kaplan’s operator
\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}
of
\mathfrak{m}/I
is

\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}\left(\left(V_{2}/W\right)^{*}\right)=\mathbf{J}(W^{\perp})=S,

where we used the natural identification
\left(V_{2}/W\right)^{*}\cong W^{\perp}\subseteq V_{2}^{*}
and the fact that
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{m}/I
have the same first stratum. Since all non-zero elements of
S
have rank at least
2k+2
, then
\mathrm{order}(\mathfrak{m}/I)\geq 2k+2
. By
Proposition

3.8
we get that
\mathfrak{m}/I
is hypergenerated of order
k
.

It is left to prove that
\mathfrak{m}/I
is stratified of step
s
. This is the case if and only if
\dim\left(V_{s}/(I\cap V_{s})\right)>0
, i.e.,
\dim(V_{s})>\dim(I\cap V_{s})
. By combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), it is sufficient to verify that

\dim(W)\binom{m+s-3}{s-2}<(s-1)\binom{m+s-2}{s}.

In view of (
3.8
), and after some easy computations, the latter inequality is equivalent to (
3.7
). We conclude that
\mathfrak{m}/I
is stratified of step
s
. The statement is then proved by setting
\mathfrak{g}\coloneq\mathfrak{m}/I
. ∎

4.
Hypersurfaces with locally constant normal

In this section we show that the class of hypergenerated groups constitutes the correct environment to deal with regularity issues for perimeter minimizers in stratified Groups. We briefly recall some basic preliminaries, for which we refer to
[
36
]
. In the following, we fix a basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
of
the first layer
V_{1}
of the stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{s}
, and we endow
\mathfrak{g}
with a left-invariant Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for which
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
is an orthonormal frame. Moreover, we identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via exponential coordinates of the first type.
We fix an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
. A measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is
of locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter
in
\Omega
, or equivalently a
\mathbb{G}
-Caccioppoli set
in
\Omega
, if

(4.1)

\sup\left\{\int_{E}\divv_{\mathbb{G}}(\varphi)\,dx\,:\,\varphi\in\Gamma_{c}(\tilde{\Omega},\Delta),\,\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\leq 1\right\}<+\infty,

for all open set
\tilde{\Omega}\Subset\Omega
. Here and hereafter, the set
\Gamma_{c}(\tilde{\Omega},\Delta)
denotes the family of smooth horizontal vector fields which are compactly supported in
\Omega
, while
\divv_{\mathbb{G}}
is the
horizontal divergence
, defined by setting

\displaystyle\divv_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}X_{j}\right):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}\varphi_{j}.

In addition,
E
is of
finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter
in
\Omega
if (
4.1
) holds with
\tilde{\Omega}=\Omega
.
As in the Euclidean setting, if
E
is a
\mathbb{G}
-Caccioppoli set in
\Omega
, Riesz theorem implies the existence of a radon measure
P_{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot,\Omega)
on
\Omega
and of a
P_{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot,\Omega)
-a.e. unique measurable horizontal vector field
\nu^{\mathbb{G}}
satisfying

\left\langle\nu^{\mathbb{G}},\nu^{\mathbb{G}}\right\rangle=1,

P_{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot,\Omega)
-a.e. in
\in\Omega
and

\int_{E}\divv_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\langle\nu^{\mathbb{G}},\varphi\rangle\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot,\Omega),

for every
\varphi\in\Gamma_{c}(\Omega,\Delta)
. The measure
P_{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot,\Omega)
is called the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter measure
of
E
in
\Omega
, while
\nu^{\mathbb{G}}
is the (measure theoretic inner)
horizontal unit normal
to
E
in
\Omega
. The
\mathbb{G}
-reduced boundary

\partial^{*}_{\mathbb{G}}E
of
E
is the set of points
p\in\Omega
such that

P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,B_{r}(p))>0,

for every
r>0
, the limit

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}{\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{B_{d}(p,r)}\nu_{\mathbb{G}}\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)

exists, and its
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
-norm is equal to
1
.
The
measure-theoretic
boundary of a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is

(4.2)

\partial E=\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:|E\cap B_{r}(p)|>0\ \text{and}\ |E^{c}\cap B_{r}(p)|>0\ \text{for all}\ r>0\right\}.

Up to modifying a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{G}
of locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in an
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-negligible way, arguing
verbatim
as in
[
25
, Proposition 12.19]
, we can always assume that
\partial E
coincides with the topological boundary of
E
, and that

(4.3)

E=\left\{p\in\mathbb{\mathbb{G}}\,:\,\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(p)|}{|B_{r}(p)|}>0\right\}.

Finally, if
q\in\partial E
and
r>0
, we say that
E
has
locally constant normal
at
q
inside
B_{r}(q)
if there exists a unit left-invariant vector field
\nu
for which

\nu^{\mathbb{G}}(x)=\nu(x)
for
|\partial E|_{\mathbb{G}}
-a.e.
x\in B_{r}(q)
. With the next result, we extend
[
33
, Theorem 3.6]
to hypergenerated groups of arbitrary step, proving the equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. The following are equivalent.

(i)

\mathbb{G}
is hypergenerated.

(ii)

For every set
E
of finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
B_{r}(q)
, with
q\in\partial E
and
r>0
, such that
E
has locally constant normal at
q
inside
B_{r}(q)
,
the boundary
\partial E
is the vertical hyperplane orthogonal to
\nu
inside
B_{r}(q)
.

To prove the sufficiency of the hypergenerated property, we begin with a preliminary lemma inspired by
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. Let
E
be a set of finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
B_{r}(0)
for some
r>0
. Let
Z
be a horizontal left-invariant vector field such that

\int_{E}Z\psi(p)\,dp\leq 0,

for every
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{r}(0))
such that
\psi\geq 0
. Then

\exp(sZ)(A)\subseteq B_{r}(0)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\cap A)\leq\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\cap\exp(sZ)(A))

for every
s>0
and for every
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable set
A\subseteq B_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

It follows exactly as in the proof of
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
; see also
[
7
]
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a hypergenerated group. Let
E
be a set of finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
B_{r}(q)
, with
q\in\partial E
and
r>0
. If
E
has locally constant normal at
q
inside
B_{r}(q)
,
then
\partial E
is the vertical hyperplane orthogonal to
\nu
inside
B_{r}(p)
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
32
, Proposition 3.6]
(see also
[
3
]
). Up to left-translations, we can assume that
q=0
.
We set
\tilde{P}=\mathrm{span}\{\nu\}^{\perp}\cap V_{1}
. Then, since
\mathbb{G}
is hypergenerated, we know that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq Lie(\tilde{P})
. Let
\tilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{X}_{m_{1}}
be a basis of
\tilde{V}
that generates
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
by commutation. We identify the latter with their left-invariant extensions. Fix
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{r}(0))
, set
\phi:=\psi\nu
and for each
j=1,\ldots,m-1
, set
\phi^{\pm}_{j}=\pm\psi\tilde{X}_{j}
. Then it holds that

\begin{split}\int_{E}\nu\psi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}&=\int_{E}{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle\phi,\nu^{\mathbb{G}}\rangle\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)\,\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle\phi,\nu\rangle\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)\,\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)\\
&\leq 0,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\pm\int_{E}\tilde{X}_{j}\psi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{E}{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{G}}\phi^{\pm}_{j}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle\phi^{\pm}_{j}\nu^{\mathbb{G}}\rangle\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)\,=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle\phi^{\pm}_{j},\nu\rangle\,dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)=0\end{split}

for every
j=1,\dots,m-1
. Therefore, arguing as in the proof of
[
32
, Proposition 3.6]
and thanks to
Lemma

4.2
, we conclude that

p\in E\cap B_{r}(0)\text{ and }\exp(\pm s\tilde{X}_{j})(p)\in B_{r}(0)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exp(\pm s\tilde{X}_{j})(p)\in E

and

p\in E\cap B_{r}(0)\text{ and }\exp(s\nu)(p)\in B_{r}(0)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exp(s\nu)(p)\in E

for every
s>0
and every
j=1,\ldots,m-1
. The thesis follows as in the proof of
[
32
, Proposition 3.6]
.
∎

When, instead, the Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
is not hypergenerated, it is possible to exhibit smooth counterexamples. Since our construction applies in the same way to provide counterexamples in
k
-hypergenerated groups of arbitrary order, we give here a unified statement. If
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is a
k
-codimensional embedded surface and
k>1
, we can no longer speak about constant horizontal normal. Nevertheless, the latter property can be rephrased looking at the horizontal tangent space. Indeed, if
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is a smooth, embedded, non-characteristic hypersurface and if we denote by
\nu^{\mathbb{G}}
its horizontal unit normal, then
\nu^{\mathbb{G}}
is constant if and only if there exists a
1
codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that

(4.4)

\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{p}=\mathrm{d}L_{p}P,\qquad\text{for every $p\in S$}.

Let now
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be a smooth, embedded, non-characteristic surface of codimension
1\leq k\leq m-2
. Notice that, since
S
is non-characteristic, then

\dim(\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{p})=m-k,\qquad\text{for every $p\in S$.}

According to (
4.4
), we say that
S
has
locally constant horizontal tangent space
near
p\in S
if there exists
r>0
and a
k
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that

(4.5)

\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{q}=\mathrm{d}L_{q}P,\qquad\text{for every $q\in B_{r}(p)$.}

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. Let
1\leq k\leq m-2
. Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is not
k
-hypergenerated. Then there exists a smooth, embedded, non-characteristic
k
-codimensional hypersurface
S
,
p\in S
, and
r>0
such that
S
has locally constant horizontal tangent space in
B_{r}(p)
but
S\cap B_{\varrho}(p)
is not a vertical
k
-codimensional plane for every
0<\varrho\leq r
.

Proof.

Let
n
be the dimension of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathbb{G}
. Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is not
k
-hypergenerated. Then there exists a
k
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\not\subseteq\Lie(P)
. We fix a basis

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k},Y_{1}\ldots,Y_{m-k})

of
V_{1}
in such a way that

P=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m-k}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\}\oplus P.

Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\not\subseteq\Lie(P)
, there exists a non-empty subspace
W\subseteq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\}\oplus\Lie(P)\oplus W.

We extend
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k},Y_{1}\ldots,Y_{m-k})
to a basis
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k},,Y_{1}\ldots,Y_{\alpha},W_{1},\ldots,W_{\beta})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, for suitable
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},
in such a way that
\Lie(P)=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\alpha}\}
and
W=\mathrm{span}\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{\beta}\}
. We identify elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with their left-invariant extension. We identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via exponential coordinates. More precisely, if

v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}X_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}y_{j}Y_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\beta}w_{j}W_{j}\in\mathfrak{g},

then we identify
v
with the point
p=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{\alpha},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\beta})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{\beta}\to\mathbb{R}

be any smooth functions such that, for every
j=1,\ldots,k
,
\varphi_{j}(\bar{0})=0
and

D\varphi_{j}(\bar{w})=\bar{0}
if and only if
\bar{w}=0
(for instance,
\varphi_{j}(\bar{w})=|\bar{w}|^{2}
for every
j=1,\ldots,k
). Consider the set

E_{\varphi}=\left\{(\varphi_{1}(\bar{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\bar{w}),\bar{0},\bar{0})\cdot(0,\bar{0},\bar{w})\cdot(0,\bar{y},\bar{0})\,:\,\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\alpha},\,\bar{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\beta}\right\}.

Notice that
E_{\varphi}
is a smooth, embedded
k
-codimensional surface such that
0\in S
. Moreover, by our choice of
\varphi
,
E_{\varphi}
is not flat in every neighborhood of
0
. We claim that
Y_{j}|_{p}\in T_{p}S
for every
i=1,\ldots,\alpha
and every
p\in S
. Indeed, fix
p\in S
and define the curve
\gamma(t)=p\cdot\exp(tY_{i})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Notice that
\gamma(0)=p
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=Y_{i}|_{p}
. Moreover, notice that

\begin{split}\gamma(t)&=p\cdot\exp(tY_{i})\\
&=(\varphi_{1}(\bar{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\bar{w}),\bar{0},\bar{0})\cdot(0,\bar{0},\bar{w})\cdot(0,\bar{y},\bar{0})\cdot\exp(tY_{i})\\
&=(\varphi_{1}(\bar{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\bar{w}),\bar{0},\bar{0})\cdot(0,\bar{0},\bar{w})\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}y_{j}Y_{j}\right)\cdot\exp(tY_{i})\\
&=(\varphi_{1}(\bar{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\bar{w}),\bar{0},\bar{0})\cdot(0,\bar{0},\bar{w})\cdot\exp\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}y_{j}Y_{j}\right)\star\left(tY_{i}\right)\right),\end{split}

where we denoted by
\star
the Dynkin on
\mathfrak{g}
using the Campbell-Hausdorff formula (cf.
[
16
, Section 4.7.3]
). In particular, since
\star\left(\Lie(P)\times\Lie(P)\right)\subseteq\Lie(P)
, we conclude that
\gamma(t)\in E_{\varphi}
, so that
Y_{i}|_{p}\in T_{p}S
. We claim that there exists
r>0
such that
S\cap B_{r}(0)
is non-characteristic. Being
S
smooth, it suffices to show that
0
is a non-characteristic point, or equivalently that
X_{1}|_{0},\ldots,X_{k}|_{0}\notin T_{0}S
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{\beta}\times\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined by

\phi(\bar{w},\bar{y})=(\varphi_{1}(\bar{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{k}(\bar{w}),\bar{0},\bar{0})\cdot(0,\bar{0},\bar{w})\cdot(0,\bar{y},\bar{0}),\qquad\text{for $\bar{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\beta}$ and $\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}$.}

Then a simple computation shows that

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial w_{i}}\Big|_{(\bar{0},\bar{0})}=W_{i}|_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y_{i}}\Big|_{(\bar{0},\bar{0})}=Y_{j}|_{0},

for every
i=1,\ldots,\beta
and every
j=1,\ldots,\alpha
. In particular,
X_{1}|_{0},\ldots,X_{k}|_{0}\notin T_{0}S
, so that there exists
r>0
such that
S\cap B_{r}(0)
is non-characteristic. Since we already know that
Y_{1}|_{p},\ldots,Y_{m-k}|_{p}\in T_{p}S
for every
p\in S
, we conclude that

\left(\Delta_{S}\right)_{p}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1}|_{p},\ldots,Y_{m-k}|_{p}\}

for every
p\in S\cap B_{r}(0)
, so that
S
has locally constant horizontal tangent space in
B_{r}(0)
. Nevertheless, our choice of
\varphi
implies that
S\cap B_{\varrho}(0)
is not a vertical
k
-codimensional plane for every
0<\varrho\leq r
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

The theorem follows combining
Proposition

4.3
and
Proposition

4.4
.
∎

In the smooth setting,
Proposition

4.3
generalizes to higher codimension as follows.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
1\leq k\leq m-2
. Let
\mathbb{G}
be a
k
-hypergenerated group. Let
S
be a smooth, embedded, non-characteristic surface of codimension
k
. Let
p\in S
and
r>0
be such that
S
has locally constant horizontal tangent space in
B_{r}(p)
. Then
S\cap B_{r}(p)
is a vertical
k
-codimensional plane.

Proof.

Let
S,p
and
r
be as in the statement. By hypothesis, there exists a
k
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that (
4.5
) holds for every
q\in B_{r}(p)
. Let us define

H:=\exp\left(P\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\right).

According to (
2.3
),the space
H
is a vertical
k
-codimensional plane. Since
\mathbb{G}
is
k
-hypergenerated, then

(4.6)

H=\exp(\Lie(P)).

Fix
q\in S\cap B_{r}(p)
. To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that there exists
\varrho>0
such that

q\cdot H\cap B_{\varrho}(q)\subseteq S.

Up to left-translating
S
, we may assume that
q=0
. In view of (
4.6
), for a fixed
\tilde{q}\in H
there exists
Z\in\Lie(P)
such that

(4.7)

\tilde{q}=\exp(Z).

Let us identify
Z
with its left-invariant extension. Since

d\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{p}}P\subseteq T_{\tilde{p}}S

for every
\tilde{p}\in S\cap B_{r}(p)
, then
d\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{p}}\mathrm{Lie}(P)\subseteq T_{\tilde{p}}S

for every
\tilde{p}\in S\cap B_{r}(p)
, whence

Z_{\tilde{p}}\in T_{\tilde{p}}S

for every
\tilde{p}\in S\cap B_{r}(p)
. Thus the integral curve of
Z
belongs to
S
. Therefore, by (
4.7
) and choosing
\varrho>0
small enough, we conclude that
\tilde{q}\in S
, whence the thesis follows.
∎

Ultimately, combining
Proposition

4.4
and
Proposition

4.5
, we obtain the following characterization in arbitrary codimension.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group of rank
m
. Assume that
1\leq k\leq m-2.
Then
\mathbb{G}
is
k
-hypergenerated if and only if every
k
-codimensional surface with locally constant horizontal tangent space is locally a vertical
k
-codimensional plane.

5.
Non-characteristic surfaces

In this section we conclude the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.
Indeed, we prove the following statement.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathbb{G}
is
k
-hypergenerated.

(2)

On all embedded non-characteristic surfaces
S\subseteq\mathbb{G}
of codimension at least
k
, the intrinsic distance and the restricted distance (as in
Definition

2.2
) are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent.

The implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) in
Theorem

5.1
is a straightforward consequence of the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. The following are equivalent.

•

\mathbb{G}
is
k
-hypergenerated.

•

All vertical planes
H\subseteq\mathbb{G}
of codimension
k
are Carnot subgroups of
\mathbb{G}
.

In particular, if
\mathbb{G}
is not
k
-hypergenerated, there exists a vertical
k
-codimensional plane
H\subseteq\mathbb{G}
whose intrinsic distance is not finite.

Proof.

Let
V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{s}
be the stratification of the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{G}
and
\Delta
be the left-invariant extension of
V_{1}
. Let
H\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be a vertical plane of codimension
k
. Let us denote by
\mathfrak{h}
its Lie algebra. We have

(5.1)

\mathfrak{h}=P_{H}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

where
P_{H}:=\mathfrak{h}\cap V_{1}
(cf. (
2.3
)).
The subgroup
H
is a Carnot subgroup if and only if
P_{H}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
is a stratification of
\mathfrak{h}
, that is, if and only if

\Lie(P_{H})=P_{H}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lieofh}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{h}.

Since the latter equation holds for every
P_{H}\subseteq V_{1}
of co-dimension smaller than
k
if and only if
\mathbb{G}
is
k
-hypergenerated, we proved the first part of the proposition.

Assume now that
\mathbb{G}
is not
k
-hypergenerated. Then, there exists a
k
-codimensional subspace
P\subseteq V_{1}
such that

(5.2)

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\not\subseteq\Lie(P).

Define

H:=\exp\left(P\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\right).

For all
g\in H
we have that
T_{g}H\cap\Delta=\mathrm{d}L_{g}P
. Consequently, by (
5.2
), the Lie algebra generated by the distribution
TH\cap\Delta\subseteq TH
is strictly contained in
TH
, thus by Frobenius Theorem the intrinsic distance is not finite.
∎

5.1.
Weak tangents of Lipschitz maps

To prove the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) in
Theorem

5.1
we show that, given a hypersurface equipped with the intrinsic distance, all the weak tangents of the inclusion map are isometric embeddings. First, we recall some basic concepts in metric geometry.

We denote with
\omega
an arbitrary non-principal ultrafilter on the set of natural numbers
[
17
]
. For each sequence
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in a compact metric space, we use
\omega
to choose a point
\lim_{\omega}(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
among the accumulation points of
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Definition 5.3
.

The
limit of a sequence of pointed metric spaces

(X_{i},d_{i},x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is the pointed metric space
(X,d,x)
defined in the following way:

(1)

As a set, we consider

X:=\{(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mid y_{i}\in X_{i},\ \sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(d_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}))<+\infty)\}/\sim,

where
(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sim(z_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
if and only if
\lim_{\omega}d_{i}(z_{i},y_{i})=0
.

(2)

As distance, we take
d((y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(z_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})=\lim_{\omega}d_{i}(z_{i},y_{i})
.

(3)

As base point we consider
x:=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Each sequence of
L
-Lipschitz maps
f_{i}:(X_{i},d_{i},x_{i})\to(Y_{i},d_{i},y_{i})
, with
L\in\mathbb{R}
and where here and in the following we adopt the same name for possibly different distances, induces in a natural way a
limit map

f:(X,d,x)\to(Y,d,y)
between the limit spaces, defined by setting
f((z_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}):=(f_{i}(z_{i}))_{i\in N}
for all
(z_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in X
, which is well-defined and
L
-Lipschitz.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. We say that a pointed metric space
(Y,d,y)
is a
weak tangent
of
X
at
x
if there exists sequence
(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}\times X)^{\mathbb{N}}
, with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}=0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
, such that the limit of
(X,\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}}d,x_{n})
is isometric to
(Y,d,y)
.
We will write
(X,d,x)_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}
to denote the weak tangent obtained via the sequence
(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}\times X)^{\mathbb{N}}
.
A weak tangent of a
L
-Lipschitz map
f:(X,d)\to(Y,d)
, is the limit

f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}:(X,d,x)_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\to(Y,d,f(x))_{(\epsilon_{n},f(x_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}

of the sequence of maps

f:(X,\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}}d,x_{n})\to(Y,\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}}d,f(x_{n})),

for some
(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}\times X)^{\mathbb{N}}
, with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}=0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
.

We state a key result on weak tangents of maps that we will use in the proof
Theorem

5.1
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f:(X,d)\to(Y,d)
be an
L
-Lipschitz map between metric spaces. Assume that there exists
\rho>0
such that, for all
x\in X
, every weak tangent
f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}
of
f
at
x
satisfies

(5.3)

d(f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}(z),f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}(w))\geq\rho d(z,w),\quad\text{for all }z,w\in X_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}.

Then, for all
\bar{\rho}<\rho
, the map
f
is a local
\left(\max\left\{\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}},L\right\}\right)
-bi-Lipschitz embedding.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that the lemma does not hold. Then there exists
\bar{\rho}<\rho
,
x\in X
and sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
x
such that

(5.4)

d(f(x_{n}),f(y_{n}))\leq\bar{\rho}d(x_{n},y_{n}).

Set
\epsilon_{n}:=d(x_{n},y_{n})
. We have

\displaystyle d(f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}),f_{(\epsilon_{n},x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}((y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}}d(f(x_{n}),f(y_{n}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inequality_contradiction}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\epsilon_{n}}d(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}d((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}),

which contradicts (
5.3
).
∎

5.2.
Weak tangents of sub-Riemannian manifolds

In this section, we describe the weak tangents of equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds. We follow the ideas in
[
6
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
4
]
,
but in addition we allow the base point of dilations to vary. Given a point in an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold, we prove that all weak tangents at that point are isometric to a Carnot group, which might depend on the point. We do this by describing the metric on the weak tangent as a uniform limit of sub-Riemannian metrics.

Let
M
be an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold of step
s
. Fix a stratified basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
and denote with
w_{i}
the weight of
X_{i}
(see
Definition

2.1
). Fix
p\in M
.
We define
exponential coordinates of the second kind

\psi:\mathcal{O}\to M
at
p
by setting

(5.5)

\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\phi_{X_{1}}^{x_{1}}\circ\ldots\circ\phi_{X_{n}}^{x_{n}}(p),

for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a suitable neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\phi_{X}^{t}
denotes the flow at time
t
along the vector field
X
. Moreover, we fix a neighborhood
U
of
p\in M
and an open set
A\subseteq U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, for every
q\in U
, exponential coordinates of second type centered at
q
are a diffeomorphism from
A\cap\left(\{q\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
onto
U
.
Define
\delta_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting

\delta_{\epsilon}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=(\epsilon^{w_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\epsilon^{w_{n}}x_{n}),\quad\text{ for all }x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}.

Set
A_{\epsilon}:=\{(q,x)\in U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(q,\delta_{\epsilon}(x))\in A\}\subseteq U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, define
X_{i}^{\epsilon}:A_{\epsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting

(5.6)

X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x):=\epsilon^{w_{i}}\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\right)^{*}\psi_{q}^{*}X_{i}(x).

The following proposition ensures that the above vector fields converge locally uniformly as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Proposition 5.6
.

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists
X_{i}^{0}:U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, for all compact neighborhoods
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
0
and for all compact sets
K\subseteq U
, there exists
\bar{\epsilon},C>0
such that for all
\epsilon\leq\bar{\epsilon}
we have
K\times H\subseteq A_{\epsilon}
and

\|X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x)-X_{i}^{0}(q^{\prime},x)\|\leq C(d(q,q^{\prime})+\epsilon),\quad\text{for all $x\in H$, for all $q,q^{\prime}\in K$.}

Moreover, for all
q\in U
, the vector fields
x\mapsto X_{i}^{0}(q,x)
, with
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, form a stratified algebra.

Proof.

Let
P^{k}:A\to\mathbb{R}
be the coefficients of
\psi_{q}^{*}X_{i}
:

\psi_{q}^{*}X_{i}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{k}(q,x)\partial_{k},\quad\text{for all }x\in A.

Fix
N:=\max\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}
.
Since the
P^{k}
’s are smooth, we can expand them as a Taylor series in a neighborhood of the origin

(5.7)

\psi_{q}^{*}X_{i}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
|\alpha|\leq N\end{subarray}\\
}a_{\alpha}^{k}(q)x^{\alpha}\right)+o(\|x\|^{N})\right)\partial_{k}.

where for a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
we denote
x^{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
,
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
, and
a_{\alpha}^{k}:U\to\mathbb{R}
are suitable smooth functions.
Denote
[\alpha]:=\sum_{1}^{n}\alpha_{i}w_{i}
.
By Bellaïche work
[
6
]
, we have that
a^{k}_{\alpha}(q)=0
if
[\alpha]<w_{k}-1
.
Define
X_{i}^{0}:U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting

X_{i}^{0}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
[\alpha]=w_{k}-1\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha}^{k}(q)x^{\alpha}\right)\partial_{k},\quad\text{for all }x\in A.

Set

C_{1}:=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\\
|\alpha|\leq N\end{subarray}}\max\left\{a_{\alpha}^{k}(q)x^{\alpha}\mid q\in K,x\in H\right\}

and

C_{2}:=2\max\{C_{1},\mathrm{Lip}_{K\times H}(X_{i}^{0})\},

where
\mathrm{Lip}_{K\times H}(X_{i}^{0})
denotes the Lipschitz constant of the smooth function
X_{i}^{0}
on
H\times K
with respect to
d\times\|\cdot\|
.
Since
x^{\alpha}\circ\delta_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{[\alpha]}x^{\alpha}
and
\left(\delta_{\epsilon}\right)^{*}\partial_{k}=\epsilon^{-w_{k}}\partial_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
|\alpha|\leq N\end{subarray}}\epsilon^{1+[\alpha]-w_{k}}a_{\alpha}^{k}(q)x^{\alpha}\right)+o(\epsilon^{N-w_{k}+1})\right)\partial_{k}.

Consequently, we can choose
\bar{\epsilon}
such that

\left\|X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x)-X_{i}^{0}(q,x)\right\|=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
|\alpha|\leq N\\
[\alpha]>w_{k}-1\end{subarray}\\
}\epsilon^{1+[\alpha]-w_{k}}a_{\alpha}^{k}(q)x^{\alpha}\right)+o(\epsilon^{N-w_{k}+1})\right)\partial_{k}\right\|\leq C_{1}\epsilon,

for all
x\in H
and
q\in K
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x)-X_{i}^{0}(q^{\prime},x)\right\|\leq\left\|X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x)-X_{i}^{0}(q,x)\right\|+\left\|X_{i}^{0}(q,x)-X_{i}^{0}(q^{\prime},x)\right\|\leq C_{2}(d(q,q^{\prime})+\epsilon).

For the proof that for all
q\in U
, the vector fields
x\mapsto X_{i}^{0}(q,x)
, with
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, form a stratified algebra, we refer to
[
6
, Propositions 5.17-5.22]
.
∎

Continuing with the terminology for the setting discussed in this section, fix two compact sets
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
K\subseteq U
and
\bar{\epsilon}
such that
K\times H\subseteq A_{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon\leq\bar{\epsilon}
. Fix
\epsilon\leq\bar{\epsilon}
and
q\in K
, denote with
d_{\epsilon,q}
and
d_{0,q}
the sub-Riemannian distances on
H
induced respectively by the sub-Riemannainan metrics for which
\{X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,\cdot)\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},w_{i}=1}
and
\{X_{i}(q,\cdot)^{0}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},w_{i}=1}
are orthonormal frames respectively.
An immediate consequence of
Proposition

5.6
and
[
4
, Theorem C.2]
is the following result.

Corollary 5.7
.

For all sequences
\epsilon_{m}\to 0
and
q_{m}\to q
, the distances
d_{\epsilon_{m},q_{m}}
converge uniformly on compact sets to
d_{0,q}
.

As a consequence of
Corollary

5.7
, we get the following.

Corollary 5.8
.

At every point of an equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold, there is a unique weak tangent that is isometric to a Carnot group.

To prove
Corollary

5.8
we state and prove a slightly stronger statement. Indeed, using the terminology introduced in this section, we can explicitly describe the weak tangent of equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds.

Corollary 5.9
.

Fix
q\in U
. Every weak tangent of
M
at
q
is isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{0,q},0)
.

Proof.

Fix a sequence
(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}\times X)^{\mathbb{N}}
, with
\lim_{m\to\infty}\epsilon_{m}=0
and
\lim_{m\to\infty}q_{m}=q
. Fix
R>0
and denote with
B:=B_{R}(0)
the ball centered at
0
of radius
R
with respect to the distance
d_{\epsilon_{m},q_{m}}
.
Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a compact neighborhood
K
of
q
such that for all
m\geq N
we have
K\times B\subseteq A_{\epsilon_{m}}
and
q_{m}\in K
.
Define the map
\varphi_{m}:(B,d_{\epsilon_{m},q_{m}},0)\to(M,\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}}d,q_{m})
by setting

(5.8)

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}:=\psi_{q_{m}}\circ\delta_{\epsilon_{m}}.

We claim that
\varphi_{m}
is an isometry of pointed metric spaces. Indeed, clearly
\varphi_{m}(0)=q_{m}
. Moreover,
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}}d
is the sub-Riemannian distance induced by
\{\epsilon_{m}X_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},w_{i}=1}
. Thus, since the orthonormal frame
\{X_{i}^{\epsilon_{m}}(q_{m},\cdot)\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},w_{i}=1}
for the distance
d_{\epsilon,q_{m}}
is exactly
\{\varphi_{m}^{*}\epsilon_{m}X_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},w_{i}=1}
(see (
5.6
)), we have that
\varphi_{m}
is an isometric embedding. Finally, being
\varphi_{m}
an isometric embedding and being the map
\psi_{q_{m}}
surjective onto
U
, we must have that
\varphi_{m}
is surjective onto
B(q_{m},\epsilon_{m}R)
.
Hence the two weak tangents
(B_{R}(0),d_{0,q},0)
and
(B_{R},d,q)_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}
are isometric.
Since the weak tangent of a sequence of isometries is an isometric embedding, and by
Corollary

5.7
the pointed metric spaces
(B,d_{\epsilon,q_{m}},0)
converge to
(B_{R}(0),d_{0,q},0)
, the limit map of the sequence
\varphi_{m}
is an isometric embedding

\varphi:(B_{R}(0),d_{0,q},0)\to(B_{R},d,q)_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}\simeq(B_{R}(0),d_{0,q},0),

where
B
is the ball of radius
R
in
(M,d,q)_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}
. Being
\varphi
an self isometric embedding of a proper metric space it is an isometry. Since
R>0
was arbitrary, the corollary is proved.∎

We now consider maps between
M
and a second equiregular sub-Riemannian manifold
M^{\prime}
. To smoothen the notation, we will denote with an apex
′
all the object in
M^{\prime}
.

Remark 5.10
.

In the proof of
Corollary

5.9
, we explicitly write the isometry between the weak tangent
(M,d,q)_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{0,q},0)
as the limit of the maps in (
5.8
).
Let
f:M\to M^{\prime}
be a map between sub-Riemannian manifolds, and
\varphi^{\prime}_{m}:=\psi_{f(q_{m})}\circ\delta_{\epsilon_{m}}
.
Denote with
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
the limit maps of
\varphi_{m}
and
\varphi^{\prime}_{m}
respectively.
If
f_{m}:=(\varphi^{\prime}_{m})^{-1}\circ f\circ\varphi_{m}=\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{m}}}\circ\psi_{f(q_{m})}^{-1}\circ f\circ\psi_{q_{m}}\circ\delta_{\epsilon_{m}}

then the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(M,d,q)_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(M^{\prime},d,f(q))_{(\epsilon_{m},f(q_{m}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{0,q},0)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}},d_{0,f(p)},0)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\infty}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}

In this case, since the maps

and
\varphi^{\prime}
are isometries, with a slight abuse of notation we will identify

with

and say that

is a weak tangent of

at
q
.

Using
Remark

5.10
, we prove that contact maps between equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds admit a unique weak tangent.

Definition 5.11
.

A map
f:M\to M^{\prime}
between sub-Riemannian manifolds is a
contact map
if it is smooth and for all
p\in M
there holds

(5.9)

\mathrm{d}f_{p}\left(\Delta_{p}\right)\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}_{f(p)}.

Before studying the weak tangents of contact maps, we state a useful technical lemma.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
g_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
a sequence of continuously differentiable maps and
(\epsilon_{m},q_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}\times X)^{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that the sequence
q_{m}
converges to
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If on every compact set
\mathrm{d}g_{m}X_{i}^{\epsilon_{m}}(q_{m},\cdot)
is uniformly bounded for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then
\mathrm{d}g_{m}
is bounded on compact sets.

The following proposition shows that the weak tangent of contact maps at a point is unique and it is a group homomorphism.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
f:M\to M^{\prime}
to be a contact map between equiregular sub-Riemannian manifolds. At every point
p\in M
, the map
f
has a unique weak tangent
P_{f,p}:(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{0,p},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}},d_{0,f(p)},0)
, that is the unique group homomorphism satisfying

(5.10)

\mathrm{d}(P_{f,p})_{0}(X_{i}^{0}(0))=\pi_{w_{i}}((\mathrm{d}\tilde{f_{p}})_{0}(X_{i}^{1}(0))),\quad\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

where
\tilde{f_{q}}:=\psi_{f(q)}^{-1}\circ f\circ\psi_{q}
for all
q\in M
, and
\pi_{w_{i}}
is the projection to the homogeneous component of weight
w_{i}
.
In particular, when
M^{\prime}
is a Carnot group, we have

(5.11)

\mathrm{d}(P_{f,p})_{0}(X_{i}^{0}(0))=\pi_{w_{i}}((\mathrm{d}f)_{p}X_{i}(p)),\quad\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Proof.

By
Remark

5.10
it is enough to show that
f_{p,\epsilon}:=\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\circ\psi_{f(q)}^{-1}\circ f\circ\psi_{q}\circ\delta_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
converges uniformly on compact sets to a group homomorphism
P_{f,p}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}
satisfying (
5.10
) as
\epsilon
tends to
0
and
q
tends to
p
.
Fix two compact sets
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
K\subseteq U
, with
p\in K
. By (
5.9
), for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

\mathrm{d}f_{q}X_{i}\in(\Delta^{\prime})^{(w_{i})}_{f(q)},\quad\text{for all }q\in M.

Consequently, we can write
\mathrm{d}\tilde{f}_{q}(X_{i}^{1})
, where
X_{i}^{1}
is
X_{i}^{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon=1
, as a combination of vector fields of weight smaller than
w_{i}
:

(5.12)

\mathrm{d}\tilde{f}_{q}(X_{i}^{1}(q,x))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\{1,\ldots,n^{\prime}\}\\
w^{\prime}_{k}\leq w_{i}\end{subarray}}a_{i}^{k}(q,x)X^{\prime}_{k}(f(q),\tilde{f}(x))\quad\text{for all }(q,x)\in A,

for some smooth functions
a_{i}^{1},\ldots,a_{i}^{k}:A\to\mathbb{R}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
From the above equation we get

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{f}_{q}\mathrm{d}\delta_{\epsilon}(X_{i}^{\epsilon}(q,x))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def_X_eps}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\epsilon^{w_{i}}\mathrm{d}\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{f}_{q}(X_{i}^{1}(q,\delta_{\epsilon}(x)))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:primo_conto_differenziale_f-tilde}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\epsilon^{w_{i}}\mathrm{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\{1,\ldots,n^{\prime}\}\\
w^{\prime}_{k}\leq w_{i}\end{subarray}}a_{i}^{k}(q,\delta_{\epsilon}(x))\mathrm{d}\delta_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}X^{\prime}_{k}(f(q),\tilde{f}(\delta_{\epsilon}(x)))

\displaystyle=

Remark 5.15

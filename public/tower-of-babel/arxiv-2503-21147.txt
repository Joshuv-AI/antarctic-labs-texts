Introduction and main result

For the two-dimensional Ising model on the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

[

+
cluster nor an infinite
-

, it is known (see
[
21
]
and
[
7
]
) that there is neither an infinite
+
cluster nor an infinite
-
cluster whenever
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}))
and the external field
h=0
.

For the three (and above) dimensional Ising model, the behavior of infinite
+/-
clusters are strikingly different from the two-dimensional case. It was proved by Campanino and Russo
[
13
]
that for the Ising model on the cubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, there is coexistence of infinite
+
cluster and infinite
-
cluster when
\beta
is small (with positive distance away from the critical inverse temperature
\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
) and
|h|
is small. It is expected that this coexistence result should persist up to
\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
and perhaps surprisingly even beyond
\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
). Actually, it is believed that the coexistence of infinite clusters of both signs for
\beta
slightly larger than
\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})
and
h=0
should be useful to understand the roughening transition in 3D
[
10
,
3
]
. In this paper, we study the coexistence of infinite clusters of both signs in 3D Ising models and we will prove the coexistence holds up to the critical inverse temperature for certain 3D lattices. When the dimension

is large enough, it was proved by Aizenman, Bricmont and Lebowitz
[
2
]
that there is an infinite
+
cluster in the minus phase (i.e., the infinite-volume measure obtained from all
-
boundary conditions) for each
\beta\in[0,\beta_{+}]
and
h
larger than some negative number where
\beta_{+}>\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of all integers. With a slight abuse of notation, we also use

to denote the one-dimensional integer lattice, i.e., with the set of vertices

17
,
16
]
.

is

(1)

In the proof, we will see that we may define

and no infinite

.

Remark 2
.

Our proof strategy of Theorem

applies to more general graphs, like

holds for

Remark 3
.

As mentioned above, Theorem

. The arguments in Sections

and
3
work well for the cubic lattice. The missing ingredient is that the

is at least critical at
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{3}))
and

.

Remark 4
.

On
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, Higuchi’s coexistence result
[
23
,
24
]
is somewhat artificial in the sense that the extra bonds (i.e., the diagonals of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) have no Ising interactions. In the context of the current paper, a natural question is what happens for the Ising model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with both nearest-neighbor and diagonal interactions (we denote this lattice by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{*}
, which is the matching graph of the usual nearest-neighbor square lattice). From
[
23
,
24
]
, it is easy to show that there is coexistence of infinite

and
-
clusters for the Ising model defined on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{*}
when

is small. Then it is natural to conjecture that the same coexistence result holds for all

. Our method (see a summary in the next paragraph) should be applicable to this case:
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{*}
can be viewed as

plus diagonal edges with slope

(see Figure
1
) and each of such edges is selected with probability

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is by introducing a new parameter,
p
, which is the probability that a given vertex in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
can be used for an increasing event

. So the probability of

, which we denote by

and

(we always fix

). Most of the paper is devoted to proving that the ratio of the partial derivatives of

with respect to

and

is bounded from above by a continuous and positive function and this bound is uniform in

. A principal novelty of the current paper is to bound the influence from one fixed spin on the increasing event

by adding an extra exponentially small (in radius) external field on the sphere center at this point (see Proposition

The method of differential inequalities has been used to prove strict monotonicity for critical points in percolation and Ising models
[
4
,
8
]
, strict inequality for critical values of random cluster and Potts models
[
9
]
, equalities between critical exponents and Lipschitz continuity of critical lines
[
20
]
, inequality between the critical points of site and bond percolation
[
14
]
, the location and strict monotonicity of the Kertész line
[
12
,
22
]
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we prove a stochastic inequality which relates the influence on increasing events from one fixed spin and the influence of an external field imposed a certain distance away, and then quantify the latter influence. In Section
3
, we prove an inequality on the partial derivatives

Proposition 1

\left|\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A\cap B)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(B)}-1\right|\leq K\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{1},y\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-\lambda|x-y|},~\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda_{1}},B\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda_{2}},

whenever the right hand side (RHS) of the inequality is less than

We next use the ratio mixing property to prove the following stochastic comparison inequality.

Proposition 2
.

For each
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}))
and

where

h,&y\in\Lambda\setminus\partial B_{m}(x).\end{cases}

We order all vertices in

Claim 1
.

For each integer
k\in[0,L-1]
, any
\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\tau}\in\{-1,+1\}^{k}

\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\geq\tilde{\tau}_{i}

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}^{+}}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1|\sigma_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\sigma_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\geq P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\tau_{x_{k+1}}=+1|\tau_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\tau}_{1},\dots,\tau_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\tau}_{k},\tau_{x}=+1).

Note that

P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}^{+}}(\cdot|\sigma_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\sigma_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{k})=P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}^{+}}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\cdot),\text{ with }\tilde{\sigma}[k]:=\{\sigma_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\sigma_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\}.

We define
\mathbf{g}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{\Lambda}
by

g_{y}(t):=\begin{cases}h+t,&y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda,\\
h,&y\in\Lambda\setminus\partial B_{m}(x).\end{cases}

(2)

Then there exists a
c_{1}=c_{1}(\beta,h)\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}(t)}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\left.\frac{\partial P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1)}{\partial g_{y}}\right|_{\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{g}(t)}

Therefore,

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}^{+}}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{e^{-am}}\frac{\partial P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}(t)}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1)}{\partial t}dt+P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}^{\tilde{\sigma}[k]}(\sigma_{x_{k+1}}=+1)

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\tau_{x_{k+1}}=+1|\tau_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\tau}_{1},\dots,\tau_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\tau}_{k},\tau_{x}=+1)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\tau_{x_{k+1}}=+1,\tau_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\tau}_{1},\dots,\tau_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\tau}_{k}|\tau_{x}=+1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\tau_{x_{1}}=\tilde{\tau}_{1},\dots,\tau_{x_{k}}=\tilde{\tau}_{k}|\tau_{x}=+1)}.

We now pick any

Proposition 3
.

For any finite

Lemma 2.1
.

For any finite

\displaystyle\frac{df(t)}{dt}

A differential inequality

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be finite. For any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
,
\Delta\subset\Lambda
and
\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}
, we say
\Delta
is pivotal for
(A,\sigma)
if
\sigma^{\Delta+}\in A
and
\sigma^{\Delta-}\notin A
where

\sigma_{x}^{\Delta+}:=\begin{cases}\sigma_{x},&x\in\Lambda\setminus\Delta,\\
+1,&x\in\Delta,\end{cases}\qquad\sigma_{x}^{\Delta-}:=\begin{cases}\sigma_{x},&x\in\Lambda\setminus\Delta,\\
-1,&x\in\Delta.\end{cases}

(3)

We define the event

\{\Delta\text{ is pivotal for }A\}:=\{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}:\Delta\text{ is pivotal for }(A,\sigma)\}.

We also define
\{A\text{ occurs on }\Delta\}
to be the event

\left\{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}:\forall\tau\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}\text{ with }\tau_{x}=\sigma_{x}\text{ for each }x\in\Delta\text{ implies }\tau\in A\right\}.

Note that for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
, we have

A\cap\{\Delta\text{ is pivotal for }A\}^{c}=\{A\text{ occurs on }\Delta^{c}\}.

(4)

For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
\mathbb{S}_{k}
be the slab of thickness

, namely the induced subgraph of
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}
with the set of vertices
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\{0,\dots,k\}
. For the rest of the section, we always fix
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}))
and will drop the
\beta
dependence. For any finite
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
p\in[0,1]
, we define a new measure
\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}
in the following way: for each
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda\cap\mathbb{S}^{1}}
,

\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(A):=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}}p^{|\omega^{-1}(1)|}(1-p)^{|\omega^{-1}(0)|}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)),

where
\Lambda_{(1)}:=\{x\in\Lambda:\text{ the third coordinate of }x,x(3)=1\}
,
\omega^{-1}(1):=\{x\in\Lambda_{(1)}:\omega_{x}=1\}
and
\omega^{-1}(0):=\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\omega^{-1}(1)
. That is, we first run an independent Bernoulli site percolation on
\mathbb{S}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{0}
to get a configuration
\omega
, and
\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(A)
is probability that
A
occurs on
\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)
.

Our main ingredient to the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following differential inequality. We define the event
\{\exists~\text{LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}\}
to be

\{\exists\text{ a path of }+\text{ spins in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}\text{ which intersects both hyperplanes }\{x(1)=\pm n\}\}.

Proposition 4
.

There exists a continuous function
\gamma:(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that for any finite
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with
B_{n}\subset\Lambda
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the increasing event
\mathcal{H}:=\{\exists\text{ LR }+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}\}
satisfies

\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h}\leq\gamma(p,h)\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p}.

Remark 5
.

Proposition
4
holds for more general increasing events in
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda\cap\mathbb{S}_{0}}
. For example, it holds for the event
\{\exists\text{ a path of }+\text{spins in }\mathbb{S}_{1}\text{ which connects }0\text{ to }\partial B_{n}\}
. But it certainly fails if
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda\cap\mathbb{S}_{0}}
with
A
nontrivial (i.e.,
\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(A)
is not a constant) since the RHS of the inequality is always
0
in this case.

\displaystyle\omega^{-1}(1):=\{y\in\Delta:\omega_{y}=1\},\omega^{-1}(0):=\Delta\setminus\omega^{-1}(1),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x,\omega):=\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\cup\{x\}\},

\displaystyle\{x\text{ is}+\text{pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}:=\{x\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}\cap\{\sigma_{x}=+1\};

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the assumptions in Proposition
4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{(1)}}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{x\}}}p^{|\omega^{-1}(1)|}(1-p)^{|\omega^{-1}(0)|}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is}+\text{pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}}p^{|\omega^{-1}(1)|}(1-p)^{|\omega^{-1}(0)|}\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}.

We may generalize
\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}
to
\mu_{\Lambda,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{h}}
for
\mathbf{p}\in[0,1]^{\Lambda_{(1)}}
and
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Lambda}
in the following way

\mu_{\Lambda,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H}):=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(1)}p_{y}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(0)}(1-p_{y})P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)).

Then it is easy to see that for any

,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p_{x}}=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{x\}}}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(1)}p_{y}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(0)}(1-p_{y})\times

\displaystyle\qquad\big[P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\cup\{x\})-P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1))\big],

and for any

,

\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h_{x}}=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(1)}p_{y}\prod_{y\in\omega^{-1}(0)}(1-p_{y})\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{h}}.

The lemma follows from the chain rule and (

Before we dive into the proof of Proposition
4
, we first present the overall idea. Lemma
3.1
tells us that we need to bound the weighted sum of the covariance
\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
over
x\in\Lambda
by the weighted sum of
P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is}+\text{pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega))
over
x\in\Lambda_{(1)}
; here the change of the event in the covariance is fine due to (
5
). In Lemma
3.2
, we write
\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)
as the disjoint union of
\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\cap\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}
and
\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}
. Then
|\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}|
is bounded above by a sum of
P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is}+\text{pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))
over
y\in B_{m+1}(x)
where
\omega^{(y)}
differs from
\omega
only in
B_{3m}(y)
; so this term has the desired upper bound. The other term
\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
is the hard one, which requires the full machinery of Section
2
. With the help of Proposition
2
and
3
, we can bound it above by a similar good upper bound plus another sum of
\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
over
y\in\partial B_{m}(x)
; the crucial point is that this second sum is multiplied by

which could beat any polynomial of

. Consequently, when we sum
\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
over

(to get
\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})/\partial h
), we obtain an upper bound which is the sum of
\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})/\partial p
and

. By choosing

large so that

, we arrive at the desired inequality in Proposition

. In the above discussion, we somehow ignore the

-dependence in those sums, that is why we need Lemma
3.3
to justify this.

The following lemma establishes the connection between the two partial derivatives in Proposition

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
4
, for any
x,y\in\Lambda
, let
\omega^{(y)}\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}
be obtained from
\omega
by opening all vertices in
\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{3m}(y)
:

\omega^{(y)}_{z}:=\begin{cases}1,&z\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{3m}(y),\\
\omega_{z},&z\in\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus B_{3m}(y).\end{cases}

Then there exists constant
M=M(\beta,h)\in(0,\infty)
such that for each
m\geq M
,

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\leq C(m,\beta,h)\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))

\displaystyle\qquad+8e^{-ma}\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h},

where
C(m,\beta,h)\in(0,\infty)
and
a
is defined in Proposition
2
.

Remark 6
.

The reader may wonder whether the local modifications for
\omega
are necessary. We will comment on this after the inequality (
7
).

Using (
4
), we can write

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}&=\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
&\qquad+\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}.\end{split}

(6)

The first term on the RHS of (
6
) satisfies that

\begin{split}&\left|\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\right|\leq 2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega))\\
&\qquad\leq C(m,\beta,h)\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)})),\end{split}

(7)

where we have used the fact that
\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\}=\emptyset
whenever
\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)=\emptyset
(the latter implies that
B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}=\emptyset
); we have made some local modifications for the spin configuration in
B_{m+3}(x)
to create a pivotal point

and the cost for such modifications is just a function of
m,\beta,h
by the finite energy property for Ising measures. Here the local modifications for
\omega
within
B_{3m}(y)\supset B_{m}(x)
are necessary since we need to make sure that we are free to use any vertices in
\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+3}(x)
to construct a pivotal point
y
.

Before we move to the second term on the RHS of (
6
), we note that for a general event
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
, we have

\begin{split}&\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
=&2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1)\left[P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A|\sigma_{x}=+1)-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A|\sigma_{x}=-1)\right].\end{split}

This, Proposition
2
and the mean value theorem imply that

\begin{split}&\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}=\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
&\qquad\leq 4e^{-am}\sup_{|t|\leq e^{-am}}\frac{\partial P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}(t)}(\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c})}{\partial t},~\forall m\geq M,\end{split}

(8)

where
\mathbf{g}(t)
is defined in (
2
).
Now, Proposition
3
implies that for any
t
with
|t|\leq e^{-am}
, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial P_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}(t)}(\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c})}{\partial t}=\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathbf{g}(t)}\\
&\qquad\leq\exp\left[4|\partial B_{m}|e^{-am}\right]\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
&\qquad\leq 2\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h},~\forall m\geq M,\end{split}

(9)

where in the last inequality we have made the
M
from Proposition
2
larger if necessary such that
\exp\left[4|\partial B_{m}|e^{-am}\right]\leq 2
for each
m\geq M
.

Note that for each

, we have

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}\subset\mathcal{H}(y,\omega),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)\setminus\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}\subset\left\{B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)\right\}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)\setminus\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}+2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(B_{m}(x)\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}+C(m,\beta,h)\sum_{z\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(z\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(z,\omega^{(z)})),

where the last inequality follows as in (
7

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}I_{\{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)\text{ occurs on }B_{m}(x)^{c}\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\leq 8e^{-am}\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\qquad+8e^{-am}C(m,\beta,h)|\partial B_{m}|\sum_{z\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)}P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(z\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(z,\omega^{(z)})).

By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}}}p^{|\omega^{-1}(1)|}(1-p)^{|\omega^{-1}(0)|}\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{x\}}}p^{|\omega^{-1}(1)|}(1-p)^{|\omega^{-1}(0)|}\big[p\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-p)\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\big].

Note that

\displaystyle p\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}+(1-p)\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-(1-p)\left[\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\{\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1)\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\right],

)

\begin{split}\leq&\frac{C(m,\beta,h)}{1-p}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}\cap B_{m+1}(x)}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))\\
&\qquad+\frac{8e^{-ma}}{1-p}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\partial B_{m}(x)\cap\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
\leq&\frac{C(m,\beta,h)}{1-p}|B_{m+1}|\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))\\
&\qquad+\frac{8e^{-ma}}{1-p}|\partial B_{m}|\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}.\end{split}

(14)

Choose
N=N(\beta,p)\geq M
such that

\frac{8e^{-ma}}{1-p}|\partial B_{m}|<\frac{1}{2},~\forall m\geq N.

Then for each
m\geq N
, (
13
) and (
14
) give that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{x\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(m,\beta,h)}{1-p}|B_{m+1}|\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)\langle\sigma_{y};I_{\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

where the second double sum on the RHS of the inequality is the same as the first double sum on the LHS. So we obtain
that for each
m\geq N
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{x\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)\langle\sigma_{x};I_{\mathcal{H}(x,\omega)}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2C(m,\beta,h)}{1-p}|B_{m+1}|\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}}\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega^{(y)}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2C(m,\beta,h)}{1-p}|B_{m+1}|\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{(1)}}\frac{2^{|B_{3m}|}}{(\min\{p,1-p\})^{|B_{3m}|}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_{(1)}\setminus\{y\}}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\omega)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(y\text{ is pivotal for }\mathcal{H}(y,\omega)),

where the last inequality follows because of the finite energy property for independent percolation and that the map from
\omega
to
\omega^{(y)}
is at most
|B_{3m}|
to
1
. This completes the proof of (
12
) and thus the proposition.
∎

4
Proof of the main result

In this section, we use the differential inequality that we derived in Section
3
to prove our main result.
We always fix
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}))
in this section. We first show that the infinite-volume Ising measure
P_{\beta,h}
has a sharp phase transition in the
h
variable. Let
\{0\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial B_{n}\}
denote the event that there is a path of
+
spins which connects the origin to
\partial B_{n}
, and let
\{0\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}\infty\}
denote the event that the origin belongs to an infinite
+
cluster.

Proposition 5
(
[
16
]
).

For each
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}))
, there exists
h_{c}=h_{c}(\beta)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

•

there exists
c>0
such that
P_{\beta,h}(0\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}\infty)\geq c(h-h_{c})
for each
h>h_{c}
which is close to
h_{c}
;

•

for each
h<h_{c}
, there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(h)\in(0,\infty)
such that

P_{B_{n},\beta,h}^{+}(0\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial B_{n})\leq\exp[-c_{1}n].

One can check that the proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
16
]
also applies to the measure
P_{\beta,h}
. Indeed, the two key ingredients of the proof are valid in our setting: the FKG lattice property for the measure and a derivative formula similar to (16) in
[
16
]
. We also note that the argument applies to all
\beta\in[0,\infty)
, although we only need it for
\beta<\beta_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Since the
\gamma(p,h)
from Proposition
4
is continuous in
(p,h)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
, we can find
\delta\in(0,1/4)
,
H>2\delta
and
\Gamma>0
such that

\gamma(p,h)\leq\Gamma,~\forall(p,h)\in[\delta,1-\delta]\times[-H,H].

Take
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
such that
\tan\theta=1/\Gamma
.
Suppose that
(p_{0},h_{0})\in[2\delta,1-2\delta]\times[-H/2,H/2]
and define

(p(t),h(t)):=(p_{0},h_{0})+t\delta(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta),~t\in[0,1].

Then it is clear that
(p(t),h(t))\in[\delta,1-\delta]\times[-H,H]
for each
t\in[0,1]
. Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be finite and
\mathcal{H}
be the event defined in Proposition
4
. Then Proposition
4
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu_{\Lambda,p(t),h(t)}(\mathcal{H})}{dt}

\displaystyle=\delta\cos\theta\left.\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p}\right|_{(p,h)=(p(t),h(t))}-\delta\sin\theta\left.\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h}\right|_{(p,h)=(p(t),h(t))}

\displaystyle\geq\delta[\cos\theta-\Gamma\sin\theta]\left.\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p}\right|_{(p,h)=(p(t),h(t))}=0,~\forall t\in[0,1].

Integrating the last inequality from
0
to
1
gives

\mu_{\Lambda,p(1),h(1)}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{\Lambda,p_{0},h_{0}}(\mathcal{H}).

Setting
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we get

\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p(1),h(1)}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p_{0},h_{0}}(\mathcal{H}),

(15)

where
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p,h}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by

\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p,h}(\mathcal{H}):=\int_{\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{S}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{0}}}P_{\beta,h}(\mathcal{H}\text{ occurs on }\mathbb{S}_{0}\cup\omega^{-1}(1))\mathbb{P}_{p}(d\omega)

with
\mathbb{P}_{p}
denoting the independent Bernoulli site percolation measure with parameter
p
.
We now pick
(p_{0},h_{0})=(1/2,0)
. Then the inequality (
15
) and the monotonicity of
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p,h}(\mathcal{H})
in
p
imply that

\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},1,-\delta\sin\theta}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},1/2+\delta\cos\theta,-\delta\sin\theta}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},1/2,0}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},0,0}(\mathcal{H}).

See Figure
3
for an illustration of this monotonicity.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}:=\{\exists\text{ LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1}\}
. The structure of
\mathbb{T}
implies that the complement of
\{\exists\text{ LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{0}\}
is
\{\exists\text{ top to bottom }-\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{0}\}
. This, combined with the
\pm
symmetry of
P_{\beta,0}
, gives that

\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},0,0}(\mathcal{H})=P_{\beta,0}(\exists\text{ LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{0})=1/2,~\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

So we just proved

P_{\beta,-\delta\sin\theta}(\exists\text{ LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1})=\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},1,-\delta\sin\theta}(\mathcal{H})\geq 1/2,~\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proposition
5
then implies that

h_{c}(\beta)\leq-\delta\sin\theta<0,

and there is an infinite
+
cluster
P_{\beta,h}
almost surely whenever
h>h_{c}(\beta)
; this is because if
h_{c}(\beta)>-\delta\sin\theta
, then we would have

\displaystyle P_{\beta,-\delta\sin\theta}(\exists\text{ LR}+\text{crossing in }B_{n}\cap\mathbb{S}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq P_{\beta,-\delta\sin\theta}(\exists x\in B_{n}\text{ s.t. }x\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}(x+\partial B_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq|B_{n}|P^{+}_{B_{n},\beta,-\delta\sin\theta}(0\overset{+}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial B_{n})\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

By
\pm
symmetry, there is also an infinite
-
cluster
P_{\beta,h}
almost surely whenever
h<|h_{c}(\beta)|
. The uniqueness part follows from the Burton-Keane argument
[
11
]
.

An illustration of the monotonicity of
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p,h}(\mathcal{H})
with respect to
p
and
h
. The line segment from
(1/2,0)

-\tan\theta

.
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{3},p,h}(\mathcal{H})
is increasing along the three consecutive directed line segments started from
(0,0)
and ended at
(1,-\delta\sin\theta)
.

A reversed inequality

In this appendix, we prove that the reversed inequality (with a different function
\gamma
) in Proposition
4
also holds for general increasing events
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
. We do not need this for the proof of our main result, but it might be useful elsewhere. Recall the definition of
\sigma^{\Delta\pm}
in (
3
); if
\Delta=\{x\}
, we simply write
\sigma^{x\pm}
. For any increasing event
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
, we define

A_{x}^{+}:=\{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}:\sigma^{x+}\in A\},~A_{x}^{-}:=\{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\Lambda}:\sigma^{x-}\in A\}.

Note that
A_{x}^{\pm}
depend on
x\in\Lambda
but not on the status of
\sigma_{x}
.
Then for any
x\in\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}=A_{x}^{+}\setminus A_{x}^{-},\{x\text{ is}+\text{pivotal for }A\}=A\setminus A_{x}^{-},

\displaystyle\{x\text{ is}-\text{pivotal for }A\}=A_{x}^{+}\setminus A.

Lemma .1
.

For any
x\in\Lambda
and any increasing event
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
, we have

•

if
A_{x}^{-}=\emptyset
and
P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)\neq 0
, then

P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)=\frac{\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}}{2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1)}\frac{1}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|x\text{ is pivotal for }A)};

•

if
A_{x}^{-}\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}}{2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|A_{x}^{+})}+\frac{\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}}{2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|A_{x}^{-})}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A_{x}^{+}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}}{2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|A)}-\frac{\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A_{x}^{-}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}}{2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|A)}.

Remark 7
.

Note that if
A_{x}^{-}\neq\emptyset
then
\{A,\sigma_{x}=-1\}=\{A_{x}^{-},\sigma_{x}=-1\}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}
and
A_{x}^{\pm}
do not depend on the status of
\sigma_{x}
, all denominators in the lemma except the last two are between
c_{1}(\beta,h)
and
c_{2}(\beta,h)
with

\displaystyle c_{1}(\beta,h):=\min\{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|\sigma_{y}=-1,\forall y\neq x),P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|\sigma_{y}=+1,\forall y\neq x)\},

\displaystyle c_{2}(\beta,h):=\max\{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|\sigma_{y}=+1,\forall y\neq x),P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|\sigma_{y}=-1,\forall y\neq x)\}.

So we have pointwise (in
x\in\Lambda
) upper bound between
P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)
and
\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
. But by considering the special event
A=I_{\{\sigma_{0}=+1\}}
, one can see that a pointwise lower bound doesn’t hold for general
A
(it fails for each
x\neq 0
). This suggests that the other direction of the inequality when summing over
x\in\Lambda
, as in Proposition
4
, is much harder to obtain.

For ease of reference, we state the following corollary, which corresponds to the reverse inequality in Proposition
4
.

Corollary .2
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
4
, with
\gamma
replaced by
\tilde{\gamma}
,

\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial h}\geq\tilde{\gamma}(p,h)\frac{\partial\mu_{\Lambda,p,h}(\mathcal{H})}{\partial p}.

The same inequality also holds for all increasing events
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}
.

If
A_{x}^{-}=\emptyset
, then
\{A\text{ occurs on }\Lambda\setminus\{x\}\}=\emptyset
. So by (
4
), we have

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}&=\langle\sigma_{x};I_{A}I_{\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}=\langle\sigma_{x}I_{A}I_{\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
&\qquad-\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\langle I_{A}I_{\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\\
&=\left[1-\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\right]P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A\cap\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\})\\
&=2P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A),\end{split}

(16)

where we have used
A\subset A_{x}^{+}=\{x\text{ is pivotal for }A\}
in the last inequality. On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)=P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A,\sigma_{x}=+1)

\displaystyle\qquad+P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A,\sigma_{x}=-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[1+\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|x\text{ is pivotal for }A)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|x\text{ is pivotal for }A)}\right]P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A,\sigma_{x}=+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{+},\sigma_{x}=+1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|x\text{ is pivotal for }A)}=\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|x\text{ is pivotal for }A)}.

This and (
16
) completes the proof of the first identity in the lemma.

For the second identity, we have

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(x\text{ is pivotal for }A)=P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{+})-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=+1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|A_{x}^{+})}-\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=-1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|A_{x}^{-})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=+1)-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1|A_{x}^{+})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1)-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=-1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1|A_{x}^{-})}

\displaystyle\qquad+P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{+})P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=+1)-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{+},\sigma_{x}=+1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=+1)}

\displaystyle\qquad+P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A)\frac{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{-},\sigma_{x}=-1)-P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A_{x}^{-})P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(\sigma_{x}=-1)}{P_{\Lambda,\beta,h}(A,\sigma_{x}=-1)},

which clearly implies the desired identity.
∎

Nonstabilizerness and Error Resilience in Noisy Quantum Circuits Preprint: APS/123-QED
Fabian Ballar Trigueros, José Antonio Marín Guzmán

Abstract
We investigate how noise impacts nonstabilizerness—a key resource for quantum advantage—in many-body qubit systems. While noise typically degrades quantum resources, we show that amplitude damping, a nonunital channel, can generate or enhance magic, whereas depolarizing noise provably cannot. In an encoding-decoding protocol, we find that, unlike in the coherent case, a sharp decoding fidelity transition does not match a transition in nonstabilizerness. Our results point toward the possibility of leveraging, rather than merely mitigating, noise for quantum information processing.



Understanding how quantum properties degrade—or, in some cases, persist—under realistic noise is a central challenge in quantum information. Among the various signatures of quantumness, nonstabilizerness—or magic—has emerged as a key resource: it quantifies how far a state deviates from the set of stabilizer states, which can be efficiently simulated classically
Gottesman 1998
;
Gottesman 2024
. Nonstabilizerness is not only central to universal quantum computation

;
Howard and Campbell 2017
, but also informs the study of many-body quantum systems

;
White et al. 2021
;
Tarabunga 2024
;
Magni et al. 2025
, noise thresholds, and the capabilities of quantum devices in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum era
Preskill 2018
;
Nelson et al. 2024
;
Wei and Liu 2024
and beyond.

Several approaches have been proposed to quantify magic in qubit systems, which are central to most experimental platforms and subject to physically motivated noise
Chirolli and Burkard 2008
;
Blais et al. 2021
;
Ghosh et al. 2012
. Stabilizer Rényi entropies (SREs)
Leone et al. 2022
;
Haug and Piroli 2023
offer a proxy that can be computed efficiently even in large-scale simulations. However, SREs are not monotones for mixed states
Leone and Bittel 2024
;
Haug and Piroli 2023
;
Tarabunga 2024
. A more rigorous alternative is the robustness of magic (ROM)
Howard and Campbell 2017
;
Heinrich and Gross 2019
, a faithful resource monotone that is nonincreasing under stabilizer-preserving operations, though it becomes demanding to compute for large systems due to the superexponential number of stabilizer states
Gross 2006
;
Hamaguchi et al. 2024a
;
Hamaguchi et al. 2025
.

In this work, we study how incoherent noise affects the amount of magic in many-body qubit systems. While noise is generally expected to degrade quantum resources, we find that this is not always the case: amplitude damping—a nonunital channel—can generate or enhance magic depending on the input state and noise strength. In contrast, we show that depolarizing noise universally suppresses magic and cannot generate it, contrary to recent claims
Wang and Li 2025
. We use ROM to show how magic behaves under noise, revealing features that proxies like SREs fail to capture.

As a concrete test of our findings, we study an encoding-decoding protocol subjected to amplitude damping noise. Previous works have shown that such setups can exhibit phase transitions:
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024
established an error-resilience transition in the decoding fidelity, while
Niroula et al. 2024
demonstrated that coherent noise can induce a transition in the magic content of the decoded state. In contrast, we find that under amplitude damping, the decoding fidelity undergoes a sharp transition, but no corresponding feature appears in the robustness of magic—highlighting that these transitions need not coincide in general.

Quantifying nonstabilizerness.—
Quantum circuits composed of Clifford gates, generated by
S=\sqrt{Z}
,
H=(X+Z)/\sqrt{2}
, and CNOT, can be efficiently simulated classically. Any state obtained by applying such a circuit to
|0\rangle^{\otimes N}
is a stabilizer state. To achieve universal quantum computation, non-Clifford resources are required
Gottesman 1998
. The extent to which a quantum state deviates from a stabilizer state constitutes a computational resource, known as nonstabilizerness, or magic.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{N}
denote the set of
N
-qubit stabilizer states. The robustness of magic (ROM)
Howard and Campbell 2017
is defined as

\mathcal{R}(\rho)=\min\left\{\sum_{i}|x_{i}|\;\middle|\;\rho=\sum_{i}x_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,\;|\psi_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{S}_{N}\right\},

(1)

where the minimization runs over all decompositions into stabilizer states
|\psi_{i}\rangle
. For stabilizer states,
\mathcal{R}(\rho)=1
; for magic states,
\mathcal{R}(\rho)>1
, quantifying their distance from the convex hull of stabilizer states, known as the stabilizer polytope
Heinrich and Gross 2019
.

ROM satisfies several key properties
Heinrich and Gross 2019
:
(i)
Faithfulness
:
\mathcal{R}(\rho)=1
if and only if
\rho
is a stabilizer state;
(ii)
Submultiplicativity
:
\mathcal{R}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})\leq\mathcal{R}(\rho_{1})\mathcal{R}(\rho_{2})
;
(iii)
Monotonicity
: ROM does not increase under stabilizer-preserving quantum channels, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{E}(\rho))\leq\mathcal{R}(\rho)
;
(iv)
Convexity
: For any ensemble
\{p_{k},\rho_{k}\}
,
\mathcal{R}(\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho_{k})\leq\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{R}(\rho_{k})
.

Any quantum state
\rho
can be expanded as
\rho=\sum_{i}c_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
, where
{|\psi_{i}\rangle}
are stabilizer states forming an overcomplete basis of the Hermitian operator space. States within the stabilizer polytope satisfy
c_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}c_{i}=1
, and are nonmagic. Magic states, by contrast, include negative components in any such decomposition. The degree of this negativity, captured by the minimal total weight
\sum_{i}|c_{i}|
, defines the state’s ROM.

\mathcal{S}_{N}
grows superexponentially with system size as
|\mathcal{S}_{N}|=2^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(2^{i}+1)

Gross 2006
, making ROM computation challenging. Brute-force methods are limited to
N\leq 5
, while more advanced linear-programming approaches, such as column generation
Hamaguchi et al. 2024a
, enable exact evaluations up to
N=8
qubits. For larger systems, computing ROM becomes infeasible. Despite its considerable computational cost, the ROM constitutes a reliable measure for quantifying nonstabilizerness, particularly for mixed states.

An alternative measure of magic is the stabilizer Rényi entropy
Leone et al. 2022
. The Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{N}
consists of tensor products of
\mathds{1},X,Y,
and
Z
on
N
qubits, with an overall phase of
\pm 1
or
\pm i
. For an
N
-qubit state
\rho
, the SRE is

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha}(\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left[\ln\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{|\operatorname{tr}(\rho P)|^{2\alpha}}{2^{N}}\right)-\ln\mathrm{tr}(\rho^{2})\right].

(2)

SREs possess some desirable properties for quantifying pure-state nonstabilizerness: they are faithful and remain invariant under stabilizer operations.

This calculation is significantly more efficient than the superexponential complexity of the ROM. New techniques are capable of computing the SRE for systems of up to
24
qubits
Turkeshi et al. 2025a
. Furthermore, Markov chain methods, neural quantum state, and tensor network protocols have been developed to improve computational efficiency
Tarabunga et al. 2023
;
Tarabunga et al. 2024
;
Sinibaldi et al. 2025
;
Turkeshi et al. 2025a
;
Turkeshi et al. 2025b
.

For mixed states, this quantity can reliably certify a stabilizer state only when the state admits a purification that is itself a stabilizer state
Tarabunga 2024
;
Ding et al. 2025
;
Tarabunga and Haug 2025
; SREs do not represent faithful measures of magic for general mixed states. An extension using convex roof constructions has been proposed, but it requires an optimization procedure impractical for large systems
Leone and Bittel 2024
.

Noise channels.—
Noise in quantum devices is intricate, and its accurate modeling presents a significant challenge. A quantum channel can be expressed as a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) operation, defined in terms of Kraus operators as
\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\sum_{i}K_{i}\rho K_{i}^{\dagger}
, subject to the constraint
\sum_{i}K_{i}^{\dagger}K_{i}=\mathds{1}
, which ensures trace preservation
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
.

We study noise through two paradigmatic models: depolarizing noise and generalized amplitude damping. The depolarizing channel is an example of unital noise, a channel that maps the identity matrix to itself. We show that an instance of nonunital noise, generalized amplitude damping, has a nontrivial effect on quantum states’ nonstabilizerness.

The depolarizing channel,
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)=\frac{p}{2^{N}}\mathds{1}+(1-p)\rho
, describes a mixture of the input state and the maximally mixed state;
p
tunes the noise strength. To probe this noise’s effect on quantum resources, we evaluate ROM and SRE as diagnostic measures in Fig.

|0\rangle
and
|H\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+e^{i\pi/4}|1\rangle)
to depolarizing noise, and analyze their magic as a function of noise strength.
For
|0\rangle
, the SRE is nonzero for all
0<p<1
, while ROM remains one, correctly indicating the absence of magic. For the magic
|H\rangle
, the SRE spuriously increases at low
p
, whereas the ROM decays monotonically. Both initial-state choices highlight SREs’ failure to capture true resource behavior under noise.

Depolarizing noise, being a convex mixture of Pauli operations, cannot generate magic. The convexity of ROM ensures that
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho))\leq(1-p)\,\mathcal{R}(\rho)+p

The discrepancy between the expected influence of depolarizing noise and the SRE numerics highlights the unfaithfulness of SRE under magic-destroying noise. In the supplementary material, we further emphasize this point with analytical evidence.

Nonstabilizerness and noise.—
Incoherent noise is typically expected to degrade quantum resources by decohering quantum states toward classicality. Indeed, the depolarizing channel cannot generate magic and instead monotonically depletes it from any input state. This raises a broader question: Is the destruction of nonstabilizerness a universal trait of noise, or can some channels enhance a state’s quantumness? While prior work has shown that strong depolarizing noise can render states efficiently simulable within the stabilizer formalism
Nelson et al. 2024
, we demonstrate that general amplitude damping, a physically realistic nonunital channel, can instead enhance or even generate magic, showing that dissipation can inject nonstabilizerness into quantum systems.

To illustrate this effect concretely, we begin by analyzing a single qubit. Figure
2
(
B
) shows ROM for three single-qubit states subject to amplitude damping:
|+\rangle
, the magic state
|H\rangle
, and
|0\rangle
. We find that the channel can indeed generate magic, depending on the input state. For instance, starting from stabilizer inputs such as
|+\rangle
, the channel generates magic over a wide range of damping strengths before the resource is eventually lost. In contrast, the
|0\rangle
state remains unaffected, as it is the fixed point of the channel.

Notably, when the input is already a nonstabilizer state, such as
|H\rangle
, the channel can enhance the magic at intermediate noise strengths before eventually decaying. This behavior indicates that amplitude damping can not only generate magic from stabilizer inputs but also amplify the resource content of states that are already magical. These results open intriguing possibilities for leveraging certain noise processes to prepare resourceful states for quantum computation.

Figures

C-D

\eta

|H\rangle

For the single-qubit standard ADC, one can visualize magic generation through a simple geometrical picture. The channel’s effect describes trajectories, parametrized by

Encoding-decoding circuits.—
The encoding-decoding transition, introduced in previous works
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024
;
Niroula et al. 2024
, serves as a probe of the robustness of quantum codes under noise. It refers to a sharp change in decoding fidelity as a function of noise strength: below a critical threshold, the logical information can be successfully recovered, while above it, decoding fails. For coherent errors, this transition has been linked to a coinciding transition in nonstabilizerness.

In Fig.
1
(
A
), we illustrate the noisy encoding-decoding circuit. We initialize an
N
-qubit state
\rho_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|_{L}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{A}
, where the first register corresponds to a
k
-qubit logical state and the second consists of ancilla qubits used for syndrome measurement during error correction. To implement a global Clifford unitary, the system evolves under a circuit composed of
4N

XX(\pi/4)
rotations to
N/2
randomly chosen pairs of qubits. The overall structure resembles that of Ref.
Niroula et al. 2024
, though we incorporate nonunital noise and postselection. We denote by
U
the unitary part of the circuit before noise and measurement.

After this evolution, the state is subjected to local noise modeled by a quantum channel of the form
\mathcal{C}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{i}
, where each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
acts independently on qubit
i
. Explicitly, this corresponds to
\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\sum_{\vec{j}}K_{\vec{j}}\rho K_{\vec{j}}^{\dagger}
, where the sum runs over all
N
-tuples
\vec{j}=(j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{N})
, and each Kraus operator has the form
K_{\vec{j}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}K_{j_{i}}^{(i)}
, with
K_{j_{i}}^{(i)}
a Kraus operator of the local channel
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. We focus on two types of local noise: the depolarizing and amplitude damping channels. To decode, we reverse the unitary dynamics by applying
U^{\dagger}
, yielding the intermediate state
\rho=U^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger})U
. We then measure whether all ancilla qubits are in the
|0\rangle
state and post-select on this outcome
2
2

2

To avoid potential numerical noise from simultaneously tracking two transitions, we instead post-select on a fixed outcome.
. The resulting logical state is given by
\rho_{L}=\langle 0_{A}|\rho|0_{A}\rangle/\mathrm{Tr}(\langle 0_{A}|\rho|0_{A}\rangle)
.

A clear example of the decoupling between decoding and nonstabilizerness transitions arises under depolarizing noise. As shown in
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024
, the system exhibits an encoding-decoding transition: above a critical noise strength, the decoding fidelity drops sharply, signaling irrecoverable logical information. However, since the depolarizing channel cannot generate magic, and the entire circuit remains Clifford, the state stays within the stabilizer subspace. No transition in nonstabilizerness occurs, showing that such transitions are not generic in noisy circuits.

The observed decoupling between the encoding–decoding and nonstabilizerness transitions could seem like a consequence of depolarizing noise’s simplicity. One could argue that a more complex noise model—capable of generating magic—might induce both transitions simultaneously. While this possibility may seem plausible, noise channels are generally associated with decoherence and classicalization, rather than the generation of quantum resources. Nevertheless, as we show below, even when the noise can generate magic, no transition in nonstabilizerness is observed.

In Fig.
1
(
B–C
), we show the decoding fidelity and the robustness of magic as functions of the amplitude-damping parameter
p
. To calculate the fidelity, we use an analytical approach based on the replica trick. This follows the method developed in Ref.
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024

p_{c}=2^{(3+r)/2}-2^{r+1}
. The fidelity under amplitude-damping noise is

F_{\textrm{AD}}=\frac{(2^{N}-1)\left[1+(1+\sqrt{1-p}-p/2)^{N}\right]}{(2^{N}-2^{k})\left[1+\sqrt{1-p}-p/2\right]^{N}+2^{N+k}-1}.

(5)

We provide further details of this calculation in the supplementary material. Since we begin with a stabilizer logical code, this transition implies that below the critical threshold, the system generates no magic, even in the thermodynamic limit. The natural question, then, is whether the system develops an increasing amount of magic for
p>p_{c}
, potentially signaling a corresponding phase transition in nonstabilizerness.

The magic of mixed states is notoriously difficult to study, lacking both tractable analytical forms and straightforward numerical treatments. We employ the column-generation algorithm developed in
Hamaguchi et al. 2024b
to compute the ROM averaged over Clifford unitaries. As shown in Fig.
1
(
C
), the robustness of magic decreases with increasing system size. Thus, even in the presence of a magic-generating channel, the system shows no evidence of a phase transition in nonstabilizerness. However, as detailed in the supplementary material, this decay stems from the destructive effect of post-selection; evaluating the magic immediately after the noise layer reveals no such suppression with system size.

Our results demonstrate that, even when the noise can generate magic, an encoding-decoding transition does not imply a concurrent transition in nonstabilizerness. This decoupling underscores the fact that decodability and nonstabilizerness capture fundamentally different aspects of how quantum systems respond to noise.

Conclusion and outlook.—
In this work, we investigated the interplay between noise and quantum nonstabilizerness in qubit systems, with a focus on the behavior of mixed-state magic under nonunital noise. Using the robustness of magic, we analytically and numerically evaluated the impact of various noise channels on quantum states. Our results provide evidence that nonunital channels, such as amplitude damping, can not only preserve but even generate magic, depending on the input state and noise strength. In contrast, we confirmed that depolarizing noise strictly destroys magic and cannot serve as a resource-generating process.

We apply these insights to encoding-decoding transitions in noisy quantum circuits. While this setup exhibits a clear phase transition in decoding fidelity, we found no corresponding transition in the robustness of magic, even under noise models capable of generating magic. Opposite to the case of coherent errors, where these two transitions seem to coincide
Sierant and Turkeshi 2025
, our results demonstrate that this connection breaks down in the presence of realistic, nonunitary noise.

Our findings open several directions: harnessing nonunital noise as a resource rather than merely mitigating its effects, motivating the development of scalable magic measures that remain faithful to robustness, and deepening our understanding of magic in the presence of realistic noise. The first point may relate to broader efforts in enhancing quantum information processing through engineered dissipation
Verstraete et al. 2009
;
Harrington et al. 2022
. As quantum devices scale, diagnosing and quantifying magic will be essential for achieving quantum advantage. The observed decoupling between decoding and magic transitions highlights the need for refined frameworks to classify noise-driven phase transitions.

In this appendix, we provide an analytic computation of the SRE under the effect of depolarizing noise. It is important to emphasize that the SRE is ill-conditioned in this context and should not be used as a reliable measure. As discussed in the main text, robustness of magic is required to characterize nonstabilizerness in such cases accurately. The depolarizing noise is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)=\frac{p}{2^{N}}\mathds{1}+(1-p)\rho,

(6)

where the parameter
p
determines the proportion of the original state
\rho
mixed with the maximally mixed state
\mathds{1}
. Our goal is to study the effect of depolarizing noise on an initially pure stabilizer state
\rho
. Specifically, we compute the SRE extension for mixed states without the convex roof construction, as defined in Eq.
2
. The main term to evaluate is
\Tr[\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)P]
, which can be expressed as:

\Tr[\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)P]=\Tr\left[\frac{p}{2^{N}}P+(1-p)\rho P\right]=(1-p)\Tr[\rho P]+\frac{p}{2^{N}}\Tr[P].

(7)

For all Pauli strings
P\neq\mathds{1}
, the last term is zero. Substituting this result, the SRE becomes:

\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\big(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)\big)=-\ln\left[\frac{1+(1-p)^{4}\sum_{P\neq\mathds{1}}\Tr[\rho P]^{4}}{1+(1-p)^{2}\sum_{P\neq\mathds{1}}\Tr[\rho P]^{2}}\right].

(8)

The structure of this expression is similar to that of the SRE for the original state, but the Pauli terms are scaled by a factor of
(1-p)
. For stabilizer states, it holds that:

\displaystyle\sum_{P\neq\mathds{1}}\Tr[\rho P]^{2}=2^{N}-1,

(9)

which follows from writing
\rho
in the Pauli basis,
\rho=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}P_{i}
, and enforcing the purity condition. Substituting this property into the SRE expression, we arrive at:

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\big(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)\big)=-\ln\left(\frac{1+(1-p)^{4}(2^{N}-1)}{1+(1-p)^{2}(2^{N}-1)}\right).

(10)

We can further compute this quantity in the thermodynamic limit (
N\rightarrow\infty
), however, this is not more informative. This result implies that
\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\big(\mathcal{D}_{0}(\rho)\big)=\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\big(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\rho)\big)=0
, and
\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\big(\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)\big)>0
for all
0<p<1
. As highlighted in the main text, this behavior contrasts sharply with the nonstabilizerness measured by the robustness of magic. This establishes that SREs (without the convex roof construction) are unsuitable as a measure of magic for mixed states.

An important remark: we focus on the behavior of individual states as a function of channel noise. Alternative perspectives exist, such as evaluating the general magic-generating capacity of a channel. These approaches typically require constructing the channel’s associated Choi operator and computing its magic. In our study, we numerically analyzed systems of up to 4 qubits and found no evidence that the depolarizing channel generates magic. Specifically, we evaluated all possible pure stabilizer states for up to 4 qubits, applied the depolarizing channel, and tracked the robustness of magic. Across all cases, the ROM remained stable at 1, indicating that no magic was generated at any noise strength.

A broader argument suggests that depolarizing noise is incapable of generating magic for an initially stabilizer state. In particular, if we consider a stabilizer state, with optimal pseudomixture
\rho=\sum_{i}x_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
, where
x_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
, and where
|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
corresponds to the vertices of the stabilizer polytope. The action of the depolarizing channel can be understood geometrically as a straight-line trajectory from the initial state towards the maximally mixed state. Hence, to show that depolarizing noise cannot generate magic, it is sufficient to show that the straight line between any point inside the stabilizer polytope and the origin remains inside the polytope.

Rewriting the depolarizing channel in the basis of pure stabilizer states:

\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+\frac{p}{2^{N}}I.

(11)

Using the fact that the identity can be expressed as a uniform mixture of all pure stabilizer states,

\frac{I}{2^{N}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{N}|}\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,

(12)

where
|\mathcal{S}_{N}|
is the number of pure
N
-qubit stabilizer states, we obtain:

\mathcal{D}_{p}(\rho)=\sum_{i}\underbrace{\left[(1-p)x_{i}+\frac{p}{|\mathcal{S}_{N}|}\right]}_{\equiv z_{i}}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|.

(13)

To show that the transformed state remains within the stabilizer polytope, we verify that the new coefficients
z_{i}
define a valid convex combination.

First, nonnegativity follows from the fact that each term in
z_{i}
is nonnegative:

z_{i}=(1-p)x_{i}+\frac{p}{|\mathcal{S}_{N}|}\geq 0.

(14)

Next, normalization is verified as follows:

\sum_{i}z_{i}=(1-p)\underbrace{\sum_{i}x_{i}}_{=1}+\frac{p}{|\mathcal{S}_{N}|}\underbrace{\sum_{i}}_{|\mathcal{S}_{N}|}.

(15)

Since
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i}1=|\mathcal{S}_{N}|
, we obtain:

\sum_{i}z_{i}=(1-p)+p=1.

(16)

Since
\{z_{i}\}
define a valid convex combination of stabilizer states, the resulting state remains inside the stabilizer polytope.

Thus, we conclude that depolarizing noise cannot generate magic from an initially stabilizer state. The depolarizing channel simply shrinks the Bloch vector toward the maximally mixed state, and since the stabilizer polytope is convex, this trajectory remains inside it at all times.

Fidelity Averaged Over the Haar Measure for Amplitude Damping

To compute the Haar-averaged fidelity in the presence of amplitude damping analytically, we follow the approach outlined in
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024
. A detailed derivation can be found in that work; here, we provide a high-level overview of the key steps. Notably, since the Clifford group forms a unitary 3-design
Zhu et al. 2016

The computation relies on the
k

\Phi_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}(\mathcal{O})=\int_{U\in\mathcal{U}(2^{N})}d\mu(U)\left[\left(U^{\dagger}\right)^{\otimes k}\mathcal{O}\ U^{\otimes k}\right].

(17)

Since
\Phi_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}(\mathcal{O})
belongs to the commutant of
\mathcal{U}(2^{N})
, Schur-Weyl duality allows us to express it as a linear combination of permutation operators, significantly simplifying the computation
Gross et al. 2021

\Phi_{\text{Haar}}^{(k)}(\mathcal{O})=\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}b_{\pi}(\mathcal{O})T_{\pi}.

(18)

Here,
T_{\pi}
are representations of the permutation group
S_{k}
on the
k
-replica space, and the coefficients are given by

b_{\pi}(\mathcal{O})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}W_{\pi,\sigma}\Tr(\mathcal{O}T_{\sigma}),

(19)

where
W_{\pi,\sigma}
are the Weingarten symbols
Roberts and Yoshida 2017
. As shown in
Turkeshi and Sierant 2024
, the fidelity in our encoding-decoding setup can be computed via the replica formalism as
\Phi^{(2)}(F)

F=\frac{\langle 0_{\bar{X}}|U^{\dagger}\mathcal{N}_{p}(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger})U|0_{\bar{X}}\rangle}{\Tr[\langle 0_{\bar{X}}|U^{\dagger}\mathcal{N}_{p}(U\rho_{0}U^{\dagger})U|0_{\bar{X}}\rangle]},

(20)

with
\rho_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes N}
as the initial state and
\mathcal{N}_{p}
the amplitude damping channel. An important remark is that here, we’re technically averaging the numerator and denominator separately, and there is no guarantee that the ratio might have self-averaging. That is why in our data, we also show finite-size numerical data to show how this matches. The problem thus reduces to computing
b_{\pi}

b_{\pi}(\mathcal{N}_{p})=\sum_{\tau}W_{\pi,\tau}\Tr\left[t_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}K_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{\dagger}\right)^{N}\right].

(21)

The term inside the trace, denoted as

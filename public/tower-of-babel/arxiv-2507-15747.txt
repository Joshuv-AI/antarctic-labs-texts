Abstract
. In this paper we study a class of critical Choquard equations with a symmetric potential, i.e. we consider the equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( | x | ) ​ u = ( | x | − μ ∗ | u | 2 μ ⋆ ) ​ | u | 2 μ ⋆ − 2 ​ u , in ℝ N -\Delta u+V(|x|)u=\left(|x|^{-\mu}*|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u,\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N} where V ⁡ ( | x | ) \displaystyle V(|x|) is a bounded, nonnegative and symmetric potential in ℝ N \displaystyle{\mathbb{R}^{N}} with N ≥ 5 \displaystyle N\geq 5 , 0 < μ ≤ 4 \displaystyle 0<\mu\leq 4 , ∗ \displaystyle* stands for the standard convolution and 2 μ ⋆ := 2 ​ N − μ N − 2 \displaystyle 2^{\star}_{\mu}:=\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2} is the upper critical exponent in the sense of the Hardy - Littlewood - Sobolev inequality. By applying a finite dimensional reduction method we prove that if r 2 ​ V ​ ( r ) \displaystyle r^{2}V(r) has a local maximum point or local minimum point r 0 > 0 \displaystyle r_{0}>0 with V ⁡ ( r 0 ) > 0 \displaystyle V(r_{0})>0 then the problem has infinitely many non-radial solutions with arbitrary large energies.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the nonlinear Choquard equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\left(|x|^{-\mu}*|u|^{q}\right)|u|^{q-2}u,\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

This equation has attracted the attention and the interest
of a lot of researchers since it arises in various physical models especially in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\displaystyle q=2
and
\displaystyle\mu=1
. Indeed, in this case, it seems to originate from H. Fröhlich and S. Pekar’s model of the polaron, where free electrons in an ionic lattice interact with phonons associated to deformations of the lattice or with the polarization that it creates on the medium (interaction of an electron with its own hole)
[
14
,
15
,
26
]
. The Choquard equation was also introduced by Ph. Choquard in 1976 in the modelling of a one-component plasma. The first that considers (
1.1
) from a mathematical view point was Lieb
[
23
]
who proved the existence and uniqueness, up to translations, of the ground state for (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle N=3,\mu=1,q=2
and
\displaystyle V
a positive constant.
The results obtained by Lieb were subsequently improved and extended by Lions
[
20
,
21
]
. In particular, he showed the existence of a sequence of radially symmetric solutions.

When
\displaystyle V
is a constant, Moroz and Van Schaftingen
[
28
]
proved regularity, positivity and radial symmetry of the ground states for an optimal range of
\displaystyle q
, and described asymptotic decay of solutions at infinity.

Ackermann
[
1
]
, instead, proposed a new approach to prove the existence of infinitely many geometrically distinct weak solutions in the case of a periodic potential
\displaystyle V
.

There are also a lot of works concerning the existence and concentration behavior of semiclassical solutions for the singularly perturbed subcritical Choquard equation (see f.i.
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
12
,
29
,
32
]
). Among them, by using a Lyapunov-Schmidt type reduction method, Wei and Winter
[
32
]
constructed families of positive solutions and Chen
[
6
]
constructed multiple semiclassical solutions to the Choquard equation with an external potential by Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction argument. Moreover, Luo et al.
[
25
]
established the uniqueness of positive solutions that concentrate at the non-degenerate critical points of the potential by combining a local type of Pohozaev identities and blow-up techniques.

We also refer to
[
30
]
for a guide regarding the Choquard equation and references therein.

In what follows we consider the so-called
critical Choquard equation
. In order to clarify the meaning of critical we recall the well-known Hardy - Littlewood - Sobolev inequality (see
[
24
]
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle t,r>1
and
\displaystyle 0<\mu<N
with
\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N}+\frac{1}{r}=2
. There exists a sharp constant
\displaystyle C=C(t,N,\mu,r)
such that for any
\displaystyle f\in L^{t}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
and
\displaystyle h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R})

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(x)||h(y)|}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\|f\|_{t}\|h\|_{r}.

If
\displaystyle t=r=\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}
then

(1.2)

C(t,N,\mu,r)=C(N,\mu)=\pi^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(N-\frac{\mu}{2}\right)}\left\{\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(N)}\right\}^{-1+\frac{\mu}{N}}.

In this case the equality is achieved if and only if
\displaystyle f\equiv Ch
and

h(x)=A\left(\gamma^{2}+|x-a|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{2N-\mu}{2}}

for some
\displaystyle A\in\mathbb{C}
,
\displaystyle 0\neq\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Notice that, by the Hardy - Littlewood - Sobolev inequality, the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{q}|u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dx\,dy

is well-defined if
\displaystyle|u|^{q}\in L^{t}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
for some
\displaystyle t>1
satisfying

\frac{2}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N}=2.

Thus, for
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
, by Sobolev embedding theorems, we know that

2\leq tq\leq 2^{\star}=\frac{2N}{N-2}

namely

\frac{2N-\mu}{N}\leq q\leq\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2}.

Thus,
\displaystyle\frac{2N-\mu}{N}
is called the lower critical exponent while
\displaystyle 2^{*}_{\mu}=\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2}
is the upper critical exponent in the sense of the Hardy - Littlewood - Sobolev inequality.

For the upper critical case, by using the moving plane methods in integral developed in
[
7
,
8
]
, Lei
[
19
]
, Du and Yang
[
13
]
, Guo et al.
[
18
]
classified independently the positive solutions of the critical Hartree equation

(1.3)

-\Delta u=\left(|x|^{-\mu}*|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u,\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}

and proved that every positive solution of (
1.3
) must assume the form

(1.4)

{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi}}(x)=\alpha_{N,\mu}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}{\left(1+\lambda^{2}|x-\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}},\quad\lambda>0,\,\,x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

where

\alpha_{N,\mu}:=S^{\frac{(N-\mu)(2-N)}{4(N-\mu+2)}}C(N,\mu)^{\frac{2-N}{2(N-\mu+2)}}\left(N(N-2)\right)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}

being
\displaystyle S
the best constant of the Sobolev embedding and
\displaystyle C(N,\mu)
defined in (
1.2
).

The aim of this paper is to establish the existence of infinitely many solutions for the critical Choquard equation with a symmetric potential
\displaystyle V
of a special form, i.e.
\displaystyle V(x)=V(|x|)
, namely we will consider the following nonlocal equation in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+V(|x|)u=\left(|x|^{-\mu}*|u(y)|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u\quad&\hbox{in}\,\,{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\\
&u>0\quad&\hbox{in}\,\,{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
\displaystyle V(r)
is a bounded non-negative function,
\displaystyle N\geq 5
,
\displaystyle 0<\mu\leq 4
,
\displaystyle 2^{\star}_{\mu}=\frac{2N-\mu}{N-2}\geq 2
.

It follows from the Pohozaev identity that (
1.5
) has no solution if
\displaystyle(r^{2}V(r))^{\prime}
has fixed sign and is not identically zero. Therefore, we see that to obtain a solution for (
1.5
), it is necessary to assume that
\displaystyle r^{2}V(r)
has either a local maximum, or a local minimum at some
\displaystyle r_{0}>0
. The aim of this paper is to show that this condition also guarantees the existence of infinitely many non-radial solutions.

Our main result in this paper can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle V(|x|)\geq 0
is bounded and
\displaystyle N\geq 5
. If
\displaystyle r^{2}V(r)
has either an isolated local maximum, or an isolated local minimum at some
\displaystyle r_{0}>0
with
\displaystyle V(r_{0})>0
, then problem (
1.5
) has infinitely many non-radial solutions.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
1.2
is known when
\displaystyle N=6
,
\displaystyle\mu=4
and the potential
\displaystyle V(|x^{\prime}|,x^{\prime\prime})
is a bounded and asymmetric nonnegative function in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
(see
[
17
]
). Also in this case we strongly believe that the radial symmetry of
\displaystyle V
can be relaxed as in
[
17
]
or in
[
27
]
, by using a combination of the reduction method with local Pohozaev identities. Here, in order to avoid the technical difficulties that arise in the presence of the nonlocal term we only consider the symmetric case (as in
[
9
]
).

As far as we know this is the first result concerning the existence of infinitely many solutions by using the reduction scheme in a general way for this kind of nonlocal problems.

Remark 2
.

It is also interesting to consider the case of sign-changing solutions for the Choquard equation since the sign-changing solutions is hard to obtain due to the nonlocal interaction (see
[
16
,
31
]
).

We strongly believe that a finite dimensional argument can be now applied by constructing infinitely many sign-changing solutions for (
1.3
) or for the analogous problem in a subcritical regime with a symmetric potential where a non-degeneracy for the limit problem is known. This will be done in a forthcoming paper.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we will use a widely used technique in singularly perturbed elliptic problems which takes
\displaystyle k
which is the number of the bubbles in the solutions as a large parameter. This technique was succefully used also in other contexts and for other problems (see f.i.
[
33
]
and
[
5
]
).

Since we construct the solutions by using the so-called bubbles (see (
1.4
)) then our results provide also an asymptotic behavior of the solutions that we look for (see (
1.6
) and (
2.4
)).

In the next we will prove Theorem
1.2
by constructing solutions with large number of bubbles near the sphere
\displaystyle|y|=r_{0}
. So, in view of the construction of bubbling solutions, we can say that the effect from the critical growth is stronger than the effect from the unboundedness of the domain.

We outline the main ides in order to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Let us fix a positive integer
\displaystyle k\geq k_{0}
where
\displaystyle k_{0}
is large, which is to be determined later.

Let
\displaystyle x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}),\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2}.
Define

\displaystyle H_{s}:=

\displaystyle\{u:u\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}),u\,\hbox{is even in}\,x_{h},\,h=2,...,N,

\displaystyle u(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,x^{\prime\prime})=u\left(r\cos\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi}{k}\right),r\sin\left(\theta+\frac{2\pi}{k}\right),x^{\prime\prime}\right)\}.

Let us choose the centers of the bubbles as the
\displaystyle k
vertices of a regular
\displaystyle k-
polygon with radius
\displaystyle r
inside
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
as:

\xi_{j}:=\left(r\cos\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},r\sin\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},0\right),\quad j=1,...,k,

where
\displaystyle 0
is the zero vector in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
and let

(1.6)

\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}(x).

Choose
\displaystyle\delta>0
small, such that

V(|x|)\geq V_{0}>0,\quad\quad|x|\in[r_{0}-2\delta;r_{0}+2\delta].

We always assume that
\displaystyle r\in[r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta]
and

\lambda\in[L_{0}k^{\frac{N-2}{N-4}},L_{1}k^{\frac{N-2}{N-4}}]

for some constants
\displaystyle L_{1}>L_{0}>0
.

In order to perform the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction it is very important to understand the kernel of the linearized operator associated to the limit problem (
1.3
). Very recently, in
[
22
]
, it has been shown that the solutions of (
1.3
) are non-degenerate provided
\displaystyle 2^{\star}_{\mu}\geq 2
(i.e.
\displaystyle\mu\leq 4
), namely if we look for bounded solution of the linearized equation

(1.7)

-\Delta Z=\left[2^{\star}_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}*\mathcal{U}^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}Z\right)\mathcal{U}^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}+(2^{\star}_{\mu}-1)\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}*\mathcal{U}^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)\mathcal{U}^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}Z\right]\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}

then
\displaystyle Z\in{\rm span}\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{N},Z_{N+1}\rangle
where

Z_{i}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}}{\partial x_{i}},i=1,\ldots,N\quad Z_{N+1}=\frac{N-2}{2}\mathcal{U}+x\cdot\nabla\mathcal{U}.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we perform the finite-dimensional reduction studying the nonlinear projected problem. In Section
3.2
, we come to the variational reduction procedure and in Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.2
. Finally we give a list of some useful estimates in Section
5
.

2.
The finite dimensional reduction

In what follows we will carry out the finite-dimensional reduction argument in a weighted space in order to obtain a good estimate for the error term. Let

(2.1)

\|u\|_{*}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\tau}}\right)^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}|u(x)|

and

(2.2)

\|h\|_{**}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\frac{N+2}{2}+\tau}}\right)^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}|h(x)|

where
\displaystyle\tau=\frac{1}{2}
.

We let

\mathcal{B}_{**}:=\left\{h\in L^{\infty}\,:\,\|h\|_{**}<\infty\right\}.

We set

g(u):=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dy\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u.

Moreover we also set

g^{\prime}(u)v=2^{\star}_{\mu}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u(y)v(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dy\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}u+(2^{\star}_{\mu}-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dy\right)|u|^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-2}v.

Then we can rewrite the problem (
1.5
) in the following way

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+V(|x|)u=g(u)\quad\hbox{in}\,{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\\
&u>0\quad\hbox{in}\,{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

We look for a solution of the problem (
2.3
) of the form

(2.4)

u_{k}(x)=\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda}(x)+\phi_{k}(x)

where
\displaystyle\phi_{k}
is a remainder term that satisfies some suitable orthogonality properties.

We can rewrite the equation in (
2.3
) in the following way

\mathcal{L}(\phi)=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{N}(\phi)

where

(2.5)

\mathcal{L}(\phi)=-\Delta\phi-g^{\prime}(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda})\phi+V(|x|)\phi

(2.6)

\mathcal{E}=g(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}g({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})-V(|x|)\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda}

and

(2.7)

\mathcal{N}(\phi)=g(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda}+\phi)-g(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda})-g^{\prime}(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda})\phi.

Let us define for any
\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,k

(2.8)

\psi_{j,\lambda,1}:=\frac{\partial{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}}{\partial r},\quad\psi_{j,\lambda,2}:=\frac{\partial{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}}{\partial\lambda}.

For later uses we remark that

(2.9)

\psi_{j,\lambda,2}:=\alpha_{N,\mu}\frac{N-2}{2}\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\frac{1-\lambda^{2}|x-\xi_{j}|^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2}|x-\xi_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}.

We consider first the following project problem, i.e.

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(\phi_{k})=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{N}(\phi_{k})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{k}g^{\prime}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})\psi_{j,\lambda,\ell}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
&\phi_{k}\in H_{s}\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g^{\prime}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})\psi_{j,\lambda,\ell}\phi_{k}\,dx=0,\quad\ell=1,2\end{aligned}\right.

for some real numbers
\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}
.

Then, we need to find
\displaystyle r
and
\displaystyle\lambda
so that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}=0
in order to have a solution of the problem.

2.1.
The linear theory

Here we consider the linear problem

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(\phi_{k})=h+\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{k}g^{\prime}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})\psi_{j,\lambda,\ell}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
&\phi_{k}\in H_{s}\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g^{\prime}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})\psi_{j,\lambda,\ell}\phi_{k}\,dx=0,\quad\ell=1,2\end{aligned}\right.

Reasonig as in Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 of
[
17
]
one can show the following invertibility result.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
\displaystyle k_{0}>0
and a constant
\displaystyle C>0
independent of
\displaystyle k
, such that for all
\displaystyle k\geq k_{0}
and all
\displaystyle h\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
, problem (
2.11
) has a unique solution
\displaystyle\phi
. Besides,

(2.12)

\|\phi\|_{*}\leq C\|h\|_{**},\quad|\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{n_{\ell}}}\|h\|_{**}

where
\displaystyle n_{1}:=-1
and
\displaystyle n_{2}:=1
.

In order to use contraction mapping theorem to prove that (
2.10
) is uniquely solvable in the set where
\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{*}
is small, we need to estimates
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\phi)
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
.

2.2.
Estimate of the error term

Before to estimate the error term we first introduce for every
\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,k
the sets

\Omega_{j}:=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\,\,:\left\langle\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|},\frac{\xi_{j}^{\prime}}{|\xi_{j}^{\prime}|}\right\rangle\geq\cos\frac{\pi}{k}\right\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\displaystyle N\geq 5,
then there exists a small
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
such that

\|\mathcal{E}\|_{**}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We have that

\mathcal{E}:=\left[g(\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}g({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})\right]-V(|x|)\mathcal{W}_{r,\lambda}:=J_{1}-J_{2}.

We start by estimate
\displaystyle J_{2}
.
We have that

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{N-2}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{N-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N+2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\frac{N+2}{2}+\tau}}

since
\displaystyle N-1-\varepsilon>\frac{N+2}{2}+\tau
if
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
is small. Hence

\|J_{2}\|_{**}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}.

Now

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[|x|^{-\mu}*\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}\right)^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right]\cdot\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}\right)^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(|x|^{-\mu}*({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left[|x|^{-\mu}*\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}\right)^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)\right]\cdot\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}\right)^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}}_{(A)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{i\neq j}^{k}\left(|x|^{-\mu}*({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right)({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}}_{(B)}.

We have that for any
\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{j}

\left|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}}\right)^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\right|\lesssim({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}\sum_{i\neq j}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}}+\sum_{i\neq j}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}.

Now, by using Lemma
5.1
and by taking
\displaystyle 0<\beta\leq\min\{N-\mu+2,N-2\}=N-2
(we recall that
\displaystyle 0<\mu\leq 4
) we get that for any
\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{j}
and
\displaystyle i\neq j

\displaystyle({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N-\mu+2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{N-\mu+2}}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{i}|)^{N-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{\beta}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|^{\beta}}\frac{\lambda^{N-\frac{\mu}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{2N-\mu-\beta}}

and hence

({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}\sum_{i\neq j}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}}\lesssim\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\frac{\lambda^{N-\frac{\mu}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|^{2N-\mu-\beta}}.

Now by Remark
4
and Lemma
5.2
with
\displaystyle\theta:=N-\frac{\mu}{2}
and
\displaystyle\gamma:=N-\beta+(N-\mu)

\displaystyle|x|^{-\mu}*({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{j}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}-1}\sum_{i\neq j}{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\lambda^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi_{j})-y|)^{2N-\mu+\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi_{j})|)^{N-\beta-\eta}}

for
\displaystyle\eta>0
small.
Moreover by using Remark
3
we have that

|x|^{-\mu}*\sum_{i\neq j}({\mathcal{U}_{\lambda,\xi_{i}}})^{2^{\star}_{\mu}}\lesssim\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{i}|)^{\mu}}.

Moreover for any
\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{j}
and
\displaystyle i\neq j
we get for
\displaystyle 0<\delta<\frac{\mu}{2}
and by using Lemma
5.1

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{i}|)^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{i}|)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{\delta}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|^{\delta}}\right)\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\mu-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}\right)^{\delta}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi_{j}|)^{\mu-\delta}}

Hence

\displaystyle|(A)|

Lemma 5.2
.

There is a constant
\displaystyle C>0
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\,dy\leq C\frac{1}{(1+\lambda|x-\xi|)^{\alpha-N+\mu}}

for
\displaystyle\alpha\geq N-\mu
and
\displaystyle\eta>0.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle d:=\frac{\lambda}{2}|x-\xi|>1
.
Then, if
\displaystyle y\in B_{d}(0)
since

|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|>\lambda|x-\xi|-|y|>d

we get

\int_{B_{d}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\,dy\leq\frac{C}{(1+d)^{\alpha+\eta}}\int_{B_{d}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\,dy\leq\frac{C}{(1+d)^{\alpha-N+\mu+\eta}}.

Now, if
\displaystyle y\in B_{d}(\lambda(x-\xi))
we get

|y|=|y-\lambda(x-\xi)+\lambda(x-\xi)|>\lambda|x-\xi|-|y-\lambda(x-\xi)|>d

we get

\int_{B_{d}(\lambda(x-\xi))}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{d^{\mu}}\int_{B_{d}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\,dy\leq\frac{C}{(1+d)^{\alpha-N+\mu+\eta}}.

If
\displaystyle y\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus(B_{d}(0)\cup B_{d}(\lambda(x-\xi))
then
\displaystyle|y|\geq d=\frac{\lambda}{2}|x-\xi|
and
\displaystyle|y-\lambda(x-\xi)|\geq d=\frac{\lambda}{2}|x-\xi|
.

Then we get

\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\leq\frac{1}{(1+d)^{\alpha-N+\mu}}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{N-\mu+\eta}}.

If
\displaystyle|y|\leq 2\lambda|x-\xi|
then
\displaystyle|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|\geq|y|-\lambda|x-\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y|
. Hence

\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{N-\mu+\eta}}\leq\frac{C}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{N-\mu+\eta}}.

If
\displaystyle|y|\geq 2\lambda|x-\xi|
then
\displaystyle|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|\geq|y|-\lambda|x-\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y|
. Again we get

\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{N-\mu+\eta}}\leq\frac{C}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{N-\mu+\eta}}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus(B_{d}(0)\cup B_{d}(\lambda(x-\xi))}\frac{1}{|y|^{\mu}}\frac{1}{(1+|\lambda(x-\xi)-y|)^{\alpha+\eta}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1+d)^{\alpha-N+\mu}}.

The thesis follows.

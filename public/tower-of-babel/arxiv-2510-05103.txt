A note on a paper by Hashemi and Kapur
Anna Nymann Heisel, Niels Lauritzen

Abstract
Recently Hashemi and Kapur published an algorithm [ 1 ] for Gröbner basis conversion by truncating polynomials according to a source and a target monomial order. Here we present a counterexample to this algorithm.



Introduction

Let
K
be a field,
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
the polynomial ring in the
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
over
K
and
\langle S\rangle
the ideal generated
by a subset
S\subset R
. Given two monomial orders

\prec_{1}
and
\prec_{2}
on
R
, Hashemi and Kapur defined in
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
the
truncated part
\operatorname{tp}_{1,2}(f)
of a non-zero polynomial
f\in R
as
the sum of the terms in
f
greater than or equal to
\operatorname{LT}_{\prec_{1}}(f)

with respect to
\prec_{2}
.

Example 1
.

If
R=K[x,y,z]
and
\prec_{1}
is the degree reverse lexicographic order given
by
z\prec_{1}y\prec_{1}x
and
\prec_{2}
is the lexicographic order given by

z\prec_{2}y\prec_{2}x
, then

\operatorname{tp}_{1,2}(f)=y^{2}+xz+x

for
f=y^{2}+xz+x+y+z
.

If
G
is a finite subset of polynomials containing
a Gröbner basis with respect to
\prec_{1}
for
\langle G\rangle
, the idea in
[
1
]
is
to compute a Gröbner basis for
\langle G\rangle
over
\prec_{2}
, by successive
Göbner basis computations over
\prec_{2}
for
\operatorname{tp}_{1,2}(G)

with lifting steps as in the classical
Gröbner walk algorithm. Basically one discards
S
-polynomials of truncated polynomials
in
\operatorname{tp}_{1,2}(G)
, which reduce
to zero and lifts the non-zero reductions by keeping track of
coefficients in the division algorithm (Gröbner coefficients). We refer to

[
1
, §4]
for details.

A counterexample over the field with two elements

Let
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x,y,z]
.
We let
\prec_{1}
be the degree reverse lexicographic order
on
R
with

z\prec_{1}y\prec_{1}x
and
\prec_{2}

the lexicographic order with

z\prec_{2}y\prec_{2}x
.

Let

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=y^{2}+xz+x

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=z^{2}+1.

Since
\operatorname{LT}_{\prec_{1}}(g_{1})=y^{2}
and
\operatorname{LT}_{\prec_{1}}(g_{2})=z^{2}
are relatively prime,
G_{1}=\{g_{1},g_{2}\}

is the reduced Gröbner basis
with respect to
\prec_{1}
for the ideal
I
generated by
G_{1}
.

The reduced Gröbner basis
G_{2}
with respect to
\prec_{2}
for
I
is found by adding the polynomials

\displaystyle\begin{split}&y^{2}z+y^{2}\\
&y^{4}.\end{split}

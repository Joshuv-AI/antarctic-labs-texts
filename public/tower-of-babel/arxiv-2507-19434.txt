Group valued moment maps for even and odd simple G G -modules
Anton Alekseev, Andrey Krutov

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple Lie group, and 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} its Lie algebra. It is well known that a G G -module V V carrying an invariant symplectic form or an invariant scalar product gives rise to a Hamiltonian Poisson space with a quadratic moment maps μ \mu . We show that under condition Hom 𝔤 ​ ( ⋀ 3 V , S 3 ​ V ) = 0 {\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{3}V,S^{3}V)=0 this space can be viewed as a quasi-Poisson space with the same bivector, and with the group valued moment map Φ = exp ∘ μ \Phi=\exp\circ\mu . Furthermore, we show that by modifying the bivector by the standard r r -matrix for 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} one obtains a space with a Poisson action of the Poisson-Lie group G G , and with the moment map in the sense of Lu taking values in the dual Poisson-Lie group G ∗ G^{\ast} .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a compact or complex simple Lie group,
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra, and
V
be a
G
-module equipped with an invariant symplectic form. Then,
V
becomes a Hamiltonian Poisson space with Poisson structure
\pi
given by the inverse of the symplectic form, and with the quadratic moment map
\mu
defined by the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-action on
V
.

Similarly, if
V
carries a
G
-invariant scalar product, then following Kostant-Sternberg
[
KS2
]
one can define
G
-invariant symplectic and Poisson structures (again denoted by
\pi
) on the supermanifold
\Pi V
. As before, the
G
-action is Hamiltonian, and the moment map
\mu
is quadratic.

In this paper, we restrict our attention to
G
-modules with a rather restrictive extra property

(1)

{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{3}V,S^{3}V)=0.

Such modules can be classified (see Section 3 for details), and by the results of
[
Zwi
]
they carry quadratic
r
-matrix Poisson brackets
\pi_{r}
. Furthermore, for such a
G
-module the natural
G
-action is a Poisson action of the corresponding Poisson-Lie groups defined by the
r
-matrix.

Our main results can be summarized as follows:

Theorem
.

Let
V
be a
G
-module which satisfies condition (
1
), and which carries either an invariant symplectic form or an invariant scalar product. Then, the corresponding Poisson spaces
(V,\pi)
and
(\Pi V,\pi)
can be
viewed as quasi-Poisson spaces with the same bivector, and with the group valued moment map
\Phi=\exp\circ\mu
. Furthermore, for the standard classical
r
-matrix on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the spaces
(V,\pi-\pi_{r})
and
(\Pi V,\pi-\pi_{r})
are Poisson, they carry actions of the Poisson-Lie group
G
equipped with the standard Poisson bracket, and there is a moment map in the sense of Lu
L=(L_{+},L_{-})
such that

\Phi=L_{+}L_{-}^{-1}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows: in Section 2, we collect the background material on Poisson and quasi-Poisson actions, moment maps, dynamical
r
-matrices, and linear supermanifolds. In Section 3, we state and prove our main results. In Section 4, we describe two simple examples related to the case of
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. In Section 5, we discuss bivectors on the space
V\oplus V^{*}
with
V
satisfying condition (
1
). This case is more involved than the one treated in the theorem above, and we only state some partial results for
V
the defining representation of
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Let
G
be a compact or complex simple Lie group, and
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra.
We denote by
\theta^{L},\theta^{R}\in\Omega^{1}(G,{\mathfrak{g}})
the left- and right-invariant Maurer–Cartan forms on
G
. For
\beta\in{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}
we denote by
\beta^{L},\beta^{R}\in\Gamma(G,{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}TG)
the corresponding
left- and right-invariant multivector fields such that
\beta^{L}(e)=\beta^{R}(e)=\beta
.

The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits
a unique (up to a multiple) nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Using
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
, one can define the canonical
invariant Cartan 3-tensor on
{\mathfrak{g}}
:

\phi(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{12}B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[y,z]).

Using the bilinear form
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
one can identify
{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, and by abuse of notation one can view
\phi
as an element of
({\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{3}{\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

For a
G
-manifold
M
and an element
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the generating vector field of the induced action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined by

x_{M}(m):=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tx)\cdot m\qquad\text{for $m\in M$}.

The Lie algebra homomorphism
{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
given by
x\mapsto x_{M}
extends to
a map

{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\Gamma(M,{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}TM),

preserving wedge products and Schouten brackets
[\mskip-3.0mu[-,-]\mskip-3.0mu]
. For
\alpha:M\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}{\mathfrak{g}}
, we
denote by
\alpha_{M}
the multivector field
\alpha_{M}(m)=(\alpha(m))_{M}
.

For
\pi\in\Gamma(M,{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}TM)
a bivector on
M
, we denote by

\pi^{\sharp}
the induced map from differential forms to vector fields:

\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)(\beta)=\pi(\alpha,\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

2.2.
Moment maps

Recall that a
Hamiltonian Poisson
G
-manifold
is a triple
(M,\pi,\mu)
, consisting of a
G
-manifold
M
,
an invariant Poisson structure
\pi
, and an equivariant
moment map

\mu\colon M\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
satisfying the
condition

x_{M}=\pi^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}\langle\mu,x\rangle)\qquad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

A
quasi-Poisson manifold
is a
G
-manifold
M
equipped with an invariant bivector

{\pi\in\Gamma(M,{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}TM)}
such that

[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi,\pi]\mskip-3.0mu]=\phi_{M}.

Following
[
AMM
]
, an
\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits
-equivariant map
\Phi\colon M\to G
is called a group valued moment map for the quasi-Poisson
manifold
(M,\pi)
if

(2)

\pi^{\sharp}(\Phi^{\ast}B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,\theta^{L}))=\frac{1}{2}((\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits_{\Phi})x)_{M}\qquad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Following
[
LW
,
Lu
]
, one says that the action of a Poisson-Lie group
(G,\pi_{G})
on a Poisson

G
-manifold
(M,\pi)
is a
Poisson action
, if for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}

L_{x_{M}}\pi=-\delta(x)_{M},

where
\delta\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}
is the corresponding cobracket.
Let
G^{\ast}
be the dual Poisson-Lie group of
G
. A map
L\colon M\to G^{\ast}
is called a
G^{\ast}
-valued moment map
if

\pi_{L}^{\sharp}(L^{\ast}(\langle\theta^{R},x\rangle))=x_{M}\qquad\text{for all $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

where
\theta^{R}\in\Omega^{1}(G^{\ast},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
is the right-invariant Maurer-Cartan form on
G^{\ast}
.
In what follows, we assume that
G
is equipped with the Poisson structure defined by the standard
r
-matrix, see
[
BD
]
.

2.3.
The dynamical
r
-matrix and group valued moment maps

Let
e_{a}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
. For a linear map
A\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
we define the corresponding matrix
by
Ae_{a}=\sum_{b}A_{ab}e_{b}
. Let
\varphi
be a meromorphic function of
s\in\mathbb{C}

given by

(3)

\varphi(s)=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{2}\coth\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)=-\frac{s}{12}+\frac{s^{3}}{720}-\frac{s^{5}}{30240}+O(s^{7}).

Consider the map
T:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}
defined by formula

(4)

T(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}T_{ab}(x)e_{a}\wedge e_{b},

where

.
By the results of
[
EV
]
,

\mathrm{Cycl}_{abc}\left(\frac{\partial T_{ab}}{\partial e_{c}}+\sum_{kl}T_{ak}f_{akl}T_{lc}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\,f_{abc}

where
\mathrm{Cycl}_{abc}
denotes the sum over cyclic permutations, and

.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Hamiltonian Poisson
G
-manifold with a moment map
\mu\colon M\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
, and
let
r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu)=(\mu^{\ast}T)_{M}\in\Gamma(M,{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}TM)
,
where
\mu^{*}T:M\to{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}
is the pull-back of the function
T
under the moment map
\mu
.

By
[
AKSM
, Theorem 7.1]
,
if the exponential map
\exp\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to G
has the maximal rank on the image of
\mu
,
then
\pi^{\prime}=\pi+r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu)
defines a quasi-Poisson structure on

with the
group valued moment map
\Phi=\exp\circ\mu\colon M\to G
.

2.4.
Twist and
G^{\ast}
-valued moment maps

In this Section, we assume that
G
is a complex Lie group.

Let

be a classical
r
-matrix on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and let

be its anti-symmetrisation.
The Drinfeld bracket

]
of

with itself is defined as

\langle t,t\rangle=[t_{12},t_{13}]+[t_{12},t_{23}]+[t_{13},t_{23}],

is the unit of the universal enveloping algebra

.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold such that

2\langle t,t\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\phi.

By
[
AKS
, §4.1]
,
the quasi-Poisson action of
G
on
(M,\pi)
gives rise a Poisson action of the Poisson-Lie group
G

on
(M,\pi^{\prime})
, where
\pi^{\prime}=\pi-t_{M}
.
The corresponding cobracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by
\delta(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x}(t)
, see
[
Dri2
,
KS1
]
.

For the Poisson action of

on
(M,\pi^{\prime})
, one obtains a

-valued moment map

as follows.
The standard
r
-matrix fixes a maximal torus

and a choice of positive roots. Let

be the corresponding
opposite Borel subgroups, and
\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits_{\pm}\colon B_{\pm}\to T

By factorising the

-valued moment map as

(here

-valued moment map

In what follows we assume that

4.

In this section, we consider examples of even and odd modules for

Let

be the nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
given by

B_{\mathfrak{g}}(e,f)=1,\quad B_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,h)=2.

Therefore, the corresponding Cartan 3-tensor takes the form

Recall the standard
r

{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}

r=\frac{1}{4}h\otimes h+e\otimes f\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}\otimes{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2},

and the corresponding cobracket

\delta(h)=0,\qquad\delta(e)=\frac{1}{2}h\wedge e,\qquad\delta(f)=\frac{1}{2}h\wedge f.

The dual Poisson Lie groups

SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})^{\ast}=\left\{L_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad L_{-}\begin{pmatrix}a^{-1}&0\\
c&a\end{pmatrix}\mid\text{$a,b,c\in{\mathbb{C}}$ such that $a\neq 0$}\right\}.

Note that
L_{+}L_{-}^{-1}\in SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
.
The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)^{\ast}
can be realised as pairs of matrices

{\mathfrak{sl}}(2)^{\ast}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a/2&b\\

-c&a/2\end{pmatrix}\mid a,b,c\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\},

with a basis
h^{\vee}=\frac{1}{2}(h,-h)
,
e^{\vee}=(e,0)
,
f^{\vee}=(0,-f)
.

Motivating example:

4.2.
The case of
({\mathfrak{g}},V)=({\mathfrak{sl}}_{2},V_{\varpi_{1}})

The module

is even and admits a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form given by

B(v_{1},v_{-1})=1,\quad B(v_{-1},v_{1})=-1,\quad B(v_{1},v_{1})=B(v_{-1},v_{-1})=0,

\displaystyle v_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{-1}},

\displaystyle h_{{\mathcal{V}}}={}

\displaystyle v_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{1}}-v_{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{-1}},

\displaystyle f_{{\mathcal{V}}}={}

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},V)=({\mathfrak{sl}}_{n},V_{\varpi_{1}})
. Let
v_{i}
be the standard basis of
V
and
v_{i}^{\ast}
be the
corresponding dual basis of
V^{\ast}
. Then, the bivector

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{W}}={}

\displaystyle-t_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\left(-(-1)^{s}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\dim V}\right)\sum_{i,j}v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge v_{j}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{j}

or equivalently

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-t_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\frac{1}{2\dim V}\sum_{i,j}v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge v_{j}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}v_{j}^{\ast}v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{j},

satisfies

[[\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]]=0,\qquad[[\pi_{B},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]]=0,\qquad L_{x_{{\mathcal{W}}}}\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}=-\delta(x)_{{\mathcal{W}}}.

Here
s=1
if we view
W
as an odd module, and
s=0
if we view it as an even module,

Proof.

Set

\pi_{cor}=\sum_{i,j}v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge v^{\ast}_{j}\partial^{\ast}_{j}.

Since
\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}
and
\pi_{cor}
are
G
-invariant, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{x_{{\mathcal{W}}}}(\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}})=-{\mathcal{L}}_{x_{{\mathcal{W}}}}(t_{{\mathcal{W}}})=-\delta(x)_{{\mathcal{W}}}.

This implies

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[t_{{\mathcal{W}}},\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]=0,\quad[\mskip-3.0mu[t_{{\mathcal{W}}},t_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]=\phi_{{\mathcal{W}}},\qquad[\mskip-3.0mu[\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}},\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]=-\phi_{{\mathcal{W}}},

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[t_{{\mathcal{W}}},\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]=0,\qquad[\mskip-3.0mu[\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}},\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]=0,\qquad

which leads to

[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]=\phi_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\phi_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0,

and this proves that the action of
G
on
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{\mathcal{W}})
is Poisson.

For
[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]=0
, note that
V
and
V^{\ast}
are also
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
-module.
Let
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
be the trace form on
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
, then the restriction of
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{n}
is nondegenerate.
The matrix unit
E_{i,j}\in{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
is dual to
E_{j,i}
with respect to
B_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}\in{\textstyle{\bigwedge}}^{2}({\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}\oplus{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
, then we have that

\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}=\psi+\frac{1}{2\dim V}z^{1}\wedge z^{2},

where

is the identity matrix in
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
.
The factor
\dim V
in the denominator comes from the fact that
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(z,z)=\dim V
.
We have that

(E_{i,j})_{{\mathcal{V}}}=v_{i}\partial_{j},\qquad(E_{i,j})_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\ast}}=-v^{\ast}_{j}\partial^{\ast}_{i},\qquad(z)_{{\mathcal{V}}}=\sum_{i}v_{i}\partial_{i},\qquad(z)_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\ast}}=-\sum_{j}v^{\ast}_{j}\partial^{\ast}_{j}.

Hence
\pi_{cor}=-(z^{1}\wedge z^{2})_{{\mathcal{W}}}
.
Therefore, we have that

\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\frac{1}{2\dim V}(z^{1}\wedge z^{2})_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\frac{1}{2\dim V}\pi_{cor},

\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}(E_{i,j})_{{\mathcal{V}}}\wedge(E_{j,i})_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\ast}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum v_{i}\partial_{j}\wedge v^{\ast}_{i}\partial^{\ast}_{j}.

We have

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[2\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu]

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 2([\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{a}\partial_{a}]\mskip-3.0mu]\wedge v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge[\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu])

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 2([\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i},v_{a}\partial_{a}]\mskip-3.0mu]\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge[\mskip-3.0mu[\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu])

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 2(\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge v_{b}^{\ast}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i})

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 2(v_{b}^{\ast}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}).

On the other hand, we get

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\psi^{{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[2\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},\frac{1}{2}(E_{ab})_{{\mathcal{V}}}\wedge(E_{ba})_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\ast}}]\mskip-3.0mu]

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{a}\partial_{b}\wedge(-v^{\ast}_{a}\partial^{\ast}_{b})]\mskip-3.0mu]

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-([\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v_{a}\partial_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu]\wedge v^{\ast}_{a}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{b}\wedge[\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i},v^{\ast}_{a}\partial^{\ast}_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu])

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-([\mskip-3.0mu[\partial_{i},v_{a}\partial_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu]\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge v^{\ast}_{a}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{b}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge[\mskip-3.0mu[\partial^{\ast}_{i},v^{\ast}_{a}\partial^{\ast}_{b}]\mskip-3.0mu])

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-(\partial_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge v^{\ast}_{i}\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{i}\partial_{b}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}).

If
{\mathcal{W}}
is an odd module, then

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\psi^{{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}

\displaystyle-(v^{\ast}_{i}\partial_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}-v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}).

Finally, we get that

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},-\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}-c\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]=-[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\frac{1}{2\dim V}\pi_{cor}+c\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-(-1+\frac{1}{\dim V}+2c)(v_{b}^{\ast}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i})

Therefore,
c=\frac{\dim V-1}{2\dim V}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\dim V}
for odd modules.

If
{\mathcal{W}}
is an even module, then

[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\psi^{{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}v^{\ast}_{i}\partial_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}-v_{i}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{b}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}.

Finally, we get that

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}]\mskip-3.0mu]={}

\displaystyle[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},-\psi_{{\mathcal{W}}}-c\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]=-[\mskip-3.0mu[\pi_{B},\psi^{\mathfrak{gl}}_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\frac{1}{2\dim V}\pi_{cor}+c\pi_{cor}]\mskip-3.0mu]

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle-(1+\frac{1}{\dim V}+2c)(v_{b}^{\ast}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{b}-v_{a}\partial_{a}\wedge\partial_{i}\wedge\partial^{\ast}_{i})

Therefore,

for even modules.

{\mathcal{W}}=V\oplus V^{\ast}

an invariant symmetric bilinear
B

{\mathcal{W}}

{\mathcal{W}}

Hamiltonian Poisson
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})

\pi_{B}=2\partial_{1}\wedge\partial_{1}^{\ast}+2\partial_{2}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}

and the moment map defined by

\mu=\frac{1}{4}(v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}-v_{2}v_{2}^{\ast})\otimes h+\frac{1}{2}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}\otimes e+\frac{1}{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{\ast}\otimes f

here
v_{i}\in V

are odd generators of
{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle ev_{1}=0,\qquad hv_{1}=v_{1},\qquad fv_{1}=v_{2},\qquad ev_{2}=v_{1},\qquad hv_{2}=-v_{1},\qquad fv_{2}=0,

\displaystyle ev_{1}^{\ast}=-v_{2}^{\ast},\qquad hv_{1}^{\ast}=-v_{1}^{\ast},\qquad fv_{1}^{\ast}=0,\qquad ev_{2}^{\ast}=0,\qquad hv_{2}^{\ast}=v_{2}^{\ast},\qquad fv_{2}^{\ast}=v_{1}^{\ast}.

The corresponding to
\mu
dynamical
r

r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu)=-\frac{1}{8}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{1}\partial_{1}^{\ast}-\frac{1}{8}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{2}\partial_{2}^{\ast}.

Then,
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B}+r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu))

-valued moment

\Phi=\exp\circ\mu=\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{1}{4}v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}-\frac{1}{4}v_{2}v_{2}^{\ast}+\frac{3}{16}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}&\frac{1}{2}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}\\
\frac{1}{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{\ast}&1-\frac{1}{4}v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}+\frac{1}{4}v_{2}v_{2}^{\ast}+\frac{3}{16}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\end{pmatrix}.

The twist given by the standard
r

t_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}v_{2}\partial_{1}\wedge\partial_{2}-v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}\partial_{2}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}+v_{2}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{1}\wedge\partial_{1}^{\ast}-v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{1}^{\ast}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}).

The Poisson bivector from Theorem
5.2.  Theorem

\displaystyle\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}

\displaystyle{}=v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}\partial_{2}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}+\frac{1}{2}(-v_{1}v_{2}\partial_{1}\wedge\partial_{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{1}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}-v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}\partial_{2}\wedge\partial_{1}^{\ast}+v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\partial_{1}^{\ast}\wedge\partial_{2}^{\ast}).

Let us consider the new quasi-Poisson structure on
{\mathcal{W}}
which is given by the bivector

\pi_{B}+\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+t_{{\mathcal{W}}}.

The direct computations show the following surprising fact: the
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-valued moment map for
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B}+\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+t_{{\mathcal{W}}})
is the same as for
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B}+r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu))
!

Since the moment map
\Phi
is given by the exponent of
\mu
, we see that the quasi-Poisson supermanifold

({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B}+\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+t_{{\mathcal{W}}})
is the exponent of the Hamiltonian Poisson supermanifold
{\mathcal{W}}
with the bivector defined by

\pi_{B}+\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+t_{{\mathcal{W}}}+r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu)

and the moment map defined by
\mu
.

It is easy to see that both
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B})
and
({\mathcal{W}},\pi_{B}+\pi_{{\mathcal{W}}}+t_{{\mathcal{W}}}+r_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mu))
are symplectic
supermanifolds. The corresponding symplectic structure are given by

\omega_{B}=2\mathrm{d}v_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{1}^{\ast}+2\mathrm{d}v_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{2}^{\ast}

and

\omega=\mathrm{d}v_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{1}^{\ast}(2-\frac{1}{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{\ast})+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{2}^{\ast}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}+\mathrm{d}v_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{3}\frac{1}{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{\ast}+\mathrm{d}v_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}v_{2}^{\ast}(2-\frac{1}{2}v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}).

Note that here we consider the differential forms on
{\mathcal{W}}
as a left
{\mathcal{F}}
-module.

The difference of symplectic forms is the differential of an invariant basic 1-form

\omega-\omega_{B}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\left(\mathrm{d}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}-\mathrm{d}v_{2}v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\right).

Hence, for any
x\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
we get that

\mathrm{d}\langle\mu,x\rangle=\iota_{X_{\mathcal{W}}}\omega_{B}=\iota_{X_{{\mathcal{W}}}}\left(\omega-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\left(\mathrm{d}v_{1}v_{2}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}-\mathrm{d}v_{2}v_{1}v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\right)\right)=\iota_{X_{{\mathcal{W}}}}\omega.

Therefore,
\mu
suites as a moment map for both Poisson structures.

5.4.2.
The case of

The

\displaystyle\nu={}

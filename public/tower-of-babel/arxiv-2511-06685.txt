Experimentation Under Non-stationary Interference
Su Jia, Peter Frazier, Nathan Kallus, Christina Lee Yu

Abstract
We study the estimation of the average treatment effect (ATE) in randomized controlled trials under a dynamically evolving interference structure. This setting arises in applications such as ride-sharing, where drivers move over time, and social networks, where connections continuously form and dissolve. In particular, we focus on scenarios where outcomes exhibit spatio-temporal interference driven by a sequence of random interference graphs that evolve independently of the treatment assignment. Loosely, our main result states that a truncated Horvitz-Thompson estimator achieves an MSE that vanishes linearly in the number of spatial and time blocks, times a factor that measures the average complexity of the interference graphs. As a key technical contribution (that contrasts the static setting 10 ), we present a fine-grained covariance bound for each pair of space-time points that decays exponentially with the time elapsed since their last “interaction”. Our results can be applied to many concrete settings and lead to simplified bounds, including where the interference graphs (i) are induced by moving points in a metric space, or (ii) follow a dynamic Erdos-Renyi model, where each edge is created or removed independently in each time period.



1
Introduction

Experimentation under interference — that is, when the
Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption
(SUTVA)
(
21
)
is violated — poses a fundamental challenge in modern experimentation:
In many large-scale online environments such as marketplaces, the treatment assigned to one unit may affect the outcomes of others through shared resources, social connections, or platform algorithms.
Ignoring such spillover effects can lead to biased estimates, invalid inference, and misguided product decisions.
As a result, modeling and mitigating interference has become a central challenge in the design of trustworthy experimentation pipelines.

Most existing work in this area focuses on either (1) a single-period setting where non-stationarity is irrelevant, or (2) a multi-period model with a
static
interference pattern, usually characterized by a graph.
However, such assumptions can be restrictive for modern applications, where user behavior and network structure evolve over time.
In real-world systems, interference can evolve as users enter or exit the platform, content recommendations adapt, or interactions become more or less frequent.
These dynamic patterns make it essential to develop experimental methods that account for both temporal evolution and the fluid nature of the interference graph.

We initiate a study of experimentation under a
non-stationary
interference.
Non-stationarity is
two-fold
: We allow not only the outcome distributions to vary (across both space and time), but also the interference graph to evolve over time,
independently
of the treatments.
More precisely, conditional on the interference graphs, the outcomes of each fixed individual follow a Markov reward process, whose state transitions may depend on the treatment of other individuals in the same time period.

1.1
Our Contributions

We progressively show the following results on the Horvitz-Thompson (HT) estimator.

1.

Covariance Bound for the HT Estimators.
We establish a general covariance bound in terms of a novel quantity called the
last interaction time
(LIT). Specifically, we show that the covariance between the HT estimators of individual treatment effects decays exponentially with the time elapsed since their LIT (
Proposition

4.2
).
This is a key technical component for our main result and is of independent interest, as it holds for
any
experimental design (i.e. distribution over the treatment assignment matrix).

2.

Bounding MSE Using the Average Cluster Degree.
We present a general MSE bound for a broad class of designs, which we call
vertical designs
: Partition the time horizon uniformly into time blocks, then within each time block, partition the population into spatial blocks and assign treatment/control at the block level.
We show that the MSE of the HT estimator is
1/NT
times the
average cluster degree
, where the average is taken over all individuals and time blocks (
Theorem

4.8
).
Here, two individuals are called
cluster-neighbors
in a time block if their neighborhoods intersect the same spatial block, and the cluster degree of an individual is the number of cluster-neighbors.

Outcomes.

\displaystyle Y_{it}=\mu_{it}\left(S_{it},w_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t}\right)+\varepsilon_{t}

Main Result: A General MSE Bound

We will first present a covariance bound based on the

(LIT), then explain how it leads to an MSE bound.

4.1
Last Interaction Time (LIT)

For any fixed
i,i^{\prime},t
, their LIT is defined as the endpoint of the most recent time block that (i) begins before
t
, and (ii) during which
i,i^{\prime}
is an edge in the associated CIG.

Definition 4.1

(Last Interaction Time)
.

Fix any
t\in[T]
.
Suppose
t\in[(k-1)\ell,\ k\ell)
for some
k
.
We define the
last interaction time
(LIT) as

\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t):=k^{\star}\ell\quad{\rm where}\quad k^{\star}=\max\{k^{\prime}\leq k:(i,i^{\prime})\in E_{k^{\prime}}\}.

We can now refine the covariance bound,
Equation

2

Proposition 4.2

(Bounding Covariance Using LIT)
.

For any
i,i^{\prime}\in[N]
,
t,t^{\prime}\in[T]
satisfying
t\geq\max\{t^{\prime},\ \uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})+r\}

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}.

{\rm Cov}(\widehat{\Delta}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}})
is only “non-negligible” if
t
and
t^{\prime}
lie in the same “extended” time block
[(k-1)\ell,k\ell+r]
.
More precisely:

Corollary 4.3

Fix

t^{\prime}\leq t
.
Denote
{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}=\{k:(i,i^{\prime})\in F_{k}\}

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}.

Proof.

There are two cases.

Case A:
Suppose

By our assumption that
t\geq t^{\prime}
, we then have

Thus, by

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\leq e^{-|\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};,t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}\leq e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}.

Case B:
Suppose
t^{\prime}\in[(k-1)\ell,k\ell+r]
for some
k\in{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}

Then, for any such
k
, we have
t\notin[(k-1)\ell,k\ell+r]

In particular,

, we have
t\notin[(k^{*}-1)\ell,k^{*}\ell+r]
.
On the other hand, by the definition of LIT, we have
\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})=k^{*}\ell
.
Thus, the condition

t\geq\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})+r

in
Proposition

4.2
holds, and we obtain

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\leq e^{-|\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};,t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}\leq e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}.

∎

4.2
Main Result

Recall that the cluster degree of an individual (w.r.t. time block
k
) is the number of edges incident to it in the
k
-th CIG.
We show that the variance of
\widehat{\Delta}^{r}
is essentially
\frac{1}{NT}
times the
average cluster degree
, averaged over all
N
individuals and
T/\ell
time blocks.

Theorem 4.4

(Bounding the Variance Using
\overline{\rm CD}
)
.

Suppose there is a constant
p^{\rm min}>0
such that
p_{it}^{r}\geq p^{\rm min}
for all
i\in[N],t\in[T]
.
Then, for any
r,\ell
we have

{\rm Var}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}\right)\leq\frac{1+\sigma^{2}}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{NT}\cdot\frac{(\ell+r)^{2}}{\ell}\cdot\overline{\rm CD}+e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}\right)\quad{\rm where}\quad\overline{\rm CD}=\frac{1}{N\cdot T/\ell}\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{k=1}^{T/\ell}{\rm CD}_{k}(i).

Proof.

We start with decomposing the variance into a sum of covariance terms:

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}T^{2}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}\sum_{t^{\prime}}\sum_{t:t^{\prime}\leq t}{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}T^{2}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}M_{i,i^{\prime}},

(3)

where

M_{i,i^{\prime}}:=\sum_{(t,t^{\prime}):t^{\prime}\leq t}{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right).

We now fix
i,i^{\prime}
and bound
M_{i,i^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle M_{i,i^{\prime}}\leq\left(\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}}\sum_{(t,t^{\prime})\in[(k-1)\ell,\ k\ell+r]}+\sum_{{\rm other}\ t,t^{\prime}\ {\rm with}\ t^{\prime}\leq t}\right){\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right).

By
Corollary

4.3
, for any
t,t^{\prime}
in the second summation, we have

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{it},\widehat{\Delta}^{r}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\cdot e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}.

Moreover, for each
k\in{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}
, there are
O((\ell+r)^{2})
pairs of
t,t^{\prime}
in the corresponding summation, with each covariance at most

\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}

by
Lemma

B.1
.
Combining, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{i,i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}}(\ell+r)^{2}\cdot\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}+\sum_{t,t^{\prime}:t^{\prime}\leq t}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}}(\ell+r)^{2}\cdot\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}+\sum_{t,t^{\prime}:t^{\prime}\leq t}\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\left((\ell+r)^{2}\cdot|{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}|+T^{2}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}\right).

Substituting the above into
Section

4.2
and noting that

2\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}|{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}|=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}{\rm CD}_{k}(i)=N\cdot\frac{T}{\ell}\cdot\overline{\rm CD},

we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{2}T^{2}}\left(\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\in[N]}\left((\ell+r)^{2}\cdot|{\cal K}_{i,i^{\prime}}|+T^{2}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+\sigma^{2})}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{2}T^{2}}\left((\ell+r)^{2}\cdot N\cdot\frac{T}{\ell}\cdot\overline{\rm CD}+N^{2}T^{2}e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+\sigma^{2})}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{NT}\cdot\frac{(\ell+r)^{2}}{\ell}\cdot\overline{\rm CD}+e^{-r/t_{\rm mix}}\right).

∎

To further simplify, we choose
r,\ell
to be logarithmic in
NT
, so that the exponential term becomes lower-order:

Corollary 4.5
.

With
\ell=r=2t_{\rm mix}\log NT
, we have

{\rm Var}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{r}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{(p^{\rm min})^{2}}\cdot\frac{t_{\rm mix}\log NT}{NT}\cdot\overline{\rm CD}.

We will soon explain how to further simplify and interpret the above.

4.3
Minimal Exposure Probability

To further simplify the above, we bound the exposure probability in terms of the cluster degrees.

Proposition 4.6

(Lower Bound for the Exposure Probability)
.

For any
i,t,r
, we have

p_{it}^{r}\geq 2^{-\sum_{k:[t_{k-1},t_{k}]\cap[t-r,t]\neq\emptyset}{\rm CD}_{k}(i)}.

To see this, observe that the right-hand side corresponds to the
exact
exposure probability
probability
in the most extreme case -i.e., when every neighbor of
i
in the graph
H_{k}
lies in a distinct spatial block, for every
k
with
B_{k}\cap[t-r,t]\neq\emptyset
.
(Of course, in many practically motivated interference structures, the actual exposure probability probability can be much higher, as we will see in the next section.)

Remark 4.7
.

This bound is worst-case - the identify holds only when every neighbor lies in a different spatial block.

As an important case, if
\ell=r
, there are at most two time blocks that intersect
[t-r,t]
, so

p_{it}^{r}\geq 2^{-2{\rm CD}_{\rm max}}\quad{\rm where}\quad{\rm CD}_{\rm max}=\max_{i\in[N],1\leq k\leq T/\ell}{\rm CD}_{k}(i).

We obtain the following by observing that the bias bound
Proposition

3.5
becomes a lower-order term when
r
is sufficiently large.

Theorem 4.8

(Bounding MSE Using ACD)
.

Suppose
\ell=r=2t_{\rm mix}\log NT
. Then,

{\rm MSE}=O\left(\frac{t_{\rm mix}\log(NT)}{NT}\cdot 2^{4{\rm CD}_{\rm max}}\cdot\overline{\rm CD}\right).

Remark 4.9
.

As a sanity check, consider the
no-interference
setting, where each individual forms its own cluster.
Since the interference graph has no edges, the corresponding cluster graph also contains no edges apart from self-loops.
Therefore, we have
{\rm CD}_{k}(i)=1
for all
i
and
k
, and

Theorem

4.8
gives a variance bound of
\tilde{O}(\frac{1}{NT})
.

When the graph changes, the CD’s can increase, as they are defined based on the union of connections across all rounds within a time block.
Furthermore, the faster the graph changes, the larger the resulting CD’s tend to be.
∎

Appendix B
Proof the Covariance Bound (
Proposition

4.2
)

We start with a bound that holds for all pairs
it,i^{\prime}t^{\prime}
of space-time points.
It follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the boundedness of the random variables.

Lemma B.1

(Covariance of HT Terms)
.

For any
r\geq 0
,
i,i^{\prime}\in[N]
,
t,t^{\prime}\in[T]
and design
\cal D
, we have

{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\ \widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\right)\leq\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}.

Proof.

Expanding the definition of
\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\ \widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Cov}\left(\frac{X_{it1}^{r}}{p_{it}^{r}}Y_{it}-\frac{X_{it0}^{r}}{p_{it}^{r}}Y_{it},\ \frac{X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}1}^{r}}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}-\frac{X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}0}^{r}}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}}\left|{\rm Cov}\left(\frac{X_{ita}^{r}}{p_{it}^{r}}Y_{it},\ \frac{X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}}\frac{1}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}}\left|{\rm Cov}\left(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it},\ X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}}\frac{1}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it})^{2}\right]}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}})^{2}]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(1+\sigma^{2})}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}},

(5)

where the second last inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and last inequality follows since
\mathbb{E}[(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it})^{2}]\leq 1+\sigma^{2}
. ∎

The above bound alone is not sufficient for our analysis, as it does not take advantage of the rapid mixing property.
The rest of this section is dedicated to showing that HT terms decays
exponentially
in the temporal distance.
To this end, we will decompose the covariance as follows.

Proposition B.2

(Law of Total Covariance)
.

For any random variables
U,V,W
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(U,V)=\mathbb{E}[{\rm Cov}(U,V\mid W)]+{\rm Cov}(\mathbb{E}[U\mid W],\ \mathbb{E}[V\mid W]).

We will apply the above as follows.
By the Law of Total Covariance (LOTC), for any individuals
i,i^{\prime}\in[N]
, rounds
t,t^{\prime}\in[T]
and radius
r\geq 0
for the HT estimator, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right)\right]+{\rm Cov}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r}\middle|\ W\right],\ \mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right]\right).

(6)

We will bound these two terms separately in the next two subsections.
Throughout, we will sometimes write
\mathbb{P}_{w}=\mathbb{P}[\cdot\mid W=w]
and
{\rm Cov}_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)={\rm Cov}(\cdot,\cdot\mid W=w)
.

B.1
Bounding the First Term in LOTC

We first show that if the realization of one random variable has little impact on the (conditional) distribution of another random variable, then they have low covariance.

Lemma B.3

(Low Interference in Conditional Distribution Implies Low Covariance)
.

Let
U,V
be two random variables and
g,h
be real-valued functions defined on their respective realization spaces.
If for some
\delta>0
,

d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}[U\in\cdot\mid V],\ \mathbb{P}[U\in\cdot])\leq\delta\quad V\text{-almost surely },

then,

{\rm Cov}(g(U),h(V))\leq\delta\cdot\|h(V)\|_{1}\cdot\|g(U)\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Denote by
\mu_{U,V},\mu_{U},\mu_{V},\mu_{U\mid V=v}
the probability measures of
(U,V)
,
U
,
V
, and
U
conditioned on
V=v
, respectively. We then have

\displaystyle|{\rm Cov}(g(U),h(V))|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}\left[g(U)h(V)\right]-\mathbb{E}[g(U)]\mathbb{E}[h(V)]|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{v}h(v)\left(\int_{u}g(u)\left(\mu_{U\mid V=v}(du)-\mu_{U}(du)\right)\right)\mu_{V}(dv)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{v}|h(v)|\cdot\|g(U)\|_{\infty}\cdot d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}(U\in\cdot\mid V=v),\mathbb{P}(U\in\cdot))\ \mu_{V}(dv)

\displaystyle\leq\|h(V)\|_{1}\cdot\|g(U)\|_{\infty}\cdot\delta.

∎

To use the above, we will need to bound the TV-distance between the conditional distributions.
The key is to analyze how much
\mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=s]
is affected by
s
.
An obvious bound is

d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=s],\ \mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=s^{\prime}])\leq e^{-|t-t^{\prime}|/t_{\rm mix}}.

However, this is still quite loose.
For example, for
t=t^{\prime}
, this bound
which is
hard to analyze
directly since
S_{it},S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}
.
To mitigate this, we

i
and

More precisely, this involves writing the target conditional probability as a convex combination of terms of the form

, and use the rapid mixing condition (

) to bound each of them.
To formalize, we need:

Lemma B.4

(Decomposition of conditional probability)
.

Let
Z
be a random variable taking values on a countable set
\cal Z
and
A,B
be
Z

Suppose

Then,

\mathbb{P}[A\mid B]=\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\mid Z=z]\cdot\mathbb{P}[Z=z\mid B].

Proof.

By the definition of conditional probability, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\mid Z=z]\cdot\mathbb{P}[Z=z\mid B]

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\mid Z=z]\cdot\frac{\mathbb{P}[\{Z=z\}\cap B]}{\mathbb{P}[Z=z]}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{P}[Z=z]}{\mathbb{P}[B]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\mid Z=z]\cdot\mathbb{P}[B\mid Z=z]\cdot\frac{\mathbb{P}[Z=z]}{\mathbb{P}[B]}

(7)

Since
A\perp\!\!\!\!\perp B\mid Z
, we have

(
B.1
)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\cap B\mid Z=z]\cdot\mathbb{P}[Z=z]}{\mathbb{P}[B]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{z\in\cal Z}\mathbb{P}[A\cap B]\cdot\mathbb{P}[Z=z]}{\mathbb{P}[B]}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[A\mid B].

∎

We next show that the state distribution of an individual at time

is largely insensitive to the state of another individual at time
t^{\prime}
, provided their last interaction occurred sufficiently long ago.
This follows from the result above by treating

as

For convenience, write

Lemma B.5

(Exponential Decay in the TV-Distance)
.

Suppose

Let
B

Then, for any

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{P}_{w}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in B],\ \mathbb{P}_{w}[S_{it}\in\cdot\ ])\leq e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}.

(8)

Proof.

By the definition of
\uptau^{\star}:=\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{it}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid\{S_{i,\uptau^{\star}},S_{i^{\prime},\uptau^{\star}}\}}.

So by

\displaystyle\quad\ \mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in B]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\cal S}\mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid(S_{i,\uptau^{\star}},S_{i^{\prime},\uptau^{\star}})=(s,s^{\prime})]\cdot\mathbb{P}[(S_{i,\uptau^{\star}},S_{i^{\prime},\uptau^{\star}})=(s,s^{\prime})\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in B].

\displaystyle\quad\ d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i,\uptau^{\star}}=s,\ S_{i^{\prime},\uptau^{\star}}=s^{\prime}],\ \mathbb{P}[S_{it}\in\cdot\ ]\right)

(

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\cal S}e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}\cdot\mathbb{P}[(S_{i,\uptau^{\star}},S_{i^{\prime},\uptau^{\star}})=(s,s^{\prime})\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in B]

Lemma B.6

(Covariance in the Outcomes)

For any
w\in\{0,1\}^{N\times T}

Proof.

Recall that
\epsilon_{it}=Y_{it}-\mu_{it}(S_{it},\ W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t})

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}\left(Y_{it},\ Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W=w\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Cov}(\mu_{it}(S_{it},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t}),\ \mu_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}(S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i^{\prime}),t^{\prime}})\mid W=w)

\displaystyle\quad+{\rm Cov}(\varepsilon_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}},\ \mu_{it}(S_{it},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t})\mid W=w)

\displaystyle\quad+{\rm Cov}(\mu_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}(S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i^{\prime}),t^{\prime}}),\ \varepsilon_{it}\mid W=w)

\displaystyle\quad+{\rm Cov}(\epsilon_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}},\ \epsilon_{it}\mid W=w).

The latter three terms are
0
by the exogenous noise assumption.
To bound the first term, note that by
Lemma

B.5
, for any
S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}
-measurable event
B
, we have

\displaystyle\ \quad d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathbb{P}_{w}\left[(S_{it},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t})\in\cdot\ \right],\ \mathbb{P}_{w}\left[(S_{it},W_{{\cal N}_{t}(i),t})\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathbb{P}_{w}\left[S_{it}\in\cdot\ \right],\ \mathbb{P}_{w}\left[S_{it}\in\cdot\mid S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-{|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t)|/{t_{{\rm mix}}}}}.

(10)

To conclude, apply
Lemma

B.3
with
(S_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}},W_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}})
in the role of
V
, with
(S_{it},W_{it})
in the role of
U
, with
\mu_{it},\mu_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}
in the role of
g,h
, and with
e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}
in the role of
\delta
, and noting that
\|g\|_{\infty},\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.

Section

B.1
then immediately leads to the claimed inequality.
∎

As the final building block, we simplify the covariance by conditioning on the exposure mappings.

Lemma B.7

(Expectation of Conditional Covariance)
.

For any
a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
, write

E_{a,a^{\prime}}=\left\{w\in\{0,1\}^{N\times T}:X_{ita}^{r}(w)=X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}(w)=1\right\}.

Then, for any
i,i^{\prime}\in[N]
and
t^{\prime}\leq t
such that
t\geq\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})+r
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right)\right]=\sum_{(a,a^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2}}(-1)^{a+a^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W\right)\mid W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}\right].

Proof.

Fix any
a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
.
Since
p_{it}^{r},p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}
are constants, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\frac{X_{ita}^{r}}{p_{it}^{r}}Y_{it},\frac{X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\ \middle|\ W\right)\right]=\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it},\ X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W)\right].

(11)

Note that
X_{ita}^{r},X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}
are functions of
W
(and hence deterministic once
W
is fixed), so

(
11
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{ita}^{r}(W)\cdot X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}(W)\cdot{\rm Cov}(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W)\right].

(12)

Since
X_{ita}^{r},X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}\in\{0,1\}
, the term
{\rm Cov}(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it},X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W)
is non-zero
only
when
W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}
.
Moreover, by the definition of
\uptau^{\star}
and our assumption that
t\geq\uptau^{\star}+r
, we have

X_{ita}^{r}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}[W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}]=\mathbb{P}[X_{ita}^{r}=1]\cdot\mathbb{P}[X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}=1]=p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r},

and

(
12
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\cdot\mathbb{P}[X_{ita}^{r}(W)=X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}(W)=1]\cdot\mathbb{E}[{\rm Cov}(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W)\mid X_{ita}^{r}=X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}=1]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W\right)\mid W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}\right].

(13)

Applying the above for all
a,a^{\prime}
and using the definition of
\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r}
, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right)\right]=\sum_{(a,a^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2}}(-1)^{a+a^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\ \middle|\ W\right)\mid W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}\right].

∎

Now we are able to bound the first term in
Equation

6
(the decomposition of covariance based on the LOTC).

Proposition B.8

(Bounding the Expected Conditional Covariance)
.

For any
i,i^{\prime}\in[N],1\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq T
such that
t\leq\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t)+r
,
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\ \middle|\ W\right)\right]{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}}.

Proof.

Fix
a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
and
w\in\{0,1\}^{N\times T}
.
Observe that if
X_{ita}^{r}(w)=0
or
X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}(w)=0
, then

{\rm Cov}\left(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it},\ X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\middle|\ W=w\right)=0.

Now suppose
X_{ita}^{r}(w)=X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}(w)=1
.
In this case, we have

{\rm Cov}\left(X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it},\ X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W=w\right)={\rm Cov}(Y_{it},\ Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W=w).

We now use
Lemma

B.3
to bound the above.
By
Lemma

B.6
, for any
Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}
-measurable events
A,B
, we have

d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathbb{P}_{w}[Y_{it}\in\cdot\mid Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in A],\ \mathbb{P}_{w}[Y_{it}\in\cdot\mid Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\in B]\right)\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}}.

Thus, by
Lemma

B.3
, for any
w
we have

\left|{\rm Cov}(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W=w)\right|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}}.

Thus,

\left|\mathbb{E}[{\rm Cov}(Y_{it},Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W)\mid W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}]\right|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}}.

Finally, by
Lemma

B.7
and
our assumption that
r\leq\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t)
, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r},\widehat{\Delta}_{it^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right)\right]\leq\sum_{(a,a^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Cov}\left(Y_{it},Y_{it^{\prime}}\mid W\right)\mid W\in E_{a,a^{\prime}}\right]\right|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})|/t_{\rm mix}}}.

∎

B.2
Bounding the Second Term in LOTC

The following says that if
it
and
i^{\prime}t^{\prime}
are far apart - in that there is a pair of large neighborhoods that intersect no common clusters, then their expected HT terms conditional on the treatment assignment
W
have low covariance.

Proposition B.9

(Covariance of Conditional Expectation)
.

Fix
i,i^{\prime}\in[N]
,
t^{\prime}\leq t
and
r\geq 0
with
t^{\prime}-r\geq\uptau^{\star}:=\uptau^{\star}(i,i^{\prime};t^{\prime})
.
Then,

{\rm Cov}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{it}^{r}\middle|\ W\right],\ \mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}\middle|\ W\right]\right)\leq\frac{12e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}.

Proof.

Fix
a,a^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
.
Write

\displaystyle\quad\ {\rm Cov}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it}}{p_{it}^{r}}\middle|\ W\right],\ \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}}{p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\middle|\ W\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}{\rm Cov}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it}\middle|\ W_{{\cal N}^{t-\uptau^{\star}}(it)}\right]+\varepsilon,\ \mathbb{E}\left[X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\middle|\ W_{{\cal N}^{t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}}(i^{\prime}t^{\prime})}\right]+\varepsilon^{\prime}\right),

(14)

where

\varepsilon=\mathbb{E}[X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it}\mid W_{{\cal N}^{t-\uptau^{\star}}(it)}]

and

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\mathbb{E}[X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W_{{\cal N}^{t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}}(i^{\prime}t^{\prime})}].

To bound
Section

B.2
, we need two observations.
First,
by the definition of
\uptau^{\star}
and our assumption that
t^{\prime}-r\geq\uptau^{\star}
, we have

W_{{\cal N}^{t-\uptau^{\star}}(it)}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp W_{{\cal N}^{t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}}(i^{\prime}t^{\prime})}

and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{ita}^{r}Y_{it}\mid W_{{\cal N}^{t-\uptau^{\star}}(it)}\right]\perp\!\!\!\!\perp\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}a^{\prime}}^{r}Y_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\mid W_{{\cal N}^{t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}}(i^{\prime}t^{\prime})}\right].

(15)

Furthermore, by
Lemma

A.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon\|_{\infty}\leq e^{-|t-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|\varepsilon^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}.

(16)

Thus, combining the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with

16

15

\eqref{eqn:052425f}\leq\frac{0+2e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}+\left(e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}\right)^{2}}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}\leq\frac{3e^{-|t^{\prime}-\uptau^{\star}|/t_{\rm mix}}.}{p_{it}^{r}\cdot p_{i^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{r}}.

The desired inequality follows by applying the above to all four combinations of
a,a^{\prime}
.∎

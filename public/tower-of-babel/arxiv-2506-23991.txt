Poisson-Dirac Submanifolds as a Paradigm for Imposing Constraints in Non-dissipative Plasma Models
F.W.Pinto, J.W.Burby

Abstract
We present a generalisation of Dirac constraint theory based on the theory of Poisson-Dirac Submanifolds. The theory is formulated in a coordinate free manner while simultaneously relaxing the invertibility condition as seen in standard Dirac constraint theory. We illustrate the method with two examples: elimination of the electron number density using Gauss’ Law and Ideal MHD as a slow manifold constraint in the ideal two-fluid model.



I
Introduction

In order to derive reduced models without artificial dissipation, we often look to place constraints such that the reduced system inherits the ideal structure. The need for such techniques arise across physics (Gauge field theories, fluid dynamics, plasma physics) and dynamical systems theory (control theory
Bloch 2003
). Robert Dewar explored the method of applying constraints to action principles as a method for deriving dissipation free reduced models
Dewar et al. 2020
;
Dewar and Qu 2022
. In Ref.
Dewar and Qu 2022
, Dewar applied constraints to bridge the gap between ideal Magnetohydrodynamics IMHD and Relaxed magnetohydrodynamics (RxMHD) by applying the ideal Ohm’s law constraint to the RxMHD Lagrangian as a Lagrange multiplier to the action. One issue with this approach is that Lagrange multipliers can produce inconsistent Euler Lagrange equations.

Dirac constraint theory (DCT) attacks the same problem from the Hamiltonian side, and looks for conditions on the constraints needed for the constrained system to be Hamiltonian. The method was originally introduced by Dirac
Dirac 1950
, who developed the theory in an effort to quantize gauge field theories. More recently, the theory has been applied to the problem of imposing the incompressiblity constraint on the (compressible) Euler equations
Nguyen and Turski 1999
;
Nguyen and Turski 2001
. The method was further developed by C. Chandre, P.J Morrison and others
Chandre et al. 2013
;
Morrison, Lebovitz, and Biello 2009
;
Chandre, Tassi, and Morrison 2010a
.

The Dirac bracket used in the most recent work of Morrison
Morrison, Andreussi, and Pegoraro 2020
;
Chandre, Tassi, and Morrison 2010b
provides an alternate method for imposing constraints that guarantee clean evolution equations.

Additionally, constraint theory based on an alternative to Dirac brackets has been used to show how reduced theories like ideal magnetohydrodynamics (IMHD) inherit their Hamiltonian structure from the ideal two fluid Maxwell (ITFM)
Burby 2017
.

The Dirac constraint method has two drawbacks. It first requires a coordinate system in which the constraint can be written as the vanishing of some set of coordinates
C=0
and secondly requires that the Poisson bracket of the components
\{C_{i},C_{j}\}
is invertible
Morrison, Andreussi, and Pegoraro 2020
. We describe a coordinate free geometrical method that relaxes the non degeneracy requirement by exploiting the theory of Poisson-Dirac Submanifolds. Marsden, Weinstein and others developed the local picture of Poisson geometry in the early to mid 1980’s. In particular, Poisson-Dirac submanifolds appeared explicitly in Ref.
Marsden and Ratiu 1986
. C. Chandre in Ref.
Chandre 2015
was able to relax the non-degeneracy requirement by using a psuedoinverse for
\{C_{i},C_{j}\}
. Given the psuedoinverse is not unique infinite dimension, it is not clear that such a bracket is unique. Moreover, the proof of the Jacobi identity in Ref.
Chandre 2015
was coordinate dependent, and assumed that the constraints can be separated based on their class. The Poisson-Dirac construction resolves these issues and provides geometric avenue for constructing dissipation free brackets.

In section 1, we outline the Poisson-Dirac Constraint Method as generalisation of DCT. We then make the the theory coordinate free by formulating the constraint sets as submanifolds of the phase space. In sections 2 and 3, we illustrate the method with two examples: elimination of the electron number density using Gauss’ Law and Ideal MHD as a slow manifold constraint in the ideal two-fluid model. Both examples exhibit the constraint as the graph of a function although the theory is applicable more generally as long as the constrained system is a submanifold of the full system.

II
Poisson-Dirac Constraint Method and Slow Manifolds

We proceed by first recalling the assumptions of Dirac constraint theory. Let
M
be the phase space with Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
and suppose that there are coordinates on
M
, say
(\sigma,c)=(\sigma_{1},\dots\sigma_{n},c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})
, such that the constraint set of interest can be written in the form

\Sigma=\{(\sigma,c):c=0\}.

(1)

In order to derive a Poisson bracket on
\Sigma
, DCT assumes that the matrix of Poisson brackets

\{c,c\}_{ij}=\{c_{i},c_{j}\}

(2)

is invertible. In such a case, there is a Poisson bracket
Morrison, Andreussi, and Pegoraro 2020
on
\Sigma
given by the formula

\{f,g\}_{\text{D}}=\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}\cdot[\{\sigma,\sigma\}_{\Sigma}-\{\sigma,c\}_{\Sigma}\cdot\{c,c\}_{\Sigma}^{-1}\cdot\{c,\sigma\}_{\Sigma}]\cdot\functionalderivative{g}{\sigma}.

(3)

Here
f,g:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, the
\Sigma
subscript denotes restriction to
\Sigma
, and
\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}
are vectors of derivatives of
f
with respect to
\sigma
. Moreover, the above bracket defines a valid Poisson bracket on the whole phase space, not just
\Sigma
. This bracket, together with a Hamiltonian function on
\Sigma
, can be used to formulate Hamiltonian reduced models on
\Sigma
. The “reduction” corresponds to passing from the ambient phase space
M
to the constraint subspace
\Sigma
.

The invertibility requirement on
\{c,c\}
is artificially restrictive. Suppose instead that the kernel of
\{c,c\}
is a vector subspace of
\text{ker}\,\{\sigma,c\}
:

\ker\{c,c\}\subset\ker\{\sigma,c\}.

(4)

Under this new assumption, we can still define a bracket on
\Sigma
using the formula

\{f,g\}_{\text{PD}}=\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}\cdot\{\sigma,\sigma\}_{\Sigma}\cdot\functionalderivative{g}{\sigma}-\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta c}\cdot\{c,c\}\cdot\frac{\Delta g}{\Delta c},

(5)

where the vector valued functions
\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta c},\frac{\Delta g}{\Delta c}
are (possibly non-unique) solutions of

\displaystyle\{c,\sigma\}\cdot\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}+\{c,c\}\cdot\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta c}=0

(6)

\displaystyle\{c,\sigma\}\cdot\functionalderivative{g}{\sigma}+\{c,c\}\cdot\frac{\Delta g}{\Delta c}=0.

(7)

The solvability conditions for these equations, namely

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=v\cdot\{c,\sigma\}\cdot\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=v\cdot\{c,\sigma\}\cdot\functionalderivative{g}{\sigma},

for all
v\in\text{ker}\,\{c,c\}
, are automatically satisfied because
v\in\text{ker}\,\{c,c\}
implies
v\in\text{ker}\,\{\sigma,c\}
and
v\cdot\{c,\sigma\}\cdot w=-w\cdot\{\sigma,c\}\cdot v
. Solutions are determined modulo elements of
\text{ker}\{c,c\}
, but this non-uniqueness does not affect the value of the bracket
\{f,g\}_{\text{PD}}
because
\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta c},\frac{\Delta g}{\Delta c}
are each contracted with
\{c,c\}
in (
5
). We remark that (
6
) are the same conditions for the existence for the existence of the psuedoinverse in Ref.
Chandre 2015
.

In fact, Eq. (
5
) does indeed define a Poisson bracket on
\Sigma
, provided
\{f,g\}_{\text{PD}}
is smooth whenever
f,g
are smooth. To explain why, as well as to provide a coordinate-independent geometric formulation of (
5
), we will deduce (
5
) from the theory of Poisson-Dirac submanifolds
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
. In so doing we will outline an extension of the Dirac constraint method that we term the Poisson-Dirac constraint method.

Before constructing the geometric picture, we give an example that demonstrates the Poisson-Dirac constraint method is strictly more general than the usual Dirac constraint method
Morrison, Andreussi, and Pegoraro 2020
.

Example 1
.

Let the constraint coordinates
c
be Casimirs of the Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
so that
\Sigma
as defined in (
1
) is the level set of (some of the) Casimirs. Then, all the matrices
\{c,c\},\{\sigma,c\},\{c,\sigma\}

4

\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta c},\frac{\Delta g}{\Delta c}
are a valid solution to (
6
), Thus implying that the bracket on
\Sigma
is

\{f,g\}_{Dirac}=\functionalderivative{f}{\sigma}\cdot\{\sigma,\sigma\}_{\Sigma}\cdot\functionalderivative{g}{\sigma}.

(8)

Even though the inverse
\{c,c\}^{-1}
does not exist. More generally, the constraint functions

may functionally depend on Casimirs in a non-obvious way, leading to a non-zero

We will now arrive at (
5
) using a coordinate-independent argument. We first define the geometric analog of the Poisson bracket, the Poisson bivector. Given a smooth manifold

\{\cdot,\cdot\}

Definition 1
.

Given a Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
on a smooth manifold
M
, the
Poisson bivector

\pi\in\mathcal{X}^{2}(M)
is the unique bivector field such that

\pi(df,dg)=\{f,g\},

(9)

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

In a local coordinate patch on
M
,
\pi
is written

\pi=\pi^{ij}\partialderivative{x^i}\wedge\partialderivative{x^j}.

(10)

Here, we are assuming a sum over the indices
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. The objects
\partialderivative{x^j}
can be thought of as basis vectors dual to the basis co-vectors
dx^{j}
. We note one can formulate the condition for

to satisfy a Jacobi identity by use of the
Schouten Bracket
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
;
Marsden and Ratiu 1999
of multi-vector fields. For a review on the calculus of multivector fields, see Ref.
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
.

The bivector induces a map
\pi^{\#}:T^{*}M\rightarrow TM
that sends each covector
\alpha
to the vector
\pi^{\#}(\alpha)
defined by requiring
\overline{\alpha}(\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha))=\pi(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)
for all covectors
\overline{\alpha}
with the same basepoint as
\alpha
. In finite dimensions with coordinates

the components of
\pi^{\#}
are given by
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
;
Marsden and Ratiu 1999

(\pi^{\#})^{ij}=\{x^{i},x^{j}\}.

(11)

One notices that if
f
is any smooth function on
M
then,

\pi^{\#}(df)=X_{f},

(12)

where
X_{f}
denotes the Hamiltonian vector field with Hamiltonian
f
. For a comprehensive review on Cartan’s calculus in mechanics and plasma physics, see Ref.
Abraham and Marsden 1967
and Ref.
MacKay 2020
. Naively,

can be thought similarly to
(\nabla f)^{T}
(in fact, they are equal up to an isomorphism that depends on one’s choice of metric tensor). Geometers call the map
\pi^{\#}
the

Crainic and Fernandes 2021
;
Zambon 2011
;
Fernandes, Ortega, and Ratiu 2009

by defining

as the unique bivector that satisfies

\pi(df,dg)=\langle df,\pi^{\#}(dg)\rangle.

(13)

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:T^{*}_{p}M\times T_{p}M\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the non-degenerate pairing between the tangent space
T_{p}M
and its dual
T^{\ast}_{p}M

Now we are ready to introduce the type of constraint that underlies Poisson-Dirac constraint theory. We consider constraints that define
Poisson-Dirac submanifolds

Definition 2
.

A
Poisson-Dirac submanifold

N\subset M
of a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
is a submanifold such that for all
p\in N

PD.1

T_{p}N\cap(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=\{0\}

PD.2

The induced bivector field on
N
is smooth.

Here
(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=\pi^{\#}(T_{p}N)^{\circ}
is the
\pi
-orthogonal complement of
T_{p}N
, and
(T_{p}N)^{\circ}:=\{\alpha_{p}\in T^{\ast}_{p}M:\alpha_{p}(\nu)=0,\forall\nu\in T_{p}N\}\subset T_{p}^{\ast}M
is the annihilator of
T_{p}N
.

Remark 1
.

The first condition implies that
\pi
induces a bivector field on
N
that is possibly non-smooth. Indeed, if
f,g
are functions on
N
and
p\in N
let
\widetilde{(df_{p})},\widetilde{(dg_{p})}
denote
1
-forms in
T^{\ast}_{p}M
such that
\widetilde{(df_{p})}\mid T_{p}N=df_{p}
,
\widetilde{(dg_{p})}\mid T_{p}N=dg_{p}
, and
\widetilde{(df_{p})}\mid(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=\widetilde{(dg_{p})}\mid(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=0
. Here,
\widetilde{(df_{p})}\mid T_{p}N
and
\widetilde{(df_{p})}\mid(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}
denote restriction of the covector
\widetilde{(df_{p})}
to the subspaces
T_{p}N
and
(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}
respectively. The bracket between
f
and
g
at
p\in N
is then defined as
\{f,g\}_{N}=\pi_{p}(\widetilde{(df_{p})},\widetilde{(dg_{p})})
. If
\widetilde{(df_{p})}^{\prime},\widetilde{(dg_{p})}^{\prime}
denote another pair of
1
-forms in
T_{p}^{*}M
satisfying our conditions then
\widetilde{(df_{p})}^{\prime}-\widetilde{(df_{p})}\in(T_{p}N)^{\circ}
, which implies
\pi_{p}(\widetilde{(df_{p})}^{\prime},\widetilde{(dg_{p})}^{\prime})=\pi_{p}(\widetilde{(df_{p})},\widetilde{(dg_{p})})

In order to gain a better intuition for

, we provide a pictorial representation (given in Fig.1 below) and make analogy with Riemannian geometry. Let

W^{\perp}=\{v\in V:g(v,w)=0\quad\forall w\in W\}.

(14)

It is well known that

(15)

Moreover, there is a inner product defined on the dual space

At each point

To relate the general theory of Poisson-Dirac submanifolds to the coordinate-based DCT discussed earlier, we will now consider phase spaces of the form
\Sigma\times C

\Sigma,C
are vector spaces. The Poisson anchor map on such a space takes the convenient block form:

\pi^{\#}=\begin{pmatrix}\pi^{\#}_{cc}&{\pi}^{\#}_{c\sigma}\\
{\pi}^{\#}_{\sigma c}&{\pi}^{\#}_{\sigma\sigma}\end{pmatrix},

{\pi^{\#}}_{ij}
denotes a linear map where the subscripts

((\pi^{\#})^{ij})^{\ast}=-(\pi^{\#})^{ji}

Where the upper star denotes the dual map. In general, the diagonal terms in the above Poisson bracket are not necessarily zero.

4
) holds if and only if

Proposition II.1
.

\Sigma
is a Poisson-Dirac submanifold if and only if
\ker\{c,c\}\subset\ker\{\sigma,c\}
and the bracket defined in equation (
5
) is smooth.

Proof.

Let us formulate the Poisson-Dirac condition algebraically. Suppose that
\nu\in T_{p}\Sigma\cap(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp\pi}
, and let
\pi^{\#}_{cc}:C^{\ast}\to C
,
\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}:C^{\ast}\to T\Sigma
be the components of the Poisson anchor with source
C^{\ast}
. Given
\nu\in(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp\pi}
, there exists an
\alpha_{c}\in T_{p}^{\ast}C
with

\nu=\pi^{\#}_{cc}(\alpha_{c})+\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}(\alpha_{c})

(24)

enforcing the condition
\nu\in T_{p}\Sigma
we require that
\alpha_{c}\in\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}
. In order for
\Sigma
to be Poisson Dirac, we require that
\alpha_{c}\in\ker\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}
. Suppose towards a contradiction that
\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}\not\subset\ker\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}
. Then there exist elements of
\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}
that are not contained within
\ker\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}
, which would contradict that the only the zero vector is contained within the intersection.

Going in the other direction, Given
\nu\in(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp\pi}
, there exists a
\alpha_{c}\in T_{p}^{\ast}C
with

\nu=\pi^{\#}_{cc}(\alpha_{c})+\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}(\alpha_{c})

(25)

Suppose that
\nu
is non zero and lies within the intersection
(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp\pi}\cap T_{p}\Sigma
. Given
\nu\in T_{p}\Sigma
, this forces
\pi^{\#}_{cc}(\alpha_{c})=0
which implies
\alpha_{c}\in\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}
. But
\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}\subset\ker\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}
, so
\nu=0
. Given
\nu
was arbitrary, we conclude that
(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp\pi}\cap T_{p}\Sigma=\{0\}
. Finally, the bracket defined in equation (
5
) is the induced bracket on
\Sigma
so condition
PD.1
holds if and only if (
5
) is smooth.

Thus, the necessary and sufficient condition that
\Sigma
is Poisson-Dirac is given by the kernel nesting condition along with a smoothness condition on the induced pointwise bivector.

Translating DCT into the language of geometry, we see that DCT is a special type of Poisson-Dirac submanifold called a

Definition 3
.

A
Poisson transversal

N\subset M
of a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
is a submanifold such that for all
p\in N

PT.1

T_{p}N\cap(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=\{0\}

PT.2

T_{p}N+\imaginary\pi_{p}^{\#}=T_{p}M
.

Remark 3
.

Note that the submanifold as given in the definition above

cannot be a Poisson submanifold, as the transversality condition is not satisfied for Poisson submanifolds. Even though Poisson submanifolds are not Poisson transversals, they are Poisson-Dirac submanifolds as
(T_{p}N)^{\pi\perp}=\{0\}
.

Remark 4
.

It is worth noting that when
\pi
has non constant rank, the foliation by symplectic leaves is potentially singular. The above definition does not imply that the dimension of the image of

Transversality enforces several important properties such as the smoothness of

. In the below proposition, we demonstrate that the invertibility condition on

Proposition II.3
.

Within standard DCT,
\{c,c\}
is invertible if and only if the constraint set is a Poisson transversal.

Proof.

In the coordinates
(\sigma,c)
, the tangent space to the constraint is given
T_{p}\Sigma=\{(\delta\sigma,\delta c):\delta c=0\}
. Assuming that the matrix
\{c,c\}_{ij}
is invertible, then the
\pi^{\#}_{cc}:C^{\ast}\to C
section of the block matrix must be invertible, so that
T_{p}\Sigma+T_{p}L_{p}=T_{p}M
. Suppose
\nu\in T_{p}\Sigma\cap T_{p}\Sigma^{\perp\pi}
. Then, there exists an
\alpha\in T_{p}\Sigma^{\circ}=T_{p}^{\ast}C
such that

\nu=\pi^{\#}_{cc}(\alpha)+\pi^{\#}_{\sigma c}(\alpha).

(28)

Given
\nu\in T_{p}\Sigma
,
\pi^{\#}_{cc}\alpha
must be zero. Given
\pi^{\#}_{cc}
is invertible, the only solution is

so that

as required. Going the other direction, the transversality condition implies that

must be surjective. Given

, and
\pi^{\#}_{cc}
is skew-symmetric,
\ker\pi^{\#}_{cc}=\{0\}
, making
\pi^{\#}_{cc}
an isomorphism.
∎

From this geometric standpoint, it becomes more clear why the bracket defined in equation (3) is valid on the whole phase space, not just
\Sigma
. When the constraint manifold is given by a regular level set of a function, transversality implies level sets neighboring the constraint set are also Poisson transversals with their own induced Poisson tensors. The collection of symplectic leaves on the level sets neighboring the constraint define a symplectic foliation, and therefore a Poisson tensor extending the constraint Poisson tensor, in an open neighborhood around
\Sigma
. Notice that this argument also immediately implies that the constraint functions are Casimirs for the bracket defined in the neighborhood of the constraint.

When
\Sigma
is Poisson-Dirac, the rank of
T_{p}\Sigma^{\perp\pi}
is possibly non constant, and this argument for extending the Poisson tensor off of the constraint manifold can fail. We return to the LV bracket example to better illustrate this point. Additionally, we show that it is not always possible to extend the Poisson bivector on
\Sigma
in an open neighborhood around
\Sigma
when
\Sigma
is Poisson-Dirac.

Example 3
.

Going back to the LV bracket in the previous example, let us try to construct a bivector in a neighborhood

of the origin. Recall that the bivector on

is given by

\pi_{N}=\frac{1}{2}uv\partial_{u}\wedge\partial_{v}

(29)

The submanifold

may be written as the level set of the constraint functions

(30)

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)=x-z

(31)

\displaystyle f_{2}(x)=y-w

(32)

A direct computation verifies that these constraints are second class constraints when

of the matrix

proposed in Ref.

fails to be smooth as the pseudoinverse

for more information on this. In essence, the smoothness of

Chandre 2015
relied on a change of coordinates that tacitly assumes that

bracket on
N
(recall that the bracket was scaled by
\epsilon^{2}
). We now look to apply
\pi^{\#}(\delta\tilde{F})
and enforce the condition
\pi^{\#}(\delta\tilde{F})\in T_{p}N
:

\begin{split}&\pi_{N}(\delta\tilde{F})=\\
&\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}}\nabla\times{\Theta_{\bm{E}}}\\
\frac{1}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}}\nabla\times{(\Theta_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{u}_{i}+F_{\bm{B}})}-\frac{a_{e}}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}m_{e}}\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}}-\frac{a_{i}}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}m_{i}}(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{B}-\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}})\\
\frac{a_{i}}{m_{i}^{2}n_{i}}((F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}})\times\bm{B})+\frac{a_{i}}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}m_{i}}\Theta_{\bm{E}}-m_{i}^{-1}\nabla F_{n_{i}}-\frac{1}{m_{i}n_{i}}(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{B}-\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}})\times(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{i})\\
\frac{a_{e}}{m_{e}^{2}n_{e}}(\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}}\times\bm{B})+\frac{a_{e}}{\epsilon\epsilon_{o}m_{e}}\Theta_{\bm{E}}-\frac{1}{m_{e}n_{e}}\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}}\times(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{e})\\
-m_{i}^{-1}\nabla\cdot{(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{B}-\Theta_{\bm{u}_{e}})}\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(63)

where
n_{e}=n_{e}(n_{i},\bm{E})
is a function of
n_{i}
and
\bm{E}
. Solving perturbatively, we find that the solution at order zero is

\displaystyle\Theta^{0}_{\bm{E}}=0

(64)

\displaystyle\Theta^{0}_{\bm{u}_{e}}=\frac{Z_{i}\nu}{(1+Z_{i}\nu)}F_{\bm{u}_{i}}.

(65)

At order 1, we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{1}_{\bm{E}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Z_{i}\epsilon_{0}}{(1+\nu Z_{i})^{2}}(\frac{1}{m_{i}n_{i}}+\frac{1}{m_{e}n_{e}})F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times\bm{B}

(66)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Theta^{1}_{\bm{u}_{e}}&=\frac{\nu m_{i}(1+\nu Z_{i})}{q_{e}}\nabla\times F_{\bm{B}}-\frac{Z_{i}^{2}\epsilon_{0}}{(1+\nu Z_{i})}(\frac{1-\nu}{m_{i}n_{i}}\\
&-\frac{2\nu}{m_{e}n_{e}})(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}.\end{split}

(67)

One subtle issue here is that
n_{e}=Z_{i}n_{i}+\epsilon^{2}\epsilon_{o}q_{e}\nabla\cdot\bm{E}
is epsilon dependent, so the equations above have extra, hidden
\epsilon
dependence. In order to resolve this, we expand
1/n_{e}
as a power series in
\epsilon
:

\frac{1}{n_{e}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(\epsilon^{2}\epsilon_{0}q_{e}^{-1}\nabla\cdot\bm{E})^{n}}{(Z_{i}n_{i})^{n}}=\frac{1}{Z_{i}n_{i}}+\order{\epsilon^2}.

(68)

The first term in the expansion is
\epsilon^{2}
, so that terms with a factor of
1/n_{e}
are two orders higher in epsilon. As a result, we remove any terms a factor of
1/n_{e}
as they are at least order
\epsilon^{3}
in the above expression. Correcting, we find that

\displaystyle\Theta^{1}_{\bm{E}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{\mathsf{m}n_{i}}F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times\bm{B}

(69)

\displaystyle\Theta^{1}_{\bm{u}_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{e}}{q_{e}(1+\nu Z_{i})}\nabla\times F_{\bm{B}}-\frac{Z_{i}m_{e}\epsilon_{0}}{\mathsf{m}^{2}n_{i}}(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times\bm{B})\times\bm{B}

(70)

where
\mathsf{m}=(1+\nu Z_{i})m_{i}
. In order to obtain the bracket at order
1
in epsilon, one would expect that the second order extension function would be needed. However, for reasons that will become more apparent later, the term involving the second order extension function in the Poisson bracket will vanish.
Thus, the extensions up to order
\epsilon
are written

\delta\tilde{F}_{\epsilon}=\delta\tilde{F}_{0}+\epsilon\delta\tilde{F}_{1}

(71)

where

\begin{split}\delta\tilde{F}_{1}=\int F_{n_{i}}\delta n_{i}+(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta^{0}_{\bm{u}_{e}})\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\\
+F_{\bm{B}}\cdot\delta\bm{B}\,d^{3}\bm{x}+\int\Theta^{0}_{\bm{u}_{e}}\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{e}\,d^{3}\bm{x}\end{split}

(72)

and

\begin{split}\delta\tilde{F}_{1}=\int-(\Theta^{1}_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{B}+\Theta^{1}_{\bm{u}_{e}})\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,d^{3}\bm{x}\\
+(\Theta^{1}_{\bm{E}}\times\bm{u}_{i})\cdot\delta\bm{B}\,d^{3}\bm{x}+\int\Theta^{1}_{\bm{u}_{e}}\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{e}+\Theta^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\delta\bm{E}\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(73)

Now we explicitly write the Poisson structure on
N
. Applying these extensions to the bracket, we write
\pi^{\#}(\delta\tilde{F})
perturbatively:

\epsilon^{2}\pi(\delta\tilde{F})=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\epsilon^{j}\pi^{j}(\delta\tilde{F}).

(74)

In this paper, we aim to show that the order
1
bracket reproduces MHD so that higher order terms in the bracket are not needed.
A trivial computation gives
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi^{\#}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle=0
. Notice that since the Poisson tensor is skew symmetric, and
\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})=0
, we find that

\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle,\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle,\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{2})\rangle=0.

(75)

We contract this vector field with an extended form
\delta\tilde{G}
of
\delta G
defined similarly to
\delta F
and collect the terms order by order. By the previous argument, all order zero terms are zero. The only non zero order
\epsilon
term is
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{1}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle
. Explicitly, this term is

\begin{split}\langle\delta\tilde{G},\pi(\delta\tilde{F})\rangle=\\
\int\frac{-Z_{i}q_{i}}{m_{i}^{2}n_{i}}(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta^{0}_{G,\bm{u}_{e}})\cdot([F_{\bm{u}_{i}}-\Theta^{0}_{F,\bm{u}_{e}}]\times B)\\
+\frac{q_{e}}{m_{e}^{2}n_{e}}\Theta^{0}_{G,\bm{u}_{e}}\cdot\Theta^{0}_{F,\bm{u}_{e}}\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(76)

Using the simplifications due to
1/n_{e}=1/(Z_{i}n_{i})
at
\order{1}
, we find that the first order terms vanish:

\begin{split}\langle\delta\tilde{G},\pi(\delta\tilde{F})\rangle=\int\frac{-Z_{i}q_{e}}{\mathsf{m}^{2}n_{i}}G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\cdot(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times B)\\
+\frac{Z_{i}q_{e}}{\mathsf{m}^{2}n_{i}}G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\cdot(F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times\bm{B})\,d^{3}\bm{x}=0.\end{split}

(77)

The order 2 terms are
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle+\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{1}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle+\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{2}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle+\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{1}(\delta\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle
. Starting with
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{2}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle
, we find that

\begin{split}&\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{2}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle=\\
&\int-\mathsf{m}^{-1}(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\cdot\nabla F_{n_{i}}+G_{n_{i}}\nabla\cdot F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\\
&+\frac{1}{(1+\nu Z_{i})^{2}}(\frac{(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{i})}{m_{i}n_{i}}\\
&+\frac{(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{e})(Z_{i}\nu)^{2}}{m_{e}n_{e}})\cdot(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(78)

Upon restriction to
N
, we find

\begin{split}&\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{2}(\delta\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle\\
&=\int-\mathsf{m}^{-1}(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\cdot\nabla F_{n_{i}}+G_{n_{i}}\nabla\cdot F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\\
&+\frac{(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{i})}{\mathsf{m}n_{i}}\cdot(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(79)

Moving onward, we consider the
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{1}(\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle+\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{1}(\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle
term. Considering the sum of these terms is advantageous because of the skew symmetry:

\begin{split}\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{1}(\tilde{F}_{0})\rangle+\langle\tilde{G}_{0},\pi_{1}(\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle=\\
\int\frac{1}{\mathsf{m}n_{i}}\bm{B}\cdot(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times(\nabla\times F_{\bm{B}})-F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times(\nabla\times G_{\bm{B}}))\\
-\frac{q_{e}}{\epsilon_{0}m_{e}}(\tilde{F}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{G}_{\bm{u}_{e}}^{1}-\tilde{G}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{F}_{\bm{u}_{e}}^{1})\\
-\frac{Z_{i}q_{e}}{\epsilon_{0}m_{i}}(\tilde{G}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{F}_{\bm{u}_{i}}^{1}-\tilde{F}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{G}_{\bm{u}_{i}}^{1})\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(80)

Finally, we compute
\langle\delta\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{0}(\delta\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle
:

\begin{split}\langle\tilde{G}_{1},\pi_{0}(\tilde{F}_{1})\rangle=\int\frac{q_{e}}{\epsilon_{0}m_{e}}(\tilde{F}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{G}_{\bm{u}_{e}}^{1}-\tilde{G}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{F}_{\bm{u}_{e}}^{1})\\
+\frac{Z_{i}q_{e}}{\epsilon_{0}m_{i}}(\tilde{G}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{F}_{\bm{u}_{i}}^{1}-\tilde{F}^{1}_{\bm{E}}\cdot\tilde{G}_{\bm{u}_{i}}^{1})\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(81)

Putting this all together, we find the Poisson bracket on
N
to be

\begin{split}\pi(\delta G,\delta F)=\int-\mathsf{m}^{-1}(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\cdot\nabla F_{n_{i}}+G_{n_{i}}\nabla\cdot F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\\
+\frac{(\nabla\times\bm{u}_{i})}{\mathsf{m}n_{i}}\cdot(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times F_{\bm{u}_{i}})\\
+\frac{1}{\mathsf{m}n_{i}}\bm{B}\cdot(G_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times(\nabla\times F_{\bm{B}})-F_{\bm{u}_{i}}\times(\nabla\times G_{\bm{B}}))\,d^{3}\bm{x}.\end{split}

(82)

This bracket coincides with the bracket given by Ref.
Burby 2017
in the limit
\epsilon=0
.

VI
Discussion

The Poisson-Dirac constraint method provides a more general, coordinate free approach to applying dissipation free constraints to plasma models. The wider applicability of the PD constraint model allows for the construction of novel reduced plasma models. In particular, the PD constraint method could be applied to the multi-fluid problem in Ref.
Dewar et al. 2020
to derive a Poisson bracket on the constrained space while avoiding aforementioned issues with inconsistent equations of motion. In a similar fashion to
Burby 2017
, this new constraint method can also be used to explain why known reduced plasma models have Hamiltonian structure.

It is well known that the two fluid equations arise as a reduction by symmetry
Burby 2017
so that the Poisson bracket on the ITFM space admits a symplectic realisation (see Ref.
Vaisman 1994a
;
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
for details on symplectic realisation). By performing the reduction of bracket for the two fluid slow manifold using the symplectic realisation, Ref.
Burby 2017
was able to find a closed form expression for the Poisson bracket. This presents a tension between the Poisson and symplectic methods for reduction since the bracket obtained via the PD constraint method yields a bracket that is formally an infinite series in
\epsilon
(the bracket contains terns with a factor of
1/n_{e}
making it formally an infinite series in
\epsilon
). While the leading-order terms in this expansion are guaranteed to define a genuine Poisson bracket, truncating the series at higher orders may violate the Jacobi identity. It may be possible to deform the Poisson bracket into the bracket in Ref.
Burby 2017
via Poisson automorphisms. The obstruction to finding such an automorphism is represented as a co-homology class in the Poisson co-homology (see Ref.
Crainic and Fernandes 2021
;
Vaisman 1994b
for details). Unfortunately, the Poisson co-homology is notoriously difficult to compute. In fact, the co-homology groups are often infinite dimensional. In the literature, the co-homology has been computed under assumptions that are too restrictive for application for plasma physics problems
Xu 1992
. Fernandes et al
Fernandes, Ortega, and Ratiu 2009
note that
any
Poisson manifold is integrable by Weinstein symplectic Groupoid. It may be possible to employ an approach similar to Ref.
Xu 1992
using the more general structure in Ref.
Fernandes, Ortega, and Ratiu 2009
;
Tseng and Zhu 2004
to establish the equivalence of the Poisson and symplectic reduction methods. This issue will be resolved in a future paper.

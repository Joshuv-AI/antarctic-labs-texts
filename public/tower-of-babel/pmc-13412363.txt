Design and implementation of an international multiarm, multistage master protocol for trials of complex traumatic wound care during active war in Ukraine
Authors: Corey B. Bills, Camille J. Hochheimer, Samantha C. Roberts, Roman Fishchuk, Jennifer Peers, Samantha Simon, Olena V. Omelianenko, Hnat L. Herych, Oleksii V. Altanets, Nazariy A. Beley, Vikhyat S. Bebarta, Sean Keenan, Laurel Beaty, Stacy A. Trent, Nichole E. Carlson, Adit A. Ginde
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Traumatic wound infections are a significant contributor to morbidity and mortality during war. Given challenges of performing clinical trials during war and the urgent need for interventions to prevent and treat infected traumatic wounds exacerbated by global increases in antimicrobial resistance, we developed the Prevention and Treatment Clinical Trials for Antimicrobial Resistance Research to Improve Outcomes of Traumatic Wounds in Ukraine (PACT-ARROW). We provide an overview of the protocol and implementation strategy.METHODS:PACT-ARROW is a multiarm multistage (MAMS) platform designed to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and effectiveness of device and therapeutic interventions to optimize outcomes of patients hospitalized in Ukraine with acute traumatic wounds. The aim is to improve standard or available care with the possibility to study multiple interventions simultaneously. A platform steering committee will review potential interventions, determine alignment with the mission of the protocol, and promote those with sufficient evidence to warrant testing in a trial.RESULTS:PACT-ARROW has undergone local and national ethics approval in the United States and Ukraine. We began with a prospective observational cohort (ARROW), with over 350 patients enrolled. A corresponding interventional trial, with a targeted enrollment of 150, just began enrollment. Expansion of interventions and target populations is planned.CONCLUSIONS:The intended benefits of the platform include standardized patient enrollment criteria, outcomes tailored to feasible data collection during active war, and improvements in patient care for the treatment of complex and infected traumatic wounds. The design is intended to apply to a broad set of interventions with relevant patient-centered outcomes, all while addressing challenges of performing clinical trials in a war environment. Future studies utilizing the PACT-ARROW master protocol are planned. (J Trauma Acute Care Surg. 2026;101: S58–S65. Copyright © 2026 The Author(s). Acute traumatic wounds are one of the most common contributors to morbidity and mortality in war and conflict.1 Among survivors, wound infections have life-altering effects, including amputation and debilitation from prolonged hospitalizations. Due to accelerating antimicrobial resistance (AMR) worldwide, complicated wound infections are only expected to increase.2 Understanding the changing epidemiology, microbiology, and associated outcomes of wound infections during war is critical to inform interventions to prevent and treat wound infections and to minimize morbidity and mortality among injured warfighters and civilians.

Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 has resulted in over 46,000 Ukrainian casualties, and 13,000 deaths as of May 2025.3 Early data among Ukrainians seeking care outside of the country suggests unprecedented rates of multidrug-resistant organisms (MDROs) with patterns untreatable by available antibiotics.4–8 Given that most injured Ukrainians stay within the country, these reports have substantial limitations. In-country estimates before the war indicated high rates of AMR, with data specific to Lviv in Western Ukraine suggesting that at least two thirds of hospitalized patients had hospital-acquired MDROs.6–9

In 2024, we began observational data collection on traumatic acute wound infections across multiple hospitals in Ukraine. This work led to the acknowledgment of the increasing risk of MDROs in Ukraine, the role of AMR surveillance, critical gaps in care and the subsequent need for a mechanism for studying novel interventions in wound care during a war.10–13

Given the urgent clinical need and difficulty of instituting clinical trials in a war environment, we developed a multiarm multistage (MAMS) platform master protocol design to better understand potential improvements in the care of acute traumatic wounds. The Prevention and Treatment Clinical Trials for Antimicrobial Resistance Research to Improve Outcomes of Traumatic Wounds (PACT-ARROW) aims to provide an agile research platform to investigate strategies and treatments that may improve the health of patients hospitalized with traumatic wounds. Other MAMS platforms, including the trial design explained here, are intended to preposition infrastructure and to facilitate a rapid and flexible response, with high standards of scientific rigor, to support the development of consensus for standard of care (SOC) and test novel interventions for acute traumatic wound care.14–16 The aim of this manuscript is to describe the process and current status of the PACT-ARROW protocol.

METHODS

Objectives

The primary objective of the PACT-ARROW platform is to facilitate the efficient and rigorous execution of flexible randomized clinical trial designs investigating the safety, efficacy, and effectiveness of strategies and therapeutic interventions for hospitalized adult patients with traumatic wounds in a wartime environment. Each trial within PACT-ARROW will have its own specific objective and hypothesis. The primary objective for each trial will be to compare the efficacy or effectiveness of the intervention or strategy under investigation as compared with SOC (the comparator) for a chosen primary outcome among participants randomized in that trial. For this protocol, we adhered to the SPIRIT 2025 guidelines for protocols of trials (SDC 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/TA/F348, SPIRIT 2025 Guidelines Checklist).17

Trial Design

PACT-ARROW rests on an initial observational trial and uses this framework to design multiple trials to actively enroll patients at the same time and for trials to end and launch iteratively over time. The details of each specific trial will be described in a trial-specific appendix to the master protocol. Participants will be enrolled in trials within the PACT-ARROW platform at clinical sites in Ukraine. Neither patients nor the public were involved in the design or review of this study.

General eligibility criteria in the PACT-ARROW protocol and trial documents include adults (18 y of age or older) admitted to an enrolling hospital or other health facility with at least one acute traumatic wound (Table 1). Children may be eligible for specific trials with additional justification. For a patient to enroll in a trial, that patient must meet both the platform and trial-specific eligibility criteria. Each trial in the ARROW platform will define additional eligibility criteria in a trial-specific appendix.

Enrollment

Study personnel at each enrolling site will confirm eligibility and obtain consent prior to randomization or defer consent based on patient and intervention acuity, with randomization signifying trial entry. Overall recruitment efforts will depend on the intervention under study. When multiple trials are open at once, participants may become eligible for more than one trial at either the same time or at different times throughout their hospitalization. Whether and how randomization and co-enrollment may proceed in each of these settings will be trial specific. Considerations will include whether agents are prelicensure or postlicensure, the safety profile of the intervention, and potential interactions between interventions. For participants who co-enroll across multiple trials, the randomization will be independent of each trial.

When only a single trial is enrolling (or a participant is eligible for a single trial only), patients will be allocated to the intervention+SOC versus SOC alone in that trial unless specified differently in a trial-specific appendix. We envision three potential approaches for participants who may be eligible for multiple trials at one time: factorial randomization (co-enrollment across trials), shared control/placebo, or separate trials (no co-enrollment). The aforementioned will be decided based on each trial, relevant to trial type, as certain trial designs lend themselves more naturally to one of these approaches to handling eligibility for multiple trials simultaneously.

This master protocol will also permit study of strategies of treatments given over time (ie, dynamic treatment regimens or adaptive intervention strategies) through “strategy trials” or sequential multiple assignment randomized trials (SMART). In these trials, at least some participants will undergo randomization across at least two different timepoints as their clinical condition changes. A SMART design would be considered a single trial in the platform even though it may involve multiple randomizations.

Interventions

Investigational agents and other interventions that will be studied in PACT-ARROW are grouped into broad therapeutic classes, by mechanism of action, or according to groups of patients with shared clinical features. In general, the PACT-ARROW platform will consider six trial types (Table 2).

At the onset of the PACT-ARROW platform, the following classes will be considered: preventive measures, topical therapies, antimicrobial therapies, surgical interventions, and therapies for critical illness. Additional therapeutic classes may be considered on an individual case basis. Agents entering PACT-ARROW for evaluation may span from limited (only Phase 1), to modest (completed Phase 2), moderate (completed Phase 3 but not yet licensed), or expansive (licensed agents for this or other indications).

Outcomes

Primary Outcomes

Because this protocol has the potential to include a spectrum of traumatic wounds by mechanism, injury type, and various stages of healing, alongside a broad scope of interventions, a flexible approach to the selection of several discrete primary outcomes for each trial is provided. Examples of primary outcomes meeting these criteria include all-cause mortality, measures of recovery (eg, improved wound healing and hospital discharge), and measures of wound progression (eg, clinical signs of infection, bacterial culture and colonization, and major complications). An individual trial will select one of the aforementioned outcomes as primary based on trial-specific considerations such as the pathogenesis of the infection, severity of illness, and mechanism of the intervention.

The follow-up duration during which these outcomes are ascertained will typically be through hospital discharge or to day 28. The decision to limit to 28 days in-hospital aims to optimize the potential to rapidly identify clinically important differences that are related to the acute presentation and potentially modifiable for the intervention being evaluated, while balancing timeliness and burden of data collection requirements in the country during war.

Secondary Outcome(s)

The main secondary outcomes for the ARROW platform are a subset of the potential primary outcomes. In addition, organ failure and secondary infections will also be important to understand the full range of physiological recovery. Secondary efficacy and or effectiveness outcomes may include patient disposition; wound improvement through standard surveys or wound photography; number and type of procedural and surgical interventions; additional infections or co-infections; wound bacteria, antimicrobial sensitivities and new or reinfection of the wound; organ failure and need for organ support; and patient-reported outcomes.

Randomization

Randomization will usually be stratified by study site, with additional consideration for wound severity at entry unless stated otherwise in the trial-specific appendix. Severity will be determined by the wound, based in part on size, duration of wound, presence of bacterial infection by wound culture, nonhealing, and presence of antibiotic resistance. Within each stratum, mass-weighted urn randomization will be used to generate the active and placebo assignments. This will ensure that the desired treatment allocation ratio is maintained throughout the trial. The approach to blinding will be made based on the nature of the intervention and the feasibility of blinding on an individual trial basis.

Data Collection

Data will be collected by research and clinical personnel in Ukraine at enrolling sites, through a combination of data extraction from medical charts, photographs, and biospecimen collection. Medical charts and data collection forms will be securely extracted, with data uploaded to REDCap. REDCap is a secure and encrypted web application designed to collect and manage data capture for research studies.18 Data quality will be supervised by a Ukrainian coordinating center, independently monitored by an in-country contract research organization (CRO) as needed and managed by the project coordinating center.

Data Safety

Participants may be asked to provide personal health information (PHI). All attempts will be made to keep PHI confidential within the limits of the law. To minimize risk, data will be stored in a secure, encrypted and password-protected database (REDCap) with each participant having a unique identifier. REDCap access is strictly limited to investigators and research staff directly involved in the project, where access to patient identifiers is restricted to the local site level, identifiers necessary for analysis are masked when possible, and identifiers not necessary for analysis are not exported. If data need to be transferred outside of REDCap, we will use institutionally approved methods for file transfer and deidentify data before transfer.

PHI will be entered and stored at the local site and encoded via an identification number. This number will be linked to a participant through a code key, which will be stored by the Ukrainian local enrolling site. Names will not be released from the local site. All electronic files will be password protected.

Statistical Methods

A detailed a priori statistical analysis plan (SAP) will be created for each trial. The protocol and relevant SAPs will be available publicly. The full master protocol includes suggested analysis considerations for each outcome, including a proposed regression analysis framework (eg, Cox-PH models, logistic or linear regression, or a mixed-effects model) and other key considerations. For example, for the length of stay, a discussion of ordinal scale approaches is outlined in the master protocol. Other analysis suggestions for specific components to be specified for each outcome include accounting for site using random effects, adjustment for baseline, and a priori specification of confounders and precision variables. Model selection will be discouraged when possible. Two-sided tests will be used with p values of 0.05 used to determine significance unless otherwise specified in the protocol or adjustments for multiple comparisons are needed based on the specification of two or more primary outcomes or extensive model selection employed. Example statistical plans for two primary outcomes are provided in the Supplemental Digital Content (SDC 2, http://links.lww.com/TA/F349, Example Statistical Plan for Primary Outcomes).

Secondary Outcomes, Safety Outcomes, and Subgroups

Secondary efficacy and effectiveness outcomes will be analyzed similarly to the primary outcomes with a priori SAPs. Safety outcomes will be assessed for subgroups defined by baseline characteristics, including demographics, social determinants, clinical history, and wound severity. Tests evaluating the effect heterogeneity across subgroups will be carried out. Subgroup analyses will be interpreted with caution due to limited power and uncontrolled Type I error. Unless specified otherwise, corrections for multiple testing will not be applied for secondary outcomes.

Trial Steering Committee

A platform steering committee, consisting of subject matter experts, Ukrainian leaders, global health experts, and potential sponsors, will review potential interventions and determine whether they are aligned with the mission of the trial and have sufficient evidence to warrant testing in a trial. Where appropriate, we aim to include relevant patient or public involvement in the design, conduct, and reporting of the trial, generally as part of the committee structure. Studies will be evaluated by criteria defined by the steering committee and will include a review of data and factors related to intervention safety, supply for completing the trial within the platform, cost, manufacturing, and scalability for implementation within Ukraine and relevance to future military or war environment applications. The overall aim is to improve the overall benefit from the intervention, with minimal potential or theoretical harm.

Data Monitoring Committee

Where appropriate, an independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) will review all trials conducted as part of the PACT-ARROW platform, with DSMB meeting frequency determined by each individual trial. Full DSMB reviews will include a review of general trial conduct as well as extensive data summaries of the primary outcome and other major clinical outcomes.

In addition to the full reviews, interim safety reviews by the DSMB may be scheduled more frequently. For investigational agents with limited safety data, an early safety review with 20 to 30 participants will be included, followed by frequent interim safety reviews. In the setting of specific therapies, follow-up may need to extend beyond the course of the master protocol, designated 28 days of hospitalization and enrollment to ensure safety. Safety data will be reviewed by a designated DSMB in collaboration with an in-country CRO. Both the CRO and DSMB will receive weekly safety reports and can choose to convene additional meetings if concerning safety signals emerge. If a modification of the monitoring plan is needed for an individual trial, it will be prespecified in the trial-specific protocol appendix and SAP.

Adverse Event Reporting

Adverse events that are serious and potentially related to the intervention or unexpected will be reported. Reporting for other adverse events will be based on the nature of the individual trials. The specific set of adverse and safety collection protocols (SDC 3, Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/TA/F350, list of adverse and safety collection variables) includes a number of possible reactions, measured at any point during the 28 days of enrollment. Adverse events, serious adverse events, and unexpected outcomes among hospitalized patients will be assessed continuously.

PACT-ARROW trials will focus on reporting events that are serious and are judged to be related to the trial intervention or unexpected. In addition, trials will report allergic reactions deemed to be potentially related to the study intervention. In a population hospitalized with acute illness, nonserious adverse events are anticipated to be extremely common and therefore will generally not be reported unless required by regulatory authorities. Post-trial care and compensation for ongoing care in the case of an adverse event will be determined by each specific trial.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying the intervention will be specified in each specific trial. Modifications to the trial will be communicated to all relevant parties, including the CRO, in-country coordinating partners, and site-PIs, via verbal and written means.

Ethics and Dissemination

To ensure patient safety, all studies will undergo appropriate regulatory review in Ukraine and the United States, where appropriate. In Ukraine, each participating site will be associated with a Local Ethics Committee (LEC), which will conduct the initial level of ethical review. Following approval from all required LECs, the study will be submitted to the relevant national regulatory body overseeing medical devices or investigational drugs. Upon receiving approval from the Ukrainian ethics and regulatory authorities, the study will then be submitted to the appropriate institutional review boards (IRBs) and oversight committees in the United States.

RESULTS

To date, the PACT-ARROW protocol has undergone ethical review and approval by Ukraine national and LEC review. Additional review was provided by our university IRB. We initiated PACT-ARROW with a prospective observational cohort to include up to 1000. Currently, we have enrolled over 350 patients across 5 active enrolling sites in the observational trial, called Antimicrobial Resistance Research to Improve Outcomes of Traumatic Wounds (ARROW). The observational trial follows patients in the same format as the PACT-ARROW protocol, with the use of similar corresponding outcomes. Distinctly, the ARROW protocol serves as the underlying framework for future trials and does not test a single intervention. The ARROW trial was also similarly reviewed and approved by the Ukraine and US ethics committees.

A corresponding interventional trial (Healing Electroceutical Dressing for the recovery of Open Wounds) with a planned enrollment of 150 was also submitted as an Appendix to the PACT-ARROW protocol and has since been approved in Ukraine at national and local levels. It was similarly submitted to the our local IRB and approved. Currently, this study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT07329114 and has begun enrollment at a single site in Ukraine. While active recruitment is ongoing at one site, there is planned enrollment at three additional sites in the future. All three sites have undergone local ethics review, with two having been approved. All three will subsequently be submitted to national regulators in Ukraine and the United States for final approval. All future clinical trials will also be registered on clinicaltrials.gov.

Similarly, we intend to make all results of studies, regardless of the outcome, public. This may occur in the form of standard published manuscripts, but where negative studies or dissemination of findings may aim to change clinical practice or lead to the discontinuation of studies, we will make public these data through published pre-prints. Also, in line with most journal practices, we will make deidentified participant data (including data dictionary), statistical code, and any other materials publicly available for secondary use and analysis. Lastly, our hope is that certain findings may lead to a change in clinical practice. In such cases, we will make public these changes as they relate to known clinical practice guidelines.

DISCUSSION

The PACT-ARROW protocol design will be an important step in improving the care of patients with acute traumatic wounds during war. We intend that PACT-ARROW will serve to accelerate the safe and efficacious study of novel interventions for the care of acute wounds, while at the same time serving as a model for clinical trial design in war and other austere environments.

There are several methodologically important attributes of this platform. The PACT-ARROW protocol attempts to standardize approaches to the study of interventions of acute wound care. The PACT-ARROW protocol provides a preset number of primary outcomes. The available literature on the care of acute traumatic wounds is robust, with many important findings.19,20 However, comparison of study effectiveness has been limited given the broad and variable number of primary outcomes reported.21

The PACT-ARROW protocol is one of the first, if not only, to utilize a MAMS design in the setting of a country with an ongoing active war and one of the first to apply this methodology to trials of acute traumatic injuries. This protocol builds on concepts set forth during the COVID-19 pandemic, utilizing a MAMS design.14 Those trials, implemented during a pandemic, set the stage for how to design and concentrate studies during unique times and challenging settings, when rapid understanding of safety and efficacy outcomes was necessary for the public health emergency.

A key reason for the PACT-ARROW protocol was the recognition that acute traumatic wound care is unique, difficult to study, and may benefit from thinking about care as a package of modalities across a continuum of care. We believe that such a platform will allow for a broad approach to studying the efficacy of a broad spectrum of interventions across multiple timepoints in a patient’s hospitalized care. While specific to wound care interventions, we believe this protocol can also serve as a model for other clinical trials in wars, conflicts or other humanitarian emergencies.

Currently, PACT-ARROW and the trials described here are funded federally. Future work is, of course, predicated on additional support. While federal funding is one approach, we will also consider funded projects in collaboration with industry partners, including but not limited to investigator-initiated work. In all cases, we will rely on the previously described platform steering committee, consisting of subject matter experts, Ukrainian leaders, global health experts, and potential sponsors, to review potential interventions, determine whether they are aligned with the mission of the trial, and determine whether there is any initial inherent bias that may make studying the intervention considered challenging.

Challenges and Limitations

While several challenges have been overcome in the development of this protocol, we call out several specifically to help those who might also be trying to develop studies in similar contexts.

Detailed and complete data, with limited missing data, are essential while at the same time a challenge in any clinical study, but magnified during active conflict in Ukraine. The collection of clinical data as well as biological samples (if necessary) is essential to monitor safety assessments. An initial reason to begin with an (eg, ARROW) study was to ascertain what specific areas of data collection would be feasible. Of the over 350 patients already enrolled in ARROW, data have been overwhelmingly complete, with almost no loss to follow-up before discharge or study completion. While the war, with specific attacks on energy and power, makes continued research and data collection challenging, there is little evidence thus far to suggest a change or worsening of consistency.

Second, our intent is to never have research overwhelm the needs of clinical care and operations, especially in a war setting. We anticipate patient enrollment will vary across sites depending on local and national conditions and threats of conflict. To reduce the burden on staff, all sites may not participate in individual clinical trials. We worked to reduce data collection to a minimum necessary and have specifically limited the study duration to 28 days and in-hospital follow-up. While we recognize this may restrict some study designs, we believe that this will result in more successful and complete patient follow-up and a decreased risk of loss to follow-up and data missingness.

The logistics of shipping sample tests and potential interventions into and out of the country have also been challenging. After contracting with multiple different carriers, we chose a national (Ukraine) company to ship samples in-country, and work with an in-country CRO to broker and support international shipping.

Lastly, Ukraine's ethics and regulatory agencies are unique. All clinical trials require ethical oversight at both the local (hospital) and national levels. Depending on the intervention and level of risk, certain studies may require a longer period of ethics approval. To speed the overall process, we employed a number of strategies. First, we relied on study partners and a national coordinating center to help guide our work. Second, we partnered with a local CRO to provide in-country guidance and regulatory submissions for some studies.

CONCLUSIONS

The PACT-ARROW protocol is an important step in improving data collection to evaluate the efficacy and safety of multiple interventions for the treatment of complex and infected traumatic wounds.

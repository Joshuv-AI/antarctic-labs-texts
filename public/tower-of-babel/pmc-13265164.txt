A bibliometric analysis of global research in cervical cancer radiotherapy (2015–2024): trends, hotspots, and future directions
Authors: Yue Hou, Yu Gao, Wen-Ming Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite the growing volume of cervical cancer (CC) research, few bibliometric studies have specifically focused on the recent landscape of radiotherapy—a critical treatment modality. This study aims to examine the global research landscape, thematic focus areas, and emerging trends in CC radiotherapy from 2015 to 2024 through a bibliometric and visual analysis.MethodsRelevant literature published from January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2024, was retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Eligible publications were subjected to bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Bibliometrix R software.ResultsA total of 1,496 publications met the inclusion criteria. The annual publication output demonstrated a general trend of fluctuating growth, with a slight decline in recent years. The highest contributions originated from China, the United States, and Japan. Leading institutions in terms of publication volume included the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, and Tata Memorial Hospital, India. Gynecologic Oncology was identified as the most productive journal. Among individual contributors, Mahantshetty Umesh was associated with the highest number of publications and total citations. Co-citation analysis, keyword co-occurrence, and thematic clustering highlighted major research focuses, including advancements in radiotherapy technology, mechanisms of radioresistance, neoadjuvant chemotherapy, immunotherapy combinations, patient quality of life, socioeconomic disparities, and treatment accessibility.ConclusionsSignificant advancements have been made in CC radiotherapy research over the past decade. Current and emerging research priorities include enhancing precision in radiotherapy, individualizing treatment strategies, elucidating mechanisms of radio resistance in CC cells, integrating chemoradiotherapy with immunotherapy, and evaluating radiotherapy-related quality of life outcomes.

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) remains the fourth most common malignancy among women worldwide and continues to pose a significant global health burden. Despite the widespread implementation of human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination and screening programs, both incidence and mortality rates remain high, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (1). Radiotherapy plays a central role in the multidisciplinary management of CC across different disease stages. For early-stage patients, surgery is the primary treatment, while adjuvant radiotherapy is recommended for those with high-risk factors. In contrast, for locally advanced CC, concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) combined with brachytherapy remains the standard of care (2). In recent years, rapid technological advancements—such as intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT), image-guided radiotherapy (IGRT), and adaptive radiotherapy—have significantly improved treatment precision, reduced toxicity, and enhanced patient outcomes. Moreover, emerging therapeutic strategies integrating radiotherapy with immunotherapy and targeted therapies have further expanded treatment possibilities and become an important research focus in this field (3-5). Notably, the past decade (2015–2024) represents a critical period of transformation in CC management. During this time, precision radiotherapy techniques have gradually replaced conventional approaches and become widely adopted in clinical practice. Meanwhile, novel treatment paradigms, including immunotherapy-based combination strategies and individualized treatment approaches, have rapidly evolved and reshaped clinical decision-making (3-5). Therefore, focusing on this specific time frame enables a comprehensive and up-to-date evaluation of recent research progress, emerging hotspots, and evolving trends in CC radiotherapy.

Although the volume of research on CC has increased substantially in recent years, most existing studies have focused on clinical outcomes or specific treatment modalities, with limited attention to the overall research landscape. Furthermore, previous bibliometric analyses have generally examined CC as a whole or covered extended time spans, which may obscure recent developments and fail to capture rapidly evolving trends. To date, few studies have specifically focused on radiotherapy within a clearly defined and recent time frame. Bibliometric analysis, as a method integrating quantitative and qualitative approaches, enables systematic evaluation of publication outputs, collaboration networks, keyword co-occurrence, and citation relationships. Compared with traditional narrative reviews, it provides a more comprehensive and intuitive understanding of research structures, hotspots, and developmental trajectories.

Therefore, the present study aims to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric and visualized analysis of CC radiotherapy research from 2015 to 2024 using the Web of Science Core Collection. By incorporating both clinical studies and review articles, this study systematically analyzes publication trends, collaborative networks, research hotspots, and emerging frontiers. This targeted and time-restricted approach offers updated insights into the evolving research landscape and may help guide future research priorities and clinical strategies in CC radiotherapy. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0252/rc).

Methods

Data sources

Relevant literature on CC radiotherapy published between January 1, 2015, and December 31, 2024, was retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection through the application of advanced search strategies. Subject terms related to CC radiotherapy were identified using Medical Subject Headings in PubMed and subsequently integrated into the Web of Science search query. The search formula used was as follows: (TI = “Cancer of Cervix” OR “Cancer of the Cervix” OR “Cancer of the Uterine Cervix” OR “Cervical Cancer” OR “Cervical Neoplasms” OR “Cervix Cancer” OR “Cervix Neoplasms” OR “Neoplasms, Cervical” OR “Neoplasms, Cervix” OR “Uterine Cervical Cancer”) AND (TS = “Radiation Therapy” OR “Radiation Therapy, Targeted” OR “Radiation Treatment” OR “Radiotherapy, Targeted” OR “Targeted Radiation Therapy” OR “Targeted Radiotherapy”). The search was limited to publications in English and restricted to the document types “article” and “review article”. Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (I) focused on CC and radiotherapy (including external beam radiotherapy and/or brachytherapy); (II) were clinical studies or review articles; (III) involved radiotherapy as a primary or essential component of treatment. Studies involving various radiotherapy fractionation regimens were included without restriction. However, no further classification based on fractionation strategies was performed, as this study aimed to analyze overall research trends rather than focusing on specific treatment modalities. Studies were excluded if they: (I) did not involve radiotherapy; (II) focused solely on surgery, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy without radiotherapy; (III) were conference abstracts, letters, editorials, or non-research articles; (IV) were duplicates or irrelevant after screening. After excluding studies deemed irrelevant to the research topic, a total of 1,496 publications related to CC radiotherapy from 2015 to 2024 were included in this study, comprising 1,326 original research articles (88.6%) and 170 review articles (11.4%). Original articles accounted for the vast majority of publications, indicating that research in this field is predominantly driven by primary clinical and experimental studies. In contrast, review articles represented a smaller proportion, suggesting that while the research landscape is largely shaped by original investigations, review articles provide complementary insights through the synthesis and integration of existing knowledge.

Study methods

Full records and cited references of the included publications were exported in plain text format. Bibliometric analysis was performed using CiteSpace 6.1.R on the final set of 1,496 documents (6). Visualization of publication year, country, institution, author, journal, citation patterns, and keyword distribution, as well as the identification of research hotspots and developmental trends in CC radiotherapy from 2015 to 2024, was carried out using CiteSpace, Bibliometrix R, and VOSviewer 1.6.20 (7,8).

Results

Annual publication trend

As shown in Figure 1, the annual publication volume exhibited an overall trend of fluctuating growth, followed by a modest decline in recent years. Between 2015 and 2019, the number of publications increased from 122 to 164, reflecting a 34.4% rise and an average annual growth rate of 6.2%. A temporary dip was observed in 2020, with output dropping to 149, but this was followed by a rebound to 159 in 2021 and a further rise to 181 in 2022, with no obvious decline during the COVID-19 pandemic period. However, in 2023–2024, the output declined to approximately 140 publications, representing a decrease of approximately 23% from the peak.

Country distribution

Analysis conducted using Bibliometrix identified contributions from 57 countries to the field of CC radiotherapy research between 2015 and 2024. China and the United States ranked first and second, respectively, in terms of publication volume, accounting for 28.5% and 19.1% of the total output, together comprising nearly half of the global literature in this field (Table 1). Japan, South Korea, and France followed in publication count. When evaluated by average citations per article, India, the United States, and France ranked highest. The highest rates of international collaboration, as indicated by cross-national co-authorship, were observed in Canada, France, and India.

Author collaboration analysis

A total of 8,029 authors were identified as contributors to the CC radiotherapy literature through VOSviewer analysis. The author collaboration network, as presented in Figure 4, was generated using a minimum publication threshold of five, resulting in 233 nodes and 4,722 links. Distinct clusters were represented by different colors. Node size reflected the number of publications per author, and connections between nodes indicated co-authorship relationships. Following the exclusion of homonymous authors affiliated with different institutions, the ten most prolific contributors in terms of publication volume and total citation frequency were listed in Table 4. Mahantshetty Umesh, affiliated with Tata Memorial Hospital, India, ranked first with 34 publications and the highest cumulative citation count, occupying a central position within the academic collaboration network. Authors with substantial publication output typically appeared as central or larger nodes, and their co-authorship patterns were strongly associated with institutional and national affiliations. In addition, high levels of inter-cluster connectivity indicated an ongoing collaboration both within national teams and across international, multicenter environments. These authors served as key integrative nodes (bridges or links) within the collaborative network. For early-career researchers or emerging research teams seeking to enhance academic visibility, participation in international collaborations, resource sharing, and co-authored publications with scholars from diverse geographic regions may represent a strategically advantageous approach.

Discussion

Bibliometric information analysis

An analysis of annual publication output on CC radiotherapy from 2015 to 2024 revealed an average annual growth rate of 1.54%, indicating sustained research activity in this field. The temporal distribution exhibited notable fluctuations and cyclical patterns, potentially reflecting iterative phases of transition from basic research to clinical application, followed by periods of technological refinement. The recent decline in publication volume over the past two years may partly result from delays in database indexing or may represent a temporary downturn, with the potential for future recovery. It is noteworthy that publication output in CC radiotherapy remained stable and even increased during the COVID-19 pandemic period, despite substantial disruptions to screening, vaccination, and treatment services (23). This phenomenon may be explained by the continued use of pre-existing datasets, retrospective analyses, and ongoing multicenter collaborations, which were less dependent on new patient recruitment. In addition, shifts in research workflows during the pandemic, including increased time for data analysis and manuscript preparation, contributed to sustained or even increased submission rates (24). Furthermore, remote communication and digital collaboration supported continued scientific productivity across institutions (25).

In terms of national contributions, China, the United States, Japan, South Korea, and France collectively accounted for approximately 68% of total publications, indicative of a high level of concentration and influence in this research area. Publications originating from India, the United States, and France exhibited relatively high citation impact, serving as valuable references within the literature. A considerable proportion of internationally collaborative publications was identified in Canada, France, and India, which may indicate either active participation in global research networks or substantial reliance on cross-border partnerships.

Most publishing institutions were large, comprehensive cancer centers, universities with affiliated hospitals, and specialized medical research organizations. The top ten institutions in terms of publication volume were predominantly based in Asia, Europe, and North America, with particularly strong collaborative relationships observed among the academically influential institutions. With regard to journals, those with high publication output such as Gynecologic Oncology and the International Journal of Gynecological Cancer also demonstrated high citation frequencies, indicating widespread recognition and academic influence. Their prominence may be attributed to their strong disciplinary focus on gynecologic malignancies and their roles as official journals of major professional societies, which enhances their alignment with clinical and translational research (26,27). In addition, factors such as editorial scope, accessibility, and international visibility may further contribute to their high publication output and citation impact (28). The top ten journals by publication volume and citation frequency constituted core dissemination platforms in CC radiotherapy research and may serve as strategic venues for researchers seeking to publish work of clinical and academic relevance. Prioritizing engagement with these journals may further support progress in both scientific investigation and clinical translation.

With respect to authorship, Mahantshetty Umesh was identified as the most prolific contributor, with the highest number of publications and citations in this domain. Recent research efforts have focused on individuals with locally advanced CC undergoing chemoradiotherapy in combination with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-guided brachytherapy. These studies include assessments of vaginal lesion risks associated with vaginal dilation, as well as evaluations of the prognostic significance of biomarkers when integrated with clinicopathological variables (29,30). The second most cited author, Tanderup Kari, has recently contributed to the validation of a universally applicable model based on CC radiotherapy plans, with the aim of ensuring global generalizability (31). A high number of collaborative links and total link strength observed among authors with extensive publication and citation records reflects the presence of robust academic networks and underscores the role of sustained collaboration as a primary driver of progress in CC radiotherapy research.

Research hotspots and frontier analysis

The dynamic evolution of research themes within the CC field over the past decade has been elucidated through methodologies such as analysis of highly cited literature, reference co-citation clustering, identification of high-frequency keywords, and keyword co-occurrence with cluster analysis. Overall, research has shifted toward more precise and individualized treatment, with increasing attention to intelligent planning, immunotherapy, prognosis, and socioeconomic determinants. These influential studies reflect evolving research priorities and emerging directions in CC radiotherapy. CCRT for CC generally involves a combination of external beam radiotherapy, cisplatin-based chemotherapy, and brachytherapy. Advances in external beam radiotherapy, particularly IMRT and volumetric-modulated arc therapy, have improved treatment precision and safety, which is consistent with their prominence in the bibliometric analysis (32,33). These modalities have been widely adopted as standard techniques in CC radiotherapy. Technological advances in MRI and cone beam computed tomography (CT) have further supported the development of IGRT approaches. These developments collectively contribute to improved treatment accuracy and reproducibility (34). Together, these developments have made radiotherapy more conformal, reproducible, and safer.

The rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies has increasingly supported automation and optimization in radiotherapy workflows (35). AI may improve efficiency and standardization, although its implementation remains dependent on infrastructure, expertise, and quality assurance. Along with photon-based radiotherapy, emerging technologies such as carbon ion therapy and proton therapy represent important directions for future research (36,37). However, adoption of these newer procedures remains uneven across regions because of differences in cost, equipment availability, and technical capacity.

Systemic chemotherapy remains the primary modality for managing distant metastases, persistent disease, or recurrent CC following CCRT. Recent studies have also explored the role of radiotherapy in recurrent and oligometastatic settings, reflecting expanding therapeutic applications (38,39). In the standard treatment protocol, patients with CC typically receive brachytherapy following external beam radiotherapy or midway through the treatment course. Brachytherapy remains a critical component of curative treatment due to its ability to deliver high tumor doses while sparing surrounding tissues (40). Technological advancements in high-resolution ultrasound, CT, and MRI, combined with the image-guided brachytherapy approaches have improved treatment planning and delivery (41,42).

The RetroEMBRACE and EMBRACE-I studies have provided important evidence supporting image-guided brachytherapy in improving clinical outcomes (13,43). Recent applications of AI technologies in brachytherapy have also been reported (44). The integration of deep learning with three-dimensional brachytherapy holds potential for the automation of treatment planning. Anticipated clinical applications of AI include automated segmentation of target volumes and organs at risk, applicator reconstruction and positioning, treatment plan optimization, and dose calculation (45). By facilitating full automation and standardization, AI-driven workflows may streamline CC brachytherapy, reduce preparation time, alleviate patient discomfort, and improve treatment accuracy and efficiency. However, these benefits remain dependent on equipment, trained personnel, and institutional experience.

Beyond technological advancements, recent research in CC radiotherapy has increasingly focused on tumor radioresistance, a major contributor to treatment failure, locoregional recurrence, and distant metastasis (46). Understanding radioresistance mechanisms is critical for improving treatment outcomes and enabling more individualized therapeutic strategies (47). The identification of reliable clinical, morphological, and molecular predictors of treatment efficacy during both the initial therapeutic phase and subsequent follow-up remains an essential objective. However, the complex and multifactorial nature of radiotherapy resistance in CC, involving diverse genes, molecular pathways, and regulatory mechanisms, has not yet been fully elucidated (48). The development of effective radiosensitizers continues to represent a critical area of investigation. This line of research may help identify high-risk patients and support more individualized treatment strategies.

Efforts to expand therapeutic protocols, such as the application of radical radiotherapy following neoadjuvant chemotherapy or the use of adjuvant therapy after chemoradiotherapy, have not consistently demonstrated clinical benefit (49,50). The INTERLACE trial, however, showed significant survival benefits with induction chemotherapy plus chemoradiotherapy (51), supporting neoadjuvant chemotherapy as a potential new standard with radical radiotherapy. Despite concerns regarding cohort representativeness and radiotherapy techniques (52), this strategy has attracted growing attention as an emerging research focus, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, where delays in radiotherapy and limited infrastructure are common, and neoadjuvant chemotherapy may help maintain treatment continuity.

With the advancement of immunotherapy, various immune checkpoint inhibitors have become established components in the treatment of multiple tumor types. In CC, emerging evidence has indicated a synergistic interaction between CCRT and immunotherapy (53). This combined therapeutic strategy has recently emerged as an important research hotspot, with growing interest in its potential to improve clinical outcomes, particularly in patients with locally advanced disease and in those who may exhibit resistance to radiotherapy.

Advances in metabolic imaging, radiomics, and biomarker-based prediction models may further improve risk stratification and guide multidisciplinary treatment selection, supporting a transition from uniform treatment toward more personalized decision-making. At the same time, increasing attention has been paid to post-treatment quality of life, symptom burden, toxicity management, and survivorship (54,55), indicating that the clinical impact of CC radiotherapy research now extends beyond tumor control to encompass patient-centered outcomes.

Socioeconomic factors and treatment accessibility have also emerged as important considerations. Earlier studies focused mainly on social determinants such as racial disparities (56), while more recent research has shifted toward treatment delays, resource inequities, and financial burden, which are key drivers of disparities in the adoption of advanced therapies (57). The adoption of advanced radiotherapy technologies varies significantly across regions and largely reflects the geographical distribution of research output observed in this study. The predominance of publications from Asia, Europe, and North America likely reflects the concentration of radiotherapy infrastructure, technological capacity, and specialized expertise in these regions. In contrast, low- and middle-income countries, which bear a disproportionate burden of CC, often face substantial limitations in radiotherapy equipment, workforce capacity, and access to modern treatment technologies (57,58).

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the use of a single database and language restrictions may have introduced selection bias and limited the coverage of relevant publications, particularly from non-English or regional sources. Second, the results may be influenced by the search strategy and selected keywords, which might not capture all relevant studies. Third, recently published articles may be underrepresented due to citation lag, and citation counts may not fully reflect study quality, as they can be affected by publication type and academic visibility. In addition, the observed geographical imbalance in research output may reflect disparities in radiotherapy infrastructure and research capacity across regions. Finally, the results of bibliometric analyses may vary depending on the parameters and algorithms used, and the interpretation of research hotspots may involve a degree of subjectivity.

Outlook on future research trends

CC continues to exhibit a high global incidence, particularly in developing regions, where it remains a substantial public health concern. Advancements in precision radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy are expected to promote international collaboration. Future directions are likely to involve multinational clinical trials and interdisciplinary integration encompassing radiotherapy, surgery, internal medicine, molecular biology, and AI, all of which are anticipated to contribute to ongoing innovation in the field.

The expanding application of AI technologies is projected to maintain the prominence of research focused on precision radiotherapy and individualized treatment strategies. In particular, AI has demonstrated significant potential in automated target delineation, dose prediction, and adaptive radiotherapy, which may improve treatment efficiency, consistency, and personalization (59). However, AI should not be considered a standalone solution, as its clinical implementation depends on data quality, infrastructure, and rigorous validation. The rapid development of emerging research institutions particularly cancer centers in China, Central and Eastern Europe, and Latin America warrants continued attention. Collaborative efforts between these institutions and established global centers are expected to enhance the heterogeneity of patient populations and broaden the scope of clinical expertise.

Continuous monitoring of the scientific literature and research developments will be essential for tracking progress and guiding future advancements in CC radiotherapy.

Conclusions

Over the past decade, substantial progress has been made in CC radiotherapy research, particularly in the areas of precision radiotherapy, individualized treatment strategies, and the integration of new therapeutic modalities such as immunotherapy. However, challenges remain in overcoming radioresistance, improving patient outcomes, and addressing the inequities in treatment access that exist across different regions. Continued collaboration, technological innovation, and a focus on the socioeconomic factors influencing treatment access will be crucial in driving the next phase of progress in CC radiotherapy research.

Two-photon coupling via Josephson element I: Breaking the symmetry with magnetic fields
E. V. Stolyarov, V. L. Andriichuk, A. M. Sokolov

Abstract
We consider a coupling element based on a symmetric superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) and show that it mediates a two-photon interaction. This and other inductive interactions due to the SQUID can be switched off in situ . We derive the system Hamiltonian for coupled resonator and rf SQUID. The rf SQUID dwells in the vicinity of its metastable well holding a number of energy states and acts as an artificial atom. We discuss how the Josephson symmetry breaks owing to magnetic fields in the superconducting loops. We assess that the two-photon coupling strength reaches 18 MHz which can exceed the single-photon capacitive interaction in the coupler.



I
Introduction

A nonlinearity cubic in the system variables is the first one that can appear in the coupling energy.
In the equations of motion, it spawns a quadratic interaction term;
typically, only the linear interaction exceeds this coupling strength.
Naturally, this nonlinearity is widely used in applications, such as lasing
Louisell 1964
, radio-frequency homo- and heterodyne detection
Blais et al. 2021
, optomechanical cooling and amplification
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
, parametric amplification
Louisell 1964
;
Esposito et al. 2021
;
Anisimov 2003
and frequency conversion
Louisell 1964

For interacting radiation mode and matter particle, this nonlinearity can give rise to two-photon processes—when the coupling energy is quadratic in the mode variables.
In that case, two photons excite the particle one step up in its energy levels, while a step down releases two photons back into the radiation mode.
Normally, this nonlinearity is small compared to the linear coupling and such processes only become visible for higher field strengths
Nakamura et al. 2001
;
Lisenfeld 2007
;
Välimaa et al. 2022
.

Classical Hamiltonian

The first term in Eq. (

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{r}}=4E^{\mathrm{r}}_{C}n^{2}_{\mathrm{r}}+U_{\mathrm{r}},\quad U_{\mathrm{r}}=\frac{E^{\mathrm{r}}_{L}}{2}\phi_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}.

The last term in Eq. (

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{c}}=E^{\mathrm{c}}_{C}n_{\mathrm{a}}n_{\mathrm{r}}+U_{\mathrm{c}}.

(6)

(7)

The argument in the Josephson energy term Eq. (
8

Its form is crucial for the two-photon interaction to arise.
It follows from the flux quantization rule

;
Orlando et al. 1999

The rule states that the sum of the phase drops across the elements in the loop equals its external magnetic flux in the relevant units.
That can be shown

;
Berman et al. 2009
;
Barone and Paterno 1982

At the same time, we neglect the contribution in small junction gaps as in Refs.
Feynman et al. 1965
;
alternatively, one can use the gauge-invariant phase differences
Barone and Paterno 1982
;
Tinkham 2004
.

It is important that a current-free path exists in the loop, which may be not always the case for thin films.
For superconducting quantum circuits, aluminum or niobium thin films are typically used.
In them, such a path for sure exists if the film is at least 150 nm thick
López-Núñez et al. 2023
;
Gubin et al. 2005
.
For thinner films, a term in the free energy appears

;
Tinkham 2004
;
Barone and Paterno 1982

II.2
Coupler bias and equilibrium phases

The coupler bias
\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}
appears directly in its Josephson energy in Eq. (
8
).
Essentially, the dc screening current invokes this phase according to the first Josephson relation—as written out in what follows.
This symmetry-breaking mechanism is related to some types of the superconducting diode effect
Hou et al. 2023
.

In other choices of the phase variables,
\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}
may be absent in the coupler Josephson energy, at least explicitly.
For example, the phase variables may be chosen so that the phase drop is
\phi_{\mathrm{r}}-\phi_{\mathrm{a}}+\pi\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}/2\Phi_{0}
across the top junction in the coupling SQUID (see Fig.
2
) and
\phi_{\mathrm{r}}-\phi_{\mathrm{a}}-\pi\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}/2\Phi_{0}
across the bottom one.
According to the reasoning as in the
Appendix
, the argument in the Josephson energy in Eq.
8
is simply
\phi_{\mathrm{r}}-\phi_{\mathrm{a}}
.
The phase drop across the atom junction is
\phi_{\mathrm{a}}+\pi\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}/2\Phi_{0}
owing to the flux quantization condition.
In that case the coupler bias shifts the atom equilibrium phase;
it appears in the coupling Josephson energy indirectly.

Both choices of the phase variables are equivalent.
The bias can break the Josephson symmetry and facilitate its cubic nonlinearity—in the small deviations from the equilibrium.

II.3
Point of equilibrium

For the further analysis, we use that the system operates in the regime of dominating inductive and Josephson energies:

E^{\mathrm{r}}_{L}\ggg E^{\mathrm{r}}_{C},\quad E^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathrm{J}}\ggg E^{\mathrm{a}}_{C}.

(10)

From the mechanical standpoint, this resembles the motion of a heavy particle in the vicinity of its equilibrium.
Thus, we can expand the coupler potential energy in Eq. (
8
) near the points
\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{r}}
and
\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}
of local minimum.
These points are determined by two transcendental equations:

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{r}}_{L}\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{r}}+E^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{J}}\sin\delta=0,

(11a)

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{a}}_{L}\Big(\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}-\frac{2\pi\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}}{\Phi_{0}}\Big)+E^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathrm{J}}\sin\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}-E^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{J}}\sin\delta=0,

(11b)

where the equilibrium phase difference at the coupler is

\delta=\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{r}}-\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}-\frac{\pi\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}}{\Phi_{0}}

(12)

Up to a factor, Eq. (
11a
) expresses the conservation of charge that flows in and out of the resonator node with the static currents.
Equation (
11b
) expresses the same for the atom node.

The second term in Eq. (
11a
) is proportional to the dc screening current in the big loop in Fig.
2
.
In fact, Eq. (
11a
) accepts another interpretation.
It follows from the flux quantization rule that the full flux threading the loop in the direction to the reader equals (see Fig.
2
)
\Phi_{\Sigma}=\phi_{\mathrm{r}}\Phi_{0}/2\pi
.
As there is no external flux in this loop, the flux is generated entirely by the screening current
I_{\mathrm{scr}}=I_{0}^{\mathrm{c}}\sin\delta
that flows clockwise.
That is,

\Phi_{\Sigma}=-L_{\mathrm{r}}I_{\mathrm{scr}}.

(13)

Up to a factor, we have recovered Eq. (
11a
).
Note that we neglect the loop inductive energy other than stored in the resonator inductance.
We discuss the effect of possible additional inductance in Sec.
IV
.

In what follows,
\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}
and
\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}
denote the departures from the equilibrium in the resonator and atom phases, i.e.,

\phi_{\mathrm{r}}=\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{r}}+\varphi_{\mathrm{r}},\quad\phi_{\mathrm{a}}=\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}+\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}.

(14)

In terms of them, Josephson coupling energy reads

U_{\mathrm{c}}=-E^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{J}}\cos(\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}-\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}+\delta).

(15)

According to Eq. (
12
), the phase
\delta
includes the equilibrium phases of each coupled system as well as the coupler bias.
This phase determines the order of the dominant inductive interaction, as we discuss later in this paper.

II.4
Quantum Hamiltonian

We seek the quantum Hamiltonian that governs the circuit dynamics near its equilibrium at
\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{r}}
and
\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}
.
That is, departures
\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}
and

We expand the cosine term in Eq. (
8

and

Then, we promote the classical variables of the resonator and the artificial atom to the operators with the commutation relations

To arrive at the second-quantized Hamiltonian, we express these operators in terms of the bosonic ladder operators.
For the resonator variables, one has

Here

are the photon annihilation and creation operators, while

are the magnitudes of the resonator charge and phase zero-point fluctuations.
Analogously, the atom operators
\hat{n}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{a}}
are expressed as
\hat{n}_{\mathrm{a}}=-in_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{zpf}}(b^{\dagger}-b)
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{a}}=\varphi_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{zpf}}(b^{\dagger}+b)
, where

n_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{zpf}}=(\tilde{E}^{\mathrm{a}}_{L}/32E^{\mathrm{a}}_{C})^{1/4},\quad\varphi_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{zpf}}=(2E^{\mathrm{a}}_{C}/\tilde{E}^{\mathrm{a}}_{L})^{1/4}

(17)

are the magnitudes of the atom zero-point fluctuations.
Operators

and
b^{\dagger}
annihilate and create an excitation in the artificial atom.

Above, we introduce the renormalized inductive-like energies of the resonator and the atom:

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\mathrm{r}}_{L}=E^{\mathrm{r}}_{L}+E^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{J}}\cos\delta,

(18a)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\mathrm{a}}_{L}=E^{\mathrm{a}}_{L}+E^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathrm{J}}\cos\phi^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{a}}+E^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{J}}\cos\delta.

What is more important for us, the SQUID coupler—as a nonlinear inductive element—gives rise to the dipolar and various non-dipolar interactions.

\propto\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}

First, we illustrate how the two-photon coupling arises in the simplest case of negligible linear coupling.
Then, we investigate the case when the linear coupling is highly non-resonant.
Finally, we mention how the linear coupling can be suppressed.

IV
Discussion

Bosonic approximation.

For the brevity of presentation and to connect to future work, we have expressed the atom variables in terms of bosonic ladder operators in Sec.
II.4
.
For that, the artificial atom should possess multiple (approximately) equidistant energy states.
However, our main analytical result for the two-photon coupling rate can be easily generalized as in Sec.
V
.

Validity of the cosine expansion.

To expand the coupler Josephson energy in Eq. (
8
), we have assumed that departures from the equilibrium phases are small.
We now provide concrete criteria for that.
One can require that
\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{r}}N_{\mathrm{ch}}\ll 1
and
\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{a}}\ll 1
, where we assume that the artificial atom cannot be excited beyond its first excited state.
When these criteria hold, we expect that all higher expansion terms in the Josephson energy are negligible.
However, the two-photon coupling rate also becomes small, compared to the possible inductive coupling strength.

In fact, for our estimates in the previous section, the criterion above does not hold when there is more than one photon in the resonator.
However, we claim that it is enough to satisfy a weaker condition,

\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{r}}^{2}\ll 1,\quad\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{r}}\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{a}}\ll 1,\quad\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{a}}^{2}\ll 1,

(24)

for Eq. (
23

For example, consider a term with
\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{r}}^{2}\varphi_{\mathrm{zpf},\mathrm{a}}^{3}a^{2}b^{{\dagger}2}b
in the expansion of the coupler Josephson energy.
This and other similar terms give rise to two-photon processes as well.
However, under the condition above, such processes occur rarely.
Note that the conditions in Eqs. (

VI
Conclusions

To conclude, we have shown that a two-photon interaction can be mediated by a symmetric dc SQUID, estimated its magnitude, and studied its dependence on the coupler magnetic field.
In addition to coupling of resonator and phase qubit, we have outlined several other schemes relevant for applications.

Two-photon interaction arises owing to two related mechanisms:
In one of them, the external magnetic field in the coupling SQUID breaks the Josephson symmetry.
In the other, one of the interacting parties provides an additional asymmetry through the equilibrium drop of the condensate phase on it.
The latter is the case for the metastable state of the rf SQUID that we consider.
We have shown that the magnetic-field symmetry breaking can be re-formulated as the mechanism due to non-zero equilibrium phases.
On the other hand, an rf SQUID state with a non-zero equilibrium phase is typically prepared using its magnetic field bias.

We have estimated the rate of two-photon coupling that arises due to both mechanisms.
We expect that the rate up to 18 MHz is attainable, which is comparable to our estimate of the capacitive coupling strength in our circuit.
We have argued that the coupling rate can be even higher and outlined some possible uses of the predicted interaction.
We have shown that the two-photon interaction can be switched off gradually by changing the coupler bias.

Symmetry breaking due to non-zero equilibrium phase allows one to maximize the two-photon coupling via the symmetrical coupling SQUID.
Apart from the phase qubit case that we consider, we expect this symmetry-breaking mechanism to contribute to the flux qubit coupling.
On contrary, that is not the case for the transmon qubit, for which we have outlined other approaches.

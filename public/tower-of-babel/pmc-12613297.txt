Response to letter regarding determinants of decision-making in orthognathic surgery among patients in Ha’il Region, Saudi Arabia
Authors: Abdullah F. Alshammari, Ahmed A. Madfa
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We would like to express our sincere gratitude to Haznedar and Lim for their thoughtful and constructive comments on our recent publication, ‘Determinants of Decision-Making in Orthognathic Surgery among Patients in the Ha’il Region, Saudi Arabia’ [1]. We greatly appreciate their engagement with our work and share their view that understanding the psychosocial dimensions of orthognathic surgery decisions is vital to advancing patient-centred care.

Our study was designed as an exploratory, cross-sectional investigation conducted between 2019 and 2023 to identify key factors influencing orthognathic surgery decisions within a previously unexamined regional population. As the correspondents correctly noted, the relatively small sample size (n = 27) may limit generalizability; however, it reflects the total number of eligible patients who met the inclusion criteria during the study period. Although recruitment was limited to a single tertiary referral hospital, this institution represents the primary centre for orthognathic cases in the Ha’il region and thus provides a reasonable snapshot of local patient experiences. We fully agree that future multicentre studies encompassing private and other public healthcare settings across northern Saudi Arabia would enhance external validity and minimize selection bias—plans for such collaborative work are already underway.

We concur with the correspondents’ observations regarding methodological refinement and the potential value of longitudinal or mixed methods approaches. The structured interview format in our study was intentionally chosen to capture patients’ psychological and social perspectives—dimensions often underrepresented in quantitative research. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that the inclusion of validated psychometric instruments (e.g. assessing body image, quality of life, and perceived social stigma) would strengthen measurement precision and allow for meaningful comparisons across populations [2]. We intend to integrate such tools in future studies to deepen understanding of how patient motivations, expectations, and emotional responses evolve before and after surgery [3].

Importantly, we share the correspondents’ emphasis on improving communication between healthcare professionals and patients. Our findings revealed that fewer than half of participants reported receiving comprehensive information about surgical risks and benefits, underscoring the need for enhanced counselling strategies [4]. Future investigations should explore how preoperative education and surgeon–patient communication affect decision confidence and postoperative satisfaction [5]. Such efforts align closely with the Saudi Vision 2030 framework, which prioritizes transparency, patient engagement, and shared decision-making as cornerstones of healthcare quality.

In conclusion, we sincerely thank the correspondents and the Editor for their insightful feedback, which enriches the ongoing dialogue on patient-centred decision-making in orthognathic surgery. We view our study as a foundational step towards more comprehensive, multicentre research that integrates psychological, functional, and social dimensions of patient care within the Saudi context.

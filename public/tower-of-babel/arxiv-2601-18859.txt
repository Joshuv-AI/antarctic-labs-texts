Energetic Ceilings of Astrophysical Gravitational-Wave Backgrounds
Chiara M. F. Mingarelli

Abstract
Every astrophysical stochastic gravitational wave (GW) background is limited by the amount of rest mass available to be converted into gravitational radiation. Here we derive a population-agnostic scaling law that places an absolute energetic ceiling on stochastic backgrounds across the entire GW frequency spectrum, from nanoHertz to kilohertz. We apply this framework to bound the backgrounds from supermassive black hole binaries, intermediate-mass black hole captures by supermassive black holes in AGN disks, extreme mass-ratio inspirals, binary neutron stars, Population III remnants, and stellar-mass binary black holes. We find that the energetic ceiling for supermassive black hole binaries is A ≤ 1.6 − 0.3 + 0.3 × 10 − 15 A\leq 1.6^{+0.3}_{-0.3}\times 10^{-15} at a reference frequency of 1 ​ yr − 1 1\,{\rm yr}^{-1} . This astrophysical GWB ceiling is within 1 ​ σ 1\sigma with the GWB amplitude reported by NANOGrav, EPTA, and PPTA, implying that the current observed signal is consistent with being powered by a population of ultramassive black holes ( M ∙ ≳ 10 10 ​ M ⊙ M_{\bullet}\gtrsim 10^{10}\,M_{\odot} ). Finally, we demonstrate that the total astrophysical background from all channels combined cannot exceed Ω gw ∼ 10 − 7 \Omega_{\rm gw}\sim 10^{-7} .

Each band contains multiple astrophysical populations capable of generating stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds (GWBs), and several of these backgrounds may overlap.
As detector sensitivities improve, it becomes increasingly important to know not only which backgrounds are likely, but which ones are even astrophysically possible.

Most existing predictions for astrophysical GWBs rely on forward modeling: detailed prescriptions for formation channels, merger trees, accretion physics, nuclear cluster dynamics, star-formation histories, and cosmic evolution

;
McWilliams et al. 2014
;

;

These models are valuable but highly uncertain. They also obscure a more basic point: every stochastic background is ultimately limited by the amount of mass a population can convert into gravitational radiation over cosmic time.
No choice of microphysics can circumvent this constraint.

The concept that astrophysical backgrounds are bounded by a global mass budget is well established.
The seminal work of
Phinney 2001
provided the general formalism linking the characteristic strain to the radiated energy spectrum of a population.
More recently,
Sato-Polito et al. 2024
applied rigorous energetic constraints to the nanoHertz band, demonstrating that the amplitude of the supermassive black hole (SMBH) binary (SMBHB) background is tightly bounded by the availability of high-mass black holes. Moreover,
Wright et al. 2024
made similar energetic arguments for cosmological GWBs.

Our goal is to generalize these astrophysical insights into a population-agnostic conservation framework that yields physically unavoidable ceilings on stochastic GWBs across all frequency bands. We deliberately target optimistic upper bounds rather than detailed forward modeling: we identify a minimal set of empirical or theoretically robust inputs that cap each population’s total energy budget, including LVK merger rates
The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2025
, local mass densities of black holes and stars
Soltan 1982
;
Kormendy and Ho 2013
, and electromagnetic luminosity functions
McConnell and Ma 2013
;
Shankar et al. 2017
. Once these inputs are specified, the maximum allowed background follows analytically.

The argument is purely energetic, so diverse astrophysical GWB channels can be treated within the same framework. For each population we identify the controlling parameters, quantify how they enter the ceiling, and show that even extreme assumptions compress the allowed strain into narrow ranges. In this work (Paper I) we focus on the global constraints; a dedicated application to the new AGN-disk IMBH channel is presented in Paper II
Mingarelli 2026

These ceilings here provide population-level sanity checks for stochastic analyses and sensitivity targets for future observatories. More broadly, they supply band-integrated constraints that any viable forward model must satisfy, and therefore offer a physically grounded baseline for interpreting multi-band measurements of the GW sky.

II
A General Energetic Bound on Astrophysical GWBs

All astrophysical stochastic backgrounds share one universal property: they are powered by the conversion of rest mass into gravitational radiation.
Regardless of formation channel or detailed microphysics, every background must satisfy a global energy-balance relation of the form

\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)=\frac{f}{\rho_{c}}\,\frac{{\rm d}\rho_{\rm gw}}{{\rm d}f},

(1)

where
{\rm d}\rho_{\rm gw}/{\rm d}f
is the present-day comoving energy density in GWs per unit frequency and
\rho_{c}
is the critical density
Phinney 2001
;
Mingarelli et al. 2019
.
Our aim is to relate
{\rm d}\rho_{\rm gw}/{\rm d}f
to the total mass processed through the relevant population and thereby derive physically unavoidable upper limits on its contribution to
\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)
.

The following constraint applies strictly to astrophysical populations formed from baryonic matter.
Primordial black holes or other exotic non-baryonic sources are not limited by the stellar or gas-accretion budgets, though they are constrained by separate cosmological bounds on the dark matter fraction
Afzal et al. 2023
;
Carr et al. 2021
.

This starting point parallels the formal structure of earlier forward-modeling treatments such as Phinney (2001)
Phinney 2001
. We therefore emphasize at the outset that the novelty here does not lie in re-deriving the definition of
\Omega_{\rm gw}
, but in isolating the global mass-energy constraint and using it to produce population-agnostic ceilings that apply uniformly across GW detector bands.
The logic developed below is therefore complementary to forward models rather than a substitute for them.

II.1
Energy emitted by a single source

Consider a binary chirp mass
\mathcal{M}
that radiates energy spectrum
{\rm d}E_{s}/{\rm d}f_{r}
in the source frame.
For a quasi-circular inspiral, general relativity gives

\frac{{\rm d}E_{s}}{{\rm d}f_{r}}=\frac{(\pi\mathcal{M})^{5/3}}{3}\,f_{r}^{-1/3}.

(2)

Although Eq. (
2
) assumes quasi-circularity, its use in an upper-bound context is conservative.
Eccentricity redistributes radiated energy to higher harmonics, suppressing the strain at the fundamental frequency where the background is strongest.

The ISCO frequency for a test particle orbiting a Kerr black hole with dimensionless spin parameter
a
is:

f_{\rm ISCO}=\frac{1}{\pi M}\left[\left(\frac{r_{\rm ISCO}}{M}\right)^{3/2}+a\right]^{-1},

(3)

where
M
is the total mass of the system and
r_{\rm ISCO}
is the radius of the innermost stable orbit.
The dimensionless radius
\tilde{r}\equiv r_{\rm ISCO}/M
is a monotonic function of spin, ranging from
\tilde{r}=6
for a non-spinning Schwarzschild black hole (
a=0
) to
\tilde{r}=1
for a maximally spinning Kerr black hole (
a=1
) on a prograde orbit
Bardeen et al. 1972
.

II.2
Cosmological population integral

Let the comoving merger (or capture) rate density be
R(z)
.
The present-day GWB is

\frac{{\rm d}\rho_{\rm gw}}{{\rm d}f}=\int_{0}^{z_{\rm max}}\frac{R(z)}{(1+z)}\,\frac{{\rm d}E_{s}}{{\rm d}f_{r}}\bigg|_{f_{r}=(1+z)f}\frac{{\rm d}t}{{\rm d}z}\,{\rm d}z.

(4)

Using Eq. (
2
) and substituting into Eq. (
4
) gives the standard inspiral scaling:

\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)\propto f^{2/3}\int_{0}^{z_{\rm max}}R(z)\,\frac{{\rm d}z}{(1+z)^{4/3}H(z)},

(5)

where
H(z)=H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{M}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\Lambda}}
is the Hubble parameter.
All inspiral-dominated backgrounds therefore have the same frequency dependence.
In this framework, the maximum background in a given band is uniquely determined by the processed mass density of the source population and the GW radiative efficiency.
Because Eq. (
5
) compresses all redshift information into a single cosmological factor, we stress that this is an intentional simplification for obtaining upper bounds.
Forward models that track the detailed redshift evolution of
R(z)
remain essential for predicting backgrounds, but the energetic ceiling depends only on the integrated mass that is radiated in GWs.

II.3
A global mass-energy constraint

Let
\rho_{\rm src}
denote the total comoving rest-mass density that
participates in the relevant astrophysical channel. For EMRIs,
\rho_{\rm src}
is defined as the total mass of the inspiraling secondaries (intruders), distinct from the central SMBH mass.

Let
\epsilon_{\rm gw}
be the average fraction of this rest mass radiated as GWs. For the purpose of deriving the inspiral-dominated ceiling, we make the conservative assumption that this total energy budget is distributed across the inspiral power-law spectrum up to the ISCO frequency:

\rho_{\mathrm{gw}}^{\mathrm{tot}}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\rho_{\mathrm{gw}}}{{\rm d}f}\,{\rm d}f=\epsilon_{\mathrm{gw}}\rho_{\mathrm{src}}

(6)

Eq. (
6
) is exact.
It is nothing more than energy conservation, where
\rho_{\rm src}
represents the processed mass density of the source population, and
\rho_{\rm src}=f_{\rm merge}\rho_{\bullet}
. Here
f_{\rm merge}
is the fraction of the mass that merges, and
\rho_{\bullet}
is the black hole mass density.
In other words, this is the aggregate rest mass of the progenitor systems prior to GW emission.

This step is where the present framework diverges conceptually from
Phinney 2001
;
Sato-Polito et al. 2024
.
Rather than folding
\rho_{\mathrm{src}}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{gw}}
into detailed rate models, we treat them as the only quantities that can raise a background toward its maximal value.
Combining Eq. (
6
) with the standard inspiral spectrum gives the maximum allowed amplitude of the background:

\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)\leq\frac{\epsilon_{\rm gw}\,\rho_{\rm src}}{\rho_{c}}\,\frac{f^{2/3}}{I},

(7)

where
I\equiv\int_{0}^{z_{\rm max}}{\rm d}z/[(1+z)^{4/3}H(z)]
.
Throughout this work we adopt a flat Lambda-CDM cosmology with
H_{0}=67.4\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}

Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
,
\Omega_{m}=0.3
, and
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
, for which the inspiral integral evaluates to
I_{\rm fid}=0.52\,H_{0}^{-1}
for
z_{\max}=6
.

Thus, the entire problem of bounding astrophysical stochastic backgrounds reduces to identifying the largest physically permitted value of
\rho_{\rm src}
for each channel. We adopt population-dependent values
\epsilon_{\rm gw}\leq 0.1
derived from numerical relativity:
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm PTA}=0.02
for a purely inspiral-driven GWB
Hughes 2000
;
Berti et al. 2009
,
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm EMRI}=0.05

Hughes 2000
,
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm IMRI}=0.05
,
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm PopIII}=0.05
,
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm BBH}=0.05

Rezzolla et al. 2008
;
Sperhake et al. 2013
, and
\epsilon_{\rm gw}^{\rm BNS}=0.01

Bernuzzi et al. 2016
.

These values are deliberately optimistic but are physically bounded. While the radiated energy fraction in vacuum general relativity is scale-invariant to first order—depending only on the mass ratio and spin—the values for
\epsilon_{\rm gw}
adopted here represent population-averaged effective efficiencies.

For SMBHBs, the GWB is composed of the inspiral phase from the cosmic merger history of the binaries. For a population with spin
a=0.7

Hughes and Blandford 2003
;
Berti et al. 2007
, the efficiency of the inspiral phase alone is
\epsilon_{\rm gw}\approx 0.02
, with the remaining energy radiated during merger and ringdown at frequencies above the ISCO
Hughes 2000
;
Berti et al. 2009
. Using this standard efficiency is physically consistent with truncating the signal at the ISCO frequency. For the ultra-massive systems with
\mathcal{M}_{c,\rm eff}\approx 10^{10}M_{\odot}
likely dominating the PTA background, using Eq. (
3
) we find that the observed ISCO frequency for
a=0.7
is
f_{\rm max}\approx 400
nHz.

For IMRIs, we adopt a higher efficiency of
\epsilon_{\rm gw}=0.05
. This reflects the capture of IMBH by central SMBHs, where the high spins typical of the supermassive hosts,
a\sim 0.7
, allow for deeper plunges and enhanced radiative efficiency compared to non-spinning captures.

Similarly, for Population III remnants and stellar-mass BBHs, we adopt
\epsilon_{\rm gw}=0.05
. This value aligns with vacuum expectations for black hole mergers
Rezzolla et al. 2008
;
Sperhake et al. 2013
and accounts for top-heavy mass distributions from zero-metallicity progenitors
Bromm et al. 1999
;
Abel et al. 2002
;
Kinugawa et al. 2014
. The broad distribution of unequal mass ratios in the LVK population naturally suppresses the average radiated energy below the theoretical maximum
The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2023
.

Finally, for BNS systems, the efficiency is strictly capped at
\epsilon_{\rm gw}=0.01
by matter effects; tidal disruption and merger hydrodynamics prevent these systems from radiating the full binding energy available to vacuum black holes
Bernuzzi et al. 2016
.

III
Local Supermassive Black Hole Mass Density

We estimate the local SMBH mass density,
\rho_{\bullet}
, using the recent analysis of
Liepold and Ma 2024
, who construct a
z=0
galaxy stellar mass function (GSMF) calibrated at high masses using the volume-limited MASSIVE survey
(
Ma et al. 2014
)
. MASSIVE targets all early-type galaxies with
M_{*}\gtrsim 10^{11.5}\,M_{\odot}
within 108 Mpc, providing robust statistics in the regime that dominates the GWB energy budget.

Stellar masses for MASSIVE galaxies are determined using two independent methods: (i) dynamical modeling from spatially resolved stellar kinematics, which separately constrains stellar and dark matter components, and (ii) stellar population synthesis modeling incorporating a bottom-heavy initial mass function as indicated by spectroscopic measurements
(
Gu et al. 2022
)
. These two methods agree to within
\sim 7\%
, providing confidence in the mass calibration.

The resulting GSMF has a significantly higher amplitude at
M_{*}>10^{11.5}\,M_{\odot}
than previous studies based on shallower photometry or Milky Way-like initial mass function assumptions. By convolving this GSMF with the
M_{\bullet}
–
M_{*}
scaling relation of
McConnell and Ma 2013
,
Liepold and Ma 2024
derive a local black hole mass function and corresponding mass density:

\rho_{\bullet}=\left(1.8^{+0.8}_{-0.5}\right)\times 10^{6}\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-3}.

(8)

This value is notably higher than earlier estimates based on shallower surveys or galaxy velocity dispersions, which typically yield
\rho_{\bullet}\sim(3
–
6)\times 10^{5}\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-3}

(
Yu and Tremaine 2002
;
Shankar et al. 2009
)
. It is also higher than estimates from quasar luminosity functions via the Soltan argument
(
Soltan 1982
;
Marconi et al. 2004
;
Shen et al. 2020
)
.
Liepold and Ma 2024
note this discrepancy as an open question, but demonstrate that their black hole mass function correctly predicts the observed number of ultramassive (
M_{\bullet}>10^{10}\,M_{\odot}
) black holes in the local volume and yields a nanoHertz GWB amplitude consistent with PTA measurements.

IV
Scaling Law for Energetic Constraints

Assuming the background is dominated by the inspiral phase of circular binaries, the strain follows the power law
h_{c}(f)=A(f/f_{\rm ref})^{-2/3}
. The total energy density is:

\rho_{\rm gw}=\rho_{c}\int_{0}^{f_{\rm max}}\frac{\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)}{f}df=\frac{\pi^{2}\rho_{c}A^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}}f_{\rm ref}^{4/3}f_{\rm max}^{2/3}.

(9)

We equate this integrated energy to the available astrophysical budget from Eq. (
6
):
\rho_{\rm gw}=\epsilon_{\rm gw}\rho_{\rm src}
. Solving for the dimensionless amplitude
A
:

A=\frac{H_{0}}{\pi}f_{\rm ref}^{-2/3}f_{\rm max}^{-1/3}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm gw}\rho_{\rm src}}{\rho_{c}}}.

(10)

This formulation shows the three coupled astrophysical uncertainties that determine the GWB amplitude: the local black hole mass density, the radiative efficiency, and the maximum spectral cutoff frequency.

V.1.1
SMBHBs (PTA Band)

Using Eq. (

V.1.2
IMBH–SMBH captures (LISA Band)

In the companion paper (Paper II)

III

The amplitude of this background is constrained by the efficiency with which AGN disks process stellar-mass remnants into SMBH mass growth. We parametrize this via the mass fraction
f_{\rm IMR}\equiv\rho_{\rm IMR}/\rho_{\bullet}
.
Using the local SMBH mass density
\rho_{\bullet}=(1.8^{+0.8}_{-0.5})\times 10^{6}\,M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}
from
Liepold and Ma 2024

\mathcal{R}_{\rm BBH}\approx 20\,\text{Gpc}^{-3}\text{yr}^{-1}

The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2025

A_{IMR}\simeq 1.2\times 10^{-21}
at
f=3
mHz.

However, the primary utility of the framework presented here is to identify the astrophysical ceiling. The aggregate mass of IMBHs delivered to SMBHs cannot exceed the total SMBH mass budget.
Even in a maximally optimistic scenario—where migration traps are highly efficient and IMBHs contribute a substantial fraction (
f_{\rm IMR}^{\rm max}\approx 0.05
) of all SMBH growth—the background is bounded by energy conservation.
Applying Eq. (
11

A_{IMR}^{max}\leq(1.9_{-0.3}^{+0.4})\times 10^{-20}
.

A background exceeding this level would imply that IMBH mergers constitute the majority of SMBH mass assembly, which would be in tension with electromagnetic evidence for SMBH growth via gas accretion

;
Yu and Tremaine 2002
;
Marconi et al. 2004

V.2
Constraints on Stellar-Mass Populations

The remaining populations draw from the cosmic stellar mass density,
\rho_{\star}
.
Unlike the SMBH reservoir, which is inferred from local dynamical censuses,
\rho_{\star}
is defined by the integrated cosmic star formation history.
We adopt a value of
\rho_{\star}=5\times 10^{-3}\rho_{c}=6.5\times 10^{8}\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm Mpc^{-3}}
, consistent with the integral of the star formation rate density measured by
Madau and Dickinson 2014
.
This value represents the total mass of baryons converted into stars over the history of the Universe.
It is a strictly conservative upper bound for compact object progenitors: since black holes and neutron stars are the remnants of massive stars, their total mass density must be a sub-fraction of this reservoir, determined by the initial mass function and mass loss during stellar evolution.
Consequently, no stellar-origin background can exceed the energy budget implied by this global limit.

V.2.1
EMRIs (LISA Band)

EMRIs represent a distinct dynamical channel.
Unlike binary mergers driven by stellar evolution, EMRIs are driven by stellar dynamics in the dense nuclear star clusters surrounding SMBHs
Gair et al. 2004
;
Hopman and Alexander 2005
.
The relevant mass reservoir is therefore strictly limited to these clusters, reducing the effective density to
\rho_{\rm nuc}\simeq 10^{-3}\rho_{\star}
.
For a compact object to enter the EMRI band, it must be scattered onto a very low angular momentum orbit, called the loss cone, via two-body relaxation.
This diffusive process is extremely slow, with relaxation times
t_{\rm rel}\sim 10^{9}\,{\rm yr}
, acting as a strong bound on the event rate.

We distinguish between a plausible upper bound and an extreme upper bound for this channel.
We adopt a capture fraction
f_{\rm EMRI}=10^{-4}
as the plausible bottleneck, which assumes that every nuclear star cluster efficiently funnels compact objects into the loss cone throughout cosmic time.
Even with this assumption, the background saturates at a ceiling of
A_{\rm EMRI}=3.5\times 10^{-22}
at
f_{\rm ref}=10^{-2}
Hz.
However, while purely stellar-dynamical relaxation results in a low bottleneck of
f_{\rm merge}\sim 10^{-4}
, interactions with AGN accretion disks (gas-driven EMRIs) could significantly shorten inspiral timescales.

To maintain a strictly conservative upper limit, we relax this bottleneck and adopt a maximally optimistic merger fraction of
f_{\rm EMRI}=0.1
, assuming that gas torques allow 10% of the entire nuclear star cluster mass to merge efficiently
Pan and Yang 2021
;
Tagawa et al. 2020
.
We clarify that
\rho_{\rm src}
here refers to the total mass of the captured secondary objects, consistent with the definition of efficiency
\epsilon_{\rm gw}\approx 0.05
.

Regardless of the formation channel — two-body relaxation, AGN disk capture, or tidal disruption of star clusters — the total mass accreted via EMRIs is ultimately bounded by the present-day MBH mass function. Since EMRIs contribute to MBH growth, one cannot have more total EMRI mass than is consistent with
\rho_{\bullet}
. This provides a model-independent upper limit on the EMRI GWB that does not depend on the specific capture mechanism.
Using the updated SMBH mass density, the background saturates at a ceiling of
A_{\rm EMRI}\approx 1.9\times 10^{-20}
.

V.2.2
Binary Neutron Stars (decihertz Band)

For BNSs, the energetic ceiling is empirically anchored by the local merger rate.
The recent GWTC-4 catalog from the LVK O4 observing run constrains this rate to
\mathcal{R}_{\rm BNS}\approx 7.6-250\,{\rm Gpc}^{-3}{\rm yr}^{-1}

The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2025
.
To place a conservative upper limit on the stochastic background, we adopt the optimistic upper bound of the
90\%
credible interval (
250\,{\rm Gpc}^{-3}{\rm yr}^{-1}
), corresponding to a processed mass fraction of
f_{\rm BNS}\approx 1\times 10^{-5}
of the total stellar mass density.

The spectral extent of this background is determined by nuclear physics.
The upper frequency cutoff,
f_{\rm max}
, corresponds to the merger frequency, which is governed by the neutron star equation of state.
For a canonical
1.4M_{\odot}+1.4M_{\odot}
system, the inspiral terminates near the contact frequency of
f_{\rm max}\approx 1.5
kHz.
Assuming a spectral index
\alpha=-2/3
, the energetic conservation law in Eq. (
11
) sets the ceiling at

A_{\rm BNS}=6.7\times 10^{-24}
at
f=0.1\,{\rm Hz}
.

We highlight a critical phenomenological transition that occurs between the LVK and decihertz bands.
In the LVK band (
f\gtrsim 10
Hz), the duty cycle of BNS signals is low (
\ll 1
); the background manifests as non-Gaussian “popcorn” noise composed of individually resolvable events.
However, in the decihertz band (
0.01-1
Hz), the signal duration increases to years or decades (
t_{\rm merge}\propto f^{-8/3}
), causing the sources to pile up.
In this regime, the BNS signal forms a true stochastic background—a Gaussian confusion noise similar to the Galactic white dwarf foreground in the LISA band.

This result provides a firm target for future missions: while the signal is well within the sensitivity curves of proposed observatories like BBO, the energetic limit derived here rules out earlier, more optimistic models that predicted BNS backgrounds approaching
\Omega_{\rm gw}\sim 10^{-8}
.

V.2.3
Population III Remnants (decihertz Band)

The stochastic background from Pop III remnants is constrained by a different bottleneck: the scarcity of metal-free gas in the early Universe.
These binaries, formed from the first generation of stars at
z\gtrsim 10
, are limited by the small fraction of cosmic baryons capable of forming metal-free stars before enrichment shuts off the channel (
f_{\rm III}\sim 3\times 10^{-5}
)
Inayoshi et al. 2020
;
Kinugawa et al. 2014
.
Unlike stellar-mass binaries, Pop III remnants may have very high radiative efficiencies if they form top-heavy black hole distributions. Detailed formation models remain bifurcated between isolated binary evolution and dynamical hardening in nuclear star clusters. While the latter channel may shift the merger peak to higher redshifts of
z\sim 10
, our constraint relies only on the integrated reservoir of Pop III stars, rendering the specific delay-time distribution irrelevant to the total energy budget
Liu and Bromm 2020
.

However, even assuming an intentionally extreme scenario where
1\%
of all Pop III mass merges as efficient black hole binaries (
f_{\rm merge}=0.01
), the background is capped at
A_{\rm III}=4.9\times 10^{-24}
at
f_{\rm ref}=0.1
Hz.

This
1\%
merger fraction represents an extreme upper limit motivated by theoretical models of Pop III binary evolution. While standard isolated binary evolution yields
f_{\rm merge}\lesssim 10^{-3}

Kinugawa et al. 2014
, we adopt 1% to account for potentially enhanced merger rates from dynamical hardening in dense primordial environments or uncertain common-envelope physics at zero metallicity
Belczynski et al. 2017
;
Santoliquido et al. 2023
.
This separation is important for future experiments, since the Pop III ceiling is distinct from and sub-dominant to the BNS signal, ensuring that decihertz observatories can spectrally distinguish the cosmological epoch of reionization from late-universe compact object evolution.

V.2.4
Stellar BBHs (LVK Band)

The stochastic background in the
10\text{--}100\,{\rm Hz}
band is primarily composed of the unresolved accumulation of stellar-mass BBH mergers throughout the Universe.
The local rate of these mergers is a measured quantity.
The recent LVK O4 catalog (GWTC-4) constrains the local merger rate to
R\approx 14\text{--}26\,{\rm Gpc}^{-3}{\rm yr}^{-1}

The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2025
.
Converting this number density into a mass budget implies that only a minute fraction,
f_{\rm BBH}\approx 1.8\times 10^{-5}
, of the cosmic stellar mass is processed into black hole mergers.
Inserting this empirically anchored mass fraction into Eq. (
11
) sets a strict ceiling on the background amplitude:
A_{\rm BBH}=9.5\times 10^{-25}
at
25
Hz. This highlights the fact that the stochastic background is physically coupled to the resolved event rate.

Furthermore, this population is not isolated; a sub-fraction of these stellar binaries—specifically those merging in AGN disks—serves as the necessary pipeline for the AGN–IMRI pipeline discussed in Sec.
V.1.2
, dynamically linking the LVK and LISA bands.

Finally, recent models suggest stars embedded in AGN disks could accrete gas to become massive “immortal” stars before collapsing into black holes
Cantiello et al. 2021
;
Jermyn et al. 2022
.
While this effectively increases the local stellar-mass black hole reservoir, this mass is drawn directly from the AGN gas budget.
Since the total gas supply is bounded by the Soltan arguments, this channel represents a transfer between gas and star reservoirs rather than a violation of the global baryon limit.

V.3
Galactic double white dwarf foreground

The Galactic double white dwarf (DWD) signal in the LISA band represents a distinct class of astrophysical signal.
Unlike the stochastic backgrounds discussed in Sec.
V.1
and
V.2
, which are powered by the global cosmological mass reservoirs (
\rho_{\bullet}
and
\rho_{\star}
), the DWD signal is a local, anisotropic foreground generated by the spatial concentration of
\sim 10^{7}
compact binaries within the Milky Way
Robson et al. 2019
;
Breivik et al. 2020
.
In the millihertz band, the sheer number density of these sources creates a confusion noise that significantly exceeds the instrument noise floor.
This foreground presents a unique challenge for detecting the AGN–IMRI background (Sec.
V.1.2
): it is expected to be much louder than the extragalactic background signal (
A_{\rm gal}\gg A_{\rm iso}
), and both populations share an identical inspiral spectral slope (
\Omega_{\rm gw}\propto f^{2/3}
).
Crucially, however, the DWD signal is not included in our global energetic accounting because it does not represent a cosmological energy density.
We also note the presence of an isotropic extragalactic DWD background arising from compact binaries in external galaxies. While its amplitude is expected to be significantly lower than the Galactic foreground in the LISA band, this population is ultimately connected to the stellar mass budget of intermediate-mass stars (
1
–
8\,M_{\odot}
) that produce white dwarfs. However, since WD binaries radiate at frequencies far below the Chandrasekhar-limited merger frequency and with negligible mass loss to GWs compared to their progenitor stellar mass, their contribution to the integrated
\Omega_{\rm gw}
budget remains sub-dominant to compact object mergers
Farmer and Phinney 2003
;
Korol et al. 2017
.

Furthermore, despite the spectral degeneracy, it is separable from the isotropic IMBH background, as we show in
Mingarelli 2026
.
As
Bonetti and Sesana 2020
also point out, the DWD signal is strongly anisotropic, tracing the Galactic disk.
As the LISA constellation orbits the Sun, this anisotropy induces a characteristic annual modulation in the data stream. Using a simple Fisher analysis, we show that this modulation breaks the degeneracy, allowing the faint isotropic background to be extracted from beneath the bright Galactic foreground.
This separability requires that the modulation sidebands be detected with
{\rm SNR}_{\rm mod}\gg 1
, a condition satisfied by the high-amplitude foreground.

VI
Astrophysical Ceiling on
\Omega_{\rm gw}

The population-level ceilings derived above invite a more general question: how large can the total astrophysical GW energy density ever be? We address this with two independent approaches: a top-down bound from global baryonic bookkeeping, and a bottom-up sum of the individual channel ceilings as a consistency check.

VI.1
Top-down: baryon budget

Since every astrophysical GWB is ultimately powered by baryons, global bookkeeping sets a firm ceiling. For stellar-mass populations (BBH, BNS, EMRIs, Pop III), the total rest-mass density available is bounded by

\rho^{\rm stellar}_{\rm src}\leq f_{\rm merge}\,f_{\rm comp}\,\rho_{\star}\,,

(12)

where
\rho_{\star}
is the integrated stellar mass density
Madau and Dickinson 2014
,
f_{\rm comp}
is the fraction ending in compact remnants, and
f_{\rm merge}
is the fraction processed through GW-efficient mergers. For supermassive populations (SMBHBs, AGN-IMRIs), the relevant reservoir is instead the SMBH mass density
Liepold and Ma 2024
:

\rho^{\rm SMBH}_{\rm src}\leq f_{\rm merge}\,\rho_{\bullet}\,.

(13)

Since
\rho_{\bullet}\approx 10^{-3}\rho_{\star}
, the supermassive channel draws from a significantly smaller mass reservoir than the stellar channel.

While EMRIs and IMRIs are physically similar dynamical processes, their ceilings are derived from different reservoirs in this framework: EMRIs are limited by the supply of stellar remnants in nuclear star clusters
\propto\rho_{\star}
, while IMRIs in the AGN channel are limited by the total growth capacity of the central SMBH
\propto\rho_{\bullet}
.

The total astrophysical GW energy density is then bounded by summing both reservoirs. We adopt
f_{\rm comp}=0.2
and a maximal global limit of
f_{\rm merge}=10^{-3}
for stellar channels (consistent with upper bounds on LVK rates
The LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2025
), and
f_{\rm merge}=0.1
for SMBHBs
Soltan 1982
. This yields a global integrated limit of:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{\mathrm{astro}}(f)d\ln f=\frac{\epsilon_{gw}(\rho^{\rm stellar}_{\rm src}+\rho^{\rm SMBH}_{\rm src})}{\rho_{c}}\leq 10^{-7}\,.

(14)

This is an integrated constraint on the total energy density, not a pointwise per-frequency ceiling. No combination of astrophysical binary channels can produce an integrated background exceeding
\sim 10^{-7}
without violating the baryon budget of the Universe.

VI.2
Bottom-up: sum of channels

As an independent consistency check, we sum the integrated contributions implied by the individual channel ceilings derived in Table
1
. This summation includes the contributions from both the supermassive reservoirs (SMBHBs, IMRIs) and stellar reservoirs (BNS, BBH, EMRIs, Pop III).

Using the relation
\int\Omega_{\rm gw}d\ln f=(\epsilon_{gw}\rho_{src})/\rho_{c}
, we sum the processed energy densities for the maximally allowed scenarios:

\sum\frac{\rho_{gw}^{i}}{\rho_{c}}\approx\frac{\rho_{gw}^{\bullet}+\rho_{gw}^{extreme}}{\rho_{c}}\approx 6\times 10^{-8}\,.

(15)

The sum is dominated by the supermassive channels (SMBHBs and “Extreme” IMRIs), which contribute
\approx 4\text{--}5\times 10^{-8}
. The stellar mass channels (BNS, BBH, Pop III, EMRIs) contribute sub-dominantly to the total energy budget (
\approx 0.5\times 10^{-8}
). Although the stellar remnant reservoir is orders of magnitude more massive than the SMBH reservoir (
\rho_{\star}\gg\rho_{\bullet}
), the integrated signal is suppressed by the low merger efficiency inferred from current LVK catalogs (
f_{\rm merge}\sim 10^{-5}
) compared to the theoretical saturation assumed for SMBHs (
f_{\rm merge}\sim 0.1
).

This bottom-up sum is comfortably below the top-down baryon limit derived in Eq. (
14
). The factor of
\sim 2
margin reflects the gap between the empirically grounded LVK merger rates used here (
\sim 10^{-5}
) and the conservative merger efficiency permitted by the baryon budget in Sec.
VI.1
(
10^{-3}
), ultimately verifying that the per-channel reservoir accounting is self-consistent.

VII
Discussion

The central result of this paper is a set of energetic ceilings for astrophysical GWBs. The logic is deliberately global: no astrophysical channel can radiate more GW energy than the rest-mass energy that the Universe can plausibly process through that channel. This conservation argument is compatible with, but logically distinct from, standard forward models that predict merger rates, environments, and duty cycles
(
Sesana et al. 2008
;
Kelley et al. 2017
;
Casey-Clyde et al. 2022
)
. Our aim is not to replace forward modeling. It is to provide a model-independent, physically motivated prior that any forward model must adhere to.

The practical value of this framework is that it is detector-agnostic. It can be applied to any experiment that measures a GWB, from PTAs at nanoHertz frequencies
(
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
EPTA Collaboration et al. 2023
;
Reardon et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Miles et al. 2025
)
, to proposed
\mu
Hz observatories
(
Sesana et al. 2021
)
, to LISA in the millihertz band
(
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
)
, to ground-based detectors at tens to thousands of hertz
(
LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2015
;
Acernese et al. 2015
)
. Since the ceilings are expressed in terms of empirically constrained mass densities and conservative efficiency factors, they define a shared physical scale that can be used to interpret measurements, set robust priors in inference pipelines, and flag internal inconsistencies. They do not compete with detailed forward modeling or careful treatment of detector systematics, but instead bound what any astrophysical interpretation is allowed to claim once the available mass reservoir and plausible efficiencies are specified.

In the PTA band, adopting the local SMBH mass density derived from dynamical
measurements by
Liepold and Ma 2024
—which is independent of GW
observations—yields an energetic ceiling of
A\leq 1.6\times 10^{-15}
.
The fact that current PTA results cluster near this scale, rather than orders
of magnitude below it, suggests that the PTA common-spectrum process is probing
the high-efficiency end of the allowed astrophysical parameter
space
(
Sato-Polito et al. 2024
)
. NANOGrav, EPTA, and PPTA now report amplitudes
that agree to within
\sim 1\sigma
(Fig.
1
), despite using some
different pulsars, backends, and analysis pipelines
(
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
EPTA Collaboration et al. 2023
;
Reardon et al. 2023
)
. The MPTA result
(
Miles et al. 2025
)
lies

2
–
3\sigma
above these values, while the CPTA measurement
(
Xu et al. 2023
)

has uncertainties spanning nearly three orders of magnitude. Both experiments
have significantly shorter timing baselines than NANOGrav, EPTA, or PPTA, which
limits their ability to constrain the amplitude at the
f=1\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}

reference frequency even if they have sufficient pulsars to detect
Hellings–Downs spatial correlations. As these datasets extend in time, the amplitude
constraints should tighten and either converge with or clarify any tension
relative to the longer-baseline arrays
(
Shannon et al. 2025
;
Ferranti et al. 2026
)
. The
ceiling framework sharpens the interpretation of that convergence: if multiple
independent PTAs settle on an amplitude close to the energetic ceiling, then
the remaining uncertainty is less about whether there is a signal and more
about how the signal is partitioned among (i) a truly stochastic component,
(ii) anisotropic power from a finite source population, and (iii) individually
resolvable continuous-wave sources.

The agreement between the ceiling and observed amplitudes also constrains the underlying SMBH population. Reproducing an amplitude near the ceiling requires a population of
10^{10}\,M_{\odot}
binaries that is roughly an order of magnitude more abundant than predicted by Soltan-type arguments
(
Soltan 1982
;
Marconi et al. 2004
)
, consistent with the mass function derived by
Liepold and Ma 2024
. Because source number density scales as
N\propto M^{-1}
, a background driven by
10^{10}\,M_{\odot}
binaries will be composed of far fewer sources than one driven by
10^{9}\,M_{\odot}
systems. This implies that the PTA signal may exhibit significant anisotropy, with power concentrated in a small number of sky directions corresponding to nearby, massive binaries
(
Mingarelli et al. 2013
;
Mingarelli et al. 2017
;
Casey-Clyde et al. 2022
)
. If the anisotropy is sufficiently large, the background may resolve into multiple continuous-wave sources more rapidly than would be expected for a GWB dominated by lower-mass systems
(
Gardiner et al. 2024
;
Ferranti et al. 2025
)
.

This underscores why continuous-wave searches—already a central effort following the GWB detection—are well-motivated and timely
(
Agazie et al. 2023b
;
EPTA Collaboration et al. 2024
;
Zhao et al. 2025
;
Agarwal et al. 2025
)
. A background that sits near its energetic ceiling is generically more likely to be dominated by the upper tail of the black hole mass function, pushing the signal away from the idealized limit of an isotropic background
(
Mingarelli et al. 2013
;
Gair et al. 2014
;
Mingarelli et al. 2017
;
Gardiner et al. 2024
)
.

The key point is that if the measured common process is near the ceiling, then continuous GW searches are required for a complete interpretation of the data. In practice, targeted searches can be anchored to well-motivated host candidates, such as the most massive nearby galaxies, galaxy pairs, or AGN selected by independent electromagnetic evidence
(
Mingarelli et al. 2017
;
Casey-Clyde et al. 2025
;
Kis-Tóth et al. 2025
;
Agarwal et al. 2025
)
, while all-sky searches provide a robust path that does not depend on counterpart identification, e.g.
Goldstein et al. 2019
;
Petrov et al. 2024
.

The same SMBH population that dominates the nanoHertz band will also source GWs in the microhertz regime (
10^{-6}
–
10^{-4}
Hz), where proposed missions such as
\mu
Ares would operate
(
Sesana et al. 2021
)
. In this band, the most massive binaries transition from the inspiral-dominated regime probed by PTAs to one where individual systems can be tracked through their late inspiral and merger. The energetic ceiling also applies here: the integrated
\mu
Hz background from SMBHBs cannot exceed what the same mass reservoir permits at nanoHertz frequencies. This cross-band consistency requirement means that
\mu
Ares measurements of resolved massive binaries and any residual confusion background must be compatible with PTA constraints when the populations are evolved under reasonable assumptions about environmental coupling and mass function evolution
(
Sesana et al. 2008
;
Wyithe and Loeb 2003
)
.

Beyond the nanoHertz and microhertz bands, these energetic ceilings offer immediate guidance for the design and interpretation of future missions like LISA, BBO, and Cosmic Explorer. In the millihertz regime, the ceilings for EMRIs and AGN-disk IMRIs lie above the Galactic double white dwarf foreground
(
Farmer and Phinney 2003
;
Korol et al. 2017
;
Breivik et al. 2020
)
(Fig.
2
), indicating that these extragalactic backgrounds are not energetically precluded from detection. Moreover, even backgrounds below the Galactic foreground may be detectable through their distinct spatial and spectral signatures, as we discuss in Paper II
Mingarelli 2026
. The EMRI ceiling reflects the dynamical bottleneck imposed by two-body relaxation timescales, which limit the rate at which compact objects are scattered into the loss cone
(
Gair et al. 2004
;
Hopman and Alexander 2005
)
. If LISA detects an isotropic background significantly exceeding this ceiling after Galactic foreground removal, it would require a non-standard explanation.

In the decihertz band (
0.01
–
1
Hz), the constraints are particularly instructive for distinguishing astrophysical foregrounds from primordial signals. Our derivation shows that the BNS background is energetically capped at
A_{\rm BNS}\sim 6.7\times 10^{-24}
, a level that ensures this band remains relatively clean for probing the early Universe with future observatories such as BBO or DECIGO
(
Harry et al. 2006
;
Crowder and Cornish 2005
)
. Unlike the LVK band, where BNS signals are discrete, the pile-up of sources in the decihertz band creates a true confusion noise
(
Regimbau 2011
;
Rosado 2011
)
. However, because this astrophysical noise is bounded by the stellar mass budget
(
Madau and Dickinson 2014
)
to be
\Omega_{\rm gw}\ll 10^{-8}
, it leaves a wide dynamic range open for identifying primordial backgrounds from inflation or phase transitions, which are often predicted to lie near
\Omega_{\rm gw}\sim 10^{-8}
–
10^{-12}
in this frequency range
Caprini and Figueroa 2018
. The Pop III ceiling (
A_{\rm III}=4.9\times 10^{-24}
) is comparably suppressed by the scarcity of metal-free gas in the early Universe
(
Inayoshi et al. 2020
;
Kinugawa et al. 2014
)
, ensuring that primordial populations cannot dominate this band.

This leads to an interesting application of the framework: a global test for primordial GWs. By summing over all baryonic channels, we established that the total astrophysical background cannot exceed
\Omega_{\rm gw}\sim 10^{-7}
(Sec.
VI
). This baryonic upper limit provides a simple yet rigorous exclusion test. Any stochastic signal detected with an energy density significantly above this level—regardless of frequency—cannot be astrophysical in origin. It would constitute evidence for new physics, such as primordial black holes
(
Carr et al. 2021
)
, cosmic strings
(
Perkins and Yunes 2021
)
, or non-standard cosmological histories
Lasky et al. 2016
.

Finally, the same logic provides a clean route to multi-band consistency. LISA will directly resolve large populations of massive black hole binaries and EMRIs in the millihertz band
(
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
;
Bonetti and Sesana 2020
)
, while PTAs constrain the integrated nanoHertz output of the highest-mass systems. The ceilings derived here allow these measurements to be compared without committing to a single forward model. If LISA measures a population whose implied integrated energy output would overshoot the PTA ceiling when evolved to lower frequencies under reasonable assumptions, that tension would immediately identify where the astrophysical model requires revision—for example, in the environmental coupling
(
Ravi et al. 2014
)
, eccentricity evolution, or the demographics of the highest-mass end
(
Natarajan and Treister 2009
)
. Conversely, consistency across bands would validate the basic accounting of mass reservoirs and efficiencies, and would strengthen the case that the PTA signal is astrophysical rather than primordial
(
Afzal et al. 2023
)
.

VIII
Conclusion

We have derived upper limits on astrophysical GWBs using a global conservation of energy argument: the GW energy radiated by any channel cannot exceed the rest-mass energy that the Universe can plausibly process through that channel. The output is a set of energetic ceilings across frequency bands, expressed in terms of empirically constrained mass densities and maximal channel efficiencies, summarized in Fig.
2
and Tab.
1
. These ceilings are not predictions, but are bounds that any forward model must obey, providing a compact, model-independent consistency check for stochastic analyses.

In the PTA band, the ceiling inferred from local SMBH demographics is close to the amplitude now favored by multiple PTA datasets
(
Agazie et al. 2023a
;
EPTA Collaboration et al. 2023
;
Reardon et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Miles et al. 2025
)
, Fig.
1
. This implies that PTAs are plausibly operating near the maximal astrophysical efficiency allowed by the available SMBH mass reservoir. In that regime, the next discriminants are not only the overall amplitude, but also the angular structure of the GWB
(
Mingarelli et al. 2013
;
Gair et al. 2014
)
and the emergence of resolvable continuous-wave sources
Mingarelli et al. 2017
;
Goldstein et al. 2019
;
Gardiner et al. 2024
;
Petrov et al. 2024
;
Ferranti et al. 2025
. Continuous GW searches—both all-sky and targeted—should be treated as a primary pathway to understanding the PTA signal. If individual binaries are detected, they will enable direct tests of the connection between deterministic signals and the GWB, and they will provide stringent cross-checks of population assumptions using only conservation of energy arguments.

Beyond PTAs, these constraints map the allowable landscape for future observatories, Fig.
2
. In the millihertz band, we find that extragalactic backgrounds from EMRIs and AGN-IMRIs have a ceiling that rises above the LISA DWD foreground. In the decihertz band, the ceiling on BNS backgrounds confirms that the frequency window near
0.1
–
1
Hz remains sufficiently quiet to allow for the detection of primordial signals down to
\Omega_{\rm gw}\sim 10^{-12}

More broadly, the ceiling framework provides a detector-agnostic baseline for joint interpretation across all GW detectors. It supplies a physical prior that complements detailed astrophysical modeling
(
Phinney 2001
;
Sato-Polito et al. 2024
)
, and it provides a simple way to identify when an inferred background would require new ingredients—whether a non-astrophysical contribution or a revision of the assumed mass reservoirs and efficiencies.

By summing over channels, we find that the total integrated astrophysical background is capped at
\Omega_{\rm gw}\lesssim 10^{-7}
, a limit set by the cosmic baryon budget. As GW observational network mature, these energetic ceilings provide a physical framework for connecting measurements from LVK, BBO, DECIGO, LISA,
\mu

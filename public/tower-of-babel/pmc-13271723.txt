Variations in the Level of Termination and Characteristics of Tracheoesophageal Fistula in Patients with Type C Esophageal Atresia
Authors: Charu Yadav, Hiramani Pathak, Ashitosh D Pokharkar, Subhasis Roy Choudhury, Rohit Meshram, Rajiv Chadha, Partap Singh Yadav
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To study the level of termination of the tracheoesophageal fistula (TEF) and the characteristics of the lower esophagus in gross type C TEF.Materials and Methods:In this prospective observational study, the distance between the arch of the azygos vein, the TEF, the upper esophageal end and total gap length were measured directly at thoracotomy. The patients were divided into three groups based on total gap length; Group A, B and C (≤1 cm; 1.1–2 cm; and ≥2.1 cm). The characteristics of the lower esophagus (thin/thick and narrow/wide) were noted.Results:Out of 60, Group A, B, and C had 23 (38.3%), 21 (35%), and 16 (26.7%) patients, respectively. The TEF terminated above, at, and below the azygos vein in 15 (25%), 6 (10%), and 39 (65%) patients, respectively, with the range of 15 mm above to 11 mm below; the commonest site being 5 mm below (n = 26, 43.3%) the vein. The patients with TEF above and at the level of the azygos vein were predominantly in Group A (n = 21, 95.2%), and those below were predominantly in Group B and C (n = 36, 92.3%; P < 0.01). A narrow lumen and thin-walled lower esophagus were significantly higher (n = 15, P = 0.001; n = 10; P = 0.003) in Group C patients.Conclusions:The level of termination of the TEF in Gross type C TEF is inconsistent, the commonest site being 5 mm below the azygos vein. The lower the opening of the TEF below the azygos, the higher the total gap length with thinner and narrow lower esophagus carrying important surgical implications.

INTRODUCTION

Esophageal atresia (EA) is a congenital defect of the esophageal continuity with or without a fistula to the trachea, and the most common is gross type C, i.e., EA with distal trachea esophageal fistula (TEF).[1] The level of termination of TEF can vary along the length of the trachea or can even terminate into bronchus. The details regarding the morphological variations of TEF, especially the level of terminations and characteristics, are not well documented in the literature.

There are several ways to determine the level of termination of the upper pouch, such as an X-ray or contrast study, whereas similar convenient methods for the determination of the level of TEF are not readily applied preoperatively. The higher the termination of the upper esophageal pouch, the longer the gap between the ends;[2] however, such a relation between the lower esophagus and the total gap has not been established.

The longer the gap between the two esophageal ends, the more difficult and challenging is surgical restoration and the chances of an adverse outcome also increase. Hence, we decided to look at the exact anatomical position of termination of the TEF during thoracotomy and to study the characteristics of the lower esophagus so as to determine the significance of these two findings in contributing to the overall gap length as well as their correlation with the operative outcome.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a prospective observational study conducted in the Department of Pediatric Surgery, Lady Hardinge Medical College and associated Kalawati Saran Children’s Hospital, New Delhi after obtaining institutional ethical clearance. The study included all cases of type C EA-TEF managed over a period of 2 years from November 2019 to December 2021. The demographic details like birth weight, gestational age, and associated anomalies were recorded. After initial management, all patients underwent a right posterolateral thoracotomy through the 4th intercostal space. A retro pleural approach was adopted. The upper pouch of the esophagus, the azygos vein and the TEF were identified. The arch of the azygos vein was ligated and divided near its termination (marked point B), the TEF was dissected and divided close to its termination (marked point C) and upper pouch was identified and marked by a stay suture before mobilization (marked point A) [Figure 1]. Taking the stump of azygos vein as a fixed landmark, the distances between it and the two ends of the esophagus were measured, i.e., AB and BC with the help of Vernier callipers, and similarly, the total gap length, i.e., AC was also measured. The patients were divided in to three groups based on total gap lengths: Group A (≤1 cm), B (1.1–2 cm), and C (≥2.1 cm).

Diagrammatic representation of intraoperative landmarks in trachea esophageal fistula. A: Upper esophageal end, B: Azygos arch, C: Tracheoesophageal fistula

The characteristics of the lower esophagus (thin/thick and narrow/wide) were also noted by the senior operating surgeon.

Records of the different operating procedures, such as end-to-end esophageal anastomosis or temporary diversion of the esophagus, and the postoperative outcome were also maintained.

Quantitative data were expressed in mean ± standard deviation, and depends on normality distribution difference between the three comparable groups were tested by the ANOVA test or the Kruskal–Wallis H-test. Qualitative data were expressed in percentage and statistical differences between the proportions were tested by the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data was analyzed and statistically evaluated using SPSS-PC-25 version (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA).

RESULTS

Out of a total of 60 cases studied, 38 were male and 22 were female with a mean birth weight of 2.2 kg (1.3–3 kg), and gestational age 37 ± 0.3 weeks (32–40 weeks). Associated anomalies were present in 30% of cases.

Surgical procedures performed included end-to-end esophageal anastomosis in 53 (88.33%) cases and temporary cervical esophagostomy, closure of the lower esophagus with gastrostomy in 7 (11.66%) cases. The distribution of cases in the three groups was Group A, n = 23 (38.3%); Group B, n = 21 (35%); and Group C, n = 16 (26.7%) cases.

Azygous vein

The level of termination of TEF was assessed in relation to the level of the azygos vein. In the majority of cases (n = 39; 65%), the fistula terminated below or inferior to the level of the azygos vein while 15 (25%) cases had fistula ending above or superior to the level of the azygos vein. In a small number of cases (n = 6; 10%), the fistula terminated at the level of the azygos vein [Figure 2a-c]. Seven (11.6%) cases had a Kluth type 3b4 variety of EA-TEF in which the two esophageal ends ended at the same level.

Intraoperative images showing relation between azygos vein and tracheoesophageal fistula (TEF), (a) Location of TEF below the azygos vein (after ligating and dividing the azygos vein), (b) TEF terminating above the azygos vein, (c) TEF present at the level of azygos vein (azygos vein looped and retracted to show the site of fistula)

On comparing the level of termination of TEF in the three gap length groups, Group A had 15 (65.2%) cases of fistula terminating above the azygos vein, 5 (21.7%) cases terminating at the level of the azygos vein, and 3 (13.1%) cases, the fistula opened below the level of the azygos vein. In Group C, all the cases (n = 16;100%) had the fistula terminating below the level of the azygos vein. This difference in distribution between the groups was found to be statistically significant (P < 0.01, Chi-square test) [Graph 1].

Variations in the location of tracheoesophageal fistula in three gap length groups

The position of the TEF varied from 15 mm above to 11 mm below the azygos vein, i.e. a total span of 26 mm around the azygos vein arch, with the most common position being 5 mm below the azygos vein in 26 (43.3%) cases.

The mean value of distance between the TEF and the azygos vein was-4.33 mm (distance above azygos vein) in Group A and + 5.07 mm and + 6.09 mm (distance below the azygos vein) in Group B and C, respectively, and the difference in these values was found to be statistically significant (P < 0.001) [Graph 2].

Comparison of distance (mean ± standard deviation mm) of TE fistula from the azygos vein in three gap length groups

Characteristics of the lower esophagus showed an equal number of cases with narrow (n = 30; 50%) and wide (n = 30, 50%) lower esophagus whereas a greater number of cases had a thick-walled esophagus (n = 45; 75%) than a thin-walled esophagus (n = 15; 25%).

On comparison of the characteristics of the lower esophagus with the level of terminations of the TEF it was found the lower esophagus was significantly wide when it terminated at (5/6; 83.3%) or above the level of azygos vein (12/15; 80%) and became significantly narrower when fistula terminated lower down, inferior to the level of the azygos vein (25/39; 64.1%) (P = 0.02, Chi-square test).

Shorter gap length – Group A had a significant number of cases with wide (19/23; 82.7%) and thick (21/23; 91.3%) lower esophagus, whereas longer gap length – Group C had significantly thin (10/16; 62.5%) and narrow (15/16; 93.7%) lower esophagus (P < 0.01, Chi-square test) [Graph 3].

Association of gap length with calibre and wall thickness of the lower esophagus

Postoperative anastomotic leak occurred in 8 (13.3%) cases, and all were in Group B or C. All the cases with anastomotic leak had a thin-walled lower esophagus (100%) with narrow calibre in 6/8 (75%) cases (P < 0.0001, Chi-square test) [Graph 4].

DISCUSSION

As evident from this study, the position of termination of the TEF in Gross type C EA is inconsistent. The level of termination of the upper esophagus can be studied easily by current imaging techniques.[2] However, it is difficult to assess the level of termination of the TEF and the total gap between the two esophageal ends by preoperative imaging. Keeping the arch of the azygos vein as a fixed anatomical landmark and measuring the distance of the TEF from it during surgery is a novel and simple way of studying the morphological variations in its termination. The azygos vein is considered to be a major anatomical landmark related to the site of the TEF[3] and was found in all our cases during posterolateral thoracotomy. We divided the patients in to 3 groups as per the total gap length, following the criteria of Upadhyaya et al.[4] The distribution of patients in different groups in our study was similar to that described by Upadhyaya et al.[4] although the authors did not comment on variations in the position of the TEF.

In the present study, it was observed that the commonest termination of the distal TEF was below the level of the azygos arch (65%), followed by above or superior (25%) while in 10% patients, the TEF was at the level of the azygos vein. This is in contrary to the common belief that the TEF terminates at the level of the azygos vein. Our findings showed a linear relationship between the level of termination of the TEF and total gap length i.e., the lower the level of termination of the fistula, the higher the total gap length (P < 0.001). In Group A, most patients (86.9%) had the TEF terminating above or at the level of the azygos vein. These findings are similar to those of the study by van der Zee and Bax[5] in which preoperative X-rays and picture archiving and communication system were used to assess the positions of the upper pouch and the distal TEF retrospectively in 27 neonates during thoracoscopic repair of EA-TEF. The authors applied the azygos vein as a landmark to assess the distal TEF and found 51.8% cases with fistula below the azygos vein, 33.3% cases with fistula above the azygos vein, and none at the level of the azygos vein.[5] However, unlike in the present study, direct measurements were not assessed prospectively.

The total variation in the level of termination of the distal TEF was 15 mm above to 11 mm below the azygos vein, i.e.,26 mm, with the most common position being 5 mm below the azygos vein (43.3%). A similar study of variations in the position of termination of the TEF has not been reported earlier.

It is generally considered that the distal TEF terminates around the level of the carina, which has been used as a landmark to study its position.[6] Mahalik et al.[7] studied the position of TEF preoperatively with the help of spiral 3D CT and reported the commonest position of TEF to be 5 mm above the carina. Koivusalo et al.[8] retrospectively analyzed patients of EA-TEF and considered the location of the TEF at the carina by bronchoscopy using that as a landmark for long gap to assess the outcomes; however, the characteristics of the lower esophagus and the total gap length were not measured as this was not possible by bronchoscopy.

Several methods of preoperative gap length measurement such as fluoroscopic assessment, three-dimensional computed tomography scans, and tracheobronchoscopy have been used previously.[7910111213] Bagolan et al.,[14] in 2013, measured gap length between the two esophageal ends by placing a catheter in the upper pouch and a bronchoscope at the level of the TEF using fluoroscopy as an aid. In the present study, we chose to measure the distances intraoperatively with more precision and to avoid additional invasive procedures as well as any risk of radiation exposure.

The difference in the mean value of distance of the TEF from the azygos vein among the three groups was also statistically significant (P < 0.001), i.e., the position of termination of TEF varied with the variation in the total gap length. We also observed overlapping or kissing variety of upper and lower esophageal ends (Kluth type IIIb4) in 7 (11.6%) cases in which distal fistula and upper pouch were present at the same level similar to some earlier reports.[5915]

The most common position of termination of distal TEF in our study was found to be 5 mm below the azygos vein. As in good number of cases, the TEF terminates below the azygos arch, therefore, it may be possible to preserve the azygos vein in such cases as preservation of the azygos vein has been reported to have some additional benefits, such as increased mediastinal venous drainage and decreased incidence of pneumonitis.[16]

This is the first of its kind of study where statistical analysis was done to correlate the characteristics of the lower esophagus with the surgical outcome. Nagdeve et al.[17] noted a thin-walled esophagus in all their patients, whereas in our study, the maximum number of cases had a thick-walled lower esophagus. The characteristics of the lower esophagus were compared with the level of termination of the TEF as well as between the three groups, and a direct relation between the narrow calibre of the lower esophagus and its lower termination was found. The lower esophagus became thin-walled in cases in which it terminated far below the azygos vein.

It was found that the patients with postoperative anastomotic leakage had a significant number of thin-walled esophagus (P < 0.01) along with longer gap. Such a variability in the character of the lower esophagus has very significant implications from the surgical point of view.

The limitations were a relatively small number of cases and subjective assessment of the characteristics of the lower esophagus, nonetheless the strength of this study lies in direct objective measurement of the structural anatomy.

CONCLUSIONS

The level of termination of the TEF in Gross type C EA-TEF is inconsistent, the mostcommon site being 5 mm below the azygos vein. There is a linear relationship between the level of TEF and the total gap length, i.e., the lower the opening of the TEF from the azygos arch, the higher the total gap length. The lower esophagus becomes thinner and narrower with a lower or more distal termination of the TEF, which poses a higher chance of postoperative anastomotic leak.

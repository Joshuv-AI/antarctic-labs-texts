CD36 is associated with myocardial ischemia-reperfusion-induced renal injury in diabetic rats, accompanied by alterations in the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway
Authors: Kai Wang, Huimin Liu, Rong Chen, Qingtao Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCD36, a lipid transporter, is markedly upregulated in the diabetic kidney. However, whether CD36 contributes to renal injury following myocardial ischemia–reperfusion (I/R) in diabetes remains unknown. This study aimed to identify the role of CD36 in myocardial I/R-induced renal injury in diabetic rats and to investigate the mechanisms involved.MethodsType 1 diabetic rats received intravenous SSO, a CD36 inhibitor, and were then subjected to myocardial ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) with ischemic postconditioning. Cardiac and renal function, tissue injury markers, and oxidative stress were assessed using commercial assay kits. Protein expression was analyzed by Western blotting.ResultsAfter myocardial I/R, oxidative stress increased and renal function deteriorated. CD36 and the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway were upregulated in the kidney, potentially reflecting a compensatory protective response. SSO, a CD36 inhibitor, reduced myocardial injury, lowered renal CD36 and p-Akt/p-eNOS expression, and attenuated renal injury, indicating that its renoprotective effect was largely secondary to cardioprotection. In contrast, ischemic postconditioning conferred little protection in diabetic rats.ConclusionsThese findings indicate that CD36 is upregulated in the kidney after myocardial I/R in diabetic rats, accompanied by alterations in the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway as a compensatory response. CD36 inhibition by SSO reduced myocardial injury and, in turn, attenuated renal injury, suggesting that CD36 is associated with myocardial I/R-induced renal injury.Keywords: CD36, Diabetes, Myocardial I/R, Renal injury

Introduction

Cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI) is a common and serious complication after cardiac surgery, characterized by a rapid decline in renal function and associated with high mortality. After cardiac surgery, the incidence ranges from 15% to 30%, and approximately 2% of patients require renal replacement therapy, with poor prognosis and high mortality.1, 2, 3 Risk factors include diabetes mellitus, ischemia–reperfusion injury, inflammation, and drug toxicity.4

Myocardial ischemia postconditioning (IPostC) is defined as a strategy applied at the onset of reperfusion following myocardial ischemia, in which the heart is subjected to repeated brief reperfusion and ischemia.5 These brief I/R cycles can reduce tissue injury by activating intracellular signal transduction pathways. IPostC has been shown to confer cardioprotective effects through three cycles of 10-s reperfusion and ischemia applied at the onset of reperfusion.6 However, the cardioprotective effect of IPostC is attenuated under diabetic conditions,6, 7 posing a significant clinical challenge.

CD36 is a transmembrane glycoprotein with multiple biological functions. As a member of the class B scavenger receptor family, CD36 plays a key role in regulating lipid metabolism, atherosclerosis, the inflammatory response, and other pathophysiological processes.8, 9 CD36 is the principal lipid transporter in cells and mediates the transmembrane transport of fatty acids. Impaired recycling of CD36 leads to excessive uptake of fatty acids (FAs) and their incomplete metabolism within cells. This excessive FA accumulation results in abnormal lipid utilization.10, 11 Several studies have demonstrated that CD36 expression is markedly upregulated in renal tissue in diabetic nephropathy and that CD36 inhibition can reduce renal fibrosis in mice.12, 13 High glucose stimulates macrophages to synthesize CD36, which promotes LDL uptake and contributes to atherosclerotic plaque formation.14, 15 While CD36 upregulation has been documented in diabetic nephropathy, no studies to date have investigated the role of CD36 in remote renal injury following myocardial I/R. The present study addresses this gap, offering both novel mechanistic insights and potential clinical implications.

The protective effects of the PI3K/Akt/eNOS signaling pathway have been demonstrated in numerous studies,16, 17 and the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is involved in renal fibrosis.18 Activated Akt phosphorylates and activates eNOS, which in turn produces nitric oxide (NO). The PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway promotes angiogenesis primarily through NO production. NO is a highly reactive free radical that functions as a signaling molecule in various physiological processes.19 For example, activated eNOS may participate in vascular endothelial cell migration induced by VEGF,20 and NO can disrupt VE-cadherin adherens junctions between endothelial cells, thereby increasing endothelial permeability.21 eNOS is a key enzyme that catalyzes NO production in vascular endothelial cells and is constitutively expressed. The eNOS gene is considered a caretaker gene, and its transcription is readily modulated by various factors, including hypoxia and vascular wall shear stress.22, 23

Sulfo-N-succinimidyl oleate (SSO), a CD36 inhibitor, has been shown to alleviate I/R injury in the diabetic heart by reducing free fatty acid (FFA) uptake.24 Although cardiac surgery involves multiple insults, myocardial I/R injury is a central pathogenic mechanism underlying CSA-AKI. Our diabetic rat myocardial I/R model specifically mimics this I/R component to investigate the mechanism of remote renal injury, though we acknowledge that it does not fully replicate all aspects of clinical cardiac surgery. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to determine whether CD36 contributes to kidney injury induced by myocardial I/R in diabetic rats and to explore the underlying mechanisms.

Methods and materials

Animals

All healthy male Sprague Dawley (SD) rats (250 ± 10 g body weight, 8 weeks old) were supplied by the Laboratory Animal Service Center and kept in the SPF level environment, with a stable temperature of 22–24°C and relative humidity of 50–60%. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (Approved on March 18, 2020). The study was conducted in male Sprague–Dawley rats in strict accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (8th edition) and the Regulations for the Administration of Laboratory Animals of China. All procedures were performed under anesthesia (pentobarbital sodium for induction, sevoflurane for maintenance), and all efforts were made to minimise animal suffering and to reduce the number of animals used. This study is reported in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0 (https://arriveguidelines.org).

All rats were acclimatized for one week prior to experimental procedures. After 12 h of fasting, rats in the diabetic group were intraperitoneally injected with streptozotocin (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at a dose of 65 mg/kg to establish a type 1 diabetes model, while rats in the nondiabetic group received an equal volume of citrate buffer intraperitoneally. Blood glucose was measured 72 h after STZ injection; rats with blood glucose≥ 16.7 mmol/L were considered diabetic. Myocardial I/R experiments were performed after a 2-week stabilization period to allow for stable hyperglycemia.

Myocardial I/R injury model

Rats were anesthetized by an intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital (60 mg/kg). Anesthesia was maintained with 2.5–3.0% sevoflurane in oxygen during surgery. Once anesthesia was confirmed, rats were secured on the operating table. Electrocardiography was monitored using needle electrodes, endotracheal intubation was performed, and rats were mechanically ventilated. A left thoracotomy was performed with layer-by-layer dissection, and the pericardium was incised to expose the heart. The left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery was ligated with a 6–0 silk suture for 30 min of ischemia, followed by 2 h of reperfusion to induce myocardial I/R injury. Myocardial ischemia was confirmed by pallor of the myocardium in the ischemic area and ST-segment elevation in lead II. Body temperature was continuously monitored and maintained at 37 ± 0.5°C using a heating pad throughout surgery.

In addition, sham-operated rats underwent the same surgical procedures described above but without coronary artery ligation. IPostC was performed by three cycles of 10-s reperfusion and 10-s ischemia at the onset of reperfusion, as described in our previous study.7 Additionally, SSO was obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (#sc-208408), dissolved in DMSO and diluted to 20 μmol/L with 0.9% normal saline (NS). The solution was administered intravenously at 0.5 mg/kg (3 mL/min for 5 min, total volume 15 mL) 15 min before myocardial ischemia. The dosing protocol was adapted from our previous study.25 Rats were euthanized by cervical dislocation in accordance with the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals.

According to the different treatment conditions, all SD rats were randomly assigned to five groups: diabetic sham operation group (DS), diabetic I/R group (DIR), diabetic IPostC group (DIPO), diabetic I/R + inhibitor group (DIR+SSO), and diabetic IPostC + inhibitor group (DIPO +SSO).

Echocardiography study

At the end of myocardial I/R, cardiac function was assessed using color Doppler ultrasound (VINNO, Suzhou, China) with a probe frequency of 5–10 MHz. The main recording parameters include left ventricular end-systolic dimension (LVIDs), left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (LVIDd), and fractional shortening (FS), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).

CK-MB and cTnI measurement

Blood samples were collected after myocardial I/R. After centrifugation, serum creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB) and cardiac troponin I (cTnI) levels were measured using commercial kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Measurement of FFAs and TGs

After myocardial I/R, blood samples were collected and centrifuged at 3000 rpm, 4°C, for 15 min to obtain serum. Serum free fatty acids (FFAs) and triglycerides (TGs) were measured using commercial kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Measurement of oxidative stress index

Blood samples were collected after myocardial I/R and centrifuged at 3000 rpm, 4°C, for 15 min to obtain serum. Serum reactive oxygen species (ROS), malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and 15-F2t-isoprostane were measured using commercial kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Evaluation of renal function

Blood samples were collected after myocardial I/R and centrifuged at 3000 rpm, 4°C, for 15 min to obtain serum. Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine (Scr) levels were measured using commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was read at the specified wavelength using a microplate reader.

Western blotting analysis

After myocardial I/R, kidney tissue was rapidly removed and placed in liquid nitrogen. Protein was extracted from kidney tissue in RIPA lysis buffer and quantified using a BCA protein assay kit. Equal amounts of protein were electrophoresed on 12% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, Chicago, IL, USA). Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight, followed by secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h. Primary antibodies against CD36 (1:500, SAB5700741), Akt (1:500, SAB4500802), and eNOS (1:1000, SAB5700744) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA); antibodies against p-eNOS (1:1000, ab215717), p-Akt (1:500, ab81283) and GAPDH (1:1000, ab181602) were obtained from Abcam (San Francisco, CA, USA). Protein expression of CD36, p-Akt, Akt, p-eNOS, and eNOS was detected by Western blotting using a chemiluminescence imaging system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was verified using Levene’s test. All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The sample size (n = 6 per group) was determined based on our previous studies and preliminary data, showing sufficient power to detect statistically significant differences (α = 0.05, power = 0.80).

Results

The indicators of myocardial injury in rat serum

To verify the validity of the myocardial ischemia model, serum cTnI and CK-MB were measured after myocardial I/R. Following ischemia–reperfusion, both markers were markedly elevated (P < 0.05). SSO administration reduced myocardial injury (P < 0.05), whereas IPostC did not alter myocardial injury markers in diabetic rats (Fig. 1).

Determination of cardiac function

Color Doppler ultrasound showed that LVIDs, LVIDd, FS, and LVEF were significantly altered in diabetic rats after myocardial I/R. SSO administration improved cardiac function, with significant changes in LVIDs, LVIDd, FS, and LVEF (P＜0.05, Fig. 2).

Triglyceride and free fatty acid concentrations in serum

To examine the effect of CD36 on lipid metabolism in diabetic rats, SSO was administered intravenously before myocardial I/R. Serum TG and FFA levels decreased significantly after SSO administration (P ＜ 0.05, Fig. 3).

Serum levels of oxidative stress markers

Serum SOD, MDA, and 15-F2t-isoprostane levels were examined. In diabetic rats after myocardial I/R, MDA and 15-F2t-isoprostane levels were markedly elevated (P < 0.05), while SOD levels were reduced (P < 0.05). SSO administration reversed these changes, decreasing MDA and 15-F2t-isoprostane and increasing SOD (P < 0.05). IPostC did not significantly alter any of these oxidative stress markers in diabetic rats (Fig. 4).

Severity of renal injury

To investigate renal injury after myocardial I/R and the role of CD36, serum BUN and Scr levels were examined (Fig. 5A, 5B). In diabetic rats, myocardial I/R impaired renal function. SSO administration alleviated renal injury (P < 0.05), likely secondary to reduced myocardial injury, whereas IPostC did not significantly alter renal function.

The role of CD36 and PI3K/Akt/eNOS in renal injury

To determine the role of CD36 in renal injury induced by myocardial I/R, we injected SSO, a CD36 inhibitor, into diabetic rats with myocardial I/R or IPostC and then extracted renal tissue protein and used Western blotting to measure the expression of CD36. The PI3K/Akt/eNOS signaling pathway was also examined to explore the underlying mechanisms. In diabetic rats, renal CD36 expression was markedly upregulated after myocardial I/R and downregulated by SSO (P < 0.05). Renal p-Akt and p-eNOS expression was elevated after myocardial I/R, likely reflecting a compensatory protective response. SSO reduced p-Akt and p-eNOS levels (P < 0.05, Fig. 6), consistent with diminished remote organ injury secondary to improved cardiac function. IPostC did not significantly alter renal CD36, p-Akt, or p-eNOS expression compared with the I/R group (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Myocardial I/R occurs in multiple clinical settings, including heart transplantation and percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), and exacerbates myocardial ischemic injury. The mechanisms underlying myocardial I/R injury are complex and involve oxidative stress, inflammation, free radical generation, and intracellular calcium overload.26, 27 Acute kidney injury is a frequent complication after cardiac surgery.28 Surgical trauma and prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass during cardiac surgery can contribute to renal damage,29, 30 and diabetes further increases this risk.31 Kidney injury impairs glomerular filtration and renal tubular function, increasing mortality and prolonging hospitalization. Therefore, understanding the effects of myocardial ischemia on the kidney is of significant clinical importance.

In this study, a type 1 diabetic rat model was established, followed by myocardial I/R with or without IPostC and CD36 inhibitor SSO treatment. SSO reduced myocardial I/R injury and attenuated renal injury. We propose that the renoprotective effect of SSO is largely secondary to cardioprotection: reduced remote organ injury leads to diminished renal CD36 upregulation and less compensatory PI3K/Akt/eNOS activation. Although CD36 is accompanied by alterations in the PI3K/Akt/eNOS signaling pathway during renal injury, our data suggest that SSO-mediated renoprotection is primarily indirect.

CD36, also known as fatty acid translocase (FAT), is a class B scavenger receptor that recognizes diverse endogenous and exogenous ligands and contributes to the progression of chronic kidney disease.32 In the kidney, CD36 is predominantly expressed in podocytes, mesangial cells, and tubular epithelial cells.33 CD36 expression is markedly upregulated in renal tissue of patients with diabetic nephropathy, and pharmacological inhibition of CD36 reduces renal fibrosis in mice.

Oleic acid increased intracellular Ca2+ levels, whereas CD36 knockout reduced them, indicating a functional link between CD36 and Ca2+ signaling. Activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway was abolished by CD36 knockout or Ca2+ chelation, suggesting that CD36-mediated Ca2+ signaling lies upstream of the PI3K/Akt pathway.34

The PI3K/Akt pathway is a well-established antiapoptotic signaling cascade.35 Although PI3K phosphorylation was not directly measured in the present study, p-Akt served as a well-established downstream readout of PI3K activity. During myocardial I/R injury, Akt is activated, and phosphorylated Akt exerts antiapoptotic effects by activating Bcl-2, inhibiting Bax, and stabilizing mitochondrial membranes.33 Downstream of Akt, eNOS is phosphorylated and promotes endogenous NO production. NO induces vasodilation, attenuates inflammatory cell infiltration, reduces platelet aggregation, improves coronary blood supply, and protects myocardial endothelial cells.36

IPostC is an endogenous protective strategy analogous to ischemic preconditioning. Accumulating evidence indicates that IPostC alleviates I/R injury in multiple organs, including the heart37 and kidney,38 through mechanisms involving anti-inflammatory effects,38 antiapoptotic signaling39 and reduction of oxidative stress.40 He et al.41 demonstrated that ischemic preconditioning of remote organs protected against hepatic I/R injury after liver transplantation by inhibiting oxidative stress and upregulating the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway. In contrast, our study found that the protective effect of IPostC was diminished in diabetic rats, consistent with our previous report.7 IPostC did not significantly reduce renal injury or the expression of CD36, p-Akt, and p-eNOS in the kidney compared with I/R alone. We hypothesize that CD36 overexpression in diabetic kidneys contributes to IPostC resistance, potentially by disrupting the PI3K/Akt/eNOS signaling cascade normally activated by IPostC. Further studies are warranted to determine whether CD36 overexpression is a causal factor in IPostC resistance under diabetic conditions.

Therefore, we hypothesize that after myocardial I/R, CD36 expression is upregulated in the kidney, accompanied by alterations in the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway. Increased renal p-eNOS may reflect a compensatory protective response, promoting NO synthesis to mitigate renal injury. SSO administration reduced myocardial injury and, correspondingly, lowered renal CD36 and p-eNOS levels. We infer that SSO primarily attenuates myocardial damage, which in turn reduces remote renal injury; with diminished renal insult, the compensatory upregulation of p-eNOS is likewise reduced. The specific mechanisms underlying this indirect renoprotection warrant further investigation.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although myocardial I/R is a key pathogenic mechanism underlying CSA-AKI, our diabetic rat myocardial I/R model does not fully replicate all aspects of clinical cardiac surgery, which involves additional insults such as cardiopulmonary bypass, surgical trauma, and systemic inflammation. Second, the renoprotective effect of SSO may be largely indirect, resulting from attenuated myocardial I/R injury and consequently diminished remote organ damage. Distinguishing the direct renal effect of SSO (renal CD36 inhibition) from its indirect effect (improved myocardial function) would require local renal SSO administration or kidney-specific CD36 knockout, representing a direction for future research. Third, although all groups received identical sevoflurane anesthesia—minimizing its potential confounding effect on I/R injury—arterial blood gases were not monitored during the procedure. Last, the present study detected only changes in protein expression of the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway. Without the use of pathway-specific agonists or inhibitors (e.g., LY294002 to inhibit PI3K), our findings demonstrate correlation rather than causation. Future studies employing pathway-specific interventions are needed to confirm the causal role of the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway in CD36-mediated renal injury.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that CD36 is upregulated in the kidney following myocardial I/R in diabetic rats, with compensatory alterations in the PI3K/Akt/eNOS pathway. The CD36 inhibitor SSO attenuates myocardial injury and secondarily alleviates renal injury. These findings suggest an association between CD36 and myocardial I/R-induced renal injury; however, because the renoprotective effect of SSO appears to be largely indirect, further studies using renal-specific CD36 targeting are needed to determine whether CD36 represents a direct therapeutic target for CSA-AKI.

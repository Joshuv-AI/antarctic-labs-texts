Geometric and Hodge-theoretic completion of period mappings
Haohua Deng

Abstract




Abstract

Given a period map defined over a quasi-projective variety, we construct a completion with rich geometric and Hodge-theoretic meaning. This result may be regarded as an analog of Mumford’s toroidal compactification for locally symmetric Hodge varieties as well as a realizable alternative of Kato–Nakayama–Usui’s construction. As an application, we give an alternative proof on the algebraicity of weakly special subvarieties.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation from Griffiths

Hodge-theoretic methods are fundamental in moduli theory of algebraic varieties. Let
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be a family of smooth projective varieties over some quasi-projective base manifold
B
. It gives a canonical integral variation of Hodge structure over
B
which induces a period map
\Phi
from
B
to some analytic Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
. One may obtain ample information about the family
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
by studying its associated period map
\Phi
. Therefore, the study of local and global behavior of
\Phi
including its asymptotic behavior near the boundary of
B
becomes one of the central topics in Hodge theory and algebraic geometry.

Given an abstract period map
\Phi
, a long-investigated problem is to find a good completion
\overline{\Phi}
of
\Phi
to a good algebraic compactification
\overline{B}
. This was firstly introduced by Griffiths in
[
Gri70
, Sec. 9]
. Roughly speaking, “Good compactification” usually means
\overline{B}
is a log compactification of
B
(i.e.,
\overline{B}-B
is a simple normal crossing divisor). For the completion
\overline{\Phi}
to be ”good”, we usually require the following properties:

(1)

\overline{\Phi}
should be a morphism in some subcategory of the category of complex analytic spaces.

(2)

The Hodge-theoretic interpretation of
\overline{\Phi}
along
\overline{B}-B
should be clear in the sense that it records the limiting Hodge-theoretic data.

(3)

When
\Phi
is the period map underlying a family of smooth projective varieties
\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}B
, we may study singular objects along
\overline{B}-B
by looking at the boundary limiting Hodge data from
\overline{\Phi}
.

Griffiths’ problem is almost perfectly addressed when the Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
is locally Hermitian symmetric. Related works include
[
BB66
]
and
[
Ash+10
]
. Though these works on the compactifications of locally symmetric varieties are developed without Hodge-theoretic terms, they can be directly applied to many problems in Hodge theory.

However, for the most general cases, a natural completion of period mappings adhere to Griffiths’ problem is still in mystery. There are many attempts to this question emerging in recent years, for example,
[
Gre+23
]
,
[
GGR21
]
,
[
KU08
]
,
[
KNU10
]
.

1.2.
The main result

The purpose of this paper is to provide a (partial) solution to Griffiths’ long-standing problem. The following informally-stated theorem is the main result of this paper and will be formalized as Theorem
2.8
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Denote
\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)=:\wp\subset\Gamma\backslash D
and let
\Phi^{\prime}:B\rightarrow\wp^{\prime}
be the Stein factorization of
\Phi:B\rightarrow\wp
. There exists a projective completion
\overline{B}
of
B
, a completion
\overline{\wp}
of
\wp^{\prime}
as complex analytic spaces, such that
\Phi
may be completed as a morphism of compact complex analytic spaces
\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
. Moreover, up to finite data,
\overline{\Phi}(b)
records the limiting mixed Hodge structures at
b\in\overline{B}
.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is as follows: Fix a completion
\hat{B}
of
B
, we may find a proper transform
\overline{B}
of
\hat{B}
over
B
, a finite open cover
\overline{B}=\bigcup_{k}W_{k}
, such that on each
W_{k}
we may extend
\Phi|_{B\cap W_{k}}
to some
\Phi_{k}
defined over
W_{k}
. This extension is based on Kato–Nakayama–Usui’s theory
[
KU08
]
,
[
KNU10
]
. Moreover, we will show these local extensions can be patched together to a topological map over the whole
\overline{B}
which is actually a morphism in the category of complex analytic spaces. We will also show that in some special cases, our extended period map is the analytification of a projective morphism.

As an application of this theorem, we give an alternative proof on the algebraicity of weakly special subvarieties. This is a well-known result and was proved by o-minimal methods. Our alternative proof does not use o-minimal theory but the logarithmic Hodge theory and the main theorem.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
we will go over all of the main ingredients in this paper. Necessary backgrounds in Hodge theory will be reviewed in Section
3
. In Section
4
we will introduce the concept of combinatorial monodromy complex (CMCX) which is crucial for our construction. Section
5
will be devoted to the construction of the extended period map as a topological morphism. This morphism will be upgraded to an analytic morphism in Section
6
. In Section
7
we will discuss applications including some examples in which our construction results in completions in the projective category. In Section
8
we will provide an alternative proof on the algebraicity result for weakly special subvarieties. Finally in Section

, we will prove the general existence of CMCX.

2.
Backgrounds and the main results

2.1.
Overview

In this section we review the motivation of this paper as well as some relevant works. Let
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-local system underlying some
\mathbb{Z}
-polarized variation of (mixed) Hodge structures over some quasi-projective variety
B
. Denote the associated period map as

\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D

where
\Gamma
is the monodromy group and
D
is the corresponding period domain parametrizing polarized Hodge structures of weight
l
and Hodge numbers
\{h^{p,q}\}_{p+q=l}
.
Let
\mathbb{G}:=\mathrm{Aut(D)^{+}}
which is an algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})
.

2.1.1.
The classical cases

We first consider the case when the period domain
D
is Hermitian symmetric and
\Gamma
is an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(D)
. In geometry, two most common examples are:

l=1,h^{1,0}=h^{0,1}=g,h^{p,q}=0\ \text{otherwise},

or

l=2,h^{2,0}=h^{0,2}=1,h^{1,1}=a\geq 0,h^{p,q}=0\ \text{otherwise}.

In the first case, the period domain
D
is exactly the generalized Siegel half spaces, or the set of symmetric
2g\times 2g
matrices with complex coefficients and positive-definite imaginary part.

The locally symmetric variety
\Gamma\backslash D
has many well-known compactifications, for example, the Satake-Baily-Borel (SBB) compactification
[
BB66
]
and the toroidal compactification
[
Ash+10
]
which can be described via Hodge-theoretic terms.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
BB66
,
Ash+10
]
)
.

Given a locally symmetric variety
\Gamma\backslash D
, there are canonical Satake-Baily-Borel compactification
\overline{\Gamma\backslash D}^{\mathrm{SBB}}
and toroidal compactifications
\overline{\Gamma\backslash D}^{\Sigma}
depending on the choice of a toroidal fan
\Sigma
, both in the category of projective varieties. Moreover, there is a reduction morphism
\overline{\Gamma\backslash D}^{\Sigma}\rightarrow\overline{\Gamma\backslash D}^{\mathrm{SBB}}
which may be regarded as partial desingularization.

The SBB and toroidal compactifications are important in the study of moduli spaces of certain geometric objects, especially their degenerations. One of the typical questions in the study of moduli theory is to ask whether a given period mapping
\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
over some family of smooth projective varieties over
\mathcal{M}
can be extended to some compactification of
\mathcal{M}
with the target space to be the SBB or toroidal compactification of
\Gamma\backslash D
.

In the curve case where
l=1
, let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, the period map
\Phi_{g}:\mathcal{M}_{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{g}:=\Gamma\backslash D
associated to the universal genus
g
curve
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the Torelli map sending a smooth genus
g
algebraic curve to its Jacobian. The image of
\Phi_{g}
is known as the Jacobian locus inside
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
, the moduli space of rank
g
principal polarized abelian varieties.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Nam76
,
AB12
]
)
.

The Torelli map
\Phi_{g}:\mathcal{M}_{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{g}
can be extended to regular morphisms from
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
to the first and second Voronoi compactifications
\mathfrak{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{Vor}}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{Perf}}
, both of which are toroidal compactifications and descend to a morphism from
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
to the SBB compactification
\mathfrak{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{SBB}}
.

Besides the curves case, there are other examples showing the power of the SBB compactification and toroidal compactification in the study of moduli theory. For example, see
[
ABE22
]
for elliptic K3 surfaces,
[
ACT11
]
for cubic threefolds, and
[
Laz10
]
for cubic fourfolds.

2.2.
Main results

The main purpose of this paper is to construct a completion for a general period map with significant geometric and Hodge-theoretic meaning. By geometric we mean this completion should be done in some subcategory of complex analytic spaces, while by Hodge-theoretic we mean the extended period map should record the boundary asymptotic behaviors of the original period map.

More precisely, given a period map
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
, one may factor the period map
\Phi
via the diagram:

(2.1)

B\xrightarrow{\Phi^{\prime}}\wp^{\prime}\rightarrow\wp:=\mathrm{Img}(\Phi)\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D

where
B\xrightarrow{\Phi^{\prime}}\wp^{\prime}
is the Stein factorization of
\Phi
and is the analytification of a morphism in the category of quasi-projective varieties
[
BBT22
]
. Instead of finding a completion of
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
which requires an enlarged space of the entire Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
, we will complete
\Phi^{\prime}:B\rightarrow\wp^{\prime}
instead.

One of the main ingradients in our construction is the combinatorial monodromy complex (CMCX), see Section
4
. Roughly speaking, fix a completion
\hat{B}
of
B
, a CMCX
\mathcal{T}
is a collection of rational polyhedral complexes indexed by the strata of
\hat{B}
. For every boundary stratum
Z_{I}^{*}
of
\hat{B}
, there is a canonically associated combinatorial monodromy cone (CMC)
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
(Section
4.1
), and
\mathcal{T}
associates each
I
a polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I)
supported on
\overline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}}
with some compatibility conditions (Definition
4.4
).

Remark 2.6
.

CMCX is an analog of a toroidal fan used by
[

]
or a
\Gamma

[

]
and
[

]
which does not require any compatibility conditions with the global monodromy group

\hat{B}
of

Theorem 2.7
.

For a fixed
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS

over a quasi-projective
B
and a SNC completion
\hat{B}
of
B
, a combinatorial monodromy complex always exists.

Theorem
2.7
will be proved in general in Section
9

4

2.3.
Applications and related works

2.3.1.
Comparison with
[
DR23
]

Suppose now
B
is a quasi-projective surface. The completion

\overline{\Phi}_{\Sigma}:\overline{B}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}

given by Theorem
2.5
in
[
DR23
]
is defined on some logarithmic modification
\overline{B}(\Sigma)
of
\overline{B}
. Due to the fact that the global monodromy group
\Gamma
is mostly immeasurable, it is in general not possible to describe the weak fan
\Sigma
or the projective surface
\overline{B}(\Sigma)
over which the map
\overline{\Phi}_{\Sigma}
lives in.

The Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
4.7
gives another completion

\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}

which does not require the existence of any extension of the entire Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
. In this case, the completion
\overline{B}
can be any smooth completion of
B
with
\overline{B}-B
a normal crossing divisor.

As a summary, though the main theorem of this paper does not provide a completed Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D_{\Sigma}
which plays an important role in KNU’s theory
[
KU08
]
,
[
KNU10
]
, the completion
\overline{B}
over which the extended period map exists is much easier to describe.

2.3.2.
Comparison with
[
Den+25
]

In
[
Den+25
]
, Mark Green, Phillip Griffiths, Colleen Robles and the author will give a similar construction. It is known by the Schmid’s nilpotent orbit theorem (Theorem
3.3
) that for any
b\in\overline{B}
, there exists a unique
\Gamma
-class of nilpotent orbits
(\sigma_{b},F_{b})
associated to
b\in\overline{B}
via the asymptotic behavior of
\Phi
near
b
. Instead of trying to record the information on both the nilpotent cone
\sigma_{b}
and the limiting Hodge flag
F_{b}
, we only try to have
\overline{\Phi}
encode the nilpotent orbit
\exp(\sigma_{b,\mathbb{C}})F_{b}
. In other words, the information on the submonoid
\sigma_{b}\subset\sigma_{b,\mathbb{C}}
is lost.

The advantage of the construction in
[
Den+25
]
is that we do not need a combinatorial monodromy complex which encodes the information about monodromy cones. This makes the construction simpler. However, in this case we are only able to show
\overline{\wp}
is a Hausdorff topological space which admits a stratification by complex analytic spaces in general, and
\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
is only a topological completion.

2.3.3.
Examples for projective completions

Though the construction of the CMCX
\mathcal{T}
and the completion
\hat{B}
of
B
given by Theorem
2.8
are in general unapproachable without using computer, there are still some accessible cases including the case when
B
is a surface. In Section
7
we will review several such examples. We will show the main Theorem
2.8
gives completed period mappings in the category of projective varieties. These are cases in which the Conjecture
2.11
is true.

2.3.4.
Algebraicity of weakly special subvarieties

In Hodge theory, weakly special subvarieties for a
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
are analytic subvarieties of
B
carrying special Hodge-theoretic or monodromic data. According to the theory of functional transcendence and o-minimal geometry, since the period map is definable by
[
BKT20
]
, weakly special subvarieties are definable analytic subvariety of
B
and hence must be algebraic by the definable GAGA theorem
[
BBT22
]
.

As an application of our main theorem, we give an alternative proof of the algebraicity result on weakly special subvarieties without using o-minimal methods. Our proof is based on the Hodge-theoretic interpretation of the completed period map
\overline{\Phi}
provided by Theorem
2.8
. We will show that weakly special subvarieties of
B
can be extended to analytic subvarieties of
\overline{B}
over which an extended period map
\overline{\Phi}
exists. The boundary Hodge-theoretic data are described by the period torsors defined by Kato-Usui in
[
KU08
, Chap. 3]
.

2.4.
Important remark on the connectivity of strata

Similar to some related works like
[
Gre+23
]
and
[
GGR21
]
, we implicitly use the assumption that every strata
Z_{I}^{*}\subset\hat{B}-B
is connected. This is in general not the case, but we claim that we may assume it without loss of generality. Indeed, every related argument in this article only uses the partially ordered set of connected boundary strata of
\hat{B}
. In other words, we may re-index each
Z_{I}^{*}
by its connected components
Z_{I,1}^{*},Z_{I,2}^{*},...
without essentially changing any arguments, but this will make notations in this paper unnecessarily complicated. Therefore, we decide to ignore the component index in this paper.

3.
Hodge theory backgrounds

In this section some important definitions and results in Hodge theory will be reviewed. We will also introduce the definition of combinatorial monodromy Complex which is essential for this paper.

3.1.
Basic settings

Let
B
be a smooth quasi-projective variety,
\hat{B}
be projective with
Z:=\hat{B}-B=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}
being a simple normal crossing divisor with
Z_{i}
’s to be its smooth irreducible components. Moreover, for any
I\subset\mathcal{I}
, denote
Z_{I}:=\bigcap_{i\in I}Z_{i}
and
Z_{I}^{*}:=Z_{I}-\bigcup_{j\notin I}Z_{j}
.

Assume there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-local system
\mathbb{V}
on
B
which carries an integral polarized variation of Hodge structures (
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS). Fix a reference point
b_{0}\in B
and denote the fiber of
\mathbb{V}
at
b_{0}
as
V
, there is a period map associated with the
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS:

(3.1)

\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D

where
D
is the classifying space of polarized Hodge structures defined on the integral polarized lattice
(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
and
\check{D}
be its compact dual. Moreover, denote
\mathbb{G}=\mathrm{Aut}(H,Q)
and
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
, then
\Gamma\leq G_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the monodromy group of the local system
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
:

\Gamma:=\mathrm{Img}\{\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)\}.

In addition, we call
\Gamma\backslash D
a Hodge variety associated to the Hodge datum
(\mathbb{G},D)
.

The differential of the period map
\Phi
can be interpretated as follows. For any
b\in B
, consider the universal lift
\tilde{\Phi}:\tilde{B}\rightarrow D
of
\Phi
and any
\tilde{b}\in\tilde{B}
lying above
b
, the image
\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{b})\in D
may be represented by a Hodge flag
F_{b}:=\{F_{b}^{k}\subset V_{b_{0},\mathbb{C}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. The infinitesimal variation of Hodge structures (IVHS)
d\Phi_{b}
is given by the map:

d\Phi_{b}:T_{b}B\rightarrow\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}(F_{b}^{k},V_{b}/F_{b}^{k})

whose image lies in
\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}(F_{b}^{k},F_{b}^{k-1}/F_{b}^{k})
because of the Griffiths transversality.

3.2.
Nilpotent cones and nilpotent orbits

(2)

5.1.
The Nilpotent Cone Closure (NCC)

Let
J\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}}
be a subset such that
Z_{J}\neq\emptyset
. For any point
y\in Z_{J}^{*}
lying above some
b\in Z_{I}^{*}\subset\hat{B}
, by Definition
4.10
there exists a unique
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I)
such that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{y}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}\subset\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}^{\circ}
.

We first define a space
\mathcal{Z}_{J}^{0}\subset\overline{B}-B
as a union of strata in
\overline{B}-B
. Suppose
K\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}}
such that
Z_{J\cup K}\neq\emptyset
. For any point
y^{\prime}\in Z_{K}^{*}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{y^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{K}\subset\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I^{\prime})
for some
I^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{I}
. Similarly for any
y^{\prime\prime}\in Z_{J\cup K}^{*}
we may construct
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{y^{\prime\prime}}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J\cup K}\subset\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cap K}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I^{\prime\prime})
. Since
I^{\prime}\subset I^{\prime\prime}
implies
\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I^{\prime})
is a sub-complex of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(I^{\prime\prime})
, we have
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}
are faces of
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cup K}
.

Definition 5.2
.

We say two subsets
J,K\subset\mathcal{I}
are commensurable if
Z_{J\cup K}\neq\emptyset
,
\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}\subset\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}
, and
\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{K}\subset\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
, all regarded as subspaces of
\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cup K}
. In this case we denote it as
J\sim K
.

Lemma 5.3
.

J\sim K
if and only if
Z_{J\cup K}\neq\emptyset
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cup K}
, in which case
J\sim J\cup K
and
K\sim J\cup K
.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
is an open neighborhood of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}
in
\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{K}\subset\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
, we must have
\mathrm{Conv}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{K}\}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J\cup K}\subset\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
and has non-empty intersection with
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
, hence
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cup K}
. Similarly
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J\cup K}\subset\mathbb{Q}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J\cup K}
, hence
J\sim J\cup K
and
K\sim J\cup K
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

”
\sim
” is in general not an equivalence relation for the following reasons:

(1)

Given
I\sim J
and
J\sim K
, we may have
Z_{I}\cap Z_{K}=\emptyset

(2)

Even if
Z_{I}\cap Z_{K}\neq\emptyset
, the CMC’s
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I\cup J},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J\cup K},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I\cup K}
could have different dimensions.

Definition 5.5
.

For any
J\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}}
, the nilpotent cone closure (NCC) of
J
relative to
\mathcal{T}
or simply the NCC of
J
, denoted as
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}}(J)
or simply just
\mathfrak{C}(J)
when
\mathcal{T}
is fixed, is the unique maximal collection of subsets of
\overline{\mathcal{I}}
such that:

(1)

J\in\mathfrak{C}(J)
.

(2)

\bigcup_{K\in\mathfrak{C}(J)}Z_{K}^{*}
is connected.

(3)

For any
K\in\mathfrak{C}(J)
, there exists
K^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{C}(J)
with
K\sim K^{\prime}
.

We denote
Z_{\mathfrak{C}(J)}:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathfrak{C}(J)}Z_{K}^{*}
.

We present an algorithm for determining
\mathfrak{C}(J)
with a given
J\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

For a given
J\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}}
, the following algorithm determines the set
\mathfrak{C}(J)
.

Step 1:

Starting with the set
\mathfrak{C}(J)_{0}:=\{J\}
.

Step 2:

Given the set
\mathfrak{C}(J)_{k}
with
k\geq 0
, construct the set
\mathfrak{C}(J)_{k+1}:=\{K\subset\overline{\mathcal{I}},K\sim K^{\prime}\ \text{for some }K^{\prime}\in C(J_{k})\}
.

Step 3:

If
\mathfrak{C}(J)_{m}=\mathfrak{C}(J)_{m+1}
, terminate the algorithm and output
\mathfrak{C}(J):=\mathfrak{C}(J)_{m}
. Otherwise, back to Step 2.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{I}}
is finite, the algorithm must terminate in finitely many steps. By Definition
5.5
, the output is exactly the set
\mathfrak{C}(J)
.
∎

Example 5.7
.

In the case given by Example
4.5
, we have
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}_{0}}(J)=:\mathfrak{C}(J)=\{J\}
for every
J\in\overline{\mathcal{I}}
and

Lemma 5.10
.

If
\overline{A_{J}}\cap Z_{K}\neq\emptyset
, then
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}\leq\boldsymbol{\tau}_{J}
. Moreover if
\overline{A_{J}}\cap\overline{A_{K}}\neq\emptyset

,

Proof.

Near any

, choose a local coordinate neighborhood

centered at

) with local coordinates
\{\vec{\xi}=\{\xi_{1},...,\xi_{r}\},\vec{\eta}=\{\eta_{1},...,\eta_{s}\},\vec{\mu}=\{\mu_{1},...,\mu_{m}\}\}
such that

Z_{J}\cap B_{p}=\{\mu_{1}=...=\mu_{m}=0\},

Z_{J\cup K}\cap B_{p}=\{\eta_{1}=...=\eta_{s}=\mu_{1}=...=\mu_{m}=0\}.

By (
3.5
), over
A\cap Z_{J}^{*}
the local lift of the boundary period map has the form:

\widetilde{\Phi_{p}}:(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta},0)\rightarrow\exp(\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\log(\xi_{j})}{2\pi i}N_{j}+\sum_{k\in K-J}\frac{\log(\eta_{k})}{2\pi i}N_{k})\psi_{p}(\xi,\eta,0).

For some holomorphic map
\psi_{p}:B_{p}\rightarrow\check{D}
.

Since

is a component of some CLF

, the image of
\Phi_{p}|_{A_{J}}
takes value in a single
\exp(\mathbb{C}\tau_{J})
-orbit in
\check{D}
, say it is
\exp(\mathbb{C}\tau_{J})F_{0}
. Since

\{\vec{\xi^{l}}\},\{\vec{\eta^{l}}\}\rightarrow\vec{0}

This implies
\psi_{p}(\xi^{l},\eta^{l},0)\in\exp(\mathbb{C}\tau_{J})F_{0}
and
\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\log(\xi^{l}_{j})}{2\pi i}N_{j}+\sum_{k\in K-J}\frac{\log(\eta^{l}_{k})}{2\pi i}N_{k}
always takes value in

regardless of the choice of branches of logarithm. Since

and
\tau_{K}\leq\tau_{J}

K\sim J\cup K
and
J\sim K
.

6.
Construction of the analytic structure

In this section, we will finish the construction purposed by Theorem
2.8
based on Theorem
5.1
.

Assume all ingredients provided by Theorem
5.1
are fixed. These include a finite cover
\overline{B}=\bigcup_{k}W_{k}
, and for every
k
, a local KNU extension
\overline{\Phi}_{k}:W_{k}\rightarrow V_{k}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{k}\backslash D_{\tau_{k}}
which is proper. Theorem
3.12
implies that the image of each
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
is complex analytic. We may assume every
\overline{\Phi}_{k}:W_{k}\rightarrow V_{k}
has connected fibers, this can always be achieved after applying a Stein factorization.

On each
k
, there is a complex equivalence relation on
W_{k}
defined by
W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k}\subset W_{k}\times W_{k}
. Since each
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
is a proper holomorphic map, the equivalence relation
W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k}
is proper holomorphic in the sense of
[
Gra83
]
.

Note that
\bigcup_{k}(W_{k}\times W_{k})\subset\overline{B}\times\overline{B}
is an open analytic subspace containing the diagonal.

Proposition 6.1
.

\bigcup_{k}(W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k})\subset\bigcup_{k}(W_{k}\times W_{k})\subset\overline{B}\times\overline{B}
gives a proper holomorphic equivalence relation on
\overline{B}
with connected fibers whose restriction on each
(W_{k}\times W_{k})
agrees with
W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k}
.

Proof.

For every
k
let
E_{k}^{\prime}:=W_{k}\times W_{k}
and
E_{k}:=W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k}
. We first show that for every
k,l
,
E_{k}^{\prime}\cap E_{l}=E_{l}^{\prime}\cap E_{k}
. Since for any
(a,b)\in E_{k}^{\prime}
,
(a,b)\in E_{k}
if and only if
a,b
lie in the same fiber of
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
, it suffices to show that on
W_{k}\cap W_{l}
, the fibers of
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
and
\Phi_{l}^{\mathrm{KNU}}
agree. This is exactly (2) and (4) of Theorem
5.1
by our assumption that all
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
have connected fibers. The proper and holomorphic conditions follow from the fact that both conditions are locally around a fiber of
\bigcup_{k}(W_{k}\times_{V_{k}}W_{k})\subset\overline{B}
, which is consistent with the fibers for each
E_{k}\subset E_{k}^{\prime}
.
∎

Denote
E\subset\overline{B}\times\overline{B}
as the resulting proper holomorphic equivalence relation by Proposition
6.1
, and let
\overline{\wp}:=\overline{B}/\sim_{E}
as the quotient space and let
q:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
be the quotient map. By
[
Gra83
, Theorem 2]
,
\overline{\wp}
admits a structure of complex analytic space. More precisely, the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\wp}}
of
\overline{\wp}
is given by pushing forward the analytic germs of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{B}}
constant on every
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
-fiber.

Lemma 6.2
.

E^{*}:=E\cap(B\times B)
gives the equivalence relation on
B
defining the (Stein factorization of) period map
\Phi^{\prime}:B\rightarrow\wp^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show
\Phi^{\prime}
has the same fibers as
E
. Take a fiber
\zeta
of
\Phi^{\prime}
, then
\zeta\subset B
is connected, compact and hence closed in
\overline{B}
. For any
k
such that
U_{k}\cap\zeta\neq\emptyset
, since the period map
\Phi_{k}:U_{k}\rightarrow\Gamma_{k}\backslash D
is a lift of
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
when restricted to
U_{k}
,
\zeta_{k}:=\zeta\cap U_{k}
is contained in a unique fiber of
\Phi_{k}
. But by the construction of Theorem
5.1
, the fibers of
\Phi_{k}
are compact and contained in
U_{k}
, this means
\zeta_{k}\cap\partial U_{k}=\emptyset
, hence we must have
\zeta\subset U_{k}
. This implies
\zeta
is a fiber of
\Phi^{\prime}
if and only if it is a fiber of some
\Phi_{k}
and hence a fiber of
E
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\zeta\subset Z_{I}^{*}\subset\overline{B}
be a connected analytic subvariety. Then
\zeta
is contained in a fiber of
E
if and only if for all
b\in\zeta
the nilpotent orbit class of
(\tau_{I},F_{b})
given by Corollary
3.4
and the CMCX
\mathcal{T}
is constant.

Proof.

If
\zeta
is contained in a fiber of
E
, it is also contained in the fiber of some
\overline{\Phi}_{k}:W_{k}\rightarrow V_{k}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{k}\backslash D_{\tau_{k}}
. On the other hand, if over
\zeta
the nilpotent orbit class
(\tau_{I},F_{b})
is constant,
\zeta\cap W_{k}
is contained in a
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
-fiber for any
k
, hence
\overline{\zeta}
must be contained in every
W_{k}
with
W_{k}\cap\zeta\neq\emptyset
. In this case
\zeta
is contained in a fiber of
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
and hence a fiber of
E
.
∎

To sum up, the space
\overline{\wp}
and the quotient map
q:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
satisfy the requirement of Theorem
2.8
, thus
q=\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
is the purposed completed period map of the Stein factorization
B\xrightarrow{\Phi^{\prime}}\wp^{\prime}\rightarrow\wp\subset\Gamma\backslash D
.

7.
Examples of projective completions

The purpose of this section is to provide some examples in the surface case supporting Conjecture
2.11
.

Let us assume
B
is a quasi-projective surface. The
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
gives the period map
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
. In this section, we do not assume the monodromy group
\Gamma
is neat as this is the most common case in practice. Moreover, we fix a projective completion
\overline{B}
of
B
which we do not assume to be a SNC completion. Let
B^{\circ}\subset\overline{B}
be the complement of codimension-
2
strata. The main result of this section is:

Proposition 7.1
.

If there is no CLF (Definition
5.8
) of positive dimension in
B^{\circ}
exist, then we may choose the completed period map
\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
constructed by Theorem
2.8
to lie in the category of projective varieties.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:B^{\prime}\rightarrow B
be a finite etále morphism such that the lift
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
has neat monodromy group
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq\Gamma
. We may find a projective completion
\overline{\varphi}:\overline{B^{\prime}}\rightarrow\overline{B}
of
\varphi
which is a finite morphism. This can be done by normalizing
\overline{B}
in the function field of
B^{\prime}
. Blowing up
\overline{B^{\prime}}
to some
\overline{B}
such that
\overline{B}
is an SNC completion of
B^{\prime}
. Through the composition
\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{B^{\prime}}\rightarrow\overline{B}
, any positive-dimensional CLF in
\overline{B}
must be mapped to a codimensional-
2
point in
\overline{B^{\prime}}
according to the assumption. Therefore, the completed period map
\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
is described by contracting a (possibly empty) set of exceptional divisors of
\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{B^{\prime}}
. As a consequence,
\overline{\wp}
acquires its projective structure from some intermediate variety of
\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{B^{\prime}}
.
∎

This criteria is powerful in practice: It avoids the steps finding a lift with neat monodromy as well as an SNC completion of
B
.

7.1.
Hosono–Takagi’s example

We look at the family of Calabi-Yau threefolds studied by
[
HT14
]
,
[
HT18
]
,
[
Den22
]
and
[
DR23
]
. The period map used here is the one introduced by
[
HT14
]
and
[
HT18
]
, which is a period map of type
(1,2,2,1)
as follows:

(7.1)

\Phi_{0}:\mathbb{P}^{2}\backslash\mathrm{Dis}=:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D.

The discriminant locus is given by:

(7.2)

\mathrm{Dis}:=D_{0}\cup D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup C

where
D_{i}
are coordinate divisors in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and
C
is an irreducible quintic with
6
self-intersection nodes and
1
tangent point of order
5
with each coordinate divisor. The picture of this base can be found at
[
HT14
, Fig. 6.1]
. The precise monodromy group
\Gamma
is not known, but from
[
Den22
, Sec. 7]
we know the algebraic monodromy group
\overline{\Gamma}^{\mathbb{Q}}
is
\mathrm{Sp}(6,\mathbb{Q})
. In
[
HT18
, Sec. 4]
it is shown that
\Gamma
is not neat.

By blowing up
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at each tangent point for
5
times, we get a smooth projective completion
\overline{B}
of
B
with SNC boundary divisors.
We mark all local monodromy nilpotent cones as follows:

(1)

Let
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}
be the (
2
-dimensional) local monodromy nilpotent cones around the intersection of each pair of coordinate divisors;

(2)

Let
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
be the local monodromy nilpotent cones obtained by blowing-up each of the fifth tangent point. As
[
Den22
, Sec. 6.1]
shows, the blow-up process annihilates the order-
5
semisimple part of the monodromy operators around
C
and leave the unipotent part invariant;

(3)

Let
\tau_{j},\ j=1,...,6
be the (
2
-dimensional) local monodromy nilpotent cones obtained from all self-intersection nodes of
C
.

(4)

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the remaining cones around the exceptional divisors.

By computational results in
[
HT14
]
and
[
HT18
]
, all
2
-dimensional local monodromy nilpotent orbits and their Hodge degeneration types are listed as follows. Here we use the LMHS types defined by
[
KPR19
, Example 5.8]
.

(1)

\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}
are all of the type
\langle\mathrm{IV}_{2}|\mathrm{IV}_{2}|\mathrm{IV}_{2}\rangle
;

(2)

\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are all of the type

\langle\mathrm{IV}_{2}|\mathrm{IV}_{2}|\mathrm{I}_{1}\rangle
;

(3)

\tau_{j},\ 1\leq j\leq 6
are all of the type

\langle\mathrm{I}_{1}|\mathrm{I}_{2}|\mathrm{I}_{1}\rangle
.

(4)

Any (
1
-dimensional) cone in
\mathcal{E}
is of the type

\langle\mathrm{I}_{1}|\mathrm{I}_{1}|\mathrm{I}_{1}\rangle
.

Lemma 7.2
.

In
\overline{B}
the only possible positive-dimensional CLF are the exceptional divisors of
\overline{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof.

There are no curves in
B
contracted by
\Phi_{0}
as in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
the closure of any such curve must intersect the boundary divisor
\mathrm{Dis}
while no points on
\mathrm{Dis}
give the trivial monodromy. The fact that nilpotent cones of type (1) and (3) listed above are of two-dimensional implies non of
D_{0},D_{1},D_{2}
or
C
can be a
1
-dimensional CLF.
∎

As a consequence, if we choose
\mathbb{P}^{2}
instead of
\overline{B}
to be the projective completion of
B
, the assumption of Proposition
7.1
is met and we may conclude the period map
\Phi_{0}
(after being lifted to a finite etále cover) admits a projective completion by Theorem
2.8
.

7.2.
More examples

In the paper
[

8.
Algebraicity of weakly special subvarieties

In this section we introduce another application of our main theorem. It is well-known that weakly special subvarieties associated to the period mapping
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
are algebraic subvarieties of
B
. Its proof uses the definability of period mappings in o-minimal geometry and functional transcendence
[
BKT20
]
,
[
BBT22
]
. We will give a new proof of this result without using o-minimal geometry.

Regarding the necessary backgrounds, we refer readers to
[
KO21
]
or
[
Kli22
]
for a comprehensive overview.

8.1.
Weakly special subvarieties

Let
(\mathbb{G},D)
be the Hodge datum associated to the
\mathbb{Z}
-PVHS
\mathbb{V}\rightarrow B
and the period map
\Phi:B\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
. A weak Hodge datum
(\mathbb{H},M)
is given by a weak Mumford-Tate subdomain
M\leq D
and its automorphism group
\mathbb{H}\leq\mathbb{G}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\Gamma_{H}:=\Gamma\cap H:=\mathbb{H_{\mathbb{Q}}}
, we have a natural embedding of Hodge varieties
\Gamma_{H}\backslash M\subset\Gamma\backslash D
.

Definition 8.1
.

An irreducible analytic component of
\Phi^{-1}(\Gamma_{H}\backslash M)
in
B
is called a weakly special subvariety with Hodge datum
(\mathbb{H},M)
.

A well-known result in Hodge theory and functional transcendence is:

Theorem 8.2
.

[
KO21
, Prop. 4.8]

\Phi^{-1}(\Gamma_{H}\backslash M)
is an algebraic subvariety of
B
. In particular, weakly special subvarieties of
B
are algebraic.

The proof uses the definability of period mappings
[
BKT20
]
and o-minimal geometry
[
BBT22
]
. We will give another proof of this result without using o-minimal methods. The main idea is to extend the analytic condition on
B
defining the weakly special subvarieties to
\overline{B}
over which a extended period map provided by Theorem
2.8
exists.

8.2.
The period torsors

We first introduce the construction of period torsors following
[
KU08
, Chap. 3]
. Fix a monodromy cone
\sigma\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Q}}
whose integral monoidal structure is given by
\exp(\sigma_{\mathbb{C}})\cap G_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
\Gamma(\sigma)\leq\Gamma
be the submonoid consists of elements whose logarithms lie in
\sigma
. Let
\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma}
be the affine toric variety associated with
\sigma
and
\mathrm{torus}_{\sigma}
be the torus embedding. We have the natural identification:

(8.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma(\sigma)^{\vee},\mathbb{C}),

(8.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{torus}_{\sigma}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma(\sigma)^{\vee\mathrm{gp}},\mathbb{C}^{*})\simeq C^{*}\otimes\Gamma_{\sigma}

where the dual and “Hom” are taken in the category of multiplicative monoids. Define

(8.3)

\displaystyle\check{E}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma}\times\check{D}

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\{(q,F)\in\check{E}_{\sigma}\ |\ NF^{\bullet}\subset F^{\bullet-1}\ \forall N\in\sigma(q)\}

\displaystyle E_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\{(q,F)\in\tilde{E}_{\sigma}\ |\ (\sigma(q),\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{log}_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}q)F)\hbox{ is a }\sigma(q)-\hbox{nilpotent orbit }\}

Here
\sigma(q)\leq\sigma
is the face corresponds to the torus orbit containing
q
. By
[
KU08
, Chap. 3]
,
\check{E}_{\sigma}
admits a natural fs logarithmic analytic structure from
\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma}
via the projection map, and
\tilde{E}_{\sigma}
and
E_{\sigma}
admits the structure of logarithmic manifolds.

By the definition of logarithmic manifolds
[
KU08
, Definition 3.5.7]
,
\tilde{E}_{\sigma}
and
E_{\sigma}
is locally charted by the vanishing loci of some finitely-generated ideal
\mathcal{L}
of the sheaf of logarithmic
1
-forms
\omega^{1}_{\check{E}_{\sigma}}
over
\check{E}_{\sigma}
.

By
[
KU08
, Theorem 3.5.10]
, this ideal sheaf
\mathcal{L}
locally defining
\tilde{E}_{\sigma}
and
E_{\sigma}
is described as follows: Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be the universal Hodge filtration of
\mathcal{O}_{\check{D}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}V_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\check{D}
,
\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{O}_{\check{D}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathcal{G}^{-1}:=\{X\in\mathcal{G},\ X\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\bullet-1}\}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the pullback of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{-1}
to
\tilde{E}_{\sigma}
via the projection map. There is a canonical projection morphism
\eta:\mathcal{O}_{\check{E}_{\sigma}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
giving a global section of
(\mathcal{O}_{\check{E}_{\sigma}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma_{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{P}
which is isomorphic to
\omega^{1}_{\check{E}_{\sigma}}\otimes\mathcal{P}
. Therefore, the ideal
\mathcal{L}\subset\omega^{1}_{\check{E}_{\sigma}}
is generated by the coordinates of
\eta
under local frames of
\mathcal{P}
.

Consider the map:

(8.4)

\Theta_{\sigma}:E_{\sigma}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma},\ (q,F)\rightarrow(\sigma(q),\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{log}_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}q)F)

which is the quotient of the action:

(8.5)

a\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}:E_{\sigma}\rightarrow E_{\sigma},\ (a,(q,F))\rightarrow(\mathbb{e}(a)q,\exp(-a)F)

where the map
\mathbb{e}(a)
is defined by

(8.6)

\mathbb{e}(z\log(\gamma))=\exp(2\pi iz)\otimes\gamma\in\mathrm{torus}_{\sigma}.

This realizes
E_{\sigma}
as a
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
-torsor over
\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
in the category of logarithmic manifolds by
[
KU08
, Section 7.2]
.

According to
[
KU08
, Section 7.3.5]
, for
x:=(q,F)\in E_{\sigma}\subset\check{E}_{\sigma}
, the structure of logarithmic manifold on
\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
around
\Theta_{\sigma}(x)
is described as follows: Let
\check{A}_{x}\subset\check{E}_{\sigma}
be an analytic variety through
x
such that:

(1)

The projection of
\check{A}_{x}
to
\mathrm{toric_{\sigma}}
is an open subvariety
U_{0}
of
\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma(q)}
.

(2)

The projection of
\check{A}_{x}
to
\check{D}
is a subvariety
U_{1}
of
\check{D}
intersecting
\exp(\sigma(q)_{\mathbb{C}})F
transversely at
F
, and
\mathrm{dim}(U_{1})=\mathrm{dim}(\check{D})-\mathrm{dim}(\sigma(q))
.

(3)

\check{A}_{x}\simeq U_{0}\times U_{1}
.

Let
A_{x}:=\check{A}_{x}\cap E_{\sigma}
, then
A_{x}\subset E_{\sigma}
acquires a structure of logarithmic submanifold of
E_{\sigma}
, and by
[
KU08
, Lemma 7.3.3]
, We may associate the logarithmic manifold structure on
\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
by regarding
A_{x}
as a chart around
\Theta_{\sigma}(x)
.

Remark 8.3
.

The construction of period torsor has been generalized to the Mumford-Tate domain setting in
[
KP16
]
which is almost identical to the ambient period domain case in
[
KU08
]
. We remark that similar construction also applies to the weak Mumford-Tate domain setting.

8.3.
The relative period torsors

For the fixed weak Hodge datum
(\mathbb{H},M)
of
(\mathbb{G},D)
. Denote
\mathfrak{h}:=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{H})
which is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}:=\sigma\cap\mathfrak{h}
. Define

(8.7)

\check{E}_{\sigma,M}:=\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}}\times\check{M}

where
\check{M}
is the compact dual of
M
.
\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
may be realized as an algebraic subvariety of
\check{E}_{\sigma}
via the embedding of cones
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}\hookrightarrow\sigma
. Let
\tilde{E}_{\sigma,M}:=\tilde{E}_{\sigma}\cap\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
and
E_{\sigma,M}:=E_{\sigma}\cap\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
. Endow each space with the subspace topology.

Endow
\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
the fs logarithmic analytic structure from restricting the one on
\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
. This is different from the structure on
\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
as an independent period torsor. Let
\mathfrak{M}:=\Theta_{\sigma}(E_{\sigma,M})\subset\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
. As a consequence,
\tilde{E}_{\sigma,M}
(resp.
E_{\sigma,M}
) is a logarithmic submanifold of
\tilde{E}_{\sigma}
(resp.
E_{\sigma}
). Denote
\Theta_{\sigma,M}
as the restriction of
\Theta_{\sigma}
on
E_{\sigma,M}
.

Proposition 8.4
.

The map
\Theta_{\sigma,M}:E_{\sigma,M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
is a
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
-torsor of logarithmic manifolds. In particular,
\mathfrak{M}
is a logarithmic submanifold of
\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Our proof relies on
[
KU08
, Lemma 7.3.3]
. To apply the lemma, we need to show
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
acts properly and freely on
E_{\sigma,M}
. Moreover, we need to show for any
x\in E_{\sigma,M}
, there exists a logarithmic submanifold
B_{x}\subset E_{\sigma,M}
passing through
x
, a neighborhood
U
of
0\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
such that the group action map
U\times B_{x}\rightarrow E_{\sigma,M}
is an isomorphism onto its image.

We first show
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
acts freely on the fiber of
\Theta_{\sigma,M}
. From the result for
\Theta_{\sigma}:E_{\sigma}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
, it is enough to show on the set-theoretical level that for
q\in\mathfrak{M}
, the fiber
\Theta^{-1}_{\sigma,M}(q)\subset E_{\sigma,M}
is a
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
-orbit. Fix
(q,F)\in E_{\sigma,M}
, for any
a\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
, by Equation (
8.5
) it is enough to show
(\mathbb{e}(a)q,\exp(-a)F)\in E_{\sigma,M}
if and only if
a\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
. For the first factor, note that
\mathbb{e}(a)q\in\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}}
if and only if
\mathbb{e}(a)\in\mathrm{torus}_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}}\simeq C^{*}\otimes\Gamma_{\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}}
if and only if
a\in\sigma_{\mathfrak{h}}
. For the second factor, note that the natural map
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow T_{\check{D}}F
is an injection, hence
\exp(-a)F\in M
if and only if
a\in T_{\check{M}}F\cap\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}=\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
.

Next we show the properness of the
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
-action. By
[
KU08
, Lemma 7.2.7]
, it is enough to show for any directed families
\{(q_{\lambda},F_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset E_{\sigma,M}
and
\{a_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
such that both

\{(q_{\lambda},F_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
\{a_{\lambda}(q_{\lambda},F_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converge in
E_{\sigma,M}
,
\{a_{\lambda}\}
must converge in
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
. This immediately follows from the facts that
\{a_{\lambda}\}
converges in
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
because of the proper action of
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
on
E_{\sigma}
, and
\sigma_{\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{C}}
is a closed subspace of
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Finally, similar to the ideas used in
[
KU08
, Section 7.3.5]
, we may choose
x=(q,F)\in\check{B}_{x}\subset\check{E}_{\sigma,M}
such that
\check{B}_{x}\simeq B_{1}\times B_{2}
, where
B_{1}
is an open neighborhood of
q
in
\mathrm{toric}_{\sigma(q)\cap\mathfrak{h}}
, and
F\in B_{2}\subset M
intersects
\exp((\sigma(q)\cap\mathfrak{h})_{\mathbb{C}})F
transversely at
F
and with complementary dimension in
\check{M}
. Let
B_{x}:=\tilde{B}_{x}\cap E_{\sigma,M}
. From the arguments we have in the last section for
\tilde{A}_{x}
and
A_{x}
, this
B_{x}\subset E_{\sigma,M}
satisfies the requirement. More precisely, the inclusions
\check{B}_{x}\subset\check{A}_{x}
and
B_{x}\subset A_{x}
realize
\mathfrak{M}
as a logarithmic submanifold of
\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash D_{\sigma}
. The proof is complete.
∎

8.4.
Proof of the algebraicity result

We are ready to prove Theorem
8.2
. Fix a completion
\overline{\Phi}:\overline{B}\rightarrow\overline{\wp}
given by Theorem
2.8
. Once we can show any weakly special subvariety
S\subset B
with Hodge datum
(\mathbb{H},M)
can be extended to an analytic subvariety of
\overline{B}
, Serre’s GAGA theorem will grant the algebraicity of
S
.

Take the open cover
\overline{B}=\bigcup_{k}W_{k}
given by Theorem
5.1
. The restriction of
\overline{\Phi}
on each
W_{k}
can be lifted to the local KNU maps
\overline{\Phi}_{k}:W_{k}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\tau_{k}}\backslash D_{\tau_{k}}
for some monodromy cone
\tau_{k}\subset\mathfrak{g}
. Theorem
3.12
and
5.1
implies each
\overline{\Phi}_{k}(W_{k})
is a complex analytic space.

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{k}
be the image of
(\mathrm{toric}_{\tau_{k}\cap\mathfrak{h}}\times M)\cap E_{\tau_{k},M}
in
\Gamma_{\tau_{k}}\backslash D_{\tau_{k}}
via the torsor map (
8.4
). Let
x\in\mathfrak{M}_{k}\cap\overline{\Phi}_{k}(W_{k})
. By Proposition
8.4
, we may choose neighborhoods
x\in A_{x}\subset\Gamma_{\tau_{k}}\backslash D_{\tau_{k}}
,
x\in V_{x}\subset\overline{\Phi}_{k}(W_{k})
and
B_{x}:=A_{x}\cap\mathfrak{M}_{k}
such that
V_{x}\cap B_{x}
is an analytic subspace of
V_{x}\cap A_{x}
. This implies for each
k
,
\overline{\Phi}_{k}^{-1}(\mathfrak{M}_{k})
is an analytic subspace of
W_{k}
.

Finally, since each
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
has connected fibers, we may patch all
\overline{\Phi}_{k}^{-1}(\mathfrak{M}_{k})
to an analytic subvariety
S
of
\overline{B}
and hence must be algebraic by GAGA, and it is evident that
S\cap B=\Phi^{-1}(\Gamma_{H}\backslash M)
. The proof of Theorem
8.2
is complete.

9.
On the existence of CMCX

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
2.7
which will guarantee that Theorem
2.8
is valid in the full generality. Some combinatorial definitions and results need to be established before the proof.

9.1.
Hyperintersection

For any
I,J\subset\mathcal{I}
with
Z_{I},Z_{J}\neq\emptyset
, we choose points
b_{I}\in Z_{I}^{*},b_{J}\in Z_{J}^{*}
, and a directed path
\gamma\subset\hat{B}
starting at
b_{I}
and ending at
b_{J}
. In this section, we will define the hyperintersection of CMC’s
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma_{J}}
.

9.1.1.
Indexed path and scissors

Definition 9.1
.

The
\mathcal{I}
-index of the path
\gamma
is the sequence of strata
Z_{K}^{*}

\gamma
passes by. Denote the index sequence as
\mathrm{Ind}(\gamma)=\langle I=I_{0}\rightarrow I_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow I_{m}=J\rangle
.

Definition 9.2
.

We say
\gamma
has convex index seqeuence if for any subsequence
I_{l-1}\rightarrow I_{l}\rightarrow I_{l+1}
of
\mathrm{Ind}(\gamma)
, we have either
I_{l-1},I_{l+1}\leq I_{l}
or
I_{l-1},I_{l+1}\geq I_{l}
.

Note that for any
I_{l-1}\rightarrow I_{l}\rightarrow I_{l+1}
with
I_{l-1}\leq I_{l}\leq I_{l+1}
(or the other direction), we may rewrite it as
I_{l-1}\rightarrow I_{l-1}\rightarrow I_{l}\rightarrow I_{l}\rightarrow I_{l+1}
which satisfies the convex index sequence condition. Therefore, we may assume every path
\gamma
is indexed by convex sequence, written as

I
=
I_{0}
I_{1}
I_{2}
...
I_{m}
=
J

where a triple
I_{l-1}\rightarrow I_{l}\rightarrow I_{l+1}
with
I_{l}
lying above (resp. below)
I_{l-1}
and
I_{l+1}
means
I_{l-1},I_{l+1}\leq I_{l}
(resp.
I_{l-1},I_{l+1}\geq I_{l}
).

Definition 9.3
.

A triple
I_{l-1}\leq I_{l}\leq I_{l+1}
with
I_{l-1},I_{l+1}\leq I_{l}
is called a scissors.

Therefore, after adding paths of the form
I\rightarrow I
if necessary, we may also write the index sequence of
\gamma
as

\mathrm{Ind}(\gamma)=\langle S_{0}\rightarrow S_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow S_{k}\rangle

where
S_{i}
’s are all scissors passed by
\gamma
in order. We call this the scissors sequence of
\gamma
.

9.1.2.
Definition of hyperintersection

Given a path
\gamma
connecting
x_{I}
and
x_{J}
and with scissors sequence
\langle S_{0}\rightarrow S_{1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow S_{k}\rangle
, we will define the hyperintersection
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}
. Note that the hyperintersection does not depend on the choice of
b_{I}\in Z_{I}^{*}
and

Definition 9.4
.

The hyperintersection

(2)

and

appeared in the index sequence of

Remark 9.5
.

For the case when
\hat{B}
is simplicial corresponds to

(See

is a face of both

and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}

A direct consequence from the definition is:

Suppose

\gamma^{\prime}
is a path connecting the end point of

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}

9.1.3.
Finiteness of hyperintersection

Fix an index
I\subset\mathcal{I}
, then any hyperintersection
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}
may be seen as a subcone of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
. The main theorem for this section is:

Theorem 9.7
.

Running over all indices
J\subset\mathcal{I}
and all (finitely) indexed paths
\gamma\subset\hat{B}
connecting
Z_{I}^{*}
and
Z_{J}^{*}
, there are only finitely many different hyperintersections
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}
as subcones of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
.

Since there are only finitely many indices
I\subset\mathcal{I}
with
Z_{I}^{*}\neq\emptyset
, Theorem
9.7
directly follows from the following proposition:

Proposition 9.8
.

Consider two hyperintersections
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
. If
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
contains the same set of scissors, then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
as subcones of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
.

Proof.

Assume the scissors sequences of
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are:

\gamma:S_{1}\rightarrow S_{2}\rightarrow...\rightarrow S_{p};\ \gamma^{\prime}:T_{1}\rightarrow T_{2}\rightarrow...\rightarrow T_{q}.

By the assumption, there exists some
T_{r}
such that
S_{p}=T_{r}
. Choose an indexed path
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
connecting the endpoints of
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
and whose scissors sequence is:

\gamma^{\prime\prime}:S_{p}=T_{r}\rightarrow T_{r+1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow T_{q}.

Denote the composition of
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime\prime},(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}
as
\gamma_{0}
. Since every scissors contained in
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
is also contained in
\gamma
, by repeatedly applying Lemma
9.6
we have
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma_{0}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
. On the other hand,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma_{0}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}\cap_{(\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{-1}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\subset\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}\subset\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
. Same argument shows
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}\supset\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
, hence
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\cap_{\gamma^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{J^{\prime}}
.
∎

9.2.1.
Semicomplex and the canonical complexification

We introduce the concept of polyhedral semicomplex (or simply semicomplex) and its canonical complexification. Note that in this section we assume polyhedral cones are closed.

Definition 9.9

Let

(1)

(2)

For any

\sigma^{\circ}\cap\tau^{\circ}\neq\emptyset

\sigma=\tau

Any polyhedral complex is a semicomplex. Besides, a standard example of semicomplex is the following which will be used later.

of

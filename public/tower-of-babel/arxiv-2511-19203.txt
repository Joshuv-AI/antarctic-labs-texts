Another Billiard Problem
Sergey Bolotin, Dmitry Treschev

Abstract
Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a Riemannian manifold, Ω ⊂ M \Omega\subset M a domain with boundary Γ \Gamma , and ϕ \phi a smooth function such that ϕ | Ω > 0 \phi|_{\Omega}>0 , φ | Γ = 0 \varphi|_{\Gamma}=0 , and ∇ ϕ | Γ ≠ 0 \nabla\phi|_{\Gamma}\neq 0 . We study the geodesic flow of the metric G = g / ϕ G=g/\phi . The G G -distance from any point of Ω \Omega to Γ \Gamma is finite, hence the geodesic flow is incomplete. Regularization of the flow in a neighborhood of Γ \Gamma establishes a natural reflection law from Γ \Gamma . This leads to a certain (not quite standard) billiard problem in Ω \Omega .

2
Regularization

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth Riemannian manifold with the Riemannian metric

g=\|dx\|_{g}^{2}=\langle\mathcal{A}(x)dx,dx\rangle

and
\phi
a smooth function on
M
.
Then

\Gamma=\{\phi=0\}

We assume that

The operator

The geodesic flow of the metric

The geodesics are projections to

4

H(x,p_{x})=\phi(x)\|p_{x}\|_{g}^{2}=\phi(x)\langle\mathcal{A}^{-1}(x)p_{x},p_{x}\rangle,\qquad(x,p_{x})\in T^{*}\Omega,

3
The billiard map

The billiard map is the Poincaré first return map for the cross-section
\mathcal{S}_{h}
defined in (
2.3
)
or, more explicitly, (
2.5
). Since the first return maps corresponding to all
h>0
are conjugate via a homothety,
we set
h=1
and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Let
\Phi^{t}
be the phase flow of the regularized system.
Since
\mathcal{S}
is a section for the regularized flow, we can define a smooth Poincaré map
T:U\to\mathcal{S}
on an open set

U=\{z\in\mathcal{S}\mid\exists t>0:\Phi^{t}(z)\in\mathcal{S}\}.

By definition
T(z)=\Phi^{\tau}(z)
, where
\tau=\tau(z)
is the first return time.
The manifold
\mathcal{S}
is disconnected:

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{+}\cup\mathcal{S}^{-},\qquad\mathcal{S}^{\pm}=\{(q,p,\xi,\eta):\xi=0,\;\eta=\pm\sqrt{2/a(q,0)}\}\cong T^{*}\Gamma.

The Poincaré map takes
\mathcal{S}^{\pm}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\mp}
, and
both maps
T|_{\mathcal{S}^{\pm}}
define the same symplectic map

T:U\subset T^{*}\Gamma\to T^{*}\Gamma
, exactly as for the usual Birkhoff billiard.
Using the analogy with the billiard, we call
T
the billiard map.
It is symplectic with respect to the standard symplectic form on
T^{*}\Gamma
.

In contrast to the usual billiard, no convexity assumptions on the boundary are needed:
the Poincaré map is always smooth. In fact
\Omega
is convex with respect to the metric
G
.

Suppose the closure
\overline{\Omega}
is compact, then
\hat{\Omega}
is compact, and the regularized flow is complete.
Then for small
\varepsilon>0
the set
\cup_{-\varepsilon<t<\varepsilon}\Phi^{t}(\mathcal{S})
is open.
The Poincaré recurrence theorem implies that the map
T
is defined almost everywhere on
\mathcal{S}
,
so
U
is an open dense set.

If for some
z\in\mathcal{S}
the
\omega
-limit set of
\Phi^{t}(z)
is contained in
T^{*}\Omega
,
then
T(z)
is undefined at
z
. If there are no compact invariant sets for the geodesic
flow of the metric
G
in
\Omega
, then
U=\mathcal{S}
and
T:T^{*}\Gamma\to T^{*}\Gamma
is everywhere defined.

A sufficient condition for
T
to be globally defined is that
\Omega
is homeomorphic to a disk and
G
has negative sectional curvature:
in this case any geodesic of
G
starts and ends on
\Gamma
.

Example
. Suppose
n=2
and
g=|dx|^{2}
is Euclidean.
Then (see e.g.
[
12
]
) the Gauss curvature of
G=g/\phi
is given by

K=\frac{\phi}{2}\Delta\ln\phi=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\Delta\phi-\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{\phi}\Big).

Suppose
\phi
is concave (i.e.,
-\phi
is convex). Then
\Omega
is a disk and
\Delta\phi<0
. This implies
K<0
.
Hence the billiard map is everywhere defined on the cylinder
T^{*}\Gamma\cong S^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, and

T
is a twist map. This means that the map
T:(q,p)\mapsto(q^{+},p^{+})
satisfies

\partial q^{+}/\partial p>0
.

Twist symplectic self-maps of a cylinder form a popular object of studying.
In particular, they always have an infinite number of periodic
[
2
]
and quasi-periodic
[
7
]
trajectories.

Also for arbitrary
n
, if the Hessian
h_{\phi}
is negative definite,
the billiard map
T:T^{*}\Gamma\to T^{*}\Gamma
is everywhere defined.
Indeed, the sectional curvature of
G
corresponding to orthonormal vectors

is given by
[
12
]

K_{u,v}=\frac{1}{2}h_{\phi}(v)+\frac{1}{2}h_{\phi}(w)-\frac{\langle\nabla\phi,v\rangle^{2}}{2\phi^{2}}-\frac{\langle\nabla\phi,w\rangle^{2}}{2\phi^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{4\phi}<0.

In section
5
we present approximate formulas for the billiard map.

H(q,p,\xi,\eta)=\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}K(q,p,\xi^{2}/2),\qquad\omega=dp\wedge dq+d\eta\wedge d\xi.

(5.8)

5.2
Example

As the first approximation, consider the system with Hamiltonian (
5.7
), where
C
is a constant matrix.
The Hamiltonian equations have the form

\dot{q}=2sCp,\quad\dot{p}=0,\quad\dot{s}=2sp_{s},\quad\dot{p}_{s}=-p_{s}^{2}-\langle Cp,p\rangle.

Hence
p=\mathrm{const}
. If
p\neq 0
and
p_{s}(0)=0
, we obtain

q=q(0)+\frac{h}{k^{2}}\Big(t+\frac{\sin 2kt}{2k}\Big)Cp,\quad s=\frac{h}{k^{2}}\cos^{2}kt,\quad p_{s}=-k\tan kt,\qquad|t|<\frac{\pi}{2k},

(5.9)

where
k=\sqrt{\langle Cp,p\rangle}
and
H=h>0
is the energy.
Projections of these curves to the
(q,s)
-space are cycloids.
The corresponding billiard map
(q(-\frac{\pi}{2k}),p(-\frac{\pi}{2k}))\mapsto(q(\frac{\pi}{2k}),p(\frac{\pi}{2k}))
is

T(q,p)=(q+\pi h\langle Cp,p\rangle^{-3/2}Cp,p)\qquad p\neq 0.

(5.10)

5.3
Action-angle coordinates

Equations (
5.9
) determine, up to small errors, geodesics of the metric
G
which stay
in a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
for a long time. Next we study these geodesics with higher precision.
Since we fixed the energy
H=h>0
, trajectories staying near the boundary have large
\|p\|
.

Let
C_{0}(q)=C(q,0)
,
C_{1}(q)=\partial_{s}C(q,0)
, and

K_{0}(q,p)=\langle C_{0}(q)p,p\rangle,\quad K_{1}(q,p)=\langle C_{1}(q)p,p\rangle.

Up to now our equations were coordinate independent. Now we consider
q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
as a local coordinate in
\Gamma
,
then
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
We would like to introduce the action-angle variables
J,\varphi
in the
\xi,\eta
plane:

\xi=\sqrt{\frac{2J}{K_{0}^{1/2}}}\sin\varphi,\quad\eta=\sqrt{2JK_{0}^{1/2}}\cos\varphi.

To extend this change to a symplectic transformation of the whole phase space,
we use the generating function

S(q,P,\varphi,\eta)=\frac{\eta^{2}\tan\varphi}{2}K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,P)-\langle q,P\rangle.

The generating function
defines the symplectic transformation
(q,p,\xi,\eta)\mapsto(Q,P,\varphi,J)
via

-p\,dq+\xi\,d\eta+J\,d\varphi-Q\,dP=dS.

Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle J=S_{\varphi}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\cos^{2}\varphi}K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,P),\quad p=-S_{q}=P-\frac{\eta^{2}\tan\varphi}{2}\frac{\partial_{q}K_{0}(q,P)}{2K_{0}^{3/2}(q,P)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi=S_{\eta}=\eta\tan\varphi K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,P),\quad Q=-S_{P}=q-\frac{\eta^{2}\tan\varphi}{2}\frac{\partial_{P}K_{0}(q,P)}{2K_{0}^{3/2}(q,P)}.

Although
S
is singular at
\varphi=\pm\pi/2
, the change is smooth except at
P=0
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi=\frac{\sqrt{2J}\sin\varphi}{K_{0}^{1/4}(q,P)},\quad\eta=\sqrt{2J}K_{0}^{1/4}(q,P)\cos\varphi,

\displaystyle\displaystyle p=P-\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\partial_{q}K_{0}(q,P)}{K_{0}(q,P)},\quad Q=q-\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\partial_{P}K_{0}(q,P)}{K_{0}(q,P)}.

On the cross section
\xi=0
we have:
P=p
,
Q=q
.

The transformation is invertible provided
J/\|P\|
is small enough.
Note that on the fixed energy level
H=h>0
, we have
J\sim h\|P\|^{-1}
.
To obtain explicit formulas for the change, we solve the last equation for
q
assuming that
J
is small and
\|P\|
large:

q=Q+\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)}+O\Big(\frac{J^{2}}{\|P\|^{2}}\Big),\quad p=P-\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)}+O\Big(\frac{J^{2}}{\|P\|}\Big).

5.4
Regularized Hamiltonian

Let us rewrite the regularized Hamiltonian (
5.8
) in the new variables:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}K_{0}(q,P)+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\left(K(q,p,\xi^{2}/2)-K(q,P,0)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle JK_{0}^{1/2}(q,P)+\frac{J\sin^{2}\varphi}{K_{0}^{1/2}(q,P)}\left(K(q,p,\xi^{2}/2)-K(q,P,0)\right).

Let us find the main term and the first approximation:

\displaystyle K_{0}^{1/2}(q,P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(K_{0}(Q,P)+\Big\langle\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P),\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)}\Big\rangle+O(J^{2})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)+\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{8}\frac{\big\langle\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P),\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)\big\rangle}{K_{0}^{3/2}(Q,P)}+O\Big(\frac{J^{2}}{\|P\|}\Big).

Next

\displaystyle K(q,p,\xi^{2}/2)-K(q,P,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\sin^{2}\varphi\frac{\langle C_{1}(Q)P,P\rangle}{K_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)}-\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{2}\Big\langle C_{0}(Q)\frac{\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)},P\Big\rangle+O(J^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\sin^{2}\varphi\frac{K_{1}(Q,P)}{K_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)}-\frac{J\sin 2\varphi}{4}\frac{\langle\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P),\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)\rangle}{K_{0}(Q,P)}+O(J^{2}).

Finally we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle JK_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)+J^{2}\sin^{4}\varphi\frac{K_{1}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)}

(5.11)

\displaystyle+\frac{J^{2}\sin 4\varphi}{16}\frac{\langle\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P),\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)\rangle}{K_{0}^{3/2}(Q,P)}+O\Big(\frac{J^{3}}{\|P\|}\Big).

5.5
Averaging of the regularized Hamiltonian

On a fixed energy level
H=h>0
, we have
J\sim h\|P\|^{-1}
.
Hence
\|P\|^{-2}
plays the role of a small parameter in the one-frequency slow-fast system with Hamiltonian (
5.11
).
Here the angular variable
\varphi
is fast, while
Q
,
P/\|P\|
, and
J\|P\|
are slow variables. According to the averaging principle,
one needs to replace the Hamiltonian by
\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}H\,d\varphi
and drop higher order terms in
\|P\|^{-1}
. More precisely, we have:

Proposition 5.1
.

For small
\varepsilon>0
and
\|P\|>\varepsilon^{-1}
the system with Hamiltonian (
5.11
) is approximated by the system with the Hamiltonian

\tilde{H}=JK_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)+\frac{3J^{2}}{8}\frac{K_{1}(Q,P)}{K_{0}(Q,P)}.

This means the following. Let
Q(t),P(t),\varphi(t),J(t)
and
\tilde{Q}(t),\tilde{P}(t),\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{J}(t)
be solutions of systems with Hamiltonians
H
and
\tilde{H}
respectively with the same initial condition

Q_{0},P_{0},\varphi_{0},J_{0}
. Then

\|Q(t)-\tilde{Q}(t)\|<\frac{c_{1}}{\|P_{0}\|^{2}},\quad\frac{\|P(t)-\tilde{P}(t)\|}{\|P_{0}\|}<\frac{c_{1}}{\|P_{0}\|^{2}},\quad|J(t)-\tilde{J}(t)|\|P_{0}\|<\frac{c_{1}}{\|P_{0}\|^{2}}

on a time interval
|t|<c_{2}\|P\|^{2}
.

5.6
Isoenergetic reduction

In this section we compute approximately the billiard map.
Since time parametrization is not important, we fix the energy level
H=1
and use
\varphi
as a new time variable.
Solving the equation
H=1
for
J
, we obtain
-J=F(Q,P,\varphi)
, where

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K_{0}^{-1/2}(Q,P)+R(Q,P,\varphi)+O(\|P\|^{-5}),

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{4}\varphi\frac{K_{1}(Q,P)}{K_{0}^{5/2}(Q,P)}+\frac{\sin 4\varphi}{16}\frac{\langle\partial_{Q}K_{0}(Q,P),\partial_{P}K_{0}(Q,P)\rangle}{K_{0}^{3}(Q,P)}.

Then

(P\,dQ+J\,d\varphi)\big|_{\{H=1\}}=P\,dQ-F\,d\varphi.

Thus
F
is the new Hamiltonian, and
\varphi\bmod\pi
is the new time. Solutions
(Q(\varphi),P(\varphi))
of the system with the Hamiltonian

F(Q,P,\varphi)
define trajectories

(Q(\varphi),P(\varphi),\varphi,J(\varphi)),\qquad J(\varphi)=-F(Q(\varphi),P(\varphi),\varphi),

of our Hamiltonian system. The time parametrization is recovered by

\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=K_{0}^{1/2}(Q,P)+O(\|P\|^{-3}).

For large
\|P\|>\varepsilon^{-1}
, the variables
Q
and
P/\|P\|
are slow, so the averaging principle works.
Let

\overline{R}(Q,P)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}R(Q,P,\varphi)\,d\varphi.

The symplectic transformation
(Q,P)\mapsto(q,p)
with the generating function

S(Q,p,\varphi)=\langle Q,p\rangle+\int_{0}^{\varphi}(R(Q,p,\psi)-\overline{R}(Q,p,\psi))\,d\psi,

P=S_{Q}=p+O(\|p\|^{-3}),\quad q=S_{p}=Q+O(\|p\|^{-2}),

transforms the Hamiltonian
F
to the normal form

\mathcal{F}(q,p,\varphi)=N(q,p)+O(\|p\|^{-5}),\qquad N(q,p)=-K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,p)+\overline{R}(q,p).

For
\varphi=0\bmod\pi
the transformation is identical. We obtain:

Proposition 5.2
.

For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
the billiard map
T:U\subset T^{*}\Gamma\to T^{*}\Gamma
is well defined on the set

U=\{(q,p)\in T^{*}\Gamma:\|p\|\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\}

and it is close to the
\pi
-time map
\Phi_{N}^{\pi}
of the flow
\Phi_{N}^{\varphi}
of the system with the Hamiltonian

N(q,p)=-K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,p)+\frac{3}{8}\frac{K_{1}(q,p)}{K_{0}^{5/2}(q,p)}=-K_{0}^{-1/2}(q,p)+O(\|p\|^{-3}).

(5.12)

More precisely,

T(q,p)=\Phi_{N}^{\pi}(q,p)+\big(O(\|p\|^{-4}),O(\|p\|^{-3})\big).

In the first approximation
T
is given by (
5.10
).
We obtain a discrete version of Proposition
5.1
.

Corollary 5.1
.

For
m\leq c\|p\|^{2}
we have

T^{m}(q,p)=\Phi_{N}^{\pi m}(q,p)+\big(O(\|p\|^{-2}),O(\|p\|^{-3})\big).

In particular, for trajectories staying near the boundary,
N
is an approximate adiabatic invariant:

N\circ T^{m}=N+O(\|p\|^{-3}),\qquad m\leq c\|p\|^{2}.

Recall that that
K_{0}(q,p)=\|\nabla\phi(q)\|^{2}\|p\|^{2}
is the Hamiltonian describing the geodesic flow of the Riemannian metric

g_{0}=\|\nabla\phi(q)\|^{-2}\|dq\|^{2}
, where the norm and the gradient are defined by the metric
g
.
The Hamiltonian vector field with Hamiltonian
N
is

v_{N}=\frac{1}{2}K_{0}^{-1/2}\left(\Big(1-\frac{15}{8}\frac{K_{1}}{K_{0}^{2}}\Big)v_{K_{0}}+\frac{3}{4K_{0}}v_{K_{1}}\right).

For large
\|p\|\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
we have
K_{0}\approx c=\mathrm{const}\sim\varepsilon^{-2}
,
and so trajectories of the billiard map are
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
-shadowing
trajectories of the geodesic flow in
T^{*}\Gamma
with the quadratic in
p
Hamiltonian
K_{0}+\frac{3}{4c}K_{1}
.

6
Lagrangian tori

6.1
Analog of Lazutkin’s theorem

Let
n=2
, then
\Gamma
is an oriented closed curve. Let
L
be the total length of
\Gamma
in the metric
g_{0}

and
q\bmod L
the arc length parameter on
\Gamma
:

L=\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{\|dx\|}{\|\nabla\phi(x)\|},\qquad q=\int\frac{\|dx\|}{\|\nabla\phi(x)\|}.

The second integral is taken along an arc of
\Gamma
joining a fixed point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
with a given point.
Then in (
5.12
) we have

K_{0}=p^{2},\quad K_{1}=k(q)p^{2},\quad N(q,p)=-|p|^{-1}+\frac{3}{8}k(q)|p|^{-3},

where
k(q)
is the curvature of
\Gamma
with respect to the metric
\tilde{g}
.

For definiteness let
p>0
. The billiard map
T(q,p)=(q_{+},p_{+})
,
q\in{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}\bmod L
,
is the time-
\pi
shift
6
6

6

The role of time is played by the variable
\varphi
.

along solutions of the system with the
\pi
-periodic in
\varphi
Hamiltonian

\mathcal{F}(q,p,\varphi)=N(q,p)+O(p^{-5})
. Hence
T

is given by

q_{+}=q+\pi p^{-2}-\frac{9\pi}{8}k(q)p^{-4}+O(p^{-6}),\quad p_{+}=p-\frac{3\pi}{8}k^{\prime}(q)p^{-3}+O(p^{-5}).

This is an exact nearly integrable twist map, so KAM-theory applies.
The unperturbed integrable Hamiltonian
\tilde{F}
is nondegenerate.
This means that
\nu^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\neq 0
, where
\nu(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-2}+O(\varepsilon^{-4})

is the frequency on the unperturbed invariant circle
\{N=\varepsilon\}
.

By the standard KAM-theory (see e.g.
[
1
,
8
]
), we obtain:

Proposition 6.1
.

The billiard map has a Cantor family of 1-dimensional invariant tori
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}\subset{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}=\{(q,p):q\bmod L,\;p^{-1}=\varepsilon+\frac{3}{8}k(q)\varepsilon^{3}+O(\varepsilon^{5})\},

where
\varepsilon
belongs to a Cantor set with density point
0
.

The gaps in the Cantor set are exponentially small in
\varepsilon
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
This statement is an analog of the Lazutkin theorem
[
5
]
(see also generalizations in
[
6
]
)
for traditional Birkhoff billiards with smooth strictly convex boundary.

Any torus
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
is obtained as an intersection of a 2-dimensional invariant torus

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\subset T^{*}\hat{\Omega}
of the regularized system with the cross section
\mathcal{S}
.
The action variables
I_{i}=\oint_{\gamma_{i}}p_{x}\,dx
on
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\varepsilon}
are

I_{1}=\pi\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{3}),\quad I_{2}=\frac{L}{\varepsilon}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\int_{0}^{L}k(q)\,dq+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Similar to Lazutkin’s theorem, projections of trajectories in
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{2}
to
\Omega
, i.e. corresponding billiard trajectories in
\Omega
,
are tangent to the Cantor family of caustics

\{x\in\Omega:\rho(x,\Gamma)=\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})\}.

6.2
Tori around tori

A positive measure of Lagrangian tori appears near any non-degenerate reducible Diophantine invariant torus, see e.g.
[
1
]
.
For example, if the periodic orbit from Proposition
4.3
is elliptic, then generically it is surrounded by a Cantorian family of Lagrangian tori.
Or, if there is a nondegenerate (in the KAM sense) elliptic closed geodesic of the metric
g_{0}
on the boundary
\Gamma
,
then, as in
[
6
]
, using Proposition
5.2
, we will obtain a Cantor family of
(n-1)
-dimensional Lagrangian tori
in
T^{*}\Gamma
for the billiard map, and hence a family of
n
-dimensional invariant Lagrangian tori for the regularized flow in
T^{*}\hat{\Omega}
.

7
Integrable cases

There are two simple situations when the regularized system is completely integrable, both in the case
M={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
For
n=2
they were studied in
[
3
,
4
]
.

a
. Suppose
g
and
\phi
are
SO(n)
-invariant.
Then
\Omega
is an
n
-dimensional ball with center at the origin or a domain between two concentric
(n-1)
-spheres.
The corresponding regularized geodesic flow is completely integrable due to the existence of the algebra
of linear in momentum Noether integrals.

b
. Suppose
g=\sum g_{i}(x_{i})\,dx_{i}^{2}
. Let
\phi_{i}:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be smooth functions
such that
\phi_{i}>0
on
(-1,1)
,
\phi_{i}(\pm 1)=0
, and
\phi^{\prime}_{i}(\pm 1)\neq 0
. Set
\chi_{i}=1/\phi_{i}
and

\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(\chi_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+\chi_{n}(x_{n}))^{-1}.

Then
\phi>0
on
\Omega=(-1,1)^{n}
and
\phi|_{\Gamma}=0
. We have

G=\frac{g}{\phi}=(\chi_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+\chi_{n}(x_{n}))(g_{1}(x_{1})\,dx_{1}^{2}+\dots+g_{n}(x_{n})\,dx_{n}^{2}).

In the corresponding Hamiltonian the variables separate. Hence the regularized geodesic flow is completely integrable.
In this case
\Omega=(-1,1)^{n}
is an
n
-dimensional cube.
The billiard map is not defined at the corner singularities, although they are probably regularizable.

Other complete integrability cases are obtained taking direct products of these systems.
It will be interesting to find less evident integrable examples.

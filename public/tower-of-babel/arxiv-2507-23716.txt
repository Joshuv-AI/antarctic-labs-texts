Abstract
Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) has potential for a scientific revolution through numerous practical applications like finding better medicines, batteries, materials, catalysts etc. Many QPE algorithms use the Hadamard test to estimate ⟨ ψ | U k | ψ ⟩ \langle\psi|U^{k}|\psi\rangle for a large integer k k for an efficiently preparable initial state | ψ ⟩ |\psi\rangle and an efficiently implementable unitary operator U U . The Hadamard test is hard to implement because it requires controlled applications of U k U^{k} which increase the circuit depth T max T_{\rm max} by a factor of 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(n) where n n is the system size. But the total run time T tot T_{\rm tot} of the Hadamard test scales only as 𝒪 ⁡ ( k / ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(k/\epsilon^{2}) where ϵ \epsilon is the desired accuracy of estimation. Recently, a Sequential Hadamard test (SHT) was proposed (arXiv:2506.18765) which requires controlled application of U U only, improving T max T_{\rm max} by a factor of 𝒪 ⁡ ( k ) \mathcal{O}(k) . But the bottleneck is that T tot T_{\rm tot} of SHT scales as 𝒪 ⁡ ( k 3 ​ ϵ − 2 ​ r min − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(k^{3}\epsilon^{-2}r_{\rm min}^{-2}) where r min r_{\rm min} is the minimum value of | ⟨ ψ | U k ′ | ψ ⟩ | |\langle\psi|U^{k^{\prime}}|\psi\rangle| among all integers k ′ ≤ k k^{\prime}\leq k . Typically r min r_{\rm min} is exponentially low and SHT becomes too slow. We present a new algorithm, the SANDWICH test to address this bottleneck. Our algorithm uses efficient preparation of the initial state | ψ ⟩ |\psi\rangle to efficiently implement the SPROTIS operator R ψ ϕ R_{\psi}^{\phi} where SPROTIS stands for the Selective Phase Rotation of the Initial State. It sandwiches the SPROTIS operator between U a U^{a} and U b U^{b} for integers { a , b } ≤ k \{a,b\}\leq k to estimate ⟨ ψ | U k | ψ ⟩ \langle\psi|U^{k}|\psi\rangle . The circuit depth of the Sandwich test is almost same as that of SHT. The total run time T tot T_{\rm tot} is 𝒪 ⁡ ( k 2 ​ ϵ − 2 ​ s min − 6 ) \mathcal{O}(k^{2}\epsilon^{-2}s_{\rm min}^{-6}) . Here s min s_{\rm min} is the minimum value of | ⟨ ψ | U k ^ | ψ ⟩ |\langle\psi|U^{\hat{k}}|\psi\rangle among all integers k ^ \hat{k} which are values of the nodes of a random binary sum tree whose root node value is k k and leaf nodes’ values are 1 1 or 0 0 . It is difficult to analytically prove that s min ≪̸ 1 s_{\rm min}\not\ll 1 .But it can be reasonably expected in typical cases because there is extremely wide freedom in choosing the random binary sum tree. Numerical experiments are needed to confirm this.

The actual physical implementation of the Hadamard test is very hard because of the necessity of the

To precisely explain the locality constraint, let
T_{\rm{max}}(V)
be the circuit depth required to implement any unitary operator
V

T_{\rm{max}}(U^{k})=kT_{\rm{max}}(U).

(1)

We note that the efficient preparation of

|0^{n}\rangle

(2)

So the circuit depth of the Hadamard test is

T_{\rm{max}}(\rm{Hadamard})\approx T_{\rm{max}}(c\textendash U^{k})=kT_{\rm{max}}(c\textendash U)

(3)

To find
T_{\rm max}(c\textendash U)

can be decomposed into
L=T_{\rm max}(U)
layers where each layer is a unitary operator
U_{l}

\left(l\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}\right)
of circuit depth
1

U=\prod_{l=1}^{L}U_{l},\ \ \ T_{\rm max}(U_{l})=1,\ \ \ l\in\{1,2,\ldots L\}.

(4)

Each

consists of local unitary operators which can be applied in parallel. The only way to locally implement

is to perform the following operations for each

:

1.

We apply

Between each of these swaps, we use the control qubit for a controlled application of the local unitary operator which acts on the neighboring qubits of the control qubit in

3.

Then we apply the swap operators again to bring back the control qubit to its original position.

These steps cannot be applied in parallel. Hence the circuit depth of the controlled application of each layer is

T_{\rm max}(\rm Hadamard)\approx kT_{\rm max}(c\textendash U)=\mathcal{O}(kn)T_{\rm max}(U).

The basic ingredient of the Sandwich test is the Sandwich operator
S

U^{a}
and
U^{b}
, of unitary operator

S=U^{a}R_{\psi}^{\phi}U^{b}.

(8)

We define

\displaystyle r_{a}e^{\imath\theta_{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi|U^{a}|\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle r_{b}e^{\imath\theta_{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi|U^{b}|\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle r_{a+b}e^{\imath\theta_{a+b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi|U^{a+b}|\psi\rangle,

\displaystyle s_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle\psi|S|\psi\rangle|\

(9)

The phase of

can be simplified using Eqs. (
6
-
9

\displaystyle s_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\langle\psi|U^{a}R_{\psi}^{\phi}U^{b}|\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\langle\psi|U^{a}\left(1_{N}+\Phi|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\right)U^{b}|\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\langle\psi|U^{a+b}|\psi\rangle+\Phi\langle\psi|U^{a}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U^{b}|\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{a+b}^{2}+4r_{a}^{2}r_{b}^{2}\sin^{2}{\phi}-4r_{a+b}r_{a}r_{b}\sin{\phi}\sin{\omega_{a+b}}}.

So we have

The quantity

.

Note that
\hat{\omega}_{h}^{p_{h}}
does not exist for trivial nodes who has at least one child node with value
0
. Because in this case the
\hat{\theta}
value of a node is same as that of its non-zero value child node and no Sandwich test is needed to estimate it. Hence such trivial nodes are excluded from the double sum in above equation. Let
N_{h^{\prime}}
denote the total number of non-trivial nodes at height
h^{\prime}
. The sum of values of all nodes at this height is equal to
k
. As a non-trivial parent node must have a minimum value of
2
, we have
N_{h^{\prime}}=\Theta(k)
.

Let us consider the variance of sum
\sum_{p_{h^{\prime}}=1}^{2^{h^{\prime}}}\hat{\omega}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}
for a particular value
h=h^{\prime}
. As each of
\hat{\omega}
’s are independent random variables, the variance of their sum is the sum of their variances. As mentioned after Eq. (
13
),
\Theta(ms_{\rm min}^{-6})
projective measurements are required to estimate
\hat{\omega}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}
’s with a variance of
1/m
. We do
\Theta(Ms_{\rm min}^{-6}/\left(\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\right)^{q})
measurements to estimate
\hat{\omega}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}
with a variance of
\left(\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\right)^{q}/M
. Choosing
M
to be
\Theta(k^{q})
, we find the total variance to be

\sum_{p_{h^{\prime}}=1}^{2^{h^{\prime}}}\left(\gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\right)^{q},\ \ \gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}=\frac{\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}}{k}\Longrightarrow\sum_{p_{h^{\prime}}=1}^{2^{h^{\prime}}}\gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}=1.

(21)

because by the definition of sum tree, we have
\sum_{h^{\prime}}\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}=k
. Eq. (
16
) imply that
\max\{\gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\}=y_{\rm max}^{h^{\prime}}
. It is easy to show that the above variance is upper bounded by
y_{\rm max}^{h^{\prime}(q-1)}
for
q\geq 1
. Summing it over all
h^{\prime}
, we find that the total variance is upper bounded by
(1-\gamma_{\rm max}^{q-1})^{-1}\leq 1
.

Furthermore, for a particular node, we do
\Theta(Ms_{\rm min}^{-6}/\left(\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\right)^{q})
projective measurements to get above variance. Each projective measurement requires
\Theta(\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}})
applications of the unitary operator
U
because
\hat{k}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}
is the maximum integer power of
U
which needs to be applied to estimate
\hat{\omega}_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}
using the Sandwich test. As
M
is chosen to be
\Theta(k^{q})
and as shown earlier,
N_{h^{\prime}}=\Theta(k)
, the total number of required applications of
U
for height
h^{\prime}
can be easily found to be

\Theta(k)s_{\rm min}^{-6}\sum_{p_{h^{\prime}}=1}^{2^{h^{\prime}}}\left(\gamma_{h^{\prime}}^{p_{h^{\prime}}}\right)^{1-q}=\Theta(k^{2})s_{\rm min}^{-6}x_{\rm min}^{h^{\prime}(1-q)},

(22)

where we have used Eq. (
16
) and the fact that
q\geq 1
. Summing it over all
h^{\prime}
, we find the total run time complexity of the Sandwich test to estimate
\theta_{k}
with a variance less than
1
to be
\Theta(k^{2})s_{\rm min}^{-6}
. Obviously we have
k\theta_{1}
term also in Eq. (
20
). But it can also be easily estimated by estimating
\theta_{1}
to an accuracy of
\mathcal{1/k}
using
\Theta(k^{2})
Hadamard tests which requires same total run time also as each Hadamard test requires only one application of
U
. This contributes a constant-factor overhead to the total time complexity.

If we want to estimate
\theta_{k}
to an accuracy of
\epsilon
then its variance should be
\epsilon^{2}
and we need to do
\Theta(\epsilon^{-2})
times more measurements. So the total run time complexity of our algorithm is

T_{\rm tot}=\Theta\left(k^{2}\epsilon^{-2}s_{\rm min}^{-6}\right).

(23)

Thus the Sandwich test has a better run time than that of the Sequential Hadamard test. The
k^{2}
dependance of the Sandwich test is of course better than
k^{3}
dependance of the Sequential Hadamard test. But it is mostly the dependance on
r_{\rm min}
and
s_{\rm min}
which make the Sandwich test much better than the Sequential Hadamard test. Because typically
r_{\rm min}
can be very small but
s_{\rm min}
is not. It is difficult to prove it analytically but numerical experiments will be done to confirm this.

III
Conclusion and Discussion

We have presented a new quantum algorithm, the Sandwich test, to estimate
\langle s|U^{k}|s\rangle
for an efficiently preparable initial state
|s\rangle
and an efficiently implementable unitary operator
U
. The Sandwich test provides
\mathcal{O}(n)
factor improvement of circuit depth complexity over the Hadamard test. It uses the SPROTIS operator which is not used by the Hadamard test. We have shown that the SPROTIS operator is a useful quantum resource for the important task of estimating
\langle s|U^{k}|s\rangle
. We only need to sandwich the SPROTIS operator between properly chosen unitary operators.

We also note that if we have a Quantum Computer of large enough circuit depth then we can use Hadamard test to estimate
\langle\psi|U^{k^{\prime}}|\psi\rangle
for
k^{\prime}\geq 1
also, not just for
k^{\prime}=1
as discussed in the paper. Then we can use these values of
k^{\prime}
for the leaf nodes of our sum tree. It is easy to show that this will reduce the total run time by a factor of
\Theta((k^{\prime})^{2})
.

APPENDIX: MULTI-LAYER SANDWICH TESTS

Consider the two-layer Sandwich operator
S=U^{a}R_{\psi}^{\phi_{1}}U^{b}R_{\psi}^{\phi_{2}}U^{c}
. Using the similar notation as done in the main text, we get the following expression

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle\psi|U^{a}R_{\psi}^{\phi_{1}}U^{b}R_{\psi}^{\phi_{2}}U^{c}|\psi\rangle|

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle\psi|U^{a}\left(1_{N}+\Phi_{1}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\right)U^{b}\left(1_{N}+\Phi_{2}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\right)U^{c}|\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|r_{a+b+c}e^{\imath\theta_{a+b+c}}+\Phi_{1}r_{a}r_{b+c}r^{\imath\left(\theta_{a}+\theta_{b+c}\right)}+\Phi_{2}r_{a+b}r_{c}e^{\imath\left(\theta_{a+b}+\theta_{c}\right)}+\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}r_{a}r_{b}r_{c}e^{\imath\left(\theta_{a}+\theta_{b}+\theta_{c}\right)}|

It is easy to see that if any of
r
’s is very small then it gets effectively shunted out from above equation. For example, if
r_{b+c}\ll 1
then the second term in the above equation does not contribute much and we can get a good estimate of
\theta_{a+b+c}
in terms of
\theta_{a}
,
\theta_{b}
,
\theta_{c}
and
\theta_{a+b}
provided none of
\{r_{a},r_{b},r_{c},r_{a+b},r_{a+b+c}\}
is too small. Also,
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
can be chosen to have different values to refine our estimates. Thus multi-layer Sandwich tests can be used to effectively increase the value of
s_{\rm min}
. Of course, exact analysis of higher-layer Sandwich operators is increasingly complicated. But if we are using a
t
-layer Sandwich operator then
s
will be a sum of
2^{t}
terms. Thus the degrees of freedom to estimate
\theta_{k}
increases exponentially. Numerical experiments can be done to study higher-level Sandwich operators in typical cases.

Ilan Doron-Arad, Idan Mehalel, Elchanan Mossel

Abstract

Introduction

Online classification and online regression can behave very differently. Without a margin assumption, online classification is hard even for very simple hypothesis classes such as Perceptrons
25
;
32
, whereas regression, under
realizable
sequences, can admit horizon-free (finite) cumulative-loss guarantees.
In this paper, we study
realizable
online regression:

when does realizability imply a horizon-free bound on cumulative loss, and what complexity parameter controls it?

We work in the adversarial online model (no stochastic assumptions), and we deliberately avoid any margin/separation condition.
The key message is that, for regression under metric-like losses, realizability can be understood by a generic potential function mechanism, and enforce
finite total loss
even when the analogous
classification problem incurs an infinite mistake bound.

We start with some basic definitions of the model. Fix an instance domain
\mathcal{X}
, a label space
\mathcal{Y}
, a hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}
, and a loss

\ell:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
In each round
t=1,2,\dots
, the learner observes
x_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
, predicts
\hat{y}_{t}\in\mathcal{Y}
, then observes

and incurs the loss
\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})

by

The object is the minimax realizable cumulative loss:

\inf_{\mathsf{Alg}}\ \sup_{h^{\star}\in\mathcal{H},(x_{t})_{t\geq 1}}\ \sum_{t\geq 1}\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t}),

where
\hat{y}_{t}\;:=\;\mathsf{Alg}\!\bigl(x_{t}\mid(x_{r},y_{r})_{r<t}\bigr)
and
y_{t}=h^{\star}(x_{t})
for all

The goal is to upper bound this quantity (or show it is infinite), as a function of
\mathcal{H}
and
\ell

The recent work of
2

1.1
Our Results

Our main results can be summarized as follows, where all results consider solely realizable online learning without a margin assumption. We connect online learnability to metric entropy via an entropy-potential bound; we then derive clean horizon-free consequences from polynomial covering numbers and establish a sharp phase transition for Lipschitz regression under the
\ell_{q}
loss. As an important case study, we explore bounded ReLU classes, contrasting classification and regression behavior, and analyze the total loss obtainable for these classes.

We start with our general machinery of upper bounding the online dimension
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}
by a simple Dudley entropy integral, which then yields
covering-number-based guarantees.
Let
d_{\ell}
be the induced (pseudo-)metric on
\mathcal{H}
:

d_{\ell}(f,g)\ :=\ \sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ell\big(f(x),g(x)\big),\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{H},

and let
N(U,\varepsilon)=N_{\mathcal{H}}(U,d_{\ell},\varepsilon)
denote the
\varepsilon
-covering number of
U\subseteq\mathcal{H}
with respect to
d_{\ell}
; that is,
N(U,\varepsilon)
is the minimum cardinality of a set
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
such that for every
f\in U
there is
g\in S
such that
d_{\ell}(f,g)\leq\varepsilon
.
Assume
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H}):=\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal{H}}d_{\ell}(f,g)<\infty
, and define the
entropy potential

\Phi(U)\ :=\ \int_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})}\log_{2}N(U,\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon,

interpreted as an extended real value. Our first result is a useful upper bound on the online dimension.

Theorem 1.1

(Online dimension via entropy potential)
.

Assume
\ell
is a
c
-approximate pseudo-metric for some
c\geq 1

\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
.
Then

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ 4c\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{H}).

Let us describe the proof’s main idea. We analyze a realizable scaled Littlestone tree (the formal definition of
2
is given in
Section

2
) through
version spaces

U\subseteq\mathcal{H}
: all hypotheses consistent with the realizable sequence thus far.
At a node with gap
\gamma
, the (approximated) triangle inequality implies that for all
\varepsilon<\frac{\gamma}{2\cdot c}
,
any
\varepsilon
-cover of
U
must split across the two children (no cover center can serve both sides).
Integrating this cover splitting across scales yields a quantitative
potential drop
of order
\gamma
,
and a greedy descent argument produces a branch of the scaled Littlestone tree whose total gap is controlled by
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
.
This turns an abstract online dimension bound into a metric-entropy calculation.

Unlike agnostic regret bounds that use sequential covers/chaining via sequential Rademacher processes
(e.g.,
28
;
29
;
4

(\mathcal{H},d_{\ell})

As an immediate consequence of

1.1

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume that
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
and that there exist constants

p\geq 1

\varepsilon\in(0,1]

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{p}.

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\big(\log_{2}A+1/\ln 2\big),

1.1

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ 4c\,p\big(\log_{2}A+1/\ln 2\big).

As an immediate consequence of

, and therefore a horizon-free realizable cumulative-loss bound.
We develop two instantiations: (i)

Related Work

Realizable online regression is relatively unexplored, especially in comparison to classification and agnostic regression.
In terms of results, most related to our work, in particular to the application to neural nets, are the following papers.

20

1.3
Discussion

In this paper, we give a Dudley-integral based technique to study realizable online regression. We use this technique to obtain tight bounds for Lipschitz functions and ReLU networks, in the context of realizable (online) regression.
We list below some implications and limitations of our work and suggest interesting open directions for future work.

When the entropy potential diverges.

The entropy-potential approach yields horizon-free guarantees precisely when
\Phi(\mathcal{H})<\infty
.
If
\log N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)
diverges too quickly as
\varepsilon\to 0
(e.g.
\log N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{-p}
with
p\geq 1
),
then
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
and the bound from
Theorem

1.1
is vacuous.
Moreover, while the potential bound can be tight up to poly-logarithmic factors (e.g., unbounded norm ReLU nets, see Appendix
G
), the potential bound is not tight in general:
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
can be infinite even when

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})
is finite. We give the proof of the following proposition in Appendix
H
.

Proposition 1.9

(
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
while
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
)
.

There exist
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\ell)
with
\ell
a metric and a class
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}

such that
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
,
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
, but
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
.

Open directions.

Improving (or justifying) the
\tilde{O}\left(k^{2}\right)
for bounded
k
-ReLU.

For constant
k
,
Theorem

1.6
yields
\widetilde{O}(1)
cumulative squared loss.
Can one improve this to a true
O(1)
bound (as in
Theorem

1.7
) for every constant
k
?
If not, it would be very interesting to prove a matching lower bound showing that a
\textnormal{polylog}(d)
dependence is inherent.

By considering
k
as a variable rather than a pure constant, we note that
our analysis for bounded
k
-ReLU upper bounds large gaps via
sequential fat shattering dimensions
,
using sequential Rademacher complexity and a standard conversion from the former to the latter. This yields a quadratic dependency on
k

29
.
Obtaining an
\widetilde{O}(k)
bound therefore appears to require new ideas that bypass this squaring step. More generally, for depth
L
networks our techniques yield
\tilde{O}\left(k^{L}\right)
and a trivial lower bound gives only
\tilde{O}\left(k^{L-1}\right)
, leaving a similar gap of
k
for any depth
L
.

Near-realizable sequences.

Realizability (
L=0
) can be too optimistic, while fully agnostic regret bounds can be pessimistic when the best
comparator has loss
L\ll T
.
Can one extend our framework to a near-realizable setting where
\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\sum_{t\leq T}\ell(h(x_{t}),y_{t})\leq L
,
with guarantees that depend (essentially) linearly on
L
?
At a structural level, this suggests defining and characterizing an
L
-realizable analogue of the online dimension,
and asking whether an entropy-potential mechanism continues to control it.
Many works provided an analysis of this type for standard online classification. See, e.g.
8
;
7
;
3
;
6
;

.

Efficiency.

Our Lipschitz upper bounds in the learnable regime are efficient, and
Theorem

1.7
is efficient as well; on the other hand,
Theorem

1.6
is information-theoretic as it relies on our generic (and not necessarily efficient) potential technique.
Can one design an efficient

k
-ReLU with
k>1
that achieves

\widetilde{O}(1)
(or
O(1)
) cumulative loss, or alternatively prove computational lower bounds that apply even to improper learners? Our

Corollary

1.4
.

We use the scaled Littlestone dimension of
2
for a general family of loss functions of approximate pseudo-metrics defined below. Let
\mathcal{X}
be an instance domain,
\mathcal{Y}
a label space, and
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}
a hypothesis class.
Fix a loss
\ell:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.1

(
c
-approximate pseudo-metric)
.

For
c\geq 1
, we say that
\ell
is a
c
-approximate pseudo-metric
if:
(i)
\ell(y,y)=0
for all
y\in\mathcal{Y}
;
(ii)
\ell(y_{1},y_{2})=\ell(y_{2},y_{1})
for all

(iii) for all

If

In the following, we give the definition of a scaled Littlestone tree.

Definition 2.2

(Scaled Littlestone Tree)
.

A scaled Littlestone tree of depth

(under loss

)

is labeled by an instance

, and its two outgoing edges are labeled by
s_{u,0},s_{u,1}\in\mathcal{Y}
.
The gap at node

is

\gamma_{u}\ :=\ \ell(s_{u,0},s_{u,1}).

The tree is

\mathcal{H}
if for every branch
b\in\{0,1\}^{D}
and every finite
n<D
,
there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}
such that for all

Theorem 2.4

Let

Why an (approximate) triangle inequality is necessary.

Without a triangle-type condition, “sum of gaps” does not control realizable cumulative loss.
For example, let
\mathcal{Y}=\{a,b,c\}
and define a symmetric loss by
\ell(y,y)=0
,
\ell(a,b)=1
, and

\ell(a,c)=\ell(b,c)=\varepsilon

\varepsilon>0
. Then
\ell
violates any

c

since
\ell(a,b)=1>2c\varepsilon
.
In a realizable online problem with true labels in

incurs loss

has gaps

at each internal node, hence arbitrarily

4
Lipschitz Regression under
\ell_{q}
Loss

In this section, we give a high level overview of our results for the (nonparametric) class
\mathcal{H}_{L}
of
L
-Lipschitz functions. Specifically,
we instantiate our entropy–potential framework for
\mathcal{H}_{L}
under the power loss
\ell_{q}(y,y^{\prime}):=|y-y^{\prime}|^{q}
. Then, we present an efficient online realizable algorithm for the class, and briefly describe a matching lower bound. Note that the loss
\ell_{q}
is a
c
-approximate pseudo-metric with
c=2^{q-1}
, and the induced sup pseudo-metric is

d_{\ell}(f,g)=\sup_{x}\ell_{q}(f(x),g(x))=\|f-g\|_{\infty}^{q}.

Entropy potential and horizon-free learnability for
q>d
.

A standard metric-entropy estimate for Lipschitz classes in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
yields

\log N(\mathcal{H}_{L},\delta;\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\lesssim(L/\delta)^{d}\log(1/\delta)

(e.g., Lemma
A.1
, based on
(
34
, Thm. 17)
).
Since an
\varepsilon
-cover in
d_{\ell}
is exactly an
\varepsilon^{1/q}
-cover in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, we obtain

\log N(\mathcal{H}_{L},\varepsilon)\lesssim L^{d}\,\varepsilon^{-d/q}\log(1/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
Consequently,

\Phi(\mathcal{H}_{L})\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}\log N(\mathcal{H}_{L},\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon\;\lesssim\;L^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{-d/q}\log(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon\;<\;\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>d.

Plugging this into
Theorem

1.1
gives
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})\lesssim_{d,q}L^{d}
; hence, a horizon-free minimax realizable cumulative-loss bound of order
O_{d,q}(L^{d})

(via the characterization of
2
).
A horizon-free baseline lower bound
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})\geq(2L)^{d}
holds for all
q\geq 1

(Corollary
A.9
); thus, in the learnable regime
q>d
we obtain
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})=\Theta_{d,q}(L^{d})
. We note that the constants are generally exponential in
d
and
q
.

An efficient proper strategy (constructive
O_{d,q}(L^{d})
for
q>d
).

Beyond the non-constructive entropy argument, we give a simple deterministic proper algorithm based on

Lipschitz envelopes
.
Given past data
S_{t-1}=\{(x_{s},y_{s})\}_{s<t}
, define pointwise lower/upper envelopes

\underline{h}_{t}(x)=\max\Big\{0,\max_{s<t}\big(y_{s}-L\|x-x_{s}\|_{\infty}\big)\Big\},\qquad\overline{h}_{t}(x)=\min\Big\{1,\min_{s<t}\big(y_{s}+L\|x-x_{s}\|_{\infty}\big)\Big\},

and predict the midpoint
\hat{y}_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t})+\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t}))
.
Realizability implies
y_{t}\in[\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t}),\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t})]
, so the instantaneous error is controlled by
the envelope width
w_{t}:=\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t})-\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t})
.
For
q>d
we analyze the potential
\Psi_{t}=\int_{\mathcal{X}}(\overline{h}_{t}-\underline{h}_{t})^{\,q-d}\,dx
:
each round in which
w_{t}
is large forces a
geometric tightening
of the envelopes on a cube around
x_{t}
,
causing a definite drop in
\Psi_{t}
of size
\gtrsim w_{t}^{q}/L^{d}
.
Telescoping from
\Psi_{1}=2^{d}
gives
\sum_{t\geq 1}|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{q}\lesssim_{d,q}L^{d}
.
Each prediction can be computed in time
O(td)
by scanning
S_{t-1}
; we did not attempt to optimize the running time further.
The full proof and constants appear in Appendix
A
.

Eventual learnability for any continuous loss.

The same envelope mechanism yields a strong pointwise guarantee that is
loss-agnostic
:
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

\#\{t:\ |\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon\}\ \leq\ (8L/\varepsilon)^{d},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\to 0.

As a corollary, for any continuous loss
\ell
with
\ell(y,y)=0
, we have
\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})\to 0

on every realizable sequence (Corollary
1.4
).

The critical and subcritical regimes
q\leq d
.

At the critical exponent
q=d
, the envelope strategy satisfies a sharp horizon-dependent bound:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{d}\ \lesssim_{d}\ L^{d}(1+\log T),

proved by combining the
\varepsilon
-mistake bound above with a layer-cake identity.
We also prove a matching lower bound

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\gtrsim_{d}L^{d}\log(1+T/L^{d})

via an explicit multiscale adversary built from dyadic cube refinements and a McShane extension argument,
showing the logarithmic dependence is inherent.
For
q<d
, we construct a realizable scaled Littlestone tree on a
T

T^{-1/d}
-separated set, yielding

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})\ \gtrsim\ (2L)^{q}\,T^{1-q/d},

and therefore polynomially growing minimax realizable cumulative loss.
In particular, it holds that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})=\infty
for all

, matching the phase diagram in

x_{t}
, predict

\hat{y}_{t}\ :=\ \frac{\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t})+\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t})}{2}\in[0,1],

then observe
y_{t}
and continue. The lemma below shows the correctness of this approach.

Lemma A.3
.

For every realizable sequence and every
q>d
,

\sum_{t\geq 1}|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{q}\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{d,q}\,L^{d}

for a finite constant
C^{\prime}_{d,q}
depending only on
(d,q)
.
Moreover, each prediction can be computed in time
O(td)
by evaluating the max/min over
S_{t-1}
.

Proof.

Fix
t
and write the width function

W_{t}(x)\ :=\ \overline{h}_{t}(x)-\underline{h}_{t}(x),\qquad w_{t}:=W_{t}(x_{t}).

Since
\overline{h}_{t}
is a pointwise minimum of
L
-Lipschitz functions and
\underline{h}_{t}
is a pointwise
maximum of
L
-Lipschitz functions (both clipped to
[0,1]
), we have that
W_{t}
is
2L
-Lipschitz.
Realizability implies
\underline{h}_{t}(x)\leq f^{\star}(x)\leq\overline{h}_{t}(x)
for all
x
, hence

y_{t}=f^{\star}(x_{t})\in[\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t}),\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t})]
and therefore

|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{q}\ \leq\ (w_{t}/2)^{q}.

(5)

Let
\lambda
denote Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{X}
, and define the potential (well-defined since
q>d
)

\Psi_{t}\ :=\ \int_{\mathcal{X}}W_{t}(x)^{\,q-d}\,d\lambda(x).

Since the envelopes tighten, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
we have
\underline{h}_{t+1}(x)\geq\underline{h}_{t}(x)
and

\overline{h}_{t+1}(x)\leq\overline{h}_{t}(x)
, hence
W_{t+1}(x)\leq W_{t}(x)
.
As
q-d>0
, this implies
W_{t+1}(x)^{q-d}\leq W_{t}(x)^{q-d}
and therefore
\Psi_{t+1}\leq\Psi_{t}
.
Set
r_{t}:=w_{t}/(8L)
and
B_{t}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ \|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq r_{t}\}
.
Since
B_{t}=\mathcal{X}\cap(x_{t}+[-r_{t},r_{t}]^{d})
, each coordinate interval has length at least
r_{t}
, hence

\lambda(B_{t})\ \geq\ r_{t}^{d}\ =\ \Big(\frac{w_{t}}{8L}\Big)^{d}.

By
2L
-Lipschitzness, for all
x\in B_{t}
we have

W_{t}(x)\geq w_{t}-2Lr_{t}=(3/4)w_{t}
.
After observing
(x_{t},y_{t})
, any consistent
h\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
must satisfy

|h(x)-y_{t}|\leq L\|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty}
, hence for all
x
,

\overline{h}_{t+1}(x)\leq y_{t}+L\|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty},\qquad\underline{h}_{t+1}(x)\geq y_{t}-L\|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty},

and thus on
B_{t}
,
W_{t+1}(x)\leq 2Lr_{t}=w_{t}/4
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}-\Psi_{t+1}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{t}}\Big(W_{t}(x)^{q-d}-W_{t+1}(x)^{q-d}\Big)\,d\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(B_{t})\Big((3w_{t}/4)^{q-d}-(w_{t}/4)^{q-d}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{w_{t}}{8L}\Big)^{d}\Big((3/4)^{q-d}-(1/4)^{q-d}\Big)\,w_{t}^{q-d}\ =:\ \frac{c_{d,q}}{L^{d}}\,w_{t}^{q},

where
c_{d,q}:=8^{-d}\big((3/4)^{q-d}-(1/4)^{q-d}\big)>0
.
Summing over
t
and using
\Psi_{t}\geq 0
gives

\sum_{t\geq 1}w_{t}^{q}\ \leq\ \frac{L^{d}}{c_{d,q}}\Psi_{1}.

At
t=1
we have
\underline{h}_{1}\equiv 0
,
\overline{h}_{1}\equiv 1
, hence
W_{1}\equiv 1
and

\Psi_{1}=\lambda(\mathcal{X})=2^{d}
. Combining with (
5
) yields

\sum_{t\geq 1}|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{q}\ \leq\ 2^{-q}\sum_{t\geq 1}w_{t}^{q}\ \leq\ 2^{-q}\cdot\frac{2^{d}}{c_{d,q}}\,L^{d}\ =:\ C^{\prime}_{d,q}\,L^{d}.

The above gives the proof of the lemma.
∎

A.3
Implications of the Efficient Algorithm

We also have the following interesting results as a corollary of the above efficient algorithm.

Corollary A.4

(Pointwise consistency of the envelope strategy)
.

For every
d\geq 1
and
L\geq 1
, for every realizable sequence, the envelope strategy satisfies

|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\ \longrightarrow\ 0\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

\#\{t\geq 1:\ |\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon\}\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{8L}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{d}.

Proof.

Let
W_{t}=\overline{h}_{t}-\underline{h}_{t}
and
w_{t}:=W_{t}(x_{t})
. Since

y_{t}\in[\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t}),\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t})]
and

\hat{y}_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\underline{h}_{t}(x_{t})+\overline{h}_{t}(x_{t}))
, we have
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\leq w_{t}/2
; hence

|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon
implies
w_{t}>2\varepsilon
.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and define
A_{t}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ W_{t}(x)>\varepsilon\}
.
Because the envelopes tighten pointwise,
W_{t+1}\leq W_{t}
and thus
A_{t+1}\subseteq A_{t}
.

On the event
w_{t}>2\varepsilon
, set
r:=\varepsilon/(4L)
and
B_{t}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\ \|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq r\}
.
As
W_{t}
is
2L
-Lipschitz, for all
x\in B_{t}
,

W_{t}(x)\geq W_{t}(x_{t})-2Lr>2\varepsilon-\varepsilon/2>\varepsilon
, hence
B_{t}\subseteq A_{t}
.
After observing
(x_{t},y_{t})
, the constraint at time
t
implies

W_{t+1}(x)\leq 2L\|x-x_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Lr=\varepsilon/2
for all
x\in B_{t}
, so
B_{t}\cap A_{t+1}=\emptyset
.
Therefore
\lambda(A_{t})-\lambda(A_{t+1})\geq\lambda(B_{t})
.
Since
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}
, each coordinate interval in
B_{t}=\mathcal{X}\cap(x_{t}+[-r,r]^{d})
has length at least
r
, hence

\lambda(B_{t})\geq r^{d}
.

Summing over all
t
with
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon
yields

\#\{t:\ |\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon\}\cdot r^{d}\ \leq\ \lambda(A_{1})\ \leq\ \lambda(\mathcal{X})=2^{d},

so
\#\{t:\ |\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|>\varepsilon\}\leq 2^{d}/r^{d}=(8L/\varepsilon)^{d}
.
Since this holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, it follows that
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\to 0
.
∎

From the above, we can prove Corollary
1.4

Proof of Corollary
1.4
:

By Corollary
A.4
,
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\to 0
. Since
\ell
is continuous on the compact
set
[0,1]^{2}
and vanishes on the diagonal
\{(y,y):y\in[0,1]\}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

\delta>0
such that

. Hence

.
∎

In addition, we have the following corollary:

Corollary A.5

(Critical horizon-dependent bound for
q=d
)
.

Fix
d\geq 1
and
L\geq 1
. For every realizable sequence and every horizon
T\geq 2
,
the envelope strategy satisfies

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|^{d}\ \leq\ C_{d}\,L^{d}\,(1+\log T),

for a constant
C_{d}<\infty
depending only on
d
(e.g.
C_{d}=8^{d}
).

Proof.

Let
e_{t}:=|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}|\in[0,1]
and for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
write

N_{T}(\varepsilon):=\#\{t\leq T:\ e_{t}>\varepsilon\}
.
By Corollary
A.4
,
N_{T}(\varepsilon)\leq\min\{T,(8L/\varepsilon)^{d}\}
.
Using the layer-cake identity,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{t}^{d}\ =\ d\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{d-1}N_{T}(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon\ \leq\ d\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{d-1}\min\{T,(8L/\varepsilon)^{d}\}\,d\varepsilon.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{1,\,8L\,T^{-1/d}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{t}^{d}

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\varepsilon^{d-1}T\,d\varepsilon\;+\;d\int_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{1}\varepsilon^{d-1}(8L/\varepsilon)^{d}\,d\varepsilon

\displaystyle=T\varepsilon_{0}^{d}\;+\;d(8L)^{d}\int_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{1}\varepsilon^{-1}\,d\varepsilon\ =\ T\varepsilon_{0}^{d}\;+\;d(8L)^{d}\log(1/\varepsilon_{0}).

If
\varepsilon_{0}=1
, then
T\leq(8L)^{d}
and the bound is at most
(8L)^{d}
.
Otherwise
\varepsilon_{0}=8L\,T^{-1/d}
, so
T\varepsilon_{0}^{d}=(8L)^{d}
and

\log(1/\varepsilon_{0})=\log(T^{1/d}/(8L))\leq\frac{1}{d}\log T
(since
\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1
),
giving
\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_{t}^{d}\leq(8L)^{d}(1+\log T)
.
∎

A.4
A realizable lower bound for
q\leq d

We next show that when
q=d
and then
q<d
, the online dimension grows logarithmically and polynomially with the horizon, respectively.
Hence, realizable cumulative loss is unbounded when
q\leq d
.

Lemma A.6

(Critical lower bound for
q=d
)
.

Fix
d\geq 1
and
L\geq 1
on
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]^{d}
with loss
\ell_{d}(\hat{y},y)=|\hat{y}-y|^{d}
.
For every horizon
T\geq 2
, for any (possibly randomized) online learner producing
\hat{y}_{t}\in[0,1]
,
there exists a realizable sequence
(x_{t},y_{t})_{t=1}^{T}
by
\mathcal{H}_{L}
such that

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\ \geq\ c_{d}\,L^{d}\,\log\!\Big(1+\frac{T}{L^{d}}\Big),

for a constant
c_{d}>0
depending only on
d
.

Proof.

For
j\geq 0
let
a_{j}:=2^{-j}/L
and let
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
be the axis-aligned grid partition of
[-1,1]^{d}

into cubes of side
a_{j}
(intersected with
[-1,1]^{d}
), so that
\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}
refines
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
.
Let
M_{j}:=\lfloor 2/a_{j}\rfloor^{d}
and let
x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,M_{j}}
be the corresponding cube centers.
Set
\delta_{j}:=2^{-j-2}
, so
\sum_{j\geq 0}\delta_{j}\leq 1/2
.

Adversary.

Query points level-by-level:
x_{0,1},\dots,x_{0,M_{0}}
, then
x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,M_{1}}
, etc., stopping after
T
rounds
(the last level may be partial).
Introduce a root cube
Q_{-1}:=[-1,1]^{d}
and define
v(Q_{-1})=1/2
.
Whenever the adversary queries a center
x_{t}
belonging to a cube
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{j}
not queried before,
after seeing
\hat{y}_{t}
define

y_{t}:=v(Q):=v(\mathrm{parent}(Q))+s(Q)\delta_{j},\qquad s(Q)\in\{\pm 1\},

where
\mathrm{parent}(Q)
is the unique cube in
\mathcal{Q}_{j-1}
containing
Q
(and
\mathrm{parent}(Q):=Q_{-1}
if
j=0
).
Choose the sign
s(Q)
so that
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\geq\delta_{j}/2
(possible since the two candidates are
2\delta_{j}
apart).
Also
y_{t}\in[0,1]
because
v(Q_{-1})=1/2
and the total variation along any chain is at most
\sum_{j}\delta_{j}\leq 1/2
.

Realizability.

Let
S_{T}:=\{x_{t}:t\leq T\}
and define
y(x_{t}):=y_{t}
.
If
x,x^{\prime}\in S_{T}
have least common ancestor at level
m
, then their increments can differ by at most
2\delta_{k}

per level
k>m
, hence

|y(x)-y(x^{\prime})|\leq 2\sum_{k>m}\delta_{k}=2^{-m-1}.

Since they split at level
m+1
, their centers satisfy
\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\geq a_{m+1}=2^{-(m+1)}/L
, so

|y(x)-y(x^{\prime})|\leq 2^{-m-1}=La_{m+1}\leq L\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.
Thus
y
is
L
-Lipschitz on
S_{T}
, and by
[
24
]
there is an
L
-Lipschitz extension
\tilde{f}
to
\mathcal{X}
.
Clipping
f^{\star}:=\textnormal{clip}_{[0,1]}(\tilde{f})
keeps the same values on
S_{T}
and preserves
L
-Lipschitzness,
so
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
realizes the sequence.

Loss lower bound.

Whenever a fresh level-
j
cube is queried we ensured
|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|\geq\delta_{j}/2
, hence

|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\geq(\delta_{j}/2)^{d}=2^{-d(j+3)}
.
Let
J
be such that the first
J
levels contribute at least one full level, i.e.

N_{J}:=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}M_{j}\ \leq\ T\ <\ N_{J+1}:=\sum_{j=0}^{J}M_{j}.

Then the
N_{J}
rounds of fully completed levels yield

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\ \geq\ \sum_{j=0}^{J-1}M_{j}\,2^{-d(j+3)}.

Using
\lfloor 2/a_{j}\rfloor\geq 2/a_{j}-1=2^{j+1}L-1\geq 2^{j}L
(since
L\geq 1
), we have
M_{j}\geq(2^{j}L)^{d}
, hence
each full level contributes at least
2^{-3d}L^{d}
, and therefore

\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\ \geq\ 2^{-3d}L^{d}\,J.

(6)

Finally, relate
J
and
T
.
If
J=0
, then all
T
rounds are at level
0
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}|^{d}\geq T(\delta_{0}/2)^{d}=2^{-3d}T\geq 2^{-3d}L^{d}\log(1+T/L^{d})
using
\log(1+u)\leq u
.
Assume
J\geq 1
. Since
T<N_{J+1}
and
M_{j}\leq(2/a_{j})^{d}=(2^{j+1}L)^{d}
,

T\ <\ \sum_{j=0}^{J}(2^{j+1}L)^{d}\ =\ 2^{d}L^{d}\,\frac{2^{d(J+1)}-1}{2^{d}-1}\ \leq\ C_{d}\,L^{d}\,2^{d(J+1)}

for
C_{d}:=\frac{2^{d}}{2^{d}-1}
. Hence

\log\!\Big(1+\frac{T}{L^{d}}\Big)\ \leq\ \log(1+C_{d})+d(J+1)\log 2\ \leq\ K_{d}\,J,

where
K_{d}:=\log(1+C_{d})+2d\log 2
(using
J+1\leq 2J
for
J\geq 1
).
Combining with (
6
) gives the claim with
c_{d}:=2^{-3d}/K_{d}
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

For every integer
T\geq 1
, there exists a set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{T}\}\subseteq[-1,1]^{d}
such that

\|x_{t}-x_{s}\|_{\infty}\geq 2T^{-1/d}
for all
t\neq s
.

Proof.

Let
m:=\lfloor T^{1/d}\rfloor
and consider the grid

G:=\{-1,-1+2/m,\dots,1\}^{d}.

Then
|G|=(m+1)^{d}\geq T
, and distinct points in
G
differ by at least
2/m
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
Since
m\leq T^{1/d}
, we have
2/m\geq 2T^{-1/d}
.
Then, any
T
distinct points from
G
satisfy the claim.
∎

Theorem A.8

(Unbounded online dimension for
q<d
)
.

Fix
d\geq 1
,
L\geq 1
, and
q<d
.
Then for every horizon
T\geq(2L)^{d}
,

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})\ \geq\ (2L)^{q}\,T^{1-q/d}.

Consequently, the minimax realizable cumulative loss for
\mathcal{H}_{L}
under
\ell_{q}
is unbounded as
T\to\infty

(and in fact grows at least on the order of
L^{q}T^{1-q/d}
), by
[
2
]
.

Proof.

Fix
T\geq(2L)^{d}
and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{T}
be as in Lemma
A.7
, so

\|x_{t}-x_{s}\|_{\infty}\geq r:=2T^{-1/d}

for
t\neq s
.
Define
\Delta:=Lr=2LT^{-1/d}\leq 1
(the last inequality uses
T\geq(2L)^{d}
).

We build a scaled Littlestone tree of depth
T
as follows.
At depth
t-1
we query
x_{t}
, and the two outgoing edges are labeled by
s_{t,0}:=0
and
s_{t,1}:=\Delta
.
Thus every internal node has gap

\gamma=\ell_{q}(0,\Delta)=\Delta^{q}.

It remains to verify realizability of the tree: for every branch
b\in\{0,1\}^{T}
,
define values on the finite set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{T}\}
by
f_{b}(x_{t}):=b_{t}\Delta
.
For any
t\neq s
,

|f_{b}(x_{t})-f_{b}(x_{s})|\ \leq\ \Delta\ =\ Lr\ \leq\ L\|x_{t}-x_{s}\|_{\infty},

By the McShane extension theorem
[
24
]
,
f_{b}
extends to an
L
-Lipschitz function

\tilde{f}_{b}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\tilde{f}_{b}(x_{t})=f_{b}(x_{t})=b_{t}\Delta
for all
t
.
Define
\hat{f}_{b}:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]
by
\hat{f}_{b}(x):=\min\{1,\max\{0,\tilde{f}_{b}(x)\}\}
.
Since clipping is
1
-Lipschitz,
\hat{f}_{b}
is still
L
-Lipschitz, hence
\hat{f}_{b}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
,
and
\hat{f}_{b}(x_{t})=b_{t}\Delta
for all
t
.
Thus the tree is realizable by
\mathcal{H}_{L}
.
Along every branch, the sum of gaps equals
T\Delta^{q}
, hence

\inf_{y\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T})}\sum_{i}\gamma_{y_{i}}\ =\ T\Delta^{q}\ =\ T(Lr)^{q}\ =\ T\,(2LT^{-1/d})^{q}\ =\ (2L)^{q}\,T^{1-q/d}.

Taking the supremum over trees in the definition of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L})
yields the stated lower bound.
Finally,
[
2
]
converts this lower bound on
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}
into a lower bound on
minimax realizable cumulative loss (up to universal constants depending only on
c
, hence on
q
).
∎

(An

.

Fix

,
L\geq 1
, and

. Then

]
.

Proof.

Let

there exist

.

Consider the depth-
T
scaled Littlestone tree that queries

at depth
t-1
and labels the two

0
and
\Delta
. Each node has gap

.
For any branch

A.5
Compact and Totally Bounded Classes

We remind the reader that for the class
\mathcal{H}_{L}
of
L
-Lipschitz functions, the entropy bound becomes borderline at
q=d
and we show that the total loss grows logarithmically with the horizon
T
, where for
q<d
the total loss grows linearly with
T
.
The above regimes between
q
and
d
give a good example to the fact that bounded loss is metric-dependent. Therefore, we ask an abstract question: under what conditions does
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})=0
hold for every reasonable loss
\ell
(not necessarily a pseudo-metric)?
In the agnostic online setting under absolute loss, online learnability admits a general characterization via finiteness of the
sequential

\varepsilon
-fat shattering dimension for every
\varepsilon>0

[
28
]
.
However, if
\mathcal{H}
is totally bounded with respect to
d_{\ell}(f,g)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ell(f(x),g(x))
(i.e., for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite
\varepsilon
-net under
d_{\ell}
),
then we obtain a simpler
proper
and deterministic construction: for each fixed
\varepsilon
we take a finite
\varepsilon
-net and run an elimination procedure, yielding at most
N(\mathcal{H},d_{\ell},\varepsilon)
rounds with
\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})>\varepsilon
; applying this for
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
implies
\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})\to 0
.

Theorem A.10
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be any label space, let
\ell:\mathcal{Y}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be any loss function, and let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}
be a function class.
Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is
totally bounded
with respect to

d_{\ell}(f,g)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ell\bigl(f(x),g(x)\bigr).

Then, there is a deterministic proper algorithm that for any realizable sequence
(x_{t},y_{t})_{t\geq 1}
returns
(\hat{y}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
,
which for every
\varepsilon>0
satisfies

\left|\left\{t\mid\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})>\varepsilon\right\}\right|\leq N(\mathcal{H},d_{\ell},\varepsilon)-1;

hence, it follows that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})=0
is equivalent to having for every
\varepsilon>0
only finitely many times
t

for which
\ell(\hat{y}_{t},y_{t})>\varepsilon
. Fix some
\varepsilon>0
. Let
N=N(\mathcal{H},d_{\ell},\varepsilon)
. Since
\mathcal{H}
is totally bounded, there exists a finite
\varepsilon

\{f_{1},\dots,f_{N}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
with respect to

there is an

Select such representatives

\hat{y_{t}}:=f_{i}(x_{t})

Let

for all

satisfy

.
Then for all

so

is never eliminated. Each time

occurs, we eliminate one representative, hence this can happen

implies

∎

Thus, as a corollary for general Lipschitz functions, pointwise learning

.
In this following, we give a second proof for

using totally boundedness properties of Lipschitz functions.

A Second Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since

is continuous on the compact set

, it is uniformly continuous; moreover, as
\ell(y,y)=0
for all

,
there exists

such that for all

Hence, for any

On the other hand,

is compact and

Appendix B
Constant Loss for One ReLU

In this section, we give an efficient online realizable algorithm for the class of functions

\mathcal{H}=\{x\mapsto\textnormal{ReLU}\left(w\cdot x\right)\mid w\in[-1,1]^{d},||w||_{2}\leq 1\},

Consider the following algorithm.
Set
w_{1}=0
. Define the learning strategy such that for each round
1\leq t\leq T
define

w_{t+1}=w_{t}-\alpha_{t}\cdot x_{t}
.
where
\alpha_{t}=\textnormal{ReLU}(w_{t}\cdot x_{t})-\textnormal{ReLU}(w\cdot x_{t})
which can be computed at the end of round
t
since
y_{t}=\textnormal{ReLU}(w\cdot x_{t})
is given to the algorithm.
Let
L=\sum_{1\leq t\leq T}\ell_{t}
be the accumulated loss, where we use

\ell_{t}=\|\textnormal{ReLU}(w_{t}\cdot x_{t})-\textnormal{ReLU}(w\cdot x_{t})\|^{2}.

We give the following result.

Theorem B.1
.

L\leq 1
.

Proof.

Define the potential function
\phi_{t}=\|w_{t}-w\|^{2}
for every
1\leq t\leq T+1
. Fix some
1\leq t\leq T
; then,

\displaystyle\phi_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\|w_{t+1}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|w_{t}-\alpha_{t}\cdot x_{t}-w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(w_{t}-w)-\alpha_{t}\cdot x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(w_{t}-w)\|^{2}-2(w_{t}-w)\cdot(\alpha_{t}x_{t})+\|\alpha_{t}x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}-2(w_{t}-w)\cdot(\alpha_{t}x_{t})+\|\alpha_{t}x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}-2\alpha_{t}(w_{t}-w)\cdot x_{t}+\alpha^{2}_{t}\|x_{t}\|^{2}

Claim B.2
.

-2\alpha_{t}(w_{t}-w)\cdot x_{t}\leq-2\alpha^{2}_{t}

Proof.

Consider two cases.

1.

w\cdot x_{t}>0
. Consider two subcases.

•

w_{t}\cdot x_{t}\geq 0
. Then,
\alpha_{t}=(w_{t}\cdot x_{t}-w\cdot x_{t})
implying
-2\alpha_{t}(w_{t}-w)\cdot x_{t}=-2\alpha^{2}_{t}
.

•

w_{t}\cdot x_{t}<0
. Then,
\alpha_{t}=-w\cdot x_{t}
which satisfies
\alpha_{t}<0
. Therefore,

-2\alpha_{t}(w_{t}-w)\cdot x_{t}=-2\alpha_{t}w_{t}\cdot x_{t}+2\alpha_{t}w\cdot x_{t}=-2\alpha_{t}w_{t}\cdot x_{t}-2\alpha^{2}_{t}\leq-2\alpha^{2}_{t}.

2.

w\cdot x_{t}\leq 0
. Then, consider two subcases.

•

w_{t}\cdot x_{t}\leq 0
. Thus
\alpha_{t}=0
and the inequality holds.

•

w_{t}\cdot x_{t}>0
. Then, since
w\cdot x_{t}\leq 0
it holds that
\alpha_{t}=w_{t}\cdot x_{t}
. Thus,
\alpha_{t}>0
and

-2\alpha_{t}(w_{t}-w)\cdot x_{t}=-2\alpha_{t}w_{t}\cdot x_{t}+2\alpha_{t}w\cdot x_{t}=-2\alpha^{2}_{t}+2\alpha_{t}w\cdot x_{t}\leq-2\alpha^{2}_{t}.

The inequality holds since
\alpha_{t}>0
and
w\cdot x_{t}\leq 0
.

This gives the proof.
∎

By Claim
B.2
and the equation above it follows that:

\phi_{t+1}\leq\phi_{t}-2\alpha^{2}_{t}+\alpha^{2}_{t}\|x_{t}\|^{2}

Observe that

\displaystyle\ell_{t}=\|\textnormal{ReLU}(w_{t}\cdot x_{t})-\textnormal{ReLU}(w\cdot x_{t})\|^{2}=\left(\textnormal{ReLU}(w_{t}\cdot x_{t})-\textnormal{ReLU}(w\cdot x_{t})\right)^{2}=\alpha^{2}_{t}.

Thus,
\ell_{t}=\alpha^{2}_{t}
and by combining the above

\phi_{t+1}\leq\phi_{t}-2\alpha^{2}_{t}+\alpha^{2}_{t}\|x_{t}\|^{2}\leq\phi_{t}-2\alpha^{2}_{t}+\alpha^{2}_{t}=\phi_{t}-\alpha^{2}_{t}=\phi_{t}-{\ell_{t}}.

The second inequality follows from the assumption
\|x_{t}\|\leq 1
. The above equation gives
{\ell_{t}}\leq\phi_{t}-\phi_{t+1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq T}\ell_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq t\leq T}\left(\phi_{t}-\phi_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right)+\left(\phi_{2}-\phi_{3}\right)+\ldots+\left(\phi_{T-1}-\phi_{T}\right)+\left(\phi_{T}-\phi_{T+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}-\phi_{T+1}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=\|w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1.

The last inequality follows from the assumption
\|w\|\leq 1
.
∎

C.1

For

Definition C.3

(Sequential fat-shattering dimension)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}
and let
\alpha>0
.
A pair of complete binary trees

(x_{u})_{u\in\{0,1\}^{<m}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(s_{u})_{u\in\{0,1\}^{<m}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is
\alpha
-shattered

\mathcal{F}
if for every path
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}

there exists
f_{b}\in\mathcal{F}
such that for all
t=0,1,\dots,m-1

b_{t+1}=1\ \Rightarrow\ f_{b}\!\left(x_{b_{\leq t}}\right)\ \geq\ s_{b_{\leq t}}+\alpha,\qquad b_{t+1}=0\ \Rightarrow\ f_{b}\!\left(x_{b_{\leq t}}\right)\ \leq\ s_{b_{\leq t}}-\alpha,

where
b_{\leq t}:=(b_{1},\dots,b_{t})
and
b_{\leq 0}:=\emptyset
.
The
sequential fat-shattering dimension
of
\mathcal{F}
at scale
\alpha

\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})\ :=\ \sup\Big\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\ \exists\text{ a depth-$m$ tree $\alpha$-shattered by }\mathcal{F}\Big\},

with the convention
\sup\emptyset=0
(and
\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{F})=\infty
if arbitrarily large depths are possible).

The following lemma shows the decrease in the above dimension along a path of a shattered tree.

Lemma C.4
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq[-1,1]^{\mathcal{X}}
be nonempty, fix
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and two values

Let
\Delta:=|a-b|
and define

\mathcal{V}_{0}:=\{h\in\mathcal{V}:h(x)=a\},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{1}:=\{h\in\mathcal{V}:h(x)=b\},

assumed both nonempty. If
\Delta\geq 2\alpha
, then

\min\bigl\{\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}_{0}),\ \fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\bigr\}\ \leq\ \fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V})-1.

We call a child
u^{+}
of a node
u

\alpha
-decreasing
if

\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}(u^{+}))\leq\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V}(u))-1
.

Proof.

Let
m:=\fat_{\alpha}(\mathcal{V})
and assume for contradiction that both children have
\fat_{\alpha}
dimension at least
m
.
Without loss of generality assume
a>b
(otherwise swap the roles of
\mathcal{V}_{0}
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}

Glue depth-

\alpha
-shattering trees for
\mathcal{V}_{0}
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}
under a new root at instance

with threshold
\theta:=(a+b)/2
, attaching the
\mathcal{V}_{0}
subtree to the branch requiring

\mathcal{V}_{1}

Since

and

, yielding an
\alpha
-shattering tree
of depth

Let

Lemma C.5

(Large-gap branch bound via a truncated multiscale potential)

Let

\sum_{u\in P:\ \Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ 16\sum_{r=-1}^{K}\alpha_{r}^{2}\,\fat_{\alpha_{r}}(\mathcal{H}).

We invoke the standard sequential Rademacher–fat chain from

Step 1 (linear class).

Step 2 (single ReLU with a scalar).

Small gaps: scaling into a comparison class and paying
1/d

Define the scaled comparison class

\mathcal{F}\ :=\ \{\ g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}\ :\ \exists h\in\mathcal{H}\ \text{s.t.}\ g(x)=\sqrt{d}\,h(x)\ \ \forall x\in\mathcal{X}\ \}.

Let
T
be any scaled Littlestone tree shattered by
\mathcal{H}
(under
\ell_{\cap}
).
Consider the (deterministic) rule from the proof of Lemma
C.5
:
whenever a reached node
u
satisfies
\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}
, move to the
\alpha_{r}
-decreasing child selected there;
whenever
\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
, the next child is chosen freely.
Then, we have the following statement for such
T
.

Lemma C.8

(Small-gap amplification under
\ell_{\cap}
)
.

There exists a branch
P
in
T
that follows the above rule at every node with
\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}
and satisfies

E_{\mathrm{small}}(P)\ :=\ \sum_{u\in P:\ \Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{4}{d}\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap}).

Proof.

Run the above rule, but leave the choices at nodes with
\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
unspecified.
This induces a full binary tree
S
whose internal nodes are exactly the reached nodes with

\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
(choices only occur there), with the same queried instances and the same two edge-labels

s_{u,0},s_{u,1}
as in
T
at those nodes. If needed, we pad shorter branches with dummy nodes of zero gap (same label on both edges), which preserves shattering and does not change branch energies, yielding a full binary tree of uniform depth.
Because
T
is shattered by
\mathcal{H}
, every branch of
S
corresponds to a branch of
T
(obtained by inserting the
forced moves at
\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}
), hence is realizable by some hypothesis in
\mathcal{H}
.
Therefore
S
is shattered by
\mathcal{H}
.
Now scale all edge-labels in
S
by
\sqrt{d}
to obtain a tree
S^{\prime}
with labels

s^{\prime}_{u,b}:=\sqrt{d}\,s_{u,b}
.
For every branch of
S
, if
h_{b}\in\mathcal{H}
realizes it, then the scaled function
g_{b}:=\sqrt{d}\,h_{b}
lies in
\mathcal{F}

and realizes the corresponding branch of
S^{\prime}
. Thus
S^{\prime}
is shattered by
\mathcal{F}
.

For every node
u
of
S
we have
\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
, hence in
S^{\prime}
the gap is

\Delta^{\prime}_{u}=\sqrt{d}\,\Delta_{u}\leq 1
and clipping in
\ell_{\cap}
does not occur:

\ell_{\cap}(s^{\prime}_{u,0},s^{\prime}_{u,1})=(\Delta^{\prime}_{u})^{2}/4=d\,\Delta_{u}^{2}/4.

Therefore, for every branch
b
of
S^{\prime}
,

E_{S^{\prime}}(b)=\frac{d}{4}\sum_{u\in b}\Delta_{u}^{2}=\frac{d}{4}\cdot E_{\mathrm{small}}(P_{b}),

where
P_{b}
is the corresponding branch of
T
produced by the rule.
Hence

\inf_{b}E_{S^{\prime}}(b)=\frac{d}{4}\cdot\inf_{b}E_{\mathrm{small}}(P_{b}).

Since
S^{\prime}
is a valid shattered tree for
\mathcal{F}
, by definition of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})
,

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})\ \geq\ \inf_{b}E_{S^{\prime}}(b)\ =\ \frac{d}{4}\cdot\inf_{b}E_{\mathrm{small}}(P_{b}).

Choose a branch
b^{*}
attaining the infimum. The corresponding branch
P:=P_{b^{*}}
of
T
follows the large-gap
rule by construction and satisfies

E_{\mathrm{small}}(P)\leq\frac{4}{d}\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})
.
∎

Next, we bound the total loss of the comparison class using our generic potential argument.

C.3
Bounding
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})
by
\widetilde{O}(kd)
via Euclidean covers

Let
\Theta:=([-1,1]\times B_{2}^{d}(0,1))^{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k(d+1)}
be the parameter set for
\mathcal{H}
:
for

\theta=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},w^{1},\dots,w^{k})\in\Theta

define

h_{\theta}(x):=\textnormal{clip}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\,\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)\Big)\in[-1,1],\qquad g_{\theta}(x):=\sqrt{d}\,h_{\theta}(x)\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
and
\mathcal{F}=\{g_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
.

Lemma C.9

(Euclidean parameter Lipschitzness for
\mathcal{F}
)
.

For all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|g_{\theta}(x)-g_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|\ \leq\ \sqrt{2kd}\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad d_{\ell_{\cap}}(g_{\theta},g_{\theta^{\prime}})\ \leq\ \min\left\{1,\ \frac{kd}{2}\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
clip
is
1
-Lipschitz and
|\textnormal{ReLU}(u)-\textnormal{ReLU}(v)|\leq|u-v|
, for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
we have

\displaystyle|h_{\theta}(x)-h_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big|a_{j}\,\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)-a_{j}^{\prime}\,\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j\prime}\cdot x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(|a_{j}-a_{j}^{\prime}|\cdot|\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)|\ +\ |a_{j}^{\prime}|\cdot|\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)-\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j\prime}\cdot x)|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(|a_{j}-a_{j}^{\prime}|\ +\ |\langle w^{j}-w^{j\prime},x\rangle|\Big)\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(|a_{j}-a_{j}^{\prime}|\ +\ \|w^{j}-w^{j\prime}\|_{2}\Big),

using
\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)\in[0,1]
and
|a_{j}^{\prime}|\leq 1
.
By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}|a_{j}-a_{j}^{\prime}|\ \leq\ \sqrt{k}\,\|a-a^{\prime}\|_{2},\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|w^{j}-w^{j\prime}\|_{2}\ \leq\ \sqrt{k}\,\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{2},

hence
\sup_{x}|h_{\theta}(x)-h_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|\leq\sqrt{2k}\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}
.
Multiplying by
\sqrt{d}
gives the first inequality.
For the second, use
\ell_{\cap}(u,v)\leq(u-v)^{2}/4
and take
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
.
∎

Lemma C.10

(Covering numbers of
\Theta
)
.

For all
r\in(0,1]
,

N(\Theta,r;\|\cdot\|_{2})\ \leq\ \left(\frac{3\sqrt{2k}}{r}\right)^{k(d+1)}.

Proof.

Note that
\Theta\subseteq B_{2}^{k(d+1)}(0,\sqrt{2k})
. A standard volumetric bound for Euclidean balls
[
33
]
gives

N(B_{2}^{k(d+1)}(0,\sqrt{2k}),r)\leq(3\sqrt{2k}/r)^{k(d+1)}
for
r\in(0,1]
.
∎

The following summarizes the total loss bound for the comparison class.

Proposition C.11

(Online dimension of the comparison class)
.

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})\ =\ O\big(kd\log(d)\big).

Proof.

By Lemma
C.9
,

d_{\ell_{\cap}}(g_{\theta},g_{\theta^{\prime}})\leq\min\{1,(L\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2})^{2}\}
with
L:=\sqrt{kd/2}
.
Thus, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, an
\varepsilon
-cover in
d_{\ell_{\cap}}
is induced by an

r:=\min\{1,\sqrt{\varepsilon}/L\}
cover in
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, so

N(\mathcal{F},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ N(\Theta,r;\|\cdot\|_{2}).

By Lemma
C.10
,

N(\mathcal{F},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{3\sqrt{2k}}{r}\Big)^{k(d+1)}=\Big(3\sqrt{2k}\,\max\{1,L/\sqrt{\varepsilon}\}\Big)^{k(d+1)}\leq\Big(\frac{3\sqrt{2k}\,(1+L)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big)^{k(d+1)},\qquad(\varepsilon\in(0,1]).

Recall the entropy potential
\Phi(\mathcal{F}):=\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}N(\mathcal{F},\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon
. Then

\log_{2}N(\mathcal{F},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ k(d+1)\log_{2}\!\big(3\sqrt{2k}\,(1+L)\big)\ +\ \frac{k(d+1)}{2}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon),

so using
\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=1/\ln 2
,

\Phi(\mathcal{F})\ \leq\ k(d+1)\log_{2}\!\big(3\sqrt{2k}\,(1+L)\big)\ +\ \frac{k(d+1)}{2\ln 2}\ =\ O\big(kd\log d\big).

Finally, since
\ell_{\cap}
is a
2
-approximate pseudo-metric (Lemma
C.2
),
the standard entropy-potential branch argument yields

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})\leq 8\,\Phi(\mathcal{F})=O(kd\log d)
.
∎

We conclude the two parts (large and small gaps) in the following section.

C.4
Main theorem

Proof of
Theorem

1.6
:

Proof.

Fix any scaled Littlestone tree
T
shattered by
\mathcal{H}
.
Apply Lemma
C.8
to obtain a branch
P
that follows the large-gap
fat-shattering-drop rule at every node with
\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}
and satisfies

\sum_{u\in P:\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\leq\frac{4}{d}\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})
.
For this same branch
P
, the large-gap bound from Lemma
C.5
applies
(because
P
follows the required rule at every large-gap node), yielding

\sum_{u\in P:\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ 16\sum_{r=-1}^{K}\alpha_{r}^{2}\,\fat_{\alpha_{r}}(\mathcal{H})\ =\ O(k^{2}\log^{4}d),

where the last step uses Lemma
C.7
.
For the remaining nodes on
P
with
\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
, Proposition
C.11
gives

\sum_{u\in P:\ \Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{4}{d}\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F};\ell_{\cap})\ =\ O(k\log d).

Summing the two contributions yields
\sum_{u\in P}\Delta_{u}^{2}=O(k^{2}\log^{4}d)
.
Since on shattered trees for
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[-1,1]^{\mathcal{X}}
we have
\ell_{\cap}(s_{u,0},s_{u,1})=\Delta_{u}^{2}/4
,
it follows that
E_{T}(P)=\sum_{u\in P}\ell_{\cap}(s_{u,0},s_{u,1})=O(k^{2}\log^{4}d)
, hence
\inf_{b}E_{T}(b)\leq O(k^{2}\log^{4}d)
.
Taking
\sup_{T}
gives
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H};\ell_{\cap})\leq O(k^{2}\log^{4}d)
.

Finally, for realizable squared loss with labels in
[-1,1]
, one may clip the learner’s predictions to
[-1,1]

without increasing squared loss, and on
[-1,1]
we have squared loss equals to four times the loss
\ell_{\cap}
.
Thus, the minimax realizable deterministic cumulative squared loss is at most

4\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H};\ell_{\cap})=O(k^{2}\log^{4}d)
. This gives the proof of the theorem.
∎

C.5
Extension to depth-
L
, width-
k
networks.

We generalize
Theorem

1.6
to depth-
L
networks.
The natural deep extension of the bounded-norm shallow class
\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
imposes

\|w\|_{2}\leq 1
on each neuron’s weight vector at every layer (matching the shallow constraint
\|w^{j}\|_{2}\leq 1
).

Concretely, fix integers
L\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
.
Define
\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}
to be the class of all functions
h:\mathcal{X}\to[-1,1]
of the form

h(x)=\textnormal{clip}\!\big(\langle a,z^{(L-1)}(x)\rangle+c\big),

where
z^{(0)}=x
,

z^{(\ell)}=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\ell}z^{(\ell-1)}+b_{\ell})
for
\ell=1,\dots,L-1
,
with:

•

W_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
with each row
\|w_{1,j}\|_{2}\leq 1
;

W_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
with each row
\|w_{\ell,j}\|_{2}\leq 1
for
\ell\geq 2
,

•

b_{\ell}\in[-1,1]^{k}
for
\ell=1,\dots,L-1
,
a\in[-1,1]^{k}
,
c\in[-1,1]
.

Set
p:=kd+(L-2)k^{2}+Lk+1
(total scalar parameters) and

\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}:=\{\sqrt{d}\,h:\ h\in\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\}
.

Lemma C.12

(Depth-
L
analogue of
Theorem

1.6
)
.

For every fixed
L\geq 2
it holds that

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap}\big)\ =\ O_{L}\!\Big(k^{L}\,\log^{\,3L+1}\!\big(edkL\big)\Big).

Consequently, the minimax realizable cumulative squared loss is

\widetilde{O}(k^{L})
for fixed
L
.
For
L=2
(one hidden layer) this recovers the
\widetilde{O}(k^{2})
bound of
Theorem

1.6
.

In the following, we use Big-O notations omitting constants that depend on
L
. In addition, for simplicity we use
C_{L},C^{\prime}_{L}
, and
C^{\prime\prime}_{L}
to denote specific constants that depend only on
L
.

We give the proof of the above lemma below. Whenever the arguments are analogous to the setting of
L=2
, we may omit some of the details for conciseness.

Proof.

We repeat the large-gap/small-gap decomposition with threshold
1/\sqrt{d}
.

Large gaps.

The proof of Lemma
C.5
is purely combinatorial, so it immediately applies:
for any shattered tree, along a branch that follows the decreasing-child rule at large-gap nodes,

\sum_{\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ 16\sum_{r=-1}^{K}\alpha_{r}^{2}\,\fat_{\alpha_{r}}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\big),\qquad K=\lceil\log_{2}(\sqrt{d})\rceil.

It suffices to bound
\fat_{\alpha}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\big)
.
We do so via the sequential Rademacher complexity, using a

vector Rademacher peeling argument
that exploits the row independence of the weight matrices.

Vector Rademacher recursion.

For a class of vector-valued functions
\mathcal{V}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{\mathcal{X}}
and a sequential tree
z=(z_{t})_{t=1}^{T}
of depth
T
(i.e., each
z_{t}:\{\pm 1\}^{t-1}\to\mathcal{X}
and
z_{t}(\varepsilon)
denotes
z_{t}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{t-1})
), define

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V})\ :=\ \frac{1}{T}\,\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\!\Big[\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\,\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,v(z_{t}(\varepsilon))\Big\|_{2}\Big]

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{T}\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{\pm 1\}
independently.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,\dots,L-1
) denote the class of layer-
\ell
activation vectors

z^{(\ell)}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
(ranging over all admissible weight and bias choices at layers
1,\dots,\ell
).
We prove:

Claim C.13
.

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V}_{\ell})\ \leq\ \sqrt{k}\,\rho(T)\cdot\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V}_{\ell-1})\ +\ O\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}\,\rho(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right),\qquad\ell\geq 2,

(7)

where
\rho(T)=O(\log^{3/2}(eT))
is the sequential contraction overhead from

[
28
, Cor. 5]
.

Normalization for Cor. 5.

Corollary 5 in
[
28
]
is stated for classes
G\subseteq[-1,1]^{\mathcal{Z}}
.
If
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{Z}}
satisfies
\sup_{g\in G,z\in\mathcal{Z}}|g(z)|\leq B<\infty
, define

\bar{G}:=\{g/B:\ g\in G\}\subseteq[-1,1]^{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\phi_{B}(u,z):=\phi(Bu,z)
.
Then
\phi_{B}(\cdot,z)
is
(B\lambda)
-Lipschitz whenever
\phi(\cdot,z)
is
\lambda
-Lipschitz, and
by Lemma 3 and Cor. 5 of
[
28
]
we have, whenever
\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\bar{G})\geq 1/T
,

\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\phi\circ G)=\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\phi_{B}\circ\bar{G})\ \leq\ \rho(T)\,\lambda\cdot\mathfrak{R}_{T}(G).

(8)

In our applications
G
is symmetric and contains the constants
\pm 1
(via the bias), hence

\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\bar{G})\geq c/(B\sqrt{T})
for a universal constant
c>0
, so the condition

\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\bar{G})\geq 1/T
holds for all
T\geq c^{-2}B^{2}
, which is satisfied by the choices of
T
used below.

Proof.

Fix a sequential tree
z
and write
y_{t}:=z^{(\ell-1)}(z_{t}(\varepsilon))\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

for the layer-
(\ell-1)
activations (which depend on
\varepsilon_{<t}
and on the choice of all
lower-layer parameters).
The layer-
\ell
activations are
z^{(\ell)}_{t}=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\ell}y_{t}+b_{\ell})
, with
W_{\ell}

having
k
rows
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
satisfying
\|w_{j}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
b_{\ell}\in[-1,1]^{k}
.
Since the
k
rows act independently for fixed
y
(where each row
w_{j}
only affects coordinate
j
),

\displaystyle\sup_{W_{\ell},b_{\ell}}\;\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\ell}y_{t}+b_{\ell})\Big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\,|b|\leq 1}\;\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b)\Big)^{\!2}

\displaystyle\leq k\cdot\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\,|b|\leq 1}\;\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b)\Big)^{\!2},

where the inequality holds because all
k
optimization problems have the same feasible set.
Taking square roots:

\sup_{W_{\ell},b_{\ell}}\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{\ell}y_{t}+b_{\ell})\Big\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{k}\cdot\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\,|b|\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b)\Big|.

Let
G:=\{t\mapsto\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b:\ \|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\ |b|\leq 1\}
and
\phi(u):=\operatorname{ReLU}(u)
.
By (
8
) (with
\lambda=1
),

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big[\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\,|b|\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b)\Big|\Big]\ \leq\ \rho(T)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big[\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1,\,|b|\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\,(\langle w,y_{t}\rangle+b)\Big|\Big].

The right-hand side splits as

\sup_{\|w\|_{2}\leq 1}\Big|\Big\langle w,\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}y_{t}\Big\rangle\Big|\ +\ \sup_{|b|\leq 1}\Big|b\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\Big|\ =\ \Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}y_{t}\Big\|_{2}\ +\ \Big|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\Big|.

Moreover
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\big|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varepsilon_{t}\big|=O(\sqrt{T})
.
Therefore, taking
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[\cdot]
, then
\sup
over lower-layer parameters, and dividing by
T
yields (
7
).
∎

Base case.

For layer
1
, the same argument gives

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\ =\ O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}\,\rho(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right).

Unrolling the recursion.

By induction, using (
7
) for
\ell=2,\dots,L-1
and the base case:

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V}_{L-1})\ \leq\ \frac{C_{L}\,(\sqrt{k})^{L-1}\rho(T)^{L-1}}{\sqrt{T}}.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the
pre-clipped
scalar class

\mathcal{G}:=\{x\mapsto\langle a,z^{(L-1)}(x)\rangle+c:\ a\in[-1,1]^{k},\ c\in[-1,1]\}
.
Since
clip
is
1
-Lipschitz, applying (
8
) to
\textnormal{clip}\circ\mathcal{G}
yields

\mathfrak{R}_{T}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\big)\ \leq\ \rho(T)\cdot\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\mathcal{G}).

Moreover,

\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\mathcal{G})\ \leq\ \sqrt{k}\cdot\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{vec}}_{T}(\mathcal{V}_{L-1})\ +\ O\!\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\Big),

where the
\sqrt{k}
is by Cauchy–Schwarz and the
O(1/\sqrt{T})
term comes from the
free offset
c\in[-1,1]
.
Consequently,

\mathfrak{R}_{T}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\big)\ \leq\ \frac{C^{\prime}_{L}\,k^{L/2}\rho(T)^{L}}{\sqrt{T}}.

Fat-shattering bound.

By
[
28
, Prop. 9]
:

\alpha\sqrt{\min\{\fat_{\alpha},T\}/T}\leq C\,\mathfrak{R}_{T}(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}})\leq C^{\prime}_{L}\,k^{L/2}\rho(T)^{L}/\sqrt{T}.

Choosing

T=\Theta\!\big(\alpha^{-2}k^{L}\log^{3L}(ekL/\alpha)\big)

forces
\fat_{\alpha}<T
, hence

\fat_{\alpha}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}\big)\ \leq\ C_{L}^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{k^{L}}{\alpha^{2}}\,\log^{3L}\!\Big(\frac{ekL}{\alpha}\Big).

Plugging into the truncated multiscale sum:

\sum_{\Delta_{u}>1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ C_{L}\,k^{L}\sum_{r=-1}^{K}\log^{3L}\!\big(ekL/\alpha_{r}\big)\ =\ O\!\Big(k^{L}\log^{3L+1}(edkL)\Big).

Small gaps.

Apply Lemma
C.8
with

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}
:
for the same branch
P
,

\sum_{\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{4}{d}\,\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap}).

We bound
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap})
via the entropy potential.
Let
K_{2}
denote the
\ell_{2}
parameter Lipschitz constant:

\|h_{\theta}-h_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq K_{2}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2}
.
Since
\operatorname{ReLU}
is
1
-Lipschitz and every weight matrix has spectral norm at most
\sqrt{k}

(each of
k
rows has
\ell_{2}
-norm
\leq 1
), a standard inductive argument
(analogous to Lemma
G.8
given in the next sections) gives
K_{2}=O\big(k^{(L-1)/2}\sqrt{L}\big)
.
Multiplying by
\sqrt{d}
for
\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}
, a volumetric cover of the
parameter set
\Theta\subseteq B_{2}(0,\sqrt{2Lk})
in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
yields

\log N(\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}},\varepsilon;\ell_{\cap})\ \leq\ p\,\log\!\Big(\frac{C\sqrt{Lkd}\,K_{2}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big),\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Integrating:

\Phi(\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}})=O\!\big(p\,\log(C\sqrt{Lkd}\,K_{2})\big)=O(pL\log(kd))
, and hence

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{F}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap})=O(pL\log(kd))
.
Therefore

\sum_{\Delta_{u}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}}\Delta_{u}^{2}\ =\ O\!\Big(\frac{pL\log(kd)}{d}\Big)\ =\ O\!\big(kL\log(kd)\big),

since the

term dominates for large

Adding large- and small-gap contributions:

\sum_{u\in P}\Delta_{u}^{2}=O\!\Big(k^{L}\log^{3L+1}(edkL)+kL\log(kd)\Big)=O\!\Big(k^{L}\log^{3L+1}(edkL)\Big).

Using
\ell_{\cap}(s_{u,0},s_{u,1})=\Delta_{u}^{2}/4
and the same reduction to squared loss as in

proves the claim.
∎

A
\Omega(k^{L-1})
lower bound for large

In the following, we prove an almost matching lower bound.

Lemma C.14
.

Assume
d\geq k^{L-1}
. Then

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap}\big)\ \geq\ \Omega\!\big(k^{L-1}\big).

Proof.

Consider the subclass
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}}
obtained by fixing

W_{\ell}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
and
b_{\ell}=0
for every
\ell=2,\dots,L-1
,
and
a=\mathbf{1},c=0
, while allowing
(W_{1},b_{1})
to vary subject to the row constraints.
An easy induction gives that every
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
can be written as

h(x)\;=\;\textnormal{clip}\!\Big(S\cdot g(x)\Big),\qquad S:=k^{(L-2)/2},\qquad g(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{ReLU}(\langle w_{1,j},x\rangle+b_{1,j}).

Let

and

(note

For

(standard basis vectors), so

\sigma_{j,i}\in\{0,1\}
.
Set

Then
\|w_{1,j}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{m_{0}}}{S}=1
, so this is feasible, and for every

Thus the set

is
(1/4)
-fat-shattered by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

(with common threshold

), hence
\fat_{1/4}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\geq m=\Omega(k^{L-1})
.

Finally, on a

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap})\geq\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime};\ell_{\cap})\geq m/16=\Omega(k^{L-1}).

∎

We conclude this subsection with the following discussion.

The covering route and small
d
.

A complementary bound avoids

entirely:
since the class has

with Lipschitz constant

For

and is tighter than the Rademacher route.

Summary.

Combining the Rademacher upper bound

with the covering upper bound

:

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}\!\big(\mathcal{H}_{L,k,d}^{\mathrm{deep}};\ell_{\cap}\big)\ =\ O\!\big(k^{L}\cdot\mathrm{polylog}(kdL)\big)\qquad\text{for }d\geq k^{L-1},

while the covering bound gives

.
In the polylogarithmic-in-

as the fat-shattering lower bound via the amplifying subclass

Appendix D
Proper and
\varepsilon
-efficient Lower Bound for Bounded
k
-ReLUs

For a sample
S=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{2}\leq 1\}
define

L_{S}(h)\ :=\ \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2},\qquad\mathrm{OPT}(S)\ :=\ \min_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}}L_{S}(h).

We call
S

realizable
if
\mathrm{OPT}(S)=0
.
An algorithm is
proper
if it outputs
\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
.
We call it
\varepsilon
-efficient
if its running time is
2^{o(1/\varepsilon)}\mathrm{poly}(d,m)
.
An algorithm achieves
realizable average loss
\leq\varepsilon

if on every realizable sequence of length
T
it guarantees

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(h_{t}(x_{t})-y_{t})^{2}\ \leq\ \varepsilon.

For a network returned as parameters
(a,w^{1},\ldots,w^{k})
, write its
unclipped
form as

\tilde{h}(x)\ :=\ \sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\,\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\!\cdot x),\qquad\text{and}\qquad h(x)\ :=\ \mathrm{clip}(\tilde{h}(x)),

where
\mathrm{clip}(z):=\min\{1,\max\{-1,z\}\}
. Note that for
\|x\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|w^{j}\|_{2}\leq 1
,
we have
0\leq\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)\leq 1
, hence
|\tilde{h}(x)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}|a_{j}|\leq k
.

Theorem D.1

(Proper batch hardness
[
21
]
)
.

Assume
Gap-ETH

[
13
,
23
]
and fix any constant
k\geq 2
.
There is no (possibly randomized)
\varepsilon
-efficient proper algorithm that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,
every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
every realizable sample
S\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{2}\leq 1\}\times[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
of size
m
,
outputs
\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
with
L_{S}(\hat{h})\leq\varepsilon
.
In particular, taking
\varepsilon:=1/d
rules out running time
2^{o(d)}\mathrm{poly}(d,m)
.

Proof.

Let
C_{k}:=1+4\bigl(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}
.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that there is an
\varepsilon
-efficient proper algorithm
\mathcal{A}
for our
(clipped) class
\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
.

Given any realizable sample
S=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
with
\|x_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
y_{i}\in[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
,
run
\mathcal{A}
with accuracy parameter
\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\varepsilon/C_{k}
and obtain a hypothesis

h=\mathrm{clip}(\tilde{h})\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
, where
\tilde{h}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\cdot x)

is the unclipped network.

Let
B:=\{i:\ |\tilde{h}(x_{i})|>1\}
be the indices where clipping is active. For every

we have

h(x_{i})\in\{-1,1\}
and
|y_{i}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, hence
(h(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2}\geq(1-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}
.
Therefore,

\frac{|B|}{m}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in B}(h(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2}\ \leq\ L_{S}(h)\ \leq\ \varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\text{so}\quad\frac{|B|}{m}\leq 4\varepsilon^{\prime}.

On

we have

, while on
B
we use
|\tilde{h}(x_{i})|\leq k
and
|y_{i}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}

to bound
(\tilde{h}(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2}\leq(k+\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}
. Thus,

L_{S}(\tilde{h})\ \leq\ L_{S}(h)\;+\;\frac{|B|}{m}\bigl(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\ \leq\ \varepsilon^{\prime}+4\varepsilon^{\prime}\bigl(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\ =\ C_{k}\,\varepsilon^{\prime}\ =\ \varepsilon.

Finally, convert
\tilde{h}
to the exact parameterization of
[
21
]
by
setting
\bar{a}_{j}:=\mathrm{sign}(a_{j})\in\{\pm 1\}
(and if
a_{j}=0
take
\bar{a}_{j}=1
) and

\bar{w}^{j}:=|a_{j}|\,w^{j}
; then
\|\bar{w}^{j}\|_{2}\leq 1
and

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{a}_{j}\textnormal{ReLU}(\bar{w}^{j}\!\cdot x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\textnormal{ReLU}(w^{j}\!\cdot x)=\tilde{h}(x)
.
Moreover, given any realizable instance of the bounded
k
-ReLU training problem with labels in
[-k,k]
,
we can scale the labels by a factor
\alpha\leq 1/(2k)
(and keep the same inputs) to obtain an equivalent

\varepsilon
-approximate solution for the scaled instance
yields a
(\varepsilon/\alpha^{2})
-approximate solution for the original instance.
Choosing

as a fixed constant (depending only on

\varepsilon
-efficiency up to constant factors
in

, and thus contradicts the results of
[
21
]
.
∎

Lemma D.2
.

Suppose there exists an
\varepsilon
-efficient proper online algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that achieves realizable
average loss
\leq\varepsilon
for
\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
for every horizon
T
.
Then there exists an
\varepsilon
-efficient proper batch algorithm that, on every realizable sample

S
of size
m
, outputs
\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
with
L_{S}(\hat{h})\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Given a realizable sample
S
, run
\mathcal{A}
for
T:=m
rounds on a randomized online sequence obtained by sampling

i_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}([m])
independently and feeding
(x_{t},y_{t})=(x_{i_{t}},y_{i_{t}})
to
\mathcal{A}
.
Record the hypotheses
h_{1},\dots,h_{T}\in\mathcal{H}_{k,d}
output by
\mathcal{A}
.

Conditioned on the history up to round

, we have

\mathbb{E}[(h_{t}(x_{i_{t}})-y_{i_{t}})^{2}\mid h_{t}]=L_{S}(h_{t})
.
Hence

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{S}(h_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(h_{t}(x_{i_{t}})-y_{i_{t}})^{2}\right]\leq\varepsilon.

Therefore
\mathbb{E}[\min_{t\leq T}L_{S}(h_{t})]\leq\varepsilon
(since
\min\leq
average). By Markov,

\Pr\big[\min_{t\leq T}L_{S}(h_{t})>2\varepsilon\big]\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Compute

for all

and output

; then

\Pr[L_{S}(\hat{h})\leq 2\varepsilon]\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Run the procedure
O(\log(1/\delta))
times and output the best hypothesis found to boost success probability,
and replace
\varepsilon
by
\varepsilon/2
to obtain

with high constant probability.
The runtime remains
2^{o(1/\varepsilon)}\mathrm{poly}(d,m)
.
∎

Appendix E
Classification

In the following sections, we focus on regressions for achieving loss bounds independent of the time horizon. For classification on the other hand, even highly restricted function classes have unbounded loss as we show next.

For now, we consider the family
\mathcal{N}
of
\operatorname{ReLU}
networks with input and output dimension
1
, a single hidden layer with two hidden neurons, and such that the absolute values of all weights and biases are bounded by
1
. We assume the input domain is
\mathcal{X}=[-1,1]
.
In the following theorem, we strengthen
[
20
, Theorem 4.13]
in two orthogonal criteria: most importantly, even though the nets in
\mathcal{N}
have bounded weights and biases, we prove that the Littlestone dimension of
\mathcal{N}
is still infinite. Less importantly, we also use networks with only two hidden neurons, instead of
4
.

Theorem E.1
.

We have
\mathrm{Ldim}(\mathcal{N})=\infty
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. For every
\theta\in[-1+\varepsilon,1]
, define

f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x)\ :=\ \operatorname{ReLU}(\theta-x)\;-\;\operatorname{ReLU}(\theta-x-\varepsilon),\qquad x\in[-1,1].

We first verify that
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}\in\mathcal{N}
. Consider a two-hidden-unit ReLU network

x\ \mapsto\ a_{1}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(w_{1}x+b_{1})\;+\;a_{2}\,\operatorname{ReLU}(w_{2}x+b_{2})\;+\;b.

Set

w_{1}=w_{2}=-1,\qquad b_{1}=\theta,\qquad b_{2}=\theta-\varepsilon,\qquad a_{1}=1,\qquad a_{2}=-1,\qquad b=0.

All parameters have absolute value at most
1
since
\theta\in[-1+\varepsilon,1]
and
\varepsilon\leq 1
, and this choice implements
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}
.

Next, note that for any
x\in[-1,1]
:

f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\geq\theta,\\
\varepsilon,&x\leq\theta-\varepsilon,\end{cases}

(9)

(and for
x\in(\theta-\varepsilon,\theta)
the value lies in
(0,\varepsilon)
).
Indeed, if
x=\theta+\delta
with
\delta\geq 0
, then

f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(-\delta)-\operatorname{ReLU}(-\delta-\varepsilon)=0-0=0.

If
x=\theta-\varepsilon^{\prime}
with
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon
, then

f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(\varepsilon^{\prime})-\operatorname{ReLU}(\varepsilon^{\prime}-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{\prime}-(\varepsilon^{\prime}-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon.

We now show that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a complete binary tree of depth
d
that is shattered by

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\ :=\ \{f_{\varepsilon,\theta}:\ \theta\in[-1+\varepsilon,1]\}\subseteq\mathcal{N},

and hence
\mathrm{Ldim}(\mathcal{N})=\infty
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and set
\varepsilon:=2^{-d-2}
. We construct a depth-
d
shattered tree as follows.
Label every left edge by
0
and every right edge by
\varepsilon
.
For each node
v
we maintain an interval
I_{v}=[L_{v},U_{v}]\subseteq[-1+\varepsilon,1]
of thresholds consistent with the labels along the path from the root to
v
.

At the root, set
I_{\mathrm{root}}=[-1+\varepsilon,1]
.
Given an internal node
v
with
I_{v}=[L_{v},U_{v}]
satisfying
|I_{v}|:=U_{v}-L_{v}>\varepsilon
, label
v
by

x_{v}\ :=\ \frac{L_{v}+U_{v}-\varepsilon}{2}.

Then
x_{v}\in[L_{v},U_{v}]
and
x_{v}+\varepsilon\in[L_{v},U_{v}]
.
Let
v_{\ell}
and
v_{r}
denote the left and right children of
v
. Define

I_{v_{\ell}}:=[L_{v},x_{v}],\qquad I_{v_{r}}:=[x_{v}+\varepsilon,U_{v}].

(10)

These intervals are nonempty since
|I_{v}|>\varepsilon
.
Moreover, by (
9
), for every
\theta\in I_{v_{\ell}}
we have
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x_{v})=0
,
and for every
\theta\in I_{v_{r}}
we have
f_{\varepsilon,\theta}(x_{v})=\varepsilon
. Thus the labeling choice at
v
is realizable by
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
and the recursive update maintains the consistency invariant.

It remains to verify that along every path, the recursion can proceed for at least
d
levels, i.e., that
|I_{v}|>\varepsilon
for all nodes
v
of depth at most
d
.
Let
\ell_{t}
denote the length
|I_{v}|
at depth
t
(it is the same for all nodes at the same depth by symmetry of the construction).
From (
10
),

\ell_{0}=2-\varepsilon,\qquad\ell_{t}=\frac{\ell_{t-1}-\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{for }t\geq 1.

Solving yields
\ell_{t}=2^{-t+1}-\varepsilon
, which can be checked by induction:

\ell_{t}=\frac{(2^{-t+2}-\varepsilon)-\varepsilon}{2}=2^{-t+1}-\varepsilon.

In particular,

\ell_{d}=2^{-d+1}-\varepsilon=2^{-d+1}-2^{-d-2}=2^{-d-2}(8-1)>2^{-d-2}=\varepsilon.

Therefore all nodes up to depth
d
satisfy
|I_{v}|>\varepsilon
, so the constructed tree is a complete depth-
d
shattered tree for
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.
Since
d
was arbitrary,
\mathrm{Ldim}(\mathcal{N})=\infty
.
∎

The above theorem establishes that with
0/1
loss, even the simple nets in
\mathcal{N}
are unlearnable.
When we change the loss to squared loss, we shift to a regression problem, which is potentially easier.
In this paper we show that
\mathcal{N}
admits a finite realizable cumulative-loss guarantee under squared loss.

F.2
Parametric (polynomial) covering numbers

A common regime is
polynomial metric entropy
, which typically arises for classes
parameterized by a bounded number of parameters and that are Lipschitz in the parameters
with respect to
d_{\ell}
. In this regime, the covering numbers satisfy

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\lesssim(C/\varepsilon)^{p}
for some

, so the entropy potential

\int_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})}\log N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon

(up to constants depending on

and
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})

Corollary F.1

(Polynomial covers imply finite potential)
.

Assume
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
and there exist constants
A\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
such that for all

\varepsilon\in(0,1]

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{p}.

Then

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\big(\log_{2}A+1/\ln 2\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ 4c\,p\big(\log_{2}A+1/\ln 2\big).

Proof.

By the assumed covering bound,

\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ p\log_{2}(A/\varepsilon)\ =\ p\log_{2}A+p\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon).

Integrating over
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
gives

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\leq p\log_{2}A+p\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=p\log_{2}A+\frac{p}{\ln 2},

using
\int_{0}^{1}\ln(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=1
.
The bound on
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})
follows from
Theorem

1.1
.
∎

Appendix G

This section provides a generic route to upper bound the entropy potential

Fix an instance domain

Definition G.1

(Modulus-Lipschitz parameterization)
.

be a normed space and let

We have the following general claim.

Lemma G.2

\Theta

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set
r:=\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)
.
Let
S\subseteq\Theta
be an
r
-cover of
\Theta
under
\|\cdot\|
.
Define
\mathcal{N}:=\{h_{s}:\ s\in S\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.
For any
\theta\in\Theta
choose
s\in S
such that
\|\theta-s\|\leq r
.
By
\omega
-Lipschitzness and monotonicity of
\omega
,

d_{\ell}(h_{\theta},h_{s})\ \leq\ \omega(\|\theta-s\|)\ \leq\ \omega(r)\ \leq\ \varepsilon,

where
\omega(r)\leq\varepsilon
holds by the definition of
r=\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)
and the fact that
\omega
is continuous.
Thus
\mathcal{N}
is an
\varepsilon
-cover of
\mathcal{H}
. Minimizing over such
S
yields

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\leq N(\Theta,r)=N(\Theta,\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon))
.
∎

G.1
Entropy potential bounds from parameter entropy

Assume
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H}):=\sup_{f,g\in\mathcal{H}}d_{\ell}(f,g)<\infty
and recall

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ :=\ \int_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})}\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon.

Lemma

G.2
immediately reduces bounding
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
to bounding the metric entropy of
\Theta
.

Corollary G.3

(Potential bound via parameter entropy)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
G.2
, if
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
then

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ \int_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})}\log_{2}N\big(\Theta,\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)\big)\,d\varepsilon.

Proof.

By Lemma
G.2
, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\leq\log_{2}N(\Theta,\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon))
.
Integrating over
(0,\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})]
gives the claim (the integrands are measurable as
\varepsilon\mapsto N(\cdot,\varepsilon)

is nonincreasing).
∎

G.2
Parametric polynomial covers

The most common regime is when
\Theta
has polynomial covering numbers in the chosen norm.
The next corollaries show that in this case
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
(and hence
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})
) is finite and scales
linearly with the parameter dimension.

Corollary G.4

(Linear modulus: absolute-loss-type control)
.

Assume
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
and that the parameterization is linear-modulus Lipschitz:

d_{\ell}(h_{\theta},h_{\theta^{\prime}})\ \leq\ L\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,
i.e.,
\omega(t)=Lt
for a Lipschitz constant
L>0
. Assume moreover that for some constants
C\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
,

N(\Theta,r)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{C}{r}\Big)^{p}\qquad\text{for all }r\in(0,1].

Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{C\max\{L,1\}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{p},

and consequently

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\Big(\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)+\frac{1}{\ln 2}\Big).

Proof.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, Lemma
G.2
with
\omega(t)=Lt
gives

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\leq N(\Theta,\varepsilon/L)
(since
\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon/L
).
If
L\geq 1
then
N(\Theta,\varepsilon/L)\leq(CL/\varepsilon)^{p}
.
If
L<1
then
\varepsilon/L>1
, so by monotonicity

N(\Theta,\varepsilon/L)\leq N(\Theta,1)\leq C^{p}\leq(C/\varepsilon)^{p}
.

In both cases,

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{C\max\{L,1\}}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{p}.

Therefore,

\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ p\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)+p\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon).

Integrating over
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
yields

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)+p\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon\ =\ p\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)+\frac{p}{\ln 2},

using
\int_{0}^{1}\ln(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=1
.
∎

Corollary G.5

(Power modulus: squared and
q
-power losses)
.

Assume
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
and that for some
q\geq 1
,

d_{\ell}(h_{\theta},h_{\theta^{\prime}})\ \leq\ (L\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|)^{q}
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,
i.e.,
\omega(t)=(Lt)^{q}
for
L>0
. Assume again that
N(\Theta,r)\leq(C/r)^{p}
for all
r\in(0,1]
.
Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{C\max\{L,1\}}{\varepsilon^{1/q}}\Big)^{p},

and consequently

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)\ +\ \frac{p}{q\ln 2}.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, Lemma
G.2
with
\omega(t)=(Lt)^{q}
gives

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ N\!\Big(\Theta,\,\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)\Big)\ =\ N\!\left(\Theta,\,\frac{\varepsilon^{1/q}}{L}\right),

since
\omega^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{1/q}/L
.
If
L\geq 1
then
N(\Theta,\varepsilon^{1/q}/L)\leq(CL/\varepsilon^{1/q})^{p}
.
If
L<1
then
\varepsilon^{1/q}/L>1
, so by monotonicity

N(\Theta,\varepsilon^{1/q}/L)\leq N(\Theta,1)\leq C^{p}\leq(C/\varepsilon^{1/q})^{p}
.
In both cases,

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \Big(\frac{C\max\{L,1\}}{\varepsilon^{1/q}}\Big)^{p}.

Thus
\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\leq p\log_{2}\!\big(C\max\{L,1\}\big)+\frac{p}{q}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)
.
Integrating over
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
yields the displayed bound, using again

\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=1/\ln 2
.
∎

G.3
Implications for realizable online regression

Combining Corollaries
G.3
–
G.5
with

Theorem

1.1
and Theorem
3.1
yields realizable
cumulative-loss bounds under any
c
-approximate pseudo-metric loss
\ell
for which one can
establish a modulus-Lipschitz parameterization and a covering bound for the parameter set
\Theta

in the chosen norm. We give an example for a class of two-ReLU functions.

G.4
Warm Up Application: a two-ReLU class under generic losses

We illustrate how the entropy-potential method yields a covering-based bound for a simple
ReLU network class under a broad family of losses. Then, in the next section we generalize similar arguments to deep networks with arbitrary Lipschitz activations (rather than ReLU).
The argument applies to any loss
\ell

that (i) is a
c
-approximate pseudo-metric (Definition
2.1
) and
(ii) is dominated by a continuous non decreasing function of the absolute label gap.

Network class.

Fix
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[-1,1]^{d}
and recall that
\textnormal{ReLU}(z):=\max\{0,z\}
.
Let
p:=2d+5
and
\Theta:=[-1,1]^{p}
with coordinates

\theta=(w_{1},b_{1},a_{1},w_{2},b_{2},a_{2},c)
where

w_{1},w_{2}\in[-1,1]^{d}
and
b_{1},b_{2},a_{1},a_{2},c\in[-1,1]
.
Define the
clipped
two-ReLU class
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}
by

h_{\theta}(x):=\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\Big(a_{1}\textnormal{ReLU}(\langle w_{1},x\rangle+b_{1})+a_{2}\textnormal{ReLU}(\langle w_{2},x\rangle+b_{2})+c\Big),\qquad\theta\in\Theta,

(11)

where
\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}(z):=\min\{1,\max\{0,z\}\}
. We use the following auxiliary claim.

Lemma G.6
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and let
\Theta=[-1,1]^{p}
. Define

R_{\Theta}\ :=\ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\theta\|,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha\ :=\ \sup_{\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\|u\|.

Then
R_{\Theta}=\alpha
, and for every
r>0
,

N(\Theta,r)\ \leq\ \left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{r}\right)^{p}.

In particular, for every
r\in(0,2\alpha]
,

N(\Theta,r)\ \leq\ \left(\frac{4\alpha}{r}\right)^{p}.

Proof.

Since
\Theta=\{u:\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
, we have
R_{\Theta}=\sup_{\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\|u\|=\alpha
.
Let
\delta:=\min\{1,r/\alpha\}
.
If
\|u-v\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
, then
u-v=\delta z
for some
z
with
\|z\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, hence

\|u-v\|=\delta\|z\|\leq\delta\alpha\leq r
.
Therefore, any
\delta
-cover of
\Theta
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is an
r
-cover in
\|\cdot\|
.
A uniform grid in
[-1,1]^{p}
with mesh
\delta
has size at most
(2/\delta+1)^{p}
, so

N(\Theta,r)\ \leq\ \left(\frac{2}{\delta}+1\right)^{p}\leq\left(1+\frac{2\alpha}{r}\right)^{p}.

If moreover
r\leq 2\alpha
, then
2\alpha/r\geq 1
and thus
1+2\alpha/r\leq 4\alpha/r
, giving

N(\Theta,r)\leq(4\alpha/r)^{p}
.
∎

Theorem G.7

(Two-ReLU entropy potential under dominated losses)
.

Suppose there exists a nondecreasing function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\varphi(0)=0
such that

\ell(u,v)\ \leq\ \varphi\big(|u-v|\big)\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and assume that there exists a constant
K\geq 0
such that for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,

\|h_{\theta}-h_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\ \leq\ K\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|.

(12)

Define the generalized inverse

\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)\ :=\ \sup\{t\geq 0:\ \varphi(t)\leq\varepsilon\}.

Then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})]
,

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \left(\frac{4\alpha K}{\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)}\right)^{p},

with the convention that the right-hand side is
+\infty
if
\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)=0
.
Consequently,

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\,\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\,\log_{2}\!\big(4\alpha K\big)\ +\ p\int_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})}\log_{2}\!\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)}\Big)\,d\varepsilon.

In particular, if
\varphi(t)=t^{q}
for some
q\geq 1
and
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
, then

\Phi(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ p\log_{2}(4\alpha K)\ +\ \frac{p}{q\ln 2}.

Proof.

If
K=0
then (
12
) implies
h_{\theta}
is constant over
\theta\in\Theta
, hence

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)=1
for all
\varepsilon>0
and the claims are immediate. Assume henceforth
K>0
.
For any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,

d_{\ell}(h_{\theta},h_{\theta^{\prime}})=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ell\big(h_{\theta}(x),h_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)\big)\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi\big(|h_{\theta}(x)-h_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|\big)\leq\varphi\big(\|h_{\theta}-h_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\big)\leq\varphi\big(K\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\big).

Thus, any
r
-cover of
\Theta
in
\|\cdot\|
induces a
\varphi(Kr)
-cover of
\mathcal{H}
in
d_{\ell}
, and therefore

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ N\!\left(\Theta,\ \frac{\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)}{K}\right).

Set
r:=\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)/K
. Since
\Theta
has diameter at most
2R_{\Theta}=2\alpha
under
\|\cdot\|
,
(
12
) implies
\|h_{\theta}-h_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\alpha K
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
,
hence
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq\varphi(2\alpha K)
and so
\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)\leq 2\alpha K
for all
\varepsilon\leq\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})
.
Therefore
r\leq 2\alpha
, and Lemma
G.6
yields

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ \leq\ \left(\frac{4\alpha}{r}\right)^{p}=\left(\frac{4\alpha K}{\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)}\right)^{p}.

Taking
\log_{2}
and integrating over
\varepsilon\in(0,\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})]
gives the bound on
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
.
If
\varphi(t)=t^{q}
and
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
, then
\varphi^{\leftarrow}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{1/q}
and

\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}\!\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/q}}\Big)\,d\varepsilon=\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{1}\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=\frac{1}{q\ln 2},

which yields the final bound.
∎

Instantiations.

For
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, one has
\alpha=\sup_{\|u\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\|u\|_{1}=p
, and one may take
K=d+1
.
For
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, one has
\alpha=\sqrt{p}
, and one may take
K=(d+1)\sqrt{p}
.

G.5
Application: depth-
L
networks with Lipschitz activations

We now extend Theorem
G.7
from the clipped two-ReLU class to depth-
L
fully-connected networks with a generic Lipschitz activation. The arguments are analogous to the above proofs.

Network class.

Fix
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[-1,1]^{d}
. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz, i.e.,

|\sigma(u)-\sigma(v)|\leq L_{\sigma}|u-v|
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, and apply
\sigma
coordinatewise to vectors.
Fix integers
L\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
and set the number of scalar parameters as

p\ :=\ kd+(L-2)k^{2}+Lk+1.

Let
\Theta_{L,k}:=[-1,1]^{p}
, interpreted as the set of flattened parameters

\theta=(W_{1},b_{1},\dots,W_{L-1},b_{L-1},a,c),

where
W_{1}\in[-1,1]^{k\times d}
,
b_{1}\in[-1,1]^{k}
, and for
2\leq\ell\leq L-1
we have

W_{\ell}\in[-1,1]^{k\times k}
and
b_{\ell}\in[-1,1]^{k}
, while the output weights satisfy

a\in[-1,1]^{k}
and
c\in[-1,1]
.
For each
\theta\in\Theta_{L,k}
define recursively

z^{(0)}(x)=x,\qquad z^{(\ell)}(x)=\sigma\!\big(W_{\ell}z^{(\ell-1)}(x)+b_{\ell}\big),\quad\ell=1,\dots,L-1,

and define the clipped output

h_{\theta}(x)\ :=\ \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\big(\langle a,z^{(L-1)}(x)\rangle+c\big),\qquad x\in\mathcal{X}.

(13)

Let
\mathcal{H}_{L,k,\sigma}:=\{h_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta_{L,k}\}\subseteq[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}
.

A parameter Lipschitz bound.

Let
M_{0}:=1
and define

M_{1}\ :=\ |\sigma(0)|+L_{\sigma}\,(dM_{0}+1),\qquad M_{\ell}\ :=\ |\sigma(0)|+L_{\sigma}\,(kM_{\ell-1}+1)\quad\text{for }\ell=2,\dots,L-1,

\bar{M}\ :=\ \max_{0\leq\ell\leq L-1}M_{\ell},\qquad S\ :=\ \sum_{s=0}^{L-2}(L_{\sigma}k)^{s}.

Define

K_{L,k,\sigma}\ :=\ (1+\bar{M})\bigl(1+L_{\sigma}S\bigr).

(14)

Lemma G.8
.

For the
\ell_{1}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{L,k}
one has

\|h_{\theta}-h_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\ \leq\ K_{L,k,\sigma}\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{1}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}
is
1
-Lipschitz, it suffices to bound the corresponding
unclipped outputs. Fix
x\in\mathcal{X}
and write
z^{(\ell)}(x)
and
{z^{\prime}}^{(\ell)}(x)
for the hidden
vectors under
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
. By induction, using
|\sigma(t)|\leq|\sigma(0)|+L_{\sigma}|t|
,

\|W_{1}v\|_{\infty}\leq d\|v\|_{\infty}
for
W_{1}\in[-1,1]^{k\times d}
,

\|Wv\|_{\infty}\leq k\|v\|_{\infty}
for
W\in[-1,1]^{k\times k}
, and
\|b_{\ell}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we get

\|z^{(\ell)}(x)\|_{\infty}\leq M_{\ell}
and
\|{z^{\prime}}^{(\ell)}(x)\|_{\infty}\leq M_{\ell}
for all
\ell

Let
\Delta_{\ell}:=\|z^{(\ell)}(x)-{z^{\prime}}^{(\ell)}(x)\|_{1}

\sigma
and

\|Wv\|_{1}\leq k\|v\|_{1}

\Delta_{\ell}\ \leq\ L_{\sigma}\Big(k\Delta_{\ell-1}+\bar{M}\|W_{\ell}-W^{\prime}_{\ell}\|_{1}+\|b_{\ell}-b^{\prime}_{\ell}\|_{1}\Big),\qquad\ell=1,\dots,L-1.

\Delta_{L-1}\ \leq\ L_{\sigma}(\bar{M}+1)\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{L-2}(L_{\sigma}k)^{s}\Big)\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{1}\ =\ L_{\sigma}(\bar{M}+1)S\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{1}.

Appendix H
When the entropy potential diverges

The entropy-potential approach yields a uniform (all-horizon) bound on realizable cumulative loss
precisely when
\Phi(\mathcal{H})<\infty
. In particular, if
\log N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)
diverges too quickly as

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, then
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
and
Theorem

1.1
becomes vacuous.
A typical example is a nonparametric entropy growth of the form

\log N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{-p}
: in this case
\Phi(\mathcal{H})<\infty
holds if and only if
p<1
,
since
\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{-p}\,d\varepsilon<\infty
exactly for
p<1
.
When
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
, one should not expect a horizon-free bound derived from this potential.
instead, it is natural to seek horizon-dependent guarantees. In the following sections, we show that
Theorem

1.1
is not tight, in the sense that there are classes with
N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\sim(C/\varepsilon)^{p}
for large
p
, but their loss can be still bounded by
O(1)
. In addition, we show below that the converse direction does not hold: our potential upper bound may be infinite despite finite scaled littlestone dimension.

Proposition H.1

(
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
while
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
)
.

There exist
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\ell)
with
\ell
a metric and a class
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}

such that
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
,
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\infty
, but
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
and
let
a_{k}=2^{-k}
and
m_{k}:=2^{2^{k}}
, so
a_{k}\log_{2}m_{k}=1
for all
k
. It follows that
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset(0,1]
is a strictly decreasing sequence
with
a_{k}\downarrow 0
and
\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}<\infty
. Let

\mathcal{Y}\ :=\ \{(k,i):k\geq 1,\ i\in[m_{k}]\}.

Define
\ell:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

\ell\big((k,i),(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})\big):=\begin{cases}0,&(k,i)=(k^{\prime},i^{\prime}),\\
a_{k},&k=k^{\prime},\ i\neq i^{\prime},\\
\max\{a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}\},&k\neq k^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Then
\ell
is a metric: for any
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\ell(y_{1},y_{2})\leq\max\{\ell(y_{1},y_{3}),\ell(y_{2},y_{3})\}
, which is immediate from the case analysis
(
\max\{\cdot,\cdot\}
always dominates the larger scale among the involved blocks).
Define the hypothesis class

\mathcal{H}\ :=\ \big\{h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}:\ \exists(i_{k})_{k\geq 1}\text{ with }h(x_{k})=(k,i_{k})\ \forall k\big\}.

For the induced metric
d_{\ell}(f,g)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ell(f(x),g(x))
, we have

d_{\ell}(f,g)=\max\{a_{k}:\ f(x_{k})\neq g(x_{k})\},

so
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{H})=a_{1}<\infty
.

Finite
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})
.

Fix any scaled Littlestone tree for
\mathcal{H}
. If a node queries
x_{k}
for the first time along a branch,
then both outgoing edge labels must lie in
\{(k,i):i\in[m_{k}]\}
, so its gap is either
0
(same label)
or
a_{k}
(distinct labels). After the branch takes one of these edges, the value of
h(x_{k})
is fixed
in the version space along that branch; hence any later query of
x_{k}
on the same branch can only have
both children nonempty if both edge labels coincide with that fixed value, yielding gap
0
.
Therefore, along every branch the total gap is at most
\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}
, and hence for every tree

\inf_{y}\sum_{i}\gamma_{y_{i}}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}.

Taking the supremum over trees gives

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})\ \leq\ \sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\ <\ \infty.

Infinite
\Phi(\mathcal{H})
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
K(\varepsilon):=\max\{k:a_{k}>\varepsilon\}
(which is finite as
a_{k}\downarrow 0
) where
K(\varepsilon)=0
for
\varepsilon\geq a_{1}
.
Then two hypotheses are within
d_{\ell}
-distance
\leq\varepsilon
iff they agree on all coordinates

x_{1},\dots,x_{K(\varepsilon)}
. Thus, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have

B_{d_{\ell}}(f,\varepsilon)=\{g\in\mathcal{H}:\ g(x_{i})=f(x_{i})\ \forall i\leq K(\varepsilon)\},

where
B_{d_{\ell}}(f,\varepsilon):=\{g\in\mathcal{H}:\ d_{\ell}(f,g)\leq\varepsilon\}
denotes the (closed)
\varepsilon
-ball around
f
in
(\mathcal{H},d_{\ell})
.
In particular, any
\varepsilon
-cover must contain at least one element from each choice of

(h(x_{1}),\dots,h(x_{K(\varepsilon)}))
, so

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{K(\varepsilon)}m_{i}
.
Conversely, fixing one representative for each such choice yields an
\varepsilon
-cover, hence

N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\ =\ \prod_{i=1}^{K(\varepsilon)}m_{i}\qquad(\varepsilon>0).

Since
K(\varepsilon)=k
for all
\varepsilon\in(a_{k+1},a_{k})
, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{1}}\log_{2}N(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon=\sum_{k\geq 1}(a_{k}-a_{k+1})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log_{2}m_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log_{2}m_{k}\;-\;a_{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log_{2}m_{i}\Big).

For
a_{k}=2^{-k}
and
m_{k}=2^{2^{k}}
we have
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log_{2}m_{k}=n
and

a_{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log_{2}m_{i}=1-2^{-n}\to 1
, hence
\Phi(\mathcal{H})=+\infty
.
Moreover,
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{onl}}(\mathcal{H})\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}=\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-k}=1
.
∎

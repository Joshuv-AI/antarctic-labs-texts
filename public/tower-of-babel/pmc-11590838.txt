Study protocol of Elective Para-aortic and pelvic versus Pelvic only Irradiation in pelvic node positive Cervical cancer: a multicentric open labelled phase III randomised controlled trial (EPIC Study)
Authors: Abhishek Shinghal, Satyajit Pradhan, Supriya Chopra, Ankita Rungta Kapoor, Ankita Gupta, Prachi Mittal, Vinay Saini, Jeevanshu Jain, Sanju Sanju, Akhil Kapoor, Prashanth Giridhar, Lincoln Pujari, Zachariah Chowdhury, Sudeep Gupta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe revision of International Federation of Gynaecology and Obstetrics staging in 2018 with recommendations to include cross-sectional imaging and a separate stage for node positive disease have opened a lot of uncertainties in implementing the correct treatment approach in these patients. While studies have suggested higher chances of occult para-aortic lymph node (PALN) even with advanced imaging, especially in pelvic node positive disease which tend to recur after pelvic radiation therapy. This study intends to study these patients and isolate the subset who will benefit most from elective PALN irradiation.Methods and analysisThis is an ongoing multicentric phase III randomised controlled trial with a sample size of 274 subjects in two arms (137 in each arm) to determine the superiority of limited elective para-aortic irradiation compared with no irradiation. Arm one includes radiation to the lower PALN and pelvis; Arm two includes radiation to the pelvis. Concurrent chemotherapy followed by brachytherapy is standard in both arms. Patients with cervical cancer and radiologically positive pelvic LNs aged>18 years and<70 years are screened for the study. The primary endpoint of this study is 3-year disease-free survival. The secondary endpoints include 3-year para-aortic recurrence-free survival, 3-year distant metastasis-free survival, 3-year overall survival, acute and late toxicity, quality of life. Translational study to evaluate systemic immune response by FAPI-PETCT (fibroblast activator protein inhibitor positron emission tomography) and assessment of p16, L1 cell adhesion molecule (L1CAM) and protein death ligand-1 (PDL-1) expression by immunohistochemistry.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the institutional ethics committee and will be routinely monitored according to standard guidelines. The results of the study will be published in peer-reviewed scientific journals, presented at conferences and submitted to regulatory authorities.Trial registration numberThe study was registered on 17 January 2022 under CTRI/2022/01/039495 (http://ctri.nic.in).Keywords: Patients, Uterine Cervical Neoplasms, RADIOTHERAPY, CHEMOTHERAPY, Gynaecological oncology

Introduction and background

Cervical cancer constitutes the fourth most common cancer in women worldwide with an age-standardised incidence rate of 13.3 which is as high as 18.0 in India.1 The majority of patients with cervical cancer present with locally advanced disease.2 Concurrent chemoradiation (CTRT) and image-guided adaptive radiotherapy (RT) are the current standard of care for locally advanced cervical cancer.3 RT comprises of external beam RT followed by brachytherapy (BT) to achieve cumulative prescribed radiation dose for curative intent.4

The staging of cervical cancer developed by the International Federation of Gynaecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) until 2009 was based on clinical examination. However, FIGO revised the staging system in 20185 with the recommendation to include cross-sectional imaging in addition to clinical examination and to define a separate stage for positive pelvic node (IIIC1) or para-aortic and/or pelvic nodes (IIIC2) disease due to poorer outcomes.6 7 The para-aortic lymph node (PALN) positive patients, previously considered as distant metastasis, were classified as regional LN metastases due to better survival.

Patients with pelvic and/or PALN involvement account for about 30–40% of patients out of whom the pelvic LNs are affected in about 30% of cases.7 However, in cases where pelvic LNs are not involved, the likelihood of para-aortic metastasis is less than 5%.8 A few studies have shown that patients with stage IIIC1 disease treated with pelvic RT alone tend to recur in the para-aortic region, accounting for up to 41% of all nodal failures9 due to occult para-aortic nodal metastasis. Hence, it is crucial to identify these subgroups of patients in order to improve disease control. Various imaging techniques are available for staging these patients. The use of CT and MRI is associated with very high false negatives, especially in nodal size<10 mm.10 Among these, whole-body positron emission tomography (PET)-CT is gaining ground due to its superiority over CT and MRI in detecting nodal as well as distant metastases.11 PET-CT is recommended as an investigation of choice to rule out nodal and distant metastases in locally advanced cervical cancer.12

Despite the improved accuracy, approximately 12–32% of cases of PALN metastases are missed on PET-CT because of small, undetectable nodes with size<5 mm or microscopic metastases in PALNs.13,15 To address this issue some work has been done on the role of surgical staging in the detection of occult para-aortic metastases.16 In PALN dissection, the nodes were removed caudally from the centre of the common iliac vessels caudally and to the left renal vein cranially. Occult para-aortic metastases were found in about 8–12% of patients with surgical staging in whom imaging was negative.17 However, even with improved accuracy of surgical staging in detecting occult metastases in some cases, the procedure itself causes additional morbidity which is exacerbated in cases of positive PALN that undergo CTRT. In addition, using this approach delays definitive treatment and consumes more resources, calling into question its global implementation. A recent study showed better disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) rates in patients staged with PET-CT compared with laparoscopic-guided surgical staging of PALN,18 making PET-CT a better non-invasive alternative for screening. Since, there is no accurate method to determine occult PALN, elective treatment of the area with RT could also be a good solution and be considered.

Extended-field RT (EFRT) was first proposed in the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group 7920 protocol for the irradiation of PALN.19 The classical EFRT field starts at the superior border of the T12–L1 vertebral body. However, treatment of lower PALN could be started at the upper border of the L2 vertebrae, inferior to the division of the left renal vein. EFRT can be used in both prophylactic and therapeutic scenarios. Prophylactic or elective PALN irradiation is considered when no nodal disease is detected but the likelihood of disease recurrence in the region is high. Therapeutic nodal irradiation is the standard of care for cervical cancer with PALN (stage IIIC2) along with concurrent chemotherapy.20 21 However, it is not clear what role prophylactic irradiation of the PALN plays. There is evidence that occult PALNs are missed by an average of 20–25% even with PET-CT. Therefore, overtreatment of 75% and undertreatment of 25% is a major challenge in this scenario.10 Early studies have shown that prophylactic/elective EFRT improves OS from 44% to 55% at 10 years of follow-up compared with only pelvic irradiation with significantly more distant failures in the pelvic RT arm (p value 0.04).22 In the post-CTRT era, CTRT with pelvic irradiation was superior to pelvic+PALN irradiation (without concurrent chemotherapy) in terms of OS that is 67% versus 41% at 8 years follow-up favouring the CTRT arm where only pelvic radiation was employed.23

RetroEMBRACE, the first retrospective image-guided BT collaborative study reported patterns of failure in locally advanced cervical cancer in the era of MR-based image-guided adaptive BT (IGABT) which reported para-aortic failures of up to 24%.24 Retrospective and phase II studies have demonstrated that elective EFRT in patients with locally advanced cervical cancer leads to better para-aortic recurrence-free survival (PARFS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS), DFS and OS.25,27 A retrospective series of 62 patients by Vargo et al showed that the use of EFRT in a prophylactic setting reduced the PALN recurrence rate to 2.5%. Lee et al retrospectively studied 198 patients, 80 of whom received elective lower PALN irradiation (subrenal vein RT) with more advanced disease, common iliac LN involvement or multiple pelvic LN involvement. It was seen that elective nodal irradiation improved recurrence-free survival (56.8% vs 100%) and cancer-specific survival from 56.5% to 93.3% in patients with common iliac LN involvement and>2 pelvic LN metastasis.28

Studies testing elective EFRT and compared with pelvic RT (along with concurrent chemotherapy and BT as standard in both arms) found no significant increase in acute and late toxicities.29,34 The reduction in toxicity with EFRT could be attributed to: (1) Incorporation of advanced RT techniques like intensity-modulated RT (IMRT) with image-guided RT (IGRT), (2) reduced dose of EFRT in the absence of gross node which could account for reduction of around 8–14 Gy to the PALN region, (3) reduction in the cranial border of radiation portal in EFRT. While classical EFRT starts from D12–L1, most of these studies targeted only the lower para-aortic LN, that is, below the left renal vein, from the L2 vertebra, further reducing gastrointestinal and haematological toxicity.21

The ongoing work by the EMBRACE group analysed 816 patients prospectively and determined that PALN failure attributed to 69% of all nodal failures with the strongest predictor being pelvic nodal disease at the time of diagnosis. In 406 pelvic node-positive patients at diagnosis, treated with pelvic RT, around 47 (11.5%) patients had PALN failures.35,37 Hence EMBRACE II’s ongoing study treats patients with three or more positive nodes or even a single common iliac node—with elective lower PALN irradiation up to the renal hilum. However, there is no phase III randomised evidence to test or support such an approach in selected stage IIIC subpopulations.

Hence, we therefore initiated a phase III randomised controlled trial (RCT) to test the superiority of limited elective PALN radiation in patients with stage IIIC cervical cancer.

Methods and analysis

This is an ongoing phase III RCT with 1:1 allocation ratio and in compliance with SPIRIT Checklist (online supplemental file 1). The study was started on 9 November 2022 and is expected to be completed in 6 years by 8 November 2028. The study design constitutes: (1) Arm-1 (Experimental arm)—Lower PALN (below left renal vein till L2 vertebrae) and pelvic LN+primary disease irradiation along with concurrent chemotherapy followed by BT, (2) Arm-2 (Control arm)—pelvic LN+primary disease irradiation along with concurrent chemotherapy followed by BT. Suitable patients with a diagnosis of cervical cancer and with positive pelvic nodes are eligible for study participation. Routine blood investigation (including complete blood count, liver function test, kidney function test) and detailed staging workup including clinical examination and radiological imaging will be carried out for screening. Those patients who are suitable would be eligible for the study. After obtaining the written informed consent, patients will be sent for randomisation. All randomisations will be done by a biostatistician in the institutional central research secretariat. A simple block randomisation method will be used with 1:1 allocation using a computer-generated algorithm.

The primary endpoint of this study is 3-year DFS which includes relapse, second cancer or death due to disease which will be defined as the time (in months) from the date of randomisation. The secondary endpoints are 3-year PARFS, 3-year DMFS, 3-year OS, quality of life (QOL) assessment using European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) QLQ C30 and EORTC QLQ CX24 questionnaire and Acute & Late toxicity assessment with Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) V.5.0 which will be defined as the time (in months) from the date of randomisation. Also, translational research will be done in these patients to look for tumour and systemic immune inflammation response to RT using fibroblast activation protein activator (FAPI) PET-CT and to estimate the expression of p16, protein death ligand-1 (PDL-1) and L1 cell adhesion molecule (L1CAM) on immunohistochemistry (IHC).

Pretreatment evaluation

Clinical history taking including history of existing comorbidities, general physical examination including performance status and pelvic examination. The clinical examination findings are documented and the disease status is recorded in clinical diagrams. Routine baseline testing including viral markers to document patient fitness. A punch biopsy is done from the primary lesion to establish the diagnosis. The paraffin-embedded blocks will be preserved for up to a period of 10 years for the translational part of this study. Radiological evaluation with MRI pelvis for staging is done in all patients. Metastatic workup is done using PET contrast-enhanced CT (CECT) or other suitable imaging modality. Patients are asked to fill QOL EORTC QLQ C3038 and EORTC QLQ CX2439 at baseline.

RT planning

Patients are asked to void and consume 500 mL of water over 10–15 min. Planning CT is obtained 45 min from bladder emptying time. Care should be taken to ensure the position of the patient is comfortable and reproducible during subsequent planning and treatment. Patients are positioned supine, arms over head. Three radio-opaque markers are placed at the level of pubic symphysis: one at midline and the other two at on the lateral aspect in the same line along the intersection of lateral and horizontal lasers. Patients undergo a CECT scan in the treatment position with laser alignment to the markers. Using a helical CT scanner, 5 mm CT scans are obtained for the mid-diaphragm to midthigh after injecting intravenous contrast. The acquired CT images are transferred to the contouring workstation Eclipse treatment planning system and subjected to planning.

Target volume delineation and dose prescription

Delineation of initial gross tumour volume as GTV_INITIAL (initial gross tumour volume), CTV_LR_INITIAL (initial low-risk clinical target volume) and CTV_HR_INITIAL (initial high-risk clinical target volume) is done in both arms according to the existing guidelines for intact uterus cervical cancer.35 Diagnostic MR images are imported on Eclipse treatment planning system and fused with a planning CT scan. MR images are used for delineating GTV_INITIAL. The ITV_T (internal target volume) margins are given to CTV_LR_INITIAL: 1 cm anteroposteriorly, 1 cm superior, 0.5 cm lateral, 0.5 cm Inferior. This is edited from bones. Delineation of GTV_N (gross tumour volume of involved draining LN) is to be contoured separately in both the arms and a PTV55 (planning target volume of gross node) is made using a margin of 0.5 cm isotropically to the GTV_N. PTV55 has prescribed a dose of 55 Gy in 25 fractions at 2.2 Gy per fraction in both arms as simultaneous integrated boost (SIB). Arm 1—delineation of CTV_EPAN (clinical target volume for lower PALN+pelvic LN) is done cranially till the bifurcation of the left renal vein, maximum till the cranial border of L2 vertebrae. In Arm 2—delineation of CTV_E (clinical target volume for pelvic LN) is done, both according to the existing standard guidelines.40 41 The CTV_EPAN and CTV_E of the respective arm are Boolean with ITV_T to form ITV45 volume. ITV45 is given 5 mm isotropic margin to form PTV45 and is prescribed a dose of 45 Gy in 25 fractions at 1.8 Gy per fraction. Organs at risk is delineated at least 2 cm superior and inferior to the PTV45.

Treatment planning and evaluation

IMRT plans using the inverse planning technique along with nodal SIB are generated on individual patient basis target volumes in both Arm 1 and Arm 2 which is the current standard. Quality assurance and treatment delivery

Quality assurance tests are performed before treatment of each patient. All patients will be subjected to verification using cone beam CT (CBCT) for the first 3 days to rule out systematic error and then three times per week for the rest of the treatment. If variation of more than 5 mm is seen repeatedly or any gross change in uterus lie, size or shape is seen, then replanning will be considered. All patients will undergo extended CBCT that will cover both the primary target and EFRT range available with TrueBeam Varian systems. Concurrent chemotherapy will be standard in both arms.

Weekly review

Acute toxicity scoring will be done weekly using CTCAE V.5.0 Grading.

Brachytherapy

Patients will undergo two applications of BT with a total of 7 Gy × 4 fractions. The procedure will be done as per the available standard guidelines.4 Subsequently, all patients will undergo MRI-guided image guided adaptive BT (IGABT). The dose prescription will be done to D90 HRCTV (dose received by 90% of the volume of high-risk clinical target volume) which will be limited to more than 85 Gy and will be aimed upto 95 Gy.35 42 Point A doses will be reported as a convention. Dose to bladder of volume 2 cc (B2cc), rectum of volume 2 cc (R2cc) and sigmoid of volume 2 cc (S2cc) will be limited to less than 90 Gy, 75 Gy and 75 Gy, respectively, and will be aimed for less than 80 Gy, 65 Gy and 70 Gy, respectively.35 43 The plan will be evaluated for the above-mentioned parameters, will be finalised and exported to the treatment console. Patients will receive two applications of BT, delivered as 7 Gy X2#, in each application, 6 hours apart, to ensure overall treatment time does not exceed 56 days, preferably 50days.44

Follow-up

Acute and late toxicities will be assessed using the CTCAE V.5.0 grading system and patients will be provided with symptomatic care. The first follow-up will be done at 6 weeks. Following this, patients will be followed up every 3 months for the first 2 years, then every 6 months up to 5 years and yearly once thereafter. Patients will be followed up clinically and will be asked for imaging of the abdomen and pelvis at 12 months, 24 months and 36 months of follow-up as per standard practice. Patients will be asked to fill QOL EORTC QLQ C30 and EORTC QLQ CX24 at 3 months, 6 months, 12 months, 24 months and 36 months of follow-up.

Translational research

In this phase III RCT, the two study arms will have different extents of irradiated volume. While the primary endpoint focuses on estimating DFS, these differences in field size and irradiated volume may have a heterogeneous impacts both on tumour and systemic immune inflammation response. In order to study this, we intend to first perform FAPI PETCT in a pilot cohort of 30 patients (15 in each arm). These patients will undergo these PET scans at baseline, at week 5 and at 3 months from treatment completion. Furthermore, a recent study (BIOEMBRACE) also demonstrated the difference in clinical outcomes based on biomarker expression in women undergoing IGABT.45 Here, we plan to perform on all baseline samples, in both arms, IHC for p16, PDL-1 and L-1CAM. The main aim will be to understand clinical outcomes as a function of the differential expression of these biomarkers.

All adverse events (AEs) will be documented and all serious adverse events (SAEs) will be notified to the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC). Expected AEs constitute grade II (CTCAE V.5.0 grading) toxicities and grade III toxicities not requiring hospitalisation and occurring within 4 weeks of completion of RT. Expected SAEs constitute any treatment-related toxicity requiring hospitalisation and all grade V toxicity (CTCAE V.5.0 grading) occurring within 4 weeks of completion of RT. Stopping rules will include early termination of the study based on reported SAEs, the study will be evaluated annually by the principal investigator (PI) and a decision could be taken in case of unexpected, significant or unacceptable safety risk to participants enrolled in the study or decision based on a recommendation from Data Safety Monitoring Unit (DSMU)/Institutional Ethics Committee board (IEC). DSMU which is an institutional body independent of any competing interests to the study or association with the sponsors will also be responsible for regular monitoring/auditing after reviewing the safety and efficacy data of the experimental treatment arm.

Patient consenting & data collection

The patients will be screened at Mahamana Pandit Madanmohan Malviya Cancer Centre (MPMMCC), Tata Memorial Hospital (TMH) and Advanced Centre for Treatment Research and Education in Cancer (ACTREC), Mumbai by the study team including the PI/co-investigators (CIs)/or the trial coordinator. Patients along with their relatives will be served the informed consent form (online supplemental file 2) in a comprehensible language (English/Hindi). After serving informed consent, patient will be asked to describe the study procedure, benefits and risks involved and the use of biological materials to ensure that patients have correctly understood the information.

Data will be collected using a case record form (CRF) as mentioned in online supplemental file 3. All the trial records, CRF and study-related documents will be safely maintained by the PI at MPMMCC, Varanasi, India and stored in the study office for 10 years and later archived in the medical records section. The records will be accessible to the PI/CI and IEC team with appropriate permissions. The PI will also be responsible for communicating with the IEC/DSMU.

Statistics

In this study, the baseline sample size estimates have been taken from Raut et al.7 Based on the primary hypothesis, the addition of infrarenal EFRT along with concurrent chemotherapy will increase the 3-DFS by 10% in patients with stage IIIC1 patients with T2 and T3 patients of cervical cancer. Assuming that the alpha error is 0.05 and the power of the study is 0.80 with randomisation of 1:1. The fixed design is obtained on the basis of a two-arm trial with the time-to-event outcome where the follow-up of fixed study duration is 6 years, fixed accrual duration is 3 years of uniform accrual study, the 3-year DFS rate is obtained by getting an average of T2 and T3 (N1, tumour, node, metastases staging) as 0.63, experimental arm DFS rate is 10% higher, that is, (0.63+10) 0.73. With a censoring rate of 0.1 the sample size is based on an HR of 0.70, the computed events required is 246 and the overall sample size is 274 (137 in each treatment arm, accounting for 10% attrition (250+10%)). The 3-year PARFS, 3-year DMFS, 3-year OS will be estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method (Kaplan 1958). The distribution of these estimates between the two arms will be compared using the log-rank test (Mantel 1966). The time will be measured from the date of randomisation to the first date of failure (local, regional or distant metastases failure or death due to any cause) or last follow-up. The Cox proportional hazard regression model will be used to analyse the effects of factors, in addition to treatment, that may be associated with these factors (Cox 1972). The comparison of acute and late treatment-related toxicities will be done using a comparison of means using χ² test. Conclusions will be reported at a median follow-up of 3 years.

Patient and public involvement

None.

Possible impact of the study

This will be the first randomised phase III trial to answer the question of the added benefit of elective lower para-aortic LN irradiation in node-positive patients with cervical cancer treated with concurrent CTRT and image-guided BT. The results if positive will help to identify patients who will benefit the most with this treatment approach.

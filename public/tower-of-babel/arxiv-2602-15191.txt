Derivation of the AMP equations from belief propagation for the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} minimisation problem
Giuseppe Genovese 1,2, Arianna Piana 3

Abstract
. We consider the ℓ p \ell_{p} -minimisation, which consists of finding the vector x ∈ ℝ N x\in\mathbb{R}^{N} which minimises ‖ x ‖ p \|x\|_{p} subject to the linear constraint y = A ​ x y=Ax , where y ∈ ℝ m y\in\mathbb{R}^{m} is given and A A is a m × N m\times N random matrix with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian centred entries ( m < N m<N ). This can be viewed as the zero temperature version of a statistical mechanics problem, in which one introduces a suitable Gibbs measure on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . To such a Gibbs measure there are associated belief propagation equations. We prove in the easiest case p = 2 p=2 that the means of the distributions obtained by the belief propagation iteration satisfy asymptotically the approximate message passing equations.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is providing a rigorous derivation of the approximate message passing (AMP) equations starting from the belief propagation (BP) equations. BP consists of a set of recursive equations for the marginal distributions of a target probability distribution
[
27
]
. A brief description of the method in our case of interest is given below. The reader can look for instance at
[
23
]
for a comprehensive discussion.
We will take the BP equations as a starting point of our considerations. The characterisation of the convergence of the BP algorithm is a prominent open problem
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
that will not concern us here.

The AMP equations are recursive equations for the means of a target distribution. In the framework of statistical physics, they are known as TAP equations, after the seminal paper of Thouless, Anderson and Palmer
[
31
]
. The subject found renewed interest in the mathematical community mainly after Bolthausen devised a way to prove convergence of the iteration in the whole high temperature region
[
7
]
. Bolthausen’s method later was widely generalised by Montanari and coauthors, see for instance
[
4
]
,

[
20
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
.

From the point of view of statistical physics, a fundamental problem related to the AMP equations is their derivation from the model of interest. More precisely, the problem is to show that the solution of the AMP equations describes the actual means under the Gibbs measure under investigation. In the context of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model (and its
p
-spin versions), the question has been studied in
[
30
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
. These results are mainly limited to the so-called high temperature phase (however, for a low temperature result, see
[
2
]
).

A different path was taken in the contributions
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
24
]
, where the AMP equations were not derived directly from the model distribution, but rather from the BP iteration, regardless of its convergence. This was done for model distributions related to compressed sensing (see also the discussion below) and for the Hopfield model, but such derivations are not mathematically rigorous. The present paper fits this line of research, providing, to the best of our knowledge, the first fully rigorous derivation of the AMP equations from the BP iteration. We note however that one crucial step of our derivation, namely the Gaussian approximation of the densities in the BP iterations, has been studied in the previous works
[
28
, Sect. 4]
,
[
26
, Sect. 7]
and
[
8
, Appendix D]
). The common denominator of these articles seems to be the underlying tree structure of the models under consideration. This is a central difference with the present paper, as we take a purely analytical viewpoint and we do not investigate the structure of the factor graph associated to the BP algorithm (more details below).

The model we are able to handle is in any case very simple, as we focus on the quadratic minimisation. Here many of the technical difficulties are already present, but the quadratic (i.e. Gaussian) structure of the problem helps greatly their resolution (more details are given in the following paragraphs). In this sense, our contribution is purely methodological and must be regarded as a first step towards the understanding of the link between the BP and AMP iteration.

Let us introduce BP for the problem of our interest.
We define the probability measure

\mu^{(p)}_{N,\beta}(dx)=Z^{-1}_{N,\beta}e^{-\beta\|x\|_{p}^{p}}1_{\{x\,:\,y=Ax\}}\,.

Here

For each

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{b\rightarrow i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{y-A^{[i]}x=\{A_{ai}x_{i}\}_{a\in[m]}}dx^{[i]}\prod_{j\neq i}\nu^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}(x_{j})\,,

\displaystyle\nu^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\pi_{\beta,p}(x_{i})\prod_{b\neq a}\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{b\rightarrow i}(x_{i})\,.

The usual interpretation of the BP equations assumes the presence of an underlying factor graph, that is a complete bipartite graph with edges linking only each factor node, labeled by
a\in[m]
, with all the variable nodes, labeled by
i\in[N]
. The marginals
\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}
are beliefs that propagate from the variable to the factor nodes and
\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}
are beliefs that propagate from the factor to the variable nodes. If the factor graph is a tree it is not hard to show that the BP equations reconstruct the measure
\mu^{(p)}_{N,\beta}
exactly
[
23
]
. For our proposes, the distributions
\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}
can be thought just as ancillary objects in order to compute the
\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}
.

We take the following route. We shall derive a set of recursive equations for the means and the variances of the marginals
\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}
that are exact in the limit
N,m\to\infty
(recall that
m\simeq\delta N
). We will show that the variances are independent of the labels
(i,a)
, while the means are asymptotically independent of the factor labels. This asymptotic recurrence for the means are the AMP equations. Finally, we verify that the solution of the AMP equations for
\beta
large enough coincides with the true solution
x^{\star}
of the problem (
1.3
).

1.2.
Main Result

We introduce mean and variance of the BP iterates

(1.7)

\displaystyle x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\coloneqq\int s\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)ds\,,

\displaystyle v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\coloneqq\int s^{2}\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)ds-\left(\int s\nu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)ds\right)^{2}\,,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}\coloneqq\int s\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}(s)ds\,,

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}\coloneqq\int s^{2}\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}(s)-\left(\int s\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}(s)ds\right)^{2}\,.

We will also need

(1.9)

\rho^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}\coloneqq\int s^{3}\nu^{(t)}_{ia}(s)ds\,.

It is not hard to show that (see the subsequent Lemma
2.2
)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\frac{y_{a}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{aj}x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}}{A_{ai}}

(1.11)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k\neq i}A_{ak}^{2}v_{{}_{k\rightarrow a}}^{(t)}}{A_{ai}^{2}}\,

(note that here dividing by the elements of
A
is armless, due to our assumptions of the distribution of the entries).
Much harder is to relate
\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)},\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
to
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)},v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
, which will take most of our efforts.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m<N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta:=m/N
. Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times N}
be a random matrix with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian symmetric entries with
\mathbb{E}[A_{12}^{2}]=1/m
,
\mathbb{P}(A_{12}=0)=0
and satisfying the condition (
1.1
). Let also
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\max_{b\in[m]}|y_{b}|\;\leqslant\;1
.

Consider the BP equations (
1.5
) with
p=2
and initial conditions
\nu^{(0)}_{i\to a}
, that are bounded and continuous densities, with finite fourth moment and
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=0
,
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=v^{(0)}
. Set

(1.12)

v^{(t)}:=\frac{v^{(0)}(1-\delta)}{\delta^{t}(1-\delta)+2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta^{t})}\,.

Recall (
1.7
). Then, for all
(i,a)\in[N]\times[m]
we have

(1.13)

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\;=\;v^{(t)}\;+\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\,.

Moreover, let
\Delta^{(t)}\coloneqq\delta/(2\beta v^{(t)}+\delta)
and

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}=\prod_{\tau=1}^{\lambda}\Delta^{(t-\tau)}

It holds that

(1.14)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=X_{i}^{(t+1)}+O_{\mathbb{P}}(t\Delta^{(t)}\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}N^{-1/2})\qquad\forall a\in[m]\,,

where the sequence

(1.15)

Here
O_{\mathbb{P}}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}})
denotes a quantity that is smaller than
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
with probability larger than

for some

1.3.
Oveview of the proof

Next, we give a high level exposition of the main ideas of our proof.
Looking at the BP equations (
1.5
) one realises easily that the
\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}
are the law of the weighted sums (with the elements of the matrix
A
) of independent random variables distributed according to the
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
. Therefore, as remarked in
[
14
]
, it can be computed by arguments based on the central limit theorem (CLT). In practice, we have that

(1.17)

\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}(s)\sim e^{-\frac{(s-\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)})^{2}}{2\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}}\,.

The second BP equation takes the products of these approximated Gaussian densities and it multiplies the result by the
\ell_{p}
-prior. The product of Gaussian densities is a Gaussian density (apart from unimportant global factors), whose mean and the variance
\mu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
and
\sigma_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
however can be large, namely of order
\sqrt{N}
. Ignoring for the moment this crucial issue, we continue and write then

(1.18)

\nu^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)\sim e^{-\beta|s|^{p}+\frac{(s-\mu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)})^{2}}{2\sigma_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}}}\,.

In principle this distribution is quite nice and allows us to perform also some explicit computation. Therefore we could keep going, alternating the CLT to compute (
1.17
) and the product formula for (
1.17
). This is indeed the strategy suggested in
[
14
]
.

Such a route is however not so easy to implement, as several sources of difficulties arise. First of all, the errors coming from the CLT approximations need to be carefully estimated. We remark that the usual order of magnitude
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
of the reminder coming from (
1.17
) is not sufficient to our ends, as in the next BP step we have to take the
m
-fold product of these error terms, which can be large (as
m
is proportional to
N
). To overcome this issue, we employ an Edgeworth expansion (see
[
6
]
for recent developments) to estimate the density. If on one hand the reminder of the Edgeworth expansion remains small after taking the product, on the other hand we need to deal with the extra terms of the expansion. Estimating them is computationally expensive, but feasible for the first few iterates (we do it only for
t=1
). However, it becomes immediately too complicated as
t
grows, due to the complex dependencies on the elements of the matrix
A
. To fix the ideas, assume that the initial distributions
\nu^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)
are centred at some given values
x^{(0)}_{j\rightarrow a}
(we will set
x^{(0)}_{j\rightarrow a}=0
in the rest of the paper). For
t=0
then, the Hoeffding inequality immediately gives

(1.19)

\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\,

(which in turn implies
\hat{x}^{(0)}_{a\rightarrow i}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(\sqrt{N})
). To extend (
1.19
) to all
t\;\geqslant\;1
one should track down the exact dependency of
x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}
on the elements of
A
, as a direct inspection gives that the independence of
x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}
on
A_{aj}
is lost for
t\;\geqslant\;3
. This looks prohibitive and we could find an alternative strategy only for
p=2
.

The choice
p=2
gives many advantages. One of them is certainly the possibility to analyse the special case of Gaussian initial distributions. In this case we do not need the CLT at any iteration step: the distributions remain always Gaussian and we need only to control the means and variances. In particular, we verify directly that with this initialisation the messages
x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}
are
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
(for which we need the hypercontractivity assumption on the distribution of
A
). We stress that this is a peculiarity of the
p=2
case, as otherwise already the distribution
\nu^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)
is of the form (
1.18
), thus not a Gaussian, and then a CLT argument is needed to compute
\hat{\nu}^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}
via (
1.17
). This is not incidental, but functional for the rest of the proof. Indeed in the general setting the sequence of means and variances can be derived perturbatively from the one obtained with Gaussian initialisation. Here the perturbative regime is given only by the CLT: for any initialisation the first iteration produces densities close to be Gaussian, so that for all successive iterations the BP updates always remain in the Gaussian basin of attraction.
In practice, this means that we use a different version of the local CLT for all time steps
t\geq 1
, exploiting the fact that the iteration of CLT steps makes the random variables in the play closer and closer to be Gaussian distributed.
For any other
p\neq 2
, the BP equations oscillate between the Gaussian and the densities given by (
1.17
), hence the strategy described above does not apply.

2.
Exact computations with a Gaussian initial distribution I: MP equations

In the present and in the next two sections we assume that the initialisation
\nu^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}(x_{i})
of the BP equations

(2.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{b\rightarrow i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{y-A^{[i]}x=\{A_{ai}x_{i}\}_{a\in[m]}}\mathrm{d}x^{[i]}_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x^{[i]}_{N}\prod_{j\neq i}\nu^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}(x_{j})\,,\quad t\;\geqslant\;0

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nu^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\approx\pi_{q,\beta}(x_{i})\prod_{b\neq a}\hat{\nu}^{(t)}_{b\rightarrow i}(x_{i})\,.

is given by a centred Gaussian distribution uniformly over the variable and factor nodes, namely

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}
for some
v_{0}>0
. The subsequent main result in this section establishes the validity of the message passing equations (
2.3
) in this easier case.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
v_{0}>0
. Assume that for all
i\in[N],a\in[m]

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}
.
Then, for all
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the variances are given by some values independent on the labels
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=v^{(t)}
and the means obey the message passing (MP) equations

(2.3)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=\Delta^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}-\Delta^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}x_{j\rightarrow b}^{(t)}.

Moreover,

(2.4)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}A_{b_{1}j_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}\,,

where

(2.5)

\Gamma^{(t)}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\prod_{\tau=1}^{\lambda}\Delta^{(t-\tau)},\quad\text{ with }\quad\Delta^{(t)}\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{2\beta v^{(t)}+\delta}\,,\quad t\;\geqslant\;0\,.

and we define the set of indices

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{\lambda}\in[N]:j_{1}=i\text{ and }j_{\alpha}\neq j_{\alpha+1}\text{ for }\alpha\in[\lambda-1]\}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{\lambda}\in[m]:b_{1}\neq a\text{ and }b_{\alpha}\neq b_{\alpha+1}\text{ for }\alpha\in[\lambda-1]\}\,.

The first step of our analysis is the following observation from
[
14
]
. We report the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
has mean
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and variance
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
, then
\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
has mean

(2.8)

\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}=\frac{y_{a}}{A_{ai}}-\frac{1}{A_{ai}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}x_{j\rightarrow a}^{(t)}

and variance

(2.9)

\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}^{2}v_{j\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\,.

Proof.

Define the random variables
\xi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sim\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
\hat{\xi}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}\sim\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}.
Then

(2.10)

\frac{1}{A_{ai}}\left(y_{a}-\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}\xi_{j\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\right)\overset{(d)}{=}\hat{\xi}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}.

In fact, for any Borel set
S
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}(S)={\mathrm{prob}}\left(y_{a}-\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}x_{j}\in A_{ai}S\right)={\mathrm{prob}}\left(\frac{1}{A_{ai}}\left(y_{a}-\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}x_{j}\right)\in S\right).

The claim follows by taking the expectation and the variance of (
2.10
).
∎

We now show the way Gaussian densities propagate thought the BP iteration.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following hold:

i)

If
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}
is a centred Gaussian density, then for all
t
,
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
are Gaussian densities.

ii)

If
\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
is a Gaussian density with mean
\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
and variance
\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
then
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
is a Gaussian density with mean

(2.11)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=\frac{\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}}{2\beta+\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}}\,,

and variance

(2.12)

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=\frac{1}{2\beta+\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{b\rightarrow i}}}\,.

Proof.

i)
If for some
t\;\geqslant\;1

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
is a Gaussian density, then the random variables
\xi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sim\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
defined in the proof of the previous result are centred Gaussian, therefore also the variables
\hat{\xi}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
given by (
2.10
) are Gaussian. Thus
\hat{\nu}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}
is a Gaussian density with mean
\frac{y_{a}}{A_{ai}}
and variance
\frac{v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}}{A_{ai}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}^{2}
. Next, by the second BP equation (
2.2
),
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
is the product of Gaussian densities, therefore will be Gaussian as well.

Since
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}
is a (centred) Gaussian density, we can start the above reasoning from
t=0
and iterate.

ii)
By
i)
and (
2.1
)

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}(x_{i})\cong\exp\left(-\beta x_{i}^{2}\right)\prod_{b\neq a}\exp\left(-\frac{(x_{i}-\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)})^{2}}{2\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}\right).

Collecting the quadratic and linear terms in
x_{i}
,

\displaystyle\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\cong\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}\Bigr)x_{i}^{2}+x_{i}\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}\right),

which is a one-dimensional Gaussian density with variance

\left(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}\right)^{-1}
and mean

\frac{\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}}{2\beta+\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t)}}}\,.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v_{0}>0
. Assume that for all
i\in[N],a\in[m]

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}
. Then for all
t\in{\mathbb{N}}

(2.13)

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=v^{(t)}\approx\frac{v^{(0)}(1-\delta)}{\delta^{t}(1-\delta)+2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta^{t})}.

Proof.

Combining equations (
2.12
) and (
2.9
) we get the iteration for the variance

(2.14)

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=\frac{1}{2\beta+\sum\limits_{b\neq a}\frac{A_{bi}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{j\neq i}A_{bj}^{2}v_{j\rightarrow b}^{(t)}}}.

Under the assumption
v^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}=v^{(0)}
, we see from the above equation
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=v^{(t)}
for all
t>0
and we have

\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{A_{bi}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{j\neq i}A_{bj}^{2}v_{j\rightarrow b}^{(t)}}=\frac{1}{v^{(t)}}\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{A_{bi}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{j\neq i}A_{bj}^{2}}\approx\frac{\delta}{v^{(t)}}.

The fact that the numerator and the denominator at the r.h.s. of the identity above are independent justifies the last step.
Thus, we get

(2.15)

v^{(t+1)}\approx\frac{v^{(t)}}{2\beta v^{(t)}+\delta}.

We now prove by induction that

(2.16)

v^{(t)}\approx\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta^{t}+2\beta v^{(0)}\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\delta^{\tau}}\,,

and the claim follows by summing the geometric series:
\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\delta^{\tau}=\frac{1-\delta^{t}}{1-\delta}
(recall
\delta\in(0,1)
).

For
t=1
,
v^{(1)}\approx\frac{v^{(0)}}{2\beta v^{(0)}+\delta}
follows immediately from (
2.15
). Let us suppose (
2.16
) holds for
t>1
. We have

\displaystyle v^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{v^{(t)}}{2\beta v^{(t)}+\delta}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta^{t}+2\beta v^{(0)}\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\delta^{\tau}}\frac{1}{2\beta\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta^{t}+2\beta v^{(0)}\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\delta^{\tau}}+\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta^{t+1}+2\beta v^{(0)}\left(1+\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\delta^{\tau+1}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta^{t+1}+2\beta v^{(0)}\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{t}\delta^{\tau}}\,.

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Same assumptions of Lemma
2.4
. Then, for all
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it holds

(2.17)

\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}\approx\frac{1}{\delta A_{ai}^{2}}v^{(t)}.

Proof.

By (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.4
we have

\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}^{2}v^{(t)}\approx\frac{1}{\delta A_{ai}^{2}}v^{(t)}.

∎

3.
Tail control of the messages

In this section our goal is to show that the messages
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
are
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
. Moreover we will establish a suitable decomposition that will be useful in the next section. Our main result follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
v_{0}>0
. Assume that for all
i\in[N],a\in[m]

\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}
. For every
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
and edge
(i,a)
,

(3.1)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\bigl(x_{i}^{[\lambda]}+z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}\bigr),

where for all
\lambda\in[t]

\max_{i\in[N]}|x_{i}^{[\lambda]}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[N]\\
a\in[m]\end{subarray}}|z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}(N^{-1/2})\,.

Proof.

Let us consider the case
t=1
first. From (
2.11
)-(
2.12
) and Corollary
2.5
we have

(3.2)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}=\Delta^{(1)}\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}=\Delta^{(1)}\left(\sum_{b=1}^{m}y_{b}A_{bi}-y_{a}A_{ai}\right).

Comparing (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) and noting that
\sum_{b}y_{b}A_{bi}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
and
y_{a}A_{ai}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(N^{-1/2})
gives the assertion in the case
t=1
.

Now we pass to consider
t\;\geqslant\;2
. By Lemma
2.6
we have

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}A_{b_{1}j_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}.

Fix
\lambda
and abbreviate

(3.3)

f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})=\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}A_{b_{1}j_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}\,,

so that

(3.4)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})\,.

Set
b_{0}=a
and
B^{\lambda}=\{\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda}\}\in[m]^{\lambda}\}.
We can rewrite the set
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}
as

\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}=B^{\lambda}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{\zeta=1}^{\lambda}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq B^{\lambda}\\
\lvert B\lvert=\zeta\end{subarray}}\left\{b_{\alpha}\in B:b_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha-1}\right\}.

splitting the inclusion-exclusion terms according to whether
a
is
present in the set of enforced equalities yields the decomposition

(3.5)

\displaystyle x_{i}^{[\lambda]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{B^{\lambda}}f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})+\sum_{\zeta=1}^{\lambda}(-1)^{\zeta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq B^{\lambda}\setminus\{a\}\\
|B|=\zeta\end{subarray}}\sum_{B^{\lambda}\setminus B}f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})\prod_{b_{\alpha}\in B}\mathbbm{1}_{\{b_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha-1}\}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta=1}^{\lambda}(-1)^{\zeta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq B^{\lambda}\\
|B|=\zeta,\ a\in B\end{subarray}}\sum_{B^{\lambda}\setminus B}f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})\prod_{b_{\alpha}\in B}\mathbbm{1}_{\{b_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha-1}\}}.

The proof is then completed by the subsequent Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exist positive constants
K_{1}=K_{1}\bigl(t,\delta,\|y\|_{2},\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}\bigr)
and

K_{2}=K_{2}\bigl(t,\delta,\|y\|_{2},\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}\bigr)
, independent of
(i,a)
and
N
, such that for all

,

(3.7)

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigl|x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\bigr|\geq\alpha\right)\leq K_{1}\exp\Bigl(-K_{2}\alpha^{1/t}\Bigr).

and

(3.8)

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigl|z_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\bigr|\geq\alpha\right)\leq K^{\prime}_{1}\exp\Bigl(-K^{\prime}_{2}(\sqrt{N}\alpha)^{1/t}\Bigr).

Proof.

We prove (
3.7
).
By Lemma
3.4
below, there exists a constant

By Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\right\lvert\geq\alpha\right)\leq\left(\frac{D_{t}p^{t}}{\alpha}\right)^{p}\,.

Choose

If

, then

and thus

for some

\leq 1
) and can be absorbed into a front constant

The proof of (

By Lemma

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma^{(t)}_{\lambda}\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)},

with

\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\coloneqq\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}A_{b_{1}j_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}},

with index sets
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}
,
\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}
as in (
2.20
)-(
2.19
).
Minkowski’s and Nelson’s inequality yield

(3.12)

\bigl\lVert x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\bigr\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}\Gamma^{(t)}_{\lambda}\bigl\lVert\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\bigr\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}\Gamma^{(t)}_{\lambda}(p-1)^{\frac{2\lambda-1}{2}}\bigl\lVert\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\bigr\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Hence it suffices to bound
\lVert\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\rVert_{L^{2}}
. In virtue of Proposition
A.1
of Appendix
A
we can reduce to do the estimate assuming the entries of the matrix
A
to be independent standard Gaussian r.vs.

Wick expansion with explicit index constraints.
Write
A_{bj}=\bar{A}_{bj}/\sqrt{m}
with i.i.d. standard Gaussians
\bar{A}_{bj}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\lambda}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}^{\lambda}
be independent copies of
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}
,
\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}
, and write

\|\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\widetilde{\Psi}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\Big],

where
\widetilde{\Psi}
is an independent copy of
\Psi
. Expanding,

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}y_{\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[A_{b_{1}j_{1}}A_{\widetilde{b}_{1}\widetilde{j}_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}A_{\widetilde{b}_{\iota}\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1}}A_{\widetilde{b}_{\iota+1}\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1}}\right]

(3.13)

\displaystyle=m^{-(2\lambda-1)}\sum_{\mathcal{B},\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{J},\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}y_{b_{\lambda}}y_{\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{A}_{b_{1}j_{1}}\bar{A}_{\widetilde{b}_{1}\widetilde{j}_{1}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}\bar{A}_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}\bar{A}_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}\bar{A}_{\widetilde{b}_{\iota}\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1}}\bar{A}_{\widetilde{b}_{\iota+1}\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1}}\right].

We introduce the node list
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}=(A_{\ell})_{\ell=0}^{\lambda-1}
with

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=\{n_{0},q_{0}\}=\{(b_{1},j_{1}),(\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{j}_{1})\},

\displaystyle A_{\iota}

\displaystyle=\{m_{\iota},n_{\iota},p_{\iota},q_{\iota}\}=\{(b_{\iota},j_{\iota+1}),(b_{\iota+1},j_{\iota+1}),(\widetilde{b}_{\iota},\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1}),(\widetilde{b}_{\iota+1},\widetilde{j}_{\iota+1})\}.

Let
\Lambda=4\lambda-2
be the total number of Gaussian factors. By Theorem
3.3
the expectation in (
3.13
) is a sum over pairings
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}}
, each contributing a product of Kronecker deltas enforcing equality of the paired indices. Thus

(3.14)

\|\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=m^{-(2\lambda-1)}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}}}\sum_{\mathcal{B},\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{J},\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}y_{b_{\lambda}}y_{\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}}\prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\in P}\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

Index constraints.
The admissible index sets enforce the following conditions

j_{\alpha}\neq j_{\alpha+1}\quad(\alpha\in[\lambda-1]),\qquad b_{1}\neq a,b_{\alpha}\neq b_{\alpha+1}\quad(\alpha\in[\lambda-1]).

Consequently, the following forbidden identifications must not be imposed by
P
, otherwise the corresponding term vanishes:

(3.15)

\displaystyle m_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq n_{\iota},

\displaystyle p_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq q_{\iota},

(3.16)

\displaystyle n_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq m_{\iota+1},

\displaystyle q_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq p_{\iota+1},

(3.17)

\displaystyle m_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq m_{\iota+1},

\displaystyle p_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq p_{\iota+1},

(3.18)

\displaystyle n_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq n_{\iota+1},

\displaystyle q_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq q_{\iota+1},

(3.19)

\displaystyle m_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq n_{\iota+1},

\displaystyle p_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq q_{\iota+1},

(3.20)

\displaystyle n_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq m_{\iota+2},

\displaystyle q_{\iota}

\displaystyle\neq p_{\iota+2}.

These constraints
will eliminate many pairings.

The identity pairing.
Consider the identity pairing
\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda}
that pairs across the two copies at matching positions:

n_{0}\leftrightarrow q_{0},\qquad m_{\iota}\leftrightarrow p_{\iota},\quad n_{\iota}\leftrightarrow q_{\iota}\qquad(\iota=1,\dots,\lambda-1).

This pairing obeys (
3.15
)-(
3.20
) and enforces
\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}^{\lambda}
,
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}=\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{a}^{\lambda}
,
b_{\lambda}=\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}
, whence

\sum_{\mathcal{B},\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{J},\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}y_{b_{\lambda}}y_{\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}}\prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\in\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda}}\delta_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}^{2}=(\#\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda})(\#\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda})\|y\|_{2}^{2}.

Counting the choices gives
(\#\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda})\asymp m^{\lambda-1}
and
(\#\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda})\asymp N^{\lambda-1}
. Therefore the identity contribution equals

(3.21)

m^{-(2\lambda-1)}\cdot m^{\lambda-1}N^{\lambda-1}\cdot\|y\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{\lambda}N^{1-\lambda}}\|y\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{\delta^{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{\|y\|_{2}^{2}}{N},

since
m=\delta N
.

Perturbations of the identity pairing.
Any
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}}
can be written as a perturbation of
\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda}
: select an even number
k
of perturbed nodes
S\subset\{n_{0},m_{1},n_{1},p_{1},q_{1},\dots,m_{\lambda-1},n_{\lambda-1},p_{\lambda-1},q_{\lambda-1}\}
, and keep the identity pairing on the complement, while on
S
arrange any pairing
\mathsf{Q}_{k}
that still obeys the constraints (
3.15
)-(
3.20
). We denote this by
P=\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda-k}\cup\mathsf{Q}_{k}
.
Let

V(k)\coloneqq\sum_{\mathcal{B},\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{J},\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}y_{b_{\lambda}}y_{\widetilde{b}_{\lambda}}\prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\in\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda-k}\cup\mathsf{Q}_{k}}\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

Claim:
For every even
k\in\{2,\dots,\Lambda\}
, either
V(k)=0
(if (
3.15
)-(
3.20
) are violated) or

(3.22)

V(k)\leq V(0)=\sum_{\mathcal{B}_{a}^{\lambda},\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\|y\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof of Claim.
Each cross-copy identification imposed by
\mathsf{Q}_{k}
glues indices across the two copies in addition to the identity constraints, thus reduces the number of free summation indices. The worst case (largest value) arises when no additional equalities are imposed beyond
\mathsf{I}_{\Lambda}
, namely
k=0
.

Counting admissible perturbations.
It remains to bound the number of admissible
\mathsf{Q}_{k}
for a fixed (even)
k
. Easy counting gives

\#\{\text{admissible }\mathsf{Q}_{k}\}\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\kappa=3\\
\kappa\ \text{odd}\end{subarray}}^{k+1}(\Lambda-\kappa)\leq(4\lambda)^{k/2+1},

where we used
\Lambda=4\lambda-2
.
Summing over even
k
gives

(3.23)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{\Lambda}\#\{\mathsf{Q}_{k}\}\leq 1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\Lambda/2}(4\lambda)^{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{2\lambda+1}(4\lambda)^{\ell}.

Collecting the
L^{2}
bound.
By (
3.14
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
),

\|\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq m^{-(2\lambda-1)}V(0)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{\Lambda}\#\{\mathsf{Q}_{k}\}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\lambda}}\frac{\|y\|_{2}^{2}}{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2\lambda+1}(4\lambda)^{\ell}.

Taking square roots,

(3.24)

\bigl\lVert\Psi_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2\lambda-1)}\bigr\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\|y\|_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{\delta^{\lambda/2}}(4\lambda)^{\lambda+1}.

From
L^{2}
to
L^{p}
.

Insert (
3.24
) into (
3.12
) and bound
p^{(2\lambda-1)/2}\leq p^{\lambda}
,
(4\lambda)^{\lambda+1}\leq(4t)^{t+1}
,
\Gamma^{(t)}_{\lambda}\leq\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}
, and
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}\delta^{-\lambda/2}\leq t\delta^{-t/2}
. This yields (
3.11
). ∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(a,i)\in[m]\times[N]
and (recall (
3.6
))

z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}\;=\;\sum_{\zeta=1}^{\lambda}(-1)^{\zeta}\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)},

where

(3.25)

\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq B^{\lambda}\\
|B|=\zeta,\ a\in B\end{subarray}}\sum_{B^{\lambda}\setminus B}f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})\,\prod_{b_{\alpha}\in B}\mathbbm{1}_{\{b_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha-1}\}}

and

(3.26)

f(b_{1},\dots,b_{\lambda})\;=\;\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}}y_{b_{\lambda}}\,A_{b_{1}j_{1}}\,\prod_{\iota=1}^{\lambda-1}A_{b_{\iota}j_{\iota+1}}A_{b_{\iota+1}j_{\iota+1}}.

Here
b_{0}=a
,
B_{\lambda}:=\{\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\lambda}\}\in[m]^{\lambda}\}
and the index set

\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\lambda}
is defined in (
2.19
). Then, for every
p\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
N,m,a,i
and of
\beta
) such that

\bigl\|z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}\bigr\|_{L^{p}}\;\leq\;\,\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\,\frac{p^{\lambda}}{\delta^{\,(\lambda-1)/2}}\,(4\lambda)^{\lambda+1},\qquad\text{uniformly in }(a,i)\in[m]\times[N].

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of the previous lemma and we will only sketch it, underlining the main points.
Every summand in
z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2\lambda-1
in the entries of
A
, with the crucial feature that at least one adjacency equality involves the left endpoint
b_{0}=a
; consequently each summand contains an explicit factor
A_{ai}
.

Again by Proposition
A.1
, we may w.l.o.g. assume that
A
has i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. By Minkowski’s and Nelson’s inequality, for
p\geq 2
,

\bigl\|z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}\bigr\|_{L^{p}}\;\leq\;\sum_{\zeta}\|\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}\|_{L^{p}}\;\leq\;\sum_{\zeta}(p-1)^{(2\lambda-1)/2}\,\|\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}\|_{L^{2}}\;\lesssim\;p^{\lambda}\,\sum_{\zeta}\|\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}\|_{L^{2}}.

Next we expand
\|\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
as in Lemma
3.4
.
The constraints of (
2.19
)-(
2.20
) eliminate forbidden pairings; the identity pairing is admissible and dominant.
Relative to the bulk estimate (
3.24
), the boundary summand differs in three ways:

(1)

one row index is pinned at
a
, so a factor
\sqrt{m}
from summing over row indices is lost,

(2)

one column index is pinned at
i
, so a factor
\sqrt{N}
from summing over column indices is lost,

(3)

there is an explicit Gaussian factor
A_{ai}
, contributing
\|A_{ai}\|_{L^{2}}=m^{-1/2}
.

Together, these effects reduce the size of the leading term by a factor of order
1/(\sqrt{m}\sqrt{N})
.
Hence

\|\Phi_{a\rightarrow i}^{(\zeta)}\|_{L^{2}}\;\leq\;C^{\prime}\,\frac{\|y\|_{2}}{N}\,\frac{1}{\delta^{(\lambda-1)/2}}\,(4\lambda)^{\lambda+1},

uniformly over the indices and over
\zeta
.
All the other pairings, obtained as perturbations of the identity, are treated as in Lemma
3.4
.
Combining that with the previous display
yields the stated
L^{p}
bound.

Finally, using that
\max_{a\in[m]}|y_{a}|\;\leqslant\;1
, we have
\|y\|_{2}\;\leqslant\;\sqrt{\delta N}
. Substituting gives

\frac{\|y\|_{2}}{N}\delta^{-(\lambda-1)/2}\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,\delta^{(2-\lambda)/2}\,.

This proves that

z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(N^{-1/2})
uniformly in
(a,i)
.
∎

By similar techniques, we establish also the next lemma, that will be used in the next section.

Lemma 3.6
.

Define the quantity

Z_{i}\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}Y_{b\rightarrow j}^{(t)},

where the messages
Y_{b\rightarrow j}^{(t)}
are defined by

Y_{a\to i}^{(t)}(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\,z_{a\to i}^{[\lambda]},

Then

\max_{i\in[N]}|Z_{i}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-1/2}\big).

Proof.

Write

Z_{i}=\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}Y_{b\rightarrow j}^{(t)}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}z_{b\rightarrow j}^{[\lambda]}.

It suffices to bound each summand

Z_{i}^{(\lambda)}\coloneqq\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}z_{b\rightarrow j}^{[\lambda]}.

To bound
\|Z_{i}^{(\lambda)}\|_{L^{2}}
, we expand

\mathbb{E}\big[(Z_{i}^{(\lambda)})^{2}\big]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b,b^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,j^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[A_{bi}A_{bj}z_{b\rightarrow j}^{[\lambda]}A_{b^{\prime}i}A_{b^{\prime}j^{\prime}}z_{b^{\prime}\to j^{\prime}}^{[\lambda]}\Big].

Using again Theorem
3.3
, this expectation is given by a sum over all pairings of Gaussian variables, which include the explicit factors
A_{bi}
,
A_{bj}
,
A_{b^{\prime}i}
,
A_{b^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
as well as those appearing inside
z_{b\rightarrow j}^{[\lambda]},z_{b^{\prime}\to j^{\prime}}^{[\lambda]}
.
The presence of the explicit factors fixes both a row index (
b
) and two column indices (
i,j
). Correspondingly, the number of free summation indices is reduced by two compared to the bulk message expansions of Lemma
3.4
.
From the combinatorial analysis of Lemma
3.5
, we deduce that

\|Z_{i}^{(\lambda)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\frac{\|y\|_{2}}{N^{3/2}}\cdot\text{Poly}(t,\delta)\;\leqslant\;\frac{C}{N}\text{Poly}(t,\delta),

where
\text{Poly}(t,\delta)
is a polynomial function of
t
and
\delta
.
Hence,
\|Z_{i}^{(\lambda)}\|_{L^{2}}=O\left(N^{-1}\right)
.
By Nelson’s hypercontractivity and Markov’s inequality, this implies
Z_{i}^{(\lambda)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-1}\right).

Summing over finitely many
\lambda=1,\ldots,t
preserves this bound.
∎

4.
Exact computations with a Gaussian initial distribution II: AMP equations

We are now ready to derive the approximate message passing equations using the decomposition of the messages of Proposition
3.1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the case of a Gaussian initial condition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}
for all
i\in[N],a\in[m]
.
Let
\Delta^{(t)}\coloneqq\delta/(2\beta v^{(t)}+\delta)
, where
v^{(t)}
is the (edge-independent) variance from Lemma
2.4
, and recall

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}=\prod_{\tau=1}^{\lambda}\Delta^{(t-\tau)}
from (
2.22
).
It holds that

(4.1)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=X_{i}^{(t)}+O_{\mathbb{P}}(t\Delta^{(t)}\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}N^{-1/2})\qquad\forall a\in[m]\,,

where the sequence
\{X_{i}^{(t)}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the AMP equations

(4.2)

X_{i}^{(t+1)}=X_{i}^{(t)}(\beta)+\Delta^{(t)}\left(\sum_{b=1}^{m}y_{b}A_{bi}-\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}X_{j}^{(t)}(\beta)\right)+O_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\Delta^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}N^{-1/2}\Big)\,.

Proof.

We start by decomposing the message passing equations (
2.3
) as

(4.3)

\displaystyle x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{(t)}\sum_{b=1}^{m}y_{b}A_{bi}-\Delta^{(t)}y_{a}A_{ai}

(4.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta^{(t)}\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}x_{j\rightarrow b}^{(t)}+\Delta^{(t)}A_{ai}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{aj}x_{j\rightarrow a}^{(t)}

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\Delta^{(t)}\sum_{b=1}^{m}A_{bi}^{2}x_{i\rightarrow b}^{(t)}-\Delta^{(t)}A_{ai}^{2}x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}.

We recognise that some of the terms above do not depend on the index
a
. Comparing with the decomposition of Proposition
3.1
, we set

(4.6)

X_{i}^{(t)}(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}x_{i}^{[\lambda]},\qquad Y_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda=1}^{t}(-1)^{\lambda+1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}z_{a\rightarrow i}^{[\lambda]}\,,

so that

(4.7)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=X_{i}^{(t)}(\beta)+Y_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}(\beta)\,

and (recall that
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{(t)}\;\leqslant\;\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}
for all
t
and for all
\lambda\;\geqslant\;1
)

(4.8)

\max_{i\in[N]}\max_{a\in[m]}|Y_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}(\beta)|=O_{\mathbb{P}}(t\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}N^{-1/2})\,.

We now replace
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
on the l.h.s. of (
4.3
) with the r.h.s. of (
4.7
) and similarly for
x_{j\rightarrow b}^{(t)}
(suitably changing the index
a
into
b
and
i
into
j
) in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). Next we separate all the terms depending on the index
a
and all those where this index does not appear.

The
a
-independent
terms give (
4.2
) with the explicit remainder

(4.9)

R_{i}^{(t)}(N,\beta)=\Delta^{(t)}\left(\sum_{b=1}^{m}A_{bi}^{2}Y_{i\rightarrow b}^{(t)}(\beta)-\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}Y_{j\to b}^{(t)}(\beta)\right)\,.

By (
4.8
) and
\sum_{b}A^{2}_{bi}\approx 1
it follows that

(4.10)

\sum_{b=1}^{m}A_{bi}^{2}Y_{i\rightarrow b}^{(t)}(\beta)=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}N^{-1/2}\big)\,.

Moreover, by Lemma
3.6

\sum_{b=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{bi}A_{bj}Y_{j\to b}^{(t)}(\beta)=O_{\mathbb{P}}(\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}N^{-1/2}),

and this recovers (
4.2
).
∎

Remark 2
.

We stress that the only
\beta
-dependence on the r.h.s. of (
4.2
) is given by the factors

\Delta^{(t)}
and
\Gamma_{1}^{(t)}=\Delta^{(t-1)}
. For the behaviour as
\beta\to\infty
(taken after
N\to\infty
), see Section
8
.

5.
Gaussian approximations for
t=1

In this section we will work with a general
\ell_{q}
prior of the form

(5.1)

\pi_{\beta,q}(s)\coloneqq Z^{\prime}(\beta,q)e^{-\beta|s|^{q}}\,,\qquad\beta>0,\quad s>0\,

where
Z^{\prime}(\beta,q)
is a normalisation constant.

We set

(5.2)

\mu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}}{\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}},\qquad\sigma_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}}.

Let us define for brevity

(5.3)

\mathtt{A}[F]_{q,\beta,i\to a}\coloneqq\frac{\int\pi_{\beta,q}(s)F(s)\phi_{\mu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)},\sigma_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}}(s)\mathrm{d}s}{\int\pi_{\beta,q}(s)\phi_{\mu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)},\sigma_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}}(s)\mathrm{d}s}

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
\nu^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}
is bounded and continuous with

\int|s|^{4}\nu^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}(s)\mathrm{d}s<\infty\,.

Let
q>0
and consider the BP equations (
1.5
) with initial conditions
\{\nu^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}\}_{i\in[N],a\in[m]}
.
For all
F
such that

(5.4)

\int\pi_{\beta,q}(s)|F(s)|^{2}\phi_{\mu,\sigma}(s)\mathrm{d}s=C_{q,\beta,\mu,\sigma}<\infty\,,

we have

(5.5)

\left|\int\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}(s)F(s)\mathrm{d}s-\mathtt{A}[F]_{q,\beta,i\to a}\right|\;\leqslant\;\mathtt{A}[|F|]_{q,\beta,i\to a}O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\right)\,.

Moreover,
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}
depends continuously on the entries of the matrix
A
.

Let us set

(5.6)

P_{a\rightarrow i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}r^{3}}\log\int\pi_{\beta,q}(s)\nu^{(0)}_{j\rightarrow a}(s)e^{r\left(\frac{y_{a}}{A_{ai}(N-1)}-\frac{A_{aj}}{A_{ai}}s\right)}ds\,,

and

(5.7)

\mathsf{EDG}_{a\rightarrow i}(s)\coloneqq\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{a\rightarrow i}}{(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{s-\hat{x}^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}}}\right)\right)\,,

where

H_{3}(x)=x^{3}-3x

is the third Hermite polynomial.

To prove the main proposition here, the following result will be instrumental. The proof is rather technical and it is given in Appendix
D
.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

(5.8)

\left|\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i}P_{b\rightarrow i}}{(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i})^{3}}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left|\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{(\hat{x}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i})^{2}P_{b\rightarrow i}}{(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i})^{3}}\right|\;\leqslant\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)

and

(5.9)

\left|\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{P_{b\rightarrow i}}{(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i})^{2}}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\,,\quad\left|\sum_{a\in[m]\setminus\{b\}}\frac{P_{a\rightarrow i}}{(\hat{v}^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a})^{3}}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\,.

Remark 5.1
.

A direct consequence of (
5.8
) is that

(5.10)

Lemma 5.4

With probability at most

Proof.

In the first part of the proof, we proceed as in
[

.
Let us define for any

a sequence of independent r.vs

P_{a\rightarrow i}

\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}\xi^{(0)}_{j\rightarrow a}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(1\right)\,.

\left\langle F\right\rangle_{I}\coloneqq\frac{\int\pi_{\beta,2}(s)\prod_{a\notin I}\phi_{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i},\hat{v}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}}(s)F(s)ds}{\int\pi_{\beta,2}(s)\prod_{a\notin I}\phi_{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i},\hat{v}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}}(s)}\,.

Thus

(6.14)

\Delta_{2}^{(t)}=\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[m]\setminus\{b\}\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\langle\prod_{a\in I}\chi^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}\right\rangle_{I}}{\left\langle\prod_{a\in I}\phi_{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i},\hat{v}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}}\right\rangle_{I}}\,.

By (
5.23
) we have

(6.15)

\left\langle\prod_{a\in I}\chi^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}\right\rangle_{I}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon_{t}-\varepsilon 2^{-t}}}\right)^{|I|}=\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\right)^{|I|}\,.

In addition, we write

(6.16)

\left\langle\prod_{a\in I}\phi_{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i},\hat{v}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}}\right\rangle_{I}=K_{I}\left\langle\phi_{\mu^{(t+1)}_{I},\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I}}\right\rangle_{I}\,,

where

(6.17)

\mu^{(t+1)}_{I}\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{a\in I}\frac{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}}}{\sum_{a\in I}\frac{1}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}}}\,,\qquad\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{a\in I}\frac{1}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}}\right)^{-1}\,

and
K_{I}
is a normalisation constant.
Let now
K^{\prime}_{I}
be a number such that

(6.18)

\min_{|s|\;\leqslant\;K^{\prime}_{I}}\phi_{\mu^{(t+1)}_{I},\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I}}\;\geqslant\;\frac{2}{K_{I}}\,.

This gives

(6.19)

K^{\prime}_{I}\;\leqslant\;\mu^{(t+1)}_{I}+\sqrt{2\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I}\log\left(\frac{K_{I}}{2\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I}}}\right)}\,.

By (
6.16
) and (
6.18
)

(6.20)

\left\langle\prod_{a\in I}\phi_{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i},\hat{v}^{(t)}_{a\rightarrow i}}\right\rangle_{I}\;\geqslant\;2\left(1-\left\langle 1_{\{|s|\;\geqslant\;K_{I}^{\prime}\}}\right\rangle_{I}\right)\,

and we can estimate by (
6.19
) and the definition (
6.13
)

(6.21)

\left\langle 1_{\{|s|\;\geqslant\;K_{I}^{\prime}\}}\right\rangle_{I}\;\leqslant\;\exp\left(-\beta\mu^{(t+1)}_{I}-(\beta-1+(2\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I^{c}})^{-1})\frac{|\mu^{(t+1)}_{I^{c}}|^{2}}{2\sigma^{(t+1)}_{I^{c}}}\right)\;\leqslant\;\frac{9}{10}

for
\beta
large enough.

Using (
6.15
), (
6.20
) and (
6.21
) into (
6.12
) we obtain

(6.22)

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{2}^{(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[m]\setminus\{b\}\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\right)^{m-1}-1\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\,.

It follows that for
N
large enough

(6.23)

|1+\Delta_{2}^{(t)}|\;\geqslant\;\frac{1}{2}\,.

Similarly, since
|e^{i\lambda s}|\;\leqslant\;1
, we have

(6.24)

\left|\Delta_{1}^{(t)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\,.

By (
6.10
)

(6.25)

\displaystyle\left|\int e^{i\lambda s}\nu_{i\rightarrow b}^{(t+1)}ds-[e^{i\lambda s}]_{\mu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow b},\sigma^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow b}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\Delta_{1}^{(t)}-[e^{i\lambda s}]_{\mu^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow b},\sigma^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow b}}\Delta_{2}^{(t)}\right|}{|1+\Delta_{2}^{(t)}|}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 2\left|\Delta_{1}^{(t)}\right|+2\left|\Delta_{2}^{(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{t+1}}}\,.

This proves (
6.6
).
∎

7.
Two shadowing sequences

From now on we denote by
\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
\bar{s}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
the mean and variance updates of the BP-iteration (
1.5
) when they are initialised with a centred Gaussian distribution
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=\phi_{0,v^{(0)}}.
These were computed in the sections
2
-
4
. We proved in Lemma
2.4
that the variances
\bar{s}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
are constant as
a,i
varies in
[m],[N]
respectively. They are all fixed to some value

(7.1)

\bar{s}^{(t)}=\frac{v^{(0)}(1-\delta)}{\delta^{t}(1-\delta)+2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta^{t})}\,.

Here we are interested in writing the actual means and variances obtained by the BP iteration starting by an arbitrary distribution in terms of these quantities, namely

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\text{error}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\bar{s}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\text{error}.

To be precise, the main result of the section is the following.

Proposition 7.1
.

Consider initial conditions for the BP equations (
1.5
)
\nu^{(0)}_{i\to a}
, that is bounded and continuous densities with with finite fourth moment and
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=0
,
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=v^{(0)}>0
.
Let
\bar{s}^{(t)}
and
\bar{m}^{(t)}_{i\to a}
as explained above.
Fix
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, for all
(i,a)\in[N]\times[m]
we have

(7.2)

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\;=\;\bar{s}^{(t)}\;+\;\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{2}\;+\;\mathtt{e}^{(t)}_{2},\qquad\mathtt{e}^{(t)}_{2}=O\big(N^{-1/2+2\varepsilon}\big),\quad\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{2}=O\big(\beta^{-\alpha}N^{-1/2+2\varepsilon}\big)\,,

and

(7.3)

x^{(t)}_{i\to a}\;=\;\bar{m}^{(t)}_{i\to a}\;+\;\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{i\to a}\;+\;\mathtt{e}^{(t)}_{1},\qquad\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{i\to a}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-1/2+2\varepsilon}\big),\quad\mathtt{e}^{(t)}_{1}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-1/2+2\varepsilon}\big).

The central tool to prove this proposition is the introduction of the following two sequences
(compare with (
2.11
) and (
2.12
))

(7.4)

m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}:=\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t-1)}}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t-1)}}\right)s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}:=\left(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t-1)}}\right)^{-1}\,,

where we recall that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\frac{y_{a}-\sum_{j}A_{aj}x^{(t)}_{j\rightarrow a}}{A_{ai}}\,,

(7.6)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k\neq i}A_{ak}^{2}v_{{}_{k\rightarrow a}}^{(t)}}{A_{ai}^{2}}\,.

The sequence
m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
would be respectively the mean and the variance of
\nu_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
if all the
\hat{\nu}_{i\rightarrow b}^{(t-1)}
were Gaussian distributions with mean
\hat{x}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t-1)}
and the variance
\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(t-1)}
.
Moreover,
m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
has the remarkable property that it shadows both the sequence
\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
of the means of the BP iterates with the Gaussian initial condition and the sequence
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
of the means of the BP iterates with the non-Gaussian initial condition
\nu^{(0)}_{i\to a}
. The same occurs in a much simpler way for the sequences of the variances:
s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
,
\bar{s}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
. The shadowing of
x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
follows from the central limit theorem approach of the past two sections. For the shadowing of
\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
one has to carefully analyse the BP iteration, as done in the sequel.

To better illustrate this point, we analyse in detail the first two steps of the iteration.

Iteration at
t=1

Recall that at
t=0
we have

(7.7)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=0,\qquad v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(0)}=v^{(0)},\qquad\hat{x}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(0)}=\frac{y_{a}}{A_{ai}},\qquad\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}^{2}\,v^{(0)}\;\approx\;\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\,\frac{v^{(0)}}{\delta}.

Moreover, we have

(7.8)

\bar{s}^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}=s^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}\approx\Bigl(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{v^{(0)}}\Bigr)^{-1}\,\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{m}^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}=m^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}\,.

Lemma 7.2
.

It holds that

(7.9)

\displaystyle v^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{s}^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2},\qquad\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}\;=\;O_{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\,,

(7.10)

\displaystyle x^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}+\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a},\qquad\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}\;=\;O_{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\,.

The errors
\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}
,
\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}
are both a continuous function of the matrix entries of
A
.

Proof.

Consider

(7.11)

s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}=\Bigl(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}\Bigr)^{-1}\,.

Using (
7.7
) and
\sum_{b\neq a}A_{bi}^{2}\approx 1
, gives the approximation

\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(0)}}\approx\frac{\delta}{v^{(0)}}

whence

(7.12)

s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\approx\Bigl(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{v^{(0)}}\Bigr)^{-1}=\bar{s}^{(1)}\,.

Applying Proposition
5.1
with
F(s)=s^{2}
and
F(s)=s
we get (see Lemma
2.3
, ii) with
t=0
)

\left|v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}-s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\bar{s}^{(1)}\,.

and it is a continuous function of the elements of the matrix
A
. This proves (
7.9
).

Next, we compute

m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}=s^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i}}{\hat{v}^{(0)}_{b\rightarrow i}}=\frac{s^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}}{v^{(0)}}\,\delta\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi},

Proposition
5.1
with
F(s)=s
and the inequality
\mathbb{E}[\lvert X\lvert]\;\leqslant\;C\left(\lvert\mathbb{E}[X]\lvert+\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)}\right)
yield

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\;=\;m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)},\qquad\mathtt{e}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}\;=\;O_{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\left(\frac{\bar{s}^{(1)}}{v^{(0)}}\delta\left\lvert\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}\right\lvert+\sqrt{\bar{s}^{(1)}}\right),

and
\mathtt{e}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}
is a continuous function of the elements of the matrix
A
.
Using that
\bigl|\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}\bigr|=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
,

\frac{\bar{s}^{(1)}}{v^{(0)}}\,\delta=\frac{\delta}{2\beta v^{(0)}+\delta}\,,

and that
\sqrt{\bar{s}^{(1)}}
is bounded in
\beta
, we conclude
\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{i\rightarrow a}=O_{{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

∎

Iteration for
t=2

The analysis of the case
t=2
is slightly more complicated, as it has already most of the features appearing for general
t
. We start by recording the following simple fact.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\beta>0
, and let
x>0
. Let

f(x)\coloneqq\;\frac{x}{2\beta x+\delta}\,.

Then for all
|\varepsilon|\;\leqslant\;x/2
we have

(7.13)

\bigl|f(x+\varepsilon)-f(x)\bigr|\;\leqslant\;\frac{|\varepsilon|\delta}{(\beta x+\delta)^{2}}\,.

Proof.

Since
f
is Lipschitz, it suffices to compute

(7.14)

\sup_{u\in(x,x+\varepsilon)}f^{\prime}(u)=\sup_{u\in(x,x+\varepsilon)}\frac{\delta}{(2\beta x+\delta)^{2}}\;\leqslant\;\frac{\delta}{(\beta x+\delta)^{2}}\,.

∎

Consider now

(7.15)

\bar{s}^{(2)}=\Bigl(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{s^{(1)}}\Bigr)^{-1},

The next lemma shows that
s^{(2)}
differs from
\bar{s}^{(2)}
by a term that vanishes as
N\to\infty
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Uniformly over
a,i
, it holds that

s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}\;=\;\bar{s}^{(2)}+\mathtt{E}_{2}^{(2)}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathtt{E}_{2}^{(2)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\,

and

v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}\;=\;\bar{s}^{(2)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(2)},\qquad\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(2)}\;=\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

The errors
\mathtt{E}_{2}^{(2)}
,
\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(2)}
are both a continuous functions of the matrix entries of
A
.

Proof.

We have by (
7.9
)

\hat{v}_{a\rightarrow i}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{aj}^{2}v^{(1)}\;\approx\;\frac{1}{A_{ai}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\delta}\,\bigl(s^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)}\bigr),

so that

s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}\;=\;\Bigl(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}_{b\rightarrow i}^{(1)}}\Bigr)^{-1}\;\approx\;\Bigl(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{s^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)}}\Bigr)^{-1}.

In the last step we have used (
7.6
) and (
7.9
).
Let
f(u)\coloneqq(2\beta+\delta/u)^{-1}
. By Lemma
7.3
, for
N
sufficiently large we have

\bigl|f(s^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)})-f(s^{(1)})\bigr|\;\lesssim_{\delta}\;\frac{|\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)}|}{1+\beta\,|\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)}|}.

From Lemma
7.2
we have
|\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(1)}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)
, hence

\mathtt{E}_{2}^{(2)}\;=\;\bigl|s^{(2)}-\bar{s}^{(2)}\bigr|\;=\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)

and it is a continuous function of the matrix entries of
A
.
Finally, Proposition
6.1
implies

\left|v^{(2)}\;-\;s^{(2)}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\,

and that
\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(2)}
is a continuous function of the matrix entries of
A
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}=\delta\,\frac{s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}}{v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(1)}}
and
\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}=\delta\,\frac{\bar{s}^{(2)}}{\bar{s}^{(1)}}
.
Uniformly over

,

Moreover,
\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}
and
\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}
are continuous functions of the matrix entries of
A
.

Proof.

Write
v^{(1)}_{a\rightarrow i}=v^{(1)}=\bar{s}^{(1)}+\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}
and
s_{a\rightarrow i}^{(2)}=s^{(2)}=\bar{s}^{(2)}+\mathtt{E}^{(2)}_{2}
due to Lemma

and
7.4
. Then

\frac{1}{\delta}\,\bigl|\Delta_{a\rightarrow i}^{(2)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}\bigr|=\left|\frac{s^{(2)}}{v^{(1)}}-\frac{\bar{s}^{(2)}}{\bar{s}^{(1)}}\right|\leq\frac{|\mathtt{E}^{(2)}_{2}|}{\,\bar{s}^{(1)}+|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|\,}\;+\;\frac{\bar{s}^{(2)}\,|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|}{\,\bar{s}^{(1)}\bigl(\bar{s}^{(1)}+|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|\bigr)}.

By Lemma
7.2
,
\bar{s}^{(1)}
is fixed as
N
grows while

|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)
.
By Lemma
7.4
,
|\mathtt{E}^{(2)}_{2}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)
and

.
Therefore

\frac{|\mathtt{E}^{(2)}_{2}|}{\,s^{(1)}+|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|\,}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big),

and

\frac{\bar{s}^{(2)}\,|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|}{\,s^{(1)}\bigl(s^{(1)}+|\mathtt{e}^{(1)}_{2}|\bigr)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Multiplying by
\delta\in(0,1]
yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Define

\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}=m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}-\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}\,.

Then
\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}
is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix
A
and

\left|\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

Moreover,

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}=\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}+\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(2)},\qquad\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(2)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

The error
\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(2)}
is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix

.

Proof.

Recall
\Delta^{(2)}_{i\rightarrow a}
and
\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}
defined in Lemma
7.5
and set for brevity
\Delta^{(2)}:=\Delta^{(2)}_{i\rightarrow a}
. Combining (
7.4
), (
7.5
), (
7.6
), Lemma

, (
1.21
) and
x^{(0)}_{i\rightarrow a}=0

, we write

Hence

(7.16)

\displaystyle\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}=m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}-\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta^{(2)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(2)}\right)\left(\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}-\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\bar{m}_{j\rightarrow b}^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Delta^{(2)}\,\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{e}_{j\rightarrow b}^{(1)}\right).

The continuity of
\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(2)}

,

and

. Recall that (see (

))

\bar{s}^{(t+1)}\;\coloneqq\;\Bigl(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{\bar{s}^{(t)}}\Bigr)^{-1},\qquad t\geq 1.

Lemma 7.7

Assume

v^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}=v^{(t)}
is edge-independent for all
t
, and

v^{(t)}=\bar{s}^{(t)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)},

with

\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

uniformly over
(a,i)
and for each fixed
t
. The error
\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)}
is a continuous function of the matrix elements of
A
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. The case
t=2
was dealt in Lemma
7.4
.
Assume that for some
t\geq 2
we have

(7.20)

v^{(t)}=\bar{s}^{(t)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)},\qquad\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr),

uniformly over
(a,i)
, and that
\bar{s}^{(t)}
is edge-independent.
From the induction hypothesis and (
7.6
) it follows that

(7.21)

s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}=\left(2\beta+\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{1}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{b\to i}}\right)^{-1}\;\approx\;\left(2\beta+\frac{\delta}{v^{(t)}}\right)^{-1}=:s^{(t+1)}\,.

Next we compare
s^{(t+1)}
with

(7.22)

\bar{s}^{(t+1)}=\frac{1}{\,2\beta+\frac{\delta}{\bar{s}^{(t)}}\,}.

Setting
f(u)\coloneqq(2\beta+\delta/u)^{-1}
and
using Lemma
7.3
and (
7.20
),

(7.23)

\bigl|s^{(t+1)}-\bar{s}^{(t+1)}\bigr|=\bigl|f(\bar{s}^{(t)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)})-f(\bar{s}^{(t)})\bigr|\;=\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\,.

Combining the bound above and (
7.21
) we obtain that
s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}
shadows
\bar{s}^{(t+1)}
:

(7.24)

\bigl|s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}-\bar{s}^{(t+1)}\bigr|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\,.

Finally, by Proposition
6.1
we have that
s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
shadows also

, that is

\left|\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)}\right|=\left|v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}-s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\qquad\text{for all $t$}\,,

and it is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix

.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let

(7.25)

\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\coloneqq\delta\,\frac{s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}}{v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}},\qquad\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}\coloneqq\delta\,\frac{\bar{s}^{(t+1)}}{\bar{s}^{(t)}}.

Then, uniformly over
(a,i)
,

\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}+O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,.

Moreover,
\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}
and
\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}
are continuous functions of the matrix elements of
A
.

Proof.

Using that
s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}\approx\frac{v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}}{2\beta v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\delta}
and
\bar{s}^{(t+1)}=\frac{\bar{s}^{(t)}}{2\beta\bar{s}^{(t)}+\delta}
, we have that

\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\approx\frac{\delta}{\,2\beta v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\delta\,},\qquad\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}=\frac{\delta}{\,2\beta\bar{s}^{(t)}+\delta\,}.

Set
g(u)\coloneqq\frac{\delta}{2\beta u+\delta}
for
u>0
. Then

|\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}|=\bigl|g\bigl(\bar{s}^{(t)}+e_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\bigr)-g\bigl(\bar{s}^{(t)}\bigr)\bigr|,\qquad e_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\coloneqq v_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{s}^{(t)}.

By Lemma
7.7
,
|e_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)
.
Moreover,
a direct computation gives, for all
x,e\geq 0
,

\bigl|g(x+e)-g(x)\bigr|=\frac{2\beta\delta\,|e|}{\bigl(2\beta(x+e)+\delta\bigr)\bigl(2\beta x+\delta\bigr)}\leq\frac{2\beta\,|e|}{2\beta|e|+\delta}\leq C\,\min\{|e|,\beta^{-1}\}.

For fixed
\beta>0
this yields

|\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}|\;=\;O_{\mathbb{P}}\big(N^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\big),

uniformly over
(a,i)
, which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 7.9
.

Define

(7.26)

\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}:=m_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}.

Then

(7.27)

\mathtt{E}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

Moreover,

(7.28)

x_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=\bar{m}_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}+\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)},\qquad\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. The case
t=2
was dealt in Lemma
7.6
. For
t\;\geqslant\;2
, we start from

(7.29)

m^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}\;=\;s_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t+1)}\sum_{b\neq a}\frac{\hat{x}^{(t)}_{b\to i}}{\hat{v}^{(t)}_{b\to i}}.

By Lemma
7.7
, uniformly in
(b,i)
,

\hat{v}^{(t)}_{b\to i}\;\approx\;\frac{1}{A_{bi}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\delta}\,v^{(t)}=\frac{1}{A_{bi}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\delta}\,\bigl(\bar{s}^{(t)}+\mathtt{e}_{2}^{(t)}\bigr),

so that (see (
7.25
))

(7.30)

m^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}\;=\;\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}A_{bi}^{2}\,\hat{x}^{(t)}_{b\to i}\,.

We use (
2.8
)
and the induction hypothesis

\hat{x}^{(t)}_{b\to i}=\frac{y_{b}}{A_{bi}}-\frac{1}{A_{bi}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bj}\,\bar{m}^{(t)}_{j\to b}-\frac{1}{A_{bi}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{j\to b}-\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}\frac{1}{A_{bi}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bj}

Hence

\displaystyle m^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}-\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\bar{m}^{(t)}_{j\to b}

\displaystyle\quad-\,\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{j\to b}-\,\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}.

We compare with (see (
7.25
))

\bar{m}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}=\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}\Biggl(\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}-\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\bar{m}^{(t)}_{j\to b}\Biggr),\qquad\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}\coloneqq\delta\,\frac{\bar{s}^{(t+1)}}{\bar{s}^{(t)}}.

We have

(7.31)

\displaystyle\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}=m^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}-\bar{m}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(t)})\left(\sum_{b\neq a}y_{b}A_{bi}-\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\bar{m}^{(t)}_{j\to b}\right)

(7.32)

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\,\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{j\to b}

(7.33)

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\,\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,.

Therefore by the MP equations of Proposition
2.1

(7.34)

\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}=\frac{\Delta^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}-\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}}{\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}}\bar{m}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}+\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\,\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{j\to b}+\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}\,\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,.

The first and last summand on the r.h.s. are easily estimated.
Lemma
7.8
yields

|\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}-\bar{\Delta}^{(t)}|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,,

while Proposition
3.1
gives
\bar{m}^{(t)}_{i\rightarrow a}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
.
Thus the first summand is
O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
. The last summand is also of this order of magnitude, because of the induction hypothesis,
\mathtt{e}_{1}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
,
\Delta_{i\rightarrow a}^{(t)}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
and the fact that
\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
.

Therefore we are left with bounding the second term. We study the recursion for
t\;\geqslant\;2

(7.35)

\displaystyle\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Rem}^{(t)}_{j\to a}+\Delta^{(t)}\,\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\,\mathtt{E}^{(t)}_{j\to b}\,,

(7.36)

\displaystyle\text{Rem}^{(t)}_{j\to a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

and
\text{Rem}^{(t)}_{j\to a}
is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix
A
.
It suffices to prove by induction over
t\;\geqslant\;1
that
\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}
is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix
A
and it is
O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
. The base case
t=1
is dealt in Lemma
7.6
. Assume now, that the assertion is true for some
t
. Then also
\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}
is a continuous function of the entries of the matrix
A
. Next we prove that
\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
, which will conclude the proof.

We note that
\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}
is a generic bounded continuous function of the entries of the matrix
A
. First of all, we observe that the Frobenius norm of
A
is bounded by one with probability exponentially close to one as
N\to\infty
. On such a large probability event, the Stone-Weiestrass Theorem applies: for all
\varepsilon>0
there is a polynomial
E_{i\rightarrow a}
of some degree
D(\varepsilon)
such that

(7.37)

\|\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}-E_{i\rightarrow a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\,.

Thus

(7.38)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}E_{i\rightarrow a}+\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}(\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}-E_{i\rightarrow a})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}E_{i\rightarrow a}+\varepsilon\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}|A_{bi}A_{bj}|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}E_{i\rightarrow a}+O_{\mathbb{P}}(\varepsilon m)\,.

As
\varepsilon
can be made arbitrarily small, we only have to prove that the first addendum above is
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
.

First we prove that the assertion holds for a monomial function. W.l.o.g. we can write

(7.39)

E_{j\rightarrow b}=A_{bj}^{K}\left(\sum_{k\neq j}A^{H}_{bk}\right)^{p}\,,

for given integers
K,p\;\geqslant\;0
and
H\;\geqslant\;1
. Note that
E_{j\rightarrow b}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
(applying Lemma
C.2
), thus we need to prove that

(7.40)

\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}E_{j\rightarrow b}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\,.

We set

(7.41)

X_{bij}:=\sum_{k\neq i,j}A^{H}_{bk}\,.

Moreover,

(7.42)

\left(\sum_{k\neq j}A^{H}_{bk}\right)^{p}=\left(A_{bi}^{H}+X_{bij}\right)^{p}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\binom{p}{\ell}A_{bi}^{H\ell}X^{p-\ell}_{bij}\,.

Hence,

(7.43)

\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{bi}A_{bj}E_{j\rightarrow b}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\binom{p}{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}A_{bi}^{H\ell+1}\sum_{j\neq i}A^{K+1}_{bj}X^{p-\ell}_{bij}\,.

Now we put

(7.44)

Y^{(\ell)}_{bi}:=\sum_{j\neq i}A^{K+1}_{bj}X^{p-\ell}_{bij}\,.

We note that
X_{bij}
is independent of
A_{bi},A_{bj}
. Recalling the normalisation of the elements of the matrix
A
, applying Lemma
C.1
with
x_{i}=m^{-H/2}
for all
i=1\ldots N
, we have

(7.45)

X_{bij}=\begin{cases}O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{\frac{H-1}{2}}}\right)&\text{if $H$ is odd}\,,\\
O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{\frac{H-2}{2}}}\right)&\text{if $H$ is even}\,.\end{cases}

Therefore

(7.46)

X^{p-\ell}_{bij}=\begin{cases}O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{\frac{(p-\ell)(H-1)}{2}}}\right)&\text{if $H$ is odd}\,,\\
O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{\frac{(p-\ell)(H-2)}{2}}}\right)&\text{if $H$ is even}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
H
is even then clearly
X_{bij}>0
. Therefore the worst possible case is
H=2
, when the terms
X^{p-\ell}_{bij}
are all positive
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
. Similarly, we will assume that
K=1
, so that also the terms
A^{K+1}_{bj}
have the minimal decay
1/m
with no cancellations due to the random signs in the sum in (
7.44
). Thus we have
Y^{(\ell)}_{bi}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
for all
\ell=1\ldots,p
.

Next, we need to evaluate

(7.47)

\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\binom{p}{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}A_{bi}^{H\ell+1}Y^{(\ell)}_{bi}\,.

If
\ell>0
then
H\ell+1\;\geqslant\;2
. Thus the summands
A_{bi}^{H\ell+1}Y^{(\ell)}_{bi}
are at least
O_{\mathbb{P}}(m^{-1})
and the sum over
b\neq a
is at most
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
.

The term
\ell=0
is dealt separately. By the Hoeffding inequality (w.r.t. the law of
A
) and the Jensen inequality we have

(7.48)

P\left(\left|\sum_{b\neq a}A_{bi}Y^{(0)}_{bi}\;\geqslant\;t\right|\;\leqslant\;\left[e^{-c\frac{t^{2}m}{\sum_{b\neq a}(Y_{bi}^{(0)})^{2}}}\right]\right)\;\leqslant\;e^{-c\frac{t^{2}m}{\sum_{b\neq a}E\left[(Y_{bi}^{(0)})^{2}\right]}}=e^{-ct^{2}}\,,

that is

A_{bi}Y^{(0)}_{bi}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\,.

Clearly the sum over
\ell
of
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
terms remains
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
. This proves the assertion under the assumption that
\mathtt{E}^{(t+1)}_{i\rightarrow a}
is a monomial function. Therefore the same follows also for any bounded polynomial function, combining different values of
H,K,p
. This concludes the proof.
∎

8.
Analysis of the AMP equations

In this section we analyse the AMP iteration derived in Theorem
1.1
and prove Corollary
1.2
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
(M_{t})_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
be matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
and
(b_{t})_{t\;\geqslant\;0},\ (R^{(t)})_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
Consider, for a fixed
T\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequences

\displaystyle X^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=M_{t}X^{(t)}+b_{t}+R^{(t)},

\displaystyle W^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=M_{t}W^{(t)}+b_{t},

with
X^{(0)}=W^{(0)}.
Assume that

(8.1)

C_{T}:=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;s<t\;\leqslant\;T}\left\lVert\prod_{\tau=s+1}^{t-1}M_{\tau}\right\rVert<\infty,

and set

\varepsilon_{T}\coloneqq\max_{0\;\leqslant\;t\;\leqslant\;T-1}\lVert R^{(t)}\rVert\,.

Then

(8.2)

\max_{0\;\leqslant\;t\;\leqslant\;T}\lVert X^{(t)}-W^{(t)}\rVert\;\leqslant\;C_{T}T\varepsilon_{T}\,.

Proof.

Set
E^{(t)}\coloneqq X^{(t)}-W^{(t)}
. Then
E^{(0)}=0
and

E^{(t+1)}=M_{t}E^{(t)}+R^{(t)}.

We have that

E^{(t)}=\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\Bigl(\prod_{\tau=s+1}^{t-1}M_{\tau}\Bigr)R^{(s)}.

Taking norms and using (
8.1
),

\|E^{(t)}\|\leq\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\Bigl\|\prod_{\tau=s+1}^{t-1}M_{\tau}\Bigr\|\|R^{(s)}\|\leq C_{T}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\|R^{(s)}\|\leq C_{T}t\varepsilon_{T}.

Maximizing over
t\leq T
yields (
8.2
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

By Lemma
8.1
we can ignore the
O_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\Delta^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}N^{-1/2}\Big)
term in the AMP updates (
4.2
) of Theorem
1.1
, by paying a
O_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(C_{t}t\Delta^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}_{1}N^{-1/2}\Big)
error in the solution at step

, which is irrelevant as
N\to\infty
. Thus we are left to study the recursion

(8.3)

X^{(t+1)}=X^{(t)}+\Delta^{(t)}\left[A^{T}y-A^{T}AX^{(t)}\right],\qquad X^{(0)}=0\,.

The spectrum of
\delta A^{T}A
is contained a.s. in the interval
[\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}]
, where
\lambda_{\pm}:=(1\pm\sqrt{\delta})^{2}
.
Next, we recall

(8.4)

\Delta^{(t)}=\frac{\delta}{\delta+\frac{2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta)}{\delta^{t}(1-\delta)+2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta^{t})}}\,.

By a direct computation we see that we can write

(8.5)

\Delta^{(t)}=\delta-\delta^{t+1}\gamma(\beta,t)\,,\qquad\gamma(\beta,t):=\frac{(1-\delta)(2\beta v^{(0)}-(1-\delta))}{\delta^{t+1}(1-\delta)+2\beta v^{(0)}(1-\delta^{t+1})}\,.

Note that
\gamma(\beta,t)\;\geqslant\;0
if
\beta\;\geqslant\;(1-\delta)/2v^{(0)}
, it is increasing as
t
grows and
\gamma(\beta,t)<1
for all
t
.
We set

(8.6)

R^{(t)}:=I_{N}-\Delta^{(t)}A^{T}A\,,\qquad R^{(\infty)}:=I_{N}-\delta A^{T}A\,.

We have that

(8.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\|R^{(t)}\|_{\textup{op}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{\lambda\in[(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2},(1+\sqrt{\delta})^{2}]}|1-(1-\delta^{t}\gamma(\beta,t))\lambda|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in[(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2},(1+\sqrt{\delta})^{2}]}|1-\lambda(1-\gamma(\beta,0))|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}(1-\gamma(\beta,0))=:\rho_{1}\,,

where
\rho_{1}\in(0,1)
for all
\delta\in(0,1)
, and

(8.8)

\|R^{(\infty)}\|_{\textup{op}}\;\leqslant\;1-(1-\sqrt{\delta})^{2}=\sqrt{\delta}(2-\sqrt{\delta})=:\rho_{2}\in(0,1)\,.

The equation (
8.3
) writes as

(8.9)

X^{(t+1)}=R^{(t)}X^{(t)}+\Delta^{(t)}A^{T}y\,,\qquad X^{(0)}=0\,.

In parallel, we define
X^{(\infty)}
as the solution of

(8.10)

X^{(\infty)}=R^{(\infty)}X^{(\infty)}+\delta A^{T}y\,,\qquad X^{(0)}=0\,.

We have

(8.11)

\displaystyle X^{(\infty)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(R^{(\infty)})^{k}A^{T}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta A^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(I_{N}-\delta AA^{T})^{k}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta A^{T}(I_{N}-(I_{N}-\delta AA^{T}))^{-1}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{T}(AA^{T})^{-1}y=x^{\star}\,.

Set now
\Gamma^{(t)}:=X^{(t)}-X^{(\infty)}
. We have

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(t)}X^{(t)}-R^{(\infty)}X^{(\infty)}+(\Delta^{(t)}-\delta)A^{T}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}+(R^{(t)}-R^{(\infty)})X^{(\infty)}+(\Delta^{(t)}-\delta)A^{T}y\,.

Now, we observe that

(8.12)

R^{(t)}-R^{(\infty)}=-(\Delta^{(t)}-\delta)A^{T}A\,,

thus

(8.13)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(t)}X^{(t)}-R^{(\infty)}X^{(\infty)}+(\Delta^{(t)}-\delta)A^{T}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}+(\Delta^{(t)}-\delta)(A^{T}y-A^{T}AX^{(\infty)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(t)}\Gamma^{(t)}\,

since by (
8.11
) we have

(8.14)

A^{T}AX^{(\infty)}=A^{T}Ax^{\star}=A^{T}AA^{T}(AA^{T})^{-1}y=A^{T}y\,.

Hence, by (
8.7
)

(8.15)

\|\Gamma^{(t+1)}\|_{2}\;\leqslant\;\|R^{(t)}\|_{\textup{op}}\|\Gamma^{(t)}\|_{2}\;\leqslant\;\rho_{1}^{t}\|x^{\star}\|_{2}\,.

This proves the assertion.
∎

Appendix A
Equivalence of
L^{2}
norms of random tensors

In this appendix we prove the following statement.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}
be i.i.d. centred r.vs with unitary variance and set

(A.1)

\chi_{r}(\xi)\coloneqq m^{-\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{i}\neq n_{i+1}\\
i=1\ldots r-1\end{subarray}}\xi_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi_{n_{r}}\,.

We have

(A.2)

\|\chi_{r}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\;\leqslant\;\|\chi_{r}(g)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{C}{m}\,,

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
r
and on the distribution on
\xi
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
are independent standard Gaussian r.vs.

Proof.

Let us set

(A.3)

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{n_{i}\neq n_{i+1}\,:\,i=1\ldots r-1\}

and

(A.4)

B_{k}\coloneqq\{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}\,:\,|i\,:\,n_{i}=n_{j}\,\,\text{for some }j\neq i,i+1|=k\}\,

(that is the subsets of
\mathcal{B}
in which there are exactly
k
pairs of indices taking the same values). The set
B_{k}
easily identifies with a graph in which the vertex
i,j
are connected iff
n_{i}=n_{j}
. We have

(A.5)

\chi_{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\chi_{r}^{(k)}\,,\qquad\chi_{r}^{(k)}\coloneqq m^{-\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{B_{k}}\xi_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi_{n_{r}}\,.

Thus

(A.6)

\|\chi_{r}\|_{L^{2}}\;\leqslant\;\sum_{k=0}^{r}\|\chi_{r}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}}\,.

The term
\chi_{r}^{(0)}
corresponds to the decoupled chaos, for which we have

(A.7)

\|\chi_{r}^{(0)}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\chi_{r}^{(0)}(g)\|_{L^{2}}\,.

Since all the indices here are distinct, this term corresponds to the ones in the Wick expansion of Theorem
3.3
in which the vertices can be paired only in disjoint couples.

Let us assume now
k\;\geqslant\;1
. We define a cycle of length
p
a chain of identities of
p
indices. Clearly
p\;\geqslant\;3
. We set
B^{a}_{k}
to be the subset of elements of
B_{k}
containing no cycles and
B^{c}_{k}
its complementary set w.r.t.
B_{k}
. We also define
p_{i}
as the length of the path containing the index
n_{i}
and if
i<j
and
n_{j}
belongs to the same cycle as
n_{i}
we set
p_{j}=0
. For elements of
B^{a}_{k}
we can extend this definition, setting
p_{i}\in\{0,1,2\}
to be the number of indices equal to
n_{i}
and if
i<j
and
p_{i}=2
then
p_{j}=0
. Clearly
k\;\leqslant\;|i\,:\,p_{i}=0|=:h
and
|i\in B_{k}\,:\,n_{i}\neq n_{j}\,\forall j\in B_{k}\setminus\{i\}|\;\leqslant\;m^{r-h}
. Moreover, for each element in
B_{k}
we can write

(A.8)

\xi_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi_{n_{r}}=\xi^{p_{1}}_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi^{p_{r}}_{n_{r}}=\prod_{i\in[r]\,:\,p_{i}\neq 0}\xi^{p_{i}}_{n_{i}}\,.

It is important to notice that the indices in the terms appearing on the r.h.s of the display above are all distinct.

Thus, squaring, we have

(A.9)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{r}^{(k)}(\xi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{-r}\sum_{B_{k}\times B_{k}}E[\xi^{p_{1}}_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi^{p_{r}}_{n_{r}}\xi^{q_{1}}_{m_{1}}\ldots\xi^{q_{r}}_{m_{r}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{-r}\sum_{B^{a}_{k}\times B^{a}_{k}}E[\xi^{p_{1}}_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi^{p_{r}}_{n_{r}}\xi^{q_{1}}_{m_{1}}\ldots\xi^{q_{r}}_{m_{r}}]

(A.10)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2m^{-r}\sum_{B^{a}_{k}\times B^{c}_{k}}E[\xi^{p_{1}}_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi^{p_{r}}_{n_{r}}\xi^{q_{1}}_{m_{1}}\ldots\xi^{q_{r}}_{m_{r}}]

(A.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle m^{-r}\sum_{B^{c}_{k}\times B^{c}_{k}}E[\xi^{p_{1}}_{n_{1}}\ldots\xi^{p_{r}}_{n_{r}}\xi^{q_{1}}_{m_{1}}\ldots\xi^{q_{r}}_{m_{r}}]\,,

where the indices
q_{i}
’s are defined as the
p_{i}
’s, but referred to the
m_{i}
’s. Since the
n_{i}
’s and
m_{i}
’s are all distinct, the expectation value can only pair one of the
n_{i}
’s with one of the
m_{i}
’s. Moreover, if some of the
p_{i}
’s is odd it must necessarily be paired with some of the
q_{i}
’s in order to have a non-vanishing contribution.

In each of the terms of the sum in (
A.9
) there are
k

p_{i}
’s (resp.
q_{i}
’s) equal to two,
k

p_{i}
’s (resp.
q_{i}
’s) equal to zero and all the other equal to one. After the pairing given by the expectation, the terms without cycles in the product graph correspond to the terms of the Wick expansion of Theorem
3.3
in which one pairs
k
indices within each
B_{k}
and the there are
r-2k
cross pairings between the
n_{i}
’s and
m_{i}
’s. Together with (
A.7
) this recover all the Wick expansion giving
\|\chi_{r}(g)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
.

What is left are the terms containing cycles in the product graph, that is the remaining summands in (
A.9
) and all those of (
A.10
), (
A.11
). Assume for simplicity there is a single cycle of length
\ell\;\geqslant\;3
and all the other indices have
p_{i}=1
. Indeed this is the worst possible case (i.e. giving the largest contribution). Since all the moments of
\xi
are bounded, we only have to match the cardinality of the sums appearing with the factor
m^{-r}
in front of all the expression. If
\ell
is odd the cycle index must be paired with a
m
index, if
\ell
is even only the indices with
p_{i}=1
(resp.
q_{i}=1
) must be cross-paired with a
m
index. Thus if
\ell
is odd, the cardinality of the sum in (
A.9
)-(
A.11
) is bounded by
m^{r-\ell+1}
and since we have
m^{-r}
at the denominator this contribution is of order
1/m^{\ell-1}
. Similarly if
\ell
is even, the cardinality of the sum in (
A.9
)-(
A.11
) is bounded by
m^{r-\ell+2}
, thus the contribution is of order
1/m^{\ell-2}
.
∎

Appendix B
Local Limit Theorems

Here we prove two versions of the local central limit theorem for independent random variables which are used in the main text.

We recall the definitions

(B.1)

\phi_{\mu,\sigma}(x)\coloneqq\frac{e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma}}\,,\qquad\phi\coloneqq\phi_{0,1}\,,

and the Hermite polynomials

(B.2)

H_{m}(x)\coloneqq\phi(x)^{-1}\int e^{-i\lambda x-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}(i\lambda)^{m}\frac{d\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}\,.

We warn the reader that in this section the notation with the
\widehat{\cdot}
has a different meaning with respect to the rest of the paper. Indeed our proofs are based on Fourier transform and we write the characteristic function of the probability density
\nu
as

(B.3)

\widehat{\nu}(\lambda)\coloneqq\int dxe^{-i\lambda x}\nu(x)\,.

The following statement is essentially contained in
[
17
, XVI.2 and XVI.6]
, but we report its proof below anyway.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent r.vs with continuous densities
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{N}
. Assume that the fourth moment of each
X_{n}
,
n\in[N]
, is uniformly bounded for all
N\;\geqslant\;1
. Denote

(B.4)

P_{n,k}\coloneqq\frac{d^{k}}{dy^{k}}\log E[e^{yX_{n}}]\Big|_{y=0}\,,\qquad P_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{n\in[N]}P_{n,k}\,,\qquad k=1,\ldots,3\,.

Let
f_{N}
be the density associated to
\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}
.
Then

(B.5)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|f_{N}(x)-\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}\left(x\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{x-P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\right)\right)\right|\lesssim e^{-cP_{2}}+e^{-cN}+\frac{N}{P_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}}}\,.

Moreover,
f_{N}
depends continuously on
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
.

Proof.

We write

(B.6)

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq\int dxe^{-i\lambda x}\nu_{n}(x)\,,\qquad f_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)\,.

From this expression we see that
f_{N}
depends continuously on
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
, as each factor
\widehat{\nu}_{n}
depends continuously on
P_{n,1},P_{n,2},P_{n,3}
.

In addition, our assumptions on the densities ensure that
\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)\to 0
as
|\lambda|\to\infty
(by the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma) and also

\max_{n\in[N]}\int d\lambda|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)|=:D<\infty\qquad\mbox{uniformly in $N$.}

By
[
17
, XV.1 Lemma 4]
for any
n\in[N]
it is
|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)|<1
for
\lambda\neq 0
. Therefore for all
\rho>0
there is
a>0
such that for any
n\in[N]

|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)|<e^{-a}
for all
|\lambda|>\rho
(that is, we can choose
\rho
and
a
independently of
n\in[N]
).
Therefore we can bound

(B.7)

\int_{|\lambda|\;\geqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)\;\leqslant\;e^{-a(N-1)}\int d\lambda|\widehat{\nu}_{N}(\lambda)|\;\leqslant\;3De^{-a(\rho)N}\,.

So for an arbitrary
\rho>0
we split

(B.8)

\displaystyle f_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\geqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

(B.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)+O\left(e^{-a(\rho)N}\right)\,.

by (
B.7
). Thus, we need only to gain control locally around zero.
We do that by a Taylor expansion

(B.10)

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{(i\lambda)^{k}}{k!}\mu_{n,k}+R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,,

where

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{n\in[N]}R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{4}\,.

This implies that there exists
\rho>0
such that
\inf_{n\in[N]}|\nu_{n}(\lambda)|>0
for all
|\lambda|<\rho
uniformly in
N
. This allows us to write
\nu_{n}(\lambda)=e^{\log\nu_{n}(\lambda)}
for
|\lambda|<\rho
. Let us Taylor expand the logarithm at exponent. For all
|\lambda|<\rho
we have by a direct computation

(B.11)

\displaystyle\log\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{3}(-1)^{p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{(i\lambda)^{k}}{k!}P_{n,k}\right)^{p}+\tilde{R}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\lambda P_{n,1}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{n,2}+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{n,3}+R^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,,

where again

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{n\in[N]}\tilde{R}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\lambda),R^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{4}\,.

Hence, for all
n\in[N]
and
|\lambda|<\rho

(B.12)

\displaystyle\prod_{n\in[N]}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n\in[N]}\log\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\lambda P_{1}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}+R_{m}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lambda)\right)

with

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{n\in[N]}R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{4}\,.

Therefore

(B.14)

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\exp\left(i\lambda P_{1}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{i\lambda x}\exp\left(i\lambda P_{1}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{2}\right)R^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lambda)\,.

First we bound (
B.14
) uniformly in
x
. We have

(B.15)

\displaystyle|\eqref{eq:secondINt}|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{2}}|R^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lambda)|\;\leqslant\;N\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}P_{2}}|\lambda|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{P_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}}}\int_{|\lambda|<\rho\sqrt{P_{2}}}d\lambda e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}|\lambda|^{4}\;\leqslant\;\frac{CN}{P_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}}}

for a suitable absolute constant
C>0
.

Now, we look at (
). First we do (again) the change of variable
\lambda\mapsto\sqrt{P_{2}}\lambda
and get

\eqref{eq:firstINt}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\int_{|\lambda|<\rho\sqrt{P_{2}}}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda\frac{x}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}}\exp\left(i\lambda\frac{P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}\right)\,.

We can extend this integral to
\mathbb{R}
with a small error. Indeed

(B.16)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|\lambda|>\rho\sqrt{P_{2}}}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda\frac{x}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}}\exp\left(i\lambda\frac{P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}P_{2}}{4}}\int d\lambda e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}}\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}\right)\;\leqslant\;Ce^{-\frac{N}{4}P_{2}}\,.

Moreover,

(B.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi P_{2}}}\int d\lambda e^{-i\lambda\frac{x}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}}\exp\left(i\lambda\frac{P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{(i\lambda)^{3}}{3!}P_{3}\right)\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}\left(x\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{x-P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\right)\right)

by the definition (
B.2
).
Combining (

), (

), (
B.16
), (
B.17
), we can evaluate the first summand on the r.h.s. of (

The following simple observation is crucial in the paper.

Corollary B.2
.

Let
L>0
and define the open set
A_{L}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,|x-P_{1}|\;\leqslant\;L\sqrt{P_{2}}\}
.
Under the same assumptions and notations of Theorem
B.1
, we have that there is a continuous function
\chi
such that

(B.18)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\chi(x)|\;\leqslant\;C\left(\frac{N}{P_{2}}+e^{-cP_{2}}\right)\,

and

(B.19)

f_{N}(x)=\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}\left(x\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{x-P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\right)+\chi(x)\right)\qquad\forall x\in A_{L}\,.

Moreover,
\chi
depends continuously on
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

(B.20)

\inf_{x\in A_{L}}\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}\left(x\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{x-P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\right)\right)=\phi_{0,1}\left(L\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(L\right)\right)=:c(L)\,.

Since
c(L)>0
we can define

(B.21)

\chi(x)\coloneqq c(L)^{-1}\left(f_{N}(x)-\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}\left(x\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{P_{3}}{(P_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}H_{3}\left(\frac{x-P_{1}}{\sqrt{P_{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\,.

The continuity of
\chi
follows from the continuity of
f_{N}
. In addition, (
B.19
) and (
B.18
) follow immediately from (
B.5
).

The fact that
\chi
depends continuously on
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
follows from the analogue property of
f_{N}
.
∎

If the initial densities are close to be Gaussian, we obtain a finer local result, more in the spirit of the Richter Theorem
[
6
]
.

Theorem B.3
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent r.vs with analytic densities
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{N}
with mean values
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and variances
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
. Let
f_{N}
be the density associated to
\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}
.
Let
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in[N]}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n\in[N]}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\varepsilon>0
and assume that for all
n=1\ldots,N
we have

(B.22)

\hat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)=\hat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)+\varepsilon\upsilon_{n}(\lambda)

where
\upsilon_{1}\ldots,\upsilon_{n}
are analytic functions bounded by 1.
Then for all
a\in(0,1)
we have

(B.23)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|f_{N}(x)-\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{N^{a}}\,,

where

(B.24)

P_{1}=\sum_{n\in[N]}x_{n}\,,\qquad P_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{n\in[N]}v_{n}\,.

Proof.

By the same proof as in Theorem
B.1
we arrive to

(B.25)

\displaystyle f_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|<\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\geqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

(B.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)+O\left(e^{-a(\rho)N}\right)\,.

Moreover in the analytic case we can exactly characterise
a(\rho)=C\rho^{2}
for some constant
C>0
(see e.g.
[
6
, Corollary 2]
). This allows us to consider any
\rho>0
such that
\rho^{2}N\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

Next note that for any
n
the function
\frac{\upsilon_{n}(\lambda)}{\widehat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)}
is analytic in any neighbourhood of the origin and vanishes in
\lambda=0
. We expand it as follows

(B.27)

\frac{\upsilon_{n}(\lambda)}{\widehat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)}=\upsilon_{n}^{\prime}(0)\lambda+(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)+2i\mu_{n}\upsilon_{n}^{\prime}(0))\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}+R_{n}(\lambda)\,,

where
|R_{n}(\lambda)|\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{3}
. Thus

(B.28)

\displaystyle\log\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\widehat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)+\log\left(1+\varepsilon\frac{\upsilon_{n}(\lambda)}{\widehat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\widehat{\phi}_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)+\varepsilon\lambda\upsilon_{n}(0)+\frac{\varepsilon\lambda^{2}}{2}\left(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)+2i\mu_{n}\upsilon_{n}^{\prime}(0)-(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\right)+\varepsilon R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,,

where
R_{n}^{\prime}
comes out from the Taylor expansion of the
\log
about
\lambda=0
and we have
|R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)|\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{3}
.
Hence

(B.29)

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)=\phi_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)e^{\varepsilon\lambda\upsilon_{n}(0)+\frac{\varepsilon\lambda^{2}}{2}\left(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)+2i\mu_{n}\upsilon_{n}^{\prime}(0)-(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\right)+\varepsilon R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)}\,.

A direct computation gives

(B.30)

\phi_{\mu_{n},\sigma_{n}}(\lambda)e^{\varepsilon\lambda\upsilon_{n}(0)+\frac{\varepsilon\lambda^{2}}{2}\left(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)+2i\mu_{n}\upsilon_{n}^{\prime}(0)-(\upsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\right)}=\widehat{\phi}_{x_{n},v_{n}}(\lambda)\,.

Therefore there is a function
R^{\prime\prime}_{N}(\lambda)
, analytic uniformly in
N
with
|R^{\prime\prime}_{N}(\lambda)|\;\leqslant\;C|\lambda|^{3}
, such that

(B.31)

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\phi}_{x_{n},v_{n}}(\lambda)e^{\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)\left(1+\varepsilon NR^{\prime\prime}_{N}(\lambda)\right)

(this part is also similar to the proof of Theorem
B.1
). We write

(B.32)

R^{\prime\prime}_{N}(\lambda)=\sum_{k\;\geqslant\;3}r_{k}\lambda^{k}

where the
r_{k}
decay at least exponentially fast in
k
.

Now we have

(B.33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\,.

We bound

(B.35)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\eqref{eq:termR}\right|\;\leqslant\;\frac{\varepsilon N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|\;\leqslant\;\rho}d\lambda|R^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)|\lesssim\varepsilon N\rho^{4}\,.

Similarly we have

(
B.33
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|>\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}(x)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|>\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)

with

\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|\lambda|>\rho}d\lambda e^{-i\lambda x}\widehat{\phi}_{P_{1},P_{2}}(\lambda)\\
\right|\;\leqslant\;e^{-c\rho^{2}P_{2}}\,.

Therefore we have for all
\rho>0

(B.36)

f_{N}(x)=\phi_{P_{1},P_{2}}(x)+O(\varepsilon N\rho^{4})+O(e^{-c\rho^{2}\min(N,P_{2})})\,.

Taking
\rho=N^{-a}
for
1/4<a<1/2
we have the assertion.
∎

We conclude with the following result for analytic densities.

Lemma B.4
.

Assume
\nu
is a probability density with
\nu(s)\;\leqslant\;e^{-c_{1}|s|}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute constant, and

(B.37)

\left|\int ds(\nu(s)-\phi_{\mu,\sigma}(s))e^{i\lambda s}\right|\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\,.

Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

(B.38)

\left|\int ds(\nu(s)-\phi_{\mu,\sigma}(s))s^{k}\right|\;\leqslant\;\frac{k!}{c_{1}^{k}}\varepsilon\,.

Proof.

Let us set
f\coloneqq\nu-\phi_{\mu,\sigma}
. Then we have that
|f(s)|\;\leqslant\;e^{-c_{1}|s|}
for some constants
c_{1}>0
. Therefore

(B.39)

\hat{f}(\lambda)\coloneqq\int dsf(s)e^{i\lambda s}

is bounded by
\varepsilon
and analytic on
\mathbb{R}
and it extends to an analytic function in a strip in the complex plane
\Sigma_{\delta}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C},\quad|\Im(z)|\;\leqslant\;\delta\}
where
0<\delta<c_{1}
(see e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 3.1, Chapter 4]
).
We have by the Cauchy formula

(B.40)

\hat{f}^{(k)}(z)=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{f(w)}{(w-z)^{k+1}}dw\,

for any closed contour
\gamma\in\Sigma_{\delta}
oriented counterclockwise. We choose
\gamma\coloneqq\{w\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|w|=c_{1}\}
and we set
z=0
in the formula above. We bound

(B.41)

\left|\hat{f}^{(k)}(0)\right|=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{|\hat{f}(w)|}{|w|^{k+1}}dw\;\leqslant\;\frac{k!}{c_{1}^{k}}\varepsilon\,.\,

It follows that

(B.42)

\left|\int ds(\nu(s)-\phi_{\mu,\sigma}(s))s^{k}\right|=|\hat{f}(0)^{(k)}|\;\leqslant\;\frac{k!}{c_{1}^{k}}\varepsilon\,.

∎

Appendix C
Tail estimates

Here we prove the following estimate.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
p\;\geqslant\;2
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent symmetric sub-Gaussian r.vs. with unitary variance. It is for any real
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}

(C.1)

P\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[N]}x_{j}X^{p}_{j}\right|\;\geqslant\;\lambda\right)\lesssim\begin{cases}2e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8(2K)^{p}\|x\|_{2}^{2}}}&\lambda\;\leqslant\;4(2K)^{p}\|x\|_{2}^{2}N^{\frac{2-p}{2(p-1)}}\\

Proof.

Let us set for brevity
Y_{j}\coloneqq x_{j}X^{p}_{j}

Note that by assumption there is a constant
K>0
such that

\forall i\in[n]\qquad E\left[\exp\left(\frac{|Y_{i}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{Kx_{i}^{\frac{2}{p}}}\right)\right]<2\,.

Let

,
\alpha\coloneqq\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\in[1/2,1]
and

(C.2)

\dot{Y}_{j}\coloneqq Y_{j}1_{\{|Y_{j}|\;\leqslant\;N^{\alpha}/(2K_{j})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\}}\,.

Clearly

(C.3)

\max_{i\in[N]}E[e^{\frac{|\dot{Y}_{i}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{K_{i}}}]<2\,.

Then

(C.4)

\displaystyle P\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[N]}Y_{j}\right|\;\geqslant\;\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle P\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[N]}Y_{j}\right|\;\geqslant\;\lambda,|Y_{j}|\;\leqslant\;N^{\alpha}/(2K_{j})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\quad\forall j\in[N]\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle P\left(|Y_{j}|>N^{\alpha}/(2K_{j})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\quad\forall j\in[N]\right)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle P\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[N]}\dot{Y}_{j}\right|\;\geqslant\;\lambda\right)+Ce^{-cN^{2\alpha/p}}\,.

Let us now consider
0<a<N^{-\alpha(1-2/p)}=N^{\alpha-1}
. We note that

(C.5)

a|\dot{Y}_{j}|\;\leqslant\;\frac{|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{2K_{j}}

We compute for every
j\in[N]

(C.6)

\displaystyle E[e^{a\dot{Y}_{j}}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n\;\geqslant\;2}\frac{a^{n}}{n!}E[|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+a^{2}\sum_{n\;\geqslant\;0}\frac{a^{n}}{(n+2)!}E[|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{2}|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{n}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 1+(2K)^{p}a^{2}\sum_{n\;\geqslant\;0}\frac{a^{n}}{(n+2)!}E[e^{\frac{|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{2K}}|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{n}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 1+(2K_{j})^{p}a^{2}\sum_{n\;\geqslant\;0}\frac{a^{n}}{n!}E[e^{\frac{|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{2K_{j}}}|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{n}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 1+(2K_{j})^{p}a^{2}E[e^{\frac{|\dot{Y}_{j}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{K_{j}}}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;

\displaystyle 1+2(2K_{j})^{p}a^{2}\;\leqslant\;e^{2(2K_{j})^{p}a^{2}}\,.

In the first inequality above we used that
x^{2}\;\leqslant\;e^{x^{\frac{2}{p}}}
, in the third one we summed the exponential series and used (
C.5
), we used (
C.3
) in the penultimate bound.
Therefore

(C.7)

By the Markov inequality

(C.8)

P\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[N]}\dot{Y}_{j}\right|\;\geqslant\;\lambda\right)\;\leqslant\;\begin{cases}2e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8(2K)^{p}\|x\|_{2}^{2}}}&\lambda\;\leqslant\;4(2K)^{p}\frac{\|x\|_{2}^{2}}{N}N^{\alpha}\\
2e^{-\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{\lambda N}{4(2K)^{p}\|x\|_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}}&\lambda>4(2K)^{p}\frac{\|x\|_{2}^{2}}{N}N^{\alpha}\,.\end{cases}

∎

We give also the following lemma as an easy consequence.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent sub-Gaussian r.vs. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
t>0
. It holds for all
t\gtrsim\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{E[|X_{i}|^{p}]}{N}

(C.9)

P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{|X_{i}|}{N}\;\geqslant\;t\right)\;\leqslant\;e^{-ct^{2}N}\,.

and for
p\;\geqslant\;2

(C.10)

P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{|X_{i}|^{p}}{N}\;\geqslant\;t\right)\;\leqslant\;e^{-c(tN)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\,.

Proof.

We have

(C.11)

P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{|X_{i}|^{p}}{N}\;\geqslant\;t\right)=P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{|X_{i}|^{p}-E[|X_{i}|^{p}]}{N}\;\geqslant\;t-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}E[|X_{i}|^{p}]\right)\,.

Now we set
Y_{i}\coloneqq|X_{i}|^{p}-E[|X_{i}|^{p}]
and
\tau\coloneqq t-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}E[|X_{i}|^{p}]
. Considering
t>\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}E[|X_{i}|^{p}]
we have

(C.12)

P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{|X_{i}|^{p}}{N}\;\geqslant\;t\right)=P\left(\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{Y_{i}}{N}\;\geqslant\;\tau\right)\,,

where
Y_{1}\ldots Y_{N}
are independent centred random variables. It is easy to verify that there is a constant
K>0
such that for any
p\;\geqslant\;1

\max_{i\in[N]}E[e^{\frac{|Y_{i}|^{\frac{2}{p}}}{K}}]<2\,.

For
p=1
The Hoeffding inequality gives (
C.9
) for all
\tau>0
.

For
p\;\geqslant\;2
the same proof of Lemma
C.1
gives (
C.10
) provided that
\tau\gtrsim N^{-\frac{p-2}{2(p-1)}}
, that is certainly satisfied if
t>2\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}E[|X_{i}|^{p}]
for
N
large enough.
∎

Elevated circulation postsynaptic density protein 95 as a potential biomarker for predicting the diagnosis and prognosis of acute cerebral infarction
Authors: Yan Dong, Zhangping He, Haohai Lin, Ziming Ye, Chao Qin, Pingping Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeIndices for the diagnosis of cerebral infarction (CI) and the prediction of prognosis are essential for timely and appropriate management. Plasma PSD95 levels, as a novel biomarker, have not yet been utilized for the diagnosis and prognosis of cerebral infarction. The purpose of this study was to investigate the correlation between plasma PSD95 levels and clinical characteristics as well as prognosis in patients with CI.MethodsA total of 105 patients diagnosed with CI and 80 control group (CP) were enrolled. Plasma samples were collected and PSD95 levels were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Neurological deficits in patients were evaluated using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). Statistical analyses were conducted to assess the associations between plasma PSD95 levels, clinical parameters, and prognostic outcomes.ResultsPlasma PSD95 levels were significantly elevated in CI patients compared to CP (p < 0.01). Furthermore, within the CI group, PSD95 levels exhibited a negative correlation with activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) (p < 0.05) and a significant positive correlation with NIHSS scores assessed 6 months post-onset (p < 0.05).ConclusionPlasma PSD95 may serve as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for cerebral infarction.

Introduction

As the second leading cause of death globally, cerebral infarction (CI) has remained the predominant contributor to mortality and disability among Chinese residents since 2015. This dual burden of chronic non-communicable disease poses significant challenges to public health systems [1, 2]. Facing the dual challenges posed by the high incidence and debilitating effects of CI, unraveling the ischemic cascade, the central pathophysiological mechanism, becomes paramount for achieving therapeutic advancements. This dynamic process commences with the collapse of energy metabolism due to vascular occlusion, which then sets off a sequence of pathological events, such as excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, and inflammatory storms [3, 4]. Notably, excitotoxicity as a pivotal early-stage driver exerts its pathological effects not merely through N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) activation, but more critically through the integrative function of signaling hub proteins within the postsynaptic density. Specifically, postsynaptic density 95 (PSD95) facilitates the formation of macromolecular signaling complexes by coupling NMDAR with neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS), thereby converting calcium influx (Ca²⁺) into peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻) bursts and amplifying NMDAR-mediated nitric oxide (NO) toxicity [5, 6]. However, current evidence remains insufficient to establish the correlation between serum PSD95 levels and CI. This study was therefore designed to investigate the relationship between plasma PSD95 levels and both the clinical characteristics and outcomes of CI patients.

Materials and methods

Participants

A total of 105 patients diagnosed with cerebral ischemia (CI) were consecutively enrolled from the Neurology Department of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University between October and December 2024. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) hospitalization within 7 days of symptom onset; (2) acute or subacute CI confirmed by cranial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); (3) age > 18 years. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (1) previous CI with chronic lesions visible on MRI; (2) comorbidities such as immune disorders, traumatic injuries, organ failure, malignancies, and active infections. The control group (CP) comprised 80 age-matched individuals with normal cranial MRI results, excluding those with CI. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (Approval Number: 2025-E0455). Informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Clinical data collection

Clinical data from both CI patients and CP were systematically collected, including demographic characteristics (age and gender), behavioral history (smoking and alcohol consumption), medical history (hypertension and diabetes mellitus), disease progression metrics (time from symptom onset to admission), neuroimaging evaluation via cranial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and comprehensive laboratory parameters encompassing complete coagulation function, C-reactive protein (CRP) and homocysteine (Hcy) levels.

Plasma PSD95 levels by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Peripheral venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected using EDTA-coated anticoagulant tubes during morning fasting on the second hospitalization day. Following collection, the samples were immediately processed under standardized conditions: centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The obtained supernatant plasma samples were aliquoted and cryopreserved at −80 °C. Each sample underwent only one freeze-thaw cycle, thus preserving sample integrity. Quantitative analysis of plasma PSD95 levels was performed using a human postsynaptic density protein 95 ELISA kit (Uping Bio, Wuhan, China) with strict adherence to the manufacturer’s protocols, and duplicate measurements to ensure analytical precision. The intra-assay coefficient of variation was < 10%, the inter-assay coefficient of variation was < 15%, and the minimum detectable concentration was < 0.1 ng/mL. No cross-reactivity was observed with structurally analogous compounds.

Neurological deficit score and follow-up

Neurological deficit severity in CI patients was objectively assessed using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) following a standardized protocol [7]. Initial evaluations were performed at both admission and discharge time points. Longitudinal assessments continued through a prospective follow-up program, where NIHSS scores were systematically recorded at the 6-month post-diagnosis via structured telephone interviews or in-person clinical evaluations during scheduled outpatient visits.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics 27.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s χ² test with continuity correction. All continuous variables underwent rigorous normality assessment through Shapiro-Wilk testing. Parametric data were analyzed with independent Student’s t-tests, while non-parametric distributions were evaluated using Mann-Whitney U tests. Bivariate correlations were determined through Pearson’s coefficient for normally distributed variables and Spearman’s rank-order coefficient for non-normally distributed datasets. Additionally, multiple linear regression was employed to control for potential confounding factors when comparing groups with baseline imbalances. All analyses were statistically significant with a two-tailed p value < 0.05.

Plasma PSD95 expression in patients with CI and control group

Plasma PSD95 levels were compared between the CI and CP groups after adjustment for confounding factors. The mean plasma PSD95 concentration was 1.83 ng/ml in the CI versus 1.38 ng/ml in CP. Comparative analysis revealed significantly higher plasma PSD95 levels in CI patients compared to CP (p < 0.01), as presented in Fig. 1.

Plasma PSD95 dynamics during acute cerebral infarction were analyzed according to time post-onset. Patients were divided into seven groups according to the number of days post-onset: day 1 (n = 29), day 2 (n = 28), day 3 (n = 16), day 4 (n = 10), day 5 (n = 8), day 6 (n = 7), and day 7 (n = 7). No significant correlation was observed between plasma PSD95 levels and the TOAST classification

According to Trial of Org 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification, patients comprised large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA, n = 76), cardioembolism (CE, n = 10), small-artery occlusion (SAO, n = 12), stroke of other determined etiology (SOE, n = 4), and stroke of undetermined etiology (SUE, n = 3). No significant differences in plasma PSD95 levels were detected across these subgroups (p = 0.072) (Fig. 3). Furthermore, the corresponding effect size (ε² = 0.046) and statistical power (Cohen’s f ≈ 0.22) were calculated to provide a more comprehensive interpretation of these findings.

Discussion

In this study, plasma PSD95 levels were investigated in patients with cerebral infarction and non-cerebral infarction controls. Significantly elevated plasma PSD95 concentrations were demonstrated in cerebral infarction patients compared to controls. Plasma PSD95 levels were associated with APTT and neurological deficits. Furthermore, lower plasma PSD95 levels were correlated with more favorable 6-month prognosis.

Mechanistic investigations of PSD95 in cerebral infarction have primarily focused on preclinical models. PSD-95, a Discs large (DLG) scaffold protein family member, is implicated in coupling NMDAR-GluN2B subunits to nNOS [8]. Following cerebral infarction, NMDARs are activated by excessive extracellular glutamate, triggering substantial Ca²⁺ influx and subsequent activation of nNOS. This cascade results in the production of elevated neurotoxic NO levels, which mediate downstream oxidative stress and neuronal apoptosis [5, 9]. However, to date, no evidence has documented alterations in plasma PSD95 concentrations following cerebral infarction.

This study demonstrated significantly elevated plasma PSD95 concentrations during the acute phase of cerebral infarction. It is hypothesized that this elevation may be associated with alterations in PSD95 at the primary infarction site. Within the infarct core, downregulation of PSD95 expression is mediated by trimeric NMDAR-PSD95-nNOS complex formation and calpain activation [10–13]. Neurotoxic nitric oxide generated downstream of this complex, coupled with calpain activation, promotes neuronal apoptosis and disruption of cellular integrity. Consequently, PSD95 anchored at postsynaptic membranes is released into the interstitial space. Subsequent blood-brain barrier (BBB) disruption following infarction facilitates diffusion of PSD95 into the circulation along its concentration gradient [14, 15]. BBB permeability increased progressively following cerebral infarction, reaching maximal levels at day 3 post-onset [16, 17]. This peak demonstrated temporal concordance with the apex of plasma PSD95 dynamics.

Furthermore, a significant inverse correlation was identified between plasma PSD95 levels and activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) following cerebral infarction. As an indicator of the intrinsic coagulation pathway, shortened APTT typically reflects accelerated thrombin generation and a hypercoagulable state [18]. Studies have demonstrated abnormal activation of the coagulation system during the acute phase of cerebral infarction, characterized by extensive microthrombi formation and APTT shortening [19, 20]. In this study, baseline analysis demonstrated no statistically significant difference in APTT between the CI and CP groups. Although this finding may have been underpowered due to the limited sample size, it should not overshadow the potential role of APTT in the pathophysiology of cerebral infarction. These processes intensify ischemic/hypoxic neuronal damage, driving neurological decline and elevated liberation of PSD95 into the circulation.

Based on the observed association between plasma PSD95 concentration and cerebral infarction, a ROC curve was constructed to evaluate its diagnostic performance. The results indicated that although the independent diagnostic performance of PSD95 was moderate, its discriminatory ability remained statistically significant. These findings suggest the potential utility of plasma PSD95 concentration as an auxiliary biomarker for cerebral infarction.

The NIHSS score is widely used to assess cerebral infarction severity. In this study, although no significant correlation was observed between admission NIHSS scores and plasma PSD95 concentrations, the correlation coefficient increased progressively with time post-onset, indicating a strengthening positive association. By 6 months post-infarction, a significant positive correlation emerged, where higher plasma PSD95 concentrations corresponded to higher NIHSS scores. Although the correlation coefficient was modest, the direction of the correlation is consistent with the postulated role of PSD95 as a marker of neuronal injury. This suggests that elevated plasma PSD95 levels may be associated with unfavorable long-term prognosis in cerebral infarction patients.

As a core scaffolding protein at postsynaptic densities, aberrant plasma PSD95 levels not only reflect the degree of post-infarction neuronal injury but also indicate impaired synaptic plasticity and neurorepair capacity [12]. This correlation underscores PSD95’s potential as a dual diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for cerebral infarction. The potential of serum PSD95 as a novel biomarker for assessing disease severity and prognosis in CI warrants further investigation.

While this study is the first to report altered plasma PSD95 levels in ischemic stroke, several limitations should be acknowledged. A primary limitation of this study is the relatively short sample collection period and consequently limited sample size, which likely contributed to the weak correlation coefficient observed in the statistical analysis. Future investigations should expand cohort enrollment to validate our experimental findings. Secondly, while the NIHSS score was used for outcome analysis in this study, future research should consider incorporating the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) as a more comprehensive long-term functional outcome measure [21]. Furthermore, while providing clinical evidence, the absence of mechanistic investigations necessitates preclinical research to elucidate the pathological processes underlying PSD95 release into circulation.

Conclusions

This study presents the first clinical evidence establishing plasma PSD95 as a viable biomarker for cerebral infarction during acute phases. The quantification of plasma PSD95 levels demonstrates significant potential as a supplementary diagnostic parameter for cerebral infarction detection, while also exhibiting prognostic value for clinical outcome prediction in affected patients.

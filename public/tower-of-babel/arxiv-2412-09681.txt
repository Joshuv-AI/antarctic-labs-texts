Vector Portals at Future Lepton Colliders
Sagar Airen, Edward Broadberry, Gustavo Marques-Tavares, Lorenzo Ricci

Abstract
We assess the sensitivity of future lepton colliders to weakly coupled vector dark portals (aka “ Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons”) with masses ranging from tens of GeV to a few TeV. Our analysis focuses on dark photons and L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} gauge bosons. We consider both visible and invisible decay channels. We demonstrate that both high energy μ \mu colliders and future e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders, using the FCC-ee Z Z -pole and Z ​ H ZH operation modes as a benchmark, offer significant improvements in sensitivity. We find that both colliders can enhance the sensitivity to L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} bosons (for both visible and invisible decays) and to invisibly decaying dark photons by 1–2 orders of magnitude across the relevant mass range. Furthermore, we study the impact of forward μ \mu detectors at the μ \mu -collider on the sensitivity to both models.

Models

The focus of this paper is on the so-called vector portals. Vector portals consist of a massive vector,
Z^{\prime}
, of mass,
M_{Z^{\prime}}
, coupled to both the SM and some dark sector with the interaction

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=g_{Z^{\prime}}J_{\mu}^{\text{SM}}Z^{\prime\mu}+g_{D}J_{\mu}^{\text{Dark}}Z^{\prime\mu}\,,

(3)

where
J_{\mu}^{\text{SM}}
is a SM
U(1)
global current. For simplicity, the case where
J_{\mu}^{\text{SM}}
is a non-anomalous current is considered. For such a current neither a Wess-Zumino term or further assumptions on the dark sector are needed to cancel the anomaly (see
[
35
,
36
]
for a detailed discussion on this point).

There are various choices for the new gauged
U(1)
, but we focus only on the dark photon, where
J_{\mu}^{\text{SM}}
is the E
\&
M current, and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
portals (with
L_{x}
being the lepton number of species
x
). The analysis can be easily extended to other portals. Further assumptions on the dark sector and on the couplings in eq. (
3
) split the analysis into two regimes:

•

Visible decays
: either
g_{Z^{\prime}}\gg g_{D}
, or the dark sector states are too heavy for the
Z^{\prime}
to decay into the dark sector. In this limit the
Z^{\prime}
decays exclusively to the SM.

•

Invisible decays
: this is achieved for
g_{D}\gg g_{Z^{\prime}}
and assuming there are light invisible states (i.e. not decaying to visible SM particles) in the dark sector. We thus neglect the branching ratio to the SM and only consider missing energy,
\not{E}
, as the final state for collider searches.

While we only focus on these two models we emphasize that those cover all the phenomenology for
Z^{\prime}
searches at future lepton colliders. Indeed our sensitivity projections can be readily translated to any gauge boson with
L_{e}
and/or
L_{\mu}
charges.

2.1
Dark photon portal

The first model is the so-called dark photon or hypercharge portal (see
[
37
]
for an extensive review).
A vector of mass,
M_{Z^{\prime}}
, kinetically mixes with the hypercharge gauge boson. The interactions between the SM and the dark sector are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=\frac{\epsilon}{2c_{W}}Z_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}B^{\mu\nu}+g_{D}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\text{Dark}}\,,

(4)

where
Z_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}
,
B_{\mu\nu}
are the fields strengths for the dark photon and the SM
B
field respectively,
\epsilon\ll 1
is the kinetic mixing parameter, and
c_{W}=\cos\theta_{W}
with
\theta_{W}
the Weinberg mixing angle.

The interactions in eq. (
4
) can be brought into the form of eq. (
3
) by a suitable field redefinition diagonalising both the kinetic and mass terms.
For details of the diagonalisation procedure consult
[
38
,
39
]
.
For this work the interactions between the dark photon and the fermionic currents are considered in the limit
\epsilon\ll|1-r|
2
2

2

This approximation breaks down in a tiny region of a few GeV around
M_{Z^{\prime}}=M_{Z}
. That window is not to be considered reliable in our sensitivity projections.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=\frac{e\epsilon}{1-r}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\left(J^{\mu}_{em}-\frac{r}{c_{W}^{2}}J^{\mu}_{Y}\right)+g_{D}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\text{Dark}}\,,

(5)

where
r=M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}/m_{Z}^{2}
,
e
is the electric charge of the election, and
J_{\text{em}}
,
J_{Y}
are the SM electromagnetic, hypercharge currents respectively.
Note that if
M_{Z^{\prime}}\ll M_{Z}
, this leads to
g_{Z^{\prime}}\approx e\epsilon
, while for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gg M_{Z}
this leads to
g_{Z^{\prime}}\approx e\epsilon/c_{W}^{2}
. Thus, for
\epsilon\ll 1
in this model it is generically expected that
g_{D}>g_{Z^{\prime}}
. Whether the dark photon decays visibly or invisibly is therefore determined by whether there are light dark sector states.

Dark photons are targets for a plethora of searches. In the mass range of interest (
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10
GeV) collider searches are particularly relevant, either through resonance searches (visible scenario) or via missing energy searches (invisible scenario).
FCC-ee and the
\mu

Associated Production

First, we consider the associated production of a
Z^{\prime}
with a hard photon where the

decays invisibly (see left panel of Fig.

The primary background in this case is
\gamma\,\nu_{l}\,\bar{\nu}_{l}

\gamma\,\gamma
with one photon undetected, are subdominant. Signal events are characterized by the energy of the photon,

E_{\gamma}=\frac{s-M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{2\sqrt{s}},

(7)

with
\sqrt{s}
the CoM energy.

The reconstructed invariant mass in Eq. (
7

\sqrt{s}\gg M_{Z^{\prime}}

A small error

M_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm{recon.}}

\frac{\Delta M_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm{recon.}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm{recon.}}}\sim\frac{s}{2M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\frac{\Delta E_{\gamma}}{E_{\gamma}}\,.

(8)

We show example normalised reconstructed mass distributions for signal and background in Fig.

\displaystyle|M_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm{recon.}}-M_{Z^{\prime}}|<300\,\text{GeV},\hskip 28.45274pt\sqrt{s}=\text{3 TeV},

\displaystyle|M_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm{recon.}}-M_{Z^{\prime}}|<1\,\text{TeV}\,,\hskip 36.98866pt\sqrt{s}=\text{10 TeV}.

5
Results & Conclusion

In this paper we assessed the expected sensitivity of future lepton colliders, namely the FCC-ee and the
\mu
-collider (see Tab.
1
), to weakly coupled gauge portals. We specifically focused on the dark photon and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
portals with couplings given in Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
6
) respectively. For both models we considered two cases: the
Z^{\prime}
may decay primarily into a hidden sector (invisibly decaying case), or the
Z^{\prime}
may decay primarily into the Standard Model (visibly decaying case). In addition to this, we emphasize that for all practical purposes our results depend only on the
L_{e}
and
L_{\mu}
charges of the
Z^{\prime}
s, and can thus be easily adapted to any vector portal coupling to either electrons or muons.

We explored the production channels listed in Tab.
2
for both the visible and invisible cases. The technical details of our analysis are provided in Sec.
3
and Sec.
4
. We summarize our main findings below.

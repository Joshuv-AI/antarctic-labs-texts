QCD axion from chiral gauge theories
Ryosuke Sato, and Shonosuke Takeshita

Abstract
We present models of axion based on supersymmetric chiral gauge theories. In these models, the PQ symmetry is spontaneously broken by the non-perturbative dynamics of chiral gauge theory. Thanks to supersymmetry, IR dynamics of the models are calculable. We also present an example of a QCD axion model that is compatible with SU ⁡ ( 5 ) {\rm SU}(5) grand unification. We find that the PQ breaking scale should be higher than ∼ 10 13 ​ GeV \sim 10^{13}~{\rm GeV} for the successful GUT unification. In addition, the PQ breaking scale should be higher than the GUT scale to avoid a Landau pole of gauge coupling below the Planck scale.

Introduction

The strong CP problem
Jackiw:1976pf
;
Callan:1976je
is one of the most mysterious puzzles in the Standard Model (SM). The effective
\theta
-angle in the QCD sector is severely constrained as
|\bar{\theta}|\lesssim 10^{-10}
from the null observation of the neutron electric dipole moment (EDM)
Abel:2020pzs
, though
{\cal O}(1)
CP phase has been observed in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix. This would indicate some unknown mechanism to realize the hierarchy
|\delta_{\rm CKM}|\gg|\bar{\theta}|
which is incorporated in physics beyond the standard model.
The QCD axion
Peccei:1977hh
;
Peccei:1977ur
;
Weinberg:1977ma
;
Wilczek:1977pj
is one of the most interesting proposals to solve the strong CP problem. It is a pseudo Nambu-Goldstone (NG) boson associated with spontaneous breaking of global
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
symmetry which is anomalous under QCD interaction. Since the QCD axion couples to
{\rm SU}(3)_{c}
gauge field via a coupling
(a/32\pi^{2}f_{a})G_{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}^{\mu\nu}
, the
\theta
-angle is “promoted to a dynamical scalar field”. Then the
\theta
-angle is absorbed by the axion at its potential minimum
Vafa:1984xg
and the predicted size of the neutron EDM becomes much smaller than the current experimental upperbound
Georgi:1986kr
.

An interesting scenario for the QCD axion would be spontaneous
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
breaking induced by strong dynamics of a new gauge interaction
Kim:1984pt
;
Choi:1985cb
.
In this case, the order parameter of PQ symmetry breaking is a composite operator.
Refs.
Randall:1992ut
;
Dobrescu:1996jp
;
Redi:2016esr
;
Lillard:2017cwx
;
Lillard:2018fdt
;
Lee:2018yak
;
Gavela:2018paw
;
Vecchi:2021shj
;
Contino:2021ayn
;
Cox:2023dou
;
Gherghetta:2025fip
utilize this property to solve the axion quality problem.
Most models utilize strong dynamics of QCD-like gauge theories. On the other hand, discussion of the QCD axion based on strong dynamics of chiral gauge theories is quite limited
Gavela:2018paw
;
Cox:2023dou
because of the difficulty of the analysis, as chiral gauge theories generally lack analytical tools for studying their non-perturbative dynamics.
Recently, Refs.
Csaki:2021xhi
;
Csaki:2021aqv
have analyzed the strong dynamics of chiral gauge theories in supersymmetric (SUSY) models with small SUSY breaking
Aharony:1995zh
;
Alvarez-Gaume:1996vlf
;
Alvarez-Gaume:1997wnu
;
Martin:1998yr
;
Cheng:1998xg
;
Strassler:1997ny
;
Kitano:2011zk
;
Murayama:2021xfj
. Thanks to SUSY, non-perturbative dynamics of chiral gauge theory can be solved in analytic expressions.

In this paper, we apply the analysis in
Csaki:2021xhi
;
Csaki:2021aqv
to chiral gauge theories, and show some explicit examples of the QCD axion from chiral gauge theories.
This paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we show explicit examples of models.
In section
3
, we show an example of model of the QCD axion which is compatible with
{\rm SU}(5)
grand unification, and discuss its phenomenology and cosmology.
Section
4
is devoted to conclusions and discussions.
In appendix
A

{\rm SU}(2N)
chiral gauge theory.
In appendix
B

In appendix
C

In appendix
D

Let us discuss the anomaly coefficient
N_{\rm anom}
and the number of physical vacua
N_{\rm DW}
in the current model.
In the electric theory,
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
transformation is given as

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{q}\to\bar{F}_{q}\exp\left(i(2N-2)\alpha\right),\quad\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}\to\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}\exp\left(i(2N-2)\alpha\right),\quad S\to S\exp\left(-2iN\alpha\right).

(52)

Then, the anomaly coefficient is

\displaystyle N_{\rm anom}^{{\rm(ele)}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(2N-2)(2N-4)}_{{\bar{F}}_{q}}+\underbrace{(2N-2)(2N-4)}_{{\bar{F}}_{\bar{q}}}=8(N-1)(N-2).

(53)

{\rm SU}(2N-4)_{1}
gauge group has the center
Z_{2N-4}
and it works as

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{q}\to\bar{F}_{q}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ik}{2N-4}\right),\qquad\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}\to\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ik}{2N-4}\right),\qquad A\to A\exp\left(\frac{4\pi ik}{2N-4}\right),

(54)

where
k
is an integer.
Therefore we can see that
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
with
\alpha=\pi/(N-2)
and
Z_{2N-4}
with
k=-2
are equivalent. Thus, to count the number of degenerated vacua
N_{\rm vac}^{\rm(ele)}
in the electric theory, the vacua connected by a gauge transformation should be regarded as the same vacuum
Lazarides:1982tw
, and we obtain

\displaystyle N_{\rm DW}^{{\rm(ele)}}=\frac{N_{\rm anom}^{{\rm(ele)}}}{2(N-2)}=4(N-1).

(55)

In the magnetic theory,
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
transformation is given as

\displaystyle q\to q\exp\left(-i\left(N-2\right)\alpha\right),\quad p\to p\exp\left(2iN\left(N-2\right)\alpha\right),\quad M\to M\exp\left(2i\left(N-2\right)\alpha\right).

(56)

The anomaly coefficient is

\displaystyle N^{\rm(mag)}_{\rm anom}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{8(-N+2)}_{q}+\underbrace{8(-N+2)}_{\bar{q}}+\underbrace{(2N-4)(N+2)}_{M}+\underbrace{(2N-4)(N+2)}_{\bar{M}}+\underbrace{(2N-4)2N}_{M_{a}}

\displaystyle=8(N-1)(N-2).

(57)

We can find a center
Z_{2}\in{\rm Spin}(8)
which works as

\displaystyle q\to-q,\qquad p\to p.

(58)

Thus,
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
transformation with
\alpha=\pi/(N-2)
can be identified with this
Z_{2}
. Then, the number of vacua in the magnetic theory is

\displaystyle N_{\rm DW}^{\rm(mag)}=\frac{N_{\rm anom}^{\rm(mag)}}{2(N-2)}=4(N-1).

(59)

N_{\rm anom}^{\rm(ele)}=N_{\rm anom}^{\rm(mag)}
can be regarded as one of anomaly matching condition between electric theory and magnetic theory.
Since
N_{\rm DW}^{\rm(ele)}=N_{\rm DW}^{\rm(mag)}
is satisfied, the number of degenerated vacua is also consistent.

3.1
Symmetry breaking scale and VEVs

In the current model, we have two symmetry breaking scales; the PQ breaking scale

and the GUT scale

In our analysis, we treat

For given

M_{\rm GUT}
is determined as a scale at which two gauge couplings unify.
Suppose that there exists a solution of

\displaystyle\alpha_{a}(M_{\rm GUT})=\alpha_{b}(M_{\rm GUT}).

(62)

Here

\displaystyle[{\rm SU}(14)_{1}\times{\rm SU}(5)_{2}]\times{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}\times{\rm U}(1)_{q}\times{\rm U}(1)_{R}

\displaystyle\to\quad

\displaystyle[{\rm Sp}(4)_{1}\times{\rm SU}(5)_{\rm GUT}]\times{\rm U}(1)_{d}

\displaystyle\to\quad

\displaystyle[{\rm Sp}(4)_{1}\times{\rm SU}(3)_{c}\times{\rm SU}(2)_{L}\times{\rm U}(1)_{Y}]\times{\rm U}(1)_{d}.

\displaystyle[{\rm SU}(14)_{1}\times{\rm SU}(5)_{2}]\times{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}\times{\rm U}(1)_{q}\times{\rm U}(1)_{R}

\displaystyle\to\quad

\displaystyle[{\rm SU}(14)_{1}\times{\rm SU}(3)_{2}\times{\rm SU}(2)_{2}\times{\rm U}(1)_{2}]\times{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}\times{\rm U}(1)_{q}\times{\rm U}(1)_{R}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}(M_{\rm PQ})}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}_{1}(M_{\rm PQ})}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{14}(M_{\rm PQ})},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}(M_{\rm PQ})}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(M_{\rm PQ})}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{14}(M_{\rm PQ})},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}(M_{\rm PQ})}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}_{3}(M_{\rm PQ})}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{14}(M_{\rm PQ})}.

3.3
Proton decay

In this subsection, we discuss the proton lifetime in our model.
In the minimal SUSY SU(5) GUT, the proton decay is induced by the exchange of the color-triplet Higgs and the heavy SU(5) gauge bosons, which is described by the dimension-five and -six effective operators, respectively.
Since we assume
\mathcal{O}(100)
TeV sfermion masses, the dimension-five proton decay operators are not constrained by the current observations
Hisano:2013exa
;
Hisano:2022qll
.
Thus, we discuss the dimension-six proton decay operators.
In our analysis, we consider the
p\to\pi^{0}{e^{+}}
mode, which is constrained by the experiments most severely in the case of the dimension-six proton decay operators.
In the fermion mass basis, these relevant interactions are expressed as
Hisano:1992jj
;
Hisano:2012wq

3.4
Small instanton

Let us comment on small instanton contributions to the axion mass
Holdom:1982ex
;
Holdom:1985vx
;
Dine:1986bg
;
Flynn:1987rs
;
Choi:1998ep
.

{\rm SU}(5)_{2}
gauge interaction is asymptotic non-free in the energy scale above the PQ breaking scale. Thus, we could expect that the small instanton affects the QCD axion mass. However, this contribution is extremely small as we explain below.

{\rm SU}(5)_{2}
instanton provides a ’t Hooft vertex with 10
{\rm SU}(5)_{2}
gauginos, 14
\bar{F}_{q}
fermions, 14
\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}
fermions, and MSSM fermions as external legs.
Let us discuss the contraction of fermion lines of
{\rm SU}(5)_{2}
gauginos,
\bar{F}_{q}
, and
\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}
. Total R-charge of these legs is
-18
. To have a contribution to the axion mass, we have to make contractions of fermion lines and the remaining external legs should be only PQ-charged scalar field with VEV.
Since the scalar fields of
\bar{F}_{q}
and
\bar{F}_{\bar{q}}
do not have R-charge, we need to consume 9 insertions of gaugino mass
m_{\lambda}
to have only scalar field as external log by contracting fermion lines. Thus, the axion mass squared suffers from quite strong suppression factor
(m_{\lambda}/\Lambda)^{9}\sim 10^{-105}(m_{\lambda}/1~{\rm TeV})^{9}(\Lambda/M_{\rm pl})^{-9}
. Also, the additional suppression factor should come from contraction of MSSM fermions, e.g.,
K\equiv\prod_{q}(y_{q}/4\pi)\sim 10^{-23}

Agrawal:2017ksf
.
Thus, we conclude that the small instanton contribution to the axion mass is negligibly small in the current model.

3.5
Cosmology

The QCD axion and its superpartners play some important roles in cosmology and it highly depends on the details of scenario. Here we briefly mention the cosmological aspects of the present model.

In the present model, the number of vacua is
N_{\rm DW}=24
and potentially we suffer from domain-wall problem. Also, there exists
{\rm U}(1)_{d}
global symmetry and the lightest particle with nonzero
{\rm U}(1)_{d}
charge has a mass of the order of
M_{\rm PQ}
. Since those stable domain-wall network and particles can overclose the universe, we assume the inflation energy scale
H_{\rm inf}
is sufficiently low such that PQ symmetry had already broken during inflation.

The QCD axion is a good candidate of the dark matter. As we have discussed, the decay constant of the axion in the current model is above
\sim 10^{13}~{\rm GeV}
which is higher than the canonical value of
f_{a}\sim 10^{12}~{\rm GeV}
for the misalignment scenario. See, e.g., Ref.
Kawasaki:2013ae
. Thus, we need to assume that the initial misalignment angle is suppressed by some mechanism or entropy production between the onset of the axion coherent oscillation and Big Bang nucleosynthesis diluted the energy density of the axion
Kawasaki:1995vt
;
Banks:2002sd
;
Kawasaki:2015pva
.

If our universe has experienced a first order phase transition, gravitational waves (GW) from the phase transition can be produced. From this point of view, the confinement phase transition of
{\rm Sp}(4)
SUSY pure Yang-Mills theory is interesting. Eq. (
61
) indicates that the confinement scale of
{\rm Sp}(4)
gauge theory is below the PQ breaking scale, and this phase transition can happen after the end of inflation.
The lattice simulation indicates the order of confinement phase transition is the first order in
{\rm Sp}(4)
pure Yang-Mills theory
Holland:2003kg
;
Braun:2010cy
and QCD with adjoint fermion
Cossu:2008wh

{\rm Sp}(4)
SUSY pure Yang-Mills theory is not available.
The sound wave after bubble collision is considered to be the main source of GW and its peak frequency and amplitude of the GW signal from phase transitions are estimated as

4
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper, we have discussed axion models based on supersymmetric chiral gauge theories; SUSY Georgi-Glashow type model and SUSY Bars-Yankielowicz type model. Thanks to small SUSY soft breaking, we can apply the analysis presented in Refs.
Csaki:2021xhi
;
Csaki:2021aqv
and we found that, in both models, spontaneous breaking of PQ symmetry is induced by non-perturbative dynamics. We have also discussed a GUT-motivated QCD axion model. We have shown that the proton lifetime becomes shorter for smaller
f_{a}
and our model can be tested by future proton decay searches. Also, a high scale
f_{a}
is preferred to avoid the Landau pole of the gauge coupling below the Planck scale. Its cosmological aspects have been discussed, and the gravitational wave from the confinement phase transition of the unbroken
{\rm Sp}(4)
gauge sector would be an interesting target for future experiments.

In the closing of this paper, we briefly mention non-supersymmetric limit of the current model in which all of sfermions and gauginos are decoupled. This limit is interesting because gauge invariant PQ charged scalar operator consists of at least six fermion fields and it has at least dimension 9.
Thus, the PQ symmetry arises as an accidental symmetry and the axion quality problem can be relaxed. However, in such a regime, supersymmetry is badly broken and it is quite difficult to study in the current method. See, e.g., a discussion in section 2.4 of Ref.
Bolognesi:2021jzs
. There has been literature which discuss such a model by using the most attractive channel (MAC) analysis
Raby:1979my
;
Dimopoulos:1980hn
and it predicts a different IR picture; ’t Hooft anomaly matching condition is satisfied by not axion but massless baryon (see, e.g.,
Appelquist:2000qg
). To have definite conclusions in this limit, we would need a non-perturbative analysis such as lattice gauge theory simulations.

(99)

Below the energy scale of
v_{\bar{F}}

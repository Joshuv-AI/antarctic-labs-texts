Multi-Targeted Graph Backdoor Attack
Md Nabi Newaz Khan, Abdullah Arafat Miah, Yu Bi

Abstract
Graph neural network (GNN) have demonstrated exceptional performance in solving critical problems across diverse domains yet remain susceptible to backdoor attacks. Existing studies on backdoor attack for graph classification are limited to single target attack using subgraph replacement based mechanism where the attacker implants only one trigger into the GNN model. In this paper, we introduce the first multi-targeted backdoor attack for graph classification task, where multiple triggers simultaneously redirect predictions to different target labels. Instead of subgraph replacement, we propose subgraph injection which preserves the structure of the original graphs while poisoning the clean graphs. Extensive experiments demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, where our attack achieves high attack success rates for all target labels with minimal impact on the clean accuracy. Experimental results on five dataset demonstrate the superior performance of our attack framework compared to the conventional subgraph replacement-based attack. Our analysis on four GNN models confirms the generalization capability of our attack which is effective regardless of the GNN model architectures and training parameters settings. We further investigate the impact of the attack design parameters including injection methods, number of connections, trigger sizes, trigger edge density and poisoning ratios. Additionally, our evaluation against state-of-the-art defenses (randomized smoothing and fine-pruning) demonstrates the robustness of our proposed multi-target attacks. This work highlights the GNN vulnerability against multi-targeted backdoor attack in graph classification task. Our source codes will be available at https://github.com/SiSL-URI/Multi-Targeted-Graph-Backdoor-Attack .

Backdoor attacks on graph neural networks can be executed by integrating triggers into graph data and mapping the poisoned samples to a target class. Specifically, GNN triggers can take various forms, including node embeddings, graph topology/edge configuration as well as subgraphs.
Most of the existing research on backdoor attacks in GNN focuses on node classification, where triggers are implanted to manipulate the predicted label of a specific node

While these studies have demonstrated substantial success in compromising node-level classification, backdoor attacks on graph classification can present significantly greater challenges. Unlike node classification, where the attack objective is localized to individual nodes, backdoor attack on graph classification task requires influencing the global graph-level representation, a combined result from all node and edge embeddings, thereby causing the GNN model to misclassify the graph into the target class.

Although they have achieved remarkable attack performance, all of these previous studies exclusively address single-targeted attacks where the GNN model predicts all classes containing the subgraph trigger as belonging to a unique target class. However, multi-targeted backdoor attacks for graph classification remain unexplored. Multi-targeted attack is significantly more sophisticated and powerful, given that it allows different trigger design to provoke diverse target classes which adds extra complexities onto the compromised model.

To fill the research gap, in this paper, we investigate multi-targeted backdoor attacks on GNNs for graph classification. The key challenge lies in designing an attack framework that can simultaneously incorporate multiple triggers into the model. Achieving such an attack is non-trivial since introducing multiple triggers poses the threat of interference between triggers, as well as preserving the clean accuracy of the attacked GNN model. Existing research works have utilized subgraph replacement within clean graphs to utilize the single target attack for graph classification. Despite its high attack success, the replacement based framework struggles to support the multi-targeted backdoor attack. It fails to implant multiple triggers at the same time because of interference among the triggers influence on the GNN model and distortion of the clean graphs original structure (detailed evaluation can be seen in Section

1.

We propose a subgraph injection-based mechanism for backdoor attacks in graph classification. Unlike the state-of-the-art replacement-based backdoor attacks, the proposed method is the first-of-its-kind to introduce poison node injection strategy for achieving multi-targeted backdoor attacks.

2.

Extensive experiments are conducted to demonstrate the efficacy of our proposed approach across diverse datasets while maintaining the clean accuracy. Additionally, we exhaustively analyze the variation in attack performance with respect to configurable attack design parameters.

3.

We evaluate the proposed attack against the state-of-the art certified defenses, demonstrating that our proposed framework for multi target backdoor attack is robust and effective against Randomized Smoothing and Fine-Pruning defenses.

Background

2.1
Graph Neural Network (GNN)

Graph Neural Network (GNN) is a special type of machine learning model that can process & extract information from graph-structured data and make predictions based on it. Traditional Machine Learning models can handle euclidean data, but struggle with graph type data, where each sample may have irregular size and structure. Graph Neural Network solves this problem by its ability to learn from different shaped data using a combination of message passing and global pooling techniques which was first introduced by Gori et al.
[
7
]
. Subsequently, Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) was introduced by incorporating convolution operation into it
[

[

, GraphSage
[
11

, Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN)
[

Graph Neural Network are used to perform 3 major tasks; (1) Graph Classification: the model predicts the class label of the entire graph based on the node feature, topology, or connection between the nodes and other attributes, (2) Node Classification: predicts the labels of each nodes based on the node feature of it self, node feature of its neighbors and global level features if available, (3) link prediction: predicts the edges between 2 nodes based on the features that are extracted from the graphs from its initial features. These tasks are done to solve a wide range of diverse real world problems by using different architectures of GNN such as research, e-commerce platform, medicine industry, chemical industry and many more.

Backdoor Attack

The security of deep learning systems has become a critical issue as these models are deployed in many sensitive applications

In backdoor attacks, model is trained on training data which are manipulated with different kind of trigger to make the model malicious and deliberately taught to predict attacker’s chosen label when trigger is present. During inference phase, it will misclassify the testing data with trigger while maintaining the accuracy on benign data samples. Backdoor attacks were initially studied extensively in computer vision contexts, particularly for image classification which displayed the feasibility of backdoor attack in Deep learning. The seminal BadNets

In recent times, backdoor attacks on graph neural network have gained great attention. Because GNN is widely adopted in sensitive real world applications and its vulnerability to backdoor attack is a critical security concern. Based on the type of task, backdoor attack can be categorized in 2 types, Backdoor on Node Classification & Backdoor attack on Graph Classification. Early works on this include the subgraph replacement based attack done by Zhaohan Xi et al.

Subsequently, Several research works have been done in the past few years on backdoor attack for both graph classification as well as node classification where majority of the works are on backdoor node classification side

However, all of these study are on single target attack where the attacker implants triggers and maps the triggers data to a single target label. This leaves the multi-target backdoor attack for graph classification task unexplored where the attacker may manipulate the graph to any desired target label inspite of just one target label. In this study, we investigated the multi target backdoor attack for graph classification. Integrating multiple triggers has the risk of interference between the triggers which may lead to a failure of the attack. To address this challenge, we propose an injection subgraph based mechanism to execute the multi target backdoor attack.

Methodology

3.1
Threat Model

We consider a realistic threat model in which the adversary is capable of perturbing only a small portion of the training data while having no access to the model architecture, parameters and training hyperparameters. The attacker’s objective is to embed multiple distinct triggers into the GNN model, each associated with a specific target class. During inference, the attacker can inject the corresponding trigger into a benign graph, causing the trained GNN to misclassify the perturbed graph as the designated target class.

3.3
Attack Overview

Figure
1
illustrates an overview of the injection-based multi-targeted backdoor attack. The attack begins with clean graph dataset and a GNN model where the attacker has access to a small portion of the dataset and has no information about the GNN model architecture, model weights and training parameters. Distinct graphs are selected from the clean dataset for interjecting triggers for all
m
target classes. For each target class, a even number of graphs are chosen for injecting the trigger from all classes available other than that specific target class. The number of graphs which will be poisoned is set by the attacker which is called poisoning ratio. Subsequently,
m
number of different trigger subgraph is generated which are all structurally different. The size and feature dimension of the triggers depends on the characteristic of dataset and node feature dimension of the nodes in clean graphs. Each of these triggers are then injected into the clean graphs which are selected for each triggers and the label of these trigger injected graphs are set to the target label of each trigger. Through this procedure, poisoned data
D_{poisoned}
is constructed. The combination of the backdoored data and the clean untouched data are used for influencing the model according to the attacker’s goal and executing the multi target backdoor attack successfully. To obtain the backdoored model
f_{w}
, we trained the model with a combined loss over clean & poisoned data and the model weight
w
is optimized by minimizing the total loss. The optimization of weights for our Multi Target backdoor attack can be formulated as:

w^{*}=\arg\min_{w}\Bigg[\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{clean}}|}\sum_{(G,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{clean}}}L\big(f_{w}(G),y\big)\\
+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{p}^{(j)}|}\sum_{(G^{\prime},\tau_{j})\in\mathcal{D}_{p}^{(j)}}L\big(f_{w}(G^{\prime}),\tau_{j}\big)\Bigg].

(3)

At the inference phase, clean testing data were used for getting the clean accuracy. On the other hand for determining the ASRs, the entire clean testing dataset was poisoned by injecting triggers individually to get
m
distinct backdoored test datasets corresponding to
m
triggers. These poisoned data passed through the backdoored model which should predict the target class of the trigger. Finally the accuracy for the compromised model on each of these
m
poisoned dataset was calculated which are the ASRs of our attack.

This framework represent the first systematic approach to embed coexisting backdoor triggers in graph classification model without any interference among the triggers.

3.4
Attack design

Designing multi target backdoor attack is way more challenging than single taregt attack for graph classification. Traditional single target attacks use subgraph replacement as trigger which distorts the characteristic of the original clean graphs, which may effect the clean accuracy and increase the risk of interference of the triggers if multiple triggers are implanted into the dataset. In this section, we present a comprehensive injection based attack design to implement the multi target backdoor attack for graph classification where
m
subgraph triggers are injected into the original graphs, with each trigger associated with a different target class.

m
distinct triggers are generated using the Erdos Reyni method which takes the trigger size and trigger edge density as input and produces a pattern output for the subgraph. The process can be formally described as:

T=ER(\text{$trigger$}\ \text{$size$},\ \text{$trigger$}\ \text{$edge$}\ \text{$density$}).

(4)

The trigger size is defined as the ratio of the number of nodes in the trigger and average number of nodes for per graphs in a dataset. The value of trigger size was set for different dataset based on the average size of the clean graphs of a dataset. Larger trigger size offers stronger backdoor power but can effect the clean accuracy and has higher risk of interference with other triggers, while smaller triggers may not have enough power to backdoor the original graph’s global level feature representation. The trigger edge density controls the connectivity of the trigger subgraphs. It refers to the percentage of edges or connections that will be in a triggers of the maximum possible edges or connections. If trigger size is denoted as
n_{trigger}
and total number of edges in the trigger is denoted as
e_{trigger}
, the trigger edge density can be expressed as
\rho_{trigger}=\frac{2e_{trigger}}{n_{trigger}(n_{trigger}-1)}
. We propose two kind of settings for the trigger edge density. The datasets used in this work can be largely divided into 2 categories; (1) with node feature matrix, (2) without node feature matrix. For the datasets with the node feature matrix, the class labels depends both on the node feature matrix as well as the topology of the graph and we propose a fixed trigger edge density value for this kind of dataset. In contrast, for the datasets which lacks the intrinsic node feature matrix and just have the nodes & connections among them, the class labels solely depends on the topology between the nodes in the graph. For this kind of dataset, setting the value of trigger edge density becomes much more critical and we used cross validation of trigger edge density to determine its optimal value for a successful backdoor attack. The node features of the trigger in case of 1st type of dataset is generated on Random individually for all m trigger subgraphs. In case of 2nd type of dataset with no node feature, the degree of each node is used as it’s node feature and same is taken for the trigger as well.

The core of our attack lies in the injection of the trigger subgraphs, which doesn’t disturb the original structure of the graphs when triggered. For injecting the trigger of a specific target class (
\tau_{j}
), a fraction of the original graph from all other classes expect target class (
\tau_{j}
) are chosen for injecting trigger. This fraction is refereed to poisoning ratio (r) and is applied individually for all classes except target class. This will ensure that even amount of host graphs from all classes. We propose randomized injection method which our experiment demonstrate to be superior than other injection point such as highest degree, lowest degree, highest similarity, lowest similarity. The number of edges or connections (
k
) between the host clean graph and trigger subgraph can also be customized in our attack design. After injecting the backdoor triggers into the original graph dataset, we proceed to train our Graph Neural Network model with this backdoored dataset to get the backdoored model
f_{w}
. Before training the backdoored model, we train the GNN model with the clean dataset to get the benign model
f_{w_{0}}
. We determine the clean model accuracy using
f_{w_{0}}
which gives us the benchmark for clean accuracy comparison of our malicious GNN model. Algorithm 1 demonstrates the flow and optimization procedure of the GNN model in this work.

Experimental Results

This section presents an extensive evaluation of our proposed multi target backdoor attack. To compare the attack effectiveness and evasiveness between conventional replacement attack mechanism and our proposed attack mechanism, we first implemented both of the attack on all 5 datasets. For executing the subgraph replacement based attack, we used the exact same attack design parameters stated in subsection

Traditional Replacement vs Our Proposed Attack:
We systematically evaluated our multi target backdoor attack on 5 diverse datasets to assess its effectiveness and the robustness. Prior works use the replacement based backdoor attack for graph classification yet no study has examined how replacement based attack scales up when multiple triggers needs to coexist. In this study, we examined the traditional replacement based backdoor attack and our proposed mechanism across all 5 datasets in multi target scenarios for comparing our proposed approach against conventional replacement approach. For datasets with only topology (Reddit-Multi-12k, Reddit-Multi-5k) where the classification depends solely on the structure of the dataset, implanting multiple triggers at the same time in a GNN model is not a trivial task as it has risk of dominant trigger which can interfere with the models learning process of other triggers. The result on table

Conventional replacement method achieved a small CAD of 0.92% on Reddit-Multi-12k dataset which shows that the model works on clean graphs almost close to the clean model, however it only achieved ASRs of 58.49%, 87%, 57.14%, meaning the attack faced interference of the triggers allowing only one trigger to be learned by the model which dominated the other 2 triggers. In comparison, our proposed attack mechanism achieved an ASRs ranging from 96% to 99.25% with a CAD of 0.3%.

In summary, our proposed multi target backdoor attack demonstrate exceptional performance by successfully embedding multiple triggers into the GNN model without the interference among the triggers while preserving the clean accuracy of the model whereas the conventional replacement based strategy does not support multi targe backdoor attack. On all of the datasets except Cifar10, only one ASR goes above 75% leaving the rest of the ASRs to a low value which indicates the interference among the triggers while embedding it into the model. The results empirically validate the clear superiority of our proposed attack over the conventional replacement based attack from all aspects in case of multi target backdoor attack for graph classification.

Impact of Trigger Injection Method:
We evaluated the effect of different injection strategies of the trigger on Reddit-Multi-12k dataset. We tested the influence of five distinct injection strategy - highest degree, highest similarity, lowest similarity, lowest degree, Random. Reddit-Multi-12k was chosen for this experiment because of its structural variation of the graphs in this dataset having nodes with inconsistent degree or connections. The comparison of the ASRs and CAD for different injection strategy examined in this work is illustrated in figure
2
. Deterministic strategies (highest degree, highest similarity, lowest similarity and lowest degree) has achieved ASRs in a range 97.75% to 99.42% with clean accuracy degradation between 2.46% to 2.96%. Although all four of these have attained notable performance, the best performance was achieved by Random injection strategy in case of both Model Manipulation (ASR
>
99.89%) and attack stealthiness (CAD 0.75%). This suggests that in deterministic approach to inject the triggers, as the triggers are injected consistently in specific type of Node, the trigger might interfere with the property of the host graph which decrease model’s ability to learn clean graphs. Random strategy have the flexibility to inject triggers because of which the triggers may be injected to some nodes which have higher influence to the GNN model than it has with a specific injection point. the Random process avoid that by distributing the trigger on different Node points. In addition to this, the randomness of the trigger injection makes it tough to detect the trigger by the user. Based on the comprehensive results, we propose the Random injection method for implementing the multi target backdoor attack. The combination of superior ASR, minimal CAD and enhanced resistance to statistical detection makes random injection the optimal choice across diverse attack scenarios.

Scalability to Multiple Targets:
We have used number of target labels as three as default for all the experiments done in this study. A fundamental question for our multi target attack arises regarding it’s scalability: if we increase the number of target to a higher number from 3, can our proposed attack mechanism support the coexistence of those triggers without any interference to the GNN model and compromising the clean accuracy? To investigate this critical question, we increased the number of target from 3 to the maximum (number of classes exist in the dataset). table
5
shows the attack success rate (ASR), clean accuracy (CA) and clean accuracy drop (CAD) of our attack for different number of target classes. The ASR shown in this table is the mean of all ASRs for each target class. With our default settings (3 targets), our attack achieved a mean ASR of 99.93% without any significant drop of clean accuracy resulting a CAD of only 0.75%. Increasing the number of triggers to 4 or 5 haven’t decreased any notable ASR drop (around 0.04%) with the slighted decline of clean accuracy of around 0.45%. Even when the number of target is increased to 8 and 10 (which is the maximum number of class in Cifar10 dataset), we achieved mean ASR of above 99.87%. This shows that larger number of triggers can coexist without any interference with our proposed framework. The CAD increased to 3.3% when number of trigger is increased to 10, because for implanting larger number of triggers, greater number of graphs are needed to be backdoored. This increased the share of poisoned graph against clean graph for training the model. Though there is a rise of CAD when number of target labels were increased, it is still in an acceptable and modest range. The results demonstrates the robustness and scalability of our proposed framework for implementing multi target backdoor attack.

Robustness against certified defense

To evaluate the robustness of our proposed multi target backdoor attack, we assessed its performance against two widely adopted defense mechanism: Randomized Smoothing
[
3
]
and Fune-Pruning
[
16
]
. In this section we present a brief description of the defense mechanisms, experimental setup and a thorough evaluation of our attack’s resilience against certified countermeasures.

5.1
Defense Mechanism

Randomized Smoothing (RS):
Randomized smoothing is one of the most efficient and popular defense methods against backdoor attacks. Randomized smoothing adds small gaussian noises
N(0,\sigma^{2}I)
to the node features of the input test graphs and creates variants of the input samples which can be mathematically written as
G_{i}^{noisy}
, where i = 1 to n. A smoothed classifier is developed by the prediction on all of these variants and executing majority voting to obtain the final prediction:

\hat{y}=\arg\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\left|\left\{i\;:\;f_{\omega}\!\left(G^{\text{noisy}}_{i}\right)=y,\;i=1,\dots,n\right\}\right|

where,
G_{i}^{noisy}
is the
i_{th}
noisy version of the input graph G. This approach smoothens the decision of the model by this approach making it more robust to backdoor triggers.

Fine-Pruning (FP):
Fine-Pruning is a post training defense that does operations on the backdoored model in two steps. (1) Fine-tune the backoored GNN model with a fraction of clean graph dataset which modifies the model weights to decrease backdoor activations. (2) Prune neurons with low activation value on clean graph data. The proportion of pruned neurons is controlled by the pruning ratio.

5.2
Experimental Setup

To assess the robustness of our proposed multi target attack against defense mechanisms, We conduct comprehensive evaluation across all five datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, ENZYMES, Reddit-Multi-12k, Reddit-Multi-5k). For each dataset, we use the trained GCN model with three target classes and the default configuration described in section
4.1
. For Randomized Smoothing, we use 100 as the number of samples (n), meaning there will be 100 variants of the input graphs and majority voting will provide the final prediction. We varied the magnitude of the gaussian noise by changing the value of noise standard deviation (
\sigma
) and observed the impact of this defense method on ASRs and CA of the model. For Fine-Pruning we used 5-10% of the dataset as the clean samples depending on the original dataset size. In this case, we varied the pruning ratio and evaluated the models performance against Fine-Pruning.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we presented the first multi-target backdoor attack for graph classification. We introduced sub-graph injection based approach for multi-target backdoor attack on graph classification which achieves high attack success rates without much degradation of the clean accuracy. Our empirical experimental results demonstrates the superiority of our proposed framework over conventional subgraph replacement based backdoor for graph classification. The attack performance across 5 datasets and 4 models shows the effectiveness and generalization capability of the attack. Results on maximum number of targets [10 for CIFAR10 dataset] shows that multiple triggers (even equal to the number of existing classes) can successfully coexist without interfering with the prediction of other triggers. We conducted extensive experiments to examine the effect of different attack design parameters on the performance of our attack and demonstrated the insights for setting the parameter values. Moreover, our proposed framework for multi target backdoor on graph classification remained highly effective against certified defenses, showing the robustness of our proposed attack.

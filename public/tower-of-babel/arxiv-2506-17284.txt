A Theoretical Framework for Virtual Power Plant Integration with Gigawatt-Scale AI Data Centers: Multi-Timescale Control and Stability Analysis
Ali Peivandizadeh

Abstract
The explosive growth of artificial intelligence has created gigawatt-scale data centers that fundamentally challenge power system operation, exhibiting power fluctuations exceeding 500 MW within seconds and millisecond-scale variations of 50–75% of thermal design power. This paper presents a comprehensive theoretical framework that reconceptualizes Virtual Power Plants (VPPs) to accommodate these extreme dynamics through a four-layer hierarchical control architecture operating across timescales from 100 microseconds to 24 hours. Building upon recent empirical findings from ( Li and Li 2025 ; Jimenez-Ruiz and Milano 2024 ) , we develop control mechanisms and stability criteria specifically tailored to converter-dominated systems with pulsing megawatt-scale loads. We prove that traditional VPP architectures, designed for aggregating distributed resources with response times of seconds to minutes, cannot maintain stability when confronted with AI data center dynamics exhibiting slew rates exceeding 1,000 MW/s at gigawatt scale. Our framework introduces: (1) a sub-millisecond control layer that interfaces with data center power electronics to actively dampen power oscillations; (2) new stability criteria incorporating protection system dynamics, demonstrating that critical clearing times reduce from 150 ms to 83 ms for gigawatt-scale pulsing loads; and (3) quantified flexibility characterization showing that workload deferability enables 30% peak reduction while maintaining AI service availability above 99.95%. Through rigorous mathematical analysis validated against recent industry deployments, we establish performance bounds and demonstrate the framework’s capability to transform AI data centers from grid destabilization threats into controllable assets capable of providing 200–300 MW of frequency regulation and substantial spinning reserve. This work establishes the mathematical foundations necessary for the stable integration of AI infrastructure that will constitute 50–70% of data center electricity consumption by 2030.

PROOF OF HIERARCHICAL STABILITY

We prove that the multi-layer control system is stable under the three stated conditions.

Given the hierarchical system with layers
i=0,1,2,3
, where each layer has dynamics:

\dot{x}_{i}=f_{i}(x_{i},u_{i},w_{i})

(A.1)

where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
is the state vector,

Condition 1: Individual Layer Stability

For each layer
i
, assume there exists a Lyapunov function
V_{i}(x_{i})
such that:

\alpha_{i}(\|x_{i}\|)\leq V_{i}(x_{i})\leq\beta_{i}(\|x_{i}\|)

(A.2)

where
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
are class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
functions, and:

\dot{V}_{i}(x_{i})\leq-\gamma_{i}(\|x_{i}\|)+\delta_{i}(\|w_{i}\|)

where
\gamma_{i}
is a class
\mathcal{K}
function and
\delta_{i}
is a class
\mathcal{K}
function bounding the disturbance effect.

With
\tau_{i+1}/\tau_{i}\geq 10

\varepsilon_{i}=\frac{\tau_{i}}{\tau_{i+1}}\leq 0.1

The system can be written in two-timescale form:

\dot{x}_{i+1}=f_{i+1}(x_{i+1},h_{i}(x_{i}),w_{i+1})

(A.5)

\varepsilon_{i}\dot{x}_{i}=f_{i}(x_{i},u_{i},w_{i})

(A.6)

where
h_{i}(x_{i})
represents the quasi-steady-state mapping from layer
i
to layer
i+1
.

For the fast subsystem (layer
i
), setting
\varepsilon_{i}=0

0=f_{i}(x_{i},u_{i},w_{i})

(A.7)

This yields the quasi-steady-state:

x_{i}^{*}=g_{i}(u_{i},w_{i})

(A.8)

For the slow subsystem (layer
i+1
), substituting (A.8):

\dot{x}_{i+1}=f_{i+1}(x_{i+1},h_{i}(g_{i}(u_{i},w_{i})),w_{i+1})

(A.9)

Condition 3: Information Consistency

The projection operator
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i+1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
satisfies:

\|x_{i}^{*}-\pi_{i}(x_{i+1}^{*})\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\text{coord}}

(A.10)

This ensures that the quasi-steady-state approximation error is bounded.

Composite Lyapunov Function:

Define the composite Lyapunov function:

V(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\mu_{i}V_{i}(x_{i})

(A.11)

where
\mu_{i}>0
are weighting factors chosen such that
\mu_{i+1}/\mu_{i}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{i})
.

Taking the time derivative:

\dot{V}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\mu_{i}\dot{V}_{i}(x_{i})

(A.12)

Substituting (A.3):

\dot{V}(x)\leq-\sum_{i=0}^{3}\mu_{i}\gamma_{i}(\|x_{i}\|)+\sum_{i=0}^{3}\mu_{i}\delta_{i}(\|w_{i}\|)

(A.13)

Under the timescale separation condition, the boundary layer corrections satisfy:

\|x_{i}-x_{i}^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{i})

(A.14)

Therefore:

\dot{V}(x)\leq-\gamma(\|x\|)+\delta(\|w\|)

(A.15)

where
\gamma
and
\delta
are appropriate class
\mathcal{K}
functions.

This establishes input-to-state stability (ISS) of the hierarchical system. For
w=0
, we have
\dot{V}(x)<0
for
x\neq 0
, proving asymptotic stability.

Appendix B
APPENDIX B

FLOQUET THEORY APPLICATION FOR PULSING LOADS

We analyze the stability of the system with periodic pulsing loads using Floquet theory.

Consider the linearized system around a periodic trajectory:

\Delta\dot{x}=A(t)\Delta x+B(t)\Delta u

(B.1)

where
A(t)=A_{0}+A_{p}\cos(\omega_{p}t)
is
T
-periodic with
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{p}}
.

Floquet Theory Framework:

The fundamental matrix
\Phi(t)
satisfies:

\dot{\Phi}(t)=A(t)\Phi(t),\quad\Phi(0)=I

(B.2)

The monodromy matrix is:

M=\Phi(T)

(B.3)

The Floquet multipliers
\mu_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
M
. For stability, all
|\mu_{i}|<1
.

Perturbation Analysis:

Let
\varepsilon=\|A_{p}\|_{2}/\|A_{0}\|_{2}
be the pulsing strength. For small
\varepsilon
, we expand:

\Phi(t)=\Phi_{0}(t)+\varepsilon\Phi_{1}(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\Phi_{2}(t)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})

(B.4)

where
\Phi_{0}(t)=\exp(A_{0}t)
.

First-order correction:

\dot{\Phi}_{1}(t)=A_{0}\Phi_{1}(t)+A_{p}\cos(\omega_{p}t)\Phi_{0}(t)

(B.5)

Using the variation of parameters formula:

\Phi_{1}(t)=\Phi_{0}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{0}^{-1}(s)A_{p}\cos(\omega_{p}s)\Phi_{0}(s)\,ds

(B.6)

Simplifying:

\Phi_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\exp(A_{0}(t-s))A_{p}\exp(A_{0}s)\cos(\omega_{p}s)\,ds

(B.7)

Monodromy Matrix Approximation:

The monodromy matrix to first order:

M\approx\exp(A_{0}T)+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\exp(A_{0}(T-s))A_{p}\exp(A_{0}s)\cos(\omega_{p}s)\,ds

(B.8)

For the eigenvalues of
M
, using perturbation theory:

\mu_{i}\approx\lambda_{i}(1+\varepsilon\kappa_{i})

(B.9)

where
\lambda_{i}=\exp(\sigma_{i}T)
are the eigenvalues of
\exp(A_{0}T)
, with
\sigma_{i}
being the eigenvalues of
A_{0}
.

Stability Condition:

For stability,
|\mu_{i}|<1
for all
i
. This requires:

|\lambda_{i}(1+\varepsilon\kappa_{i})|<1

(B.10)

Assuming
A_{0}
is stable (
\text{Re}(\sigma_{i})<0
), we have
|\lambda_{i}|<1
. The condition becomes:

|1+\varepsilon\kappa_{i}|<\frac{1}{|\lambda_{i}|}

(B.11)

For the critical mode (minimum damping
\zeta_{\min}
), we have:

\sigma_{\text{crit}}=-\zeta_{\min}\omega_{0}

(B.12)

The stability condition becomes:

\varepsilon<\frac{\exp(\zeta_{\min}\omega_{0}T)-1}{|\kappa_{\text{crit}}|}

(B.13)

Relating to the pulsing participation factor:

p_{\text{pulse}}=\frac{\varepsilon\omega_{0}T}{2\pi}

(B.14)

We obtain:

p_{\text{pulse}}<\frac{2\zeta_{\min}\omega_{0}}{M_{\text{pulse}}}

(B.15)

where
M_{\text{pulse}}=\|A_{p}\|_{2}/\|A_{0}\|_{2}
.

Appendix C
APPENDIX C

TRANSIENT ENERGY FUNCTION FORMULATION

Consider the power system with
n
generators and
m
AI data center loads:

M_{i}\ddot{\delta}_{i}+D_{i}\dot{\delta}_{i}=P_{m_{i}}-P_{e_{i}}(\delta,V)-P_{\text{pulse},i}(t)

(C.1)

Define the transient energy function:

V(\delta,\omega)=V_{\text{ke}}(\omega)+V_{\text{pe}}(\delta)+V_{\text{pulse}}(\delta,t)+V_{\text{prot}}(\delta,V)

(C.2)

Kinetic energy:

V_{\text{ke}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}M_{i}\omega_{i}^{2}

(C.3)

Potential energy:

V_{\text{pe}}(\delta)=-\sum_{i}P_{m_{i}}(\delta_{i}-\delta_{i}^{s})-\int_{\delta^{s}}^{\delta}\sum_{ij}E_{i}^{\prime}E_{j}^{\prime}Y_{ij}\cos(\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}-\alpha_{ij})\,d\delta

(C.4)

For periodic pulsing loads:

V_{\text{pulse}}(\delta,t)=\sum_{i}A_{\text{pulse},i}\int_{\delta_{i}^{s}}^{\delta_{i}}\sin(\omega_{p}t+\phi_{i}(\delta_{i}^{\prime}))\,d\delta_{i}^{\prime}

(C.5)

The protection system introduces discrete energy changes:

V_{\text{prot}}(\delta,V)=\sum_{k\in\text{tripped}}P_{k,\text{trip}}\int_{t_{\text{trip},k}}^{t}H(V-V_{\text{thresh}})\,dt

(C.6)

The critical energy is the energy at the controlling unstable equilibrium point (UEP):

W_{\text{cr},0}=V(\delta^{u},0)-V(\delta^{s},0)

(C.7)

With pulsing loads and protection:

W_{\text{cr}}=W_{\text{cr},0}-\Delta W_{\text{pulse}}-\Delta W_{\text{protection}}

(C.8)

where

\Delta W_{\text{pulse}}=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left[V_{\text{pulse}}(\delta^{u},t)-V_{\text{pulse}}(\delta^{s},t)\right]

(C.9)

\Delta W_{\text{protection}}=\sum_{k\in\text{tripped}}P_{k,\text{trip}}\,t_{\text{clear}}

(C.10)

The system is transiently stable if:

V(\delta(t),\omega(t))<W_{\text{cr}}\quad\forall t>t_{\text{clear}}

(C.11)

The critical clearing time
t_{\text{cr}}
satisfies:

V(\delta(t_{\text{cr}}),\omega(t_{\text{cr}}))=W_{\text{cr}}

(C.12)

For gigawatt-scale pulsing loads:

t_{\text{cr}}\approx t_{\text{cr},0}\left(1-k_{p}\frac{P_{\text{pulse}}}{P_{\text{base}}}\right)

(C.13)

where
k_{p}\in[0.3,0.5]
is determined from extensive simulations accounting for:

•

Pulsing amplitude and frequency

•

Protection system settings

•

System inertia distribution

•

Network topology

The factor
k_{p}
increases with:

•

Higher pulsing amplitude

•

Lower system inertia

•

Faster protection system response

•

Weaker grid connection (lower SCR)

This completes the derivation of the transient energy function incorporating AI data center dynamics.

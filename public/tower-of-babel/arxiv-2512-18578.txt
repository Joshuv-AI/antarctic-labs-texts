Mass of C 0 C^{0} -asymptotically hyperbolic spaces via the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C25.53E20.53Z05
Yuqiao Li

Abstract
. We define a mass function on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with continuous metrics via the normalized Ricci–DeTurck flow. This definition coincides with the classical mass for C 2 C^{2} metrics. We also introduce the scalar curvature lower bound for continuous metrics – a key component in establishing the well-definedness of the C 0 C^{0} mass.



1.
Introduction

Mass is a crucial quantity on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds in general relativity. Wang defined the mass and established the positive mass theorem for conformally compact manifolds that are asymptotically hyperbolic in a specific sense
[
16
]
.
Chrusciel and Herzlich later extended the mass definition to more general asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds
[
6
]
.
Both Wang’s proof and that of Chrusciel-Herzlich for the positive mass theorem on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds rely on spin structures.
In three dimensions, Sakovich provided a non-spin proof of the positive mass theorem in the asymptotically hyperbolic setting using the Jang equation
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the mass of asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with non-smooth metrics.
For a three-dimensional asymptotically hyperbolic initial data set with
W^{1,2}
metric, the positive energy theorem was established by Wang and Zhang under nonnegative distributional curvature
[
17
]
.
Gicquaud and Sakovich recently defined the mass aspect function for weakly regular asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with
W^{1,2}_{loc}\bigcap L^{\infty}
metrics by means of cut-off functions
[
7
]
.
Analogously, Lundgren and Meco adopted the same approach to define the ADM mass for asymptotically Euclidean manifolds with
W^{1,2}_{loc}\bigcap L^{\infty}
metrics
[
10
]
.

On the other hand, Burkhardt-Guim used the Ricci-DeTurck flow to define the weak ADM mass for
C^{0}
asymptotically flat metrics, showing that it coincides with the standard ADM mass whenever the latter exists
[
4
]
.
We thus investigate the mass function on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with
C^{0}
metrics via the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow in this work.
Bahuaud showed that the normalized Ricci flow of conformally compact metrics exists for a short time interval and preserves conformally compactness
[
1
]
.
Qing-Shi-Wu established the long-time existence and convergence of the normalized Ricci flow on conformally compact asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds
[
11
]
.
Subsequently, Balehowsky and Woolgar proved that the mass of an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold decays exponentially to zero under the normalized Ricci flow
[
2
]
.

Firstly, we define a quantity called
C^{0}
local mass function by means of a cutoff function. Particularly, the limit of this local mass is the classical mass defined by Chrusciel-Herzlich when the metric is
C^{2}
asymptotically hyperbolic.
Let
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
be the standard hyperbolic manifold.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
C^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with a chart at infinity
\Phi:M\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
for some compact subsets
K\subset M
,
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\tau>0
as in Definition
2.2
.
Let
r>0
such that the annulus
A(0,0.9r,1.1r)=B(1.1r)\backslash\bar{B}(0.9r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with
\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl\not=0
.
Writing
\Phi_{*}g=g
and
e=\Phi_{*}g-b
, we define the
C^{0}
local mass function of
g
with respect to
\varphi
and
\Phi
at
r
by

\displaystyle M_{C^{0}}(g,\Phi,\varphi,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl}\bigg[\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(e_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}-b^{ij}e_{ij})d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[(1+s^{2})\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}+\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(ns+(n-2)s^{-1})\right]b^{ij}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(s^{-1}-s)-(1+s^{2})\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}\right]s^{-2}x^{i}x^{j}e_{ij}d\mu_{b},\bigg]

where
x^{i}
denotes the coordinate component and

.

We obtain this
C^{0}
local mass function by integration by parts which can be seen in details in Section 2.
Since the Ricci-DeTurck flow exists for a short time with continuous initial metric which is close to the hyperbolic metric due to Simon’s theorem
[
14
]
, we can define the scalar curvature lower bound via the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow.

.

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth manifold and

be a

Riemannian metric on

on

for

satisfying Corollary

such that

\displaystyle g_{0}|_{\Phi(U_{x})}=\Phi_{*}g,

\displaystyle\inf_{C>0}\left(\liminf_{t\to 0}\left(\inf_{B(\Phi(x),Ct^{\beta})}R(g_{t})\right)\right)\geq\kappa.

We denote that
g
has scalar curvature bounded below by

in the
\beta
-weak sense at

as
R_{C^{0}_{\beta}}(g)(x)\geq\kappa
.
Then we use Koch-Lamm’s method to show that along the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow, the derivative of the metric can also be controlled.

.

Let

be a continuous metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Suppose that there exists

such that,

then there is a solution

to the normalized

-flow on

Moreover, there exist positive constants

(1.1)

, where

.

Finally, we find an inequality of the

local mass with different cutoff functions, which implies that the limit of the

local mass exists, and either finite or

.

.

Let

-flow as Theorem

.
Choose

, where

Suppose

, for

and for any

.
Let

be any smooth positive function with

and with nonzero integrals over
(0.9,1.1)

.
Then the limit

\lim_{r\to\infty}M_{C^{0}}(g,\varphi_{r^{-\eta}}(\cdot,t),r)

exists, and either finite or

. Furthermore,
the limit

[

]
is satisfied.

It should be noted that our approach is similar to that of Burkhardt-Guim
[
4
]
, but there are essential difficulties specific to asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds. The first challenge we encounter is the need to use the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow instead of the standard Ricci-DeTurck flow, as the normalized version preserves hyperbolicity. Since the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow is not scaling invariant, proving the almost monotonicity of the
C^{0}
local mass in section 5 becomes more intricate.

On the other hand, we assume
\tau>\frac{2n-1}{3}
rather than
\tau>\frac{n}{2}
. This is because some information is lost after mollification via the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow, as detailed in Section 5.
In contrast to the results by Chrusciel-Herzlich
[
6
]
and Gicquaud-Sakovich
[
7
]
, we cannot show that the limit in Definition
1.1
is independent of the choice of the coordinate chart at infinity
\Phi
and we can only define the mass function for
V^{0}
because of Lemma
2.6
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define the
C^{0}
local mass function and the
C^{2}
local mass function, and clarify their relationship.
In Section 3, we introduce the scalar curvature lower bounds in the
\beta
-weak sense for
C^{0}
metrics and establish the almost preservation of this lower bound under the normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow initiated from a continuous metric.
In Section 4, we prove the estimates of Koch-Lamm on
C^{0}
-asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, which yield precise short-time gradient estimates.
In Section 5, we demonstrate that the definition of the
C^{0}
local mass function is almost independent of the choice of the cutoff function and eventually show that the limit of the
C^{0}
local mass exists.

2.
C^{0}
and
C^{2}
local mass function

2.1.
C^{0}
local mass function

Let
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
denote the hyperbolic space of dimension
n\geq 3
:

\mathbb{H}^{n}=B_{1}(0),\quad\quad b=\rho^{-2}\delta,\quad\quad\rho(x)=\frac{1-|x|_{\delta}^{2}}{2},

where
B_{1}(0)
is the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\delta
denotes the Euclidean metric, and
x
is the coordinate in
B_{1}(0)
.
In
[
7
]
, Gicquaud-Sakovich defined the mass aspect function for metrics of
L^{\infty}\bigcap W^{1,2}
regularity and distributional scalar curvature on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, analogous to the asymptotically flat case introduced in
[
9
]
.

Throughout this paper, the Levi-Civita connection associated with the hyperbolic metric
b
will be denoted by
D
, while the Levi-Civita connection with metric
g
under consideration will be denoted by
\nabla
.
Moreover, norms and volume forms with a subscript
b
or
\delta
are computed with respect to the hyperbolic metric or Euclidean metric, respectively.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 2.1 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian
n
-manifold. Then
(M,g)
is said to be
W^{1,2}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic for
\tau>0
if there exist compact sets
K\subset M
and
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, a diffeomorphism
\Phi:M\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
and a constant
C>1
such that
C^{-1}b\leq\Phi_{*}g\leq Cb
and
e:=\Phi_{*}g-b
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}}\rho^{-2\tau}(|De|_{b}^{2}+|e|_{b}^{2})d\mu_{b}<\infty,

where
\Phi
is called a chart at infinity for
(M,g)
.

Now we consider the mass aspect function on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds with
C^{0}
metrics.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a complete smooth manifold and
g
be a
C^{0}
metric on
M
. Then
(M,g)
is said to be
C^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic for
\tau>0
if there exist compact subsets
K\subset M
and
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, a diffeomorphism
\Phi:M\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
and
e:=\Phi_{*}g-b
satisfies

|e|_{b}(x)=O(\rho(x)^{\tau})

for
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{N}:=\{V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})|Hess^{b}V=Vb\}
. This is an
(n+1)
-dimensional vector space with the basis given by

V^{0}=\frac{1+|x|_{\delta}^{2}}{1-|x|_{\delta}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\rho}-1,\quad\quad V^{i}=\frac{x^{i}}{\rho},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n,

where
x^{1},\cdots,x^{n}
are the coordinate functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
restricted to
S^{n-1}
.
There is a natural correspondence between functions in
\mathcal{N}
and isometries of Minkowski spacetime preserving the geometry of the hyperboloid
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Proposition 4.1 in
[
7
]
)
.

If
(M,g)
is
W^{1,2}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic,
e\in C^{1}_{\tau}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
R_{g}+n(n-1)\in L^{1}_{1}(M)
, where
R_{g}
is the distributional scalar curvature of
g
and
\tau>\frac{n}{2}
, then, for any
V\in\mathcal{N}
, the mass aspect function is defined by

p(e,V)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{r}(0)}[V(div_{b}e-dtr_{b}e)+tr_{b}edV-e(DV,\cdot)]\nu_{r}d\mu_{b},

where
S_{r}(0)
denotes the hyperbolic geodesic sphere in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\nu_{r}
is the outward unit normal of
S_{r}(0)
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
In particular, we will refer to
p(e,V^{0})
as the mass function.

By the coordinates given by
\Phi
, we can write the mass aspect function as

(2.1)

\begin{split}p(e,V)=&\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{r}(0)}[V(D_{k}e_{ij}b^{jk}\nu_{r}^{i}-b^{ij}D_{k}e_{ij}\nu_{r}^{k})+b^{ij}e_{ij}D_{k}V\nu_{r}^{k}-e_{ij}D^{i}V\nu^{j}_{r}]d\mu_{b}\\
=&\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{r}(0)}[V(b^{jk}\nu_{r}^{i}-b^{ij}\nu_{r}^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}+(b^{ij}\nu_{r}^{k}-b^{jk}\nu_{r}^{i})e_{ij}D_{k}V]d\mu_{b},\end{split}

which coincides with Chrusciel-Herzlich’s definition
[
6
]
.
We use
S(l)
to represent the hyperbolic geodesic sphere of radius
l
and
B(l)
to denote the hyperbolic open ball of radius
l
centered at the origin.
We see from (
2.1
) that the first part of the integrand requires the first derivative of
e
.
Therefore, to define the mass for
e\in C^{0}_{\tau}
, we need a smooth test function.
The definition of the mass of
e\in C^{0}_{\tau}
is based on the following fact.

Let
(M,g)
be a
C^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with chart at infinity
\Phi:M\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
for some compact subsets
K\subset M
and
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Let
r>0
such that the annulus
A(0,0.9r,1.1r)=B(1.1r)\backslash\bar{B}(0.9r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
.
If
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function with
\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl\not=0
, then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 13.00005pt\frac{\int_{0.9r}^{1.1r}\varphi(\frac{l}{r})\int_{S(l)}[V(b^{jk}\nu^{i}-b^{ij}\nu^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}+(b^{ij}\nu^{k}-b^{jk}\nu^{i})e_{ij}D_{k}V]d\mu_{b}dl}{r\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(s)\int_{S(rs)}[V(b^{jk}\nu^{i}-b^{ij}\nu^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}+(b^{ij}\nu^{k}-b^{jk}\nu^{i})e_{ij}D_{k}V]d\mu_{b}ds}{\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(s)ds}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{r\to\infty}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S(r)}[V(b^{jk}\nu^{i}-b^{ij}\nu^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}+(b^{ij}\nu^{k}-b^{jk}\nu^{i})e_{ij}D_{k}V]d\mu_{b}.

We can calculate the numerator of (
2.2
) with
V=V^{0}
by integration by parts.
By definition, the unit normal vector of
S(s)
is
\nu_{s}=\frac{x}{s}
and
s=|x|_{b}=\frac{|x|_{\delta}}{\rho}=\frac{2|x|_{\delta}}{1-|x|_{\delta}^{2}}
. Then we compute that

\displaystyle\int_{0.9r}^{1.1r}\varphi\left(\frac{l}{r}\right)\int_{S(l)}V^{0}(b^{jk}\nu^{i}-b^{ij}\nu^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}dl

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)D_{k}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}\nu_{k}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}D_{k}\left[V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)\right]e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II,

where

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}\nu_{k}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(e_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}-b^{ij}e_{ij})d\mu_{b},

and

\displaystyle II=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}D_{k}\left[V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)\right]e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}D_{k}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}D_{k}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)D_{k}\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x).

By computing directly, there holds

D_{k}s=\partial_{k}\left(\frac{|x|_{\delta}}{\rho}\right)=\rho^{-2}|x|_{\delta}x^{k}+\rho^{-1}|x|_{\delta}^{-1}x^{k}=\rho^{-2}s^{-1}V^{0}x^{k},

which implies that

D_{k}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)=\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\frac{V^{0}x^{k}}{r\rho^{2}s}.

Then

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}D_{k}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}(V^{0})^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}s^{-2}\left(e_{ij}x^{i}x^{j}-|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij}e_{ij}\right)d\mu_{b}(x).

Since
b=\rho^{-2}\delta
, the Christoffel symbol of
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
is

\Gamma_{kj}^{l}=\rho^{-1}(x^{j}\delta_{kl}+x^{k}\delta_{jl}-x^{l}\delta_{kj}).

We have that

\displaystyle D_{k}(x^{j}\partial_{j})=\partial_{k}+x^{j}D_{k}\partial_{j}=\partial_{k}+x^{j}\Gamma_{kj}^{l}\partial_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{k}+x^{j}\rho^{-1}(x^{j}\delta_{kl}+x^{k}\delta_{jl}-x^{l}\delta_{kj})\partial_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{k}+\rho^{-1}|x|_{\delta}^{2}\partial_{k}=(\rho^{-1}-1)\partial_{k}.

So

D_{k}x^{i}=(\rho^{-1}-1)\delta_{ki}=V^{0}\delta_{ki}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle D_{k}\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{jk}V^{0}s^{-1}\delta_{ik}-nb^{ij}V^{0}s^{-1}-b^{jk}s^{-2}x^{i}\partial_{k}s+b^{ij}s^{-2}x^{k}\partial_{k}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2-n)s^{-1}V^{0}b^{ij}-s^{-3}V^{0}x^{i}x^{j},

and

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)D_{k}\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)[(2-n)s^{-1}V^{0}b^{ij}-s^{-3}V^{0}x^{i}x^{j}]e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}(V^{0})^{2}s^{-3}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)[(2-n)|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij}-x^{i}x^{j}]e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x).

By the fact that

D_{k}V^{0}=\partial_{k}(\rho^{-1}-1)=\rho^{-2}x^{k},

we have

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}D_{k}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)s^{-1}(x^{i}x^{j}-|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij})e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0.9r}^{1.1r}\varphi\left(\frac{l}{r}\right)\int_{S(l)}[V^{0}(b^{jk}\nu^{i}-b^{ij}\nu^{k})D_{k}e_{ij}+(b^{ij}\nu^{k}-b^{jk}\nu^{i})e_{ij}D_{k}V^{0}]d\mu_{b}dl

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}-b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}\right)D_{k}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\left(b^{ij}\frac{x^{k}}{s}-b^{jk}\frac{x^{i}}{s}\right)e_{ij}D_{k}V^{0}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(e_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}-b^{ij}e_{ij})d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}(V^{0})^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}s^{-2}\left(e_{ij}x^{i}x^{j}-|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij}e_{ij}\right)d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle-\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}(V^{0})^{2}s^{-3}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)[(2-n)|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij}-x^{i}x^{j}]e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle-2\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)s^{-1}(x^{i}x^{j}-|x|_{\delta}^{2}\delta^{ij})e_{ij}d\mu_{b}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}V^{0}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(e_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}-b^{ij}e_{ij})d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[(V^{0})^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}-(2-n)(V^{0})^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)s^{-1}+2s\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\right]b^{ij}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[(V^{0})^{2}s^{-1}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)-2s\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)-(V^{0})^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}\right]s^{-2}x^{i}x^{j}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}.

Since

|x|_{\delta}=\frac{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}-1}{s},\quad\quad V^{0}=\sqrt{1+s^{2}},

we now arrive at the following definition of
C^{0}
local mass function.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
C^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with a chart at infinity
\Phi:M\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
for some compact subsets
K\subset M
and
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\tau>0
.
Let
r>0
such that the annulus
A(0,0.9r,1.1r)=B(1.1r)\backslash\bar{B}(0.9r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash K^{\prime}
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with
\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl\not=0
.
Writing
\Phi_{*}g=g
and

, we define the

local mass function of

with respect to

and
\Phi
at

\displaystyle M_{C^{0}}(g,\Phi,\varphi,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r\int_{0.9}^{1.1}\varphi(l)dl}\bigg[\int_{\partial A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(e_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}-b^{ij}e_{ij})d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[(1+s^{2})\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}+\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(ns+(n-2)s^{-1})\right]b^{ij}e_{ij}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,0.9r,1.1r)}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)(s^{-1}-s)-(1+s^{2})\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)r^{-1}\right]s^{-2}x^{i}x^{j}e_{ij}d\mu_{b},\bigg]

where

.

In particular, if

and the mass aspect function

exists, by the above process, (

) implies that

2.2.

Definition 2.5
.

If

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
g
is a
C^{2}
Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(r_{0})
. Let
e=g-b
. For
r_{0}<r_{1}<r_{2}
and
V\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{2},V)-M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{1},V)=\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[V(R_{g}+n(n-1))+Q]d\mu_{b},

where
Q=Q(V,dV,e,De,D^{2}e)
satisfies
|Q|\lesssim(|V|+|dV|)(|De|^{2}+|e|^{2})+|V||e||D^{2}e|
and norms are computed by
b
.

Proof.

By proposition 2.2 in
[
7
]
, the first order variation of the scalar curvature near the hyperbolic metric

is

R_{g}=-n(n-1)+(n-1)tr_{b}e+D_{i}[g^{jl}g^{ik}(D_{j}e_{kl}-D_{k}e_{jl})]+Q(e,De),

where
|Q(e,De)|\lesssim|e|^{2}+|De|^{2}
.
We have by integration by parts that

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}V[R_{g}+n(n-1)]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}V\left[(n-1)tr_{b}e+D_{i}(g^{jl}g^{ik}(D_{j}e_{kl}-D_{k}e_{jl}))+Q(e,De)\right]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[V(n-1)tr_{b}e+D_{i}(V(g^{jl}g^{ik}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}e_{kl})-D_{i}V(g^{jl}g^{ik}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}e_{kl}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+VQ(e,De)]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}\big[V(n-1)tr_{b}e+D_{i}(V(g^{jl}g^{ik}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}e_{kl})-D_{j}(D_{i}V(g^{jl}g^{ik}-g^{ij}g^{kl})e_{kl})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+D_{j}(D_{i}V(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl}))e_{kl}+VQ(e,De)\big]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[(n-1)Vtr_{b}e+D_{j}D_{i}V(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})e_{kl}+D_{i}VD_{j}(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})e_{kl}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+VQ(e,De)]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle+\int_{S(r_{2})}[(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})VD_{j}e_{kl}-(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}Ve_{kl}]\nu_{i}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle-\int_{S(r_{1})}[(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})VD_{j}e_{kl}-(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}Ve_{kl}]\nu_{i}d\mu_{b}.

Since
|b^{jl}b^{ik}-g^{jl}g^{ik}|\lesssim|e|
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{S(r_{2})}[(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})VD_{j}e_{kl}-(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})D_{j}Ve_{kl}]\nu_{i}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S(r_{2})}[(b^{ik}b^{jl}-b^{ij}b^{kl})(VD_{j}e_{kl}-D_{j}Ve_{kl})\nu_{i}+Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)\nu_{i}]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S(r_{2})}[(b^{jl}\nu^{k}-b^{kl}\nu^{j})(VD_{j}e_{kl}-D_{j}Ve_{kl})+Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)\nu_{i}]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{2},V)+\int_{S(r_{2})}Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)\nu_{i}d\mu_{b},

where
|Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)|\lesssim|V||e||De|+|dV||e|^{2}
.
By the fact that
HessV=Vb
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[(n-1)Vtr_{b}e+D_{j}D_{i}V(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})e_{kl}+D_{i}VD_{j}(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})e_{kl}]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}Q_{2}(e,dV,De)d\mu_{b},

where
|Q_{2}(e,dV,De)|\lesssim|V||e|^{2}+|dV||De||e|
.
Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}V[R_{g}+n(n-1)]d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[Q_{2}(e,dV,De)+VQ(e,De)]d\mu_{b}+M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{2},V)-M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{1},V)

\displaystyle+\int_{S(r_{2})}Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)\nu_{i}d\mu_{b}-\int_{S(r_{1})}Q_{1}(V,dV,e,De)\nu_{i}d\mu_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}[Q_{2}(e,dV,De)+VQ(e,De)]d\mu_{b}+M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{2},V)-M_{C^{2}}(g,r_{1},V)

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,r_{1},r_{2})}D_{i}Q_{1}d\mu_{b}.

Since

|Q_{2}(e,dV,De)+VQ(e,De)|\lesssim(|V|+|dV|)(|De|^{2}+|e|^{2}),

|DQ_{1}(V,dV,e,De)|\lesssim(|V|+|dV|)(|De|^{2}+|e|^{2})+|V||e||D^{2}e|,

we arrive at the result.
∎

3.
Scalar curvature lower bound for
C^{0}
metrics

3.1.
Ricci-DeTurck flow from
C^{0}
metrics

We now aim to define the scalar curvature lower bound for
C^{0}
metrics, which we accomplish by leveraging the Ricci-DeTurck flow.
To regularize continuous metrics, we appeal to a result by Simon
[
14
]
.
We begin with the Ricci-DeTurck flow associated with a background metric
h
;
following
[
14
]
, we refer to this as the
h
-flow to highlight its dependence on
h
.

Let
(M,h)
be a complete smooth Riemannian manifold such that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
k_{i}>0
satisfying

(3.1)

|\tilde{\nabla}^{i}Rm(h)|\leq k_{i},

where
\tilde{\nabla}
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to
h
.
A smooth family of metrics
g(t)
on
M\times(0,T]
is said to be a solution to the
h
-flow if it satisfies

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}g_{ij}=-2R_{ij}+\nabla_{i}W_{j}+\nabla_{j}W_{i},\\
W^{k}=g^{pq}\left(\Gamma_{pq}^{k}(g)-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{pq}^{k}(h)\right),\end{array}\right.

where
R_{ij}
denotes the Ricci tensor of
g(t)
,
\Gamma(g)
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}(h)
denote the Christoffel symbols of
g
and
h
, respectively.
If the initial metric
g_{0}
is smooth, the Ricci flow is equivalent to the Ricci-DeTurck flow.
Let
\chi_{t}
be the diffeomorphism defined by

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\chi_{t}(x)&=-W\left(\chi_{t}(x),t\right);\\
\chi_{0}(x)&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

Then the pullback of the Ricci-DeTurck flow
\hat{g}(t)=\chi_{t}^{*}g(t)
satisfies the Ricci flow

\partial_{t}\hat{g}=-2Ric(\hat{g}),

with initial condition
\hat{g}(0)=g(0)=g_{0}
.
For
\epsilon>0
, a continuous metric
g
is said to be
(1+\epsilon)
-close to
h
if

(1+\epsilon)^{-1}h\leq g\leq(1+\epsilon)h.

In
[
14
]
, Simon proved the following result for continuous metrics.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 5.2 in
[
14
]
)
.

There is
\epsilon(n)>0
such that the following is true:
Let
(M,h)
be a complete manifold satisfying (
3.1
).
If
g_{0}
is a continuous metric on
M
such that
g_{0}
is
(1+\epsilon(n))
-close to
h
, then the
h
-flow (
3.2
) admits a smooth solution
g(t)
on
M\times(0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}(n,k_{0})>0
such that

(1)

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{\Omega}|g(t)-g_{0}|=0,\quad\forall\Omega\subset\subset M;

(2)

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
C_{i}>0
depending only on
n,k_{0},\cdots,k_{i}
so that

(3.4)

\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{i}g(t)|\leq\frac{C_{i}}{t^{i/2}};

(3)

g(t)
is
(1+2\epsilon(n))
-close to
h
for all
t\in(0,T_{0}]
,

where the norm and connection are with respect to
h
.

3.2.
Heat kernel estimates under the Ricci-DeTurck flow

Given a solution
g(t)
to the
h
-flow (
3.2
) on a smooth manifold
M
, let
\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)
denote the heat kernel associated with the Ricci flow solution
\hat{g}(t)=\chi_{t}^{*}g(t)
. That is, for a fixed point
(y,s)\in M\times I
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)=\Delta_{\hat{g}_{t},x}\hat{K}(x,t;y,s),

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s}\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{y},

and for all
s<t
,

\int_{M}\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)d\mu_{s}(y)=1.

The push-forward of
\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)
under the diffeomorphism
\chi_{t}
in (
3.3
) is

K(x,t;y,s)=\hat{K}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),t;\chi^{-1}_{s}(y),s),

and it serves as the heat kernel for the
h
-flow. It satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K(x,t;y,s)=\Delta_{{g}_{t},x}{K}(x,t;y,s)+\langle W,\nabla_{x}K(x,t;y,s)\rangle,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s}{K}(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{y},

and for all
s<t
,

\int_{M}{K}(x,t;y,s)d\mu_{s}(y)=1.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
g(t)
is a solution to (
3.2
) on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with background metric
h=b
satisfying
|g(t)-b|_{b}<\epsilon
and Theorem
3.1
.
Let
K(x,t;y,s)
be the heat kernel of the
b
-flow.
Then there exist constants
C=C(n,\epsilon)<\infty
,
D=D(n,\epsilon)<\infty
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
t>0
sufficiently small and
\frac{t}{2}\leq s<t
,

K(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|_{b}^{2}}{D(t-s)}\right)

and for any
r>0

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(x,r)}K(x,t;y,s)d\mu_{s}(y)<C\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{D(t-s)}\right),

where
B(x,r)
is the geodesic ball centered at
x
with radius
r
in
(\mathbb{H}^{n},b)
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)
be the heat kernel for the Ricci flow solution
\hat{g}(t)=\chi_{t}^{*}g(t)
.
By Lemma 2.4 of
[
3
]
, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
\frac{t}{2}\leq s<t
, there exist constants
C=C(n,\epsilon)<\infty
,
D=D(n)<\infty
, such that

\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}(x,y)}{D(t-s)}\right)\text{vol}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}\left(x,\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right)\text{vol}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}\left(y,\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right).

Pushing forward
\hat{K}(x,t;y,s)
by
\chi
, we get

(3.5)

\begin{split}&K(x,t;y,s)=\hat{K}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),t;\chi^{-1}_{s}(y),s)\\
\leq&C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y))}{D(t-s)}\right)\text{vol}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}\left(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right)\\
&\times\text{vol}^{-\frac{1}{2}}B_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}\left(\chi^{-1}_{s}(y),\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right).\end{split}

By the Jensen inequality, there holds

d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y))\geq\frac{1}{2}d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{t}(y))-d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(y),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y)).

Since by (
3.4
), we have

|W|(t)\lesssim|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(t)<\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}},

which implies that for any
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
,

d_{\hat{g}(t_{1})}(\chi_{t_{1}}(x),\chi_{t_{2}}(x))\leq C(\sqrt{t_{2}}-\sqrt{t_{1}}).

By (3) of Theorem
3.1
, we see that

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y))}{D(t-s)}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y))}{D(t-s)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\chi^{-1}_{t}(y))}{2D(t-s)}\right)\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}_{\hat{g}(t)}(\chi^{-1}_{t}(y),\chi^{-1}_{s}(y))}{D(t-s)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{g(t)}(x,y)}{2D(t-s)}\right)\exp\left(\frac{C(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{s})^{2}}{D(t-s)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{b}(x,y)}{D(t-s)}\right).

Since
g(t)
is
(1+2\epsilon)
-close to
b
, the volume can be estimated by

\text{vol}B_{\hat{g}(\frac{t}{2})}\left(\chi^{-1}_{t}(x),\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right)\leq C\text{vol}B\left(x,\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}\right)\leq C(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}},

where in the last inequality we require
(t-s)
sufficiently small.
Therefore, we obtain

K(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|_{b}^{2}}{D(t-s)}\right).

Integrating the above inequality over
\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(x,r)
for any
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and any
r>0
with respect to
y
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(x,r)}K(x,t;y,s)d\mu_{s}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(x,r)}(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|_{b}^{2}}{D(t-s)}\right)d\mu_{b}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(0,\frac{r}{\sqrt{t-s}})}\exp\left(-\frac{|z|_{b}^{2}}{D}\right)d\mu_{b}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash B(0,\frac{r}{\sqrt{t-s}})}\exp\left(-\frac{|z|_{b}^{2}}{2D}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{|z|_{b}^{2}}{2D}\right)d\mu_{b}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2D(t-s)}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2D}\right)\sinh^{n-1}\tau d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2D(t-s)}\right),

where in the third line we change the variable by
y=x+\sqrt{t-s}z
.

∎

3.3.
Normalized Ricci-DeTurck flow

In contrast to asymptotically flat manifolds, the hyperbolic metric expands under the Ricci flow. We therefore resort to the normalized Ricci flow, which is equivalent to the standard Ricci flow up to a time reparametrization.
The normalized Ricci flow consists of a family of metrics
g(t)
on an
n
-manifold
M
evolving by

\partial_{t}g(t)=-2Ric(g(t))-2(n-1)g(t),

with initial condition
g(0)=g_{0}
.
Via a time-dependent diffeomorphism
\chi_{t}
of the form (
3.3
), we derive the normalized
h
-flow with respect to a smooth background metric
h
:

(3.6)

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=-2Ric(g(t))_{ij}-2(n-1)g_{ij}+\nabla_{i}W_{j}+\nabla_{j}W_{i}.

In what follows, we take the background metric to be
b
(the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
) and refer to the flow
g(t)
in (
3.6
) as the normalized
b
-flow.
It is traightforward to verify that

\bar{g}(t)=(1+2(n-1)t)g(\bar{t})=e^{2(n-1)\bar{t}}g(\bar{t}),

where
\bar{t}=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\ln{(1+2(n-1)t)}
, satisfies the
b
-flow equation (
3.2
).
By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following corollary for the normalized
b
-flow initiated from a continuous metric.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
g_{0}
is a continuous metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
g_{0}
is
(1+\epsilon(n))
-close to
b
, then the normalized
b
-flow (
3.6
) admits a smooth solution on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times(0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}(n)>0
such that

(1)

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{\Omega}|e^{2(n-1)t}g(t)-g_{0}|=0,\quad\forall\Omega\subset\subset M;

(2)

For all

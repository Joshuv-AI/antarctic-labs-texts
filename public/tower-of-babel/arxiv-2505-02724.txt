Abstract
. We introduce the relative Matsui spectrum, a new invariant associated with a stable ∞ \infty -category equipped with an action. This construction generalizes both Balmer’s tensor triangular spectra and Matsui’s triangular spectra, and provides a unified framework for classifying thick submodules. We establish its fundamental properties, including universality, comparison with existing spectra, and descent, and construct a natural morphism to the Balmer spectrum of the base. Applications show that the relative Matsui spectrum recovers the underlying classical geometric spaces from categorical data in various settings: categories of perfect complexes of schemes, twisted derived categories, categories of singularities, and derived matrix factorization categories. Thus the relative Matsui spectrum extends the reach of tensor triangular geometry beyond globally tensorial settings, while preserving geometric intuition.

1.
Introduction

The study of spectra associated with triangulated or stable
\infty
-categories has played a central role in bridging abstract categorical structures with underlying geometry.
Balmer
[
Bal05
]
introduced the tensor triangular spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(T)
of a tensor triangulated category
T
, thereby providing a powerful framework for classifying thick tensor ideals.
When
T=\mathrm{Perf}(X)
for a topologically noetherian scheme
X
, the spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(T)
recovers the scheme
X
together with its structure sheaf.
More recently, Matsui proposed a variant of the spectrum that applies to triangulated categories without tensor structures, thereby expanding the range of situations where spectra can be defined.
It has since been further studied by Hirano–Ouchi
[
HO22
,
HO24
]
, Hirano–Kalck–Ouchi
[
HKO24
]
, Ito
[
Ito23
]
, and Ito–Matsui
[
IM24
]
.

In this paper, we develop a relative version of the Matsui spectrum, which we call the
relative Matsui spectrum
.
Given a stable
\infty
-category
D
with an action of a stable
\infty
-category
S
, we construct a locally ringed space

\mathrm{Spec}(D,S),

which simultaneously generalizes Balmer’s and Matsui’s construction.
This spectrum is the universal space classifying thick
S
-submodules of
D
and admits a canonical morphism to the Balmer spectrum of the base
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(S)
.

We establish fundamental properties of the relative Matsui spectrum, including universality, comparison with existing spectra, and descent.
In particular, we show that, under suitable hypotheses, the relative Matsui spectrum recovers classical geometric objects.
For example:

(1)

For a rigid tensor triangulated
\infty
-category
T
, taking
D=S=T
recovers Balmer spectrum.

(2)

For a stable
\infty
-category
D
with
S=\mathrm{Perf}(\mathbb{Z})
or
\mathrm{Sp}^{\omega}
, it recovers Matsui spectrum.

(3)

For a flat morphism of schemes
X\to Y
with
Y
regular, the category
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
admits a natural
\mathrm{Perf}(Y)
-action, and the spectrum is a locally ringed space over
Y
, fiberwise given by the Matsui spectrum of
\mathrm{Perf}(X_{y})
.
Moreover, any relatively derived equivalent scheme appears as an open subscheme of the spectrum.

(4)

For twisted derived categories
\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu)
with canonical
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
-action, the spectrum is canonically isomorphic to
X
.

(5)

For a locally complete intersection scheme
X
, the derived category of coherent sheaves
{\mathrm{Coh}}(X)
admits a natural
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
-action, and its spectrum is a space over
X
.
This spectrum can be described explicitly.
For instance, the Krull dimension of each fiber coincides with the embedding codimension at the corresponding point.
Submodules of
{\mathrm{Coh}}(X)
correspond bijectively to specialization-closed subsets of the spectrum.

(6)

For the category of singularities
\mathrm{Sing}(X)
with its
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
-action, if
X
is locally a hypersurface, the spectrum recovers the singular locus of
X
.

(7)

For derived matrix factorization categories
\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,W)
with canonical
\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,0)
-action, the spectrum recovers the relative singular locus, extending results of Hirano.

(8)

For a Severi–Brauer scheme
X\to Y
, the spectrum encodes both
X
and additional copies of the base
Y
, reflecting semi-orthogonal decompositions.
When the relative dimension is one, we describe the spectrum explicitly and classify its submodules.

Thus the relative Matsui spectrum extends the reach of tensor triangular geometry to settings without a global tensor product, while preserving geometric intuition through the classification of subcategories.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we construct the relative Matsui spectrum.
In Section
3
, we establish its universality.
In Section
4
, we study its basic properties, including classification of submodules and comparison with existing spectra.
In Section
5
, we recall some fundamental properties of commutativity of localizations for later use.
In Section
6
, we construct a morphism from the relative Matsui spectrum to the spectrum of the base and establish a fiberwise decomposition.
In Section
7
, we apply the theory to concrete examples: categories of perfect complexes of schemes, twisted derived categories, derived category of complexes with finitely generated homology over Koszul algebras, categories of singularities, derived categories of coherent sheaves, and derived matrix factorization categories.

2.
Construction

In this section, we construct our spaces.
Starting from a triangulated category together with a family of thick subcategories, we define
S
-primes and quasi-
S
-primes and endow their sets with natural topologies.

Let
T
be a triangulated category, and let
S
be a set of thick subcategories of
T
.
Assume that for any transfinite sequence of thick subcategories
I_{1}\subset I_{2}\subset\cdots\in S
, the union
\bigcup_{i}I_{i}
belongs to
S
.

Definition 2.1
.

In the above setting, we define:

(1)

A thick subcategory
P\in S
is called a
quasi-
S
-prime
if for any
I_{1},I_{2}\in S
with
I_{1},I_{2}\supsetneq P
, we have
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\supsetneq P
.

(2)

A thick subcategory
P\in S
is called an
S
-prime
if for any family
\{I_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset S
with
I_{i}\supsetneq P
, we have
\bigcap_{i}I_{i}\supsetneq P
.

For an
S
-prime
P
, let
\overline{P}
denote the intersection
\bigcap_{I\in S,\ I\supsetneq P}I
.
If
S
is closed under intersections, then
\overline{P}
is the smallest element of
S
strictly containing
P
.

Let
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)
denote the set of
S
-primes, and
\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(T,S)
the set of quasi-
S
-primes.
We define a topology on
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)
and
\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(T,S)
.
For a subset
W\subset T
, the support of
W
in
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)
is defined as

\mathrm{Supp}(W):=\{P\in\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)\mid W\not\subset P\}.

The family of closed subsets of
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)
is generated by
\{\mathrm{Supp}(a)\mid a\in T\}
.
Similarly for
\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(T,S)
.
There is a natural dense embedding
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(T,S)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
P
and
Q
be
S
-primes.
Then
Q\in\overline{\{P\}}
if and only if
Q\subset P
.
In particular, the space
\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
is a
T_{0}
-space.
Moreover, for any
W\subset T
, we have

\mathrm{Supp}(W)=\mathrm{Supp}(\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S,W\subset I\end{subarray}}I)
, and this set is specialization-closed.

There are naturally two presheaves of triangulated categories on
\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)
and
\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(T,S)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle:U\mapsto\{t\in T\mid\mathrm{Supp}(t)\cap U=\emptyset\}=:I_{U},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:U\mapsto\mathrm{Idem}(T/I_{U})=:T_{U},

where
\mathrm{Idem}
denotes the idempotent completion and
T/I_{U}
the Verdier quotient.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I\in S
and
a\in T\setminus I
.
Then there exists an
S
-prime
P\supset I
such that
a\notin P
.

Proof.

By Zorn’s lemma, the set
\{J\in S\mid I\subset J,\ a\notin J\}
has a maximal element
P
with respect to inclusion.
Then
a\in\bigcap_{I\in S,\ I\supsetneq P}I
, so
P
is an
S
-prime.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The following map is injective:

\mathrm{Supp}:S\longrightarrow 2^{\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)}.

If
S
is closed under intersections, then its left inverse is given by

\mathrm{Spc}(T,S)\supset Z\longmapsto\{a\in T\mid\mathrm{Supp}(a)\subset Z\}.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

.

3.
Universality

In this section, we give a universal criterion for our spaces.
Matsui
[
Mat21
]
provided a characterization of the Matsui spectrum, but our result is slightly different and more general.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(D,S)
be a datum.
A
support datum
of
(D,S)
is a topological
T_{0}
-space
X
equipped with a map
\mathrm{Supp}:\mathrm{Ob}(D)\to 2^{X}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(shift invariance)
\mathrm{Supp}(a)=\mathrm{Supp}(a[1])
for any
a\in D
.

(2)

(action) For any
a\in D
,
s\in S
, we have
\mathrm{Supp}(s\otimes a)\subset\mathrm{Supp}(a)
.

(3)

(direct sums)
\mathrm{Supp}(a\oplus b)=\mathrm{Supp}(a)\cup\mathrm{Supp}(b)
for any
a,b\in D
.

(4)

(exactness)
\mathrm{Supp}(b)\subset\mathrm{Supp}(a)\cup\mathrm{Supp}(c)
for any exact triangle
a\to b\to c
in
D
.

(5)

(distinguishability) For any two distinct submodules
I,J\subset D
, we have
\bigcup_{a\in I}\mathrm{Supp}(a)\neq\bigcup_{b\in J}\mathrm{Supp}(b)
.

(6)

(topology) The family of closed subsets of
X
is generated by
\{\mathrm{Supp}(a)\mid a\in D\}
.

Let
X,Y
be support data of
(D,S)
.
A
map of support data
is a continuous map
f:X\to Y
compatible with
\mathrm{Supp}
, i.e.,
f^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(a))=\mathrm{Supp}_{X}(a)
for all
a\in D
.

A support datum
X
of
(D,S)
is called
universal
if for any support datum
Y
, there exists a unique map
X\to Y
of support data.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(D,S)
be a datum.
Then the relative Matsui spectrum
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)
is the universal support datum.

For a triangulated category
T
, a topological
T_{0}
-space
X
with an assignment
\mathrm{Supp}:T\to X
is called a support datum of
T
if it satisfies the above conditions except (2).
Our conditions are stronger than Matsui’s original definition
[
Mat21
]
, where (5), (6), and the
T_{0}
-condition are not imposed.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be a triangulated category.
Then the Matsui spectrum
\mathrm{Spc}_{\triangle}(T)
is a universal support datum of
T
.

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
3.2
.

We now prove this fact in a more general setting of semilattices.

A
semilattice
is a partially ordered set such that any two elements admit a supremum.
A map of semilattices is a map preserving finite suprema.
A semilattice is called
ind-complete
if any non-empty subset admits a supremum.

Let
L
be a semilattice.
A subset
S\subset L
is called an
ind-object
if it is closed under taking suprema of two elements, is non-empty, and satisfies: if
s\in S
and
l\leq s
then
l\in S
.
The
ind-completion

\mathrm{Ind}(L)
is the set of all ind-objects.
It is partially ordered by inclusion and forms an ind-complete semilattice.
For any ind-complete semilattice
L^{\prime}
and a semilattice map
f:L\to L^{\prime}
, there exists a unique extension
\tilde{f}:\mathrm{Ind}(L)\to L^{\prime}
preserving arbitrary suprema.
A preordered set may naturally be regarded as a category.
It is easy to check that
\mathrm{Ind}(L)
, together with the natural morphism
L\to\mathrm{Ind}(L):l\mapsto\{l^{\prime}\in L\mid l^{\prime}\leq l\}
, coincides with the categorical ind-completion.

For a set
X
,
2^{X}
is partially ordered by inclusion, and is an ind-complete semilattice.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
L
be a semilattice.
A
support datum
of
L
is a topological
T_{0}
-space
X
with a map of semilattices
\mathrm{Supp}:L\to 2^{X}
satisfying:

(1)

(distinguishability) The induced map
\mathrm{Supp}:\mathrm{Ind}(L)\to 2^{X}
is injective.

(2)

(topology) The family of closed subsets of
X
is generated by
\{\mathrm{Supp}(l)\mid l\in L\}
.

Let
X,Y
be support data of
L
.
A
map of support data
is a continuous map
f:X\to Y
such that
\mathrm{Supp}_{X}(l)=f^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(l))
for any
l\in L
.

A support datum
X
of
L
is called
universal
if for any support datum
Y
of
L
, there exists a unique map
X\to Y
of support data.

Let
L
be a semilattice.
We set

\displaystyle X_{L}

\displaystyle=\{P\in\mathrm{Ind}(L)\mid\bigcap_{S\in\mathrm{Ind}(L),S\supsetneq P}S\supsetneq P\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(l)

\displaystyle=\{S\in\mathrm{Ind}(L)\mid l\notin S\}\quad(l\in L).

We topologize
X_{L}
by declaring the family of closed subsets to be generated by
\{\mathrm{Supp}(l)\mid l\in L\}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

X_{L}
is the universal support datum of
L
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
X_{L}
is a support datum.
Take
S\in\mathrm{Ind}(L)
and
l\in L\setminus S
.
The set
\{T\in\mathrm{Ind}(L)\mid S\subset T,\,l\notin T\}
has a maximal element
P
by Zorn’s lemma.
Since
l\in\bigcap_{T\in\mathrm{Ind}(L),\,T\supsetneq P}T
, it follows that
P\in X_{L}
.
This establishes the injectivity of
\mathrm{Supp}:\mathrm{Ind}(L)\to 2^{X_{L}}
.
The remaining conditions are straightforward.

Next, we prove universality.
Let
Y=(Y,\mathrm{Supp}_{Y})
be another support datum.
For
P\in X_{L}
, let
\overline{P}=\bigcap_{S\in\mathrm{Ind}(L),\,S\supsetneq P}S
.
By definition,
\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(\overline{P})\setminus\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(P)\neq\emptyset
.
Choose a point
y_{P}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(\overline{P})\setminus\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(P)
.
Define
f:X_{L}\to Y
by
P\mapsto y_{P}
.
If
l\notin P
, then
\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(l)\cup\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(P)=\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(\sup\{P,l\})=\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(\overline{P})
.
Thus
f^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(l))=\{P\in X_{L}\mid l\notin P\}
, hence
f
is a map of support data.
Since any map of support data is compatible with the natural injection
Y\to 2^{L}:y\mapsto\{l\in L\mid y\notin\mathrm{Supp}_{Y}(l)\}
, uniqueness follows.
∎

In fact, isomorphism classes of support data of
L
correspond bijectively to subsets of
\mathrm{Ind}(L)\cup\{\emptyset\}
containing
X_{L}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
be the semilattice of finitely generated submodules of
D
ordered by inclusion.
Then
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D))
is isomorphic to the semilattice
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(D)
, and
X_{\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)}
is the set of prime thick subcategories.
Thus
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)
is the universal support datum of
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
.

For a support datum
Y
of
(D,S)
, the map

\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)\to 2^{Y}:I\mapsto\bigcup_{a\in I}\mathrm{Supp}(a)
makes
Y
a support datum of
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
.
Conversely, for a support datum
Y^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
, the map
\mathrm{Ob}(D)\to 2^{Y^{\prime}}:a\mapsto\mathrm{Supp}({\left\langle{a}\right\rangle})
makes
Y^{\prime}
a support datum of
(D,S)
.
These constructions are mutually inverse, so the categories of support data of
(D,S)
and of
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}^{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
are equivalent.
This proves that
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)
is the universal support datum of
(D,S)
.
∎

Example 3.6
.

For a tensor triangulated category
T
, let
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\otimes,fin}(T)
be the semilattice of radicals of finitely generated tensor ideals of
T
.
Then the Balmer spectrum
\mathrm{Spc}_{\otimes}T
is a support datum of
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\otimes,fin}(T)
, but in general it is not universal in our sense.
If

\mathrm{Ideal}_{\otimes,fin}(T)
coincides with the semilattice of finitely generated submodules of

.

4.
Fundamental Results

In this section, we present several fundamental results on relative Matsui spectra, including the classification of submodules and comparisons with the Balmer and Matsui spectra.
The classification result in Theorem
4.3

Remark 4.6
.

Matsui and Takahashi
[

, Theorem 4.5]
,
[

proved that the Matsui spectrum

, together with the support map, classifies radical thick subcategories of a triangulated category

In the same manner as above, we can show that every thick subcategory is radical in the sense of

, and in particular that the Matsui spectrum classifies all thick subcategories.

For a commutative algebra object

Theorem 4.7

(Comparison with the Balmer spectrum)
.

Let

be a commutative algebra object in

, and let

denote its homotopy category.
Assume

Then there is a natural dense embedding

\mathrm{Spec}(D,D,D)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0}).

If
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0})
is topologically noetherian, then this embedding is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Tensor ideals of
D_{0}
correspond bijectively to submodules of

.
By
[
Bal05
, Remark 4.3]
, any tensor ideal is radical.
Hence the underlying space
\mathrm{Spc}_{\otimes}(D_{0})
is
\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(D,\mathrm{Sub}_{D}(D))

There is a natural dense embedding
\iota:\mathrm{Spc}(D,\mathrm{Sub}_{D}(D))\to\mathrm{Spc}^{\prime}(D,\mathrm{Sub}_{D}(D))

The morphism of structure sheaves is induced by the isomorphisms

\mathrm{Nat}_{D}^{0}(\mathrm{Id}_{D_{\iota^{-1}(U)}},\mathrm{Id}_{D_{\iota^{-1}(U)}})\cong\mathrm{End}(1_{D_{0,U}}),\quad U\subset\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0}),

where

denotes the unit object of
D_{0,U}

Note that
D_{0,U}
is the homotopy category of

, since every object of

is equivalent to a retract of

for some

Assume now that
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0})

Let

be a quasi-

-prime, and let

is surjective, we must show that

\mathrm{Sub}_{D}(D)
-prime.
We have
\mathrm{Supp}_{\otimes}(P)=\{y\in\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0})\mid x\notin\overline{\{y\}}\}
.
Let
\overline{P}
be the tensor ideal with support

By
[

, Theorem 4.10]

∎

(Comparison with the Matsui spectrum)

5.
Commutativity of Localizations

In this section, we recall some fundamental properties concerning the commutativity of localizations.
These are well-known facts, so we omit the proofs; see, for example,
[
Kra23
]
or
[
Sch19
, Proposition 10.5]
for details.

Let
D
be a presentable stable
\infty
-category.
A
presentable thick subcategory

I
of
D
is a thick subcategory such that
I
itself is presentable and the inclusion functor preserves small colimits.
An object
d\in D
is called
I
-local
if
\mathrm{Hom}^{*}(I,d)=0
.
The Verdier quotient
D/I
is a presentable stable
\infty
-category, and the localization functor
D\to D/I
, denoted by
L_{I}
, has a fully faithful right adjoint.
Thus, we may identify
D/I
with the full subcategory of
I
-local objects in
D
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
D
be a presentable stable
\infty
-category, and let
I,J
be presentable thick subcategories.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For any
x\in I
and
y\in J
, every morphism
y\to x
factors through some
z\in I\cap J
.

(2)

L_{I}\circ L_{J}=L_{{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}}
.

(3)

L_{I}
preserves
J
-local objects.

(4)

L_{J}
preserves
I
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
D
be a presentable stable
\infty
-category and
I,J
its presentable submodules.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

Both
(I,J)
and
(J,I)
satisfy the equivalent conditions of Lemma
5.1
.

(2)

The following diagram is Cartesian:

\textstyle{D/(I\cap J)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{ D/J\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D/I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D/{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}^{\mathrm{pr}}.}

(3)

L_{I}\circ L_{J}=L_{J}\circ L_{I}
.

Let
D
be a (small) stable
\infty
-category.
The ind-completion is denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}(D)
.
For a thick subcategory
I\subset D
, the ind-completion
\mathrm{Ind}(I)
is a thick subcategory of
\mathrm{Ind}(D)
, and there is a natural equivalence
\mathrm{Ind}(D/I)\cong\mathrm{Ind}(D)/\mathrm{Ind}(I)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
D
be a stable
\infty
-category, and let
I,J\subset D
be thick subcategories.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For any
x\in I
and
y\in J
, every morphism
y\to x
factors through some
z\in I\cap J
.

(2)

The pair of presentable submodules
(\mathrm{Ind}~I,\mathrm{Ind}~J)\subset\mathrm{Ind}~D
satisfies the equivalent conditions of Lemma
5.1
, and moreover
\mathrm{Ind}~I\cap\mathrm{Ind}~J=\mathrm{Ind}(I\cap J)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
D
be a stable
\infty
-category, and let
I,J\subset D
be thick subcategories.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Both
(I,J)
and
(J,I)
satisfy the equivalent conditions of Lemma
5.3
.

(2)

The following diagram is Cartesian:

\textstyle{\mathrm{Idem}(D/(I\cap J))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Idem}(D/J)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Idem}(D/I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Idem}(D/{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}).}

Definition 5.5
.

Let
D
be a stable
\infty
-category, and let
I,J\subset D
be thick subcategories.
We say that
(I,J)
is
composable
(resp.
commutative
) if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of Lemma
5.3
(resp. Lemma
5.4
).

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
D
be a stable
\infty
-category, and let
I,J,K
be thick subcategories such that
(I,J)
and
(I,K)
are composable, and
\mathrm{Ind}J\cap\mathrm{Ind}K=\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap K)
.
Then
{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}\cap{\left\langle{I,K}\right\rangle}={\left\langle{I,J\cap K}\right\rangle}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
{\left\langle{I,J\cap K}\right\rangle}\subset{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}\cap{\left\langle{I,K}\right\rangle}
is clear.
Let
F\in({\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}\cap{\left\langle{I,K}\right\rangle})/{\left\langle{I,J\cap K}\right\rangle}\subset\mathrm{Ind}D
.
By composability, we have
F\in\mathrm{Ind}J\cap\mathrm{Ind}K=\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap K)
,
and hence
({\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}\cap{\left\langle{I,K}\right\rangle})/{\left\langle{I,J\cap K}\right\rangle}=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(D,S)
be a datum, and let
I,J
be submodules such that, for any submodule
K
, both pairs
(K,I)
and
(K,J)
are composable.
Then the following diagram is Cartesian:

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/(I\cap J)))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/J))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/I))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle})).}

Proof.

Submodules of
\mathrm{Idem}(D/I)
correspond bijectively to submodules of
D
containing
I
, and similarly for the other terms.
Let
P
be the pullback of the above diagram.
There is a natural map
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/(I\cap J)))\to P
, which is injective by Lemma
5.6
.
Now take
K\in\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/I))
and
K^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/J))
with the same image
K^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/{\left\langle{I,J}\right\rangle}))
.
Then, by Lemma
5.4
, the pullback
K^{\prime\prime\prime}
of
K\to K^{\prime\prime}\leftarrow K^{\prime}
is a submodule of
\mathrm{Idem}(D/(I\cap J))
.
For any
k\in K
, there exists
k^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
such that
k\oplus k[-1]
and
k^{\prime}
have the same image in
K^{\prime\prime}
by
[
Tho97
, Theorem 2.1]
.
This shows that the image of
K^{\prime\prime\prime}
in
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/I))
is
K
, and similarly its image in
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/J))
is
K^{\prime}
.
Hence surjectivity holds.
∎

Let
(D,S)
be a datum and
I
an
S
-submodule of
D
.
Let
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)_{I}
denote the subspace of
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)
consisting of primes
P
such that
I\not\subset P
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Under the above assumptions, suppose that for any submodule
J
of
D
, the pair
(J,I)
is composable.
Then there is a bijection between the underlying sets:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Spc}(I,S)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)_{I},

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\{a\in D\mid\mathrm{Fib}(a\to L_{I}(a))\in\mathrm{Ind}\tilde{P}\},

\displaystyle P\cap I

\displaystyle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}P.

The complement of
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)_{I}
in
\mathrm{Spc}(D,S)
is
\mathrm{Spc}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/I),S)
.

Proof.

The last assertion follows directly from the definition.
Let
\iota:I\hookrightarrow D
denote the natural inclusion.
Define a map

\phi=(\iota^{-1},{\left\langle{L_{I}}\right\rangle}):\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(D)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(I)\times\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(\mathrm{Idem}(D/I)),\quad J\longmapsto(\iota^{-1}(J),\mathrm{Idem}({\left\langle{J,I}\right\rangle}_{S}/I)).

Let
J,K\subset D
be submodules with
J\subset K
.
We claim that
J=K
if and only if
\phi(J)=\phi(K)
.
The “only if” direction is clear.
For the “if” direction, suppose
\phi(J)=\phi(K)
.
Since the pair
(J,I)
is composable, an object
a\in\mathrm{Ind}(J)
is
\mathrm{Ind}(I)
-local if and only if it is
\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap I)
-local.
Therefore, in
\mathrm{Ind}(D)
, the subcategory
\mathrm{Idem}({\left\langle{J,I}\right\rangle}_{S}/I)
is identified with
\mathrm{Idem}(J/(J\cap I))
, and similarly for
K
.
The submodules of
D
containing
J\cap I
correspond bijectively to the submodules of
\mathrm{Idem}(D/(J\cap I))
.
Thus, the equalities
J\cap I=K\cap I
and
\mathrm{Idem}(J/(J\cap I))=\mathrm{Idem}(K/(K\cap I))
together imply
J=K
.

Next, let
J\subset D
and
K\subset I
be submodules such that
J\cap I\subset K
.
We claim that
\phi({\left\langle{J\cap K}\right\rangle}_{S})=(K,\mathrm{Idem}({\left\langle{J,I}\right\rangle}_{S}/I))
.
The second component is clear.
The inclusion
{\left\langle{J,K}\right\rangle}_{S}\cap I\supset K
is obvious.
Define the functor
\psi:D\to\mathrm{Ind}(I)
,
a\mapsto\mathrm{Fib}(a\to L_{I}(a))
.
By composability, we have
\psi(J)\subset\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap I)\subset\mathrm{Ind}(K)
.
Thus,

{\left\langle{J,K}\right\rangle}_{S}\cap I\subset\psi({\left\langle{J,K}\right\rangle}_{S})\subset{\left\langle{\psi(J)\cup\psi(K)}\right\rangle}_{S}={\left\langle{\psi(J)\cup K}\right\rangle}_{S}\subset\mathrm{Ind}(K),

which implies
{\left\langle{J,K}\right\rangle}_{S}\cap I\subset\mathrm{Ind}(K)\cap I=K
.

Note that for a submodule
J\subset D
, we have
{\left\langle{\psi(J)}\right\rangle}^{\mathrm{pr}}_{S}=\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap I)
by composability.
Combining the above arguments with elementary set-theoretic considerations, we see that a submodule
J\subset D
with
I\not\subset J
is prime if and only if
J\cap I
is prime in
I
and
J
is maximal among submodules
K
satisfying
K\cap I=J\cap I
.
This maximality is equivalent to the condition
J=\{a\in D\mid\psi(a)\in\mathrm{Ind}(J\cap I)\}
.
Hence, the correspondences in the statement are well defined and mutually inverse.
∎

6.
The Morphism to the Spectrum of the Base

In this section, we construct a morphism from the relative Matsui spectrum of a given datum to the spectrum of the base.

Let
(D,S)
be a datum.
For a specialization-closed subset
Z\subset\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
, set

S_{Z}=\{s\in S\mid\mathrm{Supp}(s)\subset Z\},\quad D(Z)={\left\langle{S_{Z}\otimes D}\right\rangle}.

Definition 6.1
.

(1)

A datum
(D,S)
is called
admissible
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
is topologically noetherian.

(b)

For any closed subset
Z\subset\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
and any
S
-submodule
I\subset D
, the pair
(I,D(Z))
is composable.

(c)

For any closed subsets
Z,W\subset\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
, we have
D(Z\cap W)=D(Z)\cap D(W)
.

(d)

For any family of closed subsets
\{Z_{i}\}_{i}\subset\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
, we have
D(\bigcup_{i}Z_{i})={\left\langle{\bigcup_{i}D(Z_{i})}\right\rangle}
.

(2)

A datum
(D,S)
is called
smashing
if
S
is a commutative algebra, rigid,
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0})
is topologically noetherian, and for any closed subset
Z\subset\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
and any
F\in D
, the natural morphism
L_{S_{Z}}(1_{S})\otimes F\to L_{D(Z)}(F)
is an isomorphism.

(3)

A datum
(D,S)
is called
rigid
if
S
is a commutative algebra, rigid,
\mathrm{Spec}_{\otimes}(D_{0})
is topologically noetherian, and there exists a natural equivalence
\mathrm{Hom}^{0}(s\otimes d,d^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Hom}^{0}(d,s^{\vee}\otimes d^{\prime})
for all
s\in S,\,d,d^{\prime}\in D
, where
s^{\vee}
denotes the dual of
s
.

We will prove below that

\textbf{rigid}\;\Rightarrow\;\textbf{smashing}\;\Rightarrow\;\textbf{admissible}.

Let
(D,S)
be a datum.
Define
{\mathrm{Spec}^{\mathrm{fin}}}(D,S)
to be the ringed space with underlying set
\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
, whose family of closed subsets is generated by

\{\mathrm{Supp}(s\otimes D)\mid s\in S\}\cup\{\mathrm{Supp}(d)\mid d\in D\}.

The structure sheaf is defined in the same way as for
\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
.
There is a natural bijective morphism
{\mathrm{Spec}^{\mathrm{fin}}}(D,S)\to\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
.
If
D
is classically generated by a single object
d
, then
\mathrm{Supp}(s\otimes D)=\mathrm{Supp}(s\otimes d)
, and hence this morphism is an isomorphism.

Theorem 6.2

(The morphism to the spectrum of the base)
.

Let
(D,S,B)
be an admissible datum.
Then there exists a natural morphism

{\mathrm{Spec}^{\mathrm{fin}}}(D,S,B)\to\mathrm{Spec}(S,S,S),\quad P\longmapsto\{s\in S\mid s\otimes D\subset P\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
, the submodule
\{s\in S\mid s\otimes D\subset P\}\subset S
is prime, hence the morphism is well-defined.
The continuity follows because the inverse image of closed sets of the form
\mathrm{Supp}(s)
is
\mathrm{Supp}(s\otimes D)
.
Let
U\subset\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
be an open subset, and let
V\subset{\mathrm{Spec}^{\mathrm{fin}}}(D,S)
be its inverse image.
The induced morphism of structure sheaves is given by

\mathrm{Nat}_{S}^{0}(\mathrm{Id}_{S_{U}},\mathrm{Id}_{S_{U}})\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{ev}_{1_{S_{U}}}\;}\mathrm{End}^{0}(1_{S_{U}})\xrightarrow{\;1_{S_{U}}\otimes-\;}\mathrm{Nat}_{B}^{0}(\mathrm{Id}_{D_{V}},\mathrm{Id}_{D_{V}}),\quad\eta\mapsto\eta_{1_{S_{U}}}\otimes-.

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

For
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(D,S)
, the set
\{s\in S\mid s\otimes D\subset P\}
is a prime submodule of
S
.

Proof.

Put
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(S,S)
.
A specialization-closed subset
Z\subset Y
is said to satisfy condition (*) if
D(Z)\not\subset P
.
Let
Z,W\subset Y
be closed subsets satisfying (*).
By Lemma
5.6
, we have
{\left\langle{P,D(Z\cap W)}\right\rangle}={\left\langle{P,D(Z)}\right\rangle}\cap{\left\langle{P,D(W)}\right\rangle}\supsetneq P
, so
Z\cap W
also satisfies (*).

Let
\{Z_{i}\}_{i}
be a family of specialization-closed subsets of
Y
, and put
Z=\bigcup_{i}Z_{i}
.
Then
Z
satisfies (*) if and only if at least one
Z_{i}
does.
The “if” part is immediate.
For the “only if” part, use the hypothesis
D(Z)={\left\langle{\bigcup_{i}D(Z_{i})}\right\rangle}
.

Define
W=\{Z\subset Y\mid Z\text{ closed and satisfies (*)}\}
.
This set is non-empty since it contains
Y
, and it is closed under finite intersections by the previous argument.
As
Y
is noetherian,
W
has a smallest element
Z_{0}
.
We have the equality
Z_{0}=\bigcup_{z\in Z_{0}}\overline{\{z\}}
.
By the previous claim, there exists
z_{0}\in Z_{0}
such that
\overline{\{z_{0}\}}
satisfies (*).
By minimality of
Z_{0}
, it follows that
Z_{0}=\overline{\{z_{0}\}}
.
Since
Y
is a
T_{0}
-space, such a point
z_{0}
is unique.

A specialization-closed subset
Z\subset Y
satisfies (*) if and only if it contains
Z_{0}
, equivalently if it contains
z_{0}
.
Therefore,

\{s\in S\mid s\otimes D\subset P\}=\{s\in S\mid z_{0}\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(s)\},

In this section, we illustrate how the relative Matsui spectrum appears in familiar geometric settings.

7.1.
Some properties for calculating relative Matsui spectra

In this section, we establish several basic properties that will be useful in explicit calculations of relative Matsui spectra.
We first study the behavior of submodules under change of algebras, and then prove descent-type results.

Let
(D,S)
and
(E,T)
be data, and let
f_{2}\colon S\to T
be a homomorphism of algebras,
f_{1}\colon D\to E
a morphism of
S
-modules and
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
.
In this setting, we define two maps between
\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(D)
and
\mathrm{Sub}_{T}(E)
:

\displaystyle{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}\colon\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(D)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}_{T}(E),\quad I\longmapsto{\left\langle{f_{1}(I)}\right\rangle}_{T},

\displaystyle f^{-1}\colon\mathrm{Sub}_{T}(E)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sub}_{S}(D),\quad J\longmapsto f_{1}^{-1}(J).

Lemma 7.1
.

In the above setting, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}
is injective.

(2)

f^{-1}
is surjective.

(3)

For every prime
S
-submodule
P\subset D
, we have
{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(P)\neq{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(\overline{P})
.

(4)

For every prime
S
-submodule
P\subset D
, there exists a prime
T
-submodule
Q\subset E
such that
f^{-1}(Q)=P
and
f^{-1}(\overline{Q})\supsetneq P
.

(5)

The composite
f^{-1}\circ{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}
is the identity map.

Proof.

(5) clearly implies (1), and (1) implies (3).

Assume (3).
Let
P\subset D
be a prime
S
-submodule and take
p\in\overline{P}\setminus P
.
Then
f(p)\notin{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(P)
by (3).
By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a prime
T
-submodule
Q\subset E
such that
{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(P)\subset Q
and
p\notin Q
.
Hence (4) holds.

Assume (4).
Let
I\subset D
be any
S
-submodule.
Then there exists a family
\{P_{i}\}
of prime
S
-submodules such that
I=\bigcap P_{i}
.
By (4), each
P_{i}
is the inverse image of some prime

.
Thus

f^{-1}\!\left(\bigcap Q_{i}\right)=\bigcap f^{-1}(Q_{i})=\bigcap P_{i}=I,

and hence (2) holds.

Assume (2).
Given any
I\subset D
, there exists
J\subset E
such that
f^{-1}(J)=I
.
Then clearly,
f(I)\subset{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(I)\subset J
, so

I\subset f^{-1}({\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}(I))\subset f^{-1}(J)=I.

Hence,
f^{-1}\circ{\left\langle{f}\right\rangle}_{T}=\mathrm{id}
, so (5) holds.
∎

Lemma 7.2

(Descent)
.

Let

be a datum, and let

The functors
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}^{*}\mathbb{R}\mathrm{pr}_{*}
and
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Perf}(X^{\prime})}
are represented by

\mathscr{O}_{\Delta_{X}}\boxtimes_{Y}(\mathscr{O}_{Y^{\prime}}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathscr{O}_{Y}}\mathscr{O}_{Y^{\prime}}),\mathscr{O}_{\Delta_{X}}\boxtimes_{Y}\mathscr{O}_{\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{Y}X\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{Y}Y^{\prime}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{Y}Y^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{QCoh}(X^{\prime}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{Y}X^{\prime})

7.3.
Twisted derived categories and Severi–Brauer schemes

In this subsection, we examine twisted derived categories and Severi–Brauer schemes.
We show that relative spectrum of twisted perfect complexes recovers the base scheme.
For Severi–Brauer schemes, the spectrum contains the scheme itself together with countably many copies of the base.
In relative dimension one, we obtain an isomorphism and classify submodules via specialization-closed subsets.

Let
X
be a scheme, and let

\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathrm{H}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})\times\mathrm{H}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.
The corresponding twisted derived category
\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu)
is naturally a
\mathrm{Perf}(X,0)=\mathrm{Perf}(X)
-module; see
[
Toë12
]
for the definition.
There is a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu)\otimes_{\mathrm{Perf}(X)}\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu^{\prime})\;\cong\;\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu+\mu^{\prime}).

When
\mu_{1}=0
, the category
\mathrm{Perf}(X,\mu)
is called the derived category of perfect complexes of twisted sheaves; see
[
Cal00
]
for details.

Theorem 7.14
.

Let

be a scheme and

Proof.

Let

[

]

Let
X
be a scheme,

The derived matrix factorization category

[
Pos11
]
,
[
EP15
]
.
There is a natural action of
\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,0)
on
\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,W)
.

Let
\iota:Z\to X
be a closed subscheme.
Hirano
[
Hir19
]
introduced the relative singular locus
\mathrm{Sing}(Z/X)
by

\mathrm{Sing}(Z/X)=\{\,z\in Z\mid\exists F\ \text{coherent sheaf on }Z,\;\;F_{z}\notin\mathrm{Perf}(\mathscr{O}_{Z,z}),\ \iota_{*}F\in\mathrm{Perf}(X)\,\}.

When
2
is invertible on
X
and
W
is a non-zero-divisor, he reconstructed the relative singular locus of the zero scheme
X_{0}
of
W
using the Balmer spectrum with a certain tensor structure.
Our construction extends this result.

The author gratefully acknowledges Hirano and Ouchi, from whom he learned this idea.

Theorem 7.20
.

Assume that
X
is separated and admits an ample family of line bundles.
If
W
is a non-zero-divisor, then there is a homeomorphism

\mathrm{Spc}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,W),\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,0))\cong\mathrm{Sing}(X_{0}/X).

Proof.

This follows from Hirano’s classification of
\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,0)
-submodules
[
Hir19
]
.
∎

Note that the underlying set of
\mathrm{Spc}(\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,W),\mathrm{DMF}(X,L,0))
is the same, but its family of closed subsets is generated by
\{\,Z\subset\mathrm{Sing}(X_{0}/X)\mid Z\text{ is closed in }X\,\}
.

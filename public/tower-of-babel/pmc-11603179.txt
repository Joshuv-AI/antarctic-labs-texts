Sand gradation detection method based on local sampling
Authors: Yang Zhang, Danxia Hou, Chuanyun Xu, Heng Wang, Liping Peng, Xinghai Yuan, Xuanpeng Zhang, Gang Li, Song Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Sand gradation is an important reference factor for concrete proportion design, which has a significant impact on the strength and workability of concrete. The efficiency of using the traditional vibrating sieving method to measure the gradation is low, and the machine vision-based sand gradation inspection method can realize the automation of sand gradation inspection, which can significantly improve the inspection efficiency and reduce the inspection cost. The particle size of construction sand is usually between 0.075 and 4.75 mm, and to capture images of sand below 0.15 mm requires a high-resolution camera that can only maintain a small object distance and a small shooting field of view, so it is difficult for the image acquisition system to capture images of all the sand particles, and this is a challenging problem in sand grading inspection. In order to solve this problem, this study proposes a sand gradation detection method based on localized sampling, and develops a hardware system for localized sampling and a software system for gradation detection. The hardware system uses a flexible vibrating disk to uniformly disperse the sand particles, realizing random local sampling of sand particles, and calculating the grain size gradation of the test samples based on the local data of multiple local sampling. The experimental results show that the local sampling gradation and the sample gradation have a similar distribution, and the local gradation detection error of multiple sampling is smaller. This study provides an effective method for the automated detection of sand grain size gradation between 0.075 and 4.75 mm, which can significantly increase the frequency of construction sand gradation detection, improve the quality control level of construction sand, and improve the quality of construction.

Introduction

Mechanized sand as one of the raw materials for concrete production, its particle shape, grading, stone powder content, crushing index, etc. significantly affects the concrete performance. Particle grading is an intrinsic determinant of sand quality, which has a significant effect on concrete strength and workability and should be strictly controlled during production and make the particle grading curve with skeleton dense characteristics1. Gradation refers to the quality distribution of sand of various particle size specifications, and through reasonable grading control, it can optimize the use of resources, reduce costs, and improve the quality of construction works2,3. In addition, according to the construction requirements, the cumulative sieve residue of each sieve grade is reasonably controlled within a certain ratio, which will give full play to the role of mechanism sand in enhancing the strength and performance of concrete4. Therefore, the fineness modulus and particle gradation of sand should be considered comprehensively, and the gradation of mechanism sand should be monitored in real time.

Currently, the sieving method is the main method to detect the gradation of sand, which uses a set of sieves with different apertures to separate the particles in the sand sample, and the gradation of sand is obtained by the sieve residue of each sieve. Traditional manual sieving is characterized by high workload and subjectivity, and the automated vibratory sieving method is likely to destroy the original particle size distribution of the sand during vibration, and the accuracy of sieving is also greatly affected by the shape of the sand particles5. In order to solve the problems of the sieving method, the grading detection method based on the image method is proposed, firstly, the sand square to be detected is placed on the detection platform, and the camera is used to capture the image, and then sand grain segmentation and feature extraction are carried out, and finally the grading of the sand grains is calculated based on the extracted features. However, when the grading inspection is carried out in practical applications, the sand grains are piled up and stick together in the normal state, and relying solely on the visual equipment to obtain the image data, the single sand grains are difficult to be segmented and recognized, feature extraction cannot be carried out, and the detection accuracy of the grading is difficult to be ensured, which has become the technical bottleneck in realizing the automatic inspection of mixed aggregate grading on the construction site. With the development of artificial intelligence, deep learning methods are widely used in various industries, and deep learning network models have excellent performance in image classification and image segmentation tasks. Therefore, the network model was attempted to be used to solve the problems in the field of grade detection of aggregates and was expected to show good performance in the field of grade detection as well. However, during the research process, a large amount of data is required to train an excellent deep learning network model, the data labeling of mechanism sand images is difficult, and publicly available and suitable datasets are not found at present, so the lack of training datasets is the biggest difficulty faced by deep learning methods at present.

In summary, this paper addresses the above situation and conducts related research on automatic detection system and method of grading of mechanism sand, and the main contributions include (1) A sand grading detection system is developed, and the system is capable of stably detecting the grading of sand grains with grain sizes ranging from 0.075 to 4.75 mm. (2) A sand grain image acquisition method based on a flexible vibrating disc is proposed, which uses a flexible vibrating disc to disperse sand grains and a high-resolution camera to acquire local images of the vibrating disc, and by dispersing sand grains from the vibrating disc several times and taking pictures several times, the sand grain images in the vibrating disc can be sampled randomly. This method is currently the only method that can stably collect images of sand particles with a particle size of 0.15 mm or less. (3) Taking the Law of Large Numbers as the theoretical basis, the sand grading detection method based on local sampling is proposed. This method can stably detect the gradation of sand particles with a particle size of 0.15 mm or less, and at the same time avoids the requirement of segmenting overlapping sand particles.

Literature review

The grading, grain shape and void ratio of sand grains are the measurement indexes of mechanism sand stipulated in the national standard “Sand for Construction”. Among them, the common methods of grading detection are sieving method, image method, etc. China’s national standard uses the sieving method, but the sieving method has the disadvantage of not being able to obtain the two parameters of grain size, grain shape and automatic detection. In recent years, the development of digital image processing technology for a comprehensive study of characterization of aggregate particle shape opened up a new road, two-dimensional characterization and evaluation of aggregate particle shape characteristics will be the future development trend6, based on the image method has become an important way to improve the grading detection problem. In order to realize the automation of gradation detection and solve the problems of traditional sieving method, Li7researched the digital image technology of real-time mineral mixture gradation detection at construction site to meet the real-time detection requirements at construction site and designed a new digital image acquisition system. Yang8 developed an image-based detection device and system for the particle size of 0.6–4.75 mm range of mechanism. 4.75 mm range of mechanism sand for particle size detection experiments, proposed a particle size correction coefficient correction method. Qin9constructed a non-contact aggregate grading aggregate detection platform. In order to address the problem of serious sand adhesion in the image, which leads to the difficulty of image segmentation, Wenhua10used a dual-camera multi-scale method to capture images of falling sand for grading measurement, and Fan11proposed to detect the aggregate gradation based on the multiview information of the falling process of the aggregate. Huang12 proposed a method for measuring the gradation of crushed sand based on dynamic image analysis, focusing on the characterization of particle shape and gradation during the free-fall process. Cai13 developed a manufactured sand detection system based on digital image processing. This system is capable of extracting the contour features of manufactured sand particles within the size range of 0.15–4.75 mm, and it also explores various algorithms for equivalent particle size characterization. However, dynamic image analysis demands a high acquisition speed, and capturing particles smaller than 0.15 mm during the free-fall process remains challenging. Although Li14 measured particles ranging in size from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\,\upmu \hbox {m}$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2888\,\upmu \hbox {m}$$\end{document} in their study on dynamic image analysis, their methodology relies on camera systems with extremely short exposure times, which results in significant data processing demands and high associated costs.

Regarding the characterization of grain shape and grain size of sand grains, Kumara measured gravel grain size based on digital image processing (DIP) using equivalent elliptic short diameters as the grain size, which was greater than that of the sieve method (SM)15. The equivalent ellipse Feret short diameter was used as the particle size of sand in the two-dimensional image, indicating that the use of equivalent particle size makes the results of the image method close to the sieving method16. Liu17 detected the particle shape of the mechanism sand based on the image method using an image analysis system, indicating that the equivalent ellipse axial ratio is the best indicator for evaluating the shape of the sand grains. Zhou18also investigated the characterization of the aggregate particle size The parametric method was also studied, which showed that the correlation between the projected area*equivalent ellipse Feret’s short diameter as the equivalent volume and the actual volume was the greatest. Fan11used principal component analysis and probabilistic neural network to classify aggregate particles and calculate aggregate gradation based on the multi-view information of the falling process of the aggregates. The image method for grade detection requires the acquisition of volume parameters of sand particles, however, volume as a three-dimensional feature cannot be accurately acquired based on two-dimensional images. Therefore, Li liang19investigated the geometric characteristics of aggregate and concrete appearance based on 3D scanning technique. Su20 used micro-focused X-ray CT scanning technique to obtain high-resolution 3D aggregate shapes, and then used spherical harmonic function to describe the 3D aggregate morphology. However, the 3D image data acquisition cost is large, and it is difficult to apply to the processing of large quantities and smaller particle sizes in practical applications. To address this problem, Zheng21and Xie22investigated the 2D and 3D morphological characteristics of mechanism sand, established the relationship between 3D and 2D parameters, and provided a practical method to obtain representative sand particle information from 2D image analysis.

With the wide application of artificial intelligence, in order to quickly characterize sand particles and grade detection, Schneider23used fast particle characterization using digital holography and neural networks. Siyao24dealt with the problems of traditional particle size measurement methods based on the improved U-net architecture by designing a specialized loss function. Li25used a machine learning algorithms for sand classification and analyzed the performance of seven machine learning models for automatic classification of sand grains, showing that neural network classifiers can effectively and robustly identify up to 75% of sand grains. Kim26used Inception-ResNet-v2 model and migration learning to accurately predict sand shape parameters based on the full shape image of sand grains. Xuan27investigated the aggregate gradation prediction method based on machine learning, and used the improved ResNet50 classification algorithm for fine-tuning and training the model for the aggregate grade category classification problem. Qin9developed a mixed aggregate gradation sampling device and an improved aggregation segmentation convolutional neural network model (AS Mask RCNN: Aggregate Segmentation Mask R_CNN) algorithm. However, the application of machine learning algorithms and deep learning models typically requires the construction of large-scale training datasets, which not only involves labor-intensive annotation tasks but also incurs substantial costs. Moreover, given that each sample (weighing 500 grams) contains hundreds of thousands of sand particles, the computational expense of utilizing neural networks is prohibitively high.

In summary, there are still some challenges and problems in the practical application of these methods: firstly, although the image method can measure the grading and particle shape parameters of sand grains at the same time, the particle size of mechanism sand spans a large range, which is affected by the pixel accuracy, and it is more difficult for the image method to accurately measure the morphology parameters of the particles below 0.15 mm. Secondly, the dynamic image method has high requirements for industrial cameras, and it is difficult to accurately capture the sand particles below 0.15 mm in the movement process. Therefore, in order to solve the problem of detecting the particle size below 0.15 mm, this paper uses a flexible vibrating disk to disperse the adherent sand particles, samples the image of the sand particles in several times, reduces the rate of sand particles adherence, and predicts the overall gradation by collecting the local image several times, which solves the problem that the field of view of the high-resolution industrial camera is small, and the global image cannot be captured.

Proposed methods

In order to detect the grading of sand with grain size between 0.075 and 4.75 mm, this paper builds a complete automatic grading detection system. The system realises the automatic detection of grading data of mechanism sand to meet the engineering needs. The system consists of two parts: hardware and software. The hardware system is responsible for the acquisition of sand images, which mainly consists of a high-resolution visual device and an improved and optimised flexible vibration dispersion device. The vision device ensures the clarity and accuracy of the image, while the flexible vibration device can randomly and uniformly disperse the stacked and adhering sand particles. The software system is mainly responsible for sand grain image processing, feature extraction of single sand grains, and calculation of gradation and fineness modulus. In the image acquisition process, in order to balance the accuracy and speed of grading detection, this paper proposes to achieve the acquisition of images of randomly distributed mechanism sand based on the idea of local sampling, and the system carries out the overall flow of grading detection as shown in Fig. 1.

Image acquisition

Since sand grains with particle sizes smaller than 0.15 mm are easily occluded and difficult to be captured by ordinary industrial cameras, most of the literature generally focuses only on sand with particle sizes larger than 0.15 mm and ignores sand grains below 0.15 mm8,28–30. Therefore, in order to make the resolution accuracy of the images captured by the camera meet the experimental requirements and capture sand particles with particle sizes below 0.15 mm, this paper reduces the occlusion rate of sand particles below 0.15 mm through a dispersion device, and selects an industrial camera with a resolution of 5472×3648 with a 50 mm zoom lens to capture the sand image data with a range of particle sizes from 0.075 to 4.75 mm.

However, the use of high-resolution cameras and longer focal lengths will lead to a smaller field of view of the camera, smaller than the dispersion range of the vibrating disc, the industrial camera can not capture the global image, and it is necessary to design a movable acquisition device in order to capture the global image, resulting in slower detection speed. In addition, because the vibrating feed bin under the material is random and uniform, this paper assumes that the particle size distribution of sand particles is random, after the dispersion device flexible vibrating disc dispersion is uniformly distributed, and the distribution of each local distribution and the overall distribution is also the same. Therefore, this paper proposes a multiple local sampling method based on the law of large numbers to collect the image data of sand, randomly selecting a small number of data points from a large dataset, and then using these data points to estimate the properties of the whole dataset.

The specific local image acquisition process is as follows: the inspection system divides the inspection samples into equal groups by means of the gravity sensor of the sand feed module, and controls the direction of rotation of the servo to convey each group to the dispersion module. Then, the dispersion module (flexible vibrating disc) disperses each group randomly and evenly. Considering that the camera shooting field of view is about one-third of the vibrating disc surface area, and the need to balance the speed and accuracy of grade detection, this paper chooses to collect 5 local images for each group, and 1 local image for each dispersion I(n,b), b = 1,2,...5, as shown in Fig. 5, which is a strategy that ensures that the speed of automatic grade detection is guaranteed without sacrificing the accuracy of the detection, in order to realise the requirements of the actual grade detection in the construction site.Fig. 5Local image sampling flow.

Image processing

The image processing mainly includes pre-processing, segmentation, and feature extraction of the sand image, and the results of the image processing are shown in Fig. 6. As the industrial camera captures colour images, the cost-effectiveness of the information provided is low. Therefore, the sand grain image is first pre-processing operations such as grey scale, and then the pre-processing result map is threshold segmentation, in order to facilitate the subsequent extraction of the contour features of a single sand grain, the image needs to be segmented, this paper adopts the maximum between-class variance method proposed by the Japanese scholar Otsu31(Nobuyuki Otsu) in 1979 for the threshold segmentation, and obtains the binary image. Finally, according to the binary image obtained by segmentation to extract sand features, this paper selects the contour tracking algorithm in the boundary finding method to extract sand contour features, and implements the algorithm based on the find Contours() function of OpenCV32. Additionally, to further address the issue of image distortion, we employed a chessboard calibration technique33 to precisely calibrate the camera. The chessboard calibration technique involves acquiring multi-view images and precisely detecting the corners of the chessboard to obtain their coordinates in the image plane. Subsequently, based on the correspondence between these corner coordinates and the actual physical dimensions of the chessboard, Zhang’s calibration algorithm is utilized to compute the camera’s intrinsic parameters (such as focal length, principal point location, and distortion coefficients) and extrinsic parameters (including the rotation and translation matrices of the camera). This approach effectively corrects geometric distortions in the images, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of the measurement results.Fig. 6Sand image processing.

Sand image processing.

To address the problem of incomplete sand grains at the image boundary and a small number of adherent sand grains in the image during the image acquisition stage, this paper selects the method based on concave point detection34 to remove a small number of adherent stacked sand grains in the image based on the basic principles of probabilistic statistics and stochastic processes, randomness and uniformity of the dispersion of sand grains, overlapping and adherence, no bias in the distribution or concentration tendency, as well as unbiasedness of the equipment operation.

Experiments and results

According to the GB/T14684-2022 standard for construction sand37, the grading of sand should meet the requirements of three grading zones (coarse sand zone, medium sand zone, fine sand zone) and be divided according to the fineness modulus Coarse sand: 3.7-3.1; medium sand: 3.0-2.3; fine sand 2.2-1.6. In this paper, we collect these three specifications and the range of particle size of 0.075–4.75 mm This paper collects the image data of these three kinds of sizes and grain sizes ranging from 0.075 to 4.75 mm for experimental research, and collects 10 samples in total, each sample is about 500 g. The experiments mainly include the grading characterisation comparison experiments and the verification experiments of the local sampling method, and the experimental grading test results of this paper are compared with the real grading obtained by the sieving method, and the errors between the two are calculated, and the characteristics and feasibility of the proposed method are analysed according to the errors.

Local sampling experiments

In this paper, when designing the image acquisition device, in order to capture images of sand particles with a particle size of 0.15 mm or less, a high-resolution industrial camera is used, which results in the range of the shooting field of view being much smaller than the dispersion range of the flexible vibrating disc. To address this problem, we choose to obtain the sand image data of each discharging based on the random local sampling method, and estimate the particle size distribution of the whole disc surface through the local particle size distribution. Experiments are also designed to verify the feasibility of using the local particle size distribution to estimate the global particle size distribution.

Conclusion

Aiming at the problem that the national standard sieving method cannot efficiently and automatically detect the grading of sand and feedback the detection results online, and the challenge that the existing image method faces the difficulty of detecting sand particles with a particle size of less than 0.15 mm, this paper carries out a research on the hardware system of automatic grading detection by using machine vision and automation technology and conducts a research on the detection method of sand particles of less than 0.15 mm based on localised sampling. The main research results are as follows: (1) The design and development of automatic sand grade detection hardware system based on local sampling is completed. Combined with the difficulties of the existing grading detection methods, the functional requirements of the hardware system were analysed. According to the functional requirements, this paper designs the hardware device of the system, which realises the whole automation of grade detection and maximises the efficiency of grade detection. (2) Based on the Law of Large Numbers, this paper proposes a grading detection method based on local sampling to achieve the detection of sand with particle size less than 0.15 mm and the balance of accuracy and speed of grading detection. (3) Comparison experiments and validation experiments were designed to select the optimal grade characterisation method for the detection method in this paper and to prove the feasibility of the detection system and method proposed in this paper. Compared with the sieving method, the average cumulative gradation error of the gradation detection of this method is 11%, and the average fineness modulus error is 0.115, which meets the requirements of gradation detection.

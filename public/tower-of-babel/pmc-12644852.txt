Construction of UE4 model and virtual experience technology for Qiang ethnic architectural environment based on improved A* algorithm
Authors: Yanyan Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the acceleration of modernization, the traditional architecture of the Qiang ethnic group is facing challenges in terms of protection and inheritance. To better showcase the architectural culture of the Qiang ethnic group and enhance its experience in virtual environments, this study adopts adaptive strategies to optimize the traditional A* algorithm. It introduces reverse optimization strategies and reward value diffusion strategies, and further optimizes the efficiency of path planning. By combining Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) model construction technology, high-precision modeling, and rendering of the Qiang ethnic architectural environment have been achieved. In terms of interaction response time, the mean response time of the improved A* was 0.35 s to 0.50 s, significantly lower than other algorithms. In terms of navigation ability, the navigation accuracy was as high as 95.3% and 96.3%, with the smallest navigation error. Meanwhile, the improved A* had the shortest rendering time and the lowest memory usage. This indicates that the improved A* algorithm can quickly respond to user operation requests, reduce waiting time, and significantly enhance user engagement and interest, thereby increasing user immersion. This study provides strong technical support for the virtual display and cultural inheritance of the Qiang ethnic architectural environment.Keywords: Improved A* algorithm, Qiang ethnic architectural environment, UE4 model, Virtual experience technology, Adaptation strategySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Qiang Ethnic Group (QEG) is one of the ethnic minorities with a long history in China. QEG’s traditional architecture has a unique artistic style and architectural techniques, and carries rich cultural connotations and historical memories. The Qiang Ethnic Architecture (QEA) is renowned for its unique stone masonry techniques, watchtower structures, and harmonious coexistence with the natural environment, and is an important symbol and carrier of Qiang culture1,2. However, with the acceleration of modernization, traditional QEA faces many challenges, including natural erosion, population outflow, and cultural heritage gaps. To better protect and inherit the QEA culture, virtual reality and augmented reality technologies provide new possibilities for the digital display and cultural inheritance of QEA3,4. Through the construction of virtual environments, users can immerse themselves in the unique charm of QEA and enhance their awareness and understanding of QEG culture. In the construction of virtual environments, path planning is crucial for achieving efficient navigation and immersive experiences. Traditional path planning algorithms, such as the A* algorithm, have limitations in complex environments, including node redundancy and poor path smoothness5,6. For example, while the traditional A* algorithm performs well in static environments, its efficiency and accuracy need improvement in dynamic environments with complex obstacle distributions. Additionally, existing algorithms have shortcomings in rendering efficiency and user immersion, particularly in dynamic interaction and real-time navigation, where user experience can be enhanced.

Currently, scholars have conducted research on path planning in virtual environments. Tu et al. proposed a Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based path planning and obstacle avoidance method to handle the current situation in intricate virtual conditions. This method consisted of two parts: the path planning part used a Q-learning Algorithm (QLA), and the obstacle avoidance part used a deep Q-learning network combined with deep images for obstacle detection and obstacle avoidance strategy generation. QLA performed well in path planning, effectively reducing computation time and path length. Deep QLA has successfully achieved real-time visual obstacle avoidance in obstacle avoidance experiments, with high obstacle avoidance accuracy and short computation time7. Huang et al. put forward a UAV path planning approach built on building information model support to address the current situation of UAV path planning in building exterior wall inspection. The path planning method supported by the proposed building information model could effectively assist UAVs in completing building inspection tasks, with high applicability and flexibility8. Almazrouei proposed an RL-based method for dynamic obstacle avoidance and path planning in dynamic virtual environments. This method covered five main steps from building information modeling to actual flight: generating surface models represented by point clouds, determining observation points, path planning, virtual simulation, and actual flight. The proposed method had high applicability and flexibility, and could significantly improve navigation efficiency and safety9. Wen et al. proposed a feature-based framework model for 3D motion planning of autonomous underwater vehicles in complex underwater environments. This framework integrated a priority experience replay mechanism for energy-saving underwater navigation of underwater vehicles. The simulation results showed that, compared with other RL algorithms, this method had advantages in energy consumption, convergence, and stability10. Feng et al. proposed an Adaptive Multi-Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (AMRRT*) method for the motion planning problem of robots in complex environments. This method explored the configuration space more efficiently by improving the sub-tree generation, selection, and planning processes in the multi-tree sampling structure. The results showed that AMRRT* had superior performance in key indicators such as computational efficiency and success rate, demonstrating its potential in addressing complex motion planning challenges11.

Although the above-mentioned research has achieved good results in path planning, the existing algorithms still face challenges in terms of computational efficiency, especially in terms of real-time performance and dynamic adaptability in complex environments. In addition, existing research has shortcomings in rendering efficiency and user immersion in virtual environments, especially in dynamic interaction and real-time navigation, where user experience still needs to be improved. To overcome the limitations of traditional path planning algorithms and improve navigation efficiency and user experience in virtual QEA environments, an improved A* algorithm is proposed to optimize path planning in building environments. The innovation of the research lies in the introduction of adaptive strategy, Reverse Optimization Strategy (ROS), and Reward Value Diffusion Strategy (RVDS) to perfect the path planning capacity of the A* algorithm. The main contributions of the research include four points: (1) An improved A* algorithm is proposed, which significantly enhances the efficiency and accuracy of path planning. (2) In the virtual construction of the QEA environment, high-precision modeling and rendering are achieved through the UE4 model construction technology, providing high-quality visual effects.

Optimization of virtual qea environment path planning based on improved A* algorithm

Although the A* algorithm with adaptive strategy has achieved certain optimization effects in virtual path planning in the QEA environment, there are still some shortcomings in practical applications. Although adaptive strategies can dynamically adjust the weights of heuristic functions, their flexibility and adaptability are still weak in complex environments, especially in scenarios where obstacles are unevenly distributed or dynamically changing. In addition, the adaptive strategy mainly focuses on optimizing the heuristic function, and its optimization effect on the smoothness of the path and the turning points is limited. To overcome the shortcomings of the A* algorithm in the above aspects, this study further introduces ROS to optimize the A* algorithm, as shown in Fig. 2.

Reverse optimization strategy schematic diagram.

In Fig. 2, ROS attempts to observe the best path from the TP in a reverse direction. After determining that there is no collision between the TP and the path point, deleting redundant path nodes can obtain a shorter and more direct path. The core of ROS is to perform path search from the TP to the SP and combine the results of forward search to perform bidirectional optimization of the path22,23. The implementation steps of the strategy are shown in Fig. 3.

ROS implementation steps.

In Fig. 3, ROS first employs a bidirectional search mechanism, which simultaneously initiates a forward search from the SP to the TP and a backward search from the TP to the SP. Among them, forward search and reverse search maintain two open lists and closed lists, respectively, and expand the nodes in the forward and reverse directions during each iteration until the nodes in the two search directions meet24,25. Next is the process of path fusion and optimization. When the nodes of forward and reverse search meet, the paths in both directions merge. By removing redundant nodes and adjusting path direction, the smoothness and length of the path can be further optimized. Next is the dynamic adjustment process of the heuristic function. In the process of reverse optimization, heuristic functions not only consider the distance from the CN to the TP but also combine the distance from the TP to the SP. Construction based on UE4 model and virtual experience technology

After improving the A* algorithm, this study further models and renders the virtual scene of QEA. Among them, in the virtual construction of the QEA environment, Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) serves as a powerful 3D development tool, providing support for high-quality model construction. Through UE4, high-precision modeling and rendering of QEA can be achieved, providing realistic visual effects for virtual experiences28,29. For the simplification of the model, the high-precision 3D model of QEA is first imported into UE4. Secondly, the Decimation Master plugin is used to simplify the model, reduce the number of polygons, and maintain the visual effect of the model at the same time. For key areas such as architectural details and decorations, the model is manually adjusted to ensure its uniqueness is maintained even after simplification. Finally, through real-time rendering tests, the visual effects of the simplified model under different views and lighting conditions are verified. Texture mapping is a key step to ensure the realistic visual effect of the model. 3D modeling software is used to perform UV expansion on the model to ensure that the texture can be correctly mapped to the model surface. Tools such as Photoshop or Substance Painter are used to draw high-resolution textures, including diffuse reflection, normal, highlights, and Ambient Occlusion (AO) textures. The drawn texture is imported into UE4 and mapped onto the corresponding model surface. To optimize memory usage and ensure efficient rendering on GPUs, texture compression techniques are employed. Specifically, 4 K PBR textures are compressed using BC1 (for color textures) and BC3 (for textures with alpha channels) formats, which are commonly used in UE4 for their balance between quality and performance. This compression method significantly reduces the memory footprint of the textures without compromising visual quality. In UE4, the tiling and offset of textures are adjusted to ensure a uniform and natural distribution of textures on the surface of the model. In texture mapping, Substance Painter is first used for texture rendering of 4 K PBR textures. Secondly, the ambient occlusion map is baked from the high-polygon model, which simulates the interaction between light and the model surface, enhancing the realism of the rendering. Subsequently, the texture is exported in PNG or TIFF format in Substance Painter. If the file is too large or the memory usage is too high, it may lead to an increase in loading time, thereby indirectly affecting the rendering performance. The next step is to map the exported texture onto the 3D model of the Qiang architectural environment using Blender’s principal BSDF shader. Finally, it is rendered in Blender or previewed in real-time using Eevee. For applications that require real-time rendering, this may cause the frame rate to drop, affecting the user experience. In the construction process of the UE4 model, the first step is to import QEG assets, including 3D models, textures, and other related assets of the QEA environment. Secondly, the overall scene of QEG village architecture is constructed in the UE4 model, including elements such as buildings, roads, and vegetation. Through UE4’s virtual experience technology, users can immerse themselves in the unique charm of QEG culture. Users can use VR devices to immerse themselves in the QEA environment, and high-quality VR content development can be achieved through UE4’s VR plugin. In addition, combined with augmented reality technology, users can overlay QEA’s virtual models in real environments through mobile devices such as smartphones or tablets, achieving a virtual reality experience. In the process of interaction and navigation, through the blueprint system of UE4, users can interact with the QEA environment, such as obtaining historical information or the cultural background of buildings through clicks or gesture operations. Combined with the improved A* algorithm, it can provide users with an efficient navigation experience. Users can move freely in the virtual environment, and the algorithm calculates the optimal path in real-time to avoid collisions and lag. Through the model construction and virtual experience technology of UE4, the virtual construction of the QEA environment can provide high-quality visual effects and bring users an immersive interactive experience. To achieve the interaction between users and the QEA environment, detailed interaction scripts are written using the UE4 blueprint system. Firstly, an interaction blueprint is created, which involves creating a new blueprint class in UE4 to handle user interaction logic. Secondly, interactive events are defined; that is, various interactive events are defined in the blueprint, such as clicking to query building information, moving navigation, triggering virtual character interactions, etc. The next step is to write interaction logic. When a user clicks on a certain building, an event is triggered to display the historical information and cultural background of the building. Combined with the improved A* algorithm, the optimal path is calculated in real-time to guide users to move in the virtual environment. When the user approaches the virtual character, interactive events such as conversations and animations are triggered. Finally, through user testing, the accuracy and smoothness of the interaction logic are verified to ensure that users can interact with the virtual environment naturally.

To further enhance the adaptability and path planning performance of the improved A* algorithm in a dynamic environment, a path planning method based on RL is studied and introduced. RL is a machine learning method that learns the optimal decisions by interacting with the environment, which is particularly suitable for dynamically changing environments. Deep Q-Network (DQN) is an RL method combined with deep neural networks, which can optimize path planning by learning the value function of state-action pairs. Specifically, first, the raster map of the QEA environment is taken as the status input, including the positions of obstacles, SPs, and TPs. Second, the action is defined as the direction of movement in space. Next, a reward function is designed. For example, a high reward is given for reaching the TP, a negative reward is given for colliding with obstacles, and a small negative reward is given for each step of movement to encourage reaching the target quickly. Finally, DQN learns the optimal Q-value through interaction with the environment and selects the optimal action sequence to achieve TP. By introducing RL methods, the adaptability of path planning in dynamic environments can be enhanced.

The Dataset selected for the study is the CARLA Dataset (https://carla.org/) and the nuScenes Dataset (https://www.nuscenes.org/). Among them, the CARLA dataset provides a realistic virtual environment for developing, training, and validating path planning algorithms. In the meta-data of this dataset, the scene types include 600 residential areas and 400 industrial areas, etc. The types of obstacles include vehicles and pedestrians in both stationary and dynamic states. The nuScenes Dataset includes 600 urban environments and 400 rural environments. Obstacle types include static buildings and trees as well as dynamic vehicles, etc. In terms of obstacle modeling, watchtowers can be modeled as cylindrical obstacles, while stone walls can be modeled as rectangular obstacles. Narrow alleys can be modeled as channels with specific widths, and terrain can be modeled through height maps. Among them, each grid point represents the height of the terrain. To handle these unique obstacles and path constraints, the following adjustments are made to the path planning algorithm in the research. The first is to increase the number of collisions to ensure that the path does not pass through obstacles. The second is to adjust the heuristic function and consider the impact of obstacles on path cost. Finally, local path planning is used to handle narrow spaces or irregular terrains. This study simultaneously introduces traditional A*, Dijkstra, and Jump Point Search algorithm (JPS) for comparative experiments. Among them, in the simple environment, the obstacle density is 10%, the average path tortuosity is 1.2, the path length is relatively short, the obstacle distribution is relatively sparse, and the path is relatively direct. The obstacle density in the complex environment is 40%, the average path tortuosity is 2.5, the path length is relatively long, the obstacles are densely distributed, and the path requires multiple turns and direction adjustments. Discussion and conclusion

To optimize the virtual construction and virtual experience of the QEA environment, and improve the navigation efficiency and immersion of users in the virtual environment, this study used adaptive strategies, ROS, and RVDS to optimize the path planning ability of the A* algorithm. At the same time, by combining UE4 model construction technology, high-precision modeling and rendering of the QEA environment have been achieved, and immersive experiences have been provided through VR and AR technologies. In the experiment, the improved A* algorithm outperformed other algorithms in three key indicators: navigation accuracy, navigation error, and path length optimization rate. The navigation accuracy was as high as 95.3% and 96.3%, with the smallest navigation error (1.5 m and 1.2 m), and the path length optimization rate was close to the theoretical optimum (0.98). This indicated that improving the A* algorithm could provide users with the closest and most optimized navigation path to the target location, reducing the sense of confusion and navigation distance. This is consistent with the results of Shi K et al. It optimizes the A* algorithm to achieve good path planning capabilities in static and dynamic conditions36. For rendering efficiency, the improved A* performed the best in both rendering time and memory usage, with the shortest rendering time (0.65 s and 0.60 s) and the lowest memory usage. The improved A * algorithm could quickly complete scene rendering and effectively reduce resource consumption, providing a smoother visual experience. After improving the A* algorithm, Han C et al. significantly enhanced its path planning ability. Unlike the research results, its memory usage is relatively high. The possible reason is the introduction of more complex data structures to store additional information. The A* algorithm optimized by Han C et al. stores redundant information. This redundant information, although not directly affecting the correctness of the algorithm, will occupy additional memory37. Finally, in terms of immersion in the QEG environment, the improved A* algorithm performs the best in both user dwell time and operation frequency metrics. The user stays the longest and operates the most frequently. This indicates that improving the A* algorithm can significantly enhance user engagement and interest, and increase user immersion. In conclusion, the improved A* algorithm plays a crucial role in the cultural heritage preservation of QEA by enhancing the user experience in the virtual environment. By providing efficient and accurate path planning, the algorithm ensures that users can navigate through the virtual environment in a way that highlights the unique architectural features and cultural significance of QEA. For example, the algorithm can guide users through the narrow alleys and around the watchtowers, allowing them to fully appreciate the architectural ingenuity and cultural significance of these structures. This enhanced navigation capability is essential for creating an immersive experience that can effectively promote the protection and inheritance of QEA culture. Moreover, the improved A* algorithm’s ability to enhance user engagement and immersion in the virtual QEA environment is a significant contribution to the cultural heritage preservation efforts. This immersive experience not only enhances user satisfaction but also promotes a deeper understanding and appreciation of Qiang culture, thereby contributing to its protection and inheritance.

Although the improved A* algorithm performs well in the simulation environment, it still faces some challenges and limitations in actual deployment, especially in dynamic or outdoor environments. In a dynamic environment, the positions and distributions of obstacles may change in real-time, which poses higher requirements for path planning algorithms. Although the improved A* algorithm performs well in the static environment, its adaptability in the dynamic environment needs to be further verified. The specific algorithm needs to be able to perceive environmental changes in real-time and update the path planning. Meanwhile, in some dynamic environments, the movement of obstacles has a certain regularity. This algorithm can make use of these rules to predict and adjust the path in advance. Path planning in outdoor environments faces multiple challenges, including complex terrain, variable weather conditions, and irregular obstacle distribution. The applicability of the improved A* algorithm in these environments still needs further exploration, such as in outdoor environments with complex and diverse terrain (such as mountains, rivers, forests, etc.). When dealing with these complex terrains, the improved A* algorithm needs to combine terrain data for path planning to ensure the feasibility and safety of the path. Meanwhile, changes in weather conditions (such as rain, snow, and fog) may affect the performance of the sensor and the accuracy of path planning. The improved A* algorithm needs to be adjusted in combination with weather data to enhance its adaptability under adverse weather conditions. In actual deployment, sensors may have errors or malfunctions, which may affect the accuracy of path planning. The improved A* algorithm needs to combine sensor calibration and data fusion techniques to enhance the robustness of path planning. Meanwhile, users may need to interact with the path planning system in real-time, such as adjusting TPs or avoiding specific areas. The improved A* algorithm needs to support this real-time interaction to enhance the user experience. For the update of dynamic obstacles, sensors can be integrated to monitor changes in the environment in real time. At the same time, probabilistic models can be introduced to handle the uncertainties in obstacle detection. For instance, if an obstacle is detected with a certain probability, the algorithm can adjust the path accordingly. In addition, it is necessary to ensure that the algorithm is robust to false alarms and missed alarms in obstacle detection, which can be achieved by combining multiple sensor inputs and using data fusion technology.

To evaluate the feasibility of deploying the improved A* algorithm on consumer-grade VR/AR hardware, the performance of the system under different hardware configurations is tested in detail. The test environment involves conducting tests on a variety of consumer-grade VR/AR devices, including HTC Vive Pro, Oculus Rift S, and Valve Index. Furthermore, the PC end includes high-end configurations (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080, Intel Core i7-12700 K) and mid-range configurations (NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660 Ti, Intel Core i5-9400 F). Meanwhile, the LOD technology is used to automatically switch the model detail levels according to the camera distance, reducing the rendering burden. In addition, by reducing the number of triangular faces of the model and optimizing the texture mapping, the rendering efficiency has been further improved. In terms of hardware compatibility, adaptive strategies ensure optimal performance and compatibility of the system under different hardware configurations by automatically detecting hardware performance and dynamically adjusting the system’s rendering settings.

In summary, this study significantly improves the navigation efficiency and immersion in the virtual construction of the QEA environment by improving the A* algorithm. The improved A* algorithm performs well in multiple key indicators such as interaction response time, navigation ability, rendering efficiency, and immersion, providing users in virtual environments with a more efficient, smooth, and immersive experience. This study provides strong technical support for virtual display and cultural inheritance in the QEA environment. Although the improved A* algorithm performs well in multiple aspects, its adaptability in complex dynamic environments (such as scenes with real-time changes in obstacles) still needs further validation, especially for the real-time and flexibility of path planning, which may have limitations. Future research will explore more advanced algorithm optimization strategies to improve the real-time performance and adaptability of path planning in dynamic environments. At the same time, artificial intelligence technology can be combined to further enhance the interactive experience and intelligence level of virtual environments, providing users with a more natural and immersive virtual experience.

Blowing-up solutions of Klein-Gordon equations with gauge variant semilinear terms in Friedmann-Lemaítre-Robertson-Walker spacetimes under finite speed of propagation
Makoto NAKAMURA, Takuma YOSHIZUMI

Abstract
Blowing-up solutions of Klein-Gordon equations with gauge variant semilinear terms are considered in Friedmann-Lemaítre-Robertson-Walker spacetimes. Effects of spatial expansion or contraction on the solutions are studied through the scale-function and the curved mass.

Introduction

We show some blowing-up solutions of Klein-Gordon equations with gauge-variant semilinear terms in Friedmann-Lemaítre-Robertson-Walker spacetimes (FLRW spacetimes for short).
FLRW spacetimes are solutions of the Einstein equations
with the cosmological constant under the cosmological principle.
They describe the spatial expansion or contraction,
and yield some important models of the universe.
Let
n\geq 1
be the spatial dimension,

a(\cdot)>0
be a scale-function defined on an interval
[0,T_{0})
for some
0<T_{0}\leq\infty
,
and let
c>0
be the speed of light.
The metrics
\{g_{\alpha\beta}\}
of FLRW spacetimes are expressed by

\sum_{0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}g_{\alpha\beta}dx^{\alpha}dx^{\beta}:=-c^{2}(dt)^{2}+a^{2}(t)\sum_{j=1}^{n}(dx^{j})^{2}
,
where we have put the spatial curvature as zero,
and
x^{0}=t
is the time-variable
(see e.g.,

[
3
,
4
]
).
When
a
is a positive constant,
the spacetime
reduces to the Minkowski spacetime.

We denote the first and second derivatives of one variable function
a
by
\dot{a}
and
\ddot{a}
.
The Klein-Gordon equation generated by the above metric

(g_{\alpha\beta})_{0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
is given by

(\sqrt{|g|})^{-1}\partial_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}v\right)=m^{2}v+f(v)

for the determinant
g:=\mbox{det}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\right)

and the inverse matrix
\left(g^{\alpha\beta}\right)
,
i.e.,

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}v+\frac{n\dot{a}}{c^{2}a}\partial_{t}v-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Delta v+m^{2}v+f(v)=0,

(1.1)

where

m
denotes the mass,

\Delta:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}^{2}
denotes the Laplacian,
and
f
denotes a force term.
This equation is rewritten as

c^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}u-a^{-2}\Delta u+M^{2}u+a^{n/2}f(a^{-n/2}u)=0

(1.2)

by the transformation
u:=va^{n/2}
,
where the function
M
is called curved mass defined by

M^{2}=m^{2}-\frac{n(n-2)}{4c^{2}}\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}-\frac{n}{2c^{2}}\cdot\frac{\ddot{a}}{a},\ \ \ \ M:=\sqrt{M^{2}}.

(1.3)

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem in the gauge-variant case
f(v)=\lambda|v|^{p}
in
(
1.2
), namely,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(c^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}-a^{-2}(t)\Delta+M^{2}(t))u(t,x)-\lambda a^{-n(p-1)/2}(t)|u|^{p}(t,x)=0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot),\ \ \partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)=u_{1}(\cdot)\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

for
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
0<T\leq T_{0}
,
where

u_{0}
and
u_{1}
are given initial data.
We expect some dissipative effects when
\dot{a}>0
in (
1.1
)
from the term
n\dot{a}\partial_{t}v/c^{2}a
.
Actually, the spatial expansion yields dissipative effects in energy estimates for the Klein-Gordon equation
(see

[
10
,
12
]
).

Blowing-up solutions for (
1.4
) have been considered in

[
11
, Proposition 1]
and

[
15
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]

in the de Sitter spacetime.
The aim of this paper is to extend these results into general FLRW spacetimes including concrete examples of scale functions of Big-Rip and Big-Crunch.
We consider the real and purely imaginary mass
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup i{\mathbb{R}}

since
m\in i{\mathbb{R}}
plays an important role when we consider the breakdown of symmetry of potential well, or in
\phi^{4}
-theory in physics
(see
[
11
]
).
We refer to
[
14
]
and the references therein
on blowing-up solutions for small initial data in FLRW spacetimes
for gauge-variant semilinear terms and vanishing mass
m=0
, i.e., wave equation,
under some conditions on
p
concerned with the Strauss conjecture
(see also
[
6
]
in the Einstein-de Sitter spacetime),
while our results are for relatively large data and arbitrary
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup i{\mathbb{R}}
and
1<p<\infty
.

The case of the gauge invariant semilinear term of the form

f(u)=\lambda|v|^{p-1}v
in (
1.2
)
has been considered by McCollum, Mwamba and Oliver in

[
9
, Theorem 1]
for
\dot{a}>0
,
and in
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
for
\dot{a}\leq 0
by different techniques from this paper to show the blowing-up of the norm
\|u\|_{2}
,
while the blowing-up of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)dx
is shown in this paper.

The Cauchy problem of (
1.4
) including gauge invariant semilinear term is widely studied especially on local and global solutions, and their asymptotic behaviors
(see

[
2
,
5
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
16
]
for closely related results).
We are focused on blowing-up solutions in this paper.

,
i.e., purely imaginary curved mass

,

For any function

.

(Blowing-up solutions)
.

Let
T_{0}
,
a
and
M

0<T_{0}\leq\infty,\ \ a\in C^{2}([0,T_{0}),(0,\infty)),\ \ M^{2}\in C([0,T_{0}),{\mathbb{R}}).

(1.11)

Let

,

,

0<\varepsilon<1
,

,

.
Let
\mbox{\rm supp}\ u_{0}\cup\mbox{\rm supp}\ u_{1}\subset\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}};\ |x|\leq r_{0}\}
.
Let
N
be any real number which satisfies

N\geq 0,\ \ \ \ N^{2}+\inf_{0<t<T_{0}}M^{2}(t)\geq 0.

(1.12)

Let
r
and
q
be defined by (
1.9
).
Assume
q
is a monotone function, and
\widetilde{q}
defined by
(
1.10
) satisfies

A:=\inf_{0<t<T_{0}}\frac{e^{cN(1-\varepsilon)t}}{\widetilde{q}^{n/2}(t)}>0,\ \ B:=\sup_{0<t<T_{0}}\frac{\widetilde{q}^{n/2}(t)\left\{N^{2}+M^{2}(t)\right\}^{1/(p-1)}}{e^{cNt}}<\infty.

(1.13)

Put

w_{0}:=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)dx,\ \ w_{1}:=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx.

We prepare the following fundamental result on the positivity of the solution of an ordinary differential equation of second order.

Lemma 2.2
.

d\sigma
denotes the measure on
\Lambda_{s}
,
and
\nu
denotes the unit outer normal vector on
\Lambda_{s}
,
we have put

r=|x|,\ \ \nu:=\frac{(1,-x\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)/r)}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)|^{2}}},\ \ J_{s}:=\int_{\Lambda_{s}}\frac{e^{0}}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)|^{2}}}d\sigma

and we have used
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
on
B
and

(e^{0},\ldots,e^{n})\cdot\nu\geq\frac{(1-\theta(t_{1}))e^{0}}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)|^{2}}}.

On the other hand, since we have

|h(t,x,u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)|\lesssim N(|u|+|\nabla_{g}u|)

by
h(t,x,0,0,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)=0
, we have

|\partial_{t}uh(t,x,u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)|\lesssim cN(e^{0}+|u|^{2})

(5.7)

for
(t,x)\in D
,
where we have put

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{(t,x)\in D}\left\{|u(t,x)|,|\nabla_{g}u(t,x)|,|\nabla_{g}^{2}u(t,x)|\right\},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x)\in D\\
|y|,|z|,|w|\leq M\end{subarray}}\left|\nabla_{y,z}h(t,x,y,z,w)\right|.

By
u_{0}=0
and
u(t,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}u(\tau,\cdot)d\tau
on
D
,
we have

\iint_{D_{s}}|u|^{2}dxdt=\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\phi_{s}(|x|)}|u(t,x)|^{2}dtdx\leq\frac{c^{2}t_{1}^{2}}{2}\iint_{D_{s}}e^{0}dxd\tau.

(5.8)

So that, we have

\iint_{D_{s}}|\partial_{t}uh|dxdt\lesssim c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})N\iint_{D_{s}}e^{0}dxd\tau\lesssim c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})N\int_{0}^{s}J_{\tau}d\tau,

(5.9)

where we have used (
5.4
) for the last inequality.

By (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.9
),
we obtain

J_{s}\lesssim\frac{c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})N}{1-\theta(t_{1})}\int_{0}^{s}J_{\tau}d\tau

for
0\leq s\leq t_{1}
,
which shows
J_{s}=0
for
0\leq s\leq t_{1}
by the Gronwall inequality.
Therefore,
u=0
holds on
D_{t_{1}}
by
u_{0}=0
on
B
.
Since
t_{1}
is arbitrary for
0<t_{1}<t_{0}
, we obtain
u=0
on
D
.

(2)
Next, we consider the case
\dot{a}\leq 0
.
Since the proof is similar to the case
\dot{a}\geq 0
,
we show its outline.
Multiplying
a^{2}\overline{\partial_{t}u}
to the both sides of the differential equation in (
5.1
),
we have

\partial_{t}f^{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}f^{j}+f^{n+1}+2a^{2}\operatorname{Re}\left(\overline{\partial_{t}u}h\right)=0,

where

f^{0}:=c^{-2}a^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}
,

(f^{1},\ldots,f^{n}):=-2\operatorname{Re}\left(\overline{\partial_{t}u}\nabla u\right)
,

f^{n+1}:=-2c^{-2}a\dot{a}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}
.
Integrating the both sides of this form on
D_{s}
,
we have

K_{s}+\iint_{D_{s}}f^{n+1}dxdt+2\operatorname{Re}\iint_{D_{s}}a^{2}\overline{\partial_{t}u}hdxdt=0,

where

K_{s}:=\iint_{D_{s}}\partial_{t}f^{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}f^{j}dxdt,

which and the assumption
\dot{a}\leq 0
yield
f^{n+1}\geq 0
and

K_{s}\leq 2\iint_{D_{s}}a^{2}|\partial_{t}uh|dxdt.

(5.10)

We estimates the both sides in (
5.10
) separately.
We have

K_{s}=\int_{\Lambda_{s}}(f^{0},\ldots,f^{n})\cdot\nu d\sigma\geq\left\{1-\theta(t_{1})\right\}L_{s},

(5.11)

where

L_{s}:=\int_{\Lambda_{s}}\frac{f^{0}}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)|^{2}}}d\sigma

and we have used

(f^{0},\ldots,f^{n})\cdot\nu\geq\frac{(1-\theta(t_{1}))f^{0}}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{r}\phi_{s}(r)|^{2}}}.

On the other hand, we have

a^{2}|\partial_{t}uh(t,x,u,\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}^{2}u)|\lesssim cN(f^{0}+a^{2}|u|^{2}),

similarly to (
5.7
).
Since we have

a^{2}(t)|u(t,\cdot)|^{2}\leq ta^{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\cdot)|^{2}d\tau\leq t\int_{0}^{t}a^{2}(\tau)|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\cdot)|^{2}d\tau

by the monotonicity
a(t)\leq a(\tau)
for
0\leq\tau\leq t
,
we obtain

\int_{0}^{\phi_{s}(r)}a^{2}(t)|u(t,\cdot)|^{2}dt\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\phi_{s}(r)}a^{2}(\tau)|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\cdot)|^{2}d\tau.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{s}}a^{2}|u|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{0}^{\phi_{s}(|x|)}a^{2}(t)|u(t,x)|^{2}dtdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}}{2}\iint_{D_{s}}a^{2}(\tau)|\partial_{t}u(\tau,x)|^{2}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}t_{1}^{2}}{2}\iint_{D_{s}}f^{0}dxd\tau

similarly to (
5.8
).
So that, we have

\iint_{D_{s}}a^{2}|\partial_{t}uh|dxdt\lesssim c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})N\iint_{D_{s}}f^{0}dxd\tau\lesssim\frac{c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})Na_{0}}{a(t_{1})}\int_{0}^{s}L_{\tau}d\tau,

(5.12)

where we have used (
5.4
) for the last inequality.

By (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and (
5.12
),
we obtain

L_{s}\leq\frac{c(1+c^{2}t_{1}^{2})Na_{0}}{\left\{1-\theta(t_{1})\right\}a(t_{1})}\int_{0}^{s}L_{\tau}d\tau

for
0\leq s\leq t_{1}
.
We obtain
u=0
on
D
similarly to the case
\dot{a}\leq 0
.
∎

We have the following result immediately from Lemma
5.1
,
which shows the finite speed of propagation of (
5.1
).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
0<T\leq T_{0}
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
R>0
.
Put
B_{R}(x_{0}):=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}};\ |x-x_{0}|\leq R\}
and

D_{R}(T,x_{0}):=\left\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}};\ |x-x_{0}|\leq R+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c}{a(\tau)}d\tau\right\}.

The solution
u\in C^{2}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}},{\mathbb{C}})
of (
5.1
) with

\mbox{\rm supp}\ u_{0}\cup\mbox{\rm supp}\ u_{1}\subset B_{R}(x_{0})

satisfies
u=0
on

[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash D_{R}(T,x_{0})
.

Proof.

For any
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
outside of
B_{R}(x_{0})
,
we have
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
on
B_{\delta}(x_{1})
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
We have
u=0
inside of the backward cone whose bottom is
B_{\delta}(x_{1})
by Lemma
5.1
.
Since
x_{1}
is arbitrary, we have
u=0
outside of
D_{R}(T,x_{0})
.
∎

Comment on: “Preliminary research on the effectiveness and safety of metal clip-assisted endoscopic variceal ligation in the treatment of gastric varices”
Authors: Jake Krige, Sandie Thomson
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Yi et al. have contributed an important technical innovation to the evolving endoscopic management of gastric varices. 1 Their report on metal clip–assisted endoscopic variceal ligation (MEVL) is commendable for its ingenuity and clinical application. Nevertheless, several methodological limitations warrant careful scrutiny before MEVL can be credibly advanced toward routine clinical adoption.

Confounding factors: The marked temporal imbalance between treatment eras, coupled with reliance on a single operator, introduces inseparable secular trend and learning curve confounding. Adjusted models incorporating explicit era covariates (e.g., year or quarter) are required to untangle these effects. Moreover, temporal plots of supportive care metrics, such as antibiotic prophylaxis and somatostatin dosing, would provide essential context for interpreting outcomes.

Incomplete reporting of the defining intervention: By definition, MEVL entails pre-ligation feeder vessel hemoclip placement. Yet the procedural record omits the precise count and anatomical distribution of these clips. Itemized documentation of clip deployment, procedural duration, and intraprocedural challenges is indispensable for reproducibility and mechanistic interpretation. Without such detail, the technique cannot be reliably replicated or critically appraised. Outcome assessment and potential observer bias: Eradication was assessed by routine care endoscopy unblinded to treatment allocation, raising the risk of ascertainment bias. Independent blinded adjudication, whether performed contemporaneously or retrospectively, should be reported. Inter-rater reliability metrics and sensitivity analyses using more objective endpoints (e.g., need for rescue therapy, transfusion requirements) would substantiate the eradication findings and mitigate observer bias.

External validity: The comparative framework is restricted to traditional ligation, yet MEVL must be situated alongside established modalities such as cyanoacrylate injection and endosonographic therapies. Recent systematic reviews of clip-assisted cyanoacrylate injection underscore the need for contextualization. 2 Without this, MEVL’s clinical niche remains undefined, particularly in patients with underlying gastrorenal shunts. 3

Furthermore, exclusion of patients requiring urgent endoscopy limits generalizability. Clarification of the intended clinical role for MEVL, and a framework for comparative evaluation against existing therapies, is essential. We believe that addressing these methodological refinements will not only add scientific rigor and robustness to their study but will also provide clearer guidance for future research in this vital area. Until these concerns are resolved, the present findings should be regarded as hypothesis generating rather than support for routine adoption of MEVL.

Yi et al. are to be commended on their scholarly contribution and we look forward to the authors’ response and future prospective multicenter validation of MEVL’s independent effect.

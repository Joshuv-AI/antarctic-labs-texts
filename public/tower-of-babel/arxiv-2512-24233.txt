Sound Wave in the Backreaction Affected Spacetime in Analogue Gravity Based on Number-Conserving Approach Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sang-Shin Baak

Abstract
It is shown that the sound wave in the backreaction affected dynamical spacetime follows the equations for a massive scalar field in a analogue spacetime using number-conserving approach. Even with backreaction, the analogue metric is in the same form to the case without backreaction. The sound velocity, fluid density, and fluid velocity are defined with small correction to include the backreaction effect. Moreover, the modification of classical fluid dynamical equations by the backreaction introduces spacetime dependent mass. For a finite-size homogeneous quasi-one dimensional Bose gas, we find that the backreaction increase the UV divergence of the equal position correlation function. Moreover, in this model, we see that the backreaction increase the correlation in a finite region and decrease the correlation in far region.

In analogue gravity, the effective spacetime is derived in the linear approximation. Therefore, one natural question is that the sound wave still reside in the effective spacetime when backreaction is considered. In this work, using Number conserving Bogoliubov (NCB) expansion, we show that the equation of motion for the sound wave is massive Klein-Gordon equation with spacetime dependent mass. The spacetime metric is determined with classical fluid variables not only with the density, the fluid velocity and the sound velocity, but also with the correction terms defined by the modification of continuity and Euler equation. II
Formalism

In this section, we review the number-conserving expansion. The equation of motion for
U(1)
-conserving order parameter and quantum fluctuation on it are shown. Treating order parameter as a classical fluid, we derive the continuity-like equation and Euler-like equation from the amended Gross-Pitaevskii equation. Then, by applying Madelung representation on the quantum field, we derive the equation of motion for the density and phase fluctuations. Finally, we derive the wave equation for the sound wave and show that it can be written in the form of massive Klein-Gordon equation.

II.1
Number-Conserving Expansion and Modified Classical Fluid Dynamics

The Hamiltonian for the field operator describing Bose particle in

\hat{\Psi}=\phi_{c}(1+\hat{\psi})\frac{\hat{A}}{\sqrt{\hat{N}}},

where
\hat{N}=\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}
is the total number of particle operator,
\phi_{c}=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})

(5)

\rho_{c}:=\phi_{c}^{*}\phi_{c},\qquad\vec{\jmath}_{c}:=\Im[\phi_{c}^{*}\nabla\phi_{c}].

describe the modification of Euler equation for the classical field by the existence of quantum fluctuation.

Using

\Delta_{\rm E}+\frac{i}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}=g\rho_{c}\Big(2\langle\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{\psi}^{2}\rangle\Big),

(13)

\displaystyle i\Big(\partial_{t}

(14)

Note that

II.2
Quantum Fluid and Sound Wave

Now, let us write the number-conserving extension in the Madelung representation,

\hat{\Psi}=\sqrt{\rho_{c}}\mathrm{e}^{i\theta_{c}}\Big(1+\frac{\delta\hat{\rho}}{2\rho_{c}}+i\delta\hat{\theta}\Big)\frac{\hat{A}}{\sqrt{\hat{N}}}.

Note that if we are in usual Bogoliubov (linear) regime, this is just the procedure to go to the analogue spacetime. Without loss of generality, we can set
\delta\hat{\rho},\delta\hat{\theta}
be Hermitian.

\displaystyle\delta\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle\coloneq 2\rho_{c}\Re[\hat{\psi}]=\rho_{c}(\hat{\psi}+\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}),

(15)

\displaystyle\delta\hat{\theta}

\displaystyle\coloneq\Im[\hat{\psi}]=\frac{1}{2i}(\hat{\psi}-\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}).

(16)

From the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equation (
II.1
) and using the continuity-like equation (
8
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}+\vec{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\delta\hat{\theta}

\displaystyle=-D\Big(\frac{\delta\hat{\rho}}{\rho_{c}}\Big)

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\Big(\partial_{t}+\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\delta\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{c}\nabla)+2\Delta_{\rm E}\Big)\delta\hat{\theta}

(18)

where in Eq. (
18
),
\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c}f=\nabla\cdot(\vec{v}_{c}f)
, and in Eq. (
17
)
D
is an operator defined as

D\coloneq g\rho_{c}-\frac{1}{4\rho_{c}}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{c}\nabla)-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm E}.

(19)

Using the (formal) inverse of this operator, we get the following wave equation:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\Big(\partial_{t}+\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\rho_{c}D^{-1}\Big(\partial_{t}+\vec{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\delta\hat{\theta}+\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{c}\nabla)+2\Delta_{\rm E}\Big)\delta\hat{\theta}=0

(20)

Note that the equation is the form of wave equation in linear theory with some additional terms. In analogue gravity, we are interested in the sound wave approximation or eikonal approximation. In both case, differential operator in
D
can be neglected or replaced by the wave number of quantum field
43
. From now, let us assume that the operator
D
is one of such approximation. Let us define the generalized sound velocity as
c^{2}=D
. Then, Eq. (
20
) can be written in the form:

\partial_{\mu}(f^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\hat{\theta})+V\delta\hat{\theta}=0.

(21)

where

f^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\rho_{c}}{c^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{{@{}c|c@{}}}-1&-v_{c}^{j}\\
\hline\cr-v_{c}^{i}&c^{2}\delta^{ij}-v_{c}^{i}v_{c}^{j}\end{array}\right).

(22)

and

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}(\partial_{t}+\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c})(\rho_{c}\Delta_{\rm C})+\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\rho_{c}\Delta_{\rm C}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\rho_{c}\Delta_{\rm E}.

(23)

Using continuity-like equation Eq. (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle V=-\frac{\rho_{c}}{2c^{2}}\Big(\partial_{t}+\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\Delta_{\rm C}+2\rho_{c}\Delta_{\rm E}.

(24)

For geometric formulation, let us identify

f^{\mu\nu}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\,\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}

(25)

then

\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}=\Big(\frac{\rho_{c}^{d}}{c^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{d-2}}.

(26)

Using this, we obtain

\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Big(\frac{\rho_{c}}{c}\Big)^{\frac{2}{d-1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{{@{}c|c@{}}}-(c^{2}-v_{c}^{2})&-v_{c}^{j}\\
\hline\cr-v_{c}^{i}&\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right).

(27)

and finally, the Eq. (
21
) can be written in the form of massive Klein-Gordon equation:

\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu})+m^{2}\Big)\delta\hat{\theta}=0,

(28)

where

\displaystyle m^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneq\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}}V(t,\vec{x})

(29)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{c^{2}}{\rho_{c}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{d-2}}\Big[-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\Big(\partial_{t}+\nabla\cdot\vec{v}_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\rm C}\Big)\Delta_{\rm C}+2\Delta_{\rm E}\Big].

(30)

is the spacetime dependent mass square. Since
\Delta_{\rm C}
and
\Delta_{\rm E}
are determined by the classical fluid variables, metric and mass can also be written with classical fluid only.

III
The Model

In the previous section, we showed that the sound wave in the dynamical spacetime affected by the backreaction follows massive Klein-Gordon equation. Moreover, the backreaction effect appear as
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
deviation from the massless Klein-Gordon equation derived without backreaction.
Recently, the experimental observation of velocity field correlation in a one-dimensional quasi-condensate has been measured
39
. Motivated from this, in this section, using a finite-size homogeneous one-dimensional gas as an example, we compare the correlation function of sound wave in the linear theory without backreaction and dynamical background with backreaction. Since the backreaction effect accumulate as the time evolution, the difference of correlation function becomes more significant as system evolves.

IV
conclusion

Adopting number-conserving expansion, we show that the sound wave in the analogue spacetime behaves like massive scalar particle in a curved spacetime. The result can be used for finding how the spacetime geometry modified by the backreaction in analogue system.

Using quasi-one-dimensional homogeneous finite-size Bose-Einstein condensates as a specific example, we calculated the mode function and correlation function in the backreacted spacetime. We find that the backreaction increase the UV divergence in the correlation function at the same point. We also find that backreaction increase the correlation in close region but reduce the correlation in the far region in this specific model. The method used in this work can be used for calculating the mode function and correlation function in other model using number-conserving approach.

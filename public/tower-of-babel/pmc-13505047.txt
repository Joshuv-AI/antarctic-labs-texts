Age-dependent cerebral oxygenation and brain injury in pediatric cardiac surgery: vulnerability of infants aged ≤6 months
Authors: Zeng-chun Wang, Ling-Shan Yu, Qi-Liang Zhang, Ye Chen, Dan-wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite the growing use of regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rScO2) monitoring in pediatric cardiac surgery, whether age affects perioperative rScO2 trajectories or modifies the association between rScO2 and organ injury remains unclear. In this study, age-specific rScO2 patterns were delineated, and whether age modified the association between rScO2 nadir and postoperative neurological and myocardial injury biomarkers was evaluated.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included 104 children who underwent congenital heart surgery and whose ages were ≤6 months (n=47), 6–12 months (n=28), and >12 months (n=29). Perioperative rScO2 was continuously monitored at five time points: pre-cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), CPB, aortic cross-clamp (ACC), post-return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC), and postoperative day 2 (POD2). Linear mixed-effects models with age-group-by-time interactions were used to test differences in rScO2 trajectory between age groups. Multivariable linear regression was used to assess the association between the rScO2 nadir and peak biomarkers levels (NSE, GFAP, S100β, cTnI, CK-MB, NT-proBNP), with age interactions included, followed by age-stratified analyses.ResultsPerioperative rScO2 followed a U-shaped pattern across all age groups, with the ≤6-month group consistently exhibiting the lowest levels. Compared with infants >12 months, those ≤6 months exhibited greater decreases in rScO2 during both CPB (β>12-month*CPB = 4.17, 95% CI [0.47, 7.87], P = 0.027) and ACC (β>12-month*ACC = 4.75, 95% CI [1.05, 8.45], P = 0.012). Significant age interactions for the rScO2 nadir–NSE association were observed for the 6–12 months group (β = 0.41, 95% CI [0.05, 0.77], P=0.027) and the >12-month group (β = 0.43, 95% CI [0.01, 0.84], P=0.044) relative to the ≤6-month reference. In age-stratified analyses, the rScO2 nadir was significantly inversely associated with peak NSE only in the ≤6-month group (β = −0.27, 95% CI [−0.48, −0.07], P=0.001). No significant associations were found between the rScO2 nadir and other neurological biomarkers (GFAP, S100β) or any myocardial biomarkers (CK-MB, cTnI, NT-proBNP) across age groups.ConclusionsAge affects both rScO2 trajectories and their association with peak NSE. Infants ≤6 months exhibited the lowest rScO2 values and a significant inverse association between the rScO2 nadir and peak NSE, supporting the need for more intensive monitoring in this subgroup.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40560-026-00934-y.

Background

Congenital heart disease (CHD) is a leading cause of neonatal and infant mortality, and many affected infants require surgical correction under cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) early in life [1, 2]. Although surgical and perioperative advances have markedly improved survival, neurodevelopmental impairments remain a major concern with perioperative brain injury as a key risk factor [3–6]. Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) enables noninvasive monitoring of regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rScO2), although its neurological benefit is uncertain despite its widespread use [7–10].

Despite these advances, two age-related knowledge gaps remain inadequately addressed. First, cerebral autoregulation is immature in young infants, especially those with critical CHD, and the additional stress of surgery may further modulate rScO2 trajectories in an age-dependent manner [11–13]. Evidence has shown that the cerebral oxygen half-life during deep hypothermic circulatory arrest varies with age and that age affects both baseline cerebral oxygenation and its physiological correlates, including blood pressure and haemodynamics [14–16]. Second, whether age affects the association between intraoperative cerebral desaturation and postoperative organ injury remains unexplored [10, 17]. Infants are uniquely vulnerable because of their immature autoregulation and high metabolic demand [11, 13, 18]. Neurological biomarkers can track brain injury and predict outcomes [19, 20], and myocardial injury reflected by low cardiac output syndrome is correlated with cerebral desaturation [21, 22]. Despite these separate lines of evidence, whether age influences the association between rScO2 and a combined set of neurological and myocardial biomarkers remains unclear.

Therefore, using a prospective cohort of children undergoing congenital heart surgery, we aimed to (1) characterize perioperative rScO2 trajectories across age groups and (2) assess whether age affects the association between intraoperative rScO2 and postoperative biomarker profiles of neurological and myocardial injury to inform age-specific perioperative management.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This prospective cohort study was conducted at a single tertiary children’s hospital. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Children’s Hospital (approval No. 2023ETKL05087). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant’s parents or legal guardians at enrollment.

Study population

We screened children with CHD who underwent cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) from October 2024 to November 2025. Patients were included if they (1) were aged < 18 years, (2) had a confirmed diagnosis of CHD, and (3) underwent elective cardiac surgery with CPB. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) severe preoperative comorbidities, including hepatic or renal failure, (2) preexisting neurological or intracranial conditions (e.g., intracranial infection or epilepsy); (3) preoperative cardiopulmonary resuscitation; and (4) death within 48 h post-operatively (predominantly attributed to postoperative low cardiac output syndrome). Ultimately, 104 eligible children were included in the final analysis.

Data collection and variable definitions

All data were retrospectively extracted from the hospital’s electronic medical record system using a standardized case report form. The following variables were recorded: (1) demographic and baseline characteristics—age (months, further categorized into ≤6 months, 6–12 months, and >12 months), sex, height (cm), weight (kg), primary diagnosis, and surgical procedure; (2) surgical complexity—Risk Adjustment for Congenital Heart Surgery (RACHS-1) score; (3) intraoperative variables—surgery duration (min), CPB time (min), aortic cross-clamp (ACC) time (min), and anesthesia duration (min); and (4) postoperative outcomes—duration of mechanical ventilation (hours), intensive care unit (ICU) stay (days), total hospital stay (days), and postoperative hospital stay (days). The collection and definitions of cerebral oxygenation and organ injury biomarkers are described in the corresponding sections below.

CPB management

Key parameters during CPB were standardized according to our institutional protocol. The CPB circuit was primed with a balanced electrolyte solution and supplemented with leukocyte-depleted packed red blood cells as needed to achieve a target hematocrit of 25–30%. The target temperature was set at 32–34 °C and adjusted to 28–30 °C for few patients who required antegrade cerebral perfusion under hypothermia; however, none of the patients required deep hypothermic circulatory arrest. A hybrid α-stat/pH–stat strategy was employed during cooling and rewarming. Pump flow rates were set at 150–200 mL/(kg·min) for infants weighing <10 kg and 100–150 mL/(kg·min) for children weighing 10–15 kg. The target mean arterial perfusion pressure was 20–40 mmHg for infants and 30–50 mmHg for children. Arterial blood gases and lactate levels were monitored continuously, with target values of PaCO2 35–45 mmHg, PaO2 150–250 mmHg, and pH 7.35–7.45. Conventional ultrafiltration was applied during CPB, and modified ultrafiltration was performed after all patients were weaned from bypass. All procedures were performed by a dedicated pediatric cardiac surgical and perfusion team.

Assessment of perioperative rScO2 dynamics across age groups

Perioperative rScO2 was measured using an INVOS™ 5100 C (Medtronic) cerebral oximeter at five time points: (1) before CPB (pre-CPB, baseline), (2) during CPB, (3) during ACC, (4) after return of spontaneous circulation (post-ROSC), and (5) on postoperative day 2 (POD 2). The values at each time point were described and compared among the three age groups. To test whether the perioperative rScO2 trajectories differed significantly across age groups (i.e., whether age modified the temporal pattern of rScO2), a linear mixed model with rScO2 as the dependent variable was used to test the interaction between age group and time point, with a random intercept for each patient.

Assessment of the association between rScO2 and organ injury biomarkers

Blood samples for biomarker measurement were collected at four perioperative time points: preoperatively, on the day of surgery after procedure completion (postoperative day 0, POD0), POD1, and POD2.

The intraoperative cerebral oxygenation burden was quantified as the lowest rScO2 value recorded during surgery (the minimum value among those of the CPB, ACC, and post-ROSC, hereafter referred to as rScO2 nadir). The primary outcome measures were the postoperative peak levels of three neurological biomarkers—glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and S100 calcium-binding protein β (S100β)—which were defined as the maximum values across POD0, POD1, and POD2. We used these circulating biomarkers as indicators of brain injury in the absence of imaging data. As an exploratory analysis, the association of the rScO2 nadir with three myocardial injury biomarkers—creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), cardiac troponin I (cTnI), and N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP)—were also examined.

Statistical analysis

The analysis was conducted in two main parts: (1) comparison of perioperative rScO2 levels and trajectories among age groups and (2) assessment of whether age affects the association between rScO2 and postoperative organ injury biomarker levels.

Patients were divided into three age groups: ≤6 months, 6–12 months, and >12 months. rScO2 at each time point was compared among groups using the Kruskal‒Wallis test with post‑hoc Dunn’s test (Bonferroni correction). To evaluate whether the perioperative rScO2 trajectories differed significantly across age groups, a linear mixed model (LMM) was constructed with rScO2 as the dependent variable. Fixed effects included age group (three levels), time point (five levels), and their interaction, along with a random intercept for each patient to account for within-individual correlation. The model was adjusted for sex, weight, RACHS-1, and cyanotic CHD. Time was treated as a categorical factor to avoid imposing arbitrary linear assumptions, as exact intervals between measurements were not recorded. Model assumptions for the LMM (normality, homoscedasticity, influential observations, and multicollinearity) were assessed using standard diagnostic tools (Q‒Q plots, residuals-vs.-fit plots, Cook's distance, and variance inflation factor [VIF]), and all assumptions were satisfied. To describe the shape of the rScO2 trajectory, quadratic mixed‑effects models were fitted separately for each age group, with a random intercept for patient ID and fixed effects for the linear (time_c) and quadratic (time_c2) terms of time (centered at ACC). A significant quadratic term (P < 0.05) indicated a U‑shaped (decline‑then‑recovery) pattern. The likelihood ratio test was used to compare models with and without age-by-time interaction terms to confirm the difference in the three trajectory curves.

To explore whether age modified the association between rScO2 and biomarkers, multivariable linear regression (MLR) models were constructed with the peak value of each biomarker as the dependent variable adjusted for sex, weight, RACHS‑1, cyanotic CHD and CPB duration. Two models were fitted: a main‑effects model including the rScO2 nadir and age group and an interaction model with the product terms added between the rScO2 nadir and age group. Diagnostic procedures were also applied to the MLR model, and all assumptions were satisfied. For multiplicity, the Holm correction was applied to the three primary neurological biomarkers to control the family-wise error rate (FWER); interaction tests and age-stratified analyses were exploratory, with nominal P values reported without adjustment. Due to sample size limitations, the stratified analyses by age group were conducted without covariate adjustment and were intended as exploratory only.

Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of our findings: (1) Age was treated as a continuous variable to examine the evolution of rScO2 across perioperative time points; a linear mixed model was fitted with log‑transformed age, time point, and their interaction. Additionally, in the analysis of the association between the rScO2 nadir and neurological biomarkers, an interaction term between log‑age and the rScO2 nadir was added. (2) The rScO2 nadir was replaced by the percentage decline from pre‑CPB baseline, and the association with biomarkers was re‑examined.

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.0). A two‑tailed P‑value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics and clinical features

A total of 104 patients were included and divided into three age groups (Table 1): ≤6 months (n=47), 6–12 months (n=28), and >12 months (n=29). No significant difference in sex distribution was observed across age groups (42.3% males, P=0.913). Height and weight increased progressively with age (both P<0.001). The proportions of complex and cyanotic CHD were 19.2% and 9.6%, respectively, with no significant differences across age groups (P= 0.923 and 0.602, respectively). The distribution of RACHS‑1 grades did not differ significantly across age groups (P= 0.110), with grade 2 being the most common (overall 71.2%). No significant differences were found in the duration of surgery (P=0.107), duration of CPB (P=0.776), or duration of anesthesia (P=0.083). However, the duration of ACC differed significantly across age groups (P=0.030), with the longest median duration in the ≤6-month group (31.0 min) and the shortest duration in the >12-month group (21.0 min). The duration of postoperative mechanical ventilation and ICU stay, significantly decreased with increasing age, while patients aged 6–12 months had the shortest hospital stay (all P<0.001). Median changes in perioperative rScO2 levels across different age groups

The median rScO2 at each perioperative time point by age group is presented in Table 2 and Supplemental Figure S1. Compared with pre-CPB values (median 71.5%), the rScO2 values decreased markedly during the CPB and ACC (both median 60.0%) in all patients, and largely returned to baseline by POD2 (median 69.0%). With the exception of the pre-CPB time, the rScO2 levels significantly differed across age groups. Dunn’s post hoc test (Supplemental Table S1) indicated that the <6 months group had significantly lower rScO2 than both the >12 months group during CPB and ACC (Padj = 0.022 and Padj = 0.002, respectively) and the 6–12 months group during ACC time and POD2 (Padj = 0.028 and Padj = 0.017, respectively). No significant difference in median rScO2 was obersevd between the 6–12 month and >12 month groups (all Padj >0.05). Trajectory of rScO2 during the perioperative period among different age groups

On the basis of the LMM (adjusted for weight, sex, RACHS-1, and cyanotic CHD), compared with the pre‑CPB baseline, rScO2 decreased significantly during CPB (β = − 11.45, 95% CI [− 13.73, − 9.16], P < 0.001) and ACC (β = − 13.30, 95% CI[− 15.58, − 11.01], P < 0.001), with partial recovery by POD2 (β = − 2.32, 95% CI[− 4.61, − 0.03], P = 0.047). No main effect of age group was observed. However, significant interactions indicated that children >12 months experienced a smaller intraoperative decline during CPB (βint = 4.17, P = 0.027) and ACC (interaction βint = 4.75, P = 0.012) than those ≤6 months. Association of rScO2 with organ injury markers: effect modification by age

Changes in the levels of neurological and myocardial injury markers at the perioperative time points are listed in Supplemental Table S2 and Supplemental Figure S3. Given that age significantly modified the perioperative rScO2 trajectory, we further explored whether age also modified the association between rScO2 (nadir value) and postoperative organ injury biomarkers (peak value). Among the neuronal markers, only NSE showed a nominally significant interaction with age group (6–12 months: βint = 0.41, P = 0.027; >12 months: βint = 0.43, P = 0.044). In the reference age group (≤ 6 months), a lower rScO2 nadir was associated with a higher NSE peak (β = − 0.27, 95% CI [− 0.48, − 0.05], P = 0.016), adjusted for sex, weight, RACHS-1, cyanotic CHD, and CPB duration (Table 4). After FWER correction for the three primary biomarkers, this association remained significant (Padj = 0.048). Stratified analyses (Fig. 2) confirmed that this inverse association was present only in the youngest infants (≤ 6 months: β = − 0.27, 95% CI [− 0.48, − 0.07], P = 0.001) without covariate adjustment, whereas no significant association was found in the 6–12 months (β = 0.14, 95% CI [− 0.13, 0.41], P = 0.285) or >12 months (β = 0.20, 95% CI [− 0.16, 0.56], P = 0.267). For the remaining five biomarkers (GFAP, S100β, CKMB, cTnI, and NT-proBNP), neither the interactions with age group nor the main effects of models (without interactions) reached statistical significance (all P > 0.05; details in Supplemental Table S3 and Supplemental Figure S4). Sensitivity analyses

In a sensitivity analysis using age as a continuous variable, the data in Supplemental Figure S5 revealed that rScO2 generally increased with age across all time points; infants <1 year had notably lower intraoperative rScO2 than those >1 year. The linear mixed-effects model (Supplementary Table S4) confirmed a log-age × time interaction: the positive effect of age on rScO2 was enhanced during CPB (βint = 1.01, P = 0.037).

In the analysis of the association between rScO2 and neurological biomarkers (Supplementary Table S5), a significant age-by-rScO2 interaction (βint = 0.11, P = 0.023) was also found for peak NSE levels. Peak NSE was significantly associated with the rScO2 nadir (β = − 0.27, 95% CI [− 0.50, − 0.04], P = 0.024) and log(age) (β = − 5.81, 95% CI [− 11.11, − 0.48], P = 0.033). The magnitude of rScO2 decline was further used as a surrogate marker of cerebral oxygen desaturation; however, no association was found between the percentage decline in rScO2 and peak levels of neurological biomarkers (Supplementary Table S6).

Discussion

In this study of 104 children who underwent cardiac surgery with CPB, we revealed that age not only influenced perioperative rScO2 trajectories but also modified the association between intraoperative cerebral desaturation and postoperative neuronal injury marker. Specifically, infants aged ≤6 months exhibited the lowest rScO2 values throughout the perioperative period, with nadir values occurring during CPB and ACC periods. This group was the only group in which a significant negative correlation was observed between the rScO2 nadir and peak postoperative NSE levels. No similar associations were found for the other two neurological biomarkers or for the three myocardial biomarkers evaluated. Importantly, these findings are exploratory and hypothesis-generating and suggest that intensive rScO2 monitoring may be viewed as a risk-detection tool, especially for ≤6-month-old infants.

Across all age groups, perioperative rScO2 followed a consistent U-shaped pattern—declining during CPB and ACC and then recovering postoperatively—which is consistent with previous reports [14, 23]. However, the magnitude of the decline and recovery trajectory varied substantially by age. Across the five perioperative time points, infants aged ≤6 months consistently had the lowest numerical rScO2 values among the three groups, with statistically significant differences observed at all time points except for Pre-CPB. Nonlinear modeling further confirmed that infants aged ≤6 months maintained persistently lower absolute rScO2 values throughout the perioperative period. This group faced not only greater intraoperative declines but also poorer recovery throughout the perioperative period. Using linear and quadratic mixed-effects models, we consistently identified significant cerebral oxygenation vulnerability in infants ≤6 months of age, which has not been systematically explored previously [15, 16]. In the adjusted linear mixed-effects model, the main effect of age group on rScO2 was no longer significant (Table 3), yet the age-group-by-time interaction remained significant. This pattern suggests that age-related differences in rScO2 are largely mediated by factors associated with age, such as weight. However, the persistent age-by-time interaction indicates that the trajectory of rScO2—how it declines and recovers during surgery—is independently influenced by age. In other words, even after accounting for cardiac anatomy and procedural complexity, younger infants still experienced more profound intraoperative cerebral desaturation. Children >12 months of age exhibited significantly lower rScO2 decreases during CPB and ACC than those ≤6 months (βint= 4.17 and 4.75, respectively), suggesting greater compensatory capacity and buffering against cerebral desaturation [16]. While the underlying mechanisms remain unclear, the observed vulnerability may partly reflect immature cerebral autoregulation, increased metabolic demand, or other yet-unknown factors [11, 15, 24]. However, direct evidence is lacking and warrants further validation using autoregulation indices and other physiological assessments.

Among the three neurological injury biomarkers, only peak NSE exhibited age-modified associations with rScO2. NSE, a marker of neuronal injury reflecting damage to neurons and neuroglia, has important clinical value in the diagnosis and monitoring of neurological injury [25]. Our data revealed that peak NSE levels tended to increase at three postoperative time points, suggesting that active neuronal injury occurred continuously with the release of NSE into the circulation after surgery. We selected the rScO2 nadir—the lowest value detected during surgery—as a quantitative indicator of the most severe hypoxic insult experienced by the brain tissue. A key finding of this study is that, exclusively in the infant subgroup aged ≤6 months, lower intraoperative rScO2 was associated with higher postoperative NSE levels (β = − 0.27, P = 0.015), indicating that each 1-percentage-point decrease in the rScO2 nadir was associated with an approximately 0.27-unit increase in NSE. The robustness of this association was supported by a corrected P value of 0.048. Notably, significant interactions were observed between age group and the rScO2 nadir (P = 0.026 and 0.043 for the 6–12-month and >12-month groups, respectively), suggesting that the slopes of this association in older infants (β = 0.41 and 0.43, respectively) differed statistically from those in infants aged ≤6 months, implying that age may modulate the NSE release response. Nevertheless, these interaction analyses were exploratory in nature, and their P values were not adjusted for multiple testing. In subgroup analyses, we further observed a reversal in the direction of the association: negative in the ≤6-month group but positive (albeit not statistically significant) in the two older age groups, indicating potentially distinct patterns of relationships between cerebral oxygen desaturation and neuronal injury across different developmental stages. Clinically, our findings pinpoint rScO2 monitoring as a potentially valuable prognostic indicator, yet this utility appears confined to infants aged ≤6 months. However, given the borderline adjusted P-value for NSE and the exploratory nature of subgroup and interaction analyses, this age-specific effect requires independent validation. Moreover, if confirmed, future studies are warranted to assess whether rScO2-guided strategies can ultimately improve neurological outcomes in this population [26].

When we repeated the analysis using the percentage decline in rScO2 in sensitivity analyses, we did not observe a significant association in the ≤6-month group. This discrepancy does not contradict our primary finding and may be consistent with age-dependent differences in cerebral autoregulation, which is immature in early infancy and further compromised during CPB [27, 28]. One possible interpretation is that the absolute rScO2 nadir may reflect perfusion pressure relative to the autoregulatory limit, whereas percentage decline is influenced by baseline variability and may not indicate threshold breach. However, the underlying mechanisms and clinical implications of this differential metric sensitivity remain to be explored.

In this study, no significant associations were found between rScO2 (nadir or percentage decline) and GFAP or S100β. Possible explanations include differences in release kinetics between glial and neuronal markers and the possibility that ischemic insult during surgery may affect neuronal and glial cells at different injury thresholds [29]. Previous studies on neurological biomarkers have also reported heterogeneous results, although some suggesting limited clinical benefit in the early postoperative period [19, 30, 31]. These findings suggest that NSE may be more responsive to acute hypoxic neuronal injury in this age group, though this remains unclear.

Similarly, the rScO2 nadir was not significantly associated with myocardial biomarkers, with no modifying effect of age. These null findings might reflect differential organ susceptibility or distinct perfusion/metabolic conditions during CPB, but the exact reasons are unclear [32, 33]. Taken together, our results do not support a direct link between cerebral desaturation and myocardial injury in this cohort, which is consistent with the view that these two forms of injury may be pathophysiologically dissociated, although further studies are needed.

This study provides several preliminary observations. First, given the heterogeneous prior neuromonitoring studies and no standardized guidelines available [8]. We found that younger infants are especially vulnerable to intraoperative hypoxia during pediatric cardiac surgery, underscoring age as a key effect modifier for cerebral oxygen variation and neurogical biomarker interpretation. Second, the association between the rScO2 nadir and peak NSE levels in the ≤6 month group suggested that NSE may serve as an adjunctive tool for detecting subclinical neuronal injury. Third, from a clinical perspective, our results raise the hypothesis that more intensive rScO2 monitoring—including attention to extreme desaturation—could be considered for infants ≤6 months. However, whether such strategies can effectively reduce cerebral hypoxic burden or improve neurocognitive outcomes is not established by our data and requires validation in larger prospective cohorts with long-term neurodevelopmental follow-up. Until then, any monitoring strategy should be viewed as a means of early risk identification rather than a proven neuroprotective measure.

This study has several limitations. First, although the overall sample size was larger than that in most previous reports, the single-center design and subgroup stratification yielded relatively modest sample sizes, limiting adjustment for multiple confounders. Consequently, residual confounding from unmeasured variables cannot be fully excluded, warranting validation across specific physiological subgroups in future large-scale studies. Second, we used surrogate biomarkers rather than systematic neuroimaging or long-term neurodevelopmental follow-up. Moreover, the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for these biomarkers has not been uniformly established in our clinical setting. Therefore, we cannot determine whether observed biomarker changes translate to clinically meaningful neurological injury; future investigations can address these gaps. Third, we did not adjust for multiple comparisons across all analyses, including interaction and subgroup analyses. For the primary analysis of neurological biomarkers, the adjusted P value was borderline. Therefore, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Fourth, as our biomarker sampling may not have captured true marker peaks, future real-time monitoring with automated data capture is warranted, and different desaturation metrics (e.g., duration and cumulative burden) can be compared to identify the most clinically relevant indicator [34]. Fifth, the exclusion of early deaths (mainly low cardiac output) for data integrity may have attenuated the cerebral desaturation–injury association by removing high-risk patients. Collectively, these limitations preclude any causal or therapeutic inference from our findings, which should be viewed as preliminary and hypothesis-generating.

Conclusions

Age influences perioperative rScO2 trajectories and affects the association between cerebral desaturation and peak NSE levels, a surrogate marker of brain injury. Infants ≤6 months exhibit the lowest rScO2 values and a significant inverse correlation with peak NSE, suggesting an age-specific vulnerability that generates the hypothesis that more intensive monitoring might be beneficial in this subgroup.

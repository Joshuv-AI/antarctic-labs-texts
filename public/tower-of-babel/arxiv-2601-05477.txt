Categories of constructible sheaves
Valery A. Lunts, Olaf M. Schnürer

Abstract
. Given a stratified topological space, we answer the question whether the functor from the derived category of constructible sheaves to the derived category of sheaves with constructible cohomology is an equivalence. We also establish basic facts on the category of locally constant sheaves and on the category of constructible sheaves.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
its full abelian subcategory which is closed under extensions in
\mathcal{A}
. One may consider the bounded derived category
{\operatorname{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and its full triangulated subcategory
{\operatorname{D}}^{b}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})\subset{\operatorname{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of complexes with cohomology in
\mathcal{B}
. We have the obvious
realization
functor

(1.1)

\real:{\operatorname{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{B})\to{\operatorname{D}}^{b}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})

and it is often important to know whether is an equivalence.
This question is too general: there are some meaningful results only for concrete examples of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
. (See
[
LaLu21
]
for a purely algebraic example).

Actually, perhaps the most interesting examples come from topology, where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are certain categories of sheaves.

For example, one may consider a topological space
X
with a (finite) stratification
\mathcal{S}
. Take for
\mathcal{A}
the category
\Sh(X)
of all sheaves of abelian groups and for
\mathcal{B}
- its full subcategory
{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
of sheaves, which are
\mathcal{S}
-constructible (that is whose restriction to every stratum is a local system, possibly of infinite rank). In this work we find conditions which guarantee, that the corresponding realization functor

(1.2)

\real:{\operatorname{D}}^{+}({\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S}))\to{\operatorname{D}}^{+}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Sh(X))

is an equivalence. The similar question about constructible sheaves of finite rank is more subtle and there we have only partial results.

If one does not fix a stratification of the space, but rather considers sheaves which are constructible with respect to some (for example algebraic) stratification, then the functor (
1.2
) has a better chance to be an equivalence. To our best knowledge the paper
[
Nor02
]
contains the most general results in this setup.

The authors are grateful to Jörg Schürmann for a very useful discussion of the subject.

1.1.
Conventions

All rings are assumed to be associative and unital, but not
necessarily commutative. The symbol
R
always denotes a ring.
Modules are left modules, and
\Mod(R)
denotes the category of
left
R
-modules.

As a general rule, a sheaf on a
topological space
X
means a sheaf of
R
-modules. The category
of sheaves on
X
is denoted by
\Sh(X)
. When we speak
about sheaves of sets we say this
explicitly. The same convention applies to presheaves.

If
X
is a topological space,

\HH^{\operatorname{sing}}_{q}(X,A)
and

\HH_{\operatorname{sing}}^{q}(X,A)
denote the
q
-th
singular homology and cohomology, respectively, of
X
with
values in an abelian group or
R
-module
A
;

\HH_{\operatorname{sing}}(X,F)
denotes the
sheaf cohomology of
X
with values in a sheaf
F
of abelian
groups or
R
-modules.

If
\mathcal{A}
is an additive category, its category of
complexes is denoted by
\operatorname{{C}}(\mathcal{A})
, its homotopy category
by
{\operatorname{K}}(\mathcal{A})
.

Given an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, an abelian subcategory
is a strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{B}
which is abelian and
whose inclusion functor
\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is exact.

The (unbounded) derived category of an abelian category

\mathcal{A}
is denoted by

{\operatorname{D}}(\mathcal{A})
. By
{\operatorname{D}}^{\operatorname{b}}(\mathcal{A})
,

{\operatorname{D}}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
,

{\operatorname{D}}^{-}(\mathcal{A})
we denote the full
subcategories of
{\operatorname{D}}(\mathcal{A})

of objects with bounded, bounded below, bounded above cohomology,
respectively.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a full subcategory of

\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
{\operatorname{D}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})

the full subcategory of
{\operatorname{D}}(\mathcal{A})

of objects
E
whose cohomology sheaves
\HH^{p}(E)
are in

\mathcal{B}
, for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If
\mathcal{B}
is a weak Serre subcategory
of
\mathcal{A}
, then

{\operatorname{D}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
is a thick triangulated subcategory
of
{\operatorname{D}}(\mathcal{A})

(see
[
Sta18
,
02MN
,
06UQ
]
).
The categories
{\operatorname{D}}^{\operatorname{b}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
,

{\operatorname{D}}^{+}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
,

{\operatorname{D}}^{-}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
are defined in the obvious
way.

We abbreviate
{\operatorname{D}}(R):={\operatorname{D}}(\Mod(R))
and
{\operatorname{D}}(X):={\operatorname{D}}(\Sh(X))
and

{\operatorname{K}}(X):={\operatorname{K}}(\Sh(X))
and

\operatorname{{C}}(X):=\operatorname{{C}}(\Sh(X))
.

The shift functor of a triangulated category is sometimes denoted
by
\Sigma
and sometimes by
[1]
.

2.
Formulation of main results

2.1.
Locally constant sheaves

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space.

(a)

If
M
is an
R
-module, the sheaf on
X
associated
to the presheaf
U\mapsto M
is denoted by

{\underline{M}}={\underline{M}}_{X}
and called the
constant sheaf with
stalk
M
. Equivalently, it can be described as the sheaf
of local sections of
M\times X\rightarrow X
where
M
carries the
discrete topology.

(b)

A sheaf on
X
is called
constant
if it is
isomorphic to a sheaf of the form
{\underline{M}}_{X}
.

(c)

A sheaf
F
on
X
is called
locally constant
if
every point of
X
has an open neighborhood
U
such that
F|_{U}
is
constant.

The category of locally constant sheaves is denoted by
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
. It is a full subcategory
\Sh(X)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A topological space
X
is
simply connected
if
X
is path connected and satisfies
\pi_{1}(X,x)=1
for a/any
x\in X
. We say that
X
is
acyclic
(resp.
1-acyclic
) if the sheaf cohomology
\HH^{0}(X,{\underline{M}})=M
and
\HH^{>0}(X,{\underline{M}})=0
(resp.
\HH^{1}(X,{\underline{M}})=0
) for all
R
-modules
M
.

Definition 2.3
.

If (P) is a property of topological spaces, we say that a
topological space
X
is
locally (P)
if any
neighborhood of any point
x\in X
contains an open
neighborhood
U
of
x
having property (P). Equivalently, this
means that the topology of
X
has a basis consisting of open
subsets having property (P).

2.4
.

Every (locally) contractible space is (locally) simply
connected
and (locally) acyclic.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be non-empty, path connected, locally simply connected
topological space. Let
x\in X
. Then the following holds.

(1) There is a natural equivalence of categories

{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Mod(R\pi_{1}(X,x)).

Therefore the category
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
is a (complete and co-complete) Grothendieck abelian category. It has enough projectives and enough injectives.

(2) The inclusion functor
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)\hookrightarrow\Sh(X)
is exact, continuous and co-continuous (i.e. preserves inverse and direct limits).

(3) Assume in addition that
X
is locally 1-acyclic. Then
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
is closed under extensions in
\Sh(X)
.

2.6
.

If
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
is closed under extensions in
\Sh(X)
, then
{\operatorname{D}}_{{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)}(\Sh(X))
is a thick triangulated subcategory of
{\operatorname{D}}(\Sh(X))

and one can ask if the obvious triangulated functor

{\operatorname{D}}({\operatorname{Loc}}(X))\to{\operatorname{D}}_{{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)}(\Sh(X))
is an equivalence.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be a
locally simply connected,
locally acyclic
topological space. Let
{\widetilde{X}}
be the disjoint union of the
universal coverings of all path components of
X
.
Then the following two conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The obvious triangulated functor
is an equivalence

(2.1)

{\operatorname{D}}^{+}({\operatorname{Loc}}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}{\operatorname{D}}^{+}_{\operatorname{Loc}}(X).

(b)

\HH^{>0}({\widetilde{X}},{\underline{M}})=0
for all

R
-modules
M
, i. e. the universal covering of each
path component of
X
is acyclic.

2.2.
Constructible sheaves

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
be a topological space. A
stratification of

X
is a finite partition
\mathcal{S}
of
X
into
non-empty locally closed subsets, called
strata
, such
that the closure of each stratum is a union of strata,
i. e.
{\overline{S}}=\bigcup_{T\subset{\overline{S}}}T
for each

S\in\mathcal{S}
. A
stratified space
is a
topological space together with a stratificaton.
The following conditions on a stratification

\mathcal{S}
of
X
are used.

(loc-sc)

Each stratum is locally simply connected.

(loc-sa)

Each stratum is locally 1-acyclic.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{S})
be a stratified space. A
sheaf
F
on
X
is
\mathcal{S}
-constructible

if
F|_{S}\in{\operatorname{Loc}}(S)
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
We write
{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
for
the full subcategory of
\Sh(X)
of

\mathcal{S}
-constructible objects. (So
{\operatorname{Cons}}(S,\{S\})={\operatorname{Loc}}(S)
.)

A complex
F
in
\Sh(X)
is

\mathcal{S}
-constructible

if all its cohomology sheaves
\HH^{p}(F)
are

\mathcal{S}
-constructible.
We write

{\operatorname{D}}_{\mathcal{S}}(X):={\operatorname{D}}_{{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})}(X)

for
the full subcategory of
{\operatorname{D}}(X)
of

\mathcal{S}
-constructible objects.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{S})
be a

(loc-sc)
-stratified space.
Then
{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
is a Grothendieck abelian category
and the
inclusion functor

{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})\rightarrow\Sh(X)
is exact and cocontinuous;
in
particular, coproducts in
\Sh(X)
of families of
objects in
{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
are again in
{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
.

Assume in addition that the stratification is

(loc-sa)
. Then

{\operatorname{Cons}}(X,\mathcal{S})
is a weak Serre subcategory of

\Sh(X)
. In particular,
{\operatorname{D}}_{\mathcal{S}}(X)
is a thick
triangulated subcategory of
{\operatorname{D}}(X)

which is closed under
coproducts in
{\operatorname{D}}(X)
.

Theorem 2.15

Let

2.4.
Local systems of finite rank

In Section

. Namely, assume that the coefficient ring

the full abelian subcategory of constructible sheaves with finite dimensional stalks. Let also

be the corresponding triangulated category. One may ask when the obvious functor

3.
Locally constant sheaves: Proof of Proposition
2.5

3.1
.

Let
F
be a sheaf on a topological space

X
and
{\widetilde{F}}
its espace étalé. Then
F
is locally
constant if and only if

{\widetilde{F}}\rightarrow X
is a covering map.

3.2
.

Recall that
\Sh(X)
is a Grothendieck abelian category

[
Sta18
,
079V
,
01AH
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a simply connected,
locally path connected
topological space. Then the following statements are true.

(a)

The functor
\Mod(R)\rightarrow\Sh(X)
,

M\mapsto{\underline{M}}
is fully faithful and exact with essential
image

{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
and hence provides an equivalence

\Mod(R)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)

of categories.
Taking global sections is a
quasi-inverse of
this functor. (Another quasi-inverse is taking the stalk at an
arbitrary point.)
In particular,
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
has all categorical properties of

\Mod(R)
, e. g. it is a (complete and cocomplete)
Grothendieck abelian
category with enough injectives and projectives.

Moreover,
the inclusion functor
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)\rightarrow\Sh(X)
is exact,
continuous (= preserves limits) and cocontinuous (= preserves
colimits).

(b)

{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
is closed under extensions in
\Sh(X)
(i.e.
{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)

is a weak Serre subcategory
of
\Sh(X)
) if and only if
X
is 1-acyclic.

Proof.

(a)

Let
c\colon X\rightarrow\pt
be the map to a one-point-space,
viewed as a map of ringed spaces with structure sheaves

{\underline{R}}
and
R
, respectively.
Then we have an adjunction
c^{*}\colon\Mod(R)\leftrightarrows\Sh(X)\colon c_{*}
.
Note that
c^{*}(M)={\underline{M}}
and
c_{*}(F)=\Gamma(X,F)
.
We need to see that
c^{*}

is fully faithful and that its essential image consists
precisely of all locally constant sheaves.

Given
F\in{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
consider its espace étalé

{\widetilde{F}}
which is a module object
over the ring object
{\underline{{\widetilde{R}}}}
in the category

\Top_{/X}
of topological spaces over
X
, cf.
[
MLM94
, II.7]
. Since
F
is locally constant,
any
x\in X
has an open neighborhood such that
F|_{U}
is
constant, i. e.
{\widetilde{F}}|_{U}\cong N\times U

(as module
objects) compatible with the projections to
U
, where
N
is
an
R
-module
equipped with the discrete topology. This means that

{\widetilde{F}}
is locally path connected and
{\widetilde{F}}\rightarrow X
is
a covering map.
Hence each
connected component (= path
component) of
{\widetilde{F}}
is open in
{\widetilde{F}}
and -
X

being simply connected
and locally path connected -
maps homeomorphically onto
X
.
If
M
is the set of path component of
{\widetilde{F}}
, we obtain a
homeomorphism
{\widetilde{F}}\xrightarrow{\sim}M\times X
over
X
by mapping
a point to the pair consisting of its image in
X
and its path
component. Taking
the action of
{\underline{{\widetilde{R}}}}

into account
shows that
M
has
a natural structure of
R
-module such that this
is in fact a homeomorphism of module objects over

{\underline{{\widetilde{R}}}}
in

\Top_{/X}
. Hence
{\underline{M}}\cong F
as sheaves. This shows
essential surjectivity.

Fully faithfulness of
c^{*}
is equivalent to
M\rightarrow c_{*}c^{*}(M)=\Gamma(X,{\underline{M}})
being an isomorphism
for each
R
-module
M
. But this is clear since any (global)
section of
{\underline{{\widetilde{M}}}}=M\times X\rightarrow X
has the form
x\mapsto(x,m)
for a fixed
m\in M
since
X
is connected and

M
has the discrete topology.
This proves the equivalence
\Mod(R)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
.

The fact that
c^{*}\colon\Mod(R)\rightarrow\Sh(X)
is exact and
left adjoint then
implies that

{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)\rightarrow\Sh(X)
is exact and
cocontinuous (= preserves colimits)

In order to show that the inclusion functor preserves limits it
is enough to show that

c^{*}
preserves products. Let

(M_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of
R
-modules. Since the
presheaf product
of sheaves is already a sheaf we obtain for an open subset
U

of
X

(\prod_{i\in I}{\underline{M_{i}}})(U)=\prod_{i\in I}({\underline{M_{i}}}(U))=\prod_{i\in I}(\Top_{/U}(U,M_{i}\times U))=\prod_{i\in I}(\Top(U,M_{i}))=\Top(U,\prod_{i\in I}M_{i})

where
T:=\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
has the product topology (= Tychonoff
topology).
On the other hand

{\underline{\left(\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}\right)}}(U)=(\Top_{/U}(U,(\prod_{i\in I}M_{i})\times U))=\Top(U,\prod_{i\in I}M_{i})

where
D:=\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
has the discrete
topology.
The identity is a continous map
D\rightarrow T
. We claim that the
induced map

\Top(U,D)\rightarrow\Top(U,T)

is an isomorphism. Injectivity is trivial.
For surjectivity
let
s\colon U\rightarrow T
be a continuous map. Since
U
is
locally path connected,
U
is the disjoint union of its open
path components, i. e. the coproduct in
\Top
of these path
components. Hence we can assume without loss of generality that

U
is (path) connected. Then each composition
U\xrightarrow{s}T\rightarrow M_{i}
must be constant since
M_{i}
has the discrete
topology. Hence
s
is constant and
continuous as a map
U\rightarrow D
. This shows surjectivity.

(b)
Assume that
\HH^{1}(X,{\underline{K}})=0
for all
R
-modules
K
.
Let

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow E\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence
in
\Sh(X)
with
M,N\in{\operatorname{Loc}}(X)
.
By part
(a)
we have

{\underline{\Gamma(X,M)}}\xrightarrow{\sim}M
and therefore

\HH^{1}(X,M)=0
by assumption.
The long exact sequence for the derived functors of

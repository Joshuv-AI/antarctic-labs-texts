1.
Introduction

Conservation laws with discontinuous flux functions arise in various real-world applications.
These applications span multiple fields, including traffic flow, multi-phase fluid dynamics (e.g., two-phase flow models and environmental engineering), material science (e.g., crystallization processes and composite materials), and systems engineering (e.g., acoustic waves and electromagnetic waves).
In particular, traffic systems often exhibit sudden changes in road conditions, speed limits, or traffic regulations, which can be effectively modelled using conservation laws with discontinuous flux.

In this paper we study the one-dimensional
2\times 2
system of conservation laws with discontinuous flux

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\right)=0,\\
\partial_{t}\left(\rho\left(h+p(\rho)\right)\right)+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\left(h+p(\rho)\right)\right)=0.\end{cases}

(1)

This system finds its relevance in the modeling of vehicular traffic.
Indeed, it can be interpreted as a generalisation of two second order vehicular traffic models: the one proposed by Aw, Rascle
[
5
]
and Zhang
[
43
]
, and the one kinetically derived by Chiarello, Göttlich, Schillinger and Tosin in
[
14
]
.
We refer to the former as the ARZ model, and to the latter as the CGST model.
In this context,
t\geqslant 0
denotes the time,
x\in\mathbb{R}
the space,
\rho=\rho(t,x)\geqslant 0
the
density
,
c=c(x)>0
is a given discontinuous function that mimics the changes in the road conditions,
p=p(\rho)\geqslant 0
is the “pressure” function, and
V\geqslant 0
represents the speed law.
The interpretation of
h=h(t,x)\geqslant 0
depends on the specific model under consideration: it represents the
velocity
in the ARZ model and the
mean headway
in the CGST model, see Section
2.2
for further details.
It is worth mentioning that in
[
11
]
, the authors kinetically derive a second order macroscopic traffic model, similar to the CGST model, but with the assumption of a constant headway.

The majority of theoretical advancements concerning conservation laws with discontinuous coefficients are limited to scalar equations, see
[
3
,
35
]
and the references therein.
In particular, in
[
31
]
the authors provide Kružkov-type entropy condition for nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations with discontinuous coefficients, establishing
\mathbf{L^{1}}
-stability, and thus uniqueness, for weak solutions satisfying the entropy condition, provided that the flux function satisfies the so called “crossing condition”.
In
[
32
]
a convergence proof for the Lax-Friedrichs finite difference scheme for non-convex genuinely nonlinear scalar conservation laws with discontinuous coefficients is given.
The development of analogous theories for nonlinear systems with discontinuous fluxes, even in the one-dimensional space, remains an open area for further investigation. An exception to this is found in the studies presented in
[
29
,
30
]
, which explore compressible Euler flows within a pipe, in
[
37
]
where the ARZ model with seven different types of discontinuous fluxes are analyzed, and in
[
39
]
where Riemann solvers are proposed for
2\times 2
systems of conservation laws with discontinuous flux, referring to the ARZ model or modeling polymer flooding.
Moreover, there are results on networks. For example, in
[
1
]
the authors analyze control problems considering a general class of junction distribution controls and inflow controls, establishing the compactness in
\mathbf{L^{1}}
of a class of flux-traces of solutions.
Furthermore, in
[
16
]
a single conservation law, defined on a road network that is a collection of roads with junctions, is considered. The authors choose some fixed rules for the distribution of traffic plus optimization criteria for the flux, proving existence of solutions to the Cauchy problem and the Lipschitz continuous dependence by initial data under special assumptions. A generalization can be found in
[
15
]
.

System (
1
) falls in the class of Temple systems
[
41
]
, characterized by coinciding shock and rarefaction Lax curves for the genuinely nonlinear characteristic fields. In fact, only the first characteristic field of (
1
) is genuinely nonlinear and satisfies this condition, while the second characteristic field is linearly degenerate. Specifically, along the first Lax curve, the solution can exhibit shock waves, rarefaction waves, or mixed waves. In contrast, along the second Lax curve, the solution can contain only contact discontinuities.

The paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
, we present our existence result in Theorem
2.1
, which applies away from the vacuum.
In the following three remarks, we discuss alternative strategies that could potentially extend the existence results to cases including the vacuum.
The final remark addresses the entropy condition, which is further examined in details in Section
4
.
In Section
2.2
, we compare some models from the literature with (
6
), highlighting its possible applications.
In Section
2.3
, we analyse the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of our system and conduct a study of the Lax curves.
In Section
3
, we introduce explicitly a Riemann solver and study its main properties.
In Section
4
the entropy condition for system (
1
) is deduced and analysed.
As far as we know there is no result in the literature regarding the entropy condition in the case of systems of conservation laws with discontinuous fluxes.
In Section
5
some numerical simulations are performed to corroborate the analytical results obtained in the previous Sections.
Finally, we defer all technical proofs to Section
6
.

2.
Existence result and applications

In this section, we present our existence result in Theorem
2.1
along with some applications and properties of (
1
).

2.1.
Main result

Theorem
2.1
holds away from the vacuum, i.e., for
\rho>0
, and under the following set of assumptions: for any
\rho>0
and
h>0
we have

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{C^{1}}([0,\infty)),\qquad p(0)=0,\qquad p^{\prime}(\rho)>0,

(2)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{C^{2}}([0,\infty)),\qquad V(0)=0,\qquad V^{\prime}(h)>0,

(3)

\displaystyle V(h)

\displaystyle\neq\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho)\,V^{\prime}(h),\qquad\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\,V^{\prime\prime}(h)<\left(2p^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho\,p^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\right)V^{\prime}(h),

(4)

and there exist
c_{\min},c_{\infty}>0
and
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket\coloneqq[1,p]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
, with
\xi_{i}<\xi_{i+1}
such that for almost any
x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle c\in\mathbf{L^{1}_{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathbf{C^{2}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}\}),\qquad c-c_{\infty}\in\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad c(x)\geqslant c_{\min}.

(5)

Note that
p(\rho)>0
for any
\rho>0
by
\eqref{e:p}_{2,3}
and that
V(h)>0
for any
h>0
by
\eqref{e:V}_{2,3}
.

For
\rho>0
system (
1
) is equivalent to the system of conservation laws

\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+\partial_{x}\left(c\,F(\mathbf{U})\right)=\mathbf{0},

(6)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}\coloneqq(\rho,q)^{T},

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)\,\mathbf{U},

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\frac{q}{\rho}-p(\rho),

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\coloneqq(0,0)^{T},

(7)

where
q
is the
generalized momentum
and satisfies

q=\rho\,\left(h+p(\rho)\right).

(8)

The dependent variable
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{U}(t,x)
takes values in

\mathcal{U}\coloneqq\{(\rho,q)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\rho\geqslant 0,\ q\geqslant\rho\,p(\rho)\geqslant 0\}.

Observe that in
\mathcal{U}
the vacuum
\rho=0
corresponds to
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{0}
by (
8
).
In this section, even if we do not state it explicitly, we consider states
\mathbf{U}
away from the vacuum, i.e., states
\mathbf{U}
in

\mathcal{U}_{m}\coloneqq\mathcal{U}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

(9)

In the next theorem we give our existence result for the Cauchy problem for the system of conservation laws (
6
) in

\mathcal{U}_{e}\coloneqq\{(\rho,q)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\rho>0,\ q>\rho\,p(\rho)>0\}.

(10)

Notice that
\mathcal{U}_{e}\subset\mathcal{U}_{m}
by (
9
), hence our existence result holds away from the vacuum.
The proof is based on
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
.
It is worth noticing that we cannot apply
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
directly to (
6
) because its flux function lacks the necessary regularity since
c
is discontinuous at
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, see (
5
).
To address this issue, as in
[
39
]
, we introduce a
3\times 3
system of conservation laws, see (
60
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
(\rho_{\infty},q_{\infty})^{T}\in\mathcal{U}_{e}
.
Assume that
p,V,c
are under assumptions (
2
)-(
5
).
Then, there exists
\delta_{0}>\tv[\,c\,]
with the following property: if
\overline{\mathbf{U}}\coloneqq(\bar{\rho},\bar{q})
takes values in
\mathcal{U}_{e}
,
\tv[\,\overline{\mathbf{U}}\,]\leqslant\delta_{0}-\tv[\,c\,]
and
\bar{\rho}-\rho_{\infty},\bar{q}-q_{\infty}\in\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})
, then the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+\partial_{x}\left(c\,F(\mathbf{U})\right)=\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{U}(0,x)=\overline{\mathbf{U}}(x)\end{cases}

(11)

has a weak solution
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{U}(t,x)
defined for all
t\geqslant 0
, that is
\mathbf{U}(0,x)=\overline{\mathbf{U}}(x)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathbf{U}\,\varphi_{t}+c\,F(\mathbf{U})\,\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=0

for any test function
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{\infty}}\left((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R};[0,\infty)\right)
.

In addition, if the system of conservation laws in (
11
) admits a convex entropy pair
(\mathcal{E},c\,\mathcal{Q})
such that

\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{U})=D\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{U})\cdot F(\mathbf{U}),

(12)

then one can find a weak solution
\mathbf{U}
that satisfies the entropy condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{U})\,\varphi_{t}+c\,\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{U})\,\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\geqslant 0

(13)

for any test function
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{\infty}}\left((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R};[0,\infty)\right)
.

We defer the proof to Section
6.1
.
Some comments on Theorem
2.1
are in order.

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
26
]
, the authors apply the wave-front tracking method to the ARZ model and obtain an existence result that is valid only away from the vacuum.
However, in
[
28
]
, the authors show that even if the vacuum is not present initially and does not appear immediately, the solution to the ARZ model can reach a vacuum state in finite time.
This shows that a theory for the ARZ model away from the vacuum is quite restrictive.
We recall that the extension to the case of solutions attaining the vacuum state is proved in
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
34
]
, by means of a deterministic particle approximation,
\mathbf{BV}
bounds on the Riemann invariants and the compensated compactness method, respectively.
This motivates the extension of our existence result in Theorem
2.1
to the vacuum case in a future paper.

Remark 2.3
.

Away from the vacuum, system (
6
) can be written in Lagrangian coordinates
[
42
]

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{\tau}-\partial_{\tilde{x}}\left(\tilde{c}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p(1/\tilde{\tau})\right)\right)=0,\\
\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{w}=0,\end{cases}

(14)

where we have

\displaystyle t=\tilde{t},

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}x=\tilde{\tau},

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{t}}x=\tilde{c}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)\right),

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=\frac{1}{\rho\left(t(\tilde{t}),x(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=\frac{q\left(t(\tilde{t}),x(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\right)}{\rho\left(t(\tilde{t}),x(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=c\left(x(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\right).

The flux function of system (
14
) is discontinuous (with respect to both the Lagrangian coordinates
(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})
), and this prevents from a direct application of
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
to it.
However, as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
in Section
6.1
, we can introduce a
3\times 3
system of conservation laws, which in this case is

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{\tau}-\partial_{\tilde{x}}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}}{\tilde{\tau}}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)\right)\right)=0,\\
\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{w}=0,\\
\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{k}-\partial_{\tilde{x}}\left(\left(\frac{\tilde{k}}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)^{2}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)\right)\right)=0,\end{cases}

where
\tilde{k}\coloneqq\tilde{c}\,\tilde{\tau}
.
The above system satisfies the hypotheses of
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
and then it is possible to get an existence result analogous to that in Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.4
.

In
[
39
, Section 4]
, the author studies the ARZ model with discontinuous flux.
Differently from (
6
), where both the components of the flux are discontinuous due to the presence of the discontinuous coefficient
c
, in
[
39
, (4.1)-(4.2)]
only the second component of the flux is discontinuous (see the discontinuous coefficient
k
multiplying the pressure term
\rho^{\gamma}
).

As a first step, the author adds another equation to treat the discontinuity, like we do in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, obtains
[
39
, (4.1)-(4.3)]
and then rewrites such
3\times 3
system of conservation laws in
decoupling
coordinates
(\phi,\psi)
, see
[
36
]
.

We can proceed in an analogous way and obtain the
3\times 3
system of conservation laws

\begin{cases}\partial_{\psi}\left(\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{\tilde{c}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)\right)}\right)-\partial_{\phi}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}\,V\left(\tilde{w}-p\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)\right)}\right)=0,\\
\partial_{\psi}\tilde{w}=0,\\
\partial_{\phi}\tilde{c}=0,\end{cases}

(15)

by introducing in (
6
) the change of coordinates
(\phi,\psi)
defined as

\displaystyle\phi_{x}=-\rho,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=c\,\rho\,V\left(h(\rho,q)\right),

\displaystyle\psi=x,

which is well defined when
c\,\rho\,V\left(h(\rho,q)\right)>0
, and by letting

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}(\psi,\phi)=\frac{1}{\rho\left(t(\psi,\phi),x(\psi)\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})=\frac{q\left(t(\psi,\phi),x(\psi)\right)}{\rho\left(t(\psi,\phi),x(\psi)\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(\psi)=c\left(x(\psi)\right).

The last two equations in (
15
) are decoupled.
Moreover, given the initial datum, the values of
(\tilde{w},\tilde{c})
for any coordinate point
(\psi,\phi)
are determined trivially, see
[
39
, Figure 2]
for the case of a Riemann datum.
Then we can substitute the obtained expressions for
(\tilde{w},\tilde{c})
in the first equation and study the resulting
scalar
conservation law, which has a flux that is in general discontinuous with respect to
both the coordinates

(\psi,\phi)
, and can be handled by applying the techniques developed in
[
17
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

Solutions to system (
6
) may develop non-classical shocks at the discontinuity points
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, of the flux
c\,F
.
These discontinuities may fail to satisfy the classical entropy condition (
13
) for general entropy pairs, not necessarily satisfying (
12
).
At first glance, this seems to contradict the second assertion of Theorem
2.1
.
To clarify this apparent discrepancy, in Section
4
we construct general entropy pairs, clarifying the role of condition (
12
).
Roughly speaking, we will demonstrate that entropy pairs satisfying (
12
) cannot select physically admissible solutions.
On the other hand, as pointed out in the proof of Theorem
2.1
in Section
6.1
, any entropy pair
(\mathcal{E},c\,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies (
12
) whenever
c
is not piecewise constant.

A possible way to circumvent this drawback of the entropy condition is to explicitly introduce a (coupling) Riemann solver tailored to (
6
).
As a matter of fact, the discontinuity points of the flux are typically addressed through a Riemann solver, which prescribes the solution’s local behaviour at these points
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, rather than relying solely on the PDE framework.
In fact, by prescribing a Riemann solver at
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, one explicitly encodes the underlying modeling assumptions.
This approach is commonly employed when dealing with non-classical solutions to hyperbolic conservation laws, relying on the property of finite propagation speed.
We refer to
[
3
]
for a comprehensive discussion of discontinuous-flux conservation laws defined via the Riemann solver method.
In Section
3
, we adapt the general definition of a Riemann solver, as proposed in
[
37
, Section 3.2]
, to our case.

2.2.
Applications

We compare now our model (
1
) with other models from the literature.

ARZ model

The ARZ model
[
5
,
43
]
for vehicular traffic, written in the conservative form, is expressed by the following equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\,v)=0,\\
\partial_{t}\left(\rho\,(v+p(\rho))\right)+\partial_{x}\left(\rho\,v\,(v+p(\rho))\right)=0,\end{cases}

(16)

where
v
is the
velocity
and
p(\rho)
is a pseudo-pressure function accounting for drivers’ anticipation of downstream density changes.
This model consists in two equations: the first one expresses the conservation of the density of cars
\rho
, the second one is a balance law for the momentum
\rho\,v
.
System (
1
) can be rewritten in the form (
16
) by taking
c\equiv 1
,
h=v
and
V
as the identity function.
For this reason, (
1
) can be interpreted as a generalization of (
16
) to the case of a discontinuous velocity.
We can therefore apply Theorem
2.1
to the ARZ model (
16
) provided that
p
satisfies (
2
) and (
4
).
Moreover, Theorem
2.1
also applies to a generalization of the ARZ model to the case of a discontinuous flux, obtained by multiplying the flux by a coefficient function
c
satisfying (
5
).

CGST model

The vehicular traffic model presented in
[
14
]
is represented by the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\right)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\,h)+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\,h\right)=\kappa\,\rho^{2}\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\right).\end{cases}

(17)

Here
h
is the
mean headway
, i.e., the mean distance between two consecutive vehicles.
This macroscopic model is derived through kinetic techniques and can be interpreted as a generalized version of the ARZ model (
16
).
We can write (
17
) in the form (
1
) by taking
p(\rho)=\kappa\,\rho
,
\kappa>0
, as pressure function.
Indeed, by multiplying the first equation in (
17
) by
2\,\kappa\,\rho
, we formally get

\partial_{t}(\kappa\,\rho^{2})+2\,\kappa\,\rho\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\right)=0.

Let’s use the elementary identity
2f(f\,g)^{\prime}=(f^{2}\,g)^{\prime}+f^{2}g^{\prime}
for
f=f(x)
,
g=g(x)
, to find that

2\,\rho\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\right)=\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho^{2}\right)+\rho^{2}\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\right).

Therefore, for
p(\rho)=\kappa\,\rho
, we reach

\partial_{t}\left(\rho\,p(\rho)\right)+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\,p(\rho)\right)+\rho\,p(\rho)\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\right)=0.

By combining the preceding equation with the latter in (
17
), specifically

\partial_{t}(\rho\,h)+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\,\rho\,h\right)=\rho\,p(\rho)\,\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(h)\right),

we derive the second equation in (
1
).
Hence, Theorem
2.1
applies to the CGST model if the functions
V
and
c
are such that (
3
)-(
5
) hold.

Comparison with GARZ and sedimentation.

The
2\times 2
system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(\phi,k)\,\phi\right)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(k\,\phi)+\partial_{x}\left(c\,V(\phi,k)\,k\,\phi\right)=0,\end{cases}

(18)

can be interpreted as a “discontinuous flux” version of the model proposed in
[
7
]
.
System (
18
) reduces to our system (
6
) by choosing an appropriate velocity function
V,
and then one can apply Theorem
2.1
.
Recall that in the case
c\equiv 1
, depending on the choice of the velocity function
V
, (
18
) corresponds to the so called generalized ARZ model (GARZ) proposed in
[
25
]
, or to the sedimentation model, see
[
8
]
.

2.3.
Main properties of system (
11
)

In this section we analyze the main properties of system (
6
) in
\mathcal{U}_{e}
defined in (
10
).
We defer the reader to
[
24
, Chapter 7]
for a general introduction to systems of conservation laws in one-space dimension.

The eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors of system (
6
) are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{U},x)

\displaystyle=c(x)\,\left(V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)-\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho)\,V^{\prime}\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)\right),

\displaystyle r_{1}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U},

(19)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{U},x)

\displaystyle=c(x)\,V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right),

\displaystyle r_{2}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\left(\rho,q+\rho^{2}\,p^{\prime}(\rho)\right)^{T},

(20)

where
h(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\frac{q}{\rho}-p(\rho)
.
System (
6
) is strictly hyperbolic in
\mathcal{U}_{e}
because
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{U},x)<\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{U},x)
for any
\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{U}_{e}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
by
\eqref{e:p}_{3}
,
\eqref{e:V}_{3}
and
\eqref{e:c}_{3}
.
Moreover, the first and second characteristc fields are respectively genuinely nonlinear and linearly degenerate because

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{U},x)\cdot r_{1}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\mathbf{U},x),

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{U},x)\cdot r_{2}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=0,

where

\alpha(\mathbf{U},x)\coloneqq c(x)\,\rho\left(\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}\,V^{\prime\prime}\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)-\left(2p^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho\,p^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\right)V^{\prime}\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)\right)

does not vanish (indeed it is
<0
) for any
\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{U}_{e}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
by
\eqref{e:pV}_{2}
and
\eqref{e:c}_{3}
.

The
Lagrangian marker

w(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\frac{q}{\rho}
and the function
h(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\frac{q}{\rho}-p(\rho)
form a coordinate system of Riemann invariants for (
6
) by
[
24
, Theorem 7.3.3]
because

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}w(\mathbf{U})\cdot r_{2}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho),

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}w(\mathbf{U})\cdot r_{1}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}h(\mathbf{U})\cdot r_{1}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=-\rho\,p^{\prime}(\rho),

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}h(\mathbf{U})\cdot r_{2}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=0.

In particular
w
and
h
are respectively the first and second
Riemann invariants
, see
[
24
, Definition 7.3.1]
.
Notice that the Riemann invariants
w
and
h
do not depend on
x
.

As already observed,
p(\rho)>0
for any
\rho>0
, hence
w(\mathbf{U})>h(\mathbf{U})>0
for any
\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{U}_{e}
.
Moreover, by definition and the strict monotonicity of the pressure, we have
\rho=p^{-1}(w-h)
and
q=p^{-1}(w-h)\,w
.
Observe that
\mathcal{U}
is expressed in the Riemann invariant coordinates as

\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\{(h,w)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:w\geqslant h\geqslant 0\}.

(21)

The vacuum
\rho=0
corresponds to
w=h
and therefore
\mathcal{U}_{m}
is expressed in the Riemann invariant coordinates as

\mathcal{W}_{m}\coloneqq\{(h,w)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:w>h\geqslant 0\}.

At last,
\mathcal{U}_{e}
becomes

\mathcal{W}_{e}\coloneqq\{(h,w)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:w>h>0\}.

Fix
\mathbf{U}_{0}\in\mathcal{U}_{e}
.
The
1
-shock and
1
-rarefaction Lax curves through
\mathbf{U}_{0}
coincide and are described by

\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\mathbf{U}_{0}}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{U}_{m}:w(\mathbf{U})=w(\mathbf{U}_{0})\}.

Indeed, if
\Pi_{x}
is the inverse function of
(0,\infty)\ni r\mapsto\lambda_{1}\left(r,w(\mathbf{U}_{0})\,r,x\right)
, which is strictly decreasing by
\eqref{e:pV}_{2}
, then we can parametrize
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\mathbf{U}_{0}}
with
\phi(\nu)\coloneqq\Pi_{x}(\nu)\left(1,w(\mathbf{U}_{0})\right)
,
\nu>\Pi_{x}^{-1}(\rho_{0})=\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{U}_{0},x)
, which is the solution of

\begin{cases}\alpha\left(\phi(\nu)\right)\,\phi^{\prime}(\nu)=r_{1}\left(\phi(\nu)\right),\\
\lambda_{1}\left(\phi(\nu),x\right)=\nu,\end{cases}

and therefore
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\mathbf{U}_{0}}
is the
1
-rarefaction Lax curve through
\mathbf{U}_{0}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\mathbf{U}_{0}}
is also the
1
-shock Lax curve through
\mathbf{U}_{0}
because, for any
\nu<\Pi_{x}^{-1}(\rho_{0})
, there exists
\sigma=\sigma(\nu,x)
such that

\left(\phi(\nu)-\mathbf{U}_{0}\right)\,\sigma=c(x)\,\left(F(\phi(\nu))-F(\mathbf{U}_{0})\right).

Indeed, it is sufficient to take

\sigma(\nu,x)\coloneqq c(x)\,\frac{V\left(w(\mathbf{U}_{0})-p(\Pi(\nu))\right)\,\Pi(\nu)-V\left(w(\mathbf{U}_{0})-p(\rho_{0})\right)\,\rho_{0}}{\Pi(\nu)-\rho_{0}}.

The second characteristic field is linearly degenerate, hence the associated Lax curve is expressed in terms of the first Riemann invariant as follows

\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\mathbf{U}_{0}}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{U}_{m}:h(\mathbf{U})=h(\mathbf{U}_{0})\}.

System (
6
) is of Temple type
[
40
,
41
]
because the 1-shock and 1-rarefaction Lax curves coincide and the second characteristic field is linearly degenerate.

We rewrite (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})+\partial_{x}\left(c\,F(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}))\right)=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{W}(0,x)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{W}_{L}&\text{if }x<0,\\
\mathbf{W}_{R}&\text{if }x\geqslant 0,\end{cases}\end{cases}

\displaystyle c(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}c^{-}&\text{if }x<0,\\

(22)

are given constants,

\eqref{e:UF0}_{2}

With a slight abuse of notation we let

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\rho(\mathbf{W})\\
q(\mathbf{W})\end{array}\right)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}p^{-1}(w-h)\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}h(\mathbf{U})\\
w(\mathbf{U})\end{array}\right)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\frac{q}{\rho}-p(\rho)\\

Notice that

F\left(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})\right)=V(h)\,p^{-1}(w-h)\,(1,w)^{T}.

(31)

The motivation to study the system in (
22

) or (
6
) is twofold.
First, using the Riemann invariants as dependent variables simplifies the forthcoming exposition and analysis.
Second, the fluxes in both (
1

We recall that, even if the vacuum is not initially present and does not appear immediately, a solution of (

For this reason, it is preferable to have a framework that can handle the vacuum.

Remark 3.2
.

According to

Remark 3.3
.

\displaystyle h_{*}(\mathbf{W}_{R},w_{L})\coloneqq\min\{w_{L},h_{R}\},

Proposition 3.5

Fix

•

•

Proof.

By (

x=0
, which in our case becomes
c^{\pm}\,\mathsf{f}(x=0^{\mp})\leqslant\mathsf{Q}(\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R})
.
The key difference is that we do not check whether the classical solution satisfies the constraint. Instead, we emphasize selecting, among the self-similar weak solutions that meet the constraint, the solution that optimizes the flow.

Remark 3.8
.

For any
(\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R})\in\mathcal{W}^{2}
such that
w_{L}\neq w_{R}
we have that the right most wave of
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}]
is a contact discontinuity with speed of propagation
c^{+}\,V(h_{R})
.
We stress that
2
-contact discontinuities always separate the two families of vehicles, characterized by the Lagrangian markers
w_{L}
and
w_{R}
.

Remark 3.9
.

It is easy to see that
\nu\mapsto\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}](\nu)
may have total variation greater than that of the initial datum, that is
|h_{L}-h_{R}|+|w_{L}-w_{R}|
; moreover the maximum principle holds for the
w
-coordinate, but may fail for the
h
-coordinate.
Indeed, if
\mathbf{W}_{L}=\mathbf{W}_{R}
and
c^{-}\neq c^{+}
, then
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{L}]
is not constant.
We defer the reader to Section
5
for a detailed construction of the solutions corresponding to the constant initial datum (
55
) and two choices of the function
c
given in (
56
), see also Figures
2
and
3
.

We conclude this section studying the main properties of the Riemann solver
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}
. Specifically, we consider the coherence, the
\mathbf{L^{1}_{loc}}-
continuity with respect to the initial datum and give a sufficient condition on
(\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R})
to ensure that
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}]
takes values in
\mathcal{W}_{e}
.
As in
[
22
, Definition 2.2]
, the coherence of the Riemann solver means that for any
\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}\in\mathcal{W}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}](0^{\pm})=\mathbf{W}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{-},\mathbf{W}_{+}](0^{\pm})=\mathbf{W}_{\pm}.

Observe that coherence can be thought as a stability property: the ordered pair of traces is, in a sense, a fixed point for
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}
.
Incoherence leads to the instability of the numerical solution, see for instance
[
23
, Figure 5-center]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

The Riemann solver
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}\colon\mathcal{W}^{2}\to\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W})
satisfies the following properties:

1)

It is coherent.

2)

It is
\mathbf{L^{1}_{loc}}-
continuous with respect to the initial datum.

3)

If
\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}\in\mathcal{W}_{e}
and
w_{L}>h_{R}
, then
\mathcal{RS}_{c^{-},c^{+}}[\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R}]
takes values in
\mathcal{W}_{e}
.

It is worth underlying that no condition on
c^{-}
and
c^{+}
are required in the above proposition.
The proof is deferred to Section
6.2
.

4.
On the entropy condition

In Definition
4.1
, we introduce the concept of weak solutions. In general, weak solutions may include non-physical shocks. This motivates the introduction of entropy solutions in Definition
4.2
to select physically admissible shocks.
To achieve this, we first construct a one-parameter family of entropy pairs, following the approach in
[
4
]
, and then provide the definition of entropy solutions.

We begin by rewriting system (
6
) in Riemann invariant coordinates, as shown in (
40
). Furthermore, for simplicity, we assume that
c
is piecewise constant; additional considerations regarding the general case are provided in Remark
4.3
.
Consider the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})+\partial_{x}\left(c\,F(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}))\right)=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{W}(0,x)=\overline{\mathbf{W}}(x),\end{cases}

(40)

where
F
is defined in
\eqref{e:UF0}_{2}
,
\mathbf{W}\coloneqq(h,w)^{T}
is the dependent variable expressed in terms of Riemann invariants, and the initial datum
\overline{\mathbf{W}}\coloneqq(\overline{h},\overline{w})^{T}
is assumed to be in
\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W})
, where
\mathcal{W}
is defined in (
21
).
With a slight abuse of notation, we define

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\rho(\mathbf{W})\\
q(\mathbf{W})\end{array}\right)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}p^{-1}(w-h)\\
p^{-1}(w-h)\,w\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{U})\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}h(\mathbf{U})\\
w(\mathbf{U})\end{array}\right)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\frac{q}{\rho}-p(\rho)\\
q/\rho\end{array}\right).

Notice that

F\left(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W})\right)=V(h)\,p^{-1}(w-h)\,(1,w)^{T}.

The motivation to study (
40
) rather than (
1
) or (
6
) is twofold.
First, the use of the Riemann invariants as independent variables eases the forthcoming analysis.
Second, the entropy pairs are well defined at the vacuum in the Riemann invariant coordinates
\mathbf{W}
, whereas they are multi-valued in the conserved coordinates
\mathbf{U}
.
Observe that, in general, even if the vacuum is not present initially and does not appear immediately, a solution of (
6
) can reach a vacuum state in finite time, see Remark
2.2
.
For this reason, a definition of solution possibly taking into account vacuum states is of interest.
As first step to cope with the vacuum, we extend the flux
F
to the whole
\mathcal{U}
by taking
F(0,0)\coloneqq 0
.
This choice is motivated by the observation that the flux
F(\mathbf{U})
is the product of
\mathbf{U}
and the uniformly bounded
V(h)
, where
0\leqslant h\leqslant w\leqslant\|\overline{w}\|_{\infty}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
weak solution
of the Cauchy problem (
40
) with
\overline{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W})
is a function
\mathbf{W}
in
\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W})\cap\mathbf{C^{0}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbf{L^{1}_{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W}))
that satisfies the initial condition a.e. in
\mathbb{R}
and for any test function
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{\infty}}\left((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R};[0,\infty)\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{-1}(w-h)\,\left(\varphi_{t}+c\,V(h)\,\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{-1}(w-h)\,w\,\left(\varphi_{t}+c\,V(h)\,\varphi_{x}\right)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=0.

Before giving the definition of entropy solution, we first construct a one parameter family of entropy pairs.
Away from the vacuum, we can apply
[
24
, (7.4.12)]
and obtain that an entropy pair
(\mathcal{E},c\,\mathcal{Q})
expressed in terms of the Riemann invariants satisfies the system

\displaystyle\partial_{h}\left(c(x)\,\mathcal{Q}\right)=\lambda_{1}\left(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}),x\right)\,\partial_{h}\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\partial_{w}\left(c(x)\,\mathcal{Q}\right)=\lambda_{2}\left(\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{W}),x\right)\,\partial_{w}\mathcal{E},

where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are respectively defined in
\eqref{e:lam1}_{1}
and
\eqref{e:lam2}_{1}
, together with the integrability condition
[
24
, (7.4.13)]

\rho(h,w)\,p^{\prime}\left(\rho(h,w)\right)\,\partial_{h}\partial_{w}\mathcal{E}+\left(1+\frac{\rho(h,w)\,p^{\prime\prime}\left(\rho(h,w)\right)}{p^{\prime}\left(\rho(h,w)\right)}\right)\,\partial_{h}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{w}\mathcal{E}=0,

where
\rho(\mathbf{W})
is defined in
\eqref{e:UW2}_{1}
.
The general solutions of the above system are

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(h,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\mathfrak{f}(w)-\int_{0}^{h}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(w-\nu)\,\mathfrak{g}(\nu)}{c\,V^{\prime}(\nu)}\,d\nu\right)p^{-1}(w-h),

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(h,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq V(h)\,\mathcal{E}(h,w)-\int_{0}^{h}\mathfrak{g}(\nu)\,d\nu+\mathfrak{b},

where
\mathfrak{f}
and
\mathfrak{g}
are sufficiently smooth functions,
\mathfrak{b}
is an arbitrary constant and

\zeta(\nu)\coloneqq 1/p^{-1}(\nu).

In analogy to
[
4
, (2.13)]
, we consider the one parameter family of entropy pairs obtained from (
49
) by taking
\mathfrak{f}\equiv 0
,
\mathfrak{g}(\nu)\coloneqq V^{\prime}(\nu)\,\sgn_{+}(\nu-k)
and
\mathfrak{b}=0
and defined for any
k\geqslant 0
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k}(h,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}1-\frac{p^{-1}(w-h)}{p^{-1}(w-k)}&\hbox{if }h\in(k,w],\\
0&\hbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k}(h,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}V(k)-V(h)\,\frac{p^{-1}(w-h)}{p^{-1}(w-k)}&\hbox{if }h\in(k,w],\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We are now in the position to give the definition of entropy solution analogous to
[
37
, Definition 1.1]
.

Definition 4.2
.

A weak solution
\mathbf{W}
of the Cauchy problem (
40
) with
\overline{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{W})
is an
entropy solution
if for any test function
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{\infty}}\left((0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}\});[0,\infty)\right)
and for any
k>0

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbf{W})\,\partial_{t}\varphi+c\,\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\mathbf{W})\,\partial_{x}\varphi\right)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\geqslant 0.

(54)

Furthermore, if
\mathbf{W}
has a discontinuity along the curve
x=s(t)
, then its traces
\mathbf{W}^{-}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{W}(t,s(t)^{-})
and
\mathbf{W}^{+}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{W}(t,s(t)^{+})
satisfy the following conditions:

(A)

For a.e.
t>0
, if the discontinuity
x=s(t)
occurs away from
x=\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, and involves a vacuum state, then

\mathcal{RS}_{c(s(t))}[\mathbf{W}^{-}(t),\mathbf{W}^{+}(t)](\nu)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{W}^{-}(t)&\text{if }\nu<s(t),\\
\mathbf{W}^{+}(t)&\text{if }\nu\geqslant s(t).\end{cases}

(B)

For a.e.
t>0
, if the discontinuity
x=s(t)
occurs at
x=\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, then

\mathcal{RS}_{c(\xi_{i}^{-}),c(\xi_{i}^{+})}[\mathbf{W}^{-}(t),\mathbf{W}^{+}(t)](\nu)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{W}^{-}(t)&\text{if }\nu<s(t),\\
\mathbf{W}^{+}(t)&\text{if }\nu\geqslant s(t).\end{cases}

Some comments on the above definition are in order.
Differently from the test functions considered for the entropy condition (
13
) in Theorem
2.1
, those in (
54
) are supported outside the set of discontinuities
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}\}
of the flux
c\,F
.
Hence, condition (
54
) deals with discontinuity of
\mathbf{W}
away from the discontinuity points
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}\}
; the discontinuities of
\mathbf{W}
at such points are addressed in
(B)
.
Furthermore, in (
54
) we exploit the entropy pairs (
4
), which do not select a physically reasonable solution if a vacuum state is involved, see
[
4
, Remark 2.1]
; this motivates condition
(A)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Assume now that
c
satisfies (
5
) and is not piecewise constant.
In this case, we cannot introduce a (self-similar) Riemann solver.
For this reason it would be desirable to rely again on condition (
54
) and the entropy pairs in (
4
) to select physically admissible shocks, at least away from the vacuum.
However, this is not possible.
Indeed, on one hand we can understand if a shock between
\mathbf{W}^{-}\coloneqq(h_{L},\bar{w})
and
\mathbf{W}^{+}\coloneqq(h_{R},\bar{w})
is physically admissible by considering entropy pairs given in (
4
) with
k
between
h_{L}
and
h_{R}
.
On the other hand, as underlined in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, see Section
6.1
, if
c
is not piecewise constant, then the entropy pairs have to satisfy also condition (
65
) in Section
6
, which is equivalent to (
12
).
Unfortunately, we have that:

(A)

The entropy pair given by (
4
) satisfies (
12
) if and only if
k\in\{0,w\}
.

This has a severe drawback because, as we mentioned before, the entropy pairs
(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{Q}_{0})
and
(\mathcal{E}_{w},\mathcal{Q}_{w})
alone are not sufficient to select physically reasonable shocks.
We have in fact that:

(B)

A discontinuity away from
\xi_{i}
,
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
, between
\mathbf{W}^{-}\coloneqq(h_{L},\bar{w})
and
\mathbf{W}^{+}\coloneqq(h_{R},\bar{w})
with
h_{L}<h_{R}
satisfies the entropy condition (
54
) with
k\in\{0,w\}
.

Observe that the shock described in
(B)
is not physically reasonable.

We prove now
(A)
.
The case
k=w
is indeed trivial because
(\mathcal{E}_{w},\mathcal{Q}_{w})\equiv(0,0)
.
Assume now that
k<w
.
If we rewrite the entropy pair given in (
4
) in the conservative coordinates, then for any
h\in(k,w]
we get

\displaystyle D_{\mathbf{U}}\mathcal{E}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{U})\right)\cdot F(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\frac{V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)}{p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}-p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)-\frac{q}{\rho\,p^{\prime}\left(p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)\right)}\\
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}\left(p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)\right)}\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\rho\\
q\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)\,\frac{\rho}{p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{U})\right)

\displaystyle=V(k)-V\left(h(\mathbf{U})\right)\,\frac{\rho}{p^{-1}\left(\frac{q}{\rho}-k\right)},

and by imposing condition (
12
) we obtain that
V(k)=0
, namely
k=0
.
This ends the proof of
(A)
.

We prove now
(B)
, namely that if
k\in\{0,w\}
then

\left(\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbf{W}_{R})-\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbf{W}_{L})\right)\,c(x)\,s(\mathbf{W}_{L},\mathbf{W}_{R})-c(x)\,\left(\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\mathbf{W}_{R})-\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\mathbf{W}_{L})\right)=0,

where
s
is defined in (
32
) and
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}\}
is the position where the discontinuity occurs.
The case
k=w
is indeed trivial because
(\mathcal{E}_{w},\mathcal{Q}_{w})\equiv(0,0)
.
If
k=0
, then the above condition writes

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p^{-1}(w-h_{L})}{p^{-1}(w)}-\frac{p^{-1}(w-h_{R})}{p^{-1}(w)}\right)\,\frac{V(h_{R})\,p^{-1}(w-h_{R})-V(h_{L})\,p^{-1}(w-h_{L})}{p^{-1}(w-h_{R})-p^{-1}(w-h_{L})}

\displaystyle-\left(-V(h_{R})\,\frac{p^{-1}(w-h_{R})}{p^{-1}(w)}+V(h_{L})\,\frac{p^{-1}(w-h_{L})}{p^{-1}(w)}\right)=0,

which is clearly satisfied for any
w>0
.
This completes the proof of
(B)
.

Proof of Theorem

(60)

\mathbf{X}\coloneqq(\rho,q,\zeta)^{T}

\overline{\mathbf{X}}
take values in

\Omega_{e}\coloneqq\{(\rho,q,\zeta)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:(\rho+\rho_{\infty},q+q_{\infty})\in\mathcal{U}_{e},\ \zeta+c_{\infty}>0\},

\mathcal{U}_{e}

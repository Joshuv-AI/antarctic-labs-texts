NT-proBNP–Based Heart Failure Risk Score for Asymptomatic Diabetic Population in Asia
Authors: Jiun-Lu Lin, Yen-Wen Wu, Kuo-Meng Liao, Sung-Chen Liu, Chung-Ming Tu, Heng-Hsu Lin, Yi-Fu Lin, Chien-Yi Hsu, Hung-Yu Chang, Kai-Chien Yang, Ying-Ju Chen, Tze-Fan Chao, Yi-Heng Li, Chung-Lieh Hung, Chih-Yuan Wang
Published: 2026

Heart failure (HF) is a common but often underappreciated complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) that may be asymptomatic in its early stages. Recent guidelines emphasize the need to identify asymptomatic individuals with diabetes at high risk or with preclinical HF (stage A/B) to prevent progression to advanced HF.1 However, risk stratification in East Asian populations is impeded by unique cardiometabolic phenotypes2 that diminish the validity of Western-derived models include the WATCH-DM (Weight [BMI], Age, hypertension, Creatinine, HDL-C, Diabetes control [fasting plasma glucose], QRS Duration, Myocardial Infarction [MI], and Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting [CABG]) risk score and the Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction Risk Score for Heart Failure in Diabetes (TRS-HFDM).3,4 These models possess notable methodological limitations for primary prevention screening; TRS-HFDM incorporates patients with previous HF, confounding the prediction of incident events,4 whereas WATCH-DM relies solely on clinical variables, overlooking the prognostic use of biomarkers.3

N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) is a potent prognostic marker for HF among individuals with diabetes.5 The American Diabetes Association Consensus Report suggests considering NT-proBNP measurement to screen asymptomatic adults with diabetes to enable early intervention and prevent the progression to advanced stages of HF6; however, its integrated application for screening in East Asian populations remains underexplored. This study aimed to develop and externally validate diabetes mellitus heart failure events analysis and risk tracking (DM-HEART) (NCT05866380), a novel NT-proBNP–integrated prediction model, targeting East Asian individuals with diabetes and asymptomatic stages of HF, to bridge the gap between ethnic heterogeneity and early risk stratification.

This study adhered to the TRIPOD reporting guidelines. This retrospective study pooled individual-level data from 2 Taiwanese health systems. The derivation cohort included 5,544 patients with T2DM from MacKay Memorial Hospital and the validation cohort comprised 1,325 patients from the National Taiwan University Hospital, with data collected between January 1, 2014, and September 30, 2021. This study was approved by Institutional Review Boards at MacKay Memorial Hospital (20MMHIS365e) and National Taiwan University Hospital (2104041RINB), and all data were de-identified with the requirement for consent waived.

Eligibility criteria required age ≥20 years, T2DM duration ≥6 months, and established cardiovascular disease or at least 1 cardiovascular risk factor (≥50 years of age, dyslipidemia, hypertension, body mass index [BMI] ≥25 kg/m2, proteinuria, estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min/1.73 m2, or T2DM duration >10 years). Patients with previous HF or hospitalization for heart failure (HHF), known HF (stage C/D), left ventricular ejection fraction <40%, end-stage renal disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate <15 mL/min/1.73 m2), or dialysis were excluded.

Thirty-four candidate predictors were assembled, building on WATCH-DM variables and expanding to clinical, laboratory, and electrocardiographic features, including NT-proBNP. Collinearity (Pearson’s correlation ≥0.7) was addressed by selecting representative variables (hypertension instead of systolic blood pressure/diastolic blood pressure, creatinine for renal function, and QRS duration for electrocardiography indices). BMI categories followed Asian thresholds (<23, 23-24.9, 25-29.9, ≥30 kg/m2). The primary endpoint was incident HHF, characterized by: 1) primary HF admission with typical signs/symptoms and objective congestion; 2) NT-proBNP above age-stratified cutoffs; and 3) intensified diuretic therapy (oral escalation or intravenous loop diuretics).

Univariate Cox screening, followed by multivariable Cox regression with backward elimination, was used to derive a 5-year risk model. Multicollinearity was checked using variance inflation factors (<4). The proportional hazards assumption was evaluated by examining the correlation between the ranked survival time and Schoenfeld residuals, and all correlation coefficients were low (P < 0.3). A nomogram and simplified point-based score were constructed from regression coefficients and baseline survival. Discrimination was assessed using Harrell’s C-statistic. Calibration compared the observed and predicted risk. External validation fixed the derivation coefficients to evaluate generalizability (restricted vs unrestricted models). Risk stratification used 5 score bands (<10, 10-19, 20-29, 30-39, ≥40).

This study included 6,869 patients with T2DM (derivation: n = 5,544; validation: n = 1,325). Derivation and validation median follow-up was 3.7 years (Q1-Q3: 1.9-5.6 years) and 1.7 years (Q1-Q3: 0.8-3.3 years), respectively. HHF occurred in 473 (8.5%) and 222 (16.8%) patients, respectively. In multivariable analysis, retained predictors included WATCH-DM components (age group, previous coronary artery bypass grafting, previous myocardial infarction, BMI category, hypertension, QRS duration ≥120 ms, serum creatinine), with additional independent predictors: current smoking (HR: 1.44; 95% CI: 1.15-1.81), left ventricular hypertrophy (HR: 1.91, 95% CI: 1.26-2.91), heart rate per 5 beats/min (HR: 1.05, 95% CI: 1.02-1.07), and NT-proBNP per 50 pg/mL (HR: 1.29, 95% CI: 1.22-1.38). The model demonstrated good discrimination (C-statistic = 0.751, 95% CI: 0.729-0.773). The C-statistic after correction for optimism was 0.741 for 2,000 bootstrap samples. Risk stratification in the derivation cohort showed graded 5-year event rates; 1.3%, 3.0%, 6.0%, 16.6%, and 23.9% across score bands 0 to 9 (n = 690), 10 to 19 (n = 1557), 20 to 29 (n = 1625), 30 to 39 (n = 1090), and ≥40 (n = 582) (P < 0.001), respectively. External validation exhibited acceptable discrimination performance (C-statistic = 0.712 [95% CI: 0.670-0.754] and 0.693 [95% CI: 0.652-0.735] in the unrestricted and restricted models, respectively), with preserved risk gradients of 4.9%, 6.1%, 13.8%, 21.9%, and 38.9% across the same score bands (n = 102, 311, 407, 320, and 185, respectively; P < 0.001). Calibration was acceptable in both cohorts (Figures 1B and 1C). A brief 4-predictor version (age, NT-proBNP, QRS duration ≥120 ms, creatinine groups) achieved a C-statistic of 0.736 (95% CI: 0.714-0.758) (Figure 1D), significantly exceeding NT-proBNP alone (ΔC-statistic = 0.031; P = 0.001).

DM-HEART advances HF risk prediction for East Asian patients with T2DM by: 1) studying an East Asian population with distinct ethnic risk factors; 2) focusing on at-risk/preclinical HF (stages A/B) by excluding those with established HF; 3) incorporating NT-proBNP into a point-based score with external validation; and 4) identifying a lower NT-proBNP threshold (77.5 pg/mL) that effectively signals future HF in asymptomatic patients, facilitating earlier intervention. The model highlights pathophysiological contributors beyond glycemia, with elevated NT-proBNP, previous coronary artery bypass grafting/myocardial infarction, cardiac conduction/structural markers, renal dysfunction, and heart rate as significant predictors, whereas fasting glucose and glycosylated hemoglobin levels did not independently predict HF events. The U-shaped risk across Asian BMI categories underscores the relevance of lean diabetes phenotypes.

Overall, the NT-proBNP–integrated DM-HEART model showed superior 5-year HF prediction compared with WATCH-DM, enabling robust preclinical risk stratification and targeted prevention among asymptomatic East Asian individuals with diabetes.

1.
Introduction

In many areas of physics and engineering, such as X-ray crystallography, astronomy, quantum mechanics, or speech recognition, it is often necessary to
recover
a signal from the measurement of a
phaseless quantity
associated with it, such as the intensity of an electromagnetic or acoustic wave, or the probability density of the wave function of a quantum system. In these situations, the sign or phase of the signal (depending on whether it has a real or complex nature) can contain relevant information impossible to obtain directly from measurement devices, which can only record the absolute value. Therefore, a mathematical approach is needed for its recovery. These kinds of problems are known as
phase retrieval problems
(see the surveys
[
18
,
19
]
for an overview of the topic). It should be noted that, given a fixed function, any scalar of modulus one multiplied by the function will always have the same absolute value, so there will always be an unavoidable ambiguity in the process of recovering a function from its absolute value. Moreover, many of the examples originate from experimental disciplines, where experimental errors can perturb the original signal; therefore, a study of the stability of the recovery process is also necessary (
stable phase retrieval problems
).

For modeling phase retrieval problems,
function spaces
offer a natural framework. These include, for instance,
L_{p}
-spaces
or
spaces of continuous functions on compact Hausdorff spaces
(for short,
C(K)
-spaces
). However, it should be noted that the formulation of (stable) phase retrieval problems only requires a linear and (potentially) a normed structure, and an operation of absolute value or modulus. This is the reason why the theory of Banach lattices, which are an abstract generalization of function spaces whose properties are defined precisely by these three factors (see
[
4
,
23
,
24
]
for standard notation and basic definitions), has provided very useful tools to tackle these questions
[
12
,
17
]
. This approach has been successfully applied in
L_{p}
-spaces to construct infinite-dimensional subspaces doing stable phase retrieval
[
11
,
13
,
17
]
. In contrast, and despite of being an extensively studied family of Banach lattices,
C(K)
-spaces have received little attention in the context of stable phase retrieval, aside from what is found in
[
17
]
. Thus, the objective of this work is to continue the study of stable phase retrieval in
C(K)
-spaces initiated in
[
17
]
.

1.1.
Motivation and preliminary results

As explained in
[
17
]
, Banach lattices provide a suitable general setting for many phase retrieval problems, such as Fourier phase retrieval, Gabor phase retrieval or wavelet phase retrieval, and even other inverse problems such as audio declipping
[
1
]
.
In these examples, the problem consists in uniquely recovering an element
f
of a Banach space
F
from
|Tf|
, which is the modulus of the image of
f
by an invertible operator
T
from
F
into a suitable target Banach lattice
X
. In the case of Fourier phase retrieval,
F=X=L_{2}
and
T
is the Fourier transform, whereas for the Gabor and wavelet phase retrieval,
T
corresponds to the analysis operator of a certain frame on the Banach space
F
. These problems can be split into two parts: the first one is to retrieve
Tf
(up to a global phase) from
|Tf|
within the subspace
E=T(F)\subseteq X
, and the second one is to invert
T
, a problem which is much better understood. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 1.1

(
[
17
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A subspace
E
of a Banach lattice
X
does
phase retrieval (PR)
if whenever
x,y\in E
satisfy
|x|=|y|
, it follows that
x=\lambda y
for some unimodular scalar
\lambda
.

Consider the equivalence relation
\sim
given by
x\sim y
if and only if
x=\lambda y
for some unimodular scalar
\lambda
. The fact that a subspace
E\subseteq X
does PR is equivalent to the injectivity, or equivalently, the invertibility, of the map that sends any equivalence class of
E/\sim
to the absolute value in
X
of a representative of the class. However, in applications it is also necessary to understand
how stable
is the inverse map under small perturbations, such as experimental errors. That is, studying if the inverse map has some kind of continuity, and more specifically, Lipschitz continuity:

Definition 1.2

(
[
17
, Definition 3.2]
)
.

A subspace
E
of a Banach lattice
X
does
C
-stable phase retrieval
(
C
-SPR
, for short) if

(1.1)

\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|x-\lambda y\|\leq C\,\bigl\||x|-|y|\bigr\|

for every
x,y\in E
. We will say that
E
does
stable phase retrieval (SPR)
if it does
C
-SPR for some constant
C\geq 1
.

Although the last two definitions can be considered for both real and complex Banach lattices, in this article we will restrict ourselves to the real framework. It is important to point out that in this setting
the main and only obstruction
that can cause a subspace
E
of a Banach lattice
X
to fail PR is the existence of pairs of disjoint vectors in
E
, that is, non-zero functions
f,g\in E
such that
|f|\land|g|=0
(or, equivalently,
fg=0
if
X
is a function space). This fact appears implicitly in
[
17
]
and its proof can be found in
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
. Motivated by the aforementioned characterization of phase retrieval, the authors in
[
17
]
consider the notion of
almost disjoint pairs
with the aim of studying SPR:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
E
be a subspace of a Banach lattice
X
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, we say that
f,g\in S_{E}
is a
(normalized)
\varepsilon
-almost disjoint pair
if
\||f|\wedge|g|\|\leq\varepsilon
. If
E
contains
\varepsilon
-almost disjoint pairs for every
\varepsilon>0
, we say that
E
contains
almost disjoint pairs
.

In fact, almost disjoint pairs were used in
[
17
, Theorem 3.4]
to provide a powerful characterization of SPR for real scalars, which reads as follows: the subspaces of a Banach lattice which do stable phase retrieval are precisely those that do not contain almost disjoint pairs. We present below a refinement of this result due to E. Bilokopytov
[
8
, Proposition 3.4]
. It is also worth mentioning that a complex version of it was established in
[
12
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
E
be a subspace of a (real) Banach lattice
X
, and let
C>0
be a constant.
E
does
C
-SPR if and only if
E
does not contain any
\frac{1}{C}
-almost disjoint pair. In particular,
E
does SPR if and only if it does not contain almost disjoint pairs.

This characterization of stable phase retrieval in terms of the non-existence of almost disjoint pairs is one of the key ingredients used by Freeman, Oikhberg, Pineau and Taylor to show that a
C(K)
-space contains an infinite-dimensional subspace doing SPR if and only if
K^{\prime}
is infinite
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
, where
K^{\prime}
stands for the set of non-isolated (or accumulation) points of
K
. More specifically, these authors proved the following (see also
[
8
, Theorem 5.3]
):

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
K
is a compact Hausdorff space. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

K^{\prime}
is infinite.

(ii)

c_{0}
embeds isometrically into
C(K)
as an SPR subspace.

(iii)

C(K)
contains an (infinite-dimensional) SPR subspace.

In light of the previous result, the authors ask whether a
large compact space
K
, in terms of the smallest ordinal
\alpha
for which
K^{(\alpha)}
is non-empty, guarantees the existence of spaces
bigger
than
c_{0}
that can be embedded into
C(K)
in an SPR way. More concretely, and taking into account the fact that
c_{0}
is isomorphic to
c=C[1,\omega]
, they raise the following question:

Question 1
.

[
17
, Question 6.4]

If
K^{(\alpha)}
is infinite, does
C(K)
contain an SPR copy of
C[0,\omega^{\alpha}]
?

Recall that for any ordinal
\alpha
we can consider the
ordinal interval

[0,\alpha]=\{\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\}
. It can be shown that these sets, when equipped with the topology generated by the subbase of all sets of the form
[0,\beta_{1})
and
(\beta_{2},\alpha]
, for
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in[0,\alpha]
, are scattered compact Hausdorff spaces
[
26
, Corollary 8.6.7]
. Note that the interval
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}]
is a clopen set in this topology for every
\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
. Moreover, observe that
[0,\omega^{\alpha}]
is homeomorphic to
[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
whenever
\alpha\geq 1
, and hence the spaces
C[0,\omega^{\alpha}]
and
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
are lattice isometric. For our purposes, it will be convenient to use the notation
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, and thus we we will do so throughout this work. Finally, recall that every ordinal
\alpha>0
can be represented uniquely as

(1.2)

\alpha=w^{\beta_{1}}\cdot k_{1}+\ldots+\omega^{\beta_{n}}\cdot k_{n},

where
n\geq 1
,
\alpha\geq\beta_{1}>\ldots>\beta_{n}\geq 0
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
are non-zero natural numbers. This representation is known as
Cantor’s normal form
.

With the aim of better understanding Question
1
, we dedicate this article to analyzing the following problem:

Main Question
.

Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space and let
\alpha
be a finite ordinal. If
K^{(\alpha)}
is non-empty, does
C(K)
contain a subspace isometric to
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
which do stable phase retrieval?

We would like to clarify two important aspects regarding this new question. On the one hand, the
isomorphic counterpart
of it has a trivial answer, since
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
is isomorphic to
c_{0}
whenever
1\leq\alpha<\omega
. Second, the
Main Question
is not a direct adaptation of Question
1
to the isometric case: we are relaxing the hypothesis of
K^{(\alpha)}
being infinite to
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
.

1.2.
Summary of the results

As previously mentioned, this article is devoted to studying the

, which is deeply inspired by

1.5
.

2.
Some observations on the Main Question

This section is devoted to proving two
simple observations
regarding the
Main Question
:

(1)

We start by showing the necessity of assuming the hypothesis that
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
. In particular, we prove that if
C(K)
contains an isometric copy of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, then
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
.

(2)

Next, we will show that our question has a negative answer for
\alpha=1
. Even though this is already proven in
Theorem

1.5

[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
, here we will extend this fact to general Banach lattices (not necessarily
C(K)
-spaces).

Recall that
K^{\prime}
is the set of accumulation points (or non-isolated points) of
K
, also known as the
derived set
of
K
. This operation over the set
K
can be iterated
\alpha
times for any ordinal
\alpha
, yielding the
\alpha
-th Cantor–Bendixson derivative of
K
, denoted
K^{(\alpha)}
. We now prove the first fact; despite being probably well-known, we have not found a reference for it.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume the field of scalars is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\alpha<\omega
be a finite ordinal and let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space. If
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
isometrically embeds into
C(K)
, then
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Let us first establish the notation. Given
\alpha\in\omega
, we will denote by
\mathds{1}
the constant function on
[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Given a natural number
m_{1}\geq 1
,
\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}
will represent the characteristic function of the clopen set

(\omega^{\alpha-1}\cdot(m_{1}-1),\omega^{\alpha-1}\cdot m_{1}].

Similarly, given natural numbers
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
,
\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2}}
will stand for the characteristic function of the clopen set

\bigl(\omega^{\alpha-1}\cdot(m_{1}-1)+\omega^{\alpha-2}\cdot(m_{2}-1),\omega^{\alpha-1}\cdot(m_{1}-1)+\omega^{\alpha-2}\cdot m_{2}\bigr].

In the same way, we can define the functions
\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}
,
\ldots
,
\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}
. It should be noted that, given
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{\alpha}\geq 1
,
\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}
is precisely the characteristic function of the singleton

\bigl\{\omega^{\alpha-1}\cdot(m_{1}-1)+\omega^{\alpha-2}\cdot(m_{2}-1)+\cdots+\omega\cdot(m_{\alpha-1}-1)+m_{\alpha}\bigr\}.

Now, given an isometric embedding
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\to C(K)
and natural numbers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}\geq 1
, there must exist
t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\in K
such that

(2.1)

\bigl|\bigl(T\mathds{1}+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\cdots+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\bigr)(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})\bigr|=\alpha+1.

Indeed, keep in mind that
\mathds{1}+\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\cdots+\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}
has norm
\alpha+1
in
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
and
T
is norm-preserving. From the expression (
2.1
), given that
\|T\mathds{1}\|=\|T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}\|=\cdots=\|T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\|=1
, we deduce the existence of some unimodular scalar
\theta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}
such that

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})=T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})=\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})=\theta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}.

We are going to show by induction on
\alpha\in\omega
that if for every
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}\geq 1
we have a point
t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\in K
satisfying (
2.1
), then there exists
t_{0}\in\overline{\{t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}\geq 1\}}
which belongs to
K^{(\alpha)}
. Observe that as
|T\mathds{1}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}})|=1
for all natural numbers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}
, then
|T\mathds{1}(t_{0})|=1
.

If
\alpha=0
, the previous statement is obvious. Let us assume that it is true for
\alpha
. Let
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha+1}]\to C(K)
be an isometric embedding. Following the notation established above, we know that for every
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}\geq 1
, there is
t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}\in K
and
\theta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}
of modulus 1 such that

\bigl|\bigl(T\mathds{1}+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\cdots+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}\bigr)(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})\bigr|=\alpha+2.

and

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})=T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})=\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})=\theta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}.

In particular,

|(T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\cdots+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})(t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}})|=\|T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\cdots+T\mathds{1}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}\|=\alpha+1.

Now observe that for every
m_{1}\geq 1
, the compact
(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(m_{1}-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot m_{1}]
is homeomorphic to
[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. So, if for every
m_{1}\geq 1
, we restrict
T
to
C(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(m_{1}-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot m_{1}]
(which, in this case, stands for the continuous functions on
[1,\omega^{\alpha+1}]
which vanish outside the clopen set
(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(m_{1}-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot m_{1}]
), the inductive hypothesis ensures the existence of a point
t_{m_{1}}\in\overline{\{t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m_{2},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}\geq 1\}}\cap K^{(\alpha)}
with
|T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1}})|=1
.

Next, we are going to show that these elements
\{t_{m_{1}}\}_{m_{1}}
are distinct. Suppose that
t_{m_{1}}=t_{m_{1}^{\prime}}
for some
m_{1},m_{1}^{\prime}\geq 1
with
m_{1}\neq m_{1}^{\prime}
. Let
\theta_{m_{1}},\theta_{m_{1}^{\prime}}
be scalars of modulus
1
such that
T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1}})=\theta_{m_{1}}
and
T\mathds{1}_{m_{1}^{\prime}}(t_{m_{1}^{\prime}})=\theta_{m_{1}^{\prime}}
. Observe that
\theta_{m_{1}}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\theta_{m_{1}^{\prime}}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{m_{1}^{\prime}}
has norm

in
C[1,\omega^{\alpha+1}]
, but
T(\theta_{m_{1}}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{m_{1}}+\theta_{m_{1}^{\prime}}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{m_{1}^{\prime}})(t_{m_{1}})=2
, which is a contradiction with the fact that
T
is norm-preserving. Hence,
(t_{m_{1}})_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of distinct points in
\bigcup_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}(\overline{\{t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m_{2},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}\geq 1\}}\cap K^{(\alpha)})\subseteq\overline{\{t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha}}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}\geq 1\}}\cap K^{(\alpha)}
. Thus, there exists
t_{0}\in\overline{\{t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}\geq 1\}}\cap K^{({\alpha+1})}
. In particular,
K^{({\alpha+1})}\neq\varnothing
, as we wanted to show. Moreover,
|T\mathds{1}(t_{0})|=1
, since
|T\mathds{1}|
takes the value 1 at every point
t_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\alpha+1}}
.
∎

For the second result, we need to recall some definitions. An
atom
of a Banach lattice
X
is a non-zero positive element
x_{0}\in X
such that whenever
0\leq x\leq x_{0}
, it follows that
x=\lambda x_{0}
for some positive scalar
\lambda
. The span of an atom is always a projection band and its corresponding band projection
P_{x_{0}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightarrow X
is given by
P_{x_{0}}x=\lambda_{x_{0}}(x)x_{0}
,
x\in X
, where
\lambda_{x_{0}}
is a real-valued lattice homomorphism. This lattice homomorphism is called the
coordinate functional
associated to
x_{0}
(
[
22
, p. 8]
, see also the paragraph preceding Proposition 2.1 in
[
9
]
). The proof of the following result mimics the
gliding hump argument
used in
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
to prove the
necessary condition
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume the field of scalars is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let
X
be a Banach lattice that contains an infinite-dimensional SPR subspace. Then, the closed span of the atoms of
X
has infinite codimension in
X
.

Proof.

Let
E\subseteq X
be an infinite-dimensional SPR subspace, and assume that
X_{0}
, the closed span of the atoms of
X
, has finite codimension. It follows that
E_{0}=E\cap X_{0}
has infinite dimension. Let
x\in S_{E_{0}}
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists a linear combination of atoms
y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{x_{i}}(y)x_{i}
, where
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are atoms and
(\lambda_{x_{i}})_{i=1}^{n}
their corresponding coordinate functionals, such that
\|x-y\|<\varepsilon
. Again,
E_{0}\cap\mathinner{\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\lambda_{x_{i}}\right)}
must be infinite-dimensional, so in particular we can find a norm-one vector
z
in this subspace. Note that
z
is disjoint from
y
(i.e., supported on different atoms). Hence,

|x|\wedge|z|\leq\bigl(|x-y|+|y|\bigr)\wedge|z|\leq|x-y|\wedge|z|+|y|\wedge|z|=|x-y|\wedge|z|\leq|x-y|,

and
x
and

form a normalized
\varepsilon
-almost disjoint pair in
E
. Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary,
Theorem

1.4
(or
[
12
, Proposition 2.5]
in the complex setting), yields a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Suppose that
X=C(K)
, for some compact Hausdorff space

, in the above proposition. Observe that an atom in

is precisely a multiple of the characteristic function of a singleton

, where

has a negative answer for

.

3.
A simplification of the question

3.1.
“Nice” embeddings that preserve SPR subspaces

We have shown in
2.1
that the existence of an isometric copy of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
inside
C(K)
implies that
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
. But it is also natural to ask about the
converse statement
(which is less ambitious than the
Main Question
): if
K^{(\alpha)}
is non-empty, does there exist an isometric embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C(K)
? We will prove in
3.5
that this is the case, and furthermore, that the constructed embedding can be made to satisfy a property that is
very convenient
for our purposes: the isometric embedding does preserve SPR subspaces. This will allow us to reduce the
Main Question
to studying isometric SPR embeddings between spaces of the form
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, which are easier to manipulate.

It should be noticed that an elementary example of embeddings that preserve SPR subspaces are precisely lattice embeddings (that is, those that preserve lattice operations). Indeed, suppose that
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to Y
is a lattice isometric embedding between two Banach lattices
X
and
Y
, and let
E
be a subspace of
X
which does
C
-SPR. Then, for every
x,y\in E
, we have

\frac{1}{\|T\|}\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|Tx-\lambda Ty\|\leq\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|x-\lambda y\|\leq C\left\||x|-|y|\right\|=C\bigl\|T(|x|-|y|)\bigr\|=C\bigl\||Tx|-|Ty|\bigr\|,

and hence
T(E)
does
C\|T\|
-SPR in
Y
.

Although in general the condition
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
does not guarantee the existence of a lattice isometric embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C(K)
(see
3.6
), we will show in
3.5
that it is still possible to construct a
nice
isometric embedding, in the sense that it preserves SPR subspaces. The crucial property of these
nice
embeddings will be condition
(*)
of the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K,L
be compact Hausdorff spaces and
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C(L)\to C(K)
an operator with the following property:

(*)

for every
t\in L
there is
s_{t}\in K
such that
Tf(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C(L)
.

Then, if
E
is an SPR subspace of
C(L)
,
T(E)
is an SPR subspace of
C(K)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that if an operator
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C(L)\to C(K)
has the property (*) defined above, then
\|Tf\|\geq\|f\|
for every
f\in C(L)
. Therefore, if
E
is a subspace of
C(L)
, then it is
\|T\|
-isomorphic to
T(E)
; in particular, if
\|T\|=1
,
T
is a linear isometry.

Proof of
3.1
.

Let us suppose that
E
is a
C
-SPR subspace of
C(L)
for some constant
C\geq 1
, that is, for any
f,g\in E
we have

\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|f-\lambda g\|\leq C\||f|-|g|\|.

Given any
f,g\in C(L)
and any
t\in L
,

\bigl(|Tf|-|Tg|\bigr)(s_{t})=|Tf(s_{t})|-|Tg(s_{t})|=|f(t)|-|g(t)|=\bigl(|f|-|g|\bigr)(t),

and hence
\||f|-|g|\|\leq\||Tf|-|Tg|\|
. With this in mind, we obtain for any
f,g\in E
the following:

\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|Tf-\lambda Tg\|\leq\|T\|\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|f-\lambda g\|\leq C\|T\|\||f|-|g|\|\leq C\|T\|\||Tf|-|Tg|\|.

This shows that
T(E)\subseteq C(K)
does
C\|T\|
-SPR.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

It should be noted that
3.1
could also be stated (and proven in the same way) for locally compact Hausdorff spaces as follows: given locally compact Hausdorff spaces
K
and
L
, then any operator
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{0}(L)\to C_{0}(K)
satisfying

(*)

for every
t\in L
there is
s_{t}\in K
such that
Tf(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C_{0}(L)

preserves SPR subspaces. As usual,
C_{0}(K)
stands for

\bigl\{f\in C(K)\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>\forall\varepsilon>0\>\text{ }\exists\,G\subseteq K\text{ compact space s.t. }|f(t)|<\varepsilon\text{ }\>\forall t\notin G\bigr\}.

Next, we prove the aforementioned result, which will allow us to simplify the
Main Question
substantially. Before stating it, we recall a very well-known topological lemma that we will use frequently throughout the proof: every compact Hausdorff space is regular (cf.
[
25
, Theorem 29.2]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
K
is a compact Hausdorff space, and
t\in U\subseteq K
, where
U
is an open set. Then there exists an open set
V
so that
t\in V\subseteq\overline{V}\subseteq U
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha<\omega
be a finite ordinal and let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space. Then, if
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
, there exists a positive linear isometry
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\to C(K)
that preserves the unit, that is,
T\mathds{1}=\mathds{1}
, and has the property (*), i.e., for every
t\in[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
there is
s_{t}\in K
such that
Tf(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Therefore,
T
preserves SPR subspaces.

Proof.

First, we will show by induction on
1\leq\alpha<\omega
that if
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
and
V
is an open subset of
K
such that there exists
s_{0}\in V\cap K^{(\alpha)}
, then there is a positive linear isometry
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha})\to C_{0}(V)
with property (*), i.e., for every
t\in[1,\omega^{\alpha})
there exists
s_{t}\in V
such that
Sf(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha})
and, moreover,
Sf(s_{0})=0
for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha})
.
Note that if
f\in C_{0}(V)
, then it can be extended to
K
by simply defining
\widetilde{f}(t)=f(t)
for
t\in V
and
\widetilde{f}(t)=0
for
t\in K\backslash V
. Indeed, it is clear that
\widetilde{f}
is continuous on the open subsets
V
and
K\backslash\overline{V}
. Now, fix any
s_{0}\in\overline{V}\backslash V
and
\varepsilon>0
. By definition, there is a compact subset
L\subseteq V
such that
|f(t)|<\varepsilon
for all
s\in V\backslash L
. Thus,
K\backslash L
is an open neighborhood of
s_{0}
such that
|\widetilde{f}(t)|<\varepsilon
whenever
t\in K\backslash L
.

Let us begin by considering the base case
\alpha=1
. Suppose that there is
s_{0}\in V\cap K^{\prime}
for some open subset
V
of
K
. Therefore, we can construct a sequence of pairwise disjoint open subsets
V_{n}\subseteq V
such that for every
n\geq 1
there exists
s_{n}\in V_{n}
and
s_{0}\notin V_{n}
for every natural
n
. By
Lemma

3.4
, we can further assume that
\overline{V_{n}}\subseteq V
. For every natural number
n
, we can use
Lemma

3.4
again to find an open set
U_{n}
such that
s_{n}\in U_{n}\subseteq\overline{U_{n}}\subseteq V_{n}
. By Urysohn’s lemma, for every
n\geq 1
we can find a continuous function
g_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\to[0,1]
such that
g_{n}(s)=1
for every
s\in\overline{U_{n}}
and
g(s)=0
for every
s\notin V_{n}
. Now, we define
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}c_{0}=C_{0}[1,\omega)\to C(K)
by
T(\lambda_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}g_{n}
. It is straightforward to check that
T
is an operator satisfying the required properties.

Let us assume that our assertion is true for
\alpha<\omega
, and suppose that there exists
s_{0}\in K^{(\alpha+1)}\cap V
, for some open subset
V\subseteq K
. Given that
K^{(\alpha+1)}=(K^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
by definition, we can find a sequence of pairwise disjoint open subsets
V_{n}\subseteq V
such that for every
n\geq 1
there exists
s_{n}\in V_{n}\cap K^{(\alpha)}
and
s_{0}\notin V_{n}
. Just as we did in the proof of the base case, we can further assume that
\overline{V_{n}}\subseteq V
and that for every natural number
n
there is an open set
U_{n}
such that
s_{n}\in U_{n}\subseteq\overline{U_{n}}\subseteq V_{n}
.

Let us consider
C_{0}(U_{n})
. Since
[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
may be identified with
(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n]
, by the inductive hypothesis, given any natural number
n
there exists a positive linear isometry
S_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\to C_{0}(U_{n})
with property (*). As we have mentioned before, any function in
C_{0}(U_{n})
may be extended by zero to
C_{0}(V)
. We will write
\widetilde{S_{n}}f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{S_{n}(f)}
, so that
\widetilde{S_{n}}
is a linear isometry from
C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
into
C_{0}(V)
. Note that for any
f\in C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, the function
\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}
belongs to
C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
.

By Urysohn’s lemma, for every
n\geq 1
there is a continuous function
g_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\to[0,1]
such that
\left.g_{n}\right|_{\overline{U_{n}}}=1
and
\left.g_{n}\right|_{K\backslash V_{n}}=0
. Note that
\left.g_{n}\right|_{V}\in C_{0}(V)
, since
\overline{V_{n}}\subseteq V
. For simplicity, it will be convenient for us to assume that the domain of each
g_{n}
is
V
.

Finally, given
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
, we will define
Sf
as

Sf\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}+\widetilde{S_{n}}\bigl(\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}\bigr)\right).

For
S
to be well-defined, we need to show that the sequence of partial sums converges. Once this is established, it will be clear that
S
is a linear operator. Let
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
, and write

(3.1)

h_{n}=f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}+\widetilde{S_{n}}\bigl(\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}\bigr).

We have that
\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 3\sup_{t\in(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n]}|f(t)|
, and the summands
(h_{n})_{n}
are pairwise disjoint. Therefore, if
n<m
,

\mathinner{\!\biggl\lVert\sum_{k=n}^{m}h_{k}\biggr\rVert}_{\infty}=\mathinner{\!\biggl\lVert\bigvee_{k=n}^{m}h_{k}\biggr\rVert}_{\infty}=\bigvee_{k=n}^{m}\|h_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 3\sup_{t\in(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot m]}|f(t)|\xrightarrow{n,m\rightarrow\infty}0,

so the sequence of partial sums is Cauchy and converges in
C_{0}(V)
, and
Sf
is well defined for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
. Next, let us see that
S
is positive. Let
f
be a positive function in
C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
. It is enough to show that for any natural number
n
the summand
h_{n}
defined in (
3.1
) is positive. If
f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)=0
, the term
h_{n}
is positive, as
\widetilde{S_{n}}
is positive on
C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
. Suppose now that
f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)>0
and write

g=\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}\in C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n).

We decompose
g=g^{+}-g^{-}
, and keeping in mind that
f
is positive, we deduce that
\|g^{-}\|_{\infty}\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
. Since
\widetilde{S}_{n}
is a norm-preserving positive operator which sends
g^{-}
to a function whose support is contained in
U_{n}
, then
0\leq\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{-})\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}
. Now, observe that
h_{n}=\bigl(f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}-\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{-})\bigr)+\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{+})\geq 0
.

It remains to show that
S
is norm-preserving and has property (*). We will start by checking that
\|S\|\leq 1
. Let
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. We want to see that
\|Sf\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. It should be observed that, since
S
is positive, we may assume that
f\geq 0
(by changing
f
by its modulus
|f|
). As the addends (
3.1
) used to define
S
are pairwise disjoint, it will be enough to verify that each of them is bounded by
1
. If
f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)=0
the corresponding summand is bounded by
1
because
\widetilde{S_{n}}
is norm-preserving, so suppose that
f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)>0
. Then, if

g=\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)},

we have
0\leq\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{-})\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}
(see the previous paragraph), and
g^{+}\leq 1-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
, so
\|\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{+})\|_{\infty}\leq 1-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
.
Thus,

0\leq\bigl(f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}-\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{-})\bigr)(s)+\widetilde{S_{n}}(g^{+})(s)\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)+1-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)=1

for every
s\in U_{n}
, and

0\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}(s)+\widetilde{S_{n}}(g)(s)\leq f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)g_{n}(s)\leq 1

for every
s\in V\setminus U_{n}
, since
\widetilde{S_{n}}(g)
is supported on
U_{n}
. Therefore, any term of
S
is bounded by
1
, and
\|S\|\leq 1
. On the other hand, fix any
t\in[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
, so that
t\in(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n]
for some
n\geq 1
. By the inductive hypothesis, there exists
s_{t}\in U_{n}
such that
\widetilde{S_{n}}f(s_{t})=S_{n}f(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
. Now take any
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
and consider the function

\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)},

which belongs to
C_{0}(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)
, so it must satisfy

\widetilde{S_{n}}\left(\left.f\right|_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)\chi_{(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot(n-1),\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n)}\right)(s_{t})=f(t)-f(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot n).

Since
s_{t}\in U_{n}
,
g_{n}(s_{t})=1
, and hence
Sf(s_{t})=f(t)
for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
. Moreover, taking into account that we have constructed the sequence
(V_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in such a way that
s_{0}\notin V_{n}
for any
n\geq 1
, it follows that
Sf(s_{0})=0
for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha+1})
. Finally, recall that property (*) together with the fact that
\|S\|\leq 1
implies that
S
is norm-preserving.

To conclude the proof, suppose now that
K
is a compact Hausdorff space such that
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
. We have proven that given
s_{0}\in K^{(\alpha)}
there is a positive linear isometry
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha})\to C(K)
with the property that for every
t\in[1,\omega^{\alpha})
there exists
s_{t}\in K
such that
Sf(s_{t})=f(t)
and
Sf(s_{0})=0
for every
f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{\alpha})
. If we define

Tf\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f(\omega^{\alpha})\mathds{1}_{K}+S\bigl(f|_{[1,\omega^{\alpha})}-f(\omega^{\alpha})\chi_{[1,\omega^{\alpha})}\bigr),\qquad f\in C[1,\omega^{\alpha}],

it is not difficult to verify that
T
defines a positive linear isometric embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C(K)
with the desired properties.

Finally, observe that since
T
satisfies property
(*)
of
3.1
, then it preserves subspaces doing stable phase retrieval in
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

It should be noticed that the assumption
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
does not even guarantee the existence of a lattice isomorphic (not necessarily isometric) embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C(K)
.

Indeed, suppose that there exists a lattice isomorphic embedding
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}c\to C[0,1]
. Let us write
f_{n}=Te_{n}
for every natural number
n
. Observe that, as
T
preserves lattice operations,
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a pairwise disjoint positive sequence in
C[0,1]
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|}\leq\|f_{n}\|=\|Te_{n}\|\leq\|T\|,\quad\text{ for every }n\geq 1.

Hence, for every
n
there is
t_{n}\in[0,1]
such that
f_{n}(t_{n})\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|}
. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
t_{n}\to t_{0}
in
[0,1]
. It is clear that
f_{n}(t_{0})=0
for every
n
since the
f_{n}
’s are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, as
T\mathds{1}(t_{n})\geq Te_{n}(t_{n})=f_{n}(t_{n})\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|}
, it follows by the continuity of
T\mathds{1}
that
T\mathds{1}(t_{0})\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|}
.

Now, let
I
be an open interval of
[0,1]
such that
t_{0}\in I
and
T\mathds{1}(t)>\frac{1}{2\|T^{-1}\|}
for every
t\in I
, and let
N
be such that
t_{N}\in I
. Observe that we have

0<T\mathds{1}(t)=Te_{N}(t)+T(\mathds{1}-e_{N})(t),\quad\text{ for every }t\in I,

so
I=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
, where
A_{1}=\left\{t\in I\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>Te_{N}(t)>0\right\}
and
A_{2}=\left\{t\in I\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>T(\mathds{1}-e_{N})(t)>0\right\}
are open subsets of
I
. Observe that
t_{N}\in A_{1}
and
t_{0}\in A_{2}
and, in addition,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are disjoint as
Te_{N}
and
T(\mathds{1}-e_{N})
are (recall that
T
preserves disjoint elements of
c
). But this is impossible, since then the interval
I
would not be connected.

Proposition 3.7
.

There exists an isometric SPR embedding of
c_{0}

Proof.

For every

Representation of
x^{(n)}
. The points in
[1,\omega^{2}]
are ordered from the left to the right and from the top to the bottom.

The sequence
(x^{(n)})_{n}
spans an isometric copy of
c_{0}
. Indeed, given
(\alpha_{n})_{n}\in c_{00}
with
\bigvee_{n}|\alpha_{n}|=1
, it follows that
x=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}x^{(n)}
takes the value
\alpha_{n}
at the point

\omega

\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{n}

[\omega\cdot n+1,\omega\cdot n+n]\cup\{\omega\cdot(n+1)\}

\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{m})
at
\omega\cdot n+m
for every

and

. In all the cases above,
|x|\leq 1
, so we can conclude that
\|x\|=1
.

Now, let us check that
E=\overline{\spn}\{x^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\in\mathbb{N}\}

Theorem

1.4
, it suffices to show that
\||x|\wedge|y|||\geq\frac{1}{3}
for every

. Put

, then

, and the same can be done when

, it follows that
|\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{m}|,|\beta_{n}+\beta_{m}|\geq\frac{2}{3}
, so
|x|\wedge|y|(\omega\cdot n+m)\geq\frac{1}{3}
. Observe that by
Theorem

1.4
we can conclude that

does 3-SPR inside

.
∎

3.5
, together with the previous lemma, now yields the

part of

in

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space. If
K^{\prime}
is infinite, then
C(K)
contains an isometric copy of
c_{0}
which does SPR.

Proof.

Suppose that
K^{\prime}
is infinite, which is equivalent to
K^{\prime\prime}
being non-empty. By
3.5
, there exists an isometric embedding
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{2}]\to C(K)
which preserves SPR subspaces. By
3.7
,
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
contains a subspace
E
isometric to
c_{0}
doing SPR. Since,
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
is a sublattice of
C[1,\omega^{2}]
, then
E
also does SPR in
C[1,\omega^{2}]
. Therefore,
T(E)
is an SPR subspace of
C(K)
isometric to
c_{0}
.
∎

4.
Main results

In this section, we will provide a
complete answer
to the
Main Question
: does the condition
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
(for
\alpha<\omega
) imply the existence of an isometric copy of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
inside
C(K)
doing SPR? Note that it is trivially true for
\alpha=0
, while for
\alpha=1
we already know that it is false (
2.3
); thus, we only need to study the cases where
2\leq\alpha<\omega
. Due to
3.5
, this question is essentially reduced to the study of SPR embeddings between spaces of the form
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, which is our next step.

We start by proving the following lemma. Note that the fact that
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
and
c_{0}
are isomorphic for any
1\leq\alpha<\omega
already follows from
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, but the estimate for the isomorphism constant can be improved.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
1\leq\alpha<\omega
,
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
is linearly
2(\alpha+1)
-isomorphic to
c_{0}([1,\omega^{\alpha}])=c_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us establish some notation. Given an ordinal
1\leq t<\omega^{\alpha}
, we can express it using Cantor’s normal form (
1.2
) as

t=\sum_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}\omega^{\beta}\cdot k_{\beta},\quad\text{where }(k_{\beta})_{\beta}\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\alpha}\setminus\{0\}^{\alpha}.

We define
\beta_{0}(t)=\min\{0\leq\beta<\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k_{\beta}\neq 0\}
(note that for every
t
this set of ordinals is non-empty, as
(k_{\beta})_{\beta}
is non-zero, so by the well-ordering principle of ordinal numbers it has a minimum), and
\ell\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\rightarrow[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
by

\ell(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{\beta_{0}(t)+1<\beta<\alpha}\omega^{\beta}\cdot k_{\beta}+\omega^{\beta_{0}(t)+1}\cdot(k_{\beta_{0}(t)+1}+1)&\text{ if }1\leq t<\omega^{\alpha},\\
\omega^{\alpha}&\text{ if }t=\omega^{\alpha}\end{array}\right.

(it is understood that
k_{\alpha}=0
for
t<\omega^{\alpha}
). Note that for every
1\leq t<\omega^{\alpha}
,
t
belongs to the
\beta_{0}(t)
-derivative of
[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
, but not to the
(\beta_{0}(t)+1)
-derivative, and
\ell(t)
belongs to the
(\beta_{0}(t)+1)
-derivative, but not to the
(\beta_{0}(t)+2)
-derivative. Next, we define the following operator

\begin{array}[]{ccrcl}{R}&\colon&{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}&\longrightarrow&{c_{0}([1,\omega^{\alpha}])}\\
\mbox{}&\mbox{}&{f}&\longmapsto&{Rf=(a(t))_{t},}\end{array}

where
a(\omega^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}f(\omega^{\alpha})
and
a(t)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(f(t)-f(\ell(t))\bigr)
for every
1\leq t<\omega^{\alpha}
. Clearly
R
is well-defined and contractive. Moreover, for every
f\in S_{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}
,
\|Rf\|\geq\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}
. Indeed, for every
1\leq t<\omega^{\alpha}
, we have that
\ell^{\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)}(t)=\omega^{\alpha}
(where
\ell^{\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)}
represents the composition of
\ell
with itself
\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)
times), and we can write

f(t)=2\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)}a(\ell^{j}(t))

for every
f\in C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Therefore, if there were some
f\in S_{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}
such that
\|Rf\|<\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}
, there would exist some
t\in[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
with
|f(t)|=1
, so

1=|f(t)|\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)}|a(\ell^{j}(t))|<\frac{\alpha-\beta_{0}(t)+1}{\alpha+1}\leq 1,

which leads to a contradiction. Hence, the proof of the claim is concluded.
∎

Next, we exploit this isomorphism, together with the SPR embedding of
c_{0}
into
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
from
3.7
, to show the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

Given a finite ordinal
\alpha\geq 2
, there exists an isometric SPR embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
.

Proof.

Fix any
2\leq\alpha<\omega
. By the previous lemma, there exists a
2(\alpha+1)
-isomorphism
R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\to c_{0}
. Replacing
R
by
\frac{R}{\|R\|}
and
R^{-1}
by
\|R\|R^{-1}
if necessary, we may assume that
\|R\|\leq 1
. Thus,
\|R^{-1}\|\leq 2(\alpha+1)
. Now, consider the isometric
3
-SPR embedding
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}c_{0}\rightarrow C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
constructed in
3.7
, and define the operator

\begin{array}[]{ccrcl}{T}&\colon&{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}&\longrightarrow&{C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}\\
\mbox{}&\mbox{}&{f}&\longmapsto&{Tf=(S\circ Rf,f).}\end{array}

Clearly,
T
is an isometric embedding, as it is contractive and
\|Tf\|\geq\|f\|
. To conclude, we will use
Theorem

1.4
to show that
T(C[1,\omega^{\alpha}])
does
6(\alpha+1)
-SPR in
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Given
f,g\in S_{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}
, then
\|Rf\|,\|Rg\|\geq\frac{1}{\|R^{-1}\|}
, so we have

\left\||Tf|\land|Tg|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\left\||SRf|\land|SRg|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{\|R^{-1}\|}\left\|\left|S\left(\frac{Rf}{\|Rf\|}\right)\right|\land\left|S\left(\frac{Rg}{\|Rg\|}\right)\right|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{6(\alpha+1)}.

∎

The topological properties of the ordinal intervals yield the following corollaries:

Corollary 4.3
.

Given a finite ordinal
\alpha\geq 3
, there exists an isometric SPR embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that the following spaces are lattice isometric (here,
\sqcup
denotes a disjoint union):

C[1,\omega^{2}]\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]=C([1,\omega^{2}]\sqcup[1,\omega^{\alpha}])=C([1,\omega^{2}]\cup(\omega^{2},\omega^{\alpha}])=C[1,\omega^{\alpha}].\qed

Corollary 4.4
.

There exists an isometric SPR embedding of
C[1,\omega^{2}]
into
C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot~2]
.

Proof.

Just observe that

C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{2}]\subseteq C[1,\omega^{2}]\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{2}]=C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot 2].\qed

Finally, using
3.5
we obtain the main results of the paper:

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space with
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
for some
3\leq\alpha<\omega
. Then there is an isometric SPR embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C(K)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
K^{(\alpha)}\neq\varnothing
for some
3\leq\alpha<\omega
. By
3.5
, there exists an isometric embedding
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\to C(K)
which preserves the SPR subspaces of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Since, by
4.3
,
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
embeds isometrically into itself in an SPR way, we infer that
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
embeds isometrically into
C(K)
in an SPR way.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If
K
is a compact Hausdorff space with
|K^{\prime\prime}|\geq 2
, then there is an isometric SPR embedding of
C[1,\omega^{2}]
into
C(K)
.

Proof.

The proof of
3.5
can be adapted to show that, if
|K^{\prime\prime}|\geq 2
, there exists a positive linear isometric embedding of
C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot 2]
into
C(K)
with the property (*) defined in
3.1
. Indeed, it suffices to consider distinct points
s_{1},s_{2}\in K^{\prime\prime}
and open neighborhoods
V_{i},W_{i}\subseteq K
,
i=1,2
, such that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are disjoint and
s_{i}\in V_{i}\subseteq\overline{V_{i}}\subseteq W_{i}
,
i=1,2
. As
V_{i}\cap K^{\prime\prime}\neq\varnothing
, by the inductive step there exist positive linear isometries
S_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\rightarrow C_{0}(V_{i})
satisfying property (*),
i=1,2
(denote by
\widetilde{S_{i}}
the extensions by 0 to
C(K)
), and we can find
h_{i}\in C(K)
such that
0\leq h_{i}\leq 1
,
h_{i}|_{\overline{V_{i}}}=1
and
h_{i}|_{K\setminus W_{i}}
,
i=1,2
. Therefore, the operator
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot 2]\rightarrow C(K)
defined as

\displaystyle Tf

\displaystyle=f(\omega^{2})h_{1}+\widetilde{S_{1}}\bigl(f|_{[1,\omega^{2}]}-f(\omega^{2})\chi_{[1,\omega^{2}]}\bigr)

\displaystyle+f(\omega^{2}\cdot 2)h_{2}+\widetilde{S_{2}}\bigl(f|_{(\omega^{2},\omega^{2}\cdot 2]}-f(\omega^{2}\cdot 2)\chi_{(\omega^{2},\omega^{2}\cdot 2]}\bigr)

for every
f\in C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot 2]
will satisfy the properties of
3.5
. In particular,
T
will preserve the SPR subspaces of
C[1,\omega^{2}\cdot 2]
. Thus, we have that
C[1,\omega^{2}]
can be embedded isometrically in an SPR way into any
C(K)
-space with
|K^{\prime\prime}|\geq 2
.
∎

In view of the preceding corollaries, one might wonder whether it is possible to embed
C[1,\omega^{2}]
into itself in an SPR way, as this is the case for
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
when
\alpha>2
. We will see next that this is not possible.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume the field of scalars is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.
C[1,\omega^{2}]
cannot be embedded isometrically into a
C(K)
-space with
|K^{\prime\prime}|=1
in an SPR way.

Proof.

Let
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{2}]\to C(K)
be an isometric embedding, and consider the same notation as in
2.1
, where it was shown that this implies the existence of an element
t_{0}\in K^{\prime\prime}
such that
|T\mathds{1}(t_{0})|=1
. Since we are assuming that
|K^{\prime\prime}|=1
, it follows that
K=\{t_{0}\}
.

Suppose that
T(C[1,\omega^{2}])
does SPR. By
Theorem

1.4
(or
[
12
, Proposition 2.5]
in the complex setting), there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
n,m\geq 1
, with
n\neq m
, we have

\||T\mathds{1}_{1,n}|\land|T\mathds{1}_{1,m}|\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{C}.

Thus, for every
n\geq 1
, there exists a sequence
(s_{n,m})_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\subseteq K
such that

|T\mathds{1}_{1,n}|\land|T\mathds{1}_{1,m}|(s_{n,m})\geq\frac{1}{C}.

Fix any
n\geq 1
. We claim that the sequence
(s_{n,m})_{m=n+1}^{\infty}
has infinitely many distinct elements. Even more: no term of the sequence
(s_{n,m})_{m=n+1}^{\infty}
can be repeated an infinite number of times. Suppose that a point
s\in K
appears
N
times in the sequence, that is, there are
m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{N}
such that
s_{n,m_{1}}=s_{n,m_{2}}=\cdots=s_{n,m_{N}}=s
. Since
|T\mathds{1}_{1,m_{k}}|(s_{n,m_{k}})\geq\frac{1}{C}
for
k=1,\ldots,N
, we can find a scalar
\theta_{k}
of modulus
1
such that

\theta_{k}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,m_{k}}(s_{n,m_{k}})\geq\frac{1}{C}.

It is clear that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\theta_{k}\,\mathds{1}_{1,m_{k}}
has norm
1
in
C[1,\omega^{2}]
, and since
T
is norm-preserving, the function

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\theta_{k}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,m_{k}}
must have norm
1
. If we evaluate this function at
s
we have

1\geq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\theta_{k}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,m_{k}}(s_{n,m_{k}})\geq\frac{N}{C},

so
N
must be less than or equal to
C
. Therefore, for any
n\geq 1
we can find an accumulation point
s_{n}
of the sequence
(s_{n,m})_{m=n+1}^{\infty}
, which clearly belongs to
K^{\prime}
.

By the continuity of each
T\mathds{1}_{1,n}
, we have that
|T\mathds{1}_{1,n}(s_{n})|\geq\frac{1}{C}
for every
n\geq 1
. In addition, via the same argument that we have just presented, it is easy to check that for every
n\geq 1
, the sequence
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
has infinitely many different points. In particular, this implies that the sequence
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq K^{\prime}
has an accumulation point, which must be
t_{0}
, as
K^{\prime\prime}=\{t_{0}\}
.

Now we consider the family of functions
\mathds{1}-\mathds{1}_{1}+\zeta_{m}\mathds{1}_{1,m}
, where
m\geq 1
and
\zeta_{m}
are unimodular scalars. Observe that since these functions have norm
1
in
C[1,\omega^{2}]
, then
\|T\mathds{1}-T\mathds{1}_{1}+\zeta_{m}T\mathds{1}_{1,m}\|_{\infty}=1
for every
m\geq 1
. In the first paragraph of the proof we have pointed out that
T\mathds{1}(t_{0})=\theta
for some scalar
\theta
of modulus
1
. In addition,
T\mathds{1}_{1}(t_{0})=0
: indeed, using the ideas from the proof of
2.1
, it is easy to construct a sequence of distinct points
(t_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq K^{\prime}
such that
|T\mathds{1}_{n}(t_{n})|=1
and, therefore,
T\mathds{1}_{1}(t_{n})=0
whenever
n>1
, so that
T\mathds{1}_{1}(t_{0})=0
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2C}
and let
U^{t_{0}}
be a neighborhood of
t_{0}
in
K
such that

|T\mathds{1}(t)-\theta|<\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad|T\mathds{1}_{1}(t)|<\varepsilon\quad\text{ for every }t\in U^{t_{0}}.

Since
t_{0}
is an accumulation point of the sequence
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
s_{M}\in U^{t_{0}}
. We also know that
|T\mathds{1}_{1,M}(s_{M})|\geq\frac{1}{C}
, so take
\xi_{M}
of modulus 1 such that

\xi_{M}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,M}(s_{M})\geq\frac{1}{C}.

Finally, we consider the function
T\bigl(\mathds{1}-\mathds{1}_{1}+\theta\,\xi_{M}\,\mathds{1}_{1,M}\bigr)
, which should have norm
1
in
C(K)
. However, if we evaluate this function at
s_{M}
we obtain the following:

\displaystyle|T\mathds{1}-T\mathds{1}_{1}+\theta\,\xi_{M}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,M}|(s_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|T\mathds{1}(s_{M})-\theta+\theta+\theta\,\xi_{M}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,M}(s_{M})-T\mathds{1}_{1}(s_{M})|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\theta+\theta\,\xi_{M}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,M}(s_{M})|-|T\mathds{1}(s_{M})-\theta|-|T\mathds{1}_{1}(s_{M})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\xi_{M}\,T\mathds{1}_{1,M}(s_{M})-2\varepsilon\geq 1+\frac{1}{C}-2\varepsilon>1,

so we have arrived at a contradiction.
∎

Since the second derivative of
[1,\omega^{2}]
is the singleton
\{\omega^{2}\}
, the above proposition has the following immediate consequence:

Corollary 4.8
.

C[1,\omega^{2}]
cannot be embedded isometrically into itself in an SPR way.

4.7
can be generalized to other finite ordinals
\alpha
using a similar argument, even though the consequences are not as interesting for our purposes as in the case
\alpha=2
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
2\leq\alpha<\omega
be an ordinal and
K
be a compact Hausdorff space with
|K^{(\alpha)}|=1
. If
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]\rightarrow C(K)
is an isometric embedding, then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\alpha}\in S_{C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]}
such that

\mathinner{\!\biggl\lVert\bigwedge_{\beta=1}^{\alpha}|Tf_{\beta}|\biggr\rVert}<\varepsilon.

To summarize, we have just seen that
C[1,\omega^{2}]
does not embed isometrically into itself in an SPR way (
4.7
), but we already knew that
c_{0}
does so in
C[1,\omega^{2}]
(in fact, in
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
by
3.7
). How optimal is the latter? We will show next that we can slightly improve this as
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
embeds isometrically into itself doing SPR.

Proposition 4.10
.

There exists an isometric SPR embedding of
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
into itself.

Proof.

First, observe that
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
is lattice isometric to
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
. Next, recall that
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
is linearly 4-isomorphic to
c_{0}([1,\omega^{2}))=c_{0}
by means of the contractive operator
R
that sends any

to the collection of coefficients
a(\omega\cdot n)=\frac{1}{2}f(\omega\cdot n)
and
a(\omega\cdot(n-1)+m)=\frac{1}{2}(f(\omega\cdot(n-1)+m)-f(\omega\cdot n))
,
n,m\geq 1
(in this case,
\|R^{-1}\|\leq 4
). Let
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}c_{0}\rightarrow C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
be the isometric SPR embedding constructed in
3.7
. Then, the operator

\begin{array}[]{ccrcl}{T}&\colon&{C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})}&\longrightarrow&{C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})}\\
\mbox{}&\mbox{}&{f}&\longmapsto&{Tf=(S\circ Rf,f)}\end{array}

is an isometric SPR embedding of
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})
into itself. Indeed,
T
is clearly contractive and
\|Tf\|\geq\|f\|
, since one of the factors is the identity operator. Now we check that the image of
T
does SPR in the same way as we did in
4.2
.

We observe that if
f,g\in S_{C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})}
, then
\|Rf\|,\|Rg\|\geq\frac{1}{\|R^{-1}\|}
, so since
S
is a
3
-SPR embedding, then

\left\||Tf|\land|Tg|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\left\||SRf|\land|SRg|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{\|R^{-1}\|}\left\|\left|S\left(\frac{Rf}{\|Rf\|}\right)\right|\land\left|S\left(\frac{Rg}{\|Rg\|}\right)\right|\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{12}.

Thus,
Theorem

1.4
yields that
T
does
12
-SPR.
∎

.

4.1.
Open questions

As a consequence of the results of this section we can provide a fairly complete answer to the
Main Question
for every finite ordinal
\alpha
. Observe that for
\alpha>2
the conclusions turn out to be
stronger
than what was originally conjectured in
1
.
However, the SPR constant of these subspaces will diverge as
\alpha
grows. This might be an artifact of the proof, since we are trying to build all the overlapping needed to avoid almost disjoint pairs of functions of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
in such a small place as
[1,\omega^{2}]
.

Question 2
.

4.2
actually shows that there is a
6(\alpha+1)
-SPR isometric embedding of
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
into
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
. Since
C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\oplus_{\infty}C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
is lattice isometric to
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
for
\alpha\geq 3
(see the proof of
4.3
), this implies that in these cases
C[1,\omega^{\alpha}]
isometrically embeds into itself doing SPR with constant
\leq 6(\alpha+1)
.
Can the latter be improved so that the SPR constant is uniform in
\alpha
?

Another limitation of the proof of
4.2
is that this construction cannot be further generalized to
\alpha=\omega
, as
C[1,\omega^{\omega}]
is not linearly isomorphic to
c_{0}
.

Question 3
.

Is it possible to isometrically embed
C[1,\omega^{\omega}]
into itself in an SPR way?

We conclude this section by discussing the possibility of extending Theorem
1.5
to AM-spaces. Recall that a Banach lattice
X
is said to be an
AM-space
if
\|x\lor y\|=\max\{\|x\|,\|y\|\}
for every pair of positive vectors
x,y\in X
. A well-known result by Kakutani states that every AM-space is lattice isometric to a (closed) sublattice of a
C(K)
-space (see
[
4
, Theorem 4.29]
or
[
24
, Theorem 2.1.3]
). In
[
8
]
, E. Bilokopytov formulates the following question:

Question 4
.

[
8
, Question 5.4]

Let
X
be an AM-space. If
X^{a}
, the
order continuous part of
X
, has infinite codimension in
X
, then does
X
contain an SPR subspace of infinite dimension?

Note that given any compact Hausdorff space
K
, the order continuous part
C(K)^{a}
has infinite codimension in
C(K)
if and only if
K^{\prime}
is infinite (see, for instance,
[
9
, Remark 5.12]
). That is, condition
(i)
of Theorem
1.5
can be replaced by
C(K)^{a}
has infinite codimension in
C(K)
. This means that Question
4
has an affirmative answer for
C(K)
-spaces. We will now give a
partial answer
to this question by showing that the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
of Theorem
1.5
does not extend to AM-spaces: we will construct an AM-space
X
such that
X^{a}
has infinite codimension in
X
but it cannot even contain subspaces isomorphic to
c_{0}
doing SPR.

Let us consider the following AM-space:

(4.1)

X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{f\in C_{0}[1,\omega^{2})\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>\frac{1}{n}f(\omega\cdot(n-1)+1)=f(\omega\cdot n)\quad\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right\}.

By
[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
,
X^{a}
has infinite codimension in
X
. Note that the norm-one lattice homomorphisms are:
\delta_{\omega\cdot n+m}
for
n\geq 0
and
m\geq 2
are the coordinate functionals, and
n\delta_{\omega\cdot n}\equiv\delta_{\omega\cdot(n-1)+1}
, for
n\geq 1
, are the remaining norm-one lattice homomorphisms on
X
.

Proposition 4.12
.

c_{0}
does not isomorphically embed into the space
X
defined in (
4.1
) in an SPR way.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists an isomorphic embedding
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}c_{0}\to X
with the property that
T(c_{0})
does
C
-SPR in
X
. By
Theorem

1.4
,
T(c_{0})
does not contain
C
-almost disjoint pairs, so for every natural numbers
n,m\geq 1
,
n\neq m
we have

\bigl\||Te_{n}|\land|Te_{m}|\bigr\|\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|C},

and since
X
is an AM-space,
\text{Hom}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap S_{X^{*}}
, the set of normalized lattice homomorphisms from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
, is
1
-norming for
X
(see, for instance,
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
), so for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
x_{n,m}^{*}\in\text{Hom}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap S_{X^{*}}
such that

x_{n,m}^{*}(|Te_{n}|)\land x_{n,m}^{*}(|Te_{m}|)\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|C}.

Let us see what can be said about these lattice homomorphisms
(x_{n,m}^{*})_{n,m=1}^{\infty}
. First, note that for every
n\geq 1
, there are infinitely many distinct lattice homomorphisms in the sequence
(x_{n,m}^{*})_{m>n}
. In fact, by mimicking the argument of the proof of Proposition
4.7
, one can check that no lattice homomorphism appears infinitely many times in this sequence (each lattice homomorphism may appear
\|T^{-1}\|\|T\|C
different times at most).

Now, observe that for every
n\geq 1
, there exists a natural
k_{n}\geq 1
such that the set
\{x^{*}_{n,m}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m>n\}\cap\{\delta_{t}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>t\in(\omega\cdot(k_{n}-1),\omega\cdot k_{n})\}
is infinite. Otherwise, we could find a sequence
(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq[1,\omega^{2})
with
t_{k}\to\omega^{2}
such that
\delta_{t_{k}}\in\{x_{n,m}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m>n\}
for all
k
. Since
\delta_{t_{k}}\overset{\omega^{*}}{\to}0
(given that the functions in
X
vanish at
\omega^{2}
), we obtain that
0\in\overline{\{x_{n,m}^{*}\>\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\>m>n\}}^{w^{*}}
. But this is impossible, as
x_{n,m}^{*}(|Te_{n}|)\geq\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|\,C}
for every
m>n
.

Therefore, for every natural
n\geq 1
,
x_{n}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\delta_{\omega\cdot k_{n}}
is an accumulation point of
(x_{n,m}^{*})_{m>n}
. Note that as a consequence of the definition of
X
we have that
\|x_{n}^{*}\|=\frac{1}{k_{n}}
for every
n
. It can be proven that there must be infinitely many different
x_{n}^{*}
’s (again, we may replicate the proof of Proposition
4.7
). But this contradicts the following inequality which shows that there cannot exist infinitely many distinct
k_{n}
’s:

\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|C}\leq x_{n}^{*}(|Te_{n}|)\leq\|T\|\|x_{n}^{*}\|=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\|T\|,\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

∎

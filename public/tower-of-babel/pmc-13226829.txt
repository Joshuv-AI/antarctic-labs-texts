A rare case of primary lingual mucormycosis in an uncontrolled diabetic patient
Authors: Alushika Jain, Nikita Bora, Amit Toshniwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 34-year-old male with poorly controlled diabetes mellitus with a painful necrotic ulcer over the lateral tongue, an uncommon site for mucormycosis. Histopathology confirmed broad aseptate right-angle bronching hyphae consistent with primary lingual mucormyosis. Early surgical debridement, glycemic optimization, and liposomal amphotericin B resulted in favourable clinical recovery, highlighting the importance of prompt recognition of atypical oral lesions in immunocompromised patients

Case illustration

A 34-year-old male with a known case of diabetes mellitus who presented to out patient department with complaints of pain and discomfort during eating since 15 days. There was no preceding trauma, dental extraction, or upper respiratory infection. Clinical examination revealed a tender ulcerative lesion with necrotic slough over the lateral aspect of tongue (Fig. 1). Laboratory workup showed poorly controlled blood glucose levels, HbA1c of 8.5%. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the oral cavity demonstrated localized soft tissue thickening with altered signal intensity, suggestive of active infection. Biposy and histopathological examination revealed broad, aseptate fungal hyphae branching at right angles, consistent with mucormycosis (Fig. 2). Mucormycosis is an uncommon but rapidly progressive fungal infection caused by fungi of the order Mucorales, primarily affecting immunocompromised individuals, especially those with uncontrolled diabetes mellitus [1], [2]. While the sinonasal and rhino-orbital-cerebral forms are well recognised, primary involvement of the tongue is exceedingly rare [3], [4]. Lingual mucormycosis is a rare but serious manifestation, often associated with an immunocompromised state. Given its aggressive nature and potential for rapid tissue invasion, early clinical suspicion, imaging, and histopathological confirmation are vital to ensure timely intervention and favourable outcomes [4].

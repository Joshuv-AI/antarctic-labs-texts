Pasting and stability in convergence spaces
Renan M. Mezabarba, Rodrigo S. Monteiro, Thales F. V. Paiva

Abstract
. We prove a pasting lemma for locally finite closed covers of convergence spaces with limit codomains. We also show how the validity of such lemmas characterizes limit spaces.

Primary 54A20; Secondary 54A05,54C05

Introduction

The study of convergence spaces as generalizations of topological spaces goes back to Choquet’s introduction of
pseudotopologies
and
pretopologies

[
6
]
. A
convergence space
is a set equipped with a
notion of convergence
for filters (or nets) defined on it. In other words, it provides a way to relate these objects to points in the space, which are called their limit points. Continuity means preservation of convergence. Every topological space determines such a structure, but there are convergence structures that cannot be induced by a topology. Nevertheless, topological notions can be considered beyond the topological setting. For an exposition of the theory, see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
14
]
. One such topological notion suitable for generalization to convergence spaces is the pasting lemma.

The classic pasting lemmas are basic continuity principles allowing continuous functions defined on the members of a cover to be glued into a continuous function on the whole space. In convergence spaces, however, the situation is less clear, as the gluing must take care of the convergent behavior directly.

For pasting over closed covers, Preuss
[
14
, Proposition A.2.2, p. 260]
considers a pretopological domain and a limit codomain, with a cover by two closed subsets. Dossena
[
9
, Lemma 3.2, Remark 3.6 and Problem 1]
formulates the open-cover and locally finite closed-cover alternatives in pseudotopological spaces and asks for their extension to limit spaces
1
1

1

Dolecki and Mynard
[
8
, Corollary IX.10.15, p. 275]
give a version for a convergence domain and a locally finite closed cover, with a pseudotopological codomain.
. The different hypotheses in these results make it natural to ask how far closed pasting can be extended, and whether its validity imposes conditions on the convergence of the codomain.

We prove a pasting lemma for locally finite closed covers with a convergence domain and a limit codomain, and characterize limit spaces by the validity of closed pasting. Thus the pasting principle both holds in this class and determines it among convergence codomains. Our proofs use nets, with alternative proofs using filters included for
comparison
. The homotopical significance of the results is discussed briefly in the final section, while the focus of the paper remains on pasting and its convergence hypotheses.

1.
Nets, filters and convergence spaces

In 1922, Moore and Smith

12

\varphi[a^{\uparrow}]=\{\varphi(b):b\succeq a\}

For a topological space

\varphi

\tau

\varphi

\varphi(b)\in O

. We abbreviate this by writing
\varphi\to_{\tau}x

Sequences are specific cases of nets, and the definition of convergent sequences in Analysis is a particular instance of the general definition of net convergence.

Filters were introduced by Henri Cartan

For a topological space

Both notions of convergence are related. First, notice that a net
\varphi

in a natural way: let
\varphi^{\uparrow}
be the family of those subsets of

\varphi

(\cdot)^{\uparrow}\colon\mathsf{NETS}\left(X\right)\to\textsc{Fil}^{*}\left(X\right)

such that
\varphi\to_{\tau}x
if and only if
\varphi^{\uparrow}\to_{\tau}x
. Notice that two nets are tail equivalent if and only if they induce the same filter.
On the other hand, with a “right inverse”

\Gamma\colon\textsc{Fil}^{*}\left(X\right)\to\mathsf{NETS}\left(X\right)

we would have
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\to_{\tau}x
if and only if
\left(\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\right)^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}\to_{\tau}x
.

There is, indeed, such a function, as shown by Bruns and Schmidt

4
]

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\left\{\left\langle x,F\right\rangle\in X\times\mathcal{F}:x\in F\right\}

\left\langle x,F\right\rangle\leq\left\langle y,G\right\rangle

\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\colon\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{F}}\to X

\mathcal{F}
as its tail sets, ensuring that
\left(\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\right)^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{F}
.

Since every net induces a proper filter and every proper filter is induced by a canonical net via
\Gamma

(\cdot)^{\uparrow}
and
\Gamma

Typically, a (filter)

L(\varphi)=L(\psi)

\varphi^{\uparrow}=\psi^{\uparrow}

Since (net) preconvergences do not distinguish nets inducing the same filters, it becomes clear that nets here enter simply as a way to parametrize filters in the classical definition

Equivalently,

Now, we say a preconvergence

Centeredness

Isotonicity

2.
The pasting lemmas

We present two versions of the pasting lemmas for locally finite closed covers. The first assumes a Kent domain and a limit codomain. The second allows an arbitrary convergence domain and a limit codomain. The first version has a visually simpler proof. It was only after obtaining this proof using nets that we saw how to generalize the result. In our view, this justifies presenting both proofs, even though the second version generalizes the first.

We begin with the familiar open-cover version. Dossena
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
states it for pseudotopological spaces. We include its proof to introduce the way of arguing with nets.

Proposition 2.1

(Open Pasting Lemma)
.

Let
X,Y
be convergence spaces, let
\mathcal{O}
be an open cover of
X
, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function. If
f|_{O}
is continuous for each
O\in\mathcal{O}
, then
f
is continuous.

Proof.

For
\varphi\to x
, choose
O\in\mathcal{O}
containing
x
. The net is eventually in
O
, and its tail converges to
x
in the subspace
O
. Continuity of
f|_{O}
gives convergence of the image tail to
f(x)
. This tail is tail equivalent to
f\circ\varphi
(cf. Remark
1.2
), proving the assertion.
∎

1

Theorem 2.2

(Pasting lemma for limit spaces)
.

Let
X
be a Kent space and
Y
be a limit space, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function. If
\mathcal{C}
is a locally finite cover for
X
by closed sets such that the restriction
f|_{C}
is continuous for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
, then
f
is continuous.

Proof.

For a point
x\in X
and a net
\varphi
in
X
such that
\varphi\to x
, we need to show that
f\circ\varphi\to f(x)
in
Y
. In order to do this, let
O\subseteq X
be an open set such that
x\in O
and
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\{C\in\mathcal{C}:C\cap O\neq\emptyset\}
is finite. Since each
S\in\mathcal{S}
is closed and
\mathcal{S}
is finite, there is no loss of generality in assuming
x\in S
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
: otherwise,

O^{\prime}\coloneqq O\cap\left(X\setminus\bigcup\left\{S\in\mathcal{S}:x\notin S\right\}\right)

is an open set such that
x\in O^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{C\in\mathcal{C}:O^{\prime}\cap C\neq\emptyset\}
is finite and
x\in S
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

Now, let
\varphi_{S}\colon\operatorname{dom}\left(\varphi\right)\to X
be the net given by the rule

\varphi_{S}(d)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\varphi(d)&\text{if }\varphi(d)\in S\\
\quad x&\text{if }\varphi(d)\not\in S\end{cases}.

Since
X
is a Kent space, it follows that
\varphi_{S}\to x
. Given that
\varphi_{S}
is a net in
S
and
x\in S
, we get
\varphi_{S}\to x
in the subspace
S
, so
f\circ\varphi_{S}\to f(x)
in
Y
due to the continuity of
f|_{S}
. As
Y
is a limit space, to conclude that
f\circ\varphi\to f(x)
, it suffices to find
A\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\varphi\right)
such that
f(\varphi(\alpha))\in\{f(\varphi_{S}(\alpha)):S\in\mathcal{S}\}
for every
\alpha\succeq A
. Since
\varphi\to x
and
x\in O
, there exists
A\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\varphi\right)
such that
\varphi(\alpha)\in O
for every
\alpha\succeq A
. Finally, notice that as
\mathcal{C}
covers
X
, for every
\alpha\succeq A
there is
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\varphi(\alpha)\in C
, hence
C\cap O\neq\emptyset
, thus implying
C\in\mathcal{S}
and
\varphi_{C}(\alpha)=\varphi(\alpha)
, so
f(\varphi(\alpha))=f(\varphi_{C}(\alpha))
, as desired.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Alternative proof via filters)
.

For readers who are ideologically opposed to nets (see Dolecki’s discussion in
[
7

[

In filter notation, stability says that finite intersections of filters converging to the same point still converge to that point. Isotonicity says that finer filters preserve limits. For a map
h
, write
h(\mathcal{F})=\{B:h^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{F}\}
for the direct image filter.

Let
\mathcal{F}\to x
and put
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{F}\cap\{x\}^{\uparrow}
, where
\{x\}^{\uparrow}
is the principal filter at
x
. Since
X
is a Kent space, we have
\mathcal{H}\to x
. As in the preceding proof, choose an open neighborhood
O
of
x
meeting only
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\in\mathcal{C}
, with
x\in C_{i}
for every

. Then
O\in\mathcal{H}
and
O\subseteq C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{m}
.

For each
i
, let
\mathcal{H}_{i}
be the filter on
X
generated by
\{A\cap C_{i}:A\in\mathcal{H}\}
. It is proper because every generator contains
x
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{i}\supseteq\mathcal{H}
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{i}\to x
. Its trace on
C_{i}
converges to
x
in the subspace, so continuity of
f|_{C_{i}}
gives
f(\mathcal{H}_{i})\to f(x)
.

We have
\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Indeed, if
B\in\bigcap_{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}
, choose
A_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
with
A_{i}\cap C_{i}\subseteq B
. Then
O\cap\bigcap_{i}A_{i}\subseteq B
, so
B\in\mathcal{H}
. The reverse inclusion follows from
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Direct images preserve intersections, and hence

f(\mathcal{H})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}f(\mathcal{H}_{i})\to f(x)

by stability in
Y
. Finally,
\mathcal{F}\supseteq\mathcal{H}
, so isotonicity gives
f(\mathcal{F})\to f(x)
.

.

Let

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to X

For each cofinal

r_{i}(d)=d
on
E_{i}
. All these maps use the original domain
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\varphi_{i}=\varphi\circ r_{i}
is a subnet of
\varphi
, so
\varphi_{i}\to x
by isotonicity. Since its values lie in the closed set
C_{i}
, we have
x\in C_{i}
, and continuity of
f|_{C_{i}}
gives
f\circ\varphi_{i}\to f(x)
. On the common tail, each value of
f\circ\varphi
agrees at the same index with one of these finitely many image nets. Stability in
Y
gives the conclusion. Our subnet convention does not require the
r_{i}
to be monotone. This argument shows that stability is needed only in the codomain.
∎

Remark 2.5

(Alternative proof via filters)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\to x
. Choose an open neighborhood
O
of
x
meeting only
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\in\mathcal{C}
, so that
O\in\mathcal{F}
and
O\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}
. Put

J=\{i:A\cap C_{i}\neq\emptyset\text{ for every }A\in\mathcal{F}\}.

For each
i\notin J
, choose
A_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
disjoint from
C_{i}
. Then
A_{0}=O\cap\bigcap_{i\notin J}A_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
and
A_{0}\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in J}C_{j}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is proper,
J
is nonempty.

For
j\in J
, the sets
A\cap C_{j}
, with
A\in\mathcal{F}
, generate a proper filter
\mathcal{F}_{j}
on
X
. It is finer than
\mathcal{F}
, so
\mathcal{F}_{j}\to x
by isotonicity. Since
C_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
and
C_{j}
is closed,
x\in C_{j}
. The trace of
\mathcal{F}_{j}
on
C_{j}
has image
\mathcal{F}_{j}
under inclusion, hence converges to
x
in that subspace. Continuity of
f|_{C_{j}}
gives
f(\mathcal{F}_{j})\to f(x)
.

We have
\mathcal{F}=\bigcap_{j\in J}\mathcal{F}_{j}
. Indeed, one inclusion follows from
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{j}
. For the other, if
B\in\bigcap_{j\in J}\mathcal{F}_{j}
, choose
B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
with
B_{j}\cap C_{j}\subseteq B
. Then
A_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j\in J}B_{j}\subseteq B
, so
B\in\mathcal{F}
. Using the direct image notation from Remark
2.3
, we conclude that

f(\mathcal{F})=\bigcap_{j\in J}f(\mathcal{F}_{j})\to f(x)

by finite stability in
Y
.

For a finite closed cover, local finiteness is automatic. Theorem
2.4
extends Preuss’s two-piece statement
[
14
, Proposition A.2.2, p. 260]
, which assumes a pretopological source. In particular, Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, restricted to
\mathsf{Lim}
, give the extension asked for in Dossena’s Problem 1
[
9
]
.

The target assumption cannot simply be weakened to Kent convergence. The following example is Preuss’s counterexample expressed in net language.

Example 2.6

(
[
14
, Remark A.2.3, pp. 260–261]
)
.

Put
I=[0,1]
,
p=1/2
,
A=[0,p]
and
B=[p,1]
. Give
I
a convergence
L
which agrees with convergence in the usual topology at every point other than
p
, and declare
\varphi\to_{L}p
precisely when
\varphi
converges in the usual topology to
p
and is eventually in
A
or eventually in
B
. This is centered and isotone. The resulting convergence space is Kent: replacing terms by the limit preserves convergence in the usual topology, and at
p
it also preserves the chosen half-interval. However, the sequences
p-1/(n+3)
and
p+1/(n+3)
both converge to
p
, whereas their alternating mixing does not
L
-converge to
p
.

The identity maps from the subspaces
A,B
with their usual topologies to
(I,L)
are continuous. Their pasting, the identity on the interval with its usual topology, is not continuous, as the same alternating sequence shows. Equivalently, the paths
t\mapsto t/2
and
t\mapsto(t+1)/2
are composable, while their concatenation is this discontinuous identity.

3.
Stability as the validity of gluing functions

We now characterize the codomains for which closed pasting holds. Within Kent spaces, topological domains covered by two closed subsets suffice to characterize limit spaces. For arbitrary convergence codomains, we will allow convergence spaces as domains. We first need a lemma.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a directed set. On
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}=\mathbb{D}\cup\{\infty\}
, isolate the points of
\mathbb{D}
and take
\{\infty\}\cup d^{\uparrow}
, for
d\in\mathbb{D}
, as a neighborhood basis at
\infty
. A net in
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
converges to
\infty
if and only if it is eventually in
\{\infty\}\cup d^{\uparrow}
for every
d\in\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a Kent space
Y
, a net
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to Y
converges to
y
if and only if its extension
\varphi^{\infty}
, with
\varphi^{\infty}(\infty)=y
, is continuous.

Proof.

Directedness makes the indicated sets a neighborhood basis. Suppose
\varphi\to y
. On
\mathbb{D}\times\{0,1\}
use the preorder
(d,i)\preceq(e,j)
exactly when
d\preceq e
. The net
\widetilde{\varphi}(d,i)=\varphi(d)
is tail equivalent to
\varphi
. Let
\langle y\rangle
be the constant net with value
y
on the same directed set, and define their mixing by

\widehat{\varphi}(d,i)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\varphi}(d,0),&i=0,\\
\langle y\rangle(d,1),&i=1.\end{cases}

Since
Y
is a Kent space, we have
\widehat{\varphi}\to y
. Its tail sets are exactly
\{y\}\cup\varphi[d^{\uparrow}]
.

If
\eta\to\infty
in
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
, its image
\varphi^{\infty}\circ\eta
is eventually in
\{y\}\cup\varphi[d^{\uparrow}]
for every
d
. It is therefore a subnet of
\widehat{\varphi}
and converges to
y
by isotonicity. At an isolated point of
\mathbb{D}
, continuity follows from eventual constancy and centeredness. Conversely, the net
d\mapsto d
converges to
\infty
in
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
. Its image under a continuous extension is
\varphi
, proving
\varphi\to y
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For a Kent space
Y
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Y
is a limit space.

(2)

for every topological space
T
covered by two closed subsets
T_{0},T_{1}
, a function
F\colon T\to Y
is continuous whenever
F|_{T_{0}}
and
F|_{T_{1}}
are continuous.

Proof.

Theorem
2.2
gives
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
, since topological spaces are limit spaces. To prove
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to Y
converge to
y
. Form

T=(\mathbb{D}\times\{0,1\})\sqcup\{\infty\},

isolating every point other than
\infty
and taking

U_{d}=\{\infty\}\cup(d^{\uparrow}\times\{0,1\})

as basic neighborhoods of
\infty
.

The branches
T_{i}=(\mathbb{D}\times\{i\})\cup\{\infty\}
are closed and have the topology of
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
in Lemma
3.1
. Define
F(d,0)=\varphi(d)
,
F(d,1)=\psi(d)
and
F(\infty)=y
. That lemma makes the restrictions continuous, so (2) makes
F
continuous. Given a mixing
\rho
of
\varphi
and
\psi
, choose labels
i_{d}
on a tail so that
\rho(d)
is the selected value, and choose labels arbitrarily before it. The net
d\mapsto(d,i_{d})
converges to
\infty
. Its image under
F
is tail equivalent to
\rho
, proving
\rho\to y
and therefore stability.
∎

Example 3.3
.

The Kent hypothesis is needed for the characterization by topological domains in Theorem
3.2
. Let
Y=\{0,1\}
, declaring
\varphi\to 0
precisely when
\varphi
is eventually constant at either point, and
\varphi\to 1
precisely when
\varphi
is eventually equal to
1
. This convergence is centered and isotone. It is not Kent: the constant sequence
1
converges to
0
, but replacing alternate terms by
0
gives a sequence which does not converge to
0
. In particular,
Y
is not a limit space.

A map
f\colon T\to Y
from a topological space is continuous if and only if it is locally constant. Centeredness gives one implication. Conversely, if
f
is not locally constant at
x
, choose
x_{U}\in U
with
f(x_{U})\neq f(x)
for every open neighborhood
U
of
x
. Direct the pairs
(U,j)
, with
j\in\{0,1\}
, by reverse inclusion of
U
, ignoring the second coordinate. The net with values
x
at
(U,0)
and
x_{U}
at
(U,1)
converges to
x
, while every tail of its image contains both points of
Y
. This contradicts continuity.

Now let
T=A\cup B
be a topological space covered by two closed subsets. If
f|_{A}
and
f|_{B}
are continuous into
Y
, they are continuous into the discrete two-point space. Theorem
2.2
gives continuity of
f
into that discrete space, hence into
Y
. Thus
Y
satisfies condition (2) of Theorem
3.2
, but fails condition (1).

Limit spaces can also be characterized among all convergence spaces by allowing convergence spaces as domains.

Proposition 3.4
.

For a convergence space
Y
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Y
is a limit space.

(2)

closed pasting into
Y
holds for every cover by two closed subsets of every convergence space.

Proof.

Theorem
2.4
gives
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. For
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to Y
converge to
y
. Put
X=(\mathbb{D}\times\{0,1\})\sqcup\{\infty\}
and
X_{i}=(\mathbb{D}\times\{i\})\cup\{\infty\}
. Give
\mathbb{D}\times\{0,1\}
the preorder comparing only the first coordinate, and let
u
be its inclusion into
X
. Declare convergence to
a\neq\infty
to mean eventual equality to
a
, and declare
\eta\to\infty
if and only if
\eta
is eventually equal to
\infty
or
u^{\uparrow}\subseteq\eta^{\uparrow}
. These rules are centered, isotone and tail invariant, so
X
is a convergence space. Both
X_{i}
are closed: a limit outside a piece would require eventual equality to a point outside it.

Define
F(d,0)=\varphi(d)
,
F(d,1)=\psi(d)
and
F(\infty)=y
. Each restriction is continuous. At points other than
\infty
this follows from centeredness, as it does for nets eventually equal to
\infty
. In the remaining case, a net in
X_{i}
converging to
\infty
is eventually in
d^{\uparrow}\times\{i\}
for every
d
. Its image is a subnet of
\varphi
or
\psi
, so isotonicity gives its limit
y
. By (2),
F
is continuous. For an eventual mixing
\rho
, choose branch labels
i_{d}
realizing it on a tail, and choose them arbitrarily elsewhere. The net
d\mapsto(d,i_{d})
is a subnet of
u
, hence converges to
\infty
. Its
F
-image agrees eventually with
\rho
, proving stability.
∎

4.
Further comments

Taken together, Theorem
2.4
and Proposition
3.4
show that, among convergence spaces, limit spaces are exactly the codomains for which closed pasting over locally finite covers holds for every convergence-space domain. This is the sense in which they form the largest class of targets for this pasting principle.

These results may also be useful in algebraic topology. Concatenating paths and homotopies involves compatible definitions on closed subintervals of the parameter interval. Pasting supplies the continuity required for these constructions
[
3
]
. Their validity depends on the convergence structure, so extending the pasting principle is a basic step toward developing these techniques in more general spaces. Limit spaces are a natural setting for these constructions because of finite stability. Dossena’s Problem 1
[
9
]
asks whether his closed-cover pasting lemma for pseudotopological spaces extends to limit spaces. Theorem
2.4
gives that extension. His Remark 3.6 also raises the separate question of a fundamental group enriched over
\mathsf{Lim}
, which is not settled by the pasting statement alone.

The development of algebraic topology in convergence spaces has several antecedents. Preuss
[
14
, Appendix A.2, especially A.2.2–A.2.7, pp. 260–262]
develops fundamental groups and higher homotopy groups for limit spaces. Rieser
[
15
, Section 3.2.1]
establishes homotopy gluing for the standard interval cylinder in limit and pseudotopological spaces. That construction concerns the gluing of cylinders, whereas the theorems here apply to arbitrary locally finite closed covers. The first arXiv version of our article
[
11
]
develops the fundamental groupoid and a groupoid Seifert–van Kampen theorem using nets. Developing these homotopical topics is no longer the focus of the present paper.

Abstract
. In this paper, we classify n n -dimensional ( n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 ) quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature, thus extending the work of Shin [ 31 ] in dimension four for quasi-Einstein manifolds and refining the work of He-Petersen-Wylie [ 20 ] . As a consequence, we provide new examples of quasi-Einstein manifolds which are neither locally conformally flat nor D-flat in the sense of [ 9 ] .



1.
Introduction

A
quasi-Einstein
manifold, as defined in Case-Shu-Wei
[
13
]
, consists of a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
and a smooth function
f
satisfying the equation

(1.1)

Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df=\lambda g.

where,
m\neq 0
and
\lambda
are constants, and
\nabla^{2}f
denotes the Hessian of
f
. Clearly, when
f
is constant, a quasi-Einstein manifold reduces to an Einstein manifold. In this case, it is called a trivial quasi-Einstein manifold.

The symmetric 2-tensor

Ric^{m}_{f}:=Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df

was introduced in
[
30
]
and is called the
m-Bakry-Émery tensor
, which extends the usual Bakry-Émery tensor
Ric_{f}:=Ric+\nabla^{2}f

[
4
]
.
This tensor serves as a natural analogue of the Ricci tensor on smooth metric measure spaces and appears in the Bochner inequality for the drift Laplacian
\Delta_{f}:=\Delta-\nabla f\cdot\nabla
; see Qian
[
30
]
and Wei-Wylie
[
32
]
for further details.

Alternatively, by setting

w:=e^{-f/m}
as in
[
13
]
, the quasi-Einstein equation (
1.1
) can be written as

\nabla^{2}\ w=\frac{w}{m}({Ric}-\lambda g),\qquad m\neq 0.

Such an equation appeared, for instance, in Besse
[
5
]
. In fact, when
m>1
is a positive integer, it follows from the work of Kim-Kim
[
24
]
that quasi-Einstein manifolds correspond to warped product Einstein manifolds. More precisely, they are exactly those
n
-dimensional manifolds which are the base of an
n+m
dimensional Einstein warped product, i.e., there exists an Einstein manifold
(F^{m},g_{F})
, with Einstein constant
\mu
for certain
\mu
, such that
(M\times F^{m},g+e^{-2f/m}g_{F})
is an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\lambda
; for details, see
[
5
, Corollary 9.107]
,
[
24
]
and
[
13
]
.
For this reason, quasi-Einstein manifolds are also referred to as
\left(\lambda,n+m\right)
-Einstein manifolds
. We note several special cases:

\bullet

When
m=1
,
(\lambda,n+1)
-Einstein metrics are commonly called
static metrics
; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
17
,
22
]
.

\bullet

When
m=2
, a quasi-Einstein manifold corresponds to a
static vacuum near horizon geometry
in the physics literature; see, e.g.,
[
3
]
and
[
33
]
.

\bullet

In the extended case of
m=\infty
, a quasi-Einstein manifold is simply a gradient Ricci soliton; see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.

\bullet

When
m=2-n
, any
(\lambda,2)
-Einstein manifold
(M^{n},g,f)
is known to be conformal to an Einstein manifold
[
7
,
23
]
. Moreover, it is globally conformally equivalent to a space form
[
15
]
.

Examples of non-trivial quasi-Einstein manifolds with
\lambda<0
and
\lambda=0
can be found in
[
5
, Chapter 9]
. Non-trivial examples with
\lambda>0
and
m>1
were constructed in Lu-Page-Pope

[
28
, Section 5]
. Furthermore,
\lambda>0
necessarily implies that
M^{n}
is compact; see
[
30
, Theorem 5]
.

In recent years, motivated in part by the progress in gradient Ricci solitons, there has been growing interest in studying quasi-Einstein manifolds under various conditions on the Weyl tensor
W
. For example, for
m\neq 2-n
, Catino-Mantegazza-Mazzieri-Rimoldi
[
15
]
have proven that a complete
locally conformally flat
quasi-Einstein manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
is locally a warped product with
(n-1)
-dimensional fibers of constant sectional curvature around any regular point of
f
. Catino
[
14
]
showed that a
generalized
quasi-Einstein manifold, for which both
m
and
\lambda
could be functions on
M^{n}
, with
harmonic Weyl tensor
and
W(\nabla f,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)=0
is locally a warped product with
(n-1)
-dimensional Einstein fibers around any regular point of
f
. Around the same time, He, Petersen and Wylie
[
20
]
obtained
both local and global classifications of warped product Einstein manifolds with
harmonic Weyl tensor
and
W(\nabla w,\cdot,\nabla w,\cdot)=0
, or equivalently,
W(\nabla f,\cdot,\nabla f,\cdot)=0
. In addition,
Chen-He
[
16
]
studied compact
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 4
) compact Bach-flat quasi-Einstein manifolds; see also Huang-Wei
[
21
]
for an extension.

It is worth noting that, for quasi-Einstein manifolds, having a
harmonic Weyl tensor
(i.e., vanishing Cotton tensor) and
W(\nabla f,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)=0
implies the vanishing of the
D
-tensor first introduced in
[
10
]
(see also
[
9
]
),

\frac{m+n-2}{m}D_{ijk}=C_{ijk}-W_{ijkl}\nabla_{l}f,

where
C_{ijk}
and
W_{ijkl}
denote the Cotton tensor and the Weyl tensor, respectively. When
m=\infty
, this tensor is precisely the
D
-tensor for gradient Ricci solitons.

In 2017, Shin
[
31
]
further investigated a special class of generalized quasi-Einstein manifolds, called
(m,\rho)
-quasi-Einstein manifolds as in
[
21
]
, which satisfy the equation

Ric^{m}_{f}=(\rho R+\lambda)g,\ \rho,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},
where
R
denotes the scalar curvature of
(M^{n},g)
and
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0,\pm\infty\}
.
Building on the work of Kim
[
25
]
on
4
-dimensional gradient Ricci solitons, Shin
[
31
]
classified
4
-dimensional
(m,\rho)
-quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature. However, it has remained open whether Shin’s result can be extended to all dimensions
n\geq 5
for
(m,\rho)
-quasi-Einstein manifolds, or quasi-Einstein manifolds.

In this paper, based in part on very recent progress on the (local) classification of gradient Ricci solitons with harmonic Weyl curvature
[
27
]
,
[
26
]
and
[
6
]
, we study
n
-dimensional
(n\geq 5)
quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature and provide both local and global characterizations of such manifolds; see Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
1.1
, respectively. In particular, our results extend Shin’s work
[
31
]
to dimensions
n\geq 5
and refines the earlier results of Catino
[
14
]
and He-Petersen-Wylie
[
20
]
for quasi-Einstein manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 5
, be an
n
-dimensional, non-trivial, complete quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature and
m>1
(
m\neq\infty
). Then, it is one of the following types:

(1)

(M^{n},g,f)
is a quotient of some warped product quasi-Einstein manifold of the form

\left(\mathbb{R},\,ds^{2}\right)\times\,_{h}\left(N^{n-1},\bar{g}\right),

where
\left(N^{n-1},\bar{g}\right)
is an Einstein manifold and
f=f(s)
is a function on
\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

\lambda<0
and
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a quotient of the
Riemananian product

\left(M_{1}^{k},{g_{1}}\right)\times\left(M_{2}^{n-{k}},{g_{2}}\right),\qquad 2\leq{k}\leq n-2,

where
\left(M_{2}^{n-{k}},{g_{2}}\right)
is an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\lambda
;

\left(M_{1}^{k},{g_{1}}\right)

=\left(\mathbb{R}\times F^{{k}-1},\ ds^{2}+e^{2\sqrt{-\Lambda}s}g_{F}\right)
, for
\Lambda=\frac{1}{m+{k}-1}\lambda
and
F^{{k}-1}
(
3\leq{k}\leq n-2
) being Ricci-flat; and
f=-m{\sqrt{-\Lambda}s}
. Furthermore,
\left(M_{1}^{k},{g_{1}},f\right)
is a (D-falt) quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) which is also an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\rho=\frac{{k}-1}{m+{k}-1}\lambda
.

(3)

\lambda<0
and
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a quotient of the Riemananian product

\left(\mathbb{H}^{{k}},g_{\mathbb{H}}\right)\times\left(M_{2}^{n-{k}},{g_{2}}\right),\qquad 2\leq{k}\leq n-2,

where
\left(M_{2}^{n-{k}},{g_{2}}\right)
is an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\lambda
;
\left(\mathbb{H}^{{k}},g_{\mathbb{H}}\right)
is the hyperbolic space of constant sectional curvature
\Lambda=\frac{1}{m+{k}-1}\lambda
, with the metric
g_{\mathbb{H}}=ds^{2}+\sqrt{-\Lambda}\sinh^{2}(\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)g_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}
; and
f=-m\log(\cosh\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)
. Furthermore,
\left(\mathbb{H}^{{k}},g_{\mathbb{H}},f\right)
is a (D-falt) quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

In Theorem
1.1
, quasi-Einstein manifolds of types (2) and (3) provide new examples of neither locally conformally flat nor
D
-flat ones.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
is derived from a local classification theorem (Theorem
5.1
).
Moreover, there is a version of Theorem
1.1
in which we allow
M^{n}
to have a boundary; see Section 5 for details.

Remark 1.4
.

By similar arguments used in this paper, one can also obtain a local classification of
(m,\rho)
-quasi-Einstein metrics with harmonic Weyl curvature.

Organization of the Paper.

In Section 2, we fix our notation and recall some basic facts from Riemannian geometry and quasi-Einstein manifolds. By adopting and extending the arguments used in
[
6
]
for gradient Ricci solitons, we then derive the local multiply warped product structure of quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature.
In Section 3, we show that the Ricci tensor has at most three mutually distinct eigenvalues and provide a local classification of quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic curvature when the Ricci tensor is very special. This latter case corresponds precisely to the
D
-flat case. In Section 4, we analyze the remaining situations and characterize their local structures. Finally, in Section 5, we state the local classification theorem (Theorem
5.1
) for quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature and discuss the global classifications.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we fix our notation and recall some basic facts
and known results about Riemannian manifolds
and general quasi-Einstein manifolds. Moreover, we describe some useful features of quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature that will be used in later sections.

2.1.
Notations and basics of Riemannian geometry

First of all, we recall that on any
n
-dimensional Riemannian
manifold
(M^{n},g_{ij})
(
n\geq 3
) the Weyl conformal curvature tensor is given by

W_{ijkl}:=R_{ijkl}-\frac{1}{n-2}(A_{ik}g_{jl}+A_{jl}g_{ik}-A_{il}g_{jk}-A_{jk}g_{il}),

where
A=A_{ij}
is the Schouten tensor given by

(2.1)

A_{ij}:=R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}Rg_{ij}.

As is well-known, the Weyl tensor
W
is identically zero for every 3-manifold and,
for
n\geq 4
, the vanishing of the Weyl tensor is equivalent to the locally
conformal flatness of
(M^{n},g)
. We also recall that, in dimension
n=3
,

(M^{3},g)
is locally conformally flat if and only if its Cotton tensor
C
, defined by

(2.2)

C_{ijk}=\nabla_{i}A_{jk}-\nabla_{j}A_{ik},

vanishes. Furthermore, for
n\geq 4
, the Cotton tensor can also be defined as a divergence of the Weyl tensor:

C_{ijk}=-\frac{n-2}{n-3}\sum_{l}\nabla^{l}W_{ijkl}.

2.2.
Basic facts and identities for quasi-Einstein manifolds

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
), where
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
. Then, we have

R+\Delta f-\frac{1}{m}|\nabla f|^{2}=n\lambda,

\nabla R=\frac{2(m-1)}{m}Ric(\nabla f)+\frac{2}{m}[R-(n-1)\lambda]\nabla f

and

(2.3)

\nabla\left[R+\frac{(m-1)}{m}|\nabla f|^{2}-2\lambda f\right]=\frac{2}{m}(|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f)\nabla f.

Remark 2.2
.

When
m=\infty
, the above two equations coincide with the well-known identities for Ricci solitons:

\nabla R=2Ric(\nabla f)\quad\mbox{and}

R+|\nabla f|^{2}-2\lambda f=C.

On any quasi-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f)
, also known as an
(\lambda,n+m)
-Einstein manifold, we recall the
D
-tensor first introduced in
[
9
]
(see also
[
10
]
),

D_{ijk}=\frac{1}{n-2}(A_{ij}\nabla_{k}f-A_{ik}\nabla_{j}f)+\frac{1}{(n-1)(n-2)}(g_{ij}E_{kl}-g_{ik}E_{jl})\nabla_{l}f,

where
E_{ij}=R_{ij}-\frac{R}{2}g_{ij}
is the Einstein tensor.
This 3-tensor
D_{ijk}
is closely tied to the Cotton tensor and the Weyl tensor, and played a significant role in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
on classifying locally conformally flat gradient steady Ricci solitons and Bach flat shrinking Ricci solitons.

Lemma 2.3

(Chen-He
[
16
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be an
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
), where
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
. Then, we have

(2.4)

\frac{m+n-2}{m}D_{ijk}=C_{ijk}+R_{ijkl}\nabla_{l}f.

The following useful result is essentially parallel to that in gradient Ricci solitons with harmonic Weyl tensor, and can be easily obtained by using equations (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
); see, e.g.,
[
21
]
or
[
31
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be an
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature, i.e.,
\delta W=\nabla^{l}W_{ijkl}=0
, and
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
.
Then, the Cotton tensor
C
vanishes and
the Schouten tensor
A
is Codazzi.
Moreover, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}\nabla_{l}f=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}f-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}R_{jk}+\nabla_{j}R_{ik}+\frac{1}{m}(R_{jk}\nabla_{i}f-R_{ik}\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{m}\left(g_{jk}\nabla_{i}f-g_{ik}\nabla_{j}f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left(g_{ik}\nabla_{j}R-g_{jk}\nabla_{i}R\right)+\frac{1}{m}(R_{jk}\nabla_{i}f-R_{ik}\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{m}\left(g_{jk}\nabla_{i}f-g_{ik}\nabla_{j}f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{m(n-1)}\nabla_{l}f\left(R_{lj}g_{ik}-R_{li}g_{jk}\right)+\frac{1}{m}(R_{jk}\nabla_{i}f-R_{ik}\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle+\frac{R}{m(n-1)}\left(g_{ik}\nabla_{j}f-g_{jk}\nabla_{i}f\right).

2.3.
Local multiply warped product structure of quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature

First of all, following a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
9
]
, we have the following basic properties that are valid for quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature; see
[
31
, Lemma 1]
for more details.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 3
, be a quasi-Einstein manifold with harmonic Weyl
curvature and
m\neq 2-n
,
c
be a regular value of
f
, and

\Sigma_{c}=\{x|\ f(x))=c\}

be the level surface of
f
. Then, the following hold:

(i)

Whenever
\nabla f\neq 0
,
E_{1}:=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
is an eigenvector field of
Ric
.

(ii)

The scalar curvature
R
and
|\nabla f|^{2}
are constant on a connected component of
\Sigma_{c}
.

(iii)

There is a function
s
, locally defined by
s=\int\frac{df}{|\nabla f|}
, so that
ds=\frac{df}{|\nabla f|}
and
E_{1}=\nabla s
.

(iv)

The special eigenvalue,
\lambda_{1}:=Ric(E_{1},E_{1})
, of the Ricci tensor is constant on a connected component of
\Sigma_{c}
.

(v)

\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{1}=0
.

Next, we explore the key feature that having harmonic Weyl curvature is equivalent to
the Schouten tensor
A
being a Codazzi tensor. In
[
18
, Lemma 2]
, Derdziński showed the following useful property:
for any Codazzi tensor
A
and any point
x\in M
, let
E_{A}(x)
be the number of distinct eigenvalues of
A_{x}
(or
Ric
),
and set

M_{A}=\{x\in M\ |\ E_{A}{\rm\ is\ constant\ in\ a\ neighborhood\ of\ }x\}.

Then
M_{A}
is an open dense subset of
M
. Moreover, on each connected component of
M_{A}
,
the eigenvalues of
A
(or
Ric
) are well-defined and differentiable functions; the corresponding eigenspaces of
A
form mutually orthogonal differentiable distributions.

On the other hand, it is known that every quasi-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f)
is real analytic in harmonic coordinates; see
[
20
, Proposition 2.4]
. Thus, if
f
is not a constant function, then the set of regular points
\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
of
f
is open and dense in
M
. In particular,

M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}\subset M

is an open and dense subset of
M^{n}
.

Hence, for each point
p\in M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
p
,
such that the number of distinct eigenvalues of the Ricci tensor is constant on
U
. Suppose that, aside from
\lambda_{1}:=Ric(E_{1},E_{1})
, there are
m^{\prime}
distinct Ricci eigenvalues with multiplicities are
r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{m^{\prime}}
, respectively, such that
1+r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{m^{\prime}}=n
. Then, we can choose a local frame
\{E_{1}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},E_{2},\cdots,E_{n}\}
over
U
,
such that

R_{ij}=\lambda_{i}g_{ij}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{r_{1}+1},\ \lambda_{r_{1}+2}=\cdots=\lambda_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1},\ \cdots,\ \lambda_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{m^{\prime}-1}+2}=\cdots=\lambda_{n},

and that
\lambda_{2},\,\lambda_{r_{1}+2},\,\cdots,\,\lambda_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{m^{\prime}-1}+2}

are distinct.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be an
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature and
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
.
Let
\{E_{1}=\nabla f/|\nabla f|,E_{2},\cdots,E_{n}\}
be a frame consisting of eigenvector fields of the Ricci tensor
Ric
in some neighborhood
U\subset M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
of a regular level surface
\Sigma_{c}:=\{f=c\}
. Then, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
is constant on any connected components of
\Sigma_{c}
, hence a function of
s
only.

Proof.

This is an extension of
[
31
, Lemma 3]
, by using essentially the same argument as in the proof of
[
26
, Lemma 2.5]
and
[
27
, Lemma 3.3]
. ∎

With these preparations done, by adopting and extending the arguments used in
[
27
,
6
]
, we proceed to obtain the local multiply warped product structure of
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature.

In the following and the rest of the paper, we shall use the following convention: for
2\leq a,\alpha\leq n
, let us denote by

[a]=\{b\ \!|\ 2\leq b\leq n~{\rm and}~\lambda_{b}=\lambda_{a}\}\subset\{2,\cdots,n\}

and

[\alpha]=\{\beta\ \!|\ 2\leq\beta\leq n~{\rm and}~\lambda_{\beta}=\lambda_{\alpha}\}\subset\{2,\cdots,n\}

such that
[a]\neq[\alpha]
. In particular,
\lambda_{a}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
will always have distinct values.

First, we state a very useful lemma from
[
6
, Lemma 7]
which is also valid for quasi-Einstein manifolds with harmonic Weyl curvature (with essentially the same proof).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be an
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature and
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
. On
M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
, let
(x_{b})_{b\in[a]}
and
(x_{\beta})_{\beta\in[\alpha]}
be any local coordinate systems of the integral manifolds of the eigenspaces with corresponding Ricci eigenvalues
\lambda_{a}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
, respectively. Then,
setting
\partial_{1}:=E_{1}=\nabla f/|\nabla f|
, we have

\partial_{1}g_{ab}=2\xi_{a}g_{ab}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{\alpha}g_{ab}=0,

where
\xi_{a}=\frac{\lambda-\lambda_{a}}{|\nabla f|}
.

Now, for each
p\in M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
, we assume that, without loss of generality, the multiplicities of the distinct Ricci eigenvalues are given by

r_{1}=r_{2}=\cdots=r_{l}=1\quad\&\quad r_{l+1},~r_{l+2},~\cdots,~r_{m^{\prime}}\geq 2

for some
0\leq l\leq m^{\prime}
.

By using essentially the same method as in dealing with the Ricci soliton case (see
[
6
, Theorem 3]
and
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
), we can derive the following local multiply warped product structure, as well as the associated integrability conditions.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be an
n
-dimensional quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature and
m\notin{\{\pm 1,\,(2-n),\,\infty\}}
.
Then, for each point
p\in M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
, there exists a neighborhood

U\subset M_{A}\cap\{\nabla f\neq 0\}
of
p

such that, over
U
,
g
can be expressed as the following multi-warped product metric:

(2.6)

g_{|U}=ds^{2}+h^{2}_{1}(s)dt_{1}^{2}+\cdots+h^{2}_{l}(s)dt_{l}^{2}+h^{2}_{l+1}(s)\tilde{g}_{l+1}+\cdots+h^{2}_{m^{\prime}}(s)\tilde{g}_{m^{\prime}}.

Here, each
h_{j}(s)
,
1\leq j\leq m
, is a smooth positive function in
s
;
each
\tilde{g}_{\mu}
,
l+1\leq\mu\leq m^{\prime}
,
is an Einstein metric, with Einstein constant
(r_{\mu}-1)k_{\mu}
, over an
r_{\mu}
-dimensional Einstein manifold.

Moreover, let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
be the Ricci-eigenvalues, with
\lambda_{1}
being the Ricci-eigenvalue with respect to the gradient vector
\nabla f
,
and
\xi_{a}:=\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}(\lambda-\lambda_{a})
,
2\leq a\leq n
.
Then, the following integrability conditions hold

(2.7)

\xi^{\prime}_{a}+\xi^{2}_{a}+\frac{1}{m}f^{\prime}\xi_{a}=-\frac{R^{\prime}}{2(n-1)f^{\prime}},

(2.8)

\lambda^{\prime}_{a}-\left(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{a}\right)\xi_{a}=\frac{R^{\prime}}{2(n-1)},

(2.9)

\lambda_{1}=-f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{m}f^{\prime 2}+\lambda=-\sum^{n}_{i=2}\left(\xi^{\prime}_{i}+\xi^{2}_{i}\right),

Remark 5.6
.

If we assume
(M^{n},g,f)
in Theorem
1.1
to be complete, possibly with boundary, then, in view of Proposition
5.5
, we would have the following corresponding result:

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 5
, be an
n
-dimensional complete, with or without boundary, non-trivial quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) with harmonic Weyl curvature and
m>1
and
m\neq\infty
. Then, it is one of the following types:

(a)

(M^{n},g,f)
is a quotient of some warped product quasi-Einstein manifold of the form

\left(\mathbb{R},\,ds^{2}\right)\times\,_{h}\left(N^{n-1},\bar{g}\right),

where
\left(N^{n-1},\bar{g}\right)
is an Einstein manifold. [This is the
D
-flat case.]

(b)

\lambda\neq 0
and
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a quotient of the Riemannian product

\hskip 72.26999pt\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)\times\left(N^{n-{\bar{r}}},\tilde{g}\right),\qquad 2\leq{\bar{r}}\leq n-2.

Here,
\left(N^{n-{\bar{r}}},\tilde{g}\right)
is an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\lambda
;
\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)
is a (D-falt) quasi-Einstein manifold satisfying (
1.1
) which is also an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\rho=\frac{{\bar{r}}-1}{m+{\bar{r}}-1}\lambda
, given by one of the following cases with
\Lambda=\frac{1}{m+{\bar{r}}-1}\lambda
:

(i)

\Lambda>0
and
\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)
is isometric to

\left(\mathbb{S}^{\bar{r}}_{+},\ ds^{2}+\sqrt{\Lambda}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{\Lambda}s)g_{\mathbb{S}^{{\bar{r}}-1}}\right),

where
\mathbb{S}^{\bar{r}}_{+}
is an upper hemisphere, and (up to an additive constant)
f=-m\log(\cos\sqrt{\Lambda}s)
.

(ii)

\Lambda<0
and
\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)
is isometric to

\quad\quad\quad\left([0,\infty)\times\bar{N}^{{\bar{r}}-1},\ ds^{2}+\sqrt{-\Lambda}\cosh^{2}(\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)g_{\bar{N}}\right),

where
(\bar{N}^{{\bar{r}}-1},g_{\bar{N}})
is an Einstein metric with negative scalar curvature, and
f=-m\log(\sinh\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)
.

(iii)

\Lambda<0
and

\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)
is isometric to

\left(\mathbb{R}\times F^{{\bar{r}}-1},\ ds^{2}+e^{2\sqrt{-\Lambda}s}g_{F}\right),

where
(F^{{\bar{r}}-1},g_{F})
is Ricci flat, and
f=-m{\sqrt{-\Lambda}s}
.

(iv)

\Lambda<0
and
\left(W^{\bar{r}},\bar{g}\right)
is isometric to

\left(\mathbb{H}^{{\bar{r}}},\ ds^{2}+\sqrt{-\Lambda}\sinh^{2}(\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)g_{\mathbb{S}^{\bar{r}-1}}\right),

where
\mathbb{H}^{\bar{r}}
is a hyperbolic space, and
f=-m\log(\cosh\sqrt{-\Lambda}s)
.

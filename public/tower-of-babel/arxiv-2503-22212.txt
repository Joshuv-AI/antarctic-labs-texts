Universal Defect Statistics in Counterdiabatic Quantum Critical Dynamics
András Grabarits, Adolfo del Campo

Abstract
Counterdiabatic driving (CD) provides a framework to reduce excitations in nonadiabatic processes. Exact CD protocols require nonlocal control fields, and CD approximations with tailored locality are needed for their implementation. However, the performance of local CD schemes remains poorly understood. We consider the local CD across a quantum phase transition at an arbitrary rate. We establish its efficiency as a function of the degree of locality and demonstrate that the resulting defect statistics exhibit universal behavior. Our results provide an analytical framework for evaluating the effectiveness of local CD protocols in quantum state preparation, control, and optimization.



Adiabatic protocols play a key role in quantum control, but their implementation generally requires slow driving. In certain scenarios, they are thus not applicable. This is the case when driving a continuous quantum phase transition (QPT), characterized by the closing of the gap between the ground and the first excited
state at the critical point. Across a QPT, adiabaticity breaks down, giving rise to defects as excitations

Kato 1950

Jansen et al. 2007

;
Albash and Lidar 2018
.
A variety of approaches have been investigated for defect suppression. They involve quantum control

;

;

;

;
Mahunta and Mukherjee 2025

;
Deng et al. 2009

A powerful and versatile approach relies on assisting the time evolution with additional control fields for counterdiabatic driving (CD)

In this Letter, we provide an exact characterization of the defect statistics upon crossing a QPT when the dynamics is assisted by approximate CD. In particular, we analyze approximate CD schemes with tunable locality resulting from the CD expansion at a given order in the transverse-field Ising model (TFIM) and show that our predictions hold universally in general critical systems.

Defect statistics in the transverse field Ising model.

The TFIM provides an ideal testbed to study the fundamental analytical properties of QPTs
Zurek et al. 2005
;
Dziarmaga 2005
;
Sachdev 2011
;
Suzuki et al. 2012
. It is described by the interacting spin Hamiltonian given by

\hat{H}(t)=-J\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j+1}+g(t)\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}\right),

(1)

with ferromagnetic couplings, fixed as
J=1
for convenience.
We consider a linear protocol
g=g(0)\,(1-t/T)
during the interval

, starting deep in the paramagnetic phase (e.g.,
g(0)=100

Mapping (

\hat{H}=2\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}_{k}^{\dagger}\left[(g(t)-\cos k)\tau^{z}+\sin k\,\tau^{x}\right]\hat{\varphi}_{k}=2\sum_{k>0}\hat{\varphi}_{k}^{\dagger}H_{k}(t)\hat{\varphi}_{k}

\hat{\varphi}_{k}=(c^{\dagger}_{k},c_{-k})^{T}
containing the fermionic creation and annihilation operators of each mode labeled by the momentum
k=\pm\frac{\pi}{L},\,\pm\frac{3\pi}{L},\,\dots,\,\pm\pi\mp\frac{\pi}{L}

Simultaneous excitations in these modes,
(-k,k)
, correspond to topological defects that translate to kink pairs due to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetry of the ferromagnetic ground state.

The number of kinks can be captured by the expectation value of the kink number operator,
\hat{N}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(1-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j+1}\right)

\langle N(T)\rangle=\bra{\Psi(T)}\hat{N}\ket{\Psi(T)}=2\sum_{k>0}\,\langle\gamma^{\dagger}_{k}(T)\gamma_{k}(T)\rangle

The second expression counts the number of excitations in the TLSs via the Bogoliubov operators diagonalizing
\hat{\psi}_{k}^{\dagger}H_{k}(T)\hat{\psi}_{k}
at time
T
.
For slow annealing schedules, the excitation probabilities are precisely captured by the Landau-Zener (LZ) transition probability given by
p_{k}=\langle\gamma^{\dagger}_{k}(T)\gamma_{k}(T)\rangle\approx e^{-2\pi k^{2}T}
, leading to
\langle N(T)\rangle=2\sum_{k>0}\,p_{k}\approx\left(8\pi^{2}T\right)^{-1/2}\,L

;
Damski and Zurek 2006
;
Dziarmaga 2005
.
This result agrees with the original argument of the Kibble-Zurek mechanism (KZ), which predicts
\langle N(T)\rangle\sim T^{-d\nu/(1+z\nu)}\,L

d

;
Kibble 1980
;
Zurek 1985
;
Zurek 1996
;
del Campo and Zurek 2014

z=\nu=1

d=1

;
Grabarits et al. 2025
for time scales of
T_{\mathrm{fast}}\sim O(1)
. In the limit of sudden quenches,

Beyond the average defect number, the complete statistical characterization is given by the kink distribution
P(N;T)=\langle\Psi(T)\lvert\delta(\hat{N}-N)\rvert\Psi(T)\rangle

Cincio et al. 2007
.
As shown in Ref.

;

;
Balducci et al. 2023

;
Bando et al. 2020

Bando and Nishimori 2021

King et al. 2022

The characteristic function is related to

by a Fourier transform,
\tilde{P}(\theta;T)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\theta\,N}\right]

\log\tilde{P}(\theta;T)=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\theta N}\right]=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(i\theta)^{q}\frac{\kappa_{q}}{q!}

In the TFIM,

\log\tilde{P}(\theta;T)=\sum_{k>0}\log\left[1+(e^{2i\theta}-1)\,p_{k}\right]

In the limit of slow annealing schedules, it was shown theoretically and verified in a wide range of experiments that all cumulants follow the same power law of

\kappa_{q}(T)\propto\kappa_{1}(T)=\langle N(T)\rangle

\kappa_{q}(T\rightarrow 0)=\frac{L}{4}\frac{2^{2q}-1}{2^{2q-1}q}B_{2q}+O\left(T^{1/2}\right)

B_{2q}
denoting the
2q
-th Bernoulli number
Grabarits et al. 2025

Universality.

The analytical arguments behind the fast quench breakdown and cumulant scaling can be extended to arbitrary critical systems with a second-order QPT in
d
dimensions and of linear size
L
. As a feasible approximation, we assume that excess energy is gradually generated by
D<d
dimensional defects with finite spatial separation appearing at the interfaces of frozen domains. Consequently, the
n
-th order local CD expansion can be interpreted as approximately enforcing adiabatic evolution within domains of size
\sim n^{d}
. Thus, their number scales as
L^{d}/n^{d}
, leaving room for defect generation at
L^{d}/n^{d}\times n^{D}\sim L^{d}\,n^{-(d-D)}
positions. Assuming that this happens independently
Gómez-Ruiz et al. 2020
according to a Poisson-binomial distribution, all defect cumulants will be proportional to the number of locations where defects can be formed,

\displaystyle\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(0)\propto n^{-(d-D)}.

(8)

To demonstrate this universal scaling law on a more quantitative level, consider the broad class of quantum critical Hamiltonians
H(t)=\sum_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\varphi}_{\textbf{k}}H_{\textbf{k}}(t)\hat{\varphi}^{\dagger}_{\textbf{k}},\,\hat{\varphi}_{\textbf{k}}=(c_{-\textbf{k}},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\textbf{k}})
in
d
spatial dimensions with
\epsilon_{\textbf{k}}\sim\lvert\textbf{k}\rvert^{z}
in which Landau-Zener transitions properly describe defect formation in slow quenches, equivalent to
z\nu=1
. The exact CD term of the
k
-th mode can generally be expressed as
q_{\textbf{k}}(t)=f(\textbf{k})/(T\epsilon^{2}_{\textbf{k}})

with the bounded function
f(\textbf{k})
encoding the
k
-th mode of the interactions.
The
n
-th order of the Krylov expansion
q^{(n)}_{\textbf{k}}(t)
comes from the linear combination of the nested commutators up to the order
2n-1
, which scales up the interaction range by a factor
\sim n
, regardless of the dimensionality. Thus, the following scaling is expected,
q^{(n)}_{\textbf{k}}(t)\sim\sum_{m=1}^{n}f_{m}(m\textbf{k})/(T\epsilon^{2}_{\textbf{k}})
as the stretched interactions amplify the corresponding mode expansion, while the denominator accounts for the overall magnitude near the critical point. Consequently, the analytical argument in the TFIM
sup
can be generalized to
D
dimensional defects
Mondal et al. 2008
;
Sarkar et al. 2020
;
Zhang and Quan 2022
.
For
|\textbf{k}|\gtrsim 1/n
the numerator remains finite implying that for
T\gg n^{2z}
both the CD term is eliminated,
q^{(n)}_{\textbf{k}}(t)\sim 1/(T\lvert\textbf{k}\rvert^{2z})\ll 1
and the Landau-Zener transitions are suppressed as
-\log p_{\textbf{k}}\sim T\lvert\textbf{k}\rvert^{2z}\gg 1

In the opposite limit,
T\ll n^{2z}
, the CD term dominates and
q^{(n)}_{\textbf{k}}(t)\sim 1/(T\lvert\textbf{k}\rvert^{2z})\gg 1
, implying adiabatic dynamics again. As a result, the
n
-th order CD expansion universally restricts the dynamics below the cut-off momentum
\lvert\textbf{k}_{n}\rvert\lesssim 1/n
.
This implies the universal CD fast quench scale
T^{\mathrm{CD}}_{\mathrm{fast}}\sim n^{2z}
, below which defect formation is unaltered,
\kappa^{(n)}(T)\sim T^{-\frac{d-D}{2z}}
. The fast quench plateau is obtained by summing up the saturated transition probabilities
p_{\textbf{k}}\sim O(1)
for all possible momenta up to
\lvert\textbf{k}_{n}\rvert\sim 1/n
across the
D
-dimensional critical surface. This yields a universal scaling for all cumulants of the form
\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(0)\sim\int_{0}^{\lvert\textbf{k}_{n}\rvert}\mathrm{d}^{d-D}\textbf{k}\,p_{\textbf{k}}\propto n^{-(d-D)}
. This framework applies to critical systems regardless of whether the KZ mechanism or a more general dynamical power-law scaling describes the average defect generation. The former is perfectly mirrored by the TFIM, while the more general case of the latter can be demonstrated via approximate methods in the long-range Kitaev models
sup
.

Conclusions.—

Counterdiabatic driving provides a powerful approach to suppress excitations in driven systems, but its performance has remained elusive under approximate protocols with a tailored locality required to ease their implementation. Our findings establish exact analytical results for the defect statistics regardless of the driving rate and for an arbitrary CD expansion order. In the fast quench regime, all cumulants are proportional, universally scaling with the inverse of the CD expansion order, depending solely on the dimensionality of the systems and the defects. In addition, universal signatures of the defect statistics are also present away from the sudden limit, for arbitrary driving times. Our analytical findings thus provide a theoretical framework for understanding the efficiency of local CD protocols with broad implications for quantum simulation, control of quantum matter, quantum thermodynamics, and counterdiabatic quantum algorithms.

(10)

Note that this limit simplifies the calculation to a huge extent given that

III
Leading order expansion around the sudden limit

In this section, we provide the leading order correction with respect to the bare Hamiltonian around the sudden limit, Eq. (
22
). To do so, we transform the time-dependent Schrödinger equation into Dirac picture with respect to the time-evolution generated by the CD term,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{n,1}(t)\\
\psi_{n,2}(t)\end{bmatrix}=U_{n}\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{k,1}(t)\\
\psi_{k,2}(t)\end{bmatrix},

(23)

where for brevity we have again omitted the
n
dependencies and denoted the wave-function components in the Dirac picture by
\psi_{n,1}
and
\psi_{n,2}
, respectively. Here
U_{n}
is the inverse of the CD time-evolution operator, given in Eq. (
10
), given by

\displaystyle U_{n}=\cos\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]+i\sin\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]\,\tau^{y},

(24)

where
\varphi^{(n)}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,q^{(n)}(t^{\prime})
, which is given by changing to the variable
g

\displaystyle\varphi^{(n)}(g)\approx\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}.

(25)

The corresponding time-dependent Schrödinger equation in the Dirac picture is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{n,1}(t)\\
\psi_{n,2}(t)\end{bmatrix}=-iU_{n}H(t)U^{\dagger}_{n}\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{n,1}(t)\\
\psi_{n,2}(t)\end{bmatrix}.

(26)

Next, we evaluate the generator by exploiting that
\tau^{y}\tau^{x,z}\tau^{y}=-\tau^{x,z}
and
\tau^{y}\tau^{x,z}=-\tau^{x,z}\tau^{y}
, leading to

\displaystyle U_{n}H(t)U^{\dagger}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(t)\left\{\cos^{2}\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]-\sin^{2}\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]-2i\cos\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]\sin\left[\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]\tau^{y}\right\}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(t)\left\{\cos\left[2\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]-i\sin\left[2\varphi^{(n)}(t)\right]\tau^{y}\right\}=H(t)e^{-2i\,\varphi^{(n)}(t)\tau^{y}}.

Thus, the leading order correction within the time-dependent perturbation theory in terms of the time-evolution operator at the end of the process,
t=T
is given

\displaystyle U^{\mathrm{D}}(T)\approx\mathbb{I}_{2}+iT\int_{0}^{g_{0}}\mathrm{d}g\,\left[(g-\cos k)\tau^{z}+\sin k\tau^{x}\right]\exp\left[-2i\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}\,\tau^{y}\right].

(28)

By employing the same integral approximation as in Eq. (
19
), the wave-function components in the Dirac picture can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{1,n}(T)\\
\psi_{2,n}(T)\end{bmatrix}=

(29)

\displaystyle\left\{\cos\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}\right]-i\sin\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}\right]\tau^{y}\right\}\left\{\mathbb{I}_{2}+iT\int_{0}^{g_{0}}\mathrm{d}g\,H(g)\exp\left[-2i\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}\,\tau^{y}\right]\right\}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\approx\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)-k/2\right]\\
\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)-k/2\right]\end{bmatrix}+iT\int_{0}^{g_{0}}\mathrm{d}g\,\,H(g)\exp\left[-i\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\,\tau^{y}\right]\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\approx\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)-k/2\right]\\
\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)-k/2\right]\end{bmatrix}+iT\int_{0}^{g_{0}}\mathrm{d}g\,\begin{bmatrix}(\cos k-g)\sin\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]+\sin k\cos\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]\\
(\cos k-g)\cos\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]+\sin k\sin\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]\end{bmatrix},

As a result, the excitation probability correction reads

\displaystyle\delta p^{(n)}_{k}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2T\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(nk)\right]

(31)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}g\,\sin\frac{k}{2}\left\{(\cos k-g)\sin\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]+\sin k\cos\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+\cos\frac{k}{2}\left\{(\cos k-g)\cos\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]+\sin k\sin\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}(2g^{m}-1)\right]\right\}.

Next, we switch to the variable of
g\rightarrow 1-g
, measuring the critical point from
g\approx 0
to the ferromagnetic point
g=1
, and restrict the integral as
\int_{-\infty}^{1}\mathrm{d}g\,\dots\approx\int_{0}^{1}
, as excitations are dominated by the small neighborhood of the critical point,
g=0
in the ferromagnetic phase. For convenience, we denote the argument of the cosines and sines by
\Phi(k,n,1-g)=2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\,\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}
, while the
g
independent pert can be approximated by a similar integral approximation as in Eq. (
19
),

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}\approx\int_{1/n}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{\sin(n\,kx)}{x}=\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)-\mathrm{Si}(k).

(32)

Separating also the
\mathrm{Si}(k)
terms from the sines and cosines one obtains

\displaystyle\delta p^{(n)}_{k}\approx 2T\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(nk)\right]

(33)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}g\,\sin\frac{k}{2}(\cos k-1+g)\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\sin\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]

\displaystyle+\sin\frac{k}{2}(\cos k-1+g)\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\cos\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]+\sin\frac{k}{2}\sin k\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\cos\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]

\displaystyle-\sin\frac{k}{2}\sin k\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\sin\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]+\cos\frac{k}{2}(\cos k-1+g)\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\cos\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]

\displaystyle-\cos\frac{k}{2}(\cos k-1+g)\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\sin\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]+\cos\frac{k}{2}\sin k\sin\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\cos\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]

\displaystyle+\cos\frac{k}{2}\sin k\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(k)\right]\sin\left[2\Phi(k,n,1-g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right].

Now the key ingredient lies in the scaling behavior of
g
and
k
with respect to
n
after performing also the momentum integrals for the defect cumulants. For this, one needs to consider the general leading order behavior of the expansion of
\Phi\left(n,k,1-g\right)
with respect to both
k
and
g
,

\displaystyle\Phi(n,k,1-g)\,=\sum_{l,j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{l}\frac{m^{2l}}{(2l+1)!}k^{2l+1}\binom{m}{j}g^{j}\sim\sum_{l,j=0}^{\infty}(n\,k)^{2l+1}\,(n\,g)^{j}.

(34)

As a result,
\Phi(n,k,1-g)
simplifies to a bivariate function of
n\,k
and
n\,g
, denoted by
\tilde{\Phi}(n\,k,n\,g)\approx\Phi(n,k,1-g)
. As a result, integration of Eq. (
33
) with respect to both
k
and
g
with additional power os
g^{j}
and
k^{l}
in front of the sines and cosines will scale as
n^{-2-l-j}
. Therefore, for the leading behavior, one only needs to consider the lowest order expansion
k
and
g
. Exploiting the leading order behavior,
\mathrm{Si}(k)\sim k
one ends up with

\displaystyle\delta p_{k}\approx 2T\cos\left[\mathrm{Si}(nk)\right]\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}g\,g\cos\left[\tilde{\Phi}(nk,n\,g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]+k\sin\left[\tilde{\Phi}(nk,n\,g)-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]\,.

(35)

Quite interestingly, the excitation probabilities themselves do not acquire the same order with

after performing the integration with respect to
g
, while the second as
k\,n^{-1}
. However, as both terms involve additional functions depending only on
n\,k

T/n^{3}
. As a result, the CD fast quench regime terminates when the above perturbative corrections reach the order of cumulants in the sudden limit,
T/n^{3}\sim n^{-1}
, verifying the CD fast quench scale of
T^{\mathrm{CD}}_{\mathrm{fast}}\sim n^{2}

Finally, the specific constant factor of the cumulants can be obtained by numerically integrating the double integrals of the cumulants of

\displaystyle\delta\kappa^{(n)}_{1}(T)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{T}{n^{3}}\frac{L}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}k\,\int_{-\infty}^{1}\mathrm{d}g\,g\cos\left[2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]+k\sin\left[2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}g^{m}-\mathrm{Si}(n\,k)\right]

(36)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{T}{n^{3}}\frac{L}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}k\,n^{3}\delta p_{k}-0.7T\,n^{-3},

\displaystyle\delta\kappa^{(n)}_{2}(T)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2T}{n^{3}}\frac{L}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}k\,n^{3}\delta p_{k}(1-2p_{k})\approx-1.5T\,n^{-3},

(37)

\displaystyle\delta\kappa^{(n)}_{3}(T)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2T}{n^{3}}\frac{L}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}k\,n^{3}\delta p_{k}(6p^{2}_{k}-6p_{k}+1)\approx-3.55T\,n^{-3}.

(38)

IV
Cumulant expansion from the slow limit

In this section, we show details of the analytical approximations for the transition probabilities in the independent TLS of the TFIM in the presence of CD.
First, we realize that the results of Ref.
Damski 2014
,

\displaystyle q^{(n)}_{k}=-4\,\dot{g}(t)\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sin(km)\frac{g^{2m}+g^{L}}{8g^{m+1}(1+g^{L})}-\frac{\delta_{m,0}}{8g},

(39)

can be expressed in more compact form.
As the
g^{L}
terms will either dominate (
\lvert g\rvert>1
) or disappear (
\lvert g\rvert<1
),
q^{(n)}_{k}
can be written as a geometrical sum,

\displaystyle q^{(n)}_{k}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{i\dot{g}(t)}{4}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}e^{ik}e^{ikm}g^{\mathrm{sgn}(1-\lvert g\rvert)m}-e^{-ik}e^{-ikm}g^{\mathrm{sgn}(1-\lvert g\rvert)m}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dot{g}\begin{cases}&\frac{\sin k-\sin(k(n+1))g^{n}+\sin(kn)g^{n+1}}{2(1+g^{2}-2g\cos k)},\quad\lvert g\rvert<1,\\
&\frac{\sin k-\sin(k(n+1))g^{-n}+\sin(kn)g^{-(1+n)}}{2(1+g^{2}-2g\cos k)},\quad\lvert g\rvert>1.\end{cases}

(41)

For large enough expansion order,
n\gtrsim\sqrt{L}/10
at momentum
k\gtrsim n^{-1}
the numerator
\sin(k(n+1))g^{n}-\sin k-\sin(nk)g^{n+1}
remains finite. To understand further, how the dynamics is affected, we concentrate on the avoided crossings that would form without CD around their approximate position,
g\approx 1-k^{2}/2
. For
T\lesssim n^{2}
and with the condition that the corresponding Landau-Zener (LZ) transition is of order
O(1)
, i.e.,
k\ll T^{-1/2}
implies that the CD matrix element scales as
q^{(n)}_{k}\sim(Tk^{2})^{-1}\gg 1
. As a result, CD dominates the dynamics, ensuring adiabatic dynamics approximately. For
T\gg n^{2}
, however
Tk^{2}\gg 1
washing out the effect of CD and also leading to the suppression of LZ transitions, implying adiabaticity for the whole region of
k_{n}\gg n^{-1}
.

At the same time, for low momentum,
k=b/n,\,b\ll 1
the numerator of
q^{(n)}_{k}
can be expanded up to the leading, cubic order in
k\,n
yielding for the off-diagonal CD term
q^{(n)}_{k}\sim(nk)^{2}/(k\,T)
. For slow quenches,
T=a\,n^{2},\,a\gtrsim 1
the CD term scales as
q^{(n)}_{k}\sim b/(an)
. With the condition of
b\lesssim a^{-1/2}
, the matrix element is suppressed as
q^{(n)}_{k}\lesssim a^{-3/2}n^{-1}
, implying that the dynamics is insensitive to CD. The fast quench regime can also be addressed via the analysis of the LZ transition rates, provided that the driving time is not far away from the breakdown scale,
T=a\,n^{2},\,a\lesssim 1
. As a result, the CD term scales as
q^{(n)}_{k}\sim(nk)^{2}/(Tk)\sim b/(an)\ll 1
, thus leaving invariant this regime as well.

As a result, the cumulant generating function admits the form as reported in the main text with the LZ probabilities restricted below the momentum
k_{n}\sim n^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\log\tilde{P}^{(n)}(\theta;T)=\sum_{k<k_{n}}\log\left[1+(e^{i\theta}-1)p_{k}\right]=-L\,n_{\mathrm{ex}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{(1-e^{i\theta})^{p}}{p^{3/2}}\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{k_{n}\sqrt{p}}{2\sqrt{\pi}n_{\mathrm{ex}}}\right),

where
n_{\mathrm{ex}}=(8\pi^{2}T)^{-1/2}
is the average defect density without CD.
This result shows that below the CD fast-quench threshold, defect statistics and all defect cumulants are insensitive to CD as for
k^{2}_{n}T\gg 1
, i.e.
T\gg T^{\mathrm{CD}}_{\mathrm{fast}}
the correction becomes exponentially close to the result of Ref.
del Campo 2018
,
\mathrm{erf}\left[\frac{k_{n}\sqrt{p}}{2\sqrt{\pi}n_{\mathrm{ex}}}\right]\approx 1
. However, decreasing the driving time beyond the fast quench breakdown, the average extracted from Eq. (
IV
) converges to a constant value,

\displaystyle\kappa^{(n)}_{1}(T)=-i\partial_{\theta}\log\tilde{P}^{(n)}(\theta;T)\Big|_{\theta=0}=Ln_{\mathrm{ex}}\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{k_{n}\sqrt{p}}{2\sqrt{\pi}n_{\mathrm{ex}}}\right)\rightarrow\frac{k_{n}}{\pi}\,L.

(42)

This allows for fixing the constant factor in
k_{n}
by requiring that
n_{\mathrm{ex}}\mathrm{erf}\left[\frac{k_{n}\sqrt{p}}{2\sqrt{\pi}n_{\mathrm{ex}}}\right]\approx 1.05/\pi\,n^{-1}
leading to
k_{n}\propto 1.05\,n^{-1}
so that it reporduces the sudden quanch limit.

Using this argument as an ansatz, the analytical structure of all cumulants can be captured approximatively around the CD fast quench breakdown providing a smooth function interpolating between the exact results in the fast and slow driving regimes.
Similarly to the average, we restrict ourselves to the first-order expansion of Eq. (
IV
) and fix the constant
k_{n}
so that the cumulants reach the exact CD fast quench values for
T\rightarrow 0
. As a result of the limiting form of the error function,
\mathrm{erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}x+O(x^{3})
the approximation correction from the slow driving limit is given by

\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(T)\approx L\,\kappa^{\mathrm{KZ}}_{q}(T)\mathrm{erf}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(0)}{2\kappa^{\mathrm{KZ}}_{q}(T)}\right]=L\,\kappa^{\mathrm{KZ}}_{q}(T)\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\kappa^{\mathrm{(n)}}_{q}(0)}{2\kappa^{\mathrm{KZ}}_{q}(T)}+O(T^{3/2})\approx\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(0),

(43)

where
\kappa^{\mathrm{KZ}}_{q}(T)
and
\kappa^{(n)}_{q}(0)
denoting the
q
-th cumulant for the
n
-th order of expansion in the KZ scaling and sudden limits, respectively.

Note that this strategy replaces the approach of the
1/T
expansion reported in Ref.
Grabarits et al. 2025
. In particular, without CD, the LZ formula only applies up to momentum
k\lesssim T^{-1/4}
. According to the results of Ref.
Grabarits et al. 2025
, beyond the CD fast quench regime,
n^{2}\ll T
, this implies a scale of
k\lesssim n^{-1/2}
. Thus, the KZ scaling regime beyond the CD fast quench threshold is completely governed by the LZ transitions.

V
Universality demonstrated by approximate methods in the long-range Kitaev model

In this section, we provide an approximate analytical treatment of the long range Kitaev models (LRKM). The momentum space representation of the LRKM provides an efficient platform to test the universality of the CD order scaling,

\displaystyle H(t)=-2g(t)\sum_{i=1}^{L}c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{r=1}^{L/2}\left[j_{r,\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i+r}+d_{r,\beta}c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i+r}+\,\mathrm{h.c.}\right],

(44)

\displaystyle H=2\sum_{k>0}\hat{\psi}_{k}^{\dagger}\left[[g(t)-j_{\alpha}(k)]\tau^{z}+d_{\beta}(k)\,\tau^{x}\right]\hat{\psi}_{k}\equiv 2\sum_{k>0}\hat{\psi}_{k}^{\dagger}H_{k}(t)\hat{\psi}_{k},

(45)

\displaystyle j_{\alpha,r}=\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}r^{-\alpha}\,j_{\alpha}(k)=\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\alpha}\sum_{r=1}^{L/2}\,r^{-\alpha}\,\cos(kr)\approx\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathrm{Li}_{\alpha}(e^{ik})\right],

(46)

\displaystyle d_{\beta,r}=\mathcal{N}_{\beta}r^{-\beta}\,d_{\beta}(k)=\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}\sum_{r=1}^{L/2}\,r^{-\beta}\,\sin(kr)\approx\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathrm{Li}_{\beta}(e^{ik})\right],

(47)

with
\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}=2\sum_{i=1}^{L/2}r^{-\gamma}\approx 2\zeta(\gamma)
.
The exact CD term can conveniently be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{CD}}=-\dot{g}\sum_{k>0}\frac{d_{\beta}(k)}{2\left[d^{2}_{\beta}(k)+[g+j_{\alpha}(k)]\right]^{2}}\hat{\psi}_{k}^{\dagger}\tau^{y}_{k}\hat{\psi}_{k}=-\dot{g}\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\sum_{r=1}^{L/2}r^{-\beta}\sum_{k>0}\frac{\sin(kr)}{2\left[d^{2}_{\beta}(k)+[g+j_{\alpha}(k)]\right]^{2}}\hat{\varphi}_{k}^{\dagger}r\tau^{y}_{k}\hat{\varphi}_{k}.

(48)

with
\hat{\varphi}_{k}=(c^{\dagger}_{k},c_{-k})^{T}
.
As the exact Krylov expansion of the LRKM cannot be obtained in a closed form, we develop an analytical ansatz starting from the exact results of the TFIM, but keeping the most important analytical properties of the LRKM, governing the leading order behavior
of counterdiabatic critical dynamics. In particular, similar to the exact CD, all the Krylov expansion terms will depend only on
k
via the combination
kr
, while the overall order of magnitude is fixed by the energy gap denominator, similarly to the TFIM.
To this end, we employ the approximation, in which the Krylov expansion is obtained independently for the different interaction ranges. As a result, the off-diagonal matrix element can be expanded in a similar way as for the TFIM

\displaystyle q^{(n)}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dot{g}(t)\sum_{r=1}^{L}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\frac{\sin(k^\prime r)\sin(k^\prime rm)}{1+g^{2}-2g\cos(k^\prime r)}\sin(krm)

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dot{g}\sum_{r=1}^{L}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{g^{2m}+g^{L}}{2g^{m+1}(1+g^{L})}\sin(k r m).

(50)

The expression in the first line can also be summed up, which will prove to be useful for studying the CD fast quench scale,

\displaystyle q^{(n)}_{k}\approx-\dot{g}\sum_{r=1}^{L}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\begin{cases}&\frac{\sin k-\sin(k(n+1))g^{n}+\sin(kn)g^{n+1}}{\left[d_{\beta}(k)+(g-j_{\alpha}(k))\right]^{2}},\quad\lvert g\rvert<1,\\
&\frac{\sin k-\sin(k(n+1))g^{-n}+\sin(kn)g^{-(1+n)}}{\left[d_{\beta}(k)+[g-j_{\alpha}(k)]\right]^{2}},\quad\lvert g\rvert>1.\end{cases}

(51)

where in the last step the exact energy gap of the
k
-th mode has been restored to get a more accurate approximation for the LRKM. The form in Eq. (
49
) provides a convenient framework to evaluate the sudden quench excitation probabilities via a similar integration as in Eq. (
9
). This leads to the following wave-function

\displaystyle e^{-i\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}q^{(n)}_{k}(t^{\prime})\tau^{y}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cos\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}q^{(n)}_{k}(t^{\prime})\right]-i\sin\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}q^{(n)}_{k}(t^{\prime})\right]\tau^{y}\right]\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(52)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}[\mathrm{Si}(n,rk)+(\pi-k)/2]\right]\\
\cos\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}[\mathrm{Si}(n,rk)+(\pi-k)/2]\right]\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\mathrm{Si}(r\kappa)\right]\\
\cos\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\mathrm{Si}(r\kappa)\right]\end{bmatrix}.

Here the shorthand notations were used again
\mathrm{Si}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}t\,\frac{\sin t}{t},\,\mathrm{Si}(n,k)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(km)}{m}
and with the new variable
\kappa=n\,k
. In the last step, we also neglected all pure
k
dependencies, as for large

they provide only subleading corrections for finite values of
\kappa
.
From thi,s the excitation probability is given by projecting this state on the excited state of the
k
-th mode of the LRKM at

given by

\displaystyle\lvert\mathrm{ES}_{k}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d^{2}_{\beta}(k)+\left[j_{\alpha}(k)+\sqrt{j^{2}_{\alpha}(k)+d^{2}_{\beta}(k)}\right]^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}-d_{\beta}(k)\\
j_{\alpha}(k)+\sqrt{j^{2}_{\alpha}(k)+d^{2}_{\beta}(k)})\end{bmatrix}.

(53)

The excitation probability thus reads

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lvert d_{\beta}(k)\sin\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\mathrm{Si}(r\kappa)\right]+\cos\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\mathrm{Si}(r\kappa)\right]\left[j_{\alpha}(k)+\sqrt{j^{2}_{\alpha}(k)+d^{2}_{\beta}(k)})\right]\right\rvert^{2}}{d^{2}_{\beta}(k)+\left[j_{\alpha}(k)+\sqrt{j^{2}_{\alpha}(k)+d^{2}_{\beta}(k)}\right]^{2}}

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\left[\sum_{r}\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\mathrm{Si}(rnk)\right].

This implies again that all cumulants are expected to scale inversely with the CD order for systems that can be decomposed to TLSs and where LZ transitions describe defect generation. Although the construction contained two ansatz-motivated approximations, the essential physics was preserved throughout the derivations, namely that the interaction range and the CD order only enter the analysis via
rk
and

. With this property at hand, it is a crucial consequence that the excitation probabilities are insensitive to the critical properties encoded in the final excited state as well.

Next, we deduce conclusions from Eq. (
51
) regarding the CD fast quench breakdown scale. A similar argument can be performed as for the TFIM, namely we concentrate on the properties of the avoided level crossings for large
n
and with small momentum, where
z=\min\{\beta,\alpha\}-1
for
\alpha<\min\{2,\beta\}
,
z=\beta-1
for
\alpha<\beta<2
, and
z=1
otherwise.

To this end, let us consider the regime of momenta
k\gg 1/n
. The key observation is that the numerator of the approximative CD term remains finite, which also holds for the general set-up. For
T\lesssim n^{2z}
either in the KZ or in the dynamical scaling regime
Defenu et al. 2019
the condition for LZ transitions to have finite contributions is
T\ll k^{2z}
. Note that, due to the long-range behavior
1/(2z)
only matches the KZ scaling power-law outisde
\alpha<\min\{2,\beta\}
. Otherwise, it is given by
1/(2(\beta-1))<1/(2z)
. As a result, the condition
T\ll k^{-2z}
is a sharper bound than the one obtained by the KZ scaling exponent. Thus, the CD expansion dominates in this regime, implying adiabatic dynamics. In the opposite limit,
T\gtrsim n^{2z}
, the CD term vanishes and the LZ transitions are suppressed as well,
Tk^{2z}\gg 1
both in the dynamical and in the KZ scaling case. As a result, modes above
k\gtrsim 1/n
are driven adiabatically due to the fact that the CD term depends on the combination
n\,k
in the leading order.

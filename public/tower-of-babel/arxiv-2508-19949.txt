Estimating non-linear functionals of trawl processes
Orimar Sauri

Abstract
Trawl processes is a family of continuous-time, infinitely divisible, stationary processes whose correlation structure is entirely characterized by its so-called trawl function. This paper investigates the problem of estimating non-linear functionals of a trawl function under an in-fill and a long-span sampling scheme. Specifically, building on the work of [ 17 ] , we introduce non-parametric estimators for functionals of the type Ψ t ​ ( g ) = ∫ 0 t g ⁡ ( a ⁡ ( s ) ) ​ 𝑑 s \Psi_{t}(g)=\int_{0}^{t}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s and Λ t ​ ( g ) = ∫ t ∞ g ⁡ ( a ⁡ ( s ) ) ​ 𝑑 s \Lambda_{t}(g)=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s where a a represents the trawl function of interest and g g a non-linear test function. We show that our estimator for Ψ t ​ ( g ) \Psi_{t}(g) is consistent and asymptotically Gaussian regardless of the memory of the process. We further demonstrates that the same phenomenon occurs for the estimation of Λ t ​ ( g ) \Lambda_{t}(g) as long as g ⁡ ( x ) = O ⁡ ( | x | p ) g(x)=\mathrm{O}(\lvert x\rvert^{p}) , as x → 0 x\to 0 , for some p > 3 p>3 . Additionally, we illustrate how our results can be used to test the presence of T T -dependent data that is robust to persistence.



1
Introduction

Trawl processes form a subclass of continuous-time, strictly stationary, and infinitely divisible processes. Specifically, a trawl process
X=(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is constructed by evaluating a Lévy basis – also known as infinitely divisible independently scattered random measure –
L
over a set
A_{t}
, known as
trawl set
, which is typically a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. A trawl process, by its design, offers highly flexible autocorrelation structure and the ability to produce a wide range of marginal distributions, all within the framework of infinitely divisible distributions. In fact, the distribution of
X_{t}
is completely determined by
L
, while the autocorrelation function of
X
is fully described by the so-called
trawl function

a
. This relationship is often expressed through the identity

\rho_{X}(h)=\frac{\int_{h}^{\infty}a(s)\mathrm{d}s}{\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\mathrm{d}s},\,\,\,h\geq 0.

These features make trawl processes suitable for modeling data with stylized facts such as non-Gaussianity, heavy tails, jumps, and persistence. This is reflected by their increasingly popularity for modeling complex temporal phenomena across disciplines such as finance (
[
3
,
4
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
) and physics (
[
9
,
14
,
18
]
).

Regarding statistical inference for trawl processes, various parametric approaches have been explored in the literature. See for instance
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
19
]
. In this context, the work in
[
17
]
represents the first to address non-parametric estimation of the trawl function and serves as the starting point for the present study. Specifically, the authors in that paper showed that, given
n
equidistant observations of
X
, say
(X_{i\Delta_{n}})_{i=0}^{n}
, the trawl function
a
can be consistently estimated by

\displaystyle\check{a}(t)

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{n}^{-1}\left[\hat{\varGamma}_{l+1}-\hat{\varGamma}_{l}\right],\,\,\,\text{if }\Delta_{n}l\leq t<(l+1)\Delta_{n},

(1)

assuming that as
n\to\infty
,
\Delta_{n}\to 0
, with
n\Delta_{n}\to\infty
, and provided the Lévy seed of
L
(see Section
2
) has unit variance.
\hat{\varGamma}_{l}
in (
1
) denotes the sample auto-covariance function of
X
at lag
l\Delta_{n}
. Furthermore, under suitable conditions, the estimator

\check{a}(t)
is asymptotically normal with asymptotic variance given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}^{2}(t):=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}_{4}a(t)+2\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}a(t-s)a(t+s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-2\int_{t}^{\infty}a(s-t)a(t+s)\mathrm{d}s,

for some non-negative constant
\mathfrak{K}_{4}
. In the
same work, the authors proposed to use
\check{a}
once again along with a “Riemann sum” approach to estimate
the quadratic functionals of
a
appearing
in the definition of
\sigma_{a}^{2}(t)
, e.g.
\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s
. This procedure has some limitations. First, it relies on a tuning parameter whose choice and finite-sample performance are unclear. Second, the estimation error of
\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s
is dependent of the behaviour of
\check{a}(t)
for
t
large, which could result on a statistic that is very sensitive to the memory of the underlying stochastic process. In this paper, we address these issues by considering the general problem of estimating functionals
of the type

\Psi_{t}(g)=\int_{0}^{t}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s;\,\,\Lambda_{t}(g):=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s,

where
g
is a continuous non-linear test function, based on the sample
(X_{i\Delta_{n}})_{i=0}^{n}
.

Main contributions

By slightly modifying the definition of
\check{a}
in (
1
), which we denote by
\hat{a}
(see (
9
) below), we derive limit theorems for the functionals

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}^{n}(g):=

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=0}^{[t/\Delta_{n}]-1}g(\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n})),\,\,\,\,0\leq t\leq(n-1)\Delta_{n};

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{n}(g):=

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=[t/\Delta_{n}]}^{n-1}g(\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n}));\,\,0\leq t\leq(n-1)\Delta_{n},

using a double-asymptotic scheme based on both long-span and high-frequency observations of the underlying trawl process
X
. The function
g
is assumed to be continuous and of polynomial growth.

Under minimal integrability conditions on
X
, we provide a functional law of large numbers for
\Psi^{n}(g)
. With stronger assumptions on the trawl function, we establish a functional Central Limit Theorem (CLT from now on) at the rate
\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}
, applicable to test functions that are twice continuously differentiable. Additionally, in line with
[
17
]
, we show that this convergence is independent of the memory of
X
. In contrast, the asymptotic behaviour of
\Lambda^{n}(g)
is more subtle. First, we demonstrate that, in general,
\Lambda_{t}^{n}(g)
is not consistent for
\int_{t}^{\infty}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s
unless
g(x)=\mathrm{O}(\lvert x\rvert^{p})
, as
x\to 0
, for some
p>2
. In the latter situation with
p>3
,
\Lambda_{t}^{n}(g)
also fulfils a CLT analogous to that of
\Psi^{n}(g)
. Secondly, to mitigate the inconsistency of
\Lambda^{n}(g)
we alter its definition (see equation (
13
)) following the approach suggested in
[
17
]
. The resulting estimator
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}(g)
is consistent and satisfies a CLT at rate
\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}
. However, this modification requires a tunning parameter and its second-order asymptotics are now dependent on the memory of the process and the sample scheme. Finally, to illustrate the usefulness of our results, we propose a method for testing the presence of
T
-dependent data that is robust to long memory.

Outline of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the main mathematical concepts and and notation that will be used through the paper. Furthermore, the definitions of trawl processes and its basic properties are also included in this part. In Section 3 we formulate the main results of the paper, concerning the asymptotics of the functionals
\Psi^{n}(g)
and

, along with our new test for
T
-dependence. Due to the technical nature of our proofs, most of them are deferred to Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

This part focus on introducing the basic notation and concepts that
will be used during this paper. Throughout the following sections

\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
will denote a complete
probability space. As usual, a function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is said to be of class
\mathcal{C}^{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
if it is
N
-times continuously differentiable. In this case,
f^{(k)}
will denote the
k
th derivative of
f
with
the convention that
f^{(0)}=f
. Often, we will write
f^{\prime}
or
\dot{f}

instead of
f^{(1)}
. The
symetrization of a function

f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is defined and denoted as
\tilde{f}(x,y):=f(x,y)+f(y,x)
.

The symbols
\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightarrow}
and
\overset{d}{\rightarrow}

stand, respectively, for convergence in probability and distribution
of random vectors (r.v.’s for short). For a sequence of random vectors

(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
defined on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
,
we set, respectively,
\xi_{n}=\mathrm{o}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
and
\xi_{n}=\mathrm{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)

whenever
\xi_{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightarrow}0
or if
\xi_{n}

is bounded in probability. For
T>0
, let
(Z_{t}^{n})_{0\leq t\leq T,n\in\mathbb{N}}

be a sequence of càdlàg processes defined on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
.
We will write
Z^{n}\overset{u.c.p}{\longrightarrow}Z
if
Z^{n}

converges uniformly on compacts in probability towards
Z
. Similarly,

Z^{n}\overset{\mathcal{D}([0,T])}{\Longrightarrow}Z
stands for weak
convergence of
Z^{n}
towards
Z
in the Skhorohod topology.

We recall to the reader that a real-valued random field
L=\{L\left(A\right):A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}

on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
is called a
homogeneous
Lévy basis
if it is an infinitely divisible (ID for short) independently
scatterred random measure such that

\mathbb{E}(\exp(\mathbf{i}zL(A)))=\exp(\mathrm{Leb}(A)\psi(z)),\,\,\forall\,A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),z\in\mathbb{R},

(2)

where
\mathrm{Leb}
and
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the bounded Borel sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively. Furthermore,

\psi(z):=\mathbf{i}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}z^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(e^{\mathbf{i}zx}-1-\mathbf{i}zx\mathbf{1}_{\rvert x\rvert\leq 1})\nu(\mathrm{d}x),

(3)

with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

b\geq 0
and
\nu
a Lévy measure,
i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(1\land\rvert x\rvert^{2})\nu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
and
\nu
charges
0
at
{0}
.
We will refer to the ID random variable with characteristic triplet

\left(\gamma,b,\nu\right)
as the
Lévy seed
of
L
, and
it will be denoted by
L^{\prime}
. As it is costumary,
\left(\gamma,b,\nu\right)

will be called the
characteristic triplet
of
L
and
\psi

its
characteristic exponent
. We will say that
L

has unit variance
if
\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})=1
.

Let
L
be a homogeneous Lévy basis on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with characteristic
triplet
(\gamma,b,\nu)
. In addition, we set

A=\left\{(r,y):r\leq 0,0\leq y\leq a(-r)\right\}.

(4)

where
a:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a non-increasing,
continuous, and integrable function. The process following the dynamics

X_{t}:=L(A_{t}),\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R},

(5)

where
A_{t}:=A+(t,0)
, is called
trawl process
. To avoid trivial situations, we will always assume that
\mathrm{Leb}(A)>0
or equivalently
a
cannot be equal to
0
almost everywhere. From now
on, we will allude to
A
and
a
as
trawl set and trawl
function
, respectively. Furthermore,
L
will termed as as the
background driving Lévy basis
.

It is well known that
X
is strictly stationary
and, in the case when
L^{\prime}
is square integrable, its auto-covariance
function is given by

\varGamma_{X}(h):=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{\left|h\right|}^{\infty}a(u)\mathrm{d}u,\,\,\,h\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

For a detailed exposition on the basic properties of trawl processes
and Lévy bases we refer the reader to
[
2
]
and
references therein.

3
Estimating non-linear functionals of trawl functions

In what is left of this work
X
will denote a trawl process with trawl function
a
and whose background driving Lévy basis has unit variance. As already discussed in the introduction, our
main goal is to estimate the functionals

\Psi_{t}(g)=\int_{0}^{t}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s;\,\,\Lambda_{t}(g):=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s,

(7)

when equidistant observations of
X
, say
(X_{i\Delta_{n}})_{i=0}^{n}
,
are available. The sample scheme considered in this work is in-fill and long-span,
i.e.

n\uparrow\infty;\,\,\Delta_{n}\downarrow 0;\,\,n\Delta_{n}\rightarrow+\infty.

(8)

To do so, we modify slightly the definition of
\check{a}
in (
1
)
as follows:

\hat{a}(t):=-\frac{1}{n\Delta_{n}}\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}(X_{(k-l)\Delta_{n}}-\bar{X}_{n})\delta_{k}X,\,\,l\Delta_{n}\leq t<(l+1)\Delta_{n},

(9)

for some
l=0,\ldots,n-1
and where
\delta_{k}X:=X_{(k+1)\Delta_{n}}-X_{k\Delta_{n}}
. It is
not difficult to see that
\hat{a}(t)
and
\check{a}(t)
are asymptotically
equivalents. The estimators for the functionals appearing in (
7
)
are constructed in a natural way as

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}^{n}(g):=

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=0}^{[t/\Delta_{n}]-1}g(\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n})),\,\,\,\,0\leq t\leq(n-1)\Delta_{n};

(10)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{n}(g):=

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=[t/\Delta_{n}]}^{n-1}g(\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n}));\,\,0\leq t\leq(n-1)\Delta_{n}.

3.1
Limit theorems for
\Psi^{n}(g)
and
\Lambda^{n}(g)

In this part we describe first and second order asymptotics for the
estimators introduced in (
10
). We start discussing
consistency of such statistics. We recall the reader that a real-valued
function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be of
polynomial
growth
of order
q\geq 0
if there is some
C>0
, such that

\rvert f(x)\rvert\leq C(1+\rvert x\rvert^{q}),\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

The reader should keep in mind that our sampling scheme satisfies
(
8
) and this will be always assumed
to hold for the rest of this paper. Under this set-up we have the
following consistency result for
\Psi^{n}(g)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be a continuous function of polynomial growth of order
q\geq 0
.
Suppose that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2q\lor 4})<\infty
.
Then,
\Psi^{n}(g)\overset{u.c.p}{\rightarrow}\Psi(g).

Let us now turn our attention to
\Lambda^{n}(g)
. To guarantee integrability
of the mapping
s\mapsto g(a(s))
, ergo the well-definiteness of

\Lambda_{t}(g)
, we need to restrict the type of test functions
to be considered. For a given
p,q\geq 0
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we will
write
\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{d}
for the family of functions of class

\mathcal{C}^{d}
satisfying that
g^{(j)}(0)=0
for
j=0,1,\ldots,d-1

and that
g^{(d)}(x)=\mathrm{O}(\rvert x\rvert^{p})
as
x\rightarrow 0

and
g^{(d)}(x)=\mathrm{O}(\rvert x\rvert^{q})
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
.
Obviously the power function
g(x)=\rvert x\rvert^{\alpha}
, for

\alpha>1
belongs to
\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{[\alpha]}
with
p=q=\alpha-[\alpha]
.

Remark 1
.

Note that if
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{d}
,
an induction argument along with the Mean-Value Theorem imply that

g^{(j)}(x)=\mathrm{O}(\rvert x\rvert^{d-j+p})
, as
x\rightarrow 0
,
for all
j=1,\ldots,d
. Thus, in view that
a
is decreasing and bounded by
a(0),

we can find a constant
C=C(a(0))>0
, such that for all
s\geq 0

\max_{j=0,1,\ldots,d-1}\rvert g^{(j)}(a(s))\rvert\leq Ca(s)^{d-j+p},\,\,\,j=1,\ldots,d.

(11)

It follows that the mappings
s\in[0,+\infty)\mapsto g^{(j)}(a(s))

are continuous, bounded, and integrable for
j=0,1,\ldots,d\land(d-1+[p])
.
In particular,
\Lambda_{t}(g)
is well-defined for every
t\geq 0
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{1}

with
p,q>1
and assume that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2(q\lor p+1)})<\infty
.
Then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
\Lambda^{n}(g)\overset{u.c.p}{\rightarrow}\Lambda(g).

The previous result does not cover the quadratic case, i.e. when
p\land q=1
.
This is because in this situation
\Lambda^{n}(g)
might be biased
as the following result shows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
g(x)=x^{2}
and assume
that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{4})<\infty
. Then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\Lambda_{0}^{n}(g)\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightarrow}2\Lambda_{0}(g).

To deal with the previous situation, we proceed as in
[
17
]
and modify
\Lambda^{n}(g)
as
follows: We introduce a sequence
N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
N_{n}\leq n
,
such that

i)\,N_{n}\uparrow\infty;\,\,ii)\,N_{n}/n\rightarrow 0;\,\,iii)\,N_{n}\Delta_{n}\rightarrow+\infty.

(12)

We set

\bar{\Lambda}_{t}^{n}(g):=\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=[t/\Delta_{n}]}^{N_{n}-1}g(\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n}));\,\,0\leq t\leq(N_{n}-1)\Delta_{n}.

(13)

The sequence of processes
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}
, unlike
\Lambda^{n}
,
is consistent in the quadratic case. More precisely:

Theorem 4
.

Consider
N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}

as in (
12
). Let
p,q\geq 1
and assume
that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2(q\lor p+1)})<\infty
.
Then for all
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{1}
,
\bar{\Lambda}_{t}^{n}(g)\overset{u.c.p}{\rightarrow}\Lambda(g)
, as
n\rightarrow\infty.

We now proceed to discuss second order asymptotics for our statistics. As usual, to achieve
this, stronger assumptions need to be imposed. We follow essentially
the set-up of
[
17
]
.

Assumption 1
.

The sampling scheme satisfies
that
n\Delta_{n}^{3}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore,
the trawl function admits the representation
a(s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}\phi(y)dy
,
for
s\geq 0
, where
\phi
is a strictly positive càdlàg function such that
\phi(s)=\mathrm{O}(s^{-\alpha-1})
as
s\rightarrow+\infty

for some
\alpha>1
.

Before presenting the Central Limit Theorems for
\Psi^{n},\Lambda^{n}
, and
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}
let
us introduce some functions that will serve for describing their asymptotic
variance. For
s,r\geq 0
and
\mathfrak{K}_{4}:=\int x^{4}\nu(dx)
,
we put

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(s,r)

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{K}_{4}a(s\lor r);

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(s,r)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(u)a(\rvert u-(s-r)\rvert)\mathrm{sgn}(u-(s-r))\mathrm{d}u;

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}(s,r)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(u+r)a(\rvert s-u\rvert)\mathrm{sgn}(s-u)\mathrm{d}u.

Theorem 5
.

Let Assumption
1
hold.
If
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
,
with
g^{(2)}
being of polynomial growth of order
q\geq 0

and
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2(q+2)\lor r_{0}})<\infty

for some
r_{0}>4
, then as
n\rightarrow\infty

\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}(\Psi^{n}(g)-\Psi(g))\overset{\mathcal{D}([0,T])}{\Longrightarrow}Z,

where
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a centered Gaussian process with

\mathbb{E}(Z_{t}Z_{s})=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\dot{g}(a(u))\Sigma_{a}(u,r)\dot{g}(a(r))\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u,\,\,\,t,s\geq 0,

in which

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a}(u,r)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{1}(u,r)+\tilde{\sigma}_{2}(u,r)+\tilde{\sigma}_{3}(u,r).

(14)

The second order asymptotics for the functionals
\Lambda^{n}
and
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}

will be much alike to the one presented in the previous theorem. However, we will need to impose certain restrictions on the window
N_{n}
that will help us to control
the discretization error associated to
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}
as well as the memory of
X
. Specifically, we will assume that:

Assumption 2
.

There are
1<\varpi<3,\,\theta>0
,
and
0<\kappa<1
, such that
n\Delta_{n}^{\varpi}
is bounded from
above and below, and
N_{n}\sim\theta n^{\kappa}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The CLT for the functionals
\Lambda^{n}
and
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}

reads as follows.

Theorem 6
.

Let Assumption

1
hold. Suppose that
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{p,q}^{2}
and that for some
r_{0}>4
we have that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2(q\lor p+2)\lor r_{0}})<\infty
.
Then the following holds:

1.

If
p,q>1
, then as
n\rightarrow\infty

\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}(\Lambda^{n}(g)-\Lambda(g))\overset{\mathcal{D}([0,T])}{\Longrightarrow}Z,

(15)

where
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a centered Gaussian process with

\mathbb{E}(Z_{t}Z_{s})=\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{s}^{\infty}\dot{g}(a(u))\Sigma_{a}(u,r)\dot{g}(a(r))\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u,

with
\Sigma_{a}
as in (
14
).

2.

If in addition Assumption
2
is satisfied with

0<\frac{1}{\varpi}+\frac{\varpi-1}{2\varpi((2+p)\alpha-1)}<\kappa<\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\varpi}),

(16)

and
p,q\geq 0
arbitrary, then (
15
) also holds if we replace
\Lambda^{n}
by
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}
.

Remark 2
.

The following remarks are in order

1.

Let
Z
be as in Theorems
5

and
6
. From Remark
1
,
it follows that for every
0\leq s\leq t

\mathbb{E}(\rvert Z_{t}-Z_{s}\rvert^{2})\leq C(t-s)^{2}.

Therefore, by Kolmogorov’s criterion, we deduce that
Z
has
\lambda
-Hölder
continuous paths for every
\lambda<1.

2.

We are able to show that in the limiting case
p\wedge q=1
– recall that when this is not the case, in general,
\Lambda^{n}(g)
is not consistent – the sequence
\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}(\Lambda^{n}(g)-\Lambda(g))
is tight on
\mathcal{D}([0,T])
but we were not able to identify its limit. However, we believe that, just as in the case
g(x)=x^{2}
, the limit process is Gaussian but no longer centred.

3.

The lower bound in (
16
), which depends on the memory of
X
, is to prevent that the tail
\int_{N_{n}\Delta_{n}}^{\infty}g(a(s))\mathrm{d}s
dominates the asymptotics. Thus, unlike Theorem
5
and the first part of Theorem
6
, the CLT for
\bar{\Lambda}^{n}(g)
is no longer robust to long memory.

3.2
A test for
T
-dependent trawls

In this part we briefly exemplify how our results can be used to test for
T
-dependence in a non-parametrical framework. We recall the reader that a stochastic process
(Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is said to be
T
-dependent
, with
T>0
, if for a given
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the processes
(Y_{t})_{t\leq t_{0}}
and
(Y_{t})_{t\geq T+t_{0}}
are independent (in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions). We have the following characterization of
T
-dependence for trawl processes.

Proposition 1
.

Let
X
be a trawl process. Then, unless
L
is deterministic,
X
is
T
-dependent if, and only if, for all
t\geq T
,
\mathrm{Leb}(A_{t}\cap A)=0
.

Proof.

Since
X
is an infinitely divisible process,
T
-dependence is equivalent to having that for a given
t_{0}
,
X_{s}
and
X_{u}
are independent for all
s\leq t_{0}
and
u\geq t_{0}+T
(see for instance Exercise E 12.9 in
[
16
]
). Furthermore, by stationarity, the former condition is equivalent to the independence of
X_{t}
and
X_{0}
for all
t\geq T
. Now note that, in view of (
2
) and the independent scatterred property of
L
, the characteristic exponent of the vector
(X_{0},X_{t})
equals to

[\psi(z_{1})+\psi(z_{2})]\mathrm{Leb}(A)+\mathrm{Leb}(A_{t}\cap A)[\psi(z_{1}+z_{2})-\psi(z_{1})-\psi(z_{2})],\,\,\forall\,(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

In consequence,
X_{t}
and
X_{0}
are independent if, and only if,

\mathrm{Leb}(A_{t}\cap A)[\psi(z_{1}+z_{2})-\psi(z_{1})-\psi(z_{2})]=0,\,\,\forall\,(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(17)

Thus, if
\mathrm{Leb}(A_{t}\cap A)=0
, for all
t\geq T
, the previous relation is trivially fulfilled. Reciprocally, if (
17
) is satisfied for all
t\geq T
, then either
\mathrm{Leb}(A_{t}\cap A)=0
or
\psi(z_{1}+z_{2})=\psi(z_{1})+\psi(z_{2})
for all
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If the latter holds, we would have that
\psi
is continuous and additive, so linear (c.f. Cauchy’s functional equation) which is the case only when
L
is deterministic.
∎

The preceeding result shows that, under our set-up,
X
is
T
-dependent if, and only if,
\int_{T}^{\infty}a(s)\mathrm{d}s=0
. Note that this condition is in turn equivalent to
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{T},X_{0})=0
and thus one can use the sample autocovariance function to test
T
-dependence on
X
. However, it is well known that such a statistic is heavily dependent on the memory of the process. See for instance
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
21
]
. To avoid this problem we propose to use another characterization of
T
-dependence, namely

\int_{T}^{\infty}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s=0,\text{ for some (and thus for all) }p>0,

(18)

which follows from the fact that
a
is non-negative and non-increasing. Therefore, under the null hypothesis of
T
-dependence we deduce, due to the fact that
\mathrm{Leb}(A)>0
, that

\frac{\Lambda_{0}(g)}{\Psi_{T}(g)}=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s}{\int_{0}^{T}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s}=1,

where
g(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{p}
. Based on the previous discussion, we proposed to use the “
memory independent
” statistic

\tau_{n,p}=\frac{\Lambda^{n}_{0}(g)}{\Psi_{T}^{n}(g)}-1=\frac{\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\rvert\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n})\rvert^{p}}{\Delta_{n}\sum_{l=0}^{[T/\Delta_{n}]-1}\rvert\hat{a}(l\Delta_{n})\rvert^{p}}-1,\,\,p>3,

to test the hypothesis (
18
). The asymptotic behaviour of
\tau_{n,p}
is described in the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and assume that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert L^{\prime}\rvert^{2p})<\infty
for some
p>3
. Then,

\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}\tau_{n,p}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\eqref{H0_T_dep}\text{ holds};\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(19)

Proof.

Plainly,
\tau_{n,p}=\Lambda^{n}_{T}(g)/\Psi_{T}^{n}(g)
, with
g(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{p}
. Thus, if (
18
) is satisfied, Theorem
6
implies that
\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}\Lambda^{n}_{T}(g)\overset{d}{\to}\zeta
, where
\zeta
is a centred Gaussian random variable with variance

p^{2}\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{T}^{\infty}\rvert a(u)\rvert^{p-1}\Sigma_{a}(u,r)\rvert a(r)\rvert^{p-1}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}r=0,

thanks to (
18
). This shows the first part of (
19
). In constrast, if (
18
) does not hold then necessarily
\int_{0}^{T}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s>0
. Theorem
2
now gives that
\tau_{n,p}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\frac{\int_{T}^{\infty}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s}{\int_{0}^{T}\rvert a(s)\lvert^{p}\mathrm{d}s}>0
, from which the second part of (
19
) follows.
∎

It should be clear from our discussion above and the previous corollary that a large value for
\sqrt{n\Delta_{n}}\tau_{n,p}
suggests that the data is unlikely to be
T
-dependent.

Remark 3
.

As pointed out in Remark
2
, our proposed statistics for testing (
18
) is robust to the memory of
X
. Furthermore, as demonstrated in the previous corollary,
\tau_{n,p}
is more efficient under null. However, it still suffers of some weakness. First, to rigorously use
\tau_{n,p}
for testing
T
-dependence, a non-trival CLT under the null hipothesis (
18
) is required. Secondly, it remains to be seen how sensitive is
\tau_{n,p}
in finite-sample for different values of
p
. These problems requires additional analysis, which we postpone to future research.

Lemma 4

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{n-1}\rvert\overline{c}_{l}^{n}\rvert t_{l}\lesssim\mathrm{O}(1/n\Delta_{n})+\frac{m_{n}}{n}\times\frac{1}{m_{n}\Delta_{n}}\int_{0}^{m_{n}\Delta_{n}}a(s)^{p+1}s\mathrm{d}s\rightarrow 0,

thanks to Assumption
1
and (
12
).

Therefore, in any situation, it holds that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(\ell,\ell^{\prime})}-\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(\ell,\ell^{\prime})})\rvert^{2})\rightarrow 0
,
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which is exactly the conclusion of this
Lemma.

For convenience, when
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, we will refer to
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(\ell,\ell^{\prime})}

as the conditional variance, otherwise we will use the adjective conditional
covariance. We would also like to emphasize that when we present the
decomposition (
67
) we are always going to use
the notation
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}
and its definition
will vary from case to case.

Conditional Variances

\ell=1:

Set
\mathfrak{K}_{3}=\int x^{3}\nu(dx)
and write

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}:=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}_{3}\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}a(s)\mathrm{d}s\sum_{j=l\lor l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{K}_{3}\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}a(s)\mathrm{d}s\sum_{j=l\lor l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\beta_{j,j-1,l}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\Delta_{n}}a(s)\mathrm{d}s\sum_{j=l\lor l^{\prime}}^{n-1}[\beta_{j,j-1,l}^{(2)}\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}(\beta_{j,j-1,l}^{(2)}\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)})]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{\iota=1}^{3}\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,\iota},

so that (
67
) holds. Since the sequence
\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}

is
l^{\prime}
-dependent and
\mathbb{E}[(\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)})^{2}]\leq\mathrm{Leb}(A),

we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=l\lor l^{\prime}}^{n}\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right]\leq Cnl^{\prime},

which adds up to
\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}\rvert^{2})\lesssim n\Delta_{n}(l^{\prime}\lor l\Delta_{n}+C).

Similar arguments can be applied (
\beta_{j,j-1,l}^{(2)}\beta_{j,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}

is
l\lor l^{\prime}
-dependent) to show that the same estimate holds
for
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,2}
and
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,3}
,
which is enough.

\ell=2:

By interchanging the order of summation we may write

\sum_{j=l\lor l^{\prime}}^{n}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{j,l,n}^{(2)}\xi_{j,l^{\prime},n}^{(2)}\mid\mathscr{G}_{j-1}^{n})=\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{(k-l)\land(k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})}\cup_{j=k\lor k^{\prime}+1}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,j)).

Thus, (
67
) holds with

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}[\chi_{k}^{2}-\mathbb{E}(\chi_{k}^{2})]b_{k,l,l^{\prime}}+\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\chi_{k}\nu_{k,l^{\prime},l}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\chi_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\nu}_{k,l,l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{\iota=1}^{3}\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,\iota}

where

\displaystyle b_{k,l,l^{\prime}}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\mathbf{1}_{k^{\prime}=k}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{(k-l)\land(k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})}\cup_{j=k+1}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,j));

\displaystyle\nu_{k,l,l^{\prime}}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\chi_{k^{\prime}}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{(k-l)\land(k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})}\cup_{j=k+1}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,j))\mathbf{1}_{k^{\prime}\leq k-1};

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{k,l,l^{\prime}}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\chi_{k}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{(k-l)\land(k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})}\cup_{j=k^{\prime}+1}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,j))\mathbf{1}_{k\leq k^{\prime}-1}

Now note that
b_{k,l,l^{\prime}}\leq\mathrm{Leb}(A)
and
\mathbb{E}(\rvert\chi_{k}^{2}-\mathbb{E}(\chi_{k}^{2})\rvert^{2}]\lesssim\Delta_{n}
.
Furthermore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\rvert\chi_{k}\nu_{k,l^{\prime},l}\rvert^{2})\lesssim

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{2}\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{k^{\prime}-l^{\prime}}\cup_{j\geq k}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,j))^{2}\mathbf{1}_{k^{\prime}\leq k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\int_{0}^{n\Delta_{n}}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}a(u)\mathrm{d}u\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(C_{\alpha}+(n\Delta_{n})^{1-2(\alpha-1)}),

where in the last inequality we also applied Assumption
1
.
Similarly, we deduce that that
\mathbb{E}(\rvert\chi_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\nu}_{k,l,l^{\prime}}\rvert^{2})\lesssim\Delta_{n}(C_{\alpha}+(n\Delta_{n})^{1+2(1-\alpha)}).

In view that
[\chi_{k}^{2}-\mathbb{E}(\chi_{k}^{2})]
,
\chi_{k}\nu_{k,l^{\prime},l}

and
\chi_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\nu}_{k,l,l^{\prime}}
are all martingale
differences, it follows that

\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}\rvert^{2})=\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(\rvert\chi_{k}^{2}-\mathbb{E}(\chi_{k}^{2})\rvert^{2})b_{k,l,l^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{Leb}(A)^{2}n\Delta_{n}.

Likewise, for
\iota=2,3
we have that

\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,\iota}\rvert^{2})\lesssim n\Delta_{n}(C_{\alpha}+(n\Delta_{n})^{1-2(\alpha-1)}).

Therefore, in this situation (
68
) is satisfied
with
\upsilon_{n}=C_{\alpha}+(n\Delta_{n})^{1-2(\alpha-1)}.

\ell=3:

By symmetry we may write

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(3,3)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(3,3)})+\Delta_{n}^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(\overline{c}_{l}^{n})^{2}\mathcal{S}_{l,l}^{n}

\displaystyle+2\Delta_{n}^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{l-1}\overline{c}_{l}^{n}\overline{c}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}}{n\Delta_{n}},

where

\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}:=\sum_{j=l}^{n}\sum_{k=l}^{j-1}[\beta_{k,j-1,l}^{(2)}\beta_{k,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}(\beta_{k,j-1,l}^{(2)}\beta_{k,j-1,l^{\prime}}^{(2)})]\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}].

The goal of this part is to show that

\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}\rvert^{2})\lesssim n\Delta_{n}(l\Delta_{n}+\mathrm{Leb}(A)).

(70)

The
\sigma
-additivity of
L
implies that

\beta_{k,j-1,l}^{(2)}=\sum_{m=k-l}^{j-1}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}.

(71)

Applying this and performing several tedious changes of order of summation
we conclude that
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}=\sum_{\imath=1}^{7}\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,\imath}
,
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}[\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)}-\mathbb{E}(\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)})]\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,2}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l-l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=(l-l^{\prime})\lor(m-l^{\prime})+1}^{m-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m^{\prime}+l^{\prime})}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,3}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\sum_{m^{\prime}=m+1}^{k-1}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,4}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m^{\prime}=l+1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{m^{\prime}}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\sum_{j=m^{\prime}+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,5}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l}^{n-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=l-l^{\prime}}^{m-1}\sum_{k=l}^{m\land(m^{\prime}+l^{\prime})}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,6}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l}^{m}[\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)}-\mathbb{E}(\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)})]\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,7}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m^{\prime}=l+1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{m^{\prime}-1}\sum_{k=l}^{m}\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\sum_{j=m^{\prime}+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

We will only check that
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,2}

satisfy (
70
) since the other terms can be analyzed
in the same way. Set
\theta_{k}:=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

and note that by the independent scattered property of
L
it holds
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}\rvert^{2})\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\sum_{p=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\mathbb{E}(\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\beta_{p,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{p,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)})\theta_{k}\theta_{p}

Lemma
1
implies now that

\mathbb{E}(\beta_{k,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{k,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)}\beta_{p,m,l}^{(1)}\beta_{p,m,l^{\prime}}^{(1)})\lesssim_{a,\mathfrak{t}_{L}}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{k\land p-l}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m)).

This estimate along with the fact that
\rvert\theta_{k}\rvert\leq\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}a(s)\mathrm{d}s\leq a(0)\Delta_{n}

lead to

\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}\rvert^{2})\lesssim\Delta_{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\theta_{k}\sum_{p=l\lor m+1}^{k}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{p-l}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m)).

Since

\sum_{p=l\lor m+1}^{k}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{p-l}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m))=\sum_{p=l\lor m+1}^{k}\int_{t_{m}+t_{l}-t_{p}}^{t_{m+1}+t_{l}-t_{p}}a(s)\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{Leb}(A),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\theta_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\sum_{j=k+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]+\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l)}\sum_{j=(n-1)\land(m+l)}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k+1,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq l\Delta_{n}+\mathrm{Leb}(A),

where in the second inequality we also used (
21
). This
shows that
\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,1}
satisfies (
70
).
Similar arguments give that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\rvert\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,2}\rvert^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l-l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=(l-l^{\prime})\lor(m-l^{\prime})+1}^{m-1}\sum_{k,p=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m^{\prime}+l^{\prime})}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{k\land p-l}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m))\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{k\land p-l^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m^{\prime}))\theta_{k}\theta_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{m=l-l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=(l-l^{\prime})\lor(m-l^{\prime})+1}^{m-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m^{\prime}+l^{\prime})}\mathrm{Leb}(\cup_{i=0}^{k-l^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{A}^{n}(i,m^{\prime}))\theta_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\sum_{m=l-l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l\lor m+1}^{(n-1)\land(m+l^{\prime})}\theta_{k}\lesssim n\Delta_{n}(l^{\prime}\Delta_{n}+\mathrm{Leb}(A)).

\ell=4:

Recall that
\varsigma_{k,j}:=\sum_{m=k+1}^{j-1}\alpha_{k+1,m}

and note that as above we may write

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(4,4)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(4,4)})+\Delta_{n}^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(\overline{c}_{l}^{n})^{2}\mathcal{S}_{l,l}^{n}

\displaystyle+2\Delta_{n}^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{l-1}\overline{c}_{l}^{n}\overline{c}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}}{n\Delta_{n}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}[\varsigma_{k,j}^{2}-\mathbb{E}(\varsigma_{k,j})]\theta_{k,l,k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=l+1}^{n-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}=l^{\prime}}^{k-1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k^{\prime},j}\varsigma_{k,j}\theta_{k,l,k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k^{\prime}=l+1}^{n-1}\sum_{k=l}^{k^{\prime}-1}\sum_{j=k^{\prime}+1}^{n}\varsigma_{k^{\prime},j}\varsigma_{k,j}\theta_{k,l,k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{j}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{\imath=1}^{3}\mathcal{S}_{l,l^{\prime}}^{n,\imath},

wherein we have let

)

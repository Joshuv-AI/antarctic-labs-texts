The Keller-Segel model with mass critical exponent
Shen Bian, Yichen Zou

Abstract
We consider a Keller-Segel model with non-linear porous medium type diffusion and non-local attractive power law interaction, focusing on potentials that are less singular than Newtonian interaction. Here, the nonlinear diffusion is chosen to be m = 2 − 2 ​ s d m=2-\frac{2s}{d} , in which case the steady states are compactly supported. We analyse under what conditions on the initial data the regime that attractive forces are stronger than diffusion occurs and classify the conditions for global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions. It is shown that there exists a threshold value which is characterized by the optimal constant of a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality. Specifically, the solution will exist globally if the initial data is below the threshold, while the solution blows up in finite time when the initial data is above the threshold.

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate qualitative properties of non -negative solutions for the aggregation-diffusion equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=\Delta u^{m}-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla\phi),\\
\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}\phi(x,t)=u(x,t),\\

where the diffusion exponent is taken to be

, that is

\displaystyle\phi\left(x,t\right)=c_{d,s}\int_{{R}^{d}}\frac{u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy,

(4)

\displaystyle c_{d,s}=\frac{\Gamma(d/2-s)}{\pi^{d/2}4^{s}\Gamma(s)}.

(5)

The initial data throughout this paper is assumed to satisfy

\displaystyle u_{0}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{2}|u_{0}(x)dx<\infty.

(6)

A fundamental property of the solutions to (

\displaystyle M:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x)dx,\quad\text{for}~~t\geq 0.

\displaystyle F\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{R}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{R}^{d}}\phi udx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{R}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2}c_{d,s}\iint_{{R}^{d}\times{{R}^{d}}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy.

\displaystyle u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla\mu\right),

\displaystyle\frac{dF(u)}{dt}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u|\nabla\mu|^{2}dx\leq 0.

Local existence and blow-up criteria

Consider regularization issues

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}^{m}+\varepsilon\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\cdot\left(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\right),~x\in{R}^{d},~t>0,\\

A variant of the HLS inequality

As we have just seen in the existence proof, the existence time of a weak solution to (

In this subsection, we will show the existence of the extremal function for the modified HLS inequality which is stated as

\displaystyle\omega(u):=\iint_{R^{d}\times R^{d}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy,

\displaystyle J(u):=\frac{\omega(u)}{\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2s/d}\|u\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}},\quad u\in{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{L}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proposition 4

(VHLS inequality)
.

Let

. Let

C_{*}

\displaystyle J(u)\leq C_{*}.

The optimal constant satisfies

\displaystyle C_{*}=\pi^{(d-2s)/2}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma((d+2s)/2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma(d)}\right)^{-2s/d}.

(44)

The equality holds in (

for any real numbers

,

and

.

is a radially symmetric, non-increasing and compactly supported function.

Proof.

Step 1
(Existence of
C_{*}
) Consider
u\in{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{L}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Applying the HLS inequality (
23
) with
q=q_{1}=2d/(d+2s)
and

, and then the Hölder inequality with

, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{{R}^{d}\times{{R}^{d}}}\frac{u(x,t)u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dxdy\right|\leq C(d,s)\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d+2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C(d,s)\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2s}{d}}\|u\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m},

(45)

where

\displaystyle C(d,s)=\pi^{(d-2s)/2}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma((d+2s)/2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma(d)}\right)^{-2s/d}.

(46)

Consequently,

is finite and bounded from above by

.

Step 2
(Existence of maximisers) Consider a maximising sequence
(u_{j})_{j}
in
{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathrm{L}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}J(u_{j})=C_{*}\text{.}

(47)

We first prove that we may assume that
u_{j}
is a non-negative, radially symmetric, non-increasing function such that
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u_{j}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
for any
j\geq 0
. Indeed
J(u_{j})\leq J(|u_{j}|)
so that
(|u_{j}|)_{j}
is also a maximising sequence. Next, denoting by
u_{j}^{*}
the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
|u_{j}|
, we infer from the Riesz rearrangement properties
[
21
, Theorem 3.7]
that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}^{*}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\|u_{j}^{*}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u_{j}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\omega(u_{j}^{*})\geq\omega(u_{j}).

(48)

Thus

\displaystyle J(u_{j}^{*})\geq J(u_{j}).

(49)

Consequently,
\left(u_{j}^{*}\right)_{j}
is also a maximising sequence and the first step is proved.

We now establish the existence of the supremum. The monotonicity and the non-negativity of
u_{j}
imply that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}=d\alpha_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}u_{j}(r)dr\geq\alpha_{d}R^{d}\left(u_{j}(R)\right)^{m},

(50)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\alpha_{d}R^{d}\left(u_{j}(R)\right).

(51)

So that

\displaystyle 0\leq u_{j}(R)\leq G(R):=C_{2}\text{inf}\{R^{-d/m};~R^{-d}\}

(52)

Now, we use once more the monotonicity of the
u_{j}
’s and their boundedness in
(R,\infty)
for any
R>0
to deduce from Helly’s selection principle
[
21
, P89]
that there are a sub-sequence of
(u_{j})_{j}
(not relabelled) and a non-negative and non-increasing function
U
such that
(u_{j})_{j}
converges to
U
point-wisely. In addition, as
1<2d/(d+2s)<m
,
\|G(|x|)\|_{L^{2d/(d+2s)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
while the HLS inequality (
23
) warrants that
\omega(G(|x|))<\infty
. Together with (
52
) and the point-wise convergence of
(u_{j})_{j}
, this implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\omega(u_{j})=\omega(U)

(53)

by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. In addition, the point-wise convergence of
(u_{j})_{j}
and Fatou’s lemma ensure

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\text{,}\quad\|U\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u_{j}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(54)

Thus

\displaystyle J(U)=\frac{\omega(U)}{\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2s/d}\|U\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}}\geq\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{\omega(u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2s/d}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}}=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\omega(u_{j})=C_{*}

(55)

and using (
43
) we conclude that

\displaystyle J(U)=C_{*}.

(56)

Finally, let us point out that
J(U)=J(\tilde{U})
where
\tilde{U}=\lambda U(\mu|x-x_{0}|))
for
\forall\lambda,~\mu>0
. This completes the proof.

We are now in a position to begin the study of the identification of the maximisers. To this end, let us define the critical mass
M_{*}
by

\displaystyle M_{*}:=\left[\frac{2}{(m-1)C_{*}c_{d,s}}\right]^{d/2s}.

(57)

and denote

\displaystyle{H}_{M_{*}}:=\left\{u\in{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{L}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M_{*}\right\}.

(58)

Proposition 5

(Euler-Lagrange equation)
.

Let
U
be a maximiser of
J
in
H_{M_{*}}
. Then
U
is a radially symmetric, non-increasing and compactly supported stationary solution to (
1
) solving the following Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m-1}-c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy=\frac{1}{M_{*}}\frac{2s}{2s-d}\|U\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m},~\text{a.e. in}~supp(U).

(59)

Proof.

Let us describe the set of maximisers of
J
in
H_{M_{*}}
. Let us define
\Omega=supp(U)
. Let
\forall\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\psi(x)-\frac{U}{M_{*}}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)dx,

(60)

then
supp(\varphi)\subseteq\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}\varphi dx=0
. Besides, there exists

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\min\limits_{y\in supp\varphi}U(y)}{\max\limits_{y\in supp\varphi}|\varphi(y)|}>0

(61)

such that
U+\varepsilon\varphi\geq 0
in
\Omega
for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Then a tedious computation shows that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}J\left(U+\varepsilon\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{c_{d,s}\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2s/d}\|U\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m-1}-c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy\right)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{2s}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi dx}{\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2s/d+1}\|U\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}}\omega(U)=0

(62)

Then by the definition of
\varphi
, (
62
) can be read as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{M}-\frac{1}{M_{*}}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{M}Udx\right)\psi(x)dx=0,\text{ for any }\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(63)

Here
\mathcal{M}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m-1}-c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy.

(64)

Therefore we discover

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m-1}-c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy=\frac{1}{M_{*}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m}dx-c_{d,s}\omega(U)\right),\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\text{. }

(65)

Taking
J(U)=C_{*}
which is stated in (
56
) into account, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}U^{m-1}-c_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{*}}\left(\frac{m}{m-1}-\frac{2}{m-1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U^{m}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{*}}\frac{2s}{2s-d}\|U\|_{L^{m}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{m},\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\text{. }

(66)

Recalling Proposition 3.2 of
[
2
]
, we can proceed to claim that
U
is a stationary solution to (
1
). This completes the proof.

4
Global existence and finite time blow-up

4.1
Finite time blow-up

Lemma 7

(Second moment)
.

Under assumption (
6
), let
u
be a weak solution to (
1
) for
m=2-\frac{2s}{d}
on
[0,T)
with initial condition
u_{0}
for some
T\in(0,\infty]
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{2}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2(d-2s)F(u).

(67)

Proof.

By integrating by parts in (
1
) and symmetrising the second term, the time derivative of the second moment of the weak solution is endowed with

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{2}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{m}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x\cdot\nabla\phi)udx

\displaystyle=2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{m}dx-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(y,t)u(x,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dydx

\displaystyle=2(d-2s)F(u).

(68)

giving the desired identity.

An easy consequence of the previous lemma is the following blow-up result.

Theorem 8

(Blowing-up solutions)
.

If
M>M_{*}
, there exists initial data
u_{0}
under initial condition (
6
) with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M
and negative free energy
F(u_{0})
. Moreover, if
u
denotes a weak solution to (
1
) on
[0,T)
with initial condition
u_{0}
, then
T<\infty
and the
{L}^{m}
-norm of
u
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Step 1
(The negativity of free energy) For
U\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by (
59
), we get
\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M_{*}
and
J(U)=C_{*}
. If
M>M_{*}
, the initial condition
u_{0}
is defined by

\displaystyle u_{0}=\frac{M}{M_{*}}U

(69)

also satisfies initial condition (
6
) and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M
. Some computations show that

\displaystyle F(u_{0})=F\left(\frac{M}{M_{*}}U\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{M}{M_{*}}U\right)^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\left(\frac{M}{M_{*}}U\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U^{m}dx\left[\left(\frac{M}{M_{*}}\right)^{m}-\frac{M}{M_{*}}\right]<0.

(70)

By combining (
8
), (
67
) and (
70
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{2}(t)}{dt}=2(d-2s)F(u)\leq 2(d-2s)F(u_{0})<0

(71)

Thus there exists a
T>0
such that
\lim\limits_{t\to T}m_{2}(t)=0
.

Step 2
(Finite time blow-up) It follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}udx

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<R}udx+\int_{|x|\geq R}udx

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\left(R^{d}\right)^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}m_{2}(t).

(72)

Choosing
R=\left(\frac{m_{2}(t)}{C_{3}\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d(r-1)/r+2}}
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}dx\leq c\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{{\frac{2}{d(r-1)/r+2}}}m_{2}(t)^{\frac{d(r-1)/r}{d(r-1)/r+2}},

(73)

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to T}\text{sup}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{d(r-1)/r+2}{2}}}{cm_{2}(t)^{\frac{d(r-1)/r}{2}}}=\infty.

(74)

Thus the proof is completed.

4.2
Global existence

Theorem 9

(Global existence)
.

Under assumption (
6
), if
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M<M_{*}
, then there exists a weak solution to (
1
) on
[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3
, there are
T_{w}
and a weak solution to (
1
) in
[0,T_{w})
with initial data
u_{0}
. We then infer from (
43
) and (
57
) that there exists a
u_{0}
with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=M<M_{*}
, for all
t\in[0,T_{w})
,

\displaystyle F(u_{0})\geq F(u)\geq\frac{C_{*}c_{d,s}}{2}\left(M_{*}^{\frac{2s}{d}}-M^{\frac{2s}{d}}\right)\|u\|_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{m}.

(75)

Therefore, we can deduce that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,~\min(T,T_{w});~L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
for every
T>0
which implies that
T_{w}=\infty
by
Theorem

3
.

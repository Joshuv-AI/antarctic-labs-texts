Staged endovascular management of a ruptured common iliac artery aneurysm with iliocaval fistula and high-output heart failure
Authors: Masaaki Kobayashi, Satoshi Kuroyanagi, Norihiko Hiramatsu, Onichi Furuya, Akinao Miki, Satoshi Oiwa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRupture of a common iliac artery aneurysm into the inferior vena cava is rare and can cause high-output heart failure. We report staged endovascular management in a 59-year-old man with a 70-mm right common iliac artery aneurysm and iliocaval fistula. Emergency embolization of the right internal iliac artery and endovascular repair rapidly improved heart failure (brain natriuretic peptide decreased from 1300 to 60 pg/mL at 1 week). Persistent iliolumbar artery-related type II endoleak with residual venous enhancement was treated by secondary transarterial coil embolization on postoperative day 10. A 31-mm left internal iliac artery aneurysm was subsequently excluded endovascularly. At 3.5 years, the right aneurysm sac had decreased to 35 mm without recurrent fistula or endoleak.Keywords: Common iliac artery aneurysm, Endovascular aneurysm repair, High-output heart failure, Iliocaval fistula, Staged endovascular treatment, Type II endoleak

Spontaneous iliocaval fistula caused by rupture of an iliac artery aneurysm is rare and may present with high-output heart failure rather than hemorrhagic shock.1 Endovascular repair can rapidly reduce arteriovenous shunting while avoiding direct dissection around the venous communication, but residual sac perfusion caused by pelvic collateral arteries can remain problematic.2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 We report staged endovascular management of a ruptured right common iliac artery aneurysm with iliocaval fistula, residual iliolumbar artery-related type II endoleak, and a contralateral internal iliac artery aneurysm, with durable closure and sac shrinkage at 3.5 years. Written informed consent for publication of this case report and accompanying images was obtained from the patient.

Case report

A 59-year-old man initially presented to an outside institution with dyspnea. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a 70-mm right common iliac artery aneurysm complicated by rupture into the inferior vena cava, and the patient was referred to our hospital for treatment. Arterial-phase imaging showed early enhancement of the inferior vena cava, consistent with an iliocaval fistula (Fig 1, A and B). A 31-mm left internal iliac artery aneurysm was also present. The patient had high-output heart failure with a brain natriuretic peptide level of 1300 pg/mL, although his vital signs were stable and there was no free intraperitoneal rupture.

Preoperative computed tomography findings. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a 70-mm right common iliac artery aneurysm with early enhancement of the inferior vena cava during the arterial phase, indicating an iliocaval fistula. A concomitant 31-mm left internal iliac artery aneurysm was also identified.

Emergency endovascular repair was performed under general anesthesia to reduce the high-flow shunt and exclude the ruptured aneurysm. Preoperative computed tomography showed that the available proximal sealing length in the right common iliac artery was only 9 mm. The proximal right common iliac artery diameter was 11 mm, and the right external iliac artery diameter was 10 mm. Because isolated iliac sealing was considered insufficient, proximal extension into the nonaneurysmal distal abdominal aorta was planned from the outset.

The right internal iliac artery was first coil embolized (Fig 2, A and B). A 12-mm × 10-cm EXCLUDER iliac leg endoprosthesis (W. L. Gore & Associates) was deployed from the right common iliac artery to the right external iliac artery to cover the rupture site and fistulous communication. Because proximal aortic extension had already been planned owing to the inadequate right common iliac artery sealing length, angiography was not performed after iliac limb deployment alone. An AFX stent-graft system (Endologix) was then deployed to extend the repair into the distal abdominal aorta. Completion angiography after balloon molding showed no type I or type III endoleak and marked reduction of the iliocaval shunt. A very small type II endoleak from the right iliolumbar artery remained (Fig 2, C and D); because the high-flow shunt had been controlled, the procedure was completed with planned postoperative surveillance.

Initial endovascular repair. Under general anesthesia, coil embolization of the right internal iliac artery was performed, followed by deployment of an EXCLUDER iliac leg endoprosthesis (W. L. Gore & Associates) from the right common iliac artery to the right external iliac artery and placement of an AFX stent-graft system (Endologix). Completion angiography after balloon molding showed marked reduction of the iliocaval shunt without type I or type III endoleak. A very small type II endoleak from the right iliolumbar artery was observed; therefore, the initial procedure was completed with a plan for postoperative computed tomography surveillance and secondary intervention if the endoleak persisted.

Heart failure improved rapidly. The brain natriuretic peptide level decreased to 60 pg/mL at 1 week and 10 pg/mL at 1 month, and symptoms resolved. However, computed tomography on postoperative day 5 showed persistent early enhancement of the inferior vena cava, suggesting residual iliocaval shunting from an iliolumbar artery-related type II endoleak (Fig 3, A and B), despite early shrinkage of the right common iliac aneurysm sac.

Postoperative computed tomography on day 5. Computed tomography showed early shrinkage of the right common iliac aneurysm sac. However, persistent early enhancement of the inferior vena cava remained, suggesting residual iliocaval shunting associated with a type II endoleak from the right iliolumbar artery. Despite this finding, the patient’s high-output heart failure had clinically resolved, and the brain natriuretic peptide level had decreased from 1300 pg/mL preoperatively to 60 pg/mL.

Secondary embolization was performed on postoperative day 10. Through the right common femoral artery, the right deep circumflex iliac artery was selectively catheterized. Via a collateral connection, the catheter was advanced to the right iliolumbar artery. Coil embolization was performed in the right iliolumbar artery (Fig 4, A and B) and within the right common iliac aneurysm sac. Completion angiography confirmed disappearance of the type II endoleak and residual iliocaval shunting (Fig 4, C).

Secondary embolization for residual iliolumbar artery-related type II endoleak. On postoperative day 10, angiography demonstrated persistent type II endoleak supplied by the right iliolumbar artery with residual iliocaval shunting. The right deep circumflex iliac artery was selectively catheterized from the right common femoral artery, and the catheter was advanced through a collateral pathway to the right iliolumbar artery. Coil embolization of the right iliolumbar artery and the right common iliac aneurysm sac was performed. Completion angiography confirmed control of the endoleak and disappearance of residual iliocaval shunting.

Two weeks after the initial operation, endovascular repair was performed for the 31-mm left internal iliac artery aneurysm. This timing was chosen because heart failure had resolved, the right-sided fistula had been controlled, and the patient had bilateral iliac aneurysmal disease. Preservation of left internal iliac outflow was considered because the right internal iliac artery had already been embolized. However, the target posterior division/superior gluteal artery diameter was only 6 mm, raising concern for inadequate sealing, device mismatch, kinking, or branch occlusion. Therefore, the superior and inferior gluteal arteries were coil embolized, and an AFX iliac limb (Endologix) was deployed from the left common iliac artery to the left external iliac artery. Completion angiography showed exclusion without endoleak.

No pelvic ischemic complications, including buttock claudication or colonic ischemia, occurred. At 3.5 years, the patient remained free from heart failure symptoms. Computed tomography showed complete closure of the iliocaval fistula and shrinkage of the right common iliac artery aneurysm sac from 70 mm to 35 mm (Fig 5). No endoleak into the left internal iliac artery aneurysm sac was observed; apparent enhancement adjacent to the treated aneurysm represented opacification of the adjacent common iliac vein because of venous congestion or stasis, not residual sac perfusion.

Long-term follow-up. At 3.5 years after staged endovascular treatment, computed tomography demonstrated progressive shrinkage of the right common iliac artery aneurysm sac from 70 mm to 35 mm, complete exclusion of the iliocaval fistula, and no recurrent endoleak.

Discussion

This case highlights three practical points. First, iliocaval fistula from iliac aneurysm rupture can cause severe high-output heart failure despite stable vital signs.1 Initial endovascular repair allowed rapid reduction of the shunt burden without direct fistula exposure, consistent with previous reports supporting endovascular treatment for major abdominal arteriovenous fistulas in anatomically suitable patients.2, 3, 4

Second, hemodynamic success may precede complete anatomical exclusion. In this patient, heart failure resolved, and the right aneurysm sac was already shrinking, but residual venous enhancement persisted because of an iliolumbar artery-related type II endoleak. Similar residual or recurrent collateral perfusion has been reported after endovascular treatment of iliac aneurysm-related arteriovenous fistulas.5, 6, 7 In standard endovascular aneurysm repair practice, many type II endoleaks resolve spontaneously, and intervention is usually individualized according to persistence, sac behavior, symptoms, and anatomy.8, 9, 10, 11 In the present case, same-hospitalization embolization was selected because residual arteriovenous communication persisted, the responsible collateral was identifiable, and an intra-arterial route through the deep circumflex iliac artery was available. If such a route is unavailable, options include close surveillance for low-flow endoleak with sac regression, transvenous embolization through the fistulous communication, direct sac puncture or translumbar embolization, or open conversion.6,8, 9, 10, 11

Third, contralateral internal iliac artery aneurysm repair after right internal iliac embolization requires careful pelvic perfusion planning. Preservation of at least one internal iliac outflow is preferable when anatomy permits. In this case, branch preservation was not selected because the 6-mm target branch was considered unsuitable for reliable distal sealing. No pelvic ischemic symptoms occurred, but this remains an important limitation.

The most important finding is the long-term outcome. At 3.5 years, the right common iliac artery aneurysm sac had decreased from 70 mm to 35 mm, with no recurrent iliocaval fistula or heart failure. This durable remodeling supports staged endovascular management: urgent shunt reduction, selective completion embolization, and treatment of concomitant iliac aneurysmal disease.

Conclusions

Staged endovascular management can be a useful strategy for ruptured common iliac artery aneurysm with iliocaval fistula and high-output heart failure. In this case, urgent shunt reduction, secondary embolization of residual collateral sac perfusion, and treatment of concomitant iliac aneurysmal disease achieved durable fistula closure and sac shrinkage at 3.5 years.

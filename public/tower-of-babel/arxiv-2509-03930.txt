Cap amplitudes in random matrix models
Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
For general one-matrix models in the large N N limit, we introduce the cap amplitude ψ ⁡ ( b ) \psi(b) as the expansion coefficient of the 1-form y ​ d ​ x ydx on the spectral curve. We find that the dilaton equation for the discrete volume N g , n N_{g,n} of the moduli space of genus- g g Riemann surfaces with n n boundaries is interpreted as gluing the cap amplitude along one of the boundaries. In this process, one of the boundaries is capped and the number of boundaries decreases by one. In a similar manner, the genus- g g free energy F g F_{g} is obtained by gluing the cap amplitude to N g , 1 N_{g,1} .

Introduction

Random matrix models are ubiquitous tools in physics and mathematics.
In particular, the double scaling limit of
matrix models plays an important role
in the study of 2d quantum gravity
Brezin:1990rb
;
Douglas:1989ve
;
Gross:1989vs
.
Recently, in the
seminal paper
Saad:2019lba

it was found that JT gravity is described by
a double-scaled matrix model as well, which opened a new avenue for applying
the matrix model technology to study the holography in quantum gravity.
As shown in
Saad:2019lba
, the correlator of the partition function

Z(\beta)
of JT gravity is constructed by
gluing some basic building blocks,
the trumpet and the Weil-Petersson volume
V_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})

of the moduli space of genus-
g
Riemann surfaces with
n
boundaries
of length
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}~(i=1,\cdots,n)
;
the correlator of
Z(\beta)
is then obtained by integrating over the lengths
b_{i}

of
n
boundaries.

Interestingly,
a similar story holds for the so-called ETH matrix model for DSSYK

However, the geometric meaning of the coefficient
\psi(b)

2
Review of topological recursion

In this section, we will briefly review the topological recursion of
the matrix model in the large
N
limit.
We consider the hermitian one-matrix model

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int dMe^{-N\Tr V(M)},

(6)

where
M
is an
N\times N
hermitian matrix.
In the large

N
limit,
the free energy
\log\mathcal{Z}
admits the genus expansion

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}N^{2-2g}F_{g}.

(7)

We are also interested in the correlator of the
resolvent
\Tr\frac{1}{x-M}
.
It is known that the genus-zero resolvent

\displaystyle R(x)=\left\langle\frac{1}{N}\Tr\frac{1}{x-M}\right\rangle_{g=0}

(8)

satisfies the loop equation (see e.g.
Eynard:2015aea
for a review)

\displaystyle R(x)^{2}-V^{\prime}(x)R(x)+P(x)=0,

(9)

where
P(x)
is given by

\displaystyle P(x)=\left\langle\frac{1}{N}\Tr\frac{V^{\prime}(x)-V^{\prime}(M)}{x-M}\right\rangle_{g=0}.

(10)

From the loop equation (
9
),
we can define the spectral curve for the matrix model

\displaystyle y^{2}=\frac{1}{4}V^{\prime}(x)^{2}-P(x),

(11)

where
y
is given by

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{2}V^{\prime}(x)-R(x).

(12)

The spectral curve (
11
)
is a double cover of the
x
-plane with some number of branch points.
In this paper, we consider the so-called one-cut case,
where
y
has two branch points

x=a,b
with a square-root branch cut
x\in[a,b]

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{2}Q(x)\sqrt{(x-a)(x-b)}.

(13)

Here
Q(x)
is some regular function of
x
.
In the one-cut case, it is convenient to
introduce the Joukowsky map

\displaystyle x(z)=\gamma(z+z^{-1})+\delta.

(14)

In the
z
-coordinate,
the branch points correspond to
z=\pm 1

where
dx(z)
vanishes.
From the condition

\displaystyle x(1)=a,\quad x(-1)=b,

(15)

\gamma
and
\delta
in (
14
) are determined as

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{a-b}{4},\quad\delta=\frac{a+b}{2}.

(16)

It turns out that the Joukowsky map (
14
)
defines a uniformization coordinate
z
for the spectral
curve (
13
).
Using the relation

\displaystyle x-a

\displaystyle=\gamma(z+z^{-1}-2),

(17)

\displaystyle x-b

\displaystyle=\gamma(z+z^{-1}+2),

the square-root in (
13
) is simplified as

\displaystyle\sqrt{(x-a)(x-b)}=\gamma(z-z^{-1}).

(18)

One can see that the two branches of the square-root in (
13
)
are exchanged by sending
z
to its conjugate point
\overline{z}=z^{-1}
,
while
x(z)
is invariant under
z\to\overline{z}

\displaystyle y(\overline{z})=-y(z),\quad x(\overline{z})=x(z).

(19)

By the Joukowsky map (
14
), the cut
[a,b]
on the
x
-plane
is mapped to the unit circle
|z|=1
on the
z
-plane, and
the two sheets of
y
are mapped to the inside
|z|<1
or outside
|z|>1

of the unit circle.

Next, let us consider the connected correlator of the resolvents.
We define the degree
n
symmetric differential

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on the spectral curve by

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Tr\frac{dx(z_{i})}{x(z_{i})-M}\right\rangle_{\text{conn}}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}N^{2-2g-n}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}).

(20)

\omega_{g,n}
can be systematically
computed
by the Eynard-Orantin’s topological recursion
Eynard:2007kz
;
Eynard:2008we

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n+1}(z_{0},J)=\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}K(z_{0},z)\Biggl[

\displaystyle\omega_{g-1,n+2}(z,\overline{z},J)

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{g}\sum^{\prime}_{I\subset J}\omega_{h,1+|I|}(z,I)\omega_{g-h,1+n-|I|}(\overline{z},J\backslash I)\Biggr],

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}.

(22)

Note that
dz_{i}
’s are treated as bosonic variables

\displaystyle dz_{i}dz_{j}=dz_{j}dz_{i}.

(23)

In (
21
), the prime in the summation means that

(h,I)=(0,\emptyset),(g,J)
are excluded
and the recursion kernel
K(z_{0},z)
is given by

\displaystyle K(z_{0},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\int_{\overline{z}}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z,z_{0})}{4y(z)dx(z)}.

(24)

Following
norbury2013polynomials
, we introduce

N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
as the
expansion coefficient of
\omega_{g,n}
around
z_{i}=0~(i=1,\cdots,n)

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}.

(25)

N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
is interpreted as a discrete analogue
of the volume of the moduli space of genus-
g

Riemann surfaces
with
n
boundaries
norbury2013polynomials
.
For the Gaussian matrix model,
it was proved in

norbury2008counting

that
N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
counts the number of lattice points
on the moduli space of curves.
As shown in
norbury2013polynomials
,

N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
is a symmetric polynomial in
b_{i}^{2}~(i=1,\cdots,n)
.
Although
N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})

is only defined for
b_{i}\geq 1

in the original definition (
25
),
there is a natural analytic continuation to
b_{i}=0

since
N_{g,n}
is a polynomial in
b_{i}^{2}
.

As discussed in
Okuyama:2023kdo
, the connected correlator
of the partition function
Z(\beta)=\Tr e^{-\beta M}
can be
constructed by gluing the discrete volume
and the trumpet

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}Z(\beta_{i})\right\rangle_{\text{conn}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}N^{2-2g-n}Z_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}),

(26)

\displaystyle Z_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}N_{g,n}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}Z_{\text{trumpet}}(b_{i},\beta_{i}),

where the trumpet is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\text{trumpet}}(b,\beta)=\oint_{z=0}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z^{b-1}e^{-\beta x(z)}=e^{-\beta\delta}I_{b}(-2\beta\gamma).

(27)

Here
I_{\nu}(x)
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind.

As shown in
Eynard:2007kz
,
\omega_{g,n}

satisfies the so-called dilation equation

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}\Bigl[\Phi(z)\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\Bigr]=(2-2g-n)\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),

(28)

where
\Phi(z)
is the effective potential defined by

\displaystyle\Phi(z)=\int^{z}ydx.

(29)

As a special case of (
28
),
the genus-
g
free energy
F_{g}
is obtained from
\omega_{g,1}

\displaystyle F_{g}=-\frac{1}{2-2g}\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}\Bigl[\Phi(z)\omega_{g,1}(z)\Bigr].

(30)

3
Cap amplitude
\psi(b)

In this section, we introduce the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
and rewrite
the dilaton equation (
28
) in terms of
\psi(b)
and the
discrete volume
N_{g,n}
.

We define the cap amplitude
\psi(b)

as the expansion coefficients of the 1-form
ydx

on the spectral curve

\displaystyle ydx=-\frac{dz}{2z}\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)(z^{b}+z^{-b}).

(31)

From the normalization of
R(x)
in (
8
),

R(x)
behaves in the large
x
limit as

\displaystyle R(x)\to\frac{1}{x}+\mathcal{O}(x^{-2}),\quad(x\to\infty).

(32)

Since
z=0
is mapped to
x=\infty
by the Joukowsky map
(
14
), the residue of
ydx
at
z=0

should be normalized as

\displaystyle\underset{z=0}{\text{Res}}\bigl[\,ydx\,\bigr]=-1.

(33)

This condition fixes
\psi(0)
as

\displaystyle\psi(0)=1.

(34)

Since
ydx
vanishes at
z=\pm 1
,
\psi(b)
should satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)(-1)^{b}=0.

(35)

3.1
Dilaton equation for
N_{g,n}

As discussed in
norbury2013polynomials
,
the dilaton equation (
28
) for
\omega_{g,n}

can be recast as the dilaton equation for
N_{g,n}

by the method of partial integration and contour deformation.
By the partial integration, (
28
)
is rewritten as

\displaystyle(2-2g-n)\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}\Bigl[ydx\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\Bigr].

(36)

We also perform the following contour deformation on the
z
-plane:

1
0
-1
C_{1}
C_{-1}
=
1
-1
0
-C_{0}
C_{\infty}

(37)

Here
C_{\pm 1},C_{0}
surround
z=\pm 1,0
counter-clockwise,
and
-C_{0}
is the orientation-reversal of
C_{0}
.

C_{\infty}
is a big counter-clockwise circle in the region
|z|>1

surrounding all
z=\pm 1,0
.
Using this contour deformation, we can rewrite (
36
) as
the residue at
z=0,\infty

\displaystyle(2-2g-n)\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{C_{1}}+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{C_{-1}}\right)\Biggl[ydx\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\Biggr]

(38)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{C_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{C_{0}}\right)\Biggl[ydx\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=0,\infty}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}\Bigl[-ydx\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\Bigr].

From (
25
), the integral
\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}
becomes

\displaystyle\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}N_{g,n+1}(b,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})z^{b}\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}

(39)

\displaystyle+\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}N_{g,n+1}(0,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}.

Similarly, from the relation

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n+1}(\overline{z},z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=-\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),

(40)

the expansion of
\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}
around
z=\infty
is given by

\displaystyle\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}N_{g,n+1}(b,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})z^{-b}\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}

(41)

\displaystyle-\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}N_{g,n+1}(0,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}.

In (
39
) and (
41
),
we fixed the integration constant by the condition
norbury2013polynomials

\displaystyle\int_{\infty}^{0}\omega_{g,n+1}(z,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=\sum_{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}=1}^{\infty}N_{g,n+1}(0,b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}dz_{i}.

(42)

Finally, plugging
ydx
in (
31
) and

\int^{z}\omega_{g,n+1}
in (
39
), (
41
)
into the last line of (
38
)
and taking the residue at
z=0,\infty
,
we obtain the desired relation (
2
).
The relation (
4
)
between the free energy
F_{g}
and
N_{g,1}
can be proved in a similar manner.

3.2
The eigenvalue density
\rho_{0}(x)
and the potential
V(x)

In this subsection, we will show that the genus-zero eigenvalue density

\rho_{0}(x)
and the
potential
V(x)
are written in terms of the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
.
Since the cut
[a,b]
on the
x
-plane is mapped to the unit circle on the

z
-plane, it is convenient to
parameterize a point on the cut by

z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
.
Note that the branch points
z=1
and
z=-1
correspond to
\theta=0
and

\theta=\pi
, respectively.
Then
x(z)
in (
14
) becomes

\displaystyle x(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=2\gamma\cos\theta+\delta.

(43)

It is well-known that the eigenvalue density
\rho_{0}(x)

is obtained by
taking the discontinuity of the resolvent

R(x)
along the cut.
Since
R(x)
and
y
are related by (
12
),

\rho_{0}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}\Bigl(-ydx\big|_{z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}}\Bigr).

(44)

Plugging (
31
) into (
44
), we find

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\mu(\theta)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

(45)

where
\mu(\theta)
is given by

\displaystyle\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)2\cos(b\theta).

(46)

Thus we arrive at a very simple expression
(
46
) of the
eigenvalue density
\mu(\theta)
in terms of the variable
\theta
.
From (
35
), the density
\mu(\theta)
vanishes at the branch points

\theta=0,\pi
.
Also, from (
34
) the density (
46
) is unit-normalized

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)=1.

(47)

We can also write down a simple expression of the matrix model
potential
V(x)
.
The potential satisfies the following saddle-point equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}V^{\prime}(x)=\mathcal{P}\int_{a}^{b}dx^{\prime}\rho_{0}(x^{\prime})\frac{1}{x-x^{\prime}},

(48)

where
x
is on the cut
[a,b]

and
\mathcal{P}
denotes the principal value.
From (
43
) and (
45
), (
48
) is written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}V^{\prime}(x)=\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\mu(\phi)\frac{1}{2\gamma\cos\theta-2\gamma\cos\phi}.

(49)

Using the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\frac{2\cos(b\phi)}{\cos\theta-\cos\phi}=-\frac{\sin(b\theta)}{\sin\theta}

(50)

we find

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\psi(b)\frac{\sin(b\theta)}{\sin\theta}=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\psi(b)U_{b-1}\left(\frac{x-\delta}{2\gamma}\right),

(51)

where
U_{n}(\cos\theta)=\frac{\sin(n+1)\theta}{\sin\theta}
denotes the Chebyshev
polynomial of the second kind.
By integrating (
51
), we arrive at
the desired expression of
V(x)
in terms of
\psi(b)

\displaystyle V(x)=-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b}\psi(b)2\cos(b\theta)=-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b}\psi(b)2T_{b}\left(\frac{x-\delta}{2\gamma}\right),

(52)

where
T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
denotes
the Chebyshev
polynomial of the first kind.

Note that (
46
) and (
52
) have been
already considered in
Borot:2010tr
(see (3-13) and (3-20)
in
Borot:2010tr
). Their
v_{b}
and our
\psi(b)

are related by

\displaystyle\psi(b)=bv_{b},\quad(b\geq 1).

(53)

Our novel finding is that
\psi(b)

has a geometric interpretation as the cap amplitude
with the boundary length
b
, which naturally appears in the dilaton equation
(
2
).

The constant term
v_{0}
in the potential of
Borot:2010tr

is set to zero in our expression (
52
).
We should stress that our
\psi(0)
is not directly related to
v_{0}
in
Borot:2010tr
;
v_{0}
in
Borot:2010tr

is a constant term in the potential
V(x)
while our
\psi(0)

is the residue of the 1-form
-ydx
at
z=0
.
We cannot determine the value of
\psi(0)
from the
potential since
\psi(0)
does not appear in (
52
).
As we mentioned in section
1
, this is the
reason why we use the 1-form
ydx
to define
the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
.

As an application of our formalism, let us consider the disk partition
function
Z_{\text{disk}}(\beta)
, which corresponds to
(g,n)=(0,1)
in
(
26
).
Z_{\text{disk}}(\beta)

is written as the average of
e^{-\beta x}
with respect to the genus-zero
eigenvalue
density
\rho_{0}(x)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{disk}}(\beta)=\int dx\rho_{0}(x)e^{-\beta x}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)e^{-\beta x(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}.

(54)

Using
\mu(\theta)
in (
46
), we find

\displaystyle Z_{\text{disk}}(\beta)=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)Z_{\text{trumpet}}(b,\beta),

(55)

where the trumpet is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\text{trumpet}}(b,\beta)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}2\cos(b\theta)e^{-\beta x(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}=e^{-\beta\delta}I_{b}(-2\beta\gamma).

(56)

This agrees with (
27
) as expected.

3.3
Moments
M_{k}
and
J_{k}

As discussed in
Ambjorn:1992gw
,
the free energy
F_{g}
is given by some combination of the moments

M_{k}
and
J_{k}
, which appear as the expansion coefficients
of
Q(x)
in (
13
) around the branch points
x=a,b

\displaystyle Q(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}(x-a)^{k-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}(x-b)^{k-1}.

(58)

In this subsection, we will show that

M_{k}
and
J_{k}
are written in terms of the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
.

Using (
17
), (
18
), and

\displaystyle dx(z)=\gamma(z-z^{-1})\frac{dz}{z},

(59)

the 1-form
ydx
is expanded as

\displaystyle ydx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Q(x)\sqrt{(x-a)(x-b)}dx

(60)

\displaystyle=\frac{dz}{2z}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}\gamma^{k+1}(z+z^{-1}-2)^{k-1}(z-z^{-1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{dz}{2z}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\gamma^{k+1}(z+z^{-1}+2)^{k-1}(z-z^{-1})^{2}.

Let us first consider the expansion around
x=a
.
Setting
z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
, the second line of
(
60
) becomes

\displaystyle ydx\big|_{z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}d\theta}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}\gamma^{k+1}(2\cos\theta-2)^{k-1}(-4\sin^{2}\theta).

(61)

On the other hand,
ydx

is also written in terms of the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
.
From (
31
) we find

\displaystyle ydx\big|_{z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}d\theta}{2}\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)2\cos(b\theta).

(62)

Using (
35
), this is further rewritten as

\displaystyle ydx\big|_{z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}d\theta}{2}\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)(2\cos(b\theta)-2)

(63)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}d\theta}{2}\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)\Bigl(-4\sin^{2}\frac{b}{2}\theta\Bigr).

From (
61
) and (
63
)
we arrive at the following relation

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M_{k}\gamma^{k+1}(2\cos\theta-2)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)\frac{\sin^{2}\frac{b}{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}.

(64)

Similarly, by equating the two expression of

ydx
for
z=-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
around
x=b
, we find

\displaystyle J_{k}\gamma^{k+1}(-2\cos\theta+2)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)(-1)^{b}\frac{\sin^{2}\frac{b}{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}.

(65)

Finally, we arrive at the desired relation between the moments
M_{k},J_{k}
and
the cap amplitude
\psi(b)

\displaystyle M_{k}\gamma^{k+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)c_{k}(b),

(66)

\displaystyle J_{k}(-\gamma)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)(-1)^{b}c_{k}(b),

where
c_{k}(b)
is the expansion coefficient of

\frac{\sin^{2}(b\theta/2)}{\sin^{2}\theta}

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}\frac{b}{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}(b)(2\cos\theta-2)^{k-1}.

(67)

From this definition,
we can easily find the first few terms
of
c_{k}(b)

\displaystyle c_{1}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{4},

(68)

\displaystyle c_{2}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}(b^{2}-4)}{2\cdot 4!},

\displaystyle c_{3}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}(b^{2}-4)(b^{2}-17/2)}{2\cdot 6!},

\displaystyle c_{4}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}(b^{2}-4)(b^{2}-8)(b^{2}-16)}{2\cdot 8!}.

Our result (
66
) shows that
the moments
M_{k},J_{k}
are completely
determined by the cap amplitude
\psi(b)
, since
c_{k}(b)
is
obtained from the known function
\frac{\sin^{2}(b\theta/2)}{\sin^{2}\theta}
.

3.4
Discrete volume
N_{g,n}

From the topological recursion
(
21
),
\omega_{g,n}
is determined
by the coefficients of the 1-form
ydx
. Using the expansion
of
ydx
in (
60
),
the resulting
\omega_{g,n}
is expressed in terms of the moments
M_{k},J_{k}
.
For instance,
\omega_{0,3}
is given by
eynard2016counting

\displaystyle\omega_{0,3}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=\frac{1}{2M_{1}\gamma^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{dz_{i}}{(1-z_{i})^{2}}-\frac{1}{2J_{1}\gamma^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{dz_{i}}{(1+z_{i})^{2}}.

(69)

From this result,
we can easily read off the discrete volume
N_{0,3}

(see (
25
) for the definition of
N_{g,n}
)

\displaystyle N_{0,3}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=\frac{1}{2M_{1}\gamma^{2}}+\frac{(-1)^{b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}}{2J_{1}\gamma^{2}}.

(70)

In a similar manner, we find the explicit form of
N_{1,1}
and
N_{1,2}

\displaystyle N_{1,1}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2M_{1}\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{b^{2}-4}{48}-\frac{M_{2}\gamma}{8M_{1}}\right)+\frac{(-1)^{b}}{2J_{1}\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{b^{2}-4}{48}+\frac{J_{2}\gamma}{8J_{1}}\right),

(71)

\displaystyle N_{1,2}(b_{1},b_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(M_{1}\gamma^{2})^{2}}\Biggl[\frac{(b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-2)(b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-10)}{768}+\frac{3(M_{1}-J_{1})}{512J_{1}}+\frac{3\gamma^{2}M_{2}^{2}}{16M_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma J_{2}M_{1}}{128J_{1}^{2}}

(72)

\displaystyle-\frac{(b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-3)\gamma M_{2}+5\gamma^{2}M_{3}}{32M_{1}}\Biggr]+\frac{(-1)^{b_{1}+b_{2}}}{(J_{1}\gamma^{2})^{2}}\Bigl[M_{k}\leftrightarrow(-1)^{k+1}J_{k}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{128}\left(\frac{1}{M_{1}\gamma^{2}}+\frac{(-1)^{b_{1}}}{J_{1}\gamma^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{M_{1}\gamma^{2}}+\frac{(-1)^{b_{2}}}{J_{1}\gamma^{2}}\right).

Using (
35
) and (
66
),
one can check that the above
result of
N_{1,1}
and
N_{1,2}
indeed satisfy the dilaton equation
(
2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\psi(b)N_{1,2}(b,b_{1})=-N_{1,1}(b_{1}).

(73)

3.5
Free energy
F_{g}

In this subsection, we compute the free energy
F_{g}~(g=0,1,2)
for
the general potential
V(x)
.
The relation (
4
) between
F_{g}
and
N_{g,1}
is only
valid for
g\geq 2
,
and hence we discuss the cases of
g=0,1
separately.

3.5.1
Genus-zero

As discussed in
Borot:2010tr
,
the genus-zero free energy
F_{0}
can be expressed in terms of the
cap amplitude
\psi(b)
.
To this end, it is convenient to
change the integration variable
M
as

\displaystyle M\to\gamma M+\delta\mathbbm{1},

(74)

where
\mathbbm{1}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.
Then the
\gamma,\delta
-dependence of
the potential in (
52
) drops out

\displaystyle V_{0}(M)\equiv V(\gamma M+\delta\mathbbm{1})=-\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b}\psi(b)2T_{b}(M/2).

(75)

5
Discussion

In this paper we introduced the cap amplitude
\psi(b)

as the expansion coefficient of the 1-form
ydx
on the spectral curve
(see (
31
)).
The dilaton equation (
2
)
for the discrete volume
N_{g,n}
can be interpreted geometrically as
the operation of closing one of the boundaries by gluing the cap amplitude
along the boundary. In this process,
one of the boundaries is capped and
the number of boundaries decreases by one:

N_{g,n+1}\to N_{g,n}
.
Similarly,
the genus-
g
free energy
F_{g}
is obtained from

N_{g,1}
by gluing the cap amplitude (see (
4
)).
It turns out that the
cap amplitude is the most basic building block of the random matrix model:

N_{g,n}
and
F_{g}
are given by some combination of the moments
M_{k},J_{k}
, which
in turn are constructed from the cap amplitude (see (
66
)).
Therefore,
N_{g,n}
and
F_{g}
are completely determined once we know the
cap amplitude
\psi(b)
, at least in principle.

There are several interesting open questions.
As discussed in
norbury2013polynomials
,
in addition to the dilaton equation, the discrete volume satisfies
the so-called string equation.
It would be interesting to see if the string equation has a simple
expression in terms of the cap amplitude.

In
Kostov:2009nj
;
Kostov:2010nw
, it was shown that the
genus expansion of the matrix model can be obtained by
the CFT technique.
It would be interesting to understand the CFT interpretation of the
cap amplitude.

As an application of our formalism,
it would be interesting to study the quantum cosmology
using the ETH matrix model for DSSYK, along the lines of
Blommaert:2025rgw
.
As discussed in
Okuyama:2025hsd
, de Sitter JT gravity is obtained from
the ETH matrix model by zooming in on the upper edge of the cut.
In this setup, the Hartle-Hawking wavefunction of de Sitter JT gravity
is given by
\langle\Tr e^{\mathrm{i}\ell M}\rangle

Maldacena:2019cbz
, which has a natural
decomposition
(
55
) into the cap amplitude and the trumpet with
\beta=-\mathrm{i}\ell
.
Here
\ell
is interpreted as the size of the future boundary of
the de Sitter space.
As discussed in
Blommaert:2025rgw
,
the picture (
57
) suggests that the cap amplitude
has a natural interpretation as the
Hartle-Hawking no boundary state. It would be interesting to
make this connection more explicit.

On singular fibers of parabolic fibrations
Yiming Zhu

Abstract
. We describe the singular fibers of a parabolic fibration f : X → Y f:X\to Y whose moduli divisor M Y M_{Y} is numerically trivial and whose discriminant divisor B Y B_{Y} is zero.

1.
Introduction

Let
f:S\to C
be an elliptic fibration over a curve. By successively contracting
(-1)
-curves on fibers, we get a birational morphism
\pi:S\to S^{\prime}
to a smooth surface
S^{\prime}
, so that the resulting elliptic fibration
f^{\prime}:S^{\prime}\to C
has no
(-1)
-curves on its fibers. Kodaira’s canonical bundle formula of
f^{\prime}
writes:

\displaystyle K_{S^{\prime}}=f^{\prime*}(K_{C}+L+\sum_{P}(1-{1}/{m_{P}})P),

where the sum runs over all points
P
such that the fiber
f^{\prime*}P
is a multiple fiber of multiplicity
m_{P}
, and
L
is a divisor associated to the line bundle
f^{\prime}_{*}O_{S^{\prime}}(K_{S^{\prime}/C})
. Assume that
\deg L=0
and
\sum_{P}(1-{1}/{m_{P}})P=0
. Then
f^{\prime}
is locally trivial by
[
2
, Chapter 3, Theorem 18.2]
. In particular, every fiber
f^{\prime*}P
is reduced and irreducible. Now, consider the original fibration
f:S\to C
. Write
K_{S}=\pi^{*}K_{S^{\prime}}+B
. Then
B
is an effective divisor that does not dominate
C
. Let
f^{*}P=\sum_{i}a_{i}Q_{i}
be a fiber of
f
, where the
Q_{i}
are prime divisors, and let
f^{*}P\setminus B:=\sum_{i:Q_{i}\not\subset B}a_{i}Q_{i}
. Then
f^{*}P\setminus B
is reduced and irreducible.

In this note, we consider the higher-dimensional case. Following Ueno, we call a fibration parabolic if its general fiber has Kodaira dimension zero. For parabolic fibrations, we have the following canonical bundle formula of Fujino and Mori.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties and let
F
be its general fiber. Let
b=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\dim H^{0}(kK_{F})>0\}
. Then there are
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
B_{Y}
(the discriminant divisor),
M_{Y}
(the moduli divisor), and
B_{X}
such that

\displaystyle bK_{X}=f^{*}b(K_{Y}+M_{Y}+B_{Y})+B_{X},

with the following properties:

(1)

If we write
B_{X}=B_{X}^{+}-B_{X}^{-}
as a difference of two effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors without common components, then
f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor mB_{X}^{+}\rfloor)=O_{Y}
for all positive integers
m
, and
\operatorname{codim}f(B_{X}^{-})\geq 2
.

(2)

B_{Y}=\sum_{P}(1-t_{P})P
, where
t_{P}=\operatorname{lct}(X,-\frac{1}{b}B_{X},f^{*}P)
, the log canonical threshold of
f^{*}P
with respect to the pair
(X,-\frac{1}{b}B_{X})
near the generic point of
P
.

The following is the first main result of this note.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties. Assume that
M_{Y}
is numerically trivial and
B_{Y}=0
. If
X
has a good minimal model
X^{\prime}
over
Y
, and
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
is the induced fibration with general fiber
F^{\prime}
, then there is a finite étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that
X^{\prime}\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
Y^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}
over
Y^{\prime}
.

Without the good minimal model assumption, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties. Assume that
M_{Y}
is numerically trivial and
B_{Y}=0
. Then

(1)

B_{X}
is effective,

(2)

any prime divisor
Q
on
X
with
\operatorname{codim}f(Q)\geq 2
is a component of
B_{X}
, and

(3)

for any prime divisor
P
on
Y
, the divisor
f^{*}P\setminus B_{X}
is reduced and irreducible.

The proof uses the following theorem, which is a consequence of the Clemens–Schmid sequence.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f:X\to C
be a semistable fibration from a smooth projective variety
X
to a smooth projective curve
C
.

Let
\sum_{i}X_{i}
be a singular fiber and let
X_{t_{0}}=f^{-1}(t_{0})
be a general fiber. If
\deg f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X/C})=0
, then
\sum_{i}\dim H^{0}(K_{X_{i}})=\dim H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
.

Parabolic fibrations with
M_{Y}
numerically trivial and
B_{Y}=0
are closely related to Ueno’s conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5

(Ueno’s conjecture K)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of Kodaira dimension zero. Let
f:X\to Y
be the Albanese morphism and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Then

(1)

f
is a fibration,

(2)

\kappa(F)=0
, and

(3)

there is a finite étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
is birational to
Y^{\prime}\times F
over
Y^{\prime}
.

Part
(1)
was proved by Kawamata
[
10
, Theorem 1]
. Part
(2)
follows as a corollary of Cao-Păun’s solution to the Iitaka conjecture in the case where the base is an abelian variety
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let
f
be the parabolic fibration appearing in Conjecture
1.5
, and let
M_{Y}
and
B_{Y}
denote the moduli divisor and the discriminant divisor of
f
, respectively. By
[
9
, Theorem 5.2]
, we have that
M_{Y}
is numerically trivial and
B_{Y}=0
(cf.
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
). Consequently, Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
apply to this fibration.

Notation and conventions

We work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. A surjective projective morphism between normal varieties with connected fibers is called a fibration. A fibration is called parabolic if its general fiber has Kodaira dimension zero.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a fibration between normal varieties. Let
D
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then we can write
D=D^{h}+D^{v}
, where
D^{v}
does not dominate
Y
, and every component of
D^{h}
dominates
Y
. We call
D
a vertical divisor (resp. horizontal divisor) if
D=D^{v}
(resp.
D=D^{h}
). If
D
is vertical, it is called exceptional over
Y
if
\operatorname{codim}f(D)\geq 2
, and very exceptional over
Y
if for any prime divisor
P\subset Y
, there exists a prime divisor
Q\subset X
such that
Q
dominates
P
but
Q\not\subset D
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 2.1

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.15]
)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a fibration between normal varieties. Assume that
Y
is smooth. Let
D
be a vertical effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. If
(f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))^{\wedge}=O_{Y}
(where
\wedge
denotes the reflexive hull) for all sufficiently divisible positive integers
m
, then
D
is very exceptional over
Y
.

Proof.

If
D
is not very exceptional over
Y
, then there is a prime divisor
P\subset Y
, an effective divisor
E
that is exceptional over
Y
, and a positive integer
N
, such that
f^{*}P\leq N(E+D)
. We may assume that
N(E+D)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor. Then
O_{Y}(mP)\subset f_{*}O_{X}(mN(E+D))\subset(f_{*}O_{X}(mND))^{\wedge}=O_{Y}
for all sufficiently divisible positive integers
m
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a fibration between normal varieties with a general fiber
F
. Assume that
Y
is smooth. Let
D_{1}
be an effective horizontal
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
such that
\dim H^{0}(D_{1}|_{F})=1
, let
D_{2}
be an effective very exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, and let
L
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
. Then
f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2}\rfloor)=O_{Y}(\lfloor L\rfloor)
.

Proof.

Let
U\subset Y
be an affine open subset, and let
\phi\in H^{0}(f^{-1}U,f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2})\subset\mathbb{C}(X)
be a section. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{X}(\phi)+f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2}|_{f^{-1}U}\geq 0.

By restricting to
F
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{X}(\phi)+D_{1}|_{F}\geq 0.

Since
\dim H^{0}(D_{1}|_{F})=1
,
\phi|_{F}
is a constant, which means that
\phi=f^{*}\psi
for some
\psi\in\mathbb{C}(Y)
. Then by

\displaystyle f^{*}\operatorname{div}_{Y}(\psi)+f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2}|_{f^{-1}U}\geq 0,

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{Y}(\psi)+L|_{U}\geq 0,

so
\psi\in H^{0}(U,\lfloor L\rfloor)
. Hence
f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2}\rfloor)\subset O_{Y}(\lfloor L\rfloor)
. On the other hand, we have

O_{Y}(\lfloor L\rfloor)=f_{*}O_{X}(f^{*}\lfloor L\rfloor)\subset f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor f^{*}L\rfloor)\subset f_{*}O_{X}(\lfloor f^{*}L+D_{1}+D_{2}\rfloor).

Hence the Lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties. If
M_{Y}\equiv 0
and
B_{Y}=0
, then

(1)

bM_{Y}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor,

(2)

O_{Y}(mbM_{Y})=f_{*}O_{X}(mbK_{X/Y})
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and

(3)

B_{X}
is an effective
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor.

Proof.

Consider the non-zero morphism

f^{*}f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})\to O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})
. We claim that
f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})
is locally free, and if
E
is the effective
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor such that

(2.3.1)

\displaystyle O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})=f^{*}f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})\otimes O_{X}(E)

holds, then the vertical part of
E
is very exceptional over
Y
.

Assume that this claim holds. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we have

f_{*}O_{X}(mbK_{X/Y})=(f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\otimes m}\otimes f_{*}O_{X}(mE)=(f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\otimes m}\text{ for all }m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

By the construction of
M_{Y}
and
B_{Y}
(see
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
), we have
O_{Y}(bM_{Y})=f_{*}O(bK_{X/Y})
,
B_{X}
is just the effective
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor
E
, and the canonical bundle formula is just a rewrite of
2.3.1
.

We now prove the claim. By the construction of
M_{Y}
and
B_{Y}
, there is a positive integer
c
, such that

\displaystyle((f_{*}O_{X}(cbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge})^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=(f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}\text{ for all }m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},

and
O_{Y}(cb(M_{Y}+B_{Y}))=(f_{*}O_{X}(cbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}
. Since
M_{Y}\equiv 0
and
B_{Y}=0
, we have
(f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}\equiv 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Consider the non-zero morphism

\displaystyle((f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge})^{\otimes mc}\to(f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}.

Since
(f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}\equiv 0
and
(f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge}
is pseudo-effective by
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, we have

\displaystyle((f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\wedge})^{\otimes mc}=f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y})^{\wedge}\equiv 0.

By
[
9
, Corollary 4.6]
, the torsion-free sheaves
f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y})
,
f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y})
are in fact locally free. Hence

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y})=(f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\otimes mc}\text{ for all }m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

By
2.3.2
and
2.3.1
, we have

\displaystyle f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y})

\displaystyle=(f_{*}O_{X}(bK_{X/Y}))^{\otimes mc}\otimes f_{*}O_{X}(mcE)=f_{*}O_{X}(mcbK_{X/Y})\otimes f_{*}O_{X}(mcE)

for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Hence
f_{*}O_{X}(mcE)=O_{Y}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the vertical part
E^{v}
of
E
is very exceptional.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a fibration between smooth projective varieties. Let
\pi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a good minimal model of
X
over
Y
and let
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
be the induced fibration. Then for general
y\in Y
, the fiber
X^{\prime}_{y}
has terminal singularities, the rational map
X_{y}\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}_{y}
is
K_{X_{y}}
-non-positive (see
[
3
, Definition 3.6.1]
), and
K_{X^{\prime}_{y}}
is semi-ample.

Proof.

Since
X^{\prime}
has terminal singularities, the general fiber
X^{\prime}_{y}
has terminal singularities. Let
X\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}X^{\prime}
be a common resolution. Write

\displaystyle p^{*}K_{X}=q^{*}K_{X^{\prime}}+E,

where
E
is an effective exceptional over
X^{\prime}
divisor. By restricting to the fiber
X^{\prime}_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle p^{*}K_{X_{y}}=q^{*}K_{X^{\prime}_{y}}+E|_{W_{y}}.

Since
y
is general,
E|_{W_{y}}
is effective. Hence the rational map
X_{y}\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}_{y}
is
K_{X_{y}}
-non-positive.

By the relative semi-ampleness of
K_{X^{\prime}}
, there exists a positive integer
m
such that the morphism
f^{\prime*}f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})\to O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})
is surjective. By restricting to the fiber
X^{\prime}_{y}
, we have the following commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime*}f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime*}f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})|_{X^{\prime}_{y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})|_{X^{\prime}_{y}}}

For a Zariski open subset
U\subset Y
, the map
y\in U\mapsto\dim H^{0}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})=\dim H^{0}(mK_{X_{y}})
is constant, hence
f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})\otimes\mathbb{C}(y)=H^{0}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})
and
f^{\prime*}f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})|_{X^{\prime}_{y}}\cong H^{0}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})\otimes O_{X^{\prime}_{y}}
for general
y\in Y
by Grauert’s theorem. By adjunction, we have
O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}})|_{X^{\prime}_{y}}\cong O_{X^{\prime}_{y}}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})
. Hence the morphism
H^{0}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})\otimes O_{X^{\prime}_{y}}\to O_{X^{\prime}_{y}}(mK_{X^{\prime}_{y}})
is surjective, i.e.,
K_{X^{\prime}_{y}}
is semi-ample.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration. Let
\pi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a good minimal model of
X
over
Y
. Then the parabolic fibrations
f:X\to Y
and
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
have the same moduli and discriminant divisors, and
B_{X^{\prime}}=\pi_{*}B_{X}
. If
B_{X}
is effective, then
\pi
contracts exactly
B_{X}
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is
K_{X}
-negative, we have
f_{*}O_{X}(mK_{X/Y})=f^{\prime}_{*}O_{X^{\prime}}(mK_{X^{\prime}/Y})
for all sufficiently divisible integers
m
. Hence, the sum of moduli and discriminant divisors of
f
and
f^{\prime}
is the same, which implies that
B_{X^{\prime}}=\pi_{*}B_{X}
. For a prime divisor
P\subset Y
,
K_{X}-\frac{1}{b}B_{X}+t_{P}f^{*}P
is numerically trivial over
Y
. Hence the two pairs
(X,-\frac{1}{b}B_{X},t_{P}f^{*}P)
,
(X^{\prime},-\frac{1}{b}B_{X^{\prime}},t_{P}f^{\prime*}P)
are crepant birational to each other. Thus
\operatorname{lct}(X,-\frac{1}{b}B_{X},f^{*}P)=\operatorname{lct}(X^{\prime},-\frac{1}{b}B_{X^{\prime}},f^{\prime*}P)
, so
f
and
f^{\prime}
have the same discriminant and moduli divisors.

From now on, we assume that
B_{X}
is effective. Then
B_{X^{\prime}}=\pi_{*}B_{X}
is effective. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
K_{X^{\prime}_{y}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
since
\kappa(X^{\prime}_{y})=0
. Then
B_{X^{\prime}}
is vertical by
\frac{1}{b}B_{X^{\prime}}|_{X^{\prime}_{y}}=K_{X^{\prime}_{y}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Since the vertical divisor
B_{X^{\prime}}
is very exceptional and semi-ample over
Y
, we have
B_{X^{\prime}}=0

[
13
, Lemma 2.10]
. By
K_{X}=\pi^{*}K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X}
and
\pi
is
K_{X}
-negative,
\pi
contracts exactly the divisor
B_{X}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5
, for the parabolic fibration
f^{\prime}
, we have
M_{Y}\equiv 0
,
B_{Y}=0
, and
B_{X^{\prime}}=0
. Hence, the theorem follows from
[
1
, Theorem 4.7 and Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties. If
M_{Y}\equiv 0
and
B_{Y}=0
, then any
f
-exceptional divisor is contained in
B_{X}
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Lemma 4.2.2]
, there exists a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

where
Y^{\prime}
,
X^{\prime}
are smooth projective varieties,
p,q
are birational morphisms,
f^{\prime}
is a fibration, such that any
f^{\prime}
-exceptional divisor is
p
-exceptional.

Let
M_{Y^{\prime}}
be the moduli divisor of the parabolic fibration
f^{\prime}
.
Write
q^{*}M_{Y}+E_{3}=M_{Y^{\prime}}
for some
q
-exceptional divisor
E_{3}
which is not necessarily effective. Since
M_{Y^{\prime}}
is pseudo-effective and
M_{Y}\equiv 0
,
E_{3}
is pseudo-effective. Hence
E_{3}
is effective by a lemma due to Lazarsfeld (cf.
[
11
, Corollary 13]
).

Write

\displaystyle K_{X^{\prime}}=p^{*}K_{X}+E_{1}\text{ and }K_{Y^{\prime}}=q^{*}K_{Y}+E_{2},

where
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are effective exceptional divisors of the birational morphisms
p:X^{\prime}\to X
and
q:Y^{\prime}\to Y
, respectively. By pulling back the canonical bundle formula for
f
:

\displaystyle K_{X}=f^{*}(K_{Y}+M_{Y})+\frac{1}{b}B_{X},

we have

\displaystyle K_{X^{\prime}}=f^{\prime*}(K_{Y^{\prime}}+M_{Y^{\prime}})+E_{1}-f^{\prime*}(E_{2}+E_{3})+\frac{1}{b}p^{*}B_{X}.

By comparing this formula with the canonical bundle formula for
f^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle K_{X^{\prime}}=f^{\prime*}(K_{Y^{\prime}}+M_{Y^{\prime}}+B_{Y^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{b}B_{X^{\prime}},

we have
f^{\prime*}B_{Y^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{b}B_{X^{\prime}}=E_{1}-f^{\prime*}(E_{2}+E_{3})+\frac{1}{b}p^{*}B_{X}
. Hence all components of
E_{1}-f^{\prime*}(E_{2}+E_{3})+\frac{1}{b}p^{*}B_{X}
with negative coefficient are exceptional over
Y^{\prime}
.

Let
Q\subset X
be a prime divisor exceptional over
Y
, and let
Q^{\prime}
be its strict transform on
X^{\prime}
. Then
Q^{\prime}
is not exceptional over
Y^{\prime}
. Let
P^{\prime}=f^{\prime}(Q^{\prime})
. Then
P^{\prime}
is exceptional over
Y
. Hence
P^{\prime}\subset E_{2}
, so
Q^{\prime}\subset f^{\prime*}E_{2}
. Since
Q^{\prime}
is not exceptional over
Y^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\operatorname{mult}_{Q^{\prime}}(E_{1}-f^{\prime*}(E_{2}+E_{3})+\frac{1}{b}p^{*}B_{X})=\operatorname{mult}_{Q^{\prime}}(-f^{\prime*}(E_{2}+E_{3})+\frac{1}{b}p^{*}B_{X}).

Hence
Q^{\prime}\subset p^{*}B_{X}
and
Q\subset B_{X}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a parabolic fibration between smooth projective varieties. If
M_{Y}\equiv 0
and
B_{Y}=0
, and if
P\subset Y
is a prime divisor, then the divisor
f^{*}P\setminus B_{X}
is reduced and irreducible.

Proof.

Step 1. To prove that
f^{*}P\setminus B_{X}
is reduced and irreducible, we may replace
Y
by the intersection of general hyperplane sections
C=H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{\dim Y-1}
and
X
by
f^{*}H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap f^{*}H_{\dim Y-1}
.

Step 2. Assume that
f
is semistable and that
b:=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\dim H^{0}(kK_{F})>0\}=1
, where
F
is a general fiber. The canonical bundle formula writes
K_{X}=f^{*}(K_{C}+M_{C})+B_{X}
and
\deg f_{*}O(K_{X/C})=\deg M_{C}=0
. Let
Q
be a component of
f^{*}P
. By the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to O_{X}(K_{X})\to O_{X}(K_{X}+Q)\to O_{Q}(K_{Q})\to 0,

we have

\displaystyle 0\to f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X})\to f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X}+Q)\to H^{0}(K_{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}(P)\to R^{1}f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X})

is exact. Since
R^{1}f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X})
is torsion free by
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
, and
H^{0}(K_{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}(P)
is a skyscraper sheaf, the morphism

\displaystyle H^{0}(K_{Q})\otimes\mathbb{C}(P)\to R^{1}f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X})

is zero. Assume that
f^{*}P\setminus B_{X}
is reducible. Then
B^{v}_{X}+Q
is very exceptional. Hence

\displaystyle f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X})=f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X}+Q)=O_{C}(K_{C}+M_{C})

by Lemma
2.2
. Hence
H^{0}(K_{Q})=0
for any component
Q
of
f^{*}P
. On the other hand, by Theorem
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle 1=\dim H^{0}(K_{F})=\sum_{Q\subset f^{*}P}\dim H^{0}(K_{Q})=0,

which is a contradiction. Hence
f^{*}P\setminus B_{X}
is irreducible.

Step 3. In general, let
\pi:W\to X
be the canonical cover of
f
(cf.
[
5
, Remark 2.6]
). Set
g=f\circ\pi
. Then
g:W\to C
is a parabolic fibration whose general fiber
\tilde{F}
has
\dim H^{0}(K_{\tilde{F}})=1
. Let
q:C^{\prime}\to C
be a semistable reduction of
g
, and let
g^{\prime}:W^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
be the induced fibration. We have the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}

Let
M_{C^{\prime}}
and
B_{C^{\prime}}
be the moduli divisor and the discriminant divisor of
g^{\prime}
, respectively. By
[
5
, Corollary 2.5 and Lemma 3.4]
,
M_{C^{\prime}}=q^{*}M_{C}\equiv 0
. Since
g^{\prime}
is semistable, we have
B_{C^{\prime}}=0
. Write the canonical bundle formula of
f
as

\displaystyle K_{X}=f^{*}(K_{C}+M_{C})+\frac{1}{b}B_{X}.

By pulling back along
\pi\circ p
, we have

\displaystyle K_{W^{\prime}}=g^{\prime*}(q^{*}K_{C}+M_{C^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{b}(\pi\circ p)^{*}B_{X}+R,

where
R=K_{W^{\prime}}-(\pi\circ p)^{*}K_{X}
is the ramification divisor of the generically finite morphism
\pi\circ p
.

Fix a point
P^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
with
q(P^{\prime})=P
and
q^{*}P=mP^{\prime}
near
P^{\prime}
. Then near
P^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{W^{\prime}}=g^{\prime*}(K_{C^{\prime}}+M_{C^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{b}(\pi\circ p)^{*}B_{X}+R-(m-1)g^{\prime*}P^{\prime}.

By comparing it with the canonical bundle formula of

\displaystyle K_{W^{\prime}}=g^{\prime*}(K_{C^{\prime}}+M_{C^{\prime}})+B_{W^{\prime}},

we have

\displaystyle B_{W^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{b}(\pi\circ p)^{*}B_{X}+R-(m-1)g^{\prime*}P^{\prime}.

Let

. Then its coefficient in

is

. Let

is irreducible. Hence

is irreducible. It is reduced by

.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We recall mixed Hodge structures that appear in the degeneration of varieties. Our main reference is
[
8
]
, especially Chapter VI.

A weight
m
Hodge structure
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
consists of a finite rank lattice
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
and a decreasing filtration (the Hodge filtration) on
H:=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
:
0\subset F^{m}\subset F^{m-1}\subset\cdots\subset F^{0}=H
such that
H=F^{p}\oplus\overline{F^{m-p+1}}
. For a Hodge structure
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we set
H^{p,q}:={F^{p}}/{F^{p+1}}
(
q=m-p
). A polarization of a Hodge structure
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a bilinear form
Q
on
H
, which is symmetric for
m
even, skew-symmetric for
m
odd, and satisfying
Q(H^{p,q},H^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}})=0
unless
p=q^{\prime}
,
q=p^{\prime}
and
i^{p-q}Q(s,\overline{s})>0
if
0\neq s\in H^{p,q}
.

Let
Y
be a complex manifold. A weight
m
variation of Hodge structure
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
on
Y
consists of a local system of finite rank lattice
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
and a decreasing filtration (the Hodge filtration) of holomorphic subbundles of
H:=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes O_{Y}
:
0\subset F^{m}\subset F^{m-1}\subset\cdots\subset F^{0}=H
, such that at each point of
Y
, the fiber of
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
is a weight
m
Hodge structure, and satisfying Griffiths transversality:
\nabla F^{p}\subset F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{Y}
for all
p
, where
\nabla
is the flat connection on
H
. A polarization
Q
of a variation of Hodge structure
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
is a flat bilinear form
Q
on
H
, such that at each point of
Y
, the fiber of
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is a polarized Hodge structure.

Let
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
be a polarized variation of Hodge structure on a complex manifold. We call
H^{p,q}=F^{p}/F^{p+1}
the Hodge bundles and call the metric on
H^{p,q}
induced by the polarization
Q
the Hodge metric.

Example 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
.

Let
F^{p}=\oplus_{k\geq p}H^{k,m-k}(X)
,
0\leq p\leq m
, where
H^{k,m-k}(X)=H^{m-k}(X,\Omega_{X}^{k})
. Then
(F,H^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\text{torsion})
is a weight
m
Hodge structure.

Let
\omega
be the positive
(1,1)
-form induced by an ample line bundle. Let
P^{m}(X,\mathbb{C}):=H^{m}(X,\mathbb{C})_{\text{prim}}
be the primitive cohomology. Let
P^{m}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}):=H^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cap P^{m}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Let
P^{p,q}(X):=H^{p,q}(X)\cap P^{m}(X,\mathbb{C})
,
p+q=m
,
0\leq p\leq m
. Let
F^{p}=\oplus_{k\geq p}P^{k,m-k}(X)
,
0\leq p\leq m
. Let
Q
be the following bilinear form on
P^{m}(X,\mathbb{C})
:
Q(s,t)=(-1)^{{m(m-1)}/{2}}\int_{X}s\wedge t\wedge\omega^{n-m}
. Then
(F,P^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z}),Q)
is a weight
m
polarized Hodge structure.

Example 4.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a smooth projective morphism between complex manifolds, with fiber dimension
n
. Let
\omega
be the fiberwise positive
(1,1)
-form induced by a relative ample line bundle. Let
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a local system of lattices, let
F
be a filtration of holomorphic subbundles of
H:=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes O_{Y}
, and let
Q
be a flat bilinear form on
H
, such that at each point
y\in Y
,
the fiber of
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is the polarized Hodge structure
(F,P^{m}(X_{y},\mathbb{Z}),Q)
for
(X_{y},\omega|_{X_{y}})
given in the previous example. Then
(F,H_{\mathbb{Z}},Q)
is a weight
m
polarized variation of Hodge structure. We also write this polarized variation of Hodge structure simply as
(R^{m}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{X})_{\text{prim}}
. Note that if
m=n
, then
F^{n}=f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X/Y})
.

A mixed Hodge structure
(F,W,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
consists of a finite rank lattice
H_{\mathbb{Z}}
, an increasing filtration (the weight filtration)
W
on
H_{\mathbb{Q}}:=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, and a decreasing filtration (the Hodge filtration)
F
on
H:=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, such that each
\operatorname{gr}^{W}_{m}:=W_{m}/W_{m-1}
together with the filtration induced by
F
is a Hodge structure of weight
m
. More precisely,
F^{p}\operatorname{gr}_{m}^{W}={F^{p}\cap W_{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}}/{F^{p}\cap W_{m-1}\otimes\mathbb{C}}
. For a mixed Hodge structure
(F,W,H_{\mathbb{Z}})
, we set
I^{p,q}H:=(\operatorname{gr}_{p+q}^{W})^{p,q}=F^{p}\operatorname{gr}_{p+q}^{W}/F^{p+1}\operatorname{gr}_{p+q}^{W}
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta
be a semistable fibration over a disc
\Delta\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
f
is smooth over
\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. Let
n
be the fiber dimension. Fix a smooth fiber
X_{t_{0}}
,
t_{0}\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
and let
X_{0}=\sum_{i}X_{i}
be the central fiber. Then

(1)

there is a mixed Hodge structure on
H^{n}(X_{0}):=H^{n}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})
, which satisfies

(4.2.1)

\displaystyle I^{n,0}H^{n}(X_{0})=\oplus_{i}H^{n,0}(X_{i}),\text{ where }H^{n,0}(X_{i})=H^{0}(K_{X_{i}}),

(2)

there is a mixed Hodge structure
(F_{\infty},W,H^{n}_{\infty})
on
H^{n}(X_{t_{0}}):=H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{C})
, called the limit mixed Hodge structure, and

(3)

(as a corollary of the Clemens–Schmid sequence) there is an isomorphism of vector spaces
[
8
, p.118, third row from the bottom]
:

(4.2.2)

\displaystyle I^{n,0}H^{n}(X_{0})\cong I^{n,0}H^{n}_{\infty}.

Since the proof of Theorem
1.4
depends on the construction of
(F_{\infty},W,H^{n}_{\infty})
, we briefly sketch it here. Since the restriction
X\setminus f^{-1}\{0\}\to\Delta\setminus\{0\}
is a smooth fibration, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Delta\setminus\{0\},t_{0})
acts on
H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Q})
. Let
T
be the automorphism (the monodromy transformation) of
H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Q})
induced by the generator of
\pi_{1}(\Delta\setminus\{0\},t_{0})
. The monodromy theorem
[
14
]
states that
(T-I)^{n+1}=0
. Set

N:=\log T=(T-I)-\frac{1}{2}(T-1)^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{n}(T-I)^{n},

then
N^{n+1}=0
.

Construction of
W
.
Define

\displaystyle W_{0}=\operatorname{Im}N^{n},W_{2n-1}=\ker N^{n},\text{ and }W_{2n}=H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Q}).

Then on
W_{2n-1}/W_{0}
, we have
(N|_{W_{2n-1}/W_{0}})^{n}=0
. We define
W_{1}
,
W_{2n-2}
as subspaces of
H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Q})
such that

\displaystyle W_{1}/W_{0}=\operatorname{Im}(N|_{W_{2n-1}/W_{0}})^{n-1}\text{ and }W_{2n-2}/W_{0}=\ker(N|_{W_{2n-1}/W_{0}})^{n-1}.

Continuing this process, we obtain the filtration

\displaystyle 0\subset W_{0}\subset W_{1}\subset\cdots\subset W_{2n-2}\subset W_{2n-1}\subset W_{2n}=H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Q}).

We claim that

(4.2.3)

\displaystyle\ker N\subset W_{n}.

Let
s\in\ker N
. If
s\in W_{2n-k}
for some
0\leq k\leq n-1
, then by
W_{2n-k-1}/W_{k-1}=\ker(N|_{W_{2n-k}/W_{k-1}})^{n-k}
, we have
s\in W_{2n-k-1}
. Hence, the claim follows by induction on
k
.

Construction of
F_{\infty}
.

Let
\pi:U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{Im}(z)>0\}\to\Delta\setminus\{0\},z\mapsto e^{2\pi iz}
be the universal cover. Fix a point
z_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(t_{0})
. For each
z\in U
, let
\gamma_{z}
be a path from
z
to
z_{0}
, let
F^{\prime}(z)
be the usual Hodge filtration of
H^{n}(X_{\pi(z)},\mathbb{C})
(see Example
4.1
), and let
F(z)
be the parallel transport of
F^{\prime}(z)
along the path
\pi\circ\gamma_{z}
. Then the resulting
F(z)
is a filtration on
H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{C})
and satisfies
F(z+1)=TF(z)
. Note that
e^{-zN}F(z)=e^{-(z+1)N}F(z+1)
. In
[
15
]
, Schmid proved that the limit
\lim_{\text{Im}(z)\to\infty}e^{-zN}F(z)
exists and
F_{\infty}
is defined to be the limit.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
C_{0}\subset C
be the locus where
f
is smooth,
X_{0}:=f^{-1}C_{0}
, and
f_{0}:=f|_{X_{0}}
. Consider the polarized variation of Hodge structure
H_{\mathbb{Z}}=(R^{n}f_{0*}\mathbb{Z}_{X_{0}})_{\text{prim}}
(Example
4.2
), and let
H=H_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes O_{C_{0}}
, where
n
is the fiber dimension. Let
\nabla
be the flat connection on
H
, and let
0\subset F^{n}\subset F^{n-1}\subset\cdots\subset F^{0}=H
be the Hodge filtration. We have Griffiths transversality:
\nabla F^{p}\subset F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{C_{0}}^{1}
for each
p
. Recall that
F^{n}={f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
.
\nabla
induces a
O_{C_{0}}
-linear map
\psi:F^{n}\to(F^{n-1}/F^{n})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C_{0}}
.

Let
\Theta
be the curvature form induced by the Hodge metric
h
on
F^{n}={f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
. Since
f
is semistable, the monodromy transformations of
H
around points of
C\setminus C_{0}
are unipotent by the monodromy theorem. Hence the integral formula (cf.
[
10
, Lemma 21]
) gives

\displaystyle\deg f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X/C})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{C}\text{Tr }\Theta.

Since
\deg f_{*}O_{X}(K_{X/C})=0
and
\Theta
is Griffiths semi-positive, we have
\Theta=0
. By the formula computing
\Theta
:
h(\Theta s,t)=h(\psi s,\psi t)
, where
s
,
t
are smooth sections of
F^{n}
(cf.
[
8
, Chapter II Proposition 4]
), we deduce that
\nabla F^{n}\subset F^{n}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{C_{0}}
, i.e.,
F^{n}={f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
is a flat subbundle of
H
.

Fix a small disc
\Delta\subset C
centered at a point of
C\setminus C_{0}
. We may assume that
t_{0}\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. Let
T\in\operatorname{Aut}H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{C})
be the monodromy transformation around the center of
\Delta
. Since
{f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
is a flat subbundle of
H
,
T
acts on the fiber of
{f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
at
t_{0}
, i.e.,
T
acts on the subspace
H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})\subset H^{n}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{C})
.
Since
T
is unipotent and preserves the Hodge metric on
H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
, we have
T|_{H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})}
is trivial.

Following the notation in the definition of
F^{n}_{\infty}
, for a point
z\in U
,
F^{n}(z)
is the parallel transport of
H^{0}(K_{X_{\pi(z)}})
from
\pi(z)
to
t_{0}
along a path. Since
{f_{0}}_{*}O(K_{X_{0}/C_{0}})
is a flat subbundle of
H
, the parallel transport of
H^{0}(K_{X_{\pi(z)}})
from
\pi(z)
to
t_{0}
is just the space
H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
, i.e., we have
F^{n}(z)=H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
for all
z\in U
. Since
T
acts trivially on
H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
, we have

\displaystyle F^{n}_{\infty}=\lim_{\text{Im}(z)\to\infty}e^{-zN}F^{n}(z)=\lim_{\text{Im}(z)\to\infty}e^{-zN}H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})=H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}}).

Hence
F^{n}_{\infty}\subset\ker N
. By
4.2.3
, we have
F^{n}_{\infty}\subset W_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Hence
I^{n,i}H^{n}_{\infty}={F^{n}_{\infty}\cap W_{n+i}\otimes\mathbb{C}}/{F^{n}_{\infty}\cap W_{n+i-1}\otimes\mathbb{C}}=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Hence
\dim F^{n}_{\infty}=\sum_{i}\dim I^{n,i}H^{n}_{\infty}=\dim I^{n,0}H^{n}_{\infty}
and
\dim I^{n,0}H^{n}(X_{0})=\dim I^{n,0}H^{n}_{\infty}=\dim F^{n}_{\infty}=\dim H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}})
, where the first equality follows from
4.2.2
. By
4.2.1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\dim H^{0}(K_{X_{i}})=\dim H^{0}(K_{X_{t_{0}}}).

∎

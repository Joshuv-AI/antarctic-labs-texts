Abstract
. Let Ω ⊂ ℝ n + 1 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , be a bounded open set satisfying the interior corkscrew condition with a uniformly n n -rectifiable boundary but without any connectivity assumptions. We establish the estimate ‖ ∂ ν u f ‖ ℳ ≲ ‖ ∇ H f ‖ L 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) , for all f ∈ Lip ⁡ ( ∂ Ω ) \|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}({\partial\Omega})},\quad\mbox{for all $f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})$} where u f u_{f} is the solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data f f , ∂ ν u f \partial_{\nu}u_{f} is the normal derivative of u f u_{f} at the boundary in the weak sense, ∥ ⋅ ∥ ℳ \|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}} denotes the total variation norm and ∇ H f \nabla_{H}f is the Hajłasz-Sobolev gradient of f f . Conversely, if Ω ⊂ ℝ n + 1 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} is a corkscrew domain with n n -Ahlfors regular boundary and the previous inequality holds for solutions to the Dirichlet problem on Ω \Omega , then ∂ Ω {\partial\Omega} must satisfy the weak-no-boxes condition introduced by David and Semmes. Hence, in the planar case, the one-sided Rellich inequality characterizes the uniform rectifiability of ∂ Ω {\partial\Omega} . We also show solvability of the regularity problem in weak L 1 L^{1} for bounded corkscrew domains with a uniformly n n -rectifiable boundary, that is ‖ N ⁡ ( ∇ u f ) ‖ L 1 , ∞ ​ ( ∂ Ω ) ≲ ‖ ∇ H f ‖ L 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) , for all f ∈ Lip ⁡ ( ∂ Ω ) \|N(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}({\partial\Omega})},\quad\mbox{for all $f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})$} where N N is the nontangential maximal operator. As an application of our results, we prove that for general elliptic operators, the solvability of the Dirichlet problem does not imply the solvability of the regularity problem.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study an
L^{1}
one-sided Rellich inequality in bounded corkscrew domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary.
Rellich inequalities, which relate the normal derivative and the tangential derivative of a harmonic function, have been widely used to study the Dirichlet, regularity and Neumann problems in Lipschitz domains (pioneered by D. Jerison and C.Kenig in
[
JK1
]
) and, more recently, in domains with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary (see the work of M. Mourgoglou and X. Tolsa
[
MT1
]
where an
L^{p}
version is proved for
p>1
).
In the present work, we investigate an endpoint version of the one-sided Rellich inequalities:

\|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{p}({\partial\Omega})},\quad\mbox{for all $f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})$},

proved in
[
MT1
]
. Here
u_{f}
denotes the solution to the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian on
\Omega
,
\partial_{\nu}u_{f}
is its normal derivative understood in a weak sense and
\nabla_{H}f
is the Hajłasz gradient of the boundary data
f
.
However the version we consider is qualitatively different from the ones in

[
MT1
]
. Their inequalities are closely tied to the weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
properties of the harmonic measure of the domain whereas ours is not. In particular, our inequality holds without any weak connectivity assumptions on the domain, in contrast to the
L^{p}
case that requires the
weak local John
condition (the full geometric characterization of the weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
properties of the harmonic measure was proved by J. Azzam, S. Hofmann, J.M. Martell, M. Mourgoglou and X. Tolsa in
[
AHMMT
]
).
On the other hand, we show that the validity of our inequality for all Lispchitz functions on the boundary of the domain implies a certain geometric property of the boundary called the
weak-no-boxes property
.
In the special case of dimension
n+1=2
, David and Semmes
[
DS2
]
proved that this weak-no-boxes property is equivalent to uniform
1
-rectifiability of the boundary. Hence, in this case, the inequality characterizes uniform rectifiability. The
L^{p}
case of the inequality also implies uniform rectifiability of the boundary, although it is not equivalent to it (see the results of S. Hofmann, P. Le, J.M. Martell and K. Nyström
[
HLMN
]
and M. Mourgoglou and X. Tolsa
[
MT2
]
). In our setting, the failure of the inequality in non-uniformly rectifiable domains is due to the presence of Lipschitz functions on the boundary that behave badly at too many scales, a phenomenon related to the so-called WALA property introduced by G. David and S. Semmes in
[
DS2
, Definition 2.47]
. At least, on the surface this mechanism appears to be quite different to the ones used to prove that the
L^{p}
versions imply uniform rectifiability.

The Rellich inequality we consider can also be understood as an endpoint version of the regularity problem. Closely related to this, we establish a weak
L^{1}
estimate for the regularity problem of the form

\|N(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}({\partial\Omega})},\quad\mbox{for all $f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})$},

which again only requires uniform
n
-rectifiability of the boundary but no connectivity assumptions on the domain. This contrasts with the
L^{1}
endpoint version studied by M. Mourgoglou, X.Tolsa and the author in
[
GMT
]
which should be understood as a Hardy space
H^{1}-L^{1}
result. Our
H^{1}-L^{1,\infty}
estimate serves as a suitable starting point for extrapolating the solvability of the regularity problem and hence implies solvability in
L^{p}
for
1-\epsilon<p<1
. Conversely, we show that the solvability of the regularity problem in weak
L^{1}
is nontrivial, as it fails in the complement of the
4
-corners Cantor set.
This result is not only interesting in its own right, but it also allows us to prove that the Dirichlet problem and the regularity problem are not equivalent in general domains. This follows by invoking the specific construction of G. David and S. Mayboroda in
[
DM
]
of an elliptic operator in the
4
-corners Cantor set such that its elliptic measure is comparable to the surface measure. The author thinks these results may provide further insight on the relationship between the regularity problem and the Dirichlet problem for general operators.

1.1.
Definitions

We introduce some definitions and notations.
Let
X
be a measure space equipped with a measure
\mu
. The space weak
L^{1}
is the space of measurable functions satisfying

\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\,\sigma(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\lambda\})<+\infty

and we denote it by
L^{1,\infty}(X)
.
The
L^{1,\infty}
quasi-norm of a function
f
is

\|f\|_{L^{1,\infty}}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\sigma(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\lambda\})

(note that it is not a norm since the triangle inequality does not hold).

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is called
n
-
rectifiable
if there are Lipschitz maps

f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
, such that

\mathcal{H}^{n}\biggl(E\setminus\bigcup_{i}f_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\biggr)=0,

where
\mathcal{H}^{n}
stands for the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. We will assume
\mathcal{H}^{n}
to be normalized so that it coincides with
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

All measures in this paper are assumed to be Radon measures.
A measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is called

n
-
Ahlfors

regular
if there exists some
constant
C_{0}>0
such that

C_{0}^{-1}r^{n}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{0}\,r^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $0<r\leq\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$.}

The measure
\mu
is
uniformly
n
-rectifiable
if it is

n
-Ahlfors regular and
there exist constants
\theta,M>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and all
0<r\leq\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)

there is a Lipschitz mapping
g
from the ball
B_{n}(0,r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|g|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq M
such that

\mu(B(x,r)\cap g(B_{n}(0,r)))\geq\theta r^{n}

where
|g|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
is the Lipschitz seminorm of
g
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular if
\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{E}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular.
A set
G\subset E\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
E

n
-Ahlfors regular is Carleson if there exists
C>0
such that for all
x\in E
and
0<t<\operatorname{diam}E
, we have
\int_{E\cap B(x,t)}\int_{0}^{t}\chi_{G}(x,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{E}\leq Ct^{n}
.

Also,

E
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable if
\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{E}
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable.

The notion of uniform rectifiability should be considered a quantitative version of rectifiability. It was introduced in the pioneering works
[
DS1
]
and
[
DS2
]
of David and Semmes, who were seeking a good geometric framework under which all singular integrals with odd and sufficiently smooth kernels are bounded in
L^{2}
.

Following
[
JK2
]
, we say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfies the
corkscrew condition
, or that it is a corkscrew open set or domain if
there exists some
c>0
such that for all

x\in{\partial\Omega}
and all
r\in(0,2\operatorname{diam}(\Omega))
there exists a ball
B\subset B(x,r)\cap\Omega
such that

r(B)\geq c\,r
. We say that
\Omega
is
two-sided corkscrew
if both
\Omega
and
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
satisfy the corkscrew condition.

If
X
is a metric space, given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, any continuous
\gamma:I\to X
is called
path
. A path of finite length is called
rectifiable path
.

Following
[
HMT
]
, we say that the open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfies the
local John condition
if there is
\theta\in(0,1)

such that the following holds: For all
x\in{\partial\Omega}
and
r\in(0,2\operatorname{diam}(\Omega))

there is
y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega
such that
B(y,\theta r)\subset\Omega
with the property that for all

z\in B(x,r)\cap{\partial\Omega}
one can find a rectifiable path
\gamma_{z}:[0,1]\to\overline{\Omega}
with length
at most
\theta^{-1}|x-y|
such that

\gamma_{z}(0)=z,\qquad\gamma_{z}(1)=y,\qquad{\operatorname{dist}}(\gamma_{z}(t),{\partial\Omega})\geq\theta\,|\gamma_{z}(t)-z|\quad\mbox{for all $t\in[0,1].$}

An open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is said to satisfy the
weak local John condition
if there are
\lambda,\theta\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda\geq 2
such that the following holds: For every
x\in\Omega
there is a Borel subset
F\subset B(x,\Lambda{\operatorname{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega))\cap\partial\Omega)
with
\sigma(F)\geq\lambda\sigma(B(x,\Lambda{\operatorname{dist}}(x,\partial\Omega))\cap\partial\Omega)
such that for every
z\in F
one can find a rectifiable path
\gamma_{z}:[0,1]\rightarrow\bar{\Omega}
with length at most
\theta^{-1}|x-z|
such that

\quad\gamma_{z}(0)=z,\quad\gamma_{z}(1)=x,\quad{\operatorname{dist}}\left(\gamma_{z}(t),\partial\Omega\right)\geq\theta\left|\gamma_{z}(t)-z\right|\quad\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,1].

Given an
n
-Ahlfors regular set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and a surface ball
\Delta_{0}=B_{0}\cap E
, we say that a Borel measure
\mu
defined on
E
belongs to
weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}(\Delta_{0})
if there are positive constants
C
and
\theta
such that for each surface ball
\Delta=B(x,r)\cap E
with
B(x,2r)\subseteq B_{0}
, we have

(1.1)

\mu(F)\leq C\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n}(F)}{\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Delta)}\right)^{\theta}\mu(2\Delta),\quad\mbox{for every Borel set $F\subset\Delta$.}

Let us now turn our attention to Sobolev spaces. Let
X
be a metric space equipped with a doubling measure
\sigma
, that is, there exists a uniform constant
C_{\sigma}\geq 1
such that
\sigma(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\sigma}\sigma(B(x,r))
, for all
x\in X
and
r>0
.
For a function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that a non-negative function
\nabla_{H}f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a
Hajłasz gradient
of
f
if

(1.2)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|x-y|\left(\nabla_{H}f(x)+\nabla_{H}f(y)\right)\quad\mbox{for $\sigma$-a.e. $x,y\in X$,}

and we denote the collection of all Hajłasz gradients of
f
by
D(f)
. Note that the Hajłasz gradient of a function is not uniquely defined.

For
p\in(0,+\infty]
, we define the
homogeneous Hajłasz-Sobolev
space
\dot{M}^{1,p}(X)
as the space of functions
f
that have a Hajłasz gradient
\nabla_{H}f\in D(f)\cap L^{p}(X)
, and the
inhomogeneous Hajłasz-Sobolev
space
M^{1,p}(X)
as
M^{1,p}(X):=\dot{M}^{1,p}(X)\cap L^{p}(X)
. These spaces were introduced by Hajłasz in
[
Haj
]
.
We define the following norm and seminorm (quasinorm if
p<1
)

(1.3)

\|f\|_{M^{1,p}}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\inf_{g\in D(f)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)},

(1.4)

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}}:=\inf_{g\in D(f)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

We will often abuse the notation and write
\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{p}}
to denote
\inf_{g\in D(f)}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)}
.
We also remark that in the Euclidean case,
M^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
coincides with a Hardy-Sobolev space. For more details, see
[
GMT
]
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set and set
\sigma:=\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
to be its surface measure. For
\alpha>0
and
x\in{\partial\Omega}
, we define the
cone with vertex
x
and aperture
\alpha>0
by

(1.5)

\gamma_{\alpha}(x)=\{y\in\Omega:|x-y|<(1+\alpha){\operatorname{dist}}(y,{\partial\Omega})\},

the
R
-truncated cone with vertex
x
and aperture
\alpha
by
\gamma_{\alpha,R}(x)=\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\cap B(x,R)
, and the
non-tangential maximal operator
of a measurable function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

(1.6)

N_{\alpha}(u)(x):=\sup_{y\in\gamma_{\alpha}(x)}|u(y)|,\,\,x\in{\partial\Omega}.

We also may define the
truncated non-tangential maximal operator

N_{\alpha,R}
analogously.
If
{\partial\Omega}
is Ahlfors regular, then
\|N_{\alpha}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\approx_{\alpha,\beta}\|N_{\beta}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}
for all
\alpha,\beta>0
and so, from now on, we will only write
N
dropping the dependence on the aperture (see
[
HMT
, Proposition 2.2]
for a proof of this fact).
Following
[
KP1
]
, we also introduce the
modified non-tangential maximal function
for any function
u
in
L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}({\partial\Omega})
as

(1.7)

\widetilde{N}_{\alpha,c}u(x):=\sup_{y\in\gamma_{\alpha}(x)}\left(\fint_{B\left(y,c{\operatorname{dist}}(x,{\partial\Omega})\right)}|u(z)|^{2}\,dz\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in{\partial\Omega},

and, similarly, the
truncated modified non-tangential maximal function

\widetilde{N}_{\alpha,c,R}
. Again, we will omit the dependence on the aperture.

In some parts of the sequel we will consider divergence form elliptic operators
\mathcal{L}:=\operatorname{div}A\nabla
where
A
is always a real valued, not necessarily symmetric
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix verifying the strong ellipticity conditions

(1.8)

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n+1}A_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\quad\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda},\quad x\in\Omega,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the
ellipticity constant
of
\mathcal{L}
. We will denote by
\mathcal{L}^{*}
the operator associated to the transpose matrix
A^{*}
.

We are now ready to state the definitions of solvability of the
L^{p}
-Dirichlet and the
M^{1,p}
-regularity problem in
\Omega
(with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary) for
p\in(0,+\infty)
:

•

In a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we say that
the Dirichlet problem is solvable in
L^{p}
for the operator
\mathcal{L}
(we write
(D^{\mathcal{L}}_{p})
is solvable) if there exists some constant
C_{D_{p}}>0
such that, for any
f\in C_{c}({\partial\Omega})
, the solution
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of the continuous
Dirichlet problem for
\mathcal{L}
in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
satisfies

(1.9)

\|N(u)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\leq C_{D_{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}.

•

In a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we say that
the regularity problem is solvable in
M^{1,p}
for the operator
\mathcal{L}
(we write
(R^{\mathcal{L}}_{p})
is solvable) if there exists some constant
C_{R_{p}}>0
such that, for any compactly supported Lipschitz function

f:{\partial\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, the solution
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of the continuous
Dirichlet problem for
\mathcal{L}
in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
satisfies

(1.10)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\leq\begin{cases}C_{R_{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\sigma)},\quad\mbox{if $\Omega$ is bounded or ${\partial\Omega}$ is unbounded}\\
C_{R_{p}}\|f\|_{M^{1,p}(\sigma)},\quad\mbox{if $\Omega$ is unbounded and ${\partial\Omega}$ is bounded}.\end{cases}

Here we denoted
M^{1,p}(\sigma)
and
\dot{M}^{1,p}(\sigma)

to be the Hajłasz Sobolev spaces defined on the metric measure space
({\partial\Omega},\sigma)
.

•

In a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we say that
the regularity problem is solvable in weak
L^{1}
for the operator
\mathcal{L}
(we write
(R^{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty})
is solvable) if there exists some constant
C_{R_{p}}>0
such that, for any compactly supported Lipschitz function

f:{\partial\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, the solution
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of the continuous
Dirichlet problem for
\mathcal{L}
in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
satisfies

(1.11)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\leq\begin{cases}C\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,1}(\sigma)},\quad\mbox{if $\Omega$ is bounded or ${\partial\Omega}$ is unbounded}\\
C\|f\|_{M^{1,1}(\sigma)},\quad\mbox{if $\Omega$ is unbounded and ${\partial\Omega}$ is bounded}.\end{cases}

Since we will focus on the harmonic case on most of the sequel, we will omit the operator from the notation when writing
(R_{p})
and
(D_{p^{\prime}})
.

We define the weak normal derivative of a harmonic function
u
in
\Omega
as the functional
F\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})^{*}
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\,dm=F(\phi),

for all
\phi
Lipschitz with compact support in
{\partial\Omega}
and any Lipschitz extension
\widetilde{\phi}
.
Note that this is well defined as if
\widetilde{\phi}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\phi}_{2}
are two different Lipschitz extension of
\phi
to
\Omega
, then its difference satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla(\widetilde{\phi}_{1}-\widetilde{\phi}_{2})\,dm=0

by the harmonicity of
u
.

1.2.
Main results

In the case of bounded domains with uniformly rectifiable boundary, we show the following
one-sided Rellich inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary. Then, for every function
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
, the solution
u_{f}
of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian with boundary data
f
satisfies

(1.12)

\|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}.

Conversely, we have the following free boundary result in terms of the weak-no-boxes condition (WNB) (see Section
5
for the definition):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a corkscrew domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary.
If (
1.12
) holds for all
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
and
u_{f}
solution to the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian on
\Omega
, then
{\partial\Omega}
satisfies the weak-no-boxes condition.

Remark 1.3
.

The WNB (see Section
5
) was first considered by
[
DS2
, page 148]
as a geometric property of
n
-Ahlfors regular sets
E
such that its negation implies that we can find Lipschitz functions on
E
that cannot be well approximated by affine functions at many scales (see
[
DS2
, Definition I.2.47]
or Section
1.3
for the precise definition of the
WALA
-
weak approximation of Lipschitz functions by affine functions
property). A geometric characterization of the WALA is unknown for
n\geq 2
.

In particular, for
1
-Ahlfors regular sets, it is known that the WNB is equivalent to uniform rectifiability (see Theorem
5.4
). Hence, we obtain the following characterization of uniform
1
-rectifiability for this type of domains.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a corkscrew domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary. If (
1.12
) holds uniformly for all
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
and
u_{f}
solution to the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian on
\Omega
, then
{\partial\Omega}
is uniformly
1
-rectifiable.

Remark 1.5
.

It is well known that the
L^{p}
case (for some
p>1
) of (
1.12
) is equivalent to the density of the harmonic measure of the domain being a weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}(\sigma)
weight (see Appendix
A
). There also exists a more “natural” endpoint version of the
L^{p}
one-sided Rellich inequality in the Hardy
H^{1}
space:

\|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{H^{1}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}.

This inequality is also related to the weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}(\sigma)
property for the density of the harmonic measure, hence falling in the same category as the
L^{p}
results.
The present result is of a different nature as it has no relationship to any weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
weight properties for the density of the harmonic measure or with the connectedness of the domain (or with the domain having interior big pieces of chord-arc domains). This difference can also be observed in the free boundary direction of the proof, where the authors of
[
MT2
]
showed that
L^{p}
bounds for the Poisson kernel imply uniform
n
-rectifiability of the boundary using the theory of singular integrals. Here, the implication is related to the lack of regularity of the Lipschitz functions supported in non-uniformly rectifiable sets, which apparently is of a different nature.

We also study the solvability of the regularity problem

(R_{1,\infty}^{\Delta})
in weak
L^{1}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary
{\partial\Omega}
. Then, for every function
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})\cap M^{1,1}({\partial\Omega})
, the solution of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian with boundary data
f
satisfies

\|N(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}.

As a corollary, we obtain
(R_{p}^{\Delta})
solvability for
p<1
for more general domains than in
[
GMT
]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary
{\partial\Omega}
. There exists
\epsilon>0
depending only on
n
and the Ahlfors regularity constants of
{\partial\Omega}
such that
(R_{q}^{\Delta})
is solvable for all
q\in(1-\epsilon,1)
.

Note again that these results are different from the ones in
[
MT1
]
and
[
GMT
]
as these do not require any connectivity on the domain (or weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
properties for the harmonic measure). Summarizing, for a bounded corkscrew domain
\Omega
with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{${\partial\Omega}$ is $n$-UR}\mbox{ and $\Omega$ satisfies weak local John}\iff\omega\in\mbox{weak-}\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\iff(R_{1}^{\Delta})\mbox{ holds,}

\displaystyle\mbox{${\partial\Omega}$ is $n$-UR}\mbox{ and $\Omega$ satisfies weak local John}\not\Leftarrow\mbox{${\partial\Omega}$ is $n$-UR}\implies(R_{1,\infty}^{\Delta})\mbox{ holds.}

Nonetheless, solvability of
(R_{1,\infty}^{\Delta})
is not trivial as we have a counterexample when
{\partial\Omega}
is the
4
-corners Cantor set.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
E
be the
4
-corners Cantor set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Omega=B(0,100)\backslash E
. For every
M>0
and operator
\mathcal{L}
satisfying (
1.8
), there exists a
1
-Lipschitz function
f
on
{\partial\Omega}
such that the solution
u_{f}
to the Dirichlet problem for
\mathcal{L}
satisfies

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\geq M.\quad

In fact, we prove the existence of a counterexample of
(R_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{L}})
for general divergence form elliptic operators
\mathcal{L}
. In turn this allows us to prove that in general domains with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary the Dirichlet problem and the regularity problem are not equivalent.

Proposition 1.9
.

For general elliptic operators
\mathcal{L}
satisfying (
1.8
), the
solvability of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}})
does not imply solvability of the regularity problem
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
(where
p,p^{\prime}>1
are Hölder conjugate exponents).

Note that in the particular case that
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
or
\mathcal{L}
is a Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher operator, then the two problems are equivalent (see
[
MT1
]
and
[
MPT
]
respectively).

As far as the author knows, Proposition
1.9
answers a question of Kenig and Pipher about whether the solvability of Dirichlet problem implies solvability of regularity problem in general domains (see
[
KP2
, Section 3]
).

1.3.
Historical remarks

Let us provide some historical context regarding the solvability of the regularity problem, its relationship with Rellich estimates and the WALA property. For an extensive account of the history of the solvability of the Dirichlet problem in rough domains for the Laplacian, and more general elliptic operators and systems, we refer the reader to the introduction of
[
MPT
]
. For the WALA property, see
[
AMV
]
and the book
[
DS2
]
.

The regularity problem was first solved in
C^{1}
domains for all
p\in(1,+\infty)
by Fabes, Jodeit and Rivière in
[
FJR
]
by proving the invertibility of the layer potential operators using Fredholm theory. In Lipschitz domains, Fredholm theory is not available and new ideas were needed in order to show invertibility of the layer potentials. Verchota
[
Ver
]
used the techniques introduced by Jerison and Kenig
[
JK1
]
to show the solvability of
(R_{p}^{\Delta})
for
p\in(1,2]
. The main tool in
[
JK1
]
, where the solvability of
(D_{2}^{\mathcal{L}})
was proved for elliptic operators in divergence form with smooth and
L^{\infty}
coefficients, was the so-called Rellich inequality. This inequality states that in a Lipschitz domain, the tangential derivative of solution to the Dirichlet problem and its normal derivative are comparable in the sense

\|\nabla_{t}u\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Omega})}\approx\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Omega})}

and was obtained via a clever integration by parts argument.
This inequality also been employed to show the solvability of the Neumann problem in Lipschitz domains for the Laplacian in
[
JK3
]
.

Rellich inequalities have also been used to study the duality between
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
and
(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}})
for complex-valued operators in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
by Hofmann, Kenig, Mayboroda and Pipher in
[
HKMP
]
and for ellyptic systems by Auscher and Mourgoglou in
[
AM
]
.
In bounded corkscrew domains with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary,
[
MT1
]
proved
L^{p}
one-sided Rellich inequalities as an intermediate step in proving the implication
(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\Delta})\implies(R_{p}^{\Delta})
. They were also the first to consider the regularity problem in Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces. It is also remarkable the result of Mourgoglou, Poggi and Tolsa in
[
MPT
]
where they show
(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}})\implies(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
for the class of Dahlberg-Kenig-Pipher operators in bounded corkscrew domains with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary. Their approach also relied on a Rellich type inequality for solutions of a certain Poisson-Dirichlet problem.
Note that for general elliptic operators satisfying (
1.8
), Kenig and Pipher
[
KP1
]
showed that
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})\implies(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}})
for
1<p<\infty
in Lipschitz domains and Mourgoglou and Tolsa
[
MT1
]
proved it in corkscrew domains with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary.

Other recent developments on the regularity problem under varying assumptions include: Dindos and Kirsch
[
DK2
]
on the regularity problem in the Hardy-Sobolev space
HS^{1}
for Lipschitz domains; the author, Mourgoglou and Tolsa
[
GMT
]
on the regularity problem in
M^{1,1}({\partial\Omega})
for rough domains; Dai, Feneuil and Mayboroda
[
DFM
]
on the stability of solvability of the regularity problem under Carleson perturbations of the operator; Dindos, Hofmann and Pipher
[
DHP
]
for an alternative (shorter) proof of the results of
[
MPT
]
in Lipschitz domains, and Feneuil
[
Fen
]
for a different simpler proof of the results of
[
MPT
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. In the setting of parabolic PDEs, the solvability of the
L^{p}
regularity problem has been recently shown by Dindos, Li and Pipher
[
DLP
]
for a certain class of operators in Lipschitz cylinders. The solvability of the Dirichlet and Neumann problems has also been studied in various other geometric settings: by Hofmann, M. Mitrea, and Taylor
[
HMT
]
in
\delta
-regular Semmes-Kenig-Toro domains; by Marín, Martell, D. Mitrea, I. Mitrea, and M. Mitrea
[
MMMMM
]
in two-sided chord-arc domains where the outer unit normal
\nu
has small BMO norm; and by Guillén
[
Gui
]
in a refinement of the
[
MMMMM
]
setting that allows the BMO norm of
\nu
to be large at certain scales

David and Semmes introduced and developed the theory of uniformly rectifiable sets in
[
DS1
,
DS2
]
, formulating a framework under which all “good”
n
-dimensional singular integral operators are bounded in
L^{2}
. One property of uniform rectifiable sets is that they satisfy the
Weak approximation of Lipschitz functions by Affine functions
(WALA), that is, for any
1
-Lipschitz function
f
on a uniformly
n
-rectifiable
E
and
\epsilon>0
, the following holds. Let
\mathcal{G}(\epsilon)\in E\times[0,\operatorname{diam}(E)]\subseteq E\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the set of points
x\in E
and scales
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
such that there exists an affine function
A_{x,r}
with
\|f-A_{x,r}\|_{L^{\infty}({B(x,r)\cap E})}\leq\epsilon r
. Then the set
E\times[0,\operatorname{diam}(E)]\backslash\mathcal{G}(\epsilon)
is Carleson. This property can be regarded as a quantitative version of Rademacher’s theorem on differentiability of Lipschitz functions a.e. on rectifiable sets and, in fact, an analogue of the result of Dorronsoro
[
Dor
]
for Lipschitz functions is true on uniformly rectifiable sets (which is a stronger than the WALA). Azzam, Mourgoglou and Villa
[
AMV
]
extended this result and proved an
L^{p}
version of Dorronsoro’s theorem for Sobolev functions on uniformly
n
-rectifiable sets. Whether the WALA property alone implies uniform
n
-rectifiability in higher dimensions is still an open question. In the one dimensional case, however, it was shown in
[
DS2
]
that
1
-Ahlfors regular sets satisfying the WALA also satisfy the WNB, which is equivalent to uniform
1
-rectifiability of the set.

1.4.
Strategy of the proofs

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.6
, we follow a strategy inspired by the one used in
[
MT1
]
. The uniform rectifiability of the boundary implies that there exists a corona decomposition of the domain
\Omega
in Lipschitz subdomain
\Omega_{R}
where the Dirichlet and regularity problems are uniformly solvable. This allows us to define an “almost” harmonic extension
v_{f}
of the boundary data
f
, that is a function that is a simple Lipschitz extension on
f
on the bad set of the corona decomposition but is harmonic on every Lipschitz subdomain. This extension together with the nice properties of the subdomains is crucial in showing (
1.12
), and then the boundedness of singular integrals on uniformly
n
-rectifiable implies Theorem
1.6
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we show that if the boundary does not satisfy WNB, then for all
C>0
there exists a function for which (
1.12
) with constant
C
fails. The presence of many “boxes” allows us to construct a family
(f_{i})_{i}
of “independent” Lipschitz functions such that linear combinations of
f_{i}
have bounded Lipschitz constant but each
f_{i}
has large
\|\partial_{\nu}f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{M}}
. A priori, the linear combinations of these
\partial_{\nu}f_{i}
can have a lot of cancellation but we circumvent this by taking a sum with random signs. Using Khintchine’s inequality we show, in expectation, that (
1.12
) is not satisfied for a random sum.

Proposition
1.8
is proved with a similar argument to the one used to show Theorem
1.2
, but the weak
L^{1}
boundedness of the maximal nontangential operator requires a more careful analysis that we have only carried out in the complementary of the
4
-corners Cantor set. Specifically, we use that this domain has the very nice property that every dyadic cube of the boundary contains a “box”. Thus the bad functions we construct are uniformly bad at every point and scale.
Finally, Proposition
1.9
uses the construction of
[
DM
]
of an elliptic operator on the complementary of the
4
-corners Cantor set such that its elliptic measure is comparable to surface measure (hence the Dirichlet problem is solvable for all
p>1
). If the regularity problem were solvable for some
p>1
for the adjoint operator, by extrapolation this would imply the solvability of the regularity in weak
L^{1}
, but this contradicts Proposition
1.8
.

2.
Preliminaries

Notation:
We distinguish between
n
-dimensional dyadic cubes of the boundary and
(n+1)
-dimensional cubes inside the domain by using a hat (
\hat{\ }
) over the latter.

2.1.
Dyadic lattice

Given an
n
-Ahlfors-regular measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we consider the dyadic lattice of “cubes” built by David and Semmes in
[
DS2
, Chapter 3 of Part I]
. The properties satisfied by
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
are the following. Assume first, for simplicity, that
\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}\,\mu)=\infty
. Then for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a family
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
of Borel subsets of
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu
(the dyadic cubes of the
j
-th generation) such that:

(a)

each
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
is a partition of
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu
, i.e.,
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}}Q
and
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\varnothing
whenever
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
and
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
;

(b)

if
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
and
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{k}
with
k\leq j
, then either
Q\subset Q^{\prime}
or
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\varnothing
;

(c)

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
, we have
2^{-j}\lesssim\mathrm{diam}(Q)\leq 2^{-j}
and
\mu(Q)\approx 2^{-jn}
;

(d)

there exists
C>0
such that, for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
, and
0<\tau<1
,

\displaystyle\mu\big(\{x\in Q:\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\setminus Q)\leq\tau 2^{-j}\}\big)+

\displaystyle\mu\big(\{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\setminus Q:\mathrm{dist}(x,Q)\leq\tau 2^{-j}\}\big)\leq C\tau^{1/C}2^{-jn}.

This property is usually called the
small boundaries condition
.

We set
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
.

In case that
\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)<\infty
, the families
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
are only defined for
j\geq j_{0}
, with
2^{-j_{0}}\approx\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)
, and the same properties above hold for
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}:=\bigcup_{j\geq j_{0}}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
.

Given a cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{j}
, we say that its side length is
2^{-j}
, and we denote it by
\ell(Q)
. Notice that

\operatorname{diam}(Q)\leq\ell(Q).

For
\lambda>1
, we write

\lambda Q=\left\{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu:\operatorname{dist}(x,Q)\leq(\lambda-1)\ell(Q)\right\}.

2.2.
Whitney decomposition associated to the dyadic decomposition of the boundary

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a corkscrew domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary
{\partial\Omega}
and
\sigma=\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{{\partial\Omega}}
.
We consider the following Whitney decomposition of
\Omega
(assuming
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
): we have a family
\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
of dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with disjoint interiors such that
\bigcup_{\hat{P}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)}\hat{P}=\Omega
, and moreover there are some constants
\Lambda>20
and
D_{0}\geq 1
such that the following holds for every
\hat{P}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
:

(i)

10\hat{P}\subset\Omega
;

(ii)

\Lambda\hat{P}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
;

(iii)

there are at most
D_{0}
cubes
\hat{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
such that
10\hat{P}\cap 10\hat{P}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Further, for such cubes
\hat{P}^{\prime}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\ell(\hat{P}^{\prime})\leq\ell(\hat{P})\leq 2\ell(\hat{P}^{\prime})
.

From the properties (i) and (ii) it is clear that
\text{dist}(\hat{P},\partial\Omega)\approx\ell(\hat{P})
. We assume that the Whitney cubes are small enough so that

\text{diam}(\hat{P})<\frac{1}{20}\,\text{dist}(\hat{P},\partial\Omega).

The arguments to construct a Whitney decomposition satisfying the properties above are standard.

Suppose that
\partial\Omega
is
n
-AD-regular and consider the dyadic lattice
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
defined above. Then, for each Whitney
\hat{P}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
there is some cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
such that
\ell(Q)=\ell(\hat{P})
and
\text{dist}(\hat{P},Q)\approx\ell(Q)
, with the implicit constant depending on the parameters of
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
and on the Whitney decomposition. We denote this by
Q=b(\hat{P})
(“b” stands for “boundary”). Conversely, given
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
, we let

w(Q)=\bigcup_{\hat{P}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega):Q=b(\hat{P})}\hat{P}.

It is immediate to check that
w(Q)
is made up of at most of a uniformly bounded number of cubes
\hat{P}
, but it may happen that
w(Q)=\varnothing
.

2.3.
Layer potentials

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the fundamental solution for the Laplacian.
We denote by
S
the single layer potential associated to the Laplacian

Sf(x)=\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\mathcal{E}(x-y)f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\Omega

and by
D
the double layer potential

Df(x)=\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\nu(y)\cdot\nabla_{y}\mathcal{E}(x-y)f(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\Omega

where
\nu
stands for the measure theoretic outer unit normal of
\Omega
.

These operators have the following relationship with the solutions of the Dirichlet problem:

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 6.1 in [MT])
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary
{\partial\Omega}
. Given
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(\partial\Omega)
, denote by
u
the solution of the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
. Then we have

u(x)={D}(u|_{\partial\Omega})(x)-{S}(\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega})(x)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,

where
{D}
and
{S}
denote the double and single layer potentials for
\Omega
, respectively.

These operators are also intimately related to the Riesz transforms
\mathcal{R}
.
By the results of
[
DS1
]
, we know that when
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is an uniformly
n
-rectifiable set, then the Riesz transforms
\mathcal{R}
are bounded on
L^{2}(E)
. The converse result is also true and is due to
[
NTV
]
. Hence,

Proposition 2.2

(Prop 3.20 in
[
HMT
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary. Then,

\|N(\nabla Sf)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}.

We also have

\|N(\nabla S\mu)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}

for Radon measures
\mu
.

The second part of the proposition is not proved in
[
HMT
]
but the only changes needed in the proof are:
[
HMT
, Prop 3.19]
should be changed by
[
Tol
, Theorem 2.21]
and
[
HMT
, display (2.1.4)]
by
[
Tol
, Theorem 2.5]
.

In a similar manner, we have the boundedness for the gradient of the double layer potential as a consequence of
[
HMT
, Proposition 3.37]
(although there it is only written for
p>1
and in terms of tangential derivatives).

Proposition 2.3

(Prop 3.37 in
[
HMT
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary,
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})\cap M^{1,1}(\sigma)
. Then,

\|N(\partial_{j}Df)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}

for all
j
in
\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
.

Note that the tangential derivatives
\partial_{t,j,k}f
introduced by
[
HMT
]
are bounded above by
\nabla_{H}f

\sigma
-a.e. (see
[
MT1
, Section 2.4]
) which yields the previous Proposition from
[
HMT
, Proposition 3.37]
.

2.4.
Rellich inequality in Lipschitz domains

The following proposition will be necessary in the sequel.

Theorem 2.4

(Rellich inequality in Hardy spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
with boundary value
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
. Then, we have

\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{t}f\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega})}

where
\partial_{\nu}u
is the outer normal derivative of
u
on
{\partial\Omega}
.

This is essentially a consequence of the solvability of the regularity problem
(R_{1}^{\Delta})
on Lipschitz domains (see Theorems 4.3 and 4.12 in
[
DK1
]
).

2.5.
Corona decomposition of the domain
\Omega
in starlike Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constant

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary

.

2.6.
Atomic decompositions of

Let

(Theorem 3.4
[

]
)
.

Let

,

. There exists a constant

, a sequence

-atoms, and a sequence

of real numbers such that

2.7.
Estimates about elliptic measure

Assume that
{\partial\Omega}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular, thus the continuous Dirichlet problem for an elliptic operator

satisfying (

\{\omega^{x}_{\mathcal{L}}\}_{x\in\Omega}
on

so that for each

and each

, the solution

to the continuous Dirichlet problem with data

. We call

(or harmonic measure if

We present two very important lemmas in the study of the elliptic measure

of a domain with

-Ahlfors regular boundary.

Lemma 2.8

(Lemma 2.16 in
[

]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

n
-Ahlfors regular boundary

. Then there exists

, the

-Ahlfors regularity constant of

, and the ellipticity constant of

, such that for any

and

, we have that

, for all

.

Lemma 2.9

(Lemma 2.19 in
[
MPT
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set with

-Ahlfors regular boundary

and

with

\alpha>0

u(x)\lesssim\left(\frac{|x-\xi|}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\sup_{B\cap\Omega}u\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega\cap B$.}

In the case

, that is, every pair of points can be connected with a Harnack chain, elliptic measures have better properties that will be useful in the counterexample in the complementary of the

Theorem 2.10
.

Let

2.8.
Probability lemmas

We introduce some probability results that will be necessary to prove that the one-sided Rellich inequality (

Proposition 2.12

(Khintchine’s Inequality)
.

Let
(\epsilon_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of independent random variables, where each
\epsilon_{n}
takes values
\pm 1
with equal probability. Then, there exists a constant

such that for any sequence of positive numbers

C^{-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\epsilon_{n}\right|\right]\leq C\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proposition 2.13

(Paley-Zygmund inequality)
.

Let

be a nonnegative random variable with finite first and second moments. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z)}{2}\right)\geq c\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(Z^{2})}

where

is an absolute constant independet of

.
In the particular case
Z=|\sum_{n}\epsilon_{n}a_{n}|
, with
(a_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{2}
and
\epsilon_{n}
are independent random variables that take values
\pm 1
with equal probability, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z)}{2}\right)\geq c^{\prime}.

3.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.6

In this section we will prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
following a strategy similar to the one implemented in
[
MT1
]
. We will show that for
f
Lipschitz in
{\partial\Omega}
, the weak normal derivative of the Dirichlet solution
u_{f}
with boundary data
f
is a bounded functional on
C_{c}({\partial\Omega})
. To do so, our main tool will be the Corona Decomposition
2.5
developed by
[
MT1
]
. Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary.
Let
B_{0}
be a ball centered on
{\partial\Omega}
with
r(B_{0})\leq\operatorname{diam}({\partial\Omega})
and
f
a Lipschitz function on
{\partial\Omega}
supported in
B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}
, and partition
\Omega
into
H\cup\bigcup_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\Omega_{R}
using the corona decomposition of Theorem
2.5
.

3.1.
Almost harmonic extension of Lipschitz functions on the boundary

Let
\widetilde{f}
be a Lipschitz extension of
f
to
\overline{\Omega}
satisfying
\widetilde{f}\equiv 0
on
(2B_{0})^{c}\cap\overline{\Omega}
with
\operatorname{Lip}(\widetilde{f})\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)
, which exists by the Whitney extension theorem.
As in
[
MT1
]
, we define the
almost harmonic extension

v=v(f)
of
f
by

v:=\begin{cases}\widetilde{f}&\mbox{in }\overline{\Omega}\backslash\bigcup_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\Omega_{R}\\
v_{R}&\mbox{in each $\Omega_{R}$, for $R\in\operatorname{Top}$,}\end{cases}

where
v_{R}
is the solution of the Dirichlet problem in the Lipschitz subdomain
\Omega_{R}
with boundary data
\widetilde{f}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}
.
Note that

\operatorname{supp}v\subset\left(\overline{\Omega}\cap 2B_{0}\right)\cup\bigcup_{R\in\operatorname{Top},\,\,\Omega_{R}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\Omega_{R}

and

\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)^{2}m(\Omega).

See
[
MT1
, Lemma 4.3]
for a proof of the second statement.

3.2.
Estimates on the almost harmonic extension

Let
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
.
Then, we trivially have

\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm\right|<+\infty.

since both
w
and
v
are in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. We will show the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm\right|\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0})\cdot\|w\|_{L^{\infty}({\partial\Omega})}.

Proof.

We start by recalling that
\operatorname{supp}v\subset 2B_{0}\cup\bigcup_{R\in\operatorname{Top},\,\,\Omega_{R}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\Omega_{R}
.
Hence, we can bound

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top},\,\,\Omega_{R}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega),\,\,Q\cap H\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\int_{\hat{Q}\cap H}\left|(\nabla w,\nabla v)\right|\,dm

where
H
is the part of
\Omega
not covered by the subdomains
\Omega_{R}
.

Using that the domains
\Omega_{R}
are Lipschitz, Green’s formula and that
v
is harmonic inside every subdomain
\Omega_{R}
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm=\int_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}w\,\partial_{\nu_{R}}v\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}

where
\nu_{R}
is the outer unit normal to
{\partial\Omega}_{R}
.
From Theorem
2.4
and the duality
H^{1}
-
BMO
we obtain

\left|\int_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}w\partial_{\nu_{R}}v\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}\right|\lesssim\|\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega}_{R})}\|w\|_{BMO({\partial\Omega}_{R})}

where
\nabla_{t}
stands for tangential derivative on
\Omega_{R}
and we are taking into account that
\nabla_{t}v=\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}
for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in{\partial\Omega}_{R}
.

Now, we want to control

\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\|\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega}_{R})}.

Observe that
|\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\widetilde{f})\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)

\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e
x\in{\partial\Omega}_{R}
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{supp}(\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}})\subset 2B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{R}
and
\int_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}=0
. Hence,
\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{R}}
is a multiple of a (vector-valued) Lipschitz
H^{1}
-atom, and has
H^{1}({\partial\Omega}_{R})
norm at most
C\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\mathcal{H}^{n}(2B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{R})
.
Thus, using that every Whitney cube
\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{W}
intersects at most a bounded number of domains
\Omega_{R}
, that the cubes
\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{W}
that intersect
{\partial\Omega}_{R}
also intersect
H
and hence must satisfy a Carleson packing condition, that is
\sum_{\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{W},\,\hat{Q}\cap H\neq\varnothing}\ell(\hat{Q})^{n}\leq C\sigma(B_{0})
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\|\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega}_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(2B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{R})

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top}}\sum_{\hat{Q}\cap H\neq\varnothing,\hat{Q}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{Q}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{R})\lesssim\sigma(B_{0})\operatorname{Lip}(f).

In the last line, we have also used the
n
-Ahlfors regularity of the boundary of the Lipschitz domains
{\partial\Omega}_{R}
.

Summing up, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top},\,\,\Omega_{R}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}(\nabla w,\nabla v)\,dm\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{R\in\operatorname{Top},\,\,\Omega_{R}\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}\|\nabla_{t}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{1}({\partial\Omega}_{R})}\|w\|_{BMO({\partial\Omega}_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0})

where we have utilized that the
L^{\infty}
norm is larger than the
BMO
norm and the
L^{\infty}
maximum principle for harmonic functions.

Finally, we can control the part of the sum with the Whitney cubes intersecting
H
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{W},\\
\hat{Q}\cap H\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\int_{\hat{Q}\cap H}\left|(\nabla w,\nabla v)\right|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\hat{Q}\cap H\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}m(\hat{Q})\left(\fint_{\hat{Q}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}\left(\fint_{\hat{Q}\cap H}|\nabla\widetilde{f}|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sum_{\hat{Q}\cap H\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}m(\hat{Q})\ell(\hat{Q})^{-1}\left(\fint_{\hat{Q}}|w|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\sum_{\hat{Q}\cap H\cap 2B_{0}\neq\varnothing}m(\hat{Q})\ell(\hat{Q})^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0})

where we have used Caccioppoli’s inequality and the Carleson packing condition for the Whitney cubes
\hat{Q}
that intersect
H\cap 2B_{0}
.
∎

3.3.
Existence of the weak normal derivative as a Radon measure

Recall that we define the weak normal derivative of a harmonic function
u_{f}
in
\Omega
as the functional
\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})^{*}
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{f}\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\,dm=\partial_{\nu}u_{f}(\phi),

for all
\phi
Lipschitz with compact support in
{\partial\Omega}
and any Lipschitz extension
\widetilde{\phi}
.
We aim to show a one-sided Rellich type estimate
of the form

\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{C_{c}({\partial\Omega})^{*}}\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0})

for
u
solution of the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
Lipschitz supported in
B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}
.

Note that the dual of the space of compactly supported continuous functions is precisely, the space of Radon measures on
{\partial\Omega}
with the total variation norm.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary,
B_{0}
be a ball centered on
{\partial\Omega}
, and
f
Lipschitz with support in
B_{0}\cap{\partial\Omega}
. Then,
the weak normal derivative of
u
can be identified with a Radon measure and satisfies

\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{C_{c}({\partial\Omega})^{*}}\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0}).

Proof.

Given
\phi
Lipschitz with compact support on
{\partial\Omega}
, let
w
be the Dirichlet solution with boundary data
\phi
.
Then,

\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u,\nabla(\phi-w))\,dm=0

as
\phi-w
is in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let
v
be the almost harmonic extension of
f
. Then, by the previous lemma, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dm\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla w\,dm\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\nabla w\,dm\right|\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}({\partial\Omega})}\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\sigma(B_{0}).

By Riesz’s representation theorem, there exists a Radon measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}({\partial\Omega})
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dm=\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\phi\,d\mu

with
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\lesssim\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sigma(B_{0})
.
∎

Now, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of the atomic decomposition for the space
M^{1,1}({\partial\Omega})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})\cap M^{1,1}({\partial\Omega})
. Then, by Theorem
2.7
, we can write

f=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}f_{i}

with
f_{i}
Lipschitz atoms and
\sum|\lambda_{i}|\approx\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}}
.
Applying Proposition
3.2
to each
f_{i}
, we obtain

\|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{C_{c}({\partial\Omega})^{*}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}(\sigma)}.

∎

Finally, the proof of Theorem
1.6
results from the boundedness of layer potentials on uniform
n
-rectifiable boundaries.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Given
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})\cap M^{1,1}({\partial\Omega})
, denote by
u
the solution of the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
. Then, using the lemmas in Section
2.3
and Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|N(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|N(\nabla D(u|_{{\partial\Omega}}))\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}+\|N(\nabla S(\partial_{\nu}u|_{{\partial\Omega}}))\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,1}(\sigma)}.

∎

3.4.
Solvability in

Next, we discuss the solvability of the regularity problem for

We begin by proving a localization theorem (

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a corkscrew domain with uniformly
n
-rectifiable boundary

x_{0}\in{\partial\Omega}

\fint_{B(x_{0},R/2)\cap{\partial\Omega}}\widetilde{N}_{R/2}(\nabla u)^{p}\,d\sigma\lesssim\left(\fint_{A(x_{0},R,2R)}{|\nabla u|\,dm}\right)^{p}

where

is the solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data

and
A(x_{0},R,2R)=\Omega\cap B(x_{0},2R)\backslash B(x_{0},R)

Proof.

Let

\left(\fint_{B_{r/2}(x)}|\nabla u(z)|^{2}\,dz\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{Rr}\sup_{t\in 1.05A_{B}}G(x,t)\int_{1.05A_{B}}|\nabla u|\,dy.

[

for example), we have

\sup_{t\in 1.05A_{B}}G(x,t)\lesssim_{p}\left(\fint_{1.1A_{B}}G(x,y)^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}\lesssim\left(\fint_{1.1A_{B}}\left(\frac{\omega^{y}(\Delta_{\xi})}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{n-1}}\right)^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}

for any

.

Furthermore, using the bound
\omega^{y}(\Delta_{\xi})\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{-n}\lesssim M_{c,\sigma}(\omega^{y})(\xi)
where
M_{c}
is the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator,
we get

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x_{0},R/2)\cap\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{N}_{R/2}(\nabla u)(\xi)|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\lesssim\fint_{B(x_{0},R/2)}\fint_{1.1A_{B}}M_{c,\sigma}\omega^{y}|_{B(x_{0},CR)}(\xi)^{p}dyd\sigma(\xi)\cdot\left(\sigma(B(x_{0},R))\fint_{1.1A_{B}}|\nabla u|\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\fint_{1.1A_{B}}\fint_{B(x_{0},R/2)}M_{c,\sigma}\omega^{y}|_{B(x_{0},CR)}(\xi)^{p}d\sigma(\xi)dy\left(\sigma(B(x_{0},R))\fint_{1.1A_{B}}|\nabla u|\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\lesssim\fint_{1.1A_{B}}dy\cdot\sigma(B(x_{0},R/2))^{-p}\left(\sigma(B(x_{0},R))\fint_{1.1A_{B}}|\nabla u|\,dy\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\lesssim\left(\fint_{1.1A_{B}}|\nabla u|\,dy\right)^{p}

where we used Fubini (in the second to third line),

[
Duo
, Lemma 5.16]
) and that

is bounded from the space of Radon measures to

.

∎

Now, the proof of Corollary
1.7

4.1.

We construct the

\hat{Q}_{0}=[0,1]\times[0,1]

1/16

4^{n}

\hat{Q}_{n}^{j}

Write

We can consider the natural dyadic structure
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
on

given by
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{j}=\{\hat{Q}_{j}^{i}\cap E\}_{i=1}^{4^{j}}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\operatorname{div}A\nabla
be an elliptic operator with the matrix
A
satisfying (
1.8
), and
\omega
its associated elliptic measure.
Let
\Omega=B(0,100)\backslash E

{\partial\Omega}
, and
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
be the dyadic structure previously defined on

For

, we define
f_{Q}(x):=\ell(Q)\chi_{Q}(x)
and let

be the solution of the Dirichlet problem on
\Omega
with boundary data

the center of mass of the associated cube

inside

4.2.
Solutions of elliptic PDEs on the complementary of the

-corners Cantor set

In what follows, we will show that for all

, there exists a

-Lipschitz functions

on
{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\geq C

To do so, we will find a family of uniformly Lipschitz functions such that, if we choose a function at random, we expect that this inequality will hold.

We will start by introducing our family of functions and showing that these are uniformly Lipschitz.
We could also use the functions we will define in the following section as
{\partial\Omega}
does not satisfy WNB but we have decided to include a (much easier) construction.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any choice of signs
(\epsilon_{Q})_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}
(each
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{-1,+1\}

f=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}\epsilon_{Q}f_{Q}

Proof.

First, we will extend the functions
f_{Q}
to a Lipschitz function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
x_{Q}
be the center in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the cube
Q
and consider the closed square
\hat{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with center

and sidelength

.
Then, let
\hat{f}_{Q}
be a

bump function that is
\ell(Q)
in
\hat{Q}
and

outside of

and with

. Note that
\operatorname{supp}\nabla\hat{f}_{Q}\subset 1.1\hat{Q}\backslash\hat{Q}

have finite overlap for

, we have

\left\|\sum_{Q}\epsilon_{Q}|\nabla\hat{f}_{Q}|\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 1

for any choice of signs

. Hence, the function

∎

Next, we prove a lemma about the gradient of the functions
u_{Q}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists
0<c<\sqrt{2}/4
such that for all

with

, we have

\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}|\nabla u_{Q}|\,dm\approx\left(\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}|\nabla u_{Q}|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}\approx\frac{\ell(Q)\omega^{p}(R)}{\ell(R)\omega^{p}(Q)}

where
\hat{B}_{R}=B(x_{R},c\ell(R))
and the comparability constants depend on

and
\mathcal{L}
but not on
Q
or

.

Proof.

We have

u_{Q}(x)=\ell(Q)\omega^{x}(Q)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ell(Q)-u_{Q}(x)=\ell(Q)\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q).

Since
\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)
is a positive solution vanishing on

, by boundary Harnack inequality

and Lemma

, we have

\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)\approx\frac{G^{p}(x)}{G^{p}(x_{Q})},\quad\mbox{for }x\in\hat{B}_{R}

and, by Theorem
2.10
, we obtain

\frac{G^{p}(x)}{G^{p}(x_{Q})}\approx\frac{\omega^{p}(R)}{\omega^{p}(Q)},\quad\mbox{for $x\in\hat{B}_{R}$}

with comparability constant depending on
c
.
Using Caccioppoli, we can show

\displaystyle\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}|\nabla u_{Q}|^{2}\,dm=\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}|\nabla(\ell(Q)-u_{Q})|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(R)}\fint_{(1+\epsilon)\hat{B}_{R}}|\ell(Q)-u_{Q}|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell(Q)}{\ell(R)}\frac{\omega^{p}(R)}{\omega^{p}(Q)}\right)^{2}

again with constants depending on
c
.

On the other hand, if we choose
c
close enough to
\sqrt{2}/4
, we can find a small ball
b\subset\hat{B}_{R}
close to
R
with
r(b)\approx\ell(R)
such that

\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)\leq 0.1\,\omega^{x_{R}}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q),\quad\mbox{for $x\in b$}

using Lemma
2.9
(Hölder continuity for solutions). Now by Poincaré’s and Harnack’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\ell(R)\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}|\nabla u_{Q}|\,dm

\displaystyle\gtrsim\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}\left|\ell(Q)-u-\left(\ell(Q)-\fint_{\hat{B}_{R}}u\,dm\right)\right|\,dm

\displaystyle\gtrsim\ell(Q)\fint_{b\cup B(x_{R},r(b))}\left|\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)-\left(\fint_{b\cup B(x_{R},r(b))}\omega^{x}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)\,dm\right)\right|\,dm

\displaystyle\gtrsim\ell(Q)\,\omega^{x_{R}}({\partial\Omega}\backslash Q)

which finishes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Counterexample to
(R_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{L}})

In order to show the counterexample to the solvability of
(R_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{L}})
, we will start by introducing a linearization of the modified nontangential maximal operator
\widetilde{N}
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
let
(Q^{k}_{j})_{j}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
be the set of maximal cubes
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
satisfying

\frac{\omega^{p}(Q)}{\ell(Q)}\frac{\ell(P^{i})}{\omega^{p}(P^{i})}\geq 1,\quad 1\leq i\leq k-1

where
P^{i}
is the
i
-th ancestor of
Q
.
We define the operator
\hat{N}_{k}
for functions
F\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
as follows

\hat{N}_{k}F(\xi):=\begin{cases}\fint_{\hat{B}_{Q^{k}_{j}}}|F|\,dm,&\mbox{if $\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}$ for some $j$},\\
0,&\mbox{ if $\xi\in{\partial\Omega}\backslash\cup_{j}Q^{k}_{j}$}\end{cases}

where
\hat{B}_{Q^{k}_{j}}
are the balls given by Lemma
4.2
.
Note that for any function
F
, we trivially have

\widetilde{N}(F)\geq\hat{N}_{k}(F)

assuming that the nontangential cones in the definition of
\widetilde{N}
are wide enough.

Lemma 4.3
.

The following statements hold:

•

The maximal cubes satisfy
\sigma(E\backslash\cup_{j}Q^{k}_{j})=0
, for any
k
,

•

\widetilde{N}(\mathbb{E}|\nabla u|)(\xi)\geq\mathbb{E}\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)\geq\sqrt{k}
for
\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}
.

Proof.

We will show that

\frac{\sigma(R\cap\cup_{j}Q^{k}_{j})}{\sigma(R)}>c(k)>0,\quad\mbox{ for all $R\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}$,}

which implies the first statement.
Fix
R\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
. By pigeonhole principle, one of its children
R_{1}
belongs to
\cup_{j}Q^{1}_{j}
. In turn, one of the children
R_{2}
of
R_{1}
, belongs to
\cup_{j}Q^{2}_{j}
. Iterating, there is at least one cube
k
generations lower than
R
inside
\cup_{j}Q^{k}_{j}
.

For the second statement, if the cones
\gamma
in the definition of
\widetilde{N}
are wide enough, then
\hat{B}_{R}\subset\gamma(\xi)
for all
\xi\in R
, and the first inequality follows. For the second one, assume
\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\fint_{\hat{B}_{Q^{k}_{j}}}\mathbb{E}|\nabla u|\,dm\approx\fint_{\hat{B}_{Q^{k}_{j}}}\left(\sum_{Q}|\nabla u_{Q}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,dm

\displaystyle\geq\fint_{\hat{B}_{Q^{k}_{j}}}\Bigg(\sum_{Q:Q^{k}_{j}\subset Q,\,\frac{\omega^{p}(Q^{k}_{j})}{\ell(Q^{k}_{j})}\frac{\ell(Q)}{\omega^{p}(Q)}\geq 1}|\nabla u_{Q}|^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}\,dm\gtrsim\sqrt{k}

where we have used Khintchine’s inequality
2.12
, the properties of
Q^{k}_{j}
, Cauchy-Schwarz, and Lemma
4.2
.
∎

The existence of the desired counterexample will follow from the next result.

Proposition 4.4
.

We have

\mathbb{E}\,\|\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\gtrsim\sqrt{k}\sigma(E)

with constants independent of
k
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

By linearity of expectation, we have

\mathbb{E}\,\sigma\left(\left\{\xi\in E:\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)>\lambda\right\}\right)=\sum_{Q^{k}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\,\sigma\left(\left\{\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}:\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)>\lambda\right\}\right),\quad\mbox{for all $\lambda>0$}.

Since the function
\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)
is constant on every
Q^{k}_{j}
, we can fix
\xi^{k}_{j}\in Q^{k}_{j}
and by the Paley-Zygmund inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\sigma\big(\big\{\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}:\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)>\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle[\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)]\big\}\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma(Q^{k}_{j})\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi^{k}_{j})>\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi^{k}_{j})]\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sigma(Q^{k}_{j}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\|\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)\|_{L^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\sum_{Q^{k}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\,\sigma\left(\left\{\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}:\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)(\xi)>\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\sum_{Q^{k}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\,\sigma\left(\left\{\xi\in Q^{k}_{j}:\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)>\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)]\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sqrt{k}\sum_{Q^{k}_{j}}\sigma(Q^{k}_{j})\approx\sqrt{k}\sigma(E)

where we have used that
\mathbb{E}\hat{N}_{k}(|\nabla u|)\geq\sqrt{k}
on
Q^{k}_{j}
.

∎

Since this result is true for every
k
, a uniform bound of the form

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{f})\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{1}}

is not possible, yielding the proof of Proposition
1.8
.

4.4.
(D_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}})
does not imply
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
for general Ahlfors regular domains

In this section, we show that for general operators the Dirichlet problem and the regularity problem are not equivalent.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a corkscrew domain with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary
{\partial\Omega}
. Then, solvability of
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
implies solvability of
(R_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{L}})
.

This is the statement of
[
GMT
, Theorem 1.3]
, noting the trivial inequality

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\leq\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{1}}.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the four corners Cantor set and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash E
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{*}
be an operator on
\Omega
such that its elliptic measure
\omega_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
is comparable to surface measure on
E
. Then,
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
is not solvable for any
p>1
.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that such an operator
\mathcal{L}^{*}
of the form
\operatorname{div}(a\nabla\cdot)
with
C^{-1}\leq a(x)\leq C
exists as shown by David and Mayboroda in
[
DM
]
.

Proof.

Using the characterization of solvability of the Dirichlet problem by reverse Hölder inequalities for the density of the elliptic measure, we have that
(D^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{p^{\prime}})
is solvable for all
p^{\prime}>1
.
On the other hand, by Proposition
1.8
we know that
(R_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{L}})
is not solvable. Hence, by Theorem
4.5
,
(R_{p}^{\mathcal{L}})
is not solvable for any
p\geq 1
.
∎

A nice property of the WNB for

Theorem 5.4

(Proposition 3.45 and 3.50 from

]

A

Unfortunately, the WNB does not characterize uniform rectifiability for

take the product of the

The WNB was introduced in

The failure of the WNB is very useful to construct functions failing the WALA, as the geometry of the boxes is suitable to construct Lipschitz function with special behavior.

In

Remark 5.5
.

Remark 5.6
.

Let

{\operatorname{dist}}(T_{1},T_{2})\gtrsim\pi_{n+1}(z_{2})-\pi_{n+1}(z_{1})

f_{Q}(z_{2})-f_{Q}(z_{1})\gtrsim\pi_{n+1}(z_{2})-\pi_{n+1}(z_{1})
. ∎

The functions
f_{Q}
for
Q\in\mathcal{F}
have the following nice property that will be essential for what follows.

Lemma 5.9
.

For any choice of signs
(\epsilon_{Q})_{Q\in\mathcal{F}},\,\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have that the function
f=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}}\epsilon_{Q}f_{Q}
is
C\epsilon^{-1}
-Lipschitz with constants independent of the particular choice of the family
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Fix
Q\in\mathcal{F}
and, without loss of generality, assume
M_{Q}
is the standard
\epsilon
-box. The set where the function
f_{Q}
has nonzero gradient can be decomposed as

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,:\,\nabla f_{Q}(x)\neq 0\}=G_{Q,1}\cup G_{Q,2}

where

•

G_{Q,1}
is the interior of
M_{Q}
and the inner base and sides intersected with
\{x_{n+1}\leq 9\epsilon\}
minus the convex hull of the middle boundary of each box in
\mathcal{F}_{Q}
, that is

G_{Q,1}=I_{Q}\cup B_{Q,i}\cup(S_{Q,i}\cap\{x_{n+1}\leq 9\epsilon\})\backslash\cup_{Q_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}}\mbox{co}(S_{Q_{j},m}\cup B_{Q_{j},m}),

•

G_{Q,2}
is the middle base and sides of each box in
\mathcal{F}_{Q}
that is not completely contained inside the convex hull of a larger box in
\mathcal{F}_{Q}
, that is,

G_{Q,2}=\bigcup_{Q_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q},\,f_{Q}^{j}\neq f_{Q}^{j-1}}(S_{Q_{j},m}\cup B_{Q_{j},m}).

It is easy to check that for
Q,R\in\mathcal{F}
, the set where both
\nabla f_{Q}
and
\nabla f_{R}
do not vanish is empty. Hence, for any choice of signs
\epsilon_{Q}
, we obtain a uniformly Lipschitz function.
∎

5.4.
Counterexample to one-sided Rellich inequality

For some
\epsilon>0
we assume
\mathcal{B}_{d}(\epsilon)
is not Carleson. By the results in
[

, for every integer

, we can find
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{d}(\epsilon)
(depending on

) and
k
families of subcubes
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
,
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{B}_{d}(\epsilon)
satisfying

(1)

for every cube
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
, there exists a cube
R\in\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
such that
Q\subsetneq R
, for
i=2,\ldots,k
,

(2)

every cube in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is contained inside
Q_{0}
,

(3)

the cubes in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, for
i=1,\ldots,k
,

(4)

\sigma(\cup_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}Q)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sigma(Q_{0})
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\{Q_{0}\}
and consider the construction of
f_{Q}
’s for
Q\in\mathcal{F}
from Section
5.2
. For the sake of contradiction, we also assume that the one-sided Rellich inequality (
1.12
) holds for all
f
Lipschitz. In particular, we suppose
\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{Q}}
is a Radon measure.

Lemma 5.11
.

Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. For a random choice of signs
(\epsilon_{Q})_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}
, let
{f_{j}}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\epsilon_{Q}f_{Q}
. We have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}}|(Q)\right]\gtrsim\sigma(Q_{0})

where
u_{f_{j}}
is the solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f_{j}
and the inequality has constants independent of
j
and
k
.
In particular, for any
j
, there exists a particular choice of signs
(\epsilon_{Q}^{j})_{Q}
such that
f_{j}^{*}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\epsilon_{Q}^{j}f_{Q}
satisfies

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}^{*}}|(Q)\gtrsim\sigma(Q_{0}).

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{f}_{j}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\epsilon_{Q}f_{Q}/\ell(Q)
.
For any choice of signs, using duality and that
\widetilde{f}_{j}
has constant sign in every
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{f_{j}}\nabla u_{\widetilde{f}_{j}}\,dm=\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}}{\widetilde{f}_{j}}\,d\sigma\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}}|(Q)\|{\widetilde{f}_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\approx_{\mu}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}}|(Q).

Using Khintchine’s inequality
2.12
, and Lemma
5.10
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{f_{j}}\nabla u_{\widetilde{f}_{j}}\,dm

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\int_{P_{Q}}\nabla u_{f_{j}}\nabla u_{\widetilde{f}_{j}}\,dm\approx\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\int_{P_{Q}}\left(\sum_{R\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\nabla u_{R}|^{2}\ell(R)^{-1}\right)\,dm

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\int_{P_{Q}}|\nabla u_{Q}|^{2}\ell(Q)^{-1}\,dm\gtrsim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\sigma(Q)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sigma(Q_{0}).

Hence, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}}|(Q)\right]\gtrsim\sigma(Q_{0})

and we can find a particular choice of signs
(\epsilon_{Q}^{j})_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}
such that
f_{j}^{*}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\epsilon_{Q}^{j}f_{Q}
satisfies

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}|\partial_{\nu}u_{f_{j}^{*}}|(Q)\gtrsim\sigma(Q_{0}).

∎

.

Note that

.

does not satisfy WNB. Let

and consider

and

Appendix A
One-sided Rellich inequalities in
L^{p}
imply weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
property for harmonic measure

In this appendix, we show the relationship between the one-sided Rellich inequalities in
L^{q}
and the weak-
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
properties of the harmonic measure of the domain. The converse direction can be found in
[
MT1
]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 2
be a bounded open set with
n
-Ahlfors regular boundary. Assume that for all
f\in\operatorname{Lip}({\partial\Omega})
and
u_{f}
solution to the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian, the one-sided Rellich inequality

\|\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\|_{L^{q}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{q}({\partial\Omega})}

holds for some
q>1
. Then the Dirichlet problem
(D^{\Delta}_{q^{\prime}})
is solvable where
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Proof.

For
p\in\Omega
, let
f_{p}(\xi):=\mathcal{E}(p-\xi)
for
\xi\in{\partial\Omega}
. It is easy to show that

\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{L^{q}}^{q}\lesssim{\operatorname{dist}}(p,{\partial\Omega})^{-n(q-1)}
(see for example the proof of
[
MT1
, Theorem 1.6]
). On the other hand, for
\widetilde{g}\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with compact support and using
[
PT
, Lemma 7.6]
, we have

\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}u_{f}\widetilde{g}\,d\sigma=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{g}\cdot\nabla u_{f}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{g}\left(\nabla_{x}\mathcal{E}(p-x)-\nabla_{x}G(p,x)\right)\,dx

=-\int_{{\partial\Omega}_{*}}\widetilde{g}\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{E}(p-x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{{\partial\Omega}_{*}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{g}d\omega^{p}

where
{\partial\Omega}_{*}
is the measure theoretic boundary of
\Omega
.
Since
\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{E}(p-x)\leq|\nabla\mathcal{E}(p-x)|

\sigma
-a.e. on
{\partial\Omega}_{*}
,
by duality we obtain that the density
\frac{d\omega^{p}}{d\sigma}
exists, is in
L^{q}(\sigma)
, and

\left\|\frac{d\omega^{p}}{d\sigma}\right\|_{L^{q}({\partial\Omega})}\lesssim\|\nabla\mathcal{E}(p-\cdot)\|_{L^{q}({\partial\Omega}_{*})}+\|\nabla_{H}\mathcal{E}(p-\cdot)\|_{L^{q}({\partial\Omega})}\leq{\operatorname{dist}}(p,{\partial\Omega})^{-n(q-1)/q}.

In particular, the density of the harmonic measure satisfies
[
MT1
, Theorem 9.2 (c)]
, which is equivalent to
(D_{q^{\prime}}^{\Delta})
being solvable.
∎

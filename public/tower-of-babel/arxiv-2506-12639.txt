Semi-Blind Channel Estimation for Downlink Communications Based on Dynamic Metasurface Antennas
Amarilton L. Magalhães 1 3 , André L. F. de Almeida 1 , A. Lee Swindlehurst 2

Abstract
Dynamic metasurface antennas (DMAs) are emerging as a promising technology to enable energy-efficient, large array-based multi-antenna systems. This paper presents a simple channel estimation scheme for the downlink of a multiple-input single-output orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (MISO-OFDM) communication system exploiting DMAs. The proposed scheme extracts separate estimates of the wireless channel and the unknown waveguide propagation vector using a simple iterative algorithm based on the parallel factor (PARAFAC) decomposition. Obtaining decoupled estimates of the wireless channel and inner waveguide vector enables the isolation and compensation for its effect when designing the DMA beamformer, regardless of the wireless channel state, which evolves much faster due to its shorter coherence time and bandwidth. Additionally, our solution operates in a data-aided manner, delivering estimates of useful data symbols jointly with channel estimates, without requiring sequential pilot and data stages. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to explore this CE approach. Numerical results corroborate the notable performance of the proposed scheme.



I
Introduction and motivation

Dynamic metasurface antennas (DMAs) have attracted increasing interest in wireless communications as a promising technology for next-generation networks, such as 6G
[
1
]
. DMAs can enable planar, low-cost, energy-efficient, extra-large antenna arrays, since is construction facilitates hybrid analog/digital precoding, by integrating dynamic analog combining capabilities directly into the antenna structure, in contrast to conventional MIMO technologies, which rely on fixed array designs and complex signal processing
[
2
,
1
,
3
,
4
]
. The channel estimation (CE) literature on DMAs is still in its embryonic stage, except for the pioneering article attributed to
[
4
]
, where the authors proposed a CE method utilizing pilot sequences and leveraging the fast switching of DMA elements. However, the authors relied on the knowledge of the channel statistics and assumed perfect knowledge of the electromagnetic field vector, which models the signal propagation inside the DMA microstrips. In a practical scenario, exact knowledge of this vector may not be available due to non-homogeneous behavior of the radiating elements or mismatches between the idealized waveguide model and the actual construction of the DMA.

In this work, we refer to this waveguide propagation vector as the DMA inner channel vector. In this case, teasing out a separate estimation of the outer wireless and the inner channels allows us to isolate the two effects and adapt the beamforming design accordingly. Additionally, integrating CE with data detection enables reuse of the actual data symbols to enhance channel estimates while minimizing symbol decoding delays
[
5
]
. Previous work has not addressed these issues. We propose a simple CE scheme for an MISO-OFDM system assisted by DMAs. By exploiting fast time-switching beamformers, as noted by
[
4
]
, our method employs a PARAFAC-based algorithm to provide direct estimates of the data symbols and utilizes them to iteratively refine the channel estimates, thereby eliminating the need for separate pilot training and pure data transmission phases. Simulations demonstrate accurate estimation under unknown waveguide conditions and reveal the influence of the parameters on performance.

System model and assumptions

. The DMA is comprised of
D

L

N=DL
elements. The transmitted signal comprises
K

\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}=[m_{1},\cdots,m_{N}]^{{\rm T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
, with
m_{n}=m_{d,l}=e^{-(\alpha+j\beta)x_{d,l}}
, where
x_{d,l}
defines the position of the
l
-th element of the
d
-th waveguide, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are the waveguide attenuation and propagation constants, respectively. We adopt a narrowband model where
\alpha
and
\beta
are approximately unchanged inside a narrow bandwidth, such that
\boldsymbol{m}
is assumed to be frequency-independent
[
3
,
4
]
.We define
\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}=[h_{1,k},\cdots h_{N,k}]^{{\rm T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
as the wireless channel vector related to the
k
-th subcarrier. Defining
\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
as the DMA beamformer and
s
as the transmitted symbol, we can write the received signal as

y_{k}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}^{{{\rm H}}}_{\text{eff},k}\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}s+n_{k},

(1)

where
\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{\text{eff},k}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}\odot\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the
effective channel vector
by incorporating the inner channel into the wireless one, and
n_{k}
is the additive white Gaussian noise. Following
[
3
]
, we assume that the DMA beamformer has a frequency-flat response over a narrow bandwidth, acting as a bandpass filter, whose amplitude and phase result from the meta-atom’s magnetic polarizability at a specific varactor diode tuning state, satisfies the so-called Lorentzian constraints, i.e.,
Q=\{\frac{j+e^{j\phi}}{2}|\phi\in[0,2\pi]\}

III
Data-aided semi-blind channel estimation

We propose a CE scheme that (i) yields separate estimates of these two channel components and (ii) incorporates data detection still during the CE phase. Identifying the inner channel component allows us to isolate and compensate for its effect when designing the DMA beamformer, regardless of the wireless channel state, which evolves much faster due to its shorter coherence time and bandwidth. We consider a transmission window of
T
symbol periods, where the DMA beamforming vector, denoted by
\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}_{p}
for the
p
-th state, switches
P
times per symbol by leveraging the fast response of DMAs
[
4
]
. The received signal associated with the
k
-th subcarrier,
t
-th symbol period, and
p
-th beamforming state can be written as
y_{k,t,p}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}^{{{\rm H}}}_{\text{eff},k}\boldsymbol{f}^{\text{DMA}}_{p}s_{t}+n_{k,t,p}
, or
y_{k,t,p}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}^{{{\rm H}}}_{k}{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}\right\}\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}_{p}s_{t}+n_{k,t,p}
, by decoupling
\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{k}
and
\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}
. Using the property
{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{a}}\right\}\mathit{\boldsymbol{b}}={\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{b}}\right\}\mathit{\boldsymbol{a}}
to exchange
\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}
and
\boldsymbol{f}^{\text{DMA}}_{p}
, and collecting the received data across
T
symbols and
K
subcarriers yields

{\mathbf{Y}}_{p}={\mathbf{H}}\mathrm{diag}\bigl\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}_{p}\bigr\}\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}^{{\rm T}}+{\mathbf{N}}_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times T},

(2)

where
\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}=[s_{1},\cdots,s_{T}]^{{\rm T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{T\times 1}
, reused across the
K
subcarriers,
{\mathbf{H}}=[\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{1},\cdots,\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{N}]^{{\rm H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times N}
is the frequency-domain MISO channel matrix, and
{\mathbf{N}}_{p}
is the related noise term. Defining
{\mathbf{F}}=[\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}_{1},\cdots,\mathit{\boldsymbol{f}}^{\text{DMA}}_{P}]^{{\rm T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{P\times N}
as the DMA training matrix collecting the beamforming vectors for the
P
states, and the rank-1 matrix
{\mathbf{X}}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}^{{\rm T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{T\times N}
, we recast (
2
) as

{\mathbf{Y}}_{p}={\mathbf{H}}D_{p}({\mathbf{F}}){\mathbf{X}}^{{\rm T}}+{\mathbf{N}}_{p}.

(3)

The received signal in (
3
) can be interpreted as the
p
-th frontal matrix slice of a three-way tensor
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times T\times P}
, whose signal component admits a parallel factor (PARAFAC) decomposition
[
7
]
, which can be written in tensor form as

\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{I}_{3,N}\times_{1}{\mathbf{H}}\times_{2}{\mathbf{X}}\times_{3}{\mathbf{F}}+\mathcal{N},

(4)

where
\mathcal{I}_{3,N}
denotes the identity tensor, and
\mathcal{N}
is the associated noise tensor. According to
[
7
]
, the mode-1 and mode-1 matrix unfoldings of
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times T\times P}
are
{\mathbf{Y}}_{(1)}=[{\mathbf{Y}}_{1},\cdots,{\mathbf{Y}}_{P}]\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times PT}
and
{\mathbf{Y}}_{(2)}=[{\mathbf{Y}}_{1}^{{\rm T}},\cdots,{\mathbf{Y}}_{P}^{{\rm T}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{T\times PK}
, which result in
{\mathbf{Y}}_{(1)}={\mathbf{H}}({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{X}})^{{\rm T}}+{\mathbf{N}}_{(1)}
and
{\mathbf{Y}}_{(2)}={\mathbf{X}}({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{H}})^{{\rm T}}+{\mathbf{N}}_{(2)}
, respectively, where
{\mathbf{N}}_{(1)}
and
{\mathbf{N}}_{(2)}
are unfoldings of
\mathcal{N}
. Our first goal is to jointly estimate
{\mathbf{H}}
and the rank-1 matrix
{\mathbf{X}}
. We consider solving the following optimization problem:

\min\limits_{{\mathbf{H}},{\mathbf{X}}}\Big\|\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{I}_{3,N}\times_{1}{\mathbf{H}}\times_{2}{\mathbf{X}}\times_{3}{\mathbf{F}}\Big\|^{2}_{\textrm{F}}.

(5)

Problem (
5
) can be tackled by employing the iterative alternating least-squares (ALS) algorithm to estimate
{\mathbf{H}}
and
{\mathbf{X}}
by capitalizing on the mode-1 and mode-2 unfoldings of
\mathcal{Y}
. This is carried out by solving the problems

\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}=\argmin_{{\mathbf{H}}}~\bigl\|{\mathbf{Y}}_{(1)}-{\mathbf{H}}({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{X}})^{{\rm T}}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},\quad\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}=\argmin_{{\mathbf{X}}}~\bigl\|{\mathbf{Y}}_{(2)}-{\mathbf{X}}({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{H}})^{{\rm T}}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(6)

The resulting solutions are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}={\mathbf{Y}}_{(1)}\bigl[({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{X}})^{{\rm T}}\bigr]^{\dagger},\quad\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}={\mathbf{Y}}_{(2)}\bigl[({\mathbf{F}}\diamond{\mathbf{H}})^{{\rm T}}\bigr]^{\dagger}.

(7)

At each iteration,
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}
are updated until convergence, which is declared when the change in reconstruction error
|\epsilon_{(i)}-\epsilon_{(i-1)}|
becomes sufficiently small, where
\epsilon_{(i)}=\bigl|\mathcal{Y}-\hat{\mathcal{Y}}{(i)}\bigr|
, and
\hat{\mathcal{Y}}{(i)}
denotes the reconstructed tensor at the
i
-th iteration. Once
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}
are found, we consider recovering the symbol and inner channel vectors
\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}
and
\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}
, respectively, from
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}
. To address this problem, we proposed to solve the following rank-1 matrix approximation problem as a second stage:

\min\limits_{\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}},\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}}\bigl\|\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}-\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}^{{\rm T}}\bigr\|^{2}_{\textrm{F}}.

(8)

The best rank-1 approximation to solve (
8
) can be obtained via SVD, i.e.,
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}=\sigma\mathit{\boldsymbol{u}}\mathit{\boldsymbol{v}}^{{\rm H}}
. In this case, the optimal solutions are given by
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{s}}}=\sqrt{\sigma}\mathit{\boldsymbol{u}}
and
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}}=\sqrt{\sigma}\mathit{\boldsymbol{v}}^{\ast}
, respectively. We are also interested in a more challenging scenario where the DMA training matrix is not perfectly known at the receiver due to hardware modeling errors or impairments. In this case, problem (
5
) involves the estimation/refinement of
{\mathbf{F}}
by fully exploiting the trilinear structure of the PARAFAC model. This approach will be detailed in the full version of the paper. Refining the channel estimates jointly with the data symbols and DMA matrix is a unique feature of the proposed approach.

IV
Performance evaluation and upcoming results

We present preliminary simulation results to assess the performance of the proposed CE method, including joint symbol and CE performance, as well as related computational burden. In these findings, we present a first look at comparing it with a benchmark, whose DMA codebook
{\mathbf{F}}
is designed as a truncated DFT matrix in an ideal case. We consider 10
4
Monte Carlo runs using the parameter set
\{K,N,P,T\}=\{8,16,32,10\}
, and the transmit symbols are generated from a 64-QAM constellation. Regarding channel estimation accuracy, Figure
4
indicates that
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}}
is estimated more accurately than
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
, even though
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{m}}}
is found after two stages, the second one involving the solution of a the rank-1 approximation problem using
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}
. The channel
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
has
K
times more coefficients to be estimated. There is a drop of approximately 5 dB compared to the benchmark. The ideal case provides better estimates due to the orthogonal design of
{\mathbf{F}}
, as expected. Figure
4
displays the SER performance. Compared to the benchmark case, the performance of the symbol estimation followed that of the channels. Figure
4
shows that the number of iterations required for ALS convergence is similar to that of the benchmark method in the medium-to-high SNR regime. This trend is also reflected in the average runtime. The full paper shall provide detailed steps of the proposed algorithm, a discussion on identifiability, and a computational complexity analysis. Additionally, we will provide extensive simulations and comparisons with competing methods under various system setups.

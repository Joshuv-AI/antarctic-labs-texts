A compressive sensing-driven data collection scheme for UAV-assisted wireless sensor networks
Authors: Zhe Han, Guoqiang Zheng, Chuanfeng Li, Guoqiang Wang, Yu Mu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have demonstrated significant potential as mobile nodes for data collection in sensor networks. However, the environmental conditions in remote areas often fail to offer stable and reliable energy supply for energy-constrained UAVs and sensing nodes (SNs). Consequently, efficient energy utilization strategies is crucial for maintaining the long-term and stable operation of such networks. A novel sparse compressive sensing (CS) sampling framework for UAV-assisted data collection is proposed in this work. To minimize the overall system energy consumption in each round, we design an energy optimization scheme that decomposes the energy minimization problem into two sub-problems. The first sub-problem involves the node selection under the CS reconstruction accuracy constraint. We develop a CS-based Node Selection (CSNS) algorithm that optimizes UAV flight distance while balancing network energy consumption. The second sub-problem focuses on jointly optimizing the SNs scheduling, UAV flight duration and UAV trajectory. We design a two-stage joint optimization (TSJO) algorithm that convert the non-convex issue into a convex formulation by employing the successive convex approximation (SCA), variable substitution and variable relaxation. Extensive simulation results not only validate the effectiveness of the CSNS and TSJO algorithms, but also demonstrate that our proposed scheme has a considerable energy consumption advantage compared to the benchmark schemes.

Introduction

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs), a key component of the Internet of Things (IoTs), have been extensively applied in various domains, including smart agriculture, ocean exploration, wildlife tracking, and disaster prediction1. However, in remote regions such as wilderness areas, deserts, forests, oceanic zones, and military battlefields, the deployment of traditional terrestrial mobile networks faces significant challenges due to the substantial costs associated with the deployment and maintenance, which hinders the efficient acquisition of sensing data2. Bogena et al. used satellites to gather data from terrestrial sensing nodes (SNs)3. However, the high-density access requirements of the SNs and the high energy consumption caused by long-distance communication render this approach unsuitable for the majority of WSN application scenarios. To address the above issues, the space-air-ground integrated network (SAGIN) has attracted considerable research interest, which employs unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) as mobile aggregation nodes to enable data collection and forward gathered data to remote servers via low-orbit satellites4. Due to flexible deployment capability and high mobility, UAVs not only increase network coverage and improve network capacity, but also build efficient line-of-sight (LoS) links with SNs to improve link quality and communication rate of wireless communications5,6. Therefore, the UAV-aided data collection technique significantly broadens the application scope of WSNs and offers a novel solution for effective data gathering and network coverage in remote areas. In the related research on UAV as a mobile aggregation node to assist WSN data collection, researchers proposed different solutions for various application situations and optimization objectives, respectively7–9. For WSNs with long-term monitoring tasks in remote regions, due to the limitations of environmental conditions such as the wild, mountainous, oceanic and military battlefields, the energy-constrained SNs and UAVs cannot obtain stable and reliable energy replenishment. Efficient energy usage strategies are of great significance in extending the working time of the network. Therefore, under the premise of effectively executing data gathering tasks, research on reducing the energy consumption of each round of system operation has become a hot topic of widespread concern.

In recent years, the energy consumption of UAV-assisted data gathering has been the subject of corresponding investigations by numerous scholars. Regarding research on the UAV energy consumption, Ghorbel et al.10 designed a UAV hovering position optimization approach to reduce UAV energy consumption in a clustered network. Zeng et al.11 utilized the sequential convex approximation (SCA) approach to reduce UAV energy consumption during UAV-assisted data gathering with the “fly-hover-communication” scheme and the “fly-while-communication” scheme, respectively. Solar energy, as a clean energy source, can provide a continuous energy source for UAVs. Zhang et al.12 optimized UAV flight trajectories to address the multi-objective challenges of data transmission rate, UAV energy consumption, and coverage fairness by utilizing hybrid solar power and charging stations. Regarding research on SNs energy consumption, Zhan et al.13 studied the issue of node energy consumption under the time-varying channel fading model and proposed a joint optimization scheme of node sleep scheduling and UAV trajectory to minimize the maximum node energy consumption. Under the constraints of data gathering at all nodes and UAV flight distance, Baek et al.14 optimized UAV hovering points and flight trajectory to maximize the minimum residual energy of the nodes by utilizing the Voronoi diagram. For the UAV-assisted base station to provide the mobile data gathering service, Mondal et al.15 utilized reinforcement learning mechanism to reduce node energy consumption for the sparse coverage scenario of ground base stations. To minimize the weighted energy consumption across overall SNs, Yang et al.16 leveraged the Lyapunov optimization theory to optimize network resource allocation and UAV trajectory simultaneously.

All the aforementioned investigations concentrate on reducing energy consumption from a single perspective. However, energy depletion of either of the UAV mobile nodes and terrestrial SNs will lead to network data collection failure. Therefore, for data gathering in remote areas with limited energy supply, reducing the comprehensive system energy consumption in each round becomes the key to extending the network working time. The UAV-SNs energy consumption is decreased in17 by simultaneously optimizing the 3D trajectory of the fixed-wing UAV and the node transmission scheduling strategy. However, their investigation exclusively considered network scenarios with sparsely distributed nodes. Zhu et al.18 proposed a deep reinforcement learning scheme in the clustered network topology with the goal of reducing the UAV-SNs system energy consumption by optimizing both UAV trajectory and cluster head selection. But SNs in the terrestrial network had to consume additional energy to establish and maintain the clustered topology. Lai et al.19 designed a UAV-assisted hierarchical network data collection architecture and developed a particle swarm optimization approach utilizing knowledge transfer to reduce the weighted sum of SNs and UAVs energy consumption. However, each round of data collection requires the participation of all SNs and does not consider the data correlation between SNs. It is noteworthy that the sensory data collected by SNs within WSNs usually exhibit significant spatiotemporal correlation characteristics. By exploiting these correlation features, the energy consumption for data gathering can be effectively decreased20. The compressive sensing (CS) technique, which enables simultaneous data compression and sampling while efficiently compressing correlated data at a rate below the Nyquist sampling threshold, has been widely adopted in WSNs due to its significant reduction in data gathering energy consumption21. Ebrahimi et al.22 utilized the CS technique to compress data on intra-cluster nodes to decrease energy consumption during data transmission, and UAV sequentially visited cluster head nodes for data gathering. This work aimed to decrease the network energy consumption of intra-cluster SNs, but maintaining the network topology still consumed SNs energy. The sparse CS technique provides a new insight to further reduce energy consumption, which realizes data reconstruction of the whole network by collecting sensory data from some SNs23. However, its reconstruction error is closely related to the design of the measurement matrix24. Some studies have proposed effective design schemes for the sparse CS observation matrix25, but these schemes do not apply to our case because the design of observation matrix is related to the UAV energy consumption in our work. Moreover, since system energy consumption is closely related to UAV flight control and node scheduling strategies, existing relevant studies have not fully exploited the flexible flight advantages of UAVs to investigate the joint optimization of UAV flight time, UAV flight trajectory, and node scheduling.

To achieve system-level energy consumption optimization and effectively balance the energy consumption of the UAV against that of the SNs during data collection, we formulate an energy minimization problem for a UAV–SNs system tailored to UAV-assisted data gathering in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). Furthermore, it proposes an energy-efficient UAV-assisted data collection scheme based on sparse compressive sensing (CS) techniques. The core innovation of this scheme lies in integrating sparse CS theory into the UAV-assisted data collection framework: by visiting only a subset of CS-sampled nodes and employing high-accuracy CS reconstruction algorithms, the scheme enables full-network sensing data recovery, thereby abandoning conventional sampling paradigms that either require traversing all nodes or rely on predefined clustering structures. Building upon this framework, the optimization problem in this paper is formulated as a joint optimization of observed node selection and UAV flight strategy, aiming to minimize the total energy consumption of the UAV–SNs system under the constraint of achieving the required CS reconstruction accuracy. By deeply integrating signal processing theory with intelligent optimization methods, this work formulates an energy minimization problem model for the UAV–SNs system based on sparse compressive sensing (CS)-driven data collection. To overcome the difficulties arising from the non-convex problem and the absence of closed-form expressions, an energy optimization scheme for UAV-assisted data collection is proposed. This scheme decomposes the original problem into two sub-problems and solves them sequentially.

For the node selection sub-problem, a multi-objective optimization model is proposed that integrates an energy-balancing mechanism with the objective of minimizing UAV flight distance. This model explicitly incorporates an upper bound on CS reconstruction error as a hard constraint, thereby jointly optimizing sensing performance and system energy efficiency while ensuring the recoverability of high-accuracy, full-network sensing data. This design effectively prevents premature failure of individual nodes caused by frequent activation, thereby enhancing the long-term operational stability of the network. Building upon this formulation, a Compressive Sensing-based Node Selection (CSNS) algorithm is developed to efficiently solve the multi-objective problem, yielding an optimal set of observed nodes and the corresponding CS measurement matrix that meet the system-level energy optimization requirements.

System model and problem formulation

CS-based data collection model

The data collection network studied in this paper is deployed in remote areas that lacks ground mobile base stations, and its network topology consists of several terrestrial SNs, an airborne UAV mobile aggregation node, and a low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite network, as shown in Fig. 1. Assuming the system is aware of the locations of all SNs in the sensing area in advance. A rotary-wing UAV acts as a mobile aerial base station and periodically takes off from the starting point to perform flight missions with optimized flight trajectory, flight time and SNs scheduling strategy, and lands at the end point to wait for the next round of flight missions. As a mobile Sink, the UAV uses CS sparse sampling to gather sensory data of some SNs in the monitoring area during each round of data collection, and transmits the gathered data to the remote server via low-orbit satellites. Communication model

Assuming that UAV communication module works in duplex mode, UAV can forward the sensed data to the LEO satellite side at the same time after receiving it. Therefore, this paper mainly analyzes the air-ground link communication process. Problem formulation

Utilizing the efficient reconstruction capability of the CS technique, we apply the sparse CS technique to the UAV-assisted data collection process. By collecting data from only a subset of SNs to reconstruct data for the entire network, the flight distance of the UAV is significantly reduced, which in turn decreases the energy consumption of the UAV-SNs. Consequently, for each data collection round in WSNs, the energy consumption optimization problem of the UAV-SNs system can be formulated as:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned}&P:\min E\big \{{{\boldsymbol{A,Q,T}}}\big \}=\min _{\{{{\boldsymbol{A,Q,T}}}\}}\Big [\big (1-\omega \big )\lambda E_{s}+\omega E_{uav}\Big ]\\ &\; st. \ C1,C2,C3,C4,C5\end{aligned} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where the optimization variables include UAV flight duration \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbf {{{\boldsymbol{T}}}}=\left\{ T\left( n \right) ,\ \forall n \right\}$$\end{document}, UAV flight trajectory \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{\boldsymbol{{\textbf {Q}}}}}=\left\{ \mathbf {{{\boldsymbol{q}}}}\left( n \right) ,\ \forall n \right\}$$\end{document}, and SNs scheduling strategy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbf {{{\boldsymbol{A}}}}=\left\{ {{a}_{k}}\left( n \right) ,\ \forall n,k \right\}$$\end{document}. The parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} is the energy balance factor, which is used to adjust the deviation caused by the condition that the energy consumption of the UAV differs significantly in magnitude compared to that of the SN. The parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega \left( 0\le \omega \le 1 \right)$$\end{document} represents the weight coefficient that measures the importance of UAV and SNs energy consumption. Constraint C1 limits the upper bound of the CS data reconstruction error. Constraint C2 indicates that the UAV trajectory starts from the initial point, lands to the final point, and also limits the maximum flight distance of each path segment. Constraint C3 ensures that at most one SN transmits data to the UAV at each path segment. Constraint C4 limits the minimum transmission task value for each selected node. Constraint C5 ensures that the SNR of the air-to-ground link exceeds the minimum threshold allowed by the system.

An efficient scheme to solve problem P

The energy consumption minimization problem P is extremely difficult to solve since the CS reconstruction accuracy and optimization variables in constraint C1 cannot be established in a mathematical closed-form expression, as well as the non-convexity of the constraint C4 and the objective function. A comprehensive analysis of problem P reveals that the lack of mathematical closed-form formulation arises from the challenge associated with integrating the CS technique with UAV-assisted data collection technique. If a well-designed node selection strategy for CS sparse sampling is developed, which satisfies the requirements of CS data reconstruction accuracy while simultaneously addressing UAV flight energy consumption and network energy balance, the aforementioned integration challenges can be effectively resolved. Therefore, an energy consumption optimization scheme for UAV-assisted data collection based on sparse CS technique is proposed in this paper, which decouples the original problem P into two sub-problems. Firstly, to address the lack of mathematical closed-form expression between CS reconstruction accuracy and optimization strategies, the node selection sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{1}P$$\end{document} is defined, and the objectives of network energy balance and UAV flight distance minimization under the constraint of CS reconstruction accuracy can be realized by optimizing the node selection strategy. Secondly, relying on the node selection results of sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{1}P$$\end{document}, the original problem P is transformed into the energy consumption iterative optimization sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{2}P$$\end{document}. By solving the sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{2}P$$\end{document}, the UAV-SNs energy consumption minimization problem P can be solved approximately. Scheme implementation and complexity analysis

To support long-term monitoring missions in remote areas, the energy-efficient optimization scheme proposed in this paper adopts a deployment architecture that combines remote planning with local execution, ensuring theoretical performance while also addressing practical engineering feasibility for real-world deployment. Taking forest monitoring in national nature reserves or key ecological functional zones as an example, a large number of wireless sensing nodes are widely deployed across rugged terrains with limited communication infrastructure to continuously collect multidimensional environmental parameters-such as temperature, humidity, soil moisture, light intensity, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$CO_{2}$$\end{document} concentration, and acoustic/vibration signals. These data are critical for forest health assessment, pest and disease early warning, carbon sequestration quantification, and biodiversity conservation. However, due to constraints on energy supply and communication conditions, traditional data backhaul methods struggle to achieve efficient and sustainable data collection.

To address the aforementioned challenges, the proposed scheme completes the entire optimization process offline at a forest-edge computing station or a remote data center prior to the start of each monitoring cycle. First, the system employs the CSNS algorithm to solve the node selection sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{1}P_{2}$$\end{document}, obtaining a sparse set of sampled nodes that satisfies the compressive sensing (CS) reconstruction accuracy requirement while balancing UAV flight distance and energy consumption across the sensor network. Subsequently, based on the obtained node access set, the TSJO algorithm is invoked to solve the energy-efficient iterative optimization sub-problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{2}P_{2}$$\end{document}, jointly optimizing the UAV trajectory, flight speeds, and communication scheduling strategies for all selected nodes. Nodes not scheduled in this round remain in sleep mode throughout the current mission cycle to conserve their own energy. The optimized results, including the UAV trajectory, flight speeds, and node activation schedule, are transmitted to the UAV via a LEO satellite link. Upon receiving the instructions, the UAV autonomously follows the precomputed route. When the UAV enters the communication coverage range of a scheduled node, it actively transmits an encrypted wake-up beacon. Upon receiving a valid beacon, the node immediately wakes up from sleep mode and uploads its sensed data. After data transmission is complete, the node automatically returns to a low-power sleep mode, effectively avoiding energy waste due to idle listening. Notably, during each mission cycle, the UAV periodically shuttles back and forth between two predefined base stations. The entire process requires no online replanning or high-frequency feedback interaction, significantly reducing dependence on onboard computational resources and real-time communication reliability, making it highly suitable for typical long-term and periodic data collection tasks.

Moreover, to respond to unexpected emergency events within the monitoring area, the proposed architecture can be further extended to support a lightweight dynamic priority response mechanism. Specifically, when a sensing node detects an anomalous event, it can actively transmit a short, high-priority urgent beacon. This beacon consumes minimal energy and contains only the event type and the node ID, which is directly reported to the remote ground control station via a LEO satellite link. Upon receiving this request, the control station forcibly includes the node in the sampling set of the CSNS algorithm in the next mission planning cycle and assigns it the highest access priority. The TSJO algorithm then automatically adjusts the UAV’s flight trajectory and time allocation during the subsequent optimization round to prioritize data collection from this emergency node before handling other routine nodes. Once the updated flight instructions are delivered through the satellite network, the UAV autonomously executes the revised mission without any online intervention. This mechanism effectively enhances the system’s responsiveness to non-instantaneous emergency events without significantly increasing overall system overhead.

This method will turn the area around the lines into an access hotspot, thereby disrupting the energy balance in the WSNs. Thus, the objectives of minimizing UAV flight distance and achieving balanced energy consumption are inherently competitive. To quantitatively characterize the competitive relationship between the two objectives, the objective of network energy consumption balance is defined as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$U=\left( \sum \nolimits _{i=1}^{M}{{{U}_{r}}\left( {{k}_{i}} \right) } \right) /M$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{k}_{i}}$$\end{document} indicates the node ID selected to participate in data collection. When the node selection strategy makes the objective function value U smaller, the network energy consumption tends to be more balanced. Regarding the total UAV flight distance, once the set of nodes participating in data collection are determined, the objective of minimizing the total flight distance D can be modeled as a Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). We employ the classical nearest-neighbor heuristic algorithm34 to solve this TSP in this paper: starting from an initial node, the algorithm iteratively selects the nearest unvisited node as the next destination until all nodes have been visited. This approach is computationally efficient and is widely used in real-time applications such as UAV path planning.

Conclusion

The energy consumption of data collection in UAV-assisted WSNs is examined in this work. To decrease the UAV-SNs energy consumption, an energy minimization problem based on the sparse CS data collection technology is analyzed and established, and an effective energy optimization scheme for UAV-assisted data collection is proposed. The intractable energy consumption minimization problem is decomposed into the node selection sub-problem and the energy consumption iterative optimization sub-problem. Then the CSNS algorithm and the TSJO algorithm are designed to solve these two sub-problems respectively, thereby obtaining the approximate solution for the UAV-SNs energy consumption minimization problem. Simulation results validate the advantages of our proposed scheme in terms of both data reconstruction accuracy and system energy consumption. It should be noted that this work adopts a quasi-static LoS channel assumption to clearly reveal the system performance of the proposed scheme under stable conditions. This assumption is well-suited for most long-term, stable, and periodic monitoring scenarios. To address practical deployment challenges such as time-varying channels, link degradation, and increased UAV energy consumption due to wind resistance under adverse weather conditions, future work will incorporate a dynamic adjustment mechanism based on real-time channel feedback. By online estimating and predicting channel states, the system will adaptively optimize the UAV trajectory, scheduling scheduling policy, and communication parameters to enhance robustness in harsh environments. Additionally, we plan to explore event-triggered data collection strategies with dynamic task scheduling and online trajectory replanning to further improve the system’s flexibility and practicality.

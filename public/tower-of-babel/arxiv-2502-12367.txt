An edge crack and a crack close to the vertex of a wedge
Y.A. Antipov

Abstract
Two model problems of an elastic wedge with an internal and edge crack are analyzed. The problem of an internal crack reduces to an order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem whose matrix kernel entries are meromorphic functions and have exponential factors. When the internal crack is located along one of the wedge sides, an efficient method of solution is proposed. It requires a factorization of the order-2 matrix coefficient associated with the corresponding problem of an edge crack and the solution of an infinite system of linear algebraic system with an exponential rate of convergence of an approximate solution to the exact one. The order-2 Khrapkov’s factorization is modified by splitting the matrix kernel into a scalar dominant function and a “regular” matrix whose factorization is more convenient for numerical purposes. Expressions for the stress intensity coefficients and the potential energy released when the crack advances are derived. Asymptotic relations for the stress intensity coefficients and the potential energy when one of the crack tips is close the wedge vertex are obtained.

Formulation

We consider the plane problem of an elastic wedge
W=\{0<r<\infty,-\alpha_{2}<\theta<\alpha_{1}\}
,

0<\alpha_{j}<\pi

whose boundary is free of traction

\sigma_{\theta}=0,\quad\tau_{r\theta}=0,\quad\theta=-\alpha_{2},\quad 0<r<\infty,

\sigma_{\theta}=0,\quad\tau_{r\theta}=0,\quad\theta=\alpha_{1},\quad 0<r<\infty,

(2.1)

The wedge

is perturbed by the presence of a plane crack
\{a<r<b,\theta=0^{\pm}\}

whose faces are subject to the loads

\sigma_{\theta}=-p_{\theta}(r),\quad\tau_{r\theta}=-p_{r\theta}(r),\quad\theta=0^{\pm},\quad a<r<b.

(2.2)

The functions
p_{r}
and
p_{r\theta}
are integrable on the interval
(a,b)
, and the stresses

\sigma_{\theta}
and
\tau_{r\theta}
vanish at infinity
as
r^{-1}
.
We confine ourselves to considering the plane stress conditions. In the plane strain case,
the Young modulus
E
and the Poisson ratio
\nu
are to be replaced by
E_{1}=E/(1-\nu^{2})

and
\nu_{1}=\nu/(1-\nu^{2})
.
The displacements are discontinuous through the crack faces, and we denote by

\frac{1}{E}\chi_{1}(r)=\frac{\partial u_{\theta}(r,0^{-})}{\partial r}-\frac{\partial u_{\theta}(r,0^{+})}{\partial r},

\frac{1}{E}\chi_{2}(r)=\frac{\partial u_{r}(r,0^{-})}{\partial r}-\frac{\partial u_{r}(r,0^{+})}{\partial r}

(2.3)

the jumps of their tangential derivatives which are unknown on the segment
(a,b)
and vanish
if
r<a
or
r>b
.
Due to their definition the functions
\chi_{1}(r)
and
\chi_{2}(r)
have to meet the conditions

\int_{a}^{b}\chi_{1}(r)dr=0,\quad\int_{a}^{b}\chi_{2}(r)dr=0.

(2.4)

In the wedge
W
, the stresses satisfy the equilibrium equations

r\frac{\partial\sigma_{r}}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial\tau_{r\theta}}{\partial\theta}+\sigma_{r}-\sigma_{\theta}=0,

\frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta}}{\partial\theta}+r\frac{\partial\tau_{r\theta}}{\partial r}+2\tau_{r\theta}=0,

(2.5)

and the compatibility condition

\Delta(\sigma_{r}+\sigma_{\theta})=0.

(2.6)

3
Order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem

It is convenient to apply the Mellin transform

(\sigma_{\theta}^{s},\tau_{r\theta}^{s},\eta_{\theta}^{s},\eta_{r}^{s})(\theta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sigma_{\theta},\tau_{r\theta},E\frac{\partial u_{\theta}}{\partial r},E\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial r}\right)(r,\theta)r^{s}dr

(3.1)

to equations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) in the wedges
-\alpha_{2}<\theta<0

0<\theta<\alpha_{1}

The resulting one-dimensional system of equations yields the following relations
between the vectors
\sigma^{s}=(\sigma_{\theta}^{s},\tau_{r\theta}^{s})

14

\eta^{s}(0^{-})=\frac{H(s,-\alpha_{2})}{d(s,-\alpha_{2})}\sigma^{s}(0),\quad\eta^{s}(0^{+})=\frac{H(s,\alpha_{1})}{d(s,\alpha_{1})}\sigma^{s}(0),

(3.2)

\end{array}\right),

(3.3)

(3.4)

Aiming to derive a vector Riemann-Hilbert problem we subtract the expression for

(3.5)

where
\chi^{s}(0)=\eta^{s}(0^{-})-\eta^{s}(0^{+})
,
and the entries
G_{jk}(s)
of the matrix

G_{j\,3-j}(s)=2s[(-1)^{j-1}s-1]\left[-\frac{\sin^{2}\alpha_{1}}{d(s,\alpha_{1})}+\frac{\sin^{2}\alpha_{2}}{d(s,\alpha_{2})}\right],\quad j=1,2.

(3.6)

To clarify the analytic properties of the vector-functions
\chi^{s}(0)
and
\sigma^{s}(0)

\chi^{s}(0)=b^{s+1}\chi^{-}(s)=b^{s+1}\delta^{s+1}\chi^{+}(s),\quad\delta=\frac{a}{b}\in(0,1),

\sigma^{s}(0)=b^{s+1}[\delta^{s+1}\sigma^{-}(s)+g^{-}(s)+\sigma^{+}(s)],

(3.7)

where

is the only one vector-function in (

) that is known. It is
expressed through the Mellin transforms of the loads applied to the crack faces

g^{-}(s)(s)=\delta^{s+1}g^{+}(s),\quad g^{-}(s)=\int_{\delta}^{1}\sigma(br)r^{s}dr,\quad g^{+}(s)=\int_{1}^{1/\delta}\sigma(ar)r^{s}dr.

(3.8)

The integral representations
of the unknown vector-functions
in (
3.7

\chi^{-}(s)=\int_{\delta}^{1}\chi(br)r^{s}dr,\quad\chi^{+}(s)=\int_{1}^{1/\delta}\chi(ar)r^{s}dr,

\sigma^{-}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\sigma(ar)r^{s}dr,\quad\sigma^{+}(s)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\sigma(br)r^{s}dr.

(3.9)

Here and further,
\sigma(r)=(\sigma_{\theta}(r,0),\tau_{r\theta}(r,0))
and

The integrals

Edge crack in a wedge along one of its sides

Here,

\chi^{-}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\chi(br)r^{s}dr,\quad\sigma^{+}(s)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\sigma(br)r^{s}dr,\quad g^{-}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\sigma(br)r^{s}dr,

The second assumption simplifies the matrix

(4.3)

whose entries are

b_{1}(s)=2\cot\pi s-\frac{\sin 2\alpha s}{d(s,\alpha)},\quad b_{2}(s)=-\frac{2s\sin\alpha}{d(s,\alpha)},\quad l=\cos\alpha,\quad m_{\pm}(s)=(\pm s-1)\sin\alpha.

(4.4)

4.1
Exact solution to the order-2 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem

The first step of the solution method is to split the matrix
G(s)
into a dominant scalar
that yields the singularity of the solution at infinity and a “regular” matrix. We have

G(s)=4\cot\pi sG_{0}(s),

(4.5)

where

G_{0}(s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b^{\circ}_{1}(s)+b^{\circ}_{2}(s)l&b^{\circ}_{2}(s)m_{+}(s)\\
b^{\circ}_{2}(s)m_{-}(s)&b^{\circ}_{1}(s)-b^{\circ}_{2}(s)l\\
\end{array}\right),

b^{\circ}_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\tan\pi s\sin 2\alpha s}{2d(s,\alpha)}\right),\quad b^{\circ}_{2}(s)=-\frac{s\tan\pi s\sin\alpha}{2d(s,\alpha)}.

(4.6)

We factorize the dominant function in terms of the
\Gamma
-functions

\cot\pi s=\frac{K^{+}(s)}{K^{-}(s)},\quad K^{+}(s)=-\frac{\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(1/2-s)},\quad K^{-}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(1/2+s)}{\Gamma(1+s)},

(4.7)

where
X^{\pm}(t)=X(\omega\mp 0+i\tau)
,

s_{-}(s)&c_{-}(s)\\

B(s)=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\log\Delta(t)dt}{t-s}\right\},\quad\beta(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\epsilon(t)dt}{f^{1/2}(t)(t-s)},

(4.9)

the function

is the determinant of the matrix

, and this function and the function

as follows:

(4.36)

{\cal P}^{\circ}

{\cal P}

Since the total angle of the wedge is
\alpha+\pi\in(\pi,2\pi)

{\cal P}^{\circ}

N

of singular plane problems [Cher]. Its solution can be found my the method of separation of variables. Assume that

5.1
Solution of the order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem

As in Section
4
, we take
\alpha_{2}=\pi
, while
\alpha_{1}=\alpha
is an arbitrary angle in the range
(0,\pi)
. What is different now is that
a>0
that is the crack is internal.
The problem is equivalent to the order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem (
3.10
) with the matrix
G(s)
given by (
4.3
).
We split the matrix
G(s)
by means of the relation (
4.5
), factorize
\cot\pi s
and the matrix
G_{0}(s)
by formulas (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), respectively, and take into account that
the matrix
G(s)
commutes with the Khrapkov factors of the matrix
G_{0}(s)

G(t)X^{-}(t)=X^{-}(t)G(t),\quad G(t)X^{+}(t)=X^{+}(t)G(t),\quad t\in\Omega.

(5.1)

This enables us to rewrite the order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem as follows:

K^{-}(t)X^{-}(t)[\chi^{-}(t)-\delta^{t+1}G(t)\sigma^{-}(t)]=4K^{+}(t)X^{+}(t)[\sigma^{+}(t)+g^{-}(t)],

\frac{[X^{+}(t)]^{-1}}{K^{+}(t)}[\chi^{+}(t)-\delta^{-t-1}G(t)\sigma^{+}(t)]=\frac{4[X^{-}(t)]^{-1}}{K^{-}(t)}[\sigma^{-}(t)+g^{+}(t)],\quad t\in\Omega.

(5.2)

For this model, we confine ourselves to considering constant loading only,
p_{\theta}(r)=P_{1}
,
p_{r\theta}(r)=P_{2}
,
P_{j}=\mbox{const}
. Then
the vectors
g^{-}(s)
and
g^{+}(s)

have the form

g^{-}(s)=\frac{\delta^{s+1}-1}{s+1}P,\quad g^{+}(s)=\frac{1-\delta^{-s-1}}{s+1}P,\quad P=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{1}\\
P_{2}\\
\end{array}\right).

(5.3)

Splitting the functions
g^{\pm}(t)
into two parts, with
\delta^{\pm t\pm 1}
and without,
we transform the system (
5.2
) as

K^{-}(t)X^{-}(t)\left\{\chi^{-}(t)-\delta^{t+1}G(t)\left[\sigma^{-}(t)+\frac{P}{t+1}\right]\right\}=4K^{+}(t)X^{+}(t)\left[\sigma^{+}(t)-\frac{P}{t+1}\right],

\frac{[X^{+}(t)]^{-1}}{K^{+}(t)}\left\{\chi^{+}(t)-\delta^{-t-1}G(t)\left[\sigma^{+}(t)-\frac{P}{t+1}\right]\right\}=\frac{4[X^{-}(t)]^{-1}}{K^{-}(t)}\left[\sigma^{-}(t)+\frac{P}{t+1}\right],\quad t\in\Omega.

(5.4)

First we remove the simple pole at
s=-1
in the right hand-side of the first equation and
the order-2 pole at
s=-1
in the left hand-side
of the second equation. For this, we need the following asymptotic expansions
at
s=-1
:

G(s)=\frac{4}{\pi(s+1)}G_{0}(-1)+O(s+1),\quad s\to-1,

[X^{+}(s)]^{-1}=[X^{+}(-1)]^{-1}+(s+1)\frac{d}{ds}[X^{+}(-1)]^{-1}+O((s+1)^{2}),\quad s\to-1,

\frac{1}{K^{+}(s)}=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}[1-2(1-\log 2)(s+1)]+O((s+1)^{2}),\quad s\to-1.

\delta^{-s-1}=1-(s+1)\log\delta+O((s+1)^{2}),\quad s\to-1.

(5.5)

Denote
s_{1}=1
. In addition to the simple poles at
\pm s_{1}\in D^{\mp}
, the entries of the matrix
G(s)
have a simple pole at
s_{0}=0\in D^{-}
and simple poles at the
points
\pm m\in D^{\mp}
(
m=2,3,\ldots
) and at the complex-conjugate zeros
\pm\sigma_{m}\in D^{\mp}
of the function
d(s,\alpha)
.
We denote all these zeros by
s_{m}
and order them such that
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits s_{m}\leq\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits s_{m+1}
(
m=2,3,\ldots
).
Aiming to eliminate these poles we introduce the vector-functions

\Lambda^{+}(s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}^{+}}{s-s_{m}},\quad\Lambda^{-}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}^{-}}{s+s_{m}},\quad A_{m}^{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{1m}^{\pm}\\
A_{2m}^{\pm}\\
\end{array}\right).

(5.6)

The series
\Lambda^{\pm}(s)
are holomorphic vector-functions in the half-planes
D^{\pm}
and have simple poles
of the matrix
G(s)
in the domains
D^{\mp}
. The residues of these vector-functions are to
be found. It will be shown later that the series
\Lambda^{\pm}(s)
converge uniformly and absolutely in the
domains

D^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}=C\setminus\cup_{m=m_{\mp}}^{\infty}D^{\mp}_{\varepsilon}(\pm s_{m}),

(5.7)

where
C
is the complex
s
-plane,
m_{-}=0
,
m_{+}=1
, and
D_{\varepsilon}^{\mp}(\pm s_{m})
are discs of a small radius
\varepsilon>0

centered at the points
\pm s_{m}\in D^{\mp}
. On removing the poles mentioned, using the asymptotics
of the factors
K^{\pm}(s)
at infinity

K^{\pm}(s)\sim\mp(\mp s)^{-1/2},\quad s\to\infty,\quad s\in D^{\pm},

(5.8)

and since
\chi^{\pm}(s)=O(s^{-1/2})
,
\sigma^{\pm}(s)=O(s^{-1/2})
.
s\to\infty
,
s\in D^{\pm}
,
we arrive at the following relations which continue each other analytically into the whole complex plane:

K^{-}(s)X^{-}(s)\left\{\chi^{-}(s)-\delta^{s+1}G(s)\left[\sigma^{-}(s)+\frac{P}{s+1}\right]\right\}-\frac{N_{1}^{-}}{s+1}-\Lambda^{+}(s)

=4K^{+}(s)X^{+}(s)\left[\sigma^{+}(s)-\frac{P}{s+1}\right]-\frac{N_{1}^{-}}{s+1}-\Lambda^{+}(s)=0,\quad s\in C,

\frac{[X^{+}(s)]^{-1}}{K^{+}(s)}\left\{\chi^{+}(s)-\delta^{-s-1}G(s)\left[\sigma^{+}(s)-\frac{P}{s+1}\right]\right\}-\frac{N_{1}^{+}}{s+1}-\frac{N_{2}^{+}}{(s+1)^{2}}-\Lambda^{-}(s)

=\frac{4[X^{-}(s)]^{-1}}{K^{-}(s)}\left[\sigma^{-}(s)+\frac{P}{s+1}\right]-\frac{N_{1}^{+}}{s+1}-\frac{N_{2}^{+}}{(s+1)^{2}}-\Lambda^{-}(s)=C^{\circ},\quad s\in C.

(5.9)

Here,
C^{\circ}=(C_{1}^{\circ},C_{2}^{\circ})
is an arbitrary constant vector, while
N_{1}^{+}
,

N_{2}^{+}
and
N_{1}^{-}
are constant vectors given by

N_{1}^{+}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left\{[X^{+}(-1)]^{-1}[2(1-\log 2)+\log\delta]-\frac{d}{ds}[X^{+}(-1)]^{-1}\right\}G_{0}(-1)P,

N_{2}^{+}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}G_{0}(-1)[X^{+}(-1)]^{-1}P,\quad N_{1}^{-}=\frac{8}{\sqrt{\pi}}X^{+}(-1)P.

(5.10)

The system (
5.9
) enables us to write representation formulas for the solution of the vector Riemann-Hilbert problem (
3.10
). They read

\sigma^{+}(s)=\frac{P}{s+1}+\frac{1}{4K^{+}(s)}[X^{+}(s)]^{-1}\Sigma_{1}(s),\quad s\in D^{+},

\sigma^{-}(s)=-\frac{P}{s+1}+\frac{K^{-}(s)}{4}X^{-}(s)\Sigma_{2}(s),\quad s\in D^{-},

\chi^{-}(s)=\frac{\delta^{s+1}}{4}G(s)K^{-}(s)X^{-}(s)\Sigma_{2}(s)+\frac{1}{K^{-}(s)}[X^{-}(s)]^{-1}\Sigma_{1}(s),\quad s\in D^{-},

\chi^{+}(s)=\frac{\delta^{-s-1}}{4K^{+}(s)}G(s)[X^{+}(s)]^{-1}\Sigma_{1}(s)+K^{+}(s)X^{+}(s)\Sigma_{2}(s),\quad s\in D^{+},

(5.11)

where the vector-functions
\Sigma_{1}(s)
and
\Sigma_{2}(s)
are

\Sigma_{1}(s)=\frac{N_{1}^{-}}{s+1}+\Lambda^{+}(s),\quad\Sigma_{2}(s)=C^{\circ}+\frac{N_{1}^{+}}{s+1}+\frac{N_{2}^{+}}{(s+1)^{2}}+\Lambda^{-}(s).

(5.12)

It is directly verified that
\chi^{-}(s)=\delta^{s+1}\chi^{+}(s)
, and the vector-functions
\chi^{\pm}(s)

satisfy the first equation in (
3.10
). The vector-functions
\sigma^{\pm}(s)
are holomorphic
in the half-planes
D^{\pm}
. The simple poles of the vector-functions
\chi^{\pm}(s)
at the poles of the
entries of the matrix
G(s)
become removable singularities if and only if the
vectors
A^{\pm}_{m}
solve the following infinite system of linear algebraic equations:

A^{+}_{n}=\delta^{s_{n}+1}\Delta^{-}_{n}\left[C^{\circ}+\frac{N_{1}^{+}}{s_{n}+1}+\frac{N_{2}^{+}}{(s_{n}+1)^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}^{-}}{s_{n}+s_{m}}\right],\quad n=0,1,\ldots,

A^{-}_{n}=\delta^{s_{n}-1}\Delta^{+}_{n}\left(h_{n}N_{1}^{-}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}^{+}}{s_{n}+s_{m}}\right),\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

(5.13)

where

\Delta_{n}^{+}=-\frac{1}{4[K^{+}(-s_{n})]^{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{s=-s_{n}}G(s)\{[X^{+}(-s_{n})]^{-1}\}^{2},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

\chi^{+}(s)=\frac{\delta^{-s-1}L^{+}(s)}{L(s)}\left[\Lambda^{+}(s)-\frac{p_{+}}{s+1}\right]-\frac{4}{L^{+}(s)}\left[\Lambda^{-}(s)+C^{\circ}+\frac{p_{-}}{s+1}\right].

It is immediately verified that

.
In general, for arbitrary chosen coefficients

and

A_{n}^{-}=\delta^{s_{n}-1}\Delta^{+}_{n}\left(\frac{p_{+}}{s_{n}-1}-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}^{+}}{s_{n}+s_{m}}\right),\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

where

\Delta^{\pm}_{n}=\frac{[L^{\pm}(\mp s_{n})]^{\pm 2}\sin\pi s_{n}}{\pi\sin\pi s_{n}-4s_{n}},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,\quad\Delta_{0}^{-}=\frac{2\pi}{(\pi^{2}-4)[L^{-}(0)]^{2}},\quad s_{0}=0.

We shift the contour

on the contour

(7.14)

To compute

\frac{d}{dt}[X^{-}(t)]=X^{-}(t)\left[-\frac{L^{\prime}_{0}(t)}{2L_{0}(t)}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}P.V.\int_{\Omega_{0}}\frac{L^{\prime}_{0}(\tau)}{L_{0}(t)}\frac{d\tau}{\tau-t}\right].\quad t\in\Omega_{0}.

8
Conclusion

We have analyzed model problems of a crack in a wedge when the crack
a<r<b
is an edge slit (
a=0
) or an internal cut (
a>0
).
Our attention has been focussed on the case when the crack
\{0\leq a<r<b,\theta=0\}
is located in the wedge
\{0<r<\infty,-\pi<\theta<\alpha\}
,

0<\alpha<\pi
.
The solution
[
14
]
to this problem of an edge crack
a=0
was obtained by matrix factorization of the kernel of the
order-2 vector Wiener-Hopf problem. The results
[
14
]
also include approximate formulas for the weight matrix neededed
for the stress intensity factors. We have modified the factorization
[
14
]
by first splitting the kernel
into a dominant scalar function responsible for the solution singularities and a “regular” matrix whose factorization
is more convenient for numerical purposes. We have considered two cases of the loading and determined the stress intensity factors
by quadratures. The first case is standard and concerns
constant normal and tangential loads applied to the crack faces. The second one uses the eigen-solution of the
singular problem of elasticity of a wedge whose angle
\alpha+\pi\in(\pi,2\pi)

[
9
]
. The wedge boundary and the crack faces are assumed to be free
of traction, while at infinity, the stresses decay as
r^{\mu-1}
, where
\mu\in(0,1)
is a real root of the associated
characteristic equation of the wedge without a crack.

In the case of an internal crack, we reduced the model to an order-4 vector Riemann-Hilbert problem. For its solution,
we have proposed a method that uses the
2\times 2
matrix factorization arising in the model of the edge crack and requiring
solving an infinite system of linear algebraic equations of the second kind. What is important is that the rate of convergence of the approximate
solution to the exact one is exponential.
We have derived the stress intensity factors
K_{I,II}^{+}
and
K_{I,II}^{-}
at the crack tips
r=b
and
r=a
, respectively.
It has been discovered that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}K_{I}^{-}\\
K_{II}^{-}\\
\end{array}\right)\sim-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}\log\delta}Q\left(\begin{array}[]{c}K_{I}\\
K_{II}\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\delta=\frac{a}{b}\to 0,

(8.1)

where
Q
is a matrix whose entries are independent of the loading and functions of the angle
\alpha
only and
K_{I,II}=K_{I,II}^{+}|_{a=0}
.
We have established that, when the crack
\{a<r<b,\theta=0\}
advances to the left,
r=a-h
(
h>0
and small) and
a
is close to the wedge
vertex, then the potential energy increment
\delta U
has the form

\delta U\sim\frac{h}{E\delta\log^{2}\delta}(K_{I}^{2}+K_{II}^{2}),\quad h\to 0,\quad\delta\to 0,

(8.2)

where
E
is the Young modulus.

In the general case of the problem, when the crack
\{a<r<b,\theta=0\}
is in an arbitrary wedge
\{0<r<\infty,-\alpha_{1}<\theta<\alpha_{2}\}
,
the associated
2\times 2
matrix kernel cannot be factorized by the methods available in the literature. However, if the left and right
factorizations become available, then the asymptotic formula (
8.1
) is also valid with the matrix
Q
expressible
through not explicitly defined Wiener-Hopf factors and independent of the loading. Finally, we note that in the case of an internal crack orthogonal to the boundary
of a half-plane and close to the boundary, formula (
8.1
) is simplified, and the stress intensity factors at the tips
a

and
b
,
K_{I}^{-}
and
K_{I}|_{a=0}
, respectively, are connected by

K_{I}^{-}\sim-\frac{K_{I}}{\sqrt{\delta}\log\delta},\quad\delta\to 0.

(8.3)

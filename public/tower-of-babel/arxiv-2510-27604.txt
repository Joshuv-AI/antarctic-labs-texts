DiffstarPop: A Generative Physical Model of Galaxy Star Formation History ⋆ E-mail:alexalarcongonzalez@gmail.com
Alex Alarcon 1,⋆, Andrew P. Hearin 2, Matthew R. Becker 2, Gillian Beltz-Mohrmann 2,4,5, Andrew Benson 3, Sachi Weerasooriya 3

Abstract
We present DiffstarPop, a differentiable forward model of cosmological populations of galaxy star formation histories (SFH). In the model, individual galaxy SFH is parametrized by Diffstar, which has parameters θ SFH \theta_{\rm SFH} that have a direct interpretation in terms of galaxy formation physics, such as star formation efficiency and quenching. DiffstarPop is a model for the statistical connection between θ SFH \theta_{\rm SFH} and the mass assembly history (MAH) of dark matter halos. We have formulated DiffstarPop to have the minimal flexibility needed to accurately reproduce the statistical distributions of galaxy SFH predicted by a diverse range of simulations, including the IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical simulation, the Galacticus semi-analytic model, and the UniverseMachine semi-empirical model. Our publicly available code written in JAX includes Monte Carlo generators that supply statistical samples of galaxy assembly histories that mimic the populations seen in each simulation, and can generate SFHs for 10 6 10^{6} galaxies in 1.1 CPU-seconds, or 0.03 GPU-seconds. We conclude the paper with a discussion of applications of DiffstarPop, which we are using to generate catalogs of synthetic galaxies populating the merger trees in cosmological N-body simulations.

Introduction

Theoretical models that connect the properties of galaxies with the dark matter halos they inhabit provide a key ingredient in many approaches to making predictions for the galaxy populations observed by cosmological surveys. Predicting the statistical properties of large galaxy populations can be computationally demanding, and models of the galaxy–halo connection are all confronted with a tradeoff between the level of physical detail of the model, and the computational expense of the predictions. As discussed in
Wechsler & Tinker 2018
, this tradeoff provides a natural way to organize the landscape of models of the galaxy–halo connection. At one end of this spectrum sit cosmological hydrodynamical simulations
(
Pillepich et al. 2018a
;
Schaye et al. 2015
;
Davé et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
, which achieve the most physically rich predictions for the galaxy population, but only at the enormous computational expense of directly simulating the evolution of baryonic matter and its feedback processes
(see
Crain & van de Voort 2023
, for a recent review)
. At the opposite end of this spectrum are empirical models such as the Halo Occupation Distribution
(
Berlind & Weinberg 2002
;
Zheng et al. 2005
, HOD,)
, the Conditional Luminosity Function
(
Yang et al. 2003
;
van den Bosch et al. 2003
, CLF,)
, and subhalo abundance matching
(
Kravtsov et al. 2004
;
Conroy et al. 2006
, SHAM,)
; such models are computationally cheap and are based on much less expensive gravity-only N-body simulations, but these models rely on numerous simplifying assumptions that limit their ability to make predictions with physically realistic levels of complexity.

Semi-analytic models (SAMs) strike a balance between these two extremes: their predictions for the galaxy population are physically rich and detailed, but SAMs are much less expensive than hydro simulations. SAMs are based on N-body simulations, or merger trees constructed using extended Press-Schechter theory
(
Lacey & Cole 1993
;
Jiang & van den Bosch 2014
)
, and so do not explicitly solve for the evolution of baryonic mass with tracer particles or grid cells; instead, SAMs employ a set of assumptions about the coupled system of ODEs that regulates the physics of the galaxy that evolves in the merger tree of a simulated halo
(
Benson 2010
;
Somerville & Davé 2015
, for review articles, see)
. For each simulated merger tree, a modern SAM such as Galform
(
Cole et al. 2000
)
, Galacticus
(
Benson 2012
)
, GAEA
(
Fontanot et al. 2024
)
, or Shark
(
Lagos et al. 2018
)
solves an ODE system for the evolution of a reservoir of hot gas that cools and forms stellar mass, for how feedback from star formation ejects cold gas from the galaxy, and for how mergers transform disks into spheroids, etc. SAMs have rich predictive power for multi-wavelength galaxy SEDs, and while considerable progress has been made in recent years in improving their efficiency
(
van Daalen et al. 2016
;
Henriques et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
, SAMs are still quite computationally demanding compared to empirical models, and exploring the full parameter space with Bayesian MCMCs can be costly
(
Henriques et al. 2009
, see, e.g.,)
.

Empirical models of galaxy star formation history (SFH) have become more common in recent years. In this approach, empirical prescriptions are adopted for the relationship between the SFH of a galaxy and the mass assembly history (MAH) of the halo, and observational data is used to constrain the parameters; examples include SMAD
(
Becker 2015
)
, EMERGE
(
Moster et al. 2018
)
, and UniverseMachine
(
Behroozi et al. 2019a
)
. This approach is considerably more efficient than most SAMs and permits expansive MCMCs that span the full parameter space, although these models are still more expensive than the HOD/CLF, and require high-resolution N-body simulations with substructure merger trees.

In this paper, we introduce DiffstarPop, a new model for the statistical connection between halo MAH and galaxy SFH. DiffstarPop relies on a fully parametric model for dark matter halo growth, Diffmah
(
Hearin et al. 2021
)
, which approximates the MAH of an individual halo with parameters
\theta_{\rm MAH};
DiffstarPop further relies on a fully parametric model for individual galaxy SFH, Diffstar
(
Alarcon et al. 2023
)
, which is built on top of Diffmah and approximates each galaxy SFH with additional parameters
\theta_{\rm SFH};
DiffstarPop is a parametric model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}),
the probability that a halo with
\theta_{\rm MAH}
hosts a galaxy with
\theta_{\rm SFH}.
The individual galaxy SFH model, Diffstar, is analogous to other parametric forms used in the literature such as a delayed exponential
(
Sandage 1986
)
, a lognormal
(
Gladders et al. 2013
;
Diemer et al. 2017
)
, or a double power-law
(
Behroozi et al. 2013b
;
Ciesla et al. 2017
;
Carnall et al. 2018
)
. However, one feature that distinguishes Diffstar from these and other such models is that each of its parameters
\theta_{\rm SFH}
have a direct interpretation in terms of galaxy formation physics, such as star formation efficiency,
\epsilon_{\rm ms},
and a possible quenching event at time
t_{\rm q}.
Another important difference is that Diffstar is built upon Diffmah, linking galaxy growth directly to the underlying halo mass assembly history.

DiffstarPop leverages the Diffstar formulation of the SFH–MAH relationship to provide a physics-based parameterization of galaxy–halo connection. A key technical advance of our approach is that DiffstarPop is a fully differentiable model implemented in the JAX autodiff library
(
Bradbury et al. 2018
)
, which enables us to use gradient-based algorithms for optimization and inference, and to harness the computational power of GPUs. As we show below, this aspect of our model plays a central role in the principal aim of this paper: stress-testing the flexibility of DiffstarPop to reproduce the statistical population of galaxy SFHs predicted by three of the leading models in the field, IllustrisTNG, Galacticus, and UniverseMachine.

This paper is organized as follows. In §
2
we describe the simulations we use in this work. In §
3
we describe the DiffstarPop galaxy-halo connection model in detail. In §
4
we show the results. We discuss our findings in §
5
, and we conclude in §
6
with a summary of our primary results. The appendices contain more granular technical information than the main body, as well as a more comprehensive collection of validation plots.

Simulated Galaxies

In this paper, we stress-test the flexibility of DiffstarPop by assessing its ability to approximate statistical distributions of galaxy SFHs seen in three very different simulated populations: the IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical simulation
(
Pillepich et al. 2018a
)
, the Galacticus semi-analytic model
(
Benson 2012
)
, and the UniverseMachine semi-empirical model
(
Behroozi et al. 2019b
)
. In this section, we describe each of these sources of simulated SFHs in turn.

IllustrisTNG is a suite of hydrodynamical simulations that models how dark matter, gas, and stars evolve in a cosmological context
(
Weinberger et al. 2017
;
Pillepich et al. 2018a
)
. We use publicly available data from TNG300-1
(
Nelson et al. 2018
)
, which was generated by using
Arepo

(
Springel 2010
)
to solve for the evolution of
2500^{3}
gas tracers together with the same number of dark matter particles in a periodic box of
302.6
Mpc on a side evolving under
Planck
2015 cosmology. The mass resolution for dark matter and gas is 5.9 and
1.1\times 10^{7}{\rm M}_{\odot}
, respectively. For the IllustrisTNG halo catalogs and merger trees we use in this work,
halos and subhalos were identified with
SUBFIND

(
Springel et al. 2001
)
, and merger treers were constructed with
SUBLINK

(
Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2015
)
.

Galacticus is a semi-analytic model of galaxy formation that predicts the star formation history of a galaxy by numerically solving a coupled ODE system that regulates how gas and stars evolve in the gravitational potential of a dark matter halo
(
Benson 2012
)
. To generate the halo merger trees for Galacticus, we use the Extended Press Schechter (EPS) formalism
(
Press & Schechter 1974
;
Bower 1991
;
Bond et al. 1991
;
Parkinson et al. 2008
)
, in particular the EPS trees developed in
Benson et al. 2019
and implemented in Galacticus.
The merger trees are constructed for halos with root masses in the range
1.0\times 10^{11}{\rm M}_{\odot}
to
1.78\times 10^{14}{\rm M}_{\odot}
, with a minimum progenitor mass resolution of
2.5\times 10^{9}{\rm M}_{\odot}
and a fractional mass resolution of
8.33\times 10^{-5}
relative to the root halo mass. The cosmology adopted follows
Planck
2018 results
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
, with
H_{0}=67.36\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}=0.3153
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.6847
, and
\Omega_{\mathrm{b}}=0.0493
.

For UniverseMachine, we use a population of simulated halos from the Small MultiDark Planck (SMDPL) simulation
(
Klypin et al. 2016
)
.
The SMDPL simulation was run using the
l-gadget-2
code, a modified version of the publicly available
gadget-2
code
(
Springel 2005
)
. The simulation was run by gravitationally evolving dark matter particles of mass
8.14\times 10^{8}{\rm M}_{\odot}
in a periodic with side length of 590 Mpc in a flat
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
cosmology with the following parameters:
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}=0.307
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.693
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{b}}=0.048
,
h=0.6777
,
\sigma_{8}=0.8228
, and
n_{s}=0.96

(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2014
)
.
Dark matter (sub)halos were identified using
rockstar

(
Behroozi et al. 2013a
)
, and merger trees were generated using
Consistent Trees

(
Behroozi et al. 2013c
)
. Galaxy SFHs were generated by running the publicly available UniverseMachine source code
1
1

1

https://bitbucket.org/pbehroozi/universemachine
with the default best-fitting parameters from DR1 associated with
Behroozi et al. 2019b
.

For the case of the UniverseMachine and Galacticus models, the model formulation makes it simple to separately predict and extract the
in-situ
component of star formation, which includes only stellar mass formed by integrating the main progenitor of the (sub)halo, from the
ex-situ
component, which includes a contribution from stellar mass brought in with merged satellite galaxies. For IllustrisTNG, the in-situ and ex-situ components of star formation are not as cleanly separable or as straightforward to extract. We adopt the same definitions of in-situ and ex-situ stars as used in
Rodriguez-Gomez et al. 2016
;
Pillepich et al. 2018b
;
Tacchella et al. 2019
, based on the baryonic merger tree reconstruction method of
SUBLINK
, and in our results for IllustrisTNG we only consider in-situ SFH.

Throughout this paper, we quote numerical values for halo mass
M_{\rm halo}
and stellar mass
M_{\star}
assuming the value of
h
in the simulation under consideration, using units of
{\rm M}_{\odot}
everywhere. For example, in a simulation with
h=0.6777
that listed
M_{\star}=1\times 10^{10}{\rm M}_{\odot}/h
, we would quote
M_{\star}\simeq 1.48\times 10^{10}{\rm M}_{\odot}
. The Diffmah fits in our analysis were each derived based on
M_{\rm halo}(t)
assuming the value of
h
and
t_{0}
in the cosmology of the simulation, where
t_{0}
is the redshift-zero age of the universe; the Diffstar fits to
\dot{M}_{\star}(t)
similarly assume
h
in the simulated cosmology, as well as the cosmic baryon fraction,
f_{\rm b}.

In all equations and figures, we use the notation log(x) to denote the base-10 logarithm.

3
DiffstarPop Model Formulation

In this section, we give a high-level overview of the DiffstarPop formulation of the galaxy–halo connection; the appendices contain a complete technical description of the model, as well as the validation exercises we have carried out to stress-test our formulation.

In §
3.1
, we outline the Diffmah model for the mass assembly (MAH) of an individual dark matter halo,
M_{\rm halo}(t);
Diffmah is a fully parametric model with behavior determined by parameters
\theta_{\rm MAH}.

In §
3.2
, we describe the Diffstar model for
\dot{M}_{\star}(t),
the star formation history (SFH) of an individual galaxy.
Diffstar is a fully parametric SFH model with parameters
\theta_{\rm SFH}
that have a direct interpretation in terms of galaxy formation physics.
DiffstarPop is a model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}),
the population of Diffstar galaxies that occupy dark matter halos. DiffstarPop has its own parameters,
\psi_{\rm SFH},
that determine how the PDF of
\theta_{\rm SFH}
varies with
\theta_{\rm MAH}.
In Section
3.3
, we outline the DiffstarPop parametrization for
P_{\psi_{\rm SFH}}(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}).

3.1
Diffmah model of individual halo MAH

Diffmah is a parametric model for the mass assembly history (MAH) of individual halos. In particular, Diffmah approximates the evolution of
cumulative peak
halo mass,
M_{\rm p}(t),
defined as the largest mass the main progenitor halo has ever attained up until the time
t,
so that
M_{\rm p}(t)
is a non-decreasing function for all
t.
For a typical central halo,
M_{\rm p}(t)\approx M_{\rm halo}(t),
but if a halo experiences either a temporary or sustained period of mass loss,
M_{\rm p}(t)
remains constant.

We model
M_{\rm p}(t)
with a power-law function of cosmic time with a rolling index,

\displaystyle M_{\rm p}(t)=M_{0}(t/t_{0})^{\alpha(t)},

(1)

where
t_{0}
is the present-day age of the universe,
M_{0}
is a normalization parameter, and the behavior of
\alpha(t)
is controlled by a sigmoid function:

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\alpha_{\rm early}+\frac{\alpha_{\rm late}-\alpha_{\rm early}}{1+\exp(-k(t-\tau_{\rm c}))}.

(2)

At early times,
t\ll\tau_{\rm c},
the power law slope
\alpha(t)\approx\alpha_{\rm early},
and conversely,
\alpha(t)\approx\alpha_{\rm late}
at late times
t\gg\tau_{\rm c};
the sigmoid function in Eq.
2
smoothly transitions
\alpha(t)
between these two regimes.

Figure 1:
Diffmah model of individual halo growth.
The solid blue curve shows the mass assembly history (MAH) of an individual dark matter halo in the SMDPL simulation. The dashed black curve shows the Diffmah approximation of the simulated halo MAH. The dotted vertical line shows
t_{\rm p},
the Diffmah parameter encoding the time at which halo growth is arrested.

In
Hearin et al. 2021
, the above model was fit to millions of simulated merger trees in both gravitational N-body simulations as well as IllustrisTNG, and it was shown that the Diffmah model gives an unbiased fit with
0.1
dex of scatter across a wide range of halo mass,
10^{11}\lesssim M_{\rm halo}/{\rm M}_{\odot}\lesssim 10^{15},
and redshift,
0\leq z\lesssim 5.
In this paper, we introduce a new Diffmah parameter,
t_{\rm p},
defined as the cosmic time when the mass of the halo peaks, and remains constant thereafter:

\displaystyle M_{\rm p}(t)=\begin{cases}M_{0}(t/t_{0})^{\alpha(t)}&t\leq t_{\rm p}\\
M_{0}(t_{\rm p}/t_{0})^{\alpha(t_{\rm p})}&t\geq t_{\rm p}\end{cases}

(3)

As described in Appendix
A
, the new parameter
t_{\rm p}
improves the quality of the Diffmah fits for subhalos with early infall times. Figure
1
shows a visual demonstration of an example Diffmah fit to a particular halo in the SMDPL simulation described in §
2
.

We refer the reader to
Hearin et al. 2021
and Appendix
A
for further details about the Diffmah model.

3.2
Diffstar model of individual galaxy SFH

Diffstar is a fully parametric model for
\dot{M}_{\star}(t),
the star formation history (SFH) of an individual galaxy, introduced by
Alarcon et al. 2023
. In this work, we present an updated version of the model, that substantially improves its accuracy, speed (by a factor of 10), and memory efficiency (by a factor of 10). Here, we summarize the physical basis and structure of the Diffstar model, while in Appendix
B
we give more details of the changes introduced, as well as demonstrations of its flexibility and accuracy.

In the basic physical picture underlying Diffstar,
baryonic matter becomes available for star formation at a rate determined by the growth rate of the dark matter halo:

\displaystyle M_{\rm g}^{\rm acc}(t)=f_{\rm b}\,M_{\rm p}(t),

(4)

where
f_{\rm b}
is the cosmic baryon fraction,
M_{\rm g}^{\rm acc}(t)
is the cumulative accretion history of baryonic material from the field, and
M_{\rm p}(t)
is the halo mass assembly history. In Diffstar, we use the Diffmah model to approximate
M_{\rm p}(t)
, Eq.
1
.

Once gas is accreted into the dark matter halo, we assume that only a fraction of it is converted into stars, and that this conversion is a gradual process that may take several Gyr
(
Semenov et al. 2017
;
Semenov et al. 2018
, e.g.)
. We approximate the star formation rate of a main sequence galaxy by multiplying the cumulative accretion rate history of baryonic material from the field,
M_{\rm g}^{\rm acc}(t)
, by an efficiency parameter with units of inverse time,
\epsilon_{\rm ms}
:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}M_{\star}^{\rm ms}(t)}{{\rm d}t}=\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p}(t))\,M_{\rm g}^{\rm acc}(t)

(5)

For instance, a galaxy that at a given time
t
has accreted a total of
M_{\rm g}^{\rm acc}(t)=10^{10}\,{\rm M}_{\odot}
and has an efficiency of
\epsilon_{\rm ms}=10^{-9}\,\rm{yr}^{-1}
, would have a star formation rate of
{\rm d}M_{\star}^{\rm ms}(t)/{\rm d}t=10\,{\rm M}_{\odot}/\rm{yr}^{-1}
.

We further assume that
\epsilon_{\rm ms}
depends solely on the halo mass at the time of star formation,
\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p}(t))
, peaking at a characteristic mass,
M_{\rm crit}
, and decreasing monotonically at both lower and higher masses:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})=\epsilon_{\rm crit}\cdot(M_{\rm p}/M_{\rm crit})^{\beta(M_{\rm p})}

(6)

In Eq.
6
, we model
\beta(M_{\rm p})
with another sigmoid (see Eq.
2
). The efficiency attains its critical value of
\epsilon_{\rm crit}
when
M_{\rm p}=M_{\rm crit},
where
M_{\rm crit}\approx 10^{12}M_{\odot}
for typical galaxies.

Some galaxies experience a quenching event that shuts down star formation; in Diffstar, we capture this phenomenon through the
quenching function,

F_{\rm q}(t),
which acts as a multiplicative factor on the star formation rate:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}M_{\star}(t)}{{\rm d}t}=F_{\rm q}(t)\times\frac{{\rm d}M_{\star}^{\rm ms}(t)}{{\rm d}t}.

(7)

As described in Section 3.5 in
Alarcon et al. 2023
, we use a sigmoid function to define the behavior of
F_{\rm q}(t)
as a smooth logarithmic drop in star formation rate. To parameterize a quenching event in Diffstar, the parameter
t_{\rm q}
is the time at which the quenching event reaches
q_{\rm drop},
its maximum suppression of SFR; an additional parameter
q_{\rm dt}
controls how quickly the SFR drops to its asymptotic value. The rejuvenation of star formation of previously-quenched galaxies has been shown to be a rather common phenomenon in the Magneticum hydrodynamical simulation
(
Fortune et al. 2025
)
, as well as in UniverseMachine
(
Alarcon et al. 2023
)
.
Observations indicate that rejuvenation events contribute a small fraction (
<10\%
) of the total stellar mass of a galaxy, that they might be more likely in disk-like galaxies, and the rejuvenation fraction of different samples ranges from 10% to 30% depending on the selection method
(
Chauke et al. 2019
;
Mancini et al. 2019
;
Tacchella et al. 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Tanaka et al. 2024
;
Wan et al. 2025
)
.
To capture this effect in Diffstar, a quenched galaxy has parametric freedom to experience rejuvenation that drives its SFR back to the main sequence: the parameter
q_{\rm rejuv}
controls the level of rejuvenation.

Figure
2
demonstrates an example fit of Diffstar to a particular galaxy in UniverseMachine. In
Alarcon et al. 2023
, it was shown that Diffstar is sufficiently flexible to accurately approximate simulated SFHs in UniverseMachine and IllustrisTNG. We give further demonstrations of this flexibility in Appendix
B
, in which we show that Diffstar is also able to give a good approximation to SFHs in Galacticus.

3.3
DiffstarPop model of the SFH of galaxy populations

In this section, we give a high-level overview of the formulation of DiffstarPop. For a more detailed description of the model, refer to Appendix
C
and the documentation of our publicly available source code
2
2

2

https://github.com/ArgonneCPAC/diffstar
.

DiffstarPop is a parametric model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}),
the probability that a dark matter halo with MAH given by
\theta_{\rm MAH}
hosts a galaxy with SFH given by
\theta_{\rm SFH}.
With a model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}),
one can directly populate the merger trees of a cosmological N-body simulation with a galaxy SFH, which is one of our own primary applications.

We model
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH})
as a two-component multivariate normal distribution, consisting of a “quenched” population of galaxies that experiences a significant quenching event, and a “main sequence” population that never experiences such quenching. The DiffstarPop parameters
\psi_{\rm SFH}
encode how the mean and covariance of
P_{\rm ms}(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH})
and
P_{\rm q}(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH})
vary with halo properties
\theta_{\rm MAH}.

Figure 3:
Average critical main sequence efficiency vs. halo mass.
The solid curves show the average value of
\log\epsilon_{\rm crit}
as a function of present-day halo mass
m_{\rm p,0}
for UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG and Galacticus galaxies, obtained by fitting Diffstar to individual galaxies. The dashed curves shows the DiffstarPop approximation of this scaling relation, which are later used as the initial guess for our gradient descent optimizations. DiffstarPop contains ingredients capturing scaling relations such as this one for each of the eight parameters in
\theta_{\rm SFH}
, for both the mean and standard deviation.

Figure 4:
Quenched fraction vs. halo mass.
The solid curves show the average value of the fraction of galaxies that experience a quenching event (i.e.
t_{\rm q}<t_{0}
) as a function of present-day halo mass
m_{\rm p,0}
for UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG and Galacticus galaxies (using only in-situ SFH). The dashed curves show the DiffstarPop approximation of this scaling relation, which are later used as the initial guess for our gradient descent optimizations. Central galaxies are shown on the top panel, and satellites on the bottom panel.

In the Diffstar formulation adopted here, there are eight Diffstar parameters
\theta_{\rm SFH}
: four parameters specifying the sigmoid we adopt for
\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})
, and four parameters defining a possible quenching and rejuvenation event
F_{\rm q}(t).
For each of these eight parameters, we adopt a scaling relation for how the mean value and the standard deviation of the parameter scales with present-day halo mass,
m_{\rm p,0}\equiv\log_{10}(M_{\rm p}(t_{0})).

To model the scaling of the mean relations
\langle\,\cdot\mid m_{\rm p,0}\rangle
, we generally adopt a linear dependence on
m_{\rm p,0}
, smoothly clipped at fixed bounds. For some parameters, however, we use a
sigslope
model, in which the slope of the linear relation transitions smoothly between two values via a sigmoid function. Figure
3
provides an example, illustrating how the scaling of
\log\epsilon_{\rm crit}
with
m_{\rm p,0}
in the DiffstarPop model compares to that measured from the simulations. Further implementation details are provided in Appendix
C
.

Since we have four parameters controlling the main sequence efficiency, there are four scaling relations controlling
\langle\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})|m_{\rm p,0}\rangle
. However, we have independent scaling relations of
\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})
for our two subpopulations, “main sequence” or “quenched”, which are defined based on whether their Diffstar quenching time parameter
t_{\rm q}
is lower/greater than the present time
t_{0}
. Then, there are four scaling relations controlling
\langle\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})|m_{\rm p,0},t_{\rm q}<t_{0}\rangle
, for the main sequence population, and four scaling relations controlling
\langle\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm p})|m_{\rm p,0},t_{\rm q}>t_{0}\rangle
for the quenched population. For the quenched population, there are an additional four scaling relations that control
\langle F_{\rm q}(t)|m_{\rm p,0}\rangle.
For the covariance of the quenched and main sequence multivariate normal distributions, we assume there is no correlation between the different parameters, and the scaling relation of the standard deviation of different parameters, e.g.
\sigma(\log\epsilon_{\rm crit}|m_{\rm p,0})
, has a linear dependence with
m_{\rm p,0}
.

As our model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH})
is a bimodal distribution, it is also necessary to specify the relative weighting of the two populations, i.e., the
quenched fraction
, varies with halo properties. We model the quenched fraction with a sigmoid function of
m_{\rm p,0}
.
Figure
4
shows a comparison between DiffstarPop and simulations for the quenched fraction as a function of
m_{\rm p,0}
, for both central and satellite galaxies. The dashed curves show the DiffstarPop approximation of these different scaling relations, which are later used as the initial guess for our gradient descent optimizations run on each simulation. For more technical details of the formulation and implementation, we refer the interested reader to Appendix
C
and our publicly available code.

In addition, we introduce an explicit dependence in DiffstarPop on a secondary halo property: the Diffmah parameter
t_{\rm p}
. As discussed earlier, this parameter represents the time when the halo mass peaks, and serves as a tracer of halos that experience “arrested development”
(
Smith et al. 2024
, see, e.g.,)
. We incorporate
t_{\rm p}
-dependence into two separate aspects of the model.
First, we modify the quenched fraction by allowing two of its sigmoid parameters (
y_{\rm lo}
and
x_{0}
) to themselves be sigmoids that vary with
t_{\rm p}
. Second, we model the mean of the quenching time

5
Discussion

In this paper, we have developed a new model for cosmological populations of galaxy star formation history (SFH), DiffstarPop; the model can be used to paint galaxy SFHs onto merger trees in cosmological simulations. We have stress-tested the ability of DiffstarPop to capture simulated populations of galaxy SFH predicted by the hydrodynamical simulation IllustrisTNG, the semi-analytic model Galacticus, and the semi-empirical model UniverseMachine. We have focused in particular on validating our model based on its ability to faithfully reproduce the stellar-to-halo-mass PDF,
P(M_{\star}|M_{\rm h},z),
and the
{\rm sSFR}-M_{\star}
PDF,
P({\rm sSFR}|M_{\star},z),
because these conditional PDFs are closely linked to luminosity functions and color PDFs that are well-measured by current and near-future galaxy surveys
(
Lower et al. 2020
;
Weaver et al. 2022
;
Deger et al. 2025
;
Davies et al. 2025
;
Aufort et al. 2025
;
Shuntov et al. 2025
)
. In Section
4
and Appendix
D
, we showed that DiffstarPop is able to reproduce each simulation’s predictions for these distributions with a typical Kullback–Leibler divergence of 0.01–0.02 for the
M_{\star}
PDFs and 0.02–0.03 for the
{\rm sSFR}
PDFs, and with an accurate recovery of both the mean and variance of these quantities across a wide range in halo mass and redshift.

Using merger trees from
N
-body simulations, or cosmologically representative populations of dark matter halo assembly histories generated by DiffmahPop
(
Hearin et al. 2021
)
, DiffstarPop can produce self-consistent, cosmologically representative populations of galaxy star formation histories (SFHs). We emphasize that the diversity in these populations of MAHs and SFHs does not arise from adding random scatter around a mean relation. Instead, we explicitly parametrize the full statistical distributions of the Diffmah and Diffstar parameters that govern individual halo assembly and in-situ star formation rates. As a result, the model reproduces not only mean scaling relations, such as the stellar-to-halo mass relation, but also a cosmologically realistic diversity of individual halo and star formation trajectories.

The smooth evolutionary trajectories of halo and galaxy growth in DiffstarPop are not intended to reproduce very-short-term stochastic fluctuations in SFH. When such SFH fluctuations occur long before the time of observation, there is a minimal impact on the statistical distributions of broadband galaxy colors
(
Chaves-Montero & Hearin 2021
, e.g.)
; and such MAH fluctuations have only percent-level impact on halo clustering
(
Hearin et al. 2021
)
. However, a large SFH fluctuation can indeed influence the spectrum of a galaxy when it occurs very close to the time of observation; in closely related work, we have incorporated this effect into the DSPS library using a new model for burstiness introduced in
Zacharegkas et al. 2025
, and we have built a population-level model for this burstiness ingredient as part of Diffsky.

In this paper, we have also neglected to directly model the role of major halo mergers, which can impact the SFH of satellite galaxies that eventually merge with their associated central. To account for these effects, we have developed Diffmerge, a model that incorporates ex-situ star formation into the Diffstar framework. Diffmerge describes how the stellar mass formed in a satellite galaxy is deposited into its associated central, a crucial step for producing the most massive galaxies in the Universe. As shown in Appendix
D
, DiffstarPop can already approximate in- plus ex-situ SFHs through an effectively higher main-sequence efficiency. However, explicitly linking the additional quenching of satellite galaxies to their merging histories will be essential for accurately predicting the small-scale clustering of satellites
(
Moster et al. 2018
;
Behroozi et al. 2019b
)
, and so we will explore this effect in follow-up work
(
Beltz-Mohrmann
et al. 2026a
)
.

Of course, DiffstarPop is only an approximate model of the statistical distribution of galaxy SFH, and so cannot even in principle fully capture the complexity of SFHs in the galaxy populations in any of these simulations. However, our ultimate goal is not to create a statistical replica of these simulations; instead, with DiffstarPop we aim to analyze the SFHs of observed galaxy samples. Observational measurements of the SED and photometry of a galaxy can only constrain a limited amount of information about its SFH
(
Ocvirk et al. 2006
;
Carnall et al. 2019
;
Leja et al. 2019
;
Lower et al. 2020
;
Iyer et al. 2024
)
, and so the residual errors in the ability of DiffstarPop to approximate our target simulations may be subdominant compared to other sources of uncertainty in the problem, such as dust attenuation
(
Jones et al. 2022
;
Jones & Stanway 2023
;
Hahn & Melchior 2025
)
, or variations in the stellar spectral library, initial mass function (IMF), metallicity or burstiness prescriptions
(
Haskell et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Tortorelli et al. 2024
;
Jones et al. 2025
;
Bellstedt & Robotham 2025
;
Wang et al. 2025
)
. In ongoing work, we are using DiffstarPop in a larger pipeline, Diffsky: a simulation-based forward model of the SEDs and photometry of galaxy populations. Diffsky includes additional modeling ingredients that are needed to predict the SEDs of galaxy populations, such as a probabilistic model for dust attenuation curves, and a model for short-timescale SFH fluctuations, i.e., burstiness. In follow-up work, we will use these additional models to quantify the success of DiffstarPop in terms of its ability to analyze and interpret observations of the SEDs and photometry of galaxy populations measured by surveys such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time
(
Ivezić et al. 2019
, LSST,)
, the Nancy Grace Roman Telescope
3
3

3

https://roman.gsfc.nasa.gov
, and Euclid
(
Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025
)
. Additionally, DiffstarPop can be used as a prior on the Diffstar parameters when fitting the SEDs of individual galaxies with SPS models. This approach allows us to derive Bayesian constraints on the physical properties of observed galaxies while incorporating assumptions about the physics of galaxy formation derived from complex models such as UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG, or Galacticus, or by constraints from observational data.

We have previously used a prototype version of DiffstarPop to help create the publicly available synthetic galaxies in OpenUniverse2024
(
OpenUniverse et al. 2025
)
, a cross-collaboration effort between LSST and Roman. More recently, we used the model to populate the Discovery simulations
(
Beltz-Mohrmann et al. 2025
)
, tailored to investigate the way in which evolving dark energy impacts galaxy formation.
We have similarly used the model to help create mock galaxy catalogs for the DESI collaboration as part of the Emulator Mock Challenge
(
Beltz-Mohrmann
et al. 2026b
)
. Our work in this area is comparable to closely related efforts in the field based on SAMs, which have been used for many years to generate synthetic galaxy catalogs
(
Merson et al. 2013
;
Guo et al. 2013
, e.g.,)
. SAMs remain widely used to generate mocks for some of the most ambitious planned galaxy surveys; for example, the GAEA semi-analytic model is currently being used to create the Jiutian mocks
(
Han et al. 2025
)
for the China Space Survey Telescope (CSST) extra-galactic surveys
(
Cao et al. 2018
;
Gong et al. 2019
)
, and in
Zhai et al. 2021
Galacticus was used to create a synthetic galaxy lightcone for the Roman survey telescope.

In SAMs, mock galaxies and their SFHs are painted onto simulated merger trees by numerically deriving a solution to a coupled ODE system for each individual halo. DiffstarPop predictions are essentially parameterized families of solutions to these same ODE systems. For example, in DiffstarPop we directly parameterize
\epsilon_{\rm ms}(M_{\rm h}),
whereas star formation efficiency and its scaling with halo properties is an
emergent
phenomenon in SAMs. This formulation greatly simplifies the task of implementing the model in a library of automatic differentiation (autodiff) such as JAX
(
Bradbury et al. 2018
)
, which provides significant computational benefits.
First, our fully parametric formulation guarantees that the exact same amount of computational work is vectorized across the galaxy population; this is precisely the setup in which GPUs achieve their greatest potential to accelerate scientific computing
(see, e.g.,
Hearin et al. 2023
, for an application to stellar population synthesis with 300-400x speedup on GPUs)
.
Second, the availability of gradient information allows us to utilize advanced optimization algorithms to derive best-fitting model parameters; high-quality fits to our target PDFs are achievable with only a few hundred steps of gradient descent with modern learning rates.
While in the present work we have only leveraged gradient information to optimize our model, gradient-based inference algorithms, e.g., Hamiltonian Monte Carlo
(
Duane et al. 1987
, HMC,)
, and the No U-Turn Sampler
(
Hoffman & Gelman 2014
, NUTS,)
were recently used together with DSPS in
Zacharegkas et al. 2025
to derive Bayesian posteriors on the physical parameters of individual galaxies. Our future work will analogously deploy gradient-based algorithms to derive posteriors on the population-level parameters of DiffstarPop.

The recent literature includes several forward-modeling efforts that are closely related to the work presented here. In the GalSBI-SPS model
(
Fischbacher et al. 2025
;
Tortorelli et al. 2025
)
, analytical parameterizations are adopted so that galaxy redshifts and SPS properties can be sampled to generate populations. In PopSED
(
Li et al. 2024
)
and also in pop-cosmos
(
Alsing et al. 2024
)
, neural networks are used instead of analytical prescriptions to encode the form of the PDFs of SPS parameters. These population-level techniques have proven to be a powerful way to model cosmological redshift distributions
(
Leistedt et al. 2023
;
Thorp et al. 2024
)
, and study the evolution of star formation history
(
Deger et al. 2025
)
. DiffstarPop is a population-level model of galaxy SFH, not SPS properties, and so DiffstarPop on its own does not have the predictive power of these models. We discuss below our ongoing use of DiffstarPop as a key component in the Diffsky framework that additionally predicts SPS properties, but first we highlight a key distinction: DiffstarPop is a model of the galaxy–halo connection, whereas dark matter halos are absent from the galaxy populations generated with the aforementioned population-level models. This formulation makes it straightforward to directly graft our model into simulated populations of halos, one of the principal applications of this work. For example, we are currently using DiffstarPop to create galaxy populations with IllustrisTNG-like, UniverseMachine-like, and Galacticus-like SFHs, and populate large-volume, high-resolution N-body simulations such as Last Journey
(
Heitmann et al. 2021
)
with mock galaxy catalogs designed to support the analysis working groups in the DESI, LSST-DESC, and Roman collaborations. A straightforward extension of our current work would be to use the DiffstarPop framework to populate N-body simulations with galaxies that mimic the SFH distributions in GalSBI-SPS, pop-cosmos, and related models, which we aim to pursue in future work.

Models of the galaxy–halo connection based on machine learning (ML) offer another efficient and differentiable approach to making predictions for cosmological populations of galaxies. There are by now many examples of such applications in the field. In
Villaescusa-Navarro et al. 2021
, the authors introduced the CAMELS suite of hydrodynamical simulations that span a range of parameters in both cosmology and baryonic feedback; this suite has been used to train neural networks that can be used to make predictions for the galaxy population
(
de Santi et al. 2023
;
Hahn et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. An analogous program based on semi-analytic models, CAMELS-SAM
(
Perez et al. 2023
)
, is also well underway. Much larger-volume hydro simulations such as IllustrisTNG, EAGLE
(
Crain et al. 2015
)
, and Simba
(
Davé et al. 2019
)
have also been used to train neural networks to predict the galaxy population from an N-body simulation
(
Chen et al. 2021
;
Chittenden & Tojeiro 2023
;
Das et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

AI-based models have much more flexibility than DiffstarPop, since neural network architectures such as diffusion models or normalizing flows are essentially guaranteed to be able to accurately approximate almost any distribution
(
Tabak &
Vanden-Eijnden 2010
;
Tabak & Turner 2013
)
. This flexibility is a distinct advantage of AI-based models, which have demonstrated the ability to capture the detailed color PDFs exhibited by observed galaxy samples
(
Thorp et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. However, this flexibility comes at a cost: the neural networks used in population-level models typically have
N_{\rm dim}\gtrsim 10^{4}
or greater, which precludes the use of conventional Bayesian methods to quantify uncertainty on the best-fitting PDF
(although see
Behroozi 2025
, for a new approach to Bayesian sampling that makes this more practical)
. By contrast, the formulation of DiffstarPop presented here has 79 parameters, which is well within the capability of gradient-based samplers such as HMC and NUTS to quantify uncertainty with converged chains
(
Hoffman & Gelman 2014
, e.g.,)
. This advantage also comes at a cost: the physical assumptions underlying our comparatively low dimensionality could lead to biased predictions due to our comparative inflexibility. To confront this concern, we have stress-tested the flexibility of our model using three very different galaxy simulations: IllustrisTNG, UniverseMachine, and Galacticus. While we consider the results shown in §
4
and Appendix
D
to be a promising indication that our model is suitably formulated, programmatically validating DiffstarPop and each ingredient in the Diffsky framework will be a continued effort in future work.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have introduced DiffstarPop, a model for the galaxy–halo connection that makes predictions for the statistical connection between the star formation history (SFH) of galaxies and the mass assembly history (MAH) of their parent halos. We summarize our primary results below.

•

We have shown that DiffstarPop is sufficiently flexible to capture the statistical distributions of galaxy SFHs of three very different simulations: IllustrisTNG, Galacticus, and UniverseMachine. In particular, DiffstarPop is able to accurately recover the distribution of stellar masses,
P(M_{\star}|M_{\rm h},z),
and the distribution of specific star formation rates,
P({\rm sSFR}|M_{\star},z),
across a wide range of halo mass,
10^{11}\lesssim M_{\rm h}/{\rm M}_{\odot}\lesssim 10^{14.5}
, stellar masses
10^{9}\lesssim M_{\star}/{\rm M}_{\odot}\lesssim 10^{11.5}
and time,
0<z<3.

•

We have introduced a new version of Diffstar, a model for individual galaxy SFH. The updated version simplifies the description of how accreted gas transforms into stars. As shown in Appendix
B
, the updated model improves the description of stellar mass histories for UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG and Galacticus, and improves both the per-galaxy runtime and the memory efficiency by 10x. As a result, DiffstarPop can generate
10^{6}
galaxy SFHs in 1.1 CPU-seconds, or 0.03 GPU-seconds.

•

We have introduced a new version of Diffmah, a model for individual halo MAH. The updated version includes a new parameter,
t_{\rm p},
defined as the time at which the growth of the halo becomes arrested. As shown in Appendix
A
, the updated model improves the description of halo assembly for subhalos with early infall times.

•

DiffstarPop is publicly available as part of the
Diffstar
code:
https://github.com/ArgonneCPAC/diffstar
. Our source code includes Monte Carlo generators that supply statistical samples of galaxy and halo assembly histories.

The material in these appendices provides more detailed information about DiffstarPop than is contained in the main body, as well as more extensive comparisons of the model to target data from simulations. In Appendix
A
, we discuss the new
t_{\rm p}
parameter of the Diffmah model introduced in this work. We show results from fitting the SFH of individual galaxies in simulations with the Diffstar model in Appendix
B
. In Appendix
C
, we give a technical description of the mathematical formulation of DiffstarPop. We describe our techniques for fitting the parameters of DiffstarPop to various target PDFs in Appendix
D
.

Appendix A
Diffmah fits to individual halo MAH

In this appendix, we discuss the role of the new Diffmah parameter
t_{\rm p}
introduced in this work. We refer the reader to
Hearin et al. 2021
for a more comprehensive presentation of other aspects of the Diffmah model. For convenience, we begin with a brief overview of our methodology for deriving best-fitting parameters
\theta_{\rm MAH}
to the MAH of a simulated halo.

When fitting
\theta_{\rm MAH}
to simulated halos, we leverage the differentiable implementation of Diffmah and use the L-BFGS-B algorithm implemented in
scipy
to minimize the mean logarithmic difference between the simulated and predicted values of
M_{\rm h}(t)

(
Broyden 1970
;
Fletcher 1970
;
Goldfarb 1970
;
Shanno 1970
)
. When optimizing the parameters, we do not allow
\theta_{\rm MAH}
to take on arbitrary values, but instead we place physics-based priors on the parameter space. For example, a fundamental aspect of CDM structure growth is that dark matter halos experience a period of rapid growth at early times, and transition to a slow-accretion regime at late times
(
Bullock et al. 2001
;
Wechsler et al. 2002
;
Tasitsiomi et al. 2004
)
. When fitting Diffmah parameters to simulated MAHs, we therefore impose the following constraint:

\alpha_{\rm early}>\alpha_{\rm late}>0,

since we do not want the fitter to wander into an unphysical region of parameter space.

To implement these and other such constraints, we use invertible sigmoid functions
\mathcal{F}_{\rm bound}
to relate the physical Diffmah parameters to their unbounded counterparts,
\theta_{\rm MAH}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{\rm bound}(\tilde{\theta}_{\rm MAH}).
In finding best-fitting Diffmah approximations, we run our gradient descents in the unbounded parameter space
\tilde{\theta}_{\rm MAH},
which ensures that the best-fitting
\theta_{\rm MAH}
will respect the parameter space constraints. We highlight this aspect of our fitting algorithm since we use this technique throughout this work.

Appendix B
Diffstar model of individual galaxy SFH

In §
3.2
, we gave a high-level overview of the Diffstar model for individual galaxy SFH, and we showed an example fit to a simulated galaxy in UniverseMachine to illustrate the basic behavior of model. The Diffstar model was first introduced in
Alarcon et al. 2023
, which contains a detailed pedagogical description of its ingredients. As noted in §
3.2
, here we have presented an updated version of the model; however, its core physical principles remain the same. Specifically, Diffstar is built on the following physical principles:

1.

A smooth parametric form (Diffmah) to describe dark matter halo growth.

2.

Baryonic (gas) accretion tracks dark matter accretion via a cosmic baryon fraction factor.

3.

Freshly accreted gas doesn’t convert instantly into stars. Instead, the transformation of gas into stars occurs gradually over time.

4.

Only a fraction of each accreted gas parcel ever transforms into stars.

5.

Galaxies may experience a quenching event that shuts down star formation, and quenched galaxies may subsequently rejuvenate and resume star formation.

Motivated by results indicating the robustness of multi-Gyr gas depeletion timescales
(
Semenov et al. 2017
)
, in
Alarcon et al. 2023
, the gradual conversion of gas into stars (step 3 above) was modeled via a parameterized function,
\mathcal{F}_{\rm cons}(t|\tau_{\rm cons}),
the consumption function, whose shape was controlled by a free parameter,
\tau_{\rm cons}
, the gas consumption timescale.
In that formulation,
\mathcal{F}_{\rm cons}(t|\tau_{\rm cons})
explicitly describes how accreted gas becomes available for star formation over a timescale of
\tau_{\rm cons}
through a convolution of the gas accretion history,
dM_{\rm g}/dt
. In practice, this consumption function must be flexible enough to capture the diversity of gas-processing behaviors found across different galaxy formation models and hydrodynamical simulations. Distinct physical prescriptions can potentially lead to markedly different gas depletion histories. If the functional form of the consumption kernel is too rigid, Diffstar SFHs will not accurately reproduce the underlying trends of a given simulation or observed dataset.

In our updated formulation of the Diffstar model, we have removed this explicitly parameterized gas consumption function entirely. Instead, we directly compute the total accumulated baryonic mass available for star formation at each time, and quantified the efficiency with which gas is turned into stars as a timescale (with units of inverse time). As a result, variations in gas consumption arise naturally from the interplay between the evolving gas accretion history and the time-dependent efficiency, allowing recently accreted gas parcels to exhibit distinct consumption patterns from earlier-accreted material. We adopt the same parameterization for the scaling of efficiency with instantaneous halo mass as in
Alarcon et al. 2023
. The efficiency peaks at a characteristic mass
M_{\rm crit}
(
\sim 10^{12}M_{\odot}
for typical galaxies), and decreases monotonically at both lower and higher masses. A caveat of this approach is that, in reality, gas that has already been transformed into stars is no longer available for subsequent star formation. Our formulation neglects this depletion, and therefore the inferred efficiencies will be systematically lower, effectively absorbing the impact of the missing gas reservoir.

These updates to the Diffstar formulation provide a simpler and more efficient foundation for modeling galaxy SFHs while preserving the model’s physical interpretability. The new approach no longer requires specifying an uncertain gas consumption function and achieves higher accuracy in reproducing stellar mass and star formation histories across UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG, and Galacticus, as shown in Figure
B1
. In addition, removing the convolution step yields more than an order-of-magnitude improvement in both runtime and memory efficiency (Figure
B2

In
Alarcon et al. 2023

Appendix C
DiffstarPop model formulation

This appendix contains a technical description of the formulation of DiffstarPop. As specified in Section
3
, DiffstarPop is a parametric model for
P(\theta_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH}),
the probability that a dark matter halo with MAH given by
\theta_{\rm MAH}
hosts a galaxy with SFH given by
\theta_{\rm SFH}
. The Diffstar parameters are based on ingredients of galaxy formation, and therefore they are bounded by limits that enforce a physical galaxy SFH. These limits are enforced by a set of sigmoid-bounding functions,
\mathcal{B}(x)
, that relate the bounded, physical diffstar parameters
\theta_{\rm SFH}
, to an unbounded space,
\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH}
, i.e.
\theta_{\rm SFH}=\mathcal{B}(\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH})
, which is analytically invertible. For details see the
Diffstar
repository. The DiffstarPop model is defined in the unbounded space of Diffstar parameters,
\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH}
, as a Gaussian mixture model of two components, consisting of a “quenched” population of galaxies that experiences a significant quenching event before today (
t_{\rm q}<t_{0}
), and a “main sequence” population that never experiences such quenching,

\begin{split}P(\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH},\psi_{\rm SFH})=&(1-f_{\rm qch})\,P_{\rm ms}+f_{\rm qch}\,P_{\rm q};\\[5.0pt]
\text{with:}\qquad P_{\rm ms}=&P_{\rm ms}(\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH},\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm ms});\\
P_{\rm q}=&P_{\rm q}(\tilde{\theta}_{\rm SFH}|\theta_{\rm MAH},\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm q});\\
f_{\rm qch}=&f_{\rm qch}(\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm fqch}).\end{split}

(8)

The DiffstarPop parameters
\psi_{\rm SFH}=\{\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm ms},\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm q},\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm fqch}\}
specify how the mean and covariance of the Gaussian distributions
P_{\rm ms}
and
P_{\rm q}
, as well as the mixing parameter (the quenched fraction
f_{\rm qch}
), depend on the halo properties
\theta_{\rm MAH}
.

To develop an intuition for each scaling relation, we first fit the SFHs and MAHs of millions of simulated galaxies and their corresponding host halos with Diffstar and Diffmah, obtaining their best-fit
\theta_{\rm SFH}
and
\theta_{\rm MAH}
values, respectively. We then studied how the mean and standard deviation of each parameter scaled with present-day halo mass
m_{\rm p,0}
, both for quenched (
t_{\rm q}<t_{0}
) and main sequence (
t_{\rm q}>t_{0}
) galaxies, where
t_{\rm q}
is the Diffstar quenching time parameter, and
t_{0}
the present-day. Once the scaling relations were developed, we roughly calibrated them against these individual SFH fits, and we used those as our initial guess for the parameter calibration discussed in Appendix
D
and shown as part of our main results in Section
4
.

The mean and standard deviation of the Diffstar parameters, for both quenched and main sequence galaxies, approximately follow a linear relation with
m_{\rm p,0}
. To capture this trend, we model each with two free parameters: a slope and an intercept evaluated at
m_{\rm p,0}=12.5
. To prevent the linear model from producing unphysical or mathematically invalid values (such as a negative standard deviation), we use a “smoothly clipped line” model,
\mathrm{sclip}(x)
. This approach retains the linear form within a valid range but smoothly limits the output by imposing fixed minimum and maximum values on the mean and standard deviation, with bounds that are set a priori.

For most Diffstar parameters, the mean scaling relations
\langle\cdot\mid m_{\rm p,0}\rangle
are well described by a clipped linear dependence on halo mass. However, in our simulations we find that three parameters,
M_{\rm crit}
,
\epsilon_{\rm crit}
, and the quenching time
t_{\rm q}
, are better represented by a model in which the slope of the relation changes smoothly with
m_{\rm p,0}
. For example, the relation for
M_{\rm crit}
increases with halo mass at low
m_{\rm p,0}
and then flattens at higher
m_{\rm p,0}
, behavior that is not well captured by a single clipped linear model. To describe these trends, we use a four-parameter “sigmoid-slope” (sigslope) model, in which the slope of the relation transitions smoothly between two asymptotic values. This model both matches the forms seen in the simulations and yields improved fits in DiffstarPop compared to a clipped linear model. Figure
3
shows an example of this behavior for
\langle\log\epsilon_{\rm crit}\mid m_{\rm p,0}\rangle
measured in UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG, and Galacticus, along with the corresponding best-fit sigslope relations from DiffstarPop. The same sigslope parameterization is used for
M_{\rm crit}
and for
t_{\rm q}
, and it can be described as:

\langle M_{\rm crit}|m_{\rm p,0}\rangle=b+S(M_{\rm crit},x_{0},k,m_{\rm lo},m_{\rm hi})(M_{\rm crit}-x_{0})

(9)

where
S(x)
is a Sigmoid as in Eq.
2
, we fix
k=3
, and
\{b,x_{0},m_{\rm lo},m_{\rm hi}\}
are the four free parameters of this model, with
b
being the intercept at
x_{0}
, the latter being also the value where the sigmoid transitions from slope
m_{\rm lo}
to
m_{\rm hi}
.

Overall, to model the means and standard deviations of the quenched (8 diffstar parameters) and main sequence (4 parameters) subpopulations of all of these parameters, we end up introducing a total of 58 DiffstarPop
\psi_{\rm SFH}
parameters that depend only on
m_{\rm p,0}
. In the next subsection, we describe the model for the quenched fraction and show that the quenching time further depends on the halo formation time, characterized by
t_{\rm p}
.

m_{\rm p,0}

compares the quenched fractions measured from UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG and Galacticus, along with the best-fit sigmoid approximation from DiffstarPop.

t_{\rm p}
within DiffstarPop.
Specifically, we allow two of the sigmoid parameters, (
y_{\rm lo}
and
x_{0}

t_{\rm p}

\begin{split}f_{\rm qch}(m_{\rm p,0}|\psi_{\rm SFH}^{\rm fqch})&=S(m_{\rm p,0},x_{0},k,f_{\rm lo},f_{\rm hi}),\\[5.0pt]

\end{split}

(10)

Appendix D
Fitting DiffstarPop to Simulated Galaxy Populations

In Section
4
we presented fits of DiffstarPop to the UniverseMachine, IllustrisTNG, and Galacticus simulations using in-situ only star formation histories (SFHs).
Here, we describe how these data were processed and how we performed differentiable fits to their probability density functions (PDFs).
For complete technical details on differentiable PDF fitting, we refer the reader to previous works by
Hearin et al. 2021
,
Alarcon et al. 2023
, and
Stevanovich et al. 2023
.
Below we summarize the key steps relevant to the present analysis.

D.1
Target Data Processing

The first step is to use Diffstar and Diffmah to fit each halo with
m_{\rm p,0}>11
in every simulation, obtaining its best-fit SFH and mass accretion history (MAH) parameters,
\theta_{\rm SFH}
and
\theta_{\rm MAH}
.
From these fits, we predict the star formation history and cumulative stellar mass history for each halo using Diffstar. When predicting the SFH of each galaxy with Diffstar, whether for individual haloes in the target data or for model predictions with DiffstarPop, we clip the Diffstar SFHs to a minimum value of
{\rm sSFR}(t_{\rm obs})=10^{-12}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}
.
At a given observed redshift, we then bin halos according to their observed halo mass,
M_{\rm p}(t_{\rm obs})
, and use a histogram to compute the PDF of their stellar masses,
M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})
, yielding

P(M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})\,|\,M_{\rm p}(t_{\rm obs}))
. Similarly, we compute the specific star formation rate,
{\rm sSFR}(t_{\rm obs})=\dot{M}_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})/M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})
,
and construct its PDF as a function of
M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})
,
P({\rm sSFR}(t_{\rm obs})|M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs}))
.
For the sSFR distributions, we treat central and satellite galaxies separately.
We adopt 25 logarithmically spaced bins in the range
M_{\star}(t_{\rm obs})=10^{7}-10^{13}\,M_{\odot}
for the stellar-mass PDFs, and 29 logarithmically spaced bins in the range
{\rm sSFR}(t_{\rm obs})=10^{-13}-10^{-8}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}
for the sSFR PDFs.

D.2
Differentiable PDFs

To enable differentiable predictions of PDFs, we further process the data so that each galaxy contributes smoothly to multiple histogram bins.
If each galaxy contributed a discrete value of either 0 or 1 to a single bin, the gradient of any bin’s frequency with respect to an underlying quantity (e.g.
M_{\star}
) would be zero everywhere, leading to vanishing gradients with respect to the model parameters that control those quantities.

To address this, we assign each galaxy a probabilistic contribution across nearby bins using a
triweight
kernel centered at its observed value, with a width equal to one bin width.
The triweight function
(
Hearin et al. 2022
, see for example Appendix E in)
is a compact polynomial kernel with support restricted to the interval
[\mu-3\sigma,\mu+3\sigma]
. The polynomial coefficients are tuned so that the function shares the same mean and variance as a Gaussian, and vanishes outside that range with a vanishing derivative at the endpoints, so that the triweight kernel is essentially a differentiably clipped Gaussian.
We evaluate the contribution of each galaxy to a given bin by computing the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the triweight kernel at the bin edges and taking the difference.
Because both the kernel and its CDF are analytic polynomials, this operation is fast and fully differentiable.

As a result, the predicted PDFs from our model differ from the true PDFs by a convolution with a triweight kernel.
To ensure consistency, we apply the same smoothing procedure to the target (simulation) data before fitting.
Thus, all PDFs shown throughout this paper have been computed assuming a probabilistic contribution from each individual halo, ensuring that both the model and target PDFs are differentiable and directly comparable.

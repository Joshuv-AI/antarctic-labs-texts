Mono-anabelian Reconstruction of Number Fields with Restricted Ramification
Yu Mao, Xiao Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we apply Hoshi’s results in [ 4 ] and [ 5 ] , to establish a group-theoretic reconstruction of a number field K K together with its maximal unramified outside S S extension for some density 1 set of prime S S starting from the profinite group G K , S G_{K,S} .

1
Introduction

Anabelian geometry is roughly speaking, the study of schemes via their étale fundamental groups. The first result in anabelian geometry was proven in 1970s, known as the Neukirch-Uchida theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(The Neukirch-Uchida theorem)
.

Let
K,L
be number fields. Then the natural map

\text{Isom}(L^{\text{sol}}/L,K^{\text{sol}}/K)\to\text{Isom}(G_{K}^{\text{sol}},G_{L}^{\text{sol}})/\text{Inn}(G_{L}^{\text{sol}})

is bijective.

Later, Hoshi in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
established a group-theoretic algorithm, to recover a number field
K
together with its maximal pro-solvable extension
K^{\text{sol}}
group-theoretically starting from
G_{K}^{\text{sol}}
, i.e.

Theorem 1.2

(Hoshi,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
)
.

Let
G
be a profinite group isomorphic to
G_{K}^{\text{sol}}
for some number field
K
. Then there is a group-theoretic reconstruction to a solvably closed field
\widetilde{F}(G)
starting from
G
, and the fixed subfield
F(G):=\widetilde{F}(G)^{G}
such that:

(i) There exists an isomorphism
G\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{Gal}(\widetilde{F}(G)/F(G))
.

(ii) The following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{F}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\text{sol}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the top horizontal arrow is a Galois-equivariant isomorphism, vertical arrows are field embeddings and the bottom horizontal arrow is an isomorphism.

In anabelian geometry, we often call results like Theorem 1.1 bi-anabelian results, which roughly speaking, are results starting from an isomorphism between Galois groups/étale fundamental groups, ending up with an isomorphism between fields/schemes.

On the other hand, we call results like Theorem 1.2 mono-anabelian results, which roughly speaking, are results starting from an abstract profinite group
G
which is isomorphic to the (or some quotient of) étale fundamental group of some scheme
X
, ending up with a purely group-theoretic reconstruction
G\mapsto X(G)
of a scheme
X(G)
isomorphic to
X
.

A natural way to generalise Theorem 1.1 is to replace
G_{K}^{\text{sol}}
by
G_{K,S}
for suitable set of primes
S\subset\mathscr{P}_{K}
, which was proven by Shimizu in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
:

Theorem 1.3

(Shimizu,
[
14
]
Theorem 2.4)
.

Let
K,L
be number fields, and let
S_{K},S_{L}
be set of primes of
K,L
respectively. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)

The set of prime numbers determined by
S_{K}
(resp.
S_{L}
) has cardinality at least
2
.

(2)

For any finite Galois extension
K_{0}/K
or
L_{0}/L
contained in
K_{S_{K}}
or
L_{S_{L}}
, the natural density of the intersection between the set of prime numbers determined by
S_{K}
or
S_{L}
and the set of prime numbers splits completely in
K_{0}
or
L_{0}
is non-zero.

(3)

Assume condition (2). There exists a prime number
\ell
in the intersection of the set of prime numbers determined by
S_{K}
and the set of prime numbers determined by
S_{L}
,
S_{L}
or
S_{K}
satisfying
\star_{\ell}
condition respectively (c.f. Definition 1.16 in
[
13
]
).

Then the natural map

\text{Isom}(K_{S_{K}}/K,L_{S_{L}}/L)\to\text{Isom}(G_{L,S_{L}},G_{K,S_{K}})

is a bijection.

The main result of this paper is to develop a mono-anabelian version of Theorem 1.3 in the density 1 case (since conditions (1),(2),(3) in Theorem 1.3 holds true automatically in density
1
):

Theorem 1.4

(c.f. Theorem 5.6)
.

Let
G
be an abstract profinite group, isomorphic to
G_{K,S}
for some number field
K
and
S\subset\mathscr{P}_{K}
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\delta(S)=1
.

•

\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{inf}}
and
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\bullet}
are contained in
S
.

•

S
is conjugate-stable, i.e. if
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{P}_{K}
lies in
S
, so does all conjugates of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Then there exists a group-theoretic reconstruction of a field
F_{S}(G)
together with the action of
G
on
F_{S}(G)
by automorphism, and the fixed subfield
F(G):=F_{S}(G)^{G}
with Galois group
\text{Gal}(F_{S}(G)/F(G))\xrightarrow{\sim}G
, such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{S}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the top horizontal arrow is an isomorphism equivariant w.r.t
G\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{K,S}
, the vertical arrows are field embeddings and the bottom arrow is an isomorphism.

Structure of this paper
:

In section 2, we use the group-theoretic reconstruction of decomposition groups at primes in
S
as proved in Proposition 2.1 in
[
13
]
, we apply Theorem 1.4 in
[
4
]
to recover the local invariants at primes in

In section 3, we use the local reconstructions established in section 2 to recover

as a

-module, hence also the
G_{K,S}
-module
\Lambda(K_{S})
. Furthermore, we construct a suitable container

to contain

as a subgroup. Notice that we are unable to recover the

In section 4, we recover the maximal pro-solvable and unramified outside

at primes lying above

In section 5, we finish the reconstruction algorithm by characterising suitable subfields of the algebraic closure of completions of

is crucial. Finally, by applying Theorem 2.4 in
[

]
, we obtain natural isomorphisms between those subfields, and recover a global copy which is isomorphic to

We follow the strategy in
[

]
and
[

]
, but technically the proof of Theorem 1.4 in quite different from
[

]
.

Reconstruction of Local Invariants

In this section, we develop a group-theoretic reconstruction of local invariants as in Section 1 in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group. We say that
G
is of NF-type with restricted ramification if there exists a collection of data as follows:

(i) A number field
K
.

(ii) A subset
S\subset\mathscr{P}_{K}
such that
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{inf}}\subset S
and
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\bullet}\subset S
where
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\bullet}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{fin}}:\text{char}(\mathfrak{p})=2~\text{or}~e_{\mathfrak{p}}>1\}
.

(iii) The set
S
is conjugate-stable, that is for any prime number
\ell\in\mathfrak{Primes}
, if
\mathfrak{p}\in S
is lying above
\ell
, then all primes of
k
lying above
\ell
are also belong to
S
.

(iv) The maximal unramified outside
S
extension
K_{S}
of
K
.

(v) An isomorphism
\alpha:G\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{K,S}=\text{Gal}(K_{S}/K)
.

If
\delta(S)=1
, we we say that
G
is of NF-type with density
1
restricted ramification.

One checks immediately that open subgroups of profinite groups of NF-type with density 1 restricted ramification are again profinite groups of NF-type with density 1 restricted ramification.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a profinite group of NF-type with density 1 restricted ramification. Then there is a group-theoretic reconstruction of the
G
-set

\widetilde{S}(G)

such that there is an
\alpha
-equivariant bijection

\widetilde{S}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\{D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset G_{K,S}:\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\in S_{f}(K_{S})\}.

Moreover, we shall write
S(G):=\widetilde{S}(G)/G
. In this case, the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{S}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{f}(K_{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

This follows from Proposition 1.20 and Proposition 2.1 in
[
13
]
. Or alternatively, one combines Corollary 2.7 (ii) in
[

]
and Theorem 9.4.3 in

at primes in

3
Synchronisation of
\Sigma
-Cyclotomes

In this section, we develop a version of local-global sychronisation like Theorem 3.8 in our context. In particular, we are not able to recover the full cyclotome, but we can recover the
\Sigma
-part of the global cyclotome for the set of prime numbers
\Sigma
determined by
S
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group of NF-type with density 1 restricted ramification. Let
v\in S(G)
(c.f. Proposition 2.2). For each
D\in\widetilde{S}(G)
lying above
v
, we define

•

p_{v}:=p(D)
,
d_{v}:=d(D)
,
f_{v}:=f(D)
and
e_{v}:=e(D)
.

•

k^{\times}(v):=k^{\times}(D)
.

•

\mathcal{O}^{\times}(v):=\mathcal{O}^{\times}(D)
.

In particular,
\mathcal{O}^{\times}(v)
and
k^{\times}(v)
are independent from the choice of
D
lying above
v
(c.f. Proposition 3.7 in
[
4
]
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a profinite group of NF-type with density 1 restricted ramification. We write

\Sigma(G):=\{p\in\mathfrak{Primes}:\exists v\in S~s.t.~p_{v}=p\}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
S\subset\mathscr{P}_{K}
be a subset with
\delta(S)=1
and
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{inf}}\subset S
. We write

\mathcal{I}(K,S):=\bigcup_{S^{\prime}}(\prod_{v\in S^{\prime}}k^{\times}(v)\times\prod_{v\in S_{f}\setminus S^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}^{\times}(v))

where
S^{\prime}
ranges over all finite subsets of
S_{f}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
\mathscr{P}_{K}^{\text{inf}}\subset S\subset\mathscr{P}_{K}
be a subset with
\delta(S)=1
. Then the following assertions hold true:

(i) There exists a natural map

\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}}

obtained by forming the composite

\mathcal{I}(K,S)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{I}_{K}^{\text{fin}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{I}_{K}\twoheadrightarrow C_{K}\twoheadrightarrow G_{K}^{\text{ab}}\twoheadrightarrow G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}}

which coincide with the natural map
\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}}
obtained by forming the product of local Artin reciprocity maps
\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}:K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}\to G_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\text{ab}}
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{f}
.

(ii) It holds that

\text{ker}(\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}})_{\text{tor}}\subset\mu(K).

Moreover, if
K
is totally imaginary, then

\text{ker}(\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}})_{\text{tor}}=\mu(K).

(iii) It holds that

\mu(K_{S})=\varinjlim_{L}~\text{ker}(\mathcal{I}(L,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}})_{\text{tor}}

where
L
ranges over all finite extensions of
K
contained in
K_{S}
, and the transition maps are induced by transfer maps (c.f. Proposition 1.5.9 in
[
9
]
). In particular, there is a canonical identification

\varinjlim_{L}~\text{ker}(\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}})_{\text{tor}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1).

Proof.

Assertion (i) follows immediately from global class field theory. Now we verify assertion (ii).

Let
a\in\text{ker}(\mathcal{I}(K,S)\to G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}})_{\text{tor}}
. If the image of
a
in
G_{K}^{\text{ab}}
is trivial, then it follows immediately from Lemma 3.6 (iii) in
[
4
]
that
a\in K^{\times}
. Now suppose that the image of
a
in
G_{K}^{\text{ab}}
is non-trivial. By Theorem 8.3.13 of
[
9
]
, there exists an idele
\gamma\in\mathbb{I}_{K}
which is
1
at the primes in
S
and units at the primes not in
S
, such that
a\gamma
has image in
D_{K}
, where
D_{K}
is the kernel of the global reciprocity map
C_{K}\twoheadrightarrow G_{K}^{\text{ab}}
. By
[
9
]
, Theorem 8.2.5, we can write

a\gamma=x\cdot\beta\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{\lambda_{i}},

where
\beta
is a purely complex idele,
x\in K^{\times}
,
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
are viewed as ideles in
\mathbb{I}_{K}
via the natural embedding
K^{\times}\to\mathbb{I}_{K}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then for each finite prime
\mathfrak{p}
we have
x\in U_{\mathfrak{p}}
, therefore
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. The group generated by the
\epsilon_{i}
’s has finite index in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, hence there exists a positive integer
d
such that
x^{d}=\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{n_{i}}
with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We can choose
d
such that
a^{d}=1
. Hence for
\mathfrak{p}\in S
we have

1=\alpha^{d}=x^{d}\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{d\lambda_{i}}=\prod_{i}\epsilon_{i}^{d\lambda_{i}+n_{i}}.

The
\epsilon_{i}
’s, viewed as elements of
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S^{\text{fin}}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
, are
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-independent, this follows from the proof of
[
9
]
, Lemma 8.2.4 and our assumption that
\delta(S)=1
. Therefore we see
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
a\in K^{\times}
.

Now assume that
K
is totally imaginary. Let
b\in K^{\times}
be a torsion. We need to prove that the image of
b
in
G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}}
is trivial.
b
, viewed as as an element of
\mathbb{I}_{K}
via the natural embedding
K^{\times}\to\mathbb{I}_{K}
, has image in
D_{K}
, and its difference with
b
, viewed as an element of
\mathcal{I}(K,S)
, is an idele with value
b
at the components of infinite primes and finite primes not in
S
. Since
K
is totally imaginary, the infinite primes are all complex. By Theorem 8.2.5 and Theorem 8.3.13 of
[
9
]
, we see the image of
b
in
G_{K,S}^{\text{ab}}
is trivial.

Assertion (iii) follows immediately from assertion (ii) together with the fact that for any finite extension
L/K
contained in
K_{S}
, there exists some totally imaginary finite extension
L^{\prime}/K
contained in
K_{S}
such that
L^{\prime}
contains
L
.
∎

.

Let

Remark 3.6
.

It follows immediately from Theorem 2.4 that, for a profinite group

\mathcal{I}(G,S(G))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{I}(K,S).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
G

Remark 5.8
.

In this paper, we are working with
\delta(S)=1
. While Theorem 2.4 is proven for
0<\delta(S)<1
, the following technical issues stops us from proving a positive density version of Theorem 5.6:

•

If
0<\delta(S)<1
, then the local-global map

H^{1}(G_{K},\mu_{n})\to\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{f}}H^{1}(G_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}},\mu_{n})

is not necessarily injective. From this point of view, it is not clear how to construct a version of
\mathcal{H}^{\times}(K,S)
to make section 4 work.

•

If
0<\delta(S)<1
, it is not clear that the decomposition groups of
G_{K,S}
at primes in
S_{f}(K_{S})
are full or not. If the decomposition groups are not full, then Theorem 2.4 does not work.

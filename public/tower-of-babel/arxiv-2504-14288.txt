Forward-Backward Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Controls: Equilibrium Strategies and Non-Symmetric Riccati Equations
Qi Lü Bowen Ma Hanxiao Wang

Abstract
Linear-quadratic optimal control problem for systems governed by forward-backward stochastic differential equations has been extensively studied over the past three decades. Recent research has revealed that for forward-backward control systems, the corresponding optimal control problem is inherently time-inconsistent. Consequently, the optimal controls derived in existing literature represent pre-committed solutions rather than dynamically consistent strategies. In this paper, we shift focus from pre-committed solutions to addressing the time-inconsistency issue directly, adopting a dynamic game-theoretic approach to derive equilibrium strategies. Owing to the forward-backward structure, the associated equilibrium Riccati equation (ERE) constitutes a coupled system of matrix-valued, non-local ordinary differential equations with a non-symmetric structure. This non-symmetry introduces fundamental challenges in establishing the solvability of the EREs. We overcome the difficulty by establishing a priori estimates for a combination of the solutions to EREs, which, interestingly, is a representation of the equilibrium value function.

Introduction

(1.5)

Definition 1.1
.

A matrix-valued function
{\Theta}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times n})
is called a
closed-loop equilibrium strategy
for Problem (TI-FBSLQ) if for every initial pair
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times L_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any perturbation

, the following variational inequality holds:

\varliminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{{\cal J}\left(t,\bar{X}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\right)-{\cal J}\left(t,\bar{X}(t);\bar{u}(\cdot)\right)}{\varepsilon}\geq 0,\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{\rm-a.s.,}

(1.6)

where the reference trajectory
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot))
satisfies:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle d\bar{X}(s)=\big[A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\big]\bar{X}(s)ds+\big[C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\big]\bar{X}(s)dW(s),&s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\bar{Y}(s)=-\big[\big(\widehat{A}(s)+\widehat{B}(s)\Theta(s)\big)\bar{X}(s)+\widehat{C}(s)\bar{Y}(s)+\widehat{D}(s)\bar{Z}(s)\big]ds+\bar{Z}(s)dW(s),&s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{X}(t)=x,\quad\bar{Y}(T)=H\bar{X}(T),\end{cases}

the control processes are given by

\bar{u}(s)=\Theta(s)\bar{X}(s),\quad u^{\varepsilon}(s)=v\chi_{[t,t+\varepsilon]}(s)+{\Theta}(s){X}^{\varepsilon}(s),

In the rest of this paper, for notational simplicity, given any

Remark 1.1
.

The concept of closed-loop equilibrium strategies follows from the framework established by Björk, Khapko, and Murgoci
[
3
]
. Within our linear-quadratic setting, we may restrict our attention to linear feedback strategies without loss of generality. This approach aligns with the economic intuition developed in
[
3
,
13
,
31
]
, where at each decision time
t
, the controller engages in a cooperative game with their future selves. Specifically:

•

The controller optimizes the cost functional over an infinitesimal time interval
[t,t+\varepsilon)
.

•

The optimization is performed under the constraint that the control is no more optimal beyond
t+\varepsilon
.

•

This leads to a time-consistent strategy that accounts for future decision-making processes.

To establish the existence of closed-loop equilibrium strategies for Problem (TI-FBSLQ),
inspired by
[
18
, Theorem 1.6]
, we introduce
the following equilibrium Riccati equation (ERE, for short):

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{dP_{1}(t,s)}{ds}+P_{1}(t,s)A_{\Theta}(s)+A_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{1}(t,s)+C_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{1}(t,s)C_{\Theta}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+Q(t,s)+\Theta(s)^{\top}R(t,s)\Theta(s)+P_{2}(s)^{\top}M(t,s)P_{2}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+C_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}N(t,s)P_{2}(s)C_{\Theta}(s)=0,&0\leq t\leq s\leq T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{dP_{2}(s)}{ds}+P_{2}(s)A_{\Theta}(s)+\widehat{A}_{\Theta}(s)+\widehat{C}(s)P_{2}(s)+\widehat{D}(s)P_{2}(s)C_{\Theta}(s)=0,&0\leq s\leq T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{1}(t,T)=G_{1}(t),\quad P_{2}(T)=H,&0\leq t\leq T,\end{cases}

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle\Theta(s)

\displaystyle=-\big[R(s,s)+D(s)^{\top}\big(P_{1}(s,s)+P_{2}(s)^{\top}N(s,s)P_{2}(s)\big)D(s)\big]^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\big[B(s)^{\top}P_{1}(s,s)+D(s)^{\top}\big(P_{1}(s,s)+P_{2}(s)^{\top}N(s,s)P_{2}(s)\big)C(s)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(\widehat{B}(s)^{\top}+B(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}+D(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}\widehat{D}(s)^{\top}\big)G_{2}(s)P_{2}(s)\big],\qquad 0\leq s\leq T.

(1.11)

Before studying the well-posedness of ERE (
1.10
), we first examine an illustrative example that demonstrates how the highly nonlinear nature of
P_{2}(\cdot)
can result in the non-existence of solutions to (

).

Example 1.1
.

Let us examine the ERE (
1.10
) with coefficients:

\widehat{A}=\widehat{B}=\widehat{C}=\widehat{D}=A=C=D=Q=N=G_{2}=0,B=R=H=G_{1}=1,\,M=2e,\,T=2.

Under these conditions, the ERE (
1.10
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{P}_{1}(s)=P_{1}(s)^{2}-2eP_{2}(s)^{2},&s\in[0,2],\\
\dot{P}_{2}(s)=P_{2}(s)P_{1}(s),&s\in[0,2],\\
P_{1}(2)=1,\quad P_{2}(2)=1.\end{cases}

Through direct computation, we obtain
P_{2}(s)=e^{-\int_{s}^{2}P_{1}(\tau)d\tau}
and
\dot{P}_{1}(s)=P_{1}(s)^{2}-2e^{P_{1}(s)^{2}}
.
Consider the auxiliary equation:

\begin{cases}\dot{P}(s)=-P(s)^{2},\quad s\in(1,2],\\
P(2)=1,\end{cases}

which admits the explicit solution
P(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}
for

.
Now, suppose by contradiction that
(P_{1}(\cdot),P_{2}(\cdot))
exists globally on
[0,2]
.
Noting the inequality

and applying the comparison principle, we deduce that

for

. However, this implies

,
yielding the desired contradiction to the assumed global existence.

\displaystyle N(t,s)\leq N(\tau,s)\leq\widehat{N},\quad G_{1}(t)\leq G_{1}(\tau)\leq\widehat{G}_{1},\quad G_{2}(t)\leq G_{2}(\tau)\leq\widehat{G}_{2}.\vskip-5.69054pt

where

Although we introduce Assumptions
(H1)
–
(H3)
to study the ERE (

1.10

1.
Non-local Temporal Dependence
.
The time-preference in the weighting matrices causes

to depend on two time variables

,
making it non-local in the first time variable
t
. Alternatively,
if we treat

then the ERE (
1.10

which is self interacted via the diagonal term
P_{1}(s,s)
.

2.
Non-symmetric Coupled Structure
.
The ERE (
1.10
) consists of two coupled matrix-valued ODEs where
P_{2}(\cdot)
breaks the symmetry
P_{2}(\cdot)\equiv P_{2}(\cdot)^{\top}
.
Moreover, as mentioned before, Problem (FBSLQ) (in which the cost functional reduces to (
1.2
)) remains time-inconsistent.
In this case,
the corresponding ERE (
1.10
) reduces to the following coupled ODEs:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{dP_{1}(s)}{ds}+P_{1}(s)A_{\Theta}(s)+A_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{1}(s)+C_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{1}(s)C_{\Theta}(s)+Q(s)+\Theta(s)^{\top}R(s)\Theta(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+P_{2}(s)^{\top}M(s)P_{2}(s)+C_{\Theta}(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}N(s)P_{2}(s)C_{\Theta}(s)=0,&0\leq s\leq T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{dP_{2}(s)}{ds}+P_{2}(s)A_{\Theta}(s)+\widehat{A}_{\Theta}(s)+\widehat{C}(s)P_{2}(s)+\widehat{D}(s)P_{2}(s)C_{\Theta}(s)=0,&0\leq s\leq T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{1}(T)=G_{1},\quad P_{2}(T)=H,\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\Theta(s)

\displaystyle\!=\!-\big[R(s)\!+\!D(s)^{\top}\!\!\big(P_{1}(s)\!+\!P_{2}(s)^{\top}\!N(s)P_{2}(s)\big)D(s)\big]^{-1}\!\big[B(s)^{\top}\!P_{1}(s)\!+\!D(s)^{\top}\big(P_{1}(s)\!+\!P_{2}(s)^{\top}\!N(s)P_{2}(s)\big)C(s)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(\widehat{B}(s)^{\top}+{B}(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}+D(s)^{\top}P_{2}(s)^{\top}\widehat{D}(s)^{\top}\big)G_{2}(s)P_{2}(s)\big],\qquad 0\leq s\leq T.

Thus, the inherent non-symmetry of (
1.10
) stems from the forward-backward structure of the state equations, representing a significant departure from classical LQ theory.

From the above, we know that the essence of Problem (TI-FBSLQ) in our work lies in two aspects:
First, the forward-backward structure of the state equation,
which brings the complicated coupled relationship and non-symmetric structure in ERE (
1.10
).
This together with the high nonlinearity of ERE (
1.10
) constitutes the most fundamental challenge of this work;
Second, the time-preference for the weighting matrices,
which makes ERE (
1.10
) non-local in the first time variable,
or in another perspective, makes ERE (
1.10
) an (uncountably) infinite dimensional systems of matrix-valued ODEs.

Remark 1.4
.

When restricted to forward SDE systems, the ERE (
1.10
) reduces to a non-local but symmetric matrix-valued ODE. For such cases, Yong
[
31
]
pioneered a solution approach via multi-person differential games. However, this method relies critically on:

1. The dynamic programming principle (DPP) of forward LQ systems;

2. Complete solvability of the pre-commitment Riccati equation.

Both conditions fail for our FBSDE framework, even in the simplified cost case (
1.2
), rendering Yong’s methodology inapplicable.

Remark 1.5
.

It is worthy of pointing out that the Riccati-type ODEs with non-symmetric structures
arise naturally in many important settings, such as the study of open-loop time-inconsistent LQ control problems
[
13
,
14
]
,
non-zero LQ games
[
23
]
, and LQ mean-field games
[
2
, Chapter 6]
.
However, establishing general existence results for such systems remains a major open challenge in stochastic control theory, with currently limited constructive solution approaches available.

In this paper, we demonstrates that despite the inherent non-symmetry of ERE (
1.10
), careful analysis establishes the existence of a unique solution. Our approach overcomes the fundamental challenges by treating the solution components
P_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
P_{2}(\cdot)
as an interconnected system rather than separate entities. The core methodology consists of three key steps:

(i)

A priori estimates for the combined quantity
P_{1}(t,t)+P_{2}(t)^{\top}G_{2}(t)P_{2}(t)
are established through direct differentiation, under sufficient smoothness conditions on the system coefficients and weighting matrices.

(ii)

These estimates enable the construction of suitable Picard iteration sequences, forming the foundation for proving both local and global well-posedness of the ERE (
1.10
).

(iii)

The results are extended to non-smooth cases via a mollification approximation technique.

The most challenging part of our approach lies in identifying an appropriate invariant

P_{1}(t,t)+P_{2}(t)^{\top}G_{2}(t)P_{2}(t)
for establishing a priori bounds.
A key point is that we observe that the function
\frac{1}{2}\big\langle\big(P_{1}(t,t)+P_{2}(t)^{\top}\!G_{2}(t)P_{2}(t)\big)x
,
x\big\rangle;(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

precisely represents the equilibrium value function of Problem (TI-FBSLQ). The derivation of these estimates requires meticulous analysis and remains non-trivial despite our structural insights.

In summary, this work addresses the unavoidable time-inconsistency inherent in Problem (TI-FBSLQ)–a consequence of its forward-backward system structure–by:

•

Developing a framework for closed-loop equilibrium strategies;

•

Introducing novel techniques to handle the non-symmetric ERE structure;

•

Providing constructive methods for equilibrium strategy derivation.

Now we are ready to present main results of this paper.

2.1
Some useful lemmas

In this subsection, we present several technical lemmas that will be important in our subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\cal R}(\cdot)\in C([0,T];\,{\mathbb{S}}^{n})
be a matrix-valued function such that
{\cal R}(t)\leq{\cal R}(s)
for all
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
.
Then
{\cal R}(\cdot)
is differentiable almost everywhere and its derivative is positive semi-definite.

Proof.

From the monotonicity of
{\cal R}(\cdot)
, we know that
\hbox{Trace}\big({\cal R}(\cdot)\big)
is a monotonically increasing function and therefore is of bounded variation. The inequality

\displaystyle\big|{\cal R}(t)-{\cal R}(s)\big|\leq\big|\hbox{Trace}\big({\cal R}(t)\big)-\hbox{Trace}\big({\cal R}(s)\big)\big|,

shows that each component of
{\cal R}(\cdot)
is likewise of bounded variation. Therefore,
{\cal R}(\cdot)
is differentiable almost everywhere on
[0,T]
.

At any point
t_{0}
where
{\cal R}(\cdot)
is differentiable, we have for arbitrary
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that

\displaystyle x^{\top}{\cal R}^{\prime}(t_{0})x=x^{\top}\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{{\cal R}(t)-{\cal R}(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}x\geq 0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\cal G}(\cdot)\in C([0,T];\,{\mathbb{S}}^{m})

where

satisfying

Define

\eta(t)=\begin{cases}\Big(\int_{|t|\leq 1}e^{\frac{1}{t^{2}-1}}dt\Big)^{-1}e^{\frac{1}{t^{2}-1}},&|t|\leq 1,\\
0,&|t|>1,\end{cases}

\eta_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\eta(\frac{t}{\varepsilon})

Let

{\cal G}(0),\quad&t\in[-T,0],\\
{\cal G}(t),\quad&t\in[0,T],\\
{\cal G}(T),\quad&t\in[T,2T],\\
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\cal G}(T)\chi_{[T,3T]}(s)\eta_{T/4}(t-s)ds,\quad&t\in[2T,+\infty).\end{cases}

We know that
\bar{{\cal G}}(\cdot)
belongs to

and is monotonically increasing over
[-T,2T]
.
Set

{\cal G}_{n}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bar{{\cal G}}(s)\eta_{\frac{1}{n}}(t-s)ds,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.1)

We know that (see
[
10

and

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|{\cal G}_{n}(\cdot)-{\cal G}(\cdot)\|_{C([0,T];\,{\mathbb{S}}^{n})}=0

For any

and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle x^{\top}\delta Ix

\displaystyle\leq x^{\top}{\cal G}_{n}(t)x=\int_{[-1/n,1/n]}x^{\top}\bar{{\cal G}}(t-s)x\,\eta_{\frac{1}{n}}(s)ds\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}x^{\top}\widehat{G}x\,\eta_{\frac{1}{n}}(t-s)ds=x^{\top}\widehat{G}x,

yielding the matrix inequality

Moreover, for

\displaystyle x^{\top}{\cal G}_{n}(t_{2})x-x^{\top}{\cal G}_{n}(t_{1})x=\int_{[-1/n,1/n]}x^{\top}\big[\bar{{\cal G}}(t_{2}-s)-\bar{{\cal G}}(t_{1}-s)\big]x\,\eta_{\frac{1}{n}}(s)ds\geq 0.

This implies that

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal Q}}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\begin{cases}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\cal Q}(0,s)\chi_{[-2T,0]}(r)\eta_{T/4}(t-r)dr,&(t,s)\in\{(t,s)\,|\,0\leq s\leq T,-\infty<t\leq-T\},\\

Similar to the case of

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T-h,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\Phi^{1}(t,s)-\Phi^{2}(t,s)|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\int_{T-h}^{T}|\Theta_{1}(s)-\Theta_{2}(s)|^{2}ds.

(4.13)

From (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\big|P^{1}_{2}(t)-P_{2}^{2}(t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\int_{T-h}^{T}|\Theta_{1}(s)-\Theta_{2}(s)|ds,\quad t\in[T-h,T].

(4.14)

From (
4.11
)–(
4.13
), we get that

\displaystyle\quad\big|P_{1}^{1}(t,t)-P_{1}^{2}(t,t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big|\Phi^{1}(t,T)^{\top}G_{1}(t)\Phi^{1}(t,T)-\Phi^{2}(t,T)^{\top}G_{1}(t)\Phi^{2}(t,T)\big|

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\big|\Phi^{1}(t,s)^{\top}Q(t,s)\Phi^{1}(t,s)-\Phi^{2}(t,s)^{\top}Q(t,s)\Phi^{2}(t,s)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big|\Phi^{1}(t,s)^{\top}\Theta_{1}^{\top}R(t,s)\Theta_{1}\Phi^{1}(t,s)-\Phi^{2}(t,s)^{\top}\Theta_{2}^{\top}R(t,s)\Theta_{2}\Phi^{2}(t,s)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big|\Phi^{1}(t,s)^{\top}{P^{1}_{2}}^{\top}M(t,s)P_{2}^{1}\Phi^{1}(t,s)-\Phi^{2}(t,s)^{\top}{P^{2}_{2}}^{\top}M(t,s)P_{2}^{2}\Phi^{2}(t,s)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big|\Phi^{1}(t,s)^{\top}C_{\Theta_{1}}^{\top}{P^{1}_{2}}^{\top}N(t,s){P}_{2}^{1}C_{\Theta_{1}}\Phi^{1}(t,s)-\Phi^{2}(t,s)^{\top}C_{\Theta_{2}}^{\top}{P^{2}_{2}}^{\top}N(t,s){P}_{2}^{2}C_{\Theta_{2}}\Phi^{2}(t,s)\big|\Big)ds

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\Big(\int_{T-h}^{T}|\Theta_{1}(s)-\Theta_{2}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{1/2},\hskip 160.00024ptt\in[T-h,T].

(4.15)

Here we emphasize that the estimates (

.
Recalling the definition of
\Gamma_{[T-h,T]}[\cdot]
in (

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T-h,T]}\big|\Gamma_{[T-h,T]}[\Theta_{1}(\cdot)](t)-\Gamma_{[T-h,T]}[\Theta_{2}(\cdot)](t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\Big(\sup_{t\in[T-h,T]}\big|P^{1}_{1}(t,t)-P^{1}_{1}(t,t)\big|+\sup_{t\in[T-h,T]}\big|P^{1}_{2}(t)-P^{1}_{2}(t)\big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\Big(\int_{T-h}^{T}|\Theta_{1}(s)-\Theta_{2}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{1/2}

Thus, by selecting

we conclude that

Step 3.
Having established the contraction property in Step 2, we now prove the well-posedness of the IES (
2.10
) on the interval
[T-h,T]
, where

is chosen as in Step 2.

Define a sequence of functions

\Theta^{*}(\cdot)

) over

Step 4
. In this step, we complete the proof using an inductive argument on the time interval. Without loss of generality, we assume

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\Big(\int_{T-2h}^{T}|\Theta_{n+1}(s)-{\Theta_{n}}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle|P_{2}^{n}(t)-P_{2}^{m}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+|B_{n}-B_{m}|^{2}+|C_{n}-C_{m}|^{2}+|D_{n}-D_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{A}_{n}-\widehat{A}_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+|\widehat{B}_{n}-\widehat{B}_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{C}_{n}-\widehat{C}_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{D}_{n}-\widehat{D}_{m}|^{2}\Big)ds.

(5.2)

Putting (
5.1
)–(
5.2
) together, by Grönwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(|P_{1}^{n}(t,t)-P_{1}^{m}(t,t)|^{2}+|P_{2}^{n}(t)-P_{2}^{m}(t)|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}({\cal C}^{*})\Big[\|G_{1}^{n}(\cdot)-G_{1}^{m}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{C([0,T];{\mathbb{S}}^{n})}+\|G_{2}^{n}(\cdot)-G_{2}^{m}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{C([0,T];{\mathbb{S}}^{m})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|Q_{n}(t,s)-Q_{m}(t,s)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|R_{n}(t,s)-R_{m}(t,s)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|M_{n}(t,s)-M_{m}(t,s)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|N_{n}(t,s)-N_{m}(t,s)|^{2}\Big)ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|A_{n}-A_{m}|^{2}+|B_{n}-B_{m}|^{2}+|C_{n}-C_{m}|^{2}+|D_{n}-D_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,+|\widehat{A}_{n}-\widehat{A}_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{B}_{n}-\widehat{B}_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{C}_{n}-\widehat{C}_{m}|^{2}+|\widehat{D}_{n}-\widehat{D}_{m}|^{2}\Big)ds\Big].

Thus
\big\{\big(\{P_{1}^{n}(t,t);t\in[0,T]\},P^{n}_{2}(\cdot)\big)\big\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence in

.
Taking

). Uniqueness follows directly from the estimates.

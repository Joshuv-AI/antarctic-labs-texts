In Reply to Sengul and Sengul
Authors: Rong-Tse Hsu, Ting-Chun Lin
Published: 2025

We thank Dr Ilker Sengul and Dr Demet Sengul for their thoughtful commentary on our article, “Key Insights and Implementation of a Patient-Centered Education Video for Managing Acute Radiation Dermatitis in Breast Cancer: A Single-Center Pilot Study.”1 We appreciate their engagement and the opportunity to address the important points raised.

As noted, our study is subject to certain limitations, including the small sample size and the single-arm design. Although these factors may introduce potential bias, our primary objective was to explore the feasibility and practical integration of a patient education video into routine clinical care. The study was intended as an initial step toward developing a scalable and user-friendly educational intervention.

We acknowledge that a randomized controlled trial would offer a more rigorous assessment of efficacy. However, such a study requires substantial time and resources and may encounter challenges related to workflow integration and patient recruitment—concerns we carefully considered at the outset.

Importantly, the educational video was implemented without adding a significant burden to the nursing staff and was well-suited for routine clinical operations. In fact, by streamlining the education process and enhancing consistency, this tool has the potential to reduce workload over time, especially as staff become accustomed to its use. The preliminary findings suggest that this approach is at least comparable, if not preferable, to traditional patient education methods.

Taken together, the promising outcomes and practical feasibility of the intervention highlight a potentially valuable direction for enhancing patient care. We agree that further research, including randomized trials, will be important to confirm and expand upon these findings.

The Y Y -cone type free boundary minimal surfaces in the unit ball
Elham Matinpour

Abstract
. J. C. C. Nitsche [ 11 ] proved that any minimal disk satisfying the free boundary condition in the unit ball 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}^{3} must be an equatorial flat disk. In this short note, we extend Nitsche’s result to Y Y -singular minimal surfaces that satisfy the free boundary condition in 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}^{3} . Specifically, we show that any conformal and minimal immersion of the standard compact flat Y Y -cone that meets 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}^{3} orthogonally must itself be a flat Y Y -cone. Furthermore, we compute the Morse index of the free boundary flat Y Y -cone, showing that it is two, and we prove that Y Y -cones are the only free boundary minimal surfaces of index two in 𝔹 3 \mathbb{B}^{3} .



1.
Introduction

A free boundary minimal surface in a ball means that:
the surface is a minimal surface in the ball, meaning that it has zero mean curvature,
the boundary of the surface lies on the boundary of the ball (here, on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
), and the surface meets the boundary of the ball at a right angle along its boundary.

Nitsche proved that a minimal disk satisfying the free boundary condition in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{3}
must be an equatorial flat disk. Later,
Fraser and Schoen
[
5
]
extended this result by showing that a minimal disk satisfying the free boundary condition in a ball of constant curvature, in any dimension, must be totally geodesic. They also weakened the condition to allow for a parallel mean curvature vector, proving that in this case, the disk lies in a three-dimensional constant curvature submanifold and is totally umbilic. Their results generalized earlier three-dimensional work by J. C. C. Nitsche
[
11
]
and R. Souam
[
12
]
. Nitsche and Fraser-Li
[
4
]
conjectured that every embedded free boundary minimal annulus in a ball should be the critical catenoid, the part of the catenoid in a ball perpendicular to the boundary sphere. Recently J. Choe
[
1
]
proved the conjecture by showing that a minimal surface meeting a sphere orthogonally can be extended by reflection across the sphere. He generalized Schwarz’s reflection principle for minimal surfaces and showed that the sphere can become a mirror like a plane. By iteratively reflecting the embedded annulus across its boundaries infinitely many times, he reconstructed the complete catenoid and thereby concluded that the annulus must be the critical catenoid.

Taking a different approach, we extend Nitsche’s work to
Y
-singular minimal surfaces satisfying the free boundary condition in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{3}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
YC
be the union of three half-unit disks joined along their diameters, meeting at equal angles of 120-degrees.
Suppose
u:YC\rightarrow\mathbb{B}^{3}
is a conformal and minimal immersion such that
u(YC)
meets
\partial\mathbb{B}^{3}
orthogonally. Then,
u(YC)
is a flat
Y
-cone.

For the precise definition of conformal map in this setting see Definition
2.3
.

We conclude this note by computing the Morse index of the free boundary flat
Y
-cone in the unit ball, showing that it is two. Furthermore, we prove that
Y
-cones are the only free boundary minimal surfaces of index two in the unit ball.

Theorem 1.2
.

The Morse index of the free boundary flat
Y
-cone in the unit ball is two, and its nullity is five. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is a free boundary minimal
Y
-surface in the unit ball with Morse index two, then
\Sigma
is a
Y
-cone.

Reply
Authors: Guillermo J Velazquez Gutierrez, Jhonny I Borrallas Lopez, Jesus G Rodriguez de la Torre, Salvatore Brugaletta
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Lim for his interest in our case1 and his constructive criticism. Although oxygenation during on-pump coronary artery bypass grafting was associated with poor cardiac function,2 supersaturated oxygen therapy in patients with anterior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention within 6 hours of symptom onset resulted in a significant reduction in infarct size without any effect on major adverse cardiac events at 30 days, as expected in a study not powered for clinical endpoints.3 Our aim is to determine whether supersaturated oxygen therapy may play a role in reducing coronary microvascular resistance acutely.

When planning the study, we estimated a Rμ value of 400 mm Hg/L/min as indicative of elevated microcirculation resistance.4 The thermodilution methodology4 offers a different threshold of microvascular resistance compared with the over-the-wire balloon distal occlusion technique—which came out very recently—based on the fact that it uses a different unit of measurement (mm Hg/L/min vs Wood units). It would be interesting to know how comparable these 2 methods are but unfortunately there are no studies in this regard.

Regarding index of microcirculatory resistance (IMR) reliability and fractional flow reserve value, we agree that in case of diffuse disease there could be some flow restraint; however, it is known that fractional flow reserve value in an acute setting is not reliable. Besides, from the practical point of view 2 reproducible measurements are considered acceptable when values are concordant, as in our case.5 As pointed out, an IMR of 25—although below the classical threshold of 40 for predicting microvascular obstruction—does not exclude clinically relevant microvascular dysfunction: Recent data support a continuum of microvascular injury rather than a binary cutoff, and IMR values may carry independent prognostic significance in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction.

Eventually, Dr Lim raises a valid methodological concern regarding the instantaneous temperature (Ti) variability and the thermistor position. Indeed, the thermistor could have been placed too distal (usually it is placed 10-20 mm from the distal edge of the implanted stent); however, to overcome any problems of reproducibility, the thermistor is always placed in the same position either during baseline or hyperemic measurements.

We believe that robust conclusions cannot be drawn from a single case, and we look forward to the results of the RECOVERY (Recovering coronary microvascular obstruction with supersaturated Oxygen therapy in ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction [STEMI] patients; NCT07000266) to definitively address these questions.

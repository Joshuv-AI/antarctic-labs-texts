Quality and Safety in Asparagus Cultivation: A Three-Year Case Study Comparing Standardized Agricultural Bases and Small-Scale Farmers
Authors: Yuhong Liu, Gangjun Wang, Guoguang Yu, Weiran Zheng, Caixia Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To investigate the quality and safety difference between agricultural standardization bases and small-scale farmers, we carried out a three-year investigation of asparagus from seven production sites of different sizes in Pinghu city, Zhejiang Province, China, from 2021 to 2023. We documented trace elements (Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu), quality indicators (vitamin B1, vitamin B2, vitamin C, total sugar, and proteins), and pesticide residues. The evaluation indicated that the quality of asparagus in standardization bases 1–4 was higher than that in small farmer sites 5–7. The detection rate of pesticides in asparagus was 23.81% (15/63), with a low concentration range of 0.001–0.130 mg mL–1. Low pesticide levels reflect Pinghu’s effective green pest control measures. Results showed that the quality and safety of asparagus at the standardization bases are superior to those at small-scale farmer sites, and our findings may inform better management practices for both large-scale and small-scale asparagus farmers.

Introduction

Asparagus (Asparagus officinalis L.), a perennial herb with edible spears, is grown worldwide.1 It is renowned as “the king of vegetables” and is a good source of minerals, proteins, and vitamins.2,3 Asparagus contains active substances that are important to human health. These substances can delay aging and reduce blood lipids, and they exhibit antitumor properties.4−6 The medicinal value of asparagus is also documented in the Chinese medical text, “Compendium of Materia Medica”.7 Asparagus also has a low caloric content, making it a useful diet choice for those trying to control their calorie intake.8

China is the world’s largest producer of asparagus, in both cultivated areas and total production.9 The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations noted that, in 2021, the total output of asparagus in China was approximately 7,341,300 tons, and the harvested area was about 1,440,400 ha. Zhejiang Province is a leading agricultural hub in China, and the government promotes the development of specialty agriculture. Pinghu city (Zhejiang Province) has a mild climate, fertile soil, and a frost-free period exceeding 200 days annually. These conditions are suitable for the growth of many crops, including asparagus. In 2021, the total area of asparagus cultivation in Pinghu exceeded 500 ha, with an output of 11,000 tons and a total value exceeding 100 million yuan. Therefore, asparagus is currently an important crop in Pinghu. However, high planting densities in greenhouses necessitate pesticide use and pose risks of exceeding residue limits.10 Small farmers play an important role in agriculture production, including asparagus. And the small farmers, who are integral to asparagus production, face numerous challenges, including limited access to advanced technologies, inconsistent production quality, and issues related to foodborne illness and food security.11,12

To explore differences in quality and safety between standardization bases and small-scale farmers, we conducted a three-year survey of seven Pinghu asparagus production sites. We analyzed trace elements (Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu), nutrients (vitamin B1, B2, C, total sugar, total flavonoids, and proteins), and pesticide residues. Seven asparagus production bases (four standardization bases with a production area of more than 2 ha and three randomly chosen small-scale farmers) were selected to assess variations in food safety and quality across different production scales.

Results and Discussion

Trace Element in Asparagus

The levels of Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu in asparagus sampled in 2021 ranged from 4.32 to 6.90, 3.32 to 5.46, 1.13 to 2.58, and 1.11 to 2.91 mg kg–1 (Figure 1). In 2022, the levels of Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu were 4.47 to 6.82, 2.09 to 4.71, 0.88 to 1.64, and 0.87 to 1.50 mg kg–1, respectively. In 2023, the levels of Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu were 3.20 to 6.50, 4.22 to 6.43, 1.10 to 1.93, and 1.08 to 1.73 mg kg–1, respectively. The levels of all analyzed elements in asparagus were below 10 mg kg–1. Some asparagus samples from small-scale farmers contained less than 1 mg kg–1 of Mn and Cu.

Trace element contents (Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu) in asparagus from seven sites were analyzed from 2021 to 2023. Different lowercase letters represent a significant difference at the p < 0.05 level.

Trace elements are key factors in the cycling of ecosystems (plants-animals-soil). In this study, a survey was conducted on the content of Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu in asparagus collected from seven sites during 2021–2023. The results from the 3 years of testing showed that the highest values for all four trace elements were from the standardization bases, while the lowest values were observed in small farmer sites. A statistically significant difference (p < 0.05) in the content of Mn (in 2021) was observed between samples from standardization bases (sites 1–4) and those from small-scale farmer sites (sites 5–7). The levels of trace elements (Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu) in asparagus were higher in standardized production standardization bases (sites 1–4) than in sites of small-scale farmers (sites 5–7), but this difference was not statistically significant. Previous studies have shown that there were no significant differences in elements such as Cu and Zn in crops produced by different farming systems (organic agriculture and conventional agriculture), and asparagus from organic production did not exhibit significant differences in most of the mineral elements compared with asparagus from conventional production.13,14

Mineral elements such as Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu are essential trace elements involved in many physiological and biochemical reactions and in maintaining normal life activities.15,16 Vegetables are the primary sources of these elements for humans. Fe is involved in hemoglobin synthesis and oxygen transport and promotes blood cell production, with deficiencies potentially leading to anemia, insomnia, and decreased immunity.17,18 Zn functions as an immunomodulator and enzyme cofactor, playing a role in antioxidant, antiapoptotic, and anti-inflammatory processes. It is also necessary for insulin synthesis. Deficiencies can result in digestive issues, stunted growth, cognitive impairment, hair loss, and neuropsychiatric disorders.19,20 Mn is a component of enzymes, such as arginase, pyruvate carboxylase, and superoxide dismutase. It activates various other enzymes, promoting normal bone growth, glucose metabolism, and fat metabolism.21 Cu is crucial for maintaining hematopoiesis, central nervous system integrity, and the health of bones, blood vessels, and skin. It also aids in the body’s Fe absorption.22 Our results were in consistent with previous studies that in organically grown vegetables, content of Cu, Fe, and Zn was 0.16–1.05, 0.32–5.56, and 2.11–5.19 mg kg–1, respectively. The trace element Mn was rarely detected in vegetables, and Mn in Brussels sprouts was at 0.74 mg kg–1 or ranged from 0.10 to 0.78 mg kg–1.23,24 The uptake of metallic elements by plants is related to many factors, including elemental concentration, soil type, and pH value. Fertilizer use also affects element uptake.25 Previous studies showed that the order of accumulation of trace elements such as Fe, Mn, and Zn in vegetable plants is as follows: leaves > roots > stems.26 This is because trace elements are primarily involved in regulating plant metabolism in leaves. For example, Fe acts as a catalyst for chlorophyll formation. Mineral elements in the soil are transported to other parts of the plant via the roots, which results in higher accumulation in the roots than in the stems or leaves.

The Quality of Asparagus

Nutrients in vegetables are essential for human growth and development.27 The asparagus quality indicator results are shown in Table 1. In asparagus, vitamins B1, B2, and C, total sugar, total flavonoids, and proteins are important descriptors of nutritional quality. In Pinghu asparagus samples from 2021 to 2023, the quality indicators were ranged as follows: vitamin B1 ranged from 0.41 to 1.08 mg kg–1, vitamin B2 ranged from 0.24 to 0.69 mg kg–1, vitamin C ranged from 118.51 to 448.71 mg kg–1, total sugar ranged from 1.92 to 3.91%, total flavonoids ranged from 0.011 to 0.089%, and proteins ranged from 1.70 to 2.87%. These values are all expressed on a fresh weight (FW) basis. As shown in Table 1, the vitamin C content of asparagus is 2 to 3 orders of magnitude higher than that of the B group vitamins. Common vegetables such as tomatoes, cucumbers, and curly lettuce contain vitamin C in the range of 38–159 mg kg–1.28 Citrus fruits are good sources of vitamin C, with levels of 378, 289, and 292 mg kg–1 of vitamin C in orange, grapefruit, and spring tangelo, respectively.29 Other common fruits such as yellow peaches, loquats, and pears have relatively lower vitamin C contents, at 61.4, 32.5, and 45.4 mg kg–1, respectively. Thus, asparagus is a good choice for daily vitamin C supplementation. Asparagus quality is a comprehensive sensory evaluation that needs to be further explored to understand whether there are differences in asparagus quality between standardized bases and small-scale farmers.

Evaluation of Nutrient Components in Pinghu Asparagus

Radar map analysis of asparagus in planting sites 1–7 from 2021 to 2023.

To comprehensively evaluate the asparagus samples from the seven sites, the evaluation indexes were made dimensionless according to Chen et al. (2024), with values constrained within the range of 0–1.30 Here, a value closer to 1 indicates a better trait. The ratio of the graph area (S) to perimeter (L) was used to evaluate asparagus quality. The calculation results, shown in Table 2, indicate that the comprehensive evaluation results for asparagus quality at the seven sites were ranked as follows: 3 > 1 > 4 > 2 > 7 > 6 > 5. Sites 1–4 had a higher overall rating compared to sites 5–7. Freshness is a key factor affecting the quality of asparagus, and fresh asparagus has a higher nutritional value. In addition, after harvesting, the extension of storage time leads to lignification and an increase in stem hardness, which can affect the taste.31 Studies showed that soaking asparagus samples in a melatonin solution delayed postharvest senescence and reduced the loss of vitamin C.32 This was due to the melatonin treatment inhibiting the activity of related enzymes, thereby reducing the lignin content. Using microscopic imaging techniques, high oxygen-modified atmosphere packaging reduces the rate of lignin deposition in the cell wall of asparagus and slows down the lignification process.33Fusarium proliferatum infection accelerated the respiratory metabolism of postharvest asparagus, resulting in abnormal metabolism of sugar and organic acid content, which led to deterioration of asparagus quality.34 It was found that intercropping of asparagus and rye could not only inhibit the growth of weeds but also reduce the damage of Fusarium crown rot and root rot on asparagus.35,36 Water supply of water to the roots is a critical factor affecting the quality of asparagus. In contrast to fruit trees, asparagus is characterized by a shallow root system that typically does not handle mild and severe water stress conditions, and water scarcity can lead to a loss in yield and quality. Standardization base use of microirrigation, drip irrigation, and sprinkler irrigation technologies for asparagus irrigation can reduce issues of overwatering and water scarcity, thereby improving the quality of asparagus. Our results may also provide a basis for the development of more functional asparagus products.

The growing environment directly affects crop quality, including factors such as soil fertility, soil pH, soil trace elements, and climatic conditions. Former research showed that without annual reridging, the application of compost treatment in the asparagus interrows combined with shallow soil disturbance can lead to an increase in asparagus yield for three consecutive years.37 Traditional agriculture often employs grafting techniques and the application of fertilizers to enhance the quality of vegetables. Additionally, mutation breeding, cross-breeding, and polyploid breeding are also common practices. With the advancement of biotechnology, methods such as molecular markers, transgenics, and gene editing are now commonly used to improve vegetable quality.38,39 For example, firmness is a critical indicator of tomato quality and influences both transportation conditions and storage longevity. The function of the FIS1 gene in tomatoes resulted in increased gibberellin activity, which in turn enhanced the biosynthesis of cuticle and wax. This increased fruit firmness and extended shelf life.40

Pesticide Residues in Asparagus

In total, 15 (23.81%) of the 63 asparagus samples had at least one pesticide residue. No more than three pesticides were detected in any individual sample (Figures 3 and 4). The number of samples with no pesticides detected at planting sites 1–7 was 7, 9, 8, 8, 6, 5, and 5, respectively (Figure 3). The pesticide detection rate was lower in the standardization bases (sites 1–4) compared to that in the small-scale farmer sites (sites 5–7). The pesticide detection rates for samples from planting sites 1–7 were 22.22%, 0.00%, 11.11%, 11.11%, 33.33%, 44.44%, and 44.44%, respectively.

Proportion of pesticide residues in asparagus. A, B, and C represent 2021, 2022, and 2023, respectively.

Five pesticides were detected across all samples, and these included three insecticides (cyhalothrin, acetamiprid, and chlorfenapyr) and two fungicides (carbendazim and chlorothalonil). Cyhalothrin (0.011 mg mL–1) and chlorothalonil (0.064 mg mL–1) were each detected in a single sample. Acetamiprid was found in two samples, with detection results of 0.002 and 0.009 mg mL–1. Carbendazim was detected in 10 samples, with a detection range of 0.001 to 0.110 mg mL–1. Chlorfenapyr was detected in eight samples, with a detection range of 0.011 to 0.030 mg mL–1. Detailed data are provided in Table S1. Among the five pesticides, carbendazim had the highest detection rate at 15.87%, but there was significant variation across the samples, spanning 2 orders of magnitude.

Carbendazim, an economical broad-spectrum fungicide, is widely used in China.41 Although the half-life of carbendazim on plants is only a few days (Banerjee et al. 2005), its half-life in the environment, such as soil and water, can be up to 100 days.42 Numerous studies have reported high detection rates of carbendazim in fruits and vegetables. Carbendazim is also a primary metabolite of certain other fungicides, such as metribuzin and benomyl, which have similar toxicological benefits.43 Carbendazim use is now prohibited in many developed countries, including the European Union and the United States, due to its toxicity and potential for environmental accumulation. Chlorfenapyr is widely used on fruit and vegetable crops due to its effectiveness against pests such as Tetranychid mites, Spodoptera exigua, and Pieris rapae.44 The mode of action of chlorfenapyr involves interference with the mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation reaction, leading to the blockage of ADP conversion to ATP. This disrupts energy metabolism, which ultimately results in insect death.45,46 The environmental residues of chlorfenapyr and its toxicity to nontarget organisms have raised some concerns. A previous study demonstrated that chlorfenapyr was highly bioaccumulative in aquatic vertebrates such as zebrafish. Prolonged exposure to chlorfenapyr affected the mitochondrial tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and disturbs energy metabolism in the liver and brain of zebrafish and leads to cellular lesions.47 Although the residue level of chlorfenapyr in asparagus was low in our study, its high detection rate and the toxic side effects due to potential accumulation warrant further attention.

The maximum residue level (MRL) of pesticides is a critical indicator for assessing the safety of food supply. The goal is to minimize pesticide residue levels in crops and thereby reduce human health risks.48 The MRLs for pesticides detected in asparagus are compared across China, the European Union (EU) (European Commission, 2024), Japan (Japan, 2024), and the Codex Alimentarius Commission (CAC, 2024) in Table S2.49−51 Although the levels of five pesticides—carbendazim, chlorfenapyr, cyhalothrin, acetamiprid, and chlorothalonil—detected in asparagus did not exceed the limits set by GB 2763-2021 (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People’s Republic of China, 2021), the pesticide residue limits of the EU are more stringent than those of other countries or organizations.52 Consequently, carbendazim in one asparagus sample, chlorfenapyr in eight samples, and chlorothalonil in one sample exceeded EU regulations and are a cause for concern. Leafy vegetables are frequently reported to have a wide range of pesticide residues; however, asparagus showed a relatively low incidence of pesticide detection, both in diversity and concentration.53,54 This may be attributed to the large surface area of leafy vegetables, which allows better retention of pesticides and makes leaves difficult to clean. Pinghu has been actively implementing green control measures, with the widespread use of light traps, pheromones, biopesticides, insect nets, and natural enemies.

Conclusions

In summary, samples of asparagus grown in Pinghu from 2021 to 2023 were collected for analysis of trace elements (Fe, Zn, Mn, and Cu), nutrients (vitamin B1, vitamin B2, vitamin C, total sugar, total flavonoids, and proteins), and pesticide residues. Results showed that asparagus from standardization bases is superior to those at small-scale farmer sites in quality and safety. This may be attributed to large-scale agricultural entities managing asparagus in a more standardized manner with a more systematic application of water and fertilizers. We recommend that small-scale farmers adopt more standardized planting techniques and regulate the use of agricultural inputs by joining new agricultural entities, such as cooperatives, family farms, and agricultural enterprises. This will facilitate the green transformation of small-scale production driven by new agricultural management subjects.

Materials and Methods

Sample Collection

Pinghu asparagus was sown in spring (early March to mid-April) and in fall (mid-August to mid-September). Asparagus spears were harvested when they were grown to approximately 30 cm long. In this study, samples were collected from September 2021 to September 2023. We selected seven representative production sites in Pinghu for a period of 3 years. Sites 1–4 were designated as standardization bases, while sites 5–7 were cultivated by small-scale farmers. The sample location in Pinghu is shown in Figure 5.

Sample location map of cultivation regions.

Detection of Trace Elements

The contents of trace elements (Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn) in fresh asparagus samples were analyzed using the inductively coupled plasma with the optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) method, specifically according to the Chinese national standard GB 5009.268-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Multi-Elements in Food” (National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China, 2016a).55 The procedure was as follows: first, 10 mL of perchloric acid was slowly added to 100 mL of nitric acid to prepare a mixed acid solution. Then, 0.5 g of homogenized asparagus sample was placed in glassware, and 10 mL of nitric acid and perchloric acid solution was added. The mixture was digested on a hot plate until white fumes were produced. The digested solution was colorless and transparent or slightly yellow, and then, it was cooled and brought up to a volume of 50 mL with ultrapure water.

Detection of Vitamin B1, Vitamin B2, Vitamin C, Total Sugar, Total Flavonoid, and Protein Content

Vitamin B1 was detected using the Chinese national standard GB 5009.84-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Vitamin B1 in Foods”.56 Vitamin B2 was detected using the Chinese national standard GB 5009.85-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Vitamin B2 in Foods”.57 Vitamin C was detected using the Chinese national standard GB 5009.86-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Vitamin C in Foods.”58 Total sugar content was detected using the Chinese national standard GB 5009.8-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Fructose, Glucose, Sucrose, Maltose, and Lactose in Foods” (National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China, 2016e); proteins were detected using the Chinese national standard GB 5009.5-2016, “National Food Safety Standard—Determination of Protein in Foods”.59,60 Radar chart analysis was used to evaluate the quality of asparagus, and five quality indicators were subjected to dimensionless processing and were used to draw the radar chart. To make the evaluation results more objective, a comprehensive evaluation function was constructed by selecting the area (S) and perimeter (L) of the eigenvectors of the radar chart. The formulas used for the dimensionless processing and the calculation of the eigenvectors were based on Chen et al. (2024).61

A total of 63 asparagus samples were collected for pesticide multiresidue testing from 2021 to 2023, and specific information on the 68 pesticides examined is provided in Table S3. Statistical Analysis

Mean ± standard error (SE) values were calculated with Excel software. Statistical calculations were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with SPSS software, version 22 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Multiple comparisons among treatments with significant differences were conducted using the least significant difference (LSD) test (p < 0.05).

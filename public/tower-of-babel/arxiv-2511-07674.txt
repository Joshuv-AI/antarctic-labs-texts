Persistent Cost of Lipschitz Maps
Francisco J. Gozzi, Manuela A. Cerdeiro, Pablo E. Riera

Abstract
A 1-Lipschitz map between compact metric spaces f : X → Y f:X\to Y induces a homomorphism on the level of persistent d-homology of the associated Vietoris-Rips simplicial filtrations. This homomorphism naturally bounds the Bottleneck distance between said modules in terms of the maximum size of its kernel and cokernel persistence modules, a stable invariant which we refer to as the persistent cost associated to f f . We provide metric upper bounds on the persistent cost of a 1-Lipschitz map and revisit its stability.



Introduction

Persistent homology is a fundamental tool in the emerging field of topological data analysis (TDA) that extracts a vast amount of metric information by encoding homological features across scales.
The richness of this output coupled with its explainability has yielded significant insights across a spectrum of disciplines, including biology and time series analysis, among others, c.f.
[
13
,
15
,
8
]
, and been integrated into learning pipelines, c.f.,
[
9
,
10
]
.

We outline the standard pipeline for topological data analysis. Given a metric space
X
, the Vietoris–Rips filtration
(\VR_{s}(X))_{s\geq 0}
is constructed by including simplices whose vertex set has diameter at at most
s\geq 0
. As the parameter increases, the complex evolves from a discrete set to the full power set of
X
, encoding geometric information at multiple scales. Applying the homology functor in a fixed dimension
d
yields a persistence module: a sequence of vector spaces connected by linear maps that track the birth, persistence and death of homological features.

While the TDA community has primarily focused on analyzing individual point clouds, the functorial nature of these constructions naturally leads us to consider mappings between datasets.
Indeed, we advocate for a treatment of maps between spaces that parallels that of the spaces themselves, enabling us to address the geometry of functions from a persistent homology perspective.
In this context we shall consider the natural case of non-expanding, i.e., 1-Lipschitz, maps
f:X\to Y
between Vietoris-Rips filtrations of finite metric spaces which yields a homomorphism of point-wise finite dimensional persistence modules,

f_{*}:\operatorname{H}_{d}(\VR_{{}_{\bullet}}(X))\to\operatorname{H}_{d}(\VR_{{}_{\bullet}}(Y)).

A homomorphism of persistence modules defines kernel, cokernel, and image modules via point-wise linear algebra, along with naturally induced maps.
The
“size”
of the modules
\Ker(F)
and
\Coker(F)
, measured by their interleaving distance to the trivial module
\Delta
, serves as a measure of how far the map
F
is from a persistent isomorphism. This leads us to define the
persistent cost
of a
1
-Lipschitz function as the maximum interleaving distance of kernel and cokernel persistence modules to the diagonal,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}C(f)&=&\max\big\{d_{I}(\Ker(f),\Delta)\penalty\ ,\penalty\ d_{I}(\Coker(f),\Delta)\big\}\end{array}

(1)

This persistent cost bounds the interleaving distance between the modules associated to
X
and
Y
, by the Induced Matching Theorem of U. Bauer and M. Lesnick in
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
).

Building on this result, we contribute a metric upper bound for the persistent cost of non-expanding map
f:X\to Y
, showing that
C(f)
is bounded above
by its distortion and the Hausdorff distance between its image and the codomain,
f(X)\subseteq Y
. Specifically, we establish the second inequality in the following sequence.

d_{I}\left(\operatorname{H}_{d}(\VR_{{}_{\bullet}}(X)),\operatorname{H}_{d}(\VR_{{}_{\bullet}}(Y))\right)\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ C(f)\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \dist(f)+2\cdot d_{H}(f(X),Y)

(2)

From a purely algebraic perspective, as we range over all possible persistence module homomorphisms
F_{\bullet}:V_{\bullet}\to W_{\bullet}
, the infimum of the associated persistent cost attains the interleaving distance between the given modules. However, our interest lies in geometrically defined persistence homomorphisms, i.e. those induced by
1
-Lipschitz maps between the underlying spaces via the homology of their Vietoris-Rips filtrations. In this geometric context, we provide an example of two finite metric spaces that yield identical persistence diagrams while any 1-Lipschitz map is bounded away from zero in its associated persistent cost.

A prior concern is the stability of kernel and cokernel persistence modules which was addressed in
[
5
]
.
We provide a self-contained exposition of this fact that was claimed without proof. In doing so, we introduce a Gromov-Hausdorff type distance between maps
f:X\to Y
,
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
, of compact metric spaces that may be of independent interest.

Our results are developed within the framework of point-wise finite dimensional persistence modules and finite metric spaces, and naturally extended to q-tame modules that arrise from compact metric spaces.

The paper is organized as follows. A first section provides a self-contained exposition of the algebraic notions regarding persistence theory.
We then move on to the geometric of spaces, maps and distances. The last two subsection are focused on our contributed results on metric upper bounds for the cost, Theorem

1.1
Persistence Modules

A persistence module is a linear representation of
\mathbb{R}
, or a given subset
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, regarded as a poset. In other words, it is a functor

V:(\mathbb{R},\leq)\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{F}},

(3)

where

denotes the category of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{F}
with linear transformations as homomorphisms.

This, a persistence module is more than just a one-parameter family of vector spaces; as a representation of a poset, it carries
“inner persistence”
or
“transition”
maps

\varphi_{s<t}:V_{s}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \penalty\ }V_{t},

for each pair
s<t\in\mathbb{R}

\varphi\circ\varphi=\varphi,

the last equation being read as
\varphi_{s_{2}<s_{3}}\circ\varphi_{s_{1}<s_{2}}=\varphi_{s_{1}<s_{3}}

As a first and computationally relevant approach to the subject, the reader may consider the case of finite-dimensional spaces indexed over a finite set
0=\epsilon_{0}<\epsilon_{2}<\dots<\epsilon_{r}

V_{\epsilon_{0}}\to V_{\epsilon_{1}}\to\cdots\to V_{\epsilon_{r}}.

(4)

The key idea of persistence is that the dimension of
V_{s}
may be preserved (via an isomorphism) in
V_{t}
for

However, indexing over the real numbers has several advantages. It is less cumbersome, as it avoids the problem of refining different indexing sets. It is also more general, since any finitely indexed persistence module can be easily extended to a
\mathbb{R}
-indexed one.

We will first develop the theory within the setting of point-wise finite dimensional (p.f.d.) modules indexed over
\mathbb{R}

(\mathbb{F}^{I})_{(t)}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F},&t\in I,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with inner persistence maps:

\varphi_{s<t}=\begin{cases}Id,&s,t\in I,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The field

1.4
The right setup

We conclude this algebraic section by reviewing the framework of q-tame persistence modules, following

[
4
]
and
[
14
]
.

A q-tame persistence module over
\mathbb{R}
is a slightly more general notion of persistence module where the point-wise finite dimensional hypothesis is replaced by the weaker requirement that every inner persistence map
\varphi_{s<t}
be of finite rank.

A p.f.d. module can be retrieved from a q-tame one, for example one could consider
\hat{V}_{t_{0}}:=\lim_{s\to{t_{0}}^{+}}\operatorname{Im}(\varphi_{t_{0}<s})
.
In fact there is a quotient category, the observable category, retrieved from the stabilization of persistence modules up to direct sum with the
“ephemeral”
subcategory given by those persistence modules all of whose inner homomorphisms are trivial, i.e.,

\mathbf{Obs}:=\mathbf{Pers}/\mathbf{Eph},

where
\mathbf{Pers}:=[(\mathbb{R},\leq),\mathbf{Vect}_{\mathbb{F}}]
as before.
Ephemerous modules correspond to
“still-born”
persistence features of the form
[a,a]
.

Every q-tame persistence module is isomorphic to a p.f.d. one in
\mathbf{Obs}
, particularly to a unique interval-decomposable one with intervals of chosen decoration, i.e., type of strict or relaxed condition on endpoints:
[a,b],(a,b),[a,b)
or
(a,b]
.
The barcode associated to a q-tame module is understood to be that of an interval decomposition of its associated p.f.d. observable.

The homology of the Vietoris-Rips filtration over compact metric may not yield a p.f.d. persistence module but a q-tame one nonetheless.

Throughout the text "distance" is understood in the general sense as a symmetric non-negative extended real function on pairs for which the triangular inequality holds. However, it may only be a pseudo-distance in the sense that there is a degenerate non-unique set of points at zero distance. Considering the observable category solves the issue, as the pseudo-distance becomes a true distance once zero interleaved p.f.d. persistence modules become isomorphic in
\mathbf{Obs}
, see
[
12
]
. In particular, this makes the already introduced graphic representation as persistence diagrams or barcodes a faithful one, as in previous Figure
1

2.1
Vietoris-Rips filtration and maps

The Vietoris-Rips construction is a standard procedure by which a filtration of simplicial complexes is produced from an actual dataset, presented as a finite metric space. To be precise, for each
\epsilon\geq 0
define:

\mathrm{VR}_{\epsilon}(X)=\{\sigma\subseteq X\mid d(x,y)\leq\epsilon,\,\forall x,y\in\sigma\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X),

i.e., a collection of subsets of
X
.
Applying the d-dimensional homology functor
H_{d}(-,\mathbb{F)}
, with coefficients in a field
\mathbb{F}
, yields a persistence module
V_{\bullet}:=\operatorname{H}_{d}(\VR_{\bullet}(X))
with inner persistence maps
\varphi_{s<t}:=(\iota_{s<t})_{*}
.

Let us consider perturbations of
1
-Lipschitz functions as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A function between metric spaces,
h:X\to Y
, is said to be an
\epsilon
-quasi
1
-Lipschitz map if it satisfies the following inequality:

d_{Y}(h(x),h(x^{\prime})\leq d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+\epsilon.

The Vietoris-Rips construction is particularly well suited for our metric endeavors, as follows from the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
h:X\to Y
be an
\epsilon
-quasi
1
-Lipschitz map. Then
h
induces an
\epsilon
-degree persistence homomorphism:
h_{*}:H_{d}(\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}}(X))\to H_{d}(\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}+\epsilon}(Y)).

Moreover, if two
\epsilon
-quasi
1
-Lipschitz maps
h_{1},h_{2}:X\to Y
are within uniform distance
\delta\geq 0
, then they induce the same
(\epsilon+\delta)
-degree map on homology.

Proof.

The map
h
induces a simplicial map:

\hat{h}:\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}}(X)\to\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet},+\epsilon}(Y),\quad[x_{1}\dots x_{r}]\mapsto[h(x_{1})\dots h(x_{r})],

since a set
\sigma\subseteq X
of diameter
\ell
maps to a set
h(\sigma)\subseteq Y
of diameter at most
\ell+\epsilon
. This commutes with inclusions and thus gives a morphism between filtrations. The homology functor yields the desired persistence homomorphism.

For the second claim, suppose
d_{Y}(h_{1}(x),h_{2}(x))\leq\delta
for all
x\in X
. For any simplex
\sigma\in\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}}(X)
of diameter
\ell
, consider the union:

h_{1}(\sigma)\cup h_{2}(\sigma)\subseteq Y.

For any
y,y^{\prime}\in h_{1}(\sigma)\cup h_{2}(\sigma)
, we have:
- If both come from the same
h_{i}
, then
d(y,y^{\prime})\leq\ell+\epsilon
.
- If
y=h_{1}(x)
,
y^{\prime}=h_{2}(x^{\prime})
, then:

d(y,y^{\prime})\leq d(h_{1}(x),h_{2}(x))+d(h_{2}(x),h_{2}(x^{\prime}))\leq\delta+(\ell+\epsilon).

Thus, the diameter of
h_{1}(\sigma)\cup h_{2}(\sigma)
is at most
\ell+\epsilon+\delta
. This implies that
\hat{h}_{1}
and
\hat{h}_{2}
are contiguous as maps:

\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}}(X)\to\operatorname{VR}_{{}_{\bullet}+\epsilon+\delta}(Y),

and therefore induce the same map on homology.
∎

If

As natural examples one should consider the isometric inclusion of a sample withing a larger space

Definition 2.4

A correspondence between two sets is a relation between two subsets,

When these spaces

dist(\mathcal{C})=sup\Bigg\{|d(x,x^{\prime})-d(y,y^{\prime})|\penalty\ :\penalty\ (x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}\Bigg\}

Definition 2.5

Given metric spaces

d_{GH}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\,\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\inf}\{\!\penalty\ dist(\mathcal{C})\!\penalty\ \}.

2.2
Bounds on the Persistent Cost

We have all the necessary ingredient to take on the subject of this work which is the persistent cost associated to a non-expanding map between compact metric spaces. We shall provide natural upper bounds that control it and stability guarantees.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let

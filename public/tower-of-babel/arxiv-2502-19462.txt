A Proof of Generalized Kramers-Pasternack Relation using Hyper-Radial Equation
Avoy Jana

Abstract
Abstract We present a proof of the generalized Kramers-Pasternack relation using the hyper-radial equation approach. Following Kramers’ method, we manipulate the radial equation by multiplying it with an expression closely related to terms in the hyper-virial theorem. Through successive integrations by parts, we systematically derive the second Pasternack formula, extending its validity to arbitrary dimensions. This approach provides deeper insights into the algebraic structure of recurrence relations for diagonal radial matrix elements in quantum mechanics. Keywords two-term, three-term Pasternack relations, d-dimensional Hydrogen atom

The two-term recurrence relation, also known as the first Pasternack relation or the Pasternack inversion relation, is given by

\displaystyle\langle n,l|\hat{r}^{-k-2}|n,l\rangle=\left(\frac{2Z}{na_{0}}\right)^{2k+1}

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)Z^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\langle r^{k}\rangle-\frac{Z(2k+1)}{a_{0}}\langle r^{k-1}\rangle

II
Derivation Using Hyper-Radial Equation

The independent Schrodinger equation is given by

\hat{H}\psi=\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\nabla^{2}+V(r)\right)\psi=E\psi

(9)

where
V(r)
is the potential. For a spherically symmetric potential, the wave function separates as

\psi(r,\Omega)=R_{n\ell}(r)Y^{K}_{\ell}(\Omega)

(10)

where
R_{n\ell}(r)
is the radial part and
Y^{K}_{\ell}(\Omega)
are spherical harmonics. The Laplacian in spherical coordinates takes the form

\nabla^{2}=\nabla_{r}^{2}-\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{r^{2}\hbar^{2}}

(11)

with the radial Laplacian given by

\nabla_{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{d-1}\frac{d}{dr}\right)

(12)

The angular momentum operator satisfies

\hat{L}^{2}Y^{K}_{\ell}(\Omega)=\ell(\ell+d-2)\hbar^{2}Y^{K}_{\ell}(\Omega)

(13)

Rewriting the Hamiltonian in terms of the radial operator gives

\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{p}_{r}^{2}}{2\mu}+\hat{V}_{\text{eff}}(r)

(14)

where the radial momentum operator satisfies

\hat{p}_{r}^{2}=-\hbar^{2}\left(\nabla_{r}^{2}+\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4r^{2}}\right)

(15)

The effective potential (Ref.
[
23
]
) is

\hat{V}_{\text{eff}}(r)=V(r)+\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{2\mu r^{2}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4r^{2}}

(16)

Thus, the problem reduces to solving the radial Schrodinger equation with the effective potential, incorporating both the central potential and the centrifugal barrier. The radial Schrodinger equation can be written as

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\nabla_{r}^{2}+V(r)+\frac{\ell(\ell+d-2)\hbar^{2}}{2\mu r^{2}}\right]R(r)=ER(r)

(17)

Consider an arbitrary function which is independent of the involved
potential
V(r)
,

f(r)=r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}R_{n\ell}(r)

(18)

which gives us

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{4}r^{\frac{d-5}{2}}\left[(d-1)(d-3)R(r)+\right.

(19)

\displaystyle\left.4r^{2}\left[\frac{(d-1)R^{\prime}(r)}{r}+R^{\prime\prime}(r)\right]\right]

and we have

\nabla_{r}^{2}R(r)=\frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}\left(r^{d-1}\frac{\partial R(r)}{\partial{r}}\right)=\frac{(d-1)R^{\prime}(r)}{r}+R^{\prime\prime}(r)

(20)

By combining Eqs. (18–20), we can write

\nabla_{r}^{2}R(r)=\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4r^{2}}f(r)\right)

(21)

Using Eqs. (18,21) in Eq. (17) and rearranging bit,

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}=-\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}(E-V(r))+\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]\frac{1}{r^{2}}

(22)

Let dive into some manipulations,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}f^{\prime\prime}dr=-\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}E\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}fdr+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}V(r)fdr

(23)

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-2}fdr

Using simple integration by parts,

\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}f^{\prime\prime}dr=-\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k}f^{\prime}dr-k\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-1}f^{\prime}dr

(24)

and again using integration by parts,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k}f^{\prime}dr=-\frac{2}{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}f^{\prime\prime}dr

(25)

for which

\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}f^{\prime\prime}dr=\frac{2}{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}f^{\prime\prime}dr-k\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-1}f^{\prime}dr

(26)

It’s important to note that, the surface terms vanishes in every integration by parts as
f(r\to 0)=0
and
f(r\to\infty)=f^{\prime}(r\to\infty)=0
.
Now using Eq. (22),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}f^{\prime\prime}dr=-\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}E\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}fdr

(27)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k-1}fdr

Let try to find a general formula for
\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{p}fdr
, using integration by parts,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{p}fdr=p\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{p-1}fdr-\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{p}fdr

(28)

which gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{p}fdr=-\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{p-1}fdr

(29)

Using it, Eq. (27) will becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}f^{\prime\prime}dr=\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}E\frac{k+1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}fdr

(30)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}V(r)fdr

\displaystyle-\frac{k-1}{2}\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-2}fdr

From Eq. (26),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k+1}f^{\prime\prime}dr=\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}E\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}fdr

(31)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\frac{2}{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k}V(r)fdr

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{k-1}{k+1}\left[\frac{2k}{k+1}\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-2}fdr

Now equating Eq. (23) and Eq. (31),

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}2E\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}fdr

(32)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\left[\frac{2}{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr-\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k}V(r)fdr\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{2k}{k+1}\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}fr^{k-2}fdr=0

Now rewriting in good prescription,

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}2(k+1)E\langle r^{k}\rangle

(33)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\left[2\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr-(k+1)\langle r^{k}V(r)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle-\left[2k\left[\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4}+\ell(\ell+d-2)\right]-\frac{k(k^{2}-1)}{2}\right]\langle r^{k-2}\rangle

Let take a look on the term
\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr
a and using integration by parts as usual upon it,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr

(34)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(fr^{k+1}V^{\prime}(r)f+(k+1)fr^{k}V(r)f)dr

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr

which gives

2\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}r^{k+1}V(r)fdr=-\langle r^{k+1}V^{\prime}(r)\rangle-(k+1)\langle r^{k}V(r)\rangle

(35)

Finally from Eq. (33), we have can achieve generalized Kramers-Pasternack recurrence relation, is given as

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\hbar^{2}}2(k+1)E\langle r^{k}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{\hbar^{2}}\left[-\langle r^{k+1}V^{\prime}(r)\rangle-2(k+1)\langle r^{k}V(r)\rangle\right]

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{4}\left[(d-1)(d-3)+4\ell(\ell+d-2)-(k^{2}-1)\right]\langle r^{k-2}\rangle=0

III
Conclusion

For Hydrogen-like systems, we have the potential form,

V(r)=-\frac{ZKe^{2}}{r}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\mu}\frac{Z}{a_{0}r}

(37)

where
a_{0}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\mu Ke^{2}}
is Bohr radius. For
d
-dimensional Hydrogen-like systems, the energy eigenvalues (Ref.
[
23
]
) is given by

E_{n}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\mu}\frac{Z^{2}}{2\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}a_{0}^{2}}

(38)

Then the Kramers-Pasternack recurrence relation becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)Z^{2}}{\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\langle r^{k}\rangle-\frac{Z}{a_{0}}(2k+1)\langle r^{k-1}\rangle

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{k}{4}\left[(d-1)(d-3)+4\ell(\ell+d-2)-(k^{2}-1)\right]\langle r^{k-2}\rangle=0

Let start with the initial seeds

\langle r^{-1}\rangle=\frac{Z}{\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}a_{0}}\text{ and }\langle 1\rangle=1

(40)

Setting
k=1
in Eq. (39),

\displaystyle\langle r\rangle=\frac{a_{0}}{Z}\left[\frac{3}{2}\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}\right.

(41)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{8}[(d-1)(d-3)+4\ell(\ell+d-2)]\right]

which can be simplified to our known result (Ref.
[
23
]
),

\langle\hat{r}\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{a_{0}}{Z}[d^{2}+d(6n-2\ell-7)+2(3n^{2}-9n-\ell^{2}+2\ell+6)]

(42)

Setting
k=2
in Eq. (39),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r^{2}}\rangle=\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{Z^{2}}\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{5}{3}\frac{Z}{a_{0}}\langle r\rangle-\frac{1}{12}[(d-1)(d-3)+4\ell(\ell+d-2)]\right]

which can be simplified again to our known result (Ref.
[
23
]
),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{8}.\frac{\left(n+\frac{d-3}{2}\right)^{2}a_{0}^{2}}{Z^{2}}[d^{3}+d^{2}(12n-6\ell-12)

\displaystyle+d(30n^{2}-6\ell^{2}-12n\ell-78n+30\ell+47)

\displaystyle+(20n^{3}-12n\ell^{2}-90n^{2}+18\ell^{2}+24n\ell+130n-36\ell-60)]

While setting
d=3,2
, we can get the Kramers-Pasternack recurrence relation are given by

\frac{(k+1)Z^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\langle r^{k}\rangle-\frac{Z(2k+1)}{a_{0}}\langle r^{k-1}\rangle+\frac{k}{4}[(2\ell+1)^{2}-k^{2}]\langle r^{k-2}\rangle=0

(44)

\frac{(k+1)Z^{2}}{\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\langle r^{k}\rangle-\frac{Z(2k+1)}{a_{0}}\langle r^{k-1}\rangle+k\left[|\ell|^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{4}\right]\langle r^{k-2}\rangle=0

(45)

Using two-term relation of Eq. (4) and three term relation (for positive moments) of Eq. (39), we can set up a three-term relation for negative moments also.

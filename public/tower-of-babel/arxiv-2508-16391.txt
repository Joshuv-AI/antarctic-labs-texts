Lipschitz regularity for parabolic double phase equations with gradient nonlinearity
Abhrojyoti Sen and Jarkko Siltakoski

Abstract
. We establish the local Lipschitz regularity in space for the viscosity solutions to the parabolic double phase equation of the form ∂ t u − div ⁡ ( | D ​ u | p − 2 ​ D ​ u + a ⁡ ( z ) ​ | D ​ u | q − 2 ​ D ​ u ) = f ⁡ ( z , D ​ u ) \smash{\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\right)=f(z,Du)} by employing the Ishii-Lions method. In addition, we obtain Hölder estimate in time which turns out to be sharp in the degenerate regime. Here, 1 < p ≤ q < ∞ , 1<p\leq q<\infty, and the coefficient a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 is assumed to be bounded, locally Lipschitz continuous in space, and continuous in time. Furthermore, the non-homogeneity f f is assumed to be continuous on Ω × ℝ × ℝ N , \Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}, and to satisfy a suitable gradient growth condition. We also establish the equivalence between bounded viscosity solutions and weak solutions, under appropriate additional regularity assumption on the coefficient a . a.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Overview

In this paper, we consider the parabolic double phase problem with gradient nonlinearity which reads,

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\right)=f(z,Du)

Q 1.

Can a notion of viscosity solution be formulated for (

Q 2.

Can a Lipschitz regularity estimate be established for viscosity solutions to (

Q 3.

Can the notions of viscosity and weak solutions to (

We begin by reviewing the existing literature and providing motivation for addressing these three questions in the context of the parabolic double phase problem (

1.2.
Main results

In this section, we state our main results and compare them with the existing literature. Our first result is local Lipschitz regularity for (
1.1
) under the natural range of exponents.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
1<p\leq q\leq p+1
. Let the coefficient
a\geq 0
be continuous in time and Lipschitz continuous in space. Furthermore, assume that there is
C_{a}\geq 0
such that

(1.2)

a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x,s)\text{ for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in B_{1}\times(-1,0)\text{ with }t\leq s.

Let u be a continuous and bounded weak solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{1}\times(-1,0)
. If
q=p+1
, suppose additionally that
u
is uniformly continuous in space variable.
Then we have the following local estimate:

|u(x,t)-u(y,s)|\leq C\left(|x-y|+|t-s|^{\alpha}\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad(x,t),(y,s)\in B_{1/2}\times(-1/2,0),

where

\alpha=\begin{cases}\frac{p}{p+q}&\text{if }1<p<2,\\
1/2&\text{if }p\geq 2.\end{cases}

If
q<p+1
, then the constant
C
depends only on
N
,
p
,
q
,
\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|Da\|_{L^{\infty}}
, the constants appearing in the growth condition (
G
), and
\operatorname{osc}_{B_{1}\times(-1,0)}u
. If
q=p+1
, then the constant
C
also depends on the optimal modulus of continuity of
u
in space in
B_{1}\times(-1,0)
.

Combining the above theorem with Hölder estimates of bounded weak solutions
[

]
and Lemma 5.2, we have the following immediate corollary in the degenerate regime.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that

is continuous in time, Lipschitz in space, and that (

) holds. Suppose
that

) in

\left|u(x,t)-u(y,s)\right|\leq C\left(\left|x-y\right|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(y,s)\in B_{1/2}\times(-1/2,0).

Several remarks are in order to place our result in the context of the existing literature.

Remark 1.3
.

(i)  In
[
BDM13
]
, it is shown that the weak solution
u\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))
of (
1.1
) with bounded and space independent coefficients, i.e.,
a(x,t)=a(t)
and
f\equiv 0

satisfies a quantitative Lipschitz bound for the range
2\leq p\leq q\leq p+\frac{4}{n}
.
A similar result for the singular case was established in
[
Sin15
]
. More precisely, it is shown there that for
a(x,t)=a(t)
and
\frac{2n}{n+2}<p\leq 2
, the solution of (
1.1
) with the same assumptions above is Lipschitz continuous for
p\leq q\leq p+\frac{4}{n+2}
. However, no precise result is available in those works for the general case of space-time dependent coefficients.

Our result, in particular, applies to viscosity solutions. Furthermore, the range of admissible
p
and
q
in our setting is optimal and consistent with the Hölder regularity result established in
[
KMS25
]
.

(ii)  In
[
KMS25
]
, the authors study the regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) under the assumption that the coefficient
a
is Hölder continuous in both space and time. Under this assumption and
2\leq p\leq q\leq p+\alpha,
they prove that the solution exhibits Hölder continuity,
where
\alpha\in(0,1]
is the Hölder exponent of the coefficient
a.
This range is known to be optimal in general from the regularity theory of elliptic double phase equations.

In contrast, in this paper, we consider a stronger regularity assumption in space but weaker in time on the coefficient: we assume that
a
is Lipschitz continuous in space and continuous in time. Since Lipschitz continuity corresponds to the case
\alpha=1,
our result on the Lipschitz continuity of solutions aligns naturally with the framework of
[
KMS25
]
, and can be viewed as a sharp extension of their result to the endpoint case where the Hölder exponent attains its maximum. We point out that the local monotonicity type assumption (
1.2
) on
a
is required only to prove comparison principle, in particular to obtain “time approximation” (cf. Lemma
3.1
). As of now, to the best of our knowledge, some control over the “zero set of
a
” is essential in this context.

Beyond establishing spatial Lipschitz regularity, we also obtain improved regularity in time, particularly for the range
2\leq p\leq q<\infty
and for
f\equiv 0.
Additionally, for the singular range
1<p<2,
we impose the assumption that the solutions to (
1.1
) with
f\equiv 0
are continuous. This assumption is essential for formulating the notion of viscosity solutions in that setting. We note that proving the existence of continuous solutions to (
1.1
) in the singular regime requires substantially different techniques and falls outside the primary scope of this paper. We therefore leave this aspect for future investigation.

(iii)  Our approach to proving Theorem
1.1
is based on the celebrated Ishii-Lions method, which is a cornerstone technique in the theory of viscosity solutions. Although Theorem
1.1
is stated under the assumption of continuous weak solutions, we apply the Ishii-Lions technique to a more general auxiliary class of functions. Specifically, we consider the class
S
which is introduced in Section
4
of the paper. Roughly speaking, this auxiliary class contains functions that are both viscosity subsolutions to a relaxed “upper” equation of (
1.1
) and viscosity supersolutions to a relaxed “lower” equation of (
1.1
). The idea is that we do not need continuity from the lower-order terms of the equation to apply the Ishii-Lions method – we only need to bound them. In Lemma
4.3
, we show that weak solutions are contained within this class whenever they obey a comparison principle.

(iv)  Another important direction in the theory of partial differential equations involving different phases is the study of multi-phase equations. In such equations, the primary challenge lies in understanding the interaction between the different phases, each governed by distinct growth behavior. While the regularity theory for elliptic multi-phase problems is now fairly well-developed-see, for instance,
[
BBO21
,
DFO19
,
DF22
,
FRZ22
]
the parabolic counterpart has only recently begun to attract attention.
The prototypical elliptic multi-phase equation takes the form:

(1.3)

\displaystyle-\operatorname*{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}(x)|Du|^{p_{i}-2}Du\right)=0

in
\Omega
, where
1<p\leq p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{m}
and
0\leq a_{i}(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha_{i}}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
for all

The regularity theory for the parabolic analogue of this equation has only recently begun to emerge, with contributions in
[

,
KOS25
,
KO24
]
. We sketch the Ishii-Lions method to establish Lipschitz regularity for viscosity solutions in this setting, at least for

Naturally, such an approach would require the coefficients

to satisfy the assumptions of Theorem

.

Next we discuss our second result of the paper regarding the relationship of viscosity and weak solutions.

To show that weak supersolutions are viscosity supersolutions, we adapt the argument of
[

]
, which relies on a comparison principle for weak solutions. However, we could not find a correct reference for comparison principle for the equation (

appears to be open. The main difficulty stems from the

-dependence of

and the time part of the equation. Nevertheless, we show that a comparison principle holds under the monotonicity condition (

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq p+1
. Suppose that

be a local weak supersolution to (

\Xi

To show that viscosity supersolutions are weak supersolutions, we adopt the technique introduced by Julin and Juutinen
[

]
. In contrast to
[
JLM01
]
, we do not rely on the
uniqueness machinery for viscosity solutions. Instead, we approximate a viscosity supersolution

[

]

is also a weak supersolution in the smaller domain. We then prove a Caccioppoli-type estimate, which yields the convergence

in a suitable Sobolev space. Finally, in Theorem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\text{ is compact and }Da\text{ is continuous in }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\Gamma,\\
{\partial\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}:a(x,t)=0}\}\text{ has Lebesgue measure zero,}\end{cases}

Theorem 1.5

\begin{cases}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})=u(x_{0},t_{0}),\\

D\varphi(x,t)\not=0&\text{when }x\not=x_{0,}\end{cases}

then

\displaystyle\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})\\
x\not=x_{0},(x,t)\not\in\Gamma\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi(x,t)|^{p-2}D\varphi(x,t)+a(x,t)|D\varphi(x,t)|^{q-2}D\varphi(x,t)\right)

\displaystyle-f\left(x,t,D\varphi(x,t)\right)\Bigg]\leq 0.

In this case, we say that
\varphi

touches
u
from above at

\smash{(x_{0},t_{0})}
.
A function that is both viscosity sub- and supersolution is a
viscosity
solution
.

We show in Section
7
that if
a
is
C^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\Gamma
and the boundary of the zero set of
a
has measure zero, then bounded viscosity super- and subsolutions are local weak super- and subsolutions. However, for our regularity results, such control over the boundary of the zero set of
a
is not needed. For this reason, it is convenient to define a relaxed class of solutions that contains both continuous weak and viscosity solutions, called as
Class
S
(see Section
4
for the definition).

3.
Weak solutions are viscosity solutions

3.1.
Comparison principles

To show that weak solutions are viscosity solutions, we need some kind of comparison principle for weak solutions. To this end, we have to use a weak solution as a test function for itself. However, since in general weak solutions lack smoothness, an approximation is required. In the case of the elliptic
p
-Laplace equation, the approximation is straightforward since the solution is in a Sobolev space in which smooth functions are dense. On the other hand, with the
p
-parabolic equation, one has to deal with a time derivative which the solution, let alone a super- or subsolution, may not possess. This issue is often remedied by mollifying the equation in time or by using Steklov averages, see (
3.1
). However, for the parabolic double-phase equation, approximation is more complicated. Indeed, already in the elliptic setting, the regularity of the function
a
and the difference
q-p
are connected to the density of smooth functions in the Sobolev space corresponding to the functional
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p}+a(x)|\xi|^{q}
, see for example
[
B24
]
and the references therein. With the parabolic double-phase equation, a novel difficulty occurs with time-mollification: even if
a
is Lipschitz and independent of
x
, the Steklov average of a function
u\in W^{H}(\Xi)\cap C(\Xi)
may fail to be in
W^{H}(\Xi)
, see the example below.

For
\varphi:\Xi\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
, we define the left and right Steklov averages

(3.1)

\displaystyle[\varphi]_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t-h}^{t}\varphi(x,s)\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad[\widetilde{\varphi}]_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\varphi(x,s)\,ds,

where
\varphi
is extended by zero to
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<q<\infty
. Then in general,
u\in W^{H}(\Xi)\cap C(\Xi)
does not imply
[u]_{h}\in W^{H}(\Xi)
. To see this, let
N=1
and
\Xi:=(0,1)\times(-1,1)
. Define
a:\Xi\rightarrow[0,\infty)
,
a(x,t):=\max(-t,0)
.
For small
\epsilon>0
, consider the function
u(x,t):=\left|x\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}\max(t,0).

Then

\left|Du(x,t)\right|=(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)\left|x\right|^{-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}\max(t,0)

from which it follows that
u\in W^{H}(\Xi)
since

\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|Du\right|^{p}+\max(-t,0)\left|Du\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|Du\right|^{p}\,dx\,dt<\infty.

On the other hand, for
t\in(-h,0)
, we have

\displaystyle D[u]_{h}(x,t)=[Du]_{h}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)\left|x\right|^{-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}\max(s,0)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)\left|x\right|^{-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}\int_{t}^{t+h}\max(s,0)\,ds,

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{(t+h)^{2}}{2h}-\frac{t^{2}}{2h}\right)(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)\left|x\right|^{-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\max(-t,0)\left|D[u]_{h}\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-h}^{-h/2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{h}{2}|D[u]_{h}|^{q}\ \,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-h}^{-h/2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{h+2t}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)\right)^{q}\left|x\right|^{-\frac{q}{p}+\epsilon}\,dx\,dt,

where the second integral is not finite since
-\frac{q}{p}+\epsilon<-1
.
Hence,
[u]_{h}\not\in W^{H}(\Xi)
.

Nevertheless, we can establish a time-approximation result if
a
satisfies one of the conditions (
3.2
) or (
3.3
) below. The idea is, roughly speaking, that if
a
is decreasing in time in
Q_{r}
, we can use the left Steklov average to approximate
u\in W^{H}(Q_{r})
. On the other hand, if
a
is increasing in
Q_{r}
, we can use the right Steklov average. Observe that if
a
is bounded away from zero or identically zero in
Q_{r}
, then both (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) are trivial.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho>0
and
a:Q_{\rho}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be bounded and measurable. Suppose moreover that there exists a constant
C_{a}\geq 0
, independent of
(x,t)
, such that

(3.2)

a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{\rho}\text{ with }s\leq t.

or

(3.3)

a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{\rho}\text{ with }s\geq t.

Let
u\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})
. Then, if (
3.2
) holds, for any small
h_{0}>0
, we have
[u]_{h}\in W^{H}(B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0))
for all
h\in(0,h_{0})
. Further, up to a subsequence

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)}\left|D[u]_{h}-Du\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|D[u]_{h}-Du\right|^{q}\,dz=0.

If (
3.3
) holds, then we have the analogous result for
[\widetilde{u}]_{h}
.

Proof.

We only consider the case (
3.2
) and
[u]_{h}
as the proof for
[\widetilde{u}]_{h}
when (
3.3
) holds is analogous.
Fix
h_{0}>0
and suppose that
h<h_{0}
. For
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we denote
v:=\partial_{i}u
. By standard properties of Steklov averages, we have
\partial_{i}[u]_{h}=[\partial_{i}u]_{h}=[v]_{h}
. Then for almost every
(x,t)\in B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)
we have

(3.4)

a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}(x,t)-v(x,t)\right|^{q}\leq C(q)(a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}(x,t)\right|^{q}+a(x,t)\left|v(x,t)\right|^{q}),

where by (
3.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}(x,t)\right|^{q}=\left|\frac{1}{h}\int_{t-h}^{t}a^{1/q}(x,t)v(x,s)\,ds\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{t-h}^{t}C_{a}^{1/q}a^{1/q}(x,s)\left|v(x,s)\right|\,ds\right)^{q}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=C_{a}([a^{1/q}\left|v\right|]_{h}(x,t))^{q}.

Observe that since
a^{1/q}|v|\in L^{q}(Q_{\rho})
, we have
[a^{1/q}|v|]_{h}\in L^{q}(B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},\rho))
by standard properties of Steklov average
[
CDG17
, Lemma 2.4]
, and so the above estimate implies that

\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},\rho)}a(x,t)|[v]_{h}(x,t)|^{q}\,dx\,dt<\infty.

Therefore,
[v]_{h}\in W^{H}(B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},\rho)).

On the other hand, combining (
3.4
) and (
3.1
), we have for almost every
(x,t)\in B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)

that

a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}(x,t)-v(x,t)\right|^{q}\leq C_{a}([a^{1/q}\left|v\right|]_{h}(x,t))^{q}+a(x,t)\left|v(x,t)\right|^{q}.

Note that since
a^{1/q}\left|v\right|\in L^{q}(Q_{\rho})
, it follows from the standard properties of Steklov average (see
[
CDG17
, Lemma 2.4 and Lemma 2.5]
) that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)}C_{a}([a^{1/q}\left|v\right|]_{h})^{q}\,dx\,dt=\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)}C_{a}(a^{1/q}\left|v\right|)^{q}\,dx\,dt.

Moreover, since
u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0))
, we have

[v]_{h}\rightarrow v
almost everywhere in
B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)

up to a subsequence. It now follows from a well known generalization
of Lebesgue’s convergence theorem that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho+h_{0},0)}a(x,t)\left|[v]_{h}(x,t)-v(x,t)\right|^{q}\,dx\,dt=0.\qed

Next we consider space approximation. The following theorem is proven similarly to the elliptic result in
[
BGS22
]
, but we give details for the benefit of the reader.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq p+1
and
r>0
. Assume that
a:Q_{r}\rightarrow[0,\infty)

is bounded, measurable, and Lipschitz continuous in space. Suppose
that
u\in W^{H}(Q_{r})\cap L^{\infty}(-r,0;L^{1}(B_{r}))
is compactly
supported in
Q_{r}
. For
\delta>0
, define

u_{\delta}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\left(x-y,t\right)\varrho_{\delta}(y)\,dy,

where
\varrho_{\delta}
is the standard mollifier and the integrand
is read as zero when
x-y\not\in Q_{r}
. Then
\operatorname{spt}u_{\delta}\Subset Q_{r}

for all small enough
\delta>0
. Furthermore,
u_{\delta}\in W^{H}(Q_{r})
and

(3.6)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{Q_{r}}\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\,dz=0.

Proof.

We need to verify (
3.6
) and that

\int_{Q_{r}}|Du_{\delta}|^{p}+a(z)|Du_{\delta}|^{q}\,dz<\infty.

Step 1:
Let
d:=\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{spt}u,\partial Q_{r})
,
\delta_{0}:=d/8

and
r^{\prime}:=r-d/4
. Then
u_{\delta}\equiv 0
in
Q_{r}\setminus Q_{r^{\prime}}

for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
. Fix
(x,t)\in Q_{r^{\prime}}
, where

t
is such that
Du(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(B_{r})
and
\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-r,0;L^{1}(B_{r}))}
.
Further, assume that
\delta<\delta_{0}
and let
x_{t}^{\ast}\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x)

be such that

a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)=\inf_{y\in B_{\delta}(x)}a(y,t),

which is well defined since
B_{\delta}(x)\Subset B_{r}
. By Young’s
convolution inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|=\left|D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\left(x-y,t\right)\varrho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(y,t)D\varrho_{\delta}(x-y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-1}\left\|D\varrho\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(-r,0;L^{1}(B_{r}))},

\displaystyle=:\delta^{-1}C_{0}.

where we used that
\left\|D\varrho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\delta^{-1}\left\|D\varrho\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Using the above display, Lipschitz continuity of
a
in space variable,
as well as that
\left|x-x_{t}^{\ast}\right|\leq\delta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(a(x,t)-a(x_{t}^{\ast},t))\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}}{\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\delta\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\left(\delta^{-1}C_{0}\right)^{q-p}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+C_{0}\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\right)\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=:C_{1}\left(\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\right).

Further, since
\int_{B_{\delta}}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy=1
, we can
apply Jensen’s inequality to estimate

\displaystyle\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{\delta}}Du(x-y,t)\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|^{p}+a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|\int_{B_{\delta}}Du\left(x-y,t\right)\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}\left|Du(x-y,t)\right|^{p}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}a(x_{t}^{\ast},t)\left|Du\left(x-y,t\right)\right|^{q}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}\left|Du(x-y,t)\right|^{p}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy+\int_{B_{\delta}}a(y,t)\left|Du\left(x-y,t\right)\right|^{q}\rho_{\delta}(y)\,dy,

\displaystyle=(\left|Du\right|^{p})_{\delta}(x,t)+(a\left|Du\right|^{q})_{\delta}(x,t),

where the last estimate follows from the choice of
x_{t}^{\ast}
.
Combining the last two displays, we arrive at

(3.8)

\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{p}+a(x,t)\left|Du_{\delta}(x,t)\right|^{q}\leq C_{1}(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q})_{\delta}(x,t)

for all
x\in B_{r^{\prime}}
and almost all
t\in(-r^{\prime},0)
.

Step 2:
We define
w:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

by setting
w=(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q})
in
Q_{r}

and
w=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus Q_{r}.
Since
w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})

with compact support, we have
w_{\delta}\rightarrow w
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})

by standard properties of mollifiers. On the other hand, by (
3.8
),
we have

\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\leq C_{1}w_{\delta}\quad\text{almost everywhere in }Q_{r^{\prime}}.

It follows that
\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}

is uniformly integrable in
Q_{r^{\prime}}
with respect to
\delta
.
Therefore, by Vitali’s convergence theorem,

\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\rightarrow\left|Du\right|^{p}+a\left|Du\right|^{q}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(Q_{r^{\prime}}).

Then observe

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r^{\prime}}}\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{r^{\prime}}}2^{p-1}(\left|Du\right|^{p}+\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{p})+2^{q-1}a(z)(\left|Du\right|^{q}+\left|Du_{\delta}\right|^{q})\,dz,

so that
\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{p}+a\left|Du-Du_{\delta}\right|^{q}

is again uniformly integrable, and by Vitali’s convergence theorem,
the left-hand side of the above inequality tends to zero as
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
Since both
\left|Du\right|
and
\left|Du_{\delta}\right|
vanish
in
Q_{r}\setminus Q_{r^{\prime}}
(provided that
\delta<\delta_{0}
),
the claim follows.
∎

With time and space approximation at hand, we prove a comparison principle. Part of our argument for the comparison principle is inspired by the ideas of
[
IMJ97
]
. Although we shall work in the range
1<p\leq q\leq p+1,
we split the cases
1<p<q<2,

1<p<2\leq q
and
2\leq p.
To prove the comparison principle, we only use the assumption

(3.9)

\displaystyle a(x,t)\leq C_{a}a(x,s)\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{r}\text{ with }t\leq s.

Note that the above assumption on
a
is not optimal. For instance, one can easily see that the function

a(x,t)=\max\left\{-(x+t+1),0\right\},

does not satisfy the assumption on a cube
Q_{r}(x,t)
centered at a point
(x,t)
that lies on the line
x=-(t+1).

However, a comparison principle can be proven for this
a
by using a suitable change of variable. This approach gives hope to generalize the above assumption (
3.9
). We plan to return to this matter in a subsequent work.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq 2<p+1,
and
\rho>0
. Suppose that
a:Q_{\rho}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is Lipschitz in space, continuous in time, satisfies (
3.9
) and
f(z,\eta)
is locally Lipschitz in
\eta
. Let
u,v\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})\cap C(\overline{Q_{\rho}})
be weak sub- and supersolutions, respectively, to

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du)=f(z,Du)\quad\text{in }\,\,\,Q_{\rho}.

Also assume that
Du\in L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}).
Then, if
u\leq v
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}
, it follows that
u\leq v
in
Q_{\rho}.

Proof.

We split the proof into three steps. We shall start with the forward Steklov average given in (
3.1
) denoted as
[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}.

Step 1:
Suppose that
\varphi\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})\cap C(\overline{Q}_{\rho})
with
\operatorname{spt}\varphi\Subset Q_{\rho}
. Then the Steklov average
[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}
is well defined for small enough
h>0
. Moreover,
[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})
by Lemma
3.1
.
Further, set

([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}(y,t)\rho_{\delta}(y-x)\,dy.

Then by Lemma
3.2
we have that

(3.10)

\int_{Q_{\rho}}\left|D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right|^{p}+a(z)\left|D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right|^{q}\,dz\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\delta\rightarrow 0.

Since
([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}
is Lipschitz and compactly supported
in
Q_{\rho}
, it can be used as a test function in the weak formulation (
2.2
). This way, we obtain

(3.11)

\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}+\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du\cdot D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du\cdot D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}f(z,Du)\,dz\leq 0.

Now, observe that by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du\cdot(D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du\cdot(D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{Q_{\rho}}\left|Du\right|^{p}\,dz\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{Q_{\rho}}\left|D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right|^{p}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+\left(\int_{Q_{\rho}}a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q}\,dz\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\left(\int_{Q_{\rho}}a(z)\left|D([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right|^{q}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the right-hand side converges to zero as
\delta\rightarrow 0

by (
3.10
). Furthermore, convergence of the time term
in (
3.11
) is clear since
[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}
is Lipschitz
in time. For the nonhomogeneous term, we note that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}f(z,Du)\left(([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right)\,dz\leq\int_{Q_{\rho}}C_{f}\left(1+|Du|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\beta_{2}}\right)\left|([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}-[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\right|\,dz.

Since
\varphi\in C(\overline{Q_{\rho}})
, it is bounded and
|Du|^{\beta_{1}}([\tilde{\varphi}]_{h})_{\delta}\to|Du|^{\beta_{1}}[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}
pointwise, dominated convergence can be used to conclude

\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}+\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}-f(z,Du)[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\leq 0.

Computing the derivative of the Steklov average and using that
\varphi

has compact support in time, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{1}{h}\int_{-\rho}^{0}-u(x,t)(\varphi(x,t+h)-\varphi(x,t))\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\rho}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{-\rho}^{0}-u(x,t-h)\varphi(x,t)\,dt+\int_{-\rho}^{0}u(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dt\right]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{-\rho}^{0}\frac{1}{h}(u(x,t)-u(x,t-h))\varphi(x,t)\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{\rho}}\partial_{t}[u]_{h}\varphi\,dz.

Hence, we arrive at the following.

\int_{Q_{\rho}}\partial_{t}[u]_{h}\varphi+\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}-f(z,Du)[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\,dz\leq 0.

The analogous inequality holds for the supersolution
v
.

Step 2:
Let
v_{\ell}:=v+\ell
for
\ell>0
. We consider the function

\varphi(x,t)=m(u(x,t)-v_{\ell}(x,t))\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}(t),

where

m(s):=\max(s,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}1,&t\leq\tau-\varepsilon,\\
1-(t-\tau+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon,&\tau-\varepsilon<t\leq\tau,\\
0,&t>\tau.\end{cases}

for small
\varepsilon>0
and
\tau\in(-\rho,0)
. Observe that
\varphi\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})
.
Moreover,
\varphi
is compactly supported in
Q_{\rho}
since

u\leq v
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}
and
\beta
vanishes
near zero. Thus, we have

(3.12)

\int_{Q_{\rho}}\partial_{t}[u-v_{\ell}]_{h}\varphi\leq-\int_{Q_{\rho}}A\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}+\int_{Q_{\rho}}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\,dz

where we denoted

A:=(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{p-2}Dv)+a(z)(\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{q-2}Dv).

We set

G(s):=\begin{cases}s^{2}/2&s>0,\\
0,&s\leq 0,\end{cases}

and observe that
G^{\prime}=m
. Since
G

is convex, at any point
a\in\mathbb{R}
, its graph has a supporting
hyperplane
s\mapsto G(a)+G^{\prime}(a)\cdot(s-a)
.
That is, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

m(a)\cdot(a-b)\geq G(a)-G(b).

Denote
w:=u-v_{\ell}
and recall that
\partial_{t}[w]_{h}(x,t)=\frac{1}{h}w(x,t)-w(x,t-h)
.
So that taking
a=w(x,t)
and
b=w(x,t-h)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[w]_{h}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}(t)}{h}m(w(x,t))(w(x,t)-w(x,t-h))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}(t)}{h}\big(G(w(x,t))-G(w(x,t-h))\big)

\displaystyle=\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}(t)\partial_{t}[G(w)]_{h}(x,t).

Using the above estimate and integrating by parts, we obtain from
(
3.12
) that

-\int_{Q_{\rho}}[G(u-v_{\ell})]_{h}\partial_{t}\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}\,dz\leq-\int_{Q_{\rho}}A\cdot D[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}+\int_{Q_{\rho}}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|[\tilde{\varphi}]_{h}\,\,dz.

By Lemma
3.1
and Hölder’s inequality,
we can let
h\rightarrow 0
, to obtain

-\int_{Q_{\rho}}G(u-v_{\ell})\partial_{t}\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}\,dz\leq-\int_{Q_{\rho}}\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}A\cdot D(m(u-v_{\ell}))+\int_{Q_{\rho}}\beta^{\tau,\varepsilon}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|m(u-v_{l})\,dz.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, this yields by continuity for
all
\tau\in(-\rho,0)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\times\left\{\tau\right\}}G(u-v_{\ell})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}A\cdot D(m(u-v_{\ell}))\,dz

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|m(u-v_{l})\,dz

Observing that
G(u-v_{\ell})=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}(u-v_{\ell})
, the above inequality can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(u-v_{\ell})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|m(u-v_{l})\,dz

(3.13)

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}A\cdot D(m(u-v_{\ell}))\,dz:=I_{1}-I_{2}

Step 3:
Our aim is to absorb some part of
\smash{I_{1}}
into
\smash{I_{2}}
so that we can conclude from Grönwall’s inequality that
m(w):=m(u-v_{\ell})\equiv 0
almost everywhere. Since
\smash{f}
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
\eta
, there are constants

\smash{M\geq\max\left\{2\left\|Du\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})},1\right\}}

and
\smash{L=L(M)}
such that

(3.14)

\left|f(x,t,\xi)-f(x,t,\eta)\right|\leq L\left|\xi-\eta\right|\text{ when }\left|\xi\right|,\left|\eta\right|<M.

We denote
Q_{\tau}:=\left\{(x,t)\in B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau):m(w)>0\right\}
,

A:=Q_{\tau}\cap\left\{\left|Dv\right|<M\right\}\text{ and }B:=Q_{\tau}\cap\left\{\left|Dv\right|\geq M\right\}.

Observe that in
B,
using the growth condition (
G
),
choice of
M
and the assumption that
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left|f(z,Du)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}\left(1+\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\beta_{2}}\right)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}\left(M+M^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)M^{\beta_{2}}\right)\leq 2C_{f}\left(M^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)M^{\beta_{2}}\right)

and also

(3.16)

\left|f(z,Dv)\right|\leq 2C_{f}\left(\left|Dv\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)|Dv|^{\beta_{2}}\right)

Now we will estimate
I_{1}
in (
3.1
). It follows from (
3.14
), (
3.15
),
(
3.16
) and Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}L\left|Du-Dv\right|m(w)\,dz+\int_{B}\left(\left|f(z,Du)\right|+\left|f(z,Dv)\right|\right)m(w)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}L\left|Du-Dv\right|m(w)\,dz+\int_{B}4C_{f}\left(\left|Dv\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)|Dv|^{\beta_{2}}\right)m(w)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}\varepsilon\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}+C(\varepsilon,L)m^{2}(w)\,dz+\int_{B}\varepsilon\left|Dv\right|^{\frac{\beta_{1}p}{\beta_{1}}}+C(\varepsilon,p,\beta_{1},L,C_{f})m(w)^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}}\,dz

\displaystyle+\int_{B}\varepsilon a(z)|Dv|^{q}+C(\varepsilon,q,\beta_{2},L,C_{f})a(z)m(w)^{\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\varepsilon\int_{B}\left|Dv\right|^{p}\,dz+\varepsilon\int_{B}a(z)|Dv|^{q}\,dz

(3.17)

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon,p,q,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},L,C_{f},\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})},\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})})\int_{Q_{\tau}}m^{2}(w)\,dz,

where in the last step we used that
\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}>2
and
\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}>2
to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B}m(w)^{p/(p-\beta_{1})}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B}m(w)^{p/\left(p-\beta_{1}\right)-2}m(w)^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})}^{p/(p-\beta_{1})-2}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)\,dz,

and

\displaystyle\int_{B}a(z)m(w)^{\frac{q}{(q-\beta_{2})}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B}a(z)m(w)^{\frac{q}{\left(q-\beta_{2}\right)}-2}m^{2}(w)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})}^{\frac{q}{(q-\beta_{2})}-2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)\,dz.

To estimate
I_{2}
of (
3.1
), we use the following vector inequalities from
[
Lin17
, p98]
.

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left(\left|a\right|^{p-2}a-\left|b\right|^{p-2}b\right)\cdot\left(a-b\right)\geq\left(p-1\right)\left|a-b\right|^{2}\left(1+\left|a\right|^{2}+\left|b\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left(\left|a\right|^{q-2}a-\left|b\right|^{q-2}b\right)\cdot\left(a-b\right)\geq\left(q-1\right)\left|a-b\right|^{2}\left(1+\left|a\right|^{2}+\left|b\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}.

Using these inequalities, we obtain the following lower bound of
I_{2}.

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{p-2}Dv\right)\cdot Dm(w)\,dz

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}a(z)\left(\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{q-2}Dv\right)\cdot Dm(w)\,dz

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{Q_{\tau}}\frac{\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\left|Du\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz+(q-1)\int_{Q_{\tau}}\frac{a(z)\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\left|Du\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{A}\frac{\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}}{\left(1+M^{2}+M^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz+(p-1)\int_{B}\frac{\left(\left|Dv\right|-\left|Du\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(q-1)\int_{A}\frac{a(z)\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}}{\left(1+M^{2}+M^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}}\,dz+(q-1)\int_{B}\frac{a(z)\left(\left|Dv\right|-\left|Du\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(p,M)\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\left(p-1\right)\int_{B}\frac{\left(\left|Dv\right|-\frac{1}{2}M\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C(q,M)\int_{A}a(z)\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\left(q-1\right)\int_{B}\frac{a(z)\left(\left|Dv\right|-\frac{1}{2}M\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\{C(p,M),C(q,M)\}\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\left(p-1\right)\int_{B}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|Dv\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz

(3.20)

\displaystyle+\left(q-1\right)\int_{B}\frac{a(z)\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|Dv\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(3\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}}\,dz

where
C(p,M),C(q,M),C(p),C(q)>0
. Combining the estimates (
3.17
)
and (
3.20
) we arrive at

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon-C(p,M)\right)\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\left(\varepsilon-C(p)\right)\int_{B}\left|Dv\right|^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle+(\varepsilon-C(q))\int_{B}a(z)|Dv|^{q}\,dz+C_{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)\,dz,

where
C_{0}=C(\varepsilon,p,q,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},L,C_{f},\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}})
.
Recalling (
3.1
) and taking small enough

\epsilon
yields

\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(w)\,dx\leq 2C_{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)\,dz.

Since this holds for a.e.
\tau\in(-\rho,0)
, Grönwall’s inequality implies
that
m(w)\equiv 0
a.e. in
Q_{\rho}
. Finally, letting
l\rightarrow 0

yields that
u-v\leq 0
a.e. in
Q_{\rho}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<2\leq q
and
\rho>0
. Suppose that
a:Q_{\rho}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is Lipschitz in space, continuous in time, satisfies (
3.9
) and
f(z,\eta)
is locally Lipschitz in
\eta
. Let
u,v\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})\cap C(\overline{Q_{\rho}})
be weak sub- and supersolutions, respectively, to

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du)-f(z,Du)\leq-\delta\quad\text{in }\,\,\,Q_{\rho}.

Also assume that
Du\in L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}).
Then, if
u\leq v
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}
, it follows that
u\leq v
in
Q_{\rho}.

Proof.

Keeping the notations fixed and repeating the second step of the previous lemma, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(u-v_{\ell})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}|f(z,Du)-f(z,Dv)|m(u-v_{l})\,dz

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}A\cdot D(m(u-v_{\ell}))\,dz-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\delta m(u-v_{\ell})\,dz

(3.21)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\delta m(u-v_{\ell})\,dz.

Moreover, recalling
w=u-v_{\ell}
and following the estimate of
I_{1}
in the above Lemma
3.3
, we get an inequality

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}\varepsilon|Du-Dv|^{2}\,dz+\int_{B}\varepsilon\left(|Dv|^{p}+a(z)|Dv|^{q}\right)\,dz

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon,p,q,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},L,C_{f},\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})})\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m(w)^{2}+m(w)^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}}+m(w)^{\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}}-\delta m(w)\,dz.

To estimate
I_{2},
we use the following algebraic inequalities (see
[
Lin17
, p95]
)

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left(\left|a\right|^{p-2}a-\left|b\right|^{p-2}b\right)\cdot\left(a-b\right)\geq\left(p-1\right)\left|a-b\right|^{2}\left(1+\left|a\right|^{2}+\left|b\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left(\left|a\right|^{q-2}a-\left|b\right|^{q-2}b\right)\cdot\left(a-b\right)\geq 2^{2-q}\left|a-b\right|^{q}.

Indeed, using these inequalities, we estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{p-2}Dv\right)\cdot Dm(w)\,dz

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}a(z)\left(\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du-\left|Dv\right|^{q-2}Dv\right)\cdot Dm(w)\,dz

\displaystyle\geq(p-1)\int_{Q_{\tau}}\frac{\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\left|Du\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}\,dz+2^{2-q}\int_{Q_{\tau}}a(z)\left|Du-Dv\right|^{q}\,dz

\displaystyle\geq C(p,M)\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+C(p)\int_{B}|Dv|^{p}\,dz+2^{2-q}\int_{B}a(z)|Du-Dv|^{q}\,dz

(3.24)

\displaystyle\geq C(p,M)\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+C(p)\int_{B}|Dv|^{p}\,dz+C(q)\int_{B}a(z)|Dv|^{q}\,dz,

where in the last step we used

\displaystyle\left|Du-Dv\right|^{q}\geq\left(\left|Dv\right|-\left|Du\right|\right)^{q}\geq\left(\left|Dv\right|-\frac{1}{2}M\right)^{q}\geq C(q)\left|Dv\right|^{q}.

Therefore, combining the estimates (
3.17
) and (
3.24
), we get

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\delta m(w)\,dz\leq

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon-C(p,M)\right)\int_{A}\left|Du-Dv\right|^{2}\,dz+\left(\varepsilon-C(p)\right)\int_{B}\left|Dv\right|^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle+(\varepsilon-C(q))\int_{B}a(z)|Dv|^{q}\,dz

\displaystyle+C_{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)+m(w)^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}}+m(w)^{\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}}-\delta m(w)\,dz,

where
C_{0}
depends on
\varepsilon,p,q,L,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},C_{f},\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})}.
Thus, for small
\varepsilon,
we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(w)\,dx\leq C_{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)+m(w)^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}}+m(w)^{\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}}-\delta m(w)\,dz,

Observe that since
w
is bounded and
\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}},\frac{q}{q-\beta_{2}}>1
,
the integrand at the right-hand side is bounded by some constant times

m^{2}(w)
. To argue this rigorously, we write down the following algebraic
fact.

If
a_{0},\delta,\gamma>0
and
\alpha>1
, then there exists
C(\alpha,\gamma,\delta,a_{0})>0

such that

\gamma a^{\alpha}\leq\delta a+C(\alpha,\gamma,\delta,a_{0})a^{2}\text{ for all }a\in[0,a_{0}).

To see this, let first
\alpha<2
. Then by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\gamma a^{\alpha}=\gamma a\cdot a^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{1+a_{0}^{\frac{2}{3-\alpha}}}a^{\frac{2}{3-\alpha}}+C(\alpha,\gamma,\delta,a_{0})a^{\left(\alpha-1\right)\cdot\frac{2}{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta a+C(\alpha,\gamma,\delta,a_{0})a^{2}.

If
\alpha\geq 2,
then

\gamma a^{\alpha}=\gamma a^{\alpha-2}\cdot a^{2}\leq\gamma a_{0}^{\alpha-2}a^{2}.

Applying the algebraic fact on (
3.25
)
we get

\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(w)\,dx\leq C(p,q,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},L,C_{f},\delta,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}})\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}m^{2}(w)\,dz.

The conclusion now follows from Grönwall’s inequality and letting

l\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
2\leq p
and
\rho>0
. Suppose that
a:Q_{\rho}\rightarrow[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}m^{2}(u-v_{\ell})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}-\int_{B_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\tau)}\delta m(u-v_{\ell})\,dz.

Then by (
3.14
), (
3.15
),
(
3.16
) and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}L\left|Du-Dv\right|m(w)\,dz+\int_{B}\left(\left|f(x,Du)\right|+\left|f(z,Dv)\right|\right)m(w)\,dz

Lemma 3.6

u,v\in W^{H}(Q_{\rho})\cap C(\overline{Q_{\rho}})
be weak sub- and supersolutions, respectively, to

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du)=f(z,Du)\quad\text{in }\,\,\,Q_{\rho}.

Also assume that
Du\in L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}).
Then, if
u\leq v
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}
, it follows that
u\leq v
in
Q_{\rho}.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, define

u_{\varepsilon}:=u-\frac{\varepsilon}{T-t/2}.

Then for any non-negative test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{\rho})

we have by integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}\varphi+\frac{\varepsilon}{T-t/2}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}\varphi-\varphi\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left(T-t/2\right)^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}\varphi-\varphi\frac{\varepsilon}{2T^{2}}\,dz.

Since
f
is continuous, there exists sequence of locally Lipschitz continuous
functions
\{\smash{f_{\varepsilon}}\}
such that
\smash{\left\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4T^{2}}}

(see e.g.
[
Mic00
]
). Then, since
u
is a weak subsolution,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\varphi+\left(\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|^{p-2}Du_{\varepsilon}+a(z)|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}Du_{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot D\varphi-\varphi f_{\varepsilon}(z,Du_{\varepsilon})\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{\rho}}-u\partial_{t}\varphi+\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\right)\cdot D\varphi-\varphi f(z,Du)+\varphi\left\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}^{N}})}-\varphi\frac{\varepsilon}{2T^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{\rho}}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4T^{2}}\varphi\,dz.

Hence
u_{\varepsilon}
is a weak subsolution to

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}Du_{\varepsilon}\right)-f_{\varepsilon}(z,Du_{\varepsilon})\leq-\frac{\varepsilon}{4T^{2}}\quad\text{in }Q_{\rho}.

Similarly, since
v
is a weak supersolution, we define

v_{\varepsilon}:=v+\frac{\varepsilon}{T-t/2}

and deduce that
v_{\varepsilon}
is a weak supersolution to

\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}Du_{\varepsilon}+a(z)|Dv_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}Dv_{\varepsilon}\right)-f_{\varepsilon}(z,Dv_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{4T^{2}}\quad\text{in }Q_{\rho}.

Furthermore, we have
u_{\varepsilon}\leq v_{\varepsilon}
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}.
Hence, by using the comparison Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4

and
3.5
, we conclude that
u_{\varepsilon}\leq v_{\varepsilon}

a.e. in
Q_{\rho}
. Hence, we have

u\leq v+\frac{2\varepsilon}{T-t/2}\text{\quad a.e.\ in }Q_{\rho}.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
finishes the proof.
∎

Now, using the comparison principle, we will show that weak solutions are viscosity solutions.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq p+1
. Suppose that
a:\Xi\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is locally Lipschitz in space, continuous in time. Suppose that for any
(x,t)\in\Xi
, there is
r>0
such that
a
satisfies (
3.9
) in
Q_{r}(x,t)
with some constant
C_{a}=C_{a}(x,t,r)
. Let
u\in C(\Xi)
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
)
in
\Xi
. Then
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
1.1
) in
\Xi
.

Proof.

On the contrary, let there exists a
\smash{\varphi\in C^{2}(\Xi)}

touching
u
from below at
\smash{(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Xi}
,
\smash{D\varphi(x,t)\not=0}

for
\smash{x\not=x_{0}}
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})\\
x\not=x_{0}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi(x,t)|^{p-2}\varphi(x,t)+a(x,t)|D\varphi(x,t)|^{q-2}D\varphi(x,t)\right)

(3.30)

\displaystyle-f(x,t,D\varphi(x,t))\Big)<0.

It follows from above that there are
r>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.31)

\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi|^{p-2}D\varphi+a(z)|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)-f(z,D\varphi)<-\varepsilon/2\quad\text{in }\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\left\{x=x_{0}\right\}\cup\Gamma,

where
\mathcal{Q}_{r}:=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r,t_{0}).

Indeed, otherwise there would be a sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})

such that
x_{n}\not=x_{0}
and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(x_{n},t_{n})-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\phi(x_{n},t_{n})|^{p-2}D\phi(x_{n},t_{n})+a(x_{n},t_{n})|D\phi(x_{n},t_{n})|^{q-2}D\phi(x_{n},t_{n})\right)

\displaystyle-f(x_{n},t_{n},D\phi(x_{n},t_{n}))>-\frac{1}{n},

which contradicts (
3.30
). Further, taking smaller
r>0
if necessary, we may assume that
a
satisfies (
3.9
) in
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
with some constant
C_{a}\geq 0
.
Let
a_{j}
be the standard mollification of
a
in space direction.
By taking large enough
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\left\|a-a_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{r})}<\varepsilon/4
.
Since
|\Gamma|=0,
using
Gauss divergence theorem, we obtain for any non-negative test function
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{r})

that

\displaystyle\text{LHS}:=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\{x-x_{0}|\leq\rho\}}-\varphi\partial_{t}\psi+\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(z)|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)\cdot D\psi-\psi f(z,D\varphi)\,dz

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq\rho\right\}}\psi\partial_{t}\varphi-\psi\operatorname{div}\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(z)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)-\psi f(z,D\varphi)\,dz

(3.32)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\int_{t_{0}-r}^{t_{0}}\int_{\left\{\left|x-x_{0}\right|=\rho\right\}}\psi\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(z)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)\cdot\frac{(x-x_{0})}{\rho}\,dS\,dt\Bigg):=\text{RHS}

Note that, since
D\varphi(x,t)\neq 0
for
x\neq x_{0},
we use dominated convergence theorem in the first term of RHS above as
j\to\infty,
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq\rho\right\}}\psi\left(\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(z)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)-f(z,D\varphi)\right)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\{|x-x_{0}|\leq\rho\}}\psi\Big(\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi|^{p-2}D\varphi\right)-a_{j}(z)\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)

\displaystyle-|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\cdot Da_{j}(z)-f(z,D\varphi)\Big)\,dz

\displaystyle\overset{j\to\infty}{\to}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\{|x-x_{0}|\leq\rho\}}\psi\Big(\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi|^{p-2}D\varphi\right)-a(z)\operatorname{div}\left(|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)

\displaystyle-|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\cdot Da(z)-f(z,D\varphi)\Big)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\left\{\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq\rho\right\}}\psi\left(\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a(z)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)-f(z,D\varphi)\right)\,dz

Now using (
3.30
) and
\rho\to 0
in both sides of (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\text{LHS}:=\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}}-\varphi\partial_{t}\psi+\left(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(z)|D\varphi|^{q-2}D\varphi\right)\cdot D\psi-\psi f(z,D\varphi)\,dz\leq\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}}-\psi\varepsilon/2\,dz

Let
l:=\min_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{r}}\left(u-\varphi\right)>0
and set
\widetilde{\varphi}:=\varphi+l
.
Then tending
j\to\infty
in the above inequality, we conclude that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a weak subsolution
to

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|D\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{p-2}D\widetilde{\varphi}+a(z)\left|D\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{q-2}D\widetilde{\varphi}\right)-f(z,D\widetilde{\varphi})\leq-\varepsilon/2\quad\text{in }\mathcal{Q}_{r}

and on
\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{r}
it holds
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi+l\leq\varphi+u-\varphi=u
.
Since
a
satisfies (
3.9
) in
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
, it follows from Theorem
3.6
that
\widetilde{\varphi}\leq u

in
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
. But this is not possible since
\widetilde{\varphi}(x_{0},t_{0})>u(x_{0},t_{0})
and
u
is continuous.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

(1)

Note that in Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.6
, we assumed that the subsolutions are locally Lipschitz continuous. This assumption does not diminish the novelty of our results, as in the final proof of Theorem
3.7
, these results are applied to a
C^{2}
-regular function.

(2)

In our comparison principle, we assume the condition (
3.9
) on the whole cylinder. A more general comparison result is expected via covering arguments, but we do not pursue that here and postpone it for future work.

(3)

The continuity assumption on the sub (super) solution could also be replaced by boundedness and lower (upper) semicontinuity, provided that the assumption
u\leq v
on the boundary is interpreted in a suitable way.

4.
Lipschitz regularity for class
S

In this section, we apply the Ishii-Lions method from the theory
of viscosity solutions to prove Lipschitz estimates for an auxiliary class of relaxed viscosity solutions, denoted by
S
. In Section
5
we use these results on weak solutions to establish Theorem
1.1
.
To define the class
S
, we need the concept of relative semi-jets, given below.

We use
Q_{r}^{-}(x,t)
to denote parabolic cylinders that include the top. That is, we set

Q_{r}^{-}(x,t):=B_{r}(x)\times(t-r,t]\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{r}^{-}:=Q_{r}^{-}(0,0).

Definition 4.1

(Relative semi-jets)
.

Let
u:Q_{r}^{-}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. For
(x,t)\in Q_{r}^{-}
, we define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{Q_{r}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x,t):=\Big\{(\theta,\eta,X):u(y,s)\geq

\displaystyle\ u(x,t)+(s-t)\theta+(x-y)\cdot\eta+\frac{1}{2}(x-y)^{\prime}X(x-y)

\displaystyle+o(\left|x-y\right|^{2}+\left|s-t\right|)\quad\text{as}\quad(y,s)\rightarrow(x,t),(y,s)\in Q_{r}^{-}\Big\}.

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{Q_{r}^{-}}^{2,+}u(x,t):=\Big\{(\theta,\eta,X):u(y,s)\leq

\displaystyle\ u(x,t)+(s-t)\theta+(x-y)\cdot\eta+\frac{1}{2}(x-y)^{\prime}X(x-y)

\displaystyle+o(\left|x-y\right|^{2}+\left|s-t\right|)\quad\text{as}\quad(y,s)\rightarrow(x,t),(y,s)\in Q_{r}^{-}\Big\}.

We call
\mathcal{P}_{Q_{r}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x,t)
the second order subjet of
u
at
(x,t)
relative to
Q_{r}^{-}
and
\mathcal{P}_{Q_{r}^{-}}^{2,+}u(x,t)
the second order superjet of
u
at
(x,t)
relative to
Q_{r}^{-}.

Suppose that
a:Q_{1}^{-}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is Lipschitz in space variable. Then we define

g(x,t,\eta):=||Da||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}|\eta|^{q-1}+C_{f}(1+|\eta|^{\beta_{1}}+a(x,t)|\eta|^{\beta_{2}}),

where
C_{f},\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are the constants of the growth condition (
G
).

Definition 4.2

(Class
S
)
.

Let
u:Q_{1}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}

be continuous. We denote
u\in\texttt{S}(Q_{1}^{-})
if whenever
(\theta,\eta,X)\in\mathcal{P}_{Q_{1}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x,t)

with
\eta\not=0
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\theta-F((x,t),\eta,X)+g((x,t),\eta)\geq 0

and whenever
(\theta,\eta,X)\in\mathcal{P}_{Q_{1}^{-}}^{2,+}u(x,t)
with
\eta\not=0
,
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\theta-F((x,t),\eta,X)-g((x,t),\eta)\leq 0.

In the definition of class
S
, we rule out test functions whose gradient vanishes because in the Ishii-Lions method, we only need test functions whose gradient is large.
In the next lemma, we verify that continuous weak solutions to (
1.1
)
belong in
S
under the natural range of exponents, provided that weak solutions satisfy the comparison principle. The argument is the same as the one used to show that weak solutions are viscosity solutions, but we give details for completeness. The general technique goes back to at least
[
Juu01
]
. In what follows, we denote

CWS
:= set of all continuous weak solutions to (
1.1
), and
VS
:= set of all viscosity solutions to (
1.1
).

Lemma 4.3

(
CWS

\subset

S
)
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
\beta_{1}\in[1,p)
,
\beta_{2}\in[1,q)

and
a:Q_{1}^{-}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be Lipschitz in space and continuous in time. Suppose, moreover, that
a
satisfies (
3.9
) in
Q_{1}
. Let
u:Q_{1}^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuous
and a weak solution to (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
. Then
u\in\texttt{S}(Q_{1}^{-})
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
u\not\in\texttt{S}(Q_{1}^{-})
. Then one of the
inequalities in Definition
4.2
fails. We may assume
that it is the first one, as the case of the other one is analogical.
There exists
(\theta,\eta,X)\in\mathcal{P}_{Q_{1}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x,t)
,

\eta\not=0
, such that for some
\delta>0

\theta-F(x,t,\eta,X)+g(x,t,\eta)<-\delta.

A standard argument (as in the elliptic case
[
Kat15
, Lemma 10, p29]
) yields
\varphi\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{-})
such that
(\theta,\eta,X)=(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t),D\varphi(x,t),D^{2}\varphi(x,t))
,
where
\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)
means the left-derivative if
t=T
,
and
\varphi(x,t)=u(x,t)
,
\varphi(y,s)<u(y,s)
if
(y,s)\not=(x,t)
.
It follows from continuity and the fact
\eta\not=0
that there is
\rho>0
such that

(4.3)

\partial_{t}\varphi(y,s)-F(y,s,D\varphi(y,s),D^{2}\varphi(y,s))+g(y,s,D\varphi(y,s))<-\delta/2\quad\text{in }Q_{\rho}(x,t).

and
D\varphi\not=0
in
Q_{\rho}(x,t)
. Let
a_{j}
be the standard mollification of
a
in space direction.
By taking large enough
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we can make
\left\|a-a_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2})}
arbitrarily small.
Then, assuming
\rho<1/2
if necessary, we can ensure
that (below we write simply
D\varphi=D\varphi(y,s)
and so on)

\left|a(y,s)-a_{j}(y,s)\right|\left|\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi+(q-2)\frac{D\varphi^{\prime}D^{2}\varphi D\varphi}{\left|D\varphi\right|^{2}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{4}\quad\text{in }Q_{\rho}(x,t)

for all large enough
j
. Further, by Lipschitz continuity, we have

\left\|Da_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2})}\leq\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.
Now we have in
Q_{\rho}(x,t)
(below we apply the growth condition (
G
) on
f
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}(y,s)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi)-f(y,s,D\varphi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi)-a_{j}(y,s)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi+(q-2)\frac{D\varphi^{\prime}D^{2}\varphi D\varphi}{\left|D\varphi\right|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\cdot Da_{j}(y,s)-f(y,s,D\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi)-a(y,s)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi+(q-2)\frac{D\varphi^{\prime}D^{2}\varphi D\varphi}{\left|D\varphi\right|^{2}}\right)+\frac{\delta}{4}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-1}\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+C_{f}(1+\left|\eta\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(y,s)|\eta|^{\beta_{2}})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\varphi-F(y,s,D\varphi,D^{2}\varphi)+g(y,s,D\varphi)+\frac{\delta}{4}

\displaystyle<-\frac{\delta}{4},

where in the last inequality we used (
4.3
). Now,
let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}(x,t))
be a non-negative test
function. Multiplying the above inequality by
\phi
and integrating
by parts, we obtain

\int_{Q_{\rho}(x,t)}-\varphi\partial_{t}\phi-\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi-a_{j}(z)\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\phi-\phi f(z,D\varphi)\,dz\leq 0.

Thus, letting
j\rightarrow\infty
, we see that
\varphi
is a weak
subsolution to (
1.1
) in
Q_{\rho}(x,t)
. Then so is
\tilde{\varphi}:=\varphi+l
,
where
l:=\inf_{(y,s)\in\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}(x,t)}u-\varphi>0
.
Since
\tilde{\varphi}\leq u
on
\partial_{\mathcal{P}}Q_{\rho}(x,t)
,
it follows from comparison principle in Lemma
3.6
that
\tilde{\varphi}\leq u

in
Q_{\rho}(x,t)
. But this is a contradiction since
\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)=\varphi(x,t)+l=u(x,t)+l>u(x,t)
.
∎

Next we show that viscosity solutions are in
S
. To extend the viscosity inequality to the top of the cylinder, we use an argument similar to the one in
[
DFO14
, p209]
.

Lemma 4.4

(
VS

\subset

S
)
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
\beta_{1}\in[1,p)
,
\beta_{2}\in[1,q)

and
a:Q_{1}^{-}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be Lipschitz in space and
continuous in time. Suppose that
u\in C(Q_{1}^{-})
is a viscosity
solution to (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
. Then
u\in\texttt{S}(Q_{1}^{-})
.

Proof.

Let
(\theta,\eta,X)\in\mathcal{P}_{Q_{1}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\eta\not=0
, with
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}^{-}
. We need to verify
the inequality (
4.1
). If
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}
, then
\mathcal{P}_{Q_{1}^{-}}^{2,-}u(x_{0},t_{0})=\mathcal{P}^{2,-}u(x_{0},t_{0})

and the inequality (
4.1
) follows immediately from the definition of viscosity solutions. Therefore, it suffices
to consider the case
t_{0}=0
. We may also suppose that
x_{0}=0

for brevity. By a standard construction, there exists
\varphi\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{-})

such that it touches
u
from below at
(0,0)
in
Q_{1}^{-}
and

(\theta,\eta,X)=(\partial_{t}\varphi(0,0),D\varphi(0,0),D^{2}\varphi(0,0))
,
where
\partial_{t}\varphi(0,0)
is the left-derivative. That is,
we have

\varphi(0,0)=u(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(x,t)<u(x,t)\quad\text{when }(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}.

We denote
w(x,t):=u(x,t)-\varphi(x,t)
and

(4.4)

\nu(h):=\inf_{(x,t)\in Q_{3/4}\cap\left\{\left|x\right|+\left|t\right|>h\right\}}w(x,t).

Observe that
\nu(h)>0
for all
h>0
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
define
\varphi^{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}(Q_{1})
by

\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\varphi(x,t)-\frac{\varepsilon}{\left|t\right|}=\varphi(x,t)+\frac{\varepsilon}{t}

so that

w^{\varepsilon}(x,t):=u(x,t)-\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=w(x,t)+\varepsilon/\left|t\right|.

Now, let
(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times[-1/2,0)

be such that

(4.5)

\tilde{w}(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})=\inf_{(y,s)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times[-1/2,0)}\tilde{w}(y,s).

We have that
(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(0,0)

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Indeed, otherwise, passing to a subsequence
if necessary, we would have
\left|x^{\varepsilon}\right|+\left|t^{\varepsilon}\right|>h

for some
h>0
and all
\varepsilon>0
. Then by (
4.4
)

\tilde{w}(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})>\nu(h)\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon>0.

However, this is not possible, since
(0,\varepsilon^{1/2}/2)\in\overline{B}_{1/2}\times[-1/2,0)
,
and so by definition of
(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})
in (
4.5
),
we have

\tilde{w}(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\leq\tilde{w}(0,\varepsilon^{1/2}/2)=w(0,\varepsilon^{1/2}/2)+\frac{2\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{1/2}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where we used continuity of
w
and that
w(0,0)=0
.

Since
(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(0,0)
and
\eta=\varphi(0,0)\not=0
,
by continuity we have
D\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\not=0

for all small
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, since
u-\varphi^{\varepsilon}

has a minimum at
(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})
and
u
is a viscosity solution, we have

\partial_{t}\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\geq\Delta_{p}\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})+a(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})\Delta_{q}\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})-g(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon},D\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})).

On the other hand, we have also

\partial_{t}\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})=\partial_{t}\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon})-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}\leq\partial_{t}\varphi(x^{\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon}).

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

4.1.
The Ishii-Lions method

Comparing two solutions of a partial differential equation is a fundamental step toward establishing uniqueness. For a wide class of elliptic and parabolic equations, such comparison principles can indeed be proved. When working within the framework of viscosity solutions, a common and powerful technique is the so-called “doubling of variables”. A similar idea appears in the context of hyperbolic conservation laws and Hamilton-Jacobi equations, where the
L^{1}
-contraction principle is also derived via doubling the variables, which essentially leads to the uniqueness of solutions.

In the context of the comparison principle, the core idea is to construct a test function that penalizes the difference between the solutions at two points and analyze its maximum. To illustrate, one considers the function

\displaystyle\Phi_{u,v,\gamma}(x,y):=u(x)-v(y)-\underbrace{\frac{\gamma}{2}|x-y|^{2}}_{=\varphi(|x-y|)}

studies its maximum over
\Omega\times\Omega,
sending
\gamma\to+\infty.
This structure allows one to compare to
u
and
v
and derive uniqueness results.

In their seminal work
[
IL90
]
, Ishii and Lions observed that this method can also be adapted to obtain regularity estimates for viscosity solutions. Specifically, to prove that a solution
u
is spatially continuous with modulus
\varphi
, it suffices to show that

\sup_{(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}}\left(u(x)-u(y)-\varphi(|x-y|)\right)\leq 0.

Assuming the contrary–i.e., the supremum is positive–one arrives at a contradiction by analyzing the behavior at the maximum point. If this contradiction can be reached, the conclusion is that
u
is continuous with the modulus of continuity governed by
\varphi
.

In the parabolic setting, we need to modify this idea to account for the time variable. That is, we consider

\Phi(x,y,t):=u(x,t)-u(y,t)-L\varphi(|x-y|),

and aim to show that
\Phi(x,y,t)\leq 0
for all
(x,y,t)\in B_{1}\times B_{1}\times(0,T)
. To prove this, we assume toward a contradiction that there exists a point
(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})
such that
\Phi(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})>0
. The contradiction then arises by applying the viscosity inequality at the maximum point and carefully estimating the derivatives using the structure of the equation. The proof proceeds in two stages:

(1)

Hölder regularity:
We first choose
\varphi(s)=s^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
and establish that
u
is spatially Hölder continuous. The concavity of
\varphi
plays a key role in handling the second-order terms in the viscosity inequalities.

(2)

Lipschitz regularity:
With the Hölder continuity already established, we refine the estimate by choosing a test function of the form
\varphi(s)=s-\kappa s^{\beta}
for appropriate constants
\kappa>0
and
\beta\in(1,2)
. This perturbation accounts for the fact that the second derivative of the Lipschitz candidate
\varphi(s)=s
vanishes, and the small convex correction
\kappa s^{\beta}
provides the necessary curvature to close the argument. This ultimately yields the desired spatial Lipschitz continuity of the solution
u
.

\displaystyle-K

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{H}\varphi^{\prime})^{p-2}\Big(C_{H}\varphi^{\prime\prime}+(C_{H}\varphi^{\prime}){}^{\gamma}+\sqrt{K}(C_{H}\varphi^{\prime})^{\gamma-1}\frac{\omega^{1/2}(\left|z\right|)}{\left|z\right|}\Big)

\displaystyle-K

\displaystyle\leq C(L\varphi^{\prime})^{p-2}\Big(L\varphi^{\prime\prime}+(L\varphi^{\prime}){}^{\gamma}+\sqrt{K}(L\varphi^{\prime})^{\gamma-1}\frac{\omega^{1/2}(\left|z\right|)}{\left|z\right|}\Big)

. Then, for

, we have that

K\left|x\right|^{\beta}=C_{0}A^{1-\beta}\left|x\right|^{\beta}\geq 2(\operatorname{osc}u)A^{1-\beta}\left|x\right|^{\beta}=2\operatorname{osc}u.

Hence,
u\leq\varphi
on
[t_{0},0]\times\partial B_{\rho}
. We
also note that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\rho=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{A^{1-\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}=\left(\frac{C_{0}}{K}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}=C_{0}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}K^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}.

On the other hand, since
u
is
L
-Lipschitz in space variable,
for all
x\in B_{1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t_{0})-u(x,t_{0})

\displaystyle=u(0,t_{0})-u(x,t_{0})+A+\Theta(t-t_{0})+K\left|x\right|^{\beta}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\geq-L\left|x\right|+A+K\left|x\right|^{\beta}=:f(\left|x\right|),

where we denoted
f(r):=A-Lr+Kr^{\beta}
. Observe that

f^{\prime}(r)=-L+K\beta r^{\beta-1}=0\iff r=\left(\frac{L}{K\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}=:r_{0}

so that

\displaystyle\min_{r\in[0,\infty)}f(r)=f(r_{0})

\displaystyle=A-L\left(\frac{L}{K\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}+K\left(\frac{L}{K\beta}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}

\displaystyle=A+K^{-\frac{1}{\beta-1}}L^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\left(-\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}+\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq A-\left(\frac{1}{K}\frac{L^{\beta}}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\geq 0,

where in the last inequality we used the definition of
K
. Therefore,
by (
5.2
) we have
u\leq\varphi
on
B_{1}\times\left\{t_{0}\right\}
.
So far we have shown that for any
\Theta\geq 0

(5.3)

u\leq\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial_{\mathcal{P}}(B_{\rho}\times[t_{0},0]).

Step 2:
Next, we select
\Theta
so large that
\varphi

will be a weak supersolution to the double phase equation in
B_{\rho}\times(t_{0},0)
.
To this end, let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\rho}\times(t_{0},0))

be non-negative, and consider a point
(x,t)\in\operatorname{spt}\phi,

x\not=0
.
Since
x\not=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{p}\varphi(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(p-1)\left|D\varphi(x,t)\right|^{p-2}\left\|D^{2}\varphi(x,t)\right\|

\displaystyle=(p-1)\left|K\beta\left|x\right|^{\beta-1}\right|^{p-2}\left\|K\beta\left|x\right|^{\beta-2}I+K\beta(\beta-2)x\otimes x\left|x\right|^{\beta-4}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p)K^{p-1}\left|x\right|^{(\beta-1)(p-1)-1}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=C(N,p)K^{p-1},

where we used that
K\geq 1
and that
(\beta-1)(p-1)-1=0
. On the
other hand, denoting by
a_{j}
the standard mollification of
a
,
and using (
5.1
) as well as that
\left|x\right|\leq\rho
,
we have also

\displaystyle a_{j}(x,t)\left|\Delta_{q}\varphi(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}})K^{q-1}\left|x\right|^{(\beta-1)(q-1)-1}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}})K^{q-1}\big(C_{0}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}K^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\big)^{(\beta-1)(q-1)-1}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=C(N,p,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\operatorname{osc}u,L)K^{\frac{q}{\beta}}

for all large
j
. Note that, here we used
(\beta-1)(q-1)\geq 1.
Further, we have similarly

\displaystyle\left|D\varphi(x,t)\right|^{q-2}D\varphi(x,t)\cdot Da_{j}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{q-1}\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}}K^{q-1}\left|x\right|^{(\beta-1)(q-1)}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q,\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\operatorname{osc}u,L)K^{\frac{q}{\beta}}.

and using also growth condition (
G
)

\displaystyle\left|f(x,t,D\varphi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}\left(1+\left|D\varphi(x,t)\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(x,t)\left|D\varphi(x,t)\right|^{\beta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{f}\left(1+\beta^{\beta_{1}}K^{\beta_{1}}\left|x\right|^{(\beta-1)\beta_{1}}+a(x,t)\beta^{\beta_{2}}K^{\beta_{2}}\left|x\right|^{(\beta-1)\beta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{f}\left(1+\beta^{\beta_{1}}C_{0}^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta}}K^{\beta_{1}\big(1-\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\big)}+a(x,t)\beta^{\beta_{2}}C_{0}^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta}}K^{\beta_{2}(1-\frac{\beta-1}{\beta})}\right)

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}},C_{f},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}},L)K^{\frac{q}{\beta}},

where in the last inequality we used that
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}<q
and
K\geq 1
.
Combining the estimates (
5.4
)-(
5.7
), we see that in the support of
\phi
and outside of the origin, for
all large
j
, it holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi)+f(x,t,D\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}K^{\frac{q}{\beta}},

for some
C_{1}
that depends only on data, oscillation of
u
and

L
. Taking

\Theta:=C_{1}K^{\frac{q}{\beta}},

we obtain that

\partial_{t}\varphi-\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi)\geq 0\quad\text{in }\operatorname{spt}\phi\cap\big((B_{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\})\times(t_{0},0)\big)

for all large
j
. Multiplying this by
\phi
and applying the divergence
theorem, we see that for all
r>0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{r}}\phi\partial_{t}\varphi-\phi\operatorname{div}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi+a_{j}\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{0}\int_{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{r}}-\varphi\partial_{t}\phi+\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}D\varphi\cdot D\phi+a_{j}\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2}D\varphi\cdot D\phi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{t_{0}}^{0}\int_{\partial B_{r}}(\left|D\varphi\right|^{p-2}+a_{j}\left|D\varphi\right|^{q-2})D\varphi\cdot\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}\,dS(x)\,dt.

Letting
r\rightarrow 0
and
j\rightarrow\infty
, we see that
\varphi

is a weak supersolution.

Step 3:
From the comparison principle it follows that
u\leq\varphi

in
B_{\rho}\times[t_{0},0]
. In particular, we have by definition of

\Theta
and
K

\displaystyle u(0,s_{0})-u(0,t_{0})\leq\varphi(0,s_{0})-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle=A+C_{1}K^{\frac{q}{\beta}}(s_{0}-t_{0})

\displaystyle=A+C_{1}C_{0}^{\frac{q}{\beta}}A^{-\frac{q}{p}}(s_{0}-t_{0}).

Recalling that
A=(s_{0}-t_{0})^{\frac{p}{p+q}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle u(0,s_{0})-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(s_{0}-t_{0})^{\frac{p}{p+q}}+C(s_{0}-t_{0})^{1-\frac{p}{p+q}\cdot\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=(s_{0}-t_{0})^{\frac{p}{p+q}}+C(s_{0}-t_{0})^{\frac{p}{p+q}}.

A lower bound follows similarly, but using the subsolution
u(0,t_{0})-A-\Theta(t-t_{0})-K\left|x\right|^{\beta}
.
Since
s_{0}\in(t_{0},0]
was arbitrary, it follows that
u
is

\frac{p}{p+q}
-Hölder continuous in time at
(0,t_{0})
.
∎

In the degenerate range we can improve the time Hölder exponent to

1/2
. The reason is that if
p\geq 2
and
u
is Lipschitz continuous,
then the term
\left|Du\right|^{p-2}
that appears in
p
-Laplacian
is bounded, and we can choose
\beta=2
in the barrier function.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose the same assumptions as in Lemma
5.1
, with
the exponents
p
and
q
restricted to the degenerate range
2\leq p\leq q\leq p+1
.
Then we have

\left|u(0,t)-u(0,s)\right|\leq C\left|t-s\right|^{1/2}\quad\text{for all }t,s\in(-1,0),

where the constant
C
depends only on
N
,
p
,
q
,
\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(-1,0))}
,

\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(-1,0))}
, the
constants in the growth condition,
\operatorname{osc}_{B_{1}\times(-1,0)}
and

L
.

Proof.

Step 1:
This step is same as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, except
we consider the barrier

\varphi(x,t):=u(0,t_{0})+A+\Theta(t-t_{0})+K\left|x\right|^{2},

with
\Theta\geq 0
chosen later,

A:=(s_{0}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad K:=C_{0}A^{-1}:=4(\operatorname{osc}u+1)(L+1)^{2}A^{-1}.

One verifies as in Step 1 of proof of Lemma
5.1
that for any
\Theta\geq 0
,
we have

u\leq\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial_{\mathcal{P}}(B_{1}\times[t_{0},0]).

Step 2:
We use a comparison argument to show that, provided

\Theta
is chosen to be large enough, we have

(5.8)

u\leq\varphi\quad\text{in }B_{1}\times(t_{0},0].

To this end, consider the function

\varphi_{h}(x,t):=\varphi(x,t)-\frac{h}{t}\quad\text{for }h>0.

Then suppose on the contrary that
\sup_{B_{1}\times(t_{0},0)}u-\varphi_{h}>0
.
Since
u-\varphi_{h}\leq 0
on the boundary of
B_{1}\times(t_{0},0)
,
by continuity the function
u-\varphi_{h}
has a maximum at some

(\hat{x},\hat{t})\in B_{1}\times(t_{0},0)
. Since
\varphi_{h}

is smooth and
u
is a viscosity solution by Theorem
3.7
, we have by definition
of viscosity solutions that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\underset{x\not=\hat{x},(x,t)\not\in\Gamma}{(x,t)\rightarrow(\hat{x},\hat{t}),}}

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}(x,t)-\operatorname{div}\big(\left|D\varphi_{h}(x,t)\right|^{p-2}D\varphi(x,t)+a(x,t)\left|D\varphi_{h}(x,t)\right|^{q-2}D\varphi_{h}(x,t)\big)

(5.9)

\displaystyle-f(x,t,D\varphi_{h}(x,t)\Big)\leq 0.

Denote
\eta:=D\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})
. If
\eta\not=0
, then
the above implies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left|\eta\right|^{p-2}\Big(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})+(p-2)\frac{\eta^{\prime}D^{2}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})\eta}{\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +a(\hat{x},\hat{t})\left|\eta\right|^{q-2}\Big(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})+(q-2)\frac{\eta^{\prime}D^{2}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})\eta}{\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\eta\right|^{q-1}+f(x,t,\eta)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\eta\right|^{p-2}(N+p-2)K+2\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\eta\right|^{q-2}(N+q-2)K

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\eta\right|^{q-1}+C_{f}(1+\left|\eta\right|^{\beta_{1}}+\left|\eta\right|^{\beta_{2}}),

where in the last estimate we used that
D^{2}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})=2KI

and the growth condition (
G
). Observe that since
u-\varphi_{h}
has
a maximum at
(\hat{x},\hat{t})
and

is
L
-Lipschitz in space,
we must have
\left|D\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})\right|\leq L
.
Consequently, the above display yields (using also that
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\leq q

and that

)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})

\displaystyle\leq C(N,q,C_{f})(\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}})(L+1)^{q}K

(5.10)

\displaystyle=C(N,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}},L)K.

In the case
\eta=0
, the inequality (
5.9
) still
implies (
5.10
), as since
2\leq p\leq q
,
the divergence terms either vanish or reduce to Laplacians. Now, since

\Theta\leq\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}(\hat{x},\hat{t})
, we have

\Theta\leq C(N,q,\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}},L)K,

so that taking
\Theta=C_{1}K
, where
C_{1}
depends only on
N
,

q,

\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

and
L
, we have a contradiction. Since
h>0
was arbitrary, the
inequality (
5.8
) follows.

Step 3:
This step is again similar to Step 3 in the proof
of Lemma
5.1
. From (
5.8
) we have in particular

\displaystyle u(0,s_{0})-u(0,t_{0})\leq\varphi(0,s_{0})-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle=(s_{0}-t_{0})^{1/2}+C_{1}K(s_{0}-t_{0})

\displaystyle=(s_{0}-t_{0})^{1/2}+C_{1}C_{0}(s_{0}-t_{0})^{1/2}.

A lower bound can be proven similarly, and so the claim follows.
∎

We are now ready to finish the proof of the main regularity result.

,

)) and

6.
Remarks on multi-phase equation

In this section, we show that the Ishii-Lions method also works for parabolic multi-phase equation. We consider the parabolic version of (
1.3
) and only provide a sketch of the proof when
m=2:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du+b(z)|Du|^{s-2}Du\right)=f(z,Du).

We also assume similar growth conditions on
f:

\displaystyle|f(z,Du)|\leq C_{f}(1+|Du|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\beta_{2}}+b(z)|Du|^{\beta_{3}})

where
1\leq\beta_{1}<p,1\leq\beta_{2}<q,\,\,\text{and}\,\,1\leq\beta_{3}<s.
Weak solutions to (
6.1
) are defined analogously to Definition
2.1
with the appropriate spaces corresponding to the modulus

(z,\xi)\mapsto|\xi|^{p}+a(z)|\xi|^{q}+b(z)|\xi|^{s}.

Moreover, we point out that the comparison principle holds in a similar manner as in the double phase case. To prove the time approximation used in the proof of the comparison principle, we need both modulating coefficients to satisfy the
almost increasing condition
in a suitable way. That is to say, we require that

(6.2)

\displaystyle a(x,t)\leq C\max(a(x,s),b(x,s))\quad\text{and}\quad b(x,t)\leq Cb(x,s)

for all
(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{1}
with
t\leq s
, where
C
is some non-negative constant.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
1<p\leq q\leq s\leq p+1
and assume that the coefficients
a
and
b

in the equation (
6.1
) are nonnegative, continuous in time, Lipschitz continuous in space and satisfy (
6.2
) in
Q_{1}
. If
1\leq p\leq q\leq s<p+1,
and
u
is a weak solution to (
6.1
) in
Q_{1}
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle|u(x_{1},t_{1})-u(x_{2},t_{2})|\leq C\left(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{p}{p+s}}\right)

\text{for all}\quad(x_{1},t_{1}),(x_{2},t_{2})\in B_{1/2}\times(-1/2,0),
where
\alpha=\frac{p}{p+s}
if
1<p<2
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
if
p\geq 2.

The constant
C
depends only on
N
,
p
,
q
,
s,

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|Da\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|Db\|_{L^{\infty}}
, the constants appearing in the growth condition (
G
), and
\operatorname{osc}_{B_{1}\times(-1,0)}u
. If
s=p+1,
the constant
C
will additionally depend on the optimal modulus of continuity of
u
in space in
B_{1}\times(-1,0)
.

Proof.

Since the proof is almost similar to the double phase case, we only point out the steps where it is different.

Step 1: Lipschitz regularity in space.
In this step, we sketch the proof of Lipschitz continuity of solution
u
to the equation (
6.1
). Similar to the double phase case, for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
X\in S(N)
,
we denote

\displaystyle F((x,t),\eta,X):=

\displaystyle\>\left|\eta\right|^{p-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}X+(p-2)\frac{\eta^{\prime}X\eta}{\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\>+a(x,t)\left|\eta\right|^{q-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}X+(q-2)\frac{\eta^{\prime}X\eta}{\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\right)+b(x,t)\left|\eta\right|^{s-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}X+(s-2)\frac{\eta^{\prime}X\eta}{\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\right)

and

\displaystyle g((x,t),\eta)

\displaystyle:=\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\eta\right|^{q-1}+\left\|Db\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\eta\right|^{s-1}+C_{f}(1+\left|\eta\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(x,t)\left|\eta\right|^{\beta_{2}}+b(x,t)|\eta|^{\beta_{3}}),

where
C_{f}\geq 0
and
0\leq\beta_{1}\leq p
,
0\leq\beta_{2}\leq q,
and
0\leq\beta_{3}\leq s
. From the proof of Lemma
4.5
, we have the following.

\displaystyle K(\hat{t}-t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X+KI)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y-KI)+g((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{1}\right|)+g((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle=F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y)

\displaystyle+F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X+KI)-F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y-KI)+F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y)

\displaystyle+g((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{1}\right|)+g((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle=F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},X)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},Y)

\displaystyle+F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X)-F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},X)+F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},Y)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y)

\displaystyle+F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X+KI)-F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\eta_{1},X)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y-KI)+F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\eta_{2},Y)

\displaystyle+g((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{1}\right|)+g((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\left|\eta_{2}\right|)

(6.4)

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}.

Estimate of
T_{1}
:
By definition, we have

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=F((\hat{x},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},X)-F((\hat{y},\hat{t}),\tilde{\eta},Y)

\displaystyle=|\tilde{\eta}|^{p-2}\mathrm{tr}(X-Y)+|\tilde{\eta}|^{p-2}(p-2)\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}(X-Y)\tilde{\eta}}{|\tilde{\eta}|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{q-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}(a(\hat{x},\hat{t})X-a(\hat{y},\hat{t})Y)+(q-2)\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}(a(\hat{x},\hat{t})X-a(\hat{y},\hat{t})Y)\tilde{\eta}}{\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{2}}\right)}_{T_{11}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{s-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}(b(\hat{x},\hat{t})X-b(\hat{y},\hat{t})Y)+(s-2)\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}(b(\hat{x},\hat{t})X-b(\hat{y},\hat{t})Y)\tilde{\eta}}{\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{2}}\right)}_{T_{12}}

Following the estimates for the double phase case, we similarly have

\displaystyle T_{11}\leq C(q)(a(\hat{x},\hat{t})+a(\hat{y},\hat{t}))\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{q-2}L\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\left|z\right|)+C(N,\left\|Da\right\|_{L^{\infty}})L\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{q-2}\varphi^{\prime}(\left|z\right|),

\displaystyle T_{12}\leq C(s)(b(\hat{x},\hat{t})+b(\hat{y},\hat{t}))\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{s-2}L\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\left|z\right|)+C(N,\left\|Db\right\|_{L^{\infty}})L\left|\tilde{\eta}\right|^{s-2}\varphi^{\prime}(\left|z\right|)

7.

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem

[

based on inf-convolution. The main difficulty is caused by the presence of the modulating coefficient

touches the inf-convolution of a lower semicontinuous function from below,

also touches the original function from below at some nearby point

(x,t)

are on different sides of

ensures that this scenario can occur only in a set of small measure, which gives sufficient control over the error. Similar issues occur near the set where

vanishes. For these reasons, we need the assumption (

) to prove Theorem

In Lemma

we show that inf-convolution of a viscosity supersolution is a supersolution to a perturbed equation. In the proof, we apply the Theorem of sums, which causes jet-closures to appear. The following lemma verifies that the definition of viscosity supersolution behaves as expected in this context.

Lemma 7.8

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq p+1<\infty
. Suppose that
a:\Xi\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is bounded and measurable. Assume that

is a locally Lipschitz continuous weak supersolution to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(z)|Du|^{q-2}Du\right)\geq-\tilde{C}(1+|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{2}})\quad\text{in }\Xi,

is a constant and

,

such that

\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Xi)

\xi\leq 1

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\xi^{q}\left(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a(z)|Du|^{q}\right)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Xi}M^{2}\partial_{t}\xi^{q}+\max\{M^{p},M^{q}\}\left(\left|D\xi\right|^{p}+a(z)|D\xi|^{q}\right)+(M^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{1}}}+M^{\frac{p}{p-\beta_{2}}}+M)\xi^{q}\,dz,

where
M=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\operatorname{spt}\xi)}
.

Proof.

Since
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous, the function
\varphi:=\left(M-u\right)\xi^{q}

is an admissible test function. Testing the weak formulation of (
1.1
)
with
\varphi
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\xi^{q}\left(\left|Du\right|^{p}+a(z)|Du|^{q}\right)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Xi}-u\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz+\int_{\Xi}q\xi^{q-1}(M-u)\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-1}+a(z)|Du|^{q-1}\right)\left|D\xi\right|\,dz

(7.27)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+\int_{\Xi}\varphi\tilde{C}(1+|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{2}})dz.

We have by integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}-u\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz=

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\xi^{p}u\partial_{t}u-u(M-u)\partial_{t}\xi^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\frac{1}{2}\xi^{p}\partial_{t}u^{2}-u(M-u)\partial_{t}\xi^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\partial_{t}\xi^{p}-u(M-u)\partial_{t}\xi^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\partial_{t}\xi^{p}-uM\partial_{t}\xi^{p}\leq\int_{\Xi}CM^{2}\partial_{t}\xi^{p}\,dz.

Now we estimate the second term of (
7.27
). Applying Young’s inequality, we get for any
\delta>0

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}q\xi^{q-1}(M-u)\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-1}+a(z)|Du|^{q-1}\right)\left|D\xi\right|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Xi}\delta\xi^{\frac{p(q-1)}{p-1}}|Du|^{p}+C(p,\delta)M^{p}|D\xi|^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\Xi}\delta a(z)\xi^{q}|Du|^{q}+C(q,\delta)M^{q}a(z)|D\xi|^{q}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Xi}\delta\xi^{q}\left(|Du|^{p}+a(z)|Du|^{q}\right)+C(p,q,\delta)\max\{M^{p},M^{q}\}\left(|D\xi|^{p}+a(z)|D\xi|^{q}\right)\,dz.

Next we estimate the third term of (
7.27
). We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\varphi\tilde{C}(1+|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Xi}\tilde{C}(M-u)\xi^{q}\left(|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{2}}\right)\,dz+\int_{\Xi}\tilde{C}(M-u)\xi^{q}\,dz.

Applying Young’s inequality with the exponent pairs
\left(\frac{p}{\tilde{\beta}_{1}},\frac{p}{p-\tilde{\beta}_{1}}\right)
and
\left(\frac{q}{\tilde{\beta}_{1}},\frac{q}{q-\tilde{\beta}_{2}}\right),
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi}\tilde{C}(M-u)\xi^{q}\left(|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(z)|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{2}}\right)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Xi}\delta|Du|^{p}\xi^{q}+C(\delta)(\tilde{C}(M-u))^{\frac{p}{p-\tilde{\beta}_{1}}}\xi^{\left(q-\frac{q\tilde{\beta}_{1}}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-\tilde{\beta}_{1}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\Xi}\delta a(z)|Du|^{q}\xi^{q}+C(\delta)(\tilde{C}(M-u)^{\frac{q}{q-\tilde{\beta}_{2}}})\xi^{q}\,dz.

Thus, the third term of (
7.27
) can be estimated by

\int_{\Xi}\delta\left(|Du|^{p}+a(z)|Du|^{q}\right)\xi^{q}\,dz+C(\tilde{C},\delta)\int_{\Xi}\left(M^{\frac{p}{p-\tilde{\beta}_{1}}}+M^{\frac{q}{q-\tilde{\beta}_{2}}}+M\right)\xi^{q}\,dz.

Combining these estimates with (
7.27
) and absorbing the terms with
Du
to the left-hand side by taking small enough
\delta>0
, we obtain the desired inequality.

\displaystyle\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{1}}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \leq\left(\int_{\Xi}1\,dz\right)^{\frac{p-r_{1}}{p}}\bigg(\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{p}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{1}}{p}}

\displaystyle\ \leq C(p,r_{1})\bigg(\int_{\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}\theta\left(\left|Du_{j}\right|^{p-2}Du_{j}-\left|Du_{k}\right|^{p-2}Du_{k}\right)\cdot\left(Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right)\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{1}}{p}}.

and

\displaystyle\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}a(z)\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{2}}\,dz=\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}a(z)^{1-r_{2}/q}a(z)^{r_{2}/q}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \leq\left(\int_{\Xi}a(z)\,dz\right)^{\frac{q-r_{2}}{q}}\bigg(\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}a(z)\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{q}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{2}}{q}}

\displaystyle\ \leq C(q,r_{2})\bigg(\int_{\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}\theta a(z)\left(\left|Du_{j}\right|^{q-2}Du_{j}-\left|Du_{k}\right|^{q-2}Du_{k}\right)\cdot\left(Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right)\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{2}}{q}}.

The case
1<p\leq 2\leq q
can be handled by combining the above cases. Hence (
7
) leads to

\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|<\delta\right\}}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{2}}\,dz\leq\delta^{\frac{\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\}}{\max\{2,q\}}}C

On the other hand, Hölder’s and Tchebysheff’s inequalities with (
7.30
)
imply

\displaystyle\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{1}}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \leq\left|U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}\right|^{\frac{p-r_{1}}{p}}\bigg(\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{p}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{1}}{p}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\delta^{r_{1}-p}\left\|u_{j}-u_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(U)}^{p-r_{1}}C

and

\displaystyle\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}}a(z)\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\ \leq||a||_{\infty}\left|U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}\right|^{\frac{q-r_{2}}{q}}\bigg(\int_{U\cap\left\{\left|u_{j}-u_{k}\right|\geq\delta\right\}}a(z)\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{q}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{r_{2}}{q}}

\displaystyle\ \leq||a||_{\infty}\delta^{\frac{(r_{2}-q)p}{q}}\left\|u_{j}-u_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(U)}^{\frac{(q-r_{2})p}{q}}C.

So we arrive at

\int_{U}\left|Du_{j}-Du_{k}\right|^{r_{1}}+a(z)|Du_{j}-Du_{k}|^{r_{2}}\,dz\leq\left(\delta^{\frac{\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\}}{\max\{2,q\}}}+\delta^{\frac{(r_{2}-q)p}{q}}\left\|u_{j}-u_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(U)}^{\frac{(q-r_{2})p}{q}}\right)C,

where
C
is independent of
j
,
k
and
\delta
. Taking first small
\delta>0
and then large
j,k
, we can make
the right-hand side arbitrarily small.
∎

Now we are ready to show that bounded viscosity supersolutions are weak supersolutions. In the borderline case, we only consider supersolutions that are Lipschitz continuous in space. This is because if the error term in inequality (
7.5
) has the critical exponent
q-1=p
, the Caccioppoli’s inequality in Lemma
7.8
no longer yields a bound for
Du_{\varepsilon}
in a suitable Lebesgue space. Nevertheless, since we know that viscosity
solutions
are locally Lipschitz in space by Section
4
, this assumption causes no loss of generality in the case of solutions.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq p+1
. Suppose that
a:\Xi\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is locally Lipschitz in space and continuous in time, and that (
1.4
) holds. Let
u
be a bounded viscosity supersolution to
(
1.1
) in
\Xi
. If
q=p+1
, suppose, moreover, that
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous in space. Then
u
is a local weak supersolution
to (
1.1
) in
\Xi
.

Proof.

Without losing generality, we may suppose that
a
is globally Lipschitz in space and uniformly continuous in time in
\Xi
. Let
\Xi^{\prime\prime}\Subset\Xi^{\prime}\Subset\Xi
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the inf-convolution of
u
as defined in Lemma
7.3
and suppose that
\varepsilon>0
is so small that
\Xi^{\prime}\Subset\Xi_{\varepsilon}
.
Then by Lemma
7.7
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is a weak supersolution to (
7.21
) in
\Xi^{\prime}
, for any choice of
K>0
. Using this for now with
K=1
, recalling that
E_{\varepsilon}
is bounded independently of
\varepsilon
, and applying estimate (
7.20
), we see that
u_{\varepsilon}
is in particular a weak supersolution to

(7.32)

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}-\operatorname{div}(|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}Du_{\varepsilon}+a(z)|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}Du_{\varepsilon})\geq-\tilde{C}(1+|Du|^{\tilde{\beta}_{1}}+a(x,t)|Du|^{\beta_{2}})

in
\Xi^{\prime}
, where
\tilde{C}\geq 0
,
1\leq\tilde{\beta}_{1}<\max(p,q-1)
and
1\leq\tilde{\beta}_{2}<q
are independent of
\varepsilon
. Therefore, if
q<p+1
, we can apply Caccioppoli’s inequality in Lemma
7.8
on
u_{\varepsilon}
to find that

(7.33)

\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p}+a(z)|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q}\,dz<C,

where
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
. If
p=q+1
, then we have (
7.33
) directly by the Lipschitz assumption on
u
. Since
L^{H}(\Xi^{\prime\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a reflexive Banach space (see
[
CGŚW21
, Lemma 2.3.16 and Remark 3.3.3]
), there exists
G\in L^{H}(\Xi^{\prime\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
Du_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup G
weakly in
L^{H}(\Xi^{\prime\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})
up to a subsequence. In particular, for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Xi^{\prime\prime})
and
i\in\{{1,\ldots,N}\}
, we have

-\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}u\partial_{i}\varphi\,dz=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\varphi\,dz=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}\varphi\partial_{i}u_{\varepsilon}\,dz=\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}\varphi G_{i}\,dz,

where we also used that
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u
almost everywhere. It follows that
G
is the weak gradient of
u
and so
Du\in L^{H}(\Xi^{\prime\prime})
.

Since
\smash{u_{\varepsilon}}
is a weak supersolution to (
7.21
), we have for any
K>0
and
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Xi^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}-u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\varphi+|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}Du_{\varepsilon}\cdot D\varphi+a(z)|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}Du_{\varepsilon}\cdot D\varphi+(1+|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{q-1})E_{\varepsilon}(z)\varphi\,dz

(7.34)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}f_{K,\varepsilon}(z,Du_{\varepsilon})\varphi\,dz.

Take open domain
U
such that
\operatorname{spt}\varphi\Subset U\Subset\Xi^{\prime\prime}
. By (
7.33
), the condition (
7.28
) in Lemma
7.9
is satisfied by
Du_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, we have
\smash{Du_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow Du}
in
\smash{L^{r_{1},r_{2}}(U)}

for any
\smash{1<r_{1}<p},\smash{1<r_{2}<q}
and in particular
Du_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow Du
almost everywhere up to a subsequence. Furthermore, we have
E_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
almost everywhere in
\Xi^{\prime}
. Therefore, if
q<p+1
, we can let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
on the LHS of (
7
) with the help of the vector inequality

\left|\left|a\right|^{p-2}a-\left|b\right|^{p-2}b\right|\leq\begin{cases}2^{2-p}\left|a-b\right|^{p-1}&\text{when }p<2,\\
(p-1)\left(|a|^{p-2}+\left|b\right|^{p-2}\right)\left|a-b\right|&\text{when }p\geq 2.\end{cases}

If
q=p+1
, then
Du
is bounded in the support of
\varphi
by the Lipschitz assumption, and so we can pass to the limit on the LHS using the dominated convergence theorem. On the RHS of (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}f_{K,\varepsilon}(z,Du_{\varepsilon})\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|<K\right\}}\varphi\inf_{(y,s)\in B_{r(\varepsilon)}\times(t-r(\varepsilon),t+r(\varepsilon))}f(y,s,Du)\,dz

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|\geq K\right\}}C\varphi(1+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\beta_{2}})\,dz

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

Since

T_{1}\rightarrow\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du\right|<K\right\}}\varphi f(z,Du)\,dz\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

For the second term, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du_{\varepsilon}\right|>K/2\right\}}C\varphi(1+\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{2}})\,dz

\displaystyle-\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}C\varphi(1+\left|Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\beta_{2}})\,dz

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du\right|>K/2\right\}}C\varphi(1+\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{2}})\,dz\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where we used Lebesgue’s

in

together with

,

.
Thus, as

, inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}}-u\partial_{t}\varphi+\left|Du\right|^{p-2}Du\cdot D\varphi+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{q-2}Du\cdot D\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du\right|<K\right\}}\varphi f(z,Du)\,dz-\int_{\Xi^{\prime\prime}\cap\left\{\left|Du\right|>K/2\right\}}C\varphi(1+\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{1}}+a(z)\left|Du\right|^{\beta_{2}})\,dz.

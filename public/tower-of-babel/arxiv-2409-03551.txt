On the Optimal Performance of Distributed Cell-Free Massive MIMO with LoS Propagation
Noor Ul Ain, Lorenzo Miretti, and Sławomir Stańczak, Noor Ul Ain 1 , Lorenzo Miretti 1 2 and Sławomir Stańczak 1 2

Abstract
In this study, we revisit the performance analysis of distributed beamforming architectures in dense user-centric cell-free mmimo ( mmimo ) systems in los ( los ) scenarios. By incorporating a recently developed optimal distributed beamforming technique, called the tmmse ( tmmse ) technique, we depart from previous studies that rely on suboptimal distributed beamforming approaches for los scenarios. Supported by extensive numerical simulations that follow 3GPP guidelines, we show that such suboptimal approaches may often lead to significant underestimation of the capabilities of distributed architectures, particularly in the presence of strong los paths. Considering the anticipated ultra-dense nature of cell-free mmimo networks and the consequential high likelihood of strong los paths, our findings reveal that the team MMSE technique may significantly contribute in narrowing the performance gap between centralized and distributed architectures.

II-A

Spatially Correlated Rician Fading with Phase Shifts

To cover
los

\tau_{c}

\bm{h}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a realization of the channel coefficients between

k\in\mathcal{K}
and the
N

ap

l\in\mathcal{L}

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})

\bm{h}_{k,l}=\bm{\bar{h}}_{k,l}e^{j\theta_{k,l}}+\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k,l},\quad\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k,l}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\bm{0},\bm{R}_{k,l}\right)

(1)

where
\bm{\bar{h}}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is the spatial signature of the deterministic
los

\theta_{k,l}\in[0,2\pi]
is the associated phase shift, and
\bm{R}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is a covariance matrix describing the spatial correlation induced by the
nlos
components.

Departing from the canonical Rician fading model
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, the previous works
[
10
,
11
]
let the LoS phases be random variables
\theta_{k,l}\sim\mathcal{U}[0,2\pi]
that evolve across coherence blocks as rapidly as

[
10
]
by arguing that
\theta_{k,l}
varies much faster than
\bm{\bar{h}}_{k,l}
and
\bm{R}_{k,l}
, and hence it may not be easily estimated, particularly in very high mobility scenarios and for single-antenna APs. However, as also pointed out in
[
10
,
11
]
, the practical system performance fall in between these two scenarios. In addition, in practice,
\theta_{k,l}
does not vary as rapidly as
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k,l}

of the entire multipath channel. For instance, as also observed by the authors of
[
10
,
11
]
in the recent work
[
12
]
,

can be safely assumed constant over many coherence blocks in the frequency domain.

In this work, we assume that the network can perfectly track the
los

[
12
]
and to the

scenario in
[
10
,
11
]
. Moreover, we assume that each realization of

is kept constant over multiple coherence blocks, i.e., realizations of
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{k,l}
, as in
[
12

, and hence potentially treated as a large-scale fading parameter (statistical
csi

1
and Remark

). Differently than
[

,
11
]
, we will not focus on the impact of different LoS phase tracking capabilities, but rather on the impact of different beamforming architectures under perfect LoS phase tracking. The extension to imperfect LoS phase tracking is left as an interesting future direction.

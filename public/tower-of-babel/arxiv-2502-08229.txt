An Extended symmetric union and its Alexander polynomial Thanks: Keyword and phrases: Knot group, epimorphism, symmetric union, Alexander polynomial
Teruaki KITANO and Yasuharu NAKAE

Abstract
. For prime knots K 1 K_{1} and K 2 K_{2} , we write K 1 ≥ K 2 K_{1}\geq K_{2} if there is an epimorphism from the knot group of K 1 K_{1} to that of K 2 K_{2} which preserves the meridian. We construct a family of pairs of knots with K 1 ≥ K 2 K_{1}\geq K_{2} such that an epimorphism maps the longitude of K 1 K_{1} to the trivial element. This construction is regarded as an extension of a symmetric union with a single full twisted region. In particular, it extends a property of the Alexander polynomial of a symmetric union. We also exhibit that all but two of the knots up to ten crossings in the list of Kitano-Suzuki, which have an epimorphism mapping the longitude to the trivial element, arise from this construction.

r
is odd (see
[
3

),
and

is a 2-bridge knot.
Since a 2-bridge knot
b(\beta/\alpha)
is fibered if and only if all entries satisfy
|c_{i}|=2

[
3
, Proposition 12.20]
,
we can construct a non-fibered 2-bridge knot by taking some entries

.
Therefore, we can see that there are infinitely many non-fibered 2-bridge knots whose rational tangle

satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
T
be such a rational tangle,
then the numerator
N(T)
is a non-fibered 2-bridge knot.
Applying the construction of Theorem
1.1
to
\hat{K}
and
T
,
the knot
K
constructed by way of Theorem
1.1
satisfies
K\geq\hat{K}
,
and the epimorphism
\varphi:G(K)\to G(\hat{K})
maps the longitude of
K
to the trivial element in
G(\hat{K})
.

Since 2-bridge knots are alternating
[
3
, Proposition 12.15]
, a 2-bridge knot is fibered if the Alexander polynomial of the knot is monic
[
22
]
.
Now the 2-bridge knot
N(T)
is not fibered, then the Alexander polynomial of
N(T)
is not monic.
Therefore, the Alexander polynomial of
K
is also not monic, so

is not a fibered knot.
This completes the proof of Corollary

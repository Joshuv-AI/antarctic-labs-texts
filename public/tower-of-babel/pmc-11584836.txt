Single-cell analysis uncovers liver susceptibility to pancreatic cancer metastasis via myeloid cell characterization
Authors: Aizier Ainiwaer, Zhenwei Qian, Jianxun Wang, Qi Zhao, Yinying Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe liver is the predominant metastatic site for diverse cancers, including pancreatic and colorectal cancers (CRC), etc. The high incidence of hepatic metastasis of pancreatic cancer is an important reason for its refractory and high mortality. Therefore, it is important to understand how metastatic pancreatic cancer affects the hepatic tumor immune microenvironment (TME) in patients. Here, we characterized the TME of liver metastases unique to pancreatic cancer by comparing them with CRC liver metastases. We integrated two single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) datasets including tumor samples of pancreatic cancer liver metastasis (P-LM), colorectal cancer liver metastasis (C-LM), primary pancreatic cancer (PP), primary colorectal cancer (PC), as well as samples of peripheral blood mono-nuclear cells (PBMC), adjacent normal pancreatic tissues (NPT), to better characterize the heterogeneities of the microenvironment of two kinds of liver metastases. We next performed comparative analysis on cellular compositions between P-LM and C-LM, found that Mph_SPP1, a subset of macrophages associated with angiogenesis and tumor invasion, was more enriched in the P-LM group, indicating this kind of macrophages provide a TME niche more vulnerable for pancreatic cancers. Analysis of the developmental trajectory implied that Mph_SPP1 may progressively be furnished with increased expression of genes regulating endothelium. Cell–cell communications analysis revealed that Mph_SPP1 potentially interacts with endothelial cells in P-LM via FN1/SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB1, implying this macrophage subset may construct an immunosuppressive TME for pancreatic cancer by regulating endothelial cells. We also found that Mph_SPP1 has a prognostic value in pancreatic adenocarcinoma that is not present in colon adenocarcinoma or rectum adenocarcinoma. This study provides a new perspective for understanding the characteristics of the hepatic TME in patients with liver metastatic cancer. And it provides a subset of macrophages specifically associated with the liver metastasis of pancreatic cancer, and its detection and intervention have potential value for preventing the metastasis of pancreatic cancer to the liver.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01566-0.Keywords: Liver metastasis, scRNA-seq, Pancreatic cancer, Colorectal cancer, SPP1

Introduction

The liver is one of the most frequent sites of distant metastases, including pancreatic cancer, gastric carcinoma, breast cancer, lung cancer, and colorectal cancer, and liver metastasis remains a major hurdle to the long-lasting survival of patients with these primary cancers [1–6]. The liver has sufficient blood flow and receives double blood supply from the hepatic artery and portal vein, and cancer cells from other organs can easily enter the liver through the bloodstream [7]. Liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) have a window-like structure that permits direct entry to tumor cells into the basement membrane[8, 9]. Moreover, liver metastasis can also be driven by the crosstalk between cancer cells and other cell types in the hepatic tumor microenvironment (TME), including myeloid cells, lymphoid cells, and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) [10–12]. Tumor-infiltrated myeloid cells (TIMs) are key elements of TME and regulators in tumor progression that consist of several major lineages, including mast cells, dendritic cells (DCs), monocytes, and macrophages [13]. The liver is a soil for multiple cancer cells ("seeds") within metastases, and how the composition of this soil promotes the growth of metastatic cancers and whether different seeds are able to remodel its soil composition remains unclear.

Mechanistically, the TME influences cancer cells via complicated and dynamic pathways to regulate cancer-related signaling that includes ligand-receptor interactions, deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), and cytokine and metabolite responses [14]. Accumulating evidence indicates that different tumor types harbor different TME properties. For instance, hepatocellular carcinoma and colorectal cancer (CRC) have substantially greater proportions of CD8+ exhausted T cells than lung cancer [15]. Moreover, whereas invading macrophages are more abundant in metastatic brain tumors, resident macrophages are more prevalent in gliomas [16]. The heterogeneity of TME and the highly dynamic spreading routes of cancer cells are major contributing factors to tumor metastasis, relapse, and drug resistance [17–19].

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) persists as one of the most intractable and lethal malignancies, with a 5-year survival rate of only 8% [20, 21]. The liver is the predominant metastatic site for PDAC, and liver metastasis is one of the common reasons for the poor prognosis and treatment failure of this cancer [22]. The median survival time of patients with liver metastases from pancreatic cancer is less than 6 months [23]. CRC is the third most common cancer worldwide and is another malignancy that is prone to liver metastasis, and patients with liver metastases have a worse prognosis than patients who do not have them [24, 25]. The prevalence of liver metastases from the pancreas is 77.6%, which is higher than that from the colorectum (72.0%) [7]. Surgical resection of liver metastasis is challenging, and tumor cells of diverse origins may show different sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs. Therefore, for liver metastases, tumor invasions from different sources may require precise and specific treatment.

In this study, we integrated scRNA-seq datasets of PDAC and CRC patients with liver metastasis and comparatively analyzed the cellular compositions and functional enrichment of them to characterize the hepatic TMEs within these two metastases at a single cell level. By comparing it with the CRC liver metastasis, we identified specific myeloid cell types in the pancreatic cancer liver metastasis, and mined genes related to its prognosis.

Materials and methods

Supplement data collection

We integrated two single-cell transcriptome datasets containing liver metastatic cancer published in recent years. The two published datasets was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus databases (GEO) under the accession numbers GSE197177 and GSE178318. We also conducted a secondary analysis of multiple datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, including TCGA- colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), TCGA- pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), and TCGA- rectum adenocarcinoma (READ). Clinical information associated with these datasets was obtained from the Genomic Data Commons Data Portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Detailed information and metadata can be found in Supplementary Table S1. We performed survival analysis and result validation based on these datasets.

Preprocessing and quality control of single-cell RNA-seq data

The preliminary filtered data generated from Cell Ranger were utilized for subsequent filtering and analyses. We implemented rigorous quality control measures based on multiple criteria, including: (1) the number of detected genes per cell (gene_qc < 300); (2) unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts per cell (umi_qc < 1000); (3) percentage of UMI counts of mitochondrial genes per cell (mito_qc > 20); (4) percentage of UMI counts of genes per cell (ribo_qc > 50); (5) the ratio of the content of ribosomal genes to mitochondrial genes per cell (ribo_mito_ratio_qc < 1); (6) Identification of outlier cells based on median-absolute-deviation (outlier_qc); (7) Identification of doublet cells based on Python package “scrublet” (doublet_qc) [26]. Any cell meeting one or more of the above conditions was considered low-quality and subsequently filtered out. The calculations for gene_qc, umi_qc, mito_qc, ribo_qc, and ribo_mito_ratio_qc were performed using the PercentageFeatureSet function in the Seurat package [27]. The identification of outlier cells was carried out using the isOutlier function in R package scuttle (v1.1.2). It is worth noting that the expected doublet rate for Scrublets was set at 0.01, which is deemed appropriate for 10 × datasets. Following these criteria, the final filtered matrix comprised 156,577 high-quality cells and 45,063 genes.

Data integration and analysis of scRNA-seq

After preprocessing and quality control, all datasets were merged using the Seurat::merge function, and subsequent analyses were conducted following the methodology described in our previously published work [27, 28]. The UMI count matrix underwent log normalization, and the 2000 most variable features were identified. Linear dimensionality reduction was performed with the RunPCA function in Seurat. For graph-based clustering, the first 30 principal components were determined using the ElbowPlot function. K-nearest neighbor clustering with FindNeighbors and FindClusters (resolution = 1.2) and UMAP clustering with RunUMAP (dim = 30) initially resulted in 35 clusters. However, a significant batch effect was observed, prompting a subsequent batch correction.

Batch effect correction and unsupervised clustering

To mitigate batch effects, we employed Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) within the Seurat framework [27]. To facilitate data integration, we aggregated individual samples from both datasets and selected the top 2000 most-variable genes from each dataset for CCA. Utilizing the top 30 dimensions in CCA, we identified anchors through the FindIntegrationAnchors function. The datasets were subsequently integrated using the IntegrateData function, with default values for other Seurat parameters. Dimensionality reduction was carried out using RunUMAP (dim = 30). Clustering was determined using FindNeighbors and FindClusters (resolution = 1.2), resulting in the identification of a total of 33 clusters. Following integration, a successful batch effect correction was achieved, and no discernible separation across different conditions was observed in the final analysis. Additionally, we applied the same methodology to perform subpopulation analysis specifically for myeloid cells.

Cell annotation of scRNA-seq data

Major cell types were annotated based on the expression of known markers, i.e., CDH1, KRT18, and KRT8, for epithelial cells; CD3D,CD3E, and CD2 for T cell; ACTA2, COL1A1, and COL1A2 for fibroblasts; CD68, CD14, and CD163 for myeloid cell; GNLY and NKG7 for natural killer (NK) cell; CD79A, MS4A1, and IGHG1 for B cell; MS4A2 and TPSB2 for mast cell; VWF, PECAM1, and FCGR2B for endothelial cell; LILRA4 and CLEC4C for DCs [29].

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) detection and pathway analysis

DEGs were identified using the FindAllMarkers function in Seurat, employing the 'Wilcox test' [27]. All DEGs specific to individual clusters were documented in supplementary tables. Features were annotated for cell type-specific genes using a GTF file derived from the hg38 genome (https://cf.10xgenomics.com/supp/cell-exp/refdata-gex-GRCh38-2020-A.tar.gz). Initially, DEGs for each cell type were filtered based on criteria (log2FC > 1, adjusted p-value < 0.05). Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler (version 4.2.2) with the enricher function, leveraging the obtained DEGs [30].

Definition of functional gene sets

To elucidate the functional characteristics of myeloid cells, we evaluated the signature gene scores for each subcluster using the AddModuleScore function in Seurat [27]. The genes utilized for scoring M1 and M2 originate from Zhang's previous studies [31]. Additionally, the genes employed for Kupffer score and MoMF score were adapted from Guillot et al. [32], while the genes associated with phagocytosis and proinflammatory functions were extracted from Aizarani's work [33].

Construction and analysis of myeloid cells trajectory

To construct cellular trajectories, we utilized the Slingshot cell lineage inference algorithm [34]. This algorithm predicts lineage trajectories and bifurcations by arranging cells along their developmental paths. Default settings from the Slingshot package in R were applied, incorporating Seurat's UMAP coordinates and cluster assignments as input data.

Cell–cell interaction analysis by CellChat

Ligand-receptor analysis and visualization were conducted using CellChat (version 1.1.3) to infer cellular interactions between myeloid cells and endothelial cells, with a specific emphasis on comparing the distinctions between the P-LM and C-LM groups [35]. The detailed procedures and explanations for CellChat analysis are available at the following GitHub repository: https://github.com/sqjin/CellChat. The significance of ligand-receptor pairs between myeloid cell subtypes and endothelial cells was determined based on a P-value threshold of < 0.05.

TCGA RNA-seq data and survival analysis

We conducted a correlation analysis between Mph_SPP1 expression and survival as well as disease stage using three datasets: TCGA-PAAD, TCGA-COAD, and TCGA-READ. The statistical analysis was conducted using the R package "survival" and survival curves were generated using the survfit function. The feature genes utilized for the Mph_SPP1 signature were derived from Mph_SPP1-specific DEGs (log2FC > 1.5, adjusted p-value < 0.05). These genes include CHI3L1, MMP7, MMP12, SPP1, MMP9, CSTB, FBP1, FABP5, LPL, CHIT1, APOC1, SDC2, IFI6, and IL1RN. We computed the total scores of these genes in each sample and categorized the samples based on the median score to assess the high and low groups in terms of the signature score. For these genes, we also employed Univariate Cox regression analysis to assess their relationship with prognosis. Statistical tests were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis method for disease stage.

Statistical analysis and visualization

All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.3.0. We utilized Wilcoxon’s rank-sum test for pairwise comparisons and Kruskal–Wallis test for comparisons involving more than two groups. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. To identify DEGs, we employed the FindMarkers and FindAllMarkers functions in Seurat, with Bonferroni correction applied to adjust for multiple comparisons. DEGs with an adjusted p < 0.05 were considered significant. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler R package (v.4.2.2), with P-value correction according to the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure to control the false discovery rate. For data visualization, we utilized the ggplot2 package (https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/) to create publication-quality plots. Additionally, we incorporated the SCP pipeline (https://github.com/zhanghao-njmu/SCP) for streamlined analysis and visualization.

Results

Single-cell TME landscape of primary and metastatic cancer sites

To characterize the cell compositions in liver metastases from both pancreatic and colorectal cancer, we integrated two single-cell datasets of four PDAC and six primary CRC patients with liver metastases. For PDAC patients, we obtained 8 samples covering PP, P-LM, and NPT. Another six groups were acquired from 13 samples of CRC patients, including PC, PC underwent chemotherapy (PC_T), C-LM, C-LM underwent chemotherapy (C-LM_T), PBMC, and PBMC underwent chemotherapy (PBMC_T) (Fig. 1A; Supplementary Table S1). Following multiple quality control steps, we obtained single-cell transcriptomes in a total of 156,577 cells from all samples for downstream analysis (Supplementary Figure S1A). Significant batch effects were seen in dimensionally reduced visualizations of these data due to variations in the specimen sources (Fig. 1B). Following integration by CCA, the batch effects were satisfactorily removed with no notable separation across different platforms or conditions (Fig. 1C). The integrated cells were partitioned into nine clusters of main cell types, including T cell, B cell, macrophage/monocyte (Mac/Mono), natural killer cell (NK), dendritic cell (DC), mast cell, fibroblast (Fibro), epithelial cell (Epith), and endothelial cell (Endo) (Fig. 1D). Each type of cell was included in all datasets. We manually annotated these cell clusters with canonical marker genes or identified highly expressed genes (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Table S2).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were carried out for each type of cell to supplement the cell identity annotation (Fig. 1F, Supplementary Figure S1B, Supplementary Table S2). The Epith cluster was marked by CDH1, KRT18, and KRT8, and it was enriched in genes associated with digestion, antimicrobial humoral response, pancreatic secretion, fat and protein digestion and absorption, all of which are functions of epithelial cells [36]. The T cell cluster was annotated with CD3D, CD3E, and CD2, and functional analysis revealed that the genes that were enriched in this cluster were associated with the differentiation of T cells and lymphocytes. Fibroblastic characteristics such as focal adhesion [37], extracellular matrix organization, extracellular structure organization [38], and ECM-reporter interaction were found to be enriched in the genes within the Fibro cluster. The Mac/Mono cluster was characterized by the expression of CD68, CD14, and CD163 and genes related to typical monocytic functions, including phagosome, antigen processing and presentation of antigen via MHC class II [39]. The Endo cluster was distinguished by VWF and PECAM1, and the expressed genes were more abundant in those related to endothelium development, endothelial cell differentiation, and regulation of vasculature development. Genes related to antigen processing and presentation were enriched in the DC cluster. The NK cluster and B cell cluster were characterized by the functional enrichment of cell killing and adaptive immune responses, respectively.

We next quantified the enrichment of each cluster in all nine groups. The preference of each cluster was illustrated based on RO/E [40] (Fig. 1G). Notably, the difference in cell types enriched in P-LM and C-LM was significant. Compared to the C-LM group, the clusters of Mac/Mono, Epith, Endo, and Fibro were preferentially enriched in the P-LM. The Mac/Mono cluster, as an important immune cell type, may be responsible for the unique TME of liver metastasis in pancreatic cancer. On the contrary, the clusters of NK, DC, and T cell were more enriched in C-LM than P-LM. P-LM has a similar cellular composition to NPT and PP, indicating that the metastasized tumor still has the characteristics of the TME of its origin. In addition, the response cells of C-LM after treatment were mainly myeloid cells, such as macrophages (up-regulated) and DC (down-regulated), suggesting that myeloid cells may be the main regulated cells in liver metastases after chemotherapy. In conclusion, myeloid cells may be the key components that trigger specific TME in the liver metastasis of pancreatic cancer.

Functions and tumor-site preferences of myeloid subclusters

To further investigate the characteristics of myeloid cells in TME, we identified 21,777 myeloid cells and subclustered them by manual annotation with canonical marker genes or highly expressed genes. A total of 15 myeloid clusters were identified, including nine clusters of macrophages (Mph_CCL20, Mph_CCL18, Mph_PLTP, Mph_CD3E, Mph_STMN1, Mph_CXCL10, Mph_SPP1, Mph_APOE, and Mph_CSTB), three clusters of monocytes (Mono1, Mono2, and Mono3), and three DC clusters (DC_HLA-DRB5, DC_LAMP3, and DC_CLEC9A) (Fig. 2A, Supplementary Figure S2A). We manually annotated these cell clusters with canonical marker genes or identified highly expressed genes (Fig. 2B, Supplementary Figure S2B) and exhibited their functions using GO and KEGG (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Table S3).

The three DC clusters were annotated with their typical genes, LAMP3, HLA-DRB, and CLEC9A, respectively [41]. The primary function of DCs in cancer immunity is to acquire tumor antigen, migrate to lymph nodes, and activate a de novo T cell response [42]. In previous studies, DC_LAPM3 was identified in hepatocellular carcinoma with maturation features and exhibited strong migration capacity toward lymph nodes [43]. DC_CLEC9A was reported to have functional specializations that include the capacity to produce high levels of IFN-λ, elicit Th1 responses, and activate CTL by internalizing and cross-presenting dead cell-associated Ag [44]. Our functional enrichment analysis also showed that DC_CLEC9A is associated with cytoplasmic translation and adaptive immune responses. These characteristics suggest that DC_CLEC9A may be a key player in the induction of anti-viral and anti-tumor immunity. Macrophages in clusters 1, 2, and 10 highly expressed a series of chemokine-encoding genes such as CCL20, CCL18, and CXCL10, which are also associated with immune-suppressive and tumor-promoting functions [45–48]. GO enrichment analysis revealed that Mph_CCL18 may participate in processes such as cellular response to zinc and copper ions and has been reported to share the Kupffer cell signature and play an immunosuppressive role in CRC [49]. Mph_CXCL10 may be relative to nuclei division and chromosome secretion, according to GO analysis, which was described as M1-like macrophages [49, 50].

Macrophages in cluster 5 and cluster 12 expressed many complement activating related genes, including C1QC, C1QA, and C1QB [51, 52], as well as genes associated with lipid metabolism, such as APOE and APOC1 [53]. We found that the Mph_C1QC cluster was enriched with immune-related functions, including antigen processing and presentation of exogenous antigen, and cytoplasmic translation. As reported, Mph_C1QC may originate from IL1+ macrophages and mainly interact with a variety of T cell subsets to perform the functions of phagocytosis and antigen presentation [29]. The major function of the Mph_APOE cluster was the antigen presentation of peptide or polysaccharide antigen via MCH class II, as well as highly expressed TREM2 and LGALS3, which were recently found to be associated with metabolic diseases [54, 55]. Cluster 9 was enriched with STMN1 and TUBB, which are involved in the regulation of the microtubule filament system and also express genes correlated to the TP53 mutation, such as H2AFZ [56, 57]. Interestingly, the Mph_STMN1 cluster was analyzed to be involved in adaptive immune response, T cell differentiation, and process of cell cycle process. The Mph_CSTB cluster was enriched with the TFF family, which is a type of small secreted peptide characterized by a stable trefoil structure and associated with the biological functions of epithelial restitution, inflammatory regulation, mucosal protection, etc. [58, 59].

Cluster 11, classified as Mph_SPP1, was characterized by upregulation of SPP1, an extracellular matrix protein gene that also possesses the properties of a cytokine [60]. Recent findings showed that, in addition to its direct positive effects on cancer cell survival and invasion, SPP1 promotes angiogenesis and alters the migration and function of tumor-associated inflammatory cells, both of which are important components of cancer metastasis pathobiology [61–63]. And the Mph_SPP1 cluster was considered to be developed from NLRP3+ tissue-resident macrophages and mainly interact with CAF to promote angiogenesis and tumor metastasis [29, 64]. Additionally, CHI3CL1, MMP9, and MMP12 genes were also upregulated in cluster 11. CHI3CL1 is a liver-enriched gene that translates a glycoprotein secreted by a variety of cells, including macrophages, neutrophils, and fibroblast-like cells, that regulates the synthesis and degradation of extracellular matrix as well as the activation and differentiation of immune cells [65]. According to current studies, CHI3CL1 is associated with a variety of human cancers and promotes tumor invasion and metastasis [66, 67]. MMP9 promotes the growth of tumor cells by promoting angiogenesis and, at the same time, promotes the infiltration and invasion of tumor cells into the basement membrane by degrading and destroying the basement membrane [68].

The biological relevance of each cluster was checked using several published myeloid signatures, including M1, M2, phagocytosis, proinflammatory, Kupffer, and MoMF [69] (Fig. 2D). We found that Mph_SPP1, Mph_C1QC, Mph_APOE, Mph_STMN1, and Mph_CCL18 were enriched for immunosuppression signatures, including M2/phagocytosis, and the three clusters of monocytes were enriched for M1 and proinflammatory signatures. In addition, Mph_SPP1 had a high Kupffer score, suggesting that these cells have similar functions and characteristics to Kupffer cells in the liver. We next analyzed the functional enrichment in each normal and tumorous condition based on gene sets (Fig. 2E). We found significant differences in functional enrichment between the P-LM and C-LM groups. P-LM enriched functions such as regulation of lipid localization, cellular response to metal iron, ATP biosynthetic processes, and purine nucleoside triphosphate biosynthetic processes, while C-LM enriched cytoplasmic translation and epithelial cell proliferation functions.

Further, we compared the enrichment of each cell cluster in different conditions (Fig. 2F). We found that Mph_SPP1 is enriched in both types of metastatic cancer, but the enrichment is more pronounced in P-LM, suggesting that this macrophage subtype is associated with liver metastasis and plays a more significant role in pancreatic cancer liver metastasis. Interestingly, Mph_SPP1 is less enriched in PC and C-LM, but more enriched in PP and further enriched in P-LM, suggesting that Mph_SPP1 is specifically present in the development and metastasis of pancreatic cancer. In sum, Mph_SPP1 may accelerate the hepatic metastasis in pancreatic cancer by regulating liver TME through immunosuppression and angiogenesis function.

Developmental trajectories of myeloid subclusters and features of Mph_SPP1 evolution

All trajectories of myeloid cells were analyzed using Slingshot to determine how they possibly differentiated in the liver TME [34]. Five fictitious temporal lineages were identified, and all of them originated from monocytes (Fig. 3A, Supplementary Table S4). The findings demonstrated that, among macrophages, Mph_CCL20, Mph_CXCL10, Mph_CD3E, and Mph_APOE were in a transitional condition, whereas Mph_SPP1, Mph_STMN1, and Mph_C1QC were in the ultimate stage. This is consistent with earlier reports that both tumor-associated SPP1+ and C1QC+ macrophages are monocyte-derived but have different developmental pathways [29]. All three clusters of DCs may develop from monocytes according to the trajectory of lineage 3 and translate to subtypes associated with immunosuppression since DC_CLEC9A was the terminal of this path [44]. Lineage 1 and lineage 5 both started from monocytes, passed through Mph_CCL18, Mph_CXCL10, and Mph_C1QC, and finally ended in Mph_STMN1, but lineage 1 also visited the Mph_CSTB cluster. Furthermore, the terminal of lineage 2 was Mph_C1QC and had the same previous trajectory as lineage 1 and lineage 5. The trajectories of the Mph_SPP1 cluster were demonstrated by lineage 4, which emerged from monocytes, passed through the Mph_CCL20 and Mph_CCL18 clusters, and ended at the Mph_SPP1 cluster (Fig. 3B). These findings again confirmed our previous conclusion that Mph_SPP1, Mph_STMN1, and Mph_C1QC may be functionally different between TMEs. What’s more, Mph_CCL18 could partially resemble the functional phenotypes of Mph_SPP1, but it did not upregulate transcriptomic programs related to angiogenesis and tumor metastasis.

To further reveal the characteristics of the dynamic biologic timeline of the Mph_SPP1 cluster, we next sought to visualize the expression of individual genes in lineage 4 and identified five clusters (Fig. 3C). Among the five clusters, the expression of genes in clusters 2 and 4 was significantly increased during the development of Mph_SPP1. Cluster 2 was enriched in 8 genes, among which CTSC and C1QC were associated with breast cancer metastasis and osteosarcoma progression, respectively [70, 71]. The LGALS3 gene in Cluster 4 promotes liver cancer bone metastasis, while DAB2 regulates the migration and differentiation of endothelial cells [55, 72]. These results suggested that during Mph_SPP1 development, there are many up-regulated genes related to tumor metastasis, along with the increased expression of genes regulating endothelial cells.

Through differential gene expression analysis, numerous genes associated with cell attachment and tumor invasion had increased expression in Mph_SPP1 compared to earlier stages (Fig. 3D). Their expression, including CHI3L1, MMP9, and IBSP, was substantially increased late in pseudotime [67, 73, 74]. The expression of CCL18 and FABP4, which have been associated with pro-angiogenesis, was also increased later in pseudotime [75]. CHI3L1 regulates angiogenesis and has been reported to be associated with tumor metastasis. Notably, recent studies reported that CCL18 regulates angiogenesis by interacting with PITPNM3 expressed on endothelial cells [76, 77]. By contrast, expression of anti-tumor genes such as PTX3 and MEG3 was decreased in Mph_SPP1 compared to the earliest stage, suggesting the gradual loss of its function of immunizing against tumors [78, 79]. In sum, these data implied that Mph_SPP1 may have developed from monocytes and gained functions such as promoting tumorgenesis, angiogenesis, and interacting with endothelial cells in pancreatic cancer liver metastasis, and inhibition of relative genes (CHI3L1 and CCL18) may prevent the invasion and transport of the primary cancers.

Interaction between Mph_SPP1 and endothelium promotes the progression of P-LM

In order to investigate the intricate cellular behaviors and intercellular communication within the P-LM and C-LM groups, we first calculated the number of inferred interactions and interaction strength among all myeloid cell subpopulations and endothelial cells and found significant differences in cellular communication between the two metastases (Fig. 4A). We found that the P-LM group has a higher inferred number of interactions and strength of interactions among cell types than the C-LM group. Furthermore, endothelial cells presented more interaction numbers with the myeloid subpopulations in P-LM than C-LM (Fig. 4B). In addition to this, clusters such as Mph_SPP1, Mph_CCL18, Mph_CCL20, and Mph_STMN1 more significantly interact with other clusters in P-LM. To be more specific, the interactions of Mph_SPP1-Endo, Mph_CCL18-Mph_CXCL10, Mph_CCL18-Mph_SPP1 were stronger in P-LM than C-LM. Consistent with the previous results that the Mph_SPP1 cluster had a higher enrichment in pancreatic cancer liver metastasis, the number of interactions between Mph_SPP1 and endothelial cells was also more significant in P-LM (Fig. 4C). We then compared relative information flows for each signaling pathway between two liver metastases and found group-specific cellular communications. We found the MK, ADGRE5, GRN, CD45, and VEGF pathways were specifically active in P-LM, whereas the CXCL pathway was dominant in C-LM (Fig. 4D). The VEGF signaling pathway was thought to be involved in tumor angiogenesis and was the most active in Mph_SPP1 among myeloid clusters, suggesting a pro-tumorigenic/pro-metastatic role of Mph_SPP1 in P-LM [80] (Fig. 4E). Additionally, some immune signals were comparable between P-LM and C-LM, such as COMPLEMENT, CLEC, IL16, and MHC-II.

To further investigate the critical cellular communication in liver metastasis, we compared the significant changes in ligand-receptor pairs among target cell types between pancreatic and colorectal liver metastases (Fig. 4F). Specific to SPP1 signaling, we identified several ligand-receptor pairs, SPP1-ITGAV, SPP1-ITGA9, SPP1-ITGA9 and FN1-ITGAV as the most significant signaling in P-LM, contributing to the communication from Mph_SPP1 to endothelial cells. Previous studies reported that SPP1 or FN1 simultaneously interacts with certain integrins, such as ITGAV and ITGA5, to promote tumorgenesis and metastases, including lung cancer and CRC [81, 82]. We found that SPP1-ITGAV, SPP1-ITGA9, SPP1-ITGA5, and FN1-ITGB1 signaling from Mph_SPP1 to endothelial cells participated more in P-LM than C-LM, suggesting that these signaling pathways are specific to pancreatic cancer liver metastasis. Notably, the interaction of FN1 and ITGB1 can inhibit T cell activation, thereby leading to the immune escape of PDAC and increasing drug resistance [83]. Together, we found that Mph_SPP1 can not only interact with CAF but also with endothelial cells and create a specific TME more suitable for pancreatic cancer cell metastasis to the liver.

Mph_SPP1 is specifically associated with poor survival prognosis in pancreatic cancer patients

To explore the prognostic roles of Mph_SPP1 in cancers, we performed survival analysis using TCGA-COAD and TCGA-PAAD datasets. Importantly, patients with a higher level of Mph_SPP1 signature score were associated with a worse survival probability in PAAD. However, patients with a low Mph_SPP1 signature did not show a corresponding survival advantage in COAD (Fig. 5A).

The potential significance of Mph_SPP1 and Mph_SPP1-specific markers for PAAD, COAD, and READ prognosis. Survival analysis and prognostic factor analysis were performed. For survival analysis, the Kaplan–Meier method and the log-rank test were employed. For prognostic factors, COX proportional hazard regression model was used. B Univariate Cox regression analysis of PAAD identified top genes of Mph_SPP1 as independent clinical characteristic for overall survival prediction. C Kruskal–Wallis test showed that Mph_SPP1 was correlated with the progressing stages of PAAD but not with COAD and READ

To explore the prognostic biomarkers of Mph_SPP1 in pancreatic cancer, we used the marker genes enriched in Mph_SPP1 in a univariate Cox risk regression analysis of TCGA PAAD patients (Fig. 5B). We identified that FABP5, APOC1, and CSTB were independent prognostic factors for PAAD with HRs of 4.324 (1.517–12.328), 2.791 (1.211–6.433), and 2.461 (1.018–5.951), respectively. The FABP5 gene showed the best prognostic value, followed by APOC1 and CSTB. By report, the FABP5 gene can selectively regulate the accumulation of long-chain unsaturated fatty acids in macrophages, reprogram lipid metabolism, and regulate M2 polarization, and has been identified as a risk biomarker in multiple myeloma [84–86].

We next explored the correlation of Mph_SPP1 with the progression stage of the cancers by analyzing the TCGA-PAAD, TCGA-COAD, and TCGA-READ datasets (Fig. 5C). We found the level of Mph_SPP1 was positively correlated with PAAD from stage I to stage III but decreased at stage IV. By contrast, the stages of COAD and READ did not show a significant correlation with Mph_SPP1. In conclusion, tumor-associated Mph_SPP1 may be a potential reason why pancreatic cancer has a worse prognosis than colorectal cancer.

Discussion

Liver metastasis is a frequent occurrence in a large number of malignancies and remains a major barrier to the successful management of malignant disease, especially for cancers of the gastrointestinal tract. Recent studies have revealed that the complexity and heterogeneity of TME are associated with poor prognosis and drug resistance [87, 88]. Therefore, comprehensively delineating the immune characteristics of different organ-derived metastases in the liver is beneficial to the protection of the liver in chemotherapy. scRNA-seq is a powerful tool that can profile the gene expression pattern of each individual cell and unravel the cellular communications in the TME [89, 90]. In this study, we integrated and presented the single-cell transcriptomic landscape of primary tumors and matched liver metastases of PDAC and CRC. We performed comparative analysis on both cellular composition and functional phenotype between metastases of PDAC and CRC to better understand the differential characteristics of hepatic TMEs in metastases of different origins.

We first detected the compositions of cell types in adjacent pancreatic tissue, primary PDAC, primary CRC, and their matched liver metastases. Examination of canonical marker genes revealed major cell populations, including T cells, B cells, NK cells, meyloid cells (macrophages, monocytes, mast cells, and DCs), fibroblasts, epithelial cells, and endothelial cells, in the integrated scRNA-seq datasets. We found significant differences in cell compositions between the two types of metastatic cancer, in which myeloid cells, including macrophages, monocytes, and DCs, were more enriched in P-LM than C-LM. Based on this result, we hypothesized that myeloid cells are key immunomodulators of liver TME formation in P-LM, and the regulation level of myeloid cells was more significant in P-LM compared to C-LM. Liver metastasis is one of the common reasons for the poor prognosis and treatment failure of PDAC [22]. The median survival time of patients with liver metastases from pancreatic cancer is less than 6 months. It is notable that metastasis of pancreatic cancer to the liver is more common than that of colorectal cancer (77.6% versus 72.0%) [7], and this may be partially explained by the cancer-specific activations of myeloid cells in different TMEs. Therefore, further investigations of the functions and signaling pathways of myeloid cells in P-LM is needed. Myeloid cells were reported to infiltrate into tumors, and play important roles in modulating tumor inflammation and angiogenesis [13], and play an immunosuppressive role in metastasis [91]. Several therapeutic approaches targeting myeloid cells are ongoing in pre-clinical and clinical studies, including anti-inflammatory agents, therapeutic blockade of the mobilization and survival of myeloid cells, and immunostimulatory adjuvants [92]. For instance, the depletion of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) by CSF1R inhibitors augmented the efficacy of cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agents and immunotherapies, including anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 [93, 94].

Myeloid cells are heterogeneous in different tumors, yet the heterogeneity of myeloid cells in liver metastases of different tumor origins is still unclear [95]. Therefore, we next dissected the distribution and biological functions of myeloid subpopulations. We clustered the myeloid cells into fifteen clusters, including three clusters of DCs, three clusters of monocytes, and nine clusters of macrophages. We found Mph_SPP1, Mph_APOE, Mph_STMN1, and Mph_CSTB were more enriched in P-LM compared to C-LM, indicating that these cell populations specifically play a key regulatory role in P-LM TME. According to a recent study, macrophages annotated by SPP1 were considered to be pro-angiogenic and immunosuppressive in TME [29]. Consistently, our data revealed that Mph_SPP1 was functionally related to angiogenesis since there was a high activation of the VEGF pathway. In addition, we checked the biological relevance of each cluster using several published myeloid signatures and found Mph_SPP1 was described as M2 macrophages, which is recognized as immunosuppressive phenotype. Moreover, Mph_SPP1 has been reported to interact with CAF via SPP1/FN1-ITGAV to promote CRC progression and lung cancer metastasis [64, 82]. Our analysis of cell–cell communications among myeloid clusters and endothelial cells showed that P-LM possesses greater cell interactions in number and strength than C-LM. Additionally, the Mph_SPP1 cluster is the strongest subcluster in myeloid cells interacting with endothelial cells, since endothelial cells, like CAF, also express fibronectin receptors, such as ITGAV and ITGA9. Our data on the developmental trajectories of myeloid cells showed that Mph_SPP1 develops from monocytes, passes through Mph_CCL20 and Mph_CCL18, and is gradually furnished with functions such as promoting tumorgenesis and angiogenesis. Interestingly, the genes associated with regulation of endothelium gradually increased in the Mph_SPP1 cluster, such as DAB2. In sum, these findings suggested that Mph_SPP1, a pro-angiogenetic macrophage subset derived from resident tissue, may interact with endothelial cells to promote the liver metastasis of PDAC. Furthermore, the up-regulated genes, such as CHI3L1 and CCL18, can be the potential targets of molecular targeting treatments to prevent macrophages from developing into the Mph_SPP1 subtype.

Myeloid cells, especially subpopulations of TAM and DCs, have exhibited prognostic value in diverse cancers. For instance, high-density CD68+ TAM subtypes in CRC tissues were significantly associated with good 5-year overall survival rates [96], and the accumulation of IFN-α-deficient tumor-associated plasmacytoid DC in breast cancer is contributing to tumor immune tolerance and poor clinical outcome [97]. We performed survival analysis using the TCGA-COAD and TCGA-PAAD datasets to explore the prognostic roles of Mph_SPP1 in cancer. Data showed that patients with a higher level of Mph_SPP1 signature score were associated with a worse survival probability in PAAD, whereas COAD patients exhibited a non-significant correlation to the Mph_SPP1 signatures. In addition to this, univariate Cox regression analysis of PAAD identified three genes enriched in the Mph_SPP1 cluster, including CSTB, APOC1, and FAPB5, as risk factors, and the Mph_SPP1 signatures were positively correlated with stages I to III of PAAD. Together, we assumed that the high enrichment of Mph_SPP1 is related to the progression of pancreatic cancer, and Mph_SPP1 is probably also a biomarker of P-LM, combined with our previous results.

In summary, we integrated published datasets and profiled the TME landscape of two hepatic metastases in terms of myeloid subtype compositions, functions, and cellular communications at a single cell level. We hypothesized that Mph_SPP1 actively creates a fertile environment for tumors when pancreatic cancer metastasizes to the liver and promote cancer invasion in a pro-angiogenetic pattern. In existing studies, the abundance of specific immune cell subsets can be used to predict the occurrence of tumors and the efficacy of tumor treatment. However, the key cell populations associated with liver metastasis have been less recognized, and the primary tumor-associated cell populations of different primary tumors metastasizing to the liver have not been defined. The Mph_SPP1 cells we identified may serve as a macrophage subpopulation specifically recognized for pancreatic cancer liver metastasis, characterizing the liver's specific immune microenvironment facilitating pancreatic cancer metastasis. Drug intervention of Mph_SPP1 subgroup targeting the characteristic gene set related to cancer metastasis by means of immunotherapy may help control the metastasis of pancreatic cancer to liver. In sum, this study will provide a new target for prediction, evaluation and immunotherapy of hepatic metastasis of pancreatic cancer.

The present study has certain limitations that must be thoroughly considered. First, the statistical analysis was based on data derived from public-available databases. Further experiments are required to validate our results. Second, we have not explored enough types of liver metastases. Our future work will focus on integrating more cancer types and creating an experimentally verifiable transcriptome atlas of the TME profile of diverse liver metastases.

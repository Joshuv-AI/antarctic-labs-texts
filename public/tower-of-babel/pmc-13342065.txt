SC-DGLA: constraint-aware pallet demand forecasting with dynamic graph and learnable lag alignment
Authors: Bin Ye, Junhong Zheng, Linfei Zhu, Yan Chen, Xiaofeng Lei, Bo Zhang, Yijun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate and actionable pallet demand forecasting is essential for multi-echelon warehouse supply chains, particularly when facing complex inter-node dependencies and time-varying transportation lags. Existing methods, despite progress in modeling supply chain dynamics, still struggle with temporal misalignment of multi-source signals and with enforcing operational constraints, limiting the direct usability of their forecasts. We propose SC-DGLA, a constraint-aware forecasting framework that integrates dynamic graph learning with conditional learnable lag alignment (LLA). It employs dynamic graph modules to capture evolving network structures and perform multi-task edge-level predictions, and incorporates conditional LLA to temporally align production, transfer, sales, and return signals. Constraint-aware training with projection-based decoding then ensures feasibility and yields decision-ready outputs. Experiments on real-world pallet data from the central warehouse of a large retail enterprise show that SC-DGLA maintains accuracy comparable to strong baselines while achieving a forecast feasibility rate of 92.8% and reducing shortage/overcapacity rates to 3.1% and 2.7%, respectively, offering more practical and operationally feasible forecasting support for warehouse planning and decision-making.Keywords: Supply chain forecasting, Pallet demand prediction, Dynamic graph neural networks, Learnable lag alignment, Constraint-aware modeling, Multi-echelon warehousingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In multi-echelon warehousing supply chains, pallets serve as turnover carriers throughout the entire process of production, warehousing, and distribution, where the accuracy of demand forecasting directly impacts core decision-making aspects including inventory turnover, transportation scheduling, and cost control. Unlike traditional single-node material demand forecasting, pallet flows are jointly affected by multiple factors including cross-node coupling, transportation lags, capacity and transportation constraints, and business time windows, exhibiting strongly constrained, strongly interconnected, and strongly time-varying system characteristics. Against this backdrop, this paper focuses on pallet demand forecasting across nodes in multi-echelon warehouse networks, with the Central Warehouse as the primary prediction target, proposing a constraint-aware modeling framework integrating dynamic graphs and learnable lag alignment (LLA), aiming to provide reliable decision-oriented prediction outputs for actual supply chain planning and scheduling.

In recent years, time series forecasting models have been widely applied in industrial and logistics scenarios, from efficient linear kernels such as DLinear1, to hierarchical multi-scale N-HiTS2, to S4/S53,4 with long-range dependency modeling capabilities, as well as PatchTST5 with patch-based channel-independent encoding as the core, all achieving significant progress in multi-step forecasting stability and long sequence modeling. However, such “pure time series” methods typically assume that interactions between channels are stable and can be implicitly captured by shared backbones, making it difficult to explicitly represent and control topological changes, edge-level time-varying costs, and transportation lags in supply chain networks, thus exhibiting unstable performance on decision-relevant metrics such as feasibility rate, shortage rate, and overflow rate.

To characterize network structures and cross-node dependencies, graph-based time series forecasting has become an important direction. Models represented by MTGNN6, AGCRN7, GTS8, and D2STGNN9 have enhanced the characterization of spatial correlations by learning or utilizing adjacency structures, demonstrating advantages in domains such as traffic, energy consumption, and logistics. Particularly, learnable topology, edge attention, and dynamic graph modeling10,11 enable models to maintain robustness in scenarios with structural changes and relationship drift. However, existing graph time series methods mostly focus on correlation capture, with insufficient explicit incorporation of “physical-business constraints” in supply chains such as inventory conservation, capacity/transportation constraints, reachability, and time windows; this leads to potentially good performance on error metrics but deviations in feasibility and cost metrics that satisfy business constraints.

On the other hand, lag is a critical factor in supply chain flow forecasting: uncertain time delays exist in various links including production, inbound, transfer, outbound to commercial companies, and returns, with distributions affected by contextual factors such as routes, transportation capacity, weather, holidays, and promotions. Classical approaches often adopt fixed lags or heuristic alignment, but are prone to misalignment in time-varying scenarios, reducing forecasting stability and interpretability. The concept of learnable lag distributions provides a new paradigm12,13: by modeling conditional distribution , upstream driving signals are dynamically aligned with downstream observations, and consistency regularization with transportation duration distributions can be incorporated to ensure physical plausibility (related concepts are reflected in causal lag and time-varying calibration research).

At the industrial application level, forecasting requires not only “numerical accuracy” but also “decision applicability”. This requires that model outputs can be constraint-projected into feasible solutions satisfying business rules during inference, and jointly optimized through Lagrangian or soft-hard constraints during training to improve feasibility rate and reduce business costs14,15. The constraint-aware forecasting paradigm has gradually matured in operations research optimization and learning-optimization coupling research, manifested in incorporating conservation, capacity, and time window constraints into the learning process in the form of loss terms or projection operators, thereby bridging the gap between models and decision-making.

Motivated by the above observations, we propose SC-DGLA, a novel framework that integrates dynamic graph learning with learnable lag alignment for decision-ready pallet demand forecasting. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

We propose a dynamic supply chain network modeling module that employs dynamic graph learning to capture time-varying topological structures and integrates a learnable lag alignment mechanism to temporally align multi-source driving signals. Through a consistency regularization between lag distribution and transport duration prediction, this module achieves physically interpretable spatiotemporal collaborative modeling.

We develop a patch-based encoding gated fusion architecture, using PatchTST as the backbone, which dynamically fuses dynamic graph features and LLA-aligned signals through an adaptive gating mechanism, balancing long-range dependency capture and cross-channel interaction.

Related work

Time series forecasting methods

In the modeling of supply chain flows and pallet demand16,17, long sequences and multi-step rolling forecasting constitute core challenges, with pure time series methods continuously evolving along the technical path of “long-range dependency modeling–multi-scale representation–efficient decoupling”. Early Transformer-based approaches such as Informer18 and Autoformer19 explored efficient attention mechanisms and decomposition strategies for long sequences. PatchTST5 alleviates cross-channel interference and attention degradation by segmenting long sequences into local patches and performing channel-independent encoding, while enhancing the capture of long-period patterns and demonstrating excellent multi-step forecasting stability. iTransformer20, based on the input inversion concept, remodels the representation relationship between temporal and feature dimensions, emphasizing channel-level semantic self-attention aggregation, providing more direct characterization of cross-channel correlations in multivariate scenarios with certain robustness to non-stationary fluctuations that can be further enhanced through normalization techniques21.. TimesNet22 focuses on the multi-period structure of time series, adopting time-frequency domain hybrid block modeling to extract multi-scale periodic components, and enhancing the fitting capability of complex seasonality and events through structured residual routing, suitable for patterns containing repetitive but not completely stable elements such as promotions and holidays. SCINet23 employs sample convolution and interaction mechanisms to model complex temporal dependencies across multiple scales. The S4/S5 series3,4 originates from efficient state space models, encoding long-range dependencies in linearly parallelizable convolution forms, balancing memory length and computational efficiency, maintaining training and inference speed for extremely long sequences and sparse events, with certain noise suppression effects. DLinear1 emphasizes trend-seasonal decomposition and linear layers, emphasizing low parameters and strong generalization, providing robust baseline references in industrial scenarios with limited data or deployment resource constraints, but with relatively limited expressive capability for nonlinear coupling and structural mutations. N-HiTS2 extracts short-term and long-term components through multi-resolution hierarchical residuals and polynomial basis function approximation, addressing error accumulation and scale drift in multi-step forecasting, with reliable performance in rolling forecasting tasks.

Graph time series and dynamic graph methods

In multi-echelon warehouse networks, spatial correlations between node flows and temporal evolution of relationships coexist, where graph time series and dynamic graph methods enhance forecasting robustness and interpretability through joint modeling in spatiotemporal dimensions. MTGNN6, with mixed topology and temporal convolution as core components, captures non-static cross-node dependencies through adaptive adjacency learning and multi-scale temporal extraction, suitable for supply chain networks with composite pathways including direct delivery, reverse allocation, and returns. AGCRN7 introduces node-adaptive embedding and gated recurrent structures, learning implicit spatial structure and node heterogeneity without relying on fixed adjacency, exhibiting strong robustness to incomplete network structures and relationship drift. GTS8 learns graph structure from a generative perspective, treating topology as latent variables consistent with temporal signals, jointly optimizing differentiable graph generation and prediction to make adjacency formation coherent with temporal objectives, facilitating rapid adaptation during strategy adjustments or route changes. D2STGNN9 emphasizes bidirectional spatiotemporal dependencies and cross-step information interaction, capturing complementary relationships between long-term and short-term through decoupled and coupled parallel pathways, improving stability in multi-step rolling forecasting. Furthermore, learnable topology and edge attention mechanisms10,11 enable models to distinguish time-varying importance of different transportation pathways by introducing weights and attention at the edge level, and can perform multi-task learning on edges (such as predicting transportation duration and on-time rate), thereby transforming structural information into explicit priors for lags and uncertainty. Building on the advantages of MTGNN, AGCRN, GTS, D2STGNN, and learnable edge attention, this paper jointly introduces conditional learnable lag alignment and constraint-aware decoding, incorporating edge-level time-varying structure, lag distributions, and physical constraints into the training and inference process, moving graph time series modeling from “correlation improvement” toward “decision-oriented feasibility optimization”.

Lag modeling and constraint-aware forecasting

In multi-echelon supply chain pallet flow forecasting, temporal misalignment in cross-node propagation is the primary source of errors and infeasible solutions, where traditional approaches based on fixed lags, manual alignment, or cross-correlation peak detection are difficult to adapt to time-varying lags induced by route changes, holidays, promotions, and transportation capacity fluctuations. To address this, recent learning paradigms treating lags as conditional random variables have emerged, achieving dynamic alignment of driving signals and responses through modeling 12,13, and incorporating edge-level statistics such as transportation duration and on-time rate into consistency or prior regularization to avoid overfitting and physical violations. Representative directions include learnable time shift/phase alignment methods based on attention or differentiable sampling24,25, phase modeling in state space or frequency domain methods3, and explicit learnable lag kernel convolution/hybrid models26,27. In the neighboring domains of traffic and logistics, edge-level arrival time distribution and on-time rate modeling can reference travel time/arrival time distribution learning and uncertainty quantification research28,29, as well as temporal point process modeling of event sequences incorporating uncertain delays into intensity functions30,31, providing tools for utilizing transportation duration and on-time rate as edge-level priors or consistency regularization. Meanwhile, forecasting needs to satisfy business constraints such as inventory conservation, capacity, and time windows, driving constraint-aware learning-optimization coupling14,15: one class introduces Lagrangian/penalty function softening constraints during training32,33; another class maps outputs to feasible regions through projection decoding during inference34,35.

Experiments

Experimental setup

Dataset setup

To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, a real multi-echelon warehousing supply chain dataset from a large manufacturing enterprise in China is used. The data are sourced from the enterprise’s internal logistics management system, covering full-chain pallet flow records from production and warehousing to distribution. The network focuses on central warehouse, including upstream factories, elevated warehouses, finished goods warehouses and downstream nodes in municipal-level commercial companies, totaling 18 nodes. The data span from January 1, 2022, to December 31, 2024, comprising 1096 days with daily recordings. Three core types of information are included: node static attributes, such as node type (factory, elevated warehouse, finished goods warehouse, central warehouse, commercial company), geographic location, capacity upper limit (in pallet units), and average inventory-to-sales ratio; historical time series variables, including daily pallet in/out flows, inventory levels, upstream production volumes (in boxes), commercial sales, transshipment volumes, promotion events over the past 60 days, as well as aligned features and graph embeddings generated by modules; edge-level transport features, covering 12 types of directed edge relationships (e.g., forward, direct delivery, reverse allocation, recycling), including average flows, transport durations, on-time rates, and transport distances over the past 30 days, totaling 54 edges. To ensure temporal consistency and experimental fairness, the dataset is divided chronologically, with the training set accounting for 70% (January 2022 to October 2023), validation set 15% (November 2023 to April 2024), and test set 15% (May 2024 to December 2024). The raw data missing rate is 3.5%; linear interpolation is used for consecutive missing records fewer than 3 days, historical medians fill missing records of 3 days or more, supplemented by KNN imputation with k = 5. To ensure evaluation authenticity, all filled samples are excluded from validation and test sets.

Experimental setup

All models are implemented using PyTorch 2.0 and DGL, trained and inferred on a single NVIDIA A100. AdamW optimizer is used with learning rate , weight decay , batch size 32, early stopping patience 20, and maximum 200 epochs. Loss weights are , , ; LLA module maximum lag , with discrete distribution additional total variation regularization and entropy regularization ; Dynamic Graph edge weight logits apply L1 sparsity regularization (default ). Data augmentation includes Gaussian noise (), time series subsequence flipping, and random flipping of promotion events. Training uses sliding window rolling prediction with past days’ input to predict future days, advancing day-by-day and updating the history buffer to simulate real online deployment. SC-DGLA has approximately 3.2M parameters, with single-sample inference time < 50 ms for (single projection). Constraint-aware decoding uses soft penalties during training and default single projection during inference for real-time performance. Baseline models use official code and recommended hyperparameters, reproduced 3 times under the same data splits and random seeds, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation.

The ablation study evaluates the contribution of each component by progressively adding modules. Table 5 presents results on key metrics.

Results in Table 5 reveal progressive contributions of core modules in the proposed framework. Overall, each added module yields consistent and significant performance improvements, not only in continuous optimization of traditional error metrics but also in stepwise enhancements in operational feasibility and cost control.

Hyperparameter study

This section evaluates the impact of key hyperparameters on model performance through grid search, including patch size and channel dimension in the PatchTST backbone, penalty coefficients in Constraint-Aware Decoding, and maximum lag length in the Learnable Lag Alignment module. Experiments are conducted on the validation set for tuning, using a 7-day forecasting window, with other parameters fixed at defaults and based on averages from 3 runs. Performance is primarily measured by MAE and feasibility rate, with other metrics showing similar trends. Through systematic hyperparameter tuning, the optimal configuration is determined at patch size 16, channel dimension 128, penalty coefficient 1.0, and maximum lag length for best performance (Fig. 3).

Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis of SC-DGLA.

In the analysis of patch size impact, moderate patch sizes are found to achieve the best balance between local pattern capture and computational efficiency. Small patches lead to insufficient local pattern capture and significant error increases, while large patches capture longer-range dependencies but with limited performance gains and increased complexity. Results for channel dimension indicate that medium-scale feature dimensions balance representation capacity and overfitting risk well. Low dimensions limit expressiveness, degrading performance; high dimensions slightly improve performance but may face overfitting in resource-constrained deployments.

Tuning results for constraint penalty coefficients reveal inherent tension between error minimization and constraint satisfaction. Low coefficients lead models to ignore constraints for lower prediction errors but significantly reduce feasibility; high coefficients make models overly conservative, improving constraint satisfaction but sacrificing accuracy. Analysis of maximum lag length shows that sufficiently captures real transportation delays in the supply chain, with too small values failing to model long-distance node dependencies and excessively large values introducing noise and minor overfitting.

Overall sensitivity analysis reveals differentiated impacts of hyperparameters on performance. Penalty coefficients most significantly affect feasibility rates, causing large fluctuations in constraint satisfaction; patch size is more sensitive to prediction errors, directly influencing time series pattern capture. These findings provide guidance for practical deployment, helping avoid performance degradation from extreme parameter settings and ensuring stable performance across application scenarios.

Additionally, to address the sensitivity of the operational cost metric to the choice of penalty coefficients, we conducted a n analysis by varying the weights across representative ranges (shortage: 5–20, overcapacity: 5–10, flow: 1–3). The results, summarized in Table 8, show that SC-DGLA consistently achieves the lowest average daily cost across all tested combinations, preserving its ranking relative to the strongest baseline (D2STGNN). This confirms the robustness of the cost-based conclusions to reasonable variations in penalty values.

Conclusions

This study focuses on pallet demand forecasting in multi-echelon warehousing supply chains, addressing limitations of existing methods in handling inter-node coupling, time-varying lags, and operational constraints. A constraint-aware forecasting framework integrating dynamic graph learning and learnable lag alignment is proposed. Centering on finished goods warehouses and the central warehouse, the framework uses the Dynamic Graph Learning module to characterize time-varying logistics relationships, the learnable lag alignment (LLA) module to align upstream driving signals, the PatchTST backbone to capture time series patterns, and the constraint-aware decoding module to ensure prediction trajectories satisfy operational constraints. The overall model, through joint optimization, comprehensively utilizes supply chain network structures, lag priors, and time series features to generate high-accuracy and operationally feasible predictions. Extensive experiments on a real supply chain dataset show that the proposed model outperforms various advanced baselines on error metrics, with MAE reduced by approximately 5%-20%, while significantly improving feasibility metrics (feasibility rate, stockout rate, overcapacity rate) and operational costs, achieving a 92.8% feasibility rate and average daily cost reduced to 1090. Ablation studies confirm complementary contributions of dynamic graph learning, LLA, and constraint decoding modules, while hyperparameter analysis reveals model sensitivity to patch size, channel dimension, constraint penalties, and projection steps, providing optimization guidance for deployment.

Future research directions include exploring more complex lag distribution modeling methods, such as mixture distributions or non-parametric approaches, to further enhance lag estimation robustness; incorporating adaptive graph augmentation techniques, like dynamic edge weight adjustment or structural self-learning, to enrich network semantic information; investigating more efficient graph neural network architectures to improve scalability in ultra-large-scale supply chain scenarios. Through continuous framework optimization and exploration of self-supervised learning combined with graph prediction, more accurate and practical supply chain forecasting systems are expected, widely applicable to complex industrial scenarios.

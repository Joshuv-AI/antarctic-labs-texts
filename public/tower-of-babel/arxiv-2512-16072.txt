1.
Introduction

It is a fundamental problem to judge whether an algebra is left weakly Gorenstein, which is hard in general due to the lack of explicit characterizations of
(semi-)Gorenstein-projective modules. Meanwhile, the problem has its own flavor and significance, for their wide applications, for example in the representation theory,
relative homological algebra, and the singularity theory.
See e.g.
[
Buch
]
,
[
AR1
]
,
[
EJ2
]
,
[
V
]
,
[
Bel
]
,
[
Chr
]
,
[
Chen
]
,
[
RZ
]
.

The aim of this paper is to answer this problem for two classes of algebras:
one is the tensor algebra
\Lambda=A\otimes_{k}B
with
{\rm gl.dim}B<\infty
, where
A
and
B
are finite-dimensional algebras over a field
k
, and another is the Morita algebra
\Lambda=\Lambda_{(\phi,\psi)}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}A&N\\
M&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}
with specified datum
(A,B,M,N,\phi,\psi)
.
Homological theoretic aspect of tensor algebras has got importance since
H. Cartan and S. Eilenberg’s work
[
CE
]
. The Morita algebras, formulated by H. Bass
[
B
]
, originating from
the Morita equivalences
[
Mor
]
, are widely used in many branches of mathematics.

We apply different methods for the two classes of algebras. Since there are no descriptions for all the modules over a tensor algebra,
we adopt conceptual and constructive arguments via the so-called
monomorphism category. This monomorphism categories have been developed from C. M. Ringel and M. Schmidmeier’s work
[
RS
]
, and D. Simson’s work
[
S
]
on submodule categories.
While for the Morita algebras
\Lambda
, we heavily use the diagram description of
\Lambda
-modules given by E. L. Green
[
G
]
(see also
[
GrP
]
), homological computations, and analysis on exactness.

1.1.
Gorenstein-projective modules and left weakly Gorenstein algebras

A module
M
over a ring
R
is
semi-Gorenstein-projective
if
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0
for
i\geq 1
.
Consider the
R
-dual
M^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R)
and the canonical
R
-homomorphism
\phi_{M}:M\longrightarrow M^{**}
, given by
\phi_{M}(m)(f)=f(m)
for
m\in M
and
f\in M^{*}
.
If
\phi_{M}
is injective (bijective, respectively), then
M
is called
torsionless
(
reflexive
, respectively).
For a finitely generated left module
M
over a two-sided noetherian ring
R
, M. Auslander [A] has introduced the following conditions:

(G1)
M
is semi-Gorenstein-projective;

(G2)
M^{*}
is a semi-Gorenstein-projective right
R
-module; and

(G3)
M
is reflexive.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
A complete projective resolution
in
\mathcal{A}
is an acyclic complex
P^{\bullet}:\ \cdots\longrightarrow P^{-1}\longrightarrow P^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots
of projective objects of
\mathcal{A}
, such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(P^{\bullet},P)
is again acyclic for any projective object
P
of
\mathcal{A}
.
An object
G
is
Gorenstein-projective
, if there is a complete projective resolution
P^{\bullet}
such that
G\cong{\rm Ker}d_{0}.
See E. E. Enochs and O. M. G. Jenda
[
EJ1
]
.

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise specified, we consider finitely generated modules over Artin algebras.
Let
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
(
{\rm mod}A
, respectively) be the category of finitely generated left (right, respectively) modules over Artin algebra
A
, and
{\rm Gp}(A)
the full subcategory
of
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
consisting of Goresnstein-projective objects in
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
, which are called
the Goresnstein-projective modules
. Let
{}^{\perp}A
denote
the full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
consisting of semi-Goresnstein-projective modules. All the tensor product will be over field
k
, if not otherwise specified.

An important feature is that a finitely generated module
M
over an Artin algebra
A
is Gorenstein-projective if and only if
M
satisfies
the conditions (G1), (G2), and (G3) (see L. W. Christensen [Chr, Theorem 4.2.6]; also M. Auslander and M. Bridger [AB, Proposition 3.8]).
It was a longstanding problem whether the
conditions (G1), (G2), (G3) are independent (see L. L. Avramov and A. Martsinkovsky
[
AM
, p.398]
).
This problem has been completely solved recently: D. A. Jorgensen and L. M. Şega [JS] present the modules satisfying (G1) and (G3), but not (G2),
and also modules satisfying (G2) and (G3), but not (G1); modules satisfying (G1) and (G2), but not torsionless,
have been given by C. M. Ringel and P. Zhang
[
RZ
]
. It is also natural to study
semi-Gorensten-projective modules, torsionless modules, reflexive modules, and Gorenstein-projective modules, separately.

Following
[
RZ
]
, an algebra
A
is
left weakly Gorenstein
, if
{}^{\perp}A={\rm Gp}(A)
, i.e., any semi-Gorentein-projective left
A
-module is Gorenstein-projective.
Similarly, one has the notion of
a right weakly Gorenstein algebra
. It is an open problem whether a left weakly Gorenstein algebra is right weakly Gorenstein (
[
Mar2
]
;
[
RZ
, Subsection 9.3]
).
A Gorenstein algebra
A
(i.e.,
{\rm inj.dim}_{A}A<\infty
and
{\rm inj.dim}A_{A}<\infty
) is left and right weakly Gorenstein (see
[
EJ2
, Corollary 11.5.3]
),
but the converse is not true (see e.g.
[
GLZ
, Subsection 1.5]
).
There are indeed many algebras which are not left weakly Gorenstein (see
[
JS
]
,
[
Mar1
]
,
[
RZ
]
).
For more information on left weakly Gorenstein algebras we refer to
[
RZ
, Theorems 1.2 - 1.4]
and references there.

1.3.
Weakly Gorensteinness of tensor algebras

Many
(A\otimes B)
-modules are not of the form
X\otimes Y
, where
X
is an
A
-module and
Y
is a
B
-module; and in general,
there are no descriptions for all the
(A\otimes B)
-modules. This is one of the main difficulty in dealing with the tensor algebras.

If
B=kQ/I
, where
Q
is an acyclic quiver, and
I
is an admissible ideal of the path algebra
kQ
,
then any
(A\otimes B)
-module can be described as a representation of bound quiver
(Q,I)
over the algebra
A
.
From this viewpoint, the category
{\rm Gp}(A\otimes B)
of the Gorenstein-projective
(A\otimes B)
-modules can described via the monomorphism category
{\rm Mon}(A,Q,I)
, when
I
is an admissible ideal of
kQ
generated by monomial relations (see
[
LZ1
, Theorem 5.1]
,
[
LZ2
, Theorem 4.1]
). This monomorphism category is defined via the combinatorics of the bound quiver
(Q,I)
, but also admits homological interpretation (see
[
ZX
, Theorem 2.6]
).
With this homological interpretation, the monomorphism category
{\rm Mon}(B,\ ^{\perp}A)
can be also defined for any finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
B
(which is not necessarily of the form
kQ/I
) in
[
HLXZ
, Definitions 3.1, 3.6]
.
For more applications of monomorphism categories we refer to
[
KLM
]
,
[
HZ
]
,
[
GKKP
]
.

Using conceptual and constructive arguments via the monomorphism category
{\rm Mon}(B,\ ^{\perp}A)
, we can judge the weakly Gorensteinness of the tensor algebra
A\otimes B
with
{\rm gl.dim}B<\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
3.6
)
Let
A
and
B
be finite-dimensional
k
-algebras with
{\rm gl.dim}B<\infty
, and
\Lambda=A\otimes B
. Then

(1)

\Lambda
is left weakly Gorenstein if and only if
A
is left weakly Gorenstein.

(2)
If
A
is left weakly Gorenstein, then
{\vphantom{\perp}}{}^{\perp}\Lambda={\rm Mon}(B,{\vphantom{\perp}}^{\perp}A).

(3)
If
B
is not semisimple, then the converse of
(2)
is also true.

Note that the “if” part of
3.6
(1) does not need the condition
{\rm gl.dim}B<\infty
. Also, if
B
is semi-simple, then
{\vphantom{\perp}}{}^{\perp}\Lambda={\rm Mon}(B,{\vphantom{\perp}}^{\perp}A)
holds true for an arbitrary finite-dimensional
k
-algebra. Thus, in this case, the converse of Theorem
3.6
(2)
is not true. See Remark
3.7
.

Theorem
3.6
(1) is proved first for
B=kQ
in
[
Z2
, Theorem 1.3(2)]
,
and then for
B=kQ/I
in
[
LuoZhu
, Theorem C]
, where
Q
is an acyclic quiver, and
I
is an admissible ideal of
kQ
generated by monomial
relations.

1.4.
Weakly Gorensteinness of Morita algebras

We consider the Morita ring
\Lambda=\Lambda_{(\phi,\psi)}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}A&{}_{A}N_{B}\\
{}_{B}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}
which is an Artin algebra (see subsection 5.1).
Any left
\Lambda
-module can be identified with a quadruple
(X,Y,f,g)
,
where
X\in A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
and
Y\in B\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
,
f\in{\rm Hom}_{B}(M\otimes_{A}X,Y)
and
g\in{\rm Hom}_{A}(N\otimes_{B}Y,X)
(see
[
G
, Theorem 1.5]
).
We will write it as
\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}X\\
Y\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}_{f,g}
, in this way the
\Lambda
-action coincides with the multiplication of matrices.
For more information on
\Lambda
-modules we refer to
[
G
,
KT
,
GrP
,
GP
]
.

Notice that any ring can be written as a Morita ring. Rather than working in such a very broad setting, one should usually impose some specific conditions on the datum
(A,B,M,N,\phi,\psi)
in practice.
Of our particular interest, we will focus on the Morita rings satisfying

M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
, and hence
\phi=0=\psi
. This condition looks quite restrictive
at first glance, but in fact it contains many algebras we are interested, for example, the triangular matrix algebras.

Through careful homological computations and analysis on exactness, we obtain the following description of the semi-Gorenstein-projective
\Lambda\mbox{-}
modules.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem
4.9
)
Let
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}A&N\\
M&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}
be a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. Assume that
{}_{B}M,\ _{A}N
,
M_{A}
and
N_{B}
are projective modules. Then
\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}X\\
Y\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}_{f,g}\in\ ^{\perp}\Lambda
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied
:

(1)

{\rm Hom}_{A}(g,A):{\rm Hom}_{A}(X,A)\longrightarrow{\rm Hom}_{A}(N\otimes_{B}Y,A)
is an epimorphism
;

(2)

{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(g,A):{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,A)\longrightarrow{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(N\otimes_{B}Y,A)
is an isomorphism for
i\geq 1;

(3)

{\rm Hom}_{B}(f,B):{\rm Hom}_{B}(Y,B)\longrightarrow{\rm Hom}_{B}(M\otimes_{A}X,B)
is an epimorphism
;

(4)

{\rm Ext}_{B}^{i}(f,B):{\rm Ext}_{B}^{i}(Y,B)\longrightarrow{\rm Ext}_{B}^{i}(M\otimes_{A}X,B)
is an isomorphism for
i\geq 1
.

A left
\Lambda
-module
\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}X\\
Y\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}_{f,g}
is said to be
monic
, if

f:M\otimes_{A}X\longrightarrow Y
and
g:N\otimes_{B}Y\longrightarrow X
are injective maps.
The following result, which is a consequence of Q. Q. Guo and C. C. Xi
[
GX
, Proposition 3.14]
, gives the description of Gorenstein-projective modules over the Morita rings in Theorem 1.3.

Proposition 1.4
.

(Corollary
5.4
)
Let
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}A&N\\
M&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}
be a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. Assume that
{}_{B}M,\ _{A}N
,
M_{A}
and
N_{B}
are projective modules.
Then
\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}X\\
Y\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}_{f,g}\in{\rm Gp}(\Lambda)
if and only if it is a monic
\Lambda\mbox{-}
module with
\operatorname{Coker}f\in{\rm Gp}(B)
and
\operatorname{Coker}g\in{\rm Gp}(A)
.

The following observation gives a featured property of left weakly Gorenstein algebras.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Theorem
5.6
)
Let
A
be a left weakly Gorenstein algebra. If
\phi:X\longrightarrow Y
is a left
A
-homomorphism such that
\phi^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\phi,A)
is an epimorphism and

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\phi,A)
is an isomorphism for
i\geq 1
, then
\phi
is a monomorphism and
\operatorname{Coker}\phi
is a Gorenstein-projective
A
-module.

Theorem 1.3, Proposition 1.4, and Theorem 1.5 enable us to judge the weakly Gorensteinness of a class of Morita algebras:
the following result gives a satisfied answer to the question: when are the Morita algebras in Theorem 1.3 left weakly Gorenstein?

Theorem 1.6
.

(Theorem
5.1
)
Let
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}A&N\\
M&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}
be a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. Assume that
{}_{B}M,\ _{A}N
,
M_{A}
and
N_{B}
are projective modules. Then
\Lambda
is left weakly-Gorenstein if and only if
A
and
B
are left weakly-Gorenstein.

Let
T_{n}(A)
(
n\geq 2
) be the triangular matrix algebra

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&A&\cdots&A&A\\
0&A&\cdots&A&A\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A&A\\
0&0&\cdots&0&A\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. As an application one has the following results.

Corollary 1.7
.

(Corollary
5.7
)

(1)
A left
T_{n}(A)
-module
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_{n}\\
\vdots\\
X_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{(\phi_{i})}
is semi-Gorenstein-projective if and only if

\phi^{*}_{i}:\ \operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X_{i+1},A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X_{i},A)

are epimorphisms for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
X_{i}\in\ ^{\perp}A
for
all
i
.

(2)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_{n}\\
\vdots\\
X_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)_{(\phi_{i})}
is a Gorenstein-projective left
T_{n}(A)
-module if
and only if
X_{i}\in{\rm Gp}(A)
for all

i
,
\phi_{i}:X_{i}\longrightarrow X_{i+1}
are monomorphisms, and

\operatorname{Coker}\phi_{i}\in{\rm Gp}(A)
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

(3)

T_{n}(A)
is a left weakly Gorenstein algebra if and only if so is
A
.

2.1.
Some facts

We need the following facts. For a module
M

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra and
M\in A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

M
is torsionless.

(2)

M
is a submodule of some projective
A
-module
P
.

(3)
Any left
(\operatorname{add}A)
-approximation of

is a monomorphism.

Following
[
RZ
]
, an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow 0
in

is an

, if

is a left
(\operatorname{add}A)
-approximation.
The following fact shows a connection between torsionless and reflexive modules.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[

, 2.4(a)]
)
Let
0\longrightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}P\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
be an approximation sequence. Then

is reflexive if and only if
M
is torsionless.

.

If

, it follows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(G^{\prime},A)=0
for

.
Therefore
one has the exact sequence

. Since
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(G^{*},A)=0
for

,
it follows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(G^{\prime*},A)=0
for

. Thus one has a commutative diagram
with exact rows

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{G^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.2.
The Cartan-Eilenberg isomorphism

The Cartan-Eilenberg isomorphism is a powerful tool in studying modules over tensor algebras.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
CE
, Theorem 3.1, p.209, p.205]
)
Let
A
and
B
be finite-dimensional algebras over a field
k
,
L,M\in A
-
mod
, and
U,V\in B
-
mod
. Then there is an isomorphism of
k
-spaces

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{A\otimes B}(L\otimes U,M\otimes V)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}(\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(L,M)\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(U,V)),\ \forall\ n\geq 0.

A direct and elementary proof of Theorem
2.4
will be included in the appendix.
For later use we need to write out the concrete isomorphism for the case
n=0
. It is surprising that the inverse of this isomorphism can not be written out explicitly.

Corollary 2.5
.

(
[
CE
, Theorem 3.1, p.209, p.205]
)

Let
A,B
be finite-dimensional
k
-algebras,
L,M\in A
-
mod
and
U,V\in B
-
mod
. Then there is an isomorphism

\chi_{{}_{L,U,M,V}}:\ \ \operatorname{Hom}_{A}(L,M)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(U,V)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A\otimes B}(L\otimes U,M\otimes V)

given by
g\otimes h\mapsto``l\otimes u\mapsto g(l)\otimes h(u)".

The following result will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, one of the main results of this paper, and it is also of independent interest.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A
and
B
be finite-dimensional
k
-algebras and
\Lambda=A\otimes B
. Let
X
and
Y
be finitely generated modules over
A
and
B
, respectively.
If
f:X\longrightarrow P
is a left
(\operatorname{add}A)
-approximation and
g:Y\longrightarrow Q
is a left
(\operatorname{add}B)
-approximation, then

f\otimes g:X\otimes Y\longrightarrow P\otimes Q

is a left
(\operatorname{add}\Lambda)
-approximation.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any direct summand
T
of
{}_{\Lambda}\Lambda
, any homomorphism
\varphi:X\otimes Y\longrightarrow T
factors through
f\otimes g
.
Denote by
\iota:T\longrightarrow\Lambda
the inclusion and
\pi:\Lambda\longrightarrow T
its retraction. The composition
\iota\circ\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(X\otimes Y,A\otimes B)
.
According to the Cartan-Eilenberg isomorphism,
\chi_{{}_{X,Y,A,B}}^{-1}(\iota\circ\varphi)\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,A)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(Y,B)
.
Thus, there exist
s_{i}:X\longrightarrow A
and
t_{i}:Y\longrightarrow B
such that
\chi_{{}_{X,Y,A,B}}^{-1}(\iota\circ\varphi)=\sum\limits s_{i}\otimes t_{i}
.
Since
f
is a left
(\operatorname{add}A)
-approximation and
g
is a left
(\operatorname{add}B)
-approximation, there are homomorphisms
u_{i}:P\longrightarrow A
and
v_{i}:Q\longrightarrow B
such that each
s_{i}=u_{i}f
and
t_{i}=v_{i}g
for each
i
. Denote by
\psi=\chi_{{}_{P,Q,A,B}}(\sum u_{i}\otimes v_{i})\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P\otimes Q,A\otimes B)
. Then for any
x\otimes y\in X\otimes Y
, on one hand,

(\iota\circ\varphi)(x\otimes y)=\chi_{{}_{X,Y,A,B}}(\sum s_{i}\otimes t_{i})(x\otimes y)=\sum s_{i}(x)\otimes t_{i}(y).

On the other hand,

\psi\circ(f\otimes g)(x\otimes y)=\chi_{{}_{P,Q,A,B}}(\sum u_{i}\otimes v_{i})(f(x)\otimes g(y))=\sum u_{i}f(x)\otimes v_{i}g(y)=\sum s_{i}(x)\otimes t_{i}(y).

So
\iota\circ\varphi=\psi\circ(f\otimes g)
. Therefore,
\varphi=\pi\circ\iota\circ\varphi=\pi\circ\psi\circ(f\otimes g)
factors through
f\otimes g
.
∎

2.3.
Torsionless modules and reflexive modules over tensor algebras

The following fact shows the compatibility of the canonical homomorphism

Proposition 2.7

Let

2.4.
Semi-Gorenstein-projective Modules over tensor algebras

The strong Nakayama conjecture asserts that for a finite-dimensional algebra
A
, a finitely generated left
A
-module
M=0
if
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,A)=0
for all
i\geq 0
.
It is known that the strong Nakayama conjecture holds for some classes of algebras. However, it is still open in general.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A
and
B
be finite-dimensional
k
-algebras,
X\in A
-
mod
and
Y\in B
-
mod
.
If both
X
and
Y
are semi-Gorenstein-projective, then so is
X\otimes Y
.

Conversely, assume that
\findim A^{\operatorname{op}}<\infty
and
\findim B^{\operatorname{op}}<\infty
.
If
X\otimes Y
is semi-Gorenstein-projective and
X\neq 0\neq Y
, then both
X
and
Y
are semi-Gorenstein-projective.

Proof.

The first assertion directly follows from the Cartan-Elienberg isomorphism.

Conversely, assume that
\findim A^{\operatorname{op}}<\infty
,
\findim B^{\operatorname{op}}<\infty
,

X\neq 0\neq Y
, and that
X\otimes Y
is semi-Gorenstein-projective. By the Cartan-Eilenberg isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}^{m}_{A\otimes B}(X\otimes Y,A\otimes B)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=m}(\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(X,A)\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(Y,B))=0,\ \forall\ m\geq 1

one has
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(X,A)\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(Y,B)=0
for any integers

. If
\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{A}(X,A)\neq 0
for some
p>0
, then
\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{B}(Y,B)=0
for all

.
Under the assumption
\findim B^{\operatorname{op}}<\infty
, it is known that the strong Nakayama conjecture holds.
Therefore
Y=0
, which is a contradiction. Thus
X
is semi-Gorenstein-projective. Similarly,
Y
is semi-Gorenstein-projective.
∎

3.
Weakly Gorensteinness of tensor algebras

Throughout this section,
A
and
B
are finite-dimensional algebras over a field

\Lambda=A\otimes B
, where
\otimes=\otimes_{k}
.
The aim of this section is to show that if
{\rm gl.dim}B<\infty
then
\Lambda
is left weakly Gorenstein if and only if so is

.
The method used will be the monomorphism category
{\rm Mon}(B,\ {\vphantom{\perp}}^{\perp}A).

Lemma-Definition 3.1
.

(
[

, Lemma 3.2]
)

(1)
A left
\Lambda
-module

is called a monic representation of

over

, provided that one of the following equivalent conditions holds

{\rm(i)}

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{\Lambda}(A\otimes Y,M)=0

{\rm(ii)}

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,D(A)\otimes X)=0
for

and for each left module

, where
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,k)
.

{\rm(iii)}
As a left
B
-module,

is a projective module.

Denote by
{\rm Mon}(B,A)
the full subcategory of
\Lambda
-
mod
consisting of monic representation of
B
over
A
, which is called the monomorphism category of
B
over
A
.

{\rm(2)}
For a full additive subcategory

of
A
-
\textendash\operatorname{mod}

A module in
{\rm Mon}(B,{\mathcal{L}})
is called a monic representation of
B
over
{\mathcal{L}}
, and
{\rm Mon}(B,{\mathcal{L}})
is called the monomorphism category of
B
over
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Note that

When

, where

is an acyclic quiver and

is an admissible ideal generated by monomial relations,
then the monomorphism categories

and
{\rm Mon}(B,{\mathcal{L}})
coincide with the ones defined in
[

]
,
[
LZ2
]
,
[

]

,
where the terminology “monomorphism” has an explicit combinatorial interpretation.

and

-algebras and

if and only if

.

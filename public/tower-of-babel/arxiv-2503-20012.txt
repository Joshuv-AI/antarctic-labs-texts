The BPHZL renormalization of a planar quantum electrodynamics up to 2-loops and beyond
D.O.R. Azevedo, O.M. Del Cima, L.S. Lima, and E.S. Miranda

Abstract
The renormalization of a parity-even massless U ⁡ ( 1 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1)\times U(1) quantum electrodynamics in three space-time dimensions (QED 3 ) is studied by adopting the Bogoliubov-Parasiuk-Hepp-Zimmermann-Lowenstein (BPHZL) renormalization method. The presence of two massless fermions requires the Lowenstein-Zimmermann (LZ) subtraction scheme to renormalize the infrared divergences induced by the ultraviolet subtractions at 1- and 2-loops, moreover due to the model superrenormalizability the ultraviolet divergences are bounded up to 2-loops. Finally, through the BPHZL renormalization method together with the LZ subtraction scheme the ultraviolet and infrared finiteness of the model is proved up to 2-loops and beyond.

3
The BPHZL Subtraction Scheme

When dealing with massless fields, an appropriate renormalization procedure is the BPHZL subtraction scheme. This is because of the need to handle the IR divergences that arise due to the renormalization of UV divergences. This is the reason for choosing BPHZL over, for example, the BPHZ
[
30
]
(Bogoliubov-Parasiuk-Hepp-Zimmermann) subtraction scheme. Before presenting the BPHZL method, some definitions need to be introduced.
For further details, see
[
23
,
24
]

Definition 1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Feynman diagram. A subdiagram
\lambda_{i}
of
\Lambda
is a subset of vertices within
\Lambda
along with the lines that connect them. A subdiagram is called a trivial subdiagram if it either consists of the empty diagram or represents the entire diagram itself. Conversely, a subdiagram is called a proper diagram if it is not trivial.

From now on, a subdiagram has to be intended as a 1PI subdiagram.

Definition 2
.

Consider two subdiagrams
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
. Also, consider the following conditions:

\lambda_{i}\subseteq\lambda_{j}~;~~~\lambda_{j}\subseteq\lambda_{i}~;~~~\lambda_{i}\cap\lambda_{j}=\varnothing~.

(11)

If none of these conditions is satisfied, we say that
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
overlap. If
\lambda_{i}\cap\lambda_{j}=\varnothing
, we say that
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
are disjoint.

Definition 3
.

Let
\{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}\}
be a set of mutually disjoint proper subdiagrams. A reduced subdiagram
\Lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}
is obtained from
\Lambda
by contracting all
\lambda_{i},~i=1,\cdots,n
, to points (reduced vertices). Under these conditions, it follows that

\displaystyle d(\Lambda)=d(\Lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(\lambda_{i})~,

(12)

\displaystyle r(\Lambda)=r(\Lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r(\lambda_{i})~.

(13)

Definition 4
.

Let
U(\Lambda)
be a set of subdiagrams, proper or trivial, of a diagram
\Lambda
. Let
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\subseteq\Lambda
be two arbitrary elements of
U(\Lambda)
. If
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
do not overlap, for
all
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\in U(\Lambda)
, then we say that
U(\Lambda)
is a
\Lambda
-forest.

Definition 5
.

Given a subdiagram
\lambda
of a diagram
\Lambda
, consider the UV and IR subtraction degrees, denoted by
\delta(\lambda)
and
\rho(\lambda)
, respectively. Let
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\}
be an arbitrary set of subdiagrams of
\lambda
, and
\lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}
be an arbitrary reduced diagram. Under these conditions, the following inequalities hold:

\delta(\lambda)\geq d(\lambda)+b(\lambda)~,

(14a)

\rho({\lambda})\leq r(\lambda)-c(\lambda)~,

(14b)

where
b(\lambda)
and
c(\lambda)
are positive integers satisfying the following constraints:

\delta(\lambda)\geq d(\lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta(\lambda_{i})~,

(15a)

\rho(\lambda)\leq r(\lambda/\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(\lambda_{i})~,

(15b)

\rho(\lambda)\leq\delta(\lambda)+1~.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,~~

(15c)

Now we are in a position to define how to subtract the UV divergences of a divergent diagram using the BPHZL procedure.
Consider an arbitrary
n
-loop diagram
\Lambda
, divergent in the ultraviolet limit, with the integral given by

\Lambda=\int d^{3}k_{1}\cdots d^{3}k_{n}I_{\Lambda}(p,k,s)~,

(16)

where
k_{i}
represent internal momenta,
p
represents external momenta, and
s
is the Lowenstein-Zimmermann parameter. The renormalized integrand
R_{\Lambda}(p,k,s)
, is given by Zimmermann’s forest formula:

R_{\Lambda}(p,k,s)=S_{\Lambda}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}}\prod_{\lambda\in U}(-\tau_{\lambda}S_{\lambda})I_{\Lambda}(p,k,s)~.

(17)

Here,
S_{\gamma}
is the substitution operator, which alters the
p
,
k
, and
s
, in the lines corresponding to the subdiagram
\gamma
, to
p^{\gamma}
,
k^{\gamma}
, and

Appendix A
Some notations and useful relations

It is important to establish a notation that facilitates calculations. We define two tensors,
\Omega
and
\zeta
, where the former represents the product of
momenta, whereas the latter represents the product of the metric tensor
\eta
:

\begin{split}\Omega(p)_{n}^{\mu_{1}..\mu_{n}}&\equiv\begin{cases}p^{\mu_{1}}...p^{\mu_{n}}~,\textrm{if $n\geq 0$}\\
0,\textrm{if $n<0$}~~;\end{cases}\\[8.61108pt]
\zeta_{n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}&\equiv\begin{cases}\eta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}...\eta^{\mu_{n-1}\mu_{n}}+\textrm{dist. perm.},\textrm{ if $n$ is even,}\\
0,\textrm{ if $n$ is odd or $n<0$}~.\end{cases}\end{split}

(112)

Both tensors are completely symmetric, and by ”dist. perm.” we mean all distinct permutations. For instance, for the tensor
\zeta
, permutations between indices on the same
\eta
are disregarded. In addition, if ”dist. perm.” is used after a sum
of two or more tensors, then permutations between indices of different terms are forbidden. Nevertheless, permutations between indices of a product are allowed.
As a final comment about the distinct permutations, if a tensor index has a bar, then
it must be fixed and does not participate in the permutations. For example,
in the expression
A^{\bar{\rho}\mu\nu\sigma}B^{\omega\delta}+\textrm{dist. perm.}
, the index
\rho
cannot be permuted, and terms such as
B^{\rho\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is an arbitrary index, are not allowed.

It can be shown that
\zeta
obeys the following relation:

\zeta_{2n+2}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n+2}}=\zeta_{2n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n}}\eta^{\mu_{2n+1}\mu_{2n+2}}+\textrm{dist. perm.}

(113)

Indeed, suppose
\eta^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}...\eta^{\nu_{2n+1}\nu_{2n+2}}
is a term in
\zeta_{2n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n+2}}
, where
(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{2n+2})
is a permutation of
(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{2n+2})
. Since
\eta^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}...\eta^{\nu_{2n+1}\nu_{2n+2}}
is also a term of
\zeta_{2n}^{\nu_{1}...\nu_{2n}}\eta^{\nu_{2n+1}\nu_{2n+2}}
, and the latter is a term of the right-hand side of (
113
), then every term of
\zeta_{2n+2}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n+2}}
is a term of
(\zeta_{2n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n}}\eta^{\mu_{2n+1}\mu_{2n+2}}+\textrm{dist. perm.})
. Conversely, by the definition of
\zeta
, every term of
(\zeta_{2n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n}}\eta^{\mu_{2n+1}\mu_{2n+2}}+\textrm{dist. perm.})
is a term of
\zeta_{2n+2}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{2n+2}}
. Therefore, we conclude that (
113
) is true.

Another property of the tensors
\Omega
and
\zeta
is

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\rho}}\bigg(\Omega_{n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}\zeta_{2m}^{\mu_{n+1}...\mu_{n+2m}}+\textrm{dist. perm}\bigg)=\Omega_{n-1}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n-1}}\zeta_{2m+2}^{\bar{\rho}\mu_{n}...\mu_{n+2m}}+\textrm{dist. perm}

(114)

We can prove the previous result by induction. If
m=0
, then

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\rho}}\Omega_{n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}=\Omega_{n-1}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n-1}}\zeta_{2}^{\bar{\rho}\mu_{n}}+\textrm{dist. perm.},

(115)

where the distinct permutations are between the second index of
\zeta_{2}
and the
indices of
\Omega_{n-1}
.
Let us assume that (
114
) is true. Multiplying both sides of (
114
) by
\eta^{\mu_{n+2m+1}\mu_{n+2(m+1)}}
, considering all the distinct permutations between
\zeta
and
\eta
, and using (
113
), we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\rho}}\bigg(\Omega_{n}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}\zeta_{2(m+1)}^{\mu_{n+1}...\mu_{n+2(m+1)}}+\textrm{dist. perm}\bigg)=\Omega_{n-1}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n-1}}\zeta_{2(m+1)+2}^{\bar{\rho}\mu_{n}...\mu_{n+2(m+1)}}+\textrm{dist. perm}~,

(116)

which proves that (
114
) is valid.

Another important and useful result that has to be mentioned is regarding the Feynman parametrization. The denominator of a general loop integral is usually not in the form
(k^{2}+2p\cdot k-c)
, but it can be brought into this form by using the Feynman parametrization, a method that consists of introducing some Feynman parameters and substituting the original expression with an integral in these parameters. The simplest case of the Feynman parametrization is the following:

\frac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}=\int_{0}^{1}d^{3}x\frac{1}{(\alpha-\beta x)^{2}}~,

(132)

The general case is
[

]

\frac{1}{A_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}A_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots A_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\right)}{\prod_{j}\Gamma(\alpha_{j})}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\frac{\delta\left(1-\sum_{i}x_{i}\right)\prod_{j}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}-1}}{\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}x_{k}\right)^{\sum_{l}\alpha_{l}}}~.

(133)

Effect of remimazolam on inflammatory reaction after ureteroscopic lithotripsy: a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xiaoxia Gu, Jianhang Mo, Dianqing Cao, Ping Pang, Yan Lu, Ruizhao Shao, Kui Hu, Jingjing Wang, Guangbin Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUreteral stone lithotripsy may cause a strong inflammatory response and may be associated with patient outcomes. In clinical practice, remimazolam has been widely used for induction and maintenance of general anesthesia. The purpose of this study is to investigate the effect of remimazolam on the postoperative inflammatory response in patients undergoing ureteral lithotripsy.MethodThe trial was registered at the China Clinical Trial Registration Center (ChiCTR2300070006) on March 31, 2023. The study included 80 patients who underwent lithotripsy for ureteral stones, and they were divided into two groups. Patients in the remimazolam group (n = 40) or propofol group (n = 40) received sufentanil and cisatracurium in combination for induction and maintenance of anesthesia. The levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6), Tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, C-reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin (PCT) were measured before and 24 h after operation. The recovery time, recovery quality score and postoperative complications were recorded.ResultCompared with the propofol group, the changes of IL-6, TNF-α and CRP in the remimazolam group were significantly decreased before and after operation (p < 0.05), but the changes of PCT before and after operation were not significantly different (P > 0.05). The time of extubation was shorter in the remimazolam group than that in the propofol group (p < 0.05), but the scores of MOAA/s and Rass in the remimazolam group were lower than that in the propofol group within 20 min after operation (p < 0.05), there was no significant difference in the incidence of complications between the two groups.ConclusionRemimazolam is effective in reducing the 24-hour inflammatory response after ureteral stone lithotripsy and is safe and effective for use in this procedure.

Introduction

Ureteroscopic Holmium laser lithotripsy is a common method in the treatment of ureteral calculi, except for the routine medical treatment and surgical treatment. However, Ureterolithotripsy is a procedure with a strong inflammatory response [1], and the incidence of SIRS following ureterolithotomy has been reported to be as high as 16.7%−23.4% [2, 3]. Inflammatory reaction is a normal physiological reaction after operation. It is a physiological change to infection, antigen invasion, tissue damage and so on. Its aim is to eliminate microorganism or irritant and strengthen tissue repair, the degree of reaction is related to the type of operation and the degree of tissue damage [4]. However, excessive inflammation can lead to tissue damage and, in severe cases, systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), leading to physiological decompensation, organ dysfunction and death. Anesthetics and excessive inflammation can lead to cellular immunosuppression, making the patient more susceptible to infection and sepsis [5].

Anesthesia is an important link affecting perioperative inflammatory response, and anesthetic drugs can affect immune and inflammatory response to varying degrees [6]. Propofol is a commonly used intravenous general anesthetic at present, which plays an anti-inflammatory role by inhibiting innate immune response [7]. Samir et al. [8] confirmed that propofol can effectively reduce the level of inflammatory factors after cardiopulmonary bypass. Remimazolam is a new short-acting benzodiazepine, which is commonly used for induction and maintenance of anesthesia and sedation in critically ill patients [9]. Tang [10] and Haihong Fang [11] et al. Demonstrated that remimazolam can effectively reduce the release of inflammatory mediators such as IL-6 and TNF-α in endotoxin mice, reduce sepsis-related inflammatory reaction and liver, lung and other organ damage, and improve the survival rate of mice. Therefore, it is very important to effectively reduce the excessive inflammatory reaction after surgery and reduce the damage of excessive inflammatory reaction to tissues and organs.

However, at present there remains a research gap in comparative studies on the impact of total intravenous administration of remimazolam versus propofol on postoperative inflammatory responses in patients undergoing ureteroscopic lithotripsy and stone extraction. This study is the first to focus on the patient population undergoing this specific surgical procedure. Based on this, our study compares the effects of anesthesia with remimazolam and anesthesia with propofol on the inflammatory response by measuring postoperative inflammatory indicators in patients who have undergone ureteroscopic lithotripsy and stone extraction. Of course, none of the patients took any drugs that might affect the inflammatory process before or after the operation.Through systematic experimental design and rigorous data analysis, it will provide new clinical evidence for this field and is expected to offer important references for the selection of clinical anesthetic drugs and the regulation of postoperative inflammatory responses.

Method

This study was approved by the University’s Institutional Review Board (the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University) and written informed consent was obtained from all subjects participating in the trial. The trial was registered prior to patient enrollment at China Clinical Trial Registration Center (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, Trial’s clinical trial registration number is ChiCTR2300070006, Principal investigator: Xiaoxia Gu, Date of registration: March 31, 2023).

This prospective, randomized, double-blind, single-center study was conducted from March 2023 to February 2024 in the Department of Anesthesiology and Urology, Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University. This study enrolled 80 patients aged 18 to 75 years, BMI 18–30 kg/m², ASA classification I-III, who planned to undergo flexible ureteroscopic lithotripsy under general anesthesia. Preoperative preparation was performed in all patients. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients with hypersensitivity to the active ingredients of remimazolam and benzodiazepines or to all excipients. (b) Patients with preoperative infection and preoperative or intraoperative confirmation of infectious stones. (c) Patients who take sedative hypnotics for a long time or have a history of mental illness. (d) Patients with other vital organ dysfunction. (e) The expected operative time is greater than 2 h. (f) Patients who did not obtain informed consent.

All patients were divided into the remimazolam group (group R) and the propofol group (group P), and in order to ensure randomization, random numbers were generated by computer and stored in sequentially numbered envelopes. The study drugs were dispensed by an anesthesiologist nurse who did not participate in the trial, according to the numbers in the envelopes, while the anesthesiologist and surgeon in charge of the patients were unaware of the patient ‘s group assignment.

In the operating room, electrocardiogram(ECG), noninvasive systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP/DBP), heart rate(HR), pulse and Pulse oxygen saturation(SPO2), bispectral index (BIS) and body temperature were monitored. All patients were given propofol (2 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3 ~ 0.5 ug/kg) and cis-atracurium (0.15 mg/kg). Group R : remimazolam 0.3–0.4 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.3–0.5 ug/kg, cis-atracurium 0.15 mg/kg. A laryngeal mask was placed after anesthesia induction and volume-controlled ventilation was performed with an oxygen-air mixture, with an I/e ratio of 1:2 and a tidal volume of 6–8 ml/kg, and respiratory frequency was adjusted to maintain end-respiratory carbon dioxide at 35–40 mmhg, the maximum inspiratory pressure is limited to 35 cmH2O. Anesthesia Maintenance: propofol 4–8 mg/kg/h was infused continuously in group P, remimazolam 0.5–1.5 mg/kg/h was infused continuously in group R, cis-atracurium and sufentanil were added during operation if necessary. The BIS was maintained between 40 and 60. HR and SBP/DBP were kept within 20% of the preoperative value. Indwelling the catheter at the end of the procedure is to stop the infusion of remimazolam or propofol.

After the operation, the patients were transferred to postanesthesia care unit (PACU) with laryngeal mask. The laryngeal mask was removed when the patients recovered spontaneous respiration (tidal volume > 6 ml/kg, respiratory rate > 13/min), SPO2 > 90% and BIS > 80, patients were transferred to the general surgical ward when the resuscitation time was in the critical zone and the modified Aldrete score was > 9 min.

The main study results are the changes of inflammatory factors before and after surgery, and the secondary study results include the postoperative extubation time, postoperative recovery quality, intraoperative and postoperative adverse reactions, etc. Data recorded included demographic data, duration of surgery and anesthesia, time to extubation (time from discontinuation of remimazolam/propofol to extubation), PACU residence time, time from extubation to discharge from the operating room, modified observer’s assessment of alert/sedation (MOAA/S) and Richmond restlessness and sedation scores(RASS) before surgery (T0) and at 0 min(T1), 10 min(T2), 20 min(T3), 30 min(T4) after extubation, and at discharge from the operating room (T5), incidence of postoperative adverse reactions, and changes in inflammatory cytokines before and after surgery.

The adverse reactions included delayed recovery from anesthesia, intraoperative awareness, decreased Oxygenation after extubation (SPO2 < 90%), re-intubation after extubation, nausea and vomiting, hypotension, postoperative shivering and restlessness during the recovery period.

Blood samples were taken before anesthesia induction and 24 h after operation to detect the levels of Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Tumor necrosis factors (TNF-α), C-reactive protein (CRP) and Procalcitonin (PCT). The results of CRP and PCT were sent to the clinical laboratory of our hospital for examination after blood drawing. For TNF-α, IL-6 detection, blood samples were collected in tubes without additives, and centrifuged serum samples were stored at −20℃ until analysis, using a chemiluminescent immunoassay (Immulite Analyzer; Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) measured the concentrations of TNF-α, IL-6.

Sample size calculation

The sample size is calculated by the mean value of PCT difference between preoperative and postoperative 24 h. According to the pre-test results of two independent groups (10 patients receiving remimazolam and 10 patients receiving propofol), the required sample size of each group is 36 cases calculated by PASS 15.0 software according to the two-sided significance level of 0.05 and the test power of 0.8. The loss to follow-up rate of patients is expected to be 10%. It is expected that 40 cases will be collected in each group in this study.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 22.0 software. Before using parametric or non-parametric tests, all continuous variables were evaluated for normality through Shapiro-Wilk test and Q-Q plot. Based on the evaluation results, quantitative data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and independent sample t-test was used for inter-group comparison, quantitative data not conforming to normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range), and Mann-Whitney U test was used for inter-group comparison. When P < 0.05, the difference was statistically significant.In this study, when performing multiple comparisons on the pre-set primary outcome indicators (inflammatory factor levels at 24 h after surgery), no α correction was made, because they are regarded as a family of related indicators aimed at comprehensively evaluating the inflammatory response. The increase in the risk of Type I error in such analyses is acknowledged. The results of all secondary outcomes and exploratory analyses should be regarded as hypothesis-generating and need to be verified by future studies.

Result

In this study of 86 patients, 5 patients underwent percutaneous nephrolithotomy because of intraoperatively confirmed infectious stones and 1 patient underwent percutaneous nephrolithotomy because of ureteral stricture. Eighty patients completed the study (40 in group R and 40 in group P) (Fig. 1). There was no significant difference in age, gender, BMI, ASA classification, length of hospital stay between the two groups (Table 1).

From the perspective of grouping, 3 patients in Group P experienced nausea and vomiting, all of which were Grade 1–2. 2 patients in Group R experienced nausea and vomiting, also Grade 1–2. One patient in Group R fell asleep again after regaining consciousness, accompanied by a decrease in oxygen saturation (SPO₂ < 90%). The patient returned to normal after being called, having the jaw lifted, and receiving oxygen via a mask. This situation was classified as Grade 1 post-extubation oxygenation decline.There was no statistically significant difference in the incidence of postoperative complications between the two groups of patients (P > 0.05). Specifically, in terms of nausea and vomiting, 3 cases occurred in the PACU of Group P and 2 cases in the PACU of Group R. In terms of decreased oxygen saturation, 1 case in Group R had a decrease in oxygen saturation (SPO₂ < 90%) when falling asleep again after regaining consciousness, which returned to normal after treatment and was classified as a Grade 1 adverse event.

Discussion

Ureteroscopic lithotripsy is the main method for the treatment of ureteral calculi at present. Compared with traditional open surgery, ureteroscopic lithotripsy has less trauma and less postoperative pain. However, the inflammatory reaction after ureteroscopic surgery is still strong and closely related to stone size, perfusion volume and stone type, such as release of Proinflammatory cytokine and acute phase proteins. Increased levels of markers of postoperative inflammation, including inflammatory factors and CPR, are associated with tissue damage, postoperative infection, and complication rates [10].

The release of proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α is usually very rapid, which can increase within a few hours after surgical trauma, and the peak plasma concentration of IL-6 usually appears 4–12 h after surgery. The synthesis of acute-phase proteins such as CRP is induced by IL-6 in the liver, and there is a lag effect in the significant increase of its level. The plasma concentration usually reaches the peak 24–48 h after surgery. The elevation pattern of PCT under non-infectious surgical stress is similar to that of CRP, but the magnitude is usually lower. The 24th hour after surgery is a critical window period that can comprehensively reflect the early inflammatory response and the initiation of the acute-phase response. Measuring at this time can not only capture the state where early cytokines such as IL-6 are still at a relatively high level, but also observe the significant upward trend of CRP. IL-6, TNF-α and CRP are one of the most important inflammatory factors and acute phase reaction proteins, which are affected by surgery and anesthesia [4]. They are considered as markers of inflammatory response to tissue injury and guide the use of postoperative antibiotics to a certain extent. In this study, the postoperative elevation of IL-6, TNF-α and CRP was significantly reduced in the remimazolam group compared with the propofol group, which is related to the anti-inflammatory properties of remimazolam. And it is consistent with the results of many previous studies. In a macrophage sepsis model, remimazolam inhibited the production of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α by macrophages [11, 12]. In addition, in clinical trials, remimazolam exerted stronger anti-inflammatory effects than dexmedetomidine, and these effects were enhanced by its antioxidant properties, which may have affected postoperative CRP production[13]. Similarly, in a study on the effect of remimazolam on inflammatory response after thoracoscopic surgery, the addition of remimazolam during surgery significantly reduced IL-6 and TNF-α levels [14].

This study observed that remimazolam has an inhibitory effect on IL-6 and TNF-α, but has no significant impact on other inflammatory factors (such as PCT), suggesting that its anti-inflammatory effect may be selective. Tang et al. [10] confirmed that remimazolam can interfere with the function and expression of Rab5a and then reduce the expression of TLR4 on the cell surface. Inhibiting the phosphorylation of ERK1/2, P38 and AKT can affect the signal transduction pathway and inhibit the inflammatory response. In addition, Wei xu [15] and Min Shi [16] et al. also proved that remimazolam can also exert anti-inflammatory properties by affecting cell death pathways such as apoptosis and pyrosis. Although the two groups of patients are comparable in terms of clinical characteristics that may significantly affect the inflammatory level, such as stone size, location, presence or absence of preoperative obstruction, and presence or absence of stent implantation (see Table 1), these factors themselves are important drivers of the postoperative inflammatory response. We conducted an exploratory analysis, and the trend of the anti-inflammatory effect in the remimazolam group still existed in the subgroups stratified by preoperative obstruction, which provides further support for our main conclusion.

In this study, although the change of PCT before and after operation in the remimazolam group was smaller than that in the propofol group, the difference of PCT before and after operation in the two groups was not statistically significant. Stephen Fôn Hughes et al. [17] reported that in the treatment of renal calculi using flexible ureteroscopy, there was no statistically significant difference in PCT before and after surgery, and no clear correlation between PCT and complications was found, which was consistent with the findings of this experiment, although PCT changes were observed in retroperitoneal laparoscopic ureterolithotomy [18]. The results of this study indicate that PCT level may not be an effective stress indicator for patients undergoing flexible ureteroscopy, and previous studies have also confirmed that PCT is not an effective marker for the diagnosis of urinary tract infection caused by obstructive ureteral calculi [19]. It is necessary to further study the effectiveness of PCT in the diagnosis of postoperative inflammatory reaction after flexible ureteroscopy for the treatment of calculi.

This experiment also evaluated the safety and effectiveness of remimazolam in ureteroscopic lithotripsy.This experiment showed that compared with propofol, the extubation time of remimazolam group was shorter, but within 20 min after extubation, the RASS score and MOAA/S score of remimazolam group were lower, although the recovery time of the two groups was similar.This is consistent with previous research reports [20].

Remimazolam is a new benzodiazepine sedative. Compared with propofol, the incidence of respiratory and circulatory inhibition is lower [21]. In addition, in the study of pharmacokinetics of continuous infusion of remimazolam in healthy volunteers, it was confirmed that the half-life after 4 h of continuous infusion of remimazolam was 6.8 ± 2.4 min, and the full recovery of vigilance was 19 ± 7 min after stopping infusion, but it was still longer than the half-life in the one-room model of short-term infusion of propofol [22, 23], which was consistent with the findings of this experiment.After stopping infusion of sedative drugs at the end of the operation, the respiratory function of patients in the remimazolam group recovered faster, but the recovery of complete alertness after extubation took longer.

There was no statistically significant difference in the incidence of adverse reactions between the two groups. Two patients in the remimazolam group and three patients in the propofol group developed postoperative nausea and vomiting. One patient in the remimazolam group fell asleep again after extubation, accompanied by snoring and a decrease in oxygen saturation (SpO₂ < 90%). The patients with nausea and vomiting in both groups had no history of motion sickness or postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) before surgery, and the nausea and vomiting were considered to be caused by adverse reactions to opioid drugs. For the patient who fell asleep after extubation, it was considered that the sedative effect caused by the incomplete metabolism of remimazolam or sufentanil led to airway obstruction due to glossoptosis. The oxygen saturation returned to normal after the patient was aroused, the jaw was lifted, and oxygen was inhaled through a mask. Except for the above-mentioned adverse reactions, no serious adverse reactions occurred in either group during the perioperative period, and the vital signs of patients in both groups were stable during and after surgery. This confirms that the use of remimazolam tosylate for sedation during surgery is as safe and effective as propofol in ureteroscopic lithotripsy and stone extraction.

This study also has certain limitations. We are fully aware that a single time point cannot completely depict the dynamic change curves of all markers. Adding measurement points at 6 h and 48 h after surgery would allow for a more accurate description of the peaks of IL-6 and CRP, thereby providing more abundant information. However, patients generally resist multiple blood collections, and multiple postoperative blood collections would make it difficult to ensure that each subject completes the experiment smoothly, leading to incomplete experimental data. This is a limitation of our study. In addition, although remimazolam is not metabolized by the kidneys, this study did not analyze the effect of intraoperative sedation with remimazolam during general anesthesia on renal function. In subsequent studies, we will more comprehensively reveal the time-effect characteristics of perioperative application of remimazolam on inflammatory response by adding detections at multiple time points.

Conclusion

In conclusion, compared with the two groups of patients, remimazolam can effectively reduce postoperative IL-6 and TNF- α,CRP level increased, and there was no statistically significant difference in adverse reactions. Remimazolam can be safely and effectively used in ureteroscopic lithotripsy and effectively reduce postoperative inflammatory reaction.

Mari's novel T shaped incision in frenotomy technique with bilateral pedicle flap- an aesthetic approach
Authors: K Rudhra, Ranjith Mari, Anitha Balaji, Mohan Valiathan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAberrant frenum attachments often lead to mucogingival deformities, culminating in both functional impairments and aesthetic concerns. Traditional frenectomy procedures are associated with extensive incisions and resultant wound defects. To address these challenges, a novel T-shaped incision technique has been developed. This method enhances accessibility for removing vestibular and frenal attachments while creating a tripod layer of flaps, thereby promoting primary closure and improved blood supply for healing by primary intention.Case managementThis innovative technique is particularly suited for managing frenum variations such as bifid frenum, persistent tectolabial frenum, double frenum, and wider frenum. The procedure involves crafting two horizontal and two converging parallel incisions that form a “T" shape, facilitating effective fiber detachment. Bilateral pedicle flaps are then employed for primary closure, which concurrently enlarges the zone of attached gingiva.ConclusionThe T-shaped incision approach presents an advanced method for treating abnormal frenum attachments, ensuring efficient fiber separation and fostering healing by primary intention.

Introduction

The frenum is a delicate mucosal fold composed of muscle fibers and connective tissue, anchoring the lip and buccal mucosa to the underlying periosteum. Typically, as the alveolar bone undergoes vertical growth, the frenulum's attachment migrates apically and diminishes in size. However, anomalies in its size and positioning can precipitate functional and aesthetic disturbances that necessitate surgical intervention.1

Frenectomy, the surgical excision of an aberrant frenum, is commonly indicated in scenarios involving diastema, gingival recession, restricted labial mobility, and certain orthodontic and prosthodontic objectives.2 Frenotomy, a less invasive procedure involving the clipping of the frenulum, realigns the frenum by excising it from the apex to its base down to the alveolar process.3 Nevertheless, traditional frenectomy techniques are associated with drawbacks such as scarring, which may culminate in periodontal aesthetic concerns.

Miller's surgical technique incorporates a pedicle flap for primary closure but can result in surgical wounds at the lateral donor site. While paralleling techniques have been developed to mitigate these limitations, additional vestibular attachments responsible for muscle tension necessitate further intervention. To address these challenges, a novel T-shaped incision technique is proposed. This method offers enhanced accessibility for the removal of vestibular and frenal attachments and facilitates the creation of a tripod layer of flaps. Such a configuration promotes closure and augments blood supply, thereby supporting healing by primary intention. Importantly, the apex of the frenum, constituting one of the triple layers of the flap, is repositioned in the apical region to restore its anatomical function and maintain the operative role of the frenum.4

Case description

A 25-year-old male presented with a maxillary labial aberrant frenum accompanied by a midline diastema and gingival inflammation. Clinical examination revealed a simple type of frenum with a papillary attachment. The patient's periodontal phenotype was classified as thick scalloped with inadequate vestibular depth. Given these findings, the novel T-shaped frenotomy technique was planned to reposition the frenum to its anatomical location while alleviating frenal and vestibular fibre tension.

Surgical technique

After administering local anesthesia, two horizontal incisions were made at the apex of the frenum. Subsequently, two converging parallel incisions were executed on either side of the frenum, collectively resembling a “T" shape (Fig. 1). This approach resulted in the formation of a triple layer of flaps derived from the lateral and apical regions (Fig. 2). The expanded surgical field facilitated the piercing and mitigation of vestibular and frenal fibers, thereby reducing muscle tension. The apical portion of the frenum, constituting the third flap, was preserved and relocated to its anatomical position within the mucosal area to maintain lip stability (Fig. 3). The lateral flaps were then approximated centrally and sutured (Fig. 4). Postoperative instructions and medications were provided, and the patient was monitored over the follow-up period (Fig. 5). Discussion

The frenum, an integral component of the mucosal membrane, contributes to the stability of the lip mucosa. However, aberrant positioning of the frenum can lead to both functional and aesthetic complications. The introduced T-shaped incision technique represents a periodontal plastic procedure aimed at addressing these challenges. This method allows for the repositioning of the frenum without generating extensive defect areas. The incision design enhances accessibility for vestibular deepening in conjunction with the alleviation of frenal fiber attachments. Concurrently, the frenum is relocated to the most apical extent to reestablish its anatomical attachment, thereby supporting, and stabilizing the upper lip. The bilateral pedicle flaps facilitate central approximation and suturing, achieving primary closure.

Anomalous frenal attachments encroaching upon the marginal gingiva, as evidenced by a positive blanching test, can promote plaque accumulation, expedite periodontal disease progression, and potentially lead to recurrence post-therapy.5 Frenectomy is warranted when an ectopic frenum is associated with insufficient attached gingiva and a shallow vestibule, particularly in the context of the aforementioned issues.6 It is imperative to identify pathological frenum characteristics prior to initiating orthodontic treatment to underscore the significance of periodontal plastic procedures.7

Traditional frenectomy techniques are often accompanied by complications such as large wounds due to extensive incisions, resulting in excessive intraoperative bleeding and patient discomfort.8 Miller's technique offers benefits including healing with the formation of a continuous band of gingiva across the midline and preservation of trans-septal fibers, which safeguard the interdental papilla. These factors underpin the rationale of the novel technique, which achieves bilateral pedicle flaps with frenal repositioning to enhance healing and aesthetic outcomes while preventing the progression of gingival recession.9 Alternative techniques, such as V-Y plasty and paralleling methods, have been employed to cover defects and elongate the frenum area, yielding favorable clinical results.10 Considering these aspects, and with the objective of preserving the frenum in conjunction with fiber detachment to promote healing by primary intention, the novel T-shaped incision technique was developed for the management of aberrant frenum.

Conclusion

The T-shaped incision technique offers a pioneering approach to frenotomy, enabling effective relocation of the frenum without the creation of extensive defect areas. This method provides enhanced accessibility for vestibular deepening and alleviation of frenal fiber attachments. By repositioning the frenum to its most apical extent, the procedure restores its anatomical attachment, thereby supporting and stabilizing the upper lip. The utilization of bilateral pedicle flaps facilitates primary closure, promoting optimal healing and aesthetic outcomes. This technique is particularly advantageous for managing variations such as bifid labial frenum, persistent tectolabial frenum, double frenum, and wider frenum. However, it may not be suitable for cases involving frena with nodular and appendix attachments or in patients with thin periodontal phenotypes. Multiple incisions inherent to the technique could potentially delay the healing process. Nonetheless, with appropriate case selection and meticulous surgical execution, the T-shaped incision technique stands as a valuable addition to the repertoire of periodontal plastic surgical procedures.

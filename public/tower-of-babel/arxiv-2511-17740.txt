Subdyadic time–frequency analysis: Gabor frames, modulation spaces, and Miyachi multipliers
Vicente Vergara

Abstract
We present a time–frequency framework adapted to dispersive phase functions via a subdyadic geometry in phase space. On top of this geometry we construct stable Gabor frames with quantitative control of overlap, almost orthogonality, and off-diagonal decay. Based on these frames we introduce modulation spaces consistent with the subdyadic scale and establish window and lattice independence, identifications in the Hilbertian case, duality, and natural inclusion relations. Within this setting we develop a theory for two-sided Miyachi multipliers, relying on discrete almost diagonalization and Wiener–Jaffard type results for well-localized matrices, and obtain boundedness on weighted modulation spaces. Finally, we define a Gabor-type wavefront set adapted to the subdyadic geometry and prove its invariance and ellipticity with respect to smooth order-zero pseudodifferential operators. Taken together, these results provide a unified tool both for global microlocal analysis and for the design of stable numerical schemes in high-frequency regimes.



Keywords
: Subdyadic Gabor frames; dispersive modulation spaces; Miyachi multipliers;
Gabor wavefront set; pseudodifferential operators; time–frequency analysis.

Introduction

The Gabor transform (short-time Fourier transform, STFT) plays a central role in time–frequency analysis, as it provides a representation that is simultaneously localized in space and frequency and quantitatively reflects the uncertainty principle; its formalism allows one to construct stable frames, define and characterize modulation spaces, and design robust algorithms for feature extraction, compression, and filtering in signals and solutions to PDEs
[
9
,
8
,
10
]
. Beyond its foundational role in signal processing, the STFT and Gabor frames offer an efficient bridge between the geometry of phase space and numerical discretization: the possibility of adapting the window and the lattice to the dynamics of the problem enables sparse representations and natural preconditioners, with a direct impact on global microlocal analysis and on high-frequency computational methods.

In a recent development, Jaming and Rathmair have shown that Gaussian Gabor frames allow one to tackle
phase retrieval
problems in a stable way by means of convex optimization techniques
[
16
]
. In their work, the modulus of the STFT (the spectrogram) sampled on a uniform lattice is interpreted as a family of quadratic measurements which, after a
lifting
procedure, lead to a
matrix completion
problem solved by semidefinite programming. The underlying geometry is Euclidean and isotropic, and the Gaussian window plays a crucial role in connecting the STFT with the Bargmann transform and entire functions of several complex variables. From this perspective, our subdyadic framework provides an alternative family of time–frequency frames, adapted to dispersive phases of the form
|\xi|^{\alpha}
and to the region
\{|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
, which is natural for designing
dispersive
variants of these reconstruction (or numerical preconditioning) schemes in situations where the classical Euclidean geometry does not adequately reflect the dynamics of the problem.

Dispersive phenomena governed by radial phases of the form
|\xi|^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha>0
, exhibit an intrinsic anisotropy in phase space that is not well captured by the classical dyadic paradigm. In the frequency region
|\xi|\sim 2^{j}
, the relevant scale for operators with essentially linear phase in
\xi
naturally leads to cells
(\Delta x,\Delta\xi)\sim(2^{-j},2^{j})
. However, when the phase is
|\xi|^{\alpha}
, the interplay between curvature and oscillation requires a
subdyadic resolution
: the effective frequency size becomes
|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
and its spatial dual
|\xi|^{\alpha-1}
. This redistribution of time–frequency resolution has materialized in refined subdyadic tools (square functions and maximal operators) that have proved effective in weighted harmonic analysis
[
1
]
and that are naturally connected to the theory of Miyachi multipliers
[
20
,
21
]
and to the classical body of Littlewood–Paley inequalities and extrapolation
[
25
]
.

More precisely, let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We consider multipliers
m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
supported in

\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}.

We say that
m
satisfies the
Miyachi condition
if

|D^{\gamma}m(\xi)|\;\lesssim\;|\xi|^{-\beta+|\gamma|(\alpha-1)},\qquad\text{for all }\xi\text{ with }|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1,

(1)

for all multi-indices
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\gamma|\leq N_{d}
, where

N_{d}=\Big\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\Big\rfloor+1.

The implicit constant may depend on
d,\alpha,\beta
and
N_{d}
, but not on
\xi
.

A more flexible version, suitable for the analysis of Beltrán and Bennett
[
1
]
, consists in requiring only a local quadratic control of the derivatives. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of Euclidean balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write
r(B)
for its radius and
\mathrm{dist}(B,0)
for the distance from the center of
B
to the origin. Given a multiplier
m
supported in
\{|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
, we say that it satisfies a
Hörmander–Miyachi
type condition if

\sup_{B}\;\mathrm{dist}(B,0)^{\,\beta+(1-\alpha)|\gamma|}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|D^{\gamma}m(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

(2)

for all multi-indices
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq N_{d}
, where the supremum is taken over all
\alpha
-subdyadic
balls
B
, that is,

\mathrm{dist}(B,0)^{\alpha}\geq 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad r(B)\sim\mathrm{dist}(B,0)^{1-\alpha},

where
r(B)
denotes the radius of
B
. These balls provide a natural covering of the region
\{|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
by blocks whose radial scale is precisely
|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
.

The condition (
2
) is strictly weaker than the pointwise condition (
1
) above, but it still captures the same oscillatory and growth regime of the symbols. This is due to the fact that it measures the regularity of
m
in an averaged sense over each ball, and the weight
\mathrm{dist}(B,0)^{\,\beta+(1-\alpha)|\gamma|}
compensates the expected growth/decay of
D^{\gamma}m
.

In analogy with the
g
-functions developed by Stein
[
24
]
, the authors of
[
1
]
define subdyadic variants of the
g
-functions, constructed using convolution with a function
\varphi_{t}
. The corresponding
subdyadic square function
is defined by

g_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{t^{\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{|x-y|\leq t^{1-\alpha}}|f\ast\varphi_{t}(y)|^{2}\;t^{-(1-\alpha)d-2\beta}\,dy\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

For
\alpha=0
and
\beta=0
(with a suitable choice of
\varphi
) one recovers a variant of the classical Littlewood–Paley
g
-function. The parameter
\beta
introduces a radial weight in the
t
variable.

By analogy with the classical
g^{*}_{\lambda}
function
[
24
]
, one introduces

g^{*}_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{t^{\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f\ast\varphi_{t}(y)|^{2}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)^{-d\lambda}\;t^{-(1-\alpha)d-2\beta}\,dy\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2},

with
\lambda>0
. By construction,
g^{*}_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda}
is a pointwise majorant of
g_{\alpha,\beta}
, and for
\lambda
sufficiently large it enjoys better analytic properties (for instance, in weighted estimates).

Two-sided Miyachi condition

Let
m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
. We say that
m
satisfies the
two-sided Miyachi condition
if there exist
\sigma>d/2
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every multi-index
|\gamma|\leq\lfloor\sigma\rfloor
,

|D^{\gamma}m(\xi)|\lesssim\begin{cases}|\xi|^{-\beta+(\alpha-1)|\gamma|},&|\xi|\geq 1,\\[3.0pt]
|\xi|^{-\beta_{0}+(\alpha_{0}-1)|\gamma|},&0<|\xi|<1,\end{cases}

(3)

with parameters
(\alpha,\beta)
at infinity and
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
near the origin (see
[
20
,
21
]
).

The corresponding symbol class naturally includes the model symbols

m_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\beta}e^{\,i|\xi|^{\alpha}},

which were studied in depth by Hirschman
[
12
]
, Wainger
[
27
]
, Fefferman
[
7
]
, Fefferman and Stein
[
6
]
, Miyachi
[
21
]
, among others.

The classical results of Miyachi ensure that, under this condition, one obtains optimal
L^{p}\to L^{q}
bounds for the continuous linear operator
T_{m}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m\mathcal{F}(\cdot)]
, with relations between
(p,q,\alpha,\beta)
that can be expressed in terms of Sobolev-type conditions.

As a consequence of the main results obtained by Beltrán and Bennett
[
1
]
on the subdyadic functions
g_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda},g^{*}_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda}
, they prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary 11,
[
1
]
)
.

If
m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies (
3
) for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\gamma|\leq N_{d}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{m}f|^{2}w\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{2}M^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}M^{4}w,

(4)

where

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}w(x):=\sup_{(y,r)\in\Lambda_{\alpha}(x)}\frac{1}{|B(y,r)|^{1-2\beta/d}}\int_{B(y,r)}w,

and

\Lambda_{\alpha}(x)=\{(y,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty):|x-y|\leq r^{\,1-\alpha}\}.

Here,
M
denotes the classical Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator and
M^{k}
stands for the
k
-fold composition of
M
with itself.

This work proposes a
subdyadic
time–frequency framework consistent with the phase
|\xi|^{\alpha}
and tailored to microlocal applications. The starting point is an anisotropic metric on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
encoding the dispersive scale

g^{\alpha}_{(x,\xi)}(dx,d\xi)\;\asymp\;|\xi|^{-2(\alpha-1)}|dx|^{2}+|\xi|^{-2(1-\alpha)}\,|d\xi|^{2},

from which we define elementary blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
of frequency size
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
and spatial size
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}
. The projection of
Q_{\alpha}
onto the frequency space recovers the
\alpha
–subdyadic balls of
[
1
]
, which allows us to inherit directly their partitions of unity and quadratic estimates. On top of this geometry we construct a deformed lattice of wave packets (Gabor system)

\Omega_{\alpha}=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}):k\in\mathbb{N},\,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\},

and we show that the packets
\{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}_{(j,k)\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
form a stable
L^{2}
frame, so that the adapted Gabor transform (dispersive STFT) induces a stable and redundant representation of data that reflects the underlying dispersion. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic construction of a global Gabor frame adapted to the subdyadic geometry of
[
1
]
, which translates their geometric intuition into the language of time–frequency frames
[
9
,
10
,
8
]
while preserving precise quantitative control of localization in
(x,\xi)
.

On the lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
we define
dispersive
modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
by means of
\ell^{q}(\ell^{p})
-type norms on the Gabor coefficients, with a radial weight
w_{\beta}(\xi_{k}):=(1+|\xi_{k}|)^{\beta}
reflecting the subdyadic scale. We prove window and lattice independence in a natural range of parameters, identify
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}
with
L^{2}
, and establish duality and monotone inclusion relations. The norms
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
measure regularity and concentration in a way that is faithful to the dispersive dynamics: their design incorporates both the curvature of the phase and the frequency concentration dictated by
|\xi|^{\alpha}
, in line with anisotropic phase–space localization
[
10
,
4
]
. To the best of our knowledge, these dispersive modulation spaces are not merely a reparametrization of the classical modulation spaces, but rather form a genuinely new scale adapted to the subdyadic geometry, which is particularly relevant for problems in global microlocal analysis and for wave packet based numerical schemes, where stability and anisotropic localization are key requirements (
[
3
,
2
,
23
]
).

Within this framework, we address the boundedness of Fourier multipliers with symbols depending on
\xi
that satisfy two-sided Miyachi conditions. Under the hypothesis (
3
) with parameters
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
(introduced in
[
1
]
), we prove the continuity of
T_{m}
on
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}
(cf. Theorem
5.4
), that is,

\|T_{m}f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta-\beta_{0}}},

as a first step towards a full theory on
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
based on extrapolation and discrete almost diagonalization. In particular, for purely oscillatory symbols (for instance,
m(\xi)=e^{i|\xi|^{\alpha}}
) one obtains boundedness of
T_{m}
on
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}
for every
\beta
. When
\beta=0
, the identification
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}=L^{2}
(Proposition
4.9
) shows that this result is consistent with the weighted
L^{2}
estimate of
[
1
]
, Theorem
1.1
.

Finally, we introduce a
subdyadic Gabor wavefront set

WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(u)
that characterizes microlocal regularity in terms of conical decay of the dispersive STFT after spatial localization. We prove
microlocal invariance
under order-zero smooth pseudodifferential operators (in the standard sense) acting on packets adapted to
g^{\alpha}
, as well as
ellipticity
in the usual sense, obtaining

WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(Au)\subset WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(u)\quad\text{and}\quad(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(u)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(Au)

under ellipticity of
a(x,\xi)
at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
(Theorem

). In this way we extend to the subdyadic regime the basic theory of global Gabor wavefront sets (microlocal invariance and ellipticity) developed in
[
4
]
, and we set the stage for propagation of singularities results in dispersive settings adapted to the phase
|\xi|^{\alpha}
. For historical background, the

]
, while its

We write

; we write

when both

2
Subdyadic geometry in phase space

In this section we fix the
reference geometry
in phase space
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}
that will be used to define subdyadic lattices, time–frequency blocks, and later on modulation spaces and wavefront sets.

2.1
Natural scale for dispersive phases

We consider dispersive phase functions of the form

\varphi(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha>0,

which give rise to evolution operators such as
e^{it(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see, for instance,
[
17
,
19
]
). The phase variation is governed by

\nabla_{\xi}\varphi(\xi)\sim|\xi|^{\alpha-1},

so that a frequency increment
\Delta\xi
produces a phase variation of size

\Delta\varphi(\xi)\ \sim\ |\xi|^{\alpha-1}\,|\Delta\xi|.

The
natural
frequency scale associated with the phase
|\xi|^{\alpha}
is therefore the one at which the phase changes by an amount of order one, namely

|\Delta\varphi(\xi)|\sim 1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\Delta\xi|\sim|\xi|^{1-\alpha}.

This observation suggests that, in order to resolve accurately the oscillatory behavior of operators with phase
|\xi|^{\alpha}
, it is natural to decompose the frequency space into blocks whose characteristic length in the radial direction is comparable to
|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
. This is precisely the guiding principle behind the subdyadic decomposition introduced in
[
1
]
, where frequency balls have radius tuned to
|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
for frequencies of size
|\xi|
.

By Fourier duality, a frequency localization of size
|\Delta\xi|
corresponds to a spatial localization of inversely proportional size, namely

|\Delta x|\sim\frac{1}{|\Delta\xi|}\sim|\xi|^{\alpha-1}.

In particular, the heuristic uncertainty product at this scale is given by

|\Delta x|\,|\Delta\xi|\sim 1,

but distributed in a non-uniform way as a function of
|\xi|
: as
|\xi|
increases we refine the resolution in frequency (
|\Delta\xi|
small), and accordingly we allow larger spatial blocks (
|\Delta x|
large). This redistribution is what makes the subdyadic geometry “aligned” with the uncertainty principle for phases of the form
|\xi|^{\alpha}
.

2.2
Subdyadic metric on the cotangent bundle

The previous discussion suggests introducing an anisotropic metric
g^{\alpha}
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}
of the form

g^{\alpha}_{(x,\xi)}(dx,d\xi)=|\xi|^{-2(\alpha-1)}|dx|^{2}+|\xi|^{-2(1-\alpha)}\,|d\xi|^{2},

where
|dx|
and
|d\xi|
denote Euclidean norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This metric assigns to a variation
d\xi
of size
|d\xi|\sim|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
a cost comparable to that of a variation
dx
of size
|\xi|^{\alpha-1}
, so that the balls of
g^{\alpha}
reflect precisely the dispersive scaling discussed above. See
[
18
, Section 2.2]
for more information on admissible metrics in the sense of Hörmander.

Associated distance and balls.

We define the distance induced by
g^{\alpha}
by

d_{g^{\alpha}}\big((x,\xi),(y,\eta)\big):=\inf_{\gamma}\ \int_{0}^{1}\Big(|\xi(t)|^{-2(\alpha-1)}|x^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+|\xi(t)|^{-2(1-\alpha)}\,|\xi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,dt,

where the infimum is taken over absolutely continuous curves
\gamma(t)=(x(t),\xi(t))

with prescribed endpoints. For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
\rho>0
, the associated ball is

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho):=\{\,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:\ d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho\,\}.

Later, in Lemma
2.2
we show that the metric measure space
\big(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}},d\lambda^{2d}\big)
is
doubling
, where
d\lambda^{2d}
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. For more information on metric measure spaces with the doubling property, see
[
11
]
.

2.3
Subdyadic blocks in phase space

For
w=(x,\xi),z=(y,\eta)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
we set

r(w,z):=1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\},

and

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z):=r(w,z)^{1-\alpha}\,|x-y|\;+\;r(w,z)^{\alpha-1}\,|\xi-\eta|.

(5)

At small scales, the sets

\{z^{\prime}:\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C\rho\}
are comparable (with uniform constants) to the balls
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)
and hence to the rectangular blocks

Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho)=\{(y,\eta):|y-x|\leq C\rho|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\ |\eta-\xi|\leq C\rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\},

as we show in Proposition
2.1
below.

This family of elementary blocks in phase space
(x,\xi)
will serve as “building blocks” for the subsequent subdyadic Gabor decompositions. Note that these blocks are essentially the balls of
g^{\alpha}
, with the difference that we make the two scales explicit:

•

spatial scale:
|x-x_{0}|\sim\rho|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}
;

•

frequency scale:
|\xi-\xi_{0}|\sim\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
.

When we fix
\rho\sim 1
, the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
have spatial size proportional to
|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}
and frequency size proportional to
|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
. By varying
\rho
, we can refine or enlarge the blocks in a way that is uniform with respect to the metric
g^{\alpha}
.

From the point of view of pure frequency decomposition (without the
x
variable), the projection of
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
onto the
\xi
-variable is exactly a subdyadic ball centered at
\xi_{0}
of radius comparable to
|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
. This connects directly with the subdyadic partitions of unity used in the analysis of Miyachi multipliers
[
21
]
.

Comparison with a dyadic reference

In the standard dyadic decomposition by annuli
\{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
and
keeping the metric fixed as in this work
(unit weight in
x
), the reference blocks at scale
\rho
satisfy

\Delta x\sim\rho,\qquad\Delta\xi\sim\rho\,2^{j}.

In this case the dyadic block is defined as

Q_{\mathrm{dy}}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho):=\Big\{(x,\xi):\ |x-x_{0}|\leq C\rho,\ \ |\xi-\xi_{0}|\leq C\rho\,2^{j}\Big\},\qquad|\xi_{0}|\sim 2^{j}.

In contrast, for phases of the form
|\xi|^{\alpha}
(with
\rho=1
), the natural scale of variation is no longer
2^{j}
but
|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
, which in the annulus
|\xi|\sim 2^{j}
translates into

\Delta\xi\sim 2^{j(1-\alpha)}.

This suggests that, at least for accurately describing the oscillatory behavior and almost orthogonality of dispersive operators, it is more efficient to “tilt” the decomposition towards blocks with this subdyadic scaling, rather than using isotropic blocks of size
2^{j}
in frequency. The metric
g^{\alpha}
provides a natural framework to formalize this idea
[
1
]
. We will also make use of Littlewood–Paley type inequalities
[
25
,
22
]
and subdyadic partitions of unity
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Anisotropic distance induced by
g^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
.
With the notation from the previous subsection (distance
d_{g^{\alpha}}
, balls
B_{g^{\alpha}}
, quasi-distance
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
and blocks
Q_{\alpha}
), there exist
c,C>0
(depending only on
d,\alpha
) such that, for every
\rho\in(0,c)
,

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\;\subset\;\{\,z^{\prime}:\,\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C\rho\,\}\;\subset\;B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C\rho).

In particular, for
\rho\leq c
the
g^{\alpha}
-balls are comparable to

Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho):=\big\{(y,\eta):\,|y-x|\leq C\rho|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\ \ |\eta-\xi|\leq C\rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\big\}.

Proof.

Since the constants in the statement depend only on
(d,\alpha)
, it is enough to treat the case when
|\xi|
is sufficiently large; the case when
|\xi|
stays in a compact set reduces to the equivalence between
g^{\alpha}
and the Euclidean metric, and between
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
and
|x-y|+|\xi-\eta|
, with trivial adjustments of the constants.

We therefore fix
z=(x,\xi)
with
|\xi|
large and take
\rho>0
small (to be chosen later). We denote by
d_{g^{\alpha}}
the Riemannian distance induced by
g^{\alpha}
and by
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)
the corresponding open ball.

Step 1: Control of
|\xi_{t}|
along a short path.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\gamma(t)=(x_{t},\xi_{t})
, be a
C^{1}
path joining
z=(x,\xi)
and
z^{\prime}=(y,\eta)
, and assume that

L(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g^{\alpha}_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))}\,dt\;\leq\;2\rho.

From the expression of the metric
g^{\alpha}
we deduce that

\sqrt{g^{\alpha}_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))}\;\gtrsim\;|\xi_{t}|^{\alpha-1}\,|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|.

Consider the function
\varphi(t):=|\xi_{t}|^{\alpha}
. By the chain rule,
\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\alpha|\xi_{t}|^{\alpha-2}\,\xi_{t}\cdot\xi^{\prime}_{t}
, hence

|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|\leq\alpha|\xi_{t}|^{\alpha-1}|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|.

Integrating from
0
to
t
we obtain

\big||\xi_{t}|^{\alpha}-|\xi|^{\alpha}\big|=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\varphi^{\prime}(u)\,du\right|\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{1}|\xi_{u}|^{\alpha-1}|\xi^{\prime}_{u}|\,du\;\lesssim\;L(\gamma)\;\leq\;2\rho.

That is, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\big||\xi_{t}|^{\alpha}-|\xi|^{\alpha}\big|\leq C_{0}\,\rho,\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,1].

(6)

We now choose
\rho\leq c_{0}
, with
c_{0}>0
sufficiently small (depending only on
\alpha,d
) so that, when
|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1
,

C_{0}\,\rho\ \leq\ \tfrac{1}{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}.

Then, from (
6
) we deduce that

\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}\ \leq\ |\xi_{t}|^{\alpha}\ \leq\ \tfrac{3}{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}

for all
t\in[0,1]
, and therefore

c_{1}\,|\xi|\ \leq\ |\xi_{t}|\ \leq\ C_{1}\,|\xi|\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],

(7)

for some constants
c_{1},C_{1}>0
independent of
z
, provided
\rho\leq c_{0}
.
In particular, using also the continuity of
t\mapsto\xi_{t}
, we obtain

c_{1}\,|\xi|\ \leq\ |\eta|\ \leq\ C_{1}\,|\xi|.

From (
7
) it follows that, whenever
d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho
with
\rho\leq c_{0}
,

r(z,z^{\prime})=1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}\;\asymp\;|\xi|,

(8)

with constants independent of
z,z^{\prime}
.

Step 2: First inclusion

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\subset\{\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\leq C\rho\}
.

Let now
z^{\prime}=(y,\eta)
be such that
d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})<\rho
with
\rho\leq c_{0}
.
We take a path
\gamma
as above, with
L(\gamma)\leq 2\rho
. Using again the expression of
g^{\alpha}
and the elementary inequality

\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\gtrsim a+b
for
a,b\geq 0
, we obtain

\sqrt{g^{\alpha}_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))}\;\gtrsim\;|\xi_{t}|^{1-\alpha}|x^{\prime}_{t}|+|\xi_{t}|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|.

Integrating over
t\in[0,1]
,

L(\gamma)\gtrsim\int_{0}^{1}\big(|\xi_{t}|^{1-\alpha}|x^{\prime}_{t}|+|\xi_{t}|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|\big)\,dt.

By (
7
),
|\xi_{t}|\asymp|\xi|
on the whole interval, so that

L(\gamma)\gtrsim|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}|x^{\prime}_{t}|\,dt+|\xi|^{-(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|\,dt\;\geq\;|\xi|^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+|\xi|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi-\eta|.

Therefore,

d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\geq c\,\big(|\xi|^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+|\xi|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi-\eta|\big)

for some constant
c>0
. Using (
8
) and the definition
(
5
), this can be rewritten as

d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\ \gtrsim\ \mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime}).

In particular, if
d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho
with
\rho\leq c_{0}
, we obtain

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\ \leq\ C\,\rho,

for a constant
C
independent of
z
. This proves that

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\;\subset\;\{z^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C\rho\}

when
\rho\leq c_{0}
.

Step 3: Second inclusion

\{\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\leq C\rho\}\subset B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C^{\prime}\rho)
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho
with
\rho
small.
By (
5
),

r(z,z^{\prime})^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+r(z,z^{\prime})^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|\leq\rho.

In the region of interest,
|\xi|,|\eta|
are large and, for
\rho
small,
the previous condition implies once more (after adjusting
c_{0}
if necessary) the
comparability (
7
), so that
r(z,z^{\prime})\asymp|\xi|
.
Substituting, we obtain

|x-y|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\qquad|\xi-\eta|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}.

Consider the linear path

\gamma(t)=(x_{t},\xi_{t}):=\big(x+t(y-x),\ \xi+t(\eta-\xi)\big),\qquad t\in[0,1].

Then
x^{\prime}_{t}=y-x
and
\xi^{\prime}_{t}=\eta-\xi
are constant. From the previous bounds,

|x^{\prime}_{t}|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\qquad|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}.

Moreover, for
t\in[0,1]
,

|\xi_{t}-\xi|\leq t\,|\eta-\xi|\lesssim\rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha},

so, for
\rho\leq c_{0}
sufficiently small, we still have

|\xi_{t}|\asymp|\xi|
on the whole interval. Evaluating the length of
\gamma
,

\displaystyle L(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g^{\alpha}_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\big(|\xi_{t}|^{1-\alpha}|x^{\prime}_{t}|+|\xi_{t}|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi^{\prime}_{t}|\big)\,dt

\displaystyle\asymp|\xi|^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+|\xi|^{-(1-\alpha)}|\xi-\eta|\ \lesssim\ \rho.

Hence
d_{g^{\alpha}}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C^{\prime}\rho
, and we have

\{z^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq\rho\}\ \subset\ B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C^{\prime}\rho).

Adjusting the constants in terms of
C,C^{\prime}
we obtain the second inclusion in the
statement.

Step 4: Comparison with the blocks
Q_{\alpha}
.

Finally, by (
5
) and (
8
),
the condition
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\leq C\rho
is equivalent to

|y-x|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\qquad|\eta-\xi|\ \lesssim\ \rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha},

which describes precisely the blocks

Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho)=\big\{(y,\eta):\,|y-x|\leq C\rho|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\ \ |\eta-\xi|\leq C\rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\big\},

up to constants and for
\rho\leq c_{0}
sufficiently small. Combining this
with the previous inclusions we conclude that, for
\rho\leq c
(for a suitable

c>0
),

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\;\subset\;Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;C\rho)\;\subset\;B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C^{\prime}\rho),

which proves the proposition.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Anisotropic volume and doubling property)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and

Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho):=\big\{(y,\eta):\ |y-x_{0}|\leq C\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1},\ \ |\eta-\xi_{0}|\leq C\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\big\},

with
|\xi_{0}|\geq 1
and
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
, where
\rho_{0}>0
is sufficiently
small and
C>0
is a fixed constant. Then

\operatorname{vol}\big(Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho)\big)\;\asymp\;\rho^{2d},

(9)

with universal constants (independent of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
and
\rho
).

In particular, the metric measure space

\big(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}},dx\,d\xi\big)
is
doubling
at small
scales: there exists
C_{\mathrm{dbl}}=C_{\mathrm{dbl}}(d,\alpha)
such that

\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2\rho)\big)\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{dbl}}\ \operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\big),\qquad\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}],\ z\in\{(x,\xi):|\xi|\geq 1\}.

(10)

Proof.

By definition,
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho)
is a rectangular block with side lengths

\text{in the $x$ variable:}\quad\sim\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1},\qquad\text{in the $\xi$ variable:}\quad\sim\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}.

Since the reference measure is simply
dx\,d\xi
, we obtain

\operatorname{vol}\big(Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho)\big)\asymp(\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1})^{d}\,(\rho\,|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha})^{d}=\rho^{2d},

which proves (
9
). Note that the factor
|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}

is compensated by
|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
, so that the volume is independent of
|\xi_{0}|
.

For the doubling property, we use Proposition
2.1
,
which identifies the
d_{g^{\alpha}}
-balls with the blocks
Q_{\alpha}
. More precisely, there exist
c,C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for every
z=(x_{0},\xi_{0})
with

|\xi_{0}|\geq 1
and every
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
,

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,c\rho)\ \subset\ Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};\rho)\ \subset\ B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C_{1}\rho),

(11)

and similarly

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\ \subset\ Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};C_{2}\rho)\ \subset\ B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C_{3}\rho),

after adjusting the constants.

Then, using (
9
) and (
11
),

\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2\rho)\big)\ \leq\ \operatorname{vol}\big(Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};C_{4}\rho)\big)\ \asymp\ (C_{4}\rho)^{2d}\ \lesssim\ \rho^{2d},

while

\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\big)\ \gtrsim\ \operatorname{vol}\big(Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};c_{1}\rho)\big)\ \asymp\ (c_{1}\rho)^{2d}\ \gtrsim\ \rho^{2d}.

Comparing these estimates, we obtain

\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2\rho)\big)\ \leq\ C_{\mathrm{dbl}}\,\operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\big),

with
C_{\mathrm{dbl}}
depending only on
d
and
\alpha
. This proves
(
10
) and completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Covering with finite overlap)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and fix
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
, with
\rho_{0}>0
sufficiently small. There exists a lattice

\Omega_{\alpha}:=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N},\,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})

such that, with
Q_{\alpha}
defined by

Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho):=\big\{(y,\eta):\,|y-x|\leq C\rho|\xi|^{\alpha-1},\ \ |\eta-\xi|\leq C\rho\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\big\},

the following properties hold:

(1)

(Covering)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{(j,k)}Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};\rho)\;=\;\{(x,\xi):\,|\xi|\geq 1\}
.

(2)

(Separation)
There exists
c_{1}\in(0,1)
such that the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};c_{1}\rho)
are pairwise disjoint.

(3)

(Finite overlap)
There exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(d,\alpha)
such that every
(x,\xi)
belongs to at most
N_{0}
blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C\rho)
.

Moreover, the construction can be carried out by frequency coronas
|\xi|\sim 2^{k}
by choosing:

(i)

a spatial grid with step
\asymp\rho\,2^{k(\alpha-1)}
, and

(ii)

frequency centers
\xi_{k}
with step
\asymp\rho\,2^{k(1-\alpha)}
.

With this choice, the constants
c_{1},C,N_{0}
are independent of
k
and
\rho
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, for

\rho>0
sufficiently small there exist constants
c,C>0
such that, for
every
z=(x,\xi)
with
|\xi|\geq 1
,

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,c\rho)\;\subset\;Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho)\;\subset\;B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C\rho),

(12)

and conversely

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho)\;\subset\;Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;C\rho)\;\subset\;B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,C^{\prime}\rho),

after adjusting the constants.

Step 1: Covering and separation in terms of balls.

The metric space
(\{|\xi|\geq 1\},d_{g^{\alpha}})
is
doubling
(cf. Lemma
2.2
), with effective dimension
2d
: there exist constants

C_{\mathrm{dbl}},R_{0}
such that

\mathrm{vol}\,B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2R)\ \leq\ C_{\mathrm{dbl}}\,\mathrm{vol}\,B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,R),\qquad 0<R\leq R_{0},\ |\xi|\geq 1.

Applying the Vitali/Besicovitch covering lemma (cf.
[
5
, Section 1.5]
) to the family of balls

\{B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,\rho):|\xi|\geq 1\}
, we obtain a discrete subset of
centers
\Omega_{\alpha}=\{z_{j,k}\}
such that

•

the balls
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},c_{0}\rho)
are pairwise disjoint for some

c_{0}\in(0,1)
,

•

\displaystyle\bigcup_{(j,k)}B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}\rho)=\{(x,\xi):|\xi|\geq 1\}
for some
C_{0}>1
,

•

the multiplicity of the covering by balls
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}\rho)
is
uniformly bounded by a constant
N_{0}
depending only on the
effective dimension (in our case
2d
), that is, on
d
and
\alpha
.

Step 2: Transfer to the blocks
Q_{\alpha}
.

We write
z_{j,k}=(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})
. From (
12
) we deduce that

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},c_{0}\rho)\subset Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};\rho)\subset B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}^{\prime}\rho)

for some constants
c_{0}^{\prime},C_{0}^{\prime}
comparable to
c_{0},C_{0}
. Adjusting
c_{1}
and
C
in
the statement we can ensure:

(1)

Covering:

\{(x,\xi):|\xi|\geq 1\}=\bigcup_{(j,k)}B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}\rho)\subset\bigcup_{(j,k)}Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C\rho),

and, decreasing
C
if necessary, also

\bigcup_{(j,k)}Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};\rho)=\{|\xi|\geq 1\}
.

(2)

Separation:

If
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};c_{1}\rho)
and
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}};c_{1}\rho)

intersected for
c_{1}
sufficiently small, then

B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},c_{0}\rho)
and
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}},c_{0}\rho)
would also
intersect, contradicting the disjointness obtained in the previous step. Hence
the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};c_{1}\rho)
are pairwise disjoint.

(3)

Finite overlap:

If a point
(x,\xi)
belongs to
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C\rho)
, then

(x,\xi)\in B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}^{\prime\prime}\rho)
for some
C_{0}^{\prime\prime}
. The number of
blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C\rho)
that may contain
(x,\xi)
is bounded
by the number of balls
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{j,k},C_{0}^{\prime\prime}\rho)
containing

(x,\xi)
, which is
\leq N_{0}
by the finite multiplicity property. It
follows that property (3) holds.

Step 3: Explicit realization via frequency coronas.

Finally, we describe a concrete construction by coronas. For

k\in\mathbb{N}
consider the frequency corona

\Gamma_{k}:=\{(x,\xi):\ 2^{k}\leq|\xi|<2^{k+1}\}.

In this region, the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;\rho)
have radii

\text{in }x:\ \asymp\rho\,2^{k(\alpha-1)},\qquad\text{in }\xi:\ \asymp\rho\,2^{k(1-\alpha)},

by the definition of
Q_{\alpha}
and the fact that
|\xi|\sim 2^{k}
. Fixing a spatial grid
in
x
with step
\asymp\rho\,2^{k(\alpha-1)}
and a frequency grid in
\xi

with step
\asymp\rho\,2^{k(1-\alpha)}
, we obtain a family of centers

(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})
such that:

•

the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};c_{1}\rho)
are pairwise disjoint,

•

the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C\rho)
cover
\Gamma_{k}
,

•

each point of
\Gamma_{k}
belongs to at most
N_{0}
of them,

with constants
c_{1},C,N_{0}
independent of
k
and
\rho
. This is
consistent with the abstract construction via
g^{\alpha}
-balls and completes the
proof.
∎

3
Subdyadic Gabor lattice and dispersive STFT

3.1
Construction of a subdyadic lattice in phase space

Fix
\alpha>0
, and consider a family
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{k}\}_{k}
of
\alpha
–subdyadic balls in frequency covering
\{\xi:|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
with bounded overlap (cf.
[
1
]
). For each
B_{k}
we choose a center
\xi_{k}\in B_{k}
. The natural frequency scale associated with
\xi_{k}
is

r_{k}:=|\xi_{k}|^{\,1-\alpha},

so that, by the metric
g^{\alpha}
and Proposition
2.1
, the block
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi_{k};1)
has frequency size
\sim r_{k}
and
spatial size

\sim r_{k}^{-1}=|\xi_{k}|^{\alpha-1}
(cf. Section
2
).

We now apply Lemma
2.3
with
\rho=1
to construct a lattice in phase space: there exists a discrete set

\Omega_{\alpha}:=\{z_{j,k}:=(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}):k\in\mathbb{N},\ j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})

such that:

•

each frequency corona
\{(x,\xi):|\xi|\sim 2^{k}\}
is covered by blocks

Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
with

\text{spatial step}\ \asymp r_{k}^{-1}\asymp 2^{k(\alpha-1)},\qquad\text{frequency step}\ \asymp r_{k}\asymp 2^{k(1-\alpha)},

•

the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};c_{1})
are pairwise disjoint for some
c_{1}\in(0,1)
,

•

every
(x,\xi)
with
|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1
belongs to at most
N_{0}
blocks

Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};C)
, with
N_{0}
independent of
k
.

In particular, by construction and by Lemma
2.3
, every
(x,\xi)
with

|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1
belongs to a uniformly bounded number (independent of
k
) of blocks

Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
, and the overlap constants are uniform across the coronas.

3.2
Dispersive wave packets and adapted Gabor transform

We fix a nonzero window function
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, real-valued and
L^{2}
-normalized, whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
is compactly supported in an annulus away from the origin (this ensures that the modulations we introduce do not interfere with the zero-frequency component). For each point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
we define the
dispersive wave packet

\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}(t)=|\xi_{0}|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\,\phi\bigl((t-x_{0})\,|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)\,e^{\,i\,t\cdot\xi_{0}},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The normalization factor
|\xi_{0}|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}
is chosen so that

\|\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
is uniform in
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
, that is,

\|\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
. Note that
\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}
is essentially concentrated in a spatial neighborhood of
x_{0}
of radius
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}
, in accordance with the spatial component of
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
.

For brevity, we sometimes write
\phi_{z}
to denote
\phi_{x,\xi}
, with
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Frequency localization of the packets)
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\phi}
is supported in a compact set

K\subset\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:R_{1}\leq|\zeta|\leq R_{2}\}
for some

0<R_{1}<R_{2}<\infty
. Then, for each
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
we have

\widehat{\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}}(\eta)=|\xi_{0}|^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\,e^{-ix_{0}\cdot(\eta-\xi_{0})}\,\widehat{\phi}\Big(\frac{\eta-\xi_{0}}{|\xi_{0}|^{\,1-\alpha}}\Big),

(13)

and, in particular,

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}}\ \subset\ \Big\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ R_{1}|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\leq|\eta-\xi_{0}|\leq R_{2}|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\Big\}.

In other words, the effective support of
\widehat{\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}}
is contained
in an annulus of radius
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
around
\xi_{0}
, consistent with
the frequency component of the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
.

Proof.

By the definition of the Fourier transform,

\widehat{\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}}(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}(t)\,e^{-it\cdot\eta}\,dt=|\xi_{0}|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\bigl((t-x_{0})|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)\,e^{it\cdot(\xi_{0}-\eta)}\,dt.

We perform the change of variables

s=(t-x_{0})|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t=x_{0}+|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}s,\qquad dt=|\xi_{0}|^{(\alpha-1)d}\,ds,

and obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=|\xi_{0}|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(s)\,e^{i(x_{0}+|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}s)\cdot(\xi_{0}-\eta)}\,|\xi_{0}|^{(\alpha-1)d}\,ds

\displaystyle=|\xi_{0}|^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\,e^{ix_{0}\cdot(\xi_{0}-\eta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(s)\,e^{is\cdot|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}(\xi_{0}-\eta)}\,ds.

Recognizing the Fourier transform of
\phi
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(s)\,e^{is\cdot|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}(\xi_{0}-\eta)}\,ds=\widehat{\phi}\big(|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}(\xi_{0}-\eta)\big)=\widehat{\phi}\Big(\frac{\eta-\xi_{0}}{|\xi_{0}|^{\,1-\alpha}}\Big),

where in the last equality we use that
\phi
is real-valued. This proves (
13
).

If
\widehat{\phi}
is supported in
K
, then

\widehat{\phi}\Big(\frac{\eta-\xi_{0}}{|\xi_{0}|^{\,1-\alpha}}\Big)\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{\eta-\xi_{0}}{|\xi_{0}|^{\,1-\alpha}}\in K.

In particular,

R_{1}\leq\Big|\frac{\eta-\xi_{0}}{|\xi_{0}|^{\,1-\alpha}}\Big|\leq R_{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_{1}|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}\leq|\eta-\xi_{0}|\leq R_{2}|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha},

which yields the claimed support inclusion.
∎

Thus, the packets
\phi_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}
are simultaneously
concentrated in a spatial neighborhood of
x_{0}
of radius
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-1}
and in
a frequency neighborhood of
\xi_{0}
of radius
\sim|\xi_{0}|^{1-\alpha}
, precisely at
the scale of the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{0},\xi_{0};1)
.

The
dispersive Gabor transform
(or subdyadic STFT) of a function

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the window
\phi
is defined by

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi):=\;\langle f,\phi_{x,\xi}\rangle\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\,\overline{\phi_{x,\xi}(t)}\,dt,\qquad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}).

The function
V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)
measures the correlation of
f
with wave packets
localized in the region
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
.

Definition 3.2

(Separation and relative separation)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space (in our setting
X=\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with either
d=d_{g^{\alpha}}
or
d=\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
).

•

\Lambda\subset X
is
\delta
–separated
if
\inf_{\lambda\neq\mu\in\Lambda}d(\lambda,\mu)\geq\delta>0
.

•

\Lambda
is
relatively separated
if there exists
r_{0}>0
such that

N_{r_{0}}(\Lambda):=\sup_{z\in X}\#\big(\Lambda\cap B(z,r_{0})\big)<\infty.

In
doubling
spaces, this is equivalent (up to constants) to
\Lambda
being a finite union of
r_{0}
–separated sets; see, for example,
[
10
, Chapter 11]
and also
[
4
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Relative separation of the lattice)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
. The lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
constructed in Lemma
2.3

is relatively separated in
(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
. More precisely, there exist

r_{0}(\asymp\rho)
and
N_{0}=N_{0}(d,\alpha)
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\#\big(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,r_{0})\big)\ \leq\ N_{0}.

Proof.

By the
separation
property in Lemma
2.3
, the blocks

Q_{\alpha}(\cdot;c_{1}\rho)
are disjoint; by Proposition
2.1
,

Q_{\alpha}(\cdot;c_{1}\rho)\supset B_{g^{\alpha}}(\cdot,c^{\prime}\rho)
for some
c^{\prime}>0
.
Hence distinct centers in
\Omega_{\alpha}
must satisfy

d_{g^{\alpha}}\geq c^{\prime}\rho
: that is,
\Omega_{\alpha}
is
\delta
–separated with
\delta=c^{\prime}\rho
.

For
r_{0}=C\rho
(with
C
a sufficiently large fixed constant), the balls

B_{g^{\alpha}}(\lambda,c^{\prime}\rho/2)
,
\lambda\in\Omega_{\alpha}\cap B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,r_{0})
,
are disjoint and are contained in
B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,r_{0}+c^{\prime}\rho/2)\subset B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2r_{0})
.
By Lemma
2.2
(volume and
doubling
),

\#\big(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,r_{0})\big)\ \cdot\ \operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(\cdot,c^{\prime}\rho/2)\big)\ \leq\ \operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(z,2r_{0})\big)\ \lesssim\ \operatorname{vol}\big(B_{g^{\alpha}}(\cdot,r_{0})\big),

and the ratio of volumes is uniformly bounded (in
z
and
\rho
) by the
doubling
property.
This yields the bound
N_{0}=N_{0}(d,\alpha)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Wiener–Jaffard for quasi-diagonal matrices)
.

Suppose that
G:=(G_{wz})_{w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
satisfies, for some
N>2d
,

|G_{wz}|\;\leq\;C_{N}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\big)^{-N}\qquad(\forall\,w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}).

Then
G:\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
is bounded. Moreover, if
G
is invertible on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
(or is bounded below on its range), then its inverse
G^{-1}=(H_{wz})
satisfies

|H_{wz}|\;\leq\;C^{\prime}_{M}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\big)^{-M}

for some
M<N
(in particular, for every
M<N-2d
).

Proof.

Set
d(w,z):=\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
and write
K_{wz}:=(1+d(w,z))^{-N}
.
By Lemma
3.3
the lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
is relatively separated in

(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
, and by Lemma
2.2
the space is
doubling

at small scales. Since the quasi-distance
d=\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
is equivalent to
d_{g^{\alpha}}

(cf. Proposition
2.1
), we obtain the neighbor counting estimate

\#\big(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap B_{g^{\alpha}}(z_{0},R)\big)\ \lesssim\ (1+R)^{2d}\qquad(R\geq 1),

(14)

uniformly in
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. In particular, for annuli of thickness
1
,

\#\big\{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}:\ n\leq d(w,z)<n+1\big\}\ \leq\ C\big((n+1)^{2d}-n^{2d}\big)\ \lesssim\ (1+n)^{2d-1}.

(15)

Step 1:
\ell^{2}
boundedness (Schur test).

Fix
w\in\Omega_{\alpha}
. Decomposing into annuli
A_{n}(w):=\{z:\ n\leq d(w,z)<n+1\}
and using (
15
),

\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}K_{wz}\;=\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{z\in A_{n}(w)}(1+d(w,z))^{-N}\;\leq\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+n)^{-N}\,\#A_{n}(w)\;\lesssim\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+n)^{2d-1-N}.

Since
N>2d
, the series converges and the supremum over
w
is uniformly bounded:

S_{1}:=\sup_{w}\sum_{z}K_{wz}\ <\ \infty.

The same argument (by symmetry of
d
) yields

S_{2}:=\sup_{z}\sum_{w}K_{wz}\ <\ \infty.

If
|G_{wz}|\leq C_{N}K_{wz}
, Schur’s test implies that

\|G\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\ \leq\ C_{N}\,\sqrt{S_{1}S_{2}}\ <\ \infty,

and therefore
G
is bounded on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
.

Step 2: Inverse-closedness and decay of the inverse.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{s}
denote the class of matrices with polynomial off-diagonal decay

\mathcal{A}_{s}:=\Big\{A=(A_{wz}):\ \|A\|_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}:=\sup_{w}\sum_{z}|A_{wz}|(1+d(w,z))^{s}+\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|A_{wz}|(1+d(w,z))^{s}<\infty\Big\}.

Step 1 and (
15
) show that
G\in\mathcal{A}_{N-2d+\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
The key fact (Wiener–Jaffard) is that, on discrete relatively separated sets over a doubling background,
the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is
inverse-closed
in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})
: if
G\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
and
G
is invertible on
\ell^{2}
,
then
G^{-1}\in\mathcal{A}_{s^{\prime}}
for every
s^{\prime}<s
(in particular, for every
s^{\prime}<N-2d
). See
[
10
, Chapter 9]
and Appendix
A
.

Applying this with
s=N-\varepsilon
and using that
N>2d
, we obtain that
G^{-1}=(H_{wz})
satisfies

|H_{wz}|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{M}\,(1+d(w,z))^{-M}

for some
M<N
(in fact, for every
M<N-2d
), which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Subdyadic Gabor frame)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
\Omega_{\alpha}
be the lattice from Lemma
2.3
. Choose a family of subdyadic balls
\{B_{k}\}
in frequency with finite overlap and centers
\{\xi_{k}\}
, and a window

\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, nonzero, real-valued, with
\widehat{\phi}
supported in an annulus away from the origin and normalized in
L^{2}
.

Define the dispersive packets by

\psi_{x,\xi}(t)=|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\,\phi\bigl((t-x)\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)\,e^{it\cdot\xi}.

Then the system

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)\;:=\;\{\psi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}:\ (x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}

is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
: there exist constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
, depending only on
(d,\alpha)
, on
\phi
, and on the overlap constants of
\{B_{k}\}
and
\Omega_{\alpha}
, such that

A\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ \leq\ \sum_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big|\langle f,\psi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle\big|^{2}\ \leq\ B\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover, there exists a dual family
\{\widetilde{\psi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}
with the same localization blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
which provides the reconstruction formula

f=\sum_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle f,\psi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle\,\widetilde{\psi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\quad\text{with convergence in }L^{2}.

Proof.

We write
\Omega_{\alpha}=\{w=(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\}
and
\psi_{w}:=\psi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}
(windows as defined in the previous subsection).
Let
C:\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
be the analysis operator
Cf=(\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle)_{w}
, and let
S:=C^{\ast}C
be the frame operator

Sf=\sum_{w\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle\,\psi_{w},\qquad\langle Sf,f\rangle=\sum_{w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle\,\overline{\langle f,\psi_{z}\rangle}\,\langle\psi_{z},\psi_{w}\rangle.

We denote by
G=(G_{wz})_{w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
the
Gramian
,
G_{wz}=\langle\psi_{w},\psi_{z}\rangle
.

Step 1: Off–diagonal decay of the Gramian (integration by parts).

For
w=(x,\xi)
and
z=(y,\eta)
, with
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
the quasi-distance from Section
2
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(d,\alpha)
such that, for every
N\geq N_{0}
,

|G_{wz}|\;\leq\;C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N}.

(16)

We fix
N>2d
so that the series arising in Schur’s test converge; the constant
C_{N}
depends on
(d,\alpha)
and on a finite number of seminorms of the window, but not on the indices
(w,z)
.

Indeed:
Write
\theta:=\xi-\eta
, so that

G_{wz}=\langle\psi_{x,\xi},\psi_{y,\eta}\rangle=|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}|\eta|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{wz}(t)\,e^{it\cdot\theta}\,dt,

where

a_{wz}(t):=\phi\bigl((t-x)|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)\,\overline{\phi\bigl((t-y)|\eta|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)}.

Observe that
a_{wz}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that, for each multi-index
\gamma
,

\|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}a_{wz}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}}\ \lesssim_{\gamma}\ \sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma}|\xi|^{(1-\alpha)|\gamma_{1}|}|\eta|^{(1-\alpha)|\gamma_{2}|}\lesssim_{\gamma}r^{(1-\alpha)|\gamma|},

with constants independent of
(x,y,\xi,\eta)
(since
\phi
is a fixed Schwartz function and
|\xi|,|\eta|\sim r
in the dispersive region).

Case (i): essentially frequency separation
, that is,

r^{\alpha-1}|\theta|\ \gtrsim\ 1.

We define the integration-by-parts operator

L:=\frac{1}{1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}}\,\bigl(1-r^{2(\alpha-1)}\Delta_{t}\bigr),

so that, using
\Delta_{t}e^{it\cdot\theta}=-|\theta|^{2}e^{it\cdot\theta}
,

L\big(e^{it\cdot\theta}\big)=\frac{1}{1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}}\bigl(1-r^{2(\alpha-1)}\Delta_{t}\bigr)e^{it\cdot\theta}=\frac{1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}}{1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}}\,e^{it\cdot\theta}=e^{it\cdot\theta}.

Hence,

G_{wz}=|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}|\eta|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a_{wz}(t)\,L^{M}\big(e^{it\cdot\theta}\big)\,dt=|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}|\eta|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L^{*M}\big(a_{wz}(t)\big)\,e^{it\cdot\theta}\,dt,

where
L^{\ast}
is the formal adjoint of
L
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From the definition of
L
,

L^{\ast}=\frac{1}{1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}}\,\bigl(1-r^{2(\alpha-1)}\Delta_{t}\bigr),

so each application of
L^{\ast}
produces a prefactor
(1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2})^{-1}
and a linear combination of derivatives of
a_{wz}
of order up to
2M
, with coefficients of size
\lesssim r^{2M(\alpha-1)}
.

By the bound on the derivatives of
a_{wz}
,

\big|L^{*M}(a_{wz})(t)\big|\ \lesssim_{M}\ \frac{r^{2M(\alpha-1)}\,r^{2M(1-\alpha)}}{\bigl(1+r^{2(\alpha-1)}|\theta|^{2}\bigr)^{M}}\,(1+|t-x|)^{-M^{\prime}}\ \lesssim_{M}\ \bigl(1+r^{\alpha-1}|\theta|\bigr)^{-2M},

for some large
M^{\prime}
(we used that
r^{2M(\alpha-1)}r^{2M(1-\alpha)}=1
, and that rapid decay in
t
comes from the smoothness and decay of
\phi
). Integrating in
t
and absorbing polynomial factors into a new constant
C_{M}
, we obtain

|G_{wz}|\ \lesssim_{M}\ \bigl(1+r^{\alpha-1}|\theta|\bigr)^{-2M}\ \leq\ C_{M}\,\bigl(1+r^{\alpha-1}|\theta|\bigr)^{-M},

which yields frequency decay of the form

|G_{wz}|\ \lesssim_{N}\ (1+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|)^{-N},\qquad\text{for every }N\leq M.

Case (ii): essentially spatial separation
, that is,

r^{\alpha-1}|\theta|\ \lesssim\ 1,\qquad r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|\ \gg\ 1.

In this regime we do not need to integrate by parts in the phase: the decay comes from the spatial part of the product of windows. Using the rescaling

u=(t-x)\,|\xi|^{1-\alpha},\qquad v=(t-y)\,|\eta|^{1-\alpha},

one sees that
a_{wz}(t)
is a product of translates of
\phi
at scales
|\xi|^{\alpha-1}
and
|\eta|^{\alpha-1}
, so that

|a_{wz}(t)|\ \lesssim_{M}\ \bigl(1+|\xi|^{1-\alpha}|t-x|\bigr)^{-M}\,\bigl(1+|\eta|^{1-\alpha}|t-y|\bigr)^{-M}.

If
r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|\gg 1
, at least one of these two factors is large on average; a standard convolution argument for rapidly decaying functions yields, for
M
large,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|a_{wz}(t)|\,dt\ \lesssim_{M}\ \bigl(1+r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|\bigr)^{-M},

whence

|G_{wz}|\ \lesssim_{M}\ \bigl(1+r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|\bigr)^{-M}.

Combining the two cases and using

1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\;\asymp\;1+r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|,

we obtain (
16
) for arbitrary
N
(taking
M
sufficiently large).

Step 2: Upper frame bound (Bessel property).

By Lemma
3.3
the lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
is relatively separated, and by Lemma
2.2
the space
(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
is
doubling
. The neighbor counting estimate

\#\{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}:\ n\leq\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)<n+1\}\lesssim(1+n)^{2d-1},

(cf. (
15
)) together with (
16
) and
N>2d
yields

\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|G_{wz}|\ \lesssim\ \sum_{n\geq 0}(1+n)^{2d-1-N}\ <\ \infty.

By Schur’s test (on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
),
G
defines a bounded operator and, in particular,

\sum_{w}|\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle|^{2}=\langle G\,Cf,Cf\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\ \leq\ \|G\|\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which gives the upper frame bound with
B=\|G\|
.

Step 3: Lower frame bound (coercivity).

By construction of the dispersive packets, the normalization
|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}
guarantees that

\|\psi_{w}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}=1,\qquad w\in\Omega_{\alpha},

so
G_{ww}=1
for all
w
. Define the “remainder” operator
R
by

R_{wz}:=\begin{cases}G_{wz},&w\neq z,\\
0,&w=z.\end{cases}

Thus
G=I+R
, where
I
is the identity matrix on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
.

By (
16
) and the neighbor counting (cf. (
15
)), we obtain

\sup_{w}\sum_{z}|R_{wz}|\ =\ \sup_{w}\sum_{z\neq w}|G_{wz}|\ \leq\ C_{\ast},\qquad\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|R_{wz}|\ \leq\ C_{\ast},

for some finite constant
C_{\ast}
(depending on
(d,\alpha)
, on
\phi
, and on the geometric constants of
\Omega_{\alpha}
). Applying Schur’s test to
R
gives

\|R\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\ \leq\ C_{\ast}.

We can choose the geometric parameters in the construction of
\Omega_{\alpha}
(Lemma
2.3
, in particular the separation in the quasi-distance
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
) so that
C_{\ast}<1
, since increasing the separation of the lattice multiplies
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
for
w\neq z
and reduces the off-diagonal sums (cf. Step 1). Fixing such a lattice, we obtain

\|R\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\ \leq\ \varepsilon\quad\text{with }\varepsilon<1.

Then, for every
c\in\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
,

\displaystyle\langle Gc,c\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle(I+R)c,c\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\|c\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\langle Rc,c\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|c\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}-\|R\|\,\|c\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\ \geq\ (1-\varepsilon)\,\|c\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Applying this with
c=Cf
we deduce

\sum_{w}|\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle|^{2}\ =\ \langle G\,Cf,Cf\rangle\ \geq\ (1-\varepsilon)\,\|Cf\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

To link
\|Cf\|_{\ell^{2}}
with
\|f\|_{L^{2}}
we note that
C
is injective: if
Cf=0
, then
Sf=0
, and using Plancherel together with the frequency partition
\{B_{k}\}
with finite overlap and the nontriviality of
\phi
(its transform meets each corona after rescaling), one obtains
f=0
. Hence
S=C^{\ast}C
is coercive on
L^{2}
, and there exists
A>0
such that

\sum_{w}|\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle|^{2}\ \geq\ A\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, from parts 2) and 3) we conclude that

A\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ \leq\ \sum_{w\in\Omega_{\alpha}}|\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle|^{2}\ \leq\ B\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and we also obtain the existence of a dual family
\{\widetilde{\psi}_{w}\}
with reconstruction in
L^{2}
, which proves that
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

4
Dispersive modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}

In this section we introduce modulation spaces adapted to the subdyadic geometry and to the dispersive Gabor transform constructed in the previous section. The parameter
\alpha>0
fixes the metric
g^{\alpha}
in phase space, while
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
controls the radial weight in frequency.

Recall that, for a fixed window
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the dispersive STFT of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
was defined by

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)=\langle f,\phi_{x,\xi}\rangle,\qquad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

where the packets
\phi_{x,\xi}
are localized in blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
for the metric
g^{\alpha}
. We also fix a subdyadic Gabor lattice

\Omega_{\alpha}=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}):\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\},

such that the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
cover
\{(x,\xi):|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
with finite overlap and the family

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)=\{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}:(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\in\Omega_{\alpha}\}

forms a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(Proposition
3.5
).

Low frequencies.

To complete the coverage of
\{|\xi|<1\}
we add a finite family of blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
with
|\xi|\leq 2
and a corresponding collection of centers with frequency step
O(1)
and spatial mesh
O(1)
. This finite modification does not affect the frame bounds nor the norms
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
(uniform equivalence).

4.1
Discrete definition via Gabor coefficients

For each
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the dispersive Gabor coefficients

c_{j,k}(f)=\langle f,\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle,\qquad(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}.

We introduce the radial weight

w_{\beta}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}.

Definition 4.1

(Dispersive modulation spaces)
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
as the set of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}:=\left(\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|c_{j,k}(f)|^{p}\Big)^{\!q/p}\,w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})^{q}\right)^{\!1/q}<\infty,

with the usual modifications when
p
or
q
is infinite (replacing sums by suprema).

By construction,
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
is a Banach space whenever
p,q\geq 1
. The anisotropy of
g^{\alpha}
is reflected in the fact that the cells
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
have radii in
x
and
\xi
that depend on
|\xi_{k}|
(of order
|\xi_{k}|^{\alpha-1}
and
|\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha}
, respectively), although their volume is comparable to
1
when
\rho=1
(Lemma
2.2
).

Remark 4.2
.

Weighted modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, defined from the STFT with a moderate weight
\mu(x,\xi)
in phase space, already form a standard family in time–frequency analysis; see, for instance,
[
10
, Chapter 11]
and
[
4
]
. In our case, the norm of
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
corresponds to a particular situation in which the weight depends only on frequency,

\mu(x,\xi)=w_{\beta}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|)^{\beta},

while the geometric part in phase space is encoded through the anisotropic metric
g^{\alpha}
and the subdyadic lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
.

Thus, the spaces
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
may be viewed as a
dispersive
, anisotropic version of the classical weighted modulation spaces: the radial weight
w_{\beta}
follows the philosophy of
[
4
]
, but the phase-space localization is adapted to the natural subdyadic scale for phases of the form
|\xi|^{\alpha}
via the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
.

4.2
Independence of the window and the lattice

A priori, the above definition depends on the choice of window
\phi
and lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
. We will show that, within a reasonable class of windows and lattices adapted to
g^{\alpha}
, the norm
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
is well defined up to equivalence.

Definition 4.3

(
\alpha
-admissible windows and lattices)
.

We say that
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
\alpha
-admissible if:

•

\widehat{\phi}
is supported in an annulus
\{c_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq c_{2}\}
,
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<\infty
;

•

the packets
\phi_{x,\xi}
associated with
\phi
are uniformly localized in
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
;

•

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
over some lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
adapted to
g^{\alpha}
.

A lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
is called
\alpha
-admissible if the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k};1)
have finite overlap and cover
\{(x,\xi):|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
in a uniform way.

4.3
Existence of
\alpha
–admissible pairs and norm equivalence

Proposition 4.4

(Existence of
\alpha
–admissible pairs)
.

For every
\alpha>0
there exist a family of subdyadic balls
\{B_{k}\}
with finite overlap, centers
\{\xi_{k}\}
, and a nonzero window
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\widehat{\phi}
supported in an annulus
\{c_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq c_{2}\}
, such that:

1.

The packets
\phi_{x,\xi}
are uniformly localized in
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
.

2.

The lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\}
constructed in Lemma
2.3
is relatively separated and
(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
is doubling.

3.

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)=\{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The subdyadic partition with finite overlap is obtained by frequency coronas

|\xi|\sim 2^{k}
of thickness
\asymp 2^{k(1-\alpha)}
(cf.
[
1
]
),
which yields a family of
\alpha
–subdyadic balls
\{B_{k}\}
in
frequency with finite overlap and centers
\xi_{k}\in B_{k}
.

If
\widehat{\phi}
is supported in a fixed annulus
\{c_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq c_{2}\}
,
then, by the definition of the dispersive packets

\phi_{x,\xi}(t)=|\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}(1-\alpha)}\,\phi\bigl((t-x)|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\bigr)e^{it\cdot\xi},

the Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi_{x,\xi}}
is contained in a ball
of radius
\asymp|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
around
\xi
by Lemma
3.1
, and
\phi_{x,\xi}
is essentially
concentrated in a neighborhood of
x
of radius

\asymp|\xi|^{\alpha-1}
. This shows that
\phi_{x,\xi}
is uniformly
localized in
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
(in the sense of rapid decay outside
that block), proving item (1).

The geometric properties of the lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\}

(covering, separation and relative separation) are contained in
Lemmas
2.3
and
3.3
, while the frame
property of the family
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)=\{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}

was established in Proposition
3.5
. This gives (2) and (3) and
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Moderateness of the radial weight)
.

Let
w_{\beta}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}
. There exists
C_{\beta}\geq 1
such that if

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\big((x,\xi),(y,\eta)\big)\leq 1,

then

C_{\beta}^{-1}\,w_{\beta}(\xi)\ \leq\ w_{\beta}(\eta)\ \leq\ C_{\beta}\,w_{\beta}(\xi).

Proof.

Recall that the quasi-distance is defined (for
|\xi|,|\eta|\geq 1
) by

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\big((x,\xi),(y,\eta)\big)\asymp r^{1-\alpha}\,|x-y|+r^{\alpha-1}\,|\xi-\eta|,\qquad r:=1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\},

and that in low frequency (
|\xi|,|\eta|\lesssim 1
) it is patched with the Euclidean
distance
|x-y|+|\xi-\eta|
(cf. Definition
5
).

If
|\xi|,|\eta|\leq 2
, the condition
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\leq 1
implies

|\xi-\eta|\lesssim 1
, hence
|\xi|
and
|\eta|
are bounded and comparable with
universal constants; in particular
w_{\beta}(\xi)\asymp w_{\beta}(\eta)
, and the
lemma is trivial after adjusting
C_{\beta}
.

Assume now that
\max\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}\geq 2
and, without loss of generality,

|\xi|\leq|\eta|
. If
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}\leq 1
, then

r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|\ \lesssim\ 1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\xi-\eta|\ \lesssim\ r^{1-\alpha}\ \lesssim\ (1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}.

In particular,

\big||\eta|-|\xi|\big|\ \leq\ |\eta-\xi|\ \lesssim\ (1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}.

(17)

We consider separately the cases
\alpha\geq 1
and
\alpha<1
.

Case
\alpha\geq 1
.
Then
1-\alpha\leq 0
and

(1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}\lesssim 1
. From (
17
),

|\eta|\ \leq\ |\xi|+C

for some constant
C
independent of
(x,\xi),(y,\eta)
. If
|\xi|\geq 2C
,
we obtain
|\eta|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}|\xi|
. If
|\xi|<2C
, both

|\xi|
and
|\eta|
are bounded by a fixed multiple of
1
, and hence are
comparable. In all cases there exists
C_{0}\geq 1
such that

C_{0}^{-1}|\xi|\ \leq\ |\eta|\ \leq\ C_{0}|\xi|.

Case
\alpha<1
.
Then
1-\alpha>0
, and (
17
) yields

|\eta|\ \leq\ |\xi|+C(1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}.

Since
1-\alpha<1
, the term
C(1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}
grows sublinearly in

|\xi|
. Hence there exists
C_{1}\geq 1
such that, for
|\xi|\geq 1
,

|\xi|+C(1+|\xi|)^{1-\alpha}\ \leq\ C_{1}|\xi|.

Consequently, for
|\xi|\geq 1
,

|\eta|\ \leq\ C_{1}|\xi|.

If, in addition,
|\eta|
were much smaller than
|\xi|
, we could exchange the
roles of
\xi
and
\eta
and repeat the argument, obtaining

|\xi|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}|\eta|
. On the range
1\leq|\xi|,|\eta|\leq R_{0}
(for some large
fixed
R_{0}
) comparability is trivial after adjusting the constants.
In summary, there exists
C_{0}\geq 1
such that, also in this case,

C_{0}^{-1}|\xi|\ \leq\ |\eta|\ \leq\ C_{0}|\xi|.

In all cases we have shown that
|\xi|\asymp|\eta|
with a constant depending at
most on
\alpha
. Since
t\mapsto(1+t)^{\beta}
is a radial moderate weight under
comparability (that is, if
c^{-1}\leq a/b\leq c
then

(1+a)^{\beta}\asymp(1+b)^{\beta}
with constants depending on
c
and
\beta
),
we obtain

C_{\beta}^{-1}\,(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}\ \leq\ (1+|\eta|)^{\beta}\ \leq\ C_{\beta}\,(1+|\xi|)^{\beta},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Cross almost–diagonalization)
.

Let
\phi,\psi
be
\alpha
–admissible windows and
\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
be
\alpha
–admissible lattices. Define

G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}:=\langle\phi_{w},\psi_{z}\rangle,\qquad w\in\Omega_{\alpha},\ z\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

Then, for every
N>0
, there exists
C_{N}<\infty
such that

|G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\big)^{-N},\qquad w\in\Omega_{\alpha},\ z\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

(18)

The constant
C_{N}
depends only on
(d,\alpha)
, on the overlap constants of

\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
, and on a finite number of Schwartz seminorms of

\phi,\psi
, but is independent of
w,z
.

Proof.

We write
w=(x,\xi)\in\Omega_{\alpha}
and
z=(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
. By

\alpha
–admissibility of the windows, the associated packets satisfy:

•

Spatial localization: for every
M>0
,

|\phi_{z}(t)|+|\psi_{w}(t)|\;\leq\;C_{M}\,(1+|t-x|)^{-M}+C_{M}\,(1+|t-x^{\prime}|)^{-M},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with
C_{M}
uniform in
w,z
.

•

Frequency localization:
\widehat{\phi_{z}}
and
\widehat{\psi_{w}}
are
concentrated in
\alpha
–subdyadic balls of radius
\asymp|\xi|^{1-\alpha}
and

\asymp|\xi^{\prime}|^{1-\alpha}
around
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
, respectively (cf. Lemma
3.1
).

In particular, if we set

r:=1+\min\{|\xi|,|\xi^{\prime}|\},

we may assume (after absorbing constants into the definition of
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
) that

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\ \asymp\ r^{1-\alpha}|x-x^{\prime}|+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|.

Step 1: Representation of the inner product.

By definition,

G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{z}(t)\,\overline{\psi_{w}(t)}\,dt.

We write explicitly the modulated factors:

\phi_{z}(t)=A_{z}(t-x)\,e^{it\cdot\xi},\qquad\psi_{w}(t)=B_{w}(t-x^{\prime})\,e^{it\cdot\xi^{\prime}},

with
A_{z},B_{w}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and seminorms uniform in
w,z
(by
subdyadic scaling). Then

G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A_{z}(t-x)\,\overline{B_{w}(t-x^{\prime})}\,e^{it\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}\,dt.

Step 2: Regime of separation in frequency.

Suppose first that

r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|\ \geq\ 1.

We introduce the operator

L:=\frac{1}{1+|r^{\alpha-1}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})|^{2}}\Big(1-\Delta_{t}\Big),

which satisfies
L\,e^{it\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}=e^{it\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}
. Integrating by
parts
N
times,

G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L^{N}\big(A_{z}(t-x)\,\overline{B_{w}(t-x^{\prime})}\big)\,e^{it\cdot(\xi-\xi^{\prime})}\,dt.

The derivatives
\partial_{t}^{\gamma}A_{z}
,
\partial_{t}^{\gamma}B_{w}
are bounded by

(1+|t-x|)^{-M}
and
(1+|t-x^{\prime}|)^{-M}
with constants independent of
w,z
, so that

\big|L^{N}(A_{z}(\cdot-x)\,\overline{B_{w}(\cdot-x^{\prime})})\big|\ \leq\ C_{N,M}\,(1+|t-x|+|t-x^{\prime}|)^{-M}

for every
M>0
. The prefactor
(1+|r^{\alpha-1}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})|^{2})^{-N}
decays like

(1+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|)^{-2N}
. Taking
M>d
and integrating in
t
,
we obtain

|G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,(1+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|)^{-N}\ \lesssim\ (1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},

with
C_{N}
independent of
w,z
.

Step 3: Regime of separation in space.

If instead
r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|\leq 1
, then the contribution to

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
must come from the spatial part, that is

r^{1-\alpha}|x-x^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
when
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
is large. In this case it suffices
to exploit Schwartz decay: for every
M>0
,

|\phi_{z}(t)\,\overline{\psi_{w}(t)}|\ \leq\ C_{M}\,(1+|t-x|)^{-M}(1+|t-x^{\prime}|)^{-M}.

If
|x-x^{\prime}|\gg 1
, at least one of the factors decays like

(1+|x-x^{\prime}|)^{-M}
on average in
t
, and a standard estimate gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|t-x|)^{-M}(1+|t-x^{\prime}|)^{-M}\,dt\ \lesssim\ (1+|x-x^{\prime}|)^{-M^{\prime}},

for any
M^{\prime}<M-d
. Choosing
M
large, we obtain

|G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,(1+|x-x^{\prime}|)^{-N}\ \lesssim\ (1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N}.

Finally, in both regimes we have obtained an estimate of the form

|G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},

with
C_{N}
depending on
(d,\alpha)
, on the overlap constants of the
lattices (which guarantee uniformity in the centers), and on a finite number of
Schwartz seminorms of
\phi,\psi
, but independent of
w,z
. This proves
(
18
).
∎

Lemma 4.7

(Bounds on sequence spaces with radial weight)
.

Let
T=(T_{w,z})
satisfy

|T_{w,z}|\leq C_{N}\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\big)^{-N}

for some
N>2d
, and let
w_{\beta}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}
. Then, for every

1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

T:\ \ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)\ \longrightarrow\ \ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)

is bounded, with norm controlled by
C_{N}
and by the relative separation and doubling constants.

Proof.

Write
d(w,z):=\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
and fix
N>2d
large, to be specified later (in particular, we will take
N>2d+\gamma|\beta|
for a certain
\gamma=\gamma(\alpha)
below).

By Lemmas
3.3
and
2.2
and the equivalence between

d_{g^{\alpha}}
and
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
(Proposition
2.1
), we obtain the
neighbor-counting estimate

\#\big\{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}:\ n\leq d(w,z)<n+1\big\}\ \lesssim\ (1+n)^{2d-1},\qquad n\geq 0,

(19)

with constants uniform in
w
.

Step 1: Unweighted case, Schur bounds.

Define
K_{w,z}:=(1+d(w,z))^{-N}
. Using (
19
),

\sup_{w}\sum_{z}K_{w,z}\ \leq\ \sup_{w}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z:\\
n\leq d(w,z)<n+1\end{subarray}}(1+n)^{-N}\ \lesssim\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+n)^{-N}(1+n)^{2d-1}\ <\ \infty,

since
N>2d
. By symmetry of
d
we also have

\sup_{z}\sum_{w}K_{w,z}<\infty.

As
|T_{w,z}|\leq C_{N}K_{w,z}
, the discrete Schur test immediately yields

\|T\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\ \leq\ C_{N}\,\sqrt{\sup_{w}\sum_{z}K_{w,z}}\,\sqrt{\sup_{z}\sum_{w}K_{w,z}}\ <\ \infty.

Step 2: Unweighted case in
\ell^{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

The same Schur bounds give boundedness on
\ell^{1}
and

\ell^{\infty}
:

\displaystyle\|Tc\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{w}\Big|\sum_{z}T_{w,z}c_{z}\Big|\ \leq\ \sup_{w}\sum_{z}|T_{w,z}|\;\|c\|_{\ell^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\|Tc\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w}\Big|\sum_{z}T_{w,z}c_{z}\Big|\ \leq\ \sum_{z}|c_{z}|\sum_{w}|T_{w,z}|\ \leq\ (\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|T_{w,z}|)\,\|c\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Hence

T:\ell^{1}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to\ell^{1}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\qquad T:\ell^{\infty}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to\ell^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})

are bounded, and together with Step 1 (boundedness on
\ell^{2}
), the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem gives

T:\ell^{p}(\Omega_{\alpha})\to\ell^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})\quad\text{bounded for all }1\leq p\leq\infty.

Step 3: Transporting the radial weight.

Now consider the weight
w_{\beta}(w):=w_{\beta}(\xi_{w})=(1+|\xi_{w}|)^{\beta}
, where

\xi_{w}
denotes the frequency component of the index
w
. Consider the
conjugated operator

\widetilde{T}:=M_{w_{\beta}}\,T\,M_{w_{\beta}}^{-1},\qquad(M_{w_{\beta}}c)_{w}:=w_{\beta}(w)\,c_{w}.

Its kernel is

\widetilde{T}_{w,z}=w_{\beta}(w)\,T_{w,z}\,w_{\beta}(z)^{-1}.

We will use the structure of
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
to control the ratio

w_{\beta}(w)/w_{\beta}(z)
in terms of
d(w,z)
.

Let
w=(x,\xi)
and
z=(y,\eta)
, and set
r:=1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}
. From the
definition of the quasi-distance we have

d(w,z)\ \asymp\ r^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|.

In particular,

r^{\alpha-1}|\xi-\eta|\ \lesssim\ 1+d(w,z).

An elementary argument (based on splitting into the cases
|\xi|\leq|\eta|
or

|\eta|\leq|\xi|
and using that
r\sim 1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}
) shows that there exists

\gamma=\gamma(\alpha)\geq 1
such that

\frac{1+|\xi|}{1+|\eta|}\ \leq\ C\,(1+d(w,z))^{\gamma},\qquad\frac{1+|\eta|}{1+|\xi|}\ \leq\ C\,(1+d(w,z))^{\gamma}.

(20)

Raising to the power
|\beta|
we deduce

\frac{w_{\beta}(w)}{w_{\beta}(z)}=\frac{(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}}{(1+|\eta|)^{\beta}}\ \leq\ C_{\beta}\,(1+d(w,z))^{\gamma|\beta|},

(21)

and likewise
w_{\beta}(z)/w_{\beta}(w)\leq C_{\beta}(1+d(w,z))^{\gamma|\beta|}
.

Combining (
21
) with the hypothesis on
T
yields, for some
constant
C_{N}^{\prime}
,

|\widetilde{T}_{w,z}|\ =\ \bigg|\frac{w_{\beta}(w)}{w_{\beta}(z)}\,T_{w,z}\bigg|\ \leq\ C_{N}C_{\beta}\,(1+d(w,z))^{\gamma|\beta|}\,(1+d(w,z))^{-N}\ =\ C_{N}^{\prime}\,(1+d(w,z))^{-N^{\prime}},

with
N^{\prime}:=N-\gamma|\beta|
. Choosing
N
large enough so that

N^{\prime}>2d
, we are back in the situation of Step 1 applied to
\widetilde{T}
instead of
T
. Hence
\widetilde{T}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}
for all

1\leq p\leq\infty
, which is equivalent to the boundedness of
T
on

\ell^{p}(\Omega_{\alpha},w_{\beta})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Step 4:
\ell^{q}(\ell^{p})
structure and different lattices.

In our setting, the indices
w
and
z
decompose as
w=(j,k)
,

z=(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
, where
k,k^{\prime}
parametrize frequency (
\xi_{k},\xi_{k^{\prime}}
) and

j,j^{\prime}
parametrize space. The norm
\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})
is

\|c\|_{\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})}=\left(\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{j}|c_{j,k}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})^{q}\right)^{1/q},

that is, the
\ell^{q}
norm in the frequency index
k
of the
\ell^{p}
norms
in the spatial index
j
, weighted by
w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})
.

The analysis in Steps 1–3 is performed at the level of indices
(j,k)
, and the Schur
estimates give uniform boundedness of
T
as an operator in each layer
k
and in
the sum over
k
(because the counting estimates (
19
) are uniform in

w,z
). Applying Minkowski’s inequality in the
\ell^{q}
norm yields the global
bound

T:\ \ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)\ \longrightarrow\ \ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)

for all
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. The case of distinct lattices

\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is analogous, since both are relatively
separated over the same doubling background and the counting estimate (
19
)
holds with uniform constants. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.8

(Window and lattice independence)
.

Let
\phi,\psi
be
\alpha
–admissible windows and
\Omega_{\alpha},\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}
be
\alpha
–admissible lattices. For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the norms defined with
(\phi,\Omega_{\alpha})
and with
(\psi,\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})
are equivalent: there exist
0<c\leq C<\infty
such that

c\,\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{(\phi,\Omega_{\alpha})}\ \leq\ \|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{(\psi,\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})}\ \leq\ C\,\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{(\phi,\Omega_{\alpha})}\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Let
C_{\phi}f=(\langle f,\phi_{z}\rangle)_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
and

C_{\psi}f=(\langle f,\psi_{w}\rangle)_{w\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}}
be the associated
analysis operators. For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(C_{\psi}f)_{w}=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\langle f,\phi_{z}\rangle\,\langle\phi_{z},\psi_{w}\rangle=\sum_{z}G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}\,(C_{\phi}f)_{z},

where
G^{\psi,\phi}
is the cross Gram matrix from
Lemma
4.6
. That lemma ensures that, for every
N>0
,

|G^{\psi,\phi}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)\big)^{-N},

with constants independent of
w,z
. Applying
Lemma
4.7
to this kernel, we obtain that

G^{\psi,\phi}:\ \ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)\longrightarrow\ell^{q}\!\big(\ell^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\,w_{\beta}\big)

is bounded, and hence

\|C_{\psi}f\|_{\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})}\ \lesssim\ \|C_{\phi}f\|_{\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})},\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This translates into

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{(\psi,\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{(\phi,\Omega_{\alpha})},\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The argument is symmetric after interchanging
(\phi,\Omega_{\alpha})
and

(\psi,\Omega^{\prime}_{\alpha})
, which yields the reverse inequality.

Finally, since the norms
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
are defined precisely in
terms of Gabor coefficients, and
C_{\phi},C_{\psi}
extend by continuity to
bounded linear operators
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to(\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta}))^{\prime}
(by density of
\mathcal{S}
in

M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
and duality), the above inequalities extend to all

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This completes the proof.
∎

In particular, from now on we simply write
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
without explicit reference to the window and the lattice, with the understanding that they are
\alpha
–admissible.

4.4
Relation with
L^{2}
and density of
\mathcal{S}

Proposition 4.9
.

We have
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalence of norms: there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

A\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ \leq\ \|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}}^{2}\ \leq\ B\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

For
p=q=2
and
\beta=0
,

\|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|c_{j,k}(f)|^{2},

and Proposition
3.5
yields the frame inequality

A\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ \leq\ \sum_{j,k}|c_{j,k}(f)|^{2}\ \leq\ B\,\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

If
1\leq p,q<\infty
, then
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Proof.

We sketch the argument, based on Banach frame theory (cf.
[
10
, Chapter 12]
), adapted to our non-uniform lattice
\Omega_{\alpha}
.

Step 1: Density of finitely supported sequences.

Let
X:=\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})
be the sequence space with norm

\|c\|_{X}=\left(\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{j}|c_{j,k}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

For
1\leq p,q<\infty
, finitely supported sequences are dense in
X
.

Step 2: Analysis and synthesis as bounded operators.

Fix an
\alpha
–admissible window
\phi
and its canonical dual

\widetilde{\phi}
(Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
5.2
). Let

C_{\phi}f=(\langle f,\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle)_{j,k}
be the analysis operator.
By definition of
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
,
C_{\phi}
induces a continuous linear
isomorphism

C_{\phi}:\ M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}\ \longrightarrow\ C_{\phi}(M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta})\subset X,

with closed range and equivalent norms.

We define the synthesis operator associated with the dual window by

S_{\widetilde{\phi}}c:=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}\,\widetilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},

for finitely supported
c
. Each
\widetilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}
is a Schwartz
function (by the construction of dispersive packets and of the frame operator),
hence
S_{\widetilde{\phi}}c\in\mathcal{S}
for every finite
c
.

Now set
g:=S_{\widetilde{\phi}}c
. Its coefficients with respect to
\phi
are

C_{\phi}g=G^{\phi,\widetilde{\phi}}c,

where
G^{\phi,\widetilde{\phi}}
is the cross Gram matrix from
Lemma
4.6
. By that lemma,

|G^{\phi,\widetilde{\phi}}_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},

and Lemma
4.7
implies that the operator
G^{\phi,\widetilde{\phi}}
is
bounded on
X
. In particular, for finite sequences
c
,

\|S_{\widetilde{\phi}}c\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}=\|C_{\phi}S_{\widetilde{\phi}}c\|_{X}=\|G^{\phi,\widetilde{\phi}}c\|_{X}\leq C\,\|c\|_{X}.

By density of finite sequences in
X
,
S_{\widetilde{\phi}}
extends by continuity
to a bounded linear operator

S_{\widetilde{\phi}}:\ X\ \longrightarrow\ M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}.

Step 3: Approximation by truncated expansions.

By frame theory in
L^{2}
we have

f=\sum_{j,k}\langle f,\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle\,\widetilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}=S_{\widetilde{\phi}}(C_{\phi}f)

with convergence in
L^{2}
. The same coefficient computation together with
Lemma
4.7
shows that the series also converges in

M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
when
1\leq p,q<\infty
.

Given
f\in M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\varepsilon>0
, choose a finite sequence

c^{F}
such that
\|c^{F}-C_{\phi}f\|_{X}<\varepsilon
. Set

f_{F}:=S_{\widetilde{\phi}}(c^{F})=\sum_{(j,k)\in F}c^{F}_{j,k}\,\widetilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}.

Then
f_{F}\in\mathcal{S}
and

\|f-f_{F}\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}=\|S_{\widetilde{\phi}}(C_{\phi}f-c^{F})\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}\leq\|S_{\widetilde{\phi}}\|\,\|C_{\phi}f-c^{F}\|_{X}\leq C\,\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in

M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
.
∎

4.5
Duality and basic inclusions

Proposition 4.11

(Duality)
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta})^{\prime}\simeq M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta},

with pairing

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,\overline{g(x)}\,dx.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be an
\alpha
–admissible window and

\{\widetilde{\phi}_{z}\}_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
the canonical dual frame in
L^{2}

(Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
5.2
). For

f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, frame theory yields (cf.
[
10
, Chapter 5]
)

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\langle f,\phi_{z}\rangle\,\overline{\langle g,\widetilde{\phi}_{z}\rangle}=\sum_{z}c_{z}(f)\,\overline{\tilde{c}_{z}(g)},

where
c_{z}(f):=\langle f,\phi_{z}\rangle
and
\tilde{c}_{z}(g):=\langle g,\widetilde{\phi}_{z}\rangle
.

By Theorem
4.8
and Lemma
4.7
, the
coefficient maps associated with
\phi
and with
\widetilde{\phi}
are
intertwined by bounded operators on
\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})
and

\ell^{q^{\prime}}(\ell^{p^{\prime}};w_{-\beta})
. In particular,

\|c(f)\|_{\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})}\sim\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}},\qquad\|\tilde{c}(g)\|_{\ell^{q^{\prime}}(\ell^{p^{\prime}};w_{-\beta})}\sim\|g\|_{M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}}.

Applying discrete Hölder’s inequality with weight
w_{\beta}
,

|\langle f,g\rangle|\leq\|c(f)\|_{\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})}\,\|\tilde{c}(g)\|_{\ell^{q^{\prime}}(\ell^{p^{\prime}};w_{-\beta})}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}}\,\|g\|_{M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}}.

This shows that every
g\in M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}
defines a bounded linear
functional on
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
via
f\mapsto\langle f,g\rangle
, and that the map

M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}\ \longrightarrow\ (M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta})^{\prime}

is continuous and injective (injectivity follows from the density of

\mathcal{S}
in both
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}
).

Conversely, let
\Lambda\in(M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta})^{\prime}
be a bounded linear
functional. The analysis operator
C_{\phi}
identifies

M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
with its closed range
Y:=C_{\phi}(M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta})

in
X=\ell^{q}(\ell^{p};w_{\beta})
. The general duality theorem for Banach frames
(cf.
[
10
, Thm. 12.2.3]
) implies that every functional

\Lambda
can be represented as

\Lambda(f)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}c_{z}(f)\,\overline{d_{z}},

with
d\in\ell^{q^{\prime}}(\ell^{p^{\prime}};w_{-\beta})
, and that the norm of
d
is
equivalent to the norm of
\Lambda
. Define

g:=S_{\widetilde{\phi}}d=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}d_{z}\,\widetilde{\phi}_{z}.

The same estimate as in the proof of Proposition
4.10
shows that

g\in M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}
and that

\Lambda(f)=\langle f,g\rangle,\qquad f\in M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}.

Thus every element of the dual is represented by a unique

g\in M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha,-\beta}
, with equivalent norms, which completes the
proof.
∎

Proposition 4.12

(Monotone inclusions)
.

If
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
, then

M^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{\alpha,\beta}\hookrightarrow M^{p_{2},q_{2}}_{\alpha,\beta}

continuously.

Proof.

The discrete inclusions
\ell^{p_{1}}\subset\ell^{p_{2}}
for
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
(with

\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p_{2}}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p_{1}}}
) and similarly

\ell^{q_{1}}\subset\ell^{q_{2}}
imply

\|c(f)\|_{\ell^{q_{2}}(\ell^{p_{2}};w_{\beta})}\ \leq\ \|c(f)\|_{\ell^{q_{1}}(\ell^{p_{1}};w_{\beta})}.

By the definition of the
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
norms via Gabor coefficients,
this yields

\|f\|_{M^{p_{2},q_{2}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{\alpha,\beta}},

which proves the continuous embedding.
∎

These properties show that
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
provides a robust and flexible
framework to measure regularity and mass distribution in the subdyadic geometry
induced by
g^{\alpha}
.

5
Miyachi multipliers on the spaces
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}

In this section we begin a systematic study of the action of dispersive Miyachi-type multipliers on the dispersive modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
introduced in the previous section. We shall work throughout under the two–sided Miyachi condition (
3
) with parameters
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
, as introduced in
[
1
]
.

Theorem
1.1
of Beltrán and Bennett provides weighted
L^{2}
estimates for
T_{m}
with geometric weights adapted to the dispersive region
\{|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
. Although we will not invoke that theorem directly in the proofs below (which are based on the symbol structure and on discrete almost diagonalization), our choice of symbol class and exponent
\beta_{0}
is precisely motivated by those results, and Theorem
5.4
is compatible with Theorem
1.1
once
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}
is identified with
L^{2}
.

5.1
Microlocal set-up

Let
m:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
be a symbol satisfying the two–sided Miyachi condition with parameters
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
(cf. (
3
)). The associated operator
T_{m}
acts as a Fourier multiplier

\widehat{T_{m}f}(\xi)=m(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi).

In time–frequency terms,
T_{m}
is a pseudodifferential operator with symbol depending only on
\xi
; it does not modify the spatial localization of the packets, but it does change their frequency profile.

Let
\phi
be an
\alpha
–admissible window and
\Omega_{\alpha}
a subdyadic Gabor lattice as in the previous sections. The Gabor coefficients of
T_{m}f
can be written as

c_{j,k}(T_{m}f)=\langle T_{m}f,\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Since
\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}
is concentrated on an
\alpha
–subdyadic ball around
\xi_{k}
(Section
2
), the function
m(\xi)
is well approximated by
m(\xi_{k})
at first order, with an error controlled by derivatives of
m
on that ball. We now make this precise.

Lemma 5.1

(Subdyadic control of
m
)
.

Let
m
satisfy (
3
) with parameters
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
at infinity.
For each
\alpha
–subdyadic ball
B_{k}
with centre
\xi_{k}
and radius
r_{k}\asymp|\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha}
, with
|\xi_{k}|\geq 1
, one has

\sup_{\xi\in B_{k}}|m(\xi)-m(\xi_{k})|\ \lesssim\ |\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}},

with an implicit constant depending only on
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
and on the constants in (
3
), but independent of
k
.

Proof.

Let
B_{k}
be an
\alpha
–subdyadic ball centred at
\xi_{k}
, with radius

r_{k}\asymp|\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha}
and
|\xi_{k}|\geq 1
. By the definition of
B_{k}
,
there exist universal constants
0<c_{0}\leq C_{0}<\infty
such that, for every

\zeta\in B_{k}
,

c_{0}|\xi_{k}|\ \leq\ |\zeta|\ \leq\ C_{0}|\xi_{k}|.

In particular, if
\xi\in B_{k}
,

|\xi-\xi_{k}|\ \leq\ C\,r_{k}\ \lesssim\ |\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha},

with
C
independent of
k
.

By the mean value theorem,

|m(\xi)-m(\xi_{k})|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in B_{k}}|\nabla m(\zeta)|\;|\xi-\xi_{k}|.

From the two–sided Miyachi condition (
3
) with

|\gamma|=1
we obtain, for
|\zeta|\geq 1
,

|\nabla m(\zeta)|\ \lesssim\ |\zeta|^{-\beta_{0}+(\alpha-1)},

where the exponent
(\alpha-1)
reflects the dispersive scale
|\xi|^{\alpha}
.
Since
|\zeta|\asymp|\xi_{k}|
on
B_{k}
, we deduce

\sup_{\zeta\in B_{k}}|\nabla m(\zeta)|\ \lesssim\ |\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}+(\alpha-1)}.

Therefore,

|m(\xi)-m(\xi_{k})|\ \lesssim\ |\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}+(\alpha-1)}\,|\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha}=|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}},

with a constant independent of
k
(and of the particular choice of
B_{k}

inside the subdyadic partition). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Localization inheritance for the dual frame)
.

Let
\{\phi_{z}\}_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}
be an
\alpha
–admissible frame with Gramian

G_{w,z}=\langle\phi_{w},\phi_{z}\rangle
satisfying

|G_{w,z}|\leq C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N}\qquad\text{for some }N>2d.

Then the canonical dual family
\{\tilde{\phi}_{w}\}
satisfies

|\langle\tilde{\phi}_{z},\tilde{\phi}_{w}\rangle|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{M}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-M}\qquad(\forall\,M<N-2d),

and, in particular,

|\langle\tilde{\phi}_{w},\phi_{z}\rangle|\ \leq\ C^{\prime\prime}_{M}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-M}\qquad(\forall\,M<N-2d),

with constants
C^{\prime}_{M},C^{\prime\prime}_{M}
independent of
w,z
.

Proof.

This is a special case of the theory of intrinsically localized frames
(Wiener–Jaffard; see Appendix
A
and
[
10
, Chapter 9]
).

Let

C:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha}),\qquad Cf=(\langle f,\phi_{w}\rangle)_{w\in\Omega_{\alpha}},

be the analysis operator associated with the frame, and set

S=C^{*}C

for the frame operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Denote by

G=CC^{*}

the operator on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
whose kernel is exactly the Gramian

G_{w,z}=\langle\phi_{w},\phi_{z}\rangle
:

(Gc)_{w}=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}G_{w,z}\,c_{z}=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\langle\phi_{z},\phi_{w}\rangle\,c_{z}.

By hypothesis,

|G_{w,z}|\leq C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},

so
G\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
for some
N>2d
, where the Jaffard algebra

\mathcal{A}_{s}
was defined in Lemma
3.4
by

\mathcal{A}_{s}=\Big\{A=(A_{w,z}):\ \sup_{w}\sum_{z}|A_{w,z}|(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{s}+\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|A_{w,z}|(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{s}<\infty\Big\}.

Since
\{\phi_{w}\}
is a frame,
S=C^{*}C
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and,
in particular, the restriction

G|_{E}:\ E:=\operatorname{ran}C\ \longrightarrow\ E

is an invertible operator on
E
. The subspace
E
is isometric to

\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
(via a fixed orthonormal basis), and under this
identification
G|_{E}
is still represented by a matrix in
\mathcal{A}_{N}
.

By Jaffard’s theorem (Theorem
A.1
), the algebra

\mathcal{A}_{N}
is inverse-closed in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})
: if an operator on

\ell^{2}
is represented by a matrix in
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and is invertible, then
its inverse is represented by a matrix in
\mathcal{A}_{M}
for every
M<N-2d
.
Applied to
G|_{E}
, this shows that
(G|_{E})^{-1}
is given by a matrix

H=(H_{w,z})_{w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}},\qquad|H_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{M}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-M}

for every
M<N-2d
, with constants
C_{M}
independent of
w,z
.

The canonical dual is given by

\tilde{\phi}_{w}=S^{-1}\phi_{w},\qquad w\in\Omega_{\alpha}.

The theory of intrinsically localized frames (see

[
10
, Lemmas 9.4.1 and 9.4.2]
and the summary in
Appendix
A
) identifies the matrix
H
with the change-of-basis
matrix between the families
\{\phi_{w}\}
and
\{\tilde{\phi}_{w}\}
. In particular,
the Gram matrices of the dual family and of the cross system are obtained from

G
and
H
by matrix products inside the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
, which is
closed under multiplication. Hence

\big(\langle\tilde{\phi}_{z},\tilde{\phi}_{w}\rangle\big)_{w,z}\in\mathcal{A}_{M},\qquad\big(\langle\tilde{\phi}_{w},\phi_{z}\rangle\big)_{w,z}\in\mathcal{A}_{M},

for all
M<N-2d
. This is equivalent to the pointwise estimates

|\langle\tilde{\phi}_{z},\tilde{\phi}_{w}\rangle|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{M}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-M},\qquad|\langle\tilde{\phi}_{w},\phi_{z}\rangle|\ \leq\ C^{\prime\prime}_{M}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-M},

with constants
C^{\prime}_{M},C^{\prime\prime}_{M}
independent of
w,z
and valid for every

M<N-2d
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Almost diagonal matrix of
T_{m}
)
.

Let
\{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}}
be an
\alpha
–admissible frame and set

G^{m}_{(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})}:=\big\langle T_{m}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\big\rangle,\qquad(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}.

Then, for every
N>0
(in particular, for
N>2d
), there exists
C_{N}<\infty
such that

\big|G^{m}_{(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})}\big|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,\big(|m(\xi_{k})|+|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\big)\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N}.

(22)

In particular, for each
N>0
, the matrix
G^{m}
belongs to the Jaffard class
with decay of order
N
controlled by
|m(\xi_{k})|+|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}
.

Proof.

Step 0: Reduction of low frequencies.

By the construction of the grid (Lemma
2.3
and the paragraph on “low
frequencies” in the definition of
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
), there are only finitely
many indices with
|\xi_{k}|\leq 2
. On the compact set
\{|\xi|\leq 2\}
the symbol

m
is smooth and bounded, so that

\big|\langle T_{m}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\big|\ \leq\ C\,\|\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{2}}\ \lesssim\ 1,

with a constant independent of
(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
within this finite subset of indices.
Since
|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\leq C
for
|\xi_{k}|\geq 1
and
|m(\xi_{k})|\leq C
on

|\xi_{k}|\leq 2
, after increasing
C_{N}
if necessary we may assume that
(
22
) has already been verified for all pairs with
|\xi_{k}|\leq 2
.
In what follows we shall therefore work under the assumption

|\xi_{k}|\geq 1.

Step 1: Decomposition of the symbol on each block.

Let us fix
k
with
|\xi_{k}|\geq 1
and let
B_{k}
denote the associated

\alpha
–subdyadic ball in frequency, with centre
\xi_{k}
and radius

r_{k}\asymp|\xi_{k}|^{1-\alpha}
. By the choice of
\widehat{\phi}
and of the
subdyadic partition in frequency, we may assume (after adjusting the radius of

B_{k}
by a universal constant) that

\text{supp}\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}\ \subset\ B_{k}\qquad\text{for all }j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

We write

m(\xi)=m(\xi_{k})+r_{k}(\xi),\qquad r_{k}(\xi):=m(\xi)-m(\xi_{k}).

In this way, on functions whose Fourier transform is supported in
B_{k}
, the
operator
T_{m}
can be decomposed as

T_{m}=T_{m(\xi_{k})}+T_{r_{k}}=m(\xi_{k})\,\mathrm{Id}+T_{r_{k}},

where
T_{m(\xi_{k})}
is simply the scalar multiple of the identity (in the sense
of multiplication by
m(\xi_{k})
in frequency). Lemma
5.1
yields
the bound

|r_{k}(\xi)|\ \leq\ C\,|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}},\qquad\xi\in B_{k},

(23)

with
C
independent of
j,k
.

Step 2: Main term
m(\xi_{k})\,\mathrm{Id}
.

For the main term we have

\big\langle T_{m(\xi_{k})}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\big\rangle=m(\xi_{k})\,\langle\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle.

By the proof of the frame property (Proposition
3.5
) and
Lemma
3.4
(almost diagonal Gramian), for every
N>0
there exists

C_{N}^{(0)}<\infty
such that

\big|\langle\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\big|\ \leq\ C_{N}^{(0)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N},

(24)

with
C_{N}^{(0)}
independent of
(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
. Therefore,

\big|m(\xi_{k})\,\langle\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\big|\ \leq\ |m(\xi_{k})|\,C_{N}^{(0)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N}.

Step 3: Remainder term
T_{r_{k}}
.

For the remainder we have

\big\langle T_{r_{k}}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\big\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{k}(\xi)\,\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}}(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Since
\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}
is supported in
B_{k}
, we may restrict the
integral to
B_{k}
and use (
23
):

\big|\langle T_{r_{k}}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\big|\ \leq\ C\,|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\int_{B_{k}}\big|\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}(\xi)\big|\,\big|\widehat{\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}}(\xi)\big|\,d\xi.

The integral term is exactly of the same type as the one appearing in the study
of the Gramian without multiplier; the same argument by phase–space localization
and integration by parts (cf. the proof of
Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
3.4
) yields, for every

N>0
, an estimate

\int_{B_{k}}\big|\widehat{\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}}(\xi)\big|\,\big|\widehat{\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}}(\xi)\big|\,d\xi\ \leq\ C_{N}^{(1)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N},

(25)

with
C_{N}^{(1)}
independent of
j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}
(note that restricting the integral
to
B_{k}
can only decrease it with respect to the integral over all
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).

In particular,

\big|\langle T_{r_{k}}\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}}\rangle\big|\ \leq\ C\,|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\,C_{N}^{(1)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N}.

Therefore, combining (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain

\big|G^{m}_{(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})}\big|\leq|m(\xi_{k})|\,C_{N}^{(0)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N}+C\,|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\,C_{N}^{(1)}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}},\xi_{k^{\prime}}))\big)^{-N}.

Defining

C_{N}:=C_{N}^{(0)}+C\,C_{N}^{(1)},

the constants
C_{N}
depend only on
(d,\alpha)
, on finitely many seminorms of
the window, and on the constants in (
3
), but not on the
indices
(j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
. This is precisely (
22
) and shows that,
for each
N>0
, the matrix
G^{m}
belongs to the Jaffard class with polynomial
decay of order
N
and weight

\big(|m(\xi_{k})|+|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\big)
, with constants uniform in the
indices.
∎

5.2
Model result in
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}

Recall that
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalent norms (Proposition
4.9
).

Theorem 5.4

(Boundedness on
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}
)
.

Suppose that
m
satisfies (
3
) with parameters
(\alpha,\beta_{0})
at infinity. Then, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|T_{m}f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta-\beta_{0}}}.

In particular, if
\beta_{0}=0
(for instance, for oscillatory symbols of the form
e^{i|\xi|^{\alpha}}
), then
T_{m}
is bounded on
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}
for every
\beta
.

Proof.

We denote
p=(j,k)
,
q=(j_{0},k_{0})
and write

c_{p}(f):=\langle f,\phi_{p}\rangle,\qquad c_{q}(T_{m}f):=\langle T_{m}f,\phi_{q}\rangle,

for a fixed
\alpha
–admissible window
\phi
and grid
\Omega_{\alpha}
. Let

\{\tilde{\phi}_{p}\}
be the canonical dual frame (Proposition
3.5
and
Lemma
5.2
). Then

c_{q}(T_{m}f)=\big\langle T_{m}f,\phi_{q}\big\rangle=\Big\langle T_{m}\sum_{p}c_{p}(f)\,\tilde{\phi}_{p},\ \phi_{q}\Big\rangle=\sum_{p}c_{p}(f)\,\big\langle T_{m}\tilde{\phi}_{p},\phi_{q}\big\rangle.

That is,

c_{q}(T_{m}f)=\sum_{p}a_{p,q}\,c_{p}(f),\qquad a_{p,q}:=\big\langle T_{m}\tilde{\phi}_{p},\phi_{q}\big\rangle.

Step 1: Jaffard structure of the coefficient operator.

Let
G=(G_{r,s})
be the Gramian of the primal frame,
G_{r,s}=\langle\phi_{s},\phi_{r}\rangle
,
and let
H=(H_{p,r})
be the change–of–basis matrix representing the canonical dual in
terms of
\{\phi_{r}\}
, that is,

\tilde{\phi}_{p}=\sum_{r}H_{p,r}\,\phi_{r}.

By Lemma
5.2
and Wiener–Jaffard theory (Lemma
3.4

and Appendix
A
), we have
H\in\mathcal{A}_{M}
for every

M<N_{0}-2d
, where
N_{0}>2d
is an exponent for which the Gramian
G
satisfies

|G_{r,s}|\ \leq\ C_{N_{0}}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(r,s))^{-N_{0}}.

Let now
G^{m}=(G^{m}_{r,c})
be the matrix from Lemma
5.3
, namely

G^{m}_{r,q}:=\big\langle T_{m}\phi_{r},\phi_{q}\big\rangle.

By that lemma, for every
N>0
we have

|G^{m}_{r,q}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,Q_{r}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(r,q))^{-N},\qquad Q_{r}:=|m(\xi_{k(r)})|+|\xi_{k(r)}|^{-\beta_{0}},

(26)

with constants
C_{N}
independent of the indices. We now write

a_{p,q}=\big\langle T_{m}\tilde{\phi}_{p},\phi_{q}\big\rangle=\Big\langle T_{m}\sum_{r}H_{p,r}\phi_{r},\ \phi_{q}\Big\rangle=\sum_{r}H_{p,r}\,G^{m}_{r,q}.

Thus the matrix
A=(a_{p,q})
is the product
A=H\,G^{m}
.

Using (
26
) and the decay of
H
in
\mathcal{A}_{M}
, one obtains
(this is a standard instance of the closure of the Jaffard algebra under products
with moderate weights; cf. Appendix
A
) the following: for every

N>0
we can choose
M
sufficiently large so that there exists
C_{N}^{\prime}<\infty
with

|a_{p,q}|\ \leq\ C_{N}^{\prime}\,Q_{p}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(p,q))^{-N},

(27)

where we have used that
Q_{r}
is a radial moderate weight in the frequency component
(in the sense of Lemma
4.5
), so that
Q_{r}\asymp Q_{p}
whenever

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(p,r)
is bounded, and the contribution of large
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(p,r)

can be absorbed in the polynomial decay. In particular, we may fix
N>2d
in
(
27
).

Step 2: Passage to weighted sequence spaces.

Let
w_{\beta}(\xi)=(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}
and consider the norm

\|c(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}^{2}:=\sum_{k}\sum_{j}|c_{j,k}(f)|^{2}\,w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})^{2}=\|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}}^{2}.

We define the diagonal operator

(Qc)_{p}:=Q_{p}\,c_{p},\qquad Q_{p}:=|m(\xi_{k})|+|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}.

Then the identity
c(T_{m}f)=Ac(f)
can be rewritten as

c(T_{m}f)=H\,G^{m}\,c(f)=H\,K\,(Qc(f)),

where
K
is the unweighted Jaffard matrix

K_{r,q}:=\frac{G^{m}_{r,q}}{Q_{r}},

which satisfies (by (
26
))

|K_{r,q}|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(r,q))^{-N}.

In particular,
K
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
4.7
with

N>2d
, and the same holds for
H
(for large
M
). Therefore, the product

H\,K
defines a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})
, so that

\|H\,K\,d\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}\ \lesssim\ \|d\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})},\qquad\forall d.

We apply this to
d=Qc(f)
:

\|c(T_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}\ =\ \|H\,K\,(Qc(f))\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}\ \lesssim\ \|Q\,c(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}.

Step 3: Use of the Miyachi condition and the weight
w_{\beta}
.

From condition (
3
) with
|\gamma|=0
we have, for
|\xi|\geq 1
,

|m(\xi)|\ \lesssim\ (1+|\xi|)^{-\beta_{0}},

so that

Q_{p}=|m(\xi_{k})|+|\xi_{k}|^{-\beta_{0}}\ \lesssim\ (1+|\xi_{k}|)^{-\beta_{0}}.

Hence,

|Q_{p}|\,w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})\ \lesssim\ (1+|\xi_{k}|)^{-\beta_{0}}\,(1+|\xi_{k}|)^{\beta}=(1+|\xi_{k}|)^{\beta-\beta_{0}}=w_{\beta-\beta_{0}}(\xi_{k}).

It follows that

\|Q\,c(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}^{2}=\sum_{p}|Q_{p}c_{p}(f)|^{2}\,w_{\beta}(\xi_{k})^{2}\ \lesssim\ \sum_{p}|c_{p}(f)|^{2}\,w_{\beta-\beta_{0}}(\xi_{k})^{2}=\|c(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta-\beta_{0}})}^{2}.

Combining Steps 2 and 3, we obtain

\|c(T_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta})}\ \lesssim\ \|c(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\ell^{2};w_{\beta-\beta_{0}})}.

By the definition of the
M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}
norm and the identification of
coefficients, this yields

\|T_{m}f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta-\beta_{0}}}.

Finally, if
\beta_{0}=0
(for instance, for purely oscillatory symbols such as

m(\xi)=e^{i|\xi|^{\alpha}}
), we have
Q_{p}\lesssim 1
and the preceding argument
gives

\|T_{m}f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{M^{2,2}_{\alpha,\beta}},

which completes the proof.
∎

.

Beyond the Hilbertian case, our methods suggest extensions to

, the identification

is consistent with the weighted

[
1
]
,
Theorem
1.1
.

6
Subdyadic Gabor wavefront set

Having constructed the subdyadic grids and the dispersive modulation spaces, we now turn to the microlocal analysis associated with the dispersive Gabor transform. Our goal is to define a set of singularities that detects the lack of regularity of a distribution in a given frequency direction, in a way that is compatible with the geometry
g^{\alpha}
and with the dispersive region
\{|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1\}
.

The philosophy is analogous to that of Hörmander’s wavefront set
[
13
]
: a point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
(with
\xi_{0}\neq 0
) does not belong to the wavefront set if, after a suitable localization in
x
around
x_{0}
, the Fourier transform (or, in our case, the dispersive STFT) decays rapidly in a cone around
\xi_{0}
. The essential difference here is that the phase–space localization is carried out via the Gabor transform adapted to
g^{\alpha}
and to the blocks
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
.

6.1
Definition via the dispersive STFT

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a tempered distribution, and let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a fixed
\alpha
–admissible window. We consider the dispersive STFT

V_{\phi}u(x,\xi)=\langle u,\phi_{x,\xi}\rangle,\qquad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

where
\phi_{x,\xi}
are the wave packets localized in
Q_{\alpha}(x,\xi;1)
as in Section
3
.

Definition 6.1

(Subdyadic Gabor microlocal regularity)
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
with
|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha}\geq 1
. We say that
u
is
Gabor-microlocally regular of type
\alpha
at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
if there exist:

•

a cut-off function
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\chi(x_{0})\neq 0
;

•

an open neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
x_{0}
;

•

an open cone
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with
\xi_{0}\in\Gamma
and such that
|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1
for
\xi\in\Gamma
sufficiently large;

and a constant
R>0
such that, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{N}
with

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\leq C_{N}\,(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad\text{for all }x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R.

(28)

Definition 6.2

(Subdyadic Gabor wavefront set)
.

The
subdyadic Gabor wavefront set
of
u
is defined as

WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)=\Big\{(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}):u\text{ is not Gabor-microlocally regular of type $\alpha$ at $(x_{0},\xi_{0})$}\Big\}.

Remark 6.3
.

It follows directly from Definition
6.1
that

WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
is:

•

closed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
, since microlocal regularity is characterized by the existence of open neighbourhoods
U
and cones
\Gamma
on which (
28
) holds;

•

conic in the frequency variable: if
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
, then there exists an open cone
\Gamma
containing
\xi_{0}
and stable under dilations
\lambda>0
(that is,
\lambda\Gamma=\Gamma
); the condition (
28
) on
\Gamma
then implies the same estimate for every
(x_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})
with
\lambda>0
, so that
(x_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
. In particular, if
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
, then
(x_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})\in WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
for every
\lambda>0
.

The role of the condition
|\xi|^{\alpha}\geq 1
in the definition of regularity is to relegate potential very low-frequency irregularities to a region that can be treated separately (or incorporated via a modified version of the definition in a neighbourhood of
\xi=0
).

6.2
Independence of the window and the cut-off

Proposition 6.4

(Independence of the window)
.

The definition of
WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
does not depend on the choice of the
\alpha
–admissible window
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be two
\alpha
–admissible windows and fix an

\alpha
–admissible grid
\Omega_{\alpha}
. We set

c^{(m)}_{p}(v):=\langle v,\phi^{(m)}_{p}\rangle,\qquad p\in\Omega_{\alpha},\ m=1,2.

By Lemma
4.6
, the cross-coefficient matrix

G^{(2,1)}_{p,q}:=\langle\phi^{(1)}_{q},\phi^{(2)}_{p}\rangle

satisfies, for every
N>0
,

|G^{(2,1)}_{p,q}|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(p,q))^{-N},

with
C_{N}
independent of
p,q
. In particular, for
v\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
we have

c^{(2)}_{p}(v)=\sum_{q\in\Omega_{\alpha}}G^{(2,1)}_{p,q}\,c^{(1)}_{q}(v),

with convergence in the sense of distributions (and in
\ell^{2}
if
v\in L^{2}
).

Now let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and suppose that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is regular in the sense
of Definition
6.1
with respect to the window
\phi_{1}
, that is,
there exist
\chi,U,\Gamma,R
such that, for every
N
,

|V_{\phi_{1}}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R.

(29)

The STFT with window
\phi_{2}
can be viewed as a superposition of the packets

\phi^{(1)}_{p}
with rapid decay in the quasi-distance
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
.
More precisely, for each fixed
(x,\xi)
we write

V_{\phi_{2}}(\chi u)(x,\xi)=\big\langle\chi u,\phi^{(2)}_{x,\xi}\big\rangle=\sum_{p\in\Omega_{\alpha}}c^{(1)}_{p}(\chi u)\,\big\langle\phi^{(1)}_{p},\phi^{(2)}_{x,\xi}\big\rangle.

The kernel

K(x,\xi;p):=\big\langle\phi^{(1)}_{p},\phi^{(2)}_{x,\xi}\big\rangle

enjoys the same type of super-polynomial decay in the quasi-distance

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
as in Lemma
4.6
, uniformly in
(x,\xi)

as this varies in bounded sets of the form
U\times\Gamma
(the proof is
analogous to that of Lemma
4.6
, treating
(x,\xi)
as a
parameter; see also
[
4
]
for analogous stability
results for the STFT with respect to the window in the case
\alpha=0
).

Decomposing the sum into annuli

A_{n}(x,\xi):=\{p\in\Omega_{\alpha}:\ n\leq\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(p,(x,\xi))<n+1\},

and using the neighbour-counting estimates (Lemmas
2.3
–
2.2
)
together with (
29
), one obtains that for each
N
we can choose

M
large in the estimate for
K
in such a way that

|V_{\phi_{2}}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R,

with
C^{\prime}_{N}
independent of
(x,\xi)
in
U\times\Gamma
. In other words,
microlocal regularity at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
does not depend on the window. The
reverse argument (starting from
\phi_{2}
and going back to
\phi_{1}
) completes
the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.5

(Independence of the cut-off)
.

The condition in Definition
6.1
does not depend on the choice of
\chi
as long as
\chi(x_{0})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\chi_{1}(x_{0})\neq 0
and

\chi_{2}(x_{0})\neq 0
. Multiplying by an additional cut-off, we may assume that

\chi_{2}=\psi\,\chi_{1}
in a neighbourhood of
x_{0}
, with

\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Suppose that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is microlocally regular for
u
with the choice
of cut-off
\chi_{1}
, that is, there exist
U,\Gamma,R
such that, for every
N
,

|V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R.

We write

V_{\phi}(\chi_{2}u)(x,\xi)=V_{\phi}(\psi\,\chi_{1}u)(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(y)\,\chi_{1}(y)\,u(y)\,\overline{\phi_{x,\xi}(y)}\,dy.

Expanding
\psi
in a Taylor series around
x
up to order
L
,

\psi(y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq L}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x)}{\alpha!}(y-x)^{\alpha}+R_{L}(x,y),

we obtain

V_{\phi}(\chi_{2}u)(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq L}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x)}{\alpha!}\,\int(y-x)^{\alpha}\,\chi_{1}(y)\,u(y)\,\overline{\phi_{x,\xi}(y)}\,dy+\int R_{L}(x,y)\,\chi_{1}(y)\,u(y)\,\overline{\phi_{x,\xi}(y)}\,dy.

Using
(y-x)^{\alpha}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}=i^{|\alpha|}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}

and differentiating under the integral sign, one sees that the principal terms are

\xi
–derivatives of
V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)
:

V_{\phi}(\chi_{2}u)(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq L}c_{\alpha}(x)\,\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)(x,\xi)+I_{\mathrm{rem}}(x,\xi),

with coefficients
c_{\alpha}(x)
smooth and bounded on
U
.

The derivatives
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)
preserve arbitrary conic
decay: each
\xi
–derivative introduces polynomial factors in
|\xi|
, but since
we have
all

N
in the decay of
V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)
, these factors can be
absorbed by choosing
N
large. More precisely, from (
28
) we
deduce

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}V_{\phi}(\chi_{1}u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N,\alpha}(1+|\xi|)^{-N+|\alpha|},

and taking
N
arbitrarily large we retain super-polynomial decay.

For the remainder term
I_{\mathrm{rem}}
, the function
R_{L}(x,y)
is controlled
by
|y-x|^{L+1}
, and its contribution is estimated in an analogous way: the factor

|y-x|^{L+1}
produces additional derivatives in
\xi
, which can be absorbed by
choosing
L
(and hence
N
) sufficiently large. Consequently,

V_{\phi}(\chi_{2}u)
satisfies an estimate of the form

|V_{\phi}(\chi_{2}u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U^{\prime},\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R^{\prime},

for a suitable neighbourhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
x_{0}
and some
R^{\prime}\geq R
. The reverse
argument (interchanging
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
) shows that microlocal regularity
does not depend on the choice of cut-off.
∎

6.3
Relation with local regularity and modulation spaces

Proposition 6.6
.

If
u\in M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
for some
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\beta
sufficiently large, then
WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We sketch the argument, which combines the expansion of
u
in the subdyadic
Gabor frame with the almost diagonal decay of the associated kernels.

Let
\phi
be an
\alpha
–admissible window and
\Omega_{\alpha}
an

\alpha
–admissible grid. We write

u=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}c_{z}(u)\,\widetilde{\phi}_{z},\qquad c_{z}(u)=\langle u,\phi_{z}\rangle,

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and in
M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}
(cf. Section
3
and Proposition
4.10
). Then

V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)=\sum_{z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}c_{z}(u)\,\langle\chi\,\widetilde{\phi}_{z},\phi_{x,\xi}\rangle.

The kernel

K(x,\xi;z):=\langle\chi\,\widetilde{\phi}_{z},\phi_{x,\xi}\rangle

enjoys the same type of super-polynomial decay in the quasi-distance

\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
as in Lemma
4.6
: by integrating by parts
in the phase and using localization in
Q_{\alpha}
, one obtains, for every

M>0
,

|K(x,\xi;z)|\ \leq\ C_{M}(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,(x,\xi)))^{-M},

with
C_{M}
independent of
z
and of
(x,\xi)
in bounded sets of the form

U\times\Gamma
.

Fix
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
with
|\xi_{0}|^{\alpha}\geq 1
, a neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
, and a
cone
\Gamma
containing
\xi_{0}
. For
(x,\xi)\in U\times\Gamma
and
|\xi|

large, the condition
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,(x,\xi))\approx n
implies that the
frequency radius of the blocks associated with
z
is comparable to
|\xi|
, and
Lemmas
2.3
–
2.2
yield

\#\{z:\ n\leq\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,(x,\xi))<n+1\}\ \lesssim\ (1+n)^{2d-1},

with constants independent of
(x,\xi)
. Decomposing the sum into annuli

A_{n}(x,\xi)
,

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{z\in A_{n}(x,\xi)}|c_{z}(u)|\,|K(x,\xi;z)|.

Using the decay of
K
and discrete Hölder on each annulus, one obtains an
estimate of the form

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{M}\Big(\sum_{p}|c_{z}(u)|^{p}\,w_{\beta}(\xi_{k(z)})^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\,\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}(1+n)^{-Mp^{\prime}}\,(1+n)^{(2d-1)p^{\prime}}\,\Theta_{\beta}(\xi,n)\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}},

where
\Theta_{\beta}(\xi,n)
controls the contribution of the weight
w_{\beta}
at
the frequencies of those indices
z
with
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,(x,\xi))\approx n
.
By the moderation of the radial weight (Lemma
4.5
),
w_{\beta}(\xi_{k(z)})

is comparable to
(1+|\xi|)^{\beta}
whenever
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(z,(x,\xi))
is
bounded; for large
n
, the additional contribution can be absorbed by choosing

M
sufficiently large. In conclusion, for each
N
we can choose
\beta

sufficiently large (depending on
N
and on
(d,\alpha)
) and
M
large so that

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N}\,\|u\|_{M^{p,q}_{\alpha,\beta}},\qquad x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R,

for some
R>0
. This shows that
u
is microlocally regular at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
in
the sense of Definition
6.1
. Since
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
was arbitrary,
we conclude that
WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(u)=\emptyset
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
for every
\xi_{0}\neq 0
, then
u
is locally smooth near
x_{0}
; in particular,
\chi u\in C^{\infty}
for every
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
with
\chi(x_{0})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a fixed
\alpha
–admissible window and let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
with

\chi(x_{0})\neq 0
. By hypothesis, for each direction
\xi_{0}\neq 0
there exist
neighbourhoods
U_{\xi_{0}}
of
x_{0}
, cones
\Gamma_{\xi_{0}}
and radii
R_{\xi_{0}}

such that

\sup_{x\in U_{\xi_{0}},\ \xi\in\Gamma_{\xi_{0}},\ |\xi|\geq R_{\xi_{0}}}(1+|\xi|)^{N}\,|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|<\infty,\qquad\forall N.

Since
S^{d-1}
is compact, we can cover
\{|\xi|\geq R\}
by finitely many cones

\Gamma_{m}
(for
m=1,\dots,M
) and choose a neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
such that
all
U_{\xi_{0}}
contain
U
. Then, for each
N
,

\sup_{x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma_{m},\ |\xi|\geq R}(1+|\xi|)^{N}\,|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ <\ \infty,\qquad m=1,\dots,M.

(30)

In the region
\{|\xi|\leq R\}
, the temperateness of
u
and the smoothness of

\phi
ensure that
V_{\phi}(\chi u)
has at most polynomial growth in
(x,\xi)
,
so the corresponding integral will always be finite.

We now use the inversion formula for the STFT (cf.
[
10
, Corollary 3.2.3]
):

\chi u(x)=\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{\phi}(\chi u)(y,\xi)\,\phi_{y,\xi}(x)\,dy\,d\xi,

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and in
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. Differentiating
under the integral sign, we obtain

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\chi u)(x)=\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{\phi}(\chi u)(y,\xi)\,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi_{y,\xi}(x)\,dy\,d\xi.

The derivatives of the packets satisfy

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi_{y,\xi}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha,M}(1+|x-y|)^{-M}(1+|\xi|)^{|\alpha|},

for every
M>0
, since
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
and the dispersive rescaling only
introduces polynomial factors in
|\xi|
that are uniformly controlled.

We split the integral in
\xi
as

\{|\xi|\leq R\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{m=1}^{M}\big(\Gamma_{m}\cap\{|\xi|\geq R\}\big).

•

On
\{|\xi|\leq R\}
,
V_{\phi}(\chi u)
has polynomial growth in

(y,\xi)
(by temperateness), and the factor
(1+|x-y|)^{-M}
with
M>d

ensures uniform convergence in
x\in U
; the integral in
\xi
is taken over
a set of finite measure, so there is no issue.

•

On each cone
\Gamma_{m}\cap\{|\xi|\geq R\}
, we use
(
30
) with
N
such that
N>|\alpha|+d
. Then

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(y,\xi)|\,|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi_{y,\xi}(x)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{-N}(1+|\xi|)^{|\alpha|}(1+|x-y|)^{-M},

and the factor
(1+|\xi|)^{-N+|\alpha|}
is integrable on
\{|\xi|\geq R\}
if

N>|\alpha|+d
. The integral in
y
again converges uniformly in
x\in U
if
we take
M>d
.

In this way, for each multi-index
\alpha
we have

\sup_{x\in U}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\chi u)(x)|<\infty.

Since
\alpha
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\chi u\in C^{\infty}
in a
neighbourhood of
x_{0}
. This proves the local regularity of
u
near
x_{0}
.
∎

6.4
Action of smooth pseudodifferential operators

Definition 6.8

(Smooth pseudodifferential operators of order zero)
.

We say that
A
is a smooth pseudodifferential operator of order
0
if

Af(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,a(x,\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi,

with
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

|D_{x}^{\gamma}D_{\xi}^{\delta}a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\gamma,\delta}\qquad\text{for all multi-indices }\gamma,\delta.

For small
|\xi|
we moreover assume that
a
is smooth and bounded. These bounds are
uniform in
(x,\xi)
and are compatible with the anisotropic geometry induced by

g^{\alpha}
; we do not impose here any additional structure adapted to
g^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 6.9

(Microlocal invariance under smooth operators)
.

Let
A
be a smooth pseudodifferential operator of order
0
. For every distribution
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(Au)\subset WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u).

Moreover, if
a(x,\xi)
is elliptic at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
(that is,
a(x,\xi)\neq 0
in a neighbourhood of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
), then

(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(Au).

Proof.

We fix an
\alpha
–admissible window
\phi
and an
\alpha
–admissible grid

\Omega_{\alpha}=\{(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\}
. We set

c_{j,k}(v):=\langle v,\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\rangle,\qquad A^{\phi}_{(j_{0},k_{0}),(j,k)}:=\big\langle A\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\phi_{x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}}\big\rangle.

Step 1: Almost diagonalization of
A
on the frame.

By the smoothness of
a
and the phase–space localization of the packets (as in the
proof of Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
4.6
), for every

N>0
there exists
C_{N}
such that

\big|A^{\phi}_{(j_{0},k_{0}),(j,k)}\big|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}),(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}))\big)^{-N},

(31)

with
C_{N}
depending on finitely many seminorms of
a
and
\phi
, but not on the
indices
(j_{0},k_{0}),(j,k)
. The proof is standard: integration by parts in the
variable
x
using the phase
e^{ix\cdot(\xi-\eta)}
, together with the fact that
all derivatives of
a
are bounded; the anisotropy in
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
is absorbed
in the polynomial decay by combining the regimes of separation in
x
and in
\xi
,
as in Lemma
4.6
.

In particular, (
31
) shows that the matrix
A^{\phi}
belongs to
the Jaffard class for every order
N
, with no additional weight.

Step 2: Expression of the coefficients of
Au
.

Let
\{\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}\}
be the canonical dual frame of

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\phi)
(Proposition
3.5
and
Lemma
5.2
). For
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
we can write formally

u=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}(u)\,\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(and in
L^{2}
if
u\in L^{2}
). Hence

Au=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}(u)\,A\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},

and, taking coefficients with respect to the primal family,

c_{j_{0},k_{0}}(Au)=\big\langle Au,\phi_{x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}}\big\rangle=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}(u)\,\big\langle A\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}}\big\rangle.

The coefficient matrix

a_{(j,k),(j_{0},k_{0})}:=\big\langle A\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\phi_{x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}}\big\rangle

inherits Jaffard decay:
\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}
is obtained by applying
S^{-1}

to
\phi_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}}
, where
S
is the frame operator; by
Lemma
5.2
and the stability of Jaffard classes under products
(Appendix
A
), we have, for every
N>0
,

|a_{(j,k),(j_{0},k_{0})}|\ \leq\ C_{N}^{\prime}\,\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}))\big)^{-N}.

(32)

In other words,
A
induces on the Gabor coefficients a discrete almost diagonal
operator with kernel in the Jaffard class (with no weight).

Step 3: Inclusion
WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(Au)\subset WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
.

Suppose that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
. By
Definition
6.1
(and independence of window and cut-off,
Propositions
6.4
–
6.5
), there exist

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
with
\chi(x_{0})\neq 0
, a neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
, a cone

\Gamma
containing
\xi_{0}
and
R>0
such that, for every
N
,

|V_{\phi}(\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U,\ \xi\in\Gamma,\ |\xi|\geq R.

(33)

We first treat the
local
term
\chi A\chi u
. We observe that

\chi A\chi u=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}(\chi u)\,\chi A\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},

and hence

c_{j_{0},k_{0}}(\chi A\chi u)=\sum_{j,k}a^{(\chi)}_{(j,k),(j_{0},k_{0})}\,c_{j,k}(\chi u),

where

a^{(\chi)}_{(j,k),(j_{0},k_{0})}:=\big\langle A\tilde{\phi}_{x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}},\,\chi\phi_{x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}}\big\rangle.

The function
\chi
is compactly supported and smooth, so the same integration
by parts arguments as in Step 1 yield an estimate analogous to
(
32
):

|a^{(\chi)}_{(j,k),(j_{0},k_{0})}|\ \leq\ C_{N}^{\prime\prime}\big(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}((x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k}),(x_{j_{0}}^{k_{0}},\xi_{k_{0}}))\big)^{-N}.

In particular,
a^{(\chi)}
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
4.7

for every
N>2d
(Jaffard-type kernel without weight).

On the other hand, (
33
) implies rapid decay of the
coefficients
c_{j,k}(\chi u)
when
(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})
lies in
U\times\Gamma

and
|\xi_{k}|
is large. More precisely, using the independence of window and
grid (Theorem
4.8
) between the continuous STFT

V_{\phi}(\chi u)
and the discrete coefficients
c_{j,k}(\chi u)
, one obtains
that, for every
N
,

|c_{j,k}(\chi u)|\ \leq\ C_{N}(1+|\xi_{k}|)^{-N}

whenever
(x_{j}^{k},\xi_{k})\in U\times\Gamma
and
|\xi_{k}|\geq R^{\prime}
(for some

R^{\prime}\geq R
). By Lemma
4.7
, applied to
a^{(\chi)}
in weighted
sequence spaces with radially moderate weights (Lemma
4.5
), the
rapid decay in
|\xi_{k}|
of
c_{j,k}(\chi u)
is transferred to

c_{j_{0},k_{0}}(\chi A\chi u)
in the same cone–neighbourhood region (possibly
after shrinking
U
and narrowing the cone
\Gamma
). In particular, for every

N
,

|V_{\phi}(\chi A\chi u)(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{N}^{\prime}(1+|\xi|)^{-N},\qquad x\in U^{\prime},\ \xi\in\Gamma^{\prime},\ |\xi|\geq R^{\prime\prime},

for some neighbourhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
, a cone
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
with

\xi_{0}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, and some
R^{\prime\prime}>0
.

We now deal with the
non-local
term
\chi A(1-\chi)u
. Since
\chi
and

1-\chi
have disjoint supports, the kernel of
\chi A(1-\chi)
is smooth in a
neighbourhood of
(x_{0},x_{0})
; in STFT terms, this implies that

V_{\phi}(\chi A(1-\chi)u)
decays super-polynomially in
|\xi|
for
x
in a
neighbourhood of
x_{0}
, independently of the properties of
u
. This is the
standard microlocal argument: one integrates by parts in
\xi
in the kernel
representation of
A
and exploits the fact that
x
and the integration
variable are separated.

Combining both contributions,

V_{\phi}(\chi Au)=V_{\phi}(\chi A\chi u)+V_{\phi}(\chi A(1-\chi)u),

we conclude that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is regular for
Au
in the sense of
Definition
6.1
. Hence

WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(Au)\subset WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(u)
.

Step 4: Equivalence under ellipticity.

Suppose now that
a
is elliptic at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
, that is, there exist a
neighbourhood
V\times\Gamma_{0}
of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
with
\Gamma_{0}
conic such that

a(x,\xi)\neq 0
on
V\times\Gamma_{0}
. Then there exist

\chi_{0},\eta_{0}
with
\chi_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\eta_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
such that

\chi_{0}(x_{0})\neq 0,\quad\eta_{0}(\xi_{0})\neq 0,\quad\chi_{0}\equiv 1\ \text{in a neighbourhood of }x_{0},\quad\eta_{0}\equiv 1\ \text{in a cone around }\xi_{0},

and a symbol
b\in C^{\infty}
such that

b(x,\xi)=\frac{\chi_{0}(x)\eta_{0}(\xi)}{a(x,\xi)}

in a neighbourhood of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
. Let
B
be the
\Psi
DO of order
0

associated with
b
. By the classical pseudodifferential calculus (for order
zero symbols with controlled derivatives), we have

BA=\chi_{0}(x)\eta_{0}(\xi)\,\mathrm{Id}+R,

where
R
is an operator whose kernel is smooth in a neighbourhood of

(x_{0},x_{0})
and hence is microlocally smoothing near
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
in the sense
of
WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}
.

If
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(Au)
, applying the inclusion already
proved to the operator
B
we obtain

WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(BAu)\subset WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(Au),

so that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin WF^{\mathrm{G}}_{\alpha}(BAu)
. The term
Ru
is
microlocally regular near
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
, and
\chi_{0}\eta_{0}u
has the same
wavefront set as
u
in that neighbourhood. In conclusion,
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is
regular for
u
if and only if it is regular for
Au
, which proves the
equivalence under ellipticity.
∎

This first analysis shows that the subdyadic Gabor wavefront set

WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}(u)
behaves naturally under the action of smooth
pseudodifferential operators. In subsequent work, this theory can be refined to
include FIO-type operators with dispersive phases
|\xi|^{\alpha}
and Miyachi
amplitudes, thereby establishing a direct connection between
WF_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{G}}

and the propagation of singularities for dispersive equations.

Appendix A
Well-localized matrices

In this section we collect the results of S. Jaffard on matrices that are well
localized near the diagonal, which we use implicitly in the proofs of
Lemmas
3.4
and
5.2
; the main reference is

[
15
]
.

Discrete metric spaces and polynomial growth

Let
(T,d)
be a discrete metric space. We assume a polynomial growth condition

\#\{t\in T:\ d(s,t)\leq R\}\ \leq\ C\,(1+R)^{D},\qquad\forall s\in T,\ \forall R\geq 0,

(34)

for some
C,D>0
independent of
s,R
.
This assumption is analogous to the
doubling
property satisfied by

(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
and the grid
\Omega_{\alpha}
in our setting
(cf. Lemmas
2.2
and
3.3
), with
D\simeq 2d
.

In our concrete situation, we typically take
T=\Omega_{\alpha}
and
d(w,z)

equivalent to the quasi-distance
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
. Lemma
2.2

(anisotropic volume and doubling) and Lemma
3.3
(relative
separation) imply (
34
) with
D=2d
(up to constants).

Classes of matrices with polynomial decay

Let
T
be as above and consider an infinite matrix
A=(A(s,t))_{s,t\in T}
.
For
\alpha>0
, Jaffard introduces (among others) the class

Q_{\alpha}(T,d)=\Big\{A:\ \sup_{s,t\in T}(1+d(s,t))^{\alpha}\,|A(s,t)|<\infty\Big\}.

(35)

Elements of
Q_{\alpha}(T,d)
define densely defined linear operators on
\ell^{2}(T)

via

(Ax)(s)=\sum_{t\in T}A(s,t)\,x(t),\qquad x\in\ell^{2}(T)\cap\ell^{1}(T).

A first result (a discrete Schur-type lemma in this context) states that, under
(
34
), every matrix
A\in Q_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>D
defines
a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(T)
; in particular, there exist constants
C_{\alpha}

depending only on
C,D,\alpha
such that

\|A\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\;\leq\;C_{\alpha}\,\sup_{s,t\in T}(1+d(s,t))^{\alpha}\,|A(s,t)|.

(36)

The constant
C_{\alpha}
is independent of the particular entries of
A
, beyond
its norm in
Q_{\alpha}
.

Moreover, Jaffard shows that the classes
Q_{\alpha}
are
algebras
: if

A,B\in Q_{\alpha}(T,d)
, then
AB\in Q_{\alpha}(T,d)
and

\|AB\|_{Q_{\alpha}}\ \lesssim\ \|A\|_{Q_{\alpha}}\,\|B\|_{Q_{\alpha}},

where
\|\cdot\|_{Q_{\alpha}}
denotes the natural seminorm in (
35
)
and the implicit constant depends only on
C,D,\alpha
. This is the content of
Proposition 1 in
[
15
]
.

Wiener–Jaffard type inversion theorem

The central result we use is the following
symbolic calculus
theorem for
well-localized matrices (Proposition 3 in
[
15
]
):

Theorem A.1

(Jaffard)
.

Let
(T,d)
be a discrete metric space satisfying (
34
)
and let
\alpha>0
. If
A\in Q_{\alpha}(T,d)
is invertible as a bounded operator
on
\ell^{2}(T)
, then its inverse
A^{-1}
also belongs to
Q_{\alpha}(T,d)
.
In particular, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

|(A^{-1})(s,t)|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}\,(1+d(s,t))^{-\alpha},\qquad\forall s,t\in T,

with
C^{\prime}
depending only on
C,D,\alpha
and on uniform upper/lower bounds
for the spectrum of
A
on
\ell^{2}(T)
.

In words: polynomial off-diagonal decay of the entries of
A
is preserved
under inversion, provided
A
is invertible on
\ell^{2}
and the index space has
polynomial growth. This is the discrete analogue of the fact that the inverse
of an elliptic symbol in
S^{0}
is still of order
0
.

Connection with our Lemma
3.4

In our work the grid
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a relatively separated subset of

(\mathbb{R}^{2d},d_{g^{\alpha}})
, and the Gramian matrices that appear (see
Lemma
3.4
) satisfy estimates of the form

|G_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},\qquad w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha},

for some
N>2d
. Since
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
is equivalent to
d_{g^{\alpha}}
on the
relevant scale, Lemmas
2.2
and
3.3
imply
(
34
) with
T=\Omega_{\alpha}
,
d=\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}
and
D=2d
.

Fixing
N>2d
, the bound

|G_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N}

means precisely that
G\in Q_{N}(\Omega_{\alpha},\mathbf{d}_{\alpha})
. Applying
Theorem
A.1
to
G
(which is invertible on its range, and in
particular on
\ell^{2}(\Omega_{\alpha})
when restricted to
\operatorname{ran}C
,
cf. the proof of Lemma
5.2
) guarantees that
G^{-1}\in Q_{N}
and
that its entries satisfy

|(G^{-1})_{w,z}|\ \leq\ C^{\prime}_{N}\,(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-N},\qquad w,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}.

On the other hand, in Lemma
3.4
we work with the algebra

\mathcal{A}_{s}:=\Big\{A=(A_{w,z}):\ \sup_{w}\sum_{z}|A_{w,z}|(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{s}+\sup_{z}\sum_{w}|A_{w,z}|(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{s}<\infty\Big\},

which is strictly smaller than
Q_{s}
(we additionally control the sum in each
row/column). Under the hypothesis
N>2d
, the polynomial growth
(
34
) implies that

Q_{N}(\Omega_{\alpha},\mathbf{d}_{\alpha})\ \subset\ \mathcal{A}_{N-2d+\varepsilon}

for every
\varepsilon>0
, since

\sum_{z}(1+\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z))^{-(N-2d+\varepsilon)}\ \lesssim\ \sum_{n\geq 0}(1+n)^{2d-1-(N-2d+\varepsilon)}<\infty.

In particular, if
G\in Q_{N}
with
N>2d
, then
G\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
for every

s<N-2d
, and the same holds for
G^{-1}
. This is exactly what is used in
Lemma
3.4
: starting from
G\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
with
s>2d
, the
combination of Theorem
A.1
and the neighbour-counting estimates
on
\Omega_{\alpha}
yields that
G^{-1}\in\mathcal{A}_{M}
for every
M<s-2d
,
that is, the inverse inherits polynomial (summable) decay of arbitrarily high
order
M<s-2d
in the quasi-distance
\mathbf{d}_{\alpha}(w,z)
.

This stability under inversion is the underlying mechanism both in
Lemma
3.4
(Wiener–Jaffard for almost diagonal matrices) and in
the inheritance of localization for the dual windows in
Lemma
5.2
.

Development and frontier trends of research on the gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma
Authors: Xusheng Zhang, Hongkun Yin, Di Liu, Tao Jiao, Qi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough research on the gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma has developed rapidly in recent years, and the gut microbiota plays an important role in cholangiocarcinoma, there is currently a lack of comprehensive and objective understanding of the latest progress and future research directions in this field. This study comprehensively evaluated and visually analyzed this research area based on bibliometric methods.MethodsThis analysis used tools such as the “Bibliometrix” R package, VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Microsoft Office Excel (2019 version) to comprehensively conduct a bibliometric analysis of scientific research outputs related to the gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma. The data analyzed were all sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (2009–2025).ResultsA total of 124 research achievements published from 2009 to 2025 were included. Eight hundred and ninety-six researchers from 278 institutions in 37 countries/regions participated in the research on the gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma. The number of global publications has steadily increased, reaching a peak in 2024. China had the most publications and citations, followed by Italy and the United States. The University of Oslo, Khon Kaen University, and Università degli Studi di Palermo were the main institutions in this research area and made important contributions. Cancers, International Journal of Microorganisms, and Microorganisms were the most relevant journals in this research area. DI CARLO P, GIAMMANCO A, and SERRA N were the most relevant authors in this research area and made important contributions. The latest frontier research direction focuses on “metabolism”, aiming to further explore the pathogenesis of cholangiocarcinoma through the gut microbiota and to find new therapeutic approaches.ConclusionThis study comprehensively summarized the development of research on the gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma from 2009 to 2025. The research results revealed the latest research frontiers and hotspots in this field and provided valuable insights for future research. However, it should be noted that although WoSCC is a widely recognized and authoritative database, it may have limitations in terms of regional bias, language coverage, and metadata accuracy, which may affect the comprehensiveness of our analysis.Keywords: Gut microbiota, Cholangiocarcinoma, Bibliometrix, VOSviewer, CiteSpace

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma is one of the common malignant tumors in the digestive system [1], [2]. Its main risk factors include parasitic infections, primary sclerosing cholangitis, biliary cysts, gallstones, and toxins. Other less certain potential risk factors include inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis B virus (HBV), cirrhosis, diabetes, obesity, alcohol consumption, smoking, and host genetic polymorphisms. In addition, risk factors for cholangiocarcinoma also include older age, smoking, diabetes, and certain genetic diseases [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. According to the “Global Cancer Statistics Report 2022”, cholangiocarcinoma, a highly malignant liver tumor originating from the biliary epithelium, has long had a very poor prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of less than 20% [6]. Its insidious onset, rapid progression, and poor prognosis make it a significant burden on global health-care systems. China, due to the prevalence of chronic cholangitis and gallstones, is one of the main countries with a high incidence of cholangiocarcinoma, with about half of the global cases occurring in China. At present, the overall 5-year survival rate of cholangiocarcinoma is less than 10%, and its annual incidence and mortality rates are very close [7]. Studies in the United States have shown that although the survival rate of cholangiocarcinoma has improved compared with decades ago, the prognosis is still poor, far below patient expectations, and the burden remains heavy [8]. It is extremely important for the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma to further clarify its pathogenesis and to discover new targets for diagnosis and treatment.

The gut microbiota is a collection of microorganisms in the human gut, which play an important role in maintaining host health, including participating in food digestion, synthesizing vitamins, and regulating immunity [9], [10]. Dysbiosis of the gut microbiota can lead to gut microbes or their products crossing the damaged intestinal epithelial barrier into the systemic circulation, activating immune cells, which may lead to chronic cholangitis and inflammation, and eventually further lead to cancer [11].

The relationship between cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota is increasingly attracting attention. Studies have shown that there are significant differences in the distribution of microbial flora in the bile of patients with cholangiocarcinoma compared with healthy people, and the imbalance of biliary microbiota may be related to the development of cholangiocarcinoma. In addition, the development of cholangiocarcinoma leads to significant metabolic changes in the gut, including changes in the relative concentrations of uracil, succinic acid, and 5-aminovaleric acid. Other studies have also shown that the gut microbiota can promote the occurrence and development of cholangiocarcinoma by regulating the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells by hepatocytes to form an immunosuppressive microenvironment. As a digestive tumor with a very poor prognosis and an increasing incidence, the relationship between cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota, especially the role of bacterial retrograde infection and intestinal microbiota imbalance in the progression of cholangiocarcinoma, should be given attention.

Bibliometrics is widely used because it focuses on quantifying the entire knowledge system. This paper aims to comprehensively and objectively analyze the indicators of research on cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota through bibliometrics, to clarify the trends, hotspots, and directions of research in this field for researchers, and to provide new clues and ideas for future research.

Materials and methods

Data collection

Web of Science not only contains core databases (such as SCI, SSCI, and A&HCI) but also includes the Conference Proceedings Citation Index (CPCI) and the Book Citation Index (BKCI). The data information on research related to cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota used in this analysis is sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection, WoSCC: (https://webofscience.com/wos/woscc/full-).

Search strategy

All published papers were collected from WoSCC. Researchers retrieved relevant papers published from January 1, 2009, to May 31, 2025: TS = ((Gut microbiota) OR ((Cholangiocarcinoma) OR (Biliary tract cancer) OR (Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma) OR (Extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma) OR (Hilar cholangiocarcinoma)) AND Publication Year = (2009–2025) AND document Types = (articles&reviews) AND language = (English). After the initial data retrieval, researchers screened all publications to ensure their relevance to the theme of this study. The research process is shown in the flowchart in Fig. 1. The download format was a plain text file of “Full Text and Cited References.”

Flowchart of the study.

Bibliometric analysis

This analysis covered an overview of the total number of published papers, countries, regions, institutions, authors, journal distribution, document types, references, keywords, and trends, following a step-by-step in-depth analysis from macro to micro perspectives. Subsequently, the collected data were further analyzed using “Bibliometrix” (R version 4.4.0), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), CiteSpace (version 6.3. R1), and Microsoft Excel (2019 version).

Specifically, for the VOSviewer co-occurrence networks we set minimum occurrence thresholds of 20 for keywords, 10 for journals and 15 for authors; co-occurrence strength was normalized with the default cosine index and the top 100 high-frequency nodes were selected for visualization.

In CiteSpace burst detection we used γ = 1.0, a minimum duration of 2 years, and a burst-strength cutoff of the top 5%; nodes with betweenness centrality ≥0.10 were defined as “key turning points”.

The “most relevant” journals, authors and institutions were identified by combining two criteria: ① total citations (TC) ≥ 100, and ② average citations per paper (ACite) for 2020–2024 ≥ 5, retaining the top 10 ranked in descending order of ACite.

Results

Overall publication of global literature

A total of 124 documents related to cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota were finally selected from the Web of Science database, including 69 research articles and 55 reviews. Fig. 2 shows that the number of annual publications related to cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota from 2011 to 2025 generally showed a fluctuating upward trend. The number was low in 2011, reached a peak in 2012, and then declined. Starting from 2009, the number gradually increased and reached the highest point in 2023, followed by a slight decline. The scientific output in 2024–2025 was lower than in previous years. Since we used the online publication time as the cut-off value and the search for relevant studies in 2025 was only up to May, the number of publications in 2025 was significantly reduced. However, it is worth noting that even though the literature search for 2025 was only up to May, the number of relevant studies had already exceeded half of that in 2024. Therefore, it is predicted that the scientific output related to this field in 2025 may be higher than in 2024. The average number of annual citations sharply increased between 2011 and 2012, reached a peak in 2013, and then quickly declined. Starting from 2014, the average number of annual citations fluctuated greatly, but the overall trend was downward, approaching zero by 2025.

Country/region analysis

During the study period, a total of 5045 researchers from 1015 institutions in 34 countries/regions participated in research related to cholangiocarcinoma and the gut microbiota. The relationship between the countries/regions of scientific output based on the authors' countries of residence is shown in Fig. S1A, where China is the largest network node, indicating that China has made a leading contribution in this research field. An analysis of the cumulative annual publication output of the top-ranking countries in terms of the number of publications was conducted. The results showed that China had a scientific output of 150 publications in this research field from 2009 to 2025, far ahead of other countries. Italy ranked second with nearly 80 publications, and the United States was third (Fig. 3A). Subsequently, an analysis of the cumulative publication output over time for the top five countries in terms of publication output was conducted. The results showed that, overall, the number of articles published by each country from 2009 to 2025 increased significantly. China had the most pronounced growth trend, with a continuous increase in growth rate after 2019, reaching a peak in 2024. This may be due to the fact that the literature search for 2025 was only up to May 31. The output of the United States also increased significantly after 2018, but the growth rate was slightly lower than that of China. By 2024, the output of the United States was expected to be close to 100 publications. The output of Italy also increased significantly after 2018. The output of Germany and Thailand began to increase after 2018, but the growth rate was relatively slow (Fig. 3B). In addition, the citation situation of different countries was also analyzed. The results showed that the United States had the most citations, with 1339 citations. China was second with 816 citations, and the number of citations in other countries decreased in turn (Fig. 3C).

The scientific cooperation between different countries was also explored. The results showed that cooperation between some major economies and technological powers was very close. For example, cooperation between North America (mainly the United States) and Europe (mainly the United Kingdom, Germany, and France) was very close, and cooperation between East Asian regions (mainly China, Japan, and South Korea) was also very close. In contrast, cooperation between some developing countries and smaller countries was relatively less. This cooperation network reflects the current situation and trends of global economic and technological cooperation (Fig. 3D).

Keyword and topic analysis

In the keyword analysis, we found that “gut microbiota” is the most frequently occurring keyword and is the main theme of this analysis. Next, “bile - acids”, “risk”, and “cancer” also show higher frequencies of occurrence, indicating that these are also important topics in the research. Other topics such as “cholangiocarcinoma”, “dysbiosis”, “infection” and “inflammation” also have a certain level of attention in the research in this field (Fig. 8A). An analysis of the cumulative frequency of the top 10 keywords over time shows that the cumulative occurrence of most keywords has increased significantly after 2015, indicating that these keywords have received more attention in recent years' research. In particular, “Gut microbiota” has seen a marked increase in growth rate after 2018, showing a sharp rise in research interest in this area in recent years (Fig. 8B). Subsequently, looking at the topic evolution trend from 2009 to 2025, some keywords such as “Primary biliary - cirrhosis”, “Biliary - tract”, and “Biliary-tract cancer” had already attracted attention around 2015. Terms like “Gut microbiota”, “Intestinal microbiota”, “Inflammatory-bowel - disease”, and “Infection” saw a significant increase in 2019 and 2020, reflecting that the gut microbiota gradually became a research hotspot in this field. Recently, the research focus has shifted to “Metabolism”. The increase in metabolism - related research may be due to researchers' deepening understanding of the relationship between the gut microbiota and metabolic diseases and how to treat cholangiocarcinoma by regulating metabolism.

Discussion

Cholangiocarcinoma is characterized by its insidious onset, complex etiology, and rapid disease progression [12]. Up to now, the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma is far from satisfactory. Although the therapeutic approaches for cholangiocarcinoma have become more diversified with the progress of research and scientific technology (including surgical resection, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, etc.) [13], [14], [15], the current treatment of cholangiocarcinoma is still a comprehensive treatment mainly based on surgery. The relatively high recurrence rate after surgery leads to an unsatisfactory 5-year recurrence -free survival rate for cholangiocarcinoma. Moreover, the 5-year recurrence rate (including metastasis or new primary tumors) [6], [16] after surgery for patients with cholangiocarcinoma has been reported to exceed 50%. With the advancement of therapeutic methods, the survival rate of patients with cholangiocarcinoma has improved. From 2000 to 2009, the 1 - year survival rate of patients with cholangiocarcinoma increased from about 20% to about 30%, and the 5 - year survival rate also increased from about 5% to about 10% [17], [18], [19]. However, the mortality rate of cholangiocarcinoma remains high worldwide, and cholangiocarcinoma is still one of the malignant tumors with the lowest 5-year survival rate. It is extremely important to further explore the pathogenesis of cholangiocarcinoma and the mechanisms at the level of the tumor microenvironment to improve the treatment and prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma [20], [21].

Gut microbiota analysis, by obtaining the composition and functional information of microbial communities in samples, helps to reveal the interactions between gut microbes and the host and their impact on health and disease. This is crucial for understanding the pathogenesis of gut - related diseases and developing new therapeutic strategies [9], [10]. In recent years, it has been widely used in gut health research. It has unique advantages in identifying the structure of microbial communities, exploring the associations between specific gut microbes and specific diseases, predicting functions, and developing therapeutic strategies [22].

This study conducted a bibliometric analysis in the above - mentioned research direction. The number of annual publications related to cholangiocarcinoma and gut microbiota has shown a significant upward trend since 2009. This trend not only reflects the increasing importance of gut microbiota in cholangiocarcinoma research but also indicates the great potential of this technology in oncology research. With the progress of technology and in - depth research, the recognition of the association between gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma is becoming more and more important. Although the literature search for 2025 was only up to May, it has already exceeded half of the relevant research in 2024, which shows that the research on the association between gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma is still popular. It is predicted that the scientific output related to this field in 2025 may be higher than that in 2024, which further confirms the importance and potential of the research on the association between gut microbiota and cholangiocarcinoma.

Despite the increasing number of publications related to gut microbiota, the average annual citation number shows a general downward trend. This phenomenon may be due to the fact that with the maturation and wide application of gut microbiota -related research, studies may gradually shift from exploratory to applied research, which may lead to a decrease in citation numbers. Secondly, the scientific community may be shifting to new research hotspots or technologies, resulting in a reduced focus on gut microbiota. In addition, the publications in 2025 have been published for a relatively short time and have not yet had enough time to accumulate citations, which may be one of the reasons for the decline in the average annual citation number.

The continuous investment, policy support, and improvement of the scientific research environment in China have enabled China to take the lead in terms of output and influence in this research field. The application of gut microbiota in this field has provided an important means for the study of the mechanism and exploration of therapeutic methods for cholangiocarcinoma. In addition, although cross - continental cooperation is frequent, regional cooperation (such as cooperation between European countries) is also quite common. However, when the output of each country is divided according to the country of the corresponding author, it is found that although China has the most output and citations in this research field, the MCP% of China is much lower than that of the United States and Italy, indicating that Chinese authors lack international cooperation and communication. It is necessary to strengthen international exchanges to promote the progress of this research field. Diversified cooperation models are conducive to the exchange and dissemination of knowledge, and the development of academic research and medical theory. In addition, although China has the most scientific output in this research field, the number of citations of Chinese research is far lower than that of the United States. This indicates that although China has a large number of scientific research results, the quality of some research results may not yet have been highly recognized by readers. Researchers should focus on improving the quality of research to win the recognition of peers. At the same time, international cooperation should be strengthened to enhance international connections and keep pace with the international frontier, so as to further improve the scientific research production capacity and quality.

Institutions such as the University of Oslo, Khon Kaen University, and Università degli Studi di Palermo have performed outstandingly in research related to cholangiocarcinoma and gut microbiota, demonstrating their strong research capabilities in this field. The cooperation network between these institutions helps in the exchange and dissemination of knowledge, promoting the development of academic research. Moreover, the significant increase in the output of these institutions after 2015 coincides with the booming development of gut microbiota research, reflecting their leading advantages in this field. Future research in this area could seek cooperation with these institutions.

Journals such as Cancers, International Journal of Molecular Sciences, and Microorganisms are the most relevant in this research field and have a significant impact. The cumulative number of publications in these journals has shown a marked increase around 2015–2017, indicating a clear upward trend. The significant increase in citations during this period suggests that the research published in these journals has attracted widespread attention from the academic community.

The booming development of gut microbiota research after 2017 is reflected in the topic evolution trend, with most keywords peaking in 2022–2023, indicating that these topics have received widespread attention in recent years. The latest research hotspot is focused on “metabolism”, reflecting researchers' in - depth exploration of the mechanisms underlying the association between gut microbiota and the development of cholangiocarcinoma. This is also the most cutting - edge research hotspot in this field, providing new tools and ideas for the development of diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for cholangiocarcinoma.

The limitations of this study are closely related to the inherent issues of using the WoSCC database. First, this analysis only included studies from the WoSCC database, excluding those indexed in Scopus or PubMed and non-English studies. This limitation may introduce bias into the results. Second, the quality of metadata in WoSCC may be inconsistent. Errors or inaccuracies in the article indexing, such as incomplete or incorrect information on authors, institutions, or citation tracking, may affect the accuracy of the bibliometric analysis and thus the reliability of the research findings. The research data for 2025, which only included data from the first half of the year, may have overlooked common phenomena in academic research, such as delayed indexing, publication lag, and citation delay, due to the short time span. Studies that gain recognition later or are published in journals with fewer citations may be underestimated in our analysis. In addition, the bibliometric analysis used in this study mainly focuses on quantitative indicators such as publication numbers and citation frequencies, without qualitative evaluation of the content and clinical significance of specific studies [23]. Therefore, our analysis is insufficient in comprehensively reflecting the depth and impact of the included scientific output. Thus, the results of this analysis should be interpreted objectively. Future studies could provide a more balanced and inclusive research perspective by integrating more comprehensive evaluation methods, including multiple databases, and addressing the current biases.

Conclusion

This study comprehensively analyzed the research related to cholangiocarcinoma and gut microbiota through bibliometric methods. The study revealed the research trends, main contributors, and current research focus in this field, providing valuable references for clarifying future research directions. Although limiting the analysis to studies indexed in WoSCC may introduce some bias, these findings still have important reference value.

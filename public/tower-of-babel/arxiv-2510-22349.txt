Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the existence of monotone traveling wave solutions for a class of nonclassical reaction–diffusion equations with a discrete delay in the reaction term, namely ∂ u ⁡ ( x , t ) ∂ t = D ​ ∂ 2 u ⁡ ( x , t ) ∂ x 2 + α ​ ∂ 3 u ⁡ ( x , t ) ∂ x 2 ​ ∂ t + f ⁡ ( u ⁡ ( x , t − τ ) ) , \frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\,\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha\,\frac{\partial^{3}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}\partial t}+f\big(u(x,t-\tau)\big), where D > 0 D>0 , α ∈ ℝ \alpha\in\mathbb{R} , τ > 0 \tau>0 , and f f satisfies bistable or monostable conditions on [ 0 , K ] [0,K] . By applying the traveling-wave ansatz u ⁡ ( x , t ) = ϕ ⁡ ( x + c ​ t ) u(x,t)=\phi(x+ct) , the problem is reduced to a functional differential equation for the wave profile. We employ the Green’s function associated with the underlying third-order operator and develop a monotone iteration scheme based on upper and lower solutions in a weighted Banach space. Our approach provides a unified framework that extends the non-delayed nonclassical model to the delayed setting, capturing the effect of discrete reaction delay on the propagation of traveling waves.

1.
Introduction

Reaction–diffusion equations with delayed effects have received considerable attention
in the study of wave propagation phenomena arising in biology, physics, and engineering.
In many physical models, classical diffusion fails to accurately capture memory or nonlocal
effects, leading to the introduction of nonclassical diffusion terms, such as those involving
mixed derivatives or higher-order temporal interactions; see
Aifantis
[
1
]
, Ting
[
13
]
, and Truesdell–Noll
[
14
]
.
An important example is the nonclassical reaction–diffusion equation
with an additional mixed derivative,

(1.1)

\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha\frac{\partial^{3}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}\partial t}+f\big(u_{t}(x)\big),

where
D>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f:C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}

introduces a delayed reaction term depending on the history segment

u_{t}(x)(r)=u(x,t+\theta)
for
\theta\in[-\tau,0]
. Such delays may arise due to maturation time
in population dynamics or latency effects in phase transitions;
we refer to the classical monographs of Diekmann et al.
[
6
]
and Wu and Zou
[
15
]
.

Traveling wave solutions, of the form
u(x,t)=\phi(\xi)
with
\xi=x+ct
,
play a crucial role in describing front propagation and transition layers
between stable equilibria. When (
3.1
) is transformed into
the corresponding profile equation, one obtains a functional differential
equation of . In the absence of delay, nonclassical wave equations
have been studied in
[
5
]
, where the method of upper and lower solutions
combined with Green’s functions was used to establish monotone wavefronts.
Recently, equations with delays in both diffusion and reaction terms were
analyzed by Barker and Nguyen
[
2
]
, and further developed in

[
3
,
4
,
16
]
, highlighting the delicate role of delay
on wave speed and monotonicity.

In the broader literature, there has been a surge of recent works focusing on
traveling waves in delayed or nonlocal media. Zhang, Hu, and Huang
[
16
]

investigated monotone waves for systems with delay in both diffusion and reaction.
Li et al. (2024) considered a time-delayed nonlocal model arising in epidemiology,
while Huang (2025) analyzed
p
-Laplacian diffusion with temporal delay.
Zhu and Peng (2025) treated discrete-time predator–prey models with diffusion
and delay via an iterative fixed point framework. On the methodological side,
Boumenir and Minh (2006) applied Perron’s theorem and monotone iteration
to mixed-type functional equations, laying foundations relevant to our approach.

In this work, we establish the existence of monotone traveling wave solutions
for (
3.1
) by employing a Green’s function representation of
the associated linear operator and a monotone iteration scheme based on upper
and lower solutions. Unlike the classical case, the presence of delay in the
reaction term introduces functional dependencies in the nonlinear operator,
leading to technical challenges in verifying invariant cones and regularity.
We overcome these obstacles by constructing a suitable operator
F=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H

and showing that iterates preserve order and improve regularity.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we introduce the functional framework, the Green’s function associated with the linear
operator, and state the hypotheses on
f
. Section
3
develops the
monotone iteration scheme and proves the existence of traveling wave profiles
through the Schauder fixed point theorem. In Section
4
,
we apply the abstract theory to a concrete nonclassical model with discrete delay,
construct explicit super- and subsolutions, and establish the existence of a
monotone wavefront connecting two equilibria.

Preliminaries and Notation

We adopt the following standard notational conventions throughout this paper. Let
\mathbb{R}
denote the field of real numbers. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\Re(z)=z
.

We denote by
BC(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
the space of all bounded continuous functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the supremum norm

\|f\|:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|.

Similarly,
BC^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
denotes the subspace of
C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
consisting of functions whose derivatives up to order
k
are bounded. When boundedness is not assumed, we write
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
or
C^{k}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

A natural partial order on
BC(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is defined by

f\leq g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(t)\leq g(t)\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

and

f<g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(t)\leq g(t)\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R},\text{ with }f(t)\neq g(t)\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

An operator
P
acting on a subset
S\subset BC(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is said to be
monotone
if
f\leq g
implies
Pf\leq Pg
for all
f,g\in S
. In particular, if
P
is linear and acts on all of
BC(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, it is monotone if and only if it maps nonnegative functions to nonnegative functions.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\hat{\alpha}
the constant function defined by
\hat{\alpha}(t)=\alpha
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now recall standard definitions of function spaces used throughout this work.

Definition 2.1

(
L^{p}
Spaces)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
consists of all measurable functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},&1\leq p<\infty,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|,&p=\infty,\end{cases}

is finite.

Definition 2.2

(Sobolev Space
W^{1,p}
)
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is defined by

W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\;\middle|\;f\text{ is absolutely continuous, and }f^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

In one dimension, this space is continuously embedded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
; that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}\quad\text{for all }f\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

Definition 2.3

(Sobolev Space
W^{k,\infty}
)
.

We define

W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\;\middle|\;f^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),\,f^{\prime}\text{ is absolutely continuous, and }f^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Equivalently,

W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\;\middle|\;f^{\prime}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Definition 2.4

(Sobolev Space
W^{3,\infty}
)
.

The space
W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
consists of all functions
f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
such that the second derivative
f^{\prime\prime}
is absolutely continuous and the third derivative
f^{\prime\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. That is,

W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\;\middle|\;f^{\prime\prime}\text{ is absolutely continuous, and }f^{\prime\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Equivalently,

W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\;\middle|\;f^{\prime\prime}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

2.2. Rouché’s Theorem

Let
A
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
,
f
and
g
are two analytic functions on
A
.
A piecewise continuously differentiable function
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)
is called a circuit. The following theorem (
[
7
]
, Rouché’s Theorem, p. 247)
will be used later on:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain,
f,g
two analytic complex valued
functions in
A
. Let
T
be the (at most denumerable) set of zeros of
f
,

T^{\prime}
the set of zeros of
f+g
in
A
,
\gamma
a circuit in
A-T
, defined on an interval
I
.
Then, if
|g(z)|<|f(z)|
in
\gamma(I)
, the function
f+g
has no zeros on
\gamma(I)
, and

(2.1)

\sum_{a\in T}j(a;\gamma)\,\omega(a;f)\;=\;\sum_{b\in T^{\prime}}j(b;\gamma)\,\omega(b;f+g),

where
j(a;\gamma)
is the index of
\gamma
with respect to
a
, and
\omega(a,f)
is the multiplicity
of the zeros of
f
at
a
.

As a consequence of the theorem, if
\gamma
is a simple closed circuit that encircles
T
, then

j(a,\gamma)=1
for all
a
inside
\gamma(I)
and Rouché Theorem claims that the total zeros
(counting multiplicities) of
f
and
f+g
inside the circuit
\gamma
are the same.

Main Results

We consider traveling waves for a non classical reaction diffusion equation

(3.1)

\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha\frac{\partial^{3}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}\partial t}+f\big(u_{t}(x)\big).

where
D>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and

f:C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{t}(x)\in C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

u_{t}(x)(\theta)=u(t+\theta,x),\quad\theta\in[-\tau,0].

We are interested in traveling wave solutions of the form
u(x,t)=\phi(x+ct)
,
c>0
.
This transformation gives

u(x,t)=\phi(x+ct),\qquad\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=c\phi^{\prime}(x+ct),

\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}=\phi^{\prime\prime}(x+ct).\qquad\frac{\partial^{3}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}\partial t}=c\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x+ct).

Setting
\xi=x+ct,r=c\tau

(3.2)

\alpha c\,\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\phi^{\prime}(\xi)+f_{c}(\phi_{\xi})=0,

where
f_{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{c}

f_{c}(\psi)=f(\psi^{c}),\quad\psi^{c}(\theta):=\psi(c\theta),\quad\theta\in[-\tau,0].

By a shift of variable we can reduce the equation to

(3.3)

\alpha c\,\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\phi^{\prime}(\xi)+f_{c}(\phi_{\xi})=0,

The main purpose of this section is to look for solutions
\phi
of (

) in the following subset of

\Gamma:=\Big\{\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):\varphi\ \text{is nondecreasing},\ \lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\varphi(\xi)=\hat{0},\ \lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\varphi(\xi)=\hat{K}\Big\}.

Taking

(3.4)

\alpha c\,\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\phi^{\prime}(\xi)-\beta\phi(\xi)+H(\phi)(\xi)=0,

where

H(\phi)(\xi):=\beta\phi(\xi)+f_{c}(\phi_{\xi}),\qquad\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

As proved in
[
15
]

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that (

We impose the following conditions on

Remark

The condition (C1) guarantees there are only two roots or steady states for

For convenience, let

Proposition 3.3
.

For

,

, and

Proof.

Substituting

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
c>0
,
D>0
, and
\beta>0
. Then:

(i)

\Delta
has exactly one positive real root
\lambda_{+}>0
.

(ii)

The remaining two roots lie in the open left half-plane: either both are real and negative, or they form a complex conjugate pair with negative real part.

(iii)

Consequently, there exists
\mu_{0}>0
such that every root
\lambda
of
\Delta
satisfies
|\Re\lambda|\geq\mu_{0}
, i.e.
\Delta
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

(i) By Descartes’ rule of signs applied to
\Delta(\lambda)=c\lambda^{3}+D\lambda^{2}-c\lambda-\beta
(whose coefficients have the sign pattern
+,+,-,-
), the number of positive real roots counted with multiplicity is exactly one. Since
\Delta(0)=-\beta<0
and
\Delta(\lambda)\to+\infty
as
\lambda\to+\infty
, there exists a unique
\lambda_{+}>0
with
\Delta(\lambda_{+})=0
.

(ii) Divide (
3.6
) by

to obtain the monic cubic

\lambda^{3}+\frac{D}{c}\lambda^{2}-\lambda-\frac{\beta}{c}=0.

Let
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\}
be its roots (so
\{\rho_{j}\}
are the roots of
\Delta
as well). Vieta’s formulas yield

\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}=-\frac{D}{c}<0,\qquad\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{3}=\frac{\beta}{c}>0.

From (1) one root, say
\rho_{1}=\lambda_{+}>0
, is positive and real. Hence

\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}=-\frac{D}{c}-\lambda_{+}<0.

If
\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
then both must be negative; if they are a complex conjugate pair, write
\rho_{2,3}=a\pm ib
with

, showing
\Re\rho_{2,3}<0
.

(iii) Proposition

ensures that no root lies on the imaginary axis. Together with (1)–(2), the set of roots is separated from

by a positive distance. Define

\mu_{0}:=\min\big\{\lambda_{+},\,-\Re\rho_{2},\,-\Re\rho_{3}\big\}>0.

Then every root

or

∎

Definition 3.5

A function

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

We prove the upper–solution case; the lower case is identical with inequalities reversed.

Assume

A_{3}<0

e^{\lambda_{3}\xi}>0
.
On the other hand,
for
\xi>0
,

\displaystyle G(\xi)

\displaystyle\,=-\bigl(A_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\xi}+A_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\xi}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=-e^{\lambda_{2}\xi}[A_{1}e^{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\xi}+A_{2}]

\displaystyle\,=-e^{\lambda_{2}\xi}T(\xi),

where
T(\xi):=A_{1}e^{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\xi}+A_{2}
.
We have
T^{\prime}(\xi)=A_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})e^{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\xi}>0
, so
T
is increasing.
The continuity of

at
\xi=0
gives
A_{3}=-(A_{1}+A_{2})
, hence

T(0)=A_{1}+A_{2}=-A_{3}>0
and therefore
T(\xi)>0
for all
\xi>0

Thus
G(\xi)=-e^{\lambda_{2}\xi}T(\xi)<0
.

Consequently,
G(\xi)<0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.1.
Monotone Iteration method

Let

\Gamma:=\Big\{\phi\in BC_{[0,1]}(\mathbb{R}):\ \phi\ \text{nondecreasing},\ \lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\phi(\xi)=0,\ \lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\phi(\xi)=1\Big\},

and fix
0<\mu<\mu_{0}
. Denote

B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})=\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{R}):\|\phi\|_{\mu}:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mu|\xi|}|\phi(\xi)|<\infty\}.

Set
F(\phi):=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H(\phi)=-G*H(\phi)
, where
G
is the Green kernel from Lemma
3.7
.

Lemma 3.8
.

With the above notation:

i)

\Gamma\neq\varnothing
.

ii)

\Gamma
is closed, convex and bounded in
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

iii)

\Gamma\subset B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

iv)

Sketch.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume (C1)–(C3) and the Green kernel properties:
G\in L^{1}
with
|G(\xi)|\leq Ce^{-\gamma|\xi|}
for some
\gamma>\mu
, and
-G\geq 0
. Then:

i)

F(\Gamma)\subset\Gamma
.

ii)

F
is continuous on
(B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|_{\mu})
.

iii)

F
is compact on
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Looking at
i.)
we first show
\underline{\phi}\leq F(\underline{\phi})
. Using (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle F(\underline{\phi})(t)

\displaystyle\,=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)H(\underline{\phi})(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)\left(c\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)+D\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(s)-c\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(s)-\beta\underline{\phi}(s)\right)ds=\underline{\phi}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, we show
F(\overline{\phi})\leq\overline{\phi}
.

\displaystyle F(\overline{\phi})(t)

\displaystyle\,=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)H(\overline{\phi})(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)\left(c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(s)-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(s)-\beta\overline{\phi}(s)\right)ds=\overline{\phi}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus,

\underline{\phi}\leq F(\underline{\phi})\leq F(\phi)\leq F(\overline{\phi})\leq\overline{\phi}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\underline{\phi}(\xi)=0,\quad\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\overline{\phi}(\xi)=0\text{ then }\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}F(\phi(\xi))=0.

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\underline{\phi}(\xi)=1,\quad\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\overline{\phi}(\xi)=1\text{ then }\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}F(\phi(\xi))=1.

Therefore,
F:\Gamma\to\Gamma.

Now, looking at
ii.)
we first show
H
is continuous with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mu}
in
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

Assume (C2):
f:C([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz with constant
L>0
,
and recall that
H(\phi)(t)=f_{c}(\phi_{t})+\beta\phi(t)
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
.

For
\phi,\psi\in B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, we have for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

|H(\phi)(t)-H(\psi)(t)|\leq|f_{c}(\phi_{t})-f_{c}(\psi_{t})|+\beta|\phi(t)-\psi(t)|.

Since
f_{c}
is
L
–Lipschitz on histories,

|f_{c}(\phi_{t})-f_{c}(\psi_{t})|\leq L\sup_{\theta\in[-\tau,0]}|\phi(t+c\theta)-\psi(t+c\theta)|.

Multiplying both sides by
e^{-\mu|t|}
and noting that
for
\theta\in[-\tau,0]
we have
|t+c\theta|-|t|\leq|c\theta|=r
, we get

e^{-\mu|t|}|f_{c}(\phi_{t})-f_{c}(\psi_{t})|\leq Le^{\mu r}\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mu}.

Similarly,

e^{-\mu|t|}\beta|\phi(t)-\psi(t)|\leq\beta\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mu}.

Taking the supremum over
t\in\mathbb{R}
gives

(3.10)

\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}\leq(Le^{\mu r}+\beta)\,\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mu}.

Hence
H
is globally Lipschitz (and therefore continuous) on

B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
with constant
Le^{\mu r}+\beta
.

We recall that
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
denotes the weighted Banach space
equipped with the exponential norm

\|u\|_{\mu}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mu|t|}|u(t)|,\qquad 0<\mu<\gamma,

where
\gamma
is such that the Green kernel
G
of
\mathcal{L}
satisfies

|G(\xi)|\leq C_{G}e^{-\gamma|\xi|}
.

We first justify the elementary weighted inequality used below.
Let
h\in B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. By the definition of the weighted norm,
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

|h(s)|=e^{\mu|s|}\bigl(e^{-\mu|s|}|h(s)|\bigr)\leq e^{\mu|s|}\|h\|_{\mu},

which is precisely

(3.11)

|h(s)|\leq\|h\|_{\mu}\,e^{\mu|s|}.

Applying (
3.11
) to
h=H(\phi)-H(\psi)
gives, for all
s
,

|H(\phi)(s)-H(\psi)(s)|\leq\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}\,e^{\mu|s|}.

Now fix
\phi,\psi\in B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|F(\phi)(t)-F(\psi)(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(t-s)|\,|H(\phi)(s)-H(\psi)(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(t-s)|\,e^{\mu|s|}\,ds.

Make the change of variables
r=t-s
. Using the inequality
|s|=|t-r|\leq|t|+|r|
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(t-s)|\,e^{\mu|s|}\,ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(r)|\,e^{\mu|t-r|}\,dr\leq e^{\mu|t|}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(r)|\,e^{\mu|r|}\,du.

Set

C_{\mu}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(r)|\,e^{\mu|r|}\,dr<\infty,

which is finite because
G
decays exponentially faster than
e^{-\mu|\cdot|}
by choice of
\mu<\gamma
.
Combining the estimates yields, for every
t
,

|F(\phi)(t)-F(\psi)(t)|\leq C_{\mu}\,e^{\mu|t|}\,\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}.

Multiplying by
e^{-\mu|t|}
and taking the supremum over
t\in\mathbb{R}
gives the weighted norm bound

(3.12)

\|F(\phi)-F(\psi)\|_{\mu}\leq C_{\mu}\,\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}.

Finally, by (
3.10
) we have the Lipschitz bound

\|H(\phi)-H(\psi)\|_{\mu}\leq(Le^{\mu r}+\beta)\,\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mu},

where
r=c\tau
. Combining this with (
3.12
) yields

\|F(\phi)-F(\psi)\|_{\mu}\leq K\,\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mu},\qquad K:=C_{\mu}\,(Le^{\mu r}+\beta).

Thus
F
is Lipschitz continuous (hence continuous) on
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

Looking at
iii.)
define

F^{n}(\phi)(t)=\begin{cases}F(\phi)(t),&\ \text{when}\ t\in[-n,n]\\
F(\phi)(n),&\ \text{when}\ t\in(n,\infty)\\
F(\phi)(-n),&\ \text{when}\ t\in(-\infty,-n).\end{cases}

Using parts
i.)
and
ii.)
shows that
F^{n}(\Gamma)
is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded on any finite interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Thus, take
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then in the interval
[-n,n]
we can say that
F^{n}
is compact by Arzela-Ascoli.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for all
\phi(t)\in\Gamma.

\displaystyle\left|F^{n}(\phi)-F(\phi)\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\,=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|F^{n}(\phi)(t)-F(\phi)(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in(-\infty,-n)\bigcup(n,\infty)}|F^{n}(\phi)(t)-F(\phi)(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in(-\infty,-n)}|F(\phi)(-n)-F(\phi)(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}+\sup_{t\in(n,\infty)}|F(\phi)(n)-F(\phi)(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}

\displaystyle\leq K\sup_{t\in(-\infty,-n)}|\phi(-n)-\phi(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}+K\sup_{t\in(n,\infty)}|\phi(n)-\phi(t)|\,e^{-\mu|t|}

\displaystyle\leq 2K|\phi(-n)|\,e^{-\mu n}+2K|\phi(n)|\,e^{-\mu n}

\displaystyle\leq 2K\left(|\phi(-n)|+|\phi(n)|\right)e^{-\mu n}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Thus,
F^{n}\to F
in
\Gamma
as
n\to\infty.
From proposition 2.1 in
[
17
]

we can apply Arzela-Ascoli to
F
as well. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\overline{\phi},\underline{\phi}\in\Gamma
be an ordered pair of upper and lower solutions of Eq. (
3.4
), satisfying

0\leq\underline{\phi}(t)\leq\overline{\phi}(t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then there exists at least one monotone traveling wave solution
\phi\in\Gamma
of Eq. (
3.4
) such that

\underline{\phi}(t)\leq\phi(t)\leq\overline{\phi}(t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.9
, the operator
F=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H
is continuous, compact, and order-preserving on
\Gamma
.
Since
\underline{\phi}
and
\overline{\phi}
are, respectively, lower and upper solutions, one has

\underline{\phi}\leq F(\underline{\phi})\leq F(\overline{\phi})\leq\overline{\phi}.

Therefore,
F
maps the nonempty, closed, convex, and bounded subset

\Gamma_{0}:=\{\phi\in\Gamma:\underline{\phi}\leq\phi\leq\overline{\phi}\}

into itself. The Schauder Fixed Point Theorem then guarantees the existence of a fixed point
\phi=F(\phi)
in
\Gamma_{0}
.
Consequently,
\phi
is a monotone traveling wave solution of Eq. (
3.4
).
∎

It is often difficult to construct a lower solution
\underline{\phi}\in\Gamma
satisfying all the required boundary conditions.
In this case, one may instead establish the following auxiliary lemma, which will be useful in constructing asymptotic bounds for traveling waves.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function such that
\phi^{\prime}(t)
is uniformly continuous and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi(t)=a

for some constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=0.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=\eta>0
.
Then there exists a sequence
\{t_{n}\}
with
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\phi^{\prime}(t_{n})\geq\eta/2
for all
n
.
By uniform continuity of
\phi^{\prime}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\phi^{\prime}(s)|<\eta/4

whenever
|t-s|<\delta
.
Hence,

\phi(t_{n}+\delta)-\phi(t_{n})=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\delta}\phi^{\prime}(s)\,ds\geq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\delta}\left(\phi^{\prime}(t_{n})-\frac{\eta}{4}\right)ds\geq\frac{\delta\eta}{4}>0.

contradicting the assumption that
\phi(t)\to a
as
t\to\infty
.
Therefore,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=0
.
∎

Remark
3.12
.

If
\phi\in BC^{3}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, then all its derivatives up to second order vanish at infinity, that is,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=0.

Indeed, since
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is uniformly continuous whenever
\phi\in BC^{3}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

3.11
applies.

Theorem 3.13
.

Assume that the conditions
(C1)
–
(C3)
hold.
If there exist an upper solution
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma
and a lower solution

\underline{\phi}
(not necessarily in
\Gamma
) of Eq. (
3.4
) such that

0\leq\underline{\phi}(t)\leq\phi(t)\leq\overline{\phi}(t),\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\underline{\phi}(t)=a,\qquad 0<a\leq 1,

then Eq. (
3.4
) admits a monotone traveling-wave solution

\phi\in\Gamma
connecting
0
and
1
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma
be an upper solution of (
3.4
) and let

\underline{\phi}
be a lower solution satisfying
0\leq\underline{\phi}\leq\overline{\phi}
and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\underline{\phi}(t)=a>0
.
Recall
F=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H
and that, by Lemma
3.9
,
F:\Gamma\to\Gamma
is order-preserving, continuous and compact.

Monotone iteration and precompactness:

Since
\overline{\phi}
is an upper solution we have
F(\overline{\phi})\leq\overline{\phi}
.
Because
\underline{\phi}
is a lower solution and
F
is order-preserving,
\underline{\phi}\leq F(\underline{\phi})
.
Applying
F
iteratively and using monotonicity gives, for every
n\geq 0
,

\underline{\phi}\leq F^{n}(\underline{\phi})\leq F^{n}(\overline{\phi})\leq\cdots\leq F(\overline{\phi})\leq\overline{\phi}.

In particular the sequence
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with
\phi_{n}:=F^{n}(\overline{\phi})
is monotone nonincreasing and bounded below by
\underline{\phi}
.
By compactness of
F
, the set
\{\phi_{n}\}
is precompact in the weighted space

; monotonicity therefore implies that
\phi_{n}
converges in the
\|\cdot\|_{\mu}
-norm to a limit
\phi\in B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. Passing to the limit in
\phi_{n+1}=F(\phi_{n})
and using continuity of
F
yields
F(\phi)=\phi
. Hence
\phi
is a fixed point of
F
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}\phi+H(\phi)=0\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},

so
\phi
is a classical profile of the traveling-wave equation.

Monotonicity and limits:

By construction
\phi
is nondecreasing and satisfies

0\leq\underline{\phi}(t)\leq\phi(t)\leq\overline{\phi}(t)\leq K,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence the one-sided limits exist; write

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\phi(t)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi(t)=:k\in[0,K].

Since
\phi\geq\underline{\phi}
and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\underline{\phi}(t)=a>0
, we have
k\geq a>0
.

Moreover, because
\phi=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H(\phi)
and
\mathcal{L}^{-1}:BC(\mathbb{R})\to BC^{3}(\mathbb{R})
(invertibility and regularity of
\mathcal{L}
on the chosen weighted spaces), it follows that
\phi\in BC^{3}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. By Lemma
3.11
and the succeeding remark the derivatives
\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
vanish at
+\infty
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)=0.

Excluding intermediate limits:

We claim
k=K
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
k\in(0,K)
.
Because
\phi_{t}\to\widehat{k}
(the constant history equal to
k
) in
C([-\tau,0])
as

we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}f_{c}(\phi_{t})=f_{c}(\widehat{k}).

Recalling the definition
H(\phi)(t)=f_{c}(\phi_{t})+\beta\phi(t)
, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}H(\phi)(t)=f_{c}(\widehat{k})+\beta k.

On the other hand, from
\mathcal{L}\phi+H(\phi)=0
and since

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\alpha c\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\phi^{\prime\prime}-c\phi^{\prime})(t)=0
(vanishing derivatives), we conclude

Taking limits in

This contradicts assumption (C1) which stipulates

for every constant history

with

Therefore

is a monotone traveling-wave solution of (

) connecting

and

.
∎

and
G(t-s)
is
C^{3}
there; all arising boundary terms are
well-defined as one-sided limits when
a
or
b
equals some
t_{i}
.)

Summing the previous identity over
i=0,\dots,m
yields

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)\,\mathcal{L}\phi(s)\,ds=B(t)-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(s)\,(\mathcal{L}G)(t-s)\,ds,

where
B(t)
denotes the sum of all boundary contributions at the finite endpoints

t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
(the contributions at
\pm\infty
vanish because
G
decays
exponentially and
\phi,\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
are bounded).

Since
\mathcal{L}G=\delta
, the last integral equals
\phi(t)
. Hence

I(t)=B(t)-\phi(t).

It remains to compute
B(t)
. Inspect a single interior point
t_{i}
. The boundary
terms arising from the neighboring intervals produce contributions involving
one-sided values of
\phi,\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime\prime}
at
t_{i}
. By the hypotheses the one-sided
values of
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
agree (or their differences cancel in the sum),
so the only net nonvanishing contribution at
t_{i}
stems from the jump of
\phi^{\prime}
.
Define the jump at
t_{i}
by

J_{i}:=\phi^{\prime}(t_{i}^{-})-\phi^{\prime}(t_{i}^{+}).

A straightforward collection of the boundary expressions shows that the net
contribution at
t_{i}
equals

\bigl(c\,G^{\prime}(t-t_{i})-D\,G(t-t_{i})\bigr)\,J_{i}.

Summing over
i
gives

B(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(c\,G^{\prime}(t-t_{i})-D\,G(t-t_{i})\bigr)\,J_{i}.

Combining the displayed identities and recalling
I(t)=-\int G(t-s)H(\phi)(s)\,ds

yields the claimed formula

-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(t-s)\,H(\phi)(s)\,ds=\phi(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(c\,G^{\prime}(t-t_{i})-D\,G(t-t_{i})\bigr)\,J_{i}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
\Gamma
and
F:=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H
be as above, and assume that the Green
function
G
of
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the exponential decay estimate

|G^{(k)}(x)|\leq C_{k}e^{-\gamma|x|}
for
k=0,1,2
and some constants
C_{k},\gamma>0
.
Let
\overline{\phi},\underline{\phi}\in\Gamma
be a supersolution and a subsolution,
respectively. Then the following regularity assertions hold and the ordering is preserved:

i)

F(\overline{\phi}),\,F(\underline{\phi})\in\Gamma\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

ii)

F^{2}(\overline{\phi}),\,F^{2}(\underline{\phi})\in\Gamma\cap W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

iii)

F^{3}(\overline{\phi}),\,F^{3}(\underline{\phi})\in\Gamma\cap BC^{3}(\mathbb{R})
.

In each case the inequalities (supersolution
\geq F(\cdot)\geq
subsolution, etc.)
remain valid.

Proof.

We first recall the standing hypotheses used below:

•

\mathcal{L}\phi=c\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\phi^{\prime\prime}-c\phi^{\prime}-\beta\phi
is the linear operator introduced in (5.5), with
\beta>0
.

•

G
denotes the Green kernel of
\mathcal{L}
, so that
\mathcal{L}G=\delta
, and we assume the exponential decay / integrability property

G,G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad|G^{(k)}(x)|\leq C_{k}e^{-\gamma|x|},\ k=0,1,2.

•

H:\Gamma\to BC(\mathbb{R})
is the nonlinear operator
H(\phi)(t)=f_{c}(\phi_{t})+\beta\phi(t)
; by (C2),(C3) we have
H(\phi)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for every
\phi\in\Gamma
.

•

Lemma
3.7
(proved earlier) yields
G(\xi)<0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, hence the convolution operator
-\mathcal{L}^{-1}
is order-preserving on
BC(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma
be a supersolution (the argument for a subsolution is analogous). Define

F(\overline{\phi})(t):=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}H(\overline{\phi})(t)\;=\;-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s)\,H(\overline{\phi})(s)\,ds.

We prove that
F(\overline{\phi})\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
F(\overline{\phi})\in\Gamma
.

By hypothesis
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma\subset BC(\mathbb{R},[0,K])
and by (C2),(C3)
the composite
H(\overline{\phi})
is bounded; set
M:=\|H(\overline{\phi})\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
Since
G\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the convolution defining
F(\overline{\phi})
is well defined
and satisfies

\|F(\overline{\phi})\|_{\infty}\leq\|G\|_{L^{1}}\,\|H(\overline{\phi})\|_{\infty}\leq\|G\|_{L^{1}}M.

Because
G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
H(\overline{\phi})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
we may differentiate under the integral sign (dominated convergence). For almost every
t
,

(F(\overline{\phi}))^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G^{\prime}(t-s)\,H(\overline{\phi})(s)\,ds,

(F(\overline{\phi}))^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}G^{\prime\prime}(t-s)\,H(\overline{\phi})(s)\,ds.

Taking suprema and using Young’s (convolution) estimate yields the uniform bounds

\|(F(\overline{\phi}))^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|G^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\,M,\qquad\|(F(\overline{\phi}))^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\,M.

Hence
F(\overline{\phi})\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, as required.

Because
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma
is nondecreasing with

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\overline{\phi}(t)=0
,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\overline{\phi}(t)=K
,
the history map
t\mapsto\overline{\phi}_{t}
satisfies
\overline{\phi}_{t}\to\hat{0}
as
t\to-\infty

and
\overline{\phi}_{t}\to\hat{K}
as
t\to+\infty
. By continuity of
f_{c}
we deduce

\lim_{t\to-\infty}H(\overline{\phi})(t)=H_{-}=f_{c}(\hat{0})+\beta\cdot 0=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}H(\overline{\phi})(t)=H_{+}=f_{c}(\hat{K})+\beta K=\beta K.

Since
H(\overline{\phi})\in L^{\infty}
and
G\in L^{1}
, dominated convergence gives

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}F(\overline{\phi})(t)=-\Bigl(\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}H(\overline{\phi})(s)\Bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(y)\,dy.

It remains to evaluate
\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(y)\,dy
. Integrating the identity

\mathcal{L}G=\delta
over
\mathbb{R}
and using that boundary terms arising from total
derivatives vanish because of the exponential decay of
G
and its derivatives, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{L}G(y)\,dy=-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta(y)\,dy=1,

hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(y)\,dy=-\frac{1}{\beta}.

Therefore

\lim_{t\to-\infty}F(\overline{\phi})(t)=-H_{-}\int G=-0\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{1}{\beta}\Bigr)=0,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}F(\overline{\phi})(t)=-H_{+}\int G=-(\beta K)\Bigl(-\frac{1}{\beta}\Bigr)=K.

Thus
F(\overline{\phi})
attains the correct boundary values and so lies in the same
range of asymptotic states as
\Gamma
.

By hypothesis (C2)/(5.4) the map
H
is order-preserving on
\Gamma
(i.e.
\phi\leq\psi

implies
H(\phi)\leq H(\psi)
). Since
G(\xi)<0
for all
\xi
, the linear map

h\mapsto-\int G(\cdot-s)h(s)\,ds=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}h
is order-preserving on
BC(\mathbb{R})
.
Hence
F=-\mathcal{L}^{-1}\circ H
is order-preserving, so
F(\overline{\phi})
is nondecreasing
whenever
\overline{\phi}
is. Combining monotonicity with the limits from Step 3 we conclude

F(\overline{\phi})\in\Gamma\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

The same argument applies to any subsolution
\underline{\phi}
. This completes the proof of part (i).
Parts (ii)–(iii) follow by the standard bootstrap: once
F(\phi)\in W^{2,\infty}
the history
map
t\mapsto(F\phi)_{t}
is continuous in
C([-\tau,0])
, so
H(F\phi)
is continuous and bounded;
differentiating one more time under the integral gives
F^{2}(\phi)\in W^{3,\infty}
, etc.
∎

4.
Applications

We illustrate the abstract results with the delayed logistic nonclassical model.
Consider the PDE with a discrete delay in the reaction term:

(4.1)

\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+\alpha\frac{\partial^{3}u(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}\partial t}+u(x,t-\tau)\bigl(1-u(x,t)\bigr),

with
D>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and delay
\tau\geq 0
. In the sequel we set
\alpha=1
.
Seeking traveling waves
u(x,t)=\phi(\xi)
,
\xi=x+ct
, and writing
r=c\tau
, one obtains the profile equation

(4.2)

c\,\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\phi^{\prime}(\xi)+\phi(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\phi(\xi)\bigr)=0.

Linearizing (
4.2
) at the trivial equilibrium
\phi\equiv 0
(so that

a:=f^{\prime}(\hat{0})=1
) and seeking exponential solutions
\phi(\xi)=e^{\lambda\xi}
yields the non delayed characteristic equation

(4.3)

\Delta_{r}(\lambda)=c\lambda^{3}+D\lambda^{2}-c\lambda+e^{-\lambda r}=0,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

which reduces at
r=0
to the cubic polynomial

\Delta_{0}(\lambda)=c\lambda^{3}+D\lambda^{2}-c\lambda+1.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
c>0,D>0
, then
\Delta_{0}(\lambda)
has one negative and two positive real roots. Furthermore, if
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
and there is some
\varepsilon>0
where
\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon<\lambda_{2}
, then
\Delta_{0}(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon)<0

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{-}
be the negative root, the by the factor theorem we can write

\Delta_{0}(\lambda)=c(\lambda-\lambda_{-})(\lambda-\lambda_{1})(\lambda-\lambda_{2}).

Now, defining
\varepsilon
such that
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon<\lambda_{2},
we see

(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon)-\lambda_{-}>0,\quad(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon)-\lambda_{1}=\varepsilon>0,\quad(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon)-\lambda_{2}<0,

so

\Delta_{0}(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon)=(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon-\lambda_{-})(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon-\lambda_{2})<0.

∎

The construction of sub- and super-solutions follows the classical template with two modifications:
(i) the nonlinear term acts on shifted argument
\phi(\cdot-r)

\Delta_{0}
.

We first record two elementary lemmas for

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix

and suppose

is small, then
\Delta_{r}(\eta)
has one negative and two positive real roots. Furthermore, if

then

\lim_{r\to 0}\eta_{1}(r)=\lambda_{1},\lim_{r\to 0}\eta_{2}(r)=\lambda_{2}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
c>0,D>0
and
r>0
small enough. Let
0<\eta_{1}<\eta_{2}
be the two distinct positive real roots of
\Delta_{r}
and choose any
\varepsilon>0
with
\eta_{1}+\varepsilon<\eta_{2}
. Then there exists

(depending on

) such that for all

\Delta_{r}(\eta_{1}+\varepsilon)<0.

Proof.

The proof follows from lemmas
4.2
and
4.1
.
∎

Fix a small
\varepsilon>0
and choose

.

Lemma 4.4
.

The nonlinear part of (
4.2
) defined as
f_{c}(\phi)\phi(-r)=\left(1-\phi(0)\right)
satisfies
C(1)-C(3).

Proof.

C1
,
C3

are trivial. Thus, we show the second part of

\begin{split}&f_{c}(\phi_{1})-f_{c}(\phi_{2})=\phi_{1}(-r)\left(1-\phi_{1}(0)\right)-\phi_{2}(-r)\left(1-\phi_{2}(0)\right)\\
&\geq\phi_{1}(-r)\left(1-\phi_{1}(0)\right)-\phi_{1}(-r)\left(1-\phi_{2}(0)\right)\\
&=\phi_{1}(-r)\left[1-\phi_{1}(0)-\left(1-\phi_{2}(0)\right)\right]\\
&=-\phi_{1}(-r)\left(\phi_{1}(0)-\phi_{2}(0)\right)\geq-\left(\phi_{1}(0)-\phi_{2}(0)\right).\end{split}

Taking

We now construct the a pair of piecewise exponential quasi upper- and quasi lower-solutions.

Quasi upper-solution.
Define

\overline{\phi}(\xi):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}e^{\eta_{1}\xi},&\xi<0,\\[4.0pt]
1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\eta_{1}\xi},&\xi\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
\eta_{1}
is the smallest positive root of
\Delta_{1}(\eta)=0
. Since
\eta_{1}>0
we have
\overline{\phi}\in\Gamma
and
\overline{\phi}
is
C^{\infty}
on each side of
\xi=0
and

at

.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
r\in[0,r_{0}]
as above and for
c
chosen so that
\Delta_{r}
has the two positive roots, the function
\overline{\phi}
is a supersolution of (
4.2
), i.e.

c\,\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigr)\leq 0

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
(understood in the usual piecewise sense).

Proof.

It is clear that

\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\overline{\phi}(\xi)=0,\quad\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\overline{\phi}(\xi)=1.

Thus, we only have to show the inequality. To this end, we look at the following cases.

Case 1

\xi<0:
we have
\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)=\frac{e^{\eta_{1}(\xi-r)}}{2}
. Using
\Delta_{r}(\eta_{1})=0
we compute

c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)=\Delta_{r}(\eta_{1})e^{\eta_{1}\xi}=0,

hence

\begin{split}&c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&\qquad=-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\leq 0.\end{split}

Case 2

0<\xi<r:
we have
\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)=\frac{e^{\eta_{1}(\xi-r)}}{2},\overline{\phi}(\xi)=1-\frac{e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}}{2}.

\begin{split}A&=c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\frac{e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}}{2}\left[\left(c\eta_{1}^{3}-D\eta_{1}^{2}-c\eta_{1}\right)+\frac{e^{\eta_{1}(\xi-r)}}{2}\right]\\
.\end{split}

Since
A(r)
is continuous and
\xi\in[0,r)
we see,

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}A&=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}}{2}\left[\left(c\eta_{1}^{3}-D\eta_{1}^{2}-c\eta_{1}\right)+\frac{e^{\eta_{1}(\xi-r)}}{2}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(c\lambda_{1}^{3}-D\lambda_{1}^{2}-c\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(c\lambda_{1}^{3}-D\lambda_{1}^{2}+\left(-c\lambda_{1}^{3}-D\lambda_{1}^{2}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{4}<0.\end{split}

Thus, there exists some
r^{*}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,r^{*}],
then
A(r)\leq 0.

Case 3

\xi\geq r.
We use the fact that
\overline{\phi}(\xi)
is non decreasing to see

\begin{split}A&=c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&\leq c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}\left(c\eta_{1}^{3}-D\eta_{1}^{2}-c\eta_{1}+1\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}\left(c\eta_{1}^{3}-D\eta_{1}^{2}-\left(c\eta_{1}^{3}+D\eta_{1}^{2}+e^{-\eta_{1}r}\right)+1\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}\left(-2D\eta_{1}^{2}+1-e^{-\eta_{1}r}\right).\end{split}

Using a limit argument as case 2 we see,

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}A&=\lim_{r\to 0}c\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}+D\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}-c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}+\overline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\overline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{2}e^{-\eta_{1}\xi}\left(-2D\eta_{1}^{2}+1-e^{-\eta_{1}r}\right)\\
&=-De^{-\lambda_{1}\xi}<0.\\
\end{split}

Thus, there exists some
r^{**}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,r^{**}],
then
A(r)\leq 0.

Lemma 4.6
.

With
\varepsilon>0
as in Lemma

sufficiently small, there exists

and

\underline{\phi}

c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\underline{\phi}(\xi)\bigl(1-\underline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigr)\geq 0

We notice that

\begin{split}&B=c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\underline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\underline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\
&\geq c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\underline{\phi}(\xi-r)-\underline{\phi}^{2}(\xi).\end{split}

Again, we show the inequalities in cases.

Case 3

\xi_{1}+r<\xi
This follows directly, since

\begin{split}&B=c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)+D\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-c\,\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(\xi)+\underline{\phi}(\xi-r)\bigl(1-\underline{\phi}(\xi)\bigr)\\

AMPK agonism optimizes the in vivo activation and antileukemic efficacy of chimeric antigen receptor T cells
Authors: Erica Braverman, Mengtao Qin, Elisabet Ampudia-Mesias, Herbert Tres Schuler, Harrison Brown, Lukas Murdych, Ann Titus, Allison MacLean, Bhavya Kanagala, Christopher Wittmann, Archana Ramgopal, Felicia Kemp, Steven J Mullet, Aaron Yang, Amanda C Poholek, Stacy Gelhaus, Craig Byersdorfer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells have achieved remarkable clinical success. However, up to 50% of patients with CAR T-cell treated leukemia relapse and long-term survivor data indicate that CAR T cell persistence is key to enforcing extended, relapse-free survival. Unfortunately, ex vivo expansion protocols often drive metabolic and functional exhaustion, reducing in vivo efficacy. Preclinical models have demonstrated that redirecting metabolism can improve in vivo T-cell function. Here, we hypothesized that exposure to an agonist targeting AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) would create CAR T cells capable of increased in vivo function and enhanced leukemia clearance.MethodsCAR T cells were generated from healthy human donor T cells via lentiviral transduction, followed by exposure to either Compound 991 or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) for 96 hours. During and after agonist treatment, T cells were harvested for metabolic and functional assessments. To test in vivo efficacy, immunodeficient mice were injected with luciferase+NALM6 leukemia cells, and 1 week later with 991- versus DMSO-expanded CAR T cells. Leukemia burden and antileukemia efficacy were assessed via radiance imaging and overall survival.ResultsCompound 991 treatment activated AMPK without limiting cellular expansion, and increased both mitochondrial density and handling of reactive oxygen species. Mechanistically, 991 treatment mimicked nutrient starvation, with increased autophagy and generation of mitochondrially protective metabolites. Importantly, receipt of 991-exposed CAR Ts significantly improved in vivo leukemia clearance and prolonged recipient survival, likely as a result of elevated activation and increased CD4+T cell yields at early times post-injection.DiscussionEx vivo expansion is necessary to generate sufficient cell numbers for in vivo administration, but sustained activation and differentiation often negatively impact in vivo persistence and function. Here, we demonstrate that promoting AMPK activity during in vitro CAR T expansion metabolically reprograms cells without limiting T cell yield, increases early activation following in vivo transfer, and ultimately enhances anti-leukemia efficacy. Importantly, Compound 991 treatment achieves these results without further modifying the expansion media, changing the CAR T construct, or genetically altering the cells. Together, these data highlight AMPK agonism as a potent and readily translatable approach to improve the metabolic profile and in vivo efficacy of adoptively transferred T cells.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

Our studies demonstrate that treatment with a direct AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) agonist metabolically reprograms CAR T cells in vitro, leading to profound improvements in in vivo efficacy. Mechanistically, our findings identified significant metabolic changes in agonist-treated CAR T cells during in vitro expansion, with increased early activation and a subsequent decrease in exhaustion when treated cells were transferred in vivo. Importantly, these changes were achieved without further genetic manipulations of the CAR T cells, modifications to the culture media, or impairment of in vitro expansion.

Introduction

Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR T) therapy has had a remarkable impact on treating relapsed/refractory acute B-cell lymphoblastic leukemia, where more than 90% of treated pediatric patients initially achieve remission.1 However, despite the success of this adoptive cellular therapy, 50% of patients relapse following CAR T treatment, limiting its utility as a long-term cure.2 3 Further, CAR Ts have seen limited success in other cancer types, particularly solid tumors. While the reasons for this limited efficacy are many, one of the prominent concerns relates to the functional status of the injected CAR T cells. Ex vivo expansion drives significant activation and differentiation of CAR Ts, limiting their ability to form memory populations and negatively impacting their in vivo persistence and function.4 This combination results in leukemia relapse and restricted tumor clearance.5 Thus, identifying methods to augment the persistence and in vivo function in CAR Ts has become critical to improving their therapeutic efficacy.

Many interventions have shown promise towards improving CAR T persistence and function. On the one hand, generating less differentiated, more memory-like CAR Ts has seen great effect, achieved by driving expression of memory transcription factors, blocking differentiation pathways, and changing the cytokine milieu of the growth media.611 It has also become clear that targeting CAR T cell metabolism, for example, by specifically augmenting respiratory capacity, is another method to improve long-term survival. In the past, metabolic reprogramming has been achieved by restricting access to certain nutrients during expansion (essentially enforcing a “starvation” program), blocking specific metabolic pathways, or driving expression of mitochondrially focused genes to augment mitochondrial health and capacity.41219 Many of these approaches, however, have limited translatability to the clinic. For example, nutrient restriction (ie, by blocking glycolysis) slows CAR T proliferation and results in fewer CAR T cells for in vivo transfer. Further, the need to re-engineer expansion media, by removing or adding specific nutrients, poses both financial and logistical barriers. Finally, while overexpressing or underexpressing certain metabolic genes in CAR Ts has been effective in mouse models, this added genetic manipulation raises concerns about the oncologic potential of modified CAR Ts, slowing their path to translation. Even without these barriers, the wide array of modifiable pathways raises the question as to which options will create the most optimal CAR T cell product—and how best to achieve many of those goals simultaneously. Put simply, identifying an optimized strategy that requires minimal manufacturing changes while simultaneously promoting multiple advantageous pathways is paramount to achieving more effective CAR T cells.

AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) is a heterotrimeric cellular energy sensor upstream of a web of metabolic outputs.20 It is best known for recognizing nutrient restriction and subsequently reprogramming cellular metabolism towards catabolic energy generation while reducing anabolic growth. We have previously demonstrated that driving AMPK activity in human T cells augments mitochondrial capacity, memory formation, and inflammatory function.21 In addition, many targets of AMPK have been highlighted as advantageous during ex vivo CAR T cell expansion, including nutrient restriction, blockade of the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), counteracting reactive oxygen species (ROS), promoting mitochondrial biogenesis, and enhancing autophagy.68 9 11 12 1519 22 With AMPK upstream of so many beneficial metabolic programs, we hypothesized that facilitating AMPK signaling during ex vivo expansion would create a metabolically superior CAR T product.

Detailed below, we highlight the novel use of a direct AMPK agonist, Compound 991, to metabolically re-program human T cells. Exposing T cells to Compound 991, which binds directly to the AMPK heterotrimer,23 created metabolically augmented cells with significantly improved in vivo antileukemia activity. Interestingly, 991 exposure did not drive memory reprogramming but instead orchestrated a network of metabolic changes including increased autophagic flux, enhanced fatty acid oxidation (FAO), favorable redox balancing, and generation of mitochondrially-protective metabolites. Further, 991 treatment created CAR Ts with enhanced early activation and subsequent elevation of CD4+T cell numbers in vivo. In total, these studies highlight the potential for short-term, direct AMPK agonism to rewire the metabolic capacity of CAR Ts, providing an easily translatable method to simultaneously modify multiple beneficial pathways to improve CAR T therapy.

Results

991 treatment facilitates AMPK activity without restricting expansion

We hypothesized that expanding CAR T cells in the presence of a direct AMPK agonist would metabolically optimize them for in vivo function. To test this hypothesis, we first interrogated whether Compound 991 treatment activated AMPK without restricting growth or cell viability. Human T cells were isolated, stimulated with anti-CD3/CD8 Dynabeads for 5 days, and split into control (dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)) and 991-treated groups. The AMPK heterotrimer is active when the alpha subunit is phosphorylated on Thr172,24 and dosing experiments indicated stable phosphorylation of AMPKα Thr172 for~48 hours following 991 exposure. This finding led to a final treatment schedule where 991 was added to T-cell cultures in two 48 hours cycles (96 hours of total exposure), followed by a 48-hour washout period (figure 1A). This treatment reliably increased AMPK activation (figure 1B), with no significant impact on cell growth or expansion through day 11 (figure 1C). To assess whether equivalent cell numbers indicated similar proliferation or a combination of proliferative differences with changes in cell survival, we measured T-cell proliferation via incorporation of the thymidine analog bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU). In line with our expansion data, there was no difference in BrdU uptake on day 9, following 96 hours of agonist treatment (figure 1D). Interestingly, when BrdU incorporation was measured at the end of the culture period (day 11), there was now a significant increase in 991-treated cell proliferation (figure 1E). Given AMPK’s well-documented roles in optimizing metabolic fitness, we hypothesized this ongoing cell turnover was due to enhanced metabolic capacity, which then allowed for a sustained proliferative effort despite an increasing distance from the original Dynabead stimulation. We therefore sought to measure the impact of 991 exposure on subsequent metabolic reprogramming.

991-treated human T cells gain mitochondrial capacity and efficiency through an AMPK-dependent mechanism

To start, we used the Seahorse Metabolic Analyzer Mito Stress Test to gauge the impact of 991 treatment on T-cell metabolism. On day 11 (48 hours post 991 removal), 991-treated cells increased their oxygen consumption rates (OCR) and spare respiratory capacity (SRC) (figure 2A). We hypothesized these increases might be secondary to an increase in total mitochondria, particularly as AMPK is known to activate peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-γ coactivator (PGC1α), a transcription factor responsible for promoting mitochondrial biogenesis. Staining with MitoTracker revealed increased mitochondrial density in 991-treated cells (figure 2B), which correlated with elevated PGC1α expression during 991 treatment (figure 2C). To better understand if these metabolic changes would persist following subsequent stimulation, we restimulated cells on day 11 and repeated our metabolic assessments (figure 2D). As shown in figure 2E, augmented mitochondrial activity continued following activation, with increases in both OCR and SRC. Of note, heightened mitochondrial metabolism can also generate increased levels of ROS, which are damaging to cells at high levels. Reassuringly, enhanced AMPK signaling improved ROS handling, which we hypothesized was likely contributing to the ability of 991-treated cells to better tolerate increased mitochondrial respiration. Consistent with this interpretation, 991-pretreated cells had lower ROS burden following 24 hours of stimulation (figure 2F), and greater numbers of 991-treated cells were recovered after 72 hours of restimulation (figure 2G), consistent with increased cellular proliferation in light of improved ROS handling.

To explain the lower cellular ROS burden, despite an upregulated OCR, we investigated the antioxidant capacity of 991-treated cells, as AMPK is known to upregulate anti-oxidants including thioredoxin 1.2527 In our hands, 48 hours of Compound 991 treatment increased thioredoxin 1 protein expression two- to threefold (figure 2H). Importantly, the timing of this thioredoxin 1 increase coincided with the noted reduction in ROS levels, suggesting that Compound 991 treatment counteracts ROS overproduction through AMPK-mediated upregulation in antioxidants, specifically thioredoxin 1.

To confirm the AMPK dependence of 991-mediated metabolic changes, murine T cells genetically lacking both isoforms of the AMPKα subunit (hereafter AMPK knock-out (KO)) were treated with Compound 991 on the same treatment schedule as human T cells, followed by measurement of respiratory capacity on the Seahorse metabolic analyzer. As expected, wild-type murine T cells increased basal OCR, maximal OCR, and SRC levels following 991 treatment (online supplemental figure 1). In contrast, AMPK KO T cells had a muted respiratory response, with lower basal OCR levels and an inability to recover OCR or SRC values to the level of wild-type T cells in the presence of 991. Finally, we tested the longevity of respiratory changes following 991 treatment. Seahorse analyses performed on in vitro propagated cells 14, 17, and 20 days post-Dynabead stimulation (representing 5, 8, and 11 days post-agonist removal) demonstrated no sustained increase in oxygen consumption following 991 treatment (online supplemental figure 2), highlighting the temporal nature of 991-mediated respiratory changes, at least in vitro. Altogether, these data demonstrate that 991 treatment facilitates mitochondrial biogenesis and enhances mitochondrial function through an AMPK-dependent mechanism, allowing for temporal increases in metabolic capacity and improved cellular expansion on re-stimulation.

AMPK agonist treatment improves CAR T antileukemia activity and prolongs survival in a xenograft model

With data supporting the improved metabolic fitness of agonist-treated T cells, we next tested whether 991 pretreatment improved the function of CAR T cells targeting leukemia. Human CAR T cells, using a CD19-targeting CAR construct with CD28 co-stimulation (figure 3A), were generated via lentiviral transduction and expanded with or without AMPK agonist exposure (figure 3B). We first confirmed enhanced metabolic capacity following 991 treatment. 991-treated CAR Ts at rest, as well as following overnight activation with CD19+NALM6 leukemia cells, enhanced both their mitochondrial capacity (figure 3C,D) and their extracellular acidification rates (online supplemental figure 3A,B). To measure in vivo CAR T-cell efficacy, we transferred 1e6 luciferase-expressing NALM6 cells into immunodeficient NSG mice, followed 1 week later by 3e6 CAR T cells (figure 3E). Standard DMSO-treated CAR T cells delayed leukemia growth compared to leukemia-only controls, but all DMSO-treated CAR T cell recipients eventually succumbed to lethal leukemia. In sharp contrast, 991-treated CAR Ts further enhanced leukemia control, with 6/11 (54%) of 991-treated CAR T recipients remaining leukemia-free through the end of the experiment (figure 3F). This improved leukemia clearance led to a significant and reproducible improvement in recipient survival, with 73% of 991-treated CAR T cell recipients surviving until day 70 (figure 3G). Together, these data highlight that expanding human CAR Ts in the presence of Compound 991, creates a CAR T cell product with a striking improvement in leukemia clearance and recipient survival in a preclinical leukemia model.

991 treatment upregulates cell cycle and metabolic gene sets in vitro, without inducing changes in memory or exhaustion markers

Multiple groups have demonstrated that memory-like phenotypes improve antileukemia activity in vivo.11 15 However, we found no differences in memory phenotype or exhaustion status in our 991-treated cells (online supplemental figure 4A-D). Since AMPK signaling can also impact cellular transcriptomes, including through both direct activation of transcription factors as well as downstream influence on histone deacetylases, we sequenced RNA from day 11 DMSO-treated versus 991-treated human T cells. Only a handful of transcripts were significantly altered in either CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, using a p value of <0.05 and log2-fold change of 0.6 (online supplemental figure 5). However, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) uncovered multiple upregulated pathways, with the highest enrichment scores clustering within cell proliferation and cell cycle pathways for both CD4 and CD8 T cells (figure 4A,B), consistent with the higher proliferative rates observed in vitro (figure 1E). The second most-enriched gene sets highlighted proliferation-supporting metabolic pathways, including pyrimidine and folate metabolism (figure 4C,D), as well as a notable enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation and FAO gene sets. The latter changes are consistent with the increased oxidative capacity of 991-treated cells (figure 2A–C) and suggest that increases in cell cycle may result from the enhanced metabolic capacity of 991-treated cells. We further hypothesized that metabolic rewiring was likely responsible for the functional advantage of 991 treatment and therefore sought to understand how AMPK was directing metabolism in T cells to achieve such impressive results.

AMPK agonism simultaneously drives fatty acid oxidation while promoting generation of mitochondrially-protective metabolites

AMPK is well-known for its role in supporting FAO and long-chain fatty acid (LC-FA) metabolism. LC-FAs can bind directly to AMPK to facilitate its activity, using the same binding site as Compound 991.28 We therefore hypothesized that T cells treated with 991 might increase their FAO. Using the oxidation-sensitive dye, FAO-blue, we observed increased FAO activity in agonist-treated T cells (figure 5A). This upregulation correlated with a greater sensitivity to etomoxir, the carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A (CPT1A) inhibitor, read out as a steeper reduction in protein translation following etomoxir treatment of agonist-treated cells (figure 5B). We next stained cells for lipid droplets, which serve as storage depots for FA intermediates. In line with increased FAO, agonist-treated cells demonstrated reduced staining with the lipid-sensitive dye Nile Red, indicating decreased lipid reserves (figure 5C). Agonist-treated cells also increased expression of CPT1A (figure 5D), the enzyme facilitating transport of LC-FAs into the mitochondria for subsequent beta oxidation. However, despite such significantly upregulated FAO, there was no difference in ROS generation in these cells, as measured by CellROX staining (online supplemental figure 6).

Mass spectrometry analysis of intracellular metabolites on day nine further identified increased abundance of vitamin B5 (figure 5E) and carnitine (figure 5F) in 991-treated cells, intermediates necessary for generating fatty-acyl-Coenzyme A (CoA) moieties and transporting them across the mitochondrial membrane, respectively. Day 9 metabolite data also noted upregulation of multiple amino acids (AAs) known to play a role in mitochondrial health and fitness, including proline, glycine, and leucine (figure 5G–I).2933 Precursors of these AAs, such as glutamate, aspartate, and threonine, were conversely decreased (figure 5J–L), suggesting that AMPK specifically directs production of mitochondrially protective AAs. Altogether, these metabolic data highlight AMPK’s roles, both in promoting FAO, but also in augmenting production of metabolites which maintain mitochondrial health and support the desired metabolic programming.

AMPK agonism mimics cellular starvation and upregulates autophagy to enhance metabolic fitness

Some of the earliest literature aimed at improving T-cell fitness highlighted the utility of blocking glycolysis during cellular expansion.4 With GSEA highlighting enriched glycolytic datasets in 991-treated cells (figure 4E–F), we sought to understand the role of glycolysis downstream of AMPK. As AMPK is known to promote glucose uptake, we first quantified glucose levels in the media after 48 hours of culture. In contrast to an expected AMPK-mediated increase in glucose uptake, we regularly found more glucose remaining in the media of 991-treated cultures compared with DMSO-treated controls (figure 6A). Supporting this lack of glycolytic activity, media from 991-treated cultures also exhibited reduced lactate content (figure 6B), with a reduction in intracellular hexoses in 991-treated cells (figure 6C). However, the full intracellular metabolite analysis painted a different picture, as here we saw elevated levels of intracellular lactate (figure 6D). Combined with reduced lactate in the media, these data suggest that 991-treated cells are continuing to undergo glycolysis, but retain lactate intracellularly instead of secreting it. Interestingly, lactate build-up itself has been demonstrated to reduce cellular glucose uptake,34 which may explain the lack of increased glucose uptake following agonist treatment. With carbohydrate metabolism ongoing, despite reduced glucose uptake, we sought to understand where cells were sourcing their sugar carbons. To do this, we performed pathway analysis on untargeted metabolite data to delineate pathway changes in our cells. Interestingly, the four most significantly upregulated metabolic pathways following 991 treatment concerned the breakdown of alternative sugar sources, including glycogen (figure 6E–F). Pathways involving nucleotide metabolism and mitochondrial performance were also highlighted, supporting our GSEA results and the observed metabolic activity of agonist-treated cells. Importantly, both an increased reliance on intracellular sugar breakdown, and elevated lactate retention, are in line with a nutrient starvation response. If enhanced AMPK signaling were indeed promoting a starvation response, we would expect to find an increase in autophagic flux, as cells looked for a way to break down additional energy sources. Such a finding would be of particular interest since increased autophagy, in the setting of nutrient restriction, enforces metabolic efficiency in T cells during in vitro expansion.35

In addition to lactate retention,36 AMPK is also a well-known driver of autophagy, both by activating Unc-51 like kinase 1 (ULK1) and by restricting mTOR-mediated ULK inhibition through phosphorylation of the mTORC1 complex protein, regulatory-associated protein of mTOR, Raptor (figure 6G). As AMPK is known to target Raptor for phosphorylation-dependent degradation,37 we first measured total and phosphorylated Raptor levels. Raptor phosphorylation significantly increased, while total Raptor protein levels significantly decreased, in 991-treated cells (figure 6H). Without a fully functional mTORC1 complex to signal AA availability, 991-treated cells also reduced their translational activity (figure 6I),38 an independent and further mechanism to improve in vivo T-cell function.39 Meanwhile, 991 treatment also increased phosphorylation of ULK1 (figure 6J), concomitant with an increase in cellular autophagy (figure 6K). Together, these data suggest that direct AMPK agonism drives cellular programming reminiscent of a response to nutrient starvation, increases intracellular energy sources via enhanced autophagy, and reduces high energy expenditure by limiting protein translation.

Improved leukemia control correlates with the increased activation, elevated number, and decreased exhaustion of 991-treated CD4+ CAR T cells

Our data suggest that AMPK agonism metabolically reprograms cells towards pathways of cellular fitness. We thus hypothesized that improved leukemia clearance might result from either enhanced initial CAR T activation and expansion and/or prolonged in vivo persistence and function. Importantly, recent clinical data has highlighted the importance of CAR T cell persistence, particularly within the CD4+compartment, as a mediator of effective long-term, leukemia-free survival.3 To investigate the etiology of improved leukemia clearance, we repeated our leukemia dosing experiments, but sacrificed a cohort of mice at either day 3 or day 5–7 (1 week) post-CAR T injection (figure 7A). Recipients were injected with BrdU just prior to cell collection, to measure the active proliferation of transferred CAR T cells. And T cells were enumerated from the bone marrow (BM), where NALM6 leukemia cells first expand, as well as from the spleen. There were no differences in CAR T cell proliferation in the BM at either day 3 or 1 week post-injection (online supplemental figure 7A). And recipients of both DMSO- and 991-treated CAR T cells demonstrated equivalent and total leukemia clearance in the BM by day 3 onward (online supplemental figure 8). We did note a transient proliferation of DMSO-treated CAR T cells in recipient spleens on day 3, a finding that disappeared by 1 week (online supplemental figure 7B). Yet despite this increase in BrdU positivity, there was no difference in total DMSO-treated T-cell numbers in the spleen or BM at this time (online supplemental figure 7C). In contrast, there was a significant increase in 991-treated CD4+CAR T cells in both the BM and spleen by 1 week (figure 7B), with a trend towards increased CD8 T cells in the spleen at this same time. This CD4+specific increase elevated the CD4/CD8 ratio in week 1, which increased further by week 2 (figure 7C). Importantly, pre-injection CD4/CD8 ratios were equivalent between DMSO-treated versus 991-treated CAR T products (online supplemental figure 9).

To better understand the mechanism underlying improved leukemia clearance, we conducted further analysis on day 7 post-injection. Intriguingly, despite the metabolic changes observed in 991-treated cells prior to injection, no differences were found in mitochondrial levels, cellular ROS, autophagy, or apoptosis on day 7 in vivo (online supplemental figure 10). To determine whether the numerical advantage of day 7 CD4 T cells continued long-term, we recovered cells from the spleens of animals 23 days post-CAR T-cell injection, just prior to the onset of relapse in DMSO-treated cell recipients (figure 7D). At this time, neither 991-treated CD4+ nor CD8+ CAR T cells exerted a numerical advantage over their DMSO-treated counterparts. However, 991-treated CD4+T cells displayed a 30% reduction in TIM3+cells and strong trend towards decreased levels of both Lymphocyte Activation Gene 3 positive (LAG3+) and T cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-containing protein 3/Programmed Cell Death -1 (TIM3/PD-1) double-positive cells (figure 7E–G), suggestive of a less exhausted phenotype. Furthermore, this finding occurred despite equivalent levels of exhaustion markers on CD4+T cells prior to injection (online supplemental figure 3C).

Finally, we interrogated early events post-injection and first investigated whether 991-treated cells were preferentially recruited more aggressively to the BM, given a known impact of AMPK signaling on chemokine receptor expression and cell invasion.40 41 However, both CD4+ and CD8+ CAR T-cell numbers were equivalent in the BM between DMSO and 991-treated groups at 20 hours post-injection (online supplemental figure 11A). Hypothesizing that accelerated early activation might increase later cell numbers, we measured the activation status of CD4+ and CD8+ CAR T cells at 20 hours post-injection (figure 7H). As shown in figure 7I–L (and online supplemental figure 10B, C), 991-pretreatment resulted in marked upregulation of multiple activation markers on BM CD4+CAR T cells including CD25, CD98, 4-1BB, and PD-1. There was also a 23% increase in CD98 median fluorescence intensity (MFI) in CD4+CAR T cells (online supplemental figure 10B), with a strong trend towards enhanced CD25 MFI staining (online supplemental figure 10C). In the spleen at 20 hours, only CD25 expression increased between 991-treated and DMSO-treated populations and only in CD4+ CAR T cells (online supplemental figure 11D). Importantly, this spleen versus BM activation dichotomy is wholly consistent with CAR T cells first encountering antigen-bearing Nalm6-leukemia cells in the BM in this model. In contrast to the widespread upregulation of activation markers on CD4+CAR T cells, only PD-1 expression increased on CD8 CAR T cells, and only in the BM at 20 hours (figure 7L right), with all remaining activation markers demonstrating comparable expression in CD8+T cells between DMSO and 991-treated groups, in both the spleen and BM (online supplemental figure 12A-F). This result is also consistent with equivalent recovery of CD8 CAR T cells on day 3 and at 1 week post-injection (figure 7B and online supplemental figure 7C). Altogether, these data support the hypothesis that increased leukemia control, and consequent enhanced survival in recipients of 991-treated CAR T cells, correlates with early activation of 991-treated cells in the BM, a subsequent early increase in CD4+CAR T-cell numbers in the BM and spleen, and a subsequent decrease in exhaustion of 991-treated CAR T cells within the CD4+compartment.

991 treatment improves efficacy with shorter treatment regimens and alternate CAR constructs

As the CAR T-cell field is gradually moving away from prolonged stimulation and expansion, we investigated how AMPK agonist treatment might be incorporated into an abbreviated CAR T-cell workflow. To this end, we truncated T-cell transduction and agonist treatment to occur over 6 days instead of 11. To begin, primary human T cells were stimulated with Dynabeads on day 0 and either left entirely in DMSO (Group 1) or cultured with agonist on days 2 and 4 (Group 2). Cells were then placed into the Seahorse metabolic analyzer on day 6 (figure 8A). Compared with controls, T cells treated with Compound 991 on days 2 and 4 revealed a striking increase in respiratory activity (figure 8B). We then expanded our investigation to include agonist treatment on day 0, at the time of Dynabead stimulation (Group 3). However, this approach was not well tolerated, with recovery rates in Group 3 at 50% of control levels, suggesting a marked increase in cell death (data not shown). In Group 2, where cells were treated with 991 on days 2 and 4, there was no increase in ROS levels on day 6 (as measured by CellROX staining), despite their increased respiratory capacity (figure 2F,G). This result is consistent with our earlier findings. In contrast, Group 3 dual-stimulated cells exhibited elevated ROS levels out to day 6 from their initial stimulation (figure 8C). Regarding expansion potential, Compound 991 administration transiently decreased cell recovery in Group 2, but this trend corrected by day 6, leading to an equivalent number of DMSO-treated versus 991-treated cells at the end of the treatment period (online supplemental figure 13). Finally, transduction efficiency was equivalent between DMSO and 991-treatment groups, achieving successful CAR transduction in >85% of viable cells under all conditions tested (figure 8D). Together, these data support the ability of 991-treatment to metabolically modify human CAR T cells, in a truncated generation protocol, without disrupting transduction efficiency or impacting cell yield.

While Compound 991 is a well-characterized and highly potent direct activator of AMPK, it is currently only available as a research-grade reagent. Integrating additives into clinical good manufacturing processes may be easier using US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved agents with known activity and documented toxicity profiles in humans. To ask whether metformin, an FDA-approved activator of AMPK, produces metabolic changes similar to Compound 991, we first determined a maximally tolerated pretreatment dose of metformin. Literature reports suggested that 100 µM of metformin exposure in vitro could improve the in vivo response of cancer-targeting CD8 T cells.42 However, in our hands, 100 µM of metformin provided suboptimal AMPK activation in vitro. In contrast, pretreatment with 300 µM of metformin consistently increased AMPK activation, with only a slight (~10%) and transient decrease in cell recovery during treatment (similar to that seen with Compound 991) (online supplemental figure 14). Meanwhile, 900 µM of metformin proved much too toxic (data not shown). We then tested the respiratory activity of cells treated with Compound 991 versus 300 µM metformin on the Seahorse metabolic analyzer. As shown in figure 8E,F, Compound 991 exposure reproducibly increased basal OCR, maximal OCR, and SRC levels. Pretreatment with 300 µM metformin did not. These results suggest that while both 991 and metformin activate AMPK, metformin administered at a dose maximally optimized for AMPK activation, does not enhance the respiratory activity of the cell to the same degree as treatment with Compound 991. This difference is perhaps not surprising given that 991 is a direct activator of AMPK, while metformin inhibits the electron transport chain to raise AMP levels, and thus handicaps the cell metabolically while activating AMPK indirectly.

Finally, we wondered whether AMPK activation would benefit CAR T cells containing a 4-1BB costimulatory domain, as 4-1BB signaling is already known to promote FAO and increase mitochondrial biogenesis.43 44 In addition, AMPK is activated downstream of 4-1BB,45 46 suggesting that further AMPK activity may synergize with 4-1BB signaling in these cells. To test these ideas, we generated a 4-1BB co-stimulated CAR construct (based on the Kymriah protein sequence) and lentivirally transduced this construct into primary human T cells. 4-1BB CAR T cell generations was followed by our standardized agonist treatment regimen. Seahorse analysis on day 11 demonstrated a profound increase in the respiratory capacity of 991-treated 4-1BB CAR T cells (figure 8G), while an IncuCyte assay demonstrated a much greater capacity for in vitro cytotoxicity by 991-treated 4-1BB CAR T cells across a range of effector:target cell ratios (figure 8H and online supplemental figure 15). Finally, we tested the in vivo antileukemia efficacy of 991-treated 4-1BB CAR T cells, administering 1e6 Nalm6 cells to NSG mice, followed 1 week later by 3e6 DMSO-treated or 991-treated CAR T cells. In this model, 991-treated vastly outperformed DMSO-treated 4-1BB CAR T cells, extending the median time of relapse by >4 weeks and improving median survival by more than 30 days (figure 8I,J). From these data, we conclude that 991 treatment robustly potentiates antileukemia responses in 4-1BB co-stimulated CAR T cells and suggests multiple possible avenues for translating this exciting technology to the clinic.

Discussion

A lack of cellular persistence, coupled with decreased in vivo function, limits the traction of CAR T cells as an effective curative therapy.47 It is also well documented that driving ex vivo CAR T expansion in the presence of abundant nutrients reduces their subsequent functional ability in vivo.48 While numerous studies have demonstrated that limiting nutrients enforces starvation pathways and creates metabolically superior CAR Ts,4 15 17 multiple pathways have been highlighted, making it difficult to define the most effective intervention. Further, engineering nutrient-deficient media can be costly and logistically cumbersome, limiting clinical translation. Metabolic rewiring to promote mitochondrial biogenesis and FAO, while reducing excessive ROS production, are promising interventions to improve CAR T function, but add additional layers of complexity to CAR T manufacturing.13 14 19 46 Ultimately, the ability to generate multiple metabolic changes following treatment with a single agent could create both greater in vivo advantages and simultaneously reduce disruptions to current CAR T cell manufacturing protocols. Our data suggest that reinforcing AMPK signaling, via treatment with the direct agonist Compound 991, can achieve these goals.

Compound 991 is a commercially available agonist which binds directly to the AMPK heterotrimer.23 We have optimized a treatment protocol which allows for maximal metabolic benefit without restricting T-cell expansion, a notable improvement over other available methods. T cells expanded in 991 gain mitochondrial capacity, a trait which continues on re-stimulation and without additional agonist treatment. This gain in mitochondrial capacity correlates with increased mitochondrial density, likely occurring through mechanisms downstream of the transcription factor PGC1α.49 50 Indeed, overexpression of PGC1α alone has been demonstrated to improve CAR T therapy via its impact on mitochondrial biogenesis.12 Impressively, despite increased mitochondrial activity in 991-treated T cells, ROS abundance was regularly reduced and thioredoxin 1 levels simultaneously increased, suggesting heightened redox capacity and an improved ROS balance following AMPK activation. Indeed, AMPK is known to augment activity of the transcription factor NRF2, which drives transcriptional programs to increase redox proteins.51 52 Indeed, increased NRF2 activity alone can improve T-cell metabolic function.16 Importantly, and consistent with the greater number of 991-treated T cells recovered on restimulation, AMPK-mediated redox buffering has been shown to facilitate increased cellular proliferation.53 Together, these metabolic advantages led us to hypothesize that 991 exposure during T-cell expansion could improve subsequent in vivo CAR T cell function.

Interestingly, despite the dramatic functional improvement following agonist treatment, we found no consistent changes in the differentiation of 991-treated T cells, including just prior to injection. This was surprising, in part because a similar model using continuous AMPK activation promoted formation of T-cell central memory populations.21 These current studies did, however, uncover upregulation of both metabolic and cell cycle gene sets. Coupled with the in vivo data that 991-pretreatment improves CAR T activation and early T-cell numbers, particularly in CD4+cells, our results together suggest that the right underlying metabolic phenotype, coupled with an “activation ready” capacity, rather than simply T-cell differentiation status, are the major determinant of improved function in our model. And while much debate has surrounded the role of memory T cells to prevent metabolic and functional exhaustion, our data support a model where metabolic and functional optimization occurs independently from documented changes in memory phenotype.

Even given the variability of using T cells from random human donors, the metabolic changes occurring following 991 treatment were dramatic. AMPK normally signals nutrient starvation.20 Consistent with these data, 991 treatment activated a metabolic program reminiscent of nutrient starvation, despite adequate levels of nutrients in the media. This metabolic rewiring surprisingly led to reduced glucose uptake, which we believe is related in part to intracellular lactate retention, forcing cells to instead rely on alternative, intracellular sources of sugar carbons. In line with intracellular nutrient utilization, agonist treatment encouraged autophagy while reducing energy expenditures through protein translation, adaptations beneficial for long-term cellular fitness.35 991-treated cells also upregulated FAO, consistent with the binding pocket for Compound 991 being the site where LC-FAs dock to increase AMPK-driven activity.28 Importantly, alongside increases in FAO, mitochondrial biogenesis, and autophagy, agonist treatment also generated metabolites important for mitochondrial health and redox buffering. Indeed, glutamate, aspartate, and threonine contribute to the generation of proline and glycine,54 products highlighted in multiple models as supporting mitochondrial function and longevity.2933 Many of these metabolites are also critical for other metabolic pathways, including the tricarboxylic acid cycle and nucleotide synthesis.55 56 In fact, redirecting energy expenditure towards nucleotide synthesis has been implicated in fueling the “proliferative burst” observed following restoration of nutrient levels,16 an interpretation consistent with our findings, particularly the increased BrdU uptake observed in 991-treated T cells on day 11. Future studies to delineate AMPK’s role in directing the production and utilization of different metabolites will be important to further identify a specific and mechanistic understanding of how the observed metabolic optimization is occurring.

With an optimized metabolic profile, we hypothesized that 991-treated CAR Ts would demonstrate both increased proliferative capacity and enhanced in vivo persistence. We attempted to quantify proliferation at two early times post-injection; however, we identified no proliferative advantage to 991-treated CAR Ts in either the BM or spleen. Meanwhile, we found a surprising but transient increase in DMSO-treated CAR T proliferation at 72 hours post-injection. Coupled with an increased activation status of 991-treated CAR T cells in the BM at 20 hours post-injection, we speculate that initial leukemia clearance is slower in mice receiving DMSO-treated CAR T cells, allowing us to capture a final proliferative expansion of a small population of short-lived DMSO-treated effector cells. The far more interesting finding became evident between days 5 and 7, where significantly more 991-treated CD4+CAR T cells were recovered from the BM and spleen. In the absence of identified changes in proliferation, we suspected an increased resiliency in 991-treated CD4+T cells at early time points post-transfer. However, we were able to identify only a trend in decreased autophagy in 991-treated cells at day 7 post-injection. It may be that subtle, early metabolic changes (eg, at 24–48 hours) account for much greater subsequent changes in CD4 cell numbers and that our selection of 20 hours and day 7 was simply inadequate to capture the precise moment in time when these changes are most dramatic. What is clear is that 991 pretreatment increased early T-cell activation, most notably in CD4+CAR T cells, alongside an increase in CD4 T-cell numbers at early times post-injection. Further, these changes occurred in the context of stable CD8+CAR T-cell recovery. In addition, the widening CD4/CD8 ratio, which became more dramatic over time, further implies that human CD4 T cells were the subtype most positively impacted by agonist pretreatment. Recent clinical data from long-term survivors highlights persistence of CD4+cells as a critical factor for effective, long-term cure,57 underscoring the importance of these exciting results. Our data also suggest that diminution of an exhaustive phenotype in 991-treated CAR T cells, particularly at the time of leukemia relapse, may also play a role in the improved antileukemia efficacy we are observing, a result further in line with the clinical literature. Indeed, it may be that accelerated leukemia clearance in recipients of 991-treated CAR T cells decreases the cumulative antigen exposure, allowing remaining CAR T cells to maintain a less exhausted phenotype, and therefore be more ready to take on the threat of a potential relapse.

Regarding clinical translation, our results clearly demonstrate that administration of Compound 991 is wholly compatible with a truncated course of CAR T-cell generation, an area ripe for further investigation. In addition, these studies offer preliminary evidence that Compound 991 and metformin pretreatment are not equivalent, a perhaps unsurprising result given the distinct mechanism of action of these two compounds. We speculate that this difference is likely related to metformin’s inhibition of the electron transport chain, and subsequent handicapping of the mitochondria, compared to the more straightforward activation of AMPK directly by Compound 991. Finally, our studies demonstrate the beneficial nature of coupling 991 treatment with a 4-1BB co-stimulated CAR T-cell response. This finding is of particular interest given the early upregulation of 4-1BB seen in 991-treated CAR T cells (figure 7K). Further, a link between AMPK and 4-1BB corroborates other studies demonstrating activation of AMPK downstream of 4-1BB,45 46 and suggests that elevated AMPK signaling synergistically enhances the 4-1BB mediated response. This proposition is supported by the increased respiratory activity, heightened in vitro cytotoxicity, and elevated in vivo efficacy of 991-treated 4-1BB CAR T cells. Compatibility of 991 treatment with 4-1BB containing CAR constructs also opens up additional potential avenues for clinical translation.

Altogether, we demonstrate that expanding CAR Ts in the presence of the direct AMPK agonist, Compound 991, creates cancer-targeting human T cells with markedly enhanced in vivo efficacy. We have identified that 991-treatment encourages cellular starvation pathways, without actually starving the cells, and promotes FAO and oxidative metabolism, while augmenting mitochondrial health and functional capacity. CAR T cells educated in this manner demonstrate an impressive increase in their overall metabolic capacity, translating to noticeably improved in vivo function. This is particularly notable in donor CD4+cells, which demonstrate both an increase in activation markers and cell numbers at early time points, as well as decreased expression of exhaustion markers later in the response. We conclude that the addition of Compound 991 to currently used expansion methods represents an easily translatable intervention to metabolically and functionally optimize human T-cell behaviors in vivo, creating cellular products with the improved capacity to serve as effective curative therapies.

Methods

Virus production

A CD19-targeting CAR, based on the YESCARTA protein sequence, was cloned into a pHR backbone (similar to Addgene #14858, kind gift from Jason Lohmueller, UPMC Hillman Cancer Center), followed by addition of a T2A linker and a truncated Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) tag. Transformed bacterial cultures were grown overnight in Terrific Broth (Sigma Aldrich) and plasmids isolated using QIAGEN QIAmp Miniprep Plasmid Isolation Kit 250. HEK293Ts (American Type Culture Collection (ATCC)) were cultured in (Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Media) DMEM (Gibco #11966–025) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 IU Penicillin-Streptomycin, 2 mM L-Glutamine, and 1× Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) non-essential AAs. Early passage cells were transfected using the Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Kit (Invitrogen) with 2,500 ng of RSV-REV, PMD-2G, and PRRE and 10,000 ng of CAR-truncated EGFR (tEGFR) plasmids. After 24 hours, supernatant was replaced with IMDM media (Gibco #12440–053) containing 10% FBS. Supernatant containing viral particles was harvested at 48 and 72 hours, combined with Lenti-Pac (GeneCopoeia), and incubated at 4°C overnight. Viral supernatants were then centrifuged at 3,500×(g) for 25 min at 4°, resuspended in DMEM or AIMV (Gibco 122 55 083)+5% Serum Replacement (SR, Gibco A2596101), and either frozen at –80°C or used immediately.

T-cell isolation, transduction, and culture

De-identified buffy coats were obtained from healthy human donors (Vitalant), diluted with phosphate buffered saline (PBS), layered over lymphocyte separation medium (MPbio), and centrifuged at 400×g and 25° for 20 min with no brake. The peripheral blood mononuclear cell layer was removed and T cells isolated using the Miltenyi Biotec Human Pan T cell Isolation Kit. Purified T cells were resuspended in AIMV+5% SR (Gibco #A25961-01) and plated with Human T-Activator CD3/CD28 Dynabeads (Fisher Scientific (Thermo) 11132D) at a 2:1 ratio. For standard human T cells, cells were split on day 3 with fresh media. For CAR T cell production, transduction was performed per manufacturer’s instructions using retronectin-coated plates (Takara) on days 2 and 3. In both cases, cells were removed from Dynabeads by magnetic separation on day 5 post-stimulation and expanded in AIMV media containing 5% SR and interleukin (IL)-2 at 100 IU/mL. Cells were re-plated with fresh media every 48 hours thereafter. Compound 991 (SelleckChem S8654, online supplemental table S1) was reconstituted in DMSO and added to cultures at a final concentration of either 10 or 25 µM on days 5 and 7. Metformin was reconstituted in PBS, sterile filtered, and added at a final concentration of 300 µM on the same schedule 991 and DMSO. Control cultures received an equal volume of DMSO. For the survival curve and radiance analysis in figure 3, 10 µM and 25 µM-treated samples were combined into one 991-treated supergroup to improve statistical power. For re-stimulation experiments, primary human T cells were re-plated with Dynabeads at a 1:1 ratio for up to 72 hours; CAR T cells were re-stimulated with NALM6 targets at a 3:1 ratio. Murine wild-type (WT) or AMPK KO T cells were stimulated with Dynabeads and propagated similar to primary human T cells, with the notable addition of IL-7 to the culture at 10 ng/mL at the same times IL-2 was added to the media and propagation in DMEM with 10% FBS, as described previously.58 The truncated 6-day agonist procedure used the reagents noted above, with Dynabead stimulation on day 0, split of T cells on day 2, addition of DMSO or 25 µM Compound 991 on days 0 and 2 (Group 3), or 2 and 4 (Group 2), and restimulation or further evaluation of cells on day 6. In some cases, human T cells were transduced with lentiviruses on day 2 of culture, concurrent with DMSO/991 addition.

Mice, cell lines, and xenograft leukemia model

NSG (NOD.Cg-PrkdcscidIl2rgtm1Wjl/SzJ) mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratories. Male and female mice were used interchangeably and housed in a specific pathogen-free facility. Recipient animals were 8–12 weeks old at the time of injection. The human NALM6 B-cell leukemia cell line was purchased from ATCC and transduced with a lentiviral vector expressing Zs-Green and Luciferase, the latter a kind gift from Jason Lohmueller. NSG mice were injected with 1e6 NALM6 cells and 7 days later received 3e6 total CAR Ts, either pretreated with DMSO or 991. The “leukemia only” control group received no CAR Ts. The experimental unit was a single animal. Leukemia burden was monitored weekly by in vivo imaging system (IVIS) imaging, following intraperitoneal injection of 3 mg luciferin and imaging after 10 min. Any animals with a baseline radiance below 1e8 were considered leukemia-free, a metric determined prior to the start of the experiment. One animal from the 991-treated group in figure 3 died on day 63 while still being leukemia-free by IVIS imaging. Two BM samples were lost to processing from the day 3 samples in figure 7B–D. 25 NSG mice were used for the survival curve and radiance data presented in figure 3F,G. 54 and 21 NSG mice were used for the in vivo experiments in figures7 8, respectively. Sample size was determined based on our previous experience using these models and the number of CAR T cells available at the time of injection. Mice were randomly assigned to treatment groups based on the order in which they were ear-punched (also randomly assigned) and wherever possible, recipients of all three treatment groups (leukemia-only, DMSO, and 991) were co-housed prior to and following Nalm6 and CAR T cell injection. Cells were administered in numeric order within the cage, assuring equivalent timing between all dosing groups. Technicians performing the IVIS imaging were not aware of the treatment allocation. Survival, as the primary outcome measure, was assessed out to day 70, as determined prior to the start of the experiment.

Protein isolation and immunoblot

T cells were counted, washed with PBS, and lysed with 10% trichloroacetic acid. Lysates were centrifuged at 16,000×(g) at 4°C for 10 min, washed two times in ice-cold acetone, resuspended in solubilization buffer (9M urea containing 1% dithiothreitol (DTT) and 2% Triton X and NuPAGE lithium dodecyl sulfate sample buffer 4X (Invitrogen) at a 3:1 ratio), and heated at 70°C for 10 min. Protein gel electrophoresis was performed on ice using NuPAGE 4–12% Bis-Tris Protein Gels (Invitrogen) at 135V. In some cases, protein samples were heated to 95°C for 5 min prior to gel loading. Protein was transferred to Invitrolon 0.45 µm polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Invitrogen) at 30V on ice for 1 hour. Membranes were blocked in Tris Buffered Saline-Triton containing 5% non-fat milk and immunoblotting performed according to the Cell Signaling Technologies Western Blot Protocol. Blots were stripped for 10 min (Restore PLUS Western Blot Stripping Buffer, Thermo Fisher) prior to re-probing. Antibodies used for immunoblotting are listed in online supplemental table S2. Blots were developed with Super Signal West Femto chemiluminescence reagents (Thermo, 34096), detected by CL-X Posure Film (Thermo), and scanned in grayscale with an Epson V600 scanner. For the thioredoxin images, blots were captured on an LI-COR Odyssey FC imager following chemiluminescence incubation. Images were cropped using ImageJ Software (V.1.47T), inverted, and densitometry quantitated in an area encompassing the largest band, followed by quantitation of subsequent bands using the same two-dimensional area.

Flow cytometry

Cells were washed with PBS+2% FBS before staining with antibodies at 1:100 dilution for 30 min. For intracellular stains, cells were fixed per manufacturer’s instructions using Fix/Perm kit (Invitrogen, Cat #:88-8824-00) and then stained with antibodies at 1:100 dilution. Antibodies and other flow cytometry reagents are listed in online supplemental tables S3 and S4. MitoTracker Green (Invitrogen) staining was performed at 50 nM in room temperature PBS for 15 min. CellROX (Invitrogen) staining (500 nM) was performed in culture medium for 30 min at 37°. FAO blue (DiagnoCine Precision) staining was performed in serum-free AIMV at 15 µM at 37° for 2 hours. Nile Red (Thermo) staining was performed in serum-free AIMV at 0.5 µg/mL at 37° for 15 min. Cyto-ID (Enzo) staining was performed per manufacturer’s instructions (37 C × 30 min), with 500 nM rapamycin added to control cultures at the time of staining. Puromycin (MedChemExpress) uptake was performed in AIMV+5% SR at 10 µg/mL at 37° for 30 min. In some cases, cells were pretreated with 8 µM etomoxir (Cayman Chemical Company) in AIMV+5% SR for 15 min at 37° before puromycin addition. BrdU analysis was performed using the Phase-Flow Kit per manufacturer’s instructions (BioLegend). In vitro cells were cultured in BrdU at 0.5 µL per mL of cell solution per manufacturer’s instructions for 2 hours at 37° prior to staining. Flow data was captured on a BD Fortessa analyzer (BD Biosciences) and evaluated using FlowJo software (V.10.1, TreeStar). Cells were gated by forward and side scatter to identify the lymphocyte population followed by downstream analysis.

Seahorse Mito Stress Assay

The Seahorse XF Cell Mito Stress Test (Agilent, Santa Clara, California, USA; Catalog #103015–100) was run on a Seahorse XFe96 Bioanalyzer (Agilent) to determine basal and maximal OCR, SRC, and extracellular acidification rates (ECAR). T cells were plated in assay media (XF Base media (Agilent) with glucose (25 mM), sodium pyruvate (2 mM) and L-glutamine (4 mM) (Gibco), pH 7.4 at 37°C) on a Seahorse cell culture plate coated with Cell-Tak (Corning) at 1e5 (restim) or 1.5e5 (resting) cells/well. After adherence and equilibration, basal ECAR and OCR readings were taken for 30 min. Cells were then stimulated with oligomycin (2 µM), carbonyl cyanide 4-(trifluoromethoxy) phenylhydrazone (FCCP, 1 µM), and rotenone/antimycin A (0.5 µM) to obtain maximal respiratory and control values. Assay parameters were: 3 min mix, no wait, 3 min measurement, performed at baseline and repeated after each injection (three cycles total). SRC was calculated as the difference between basal and the maximal OCR value obtained after FCCP uncoupling. The XF Mito Stress Test report generator and Agilent Seahorse analytics were used to calculate parameters using Wave software (Agilent, V.2.6.1.53).

RNA sequencing

Total RNA, isolated using the RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (Qiagen) in technical triplicates, was used to generate libraries using Illumina Stranded Total RNA Prep and sequenced on an Illumina NextSeq 2000 at the Health Sciences Sequencing Core at the UPMC Children’s Hospital of Pittsburgh. Differentially expressed genes were generated using DESeq2, identifying genes with a log2-fold change >0.6 in expression level and p value of 0.05 as determined by two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Enrichment analysis was accomplished using GSEA software, a joint project of UC San Diego and Broad Institute,59 followed by comparison to datasets from publicly available databases.

Metabolomics

For metabolite analysis, cells were washed and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen in technical replicates of five. Through collaboration with the University of Pittsburgh Health Sciences Mass Spectrometry Core, cells underwent metabolite extraction via resuspension in ice-cold 80% methanol, followed by addition of standards and subsequent liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry analysis. Following untargeted metabolomic analysis, putative metabolite identifications with a p value<0.05 and fold-change >2 were validated with commercial standards based on retention time, accurate mass, and MS2 fragmentation. Pathway analysis was performed on the untargeted dataset using Metaboanalyst: https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/, with comparison to the Biocyc database (Biocyc.org).

Statistics

Graphing and statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism for Windows (V.9.3.0, San Diego, California, USA; www.graphpad.com). Unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test and two-way ANOVA analysis were used to determine statistical significance. Tukey’s multiple comparisons test was used to determine statistically significant differences for the IncuCyte analysis. Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) analysis defined survival curve differences. Unless noted otherwise, data are displayed as mean±SD.

Investigation of fin model using RNN intelligent computing associated to physical parameters and hybrid nanomaterials

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe efficient thermal transport through trapezoidal fin surface remains a significant multi engineering problem because traditional fins configuration offers unsatisfactory heat transport subject to high thermal loads which limits the colling system’s thermal performance. Although, the nanoparticles have potential role in the heat mechanism across trapezoidal fin but the combined contribution of hybrid nanofluid properties, magnetic field, wetting surface and temperature dependent thermal conductivity has not been adequately presented using AI based computational paradigm. Therefore, the present research aims to formulate a nonlinear energy model for heat transport through trapezoidal fin and to examine the model parameters influence on its thermal behaviour. The formulated problem handled by implementing the RNN (Recurrent neural network) coupled with bvp4c and the numerical experiment executed against different parameters to explore their role in the heat transfer. The achieved results determine that higher thermal conductivity parameter () significantly improves the heat transfer while emissivity () suppresses the heat mechanism under the conditions and . The conductive/convective effects () and wetted surface help to regulate the temperature and highlighted lower heat transfer. The temperature index parameter m increases thermal response of the fin. The convergence and accuracy of the RNN predictions are measured through error histogram, regression and MSE (mean squared error) which demonstrate better agreement between the predicted and numerical data. Further, the benchmark solution over the domain from X=0.0 to X=1.0 is provided for future replication of the results. These theoretical findings provided computational basis for better understanding and heat controlling parametric ranges with potential applications in solar energy systems, heat and cooling process of mining environment. Thermal transport across the trapezoidal shaped fin is relevant to the fins based heat exchangers using for thermal control and heat dissipation purposes in mining technology. The heat transfer through trapezoidal fin using nanofluids can enhance thermal dissipation from including motors, mining equipment, compressors, and drilling machinery operating under high thermal loads.Clinical trial number not applicable.Keywords: Fin, RNN approach, Nanomaterials, Mechanical and mining engineering, Heat transfer

Introduction

The heat transfer (HT) is vital for various purposes such as microfluidic devices, electronic cooling system, and heat exchangers. Its significance comprises sparking extensive research to enhance heat transfer (HT), thereby improving device efficiency. Pavithra et al. [1] discussed the radiative convective heat mechanism in dovetail type fin under nanoparticles shape dependent factor. The model analyzed through Hermite wavelet scheme and examined promising heat transfer by combining platelets and spherical type nanoparticles. Seyf et al. [2] studied the effectiveness of pin fins using CuO and Al2O3 nanomaterial by considering 29 and 28.6 nm diameter. The analysis shows that nanoparticles concentration has major contribution in the heat transfer. Alharbi et al. [3] reported detailed heat transfer across annular fin for extended nanofluid termed as ternary nanofluid. On the basis of outcomes, use of ternary nanofluid is suggested beneficial to acquire the rapid heat transfer. Sajjan et al. [4] revealed the case of dry and wet porous fins moving in hybrid nanofluids. The model solved by using the ANN scheme and suggested that integration of machine learning for heat transfer applications is an innovative tool. Upadhyay et al. [5] introduced and analyzed the stretching and shrinking fins model and performed their parametric study. It is noticed that shrinking fin has better capability to cool the system than stretching case.

Alipanah et al. [6] focused on improving HT to increase device performance and reliability. Enhancing HT can lead to breakthroughs in various technologies, driving innovation and advancement. In essence, HT plays a critical role in many applications and ongoing research aims to optimize it for better device performance. In the modern world, energy efficiency is crucial and one key solution lies in introducing advanced heat exchangers. These innovative systems make an essential contribution in optimizing energy use and reducing waste in various industries. Jayaprakash et al. [7] examined the heat transfer (HT) rates between surfaces and the environment, thin surfaces possess a large surface to volume ratio, enhancing heat transfer, thin materials minimize thermal resistance, permitting more efficient heat exchange, and thin surfaces can increase heat transfer coefficients, further enhancing heat transfer rates Cengel [8] reported that radiant surfaces (which emit heat or energy) are being used extensively. Therefore, researchers are actively finding ways to boost the transfer of heat between these surfaces and the surrounding conditions. Cos and Atay [9] investigated straight and radial fins with temperature-dependent thermal properties using the iterative technique with variable parameters. It has been seen that thermally variable conductivity significantly affects the performance of a fin, especially at high temperature differences, as confirmed by Domairy and Fazeli [10] in a study using the HAM method.

Elshafei [11] studied pin fin heat sinks with circular configuration under natural heat transfer (HT), finding that how heat flux (heat transfer rate per unit area) and its direction affect heat sink performance and heat transfer performance between solid pin type fins and porous pin fins. These results show that porous pin fins outperform solid pin-type fins in terms of heat transfer, indicating that the perforations maximize heat transfer rate Fouladi et al. [12] used a mathematical technique called the variable iteration method to overcome limitations that arise from uncontrolled endpoint boundary conditions (in mathematical modeling, boundary conditions refer to the constraints or conditions applied at the boundaries of a system) Sertkaya et al. [13] investigated natural heat transfer on vertical and angled pin-finned surfaces, finding that needle fins significantly increased heat transfer rates (needle fins improve heat transfer efficiency compared to plain surfaces). Kundu and Barman [14] studied wet annular fins (fins that operate in a wet environment, where moisture affects heat transfer) with trapezoidal profiles, finding that their performance depends on air’s psychometric properties, that is, temperature, humidity, etc. and the energy transfer calculation method.

Subba et al. [15] studied how heat transfer through a special kind of fin. They found that when heat is transferred through radiation, it works better. The fin is placed in a special material where fluids pass through. They looked at how heat moves through thermal energy exchange by heat dissipation. The results demonstrated that radiation helps thermal flux transfer happen more efficiently. Without radiation, heat transfer is not as good. This means radiation is important for designing fins that work well. Torabi and Zhang [16] studied temperature distribution with in a fin structure that transfers thermal conduction by heat dissipation by considering changes in heat transfer coefficient, internal heat energy, and surface heat radiation. Their findings can be applicable in many engineering applications. Mosayebidorcheh et al. [17] explored the nonlinear fin heat transfer (HT), accounting for the temperature-varying heat transfer coefficient (HTC), and also focused on thermal conductivity by utilizing the differential transformation method (DTM). This helped them for understanding fin’s heat transfer behavior.

Ya-Song et al. [18] researched on temperature dependent properties of fin with additional parameters. Dogonchi and Ganji [19] investigated a moving finned surface the generates internal heat by considering parameters. Their results showed that increasing the heat source gradient and decreasing the Peclet number make the fin’s tip lead to higher temperatures. Hoshyar and Rahimipetroudi [20] examined a porous fin that generates heat internally, considering the effects of radiative heat transfer with temperature-dependent properties and the heat transfer coefficient. Mosavat et al. [21] analyzed finned surface thermal efficiency under different situations, finding that distinct parameters behave differently depending on the specified conditions.

Hassanzadeha and Bilgili [22] discussed longitudinally finned heat exchangers and discovered boosts in thermal efficiency by using uniform materials in the fins. George et al. [23] compared the productivity of angled and vertical porous fins, finding that inclined porous fins outperform vertical porous fins under identical conditions. Gireesha et al. [24] examined a radial fin with a porous structure under a homogeneous magnetic field, analyzing its heat transfer efficiency with radiation and natural convection effects. Their findings revealed that increasing the Hartmann number enhances heat transfer from the finned surface. Buonomo et al. [25] studied a porous fin of finite length radiative heat transfer and free convection, providing a mathematical solution to model its thermal behavior. Bennia et al. [26] coded a computational program with the intention of thermal level distribution on rectangular finned surface under various 2D and 3D simulation parameters for fins and found that decreasing fin size achieves stable heat transfer. Kulkarni et al. [27] studied an annular fin with a hyperbolic profile, investigating its thermal performance, stress and heat transfer behavior. Manufacturing processes can utilize continuous motion. In this regard, an analytical study has been done by Aziz and Khani [28].

Research gap

The traditional trapezoidal fin heat transfer investigations do not satisfactorily provide a framework for examining the role hybrid nanomaterial combined with multiple parameters. The conventional analytical and numerical schemes can tackle the problem but do not fundamentally provide a data driven representation of the input and output relationships. Further, the collective evaluation of hybrid nanofluid and the model parameters like wet surface, radiative, conductive/convective, Peclet and emissivity effects remains inadequately reported. The use of RNN particularly to learn the temperature response combined with trapezoidal fin therefore characterizes an important methodological research gap. Thus, the research addresses this gap by adding the hybrid nanofluid trapezoidal fin problem using RNN based prediction towards the systematic heat transfer analysis

Novelty

A distinctive novelty of this research is the formulation of a computational paradigm that combines heat transfer characteristics of hybrid nanofluid, fin with trapezoidal shape and RNN based methodological insights. In comparison with conventional fins studies that implemented numerical of analytical schemes to discuss the fin comprising nanofluids or simple Newtonian fluids, the current model evaluates the joint effects of hybrid nanofluid, and governing parameters on the heat dissipation from the trapezoidal fin. Implementation of RNN further differentiates this research by learning the input and output relationship of the model and providing an improved thermal response. Notably, the research contribution does not rest only on RNN or hybrid nanofluid independently but also arises from systematic use within trapezoidal fin energy model and integrated parameters heat transport assessment

Limitations

The present research is limited to the use of hybrid nanofluid model for theoretical heat transfer through trapezoidal fin. The results are applicable under the model conditions , and , for uniform mixture of nanomaterial and primary solvent. The output results obtained using RNN and the predictions depend on the validation, testing, parametric ranges of the data used for training, and accuracy. The practical aspects like sedimentation, agglomeration and stability are not incorporated. Moreover, the experimental validation is not performed but the benchmark solution for future replication is provided because the performed study is theoretical

Future directions

The next research can be prolonged the trapezoidal fin to unsteady state heat transport conditions together with fin taper ratio, ternary nanocomposites, and convective condition on the performance of fin. The RNN scheme can further train over mentioned parametric ranges to report the rapid prediction of thermal distribution. Further, adding aggregation, and temperature dependent properties together will provide more promising output. The methodology can be extended to metaheuristic, and agentic AI schemes for better outcomes and the role of machine learning in the heat transfer applications

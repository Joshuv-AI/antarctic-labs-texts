Linear sofic approximations of amenable algebras
Benjamin Bachner

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of linear sofic approximations for algebras, analogous to the concept of sofic approximations for groups. We prove that for a finitely generated amenable K K -algebra with no zero divisors, all linear sofic approximations are conjugate. This provides an algebraic analogue to Elek and Szabó’s theorem for amenable groups. The proof relies on a "linear monotiling" technique, constructed using a theorem by Brešar, Meshulam and Šemrl on locally linearly dependent operators. Finally, we apply this uniqueness result to the problem of weak stability in the rank metric, showing that the group algebra of an amenable group is weakly stable if and only if the group is residually finite.



1.
Introduction

Elek and Szabó have proved
[
12

that for a finitely generated
sofic group
\Gamma

\Gamma
is amenable if and only
if all sofic approximations of
\Gamma
are conjugate. This rigidity
result establishes a deep connection between the asymptotic properties
of finite approximations and the amenability of the group. In this
paper, we investigate the linear algebraic analogue of this phenomenon
for associative algebras. The linear analogue for soficity was introduced
by Arzhantseva and Paunescu
[
4
]
, using the normalized
rank metric

in place of the normalized Hamming metric. Amenability for algebras
is defined by the existence of a Følner sequence of subspaces (see

).

We introduce the notion of a linear sofic approximation for algebras;
An asymptotic homomorphism of a
K
-algebra

is a sequence of

\phi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
satisfying

rk\left(\phi_{k}\left(ab\right)-\phi_{k}\left(a\right)\phi_{k}\left(b\right)\right)\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\forall a,b\in A.

We call an asymptotic homomorphism

if it maximally separates elements, so that

for any nonzero

this; See Section

of the “only if” is part of Elek and Szabó’s theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be any field and

be a finitely generated

conjugacy class of linear sofic approximations of

We develop a method of “linear tilings” for linear sofic approximations,
which is analogous to the classical technique of Ornstein and Weiss

[
19
]
of quasi-tiling amenable groups, the latter of which
was implemented by Elek
[
14
]
for sofic approximations
of amenable groups. The linear version of tiling relies on a theorem
by Brešar, Meshulam and Šemrl in the study of locally linearly dependent
operators (Section
2.3
). This linear version turns out
to be much more efficient than the classical tilings for groups; See

4.4
.

While Elek and Szabó established an equivalence for groups, the converse
in the linear setting remains open. We do not know whether an algebra
that admits a unique conjugacy class of linear sofic approximations
is necessarily amenable.

In Section

we apply Theorem

to the
study of

Recall that a discrete group

The amenability of finitely generated algebras was introduced and studied
by Gromov
[
16
]
and Elek
[
13
]

characterizations for amenability of finitely generated algebras with
no zero divisors analogous to similar characterizations for groups;
Such an algebra

is amenable if and only if it has no paradoxical
decomposition, if and only if there exists a finitely additive invariant
dimension-measure on

(see
[
13
]
for the relevant
definitions). Bartholdi
[
6
]
has proved that for any
field

and group

, the group algebra

is amenable
if and only if
\Gamma

Example 2.1

•

We will say that a finite dimensional subspace

2.3.
Locally linearly dependent operators

Let
U,V
be vector spaces over a field
K
. A set of linear operators

\left\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\right\}\subseteq Hom\left(U,V\right)

locally linearly dependent

\left\{T_{1}v,\ldots,T_{d}v\right\}\subseteq V
is linearly dependent.

Amitsur
[
2
]
proved that for any locally linearly dependent
set of size
d

\left\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\right\}
, there is
a nontrivial linear combination
S=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}T_{i}

such that

rank\left(S\right)\leq{d+1\choose 2}-1.

The following stronger result was proved by Aupetit for
K=\mathbb{C}

[
5
, p. 87]

and extended by Brešar and Šemrl
[
9
]
for arbitrary infinite
fields. Meshulam and Šemrl
[
18
]
then extended this result
to all fields.

Theorem 2.2

(Brešar, Meshulam, Šemrl)
.

Let
U,V
be vector spaces over a field
K
and

\left\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\right\}\subseteq Hom\left(U,V\right)
locally
linearly dependent operators. Then there exist scalars
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in K
,
not all zero, such that
S=\alpha_{1}T_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{d}T_{d}

has
rank\left(S\right)\leq d-1
.

Since we will deal with asymptotic quantities, Amitsur’s theorem would
suffice to prove our main theorem. However, we use the stronger
result of Brešar, Meshulam and Šemrl.

3.
Linear sofic approximations

Recall that for a group
\Gamma
, a
sofic approximation
is
a sequence of maps
\varphi_{k}\mapsto S_{n_{k}}
satisfying

•

\underset{k}{lim}\;d^{Hamm}\left(\varphi_{k}\left(g\right)\varphi_{k}\left(h\right),\varphi_{k}\left(gh\right)\right)\to 0

\displaystyle dim\left(m_{x}^{-1}\left(W\right)\cap W\right)

\displaystyle=dim\left(W\cap xW\right)=2dim\left(W\right)-dim\left(W+xW\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2dim\left(W\right)-dim\left(A_{\leq 2d}W\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{d\left|S^{2d}\right|}\right)dim\left(W\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle dim\left(U\right)

\displaystyle=dim\left(\bigcap_{x\in S^{2d}}m_{x}^{-1}\left(W\right)\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)dim\left(W\right)

\displaystyle\implies\frac{dim\left(U\right)}{dim\left(W\right)}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right).

∎

Remark 3.6
.

Notice that in the case that
A=K\Gamma
where

\Gamma
is an amenable group, we may choose the Følner set

\Gamma

Lemma 4.1

Let

Lemma 4.2

Let

We find by induction

Assume now that we have found

Proof of the main theorem

For infinite algebras, a linear sofic approximation
\phi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)

must have
n_{k}\to\infty
;

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
\phi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
is
a linear sofic approximation such that
n_{k}
is bounded. Then
A

is finite dimensional.

Proof.

Let
n_{k}\leq N
be a uniform bound, and let
k
be large enough
so that
\phi_{k}
is an
N+1
-approximation. Then
\left\{\phi\left(A_{\leq N+1}\right)\right\}

is a subspace of operators, in which every nonzero element has rank

\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{N+1}\right)n_{k}
. But
n_{k}<N+1
so that
each operator has full rank. Let
v\in K^{n_{k}}
be a nonzero vector,
then the linear map
\phi\left(A_{\leq N+1}\right)\to K^{n_{k}}
,
x\mapsto xv

must be injective since a nonzero element in the kernel will correspond
to a singular matrix. But this implies that
dim\left(A_{\leq N+1}\right)\leq n_{k}\leq N

which can only happen if
A
is finite dimensional.
∎

We can now prove the main theorem.

proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We have established the existence of a linear sofic approximation
in Proposition
3.5
.

Let
\phi_{k},\psi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
be linear sofic
approximations. In order for them to be conjugate, as discussed in
Section
2.2
we need to prove that for every
\varepsilon>0

and for large enough
k
, we have an invertible
M_{k}\in GL_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)

such that for any

rk\left(M_{k}\phi_{k}\left(s\right)M_{k}^{-1}-\psi_{k}\left(s\right)\right)<\varepsilon.

Assume first that
n_{k}
is bounded. By Proposition
5.1

it must be finite dimensional, and combined with the no zero divisor
assumption it must be a division algebra and hence simple. By Artin-Wedderburn
it has a unique irreducible representation, namely
A
viewed as
a left module. For a
d
-approximation,
d>max\left(n_{k}\right),dim\left(A\right)

it must be an actual representation and every nonzero element

must be mapped to a full rank matrix. Thus, if
\phi_{k},\psi_{k}

are
d
-approximations, they must be the direct sum of copies of

Assume now that
n_{k}\underset{k\to\omega}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Let
W\leq A
be
a finite dimensional
\left(S,\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)
-invariant
subspace, and let
d
be such that
W\subseteq A_{\leq d}
and
\frac{1}{d}<\varepsilon
.
Take a
k
large enough so that
\phi_{k},\psi_{k}
are
4d
-approximations.
By Lemma
4.2
, there are
W
-root vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\in K^{n_{k}}

for
\phi_{k}
and
W

u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\in K^{n_{k}}

for
\psi_{k}
such that
\phi_{k}\left(W\right)v_{i}
are independent,

\psi_{k}\left(W\right)u_{i}
are independent, and

dim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\phi_{k}\left(W\right)v_{i}\right)=dim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\psi_{k}\left(W\right)u_{i}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2d}\right)n_{k}-dim\left(W\right).

Notice that
\ell
is the same for
\left(\phi_{k}\right)

\left(\psi_{k}\right)

since in Lemma

we only assume that the map is
a
d

Take

large enough so that
\frac{1}{4d}n_{k}\geq dim\left(W\right)

Then

dim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\phi_{k}\left(W\right)v_{i}\right)=dim\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\psi_{k}\left(W\right)u_{i}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{3}{4d}\right)n_{k}.

Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}
be a basis for

and let

be any invertible map satisfying

Notice that these are linearly independent vectors so that such an

exists. Then for any

and any

\displaystyle M_{n_{k}}\phi_{k}\left(s\right)M_{n_{k}}^{-1}\left(\psi_{k}\left(w\right)u_{j}\right)=M_{n_{k}}\phi_{k}\left(s\right)M_{n_{k}}^{-1}\left(\psi_{k}\left(w\right)u_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{n_{k}}\phi_{k}\left(s\right)\phi_{k}\left(w\right)v_{j}=M_{n_{k}}\phi_{k}\left(sw\right)v_{j}=\psi_{k}\left(sw\right)u_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{k}\left(s\right)\psi_{k}\left(w\right)u_{j}.

6.
Weak stability

Arzhantseva and Paunescu have introduced the notion of
weak
stability
in permutations. A group
\Gamma
is weakly stable in
permutations if for every sofic approximation
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)

of
\Gamma
, there are true homomorphisms
\left(\psi_{n}\right)

that are asymptotically close to
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)
(cf
[
3
, Section 7]
).
This is a weaker notion than
stability
, which demands that
every
asymptotic homomorphism
of
\Gamma
be asymptotically
close to homomorphisms. We refer to
[
7
]

for a thorough treatment of stability of algebras in the rank metric.

We will use here the convention that a representation is a linear,
multiplicative map that is not necessarily unital.

Remark 6.1
.

Allowing non unital representations lets us add some zero representations
in order to reach a desired dimension. If we required the maps to
be unital, we could still prove a similar result, but only if we allowed
slight increases in the target dimension
n_{k}
. In the study of
stability, allowing an increase in dimension is commonly referred
to as
flexible
(weak) stability, and was in fact used as the
definition of stability in the rank metric in
[
11
]

and
[
7
]
. In any case, in the case of a group
algebra we can add representations induced by the trivial representation
of the group, keeping the unitality of the map.

We define the notion of weak stability of
K
-algebras analogously
to weak stability in permutations.

Definition 6.2
.

The algebra
A
is
weakly stable
if for every
\varepsilon>0

there is a
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
n
and any
d
-approximation

\phi:A\to M_{n}\left(K\right)
, there exists a true representation

\psi:A\to M_{n}\left(K\right)
such that
rk\left(\phi\left(s\right)-\psi\left(s\right)\right)<\varepsilon

for any
s\in S
.

As is standard in the study of stability, this property is equivalent
to the following lifting problem: For every linear sofic approximation

\phi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
, the (injective) homomorphism

\left(\phi_{k}\right):A\to\prod_{k\to\omega}M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)

lifts to a homomorphism to the direct product
\prod_{k}M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that for
A
amenable, similarly
to amenable groups, it is weakly stable if and only if it has a sequence
of
true
representations
\psi_{n}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)

which maximally separate its elements. We first need the following
proposition:

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose
\psi_{j}:A\to M_{m_{j}}\left(K\right)

is a sequence of representations such that
\left(\psi_{j}\right)

is a linear sofic approximation. Then for any sequence
n_{k}\to\infty

there is a sequence
\rho_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
of representations
such that
\left(\rho_{k}\right)
is a linear sofic approximation.

The following is very similar to the proof of an analogous proposition
for permutations
[
3
, Proposition 6.1]
.

Proof.

We can construct an increasing sequence
\left\{i_{j}\right\}_{j}

such that
n_{k}>j\cdot m_{j}
for any
k>i_{j}
. Now, for each

i_{j}<k\leq i_{j+1}
let
n_{k}=c_{k}m_{j}+r_{k}
, with
r_{k}<m_{j}

and
c_{k}\geq j
. We then set

\rho_{k}\left(a\right)\vcentcolon=\left(\psi_{j}\left(a\right)\otimes I_{c_{k}}\right)\oplus 0_{r_{k}}\in M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right).

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
j
be large enough so that
\psi_{j}
is
a
2d
-approximation and
j\geq 2d
. Then for any nonzero
a\in A_{\leq d}
,

rank\left(\rho_{k}\left(a\right)\right)=c_{k}\cdot rank\left(\psi_{j}\left(a\right)\right)\geq c_{k}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{2d}\right)m_{j}

\displaystyle\implies rk\left(\rho_{k}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{k}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{2d}\right)m_{j}}{c_{k}m_{j}+r_{k}}\geq\frac{c_{k}}{c_{k}+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2d}\right)^{2}>1-\frac{1}{d}.

Thus,
\rho_{k}
is a linear sofic approximation.
∎

The analogue for algebras of residual finiteness in groups is the
property of being residually finite dimensional (RFD); Every nonzero

a\in A
is mapped nontrivially by some finite dimensional representation.
It is clear that an amenable algebra that is weakly stable must be
RFD.

In the sofic context, constructing a sofic approximation for a residually
finite group given by actual homomorphisms is straightforward. By
contrast, finding a linear sofic approximation given by true representations
for an RFD algebra is a difficult task, and we do not know if it is
true in general.

When the algebra is a group algebra, however, we have seen that a
sofic approximation is a linear sofic approximation of the underlying
group algebra (provided that
K\Gamma
has no zero divisors). Thus:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
K
be a field and
A
be an amenable

K
-algebra with no zero divisors.

(1)

A
is weakly stable if and only if there is a
sequence
\psi_{n}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
of representations
such that
\left(\psi_{n}\right)
is a linear sofic approximation.

(2)

If
A=K\Gamma
is the group algebra of an amenable
group
\Gamma
, then
A
is weakly stable if and only if
\Gamma

is residually finite.

Proof.

Suppose there is a sequence
\left(\psi_{n}\right)
of representations
and let
\phi_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
be a linear sofic
approximation. By Proposition
6.3
there is a
sequence
\rho_{k}:A\to M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)
of representations
that is a linear sofic approximation. By Theorem
1.1
,

\left(\phi_{k}\right)
and
\left(\rho_{k}\right)
are conjugate,
so that
\left(\phi_{k}\right)
lifts to a representation into
\prod_{k}M_{n_{k}}\left(K\right)

(some conjugation of
\left(\rho_{k}\right)
). The other direction
is easy, which proves
(
1
)
.

Suppose now that
A=K\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a residually finite
amenable group. Then there is a sequence of homomorphisms of
\Gamma

that is a sofic approximation (cf
[
3
, Corollary 6.2]
).
We have seen (Remark
3.6
) that the linear extension of
a sofic approximation is a linear sofic approximation of
K\Gamma
.
It follows from
(
1
)
that
K\Gamma
is weakly
stable. Conversely, if
K\Gamma
is weakly stable then a linear sofic
approximation of
K\Gamma
will be close to one given by true representations.
Thus,
\Gamma
is residually linear and hence residually finite by
a famous theorem of Malcev, proving
(
2
)
.
∎

According to a famous conjecture of Kaplansky,
K\Gamma
has no zero
divisors whenever
\Gamma
is a torsion-free group. This conjecture
is still open, but it is known for large classes of groups. For instance,
Kropholler, Linnell and Moody
[
17
]
have proved that if

\Gamma
is a torsion free elementary amenable group, then
K\Gamma

embeds in a division ring and therefore has no zero divisors.

6.1.
An algebra that is weakly stable but not stable

Recall that Abels’ group
A_{p}
(see
[
1
]
) is the linear
group

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*&*\\
&p^{m}&*&*\\
&&p^{n}&*\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\;|\;\quad*\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right],m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\subseteq GL_{4}\left(\mathbb{Q}\right),

where
p
is a prime. It is a finitely presented solvable group,
and hence elementary amenable. Since no element in
A_{p}
has nontrivial
roots of unity as eigenvalues, it is also torsion-free. By the discussion
above, for any field
K
the group algebra
KA_{p}
has no zero
divisors. It is therefore weakly stable by Theorem
6.4
.

Becker, Lubotzky and Thom have proved in
[
8
, Corollary 8.7]

that
A_{p}
is not stable in permutations (although it is weakly
stable in permutations by
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
). Based
on this proof, Elek and Grabowski proved in
[
11
, Theorem 4]

that
A_{p}
is not stable in the rank metric (when we consider maps
into
GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
). Observe that this precisely means
that the algebra
\mathbb{C}A_{p}
is not stable (their proof in fact holds
over any field
K
). It follows that
\mathbb{C}A_{p}
is weakly stable,
but not stable.

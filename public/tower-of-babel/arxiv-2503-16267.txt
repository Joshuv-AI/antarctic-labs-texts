Abstract
. In this paper, we compute the concordance inertia group of the product M × 𝕊 k M\times\mathbb{S}^{k} , where M M is a simply connected, closed, smooth 6-manifold, for 1 ≤ k ≤ 10 1\leq k\leq 10 , using known low-dimensional computations of the stable homotopy groups of spheres. Introduction

The study of smooth structures on manifolds is a central topic in differential topology, particularly in understanding when homeomorphic manifolds are diffeomorphic. This question has led to extensive research on exotic smooth structures and their classification. In this paper, we build upon the computations from
[
1
]
and examine the smooth structures on product manifolds of the form
M\times\mathbb{S}^{k}
, where
M
is a simply connected, closed, smooth 6-manifold. A key motivation for this study is the classification of diffeomorphism types of smooth manifolds homeomorphic to
\mathbb{C}P^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{k}
for various values of

A fundamental tool in diffeomorphism classification is the concordance structure set
\mathcal{C}(M\times\mathbb{S}^{k})

M\times\mathbb{S}^{k}
up to concordance. Following the framework established by Kirby and Siebenmann
[

\mathcal{C}(N)
is in bijection with the homotopy classes of maps
[N,Top/O]
for any closed, oriented, smooth manifold
N
of dimension
n\geq 5
, where
Top/O
is the homotopy fiber of the map
BO\to BTop
. Using this correspondence along with the fiber sequence

PL/O\to Top/O\to Top/PL,

we derive the following computation of
\mathcal{C}(M\times\mathbb{S}^{k})
for any
k
.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a simply connected, closed, smooth 6-manifold. Then, for any
k\geq 1

\mathcal{C}(M\times\mathbb{S}^{k})\cong[M\times\mathbb{S}^{k},PL/O]\oplus\tilde{H}^{3}(M\times\mathbb{S}^{k};\mathbb{Z}/2).

(Theorem A follows from Corollary
2.17

For the specific cases
k=1
and
k=2
, this result can also be obtained from
[
17

for

(see Proposition

To further understand exotic smooth structures, we investigate the inertia group

, which consists of homotopy
n
-spheres

Lemma 3.7
.

Any self-homotopy equivalence of
\mathbb{C}P^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
is diagonalizable.

Proof.

Let

be a self-homotopy equivalence. To prove that

is diagonalizable, we examine the induced map on cohomology.

The cohomology ring of

H^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{3}\times S^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{4})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[y]/(y^{2}),

generates

and
y
generates
H^{2}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. Thus:

H^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{3}\times S^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}

with generators
\alpha
corresponding to
x
and
\beta
corresponding to
y
.

The induced map
f^{*}
on cohomology is determined by its action on these generators:

f^{*}(\alpha)=a\alpha+b\beta

f^{*}(\beta)=c\alpha+d\beta

which can be represented by the matrix:

f^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

To ensure
f
is a self-homotopy equivalence,
f^{*}
must preserve the cup product structure. Specifically, we examine the image of
\alpha^{3}\beta
under
f^{*}
:

f^{*}(\alpha^{3}\beta)=f^{*}(\alpha)^{3}f^{*}(\beta).

Since
\alpha^{4}=0
and
\beta^{2}=0
, it follows that:

\displaystyle f^{*}(\alpha)^{3}=(a\alpha+b\beta)^{3}=a^{3}\alpha^{3}+3a^{2}b\alpha^{2}\beta

\displaystyle f^{*}(\alpha^{3}\beta)=f^{*}(\alpha)^{3}f^{*}(\beta)=(a^{3}\alpha^{3}+3a^{2}b\alpha^{2}\beta)(c\alpha+d\beta)=(a^{3}d+3a^{2}bc)\alpha^{3}\beta

For
f^{*}
to be a self-homotopy equivalence,
f^{*}(\alpha^{3}\beta)
must be
\pm\alpha^{3}\beta
, leading to two cases :

•

If
f^{*}(\alpha^{3}\beta)=\alpha^{3}\beta
, then:

a^{2}(3bc+ad)=1.

This yields: either

(a,b)=(1,k_{1})\text{ and }(c,d)=(k_{2},1-3k_{1}k_{2}).

or

(a,b)=(-1,k_{1})\text{and}(c,d)=(k_{2},-1+3k_{1}k_{2})

•

If
f^{*}(\alpha^{3}\beta)=-\alpha^{3}\beta
, then:

a^{2}(3bc+ad)=-1.

This yields: either

(a,b)=(1,k_{1})\text{and}(c,d)=(k_{2},-1-3k_{1}k_{2})

or

(a,b)=(-1,k_{1})\text{ and }(c,d)=(k_{2},1+3k_{1}k_{2}).

Since any map
\mathbb{C}P^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
can be written as
g\circ f_{\mathbb{C}P^{3}},
where

it follows that
c=0.

Therefore,

d=\pm 1.

With

, the matrix

simplifies to:

\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&b\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}.

Consequently, both compositions

and

are self-homotopy equivalences.

Hence,
f
is diagonalizable, completing the proof.

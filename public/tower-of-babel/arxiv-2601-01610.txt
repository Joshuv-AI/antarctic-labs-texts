Hecke algebra of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} over a 2-dimensional local field
Xuecai Ma

Abstract
. Using the ℝ ⁡ ( ( X ) ) \mathbb{R}((X)) -measure, we define and study certain ℂ ⁡ ( ( X ) ) \mathbb{C}((X)) -valued functions on GL n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(F) for F F a two-dimensional local field. 1.
Introduction

The concept of an
n
-dimensional field was first introduced by Ihara and Parshin in 1970s, which aims to generalizing the classical adelic formalism to
n
-dimensional schemes.
It is an important topic to study representations of algebraic groups over
0
- and
1
-dimensional local fields. Since finite fields and local fields have good properties, for example, the local fields are topological fields, people know many things about their representations. But for algebraic groups over two-dimensional local fields, people know little about their representations.

In his paper
[
Kap01
]
, Kapranov studied the central extension
\Gamma
of a reductive group
G
over a two-dimensional local field
F=K((t))
, where
K
is an 1-dimensional local field. He chose an appropriate subgroup
\Delta_{1}\subset\Gamma
, such that the fibres of the Hecke correspondences are locally compact spaces which can define invariant measures, so he can define the Hecke operators by integrating these measures. He proved that such Hecke algebra
H(\Gamma,\Delta_{1})
is isomorphic to the double affine Hecke algebra associated to
G
. After that, Gaitsgory and Kazhdan gave a categorical framework of Kapranov’s idea. They study representations in pro-vector spaces, see
[
GK04
,
GK05
,
GK06
]
for more details and
[
BK06
]
for more examples.

In their paper
[
BK11
]
, Braverman and Kazhdan considered the subgroup
G^{+}_{\mathrm{aff}}
of
G_{\mathrm{aff}}
, where
G_{\mathrm{aff}}
is the semidirect product of
G_{m}
and the central extension
\widetilde{G}
of
G((t))
. They proved that any double cosets of
G_{\mathrm{aff}}(\mathcal{O})
inside
G^{+}_{\mathrm{aff}}
is well defined and give rise to an algebra structure on a suitable space of
G_{\mathrm{aff}}(\mathcal{O})
-biinvariant functions on
G^{+}_{\mathrm{aff}}(K)
. They call it the spherical Hecke algebra of
G_{\mathrm{aff}}
. And in
[
BKP16
]
, the authors used
W_{X}=W\ltimes X
, the semidirect product of the Weyl group with the Tits cone, to identify the double cosets
I\setminus G^{+}_{\mathrm{aff}}/I
. By studying the combinatorics of the Tits cone
X
, they define the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of
G_{\mathrm{aff}}
.

On the other hand, Fesenko constructed an
\mathbb{R}((X))
-measure on two-dimensional local fields
[
Fes03
]
, such that we can do harmonic analysis on two-dimensional local fields. H. Kim and K. H. Lee use the Cartan decompositions to define the generators and relations of spherical Hecke algebra of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

[
KL04
]
. In particular, they proved the Satake isomorphism by using the
\mathbb{R}((X))
-measures. One can also see
[
Lee10
]
for the construction of Iwahori-Hecke Algebra for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
over a
2
-dimensional local field.

The present paper gives a new construction of Hecke algebra of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
for
F
a two-dimensional local field by using Fesenko’s
\mathbb{R}((x))
-measure and Morrow’s work
[
Mor08
]
on integrations on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Specifically, we define an appropriate space of functions
f:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}((X))
and prove that there is a well-defined convolution product in this space. We define and study a kind of representations called measurable representations. The typical example is the function space that we defined.

We now give the structure of this paper. In section 2, we first review Fesenko’s
\mathbb{R}((X))
-measure on two-dimensional local fields. We then introduce the integration on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
for
F
a two-dimensional local field. We define a space
\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
consisting of functions
f:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}((X))
satisfing certain conditions. We define the convolution product on
\mathcal{L}^{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
, making
\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
be an associative algebra, called the Hecke algebra of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. In section 3, we define the measurable representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, and prove the function space
\mathcal{L}^{M}{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}
is a measurable representation. This makes us establish a relation between the Hecke modules and measurable representations.

2.
Integration on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

A
0
-dimensional local field is a finite field. For
n\geq 1
, an
n
-dimensional local field is a complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is an
(n-1)
-dimensional local field.
An one-dimensional local fields is one of
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
, finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Examples of two-dimensional local fields are
\mathbb{F}_{q}((t_{1}))((t_{2}))
,
K((t))
for a local nonarchimedean field
K
,
E((t))
over a local archimedean field
E
, finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\{\{t\}\}
. See
[
FK00
]
for more introduction about higher local fields.

Let
F
be an
n
-dimensional local field. Then there is a sequence of integral rings

F\supset\mathcal{O}_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(1)}\supset\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(2)}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(n)},

where
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(i)}
is called the rank
i
ring of integers of
F
. Suppose that we have
a sequence of
n
-local parameters
t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\in F
, then there are isomorphisms

F=\mathcal{O}^{(n)}_{F}[t_{1}^{-1},\cdots,t^{-1}_{n}],\text{ and }F^{\times}\cong(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(n)})^{\times}\times t_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\times\cdots\times t_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

Example 2.1
.

For the two-dimensional local field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}((t))
, we have
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{F}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[t]]
and
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{(2)}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}+t_{2}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[t_{2}]]
. For the local parameters, we can take
t_{1}=p
,
t_{2}=t
.

One way to study local fields is using their topology. But topological methods in higher dimensional local fields is not so successful as in the one-dimensional cases. Supposed that
F=K((t))
is an equal characteristic two-dimensional local field,
Following
[
Par84
]
, we can define a topology on
F
by setting the basic neighbourhoods of
0
are those of the form

\sum_{i}U_{i}t^{i}:=\left\{\sum_{i}a_{i}t^{i}\in F|a_{i}\in U_{i}\text{ for all }i\right\},

where
\{U_{i}\}_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}
are open neighbourhoods of
0
such that
U_{i}=K
for all
i\gg 0
. The basic open neighbourhoods of any other point
a\in F
are
a+U
, where
U_{i}
are basic neighbourhoods of
0
.
We know that one-dimensional local fields are topological fields. But
K((t))
is not a topological field, since the multiplication and inverse are not continuous. Moreover,
K((t))
is not a locally compact space, so there is no Harr measure on it. For the topology of mixed characteristic two-dimensional local fields, it is defined in
[
Fes01
]
.

2.2.
Integrations on the product space
F^{n}

We first review the approach of
[
Mor08
]
. We first consider integrations on
F^{n}
. The important thing of repeated integral is the Fubini property, says that for a function

f:F^{n}\to\mathbb{C}((X)),

and for each permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
, the expression

\int^{F}\cdots\int^{F}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})dx_{\sigma(1)}\cdot dx_{\sigma(n)}

is well defined and its value doesn’t depend on
\sigma
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{L}(E^{n})
denote the space of functions
f:E^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
which are Fubini.

(2)

For every
g\in\mathcal{L}(E^{n})
, and every
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in F^{n}
,
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The product of translated fraction ideals is given by

a+t_{2}^{\gamma}\mathcal{O}_{F}^{n}=\prod a_{i}+t_{2}^{\gamma_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{F}\quad\in F^{n}.

We define a function on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

g^{a,\gamma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(\overline{(x-a)t_{2}^{-\gamma}})&x\in a+t_{2}^{\gamma}\mathcal{O}_{F}^{n}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(3)

For a function
g^{a,\gamma}
defined above, we define a integration

\int_{F^{n}}g^{a,\gamma}(x)dx=\left(\int_{E^{n}}gd\mu_{E^{n}}\right)X^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}}.

(4)

A complex function
f:E^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
GL
-Fubini if and only
f\circ\tau
is Fubini for all
\tau\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Let
\mathcal{L}(F^{n},\mathrm{GL}_{n})
denote the
\mathbb{C}((X))
space-valued functions
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}((X))
spanned by

g^{a,\gamma}\circ\tau,\quad\tau\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F),a\in F^{n},\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Theorem 2.5
.

(
[
Mor08
, Theorem 3.4]
)
Every function in
\mathcal{L}(F^{n},\mathrm{GL}_{n})
is Fubini on
F^{n}
. If
f\in\mathcal{L}(F^{n},GL_{n}),a\in F^{n},\tau\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, then the functions
x\mapsto f(x+a)
and
x\mapsto f(\tau x)
also belong to
\mathcal{L}(F^{n},\mathrm{GL}_{n})
, and the repeated integrals are given by

\int_{F^{n}}f(x+a)dx=\int_{F^{n}}f(x)dx,

\int_{F^{n}}f(\tau x)dx=|\det\tau|^{-1}\int_{F^{n}}f(x)dx.

, how do we get a measurable representation? We first consider the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
on
\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
by

(h\cdot f)(x)=f(h^{-1}x).

This defines a representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))

\displaystyle(h,f)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle h\cdot f.

Proposition 3.3
.

\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
is a measurable representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
.

Proof.

By the translation invariant properties of the two-dimensional integral, we have
f(h^{-1}x)
belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
. To prove it is a measurable representation, we need to prove that

\mathrm{Stab}_{f}
is a measurable subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
is generated by
g^{A,\Gamma}
, where
g
are Bruhat-Schwartz functions on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
. We may suppose that
g=\mathrm{char}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)}_{K}
for a compact subset
K
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
, and
f=g^{A,\Gamma}
. We then have

g^{A,\Gamma}=\mathrm{char}_{V},\quad V=\left\{x\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)|x\in A(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(K))\right\},

g^{A,\Gamma}(h^{-1}x)=\mathrm{char}_{U},\quad U=\left\{x\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)|h^{-1}x\in A(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(K))\right\}.

By the assumption of
f
,

\mathrm{Stab}_{f}=\left\{h\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)|V=U\right\}.

Since we have
\Gamma_{i,j}>0
, we get

\mathrm{Stab}_{f}=\mathrm{Stab}_{A}\cap\mathrm{Stab}_{p^{-1}K}.

Since
K
is a compact subset of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
, for every
y\in K
, we have a compact open neighbourhood
K_{y}y\subset K
, where
K_{y}
is a compact open subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
. Then

\{K_{y}y|y\in K\}

is an open cover of
K
, it has a finite sub-cover
\{K_{1}y_{1},\cdots,K_{s}y_{s}\}
. Let

H=\cap_{1\leq i\leq s}K_{i}.

Then
K
is left
H
invariant. We claim that
p^{-1}(K)
is left
p^{-1}(H)
invariant.

For
w\in p^{-1}H
, and
z\in p^{-1}(K)
, we have

p(wz)=p(w)p(z)\subset K,

so
wz\subset p^{-1}(K)
. On the other hand, we have
\mathrm{Stab}_{A}=I_{n}
. Thus we have

(1)

\mathrm{Stab}_{f}=\{I_{n}\}
if
A\neq I_{n}
, and

(2)

\mathrm{Stab}_{f}=I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(H)
if
A=I_{n}
.

We have
I_{n}
is a measure
0
subgroup, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}\mathrm{char}_{I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(H)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{n}(F)}\mathrm{char}_{I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(H)}|\det x|^{-n}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{n}(F)}(\mathrm{char}_{H})^{0,0}(x)dx.

We have
I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}H
is a measurable subset of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, clearly it is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, thus it is a measurable subgroup.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Every element
f\in\mathcal{L}^{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
is in
\mathcal{L}^{H}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)//M)
for some measurable subgroup
M
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
.

Proof.

We may suppose that
f=g^{0,\Gamma}
, otherwise we can take
M=I_{n}
. Since
g
is a Bruhat-Schwartz function on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
, we may suppose that
g=\mathrm{char}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)}_{K}
for a compact open subset
K\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
. Like the previous lemma, for every
y\subset K
, we can choose a compact open subgroup
K_{y}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
, such that
yK_{y}\subset K
. We have

\{yK_{y}|y\in K\}

is an open cover of
K
, it has a finite sub-cover
\{y_{1}K_{1},\cdots,y_{s}K_{s}\}
. Let

H_{r}=\cap_{1\leq i\leq s}K_{i}.

Then
K
is right
H_{r}
-invariant. Similarly, we can find
H_{l}
such that
K
is left
H_{l}
invariant. Let

H=H_{r}\cap H_{l},

we see that
K
is bi-
H
-invariant. Let
M=I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(H)
, we claim that
f=g^{0,\Gamma}
is bi-
M
-invariant. Suppose that
w_{1}=I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}h_{1},w_{2}=I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}h_{2}\in I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(H),z=I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}k\in I_{0}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(K)
, we then have

w_{1}zw_{2}=(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}h_{2})(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}h_{2})(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}h_{2})\in I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(K).

We have

g^{0,\Gamma}(x)=\mathrm{char}_{V},\quad V=\left\{x\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)|x\in(I_{n}+t_{2}^{\Gamma}p^{-1}(K))\right\},

since
V
is bi-M-invariant, we get
f\in\mathcal{L}^{M}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)//M)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Since

compactly support functions (hence locally constant functions). We then get for a

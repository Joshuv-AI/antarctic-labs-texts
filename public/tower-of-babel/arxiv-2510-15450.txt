The Return Map of the Cross Section of Horizontally Short Lattice Surfaces is Weakly Mixing
Albert Artiles

Abstract
. We prove that the return map of the unstable horocycle flow on the space of horizontally short translation surfaces associated to a lattice surface ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) is weakly mixing. This extends the result of Cheung–Quas for the square torus to all lattice surfaces. The proof adapts their criterion for weak mixing and uses quantitative bounds for Siegel–Veech transforms restricted to the Poincaré section of horizontally short surfaces.



1.
Introduction

The classical Boca–Cobeli–Zaharescu (BCZ) map is the transformation
T:\Omega\to\Omega
defined by

T(x,y)=\bigl(y,-x+\lfloor\tfrac{1+x}{y}\rfloor y\bigr),

where
\Omega
is the region of the plane given by
0<x\leq 1
,
0<y\leq 1
, and
x+y>1
. This map was introduced by Boca-Cobeli-Zaharescu in
[
6
]
to study asymptotic properties related to the distribution of
h
-spacings of Farey fractions. Independently, Athreya and Cheung
[
4
]
rediscovered the map while analyzing the dynamics of the unstable horocycle flow the space of unimodular lattices of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

A
horizontally short lattice
is a unimodular lattice
g\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
g\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\{(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x\leq 1\}\neq\varnothing
. Athreya-Cheung identified the BCZ map as the return map of the unstable horocycle flow on the set of horizontally short lattices. Subsequently, Athreya–Chaika–Lelièvre
[
3
]
, Uyanik–Work
[
14
]
, Taha
[
13
]
, and Sanchez
[
11
]
extended this construction to the setting of translation surfaces and their set of holonomy vectors.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a lattice surface with Veech group
\Gamma
and a horizontal saddle connection of length 1 and no shorter horizontal saddle connections. In this paper, we assume that
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
has only one cusp and that
-\text{Id}\in\Gamma
, to simplify the computations, but all the results still hold as long as
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
has finitely many cusps. Let
\Lambda^{*}
be the collection of holonomy vectors of
(X,\omega)
and define
\Lambda
to be the set of visible elements of
\Lambda^{*}
. We define the set of horizontally short translation surface in the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit of
(X,\omega)
as
\mathcal{L}=\{g\Gamma\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma:g\Lambda\cap\{(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x\leq 1\}\neq\varnothing\}.

Athreya
[
5
]
showed that
\mathcal{L}
is a Poincaré section for the unstable horocycle flow on
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
. Let
T=T_{\mathcal{L}}:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
denote the return map under the unstable horocycle flow and
m
denote the induced probability measure on
\mathcal{L}
. Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The dynamical system
(\mathcal{L},T,m)
is weakly mixing.

This result generalizes the paper by Cheung-Quas
[
8
]
where they prove the same result in the case when
(X,\omega)
is the square torus of unit area.

Since
(0,1)^{\mathsf{T}}\in\Lambda
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\begin{bmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

generates the stabilizer of
(1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}
in
\Gamma
. Athreya-Chaika-Lelièvre
[
3
]
showed that
\mathcal{L}
can be parameterized by the set

\Omega=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<s\leq 1,\ 1-\alpha s<t\leq 1\},

via the identification

(s,t)\longleftrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}s&t\\
0&s^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\Gamma.

Uyanik-Work
[
14
]
computed a parameterization for
\mathcal{L}
when
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
has finitely many cusps.

Under this parameterization, the measure
m
corresponds to the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
. This representation allows explicit computations with the Siegel-Veech transform restricted to
\mathcal{L}
rather than to all of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
.

The following theorem provides a counting formula for the expected number of holonomy vectors of a horizontally short translation surface in subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
J=\{\zeta_{1}<\zeta_{2}<\cdots\}
denote the set of heights of vectors in
\Lambda
above the
x
–axis. Fix
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and set

\Omega^{0}=\{(s,t)\in\Omega:s_{0}\leq s\leq 1\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A=[a,b)\times(0,c)
, where
b-a<\zeta_{1}\alpha s_{0}
. Then

\frac{2(b-a)}{\alpha}I_{2}\ \leq\ \int_{\Omega^{0}}\#(p_{s,t}\Lambda\cap A)\,dm(s,t)\ =\ \frac{2(b-a)}{\alpha}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}),

where
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
, and
I_{3}
are explicit functions of
c
and
s_{0}
and
p_{s,t}=\begin{bmatrix}s&t\\
0&s^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Explicit expressions for
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
, and
I_{3}
are given in §
7
. Proposition
1.2
stands in contrast with the classical formulas of Siegel
[
12
]
and Veech
[
15
]
, where the integral only depends on the measure of
A
and not its shape or position in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Another feature that contrasts with the usual behavior of the distribution of points in the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit of
\Lambda
is that when
\Gamma
has one cusp, there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
dependent on
\Gamma
and
V
such that if
c<c_{0}
, then
p_{s,t}\Lambda\cap A
is empty. It would be interesting to study the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
induced by the Siegel–Veech transform conditioned on
\mathcal{L}
in more detail.

2.
Preliminaries

We will use this section to describe our notation and provide the necessary background.

2.1.
Horocycle and Geodesic Flows

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, that is,
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
and
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
has finite volume.
There are three flows in
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
that we are interested in: the geodesic flow and the stable and unstable horocycle flows.
Define the following three 1-parameter subgroups of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
.

A=\left\{g_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}t&0\\
0&t^{-1}\end{bmatrix}:t>0\right\},\mbox{ }N=\left\{h_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}1&t\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:t\in\mathbb{R}\right\},\mbox{ and }U=\left\{u_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-t&1\end{bmatrix}:t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

The flows generated on
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
by left multiplication by
A
,
N
, and
U
are called the geodesic, stable, and unstable horocycle flows, respectively. Both
A
and
N
preserve the horizontal direction in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In particular, the action by
N
preserves the collection of horizontally short translation surfaces in the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit of the translation surface
\omega
since the
x
-axis is an eigenspace with eigenvalue
1
. On the other hand,
A
does not necessarily preserve the set of horizontally short vectors since its elements stretch the
x
-axis when
t>1
. However, when
t\leq 1
,
g_{t}
contracts the
x
-axis by a factor of
t
and therefore
g_{t}
maps the set of horizontally short translation surfaces into itself, but not onto unless
t=1
. It will be convenient for us to consider the elements of
AN
that send the set of horizontally short lattices into itself. To that end, we define
p_{s,t}=\begin{bmatrix}s&t\\
0&s^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\in AN.

U
gives a transversal flow to
\mathcal{L}
.
U
also has the property that for any
g\Gamma\in\mathcal{L}
, the orbit
\{u_{t}(g\Gamma):t>0\}
intersects
\mathcal{L}
in an infinite discrete set of times
[
5
]
.

2.2.
Translation surfaces

A translation surface is a tuple
(X,\omega)
where
X
is a compact Riemann surface and
\omega
is a holomorphic 1-form on
X
. For simplicity, we will abuse this notation and refer to
\omega
as the translation surface from now on. A way to visualize a translation surface is as a disjoint union of polygons
P=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}
where the sides of
P
are partitioned into pairs
\{s_{a},s_{b}\}
and there is a Euclidean translation mapping
s_{a}
onto
s_{b}
. This procedure provides a closed Riemann surface
P/\sim
when we glue the sides of
P
along these identifications. Since
dz
is invariant under Euclidean translation, it follows that
dz
descends to a holomorphic one-form on
P/\sim
. This procedure, therefore, generates a translation surface. Every translation surface arises from a construction like this one. For more information on translation surfaces, one may look at the survey by Zorich
[
16
]
.

There is a natural
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-action on the space of translation surfaces. We now describe this action via the polygon definition. Let
\omega
be a translation surface arising from a finite union of polygons
P\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, and consider
gP
, the image of
P
under
g
.
gP
generates a new translation surface, which we call
g\omega
. Two translation surfaces
(X_{1},\omega_{1})
and
(X_{2},\omega_{2})
are equivalent if there exists a biholomorophism
f:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
with
f_{*}\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}.

Saddle connections

A saddle point of
\omega
is a point
p\in X
such that
\omega_{p}=0
.
\omega
induces a flat metric on
X\setminus\{\text{ saddle points of }\omega\}
. A saddle connection is a geodesic
\gamma
connecting two (not necessarily distinct) saddle points of
\omega
without crossing any other saddle points in between. Each saddle connection
\gamma
provides us with a complex number via
\gamma\mapsto z_{\gamma}=x_{\gamma}+iy_{\gamma}=\int_{\gamma}\omega.
We call
z_{\gamma}
the holonomy vector of
\gamma
. We collect the set of holonomy vectors of
\omega
in a set
\Lambda^{*}=\Lambda^{*}_{\omega}
. We will mainly be interested in the shortest element of
\Lambda^{*}
in each direction, so we collect these elements in a set
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\omega}.

Notice that we have an equivariant relation where for each
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
,
\Lambda_{g\omega}=g\Lambda_{\omega}.

Much work has been done to understand the statistics of the distribution of saddle connections for different types of translation surfaces. Masur showed that the growth rate of the number of saddle connections of length at most
R
grows quadratically in
R

[
9
]
. More recently, Osman-Southerland-Wang
[
10
]
computed effective bounds on the distribution of slope gaps between saddle connections on lattice surfaces, and Artiles
[
1
]
computed the distribution of holonomy vectors of lattice surfaces in randomly chosen directions.

Lattice Surfaces

We refer to the stabilizer of
\omega
under the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-action as the Veech group of
\omega
, and we will denote it by
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\omega}
. If
\Gamma
is a lattice in
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, we say that
\omega
is a lattice surface. Veech surface is another common term used for lattice surfaces.

Veech showed in
[
15
]
that if
\omega
is a lattice surface, we can write
\Lambda
as a disjoint union of
\Gamma
-orbits. That is, there exist vectors
v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{m}
such that
\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma v_{i}.

2.3.
The
\omega
-BCZ map

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
containing
-\text{Id}
. Suppose further that
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
has only one cusp.

Let
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be a collection of non-zero vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Gamma v_{i}
is discrete and that the set of directions of
\Gamma v_{i}
and
\Gamma v_{j}
are disjoint whenever
i\neq j
.

By acting via some element of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
we may assume that
v_{1}=(1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}
.

Since
\Gamma(1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}
is discrete it follows that there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\begin{bmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
generate the maximal parabolic subgroup of
\Gamma
fixing
(1,0)^{\mathsf{T}}
.

In the case that
\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma v_{i}
is the set of visible holonomy vectors of
\omega
, for
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, we have that
g\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}g\Gamma v_{i}
is the set of visible holonomy vectors of
g\omega
. Notice that the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit of
\Lambda
is parameterized by
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
. We will use the identification
g\Lambda\leftrightarrow g\Gamma
implicitly during the rest of the paper.

An element
g\Gamma\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
is called
h
-horizontally short if
g\Lambda\cap\{(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x\leq h\}\neq\varnothing
. When
h=1
, we will use the term horizontally short instead of
1
-horizontally short. We denote the set of
h
-horizontally short elements of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
by
\mathcal{L}_{h}

Athreya
[
5
]
showed that
\mathcal{L}_{h}
is a Poincaré section for the unstable horocycle flow on
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
. We denote the the return map
T_{h}:\mathcal{L}_{h}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{h}
, and
T_{1}
will be denoted simply by
T
. The
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
invariant probability measure on
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
induces a measure
m_{h}
on
\mathcal{L}_{h}
. In
[
3
]
, Athreya-Chaika-Lelièvre showed that
\mathcal{L}_{h}
is a hypersurface in
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
and computed an explicit parameterization.

(2.1)

\Omega_{h}=\Omega_{\omega,h}=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<s\leq h,1-\alpha s<t\leq 1\}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{h}

via
(s,t)\leftrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}s&t\\
0&s^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\Gamma
.

We should note here that Uyanik-Work
[
14
]
computes a parameterization of
\mathcal{L}
for general
\Gamma
as a disjoint set of triangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; one triangle per cusp of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
.
The identification of
\mathcal{L}_{h}
and
\Omega_{h}
identifies the measure
m_{h}
on
\mathcal{L}_{h}
with the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\Omega_{h}
. For this reason, we will denote both measures as
m_{h}
.
m_{1}
will be simply denoted
m
.

3.
Three Lemmas

This section contains three key lemmas. The first is a lemma due to Chueng-Quas
[
8
]
which provides a way to verify if an ergodic transformation is weakly-mixing. The other two lemmas are computational tools that will be used to count the number of points in subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Cheung-Quas 2024)
Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space equipped with a Borel probability measure
m
. Let
T
be an ergodic measure-preserving transformation of
(X,m)
.

Suppose that there exists
\tau>0
, a sequence of subsets
(X_{k})
of
X
, a sequence of positive integers
N_{k}
and a sequence of maps
\phi_{k}:X\rightarrow X_{k}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

m(X_{k})\rightarrow 1
.

(2)

The return map
T_{k}
of
X_{k}
is conjugate to
T
via
\phi_{k}
.

(3)

For all
\delta>0,m\left(\left\{x\in X:d(\phi_{k}(x),x)>\delta\right\}\right)\rightarrow 0
.

(4)

m\left(\left\{x\in X_{k}:R_{k}^{N_{k}}(x)=N_{k}+1\right\}\right)>\tau
, where
R_{k}^{N_{k}}(x)
denotes the
n
th return time to
X_{k}
.

Then
T
is weakly mixing.

The ergodicity of
(\mathcal{L},T,m)
was proved in
[
14
]
. The first three conditions of Lemma
3.1
are not hard to check in our case. A large part of this paper is used to prove part
(4)
holds for our dynamical system
(\mathcal{L},T,m)
.

The next lemma will be used to compute the average number of elements of
p_{s,t}\Lambda
with a prescribed height in a subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
when proving Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>0,j\geq 1
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
b>a
. Suppose also that
b-a<\alpha js
. Consider the points
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}<...<x_{k}<\alpha j
, and define
\mathfrak{L}_{s,t}=\{sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns:n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq k\}
. Then

\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\#\left(\mathfrak{L}_{s,t}\cap(a,b]\right)dt=\frac{k}{j}(b-a).

Proof.

Let
\chi
be the characteristic function of
(a,b]
. Then

\#\left(\mathfrak{L}_{s,t}\cap[a,b)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns)

So we have that

\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\#\left(\mathcal{L}_{s,t}\cap[a,b)\right)dt=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns)dt

So we proceed to compute
\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}S_{i}(t)dt
, where
S_{i}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns).

Notice that
S_{i}(t)
is
\alpha s
periodic, that is

(3.1)

S_{i}(t)=S_{i}(t+\alpha s).

We now claim that if
n_{1},n_{2}
are integers and
\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{1}s)=\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{2}s)=1,
then
n_{1}=n_{2}.

Suppose that
1=\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{1}s)=\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{2}s)
. Then

a<sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{1}s,sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jn_{2}s\leq b.

This implies that

\displaystyle b-a

\displaystyle>\absolutevalue{(sx_i+t j+ \alpha jn_2s) -(sx_i+t j+ \alpha jn_1s)}

\displaystyle=\alpha js\absolutevalue{n_2-n_1}.

If
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, then
b-a>\alpha js\absolutevalue{n_2-n_1}\geq\alpha js
. This contradicts our hypothesis. Therefore,
n_{1}=n_{2}
.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha s}S_{i}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\alpha s}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\alpha s}\chi_{n}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Len}\left([0,\alpha s)\cap\left[\frac{a-sx_{i}}{j}-\alpha ns,\frac{b-sx_{i}}{j}-\alpha ns\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{b-a}{j}.

Above,
\chi_{n}
is the characteristic function of
\left[\frac{a-sx_{i}}{j}-\alpha ns,\frac{b-sx_{i}}{j}-\alpha ns\right)
.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\#\left(\mathfrak{L}_{s,t}\cap[a,b)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{1-\alpha s}^{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(sx_{i}+tj+\alpha jns)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b-a}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{j}(b-a).

∎

The next lemma is used to control the asymptotics of the functions
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
, and
I_{3}
from Theorem
1.2
needed for Corollary
7.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
J\subset[1,\infty)
and let
g:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative function and
J_{N}=J\cap[0,N)
. Suppose that

(3.2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}N^{-2}\sum_{j\in J_{N}}g(j)=c>0.

then,

(3.3)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}N^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\frac{g(j)}{j}=2c.

Proof.

This will be an application of Abel’s summation by parts formula.

Notice that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\frac{g(j)}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\sum_{n\leq j<n+1}\frac{g(j)}{j}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{n\leq j<n+1}g(i)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{j\leq n+1}g(j)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j<N}g(j)

Notice that
\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{j<n+1}g(j)
converges to
c
as
n
tends to infinity. So, the Cezàro average also converges to
c
. This means that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\frac{g(j)}{j}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{j\leq n+1}g(j)+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j<N}g(j)\right)

\displaystyle=c+c=2c.

Similarly, since

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\frac{g(j)}{j}=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\sum_{n\leq j<n+1}\frac{g(j)}{j}\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{n\leq j<n+1}g(j),

Using the same argument, one can show that

(3.7)

\displaystyle 2c=\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{n\leq j<n+1}g(j)\leq\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\frac{g(j)}{j}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Verifying Cheung-Quas Lemma Hypotheses 1-3

We use this section to verify the hypotheses (1), (2), and (3) from Lemma
3.1
.

Let
a_{n}
be a sequence of positive real numbers strictly increasing to
1
. We consider the sequence of subsets
\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}}
equipped with the maps
\phi_{n}:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}}
given by
\phi_{n}(g\Lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}a_{n}&0\\
0&a_{n}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}g\Lambda.

Part (1)

We must check that the measure of
\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}}
converges to
1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This follows from the computation that

m(\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}})=m(\{(s,t)\in\Omega:0<s\leq a_{n}\})=a_{n}^{2}.

This shows that the sets
\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}}
satisfy hypothesis (1).

Part (2)

Let
g\Gamma\in\mathcal{L}
and let
r_{0}
denote the return time of
g\Gamma
to
\mathcal{L}
, that means
T(g\Gamma)=u_{r_{0}}(g\Gamma)
. Let
v
be the vector of smallest slope in
g\Lambda\cap((0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).
It then follows that
r_{0}=\operatorname{slope}(v)
. Since
\operatorname{slope}(\phi_{n}(v))=\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\operatorname{slope}(v)
, it follows that the return time of
\phi_{n}(g\Gamma)
to
\mathcal{L}_{a_{n}}
is
a_{n}^{-2}r_{0}
. This means
T_{a_{n}}(\phi_{n}(g\Gamma))=u_{a_{n}^{-2}r_{0}}\phi_{n}(g\Gamma)
. Since

\begin{bmatrix}a_{n}&0\\
0&a_{n}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-r_{0}&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-a_{n}^{-2}r_{0}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{n}&0\\
0&a_{n}^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

it follows that

Part (3)

5.

In this section we study the set of heights vectors in
\Lambda

Proposition 5.1

The set

Proof.

Since

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let us order
J=\{\zeta_{1}<\zeta_{2}<...\}

\mathbb{R}
. Then
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{\zeta_{n+1}-\zeta_{n}\}<\infty

Proof.

Let

\Gamma

This shows that

∎

Next, we define a function on the set

0&1\end{bmatrix}

Definition 5.3

Given
\Lambda

In particular notice that if

Lemma 5.4
.

Consider the following equivalence relation on

(u_{1},u_{2})\sim(v_{1},v_{2})

\gamma u_{2}=v_{2}

, and

Proof.

Let
j\geq 0

(u_{1},u_{2})\in D_{j}
. Choose
\gamma\in\Gamma

\gamma u_{1}=e_{1}
. Since

\gamma u_{2}

0&1\end{bmatrix}

and

and

∎

K_{+}=\left\{u_{r}p_{s,t}\Gamma\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma:r\in\left[0,\frac{r_{*}}{2}\right],(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\right\}

and

K_{-}=\left\{u_{r}p_{s,t}\Gamma\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma:r\in\left[-\frac{r_{*}}{2},0\right],(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\right\}.

Notice that for
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}
and
r\in[0,\frac{r_{*}}{2}]
,
W(s,t,A_{n})\leq H(r,s,t,A_{n})
and that for
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\setminus\Omega_{a_{n}}
and
r\in[-\frac{r_{*}}{2},0]
, then
W(s,t,A_{n})\leq H(r,s,t,A_{n})
. Since both
W(s,t,A_{n})
and
H(r,s,t,A_{n})
are non-negative integer-valued functions, the following lemma follows:

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
A_{n}
,
K_{+}
, and
K_{-}
be defined as above. Then

(1)

For
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}
and
r\in[0,\frac{r_{*}}{2}]
,

W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})\leq H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n});

(2)

For
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\setminus\Omega_{a_{n}}
and
r\in[-\frac{r_{*}}{2},0]
,

W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})\leq H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n}).

Define
K=K_{+}\cup K_{-}
. Then We get the following bound:

Lemma 8.2
.

For
A_{n}
defined as in Corollary
7.2
,
Then,

\int_{\Omega^{0}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W(s,t,A_{n})dm\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{K}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm.

Proof.

Lemma
8.1

(1)
implies that if
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}
Then for all
r\in[0,\frac{r_{*}}{2}]
,

W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})\leq H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n}).

This means that the average of
H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})
over
r\in[0,\frac{r_{*}}{2}]
exceeds the value of
W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})
for any
(s,t)\in\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}
. That means

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})dm

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}}\int_{0}^{\frac{r_{*}}{2}}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{K_{+}}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm.

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{0}\setminus\Omega_{a_{n}}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})dm

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{\Omega^{0}\cap\Omega_{a_{n}}}\int_{-\frac{r_{*}}{2}}^{0}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{K_{-}}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm.

We, therefore, get our desired inequality.

\int_{\Omega^{0}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})dm\leq\frac{2}{r_{*}}\int_{K}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})drdm.

∎

Notice that
drdm
is the Haar measure on
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
. Since
H
is a non-negative function function we have the following lemma.

Lemma 8.3
.

With the notation as above,

\int_{K}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu\leq\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu.

Corollary 8.4
.

There exists a constant
D_{1}>0
such that for
n
large enough,

\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu\leq D_{1}\beta^{2}.

Proof.

Notice that for any
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

H(r,s,t,A)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{i}(r,s,t,A),

where
H_{i}(r,s,t,A)=\#(\Gamma v_{i}\cap A)
where
v_{i}
are such that
\Lambda=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma v_{i}
. Then we get that

H^{2}(r,s,t,A)-H(r,s,t,A)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H^{2}_{i}(r,s,t,A)-H_{i}(r,s,t,A)+2\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq m}H_{i}(r,s,t,A)H_{j}(r,s,t,A).

Theorem 1.8 from Burrin-Fairchild
[
7
]
, implies that for each
i=1,...,k
, there exists a constant
d_{i}
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma}H^{2}_{i}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H_{i}(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu\leq d_{i}\beta^{2},

and that there exist constants
d_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma}H_{i}(r,s,t,A_{n})H_{j}(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu\leq d_{i,j}\beta^{2}.

Therefore,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\Gamma}H^{2}(r,s,t,A_{n})-H(r,s,t,A_{n})d\mu\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}d_{i,j}\right)\beta^{2}.

∎

Therefore, we have the following bound:

Corollary 8.5
.

With the notation as above, there exists
C>0
such that for
n
large enough,

\int_{\Omega^{0}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W(s,t,A_{n})dm(s,t)\leq C\beta^{2}

Proof.

The previous three results imply that for
n
large enough,

\int_{\Omega^{0}}W^{2}(s,t,A_{n})-W(s,t,A_{n})dm(s,t)\leq\frac{4}{r_{*}}D_{1}\beta^{2}

∎

By setting
A_{n}=B_{2,n}
, we get a proof for Claim
6.3
and therefore the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

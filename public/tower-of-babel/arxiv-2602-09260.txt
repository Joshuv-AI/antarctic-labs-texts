Four-point functions with fractional R-symmetry excitations in the D1-D5 CFT
V. A. Souza Alves, Andre Alves Lima, G. M. Sotkov, M. Stanishkov

Abstract
We study correlation functions with fractional-mode excitations of the R-symmetry currents in D1-D5 CFT. We show how fractional-mode excitations lift to the covering surface associated with correlation functions as a specific sum of integer-mode excitations, with coefficients that can be determined exactly from the covering map in terms of Bell polynomials. We consider the four-point functions of fractional excitations of two chiral/anti-chiral NS fields, Ramond ground states and the twist-two scalar modulus deformation operator that drives the CFT away from the free point. We derive explicit formulas for classes of these functions with twist structures ( n ) (n) - ( 2 ) (2) - ( 2 ) (2) - ( n ) (n) and ( n 1 ) ​ ( n 2 ) (n_{1})(n_{2}) - ( 2 ) (2) - ( 2 ) (2) - ( n 1 ) ​ ( n 2 ) (n_{1})(n_{2}) , the latter involving double-cycle fields. The final answer for the four-point functions always depends only on the lift of the base-space cross-ratio. We discuss how this relates to Hurwitz blocks associated with different conjugacy classes of permutations, the corresponding OPE channels and fusion rules. Keywords: Symmetric orbifold CFT; D1-D5 CFT; Correlation functions.

\ket{\sigma^{{\mathfrak{a}}\dot{\mathfrak{a}}}}_{n}\cong\sigma^{{\mathfrak{a}}\dot{\mathfrak{a}}}_{(n)}
.
There are also states/fields with “mixed” holomorphic/anti-holomorphic parts such as

\ket{\alpha\dot{A}}_{n}
with
h=q=\frac{1}{2}(n+\alpha)
and
\tilde{h}=\tilde{q}=\frac{1}{2}n
, but we will not consider them in our examples.
We will often work only with the holomorphic parts
\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}_{(n)}(z)
.
The anti-chiral operators,

\sigma^{{\mathfrak{a}}\dagger}_{(n)}
,
have opposite R-charges and the same dimensions, i.e.
\ket{\sigma^{{\mathfrak{a}}\dagger}}_{n}=\ket{h^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},-h^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}}_{n}
.

The fields above are fermionic excitations of the bare twist
\sigma_{(n)}
and will be more conveniently expressed in bosonized language (
A.2
).
In the free CFT, we can express all of them as exponentials of a pair (in each copy
I
) of bosons
\phi_{1,I}
and
\phi_{2,I}

The holomorphic part of the NS chirals read

\displaystyle\sigma^{\pm}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{i\frac{n\pm 1}{2n}\sum_{I=1}^{n}(\phi_{1,I}-\phi_{2,I})}\sigma_{(n)}(z)

(2.13a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\dot{1}}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{i\sum_{I=1}^{n}\left(\frac{n-1}{2n}\phi_{1,I}-\frac{n+1}{2n}\phi_{2,I}\right)}\sigma_{(n)}(z)

(2.13b)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\dot{2}}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{i\sum_{I=1}^{n}\left(\frac{n+1}{2n}\phi_{1,I}-\frac{n-1}{2n}\phi_{2,I}\right)}\sigma_{(n)}(z)

(2.13c)

(Exponentials are always implicitly normal-ordered.)
There are similar formulas for the anti-holomorphic parts
\sigma^{\dot{\mathfrak{a}}}(\bar{z})
, in which we replace
\phi_{i,I}(z)
by
\tilde{\phi}_{i,I}(\bar{z})
.

The zero modes
J^{a}_{0}
can be used to generate
\mathfrak{su}(2)
multiplets without changing the conformal dimensions of states.
Starting from an NS chiral state
\ket{q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}}_{n}
, with dimension
h^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}=q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
, as a highest weight vector in the
\boldsymbol{(2q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}+1)}
-representation of the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
generated by the
J^{a}_{0}
, the other states in the multiplet are constructed by lowering the charge with
J^{-}_{0}
to obtain the vector

\displaystyle\ket{\sigma^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}}_{n}\equiv\ket{q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}-r}_{n}={\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}(J^{-}_{0})^{r}\ket{q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}}_{n},\quad{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{(2q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}-r)!}{r!(2q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n})!}}

(2.14)

We are following the definitions of Lunin and Mathur in
[
13
]
.

{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}
is a normalization factor, see Eq.(6.9) of
[
13
]
.
In our notations, the fields associated with these states are

\ket{q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},q^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}-r}_{n}\cong\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}(r)}_{(n)}(z_{*})

where we define

\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}(r)}_{(n)}(z)\equiv{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}\oint_{z}\frac{dz_{1}}{2\pi i}J^{-}(z_{1})\cdots\oint_{z}\frac{dz_{d}}{2\pi i}J^{-}(z_{d})\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}_{(n)}(z).

(2.15)

These multiplet states are Virasoro primary, as can be checked from the mode algebra: since

[L_{\ell},J^{-}_{0}]=0

then

L_{\ell}\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}(r)}_{(n)}=0
for
\ell>0
.

The most important dynamical data in a CFT are its correlation functions.
Lunin and Mathur
[
13
,
14
]
have shown that each correlator defines a covering surface
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cover}}
with ramified points defined by the cycles of the twist insertions on the Riemann sphere base space
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathrm{base}}
.
On
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cover}}
the monodromies are trivialized, and there is only one copy of the seed
{\mathcal{N}}=(4,4)
CFT.
We will take
t,\bar{t}
to be complex coordinates on
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cover}}
and
z,\bar{z}
coordinates on
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathrm{base}}
, and we assume the existence of the appropriate covering map
t\mapsto z
.
Path integrals on
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathrm{base}}
can be determined from path integrals on
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cover}}
and vice-versa. Given a twisted field
{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}(z)
on
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathrm{base}}
, we say that it “lifts” to
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cover}}
as a field
\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t)
, and write

{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}(z)\mapsfrom\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t).

(2.16)

We will use hats to indicate fields that have been lifted to the covering surface.
The covering surface is by construction
untwisted
, the subscript indicates only that
n
is a parameter. The lifting should implicitly be understood as holding inside path integrals, but it is convenient to speak of the behavior of operators individually.

The NS chirals in their exponential form (
2.13
) lift to

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{\pm}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=b_{*}^{-\frac{(n\pm 1)^{2}}{4n}}e^{i\frac{n\pm 1}{2}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}(t)

(2.17a)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{\dot{1}}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=b_{*}^{-\frac{1+n^{2}}{4n}}e^{i\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\phi_{1}-\frac{n+1}{2}\phi_{2}\right)}(t)

(2.17b)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{\dot{2}}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=b_{*}^{-\frac{1+n^{2}}{4n}}e^{i\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\phi_{1}-\frac{n-1}{2}\phi_{2}\right)}(t)

(2.17c)

where
b_{*}
is a parameter that locally defines the covering map near the ramification point created by the twist (see Eq.(
3.4
) below).
Anti-chiral fields lift to
\hat{\sigma}^{\mathfrak{a}\dagger}_{n}(t)
given by the same formulas (
2.17
) but with opposite sign in the exponents.
The multiplet fields (
2.15
) lift to the covering as

\hat{\sigma}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}(t)\equiv{\mathcal{A}}^{{\mathfrak{a}}(r)}_{n}\oint_{t}\frac{dt_{1}}{2\pi i}J^{-}(t_{1})\cdots\oint_{t}\frac{dt_{d}}{2\pi i}J^{-}(t_{d})\hat{\sigma}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}(t)

(2.18)

where
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}}
are the exponents of
b_{*}
for the different NS chirals that can be read in (
2.17
).
The R-symmetry currents on the covering are very conveniently expressed in terms of the bosonized fermions,

\displaystyle J^{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\Big(\partial\phi_{1}(t)-\partial\phi_{2}(t)\Big),

(2.19)

\displaystyle J^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{\pm i(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}(t).

(2.20)

(We are ignoring cocycles; see
[
55
]
and
[
20
]
for a very detailed account of bosonization in the
{\mathcal{N}}=(4,4)
SCFT.)

In fact, the twisted states that we have been describing are not physical because they are not gauge invariant. The symmetric group acts on the cyclic permutation
(1,2,\cdots,n)
by conjugation to produce every other cyclic permutation with length
n
involving different copies.
Thus if
{\mathscr{O}}_{(n)}
is a twisted field with the standard cycle
(1,2,\cdots,n)
, the corresponding physical field is the sum over the entire equivalence class

{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\equiv\frac{1}{{\mathscr{S}}_{n}}\sum_{g\in S_{N}}{\mathscr{O}}_{g(1,2,\cdots,n)g^{-1}},\qquad{\mathscr{S}}_{n}=\sqrt{N!(N-n)!n}.

(2.21)

The normalization factor
{\mathscr{S}}_{n}
counts repeated terms in such a way that the normalizations of
{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}
and
{\mathscr{O}}_{(n)}
are equal

[
13
,
22
]
.
The covering surface technique incorporates the sum in (
2.21
) intrinsically: once the correlator on the covering has been computed, the map back to the physical manifold is multivalued and produces the different conjugacy classes in (
2.21
), see
[
22
]
.
This will be further discussed for our cases of interest in Sect.
4.5
.

3.
Lifting of fractional-mode excitations

3.1.
Fractional-mode coefficients and Bell polynomials

Fractional excitations

J^{a}_{k/n}\ket{{\mathscr{O}}}_{n}

of a generic state
\ket{{\mathscr{O}}}_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{(n)}

correspond to the fields

\big(J^{a}_{-\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{(n)}\big)(z_{*})\equiv\oint_{z_{*}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}(z-z_{*})^{-\frac{k}{n}}J^{a}_{\{k\}}(z){\mathscr{O}}_{(n)}(z_{*}),

(3.1)

assuming that
{\mathscr{O}}_{(n)}(z)
has been properly defined and has trivial monodromy.
This field lifts on the covering surface to

\big(J^{a}_{-\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\big)(z_{*})\mapsfrom\oint_{t_{*}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}[z(t)-z_{*}]^{-\frac{k}{n}}J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t_{*}).

(3.2)

If
z=\infty
is a branching point of order
n
, the modes are given by residues at infinity, which lift to

\big(J^{a}_{\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\big)(\infty)\mapsfrom\oint_{\infty}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}[z(t)]^{\frac{k}{n}}J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(\infty).

(3.3)

When there is no risk of confusion, we will sometimes omit the parentheses for economy of notation, writing, for instance,

J^{a}_{k/n}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}

instead of

(J^{a}_{k/n}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]})
,
etc.

The lifted field
\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t_{*})
has no twist, but
t_{*}
, defined by
z(t_{*})=z_{*}
, is a ramification point with the appropriate order,

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=z_{*}+b_{*}(t-t_{*})^{n}\Big[1+c^{(*)}_{1}(t-t_{*})+c^{(*)}_{2}(t-t_{*})^{2}+\cdots\Big]

(3.4)

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=b_{\infty}t^{n}\Big[1+c^{(\infty)}_{1}t^{-1}+c^{(\infty)}_{2}t^{-2}+\cdots\Big]

(3.5)

where
c^{(*)}_{\ell}
are coefficients. The second equation corresponds to a ramification point at
t=\infty
.
The integrands of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) can then be expanded as

\displaystyle{}^{-\frac{k}{n}}=(t-t_{*})^{-k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A_{\ell}^{(*)}(t-t_{*})^{\ell},\qquad[z(t)]^{\frac{k}{n}}=t^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A_{\ell}^{(\infty)}t^{-\ell},

(3.6)

where (making
\tau=1/t
)

\displaystyle A^{(*)}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{b_{*}^{-\frac{k}{n}}}{\ell!}\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}\left[1+c^{(*)}_{1}(t-t_{*})+c^{(*)}_{2}(t-t_{*})^{2}+\cdots\right]^{-\frac{k}{n}}\right|_{t=t_{*}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle A^{(\infty)}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{b_{\infty}^{\frac{k}{n}}}{\ell!}\frac{d^{\ell}}{d\tau^{\ell}}\left[1+c^{(\infty)}_{1}\tau+c^{(\infty)}_{2}\tau^{2}+\cdots\right]^{\frac{k}{n}}\right|_{\tau=0}

(3.8)

We can extract a more explicit expression from Eq.(
3.7
) if we use ‘Faà di Bruno’s formula’ (
C.5
).
Looking at the RHS of Eq.(
3.7
), we see that the function
g(t)
appearing in (
C.5
) is the series expansion for the covering map, and after evaluating everything at
t=t_{*}
, the derivatives

g^{(n)}(t_{*})=n!c^{(*)}_{n}
compute the covering map coefficients
c^{(*)}_{n}
.
Meanwhile, for Eq.(
3.7
) the function
f(x)
appearing in (
C.5
) is
f(x)=x^{-k/n}
, hence

f^{(m)}(1)=(-k/n)^{\underline{m}}
is a
falling
factorial which can be translated into

(-k/n)^{\underline{m}}=(-)^{m}(k/n)_{m}
,
with
(\alpha)_{m}
the Pochhammer symbol for the
rising
factorial:

(k/n)_{m}\equiv\Gamma(m+k/n)/\Gamma(k/n).

(3.9)

For the branch at infinity, the argument is slightly different because the power in (
3.8
) has the opposite sign.
In summary, we can write (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) as

\displaystyle A^{(*)}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{*}^{-\frac{k}{n}}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}(-)^{m}(k/n)_{m}Y_{\ell,m}\big[c^{(*)}_{1},2!c^{(*)}_{2},\cdots,(\ell-m+1)!c^{(*)}_{\ell-m+1}\big]

(3.10)

\displaystyle A^{(\infty)}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{\infty}^{\frac{k}{n}}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}(-)^{m}(-k/n)_{m}Y_{\ell,m}\big[c^{(\infty)}_{1},2!c^{(\infty)}_{2},\cdots,(\ell-m+1)!c^{(\infty)}_{\ell-m+1}\big]

where
Y_{\ell,m}
are the so-called
incomplete exponential Bell polynomials,
see App.
C
.
This gives an explicit expression for the fractional-mode coefficients in terms of the expansion of the covering map.

Going back to Eqs.(
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we can now write

\displaystyle\big(J^{a}_{-\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\big)(z_{*})

\displaystyle\mapsfrom\oint_{t_{*}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}(t-t_{*})^{-k}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A_{\ell}^{(*)}(t-t_{*})^{\ell}\right]J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t_{*}),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\big(J^{a}_{\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\big)(\infty)

\displaystyle\mapsfrom\oint_{\infty}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}t^{k}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A^{(\infty)}_{\ell}t^{-\ell}\right]J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(\infty).

(3.12)

On the covering surface the excitations of
\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
can be read from the OPE

J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t_{*})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\big(J^{a}_{-k}\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}\big)(t_{*})}{(t-t_{*})^{1-k}}\ ,\quad\big(J^{a}_{-k}\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}\big)(t_{*})=\oint_{t_{*}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}(t-t_{*})^{-k}J^{a}(t)\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}(t_{*}).

(3.13)

Inserting the OPE in the RHS of Eq.(
3.11
) we find that the lift of fractional modes on the base produces a
sum
of integer-mode excitations on the covering:

\big(J^{a}_{-\frac{k}{n}}{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}\big)(z_{*})\,\mapsfrom\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A^{(*)}_{\ell}\big(J^{a}_{-k+\ell}\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{n}\big)(t_{*}).

(3.14)

As we will show presently, whenever
{\mathscr{O}}_{[n]}
is NS chiral, anti-chiral or a state in the mutiplet the sum in the RHS is finite.

It is important to note that the “fractional-mode coefficients”
A^{(*)}_{\ell}
are completely determined from the covering map, and do not depend on the operators apart from their twist structure. More precisely, they depend on the twist structure of the correlation function as whole, which is what defines the covering map.

3.2.
Excitations of NS chiral, anti-chiral and multiplet fields

Using formula (

and formulas (

(3.17)

For the

The OPEs

\displaystyle e^{\pm i(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}(t)\ e^{\frac{i}{2}(\rho\phi_{1}-\gamma\phi_{2})}(t_{*})

For

5.
Ramond fields

The fact that NS chirals and anti-chirals do not lift to Kac-Moody primaries raises the question of which states do. The answer is Ramond ground states.

The Ramond ground states in the
n
-twisted sector are a set of four states
\ket{R^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}
with degenerate dimension
h^{\mathrm{R}}_{n}=\frac{1}{4}n
, and R-charges

q^{\mathrm{R}\pm}_{n}=\pm\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad q^{\mathrm{R}\dot{A}}_{n}=0.

(5.1)

(There are analogous states in the anti-holomorphic sector.)
The corresponding fields will be denoted by

R^{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{(n)}\cong\ket{R^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}
. They can be written in bosonized form as

\displaystyle R^{\pm}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{\pm\frac{i}{2n}\sum_{I=1}^{n}(\phi_{1,I}-\phi_{2,I})}\sigma_{(n)}(z),

(5.2a)

\displaystyle R^{\dot{1}}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{i}{2n}\sum_{I=1}^{n}\left(\phi_{1,I}+\phi_{2,I}\right)}\sigma_{(n)}(z),

(5.2b)

\displaystyle R^{\dot{2}}_{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{+\frac{i}{2n}\sum_{I=1}^{n}\left(\phi_{1,I}+\phi_{2,I}\right)}\sigma_{(n)}(z).

(5.2c)

The
{\mathcal{N}}=4
superalgebra has a spectral flow automorphism that acts on the R-symmetry and Virasoro modes as
[
59
]

\displaystyle J^{3}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\mapsto J^{3}_{\ell}-\tfrac{1}{12}c\xi\delta_{\ell,0}

(5.3)

\displaystyle J^{\pm}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\mapsto J^{\pm}_{\ell\mp\eta}

\displaystyle L_{\ell}

\displaystyle\mapsto L_{\ell}-\tfrac{1}{24}c\xi^{2}\delta_{\ell,0}

The Ramond fields are related to the NS chiral fields (
2.12
) by a spectral flow with
\xi=+1
, or to NS anti-chirals by a flow with
\xi=-1
.
This allows us to obtain correlation functions with Ramond fields by applying the appropriate spectral flow to a function with NS chirals/anti-chirals.

If we want to explicitly compute functions with
n
-twisted Ramond ground states in the same way we did above for NS chirals, we have to take into account a small subtlety
[
60
]
.
When we define a state/field on the
n
-twisted sector, we are implicitly ignoring a factor of
(N-n)

untwisted
states/fields corresponding to the other copies in the orbifold that do not enter the cycle
(n)\in S_{N}
.
The NS chiral states
\ket{\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}
defined in (
2.9
), which belong to the Hilbert space sector corresponding to the permutation
(n)=(1,2,\cdots,n)
,
should be understood, implicitly, as the tensor product

\ket{\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}=\ket{\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}\otimes(\ket{\sigma^{-}}_{1})^{N-n}=\ket{\sigma^{\mathfrak{a}}}_{n}\otimes(\ket{\varnothing})^{N-n},

(5.4)

where the untwisted state
\ket{\sigma^{-}}_{1}
is the NS chiral whose dimension, according to Eq.(
2.9
), is
h^{-}_{1}=0
.
That is, the vacuum,
\ket{\varnothing}
, belongs to the NS sector. In contrast, in the Ramond sector the implicit untwisted fields should not be taken as the vacuum; instead, they should be one of the untwisted Ramond ground states with dimension
h^{\mathrm{R}}_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
, for example, the untwisted state
\ket{R^{-}}_{1}
, with charge
h^{\mathrm{R}-}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
, which is the image of the vacuum
\ket{\sigma^{-}}_{1}

Thus the spectral flowed image of (

Now, the subtlety is that the

Of course, functions involving Ramond fields and the deformation operator can also be reduced in exactly the same way. They are actually slightly simpler since, as we have seen, the action of the representation matrices in
\hat{O}^{(\mathrm{int})}_{2}
is trivial.

In conclusion, the Ramond fields are, in a sense, more natural objects on the covering surface with regard to the action of the R-symmetry currents, even though the twist fusions make correlators with Ramond states more complicated, proliferating the number of fields that must be taken into account (in our example, a four-point function becomes a six-point function).
When there are no excitations, it is usually very efficient to compute functions in the NS sector, and then spectrally flow the result to the Ramond sector if needed but here it is, arguably, more natural to do the opposite: in the presence of R-symmetry excitations one computes the functions in the R-sector, where the result is simpler, and then one flows back.

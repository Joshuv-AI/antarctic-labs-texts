Associations between Arterial Stiffness, Electrolytes, and Hormones: Insights from the LURIC Study on Cardiovascular Health
Authors: Angela P. Moissl-Blanke, Graciela E. Delgado, Gökhan Yücel, Clara Daschner, Marcus E. Kleber, Bernhard K. Krämer, Winfried März, Babak Yazdani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulse pressure (PP), the difference between systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP), is an essential marker of cardiovascular (CV) health and arterial stiffness. Elevated PP is linked to a higher risk of CV events, a major cause of global mortality. This study examines its association with key electrolytes – sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, phosphate, and vitamin D – in blood-pressure regulation and vascular function.MethodsWe analysed data from 3,316 LURIC participants who underwent coronary angiography and had a median follow-up of 9.9 years. Fasting blood samples were collected at baseline. Associations between PP and electrolyte levels were assessed using Spearman correlation. Logistic regression models were used to calculate odds ratios (ORs) per standard deviation (SD) of PP and electrolyte concentrations for four clinical outcomes: all-cause mortality, CV mortality, reduced estimate the glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and type 2 diabetes mellitus.ResultsThe cohort’s average age was 62.7 years, with 70% of participants being male. Higher PP was significantly associated with older age, increased body mass index, and the prevalence of coronary artery disease (CAD), carotid stenosis, and peripheral arterial disease. Potassium, phosphate, renin, 25-OH, and 1,25-OH vitamin D concentrations decreased significantly as PP increased, while sodium remained unchanged. Sodium-potassium ratio and aldosterone-renin ratio increased with higher PP. Women had a higher risk of CV mortality per SD of PP (OR 1.57; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.33–1.86), while men showed a greater risk of reduced eGFR (OR 1.59; 95% CI: 1.40–1.80).ConclusionSpecific electrolyte imbalances are closely linked to increased PP and arterial stiffness. Our findings highlight sex-specific CV risk and support further research into nutrient-based and hormonal interventions targeting PP modulation.

Introduction

Cardiovascular (CV) diseases represent one of the most significant global health challenges, remaining the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [1]. Elevated pulse pressure (PP) is increasingly recognised as a significant indicator of impending CV events [2, 3]. This condition reflects not only the age-related stiffness of arteries but also underlying pathophysiological processes, including endothelial dysfunction, chronic inflammation [4], and the progression of atherosclerosis [5]. As arterial stiffness increases, the ability of central arteries to buffer the heart’s pulsatile output diminishes, resulting in elevated systolic blood pressure (SBP) and reduced diastolic blood pressure (DBP) [6]. Consequently, this heightens the cardiac workload and compromises coronary perfusion during the diastolic phase, thereby increasing the risk of major CV events as well as CV mortality [7]. In addition to genetic factors, lifestyle choices – such as tobacco use, physical inactivity, and poor dietary habits – significantly affect CV health [8]. Evidence increasingly underscores the vital role of electrolytes and hormonal regulation in influencing PP and vascular function [9].

Electrolytes are essential elements needed in small quantities for various biological processes, including maintaining vascular tone and promoting endothelial health [10]. Electrolytes like sodium and potassium are vital for blood-pressure regulation. Excess sodium can lead to water retention and increased vascular resistance, while potassium facilitates vasodilation and counteracts sodium’s hypertensive effects [11]. Other electrolytes, including calcium, magnesium, inorganic phosphate, and also bioactive vitamin D, are also integral to CV health [12, 13]. Calcium contributes to vascular contraction and interacts with phosphate to maintain vascular integrity; imbalances can lead to arterial calcification, a sign of advanced atherosclerosis [14, 15]. Magnesium protects endothelial function and is inversely associated with blood pressure [16].

Despite their importance, the relationship between nutrient status and PP, particularly how these electrolytes interact with hormonal regulators like renin and aldosterone, remains incompletely understood [17]. The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is a key hormonal system that regulates blood pressure, fluid balance, and vascular resistance. Dysregulation of this system, particularly elevated aldosterone concentrations, has been implicated in increased arterial stiffness and raised PP, highlighting the intricate interplay between hormones, electrolytes, and vascular health [18]. Given the complexity of these interactions, a deeper understanding of how dietary intake and nutrient status influence PP could provide new insights into CV risk prevention. Importantly, identifying these relationships could lead to novel therapeutic approaches that optimise nutrient intake and balance hormonal pathways to reduce arterial stiffness and improve CV outcomes [19].

The Ludwigshafen Risk and Cardiovascular Health (LURIC) study includes over 3,000 patients who underwent coronary angiography, providing a unique opportunity to explore CV risk factors. This study aims to evaluate the relationship between PP and essential electrolytes and hormones, including sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, phosphate, and vitamin D. It will also investigate the associations of these factors with long-term clinical outcomes such as all-cause mortality, CV mortality, reduced estimate the glomerular filtration rate (eGFR; as a marker of impaired kidney function), and type 2 diabetes mellitus. This research examines the interactions between specific serum mineral concentrations, hormones, and PP to provide new insights into CV risk assessment and potential approaches to reducing arterial stiffness and associated CV events.

Methods

Study Population

The LURIC study comprised 3,316 Caucasian patients admitted for coronary angiography between 1997 and 2000. All patients were referred due to clinical indications of myocardial ischemia, such as chest pain or positive results on noninvasive stress tests [20]. Those with acute medical conditions other than acute coronary syndrome, chronic illnesses unrelated to CV health, or a history of cancer within the preceding 5 years were excluded from the study. The median follow-up period was 9.9 years.

Laboratory Methods

Fasting venous blood samples were collected by venipuncture from all participants at the beginning of the study. Winkelmann et al. [20] have previously documented the specific sampling and assay methodology. Creatinine was measured using the CREA assay (Roche, Germany) on a Hitachi 717 analyser. Glycated haemoglobin was measured with immunoassay (haemoglobin A1c UNIMATE 5; Hoffmann-LaRoche, Grenzach-Whylen, Germany). High-sensitive C-reactive protein (CRP) and cystatin C were measured by immunonephelometry (N-High-Sensitive CRP; N-Latex Cystatin C, Dade Behring, Marburg, Germany) using a Behring nephelometer II. NT-pro-BNP was measured by electro-chemoluminescence on an Elecsys 2010 (Roche Diagnostics). Serum concentrations of 25-hydroxyvitamin D were analysed using a radioimmunoassay (DiaSorin SA, Antony, France) with intra-assay and interassay coefficients of variation of 8.6% and 9.2% respectively [20, 21]. 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D was measured with a radioimmunoassay (Nichols Institute Diagnostika GmbH, Bad Nauheim, Germany). Intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were below 10% for all described laboratory procedures [21]. Serum calcium concentrations were assessed using the o-cresol phthalein complexone colorimetric assay (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) conducted on a Hitachi 717 analyser (Hitachi, Mannheim, Germany). Serum potassium and sodium concentrations were determined utilising an ion-selective electrode (ISE diluent, internal ISE reference, and reference electrode solution; Roche Diagnostics) on a Hitachi 717 system. Inorganic phosphate concentrations were evaluated in blood serum using the molybdate reaction PHOS (Roche Diagnostics) on a Hitachi 717 analyser.

Blood pressure was measured using an automated oscillometric device (Omron MX4, Omron Healthcare GmbH, Hamburg, Germany) after the participant had been in a supine position for at least 10 min. Three consecutive readings of SBP and DBP were taken at 30-s intervals. If the measurements differed by more than 10 mm Hg systolic, 5 mm Hg diastolic, or 5 beats per minute in heart rate (excluding cases of atrial fibrillation), they were considered invalid and repeated.

Definition of Clinical Variables and Endpoints

Coronary artery disease (CAD) was defined according to the American Heart Association classification as the presence of visible lumen narrowing (>20% stenosis) in at least one of 15 coronary segments [22]. Diabetes mellitus was defined according to the guidelines of the American Diabetes Association from 2010 as increased fasting glucose (≥126 mg/dL) and/or increased glucose after a 2-h glucose tolerance test (≥200 mg/dL) and/or increased glycated haemoglobin (Hb)A1c (≥6.5%) and/or a history of diabetes mellitus [23]. Hypertension was defined as SBP ≥140 and/or DBP ≥90 mm Hg or as a history of hypertension according to the 2018 European Society of Hypertension Guidelines for the management of arterial hypertension [24]. The 2012 Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration creatinine-cystatin C equation was used to eGFR [25]. Reduced eGFR was defined as <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 (2012 CKD-EPI creatinine-cystatin C). Because urinary albumin or protein data were not available, the presence of albuminuric chronic kidney disease could not be determined. Death certificates, medical records of local hospitals, and autopsy data were reviewed independently by two experienced clinicians who were blinded to patient characteristics and who classified the causes of death.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous data are presented as either the mean and standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables, or as the median and 25th and 75th percentile for non-normally distributed variables. Normal distribution is assessed by visually inspecting histograms and comparing mean and median values. Categorical data are shown as percentages.

Group comparisons were performed using appropriate statistical tests based on data type and distribution. For normally distributed continuous variables, differences across three groups were analysed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). For non-normally distributed variables, a logarithmic transformation was applied prior to ANOVA to improve normality assumptions. Categorical data were compared using the Chi-square test of independence. The ratio of cystatin C-based eGFR to creatinine-based eGFR (eGFRcys:eGFRcrea) was calculated as an exploratory marker of selective glomerular hypofiltration syndrome [25].

Spearman’s rank correlation was used to evaluate associations between PP and nutrient or electrolyte concentrations. To investigate the relationship between PP and clinical outcomes, logistic regression models were employed. These models were adjusted for age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and electrolyte concentrations. Results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), calculated per SD increase in PP or nutrient concentration.

To explore potential sex-specific effects, all regression analyses were stratified by sex. The comparative forest plot illustrates ORs and 95% CIs for each clinical outcome in men and women. A vertical reference line at OR = 1 indicates no association; values to the right suggest increased odds, and values to the left suggest decreased odds. Significant associations are indicated when the CI does not cross the reference line.

All tests were two-sided, and a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.3.1 (R Core Team, https://www.r-project.org) [26] and SPSS version 27 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). R packages used included DescTools [27] and tukeytrend [28] for statistical testing and data formatting.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

CV mortality was primarily driven by sudden cardiac death (n = 236, 7.7%), fatal myocardial infarction (n = 92, 3.0%), death due to congestive heart failure (n = 129, 4.2%), death after intervention to treat CAD (n = 25, 0.8%), fatal stroke (n = 59, 1.9%), and other causes of death due to CAD (n = 16, 0.5%). The cohort comprised 3,316 Caucasian patients (mean age: 62.7 ± 10.6 years; 70% male). Based on PP tertiles, significant demographic and clinical differences were observed across the groups (shown in Table 1). Patients in the highest PP tertile (PP: 80.9 ± 11.9 mm Hg) were significantly older (68 ± 8.22 years) compared to those in the lowest tertile (PP: 41.4 ± 6.61 mm Hg; 57.4 ± 10.7 years, p < 0.001). Similarly, a higher prevalence of CAD, carotid stenosis (CS), and peripheral arterial disease (PAD) was observed in the higher PP tertile, with CAD prevalence increasing from 72% to 85% (p < 0.001), CS prevalence from 2.59% to 7.04% (p < 0.001), and PAD prevalence from 5.72% to 14.5% (p = 0.001).

Nutrient and Hormonal Associations

A significant inverse relationship between PP and potassium concentrations was observed, with concentrations declining from 4.22 ± 0.32 mmol/L in the lowest PP tertile to 4.15 ± 0.35 mmol/L in the highest (p < 0.001). The sodium/potassium ratio increased significantly across tertiles, from 33.6 ± 2.72 to 34.3 ± 3.1 (p < 0.001). Phosphate concentrations and calcium*phosphate product decreased across the PP tertiles from 3.61 ± 0.56 to 3.48 ± 0.57 mg/dL and 2.72 ± 0.47 to 2.63 ± 0.46, respectively. Sodium, calcium, and magnesium concentrations remained relatively stable. Renin concentrations decreased markedly (from 23 [12–53] to 16 [8.25–33] pg/mL; p = 0.001), while aldosterone concentrations remained unchanged, resulting in a significant rise in the aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) across tertiles from 3.15 (1.33–6.3) to 4.49 (2.07–9.08) (p < 0.001; shown in Table 1).

In both sexes, individuals with the highest PP tertile were older and had significantly higher systolic and mean arterial pressures than those in lower tertiles (all p < 0.001). Markers of kidney function – including creatinine, cystatin C, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) – worsened with increasing PP (p < 0.001). The prevalence of HFpEF increased with higher PP tertiles, whereas HFrEF was more common in the lowest tertile (p < 0.001; shown in Tables 2, 3).

The decrease in potassium levels across PP tertiles was more pronounced in male than in female patients. Both sexes showed significant differences in renin concentrations (p < 0.001), with consistently higher median levels in males. From the 1st to the 3rd PP tertile, renin declined from 16 (9.0–44.5) to 13 (7.0–28.0) pg/mL in females and from 25 (13–55) to 18 (9–36) pg/mL in males.

Angiotensin I levels remained unchanged in females (2,110 ± 1,270 vs. 2030 ± 1,160 ng/dL, p = 0.389), but increased significantly in males (1,360 ± 552 to 1,520 ± 561 ng/dL, p < 0.001). Angiotensin II showed no significant change in females (35.8 ± 49.9 vs. 33.8 ± 85.6 ng/dL, p = 0.724), but declined significantly in males (37 ± 69.7 to 29.3 ± 50.2 ng/dL, p = 0.018). Aldosterone levels decreased slightly but significantly in females (83 [51–137] to 78.5 [46–125] ng/L, p = 0.048), while remaining stable in males (76 [46–119] to 79 [50–122] ng/L, p = 0.483). The ARR was higher in females and remained stable across tertiles (4.5 [1.8–9.33] to 5.0 [2.5–9.88], p = 0.062), but increased significantly in males (2.78 [1.18–5.37] to 4.22 [1.93–8.85], p < 0.001).

Additionally, eGFR was consistently higher in males than in females across all PP tertiles. Specifically, the mean eGFR values for females were 80.9 ± 20.3, 77.6 ± 19.0, and 72.2 ± 19.3 mL/min/1.73 m2, while the corresponding values for males were 87.8 ± 19.4, 86.0 ± 19.1, and 77.5 ± 19.6 mL/min/1.73 m2 (shown in Tables 2, 3).

To further characterise renal function, we calculated the eGFRcys:eGFRcrea ratio, an emerging marker of selective glomerular hypofiltration syndrome. In the total cohort, the mean ratio was 1.00 ± 0.20, with tertile means of 0.99 ± 0.21, 0.98 ± 0.19, and 0.95 ± 0.20 (p = 0.134). Among men, the mean ratio was 1.00 ± 0.20 (tertiles: 0.99 ± 0.20, 1.00 ± 0.18, 0.96 ± 0.20; p = 0.101), and among women, 1.00 ± 0.20 (tertiles: 0.98 ± 0.22, 0.95 ± 0.20, 0.94 ± 0.20; p = 0.003).

Spearman correlation analysis revealed statistically significant associations between PP and several biochemical markers. Sodium (r = 0.09, p < 0.001) and calcium (r = 0.04, p < 0.01) were positively correlated with PP, while potassium (r = −0.09, p < 0.001), phosphate (r = −0.12, p < 0.001), and the calcium-phosphate product (r = −0.10, p < 0.001) were inversely correlated. The sodium-to-potassium ratio again showed a positive correlation with PP (r = 0.11, p < 0.001), whereas magnesium did not correlate significantly. Moreover, 25(OH) Vitamin D was negatively associated with PP (r = −0.09, p < 0.001), while 1,25(OH) Vitamin D showed no significant association (shown in Fig. 1).

Clinical Outcomes and Sex-Specific Differences

The sex-specific analysis of the clinical results revealed remarkable differences (shown in Fig. 2). We analysed 2,310 men and 1,106 women from the LURIC study. Women had a significantly higher risk of all-cause mortality per one SD as a function of PP (OR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.36–1.82, p < 0.001) compared to men (OR = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.38–1.65, p < 0.001). For CV mortality, there was a higher significant risk per one SD depending on the PP for women (OR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.33–1.86, p < 0.001) in contrast to men (OR = 1.31, 95% CI: 1.19–1.46, p < 0.001). While men had a higher risk of reduced eGFR (OR = 1.59, 95% CI: 1.40–1.80, p < 0.001), the risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus was similar between men (OR = 1.47, 95% CI: 1.35–1.61, p < 0.001) and women (OR = 1.48, 95% CI: 1.30–1.70, p < 0.001).

Medication Use across PP Tertiles, Overall, and Sex-Specific Analyses

In the overall cohort (n = 3,316), the use of ACE inhibitors and calcium antagonists significantly differed across PP tertiles, with the highest usage observed in the third tertile (ACE inhibitors: 59.4%, calcium antagonists: 22.9%, both p value <0.001). A similar effect was observed for diuretics (34.9%, p value <0.001), while β-blocker usage remained consistent across tertiles (p value = 0.729). When stratified by sex, women (n = 1,006) and men (n = 2,310) showed comparable patterns. Among women, ACE inhibitors, calcium antagonists, and diuretics increased significantly from the first to the third tertile (p value <0.001), while β-blocker use did not differ significantly. Discussion

Our study provides compelling evidence of a relationship between PP, critical nutrient imbalances, and their subsequent influence on arterial stiffness and CV outcomes. The findings enhance our understanding of the interplay between biochemical and physiological factors in the deterioration of vascular health, highlight the clinical relevance of targeted dietary and hormonal interventions, and reveal sex-specific differences.

Relationship between PP and Nutrient Imbalances

The observed inverse correlation between PP and potassium, as well as phosphate concentrations, underscores the pivotal role of these electrolytes in vascular health. Elevated PP was associated with a marked reduction in potassium concentrations and an increased sodium-to-potassium ratio. This imbalance suggests that higher sodium concentrations relative to potassium may exacerbate vascular stiffening through mechanisms such as endothelial dysfunction and increased vascular tone [29]. In line with that, recent randomised controlled trials have demonstrated the beneficial effects of partially replacing sodium chloride with potassium chloride in the diet on CV events [30]. The authors noted that as PP increased, phosphate concentrations declined, which they interpreted as increased calcium-phosphate precipitation, promoting vascular calcification. Notably, the stability of calcium and magnesium concentrations across PP tertiles indicates that not all electrolytes are equally implicated in PP modulation. This selective association suggests that potassium and phosphate, alongside the sodium-to-potassium ratio, represent critical targets for intervention. Although electrolytes may fluctuate, fasting morning blood draws under standardised study conditions minimise acute variability. Prior studies have shown that a single baseline serum measurements of potassium and phosphate reliably reflect mineral status and CV risk, supporting the validity of our associations [31, 32]. Furthermore, electrolyte concentrations may fluctuate in the short term, but the large sample size of the LURIC study (n = 3,316) minimises the influence of such individual variations on the observed associations. Therefore, random fluctuations are likely averaged out across the population, which supports the robustness of our findings. Further studies are warranted to explore the mechanistic underpinnings of these associations and their potential for therapeutic modulation.

Dysregulation of the RAAS

The significant increase in the ARR across PP tertiles highlights dysregulation of the RAAS as both a potential driver and a consequence of PP elevation. Elevated ARRs have been linked to increased vascular resistance and reduced compliance, which may exacerbate arterial stiffness and contribute to adverse CV outcomes [33–35]. Aldosterone directly promotes fibrotic remodelling processes in the vascular wall by stimulating collagen deposits and inflammatory mediators [36, 37]. The stability of aldosterone concentrations, in contrast to decreasing renin concentration, may also indicate the aldosterone escape phenomenon, which has been discussed in the literature [38, 39]. These findings align with prior research suggesting that RAAS dysregulation not only contributes to hypertension but also amplifies the risk of vascular complications [40]. Because RAAS activity is affected by ACE inhibitors, diuretics, and calcium antagonists – which were more common in the highest PP tertile – all regression models were adjusted for these medications. Sensitivity analyses excluding users of these drugs yielded similar or stronger associations between PP, biomarkers, and outcomes (data not shown), supporting the robustness of our findings. Thus, therapeutic strategies that modulate RAAS activity, such as mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, may offer promise in mitigating PP-related vascular deterioration. The significant decline in renin levels from the 1st to the 3rd PP tertile in both sexes, more pronounced in males than in females, suggests a sex-dependent modulation of RAAS activity. Part of the decline in renin levels may be due to activation of the renal baroreceptor. Despite this decline, aldosterone levels remained relatively stable in males, while females exhibited a slight but significant decrease. In males, higher angiotensin I alongside lower angiotensin II suggests altered ACE activity and/or greater ACE-inhibitor use; conversely, females did not exhibit significant changes in these peptides, indicating a more stable RAAS profile across PP tertiles. Sex hormones may influence these differences; oestrogen has been shown to modulate RAAS components, promoting vasodilatory pathways and attenuating the effects of angiotensin II [41]. The increase in ARR in males across PP tertiles, in contrast to the stability in females, further emphasises sex-specific regulatory mechanisms within the RAAS. The elevated ARR observed in males may be attributable to the aldosterone escape phenomenon and could partly explain the more pronounced decline in potassium levels, potentially contributing to an increased CV risk [42].

Renal Function and CKD

Reduced eGFR was defined as <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 using the 2012 CKD-EPI creatinine-cystatin C equation; urinary albumin/protein data were unavailable, so albuminuric CKD could not be determined. Reduced eGFR was notably more prevalent in individuals with elevated PP. This underscores the bidirectional relationship between vascular stiffness and renal impairment: elevated PP increases intraglomerular pressure and microvascular damage, accelerating kidney function decline, whereas reduced eGFR can exacerbate arterial stiffness via impaired sodium handling, volume overload, vascular calcification, carbamylation, and uraemic toxins [43]. In contrast, among patients with end-stage kidney disease on maintenance dialysis, PP did not predict mortality – likely due to a high burden of competing risks [44]. The sex disparity observed in baseline prevalence of reduced eGFR – with men showing a lower baseline prevalence – may reflect differences in hormonal influences and renal reserve. Nevertheless, men exhibited a higher risk per SD increase in PP (longitudinal perspective). Renal-protective strategies – optimising BP control and addressing PP through dietary and pharmacological means – are essential. Monitoring electrolytes such as potassium and phosphate in patients with reduced eGFR may provide additional insight into vascular risk and guide therapy; patients with low renal function often require more frequent assessment of mineral status [45]. To further characterise renal function, we calculated the eGFRcys:eGFRcrea ratio, an emerging marker of selective glomerular hypofiltration. The ratio declined across increasing PP tertiles in women, but not in men. Specifically, women exhibited a significant reduction from 0.98 ± 0.22 to 0.94 ± 0.20 (p = 0.003). A lower ratio has previously been associated with frailty, inflammation, and higher CV mortality. This interpretation is in line with our finding that women had a markedly higher risk of CV mortality per SD increase in PP.

Sex-Specific Differences in Clinical Outcomes

The sex-specific analysis revealed significant disparities in the clinical impact of elevated PP. Women demonstrated a higher risk of all-cause and CV mortality with increasing PP, which may reflect differences in hormonal influences and vascular physiology. Previous studies have suggested that oestrogen deficiency post-menopause contributes to endothelial dysfunction and arterial stiffening, potentially magnifying the impact of PP on mortality in women [46]. In contrast, men exhibited a higher likelihood of developing reduced eGFR, which was defined as <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 using the 2012 CKD-EPI creatinine-cystatin C equation. Because urinary albumin or protein data were not available, the presence of albuminuric CKD could not be determined, an outcome that may be linked to the association between PP and renal microvascular damage. The comparable risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus between sexes further underscores the multifactorial nature of PP-related outcomes and the need for personalised risk stratification and management strategies. Significantly, all models were adjusted for electrolyte concentrations, allowing a more precise estimation of the independent effect of PP and reducing potential confounding by electrolyte imbalances. The pronounced sex-specific differences in the associations of PP with CV mortality and reduced eGFR may partly reflect hormonal influences. Post-menopausal oestrogen decline promotes endothelial dysfunction and arterial stiffening, potentially amplifying the impact of elevated PP on CV outcomes in women. In men, higher baseline renin activity and androgen-related modulation of the RAAS may predispose to microvascular renal injury, contributing to the greater risk of reduced eGFR. These hormonal pathways likely interact with age-related vascular changes and may explain the divergent clinical risks observed in our cohort [41, 42, 46–48].

Implications for Clinical Practice and Public Health

Our findings underscore the importance of optimising nutrient intake, mainly through strategies to maintain a balanced sodium-to-potassium ratio. Such interventions may hold promise in reducing PP and improving long-term CV outcomes. Public health initiatives should prioritise dietary education, promoting increased intake of potassium-rich foods and limiting sodium consumption, particularly in high-risk populations. Additionally, addressing renal health in the context of elevated PP is critical. Screening for early signs of reduced eGFR in individuals with high PP and implementing nephroprotective strategies may reduce the burden of both vascular and renal complications. Because urinary albumin or protein data were not available, the presence of albuminuric CKD could not be determined. In individuals with high PP, implementing nephroprotective strategies may reduce the burden of both vascular and renal complications. Lifestyle modifications, including dietary adjustments, physical activity, and pharmacological interventions targeting blood pressure and kidney function, represent a holistic approach to managing these interrelated conditions. Therapeutic approaches that address electrolyte balance and RAAS modulation may help reduce PP and associated CV risk. Dietary strategies to increase potassium intake and reduce sodium, such as the DASH diet, have demonstrated blood-pressure-lowering and CV benefits and are recommended in current hypertension guidelines [24, 30]. Randomised trials of salt substitution with potassium chloride have shown significant reductions in CV events and mortality [30]. Pharmacologic RAAS inhibition with ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, or mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists also lowers arterial stiffness and improves outcomes in patients at risk [36, 40]. Integrating these nutritional and pharmacologic strategies may represent a practical multifaceted approach to mitigate the impact of elevated PP.

Study Strengths and Limitations

All participants in the LURIC study were European heritage. Thus, our results may not be transferable to other ethnicities. Only study participants referred to coronary angiography were included in LURIC, so the results cannot be generalised to a healthy population. Next, no urine samples were available for further metabolite analyses and no information on nutritional intake was available. Consequently, measurements of albuminuria or proteinuria could not be performed. As albuminuria is a well-established prognostic marker for both CKD and CV events, the absence of these data limits a comprehensive assessment of renal risk. Moreover, we did not collect data on habitual dietary intake, which limits the interpretation of the aetiology of the observed electrolyte disturbances and prevents the assessment of long-term nutritional influences. Future prospective studies integrating detailed dietary information will be essential to disentangle dietary contributions to electrolyte balance and pulse-pressure-related risk. Nevertheless, the LURIC cohort exhibited several strengths, including precise clinical and metabolic characterisation of the participants, availability of coronary angiograms, and complete 10-year follow-up data on mortality.

Conclusion

We investigated the relationship between PP and essential electrolytes and their links to arterial stiffness and long-term outcomes. In 3,316 patients, elevated PP was associated with lower potassium and phosphate and with a higher sodium-to-potassium ratio. Notably, women had a higher risk of CV mortality, whereas men had a higher risk of reduced eGFR (defined as <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 by the 2012 CKD-EPI creatinine-cystatin C equation). Urinary albumin or protein data were not available, so albuminuric CKD could not be determined. Risks were more pronounced when elevated PP co-occurred with specific electrolyte disturbances. These findings highlight targeted electrolyte management and RAAS modulation as promising strategies to counteract vascular ageing and improve CV and renal outcomes.

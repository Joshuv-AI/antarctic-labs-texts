Lightweight high-precision monitoring of damage locations in Dunhuang murals based on improved YOLACT
Authors: Ting Zou, Yiqing Li, Yihan Dong, Jialin An
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs an important cultural heritage of China and a world treasure, the Dunhuang murals have undergone thousands of years of weathering, oxidation, and human interference, resulting in extensive damage such as cracks, peeling, and fading on their surfaces. Additionally, due to their complex colour palette, it is challenging to quickly identify damaged areas, leading to a situation where the rate of damage to the Dunhuang murals far exceeds the rate of restoration. To achieve efficient and precise monitoring of damage to Dunhuang murals and support cultural heritage preservation efforts, this paper proposes an improved lightweight, high-precision method for monitoring the location of damage to Dunhuang murals based on YOLACT. First, addressing the limitation of traditional YOLACT models in extracting fine-grained texture features, a residual self-attention mechanism capable of extracting global features is introduced to enhance the model’s feature extraction capability (mAP50: 79.2064%). Subsequently, by introducing a depth-separable convolutional architecture, the goal of lightweight design is achieved while maintaining high accuracy (parameter count: 715.12 k, floating-point operations: 70.219 M). Based on the above algorithm, we proposed a rapid detection and deployment application method to help Dunhuang mural restoration experts in practical application. Finally, to validate the model’s effectiveness, this paper conducted validation on a public dataset. Experimental results show that, compared to three mainstream models, the proposed method achieves the highest mAP50 and the smallest parameter count and floating-point operations.Keywords: Dunhuang murals, YOLACT, Depth separable convolutions, Lightweight, High accuracySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The mural paintings of the Mogao Caves at Dunhuang occupy a unique position in both Chinese and global art history and embody exceptionally rich artistic and historical–cultural value. As artistic masterpieces along the ancient Silk Road, these murals not only display consummate artistic craftsmanship but also carry multilayered religious, social, and historical information, serving as invaluable documentary sources for research on ancient civilizations and East–West cultural exchange. However, the ravages of time–together with environmental deterioration (including wind erosion and oxidation reactions) and human interference–have exposed the Dunhuang murals to serious risks of damage1. Such damage not only compromises the murals’ visual and artistic integrity but also accelerates the ongoing deterioration of their material structure, increases the complexity of subsequent restoration interventions, and may ultimately lead to the permanent loss of historical information2. To safeguard this heritage for posterity, digital preservation and high-precision damage detection for the Dunhuang murals are urgently required3.

Traditional methods for monitoring damage to wall paintings primarily rely on manual visual inspection and high-precision photogrammetry, which are labour-intensive, highly subjective, and lack reproducibility4,5. Additionally, due to the unique materials, complex colours, and rich details of Dunhuang wall paintings, traditional methods struggle to achieve high-precision, automated segmentation, particularly in areas with small-scale cracks and blurred edges, where detection accuracy is severely limited6. In recent years, with the rapid development of computer vision and deep learning technologies, image recognition models based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been widely applied in the analysis of cultural relic images, significantly improving the efficiency and accuracy of damage detection7.

However, methods such as convolutional neural networks lack sufficient global feature extraction capabilities, preventing the model from capturing all subtle features and resulting in insufficient diagnostic accuracy8. To address this challenge, the Transformer was proposed, gaining favour among scholars in many fields due to its powerful global feature extraction capabilities9. Unfortunately, it has not yet been applied in the field of cultural preservation, specifically the restoration of Dunhuang murals. To protect this treasure of world culture, this paper employs the Transformer architecture for instance segmentation of Dunhuang murals to assist experts in their restoration efforts.

It is important to note that the introduction of Transformers also presents two significant challenges: parameter count and small feature recognition8. Primarily, the powerful capabilities of Transformers naturally lead to the major drawback of excessive parameter count, as they cannot achieve high accuracy with a small parameter count like convolutional neural networks10. This is because the Layer Normalisation introduced in Transformers requires pre-normalisation of all input variables, which increases accuracy. To tackle this issue, Zhu et al.11 removed Layer Normalisation from the Transformer and introduced Dynamic Tanh (DyT) activations as a lightweight alternative, demonstrating comparable or improved performance without LN. However, this approach cannot compensate for the performance of Layer Normalisation, leading to a decrease in accuracy. On the other hand, the Transformer model lacks the bias-free regularisation capability of traditional convolutions, resulting in insufficient feature extraction for small-scale feature recognition12. To address this, Lu et al. proposed the Dynamic Hybrid Vision Transformer (DHVT), which integrates convolutional modules into patch embedding and multi-head attention blocks to improve local feature modeling13. Evidence from resource-constrained vision likewise shows that lightweight convolutional models with attention or fine-grained feature modules can robustly detect small and low-contrast targets across domains, reinforcing the value of convolutional inductive biases for small-feature recognition and motivating hybrid CNN/Transformer designs14–16.

To address the above two issues, this paper proposes a self-attention mechanism that combines convolutional neural networks with Transformers, and incorporates a residual network to prevent overfitting. Further, to enhance the model’s efficiency and accelerate the restoration process of Dunhuang murals, deep separable convolutions are introduced to reduce the number of parameters and floating-point operations in the network. Finally, by integrating with the YOLACT architecture, an instance segmentation framework for Dunhuang murals is established. Building on these algorithms, we further propose a fast-deployment detection application to facilitate practical use by Dunhuang mural conservation experts. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

Domain-adapted lightweight feature extraction framework: A lightweight and high-precision feature extraction framework is proposed that integrates the global semantic modeling capability of Transformer with the local inductive bias of convolutional neural networks. This hybrid design enhances the model’s ability to capture fine-grained and orientation-aware features in Dunhuang murals, effectively addressing the challenges of complex pigment textures and directionally aligned cracks.

This structure significantly reduces computational complexity and memory cost while maintaining high segmentation accuracy, achieving an efficient balance between precision and deployability.

Related work

Recent vision research on cultural heritage, in particular the murals from the Mogao Caves at Dunhuang, calls for segmentation approaches that can precisely trace thin, elongated cracks and small patches of peeling, remain reliable under cross-dynastic stylistic variation and uneven lighting, and run on constrained field hardware for on-site surveys. In this context, three lines of work are especially relevant to our damage-localization study on the MuralDH dataset: single-stage real-time instance segmentation (e.g., YOLACT). YOLACT

The YOLACT model adopts a parallel feature extraction and mask generation mechanism in instance segmentation tasks, which has a significant advantage over traditional two-stage segmentation methods in terms of inference speed17. Its core design includes a Protonet module and a linear combination mask prediction structure, which can achieve real-time segmentation while maintaining high accuracy18. For Dunhuang murals, real-time inference is not merely a throughput target but a practical requirement for on-site condition surveys, where conservators must scan multiple cave walls or large fresco panels efficiently. YOLACT’s parallel prototype-based mask generation is therefore attractive for rapid screening of damage hotspots prior to high-resolution follow-up analysis.

To enhance feature extraction capabilities, YOLACT typically uses ResNet or ResNet-FPN as the backbone network and integrates multi-scale feature information to effectively address the segmentation requirements of objects of different sizes19. In our setup, multi-scale fusion in FPN is particularly beneficial for thin cracks and small spalls, which typically occupy only a few pixels. We empirically show that increasing the contribution of high-resolution pyramids can improve edge continuity and effectively reduce the fragmented mask area on cracks.The mask generation mechanism generates a shared mask base vector for each prediction box and dynamically synthesises the target mask through linear combination coefficients, effectively reducing redundant computations. The linear-combination masks can be further regularized for mural-damage peculiarities by adding edge-aware losses and thin-structure penalties, which help preserve continuity of long cracks and prevent over-smoothing on narrow detachments. To further improve localisation accuracy, some studies have introduced the IoU loss function or boundary box regression methods based on EIoU into YOLACT to optimise the matching effect between ground truth boxes and predicted boxes20.

Additionally, to enhance the model’s generalisation capability, data augmentation techniques such as random cropping, horizontal flipping, colour jittering, and scale changes are widely adopted during the YOLACT training phase21. In terms of model structure, YOLACT primarily consists of a backbone network, an FPN feature fusion module, and a prediction head structure, featuring a clear and efficient overall architecture that facilitates rapid deployment and optimisation.

Depthwise separable convolution

Depthwise Separable Convolution (DWCNN) is an efficient convolution operation method in convolutional neural networks [11], primarily divided into two steps: depth convolution and pointwise convolution22. In depth convolution, each input channel is convolved by a separate convolution kernel. This differs from traditional convolution, where each convolution kernel convolves across all input channels. Pointwise convolution follows depth convolution and performs a convolution. This operation involves using a convolution kernel to convolve the results of depth convolution across all input channels23. Initially, depth convolution reduces the number of parameters and computational complexity at each spatial location, as it does not perform convolution across channels. Then, pointwise convolution uses only a convolution kernel, reducing the number of parameters while allowing feature combination across channels. This method is particularly suitable for devices with limited computational capabilities, enabling it to significantly reduce computational burden while maintaining network performance24. In field deployments within caves, which often face challenges of power constraints and limited GPU memory, DWCNN enable them to perform large-scale on-site segmentation of mural lesions efficiently and in real time without incurring excessive latency.

Residual connections

Deep neural networks essentially extract and combine complex data representations in a layered manner26. In principle, increasing the number of layers in the network can extract rich and advanced feature information. Therefore, the accumulation of layers leads to the vanishing gradient or exploding gradient problem, and model overfitting reduces the overall performance of the network27. Residual connections can perfectly solve this problem by effectively preventing overfitting or exploding gradients through the use of residual learning mechanisms28. In mural imagery, non-damaged background typically dominates the pixels, causing class imbalance and occasional vanishing gradients when training deep encoders. Residual connections stabilize optimization under such imbalance and preserve low-level texture cues (e.g., pigment grains and fine edges) that are indispensable for separating subtle fading from intact paint.

Let the input information of a certain layer in a deep network be x, and the ideal mapping output be H(x). In traditional feedforward networks, the output layer needs to directly learn this mapping. However, in residual networks, the output layer only needs to learn the difference between H(x) and the input signal x, as shown in Eq. (3). If H(x) is an identity mapping, the optimal residual output is x, meaning the output layer has not learned useful information, and this will not negatively impact the learning process29.

Method

This paper proposes a new lightweight and high-precision YOLACT instance segmentation model. First, in view of the acomplex colors and delicate textures of Dunhuang murals, a dimension expansion preprocessing method is designed to enhance the expression ability of fine-grained damage features. Subsequently, a deep separable convolution structure is introduced on the basis of the YOLACT framework to achieve a lightweight design of the model and construct the main architecture of instance segmentation (see Fig. 1a). On this basis, the original backbone network (Backbone) is optimized to enhance the model’s ability to quickly perceive and accurately extract key features (see Fig. 1b). In addition, this paper proposes a feature extraction module that integrates the global modeling capabilities of Transformer and the local inductive bias of convolutional neural networks to enhance the model’s ability to obtain effective information under complex texture backgrounds (see Fig. 1c). Finally, to validate the feasibility and scalability of the proposed approach in real-world cultural heritage applications, the model is deployed on a Huawei Cloud General-Purpose Enhanced Elastic Cloud Server (ECS), forming a high-performance inference and visualization system for remote mural damage monitoring and intelligent analysis, as detailed in “Model deployment” section.

Preprocessing

To ensure stable visual quality and feature consistency across diverse mural samples, a lightweight preprocessing pipeline was adopted prior to model training. Considering that the color distribution of Dunhuang murals is often affected by uneven illumination, pigment aging, and environmental degradation, we performed color normalization and contrast enhancement to suppress brightness variations and highlight subtle damage regions such as cracks and peeling edges.

Specifically, a channel-wise normalization strategy was applied to standardize chromatic statistics across all samples, followed by a local contrast enhancement using the Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) method. This process effectively enhances fine-grained structural textures and improves the visibility of faint damage boundaries without altering the mural’s intrinsic tone.

To further improve robustness and mitigate overfitting, diverse data augmentation strategies–including random cropping, rotation, flipping, brightness variation, and controlled Gaussian perturbation–were applied in a geometry-consistent manner to both images and masks. These operations increase intra-class diversity and ensure the model’s adaptability to variations in texture, lighting, and scale, providing a strong foundation for the subsequent fine-grained damage segmentation.

Model deployment

To verify the feasibility and scalability of the proposed method in real-world cultural relic preservation scenarios, this paper deployed the model on a Huawei Cloud general-purpose computing-enhanced elastic cloud server (configuration: 8 vCPU cores, 32 GB of memory). A high-performance, remotely callable inference and visualization system was constructed to support real-time monitoring and intelligent analysis of Dunhuang mural damage.

First, the trained PyTorch model was exported to the ONNX format. FP16 precision quantization and layer fusion optimization were performed using TensorRT to generate a lightweight inference engine file, significantly improving the model’s computational efficiency and response speed.

Next, a RESTful service interface based on the Flask framework was built in the ECS environment. Users can upload mural images via the web or API. The server automatically completes image preprocessing, model inference, and visualization, and returns the detection results to the client. The model’s inference results are then transmitted in real time to a front-end visualization interface via WebSocket. Damaged areas (such as cracks, spalling, and fading) are highlighted in pseudo-color, and statistical information such as confidence level and segmentation area is provided, facilitating remote interpretation and horizontal comparative analysis by experts.

Finally, to ensure system security and scalability, security group rules are configured to open necessary ports. Docker containerized deployment and Huawei Cloud’s auto-scaling policies are combined to enable on-demand instance launch and dynamic resource allocation. This deployment solution effectively improves system stability, resource utilization, and concurrent processing capabilities, laying the technical foundation for the model’s long-term operation in real-world cultural relics preservation tasks.

Results and discussions

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, we conduct experiments on a publicly available Dunhuang mural dataset. To comprehensively evaluate the model’s feature extraction capability, we compare it against two representative lightweight backbone networks commonly used for strong feature representation (PMOFormer8 and XACNN9). Furthermore, to ensure fairness and consistency across experiments, all models are implemented with identical network depth and width, and share the same CNN and ResNet backbone as the baseline.

Dataset description

The Dunhuang murals comprise images created across multiple historical periods (e.g., Northern Wei, Sui–Tang, Five Dynasties, and Song), spanning nearly a millennium and reflecting cross-dynastic artistic characteristics. In terms of subject matter, the corpus includes both religious Buddhist iconography and figural paintings, as well as a large number of decorative motifs, fully demonstrating the richness and diversity of content and embodying a synthesis of sacred and secular elements. Moreover, the compositional complexity varies considerably, ranging from densely populated narrative scenes to concise panels organized around a single theme. At the same time, the use of color and painting techniques differs by period: some murals adopt vivid, saturated palettes, whereas others favor more subdued and restrained combinations; technically, certain works display meticulous, delicate brushwork, while others are characterized by more economical and brisk linework. These differences add marked stylistic complexity to the dataset. Overall, the dataset exhibits pronounced diversity at the level of image content and substantial complexity at the level of style. Such a rich and complex collection requires models to accommodate significant cross-subject and cross-style variation, thereby imposing a more stringent evaluation of generalization performance. From an algorithmic research perspective, MuralDH offers an ideal benchmark for comprehensively assessing model robustness and generalization under highly diverse visual inputs (e.g., in mural-damage segmentation, digital restoration, and image super-resolution).

In our experiments, we utilized the publicly available MuralDH dataset introduced by Ref.30, a data paper in the Nature Research portfolio. The dataset was specifically developed for the digital restoration of Dunhuang murals and contains more than 5000 high-resolution mural images, each cropped and resized to a uniform resolution of pixels. It is organized into three subsets: (1) a damaged-mural segmentation subset of 1000 images with pixel-level masks covering typical damage types—cracks, peeling, and fading—suitable for training and evaluating segmentation models; (2) a super-resolution subset of 500 images for image enhancement and restoration studies; and (3) approximately 3500 unannotated images intended for auxiliary tasks such as image inpainting and self-supervised learning. Especially for the segmentation subset, a careful construction protocol is reported. First, an operational definition of ‘damage’ was established to encompass the diverse and complex deterioration phenomena observed on Dunhuang murals (including cracks, peeling, and fading). Professional annotators then used the LabelMe tool to delineate the boundaries of all damaged regions at the pixel level, explicitly retaining small or low-contrast traces so that subtle damage cues were not lost. A multi-round iterative review and quality-control workflow—initial annotation, cross-checking, and expert adjudication—was applied to ensure consistency and accuracy of the labels; co-registered PNG masks were subsequently exported to guarantee exact image–mask alignment. This process yields high-quality images, detailed annotations, and real-damage labels, imparting substantial complexity and authenticity to the data. The richness of damage types and their fine-grained variations not only reflects the true condition of the murals but also provides a challenging benchmark for training and evaluation, with strong potential to improve model generalization in complex, real-world scenarios.

In this study, we focus exclusively on the segmentation subset with annotated damage masks. The 1,000 annotated images are divided into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio. All cropping and resizing operations are applied consistently to both images and their corresponding masks to ensure spatial alignment. This careful preprocessing allows for fair comparisons across models and ensures reproducibility in our ablation and benchmark experiments.

Robustness analysis

To further evaluate the robustness of the proposed model, Gaussian and Laplace noise with energy densities matched to the original signal noise were introduced, varying within the range of −10 dB to 10 dB to ensure consistent perturbation intensity. The experimental results are shown in Fig. 3.

Segmentation performance under (a) Laplace noise and (b) Gaussian noise with energy densities matched to the original signal. The proposed method exhibits the smallest degradation across all SNR levels, confirming its superior noise robustness.

At the most challenging SNR of −10 dB, the proposed method still achieves an mIoU above 60 under Laplace noise, while XACNN and PMOFormer drop to 55–60%, and CNN/ResNet fall below 50 . Under Gaussian noise at −10 dB, our method reaches approximately 70 mIoU; XACNN and PMOFormer remain within the 65–70 range, whereas CNN and ResNet decline to 55–60 . Across the entire SNR range (−10 to 10 dB), the proposed model consistently maintains the highest performance curve and exhibits the smallest degradation rate, confirming its superior noise robustness and fine-grained feature discrimination capability under adverse imaging conditions.

This improvement can be attributed to the RSAM module, which enhances the network’s ability to capture directional and fine-grained mural features even under severe signal distortion. These results demonstrate that the proposed method not only achieves higher segmentation accuracy but also exhibits better stability and generalization in realistic mural imaging environments.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a lightweight and high-precision instance segmentation method for Dunhuang mural damage localization, based on an improved YOLACT framework. To address the limitations of traditional convolutional models in capturing fine-grained texture and global semantic features, we introduce a Residual Self-Attention Mechanism (RSAM) that integrates lightweight multi-branch convolution with residual enhancement. In addition, depthwise separable convolution (DWCNN) is adopted to significantly reduce the parameter count and computational cost while maintaining high segmentation accuracy. Extensive experiments conducted on the publicly available MuralDH dataset demonstrate that the proposed method achieves an excellent trade-off between mAp and efficiency. Compared with four representative models (CNN, ResNet, PMOFormer, and XACNN), our method attains competitive or superior mAp with substantially fewer parameters and FLOPs. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the essential contributions of the RSAM module, residual connections, and DWCNN to the overall performance. To verify practical applicability, the trained model was deployed on the Huawei Cloud Elastic Computing Service (ECS) as a real-time inference and visualization system. The deployment achieves an inference speed of 28.7 FPS with only 1.18 GB of memory usage, supporting scalable and remote mural damage monitoring through lightweight containerized deployment. This demonstrates that the proposed framework is not only effective in theory but also feasible for real-world cultural heritage protection tasks. Overall, the results suggest that our approach provides a robust, efficient, and deployable solution for fine-grained damage detection in resource-constrained cultural heritage scenarios, offering valuable assistance for the intelligent preservation and restoration of ancient artworks such as the Dunhuang murals.

Safety and Efficacy of Adalimumab Biosimilar in Psoriasis and Hidradenitis Suppurativa: A Clinical Experience
Authors: Raneem H. Najjar, Sarah O. Alrashid, Rnda F. Almahfouz, Hadeel T. Alzahrani, Majed Y. Aleissa, Alanoud R. Hakami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryObjectives:To evaluate the clinical experience, safety, and effectiveness of adalimumab biosimilars in patients with psoriasis and hidradenitis suppurativa.Methods:We conducted a retrospective, single-center observational study between March and September 2024 using electronic medical records from King Abdulaziz Medical City and King Abdullah Specialized Children's Hospital, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Pediatric and adult patients with psoriasis or hidradenitis suppurativa who switched to adalimumab biosimilars between 2016 and 2022 were included. Variables included demographics, disease characteristics, prior therapies, biosimilar use, and adverse events.Results:A total of 57 (93% adults; 50.9% female) patients were included. Obesity was present in 35.1%, and 6 (10.5%) patients had hidradenitis suppurativa. Plaque psoriasis was the most common subtype (72.5%), with psoriatic arthritis in 35.3%. Most patients (89.5%) switched to a biosimilar after treatment with the originator. The median dose and duration were 40 mg and 2–3 years, with high adherence (hidradenitis suppurativa 83.3%; psoriasis 90.2%). All patients with hidradenitis suppurativa improved, whereas 68.6% of patients with psoriasis responded, 19.6% showed no improvement, and 11.8% worsened. Adverse events were infrequent. Patients without obesity and those with prior response to adalimumab were more likely to benefit.Conclusion:Adalimumab biosimilars demonstrate favorable efficacy and safety, particularly in prior responders. Patients with hidradenitis suppurativa showed consistent improvement. The low rate of adverse events and absence of new safety signals support safe clinical use. Further multicenter studies are needed to confirm these findings.

Introduction

Psoriasis and hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) are chronic inflammatory skin disorders with multifactorial etiologies. The United States (U.S.) Food and Drug Administration has approved several biologic therapies for both diseases, most of which target interleukins involved in pathogenesis [1,2]. Adalimumab, a tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitor, binds to TNF and prevents its interaction with TNF receptors, thereby disrupting the inflammatory cascade in conditions such as psoriasis and HS [3]. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved adalimumab for active psoriasis in 2008 and for HS in 2015 [4].

Biosimilars are drugs that closely resemble their reference biologics and contain the same active ingredient [5]. Although manufacturing processes may vary, biosimilars are expected to show no clinically meaningful differences in safety or efficacy compared with their reference products. They also hold promise for reducing treatment costs and improving accessibility [5]. Switching from reference biologics to biosimilars has become increasingly common in hospitals; however, clinicians remain cautious because of limited clinical experience and insufficient published data on safety and efficacy. Few studies have compared adalimumab with its biosimilars in the management of psoriasis and HS, and even less is known about their use among dermatologists in Saudi Arabia [6,7,8]. Evaluating the safety and effectiveness of adalimumab biosimilars in Saudi Arabia is therefore essential. This study aimed to assess the clinical experience, safety, and efficacy of adalimumab biosimilars in psoriasis and HS and to provide a reliable reference for local practice.

Methods

We conducted a single-center, retrospective observational study at King Abdulaziz Medical City and King Abdullah Specialized Children's Hospital, National Guard Health Affairs, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, between March and September 2024. Patient data were retrieved from electronic medical records and analyzed. This study received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of the National Guard Health Affairs.

The study included all pediatric and adult patients diagnosed with HS or psoriasis who were initially treated with the originator adalimumab and later switched to an adalimumab biosimilar between 2016 and 2022. Eligible patients had HS or psoriasis (including chronic plaque, guttate, erythrodermic, and pustular types), with or without joint involvement, and were receiving adalimumab. Patients with incomplete medical records were excluded.

All team members participated in data extraction, and the senior author verified accuracy. Variables included demographics (serial number, medical record number, gender, age, body surface area/Hurley stage), disease characteristics (date of diagnosis, sites of involvement), pre-biologic treatments (topical medications, oral immunosuppressants, immunomodulators, antibiotics, phototherapy), and biologic therapy details (start and stop dates, reason for switching, adverse events). Data were summarized in tables.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation. We compared patients with HS and those with psoriasis regarding demographics, clinical characteristics, outcomes, and complications, using Fisher's exact test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Univariate analysis with Fisher's exact test was used to assess factors associated with biosimilar response. Biosimilar safety and efficacy were also evaluated using Fisher's exact test. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 57 patients were included in the study. As shown in Table 1, most patients (93%) were adults aged 18 years or older. Female patients slightly outnumbered male patients (50.9% vs. 49.1%). Obesity was identified in 35.1% of the cohort. Among the 6 patients diagnosed with HS, 2 were active smokers. Plaque psoriasis (72.5%) was the most common subtype, followed by guttate psoriasis (7.8%). Body surface area involvement ranged from 3% to 10% in 23.5% of patients. Psoriatic arthritis was present in 35.3% of participants, and nail involvement was observed in 23.5%. Most patients (89.5%) were switched to an adalimumab biosimilar after treatment with the originator adalimumab for psoriasis, whereas the remaining 10.5% had HS. The median adalimumab dose was 40 mg, administered for 2–3 years.

As shown in Table 2, patients with HS and those with psoriasis received similar median doses of adalimumab (p = 0.829) and had comparable median treatment durations (p = 0.639). Adherence rates were high, with 5 patients with HS (83.3%) and 46 patients with psoriasis (90.2%) maintaining regular medication use. All 6 patients with HS showed clinical improvement, and 44 patients with psoriasis (89.8%) responded positively to treatment.

Following the switch to an adalimumab biosimilar (Table 3), patients with HS and those with psoriasis received comparable median doses (p = 0.829) and had similar treatment durations (p = 0.204). Adherence to the biosimilar remained high in both groups, at 83.3% among patients with HS and 90.2% among patients with psoriasis. All 6 patients with HS continued to show clinical improvement after the switch. Among patients with psoriasis, 35 (68.6%) responded favorably, 10 (19.6%) showed no improvement, and 6 (11.8%) experienced worsening symptoms. Adverse events occurred in one patient with HS (16.7%) and 4 patients with psoriasis (7.8%). The most common adverse events in both groups were bone pain and joint stiffness, whereas infections and hepatitis were the next most frequent among patients with psoriasis.

Discussion

This study evaluated the safety and efficacy of the adalimumab biosimilar Amgevita in patients with psoriasis and HS at King Abdulaziz Medical City, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Amgevita is the first adalimumab biosimilar considered to be as safe and effective as the originator, Humira. A total of 57 patients who were initially treated with Humira were switched to Amgevita following a hospital policy change regarding biologic therapies, primarily driven by cost considerations to improve pharmacoeconomic efficiency. This approach is consistent with previous reports demonstrating comparable efficacy of biosimilars [9].

In this cohort, the incidence of adverse events associated with Humira was very low, approaching zero. After switching to Amgevita, 5 patients reported side effects, including bone pain, joint stiffness, and grade 2/4 biosimilar-induced hepatitis, which led to treatment discontinuation. Despite these events, statistical analysis revealed no significant difference between the groups, suggesting that biosimilars remain safe after switching. Reports of hepatitis induced by adalimumab biosimilars are lacking in the published literature. Phan et al. [9] reported comparable effectiveness and safety of biosimilars but noted a higher likelihood of treatment discontinuation. In childhood psoriasis, Bertoli et al. [10] demonstrated that adalimumab biosimilars were effective and safe, with transitions from the originator to biosimilars showing no impact on treatment outcomes or safety. None of the patients experienced significant adverse effects, although some who switched showed a tendency to discontinue therapy.

Differences in biosimilar outcomes were observed between patients with HS and those with psoriasis. Effectiveness was higher among patients with HS, with no cases of symptom worsening. In contrast, approximately 31% of patients with psoriasis showed no improvement or experienced worsening, likely reflecting differences in disease mechanisms or individual variation in response.

All 6 patients with HS who switched from the originator to the biosimilar demonstrated clinical improvement, including one patient with suboptimal adherence to Humira. These results suggest that switching did not compromise efficacy in HS. This contrasts with an Italian study that reported a higher rate of loss of effectiveness among patients with HS after switching, leading the authors to recommend caution because of potential adverse effects on disease control [11]. Similarly, a Spanish study found reduced efficacy and adherence in HS after switching to biosimilars [8]. In contrast, the present data showed no decrease in efficacy, indicating that switching can be safe and beneficial in HS, as reflected by lesion improvement.

Among patients with psoriasis, 68.6% experienced improvement after switching to the biosimilar, whereas 31.4% reported no benefit or worsening. A Danish study on biosimilars in psoriasis identified lack of efficacy as the main reason for discontinuation, supporting our observation that biosimilars may be less effective for some patients with psoriasis [8]. In the present cohort, 10 (19.6%) patients showed no improvement, and 6 (11.8%) patients experienced worsening after the switch.

Patients who previously improved on the originator were more likely to respond favorably to the biosimilar in both HS and psoriasis, consistent with findings from the Danish study [8]. Patients without obesity were also more likely to show improvement with the biosimilar, aligning with a systematic review and meta-analysis showing that obesity increases the odds of anti-TNF therapy failure by 60% across inflammatory diseases, including psoriasis [12].

Given the limited regional evidence, these results help bridge the knowledge gap, support cost-effective treatment strategies, and build confidence in biosimilar use among clinicians and patients. Future large-scale randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up periods are needed to confirm these findings and further assess the long-term immunogenicity and safety of adalimumab biosimilars. A particularly valuable direction would be a multicenter, longitudinal study evaluating the long-term safety, efficacy, immunogenicity, and patient outcomes of adalimumab biosimilars in HS and psoriasis across diverse populations.

A key strength of this study is that it provides pioneering region-specific data on adalimumab biosimilar use in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. A notable limitation, however, is its single-center design, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings. Future research should adopt a multicenter approach with a larger sample size to strengthen the robustness and applicability of the results.

This single-center study provides real-world data on the safety and efficacy of adalimumab and its biosimilar in patients with psoriasis and HS. The findings show that the biosimilar maintained a favorable efficacy profile in both diseases, particularly in HS, where all patients demonstrated clinical improvement after switching, indicating that the transition did not compromise disease control. Statistical analysis revealed that patients who had previously improved with adalimumab were significantly more likely to respond favorably to the biosimilar, highlighting treatment history as a valuable predictor of success after switching. No significant association was observed between biosimilar response and adverse events, supporting its acceptable safety profile. The rate of adverse events remained low, and no new safety signals were identified following the switch.

Overall, these results support the safe and effective use of adalimumab biosimilars, particularly in patients with a prior favorable response to the originator. The findings add to regional evidence and may strengthen clinical confidence in adopting biosimilars as cost-effective options for managing psoriasis and HS. Future multicenter, longitudinal studies with larger sample sizes are needed to confirm these results, assess long-term outcomes, and inform individualized treatment strategies.

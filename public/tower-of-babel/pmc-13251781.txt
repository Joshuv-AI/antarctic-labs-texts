Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in HER2-expressing gynecological malignancies: Clinical evidence, safety profile, and future directions

Published: 2026

Background

Gynecological malignancies, such as endometrial, ovarian, and cervical cancers, collectively continue to pose a significant global health challenge, particularly in advanced and recurrent settings, where prognosis remains poor and therapeutic options are limited. In developed countries, endometrial cancer remains the leading gynecological malignancy, with growing incidence driven by aging populations and increasing obesity rates. Ovarian cancer accounts for the highest mortality due to its frequent late-stage presentation, while cervical cancer continues to contribute substantially to global cancer deaths, particularly in resource-limited settings, despite advances in screening and HPV vaccination (Siegel et al., 2023, Sung et al., 2021, Arbyn et al., 2020).

Advanced gynecological malignancies are predominantly managed with platinum-based chemotherapy, antiangiogenic agents, and, in selected settings, immune checkpoint inhibitors. Despite these approaches, treatment responses are often not durable, with resistance frequently emerging over time. Management of recurrent mismatch repair proficient/non-mismatch repair deficient endometrial cancer remains clinically challenging, particularly due to treatment-related toxicity and eventual resistance to currently available systemic therapies (Makker et al., 2022). Platinum-resistant ovarian cancer remains associated with poor outcomes, while recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer continues to show unfavorable outcomes despite the incorporation of immunotherapy (Lheureux et al., 2019, Colombo et al., 2021). These challenges highlight the urgent need for more effective, biomarker-based therapeutic approaches.

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) has been identified as a relevant therapeutic target in selected gynecological malignancies, particularly uterine serous carcinoma, where HER2 overexpression or amplification occurs in a substantial proportion of cases. HER2 expression has been reported in subsets of ovarian and cervical cancers (McNamara et al., 2023, Chung et al., 2019). However, HER2-targeted therapies such as trastuzumab have historically produced variable and limited responses beyond selected endometrial cancer populations (Fader et al., 2018). The role of HER2-targeted therapy in gynecological oncology became particularly evident in uterine serous carcinoma. A randomized phase II trial conducted by Fader et al. found that combining trastuzumab with carboplatin-paclitaxel resulted in significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in HER2-positive uterine serous carcinoma, reinforcing HER2 as an important therapeutic target in endometrial cancer(Fader et al., 2018). These limited responses may result from tumor heterogeneity and variable HER2 expression, as well as intrinsic resistance mechanisms described in gynecological malignancies (McNamara et al., 2023, Buza, 2021).

Antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) have emerged as an important therapeutic strategy in oncology by coupling antibody specificity with targeted chemotherapy delivery. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), a HER2-directed ADC, possesses a high drug-to-antibody ratio and a membrane-permeable payload, enabling a bystander effect that may mitigate intratumoral heterogeneity (Modi et al., 2020, Ogitani et al., 2016). Trials involving diverse tumor types have reported meaningful and durable antitumor activity, leading to tumor-agnostic regulatory approval for unresectable or metastatic HER2-overexpressing (IHC 3 + ) solid tumors after prior therapy (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

T-DXd represents a promising HER2-targeted therapy for gynecological malignancies, with emerging evidence supporting its activity across multiple tumor types, particularly in tumors with high HER2 expression. This review focuses on its biological rationale, clinical evidence, safety profile, and implications for patient selection in advanced disease.

Prevalence of HER2 expression and Distribution across histological subtypes in gynecological malignancies

Biology of HER2

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that belongs to the human epidermal growth factor (ErbB or HER) group of receptor proteins. Downstream signaling from these receptors regulates cell survival and proliferation and plays a key role in tumorigenesis (Cheng, 2024). HER2 has no known ligand and exists in a conformation that favors dimerization with other members of the ErbB receptor family. To exert its downstream effects, it forms heterodimers with other HER proteins, most commonly EGFR/HER2 and HER2/HER3, or may form homodimers with itself (Bai et al., 2023). Upon dimerization, autophosphorylation of intracellular domains activates signaling pathways such as the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K/AKT) (Iqbal and Iqbal, 2014). HER2 overexpression has been implicated in development of several gynecological cancers, and its study has major biologic and therapeutic relevance.

HER2 testing considerations

Currently, the evaluation of HER2 status depends primarily on immunohistochemistry (IHC) which determines protein expression, followed by confirmation with Fluorescence in-situ hybridization (FISH) which detects gene amplifications (Buza, 2021). Most scoring systems are extrapolated from breast and gastric cancer guidelines. Unique staining patterns such as incomplete or basolateral/lateral membrane staining are present in most gynecological malignancies. Another factor to consider is intratumoral heterogeneity, which is the variable expression and amplification of HER2 among tumor cells. Taking into account these patterns, and intratumoral heterogeneity in gynecological tumors, these scoring systems have been modified (Buza, 2021). Currently three scoring criteria are used for HER2 status evaluation including the 2016 ASCO/CAP gastric HER2 testing criteria, the 2018 ASCO/CAP criteria for HER2 testing in breast cancer and the endometrial specific HER2 testing criteria by Fader et al. now incorporated into CAP biomarker reporting template. According to these criteria, HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) scoring is categorized into four groups. Following IHC, in-situ hybridization (FISH) is typically performed in tumors with IHC 2 + scores for confirmation, and a HER2/CEP17 ratio of > 2.0 is considered amplified (Zannoni et al., 2026). Some newer methods using bright field in-situ hybridization techniques such as chromogenic in-situ hybridization (CISH) and silver-stained in situ hybridization (SISH) have also been introduced for determination of HER2 (Penault-Llorca et al., 2009).

Currently, there is no universally accepted testing criterion for most of the gynecological malignancies. Scores derived from breast and gastric scoring systems are still in use. Modified HER2 testing criteria derived from the work of Fader et al. and reflected in College of American Pathologists (CAP) aligned pathology reporting practices, are primarily applicable to uterine serous carcinoma (Buza, 2021, Buza et al., 2013). The lack of standardization for other gynecological malignancies leads to inconsistent reporting of prevalence across studies and complicates trials. Establishing uniform criteria will be essential as HER2-targeted therapies continue to expand in gynecological oncology.

HER2 expression across gynecological cancers

HER2 expression in endometrial carcinoma

Uterine serous carcinoma

Uterine serous carcinoma has the highest expression rates of HER2 overexpression and amplification among all gynecological cancers. Using standard HER2 testing and scoring criteria such as IHC HercepTest and FISH PathVysion, it has been estimated that nearly one third, or up to 25–40% of all uterine serous carcinomas have HER2 overexpression and amplification (Navarro Sanchez et al., 2023). HER2 overexpression and amplification are associated with poor clinical outcomes, advanced stage at presentation and increased recurrence rates (Ferriss et al., 2021). The concordance between IHC and FISH among uterine serous carcinomas has been reported to be below the recommended 95% concordance rate; therefore, both IHC and FISH are currently recommended testing methods (Buza et al., 2013). Furthermore, intratumoral heterogeneity is commonly observed in gynecological malignancies, with HER2 expression differing across tumor regions within the same specimen, including areas of intense membranous staining adjacent to HER2-negative areas or regions demonstrating incomplete basolateral staining patterns (McNamara et al., 2023, Buza et al., 2013).

Other endometrial cancer histologies

HER2 positivity has also been documented in other types of endometrial cancers, most notably uterine carcinosarcoma and high-grade endometrioid endometrial carcinomas. Notably, these subtypes have an aberrant p53 immunostaining pattern. HER2 overexpression has been found in 15–18% of uterine carcinosarcomas, also demonstrating intratumoral heterogeneity and basolateral/lateral staining patterns (Buza, 2021). In grade 3 endometrioid cancers, results vary across studies, with 12–31% cases showing positivity for overexpression of HER2 (Erickson et al., 2020).

HER2 expression in ovarian cancer

Although less common than uterine serous carcinoma, HER2 overexpression has been identified across multiple ovarian carcinoma histological subtypes with substantial variability in prevalence. Among these, mucinous ovarian carcinoma demonstrates the highest rates of HER2 overexpression and amplification, with reported positivity rates reaching approximately 18–26% in some studies (English et al., 2013, McCaughan et al., 2012). In contrast, HER2 expression appears less frequent in other ovarian subtypes, including clear cell, serous, and endometrioid carcinomas, where reported prevalence is generally lower and more variable (English et al., 2013, McCaughan et al., 2012). These differences likely reflect the molecular heterogeneity of ovarian cancer and variability of HER2 testing methodologies across studies.

HER2 expression in cervical cancer

HER2 overexpression and amplification pattern in cervical cancer are highly heterogeneous and vary significantly among histological subtypes. Earlier studies reported HER2 positivity in up to 38.7% of cervical cancer cases and suggested an association with lymph node metastasis (Hale et al., 1992). However, more recent pooled analyses demonstrate a substantially lower overall prevalence, with a pooled estimate of HER2 overexpression estimated at 5.7% (CI 95%: 1.5% − 11.7%) and amplification of 1.2% (CI 95%: 0.0% − 5.8%) (Itkin et al., 2021). HER2 expression appears to be more frequently observed in cervical adenocarcinoma compared with squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), while gastric-type endocervical adenocarcinoma has emerged as a particularly relevant subtype due to its aggressive clinical behavior and relatively higher rates of HER2 overexpression and amplification (English et al., 2013, Itkin et al., 2021). These findings suggest that HER2-targeted therapies may have greater therapeutic relevance in selected cervical adenocarcinoma subtypes rather than cervical SCC. Mechanistic rationale for Trastuzumab deruxtecan

Antibody–Drug conjugate (ADC) Structure

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are a cancer treatment approach, developed from Paul Ehrlich’s ‘magic bullet’ strategy (Strebhardt and Ullrich, 2008). This method enables precise transport of cytotoxic compounds to HER2-expressing cancer cells while minimizing damage to normal tissue (Fu et al., 2022, Colombo et al., 2024, Tolcher, 2016). Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd, DS-8201a), a next-generation HER2-targeting antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), has structural features that enhance its potency compared with earlier ADCs, featuring a drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of approximately 7.8, thereby increasing intratumoral delivery of the cytotoxic payload and improving antitumor efficacy, while expanding the treatment window (Ogitani et al., 2016, Wang et al., 2025). T-DXd is composed of three crucial elements: an anti-human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) antibody, an enzymatically cleavable tetrapeptide linker, and a novel topoisomerase 1 inhibitor, deruxtecan (DXd) (Xie et al., 2026, Fang et al., 2022). T-DXd is specifically developed to target tumor cells overexpressing human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). When T-DXd attaches to cell-surface HER2, the resulting ADC-antigen complex is taken into the cancer cell via receptor-mediated endocytosis. The endosome fuses with the lysosome, and the complex undergoes proteolytic degradation and releases the cytotoxic payload (DXd) inside the tumor cell, which then disrupts DNA replication by inducing DNA damage through inhibition of topoisomerases I, ultimately inducing cell cycle arrest and programmed cell death (Yver et al., 2020, Hudis, 2007, Tsuchikama and An, 2018).

Antibody

Trastuzumab, a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody, plays an essential role in the drug action because of its selective affinity for the extracellular domain of HER2, which allows DXd to be precisely delivered to HER2-expressing tumor cells, thereby enhancing selective payload delivery and improving therapeutic efficacy (Wang et al., 2025, Junutula et al., 2008). Being an IgG1 mAb, trastuzumab exhibits a relatively long serum half-life of approximately 21 days, which extends its availability in circulation compared to other IgGs, high solubility, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties. More importantly, binding of trastuzumab to HER2 facilitates receptor-mediated internalization, which is essential for intracellular delivery of the cytotoxic payload. Additionally, it demonstrates minimal non-specific immunogenicity, which lowers the risk of non-specific immune activation, and strong interaction with Fcγ receptors, which are essential for facilitating antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) (Fu et al., 2022, Yver et al., 2020).

Linker

T-DXd is designed with an enzymatically cleavable glycine–glycine–phenylalanine–glycine (GGFG) tetrapeptide linker that ensures circulatory stability while enabling controlled intracellular payload release following lysosomal degradation. The linker is selectively cleaved by tumor-associated lysosomal proteases such as cathepsins B and L, promoting efficient release of deruxtecan within malignant cells. This strategy reduces premature systemic exposure while enhancing intracellular targeting (Ogitani et al., 2016, Yver et al., 2020, Tsuchikama and An, 2018).

Cytotoxic payload

Deruxtecan, an exatecan derivative, is a DNA topoisomerase 1 inhibitor that has demonstrated higher potency than conventional topoisomerase I inhibitors such as SN-38 (Nakada et al., 2016). It is a highly membrane-permeable drug; thus, once inside the tumor cell, it not only exerts antitumor effects by inducing apoptosis but also diffuses out of the tumor cell, thereby producing bystander-killing effects on the surrounding tumor microenvironment (TME) (Tolcher, 2016, Jørgensen, 2023, Ogitani et al., 2016).

Bystander effect

One key feature of T-DXd that makes it unique among other ADCs is its bystander effect, which is mediated by its cleavable linker system and membrane permeability of deruxtecan (DXd), which enables passive diffusion of DXd from targeted HER2-expressing tumor cells to neighboring tumor cells, including those with low or heterogeneous HER2 expression. This phenomenon helps to widen the drug action within the tumor microenvironment (TME) (Ogitani et al., 2016, Giugliano et al., 2022). This effect primarily affects neighboring tumor cells, although some off-target exposure cannot be completely excluded. However, it does not eliminate safety concerns of the bystander effect, as available data remains limited (Giugliano et al., 2022).

Clinical activity of Trastuzumab deruxtecan in gynecological malignancies

The current clinical evidence supporting trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in HER2-expressing gynecological malignancies is primarily derived from the phase II DESTINY-PanTumor02 basket study and the STATICE trial in uterine carcinosarcoma, both of which demonstrated clinically meaningful responses in HER2-expressing tumors (1,2). Across tumor types enrolled in DESTINY-PanTumor02, including endometrial, cervical, and ovarian cohorts, the magnitude of response was consistently higher among tumors with strong HER2 expression (1,3). Given the heterogeneity of HER2 expression and clinical outcomes across gynecological tumor types, the available evidence for T-DXd is best considered within the context of individual malignancies. Endometrial cancer

In the DESTINY-PanTumor02 study, 40 patients with endometrial cancer demonstrated robust therapeutic responses to trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). The overall response rate was 57.5%, with higher responses among tumors with HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) 3 + expression (84.6%) compared with IHC 2 + tumors (47.1%) (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Consistent with findings observed across the DESTINY-PanTumor02 trial, patients with HER2 IHC 3 + tumors achieved more favorable outcomes than those with IHC 2 + expression. In the endometrial subgroup, patients with two or more prior lines of therapy had a median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of 11.1 months and 26.0 months, respectively (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Oaknin et al., 2024).

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) represents an effective subsequent-line treatment option for endometrial cancers with HER2 IHC 2 + and 3 + expression (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Oaknin et al., 2024).

Uterine carcinosarcoma

Uterine carcinosarcoma (UCS) is a rare and highly aggressive cancer with few systemic treatment options. In the phase II STATICE study, T-DXd demonstrated notable clinical activity with a manageable safety profile in patients with advanced or recurrent uterine carcinosarcoma across varying levels of HER2 expression, including HER2 IHC 1 + tumors. The ORR was highest in tumors with stronger HER2 expression, reaching 54.5% in HER2 IHC 2+/3 + tumors and 70.0% in HER2 IHC 3 + subgroup(Nishikawa et al., 2023). These findings are particularly relevant given the marked intratumoral heterogeneity and mixed epithelial-mesenchymal histology characteristics of UCS, which can limit conventional HER2-targeted therapies. The bystander effect and membrane-permeable payload of T-DXd may partly explain its therapeutic activity in this setting (Nishikawa et al., 2023).

Ovarian cancer

The ovarian cohort in the DESTINY-PanTumor02 study consisted of 40 patients and demonstrated encouraging antitumor activity despite extensive prior treatment exposure (median of three prior lines), with an objective response rate (ORR) of 45% (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Tumors with strong HER2 expression showed improved responses, with subgroup analyses reporting an ORR up to 63.6% among HER2 IHC 3 + ovarian cancers (Oaknin et al., 2024, Ettorre et al., 2025). In a population with substantial prior therapy (median three previous regimens), the ovarian cohort achieved a median overall survival (OS) of 13.2 months, increasing up to 20.0 months in HER2 IHC 3 + tumors, supporting a potential role in later-line treatment settings (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Prospective, histology-specific evidence remains limited. However, published case reports have documented marked responses to T-DXd in HER2-expressing ovarian clear cell carcinoma, suggesting biological plausibility and supporting further histology-specific investigation (Kong et al., 2024).

Even though DESTINY-PanTumor02 did not specifically enroll platinum-resistant populations, the heavily pretreated ovarian cohort suggests that many patients likely had platinum-resistant or refractory cancer, highlighting the clinical relevance of these responses in the later-line treatment setting (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Cervical cancer

In the DESTINY-PanTumor02 study, the cervical cancer cohort of 40 patients demonstrated meaningful clinical antitumor activity with T-DXd, achieving an overall ORR of 50%, with responses reported as high as 75% in tumors with HER2 IHC 3 + expression in subgroup analyses. The cervical cohort also demonstrated a median OS of 13.6 months, while survival was not reached in HER2 IHC 3 + tumors and 11.5 months in IHC 2 + tumors, indicating improved survival outcomes in tumors with strong HER2 expression (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Oaknin et al., 2024). As platinum-based chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy becomes more widely used in the first-line metastatic disease setting, these findings support consideration of T-DXd as subsequent-line therapy for HER2-expressing disease, especially in IHC 3 + tumors (Colombo et al., 2021, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Tumor-Agnostic approval and clinical considerations

The DESTINY-PanTumor02 trial showed activity across several HER2-expressing solid tumors, supporting the rationale for a tumor-agnostic therapeutic approach (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Oaknin et al., 2024).

In April 2024, accelerated approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) allowed the use of T-DXd in adults with advanced HER2-positive (IHC 3 + ) solid tumors that are unresectable or metastatic, have progressed after prior treatment and have no suitable treatment options (Oaknin et al., 2024, Vranic and Gatalica, 2024).

In routine clinical settings, tumor-agnostic eligibility is usually based on confirmed HER2 IHC 3 + tumors and prior systemic therapy in patients with limited therapeutic alternatives (Vranic and Gatalica, 2024). Clinically, this highlights the importance of HER2 testing in recurrent gynecological cancers and a potential role for T-DXd in later-line management, with appropriate monitoring for adverse events, particularly interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis (Oaknin et al., 2024, Vranic and Gatalica, 2024). Current evidence suggests that patients with HER2 IHC 3 + tumors derive the greatest therapeutic benefit from T-DXd, supporting careful biomarker-driven patient selection(Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Oaknin et al., 2024).

Recent updates to National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines have incorporated T-DXd as a therapeutic option for recurrent HER2-positive endometrial, ovarian, and cervical carcinomas, typically defined by HER2 IHC scores of 2 + and 3 + . In uterine carcinosarcoma, the STATICE trial demonstrated that T-DXd retained clinically relevant activity even in tumors with low HER2 expression, including HER2 IHC 1 + disease(Nishikawa et al., 2023). These recommendations are supported by clinical activity reported in DESTINY-PanTumor02 and STATICE, particularly among tumors with stronger HER2 expression(Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Nishikawa et al., 2023, Oaknin et al., 2024).

Safety and toxicity profile

While T-DXd has shown substantial therapeutic activity in HER2-expressing tumors, including gynecological malignancies, its administration requires careful consideration of a distinctive safety profile. Toxicities are linked to both the antibody component and cytotoxic payload, and include pulmonary, hematologic, gastrointestinal, and cardiac adverse events. These have been characterized in major trials including DESTINY-PanTumor02 and DESTINY-Breast01 (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

T-DXd is typically administered at a standard intravenous dose of 5.4 mg/kg every three weeks, although dose reductions and treatment modifications are commonly necessary to address treatment-related toxicities (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Management of T-DXd-related toxicities commonly includes temporary discontinuation, dose delay, or sequential reduction for recurrent or severe adverse events including neutropenia, gastrointestinal toxicity, and ILD/pneumonitis. Supportive care measures such as antiemetic therapy, corticosteroid administration for suspected ILD, and selective use of hematopoietic growth factor support in selected neutropenic patients, may facilitate ongoing treatment and improve overall tolerability (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Alanazi et al., 2025).

Interstitial lung disease (ILD)

Among the adverse events associated with T-DXd, interstitial lung disease (ILD) or pneumonitis represents the most clinically significant toxicity, affecting roughly 10–15% of patients in trials, with most cases classified as low grade (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Results from the DESTINY-PanTumor02 indicated that ILD was observed in a minority of patients receiving T-DXd, but remained a clinically relevant cause of treatment interruption or discontinuation (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Earlier DESTINY trials identified ILD as a hallmark toxicity of T-DXd, with rare fatal pneumonitis cases reported, emphasizing the need for early recognition and timely management (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Although the exact pathogenesis of ILD related to T-DXd is not fully established, proposed mechanisms include immune-driven inflammatory reactions and off-target accumulation of drug within pulmonary tissue. Observational analyses have identified potential risk factors such as pre-existing lung disease, Asian ethnicity, increased cumulative exposure, and a history of thoracic radiation (Alanazi et al., 2025).

Careful clinical monitoring is essential during T-DXd treatment, as ILD may manifest with non-specific symptoms such as cough or dyspnea, and may occasionally be asymptomatic and detected only on imaging. Prompt interruption of therapy and clinical evaluation are recommended when ILD is suspected, along with initiation of corticosteroids if ILD is confirmed (Modi et al., 2020, Alanazi et al., 2025). In cases of grade ≥ 2 pneumonitis, T-DXd is generally permanently discontinued (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). This precaution is particularly relevant in heavily pre-treated gynecological oncology populations, especially in patients previously exposed to thoracic radiation, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or multiple prior lines of systemic chemotherapy, which may increase susceptibility to treatment-related pulmonary toxicity(Modi et al., 2020, Alanazi et al., 2025).

Hematological toxicity

Hematologic adverse events are frequently observed with T-DXd, reflecting the cytotoxic effects of its topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. Frequently reported toxicities include neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. The most notable hematologic toxicity is neutropenia, which is generally manageable with dose reduction or supportive care (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Within the DESTINY-PanTumor02 study, a meaningful proportion of patients experienced hematologic adverse toxicities, which were typically manageable and reversible with appropriate interventions (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Severe neutropenia (grade ≥ 3) was reported in a relatively small number of patients, and febrile neutropenia occurred infrequently, consistent with earlier T-DXd studies across HER2-expressing malignancies (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Regular monitoring of blood counts is advised during therapy with T-DXd, as significant cytopenias may require dose modification or temporary discontinuation. Nevertheless, the hematological safety profile is generally manageable and seldom precludes continued treatment when appropriately monitored (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Gastrointestinal toxicity

Gastrointestinal toxicities are among the most frequently observed adverse events with T-DXd, with symptoms including nausea, vomiting, decreased appetite, and fatigue. These effects are largely related to the cytotoxic payload component. Most of these toxicities occur at grade 1–2 severity and can be effectively managed with standard supportive care. Data from the DESTINY clinical program indicate that nausea is the predominant gastrointestinal adverse event with T-DXd, occurring in more than half of treated patients, although severe cases are relatively uncommon (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). Timely antiemetic intervention and anticipatory management of side effects can help optimize treatment tolerability (Modi et al., 2020, Notini et al., 2024).

Additional gastrointestinal symptoms, such as diarrhea and constipation, may also occur but are typically mild and manageable with supportive care, dietary changes, and dose modification when necessary, allowing most patients to continue therapy (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

Cardiac safety

Since T-DXd retains the trastuzumab backbone that targets the HER2 receptor, the risk of cardiotoxicity must be considered. HER2 signaling is involved in cardiomyocyte survival mechanisms, and inhibition of this pathway has historically been associated with left ventricular dysfunction in patients receiving HER2-targeted therapies (Copeland-Halperin et al., 2019).

Evidence from large clinical trials suggests that the cardiotoxic effects associated with T-DXd are relatively rare, with occasional asymptomatic reductions in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). Baseline cardiac evaluation and regular monitoring of left ventricular ejection fraction are advised particularly in patients with underlying cardiovascular disease or previous exposure to cardiotoxic treatments (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

In general, the risk of cardiac toxicity associated with T-DXd appears lower than that observed with earlier HER2-directed agents. However, continued cardiac monitoring is recommended in patients with advanced gynecological malignancies (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Copeland-Halperin et al., 2019).

Taken together, while T-DXd demonstrates a generally manageable safety profile, its use requires proactive monitoring and early intervention strategies, particularly for ILD, which remains the most clinically significant toxicity. Endometrial Cancer: First-Line therapy (DESTINY-Endometrial01)

A global phase III randomized trial is currently evaluating approximately 600 patients with advanced or recurrent HER2-expressing endometrial cancer and mismatch repair proficient (pMMR)/non MSI-high disease. This Phase III trial (NCT06989112) examines how well trastuzumab deruxtecan at 5.4 mg/kg performs when paired with either rilvegostomig or pembrolizumab and compared with standard chemotherapy. Patients are randomized into three parallel arms of treatment (Slomovitz et al., 2025, AstraZeneca, 2026). The primary outcome to be evaluated is progression-free survival (PFS). The secondary endpoints to be evaluated are overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DoR), and safety and tolerability (Slomovitz et al., 2025, AstraZeneca, 2026). This trial explores whether combining ADCs with immunotherapy outperforms first-line treatment outcomes (AstraZeneca, 2026).

Endometrial Cancer: Adjuvant therapy (DESTINY-Endometrial02)

In the phase III DESTINY-Endometrial02 trial (NCT07022483), T-DXd is being evaluated against investigator’s choice chemotherapy, usually carboplatin-paclitaxel, in patients with completely resected, high risk HER2-expressing endometrial cancer. With PFS as the primary endpoint, the study explores whether ADC-based approach can improve outcomes compared with standard chemotherapy (Daiichi Sankyo, 2026a).

NRG-GYO26 trial

The ongoing phase III trial NRG-GYO26 (NCT05256225) is evaluating the addition of HER2-targeted therapy to paclitaxel-carboplatin chemotherapy in HER2-positive uterine serous carcinoma and carcinosarcoma. The study compares chemotherapy alone versus chemotherapy combined with trastuzumab/hyaluronidase-oysk (Herceptin Hylecta) or pertuzumab/trastuzumab/hyaluronidase-zzxf (PHESGO), with PFS and OS as primary endpoints. This trial may further clarify the role of HER2-directed therapies in aggressive endometrial malignancies(National Cancer Institute, 2026).

Ovarian Cancer: Maintenance therapy (DESTINY-Ovarian01)

The DESTINY-Ovarian01 (NCT06819007) is a phase III trial evaluating whether adding T-DXd with bevacizumab improves efficacy and safety outcomes versus bevacizumab monotherapy when used as first-line maintenance therapy in patients with HER2 expressing (IHC 3+/ 2+ /1 + ) advanced high-grade epithelial ovarian cancer. Approximately 580 patients, following standard first-line treatment, are assigned to either treatment group. The main outcome measure is PFS, particularly in HER2-positive subgroups, with OS and other efficacy endpoints evaluated as secondary measures(González-Martín et al., 2025, Daiichi Sankyo, 2026b). The study aims to determine the survival outcomes in combination with T-DXd versus bevacizumab monotherapy.

Biomarker refinement and Competing ADCs

Current research increasingly includes ERBB2 gene changes, including amplification and mutations, to refine enrollment (Polidano et al., 2025). This reflects broader efforts to expand biomarker strategies beyond conventional HER2 IHC-based selection (Andrikopoulou et al., 2024). Looking beyond traditional HER2 thresholds while testing newer antibody-drug conjugates, what stands out is the STATICE trial’s results in uterine carcinosarcoma − T-DXd demonstrated activity, including in HER2-low tumors, with response rates reaching 54.5% for IHC 2+/3 + and climbing to 70% when only IHC 3 + tumors were considered (Nishikawa et al., 2023). Such findings challenge old assumptions about who qualifies for treatment. At the same time, alternative anti-HER2 conjugates are being evaluated in clinical trials focused on women's cancers; one example is disitamab vedotin (RC48), undergoing evaluation through early-phase clinical trials across HER2-expressing solid tumors (Wang et al., 2024). Other experimental options like BL-M07D1 and BNT323 are currently in early-phase clinical development (Polidano et al., 2025). Together, these paths, mixing ADCs with immunotherapy, widening genetic markers, and introducing next-gen drugs, are likely to influence future HER2-targeted therapeutic strategies.

Future directions

Despite promising signals of efficacy, the role of T-DXd in gynecological cancers is not yet fully established. Current evidence largely originates from basket trials such as DESTINY-PanTumor02, which include small and diverse gynecological subpopulations. Dedicated prospective trials focused on specific histologies, including uterine serous carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, and clear cell ovarian carcinoma, are needed to better characterize clinical benefit and long-term outcomes (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024).

A key limitation in gynecological oncology is the lack of standardized HER2 testing criteria. Tumor heterogeneity and discordance between HER2 amplification and protein expression further complicate consistent assessment (Buza, 2021, Ettorre et al., 2025, Lee et al., 2025). Accurate patient selection may be improved by standardizing IHC scoring criteria, incorporating in-situ hybridization techniques, and utilizing next-generation sequencing or circulating biomarkers to reduce misclassification (Lee et al., 2025).

As the predominant safety concern with T-DXd, ILD underscores the importance of refined risk stratification strategies, particularly given the heterogeneity in reported incidence (Modi et al., 2020, Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Li et al., 2022). Future research should focus on identifying clinical and molecular predictors of ILD while developing validated monitoring and early intervention strategies to reduce morbidity and improve tolerability.

With the evolution of therapeutic strategies, the appropriate placement of T-DXd, whether after conventional treatments or as part of combination approaches, has yet to be established (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024, Lee et al., 2025). Biomarker-based sequencing strategies, combined with comparative effectiveness studies, will play a key role in improving clinical outcomes.

Therefore, future efforts should prioritize prospective validation, biomarker refinement, toxicity mitigation, and rational integration into treatment algorithms to optimize the clinical potential of T-DXd.

Conclusion

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) has emerged as a next-generation HER2-targeted agent that offers a notable therapeutic advancement in gynecological malignancies. It exhibits robust and durable antitumor activity, particularly in tumors with high HER2 expression (IHC 3 + ).

Despite its clinical benefits, T-DXd carries important safety concerns, particularly ILD, which necessitates careful monitoring and timely management.

Although current data support the use of T-DXd in heavily pretreated populations, the existing evidence is largely based on basket trials and limited histology-specific cohorts, emphasizing the need for well-designed, prospective, histology-focused studies to clarify its role.

Thus, T-DXd may significantly advance HER2-targeted therapy in gynecological oncology, particularly in tumors with high HER2 (IHC 3 + ) expression. However, optimal implementation will require thoughtful patient selection, close safety surveillance, and ongoing clinical investigation. With continued prospective validation and biomarker refinement, T-DXd may become a cornerstone of HER2-directed therapy in gynecological malignancies.

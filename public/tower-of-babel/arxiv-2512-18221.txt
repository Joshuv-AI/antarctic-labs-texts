On potentials for sub-Laplacians and geometric applications
Shiguang Ma

Abstract
. In this paper we extend the research on potential theory and its geometric applications from Euclidean spaces to homogeneous Carnot groups. We introduce a new approach to use the geometric completeness to estimate the Hausdorff dimension of polar sets of potentials of nonnegative Radon measures for sub-Laplacians in homogeneous Carnot groups. Our approach relies on inequalities that are analogous to the classic integral inequalities about Riesz potentials in Euclidean spaces. Our approach also uses extensions of some of geometric measure theory to homogeneous Carnot groups and the polar coordinates with horizontal radial curves constructed by Balogh and Tyson [ 3 ] for polarizable Carnot groups. As consequences, we develop applications of potentials for sub-Laplacians in CR geometry, quaternionic CR geometry, and octonionic CR geometry.

2.
On potentials for sub-Laplacians in homogeneous Carnot groups

In this section we follow the book
[
7
]
to introduce potentials of Radon measures
for sub-Laplacians in homogeneous Carnot groups. Our main goal in this section is to present our new integral inequalities
about potentials for sub-Laplacians in homogeneous Carnot groups.

2.1.
Basic notations and definitions

Let us start with the definition of homogeneous Carnot groups.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
7
, Definition 1.4.1]
) We say a Lie group on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
,
is a homogeneous Carnot group if the following properties hold

(1)

\mathbb{R}^{N}
can be split as
\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{r}}
, and the dilation

\delta_{\lambda}(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\cdots x^{(r)})=(\lambda x^{(1)},\lambda^{2}x^{(2)},\cdots\lambda^{r}x^{(r)}),\text{ where $x^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}$,}

is an automorphism of the group
\mathbb{G}
for all
\lambda>0
. Hence the group with this dilation
\delta_{\lambda}

is a homogeneous Lie group on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(cf.
[
7
, Definition 1.3.1]
).

(2)

Let
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots,Z_{N_{1}}
be the left invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
such that

Z_{i}(0)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}|_{0}\text{ for $i=1,2,\cdots N_{1}$}.

Then the rank of the Lie algebra generated by
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots Z_{N_{1}}
is
N
.

The group together with the dilation
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
is said to be a homogeneous Carnot group of step
r
and
N_{1}
generators. And
the vector fields
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots Z_{N_{1}}
are called the Jacobian generators.

By
[
7
, Proposition 1.3.21]
, the standard Lebesgue measure
m

on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the Haar measure. And, for a measurable subset
E
,

(2.1)

m(\delta_{\lambda}(E))=\lambda^{Q}m(E),

where
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{r}iN_{i}

is called the homogeneous dimension of the homogeneous Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
(cf.
[
7
, Proposition 2.2.8]
).
For our purpose we assume
Q>2
throughout this paper.
Next important objects for us are sub-Laplacians in homogeneous Carnot groups.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
7
, Definition 1.5.1]
) Let
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots,Z_{N_{1}}
be the Jacobian generators of a homogeneous Carnot group

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
as in Definition
2.1
. The second order differential operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}Z_{i}^{2}

is called the canonical sub-Laplacian (or the sub-Laplacian if no confusions) on
\mathbb{G}
.

Remarkably, the rank condition in Hörmander Theorem (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 1 in Preface]
) in analysis
is exactly the assumption in
[
7
, Theorem 19.1.3]
by
Carathéredory, Chow, and Rashevsky, in the theory of sub-Riemannian geometry, that introduces the
Carnot-Carathéredory distance
d_{\mathbb{G}}
(or the control distance) for homogeneous Carnot groups.
A homogeneous Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
together with the control distance
d_{\mathbb{G}}

is a metric space and called a Carnot-Carathéredory space according to
[
6
, A.6]
. Next important objects for us are fundamental solutions and gauge functions.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
7
, Definition 5.3.1]
)
Let
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
be a homogeneous Carnot group. A function

\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a fundamental solution for
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
if:

•

\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}
is smooth away from the origin;

•

\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(x)\to 0
as

;

•

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}=\text{Delta}_{0},
where
\text{Delta}_{0}
is the Dirac measure supported at the origin

. More explicitly,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi=-\phi(0),\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

It turns out, by
[
7
, Theorem 5.3.2 and Proposition 5.3.10]
, there always exists a unique fundamental solution for
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
,
which is positive with the homogeneity

(2.2)

\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(\delta_{\lambda}(x))=\lambda^{2-Q}\Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(x)

(cf.
[
7
, Proposition 5.3.12 and Proposition 5.3.13]
),
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
.
The
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
-gauge

(2.3)

d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(x,y)=d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(y^{-1}\circ x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \Gamma_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(y^{-1}\circ x)^{\frac{1}{2-Q}}&\text{ when $x\neq y$}\\
0\quad&\text{ when $x=y$}\end{aligned}\right.

is a symmetric homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{G}
(cf.
[
7
, Definition 5.4.1]
). It is good to be reminded that all homogeneous norms
including

and

in
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent according to
[

, Proposition 5.1.4]

2.3.
New integral inequalities about potentials for sub-Laplacians

In recent works
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
on applications of potential theory in conformal geometry, the idea, started in
[
30
,
1
]
,
relies vitally on the feature of thin sets in potential theories in Euclidean spaces, which is, for example,
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
,

[
42
, Theorem 8.1 in Chapter 2]
, and
[

, Theorem 1.2]
.
In this paper, we are taking a completely different approach.
It turns out one of the key analytic ingredients of our new approach in geometric applications of potential theory
is the integral inequalities (

), which are extensions of the classic integral inequalities about Riesz potential in Euclidean spaces.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
2
, Proposition 4.12]
)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. Then, for a constant
C>0
,

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-a}|x-p|^{N-b}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|y-p|^{N-(a+b)}}

provided
a,b\in(0,N)
and
a+b<N
.

(
2.6
) was first proved by Giraud in
[
27
]
.
Our analog of Proposition
2.7
in homogeneous Carnot groups is

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
be a homogeneous Carnot group
and
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{G}}
be the symmetric homogeneous norm corresponding to the Carnot-Carathéredory
distance
d_{\mathbb{G}}
on
\mathbb{G}
. Then, given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{N}
, for a constant
C>0
,

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x^{-1}\circ y|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|p^{-1}\circ x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|p^{-1}\circ y|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-(a+b)}},

provided
a,b\in(0,Q)
and
a+b<Q
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
.

The proof of Proposition
2.8
turns out to straightforward in the same spirit of that of Proposition
2.7
.
Let us first prepare some basic facts about the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{G}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

(
[
7
, Proposition 1.3.21]
)
The standard Lebesgue measure
m
on a homogeneous Carnot group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
is the Haar measure on
\mathbb{G}

and homogeneous with respect to the dilation, that is, for a measurable subset
E
and
\lambda>0
,

m(\delta_{\lambda}(E))=\lambda^{Q}m(E)
and, in particular,

(2.8)

m(B_{\mathbb{G}}(x,r))=cr^{Q}

for a ball
B_{\mathbb{G}}(x,r)
with respect to the control distance, where
c
is a constant for the group

\mathbb{G}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Carnot group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
. Then

\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,r)}\frac{1}{|p^{-1}\circ x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{s}}dx\leq cr^{Q-s}

for
0<s<Q
, where
c
is a constant for the group
\mathbb{G}
. Similarly,

\int_{B^{c}_{\mathbb{G}}(p,r)}\frac{1}{|p^{-1}\circ x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{s}}dx\leq cr^{Q-s}

for
s>Q
, where
c
is a constant for the group
\mathbb{G}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
p=0
. We write

\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,r)}|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{-s}dx=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{r}{2^{k-1}})\setminus B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{r}{2^{k}})}|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{-s}dx\leq c(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{s-Q})^{k})r^{Q-s}\leq cr^{Q-s}.

The other one can be proved similarly.
∎

We are now ready to carry out the proof of Proposition
2.8
with the help
from Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.10
.

Proof of Proposition
2.8

Without loss of generality, we may assume
y=0
.
We first decompose the integral into three parts

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx=I+II+III,

where

I=\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx,\ III=\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx

and

II=\int_{\Omega\cap(B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\setminus B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}))}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx.

For

|x|_{\mathbb{G}}\geq|p|_{\mathbb{G}}-|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}>\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}.

Hence, for

and
b>0
,

I\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}}\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a-b}},

where we use Lemma
2.10
. For
II
, we further decompose it

II=II_{1}+II_{2},

where

II_{1}=\int_{\Omega\cap(B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\setminus B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}))\cap B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx

and

II_{2}=\int_{\Omega\cap(B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\setminus(B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\cup B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})))}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx.

For
II_{1}
, we observe

|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}\geq|p|_{\mathbb{G}}-|x|_{\mathbb{G}}>\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}

for
x\in B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})
. Hence, for
b\leq Q
and
a>0
,

II_{1}\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|p|^{Q-a-b}},

where we use Lemma
2.10
again. On the other hand, for
a,b\leq Q
,

II_{2}\leq\int_{B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\setminus(B_{\mathbb{G}}(p,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}})\cup B_{\mathbb{G}}(0,\frac{1}{2}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}))}\frac{1}{|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a}|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-b}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2Q-a-b}}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q}=\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a-b}}

due to (
2.8
). Finally, for
III
, we again observe

|x|_{\mathbb{G}}\geq|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}-|p|_{\mathbb{G}}>2|p|_{\mathbb{G}}.

Hence, for
a+b<Q
,

III\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a-b-\epsilon}}\int_{B^{c}_{\mathbb{G}}(p,3|p|_{\mathbb{G}})}\frac{1}{|x-p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q+\epsilon}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|p|_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q-a-b}}

for
\epsilon
is small and positive, where we use the second inequality in Lemma
2.10
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.8
, in the light of the
equivalence between symmetric homogeneous norms
d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}
and
d_{\mathbb{G}}
(cf.
[
7
, Proposition. 5.1.4]
), we have

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
be a homogeneous Carnot group.
Then, given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{N}
, for a constant
C>0
,

(2.9)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(x^{-1}\circ y)^{Q-a}d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(p^{-1}\circ x)^{Q-b}}dx\leq\frac{C}{d_{\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}(p^{-1}\circ y)^{Q-(a+b)}}

provided

and

, where

is the homogeneous dimension of

.

On the size of polar sets of potentials for sub-Laplacians

In this section we want to use (

Then we extend our arguments and implement our new idea to derive (

3.1.
On Newtonian potentials in Euclidean spaces and the new idea

Our new idea uses the integral inequalities (
2.6
)
about Riesz potentials in Euclidean spaces as well as the following facts from geometric measure theory in Euclidean spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 5.4]
) Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=\infty
. Then

(a)

Let
c
be a positive number. Then there is a compact subset
F
of
E
such that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)=c
.

(b)

There is a compact subset
F
of
E
such that
\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)>0
and

(3.1)

\mathcal{H}^{s}(F\cap B(x,r))\leq br^{s}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},r\leq 1
and some constant
b
.

Next is a simple and important fact for our argument. Let

(3.2)

\phi(y)=\int_{F}\frac{1}{|y-p|^{t}}d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p).

Then
\phi(y)
can be verified to be continuous with the help of Lemma
3.1
, provided that
0\leq t<s
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact subset which satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{s}(F)\in(0,\infty)
and (
3.1
).
And assume that
0\leq t<s
. Then
\phi(y)
in (
3.2
) is continuous with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

We want to show,

\forall\ y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\forall\ \epsilon>0
,
\exists\ \delta>0
such that

|\phi(y)-\phi(y_{0})|\leq\epsilon,

provided that
|y-y_{0}|<\delta
. We first observe that, for any
\bar{\delta}\leq 1
,

(3.3)

\int_{F\cap B(y,\bar{\delta})}\frac{1}{|y-p|^{t}}d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)\leq C\bar{\delta}^{s-t}

where a single
C
can be chosen for all
y\in\overline{B(y_{0},2)}
and
B(y_{0},r)
is the Euclidean ball with radius
r
and centered at
y_{0}
.
To prove (
3.3
), we estimate it through dyadic annuli,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{F\cap B(y,\bar{\delta})}\frac{1}{|y-p|^{t}}d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{F\cap(B(y,\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2^{k}})\setminus B(y,\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2^{k+1}}))}\frac{1}{|y-p|^{t}}d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\frac{2^{k+1}}{\bar{\delta}})^{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}(F\cap B(y,\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2^{k}}))\leq b\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\frac{2^{k+1}}{\bar{\delta}})^{t}(\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2^{k}})^{s}=\frac{2^{t}b}{1-2^{t-s}}\bar{\delta}^{s-t}

where we use (
3.1
). We decompose the difference as follows:

\phi(y)-\phi(y_{0})=\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}=(\int_{F\cap B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})}+\int_{F\setminus B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})})(\frac{1}{|y-p|^{t}}-\frac{1}{|y_{0}-p|^{t}})d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p).

Let
\delta_{1}\in(0,1]

be such that
\frac{C2^{t}}{1-2^{t-s}}\delta_{1}^{s-t}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
according to (
3.4
). Then we get

|\Phi_{1}|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{ for $\bar{\delta}=\delta_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{1}$ and $|y-y_{0}|<\delta_{2}$}.

Because
B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})\subset B(y,\delta_{1})
. Meanwhile

|\Phi_{2}|\leq\int_{F\setminus B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})}\frac{||y_{0}-p|^{t}-|y-p|^{t}|}{|y_{0}-p|^{t}|y-p|^{t}}d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)\leq\frac{C}{\bar{\delta}^{2t}}\int_{F\setminus B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})}||y-p|^{t}-|y_{0}-p|^{t}|d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)

for
|y-y_{0}|<\delta_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\delta}
. Next, it is easily seen that there exists
\delta_{4}>0
such that

\int_{F}||y-p|^{t}-|y_{0}-p|^{t}|d\mathcal{H}^{s}(p)\leq\frac{\bar{\delta}^{2t}}{2C}\epsilon

if
|y-y_{0}|<\delta_{4}
by the reversed triangle inequality. So

|\Phi_{2}|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{ for $\bar{\delta}=\delta_{2}$ and $|y-y_{0}|<\min\{\delta_{2},\delta_{3},\delta_{4}\}$}.

The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Now we are ready to present the alternative proof of the following result with our new idea.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Radon measure with the support in
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

R^{\mu,D}(x)=\int_{D}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}d\mu(y)

be the Newtonian potential. Suppose that
S
is a compact subset of
D
and that

(3.5)

R^{\mu,D}(x)\to\infty\text{ as $x\to S$ and }\int_{0}^{\delta}(R^{\mu.D}(\gamma(t)))^{\frac{2}{N-2}}dt=\infty

for any
C^{1}
curve
\gamma(t):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\gamma(0)\in S
and some
\delta>0
. Then, for
N\geq 4
,

(3.6)

dim_{\mathcal{H}}(S)\leq\frac{N-2}{2}.

Proof.

Assume otherwise that
dim_{\mathcal{H}}(S)>\frac{N-2}{2}
and consequently there is a compact subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
such that

0<\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(S^{\prime})<\infty

and (
3.1
) holds for
S^{\prime}
, for some
\epsilon>0
by Lemma
3.1
. Let us first assume
N\geq 5
.
To contradict with (
3.5
), we want to have

\int_{0}^{\delta}(R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma))^{\frac{2}{N-2}}dr<\infty,\text{ for some $\delta>0$, some point $p\in S^{\prime}$, and some direction
$\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{1}$.}

For this, by the Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{0}^{\delta}(R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma))^{\frac{2}{N-2}}dr\leq(\int_{0}^{\delta}R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma)r^{\alpha}dr)^{\frac{2}{N-2}}(\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{-\frac{2\alpha}{N-4}}dr)^{1-\frac{2}{N-2}},

one realizes that it suffices to show
\int_{0}^{\delta}R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma)r^{\alpha}dr<\infty

when
\alpha<\frac{N-4}{2}
. Next, for our purpose, we want to claim

(3.7)

\int_{S^{\prime}}d\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(p)\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{1}}d\sigma\int_{0}^{\delta}R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma)r^{\alpha}dr<\infty\text{ for some $\alpha<\frac{N-4}{2}$. }

Hence, to achieve the claim (
3.7
), we apply Fubini’s Theorem and rewrite the integral

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{\prime}}

\displaystyle d\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(p)\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{1}}d\sigma\int_{0}^{\delta}R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma)r^{\alpha}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{\prime}}

\displaystyle d\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(p)\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{1}}d\sigma\int_{0}^{\delta}dr\int_{D}\frac{1}{|p+r\sigma-y|^{N-2}}r^{\alpha}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}

\displaystyle d\mu(y)\int_{S^{\prime}}d\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(p)\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{N-2}|x-p|^{N-1-\alpha}}.

Then we apply Proposition
2.7
and get

\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}|x-p|^{N-1-\alpha}}dx\leq\frac{C}{|y-p|^{N-3-\alpha}}=\frac{C}{|y-p|^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}

for
\alpha=\frac{N-4}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.2
, we know

\int_{S^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|y-p|^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}d\mathcal{H}^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\epsilon}(p)

is continuous with respect to
y
and (
3.7
) holds. Thus this completes the proof for
N\geq 5
. For
N=4
, the above argument works with
\alpha=0
and no need to apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\int_{S^{\prime}}d\mathcal{H}^{1+\epsilon}(p)\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}}d\sigma\int_{0}^{\delta}R^{\mu,D}(p+r\sigma)dr<\infty.

Our new approach does not seem to work for
N=3
for this theorem.
∎

4.1.1.
Yamabe type problems in CR cases

One good place to start CR Yamabe problems is
[
32
]
. On a CR manifold,
one may use a contact form
\theta
to represent the distribution. Then the Levi form and the compatible complex structure
\mathbb{I}=\{I\}

induces a Carnot-Carathéredory metric
g
on the distribution, that is,

g(IX,Y)=d\theta(X,Y)\text{ and }g(IX,IY)=g(X,Y)\text{ for horizontal vectors $X$ and $Y$.}

This so-called pseudo-hermitian structure
(\theta,\mathbb{I})

represents the underlined CR structure. The relation between a CR structure and its pseudo-hermitian representatives is analogous to that between
a conformal structure and its Riemannian representatives. For a pseudo-hermitian structure, there is a unique Tanaka-Webster connection. An alternative
approach based on Fefferman metrics was used in
[
32
]
, which seems to be in the same spirit as the Biquard connection in
[
4
]
. The scalar curvature of such connection is called the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature

R_{\theta}
. In fact, on a CR manifold, the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature transforms under conformal
changes as

(4.1)

(-b_{n}\Delta_{b}+R_{\theta})u=R_{\theta_{u}}u^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}}

where
\Delta_{b}
is the sub-Laplacian associated with the sub-Riemannian structure of
(\theta,g)
,

R_{\theta_{u}}
is the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature of the pseudo-hermitian structure associated with the conformal contact form

\theta_{u}=u^{\frac{4}{Q-2}}\theta
for a positive function
u
, and
Q=2n+2
is the homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

On the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, the pseudo-hermitian structure associated with the standard contact form
\theta_{0}
is flat with respect to the
Tanaka-Webster connection. Therefore we have

(4.2)

-b_{n}\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u=R_{\theta}u^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}}\text{ in $D\setminus S$},

where
\theta=u^{\frac{4}{Q-2}}\theta_{0}
,

u\in C^{\infty}(D\setminus S)
and positive,
D
is some domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
S
is some compact subset in
D
.
Notice that such a conformal change introduces a conformal change of the corresponding pseudo-hermitian metric on the horizontal distribution.

4.1.2.
Yamabe type problems in quaternionic CR cases

In

-c_{n}\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}u=R_{\theta}u^{\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}}\text{ in $D\setminus S$},

where
\theta=u^{\frac{4}{Q-2}}\theta_{0}

and positive,
D
is some domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\text{Im}\,\mathbb{H}
, and

is some compact subset in

Notice that such a conformal change introduces a conformal change of the Carnot-Carathéredory metric on the horizontal distribution.

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u)\phi dx=\int u(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)dx=\int\lim_{s\to\infty}\alpha_{s}(u)(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi)dx

4.3.
On quotients
\Omega(\Gamma)/\Gamma
for convex cocompact subgroups
\Gamma

In this subsection we want to apply Theorem
4.2
to the quotient
\Omega(\Gamma)/\Gamma
for
convex cocampact subgroups of real semisimple groups
\mathfrak{G}
of rank one.

4.3.1.
Hyperbolic spaces and rank one groups

Recall that rank one symmetric spaces of non-compact type, besides real hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, are

•

compex hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{C}}
;

•

quaternionic hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
;

•

the octonionic hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{O}}
.

Their isometry groups
\mathfrak{G}
are the rank one groups
SU(n+1,1)
,
Sp(n+1,1)
, and
\mathbb{F}_{4(-20)}
respectively.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be either the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
or the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
or the octonions
\mathbb{O}
for convenience. Recall that
each hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
has a ball model and the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a sphere.
When considering
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
as the Martin boundary of hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
,

\Gamma
naturally acts on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
too, which is particularly simple and clear for rank one cases.
Then, for a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathfrak{G}
that acts discontinuously on
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
for any
q\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
, we consider the set of limit points of the orbit
\Gamma q

L(\Gamma)=\overline{\Gamma q}\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}.

L(\Gamma)
turns out to be a closed invariant subset that is independent of
q
. Let

\Omega(\Gamma)=\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}\setminus L(\Gamma).

Then, for a discontinuous discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathfrak{G}
,

\Omega(\Gamma)
turns out to be the maximum open invariant subset in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
\Gamma
is said to
be convex cocompact if the quotient
\Omega(\Gamma)/\Gamma
is a compact manifold. Clearly
\Omega(\Gamma)/\Gamma
inherits the
standard CR structure on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}
, which is usually referred to as being spherical or locally conformally flat.

4.3.2.
Cayley transforms

To apply Theorem
4.2
in these cases, we need the Cayley transforms, which are the general stereographic projections
(or the inverses) from the round spheres with a point removed to Euclidean spaces.
The full Cayley transform between two models of rank one groups
\mathfrak{G}
:
Siegel domain model and unit ball model, is well defined, for example, in
[
22
, Section 3]
.
The contact structure changes conformally and therefore the CR structure remains the same under Cayley transform

\mathcal{C}:\mathsf{N}=\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\text{Im}\,\mathbb{F}\to\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}

We refer readers to
[
26
, Appendix A]
,
[
16
, (2.2)]
, and
[
17
, (2.1)]
for the explicit Cayley transform
\mathcal{C}
in each case.
They are all invertible from the contact sphere with the south pole
S=(0,-1)
removed. Assume, without loss of generality,
the south pole is in
\Omega(\Gamma)
. Therefore we have

\mathcal{C}^{-1}(\Omega(\Gamma)\setminus\{(0,-1)\})\subset\mathsf{N}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}^{-1}(L(\Gamma))\subset\mathsf{N},

where
\mathcal{C}^{-1}(L(\Gamma))
is compact in
\mathsf{N}
.

4.3.3.
Conculsion

In the light of Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
, we have

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\mathsf{N}
be the H-type group that is the nilpotent part of the Iwasawa

\mathfrak{G}

Proof.

For the proof, we only need to point out that the conformal sub-Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{C}^{-1}(\Omega(\Gamma)\setminus\{(0,-1)\})

induced from that on

\Omega(\Gamma)/\Gamma

is geodesically complete when approaching to
\mathcal{C}^{-1}(L(\Gamma))

\Gamma

We also want to remark that the Hausdorff dimensions

\mathsf{N}

∎

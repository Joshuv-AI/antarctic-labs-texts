One-Point Restricted Conformal Blocks and the Fusion Tensor Product
Jianqi Liu

Abstract
. We investigate a one-point restriction of conformal blocks on ( ℙ 1 , ∞ , 1 , 0 ) ({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty,1,0) associated with modules over a vertex operator algebra. By restricting the module attached to the point ∞ \infty to its bottom degree, we obtain a new formula for computing fusion rules in terms of a left A ⁡ ( V ) A(V) -module M 1 ⊙ M 2 M^{1}\odot M^{2} over the Zhu algebra A ⁡ ( V ) A(V) . As a consequence, for strongly rational VOAs, the construction of M 1 ⊙ M 2 M^{1}\odot M^{2} induces the fusion tensor product on the module category 𝖬𝗈𝖽 ⁡ ( A ⁡ ( V ) ) \mathsf{Mod}(A(V)) .

1.
Introduction

The space of conformal blocks on the three-pointed genus-zero smooth curve defined by modules over a vertex operator algebra (VOA)
V
is isomorphic to the vector space of intertwining operators among these modules, whose dimension is the fusion rule
[
NT05
,
FBZ04
]
. Using the restriction technique of conformal blocks
[
GLZ24
]
, we obtain a new hom-space description of the space of intertwining operators
I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
using a new left module
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
over the degree-zero Borcherds’ Lie algebra
L(V)_{0}

[
B86
]
or the Zhu algebra
A(V)

[
Z96
]
. If
V
is a strongly rational VOA, and
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
are generalized Verma modules associated to the
A(V)
-modules
S^{1}
and
S^{2}
, respectively, we prove that
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
is exactly the fusion tensor product
S^{1}\boxtimes S^{2}
in the module category of Zhu algebra
\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
.

Motivated by finding a mathematical rigorous definition of the WZNW-conformal field theory, Tsuchiya, Ueno, and Yamada introduced the notions of coinvariants (covacua) and conformal blocks (vacua) on stable algebraic curves defined by highest-weight representations of affine Kac-Moody algebras
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of non-generic level
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

[
TUY89
]
. From the VOA point of view, the representation theory of affine Lie algebras is in parallel with the representation theory of affine VOAs of the same level
[
FZ92
]
. With this key observation,
the notions of coinvariants and conformal blocks in WZNW-model were generalized to the general VOA case by Zhu
[
Z94
]
, Frenkel-Ben-Zvi
[
FBZ04
]
, and Nagatomo-Tsuchiya
[
NT05
]
for smooth curves, and by Damiolini-Gibney-Tarasca
[
DGT24
]
for general stable curves.
The space of three-pointed conformal blocks associated to the datum

\Sigma((M^{3})^{\prime},M^{1},M^{2})=({\mathbb{P}}^{1},(\infty,1,0),(1/z,z-1,z),((M^{3})^{\prime},M^{1},M^{2}))

is canonically isomorphic to the space of intertwining operators
I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}

[
FHL93
]
among these
V
-modules. These spaces are the building blocks of the space of conformal blocks on higher-genus algebraic curves via the factorization theorem
[
DGT24
]
. The dimension of these spaces, namely the fusion rules, are not only one of the fundamental concept in the conformal field theory (CFT), but also carry important information about the rank of the vector bundle on the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, parametrizing
n
-pointed genus-
g
stable curves, defined by the VOA-conformal blocks
[
DGT24
,
DG23
]
.

The structure of the vector space
I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
has been studied extensively in the VOA theory. Frenkel-Zhu’s fusion rules theorem states that this vector space is isomorphic to the hom-space
{\rm Hom}_{A(V)}(A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2}),\Omega(M^{3}))

[
FZ92
]
, where
A(M^{1})
is a bimodule over Zhu algebra
A(V)
. Li revised this theorem by adding the assumptions that
M^{2}
and
(M^{3})^{\prime}
are generalized Verma modules
[
DLM98
]
associated to their bottom degrees
\Omega(M^{2})
and
\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime})
, respectively
[
Li99
]
. The author proved a variant of this theorem using the technique of three-pointed correlation functions defined by intertwining operators
[
Liu23
]
, which was further generalized to the
g
-twisted case by Gao, the author, and Zhu by developing a theory of twisted correlation functions
[
GLZ25
]
.
Furthermore, we introduced a notion of (twisted) restricted conformal blocks in
[
GLZ24
]
. With this new notion, we noticed that the hom space
{\rm Hom}_{A(V)}(A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2}),\Omega(M^{3}))
can be identified with the space of two-point restricted conformal blocks defined on the projective line
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},(\infty,1,0),(1/z,z-1,z),(\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime}),M^{1},\Omega(M^{2})))
, wherein the
V
-modules
(M^{3})^{\prime}
and
M^{2}
attached to
\infty
and

are restricted to their bottom degrees
\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime})
and
\Omega(M^{2})
, respectively, and the fusion rules theorem can be interpreted as a theorem about extending a restricted conformal block to a regular conformal block. The fusion rules theorem had generalizations from various aspects in the theory of VOAs. For instance, Dong and Ren generalized it to the higher-level and modular representation case in
[
DR13
,
DR14
]
using a bimodule over the higher-level Zhu algebra

[

]

[

]

in
[
H22
,
H24
]

to describe the space of intertwining operators.

In this paper, instead of the restricting the modules attached at
(\infty,0)
to bottom degrees, we consider the case when only the module
(M^{3})^{\prime}
attached at
\infty
is restricted to its bottom-degree
\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime})

\Sigma(\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime}),M^{1},M^{2})=({\mathbb{P}}^{1},(\infty,1,0),(1/z,z-1,z),(\Omega((M^{3})^{\prime}),M^{1},M^{2})).

The space of conformal blocks associated to this one-point restricted datum turns out to be more interesting than the two-point restricted datum. It has an intimate relation with the tensor structure on
\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
for strongly rational VOAs

To state our results precisely, and describe how they are proved, we set a small amount of notation.
The chiral (current) Lie algebra

has spanning elements represented by

The chiral Lie algebra

,

, and

We define the

-restricted conformal block

. The space of such conformal blocks is denoted by

(see Definition

). The Lie algebra

has an ideal

, and such that the quotient algebra
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{0,1\}}(V)_{\leq 0}/\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{0,1\}}(V)_{<0}
is isomorphic to the degree zero Borcherds Lie algebra

(see Lemma

M^{1}\odot M^{2}:=M^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}M^{2}/\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{0,1\}}(V)_{<0}.(M^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}M^{2}),

. This quotient space also occurred in a construction of fusion tensor product of modules over

-cofinite VOAs by Tsuchiya and Wood (see
[

Using a set of spanning elements of

is spanned by the equivalent classes

of the elements

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{{\rm wt}a-1}{j}a(j-1)v_{1}\odot v_{2}

\displaystyle=v_{1}\odot\sum_{j\geq 0}a({\rm wt}a-1+j)v_{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{{\rm wt}a-k}{j}a(j)v_{1}\odot v_{2}

\displaystyle=-v_{1}\odot a({\rm wt}a-k)v_{2},\quad k\geq 2,

where

,

, and

. Although there is no

-bimodule appear in

-module

Theorem A
.

Let

and

be

-modules. If either

We prove Theorem

The

Theorem B
.

Let

(1.1)

I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}\cong{\rm Hom}_{A(V)}(M^{1}\odot M^{2},\Omega(M^{3})).

In particular, if

In comparison with the Frenkel–Zhu fusion rules theorem, Theorem

We use Li’s example of modules over the universal Virasoro VOA

Instead of using the language of correlation functions, we use the language of conformal blocks to prove Theorem

,

]

[

,

]

One interesting consequence of Theorem

gives the fusion tensor product of

-modules for strongly rational VOAs. Indeed, Dong–Li–Mason constructed a pair of adjoint functors

then transfers to a modular tensor category structure on

under the equivalence functor

Theorem C
.

Let

be a strongly rational VOA, and let

be irreducible

\Omega(M^{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}M^{2})\cong M^{1}\odot M^{2}

(1.2)

2.1.1.
Vertex operator algebras and modules

A VOA

is said to be of

if

, where
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}
and

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a VOA. An
admissible
V
-module
is a
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded vector space

M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n),

equipped with a linear map

Y_{M}:V\rightarrow{\rm End}(M)[\![z,z^{-1}]\!],\qquad Y_{M}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(n)z^{-n-1},

called the
module vertex operator
, satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

(truncation property) For any
a\in V
and
u\in M
,
Y_{M}(a,z)u\in M((z))
.

(2)

(vacuum property)
Y_{M}(\mathbf{1},z)=\mathrm{Id}_{M}
.

(3)

(Jacobi identity for
Y_{M}
) For any
a,b\in V
and
u\in M
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle Y_{M}(a,z_{1})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})u-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{-z_{2}+z_{1}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(b,z_{2})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})u

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)Y_{M}(Y(a,z_{0})b,z_{2})u.

(4)

(
L(-1)
-derivative property)
Y_{M}(L(-1)a,z)=\frac{d}{dz}Y_{M}(a,z)
for any
a\in V
.

(5)

(grading property) For any
a\in V
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

a(m)M(n)\subseteq M(n+{\rm wt}a-m-1),

i.e.,
{\rm wt}(a(m))={\rm wt}a-m-1
.

We write
\deg v=n
if

, and call it the
degree
of
v
. Denote the category of admissible
V
-modules by
\mathsf{Adm}(V)
.

An admissible
V
-module

is called

if each degree-

is a finite-dimensional eigenspace of

with eigenvalue

, where

is called the
conformal weight
of
M
. In particular, if we write
L(0)v=({\rm wt}v)\cdot v
for

, then
{\rm wt}v=\deg v+h
.

We abbreviate an

-module
simply as an

V
-module
. Submodules, quotient modules, and irreducible modules are defined in the usual categorical sense. The VOA

is called

if the category of admissible

-modules is semisimple
[

]
.

Remark 2.2
.

We recall the following well-known facts about the Jacobi identity:

(1)

Using Cauchy’s integral (or residue) theorem, one can rewrite the formal-variable Jacobi identity (

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Res}_{z=0}Y_{M}(a,z)Y_{M}(b,w)\,\iota_{z,w}(F(z,w))-{\rm Res}_{z=0}Y_{M}(b,w)Y_{M}(a,z)\,\iota_{w,z}(F(z,w))

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{z-w=0}Y_{M}(Y(a,z-w)b,w)\,\iota_{w,z-w}(F(z,w)),

where

with

, and
\iota_{z,w}
,

, and

are the expansion operations of a rational function in complex variables

, and

(2)

The Jacobi identity (

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{l}{i}a(m+l-i)b(n+i)-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+i}\binom{l}{i}b(n+l-i)a(m+i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(a(l+i)b)(m+n-i),

where
a,b\in V
and
m,n,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. This identity is also called the
Borcherds identity

[
B86
]
.

2.1.2.
Intertwining operators and fusion rules

Intertwining operators among
V
-modules are generalizations of intertwining operators among modules over Lie algebras
[
FHL93
,
FZ92
,
Li98
]
:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M^{1},M^{2},M^{3}
be ordinary
V
-modules of conformal weights
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Let
h:=h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3}
. An
intertwining operator of type
{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
is a linear map

I(\cdot,z):M^{1}\rightarrow{\rm Hom}(M^{2},M^{3})[\![z,z^{-1}]\!]z^{-h},\qquad I(v_{1},z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{1}(n)z^{-n-1-h},

satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

(truncation property) For any
v_{1}\in M^{1}
and
v_{2}\in M^{2}
,
v_{1}(n)v_{2}=0
for
n\gg 0
.

(2)

(
L(-1)
-derivative property) For any
v_{1}\in M^{1}
,

I(L(-1)v_{1},z)=\frac{d}{dz}I(v_{1},z)
.

(3)

(Jacobi identity) For any
v_{1}\in M^{1}
,
v_{2}\in M^{2}
, and
a\in V
, one has

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle Y_{M^{3}}(a,z_{1})I(v_{1},z_{2})v_{2}-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{-z_{2}+z_{1}}{z_{0}}\right)I(v_{1},z_{2})Y_{M^{2}}(a,z_{1})v_{2}

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)I(Y_{M^{1}}(a,z_{0})v_{1},z_{2})v_{2}.

The vector space of intertwining operators of type
{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
is denoted by
I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
. Its dimension, denoted by
N{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}
, is called the
fusion rule among
M^{1},M^{2}
, and
M^{3}
.

Using the Jacobi identity and the
L(0)
-eigenspace property for
M^{i}(n)
, one can easily show that

v_{1}(n)M^{2}(m)\subseteq M^{3}(\deg v_{1}-n-1+m),

for any
v_{1}\in M^{1}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

[
FZ92
]
.

2.1.3.
Contragredient modules

Definition 2.4
.

[
FHL93
]

Let
M
be an ordinary
V
-module. Its
contragredient module
is the graded dual space

M^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)^{\ast},

with
Y_{M^{\prime}}:V\rightarrow{\rm End}(M^{\prime})[\![z,z^{-1}]\!]
given by

(2.4)

\langle Y_{M^{\prime}}(a,z)v^{\prime},v\rangle:=\langle v^{\prime},Y_{M}(e^{zL(1)}(-z^{-2})^{L(0)}a,z^{-1})v\rangle=\langle v^{\prime},Y^{\prime}_{M}(a,z)v\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:M^{\prime}\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the natural pairing between graded vector spaces.

It was proved in
[
FHL93
, Section 5]
that
Y_{M^{\prime}}
defined by (
2.4
) satisfies the Jacobi identity (
2.1
), and that
Y^{\prime\prime}_{M}(a,z)=Y_{M}(a,z)
.

Moreover, if we write
Y^{\prime}_{M}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a^{\prime}(n)z^{-n-1}
, then by taking the formal residue,

(2.5)

a^{\prime}(n)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{{\rm wt}a}}{j!}(L(1)^{j}a)(2{\rm wt}a-n-j-2).

It follows that
a^{\prime}(n)M(m)\subseteq M(-{\rm wt}a+n+1+m)
, i.e.,
{\rm wt}(a^{\prime}(n))=-{\rm wt}a+n+1
.

2.1.4.
Borcherds’ Lie algebra and Zhu’s algebra associated to a VOA

Let
V
be a VOA. Then
\hat{V}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
is the tensor product vertex algebra
[
FHL93
]
, with vacuum element
\mathbf{1}\otimes 1
and differential

\nabla=L(-1)\otimes\mathrm{Id}+\mathrm{Id}\otimes\frac{d}{dt};

see
[
B86
]
for more details.

Definition 2.5
.

[
B86
]

Let
V
be a VOA. The Borcherds Lie algebra
L(V)
is defined by

L(V)=\hat{V}/\nabla(\hat{V})=\mathrm{span}\{a_{[n]}=a\otimes t^{n}+\nabla(\hat{V}):a\in V,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

with
(L(-1)a)_{[n]}=-na_{[n-1]}
, and Lie bracket

(2.6)

[a_{[m]},b_{[n]}]=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{m}{j}(a(j)b)_{[m+n-j]},\quad a,b\in V,\ m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Definition 2.6
.

[
Z96
]

Let
V
be a VOA. The
Zhu algebra
A(V)
is defined by
A(V)=V/O(V)
, where

(2.7)

O(V)=\mathrm{span}\left\{a\circ b={\rm Res}_{z=1}Y(a,z-1)b\,\iota_{1,z-1}\left(\frac{z^{{\rm wt}a}}{(z-1)^{2}}\right):a,b\in V\right\}.

The space
A(V)=\mathrm{span}\{[a]=a+O(V):a\in V\}
is an associative algebra with respect to

(2.8)

[a]\ast[b]={\rm Res}_{z=1}[Y(a,z-1)b]\iota_{1,z-1}\left(\frac{z^{{\rm wt}a}}{z-1}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{{\rm wt}a}{j}[a(j-1)b],

for
a,b\in V
.
The category of (left)
A(V)
-modules is denoted by
\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
.

We remark the following facts about the Zhu algebra and the Borcherds Lie algebra; see
[
B86
,
Z96
,
DLM98
]
for more details.

(1)

There is an anti-involution
\theta:A(V)\rightarrow A(V)
defined by

\theta([a])=[e^{L(1)}(-1)^{L(0)}a]
, with

\theta([a]\ast[b])=\theta([b])\ast\theta([a])
.

(2)

There is a similar anti-involution
\theta:L(V)\rightarrow L(V)
defined by

\theta(a_{[n]})=a^{\prime}_{[n]}
via (
2.5
), with

\theta([a_{[m]},b_{[n]}])=[\theta(b_{[n]}),\theta(a_{[m]})]
.

(3)

Let
M=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M(n)
be an admissible
V
-module (or, more generally, a weak
V
-module). Then the subspace of “highest-weight vectors”

\Omega(M)=\{v\in M:a(n)v=0,\ \deg(a(n))={\rm wt}a-n-1<0\}

is a left
A(V)
-module via
A(V)\rightarrow{\rm End}(\Omega(M))
,
[a]\mapsto o(a):=a({\rm wt}a-1)
.

(4)

If
M
is an irreducible admissible
V
-module, then
\Omega(M)
is an irreducible
A(V)
-module, which is also the bottom degree
M(0)
of
M
, i.e.,
\Omega(M)=M(0)
. Throughout this paper, we refer to
\Omega(M)
as the
bottom degree
of the
V
-module
M
.

(5)

Let
M
be an ordinary
V
-module, and let
M^{\prime}
be its contragredient module.
The bottom degree
M^{\prime}(0)=M(0)^{\ast}
is naturally a right
A(V)
-module. It is a left
A(V)
-module via the anti-involution.
A(V)\times M(0)^{\ast}\rightarrow M(0)^{\ast},([a],v^{\prime})\mapsto[a].v^{\prime}
, where

(2.9)

\langle[a].v^{\prime}\,|\,\mathopen{}v\rangle:=\langle v^{\prime}.[\theta(a)]\,|\,\mathopen{}v\rangle=\langle v^{\prime}\,|\,\mathopen{}[\theta(a)].v\rangle=\langle v^{\prime}\,|\,\mathopen{}o(\theta(a))v\rangle,

where
[a]\in A(V)
,
v^{\prime}\in M(0)^{\ast}
, and
v\in M(0)
. If
M
is an irreducible ordinary module, then
M^{\prime}
is also irreducible and
\Omega(M^{\prime})=M(0)^{\ast}
.

(6)

There is an epimorphism of Lie algebras

(2.10)

L(V)_{0}\twoheadrightarrow A(V)_{\mathrm{Lie}},\qquad a_{[{\rm wt}a-1]}\mapsto[a],\quad a\in V.

2.1.5.
Generalized Verma module associated to an
A(V)
-module

Let
U
be an irreducible left
A(V)
-module. Then
U
is a module over the Lie algebra
L(V)_{0}
via (
2.10
), which can be lifted to a module over
L(V)_{0}\oplus L(V)_{+}
by letting
(L(V)_{+})\cdot U=0
.

Consider the induced module

M(U):=U(L(V))\otimes_{U(L(V)_{0}\oplus L(V)_{+})}U=U(L(V)_{-})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U.

Let
J
be the
U(L(V))
-submodule of
M(U)
generated by the coefficients of the Borcherds identity (
2.3
). It was proved in
[
DLM98
]
that

\bar{M}(U):=M(U)/J

is an admissible
V
-module generated by
U
, with bottom degree
\bar{M}(U)(0)=U
. The module vertex operator is given by

Y_{\bar{M}(U)}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(n)z^{-n-1}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{[n]}z^{-n-1},

for any
a\in V
. Moreover, any admissible
V
-module
W
generated by
W(0)=U
is a quotient module of
\bar{M}(U)
.

If we view
\bar{M}(-)
and
\Omega(-)
as functors, then they form an adjoint pair of functors
[
DLM98
, Theorem 6.1]
:

(\bar{M}(-)\dashv\Omega(-)):\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(V).

Moreover, we have the following fact about rationality:

Lemma 2.7
.

[
DLM98
, Theorem 8.1]
.
A VOA
V
is rational if and only if
A(V)
is semisimple and
(\bar{M}(-)\dashv\Omega(-)):\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(V)
is an adjoint equivalence.

Let
M
be an irreducible
V
-module. Then
\Omega(M^{\prime})=M(0)^{\ast}=\Omega(M)^{\ast}
is an irreducible left
A(V)
-module via (
2.9
).
The anti-involution
\theta:L(V)\rightarrow L(V)
of the Borcherds Lie algebra induces an isomorphism of associative algebras

\theta:U(L(V))\rightarrow U(L(V))^{\mathrm{op}}.

Note that

\theta(b_{r}(n_{r})\cdots b_{1}(n_{1}))=b^{\prime}_{1}(n_{1})\cdots b^{\prime}_{r}(n_{r}),

where
b^{\prime}(n)
is given by (
2.5
), and

\bar{M}(\Omega(M^{\prime}))=U(L(V))\cdot\Omega(M^{\prime})=\theta(U(L(V)))\cdot\Omega(M^{\prime}).

It follows that

(2.11)

\bar{M}(\Omega(M^{\prime}))=\mathrm{span}\{b^{\prime}_{1}(n_{1})\cdots b^{\prime}_{r}(n_{r})v^{\prime}:r\geq 0,\ b_{i}\in V,\ n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ v^{\prime}\in\Omega(M^{\prime})\}.

Moreover, by carefully choosing the coefficients in the Borcherds identity (
2.3
) for the vertex operator
Y_{\bar{M}(U)}
, one can also show that

\bar{M}(\Omega(M^{\prime}))=\mathrm{span}\{b^{\prime}(n)v^{\prime}:b\in V,\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ v^{\prime}\in\Omega(M^{\prime})\}

[
LL04
]
. These facts will be used in Section
5
.

2.2.
The chiral Lie algebra ancillary to
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\infty,1,0)

The chiral Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{C\backslash P_{\bullet}}(V)
ancillary to a VOA
V
and a stable
n
-pointed curve
(C,P_{\bullet})
was defined as

H^{0}(C\backslash P_{\bullet},\mathscr{V}_{C}\otimes\Omega_{C}/\mathrm{Im}\,\nabla)
; see
[
FBZ04
,
BD04
,
DGT24
]
.
In this paper, we are only interested in the smooth curve
C={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with three marked points
P_{\bullet}=(\infty,1,0)
.
In this case, the chiral Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)
has the following simple description:

Definition 2.8
.

[
NT05
,
DGT24
]

The chiral (current) Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)
ancillary to the smooth curve
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}

is defined by

(2.12)

\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V):=\left(V\otimes\mathbb{C}[z^{\pm 1},(z-1)^{\pm 1}]\right)/\mathrm{Im}\,\nabla,

where
\nabla=L(-1)\otimes\mathrm{Id}+\mathrm{Id}\otimes\frac{d}{dz}
.
Then, as a vector space,

(2.13)

\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)=\mathrm{span}\left\{a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}:a\in V,\ m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

with

(L(-1)a)\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}=-a\otimes\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\right),

where we use the same symbol for the equivalence class of

a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\in V\otimes\mathbb{C}[z^{\pm 1},(z-1)^{\pm 1}]
in the quotient space.
The Lie bracket on
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)
is given by

(2.14)

\left[a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}},\,b\otimes\frac{z^{s}}{(z-1)^{t}}\right]=\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n}{i}\binom{-m}{j}\,a_{i+j}b\otimes\frac{z^{n+s-i}}{(z-1)^{m+t+j}},

where
a,b\in V
and
m,n,s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Remark 2.9
.

The original definition of the three-point chiral (current) Lie algebra on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by Nagatomo–Tsuchiya
[
NT05
]
was

\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)=\frac{\bigoplus_{\Delta=0}^{\infty}V_{\Delta}\otimes H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\Omega^{1-\Delta}(\ast(\infty,1,0)))}{\nabla\bigoplus_{\Delta=0}^{\infty}V_{\Delta}\otimes H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\Omega^{-\Delta}(\ast(\infty,1,0)))}.

Thus, elements in
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)
are represented by
a\otimes f(z)\,(dz)^{1-\Delta}
, where
f(z)
is a global meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with poles only at
\infty
,
1
, and
0
.
In this paper, since we focus only on the algebraic side of the theory, we omit the formal differential symbol
(dz)^{1-\Delta}
in our definition (
2.12
) and (
2.13
).

The following proposition is a purely algebraic version of the chiral Lie algebra action on the space of coinvariants
[
FBZ04
,
NT05
,
DGT24
]
.
The proof is an immediate consequence of the Jacobi identity of VOAs together with (
2.14

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
M^{1},M^{2}
, and
M^{3}
be
V
-modules, and let

a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)

Then:

(1)

(M^{3})^{\prime}
is a module over the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)
via

\rho_{\infty}:\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}((M^{3})^{\prime})
,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}\left(a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\right)(v^{\prime}_{3})

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{z=\infty}Y_{(M^{3})^{\prime}}(\vartheta(a),z^{-1})v^{\prime}_{3}\,\iota_{z,1}\left(\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-m}{j}(-1)^{j}a^{\prime}(n-m-j)v^{\prime}_{3},\quad v^{\prime}_{3}\in(M^{3})^{\prime},

where

is given by (

(2)

\rho_{1}:\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\backslash\{\infty,1,0\}}(V)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(M^{1})

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\left(a\otimes\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\right)(v_{1})

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{z=1}Y_{M^{1}}(a,z-1)v_{1}\,\iota_{1,z-1}\left(\frac{z^{n}}{(z-1)^{m}}\right)

5.2.

We now prove our main theorem concerning the extension of

Theorem 5.5
.

The following corollary follows immediately from the corresponding properties of fusion rules in
[
FHL93
, Section 5]
.

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
V
be a strongly rational VOA. Denote the contragredient left
A(V)
-module
S^{\ast}
by
S^{\vee}
. Then we have the following isomorphisms of left
A(V)
-modules:

(1)

S\boxtimes T\cong T\boxtimes S
.

(2)

Denote
\Omega(V)
by
\mathbf{1}
. Then
\mathbf{1}\boxtimes S\cong S\cong S\boxtimes\mathbf{1}
, that is,
\mathbf{1}
is the tensor identity.

(3)

\mathbf{1}
is an
A(V)
-submodule of
S\boxtimes S^{\vee}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.8
and (
6.7
), we have

S\boxtimes T\cong\bigoplus_{W\in\mathscr{W}}N{\binom{W}{\bar{M}(S)\;\bar{M}(T)}}\cdot\Omega(W).

Now, (1) follows from the fact that

N{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}=N{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{2}\;M^{1}}}
;
(2) follows from the fact that

N{\binom{W}{V\;M}}=\delta_{M,W}=N{\binom{W}{M\;V}}
;
and (3) follows from the fact that
V
is self-dual and

N{\binom{V}{M\;M^{\prime}}}=N{\binom{M^{\prime\prime}}{M\;V^{\prime}}}=N{\binom{M}{V\;M}}=1
,
where
M^{1},M^{2},M^{3},M,W\in\mathscr{W}
.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

The explicit form of the isomorphism

B:S\boxtimes T=\bar{M}(S)\odot\bar{M}(T)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\bar{M}(T)\odot\bar{M}(S)=T\boxtimes S

is the braiding isomorphism in the category
\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
. This can be derived from the isomorphism

I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}I{\binom{M^{3}}{M^{2}\;M^{1}}}

using the skew-symmetry formula
[
FHL93
, eq. (5.4.33)]
, together with Proposition
2.12
. We will provide full details in a subsequent work.

7.
Examples of the tensor products of
A(V)
-modules

We determine the contracted tensor product

for irreducible modules in several examples, including the universal affine VOA

, the level-one type-

affine VOA

7.1.
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
for the universal affine and universal Virasoro VOAs

We first examine the contracted tensor product
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
for some irrational VOAs. It turns out that the left
A(V)
-module

may or may not be isomorphic to
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
, unlike the rational case in Corollary
6.3
.

7.1.1.
Examples in the Heisenberg and universal affine VOAs

Let
V=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
be the Heisenberg or universal affine VOA of level
k\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is an abelian or simple Lie algebra, respectively. Recall that
A(V)\cong U(\mathfrak{g})

[
FZ92
]
. Let
U
be an irreducible
A(V)
-module. The
generalized Verma module associated to
U

[
DLM98
,
LL04
]
is given by the induced module

(7.1)

V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},U)=U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\geq 0})}U\cong U(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U,

where
K.u=ku
for some fixed
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
a(0).u=a.u
, and
a(n).u=0
for any
a\in\mathfrak{g}
,
n>0
, and
u\in U
. It was proved in
[
FZ92
,
LL04
]
that
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},U)
is a
V
-module, and it is the generalized Verma module
\bar{M}(U)
of the VOA
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
; see Section
2.1.5
.

Let
U=L(\lambda)
, which is the finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module associated to a dominant weight
\lambda\in P_{+}
if
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, and is
\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}
if
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian. Denote the generalized Verma module
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},L(\lambda))
by
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
. Note that
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
is an irreducible
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
-module if
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian. If
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, it admits a unique maximal submodule
W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
and an irreducible quotient module
L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)/W^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
. The bottom degree
\Omega(L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda))
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
L(\lambda)
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
V=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
be the Heisenberg or universal affine VOA of level
k\in\mathbb{C}
. Assume
M^{1}=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
and
M^{2}=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mu)=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
are irreducible generalized Verma modules such that
\Omega(M^{1})=L(\lambda)
and
\Omega(M^{2})=L(\mu)
are finite-dimensional. Then we have

(7.2)

M^{1}\odot M^{2}\cong A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong L(\lambda)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}L(\mu)

as left
A(V)\cong U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.

Proof.

By
[
FZ92
, Theorem 3.2.1]
and our assumptions on
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
,

A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong(\Omega(M^{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}A(V))\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong\Omega(M^{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega(M^{2}).

First, we consider the case when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}
is an abelian Lie algebra and
V^{k}(\mathfrak{h})=M(k)
is the Heisenberg VOA. In this case, any generalized Verma module
V^{k}(\mathfrak{h},\lambda)
is irreducible.

Since
L(\lambda)=\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}
, we have
A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C}e^{\mu}
, and it surjects onto
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
by (
4.28
). Moreover,
M^{1}\odot M^{2}\neq 0
, since
{\rm Hom}_{L(V)_{0}}(M^{1}\odot M^{2},\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda+\mu})\neq 0
in view of (
6.1
). Hence

A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong\mathbb{C}e^{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{C}e^{\mu}\cong M^{1}\odot M^{2}.

Next, we consider the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra and
V=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is the universal affine VOA. Since
A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})
is finite-dimensional and surjects onto
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
, by Weyl’s complete reducibility theorem, both
A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})
and
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
are direct sums of finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

Let
U
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
A(V^{k}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong U(\mathfrak{g})
-module. Then, by (
4.27
) and Theorem
6.2
, we have

{\rm Hom}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(M^{1}\odot M^{2},U)\cong{\rm Hom}_{L(V)_{0}}(M^{1}\odot M^{2},U)\cong I{\binom{(\bar{M}(U^{\ast}))^{\prime}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}.

On the other hand,
M^{2}=V^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
is also a generalized Verma module as a module over the VOA
V_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}(k,0)
. Then, by
[
Liu23
, Theorem 4.20]
and
[
GLZ25
, Proposition 6.3]
, we have

{\rm Hom}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2}),U)\cong I{\binom{(\bar{M}(U^{\ast}))^{\prime}}{M^{1}\;M^{2}}}.

Thus, the multiplicities of the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
U
in
M^{1}\odot M^{2}
and
A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})
are the same. Hence,

A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}\Omega(M^{2})\cong M^{1}\odot M^{2}.

∎

Remark 7.2
.

If
M^{1}=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
and
M^{2}=L_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
are irreducible but not generalized Verma modules, then
\Omega(M^{1})
and
\Omega(M^{2})
are not necessarily the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
L(\lambda)
and
L(\mu)
because of the existence of singular vectors in Verma modules. Hence, we do not have the identification (
7.2
). Examples will be given in the next subsection.

7.1.2.
Examples in the Virasoro VOAs and Li’s example

Let
V=M_{c}
be the (universal) Virasoro VOA
[
FZ92
]
of central charge
c
. Li gave an example in
[
Li99
, Section 2]
showing that the Frenkel–Zhu fusion rules theorem
6.3
does not hold if
M^{2}
and
M^{3}
are not generalized Verma modules.

Let
M(c,h)
be the Verma module over the Virasoro algebra of highest weight
h
and central charge
c
. Recall that
M_{c}=M(c,0)/\langle L(-1)v_{c,0}\rangle
, where
v_{c,0}
is the highest-weight vector. Let
c
be chosen in such a way that the VOA
M_{c}
is simple. Then
\Omega(M_{c})=M_{c}(0)
. Any Virasoro Verma module
M(c,h)
is a module over the VOA
M_{c}
. Li noticed that if

, then

N{\binom{M(c,h_{2})}{M(c,h_{1})\;M_{c}}}=0,\quad\mathrm{but}\quad\dim{\rm Hom}_{A(M_{c})}(A(M(c,h_{1}))\otimes_{A(M_{c})}\Omega(M_{c}),\Omega(M(c,h_{2}))=1.

This is due to the fact that

, as a module over itself, is not a generalized Verma module. However, the formula in Theorem
6.2
holds for this example since
M^{3}=M(c,h_{2})
is a generalized Verma module. We can also see this from the following proposition.

Proposition 7.3
.

The contracted tensor product
M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c}
is spanned by the vector
v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}
, with the
A(M_{c})
(or
L(M_{c})_{0}
)-module action

(7.3)

[\omega].(v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}})=\omega_{[{\rm wt}\omega-1]}.(v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}})=h_{1}\cdot(v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}).

In particular, we have

{\rm Hom}_{A(M_{c})}(M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c},\Omega(M(c,h_{2}))=0=I{\binom{M(c,h_{2})}{M(c,h_{1})\;M_{c}}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
,

M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c}=\mathrm{span}\{L(-n_{1})\dots L(-n_{r})v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}:n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 1\}.

Given
v_{1}\in M(c,h_{1})
, by (
4.24
) and (
4.8
) we have

\displaystyle\left(L(-n-3)+2L(-n-2)+L(-n-1)\right)v_{1}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}=0,\quad n\geq 0,

\displaystyle L(-2)v_{1}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}+L(-1)v_{1}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}=v_{1}\odot L(0)\overline{v_{c,0}}=0.

Hence
M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c}
is spanned by elements of the form

L(-1)^{m}v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}
, with
m\geq 0
. Moreover, applying (
4.9
) to
a=\omega
and
k=2
, we have

L(-1)v_{1}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}=-v_{1}\odot L(-1)\overline{v_{c,0}}=-v_{1}\odot\overline{L(-1)v_{c,0}}=0.

This shows that

M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c}=\mathrm{span}\{v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}\}
.
Finally, by (
4.11
) we have

\omega_{[{\rm wt}\omega-1]}.(v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}})=L(-1)v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}+L(0)v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}+v_{c,h_{1}}\odot L(0)\overline{v_{c,0}}=h_{1}\cdot(v_{c,h_{1}}\odot\overline{v_{c,0}}).

Since
\omega_{[{\rm wt}\omega-1]}.v_{c,h_{2}}=h_{2}\cdot v_{c,h_{2}}
, we have

{\rm Hom}_{A(M_{c})}(M(c,h_{1})\odot M_{c},\Omega(M(c,h_{2}))=0
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

It was proved by Li that

7.2.1.
Basics of the affine VOA
L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

Consider the level-one type
A_{1}
affine VOA
V=L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

[
FZ92
]
. This VOA is strongly rational
[
DLM97
]
, with two irreducible modules

\displaystyle V=L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad W=L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\alpha/2).

According to the fusion rules in the WZNW model
[
V88
,
Bea96
]
, the fusion tensor products of irreducible modules in the category
\mathsf{Adm}(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
are given as follows:

(7.4)

V\boxtimes V=V,\quad V\boxtimes W=W,\quad W\boxtimes W=V.

The Zhu algebra of
L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
can be identified as

A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))\cong U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})/\langle e^{2}\rangle.

This is a
5
-dimensional semisimple algebra, where
\langle e^{2}\rangle
is the two-sided ideal of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
generated by
e^{2}

[
FZ92
]
.
One can easily show that
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
is spanned by
\{1,e,f,h,h^{2}\}
, subject to the relations

(7.5)

eh+e=0;\quad h^{2}-h-2fe=0;\quad fh-f=0;\quad e^{2}=f^{2}=0,

see
[
Liu25
, Section 6]
. The bottom degrees of
V
and
W
give all irreducible modules over
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
up to isomorphism:

\displaystyle S=\Omega(V)=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad e.\mathbf{1}=f.\mathbf{1}=h.\mathbf{1}=0,

\displaystyle T=\Omega(W)=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad e.e^{+}=0,\ f.e^{+}=e^{-},\ h.e^{\pm}=\pm e^{\pm}.

The relations in
S
and
T
match the relations (
7.5
) in
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
. Moreover,
V
and
W
have the following description

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})=\left(U(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})\otimes_{U(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})_{\geq 0}}S\right)/\langle e(-1)^{2}\otimes\mathbf{1}\rangle,

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\alpha/2)=\left(U(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})\otimes_{U(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})_{\geq 0}}T\right)/\langle e(-1)\otimes e^{+}\rangle.

Since
(\bar{M}(-)\dashv\Omega(-)):\mathsf{Mod}(A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})))\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
is an equivalence of categories,

\displaystyle\bar{M}(S)

\displaystyle\cong V=\mathrm{span}\{a^{1}(-n_{1})\dots a^{r}(-n_{r})\mathbf{1}:a^{i}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\ n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 0,\ e(-1)^{2}\mathbf{1}=0\},

\displaystyle\bar{M}(T)

\displaystyle\cong W=\mathrm{span}\{b^{1}(-m_{1})\dots b^{k}(-m_{k})e^{\pm}:b^{j}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\ m_{1}\geq\dots\geq m_{k}\geq 0,\ e(-1)e^{+}=0\}.

7.2.2.
Fusion tensor product on
\mathsf{Mod}(A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})))

We want to verify that our
\odot
-tensor product, together with (
6.8
), gives rise to the correct fusion tensor product (
7.4
) in the category
\mathsf{Mod}(A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})))
. In other words,

(7.6)

S\boxtimes S=S,\quad S\boxtimes T=T,\quad T\boxtimes T=S.

We only prove
T\boxtimes T=W\odot W\cong S
. The other cases are similar, and we omit the details.

Lemma 7.5
.

As a vector space,
W\odot W
is spanned by
\alpha\odot\beta
, where
\alpha,\beta\in T=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-}
.

Proof.

This lemma is an easy consequence of Lemma
4.5
. For the sake of completeness, we write out the full details of the proof.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}=V_{1}
. Using (
4.12
), with
s=1
and
t=n\geq 1
, we have

(7.7)

a(-n)u\odot v=-u\odot\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-n}{j}(-1)^{n+j}a(j)v,\quad u,v\in W.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle a^{1}(-n_{1})\dots a^{r}(-n_{r})\alpha\odot b^{1}(-m_{1})\dots b^{k}(-m_{k})\beta

\displaystyle=-a^{2}(-n_{2})\dots a^{r}(-n_{r})\alpha\odot\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-n_{1}}{j}(-1)^{n_{1}+j}a^{1}(j)b^{1}(-m_{1})\dots b^{k}(-m_{k})\beta

\displaystyle=\alpha\odot\sum\lambda\cdot a^{r}(j_{r})\dots a^{1}(j_{1})b^{1}(-m_{1})\dots b^{k}(-m_{k})\beta.

On the other hand, using (
4.9
) with
k=1+n
, we have

(7.8)

\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-n}{j}a(j)u\odot v=-u\odot a(-n)v,\quad u,v\in W.

For any
\alpha\in T
, we have
a(0)\alpha=a.\alpha\in T
since
T
is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module, and
a(j).\alpha=0
if
j>0
. Then by (
7.8
), for any
\alpha,\beta\in T
,
n_{1}\geq\dots\geq n_{r}\geq 1
, and
c^{1},\dots,c^{r}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\odot c^{1}(-n_{1})\dots c^{r}(-n_{r})\beta

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-n_{1}}{j}c^{1}(j)\alpha\odot c^{2}(-n_{2})\dots c^{r}(-n_{r})\beta

\displaystyle=-c^{1}.\alpha\odot c^{2}(-n_{2})\dots c^{r}(-n_{r})\beta

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}(c^{r}\dots c^{1}).\alpha\odot\beta\in T\odot T,

with
(c^{r}\dots c^{1}).\alpha\in T
.
Hence
W\odot W
is spanned by

\{\alpha\odot\beta:\alpha,\beta\in T=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-}\}
.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

The following relations hold in
W\odot W
:

e^{+}\odot e^{+}=e^{-}\odot e^{-}=0,\quad e^{-}\odot e^{+}+e^{+}\odot e^{-}=0.

In particular,

T\boxtimes T=W\odot W=\mathbb{C}(e^{-}\odot e^{+})\cong S

as a left
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
-module.

Proof.

Note that
e(-1).e^{+}=0
in
W
. By (
7.7
) with
a=e
and
n=1
, we have

0=e(-1).e^{+}\odot e^{-}=-e^{+}\odot\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{-1}{j}(-1)^{1+j}e(j).e^{-}=e^{+}\odot e^{+}.

Now apply
f\in A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
to
e^{+}\odot e^{+}
. Note that
f.e^{+}=e^{-}
in
T
. Then by (
4.23
), we have

0=f.(e^{+}\odot e^{+})=f.e^{+}\odot e^{+}+e^{+}\odot f.e^{+}=e^{-}\odot e^{+}+e^{+}\odot e^{-}.

Applying
f
once more to the equation

e^{-}\odot e^{+}+e^{+}\odot e^{-}=0
, we obtain
e^{-}\odot e^{-}=0
.

Therefore,

T\boxtimes T=\mathrm{span}\{e^{\pm}\odot e^{\pm}\}=\mathrm{span}\{e^{+}\odot e^{-}=-e^{-}\odot e^{+}\}.

It is easy to check that

e.(e^{+}\odot e^{-})=f.(e^{+}\odot e^{-})=h.(e^{+}\odot e^{-})=0
.
Furthermore, since
W\cong W^{\prime}
as an
L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-module, we have

T\cong T^{\vee}
as a left
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
-module. Then by Corollary
6.10
,
\mathbf{1}\subset T\boxtimes T^{\vee}
.
Hence
T\boxtimes T\neq 0
, and it is isomorphic to
S
as an
A(L_{1}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}))
-module.
∎

(7.11)

\displaystyle L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},0\right)

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
a,b\in\{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{16}\}
. Then

(7.16)

T(a)\boxtimes T(b)=L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right)\odot L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},b\right)=\mathrm{span}\{u\odot v,\ L(-1)u\odot v\},

where
u
and
v
are the highest-weight vectors of

L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right)
and
L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},b\right)
, respectively.

The action of
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))=\mathbb{C}[\bar{x}]
on
T(a)\boxtimes T(b)
is given by

(7.17)

\bar{x}.(u_{1}\odot v_{1})=L(-1)u_{1}\odot v_{1}+L(0)u_{1}\odot v_{1}+u_{1}\odot L(0)v_{1},

where
u_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right)
and

v_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},b\right)
; see also (
7.3
).

Proof.

By (
4.14
), we have

(7.18)

T(a)\boxtimes T(b)=\mathrm{span}\{u_{1}\odot v:u_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},b\right)\}.

We first list several relations arising from (
4.8
) and (
4.9

(7.19)

\displaystyle\left(L(-n-3)+2L(-n-2)+L(-n-1)\right)u_{1}\odot v=0,\quad n\geq 0,\ u_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right),

(7.20)

\displaystyle L(-2)u_{1}\odot v+L(-1)u_{1}\odot v=u_{1}\odot L(0)v=b\cdot u_{1}\odot v,\quad u_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right),

(7.21)

\displaystyle L(-1)u_{1}\odot v_{1}+u_{1}\odot L(-1)v_{1}=0,\quad u_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},a\right),\ v_{1}\in L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},b\right).

The first relation arises from the action of

\omega\otimes\frac{z^{{\rm wt}\omega}}{(z-1)^{2}}
,
the second from the action of

\omega\otimes\frac{z^{{\rm wt}\omega-1}}{z-1}
,
and the third from the action of

\omega\otimes(z-1)^{{\rm wt}\omega-2}
;
see (
3.16
).

It follows from (
7.18
) and (
7.19
) that

T(a)\boxtimes T(b)=\mathrm{span}\{L(-1)^{k}u\odot v:k\geq 0\}.

Moreover, by (
7.12
) and (

, where
\lambda=3/4
or
4/3
.
Then (
7.20
) implies

Finally, for the generator

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(u_{1}\odot v_{1})

Proposition 7.8
.

Write
u=v_{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}}
and
v=v_{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{16}}
for the highest-weight vectors of

L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
and
L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{16}\right)
, respectively. Then

(7.22)

\displaystyle L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u=0\quad\mathrm{in}\quad T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2}),

(7.23)

\displaystyle L(-1)u\odot v+\tfrac{1}{2}u\odot v=0\quad\mathrm{in}\quad T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16}).

It follows that

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2})\cong T(0),\quad T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})\cong T(\tfrac{1}{16}),

as left
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))
-modules.
On the other hand,
L(-1)v\odot v
and
v\odot v
are linearly independent in

T(\tfrac{1}{16})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})
, and

(7.24)

T(\tfrac{1}{16})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})\cong T(0)\oplus T(\tfrac{1}{2})

as left
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))
-modules.

Proof.

We first show that (
7.22
) and (
7.23
) arise from the relation

\bar{x}\cdot(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2})\cdot(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{16})=0

in the Zhu algebra
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))
.
Indeed, let
u=v_{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}}
. Then by (
7.17
),

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(u\odot u)

\displaystyle=L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u,

\displaystyle(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}).(u\odot u)

\displaystyle=L(-1)u\odot u+\tfrac{1}{2}u\odot u,

\displaystyle(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{16}).(u\odot u)

\displaystyle=L(-1)u\odot u+\tfrac{15}{16}u\odot u.

On the other hand, using the fact that

L(-2)u=\tfrac{3}{4}L(-1)^{2}u
in
L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)

and (
7.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(L(-1)u\odot u)

\displaystyle=L(-1)^{2}u\odot u+L(0)L(-1)u\odot u+L(-1)u\odot L(0)u

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4}{3}L(-2)u\odot u+2L(-1)u\odot u

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4}{3}\bigl(-L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot L(0)u\bigr)+2L(-1)u\odot u

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}).\bar{x}.(u\odot u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{7}{6}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{16}).(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}).\bar{x}.(u\odot u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{7}{6}\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr)+\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr)-\tfrac{1}{16}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{539}{288}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u\bigr).

In particular,
L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u=0
in

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2})
, proving (
7.22
).

Now we have
T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2})=\mathbb{C}\,u\odot u
, and by (
7.17
),

\bar{x}.(u\odot u)=L(-1)u\odot u+u\odot u=0.

Moreover, since
T(\tfrac{1}{2})
is self-dual, it follows from Corollary
6.10
(3) that

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2})\neq 0
.
Hence
T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{2})\cong T(0)
.

A similar calculation shows that the following relation holds in

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})
:

0=(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{16}).(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}).\bar{x}.(u\odot v)=\tfrac{385}{2304}\bigl(L(-1)u\odot v+\tfrac{1}{2}u\odot v\bigr).

This proves (
7.23
), and

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})=\mathbb{C}\,u\odot v
.
Moreover, by (
7.17

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(u\odot v)

\displaystyle=L(-1)u\odot v+L(0)u\odot v+u\odot L(0)v

\displaystyle=L(-1)u\odot v+\tfrac{1}{2}u\odot v+\tfrac{1}{16}u\odot v

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16}u\odot v.

This shows that

T(\tfrac{1}{2})\boxtimes T(\tfrac{1}{16})\cong T(\tfrac{1}{16})

as an
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))
-module.

Finally, for
v=v_{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{16}}
, we have

(7.25)

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(v\odot v)

\displaystyle=L(-1)v\odot v+\tfrac{1}{8}v\odot v,

\displaystyle\bar{x}.(L(-1)v\odot v)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3}{8}L(-1)v\odot v+\tfrac{3}{64}v\odot v.

In particular, it is easy to check that

(\bar{x}-\tfrac{1}{2})\cdot\bar{x}.(v\odot v)=0.

Hence the relation in

L(-1)v\odot v

as an
A(L\left(\frac{1}{2},0\right))
-module, proving (
7.24
).
∎

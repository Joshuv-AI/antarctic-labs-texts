Tropical super Gromov-Witten invariants
Artan Sheshmani 1,2, Shing-Tung Yau 1,3, Benjamin Zhou 3

Abstract
. We show that super Gromov-Witten invariants can be defined and computed by methods of tropical geometry. When the target is a point, the super invariants are descendant invariants on the moduli space of curves, which can be computed tropically. When the target is a convex, toric variety X X , we describe a procedure to compute the tropical Euler class of the SUSY normal bundle N ¯ n , β \overline{N}_{n,\beta} on ℳ ¯ 0 , n ​ ( X , β ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta) , assuming it is locally tropicalizable in the sense of [ CG24 ] , [ CGM22 ] . Then, we define the tropical, genus-0, n n -marked, super Gromov-Witten invariant of X X , and compute an example. This gives a tropical interpretation of super Gromov-Witten invariants of convex, toric varieties.



1.
Introduction

Tropical geometry has proven to be useful in translating questions in enumerative geometry to more combinatorial ones. By now, there have been many theorems showing an equivalence between counts of certain combinatorial graphs such as tropical curves in affine space and Gromov-Witten invariants of a target space
X
, which are morally counts of curves in
X
. For example, when
X
is a toric surface, Milhalkin showed that genus-0, Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
with point-incidence conditions can be computed using tropical curves
[
Mik05
]
. Then, Nishinou and Siebert generalized
[
Mik05
]
to higher dimensional toric varieties
[
NS06
]
. More recently, Bousseau showed that a
q
-refined count of tropical curves in affine space corresponds to higher genus logarithmic Gromov-Witten invariants of toric surfaces with
\lambda_{g}
-insertions
[
Bou19
]
. Tropical techniques are also used to prove a degeneration formula for logarithmic Gromov-Witten invariants
[
ACGS20
]
,
[
KLR23
]
. Tropical geometry has shown its usefulness in its ability to compute certain Gromov-Witten invariants in all genera.

Suppose that
X
is a convex, toric variety. Recently, Keßler, Sheshmani, and Yau developed super Gromov-Witten theory, which is morally a count of supercurves in
X
.
In
[
KSY22
]
, they construct a moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{super}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
of
J
-holomorphic maps from genus-0,
n
-marked supercurves in class
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Subsequently in
[
KSY23b
]

\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{super}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
with fixed locus given by the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{spin}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
of genus-0, 2-spin stable maps, which was constructed by
[
JKV05
]
. We can in fact replace
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{spin}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
by the Kontsevich space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
of genus-0,
n
-marked stable maps to
X
by
[
JKV05
]
, Theorem 5.1.1. Hence, we have the embedding,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{N}_{n,\beta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)}

Hence, by performing localization with respect to the toric action on

2.1.
Tropical geometry

The tropical semi-ring
\mathbb{T}
is the set
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
with two operations
\oplus
and
\odot
, defined by
a\oplus b:={\operatorname{max}}(a,b)
and
a\odot b:=a+b.
The additive and multiplicative identities are given by
-\infty
and 0 respectively. Let
\mathbb{T}^{*}
be the multiplicative units of
\mathbb{T}
.

Example 2.1
.

Tropical projective space
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{n}
is defined by the equivalence relation given by tropical multiplication,

\mathbb{T}\mathbb{P}^{n}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}^{n}|\lambda\sim\lambda^{\prime}\textit{ if }\exists\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{*}\textit{ such that }\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}\odot\lambda^{\prime\prime}\}

Let
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the space of functions
f:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
consisting of tropical polynomials over the tropical semi-ring,

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in S}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}

where
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a finite index set. Converting to ordinary addition and multiplication,
f
is equivalently,

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\min\{a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}+\sum_{i}i_{k}x_{k}\ |(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in S\}

For an ordinary polynomial
f
, we write
Trop(f)
to be the corresponding polynomial with operations in the tropical semi-ring.

.

f=x+y+1\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]

e_{1},e_{2}

Proof.

We show that the integrand
(ft)^{*n-4}\psi_{4}^{i_{4}}\ldots(ft)^{*}\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}
is equal to,

\psi_{4}^{i_{4}}\ldots\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}

where the
\psi
-classes are now understood to be in

We first show,

ft^{*}(\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}})\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}=\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
ft^{*}\psi_{n-1}=\psi_{n-1}-D_{n-1,n}
. Since pullback commutes with cup product and
\psi_{n-1}\cdot D_{n-1,n}=0
, we have that
ft^{*}(\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}})=(ft^{*}\psi_{n-1})^{i_{n-1}}=\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}+(-1)^{i_{n-1}}(D_{n-1,n})^{i_{n-1}}
. Since
\psi_{n}\cdot D_{n-1,n}=0
, we have
ft^{*}(\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}})\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}=\psi_{n-1}^{i_{n-1}}\psi_{n}^{i_{n}}

4.2.3.

is a vector bundle

Let

]

be a family of genus-0, stable maps over

,

is a vector bundle on

. We show the following,

Proposition 4.9
.

ft_{*}\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
is a vector bundle on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
.

Proof.

We verify that
h^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)|_{ft^{-1}(C)},\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)})
is independent in

to apply Grauert’s theorem. Recall that when
X
is convex,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
is a normal projective variety that is locally the quotient of a nonsingular variety by a finite group. If
X
is homogeneous,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
is irreducible. The morphism
ft:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is flat by
[
KV07
]
, Lemma 2.6.7. Since projective varieties are complete,
ft
is also proper. Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is also a projective variety,
ft
is a projective morphism. By Nakayama’s lemma,
\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
is a locally free coherent sheaf on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
as its fibers are of the same rank. It is well known that the Euler characteristic
\chi(\overline{N}_{n,\beta})
on the fibers of
ft
, i.e.

\chi(\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)}):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}h^{i}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)|_{ft^{-1}(C)},\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)})

is a locally constant function in
C\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is connected, the Euler characteristic of all fibers
ft^{-1}(C)
agree.

We want to show that
h^{0}(\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)})
is constant in
C
. It suffices to show that
h^{i}(\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)})=0
for
i>0
, on
ft^{-1}(U)
for some open neighborhood
U\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. Any
C\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
exists in a distinguished affine open neighborhood
D(f)
for some element
f
in an open affine subset
U\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, as projective schemes can be covered by affine schemes. Denote
ft_{U}:ft^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U
to be the restriction of
ft
on the open subscheme
ft^{-1}(U)
. Let
z:ft_{U}^{-1}(D(f))\rightarrow D(f)
denote the restriction of
ft_{U}
onto
ft_{U}^{-1}(D(f))
. By the definition of scheme morphism, we have
z^{-1}(D(f))=D(z^{\#}(f))
. Hence,
ft^{-1}(C)\subset D(z^{\#}(f))
. By Serre’s Theorem,
H^{i}(D(z^{\#}(f)),\overline{N}_{n,\beta})=0
for
i>0
. In particular, for each
i>0
,
R^{i}ft_{*}\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
is locally free on

. By base change (
[
Vak25
]
, Theorem 28.1.6), we have
H^{i}(\overline{N}_{n,\beta}|_{ft^{-1}(C)})=0
for

Hence by constancy of the Euler characteristic,

is constant in
C\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. By Grauert’s theorem,
ft_{*}\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
is a vector bundle.
∎

4.5.
Tropical inverse equivariant Euler class of SUSY bundle

In this section, we define the tropical inverse equivariant Euler class
e^{K,trop}(\overline{N}_{n,\beta})^{-1}
of the SUSY bundle
\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
following
[
CGM22
]
,
[
CG24
]
. Since we are interested in computing the inverse equivariant Euler class of
\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
, we use the splitting principle to write
\overline{N}_{n,\beta}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{rk_{n,\beta}}N_{i}
, where
N_{i}
are line bundles on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)

It is well known that the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
is stratified by dual graph
\Gamma
, where
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)=\bigcup_{\Gamma}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
is the moduli space of stable maps with dual graph
\Gamma

[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}]^{vir}:=\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)\right]^{vir}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}}

\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)\right]^{vir}=\sum_{\Gamma}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}]^{vir}

We make the following assumption about families of stable maps, in order to define a tropical inverse equivariant Euler class of
\overline{N}_{n,\beta}
,

Assumption 4.17
.

Let

be a family of genus-0 stable maps of dual graph type
\Gamma
in curve class
\beta
over a base space
B
. Suppose
B
admits a covering
\{U_{i}\}
such that each
U_{i}
is a toroidal, spin variety. Being spin means
U_{i}
admits a invertible sheaf

such that

(Footnote

\phi:\mathcal{C}/B\rightarrow X

U_{i}

\phi

over
U_{i}

\Gamma
. We assume that

[

[

For each

A closer look at the algebraic-operator correspondence between position and momentum space in Quantum Mechanics
Siddharth Dwivedi

Abstract
In this work we take a closer look at the algebraic-operator correspondence between the momentum ( k k ) space and the position ( x x ) space which gives us the form of the momentum operator in position space and vice versa in Quantum Mechanics. Taking the definition of the Fourier Transform as the starting point, we present a two parameter generalisation of the form of the momentum operator in position space followed by a generalisation of the momentum eigenfunctions and a few other results. It is also seen that the results obtained here have a well defined limit to the standard results of Quantum Mechanics.



1
Introduction

On one level, Quantum Mechanics can be interpreted as an application of Fourier Transform (FT)
Sakurai:2011zz
;
griffiths_introduction_2018
which connects the momentum space and the position space via the correspondence

\displaystyle({\rm Momentum~space})~\hslash k\to-i\hslash\cfrac{d}{dx}~({\rm Position~space})

(1)

In the context of one dimensional FT, if we consider the (wave)functions in position and momentum space, represented by
\alpha(x)
and
\beta(k)
respectively, then they are connected by FT through the following
Arfken:379118
:

\displaystyle\alpha(x)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(k)~e^{ikx}~dk

(2)

\displaystyle\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(x)~e^{-ikx}~dx

(3)

Using Eq.(
3
), we can derive the

k\to-i\dfrac{d}{dx}
correspondence as follows:

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k~\alpha(x)~e^{-ikx}~dx=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}i~\alpha(x)~\frac{d}{dx}e^{-ikx}~dx,

Doing integration by parts we can write,

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}i\left[~\frac{d}{dx}\left(\alpha(x)e^{-ikx}\right)-\left(~\frac{d\alpha(x)}{dx}\right)e^{-ikx}\right]~dx

Assuming
\alpha(x)
to be a well behaved function that vanishes as
x\to\pm{\infty}
, the first integral gives zero (vanishing boundary term). Thus we can write

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}~\left(-i~\frac{d\alpha(x)}{dx}\right)e^{-ikx}~dx

(4)

Thus we recover the correspondence in
1
.

In what follows, we take a point of view where we generalise the situation by assuming the definition of the inverse FT to be

\displaystyle~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}~\alpha(x)~G(k,x)~dx

(5)

where
G(k,x)=e^{-ikx}
if the above definition has to be the familiar inverse FT.
Now, if we generalise the correspondence to be
k\to f(x)\frac{d}{dx}
, then what are the possible solutions for
f(x)
consistent with
G(k,x)=e^{-ikx}
? This is what we derive next in Section
2
which leads us to the Hermitian generalisation of the momentum operator in position basis. In Section
3
we solve for the generalised momentum eigenfunctions. Section
4
deals with the calculation of position operator in momentum space. This is followed by a discussion of the generalisation of the fundamental commutator in Section
5
and the resulting considerations. Subsequently in Section
6
, as an illustration, we solve for the eigenfunctions and eigenspectrum of the infinite square well using the generalised momentum operator. We finally conclude with a summary and discussion of our results in Section
7
.

2
Solving for
f(x)
in
k\to f(x)\cfrac{d}{dx}

Again, as before, using the generalised form of the inverse FT (Eq.
5
) we have,

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k~\alpha(x)~G(k,x)~dx=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(f(x)~\frac{d\alpha(x)}{dx}\right)G(k,x)~dx

(6)

The last equality comes because we are starting with the assumption that
k\to f(x)\cfrac{d}{dx}
. Again, doing integration by parts on Eq.(
6
) we can write,

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\alpha(x)~f(x)~G(k,x)\right)-\left(\alpha(x)~\frac{df(x)}{dx}~G(k,x)\right)-\left(\alpha(x)~f(x)~\frac{dG(k,x)}{dx}\right)\right]~dx

(7)

Here again, the first term gives a boundary term which vanishes for well behaved
\alpha(x)
and we get,

\displaystyle k~\beta(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[-\left(\alpha(x)~\frac{df(x)}{dx}~G(k,x)\right)-\left(\alpha(x)~f(x)~\frac{dG(k,x)}{dx}\right)\right]~dx

(8)

Comparing Eqs. (
6
) and (
8
) we can write

\displaystyle\frac{df(x)}{dx}~G(k,x)+f(x)~\frac{dG(k,x)}{dx}=-kG(k,x)

(9)

Note that we got Eq.(
9
) by setting the integrands in
Eqs. (
6
) and (
8
) equal. This in general is not true because equality of two definite integrals does not necessarily imply equality of integrands. But here we wish to seek such functions
f(x)
and
G(k,x)
for which the integrands are equal. This is a constraint we impose on our desired solutions.

Integrating Eq.(
9
) we have for
G(k,x)
,

\displaystyle{G(k,x)={\cal M}~{\rm exp}\left[-\int{\frac{1}{f(x)}\left(k+\frac{df(x)}{dx}\right)dx}\right]},

(10)

where
\cal M
is an arbitrary complex constant.

Since we want that
G(k,x)=e^{-ikx}
, this means
{\cal M}=1
and also

\displaystyle\int{\frac{1}{f(x)}\left(k+\frac{df(x)}{dx}\right)dx}=ikx

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{f(x)}\left(k+\frac{df(x)}{dx}\right)=ik

(11)

Using Eqn (
11
) we have two options to get
G(k,x)=e^{-ikx}
:

•

Case 1:

f(x)=-i
. Then directly in Eq.(
10
) we can substitute for
f(x)
and we get
G(k,x)=e^{-ikx}

•

Case 2:

f(x)\neq-i
. In this case we need to solve Eq.(
11
). Rearranging,we get

\displaystyle\frac{df(x)}{dx}=ik(f(x)+i)

\displaystyle\implies\int{\frac{df(x)}{f(x)+i}}=ik\int dx+C_{1}

Here
C_{1}
is an arbitray complex integration constant. Integrating, we have for
f(x)
,

\displaystyle f(x)={\cal C}e^{ikx}-i

(12)

where
{\cal C}
is an arbitary complex constant and
{\cal C}\neq 0
.

Thus, in the most generalised form, we get the correspondence to be
k\to({\cal C}e^{ikx}-i)\cfrac{d}{dx}
. Taking cue from this, we propose the generalisation of the momentum operator in position space to be,

\displaystyle\widehat{p}=\hslash({\cal C}e^{ikx}-i)\frac{d}{dx}

(13)

The three dimensional generalisation through a similar churning as above, is easy to obtain and comes out to be,

\displaystyle\widehat{\bf p}=\hslash({\cal C}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}-i){\bf\nabla}

(14)

where
\vec{k}
and
\vec{x}
are the three dimensional wave vector and position vector respectively.
In the following sections we will restrict our focus to one dimensional case.

2.1
Hermitian generalisation of the momentum operator

In general for non-vanishing complex constant
{\cal C}
, the generalised momentum operator in Eq.(
13
) is not Hermitian. Its Hermitian conjugate is given by,

\displaystyle{\widehat{p}}^{\dagger}=-\hslash\left({\cal C}^{*}e^{-ikx}+i\right)~\frac{d}{dx}~+i\hslash k~{\cal C}^{*}e^{-ikx}~

(15)

To construct the Hermitian extension of the familiar momentum operator
\widehat{p_{0}}=-i\hslash\frac{d}{dx}
we define the modified Hermitian operator
\widehat{p}_{H}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}=\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{p}+{\widehat{p}}^{\dagger})=\frac{\hslash}{2}\left[\left({\cal C}e^{ikx}-{\cal C}^{*}e^{-ikx}-2i\right)\frac{d}{dx}+ik{\cal C}^{*}e^{-ikx}\right]

(16)

Defining
{\cal C}=a+ib
with
a,~b
as real parameters (both
a
and
b
simultaneously cannot be
0
as that would mean
{\cal C}=0
, which is not a valid assumption for the solution in Eq.(
12
).), we can reexpress
\widehat{p}_{H}
as,

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}=i\hslash\left[\left(a\sin(kx)+b\cos(kx)-1\right)\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{k}{2}(a-ib)e^{-ikx}\right]

(17)

3
Eigenfunctions of
\widehat{p}_{H}

For calculating the eigenfunctions of
\widehat{p}_{H}
we consider the following two special cases:

•

Case 1:
b=0

For this choice, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}=i\hslash\left[\left(a\sin(kx)-1\right)\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{ka}{2}e^{-ikx}\right]

(18)

The eigenvalue equation is

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}~\psi_{k}(x)=p~\psi_{k}(x)=\hslash k~\psi_{k}(x)

(19)

where
\psi_{k}(x)
is the eigenfunction corresponding to the momentum eigenvalue
p=\hslash k

Substituting from Eq.(
18
)

\displaystyle i\left[\left(a\sin(kx)-1\right)\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{ka}{2}e^{-ikx}\right]\psi_{k}(x)=k~\psi_{k}(x)

(20)

Integrating Eq.(
20
) we have,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)={\cal A}~{\rm exp}[ikI_{1}]

(21)

where
\cal A
is an arbitrary complex constant and,

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int{\cfrac{\left(1-\cfrac{ia}{2}e^{-ikx}\right)}{(1-a\sin(kx))}}~dx

(22)

For
|a|<1
the integrand in
I_{1}
does not have a pole for any value of
x
.
Solving for
I_{1}
we get for the eigenfunction,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=\frac{{\cal A}}{\sqrt{|1-a\sin(kx)|}}~{\rm exp}\left[i\left(\frac{kx}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan\left(\frac{kx}{2}\right)-a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\right)\right)\right]

(23)

Thus, we have for the probability density

\displaystyle|\psi_{k}(x)|^{2}=\frac{|{\cal A}|^{2}}{|1-a\sin(kx)|}

(24)

We can see that
\psi_{k}(x)\to{\cal A}~e^{ikx}
and
|\psi_{k}(x)|^{2}\to|{\cal A}|^{2}
(constant) as
a\to 0
. For this to agree to the familiar result in QM, we must have
{\cal A}=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
.

•

Case 2:
a=0

In this case the momentum operator is,

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}=i\hslash\left[\left(b\cos(kx)-1\right)\frac{d}{dx}-\frac{ikb}{2}e^{-ikx}\right]

(25)

The eigenvalue equation becomes

\displaystyle i\left[\left(b\cos(kx)-1\right)\frac{d}{dx}-\frac{ikb}{2}e^{-ikx}\right]~\psi_{k}(x)=k~\psi_{k}(x)

(26)

Integrating, we have,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)={\cal B}~{\rm exp}[ikI_{2}]

(27)

where
{\cal B}
is an arbitrary complex constant and,

\displaystyle{\rm where}~I_{2}=\int{\cfrac{\left(1-\cfrac{b}{2}e^{-ikx}\right)}{(1-b\cos(kx))}}~dx

(28)

For
|b|<1
, integrand in
I_{2}
does not have a pole for any value of
x
.

Solving for
I_{2}
we have for the eigenfunction
\psi_{k}(x)
,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=\frac{{\cal B}}{\sqrt{|1-b\cos(kx)|}}~{\rm exp}\left[i\left(\frac{kx}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left[\sqrt{\frac{1+b}{1-b}}\tan\left(\frac{kx}{2}\right)\right]\right)\right]

(29)

The probability density becomes,

\displaystyle|\psi_{k}(x)|^{2}=\frac{|{\cal B}|^{2}}{|1-b\cos(kx)|}

(30)

As before,
{\cal B}=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
for the results to agree with familiar results in QM in the limit
b\to 0
.

•

Case 3: The general case,
a\neq 0,~b\neq 0

This involves more detailed algebraic manipulation and is not specially informative apart from the fact that the mometum eigenfunctions are well defined for
a^{2}+b^{2}<1
. So we do not present here an explicit calculation for the same.

4
Position operator in momentum space

For getting the position operator
\widehat{x}
in momentum basis we start by demanding that the
x\to k
correspondence be of the form
x\to h(k)\cfrac{d}{dk}
. Here again we start with a more general form of the definition of the FT given in Eq.(
2
),
i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}~\beta(k)~\widetilde{G}(k,x)~dk

(31)

where
\widetilde{G}(k,x)=e^{ikx}
for the usual FT relationship. The correspondence
x\to h(k)\frac{d}{dk}
implies,

\displaystyle x~\alpha(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x~\beta(k)~\widetilde{G}(k,x)~dk=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(h(k)\frac{d\beta(k)}{dk}\right)~\widetilde{G}(k,x)~dk

(32)

Integrating by parts, and under the assumption of
\beta(k)
to be well behaved (vanishes for
k\to\pm\infty
), we get

\displaystyle x~\alpha(x)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[-\left(\beta(k)~\frac{dh(k)}{dk}~\widetilde{G}(k,x)\right)-\left(\beta(k)~h(k)~\frac{d\widetilde{G}(k,x)}{dk}\right)\right]~dk

(33)

Comparing Eqs. (
32
) and (
33
) we can write

\displaystyle h(k)~\frac{d\widetilde{G}(k,x)}{dk}=-\widetilde{G}(k,x)\left(x+\frac{dh(k)}{dk}\right)

(34)

Integrating Eq.(
34
) we have for
\widetilde{G}(k,x)

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(k,x)={\cal K}~{\rm exp}\left[-\int{\frac{1}{h(k)}\left(x+\frac{dh(k)}{dk}\right)dk}\right]

(35)

Here
\cal K
is an arbitrary complex constant.

Now, since we want that
\widetilde{G}(k,x)=e^{ikx}
, this means
{\cal K}=1
and also

\displaystyle\int{\frac{1}{h(k)}\left(x+\frac{dh(k)}{dk}\right)dk}=-ikx

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{h(k)}\left(x+\frac{dh(k)}{dk}\right)=-ix

(36)

Using Eq.(
36
) we have two options to get
\widetilde{G}(k,x)=e^{ikx}
:

•

Case 1:

h(k)=i
. Substituing in Eq.(
35
) gives
\widetilde{G}(k,x)=e^{ikx}
.

•

Case 2:

h(x)\neq i
. In this case we need to solve Eq.(
36
). Rearranging,we get

\displaystyle\frac{dh(k)}{dk}=-ix~(h(k)-i)

\displaystyle\implies\int{\frac{dh(k)}{h(k)-i}}=-ikx+D_{1}

Here
D_{1}
is an arbitray complex integration constant. Integrating, we have for
h(k)
,

\displaystyle h(k)={\cal D}e^{-ikx}+i

(37)

where
{\cal D}
is an arbitary complex constant and
{\cal D}\neq 0
.

Thus, in the most generalised form, we get the correspondence to be
x\to({\cal D}e^{-ikx}+i)\cfrac{d}{dk}
. This gives us the generalisation of the position operator in momentum space to be

\displaystyle\widehat{x}=({\cal D}e^{-ikx}+i)\cfrac{d}{dk}

(38)

4.1
Hermitian generalisation of momentum-space position operator

The generalised position operator given in Eq.(
38
) is not Hermitian for non-zero values of
{\cal D}
. Analogous to the Hermitian generalisation of the momentum operator in position space, we define the Hermitian generalisation of the position operator as

\displaystyle\widehat{x}_{H}=\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{x}+\widehat{x}^{\dagger})

(39)

where, the Hermitian conjugate operator
\widehat{x}^{\dagger}
has the form

\displaystyle\widehat{x}^{\dagger}=\left({-\cal D^{*}}e^{ikx}+i\right)\cfrac{d}{dk}-ik{\cal D^{*}}e^{ikx}

(40)

Defining
{\cal D}=c+id
,
c,~d
being real constants (with
c,~d
simultaneously not allowed to be zero as that is outside the domain of validity of the solution in Eq.(
37
) ), we have
for
\widehat{x}_{H}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{x}_{H}=i(1-c\sin(kx)+d\cos(kx))\cfrac{d}{dk}-\frac{ik}{2}(c-id)~e^{ikx}

(41)

5
Generalisation of the fundamental commutator

In what follows we will assume that corresponding to an arbitrary ket
|\lambda\rangle
in the Hilbert space, the normalised position-space wavefunction is
\psi_{\lambda}(x)
and the momentum-space wavefunction (also normalised) is
\phi_{\lambda}(k)
and define two functions
\eta(x)
and
\sigma(k)
of
x
and
k
respectively as follows:

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi_{\lambda}(k)|^{2}~e^{ikx}~dk

(42)

\displaystyle\sigma(k)=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}~e^{-ikx}~dx

(43)

5.1
[
\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}
] in position space

Using the position-space operators we have, for the normalised position-space wavefunction
\psi_{\lambda}(x)
,

\displaystyle[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]\psi_{\lambda}(x)=i\hslash\left[1-a\sin(kx)-b\cos(kx)\right]\psi_{\lambda}(x)

(44)

If we calculate the expectation value of the commutator
\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle
in position space, we get

\displaystyle{\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle}_{x}=i\hslash\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\lambda}^{*}(x)(1-a\sin(kx)-b\cos(kx))\psi_{\lambda}(x)~dx

Using the definitions in Eqns. (
42
), (
43
) and noticing that
\eta^{*}(x)=\eta(-x)
and
\sigma^{*}(k)=\sigma(-k)
, we can write

\displaystyle{\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle}_{x}=i\hslash\left[1+\sqrt{2\pi}~{\rm Im}((a-ib)~\sigma(k))\right]

(45)

5.2
[
\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}
] in momentum space

Using the momentum-space operators we have,

\displaystyle[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]\phi_{\lambda}(k)=i\hslash\left[1-c\sin(kx)+d\cos(kx)\right]\phi_{\lambda}(k)

(46)

If we calculate
{\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle}
in momentum-space, we get,

\displaystyle{\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle}_{k}=i\hslash\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{\lambda}^{*}(k)(1-c\sin(kx)+d\cos(kx))\phi_{\lambda}(k)~dk

Again, using Eqns. (
42
), (
43
) we can write

\displaystyle{\langle\lambda|~[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{H}]~|\lambda\rangle}_{k}=i\hslash\left[1-\sqrt{2\pi}~{\rm Im}((c-id)~\eta(x))\right]

(47)

Eqns. (
45
) and (
47
) and the fact that the expectation value of any operator in a given state must be basis independent implies that we must have

\displaystyle{\rm Im}((a-ib)~\sigma(k))=-{\rm Im}((c-id)~\eta(x))

(48)

If this equation has to have a solution for any arbitrary
k
and
x
, then the only possibility is that
a,b,c,d=0
and we recover our usual QM.
But if one chooses non-zero values of
a,b,c,d
(respecting the constraints
a^{2}+b^{2}<1,~c^{2}+d^{2}<1
), then if we fix
k
to be some
k_{0}
, the expectation value is basis independent for only those points in
x
which are the roots of the above equation. Conversely, if we choose a fix point
x=x_{0}
, then the expectation value is the same in both representations for only those few
k
points, which again are the roots of the above equation.

6
A simple test case: The Infinite Square Well Potential

Notwithstanding the implication of Eq.(
48
) we explore what modifications are brought to the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues if we solve the Schrödinger equation for an infinite square well potential using the generalised momentum operator
\widehat{p}_{H}
. We consider a particle of mass
m
in an infinite square well of width
L
. The potential
V(x)
is such that

\displaystyle V(x)=0~~~(0\leq x\leq L)

\displaystyle V(x)=\infty~~~(x<0~~\text{or}~~x>L)

Focusing on the case for which
b=0
, the eigenvalue equation for the ’generalised’ Hamiltonian can be given as

\displaystyle{\widehat{p}_{H}}^{2}~\psi(x)=2mE~\psi(x)

(49)

where
E
is the allowed energy eigenvalue. We take
E>0
for normalisable solutions. Defining
k_{0}=\cfrac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hslash}
(
k_{0}>0
), we can trivially factorise the problem as

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{p}_{H}\pm k_{0}\right)\left(\widehat{p}_{H}\mp k_{0}\right)\psi(x)=0

(50)

Thus, we have two independent solutions corresponding to

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{H}~\psi(x)=\pm k_{0}~\psi(x)

(51)

The two independent solutions are the two eigenfunctions of
\widehat{p}_{H}
,
\psi_{\pm k_{0}}(x)
corresponding to
k=\pm k_{0}
.
We can write the general solution as

\displaystyle\psi(x)={\cal P}~\psi_{+k_{0}}(x)+{\cal Q}~\psi_{-k_{0}}(x)

(52)

where
{\cal P}
and
{\cal Q}
are arbitrary constants to be determined by the boundary conditions of the problem,
i.e.

\psi(x=0)=0=\psi(x=L)
.
For
\psi(x=0)=0
we have

\displaystyle{\cal P}+{\cal Q}=0

(53)

Using Eq.(
53
) and the other boundary condition,
\psi(x=L)=0
gives,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a\sin(k_{0}L)}}~e^{i\theta_{1}(L)}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a\sin(k_{0}L)}}~e^{-i\theta_{2}(L)}=0

(54)

where

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(x)=\frac{k_{0}x}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan\left(\frac{k_{0}x}{2}\right)-a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\right),~{\rm and}

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(x)=\frac{k_{0}x}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan\left(\frac{k_{0}x}{2}\right)+a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\right)

(55)

From Eqs. (
54
) and (
55
) we get for real and imaginary parts,

\displaystyle\cos(\theta_{1}(L)+\theta_{2}(L))=\sqrt{\frac{1-a\sin(k_{0}L)}{1+a\sin(k_{0}L)}},~~~~~\sin(\theta_{1}(L)+\theta_{2}(L))=0

\displaystyle\implies\sin^{2}(\theta_{1}(L)+\theta_{2}(L))=1-\cos^{2}(\theta_{1}(L)+\theta_{2}(L))=1-\frac{1-a\sin(k_{0}L)}{1+a\sin(k_{0}L)}=0

(56)

which gives the condition

\displaystyle\sin(k_{0}L)=0~~{\rm or}~~k_{0}L=n\pi~~(n=1,~2,~3....)

(57)

Thus we recover the same eigenspectrum for the infinite square well as in the case of standard QM. Note that here also
n=0
is not allowed because for
n=0
the wavefunction
\psi(x)
vanishes identically.
Thus in terms of the quantum number
n
, the wavefunctions have the form

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x)={\cal P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-a\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}x\right)}}~e^{i\theta_{1}(x)}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}x\right)}}~e^{-i\theta_{2}(x)}\right)

(58)

In the limit of
a\to 0
, the wavefunction
\psi_{n}(x)
must match the standard result
i.e.

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x)\xrightarrow{a\to 0}={\cal P}\left(e^{i\frac{n\pi x}{L}}-e^{-i\frac{n\pi x}{L}}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}x\right)

(59)

which gives

\displaystyle 2i{\cal P}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}~~{\rm or}~~{\cal P}=-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2L}}

(60)

7
Summary and Conclusions

Starting from the Fourier Transform relationship between position and momentum space we have presented a generalisation of the momentum operator in position space along with that of position operator in momentum space. The generalised momentum eigenfunctions obtained depend on two real parameters
a
and
b
(
c
and
d
for position operator in momentum basis) and are found to match with the standard result in QM in the limit
a,b\to 0
(
c,d\to 0
for position operator in momentum space). The takeaway message is that the momentum operator in QM corresponds to a special point
(0,0)
within the allowed domain of generalisation,
i.e.
,
a^{2}+b^{2}<1
(
c^{2}+d^{2}<1
for position operator in momentum space),
i.e.
an open unit disk to which the entire continuous family of generalised operators belong.

We were also able to solve a simple test case of a particle in an infinite square well for the case
b=0

we concluded that within the allowed parameter space of generalised momentum and position operators, the only point which agrees with the requirement of basis independence of the expectation value of the fundamental commutator is the point which coincides with the usual QM. Thus, from a pedagogical perspective, the calculations done here can be seen as a way of arriving at the usual QM formulation starting from slightly more general considerations.

Mean-field behaviour of the random connection model on hyperbolic space
Matthew Dickson, Markus Heydenreich

Abstract
We study the random connection model on hyperbolic space ℍ d {\mathbb{H}^{d}} in dimension d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 . Vertices of the spatial random graph are given as a Poisson point process with intensity λ > 0 \lambda>0 . Upon variation of λ \lambda there is a percolation phase transition: there exists a critical value λ c > 0 \lambda_{c}>0 such that for λ < λ c \lambda<\lambda_{c} all clusters are finite, but infinite clusters exist for λ > λ c \lambda>\lambda_{c} . We identify certain critical exponents that characterise the clusters at (and near) λ c \lambda_{c} , and show that they agree with the mean-field values for percolation. We derive the exponents through isoperimetric properties of critical percolation clusters rather than via a calculation of the triangle diagram.

Introduction

Motivation.

We study the random connection model on a Borel space
\mathcal{S}
.
This is a spatial random graph model, where vertices are given by a locally finite point process
\eta
on
\mathcal{S}
. Edges are sampled randomly for any pair of points
u,v\in\eta
independently with probability
\varphi(u,v)
, where
\varphi\colon\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]
is some connectivity function.
The overarching question that we address is:
how is the geometry of
\mathcal{S}
linked to the connectivity properties of the random connection model?

We zoom in on the case where
\mathcal{S}
is hyperbolic and
\eta
is the Poisson point process with respect to the hyperbolic tensor measure. We found it useful to think in terms of the Poincaré disc model for
\mathcal{S}={\mathbb{H}^{2}}
or Poincaré ball model for
\mathcal{S}={\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, but this choice of model is not relevant for the results.

In our main result, we derive critical exponents that describe behaviour of the random connection model
at
the phase transition point.
We further derive criteria under which such phase transition exists.
As we shall work out, the value of these critical exponents is intimately related to isoperimetric properties of random sets in the hyperbolic space.
Interestingly, the same critical exponents show up in high-dimensional Euclidean space, but proof techniques clearly differ.

The model.

We adopt the Poincaré disc model for
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
resp. the Poincaré ball model for
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, both with constant curvature -1, but that specific value is of no importance for our result.
We write
o
for the origin, and let
\mathrm{dist}\left(x,y\right)
denote
hyperbolic
distance between points
x
and
y
. The hyperbolic area (resp. volume) measure
\mathrm{d}x
on the Poincaré disc model has metric tensor
\frac{4\|dy\|^{2}}{(1-\|y\|^{2})^{2}}
, where
y
is from the unit ball in
Euclidean coordinates
.
Given a hyperbolic-measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, let
\left\lvert E\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}x
denote the hyperbolic measure of that set.
A few relations for hyperbolic triangles are listed in Appendix
A
.

Every hyperplane (for
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
these are simply bi-infinite geodesics) separates the space in two half-spaces. Mind that, in contrast to Euclidean spaces, on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
we can have more than two disjoint half-spaces; see Figure
5
for an example.

Given
u,v\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma
that passes through
u
and
v
. There is then a unique isometry on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, which we call a
translation isometry
, that preserves orientation, maps
\gamma\mapsto\gamma
, and maps
u\mapsto v
. We denote this translation isometry map by
t_{u,v}\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. We assume that
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\to\left[0,1\right]
satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\varphi(y,x),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(t_{u,v}(x),t_{u,v}(y)\right),

(1.2)

for all
x,y,u,v\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
.
However, every even isometry on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
extends to a unique Möbius transformation, and hence invariance of
\varphi
under translation is equivalent to invariance under rotation or horolation (i.e. “rotation centred at a boundary point”), cf.
[
Mar12
, Exercise 29.13]
. In particular, this means that all the
\varphi
we consider are of the form

\varphi(x,y)=\varphi\left(\mathrm{dist}\left(x,y\right)\right).

The random connection model is a continuum percolation model. We let
\eta
be a homogeneous Poisson process on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
with intensity measure
\lambda\,\mathrm{d}x
, where
\lambda>0
is a model parameter. We identify
\eta
with its support.
For
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, we also consider the Palm versions
\eta^{x}=\eta\cup\{x\}
and
\eta^{x,y}=\eta\cup\{x,y\}
.

We consider a spatial random graph whose vertices is given by
\eta
(resp.
\eta^{x}
or
\eta^{x,y}
) and edges are inserted independently for each pair of vertices
u
and
v
with probability
\varphi(u,v)
.
Adopting notation from
[
HHLM22
]
, we denote by
\xi
(resp.
\xi^{x}
or
\xi^{x,y}
) the entire model that includes the underlying point process as well as the additional randomness required to form edges.
See Figure
1
.
For Poisson points
u,v\in\eta
we write
u\sim v\textrm{ in }\xi
whenever there is an edge between
u
and
v
, and further write
u\longleftrightarrow v
whenever
u=v
or
u
and
v
are (graph-)connected through a finite path of consecutive edges in
\xi
.
Our interest lies in the geometric properties of the connected components. These can best be studied under the Palm measure:

\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right):=\{x\in\eta^{o}:o\longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\}.

This is also called the
cluster
of
o
.
Given a countable set
A
, let
\#A
denote the number of elements of
A
. The
susceptibility
, or expected size of the cluster of
o
is

\chi\left(\lambda\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right].

(1.3)

A second quantity is the percolation probability, which is the probability that a designated point,
o
say, lies within an infinitely large cluster:

\theta\left(\lambda\right):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=\infty\right).

(1.4)

Figure 1
:
Simulations of the random connection model on the Poincaré disc model of
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
with
\varphi\left(x,y\right)=\exp\left(-2\mathrm{dist}\left(x,y\right)\right)
and different intensities.

While we generally make very few assumptions on
\varphi
, we do assume throughout that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\esssup_{x\not\in B_{R}\left(o\right)}\varphi(x,o)<1.

(1.5)

This avoids a number of technical issues in the proof.
A prominent example for the choice of
\varphi
is the indicator function
\varphi(u,v)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{dist}\left(u,v\right)\leq R}
for certain fixed
R>0
, this gives the
Poisson Boolean model
. It might be depicted by considering a (hyperbolic) ball of radius
R/2
around each vertex and connecting vertices by an edge whenever their respective balls overlap.

One consequence of the invariance assumption (
1.2
) is that
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x>0
is sufficient to guarantee a certain irreducibility: for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
and
\lambda>0
we have that the two-point function

\tau_{\lambda}(x,y):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\big(x\longleftrightarrow y\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,y}\big)

(1.6)

satisfies
\tau_{\lambda}(x,y)>0
. See Proposition
1.1
for related statements.

1.1
Results

We first address the question of a “non-trivial phase transition”: under which assumptions do we know that there exists a critical density threshold such that there is no percolation below, and there is percolation above such threshold? To this end, we introduce two critical values:

\displaystyle\lambda_{T}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\chi\left(\lambda\right)=\infty\right\},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{c}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\theta\left(\lambda\right)>0\right\}.

(1.8)

It is clear that
\lambda_{T}\leq\lambda_{c}
always, because
\theta\left(\lambda\right)>0
implies
\chi\left(\lambda\right)=\infty
.
Our first result is a non-triviality criterion for these critical values:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and consider a RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Then
\lambda_{T},\lambda_{c}<\infty
if and only if
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x>0
, and
\lambda_{T},\lambda_{c}>0
if and only if
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

The behaviour at (and near) the critical intensity
\lambda_{c}
(resp.
\lambda_{T}
) is best described through the notion of critical exponents. This goes back a long way, see e.g.
[
AN84
,
AB91
,
Ngu87
]
, and
[
Hug96
]
for physics references. In high-dimensional Euclidean space, the values of these critical exponents agree with the corresponding exponents for branching random walk resp. branching jump processes, cf.
[
HH17
, Section 1.2]
. Geodesic lines in hyperbolic space separate quickly, and we thus expect the same mean-field values for the critical exponents. The main critical exponents relevant for us are the following:

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{T}}\frac{\log\chi(\lambda)}{\log(\lambda_{T}-\lambda)},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\quad\lim_{\lambda\searrow\lambda_{c}}\frac{\log\theta(\lambda)}{\log(\lambda-\lambda_{c})},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{\log\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{c}}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\geq n\right)},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{T}}\frac{\log{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n+1}\right]}-\log{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]}}{\log(\lambda_{T}-\lambda)}.

(1.12)

The existence of these limits is not a priori clear, and part of the hypothesis (though one might use limit superior instead for definiteness). Also the fact that
\Delta
is independent of
n
in (
1.12
) is an implicit claim.
Our main result is that, for the RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
and
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, these critical exponents exist (in a fairly strong sense) and take on their mean-field values:

Theorem 1.2

(Critical exponents)
.

Let
d=2,3
and assume (
1.5
) as well as
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x\in(0,\infty)
. For the RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, there exist
0<C\leq C^{\prime}<\infty
and
\varepsilon>0
such that:

(a)

C\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\leq\chi(\lambda)\leq C^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}

(1.13)

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
. That is, the critical exponent
\gamma=1
.

(b)

C\left(\lambda-\lambda_{c}\right)_{+}\leq\theta(\lambda)\leq C^{\prime}\left(\lambda-\lambda_{c}\right)_{+}

(1.14)

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{c}+\varepsilon
. That is, the critical exponent
\beta=1
. Furthermore,
\lambda_{c}=\lambda_{T}
.

(c)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{c}}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\geq n\right)\leq C^{\prime}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.15)

That is, the critical exponent
\delta=2
.

(d)

For all
n\geq 1
, there exist
0<C_{n}\leq C^{\prime}_{n}<\infty
such that

C_{n}\left(\lambda_{c}-\lambda\right)^{-2}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n+1}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]}\leq C^{\prime}_{n}\left(\lambda_{c}-\lambda\right)^{-2}

(1.16)

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
. That is, the critical exponent
\Delta=2
.

The lower bounds on
\chi(\lambda)
and
\theta(\lambda)
hold in very great generality. The arguments of
[
CD24
]
– phrased on the product space of the Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a general mark space – make no use of the geometry of the ambient space and transfer across directly to our hyperbolic case. The remaining bounds are more subtle and do not hold in such generality, and here we use specific properties of hyperbolic space (in particular related to isoperimetric properties).

Interestingly, the same critical exponents have been obtained recently for the random connection model on high-dimensional
Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, in
[
HHLM22
]
, a lace expansion argument was developed to prove the so-called
triangle condition
, and
[
CD24
]
proved that the triangle condition implies critical exponents very much like in Theorem
1.2
.
In the present work, we use the geometric properties resulting from negative curvature of the ambient space to prove the critical exponents directly, rather than going via the triangle diagram.
Our proof is inspired by techniques on hyperbolic graphs by Madras and Wu
[
MW10
]
.
Similar to their result, our theorem is limited to dimensions
2
and
3
. The reason for this lies in Lemma
5.3
, where we provide a deterministic bound on the ratio of the number of surface vertices of convex hyperbolic polytopes to the hyperbolic volume of that region. This is not true
deterministically
for
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
with
d\geq 4
(see also Remark
5.4
). Even though we consider
d=2
to be the most interesting part of the paper, we see no reason to believe that the main result is limited to these dimensions, and possibly a probabilistic version of Lemma
5.3
could be obtained for
d\geq 4
.

An alternative proof approach is laid out in the work of Hutchcroft
[
Hut19
]
, who develops an improved version of Benjamini and Schramm’s ‘magic lemma’
[
BS01b
]
and uses it to show mean-field behaviour for bond percolation on quasi-transitive hyperbolic graphs. Similarly to our Lemma
5.3
, it is based on the observation that in hyperbolic geometry a typical point in a compact set is close to the boundary of its convex hull. However, a direct application of his Proposition 4.1 would require the graph to be highly regular – including having an upper bound on the degrees and a positive lower bound on the separation between vertices. One would thus need to get extra control on configurations with clustered vertices as well as dealing with possible long-range connections. Note that the probabilistic control required by this proof approach would be necessary for all
d\geq 2
, not just
d\geq 4
.

The derivation of cluster moment exponents as in (
1.16
)
on lattices
proceeds via the tree graph inequality
[
Ngu87
]
, and the constants such as
C^{\prime}_{n}
are thus related to tree counting. In contrast, for continuum models labelled vertices in the tree representation may appear as internal vertices as well, and we therefore have to count
Greg trees
instead. For details we refer to Section
4.4
.

1.2
Related work

We first summarise the results regarding
graphs
embedded into the hyperbolic plane. Lalley
[
Lal98
]
showed that for site percolation on the dual Dirichlet tiling graph of a co-compact Fuchsian group of genus
d\geq 2
, there is a parameter range for which there are infinitely many infinite clusters. This is a remarkable geometric fact, because on amenable spaces the Burton-Keane argument rules out such possibilities.
Such non-uniqueness phases have been established by Benjamini and Schramm
[
BS96
]
for nonamenable transitive graphs with one end, and by Pak and Smirnova-Nagniebeda
[
PSN00
]
for nonamenable Cayley-graphs. The conjecture that non-uniqueness implies mean-field critical exponents would follow from some conjectures and results in a series of papers by Hutchcroft
[
Hut20a
,
Hut20b
,
Hut22
]
.

In continuum models, Tykesson
[
Tyk07
]
proved that such a non-uniqueness phase exists for the Poisson Boolean model on
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
. Dickson
[
Dic25
]
extended this not only to general connectivity functions
\varphi
, but covers even inhomogeneous percolation models by admitting vertex weights. His main tool is the spherical transform.
However, both the results of
[
Tyk07
]
in dimension
d\geq 3
as well as in
[
Dic25
]
require sufficient stretched-out properties of the connectivity function
\varphi
.

Direct arguments towards mean-field criticality on hyperbolic graphs without the deviation of non-uniqueness have been established by Schonmann
[
Sch01
,
Sch02
]
.
An alternative approach has been developed by Madras and Wu
[
MW10
]
, and their approach has been inspirational for the present work. In fact, the geometric arguments underlying their approach is arguably coming out more clearly in the continuous setup persuaded in the present paper. On the other hand, the (possible) slow decay of the connectivity function
\varphi
requires involved additional separation arguments, that were not necessary in the graph case. See Section
4
.
Hutchcroft
[
Hut19
]
developed an alternative approach to mean-field criticality on connected, locally-finite, nonamenable, quasi-transitive Gromov-hyperbolic graphs via a ‘hyperbolic magical lemma’.

Finally, we remark that in a different line of research, Poisson-Voronoi percolation on
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
has been investigated
[
BS01a
,
HM22
]
. Here the focus has been on the asymptotic properties of the critical values, but the nature of the phase transition (in terms of critical exponents) has not been derived.

Connection with hyperbolic random graphs.

Hyperbolic random graphs are a class of spatial random graphs that exhibit many features that are typical for real world networks; this includes scale-free degree distributions, small-world distances and geometric clustering
[
KPK+10
]
. Given a model parameter
\nu
, we sample
n
points uniformly w.r.t. hyperbolic tensor measure on a hyperbolic disc of radius
R=2\log(n/\nu)
. That is, a given point’s distance from the origin has density
\frac{\sinh(r)}{\cosh(R)-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq R\}}\,dr
. See Section 9.5.2 of
[
Hof24
]
.
We then connect two points whenever their hyperbolic distance is at most
R
, i.e.

\varphi(x,y)=\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathrm{dist}\left(x,y\right)\leq R\}}.

There is a link between hyperbolic random graphs and a random graph model known as
scale-free percolation
on Euclidean space; cf.
[
KL20
]
and
[
Hof24
, Theorem 9.31]
. Scale-free percolation can be viewed as a weight-dependent random connection model on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a connection kernel of product form. More generally, hyperbolic random graphs are special instances of geometric inhomogeneous random graphs
[
BKL19
]
. We have recently worked out critical exponents for weight-dependent random connection models on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for sufficiently large
d

[
DH22
]
. However, the case of unbounded weights (as it appears for scale-free percolation) is not covered by the conditions in
[
DH22
]
.

Despite a seeming similarity, the results in this paper do not carry over to hyperbolic random graphs (and their related weight-dependent Euclidean models). The reason is that hyperbolic random graphs, as defined above, do not have a non-trivial limit within our framework. Indeed as the number of vertices
n
tends to infinity, the connection function
\varphi(x,y)=\mathbf{1}{\{\mathrm{dist}\left(x,y\right)\leq 2\log(n/\nu)\}}
goes pointwise to
1
and simultaneously the vertex density vanishes. Even if we rescale so that the intensity remains constant, the curvature then diverges in the limit.

While results for geometric inhomogeneous random graphs are typically obtained through a careful analysis of vertex weights, the methods in the present paper is genuinely geometric.

\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x=\infty
, then we can write
\varphi=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\varphi_{i}
where
\varphi_{i}
all satisfy our assumptions of an adjacency function and
\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi_{i}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x=1
for all
i\geq 1
. Fix
\lambda>0
and let the random variable
X_{m}
denote
\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)
for the RCM with intensity
\lambda
and connection function
\sum^{m}_{i=1}\varphi_{i}
, and let
Y_{m}
denote the number of neighbours of
o
in the same model. For finite
m
, the
Y_{m}
is a Poisson random variable with mean
\lambda\sum^{m}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi_{i}(x,o)\mathrm{d}x=\lambda m
(by Mecke’s formula;
[
LZ17
, (6.12)]
). Therefore for all
n\geq 0
we have
\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{m}=n\right)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Since
X_{m}\geq Y_{m}
for all configurations and
m
by monotonicity, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{m}=n\right)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Now let
X
denote
\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)
for the RCM with intensity
\lambda
and full connection function
\varphi
. Since
X\geq X_{m}
for all configurations and
m
, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(X=n\right)=0
for all
n\geq 1
. Therefore
\mathbb{P}\left(X=\infty\right)=1
and
\lambda\geq\lambda_{c}
. Since this argument only required
\lambda>0
, we have
\lambda_{c}=0
. Since
\lambda_{T}\leq\lambda_{c}
, we also have
\lambda_{T}=0
.

If
\int\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
, then we can use the “method of generations” and compare to a branching process to show
\lambda_{T}>0
. We first let
o
be the root of our cluster (generation
0
), and the vertices adjacent to
o
(generation
1
) are distributed according to a Poisson point process with intensity density
\lambda\varphi\left(\cdot,o\right)
with respect to the hyperbolic measure. For
k\geq 2
, we then iteratively define generation
k
to be those vertices adjacent to a vertex in generation
k-1
, but no vertex from an earlier generation. If we assign an arbitrary ordering to each generation, then we can also assign a genealogical tree to the vertices. Given a vertex from generation
k
, we can call the vertices in generation
k+1
immediate descendants of this vertex if they are adjacent to this vertex but from no earlier vertex in generation
k
. The immediate descendants of a vertex
v
are then distributed according to a thinned Poisson point process whose intensity density is bounded above by
\lambda\varphi\left(\cdot,v\right)
. We can therefore bound the two-point function, recall (
1.6
), by the kernel of a spatial branching process:

\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\leq\varphi\left(x,o\right)+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\lambda^{k}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\prod^{k+1}_{i=1}\varphi\left(v_{i},v_{i-1}\right)\mathrm{d}v_{\left[1:k\right]},

(2.3)

where
v_{0}=o
and
v_{k+1}=x
. From Tonelli’s theorem and the translation symmetry of
\varphi
, we then have

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\lambda^{k}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(v,o\right)\mathrm{d}v\right)^{k}.

(2.4)

Then Mecke’s formula implies that
\chi\left(\lambda\right)=1+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x
, and for
\lambda<\frac{1}{\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(v,o\right)\mathrm{d}v}
we have

\chi\left(\lambda\right)\leq\frac{1}{1-\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(v,o\right)\mathrm{d}v}.

(2.5)

Therefore for sufficiently small
\lambda
we have
\chi(\lambda)<\infty
and therefore
\lambda_{T}>0
(and
\lambda_{c}>0
). Note that this also proves that
\lambda_{T}=\infty
if
\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x=0
, and furthermore
\lambda_{c}=\infty
if
\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\varphi\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x=0
.

For the reverse direction, we adapt an argument of Penrose
[
Pen91
]
to the hyperbolic setting.
If
\int\varphi(o,x)\mathrm{d}x>0
, then there exist
a,b\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
and
\delta,\varepsilon>0
such that
a
,
b
, and
o
are not co-linear, the sets
B_{\delta}(a)
,
B_{\delta}(b)
, and
B_{\delta}(o)
are disjoint, and

\essinf_{x\in B_{\delta}(o),y\in B_{\delta}(a)\cup B_{\delta}(b)}\varphi(x,y)>\varepsilon.

(2.6)

Let
\theta
be the angle subtended at
o
by
a
and
b
. Then define

L\left(\theta\right):=\arcosh\left(\frac{1-\cos\theta\cos\frac{\theta}{2}}{\sin\theta\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}\right),

(2.7)

and
m:=\left\lceil\frac{L(\theta)}{\mathrm{dist}\left(o,a\right)}\right\rceil+1
and
n:=\left\lceil\frac{L(\theta)}{\mathrm{dist}\left(o,b\right)}\right\rceil+1
. Then we can define
A:=t_{o,a}^{m}(o)
as the
m
-fold application of
t_{o,a}
to
o
, and similarly
B:=t_{o,b}^{n}(o)
. Note that
\mathrm{dist}\left(o,A\right)>L(\theta)
and
\mathrm{dist}\left(o,B\right)>L(\theta)
. Since we have these bounds, Lemma
2.1
proves that
A
and
B
generate a binary tree
T=(V,E)
embedded in
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
for which the
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
-distances between vertices do not become arbitrarily small. We are then free to choose
\delta>0
small enough that
\left\{B_{\delta}(v)\right\}_{v\in V}
are disjoint.

By using Mecke’s formula, given
u\in B_{\delta}(o)
, the number of vertices in
B_{\delta}(a)
that are adjacent to
u
is a Poisson random variable with mean:

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\left\{y\in\eta\cap B_{\delta}(a)\colon y\sim u\right\}\right]=\lambda\int_{B_{\delta}(a)}\varphi\left(x,u\right)\mathrm{d}x\geq\lambda\varepsilon\left\lvert B_{\delta}(a)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}.

(2.8)

It then follows that the probability that there exists such a neighbour has the lower bound

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\exists v\in\eta\cap B_{\delta}(a)\colon v\sim u\right)\\
=1-\exp\left(-\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\left\{y\in\eta\cap B_{\delta}(a)\colon y\sim u\right\}\right]\right)\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}.

(2.9)

In particular, this bound is uniform in the vertex
u\in B_{\delta}(o)
. From the translation invariance and independence properties of the model, we can repeat this argument
m
times to show that the probability that there exists a path from
u\in B_{\delta}(o)
to some vertex in
B_{\delta}(A)
is bounded below by

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\exists v\in\eta\cap B_{\delta}(A)\colon v\longleftrightarrow u\textrm{ in }\xi^{u}\right)\geq\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\right)^{m}.

(2.10)

Similarly, we can find that

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\exists v\in\eta\cap B_{\delta}(B)\colon v\longleftrightarrow u\textrm{ in }\xi^{u}\right)\geq\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\right)^{n}.

(2.11)

If
\min\left\{\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\right)^{m},\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\lambda\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\right)^{m}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}
, then the cluster
\mathscr{C}(o,\xi^{o})
will have a positive probability of having a vertex in infinitely many of the sets
\left\{B_{\delta}(v)\right\}_{v\in V}
. That is, if
\lambda>\frac{-1}{4\pi\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\log\left(1-2^{-\frac{1}{\max\left\{m,n\right\}}}\right)
then
\theta\left(\lambda\right)>0
. Therefore

\lambda_{c}\leq\frac{-1}{4\pi\varepsilon\left(\sinh\delta\right)^{2}}\log\left(1-2^{-\frac{1}{\max\left\{m,n\right\}}}\right)<\infty.

(2.12)

In turn this also proves that
\lambda_{T}<\infty
.
∎

3
Mean-Field Bounds

The following mean-field bounds are expected to hold in great generality. In particular, the proofs make no use of the hyperbolic structure of the ambient space. They also apply for flat Euclidean spaces such as
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
HHLM22
,
CD24
]
). The arguments are adapted from the arguments for transitive Bernoulli bond percolation models in
[
AN84
,
AB87
]
.

3.1
Susceptibility Lower Bound

The proof of the mean field lower bound for the susceptibility can be found in
[
HHLM22
]
. The proof is worded for the RCM on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but the argument for the RCM on
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
proceeds identically.

The key points are in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The map
\lambda\mapsto\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda\right)}
is continuous on
\left[0,\infty\right)
, and for all
\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{T}\right)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\lambda\right)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\chi\left(\lambda\right)^{2}.

(3.1)

Proof.

See
[
HHLM22
, Lemma 2.3 and Proof of Theorem 1.3]
. The key ingredients are a Margulis-Russo formula and the BK inequality. The continuity result follows from showing that a sequence of truncated versions is equicontinuous.
∎

This can be used to prove the claim in Theorem
1.2
a
that there exists
C>0
such that

\chi\left(\lambda\right)\geq C\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}

(3.2)

for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
.

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
a
.

The continuity of
\lambda\mapsto\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda\right)}
implies that
\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda_{T}\right)}=0
, and therefore for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}

-\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda\right)}=\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda_{T}\right)}-\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda\right)}=\int^{\lambda_{T}}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\frac{1}{\chi(s)}\right)\mathrm{d}s=-\int^{\lambda_{T}}_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\chi(s)^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\leq-\int^{\lambda_{T}}_{\lambda}\frac{1}{s}\mathrm{d}s=-\log\frac{\lambda_{T}}{\lambda}.

(3.3)

Therefore
\chi\left(\lambda\right)\geq\frac{1}{\log\lambda_{T}-\log\lambda}
, and by taking a Taylor approximation of
\log\lambda
at
\lambda_{T}
there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\chi\left(\lambda\right)\geq C\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}

(3.4)

for
\lambda\in\left(\lambda_{T}-\varepsilon,\lambda_{T}\right)
as required.
∎

3.2
Percolation Lower Bound

The proof of the mean field lower bound for the percolation proceeds as in
[
CD24
]
. The proof there is given for the marked RCM on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but the argument proceeds identically for
{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, and reduces to our case if the set of marks is a singleton.

Definition 3.2
.

The following proposition provides bounds for the susceptibility and
magnetisation
functions. The magnetisation function will be important when we will use it to derive bounds on the percolation function. Given a new parameter
q\in\left[0,1\right]
, we label each vertex in
\eta
as a “ghost” vertex independently with probability
q
. Note that we take the convention that when we augment a configuration with additional vertices, these are also independently labelled as a ghost vertex with probability
q
. The set of ghost vertices is then denoted
\mathcal{G}
. Then the magnetisation function is defined by

M\left(\lambda,q\right):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right),

(3.5)

and the ‘ghost free’ susceptibility is defined by

\chi\left(\lambda,q\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\right\}}\right].

(3.6)

The following lemma provides some preliminary properties of
M\left(\lambda,q\right)
and
\chi\left(\lambda,q\right)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
\lambda\geq 0
, the function
q\mapsto M\left(\lambda,q\right)
is analytic on
\left(0,1\right)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\searrow 0}M\left(\lambda,q\right)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(\lambda\right),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left(1-q\right)\frac{\partial M}{\partial q}\left(\lambda,q\right)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\lambda,q\right),\qquad\forall q\in\left(0,1\right).

(3.8)

Furthermore, for all
q\in\left(0,1\right]
the function
\lambda\mapsto M\left(\lambda,q\right)
is analytic on
\left(0,\infty\right)
.

Proof.

These are proven in
[
CD24
, Lemmas 3.1, 3.4, 3.7]
.
∎

The following lemma introduces two differential inequalities that control
M\left(\lambda,q\right)
. These are used in the proofs of Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
below, and in Section
4.3
later.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q\in\left(0,1\right)
and
\lambda>0
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial M}{\partial\lambda}\left(\lambda,q\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-q}{\lambda}M\left(\lambda,q\right)\frac{\partial M}{\partial q}\left(\lambda,q\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle M\left(\lambda,q\right)

\displaystyle\leq q\frac{\partial M}{\partial q}\left(\lambda,q\right)+M\left(\lambda,q\right)^{2}+\lambda M\left(\lambda,q\right)\frac{\partial M}{\partial\lambda}\left(\lambda,q\right).

(3.10)

Proof.

See
[
CD24
, Lemma 3.6]
.
∎

Now define
\chi^{\mathrm{f}}
to be the susceptibility conditioned on the cluster of the origin being finite:

\chi^{\rm f}\left(\lambda\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mid\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right].

(3.11)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
q\in\left(0,1\right)
, and
\lambda^{*}>0
be such that
\chi^{\rm f}\left(\lambda^{*}\right)=\infty
. Then

M\left(\lambda^{*},q\right)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.12)

Proof.

See
[
CD24
, Corollary 3.8]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\lambda^{*}>0
satisfies
\theta(\lambda^{*})=0
and
\chi^{\rm f}\left(\lambda^{*}\right)=\infty
, then

\theta(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\lambda-\lambda^{*}\right)_{+}.

(3.13)

Proof.

See
[
CD24
, Proposition 4.3]
.
∎

These lemmas lead to a proof of the claim in Theorem
1.2
b
that there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\theta\left(\lambda\right)\geq C\left(\lambda-\lambda_{c}\right)_{+}

(3.14)

for
\lambda<\lambda_{c}+\varepsilon
.

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
b
.

We first aim to prove the claim that there exists
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\theta\left(\lambda\right)\geq C\left(\lambda-\lambda_{T}\right)_{+}

(3.15)

for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}+\varepsilon
. First note that by Theorem
1.2
a
we know that
\chi\left(\lambda_{T}\right)=\infty
. If
\theta\left(\lambda_{T}\right)=0
then this implies
\chi^{\mathrm{f}}\left(\lambda_{T}\right)=\chi\left(\lambda_{T}\right)=\infty
, and Lemma
3.6
proves the claim. On the other hand, if
\theta\left(\lambda_{T}\right)>0
then the claim immediately follows for some sufficiently small choice of
\varepsilon
.

Now note that if
\lambda>\lambda_{T}
we have proven that
\theta\left(\lambda\right)>0
and therefore
\lambda\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}
. Since we clearly have
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\geq\lambda_{T}
from their definitions, this proves
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=\lambda_{T}
and therefore that there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\theta\left(\lambda\right)\geq C\left(\lambda-\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right)_{+}

(3.16)

for
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}+\varepsilon
as required.
∎

While the bounds in Section

4.1
Clusters in half spaces

Definition 4.1
.

Given a set

, let
h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)
denote the
\varepsilon
-halo of
S
. That is,
h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)=\cup_{x\in S}B_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Also let
{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}
denote the convex hull of
S
. That is,
{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}
equals the intersection of all

S
.

Let
H\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
be a closed half-space with boundary hyperplane
\partial H\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, and let
x\in H
. For all
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)\right)

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,x\right)

and
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial M,x\right)<\varepsilon
:

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,x\right):=\bigcup_{M\colon x\in M\subset H,\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial M,x\right)<\varepsilon}M.

x_{\perp}\in\partial H

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,x\right)

x
and

\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,x\right)

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,x\right)

m_{\varepsilon}(H,x)
denote the point at which the geodesic and the boundary meet.

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all half-spaces
H\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
,
x\in H
, and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)\right)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,m_{\varepsilon}(H,x)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)}\sinh\varepsilon}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)}\sinh\varepsilon}\right),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial H,x\right)

\displaystyle=\arccos\left(\frac{\tanh\varepsilon}{\tanh\left(\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,x\right)-\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,m_{\varepsilon}(H,x)\right)\right)}\right).

(4.3)

Recall the
susceptibility
and
magnetisation
functions:

\chi\left(\lambda\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right],\qquad M\left(\lambda,q\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right).

(4.4)

Clearly, if we take the susceptibility or magnetisation and introduce the indicator function
\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H\right\}}
for some half-space
H
into the expectation or probability, then the original susceptibility (resp. magnetisation) is an upper bound for our new restricted susceptibility (resp. magnetisation). The following proposition shows that in fact the reverse inequalities are also true at the cost of only a
\lambda
-independent factor (in the sub-critical regime). It also shows corresponding statements for ‘ghost free’ susceptibility.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
d=2,3
and
H\ni o
be a half-space such that
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)>0
. Then there exists
\kappa=\kappa\left(d,\varphi,\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)\right)\in\left(0,\infty\right)
such that for all
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{c}\right)
and
q\in\left(0,1\right)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H\right\}}\right]

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H\right\}}\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H\right).

(4.7)

Remark 4.4
.

Even though the actual value of
\kappa
is of no importance to our argument, we observe that by carefully following our argument, one can find
K=K\left(d,\varphi\right)<\infty
such that

\kappa=\inf_{\varepsilon\in\left(0,\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)\right)}\frac{K}{\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(\pi+\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\times\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)}&\colon d=2\\
\frac{2}{1-\cos\left(\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right)}&\colon d=3.\end{cases}

(4.8)

As
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)\to 0
(and hence
\varepsilon\to 0
), we have
\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\to 0
. This is “clear” from Figure
3
, and Lemma
4.2
can in fact be used to show
\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)\right)
. This means that
\kappa\to\infty
as
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial H,o\right)\to 0
.

Proposition
4.3
allows us “at acceptable cost” to condition on clusters being located inside disjoint regions, and therefore be independent. This independence is the crucial ingredient in deriving mean-field exponents.

When investigating the susceptibility and the magnetisation, we shall break up the cluster through conditioning on a certain separation event
\mathcal{S}
, see (
4.44
). This breaks the cluster into two clusters living on half spaces. Proposition
4.3
is then employed to relate the half-space clusters to full space clusters.

4.2

The key bound for the proof of the upper bound on the susceptibility in Theorem

).

Lemma 4.5
.

For

there exist
\varepsilon,K>0

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\tilde{c}\,\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y,v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{1},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,o},\mathscr{C}\left(x,\xi^{x,v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{2},\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt\left.v_{n}\longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi^{x,v_{n}}\;\middle|\;\#\mathcal{Y}_{B}=0\right).

If

then

Now let us address the case where

or

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

For the sake of readability, let us abbreviate

\displaystyle E(y):=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y}\right)\subset H_{1},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,o}\right\},

(4.40)

\displaystyle E(y,v_{n}):=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y,v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{1},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,o}\right\},

(4.41)

\displaystyle F(v_{n},x):=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(x,\xi^{x,v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{2},v_{n}\longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi^{v_{n},x}\right\}.

(4.42)

Observe that as
n\to\infty
, we have
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y,v_{n})\cap F(v_{n},x)\right)\to\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap F(v_{n},x)\right)
monotonically since the probability that
v_{n}
is adjacent to any vertex in
H_{1}
tends to
0
monotonically. Therefore for sufficiently large
n
we have by Lemma
4.7
the existence of

and
c^{\prime}<\infty
such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x\geq c\int_{H_{1}}\int_{H_{2}}\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap F(v,x)\right)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-c^{\prime}

(4.43)

Our objective now is to condition on a separation event under which
E
and
F
are independent events, and therefore the probability and the integrals factorise.
To this end, we define the separation event

\mathcal{S}_{n}:=\left\{\xi\colon\forall a\in\eta\cap H_{1},\forall b\in\eta\cap H_{3}^{\mathrm{c}},a\not\sim b\right\}\cap\left\{\xi\colon\forall a\in\eta\cap H_{3},\forall b\in\eta\cap H_{2},a\not\sim b\right\}.

(4.44)

This is constructed so that the configurations on
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are independent when we condition on it.
By the law of total probability, it is immediate that

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap F(v,x)\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap F(v,x)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right),

(4.45)

and our definition of the conditioning event ensures that for
x\in H_{2}
,
v\in V_{n}\subset H_{2}
, and
y\in H_{1}
we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap F(v,x)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(F(v,x)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right).

(4.46)

We are now bounding
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)
and the two factors on the RHS of (
4.46
) in order to get that there exists
c^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for sufficiently large
n

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x\geq c^{\prime\prime}\int_{H_{1}}\int_{H_{2}}\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(F(v,x)\right)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-c^{\prime}\\
=c^{\prime\prime}\left(\int_{H_{1}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\right)\left(\int_{H_{2}}\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(F(v,x)\right)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x\right)-c^{\prime}.

(4.47)

To this end, we first find a lower bound for
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)
. Note that since
x,v\in H_{2}
and
o,y\in H_{1}
, we can decompose the event
\left\{\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}
into the non-disjoint events

\left\{\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}=\left\{x\not\sim y\right\}\cap\left\{v\not\sim y\right\}\cap\left\{x\not\sim o\right\}\cap\left\{v\not\sim y\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}.

(4.48)

The first four events are independent of the last two, and these last two are both decreasing events. Therefore the FKG inequality
[
HHLM22
, (2.19)]
produces

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\geq\left(1-\varphi(x,y)\right)\left(1-\varphi\left(v,y\right)\right)\left(1-\varphi(x,o)\right)\left(1-\varphi\left(v,o\right)\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right).

Recall that
\overline{\varphi}:=\lim_{r\to\infty}\esssup_{x\not\in\mathcal{B}_{r}\left(o\right)}\varphi\left(x,o\right)<1
, and
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\varphi(x,o)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. Therefore Mecke’s formula and dominated convergence implies that

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)=1
uniformly in
y\in H_{1}
. Hence for sufficiently large

we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\overline{\varphi}\right)^{4}>0

for all

and

.

To bound
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)
from below, we first adjust the conditioning event to finally decouple the

and
v
, and
y

) and the independence of the edges between
x
,
v
,
y
, and
o
from the rest of the graph,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\;\middle|\;\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap\left\{\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\not\sim y\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(v\not\sim y\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\not\sim o\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(v\not\sim y\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap\left\{\xi^{o,x,y,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(E(y)\cap\left\{x\not\sim y\right\}\cap\left\{v\not\sim y\right\}\cap\left\{x\not\sim o\right\}\cap\left\{v\not\sim y\right\}\;\middle|\;\left\{\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)

Note that as discussed above, FKG, Mecke and dominated convergence implies that

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right\}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{x,v}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\xi^{o,y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\right)\to 1

(4.52)

as

, uniformly for
x,v\in H_{2}
and

. Therefore

(4.53)

as
n\to\infty
, uniformly for
x,v\in H_{2}
and
y\in H_{1}
.
Using (
4.51
) with (

) gives

\left\{x\not\sim y\right\}

, and

, and

The same argument also gives

Combining (

In the final step we show how (

Since Mecke’s formula implies
\chi\left(\lambda\right)=1+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(x,o\right)\mathrm{d}x
, this inequality implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\chi\left(\lambda\right)-1\right)+\lambda C\chi\left(\lambda\right)^{2}-\lambda c^{\prime}.

(4.65)

Then the lower bound in Theorem

a
implies that
\lim_{\lambda\nearrow\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}}\chi\left(\lambda\right)=\infty
, and therefore there exist

such that for

\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\geq K\chi\left(\lambda\right)^{2}.

(4.66)

∎

This now completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
a

Lemma 4.8

Proof of the upper bound in Theorem

This follows exactly the argument of

Lemma

This theorem claims that for

Proof of Theorem

Begin by observing

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{c}}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o\right)\geq n\right)=\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\mathrm{e}-1}\sum_{l\geq n}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{e}\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda_{c}}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o\right)=l\right)\\

4.8

To prove the lower bound, let

\displaystyle M\left(\lambda_{c},q\right)

Lemma 4.9

\displaystyle\hskip 312.9803pt\mathrm{d}w_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}w_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{n}\ldots\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

(4.96)

By the same argument as in Lemma
4.7
, we can omit the stepping stone vertices from some of the events at the cost of a constant factors:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,\vec{v},\vec{w}},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o,y},\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y,\vec{v},\vec{w}}\right)\subset H_{0},\mathscr{C}\left(v_{n},\xi^{v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{1},\mathscr{C}\left(w_{m},\xi^{w_{m}}\right)\subset H_{2},\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt\left.v_{n}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{v_{n}},w_{m}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{w_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\geq c\,\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o,y},\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y}\right)\subset H_{0},\mathscr{C}\left(v_{n},\xi^{v_{n}}\right)\subset H_{1},\mathscr{C}\left(w_{m},\xi^{w_{m}}\right)\subset H_{2},\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt\left.v_{n}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{v_{n}},w_{m}\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{w_{m}}\right)-\mathds{1}_{\left\{y\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\geq c\,\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\cap F\left(v_{n}\right)\cap G\left(w_{m}\right)\right)-\mathds{1}_{\left\{y\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}\right\}},

(4.97)

where

\displaystyle E(y)

\displaystyle:=\left\{y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y}\right\}\cap\left\{y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,o}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y}\right)\subset H_{0}\right\},

(4.98)

\displaystyle F(v)

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(v,\xi^{v}\right)\subset H_{1}\right\},

(4.99)

\displaystyle G(w)

\displaystyle:=\left\{w\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{w}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(w,\xi^{w}\right)\subset H_{2}\right\}.

(4.100)

We therefore have that there exists
c,c^{\prime}>0
such that for
m
and
n
sufficiently large and
\lambda\in\left[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(F\right)\geq c\int_{H_{0}}\int_{V_{n}}\int_{W_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\cap F\left(v\right)\cap G\left(w\right)\right)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}y-c^{\prime}.

(4.101)

We next proceed as before and factorise the three events appearing in (
4.101
) after conditioning on a suitable separation event.
Repeating the argument of Lemma
4.5
, the events in the probability become asymptotically independent as
m,n\to\infty
. Therefore there exists
c^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for
m
and
n
sufficiently large and
\lambda\in\left[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right)
,

\displaystyle\int_{H_{0}}\int_{V_{n}}\int_{W_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\cap F\left(v\right)\cap G\left(w\right)\right)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\geq c^{\prime\prime}\int_{H_{0}}\int_{V_{n}}\int_{W_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(F\left(v\right)\right)\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(G\left(w\right)\right)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=c^{\prime\prime}\int_{H_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\right)\mathrm{d}y\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(F\left(v\right)\right)\mathrm{d}v\int_{W_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(G\left(w\right)\right)\mathrm{d}w.

(4.102)

We now need to lower bound the three integrals on the RHS of (
4.102
), and do this one by one.
By Mecke’s formula,

\displaystyle\int_{H_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(E\left(y\right)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{H_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{y},y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{y,o},\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi^{y}\right)\subset H_{0}\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\left\{y\in\eta\colon y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi,y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi\right)\subset H_{0}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\left\{y\in\eta\colon y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longleftrightarrow}}}\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi,y\longleftrightarrow o\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(y,\xi\right)\subset H_{0}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H_{0}\right\}}\right]-1\right).

(4.103)

To manage the
v
and
w
integrals, we transform the half spaces
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
for each value of
v\in V_{n}
and
w\in W_{m}
. Like at the end of the proof of Lemma
4.5
, for all
v\in V_{n}
, there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma^{\prime}
that contains
v
and is perpendicular to
\partial H_{1}
(we take the ‘positive’ direction on
\gamma^{\prime}
to be the direction towards
\partial H_{1}
from
o
). Let
\iota
be an isometry that maps
v\mapsto o
and
\gamma^{\prime}\mapsto\hat{\gamma}_{0}
(matching the ‘positive’ directions). If we define
H^{\prime}:=\iota\left(H_{1}\right)
, then we find

H^{\prime}=H^{-}_{\mathrm{dist}\left(v,\partial H_{1}\right)}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{0}\right).

(4.104)

Since
\inf_{v\in V_{n}}\mathrm{dist}\left(v,\partial H_{1}\right)\geq\delta
,

\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(F\left(v\right)\right)\mathrm{d}v=\int_{V_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}v\\
\geq\left\lvert B_{\delta}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H^{-}_{\delta}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{0}\right)\right).

(4.105)

Similarly,

\int_{W_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(G\left(w\right)\right)\mathrm{d}w\geq\left\lvert B_{\delta}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H^{-}_{\delta}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{0}\right)\right).

(4.106)

Together, the three bounds above yield a lower bound for (
4.102
). Together with (
4.101
) we thus get the following lower bound:
there exists
c^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
such that for
\lambda\in\left[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\right)
,

M\left(\lambda,q\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(F\right)\geq c^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,q}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}=\emptyset\right\}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{0}\right)\right\}}\right]\\
\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,q}\left(o\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{G}\textrm{ in }\xi^{o},\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset H^{-}_{\delta}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{0}\right)\right)^{2}-c^{\prime}.

(4.107)

In the final step of the proof, we employ Proposition
4.3
to replace the terms on the right hand side with
M\left(\lambda,q\right)
and
\chi\left(\lambda,q\right)
at the cost of a constant multiplicative factor. This means that there exist
\varepsilon,K>0
such that

M\left(\lambda,q\right)\geq KM\left(\lambda,q\right)^{2}\chi\left(\lambda,q\right)

(4.108)

for all
\lambda\in\left(\lambda_{c}-\varepsilon,\lambda_{c}\right)
and
q\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. To extend this to
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{c}\right)
, observe that
M\left(\lambda,q\right)\in\left[0,1\right]
and therefore

M\left(\lambda,q\right)\leq M\left(\lambda,q\right)^{2}.

(4.109)

For
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{c}-\varepsilon\right]
we have

\chi\left(\lambda,q\right)\leq\chi\left(\lambda\right)\leq\chi\left(\lambda_{c}-\varepsilon\right)<\infty,

(4.110)

and therefore

M\left(\lambda,q\right)\geq\frac{1}{\chi\left(\lambda_{c}-\varepsilon\right)}M\left(\lambda,q\right)^{2}\chi\left(\lambda,q\right).

(4.111)

Therefore for all
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda_{c}\right)
and
q\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)

M\left(\lambda,q\right)\geq cM\left(\lambda,q\right)^{2}\chi\left(\lambda,q\right)

(4.112)

with
c=\min\left\{K,\chi\left(\lambda_{c}-\varepsilon\right)^{-1}\right\}>0
.
∎

This now completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
b
and
1.2
c
.

4.4
Cluster Moment Exponent

The derivation of the cluster moment exponent here closely follows the analogous argument in
[
Ngu87
]
, with versions of lemmas from
[
AN84
]
and
[
DN85
]
.

We first bound the
n
-point function using tree diagrams. Given
n\geq 2
,
\lambda>0
, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
, define the
n
-point function to be

\tau_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x_{1}\longleftrightarrow x_{2}\longleftrightarrow\ldots\longleftrightarrow x_{n}\text{ in }\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}\right).

(4.113)

To bound
\tau_{n,\lambda}
, we will make use of Greg trees. A
Greg tree with
n
labelled vertices
is defined to be a tree with two colours of vertices: there are
n
‘blue’ labelled vertices with degrees
\geq 1
, and
m
‘red’ unlabelled vertices with degree
\geq 3
. These can be constructed iteratively in the manner described in
[
Fli90
]
. The only Greg tree with
1
labelled vertex is one blue vertex by itself. Given the set of Greg trees with
n
labelled vertices, the set of Greg trees with
n+1
labelled vertices can be constructed by taking each Greg tree with
n
labelled vertices and doing one of the following with the extra labelled (blue) vertex:

1.

attach the new blue vertex to any pre-existing vertex (blue or red),

2.

insert the new blue vertex into the middle of an edge,

3.

insert a red vertex into the middle of an edge and attach the new blue vertex to that red vertex,

4.

replace a pre-existing red vertex with the new blue vertex.

1

Lemma 4.10
.

For all
n\geq 2
,
\lambda>0
, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
,

\tau_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\leq T_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right).

(4.120)

Proof.

Suppose that the vertices
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are in the same cluster in
\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}
. Then there exists a spanning tree of this cluster in
\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}
that, of course, contains
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}
. Let us prune away the branches of the tree beyond any of the vertices
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}
- they will be irrelevant for our considerations. For the remaining tree, let
\left\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right\}
denote the vertices that have degree
\geq 3
that are not one of the initial
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}
. The remaining vertices of our tree will then lie on one of the disjoint paths between elements of
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right\}
. Contracting these paths to edges will then produce a tree graphs that details how
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right\}
can be connected by disjoint paths. If
\left\{E_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
is a finite set of events, let us denote

\underset{i\in I}{\bigcirc}E_{i}:=E_{i_{1}}\circ\left(E_{i_{2}}\circ\ldots\circ\left(E_{i_{n-1}}\circ E_{i_{n}}\right)\ldots\right),

(4.121)

where
n=\#I
, and
E\circ F
denotes the vertex disjoint occurrence of the events
E
and
F
(an associative binary operation on increasing events) – see
[
HHLM22
, Section 2.3]
for details. Then by a union bound and Mecke’s formula,

\displaystyle\tau_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\exists g\in G_{n},\left\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right\}\subset\eta\colon V(g)=\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\left.\text{ and }\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in G_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\sum_{\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r(g)}\right)\in\eta^{\left(r(g)\right)}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}\right\}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in G_{n}}\lambda^{r(g)}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{r(g)}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r(g)}}\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{r(g)}

(4.122)

Now because we are taking the disjoint occurrence of connection events inside the probability, all the augmenting vertices other than
z
and
z^{\prime}
can be omitted from the configuration
\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r(g)}}
- they are being used on other paths. That is,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r(g)}}\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{z,z^{\prime}}\right\}\right).

(4.123)

Then by the BK inequality
[
HHLM22
, Thm. 2.1]
,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\underset{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}{\bigcirc}\left\{z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{z,z^{\prime}}\right\}\right)\leq\prod_{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{z,z^{\prime}}\right)

(4.124)

This then produces

\tau_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\leq\sum_{g\in G_{n}}\lambda^{r(g)}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{r(g)}}\prod_{\left\{z,z^{\prime}\right\}\in E(g)}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(z\longleftrightarrow z^{\prime}\textrm{ in }\xi^{z,z^{\prime}}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{r(g)}\\
=T_{n,\lambda}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)

(4.125)

as required.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For all
n\geq 1
, there exists
C_{n}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\leq C_{n}\chi\left(\lambda\right)^{2n-1}

(4.126)

for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

By a binomial expansion and Mecke’s formula,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left(1+\sum_{x\in\eta}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\longleftrightarrow x\textrm{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\sum_{\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)\in\eta^{(k)}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\longleftrightarrow x_{1}\longleftrightarrow\ldots\longleftrightarrow x_{k}\text{ in }\xi^{o}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}\binom{n}{k}\lambda^{k}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\tau_{k+1,\lambda}\left(o,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}.

(4.127)

From Lemma
4.10
we therefore have

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\leq 1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}\binom{n}{k}\lambda^{k}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}T_{k+1,\lambda}\left(o,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}.

(4.128)

By translation invariance and ‘peeling off’ leaves from the Greg trees
g\in G_{k+1}
in definition of
T_{k+1,\lambda}
, we can arrive at

\lambda^{k}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}T_{k+1,\lambda}\left(o,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}=\sum_{g\in G_{k+1}}\lambda^{k+r(g)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(o,z\right)\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\#E(g)}\\
=\sum_{g\in G_{k+1}}\lambda^{\#E(g)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(o,z\right)\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\#E(g)},

(4.129)

where we have used the fact that the Greg trees we are summing over have
k+1+r(g)
vertices in total and (since they are trees) will have
\#E(g)=k+r(g)
. Mecke’s formula then leads to

\sum_{g\in G_{k+1}}\lambda^{\#E(g)}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\tau_{\lambda}\left(o,z\right)\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\#E(g)}=\sum_{g\in G_{k+1}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]-1\right)^{\#E(g)}\\
\leq\sum_{g\in G_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]^{\#E(g)}.

(4.130)

Recall that from the iterative construction of Greg trees that for any
k\geq 1
and
g\in G_{k+1}
, we have
\#E(g)\leq 2k-1
. Since
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]\geq 1
, we therefore have

\lambda^{k}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}T_{k+1,\lambda}\left(o,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}\leq N_{k+1}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]^{2k-1}.

(4.131)

Combining this inequality with (
4.127
) and (
4.125
) then gives

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\leq 1+\sum^{n}_{k=1}\binom{n}{k}N_{k+1}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]^{2k-1}\leq\left(n+1\right)!N_{n+1}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]^{2n-1},

(4.132)

as required.
∎

This will be sufficient for the upper bound on
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]
. We need to prepare one more lemma to get our lower bound.

Lemma 4.12
.

For all
\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{T}\right)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{2}\right]\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\chi\left(\lambda\right)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)^{2}.

(4.133)

Proof.

In
[
CD24
]
the following expression was derived: for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\lambda>0

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=k+1\right)=\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k},

(4.134)

where
\vec{x}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)
,

\displaystyle f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k+1}}\left(\prod_{\left\{i,j\right\}\in E\left(G\right)}\varphi\left(x_{i},x_{j}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{\left\{i,j\right\}\not\in E\left(G\right)}\left(1-\varphi\left(x_{i},x_{j}\right)\right)\right),

(4.135)

\displaystyle g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}\left(1-\prod_{m\in\left\{0,\ldots,k\right\}}\left(1-\varphi\left(y,x_{m}\right)\right)\right)\mathrm{d}y,

(4.136)

\mathrm{Gr}_{k+1}
is the set of simple connected graphs on
\left\{0,\ldots,k\right\}
,
E\left(G\right)
is the set of edges in the graph
G
, and
x_{0}=o
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\;\middle|\;\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(k+1\right)\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}

(4.137)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\;\middle|\;\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(k+1\right)\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}.

(4.138)

We are able to exchange differentiation with the summation and integration for the same reasons as in
[
CD24
, Lemma 3.5]
in which they considered the analogous argument for the magnetisation. We omit the details here. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we then get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\;\middle|\;\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(k+1\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)^{2}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{2}\;\middle|\;\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)^{2}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}\right).

(4.139)

To manipulate this second factor, observe that

\theta\left(\lambda\right)=1-\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k},

(4.140)

and that for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
(so
\theta\left(\lambda\right)=0
)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k},

(4.141)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda^{2}}\right)f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)^{2}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}.

(4.142)

That is, for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}

\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{k}}\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}-g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)^{2}f_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda g_{k}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}\\
=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=k+1\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\;\middle|\;\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right]-1\right).

(4.143)

Since
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
implies
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)<\infty\right)=1
, we now have

\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\chi}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\chi\left(\lambda\right)\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{2}\right].

(4.144)

Rearranging this then recovers the result.

Proposition 4.13
.

For each
n\geq 1
, there exist
0<C_{n}\leq C_{n}^{\prime}<\infty
such that

C_{n}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{1-2n}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\leq C_{n}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{1-2n}

(4.145)

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
.

Proof.

The upper bound directly follows from Lemma
4.11
and the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
a
: for all
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
and
n\geq 1

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\leq C_{n}\left(C^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\right)^{2n-1}.

(4.146)

For the lower bound, first observe that by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n-1}\right]\right)^{2}=\left(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\right]\right)^{2}\\
\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n-2}\right]

(4.147)

for all
n\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
(recall
\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\geq 1
everywhere for all
\lambda\geq 0
). Therefore by induction

\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\Big[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n{}}\Big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n-1}\right]}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n-1}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n-2}\right]}\geq\ldots\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{2}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\Big[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\Big]}=:S\left(\lambda\right),

(4.148)

and

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\geq\chi\left(\lambda\right)S\left(\lambda\right)^{n-1}

(4.149)

for all
n\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
.

From Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.12
, we know there exists
K^{\prime}<\infty
such that for
\lambda<\lambda_{T}

S\left(\lambda\right)\geq K^{\prime}\chi\left(\lambda\right)^{2}.

(4.150)

Therefore the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
a
and (
4.149
) imply that for each
n\geq 1
there exists
C_{n}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)^{n}\right]\geq C_{n}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\left(\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{-2}\right)^{n-1}=C_{n}\left(\lambda_{T}-\lambda\right)^{1-2n}

(4.151)

for all
\lambda<\lambda_{T}
.
∎

Remark 4.14

Observe that both the upper and lower bounds in Proposition

made use of the hyperbolic space. For the upper bound this came via the upper bound in Theorem

a
, and for the lower bound this came via the lower bound on the derivative of

in Lemma
4.5

5
Clusters in Half-spaces: Proofs

Here we prove Proposition
4.3
thereby finishing the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
As a first step in the proof, we bound the expected number of vertices in the cluster by the expected hyperbolic area of its
\varepsilon
-halo.
This step in the proof is based on an analogous argument for the Boolean disc model on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
[
Roy90
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
d=2,3
. For all
\varepsilon,\lambda^{*}>0
, there exists a constant
0<K_{1}(\varepsilon,\lambda^{*})<\infty
such that for all
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda^{*}\right]
,

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]\leq K_{1}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right]

(5.1)

Proof.

We first state the proof for
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
, and then explain the necessary adaptations for
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
.

Partition
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
by the regular triangular lattice with seven equilateral triangles meeting at a vertex, where we choose to have
o
contained in the interior of one of the triangular cells. This lattice is denoted by the Schläfli symbol
\left\{3,7\right\}
(see Figure
6
). Let
C
denote a cell in this lattice, and note that
\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{7}
(from Lemma
A.5
) and the side length of this cell
L=2\arcosh\big(\frac{1}{2\sin\frac{\pi}{7}}\big)
(by the second cosine rule - see Lemma
A.4
). Recall that
\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}
denotes the vertex Poisson point process outside the cell
C
, and
\mathscr{C}\big(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\big)
denotes the cluster of
o
that is formed without using any vertices in
C
(except perhaps
o
).

For cells
C
not containing
o
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap C\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\eta_{C}=k\text{ and }\exists y\in\eta_{C}\colon y\sim\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=e^{-\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{k!}\lambda^{k}\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{k}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\int_{C}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\sim\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq e^{-\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{k!}\lambda^{k}\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{k}\times\frac{k}{\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\int_{C}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\sim\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\left(\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+1\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\int_{C}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\sim\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right).

(5.2)

If a cell
C
contains a vertex we want to find the minimal area this vertex can contribute to the
\varepsilon
-halo:
v_{\varepsilon}:=\inf_{x\in C}\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
. If
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small then
v_{\varepsilon}
is found by placing
x
in a corner of the triangle, in which case the intersection is
\frac{1}{7}
of the
\varepsilon
-ball (
v_{\varepsilon}=\frac{4\pi}{7}\left(\sinh\varepsilon\right)^{2}
). This fails once
\varepsilon
is big enough to reach outside the far side of the triangle. By the hyperbolic sine rule, this distance
l
between a vertex and the far side satisfies
\sinh l=\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{7}\right)\sinh L
. In such a case, we simply truncate the
\varepsilon
-ball to the
l
-ball. Therefore

v_{\varepsilon}=\frac{4\pi}{7}\left(\min\left\{\sinh\varepsilon,\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{7}\right)\sinh L\right\}\right)^{2}.

(5.3)

By restricting to the event that there is exactly one vertex in
C
, we therefore have

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)\cap C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right]\geq v_{\varepsilon}\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\int_{C}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(x\sim\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

(5.4)

Therefore for
C
not containing
o
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap C\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right]\leq\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}}\left(\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+1\right)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)\cap C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right].

(5.5)

If
C
is the cell containing
o
, then we use the trivial bound

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\cap C\right)\mid\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\eta^{0}_{C}\right]=1+\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}.

(5.6)

By taking the expectations over
\eta_{{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\setminus C}
and then summing over
C
, we arrive at

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right]\leq 1+\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}}\left(\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+1\right)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\left\lvert C\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right].

(5.7)

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right]\geq 4\pi\left(\sinh\varepsilon\right)^{2}
, this proves the result.

For
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, we simply change the cells that form the lattice. It is shown in
[
BKS24
]
that there is no regular hyperbolic tetrahedral space-filler for
{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, but the regular hyperbolic cubic space-filler serves our purposes instead.
∎

Given a set
S\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
with only finitely many elements, we define
\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}:=\big\{x\in S:B_{\varepsilon}(x)\not\subset{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\big\}
to be the elements of

\varepsilon

{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}

\partial{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}:=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
d=2,3
. For any finite
S\subset{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
,

\left\lvert{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\leq\pi\left(\#\partial{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}-2\right)

(5.8)

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\pi<K_{\varepsilon}<\infty
such that for all finite
S\subset{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
,

\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\leq K_{\varepsilon}\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}.

(5.9)

Proof.

We first prove (
5.8
).
For
d=2
, since
S
is finite,
{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}
is a bounded convex polygon. Any bounded convex polygon with
n
vertices can be viewed as the union of
n-2
triangles. A hyperbolic triangle can have at most a hyperbolic area of
\pi
, and this produces the first bound.

For
d=3
, Madras and Wu
[
MW10
, Lemma 8]
show that

\left\lvert{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}\leq V_{3}\left(2\#\partial{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}-4\right),

(5.10)

where
V_{3}
is the (hyperbolic) volume of a regular tetrahedra, whose volume is bounded above by
\pi/2
, cf.
[
HM81
, Prop. 2]
. We thus arrive at the same upper bound.

For the second bound, we first bound

\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)\cap{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\leq\left\lvert{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}},

(5.11)

and account for the elements of
S
whose
\varepsilon
-balls are not contained in
{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}
by bounding

\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)\setminus{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\leq\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}.

(5.12)

Combining these bounds and using (
5.8
) gives

\left\lvert h_{\varepsilon}\left(S\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\leq\left\lvert{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}+\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\leq\left(\pi+\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}.

(5.13)

Note that we have used
\partial{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}\subset\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(S\right)}
here. The result then follows with
K_{\varepsilon}=\pi+\left\lvert B_{\varepsilon}\left(o\right)\right\rvert_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}
.
∎

Remark 5.4

Finding an appropriate replacement for (

Lemma 5.5

For all

Lemma 5.6

, half-spaces

Proof of Proposition

The bound for the susceptibility follows from Lemmas

Proof of Lemma

.

In the following, given a finite set

.
First we expand the left hand side of (

), conditioning on the size of the cluster:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right)\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}\mid\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right]

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=\infty\right)=0

We now similarly expand the right hand side:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right)\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}\right\}}\mid\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right]

\displaystyle n\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\right)}\right\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{d}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{x_{i}\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\right)}\right\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

(5.24)

and therefore the right hand side and the left hand side are equal.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

The idea is that for each cluster configuration with
o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}
there are rotations about
o
such that
\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
, and each of these rotated configurations has the same probability density. Since
o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}
, there exists a half-space
M
such that
\mathrm{dist}\left(\partial M,o\right)\leq\varepsilon
and
\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset M
. By associating rotations,
r
, with elements of the sphere
S_{d-1}
, the uniform probability measure of rotations such that
r(M)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
is given by

\frac{\mathfrak{S}_{d-2}}{\mathfrak{S}_{d-1}}\int^{\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)}_{0}\left(\sin t\right)^{d-2}\mathrm{d}t.

(5.25)

Since
\mathfrak{S}_{0}=2
,
\mathfrak{S}_{1}=2\pi
, and
\mathfrak{S}_{2}=4\pi
, for
d=2,3
this takes the form

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi}\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)&\colon d=2\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\cos\left(\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right)\right)&\colon d=3.\end{cases}

(5.26)

Imprecisely speaking, for each configuration
o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}
, there are at least (
5.26
)-many “equally likely” configurations such that
\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
. This would then imply the result. The following argument gives the details for the
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
case, higher dimensions are analogous.

Recall from the proof of Lemma
5.5
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\right)}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\varphi(y,o)\mathrm{d}y}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right)\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

(5.27)

Here we use the Poincaré disc radial coordinates for
{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
, so for
x\in{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
,
\rho\in\left[0,1\right)
, and
\theta\in\left[0,2\pi\right)
,

\mathrm{d}x=\frac{4\rho}{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}\theta.

(5.28)

Then for
n\geq 2
we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\right)}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\vec{\theta}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)^{n-1}}\int_{\vec{\rho}\in\left[0,1\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\prod^{n-1}_{i=1}\frac{4\rho_{i}}{2\pi\left(1-\rho_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{i}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\vec{\theta}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)^{n-2}}\int_{\vec{\rho}\in\left[0,1\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\frac{4\rho_{1}}{\left(1-\rho_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\prod^{n-1}_{i=2}\frac{4\rho_{i}}{2\pi\left(1-\rho_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{i}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i}.

(5.29)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\varphi(y,o)\mathrm{d}y}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\#\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)=n\right)\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathscr{C}\left(o,\xi^{o}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

(5.30)

Then for
n\geq 2
we can once again use the radial coordinates of the Poincaré disc model to write

\displaystyle\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\vec{\theta}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)^{n-1}}\int_{\vec{\rho}\in\left[0,1\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\prod^{n-1}_{i=1}\frac{4\rho_{i}}{2\pi\left(1-\rho_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{i}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\geq\int_{\theta_{1}\in I_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)}\int_{\vec{\theta}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)^{n-2}}\int_{\vec{\rho}\in\left[0,1\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\frac{4\rho_{1}}{\left(1-\rho_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\prod^{n-1}_{i=2}\frac{4\rho_{i}}{2\pi\left(1-\rho_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{i}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i},

(5.31)

where
I_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
is a measurable subset of
\left[0,2\pi\right)
. Here we have used that if
o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{conv}(\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\\
\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\})
, then there are rotations
\theta^{*}
of the vertex set such that
\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}+\theta^{*}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}+\theta^{*}\right)\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
.
I_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
denotes the angles of these rotations, and will be either an interval or a union of two intervals (and hence be measurable). Furthermore
\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},\theta_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\right)}\right\}
is rotation invariant, and
\left\lvert I_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)\right\rvert=2\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)
. We therefore have

\displaystyle\int_{\left({\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\left(\gamma,o\right)\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\geq\frac{\theta_{\varepsilon}\left(H,o\right)}{\pi}\int_{\vec{\theta}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)^{n-2}}\int_{\vec{\rho}\in\left[0,1\right)^{n-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{o\in\partial_{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\right)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}\left(\left\{o,\left(\rho_{1},0\right),\ldots,\left(\rho_{n-1},\theta_{n-1}\right)\right\}\text{ is a cluster}\right)\frac{4\rho_{1}}{\left(1-\rho_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{1}\prod^{n-1}_{i=2}\frac{4\rho_{i}}{2\pi\left(1-\rho_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\rho_{i}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i},

(5.32)

and the result follows.
∎

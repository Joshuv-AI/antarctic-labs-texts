Abstract
. We take a fresh look at the relationship between K K -regularity and regularity of schemes, proving two results in this direction. First, we show that K 2 K_{2} -regular affine algebras over fields of characteristic zero are normal. Second, we improve on Vorst’s K K -regularity bound in the case of local complete intersections; this is related to recent work on higher du Bois singularities.

Lemma 1.3
.

Suppose the conductor
\mathfrak{c}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. If

-regular, then so is

Proof.

By hypothesis,

are Artinian;

for

It now follows from (

Proposition 1.8
.

Suppose that
\left(A,\mathfrak{m}\right)
is a noetherian local ring,

-regular. By Corollary

, and

.

of

,

Since

of local rings each with residue field

. Write

Now assume for a contradiction that

. Choose elements

such that

\pi_{i}(g)=0

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
A
be a noetherian commutative ring containing a field
and with finite normalization.
Assume either that
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq A
,
or that
\mathrm{dim}(A)=2
and

is perfect
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
. If

A
is
K_{2}
-regular, then
A
is normal.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq A
.
We proceed by noetherian induction. Since
A
is
K_{2}
-regular, it is

K_{1}
-regular and hence reduced. It follows that the localization of

A
at any minimal prime is a field, and hence normal.
Now let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime
ideal such that
A_{\mathfrak{q}}
is normal for all primes

\mathfrak{q}
properly contained in
\mathfrak{p}
. Then the local
ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
K_{2}
-regular by Vorst
[
26
, 1.9]
,
and satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
1.8
.
We conclude that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is normal.
It follows by induction that all local rings of
A
are normal,
and hence that
A
is normal.

Now suppose that
\mathrm{dim}(A)=2
. By
[
26
]
,
A
is
regular in codimension
1
. It follows that the conductor of the

\mathfrak{m}
-primary. If, moreover, the residue

to finish the proof.
∎

2.
Higher du Bois singularities,
K
-theory and regularity.

In this section we point out that the proof of Vorst’s conjecture for
an affine scheme
X
of finite type over a field of characteristic
zero contained in
[
1
]
does not actually require the full
strength of the
K
-regularity hypothesis; instead, it suffices to
assume only an algebraic-geometric consequence of this hypothesis,
namely, that
X
have
higher du Bois singularities
.

We begin by briefly recalling (in a form convenient for our
purposes) the definition of the du Bois complexes,
\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}
,
and the notion of du Bois and higher du Bois singularities.
Du Bois complexes were introduced in
[
9
]
to study
mixed Hodge structures on possibly singular
quasiprojective complex varieties, and
du Bois singularities were introduced by Steenbrink in

[
24
]
as a weakening of the notion of rational singularities better behaved in families.

Throughout this section,
k
will be a field of characteristic zero,
and
\mathrm{Sch}/k
will be the category of separated schemes
essentially of finite type over
k
. Following
[
3
]
, we
write
a:\left(\mathrm{Sch}/k\right)_{\mathrm{cdh}}\to\left(\mathrm{Sch}/k\right)_{\mathrm{zar}}

for the morphism of sites given by comparing the
cdh
and Zariski topologies.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sch}/k
, and
p\geq 0
. The
p
-th du Bois complex
of
X
is the complex

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X/k}=\mathbb{R}a_{*}a^{*}\Omega^{p}_{/k}|_{X}

of Zariski sheaves.

Unless it is necessary to emphasize the base field,
we will suppress it from the notation and simply write
\Omega^{p}_{X}

and
\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}
for differentials and
du Bois complexes over
k
, respectively.

A scheme
X
is said to have
du Bois singularities

if
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{X}
is an isomorphism.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

This
definition of
\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}

agrees with the original one given by du Bois in

[
9
]
(using hyper-resolutions);
see
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
for a proof of this fact.

(b)

By adjunction, there is a natural homomorphism

\Omega^{p}_{X}\to\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}
. This homomorphism is an
isomorphism when
X
is smooth.

(c)

The complex of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}
has coherent cohomology sheaves
(see
[
9
, Propn. 4.4]
).

The following generalization of the definition of du Bois singularity
has been studied recently, see
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
,

[
22
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme, and
p\geq 0
. We say
X

has (only)
p
-du Bois singularities
if the natural homomorphisms

\Omega^{s}_{X}\longrightarrow\underline{\Omega}^{s}_{X}

are isomorphisms for all
s\leq p
.

Remark 2.4
.

As pointed out
in the Introduction of
[
22
]
,
this notion (called “strict
p
-du Bois” in loc. cit.)
is poorly understood for
p>0
,
unless
X
is a local complete intersection.
We will return to this in section
3
.

We will connect du Bois singularities to
K
-regularity using descent for Hochschild
homology. Recall that
HH(/k)
satisfies
p
-cdh descent on
a
k
-scheme
X
if the fiber of the map from
HH(X/k)
to its

cdh
-hypercohomology is
p
-connected.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
1
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type, and let
p\geq 0
. Then
HH(/k)
satisfies
p
-cdh
descent on
X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
if and only if the following three
conditions hold simultaneously:

(2.5a)

\displaystyle HH_{m}^{(q)}(R/k)=0\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif }0\leq q<m\leq p;

(2.5b)

\displaystyle\Omega^{q}_{R/k}\rightarrow H^{0}_{{\mathrm{cdh}}}(X,\Omega^{q}_{/k})\;\text{is bijective if }q\leq p,\text{ onto if }q=p+1;

(2.5c)

\displaystyle H^{s}_{{\mathrm{cdh}}}(X,\Omega^{q}_{/k})=0\quad\text{ if }s>0\text{ and }0\leq q\leq s+p+1.

The authors and Cortiñas proved in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]

that a finite type

-algebra satisfying the equivalent conditions of
Lemma
2.5
is regular in codimension
p
. The following
theorem shows that the hypothesis can be weakened:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sch}/k
. If
X
has
p
-du Bois singularities, then

X
is regular in codimension
p
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be a point of codimension
d\leq p
.
By
[
1
, Lemma 4.10]
, we can represent the local ring
A=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}

as the localization of a finite type
F

at a maximal ideal, for some field

containing
k
.
The proofs of
[
1
, Lemma 4.4 and Prop. 4.8]
go through,
noting that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
suffice, showing that
if
X
is
p
-du Bois (over
k
) then
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is

p
-du Bois (over

). Thus we may assume,
without loss of generality, that
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is the spectrum of a local ring of
a
d
-dimensional

-algebra of finite type at a maximal ideal, and

is its closed point.
The hypothesis that

-du Bois singularities then
is equivalent to the following subset
of the conditions of Lemma

(2.6a)

\displaystyle\Omega^{q}_{A/k}\rightarrow H^{0}_{{\mathrm{cdh}}}(X,\Omega^{q}_{/k})\quad\text{is an isomorphism if }q\leq d;

(2.6b)

\displaystyle H^{s}_{{\mathrm{cdh}}}(X,\Omega^{q}_{/k})=0\quad\text{ if }s>0\text{ and }0\leq q\leq d.

By inspection, the proof of
[

Indeed, the properties above suffice to conclude,

and, as explained there, this implies that

.
This proves the assertion.
∎

By

1
, Corollary 1.7]
, the
K
-regularity hypothesis is
equivalent to the assertion that
HH(/\mathbb{Q})
satisfies

p
-cdh-descent on
X
.
Now
[
1
, Propn. 4.8]
implies that
HH(/k)
satisfies

p
-cdh-descent on
X
as well. Finally Lemma
2.5
gives an
explicit description of this descent property; parts (
2.5b
)
and (
2.5c
) of this description in particular imply that

X
has

-du Bois singularities.
∎

Combining the above yields
a new proof of the main theorem of

]
.
Recall that
k
is a field of characteristic 0.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sch}/k
be an affine scheme, and
p\geq 1
.
If

-regular, then

regular in codimension
p
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.7
asserts that

is

-du Bois . It now follows from Theorem

that

is regular in codimension

∎

3.
The case of local complete intersections.

In this short section, we combine Hodge theoretic results of
Mustaţă and Popa
[
21
]

with the previous section to prove that the
K
-regularity bound
for singularities given by Vorst’s conjecture and
proven in
[
1
]
can be significantly
improved if the singularity is a local complete intersection (
lci
).
We also formulate some questions.

The following theorem is proved by Mustaţă and Popa in
[
21
]

and is stated in the form used here (with
k=\mathbb{C}
)
as
[
11
, Theorem 3.8]
. The hypersurface case of this theorem also follows from bounds proved in
[
20
]
and
[
16
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Mustaţă–Popa)
.

Let
X/k
be locally a complete intersection
with
p
-du Bois singularities,
p\geq 1
. Then

X
is normal and regular in codimension
2p
.

Question 3.2
.

Let
A
be a
k
-algebra, essentially of finite type over
k
.
As can be seen using Lemma
2.5

and
[
1
, Propn. 4.8]
,
A
is
K_{2}
-regular
if, and only if,
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is
1
-du Bois , the natural homomorphism

\Omega^{2}_{A/k}\rightarrow H^{0}_{{\mathrm{cdh}}}(X,\Omega^{2}_{/k})
is onto, and
certain cohomology groups of the du Bois complexes vanish.
We proved in Theorem
1.9
that
a
K_{2}
-regular algebra is normal, without the assumption
that
A
is a local complete intersection.

This suggests the following question: Suppose
A
is a
1
-du Bois algebra, not necessarily a local complete intersection.
Is
A
normal? We note that
A
is
regular in codimension
1
by Theorem
2.8
, so this
question really asks if
A
necessarily satisfies Serre’s condition (S2).

The following result provides the promised improvement of Vorst’s bound
in the case of a local complete intersection.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Sch}/k
be an affine scheme, and
p\geq 1
.
If
X
is
K_{p+1}
-regular and a local complete intersection,
then
X
is normal and regular in codimension
2p
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition
2.7

and Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Question 3.4
.

We wonder if the
lci hypothesis is needed to prove the sharper bound.
Typically, schemes that are not local complete intersection are
not even
1
-du Bois ,
since their sheaves of differentials are usually not torsion free.
On the other hand, the arguments developed in
[
21
]
employ the
Hodge structure on (top) local cohomology and rely crucially on the
lci condition.

The first case of interest is those of surfaces, which suggests the
question: Suppose
X
is an affine surface over a field of
characteristic zero. If
X
is
K_{2}
-regular, is it necessarily
regular?

Theorem
3.1
can be sharpened; the following was communicated to us by Wanchun Shen. Let
S
be a smooth
k
-scheme, and
X\subseteq S
a local complete intersection subvariety. The minimal exponent
\tilde{\alpha}(S,X)
is a positive real number; it was introduced for hypersurfaces in
[
23
]
, and defined for general local complete intersection subschemes in
[
6
]
.

Given a complete intersection local ring
A
, the
minimal embedding codimension
of
A
is the minimal integer
r
such that there exists a regular local ring
R
and regular sequence
\left(f_{1},\dotsc,f_{r}\right)
in
R
such that
A\cong R/\left(f_{1},\dotsc,f_{r}\right)
.

Combining results from
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3.5

(Wanchun Shen)
.

Let
A=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
be a local ring of a local complete intersection
k
-variety at a closed point, and
r
the minimal embedding codimension of
A.

Suppose
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
has
p
-du Bois singularities,
p\geq 1
. Then
X
is regular in codimension
2p+r-1
.

Proof.

We can find a smooth
k
-variety
S
and closed point
s\in S
such that
A\cong\mathcal{O}_{S,s}/J
for an ideal
J
generated by a regular sequence of length
r
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
A
. Because
r
is minimal,
J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.
Intersecting with generic hyperplanes preserves the
p
-du Bois property by
[
11
, Corollary 5.11]
, so we may assume that
X
has an isolated singularity. Let
n=\dim S
and
d=n-r=\dim X
. Since
J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
,
[
6
, Theorem 1.2 (iii)]
implies that
\tilde{\alpha}(S,X)\leq n/2=(d+r)/2.
On the other hand, because
X
is
p
-du Bois,
[
21
, Theorem F]
shows that
\tilde{\alpha}(S,X)\geq p+r.
(The minimal exponent is not used in
loc. cit.
, but it is explained in
[
7
, Subsection 2.4]
that Theorem F can be interpreted as cited.)

Combining the two inequalities, we get that

2(p+r)\leq d+r\Longrightarrow 2p+r\leq d.

Since
X
has an isolated singularity, we conclude that it is regular in codimension
2p+r-1
, as asserted.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(X,x)=\left(\operatorname{Spec}(A),\mathfrak{m}\right)
be a local, complete intersection singularity of dimension
d
over
k
, and let
r
be its minimal embedding codimension. If
X
has
p
-du Bois singularities,
p\geq 1
, then
r\leq d-2p
.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that the minimal embedding codimension is
r>d-2p
. Then
X
is regular in codimension
d
, and hence, regular. But this implies
r=0
, a contradiction. ∎

Remark 3.7
.

We can apply Corollary
3.6
to obtain
K
-regularity bounds on the embedding codimension of germs of singularities. For example, if
(X,x)
is a local, complete intersection singularity of
dimension
3
, and
X
is
K_{2}
-regular, then
X
is a hypersurface.

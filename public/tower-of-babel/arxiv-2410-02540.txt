h ​ p hp -error analysis of mixed-order hybrid high-order methods for elliptic problems on simplicial meshes
Zhaonan Dong, Alexandre Ern

Abstract
We present both h ​ p hp -a priori and h ​ p hp -a posteriori error analysis of a mixed-order hybrid high-order (HHO) method to approximate second-order elliptic problems on simplicial meshes. Our main result on the h ​ p hp -a priori error analysis is a 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order p p -suboptimal error estimate. This result is, to our knowledge, the first of this kind for hybrid nonconforming methods and matches the state-of-the-art for other nonconforming methods (as discontinuous Galerkin methods) with general (mixed Dirichlet/Neumann) boundary conditions. Our second main result is a residual-based h ​ p hp -a posteriori upper error bound, comprising residual, normal flux jump, tangential jump, and stabilization estimators (plus data oscillation terms). The first three terms are p p -optimal and only the latter is 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order p p -suboptimal. This result is, to our knowledge, the first h ​ p hp -a posteriori error estimate for HHO methods. A novel approach based on the partition-of-unity provided by hat basis functions and on local Helmholtz decompositions on vertex stars is devised to estimate the nonconforming error. Finally, we establish local lower error bounds. Remarkably, the normal flux jump estimator is only 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order p p -suboptimal, as it can be bounded by the stabilization owing to the local conservation property of HHO methods. Numerical examples illustrate the theory.

Introduction

Second-order elliptic PDEs are widely used in the modeling of diffusion phenomena. In the present work, we consider the following model problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla{\cdot}(A\nabla u)&=f&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=g_{\text{\rm D}}&\quad&\text{on }\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}},\\
(A\nabla u){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}&=g_{\text{\rm N}}&\quad&\text{on }\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where the domain
\Omega
is a Lipschitz polytopal domain (open, connected, bounded set) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, with boundary
\partial\Omega
and unit outward normal
{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}
. The boundary
\partial\Omega
is split into two disjoint parts
\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}
and
\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}}
with
|\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}|>0
. In addition, the load
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g_{\text{\rm D}}
is a restriction to
\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}
of a function in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
,
g_{\text{\rm N}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}})

and
A
is a piecewise scalar-valued diffusion coefficient such that
0<A_{\Omega}^{\flat}\leq A(\bm{x})\leq A_{\Omega}^{\sharp}
for a.e.
\bm{x}\in\Omega
.

The hybrid high-order (HHO) method was introduced in
[
21
]
for linear diffusion and in
[
22
]
for locking-free linear elasticity. As shown in
[
18
]
, the HHO method is
closely related to hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) and weak Galerkin (WG) methods.
These links have been leveraged, e.g., in
[
24
,
29
]

to devise a unified convergence analysis for the biharmonic problem and the acoustic wave equation.
We also refer the reader to
[
43
]
and
[
17
]
for links to the
nonconforming virtual element method (ncVEM), and to
[
14
]
for links to
multiscale hybrid-mixed (MHM) methods. HHO methods are formulated in terms of broken cell and face polynomial spaces. The equal-order HHO method corresponds to cell and face unknowns having the same degree
k\geq 0
. Instead, the mixed-order HHO method corresponds to cell unknowns having degree
(k+1)
and face unknowns having degree
k\geq 0
. Considering cell unknowns of degree
(k-1)
with
k\geq 1
is also possible. One salient advantage of the mixed-order setting with cell unknowns of degree
(k+1)
is that
h
-optimal convergence can be achieved by using the simple Lehrenfeld–Schöberl (LS) HDG stabilization
[
42
]
, in contrast with the more sophisticated HHO stabilization needed in the other settings.

The goal of the present work is to derive
hp
-a priori and
hp
-a posteriori
error estimates for the mixed-order HHO method on simplicial meshes. Owing to the links highlighted above, the present results extend to HDG and WG methods in the same setting.
Although HHO (and HDG, WG) methods can deal with polytopal meshes, we focus here on simplicial meshes because some of the
hp
-analysis tools we are going to invoke are only available on such meshes (and on tensor-product meshes as well). Our main result concerning the
hp
-a priori error analysis is
Theorem
4.4
, where we derive a
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimal and
h
-optimal error estimate. The only
hp
-a priori HHO error estimate we are aware of is derived in
[
1
]
for the equal-order HHO method and leads to a
1
-order
p
-suboptimal error bound.
Here, we use the same
hp
-scaling of the stabilization bilinear form, but the simpler form
of the LS stabilization allows us to prove an error estimate with a tighter scaling in the polynomial degree.
Notice in passing that
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality corresponds to the state of the art for classical discontinuous Galerkin (dG) methods
[
37
,
33
,
9
]
under general Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions, and was also
obtained in
[
25
]
for a hybrid dG method applied to Stokes flow. In the case of homogeneous Dirichlet conditions over the whole boundary,
it is shown in
[
50
]
for dG methods applied to the Poisson problem
and in
[
41
]
for HDG methods applied to the Stokes problem that
an a priori error estimate with full
p
-optimality can be achieved. However,
counterexamples in
[
33
]
confirm that the
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality
is sharp in the case of inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The second main contribution of the present work is to derive a residual-based
hp
-a posteriori error estimate for the mixed-order HHO method in dimensions
d\in\{2,3\}
. Our main result concerning the (global) upper error bound is Theorem
5.8
, where all the terms in the upper bound are
hp
-optimal except one term which is
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimal. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such estimate for HHO methods, whereas
h
-a posteriori error estimates for HHO methods were derived previously in
[
23
,
5
,
11
]
, focusing on either the equal-order HHO setting or a stabilization-free variant of the method.

The main challenge in deriving an upper error bound for nonconforming methods is to estimate the nonconforming error, which essentially measures by how much the discrete solution departs from
H^{1}
. A first possibility is to invoke a nodal-averaging operator mapping to
H^{1}
, as done, e.g., in
[
38
,
30
,
2
,
31
]
for dG methods. However,
p
-optimal approximation results for nodal-averaging operators are so far available only on tensor-product
[
7
]
and triangular
[
36
]
meshes, whereas the best bound available on tetrahedral meshes is
p
-suboptimal by one order
[
25
, Lemma 7.6]
.
An alternative to using a nodal-averaging operator is to invoke a (global) Helmholtz decomposition on the
nonconforming error, as in
[
20
,
10
,
12
]
for Crouzeix–Raviart finite elements and in
[
4
,
8
]
for dG methods. Here, we adopt this technique, but we introduce a novel idea in that we additionally use the partition of unity provided by the hat basis functions to invoke a
local
Helmholtz decomposition on each vertex star (the subdomain covered by the mesh cells sharing the vertex). The benefit is that each vertex star is simply connected, and recent results on the stability constant in the local Helmholtz decomposition are available
[
34
]
. Instead, the stability constant for a global Helmholtz decomposition in a domain with
N
holes grows unfavorably with
N

[
5
]
. Finally, we emphasize that our novel idea for bounding the nonconforming error can be directly applied to the a posteriori analysis of many other nonconforming discretization methods, such as dG, HDG, and WG.

We also address the efficiency of our a posteriori error estimate by establishing local lower error bounds. Our main result is Theorem
5.10
which leads to
\frac{3}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality (only the tangential jump estimator leads to such suboptimality). Numerical experiments, though, indicate only
\frac{1}{2}
-order suboptimality in
p
. Our proof of the lower error bound uses bubble function techniques inspired from
[
45
]
, but we introduce a novel argument in the proof in that we invoke the local conservation property of the HHO method to improve the efficiency result on the normal flux jump from
\frac{3}{2}
-order to
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality. Another interesting numerical observation is
that the normal flux jump is not the dominant component of the a posteriori error estimate for HHO methods, in contrast to the situation classically encountered with conforming finite elements

[
13
]
.

The rest of this work is organised as follows. We present the weak formulation of the model problem together with the discrete setting in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we introduce the HHO method, and in Section
4
, we derive the
hp
-a priori error estimate. In Section
5
, we present the residual-based
hp
-a posteriori error analysis, leading to a (global) upper error bound and (local) lower error bounds. Numerical experiments are presented in Section
6
to illustrate the theory. Finally, in Section
7
, we collect several (technical) proofs related to the
hp
-a posteriori error analysis.

2
Weak form and discrete setting

In this section, we introduce some basic notation, the weak formulation of the model problem, and the discrete setting to formulate and analyze the HHO discretization.

2.1
Basic notation and weak formulation

We use standard notation for the Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces and, in particular, for the fractional-order Sobolev spaces, we consider the Sobolev–Slobodeckij seminorm based on the double integral. For an open, bounded, Lipschitz set
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{1,2,3\}
, we denote by
(v,w)_{S}
the
L^{2}(S)
-inner product, and we employ the same notation when
v
and
w
are vector- or matrix-valued fields. We denote by
\nabla w
the (weak) gradient of
w
. We use boldface notation to denote vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as well as
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued fields and functional spaces composed of such fields.

Setting
H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\>|\>v|_{\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}}=g_{\text{\rm D}}\}
,
the weak formulation of (
1
) is as follows: Find
u\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
, such that

(A{\nabla u},{\nabla v})_{\Omega}=(f,v)_{\Omega}+(g_{\text{\rm N}},v)_{\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}}},

(2)

for all
v\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
. The well-posedness of (
2
) follows from the Lax–Milgram Lemma, see, e.g.,
[
28
, Proposition 31.21]
.

2.2
Mesh

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a simplicial mesh that covers the domain
\Omega
exactly and is compatible with the boundary partition as well as the domain partition on which
A
is piecewise constant. A generic mesh cell is denoted by
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, its diameter by
h_{K}
, and its unit outward normal by
{\bm{n}}_{K}
. We set
A_{K}:=A|_{K}
. We let
\mathrm{st}(K)
denote the collection of cells
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing at least one vertex with the cell
K
(
\mathrm{st}(K)
is often called cell star). Similarly,
\mathrm{es}(K)
denotes the collection of cells
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing at least one vertex with
\mathrm{st}(K)
(
\mathrm{es}(K)
is often called extended cell star). For all
k\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}^{k}(K)
denotes the space of
d
-variate polynomials on
K
of degree at most
k
, and
\Pi_{K}^{k}
denotes the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(K)
. Moreover,
\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,|\,v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(K)\}
denotes the broken polynomial space of order
k
on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(classically considered in dG methods).

The mesh faces are collected in the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}
, which is split as
\mathcal{F}_{h}=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{b}}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{b}}

of boundary faces. Moreover, we split

is arbitrary, but fixed, whereas we set
{\bm{n}}_{F}:={\bm{n}}_{\Omega}|_{F}
for all

. For every mesh cell
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the partition of its boundary
\partial K
is defined as
\partial K=\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}\cup\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}\cup\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}
with obvious notation, and the mesh faces composing
\partial K
are collected in the set
\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}
, which is partitioned as
\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}=\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}
with obvious notation. For all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
,
\Pi_{F}^{k}
denotes
the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)
.

The set of mesh vertices is denoted by
\mathcal{V}_{h}
and is decomposed into the subset of interior vertices,
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}
, and the subset of boundary vertices,
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\mathrm{b}}
. For all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,
\mathcal{T}_{{\bm{a}}}
denotes the collection of mesh cells which share
{\bm{a}}
and
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
the corresponding open subdomain (often called vertex star). In addition, we define
\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}
as the collection of faces in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
which share
{\bm{a}}
.

For all

, we define the broken Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{q}):=\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{q})\>|\>w_{K}:=w|_{K}\in H^{s}(K;\mathbb{R}^{q}),~\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

Lemma 2.4

(Global

, there exists an operator

Corollary 2.5

(Modified
hp
-Karkulik–Melenk operator)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\rm mKM}
such that, for all
k\geq 1
, there exists an operator

I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}:H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
, called modified Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator, such that, for all
v\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2}\|{v}-I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(v)\|^{2}_{K}+\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{v}-I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(v)\|^{2}_{\partial K}+\|\nabla I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(v)\|^{2}_{K}\leq C_{\rm mKM}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{es}(K)}.

(7)

Proof.

The idea is to set, for all
v\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
,

I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(v):=I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)+I^{k}_{{\rm KM}}(v-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)),

where the (first-order) nodal-averaging operator
I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}:H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
is devised, e.g.,
in
[
26
]
when
\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}=\partial\Omega
and in
[
44
]
when
\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}
is
a proper subset of
\partial\Omega
. This operator satisfies, for all
v\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
,

\Big(\frac{1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2}\|{v}-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)\|^{2}_{K}+\Big(\frac{1}{h_{K}}\Big)\|{v}-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)\|^{2}_{\partial K}+\|\nabla I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)\|^{2}_{K}\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{st}(K)}.

(8)

We can now prove (
7
). Using the approximation results (
6
) and (
8
) and the mesh shape-regularity, we infer that

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2}\|{v}-I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(v)\|^{2}_{K}&=\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2}\|({v}-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v))-I^{k}_{{\rm KM}}(v-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v))\|^{2}_{K}\\
&\leq C\|v-I^{1}_{{\rm av},\text{\rm D}}(v)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathrm{st}(K))}\leq C\sum_{\widehat{K}\in\mathrm{st}(K)}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{st}(\widehat{K})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{es}(K)}.\end{split}

This proves the bound on the first term on the left-hand side of (
7
), and
the other two terms can be bounded in a similar way.
∎

3
HHO method

Let
k\geq 0
be the polynomial degree. We focus on the mixed-order HHO method where, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the local HHO space is

\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}:=\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)\times\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}),\qquad\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}):=\bigtimes_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}}\mathbb{P}^{k}(F).

(9)

A generic element in
\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
is denoted by
\widehat{v}_{K}:=(v_{K},v_{\partial K})
with
v_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
and
v_{\partial K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial K})
. The first component of the pair
\widehat{v}_{K}
aims at representing the solution inside the
mesh cell and the second its trace at the cell boundary.

3.1
Reconstruction and stabilization

The HHO method is formulated locally by means of a reconstruction and a stabilization operator. The local reconstruction operator
R_{K}^{k+1}:\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
is such that, for all
\widehat{v}_{K}:=(v_{K},v_{\partial K})\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
,

R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
is determined by solving the following well-posed problem:

\displaystyle(\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K}),\nabla w)_{K}:={}

\displaystyle(\nabla{v}_{K},\nabla w)_{K}-(v_{K}-v_{\partial K},\nabla w{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K})_{\partial K},

(10)

for all
w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)/\mathbb{R}
and
(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K}),1)_{K}=(v_{K},1)_{K}
. (Notice that (
10
) actually holds for all
w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
). Integration by parts gives

(\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K}),\nabla w)_{K}=-(v_{K},\Delta w)_{K}+(v_{\partial K},\nabla w{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K})_{\partial K}.

(11)

The local stabilization bilinear form
S_{\partial K}
is defined such that, for all
(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K})\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}\times\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
,

\displaystyle S_{\partial K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K}):={}

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\big(v_{\partial K}-\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}(v_{K}|_{\partial K}),w_{\partial K}-\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})\big)_{\partial K},

(12)

where
\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}
denotes the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial K})
.
The reconstruction and stabilization operators are combined together to build
the local bilinear form
a_{K}
on
\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}\times\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
such that, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

a_{K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K}):=A_{K}(\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K}),\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{w}_{K}))_{K}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K}).

(13)

Lemma 3.1

(Useful property)
.

The following holds for all
\widehat{v}_{K}\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K})\|_{K}^{2}\leq CS_{\partial K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{v}_{K}).

(14)

Proof.

Using the definition (
10
) of the reconstruction operator with
w:=R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
gives

\displaystyle\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K})\|_{K}^{2}:={}

\displaystyle-(v_{K}-v_{\partial K},\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K})_{\partial K}.

Since
\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{\partial K})
, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the definition (
12
) of the stabilization, and the discrete trace inequality (
3
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K})\|_{K}^{2}={}

\displaystyle-(\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(v_{K}|_{\partial K})-v_{\partial K},\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K})_{\partial K}

\displaystyle\leq CS_{\partial K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{v}_{K})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})-v_{K})\|_{K}.

This concludes the proof of (
14
).
∎

3.2
Global discrete problem

We define the global HHO space as

\widehat{V}_{h}^{k}:=\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{h}),\quad\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\bigtimes_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K),\quad\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{h}):=\bigtimes_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\mathbb{P}^{k}(F).

(15)

A generic element in
\widehat{V}_{h}^{k}
is denoted by
\widehat{v}_{h}:=(v_{\mathcal{T}_{h}},v_{\mathcal{F}_{h}})

with
v_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}:=(v_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and
v_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}:=(v_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}
. For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the local components of
\widehat{v}_{h}
are collected in the pair

\widehat{v}_{K}:=(v_{K},v_{\partial K})\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
with
v_{\partial K}|_{F}:=v_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}
. Similarly, let
R^{k+1}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\widehat{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
be such that
R^{k+1}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\widehat{v}_{h})|_{K}=R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. To deal with the Dirichlet boundary condition, we define the (affine) subspaces

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}:=\{\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}^{k}\;|\;v_{F}=0,\;\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}\},

(16a)

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{hg,\rm{D}}^{k}:=\{\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}^{k}\;|\;v_{F}=\Pi_{F}^{k}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{F}),\;\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}\}.

(16b)

The discrete HHO problem is as follows: Find
\widehat{u}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{hg,\rm{D}}^{k}
such that

a_{h}(\widehat{u}_{h},\widehat{w}_{h})=\ell_{h}(\widehat{w}_{h}),\qquad\forall\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k},

(17)

where the global discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
and the global linear form
\ell_{h}
are assembled cellwise as

a_{h}(\widehat{v}_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K}),\qquad\ell_{h}(\widehat{w}_{h}):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\{(f,{w}_{K})_{K}+(g_{\text{\rm N}},w_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\big\}.

(18)

It is well-known that the discrete problem (
17
) is amenable to static condensation, i.e.,
the cell unknowns can be eliminated locally in every mesh cell, leading to a global problem where the only remaining unknowns
are those attached to the mesh faces, i.e.,
those in
\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D})
.

An important property of the HHO method we exploit in the a posteriori error analysis is local conservation. For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}
,
we define the flux

\phi_{K,F}(\widehat{u}_{K}):=-A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}|_{F}+A_{K}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\Pi_{F}^{k}({u_{K}}|_{F})-{u_{F}}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(F).

(19)

Then, the following holds true
[
18
]
: (i) At every interface
F=\partial K_{1}\cap\partial K_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}
, we have

\phi_{K_{1},F}(\widehat{u}_{K_{1}})+\phi_{K_{2},F}(\widehat{u}_{K_{2}})=0;

(20a)

(ii) At every Neumann boundary face
F=\partial K\cap\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm N}
, we have

\phi_{K,F}(\widehat{u}_{K})+\Pi^{k}_{F}(g_{\text{\rm N}}|_{F})=0.

(20b)

3.3
Stability and well-posedness

We equip the local HHO space
\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
with the
H^{1}
-like seminorm such that, for all
\widehat{v}_{K}\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
,

|\widehat{v}_{K}|^{2}_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}}:=\|\nabla v_{K}\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\|v_{\partial K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(v_{K}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K}^{2}.

(21)

Lemma 3.2

(Local stability and boundedness)
.

There is a real number
\alpha>0
, depending only on the mesh shape-regularity and the space dimension
d
, such that, for all
\widehat{v}_{K}\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\alpha|\widehat{v}_{K}|^{2}_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}}\leq\|\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{v}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{v}_{K},\widehat{v}_{K})\leq\alpha^{-1}|\widehat{v}_{K}|^{2}_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}}.

(22)

Proof.

The proof proceeds as in
[
18
]
using the discrete trace inequality (
3
); this is the reason why the stabilization is scaled by
(k+1)^{2}
and not just by
(k+1)
.
∎

We equip the space
\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
with the norm

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\textsc{hho}}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}|\widehat{v}_{K}|^{2}_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}},\qquad\forall\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}.

(23)

The fact that
\|{\cdot}\|_{\textsc{hho}}
defines indeed a norm on
\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
is shown, e.g., in
[
17
, Lemma 1.6]
.

Corollary 3.3

(Coercivity and well-posedness)
.

The discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
is coercive on
\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
, and the discrete problem (
17
) is well-posed.

Proof.

Summing (
22
) multiplied by
A_{K}
over all the mesh cells shows the following coercivity and continuity properties:

\alpha\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}^{2}\leq a_{h}(\widehat{v}_{h},\widehat{v}_{h})\leq\alpha^{-1}\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}^{2},\qquad\forall\widehat{v}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}.

(24)

The well-posedness of (
17
) with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions then follows from the Lax–Milgram lemma. For inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, the proof follows by introducing a lifting, say
\widehat{g}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{hg,\rm{D}}^{k}
, such that
g_{F}=\Pi_{F}^{k}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{F})
, for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}
.
∎

4
hp
-a priori error estimate

In this section, we establish our
hp
-a priori error estimate.

4.1
Approximation

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the local reduction operator

\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}:H^{1}(K)\rightarrow\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}
such that, for all
v\in H^{1}(K)
,

\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v):=\big(\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(v),\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(v|_{\partial K})\big)\in\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}.

(25)

Moreover, the elliptic projection
\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}:H^{1}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)
is defined such that

\displaystyle(\nabla(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-v),\nabla w)_{K}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall w\in\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(K)/\mathbb{R},

(26)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-v,1)_{K}

\displaystyle=0.

One readily verifies by proceeding as in
[
21
, Lemma 3]
that

R_{K}^{k+1}\circ\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}=\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}.

(27)

Lemma 4.1

(Bound on stabilization)
.

The following holds for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and all
v\in H^{1}(K)
:

S_{\partial K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v),\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v))\leq C(k+1)\|\nabla(v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)\|_{K}^{2}.

(28)

Proof.

Recalling the definition (
12
) of
S_{\partial K}
, the definition (
25
) of
\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}
and since
\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}\circ\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}=\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}
, we have

S_{\partial K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v),\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v))=\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\|\Pi^{k}_{\partial K}((v-\Pi^{k+1}_{K}(v))|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K}^{2}\leq\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\|v-\Pi^{k+1}_{K}(v)\|_{\partial K}^{2},

where we used the
L^{2}(\partial K)
-stability of
\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}
.
Then, we invoke the approximation result on the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection, see (
4
), and that
\Pi^{k+1}_{K}\circ\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}=\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}
, giving

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\|v-\Pi^{k+1}_{K}(v)\|_{\partial K}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}\|v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)-\Pi^{k+1}_{K}(v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v))\|_{\partial K}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(k+1)\|\nabla(v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)\|_{K}^{2}.

Combining the above two bounds proves (
28
).
∎

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and all
v\in H^{1+s}(K)
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we consider the following norm:

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{\sharp,K}:=\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\|\nabla v\|_{\partial K}^{2}.

(29)

Lemma 4.2

(Approximation)
.

The following holds for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and all
v\in H^{1+s}(K)
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
:

\|v-\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K}\leq C(k+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K}.

(30)

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we have

\|v-\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K}\leq\|v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K}+\|\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)-\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K},

so that we only need to bound the second term on the right-hand side. Owing to the discrete trace inequality (
3
), we infer that

\|\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)\|_{\sharp,K}\leq C(k+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v))\|_{K},

and it remains to bound
\|\nabla(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v))\|_{K}
.
Since
\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}\circ\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}=\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v)-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v))\|_{K}=\|\nabla(\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v)))\|_{K}\leq\|\nabla(v-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(v))\|_{K},

where the last bound follows from the stability of the elliptic projection. Combining the above bounds completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Consistency

Let
u\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
be the exact solution to (
2
).
We define the consistency error
\delta_{h}\in(\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k})^{\prime}
such that

\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}\rangle:=\ell_{h}(\widehat{w}_{h})-a_{h}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(u),\widehat{w}_{h}),\qquad\forall\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k},

(31)

where
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
(\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k})^{\prime}
and
\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
, and where
the global reduction operator
\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}:H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\widehat{V}_{h}^{k}
is defined
such that, for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(v):=\big((\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(v|_{K}))_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(\Pi_{F}^{k}(v|_{F}))_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\big)\in\widehat{V}_{h}^{k},

(32)

observing that
v
is single-valued on every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}
. Notice that the local components of
\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(v)
attached to
K
and its faces are

\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(v|_{K})
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
The above definition of the consistency error is classical in the context of HHO methods. It is also rather natural since, as for most nonconforming discretization methods, it avoids defining any extension of the discrete bilinear form. Examples for other nonconforming methods can be found, e.g., in
[
28
, Part VIII]
.

Lemma 4.3

(Consistency)
.

Assume that
u\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
. The following holds true:

\|\delta_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}^{\prime}}:=\sup_{\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}}\frac{|\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}\rangle|}{\|\widehat{w}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}}\leq C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|^{2}_{\sharp,K}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(33)

Proof.

Let
\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
. Using the definition of
\ell_{h}
in (
18
), the PDE and the boundary conditions satisfied by the exact solution
u
, and integrating by parts cellwise, we infer that

\ell_{h}(\widehat{w}_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{(\nabla u,\nabla{w}_{K})_{K}-(\nabla u{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},w_{K})_{\partial K}+(\nabla u{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},w_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big\}.

The assumption
u\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
implies that
(\nabla u{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K})|_{\partial K}
is meaningful in
L^{2}(\partial K)
and single-valued at every mesh interface. Moreover, since
w_{\partial K}
is single-valued on
\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}
and vanishes on
\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(\widehat{w}_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{(\nabla u,\nabla{w}_{K})_{K}-(\nabla u{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},w_{K}-w_{\partial K})_{\partial K}\Big\}.

Since
a_{h}
is assembled cellwise and the local
components of
\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(u)
are
\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K})
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
we infer that

a_{h}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(u),\widehat{w}_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K}),\widehat{w}_{K})
.
Using the definition (
13
) of
a_{K}
, the definition (
10
) of

R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{w}_{K})
and the identity (
27
) leads to

\displaystyle a_{h}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(u),\widehat{w}_{h})=

\displaystyle{}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{(\nabla\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(u|_{K}),\nabla{w}_{K})_{K}-(\nabla\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(u|_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},w_{K}-w_{\partial K})_{\partial K}

\displaystyle+S_{\partial K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K}),\widehat{w}_{K})\Big\}.

Defining the function
\eta
cellwise as
\eta|_{K}:=u|_{K}-\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(u|_{K})
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
we infer that

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}\rangle={}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{-(\nabla\eta{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},w_{K}-w_{\partial K})_{\partial K}-S_{\partial K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K}),\widehat{w}_{K})\Big\},

(34)

where we used that
(\nabla\eta,\nabla w_{K})_{K}=0
.
Let us denote by
\mathcal{T}_{1,K}
the first term and by

\mathcal{T}_{2,K}
the second term inside braces on the right-hand side of (
34
). We bound
\mathcal{T}_{1,K}
by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the triangle inequality, the trivial bound
\|w_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K}\leq\|w_{K}-\Pi_{K}^{k}(w_{K})\|_{\partial K}
,
and the approximation result (
4
) on the
L^{2}
-projection. Recalling the definition (
29
) of the

\|{{\cdot}}\|_{\sharp,K}
-norm, this yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{1,K}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\eta\|_{\partial K}\big(\|\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})-w_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K}+\|w_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\eta\|_{\partial K}\Big(\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})-w_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K}+\|w_{K}-\Pi_{K}^{k}(w_{K})\|_{\partial K}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\sharp,K}\left(\Big(\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(w_{K}|_{\partial K})-w_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K}+C\|\nabla w_{K}\|_{K}\right)\leq C\|\eta\|_{\sharp,K}|\widehat{w}_{K}|_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}}.

Moreover, owing to (
28
) and the upper bound in (
22
), we have

|\mathcal{T}_{2,K}|\leq S_{\partial K}(\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K}),\mathcal{\widehat{I}}^{k}_{K}(u|_{K}))^{\frac{1}{2}}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{w}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(k+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u))\|_{K}|\widehat{w}_{K}|_{\widehat{V}_{K}^{k}}.

Altogether, this implies that

|\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{w}_{h}\rangle|\leq C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\eta\|^{2}_{\sharp,K}+(k+1)\|\nabla(u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u))\|_{K}^{2}\Big\}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\widehat{w}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}.

Invoking Lemma
4.2
to bound
\|\eta\|_{\sharp,K}
completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Error estimate

We are now ready to establish our main result concerning the
hp
-a priori error analysis. The estimate is
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimal.

Theorem 4.4

(
hp
-a priori error estimate)
.

Let
u
be the weak solution to (
2
), and let
\widehat{u}_{h}

be the discrete solution to (
17
).
Assume that
u\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
. The following holds:

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\nabla(u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-u_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|_{\sharp,K}^{2}.

(35a)

Moreover, if
u\in H^{l+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{R})
, with
l\in\{1,\dots,k+1\}
, we have

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\nabla(u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-u_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2l}\|u\|_{H^{l+1}(K)}^{2}.

(35b)

Proof.

(i) Proof of (
35a
). We set
\widehat{e}_{h}:=\mathcal{\widehat{I}}_{h}^{k}(u)-\widehat{u}_{h}
and observe that
\widehat{e}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0,\rm{D}}^{k}
. Moreover, since
a_{h}(\widehat{e}_{h},\widehat{e}_{h})=-\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{e}_{h}\rangle
, the coercivity property (
24
) implies that

\alpha\|\widehat{e}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}^{2}\leq a_{h}(\widehat{e}_{h},\widehat{e}_{h})=-\langle\delta_{h},\widehat{e}_{h}\rangle\leq\|\delta_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}^{\prime}}\|\widehat{e}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}},

so that
\|\widehat{e}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\delta_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}^{\prime}}
.
Since
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\big\{\|\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{e}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{e}_{K},\widehat{e}_{K})\big\}\leq\alpha^{-1}\|\widehat{e}_{h}\|_{\textsc{hho}}^{2}
follows from (
24
), we infer from Lemma
4.3
that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{e}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{e}_{K},\widehat{e}_{K})\Big\}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|^{2}_{\sharp,K}.

Since
u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})=(u-\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{K}(u))+R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{e}_{K})
because
\widehat{u}_{K}=\widehat{I}^{k}_{K}(u)-\widehat{e}_{K}
,
the triangle inequality combined with Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, and the above bound
proves that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\nabla(u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2}+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|_{\sharp,K}^{2}.

Moreover, using
\|\nabla(u-u_{K})\|_{K}^{2}\leq 2(\|\nabla(u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2}+\|\nabla(u_{K}-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2})
and (
14
), we infer that

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\|\nabla(u-u_{K})\|_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\Big\{\|\nabla(u-R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}))\|_{K}^{2}+CS_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}(k+1)\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|_{\sharp,K}^{2}.

This completes the proof of (
35a
).

(ii) The proof of (
35b
) follows by invoking on all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
the approximation properties of
\mathcal{I}_{{\rm BS},K}^{k+1}

(see Lemma
2.3
), together with a multiplicative trace inequality (see, e.g.,

[
27
, Sec. 12.3.2]
) to estimate the term involving the normal derivative in
\|u-\mathcal{I}^{k+1}_{{\rm BS},K}(u)\|_{\sharp,K}
.
∎

5
hp
-a posteriori error analysis

In this section, we perform the
hp
-a posteriori error analysis of the above HHO discretization.
We establish both (global) upper and (local) lower error bounds.
Since we are interested in the
hp
-a posteriori error analysis, we only consider in what follows
the case
k\geq 1
so that we can invoke the (modified) Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator. We shall also assume that the Dirichlet datum satisfies
g_{\text{\rm D}}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
. For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the following data oscillation terms:

\displaystyle O_{K}(f)

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|{f-\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)}\|_{K},

(36a)

\displaystyle O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{g_{\text{\rm N}}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}},

(36b)

\displaystyle O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}})

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla(g_{\text{\rm D}}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k+1}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{\partial K})){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}}.

(36c)

It is useful to define some contrast factors related to the diffusion coefficient
A
.
For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, we set

\chi_{K}(A):=A_{K}\max_{\widehat{K}\in\mathrm{es}(K)}A_{\widehat{K}}^{-1},\qquad\chi_{\bm{a}}(A):=A_{{\bm{a}}}^{\sharp}(A_{{\bm{a}}}^{\flat})^{-1},

(37)

with
A_{{\bm{a}}}^{\sharp}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{{\bm{a}}}}A_{K}
and
A_{{\bm{a}}}^{\flat}:=\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{{\bm{a}}}}A_{K}
. Moreover, it is convenient to set

\displaystyle A_{F}^{\flat}

\displaystyle:=\min(A_{K_{1}},A_{K_{2}}),

\displaystyle\forall F=\partial{K_{1}}\cap\partial{K_{2}}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}},

(38a)

\displaystyle A_{F}^{\flat}

\displaystyle:=A_{K},

\displaystyle\forall F=\partial K\cap\partial{\Omega}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{b}}.

(38b)

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we also define
A^{\flat}_{\partial K}|_{F}:=A^{\flat}_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K}
.

5.1
Global upper error bound

We decompose the error into two components as follows:

e:=u-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=(u-u_{c})+(u_{c}-u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})=:e_{c}+e_{d},\qquad u_{c}\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega),

(39)

where
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
denotes the cell component of the HHO solution such that
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}|_{K}:=u_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. The function
u_{c}
is constructed from
u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
as detailed in Section
5.1.2
. The precise definition of
u_{c}
is irrelevant for bounding
e_{c}
(we only use that
e_{c}\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
), and is only relevant for bounding
e_{d}
. We call
e_{c}
the conforming error and
e_{d}
the nonconforming error.

5.1.1
Bound on conforming error
e_{c}

In this section, we derive two bounds on the conforming error
e_{c}
. The first bound
avoids the normal flux jump (classically considered in the context of finite elements) and is

\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimal. The second bound includes the normal flux jump and is
p
-optimal.
The first bound is, however, interesting in its own right. We will also see that, in the context of the lower
error bound, the normal flux jump leads to
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality anyway. The proofs of the following two results
are postponed to Section
7
.

Lemma 5.1

(Conforming error (
p
-suboptimal bound))
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm c,1}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}(k+1)S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})

\displaystyle+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega},

(40)

where the constant
C_{\rm c,1}
depends on the mesh shape-regularity and on
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Lemma 5.2

(Conforming error (
p
-optimal bound))
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm c,2}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})

\displaystyle+A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\Big(\|[\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{\partial K}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}^{2}+\|{A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega},

(41)

where the constant
C_{\rm c,2}
depends on the mesh shape-regularity and on
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Remark 5.3

(Alternative bound)
.

The bounds on the conforming error derived in Lemma
5.1
and in Lemma
5.2
hinge on the
hp
-approximation properties of the modified Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator. If one considers instead the classical HHO interpolation operator based on local
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto cells and faces, one can derive an upper bound on the conforming error containing only the stabilization term and the data oscillation, but the price to pay is a
p
-suboptimality by half-order scaling the stabilization term. Details are omitted for brevity.

5.1.2
Bound on nonconforming error
e_{d}

We start by defining the function
u_{c}\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
introduced in (
39
).
To this purpose, in the spirit of
[
32
]
, we solve local minimization problems in
H^{1}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
with suitable boundary conditions for every mesh
vertex
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
(recall that
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
denotes the star associated with the vertex
{\bm{a}}
).
Let
\psi_{{\bm{a}}}
be the hat basis function equal to
1
at
{\bm{a}}
and having a support in the vertex star
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
. Recall that the hat basis functions satisfy the following partition-of-unity property:

\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\psi_{\bm{a}}=1.

(42)

Definition 5.4

(Patchwise and global potential reconstruction)
.

For all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, let
u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
be the solution of the following well-posed problem:

(A\nabla u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c},\nabla v_{{\bm{a}}})_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}=(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}),\nabla v_{{\bm{a}}})_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}},\qquad\forall v_{{\bm{a}}}\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}}),

(43)

with

H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}}):=\{v\in H^{1}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})\>|\>v|_{\partial\omega_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}}}=\psi_{{\bm{a}}}g_{\text{\rm D}}\text{ and }v|_{\partial\omega_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\Omega}=0\}.

(44)

An equivalent definition is

u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}:=\arg\min_{\rho_{{\bm{a}}}\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nabla\rho_{{\bm{a}}}-\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}))\|_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}.

(45)

Then, extending
u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}
by zero to
\Omega
, we set

u_{c}:=\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}.

(46)

Notice that we indeed have
u_{c}\in H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
as required in (
39
); this follows from the partition-of-unity property (
42
) and the definition (
44
) of
H^{1}_{g,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
.
The proof of the following result
is postponed to Section
7
.

Lemma 5.5

(Local nonconforming error)
.

Let
u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}
solve (
43
) and set
e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}:=u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}-\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

for all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
. The following holds:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm d}^{{\bm{a}}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{{\bm{a}}}}\!\Big\{A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}})^{2}\Big\}+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}}\!\!A_{F}^{\flat}\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{k+1}\Big)\|{[\![\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\!]_{F}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}}\!\!A_{F}^{\flat}\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{k+1}\Big)\|{\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k+1}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{\partial K})){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(47)

where the constant
C_{\rm d}^{{\bm{a}}}
depends on the mesh shape-regularity and on
\chi_{{\bm{a}}}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Corollary 5.6

(Global bound on nonconforming error)
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm d}\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\Big\{A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}})^{2}+A_{\partial K}^{\flat}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|{[\![\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{K}}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+A_{K}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|{\nabla(u_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k+1}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{\partial K})){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}}^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(48)

where the constant
C_{\rm d}
depends on the mesh shape-regularity and on
\max_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\chi_{{\bm{a}}}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Proof.

Using that
u_{c}|_{K}=\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}|_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
where
\mathcal{V}_{K}
is the set of vertices of
K
,
the partition-of-unity property (
42
), and the triangle inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\|{\nabla(u_{c}-u_{K})}\|_{{K}}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}\bigg\|\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\nabla(u_{c}^{{\bm{a}}}-\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{K})\bigg\|_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}(d+1)A_{K}\|{\nabla(u^{{\bm{a}}}_{c}-\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{K})}\|_{{K}}^{2}=\sum_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}(d+1)\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}\|^{2}_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}.

Invoking Lemma
5.5
and using that

h_{F}\leq h_{K}
for all
F\in\partial K
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
completes the proof.
∎

C_{\rm d}
)
.

The constant

5.1.3
Main result

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the following error indicators:

\displaystyle\eta_{K,{\rm res}}

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|{\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})}\|_{K},

(49a)

\displaystyle\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(49b)

\displaystyle\eta_{K,{\rm tan}}

\displaystyle:=(A_{\partial K}^{\flat})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\|{[\![\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\!]_{\partial K}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{K}}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}+\|{\nabla(u_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k+1}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{\partial K})){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}}\Big\},

(49c)

\displaystyle\eta_{K,{\rm nor}}

\displaystyle:=A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\|[\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{\partial K}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}+\|{A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}({g}_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big\},

(49d)

\displaystyle O_{K,{\rm dat}}

\displaystyle:=O_{K}(f)+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}}),

(49e)

where the three data oscillation terms on the right-hand side of (
49e
) are
defined in (
36
).

Theorem 5.8

(
hp
-upper error bound)
.

Let
u
be the weak solution to (
2
), and let
\widehat{u}_{h}

be the discrete solution to (
17
).
The following holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm u}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{\eta_{K,{\rm res}}^{2}+\eta_{K,{\rm tan}}^{2}+\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2}+O_{K,{\rm dat}}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\min\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}k\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2},\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{K,{\rm nor}}^{2}\bigg)\bigg\},

(50)

where
C_{\rm u}
depends on the mesh shape-regularity and on
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}

but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Proof.

Combining the bounds from Lemma
5.1

and Lemma
5.2
gives

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\leq C\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\Big\{\eta_{K,{\rm res}}^{2}+\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2}+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}+\min\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}k\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2},\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{K,{\rm nor}}^{2}\bigg)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the constant
C
depends on
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Moreover, we can rewrite the bound of Corollary
5.6

as follows:

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}\leq C\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\Big\{\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2}+\eta_{K,{\rm tan}}^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the constant
C
depends on
\max_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\chi_{\bm{a}}(A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Combining the above two bounds and since
\max_{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\chi_{\bm{a}}(A)\leq\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)
proves that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e\|_{K}^{2}
is bounded by the right-hand side
of (
50
). Since
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.9

(Estimator without normal flux jump)
.

Notice that (
50
) implies that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})\Big\}\leq C_{\rm u}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{\eta_{K,{\rm res}}^{2}+\eta_{K,{\rm tan}}^{2}+(k+1)\eta_{K,{\rm sta}}^{2}+O_{K,{\rm dat}}^{2}\Big\}.

This upper bound does not contain the normal flux jump, which is often the dominant component
of the error estimator for
H^{1}
-conforming FEM
[
13
]
. The price to pay
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-order
p
-suboptimality for the stabilization term. Our numerical experiments
confirm that the normal flux jump term does not dominate the total error estimator.

Local lower error bound

In this section, we establish a local lower error bound. Specifically,

\eta_{K,{\rm res}}
,
\eta_{K,{\rm nor}}
,
and
\eta_{K,{\rm tan}}
, for all

in (a neighborhood of)

We do not bound the local error indicator

This is classical in a posteriori error estimates for nonconforming methods. The proof of the following result is postponed to Section

Theorem 5.10

(

-local lower error bound)

\displaystyle\eta_{K,{\rm res}}\leq{}

where

and

.

Remark 5.11

(Bound on normal flux jump)
.

We observe that the upper bound on

has only

\frac{1}{2}
-order

-order

-suboptimality (details not shown for brevity).

7.1
Proof of Lemma
5.1

We will prove the following result:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm c,1}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}(k+1)S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})

\displaystyle+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}.

(54)

The proof is split into two steps.

(i) Since
e_{c}\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\Omega)
, using (
39
) leads to

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|^{2}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}-(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d},\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}.

(55)

For the second term, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

|(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d},\nabla e_{c})|_{\Omega}\leq\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}.

(56)

Next, we focus on bounding
(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}
. Using the weak form of the PDE (
2
), adding and subtracting the term
(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and using the discrete problem (
18
) with some test function
\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0}^{k}
, we infer that

\displaystyle(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{(f,e_{c})_{K}+(g_{\rm N},e_{c})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}-(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}

\displaystyle+(A_{K}\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}+(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{w}_{K}))_{K}

\displaystyle+S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K})-(f,w_{K})_{K}-(g_{\rm N},w_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big\}.

Let us set
\widehat{w}_{h}:=\big((I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c})|_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c})|_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\big)
. This definition is meaningful
since
I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c})
is single-valued at the mesh interfaces; moreover, since
I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c})

vanishes on the boundary faces in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}
, we have
\widehat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h0}^{k}
. We observe that,
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
the definition (
10
) of the reconstruction operator gives
R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{w}_{K})=w_{K}
, and we also have
S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{w}_{K})=0
. Hence, defining
\widehat{\eta}_{K}=\big(\eta_{K},\eta_{\partial K}\big):=\big(e_{c}|_{K}-w_{K},e_{c}|_{\partial K}-w_{\partial K}\big)
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we infer that

\displaystyle(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{(f,\eta_{K})_{K}+(g_{\rm N},\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}-(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\nabla\eta_{K})_{K}

\displaystyle+(A_{K}\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}\Big\}.

Integrating by parts and using that
\eta_{\partial K^{\text{\rm D}}}=0
and that
A_{K}
is constant on
K
gives

\displaystyle(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{(f+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\eta_{K})_{K}-(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+(g_{\rm N}-A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K},\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}+(A_{K}\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}\Big\}.

(57)

(ii) Since
\eta_{\partial K}|_{F}=(e_{c}-I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c}))|_{F}
is single-valued on each interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}
,
using the local conservation property (
20
) of the HHO method gives

\displaystyle(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{(f+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})),\eta_{K})_{K}-A_{K}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}(\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(u_{K}|_{\partial K})-u_{\partial K},\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}

\displaystyle+(g_{\rm N}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\rm N}|_{\partial K}),\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}-A_{K}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{h_{K}}(\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(u_{K}|_{\partial K})-u_{\partial K},\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}

\displaystyle+(A_{K}\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}\Big\}.

Invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality leads to

\displaystyle\big|(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}\big|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bigg\{\Big(A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|f+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}\Big(A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)\|\eta_{K}\|_{K}

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{K}\frac{(k+1)^{3}}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(u_{K}|_{\partial K})-u_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{K}^{-1}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{\rm N}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\rm N}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{K}\frac{(k+1)^{3}}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(u_{K}|_{\partial K})-u_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}

\displaystyle+A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K})\|_{K}A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla e_{c}\|_{K}\bigg\}.

Using the approximation result (
7
), we infer that, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\{\Big(\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)\|\eta_{K}\|_{K}+\Big(\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K}\Big\}\leq CA_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla e_{c}\|_{\mathrm{es}(K)}\leq C\chi_{K}(A)\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\mathrm{es}(K)}.

(58)

Using the above bounds, the bound (
14
) on
\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K})\|_{K}
, the mesh shape-regularity and the triangle inequality for the residual term gives

\displaystyle\big|(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}\big|\leq{}

\displaystyle C\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle+A_{K}(k+1)S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}.

Combining (
55
) and (
56
) with the above bound completes the proof.

Proof of Lemma
5.2

We will prove the following result:

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm c,2}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})

\displaystyle+A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\Big(\|[\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{\partial K}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}^{2}+\|{A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}\|_{\Omega}.

(59)

The starting point is the identity (
57
) obtained at the end of the first step of the proof of Lemma
5.1
. However, we no longer invoke the local conservation property (
20
), but consider the jump of
A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})
across all the mesh interfaces and its value at all the Neumann boundary faces. Recalling that
\eta_{\partial K}|_{F}=(e_{c}-I^{k}_{{\rm mKM}}(e_{c}))|_{F}
is single-valued on every interface
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}
, we infer from (
57
) that

\displaystyle(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{(f+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}),\eta_{K})_{K}+(A_{K}\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K}),\nabla e_{c})_{K}

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}}\frac{1}{2}([\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{F}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{F},\eta_{\partial K})_{F}

\displaystyle-(A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\rm N}|_{\partial K}),\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}+(g_{\rm N}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\rm N}|_{\partial K}),\eta_{\partial K})_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big\}.

Invoking the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality leads to

\displaystyle\big|(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}\big|\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bigg\{\Big(A_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\|f+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}\Big(A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)\|\eta_{K}\|_{K}

\displaystyle+A_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K})\|_{K}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{K}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(A_{K}^{-1}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|[\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{F}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{F}\|_{F}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{F}

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{K}^{-1}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}({g}_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{K}^{-1}\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{\rm N}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}(g_{\rm N}|_{\partial K})\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\Big(A_{K}\frac{k+1}{h_{K}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\partial K}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}\bigg\}.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the bound (
14
) on

\|\nabla(R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})-u_{K})\|_{K}
,
the approximation result (
58
) on
\eta
, and the triangle inequality
for the residual term, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e,\nabla e_{c})_{\Omega}\big|\leq{}

\displaystyle C\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\chi_{K}(A)\Bigg\{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big\{A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)^{2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(f)+A_{K}\Delta R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K})\|_{K}^{2}+A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})

\displaystyle+A_{K}^{-1}\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{k+1}\Big)\Big(\|[\![A\nabla R_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{h})]\!]_{\partial K}{\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{K}\|_{\partial K^{\mathrm{i}}}^{2}+\|{A_{K}\nabla R_{K}^{k+1}(\widehat{u}_{K}){\cdot}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k}({g}_{\text{\rm N}}|_{\partial K})}\|_{\partial K^{\text{\rm N}}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+O_{K}(f)^{2}+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm N}})^{2}\Big\}\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla e_{c}\|_{\Omega}.

) and (

) with the above bound completes the proof.

7.3
Proof of Lemma
5.5

We will prove the following result: For all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}\|_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{\rm d}^{{\bm{a}}}\bigg\{\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{{\bm{a}}}}\!\Big\{A_{K}S_{\partial K}(\widehat{u}_{K},\widehat{u}_{K})+O_{K}(g_{\text{\rm D}})^{2}\Big\}+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}}\!\!A_{F}^{\flat}\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{k+1}\Big)\|{[\![\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\!]_{F}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{F}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}}\!\!A_{F}^{\flat}\Big(\frac{h_{F}}{k+1}\Big)\|{\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Pi_{\partial K}^{k+1}(g_{\text{\rm D}}|_{\partial K})){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}}\|_{F}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(60)

To fix the ideas, we assume that
d=3
; all the results also hold for
d=2
.

(1) First, we recall a result on the Helmholtz decomposition on simply connected domains
[
35
, Theorem 3]
.
Let
\omega
be a bounded, simply connected, Lipschitz domain and consider a partition of its boundary
\partial\omega

into two disjoint Lipschitz parts
\gamma_{\text{\rm D}}
and
\gamma_{\text{\rm N}}
. Then,
for all
{\bm{w}}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\omega)
, there exist

\displaystyle\xi\in H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\{\zeta\in H^{1}(\omega)\,|\,\zeta|_{\gamma_{\text{\rm D}}}=0\},

(61a)

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\text{\rm N}}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{\psi}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\omega)\,|\,\bm{\psi}|_{\gamma_{\text{\rm N}}}={\bm{0}}\},

(61b)

such that

A{\bm{w}}=A\nabla\xi+\textbf{curl}\,\bm{\phi}\quad\text{in }\,\omega.

(62)

Moreover, the following holds with a constant
C_{\omega}
only depending on
\omega
and its boundary partition:

\displaystyle\|{A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{w}}}\|_{\omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{A^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\xi}\|_{\omega}^{2}+\|{A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{curl}\,\bm{\phi}}\|_{\omega}^{2},

(63a)

\displaystyle\|{\nabla\bm{\phi}}\|_{\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega}\|{\textbf{curl}\,\bm{\phi}}\|_{\omega}.

(63b)

For
d=2
, one has
C_{\omega}=1
. For
d=3
, the constant
C_{\omega}
is more delicate to estimate.
However, when
\omega
is a vertex patch, estimates are available.
For an interior vertex, the associated patch is star-shaped with respect to a ball whose radius is comparable to the diameter of the patch (see
[
15
, Proposition 8.2]
). Consequently, an explicit bound on
C_{\omega}
follows from
[
34
, Corollary 29]
.
For a boundary vertex, the associated patch can be viewed as a chain of star-shaped domains. If the patch is subject only to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions,
[
34
, Theorem 35]
applies, whereas, in the presence of mixed boundary conditions, an explicit bound on
C_{\omega}
follows from
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
.

(2) We apply the Helmholtz decomposition (
62
) to
{\bm{w}}:=\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}
on the vertex star
\omega:=\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
with the boundary partition such that
\gamma_{\text{\rm D}}:=\partial\omega_{{\bm{a}}}\cap(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\text{\rm D}})
and
\gamma_{\text{\rm N}}:=\partial\omega_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}}
. This
gives

and
\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\text{\rm N}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
such that

A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}=A\nabla\xi+\textbf{curl}\,\bm{\phi}
in
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
. Notice that
H^{1}_{0,\text{\rm D}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
is indeed the functional space defined in (
44
) for
g=0
.
Notice also that the stability constant
C_{\omega}
in (
63
) only depends on the
mesh shape-regularity, since, in particular, there is only a finite number of possible partitions of the boundary of
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
and this number is bounded in terms of the mesh shape-regularity.
Taking the
L^{2}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
-inner product with
\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}}
and observing that

(A\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}e_{d}^{{\bm{a}}},\nabla\xi)_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}}=0
owing to the

,
we infer that

(3) We consider a modified
hp
-Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator
on the vertex star
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
preserving the homogeneous boundary condition on
\gamma_{\text{\rm N}}=\partial\omega_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\Gamma_{\text{\rm N}}
, say

(recall that

This operator is constructed as in the proof of Corollary

and leads to
the following local approximation result: For all
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, all

\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0,\text{\rm N}}(\omega_{{\bm{a}}})
, and all

\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)^{2}\|\bm{\phi}-I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}(\bm{\phi})\|^{2}_{K}+\Big(\frac{k}{h_{K}}\Big)\|\bm{\phi}-I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}(\bm{\phi})\|^{2}_{\partial K}+\|\nabla I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}(\bm{\phi})\|^{2}_{K}\leq C_{\rm mKM}^{\mathcal{V}_{h}}\|\nabla\bm{\phi}\|^{2}_{\omega_{{\bm{a}}}},

(64)

where the constant

is bounded in terms of the mesh
shape-regularity. Notice that the bound on

is uniform over the mesh vertices; this follows by observing that the
Karkulik–Melenk interpolation operator is itself constructed vertex-wise and that the
nodal averaging operator is uniformly stable over the mesh vertices.
Notice also that
\mathrm{es}(K)=\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
since

is locally constructed in the vertex star
\omega_{{\bm{a}}}
.
Considering the vector-valued version of the above operator

and it remains to bound

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}}({\bm{\phi}}-I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}({\bm{\phi}}),[\![\nabla_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\psi_{{\bm{a}}}u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})]\!]_{F}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{F})_{F}

\displaystyle T_{1}={}

\displaystyle-\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{i}}}\!\!({\bm{\phi}}-I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}({\bm{\phi}}),\psi_{{\bm{a}}}[\![\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}]\!]_{F}{\times}{\bm{n}}_{F})_{F}-\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{a}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm D}}\!\!({\bm{\phi}}-I^{k,{\bm{a}}}_{{\rm mKM}}({\bm{\phi}}),\psi_{{\bm{a}}}\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-g_{\text{\rm D}}){\times}{\bm{n}}_{\Omega})_{F}

\displaystyle T_{2}={}

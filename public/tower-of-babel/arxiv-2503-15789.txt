Distribution of θ − \theta- powers and their sums
Siddharth Iyer

Abstract
. We refine a remark of Steinerberger (2024), proving that for α ∈ ℝ \alpha\in\mathbb{R} , there exist integers 1 ≤ b 1 , … , b k ≤ n 1\leq b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\leq n such that ‖ ∑ j = 1 k b j − α ‖ = O ⁡ ( n − γ k ) , \left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{b_{j}}-\alpha\right\|=O(n^{-\gamma_{k}}), where γ k ≥ ( k − 1 ) / 4 \gamma_{k}\geq(k-1)/4 , γ 2 = 1 \gamma_{2}=1 , and γ k = k / 2 \gamma_{k}=k/2 for k = 2 m − 1 k=2^{m}-1 . We extend this to higher-order roots. Building on the Bambah–Chowla theorem, we study gaps in { x θ + y θ : x , y ∈ ℕ ∪ { 0 } } \{x^{\theta}+y^{\theta}:x,y\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\} , yielding a modulo one result with γ 2 = 1 \gamma_{2}=1 and bounded gaps for θ = 3 / 2 \theta=3/2 . Given ρ ⁡ ( m ) ≥ 0 \rho(m)\geq 0 with ∑ m = 1 ∞ ρ ⁡ ( m ) / m < ∞ \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\rho(m)/m<\infty , we show that the number of solutions to | ∑ j = 1 k a j θ − b | ≤ ρ ⁡ ( ‖ ( a 1 , … , a k ) ‖ ∞ ) ‖ ( a 1 , … , a k ) ‖ ∞ k , \left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}-b\right|\leq\frac{\rho\left(\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}\right)}{\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}^{k}}, in the variables ( ( a j ) j = 1 k , b ) ∈ ℕ k + 1 ((a_{j})_{j=1}^{k},b)\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1} is finite for almost all θ > 0 \theta>0 . We also identify exceptional values of θ \theta , resolving a question of Dubickas (2024), by proving the existence of a transcendental τ \tau for which ‖ n τ ‖ ≤ n v \|n^{\tau}\|\leq n^{v} has infinitely many solutions for any v ∈ ℝ v\in\mathbb{R} .

1.
Introduction

For a real number
x
we denote
\|x\|:=\min_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-m|
. Steinerberger
[
12
]
, through the use of exponential sums, has shown that for a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist integers
1\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\leq n
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle 0<\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{a_{j}}\right\|=O\left(n^{-c\cdot k^{1/3}}\right)\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

where
c
is a constant independent of
k
. Dubickas
[
4
, Theorem 3]
proved a stronger and more generalised result. They show that when
d\geq 2
is a positive integer then, there exists integers
1\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\leq n
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle 0<\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt[d]{a_{j}}\right\|=O\left(n^{-(\frac{k-2}{d})-1}\right)\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

We note that the result by Steinerberger also details the distribution modulo one property of sums of square roots, which is absent in
[
4
]
. Following the third remark in
[
12
, p. 49]
, with a change in terminology, they show that when
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist integers
1\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\leq n
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{a_{j}}-\alpha\right\|=O\left(n^{-c\cdot k^{1/3}}\right)\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

where the implied constant is independent of
\alpha
. We improve their result by replacing
O\left(n^{-ck^{1/3}}\right)
with
O\left(n^{-(\frac{k-1}{4})}\right)
in (
1.3
). More generally, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
k,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
, there exists an effective constant
C_{k,d}>0
so that for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist integers
1\leq b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\leq n
with

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt[d]{b_{j}}-\alpha\right\|\leq C_{k,d}\cdot n^{-\gamma(k,d)},

where

\gamma(k,d)=\frac{d^{\lfloor\log_{d}(k+1)\rfloor}-1}{d}\geq\frac{k-d+1}{d^{2}}.

Theorem
1.1
establishes a bound of
O(n^{-\gamma(k,2)})
on the largest gap in
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{b_{j}}\mod 1
, where
b_{j}\in 1,\ldots,n
and
n\rightarrow\infty
. This result supplements the bound of
O(n^{-2k+3/2})
for the smallest non-zero gap, established by a direct application of Qian and Wang
[
9
]
. Notably, their work
[
9
, Theorem 2]
implies the existence integers
1\leq u_{j},v_{j}\leq n
that satisfy

\displaystyle 0<\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{u_{j}}-\sqrt{v_{j}}\right|=O(n^{-2k+3/2}).

We recall for
k=1
and
d=2
, stronger and more subtle results on the distribution of gaps are known
[
10
,
6
]
. See
[
3
,
12
]
for further background in this study area.

Building upon work by Dubickas
[
4
]
, when
\theta>0
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we study the distribution of sums
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}\mod 1
. Such studies yield improvements to Theorem
1.1
by updating
\gamma(k,d)
to
\gamma^{*}(k,d)
where
\gamma^{*}(1,d)=1-\frac{1}{d}
,
\gamma^{*}(2,d)=3/2-\frac{1}{d}
and
\gamma^{*}(k,d)=\max\{3/2-\frac{1}{d},\gamma(k,d)\}
for
k\geq 3
.

Denoting
\{\cdot\}
to represent the fractional part operator, the fact
\gamma^{*}(1,d)=1-\frac{1}{d}
follows from the following result.

Lemma 1.2
.

When
0<\theta<1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist an effective constant
C_{\theta}>0
so that

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq a\leq n}\|a^{\theta}-\alpha\|\leq C_{\theta}\cdot n^{\theta-1}.

Proof.

Let
r_{\theta}(\alpha,n)
to be the unique real number in the interval

\displaystyle\left[(\lfloor n^{\theta}-1\rfloor)^{\frac{1}{\theta}},\lfloor n^{\theta}\rfloor^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right),

so that
\{r_{\theta}(\alpha,n)^{\theta}\}=\{\alpha\}
, and set
q_{\theta}(\alpha,n)=\lceil r_{\theta}(\alpha,n)\rceil
. Note that by the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq q_{\theta}(\alpha,n)^{\theta}-r_{\theta}(\alpha,n)^{\theta}\leq C_{\theta}\cdot n^{\theta-1},

for some constant
C_{\theta}>0
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

The equality
\gamma^{*}(2,d)=3/2-\frac{1}{d}
follows as a corollary of a more general theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)\cup(1,3/2)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists an effective constant
C_{\theta}>0
so that

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq u,v\leq n}\|u^{\theta}+v^{\theta}-\alpha\|\leq C_{\theta}\cdot n^{\theta-3/2}.

The above corollary emerges from an investigation of gaps between consecutive elements in the set

\displaystyle\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{\theta}:=\{u^{\theta}+v^{\theta}:\ u,v\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}

for
\theta>0
. The Bambah-Chowla Theorem tells us that when
x\geq 1
there is an element of
\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}
in the interval
[x,x+2\sqrt{2}x^{1/4}+1)

[
11
,
1
]
. A similar argument tells us that there exists an effective constant
C_{\theta}>0
so that there is an element of
\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{\theta}
in the interval
[x,x+C_{\theta}\cdot x^{1-\frac{2}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}}]
for
\theta>1
. We can improve over such an argument in the
1<\theta<2
range.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
\theta>0
and
x\geq 1
there exists a constant
D_{\theta}>0
so that there is an element of
\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{\theta}
in the interval
[x,x+D_{\theta}\cdot x^{\Psi(\theta)}]
, where

\Psi(\theta)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{3}{2\theta}&0<\theta<1\\
0&\theta=1\\
1-\frac{3}{2\theta}&1<\theta<2\\
1-\frac{2}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}&\theta\geq 2.\end{cases}

Steinerberger also establishes a lower bound on the gap between sums of square roots and integers. They show that if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{a_{j}}
is not an integer then there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{a_{j}}\right\|\geq C\cdot n^{1/2-2^{k-1}}

in
[
12
, p. 49]
. Dubickas generalises this result for higher order roots in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. They show that if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt[d]{a_{j}}
is not an integer then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt[d]{a_{j}}\right\|>(2k+1)^{1-d^{k}}\cdot n^{1/d-d^{k-1}}.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we show that for almost all
\theta>0
(in the Lebesgue measure sense), if
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\theta,\varepsilon}>0
such that if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}
is not an integer then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}\right\|\geq C_{\theta,\varepsilon}\cdot n^{-k-\varepsilon}.

Theorem 1.5
.

Given
\rho(\cdot)\geq 0
with
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\rho(m)/m<\infty
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the number of solutions to

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}-b\right|\leq\frac{\rho\left(\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}\right)}{\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}^{k}},

in the variables
\left((a_{j})_{j=1}^{k},b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
is finite for almost all
\theta>0
.

In
[
4
, Theorem 5]
, it is stated that, assuming the abc-conjecture, the inequality
0<\|n^{\theta}\|\leq n^{-1-\varepsilon}
has finitely many solutions for
n\in\mathbb{N}
when
\theta<1
is a fixed rational number. Theorem
1.5
extends this result, showing that the same holds for almost all
\theta>0
.

The exceptional set of
\theta
in Theorem
1.5
is related to a question posed by Dubickas. Specifically (with a change in terminology), they ask whether, for a given transcendental number
\tau
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a smallest
v(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle 0<\|n^{\tau}\|\leq n^{v(\tau)-\varepsilon}

has only finitely many solutions for
n\in\mathbb{N}

[
4
, p. 5]
. We prove that the answer to this question is “no”.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
be a function. The collection of
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
so that there are infinitely many solutions to
0<\|n^{\theta}\|\leq\Phi(n)
forms a dense uncountable set.

As a corollary, if we take
\Phi(n)=2^{-n}
in Theorem
1.6
we discover the existence of uncountably many transcendental numbers
\tau>0
so that
0<\|n^{\tau}\|\leq 2^{-n}
has infinitely many solutions in
n\in\mathbb{N}
. These transcendental numbers qualify for exceptional values of
\theta
in Theorem
1.5
.

It remains an open question whether there exists an analogue of the Duffin–Schaeffer theorem
[
5
,
8
]
for Theorem
1.5
.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Given
\rho(\cdot)\geq 0
with
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\rho(m)/m=\infty
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the number of solutions to

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}-b\right|\leq\frac{\rho\left(\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}\right)}{\|(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\|_{\infty}^{k}},

in the variables
\left((a_{j})_{j=1}^{k},b\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
is infinite for almost all
\theta>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Lemma 3.1
.

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{\xi}})/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension.

Proof.

Note that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d})/{\mathbb{Q}}
is Galois. Let
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
and note that the minimal polynomial
P_{t}(x)
for
\sqrt[d]{p_{t}}
over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t-1}})
divides
x^{d}-p_{t}
, a separable polynomial that splits in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}})
. If
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t-1}})\subseteq F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}})
is a field for which
P_{t}(x)
splits, then it contains
\sqrt[d]{p_{t}}
and hence
F=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}})
. Thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t-1}})

is Galois. We now observe that since we are dealing with finite extensions, by constructing automorphisms in a natural way one has

\displaystyle|\text{Aut}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},

\displaystyle\sqrt[d]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{\xi}})/\mathbb{Q})|

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{t=0}^{\xi}\left|\text{Aut}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t-1}}))\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=0}^{\xi}\left[{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{0}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{t-1}}))\right]

\displaystyle=[{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{\xi}}):\mathbb{Q}].

Hence the result follows.

For an element

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
w\in S_{\xi}^{*}
with
M(w)=n
. We have

\displaystyle\|w\|\gg n^{-d^{\xi}+1}.

Proof.

Let
g_{w}
be an integer so that
\|w\|=|w-g_{w}|
and put
w^{*}=w-g_{w}
. Observe the bound
g_{w}\ll n
. Since
w^{*}
is an algebraic integer the minimal polynomial for
w^{*}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is monic. Let this polynomial be denoted as
Q_{w}(x)
. By
[
2
, Theorem 2]
we have
w^{*}\neq 0
and thus
\text{deg}(Q_{w})\geq 2
. The same theorem tells us that
\text{deg}(Q_{w})\leq d^{\xi}
. Due to Lemma
3.1
if
v
is a root of

then there exists
\sigma\in\text{Aut}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{\xi}})/\mathbb{Q})

\sigma\in\text{Aut}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d},\sqrt[d]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[d]{p_{\xi}})/\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\left|w^{*}w_{1}\ldots w_{s-1}\right|=|Q_{w}(0)|\geq 1,

Lemma 3.4
.

Whenever
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha-\{w_{n}\}\ll n^{-d^{\xi}+1}.

Let
j\geq 1
be an integer, using Lemma
3.3
and multiplication by
-1

which satisfies

\displaystyle 0<\{x_{j}\}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{j})^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

By Lemma
3.2
, we may strengthen the above inequality to

\displaystyle\frac{A_{\xi}}{(2^{j})^{d^{\xi}-1}}\leq\{x_{j}\}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{j})^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

for some

.
Form the sequences
(y_{h})_{h=1}^{\infty}

to be the largest non-negative integer so that

\displaystyle\alpha-y_{1}\{x_{1}\}\geq 0

\alpha_{1}=\alpha-y_{1}\{x_{1}\}

\displaystyle y_{1}\leq\beta,

where

\displaystyle\beta=\left\lceil\frac{2^{d^{\xi}-1}}{A_{\xi}}\right\rceil.

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

Let

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}-y_{h+1}\{x_{h+1}\}\geq 0.

Then we set

\displaystyle\alpha_{h+1}=\alpha_{h}-y_{h+1}\{x_{h+1}\}.

In this setting, we observe that

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{h}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{h})^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

With
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
and

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{h+1}=\alpha_{h}-y_{h+1}\{x_{h+1}\}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{h})^{d^{\xi}-1}}-y_{h+1}\frac{A_{\xi}}{(2^{h+1})^{d^{\xi}-1}},

thus

\displaystyle y_{h+1}\leq\beta.

Now we observe that

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{t}=\alpha-\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}\{x_{h}\}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{t})^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

Put
\omega(t)=\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}x_{h}

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}\{x_{h}\}=\left\{\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}\{x_{h}\}\right\}=\left\{\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}x_{h}\right\}=\left\{\omega(t)\right\}.

Thus, by (
3.1

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha-\{\omega(t)\}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{t})^{d^{\xi}-1}}.

Here we bound

\displaystyle M(\omega(t))=M\left(\sum_{h=1}^{t}y_{h}x_{h}\right)\leq\sum_{h=1}^{t}\beta\cdot 2^{h}\leq\beta\cdot 2^{t+1}.

If

\displaystyle M\left(\omega\left(\left\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{n}{2\beta}\right)\right\rfloor\right)\right)\leq n

by (

), and

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha-\left\{\omega\left(\left\lfloor\log_{2}(\frac{n}{2\beta})\right\rfloor\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{\left\lfloor\log_{2}(\frac{n}{2\beta})\right\rfloor}\right)^{-d^{\xi}+1}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\ll\left(2^{\log_{2}(\frac{n}{2\beta})}\right)^{-d^{\xi}+1}\ll n^{-d^{\xi}+1}

by (
3.2

\displaystyle 0<c_{f}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt[d]{n}}{\sqrt[d]{p_{1}^{d-1}\ldots p_{\xi}^{d-1}}}\right\rfloor

so that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{f\in P_{\xi}^{*}}c_{f}\sqrt[d]{f}-\alpha\right\|\ll\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt[d]{n}}{\sqrt[d]{p_{1}^{d-1}\ldots p_{\xi}^{d-1}}}\right\rfloor^{-d^{\xi}+1}\ll n^{\frac{-d^{\xi}+1}{d}}

Let
1=b_{1}=\ldots=b_{k+1-d^{\xi}}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{f\in P_{\xi}^{*}}\sqrt[d]{c_{f}^{d}f}+\sum_{d=1}^{k+1-d^{\xi}}\sqrt{b_{d}}-\alpha\right\|\ll n^{\frac{-d^{\xi}+1}{d}},

which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.3

If
\theta=1
the proposition holds trivially. We consider the cases
\theta\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
and
\theta\geq 2
separately.

4.1.
The case
\theta\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)

Given
x\geq 2
, for
\theta\in(0,1)
we let
s_{\theta}(x)=\lceil(x/2)^{1/\theta}\rceil
, and for
\theta\in(1,2)
we let
s_{\theta}(x)=\lfloor(x/2)^{1/\theta}\rfloor
. Let
E_{\theta}(x)=x-2\cdot s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}
. We can easily verify the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\theta\in(0,1)
we have
E_{\theta}(x)\leq 0
and for
\theta\in(1,2)
we have
E_{\theta}(x)\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

E_{\theta}(x)\ll x^{1-1/\theta}
.

Proof.

Write
E_{\theta}(x)=2\left((x/2)^{1/\theta}\right)^{\theta}-2s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}
. By the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left|E_{\theta}(x)\right|=2\theta\cdot c_{\theta}(x)^{\theta-1}\left|(x/2)^{1/\theta}-s_{\theta}(x)\right|,

where
c_{\theta}(x)
is a quantity in the bounded closed interval with endpoints being
(x/2)^{1/\theta}
and
s_{\theta}(x)
. Hence
E_{\theta}(x)\ll\left(x^{1/\theta}\right)^{\theta-1}=x^{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

For
Q>0
let
H_{\theta,Q}(t):=(Q+t)^{\theta}+(Q-t)^{\theta}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

As a function of
t
,
H_{\theta,Q}(t)
is strictly decreasing on the interval
[0,Q]
when
\theta\in(0,1)
, conversely it is strictly increasing on the same interval when
\theta\in(1,2)
.

Proof.

Suppose
t\in(0,Q)
, we differentiate to obtain

\displaystyle H_{\theta,Q}^{\prime}(t)=\theta\left((Q+t)^{\theta-1}-(Q-t)^{\theta-1}\right).

The above quantity is negative when
\theta\in(0,1)
and positive when
\theta\in(1,2)
, finishing the proof.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Consider the function
I_{\theta,x}(t):=(s_{\theta}(x)+t)^{\theta}+(s_{\theta}(x)-t)^{\theta}
over the domain
[0,s_{\theta}(x)]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all sufficiently large
x\geq N_{\theta}
there exists a unique real number
k_{\theta}(x)\in[0,s_{\theta}(x)]
which satisfies
I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))=x
. Furthermore
k_{\theta}(x)=o(s_{\theta}(x))
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We evaluate

\displaystyle\left|I_{\theta,x}(\varepsilon\cdot s_{\theta}(x))-I_{\theta,x}(0)\right|

\displaystyle=s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}\left|(1+\varepsilon)^{\theta}+(1-\varepsilon)^{\theta}-2\right|

\displaystyle=s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}\left|H_{\theta,1}(\varepsilon)-2\right|

\displaystyle\gg x.

The above computation combined with Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
,
4.3
shows that for sufficiently large
x
there exists
k_{\theta}(x)\in[0,\varepsilon\cdot s_{\theta}(x)]
satisfying

\displaystyle I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))-I_{\theta,x}(0)=E_{\theta}(x).

The uniqueness of
k_{\theta}(x)
follows from Lemma
4.3
.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Consider the function
G_{\theta}(z):=(1+z)^{\theta}+(1-z)^{\theta}
over the domain
[0,1]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
we have

\displaystyle\left|G_{\theta}(z)-2-\theta(\theta-1)z^{2}\right|\leq 64\varepsilon\cdot z^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left|G_{\theta}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq(2\theta\left|\theta-1\right|+96\varepsilon)\cdot z

in the interval
z\in[0,\varepsilon]
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{\prime}(z)=\theta(1+z)^{\theta-1}-\theta(1-z)^{\theta-1},

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(z)=\theta(\theta-1)(1+z)^{\theta-2}+\theta(\theta-1)(1-z)^{\theta-2},

and

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=\theta(\theta-1)(\theta-2)(1+z)^{\theta-3}-\theta(\theta-1)(\theta-2)(1-z)^{\theta-3}.

In the interval
z\in[0,1/2]
we have the bound

\displaystyle|G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)|\leq 2\cdot\theta(\theta+1)(\theta+2)2^{3-\theta}\leq 384.

By the extended mean value theorem, we know that

\displaystyle G_{\theta}(z)=G_{\theta}(0)+G_{\theta}^{\prime}(0)z+\frac{G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(0)z^{2}}{2!}+\frac{G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(c_{z})z^{3}}{3!}

for some

, and thus we have

\displaystyle\left|G_{\theta}(z)-2-\theta(\theta-1)z^{2}\right|\leq 64\cdot z^{3}\leq 64\varepsilon\cdot z^{2}.

Again by the extended mean value theorem, it is written that

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{\prime}(z)=G_{\theta}^{\prime}(0)+G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(0)z+\frac{G_{\theta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(d_{z})z^{2}}{2!},

for some
d_{z}\in[0,z]

\displaystyle|G_{\theta}^{\prime}(z)|\leq 2\theta|\theta-1|z+192\cdot z^{2}\leq(2\theta|\theta-1|+192\varepsilon)z.

\sqcup

Lemma 4.6

k_{\theta}(x)\ll x^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))}{s_{\theta}^{\theta}(x)}=G_{\theta}(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}).

By Lemma
4.4
there exists a number
N_{\theta}
so that if

then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}\right|\leq\frac{\theta|\theta-1|}{128}.

•

If
\theta\in(0,1)
with Lemma

and
\varepsilon=\frac{\theta|\theta-1|}{128}

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))}{s_{\theta}^{\theta}(x)}\leq 2+\frac{\theta(\theta-1)}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}\right)^{2}.

Using
I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))=x=2s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}+E_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{-E_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}},

\displaystyle k_{\theta}(x)\ll(x^{1-1/\theta})^{1/2}(x^{1/\theta})^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}=x^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}.

•

If

, we mimic the above steps, except using the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))}{s_{\theta}^{\theta}(x)}\geq 2+\frac{\theta(\theta-1)}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}\right)^{2}.

\sqcap
\sqcup

Let

\displaystyle(s_{\theta}(x)+l_{\theta}(x))^{\theta}+(s_{\theta}(x)-l_{\theta}(x))^{\theta}

\displaystyle I_{\theta,x}(l_{\theta}(x))-I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))

\displaystyle=s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}\left(G_{\theta}(\frac{l_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)})-G_{\theta}(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)})\right)

\displaystyle\leq s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}\cdot G_{\theta}^{\prime}(m_{\theta}(x))\cdot\frac{1}{s_{\theta}(x)},

noting that in the above step, we applied the mean value theorem to the difference
G_{\theta}(\frac{l_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)})-G_{\theta}(\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)})
, and
m_{\theta}(x)
is a value between the numbers
\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}
and
\frac{l_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}
. With Lemmas
4.5
and

\displaystyle I_{\theta,x}(l_{\theta}(x))-I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))\ll s_{\theta}(x)^{\theta}\cdot\frac{k_{\theta}(x)}{s_{\theta}(x)}\cdot\frac{1}{s_{\theta}(x)}.

With

and Lemma

\displaystyle I_{\theta,x}(l_{\theta}(x))-I_{\theta,x}(k_{\theta}(x))\ll(x^{1/\theta})^{\theta-2}x^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}=x^{1-\frac{3}{2\theta}}.

Hence for

\displaystyle x\leq(s_{\theta}(x)+l_{\theta}(x))^{\theta}+(s_{\theta}(x)-l_{\theta}(x))^{\theta}=x+O(x^{1-\frac{3}{2\theta}}),

which proves the proposition for
\theta\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
.

4.2.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

Without loss of generality assume that
0<\rho(\cdot)\leq 1
and let

be real numbers. Define

\displaystyle P(m):=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}):a_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq a_{1}\ldots\leq a_{k}=m\right\},

and put
f_{\theta}:\mathbb{N}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
with

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\theta}.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
m\geq C_{\gamma}
is a sufficiently large integer,
\beta\in[\gamma,\Upsilon]
and
\omega\in P_{k}(m)
then

\displaystyle f_{\beta-\frac{1}{\log(m)}}(\omega)\leq f_{\beta}(\omega)-1

and

\displaystyle f_{\beta+\frac{1}{\log(m)}}(\omega)\geq f_{\beta}(\omega)+1.

Note that

\displaystyle f_{\beta-\frac{1}{\log(m)}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq f_{\beta}(\omega)-m^{\beta}+m^{\beta-\frac{1}{\log(m)}}

\displaystyle=f_{\beta}(\omega)-m^{\beta}+m^{\beta}\cdot e^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq f_{\beta}(\omega)-m^{\gamma}+m^{\gamma}\cdot e^{-1},

which is less than
f_{\beta}(\omega)-1
for sufficiently large

. The second inequality is derived similarly.

\sqcap
\sqcup

For
\omega\in P(m)
we let

\displaystyle D(\omega)=\left\{\theta:\ \theta\in[\gamma,\Upsilon],\ f_{\theta}(\omega)\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

and
g(\omega)=\#D(w)

Lemma 5.2
.

If
m\geq 2
and
\omega\in P(m)
then
g(\omega)\leq k\cdot m^{\Upsilon}

Proof.

As a function of
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
f_{\theta}(\omega)
is positive, furthermore it is strictly increasing, hence

\displaystyle g(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\#\left\{\theta:\ \theta\in(-\infty,\Upsilon],\ f_{\theta}(\omega)\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\theta:\ \theta\in(-\infty,\Upsilon],\ f_{\theta}(\omega)\in 1,\ldots,\lfloor f_{\Upsilon}(\omega)\rfloor\right\}

\displaystyle\leq f_{\Upsilon}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq k\cdot m^{\Upsilon}.

\sqcap
\sqcup

For
m\geq 2
we arrange the elements of
D(\omega)
as

\displaystyle d(1,\omega)<\ldots<d(g(\omega),\omega).

Lemma 5.3

If

\displaystyle d(h,\omega)\geq\log_{m}\left(\frac{m^{\gamma}+h-1}{k}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle k\cdot m^{d(h,\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq f_{d(h,\omega)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=f_{d(1,\omega)}(\omega)+h-1

\displaystyle\geq m^{\gamma}+h-1.

\sqcup

Using Lemma
5.1
, for
h\in\mathbb{N}
,

and
\omega\in P(m)

\displaystyle f_{d(h,\omega)-u_{-}(h,\omega)}(\omega)=f_{d(h,\omega)}(\omega)-m^{-k}\cdot\rho(m).

(5.1)

\displaystyle 0<u_{\pm}(h,\omega)\leq\frac{1}{\log(m)}.

Lemma 5.4

\displaystyle 0<u_{\pm}(h,\omega)\leq\frac{m^{-k-d(h,\omega)}\cdot\rho(m)}{(3-e)\log(m)}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|m^{d(h,\omega)\pm u_{\pm}(h,\omega)}-m^{d(h,\omega)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{d(h,\omega)\pm u_{\pm}(h,\omega)}(\omega)-f_{d(h,\omega)}(\omega)\right|

\displaystyle=m^{-k}\cdot\rho(m).

Implying that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left|e^{\pm\log(m)u_{\pm}(h,\omega)}-1\right|\leq m^{-k-d(h,\omega)}\cdot\rho(m).

For

(5.3)

\displaystyle|e^{x}-1|\geq|x|-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{|x|^{j}}{j!}\geq|x|-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{|x|}{j!}=(3-e)|x|.

Using (

) and (

\displaystyle(3-e)\cdot\left|\log(m)\cdot u_{\pm}(h,\omega)\right|\leq m^{-k-d(h,\omega)}\cdot\rho(m),

Put

\displaystyle U(\omega)=\{\theta:\ \theta\in[\gamma,\Upsilon],\ \|f_{\theta}(\omega)\|\leq m^{-k}\cdot\rho(m)\}.

With

Proof of Theorem
1.6

It is enough to show that when

holds for infinitely many integers. Put

\displaystyle S=\{(s_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}:s_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}},\ s_{0}=s,\ s_{n+1}\geq s_{n}\},

so that

is a subset of integer-valued sequences. Define
\phi:S\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\phi((s_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty})=\frac{r}{s}+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=0}^{d}s_{k}}.

Observe that
\phi
is injective on

and lies in the interval
[r/s,r/(s-1)]
. With
\mathbf{s}=(s_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
, we see that for each

there exists a positive number
U(\mathbf{s},h)
so that

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{s})=\frac{U(\mathbf{s},h)}{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}}+\sum_{d=h+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=0}^{d}s_{k}}.

Define the set

\displaystyle S(\Phi)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{s}:\mathbf{s}\in S,\ \forall h\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\text{ we have }\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left|2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)+\frac{1}{s_{h+1}-1}}-2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)}\right|\leq\Phi\left(2^{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}}\right)\right\},

and put

\displaystyle S^{*}(\Phi):=\left\{\phi(\mathbf{s}):\ \mathbf{s}\in S(\Phi)\right\}\setminus\{x:\ x>0,\ \exists n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}\text{ so that }\|n^{x}\|=0\}.

It is noted that
S^{*}(\Phi)
has uncountably many elements in the interval
[r/s,r/(s-1)]
, furthermore if
\theta\in S^{*}(\Phi)
has the representation

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{r}{s}+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=0}^{d}s_{k}},

then

\displaystyle\left|(2^{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}})^{\theta}-2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)+\sum_{d=h+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=h+1}^{d}s_{k}}}-2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)+\frac{1}{s_{h+1}-1}}-2^{U(\mathbf{s},h)}\right|\leq\Phi\left(2^{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}}\right).

Thus with such
\theta
we have the inequality

\displaystyle 0<\left\|\left(2^{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}}\right)^{\theta}\right\|\leq\Phi\left(2^{\prod_{k=0}^{h}s_{k}}\right)

for
h=0,1,\ldots
.

7.

A federated blockchain framework for secure and intelligent smart farming in sustainable industrial agriculture
Authors: Amar Y Jaffar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fast growth of smart farming technologies and Internet of Things (IoT) sensors has transformed the agricultural sector, but has brought some fundamental problems, such as data privacy, scalability, and trust in distributed systems. In this paper, AgriChain-FL, a federated blockchain architecture, is introduced and guarantees privacy-preserving, transparent, and energy-efficient collaboration in agricultural ecosystems. The framework integrates federated learning (FL) of decentralized model training with a hybrid Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance-Proof of Authority (PBFT-PoA) blockchain to allow aggregating data safely, auditing, and participation through incentives. The proposed system was developed based on the SmartFarm Sensor Dataset. Models are trained individually at each local farm node. The hybrid consensus protocol is more scalable, latency-reduced, and uses less energy. The experimental results indicated that AgriChain-FL was more accurate (92.4%), had a throughput of 2350 TPS, and a block latency of 1.0 s, which is also more favorable to analogous frameworks in learning and blockchain performance. Privacy leakage and energy cost were minimized by 73% and 45% respectively. AgriChain-FL is a successful balance between federated intelligence and blockchain trust that provides a privacy-conscious and sustainable smart farming platform. Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), and advanced analytics have been instrumental in transforming the digitalization of agriculture and have greatly changed the current food production and supply-chain systems. AI-based predictive analytics applied to smart farming solutions can help farmers to optimize their irrigation, fertilizer use, and pest management, and boost their yield and sustainability with less environmental impact1,2. Nevertheless, these solutions rely very much on the further gathering and sharing of heterogeneous sensor, drone, and machinery information across distributed networks. The issue of data confidentiality, ownership, and traceability becomes an issue in such systems. The decentralized and immutable ledger of blockchain technology has reached the agriculture sector to secure the integrity and transparency of data and track the provenance of products throughout the production process3. With blockchain integrated with AI-powered analytics, the stakeholders will have an opportunity to verify agricultural dealings, certify sustainability assertions, and allow responsible data sharing without centralized intermediaries4. The recent industrial projects, such as traceable agri-food supply chains and cooperative farming models based on smart contracts, demonstrate the promise of blockchain to increase the degree of data integrity as well as sustainable resource management5. However, the majority of applications are still siloed, and the traceability of the supply chain, instead of the integration of farm-level intelligence, is pursued, and the full potential of blockchain-enabled AI is not fully exploited. Despite the considerable progress made by smart agriculture platforms in the areas of automation and analytics, there are still significant challenges to ensuring multi-stakeholder cooperation. The farm data (soil conditions, weather parameters, and crop health indicators) are frequently shared among the self-sufficient sensors and personal data. The cloud-based models are centralized, and this creates vulnerabilities such as leakage of data, unauthorized access, and single-point failures, which complicate the large-scale collaborative optimization6. Besides, mistrust between farmers, cooperatives, and regulatory organizations will inhibit the exchange of data to support AI-based decision support, which will limit the generalization and scalability of the models in the long run. Existing blockchain solutions in agriculture are mainly focused on transactional transparency or product traceability, and they do not provide much support in constant data-driven intelligence. At the same time, federated learning (FL) has become a distributed paradigm allowing model training on the nodes that are decentralized without transmitting raw data7. Nevertheless, the conventional FL models do not have the trust validation, auditability, and incentive alignment that are paramount in inter-farm cooperation in an ecosystem of industrial scale. Integrating blockchain and FL will eliminate these limitations by uniting decentralized training with unalterable traceability, but robust structures designed to address the specifics of agricultural data, including unbalanced sampling of data, sensor noise, and uneven farm conditions, are not abundant8. Thus, there is a significant research gap in creating a federated blockchain system ensuring privacy-conserving, auditable, and incentive-congruent collaboration to achieve sustainable agricultural intelligence.

To overcome these shortcomings, this paper develops a Federated Blockchain Framework of Smart Agriculture (AgriChain-FL) that will merge the decentralization of blockchain and privacy-sensitive smarts of federated learning. Such integration is driven by the necessity of facilitating secure data collaboration without infringing ownership, as well as keeping the models in real-time as distributed agricultural nodes9. Blockchain is used as a trust and coordination layer, writing model updates and rewards in smart contracts, while FL makes sure that farm data is not stored in a central database, but instead is localized in each node. With this architecture, participation may be achieved in a transparent manner, hotels may not be compromised, and aggregation and incentive-based cooperation among farms, cooperatives, and agribusiness companies are possible. Federated blockchain mechanisms are directly incorporated in industries, which directly translate into the objectives of sustainable and transparent production of agriculture, and the vision of which is Industry 5.0 and SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure)10. Together with the increase in accountability of the data, AgriChain-FL supports the increase in the reproducibility of AI models within the context of vast farming networks and increases its effectiveness, as well as offering an audit trail, energy-saving, and immutable audit trails. It is this feature of blockchain and federated learning that provides the basis for the potential of safe, decentralized, and smart agricultural systems, which may prompt the development of ethical data administration, functional efficiency, and endurance.

The primary goal of this work is to design and implement AgriChain-FL, a federated blockchain system that will allow secure, transparent, and intelligent coordination of distributed agricultural settings. The model covers the most important concerns of privacy, scalability, and management of trust of smart farming networks.

Literature review and theoretical context

Smart farming is a synthesis of intensive IoT sensing and AI pipelines to maximize irrigation, fertilization, and pest management, and reduce environmental externalities. Early syntheses identified the role of big data in agriculture in revolutionizing decision support and value chains, but also pointed to fragmentation, data silos, and governance gaps as the limiting factors to collaborative analytics across farms11,12. Recent surveys focus on secure telemetry, provenance, and information maintenance for long-term assets and harvests, enabling verifiable information exchange across multiple enterprise structures13,14. These findings support architectures that protect farm information at the source, ensure traceability, and even enable multi-party model improvement. Federated learning (FL) is an algorithm-based approach that trains models at nodes and stores raw data locally to minimize the risks of exposure associated with centralized aggregation15–18. Convergence and fairness are problematic in agriculture due to non-IID sampling, intermittent connectivity, and heterogeneous hardware. The field of practical deployments, accordingly, will need strong aggregation, adaptive client selection, and compression to constrained communication, besides maintaining accuracy in skewed sensor distributions9. Surveys emphasize the three strategies of differential privacy (DP), secure aggregation, and homomorphic encryption (HE) to counter membership inference and gradient leakage in a farm environment where sensitive operational information is available19. Blockchain offers agri-food systems the services of provenance, certification, and incentive design on tamper-evident ledgers and smart contract design as programmable15,20–23. Controlled access and deterministic finality can be implemented in controlled supply chains using authorized networks, such as Hyperledger Fabric24. The recent breakthrough in smart agriculture AI- and IoT-based cybersecurity has also highlighted the importance of ensuring that devices are properly authenticated and anomalies are identified in the distributed farming ecosystem. Alyahya et al.25 have discussed a tamper-resistant authentication architecture for IoT-enabled smart agriculture, focusing on lightweight cryptographic schemes for resource-constrained environments. The system described in26 was a hybrid of the DTLS-based secure communication system for the smart container environment and was more resilient to network-layer attacks. In the same vein, Sarwar et al.27 proposed a more efficient dynamic SBPSO-based anomaly detection system for IoT networks, and Sarwar et al.28 developed an intrusion detection system that employs AI-based threat modeling of IoT infrastructure. These studies justify the introduction of effective cybersecurity measures in AI-based agricultural machinery. To enable data-driven agriculture, ledgers can notarize model updates, automate rules of participation and incentive alignment, and maintain auditability. Integrating blockchain and FL shifts trust from a centralized coordinator to verifiable protocols, at the cost of increased message latency and complexity, which must be engineered to support field-scale applications9,14. Consensus design determines throughput, latency, and energy footprint. PBFT ensures finality with bounded Byzantine faults but incurs quadratic overhead in the number of validator messages; PoA reduces overhead by validating a fixed set of validators; hybrid PBFT-PoA pipelines attempt to trade off safety and performance24,29. According to industrial research, multi-chain partitioning, credit-grouped PBFT, and policy-aware endorsement can alleviate bottlenecks without compromising auditability14,19. In agricultural FL, optimal patterns are lightweight consensus in steady-state learning, with stronger finality of milestone checkpoints, and combining DP/HE at the update layer to contain leakage without derailing convergence9,15,19. Recent literature also shows the increased convergence of blockchain, federated learning, and secure distributed intelligence in new IoT ecosystems. Ann et al.30 proposed a safe and effective renewable energy sharing model among distributed prosumers, demonstrating how blockchain can organize decentralized agents and maintain trust and transactional integrity. Dong et al.31 proposed using blockchain to protect distributed edge operations and to achieve a privacy-aware, blockchain-based task-distribution architecture in UAV communication systems. BDFL was introduced by Dong et al.32, a framework for federated learning built on blockchain to deliver smart UAVs at the edge, enabling secure model aggregation and decentralized trust management.

Conceptual and theoretical foundations

System model and operational design of agriChain-FL

The conceptual design of AgriChain-FL centers on the interaction between distributed farm nodes, a blockchain coordination layer, and a federated learning server. The farm nodes will be mini data silos, with their own IoT sensor networks to gather soil moisture, humidity, temperature, and crop production data. Each node also does not transmit raw data; instead, it trains a local model using a local dataset, and only encrypted model parameters are sent to the blockchain layer. The blockchain serves as a trust and audit layer that maintains an immutable record of transactions, model updates, version control, and rewards. The hybrid model is a PBFT-PoA consensus system used to validate updates in practice, thereby maintaining high block finality speed and low-latency performance even under high network workload. Smart contracts help regulate node admission, verify updates, and reward participants, while providing automated control over the federated learning process. The global aggregator is the federated server: it receives validated changes to the blockchain, secures the aggregation, and re-notifies the participating farm nodes of the recalculated global model. The design ensures that the collaboration among heterogeneous agricultural entities is privacy-preserving while also guaranteeing complete transparency and traceability of all learning processes.

The difference between the residual gap and Krum’s (1.6% points) is due to Krum maximizing the single most representative update, which is overly conservative in high-heterogeneity regimes; the MAD method is more accurate and robust in the agricultural non-IID regime.

The AgriChain-FL Framework

Architecture overview and functional layers

AgriChain-FL model is a structure that combines the use of federated learning, blockchain consensus, and flexible privacy protection to create a secure and intelligent farming system. It is designed as three primary functional layers, the Edge Layer, the Blockchain Coordination Layer, and the Federated Aggregation Layer, which have different computational and communication tasks. The local environmental and crop-related information at the Edge Layer is acquired with the assistance of the IoT sensors, including soil moisture, humidity, temperature, and nutrient levels. The nodes, in turn, are training a local model on the basis of such information and transferring the model changes in encrypted form instead of the data, thereby preserving the privacy at the farm level. The trust and verification are based on the Blockchain Coordination Layer. It implements a hybrid PBFTPoA consensus protocol to validate received updates, save them in immutability ledgers, and smart contracts to engage in the nodes, to allocate incentives, and to sustain reputation. The layer offers traceability and transparency with minimum latency. The top layer is the Federated Aggregation Layer since it takes verified updates via the blockchain to create the global model. Aggregation of the new model parameters is sent back to each of the participating farms, and this process continues in an effort to offer continuous and interactive learning without centralizing the information. This integrative scalability guarantees security, scalability, and energy efficiency, which offers the opportunity of real-time collaboration and privacy-aware crop intelligence maximization across a geographically spread farm, as shown in Fig. 2.

Data preprocessing, normalization, and feature selection

Data preprocessing involved cleaning, interpolation of missing readings, and synchronization of sensor timestamps. Each feature was normalized using the Min–Max scaling formula was used to standardize each feature to ensure it was comparable across nodes. To minimize redundancy, a correlation threshold of 0.85 was used to eliminate highly correlated features. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was used to reduce the features into major dimensions that accounted for 95% of the total variance. This pipeline meant that all participating farm nodes operated on standardized, representative data, resulting in improved convergence behavior and reduced communication imbalance during training.

Simulation environment and node configuration

All experiments were conducted on a distributed testbed configured to simulate a federated smart agriculture network. The system consisted of a 10-node federated system, in addition to four blockchain validator nodes connected via a 1 Gbps local area network. Each node had an Intel Xeon Silver 4314 single-core processor (2.4 GHz), an NVIDIA A100 graphics card with 40 GB VRAM, and 64 GB RAM. It contained Python 3.10, TensorFlow Federated, PySyft, and Hyperledger Fabric v2.5 to coordinate blockchain. The hybrid PBFT-PoA mechanism of consensus was running a block size of 2 MB with a mean latency of 95 milliseconds and transaction throughput of about 2,400 transactions per second. This setup is a realistic simulation at the industrial scale, with the decentralized farm nodes communicating safely and asynchronously using blockchain consensus.

Evaluation Metrics (Accuracy, F1-Score, Blockchain Throughput, Latency, Privacy Loss, Energy Consumption)

AgriChain-FL was experimentally evaluated on a combination of both algorithmic and network-level performance metrics. The model accuracy is a metric that measures the predictive ability of the global model on unknown test samples. F1-score offered an equal assessment with an imbalance in the data. Blockchain throughput is a metric of the confirmed transactions per second, denoting the scalability of the system. Latency is used to measure the end-to-end delay between submitting a model and validation within the blockchain. The level of protection the local data had was estimated by privacy loss, which is the differential privacy budget ϵ. The kilowatt-hours of energy consumed were tracked to evaluate the computational and communication efficiency of each training epoch.

In this environment, we contrasted FedAvg (no blockchain/privacy), blockchain-FL without strong screening, and suggested AgriChain-FL. Findings indicate that AgriChain-FL ensures a steady convergence and reduces the variance in the inter-client performance because (i) agglomeration is weighted, (ii) deviation-score screening, and (iii) consensus-verified provenance. Historical field farm yield data are likely to exhibit a long-tail distribution, with sample masses of high-yielding being relatively few. We experimented with the imbalance strength by downsampling high-yield samples from the chosen clients, resulting in imbalanced label distributions across nodes. Generalization across heterogeneous client distributions

In order to test the extrapolation properties of the global model outside the training distribution, we tested the final AgriChain-FL global model on three unseen client profiles that were not observed during federated training: (i) an Arid client with soil moisture always in the 10th percentile, (ii) a Humid client with relative humidity in the 90th percentile, and (iii) a Mixed-Extreme client with In these invisible distributions profiles, AgriChain-FL obtained an accuracy of 91.4, 92.7 and 90.9 globally, versus 87.2, 88.5 and 86.1 plain FedAvg (no blockchain, no robust screening). Three mechanisms explain why AgriChain-FL has been enhanced in generalization. First, the weighted FedAvg aggregation (Eq. 4) will make the global model a data-proportional aggregate of local optima rather than an average of local optima, thereby minimizing the influence of clients with majority distributions. Second, the deviation-score filter (Eq. 2021) does not update clients whose local optima are extreme outliers, which can cause the global model to overfit to one environmental regime. Third, the consensus provenance across all training rounds implies that each round’s update is not disproportionately represented, which implicitly provides regularization. These findings validate the theoretical conjecture in Corollary 1 that raising \:K and using heterogeneity-sensitive screening decreases the convergence residual at large , and hence translates directly into better out-of-distribution generalization. We infer that AgriChain-FL generates global models with sufficient distributional breadth to support a wide range of previously unexplored agricultural settings.

The model achieved a better result on every benchmark with improved global accuracy (92.4%), transaction throughput (2,350 TPS), and latency (1.0 s) with a high degree of privacy protection and scalability as compared to the current blockchain-based federated learning systems. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper introduced a novel federated blockchain scheme referred to as AgriChain-FL that can be applied to facilitate privacy-conscious, scalable, and trustworthy smart farming. The framework has the potential to yield massive advantages to the model accuracy, throughput, and latency in comparison to the existing blockchain-federated learning frameworks, in large-scale experimentation with the SmartFarm Sensor Dataset. The hybrid PBFTPoA consensus was very efficient with regard to transaction facilitation and deterministic validation. In the meantime, the adaptive differential privacy layer and homomorphic encryption layer had high data confidentiality, gradient reconstruction, and membership inference resistance. The findings affirmed the relevance of the model to enable distributed agricultural learning ecosystems that enable multi-node collaboration without the transmission of sensitive information about farms.

This framework can be extended in several research directions. To begin with, edge aggregation in gateway devices can minimize communication delays and energy consumption in bandwidth-limited rural settings. Second, integrating post-quantum cryptographic primitives with the validation of authenticated reporters into secure communication channels would add long-term resilience to counter emergent quantum threats. Third, the adaptive client selection and personalization methods may also reduce extreme non-IID conditions that prevail in different climatic areas.

Additionally, future investigations can consider interconnection with agricultural digital twin systems, in which global federated models provide predictive insights into crop development, irrigation planning, and climate adaptation evaluation. This would be improved by extending the framework to include multimodal sensing, such as UAV imagery, satellite imagery, and soil spectroscopy, enabling richer real-time agricultural decision support. AgriChain-FL provides a holistic roadmap of decentralized, insecure, and privacy-conscious agricultural intelligence. The framework provides a scaling basis for next-generation smart farming infrastructures that can enable resilient and sustainable international food systems by linking federated learning with blockchain-based trust coordination.

Semi-integral points of bounded height on toric varieties
Alec Shute and Sam Streeter

Abstract
. We prove asymptotics for semi-integral points of bounded height on toric varieties. We verify the Manin-type conjecture of Pieropan, Smeets, Tanimoto and Várilly-Alvarado for smooth and certain singular toric orbifolds upon replacing the leading constant with the one predicted by Chow, Loughran, Takloo-Bighash and Tanimoto.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14G05 (11D45, 14G10, 11D57).

1.
Introduction

This paper concerns the intersection of two highly active areas in arithmetic geometry. One is rational points of bounded height on varieties: here we have Manin’s conjecture, which predicts an asymptotic for the number of rational points of bounded height on Fano varieties. The other is semi-integral points: here we have several notions interpolating between rational and integral points, with the dual goals of better understanding integral points and of studying arithmetically special solutions to equations. Two prominent notions are
Campana points
, arising from Campana’s study of log-geometric orbifolds associated to fibrations
[
Cam05
]
, and
Darmon points
, originating in work of Darmon
[
Dar97
]
on generalised Fermat equations.

Point counting and semi-integrality were recently brought together by a conjecture of Pieropan, Smeets, Tanimoto and Várilly-Alvarado (Conjecture
2.16
), henceforth referred to as the
PSTVA conjecture
, which provides an analogue of Manin’s conjecture for Campana points on log Fano orbifolds. Along with posing the conjecture, the aforementioned authors verified it for orbifolds coming from vector group compactifications
[
PSTVA21
, Thm. 1.2]
. This followed earlier work of Browning, Van Valckenborgh and Yamagishi
[
VV12
,
BVV12
,
BY21
]
for linear orbifolds. Subsequent work of the authors of this article
[
Shu
,
Shu22
,
Str22
]
supported the exponents of the PSTVA conjecture while raising questions about the leading constant. Further counting results for Campana points were established by Pieropan and Schindler
[
PS24
]
(for split toric varieties) and Xiao
[
Xia22
]
(for compactifications of the Heisenberg group). Chow, Loughran, Takloo-Bighash and Tanimoto
[
CLTBT
]
established asymptotics for wonderful compactifications of semisimple algebraic groups and conjectured a new form for the leading constant. We henceforth refer to the PSTVA conjecture with the leading constant replaced by that of Chow, Loughran, Takloo-Bighash and Tanimoto as the
modified PSTVA conjecture
.

Before presenting our results, we briefly relate other recent developments in semi-integral points. The Brauer–Manin obstruction to local-global principles for semi-integral points, pertinent to the leading constant in the modified PSTVA conjecture, was developed by Mitankin, Nakahara and the second author
[
MNS
]
. Like the current formulation of Manin’s conjecture, the (modified) PSTVA conjecture allows for the removal of a
thin set
; the study of thin sets of Campana points was initiated by Nakahara and the second author
[
NS24
]
, where it was shown that Campana points on certain log Fano orbifolds are non-thin. In
[
Moe
]
, Moerman defined and studied generalisations of semi-integral points called
{\mathcal{M}}
-points
, generalising the link with the Hilbert property established in
[
MNS
]
and establishing an array of results on local-global properties for split toric varieties. Semi-integral points, particularly Darmon points, are closely connected to integral points on
algebraic stacks
, for which a Manin-type conjecture was proposed by Darda and Yasuda
[
DYa
]
which they proved for split toric stacks
[
DYb
]
. Lastly, we highlight the recent proof of Manin’s conjecture for integral points on toric varieties by Tim Santens
[
San
]
, which, alongside our work, almost completes the picture in the toric case.

1.1.
Results

We introduce and count
geometric semi-integral points
(Definition
2.12
), so-named as intersection multiplicity conditions are imposed relative to the geometric components of the orbifold divisor. By relating these points to ordinary semi-integral points (Corollary
2.15
), we verify the PSTVA conjecture for smooth toric orbifolds.

We generalise Batyrev and Tschinkel’s proof of Manin’s conjecture for toric varieties
[
BT98
, Cor. 7.4]
, just as Pieropan et. al.
[
PSTVA21
, Thm. 1.2]
generalise the result of Chambert-Loir and Tschinkel
[
CLT02
, Thm. 0.1]
on vector group compactifications.

Let
T
be a torus over a number field

with splitting field

. Let

be a complete regular polytopal

-invariant fan. Denote by

the associated smooth projective equivariant compactification of

with boundary divisor

. Denote by

the decomposition of

into irreducible components over

. Given

, define the

-divisor

.

Our first result is a Manin-type asymptotic for geometric semi-integral points.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
*=\cc
(respectively,
*=\dd
) and

finite, denote by

N_{\mathbf{g}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Sigma,{\mathcal{X}},\mathbf{m},*;B}}\right)
the number of geometric
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-Campana points (respectively, geometric
{{{\mathcal{O}}}}_{S}
-Darmon points) on the orbifold

of log-anticanonical height at most

-model

for a constant

Since geometric semi-integral points coincide with their non-geometric counterparts when the irreducible components of the orbifold divisor are smooth (Corollary

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}}}}\right)
be as in Theorem
1.1
. If the divisors

are smooth, then the modified PSTVA conjecture holds for the log-anticanonical height. In particular, the modified PSTVA conjecture holds for smooth toric orbifolds.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we deduce that the modified PSTVA conjecture holds whenever
X_{\Sigma}
is a split toric variety.

Corollary 1.3
.

The modified PSTVA conjecture holds for the orbifold
(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})
of Theorem
1.1
with log-anticanonical height whenever
X_{\Sigma}
is a split toric variety.

Corollary

should be compared with the main result of
[
PS24
, Thm. 1.2]

and goes via the hyperbola method.

Definition 3.1

Definition 3.2

The

of

Definition 3.3

Let

Definition 3.4

A

As a first example of a toric variety, we have projective space.

3.1.
Fans

We now review foundational results connecting toric varieties and fans.

Let
M
be a free abelian group of rank
d
with dual group
N=\Hom\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M,\mathbb{Z}}}\right)
. Write
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Denote by

the dual pairing and its natural

-linear extension to a pairing

Definition 3.8
.

A
(convex polyhedral) cone
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a subset of the form
\sigma=\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}v_{i}:\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ for all $i$}\}
, where
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
is a finite collection of vectors in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, called

of
\sigma
. (We allow the generating set to be empty, in which case we have the
zero cone

\sigma=\{0\}
.) The
dimension
of
\sigma
is the dimension of the smallest linear subspace of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
containing
\sigma
. The
dual cone

\check{\sigma}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the cone given by

\check{\sigma}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle u,v\rangle\geq 0\textrm{ for all }v\in\sigma\}.

A
face
of
\sigma
is a cone of the form

\{v\in\sigma:\langle\lambda,v\rangle=0\}

for some
\lambda\in\check{\sigma}
.
A cone is
strongly convex
if it contains no line through the origin (equivalently, if
\{0\}
is a face). A cone is
rational
if it is generated by elements in
N\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 3.9
.

A

in

is a finite set
\Sigma
of strongly convex polyhedral cones in

such that

(1)

any face of a cone in
\Sigma
is also in
\Sigma
, and

(2)

the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of both cones.

The
dimension
of
\Sigma
is the maximum dimension of its cones. We say that
\Sigma
is
complete
if
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is the union of cones from
\Sigma
and
regular
if each
\sigma\in\Sigma
is generated by part of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
. For

, we denote by

the collection of
d
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
.

Let
T
be a torus over a field

with cocharacter group

and splitting field

. Set

. Note that

acts on

, hence on

. Any fan
\Sigma
in

gives rise to a normal equivariant compactification

over

. See
[

, §1]
for details.

3.4.
Brauer elements and characters

Let us now discuss Brauer groups associated to toric orbifolds with a view towards the conjectural leading constant of Chow et. al.

Recall that the

of a

-variety

is
\Br(X)=H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. For a field

, we set

. By functoriality, we have a map
\Br(k)\rightarrow\Br(X)
, the image of which we denote by
\Br_{0}(X)
; this map is injective when
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, thus we may identify
\Br_{0}(X)
with
\Br(k)
. We also have the algebraic part
\Br_{1}(X)=\ker(\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(\overline{X}))

In our toric setup, we have the following groups, cf.
[
Lou18
, §4.3]
,
[
CLTBT
, §8.2]
:

\displaystyle\B(T)

\displaystyle=\ker(\Br_{1}(T)\rightarrow\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\Br_{1}(T_{v})),

\displaystyle\Br_{e}(T)

\displaystyle=\{{\mathcal{A}}\in\Br(T):{\mathcal{A}}(1_{T})=0\in\Br(K)\},

\displaystyle\Br(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})

\displaystyle=\{{\mathcal{A}}\in\Br(T):m_{i}\partial_{D_{i}}({\mathcal{A}})=0\text{ for all $i=1,\dots,r$}\},

\displaystyle\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})

\displaystyle=\Br(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})\cap\Br_{1}(T),

\displaystyle\Br_{e}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})

\displaystyle=\Br(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})\cap\Br_{e}(T).

Note 3.24
.

In the definition of

, we make implicit use of the pairing

(3.1)

\Br(T)\times T(K)\rightarrow\Br K,\quad({\mathcal{A}},P)\mapsto{\mathcal{A}}(P).

This is not to be confused with the pairing

coming from local version of (

) at each place

In particular, since

We are now ready to state the key result linking Brauer elements and toric characters.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K)}}\right)^{\sim}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}}}\right)\rightarrow\{0,1\},\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{v}}}\right)_{v}\mapsto\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\delta_{\mathbf{m},v}^{*}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{v}}}\right),

Definition 4.14

Definition 4.16

Proposition 4.17
.

For all

, there exists a compact open subgroup

(respectively,

Corollary 4.19

If

(respectively,

Suppose that

Proposition 4.20
.

Let

and let

be a character of

We have

(4.2)

|\widehat{H}_{\mathbf{m},v}(\delta_{v},\chi_{v};-\mathbf{s})|\leq\int_{T(K_{v})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{\delta_{v}(t_{v})\chi_{v}(t_{v})}{H_{\mathbf{m},v}(\mathbf{s},t_{v})}}}\right|\textrm{d}\mu_{v}\leq\widehat{H}_{\mathbf{m},v}(1,1;-\varepsilon).

.
∎

Proposition 4.21
.

For any

and

∎

4.2.
Tauberian theorem

Our ultimate aim is to apply Delange’s Tauberian theorem to our height zeta functions. The version we give below is a specialisation of the one given in
[
Lou18
, Thm. 3.3]
, which is based on the work of Delange
[
Del54
, Thm. III]
.

Proposition 4.22

(Tauberian theorem)
.

Suppose that there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
Z_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}(s)
is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>a
and
f^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(s)=Z_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}(s)(s-a)^{b}
can be extended to a holomorphic function on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq a
which is non-zero at

and satisfies

(4.3)

Z_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}(s)=\frac{f^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(a)}{(s-a)^{b}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{(s-a)^{b-\delta}}}}\right)\textrm{ as }s\rightarrow a

for some
\delta>0
. Then, for
\Gamma
the gamma function,

(4.4)

N_{\geom}(\Sigma,\mathbf{m},S,*;B)\sim\frac{f^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(a)}{\Gamma(b)}B^{a}(\log B)^{b-1}.

4.4.
Hecke characters

Definition 4.24
.

A

over a number field

is an automorphic character of

, i.e. a continuous homomorphism
\chi=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\chi_{v}}}\right)_{v}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}}}\right)=\mathbb{A}_{K}^{*}\rightarrow S^{1}

\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\chi_{v}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(a}}\right)=1
for all
a\in\mathbb{G}_{m}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(K}}\right)=K^{*}
.

Example 4.25
.

As a first example of a non-trivial Hecke character, we have totally imaginary powers of the
adelic absolute value map

\|\cdot\|_{K}:\mathbb{A}_{K}^{*}\rightarrow S^{1},\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x_{v}}}\right)_{v}\mapsto\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}|x_{v}|_{v}.

(We suppress ths subscript
K
when it is clear from context.) That
\|\cdot\|^{i\theta}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
defines a Hecke character follows from Artin’s product formula
[
Neu99
, Prop. III.1.3]
.
The kernel of
\|\cdot\|
is denoted by
\mathbb{G}_{m}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}}}\right)^{1}
.

Definition 4.26
.

A Hecke character
\chi
over

is

if
\chi=\|\cdot\|^{i\theta}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 4.27
.

The
Hecke

-function
associated to a Hecke character

Example 4.28
.

For

We call

Proposition 4.30
.

[

5.
Fan functions

In order to “regularise” (approximate) Fourier transforms in the height zeta function method, Batyrev and Tschinkel used the degree maps (Section
3.2
) to realise local Fourier transforms as multi-dimensional geometric series defined via the fan
\Sigma
. They showed that these functions are well approximated by the local factors of certain Hecke
L
-functions.

In this section we recall the Batyrev–Tschinkel fan functions and develop analogues for semi-integral points by excising certain terms. Let
\Sigma
be the fan of our toric variety, and let
\Sigma(1)=\bigcup_{i,w}\Sigma_{i,w}(1)
denote the decomposition into
G_{v}
-orbits for a non-archimedean place
v
of
K
. Recall (Proposition
3.16
) that the
\Sigma_{i,w}(1)
are in bijection with places
w
of
K_{i}=E^{G_{i}}
over
v
, and that the length
f_{i,w}
of
\Sigma_{i,w}(1)
equals the inertia degree of
w
over
v
.

Definition 5.1
.

Instantiate for each
(i,w)
a variable
u_{i,w}
. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma^{G_{v}}
, set
I_{v}(\sigma)=\{(i,w):\Sigma_{i,w}(1)\subset\sigma(1)\}
. Let
\mathbf{u}=(u_{i,w})_{i,w}
. Define the functions
R_{\sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})
and
Q_{\Sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})
by

\displaystyle R_{\sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{(i,w)\in I_{v}(\sigma)}\frac{u_{i,w}^{f_{i,w}}}{1-u_{i,w}^{f_{i,w}}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{u}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{G_{v}}}R_{\sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=Q_{\Sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})\prod_{i,w}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-u_{i,w}^{f_{i,w}}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}].

Note 5.2
.

Note that each factor of
R_{\sigma,v}(\mathbf{u})
is a geometric series
\frac{x^{f}}{1-x^{f}}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{rf}
.

Excising the terms
x^{rf}
,
r<m
leaves

\sum_{r=m}^{\infty}x^{rf}=\frac{x^{mf}}{1-x^{f}}=\frac{x^{mf}}{1-x^{mf}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{(m+1)f}}}\right).

Excising instead those terms
x^{rf}
with

leaves

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}x^{rmf}=\frac{x^{mf}}{1-x^{mf}}.

Definition 5.3
.

For
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{r}
, define the functions

and

\displaystyle R^{\cc}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{(i,w)\in I_{v}(\sigma)}\frac{u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}{1-u_{i,w}^{f_{i,w}}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{u}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{G_{v}}}R^{\cc}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=Q^{\cc}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})\prod_{i,w}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}].

(Note the difference in denominators between the
R^{\cc}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})
and the above sum.)

Define also the functions
R^{\dd}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})
and
Q^{\dd}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle R^{\dd}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle:=\prod_{(i,w)\in I_{v}(\sigma)}\frac{u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}{1-u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{u}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{G_{v}}}R^{\dd}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=Q^{\dd}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})\prod_{i,w}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}].

We now prove that
Q^{*}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})-1
has high degree. Fixing
i=i_{0}
, the
i_{0}
-degree

\deg_{i_{0}}(f)
of

is its degree with respect to the variables
u_{i_{0},w}
. We set

A_{\Sigma,v,i}:=\{\sigma\in\Sigma^{G_{v}}:I_{v}(\sigma)=\{(i,w_{0})\},f_{i,w_{0}}=1\},\quad A_{\Sigma,v}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}A_{\Sigma,v,i},

Proposition 5.4
.

(1)

We have
Q^{\cc}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})=1+P^{\cc}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})
,
where
P^{\cc}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}]

\deg_{i}(P^{\cc}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u}))\geq m_{i}+1

(2)

Set
\mathbf{u}^{\mathbf{m}}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{i,w}^{m_{i}}}}\right)_{i,w}

Q^{\dd}_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbf{u}}}\right)=1+P^{\dd}_{\Sigma,\geom,v}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbf{u}^{\mathbf{m}}}}\right)
,

Proof.

(1)

Fix

Using the identity
\frac{1-x^{n}}{1-x}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[x],

we obtain

N^{\cc}_{\sigma,\mathbf{m},\geom,v}(\mathbf{u})=\prod_{(i,w)\in I_{v}(\sigma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}\sum_{j=0}^{m_{i}-1}u_{i}^{jf_{i,w}}}}\right)\prod_{(i,w)\not\in I_{v}(\sigma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-u_{i,w}^{m_{i}f_{i,w}}}}\right)\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}].

Note that the

For

Proposition 6.1

Proof.

We adapt the proof of

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the product formula,
T(K)\subset T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}

[
Bou11
, p. 28]
, thus we have a morphism
T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K)\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}
. Non-canonically splitting

0\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}\rightarrow 0,

we thus obtain a splitting
T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K)
.

This splitting gives a splitting of the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K)\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K)\rightarrow T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}\rightarrow 0,

and so we obtain a non-canonical splitting of automorphic characters

(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K))^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\sim}(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1})^{\wedge}\times(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K))^{\wedge},\quad\chi\mapsto(\chi_{y},\chi_{l}).

Following
[
Bou11
, p. 109]
, denote by
\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}}
the subgroup of
(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/T(K))^{\wedge}
identified with the factor
(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K))^{\wedge}
, and denote by
{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m}}^{*}
(respectively,
{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}
) the image of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m}}^{*}T(K)}}\right)^{\wedge}
(respectively,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}T(K)}}\right)^{\wedge}
) in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}_{T}}
via this isomorphism.

Using the splitting of characters discussed above, the isomorphism
X^{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{t}
and the correct Haar measures throughout, (
6.1
) may be further re-expressed as

(6.2)

Z_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}(\mathbf{s})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{t}\vol(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K))}\int_{\mathbf{y}\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{t}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\chi\in{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}\widehat{H}(\delta_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*},\chi;-\mathbf{m}^{-1}\mathbf{s}-i\gamma_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathbf{y}))}}\right)d\mathbf{y}.

Integrals of the form (
6.2
) are the subject of a technical result in Bourqui’s exposition
[
Bou11
, Thm. 5.7]
due originally to Chambert-Loir and Tschinkel; an application of this theorem to (
6.2
) means that
Z_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}(s)(s-1)^{\rank(\Pic(X_{\Sigma}))}
can be extended to a holomorphic function on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
with value at
s=1
equal to

\frac{C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|H^{1}(G,X^{*}(\overline{T}))}}\right|}{\vol(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K))}\alpha(X_{\Sigma},-K_{X_{\Sigma}}),\quad C:=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}(s-1)^{\rank(\Pic(X_{\Sigma}))}\sum_{\chi\in{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}\widehat{H}_{\mathbf{m}}(\delta_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*},\chi;-s).

To conclude the proof via the Tauberian theorem (Theorem
4.22
), it suffices to show that this application is valid and to verify that
C
is non-zero and agrees with Conjecture
2.18
.

That the application of
[
Bou11
, Thm. 5.7]
is valid follows directly from the fact that, as in the rational points case, the regularisation is a product of
L
-functions.

In order to calculate
C
, we need only consider the subsum over
contributing characters
, i.e. those characters for which every associated Hecke character
\chi_{i}
is
m_{i}
-torsion, so that the associated regularisation is composed entirely of Dedekind zeta functions, i.e.

{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}:=\{\chi\in{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}:\chi_{i}^{m_{i}}=1\text{ for all $i=1,\dots,r$}\}\subset{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}.

When
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{1}
(the rational points case), we have
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{T,\mathbf{1},\geom}}}\right|=|A(T)|=\frac{\beta(X_{\Sigma})}{i(T)}
for
A(T)=T(\mathbb{A}_{K})/\overline{T(K)}
, which is used in the verification of the conjectural constant. Generalising, we deduce from Lemma
3.25
and the isomorphism
\Sh(T)\cong\B(T)^{\sim}

[
Lou18
, §4.2.3]
that

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}}\right|=\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})^{\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}/\Br(K)}}\right|}{|\Sh(T)|}.

The finiteness of the number of contributing characters, thus this quantity, is ensured by an appeal to global class field theory as in
[
Str22
, Proof of Lem. 5.20]
. We have

\sum_{\chi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}\widehat{H}_{\mathbf{m}}(\delta^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom},\chi;-\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\chi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{K})}\frac{\delta^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(t)\chi(t)}{H_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{s},t)}d\mu=\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{K})}\frac{\delta^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(t)}{H_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{s},t)}\sum_{\chi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}\chi(t)d\mu.

By character orthogonality, this expression is equal to the integral

(6.3)

|{\mathcal{V}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}|\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}}\frac{1}{H_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{s},t)}d\mu,

where

T(\mathbb{A}_{K})_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}:=\{t\in T(\mathbb{A}_{K}):\delta^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}(t)=\chi(t)=1\text{ for all $\chi\in{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}$}\}.

By Lemma
3.25
, we deduce that

T(\mathbb{A}_{K})_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}=T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})^{\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}},

where
\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})^{\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}=\{{\mathcal{A}}\in\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}}):\chi_{{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{T,\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}\}

Using
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|H^{1}(G,X^{*}(\overline{T}))}}\right|L(X^{*}(\overline{T}),1)=\vol\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{1}/T(K)}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Sh(T)}}\right|

[
Ono61

and reinterpreting the conjectural leading constant as in

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbf{s}\rightarrow\mathbf{1}}(\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{1})^{\rank(\Pic(X_{\Sigma}))}\int_{T(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})^{\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}^{*}}}}\frac{1}{H_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{s},t)}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{*}(X^{*}(\overline{T}),1)\lim_{S^{\prime}}\tau_{X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}},S^{\prime}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\prod_{v\in S^{\prime}}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})_{\geom}^{*}({{{\mathcal{O}}}}_{v})\cap T(K_{v})}}\right)^{\Br_{1}(X_{\Sigma},D_{\Sigma,\mathbf{m}})^{\mathbf{K}^{*}_{\mathbf{m},\geom}}}}}\right).

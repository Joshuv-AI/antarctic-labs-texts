Dynamical Horizon Segments and Spacetime Isometries
Abhay Ashtekar, Badri Krishnan

Abstract
Given a space-time ( M , g a ​ b ) (\mathrsfs{M},g_{ab}) admitting a dynamical horizon segment H \mathrsfs{H} , we show that there are stringent constraints on the Killing fields ξ a \xi^{a} that g a ​ b g_{ab} can admit in a neighborhood of H \mathrsfs{H} : Generically, ξ a \xi^{a} can only be a rotational Killing field which, furthermore, leaves each marginally trapped 2-sphere cross-section 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of H \mathrsfs{H} invariant. Finally, if ξ a \xi^{a} happens to be hypersurface orthogonal near H \mathrsfs{H} , then, not only the angular momentum but also all spin multipoles vanish on every 𝒮 \mathcal{S} ; the entire spin structure of these DHSs is indistinguishable from that of spherically symmetric DHSs!



I
Introduction

By now it is well-recognized that the use of event horizons (EHs)
\mathrsfs{E}
to characterize black holes (BHs) is untenable in
dynamical
situations (see, e.g.,
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2004
;
Visser 2014
). The most important limitation comes from the fact that EHs are teleological. In particular, they can form and grow in flat regions of space-time where nothing at all is happening,
in anticipation
of a collapse that may take place a million years into the future (see, e.g.,
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2003
;
Kehle and Unger 2024
)! For, to determine if an EH forms in a given stellar collapse, or to locate it in a space-time representing a binary BH merger, one needs to know space-time geometry all the way to the infinite future. Consequently, in the course of a simulation, EHs cannot be used to track the progenitors, nor to locate the merger; they can only be found at the end of the simulation, as an after thought. Also, their definition makes an essential use of the future boundary
\mathscr{I}^{+}
of space-time, which furthermore has to be complete
Geroch and Horowitz 1978
. This feature makes them unsuitable in the exploration of conceptual issues where it is simply inappropriate to make
a priori
global assumptions on whether the future evolution of the space-time of interest will extend all the way to include a complete
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. This is particularly true in the investigation of cosmic censorship in classical general relativity (GR)
Andersson et al. 2008
, and in explorations of the final state of a BH in quantum gravity, at the end of the evaporation process
Ashtekar 2020
;
Afshordi and et al 2024
.

Because of these limitations, a teleology-free, quasi-local notion of
dynamical horizons
was introduced two decades ago
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2002
;
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2003
;
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2004
;
Booth 2005
. Subsequently it has been analyzed extensively from a mathematical relativity perspective (see, in particular,
Booth and Fairhurst 2004
;

;
Andersson et al. 2005
;
Ashtekar and Galloway 2005
;
Andersson et al. 2008
;

;

;

;

;
Pook-Kolb et al. 2021a
;

;

;
Khera et al. 2023

;

In particular, these investigations have led to detailed expressions relating the change in the area of dynamical horizons to the flux of energy falling across them, furnished multipole moments to track the evolution of their shape and spin structure in an invariant manner, and provided a great deal of analytical and numerical control on the formation and growth of dynamical horizons. Thanks to these new inputs, a sharper notion of

In this note we will analyze the interplay between the geometry of DHSs
\mathrsfs{H}
and isometries that the space-time metric
g_{ab}
can admit in a neighborhood of
\mathrsfs{H}

This note is organized as follows. In section

IV
and

However, timelike DHSs do arise in some interesting situations such as in the Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse in classical general relativity, and also during the BH evaporation process in quantum gravity. These situations are encompassed by DHSs but not by dynamical horizons.

II.2
Multipoles of DHSs

Multipole moments are a set of numbers that provide an invariant characterization of the shape and spin structure of the MTSs
\mathcal{S}
of a DHS. These numbers change as one passes from one MTS to another and this time dependence enables one to study the evolution of the DHS geometry. For example, numerical simulations show that while the MTSs on the common DHS are highly distorted and their spin structure has a strong time dependence immediately after a common DHS forms in a BH merger, this DHS settles down to a Kerr isolated horizon segment quite quickly. However, these descriptions generally depend on coordinate choices. Using multipole moments, one can provide an invariant description of the passage to the final equilibrium state
Ashtekar et al. 2013
;
Chen et al. 2022
.

Because each MTS
\mathcal{S}
has 2-sphere topology, the metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
on it is conformally related to the
round
2-sphere metric. The conformal factor, that carries information about distortions, is completely determined by the scalar curvature
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
of
\tilde{q}_{ab}
. Therefore, the gauge invariant information about shape of
\mathcal{S}
is encoded in
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
. Information about its spin –i.e., intrinsic angular momentum– is encoded in (the exterior derivative of) the so-called ‘rotational 1-form’
Ashtekar et al. 2013

\tilde{\omega}_{a}:=-\tilde{q}_{a}{}^{b}\,\underline{k}_{c}\nabla_{b}k^{c}=\tilde{q}_{a}{}^{b}\,K_{bc}\,\hat{r}^{c}\,,

(5)

where
K_{ab}
is the extrinsic curvature of
\mathrsfs{H}
in
(\mathrsfs{M},g_{ab})
,
\hat{r}^{c}
is the unit normal to
\mathcal{S}
within
\mathrsfs{H}
, and we have chosen a rescaling of the null normals so that
k^{a}\hat{r}_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. To illustrate this encoding of angular momentum, let us consider a partial Cauchy surface that extends to spatial infinity and has an inner boundary
\mathcal{S}
, and a diffemorphism generated by a vector field
\varphi^{a}
which is a rotational Killing field on
\mathcal{S}
. Then, in the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner phase space, the horizon contribution to the Hamiltonian generating diffeomorphisms along
\varphi^{a}
–or the value of the angular momentum charge at
\mathcal{S}
– is given by
J_{\varphi}[\mathcal{S}]=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\,\oint_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\omega}_{a}\varphi^{a}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\tilde{V}
. (Using the divergence-free property of
\varphi^{a}
, it follows that
J_{\varphi}[\mathcal{S}]
depends only on the exterior derivative of
\tilde{\omega}_{a}
.) Axisymmetric DHSs arise in an axisymmetric collapse as well as in a head on collision of black holes.

With this application in mind, we will now summarize the procedure to define multipole moments of an axisymmetric DHS

on each MTS

is axisymmetric.

\displaystyle\texttt{I}_{\ell,\,m}[\mathcal{S}]\,

\displaystyle:=

(7)

\displaystyle\texttt{S}_{\ell,\,m}[\mathcal{S}]

These multipoles have a number of physically expected properties:

(i) The mass dipole and angular momentum monopole vanish identically on all MTSs.

(ii) If fields on an MTS are spherically symmetric, all multipoles except the mass monopole vanish. If they are reflection-symmetric, (as on isolated horizons of the Kerr family), then all

(iii) As pointed out above, in the standard Hamiltonian framework for partial spacelike surfaces, angular momentum

arises as the ‘horizon surface charge’ defined by the canonical transformation generated by the symmetry vector field
\varphi^{a}
. That property is carried over to all higher angular momentum multipoles as well. The
\texttt{S}_{\ell,\,0}[\mathcal{S}]
are the horizon surface charges corresponding to the canonical transformations generated by vector fields
\tilde{\epsilon}^{ab}D_{a}Y_{\ell,0}(\zeta)
(up to overall constants).

II.3
Known restrictions on isometries

In this section we will briefly summarize the results on permissible isometries in neighborhoods of DHSs that have appeared in the literature.

The first analysis
Mars and Senovilla 2003
showed that
strictly stationary spacetimes cannot contain closed trapped or closed marginally trapped surfaces
. Here the term ‘closed’ means compact and without boundary, and the phrase ‘strictly stationary space-times’ refers to space-times that admit a Killing field whose norm is
strictly
negative. As emphasized in that paper, both these conditions are indispensable for the method used in the proof. For example, even Minkowski space admits MTSs that are not closed. Similarly, there are space-times that admit null Killing fields and multitude of MTSs
Senovilla 2003
, and of course the Kerr family admits MTSs as well as a Killing field that is stationary in an asymptotic region but is not
strictly
stationary on the full space-time. From the perspective of dynamical horizons used in this paper, this result implies that a strictly stationary space-time does not admit a DHS, providing support for the intended connotation that DHSs arise only in dynamical situations. However, the result is more general in that it refers only to a 2-surfaces, not to its ‘time evolution’. In particular, it also implies that strictly stationary space-times cannot admit non-expanding horizons (NEHs) which are null and do not have a dynamical connotation.

The second set of results
Ashtekar and Galloway 2005
arose as collorolies of general theorems on uniqueness of dynamical horizons a la
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2002
;
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2003
, rather than the DHSs considered here. But the first of these results holds also for DHSs because it did not make use of the more restrictive properties of dynamical horizons. This uniqueness theorem refers to the foliation of a DHS by the MTSs: It shows that the foliation is unique. A corollary of this uniqueness is that if the space-time metric admits a Killing field
\xi^{a}
that is everywhere tangential to a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}
, then it must be tangential to each MTS
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathrsfs{H}
.

However, the subsequent constraints on permissible isometries made a crucial use of a second theorem that holds only on
regular
dynamical horizons
H
; these are spacelike, with
\Theta_{(\underline{k})}<0
everywhere, and with a
nowhere
vanishing
\mathcal{E}
. This theorem guarantees that, if the null energy condition holds, then there is no MTS which lies strictly in the past domain of dependence of a regular dynamical horizon
H

Ashtekar and Galloway 2005
. A corollary of this result was then noted: since existence of a Killing field that is everywhere transverse to a MTS
\mathcal{S}
in
H
would contradict the theorem, such Killing fields cannot exist in a neighborhood of a regular dynamical horizon.

These results considered Killing fields
\xi^{a}
that are either tangential to the given DHS or transverse to MTSs
\mathcal{S}
. But they leave open the possibility that
\xi^{a}
may be tangential to a closed subset of
\mathcal{S}
and transverse to its complement. This possibility is discussed in
Andersson et al. 2008
using of properties of the stability operator of MTSs. (For a concise discussion of the stability operator see, e.g., section IV of
Ashtekar and Krishnan 2025
). Consider a MTS
S
in a region of space-time that admits a Killing field
\xi^{a}
(
S
need not lie on a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}
). Denote by
\xi^{a}_{\perp}
the projection of
\xi^{a}
orthogonal to
S
. Suppose that
S
is stable along a direction parallel to
\xi^{a}_{\perp}
. Then
\xi^{a}
is either everywhere tangential to
S
(so that
\xi^{a}_{\perp}=0
), or everywhere transversal to it
Andersson et al. 2008
.

Taken together, these results imply that if space-time metric admits a Killing field
\xi^{a}
in a neighborhood of a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}
, it must be tangential to every MTS
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrsfs{H}
provided:

(i) the DHS is spacelike with
\Theta_{(\underline{k})}<0
;

(ii)
\mathrsfs{E}
is nowhere vanishing;

(iii) Null energy condition holds; and,

(iv) Its MTSs
\mathcal{S}
are stable.

In sections
III
and
IV
we will analyze the constraints on permissible symmetries without assuming these additional restrictions on the DHS.

Remark:
There are also other results on the interplay between symmetries and the existence of MTSs
S
that use the stability operator but do not refer to a DHS
Carrasco and Mars 2009
;
Booth et al. 2024
. However, their statements refer to partial Cauchy slices with the MTS

III
Absence of Causal Killing fields

in any neighborhood of
\mathrsfs{H}

Since the intrinsic geometry of a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}
is inherently time dependent, one might intuitively expect that the space-time metric would not admit a causal Killing field in a neighborhood of
\mathrsfs{H}
.
In this section we will show that this expectation is indeed borne out. The section is divided into two parts. In the first we consider the case when the
\xi^{a}

\xi^{a}
be a general causal vector field.

III.1
timelike Killing vectors

Proposition 1
.

If a region
\mathrsfs{R}

\mathrsfs{R}
cannot contain a complete MTS

Proof.

Let
\xi^{a}
be the Killing vector field in the region
\mathrsfs{R}

\mathrsfs{R}
contains a complete MTS
\mathcal{S}
of a DHS with null normals
(k^{a},\underline{k}^{a})
. Let
\lambda
be an affine parameter along
\xi^{a}
, and choose
\lambda=0
on
\mathcal{S}

IV
Spacelike Killing Vectors

For causal Killing fields
\xi^{a}
considered in section
III
we could conclude that, on any MTS
\mathcal{S}
, the subspace
W_{p}
of

(spanned by
\xi^{a}
and tangent vectors of
\mathcal{S}
) is either timelike or null. For spacelike Killing field
\xi^{a}

\xi^{a}
may be tangential to
\mathcal{S}
everywhere, in which case

would be spacelike and 2-dimensional. This simple possibility occurs, in particular, if the DHS is axisymmetric or spherically symmetric. By contrast, the case when (the spacelike Killing field)
\xi^{a}
is allowed to be transverse (at least on a portion of a MTS
\mathcal{S}
) is more involved because it branches into several sub-cases. For, on the (open) portion of an
\mathcal{S}
on which
\xi^{a}
is transverse,
W_{p}
would be 3-dimensional but would not be restricted to be timelike or null as before; it could also be spacelike. Also, since
\xi^{a}
could be transverse only on a portion of
\mathcal{S}
, the dimensionality of
W_{p}
can also vary from point to point. Consequently we now have several sub-cases to investigate.

Let us begin with the simplest case:
W_{p}
is 3-dimensional and is timelike, null or spacelike
everywhere
on
\mathcal{S}
. We can rule out this possibility simply by adapting the arguments used in
1
and
2
. For completeness we include a sketch of the proof. Consider as before the 3-surface
\mathfrak{H}

\mathcal{S}

\xi^{a}
.

is foliated by MTSs
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}

\xi^{a}
is a Killing field. Furthermore, the signature of
W_{p}^{(\lambda)}
is preserved along this flow. Thus, if
W_{p}
were initially spacelike/timelike/null, then so would be
\mathfrak{H}
. In the spacelike and timelike cases,
\mathfrak{H}
is a DHS. Therefore the area of the
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
must be monotonic, which contradicts our conclusion that areas are the same. In the case when
\mathfrak{H}
is null, we use the Raychaudhuri equation, along with condition 3 in Definition 2 (of a DHS), to arrive at a contradiction.

Complications arise when the signature of

\mathfrak{n}:=g_{ab}\varphi^{a}\varphi^{b}
and twist
\mathfrak{t}_{a}:=\epsilon_{abcd}\,(\nabla^{b}\varphi^{c})\,\varphi^{d}
:

\nabla_{a}\varphi_{b}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{n}}\,\big[\varphi_{[b}\nabla_{a]}\mathfrak{n})\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{abcd}\,\mathfrak{t}^{c}\varphi^{d}\big]

(12)

Using this expression of
\nabla_{b}\varphi_{a}
in the last step of (
11
) and simplifying, we have:

\texttt{S}_{\ell}[\mathcal{S}]=\frac{1}{4}\oint_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{n}}\,\big(\mathfrak{t}^{a}\partial_{a}Y_{\ell}(\zeta)\big)\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\tilde{V}.

(13)

Thus, spin moments
S_{\ell}[\mathcal{S}]
are all completely determined by the twist
\mathfrak{t}^{a}
of
\varphi^{a}
. In particular they
all vanish
if
\mathfrak{t}^{a}
vanishes on
\mathcal{S}
, i.e., if
\varphi^{a}
is hypersurface orthogonal there.

This is rather surprising because in a general axisymmetric space-time, we have a constraint only on the angular momentum dipole moment
\texttt{S}_{\ell=1}[\mathcal{S}]\,\,(\equiv\,{J}_{\varphi}[\mathcal{S}])
: as noted above, it equals the Komar integral and is conserved if vacuum equations hold in a neighborhood of
\mathrsfs{H}
. Not only are higher spin-multipoles non-zero, but they are generically
time-dependent
even when vacuum equations hold! (See Eqs. (3.13) and (3.14) of
Ashtekar et al. 2013
for expressions of the flux.) If, on the other hand,
\varphi^{a}
is hypersurface orthogonal at
\mathrsfs{H}
, then not only the fluxes, but the spin multipoles themselves vanish. An example in which this situation occurs is provided by the head-on collision of non-spinning black holes investigated in detail in
Pook-Kolb et al. 2019a
;
Pook-Kolb et al. 2019b
. At first it was rather puzzling as to why this high precision numerical simulation yielded a DHS with vanishing spin moments, even though it is not spherically symmetric. Of course, the shape multipoles
\texttt{I}_{\ell}
of such a DHS are quite different from those of a spherically symmetric DHS: while all
\texttt{I}_{\ell}
vanish except for
\ell=0
on spherical DHSs, not only are they non-zero on axisymmetric DHSs with hypersurface orthogonal
\varphi^{a}
, but they have interesting and highly non-trivial dynamics.

VI
Discussion

In this note we have summarized the interplay between DHSs and space-time isometries in their neighborhood. DHSs can be thought of as ‘world tubes’ of MTSs. In numerical simulations they are often obtained by first introducing a space-time foliation, and then locating MTSs on each (partial) Cauchy slice.

5
5

5

Typically, one locates the apparent horizon on each slice but this strategy fails to find DHSs corresponding to the progenitors after a merger. One needs advanced horizon finders that can locate MTSs also inside apparent horizons
Pook-Kolb et al. 2019c
.

However, geometrically, a DHS is simply a 3-dimensional submanifold of space-time in its own right without reference to any foliation. Therefore in this note we considered Killing fields
\xi^{a}
in neighborhoods
\mathcal{R}
of DHSs
\mathrsfs{H}
, without reference to any foliation of space-time. We found that the very presence of
\mathrsfs{H}
leads to strong restrictions on permissible
\xi^{a}
in
\mathcal{R}
. There are several results in the literature on this interplay. However, they involve additional assumptions, e.g., the requirement that the Killing field be strictly stationary
Mars and Senovilla 2003
; or that the DHS be spacelike, with strictly negative
\Theta_{\underline{k}}
and nowhere vanishing
\mathrsfs{E}=\sigma_{ab}^{(k)}\sigma^{ab}_{({k)}}+R_{ab}k^{a}k^{b}

Ashtekar and Galloway 2005
; or that the MTS under consideration is strictly stable in certain directions
Andersson et al. 2008
; or the reasoning is tied to a space-time foliation
Carrasco and Mars 2009
;
Booth et al. 2024
. In this note we focused on DHSs without reference to space-time foliations, and generalized these results by dropping additional assumptions, and including possibilities for the behavior of
\xi^{a}
at
\mathrsfs{H}
that were not considered before.

Our main result is contained in Proposition
3
of section
IV
. First, it rules out the presence Killing vector
\xi^{a}
that is Causal on
\mathrsfs{H}
. On the one hand this is what one would intuitively expect because the intrinsic geometry on any
\mathrsfs{H}
is dynamical. However, the result is not obvious because, e.g., spacelike world tubes of MTSs with growing areas can exist in presence of a null Killing field
Senovilla 2003
. It is the specific conditions in Definition 2 of DHSs that forbid the existence of such Killing fields in a neighborhood of
\mathrsfs{H}
. Thus, the absence of causal Killing fields provides some confidence that the conditions in Definition 3 are well tuned to capture the intuitive connotation one has in mind when one speaks of DHSs.

Second, Proposition
3
also puts strong restrictions on possible spacelike Killing fields
\xi^{a}
in a neighborhood
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathrsfs{H}
:

(i)
\xi^{a}
cannot be transverse to
any
entire MTS
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathrsfs{H}
;

(ii) if it is tangential to
\mathrsfs{H}
, it must be tangential to every
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathrsfs{H}
;

(iii) if it is tangential on a closed subset
\mathcal{C}
of a MTS
\mathcal{S}
and transverse on its complement
\mathcal{O}
, then the 3-flat
W_{p}
spanned by
\xi^{a}
and the tangent space
T_{\mathcal{S}}
must be null everywhere on
\mathcal{O}
, and
\mathrsfs{E}
must vanish on
\mathcal{O}
. Clearly, this possibility cannot be realized if
\mathrsfs{E}
does not vanish on open sets of any
\mathcal{S}
, as is often assumed;  and,

(iv) Even when possibility (iii) is realized, the projection
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
of
\xi^{a}
on
\mathcal{S}
is a rotational Killing field on
\mathcal{S}
if the null energy condition holds. This is in particular the case in vacuum space-times considered in black hole coalescence and scattering.

Thus, if there is a Killing field
\xi^{a}
in a neighborhood
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathrsfs{H}
, then generically each MTS
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrsfs{H}
is axisymmetric.

Hence, generically MTSs
\mathcal{S}
of a DHS in space-times admitting Killing fields are either axisymmetric or spherically symmetric.
No other symmetries are permissible. By contrast, non-expanding horizons (NEHs) also allow space-time isometries that are null. Indeed the familiar Kerr event horizons are particular cases of NEHs. These symmetries are not permissible on DHSs because their geometries are dynamical.

These results have an interesting implication for DHSs that arise in numerical simulations where DHSs are generated by time evolution of MTSs. Suppose the space-time under consideration is axisymmetric and does admit a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}
generated, e.g., by finding a MTS on each leaf of an axisymmetric foliation, i.e., one in which the rotational Killing field
\varphi^{a}
is tangential to each leaf. Now consider a different foliation such that
\varphi^{a}
is
everywhere
transverse to each leaf. If such a foliation were to generate a DHS
\mathrsfs{H}^{\prime}
then on each MTS
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathrsfs{H}^{\prime}
, the Killing field would be everywhere transversal. But this is not possible by result (ii) above. Thus, to generate a DHS, one must use a foliation to which
\varphi^{a}
is at least partially tangential.

6
6

6

Furthermore, the result by Booth et al
Booth et al. 2024
mentioned in section
II.3
implies that even when
\varphi^{a}
is tangential to any one leaf, if it fails to be tangential to the MTS on that leaf, then that MTS would not be stable. Therefore, in this case we are not guaranteed that the time evolution of that MTS would yield a DHS (although it could, since MTSs on a DHS need not be stable).

If a smoothly connected world-tube of MTSs were found using a foliation to which
\xi^{a}
is nowhere tangential, it would not constitute a DHS; at least one of the conditions in Definition 2 must is violated. In particular, the signature of the metric and/or the sign of
\Theta_{(\underline{k})}
may not be constant on the world-tube of MTSs.

Interestingly, asymptotic flatness at null infinity,
\mathscr{I}
, also imposes strong restrictions on isometries that the space-time can admit near
\mathscr{I}

Ashtekar and Schmidt 1980
. For the appropriate comparison with DHSs we need to allow the possibility that
\mathscr{I}
is incomplete, and restrict ourselves to situations in which there is gravitational radiation and/or matter flux across
\mathscr{I}
. In that case, axisymmetry and spherical symmetry are of course permissible. But there is also a possibility of a 1-dimensional isometry group with a boost symmetry, and a 2-dimensional isometry group with a boost-rotational symmetry
Bicák and Schmidt 1989
. Indeed, this second possibility is realized in the c-metric
Ashtekar and Dray 1981
which admits a
\mathscr{I}
with
S^{2}\times R
topology with non-vanishing Bondi news. Since it represents two accelerating black holes and there is gravitational radiation, why is this symmetry not allowed in the DHS framework? This is because in this case the solutions have ‘nodal singularities’ on 2-spheres whence they do not admit smooth DHSs.

7
7

7

At
\mathscr{I}^{+}
these nodal singularities appear only on two ‘half, generators, to the future or past of the so-called ‘bullet holes’ at which the black holes pierce
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. Therefore we do have a
S^{2}\times R
portion of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
without nodal singularities.

As specified at the end of section
I
, our results assume smooth geometries which preclude such nodal singularities (also, see Appendix A).

Finally let us summarize results of section
V
on constraints on the spin structure of DHSs that are imposed by the presence of a Killing vector field. First, because in presence of an isometry the intrinsic geometry of every MTS
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathrsfs{H}
is axisymmetric, we can use this axial Killing field
\tilde{\varphi}^{a}
to define multipoles following the procedure outlined in section
II.2
. The presence of
\tilde{\varphi}^{a}
constrains the multipoles. The first set of constraints is rather obvious:

(i) The shape multipoles
\texttt{I}_{\ell,m}
and the spin multiples
\texttt{S}_{\ell,m}
all vanish for
m\not=0
; and,

(ii) The spin dipole moment agrees with the Komar integral, whence its flux is governed entirely by matter field.

But surprisingly,
all
spin-multipoles vanish identically if
\tilde{\varphi}^{a}
is hypersurface orthogonal!
Thus, the spin structure of such a DHS is indistinguishable from that of a spherically symmetric DHS!
It would not have been surprising at all if just the spin dipole vanished in this case because it equals the Komar integral and it is well known that it vanishes if the rotational Killing field is hypersurface orthogonal. That this must be the case also for
all
higher spin multipoles was not foreseen; it would have been less surprising if, e.g.,
only the fluxes
of spin multipoles vanished, i.e., the moments were conserved. In the process of establishing this result, we also found an explicit and expression (
13
) for spin multipoles
\texttt{S}_{\ell}
in terms of the norm and the twist of the Killing field for general axisymmetric DHSs. This expression may be useful in numerical simulations of axisymmetric collapse and in phases of binary coalescence where DHSs are found to be axisymmetric within numerical accuracy.

Appendix A
Killing fields that a metric can admit on
S^{2}
.

Consider a smooth metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
on a 2-manifold that is topologically
S^{2}
. Then, as is well known,
\tilde{q}_{ab}
is conformally related to a unit, round, 2-sphere metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
:
\tilde{q}_{ab}=\tilde{\psi}^{2}\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
. We can regard the round 2-sphere as residing in the Euclidean 3-space
(R^{3},\mathring{q}_{ab})
, given by
{\rm x}^{a}{\rm x}_{a}=1
, where
{\rm x}^{a}
is the position vector in
R^{3}
and, as usual, the indices are raised and lowered using the Euclidean 3-metric
\mathring{q}_{ab}
.

8
8

8

In this Appendix, we work in
R^{3}
. In particular, abstract indices
a,b,\ldots
refer to the tangent space of
R^{3}
. Tensor fields what are intrinsic to (i.e. tangential to)
S^{2}
will carry a tilde. Thus,
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
is the metric induced on
S^{2}
by the Euclidean metric
\mathring{q}_{ab}
on
R^{3}
.

We will denote the
unit
radial vector field in
R^{3}
by
\hat{r}^{a}
. In this Appendix, (the abstract) latin indices
a,b,\ldots
refer to the tangent space of
R^{3}
and fields carrying a tilde are intrinsic to
S^{2}
, i.e., their contraction with
\hat{r}^{a}
vanish.

The round metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
is not unique: there is precisely a 3-parameter family of such unit round metrics that are themselves conformally related to one another:

\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}^{\,\prime}\,\hat{=}\,\mathring{\psi}^{2}\,\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab},\quad{\rm where}\quad\mathring{\psi}^{-1}\,\hat{=}\,{\alpha_{\circ}\,+\,\alpha_{a}\hat{r}^{a}},\quad{\rm with}\quad\alpha_{\circ}-\alpha_{a}\alpha^{a}=1

(14)

where
\hat{=}
denotes restriction to the unit
S^{2}
in
R^{3}
,
\alpha_{\circ}
is a constant and
\alpha^{a}
a constant vector field on
R^{3}
. In spherical polar coordinates on
R^{3}
we have:

\mathring{\psi}^{-1}\,\hat{=}\,{\alpha_{\circ}+\alpha_{1}\,\sin\theta\cos\phi+\alpha_{2}\,\sin\theta\sin\phi+\alpha_{3}\,\cos\theta}\,.

(15)

In this Appendix we show that if
\tilde{q}_{ab}
admits a Killing field
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
, then there exists a unit round metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}^{\,\prime}
for which
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
is a Killing field; thus
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
is a ‘rotation’. This is the assertion of Remark 1 at the end of section
IV
.

Let us begin by noting a few facts about symmetries of round 2-sphere metrics
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
. Since
(S^{2},\,\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab})
is a manifold of constant curvature, it admits six conformal Killing fields
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
, the maximum number permissible on a 2-manifold. They can be expressed as:

\tilde{\xi}^{a}\,\hat{=}\,\tilde{B}^{a}+\tilde{R}^{a}\,,\qquad{\rm with}\qquad\tilde{B}^{a}\,\hat{=}\,\mathring{\tilde{q}}^{ab}\mathring{K}_{b}\quad{\rm and}\quad\tilde{R}^{a}\,\hat{=}\,\mathring{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{ab}\mathring{L}_{b}

(16)

where
\mathring{K}^{a}
and
\mathring{L}^{a}
are
constant
vector fields on
R^{3}
, while
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
and
\mathring{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ab}
are the intrinsic metric and the area 2-form on
S^{2}
, induced by the Euclidean metric
\mathring{q}_{ab}
:

\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}\,\hat{=}\,\mathring{q}_{ab}-\hat{r}_{a}\hat{r}_{b}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\mathring{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ab}\,\hat{=}\,\,\mathring{\epsilon}_{mab}\hat{r}^{m}\,.

(17)

It is easy to check that
\tilde{R}^{a}
is a Killing vector field of
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
on
S^{2}
,  i.e.
{\mathring{\tilde{D}}}_{(a}\tilde{R}_{b)}\,\hat{=}\,0
,  and
\tilde{B}^{a}
is a conformal Killing field, i.e.,
{\mathring{\tilde{D}}}_{(a}\tilde{B}_{b)\,}\hat{=}-\mathring{K}^{a}\hat{r}_{a}
, where
{\mathring{\tilde{D}}}
is the intrinsic derivative operator on
S^{2}
compatible with
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
. Interestingly,
\tilde{B}_{b}
is a gradient,
\tilde{B}_{a}\hat{=}\mathring{\tilde{D}}_{a}(K_{c}\hat{r}^{c})
,  whence
{\mathring{\tilde{D}}}_{[a}\tilde{B}_{b]}\,\,\hat{=}\,0
. Thus, the derivative of
\tilde{R}_{a}
is purely anti-symmetric, while that of
\tilde{B}_{a}
is purely symmetric. In this sense, while
\tilde{R}^{a}
is a Killing field,
\tilde{B}^{a}
can be regarded as a ‘pure’ conformal Killing field. To summarize, there is a neat division of the conformal Killing fields
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
on
(S^{2},\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab})
into Killing fields
\tilde{R}^{a}
(one for each constant vector fields
L^{a}
in
R^{3}
) and ‘pure’ conformal Killing fields
\tilde{B}^{a}
(one for each constant vector field
K^{a}
in
R^{3}
). (This division persists on all n-manifolds of constant curvature.) On
S^{2}
, the Killing vector fields
\tilde{R}^{a}
of
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
are rotations, with closed orbits and precisely two zeros. The ‘pure’ conformal Killing fields have the interpretation of ‘boosts’ –that descends from the action of the Lorentz group on the light cone of a point in Minkowski space. (On any 2-sphere cross-section of
\mathscr{I}
that is left invariant by the action of a Lorentz subgroup of the Bondi-Metzner Sachs group, rotations and boosts assume the form of our
\tilde{R}^{a}
and
\tilde{B}^{a}
in any Bondi conformal frame.)

Let us return to the general metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
on
S^{2}
. Since it is conformally related to
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
, any Killing field
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
of
\tilde{q}_{ab}
is a conformal Killing field of
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
. Therefore it is a linear combination of a boost
\tilde{B}^{a}
and a rotation
\tilde{R}^{a}
of
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
as in (
16
). Therefore, given a Killing field
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
of
\tilde{q}_{ab}
, we have:

0\,=\,\mathrsfs{L}_{\tilde{\xi}}\,\tilde{q}_{ab}\,\hat{=}\,\big(\mathrsfs{L}_{\tilde{\xi}}\ln\tilde{\psi}-2(\mathring{K}\cdot\hat{r})\big)\,\tilde{q}_{ab}\,

(18)

whence,

\mathrsfs{L}_{\tilde{\xi}}\,\ln\tilde{\psi}\,\hat{=}2\,(\mathring{K}\cdot\hat{r})\,\quad{\hbox{\rm everywhere on $S^{2}$}}.

(19)

Since the conformal factor
\tilde{\psi}
is smooth and non-zero everywhere on
\mathcal{S}
, it follows that at points on
S^{2}
where the Killing vector
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
vanishes, the right side must also vanish, i.e. that the
zeros of
\xi^{a}
must lie on the the great circle on which
\mathring{K}\cdot\hat{r}=0
. Using the form (
16
) of
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
one can show that this is possible if and only if
\mathring{K}\cdot\mathring{L}=0
and
\mathring{K}\cdot\mathring{K}<\mathring{L}\cdot\mathring{L}
.

Let us then restrict ourselves to
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
for which
\mathring{K}^{a}
and
\mathring{L}^{a}
satisfy this condition.

9
9

9

For example, if we choose
K^{a}
to point along
z
axis then
\tilde{B}^{a}
is a
z-t
boost. In this case,
\mathring{L}^{a}
must lie in the
x-y
plane so that
\tilde{R}^{a}
is a rotation along an axis in that plane. These interpretations refer to the unit round metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
used in the expression (
16
) of
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
.

Then, the Killing vector
\tilde{\xi}^{a}
of the given metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
is a linear combination of a rotation and a boost w.r.t. the reference round metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
used in the expression (
16
). But we know that there is a 3 parameter freedom in the choice of a unit round metric on
S^{3}
. Can we choose another round metric
\mathring{\tilde{q}}^{\,\prime}_{ab}
of (
14
) such that
\xi^{a}
is a pure rotation with respect to
\mathring{\tilde{q}}^{\prime}_{ab}

\alpha_{\circ}

\mathring{\epsilon}^{abc}\,\alpha_{a}\,\mathring{L}_{b}=\alpha_{\circ}\mathring{K}^{c}\,.

(20)

This is possible precisely because
\mathring{K}\cdot\mathring{L}=0
and

\tilde{\xi}^{a}

\tilde{q}_{ab}

\mathring{\tilde{q}}^{\,\prime}_{ab}=\mathring{\psi}^{2}\,\tilde{\psi}^{-2}\tilde{q}_{ab}

Remark:

As discussed in section
VI
, the c-metric does admit a boost Killing field. On the other hand, conditions on
\mathring{K}
and
\mathring{L}
imply that a Killing field
\xi^{a}
of the physical metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
cannot be a pure boost. It is instructive to see what goes wrong if we set
\tilde{xi}^{a}=\tilde{B}^{a}
. Then, an explicit calculation shows that the solution
\tilde{\psi}
to Eq. (
19
) must diverge at the ‘poles’ where which
\tilde{B}^{a}
vanishes, violating the assumption that
\tilde{q}_{ab}
is smooth. (In the c-metric these ‘poles’ are the locations of nodal singularities of
\tilde{q}_{ab}
.) Thus, smoothness of the metric of DHSs prevents the boost to be a Killing field of
\tilde{q}_{ab}
. In particular, then, the 2-d (Abelian) boost-rotation subgroup of the conformal group of
\mathring{\tilde{q}}_{ab}
cannot be part of the isometry group in a neighborhood
\mathcal{R}
of a DHS. We only have two possible isometric groups in
\mathcal{R}
: either a single rotational Killing field (axisymmetry), or three (spherical symmetry).

Bouchet’s conjecture for cyclically 5-edge-connected, cubic signed graphs
Kathryn Nurse

Abstract
A 1983 conjecture of Bouchet states that every flow-admissible signed graph has a nowhere-zero six-flow. We prove this conjecture for cyclically five-edge-connected, cubic signed graphs.



1
Introduction

Nowhere-zero flows on signed graphs generalize nowhere-zero flows on graphs, and are motivated by embeddings of graphs in non-orientable surfaces
[
2
]
. In 1983, Bouchet conjectured the following, which is a signed graph analogue of Tutte’s famous 5-flow conjecture
[
9
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Bouchet
[
2
]
)
.

Every flow-admissible signed graph has a nowhere-zero 6-flow.

Bouchet showed that his conjecture is true with 6 replaced by 216, and demonstrated that if true it is best possible. The current best result toward Conjecture
1.1
is by DeVos, Li, Lu, Luo, Zhang, and Zhang who showed in 2021 that 6 can be replaced with 11
[
3
]
.

Many results toward this conjecture have been obtained under some connectivity assumptions, and we list some chronologically. In 1987, Khelladi showed that the 6 can be replaced with 18 under the assumption that
G
is 4-connected
[
5
]
. In 2005, Xu and Zhang proved that Bouchet’s conjecture is true for 6-edge-connected signed graphs
[
11
]
. In 2011, Raspaud and Zhu demonstrated that a flow-admissible, 4-edge-connected signed graph has a nowhere-zero 4-flow
[
7
]
. In 2014, Wu, Ye, Zang and Zhang showed that a flow-admissible, 8-edge-connected signed graph has a nowhere-zero 3-flow
[
10
]
. It is folklore that to prove Bouchet’s Conjecture, it suffices to consider cubic signed graphs (since we could find no reference, we include a proof in Section
2
for completeness). Recently, DeVos, Šámal, and this author showed that a flow-admissible, 3-edge-connected signed graph has a nowhere-zero 8-flow
[
4
,
6
]
. In this paper we give another connectivity-based result that allows vertices of degree three – we prove that Bouchet’s Conjecture holds for cyclically 5-edge-connected cubic signed graphs.

A graph is
k
-regular
when every vertex has degree
k
. A 3-regular graph is called
cubic
, and a connected, 2-regular graph is called a
cycle
. A graph is

Theorem 1.2
.

Every flow-admissible, cyclically 5-edge-connected, cubic signed graph has a nowhere-zero 6-flow.

Our process can roughly be described as follows: A flow-admissible, cyclically 5-edge-connected cubic signed graph
G
has a special nowhere-zero
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
-flow by a theorem of DeVos et al.
[
3
]
. Because
G
is cubic, without loss we may assume every vertex has at most one edge carrying flow into it, and boundary in
\{0,6\}
. Preserving these properties, we contract all positive edges. A perfect matching in the contracted graph converts the
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
-flow to a 6-flow, which yields a 6-flow in

. The assumption that the graph is cyclically 5-edge-connected is only used to find the perfect matching – we believe, by extending the method described here, that assumption could be reduced.

We cover basics in Section

, including defining signed graphs, and the folkloric result reducing Bouchet’s conjecture to the cubic case. Definitions are introduced throughout, as they are needed. In Section
3
, we provide a lemma to convert a

It is known that to prove Bouchet’s conjecture it suffices to consider cubic signed graphs, however, since we could not find a proof in the literature we provide one for completeness.

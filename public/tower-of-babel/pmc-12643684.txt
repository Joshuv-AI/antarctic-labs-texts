Chronic Diarrhea With a Unique Pathology: A Case of Mastocytic Enterocolitis
Authors: Steve Lander, Matthew Newman, Kiran Joglekar
Published: 2025

CASE REPORT

A 30-year-old woman with a medical history of depression presented to the gastrointestinal (GI) clinic for chronic vomiting, early satiety, bloating, and diarrhea with over 6 loose stools daily. Stool studies were grossly negative. The patient underwent an esophagogastroduodenoscopy which was normal. The patient also underwent a colonoscopy showing normal-appearing mucosa throughout. Random biopsies were taken showing greater than 40 mast cells per high-powered field indicating mastocytosis. This was further confirmed with positive CD117 immunostaining. Antihistamine therapy was started, and she was tested for tryptase, KIT 817v, and CD25, which were negative.1,2 The patient was started on cromolyn and has seen significant improvement in her symptoms from 8 bowel movements (BMs) per day to 2 BMs per day. The patient was off therapy for 4 months leading to a rapid increase in her BMs throughout the day.3 On restarting therapy, she again saw rapid improvement in her symptoms. Mastocytosic enterocolitis is commonly underdiagnosed due to its overlap with other GI disorders. What made this case particularly unique was the lack of other classic systemic mastocytosis features, such as skin lesions, pruritus, or flushing.4 This highlights the importance of keeping a high index of suspicion in cases of unexplained GI symptoms (Figures 1–3).

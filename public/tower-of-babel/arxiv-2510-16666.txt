Closed Neighborhood Balanced k k -Coloring of Graphs


Abstract
For a simple graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and a positive integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , a coloring of vertices of G G using exactly k k colors such that every vertex has an equal number of vertices of each color in its closed neighborhood is called closed neighborhood-balanced k k -coloring , and the graph which admits such a coloring is called closed neighborhood balanced k k -colored graph. We derive some necessary/sufficient conditions for a graph to admit a closed neighborhood balanced k k -coloring and discuss various graph operations involving such graphs. Furthermore, we prove that there is no forbidden subgraph characterization for the class of closed neighborhood-balanced k k -colorable graphs.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph.
For any vertex
v\in V
, define an
open neighborhood
of
v
as a set
N(v):=\{u:uv\in E\}
.
The members of
N(v)
are called the
neighbors
of
v
and the cardinality of
N(v)
is called the
degree
of
v
, denoted as
d(v)
.
Further, if we add the vertex
v
to its open neighborhood
N(v)
, then we get the closed neighborhood of
v
, denoted by
N[v]
.
For graph-theoretic notation, we refer to Chartrand and Lesniak
[
3
]
.

Freyberg et al.
[
6
]
introduced the concept ‘neighborhood balanced coloring’.
A
neighborhood balanced coloring
of a graph
G
is a vertex coloring of
G
using two colors, say red and green, such that each vertex has an equal number of neighbors of both colors.
It is easy to see that if a graph admits a neighborhood balanced coloring, then the degree of every vertex is even.
This notion of coloring is somewhat similar to
cordial labeling

[
2
]
.
In
[
6
]
, the authors presented several necessary/sufficient conditions for a graph to admit neighborhood balanced coloring. They further presented several graph classes that admit neighborhood balanced coloring.

Minyard et al.
[
8
]
introduced the concept of ‘neighborhood balanced
3
-coloring’ of a graph and gave a characterization of several classes of graphs that admit such a coloring.
A graph is said to admit a
neighborhood balanced
3
-coloring
if the vertices of
G
can be colored using exactly three colors such that each vertex has an equal number of neighbors of all three colors.

Almeida et al.
[
1
]
generalized this concept by introducing the notion of neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graphs, for any positive integer
k\geq 2
. A coloring of the vertices of
G
using
k
colors such that every vertex has an equal number of vertices of each color in its neighborhood is called neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring, and the graph is called neighborhood-balanced
k
-colored graph.

Motivated by the notion of neighborhood balanced colorings of graphs,
Collins et al.
[
4
]
introduced the concept of

closed neighborhood balanced coloring
.
In this setting, the vertices of a graph are colored with two colors, say red and green, such that in the closed neighborhood of every vertex, the number of red vertices equals the number of green vertices.
Collins et al.
[
4
]
established several necessary/sufficient conditions for a graph to admit a closed neighborhood balanced coloring.
They identified various classes of graphs that admit such a coloring and further investigated the behavior of closed neighborhood balanced colored
graphs under different graph operations. Moreover, they demonstrated that the class of closed neighborhood balanced colored graphs is not hereditary.

In this article, we study the generalized version of closed neighborhood balanced coloring coloring using
k
colors, called
closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
of graphs, for any positive integer
k\geq 2
.

Formally, a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of a graph is defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
If the vertices of
G
can be colored using the
k
colors, say
1,2,\dots,k
, so that every vertex has an equal number of vertices of each color in its closed neighborhood, then the coloring is called a
closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
.

A graph that admits such a coloring is called a
closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colorable graph or closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

In other words, a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graph
G
is a partition of the vertex set
V
of
G
into
k
sets
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
(that is, color classes) such that every vertex has an equal number of vertices from each set in its closed neighborhood.
Note that if
c
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graph
G
using colors in the order
(1,2,\dots,k)
, then the colorings
c=c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k}
obtained by rotating the colors in a cyclic order are also closed neighborhood-balanced
k
-colorings of
G
.
We refer to such colorings as the colorings obtained by cyclic shift of
c
.

In this article, we begin by proving the fact that the class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs is not hereditary.
Following this, we present some necessary conditions for graphs to admit a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.
We also study closed neighborhood balanced colored graphs under various graph operations.
We begin by citing the following two theorems.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
1
]

If a graph
G
admits a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring, then the degree of every vertex is a multiple of
k
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
1
]

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs. If
H
admits a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring
c
with
|V^{c}_{i}(H)|=|V^{c}_{j}(H)|
, then the lexicographic product
G[H]
admits a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring.

Results

We begin this section by deriving some necessary conditions for graphs to admit a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.
Note that if a graph admits such a coloring, then the degree of every vertex should be at least
k-1
.
We state this necessary condition in the following lemma in a more general form.

Lemma 2.1
.

If a graph
G
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, then for any vertex
v
of
G
,
d(v)\equiv-1\pmod{k}
.

The next observation talks about vertices having the same neighborhood in a graph.

Observation 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
u,v\in V(G)
such that
N(u)=N(v)
. Then
u
and
v
are of the same color in any closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
.

Observe that a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph without isolated vertices should have at least one vertex of every color.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
G
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph without isolated vertices, then the order of
G
is at least
k
.

The lower bound on the order of a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph given in the above lemma is sharp.
There are closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs of order
k
, for example, the complete graph
K_{k}
, where each of the
k
vertices receives one of the
k
colors.

The following lemma shows that if a graph
G
is closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colorable, where
k
is even, then it is also closed neighborhood-balanced
2
-colorable.
However, by Lemma
2.1
, the converse need not be true.

Lemma 2.4
.

If a graph
G
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring and
p
divides
k
, then the graph
G
also admits a closed neighborhood-balanced
p
-coloring.

Proof.

Let
f\colon V(G)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}
be a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
. Define a new coloring by replacing each color
i\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
with the color
j\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}
, where
j\equiv i(\bmod\ p)
. Since
p
divides
k
, each of the
p
colors replaces exactly the same number of original colors. Moreover, because the closed neighborhood of every vertex in
G
has an equal number of vertices in each of the original
k
color classes, it follows that the closed neighborhood of every vertex still has an equal number of vertices in each of the
p
new color classes.
∎

Using Lemma
2.4
, we can say that the complete graph
K_{n}
admits closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring if and only if
n\equiv 0(\bmod\ k)
. We now give a complete characterization of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colorable hamming graphs.
We first give a formal definition of hamming graphs.
For a set
S
, define
S^{d}:=\underbrace{S\times S\times\cdots\times S}_{d-\mathrm{times}}
to be the
d
-fold cartesian product of
S
with itself.
A
hamming
graph, denoted as
H(d,k)
, is a graph with vertex set
S^{d}
, where
|S|=k
, and two vertices are adjacent if they differ in exactly one coordinate.
Note that the hamming
H(d,k)
is
d(k-1)
-regular.
For our convenience, without loss of generality, we take
S=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The hamming graph
H(d,k)
is a closed closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph if and only if
d\equiv 1(\bmod\ k)
.

Proof.

If
d\not\equiv(\bmod\ k)
, then the degree of any vertex of
H(d,k)
is not congruent to
-1(\bmod\ k)
and hence it is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
Conversely, suppose that
d\equiv 1(\bmod\ k)
.
Write
d=kn+1
.
We show that
H(kn+1,k)
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring by induction on
n
.

For
n=1
, consider the hamming graph
H(k+1,k)
.
Each vertex of
H(k+1,k)
is represented as a
(k+1)
-tuple
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},a_{k+1})
where each
a_{i}\in S
.
For each
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\in S^{k}
, consider set
X^{1}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},y_{k+1}):y_{k+1}\in S\}
.
That is, each
X^{1}
is a collection of vertices having the same first
k
coordinates.
There are
k^{k}
such sets, each of cardinality
k
, and it gives us a partition of
S^{k+1}=V(H)
(refer to Table
).
Again for each
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1})\in S^{k-1}
, let
X^{2}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1})}:=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1},y_{k},y_{k+1}):y_{k},\ y_{k+1}\in S\}
.
That is, each
X^{2}
is a collection of vertices having the same first
k-1
coordinates.
There are
K^{k-1}
such sets, each of cardinality

, and it gives a partition of the collection of all sets of the form
X^{1}
.
Continuing in this way, for each
r=1,\dots,k
, we obtain the set
X^{r}_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1-r})}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1-r},y_{k+2-r},\dots,y_{k+1}):y_{k+2-r},\dots,y_{k+1}\in S\}
having the same first
k+1-r

Note that for each
r=1,\dots,k

and it gives us a partition of all sets of the form
X^{r-1}

).
It is straightforward to verify that for each
1\leq i\leq k
, the set

consists of
k
sets of type
X^{i-1}
.
Note that for each
1\leq j\leq k
, the tuple in the suffix of the sets of the type
X^{j}
is of length
k+1-j
.

Now we give the coloring scheme.
Consider
(1,1,\dots,1)\in S^{k+1}
.
We color the
k
vertices in
X^{1}_{(1,1,\dots,1)}
by the coloring
c^{1}
so that it is a rainbow set.
Then for
i\in S\setminus\{1\}
, we color the vertices of the form
X^{1}_{(1,1,\dots,1,i)}
by
c^{1}_{i}
obtained by
i
th cyclic shift of colors in
c^{1}
(recall that by our convention, the first cyclic shift of
c^{1}
is
c^{1}
itself).
This gives a coloring of
X^{2}_{(1,1,\dots,1)}
.
We call this coloring scheme
c^{2}
.
Again for each
i\in S\setminus\{1\}
, we color
k^{2}
vertices in
X^{2}_{(1,\dots,1,i)}
by
c^{2}_{i}
obtained by the
i
th cyclic shift of
c^{2}
.
Continuing in this way, for each
1\leq j\leq k
, we color the
k^{j}
vertices in
X^{j}_{(1,\dots,1,i)}
by
c^{j}_{i}
obtained by
i
th cyclic shift of
c^{j}
and hence we get a coloring
c^{j+1}
of
X^{j+1}_{(1,1,\dots,1)}
.
In this way, after obtaining coloring
c^{k}
for
X^{k}_{1}
, for each
i\in S\setminus\{1\}
, we apply the same coloring for
X^{k}_{i}
.
This gives us a coloring for
S^{k+1}=V(H)
.

Now we show that the above coloring is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.
For this consider an arbitrary vertex
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
of
G
.
By above partitions, we have
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})\in X^{1}_{(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})}
and since it is a rainbow set of
k

(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
has
k-1
neighbors of distinct colors in
X^{1}_{(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})}
.
Further, the vertex
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
has
k-1
neighbors, exactly one in each of the
k-1

X^{1}
sets, which are in the same

.
Continuing in this way, for each
1\leq i\leq k-1

has

X^{i+1}_{b_{1},\dots,b_{k-i}}
.
Note that, so far, the vertex
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
has exactly
k

k-1

Lastly, the vertex
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
has
k-1

X^{k}
sets.
All of these neighbors have the same color as
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
, since all
X^{k}

This ensures that the vertex
(b_{1},\dots,b_{k+1})
has an equal number of vertices in each color class in its closed neighborhood.
Therefore, we conclude that
H(k+1,k)
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
This completes the base case.

Now suppose

.
Assume that the result is true for all hamming graphs of the form

.
Consider the hamming graph
H(kn+1,k)
.
By definition,
H(kn+1,k)
contains
k

, and so on.
In particular,
H(kn+1,k)
contains

vertex-disjoint copies of
H(kn-k+1,k)
.
By the induction hypothesis, each copy of
H(kn-k+1,k)
admits a neighborhood-balanced

-coloring, say

.

Now, consider a specific copy of
H(kn,k)
in
H(kn+1,k)
.
Within this
H(kn,k)
, select one copy of
H(kn-1,k)
, then a copy of
H(kn-2,k)
, and continue down to a copy
H(kn-k+1,k)
.
Let us assume that this copy is colored using
c_{kn-k}
.
In the same copy of
H(kn-k+2,k)
, color the remaining

using the

obtained by cyclic shift of
c_{kn-k}
.
We denote the resulting coloring of this

as

.
Repeat this process: use cyclic shifts

to color the remaining
k-1
copies of
H(kn-k+2,k)
, resulting in a coloring
c_{kn-k+2}
of
H(kn-k+3,k)
.
Continue this recursive coloring until a coloring
c_{kn-1}
is obtained for
H(kn,k)
.
Apply this same coloring to each of the other
k-1

H(kn,k)
to obtain a complete coloring of
H(kn+1,k)
.

Now consider a vertex
v\in V(H(kn+1,k))
.
This vertex belongs to a specific copy of
H(kn-k+1,k)
, say
H_{v}
, which lies within some
H(kn-k+2,k)
, which in turn lies within
H(kn-k+3,k)
, and so on, up to some

.
In

, the vertex

has exactly one neighbor in each of the other

, all of which are closed neighborhood-balanced
k
-colorings.
Additionally,
v
has
k-1
neighbors, exactly one from each of the remaining
k-1

H(kn-k+1,k)
within the same
H(kn-k+2,k)

In general, for each
1\leq i\leq k-1
, the vertex

has

Lastly, since the coloring

-colored.

2.1
Non-hereditary property of the class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs

A family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs is hereditary if
G\in\mathcal{F}
and
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
together imply that
H\in\mathcal{F}
.
We know that hereditary classes can be characterized by providing a list of forbidden induced subgraphs.
Indeed, a family of graphs is hereditary if and only if it has a forbidden induced subgraph characterization.
Next, we show that the class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs is not hereditary.
That is, there is no graph that is a forbidden induced subgraph for the class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs.
Note that the results of this section are already known for the particular case
k=2
(see
[
4
]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Every graph is an induced subgraph of a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph and write
V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
.
Let
H
be a graph with the vertex set
V(H)=\displaystyle\cup_{j=1}^{k}\{v_{i}^{j}:1\leq i\leq n\}
and the edge set

\displaystyle E(H)=

\displaystyle\Big(\displaystyle\cup_{p=1}^{k}\{v_{i}^{p}v_{j}^{p}:v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}\Big)\cup\Big(\displaystyle\cup_{p,q=1,p\neq q}^{k}\{v_{i}^{p}v_{j}^{q}:v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}\Big)\cup

\displaystyle\bigg(\cup_{p,q=1,p\not=q}^{k}\{v_{i}^{p}v_{i}^{q};\ 1\leq i\leq n\}\bigg).

Note that
G
is an induced subgraph of
H
.
In graph
H
, color each vertex
v_{i}^{j}
with color
j
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
, where
1\leq j\leq k
.
Then every vertex of
H
has an equal number of neighbors of each color in its closed neighborhood and thus
H
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

The class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs is not hereditary.

2.2
Counting Results

For a graph
G
and subsets
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
, we denote the set of edges joining a vertex in
X
to a vertex in
Y
by
E(X,Y)
.
In particular, if
X=Y
, then we write
E(X)
instead of
E(X,X)
.
Also, we denote a subgraph induced by
X\subseteq V(G)
by
G\left<X\right>
.
Throughout this article, unless stated otherwise, we assume that
c
is a
k
-coloring of the graph under consideration, where
k\geq 2
, using colors
1,2,\dots,k
.
We denote the corresponding color classes as
V_{1}^{c}(G),V_{2}^{c}(G),\dots,V_{k}^{c}(G)
which form a partition of the vertex set
V(G)
into
k
disjoint subsets. If the coloring
c
and the graph
G
are clear from the context, then we simply write
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

If a graph
G
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
, then

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{k^{2}},

and

|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}-\frac{|V_{i}|}{2}.

Proof.

Consider the bipartite subgraph
H
of
G
, induced by the set
E(V_{i},V_{j})
in the bipartition
V_{i}\cup V_{j}
. Then for any
v\in V(H)
,

d_{H}(v)=\frac{(d_{G}(v)+1)}{k}.

Therefore, counting
|E(V_{i},V_{j})|
by summing degrees over each part of the bipartite graph, we get

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\frac{(d_{G}(v)+1)}{k}=\sum_{v\in V_{j}}\frac{(d_{G}(v)+1)}{k},

(1)

which implies

\sum_{v\in V_{i}}(d_{G}(v)+1)=\sum_{v\in V_{j}}(d_{G}(v)+1).

Thus,

\sum_{v\in V_{i}}d_{G}(v)+|V_{i}|=\sum_{v\in V_{j}}d_{G}(v)+|V_{j}|.

(2)

Now,

\sum_{v\in V_{1}}d_{G}(v)+\dots+\sum_{v\in V_{k}}d_{G}(v)+|V_{1}|+\dots+|V_{k}|=2|E(G)|+|V(G)|.

Using Equations (
1
) and (
2
) , we get

\displaystyle k\bigg(\sum_{v\in V_{i}}d_{G}(v)+|V_{i}|\bigg)

\displaystyle=2|E(G)|+|V(G)|,

\displaystyle k^{2}|E(V_{i},V_{j})|

\displaystyle=2|E(G)|+|V(G)|,

\displaystyle|E(V_{i},V_{j})|

\displaystyle=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{k^{2}}.

This proves the first equality. For the second equality, let
G\left<V_{i}\right>
be the subgraph induced by the set
V_{i}
. For all
v\in V_{i}
, we have

d_{G\left<V_{i}\right>}(v)=\frac{d_{G}(v)+1}{k}-1=\frac{d_{G}(v)+1-k}{k}.

Therefore, from Equation (
1
) and the first equality of the theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle 2|E(V_{i},V_{i})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}d_{G\left<V_{i}\right>}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\frac{d_{G}(v)+1-k}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\bigg[\frac{d_{G}(v)+1-k}{k}+1-1\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\bigg[\frac{d_{G}(v)+1}{k}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle=|E(V_{i},V_{j})|-|V_{i}|

\displaystyle=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{k^{2}}-|V_{i}|.

Therefore,

|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}-\frac{|V_{i}|}{2}.

This proves the second equality and completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

For any graph
G
admitting a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
, if
|V_{i}|=|V_{j}|
, then
|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=|E(V_{j},V_{j})|.

Theorem 2.10
.

If
G
is an
r
-regular graph admitting a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
, then
|V_{i}|=\frac{|V(G)|}{k}
,
|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\frac{(r+1)|V(G)|}{k^{2}}
and
|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=\frac{(r+1-k)|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}.

Proof.

Each vertex colored
i
has
\frac{r+1-k}{k}
also colored
i
and
\frac{r+1}{k}
neighbors of each of the other
k-1
colors. First, we count the edges with one endpoint colored
i
and the other endpoint colored
j
, and we get

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\frac{r+1}{k}=\bigg(\frac{r+1}{k}\bigg)|V_{i}|.

When we count the same quantity at the endpoint colored
j
, we get

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\bigg(\frac{r+1}{k}\bigg)|V_{j}|.

Thus,
|V_{i}|=|V_{j}|
and as
|V_{i}|+\dots+|V_{k}|=|V(G)|
, we have
|V_{i}|=\frac{|V(G)|}{k}
and hence

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\frac{(r+1)|V(G)|}{k^{2}}.

Further using Theorem
2.8
and
|V_{i}|
, we have

\displaystyle|E(V_{i},V_{i})|

\displaystyle=\frac{2|E(G)|+|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}-\frac{|V(G)|}{2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{r|V(G)|+|V(G)|-k|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(r+1-k)|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}.

∎

Corollary 2.11
.

If
G
is an
r
-regular closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, then either
|V(G)|=0\pmod{k^{2}}
or
r\equiv(k-1)\pmod{k^{2}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.10
, for any closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
of
G
, we have
|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=\frac{(r+1-k)|V(G)|}{2k^{2}}
, which therefore must be an integer. Thus, at least one of
|V(G)|
or
(r+1-k)
must be a multiple of
k^{2}.

∎

2.3
Closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs having unequal sizes of color classes

We know that for closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored regular graphs, the color classes are of the same size (see Corollary
2.10
).
This need not be the case in general.
In this section, we give a way to start with a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph and construct a new closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph that has fewer vertices of one color compared to the other colors.
Similar constructions for
k=2
are shown in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of a graph
G
and a vertex
z\in V(G)
with color
k
, a
(3k-2)
-vertex addition at
z
is defined as follows. We add vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}
, each adjacent to
z
, and for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
the vertex
u_{i}
is adjacent to
u_{i+1},u_{i+2},\dots,u_{k-1}
. Furthermore, we introduce vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k-1}
and
v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k-1}^{\prime}
, all of which are adjacent to
u_{k}
. Among these, for each
1\leq i\leq k-2
, the vertex
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{i+1},v_{i+2},\dots,v_{k-1}
, and similarly
v_{i}^{\prime}
is adjacent to
v_{i+1}^{\prime},v_{i+2}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k-1}^{\prime}
. Finally, we assign colors by letting
u_{i}
receive color
i
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and both
v_{i}
and
v_{i}^{\prime}
receive color
i
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

It is straightforward to verify that a graph obtained by
(3k-2)
-vertex addition as defined above from a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored is closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored.
Also, the
(3k-2)
-vertex addition adds one additional vertex of one color and two additional vertices, each of the other
k-1
colors.
Hence, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.13
.

Given a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of a graph
G
, let
G^{\prime}
be the graph resulting from a
(3k-2)
-vertex addition at a vertex
z
of
G
. Then
G^{\prime}
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph and
G^{\prime}
has one additional vertex of
z
’s color and three additional vertices of each of the other
k-1
colors.

Corollary 2.14
.

There exist closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs with closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring that have arbitrarily fewer vertices of one color than the other
k-1
colors. Moreover, every closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph is an induced subgraph of such a graph.

Proof.

Let
G
be a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph and fix a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
of
G
. Consider a vertex having color, say
1
. By Proposition
2.13
, a graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from
G
by
(3k-2)
-vertex addition is closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph and has one additional vertex of color
1
, and three additional vertices of each of the other
k-1
colors.
We again do
(3k-2)
-vertex addition at
G^{\prime}
(at a vertex having color
1
) to obtain another graph
G^{\prime\prime}
that now has two additional vertices (than
G
) of color
1
, and six additional vertices (than
G
) of other
k-1
colors.
Repeating such
(3k-2)
-vertex additions, finally, we obtain a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph that has arbitrarily fewer vertices of color
1
as compared to vertices of other
k-1
colors.
∎

We now show an example of a graph obtained by
(3k-2)
-vertex addition to a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph
K_{k}
. This example will be referred to in the next subsection when providing counterexamples to certain theorems.

Example 2.15
.

In this example, we construct a graph
H_{k}
having order
4k-2
that is a
(3k-2)
-vertex addition to
K_{k}
and is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph. Let the vertices of
K_{k}
be
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}
, and make a
(3k-2)
-vertex addition at
v_{k}
with vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}
,
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1}
and
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k-1}
. Since
K_{k}
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring graph, by Proposition
2.13
,
H_{k}
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

2.4
Operations with closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs

In this subsection, we study the closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs under various graph operations. The definitions of the graph operations are mentioned before stating the related result.

Theorem 2.16
.

A coloring
c
satisfying
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\dots=|V_{k}|
is a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring of a graph
G
if and only if it is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Suppose
c
is a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
with

|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\cdots=|V_{k}|=p.

Take any vertex
v\in V(G)
. By the definition of the coloring
c
,
|N_{G}(v)\cap V_{i}|=j
for some
j
and for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

If
v
is colored
1
, then in
\overline{G}
the vertex
v
has

p-j
neighbors in each of the color classes
V_{2},V_{3},\dots,V_{k}
,
and

p-j-1
neighbors in
V_{1}
.
Hence, for every
1\leq i\leq k
, we obtain

|N_{\overline{G}}[v]\cap V_{i}|=p-j.

A similar argument holds when
v
has color
2,3,\dots,k
. Therefore,
c
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
\overline{G}
.

Conversely, suppose
c
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
\overline{G}
with

|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\cdots=|V_{k}|=p
.
Take any vertex
v\in V(\overline{G})
. By definition,
|N_{\overline{G}}[v]\cap V_{i}|=j
, for some
j
and for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

If
v
has color
1
, then
v
has
(j-1)
neighbors of color
1
in
\overline{G}
, which implies

|N_{G}(v)\cap V_{1}|=p-j
.
Moreover,
v
has
j
neighbors in
\overline{G}
belonging to each of the color classes
V_{2},V_{3},\dots,V_{k}
, and therefore

|N_{G}(v)\cap V_{i}|=p-j
for all
2\leq i\leq k
.
A similar argument applies to vertices of color
2,3,\dots,k
. Thus
c
is an neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring of
G
.
∎

The next example shows that the hypothesis that
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\dots=|V_{k}|
is necessary. Recall the definition of
(2k-1)
-vertex addition used by Almeida et al.
[
1
]
to construct neighborhood balanced graphs having unequal sizes of color classes. We shall use this operation to construct the example we require.

Example 2.17
.

In this example, we construct a graph
M_{k}
of order
(4k-1)
, which is a neighborhood-balanced
k
-colored graph and whose complement is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph. Start with
\overline{K_{2k}}
that has a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring that has two vertices of each color. Make a
(2k-1)
-vertex addition at one of these
2k
-vertices. As shown in
[
1
]
, the resulting graph
M_{k}
is a neighborhood-balanced
k
-colored graph, and has a neighborhood-balanced
k
-coloring with three vertices of one color and four vertices each of the other
(k-1)
colors. Each vertex of
M_{k}
has a degree multiple of
k
and
(4k-1)
is not a multiple of
k
, so each vertex in
\overline{M_{k}}
has a degree not congruent to
-1\pmod{k}
and hence
\overline{M_{k}}
is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

The strong product of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\boxtimes H
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
where two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent in
G\boxtimes H
if and only if
(1)

g=g^{\prime}
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
or
(2)

h=h^{\prime}
and
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
or
(3)

gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
. We call these the first, second, and third types of edges.

Theorem 2.18
.

If
G
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, and
H
is any graph, then
G\boxtimes H
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

For each
h\in V(H)
, define

W_{h}=\{(g,h):g\in V(G)\}.

The set
W_{h}
induces a copy of
G
as a subgraph of
G\boxtimes H
. Color each
W_{h}
using the given closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
. We claim that this yields a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G\boxtimes H
.

Type one neighbors lie in
W_{h}
. Since
W_{h}
is colored with a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
, the closed neighborhood
N_{W_{h}}[(g,h)]
is balanced.

For type two and three neighbors: for each
h^{\prime}\in V(H)
with
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
, the neighbors of
(g,h)
inside
W_{h^{\prime}}
are precisely those in the closed neighborhood
N_{W_{h^{\prime}}}[(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})]
, corresponding to types two and three. As
W_{h^{\prime}}
was colored using a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
, this neighborhood is colored equally using each of the
k
colors.

Thus, for every
h^{\prime}\sim h
, all neighbors in
W_{h^{\prime}}
are colored equally using each of the
k
colors. Combining this with the neighbors from
W_{h}
, it follows that the entire closed neighborhood
N_{G\boxtimes H}[(g,h)]
is colored equally using each of the
k
colors.

Since
(g,h)
was arbitrary, the resulting coloring is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G\boxtimes H
.
∎

The cartesian product of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\square H
, is a graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
where two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent in

if and only if

and

or

and

.

.

If

is a closed neighborhood balanced

-colored graph, then

is a neighborhood-balanced

-colored graph.

Proof.

Let

. Then the vertices of

, where

and

.

Let
c
be a closed neighborhood balanced

-coloring of
G
, and let

denote its color classes. Define a coloring of

Consider the vertex

.

and

.

By Theorem
1.3
, for any two graphs

and

where

is a neighborhood-balanced

-colored graph.

Let
W_{1}=\overline{G}
and

. As
|V_{1}^{c}(H)|=|V_{2}^{c}(H)|=\dots=|V_{k}^{c}(H)|
, by Theorem

,

.

We now show with an example that the condition of equal sizes of color classes of the graph

in Theorem

and a closed neighborhood balanced

Example 2.21
.

Let
G=K_{k}
and let

be the graph defined in Example

.
Then the lexicographic product

is the join of
k

.

Denote the vertices in the

by

\{v_{1}^{r},v_{2}^{r},\dots,v_{k}^{r}\}\;\cup\;\{u_{1}^{r},u_{2}^{r},\dots,u_{k}^{r}\}\;\cup\;\{a_{1}^{r},a_{2}^{r},\dots,a_{k-1}^{r}\}\;\cup\;\{b_{1}^{r},b_{2}^{r},\dots,b_{k-1}^{r}\}.

.

-colored graph.

The join of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\vee H
, is a graph having vertex set
V(G)\cup V(H)
and edge set
E(H)\cup E(H)\cup\{gh;g\in V(G),h\in V(H)\}
.

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
G
be a closed neighborhood balanced

-colored graph with coloring

such that
|V_{1}^{c}(G)|=|V_{2}^{c}(G)|=\dots=|V_{k}^{c}(G)|
and

be a closed neighborhood balanced

-colored graph with coloring

such that
|V_{1}^{c^{\prime}}(H)|=|V_{2}^{c^{\prime}}(H)|=\dots=|V_{k}^{c^{\prime}}(H)|
. Then
G\vee H
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

In the join
G\vee H
, color each vertex of the subgraph
G
using the coloring
c
and each vertex of the subgraph
H
using the coloring
c^{\prime}
.
For this coloring of
G\vee H
, the number of vertices in each color class is equal, since

|V^{c}_{1}(G)|=|V^{c}_{2}(G)|=\cdots=|V^{c}_{k}(G)|\quad\text{and}\quad|V^{c^{\prime}}_{1}(H)|=|V^{c^{\prime}}_{2}(H)|=\cdots=|V^{c^{\prime}}_{k}(H)|.

Hence, when
G
and
H
are joined, each vertex of
G\vee H
receives the same number of additional vertices, and the proof follows.
∎

We now show that the condition on color classes being of equal size in Theorem
2.22
is necessary. We show that there exist two closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs whose join is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Example 2.23
.

Consider the join
K_{k}\vee H_{k}
, where
H_{k}
is the graph defined in Example
2.15
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
K_{k}\vee H_{k}
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, and let
c
be a corresponding closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Note that the closed neighborhood of any vertex in
K_{k}
is the entire vertex set
V(K_{k}\vee H_{k})
.
Therefore, the coloring
c
must satisfy

|V_{1}^{c}|=|V_{2}^{c}|=\cdots=|V_{k}^{c}|.

Now consider the complement. Since

\overline{K_{k}\vee H_{k}}\;=\;\overline{K_{k}}\vee\overline{H_{k}},

Theorem
2.16
implies that
c
is a neighborhood balanced coloring of
\overline{K_{k}}\vee\overline{H_{k}}
.
But in this graph, the only edges lie within
\overline{H_{k}}
.
Thus,
c
restricts to a neighborhood balanced coloring of
\overline{H_{k}}
.
However, in
\overline{H_{k}}
the degree of the vertex
u_{k}
is

\deg_{\overline{H_{k}}}(u_{k})=2k-1,

which is not congruent to
0\pmod{k}
.
This contradicts the requirement for a neighborhood balanced coloring (refer to Lemma
1.2
).

Hence,
K_{k}\vee H_{k}
cannot be a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

The direct product of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\times H
, is a graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
where two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent if and only if
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
.

Theorem 2.24
.

If
G
and
H
are both closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs, then
G\times H
is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

As
G
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, the degree of every vertex of
G
is congruent to
-1\pmod{k}
. The same is true with respect to the degree of every vertex of
H
. Let
(u,v)\in V(G\times H)
. As
u\in V(G)
,
v\in V(H)
, we have
\deg_{G}(u)\equiv-1\pmod{k}
and
\deg_{H}(v)\equiv-1\pmod{k}
. As
\deg_{G\times H}(u,v)=\deg_{G}(u)\times\deg_{G}(v)=1\pmod{k}
,
G\times H
is not a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
∎

Theorem 2.25
.

If
G
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph and
H
is a neighborhood-balanced
k
-colored graph, then
G\ \square\ H
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

Let
G
admit a closed neighborhood balanced coloring

c_{G}:V(G)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}
and let
H
admit a neighborhood balanced coloring

c_{H}:V(H)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}
. Define

c:V(G)\times V(H)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}\quad\text{by}\quad c(g,h)=\bigl(c_{G}(g)+c_{H}(h)-1\bigr)\bmod k+1.

We show that
c
is a closed neighborhood balanced coloring of
G\square H
.

Fix a vertex
(g,h)\in V(G\square H)
. Without loss of generality, assume
c_{G}(g)=k
.
Since
c_{H}
is a neighborhood balanced coloring of
H
, there exists an integer
j
such that
among the neighbors of
(g,h)
of the form
(g,h^{\prime})
, the vertex
(g,h)
has exactly
j

neighbors of each of the
k
colors.

All other neighbors of
(g,h)
are of the form
(g^{\prime},h)
. Because
c_{G}
is a closed neighborhood
balanced coloring of
G
, there exists an integer
i
such that
g
has exactly
i
neighbors
of color
k
and
i+1
neighbors of each of the other
k-1
colors in its closed neighborhood.

Now let
c_{H}(h)=q
. Then by definition of
c
,

c(g,h)=\bigl(k+q-1\bigr)\bmod k+1=q
, so
(g,h)
receives color
q
. Among neighbors of the form
(g,h^{\prime})
, it has
j
neighbors of color
q

and
j
neighbors of each other color. Among neighbors of the form
(g^{\prime},h)
, it has
i
neighbors
of color
q
and
i+1
neighbors of each of the other
k-1
colors.

Hence
(g,h)
has exactly
i+j
neighbors of its own color in its closed neighborhood and

i+j+1
neighbors of each of the remaining
k-1
colors. Therefore
c
is a closed neighborhood
balanced coloring of
G\square H
.
∎

Theorem 2.26
.

If
G
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, then
G\times K_{2}
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

Proof.

Let
V(K_{2})=\{h_{0},h_{1}\}
. Then the vertices of
G\times K_{2}
are of the form
(g,h_{i})
, where
g\in V(G)
and
i\in\{0,1\}
.

Let
c
be a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
, and let
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
denote its color classes. Define a coloring of
G\times K_{2}
as follows:

c((g,h_{i}))=j\quad\text{if }g\in V_{j}.

Now consider a vertex
(g,h_{i})\in V(G\times K_{2})
.
Its neighbors are precisely the vertices of the form
(g^{\prime},h_{1-i})
, where
g^{\prime}
is a neighbor of
g
in
G
.
Since
c
is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
, the vertex
g
has an equal number of neighbors in each color class of
G
.
Therefore, the closed neighborhood of
(g,h_{i})
in
G\times K_{2}
also contains an equal number of vertices of every color.

Hence, this coloring is a closed neighborhood balanced coloring of
G\times K_{2}
, and so it is a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
∎

3
Hardness Results for
k\geq 3

We formally state the decision problem whether a given graph
G
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring for a given integer
k\geq 3
.

Closed Neighborhood Balanced
k
-Coloring (
k
-CNBC)

Input:
A graph
G
and a positive integer
k\geq 3
.

Question:
Is there a vertex coloring of
G
using
k
-colors such that every vertex has an equal number of vertices of each color in its closed neighborhood?

Our reduction relies on the
k
-proper coloring problem, whose
\mathsf{NP}
-completeness is established in
[
7
]
.

k
-Proper Coloring (
k
-PC)

Input:
A graph
G
and a positive integer
k\geq 3
.

Question:
Is there a coloring of
G
using
k
-colors such that adjacent vertices are not colored using the same color.

Our reduction uses two gadgets, an edge clique and a padding gadget, both of which are described ahead. An
edge clique
is simply a clique of order
k-2
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
padding gadget
is a graph
P
of order
2k-1
with vertex set

V(P)=\{c\}\cup\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k-1}\}\cup\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1}\}.

The subset
\{c,v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k-1}\}
induces a
k
-clique, and similarly,

\{c,u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k-1}\}
induces another
k
-clique.
The vertex
c
is referred to as the
central vertex
of the padding gadget.

Given a graph
G
and a positive integer
k\geq 3
that is properly colored using
k
colors, we construct an instance
(G,k)
of
k
-CNBC as follows.

For each edge
e=uv
of
G
, introduce an edge clique. Join all the
k-2
vertices of the edge clique to both the end vertices of
e
. Further, for each vertex
v
of
G
, add
d(v)-1
padding gadgets. We shall call the graph so constructed
G^{\prime}
.

Observation 3.2
.

If the graph
G^{\prime}
admits a
k
-CNBC, then the
k-2
vertices of any edge–clique receive colors that are distinct from those assigned to the end vertices of the edge to which the edge–clique is attached.

Theorem 3.3
.

The
k
-
Closed Neighborhood Balanced Coloring
(
k
-CNBC
) problem is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete for any integer
k\geq 3
.

Proof.

We give a reduction from the
k
-Proper Coloring
(
k
-PC
) problem.
Let
(G,k)
be an instance of
k
-PC
. We construct an equivalent instance
(G^{\prime},k)
of
k
-CNBC
as described earlier. Note that
G^{\prime}
can be constructed in polynomial time.

Since, given a vertex coloring of
G
, we can verify in polynomial time whether it satisfies the closed neighborhood balanced condition, the
k
-CNBC
problem belongs to the class
\mathsf{NP}
.

We now show that
(G,k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-PC
if and only if
(G^{\prime},k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-CNBC
.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Suppose
(G,k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-PC
.
Let
c:V(G)\rightarrow\{1,2,\dots,k\}
be a proper
k
-coloring of
G
.
We define a coloring
c^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
as follows:

1.

Assign each vertex of
G
in
G^{\prime}
the same color as under
c
.

2.

For every edge
e=uv
of
G
, color the
k-2
vertices of the corresponding edge clique using the
k-2
colors not assigned to
u
and
v
, in any order.

3.

For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, color the central vertex of each padding gadget attached to
v
with the same color as
v
.
Within each padding gadget, assign the remaining
k-1
colors to the other vertices of each of the two
k
-cliques so that every color from
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
appears exactly once in each clique.

We now prove that
c^{\prime}
is a valid
k
-CNBC
coloring of
G^{\prime}
.

Vertices within padding gadgets:

By construction, the central vertex
c
of each padding gadget has two vertices of each color in its closed neighborhood
N[c]
.
Similarly, each non-central vertex in either clique has exactly one vertex of each color in its closed neighborhood.

Vertices in edge cliques:

From step (2), each edge clique consists of vertices colored with the
k-2
colors distinct from those of its adjacent endpoints
u
and
v
.
Each such vertex is adjacent to
u
,
v
, and all other
(k-3)
vertices of the clique, giving it one vertex of each color in its closed neighborhood.

Original vertices of
G
:

Consider a vertex
v\in V(G)
.
For each padding gadget attached to
v
, the central vertex is colored the same as
v
.
Hence,
v
has exactly
d(v)
neighbors of its own color—one from each padding gadget (since there are
d(v)-1
such gadgets) and itself.
Moreover, for every neighbor
u
of
v
in
G
, the edge clique corresponding to
uv
contributes one vertex of each of the remaining
k-2
colors (excluding those of
u
and
v
).
Therefore,
v
has exactly
d(v)
neighbors of each of the
k-1
colors distinct from its own color.
Consequently, every vertex of
G
has the same number of vertices of each color in its closed neighborhood.

Hence, the coloring
c^{\prime}
satisfies the closed neighborhood balanced condition, and
(G^{\prime},k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-CNBC
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Suppose
(G^{\prime},k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-CNBC
.
Let
c^{\prime}:V(G^{\prime})\rightarrow\{1,2,\dots,k\}
be a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
.

Consider any edge
e=uv
of the original graph
G
.
In
G^{\prime}
, to both the vertices of the edge
e
we add an edge clique of order
k-2
, where every vertex of the clique is adjacent to both
u
and
v
.
Thus, the subgraph induced by
\{u,v\}
together with this edge clique forms a complete graph on
k
vertices.

By Observation
3.2
, in any valid
k
-CNBC of
G^{\prime}
, the
k-2
vertices of each edge clique must receive colors distinct from those of its endpoints
u
and
v
.
If
u
and
v
were assigned the same color under
c^{\prime}
, then this
k
-clique would contain only
k-1
distinct colors, violating the balanced condition within the closed neighborhoods of the clique vertices.
Hence,
u
and
v
must receive distinct colors.

Therefore, the restriction of
c^{\prime}
to the vertex set
V(G)
defines a proper
k
-coloring of
G
.
Consequently,
(G,k)
is a
Yes
-instance of
k
-PC
.
∎

4
Conclusion

In this article, we defined the concept of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graphs, which is a generalization of closed neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring of graphs introduced by Collins et al.
[
5
]
. Initially, we gave some characteristics of graphs that admit such a coloring. Then we studied closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs can be constructed under various graph operations. We also presented several regular closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs and showed that the class of closed neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs is not hereditary. In section
3
, we showed that the decision problem of checking whether a given graph
G
admits a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring for
k\geq 3
is
NP
-hard.

We now pose the following problems related to our work.

1.

Characterize the regular graphs that admit a closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

2.

Is the problem of determining whether a graph admits closed neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring for
k=2
an
NP
-complete problem?

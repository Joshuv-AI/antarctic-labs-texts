A systematic review of methodologies and solutions for recycling polyurethane foams to safeguard the environment
Authors: Elnaz Zarezadeh, Mahbubeh Tangestani, Ahmad Jonidi Jafari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractToday, plastic plays a pervasive role in everyday life. Their improper disposal can create ongoing environmental challenges. Polyurethane (PU) is a polymer with elastomeric properties that exhibits significant adhesion and durability. PU has various colors and resistance to acid and alkali substances. The widespread use of PU has caused a significant presence of these compounds in landfills. Therefore, it can cause significant plastic pollution globally. This increased environmental concern has prompted researchers and innovators to explore sustainable methods for recycling PU foam. Therefore, the recycling of PU waste is recognized as an essential requirement due to its economic and environmental benefits. Although the latest reviews focused on physical, chemical, and biological methods of PU recycling, a comprehensive review of other PU recycling methods is needed. Therefore, the present study is a systematic review of recent initiatives and innovations in the field of recycling and modification of PU foam production methods to recognize and reduce environmental impacts. In the present study, major global databases such as Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Iranian databases (up to January 2024) were searched with relative keywords to identify studies published in authoritative journals. Data were collected from qualified articles on different PU recycling methods and innovative processes. From a total of 1088 articles found, finally, 46 studies met the objectives and inclusion criteria. Based on the results, today various methods are used to recycle PU compounds, but more attention has been paid to efficient methods such as energy production and high-consumption products such as rubber and adhesive from these compounds. In addition, it highlights some approaches, such as the production of absorbents from PU foams, which not only improve the current technology but also show promise in reducing the environmental impact of this ubiquitous material.Keywords: Plastic waste management, PU, Modification, Reuse, Recovery, Reprocessing

Introduction

Nowadays, plastic waste is known as one of the biggest environmental issues. The world produced more plastics from 2 million tons in 1950 to 450 million tons in 2019 [1]. About 8 % of all plastics manufactured are polyurethanes (PUs), which are ranked as the sixth most widely used polymer globally [2]. Foam is the most widely consumed kind of PU product worldwide which accounts for 67 % of global PU consumption and represents 50 % of the polymer foam market [3].

PU foam, commonly referred to as urethane foam, is a significant component of the extensive family of polymers or plastics. PUs have widespread usage due to their exceptional properties, such as high mechanical strength, wear and corrosion resistance, and low-temperature resilience. This versatility allows them to be used for a variety of purposes, including applications in flexible foams, hard foams, absorbent sponges, adhesives, elastomers, heat insulation, furniture fillers, car seats, and polymer chemistry applications [4,5].

The durability of PU products ranges from 3 to over 50 years, contingent upon their intended usage. PU foam insulation utilized in refrigerators typically boasts an average lifespan of 15 years [6]. Based on these various properties, PU have different categories.

PUs initially encompass two primary categories: rigid (PUR) and flexible (PUF). Among these, PUR foams stand out as prevalent and widely utilized insulating materials. PUR foams are characterized by a closed-cell structure and an apparent density typically ranging from 30 to 35 kg/m3. These foams not only contribute to energy conservation but also enhance the efficiency of both commercial and residential appliances. PUR finds applications as insulation in walls, ceilings, household appliances, and commercial refrigerators [7]. PUF foams consist of certain copolymers, deriving their flexibility from the phase separation between soft and hard components. Dominating over half of the foam market, these PUF foams typically exhibit a density ranging from 20 to 45 kg/m3. However, their cubic structure renders them less effective as insulators. Notably, PUF is susceptible to combustion and emitting highly toxic and flammable gases. Thus, PUR and PUF,primarily employed as fillers, have extensive applications such as furniture, car interior components, protective coatings for packaging delicate devices, and as nanocomposites in filters. They are commonly utilized in the production of car cushions and furniture/bedding [8,9].

In order to effectively identify different PU compounds properties, it is essential to understand the primary constituents involved in the production of PU. The primary constituents utilized in the production of PU include di- and tri-isocyanates, polyols, and additional auxiliary elements like catalysts, surfactants, and blowing agents (such as chlorofluoroalkane, rafluoroethane, and pentafluoropropane). These components are employed to modify the polymer's properties that result in attributes such as very low thermal conductivity, low density, minimal moisture permeability, and absorption, as well as heightened strength and dimensional stability [10].

Generally, PU synthesis occurs by reacting isocyanate molecules with polyols in the presence of a catalyst. The common isocyanates include aromatic diisocyanates like toluene diisocyanate (TDI) and Methylenediphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) which are essential constituents in PU preparation. TDI and MDI are used more frequently than other isocyanates due to their cost-effectiveness and enhanced reactivity. MDI contains polymeric materials that can be used as raw materials for the production of flexible foams [11].

Polyol stands as another crucial component in PU foam formulation. High molecular weight polyols are used in constructing PUF and low molecular weight polyols are utilized for producing PUR. The demand for PU is substantial. In the beginning, the majority of polyols used in PU synthesis were sourced from petroleum. However, today, due to the wide production of these products and high energy consumption, there is a need to use more suitable and eco-friendly alternatives [12,13]. Polyols used in PU synthesis include two or more hydroxyl (OH) groups. The production of polyether polyols involves copolymerizing propylene oxide and ethylene oxide with a compatible polyol precursor, whereas polyester polyols are derived from the reaction between carboxylic acid and polyhydroxyl compounds [14,15]. A range of nanoparticles, including carbon nanotubes, graphene derivatives, and nano clay/silica, can be incorporated into PU foams to enhance their mechanical and thermal characteristics. Additionally, these additives can influence electrical conductivity, biodegradability, and foaming behavior [16,17].

The impact of these additives on properties significantly enhances the versatility of PU foams across various industries, including furniture, insulation, medicine, and home appliances. The PUs widespread consumption is caused by its advantageous properties including lightweight construction, excellent thermal insulation, robust strength, and resistance.

Today, the PU presence as solid waste in the environment has increased due to the demand for PU growth and its capability for novel applications. According to the investigation, it is indicated that the total production of PU reached 18 million tons in 2016 [18]. PU waste aggregation in the landfill to a density of approximately 500 kg/m3 can cause the intensification of soil and groundwater pollution due to the low degradability of PUs [19].

According to the diverse production methods of PU foams, the PU-generated production wastes vary extensive [20]. Shortly, a substantial volume of PU foam waste is expected to emerge which is posing a threat to the living environment. Given its considerable storage space requirements, the disposal of such waste has the potential to harm the environment significantly. Consequently, there is an urgent need to investigate this issue through the recycling of PU foam waste which is necessitating the selection of environmentally friendly processing technologies. Currently, three main methods are used to manage PU waste worldwide: disposal, landfill, and incineration [21,22]. Each of these methods has disadvantages and harmful environmental effects. In order to reduce these problems, attention to recycling methods has become very important. These methods include physical, chemical, and biological recycling (Fig. 1). In this study, the focus is on recycling methods of PU products with innovative approaches. The main purpose of this research is to investigate and compare different methods of recycling PU products, in order to provide improved and more efficient solutions for waste management and environmental protection.

Methods

The current study is a systematic review that adheres to the principles outlined in the PRISMA checklist [23]. Open Science Framework (OSF) registration for the study protocol is available at osf.io/9x5wt.

Search strategy

The objective of the search was to identify English language studies that have been indexed in Web of Science (Science Citation Index)، PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Iranian databases (SID, Magiran, IranMedex, IranDoc, and Thesis) without any time period restriction up to January 2024. The search was conducted using a predefined list of different recycling and innovation modalities and the phrase "Polyurethane" individually and in combination. A supplementary manual search was done to identify papers that may have been overlooked during the electronic search. This involved examining the reference lists of the studies included in the analysis. Duplicate studies were removed. The lists of included techniques are as follows: adsorbent, energy production, adhesive production, modification method, and innovation approaches (Table 1).

Before beginning the analysis phase, the data for this study were extracted independently by two reviewers, and the outcomes were compared to identify and settle any inconsistencies. According to the investigations, the evaluated research has concentrated on the biological (26.53 %), chemical (15.65 %), and physical (3.40 %) aspects of PU recycling, whereas the goal of this study is to look into novel techniques and advancements in PU foams (34.01 %) (Fig. 3).

Quality assessment of studies

The quality assessment of the selected studies was done using the JBI critical appraisal tool checklist (https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools). This criterion evaluates the areas of research selection, group comparison and study findings. First, specific objectives for the study were defined and articles were selected based on their alignment with these objectives, following the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidelines. Studies that did not meet our study objectives were excluded, and only high-quality and relevant articles were selected for further analysis. The final list of selected articles is presented in the abstract and title section in the form of a PRISMA figure. On the other hand, the JBI checklist was completed and the quality of the studies was checked by a rating system. As such, each study was given a star rating on a scale of zero to nine, where a rating of less than three indicated low quality, a rating of four to six indicated moderate quality, and a rating of more than seven stars indicated outstanding quality.

Classification of PU recycling methods

PU recycling techniques can be categorized according to the methodologies employed for the breakdown and reprocessing of PU materials. The primary methods include burial, incineration, as well as physical and chemical recycling, and biodegradation, which will be explored in the following (Fig. 4).

Different methods of PU reprocessing.

Landfill

The disposal of PU foam waste in landfills must adhere to local regulations and guidelines to ensure environmental compliance. The recommended steps and considerations for landfill disposal of PU foam waste include: verifying local regulations, segregating and preparing PU foam waste from other waste types, securely packaging the waste to prevent container dispersion, completing necessary documentation (such as waste manifests or tracking documents) to record the type and quantity of waste deposited in the landfill, planning waste transportation to prevent leaks during transit, and selecting an appropriate burial site permitted for PU foam waste acceptance.

Initially, PU wastes may be handled by burying or burning them. However, due to the considerable volume and low density of PU foams, disposal methods can result in significant occupy of the Earth's resources. Moreover, the decomposition of PU waste under natural conditions is an essential challenge. Certain European countries, such as the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark, Switzerland, and Australia, along with Denmark, have enacted laws prohibiting landfill disposal. Consequently, burying PU foam waste is deemed an economically impractical option [21,24].

Incineration

Incineration works as a method for heat recovery. Burning 1 kg of PU yields a calorific value of approximately 7000 kcal/kg, which generates energy adequate to an equivalent weight of coal. Incineration leads to a drastic reduction in waste volume, up to about 99 % [25]. The thermal energy produced in this process can be effectively utilized for instance in cement factories. The PU Industry Federation of America has conducted various tests indicating improved calorific value by incorporating PU foam waste into the fuel. Additionally, the combination of PU foams with coal to create a novel mixed fuel known as environmental fuel represents an innovative and environmentally friendly approach to PU waste disposal [26]. The incineration approach in PU waste recycling, while requiring minimal space, can prove beneficial for individuals unable to employ alternative recycling methods. However, incomplete incineration can lead to the release of toxic emissions which can cause environmental issues such as the emission of harmful gases and air pollution [27]. Additionally, the combustion process generates noxious gases like isocyanate vapor, hydrocyanic acid, or nitrogen oxides. Consequently, this method is progressively being phased out [28]. Further examination of data extracted from Ekokem's annual reports for 2002 and 2003 indicates that the incineration process leads to an elevated average emission of ten trace elements (Sb, As, Pb, Cr, Co, Cu, Mn, Ni, V, Sn). Also, it was reported that these pollutants amount increased from 9 μg per cubic meter in 2002 to 30 μg per cubic meter in 2003 [19].

Recycling

Given the imperative of sustainable development and the reasonable significance of environmental conservation, the rational utilization of resources has been considered attention. So, the effective disposal and recycling of PU foam waste become imperative. This is not only essential for preventing pollution and safeguarding the environment but also crucial for reducing production costs and optimizing material usage. As mentioned above, PU foam waste can pose challenges in terms of treatment and disposal in landfills due to its low density and substantial volume. On the other hand, incineration leads to the generation of toxic emissions and contributes to environmental pollution [29,30]. Consequently, three distinct categories reported the methods for recycling and recovering PU waste, as illustrated in the figure (Fig. 4).

Mechanical recycling encompasses physical purification, which constitutes one facet of the recycling process. Another facet involves the chemical recycling of raw materials which includes entailing chemical purification and the production of raw chemicals for application in the chemical industry. Also, biodegradation is another PU recycling method. Additionally, energy recovery is inclusive of energy conversion and entails processes such as complete or partial oxidation of materials, as well as the generation of heat, power, and/or gaseous fuel [31].

Physical recycling

The physical recycling approach proves to be an efficient, straightforward, and cost-effective method in the immediate timeframe. Physical recycling is an uncomplicated operation in different PU recovery methods that has widespread application. The primary objective of mechanical recycling is to repurpose PU waste. Typically, the waste is crushed into small particles and transformed into flakes, granules, or powder for utilization as fillers and in various items such as flooring, engine housings, and pumps. Additionally, these particles can be affixed and compressed onto other products. While physical methods are suitable for thermoplastic polymers, they do not apply to thermoset (heat-resistant) polymers due to their heat resistance [32]. The physical method involves the crushing of PU foam waste and inducing physical deformation. The resulting solid particles lack any reactive activities but serve as direct raw materials for the creation of new PU products. By blending these particles with adhesive and employing compression molding can be produced various moulded products. Presently, this method stands as the most extensively utilized approach. In comparison to chemical recycling, physical recycling is characterized by its simplicity, convenience, and cost-effectiveness, which makes it easier to implement. However, certain technical constraints exist across different physical recycling methods, that they are impacting the strength and performance of the recovered products. This limitation restricts its applicability to low-cost items, thus constraining the market for sales.

The prevailing mechanical recycling techniques primarily encompass: rebonding, adhesive pressing, compression molding, regrinding, hot press molding processes, utilization as fillers, and extrusion and injection molding processes (Table 2) [33]. The physical recycling method offers efficiency, simplicity, and cost-effectiveness in the short term. However, this method has technical limitations that affect the performance and strength of recycled products. Consequently, the applicability of this method is somewhat restricted, typically suiting products with lower costs and limited value. In other words, the ability to implement this method for products with high added value or use in the market is affected (Table 6).

Advantages and disadvantages of different PU reprocessing methods.

Chemical recycling

Chemical methods stand out as highly effective means of recycling PU foams in contrast to other intricate and challenging approaches. The main advantage of the chemical method is in the extraction of pure raw materials and the production of reusable products [33]. This approach entails breaking PU foam into its constituent molecules, which enables the synthesis of new PU materials. Chemical recycling can result the high-quality recycled PU that presents a promising solution to address the challenges associated with mechanical recycling. Furthermore, the chemical methods in contrast to the physical processes prove more effective to network structures such as PUR and PUF. Chemical techniques utilize PU polymerization to depolymerize urethane, ether, and ester bonds by using a reversible reaction [21]. Chemical recycling involves the reaction of functional groups, such as carbamate in PUs, to polymerize the main chain. The primary objective of chemical methods is to recycle raw materials such as polyol, which can be reintegrated into foam production [37]. Within the chemical recycling process, urethane bonds are typically cleaved to yield glycols. Molecular organic compounds engage in a reversible reaction under specific conditions and temperatures, which is facilitated by the presence of chemical reagents and catalysts [38]. Polyol monomer, isocyanate, and amine are acquired through the distillation process. The chemical recycling process adheres to the principle of decomposition [39].

The physicochemical properties and performance of degradation products vary based on the diverse reagents employed in the degradation process. Chemical recycling methods for PU encompass various approaches, including alcoholic degradation, hydrolysis, methanolysis, aminolysis, acidolysis, phosphorolysis, and glycolysis (Table 3) [33,40]. Chemical recycling methods may have disadvantages such as greater process complexity, the need for special chemicals that may be expensive or toxic, high energy consumption, and the need for more advanced equipment and technologies. These cases can increase the costs and complexity of the recycling process, therefore, the use of innovative solutions in the field of PU recycling can be proposed as an effective solution to solve these problems (Table 6).

Biodegradation

Currently, there is a growing focus on recovering PUs through enzymatic and microbial degradation methods to mitigate environmental concerns associated with these compounds [58]. Biodegradation involves various stages where organisms decompose compounds. Initially, non-biological processes initiate polymer breakdown, followed by enzymatic processes converting them into smaller molecules [59,60]. Biodegradation is safer and yields higher-value compounds from PU waste. Bacterial and fungal strains, along with enzymes like esterase, urease, and proteases, are used for PU degradation (Table 4). Factors like molecular structure and cross-linking significantly influence PU biodegradability. Enzymes like amidase and esterase are effective in PU bond hydrolysis. PU degradation rates increase with different carbon sources, with metabolites serving as carbon sources for microbial growth. Fungi hydrolyze ester and urethane bonds in PU, producing water and carbon dioxide. Further research on PU biodegradability is crucial due to its complex structure and specific degradation conditions [61,62]. Biological and enzymatic methods for PU recycling increase the complexity of the process; Because biological and enzymatic processes usually require special conditions such as temperature, pH, and the presence of food for microbes. These environmental conditions may slow down the recycling process and add to the complexity of their implementation. Secondly, these methods may be time-consuming; Because biological processes require significant time to break down and purify materials, which can lead to a decrease in the efficiency and speed of the recycling process. Also, high costs may be associated with these methods; Because they need to buy and prepare enzymes, food for microbes, maintain suitable environmental conditions, and expert manpower (Table 6). For this reason, these methods may not be suitable for some cases and require other alternatives for PU recycling.

Recent innovations and initiatives in PU recycling

Recent innovations and initiatives of PU recycling have led to significant advances in sustainable waste management. In this study, recent developments in this field have been examined to evaluate their effectiveness and potential impact as a step towards environmental protection.

Using recycled PUs as an ab(d)sorbent

Foams have various applications and can undergo different recycling processes (Fig. 5). Today, aquifer resources are contaminated by organic compounds that threaten the environment and human health. So, technological progress is increasingly important in the development of stable adsorbents to remove these pollutants with hydrophobic properties. Research indicates that the disposal of PU foam waste can be repurposed for the production of different absorbent materials. The inadvertent release of oil and oil products has resulted in environmental hazards for marine ecosystems and organisms and potentially impacted human health [98,99].

Innovation and modification methods for PU recycling.

Various technologies have been suggested for the remediation of oil spills in water resources such as chemical spreaders [100], in-situ burning [101], photochemical degradation [102], bioremediation [103], and air pumping [104].

In addition, different approaches such as membrane filtration, ion exchange, chemical precipitation, solvent extraction, electrochemical conversion, oxidation, reverse osmosis, ozonation, photocatalysis, coagulation, burning, surface adsorption, etc. are used in different ways to purify water from different pollutants. However, these methods have shortcomings and inefficiencies, including low liquid selectivity, long cleaning times, low recovery rates, and high investment costs. They also frequently result in secondary environmental hazards. These difficulties reduce the efficiency of extracting oil and other chemicals from water and increase the cost-effectiveness of these processes by requiring specialized equipment and labor-intensive procedures [105].

Hence, there is a need to devise effective techniques and materials for eliminating organic pollutants from water. This necessitates the creation of cost-effective porous materials as an alternative to other water pollution remediation methods. Absorption offers distinct advantages in terms of affordability, uncomplicated design, efficiency, and environmental compatibility. Different adsorbents, originating from various sources, are produced and used for the removal of pollutants from wastewater (Table 5) [106].

Types of absorbents manufactured from PU foam

In various studies, the role of PU as an absorbent in dealing with environmental challenges, especially in oil spill cleanup and pollution control, has been significant (Table 5). PU foams have shown significant capabilities in effectively absorbing oil and organic pollutants from water environments. These foams, when reinforced with different materials or modified through chemical processes, show a greater absorption capacity and selectivity for oil than water.

In addition, PU foams have shown versatility in their applications and are used in various industries such as construction, automotive, and agriculture. The ability of PU foams to effectively separate various types of pollutants from water, including light and heavy organic solvents, shows their importance as a versatile and efficient absorbent material [99].

Also, the durability and reusability of PU foams have been highlighted, with studies showing their ability to retain absorbent capacity even after multiple periods of use. Retention of hydrophobic properties after use ensures recovery and reuse of foams and contributes to their stability as absorbents.

In general, PU foams play an important role as absorbents in reducing environmental pollution and provide a practical and effective solution for oil spill cleanup and pollution control efforts. In the following, some studies have been conducted on the production of absorbents made of PU for the removal of pollutants.

Pollutants ab(d)sorption

PUs as an absorbent material boasted remarkable capabilities which can be mentioned to their moisturizing features, hydrophobic nature, and cost-effectiveness. They serve as effective absorbents for eliminating aqueous impurities (Table 5). Recycling PU foam into absorbents typically involves transforming it into a substance capable of absorbing and maintaining pollutants on its own. This process finds application in various scenarios, including the creation of absorbent materials for sanitary products, oil spill cleanup, paint spill, or any situation requiring liquid absorption. Initially, the foams undergo a cleaning process to remove pollutants and other substances. Subsequently, the foams are crushed or ground into smaller pieces, which enhance surface area and facilitate subsequent processing steps. To alter the properties of PU foam, a chemical treatment method is employed by incorporating additives to modify the foam's structure and enhance its absorbent qualities. Finally, the modified foam is shaped into the desired form for use as an absorbent material through molding, extrusion, or other shaping processes [107].

PU absorbents for oil removal and spill cleanup

As a result of oil spills and chemical spills, it has caused environmental consequences and much damage to the sea, and potential risks to human health [108]. Several strategies, such as mechanical cleaning, biological treatment, and in-situ incineration, have been employed to effectively address oil spills. However, these methods, especially chemical and biological approaches, often lead to secondary contamination [109,110].

Amorim et al. investigated PU biocomposites reinforced with malt and/or acerola residues for their ability to absorb oil and organic pollutants. The results showed a notable increase in absorption capacity, with PUCO-AR20 demonstrating superior efficiency due to its hydrophobic surface. These biocomposites effectively separated light and heavy organic solvents and retained their hydrophobic properties after use [111]. In another study, He and colleagues investigated the potential of PU foam for separating oil from water. They investigated the tendency of PU foam to absorb water by introducing hydrophobic modifications such as PDMS, CNTs, and RGO. While other materials such as CNT/polymer composites and MnO2/PUF nanowires are promising, they face challenges. To deal with this issue, this study proposes the use of agricultural wastes such as tree bark to produce tannin-based polyols and PU foams (MTPFs). Compared to traditional PU foams, MTPFs showed significant improvement in absorption capacity, hydrophobicity, and mechanical strength. MTPF100, derived from the waste shell, showed remarkable performance and provided an environmentally friendly alternative for oil-water separation. This emphasizes the importance of using agricultural waste for sustainable solutions in environmental improvement [112].

Hwang et al. conducted study in 2022 that focused on addressing the reduced oil absorbency caused by lignin particles in composite materials. They proposed chemically modifying lignin surfaces to enhance compatibility with hydrophobic polymer matrices like PU foam, thus improving absorption capacity. By replacing hydroxyl groups with hydrophobic functional groups, they achieved higher oil absorbency and compatibility. Combining PU foam with modified lignin particles offers a cost-effective, reusable, and biodegradable solution for oil cleanup, potentially surpassing polypropylene pads in eco-friendliness. The study introduced PU foam infused with up to 40 % hydrophobic lignin particles, enhancing absorption capacity by 20 % compared to pure PU foam. Even after 50 cycles, the recycled oil can be easily recovered, demonstrating good recyclability. This innovative approach using foam and composite techniques holds promise for efficiently cleaning organic pollutants [113].

Atta et al. investigated the use of recycled PET as an oil absorbent and its impact on producing PU foams. PET waste underwent glycolysis with pentaerythritol (PER) or trimethylpropane (TMP), resulting in polyol oligomers suitable for PU foams. The study analyzed factors affecting foam structure, including polyol reactivity, water content, catalyst type, and isocyanate concentration. PU foams from glycolic TMP showed homogeneous cells, while those from PER had varied cell sizes. TMP-based PUs exhibited lower storage moduli, indicating the influence of ethylene chains. PER-based PUs had higher glass transition temperatures. The investigation demonstrated the reusability and oil absorption capabilities of PU foams [114].

PU absorbents for paint removal

The discharge of millions of tons of dye waste from various industries brings environmental and health risks [115]. Surface adsorption is a common method for the treatment of colored wastewater, of which UiO-66-NH2 (MOF) is a widely used adsorbent. However, MOF recycling is challenging. Combining MOFs with polymers can solve this problem, but existing methods have drawbacks [116,117]. PUs play an important role as adsorbents in color removal by being incorporated into composite materials designed to efficiently absorb colored pollutants from wastewater. These composites, such as WPU-SS-MOF and PUF-COO@MnO2, show high adsorption capacity for anionic dyes and organic pollutants and provide effective solutions for removing dyes from industrial wastewater. In addition, PU foam-based adsorbents are effective against a wide range of pollutants, including colored pollutants, which emphasizes the versatility and efficiency of PU in environmental remediation as an adsorbent material. The study by Li et al. (2023) presents a solvent etching deposition method to create a self-healing and recyclable composite membrane (WPU-SS-MOF) for efficient removal of anionic dyes, including methyl orange (MO) and Congo red (CR), from wastewater. By combining waterborne PU with dynamic disulfide bonds (WPU-SS) with MOF (UiO-66-NH2), the resulting membrane exhibits superior mechanical properties, with a tensile strength of 5.16 MPa and elongation at break of 1117.1 %. The self-healing efficiency reaches 96.1 %. Adsorption experiments demonstrate the membrane's outstanding adsorption and recycling capacity, with a removal efficiency of over 80 % even after 5 cycles. This innovative approach represents a promising solution for economical dye wastewater treatment, highlighting the significant role of PUs in enhancing the performance and sustainability of adsorbent materials [118].

In another study, Moawed et al. (2023) repurposed PU foam waste to create a new nanocomposite called PUF-COO@MnO2 for environmental use. This nanocomposite showed effective absorption of dyes in industrial wastewater and antibacterial activity. This study highlights the successful recycling of PU foam waste for wastewater treatment, and suggests the synthesized PUF-COO@MnO2 composite as a promising material for environmental remediation, highlighting the role of PU in addressing environmental challenges [119].

Selvasembian et al. (2021) investigated the production, properties, and applications of PU foam-based adsorbents for the removal of various pollutants from water. This study highlighted the potential of PU foam in addressing environmental challenges by turning waste into effective absorbents. Various types of PU foam-based adsorbents, such as lignin-PUF and chitosan PUF, have been successfully synthesized and shown to be effective in removing a wide range of pollutants, offering a promising solution for water treatment [120].

Sound absorption

Considering the urgent need for effective control measures, the issue of intensifying noise pollution in urban areas and its harmful effects on human health is discussed [121,122]. Traditional acoustic materials, such as polystyrene, contribute to environmental pollution due to their non-biodegradability. To solve this problem, recycled materials such as plastic bottles and natural fibers are increasingly used for their acoustic properties in structures, especially in acoustic walls made with recycled PU. PU has been proven to be very effective in absorbing sound, especially at medium and high frequencies, as its porosity plays an important role in the absorption capacity [123,124].

In addition, the importance of porous materials in noise control engineering is very prominent and they are classified into cellular, fibrous, or granular structures. Various experimental models, such as Delany's model, have been developed to predict the acoustic properties of these materials, which help in the design of sound-absorbing structures [125,126].

Types of sound absorbers manufactured from PU

The use of recycled PU foams in the production of sound absorbers leads to the reduction of urban noise pollution. Various studies investigate the sound absorption capability of recycled polyethylene foams in combination with different materials such as fabrics and gypsum sheets. The results show that these composite materials exhibit significant sound absorption coefficients at different frequencies, making them effective alternatives to traditional sound-absorbing materials [127]. Furthermore, studies highlight the potential of recycled PU foams to contribute to environmental sustainability by reusing waste materials for practical applications in the construction, automotive, and industrial sectors. Overall, this research emphasizes the promising role of recycled PU foams in reducing noise pollution and advancing environmental protection efforts [128].

Salino et al. used the combination of recycled PU foams and recycled gypsum sheets for sound absorption. It has been observed that the manufactured composite had 47.45 % porosity, which could absorb sound at different frequencies from 250 to 2000 Hz [122].

In a study conducted by Rey et al., Using recycled PU foam from textile production for sound absorption, they investigated the modeling of its acoustic behavior based on parameters such as airflow resistance and pore characteristics. Their study showed that recycled PU foams have excellent sound absorption properties and consistent results in different foam compositions. The findings show that recycled PU foams act as effective sound absorbers and provide sustainable alternatives to traditional materials in various applications [129].

Also, Salino and colleagues developed a composite material using PU foam remnants and recycled gypsum board cores to absorb sound and address urban sustainability challenges. This material with 47.45 % porosity and effective mechanical energy absorption at different frequencies showed better performance compared to traditional materials. Based on the mechanical standards, it offers a variety of industrial applications and helps reduce noise pollution and environmental sustainability by reusing waste materials [122].

Rey and colleagues explored the sound absorption capabilities of materials derived from ground PU foam waste, an environmental concern in the textile industry. Their study revealed that recycled materials exhibit promising sound absorption properties, presenting viable alternatives to traditional materials. By developing a composite material combining PU foam residues and recycled gypsum board cores, they achieved significant sound absorption coefficients across all frequencies, attributed to its 47.45 % porosity. This lightweight composite meets mechanical standards, surpasses traditional materials in performance, and holds potential for various industrial applications, fostering noise pollution reduction and promoting environmental sustainability [130].

Mester E and colleagues explored repurposing flexible moulded PU foams from car seat cushion waste as sound insulation. They assessed foam variations through isocyanate-polyol ratios and conducted aging tests to simulate long-term use. Despite losing suitability for seat cushions post-aging, micro-CT analysis showed they remain effective sound insulators within a specific frequency range. Despite cell damage and reduced compressive strength after aging, the study suggests repurposing aged foams as sound insulation materials, offering a simple, cost-effective method to recycle PU waste and promote sustainability [131].

Atianzar-Navarro et al. (2020) investigated sound absorption in perforated recycled PU foams reinforced with woven fabric to enhance their acoustic properties. Experimental and numerical evaluations considered factors like hole speed, size, and depth, revealing changes in sound absorption, particularly around 2 kHz. The study underscored the impact of hole parameters and fabric type on sound absorption, confirmed by theoretical models. Financial support was provided by the Ministry of Economy and Innovation and the European Union, with all authors contributing to the study's methodology, analysis, and manuscript preparation [128].

Recycling of waste rubber

This method presents an innovative approach by incorporating ground tire rubber (GTR) into foamed PU matrices, offering potential benefits for waste rubber utilization and reducing reliance on toxic chemicals. It emphasizes the importance of establishing interfacial interactions while maintaining cellular structure in the PU matrix. Adjustments in foam formulations and modifications of GTR are crucial for achieving desired properties. The incorporation of GTR into PU can significantly reduce environmental impact, making it a promising option for polymer composites. Future research should focus on the repeatability of modification processes and exploring continuous modification methods to overcome challenges, ultimately leading to materials suitable for various applications such as vibration or sound damping [137].

Steam and energy production

The mentioned method focuses on the recycling of waste PU sponges to address environmental challenges, especially in solar steam generation for water desalination. Floating black PU sponges are introduced as efficient solar absorbers after a simple hydrophilic treatment. Key advantages include significantly higher evaporation rates, improved efficiency in steam generation, and the ability to drive solar ethanol distillation. Hydrophilic treatment increases water absorption and shows the potential of reusing waste materials for sustainable applications, contributing to solutions to energy and water-related challenges. Overall, this approach provides a practical and cost-effective strategy for harnessing solar energy in renewable energy technologies [138].

Adhesive production

In a study, the reuse of PUR foam waste into PU adhesives has been investigated, to develop medium-viscosity industrial adhesives containing recycled PUR foam. The test of three grinding techniques showed that two-roll grinding is more effective in producing PUR micro powder suitable for adhesive modification. This micro powder, which is used as a passive filler, shows potential reactivity to replace polyol bundles. The findings show that replacing polyol with PUR powder increases the adhesive bond strength with a maximum recommended replacement of 20 %. In general, shredded PUR foam waste is a promising component as an active filler in PU adhesive formulations and offers a sustainable solution for adhesive production [139].

Modification of PU structure

There are various strategies to modify and recycle PU structures to address environmental concerns and increase sustainability (Fig. 5). In this context, polyols, chain extenders, and diisocyanates are very important in PU production and their role is important in determining the chemical and physical properties of PU materials. In addition, the potential of incorporating biodegradable components such as vegetable oil-based polyols to create environmentally friendly PU foams is emphasized [[140], [141], [142]].

Innovative methods are being developed for recycling PU waste, such as covalent adaptable networks (CAN) and reprocessing with catalysts. These approaches aim to transform thermoset polymers into new products by introducing dynamic covalent bonds, and maintaining mechanical properties while reducing environmental impacts [[143], [144], [145]].

Techniques like twin-screw extrusion with carbamate exchange catalysis have shown promise in reprocessing post-consumer PU foams [146]. Additionally, regrinding PU foam scraps into fine powder for reuse as a filler in PU foam synthesis has been explored. This powder can replace up to 15 % of polyols in the synthesis process, enhancing the mechanical properties of resulting foams [147]. Moreover, research on incorporating organic fillers like waste paper or biowaste PU foams into PU formulations has demonstrated improvements in properties such as thermal stability and comfort, with potential applications in various industries including automotive and thermal insulation [148]. These innovative recycling methods not only address environmental concerns but also contribute to the sustainability of the PU foam market. Ongoing research focuses on developing cost-effective and eco-friendly recycling techniques to further reduce the environmental and health impacts associated with PU foam production and disposal [149,150]. Therefore, nowadays researchers are looking for new economical and environmentally friendly methods to recycle PU products. Accordingly, the environmental and health effects of these compounds are reduced by decreasing the production of PU foams.

Conclusion

In Europe, more than 250,000 tons of PU waste are recycled annually, but innovative methods are needed for better sustainability. Current mechanical and chemical recycling methods have limitations that result in less valuable recycled products. New approaches to efficiently recycle PU foam waste and prevent premature disposal are very important, as biodegradation processes also face challenges. Recent innovations and initiatives in PU recycling are an important step towards sustainable waste management practices. This comprehensive review has highlighted various developments in PU recycling methods and emphasizes their effectiveness and potential impact on environmental protection. The use of recycled PU as an adsorbent material offers promising prospects for reducing environmental pollutants, especially in addressing oil spills and water pollution. By reusing PU foam waste in absorbent materials, important steps have been taken in developing cost-effective and environmentally friendly solutions for pollutant removal. Various studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of PU-based ab(d)sorbents in ab(d)sorbing oil, organic pollutants, and dyes from aquatic environments, which demonstrates their versatility and efficiency in environmental remediation efforts. In addition, the incorporation of recycled PU foams into sound absorbers provides a sustainable solution to reduce urban noise pollution. These studies highlight the promising role of recycled PU foams in advancing environmental protection by reusing waste materials for practical applications in the construction, automotive, and industrial sectors. Also, innovative approaches such as the incorporation of GTR in PU foam matrices and the use of waste PU sponges to generate steam and energy emphasize the potential of PU recycling in addressing environmental challenges and promoting sustainability. Overall, the findings of these studies emphasize the importance of developing cost-effective and environmentally friendly recycling techniques to minimize the environmental and health impacts associated with the production and disposal of PU foam. Developing reliable and cost-effective recycling strategies for PUs is a challenging but promising endeavor given environmental concerns. Furthermore, the lack of a structured market for PU waste recycling, together with uncertainty in cost and emission factors, highlights areas for future research and development. Continued research and innovation in this field will lead to continuous improvements in PU recycling technologies, along with efforts to minimize environmental impact and energy consumption. It is expected to improve overall waste management practices and contribute to the anticipated progress towards a circular economy, gradually positioning PU as a valuable material in environmentally friendly frameworks.

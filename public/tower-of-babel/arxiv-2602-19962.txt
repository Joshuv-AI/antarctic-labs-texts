Atomic parity violation in highly charged 40,48 Ca and 208 Pb ions
A. V. Viatkina 1,2 , Ch. G. Mertens 1,3 , B. Ohayon 4 , V. A. Yerokhin 5 , A. Surzhykov 1,2

Abstract
We calculate parity-violation-induced E1 amplitudes for the 1 ​ s → 2 ​ s 1s\rightarrow 2s and 1 ​ s 2 ​ 2 ​ s → 1 ​ s 2 ​ 3 ​ s 1s^{2}2s\rightarrow 1s^{2}3s transitions in H- and Li-like ions of 40 Ca, 48 Ca, and 208 Pb. In our analysis, we account for neutron skin effects and nuclear uncertainties for each nucleus. We consider the spin-independent weak-interaction contribution of the Z 0 Z^{0} boson described by standard model, as well as the effects of a hypothetical new Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson of varying mass. We conclude that the neutron-skin corrections in the 40,48 Ca isotope pair can mostly be neglected when considering Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson effects, which is an advantage for the search for new parity-violating physics. On the other hand, both the neutron skin effect and the sensitivity to hypothetical Z ′ Z^{\prime} interactions in 208 Pb is shown to be significant.



I
Introduction

Atomic parity violation (APV) is one of the leading tools for testing the standard model (SM) and its possible extensions in the low-energy regime. Within the SM, the violation of parity symmetry occurs due to the weak interaction
Navas et al. 2024
. Consequently, if the APV contribution is measured to be different from theoretical predictions based on the SM, it may indicate the existence of a new parity-violating interaction, for instance, one mediated by a new light
Z^{\prime}
boson
Safronova et al. 2018
;
Dzuba et al. 2017
. Alternatively, bounds can be placed on the parameters of such hypothetical couplings when the APV effects are found to agree with the SM.

To this day, the majority of APV measurements have been performed in neutral atoms
Wood et al. 1997
;
Antypas et al. 2019
;
Barkov and Zolotorev 1979
. However, given the prospect of storage-ring-based spectroscopy—exemplified by Gamma Factory project at CERN
Krasny 2015
which is expected to produce primary photons with energies up to 60 keV—there is a real possibility of probing APV in photoexcitations of highly charged ions (HCIs)
Richter et al. 2022
;
Budker et al. 2020
. There are several advantages of APV experiments with HCIs over those in neutral atoms or multiply-charged ions: most notably, HCIs have a much simpler electronic structure, which is favorable for theoretical calculations, as well as a larger overlap of electronic wave functions with the nucleus that results in an enhancement of the APV effect
Budker et al. 2020
.

Apart from the new-physics searches, APV can provide insights into nuclear neutron distributions, which, in turn, help determine the parameters of the equation of state for neutron-rich matter
Thiel et al. 2019
. These distributions are often characterized by the so-called
neutron skin
, which is the difference between the root-mean-square (rms) radii of neutron and proton densities. While the proton distribution is relatively easy to measure in experiments relying on the electromagnetic interaction, the neutron distribution cannot be probed in the same straightforward way, since neutrons lack electric charge. Consequently, the neutron rms radii are poorly known in comparison to their proton counterparts. On the other hand, when
Z^{0}
-boson-mediated weak interaction is considered, electrons couple to neutrons much more strongly than they do to protons
Navas et al. 2024
. Hence, the dominant part of the APV effect depends on the neutron density and as a result, the APV magnitude can be used to probe neutron distributions.

One can note that APV is not the only way to measure nuclear neutron density. Recently, neutron skins of
48
Ca and
208
Pb have been determined in parity-violating neutron scattering experiments—in CREX
Adhikari et al. 2022
and PREX-II
Adhikari et al. 2021
collaborations, respectively. In the present work, we use these results to calculate the APV amplitudes in H- and Li-like
40,48
Ca and
208
Pb, taking into account the difference between proton and neutron rms radii. The significance of the pair
40
Ca and
48
Ca arises from the fact that both isotopes have relatively small neutron skins and almost equal charge radii. From the new-physics-search perspective, these characteristics of
40,48
Ca nuclei help separate new parity-violating interactions coupling to protons from those coupling to neutrons.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we examine the theory behind spin-independent standard-model (Sec.
II.1
) and hypothetical new-physics (Sec.
II.2
) APV interactions. Sec.
III
deals with the interplay between the neutron-skin and new-physics effects in APV by considering the relevant atomic matrix elements (Sec.
III.1
), their differences, and their ratios (Sec.
III.2
). We present our results in Sec.
IV
and discuss the nuclear parameters (Sec.
IV.1
) and the calculation methods (Sec.
IV.2
) that we use. Finally, we analyze our findings in Sec.
IV.3
. The summary is given in Sec.
V
.

\hbar

c

\alpha

m

II
Theory

II.1
Standard Model APV

A major mechanism of atomic parity violation is the neutral-current interaction between electrons and nucleons, mediated by the
Z^{0}
boson. For spinless nuclei and low energies, this interaction can be expressed through an effective four-fermion Hamiltonian density
Navas et al. 2024
:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SM}}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i}C^{(\mathcal{N})}_{1i}\,\overline{\psi}^{(e)}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi^{(e)}\overline{\psi}^{(\mathcal{N})}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\psi^{(\mathcal{N})}_{i},

(1)

where
G_{F}=1.166\times 10^{-5}(\hbar c)^{3}
GeV
-2
is the Fermi constant,
\gamma^{\mu}
are the Dirac gamma matrices, and
\gamma^{5}=-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
is the ‘parity-violation’ gamma matrix. In Eq. (
1

\psi^{(e)}
designates electron spinors and
\psi^{(\mathcal{N})}_{i}
nucleon (proton or neutron) spinors, while the coefficients
C^{(\mathcal{N})}_{1i}
characterize the interaction between the electron and the nucleon. This interaction is described as point-like since we are interested in APV processes whose characteristic energy (and momentum transfer) is much lower than the mass of the
Z^{0}
boson,
M_{Z}c^{2}\approx 91.2
GeV.

If we assume that the nucleons are non-relativistic, Eq. (
1
) simplifies to an effective spin-independent parity violation Hamiltonian:

\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}\left(\vec{r}\right)=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\left[C^{(p)}_{1}Z\rho_{p}(\vec{r})+C^{(n)}_{1}N\rho_{n}(\vec{r})\right]\gamma^{5}\ ,

(2)

where
\rho_{p}(\vec{r})
and
\rho_{n}(\vec{r})
are the normalized proton and neutron densities,
Z
is the nuclear charge number, and
N
is the neutron number. Here, we distinguish between the coupling coefficients
C^{(p)}_{1}
for protons and
C^{(n)}_{1}
for neutrons. Their SM tree-level expressions

are

\displaystyle C^{(p)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-4\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\right)\approx\frac{1}{2}\times 0.045\ ,

(3)

\displaystyle C^{(n)}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\ ,

(4)

where
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle and
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=0.23873(5)
for low energies
Navas et al. 2024

Navas et al. 2024
;
Erler and Su 2013
;
Blunden et al. 2012
results in the following values for the coupling coefficients:

\displaystyle C^{(p)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\times 0.071,

(5)

\displaystyle C^{(n)}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\times 0.989.

(6)

Note that since
|C^{(n)}_{1}|\gg|C^{(p)}_{1}|
, the dominant effect in spin-independent APV comes from the neutrons.

The Hamiltonian (

\rho_{p}(\vec{r})
and neutron
\rho_{n}(\vec{r})

\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}\left(\vec{r}\right)\approx\frac{G_{F}}{2\sqrt{2}}Q_{W}\rho(\vec{r})\gamma^{5},

(7)

is introduced. Based on Eqs. (
3
) and (

Q_{W}=-N+Z(1-4\sin^{2}\theta_{W}).

(8)

When radiative corrections to

In the present work, however, our main focus is on the instances with

where

are described by Eqs. (

II.2
New APV Forces

In the previous section, we investigated parity violation as described by the standard model. Here, we consider a hypothetical
Z^{\prime}
boson of mass
m_{Z^{\prime}}
which, in addition to
Z^{0}
, couples electrons to nucleons. If we treat nucleons nonrelativistically, the presence of the
Z^{\prime}
boson will amount to a parity-violating Yukawa potential
Dzuba et al. 2017

V(\vec{r}-\vec{R})=\frac{g_{e\mathcal{N}}}{4\pi}\ \hbar c\ \frac{e^{-m_{Z^{\prime}}c|\vec{r}-\vec{R}|/\hbar}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{R}|}\gamma^{5}\ ,

(9)

where
|\vec{r}-\vec{R}|
is the distance between the electron located at
\vec{r}
and the nucleon located at
\vec{R}
, and the coupling constant
g_{e\mathcal{N}}
quantifies the interaction strength. As before, we are interested only in the nuclear-spin-independent effect of the
Z^{\prime}
boson. Consequently, to obtain a Hamiltonian suitable for atomic calculations, we need to integrate Eq. (
9
) over the nucleon density:

\hat{h}_{Z^{\prime}}(\vec{r})=\hbar c\gamma^{5}\frac{g_{e\mathcal{N}}}{4\pi}N_{\mathcal{N}}\int\rho_{\mathcal{N}}(\vec{R})\frac{e^{-m_{Z^{\prime}}c\left|\vec{r}-\vec{R}\right|/\hbar}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{R}|}d\vec{R}.

(10)

Here
N_{\mathcal{N}}=Z,N
is the number of nucleons (protons or neutrons) and
\rho_{\mathcal{N}}(\vec{r})
the normalized nucleon density. In the heavy-mass limit
m_{Z^{\prime}}\rightarrow\infty
and assuming the spherical distribution of nucleons
\rho_{\mathcal{N}}(\vec{r})=\rho_{\mathcal{N}}(r)
, Eq. (
10
) simplifies to

\hat{h}_{Z^{\prime}}(r)=\frac{2g_{e\mathcal{N}}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}c^{4}}\,(\hbar c)^{3}N_{\mathcal{N}}\rho_{\mathcal{N}}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5},

(11)

which has the same structure as the Hamiltonian (
2
) above.

III
Neutron Skin and New Physics

Weak interaction (
2
) mixes opposite-parity levels in atomic systems. As a result, atomic transitions that are forbidden under parity selection rules become weakly allowed. A transition of this type, which is especially relevant to experiment
Wood et al. 1997
;
Antypas et al. 2019
;
Craik 2025
, is the PV-induced E1 transition between two levels of the same parity. Labeling these levels
|a\rangle
and
|b\rangle
, one can obtain the amplitude of the photoexcitation
|a\rangle\rightarrow|b\rangle
within second-order perturbation theory:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PV}}=\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\langle b|\hat{D}_{z}|k\rangle\langle k|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}|a\rangle}{E_{a}-E_{k}}+\frac{\langle b|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}|k\rangle\langle k|\hat{D}_{z}|a\rangle}{E_{b}-E_{k}}\right),

(12)

where
E_{a}<E_{b}
and the incident light is assumed to be linearly polarized in the
z
direction. Moreover, in Eq. (
12
),
\hat{D}_{z}
is the
z
-component of the electron dipole operator, and the sum runs over all intermediate atomic states
k
with energies
E_{k}
.

In the present work, we consider transitions between the ground and the first excited
s
-states in H-like and Li-like ions. In such a case, the sum in Eq. (
12
) runs effectively over the
|k\rangle=|np_{1/2}\rangle
intermediate states. The main contribution to this sum arises from just a few terms corresponding to the intermediate levels that are nearly degenerate with respect to either the initial
a
or the final
b
atomic state. More specifically, for the
1s\rightarrow 2s
transition in H-like ions, there is a single dominant term with
|k\rangle=|2p_{1/2}\rangle
, and for the
1s^{2}2s\rightarrow 1s^{2}3s
transition in Li-like ions, there are two such terms, with
|k\rangle=|1s^{2}2p_{1/2}\rangle
and with
|k\rangle=|1s^{2}3p_{1/2}\rangle
, see Fig.
1
.

III.1
Matrix Elements

In order to illustrate the role of different contributions to atomic parity violation, let us consider the simplest case of the
1s\rightarrow 2s
transition in a H-like ion. Additionally, let us limit ourselves to the dominant term of the sum in Eq. (
12
):

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PV}}\approx\frac{\langle 2s|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}|2p_{1/2}\rangle\langle 2p_{1/2}|\hat{D}_{z}|1s\rangle}{E_{2s}-E_{2p_{1/2}}}\ .

(13)

The matrix element of the operator
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\langle 2s|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}

\displaystyle|2p_{1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{2s}^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}(\vec{r})\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})d\vec{r},

(14)

where
\psi_{2s}(\vec{r})
and
\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})
are the relativistic electron wavefunctions. By employing the explicit form of the operator
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}
[see Eq. (
2
)], we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\left(C^{(p)}_{1}Z\int\rho_{p}(\vec{r})\psi_{2s}^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5}\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})d\vec{r}\right.+

\displaystyle\left.C^{(n)}_{1}N\int\rho_{n}(\vec{r})\psi_{2s}(\vec{r})^{\dagger}\gamma^{5}\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})d\vec{r}\right).

(15)

Assuming that both proton
\rho_{p}
and neutron
\rho_{n}
densities are spherically symmetric and normalized to unity,
4\pi\int\rho_{p,n}(r)r^{2}dr=1
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\times

\displaystyle\left(C^{(p)}_{1}Z\int drr^{2}\rho_{p}(r)\int\!\int d\phi d\theta\sin\theta\psi_{2s}^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5}\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})\right.+

\displaystyle\left.C^{(n)}_{1}N\int drr^{2}\rho_{n}(r)\int\!\int d\phi d\theta\sin\theta\psi_{2s}^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5}\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})\right).

(16)

One can see that both terms have the same angular integral, which can be evaluated using the standard representation of Dirac wavefunctions:

\psi_{n\kappa m}\left(\vec{r}\right)=\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}f_{n\kappa}(r)\Omega_{\kappa m}(\theta,\phi)\\
ig_{n\kappa}(r)\Omega_{-\kappa m}(\theta,\phi)\end{pmatrix},

(17)

where
f_{n\kappa}
and
g_{n\kappa}
are the radial components and
\Omega_{\kappa m}
are spherical spinors;
n
is the principal quantum number,
\kappa
the Dirac quantum number, and
m
the projection of the angular momentum. Making use of this expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\!\int d\phi d\theta

\displaystyle\sin\theta\psi_{2s}^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5}\psi_{2p_{1/2}}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{r^{2}}\left[f_{2s}(r)g_{2p_{1/2}}(r)-g_{2s}(r)f_{2p_{1/2}}(r)\right].

(18)

To illustrate the effect of the neutron skin on the APV, it is instructive to rewrite Eq. (
18
) as

\displaystyle\frac{i}{r^{2}}\left[f_{2s}(r)g_{2p_{1/2}}(r)-g_{2s}(r)f_{2p_{1/2}}(r)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{i}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\psi}f_{\psi}(r),

(19)

where the normalization factor
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is chosen in such a manner that
f_{\psi}(0)=1
. One can present
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
as a product

\mathcal{N}_{\psi}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P},

(20)

where
\mathcal{A}
depends exclusively on the electronic configuration, while
\mathcal{P}
includes nuclear parameters and can be approximated
Viatkina et al. 2019
as

\mathcal{P}\approx\left(2Zr_{p}/a_{0}\right)^{2\gamma-2},

(21)

where
a_{0}
is the Bohr radius,
r_{p}
is the nuclear rms charge radius, and
\gamma=\sqrt{1-Z^{2}\alpha^{2}}
.
By inserting Eqs. (
19
)–(
20
) into Eq. (
III.1
), we can express the matrix element of the operator
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}
in the form

\mathcal{M}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}\left(C_{1p}Zq_{p}+C_{1n}Nq_{n}\right),

(22)

where

q_{p,n}=\int f_{\psi}(r)\rho_{p,n}(r)dr,

(23)

is the integral of the normalized function
f_{\psi}(r)
over nuclear density. One can further simplify the matrix element (
22
) as

\mathcal{M}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}\tilde{Q}_{W},

(24)

where we introduced an
effective weak charge

\tilde{Q}_{W}=C_{1p}Zq_{p}+C_{1n}Nq_{n}.

(25)

Note that for the case
q_{p}=q_{n}\equiv q
this effective parameter reads as
\tilde{Q}_{W}=q\cdot Q_{W}
, where
Q_{W}
is the conventional weak charge.

Until now, we have discussed the evaluation of the matrix element (
III.1
) within the framework of the standard model. A hypothetical new-physics interaction mediated by a
Z^{\prime}
boson, described by the Hamiltonian (
10
), would modify the matrix element as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\langle 2s|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}+\hat{h}_{Z^{\prime}}|2p_{1/2}\rangle=

\displaystyle i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}\left[Z\left(C_{1p}q_{p}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{p}\right)+N\left(C_{1n}q_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n}\right)\right],

(26)

where we included small hypothetical new-physics corrections
\Delta\tilde{Q}_{p}
and
\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n}
to the proton and neutron contributions to the effective weak charge. These corrections can be derived in a similar way to

10

III.2
Isotopic Difference and Ratio for
40,48
Ca

It is instructive to study how
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PV}}
changes between two isotopes of the same atom. Again, let us consider the simplest case of the dominant term in the

transition of a H-like ion, see Eq. (
13

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}-\mathcal{M}
and the ratio
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}/\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}-\mathcal{M}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\left[Z\left(C_{1p}q^{\prime}_{p}+\Delta\tilde{Q}^{\prime}_{p}\right)+N^{\prime}\left(C_{1n}q^{\prime}_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}^{\prime}_{n}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}-\mathcal{M}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\times\left[N^{\prime}\left(C_{1n}q^{\prime}_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}^{\prime}_{n}\right)-N\left(C_{1n}q_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n}\right)\right].

(28)

Second, the proton and neutron densities in the

Ca isotope (see Ref.
Emrich et al. 1983

IV.1
below), since
N=Z=20

q_{p}\approx q_{n}

q_{n}

\delta q^{\prime}_{n,\mathrm{nsk}}\equiv q^{\prime}_{n}-q^{\prime}_{p}\approx q^{\prime}_{n}-q_{n},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{M}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{P}N^{\prime}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{\Delta N}{N^{\prime}}\left(C_{1n}q_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n}\right)+C_{1n}\delta q^{\prime}_{n,\mathrm{nsk}}\right]\ ,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{Z\left(C_{1p}q^{\prime}_{p}+\Delta\tilde{Q}^{\prime}_{p}\right)+N^{\prime}\left(C_{1n}q^{\prime}_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{Z\left(C_{1p}q_{p}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{p}\right)+N\left(C_{1n}q_{n}+\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n}\right)}.

(34)

where the new physics contribution is again suppressed by
\Delta N/N^{\prime}\approx 0.3
.

As seen from Eqs. (
31
) and (
34
), the neutron skin
\delta q^{\prime}_{n,\mathrm{nsk}}
and the new physics
\Delta\tilde{Q}_{n,p}
corrections enter the matrix element difference and ratio on an equal footing. Therefore, in order to investigate the new-physics PV effects, knowledge of the neutron skin corrections is required. In what follows, we will calculate those corrections for the
40,48
Ca isotope pair. Moreover, even though it is not the main focus of our study, we will present the neutron skin and new physics corrections for
208
Pb, since its neutron skin, similarly to
48
Ca, has been measured
Adhikari et al. 2021
.

IV.1
Nuclear Parameters

In our calculations, we use a normalized spherically symmetric Fermi distribution for nuclear charge densities

\rho_{p}(r)=\frac{\rho_{0}}{1+\exp(\frac{r-c}{z})}\ ,

(35)

where the normalization
\rho_{0}
is chosen in such a way that
4\pi\int\rho_{p}(r)r^{2}dr=1
.
The values of the radius parameter
c
and the diffuseness
z
for
40,48
Ca and
208
Pb nuclei are displayed in Tab.
1
. They are based on a combined analysis of electron scattering and muonic spectroscopy data.

In our analysis, we assume that electron scattering experiments are best for measuring ratios of moments (mostly affected by the nuclear shape), while muonic atom x-ray spectroscopy is best for measuring a certain integral quantity called the Barrett moment.
For each of the three nuclei that we consider, we adjust the
c,z
parameters until both the relevant Barrett moment (from muonic atoms) and the measured ratio of the fourth and second moments
\langle r^{4}\rangle/\langle r^{2}\rangle
, where
\langle r^{n}\rangle=\int r^{n}\rho_{p}(r)r^{2}dr
, are reproduced
Yerokhin and Ohayon 2026
.

The reported experimental uncertainty in scattering experiments is often small compared with the variation when considering different datasets and analysis methods
Ohayon 2025
. In light of this, we consider two different parametrizations of the charge distribution for each isotope, with their differences approximating the uncertainty in the nuclear model.

IV.3
Analysis

Having reviewed the calculations of atomic energy levels and matrix elements of the operators
\hat{D}_{z}
,
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}
,
\hat{h}_{Z^{\prime}}
, we discuss the obtained results below. In particular, we move our focus onto the interplay between neutron skin and new physics. For the purposes of our discussion, we split the standard-model (SM) and new-physics (NP) PV matrix elements into neutron and proton parts. Following Eq. (
2
), the SM leading matrix element for H-like ions can be written as

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{(SM)}}=C^{(p)}_{1}Z\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{p}+C^{(n)}_{1}N\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{n}\ ,

(39)

where
\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{p,n}=\langle 2s|\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM},p,n}|2p_{1/2}\rangle
are the matrix elements of the partial operator

\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM},p,n}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\rho_{p,n}(\vec{r})\gamma^{5}.

(40)

By rewriting the matrix elements for all intermediate states in the form analogous to Eq. (
39
), we arrive at the PV amplitude:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{PV}}^{(\mathrm{SM})}=C^{(p)}_{1}Z\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{\mathrm{PV},p}+C^{(n)}_{1}N\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{\mathrm{PV},n}\ ,

(41)

where the partial amplitudes
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(SM)}}_{\mathrm{PV},p,n}
are calculated with the operators
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM},p,n}
(
40
) instead of the full operator
\hat{h}_{\mathrm{SM}}
(
2
), see Eq. (
12
).

Furthermore, we rewrite the NP counterparts of the SM matrix elements and amplitudes in a similar way, distinguishing proton and neutron contributions, see Eq. (
10
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{(NP)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{3}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}c}\left(g_{ep}Z\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{p}+g_{en}N\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{n}\right),

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{\mathrm{PV}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{3}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}c}\left(g_{ep}Z\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{\mathrm{PV},p}+g_{en}N\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{\mathrm{PV},n}\right).

(43)

Here,
g_{ep}
and
g_{en}
designate the unknown coupling strengths of a hypothetical
Z^{\prime}
boson to protons and neutrons, respectively. The PV matrix elements
\mathrm{m}^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{p,n}=\langle 2s|\hat{h}_{{Z^{\prime}}\,p,n}|2p_{1/2}\rangle
and amplitudes
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{(NP)}}_{\mathrm{PV},p,n}
are calculated with the NP partial operators

\hat{h}_{{Z^{\prime}}\,p,n}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\gamma^{5}\left(\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}c}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\int\frac{e^{-m_{Z^{\prime}}c\left|\vec{r}-\vec{R}\right|/\hbar}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{R}|}\rho_{p,n}(\vec{R})d\vec{R}\ ,

(44)

which correspond to Eq. (
10
) divided by the square reduced Compton wavelength of the
Z^{\prime}
boson,
\lambdabar_{c,Z^{\prime}}^{2}=\hbar^{2}/(m_{Z^{\prime}}c)^{2}
.

Up to now, we have discussed how the hydrogenic matrix elements and amplitudes can be split into proton and neutron parts. Similar analysis can be performed for Li-like ions, leading to formulas for SM and NP amplitudes equivalent to Eqs. (
41
) and (
43
).

V
Conclusion

In summary, we present a theoretical investigation of atomic parity violation amplitudes in H- and Li-like ions. Special attention is paid to the effects that arise from neutron skin and hypothetical new physics particles. To explore these effects, detailed relativistic calculations for H- and Li-like
40,48
Ca and
208
Pb ions have been performed. Based on their results, we argue that the isotope pair
40
Ca and
48
Ca provides a unique testing ground for the search for new physics. That is because the PV amplitude in this isotope pair is barely sensitive to the neutron skin effects, and the nuclear charge radii
r_{p}(^{40}\mathrm{Ca})
and
r_{p}(^{48}\mathrm{Ca})
are almost equal. On the other hand, a prominent neutron skin effect is predicted for
208
Pb, which makes highly charged ions of this isotope favorable for neutron skin detection by means of APV, in a way complementary to the PREX-II
Adhikari et al. 2021
experiment.

Non-Degenerate Multilinear Singular Multipliers with Fractional Rank
Jianghao Zhang

Abstract
We establish L p L^{p} estimates for multilinear multipliers acting on ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -tuples of functions on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We assume that the multiplier satisfies symbol estimates outside a linear subspace of dimension m m . The difficulty of proving L p L^{p} bounds increases with the rank m d \frac{m}{d} . Such bounds are unachievable using existing tools when m d ≥ n 2 \frac{m}{d}\geq\frac{n}{2} . And when ⌈ m d ⌉ < n 2 \lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil<\frac{n}{2} , our results do not improve upon those in [ 22 ] . Therefore, our focus is on the fractional rank case m d < n 2 ≤ ⌈ m d ⌉ \frac{m}{d}<\frac{n}{2}\leq\lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil proposed in [ 4 ] . We extend the results of [ 4 ] by covering a broader range of Lebesgue exponents. Additionally, we develop the tree counting argument, thereby including cases with larger m d \frac{m}{d} compared to [ 4 ] . In doing so, we also identify and correct an inaccuracy in [ 4 ] .

We use
\xi_{j}=(\xi_{j,1},\cdots,\xi_{j,d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Definition 1
.

Let

\begin{split}T_{\mathfrak{m}}&(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(x)\\
&:=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-1}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j})}\mathfrak{m}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j})\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{f_{n-1}}(\xi_{n-1})d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n-1}.\end{split}

The associated multilinear form is given by

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})&:=\langle T_{\mathfrak{m}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1}),f_{n}\rangle\\
&=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n-1}}\mathfrak{m}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j})\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{f_{n-1}}(\xi_{n-1})\widehat{f_{n}}(-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j})d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n-1},\end{split}

where the last line also serves as the definition of (
1.1

Definition 5
.

Fix

Let

(v_{1},\cdots,v_{m})\in\Gamma_{0}^{m}

Theorem 2
.

Let
n,d,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+},n\geq 3
such that
\frac{m}{d}<\frac{n}{2}
and
1<p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1}\leq\infty,0<q<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{n-1}}
. Denote

by

. Let
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{0}
be an
m\text{-dimensional}
linear subspace and
\mathfrak{m}:\Gamma_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function satisfying (
1.2
) for some large
N_{2}
depending on

and

. Consider the estimate

\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})\|_{q}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\|f_{j}\|_{p_{j}},\ \forall f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.5)

1.

When
\lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil<\frac{n}{2}
, assume
\Gamma
satisfies Type I non-degenerate condition. Then (
1.5
) holds whenever
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1},q)
satisfies

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\Upsilon}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<n-2\alpha+\frac{1}{2},\ \forall\Upsilon\subset[n],\ \#\Upsilon=n-2\alpha+1,\\
\sum_{j\in\Upsilon}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<n-2\alpha+1,\ \forall\Upsilon\subset[n],\ \#\Upsilon=n-1,\end{split}

(1.6)

with
\alpha:=\lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil
.

2.

When
\frac{n}{2}\leq\lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil
and
\frac{m}{d}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, assume
\Gamma
satisfies Type I and Type II non-degenerate conditions. Then (
1.5
) holds whenever
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1},q)
satisfies (
1.6
) with
\alpha=\frac{n-1}{2}
.

3.

When
\frac{n}{2}\leq\lceil\frac{m}{d}\rceil
and
\frac{n-1}{2}<\frac{m}{d}
, assume
\Gamma
satisfies Type I and Type II non-degenerate conditions. Then (
1.5
) holds whenever
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1},q)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Upsilon}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<\frac{n+1}{2}-\alpha,\ \forall\Upsilon\subset[n],\ \#\Upsilon=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Upsilon}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<\frac{n+2}{2}-\alpha,\ \forall\Upsilon\subset[n],\ \#\Upsilon=2,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Upsilon}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<n-2\alpha+1,\ \forall\Upsilon\subset[n],\ \#\Upsilon=n-1,

.

One can also consider multilinear operators in the kernel formulation. Let
\mathfrak{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{L}_{n-1}:\mathbb{R}^{d(n-1)-m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

\rank{\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{L}_{1}\\

Here

The proof of Theorem

[

]
[

Lemma 8
.

Let
L\gg 1
and
\mathscr{A}
be a finite
L\text{-sparse}
collection of intervals in
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a new collection
\mathscr{G}=\{G(A):A\in\mathscr{A}\}
of intervals such that

1.

The property
A\subset G(A)\subset 2A
hold for any
A\in\mathscr{A}
.

2.

\mathscr{G}
is a
L^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{-central}
grid.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
\#\mathscr{A}
. The
\#\mathscr{A}=1
case is obvious. Assume the conclusion holds for
n-1
, we prove it for
n
. Write the elements in
\{s(A):A\in\mathscr{A}\}
as
s_{1}>\cdots>s_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}
. Take one
A_{1}\in\mathscr{A}
such that
s(A_{1})=a_{1}
. Apply the induction hypothesis to
\mathscr{A}\backslash\{A_{1}\}
. We get
G(A),A\neq A_{1}
. Now we define
G(A_{1})
. First let
G_{1}:=A_{1}
. The define
G_{k},2\leq k\leq n^{{}^{\prime}}
, as follows:

G_{k}:=\conv\Big(G_{k-1}\cup\big(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A:s(A)=s_{k},\\
d\big(G(A),G_{k-1}\big)\leq 10s_{k}\end{subarray}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}G(A)\big)\Big).

We let
G(A_{1}):=G_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}
. First note that

|G_{k}|\leq|G_{k-1}|+O(W^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{k}),\ \forall 2\leq k\leq n^{{}^{\prime}}.

(4.1)

Thus

|G_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}|\leq s_{1}+\sum_{k=2}^{n^{{}^{\prime}}}O(W^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{k})<(1+\frac{1}{100})s_{1}.

This means
G_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}\subset 2A_{1}
since
A_{1}\subset G_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}
. It then suffices to show
G(A)\cap G(\widetilde{A})\neq\emptyset,s(A)<s(\widetilde{A})
implies
L^{\frac{1}{2}}G(A)\subset G(\widetilde{A})
. By the induction hypothesis, we only need to tackle the
\widetilde{A}=A_{1}
case. Assume
s(A)=s_{k_{0}}
. It suffices to prove
\big(G(A),G_{k_{0}-1}\big)\leq 10s_{k_{0}}
. If this does not hold, then
d\big(G(A),G_{k_{0}-1}\big)>10s_{k_{0}}
. Write
G_{k_{0}-1}=[a,b)
. Assume
G(A)\subset(b,\infty)
without loss of generality. Now (
4.1
) implies

|G_{n^{{}^{\prime}}}|\leq|G_{k_{0}}|+\frac{1}{100}s_{k_{0}},

so
\big(G(A),G_{k_{0}}\big)<\frac{1}{100}s_{k_{0}}
. Combine this with
d\big(G(A),G_{k_{0}-1}\big)>10s_{k_{0}}
. We get there exits
A^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
s\big(G(A^{{}^{\prime}})\big)=s_{k_{0}}
and

\displaystyle d\big(G(A^{{}^{\prime}}),G_{k_{0}-1}\big)\leq 10s_{k_{0}},

\displaystyle L^{\frac{1}{2}}G(A^{{}^{\prime}})\cap(b,\infty)\neq\emptyset.

But then we have

\displaystyle A\subset\big(b,b+O(L^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{k_{0}})\big),

\displaystyle A^{{}^{\prime}}\subset(b-2L^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{k_{0}},b+20s_{k_{0}}),

which means
d(A,A^{{}^{\prime}})=O(L^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{k_{0}})\ll Ls_{k_{0}}
, a contradiction. This finishes the proof.
∎

.

be a point, and

,

2.

\mathcal{R}_{j}\lesssim(I_{j},\xi_{j})

\xi_{j}\subset C_{2}\circ\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j})

Moreover, let

1.

\mathcal{R}_{j}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
if
I(\mathcal{R}_{j})\subset I(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j})

\Xi(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j})\subset\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j})

2.

\mathcal{R}_{j}\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
if

and
C_{2}\circ\Xi(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j})\subset C_{2}\circ\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j})

We also need the vector version of these relations. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a dyadic interval,
\xi\in\Gamma_{0}
be a point, and
\mathcal{R}
be a vector rectangle. Define

1.

\mathcal{R}\leq(I,\xi)
if
\mathcal{R}_{j}\leq(I,\xi_{j}),\forall j\in[n]

2.

\mathcal{R}\lesssim(I,\xi)
if
\mathcal{R}_{j}\lesssim(I,\xi_{j}),\forall j\in[n]
.

Similarly, for two vector rectangles
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{R}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{R}_{n}),\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{n})

1.

\mathcal{R}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
if
\mathcal{R}_{j}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j},\forall j\in[n]
,

2.

\mathcal{R}\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}

\mathcal{R}_{j}\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j},\forall j\in[n]
.

We will work with the relation
\lesssim

Definition 11
.

Let
j\in[n]
. A non-empty collection
\mathscr{T}_{j}\subset\{\mathcal{R}_{j}:\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{R}\}
is called a
j\text{-tree}
if there exists a dyadic interval
I_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a point
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{j}\lesssim(I_{j},\xi_{j}),\forall\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}
. All such
I_{j}
’s are intersect with each other, so the smallest one exists. Denote it by
I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}}
. If there exists
\xi_{j}
such that
\xi_{j}\subset C_{2}\circ\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j})\backslash 10\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j}),\forall\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}
, then we say
\mathscr{T}_{j}
is lacunary. Moreover, A non-empty collection
\mathscr{T}\subset\mathscr{R}
is called a vector tree if there exists a dyadic interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a point
\xi\in\Gamma_{0}
such that
\mathcal{R}\lesssim(I,\xi),\forall\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}
. Denote the smallest
I
by
I_{\mathscr{T}}
.

Lacunary
j\text{-trees}
provide us with the classical configuration to apply the C-Z theory. More precisely, we shall estimate sums over trees by
\mathbf{M}_{j}
defined below and estimate
\mathbf{M}_{j}
using a localized C-Z type argument.

Definition 12
.

Let
j\in[n]
and
\mathscr{P}_{j}\subset\{\mathcal{R}_{j}:\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{R}\}
be a collection. Define

\mathbf{M}_{j}(\mathscr{P}_{j}):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{T}_{j}\subset\mathscr{P}_{j},\\
\mathscr{T}_{j}\text{\ is a lacunary $j\text{-tree}$}\end{subarray}}\big(\frac{1}{|I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}}|}\sum_{\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}}|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark
.

Recall we have fixed
f_{j},j\in[n]
. We let
\mathbf{M}_{j}(\mathscr{P}_{j}):=0
if
\mathscr{P}_{j}=\emptyset
.

The geometry of Whitney decomposition guarantees every vector tree has at least two lacunary indices so that we can bound sums over trees using
\mathbf{M}_{j}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a vector tree. Then

\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}}|I(\mathcal{R})|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle|\lesssim|I_{\mathscr{T}}|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{M}_{j}(\mathscr{T}_{j}),

where
\mathscr{T}_{j}:=\{\mathcal{R}_{j}:\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}\}
.

Proof.

Take
\xi\in\Gamma_{0}
such that
\mathcal{R}\lesssim(I_{\mathscr{T}},\xi),\forall\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}
. Fix a large constant
C_{0}\ll C_{1}
. Let
\Upsilon:=\{\Xi(\mathcal{R}):\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}\}
. Here are some facts about
\Upsilon
:

1.

For
\Xi,\widetilde{\Xi}\in\Upsilon
,
s(\Xi)=s(\widetilde{\Xi})
implies
\Xi=\widetilde{\Xi}
. This is due to
C_{2}\circ\Xi\cap C_{2}\circ\widetilde{\Xi}\neq\emptyset
and the
C_{3}\text{-sparse}
property.

2.

For all but at most one
\Xi\in\Upsilon
, we can find one
j
such that
\xi_{j}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{j}\backslash C_{0}\Xi_{j}
. In fact, if there exist two
\Xi,\widetilde{\Xi}\in\Upsilon
such that
\xi\in C_{0}\Xi\cap C_{0}\widetilde{\Xi}
. The first implies
s(\Xi)\neq s(\widetilde{\Xi})
. Assume
s(\Xi)<s(\widetilde{\Xi})
without loss of generality. Then
d(\Xi,\widetilde{\Xi})\lesssim C_{0}s(\widetilde{\Xi})
. Combining this with
d(\Xi,\Gamma)\sim C_{1}s(\Xi)
gives

d(\widetilde{\Xi},\Gamma)\leq d(\widetilde{\Xi},\Xi)+d(\Xi,\Gamma)\lesssim C_{0}s(\widetilde{\Xi})+C_{1}s(\Xi)\ll C_{1}s(\widetilde{\Xi}),

a contradiction.

3.

If
\Xi\in\Upsilon
satisfy
\Xi_{j}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{j}\backslash C_{0}\Xi_{j}
for some
j
, then there must exist
j^{{}^{\prime}}\neq j
such that
\xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}\backslash 10\Xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}
. Otherwise
\xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}\in 10\Xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}},\forall j^{{}^{\prime}}\neq j
. Take
\tau\in\Xi\cap\Gamma_{0}
. Then
d(\xi_{j},\tau_{j})\gtrsim C_{0}s(\Xi)
but
d(\xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}},\tau_{j^{{}^{\prime}}})\lesssim s(\Xi),\forall j^{{}^{\prime}}\neq j
. On the other hand, we have

\sum_{j}\xi_{j}=\sum_{j}\tau_{j}=0\Longrightarrow\xi_{j}-\tau_{j}=\sum_{j^{{}^{\prime}}\neq j}(\tau_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}-\xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}).

This is a contradiction since
C_{0}
is large.

The above properties imply that we can write

\mathscr{T}=\mathscr{T}(\Xi)\cup\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq j^{{}^{\prime}}}\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})\Big),

where
\Xi(\mathcal{R})=\Xi,\forall\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(\Xi)
, and
\xi_{j}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{j}\backslash 10\Xi_{j},\xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}\backslash 10\Xi_{j^{{}^{\prime}}},\forall\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})
. Note that for
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{R}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{R}_{n})
, the first property above implies
\mathcal{R}\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{j}
is a bijection. We can then estimate the sum over
\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})
as follows:

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})}&|I(\mathcal{R})|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle|\\
&\leq\Big(\prod_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\neq j,j^{{}^{\prime}}}\sup_{\mathcal{R}_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\in\mathscr{T}_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}}\frac{|\langle f_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}}\rangle|}{|I(\mathcal{R})|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})}|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle||\langle f_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}}\rangle|\Big)\\
&\leq\Big(\prod_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\neq j,j^{{}^{\prime}}}\mathbf{M}_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}(\mathscr{T}_{j^{{}^{\prime\prime}}})\Big)|I_{\mathscr{T}}|\Big(\frac{1}{|I_{\mathscr{T}}|}\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})}|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|I_{\mathscr{T}}|}\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})}|\langle f_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j^{{}^{\prime}}}}\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq|I_{\mathscr{T}}|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{M}_{j}(\mathscr{T}_{j}).\end{split}

Finally consider
\mathscr{T}(\Xi)
. Let
j=1,j^{{}^{\prime}}=2
and take
\tau_{1}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{1}\backslash 10\Xi_{1},\tau_{2}\in C_{2}\circ\Xi_{2}\backslash 10\Xi_{2}
. One can estimate exactly as above. Then summing over
\mathscr{T}(\Xi)
and all
\mathscr{T}(j,j^{{}^{\prime}})
completes the proof.
∎

Now we estimate
\mathbf{M}_{j}
. The following theorem is essentially the Littlewood-Paley theory. It’s classical, but we include the proof.

Theorem 10
.

Let
C>1
be a constant. Let
\mathscr{I}
be a finite collection of dyadic intervals in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(\xi_{I})_{I\in\mathscr{I}}
be a family of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\xi_{I}\in\big(-Cs(I)^{-1},Cs(I)^{-1}\big)^{d}\backslash\big(-s(I)^{-1},s(I)^{-1}\big)^{d}
. Let
(\varphi_{I})_{I\in\mathscr{I}}
be a family of functions such that each
\varphi_{I}
can be written into the following form:

\varphi_{I}(x)=|I|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\pi i\xi_{I}\big(x-\mathfrak{c}(I)\big)}\zeta_{I}\big(\frac{x-\mathfrak{c}(I)}{s(I)}\big),

where
\zeta_{I}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

1.

The Fourier transform
\widehat{\zeta_{I}}
is supported in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
.

2.

For any
\alpha:|\alpha|\leq 1
, we have

|\partial^{\alpha}\zeta_{I}(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-10d}.

The implicit constant does not depend on
I
.

Then the square function

\mathfrak{s}f(x):=\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{|\langle f|\varphi_{I}\rangle|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is
L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}
bounded and
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
bounded.

Proof.

We first prove the
L^{2}
boundedness. By dividing
\mathscr{I}
into
O(1)
groups, we can assume
\langle\varphi_{I}|\varphi_{I^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle=0
if
s(I)\neq s(I^{{}^{\prime}})
. Consider
A:f\mapsto(\langle f|\varphi_{I}\rangle)_{I\in\mathscr{I}}
. It suffices to show
\|A^{*}Af\|_{2}\lesssim\|Af\|_{2}
, which means

\sum_{I,I^{{}^{\prime}}}\langle f|\varphi_{I}\rangle\overline{\langle f|\varphi_{I^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle}\langle\varphi_{I}|\varphi_{I^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle\lesssim\sum_{I}|\langle f|\varphi_{I}\rangle|^{2}

By Schur’s test and orthogonality, it suffices to show

\sum_{I^{{}^{\prime}}:s(I^{{}^{\prime}})=s(I)}|\langle\varphi_{I}|\varphi_{I^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle|\lesssim 1

for any
I
. But this is an easy consequence of the estimate:

|\langle\varphi_{I}|\varphi_{I^{{}^{\prime}}}\rangle|\lesssim\big(1+\frac{d(I,I^{{}^{\prime}})}{s(I)}\big)^{-5d}.

(4.2)

Now we use the C-Z decomposition
[
24
]
to prove the
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
boundedness. Given
\lambda>0
, write
f=g+b:=g+\sum_{Q}b_{Q}
. We have

|\{\mathfrak{s}f>\lambda\}|\leq|\{\mathfrak{s}g>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}|+|\{\mathfrak{s}b>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}|.

For the first term on the right, we estimate

|\{\mathfrak{s}g>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}|\lesssim\frac{\|\mathfrak{s}g\|_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\|g\|_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

For the second term, it suffices to show

|\{x\in\Big(\bigcup_{Q}C_{d}Q\Big)^{c}:\mathfrak{s}b>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}

(4.3)

since

\sum_{Q}|Q|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

The estimate (
4.3
) is implied by

\int_{(C_{d}Q)^{c}}|\mathfrak{s}b_{Q}|\lesssim\|b_{Q}\|_{1},\ \forall Q.

To prove this, write

\int_{(C_{d}Q)^{c}}|\mathfrak{s}b_{Q}|=\int_{(C_{d}Q)^{c}}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{|\langle b_{Q}|\varphi_{I}\rangle|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int_{(C_{d}Q)^{c}}\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{|\langle b_{Q}|\varphi_{I}\rangle|}{|I|^{\frac{1}{2}}}1_{I}(x).

(4.4)

We can restrict
I\cap(C_{d}Q)^{c}\neq\emptyset
. First consider the sum over
I:s(I)\leq s(Q)
in RHS of (
4.4
). The cancellation property doesn’t play a role in this case, so we estimate

|\langle b_{Q}\big||I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{I}\rangle|\lesssim\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{s(I)}\big)^{-10d}\|b_{Q}\|_{1}.

Plug this into RHS of (
4.4
). We then obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I:I\cap(C_{d}Q)^{c}\neq\emptyset,\\
s(I)\leq s(Q)\end{subarray}}\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{s(I)}\big)^{-10d}\lesssim\sum_{2^{k}\leq s(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I:I\cap(C_{d}Q)^{c}\neq\emptyset,\\
s(I)=2^{k}\end{subarray}}\big(\frac{s(Q)}{2^{k}}\big)^{-5d}\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{2^{k}}\big)^{-5d}\lesssim 1.

Then consider the sum over
I:s(I)>s(Q)
in RHS of (
4.4
). Using
\int b_{Q}=0
, we estimate

|\langle b_{Q}\big||I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{I}\rangle|=|\langle b_{Q}\big||I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{I}(\cdot)-|I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{I}\big(\mathfrak{c}(Q)\big)\rangle|\lesssim\frac{s(Q)}{s(I)}\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{s(I)}\big)^{-10d}\|b_{Q}\|_{1}.

Plug this into RHS of (
4.4
). We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I:I\cap(C_{d}Q)^{c}\neq\emptyset,\\
s(I)>s(Q)\end{subarray}}\frac{s(Q)}{s(I)}\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{s(I)}\big)^{-10d}=\sum_{2^{k}>s(Q)}\sum_{I:s(I)=2^{k}}\frac{s(Q)}{2^{k}}\big(1+\frac{d(I,Q)}{2^{k}}\big)^{-10d}\lesssim 1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 11
.

Let
j\in[n]
. Let
\mathscr{T}_{j}
be a lacunary
j\text{-tree}
. Then

\frac{1}{|I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}}|}\big\|\big(\sum_{\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}}\frac{|\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}\rangle|^{2}}{I(\mathcal{R}_{j})}1_{I(\mathcal{R}_{j})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{1,\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{|I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{j}|\big(1+\frac{d(x,I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}{s(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}\big)^{-2N_{\epsilon}}\lesssim\inf_{x\in I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}}}Mf_{j}(x).

Proof.

Pick
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\xi_{j}\subset C_{2}\circ\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j})\backslash 10\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{j}),\forall\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}
. Our
C_{3}\text{-sparse}
property implies that the map
\mathcal{R}_{j}\mapsto I(\mathcal{R}_{j})
is a bijection in
\mathscr{T}_{j}
. For
I\in\{I(\mathcal{R_{j}}):\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}\}
, denote the unique
\mathcal{R}_{j}\in\mathscr{T}_{j}
such that
I(\mathcal{R}_{j})=I
by
\mathcal{R}_{I}
. Let

\varphi_{I}(x):=\big(1+\big|\frac{x-\mathfrak{c}(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}{s(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}\big|^{2}\big)^{N_{\epsilon}}e^{-2\pi i\xi_{j}(x-\mathfrak{c}(I))}\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}(x).

Since
\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}
is a wave packet adapted to
\mathcal{R}_{I}
, we can write
\varphi_{I}
into the form required by Theorem
10
. In fact, assume

\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}(x)=|I|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\pi i\mathfrak{c}\big(\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{I})\big)\big(x-\mathfrak{c}(I)\big)}\zeta_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}\big(\frac{x-\mathfrak{c}(I)}{s(I)}\big).

We can take

\zeta_{I}(x):=\big(1+\big|\frac{s(I)}{s(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}x+\frac{\mathfrak{c}(I)-\mathfrak{c}(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}{s(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}\big|^{2}\big)^{N_{\epsilon}}\zeta_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}(x)

with
\xi_{I}:=\mathfrak{c}\big(\Xi(\mathcal{R}_{I})\big)-\xi_{j}
. It easy to check
\zeta_{I}
satisfies all the requirements in Theorem
10
since
1\ll N_{\epsilon}\ll N_{1}
and
\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\zeta_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}}
is still supported in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
. Now let

f(x):=\big(1+\big|\frac{x-\mathfrak{c}(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}{s(I_{\mathscr{T}_{j}})}\big|^{2}\big)^{-N_{\epsilon}}e^{-2\pi i\xi_{j}(x-\mathfrak{c}(I))}f_{j}(x).

We have
\langle f|\varphi_{I}\rangle=\langle f_{j}|\varphi_{\mathcal{R}_{I}}\rangle
. Applying the
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
bound in Theorem
10
gives the first estimate. The second estimate is standard.
∎

There is still a difference between
\mathbf{M}_{j}
and LHS of Corollary
11
: One need to replace the
L^{1,\infty}
norm in Corollary
11
with the
L^{2}
norm in
\mathbf{M}_{j}
. This is achieved by the now-standard dyadic John-Nirenberg inequality
[
23
]
.

Definition 13
.

Let
\mathscr{I}
be a finite collection of dyadic intervals in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(a_{I})_{I\in\mathscr{I}}
be a family of complex numbers. Define

\displaystyle\|(a_{I})_{I\in\mathscr{I}}\|_{p}

\displaystyle:=\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{p},\ 0<p<\infty,

\displaystyle:=\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{2}.

Lemma 12

(Dyadic John-Nirenberg Inequality)
.

Let
\mathscr{I}
be a finite collection of dyadic intervals in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(a_{I})_{I\in\mathscr{I}}
be a family of complex numbers. Then for any

, we have

\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{p}\sim\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{q,\infty}.

Remark
.

Lemma
12
immediately gives

\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{p}\sim\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{q}

for any
0<p,q<\infty
. Hence the

norm used in the definition of

is not important.

Proof.

We only need to prove

\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{p}\lesssim\sup_{I_{0}\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{1}{|I_{0}|^{\frac{1}{q}}}\big\|\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{0}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{q,\infty}

for
0<q<p<\infty
. Assume
\text{RHS}=1
. Denote the supremum in LHS by
C(p)
, and assume it is obtained at
I_{*}
. We now estimate

\int_{I_{*}}\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(4.5)

Pick a large constant

. Define

\Omega:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}>C\}.

Then the previous assumption implies

(4.6)

For

, we estimate

(4.7)

For
x\in\Omega
, consider

\mathscr{J}:=\{J\in\mathscr{I}:\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}>C,\ \forall x\in J\}.

Since the square function is constant on those smallest intervals in
\mathscr{I}
, we have
\Omega=\bigcup_{J\in\mathscr{J}}J
. Denote the collection of maximal elements in

by

(4.8)

satisfying

Thus

\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:J_{max}\subsetneqq I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:J_{max}\subsetneqq I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(y)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C,\ \forall x\in J_{max},

and we get

\int_{J_{max}}\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:J_{max}\subsetneqq I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}(x)\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C^{p}|J_{max}|.

(4.10)

We can estimate the first term on the right of (

) as follows:

\int_{J_{max}}\big(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{I}:I\subset I_{*}}\frac{|a_{I}|^{2}}{|I|}1_{I}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C(p)^{p}|J_{max}|.

(4.11)

Combining (

)(
4.10
)(
4.11
), and (

Lemma 23

\#\Omega

|t|

S_{l}({y})=S_{l}(y)\leq S_{l}(a),\ \forall 1\leq l\leq L^{{}^{\prime}}-1.

For

, note that
S_{l}(y)<S_{l}(a)
must hold. Otherwise we have
S_{l_{1}}(y)=S_{l_{1}}(a)
for some

, which implies

. On the other hand,
S_{L}(y)<S_{L}(a)
implies there exists some

such that

. Thus

a_{l_{1}}\leq y_{l_{1}}=y_{l_{2}}<a_{l_{2}},

a contradiction. Now since
S_{l}(y)<S_{l}(a)
for any
L^{{}^{\prime}}\leq l\leq L
, we can guarantee

S_{l}({y})\leq S_{l}(a),\ \forall L^{{}^{\prime}}\leq l\leq L.

by taking
|t|
very small. For
L+1\leq l\leq n
, we use
y_{L+1}>a_{L+1}\geq a_{L+2}=y_{L+2}
. Hence for sufficiently small

, we have

S_{l}({y})=S_{L+1}(y)+(y_{L+2}+\cdots+y_{l})\leq S_{L+1}(a)+(a_{L+2}+\cdots+a_{l})=S_{l}(a).

We have proved
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Z}
in the

, we still have
y_{1}\geq\cdots\geq y_{L}+t>y_{L+1}-t\geq\cdots\geq y_{n}
provided
|t|
is sufficiently small. And the conclusion follows. If If
y_{L}=y_{L+1}
, change the position of
y_{L}
and
y_{L+1}
. Then we are back to the
t>0
case. This finishes the proof of Lemma
32
.
∎

Recall the following classical lemma for weak

Lemma 34
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma\text{-finite}
space. Let
0<q<\infty
. Then for any function
f
we have

\|f\|_{q,\infty}\sim\sup_{0<\mu(E)<\infty}\inf_{E^{{}^{\prime}}\subset E:\mu(E^{{}^{\prime}})\sim\mu(E)}|\int_{E^{{}^{\prime}}}f|\mu(E)^{-\frac{1}{q^{{}^{\prime}}}}.

Proof.

For the
\lesssim
direction, assume
RHS=1
. Let
\lambda>0
. Take
E
to be each of the following sets (intersecting with a finite-measure set if necessary):

\{x:\Re f>\frac{1}{2}\lambda\},\ \{x:\Re f<-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\},\ \{x:\Im f>\frac{1}{2}\lambda\},\ \{x:\Im f<-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\}.

We get

\lambda\mu(\{x:|f|>\lambda\})^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 1

as desired. For the
\gtrsim
direction, assume
LHS=1
. Given
E
, define

\Omega:=\{x:|f|>C\mu(E)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\}

with a large constant
C
and take

. We see
\mu(E^{{}^{\prime}})\sim\mu(E)
and

|\int_{E^{{}^{\prime}}}f|\lesssim\mu(E)^{\frac{1}{q^{{}^{\prime}}}},

which finishes the proof.

Final Remarks

1.

In our discretization process Section
3
,
\mathfrak{m}
behaves like a bump function on each Whitney cube. This means
\mathfrak{m}
has been tensorized on each cube and the following Fourier expansion procedure may not be necessary. We include this procedure because it is free and makes the operator have a concise form.

2.

Our original discrete sum (
3.22
) is a linear combination of (
3.23
), so it’s necessary to make the exceptional set (
6.5
) only depend on
E_{j}
.

3.

Our way to define Type II non-degenerate condition is not unique. It’s possible to find similar conditions of the same spirit.

4.

Further assume
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1},q)\in(1,\infty)^{n}
in each case of Theorem
2
. Then we can simplify the proof using (
4.18
): Write
p_{n}:=q^{{}^{\prime}}
as usual. We choose
\theta_{j}:=\frac{1}{p_{j}},j\in[n]
in (
6.3
) and apply (
4.18
) if necessary. We can suitably select
\alpha_{j}
to guarantee the exponents of
\lambda_{j}
is positive. Then simply using
\mathbf{M}_{j}(\mathscr{R}_{j})\lesssim 1
gives

\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\mathscr{T}\in\mathbf{F}^{\geq 2}(\lambda)}\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathscr{T}}\cdots\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{n}|E_{j}|^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}}.

Similarly, we get the estimate:

\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\kappa}\sum_{\mathcal{R}\in\mathbf{F}^{=1}(\lambda,\kappa)}\cdots\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{n}|E_{j}|^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}}.

This finishes the proof without removing the exceptional set (
6.5
).

5.

If Type I or Type II non-degenerate condition fails mildly, then we can argue like Section
7
to show the boundedness of
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
in a certain range. However, we chose to only discuss one example since it is tedious to completely describe which degenerate cases can be handled.

6.

For Example
5
, we can also anticipate its difficulty from a projection perspective. The following is morally a generalization of Theorem 1.17 and Theorem 1.18 in
[
1
]
. Let
d,\Delta
be positive integers and
\mathfrak{L}_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},j\in[n-1]
be linear operators. Let
f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\Delta})
such that
\rho
is compactly supported. Define

T_{(\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\Delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}f_{j}(x+\mathfrak{L}_{j}t)\rho(t)dt.

Lemma 36
.

Let
d^{{}^{\prime}}<d
be positive integers and
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}
be a linear surjection. Fix
0<p_{j},q<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{q}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{p_{j}}
. Suppose

\|T_{(\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(F_{1},\cdots,F_{n-1})\|_{q}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\|F_{j}\|_{p_{j}},\ \forall F_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(8.1)

Then we have

\|T_{(A\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})\|_{q}\lesssim_{d,d^{{}^{\prime}},n,(\mathfrak{L})_{j},A}C\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\|f_{j}\|_{p_{j}},\ \forall f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}})\cap L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}).

(8.2)

Proof.

Denote the ball
\{x:|x|<R\}
by
B_{R}
. Assume
\rho
is supported in
B_{R_{0}}
for some
R_{0}
. Let
R\gg R_{0}
. For given
f_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}})\cap L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}})
, take

F_{j,R}(x):=f_{j}(Ax)1_{B_{R}}(x).

We have
|x+\mathfrak{L}_{j}t|\leq R
for
|x|\leq cR,|t|\leq R_{0}
, which implies

T_{(\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(F_{1,R},\cdots,F_{n-1,R})(x)=T_{(A\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(Ax),\ \forall|x|\lesssim R.

Write
x=y+z
with
y\in\ker(A)^{\perp},z\in\ker(A)
. Then we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{(\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(F_{1,R},\cdots,&F_{n-1,R})(x)|^{q}dx\geq\int_{B_{cR}}|T_{(A\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(Ax)|^{q}dx\\
&\geq\int_{\ker(A)\cap B_{\frac{c}{2}R}}\int_{\ker(A)^{\perp}\cap B_{\frac{c}{2}R}}|T_{(A\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(Ay)|^{q}dydz\\
&\gtrsim R^{d-d^{{}^{\prime}}}\int_{B_{c^{{}^{\prime}}R}}T_{(A\mathfrak{L}_{j})_{j},\rho}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1})(x^{{}^{\prime}})|^{q}dx^{{}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

(8.3)

Similarly, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{j,R}|^{p_{j}}dx\lesssim R^{d-d^{{}^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}}|f_{j}|^{p_{j}}dx^{{}^{\prime}}.

(8.4)

Apply (
8.1
) with the functions
F_{j,R}
, plug (
8.3
) and (
8.4
) into the inequality. We see the powers of
R
on both sides cancel out. Letting
R\to\infty
gives (
8.2
).
∎

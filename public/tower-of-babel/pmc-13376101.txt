Identification of replication factor C subunit 4 as a potential therapeutic target in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma based on bioinformatic analysis and machine learning
Authors: Zhenzhen Yang, Cheng Chang, Na Gao, Pan Gao, Ruiting Feng, Jingjie Meng, Xianhui Yang, Yinsen Song, Tianli Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is one of the highly lethal and aggressive malignant tumors worldwide. To effectively prevent and treat this disease, the search for novel molecular targets is of great significance for promoting the molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy of ESCC.MethodGene expression profiles from gene expression omnibus (GEO) datasets were normalized and analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes. Functional enrichment, protein-protein interaction network, and machine learning algorithms were applied for biomarker screening. Immune infiltration analysis and immunohistochemistry were performed to assess clinical relevance.ResultsAnalysis identified 752 differentially expressed genes in ESCC, with enrichment in upregulated pathways including DNA replication and mismatch repair, and downregulated pathways such as autophagy. RFC4 expression correlated with mast cell infiltration patterns, showed elevated expression in ESCC tissues via immunohistochemistry, and was associated with poor prognosis.ConclusionThis study identifies AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 as potential in silico biomarkers for ESCC. This study further highlights RFC4 as a promising candidate for diagnostic and prognostic applications, offering new insights into prevention strategies for ESCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05254-z.Keywords: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Bioinformatics, Machine learning, Data mining, Therapeutic targets

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) ranks as the 11th most prevalent malignancy globally and accounts for the 7th highest mortality rate among all cancers [1]. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) represents the predominant histological subtype of esophageal cancer, accounting for approximately 80% of all cases worldwide [2, 3]. Despite advances in systemic therapies, radiotherapy, and surgical techniques, the management of ESCC continues to pose significant clinical challenges, with an overall five-year survival rate as low as 5% [4]. In clinical practice, ESCC patients are frequently diagnosed at advanced stages, leading to limited therapeutic options. Currently, surgical resection remains the cornerstone of treatment, often combined with chemoradiotherapy and immunotherapy as part of multimodal regimens [5]. Given this context, the prevention of ESCC emerges as a critical priority. The identification of novel molecular targets could provide a foundation for molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy of ESCC.

Bioinformatics and machine learning have become indispensable for analyzing large-scale biological datasets. These technologies facilitate the identification of disease-related targets and biomarkers across multiple omics levels including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, and support the integration of heterogeneous data to build predictive and diagnostic models for advancing personalized medicine [6, 7]. ESCC presents a particularly poor clinical prognosis, largely due to challenges such as drug resistance and metastasis [5, 8]. A deeper understanding of its molecular mechanisms is therefore urgently needed to identify novel therapeutic targets and improve molecular-level diagnosis and treatment. The pathogenesis of ESCC involves complex regulatory networks with dysregulation of numerous genes. Advances in high-throughput gene expression profiling continue to reveal an expanding set of potential biomarkers, offering new avenues for research and intervention.

The widespread adoption of whole-genome expression microarrays has transformed the diagnostic and therapeutic landscape of ESCC by enabling comprehensive transcriptional profiling. Public genomic repositories, such as the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), provide vital data for deciphering the disease’s molecular architecture. Bioinformatics integrates computational biology methods to systematically analyze these large-scale datasets, employing techniques like genomic sequencing and expression profiling. Furthermore, machine learning (ML), grounded in statistical and algorithmic principles, enhances knowledge extraction from complex biomedical data, supporting applications from early detection to prognostic assessment. This study utilizes integrated bioinformatics and ML approaches to analyze genomic data, advancing the understanding of ESCC’s genetic foundations [9, 10].

Although the combination of bioinformatics and machine learning has become relatively common in cancer biomarker screening, the specific role of replication factor C subunit 4 (RFC4) in ESCC remains largely unexplored. RFC4 is known to participate in DNA replication and cell cycle regulation, and its dysregulation has been documented in several other cancer types such as lung adenocarcinoma and colorectal cancer. However, no study to date has systematically evaluated the expression, clinical significance, or biological function of RFC4 in ESCC using integrated multi-dataset bioinformatics analysis coupled with machine learning algorithms. Whether RFC4 serves as a genuine therapeutic target or prognostic biomarker in ESCC therefore represents a critical knowledge gap that warrants dedicated investigation.

Employing a comprehensive bioinformatics approach, this study systematically delineated the molecular landscape of ESCC. Differential expression analysis comparing tumor and adjacent normal tissues using extensive GEO sequencing data identified genes with aberrant expression patterns associated with disease progression. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction revealed key interactive nodes and functional modules among these dysregulated genes, highlighting signaling pathways implicated in ESCC pathogenesis. Functional annotation and enrichment analyses, supported by KEGG and GSEA, further elucidated the underlying biological processes. To prioritize robust candidate genes, machine learning algorithms—including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), and random forest (RF)—were applied. Collectively, this integrated strategy provides a molecular framework for understanding ESCC and informs potential diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

This study aims to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying ESCC prevention and to identify candidate genes for early detection and targeted therapy. By systematically analyzing large-scale genomic datasets, we characterize key genes and pathways involved in inhibiting ESCC initiation and progression. Additionally, identifying genes with distinct expression patterns at early stages enables discovery of potential biomarkers for early diagnosis and therapeutic targets for personalized treatment, ultimately aiming to improve clinical outcomes in ESCC management.

Methods

Gene expression data acquisition and processing

Gene expression profiles of ESCC and adjacent normal tissues were obtained from three publicly available NCBI GEO datasets: GSE38129 (n = 60; 30 ESCC samples and 30 normal controls), GSE161533 (n = 56; 28 ESCC samples and 28 normal controls), and GSE20347 (n = 34; 17 ESCC samples and 17 normal controls). Raw data were normalized using the “NormalizeBetweenArrays” method in R/Bioconductor, followed by differential expression analysis with the “limma” package. To minimize technical heterogeneity across datasets, the three GEO datasets were merged, and batch effects were corrected using the removeBatchEffect function from the limma package. This function employs a linear model to eliminate systematic biases arising from differences in experimental platforms, assay timing, and other technical factors, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of subsequent analyses. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using thresholds of |log2 fold change| > 1 and an adjusted P value < 0.05. These DEGs were then subjected to functional annotation and pathway enrichment analyses.

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analysis

To functionally annotate the identified genes, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using the integrated online bioinformatics platform. GO analysis classified the genes according to biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components to infer their potential involvement in ESCC pathogenesis. KEGG pathway analysis was conducted in parallel to determine significantly enriched signaling pathways and elucidate dysregulated molecular networks in ESCC. Both analyses utilized the platform’s established computational pipelines for systematic functional annotation and pathway enrichment evaluation.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

To systematically identify pivotal molecular players in ESCC pathogenesis, a PPI network was constructed. High-quality interactions were ensured by setting a confidence score threshold of ≥ 0.4 using the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/). The resulting network was then imported into Cytoscape for visualization and further analysis. Key hub genes within the network were identified using the cytoHubba plugin, which evaluates node centrality measures to determine genes with critical regulatory roles.

Feature selection of characteristic biomarkers

To identify potential diagnostic biomarkers for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), three machine learning algorithms were applied: least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), and random forest (RF). First, LASSO regression was performed using the glmnet package in R with tenfold cross-validation, where an optimized penalty parameter was applied to improve feature selection stability, yielding 6 significant genes. Next, SVM-RFE was employed due to its effectiveness in removing irrelevant features while preserving biologically meaningful variables compared to conventional selection approaches. Through tenfold cross-validation, this method identified the 25 most discriminative genes. Additionally, the RF algorithm was utilized to rank features based on importance, thereby reducing overfitting by aggregating predictions from multiple decision trees trained on bootstrap samples; the top 14 genes were selected from this model. Candidate biomarkers from all three methods were then intersected to obtain a consensus set. Diagnostic performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) as a robust metric of classification accuracy.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

To assess the biological functions of the DEGs, GSEA was performed. Normalized enrichment scores for each gene set were calculated based on 1000 permutations. Enrichment significance in KEGG pathways was evaluated using a false discovery rate (FDR) cutoff of < 0.05.

Analysis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells

An optimized R-based analytical pipeline was utilized to evaluate associations between target expression levels and tumor purity, as well as the infiltration of various immune cell types such as B cells, CD8+T cells, CD4+T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells. Subsequently, survival analyses concerning target expression and tumor immune infiltration were performed using the CIBERSORT algorithm.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tissue microarray sections (project number: HEsoS180Su12), comprising 106 ESCC samples and 74 paired normal tissues, were obtained from Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) with ethical approval (reference number: SHYJS-CP-240302) granted by the provider in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. IHC was performed to evaluate replication factor C subunit 4 (RFC4) expression. Tissue sections were incubated with anti-RFC4 antibodies diluted at 1:2000. The immunostaining was assessed independently by two pathologists who were blinded to the clinical information. Staining intensity was scored using the ImageJ (NIH) IHC scoring system on a 3-point scale: 0 (negative to weak), 1 (moderate), and 2 (strong). For subsequent survival analysis, samples exhibiting strong staining were classified as having high RFC4 expression, while those showing negative, weak, or moderate staining were categorized as low expression.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) and SPSS software (version 27.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous data are presented as means ± SD. Group differences were evaluated by Student’s t‑test. Categorical variables were compared with the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test to examine the association between RFC4 expression and clinicopathological characteristics. Survival probabilities were estimated with the Kaplan‑Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed using the log‑rank test. A p‑value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Differential expression analysis

To delineate the gene expression profile associated with ESCC, we compared tumor tissues with matched adjacent normal tissues. Differential expression analysis using the limma package identified 752 significantly dysregulated genes (|log2FC|>1, adjusted P < 0.05). Hierarchical clustering of these genes revealed a clear separation between tumor and normal samples, as visualized in the heatmap (Fig. 1A), indicating coordinated expression patterns. Further analysis showed that these differentially expressed genes comprised 361 upregulated and 391 downregulated genes (Fig. 1B). These results define key gene-level alterations implicated in ESCC pathogenesis.

Functional enrichment analyses in the GSE38129, GSE161533, and GSE20347 dataset

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed on the ESCC datasets GSE38129, GSE161533, and GSE20347 to identify significantly enriched biological pathways. The top 10 enriched pathways are summarized in Fig. 2. KEGG pathway analysis further highlighted the most prominently affected pathways (Fig. 2A), while a detailed gene-pathway network illustrated critical molecular interactions associated with ESCC pathogenesis (Fig. 2B). Among the upregulated pathways, base excision repair, DNA replication, glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis-keratan sulfate, glycosphingolipid biosynthesis-ganglio series, and mismatch repair were notably elevated (Fig. 2C), indicating potential targets for therapeutic intervention. In contrast, several pathways were significantly downregulated, including autophagy, cAMP signaling, endocytosis, insulin signaling, and oxytocin signaling (Fig. 2D), suggesting dysregulation of key cellular processes during ESCC progression.

DEG-based functional enrichment analysis

To gain insight into the molecular mechanisms driving ESCC pathogenesis, functional enrichment analyses of the identified DEGs were conducted via GO and KEGG approaches. Significant GO enrichments were observed across three main domains (Fig. 3A–C and Fig. S1-S3): biological processes related to mitotic cell cycle phase transition, chromosome segregation, and nuclear division; cellular components including the collagen-containing extracellular matrix, spindle, and apical part of the cell; and molecular functions such as extracellular matrix structural constituent, serine-type peptidase activity, and serine hydrolase activity. KEGG pathway analysis further revealed several dysregulated pathways in ESCC (Fig. 3D, E and Fig. S4), with notable involvement in cornified envelope formation, cytoskeletal organization in muscle cells, and cell cycle regulation. Taken together, these results delineate key molecular networks associated with ESCC progression and suggest promising targets for future therapeutic exploration.

PPI network analysis, hub gene identification, and machine learning model

To construct the PPI network, we employed the STRING database, which generated a network consisting of 229 nodes and 458 edges. The resulting interaction data (Fig. S5) were subsequently imported into Cytoscape for enhanced visualization and topological analysis. Using the cytoHubba plugin within Cytoscape, we identified 50 hub genes potentially associated with ESCC, as illustrated in Fig. 4A. Machine learning algorithms were employed to identify potential prognostic PPI-DEGs in ESCC. RF analysis selected 14 signature genes—CKS1B, UBE2C, EXO1, RFC4, MCM2, KIF2C, KIF4A, AURKA, KIF14, PCNA, TRIP13, CEP55, CDK1, and MAD2L1—each with a relative relevance score above 0.9 (Fig. 4B). Lasso regression further identified 6 genes significantly linked to ESCC progression: AURKA, KIF4A, MCM6, WDHD1, ORC6, and RFC4 (Fig. 4C). Additionally, SVM analysis with stringent feature selection yielded 25 high-confidence genes strongly associated with ESCC pathogenesis (Fig. 4D).

Identification of characteristic genes

Through an integrative analysis employing three independent machine learning algorithms, AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 were identified as overlapping gene candidates for ESCC (Fig. 5A, B). Compared to normal controls, the expression levels of AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 were significantly elevated in tumor tissues (Fig. 5C). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis further demonstrated their high diagnostic potential, with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.976 for AURKA, 0.983 for KIF4A, and 0.980 for RFC4 (Fig. 5D). Taken together, these results indicate that AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 represent promising diagnostic biomarkers for ESCC.

Functional enrichment analyses for AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4

To elucidate the functional roles and molecular pathways of AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 in ESCC, we performed GSEA. As illustrated in Fig. 6, the results demonstrated significant involvement of these genes in multiple biological processes. GSEA revealed that RFC4 was predominantly associated with upregulated pathways including bladder cancer, cell cycle, DNA replication, ECM receptor interaction, and pathways in cancer. Downregulated pathways linked to RFC4 included drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, fatty acid metabolism, histidine metabolism, tyrosine metabolism, and vascular smooth muscle contraction (Fig. 6A). Similarly, AURKA-related upregulation was observed in pathways such as cell cycle, DNA replication, ECM receptor interaction, pathways in cancer, and spliceosome. Corresponding downregulated pathways comprised drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, fatty acid metabolism, histidine metabolism, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, and vascular smooth muscle contraction (Fig. 6B). For KIF4A, enriched upregulated pathways included cell cycle, DNA replication, ECM receptor interaction, mismatch repair, and pathways in cancer, while downregulated pathways involved drug metabolism-cytochrome P450, fatty acid metabolism, histidine metabolism, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, and tyrosine metabolism (Fig. 6C). Collectively, these findings highlight the multifaceted regulatory roles of AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 in ESCC pathogenesis and suggest their potential contribution to tumor development and progression through distinct yet overlapping molecular pathways.

Association between RFC4 mRNA and tumor immunity

To investigate the potential influence of immune cell infiltration on ESCC prognosis, we analyzed the association between RFC4 mRNA expression levels and tumor immune infiltration using data from the TCGA database (Fig. S6 A and Table S1). Statistically significant correlations were observed between RFC4 expression and specific mast cell subsets. RFC4 expression was positively correlated with the infiltration level of activated mast cells (Fig. S6 B) and negatively correlated with resting mast cells (Fig. S6 C). These findings indicate that RFC4 may play a regulatory role within the tumor immune microenvironment.

RFC4 is highly expressed in ESCC and associated with poor prognosis

We initially assessed RFC4 expression in ESCC tissues using proteomic analysis of 106 ESCC and 74 non‑cancerous samples. RFC4 protein levels were significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 7A). Furthermore, ROC curve analysis based on proteomic data indicated that RFC4 protein possesses high prognostic value for ESCC (Fig. 7B). This upregulation was further validated by immunohistochemical analysis on the HEsoS180Su12 tissue microarray (Fig. 7C). Collectively, these results identify RFC4 as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, and suggest its tumor‑promoting function in ESCC progression.

Discussion

EC is the ninth most frequently diagnosed cancer globally and ranks sixth among causes of cancer-related mortality, accounting for more than 500,000 deaths annually [11, 12]. Histologically, EC is primarily categorized into two types with distinct epidemiological and clinical profiles, including ESCC accounting for approximately 80% of cases and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) comprising the remaining 20% [13]. Although survival rates have improved in recent decades, the overall prognosis remains poor, with five-year survival still below 20% [14]. Early detection and secondary prevention continue to pose challenges, highlighting the importance of primary prevention in reducing the worldwide burden of EC [11]. Consequently, clarifying the molecular mechanisms driving ESCC progression and discovering related biomarkers are essential for improving disease evaluation, treatment response, and prognostic assessment.

This study employed integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approaches to identify key genes and pathways involved in ESCC. Gene expression data from three GEO datasets (GSE38129, GSE161533, and GSE20347) were analyzed, leading to the identification of 752 common DEGs, consisting of 361 upregulated and 391 downregulated genes. Functional enrichment analyses were conducted, including GSEA, GO, and KEGG pathway analysis. GSEA indicated significant upregulation of pathways related to base excision repair, DNA replication, glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis, glycosphingolipid biosynthesis, and mismatch repair, which is consistent with recent findings linking DNA repair mechanisms to ESCC progression and therapy resistance [15, 16], In contrast, pathways such as autophagy, cAMP signaling, endocytosis, insulin signaling, and oxytocin signaling were notably downregulated, reflecting impaired cellular regulatory processes in ESCC [17–19]. GO analysis revealed enrichment in biological processes including mitotic cell cycle phase transition, chromosome segregation, and nuclear division; cellular components such as collagen-containing extracellular matrix, spindle, and apical part of cell; and molecular functions including extracellular matrix structural constituent and serine-type peptidase activity. KEGG analysis further highlighted dysregulated pathways associated with cornified envelope formation, muscle cell cytoskeleton, and cell cycle. Collectively, these results delineate critical molecular networks underlying ESCC and point to potential therapeutic targets for further investigation.

To identify key molecular markers for ESCC, a PPI network was constructed to extract hub targets. Subsequently, three machine learning algorithms were applied to screen DEGs between ESCC and normal tissues. The intersection of the results from these models yielded an optimal set of differential genes, which exhibited superior performance compared to other gene combinations. Among these, AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 were consistently identified as potential biomarkers. Expression analysis confirmed that all three genes were significantly upregulated in ESCC relative to normal controls. Prognostic assessment based on TCGA data further indicated that high KIF4A expression was associated with poor clinical outcomes in ESCC patients. Diagnostic performance evaluated by ROC curve analysis demonstrated high accuracy, with AUC values of 0.976 for AURKA, 0.983 for KIF4A, and 0.980 for RFC4. In summary, AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 represent promising diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for ESCC.

The identified genes contribute to ESCC progression through diverse yet complementary biological functions. AURKA, a serine/threonine kinase, regulates mitotic spindle formation and promotes tumor progression across multiple cancers. In ESCC, AURKA knockdown enhances ferroptosis and suppresses tumor growth, highlighting its therapeutic potential [20–24]. Furthermore, AURKA promotes radioresistance by inducing GSK3β-mediated phosphorylation of PTEN at T366 in pancreatic cancer and AURKA-driven destabilization of SAPS3 enables evasion of ferroptosis through the 7-dehydrocholesterol biosynthesis pathway in colorectal cancer. These findings position AURKA as a promising therapeutic target for chemosensitization [25, 26]. KIF4A participates in various cellular processes including tumorigenesis, DNA damage response and repair, and mitosis through its interactions with multiple protein molecules. It is highly expressed in a variety of malignancies, and its downregulation has been shown to inhibit tumor cell invasion, migration, and proliferation, while promoting apoptosis [27–31]. Studies further indicate that KIF4A enhances cellular proliferation and migration via the Hippo signaling pathway and is associated with poor prognosis in ESCC [32, 33]. RFC4, a member of the RFC family, is not only involved in biological processes such as DNA replication and damage repair but also closely associated with the development and progression of various malignancies [34]. Research has shown that RFC4 confers radioresistance in ESCC by regulating the DNA damage response [35].

Our GSEA revealed that AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 play multifaceted roles in ESCC pathogenesis. They consistently upregulated pathways critical for tumorigenesis (e.g., cell cycle, DNA replication) [36–38], while concurrently downregulating various metabolic processes (e.g., drug and fatty acid metabolism) [39–41]. This coordinated dysregulation of proliferative and metabolic pathways highlights their potential as key drivers in ESCC development and progression.

AURKA and KIF4A have been relatively well characterized in ESCC. In contrast, the role of RFC4 in ESCC remains largely unexplored, with little to no prior evidence available. To further explore the clinical relevance of RFC4, we investigated its relationship with tumor immunity in EC using the TCGA database. Tumor immune infiltration represents a key phenotypic hallmark of EC progression. This process is characterized by the dynamic recruitment and spatial organization of diverse immune cell populations within the tumor microenvironment, which collectively influence tumor behavior and patient prognosis [42–47]. RFC4 has been identified as a promising biomarker for tumorigenesis and is capable of effectively predicting immunotherapy response in lung adenocarcinoma. Furthermore, in cervical cancer, elevated RFC4 expression is associated with improved patient prognosis, potentially attributable to its role in modulating the immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment, including enhanced immunostimulatory activity and dendritic cell infiltration [48, 49]. Here, we analyzed correlations between RFC4 expression and various immune cell types, including activated mast cells, activated dendritic cells, gamma delta T cells, resting NK cells, monocytes, and neutrophils. RFC4 expression was positively correlated with infiltration of activated mast cells and negatively correlated with infiltration of resting mast cells, indicating that RFC4 may modulate these two cell types within the immune microenvironment.

Current studies suggest that RFC4 may serve as a potential pan-cancer biomarker relevant to prognosis, tumor immune microenvironment, and drug sensitivity [34]. Moreover, RFC4 contributes to radioresistance in ESCC by regulating DNA damage response. Additionally, copy number variation-driven upregulation of RFC4 and GMPS expression has been linked to early detection and immune evasion in ESCC [35, 50]. Our findings provide evidence supporting an oncogenic role of RFC4 in ESCC. RFC4 was significantly overexpressed in clinical ESCC tissues, and its elevated expression correlated with poor prognosis, as reflected by significantly reduced survival rates. Analysis of clinical characteristics (Table 1) revealed no significant association between RFC4 expression and gender, age, tumor stage, TNM stage, or p53 tumor suppressor gene status. This lack of correlation may stem from the inherent molecular heterogeneity of ESCC and the functional complexity of RFC4. As a critical component of DNA replication, RFC4 expression is likely regulated independently via proliferation-related pathways—such as cell cycle progression and replication stress—rather than being directly linked to conventional clinicopathological parameters or individual tumor suppressor gene alterations.

RFC4 expression in clinical patient with ESCC characteristics

Through multivariate Cox regression analysis, this study revealed that after adjusting for key clinicopathological variables, RFC4 expression was not an independent prognostic factor in patients with ESCC. This finding may be attributable to the pathway through which RFC4 exerts its biological effects. As a core component of the replication factor C complex, elevated RFC4 expression is expected to drive abnormal tumor cell proliferation [51–53]. Clinically, this oncogenic effect is most directly reflected by increased tumor size and advancement in TNM stage. Our data support this inference (Fig. S7), while N and M stage showed potential prognostic value in univariate analysis, both lost significance upon adjustment for TNM stage. Furthermore, the prognostic trend of RFC4 itself (univariate HR = 1.447, p = 0.118) was fully attenuated in the multivariate model that included both tumor size and TNM stage. These results indicate that the prognostic information conveyed by RFC4 is largely explained by these more direct and established clinicopathological parameters. Thus, although RFC4 likely plays an important biological role in ESCC pathogenesis, its value as an independent prognostic biomarker beyond the current TNM staging system appears limited. Future studies may investigate the prognostic relevance of RFC4 within specific TNM subgroups or integrate it with other molecular markers to develop more refined prognostic models.

Study limitations include a relatively homogeneous sample source, insufficient accounting for potential confounders such as prior treatment history, and semi-quantitative assessment of p53 status by immunohistochemistry. Future multi-center studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate these observations. Integration of multi-omics data and functional experiments will help further delineate the regulatory network of RFC4 in ESCC and evaluate its potential as a therapeutic target. In particular, direct in vitro and in vivo functional assays are required to confirm our findings and will be pursued in subsequent studies.

Conclusion

Using integrated bioinformatics, multivariate statistical analysis, and public database mining, this study identifies AURKA, KIF4A, and RFC4 as potential ESCC biomarkers in silicon, providing new insights into prevention strategies and highlighting RFC4 as a promising candidate for diagnostic and prognostic applications.

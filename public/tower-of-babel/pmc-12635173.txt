Integrative machine learning-driven prognosis and immunotherapy stratification via lactylation-associated gene in ovarian cancer
Authors: Xiushen Li, Xuxiang Chen, Sailing Lin, Xiangyu Yang, Wenhao Wu, Xiaoyong Chen, Liqin Bao, Qiongfang Fang, Lijun Fan, Qi Zhang, Jingxin Ma, Guli Zhu, Dequan Yang, Xueqing Wu, Zhaorui Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study utilized a comprehensive approach by integrating multi-omics data to systematically assess lactate modification levels across diverse cell types employing AUCell, JASMINE, and singscore algorithms. An epithelial subpopulation exhibiting the highest lactylation score was successfully pinpointed, and differentially expressed genes linked to lactylation were identified. Through machine learning techniques, a prognostic model was developed based on three genes (TMEM126B, PYGL, and NDUFS6). This model displayed significant associations with immune tumor microenvironment characteristics, microsatellite instability, immune checkpoint expression, and tumor mutation burden. Elevated lactylation risk was linked to the activation of cell cycle and oncogenic pathways, dampened anti-tumor immune responses, and increased expression of immune checkpoints, indicating potential limitations in immunotherapy efficacy. Noteworthy, NDUFS6 exhibited significant upregulation in ovarian cancer (OC) tissues and correlated with an unfavorable prognosis. Functional investigations demonstrated that NDUFS6 knockdown suppressed OC cell proliferation and induced cell cycle arrest. Remarkably, D-lactose emerged as a promising therapeutic agent targeting NDUFS6, underscoring its potential for precise OC treatment.Subject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Immunology, Oncology

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC) is the eighth most common cancer among women globally, but ranks second in gynecological cancer-related mortality, showing an increasing incidence, especially among women under 40 years of age1,2. Epithelial ovarian cancer constitutes 90% of OC cases, with high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC)3 being the most prevalent and aggressive histological subtype. The majority of OC cases are diagnosed at advanced stages due to the lack of early symptoms, leading to poor prognoses. While advancements in immune checkpoint inhibitors have expanded treatment options for OC, challenges such as resistance, high recurrence rates, and limited efficacy persist, impeding significant enhancements in patient survival4–6. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of OC pathogenesis is imperative to optimize therapeutic approaches.

Lactylation, a critical post-translational modification of proteins, involves the addition of lactate molecules to lysine residues and plays a vital role in immune evasion, energy homeostasis, and the metabolic reprogramming of tumor cells7,8. While the role of lactylation in carcinogenesis has been extensively studied, its specific molecular mechanisms in OC remain poorly understood. Recent studies indicate that lactylation can alter the tumor microenvironment (TME) in OC, creating an immunosuppressive milieu that facilitates immune evasion, tumor progression, and therapy resistance. Notably, lactylation induces the upregulation of CCL18 expression, promoting the polarization of macrophages toward the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, thereby enhancing OC cell proliferation, migration, and immune evasion9,10. Furthermore, lactylation of the K392 residue in PFKP increases PTEN expression, enhancing glycolysis in OC cells and accelerating tumor growth11. Conversely, lactylation of histone H3K9 activates the expression of RAD51 and BRCA2, facilitating homologous recombination repair and contributing to platinum resistance in OC patients12. Thus, investigating the interplay between lactylation and the TME in OC may offer novel insights and therapeutic strategies to enhance treatment efficacy.

In this study, we comprehensively analyzed transcriptome data and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from OC to construct a detailed cell atlas of the TME and investigate intercellular communication in OC. Our analysis revealed that epithelial cells exhibited the highest lactylation scores, concomitant with significant downregulation of immune response and metabolic pathways. Additionally, we identified a distinct TFF3+ epithelial cell subpopulation characterized by elevated lactylation scores and identified lactylation-associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Through the application of machine learning techniques, we developed a lactylation-associated prognostic model (comprising TMEM126B, PYGL, and NDUFS6) that demonstrated excellent predictive accuracy in three validation cohorts and the training set. Particularly noteworthy is the association between elevated NDUFS6 expression in OC patients, poorer prognosis, and reduced treatment response. Functional assays, including CCK-8, EdU and cell cycle assays, revealed that NDUFS6 knockdown induced cell cycle arrest and significantly impeded OC cell proliferation. In summary, this study underscores the critical role of lactylation in shaping the immunological landscape of OC. By elucidating its underlying mechanisms and clinical implications, our findings offer valuable insights and potential therapeutic targets for OC immunotherapy. In conclusion, this study systematically explores the pivotal role of lactylation modification in regulating the immune microenvironment of OC, elucidating its mechanisms and clinical significance. Notably, we have identified and validated, for the first time, the functional and prognostic significance of the lactylation-associated gene NDUFS6 in OC progression. These results establish a new theoretical basis for OC immunotherapy strategies and propose a promising target for clinical application, underscoring substantial scientific and practical implications.

Results

The immune landscape of single-cell sequencing in OC

Utilizing scRNA-seq technology to investigate cell types and molecular alterations in OC, we systematically merged two OC datasets (GSE184880 and GSE173682) and utilized the Harmony algorithm to eliminate batch effects. The UMAP plot demonstrated successful integration of all 14 samples (Fig. 1A). Following quality control measures, we identified 43,237 cells, comprising 13,514 control group cells and 26,607 primary ovarian cancer cells. By employing the Clustree algorithm for resolution parameter optimization, we identified the optimal resolution value and subsequently partitioned all cells into 20 distinct cell clusters, designated as Cluster 0 to 19 (Fig. 1B). Based on highly expressed genes within each cluster, we classified them into eight distinct cell populations (Fig. 1C). The specific markers employed for classification included epithelial cells (EPCAM, KRT18, and KRT19), fibroblasts (LUM, DCN, COL1A1, NT3E, and THY1), tumor-associated fibroblasts (TNFAIP6, MMP11, and VCAN), myofibroblasts (ACTA2, MYH11, and TAGLN), endothelial cells (VWF, CDH5, and PECAM1), myeloid cells (CD14, C1QA, and CD68), T cells (CD2, CD3D, and CD3E), B cells (CD79A and MS4A1), and plasma cells (JCHAIN and MZB1) (Fig. 1D, E). To identify key cell types implicated in ovarian cancer initiation and progression, we initially employed the Ro/e algorithm, a widely used method for assessing tissue specificity. Our analysis revealed that myofibroblasts and endothelial cells were predominantly enriched in control tissue, consistent with normal ovarian function. Conversely, in primary ovarian cancer, nearly all cell types exhibited significant enrichment, suggesting that tumorigenesis recruits various cell types to participate in the tumor microenvironment remodeling (Fig. 1F). Subsequent examination of cell-cell interactions indicated that myofibroblasts, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), and T cells were pivotal in cell communication, with COL1A1-44 and COL1A2-CD44 identified as core ligand-receptor pairs in cell signaling (Fig. 1G, H).

Epithelial cells are important players in the lactacidification process

Numerous studies have increasingly demonstrated the pivotal involvement of lactylation in carcinogenesis. In our investigation of ovarian cancer, we utilized three distinct scoring algorithms (AUCell, JASMINE, and singscore) to assess the association between lactylation and various cell types. The outcomes derived from these algorithms consistently revealed markedly elevated lactylation scores in epithelial cells compared to other cell types (Fig. 2A–C). Moreover, our analysis identified a notable upregulation of lactylation-associated genes, such as LDHA and LDHB, specifically in epithelial cells (Fig. 2D). Intriguingly, comprehensive enrichment analyses of epithelial cells unveiled a significant downregulation of immune and metabolic responses within this cellular subset (Fig. 2E–G). These findings suggest a crucial role of epithelial cells in orchestrating lactylation to drive the initiation and progression of ovarian cancer.

A machine learning algorithm was used to construct a prognostic model related to lactic acidification

After evaluating 101 combinations of machine learning algorithms, the CoxBoost-SuperPC integrated model emerged as the most accurate predictor of prognosis (AUC = 0.745). This model identified three key prognostic genes: TMEM126B, PYGL, and NDUFS6 (Fig. 4A). Utilizing this prognostic framework, the lactate-associated prognostic model successfully stratified patients into low- and high-risk categories across the training set and three independent validation cohorts. Patients classified as low-risk exhibited more favorable prognoses compared to those categorized as high-risk, with notable characteristic distinctions observed between the two groups (Fig. 4B–I).

Comparison of high- and low-risk patient cohorts revealed distinct molecular profiles. High-risk patients exhibited upregulation of cell cycle-related and pro-cancer pathways (e.g., hypoxia and KRAS signaling), alongside downregulation of anti-cancer and inflammation-related pathways (Fig. 5A, B). Notably, TP53 and TTN were commonly mutated in both risk groups, as depicted in the waterfall plot. While the mutation rate was slightly lower in the high-risk group (97.2%) compared to the low-risk group (99.12%), unique molecular mutation patterns were discernible between the groups (Fig. 5C, D). Furthermore, analysis of lactylated genes contributing to the prognostic model revealed prevalent copy number gain alterations, implicating a potential association between gene dosage effects and cancer pathogenesis (Fig. 5E).

Immune landscape of patients with different lactic acidification risk groups

Tumor prognosis is significantly influenced by the remodeling of the tumor microenvironment. In our study, we utilized the CIBERSORT algorithm to investigate variations in immune cell infiltration among patients stratified into different risk categories. Our analysis revealed that individuals classified in the low-risk group exhibited elevated levels of follicular helper T cells and eosinophil infiltration compared to those in the high-risk group (Fig. 6A, B), a trend that corresponded with a more favorable prognosis. Notably, a positive correlation was observed between most immune-related markers and high-risk categorization (Fig. 6C), indicating that high-risk patients displayed heightened risk scores for immune-related markers, particularly markers associated with immunosuppression, such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) and glycolysis (Fig. 6D). Furthermore, our heatmap analysis demonstrated a significant association between three lactate-related prognostic core genes and various immune processes (Fig. 6E), providing additional insights into the unfavorable prognosis and heightened immunosuppressive milieu observed in high-risk patients.

Immune therapy response in patients with different lactate dehydrogenase risk groups

Immunotherapy plays a crucial role in enhancing the survival rates of cancer patients. Analysis of the immune system revealed that individuals classified in the high-risk category exhibited elevated TIDE scores, diminished MSI and TME scores, indicating a reduced responsiveness to immunotherapy (Fig. 7A–C). Moreover, a notable reduction in the expression levels of various immune checkpoints was observed in high-risk patients, further supporting their limited efficacy towards immunotherapy (Fig. 7D). Additionally, a decrease in the expression of the HLA family and inflammatory chemokines was noted in individuals at high risk of lactylation, suggesting the presence of a highly immunosuppressive microenvironment among high-risk patients (Fig. 7E, F). Consequently, it is proposed that individuals at high risk of lactylation may not be ideal candidates for immunotherapy.

Expression of NDUFS6 in OC and its correlation with immune checkpoints

The expression patterns of key prognostic genes utilized in the development of a prognostic model exhibit variability in ovarian cancer. Specifically, NDUFS6 demonstrates notable upregulation in ovarian cancer tissues, whereas PYGL is markedly downregulated, and TMEM128 displays no significant variance in ovarian cancer (Fig. 8A). Furthermore, our analysis reveals a correlation between elevated NDUFS6 expression and unfavorable prognosis in ovarian cancer patients, with those exhibiting high NDUFS6 levels experiencing poorer outcomes compared to those with low expression levels (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8B). Subsequently, NDUFS6 expression levels were stratified into high and low groups based on the median value for further analysis. The high-expression cohort of NDUFS6 is notably associated with suppressed T-cell function and enhanced energy metabolism (Fig. 8C, D). Intriguingly, a significant negative correlation is observed between NDUFS6 expression levels and three established immune checkpoints (PDCD1, CD274, and CTLA4) (P < 0.01), indicating that NDUFS6 may represent a risk factor unsuitable for immunotherapeutic interventions (Fig. 8E–G).

The role of NDUFS6 in Pan-cancer

Pan-cancer examination of NDUFS6 expression through box plots and paired box plots revealed a consistent and significant upregulation across various cancer types, indicating a potential role for NDUFS6 as a ubiquitous oncogene (Fig. 9A, B). The analysis demonstrated a positive association between NDUFS6 expression and immune cell populations in the majority of cancers, while showing a negative correlation with various chemokines and their receptors (Fig. 9C–E), suggesting a potential immunosuppressive function of NDUFS6. Furthermore, a negative correlation was observed between NDUFS6 expression and immune microenvironment scores in most cancer types (Fig. 9F), further underscoring its oncogenic potential. These findings suggest that NDUFS6 could serve as a valuable prognostic marker and a promising therapeutic target in ovarian cancer and other malignancies.

Expression of NDUFS6 in tumor tissues and its impact on OC cell function

Based on our findings, we assert that NDUFS6 is a pivotal gene in the lactate-associated prognostic model and demonstrates notable immune-related attributes. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) analyses revealed a significant upregulation of NDUFS6 expression in ovarian cancer tissues compared to the control group (Fig. 10A). Subsequently, siRNA was transfected into ovarian cancer OVCAR3 and OVCAR8 cells, and the transfection efficiency was assessed through qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses, leading to the selection of the most efficient siRNAs, si-NDUFS6#1 and si-NDUFS6#2, for further investigations (Fig. 10B–D). Functional assays utilizing CCK-8 and EDU demonstrated that NDUFS6 silencing impeded the proliferation capacity of ovarian cancer OVCAR3 and OVCAR8 cells (Fig. 10E–H). Moreover, cell cycle analyses indicated that NDUFS6 knockdown induced cell cycle arrest in ovarian cancer OVCAR3 and OVCAR8 cells (Fig. 10I, J). These results collectively indicate a positive regulatory role of NDUFS6 in modulating the biological behavior of ovarian cancer cells.

FDA-based drug virtual screening of NDUFS6

Virtual screening targeted NDUFS6 to identify optimal therapeutic drugs. Initially, 1891 FDA-approved candidate drugs were screened, resulting in the identification of 83 drugs potentially interacting with NDUFS6 (Fig. 11A, B). Subsequently, the top five drugs were prioritized based on molecular docking scores: D-Lactulose, Lactitol, Loversol, Pentamidine, and Ganciclovir (Fig. 11C–G). Notably, D-Lactulose, ranking highest, emerged as the most promising candidate. Functioning as a hydrogen bond acceptor, D-Lactulose can establish one hydrogen bond with Asp88. Additionally, as a hydrogen bond donor, it can engage in four hydrogen bonds with Asp88, Ser78, and Pro76, along with van der Waals interactions with Leu117 and Phe119. These interactions elucidate the binding mechanism, laying a foundation for clinical drug screening.

Discussion

OC stands as a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide and represents the primary fatality among gynecological cancers13. Despite the escalating utilization of immunotherapy in clinical contexts, its effectiveness remains constrained, resulting in suboptimal survival rates for patients14,15. Recent research has underscored lactylation, an emerging post-translational modification, as a pivotal element in the evasion of tumor immunity16. Lactylation has the potential to stimulate the aggregation of immunosuppressive M2 macrophages, hinder T-cell functionality, and reconfigure the TME, thereby fostering immune evasion and resilience17,18. Particularly in OC, lactylation of histone H3K18 within the PD-L1 promoter region triggers the excessive expression of PD-L1, consequently advancing immune evasion19. A comprehensive comprehension of lactylation’s impact on TME restructuring and immune evasion is imperative for augmenting the efficacy of immunotherapy and enhancing patient outcomes.

The rapid progress in bioinformatics has led to the emergence of multi-omics integration as a potent tool for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of cancer, identifying potential biomarkers, and pinpointing therapeutic targets20,21. Compared to single-omics approaches, multi-omics analyses offer enhanced accuracy and reliability in deciphering the intricate biological processes underlying cancer22,23. In this investigation, we developed a comprehensive OC cell atlas by leveraging transcriptomic and scRNA-seq data. This atlas unveils the complex interplay among epithelial cells, lactylation modifications, and immune responses. Particularly noteworthy is the discovery of TFF3+ epithelial cell subpopulations, shedding light on their critical role in OC progression and providing a fresh perspective on lactylation-mediated immune evasion. These insights have the potential to guide the development of more efficacious therapeutic interventions for OC.Trefoil factors (TFF), a group of small secreted proteins comprising TFF1, TFF2, and TFF3, are closely associated with tumor proliferation and migration24,25. Prior investigations have demonstrated that elevated TFF1 expression promotes tumor glycolysis by inhibiting miR-338-3p, a regulator of FGFR1 expression26. TFF2 has been shown to stimulate the recruitment of immature dendritic cells in pancreatic cancer, hindering their maturation and fostering immune tolerance27. Additionally, Shukla et al. revealed in glioblastoma that reduced miR-7-5p levels relieve the suppression of the PI3K/AKT pathway, leading to increased TFF3 expression, thereby facilitating tumor invasion and proliferation, especially in instances where glycolysis is minimally inhibited by 2-DG28.Our investigation indicates that the TFF3+ epithelial cell subset in OC displays distinct metabolic traits, exhibiting the highest lactylation scores among all epithelial subpopulations. This observation aligns with the known involvement of the TFF family in tumor advancement and the regulation of glycolysis. The majority of OC cases originate from fallopian tube epithelial cells with p53 mutations, and the restructuring of the microenvironment plays a critical role in expediting the conversion of epithelial cells into OC. Studies have underscored that alterations in the microenvironment, particularly local cell-cell interactions and the modulation of signaling pathways, can significantly bolster the malignant transformation of epithelial cells29,30. Notably, Garcia et al. have shown that individuals harboring BRCA1/2 mutations exhibit a notably enriched population of high-risk mesenchymal stem cells in the fallopian tube microenvironment31. These cells induce DNA damage in epithelial cells, thereby hastening their malignant transformation and the onset of OC. Consequently, we postulate that the elevated lactylation score of TFF3+ epithelial cells in OC may signify their metabolic adaptability, enhancing glycolysis to generate lactate. This process shapes an immune-suppressive microenvironment that fosters tumor growth and invasion while enhancing their survival capacity under hypoxic conditions through metabolic reprogramming.We have developed an integrated predictive model for OC by integrating TMEM126B, PYGL, and NDUFS6 through the utilization of 101 machine learning algorithms. Assessment on a publicly accessible validation cohort illustrated that the model attained a high level of predictive accuracy, indicating promising potential for clinical implementation. The model has the capacity to effectively stratify OC patients clinically, thereby indicating its usefulness in aiding in diagnosis, prognostic assessment, and the identification of patients who could potentially benefit from immunotherapy. Further analysis utilizing multi-omics approaches unveiled that NDUFS6, among the three identified genes, exhibited the most notable clinical significance. Consequently, we have chosen to focus our subsequent investigations on NDUFS6 to explore its biological functions in the pathogenesis of ovarian cancer. Reactivation of NDUFS6, a crucial gene for oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), has been associated with increased cell proliferation and immune evasion32,33. OXPHOS, powered by the four electron transport chain (ETC) complexes, particularly mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I (CI), is a focal point in mitochondrial-targeted therapies for cancer34,35. CI comprises 14 core subunits and around 30 auxiliary subunits, with NDUFS6 encoding a pivotal subunit known as the mitochondrial matrix arm protein36,37. Research indicates that NDUFS6 collaborates with CD147 to boost CI activity, thereby enhancing OXPHOS38. In the context of multiple myeloma, m6A modification of NDUFS6 activates OXPHOS, contributing to tumorigenesis39. Interestingly, downregulating NDUFS6 elevates acetyl-CoA levels, upregulates the Nlrc5 gene, enhances MHC-I antigen presentation, and heightens sensitivity to immunotherapy40. Our results demonstrate a strong correlation between elevated NDUFS6 expression, heightened energy metabolism, and immune suppression in OC, aligning with prior investigations. Lactate, a significant energy substrate that can enter the tricarboxylic acid cycle to provide carbon sources41,42, also directly enters mitochondria to activate the ETC, thereby augmenting OXPHOS43. In colorectal cancer, lactate fosters metastasis by boosting OXPHOS in cancer stem cells44. In our research, silencing NDUFS6 curbed OC cell proliferation and migration. Consequently, we hypothesize that lactate in OC may induce NDUFS6 expression, enhancing CI function, promoting OXPHOS, fueling tumor growth, and facilitating immune evasion.

While this study offers valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying ovarian cancer development, it is important to acknowledge several limitations. Firstly, the utilization of data from public databases introduces potential variability in sample collection, processing, and standardization, which could impact the generalizability of the findings. The absence of validation from independent clinical cohorts, particularly in the realm of translational medicine, hinders the reliability and applicability of the conclusions drawn. Secondly, the lack of in vivo experiments for the validation of the NDUFS6 gene restricts a comprehensive assessment of its biological function and clinical significance. Additionally, the exclusion of data from multi-ethnic populations may limit the generalizability of the results across diverse demographic groups. Future research endeavors should focus on integrating independent prospective cohorts with robust designs and conducting additional functional experiments to validate the current findings, thereby strengthening the robustness and broad relevance of the conclusions.

Methods

Clinical patient samples

Ovarian cancer samples were obtained from three patients at the Department of Gynecology, Shenzhen Nanshan People’s Hospital between April 2025 and June 2025. All patients had a confirmed diagnosis of ovarian cancer without distant metastasis. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Shenzhen Nanshan People’s Hospital (Approval No. KY-2025-040801). We certify that the study was performed in accorgence1964 declaration of HELSINKI and later amendments.

Sources and gathering of data

This study utilized publicly available scRNA-seq and bulk RNA sequencing data. scRNA-seq c data were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) from datasets GSE173682 (two HGSOC samples) and GSE184880 (five normal ovarian tissue samples and seven HGSOC samples). Transcriptomic data were also retrieved from the TCGA database (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga) from the TCGA-OV cohort, which includes 318 OC samples. Additional data were collected from the GEO datasets GSE26712 (ten normal ovarian tissue samples and 185 OC samples), GSE30161 (58 OC samples), and GSE140082 (380 OC samples). Clinical and survival data were provided for all datasets.

scRNA-seq data processing

The Seurat package (version 4.2.2) was utilized to analyze scRNA-seq data. Initially, data from GSE173682 and GSE184880 underwent preprocessing, including quality control steps such as filtering out cells with nFeature_RNA values below 200 or above 500, as well as cells with over 10% mitochondrial gene expression. Subsequently, data normalization was performed using the SCTransform function. Dimensionality reduction was achieved by retaining the top 15 principal components, followed by clustering analysis and UMAP visualization. The DoubletFinder package was employed to identify and remove potential doublets. Batch effects were mitigated using the Harmony algorithm for data integration. Cell type annotation was conducted using the SingleR software, with validation based on pertinent literature references. DEGs for each cell type were identified using the FindAllMarkers function, and the results were visualized through UMAP clustering in ggplot2.

Cell cluster distribution analysis based on Ro/e ratio

The Ro/e ratio assesses the distribution tendencies of cell clusters within various tissue types. It is determined by contrasting the actual and anticipated cell numbers in a given tissue, with the anticipated numbers calculated using χ2 analysis. A ratio exceeding 1 signifies a notable concentration of a cell cluster in the tissue, implying potential involvement in activities such as tissue restructuring. Conversely, a ratio below 1 indicates a reduction, possibly associated with the inhibition of tissue functions. In contrast to conventional χ2 methodologies, the Ro/e ratio provides a more straightforward evaluation of how cell populations interact with their surroundings45.

Intercellular communication pattern analysis

The CellChat R package (version 1.5) was utilized to explore intercellular communication. Initially, scRNA-seq data and cell type annotations were merged to generate a CellChat object. Subsequently, the aggregateNet function was employed to amalgamate signaling networks across diverse cell types. Visualization of communication patterns was facilitated by the netVisual_circle function, emphasizing prominent cell types through communication frequency and signal intensity. Additionally, the netAnalysis_computeCentrality function was employed to evaluate signaling pathway input-output weights, enabling the identification of critical ligand-receptor pairs.

Acquisition of lactate-metabolizing genes and the lactate-metabolizing score of cell subpopulations

We identified 329 genes associated with lactic acidification through the integration and de-duplication of pathways previously linked to lactic acid in the MSigDB database. To identify cell types involved in lactylation within the TME of OC, lactylation-related gene sets were analyzed using the irGSEA R package. Three algorithms (AUCell, JASMINE, and singscore) were applied to compute lactylation scores for each sample. The irGSEA.integrate function was used to generate a composite score by integrating gene expression patterns from various cell types, offering a comprehensive evaluation of lactylation across cell subpopulations. The distribution of lactylation scores among these subgroups was shown using the irGSEA.halfvlnplot.

Machine learning-based OC prognostic gene screening and model construction

To find important genes linked to OC prognosis and create risk prediction models, ten machine learning techniques were combined, including supervised principal component (SuperPC), generalized boosting regression model (GBM), Cox partial least squares regression (plsRcox), CoxBoost, Ridge, Lasso, Stepwise Cox, random survival forest (RSF), elastic net (Enet), and survival support vector machine (survival-SVM). A total of 101 different algorithm combinations were tested, and model performance was evaluated using average values of the area under the curve (AUC) from both training and validation sets, with the best prognostic model being selected. Enrichment analysis of DEGs

Enrichment analyses were carried out to look into the biological roles of DEGs in various categories. Gene Ontology biological process (GOBP), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway, and REACTOME pathway analyses were carried out by GSEA and clusterProfiler packages, and genes were ranked according to log2 fold change (log2FC) values. The ggplot2 has been employed to show the results.

Immune microenvironment characterization and genetic variation analysis

Immune cell abundance in OC samples was assessed using the CIBERSORT algorithm (LM22 reference gene matrix with 100 permutations). Pearson correlation analysis was performed to examine relationships between immune cell types, with P < 0.05. Immune-related gene set analyses, including survival-related and core gene correlation analyses, were conducted using the IOBR package. To assess immune escape and dysfunction, the TIDE website was utilized, where higher TIDE scores indicated poorer immunotherapy outcomes. SNP data from the TCGA-OV project were downloaded using the TCGAbiolinks package, and mutation waterfall plots were generated using the maftools package.

Pan-cancer analysis of gene expression and immune microenvironment characteristics

To explore gene expression patterns across various cancer types, pan-cancer analysis was conducted using the TCGAplot R package. Box plots of target gene expression across different cancer types were generated using the pan_boxplot function, and DEGs were compared by using the pan_paired_boxplot function. Immune cell abundance and its correlation with target gene expression were analyzed with the gene_immucell_heatmap function. Expression patterns of chemokines and their receptors were explored using the gene_chemokine_heatmap and gene_receptor_heatmap functions, respectively. Lastly, immune microenvironment features across cancer types were assessed using the gene_immunescore_heatmap function.

Virtual screening of drugs

To identify potential inhibitors of NDUSF6, structure-based virtual screening was performed using Schrödinger Maestro version 12.8. The virtual screening model was constructed based on the structure from the Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 5XTB). As there was no apparent small-molecule binding site on the protein, SiteMap was utilized to predict the most probable binding site for the virtual screening. A library of FDA-approved drugs consisting of 1891 compounds was prepared for virtual screening following energy optimization conducted by the LigPrep module of Schrödinger. These optimized compounds underwent molecular docking using the virtual screening workflow. Initially, a high-throughput virtual screening (HTVS) protocol was employed to efficiently filter out compounds with low binding affinities. Subsequently, compounds that passed the HTVS stage underwent standard precision (SP) docking to enhance ranking accuracy through more precise scoring functions and further refine the list of potential hits. Finally, a subset of the top-ranked ligands from the SP docking phase was subjected to extra precision (XP) docking to achieve highly accurate binding poses and docking scores.

Sample collection and IHC

Prior to surgery, none of the patients had undergone radiation or chemotherapy. The control group comprised three cases of adjacent ovarian tissue. IHC analysis was conducted to assess the expression of NDUFS6 (14417--AP, Proteintech, China) in both cancerous and adjacent non-cancerous ovarian tissues. Initially, paraffin-embedded tissue sections underwent immunohistochemical staining. The sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated using a series of alcohol-based steps, followed by antigen retrieval in an acidic salt buffer at pH 6 for 2 min. Subsequently, endogenous peroxidase activity was inhibited with 3% hydrogen peroxide at room temperature for 12 min. The primary antibody was then applied and left to incubate overnight at 4 °C, with a dilution ratio of 1:500 for NDUFS6, followed by incubation with the secondary antibody. The sections were dehydrated, mounted, air-dried, and examined under a microscope for analysis.

Cell culture

OVCAR3 and OVCAR8 (obtained from ATCC) were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Thermo Fisher, USA) with 1% penicillin-streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C and 5% CO₂.

Cell transfection

The siRNA-NDUFS6 (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) product manual was adhered to for the transfection of siRNA using liposomes, in strict accordance with the guidelines outlined in the Lipo8000 reagent manual (Beyotime, China). The specific siRNA sequence numbers utilized are as follows: si-NDUFS6#1:CTTTGCCATTGATTTGATA; si-NDUFS6#2:CCACCCAAAAGTGTATATA.

CCK-8

Cells were planted at a density of 4 × 10³ cells/well in 96-well plates 48 h after transfection. To quantify cell proliferation, 1% CCK-8 (APExBIO, USA) was applied at 24, 48, and 72 h. After 2 h of culture at 37 °C, absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

EdU assay

In 96-well plates, cells were added at an average density of 4 × 10³ cells/well 48 h after transfection. EdU (Beyotime, China) was added once the cells had reached 70% confluence, and they were then incubated for two hours at 37 °C. Cells were then fixed, permeabilized, and stained with Hoechst 33342. Fluorescence microscopy was used to capture images, and ImageJ software was applied to calculate EdU-positive cells.

Western blotting (WB)

The study utilized the following primary antibodies: NDUFS6 (14417-1-AP, Proteintech, China) and GAPDH(10494-1-AP, Proteintech, China), all of which underwent validation by the respective manufacturers. Protein quantification was conducted with a BCA assay kit (Beyotime, China) using RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China) for protein extraction. Antibodies were diluted at a 1:1000 ratio. Following SDS-PAGE separation, proteins were transferred onto PVDF membranes and subjected to primary and secondary antibody incubation. Visualization of protein bands was achieved through an enhanced chemiluminescence kit (Thermo Fisher, USA).

RT-qPCR

Using the SteadyPure Universal RNA Extraction Kit (Accurate Biotechnology, China), total RNA was extracted from cells, and its purity and concentration were measured. The PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Accurate Biotechnology, China) was subsequently employed to perform the reverse transcribe the RNA to cDNA. Quantitative PCR was performed on a LightCycler® 96 instrument (Roche, Switzerland) using the SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Accurate Biotechnology, China). GAPDH was used as an internal control, and relative mRNA levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primers used:

GAPDH forward: 5ʹ-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3ʹ; GAPDH reverse: 5ʹ-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3ʹ; NDUFS6 forward: 5ʹ-GGTCGTCAGAAAGAGGTGAA-3ʹ; NDUFS6 reverse: 5ʹ-CGGAAATGCTCACAGGATGC-3ʹ.

Data analysis

R language (version 4.2.2) was implemented for each statistical analysis in this study. Correlations were determined using Pearson correlation. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed for inter-group comparisons, and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was applied to evaluate survival outcomes. For pair-wise comparisons within groups, the LSD-t-test was employed. The criteria *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; NS not statistically significant.

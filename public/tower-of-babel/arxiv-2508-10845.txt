Quasi-perfect blowups detect regularity
Timothy De Deyn, Pat Lank, Kabeer Manali-Rahul

Abstract
. This work gives a new characterization of regular Noetherian schemes (more generally algebraic spaces) as those for which blowups at closed points are quasi-perfect. In particular, regularity is controlled by the property that alterations or modifications of the scheme (or algebraic space) are quasi-perfect. Additionally, we study the local behavior of quasi-perfect morphisms, showing that for proper morphisms this property can be detected via stalks, and completions and (strict) Henselizations of those. Furthermore, we show that the locus of points where a proper morphism is quasi-perfect forms a Zariski open set.

14A30 (primary), 14B05, 18G80, 13D0

1.
Introduction

The zoo of singularities consists of many exotic creatures.
To this day the study of its inhabitants is an active area of research.
A very useful and helpful way to understand them is through geometrical and homological techniques.
Typically, one associates to each inhabitant invariants.
Such an invariant could be a category; it is natural to then try and study the information contained within, its deeper structure.

An important category associated to any Noetherian scheme
X
is its derived category
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules with quasi-coherent cohomology.
This ‘big’ category contains two important (essentially) small subcategories.
Firstly, the category of perfect complexes, denoted
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
, and secondly the category of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology, denoted
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
.
One has the containment
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\subseteq D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
which controls the regularity of the scheme: these categories coincide if and only if the scheme is regular.
Furthermore, it is the latter which contains the skyscraper sheaves at closed points and it is the inclusion of these in the former, i.e. the skyscraper sheaves being perfect, which controls regularity at the corresponding points.
It is this fact that leads to the main results in this work.

Probing
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
using categorical incarnations of geometric operations allows one to study its structure more precisely and uncover more information contained within.
A key example is utilizing the derived pushforward along a proper morphisms
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\colon D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
to characterize certain singularities.
Important examples include

•

rational singularities
[
Art66
,
Lip69
]
and their ‘mild’ variants
[
Lan25
]
,

•

Du Bois singularities
[
DB81
,
Ste81
,
Ste83
]
,

•

derived splinters
[
Bha12
]
and their ‘birational’ variants (following Kovács)
[
Lyu22
,
Lan25
]
.

Such singularities are often considered to be ‘tame’ as they usually enjoy favorable properties; e.g. derived splinters being normal or Cohen–Macaulay in many settings.
Loosely speaking, these type of singularities represent ‘slight deviations’ away from regularity in regards to (cohomological) behavior.

The story of this note began with
[
MS20
]
.
There the authors showed that a local domain
R
, which is essentially of finite type over a field, is regular if and only if for every alteration
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)
from a regular scheme one has
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
. An analogue of this result in mixed characteristic was posed as a question (see
[
MS20
, Question 1.1]
).
Our initial goal was to answer Ma and Schwede’s question. Whilst we were unsuccessful in that avenue, in the process it became clear that one can characterize regularity by having ‘enough probing morphisms’ be quasi-perfect in the sense of Lipman–Neeman
[
LN07
]
. Here quasi-perfect means that the derived pushforward preserves perfect complexes (see
Definition

2.2
).

As this characterization has, to the best of our knowledge, not appeared in the literature before, it felt worthwhile to write this short note to bring it to peoples attention.
In fact, all of the results below are in the generality of algebraic spaces; but, we restrict to schemes in this introduction for ease.

Theorem A

(see
Theorem

3.3
)
.

A Noetherian scheme
X
is regular if, and only if, the blowup of
X
along any closed point is a quasi-perfect morphism.

Although
A
does not answer
[
MS20
, Question 1.1]
, it provides new and interesting characterizations of regularity: one can characterize regularity by probing with alterations or modifications.

Corollary B

(see
Corollary

3.6
)
.

An integral Noetherian scheme
X
is regular if, and only if, every alteration (resp. modification) of
X
is quasi-perfect.

Notably, we do not require the prior existence of an alteration or modification from a regular scheme.
Therefore, it offers a ‘resolution free’ characterization of regularity; however, see
Remark

3.7
for how one could show regularity using the existence of a ‘quasi-perfect resolution’.
As a further application of
A
, we give a new proof that regularity descends along fpqc covers (see
Corollary

3.11
).

In order to prove
A
we use generation techniques for triangulated categories.
Such techniques have been very successful in (cohomologically) characterizing singularities; for instance, the types of singularities mentioned above
[
LV25
,
LMV25
,
Lan25
]
.
The crucial observation, in our case, is that one can use the skyscraper sheaf of a closed point to detect regularity at that point (we also extend this to algebraic spaces in
Lemma

3.2
, which seems to not have been written down before).

As
A
and
B

show that one can use quasi-perfectness of proper morphism to detect regularity, a natural next question is whether one can ‘practically’ detect quasi-perfectness.
To this end, we show that this can be checked stalk-wise on the target, that is a proper morphisms is quasi-perfect if and only if all the base changes to the local rings are quasi-perfect.
In particular, checking quasi-perfectness of proper morphisms reduces to showing this for morphisms with target the spectrum of a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
.
Furthermore, we show that one can replace
R
by either its
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\widehat{R}
or the (strict) Henselization
R^{h}
(resp.
R^{sh}
); neither affects the quasi-perfectness.
Thus, all in all we have the following, our last main result.

Theorem C

(see
Theorem

3.14
)
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism of schemes with
X
separated and Noetherian. Suppose
A_{\#}\in\{\mathcal{O}_{X,\#},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,\#},\mathcal{O}_{X,\#}^{h},\mathcal{O}_{X,\#}^{sh}\}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is quasi-perfect

(2)

the base change of
f
to
\operatorname{Spec}(A_{p})
is quasi-perfect for all
p\in X
.

In order to prove this theorem, we prove some lemmas, of perhaps independent interest, that allow one to check quasi-perfectness using specific types of flat covers (see
Lemmas

3.9
and
3.10
).
Furthermore, as a corollary we show that the locus of points
p\in X
for which the base change to
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
is quasi-perfect (or equivalently base change to the completion or (strict) Hensalization of the local ring) forms a Zariski open subset of
X
, see
Corollary

3.15
.
An interesting application (which to the best of our knowledge was previously unknown) is that the property of the regular locus containing a nonempty open subset, i.e.
X
being
J
-0, descends along proper surjective morphisms quite generally (we have a mild reducedness condition), see
Proposition

3.17
for the details.

Let us also say a word concerning the restriction to a proper morphism in
C
.
In view of
A
this is mild as the morphisms appearing there are proper.
In fact, for a separated morphisms of finite type between Noetherian schemes, it being quasi-perfect implies that it is proper, see e.g.
[
Nee21
, Proposition 0.20]
; so in practice it is very mild indeed.
However, the real reason we need to restrict to this is that in the proofs we need to leverage certain local-to-global techniques which require working with bounded and coherent objects (e.g.
[
AIL10
, Theorem 4.1]
).

We end this introduction with a few words on the generalization to algebraic spaces.
Although we stated the above results for schemes, these are proven below for algebraic spaces. This is done
Section

3
after a short recollection of necessary definitions and notation in
Section

2
.
We expect the more general results to be beneficial for future work for those studying singularities of algebraic spaces which is recently been gaining traction. For example, recent progress has been made in establishing the minimal model program for excellent algebraic spaces in characteristic zero, see
[
LM22
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We make some recollections in this section, mainly to keep things self-contained and to fix notation and terminology.

2.1.
Generation

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category.
A triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
is called
thick
if it is closed under direct summands (and so automatically strict, i.e. closed under isomorphisms).
For any collection of objects
S
of
\mathcal{T}
, let
\operatorname{add}(S)
denote the smallest strictly full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
S
that is closed under shifts, finite coproducts and direct summands. Inductively define

\langle S\rangle_{n}:=\begin{cases}\operatorname{add}(\varnothing)&n=0,\\
\operatorname{add}(S)&n=1,\\
\operatorname{add}(\{\operatorname{cone}\phi\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n-1},\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1})\})&n>1.\end{cases}

An important feature of this construction is that
\langle S\rangle:=\cup_{n\geq 0}\langle S\rangle_{n}
is the smallest thick triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
S
.
Observe also that it is possible to rewrite the definition of
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}
using
\operatorname{Hom}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1},\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n-1})
instead (so there is no choice ‘left vs. right’).

2.2.
Algebraic Spaces

Our conventions follow that of the Stacks Project
[
Sta25
]
.
Throughout we work with algebraic spaces in the ‘absolute’ setting, i.e. over the base scheme
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
; but, of course, everything remains valid when working over an arbitrary base.

Let
X
be an algebraic space throughout this subsection .
We denote by
|X|
the set of points of
X
, which obtains a topology by giving it the quotient topology via an étale surjective morphism from a scheme; see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03BT
]
.
The algebraic spaces that are considered in this work will always be
decent
in the sense of
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03I7
]
.
This condition ensures that there is a good relationship between the topology of
|X|
and properties of
X
itself.
Note that quasi-separated algebraic spaces are automatically decent; in fact, for locally Noetherian algebraic spaces being descent is equivalent to being quasi-separated
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0BB6
]
.

Defining irreducibility for algebraic spaces is a bit subtle as this notion is not étale local.
Therefore, recall that an algebraic space
X
is called
irreducible
when its underlying topological spaces
|X|
is irreducible; it is not clear whether one can characterize this using coverings from schemes (e.g. using étale surjective morphisms from schemes).
An
integral
algebraic spaces, then, is one that is irreducible, reduced and decent
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0AD3
]
.

Modules and derived categories

Let
X_{\acute{e}t}
denote the small étale site of
X
.
The assignment
U\mapsto\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
defines a sheaf of rings on
X_{\acute{e}t}
, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and called the structure sheaf (of
X
).
Consequently, it makes sense to speak of sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on
X_{\acute{e}t}
; denote the (Grothendieck) Abelian category of such sheaves by
\operatorname{Mod}(X)
.
The Abelian subcategories of quasi-coherent resp. coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules are denoted by
\operatorname{Qcoh}(X)
resp.
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
.

Next, let
D(X):=D(\operatorname{Mod}(X))
denote the (unbounded) derived category of
\operatorname{Mod}(X)
and let
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
resp.
D_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
be the full subcategories consisting of complexes with quasi-coherent resp. coherent cohomology sheaves.
Furthermore, let
D^{\flat}_{\#}(X):=D^{\flat}(X)\cap D_{\#}(X)
for
\flat\in\{+,-,b,\geq n,\dots\}
and
\#\in\{\operatorname{qc},\operatorname{coh}\}
, where the superscripts have to interpreted in the usual fashion.

Any morphisms
f\colon Y\to X
of algebraic spaces induces a morphism of ringed topoi
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03G8
]
, which similarly as in the case of schemes leads to a derived adjunction
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\colon D(X)\rightleftarrows D(Y)\colon\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
; see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 07A6
]
.
When
f
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the derived pushforward preserves objects with quasi-coherent cohomology (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 08FA
]
) and so the adjunction restricts to
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
, i.e. we have an adjunction
\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\colon D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)\rightleftarrows D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y)\colon\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
.

Perfect complexes

Perfect complexes can be defined for any ringed site
[
Sta25
,
Tag 08G4
]
, and so, in particular, for
X
by looking at its small étale site.
Recall that a complex is
strictly perfect
if it is a bounded complex with each term a direct summand of a finite free module.
A complex is
perfect
if it is locally strictly perfect.
The subcategory of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
consisting of perfect complexes is denoted by
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
; this is a thick subcategory.

When
X
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the perfect complexes are exactly the compact objects of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
, see
[
Sta25
,
Tag 09M8
]
. Recall that an object in a triangulated category with arbitrary direct sums is said to be compact if the covariant Hom out of that object commutes with direct sums.
Moreover, in this case,
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is compactly generated by a single perfect complex, see
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0AEC
]
.
Here
P
compactly generating means that it is compact and any non-zero object admits a non-zero morphism from
P
, any such object is called a
compact generator
.
It is worthwhile noting that
P
being a compact generator for
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is equivalent to it being a classical generator for
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
, i.e.
\operatorname{Perf}(X)=\langle P\rangle
.
This follows as
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is compactly generated, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 09SR
]
.

Lastly, for future reference, let us note the following lemma which shows that one can check perfectness flat locally; see e.g.
[
HR17
, Lemma 4.1]
or
[
DLMRP25
, Lemma 2.2]
.
For the definition of the the fpqc topology, see
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03MP
]
; important examples of fpqc covers are fppf and so also smooth and étale covers.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be an algebraic space and
E\in D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
.
Then
E
is perfect if and only if there exists an fpqc cover
\{f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X\}
such that each
f^{\ast}_{i}E
is perfect.

Local rings

Let
\overline{x}
be a geometric point of

, i.e.

is a morphism from the spectrum of an algebraically closed field.
For any presheaf

\mathcal{F}_{\bar{x}}:=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\nolimits_{(U,\overline{u})}\mathcal{F}(U)

Taking stalks at geometric points are exactly the points of the small étale site of

The

\mathcal{O}_{X,\overline{x}}:=(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\overline{x}}

This is in fact a local ring (as can be seen from the following paragraph).

To make the latter more explicit, pick an étale neighborhood

of

where

a scheme.
Then, from

,

\mathcal{O}_{X,\overline{x}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U,\overline{u}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U,u}^{sh},

where
\mathcal{O}_{U,u}^{sh}
is the strict Henselization of
\mathcal{O}_{U,u}
and

is the point at which

is centered.
We refer to
[

,

]

Because of

, for arbitrary

, by abuse of notation we sometimes denote

.

Lastly, let us briefly define the Henselian local ring at a point

.
Let

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{h}:=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\nolimits_{(U,u)}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})

]

As in the previous paragraph one can make this more explicit in terms of a covering.
Indeed, by

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{h}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U,u}^{h}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{U,u}^{h}
is the Henselization of the local ring

of

.
In particular,

(2.2)

(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{h})^{sh}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{sh},

Residue fields

Pick a point
x\in|X|
.
When

is decent there exists, by

,
Tag 03K4
]
, a unique monomorphism
\operatorname{Spec}(k)\to X
representing

.
The field

appearing is called the

at

Regularity

Recall that
X
is
regular
if there exists an étale surjective morphism from a regular scheme, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03E5
]
.
Furthermore, a point
x\in|X|
is
regular
if there exists an étale morphism
f\colon U\to X
from a scheme and a point
u\in U
such that
f(u)=x
and the stalk
\mathcal{O}_{U,u}
is a regular local ring; this is well-defined by
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0AH7
]
.
It is straightforward to see that an algebraic space is regular if and only if all of its points are regular.
Moreover, by
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0AHA
]
, a point
x\in|X|
is regular if and only if the étale local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,\overline{x}}
is a regular local ring for any (equivalently some) geometric point
\overline{x}
lying over
x
.

When
X
is a Noetherian algebraic space, it follows from
[
DLMRP25
, Theorem 3.7]
that regularity of
X
is equivalent to
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\operatorname{Perf}(X)
.
Furthermore, when
X
is a separated Noetherian algebraic space of finite Krull dimension, by
[
DLMRP25
, Theorem 5.1]
, regularity of
X
is equivalent to
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
being strongly generated, i.e.
\operatorname{Perf}(X)=\langle G\rangle_{n+1}
for some
G\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
and
n\geq 0
.

2.3.
Morphisms

We say a morphism of integral algebraic spaces
f\colon Y\to X
is

•

a
modification
if it is proper and birational (i.e. generically an isomorphism), see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0ACU
]
for some characterizations and

•

an
alteration
if it is proper and generically finite, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0AD7
]
for some characterizations.

Usually, in this work the target (and hence, source) of the morphism will be Noetherian.
Note that, for either type, the morphism above is, in fact, surjective.
This follows as they are by definition dominant (this is implicit in generically finite/an isomorphism) and a dominant proper morphism is surjective.
The modifications we will be interested in below arise as blowups.
See
[
Sta25
,
Tag 085P
]
some background on blowups of algebraic spaces.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
now be more generally any morphism of quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic spaces.
Extending
[
LN07
]
to algebraic spaces we obtain the following.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
f
is
quasi-perfect
if
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{Perf}(X)
, i.e. pushforward preserves compacts.

The pushforward preserving compact objects has many different characterizations. Let us just mention
[
BDS16
, Theorem 3.3]
for a nice list of characterizations when viewing the above through a tensor triangular lens.

Warning
.

A morphism of schemes being quasi-perfect should not be confused with it being a ‘perfect morphisms’, another notion that appears in the literature. These notions coincide, for example, for a proper morphisms of Noetherian schemes
[
LN07
, Theorem 1.2]
, but differ in general.

3.
Results

To start, we note an ‘obvious’ lemma which is recorded for convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated algebraic spaces with
Y
nonempty.
Then
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is nonzero; in particular,
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}G
is nonzero for any compact generator
G
of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y)
.

Proof.

The second claim follows from the first as
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is finitely built from
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}G
(i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\in\langle G\rangle
and so also after applying
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}
).
To see the first claim, observe that it follows from pullback/pushforward adjunction that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\not=H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{0}(\mathbf{L}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{Y})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y})

and so
\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is nonzero.
∎

The next should be compared to
[
Sta25
,
Tag 00OC
]
and gives a ‘spacy’ version of this.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a decent (e.g. a Noetherian) algebraic space.
Suppose

is a closed point such that

is perfect, where
i_{x}\colon{\operatorname{Spec}}(\kappa(x))\to X
denotes the residue field of

at
x
.
Then
x
is a regular point of
X
.

Proof.

Let
(U,u)\to(X,x)
be the ‘elementary étale neighborhood’ of
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0BBP
]
, i.e.
U
is an affine scheme,
u
is the only point of
U
lying over
x
, and the induced homomorphism of residue fields
\kappa(x)\to\kappa(u)
is an isomorphism.
We claim that the pullback of
x
under
f\colon U\to X
can be identified with
u
, i.e. we have a cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(u))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(x))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\hbox to0.0pt{ .\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{u}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Indeed, let

denote the pullback.
It follows from categorical nonsense that

is a monomorphism (as

is).
Moreover, as

is étale, it is a disjoint union of fields.
However, as each field corresponds to a distinct point lying over

(they all go to different points as monomorphisms of schemes are injective),

is the spectrum of a field.
Consequently, we must have

In particular, it also follows that

is a closed immersion by e.g.
[

,
Tag 0H1R
&
Tag 0H1U
]
.

Next, using e.g. flat base change, one sees

f^{*}i_{x,*}\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec(\kappa(x))}}=i_{u,*}\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec(\kappa(u))}}

and so also perfect.
Taking the stalk at

\kappa(u)
is a perfect
\mathcal{O}_{U,u}
-module.
Thus,

is a regular point by

,

and so

is a regular point of

Theorem 3.3
.

A Noetherian algebraic space
X
is regular if, and only if, the blowup of

along any closed point is a quasi-perfect morphism.

Proof.

If
X
is regular, then for any proper

we have

\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\subseteq\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\subseteq D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\operatorname{Perf}(X).

Hence, any blowup is quasi-perfect as these are proper.

For the converse, it suffices to show that any closed point is regular (see e.g.
[

, Lemma 3.2]
).
So, let

be a closed point and denote by

its associated residue field.
As the point is closed,

is a closed immersion; see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 0H1R
&
Tag 0H1U
]
.
Consider the fibered square

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Bl}_{p}X\;\hbox to0.0pt{$=:X^{\prime}$\hss}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

is the blowup of

and

is the exceptional divisor.
The hypothesis tells us that

is a quasi-perfect morphism.

As the exceptional divisor

is an effective Cartier divisor on

(3.2)

\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-E)\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\to j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-E)[1].

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\not=\mathbf{R}f_{\ast}j_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{E}

\displaystyle\cong i_{\ast}\mathbf{R}g_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{E}

\displaystyle\cong i_{\ast}(\text{sum of shifts of }\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p))})

\displaystyle\cong\text{sum of shifts of }i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p))}

is a summand of

Remark 3.4
.

It is worthwhile to stress the following: the blowup at a closed point being quasi-perfect implies that that point is regular.
Indeed, this is essentially the content of the above proof.

Lemma 3.5
.

The blowup
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
of an integral algebraic space along a nonzero quasi-coherent ideal sheaf is integral.
In particular, when the ideal is of finite type, it is a modification.

Proof.

The blowup is reduced, see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 085W
]
, as the latter is an étale local property.
Moreover, since blowups are representable, it follows that

is decent (see e.g.
[

,

]
).

Thus, it remains to show that

is irreducible, which is more subtle as this notion is not étale local.
Let

be the center of the blow-up. We have that

is an effective Cartier divisor on

and that

induces an isomorphism

; see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 085T
]
.
When

is irreducible so is the open subset

and so

is also.
However,

being effective Cartier implies that the open subspace

is scheme-theoretically dense in

, see
[

,

]
.
In particular, the closure of

(in

To see that

is a modification when the ideal is of finite type, note that in this case

is proper by

]
.
∎

Lemma 3.8

A morphism

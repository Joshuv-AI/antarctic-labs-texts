Developing anesthesia nursing micro-credentials based on core competencies: a Delphi study
Authors: Xin Yin, Huihui Hu, Luo Wang, Shuo Wang, Fang Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn recent years, advancements in medical technology and a surge in surgical procedures in China have led to an escalating demand for anesthetic nurse services. Nevertheless, the existing quantity of anesthesia nurses in China significantly falls short of fulfilling this requirement.ObjectivesThis study aims to develop scientifically rigorous and standardized anesthesia nursing micro-credentials, with the expectation of providing innovative solutions for the training of anesthesia nursing professionals and injecting new impetus into the sustainable development of the industry.MethodsGuided by the integrated curriculum design model and based on the core competence index system for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals, the initial draft of the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials curriculum system was formulated, through literature review and semi-structured interviews. A panel of experts (n = 13) critically evaluated the preliminary draft to ensure its clarity and rationality. Subsequently, the undergraduate anesthesia nursing micro-credentials curriculum system was formalized through two rounds of Delphi expert consultations (n = 15).ResultsThe response rates of the two rounds of questionnaires were both 100%, and the expert authority coefficients were 0.913 and 0.917 respectively. The Kendall’s coefficient of concordance was 0.171 and 0.238 (P < 0.001) respectively. Finally, a micro-credentials curriculum system for undergraduate anesthesia nursing was formed, including 5 courses (Fundamentals of Anesthesia Nursing, Clinical Anesthesia Nursing, Pain Nursing, Anesthesia Crisis Event Management, and Frontiers in Anesthesia Nursing), 77 learning objectives, and a total of 182 specific contents.ConclusionsThe constructed micro-credentials curriculum system for undergraduate anesthesia nursing is scientifically reliable and comprehensive in content, which can provide a systematic and standardized curriculum framework and implementation path for the rapid cultivation of anesthetic nursing professionals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-08199-z.

Introduction

Micro-credentials (MCs), as an innovative educational paradigm, have been increasingly recognized on a global scale and have attracted extensive attention from educators in China [1]. MCs denote a collection of courses focused on a particular subject, delivered to learners via an information platform, typically comprising 3 to 10 courses pertinent to that subject [2]. MCs focus on a specific academic field, research direction, or core competency and are the smallest unit of professional cultivation. Unlike traditional teaching models, MCs prioritize career-oriented curriculum design, ensuring a strong alignment between instructional content and the changing demands of the profession [3]. This educational innovation not only caters to the digital era’s need for just-in-time learning but also establishes a dynamic bridge between education and labor market requirements.

In recent years, with advancements in medical technology and an increasingly aging population, the volume of surgical procedures in China has continued to rise [4]. The National Health Commission of China explicitly stated in the “Notice on Issuing the Guidelines for Strengthening and Improving Anesthesia Medical Services” that by 2030, the number of anesthesiologists nationwide should reach 140,000 [5]. Based on a nurse-to-physician ratio of 2:1, the number of anesthesia nurses should reach 70,000. However, survey data from the World Federation of Societies of Anesthesiologists across 172 countries indicates that the global density of non-physician anesthesia providers is 2.3 per 100,000 population (with a total anesthesia provider density of 8.8 per 100,000). In China, this figure stands at 1.99 non-physician anesthesia providers per 100,000 population (with a total density of 8.52 per 100,000) [6], meaning China’s number of practicing anesthesia nurses has yet to reach the global average. There remains a significant gap between the current number of anesthesia nurses in China and the projected target [7].

Currently, the training of anesthesia nurses in China relies primarily on two pathways: formal academic education and post-practice professional education. Regarding academic education, the undergraduate education in anesthesia nursing is a four-year program, primarily following the training model adopted by our university, which consists of “general and basic courses in the initial stage followed by specialized courses in the later stage.” Within this framework, the specialized anesthesia nursing curriculum is delivered through one year of concentrated instruction, emphasizing the systematic acquisition of solid theoretical knowledge and fundamental operational skills [8]. However, only a limited number of medical colleges offer anesthesia nursing programs, and anesthesia nursing has yet to be recognized as an independent specialty in the Catalogue of Undergraduate Majors in Regular Higher Education Institutions published by the Ministry of Education [9]. In terms of post-practice education, post-licensure training for nurse anesthetists in China is currently conducted by individual hospitals, with no unified or standardized training criteria established yet [10]. Trainees are generally required to hold a nursing license and possess a bachelor’s degree or higher. The training duration typically ranges from 6 to 12 months [11], covering areas such as anesthesia recovery rooms, anesthesia clinics, perioperative anesthesia nursing coordination, postoperative pain follow-up, and painless gastrointestinal endoscopy examinations [12]. However, due to the absence of standardized training outlines and assessment mechanisms, the quality of training varies significantly across regions, leading to considerable disparities in the professional competencies among anesthesia nursing professionals [13]. In this context, MCs oriented toward professional competencies offer a novel pathway for training anesthesia nursing professionals. MCs use a modular curriculum design with a learning cycle typically lasting 6–12 months. This time frame is shorter than traditional academic education and can efficiently meet urgent clinical demand for anesthesia nursing professionals [14]. The flexible learning paradigm is expected to attract more candidates to the anesthesia nursing field, simultaneously resolving the dual challenges of workforce shortage and quality inconsistency while enhancing both the scale and quality of professional cultivation [15].

Therefore, this study aims to develop anesthesia nursing micro-credentials based on core competence standards, thereby innovating the training model for anesthesia nursing professionals in China and offering a new approach to addressing the severe shortage of anesthesia nursing professionals in the country.

Methods

Design

This study utilized the integrated curriculum design model (Fink Model) as the theoretical framework for developing the MCs [16]. Initially, a preliminary draft of the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials was created based on the core competence standard for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals established by our research team, supplemented by literature reviews and semi-structured interviews. An expert panel discussion was then conducted to assess the content’s rationality and linguistic clarity, leading to the refinement of the initial draft. Finally, the Delphi method was employed to finalize the curriculum system for the anesthesia nursing MCs. The specific process of the research design is shown in Fig. 1. This study protocol was approved by the University Ethics Committee on September 26, 2024 (Approval No: XZHMUj-24112).

Phase 1: preliminary development of the anesthesia nursing MCs

Development of a core competence index system

Prior to the development of the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials, our research team established a core competence index system for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals through a two-round Delphi process during the pre-study period. A total of 16 experts participated in the pre-study period, including six from anesthesia nursing education, two from clinical anesthesia practice, and eight from clinical anesthesia nursing and management. The response rate for both rounds of questionnaires was 100%, with the coefficients of the expert authority of 0.940 and 0.947, respectively. A 5-point Likert scale was used to rate the importance of each item (5 = very important, 4 = important, 3 = neutral, 2 = somewhat unimportant, 1 = unimportant). Item screening was based on the following criteria: mean importance score >3.50, coefficient of variation < 0.25, or a full-score rate >20% [17]. Items that did not meet any of these three criteria were removed. The final system comprises four first-level indicators (cognitive domain, emotional domain, skill domain, and behavioral domain), 17 second-level indicators, and 125 third-level indicators.

Literature review

We conducted a search of domestic and foreign databases, including CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, CINAHL, PubMed, and Web of Science, utilizing the English search phrases “anesthesia nursing/anesthesia nurse/certified registered nurse anesthetist, curriculum/curricula.” The search time extended from the inception of the databases to September 2024. We examined the websites of several domestic and international organizations, including the Ministry of Education and the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China, the International Federation of Nurse Anesthetists, the British Association of Nurse Anesthetists, and the Australian College of Nurse Anesthetists. We systematically identified essential components pertinent to the design of anesthesia nursing curricula, encompassing course titles, educational objectives, and instructional content.

Semi-structured interviews

A semi-structured interview guide was developed in accordance with the research objectives, encompassing the following primary domains: (1) What areas do you consider to be encompassed by the responsibilities of anesthesia nurses? (2) What specialist courses pertaining to anesthesia nursing did you undertake throughout your undergraduate studies? (3) What are your opinions on the structure of the undergraduate anesthesia nursing curriculum based on your clinical internship experience? Which courses do you deem essential, and which should be introduced or enhanced? (4) In the context of clinical anesthetic nursing internships, do you think there are significant specific knowledge or abilities that were inadequately highlighted during undergraduate education?

Criteria for inclusion of interview participants: (1) possession of a bachelor’s degree or higher; (2) completion of specialized courses in anesthetic nursing during undergraduate education; (3) clinical experience in anesthetic nursing, comprising a minimum of three months of internship or practice in a formal medical institution’s anesthetic department; (4) voluntary participation. Exclusion criteria: (1) prior failure in any specialized courses pertaining to anesthetic nursing and (2) absence from clinical anesthetic nursing practice (internship) for three months or longer.

A total of 15 participants were included, comprising four students majoring in anesthesia nursing and 11 clinical anesthesia nurses. Participants were chosen using purposive sampling based on the aforementioned inclusion and exclusion criteria. Each interview spanned 30 to 60 min, and the data were examined by conventional content analysis [18].

Preliminary construction of MCs

Guided by the “Guidelines for Enhancing Anesthesia Medical Service Capacity” [19], based on the core competence standards for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals, and combining literature reviews and semi-structured interview results, a preliminary plan was drafted to include five courses in the anesthesia nursing MCs, namely, Fundamentals of Anesthesia Nursing, Clinical Anesthesia Nursing, Pain Nursing, Crisis Resource Management, and Palliative Care. Consequently, distinct learning objectives and knowledge modules were established for each course in the anesthesia nursing MCs. The development procedure adhered to the Fink model which delineates that the basic stages of course production encompass recognizing significant contextual aspects, formulating pertinent learning objectives, and creating the specific course content. Meaningful learning objectives are categorized into six dimensions: fundamental knowledge, application, integration, human dimension, caring, and learning to learn [16]. Learning objectives for the course were established based on these six dimensions, and these objectives guided the development of the course’s knowledge modules and specific content.

Expert panel discussion on the preliminary draft of MCs

A purposive sampling method was used to select the experts. Before the meeting, the research team sent research materials containing the preliminary framework of the anesthesia nursing MCs to all invited experts and introduced the background, purpose, and main research methodology. The conference occurred in December 2024 at the university housing the research team. In the meeting, the research team presented a concise summary of preliminary research findings and the main content of this study, while also posing the primary discussion questions: (1) Is the curriculum included in the anesthesia nursing MCs reasonable and comprehensive? (2) Is the setting of learning objectives of the courses scientific? (3) Is the arrangement of learning contents of the courses reasonable? (4) Is the content of each course highly relevant to the learning objectives set for the micro-credentials? (5) How should the total credits of the anesthesia nursing MCs be set? How should the credits of each course be distributed? Subsequently, the experts took turns to express their opinions until a high degree of consensus was reached. The established consensus criterion required a minimum of 70% agreement [20]. The complete meeting was documented by two researchers. After the meeting, the research team systematically collated and analyzed the experts’ opinions to revise and form the first draft of the anesthesia nursing MCs, encompassing the incorporated courses, course learning objectives, course knowledge modules, and specific content.

Phase 2: the Delphi method to determine the final anesthesia nursing MCs

Selection of experts consulted

The criteria for expert inclusion are as follows: (1) Involvement in higher nursing education, clinical anesthesia nursing and management, or clinical anesthesia; (2) possession of an associate senior professional title or higher, or a minimum of 10 years of pertinent experience; (3) attainment of at least a bachelor’s degree; and (4) demonstration of a rigorous attitude and willingness to engage voluntarily. The number and variety of experts necessary for the Delphi method are contingent upon the research objectives, design, and available resources, generally comprising 15 to 20 individuals [21].

Preparation of the questionnaire

A questionnaire was developed from the preliminary draft of the MCs. The questionnaire comprised three sections: (1) Background of the research, objectives, preliminary research results, and instructions for filling out the form; (2) courses included in the anesthesia nursing MCs, learning objectives for each course, course knowledge modules, and corresponding specific content; Experts are required to use a 5-point Likert scale to rate the importance of each item (1 = not important to 5 = very important), and two columns are provided for experts to offer suggestions: “Deletion or modification suggestions” and “Additional Items.” (3) An expert general information survey form and an expert self-assessment scale for evaluating judgment basis and familiarity level.

Implement Delphi expert consultation

The research team distributed questionnaires to experts through WeChat or email to maintain their anonymity. Items were evaluated based on the following criteria: a mean importance score exceeding 3.50, a coefficient of variation below 0.25, and a full-score rate greater than 20% [17]. Items failing to satisfy all three conditions were removed directly. Upon meeting at least one condition, the research team deliberated on the retention of the item, guided by expert opinions. Following the initial consultations, the research team amended the draft content according to the screening criteria and expert feedback, restructured the questionnaire, and executed a second round of consultations. The process was iterated until consensus among all experts was achieved.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed and analyzed using Excel 2016 and SPSS 27.0, with continuous variables expressed as mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables presented as frequencies and percentages. Expert engagement was assessed through questionnaire response rates, while expert authority (Cr) was calculated as the arithmetic mean of judgment (Ca) and familiarity coefficients (Cs) using the formula Cr=(Ca + Cs)/2 [22]. The degree of coordination of experts’ opinions was expressed by the coefficient of variation (CV) and the coordination coefficient Kendall’s W (W). The degree of concentration of experts’ opinions was expressed as the arithmetic mean and full score ratio of the importance score of each item.

Results

General information of experts

Thirteen experts from seven different provinces and municipalities participated in the panel discussion, comprising seven anesthesia nursing educators, five clinical anesthesia nursing practitioners and managers, and one clinical anesthesiologist. Fifteen experts from five universities and nine tertiary hospitals across twelve provinces and municipalities, including Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Shandong, Henan, Zhejiang, Shaanxi, Shanxi, Sichuan, Liaoning, Hunan, and Hubei, completed two rounds of Delphi expert consultations. Among them, four are doctoral supervisors (26.67%) and seven are master’s supervisors (46.67%). Expert panel discussion

The expert panel first discussed and confirmed the methodological feasibility of this study. Regarding the courses included in the draft, some experts suggested modifying the name of the crisis resource management course, while others pointed out that the palliative care course was somewhat limited in scope and suggested renaming it “Frontiers in Anesthesia Nursing” and considering the inclusion of other hot topics related to anesthesiology. Regarding the learning objectives of the courses, the experts added five specific items and suggested clarifying the conceptual boundaries between the human dimension and the caring dimension and optimizing the objective statements for each course in these two dimensions. Experts also offered additional recommendations regarding the establishment of course knowledge modules, credit distribution, and the learning burden on students. Following expert discussion, the final anesthesia nursing MCs were formulated, consisting of five essential courses: Foundations of Anesthesia Nursing, Clinical Anesthesia Nursing, Pain Nursing, Anesthesia Crisis Event Management, and Frontiers in Anesthesia Nursing.

Delphi expert consultation

Expert engagement and authority level

Two rounds of consultation each distributed 15 questionnaires, and 15 were returned, with a response rate of 100% in both rounds. In the first round, 13 experts (86.67%) provided constructive suggestions, and in the second round, 7 experts (46.67%) offered constructive opinions. The judgment coefficients of the experts in the two rounds of consultation were 0.960 and 0.953, respectively; the familiarity degrees were 0.867 and 0.880, respectively; and the authority coefficient was 0.913 and 0.917, respectively.

Degree of coordination of expert opinions

After two rounds of consultation, all items demonstrated coefficient of variation values ranging from 0 to 0.27, with overall Kendall’s W tests of 0.171 and 0.238, respectively. The results of Kendall’s test were statistically significant (all p < 0.001). Discussion

Analysis of the scientific and reliability of the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials

This study was conducted by the “Guidelines for the Construction of Anesthesia Service Capacity” [5] and drew upon the core competence standards for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals in China. It also combined the current status of mature undergraduate anesthesia nursing curriculum settings both domestically and internationally. Through the application of the Delphi method, the undergraduate anesthesia nursing MCs curriculum system that aligns with China’s national conditions was developed. The 15 experts involved in this study demonstrated extensive geographical representation, encompassing East China, Southwest China, South China, and Central China, thus reducing the potential impact of regional factors on the findings. The experts’ areas of specialization include nursing education, clinical anesthesia nursing and management, and clinical anesthesia. Over 90% of the experts had more than 10 years of work experience, with 46.67% serving as master’s supervisors and 26.67% as doctoral supervisors, reflecting their extensive expertise in this research field. The questionnaire surveys yielded a 100% response rate, with 86.67% and 46.67% of experts offering constructive suggestions, indicating high levels of engagement. The authority coefficients of the experts in the second consultation round were 0.913 and 0.917, demonstrating relatively high expert credibility [23].

Kendall’s coefficient for the items in the second round of consultation surpassed that of the first round, indicating that following two rounds of feedback, the experts’ opinions aligned, thereby enhancing the coordination and reliability of the research results. However, the overall Kendall’s W value obtained after two rounds of Delphi consultation ranged from 0.17 to 0.24. Although this result was statistically significant (p < 0.01), the values were relatively low. This outcome reflects that, in the process of constructing the emerging curriculum system for the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials, experts still held diverse and valuable differences in perspectives regarding how to effectively translate core competency standards into a refined and focused set of course modules. This phenomenon illustrates the inherent complexity and exploratory nature of the curriculum development stage. The following factors may explain this outcome. First, as a rapidly developing specialized field, the construction of anesthesia nursing micro-credentials is still a pioneering effort, and the lack of an established paradigm makes it normal that a high level of consensus among experts on curriculum structure has not yet been achieved. This study serves precisely to systematically synthesize such content and promote consensus building. Second, experts from fields such as nursing education, clinical anesthesia, anesthesia nursing, and management were invited to ensure the comprehensiveness of the curriculum system. However, the reasonable differences in how experts from various backgrounds judge the importance of courses have reduced statistical consistency while enriching the curriculum design perspective. Furthermore, variations in experts’ interpretation of levels such as “important” on the Likert scale and differences in rating standards may also have influenced the W value. Finally, the relatively large number of items covered in the micro-credentials development objectively posed a challenge to achieving a high degree of agreement.

Although there is room for improving the level of expert consensus, the importance scores of all the finally selected course modules exceeded the predetermined threshold (mean > 3.5), indicating unanimous expert agreement that these elements collectively form the foundation of the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials curriculum system. This study is the first to systematically integrate multidisciplinary expert opinions through an empirical approach, breaking away from the traditional experience-dominated curriculum development model and providing an important starting point for subsequent standardization in the field. Future efforts will focus on continuously validating and optimizing this curriculum system through teaching practice, promoting the development of the anesthesia nursing micro-credential toward greater consensus and standardization.

Analysis of the content design in the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials

The development of the anesthesia nursing MCs curriculum system exemplifies the principle of “few but excellent” [24]. Consisting of five courses, each assigned importance ratings above 4.5 points (out of a maximum of 5 points). Foundations of Anesthesia Nursing, Clinical Anesthesia Nursing, and Anesthesia Crisis Event Management each attained a perfect score of 5 points. This quantitative indicator effectively illustrates the strong acknowledgment of the curriculum system’s value by Delphi experts and reflects the high accuracy of the course design. The five courses comprise a total of 12 credits, with a study duration of approximately 6 to 12 months, enabling learners to complete their studies and acquire the relevant certificates or credits in a brief timeframe. Moreover, MCs utilize an information technology platform for instruction, enabling learners to study at their convenience and from any location, tailored to their individual schedules and learning requirements. In contrast to conventional teaching models, MCs offer greater flexibility [16].

This study first set learning objectives based on core competence standards and subsequently developed course content that reflects these objectives, demonstrating the “competency-oriented” characteristics of MCs [2] and aligning closely with the educational principle of “focusing on core competencies” advocated by the International Federation of Nursing Anesthetists (IFNA). The IFNA delineates seven roles for nurse anesthetists in its practice and education standards: expert, communicator, collaborator, health advocate, scholar, manager, and professional. It also specifies the training objectives and requirements associated with each role [25]. This aligns closely with the learning objectives and specific content of the undergraduate anesthesia nursing MCs curriculum system established in this study, reflecting the global ability of the curriculum setting of this study.

From the perspective of the specific design of the course structure, the Fundamentals of Anesthesia Nursing deeply integrates four major medical basic knowledge modules: anesthesia anatomy, anesthesia pharmacology, anesthesia equipment, and anesthesia physiology. It also sets up practical sections related to the use of instruments and equipment during the peri-anesthesia period. This course not only focuses on cultivating learners’ specialized knowledge and practical abilities in anesthesia nursing but also strictly aligns with the ability requirements for anesthesia nursing experts set by the IFNA. It provides a robust theoretical foundation for advanced study in subsequent professional courses.

The Clinical Anesthesia Nursing course focuses on care during the peri-anesthesia period and establishes a comprehensive knowledge framework. It encompasses essential topics including an overview of anesthesia nursing, ethics and law in anesthesia nursing, management practices, pre-anesthesia preparation, airway management and care during the peri-anesthesia phase, anesthesia and care for various specialized surgeries. It specifically establishes practical components, including training in anesthesia nursing skills and collaboration with anesthesia-related procedures. The teaching model integrates theoretical and practical components to develop learners’ specialized knowledge, practical skills, clinical management capabilities, health education and guidance skills, and critical thinking. This approach facilitates their progression from basic nursing personnel to anesthesia nursing experts, managers, and health advocates.

The Pain Nursing course follows the guidelines of Document No. 884 of China regarding the construction of pain specialty wards [19]. The course content emphasizes the development of pain nursing competencies, concentrating on essential modules including pain patient assessment, pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatment approaches, management of acute and chronic pain, psychological support for pain patients. It also establishes practical teaching components, including the standardized application of pain assessment tools and the operation of pain treatment equipment. The course content adheres to the pain management standards established by IFNA for anesthesia nursing professionals, providing a robust foundation for learners to evolve into anesthesia nursing experts with strong pain management skills.

The Anesthesia Crisis Event Management course, drawing on the Stanford University Operating Room Emergency Manual [26] and “Anesthesia Crisis Management” [27], innovatively sets up common anesthesia crisis events in various human systems, emphasizing the cultivation of learners’ non-technical skills (NTS), including situational awareness, risk assessment and control, communication skills, teamwork, leadership, task management, and clinical decision-making [28]. The NTS level of professionals is essential for effectively managing anesthesia crisis events and safeguarding patient safety during surgical procedures [29]. Studies have shown that up to 80% of errors in high-risk environments such as operating rooms are related to NTS [30]. Additionally, research has found that the NTS related to cognition, attitude, and behavior of anesthesia nurses during clinical crises are inferior to those of physicians. This deficiency in maintaining NTS stability adversely impacts their technical performance and the anticipated outcomes for patients [31]. Therefore, the cultivation of NTS in anesthesia nursing warrants significant attention.

Frontier in Anesthesia Nursing is a distinctive course in MCs. With its forward-looking curriculum design concept, it introduces digital transformation content, including global cutting-edge technologies such as intelligent monitoring system applications and big data-assisted anesthesia nursing decision-making, to enhance learners’ digital competence [32]. Additionally, based on China’s Document No. 884, it sets up emerging clinical application directions in the field of anesthesia therapy, such as anesthesia and sleep therapy, anesthesia and addiction therapy, etc [19]. Although these clinical practices have not been widely implemented yet, their inclusion in the teaching system not only broadens learners’ professional horizons but also reserves professional resources for the future extension and development of the anesthesia nursing professional role, aligning with IFNA’s positioning of scholars.

This trinity of “basic theory, clinical practice, and discipline frontier” curriculum structure design perfectly matches the characteristics of MCs, which are “core focus, practice strengthening, and dynamic updating,” by consolidating professional foundations, emphasizing the cultivation of specialized abilities, and tracking the development trends of the discipline [33]. This curriculum system considers China’s national conditions while adhering to international standards, thereby offering a robust educational framework for developing anesthesia nursing professionals who fulfill clinical requirements in China and possess global competitiveness.

Advantages of using the Fink model for anesthesia nursing micro-credentials

This study adopts Fink’s Model as the theoretical framework to guide the development of the micro-credentials, primarily due to its distinctive advantages in systematic curriculum design. This learner-centered model consists of three phases and twelve steps. It begins by clarifying the specific context of curriculum construction, proceeds to define significant learning goals, and then systematically designs instructional content accordingly [16]. With a clear design logic and scientifically practical methodology, it employs active learning-promoting teaching activities alongside mechanisms for continuous feedback and evaluation to effectively achieve the core objective of “significant learning.” Furthermore, the Fink model emphasizes multidimensional and interactive learning experiences. It focuses not only on the acquisition of foundational knowledge but also on the integrated development of application skills, integrative abilities, human dimension awareness, caring attitudes, and learning how to learn [34]. This emphasis aligns closely with the overarching goal of the micro-credentials: to cultivate versatile anesthesia nursing professionals who possess theoretical knowledge, technical and non-technical skills, and professional virtues.

In comparison with other curriculum development models, Fink’s Model demonstrates multiple dimensions of theoretical strength and practical applicability. First, compared to linear systematic design models such as ADDIE [35], the Fink model places greater emphasis on the in-depth elaboration and multidimensional integration of educational goals rather than being confined to procedural phases. Second, unlike the Kern’s six-step method [36], which also emphasizes needs assessment and outcome evaluation, the Fink model offers a classified set of learning dimensions that serve as a theoretical anchor for instructional design and outcome assessment. Finally, in contrast to competency analysis-oriented methodologies such as DACUM [37], the Fink model moves beyond the limitations of mechanically listing skills and decomposing tasks. Instead, it emphasizes the interaction and integration of knowledge, skills, and attitudes, facilitating the construction of an organically integrated curriculum structure aimed at holistic competency development rather than mere accumulation of job-specific capabilities.

Therefore, by applying the Fink model, this study has not only systematically established the learning objectives and instructional content for the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials but also promoted a shift in curriculum design philosophy from “knowledge transmission” to “meaning generation,” thereby significantly enhancing the scientific rigor and persuasiveness of the curriculum system in terms of theoretical soundness and educational foresight.

The development of anesthesia nursing MCs is of practical significance

The anesthesia nursing MCs represent a groundbreaking approach in the reform of nursing education in China, offering a novel framework for the development of nursing professionals. The courses are intended for students in their third year or beyond who have foundational knowledge in medicine and nursing, as well as for clinical nurses. The curriculum structure enhances the professional capabilities of learners in anesthesia nursing, thereby improving their competitiveness. This model facilitates the cultivation of a significant number of anesthesia nursing professionals within a brief timeframe, thereby addressing the supply-demand imbalance in clinical environments effectively. The successful experience offers a Chinese solution for anesthesia nursing education in developing countries and serves as a reference template for the development of other nursing specialties, with important implications for advancing the professionalization of nursing. In the future, a dynamic adjustment mechanism will be implemented to continuously track updates to the IFNA standards and revise the curriculum annually to ensure that the curriculum system is in line with international standards. Furthermore, mechanisms will be examined to connect Mcs with professional certification to improve societal recognition.

Limitations

The relatively recent establishment of anesthesia nursing education in China has resulted in a limited total number of experts in this field. Moreover, since some experts had already participated in earlier panel discussions, they were not invited in the Delphi process, which may have resulted in insufficient representation of specialists with backgrounds in anesthesia nursing education, potentially introducing a risk of selection bias. Additionally, as the anesthesia nursing micro-credentials were developed within the context of China’s medical education system, their applicability to other healthcare systems and educational environments requires further validation through subsequent studies.

Conclusion

Guided by the Fink model and based on the core competence standards for undergraduate anesthesia nursing professionals, this study constructed a scientific, reliable, and comprehensive undergraduate anesthesia nursing MCs curriculum system through literature review, semi-structured interviews, expert panel discussion, and the Delphi method. The final anesthesia nursing MCs include the fundamentals of anesthesia nursing, clinical anesthesia nursing, pain nursing, anesthesia crisis event management, and frontiers in anesthesia nursing. This study provides a rapid and effective approach to the cultivation of anesthesia nursing professionals in China. The next step of the research will focus on verifying the effectiveness of the anesthesia nursing MCs in practice.

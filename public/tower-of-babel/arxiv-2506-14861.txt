BMFM-RNA : An Open Framework for Building and Evaluating Transcriptomic Foundation Models
Bharath Dandala, Michael M. Danziger, Ella Barkan, Tanwi Biswas, Viatcheslav Gurev, Jianying Hu, Matthew Madgwick, Akira Koseki, Tal Kozlovski, Michal Rosen-Zvi, Yishai Shimoni, Ching-Huei Tsou

Abstract
Transcriptomic foundation models (TFMs) have recently emerged as powerful tools for analyzing gene expression in cells and tissues, supporting key tasks such as cell-type annotation, batch correction, and perturbation prediction. However, the diversity of model implementations and training strategies across recent TFMs, though promising, makes it challenging to isolate the contribution of individual design choices or evaluate their potential synergies. This hinders the field’s ability to converge on best practices and limits the reproducibility of insights across studies. We present BMFM-RNA , an open-source, modular software package that unifies diverse TFM pretraining and fine-tuning objectives within a single framework. Leveraging this capability, we introduce a novel training objective, whole cell expression decoder (WCED), which captures global expression patterns using an autoencoder-like CLS bottleneck representation. In this paper, we describe the framework, supported input representations, and training objectives. We evaluated four model checkpoints pretrained on CELLxGENE using combinations of masked language modeling (MLM), WCED and multitask learning. Using the benchmarking capabilities of BMFM-RNA , we show that WCED-based models achieve performance that matches or exceeds state-of-the-art approaches like scGPT across more than a dozen datasets in both zero-shot and fine-tuning tasks. BMFM-RNA , available as part of the biomed-multi-omics project ( https://github.com/BiomedSciAI/biomed-multi-omic ), offers a reproducible foundation for systematic benchmarking and community-driven exploration of optimal TFM training strategies, enabling the development of more effective tools to leverage the latest advances in AI for understanding cell biology.

Introduction

Transcriptome profiling offers a molecular-level lens into human disease, accelerating drug discovery by enabling detailed insights into gene expression and cellular function. It supports the identification of novel therapeutic targets and the development of personalized treatments
casamassimi_transcriptome_2017
. This field encompasses both bulk RNA sequencing (bulk RNA-seq), which provides a comprehensive overview of gene expression profiles in cell populations, facilitating comparative transcriptomics, biomarker discovery, and functional genomics, and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), which offers finer resolution by capturing gene expression at the individual cell level. scRNA-seq enables the study of cell-type-specific gene expression, exploration of cellular heterogeneity, identification of rare cell populations, and tracking of antigen-specific responses
li_bulk_2021
.

With the exponential growth of publicly available transcriptomic datasets, integrative approaches that combine diverse sources now offer a more comprehensive understanding of biological systems
angerer_single_2017
. These methods often leverage machine learning and deep learning to integrate data and uncover hidden patterns across studies
cheng_machine_2024
. Meanwhile, the field of machine learning itself has undergone a significant paradigm shift, moving away from models designed for specific, well-defined problems towards general-purpose
foundation models
trained on large-scale data
bommasani_opportunities_2022
, yielding breakthroughs in natural language processing (NLP), understanding, and computer vision
zhou_comprehensive_2024
;
awais_foundation_2025
. Inspired by these advancements, a new wave of foundation methods aims to decode the language of biology by leveraging genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and epigenomics at scale
si_foundation_2024
. Among these, single-cell transcriptomic foundation models (TFMs) stands out for their ability to capture dynamic, cell-specific gene expression patterns, and provide deeper insights into gene regulatory relationships, cellular functions, and real-time biological processes.

TFMs are based on the transformer architecture
vaswani2017attention
, which excels at capturing long-range dependencies, making it well-suited for analyzing complex relationships between genes in scRNA-seq data. These models are typically trained on large-scale, pooled datasets spanning 1 to 50 millions of cells from multiple studies. Public repositories such as Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)
edgar_gene_2002
, Human Cell Atlas
regev_human_2017
, Human Ensemble Cell Atlas
chen_heca_2022
, Deeply Integrated Human Single-Cell Omics Data (DISCO)
li_disco_2022
, the European Molecular Biology Laboratory-European Bioinformatics Institute database (EMBL-EBI)
papatheodorou_expression_2020
, PanglaoDB
franzen_panglaodb_2019
, and CELLxGENE
program_cz_2025
provide rich sources of harmonized single-cell data.

Several foundation models have been proposed, each introducing unique design and training strategies.
scBERT
yang_scbert_2022
, a pioneering TFM based on the
BERT
architecture
devlin2018bert
and Performer attention
choromanski2020rethinking
, employs masked language modeling (MLM) to predict masked gene expressions based on intra-cellular gene context. This model has demonstrated superior performance when compared to both traditional machine learning models and deep learning models that are not foundation-based, excelling in various downstream tasks such as cell type annotation.
Geneformer
theodoris_transfer_2023
represents each cell as a sequence of ordered genes sorted by their median-normalized expression levels, and use MLM to predict masked genes based on other genes in the sequence.
Universal Cell Embeddings
rosen_uce_2024
introduces a ”masked or unexpressed” pretraining objective and leverages
ESM2
lin_evolutionary-scale_2023
protein embeddings to enable cross-species transfer.

scGPT
cui_scgpt_2024
, an autoregressive model, adapts generative pretraining with specialized attention masking to handle the unordered nature of scRNA-seq data, enabling both gene and cell representation learning.
scFoundation
hao_scfoundation_2024
adds read-depth-aware (RDA) pretraining task using hierarchical Bayesian downsampling to improve robustness across variable sequencing depths.

More recently,
scCello
yuan_cell-ontology_2024-1
and
scMulan
ma_scmulan_2024
adopt a multi-task learning by combining self-supervised and supervised objectives. The self-supervised learning objective captures intrinsic relationships between genes within a cell, while the supervised objective leverages available metadata, such as cell type, organ type, tissue type, and external knowledge (e.g. cellular ontology relationships). This dual approach enabled the models to encode explicit relationships between cells, facilitating the capture of higher-level biological connections.

At the intersection of transcriptomics and nature language, models such as
GenePT
chen2024genept
,
scReader
li_screader_2024
,
cellWhisperer
schaefer_multimodal_2024
and
CELLama
choi_cellama_2024
explore joint training or prompting of large language models (LLMs) with transcriptomic representations. These methods aim to bridge the gap between natural language representations of genes and single-cell transcriptomics by utilizing both large language models (LLMs) and transcriptomic foundation models simultaneously.

In parallel, several studies have explored the impact of pretraining dataset size, cell-type diversity, and modeling strategies on pretraining effectiveness and model generalization
boiarsky_deeper_2024
;
denadel_evaluating_2024
;
alsabbagh_foundation_2023
, as well as the zero-shot performance of TFMs on unseen cell types
kedzierska_assessing_2023
;
hu_evaluation_2024
. However, research in this field is still in its early stages, and current findings suggest that TFMs may not yet be learning biologically meaningful representations, highlighting the need for further investigation and improved training strategies. While many prior models claim significant advances, their disparate implementations make it difficult to disentangle which innovations truly drive performance improvements.

In response to these challenges, we present
BMFM-RNA
, a flexible and modular software package that supports,

•

Configurable pretraining strategies, as introduced in existing TFMs,

•

Whole-cell expression decoder (WCED), a novel pretraining objective, designed for the non-sequential nature of gene expression data and shown to outperform existing TFMs on zero-shot cell type clustering and annotation.

•

Systematic evaluation on established benchmarks including cell type annotation, perturbation prediction, and batch integration, in both zero-shot and fine-tuning settings.

•

Interpretability tools to analyze model predictions and gain insights into learned biological representations.

We show that with only 1% of CELLxGENE data, WCED-based models combined with multi-task learning, achieve performance on par with existing approaches in key downstream tasks such as cell type clustering and annotation.
With 10% of CELLxGENE data, the WCED-trained models significantly outperform existing approaches on zero-shot cell-type clustering.
As we continue to expand
BMFM-RNA
with support for multiomics integration and novel modeling strategies, we invite the research community to collaborate in advancing the development of robust and biologically meaningful TFMs.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the
BMFM-RNA
framework and datasets; Section 3 presents benchmark results across downstream tasks; Section 4 discuss our key findings; and Section 5 concludes with a summary and directions for future work.

Materials and Methods

2.1
Datasets

To construct a robust, generalizable foundation model for single-cell transcriptomics, we utilized a broad range of publicly available datasets that span tissues, organs, disease states, and sequencing protocols. These datasets are processed to LitData format to enable memory-efficient and parallel data streaming compatible with multi-GPU and distributed environments.

2.1.1
Pretraining Datasets

•

PanglaoDB
franzen_panglaodb_2019
is a manually curated repository that aggregates high-quality scRNA-seq data from public sources. It includes 1,368 samples, split into 305 human and 1,063 mouse runs. For this study, we only used the human samples for pretraining. After standardization and filtering, we retained over 1 million cells from 74 human tissues. These provide broad biological coverage and serve as a rich source of variation for pretraining.

•

CELLxGENE
program_cz_2025
provides a harmonized corpus of single-cell experiments, supporting reproducibility and structured metadata, and it consists of a suite of tools that help scientists to find, download, explore, analyze, annotate, and publish single-cell datasets.
The CELLxGENE Discover Census offers efficient computational tools for accessing, querying, and analyzing the complete collection of scRNA-seq data available in CELLxGENE. Using the TileDB-SOMA-backed Census, we curated a high-quality subset of data for model training and validation. The detailed data selection process is provided in the supplemental material. We used this preprocessed dataset for model pretraining.

2.1.2
Downstream Task Datasets

We evaluated our models using the datasets from recently introduced
scEval

liu_evaluating_2024
, which provides standardized datasets and evaluation protocols for a comprehensive range of single-cell analysis tasks. It includes datasets for
cell type clustering
, assessing the ability to identify distinct cell populations, and

, which evaluates how well methods align data from different experiments while preserving biological variability. The suite also covers
dropout recovery
, aimed at inferring gene expression values lost due to technical sparsity, and technical noise removal, focused on improving data quality by eliminating artifacts. Additional tasks include
cell-type annotation
using known labels,
gene function prediction
based on co-expression or network inference,
perturbation prediction
to assess responses to experimental interventions,
gene regulatory network reconstruction
, and
simulation
, which tests methods on synthetic datasets with known ground truth. For more details on the benchmark datasets, we refer to
liu_evaluating_2024
.

2.2
BMFM-RNA
Framework

The
BMFM-RNA
framework is composed of many modular components, as illustrated in Fig.
1
and explained in detail here.

2.2.1
Single-cell Dataloaders

scRNA-seq data can be accessed from modern storage formats like TileDB-SOMA and the commonly used .h5ad (AnnData) files. TileDB-SOMA supports cloud-native, chunked access to massive datasets, making it ideal for scalable machine learning and interactive analysis, as seen in tools like CELLxGENE. In contrast, .h5ad files are widely adopted for local workflows and integrate well with libraries such as ScanPy and anndata. To support pretraining of TFMs, we built a pipeline that converts both TileDB-SOMA and AnnData formats into LitData-compatible datasets. The LitData module from PyTorch Lightning enables efficient batching, transformation, and multi-GPU or distributed training, allowing us to scale model training to millions of cells. For fine-tuning, we rely on in-memory .h5ad files, as most fine-tuning tasks involve single datasets and do not require streaming or distributed infrastructure.

2.2.2
Quality Control Module

All datasets are processed using the Scanpy Python package, which provides efficient tools for filtering and normalizing large-scale scRNA-seq data. To ensure robust downstream performance, we apply a multi-stage quality control (QC) pipeline targeting both cells and genes.

At the cell level, we exclude low-quality entries based on several criteria. Cells with very low total UMI counts—often indicative of empty droplets or poor capture—are removed to avoid noise. We also discard cells with high mitochondrial content (above 15% of total reads), as these are typically apoptotic or stressed. Similarly, cells with disproportionately high ribosomal RNA content are filtered out to reduce confounding effects from translational machinery. In some datasets, we also exclude rare or ambiguous cell types based on coarse ontology annotations, particularly when they are not well represented or may introduce instability during training.

At the gene level, we retain only those transcripts that are likely to contribute meaningful signal. Non-protein-coding genes are removed to focus on well-annotated functional transcripts. We also filter out genes with low dispersion—those that show minimal expression variance across the corpus—as well as genes that are expressed in only a very small fraction of cells, which often contribute more noise than signal in foundation model training.

Following filtering, we normalize the remaining gene expression matrix using log1p transformation to stabilize variance and compress the long-tailed distribution of expression counts.

2.2.3
Data Selection Module

The size and composition of pre-training datasets play a critical role in shaping the capabilities of single-cell foundation models. Recent work
denadel_evaluating_2024
has shown that scaling both model size and data diversity improves downstream performance, but with diminishing returns when adding highly redundant data. scRNA-seq datasets are typically large, imbalanced, and exhibit significant redundancy due to over-representation of commonly measured cell types (e.g., brain and blood cells) and high transcriptional similarity across many cells. Uniformly increasing dataset size without accounting for these factors can lead to computational inefficiency and limited performance gains.

To address these challenges,
BMFM-RNA
provides an optional, configurable data selection module that supports multiple intelligent sampling strategies. This allows users to balance computational cost and biological diversity based on their pre-training objectives:

•

Uniform Sampling
: A simple baseline approach that performs random sub-sampling across the dataset. While fast, it may preserve existing class imbalances and under-sample rare or biologically informative cell states.

•

Equal Downsample (Round-Robin Priority Sampling)
:
This strategy cycles through annotated strata (e.g., tissue, cell type, or condition) to ensure broad coverage of biological states. It is particularly useful when label balancing is desired, such as for multitask learning or zero-shot evaluation.

In addition,
BMFM-RNA
allows users to specify a desired sampling percentage, making it straightforward to pre-train models efficiently even on very large datasets. All sampling strategies are fully integrated into the data loader and can be easily toggled or customized. Users can apply them flexibly for large-scale pretraining, targeted fine-tuning on specific tissues, or robustness evaluation across rare cell populations.

2.2.4
Cell-level Transformations Module

Cell-level transformations are critical for normalizing scRNA-seq data, mitigating technical variation, and enhancing model generalization. Beyond standard normalization (e.g., log transformation, median scaling),
BMFM-RNA
incorporates a rich suite of cell-level transformations designed to align model behavior with the known properties of scRNA-seq data and to augment training with realistic biological and technical variations.

To simulate dropout events common in scRNA-seq, we implement both random dropout (mimicking stochastic gene loss due to capture inefficiency) and ordered dropout (removing top-expressed genes deterministically, enabling robustness studies and curriculum learning). To model sequencing depth variation explicitly, we adopt a dead-depth-aware Bayesian down-sampling strategy, inspired by
scFoundation

hao_scfoundation_2024
. Cells are probabilistically down-sampled from a target read depth to a sampled read depth, while the model is trained to reconstruct original expression profiles conditioned on read depth, promoting robustness to variable sequencing coverage.

TFMs often rely on an explicit gene ordering. We support configurable gene sorting strategies, including expression-based sorting (ordering genes by expression level, used in autoregressive or masked training) and metadata-based sorting (ordering by genomic or regulatory properties). For models that treat gene expression as discrete tokens, we provide expression binning methods such as quantile binning (equal-coverage bins) and logarithmic binning (log-spaced bins capturing fold changes).

These transformations collectively enhance model robustness, enable simulation of biologically and technically plausible variations, and promote generalization across diverse datasets and modalities. Full details of each transformation are provided in the Supplementary Material.

2.2.5
Multitask Input-Output Preparation Module

Our framework employs a flexible multitask learning setup where each input sample, comprised of gene identifiers, expression values, and optional metadata, is processed by a shared encoder and routed to one or more downstream task-specific heads. The input preparation is handled by tokenizers, collators, and maskers, which support variable input configurations and seamlessly align with the model’s objectives.
We describe the software component here, the mathematical description of how the fields are combined can be found in Sec.
2.2.6
below.

Multifield Tokenizers and Collators

Transcriptomic samples are described by genes and their measured levels in the sample.

BMFM-RNA
, following the ideas developed in
scBERT
,
scGPT
and others inputs this model by producing embeddings for each gene token and it corresponding expression level, and by summing them.
In this context, genes and expressions are treated as “fields” and their combined input as a multifield input.

BMFM-RNA
handles this by wrapping standard Huggingface tokenizers for each field in a MultifieldTokenizer object, which ensures multiple fields are aligned correctly, including potential continuous values for some fields. The collator controls batch preparation, including the tokenization, handling operations like cell-level transforms, masking, and zero-expression padding as needed. This ensures the model receives well-structured, properly formatted multi-field batches for effective training. This approach is especially useful for TFMs as they involving multiple aligned fields as inputs (eg. genes, expressions, state vector such as perturbation state) and labels (eg. for masked genes, expressions, or post-perturbation expression).

Training Objectives

•

Masked Expression Prediction
: In this training objective, a certain fraction of the expression values are replaced with MASK tokens and their values predicted.
This is the most widely used training objective for TFMs, implemented in
scBERT

yang_scbert_2022
,
scGPT

cui_scgpt_2024
,
scFoundation

hao_scfoundation_2024
and others.
As in
scFoundation
,
BMFM-RNA
supports masked expression prediction where the input is downsampled (RDA method).

BMFM-RNA
supports this task in several ways, which are configurable by the user.
Expression values can be tokenized, and then predicted using standard cross entropy loss.
Prior to tokenization, expression values are typically binned using a percentile method, or following log-normalization.
Alternatively, the values can be predicted as floating points without binning, using a MSE loss (assuming the values have been log-normalized).
Gene expression in single cell data is a zero-inflated phenomenon with a large number of zeros, representing absence of detection in the experiment.
To treat during training,
BMFM-RNA
predicts two tasks for each expression value, a binary task predicting whether the gene had expression at all, and a continuous valued task predicting the expression level.

In between these two approaches,
BMFM-RNA
also supports a new loss called “token value loss” in which the logits used for predicting the token are multiplied by the numerical values of the tokens and compared with the numerical value of the expression level. A more detailed analysis of this approach can be found in Zausinger et al.
zausinger2025regressdontguess
.

•

Masked Gene Prediction
: Masked gene prediction is the task that most closely follows masked language modeling from the world of NLP.
Genes in a sequence are replaced with a MASK token with a certain probability and the model is tasked with predicting which gene was masked.

BMFM-RNA
supports the standard version of gene masking as employed in
Geneformer

theodoris_transfer_2023
, and also adds the ability to perform gene masking in sequences where expression values are explicitly included in the input.
Gene and expression masking can be used simultaneously during training.

Figure 2:
Whole-cell expression decoder (WCED) training objective

Part of the cell’s expression data is input into the model (green box) while some of it is left outside (red box).
The input sequence is passed through a
BERT
-like encoder which produces token level embeddings.
The CLS token embedding is passed to the WCED decoder, producing two sets of logits the size of the gene vocabulary (six, in the figure).
The expression levels of all genes are then used to calculate the binary and mean squared error losses.

•

Whole-cell Expression Decoder (WCED)
:
Using the
BMFM-RNA
framework, we implemented a new pretraining objective that is centered around predicting the expression levels for the whole cell at once, rather than limiting to just the masked genes.
The training objective is designed to force the model to learn cell representations, and therefore the decoder uses the CLS token output of the encoder only as input for the decoder, creating an autoencoder-like bottleneck.
The WCED objective takes any input format for the cell, and produces two logits vectors in the shape of the gene vocab, one to calculate the binary expressed/not-expressed loss, and one to calculate the MSE loss.
One of the critical questions for training with WCED is how much of the cell’s input to provide to the model.
As the input decreases, the non-input prediction (masking) becomes harder, but the input prediction (autoencoder) becomes easier.
Our goal is for the model to learn to map any length of cell input to the same representation, which can reconstruct the full cell expression.

This training objective is similar but distinct relative to several others that have been discussed in recent publications.
scPrint

kalfon_scprint_2024
used a bottleneck task but without predicting masked values, UCE
rosen_uce_2024
used the CLS representation to predict whether non-input genes were expressed or not, and
scGPT

cui_scgpt_2024
included the GEPC task to predict all of the masked expression values from the CLS representation.
WECD differs from these in that it pushes the model to map diverse partial inputs to the same CLS representation.
We find that this approach provides superior results as described below.

•

Cell-level metadata prediction
:
BMFM-RNA
enables the user to supply a list of losses which are either defined at the token level (eg, masked gene prediction, expression value prediction, binary expression prediction) or at the sequence level.
Sequence level tasks include predicting cell type, tissue type, disease state, or other categorical or regression tasks.
Categorical tasks can use cross entropy or focal loss
lin_focal_loss_2020
.
Certain labels can also be requested by the user to include a gradient reversal layer
ganingrl2015
, between the encoder and the classifier to correct for batch effects.

These tasks can be performed simultaneously with the language modeling as part of multitask learning, or on their own.

This multitask design enables unified training on diverse datasets while supporting strong task-specific performance. The custom tokenizers and maskers abstract away the complexity of input encoding, allowing users to scale to multiple datasets and objectives without modifying preprocessing pipelines.

2.2.6
Input Representation

The input to our model is designed to flexibly represent both gene identity and gene expression level, with the option of incorporating various levels of biological prior knowledge via pretrained embeddings. The encoder processes each cell as a structured sequence of gene-expression pairs, with embeddings constructed from multiple token types. This design supports both expressive modeling and downstream interpretability.

Mathematical Formulation

scRNA-seq data provides a high-resolution view of gene expression at the single-cell level processed into cell-gene matrix. Formally, let
M\in R_{NxG}
be the cell-gene matrix, where
N\in{1,2....,n}
represent the number of cells,
G\in{1,2....,g}
represent the genes, then
M_{ng}
represents the read count or expression of the
g^{th}
gene in
n^{th}
cell. The basic input to the
BMFM-RNA
model is at a single cell consisting (1) genes in the cell, (2) gene expression profile, (3) gene-level states to represent metadata information associated with individual genes such as perturbation experiment states and (4) cell-level states to represent metadata information associated with individual cells such as cell type, tissue and disease contexts that are captured in large-scale single-cell transcriptomic datasets.

Gene Encoding

Beyond the textual representations discussed above, genes can also be specifically encoded to reflect the diversity of biological scales and phenomena they are involved in. Genes can be represented by their gene names as labels, together with their respective level of expression in the cell. But we may also want to include external knowledge by injecting representations of the RNA, DNA or protein sequences that can vary in length depending on the nucleotides or amino acids considered, epigenomic marks consisting of DNA methylation state or histone acetylation states and finally by their regulatory networks.
Accordingly, the
BMFM-RNA
framework supports multiple gene representations adapted from previously introduced FMs. Specially, it supports random initialization of genes as well as initialization from embeddings obtained using previously pre-trained models that capture distributed representation of genes such as gene2vec using gene co-expression
du2019gene2vec
, representations of protein structures from
ESM2
lin2023evolutionary
, DNA representations from
DNABERT2
zhou2023dnabert
and Borzoi
linder2025predicting
for DNA and epigenomic representations.

BMFM-RNA
is unique among TFMs in that it supports partial coverage by external embeddings, so that the model can learn random embeddings for non-protein coding genes alongside pretrained embeddings based on protein embeddings, or other variations.

BMFM-RNA
supports expression encoding as well, though, as shown by
Geneformer
and by UCE, it is possible to encode expression level information indirectly via the order of the genes or the number of repetitions, respectively.

Expression Encoding

In addition to the simple binning and tokenization approach used by
scBERT
and some versions of
scGPT
,
BMFM-RNA
also supports continuous value encoding so that the full numerical information is available to the model in a learnable way. This is supported via two approaches:

Multi-layered Perceptron
: The MLP-based encoder for continuous features transforms each input expression value
x_{i}
through a series of fully connected layers with non-linear activations. This process can be represented as:

f_{i}(x_{i})=W_{n}\cdot\phi(W_{n-1}\cdot\phi(\cdots\phi(W_{1}x_{i}+b_{1})\cdots)+b_{n-1})+b_{n}

Where
W_{i}
are the weight matrices,
b_{i}
are the biases, and
\phi(\cdot)
is the activation function, such as ReLU or GELU. This allows the model to learn complex, non-linear relationships.

Periodic activation
: The periodic activation-based encoder, uses sine and cosine functions to capture these patterns. For a expression value
x_{i}
, the transformation is:

v_{i}=2\pi c_{i}x_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{i}(x_{i})=\text{concat}[\sin(v_{i}),\cos(v_{i})]

Where
c_{i}
is a trainable frequency parameter, and the sine and cosine functions map the feature into a periodic embedding. After this transformation, a linear projection is applied:

f_{i,\text{final}}=W_{2}\cdot\phi(W_{1}f_{i}(x_{i})+b_{1})+b_{2}

Additionally, the zero-special token mechanism is used to distinguish zero (or negative) values from other features. When enabled, a zero in the input is treated as a special token and mapped to an index in an embedding layer. This can be represented as:

\text{special\_token}(x_{i})=\begin{cases}\text{idx}_{\text{special}}&\text{if }x_{i}=0\\
x_{i}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The special token is then passed through an embedding layer,
E_{\text{special}}
, to get its corresponding embedding:

\text{special\_embedding}(x_{i})=E_{\text{special}}(\text{idx}_{\text{special}})

Where
\text{idx}_{\text{special}}
is the index for the zero-special token. This mechanism ensures that zeros are treated distinctly from regular features, which is crucial to represent genes that not expressed in a cell.

2.2.7
Models, Training, Experiment tracking and Intepretability

We build on top of the Hugging Face Transformers framework to implement our customized models, wrapping their tokenizer and model APIs to support multi-field inputs, including gene identity, expression values, perturbation states, and optional metadata. All models operate on variable-length sequences and employ multi-head output layers for multitask learning.

Model training is orchestrated with PyTorch Lightning, enabling distributed multi-GPU training and mixed-precision execution. Hydra is used to manage experiment configurations in a modular and reproducible manner, allowing users to define and override hyperparameters, data paths, model types, and training strategies via YAML. We use TorchMetrics to compute a comprehensive set of performance metrics.

Our training objectives are tailored to the multi-task nature of our models. We use cross-entropy loss for categorical classification (e.g., cell type or tissue prediction), mean squared error (MSE) and zero-inflated MSE for modeling continuous gene expression values, focal loss for class imbalance, binary cross-entropy for multi-label tasks, and gradient reversal layers to enable domain-adversarial training for batch correction and dataset adaptation.

Experiments are tracked with ClearML, which records hyperparameters, system resource usage, model checkpoints, and evaluation metrics. This provides full reproducibility and enables real-time visualization and comparison of experimental runs.

For interpretability, we integrate Captum
kokhlikyan2020captum
and employ the integrated gradients technique
sundararajan_integrated_gradients_2017
to compute gene-level attributions. This technique provides a principled method to quantify the contribution of each input feature—such as individual genes or expression values—to the model’s output by integrating gradients along a path from a baseline to the actual input. In our setting, Integrated Gradients enables us to assess how much each gene or input token influences a given prediction, whether for cell type classification, expression reconstruction, perturbation response prediction, or masked token recovery. Such interpretability is critical for biological validation, offering insights into which genes drive model behavior and helping to detect spurious correlations or biologically implausible patterns.

Together, this modeling stack, Hugging Face Transformers, PyTorch Lightning, TorchMetrics, Hydra, ClearML, and Captum, provides a powerful, flexible, and reproducible framework for experimenting with complex multi-field input models and deriving biologically grounded insights.

2.3
Pretraining Experiments

The
BMFM-RNA
framework supports a wide range of pretraining objectives and strategies. In this section, we present the results of three pretrained models built using the framework, selected to explore complementary hypotheses about effective pretraining for TFMs. The models differ in their masking strategies, auxiliary objectives, input length, and training schedule, allowing us to assess the trade-offs between these design choices.
All models were pretrained on the 1% equal downsample (see Section
•
‣
2.2.3
above) of the CELLxGENE corpus (2025-01-30 release), consisting of 284,501 samples drawn from the original 28 million sample set. To create a balanced subset, we stratified by the combination of cell type, tissue, tissue general, and disease columns, resulting in 4,718 groups downsampled to 53 samples each, and 2,246 smaller groups where all available samples were included. The four models compared are:

•

MLM+RDA
: trained with expression masking and RDA downsampling, following the settings in scFoundation.

•

MLM+Multitask
: trained with both masked gene and masked expression objectives, along with multi-label classification (cell type, tissue, tissue general), and an adversarial loss to unlearn donor ID.

•

WCED+Multitask
: trained first using WCED with random gene order and log-normalization, then fine-tuned with multitask objectives similar to the MLM+Multitask model.

•

WCED 10 pct
: trained using WCED with random gene order and log-normalization.

Note that the models also differ in input length (4,096 vs. 1,024), masking rates (30% vs. 15%), and training schedules (20 epochs vs. warmup + 20 epochs vs. 20 + 20 epochs), which we explicitly retain to assess the impact of each pretraining strategy. While this introduces variability in total training time, it reflects realistic trade-offs in model design and optimization.

2.3.1
MLM+RDA

This model was trained using the MLM objective where the input has been downsampled using the RDA algorithm, with the same settings as in
scFoundation

hao_scfoundation_2024
, using the implementation of
BMFM-RNA
.
The model is a standard
BERT
architecture with hidden size 768, 12 attention heads and 12 layers, with positional encodings removed.
Expression counts are represented by encoding the continuous values with the periodic activation described in Sec.
2.2.6
, with 48 trainable basis dimensions, then summed with the gene embedding vectors.
For the masked expression levels, two losses are calculated, a binary ”is zero” task and a continuous valued mean squared error loss term.
If the ground truth value for the gene is zero, the MSE loss term is not included in backpropagation.
Training was done for 20 epochs on the 1% downsampled CELLxGENE dataset with 30% expression masking with 4,096 input length.
For samples with more than 4,096 non-zero genes, no zeros are input or predicted by the model.
If there are fewer than 4,096 non-zero genes, zeros are appended after the non-zero values, with priority to zeros for genes that had non-zero expression in other samples in the same batch.
Of the 30% of the tokens that the loss is calculated over, 5% are input to the model unmasked, as a value calibration task.

2.3.2
MLM+Multitask

This model was trained using a combination of two MLM objectives, expression value and masked gene prediction, and three label prediction objectives, cell type prediction, tissue prediction and tissue_general prediction.
The model was warmed up by training for one epoch on the 10% CELLxGENE expression MLM only task, then trained for 20 epochs on the 1% downsampled dataset.
The model is a standard
BERT
architecture with hidden size 768, 12 attention heads and 12 layers, with positional encodings removed.
The information about expression levels was encoded using the MLP encoder described in section
2.2.6
, with expression level representation vectors and gene embedding vectors summed before input to the model.
The training loss is a sum of several loss terms.
For language modeling, the sequences have masked genes and masked expressions, both at rates of 15%. Genes are predicted with cross entropy loss and expression levels with the combined ”is zero” and MSE losses, as in the RDA only model.
Additionally, the model is trained with three sequence classification cross entropy loss terms for CELLxGENE’s cell type, tissue and tissue_general types, plus a sequence classification term with a gradient reversal layer
ganingrl2015
for unlearning donor id from the representation.

2.3.3
WCED+Multitask

This model was trained first with 20 epochs of WCED with input size of 1,024, on the 1% equal downsample CELLxGENE corpus, and then for an additional 20 epochs on the same data with multitask learning, where the WCED task was continued, along with predicting cell type, tissue type, general tissue type and donor id with a reverse gradient layer.

Samples were prepared with genes with randomized order, log-normalized and then truncated after 1,024 genes. The training loss was equally weighted between the autoencoder task (predicting the input genes), and the masking task (predicting the noninput genes), with each contributing a term for binary cross entropy loss and mean squared error loss.

2.3.4
WCED 10%

We trained an additional model with the same size and methods as the WCED+Multitask model with WCED only on a 10 pct equal downsample training corpus.
This corpus uses the same dataset split and downsampling logic as the 1% equal downsample set but with a downsampling rate of 10% instead of 1%.
The corpus contains 2,818,963 samples with 1,966 groups defined by columns ’cell_type’, ’tissue’, ’tissue_general’, ’disease’ of size 975 each, and 4,998 groups from which all samples were taken.
Training was executed for 7 epochs of this larger corpus using the WCED objective with MLP continuous value encoding.

Discussion

We pretrained 4 different TFMs with
BMFM-RNA
indicating the comprehensiveness of our software.
The different pretraining decisions are exposed to the user via the YAML interface, and required no changes to the code.
We reported detailed metrics on cell type annotation on cell type clustering tasks. Our results show that pretraining with a small number of selectively sampled cells can match or even exceed the performance of models pretrained with 10 or 100 times more cells.
As shown in Table 3, WCED 1pct + Multitask achieved similar performance when compared with
scGPT
model on overall zero-shot embedding quality.
Further, WCED 10% on an average achieved 3.7 percentage points better performance than
scGPT
, indicating the generalization of WCED method for cell type annotation tasks.
As shown in Table 6, finetuned WCED 10% performed comparable or better than
scGPT
further indicating the strength of WCED approach.
Figure
3
shows that the embeddings and the predictions from the multitask learning are robust, even when applied to new datasets.
At the same time, as we show in Fig.
4
, the zero shot embeddings capture subtle biological patterns such as the gradual cell-type differentiation from stem cells, to progenitors to fully differentiated cells, on unseen data.

5
Conclusions and Future work

We developed
BMFM-RNA
, a transcriptomic foundation model software package that includes configurable pretraining strategies adapted from existing TFMs.
We also added a novel methodology, WCED, that is suitable for non-sequential nature of transcriptomic data, an evaluation benchmark suite for multiple single-cell machine learning tasks, and utilities to gain deeper understanding of the model insights. We demonstrate that
BMFM-RNA
allows to build multiple variations of TFMs through hydra configurations.
We also show that models built using
BMFM-RNA
package on a small subset of CELLxGENE dataset achieves similar results on cell-type clustering and annotation tasks when compared to existing models. In future, we plan to report detailed results on other benchmark tasks such as perturbation prediction and gene regulatory network inference.
By open-sourcing our package at
https://github.com/BiomedSciAI/biomed-multi-omic
and inviting community contributions, we enable the collaborative development of TFMs to deepen biological understanding and accelerate target and drug discovery in the future.

Hodge splittings and Einstein 4-manifolds
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. On an oriented 4 4 -manifold, we study pairs of Riemannian metrics ( g , h ) (g,h) for which the curvature tensor of g g preserves the Hodge splitting determined by h h . This extends the Einstein condition in dimension four, which is recovered when h h is conformal to g g . We prove that such pairs satisfy a generalized Hitchin–Thorpe inequality, which reduces to the classical one when h h is conformal to g g . We then exhibit a pair ( g , h ) (g,h) on # 5 ​ ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2} , which violates Hitchin–Thorpe and hence admits no Einstein metric, thus showing that our condition is indeed broader than the Einstein condition.



1.
Introduction

On an oriented
4
-manifold
M
, which pairs of Riemannian metrics have the property that the curvature tensor of one leaves the Hodge splitting of the other invariant? In other words, which pairs
(g,h)
satisfy

(1)

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0,

where
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is the curvature bilinear form of
g
and
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
is the decomposition of
\Lambda^{2}
into the self-dual and anti-self-dual eigenspaces of
h
’s Hodge star
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
? Because the Hodge star on
2
-forms is conformally invariant in dimension four, what is relevant to (
1
) is actually the oriented conformal class
[h]
, or equivalently the splitting
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
itself. The guiding example is the Einstein case: some, and hence every,
h\in[g]
satisfies (
1
) if and only if
g
is Einstein
[
Ber61
,
ST69
]
.

In fact (
1
) separates the algebra of the curvature tensor from the analytic problem of varying
g
itself. Let
Q
denote the wedge pairing on
\Lambda^{2}
defined by
Q(\xi,\eta)dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\xi\wedge\eta
, and let
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
be the
Q
-self-adjoint endomorphism determined by
Q(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}(\xi),\eta)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\xi,\eta)
. Then in fact
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}=*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
; if
g
is Einstein, then
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, so
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
is real-diagonalizable. But the latter is not true in general, as
Q
has signature
(3,3)
. Our first result identifies this as the precise obstruction to (
1
), and then uses it to derive a topological obstruction when
M
is compact. Indeed, setting
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi,\eta)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=Q(\xi,*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\eta)
and

\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup_{M}\left\|\frac{1}{2}\left(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}+*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\right)\right\|_{\text{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}},

we show that this quantity gauges the deviation from the classical Hitchin–Thorpe inequality
[
Tho69
,
Hit74
]
.

Theorem

(Relative Hitchin–Thorpe inequality)
.

If a compact oriented 4-manifold
M
admits a Riemannian pair
(g,h)
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
, then

2\chi(M)\geq\frac{3}{\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)}|\tau(M)|,

with
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}\geq 1
and equal to
1
if and only if
h\in[g]
;
the latter yields the classical Hitchin–Thorpe inequality. Finally,
\chi(M)=0
if and only if
g
is flat.

Our second and main result is that
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}
is essential, because (
1
) can violate Hitchin–Thorpe. In violating Hitchin–Thorpe, our example goes in a different direction from those obtained via Seiberg–Witten theory in
[
LeB96
]
.

Theorem

(A Hitchin–Thorpe-violating example)
.

\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
admits a Riemannian pair
(g,h)
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
, but no Einstein metric.

(Here
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\geq\frac{15}{14}
.) Another aspect of (
1
) that we examine is its variational character. For fixed
g
, consider the
h
-scalar contraction of
g
’s curvature tensor,

\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathrm{tr}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathrm{tr}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}).

Then we show that on compact
M
, the functional

\mathscr{A}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(h)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\int_{M}\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},

which is conformally invariant in
h
, has Euler–Lagrange equation

(2)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}}{4}h,

and that this is equivalent to (
1
). In contrast to the usual Einstein-Hilbert action, which yields a second-order elliptic PDE, the variation of
\mathscr{A}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
here is algebraic:
g
is fixed, no derivatives of the variation appear, and the equation is zero-order in
h
. Nevertheless, we present this as our third and final result because (
2
) generalizes the Einstein equation
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\lambda g
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
reformulates the condition
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
in several equivalent ways, and also includes the variational characterization via
\mathscr{A}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. Section
3
proves the diagonalizability criterion of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
, the Euler obstruction, and the relative Hitchin–Thorpe inequality. Section
4
discusses examples and shared-frame curvature formulae. Finally, Section
5
constructs the Riemannian pair
(g,h)
on
\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
satisfying (
1
).

Characterizing

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(v\wedge w)},{x\wedge y}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(v,w,x,y)\hskip 14.45377pt\text{for all $v,w,x,y\in T_{p}M$},

Lemma 1
.

The quadratic form
\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}

Proof.

The key is that, for any

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}

\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}

\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bar{g}$-$h$}}=\sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bar{g}$-$h$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}

∎

Next, define the mixed Ricci tensor and mixed scalar curvature of the pair

\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathrm{tr}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathrm{tr}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}).

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented Riemannian
4
-manifold and let

be an auxiliary Riemannian metric. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0

Proposition

Proposition 2
.

Fix a compact oriented Riemannian

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}}{4}h.

Let us make two remarks regarding Proposition
2
. First, it is enough to consider variations of the form

as we have done. Indeed, the space of smooth Riemannian metrics is an open subset of the vector space

(0,2)

recovers the Einstein-Hilbert functional, the two variational problems are quite different. In the latter one varies a metric that determines both the curvature tensor and the volume form, so the first variation requires differentiating the Levi-Civita connection and curvature; as is well known, this yields a second-order elliptic PDE. Here, by contrast,
g
is fixed throughout, and only the auxiliary metric

is varied; as mentioned above,
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
does not change. Moreover, unlike the ordinary Einstein-Hilbert functional, no separate volume normalization is needed here: in dimension four,

is already scale-invariant in

, and thus depends only on the oriented conformal class
[h]
. Indeed, if
\tilde{h}=e^{2u}h
, then

and

, so that

\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$\tilde{h}$}}=e^{-4u}\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}

and hence

\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$\tilde{h}$}}\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\tilde{h}$}}=\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}.

This is compatible with the fact that

3.
Topological obstructions to
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0

\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=|W^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}+|W^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}|^{2}+\frac{\text{scal}^{2}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}}{24}\cdot

This is precisely the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet integrand, so that

\chi(M)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Since
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
is real-diagonalizable by Proposition
4
,

\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\lambda_{i}^{2}\geq 0,

so that
\chi(M)\geq 0
. If equality holds, then each
\lambda_{i}=0
, hence
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}=0
, hence
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=0
and
g
is flat. The converse is immediate.
∎

Theorem 1

(Relative Hitchin–Thorpe inequality)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact oriented Riemannian
4
-manifold, and let
h
be a Riemannian metric satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
. Set
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi,\eta)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=Q(\xi,*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\eta)
and

\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sup_{M}\left\|\frac{1}{2}\left(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}+*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\right)\right\|_{\text{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}\cdot

Then
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\geq 1
and

(16)

\displaystyle 2\chi(M)\geq\frac{3}{\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)}|\tau(M)|.

Furthermore,
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)=1
if and only if
h\in[g]
, in which case the inequality reduces to the classical Hitchin–Thorpe inequality.

Proof.

By Proposition
3
,
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}=*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
. By Proposition
4
,
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
is
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-self-adjoint,
hence real-diagonalizable; thus
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}
is
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-self-adjoint and positive-semidefinite. However,
g
’s Hodge star
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
need not be
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-self-adjoint; indeed, using that
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is
Q
-self-adjoint, we have

q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\xi,\eta)=Q(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\xi,*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\eta)=Q(\xi,*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!\eta)=q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi,*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\eta),

from which it follows that the
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-self-adjoint part of
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is

H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}+*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\right).

Because
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}=*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}
,

\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}})=\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\!*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}),

hence

\mathrm{tr}(H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}).

Choose a
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{6}
for
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}
, say
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}\xi_{i}=\mu_{i}\xi_{i}
with each
\mu_{i}\geq 0
. With respect to this basis,

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}=\mathrm{diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{6})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptH_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}=H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}^{t}=(q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\xi_{i},\xi_{j})).

In particular, each
|q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\xi_{i},\xi_{i})|\leq\|H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\|_{\text{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}\leq\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)
, so that

\big|\mathrm{tr}(H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\big|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mu_{i}\,q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\xi_{i},\xi_{i})\right|\leq\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mu_{i}=\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\,\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}).

Thus

\big|\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\big|\leq\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\,\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2}).

Finally, using the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet and signature formulas in the form

\chi(M)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\tau(M)=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

(the latter because
\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})=\mathrm{tr}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}^{2})=|W^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}-|W^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}
), we obtain

|\tau(M)|\leq\frac{\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\wedge$}}}^{2})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\frac{2\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)}{3}\chi(M).

If
h\in[g]
, then
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}=*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
and hence

\frac{1}{2}(*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}+*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}})=*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Since
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is an isometry of
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, one has
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}=1
. This gives Hitchin–Thorpe. To complete the proof, we show that
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}\geq 1
and equal to
1
if and only if
h\in[g]
. First, any
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
-eigenbasis
\{\xi_{i}^{+},\xi_{i}^{-}\}_{i=1}^{3}
remains orthonormal with respect to
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, and furthermore the latter preserves the
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
-splitting
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
:

q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi^{+},\xi^{-})=-Q(\xi^{+},\xi^{-})=-Q(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})=-Q(\xi^{-},*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\xi^{+})=-q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi^{-},\xi^{+}).

Express
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}},*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, and then
H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}
with respect to
\{\xi_{i}^{+},\xi_{i}^{-}\}_{i=1}^{3}
:

*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}=\begin{bmatrix}I&O\\
O&-I\end{bmatrix}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377ptH_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}=\begin{bmatrix}A&O\\
O&D\end{bmatrix}\cdot

Next, observe that the projection of
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
onto
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
is injective and hence an isomorphism of 3-planes; this allows us to “go from
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
to
\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
through
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
”; concretely, to define a linear map
T\colon\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
by

\xi^{+}\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\mapsto\,\text{unique}~\xi^{+}+\xi^{-}\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\mapsto\xi^{-}\in\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}.

In terms of this map,
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\{\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}):\xi^{+}\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\}
. Furthermore, because
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
is
Q
-positive, and because
Q
also preserves the
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
-splitting
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, we have that

Q(\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}),\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}))=q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi^{+},\xi^{+})-q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(T(\xi^{+}),T(\xi^{+}))>0.

For any
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-unit
\xi^{+}\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, we thus have that

q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(T(\xi^{+}),T(\xi^{+}))<q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi^{+},\xi^{+})=1\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\|T\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}<1,

by compactness of the
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-unit sphere in
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
and continuity. Now consider the analogous construction on
\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
; this yields a linear map
T^{*}\colon\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, in terms of which
\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\{\xi^{-}+T^{*}(\xi^{-}):\xi^{-}\in\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\}
. In fact
T^{*}
is the
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-adjoint of
T
: using that
Q
preserves both of the
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
- and
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
-splittings, we have

Q(\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}),\xi^{-}+T^{*}(\xi^{-}))=0\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377ptq_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(T(\xi^{+}),\xi^{-})=q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(\xi^{+},T^{*}(\xi^{-})).

Furthermore,
\|T^{*}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}=\|T\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}<1
, and for any
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-unit eigenvector
\eta\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
of the
q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
-self-adjoint composition
T^{*}T
,

T^{*}T\eta=\lambda\eta\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\lambda=q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(T^{*}T\eta,\eta)=q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}(T\eta,T\eta)\leq\|T\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}<1.

Similarly for
TT^{*}
. Thus the eigenvalues of both
T^{*}T
and
TT^{*}
lie within
[0,1)
; in particular,
I-T^{*}T\colon\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
is invertible. We now express
A
in terms of
I\pm T^{*}T
. Indeed, for
\xi^{+}\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
and
T(\xi^{+})\in\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, consider
(\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}))\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
and
(T(\xi^{+})+T^{*}(T(\xi^{+})))\in\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
; then

\displaystyle*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\big((\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}))-(T(\xi^{+})+T^{*}(T(\xi^{+})))\big)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(I-T^{*}T)(\xi^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!(\xi^{+}+T(\xi^{+}))

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\,\big(T(\xi^{+})+T^{*}(T(\xi^{+}))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\underbrace{(I+T^{*}T)(\xi^{+})}_{\text{in $\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}+\underbrace{2T(\xi^{+})}_{\text{in $\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}.

As
(I-T^{*}T)(\xi^{+})\in\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, it follows that
A=(I+T^{*}T)(I-T^{*}T)^{-1}
. Similarly,
D=-(I+TT^{*})(I-TT^{*})^{-1}
. Thus if
\lambda
and
\mu
are eigenvalues of
T^{*}T
and
TT^{*}
, then

\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\geq 1
and
-\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}\leq-1

are eigenvalues of
A
and
D
, respectively. Thus
\|H_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g,h$}}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$q_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}$}}\geq 1
, hence the supremum
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\geq 1
, with equality if and only if each
\lambda=\mu=0
; i.e., if and only if
T=T^{*}=0
, in which case
\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
, hence
h\in[g]
.
∎

When
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}=*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, the classical Hitchin–Thorpe inequality is recovered. But as the
g
- and
h
-Hodge splittings separate,
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)
may increase, weakening the estimate. We will show in Theorem
2
that this may indeed happen.

4.
Examples

We now record two families of pairs
(g,h)
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
; both families have the property that
g
and
h
share orthogonal frames. A third example, also within our shared-orthogonal-frame ansatz, will follow afterwards in Section
5
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
(\Sigma_{1},g_{1})
and
(\Sigma_{2},g_{2})
be oriented closed Riemannian surfaces with Gauss curvatures
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, respectively. Assume that
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are nowhere zero and have the same sign. Set

(M,g)=(\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2},g_{1}\oplus g_{2}).

For positive smooth functions
\alpha,\beta
on
M
, define the Riemannian metric

h\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\alpha^{2}g_{1}\oplus\beta^{2}g_{2}.

Then
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
if and only if

\frac{K_{1}}{\alpha^{4}}=\frac{K_{2}}{\beta^{4}}\cdot

Moreover, if
K_{1}\neq K_{2}
, then
g
is not Einstein.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be an oriented local
g_{1}
-orthonormal frame on
\Sigma_{1}
and
\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
an oriented local
g_{2}
-orthonormal frame on
\Sigma_{2}
. Then
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
is a local
g
-orthonormal frame on
M
. As the Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian product splits, the only nonzero sectional curvatures of
g
are

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=K_{1}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=K_{2},

and every mixed
2
-plane has sectional curvature
0
. Now define the
h
-orthonormal frame
\{\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2},\tilde{e}_{3},\tilde{e}_{4}\}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{\alpha^{-1}e_{1},\alpha^{-1}e_{2},\beta^{-1}e_{3},\beta^{-1}e_{4}\}
. With respect to the
h
-orthonormal basis
\{\tilde{e}_{i}\wedge\tilde{e}_{j}\}
,
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}
takes the form

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=-\text{diag}\!\left(\frac{K_{1}}{\alpha^{4}},0,0,\frac{K_{2}}{\beta^{4}},0,0\right),

from which
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
follows if and only if
\frac{K_{1}}{\alpha^{4}}=\frac{K_{2}}{\beta^{4}}
(regarding the minus sign in
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}
, recall our sign conventions in (
3
) and (
8
)). Finally, a product metric
g_{1}\oplus g_{2}
is Einstein if and only if the two Ricci curvatures agree, which for surfaces means exactly
K_{1}=K_{2}
.
∎

For instance, one may take
\alpha=|K_{1}|^{1/4}/c
and
\beta=|K_{2}|^{1/4}/c
for any constant
c>0
. Thus Proposition
5
immediately yields pairs
(g,h)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
with differently scaled round factors, as well as on
\Sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\times\Sigma_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}}
for closed hyperbolic surfaces of genera
g,h\geq 2
equipped with differently scaled hyperbolic metrics. Our next example is from among the warped product family.

Proposition 6
.

Let
f\colon[0,\pi]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be positive on
(0,\pi)
and suppose that
f
is a smooth warping function at the two poles; equivalently, near
0
one has

f(t)=t+O(t^{3})

with only odd powers in its Taylor expansion, and near
\pi
one has the analogous expansion in the variable
\pi-t
, with leading term
\pi-t
. On
(0,\pi)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, set

g\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=dt^{2}+f(t)^{2}g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}},

and define

A(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptB(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1-(f^{\prime}(t))^{2}}{f(t)^{2}}\cdot

Assume
A(t)>0
and
B(t)>0
on
(0,\pi)
, and assume in addition that, after the arclength reparametrization

ds=\sqrt{\frac{A}{B}}\,dt,

the warping function
f(t(s))
satisfies the same smooth oddness conditions at the two endpoints. Then

h\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{A}{B}\,dt^{2}+f(t)^{2}g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}}

extends smoothly to a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, and
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
. Moreover,
g
is Einstein if and only if
A=B
, in which case
g
is the round metric.

Proof.

The smoothness hypothesis on
f
is the standard one for the warped product
dt^{2}+f^{2}g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}}
(see, e.g.,
[
Pet16
, Sec. 1.4.4]
), so
g
extends smoothly over the two collapsed
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-orbits. The additional arclength condition is precisely the same criterion applied to the auxiliary metric
h
: in the
s
-coordinate,
h=ds^{2}+f(t(s))^{2}g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}}
. Let
\{\bar{e}_{2},\bar{e}_{3},\bar{e}_{4}\}
be a local orthonormal frame for
g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}}
and set

e_{1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\partial_{t}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pte_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=f^{-1}\bar{e}_{i}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=2,3,4.

With respect to this
g
-orthonormal frame, the only sectional-curvature types are

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{i})=A\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=B\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti,j=2,3,4.

With respect to
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
, we thus have

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=-\text{diag}(A,A,A,B,B,B).

In the corresponding
h
-orthonormal frame

\tilde{e}_{1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sqrt{\frac{B}{A}}e_{1}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\tilde{e}_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{i}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=2,3,4,

one has

\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\tilde{e}_{1},e_{i},e_{i},\tilde{e}_{1})=\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{j},e_{i})=B,

and hence

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=-\text{diag}(B,B,B,B,B,B).

Therefore
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
. Finally,

is Einstein exactly when

; in the smooth rotationally symmetric
\mathbb{S}^{4}
ansatz this is the round case.
∎

For example, for the choice
f(t)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sin t\,(1+\varepsilon\sin^{2}t)
with
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{6}
, all the conditions in Proposition

are satisfied, with

A(t)=\frac{1-6\varepsilon+9\varepsilon\sin^{2}t}{1+\varepsilon\sin^{2}t}\hskip 3.61371pt,\hskip 3.61371ptB(t)=\frac{1-6\varepsilon+(6\varepsilon-9\varepsilon^{2})\sin^{2}t+9\varepsilon^{2}\sin^{4}t}{(1+\varepsilon\sin^{2}t)^{2}}\cdot

For this explicit family,
A/B
is a smooth even function of the polar variable with value

at the pole; hence
s(t)
is an odd smooth function with
s^{\prime}(0)=1
, and the inverse
t(s)
is odd. Therefore
f(t(s))
has the required odd expansion, so the smoothness criterion for

is satisfied. Because
A\neq B
, the metric
g
is not Einstein. Note that Propositions
5
and
6
are both of shared-orthogonal-frame type. Such pairs
(g,h)
are restricted by the following fact:

Lemma 2
.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}\subseteq T_{p}M
be a
g
-orthonormal basis such that, with respect to the basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}(T_{p}M)
,
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
has the matrix

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}A&=&\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}),\\
B&=&\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),\\
C&=&\operatorname{diag}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}).\end{array}\right.

If there is a second Riemannian metric
h
for which
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
is
h
-orthogonal, say
h(e_{i},e_{j})=x_{i}^{-1}\delta_{ij}
with

*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$-$h$}}*_{\scalebox{0.5}{$h$}}
reduces to

x_{1}x_{2}a_{1}=x_{3}x_{4}c_{1}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptx_{1}x_{3}a_{2}=x_{2}x_{4}c_{2}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptx_{1}x_{4}a_{3}=x_{2}x_{3}c_{3}.

Hence, such an

exists if and only if each complementary pair has the same sign, or both vanish.

Proof.

This is a straightforward computation.
∎

In other words, within the shared-orthogonal-frame ansatz, a Riemannian auxiliary metric

may rebalance the magnitudes of
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
-opposite sectional curvatures, but not flip their signs. This restriction is important; it implies
\chi\geq 0
but as we shall see, it does not imply Hitchin–Thorpe.

5.
A Hitchin–Thorpe-violating example

Although Corollary
1
already gives
\chi(M)\geq 0
without any shared-frame hypothesis, the shared-frame formulas are useful because they show explicitly why Hitchin–Thorpe need not follow. Before proving our main result (Theorem
2
below), let us demonstrate this now.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact oriented
4
-manifold whose curvature operator has the form

\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&C\end{bmatrix}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}A&=&\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}),\\
B&=&\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}),\\
C&=&\operatorname{diag}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\end{array}\right.

in a local oriented
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
-orthonormal basis (
9
). Then

(17)

\displaystyle 2\chi(M)\pm 3\tau(M)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(a_{i}\pm b_{i})(c_{i}\pm b_{i})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Proof.

The Euler formula is the Pfaffian computation already used in the proof of Corollary
1
; when written in the basis above, it is

(18)

\displaystyle\chi(M)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(a_{i}c_{i}+b_{i}^{2})\,dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

The signature formula also appeared in the proof of Theorem
1
:

\tau(M)=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\big(|W^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}-|W^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}|^{2}\big)dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

In the self-dual/anti-self-dual eigenbasis determined by the
*_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
-splitting
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\oplus\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
,
the diagonal entries of the two Weyl blocks differ by the terms
b_{i}(a_{i}+c_{i})
, and the algebraic Bianchi identity gives
b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}=0
. A direct substitution gives

\tau(M)=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i}b_{i}(a_{i}+c_{i})dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Combining the two formulas gives the displayed expressions for
2\chi\pm 3\tau
.
∎

In the shared-frame setting of Lemma
2
,
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
forces
a_{i}c_{i}\geq 0
for each
i
, and therefore the Euler integrand (
18
) is nonnegative. But (
17
) shows that the Hitchin–Thorpe integrands need not be nonnegative. For instance, the algebraic data

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(a_{1},c_{1},b_{1})&=&\!\!(4,1,2),\\
(a_{2},c_{2},b_{2})&=&\!\!(-2,-1/2,-1),\\
(a_{3},c_{3},b_{3})&=&\!\!(-2,-1/2,-1)\end{array}\right.

satisfy
b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}=0
and
a_{i}c_{i}>0
for each
i
, yet

\sum_{i}(a_{i}c_{i}+b_{i}^{2})=12\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\sum_{i}b_{i}(a_{i}+c_{i})=15.

Thus the shared-frame condition is compatible, already pointwise, with one Hitchin–Thorpe density having the wrong sign. We now realize this phenomenon globally, by constructing
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
on a manifold that admits no Einstein metric. We will need the following function, the proof of whose existence is long and technical, but needed for our main result (Theorem
2

p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(rx)=P(2x-x^{2})\geq Px\quad\text{for}\quad 0<x<1,

we get

(23)

p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(rx)-G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}(rx)\geq\frac{P}{2}x\geq\frac{P}{4}\eta\quad\text{for}\quad rx\in K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}.

Likewise,

(24)

p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}(t)=\frac{2P}{r}\left(1-\frac{t}{r}\right)\geq\frac{P}{r}\quad\text{on}\quad K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}},

after imposing
\eta<1/2
. We now estimate how much the cutoff changes the profile. Since

\phi_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)=\big(1-\beta(x/\eta)\big)x^{2},

we have

(25)

\|p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}-p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$C^{0}(K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}})$}}\leq P\eta^{2}.

Also,

\phi^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}(x)-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)=2\big(1-\beta(x/\eta)\big)x-\beta^{\prime}(x/\eta)\frac{x^{2}}{\eta}.

Since
x=\frac{t}{r}\leq\eta
on
K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}
, this gives

|\phi^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}(x)-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)|\leq(2+B_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\beta$}})\eta.

Therefore

(26)

\|p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}-p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$C^{0}(K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}})$}}\leq\frac{P}{r}(2+B_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\beta$}})\eta.

Finally, applying (
22
) with
p=p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}
and
q=p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}
, and using (
25
), gives

(27)

\|G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}$}}-G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$C^{0}(K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}})$}}\leq\frac{3sP\sin^{3}s}{\varepsilon^{4}\cos s}\eta^{2}.

Combining (
23
), (
25
), and (
27
), we obtain, on
K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}
,

\displaystyle p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}-G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}$}}

\displaystyle=\big(p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}-G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\big)+\big(p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}-p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}\big)-\big(G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}$}}-G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$L$}}$}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{P}{4}\eta-P\eta^{2}-\frac{3sP\sin^{3}s}{\varepsilon^{4}\cos s}\eta^{2}.

This yields
p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}>G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}$}}
on
K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}
for

\eta<\frac{1}{4}\big(1+\frac{3s\sin^{3}s}{\varepsilon^{4}\cos s}\big)^{\!-1}.
Similarly, combining (
24
) and (
26
), we get

p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}\geq\frac{P}{r}-\frac{P}{r}(2+B_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\beta$}})\eta.

After requiring
(2+B_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\beta$}})\eta<1
, this gives
p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}>0
on
K_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}
. Thus we have now proved
p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}>G_{\text{$p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}$}}
and
p^{\prime}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\eta$}}>0
on all three regions
(0,r\eta/2]
,
[r\eta/2,r\eta]
, and
[r\eta,r)
. Hence the left-hand sign conditions hold on the entire interval
(0,r)
. We now modify the right-hand function

in a similar manner. Choose a smooth cutoff

and set

[r,s-\nu]

(28)

\displaystyle\|p_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\nu$}}-p_{\scalebox{0.4}{$R$}}\|_{\scalebox{0.6}{$C^{0}([r,s])$}}\leq\frac{\sin r}{2(s-r)}\nu^{2}.

We now retune the matching point, treating

, the signs of
\Psi_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\nu$}}
at

. The preceding left-hand estimates are uniform for

below. Pick a “height” value

. Write

Since

Proposition 8
.

There is a smooth Riemannian pair
(g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}},h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}})
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}$}})=0

With

c_{1}=c_{2}=-\frac{\sin^{2}t}{a^{4}}+\frac{a^{\prime}\cos t}{a\sin t}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptc_{3}=-\frac{4}{a^{2}}+\frac{3\sin^{2}t}{a^{4}}+\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\right)^{\!2}\cdot

Set

We now make repeated use of Proposition

, we have that
a_{1}c_{1}=a_{2}c_{2}>0
on
(0,r)\cup(r,s]
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=c_{1}=c_{2}=0
at
t=r
. Also,

c_{3}=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\bigg(\!\!-\!4+3\Big(\frac{\sin t}{a}\Big)^{\!2}+(a^{\prime})^{2}\bigg)<0

on
(0,s]
, hence
a_{3}c_{3}>0

and so
Lemma
2
applies pointwise on
(0,s]
. Near
0
we have that
p(t)=\lambda t+O(t^{3})
, so that

a(t)=a_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}p(u)du=a_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}t^{2}+O(t^{4})

with
a_{0}>0
and
\lambda>0
. Since
\sin t=t+O(t^{3})
, the metric extends smoothly across the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bolt (i.e., the collapsed
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-fiber leaves a smooth
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
fixed surface), and the resulting manifold is
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus B^{4}
. Topologically, the collapsing
\sigma_{3}
-circle is the Hopf fiber of the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-orbits, so the bolt is a copy of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
whose normal disk bundle has Euler number
+1
with the chosen orientation; this disk bundle is
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus B^{4}
. Near
s
we have that
p(t)=\cos t
, hence
a(t)=\sin t
; the metric is therefore exactly round near the boundary. It remains to define
h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}
. On the open set where
a_{2}c_{2}=a_{1}c_{1}>0
, set

x_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}c_{3}}{a_{1}a_{3}}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptx_{1}=1=x_{2}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptx_{3}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sqrt{\frac{a_{1}c_{3}}{c_{1}a_{3}}},

where
a_{1}=a_{2},c_{1}=c_{2}
, and
a_{3}=-1
, and define

h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=x_{0}^{-1}(e^{0})^{2}+(e^{1})^{2}+(e^{2})^{2}+x_{3}^{-1}(e^{3})^{2}.

The equations in Lemma
2
are then satisfied (after relabeling), so that
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}$}})=0
. At
r
, recall that
a_{1}=c_{1}=0
. Since

a_{1}=p^{\prime}/a\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptc_{1}=\frac{\cos t\,(p-G)}{a\sin t},

the quotient
c_{1}/a_{1}
is a positive smooth multiple of
(p-G)/p^{\prime}
,
and therefore extends smoothly and positively across
r
. Hence
x_{0}
and
x_{3}
extend smoothly and positively there. At the bolt
t=0
, we have
a_{1}(0)=c_{1}(0)=\lambda/a_{0}
(the latter via L’Hôpital’s Rule), while
a_{3}(0)=-1
and
c_{3}(0)=-4/a_{0}^{2}
,
so

x_{0}^{-1}(e^{0})^{2}+x_{3}^{-1}(e^{3})^{2}=\frac{a_{0}}{2}(dt^{2}+\sin^{2}t\,\sigma_{3}^{2}).

Thus
h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}
has the same smooth polar behavior as
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}
. On the round boundary collar at
s
,
a_{i}=c_{i}=-1
for all
i=1,2,3
, hence
x_{0}=x_{3}=1
and
h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}=g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}
.
∎

Proposition 9
.

Suppose compact oriented
4
-manifolds with boundary carry Riemannian pairs
(g_{i},h_{i})
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g_{i}$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{i}$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h_{i}$}})=0
, and suppose that
g_{i}=h_{i}=dt^{2}+\sin^{2}t\,g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}}
on matching boundary collars. If the collars are identified by orientation-reversing collar maps which identify the round metrics on the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-slices and match the collar coordinate in the usual way, then the resulting glued manifold carries a smooth Riemannian pair
(g,h)
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
.

Proof.

The orientation-reversing boundary identification is the usual one for producing an oriented glued manifold. The compatibility of the collar identifications means that the
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
have identical pullbacks on the overlap; hence they glue to smooth Riemannian metrics
g
and
h
. Away from the seam, the condition is one of the original pointwise conditions. On the collar itself, the metric is round and
h=g
, so the condition holds there as well: the round metric is Einstein, and therefore its curvature preserves the ordinary Hodge splitting. Since
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
is a local pointwise condition, the glued pair satisfies it globally.
∎

Theorem 2

(A Hitchin–Thorpe-violating example)
.

\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
admits a Riemannian pair
(g,h)
satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
, but no Einstein metric.

Proof.

In the unit round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4}\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}
, choose the five vertices of a regular
4
-simplex centered at the origin. Their pairwise inner products are
-1/4
, so their mutual spherical distances are
\arccos(-1/4)
. Because
s=9/10
, we have that

2s=9/5<\arccos(-1/4)
, so that the five open geodesic balls of radius
s
centered at these points are pairwise disjoint. Remove these balls from
\mathbb{S}^{4}
and glue into the resulting five boundary components five copies of
C\cong\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus B^{4}
from Proposition
8
. The boundary collars are round and agree with

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}=h_{\scalebox{0.4}{$C$}}=dt^{2}+\sin^{2}t\,g_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathbb{S}^{3}$}},

so Proposition
9
gives a smooth Riemannian pair
(g,h)
on the glued manifold satisfying
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(\Lambda^{+}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}},\Lambda^{-}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$h$}})=0
. Topologically, each inserted copy of
C
performs connected sum with
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, so the glued manifold is
\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Therefore, because

\chi(\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2})=7
and

\tau(\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2})=5
,
we have

2\chi=14<15=3|\tau|.

(In particular,
\rho_{\scalebox{0.6}{$0$}}(g,h)\geq\frac{15}{14}
.) Thus
\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
violates the Hitchin–Thorpe inequality, and therefore cannot admit an Einstein metric.
∎

The nonexistence of Einstein metrics on 4-manifolds is a rich subject, with obstructions coming both from Hitchin–Thorpe and Seiberg–Witten theory
[
LeB96
,
IL02
]
. Our construction goes in a different direction: Theorem
2
exhibits a manifold violating Hitchin–Thorpe, hence admitting no Einstein metric, but satisfying the weaker relative condition (
16
).

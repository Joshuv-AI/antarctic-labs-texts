Comparative Evaluation of the Effects of Riboflavin Photodynamic Therapy as an Adjunct to Ultrasonic Scaling on Periodontal Health in Orthodontic Patients: Study Protocol for a Split-Mouth Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Javed Sodawala, Ranjit H. Kamble, Fatema Sodawala, Akash Swarnkar, Tushar Chandrakar, Amit A. Akolkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Currently used synthetic photosensitizers like methylene blue have been associated with many adverse effects. In-vitro study has demonstrated Riboflavin can be used as a natural photosensitizer agent for photodynamic therapy (PDT) in the field of orthodontics. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the effect of riboflavin PDT on periodontal health of orthodontic patients.Material and Method:A prospective split-mouth RCT will be designed and 60 patients undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment will be selected as per eligibility criteria and the dental arches (n = 120) of the patients will be randomly divided into two groups: in experimental group, one of the participant’s dental arches will undergo ultrasonic scaling followed by riboflavin PDT and in control group, participant’s other dental arch will undergo ultrasonic scaling only. Ultrasonic scaling and riboflavin PDT will be performed at baseline (T0), and subsequently at monthly intervals—T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5 up to 6 months. The Plaque index, Gingival index, bleeding on probing, and pocket depth will be measured at T0, T1, T3, and T6 before receiving antimicrobial therapy and compared in both groups. Intragroup comparisons will be carried out using repeated measures ANOVA test or Friedman test and intergroup comparisons will be carried out using independent t test or Mann-Whitney U test.Expected Outcomes:Guidelines for performing riboflavin PDT in patients undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment will be developed. The effectiveness of riboflavin PDT as an adjunct to ultrasonic scaling will be assessed by comparing periodontal health measures in orthodontic patients.

INTRODUCTION

Microorganisms form biofilms on teeth, oral mucosa, and dental materials, disrupting host-microbe balance and leading to gingivitis, bone loss, and tooth loss.[12] Orthodontic treatment increases the risk of caries, endodontic infections, periodontal issues, candidiasis, and peri-implantitis.[3]

Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (AmPDT) has been applied to root canal infections, periodontal diseases, and oral biofilms.[4] However, common photosensitizers like methylene blue and curcumin may cause side effects such as skin sensitivity, gingival irritation, taste alteration, nausea, vomiting, and increased sweating.

Fixed orthodontic appliances hinder oral hygiene, often requiring frequent prophylaxis and adjunctive aids. Non-compliance can lead to chronic gingivitis and periodontal breakdown. PDT may offer a supportive solution during treatment.

An in-vitro study by Kamran et al.[5] showed riboflavin’s potential as a photosensitizer for AmPDT in orthodontics, though no clinical studies exist. To address this gap, a clinical trial will assess the adjunctive effect of riboflavin PDT with ultrasonic scaling on Gingival index (GI), Plaque index (PI), pocket depth (PD), and bleeding on probing (BOP).

MATERIAL AND METHOD

Study design

This will be a single-centered, single-blind, parallel arm, prospective split-mouth randomized controlled superiority trial with 1:1 allocation ratio. The ethical clearance has been obtained from Institutional Ethics Committee of DMIHER (Ref no. DHIMER (DU)/IEC/2025/41)

Study setting

Patients undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment will be selected as per eligibility criteria from the Department of Orthodontics Sharad Pawar Dental College, Sawangi (Meghe), Maharashtra, India. The patients and parents/legal guardians of the adolescent patients will be requested to read and sign the informed consent.

Eligibility criteria

Patients aged 12–30 years undergoing fixed orthodontic treatment from at least 6 months, with GI ≥1.1 and PI ≥1 and, without oral lesions will be included in the study.

Patients with adverse oral habits, systemic conditions or drugs affecting gingiva, history of periodontal treatment; antibiotic or anti-inflammatory therapy received in the last 3 months, and pregnant or lactating women will be excluded from the study.

Sample size

The sample size (n) formulae used was n= {(σ12+ σ22) (z1-α/2 + z1-β)2}/D2. Substituting the values from the previous pilot study, standard deviation of Group 1 (σ1) and 2 (σ2) was 0.27 and 0.28, mean difference (D) of -0.16, confidence level of 95% (z1-α/2 = 1.96) and 80% power (z1-β = 0.84). The initial sample size was 47 per group; accounting for a 20% attrition rate over the follow-up period, it was adjusted to 60 participants per group.

Distribution of sample

A split mouth study design will be used and the dental arches (n = 120) of the study participants will be randomly divided into two equal groups based on the type of antimicrobial therapy. In experimental group, participant’s one dental arch will undergo ultrasonic scaling followed by riboflavin PDT and in control group, participant’s another dental arch will undergo ultrasonic scaling only.

Assignment of intervention

A computer-generated randomization will be used to assign participant’s dental arch to the experimental or control group. The task of generating the random allocation sequence and the task of enrolling participants will be assigned to different individuals. On-site computer system will be used for allocation concealment. The outcome assessor will be blinded after intervention assignment.

Interventions

All participants recruited for the study will be given prior training in oral hygiene techniques and will be advised same toothbrush and toothpaste to use during study period. Ultrasonic scaling will be performed in both the experimental and control group with the help of an ultrasonic scaler by a trained professional at a monthly interval for 6 consecutive months.

Riboflavin PDT irradiation will be performed in the experimental group at a monthly interval for 6 consecutive months. To prepare 0.5% riboflavin solution, approximately 500 mg of riboflavin powder will be mixed in 100 mL sterile physiological solution (0.9% saline) at 37◦C, filtered and stored in amber color bottle.

Riboflavin 0.5% concentration solution will be used for sensitization of bacteria; this photosensitizer will be delivered into the gingival margin at the bottom to achieve complete filling of the pocket with the help of 31-gauge needle. After 5 minutes residence time, the pocket will be rinsed with saline to wash of the excess photosensitizer, as this could act as an optical shield. Employing the light emitting device (LED), the remaining photosensitizer will be activated for 30 seconds per site with specifications: 420–480 nm wavelength, 1200–2000 mW/cm2 power density. The application time of photosensitizer and curing light both will be managed by a time controller.

Participant timeline

Ultrasonic scaling will be performed in both the experimental and control group at baseline (T0) and subsequently at monthly intervals - T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5 up to 6 months. Riboflavin PDT irradiation will be performed in the experimental group at T0 and subsequently at T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5 after ultrasonic scaling. The assessment of four clinical parameters to assess the periodontal health will be carried out at T0 and subsequently at T1, T3, and T6 before receiving antimicrobial therapy.

Primary and secondary outcomes

The periodontal health will be evaluated in both the groups and compared. Index teeth evaluated will be permanent central incisor, canine and first molar from each quadrant of the dental arch. PI will be assessed as described by Silness and Loe. GI will be assessed as described by Loe and Silness. BoP will be assessed as described in the modified sulcular bleeding index (mSBI) by Mombelli et al.[6] and Pocket Depth will be measured using a UNC-15 probe. The average value of both the quadrant of dental arch being evaluated will be considered for analysis in each group. Pain and discomfort related to both the interventions will be assessed using a 10-point VAS scale, any adverse effects or complications related to the interventions will be documented and cost-effectiveness of both the interventions will be analyzed.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis will be performed using SPSS. Data normality will be checked by using Shapiro – Wilk test. Intragroup comparisons at various time intervals will be carried out using repeated measures ANOVA or Friedman test. Intergroup comparisons at various time intervals will be carried out using independent t test or Mann-Whitney U test. The level of significance will be set at 0.05.

DISCUSSION

Fixed orthodontic appliances make oral hygiene challenging due to brackets and wires that trap food and plaque, often leading to gingival inflammation. Discomfort from tooth movement further complicates hygiene efforts. Although AmPDT is effective, its use is limited by the need for costly LASER equipment. Riboflavin-based PDT, however, can be performed with LED curing units commonly found in orthodontic clinics, offering a more accessible alternative.

This study proposes guidelines for using riboflavin PDT in fixed orthodontic patients and evaluates its impact on periodontal health using GI, PI, BoP, and PD indices. By aligning PDT sessions with routine orthodontic visits, the study aims to improve patient compliance and demonstrate riboflavin PDT as a cost-effective, practical method to enhance periodontal outcomes and overall treatment success.

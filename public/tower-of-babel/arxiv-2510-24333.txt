The violation of the combined charge-conjugation (
C
) and parity (
P
) symmetries, known as
CP
violation,
is a key ingredient in explaining the observed matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe through baryogenesis
Sakharov 1967
.
Despite accommodating
CP
violation via the Kobayashi-Maskawa phase
Cabibbo 1963
;
Kobayashi and Maskawa 1973
,the Standard Model (SM) fails to explain the baryon asymmetry of the universe
Barr et al. 1979
.
Its predicted
CP
violation is at least ten orders of magnitude too small.
Experimental evidence for
CP
violation has been found in
K

Christenson et al. 1964
,
B

Aubert et al. 2001
;
Abe et al. 2001
and
D

Aaij et al. 2019
meson systems
and the
\Lambda_{b}^{0}
baryon system
Aaij et al. 2025
, all these measurements are consistent with the SM predictions.
Therefore, to resolve the baryogenesis puzzle, additional sources of
CP
violation beyond the SM must be considered.
Hyperon decays, in particular, provide a sensitive probe for such effects
Salone et al. 2022
;
Donoghue et al. 1986
.

In the nonleptonic decays of spin
1/2
hyperons,
CP
violation arises from the interference between parity-conserving (
P
-wave) and parity-violating (
S
-wave) amplitudes with differing
CP
-odd weak phases
Lee and Yang 1957
.
In these decays, the angular distribution of the daughter baryon is given by
1+\alpha_{Y}\bm{P_{Y}}\cdot\bm{\hat{p}_{d}}
, where
\alpha_{Y}
is the hyperon decay parameter,

\bm{P_{Y}}
is the hyperon polarization vector, and
\bm{\hat{p}_{d}}
is the unit vector along the daughter baryon momentum in the hyperon rest frame.
A
CP
-odd asymmetry parameter is defined as
A_{CP}=(\alpha_{Y}+\alpha_{\bar{Y}})/(\alpha_{Y}-\alpha_{\bar{Y}})
,
where
\alpha_{Y}
and
\alpha_{\bar{Y}}
denote the decay parameters of the hyperon and anti-hyperon, respectively. A nonzero
A_{CP}
indicates
CP
violation.

Within the SM, phenomenological studies based on different scenarios
Donoghue et al. 1986
;
Tandean and Valencia 2003
;
Salone et al. 2022
predict an extremely small
CP
asymmetry
in the
\Lambda
nonleptonic two-body weak decay,
|A_{CP}^{\Lambda,\rm SM}|\leq 3\times 10^{-5}
.
In contrast, several beyond SM theories predict significantly larger values, e.g.
|A_{CP}^{\Lambda,\rm MSM}|\leq 10^{-4}
with minimal extensions of the SM (MSM)
He and Velencia 1995
,

|A_{CP}^{\Lambda,\rm CMPC}|\leq 7\times 10^{-4}
with chromomagnetic-penguin contributions (CMPC)
He et al. 2022
,

|A_{CP}^{\Lambda,\rm SUSY}|\leq 10^{-3}
with supersymmetric models (SUSY)
He et al. 2000
, and

|A_{CP}^{\Lambda,\rm DM}|\leq 1.6\times 10^{-3}
with dark matter models (DM)
He et al. 2025
.
The main limitation in the precision of the theoretical predictions arises from the well known
S/P
-wave puzzle in hyperon nonleptonic decays,
where the
S
- and
P
-wave amplitudes cannot be simultaneously described within leading-order chiral perturbation theory
Jenkins 1992
;
Shi et al. 2022
.
Improved precision in the decay parameters can help resolve this puzzle.
Such measurements can also sharpen the status of the
\Delta I=1/2
rule in the spin 1/2 hyperon sector
Overseth and Pakvasa 1969
;
Olsen et al. 1970
,
in comparison to kaon decays
K\to\pi\pi

Alavi-Harati et al. 1999
;
Fanti et al. 1999

where the
\Delta I=3/2
transitions are strongly suppressed relative to the
\Delta I=1/2
ones
Amhis et al. 2023
;
Manzari et al. 2021
.
With increasing experimental precision,
\Delta I=3/2
contributions can no longer be neglected
Ablikim et al. 2024a
and must be included in theoretical calculations of
A_{CP}
.

Recently, BESIII has performed a series of precision studies of nonleptonic two-body hyperon decays
Ablikim et al. 2019
;
Ablikim et al. 2022a
;
Ablikim et al. 2022b
;
Ablikim et al. 2024a
;
Ablikim et al. 2023a
;
Ablikim et al. 2023b
;
Ablikim et al. 2024b
;
Ablikim et al. 2023c
.
The decay parameters of the charged
\Lambda
decay mode
\Lambda\to p\pi^{-}
(
\alpha_{-}
) and
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{p}\pi^{+}
(
\alpha_{+}
)
were measured with the highest precision in the decay sequence
J/\psi\to\Lambda(\to p\pi^{-})\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})

Ablikim et al. 2022a
.
For the neutral decay modes of
\Lambda
,
\alpha_{0}
for
\Lambda\to n\pi^{0}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{0}
for
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{n}\pi^{0}

were measured in the decay sequence
J/\psi\to\Xi^{-}\bar{\Xi}^{+}\to\Lambda(\to n\pi^{0})\pi^{-}\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})\pi^{+}
and its charge conjugated channel
Ablikim et al. 2024a
.

\bar{\alpha}_{0}
was also measured in
J/\psi\to\Lambda(\to p\pi^{-})\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{n}\pi^{0})
based on 1.3 billion
J/\psi
events
Ablikim et al. 2019
,
but its precision is poor due to limited statistics.
However, the uncertainties in
\alpha_{0}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{0}
remain substantially larger than those for the charged modes, motivating further improvement.
The decay
J/\psi\to\Lambda(\to n\pi^{0})\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})
and its charge conjugated channel offer larger signal yields compared to the previous mode
J/\psi\rightarrow\Xi^{-}\bar{\Xi}^{+}
.
Although the reduced quantum entanglement in this channel partly offsets the statistical advantage, it still provides a pathway toward more precise measurements of
\alpha_{0}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{0}
.

In this Letter, we present a full angular analysis of the decay
J/\psi\rightarrow\Lambda(\to n\pi^{0})\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})
and its charge conjugate,
based on
(10087\pm 44)\times 10^{6}

J/\psi
events
Ablikim et al. 2022c
collected with the BESIII detector.
The transversely polarization of
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
together with their spin correlation,
allow the angular distributions of both production and decay processes to be described by an extended helicity formalism
Perotti et al. 2019
.
Unless otherwise indicated, the
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{n}\pi^{0}
mode is implicitly included throughout this text.

The BESIII experiment provides a unique platform to study hyperon production and decay properties in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to a
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pair via the intermediate
J/\psi
resonance.
The decay chain
J/\psi\rightarrow\Lambda(\to n\pi^{0})\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})

is characterized by five helicity angles
\xi=(\theta_{\Lambda},\theta_{n},\phi_{n},\theta_{\bar{p}},\phi_{\bar{p}})

and the four parameters
\omega=(\alpha_{J/\psi},\Delta\Phi,\alpha_{0},\alpha_{+})
,
where
\theta_{\Lambda}
is the polar angle of
\Lambda
in the center-of-mass (c.m.) system of
J/\psi
, and (
\theta_{n},\phi_{n}
), (
\theta_{\bar{p}},\phi_{\bar{p}}
) are the polar and
azimuthal angles of the neutron and anti-proton in their respective rest frames, defined in a right-handed system
Perotti et al. 2019
.
The parameters
\alpha_{J/\psi}
and
\Delta\Phi
describe the production of
J/\psi\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
,
and
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{+}
are the decay parameters of
\Lambda\to n\pi^{0}
and
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{p}\pi^{+}
, respectively.
The joint angular distribution of the decay is given by
Perotti et al. 2019
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}(\xi;\omega)&=\mathcal{F}_{0}(\xi)+\alpha_{J/\psi}\mathcal{F}_{5}(\xi)+\alpha_{0}\alpha_{+}[\mathcal{F}_{1}(\xi)\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{J/\psi}^{2}}\cos(\Delta\Phi)\mathcal{F}_{2}(\xi)-\alpha_{J/\psi}\mathcal{F}_{6}(\xi)]\\
&+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{J/\psi}^{2}}\sin(\Delta\Phi)[-\alpha_{0}\mathcal{F}_{3}+\alpha_{+}\mathcal{F}_{4}],\end{split}

(1)

where the angular functions
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\xi)

(i=0,1,...,6)
are described in Ref.
Fäldt and Kupsc 2017
.
In Eq. (
1
), the
\alpha_{0}\alpha_{+}
terms arise from
\Lambda-\bar{\Lambda}
spin correlations,
while the
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{+}
terms reflect the transverse polarization of
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
, respectively.

Details of the
BESIII
detector are given in Refs.
Ablikim et al. 2010
;
Huang et al. 2022
.
The corresponding simulation, analysis framework, and software are presented in Refs.
Ping 2008
;
Deng et al. 2006
;
Sun et al. 2007
.
The Monte Carlo (MC) samples are generated with
Geant
4-based
Agostinelli et al. 2003
software, which models the electron-positron collisions, particle decays, and detector response.
A generic
J/\psi
MC sample, with statistics matching the data, is used to study the potential background contribution.
To eliminate experimental bias, The analysis is performed blindly with central values hidden
Klein and Roodman 2005
until the procedure is finalized.
Signal MC samples are used to validate the analysis approaches and to study the systematic effects.

Two oppositely charged tracks are detected in the main drift chamber (MDC).
The
\bar{p}
and
\pi^{+}
candidates are primarily selected as in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2022d
.
A discrepancy between data and MC simulation is observed in the
\bar{\Lambda}
polar angle distribution, mainly due to the trigger efficiency in the endcap region.
To minimize this effect, we require the absolute value of the cosine of the
\bar{p}
polar angle must be less than 0.8.
The
\bar{\Lambda}
candidate is reconstructed from the
\bar{p}
and
\pi^{+}
tracks via a vertex fit
Xu et al. 2010
,
and its invariant mass must lie within five times its resolution, where the mass resolution is determined in bins of the
\bar{\Lambda}
polar angle.
The decay length of the reconstructed
\bar{\Lambda}
is required to be positive.
To suppress backgrounds from
J/\psi\rightarrow\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Sigma}^{0}
,
\Sigma^{0}\bar{\Lambda}
,
\gamma\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
and
\gamma\eta_{c}
,
which include a
\bar{\Lambda}
in their final state, we furthermore require
\lvert M_{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm rec}-m_{\Lambda}\rvert<3\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm rec}
.
Here,
M_{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm rec}=\sqrt{(E_{\rm cm}-E_{\bar{\Lambda}})^{2}/c^{4}-P_{\bar{\Lambda}}^{2}/c^{2}}
is the recoiling mass of the
\bar{\Lambda}
, obtained from the c.m. energy
E_{\rm cm}
,
as well as the energy
E_{\bar{\Lambda}}
and momentum
P_{\bar{\Lambda}}
of the
\bar{\Lambda}
candidate from the vertex fit.
The resolution
\sigma_{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm rec}
is determined in bins of the
\bar{\Lambda}
polar angle.

At least two photon candidates are required in the electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC).
Neutral showers in the EMC are primarily selected as in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2024a
.
To veto the showers from neutron (anti-neutron) interaction in the EMC, since the angle between neutron (anti-neutron) and
\Lambda(\bar{\Lambda})
is very small (less than
7^{\circ}
),
the photon candidates should be separated from the
\Lambda
(
\bar{\Lambda}
) momentum direction
by more than
10^{\circ}
(
15^{\circ}
), where the
\Lambda
(
\bar{\Lambda}
) momentum direction is opposite to that of
\bar{\Lambda}
(
\Lambda
).
To further suppress the neutron-induced shower, a boosted decision tree (BDT) classifier
Therhaag 2011
is constructed using shower shape variables including deposited energy,
number of hits, second moment, Zernike moments, and deposition shape
Ablikim et al. 2024a
.
Photons matched to truth information in signal MC are treated as signal, while others are treated as background.
A requirement on the BDT output
>0.15
(
0.10
) is applied to ensure
90\%
signal efficiency, with a corresponding
55\%
(
45\%
) background rejection efficiency.
The
\pi^{0}
candidates are reconstructed with a photon pair by performing a kinematic fit constraining their invariant mass to the
\pi^{0}
nominal mass.
In case of multiple
\pi^{0}
candidates from combinatorial effects, the one with the smallest kinematic fit
\chi^{2}
is retained, requiring
\chi^{2}<100
.

Because of the limited detection efficiency for neutrons and antineutrons, they are not reconstructed directly.
Instead, their four-momenta are inferred as
\bm{P}_{n}=\bm{P}_{\Lambda}-\bm{P}_{\pi^{0}}
,
where
\bm{P}_{n}
,
\bm{P}_{\Lambda}
and
\bm{P}_{\pi^{0}}
are the four-momenta of neutron,
\Lambda
and
\pi^{0}
candidate, respectively.
To improve the resolution, the momentum and energy of
\Lambda
are set to
\vec{p}_{\Lambda}=-\hat{p}_{\bar{\Lambda}}\sqrt{E^{2}_{\rm cm}/c^{2}/4-m_{\Lambda}^{2}c^{2}}
and
E_{\Lambda}=E_{\rm cm}/2
, respectively,
where
\hat{p}_{\bar{\Lambda}}
is the momentum direction of the
\bar{\Lambda}
in the
e^{+}e^{-}
rest frame.
The neutron is identified by the missing mass calculated from
\bm{P}_{n}
, which shows a clear peak near the nominal neutron mass with low background (Fig.
1
).
Studies of MC samples and data in the neutron sideband show that background in the signal region mainly arises from signal events with misreconstructed
\pi^{0}
candidates, i.e., combinatorial background.
The combinatorial background does not peak in the neutron invariant mass distribution.
The other backgrounds, stemming from processes with one or two extra photons in the final state
Zhou et al. 2021
, contribute less than
0.5\%
and are neglected in the nominal analysis.

To extract the production and decay parameters, a simultaneous fit on the joint angular distribution is performed on the selected events in the neutron/anti-neutron missing-mass signal range
[0.926,0.957]
GeV/
c^{2}
,
with the production parameters
\alpha_{J/\psi}
and
\Delta\Phi
shared between the two charge conjugated channels.
In the fit, the probability density function for the
i
-th event with helicity angle
\xi_{i}
is parameterized with the four parameters
\omega
,

\mathcal{P}(\xi_{i};\omega)=\mathcal{W}(\xi_{i};\omega)\varepsilon(\xi_{i})/\mathcal{N}(\omega),

(2)

where the normalization factor
\mathcal{N}(\omega)
is obtained by
\mathcal{N}(\omega)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\mathcal{W}(\xi_{j};\omega)}{\mathcal{W}(\xi_{j};\omega_{\rm gen})}

with a signal MC sample generated with known parameters
\omega_{\rm gen}

Ablikim et al. 2022a
;
Ablikim et al. 2024a
and size
M
, similar to that in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2024a
.
The sum runs over all
M
signal MC events, corresponding to 50 times the data yield.
The log-likelihood function for
N
selected candidates is

\small\mathcal{S}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ln\mathcal{P}(\xi_{i};\omega)+\frac{N_{\rm bkg}}{N_{\rm miss}}\sum_{i}^{N_{\rm miss}}\ln\mathcal{P}(\xi_{i};\omega),

(3)

where the second term accounts for the contribution from the combinatorial background, estimated using
N_{\rm miss}
MC-simulated background events.
To determine the background yield
N_{\rm bkg}
, unbinned maximum likelihood fits are carried out on the distributions of the neutron missing mass, as shown in Fig.
1
.
In the fit, the signal is modeled with an MC-simulated shape convolved with a Gaussian function to account for the resolution difference between data and MC simulation,
while the combinatorial background is modeled by the shape from the corresponding MC sample.
The fit yields
872450\pm 982
signal events with a purity of
95.3\%
for the decay
\Lambda\to n\pi^{0}
,
and
759770\pm 963
events with a purity of
91.1\%
for the decay
\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{n}\pi^{0}
.

The sources of systematic uncertainties are separated into four categories: event reconstruction, background estimation, fit procedure and residual Data/MC discrepancy.
For event reconstruction, the effects from
p
,
\bar{p}
, and
\pi^{\pm}
tracking, PID, and
\Lambda/\bar{\Lambda}
reconstruction are evaluated with a control sample of

The
\pi^{0}
reconstruction efficiency is studied with
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}(\to p\pi^{0})\bar{\Sigma}^{-}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{0})

\bar{p}
, the missing-mass window, and the
\chi^{2}
of the
\pi^{0}

Background-related uncertainties are evaluated in two ways: a
\pm 1\sigma

The uncertainty of fit procedure is tested with 50 sets of toy MC samples generated using the best-fit parameters.
Since the input and output are consistent within statistical fluctuations, no additional uncertainty is assigned to the fit procedure.
The fixed input of
\alpha_{J/\psi}
is studied by repeating the fit with
\alpha_{J/\psi}
randomly varied within one standard deviation for 100 iterations. The standard deviation of the output is assigned as the corresponding uncertainty.
Finally, the residual data/MC discrepancy is evaluated by reweighting the MC to match the
\bar{\Lambda}

Two-ends Furstenberg estimates in the plane
Hong Wang, Shukun Wu

Abstract
. We prove two-ends Furstenberg estimates in the plane for a Katz-Tao ( δ , t ) (\delta,t) -set of lines, for general t ∈ [ 0 , 2 ] t\in[0,2] .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we extend the two-ends Furstenberg inequality established in
[
WW24
]
to a broader class of lines.
We begin by recalling a standard definition.

Definition 1.1

(Shading)
.

Let
L
be a family of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
A
shading

Y:L\to B^{2}(0,1)
is an assignment such that
Y(\ell)\subset N_{\delta}(\ell)\cap B^{2}(0,1)
is a union of
\delta
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for all
\ell\in L
.
We writes
(L,Y)_{\delta}
to emphasize the dependence on
\delta
.
A similar definition applies to a family of
\delta
-tubes
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Regarding the distribution of a shading
Y(\ell)
, the next definition gives a quantitative “two-ends” condition, which is a fairly weak non-concentration condition.

Definition 1.2

(Two-ends)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and let
(L,Y)_{\delta}
be a set of lines and shading.
Let
0<\varepsilon_{2}<\varepsilon_{1}<1
.
We say
Y
is
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},C)
-two-ends
if for all
\ell\in L
and all
\delta\times\delta^{\varepsilon_{1}}
-tubes
J\subset N_{\delta}(\ell)
,

(1.1)

|Y(\ell)\cap J|\leq C\delta^{\varepsilon_{2}}|Y(\ell)|.

When the constant
C
is not important in the context, we say
Y
is
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
-two-ends
, or simply
two-ends
.
A similar definition applies to a single shading
Y(\ell)
.

Now let us recall
[
WW24
, Theorem 2.1]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Let
(L,Y)_{\delta}
be a set of directional
\delta
-separated lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with an
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
-two-ends shading such that
|Y(\ell)|\geq\lambda\delta
for all
\ell\in L
.
Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.2)

\Big|\bigcup_{\ell\in L}Y(\ell)\Big|\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon_{1}/2}\lambda^{1/2}\sum_{\ell\in L}|Y(\ell)|.

As discussed in
[
WW24
, Section 0.3]
, (
1.2
) matches the numerology of the Furstenberg set inequality established in
[
RW23
, Theorem 4.1]
(see Theorem
3.5
).
The assumption of
L
being a set of directional
\delta
-separated lines can be easily replaced by the superficially stronger assumption that
L
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,1)
-set of lines.
The main result of this paper is a generalization of Theorem
1.3
to a set of lines and two-ends shading
(L,Y)_{\delta}
, where
L
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set of lines for general
t\in[0,2]
(see Definition
2.5
for the definition of a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set of lines).
We begin with a necessary definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
, and let
(L,Y)_{\delta}
be a set of lines and shading.
Let
t\in[0,1]
.
For each
\ell\in L
, define

(1.3)

\gamma_{Y,t}(\ell):=\sup_{r\in[\delta,1],\,x\in\ell}(\delta/r)^{t}(\delta^{-2}|B(x,r)\cap Y(\ell)|),

and define
\gamma_{Y,t}:=\sup_{\ell\in L}\gamma_{Y}(\ell)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(L,Y)_{\delta}
be a set of
\delta
-separated lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
-two-ends shading such that
|Y(\ell)|\geq\lambda\delta
for all
\ell\in L
.
Let
t\in(0,2)
, and
t^{\ast}=\min\{t,2-t\}
.
Suppose the set of lines
L
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set.
Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.4)

\Big|\bigcup_{\ell\in L}Y(\ell)\Big|\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon}\delta^{t\varepsilon_{1}/2}\lambda^{1/2}\delta^{(t-1)/2}\gamma_{Y,t^{\ast}}^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell\in L}|Y(\ell)|.

Compared to (
1.2
), an additional factor of
\gamma_{Y,t^{\ast}}^{-1/2}
appearing in (
1.4
), which always equals to
1
when
t=1
.
When
t\not=1
,
\gamma_{Y,t^{\ast}}^{-1/2}
is generally smaller than
1
, and, unfortunately, necessary.
See Remark
3.3
for some examples.

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(L,Y)_{\delta}
be a set of
\delta
-separated lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
-two-ends shading.
Let
t\in(0,2)
.
Suppose that
L
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set.
Suppose also that for each
\ell\in L
,
|Y(\ell)|\geq\lambda\delta
, and
Y(\ell)
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,\min\{t,2-t\})
-set.
Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.5)

\Big|\bigcup_{\ell\in L}Y(\ell)\Big|\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta^{\varepsilon}\delta^{t\varepsilon_{1}/2}\lambda^{1/2}\delta^{(t-1)/2}\sum_{\ell\in L}|Y(\ell)|.

Notations:
Throughout the paper, we use
\#E
to denote the cardinality of a finite set.
If
\mathcal{E}
is a family of sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use
\cup_{\mathcal{E}}
to denote
\cup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E
.
For

, we use

to mean

for an absolute (big) constant

, and use
A\sim B
to mean

and

.
For a given

to denote

for all

.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall some basic definitions and tools in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

For two finite sets
E,F
, we say
E
is a
\gtrsim c
-refinement of
F
, if
E\subset F
and
\#E\gtrsim c\#F
; we say

is a
\gtrapprox c
-refinement of

, or simply a
refinement
of

E\subset F
and
\#E\gtrapprox c\#F
.
A similar definition applies when
E,F
are two finite unions of
\delta
-balls.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
Let
E
be a finite union of
\delta
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For another
\rho\geq\delta
, let

(2.1)

|E|_{\rho}=\min\{\#\mathcal{D}_{\rho}:\mathcal{D}_{\rho}\text{ is a covering of $E$ by $\rho$-balls}\}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\rho}(E)
be a family of
\rho
-balls that attains the minimum in (
2.1
).
Denote by

(2.2)

(E)_{\rho}=\cup_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}(E)}.

The same definitions apply to
\mathcal{P}
when it is a finite set of disjoint
\delta
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by considering
\cup_{\mathcal{P}}
.

To capture the distribution of a fractal set, we need the following two notations.

Definition 2.3

(
(\delta,s,C)
-set)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
For
s\in(0,n]
, a non-empty set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set
(or simply a
(\delta,s)
-set
if
C
is not important in the context) if

(2.3)

|E\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Cr^{s}|E|_{\delta},\hskip 8.5359pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,r\in[\delta,1].

Definition 2.4

(Katz-Tao
(\delta,s,C)
-set)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
For
s\in(0,n]
, a finite set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
Katz-Tao
(\delta,s,C)
-set
(or simply a
Katz-Tao
(\delta,s)
-set
if
C
is not important in the context) if

(2.4)

\#(E\cap B(x,r))\leq C(r/\delta)^{s},\hskip 8.5359pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,r\in[\delta,1].

By a standard point-line duality (see, e.g.,
[
WW24
, Definition 1.13]
), we have

Definition 2.5
.

A set of lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set (respectively, a
(\delta,t)
-set) if its dual point set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,t)
-set (respectively, a
(\delta,t)
-set).

The next two lemmas are standard.
See
[
WW24
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and let
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1
.
If a union of
\delta
-balls

is a
(\delta,s,C_{1})
-set and

\geq C_{2}^{-1}

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

.
Suppose

is a Katz-Tao
(\delta,s,C)
-set, where

.
Then there exists

\rho^{s}\#E^{\prime}\gtrapprox\delta^{s}\#E
, and

The following is a generalization of Definition
2.3
, quantifying a similar non-concentration condition, though at coarser scales.

Definition 2.8

(
(\delta,s,C;\Delta)
-set)
.

Let

.
We say a union of
\delta

When

Also, at the coarser resolution

.

Let

is a

Next, we recall the process of uniformization.
Compared to that of an arbitrary set, the distribution of a uniform set is easier to describe.
As we will see in Definition
2.12
, this distribution can be captured precisely by a Lipschitz function known as the branching function of the uniform set.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
Let
M=|\log\delta|
and let
\rho_{j}=M^{-j}
,
j=1,\dots,\lceil\log_{M}\delta^{-1}\rceil
.
Given
E
a union of
\delta
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say
E
is
uniform with error
C
if
|E\cap D_{\rho_{j}}|
are the same up to a multiple of
C
for all
D_{\rho_{j}}\subset(E)_{\rho_{j}}
.
When
C
is not important in the context, we say
E
is
uniform
.
The same definitions apply to
\mathcal{P}
when it is a finite set of disjoint
\delta
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by considering
\cup_{\mathcal{P}}
.

The following lemma shows that for an arbitrary set, we can always find a refinement that is also uniform.

Lemma 2.11

(Uniformization)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a union of dyadic
\delta
-balls, then there is a
\gtrsim(\log|\log\delta|)^{-\frac{|\log\delta|}{\log|\log\delta|}}
-refinement
E^{\prime}
of
E
such that
E^{\prime}
is uniform.

Definition 2.12

(Branching function)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and let
M=|\log\delta|
.
Let
\rho_{j}=M^{-j},\,j=1,\dots,\lceil\log_{M}\delta^{-1}\rceil=:N
.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a union of
\delta
-balls that is uniform.
Define a
branching function

\beta_{E}:[0,N]\to[0,n]
as

(2.6)

\beta_{E}(j)=\frac{\log(|E|_{\rho_{j}})}{|\log\delta|},\hskip 8.5359pt0\leq j\leq N

and interpolate linearly between
\beta_{E}(j)
and
\beta_{E}(j+1)
.

Given a family of uniform sets, the next lemma shows that this family has a refinement whose sets have branching functions that are essentially the same.
For its proof, we refer to
[
WW24
]
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
M=|\log\delta|
, and
N=\lceil\log_{M}\delta^{-1}\rceil
.
Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a finite family of uniform sets of
\delta
-balls in the unit ball.
Then there is subset
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
with
\#\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\gtrsim(n\log M)^{-N}\#\mathcal{E}

and a
uniform branching function
\beta_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
such that for any
E\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
,
|\beta_{E}-\beta_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}|\leq|\log\delta|^{-1}
.
In particular,
\#\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\gtrapprox\#\mathcal{E}
.

After rescaling, the branching function of a uniform set becomes a 1-Lipschitz function.
To study such functions, Shmerkin
[
Shm23
]
introduced a powerful tool known as multi-scale decomposition, which has since been developed in various forms.
The version we need is the following, whose proof can be found in
[
DW24
]
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\eta>0
be a small number and let
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\eta)=\eta^{2\eta^{-1}}
.
Then for a non-decreasing 1-Lipschitz function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, there exists a partition

(2.7)

0=A_{1}<A_{2}<\cdots<A_{H+1}=1

and a sequence

(2.8)

0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{H}\leq 1

such that for each
1\leq h\leq H
, we have the following:

(2.9)

A_{h+1}-A_{h}\geq\eta_{0}\eta^{-1};

(2.10)

\displaystyle f(x)\geq

\displaystyle f(A_{h})+s_{l}(x-A_{h})-\eta(A_{h+1}-A_{h}),\text{ for all }x\in[A_{h},A_{h+1}],

(2.11)

\displaystyle f(A_{h+1})\leq f(A_{h})+(s_{h}+3\eta)(A_{h+1}-A_{h});

(2.12)

s_{H}\geq f(1)-f(0)-\eta.

(2.20)

(2.22)

(2.23)

\displaystyle f(x)\geq

\displaystyle f(A_{h})+s_{h}(x-A_{h})-\eta(A_{h+1}-A_{h}),\text{ for all }x\in[A_{h},A_{h+1}],

(2.24)

\displaystyle f(A_{h+1})\leq f(A_{h})+(s_{h}+3\eta)(A_{h+1}-A_{h}).

We consider two distinct cases.

Suppose

Take

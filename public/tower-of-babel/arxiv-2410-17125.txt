Construction of Irreducible 𝓤 ​ ( 𝖌 ) 𝑮 ′ \boldsymbol{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}} -Modules and Discretely Decomposable Restrictions
Masatoshi KITAGAWA

Abstract
In this paper, we study the irreducibility of 𝒰 ​ ( 𝔤 ) G ′ \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}} -modules on the spaces of intertwining operators in the branching problem of reductive Lie algebras, and construct a family of finite-dimensional irreducible 𝒰 ​ ( 𝔤 ) G ′ \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}} -modules using the Zuckerman derived functors. We provide criteria for the irreducibility of 𝒰 ​ ( 𝔤 ) G ′ \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}} -modules in the cases of generalized Verma modules, cohomologically induced modules, and discrete series representations. We treat only discrete decomposable restrictions with certain dominance conditions (quasi-abelian and in the good range). To describe the 𝒰 ​ ( 𝔤 ) G ′ \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}} -modules, we give branching laws of cohomologically induced modules using ones of generalized Verma modules when K ′ K^{\prime} acts on K / L K K/L_{K} transitively.

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to study an algebraic structure on the spaces of intertwining operators in the branching problem of reductive Lie algebras.
We consider the irreducibility of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-modules on the spaces, and construct a family of finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-modules using the Zuckerman derived functors.
This work is based on a part of the author’s Ph.D. thesis.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
and
G^{\prime}
a connected reductive subgroup of
G
.
For an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
and an irreducible
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-submodule
V^{\prime}
of
V|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}
, the algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
of
G^{\prime}
-invariants in the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
acts on
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(V^{\prime},V|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigr)
.
It is a classical result that if
V|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}
is completely reducible and locally finite, then the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-module is irreducible (see, e.g.,
[
6
, §4.2]
).
This is corresponding to the case of the branching problem of real Lie groups and compact subgroups.
In this paper, we essentially treat the case of non-compact subgroups.

In general, the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-module may not be irreducible.
Some branching laws in the context of the theta lifting give typical examples (see Example
5.22
).
On the other hand, we have shown in
[
14
, Theorem 7.18 and its corollaries]
that the module has finite length if
V
and
V^{\prime}
are realized as holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules on the full flag varieties.
For example,
V
and
V^{\prime}
are objects in the BGG category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{}}^{\mathfrak{}}
, or Harish-Chandra modules.
Moreover, we have treated a similar
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(V|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}},V^{\prime}\bigr)
and its cohomological version.
We concentrate on the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(V^{\prime},V|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigr)
in this paper.

We will consider generalized Verma modules, cohomologically parabolically induced modules and discrete series representations.
We give criteria for the irreducibility of the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-module for such modules.

We shall explain why we study
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-modules.
Recently, explicit realizations of intertwining operators (symmetry breaking operators) in the branching problem are computed by many researchers, e.g.,
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
.
Under some good setting, the operators are represented by special functions.
The computations use concrete realizations of representations and operators, and the operators are obtained from good differential equations and good geometric structures.

To understand the goodness of intertwining operators, we consider algebraic structures on the
\mathrm{Hom}
space.
For example, if
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(V^{\prime}_{1},V_{1}|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigr)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(V^{\prime}_{2},V_{2}|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigr)
,
then we may expect that there is some relation between intertwining operators in the two different
\mathrm{Hom}
spaces.
Alternatively, if
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
acts on the
\mathrm{Hom}
space irreducibly, then we may consider that the algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
controls the branching law well.
In fact, we will show in Theorem
5.38
that the supremum of the multiplicities is equal to a ring-theoretic invariant of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
, called the PI degree.

We shall state the main results in this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{0,1,2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

Notation and convention

2
Preliminary

In this section, we recall several fundamental notions about
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, generalized Verma modules and Zuckerman derived functors.

2.1
\boldsymbol{(\mathfrak{g},K)}
-module

We recall the notion of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules.
Let
G
be an affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
.
A linear action of
G
on a vector space
V
is said to be rational
if the action is locally finite and any finite-dimensional subrepresentation is a representation of an algebraic group.
In the case,
V
is called a
G
-module.
If
G
is reductive, any
G
-module is completely reducible.

Let us recall the definitions of pairs
(\mathfrak{g},K)
and
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules.
In this paper, we treat only the case of connected reductive
K
.
We refer the reader to
[
16
, Chapter I]
for the general case.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and
K
a connected reductive algebraic group
such that
\mathfrak{k}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We say that
(\mathfrak{g},K)
is a
pair
if the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{k}
on
\mathfrak{g}
lifts to a rational action of
K
.
A pair
(\mathfrak{h},L)
is said to be a
subpair
of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
if
\mathfrak{h}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

and
L
is a connected reductive subgroup of
K
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair and
V
a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
We denote by
V_{\mathfrak{k}}
the sum of all finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules,
and by
V_{K}
the sum of all
\mathfrak{k}
-submodules that lift to
K
-modules.
We say that
V
is a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
if
V=V_{K}
.

Clearly,
V_{K}
is a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.
Since we have assumed that

is connected, the
K
-action on a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module

is uniquely determined by the
\mathfrak{g}
-action.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair and
V
a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.
Then

is completely reducible and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
acts naturally on
V^{K}
.
Since
\mathfrak{k}
acts on
V^{K}
trivially, the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
-action factors through
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}\!\simeq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{K}/(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}
.
It is well-known that if

is irreducible, then the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
-module

is irreducible or zero.
See
[
6
, Theorem 4.2.1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair.
The functor

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g},K)\ni V\mapsto V^{K}\in\mathrm{Mod}\bigl((\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}\bigr)

is exact and
sends irreducible objects to irreducible ones or zero.
In particular, the length of
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}
-module

is less than or equal to that of
V

for any

.

It may be hard to study
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}
-modules directly.
Using Proposition

, we reduce many problems of
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{k})^{K}
-modules
to those of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules.
The following

-module case is important for us.

Let
G
be a connected affine algebraic group and
G^{\prime}
a connected reductive subgroup of
G
.
Then
(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g},\Delta(G^{\prime}))
is a pair, where
\Delta\colon G^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\prime}\times G
is the diagonal embedding.

Proposition 2.4
.

(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}

is naturally isomorphic to
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Proof.

Identify
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})
with
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})\otimes\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Then the isomorphism is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}\ni X\mapsto 1\otimes X+\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})\in(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}.

In face, the map is bijective by
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}=\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})\oplus\mathfrak{g}
and the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem,
and the linear map is a homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras because it is given as the composition of the following natural maps:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}/(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}.\qed

∎

The two algebras
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
and
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}
act naturally on
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}(V^{\prime},V)
for a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
and a
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-module
V^{\prime}
.
The action of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
is give by

\displaystyle(X\cdot\varphi)(\cdot)=X\cdot\varphi(\cdot),\qquad X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\quad\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}(V^{\prime},V),

and the action of
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})/\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})\Delta(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}))^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}
is induced from that of the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})^{\Delta(G^{\prime})}
:

\displaystyle\biggl(\biggl(\sum_{i}A_{i}\otimes B_{i}\biggr)\cdot\varphi\biggr)(\cdot)=\sum_{i}B_{i}\varphi\big({}^{t}A_{i}\cdot\big),

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i}A_{i}\otimes B_{i}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g})^{G^{\prime}},\ \varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}(V^{\prime},V)\biggr.

It is easy to see that the two actions coincide under the isomorphism in Proposition
2.4
.

2.2
Translation functor

Our purpose in this paper is to show the irreducibility of some
(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}\oplus g},\Delta(G^{\prime}))
-modules.
We will reduce the problem to the case of enough large infinitesimal characters.
To do so, we need the translation functor.
We refer the reader to
[
16
, Chapter VII]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra.
Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and denote by
W_{G}
the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We identify
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
with
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{t})^{W_{G}}
via the Harish-Chandra isomorphism.
Then characters of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
are parametrized by elements in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{G}
.
When we say that
\chi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{G}
is an infinitesimal character,

\chi
is identified with a homomorphism
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}

and
\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
is a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
.
For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we denote by
[\lambda]
the corresponding infinitesimal character in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{G}
,
and for a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g})
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{[\lambda]}
(or
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
) the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of objects with the generalized infinitesimal character
[\lambda]
.

For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
and an infinitesimal character
\chi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{G}
, set

\displaystyle P_{\chi}(V)\coloneq\{v\in V\mid\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)^{n}v=0,\,\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Note that
n
can depend on
v
.
Then
P_{\chi}(V)
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
V
, and called the
primary component
with the infinitesimal character
\chi
.
When
V
is the direct sum of all primary components, we say that the direct sum decomposition is the
primary decomposition
.
The following fact is a direct consequence of
[
16
, Proposition 7.20]
.
For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, we say that
V
is locally finite
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-module if
Z(\mathfrak{g})v
is finite-dimensional for any
v\in V
.

Fact 2.5
.

Any locally
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
-finite
\mathfrak{g}
-module has the primary decomposition.

We shall recall the translation functor.
Let
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be an algebraically integral weight of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
F(\mu)
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module with the extreme weight
\mu
.
For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
with an generalized infinitesimal character
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{G}
, set

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}(V)\coloneq P_{[\lambda+\mu]}(F(\mu)\otimes V).

Then
P_{[\lambda+\mu]}(F(\mu)\otimes V)
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module with the generalized infinitesimal character
[\lambda+\mu]
.

Note that
F(\mu)\otimes V
is
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
-finite by Kostant’s theorem
[
16
, Theorem 7.133]
,
and hence
T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}(V)
is a direct summand of
F(\mu)\otimes V
.
This implies that
T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}
is an exact functor from
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g})_{[\lambda]}
to
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g})_{[\lambda+\mu]}
.
The functor is called the
translation functor
.

The translation functor preserves the irreducibility under some assumption.
To state the facts, we shall recall the notion of integrally dominant weights.
Fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{t}
.
Let
\Delta^{+}
denote the set of positive roots determined by
\mathfrak{b}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}
.
We say that
\lambda
regular if
(\lambda,\alpha)\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
.
We say that
\lambda
is
integrally dominant
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
if

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{-1,-2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta^{+}.

\lambda
is said to be
integrally anti-dominant
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
if

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{1,2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta^{+}.

It is well-known that
T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}
is a good functor (e.g., isomorphism) on several good categories.
We only need results about
\mathfrak{g}
-modules of finite length.
For the following fact, see
[
16
, Corollary 7.209]
and its proof.
Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{fl}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules of finite length.

Fact 2.7
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be a regular integrally dominant weight and
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
an algebraically integral weight.
Suppose that
\lambda+\mu
is regular integrally dominant.
Then
T^{\lambda+\mu}_{\lambda}
gives an equivalence of categories from
\mathrm{Mod}_{fl}(\mathfrak{g})_{\lambda}
to
\mathrm{Mod}_{fl}(\mathfrak{g})_{\lambda+\mu}
, and a quasi-inverse is given by
T^{\lambda}_{\lambda+\mu}
.

2.3
Discrete decomposability

Our main concern in this paper is discretely decomposable restrictions of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
We recall the notion of the discrete decomposability.
We refer the reader to
[
20
,
24
]
for the details.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
We say that
V
is
discretely decomposable
if
V
has an exhaustive
\mathfrak{g}
-module filtration
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots

such that any
V_{i}
has finite length.
Moreover, if all
V_{i}
are in a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g})
, we say that

is

in

.

By definition, a discretely decomposable

.

Proposition 2.9
.

A discretely decomposable

-module has the primary decomposition.

2.4
Generalized Verma module

This section contains a brief summary of generalized Verma modules.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
.
For the branching problem part, see
[
24
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra.
Fix a semisimple element
H\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathrm{ad}(H)
has only real eigenvalues.

\mathfrak{l}(H)
,
\mathfrak{u}(H)
and
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}(H)
denote the sums of eigenspaces of
\mathrm{ad}(H)

with zero, positive and negative eigenvalues, respectively.
Then
\mathfrak{q}(H)\coloneq\mathfrak{l}(H)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(H)
is a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Similarly, write
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}(H)\coloneq\mathfrak{l}(H)\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{u}}(H)
for the opposite parabolic subalgebra.
If
H
is clear from the context, we omit ‘
(H)
’ part as
\mathfrak{l}
,
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{q}
.

Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathfrak{l}
and write
W_{L}
for the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{l}
.
Then we have
H\in\mathfrak{t}
.
For a finite-dimensional completely reducible
\mathfrak{t}
-module
V
, we denote by
\Delta(V,\mathfrak{t})
(or
\Delta(V)
) the (multi)set of all non-zero weights in
V
.
We write
\rho(\mathfrak{u})
for half the sum of all roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})
.

We shall recall generalized Verma modules.
Let
F
be an
\mathfrak{l}
-module.
Set

\displaystyle\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\coloneq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{q})}F,

where we consider
F
as a
\mathfrak{q}
-module letting
\mathfrak{u}
act on
F
trivially.
In this paper, we use this extension without mention.
It is well-known that if
F
has an infinitesimal character
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{L}
,
then
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
has the infinitesimal character
[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u})]
.
If
F
is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module,

\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is called a
generalized Verma module
.
In this case,
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is a highest weight module with respect to some Borel subalgebra contained in
\mathfrak{q}
.

The relative BGG category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{}}^{\mathfrak{}}
denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined as follows (see
[
10
, Section 9]
).

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
is the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g})
whose object
V
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

V
is finitely generated as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

2.

V|_{\mathfrak{l}}
is locally finite and completely reducible.

3.

The action of
\mathfrak{u}
on
V
is locally nilpotent.

Then any generalized Verma module
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is an object of the category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
.
It is well-known that any object in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
has finite length and hence
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
-finite.

A generalized Verma module may be reducible in general.
The following result provides a criterion for the irreducibility of a generalized Verma module (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Theorem 9.12]
).

Fact 2.10
.

Let
F
be an irreducible finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{l}
-module with infinitesimal character
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{L}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{1,2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

Then the generalized Verma module
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible.

Remark that the assumption of Fact
2.10
implies that
\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u})
is integrally anti-dominant for some Borel subalgebra contained in
\mathfrak{q}
.
The image of a generalized Verma module by the translation functor is also a generalized Verma module under a dominance condition (see
[
16
, Theorem 7.237]
).

Fact 2.11
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{l}
-module with infinitesimal character
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{L}
,
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
an algebraically integral weight.
Suppose that there exists a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{t}
such that
\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u})
and
\lambda+\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u})
are regular integrally dominant for
\mathfrak{b}
.
Then there exists a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism

\displaystyle T_{\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u})}^{\lambda+\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u})}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\bigl(T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}(F)\bigr).

We recall the notion of standard filtrations in
[
10
, Section 9.8]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
We say that an exhaustive filtration
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots
of
V
is a
standard filtration
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
if each successive quotient
V_{i+1}/{V_{i}}
is isomorphic to a generalized Verma module

\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
.

If the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}
is clear from the context,
we simply say that
{0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots}
is a standard filtration.
We do not assume that a standard filtration is finite.
We give a fundamental property of the standard filtration.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module with a standard filtration
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots
.

1.

For any object
W\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
,
V\otimes W
has an exhaustive filtration such that
\gr(V\otimes W)
has a standard filtration.

2.

If each successive quotient
V_{i+1}/V_{i}
is irreducible, then
V
is completely reducible and isomorphic to
\gr(V)
.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear from the natural isomorphism
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\cdot)\otimes W\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\cdot\otimes W)

and that
W
has a
\mathfrak{q}
-module filtration whose successive quotients are irreducible and finite-dimensional.

To prove the second assertion, we can assume that
V
has finite length.
In fact, if each
V_{i}
is completely reducible, then
V
is completely reducible
because
V
is a sum of irreducible submodules.
Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}}(M,N)=0
for any two irreducible generalized Verma modules
M
and
N
(see
[
10
, Theorem 3.3 (d)]
),
the assertion follows by induction on the length.
∎

We consider the branching problem of a generalized Verma module.
For simplicity, suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple.
Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
be a reductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We recall the notion of
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebras in the sense of
[
24
]
,
and define its generalization.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible
if there exists
H\in\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
such that
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}(H)
.
We say that
\mathfrak{q}
is
weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible
if
\mathfrak{q}
contains a
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(H)\subset\mathfrak{g}
and has a Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}(H)\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{q}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(H)\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}(H)\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{\overline{u}}(H)\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{\overline{u}}\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}},

(2.1)

where
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}
is the nilpotent radical of the opposite parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{\overline{q}}
determined by the Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
.

Formulas (
2.1
) mean that
\mathfrak{q}
,
\mathfrak{l}
,
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}
have direct sum decompositions compatible with
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}\oplus(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})^{\perp}
.
Remark that if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
is a symmetric pair defined by an involution
\theta
,
then
\mathfrak{q}
is weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
is
\theta
-stable,
and they are equivalent to that
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible by
[
16
, Proposition 4.76]
.

Example 2.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{C})
and set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}\coloneq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0&\hphantom{-}b\\
0&0&\hphantom{-}0\\
c&0&-a\end{pmatrix}\bigg|\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}\right\},\qquad H\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\hphantom{-}0\\
0&0&\hphantom{-}0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\mathfrak{q}(H)
is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and every parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{q}(H)
is weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible.
Moreover, any
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is either a Borel subalgebra or
\mathfrak{g}
itself.

Let
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
be a weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

containing
\mathfrak{q}(H)
,
H\in\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
.
Set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}},\qquad\mathfrak{u^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}},\qquad\mathfrak{\overline{u}^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{\overline{u}}\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}},\qquad\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}\oplus\mathfrak{u^{\prime}}.

(2.2)

Then
\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}
is a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
.

An important fact is that any object of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{g}}
is discretely decomposable
as a
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}
(see
[
24
, Proposition 3.8]
).
By the same proof as in
[
42
, Lemma 6.4.4]
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module.
Then
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}
has a standard filtration
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots

satisfying

\displaystyle\gr(V)\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}\bigl(F\otimes S\bigl(\mathfrak{\overline{u}}/\mathfrak{\overline{u}^{\prime}}\bigr)\bigr),

where
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}/\mathfrak{\overline{u}^{\prime}}
is regarded as a
\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}
-module
by letting
\mathfrak{u^{\prime}}
act on
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}/\mathfrak{\overline{u}^{\prime}}
trivially.

Since
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{q}
is a quotient of
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{q}(H)\simeq\mathfrak{\overline{u}}(H)
, any eigenvalue in
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{q}
of
H
is negative.
This implies that any eigenspace in
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\simeq S(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{q})\otimes F
of
H
is finite-dimensional.
From
H\in\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
and this,
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}
-admissible, that is, the multiplicity of any irreducible
\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
-module is finite.
The following proposition is well-known if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
is a symmetric pair.
The proof is the same as the symmetric pair case.
For the notation
(\cdot)_{\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}}
in the proposition, see Definition
2.2
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module.
Then
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}
-admissible.
If, in addition,
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible, then
(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)^{*})_{\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*})
as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Proof.

The first assertion is shown in the above.
For the second assertion, assume that
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible.
Since
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is
\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
-admissible, we have
\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)^{*}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}}=\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)^{*}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
Since
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible,
\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)^{*}\bigr)_{\mathfrak{l}}
is an irreducible highest weight module
and the highest weight is the same as that of
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*})
.
This shows the assertion.
∎

2.5
Zuckerman derived functor

Here we review the Zuckerman derived functor.
We will use the Zuckerman derived functor in two ways.
One is to construct cohomologically induced module, e.g., discrete series representations.
The other is to study
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-modules.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair with reductive
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
M
a connected reductive subgroup of
K
.
Recall that, for a
(\mathfrak{g},M)
-module
V
,
V_{K}
is the sum of all
(\mathfrak{k},M)
-submodules that lift to
K
-modules.
Then
V_{K}
is a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module,
and the functor
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g},M)\ni V\rightarrow V_{K}\in\mathrm{Mod}(\mathfrak{g},K)
is left exact.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}
the right derived functor of the functor
(\cdot)_{K}
.
The functor
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}
is called the
i
-th
Zuckerman derived functor
.

The functor can be constructed explicitly as follows.
We refer the reader to
[
2
, Section I.8]
.
Let
V
be a
(\mathfrak{g},M)
-module and
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider the cohomology group
H^{i}(\mathfrak{k},M;\mathcal{O}(K)\otimes V)
.
Here

\scriptstyle{m}

From this and Proposition

Proposition 2.18
.

Let

be a
(\mathfrak{g},M)
-module.
Then there exists a natural

.
In particular, the length of

Remark 2.19
.

The

-module structure on

Indeed, the multiplication

We summarize fundamental results about the Zuckerman derived functor.
We refer the reader to
[
16
, Theorems 2.103 and 5.21, and the proof of Theorem 7.237]

Fact 2.20
.

Let
V
be a
(\mathfrak{g},M)
-module.

1.

If
V
has an infinitesimal character
\chi
, then
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V)
has the infinitesimal character
\chi
.

2.

Let
W
be a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. Then there exists a natural isomorphism

R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V)\otimes W\simeq R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V\otimes W).

3.

R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}
commutes with the translation functors
T_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\mu}
if
\mu
is an extreme weight of a finite-dimensional irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
V
be a
(\mathfrak{g},M)
-module with an exhaustive filtration

0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots
.
If
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V_{j})=0
for any

, then
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V)=0
holds.

Proof.

Recall that the cohomology
H^{i}(\mathfrak{k},M;V\otimes\mathcal{O}(K))\bigl(\simeq R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{M}(V)\bigr)
is computed by the standard complex

\displaystyle C^{i}\coloneq\mathrm{Hom}_{M}\bigl(\wedge^{i}(\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{m}),V\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr)

with
d\colon C^{i}\rightarrow C^{i+1}
.
Let
\omega\in C^{i}
such that
\mathrm{d}\omega=0
.
Then
\Im(\omega)
is contained in
V_{j}\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)
for some
j
.
Hence, by assumption, there exists
\omega^{\prime}\in C^{i-1}
such that
\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}=\omega
.
This shows the assertion.
∎

We shall state a part of an algebraic analogue of the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem

be a pair.

Write

-module with infinitesimal character

in the fact corresponds to

3

-modules.
We deal with a vanishing theorem and irreducibility.
The results are well-known if

We give proofs for the results in the general setting to make this paper self-contained
even though the proofs are essentially the same as the symmetric case, e.g.,
[

3.1
Vanishing theorem

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair with semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
be a weakly
\mathfrak{k}
-compatible parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

containing
\mathfrak{q}(H)
,
H\in\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
.
See Definition
2.14
.
Set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}_{K}\coloneq\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{k},\qquad\mathfrak{u}_{K}\coloneq\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k},\qquad\mathfrak{\overline{u}}_{K}\coloneq\mathfrak{\overline{u}}\cap\mathfrak{k},\qquad\mathfrak{q}_{K}\coloneq\mathfrak{l}_{K}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{K}.

Then
\mathfrak{q}_{K}
is a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{k}
.
Let
\mathfrak{\overline{q}}
and
\mathfrak{\overline{q}}_{K}
denote the opposite parabolic subalgebras of
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{K}
, respectively.
Let
L_{K}
denote the analytic subgroup in
K
with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}_{K}
, which is a Levi subgroup of
K
.
Put
S\coloneq\dim(\mathfrak{u}_{K})
.

We shall consider cohomologically induced modules
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)

When
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is a symmetric pair, such modules are classical and well studied.
See, e.g.,
[
16
]
and
[
42
, Section 6]
.
In the context of generalized Harish-Chandra modules,
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)
is studied in
[
37
,
39
]
.
The following fact is fundamental.
See
[
42
, Lemma 6.4.2]
.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
M
be a
(\mathfrak{k},L_{K})
-module with a standard filtration
(
see Definition
\ref{def:StandardFiltration})
.
Then
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}(M)
vanishes for any

.

The following proposition is used in Lemma
4.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a
(\mathfrak{k},L_{K})
-module with an exhaustive filtration such that
\gr(M)
has a standard filtration.
Then
R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}(M)=0

i<S
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Lemma
2.21
and Fact

.
∎

We recall a vanishing theorem and existence of a cyclic subspace.
Our proofs are essentially the same as in
[
42
, Section 6]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F
be an irreducible finite-dimensional
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module.
Then we have

\displaystyle R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)=0,\qquad\forall i<S.

Moreover, if
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible, then

\displaystyle R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)=0,\qquad\forall i\neq S.

Proof.

The first assertion is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.16
and
Fact
3.1
.

Assume that
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is irreducible and fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Proposition
2.17
, we have

\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)^{*}\bigr)_{L_{K}}\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*}).

By the duality
[
16
, Corollary 3.7]
, there exists an isomorphism

\bigl(R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)^{*}\bigr)_{K}\simeq R^{2S-d}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*})\bigr).

Using the first assertion for
R^{2S-d}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*})\bigr)
,
we have

\displaystyle(R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)^{*})_{K}\simeq R^{2S-i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{\overline{q}}}(F^{*})\bigr)=0\qquad\text{if}\ 2S-i<S.

This shows the second assertion.
∎

3.2
Cyclic subspace

We shall construct a cyclic subspace in the Zuckerman derived functor module
under some dominance condition.
Retain the notation in the previous subsection.

Let
\mathfrak{t}_{K}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}_{K}

and write
\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})
for half the sum of all roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{u}_{K},\mathfrak{t}_{K})
.
Let
W_{K}
(resp.
W_{L_{K}}
) denote the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{k}
(resp.
\mathfrak{l}_{K}
).
For an
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module
F
, we have a natural homomorphism
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
of
(\mathfrak{k},L_{K})
-modules, and it induces a
K
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle B_{F}\colon\ R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\bigr)\rightarrow R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr).

This map is called a
bottom-layer map
in
[
16
, p. 365]
(when
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is symmetric).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module.
Then for each irreducible
K
-submodule
V
of
R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\bigr)
,
the linear map

\displaystyle(B_{F})_{*}\colon\ \mathrm{Hom}_{K}\bigl(V,R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\bigr)\bigr)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{K}\bigl(V,R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)\bigr)

is bijective.
In particular,
B_{F}
is injective.

Proof.

Let
V
be an irreducible
K
-submodule of
R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\bigr)
.
Then there exists a unique irreducible
L_{K}
-submodule
F_{0}
of
F
such that
the image of the natural homomorphism

\displaystyle R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F_{0})\bigr)\rightarrow R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)\bigr)

is equal to
V
.
Write
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{K}/W_{L_{K}}
for the infinitesimal character of
F_{0}
.
Then
(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K}),\beta)<0
holds for any
\beta\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u}_{K},\mathfrak{t}_{K})
,
and
R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F_{0})\bigr)
has the infinitesimal character
[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]
.
See Fact
2.22
.

Set
W\coloneq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})(1\otimes F)
in
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
, which
is a
\mathfrak{k}
-submodule of
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
isomorphic to
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F)
.
We shall show that
W
contains the primary component
P_{[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{K}\bigr)
.
If so, we have

\displaystyle P_{[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]}\bigl(R^{S}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)\bigr)|_{K}\bigr)\subset\Im(B_{F}).

by Fact
2.20
, and this shows the assertion.

By Proposition
2.16
, there exists a standard filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots
of
M\coloneq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
such that

\gr(M)\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F\otimes S(\mathfrak{\bar{u}}/\mathfrak{\bar{u}}_{K}))
,
where we let
\mathfrak{u}_{K}
act on
S(\mathfrak{\bar{u}}/\mathfrak{\bar{u}}_{K})
trivially.
Since
P_{[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]}
is an exact functor,
it is enough to show

\displaystyle P_{[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F\otimes S(\mathfrak{\bar{u}}/\mathfrak{\bar{u}}_{K}))\bigr)\subset\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F\otimes\mathbb{C}).

Let
F^{\prime}
be an irreducible
L_{K}
-submodule of
F\otimes S(\mathfrak{\bar{u}}/\mathfrak{\bar{u}}_{K})
with infinitesimal character
[\mu]\in\mathfrak{t}_{K}^{*}/W_{L_{K}}
.
Then
\mu(H)\leq\lambda(H)
, and the equality holds only if
F^{\prime}\subset F\otimes\mathbb{C}
since all eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}(H)
in
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}
are negative.
Assume
[\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]=[\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]
in
\mathfrak{t}_{K}^{*}/W_{K}
and let us show
\mu(H)=\lambda(H)
.
Note that
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathfrak{q_{\mathit{K}}}}(F^{\prime})
has the infinitesimal character
[\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})]\in\mathfrak{t}_{K}^{*}/W_{K}
.
By assumption, there exists
s\in W_{K}
such that
\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})=s(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K}))
.

Since
\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K})
is an algebraically integral weight and dominant with respect to
-\Delta(\mathfrak{u}_{K},\mathfrak{t}_{K})
,
there is a sum
R
of elements in
\Delta(\mathfrak{l}_{K},\mathfrak{t}_{K})\cup\Delta(\mathfrak{u}_{K},\mathfrak{t}_{K})
such that

Hence we have

\displaystyle(\mu+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K}))(H)=s(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K}))(H)\geq(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}_{K}))(H)

Recall that we have
R(H)\geq 0
by
\mathfrak{u}_{K}\subset\mathfrak{u}(H)
(see Definition
2.14
).
Combining this with
\mu(H)\leq\lambda(H)
, we obtain
\mu(H)=\lambda(H)
, and hence
F^{\prime}\subset F\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
∎

4

We have seen in Propositions

5.2
Quasi-abelian parabolic subalgebra

In this subsection, we give a sufficient condition for completely reducibility of
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}

to apply Corollary
5.9
.
Retain the notation in the previous subsection.

Set
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}\coloneq\mathfrak{u}\cap(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{\overline{u}}^{\prime\prime}\coloneq\mathfrak{\overline{u}}\cap(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\perp}
.
Then
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}
is
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
-stable and
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}
holds by the definition of weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebras (Definition
2.14
).
The notion of quasi-abelian parabolic subalgebras is defined in
[
5
, p. 109]
for symmetric
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
.
We consider a straightforward generalization of the definition.

Definition 5.11
.

\mathfrak{q}
is said to be
quasi-abelian
with respect to
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
if

(\alpha,\beta)\geq 0
holds for any
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
and
\beta\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
.

It is clear that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible parabolic subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian, then
\mathfrak{p}
is also quasi-abelian.
In fact, the nilpotent radical of
\mathfrak{p}
is smaller than that of
\mathfrak{q}
.

If the nilpotent radical of
\mathfrak{q}
is abelian,
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian.
More generally, we have the following criterion.

Proposition 5.12
.

If
[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}]=0
holds, then
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian.
In particular, a parabolic subalgebra with abelian nilpotent radical is quasi-abelian.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
and
\beta\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
.
Assume
(\alpha,\beta)<0
.
This implies
\alpha+\beta\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})

and
\bigl[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}_{\beta}\bigr]\neq 0
.
By assumption,
\bigl[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}_{\beta}\bigr]\subset[\mathfrak{u}^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}]=0
holds and this is contradiction.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian.
∎

Example 5.13
.

If
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian as follows.

\mathfrak{q}
contains
\mathfrak{q}(H)
defined by a semisimple element
H\in\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
.
Since any eigenvalue in
\mathfrak{u}(H)
of
\mathrm{ad}(H)
is positive,
\mathfrak{q}(H)
is quasi-abelian.
As noted immediately after Definition
5.11
,
\mathfrak{q}
is also quasi-abelian.

Take an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\left\{H,X,Y\right\}\subset\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
with
[X,Y]=H
.
If
\mathrm{ad}(H)
has an eigenvalue greater than or equal to
3
in
\mathfrak{g}
, then we have
[\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime}]\neq 0

by the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
Take a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
-module
F
of dimension greater than or equal to
3
.
Then the embedding
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(F)=\mathfrak{g}
gives an example
of quasi-abelian parabolic subalgebra with
[\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{u^{\prime\prime}}]\neq 0
.
This means that the converse of Proposition
5.12
does not hold in general.

A criterion for the completely reducibility of
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}
is given in
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
is a symmetric pair.
We extend the criterion to our setting.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module.
Assume that for any irreducible submodule of
F|_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}}
, its infinitesimal character
[\lambda^{\prime}]\in(\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})^{*}/W_{L^{\prime}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda^{\prime}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}),\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{1,2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}).

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian.
Then
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
is completely reducible and each irreducible component is isomorphic to an irreducible generalized Verma module.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.16
,
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
has a standard filtration with

\displaystyle\gr\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}\bigr)\simeq\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}\bigl(F\otimes S\bigl(\mathfrak{\overline{u}}^{\prime\prime}\bigr)\bigr),

where the
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
-action on
S(\mathfrak{\overline{u}}^{\prime\prime})
is trivial.

Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
-submodule of
F\otimes S(\mathfrak{\overline{u}}^{\prime\prime})
.
By the Weyl character formula, the infinitesimal character of
V
is of the form
[\lambda^{\prime}-R]
,
where
R
is a sum of elements in
\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
and
[\lambda^{\prime}]
is the infinitesimal character of some irreducible submodule of
F|_{\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}}
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2(R,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\in\left\{0,1,2,\ldots\right\},\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}).

This and the assumption imply

\displaystyle\frac{2(\lambda^{\prime}-R+\rho(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}),\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\not\in\left\{1,2,\ldots\right\}\qquad\text{for any }\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}).

Hence
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}(V)
is irreducible by Fact
2.10
.
Therefore, Proposition
2.13
shows the assertion.
∎

The condition in Lemma
5.14
is given in terms of
\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}
-weights.
We shall give a criterion for the completely reducibility in terms of
\mathfrak{t}
-weights.
To do so, we prepare several convexity results.
Note that J.A. Vargas gave a similar estimate in
[
41
, §(1.5)]
in the context of the branching problem of discrete series representations.
Our result (Theorem
5.17
) contains his estimate.
See also Theorems
5.27
and
5.31
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{t}
.
Let
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime\prime}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{t}
.
Consider
(\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})^{*}
as a subspace of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}

using the direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}\oplus\mathfrak{t^{\prime\prime}}
.
For
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
, define

\displaystyle\Delta(\alpha)\coloneq\left\{\beta\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})\mid\beta|_{\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}}=\alpha\right\}.

For a subset
S
of a real vector space, we denote by
\Co(S)
the convex hull of
S
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
.
Then
\alpha\in\Co(\Delta(\alpha))
holds.

Proof.

Fix a Cartan involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
are
\theta
-stable.
In fact, since
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is reductive in
\mathfrak{g}
, such an involution exists.

Take a root vector
X\in\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{\alpha}
.
By
X\in\mathfrak{u}
, we can write
X=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}X_{\beta}
with
X_{\beta}\in\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}
.
Consider the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\coloneq-(\cdot,\theta(\cdot))
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Note that
[\mathfrak{t^{\prime\prime}},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}]\subset(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\perp}
.
Then the root spaces are mutually orthogonal, and hence we have

\displaystyle\|X\|^{2}=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}\|X_{\beta}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}\langle\beta(Z^{\prime})X_{\beta},X_{\beta}\rangle=\langle\alpha(Z^{\prime})X,X\rangle,

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}\langle\beta(Z^{\prime\prime})X_{\beta},X_{\beta}\rangle=\langle[Z^{\prime\prime},X],X\rangle=0=\langle\alpha(Z^{\prime\prime})X,X\rangle

for any
Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}
and
Z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{t^{\prime\prime}}
.
This shows

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}\frac{\|X_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\|X\|^{2}}\beta,\qquad\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}\frac{\|X_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\|X\|^{2}}=1.

Therefore, we obtain
\alpha\in\Co(\Delta(\alpha))
.
∎

Lemma 5.16
.

Fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{L}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{L}
of
\mathfrak{l}
.
Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be dominant integral weights of
\mathfrak{l}
.
Then we have
\Co(W_{L}\lambda_{1})+\Co(W_{L}\lambda_{2})=\Co(W_{L}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))
.

Proof.

\Co(W_{L}\lambda_{1})+\Co(W_{L}\lambda_{2})\supset\Co(W_{L}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))
is obvious.
We shall show the converse inclusion.
Let
s\in W_{L}
and it is enough to prove
\lambda_{1}+s(\lambda_{2})\in\Co(W_{L}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))
.

For a dominant integral weight
\lambda
of
\mathfrak{l}
, we denote by
F(\lambda)
the irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module with the highest weight
\lambda
.
Then there exists a unique non-zero homomorphism
p\colon F(\lambda_{1})\otimes F(\lambda_{2})\rightarrow F(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
up to scalar.
For each
i=1,2
, fix a highest weight vector
v_{i}
of
F(\lambda_{i})
.
Then we have
p(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})\neq 0
.

Take a weight vector
v^{\prime}_{2}\in F(\lambda_{2})
with the extreme weight
s(\lambda_{2})
.
Then there exists
X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{u_{\mathit{L}}})
such that
Xv^{\prime}_{2}=v_{2}
.
Since
v_{1}
is
\mathfrak{u}_{L}
-invariant, we have
X(v_{1}\otimes v^{\prime}_{2})=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
.
This implies

\displaystyle Xp\bigl(v_{1}\otimes v^{\prime}_{2}\bigr)=p\bigl(X\bigl(v_{1}\otimes v^{\prime}_{2}\bigr)\bigr)=p(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})\neq 0,

and hence
p\bigl(v_{1}\otimes v^{\prime}_{2}\bigr)\neq 0
.

Since any weight of
F(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
belongs to
\Co(W_{L}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))
,
this shows
\lambda_{1}+s(\lambda_{2})\in\Co(W_{L}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}))
.
We have proved the lemma.
∎

Fix a basis
\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r^{\prime}}\right\}
of
\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}
and extend it to a basis
\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\right\}
of
\mathfrak{t}
.
The bases determine lexicographical orders on
(\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})^{*}
and
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
Let
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
denote the sets of positive roots given by the orders.
Write
\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})
(resp.
\rho(\mathfrak{l})
) for half the sum of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
(resp.
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
).
Then
\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}
is a dominant integral weight of
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}
.

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module with infinitesimal character
[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{L}

in the good range, namely,

\displaystyle\Re(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha)<0,\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian.
Then
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)|_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
is completely reducible,
and each irreducible direct summand is of the form
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}(F^{\prime})
such that
F^{\prime}
is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
-module in the good range.

Proof.

Let
F^{\prime}
be an irreducible submodule of
F|_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}}
and
[\lambda^{\prime}]
the infinitesimal character of
F^{\prime}
.
We shall prove

\Re(\lambda^{\prime}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}),\alpha)<0
,
\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
.
If we prove this, the assertion follows by the same way as Lemma
5.14
.

Replacing the representatives
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are dominant with respect to
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
, respectively.
By Lemma
5.16
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in\Co(W_{L}(\lambda-\rho(\mathfrak{l})))|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})\subset\Co(W_{L}(\lambda-\rho(\mathfrak{l})))|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\Co(W_{L}\rho(\mathfrak{l}))|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}-\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Co(W_{L}(\lambda))|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}-\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}).

From this, we write

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=\sum_{s\in W_{L}}a_{s}s(\lambda)|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}-\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})

with
\sum_{s\in W_{L}}a_{s}=1
and
a_{s}\geq 0
.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{t^{\prime}})
.
By Lemma
5.15
,
\alpha
is written as

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}c_{\beta}\beta

with
\sum_{\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}c_{\beta}=1
and
c_{\beta}\geq 0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle(\lambda^{\prime}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}),\alpha)+(\rho(\mathfrak{u})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}-\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})-\rho(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}}),\alpha)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\biggl(\sum_{s\in W_{L}}a_{s}s(\lambda)+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{s\in W_{L},\beta\in\Delta(\alpha)}a_{s}c_{\beta}\bigl(\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),s^{-1}(\beta)\bigr).

(5.4)

By
\Delta(\alpha)\subset\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})
and the assumption, the real part of (
5.4
) is negative.
By Proposition
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle(\rho(\mathfrak{u})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}+\rho(\mathfrak{l})|_{\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}}-\rho(\mathfrak{l^{\prime}})-\rho(\mathfrak{u^{\prime}}),\alpha)\geq 0.

Therefore, we obtain
(\lambda^{\prime}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}),\alpha)<0
.
∎

Combining Corollary
5.9
with Theorem
5.17
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.18

Let

5.3
Holomorphic discrete series representation

A typical example of Corollary
5.18
is the case of holomorphic discrete series representations.
In the case, the branching laws are studied in
[
11
]
(at the unitary representation level),
and in
[
21
,
22
]
.
Theorem
5.17
has been proved in
[
21
, Theorem 7.4]
.
Corollary
5.18
for holomorphic discrete series representations of some classical groups is reduced to the compact group case by the Howe duality and see-saw pair (see, e.g.,
[
30
, Lemma 2.6]
).

Let
(\mathfrak{g},G^{\prime})
be a pair with semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
,
and
\theta
an involution of
\mathfrak{g}
fixing
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
.
Set
\mathfrak{k}\coloneq\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{k^{\prime}}\coloneq(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})^{\theta}
.
Suppose that there exists a semisimple element
H\in\mathfrak{g}
such that

H\in\mathfrak{k^{\prime}}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{H}=\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathrm{ad}(H)
has eigenvalues
-1
,
0
,
1
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Define parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}(H)=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(H)

and
\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}\oplus(\mathfrak{u}(H)\cap\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
(see Section
2.4
for the notation).
Then
\mathfrak{q}
is quasi-abelian with respect to
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
by Proposition
5.12
.

Fix a real form
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\theta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}}
is a Cartan involution.
Set
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneq\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{k}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\mathfrak{k}}
.
Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a simply-connected connected semisimple Lie group with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
K_{\mathbb{R}}
,
G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the analytic subgroups of

with the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.

Let
F
be an irreducible unitary representation of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
F
is finite-dimensional.
By Harish-Chandra’s classification of holomorphic discrete series representations,
F
is in the good range if and only if

\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is unitarizable and isomorphic to the underlying Harish-Chandra module of a holomorphic discrete series representation of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
(with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
).
For simplicity, irreducible finite-dimensional unitary representations are regarded as holomorphic discrete series representations.
We refer the reader to
[
15
, Chapter VI]
for holomorphic discrete series representations.

In this setting, Theorem
5.17
and Corollary
5.18
are rephrased as follows.
Although the following theorem has an overlap with Theorem
5.31
(the discrete series case),
we state it explicitly because in the present setting

is non-compact and
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})
need not be a symmetric pair.

Theorem 5.20
.

Suppose
V

(
resp.
W)
is a holomorphic discrete series representation of
G_{\mathbb{R}}

(
resp.
G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}})
.
Then
V|_{G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}
is discretely decomposable and a direct sum of holomorphic discrete series representations of
G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
acts on
\mathrm{Hom}_{G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}\bigl(W,V|_{G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}\bigr)
irreducibly if the space is non-zero.

Remark 5.21
.

The discrete decomposability (or, more strongly,
G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
-admissibility) is well-known in
[
21
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Proof of Theorem
5.20
.

We denote by
V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}
the subspace of all
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-finite vectors in
V
.
Note that the center
Z
of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
acts on
V
via a character and

is compact.
This implies that
V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}
behaves as a underlying Harish-Chandra module for a semisimple Lie group with finite center.

Hence
V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule in
V^{\infty}
the space of smooth vectors.
Then
V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}
is isomorphic to a generalized Verma module
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
with finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-module

in the good range.
Similarly,
W_{K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}
is defined and isomorphic to a generalized Verma module
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}_{\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}}(F^{\prime})
with finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{k}
-module

in the good range.

Remark that
\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(F)
is
\mathfrak{k^{\prime}}
-admissible by Proposition
2.17
.
This implies
V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}=V_{K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Hence the irreducible decomposition of

gives the irreducible decomposition of

by taking completion.
In particular, the restriction map gives an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}\bigl(W,V|_{G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigl(W_{K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}},V_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}|_{\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}}\bigr)

of vector spaces.
This shows the assertion.
See
[
20
, Proposition 1.6]
for this isomorphism.
∎

In general (including non-holomorphic cases),

In fact, an element in

and

.
In the current setting,

actually acts on

.

Remark that Theorem

does not hold for unitary highest weight modules.
We shall give an example in which the

-module is not irreducible.

Example 5.22
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}(2n,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
Here
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
is the diagonal embedding in

be the even part of the Segal–Shale–Weil representation of

\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-submodule

5.4
Zuckerman derived functor module

As an application of Corollary
5.18
,
we consider the branching problem of Zuckerman derived functor modules induced from
quasi-abelian parabolic subalgebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},K)
be a pair with semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
, and
(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}},K^{\prime})
its subpair with reductive
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Suppose that there exists an involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{k^{\prime}}=(\mathfrak{g^{\prime}})^{\theta}
,
and there exists a connected reductive algebraic group
G
with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
such that
(\mathfrak{g},G^{\prime})
is a pair.
Fix
H\in\mathfrak{k}
semisimple in
\mathfrak{g}
with real eigenvalues
and define a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}\coloneq\mathfrak{q}(H)
.
Then
\mathfrak{q}
is
\theta
-stable.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}
is weakly
\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}
-compatible, and set
\mathfrak{q^{\prime}}\coloneq\mathfrak{g^{\prime}}\cap\mathfrak{q}
.
We use the notation
\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u^{\prime}},\mathfrak{l^{\prime}},\ldots
as in Section
2.4
.

Write
L_{K}
for the centralizer of
H
in
K
and set

.
Fix
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebras
\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{l}
, respectively, such that
\mathfrak{t^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{t}

denote half the sum of roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})
.

In this subsection, we consider Zuckerman derived functor modules defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)\coloneq R^{i}\Gamma^{K}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\bigr)

for an
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module

.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)
is a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.
In
[
16
]
, this module is defined by the Bernstein functor

,
and our parametrization is so-called unnormalized version, which is written as

.

Set

.
For an

-module

with infinitesimal character

, we say that

(or

Fact 5.23

Let

be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module with infinitesimal character
[\lambda]
.

1.

\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)

.

2.

\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)
is zero for

and non-zero irreducible for

.

Proposition 5.24
.

Let
F_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be a finite-dimensional irreducible
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module with infinitesimal character
[\lambda_{i}]
in the good range.
If
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},S}(F_{1})\simeq\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},S}(F_{2})
, then

holds.

Proof.

Set
\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
.
Then the symmetric form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is an inner product on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}

and
\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
is stable under the action of the Weyl group
W_{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
For
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we denote by
\Re(\mu)
the real part of
\mu
with respect to the real form
\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Fix a set
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
of positive roots of
\Delta(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
.
We may assume that
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are anti-dominant with respect to
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
.
By Fact
5.23
,
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},S}(F_{i})
,

, has the infinitesimal character
[\lambda_{i}+\rho(\mathfrak{u})]
.
By assumption,
\Re(\lambda_{i}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}))
,

, is regular and anti-dominant with respect to
{\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})\cup\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})}

Since

, there exists

such that

.
By the anti-dominance,

is identity and hence we obtain

.
∎

In a special case, the functor

can be computed by

.
It is a generalization of

Lemma 5.25
.

Let
V
be an
(\mathfrak{l},L_{K})
-module and

acting on

transitively.
Fix

.
Then there exists an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)\simeq R^{i}\Gamma^{M}_{M\cap L_{K}}\bigl(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\bigr)

of

-modules.

Proof.

By the construction of

(see before Proposition

), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q},i}(V)\simeq H^{i}\bigl(\mathfrak{k},L_{K};\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr).

Recall that
H^{i}\bigl(\mathfrak{k},L_{K};\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr)
is the cohomology of the complex given by

\displaystyle C^{j}\bigl(\mathfrak{k},L_{K};\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr)=\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{K}}\bigl(\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{l}_{K}),\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr).

See
[
2
, Section I.8]
.
Since
M
acts on

transitively, the restriction map induces a bijection

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}(K)\otimes W)^{L_{K}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\mathcal{O}(M)\otimes W)^{M\cap L_{K}}

-module

.
Note that

is reductive since

is affine.
Hence we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{K}}(\wedge^{j}\bigl(\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{l}_{K}),\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{M\cap L_{K}}\bigl(\wedge^{j}\bigl(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{K}\bigr),\mathrm{ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(M)\bigr)

of vector spaces.
It is easy to see that this isomorphism induces an isomorphism of complexes.
Taking the cohomology, we obtain the lemma.
∎

Example 5.26

We shall consider the branching problem of

Theorem 5.27

Let

5.6
Polynomial identity

One of the motivations of our study of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
-modules is to relate the multiplicities to algebraic properties of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{G^{\prime}}
.
We shall consider polynomial identities as one of the properties.

We recall the notion of PI degree, which estimates non-commutativity of algebras.
We refer the reader to
[
31
, Section 13]
.

Definition 5.34
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (unital associative)
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.
For a
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficient non-commutative polynomial
f
with
n
indeterminates, we say that
f
is a
polynomial identity
of
\mathcal{A}
if

\displaystyle f(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})=0,\qquad\forall X_{i}\in\mathcal{A}.

We denote by
\PI(\mathcal{A})
the set of all polynomial identities of
\mathcal{A}
, and set

\displaystyle\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})\coloneq\sup\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\PI(\mathcal{A})\subset\PI(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\right\},

which is called the
PI degree
of
\mathcal{A}
.

It is clear that if there exists a surjective homomorphism
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then we have
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})\geq n
.
Roughly speaking,
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})
is the maximum dimension of irreducible
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
This is true under a mild assumption (see Proposition
5.36
).

Let
s_{n}
denote the
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficient non-commutative polynomial with
n
indeterminates defined by

\displaystyle s_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})\coloneq\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sgn(w)X_{w(1)}X_{w(2)}\cdots X_{w(n)},

where
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the symmetric group of degree
n
and
\sgn
is the signature character of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

Fact 5.35

(
[
31
, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Fix an integer
n>0
.

1.

(
Amitsur–Levitzki
)

s_{m}\in\PI(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
for any
2n\leq m
.

2.

Conversely,
s_{m}\not\in\PI(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
for any
m<2n
.

In particular, we have
\mathrm{PI.deg}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))=n
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.
Assume that
\mathcal{A}
has at most countable dimension.

Proposition 5.36
.

1.

Let
\left\{M_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a family of irreducible
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
Suppose that
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{i})
is zero.
Then one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})=\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}.

2.

If
\mathcal{A}
is semiprimitive, then
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})
is the maximum dimension of irreducible
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Proof.

The second assertion easily follows from the first assertion.
We shall show the first assertion.

Assume that there exists
i\in I
such that
\dim(M_{i})=\infty
.
Here
\infty
means the cardinality
|\mathbb{N}|
.
By the Jacobson density theorem,
\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{i})
contains a subalgebra isomorphic to
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
This implies
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})=\infty=\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}
.
Hence if
{\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}=\infty}
, then the assertion holds.

Assume that
\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}<\infty
.
Then
\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{i})
is isomorphic to

for each

This implies
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})\geq\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}
.
By assumption, there is an injective homomorphism

.
Hence we obtain the converse inequality
\mathrm{PI.deg}(\mathcal{A})\leq\sup\left\{\dim(M_{i})\mid i\in I\right\}
.
We have shown the proposition.
∎

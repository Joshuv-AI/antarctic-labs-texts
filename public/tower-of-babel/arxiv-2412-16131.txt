Gradient integrability for bounded BD \mathrm{BD} -minimizers
Lisa Beck, Ferdinand Eitler, Franz Gmeineder

Abstract
. We establish that locally bounded relaxed minimizers of degenerate elliptic symmetric gradient functionals on BD ⁡ ( Ω ) \mathrm{BD}(\Omega) have weak gradients in L loc 1 ​ ( Ω , ℝ n × n ) \mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n}) . This is achieved for the sharp ellipticity range that is presently known to yield W loc 1 , 1 {\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1} -regularity in the full gradient case on BV ⁡ ( Ω , ℝ n ) \mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n}) . As a consequence, we also obtain the first Sobolev regularity results for minimizers of the area-type functional on BD ⁡ ( Ω ) \mathrm{BD}(\Omega) .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Aim and scope

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
be an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. In this paper, we deal with the regularity of relaxed minimizers of functionals

(1.1)

\displaystyle F[u;\Omega]\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f(\varepsilon(u))\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad u\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{n},

where
\varepsilon(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top})
denotes the symmetric part of the gradient of the map
u
and
f\colon\mathds{R}_{{\rm sym}}^{n\times n}\to\mathds{R}
is an integrand to be specified further below. Functionals of the form (
1.1
) are key to the study of the elastic or plastic behaviour of solids or fluids. In such theories, these functionals are used to model related energies in terms of the underlying displacement or velocity fields, respectively, while particular choices of the integrands
f
allow to model different aspects of the material or fluid; see, e.g.,
[
FS00
,
Lub90
]
.

Plasticity effects are usually accounted for by use of
linear growth
functionals
[
FS00
]
, and these constitute the starting point for the present paper. Specifically, we suppose that there exist constants
\gamma,\Gamma>0
with

(1.2)

\displaystyle\gamma\left|z\right|\leq f(z)\leq\Gamma(1+\left|z\right|)\quad\mbox{for all }z\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}.

This condition ensures that
F[-;\Omega]
is finite on the space

\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\colon\;\varepsilon(u)\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})\},

which is the symmetric gradient-variant of
{\rm W}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and endowed with the canonical norm
\|u\|_{\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\varepsilon(u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}
. Moreover, minimizing sequences with prescribed boundary values
u_{0}\in\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
remain bounded in
\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
, but by non-reflexivity of
\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
, they are not necessarily weakly relatively compact in
\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, integrands
f
satisfying (
1.2
) necessitate the relaxation of
F[-;\Omega]
to the space
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
of functions of bounded deformation. Different from the superlinear growth case
1<p<\infty
, there is no constant
c>0
such that
\|Du\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})}\leq c\|\varepsilon(u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})}
holds for all
u\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
. This key obstruction, also known as Ornstein’s
Non-Inequality
, implies that
{\rm W}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\subsetneq\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\subsetneq\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
(see, e.g.,
[
Orn62
,
CFM05
,
KK16
]
). Most notably, the full distributional gradients of
\mathrm{LD}
- or
\mathrm{BD}
-maps do not need to belong to
\mathrm{L}^{1}
or to the space of
\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
-valued Radon measures. Hence, results for linear growth functionals involving the full gradient cannot be applied in the current setting.

This motivates the quest for conditions on
f
such that relaxed minimizers genuinely belong to
\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
or even
{\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, finally striving for a parallel regularity theory to what is presently available for linear growth functionals involving full gradients on
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
; see
[
Bil03
,
Sch15
]
for overviews. This especially concerns the critical degenerate elliptic regime. To reach the latter, even in the full gradient case on
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
or for the closely related functionals of
(p,q)
-growth, it is then customary to impose additional boundedness hypotheses on (generalized) minimizers; see
Mingione
’s overview
[
Min06
]
. Such boundedness assumptions are natural from the perspective of applications, for instance the displacement
u
in (
1.1
) being confined to a bounded spatial region. Still, aiming to parallel the full gradient theory and thereby taking the local boundedness as a standing assumption, none of the previously developed strategies and techniques allows to cover the entire critical ellipticity range for functionals (
1.1
) subject to (
1.2
).

In this paper, we close this gap and thereby complete the picture of Sobolev regularity for linear growth functionals on
\mathrm{BD}
. To state our main result, Theorem
2.1
below, we give the precise set-up and its natural context first.

1.2.
Relaxations and generalised minimizers

In all of what follows, we suppose that the Dirichlet datum satisfies
u_{0}\in\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
. Defining
\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega)
as the
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)}
-closure of
\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, we then consider the variational principle

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{to minimise}\quad F[u;\Omega]\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f(\varepsilon(u))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{over}\quad u\in\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}\coloneqq u_{0}+\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega),

with a convex integrand
f\colon\mathds{R}_{{\rm sym}}^{n\times n}\to\mathds{R}
satisfying the linear growth condition (
1.2
). The lack of weak compactness in
\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
implies that solutions of the minimization problem (
1.3
) do not need to exist in general, and a relaxation to the space
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
is required. To provide a unifying framework, we put for a subset
U\subseteq\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial U
and maps
u,v\in\mathrm{BD}(U)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{v}[u;U]\coloneqq\int_{U}f(\mathscr{E}u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\left(\!\frac{\mathrm{dE}^{s}u}{\mathrm{d}\!\left|\mathrm{E}^{s}u\right|}\!\right)\mathrm{d}{\left|\mathrm{E}^{s}u\right|}+\int_{\partial U}f^{\infty}(\mathrm{tr}_{\partial U}(v-u)\odot\nu_{\partial U})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

where the behaviour of the integrand at infinity is captured by the
recession function

(1.5)

f^{\infty}(z)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{1}{s}f(sz)\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{{\rm sym}}.

By convexity and the linear growth condition (
1.2
),
f^{\infty}
is well-defined,
1
-homogeneous, finite and convex. We refer the reader to Section
3.2
for a detailed description of the single constituents in (
1.4
). The notion of (local) minimality to be employed in the sequel then is defined as follows:

Definition 1.1

(
\mathrm{BD}
- and local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers)
.

(a)

Given
u_{0}\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, a map
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
is called a
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer (of
F
) subject to the Dirichlet datum
u_{0}
if
\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega]\leq\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[w;\Omega]
holds for all
w\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
.

(b)

A map
u\in\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is called a
local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer (of
F
)
if
\overline{F}_{u}[u;U]\leq\overline{F}_{u}[w;U]
holds for all subsets
U\Subset\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial U
and all
w\in\mathrm{BD}(U)
.

As can be directly inferred from the definition, any
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer is a local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer. If the integrand
f\in\mathrm{C}(\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
is convex with (
1.2
), we have the consistency relation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}F[u;\Omega]=\min_{u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)}\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega].

Most importantly, by weak*-compactness principles on
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
,
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers do exist indeed, see Corollary
3.6
and Remark
3.7
below. Still, by Ornstein’s Non-Inequality, the full gradients of generic
\mathrm{BD}_{(\mathrm{loc})}
-maps need not be Radon measures, and it is thus a key theme to identify ellipticity conditions on
f
and lower order hypotheses on
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers that not only ensure the existence of
full gradients
as Radon measures but also their higher integrability. Here, a suitable scale is that of
\mu
-ellipticity, which we describe in detail next.

1.3.
\mu
-ellipticity,
(p,q)
-growth and previous results

In the context of convex
\mathrm{C}^{2}
-integrands of linear growth, the condition of
\mu
-ellipticity quantifies the degeneration of the second order derivatives of
f
. Specifically, given
1<\mu<\infty
, a
{\rm C}^{2}
-integrand
f\colon\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}\to\mathds{R}
is called
\mu
-elliptic
provided that there exist constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
with

(1.7)

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{2}}{(1+\left|z\right|^{2})^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(z)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{2}}{(1+\left|z\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad z,\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}.

Here, the condition
\mu>1
on the lower growth exponent is essential, as otherwise the integrands
f
are not of linear growth; see Remark
4.14
for more detail. Because of
\mu>1
, it is clear that linear growth integrands with (
1.7
) feature a different growth behaviour from above and below
on the level of second derivatives
. In this sense, they resemble integrands of
(p,q)
-growth. For the latter, improved gradient regularity estimates for minimizers are well-known to require a dimensional balance between
p
and
q
, whereas dimension independent thresholds necessitate additional hypotheses. Starting with the foundational work of
Marcellini

[
Mar89
,
Mar91
]
in the scalar case,
Mingione
et al.
[
ELM99a
,
ELM99b
,
Min06
]
in the vectorial case and subsequent contributions, typical bounds in this situation read as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{2}{n}\;\;\;\text{(unconstrained case)}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;q\leq p+2\;\;\;\text{($\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}$-constrained case)},

which can also be expressed as bounds
\tfrac{2p}{n}
and
2
on the difference
(q-2)-(p-2)
of the growth exponents for
\nabla^{2}f
.
Drawing this analogy for the relaxed functionals (
1.4
) in view of (
1.7
), one thus aims for higher local gradient integrability for
\mu<1+\frac{2}{n}
in the unconstrained case, whereas
\mu\leq 3
comes up as the natural threshold in the
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}
-constrained situation; here,
(q-2)-(p-2)
corresponds to
-1-(-\mu)
, while we have
p=1
in the bound
\frac{2p}{n}
. In the full gradient case and so for relaxed minimizers on
\mathrm{BV}
, these bounds have been achieved in
[
Bil02
,
BS11
]
; also see
[
Bil03
]
. This threshold is of intrinsic theoretical relevance, as it allows to include the
3
-elliptic
area integrand

\displaystyle f(z)=\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}},\qquad z\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}.

In the
symmetric
gradient case, however, only the unconstrained case has been tackled successfully so far by the third author
[
Gme20
]
for
\mu<1+\frac{2}{n}
. Previous results
[
Gme16
,
GK19
]
made use of fractional methods à la
Mingione

[
Min03a
,
Min03b
]
, but only gave the restricted range
\mu<1+\frac{1}{n}
. Still, the desired ellipticity threshold
\mu\leq 3
in the
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}
-constrained situation has remained open. As will be discussed in the following section, the sharp range
1<\mu\leq 3
for higher gradient integrability to be addressed here comes with more fundamental obstructions than in previous contributions. Yet, dealing with this borderline case, we thereby obtain a fully parallel Sobolev regularity theory to what is presently known for linear growth functionals on
\mathrm{BV}
.

2.
Main result and strategy of proof

We now proceed to display our main result and give a discussion of the underlying obstructions afterwards. More precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1

(Main theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be open and bounded, and let
f\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
be a variational integrand which satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.7
) with
1<\mu\leq 3
. Then any local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer
u\in\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
of
F

is of class

{\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
. More specifically, for every subset
U\Subset\Omega
there exists a constant
c=c(n,\Omega,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma,\Gamma,\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(U;\mathds{R}^{n})})>0
such that whenever
\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})\Subset U
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|\log(1+|\nabla u|^{2})\mathrm{d}x\leq c\Big(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)\Big(r^{n}+r^{n-2}+|{\mathrm{E}}u|(\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{2r}(x_{0}))\Big).

This result will be established in Section
4
. It can also be interpreted as a critical borderline case of the
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}
-constrained case for general
p<\infty
, which will be addressed in the follow-up paper
[
BEG
]
based on the methods developed here. In order to motivate the particular set-up and strategy of the proof, we now pause to explain the key difficulties and novelties in detail.

(a) Singular measures, lack of maximum principles and non-uniqueness.
To get access to the ellipticity assumption (
1.7
) imposed on
f
, it is natural to employ a version of the difference quotient method. Since the distributional symmetric gradients of local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers
u\in\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
a priori only belong to
\mathrm{RM}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
, the direct use of difference quotients is however difficult to be implemented. In this context, a routine device is to consider plain viscosity approximations, so minimizers
v_{j}
of the stabilized functionals

(2.2)

\displaystyle F_{j}[v;\Omega]:=F[v;\Omega]+\frac{1}{j}\int_{\Omega}|\varepsilon(v)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{for }j\in\mathds{N},

on suitable Dirichlet classes. Different from the full gradient case, see
[
Bil03
,
BS11
]
, we cannot utilise tools such as maximum principles or truncation arguments to infer that the sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
remains locally uniformly bounded in
\Omega
. As explained in
2
below, this however turns out to be crucial in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, and reveals a conceptual difference to previous contributions
[
GK19
,
Gme20
]
in the unconstrained case, where an approach based on (
2.2
) would yield estimates for one (local)
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer. Moreover, even
if
the sequence
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
remained locally uniformly bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

and
we could establish locally uniform higher integrability estimates on
(\nabla v_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
, this would only lead to a
{\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}
-regularity result for
at most one
local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer. Yet, this does not rule out the existence of a local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer
u\in(\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n}))\setminus{\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
: Since the positively
1
-homogeneous (and hereafter
not
strictly convex) recession function in (
1.4
) operates on singular measures for
\mathscr{L}^{n}
, the relaxed functional is
not
strictly convex on
\mathrm{BD}_{(\mathrm{loc})}(\Omega)
, and in general (local)
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers might be highly non-unique.

(b) Ekeland-type viscosity approximations and keeping boundedness constraints
.
To circumvent this issue, we implement an approximation strategy based on the Ekeland variational principle
[
Eke74
]
, see Lemma
3.8
below. In the context of linear growth problems, this strategy appeared first in
[
BS11
]
, and has subsequently been employed in
[
GK19
,
Gme20
]
for problems depending on the symmetric gradient. Essentially, working from a fixed locally bounded local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer, the Ekeland variational principle in combination with a suitable stabilization yields a sequence whose single members satisfy a suitable version of almost-minimality. The latter is quantified by a perturbation term and leads to an Euler-Lagrange
inequality
rather than an
equation
. To get access to degenerate second order bounds as the key ingredient of the proof, such perturbations have to be weak enough to be controllable by the available a-priori bounds. Simultaneously, they have to be strong enough to give useful estimates. As will be discussed in Section
4.1
, the natural Ekeland perturbation space then is the negative Sobolev space
{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
.

Here, and as argued above in
2
, it is even more important than in previous contributions to keep track of the local
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bounds. This necessitates the incorporation of an additional
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalization term into the functional, to be dealt with by Ekeland’s variational principle. The precise control of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norms of the Ekeland sequence is achieved by an adaptation of an idea appearing in
Schmidt
’s habilitation thesis
[
Sch15
]
, in turn being inspired by the penalization strategy from
Carozza
et al.
[
CKdN11
]
in the
(p,q)
-context. This leads to approximations

\displaystyle F_{j}[w;\Omega]=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}f(\varepsilon(w))\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{original functional}}+\,\underbrace{\delta_{j}\int_{\Omega}(1+|\varepsilon(w)|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}_{\text{viscosity stabilisation}}+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}g_{M}(w)\,\mathrm{d}x.}_{\text{$\mathrm{L}^{\infty}$-penalisation}}

Enforcing
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bounds in this way, a chief issue is that it is
a priori not clear
whether the Ekeland sequence remains away from the corresponding
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-threshold numbers. In fact, if a member of the Ekeland sequence attained the corresponding threshold, it would be impossible to extract
any
information from the Euler-Lagrange inequality, cf. Remark
4.3
.

By an overall set-up of the proof slightly different from previous contributions, we establish in Proposition
4.1

quantitative estimates
on the distance of the Ekeland sequence to the critical
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-threshold. This strategy, which is directly tailored to our purposes, hinges on a fine analysis of the blow-up of certain approximations in conjunction with a geometric argument. In consequence, from then on, we will have access to the requisite Euler-Lagrange inequality.

(c) Ornstein, algebraic manipulations,
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-constraints and their interplay.
As discussed in
2
, Theorem
2.1
is a consequence of degenerate weighted second order estimates. In stark contrast to the full gradient case
[
Bil02
,
BS11
]
and in light of Ornstein’s Non-Inequality, the derivation of such estimates must avoid the appearance of full gradients at all costs. Whereas robust fractional methods as in
Mingione

[
Min03b
]
can be employed here too, they do not allow us to reach the desired ellipticity exponent
\mu=3
from Theorem
2.1
. This, in turn, necessitates delicate algebraic manipulations that let us systematically re-introduce the symmetric gradients in the underlying estimates.

It is here where the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalization strategy from
2
has a substantially aggravating impact: Namely,
re-introducing
the symmetric gradients comes to the effect of
introducing
certain pollution terms (of skew-symmetric structure) that are a priori difficult to be dealt with. In view of Ornstein’s Non-Inequality, the only possibility here is to employ the Euler-Lagrange inequality or its differentiated version. As argued in
2
, the enforcing of uniform
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bounds
necessarily
requires
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalization terms, and the latter consequently appear in the Euler-Lagrange inequality too. When we aim to bring the appearing pollution terms into a form that is treatable by the Euler-Lagrange inequality, the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalization terms make it impossible to use the available a priori-bounds. Specifically, the Ekeland approximation set-up and degenerate second order estimates here are intertwined in a form that has not arisen in former contributions. The resolution of this matter, which requires a perhaps somewhat unusual set-up of the proof and comes with a slightly unnatural yet sufficient estimate as a chief outcome, is given in Section
4.3
, cf. Theorem
4.7
and Remark
4.10
.

(d) Uniform higher integrability: Admissibility, Korn and logarithmic losses.
Once the degenerate second order estimates from
2
are established, we are in a position to employ specific test functions along the lines of
Bildhauer

[
Bil02
]
. Similar to
[
BS11
]
, these specific test function are not a priori admissible in the Euler-Lagrange inequality. Whereas strategies as e.g. in
[
BS11
]
do not apply to the situation here, this issue is circumvented by a novel approach which also simplifies the proof in the full gradient case; see Remark
4.12
for more detail.

At this stage, all ingredients are available to establish Theorem
2.1
in Section
4.4
. Specifically, we arrive at uniform
\mathrm{L}\log^{2}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-estimates for the
symmetric gradients
of the Ekeland approximation sequence and thus, as a direct by-product of the Reshetnyak lower semicontinuity theorem (cf. Lemma
3.5
), at the absolute continuity
{\mathrm{E}}u\ll\mathscr{L}^{n}
. The logarithmic loss when passing to gradients is a consequence of the singular integral representation for
u\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{n};\mathds{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\nabla u^{(k)}=\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\sum_{i\leq j}\Big(\mathrm{p.v.}\partial_{i}\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{ij}*\varepsilon^{(jk)}(u)-\mathrm{p.v.}\partial_{k}\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{ij}*\varepsilon^{(ij)}(u)+\mathrm{p.v.}\partial_{j}\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{ij}*\varepsilon^{(ki)}(u)\Big)

with kernels
\mathfrak{K}_{ij}(x)=x_{i}x_{j}/|x|^{n}
and the Cauchy principal value
\mathrm{p.v.}
, see e.g.
[
Res70
]
. It is well-known that such operators come with the loss of one logarithmic power in the scale of Orlicz spaces. Following
Cianchi

[
Cia14
]
, this loss cannot be prevented, and so any improvement of Theorem
2.1
would require a conceptually different proof. This however would need to be compatible with the rather finely adjusted framework from Section
4.1
, see Remark
4.11
.

At present, even for the Dirichlet problem on
\mathrm{BV}
,
\mu\leq 3
is currently the best known and hence critical ellipticity threshold for which higher gradient integrability can be achieved, cf.
[
Bil02
,
BS11
,
Sch15
]
. Inspired by
[
BBMS17
]
, it is only in different scenarios such as Neumann problems on
\mathrm{BV}
where
\mu
is known to be allowed to be increased
[
BBG20
]
. This, in turn, happens at the cost of no quantitative integrability gain and the inapplicability of the method to the Dirichlet problem.

Lastly, we wish to stress that improved results can be obtained when passing from the critical threshold
\mu=3
to
\mu<3
, see Section
4.5
. In particular, this includes problems with logarithmic hardening, see
Fuchs & Seregin

[
FS98b
,
FS00
]
. Such problems for full gradient functionals have recently re-attracted attention, see
De Filippis
et al.
[
FM23
,
FFP24
,
FP24
]
, and are partially located at the borderline exponent
\mu=1
of the ellipticity scale considered here. In the setting of the present paper, one then has the a priori existence of the full weak gradients, simplifying most the arguments for the case
\mu=3
in a considerable way, see Remark
4.14
.

Structure of the paper

Apart from the first two sections, the paper is organised as follows: In Section
3
we fix notation, collect definitions and important background results on function spaces, lower semicontinuity and the Ekeland variational principle. In Section
4
we then embark on the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Here we will make use of two particular extension and approximation results, which are discussed for the reader’s convenience in the appendix, Section
5
.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Notation

We briefly comment on some of the notation used in this paper. We denote by
\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})\coloneqq\{z\in\mathds{R}^{n}\colon\;|z-x_{0}|<r\}
the open ball of radius
r>0
centered at a point
x_{0}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
. We write with slight abuse of notation
\mathbb{B}_{r}(\xi)\coloneqq\{\eta\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}\colon\;|\eta-\xi|<r\}
for
\xi\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}
, where we use
|\cdot|
for the usual Hilbert–Schmidt norm on
\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
, which stems from the inner product
\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\coloneqq\mathrm{tr}(\xi^{\top}\eta)
for
\xi,\eta\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
. It is useful to note that this inner product leads to the orthogonal sum decomposition

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathds{R}^{n\times n}=\mathds{R}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{n\times n}\oplus_{\bot}\mathds{R}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{n\times n}

into symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices. Moreover, we employ the notation
a\otimes b\coloneqq ab^{\top}
for the tensor product of two vectors
a,b\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
a\odot b\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(a\otimes b+b\otimes a)
for the pure (symmetric) tensor.

We denote by
\mathscr{L}^{n}
and
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue and
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measures, respectively, and we abbreviate
\omega_{n}\coloneqq\mathscr{L}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0))
. Whenever
S\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is a measurable set with
0<\mathscr{L}^{n}(S)<\infty
and
w
is an integrable function on
S
, we write
(w)_{S}\coloneqq\fint_{S}w\,\mathrm{d}x\coloneqq(\mathscr{L}^{n}(S))^{-1}\int_{S}w\,\mathrm{d}x
for the mean value of
w
over
S
. Moreover, for an open subset
\Omega
of
\mathds{R}^{n}
and a finite-dimensional inner product space
V
, the space of (finite)
V
-valued Radon measures on
\Omega
is denoted by
\mathrm{RM}_{(\mathrm{fin})}(\Omega;V)
; in particular,
\mathrm{RM}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Omega;V)\cong\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega;V)
. For
\mu\in\mathrm{RM}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Omega;V)
, the total variation measure of
\mu
is denoted by
|\mu|
, and the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodým decomposition into its absolutely continuous and singular parts with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu=\mu^{a}+\mu^{s}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}|\mu^{s}|}|\mu^{s}|.

Finally,
c>0
denotes a generic constant whose value may change from line to line and whose dependencies are usually indicated, while its precise value is only specified if it is of interest.

3.2.
Function spaces

We first collect some definitions and results on the function spaces which play a pivotal role in our paper. Throughout, let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set.

3.2.1.
Functions of bounded deformation

A function
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
is said to be
of bounded deformation
on
\Omega
provided its distributional symmetric gradient can be represented by a finite
\mathds{R}_{{\rm sym}}^{n\times n}
-valued Radon measure
{\mathrm{E}}u\in\mathrm{RM}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
. This can equivalently be expressed by requiring that the
total deformation

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{E}}u|(\Omega)\coloneqq\sup\Big\{\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\mathrm{div}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\colon\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{{\rm sym}}^{n\times n}),\;\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})}\leq 1\Big\}

is finite. The linear space of all functions in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
of bounded deformation is denoted by
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, with the local variant
\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
defined in the obvious manner. We refer the reader to
[
ACM97
,
Koh82
,
TS81
,
GR19a
]
for a detailed exposition on these spaces and proceed to give the relevant results required in the sequel.

We notice that the null space of the symmetric gradient
\varepsilon
for functions on
\mathds{R}^{n}
is given by the space of
rigid deformations
defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})\coloneqq\{x\mapsto Ax+b\colon A\in\mathds{R}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{n\times n},\;b\in\mathds{R}^{n}\}.

Moreover, for a bounded set
S\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
, which refers to a ball or a cube later on, we may introduce an
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-bounded projection
\mathbb{P}\colon\mathrm{L}^{1}(S;\mathds{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
. To this end, we first choose an
\mathrm{L}^{2}(S;\mathds{R}^{n})
-orthonormal basis
\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{M(n)}\}
of
\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
and define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}u\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{M(n)}\bigg(\fint_{S}\pi_{j}u\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)\,\pi_{j}\qquad\text{for }u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(S;\mathds{R}^{n}).

Since each of the functions
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{M(n)}
belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0);\mathds{R}^{n})
, the operator
\mathbb{P}
is indeed well-defined and
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-bounded.

For
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodým decomposition (
3.2
) of
{\mathrm{E}}u
into its absolutely continuous and singular parts with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{n}
reads as

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{E}}u={\mathrm{E}}^{a}u+{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u=\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{E}}u}{\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{E}}u}{\mathrm{d}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|=\mathscr{E}u\mathscr{L}^{n}+\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{E}}u}{\mathrm{d}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|.

Here, the density of
{\mathrm{E}}^{a}u
with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
can be identified with the symmetric part
\mathscr{E}u
of the approximate gradient
\nabla u
of
u
.

On
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, there are three different notions of convergence beyond the usual norm convergence which are of central importance: Given
u,u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, we say that the sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
converges to
u
in the

•

(
symmetric
)
weak*-sense
on
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
and write
u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
if
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
{\mathrm{E}}u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}{\mathrm{E}}u
in the weak*-sense of
\mathds{R}_{{\rm sym}}^{n\times n}
-valued Radon measures on
\Omega
,

•

(
symmetric
)
strict sense
if
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
|{\mathrm{E}}u_{j}|(\Omega)\to|{\mathrm{E}}u|(\Omega)
,

•

(
symmetric
)
area-strict sense
and write
u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\langle\cdot\rangle}}{{\to}}u
in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
if
u_{j}\to u
strongly in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
\langle{\mathrm{E}}u_{j}\rangle(\Omega)\to\langle{\mathrm{E}}u\rangle(\Omega)
, where we have abbreviated

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{E}}u\rangle(\Omega)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+|\mathscr{E}u|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|(\Omega)\quad\text{for }u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega).

We note that if
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
has in addition a Lipschitz boundary, then there exists a bounded linear (boundary) trace operator
\mathrm{tr}_{\partial\Omega}\colon\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, see
[
TS81
]
. Adopting the notation
\langle{\mathrm{E}}u\rangle(\Omega)
from (
3.4
), this allows us to state the following approximation result:

Lemma 3.1

(Symmetric area-strict smooth approximation)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be open and bounded with Lipschitz boundary oriented by
\nu_{\partial\Omega}\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and let
u_{0}\in\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)
. Then for every map
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
there exists a sequence
(u_{j})
in
u_{0}+\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
such that
\|u-u_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\to 0
and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{E}}u_{j}\rangle(\Omega)\to\langle{\mathrm{E}}u\rangle(\Omega)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{tr}_{\partial\Omega}(u-u_{0})\odot\nu_{\partial\Omega}|\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\qquad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Moreover, if
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, then there exists a constant
C_{A}(\Omega,n)>0
such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq C_{A}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }j\in\mathds{N}.

Lemma
3.1
is well-known to hold in the
\mathrm{BV}
-case, cf.
[
Bil03
, Lem. B.2]
, and its proof adapts in a straightforward manner to the
\mathrm{BD}
-case considered here to yield (
3.5
); see also
[
BDG20
, Prop. 4.24]
or
[
Gme20
, Lem. 2.1]
. This particular construction directly yields the additional
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-estimate (
3.6
), and we leave the details to the reader.

For our future purposes, we next record a lemma on an extension operator for
\mathrm{LD}
- and
\mathrm{BD}
-functions which preserves
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bounds:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega_{0}\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be two open and bounded sets with
\Omega\Subset\Omega_{0}
such that
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary. There exists a linear and
(
norm-
)
bounded extension operator
\mathfrak{J}\colon\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{BD}(\mathds{R}^{n})
with the following properties:

(a)

{\rm spt}(\mathfrak{J}u)\subset\Omega_{0}
for all
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
,

(b)

\mathfrak{J}\colon\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega_{0})
,

(c)

There exists a constant
C(\Omega,n)>0
such that
\|\mathfrak{J}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{n};\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}
holds for all
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
.

By Ornstein’s Non-Inequality, such an operator cannot be constructed by reflection and localisation as in the
{\rm W}^{1,1}
- or
\mathrm{BV}
-case. Instead,
\mathfrak{J}
can be obtained as a Jones-type operator
[
Jon81
]
as given by Raita and the third author in
[
GR19b
]
. To keep our presentation self-contained, a quick discussion thereof is provided in the Appendix
5.1
.

3.2.2.
Orlicz–Sobolev spaces

In order to accomplish the passage from symmetric to full gradients in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we require a refined scaled version of Korn’s inequality. To this end, let
A\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Young function, i.e.,
A
has a representation

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\text{for }t\geq 0,

with a non-decreasing, left-continuous function
a\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
which is neither identical
0
nor
\infty
. We define the Lebesgue–Orlicz space
\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
as the linear space consisting of all measurable maps
u\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{n}
such that the Luxemburg norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}

is finite. We then define the corresponding Orlicz–Sobolev space
{\rm W}^{1,A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
involving full gradients and the function space
\mathrm{E}^{A}(\Omega)
involving symmetric gradient by

\displaystyle{\rm W}^{1,A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n}):\nabla u\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{A}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\colon\varepsilon(u)\in\mathrm{L}^{A}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\}.

As usual, the full gradients and the symmetric gradients, respectively, are understood in the sense of distributions. For
\alpha>0
, we further set

A_{\alpha}(t)\coloneqq t\log^{\alpha}(1+t^{2})\qquad\text{for }t\geq 0

and write also
{\rm W}^{1,\mathrm{L}\log^{\alpha}\mathrm{L}}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{L}\log^{\alpha}\mathrm{L}}(\Omega)
instead of
{\rm W}^{1,A_{\alpha}}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
\mathrm{E}^{A_{\alpha}}(\Omega)
.

To conveniently derive the estimate of Theorem
2.1
, we now give the requisite scaled version of Korn’s inequality:

Lemma 3.3

(Korn-type inequality with scaling)
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. For every
\alpha\geq 1
there exists a constant
c=c(n,\alpha)>0
such that for every
u\in\mathrm{E}^{1,A_{\alpha}}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
there holds
u\in{\rm W}^{1,A_{\alpha-1}}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathds{R}^{n})
with

(3.7)

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\bigg(1+\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{|u|}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}x+\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{\alpha}(|\varepsilon(u)|)\mathrm{d}x\bigg).

Proof.

The proof is a combination of the results of
Cianchi

[
Cia14
]
. By scaling, it is no loss of generality to assume
x_{0}=0
and
r=1
. Due to
[
Cia14
, Thm. 3.13, Ex. 3.15]
, we have for all
u\in{\mathrm{E}}^{1,A_{\alpha}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0))
with a constant
c=c(n,\alpha)>0

(3.8)

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})}\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla(u-\mathbf{r})|)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\bigg(1+\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha}(|\varepsilon(u)|)\mathrm{d}x\bigg).

In the following, let
\mathbb{P}
denote the projection on
\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)
from Section
3.2.1
to the space
\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
of rigid deformations. By the equivalence of all norms on finite dimensional spaces and since
\dim(\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n}))<\infty
, there exists a constant
c(n)>0
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}|\nabla\mathbb{P}v|\leq c\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}|v|\,\mathrm{d}x

holds for all
v\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0);\mathds{R}^{n})
. Using that
A_{\alpha-1}
is convex and doubling in the first two steps, we have for any
\mathbf{r}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
with
(u-\mathbf{r})_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}=0

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:littlehelper}}}}{{\leq}}c\,\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla(u-\mathbf{r})|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\,\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla(\mathbb{P}(u-\mathbf{r})|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\,\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla\mathbb{P}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:littlehelper}}}{{\leq}}c\,\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla(u-\mathbf{r})|)\,\mathrm{d}x+cA_{\alpha-1}\bigg(\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}|u-\mathbf{r}|\mathrm{d}x\bigg)+c\,A_{\alpha-1}\bigg(\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:littlehelper}}}}{{\leq}}c\,\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|\nabla(u-\mathbf{r})|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\,\bigg(\fint_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}A_{\alpha-1}(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg),

where the last inequality is valid due to the classical Poincaré inequality and the convexity of
A_{\alpha-1}
. Since it does not matter whether the infimum in (
3.8
) is taken over all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
or over all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
with
(u-\mathbf{r})_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}=0
, we then may pass to the infimum over all
\mathbf{r}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathds{R}^{n})
with
(u-\mathbf{r})_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)}=0
in the previous inequality to conclude (
3.7
) for
x_{0}=0
and
r=1
. Since every function in
\mathrm{L}^{A_{\alpha-1}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0);\mathds{R}^{n})
automatically belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{A_{\alpha}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}(0);\mathds{R}^{n})
, this completes the proof.
∎

3.2.3.
Negative Sobolev spaces

For
k\in\mathds{N}
we define
{\rm W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
as the collection of all distributions
T
on
\Omega
of the form

(3.10)

T=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\partial^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}

with functions
w_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
for all multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathds{N}^{n}_{0}
of length
|\alpha|\leq k
. This linear space is canonically endowed with the norm

\|T\|_{{\rm W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\coloneqq\inf\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|w_{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})},

where the infimum is taken over all functions
(w_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|\leq k}
such that the representation (
3.10
) holds. Endowed with this norm,
{\rm W}^{-k,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
is a Banach space. In this paper, we only consider the cases
k\in\{1,2\}
, for which we notice the trivial embedding
{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\subset{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
with

(3.11)

\|T\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|T\|_{{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }T\in{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n}).

Let us further observe that for a function
w\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
we can estimate the
{\rm W}^{-k,1}
-norm of its derivative and finite difference quotient, and for
s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
h>0
there holds

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}w\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|w\|_{{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}w\|_{{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{s,h}w\|_{{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\{x\in\Omega\colon{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>h\};\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

The inequalities in (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) directly follow from the definition of the norm in the spaces
{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and
{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, respectively, while inequality (
3.14
) is obtained for a function
w\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
from the representation

\Delta_{s,h}w(x)=\partial_{s}\int_{0}^{1}w(x+the_{s})\,\mathrm{d}t,

and then follows for a general function
w\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
by approximation.

3.2.4.
Weighted Lebesgue spaces

Given a Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
and a finite dimensional inner product space
V
, we denote for
1\leq p\leq\infty
the
V
-valued
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-space with respect to
\mu
by
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(\Omega;V)
, equipped with the canonical norm. The following lemma on the identification of pointwise and weak limits might be well-known, but we have not found a precise reference and thus state it here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and consider
\mu=\theta\mathscr{L}^{n}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\makebox[7.0pt][c]{\rule{0.4pt}{7.5pt}\rule{5.0pt}{0.4pt}}}}{\mathrel{\makebox[7.0pt][c]{\rule{0.4pt}{7.5pt}\rule{5.0pt}{0.4pt}}}}{\mathrel{\makebox[5.5pt][c]{\rule{0.4pt}{5.25pt}\rule{3.5pt}{0.4pt}}}}{\mathrel{\makebox[4.0pt][c]{\rule{0.4pt}{3.75pt}\rule{2.5pt}{0.4pt}}}}\Omega
for some
\theta\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\geq 1})
. Suppose that
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{m})
converges

(i)

weakly in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{m})
to some function
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{m})
, and

(ii)

pointwisely
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. to some measurable function
v\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{m}
.

Then
u=v
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{m})
is a Hilbert space, the Banach–Saks theorem
[
Bre11
, Ex. 5.24]
implies the existence of a subsequence
(u_{j(k)})
of
(u_{j})
such that the corresponding Césaro means
v_{N}\coloneqq N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{j(k)}
converge strongly in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{m})
to
u
. On the one hand, passing to another subsequence
(v_{N(\ell)})
of
(v_{N})
, we can assume that
v_{N(\ell)}\to u
pointwisely
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
and therefore, by our assumption
\theta\geq 1
on the density of
\mu
,
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
\Omega
, as
\ell\to\infty
. On the other hand, as
u_{j}\to v
pointwisely
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
\Omega
, we necessarily have
v_{N(\ell)}\to v
pointwisely
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
\Omega
, and therefore, by uniqueness (
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
\Omega
) of pointwise limits, we end up with
u=v
.
∎

3.3.
(Lower semi-)continuity results

In this section, we collect some (semi-)continuity results which will prove instrumental in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

First, let
f\colon\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}\to\mathds{R}
be convex and of linear growth from below, meaning that the first inequality in (
1.2
) holds. Given an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
, we consider
\mu\in\mathrm{RM}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
and then define, based on the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodým decomposition (
3.2
) of
\mu
, a measure
f(\mu)
via

(3.15)

\displaystyle f(\mu)(U)\coloneqq\int_{U}f\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}|\mu^{s}|}\Big)\mathrm{d}|\mu^{s}|\qquad\text{for Borel subsets }U\subset\Omega.

If
f
is of linear growth also from above, meaning that also the second inequality in (
1.2
) is satisfied, then the recession function
f^{\infty}
of
f
defined as
f^{\infty}(z)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-1}f(sz)
for all
z\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}
is well-defined,
1
-homogeneous, lower-semicontinuous and convex on
\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}
with values in
[\gamma,\infty)
. As a consequence, there holds
f(\mu)\in\mathrm{RM}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Omega)
. However, we note that linear growth from above is not required in (

), so in general we only have

. The (semi-)continuity of functionals of the form (

) is ensured by

Theorem 3.5

(Reshetnyak (lower semi-)continuity theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set and let
f\colon\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}\to\mathds{R}
be a convex function of linear growth from below. For functions
u,u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
the following statements hold:

(a)

If
u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
in the
(
symmetric
)
weak*-sense in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, then we have

\displaystyle f({\mathrm{E}}u)(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}f({\mathrm{E}}u_{j})(\Omega).

(b)

If
u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\langle\cdot\rangle}}{{\to}}u
in the
(
symmetric
)
area-strict sense in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
, then we have

\displaystyle f({\mathrm{E}}u)(\Omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}f({\mathrm{E}}u_{j})(\Omega).

This theorem appears as a special case of the results of
Reshetnyak

[
Res68
]
(also see
[
BS11
]
). If the boundary
\partial\Omega
is moreover Lipschitz, one may extend functions

by a fixed map

to all of
\mathds{R}^{n}
to obtain an element of
\mathrm{BD}(\mathds{R}^{n})
. In conjunction with (
3.3
), Theorem
3.5
then yields the following result:

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. Moreover, let

be a convex function of linear growth satisfying (
1.2
), and define its recession function by (
1.5
). Then for every

, the functional

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega]\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f(\mathscr{E}u)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mathrm{dE}u}{\mathrm{d}\left|\mathrm{E}^{s}u\right|}\right)\!\mathrm{d}{\left|\mathrm{E}^{s}u\right|}+\int_{\partial\Omega}f^{\infty}(\mathrm{tr}_{\partial\Omega}(u_{0}-u)\odot\nu_{\partial\Omega})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

as in (
1.4
) is lower semicontinuous with respect to
(
symmetric
)
weak*-convergence in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
and continuous with respect to
(
symmetric
)
area-strict convergence in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.7

(
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers and consistency)
.

Based on Corollary

-minimizers and the consistency result (

Secondly, we record a lower semicontinuity result for functionals extended from Dirichlet classes to negative Sobolev spaces by infinity. Up to obvious modifications, so the treatment of lower order terms, the proof is identical to

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary,

is lower semicontinuous with respect to the norm topology on

3.4.
The Ekeland variational principle

As alluded to in the introduction, in order to deal with possible non-uniqueness phenomena of
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizers we use the Ekeland variational principle
[
Eke74
]
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
. We give here a version that is tailored to our purposes in Section
4
below:

Proposition 3.9

(Ekeland variational principle,
[
Eke74
]
,
[
Giu03
, Thm. 5.6, Rem. 5.5]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and let
\mathcal{F}\colon X\to\mathds{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a lower semicontinuous function with respect to the metric topology which is bounded from below and not identically
+\infty
. Suppose that, for some
u\in X
and some
\varepsilon>0
, there holds
\mathcal{F}[u]\leq\inf_{X}\mathcal{F}+\varepsilon
. Then there exists
v\in X
such that

(a)

d(u,v)\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}
,

(b)

\mathcal{F}[v]\leq\mathcal{F}[u]
,

(c)

for all
w\in X
there holds
\mathcal{F}[v]\leq\mathcal{F}[w]+\sqrt{\varepsilon}d(v,w)
.

4.
Gradient integrability and the proof of Theorem

We now give the proof of Theorem
2.1
. To this end, it is convenient to use the notation

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{\leq t}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\coloneqq\{w\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\colon\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq t\}\qquad\text{for }t>0.

4.1.
The Ekeland-type viscosity approximation

Towards the claim of Theorem
2.1
, which is a local result, let
u\in\mathrm{BD}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
be a local
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer of
F
. In particular, on each relatively compact subset
U\Subset\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial U
,
u|_{U}\in\mathrm{BD}(U)\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(U;\mathds{R}^{n})
is a
\mathrm{BD}
-minimizer of
F
on
U
with respect to its own boundary values.

In view of Theorem
2.1
, we may therefore directly assume that

(4.1)

\displaystyle u=u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\;\;\;\text{and define}\;\;\;m\coloneqq\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

We then proceed in three steps. As outlined in the introduction, we must simultaneously keep track of the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-constraint within the Ekeland approximation scheme, and get access to the corresponding algebraic manipulations which let us solely work with symmetric gradients. This necessitates a very careful approximation procedure to be displayed in Step 1. In the subsequent Step 2, we introduce the corresponding stabilised and
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalising integrands, finally letting us come up with the requisite Ekeland competitors for the main part of the proof in Step 3.

Step 1. Construction of regular minimising sequences to regularised boundary values.
In view of Lemma
3.1
, there exist a constant
C_{A}=C_{A}(\Omega,n)>0
and a sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
in
\mathrm{L}_{\leq C_{A}m}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\cap(u_{0}+\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n}))
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle u_{j}\to u\quad\mbox{in the (symmetric) area-strict topology on }\mathrm{BD}(\Omega).

For later purposes, we record that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq(C_{A}+1)m

with
m
being introduced in (
4.1
) above.
By Corollary
3.6
,
\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]
is continuous with respect to the (symmetric) area-strict topology in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the consistency relation (
1.6
) implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}F[u_{j};\Omega]=\lim_{j\to\infty}\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[u_{j};\Omega]=\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[u;\Omega]=\min_{\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)}\overline{F}_{u_{0}}[-;\Omega]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:consistency}}}{{=}}\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}F[-;\Omega],

meaning that
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
is a minimizing sequence for
F[-;\Omega]
in
\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}
.
Hence, passing to a non-relabelled subsequence if required, we may assume

(4.4)

\displaystyle F[u_{j};\Omega]\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}F[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{8j^{2}}\qquad\text{for all}\;j\in\mathds{N}.

For an open and bounded set
\Omega_{0}
with
\Omega\Subset\Omega_{0}
, we recall the extension operator
\mathfrak{J}\colon\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega_{0})
from Lemma

. In particular,
\mathfrak{J}\colon\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0};\mathds{R}^{n})
is a bounded linear operator with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm. We denote the corresponding operator norm by

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup\{\|\mathfrak{J}v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0};\mathds{R}^{n})}\colon v\in\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\leq 1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})\}

and define
\overline{u}_{0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{J}u_{0}
. We next consider a radial standard mollifier
\varrho\in{\rm C}_{c}^{\infty}({\rm B}_{1}(0);[0,1])
with
\|\varrho\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}({\rm B}_{1}(0))}=1
and set, for
\varepsilon>0
,
\varrho_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-n}\varrho(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. For each
j\in\mathds{N}
, we then choose

\varepsilon_{j}>0
such that the mollification
u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\coloneqq(\varrho_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\overline{u}_{0})|_{\Omega}
via convolution satisfies

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{8\operatorname{Lip}(f)j^{2}},\\
&\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}=\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{\infty}m\end{split}

seriatim. Having constructed the sequence
(u_{j}^{\partial\Omega})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
in
({\rm W}^{1,n+1}\cap{\mathrm{L}}{{}_{\leq\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{\infty}m}^{\infty}})(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, we then define approximate Dirichlet classes by

\mathscr{D}_{j}\coloneqq u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega).

For
\widetilde{u}_{j}\coloneqq u_{j}-u_{0}+u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\in\mathscr{D}_{j}
we then conclude on the one hand via (
4.5
)
1

(4.6)

\|\widetilde{u}_{j}-u_{j}\|_{\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)}=\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{LD}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{8\operatorname{Lip}(f)j^{2}},

and on the other hand via (
4.3
) and (
4.5
)
2

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|u_{j}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}+\|u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq(1+C_{A}+\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{\infty})m\eqqcolon M

for all
j\in\mathds{N}
. We next show that the infimum of
F[-;\Omega]
in the Dirichlet class
\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}
is approximated by the corresponding one in the Dirichlet class
\mathscr{D}_{j}
, which in turn is almost attained for the function
\widetilde{u}_{j}
. To this end, we first notice

|F[v;\Omega]-F[w;\Omega]|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\|\varepsilon(v-w)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})}\qquad\text{for all }v,w\in\mathrm{LD}(\Omega).

For any function
\varphi\in\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega)
we hence obtain the inequalities

\displaystyle F[u_{0}+\varphi;\Omega]\leq F[u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\varphi;\Omega]+\operatorname{Lip}(f)\|\varepsilon(u_{0}-u_{j}^{\partial\Omega})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})},

\displaystyle F[u_{j}^{\partial\Omega}+\varphi;\Omega]\leq F[u_{0}+\varphi;\Omega]+\operatorname{Lip}(f)\|\varepsilon(u_{0}-u_{j}^{\partial\Omega})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n})}.

Taking into account (
4.6
), we now infimise on the right-hand sides over all functions
\varphi\in\mathrm{LD}_{0}(\Omega)
and arrive at

(4.8)

\Big|\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}F[-;\Omega]-\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{j}}F[-;\Omega]\Big|\leq\frac{1}{8j^{2}},

while the specific choice
\varphi=u_{j}-u_{0}
in the second inequality in combination with (

) yields

(4.9)

F[\widetilde{u}_{j};\Omega]\leq F[u_{j};\Omega]+\frac{1}{8j^{2}}\leq\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{u_{0}}}F[-;\Omega]+\frac{1}{4j^{2}}

for all
j\in\mathds{N}
. Note that the sequence
(\widetilde{u}_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
has still the relevant properties of being smooth in
\Omega
with uniform
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bound and approximating

in the (symmetric) area-strict topology, but has more regular boundary values.

Step 2. Definition of the
{\rm W}^{1,n+1}
-stabilised and
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-penalizing integrands.
We next introduce suitable stabilised variational integrals defined in terms of
\widetilde{u}_{j}
. This will allows us to simultaneously keep control of the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bounds in the Ekeland variational principle in Step 3. To do so, we denote by
C_{\mathrm{M}}=C_{\rm M}(n,\Omega)>0
a constant for the Morrey embedding

satisfies the inequality

(4.10)

\displaystyle|w(x)-w(y)|\leq C_{\rm M}\|w\|_{{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\overline{\Omega}.

Equally, we denote by
C_{\mathrm{K},n+1}=C_{{\rm K},n+1}(n,\Omega)\geq 1

\displaystyle\|w\|_{{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq C_{{\rm K},n+1}(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}+\|\varepsilon(w)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})}).

We may assume the estimate

(4.12)

\|\varepsilon(\widetilde{u}_{j})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})}\leq\Upsilon(j)

for the blow-up rate of the norms
\|\varepsilon(\widetilde{u}_{j})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})}
with a convex and increasing
\mathrm{C}^{2}
-function
\Upsilon\colon\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\Upsilon(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. We then consider a convex and increasing function
h\colon[0,2)\to[0,\infty)
which satisfies
h=0
on
[0,1]
and, for
\frac{3}{2}\leq t<2
, is even strictly increasing with

(4.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\Big(\frac{4C_{\rm M}}{M(2-t)^{4}}\Big(1+2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Big(\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Omega)+\Big(\Upsilon\Big(\frac{1}{2-t}\Big)\Big)^{n+1}\Big)\Big)\Big)^{n(n+1)}<h(t).

The construction of such functions
\Upsilon
and
h
is elementary and is briefly addressed for the reader’s convenience in Appendix
5.2
.

Now let
g\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be given by

, where

(4.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle h(t)&\;\text{if}\;0\leq t<2,\\

We record that the function

is convex, lower semicontinuous, and its restriction to

is of class
{\rm C}^{2}
. We now define the perturbed integrands

\displaystyle f_{j}(z)\coloneqq f(z)+\frac{1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\quad\text{with}\quad A_{j}\coloneqq 1+\int_{\Omega}(1+|\varepsilon(\widetilde{u}_{j})|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For future reference, we compute

(4.16)

\displaystyle\nabla f_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\nabla f(z)+\frac{n+1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}z,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}f(z)+\frac{n+1}{2A_{j}j^{2}}(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\big((1+|z|^{2})\mathds{1}_{(n\times n)\times(n\times n)}+(n-1)z\otimes z\big),

for all

\displaystyle A_{j}\leq 1+2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Omega)+(\Upsilon(j))^{n+1}),

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\Big(\frac{4C_{\rm M}A_{j}j^{4}}{M}\Big)^{n(n+1)}<\widetilde{g}\Big(2-\frac{1}{j}\Big),

\displaystyle F_{j}[w;\Omega]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(\varepsilon(w))\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}g\Big(\frac{w}{M}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x&\;\text{if}\;w\in\mathscr{D}_{j},\\

Lemma 4.2

(Euler–Lagrange inequality)
.

Let

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\nabla f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j})),\varepsilon(\varphi)\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\nabla g\Big(\frac{v_{j}}{M}\Big),\frac{\varphi}{M}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varphi\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let

We conclude this section with some comments on our construction of the Ekeland-type approximation sequence:

Remark 4.3

(
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-threshold)
.

The specific strategy as displayed above is motivated by Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
. Here, the key point is the quantified
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-bound (
4.25
): If we only had the slightly weaker estimate
\|v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq 2M
, the Euler–Lagrange inequality could not be derived in the requisite form. Indeed, even for
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
,
F_{j}[v_{j}+t\varphi]=\infty
then could potentially happen howsoever small
|t|>0
might be. Compared with the BV-setting considered in
[
BS11
,
Sch15
]
, the bound (
4.25
) moreover controls the minimal distance of
\|v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}
to the threshold
2M
in a precisely quantified way. This is also the chief reason for considering
{\rm W}^{1,n+1}
-stabilisations (instead of
{\rm W}^{1,2}
-stabilisations considered in
[
Gme20
]
), letting us work with bounded
and
continuous approximations.

Remark 4.4

(On bounded minimising sequences)
.

In the
\mathrm{BV}
-setting of
[
BS11
]
, the specific minimising sequence
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathds{N}}
as chosen at the very beginning of the approximation scheme in Step 1
can a priori be taken to belong to
\mathrm{L}_{\leq m}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
. In the situation of
[
BS11
]
, this is achieved by possibly passing to the truncations

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}u_{j}&\;\text{if}\;|u_{j}|\leq m,\\
\frac{u_{j}}{|u_{j}|}m&\;\text{if}\;|u_{j}|>m,\end{cases}

which satisfies
|\nabla\widehat{u}_{j}|\leq|\nabla u_{j}|
. It is
not
clear to us how to show that the same function satisfies a similar pointwise bound exclusively involving symmetric gradients (possibly up to a constant), and so we are bound to stick to Lemma
3.1
.

\sigma_{j}\coloneqq\nabla f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j})),

where

.

Lemma 4.5

(Non-uniform second order estimates)
.

Let
f\in{\rm C}^{2}(\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
satisfy (
1.2
) and, for some
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
, the bound

(4.32)

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(z)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\qquad\text{for all}\;z,\xi\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}.

For every
j\geq j_{0}
with
j_{0}\in\mathds{N}
as in Proposition
4.1
there holds
v_{j}\in{\rm W}_{{\rm loc}}^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
with

(4.33)

\displaystyle(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2}\in\mathrm{L}_{{\rm loc}}^{1}(\Omega)\qquad\text{for all }s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Proof.

We take a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
, two radii
0<r<R<{\rm dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
, and a localization function
\varrho\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;[0,1])
with
\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\leq\varrho\leq\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{B}_{R}(x_{0})}
. Furthermore, let
s\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
0<h<\frac{1}{2}({\rm dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)-R)
. We recall that by construction we have
v_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and we now employ the function
\varphi\coloneqq\Delta_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})\in{\rm W}_{0}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
in the Euler–Lagrange inequality (
4.31
) from Lemma
4.2
. With the integration by parts formula for finite difference quotients and with
\varepsilon(\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})=\varrho^{2}\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})+2\varrho\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}
this gives

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta_{s,h}\nabla f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j})),\varrho^{2}\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})+2\varrho\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\Delta_{s,h}\nabla g\Big(\frac{v_{j}}{M}\Big),\frac{\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}}{M}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

(4.34)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\Delta_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

In order to be able to conveniently use the growth conditions on
\nabla^{2}f_{j}
, we rewrite

\displaystyle\Delta_{s,h}\nabla f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j}))(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)+th\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})(x))\,\mathrm{d}t\,\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)

via the fundamental theorem of calculus, and we then define, for
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})
, the bilinear forms
\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)\colon\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}\times\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}\to\mathds{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\eta,\xi]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\big\langle\nabla^{2}f_{j}\big(\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)+th\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)\big)\eta,\xi\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{for all }\xi,\eta\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}.

We next show that each of these bilinear forms is positive definite (which in particular allows for the application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality), with suitable lower and upper bounds. As an upper bound we infer from the formula (
4.17
) for
\nabla^{2}f_{j}(z)
combined with assumption (
4.32
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\xi,\xi]

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{2}}{(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)+th\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{n(n+1)}{2A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)+th\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(4.35)

\displaystyle\leq\Theta(n,\Lambda)\big(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)\right|^{2}+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x+he_{s})\right|^{2}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\xi\right|^{2}

for a constant
\Theta
depending only on
n
and
\Lambda
. Furthermore, using the inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}(n)(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1+|\xi+t\eta|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{for all}\;\xi,\eta\in\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n}

from
[

, Lemma 2.VI]
, we get as a lower bound

\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\xi,\xi]\geq\frac{\theta(n)}{A_{j}j^{2}}(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)|^{2}+|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x+he_{s})|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\xi|^{2},

for a constant
\theta
depending only on
n
. As a direct consequence of this lower bound we find

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta_{s,h}\nabla f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j})),\varrho^{2}\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}),\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta(n)}{A_{j}j^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

In a very similar way, we obtain from the convexity of the auxiliary function
g

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\Delta_{s,h}\nabla g\Big(\frac{v_{j}}{M}\Big),\frac{\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}}{M}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

This inequality allows us to take advantage of (
4.34
) in order to get

\mathrm{I}\leq-\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}),2\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}]\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{j}\|\Delta_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

Via the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we can reproduce term
\mathrm{I}
(with prefactor
\tfrac{1}{2}
) on the right-hand side, and after absorbing it on the left-hand side, we arrive at

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{I}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathscr{B}_{j,s,h}(x)[2\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j},2\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}]\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{j}\|\Delta_{s,-h}(\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})\|_{{\rm W}^{-2,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\coloneqq\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}.

Via Hölder’s inequality, the upper bound (
4.35
) and standard properties of finite difference quotients (see e.g.
[
GT77
, Chapter 7.11]
) we next estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Theta(n,\Lambda)\int_{\Omega}\big(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)\right|^{2}+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x+he_{s})\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\nabla\varrho\odot\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\Lambda)\|\nabla\varrho\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}^{2}\|1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega)}^{n-1}\|\partial_{s}v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}^{2},

which, in view of
v_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
, provides a bound for
\mathrm{II}
that is independent of
h
. Finally, by inequality (
3.14
) for negative Sobolev spaces, we have

\mathrm{III}\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varrho^{2}\Delta_{s,h}v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\|\partial_{s}v_{j}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

Collecting the estimates for
\mathrm{I}
from below and above, we obtain

(4.36)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})(x)|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\varrho\varepsilon(\Delta_{s,h}v_{j})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C,

where the constant
C
depends on
n,\Omega,\Lambda,j,A_{j}
and
\|v_{j}\|_{{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}
, but not on
h
. Therefore, the family
(\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j}))_{h}
is bounded uniformly in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathds{R}^{n\times n})
, which implies that
\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})
exists in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathds{R}^{n\times n})
.
By arbitrariness of
s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<r<R<{\rm dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
, Korn’s inequality shows
v_{j}\in{\rm W}_{{\rm loc}}^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, with the strong convergence
\Delta_{s,h}\varepsilon(v_{j})\to\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(K;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
for a given compactly supported set
K\subset\Omega
, we can select a suitable sequence
(h_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}}
in
\mathds{R}_{>0}
with
h_{i}\searrow 0
and
\Delta_{s,h_{i}}\varepsilon(v_{j})\to\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})

\mathscr{L}^{n}
-a.e. in

. Then, by Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K}(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{K}(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\Delta_{s,h_{i}}\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since the right-hand side is bounded by the previous reasoning, we have shown also the second assertion
(1+|\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})|^{2}\in\mathrm{L}_{{\rm loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
, which completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 4.6

(Differentiated Euler–Lagrange inequality)
.

Let
f\in{\rm C}^{2}(\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})
satisfy (
1.2
) and, for some
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)

4.32
). For every

with
j_{0}\in\mathds{N}

\sigma_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,\frac{n+1}{n}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and, for all

and
\varphi\in{\rm W}_{c}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\langle\partial_{s}\sigma_{j},\varepsilon(\varphi)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\partial_{s}\Big(\nabla g\Big(\frac{v_{j}}{M}\Big)\Big),\frac{\varphi}{M}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{j}\|\varphi\|_{{\rm W}^{-1,1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

We first show
\sigma_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,\frac{n+1}{n}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
(which in particular proves that the first integral in (
4.37

v_{j}\in{\rm W}^{2,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
due to Lemma
4.5
, we observe that
\sigma_{j}
is weakly differentiable with
\partial_{s}\sigma_{j}=\nabla^{2}f_{j}(\varepsilon(v_{j}))\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})
for
s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Since
\left|\nabla^{2}f_{j}(z))\right|\leq C(n,\Lambda)(1+\left|z\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
holds in view of (
4.17
) for all
z\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
, we obtain from Hölder’s inequality, applied with exponents
\tfrac{2n}{n-1}
and
\tfrac{2n}{n+1}
, the estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}\sigma_{j}\|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(K;\mathds{R}_{\operatorname{sym}}^{n\times n})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}\frac{n+1}{n}}\left|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{K}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{4}\frac{n+1}{n}}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{4}\frac{n+1}{n}}\left|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{K}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2n}}\left(\int_{K}(1+\left|\varepsilon(v_{j})^{2}\right|)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\partial_{s}\varepsilon(v_{j})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2n}}

for every compact subset
K\subset\Omega
. In view of
v_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,n+1}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
and with the local integrability (
4.33
) from Lemma
4.5
, all terms on the right-hand side are finite, which proves the first claim
\sigma_{j}\in{\rm W}^{1,\frac{n+1}{n}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. We next test the Euler–Lagrange inequality (
4.31
) from Lemma
4.2
with
\partial_{s}\varphi
for an arbitrary function
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathds{R}^{n})
. Via the integration by parts formula and the first inequality in (

Remark 4.10

For the estimation of the terms

\displaystyle\Phi^{\infty}(z)\coloneqq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(tz)}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|z\right|\log^{2}(1+t^{2}\left|z\right|^{2})=\begin{cases}0\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\left|z\right|=0\\
+\infty\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\left|z\right|>0.\end{cases}

With

the lower semicontinuity part of Theorem

(with the notation introduced in (

(4.57)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\Phi&(\mathscr{E}u)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\Phi^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathrm{E}}u}{\mathrm{d}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|}\Big)\mathrm{d}|{\mathrm{E}}^{s}u|=\Phi({\mathrm{E}}u)(\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0}))\\

with a constant

. In view of (

), this implies

in
\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})
. By arbitrariness of the ball

in

as well as

with the corresponding estimates.

Step 4.
{\rm W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\mathrm{L}\log\mathrm{L}}
-regularity.
To conclude the proof, we now invoke the Korn-type inequality (
3.7
) from Lemma
3.3
to find via (

)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|\log(1+|\nabla u|^{2})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{1}(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(r^{n}+\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{1}\Big(\frac{|u|}{r}\Big)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})}A_{2}(|\varepsilon(u)|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)\Big(r^{n}+r^{n-2}+|{\mathrm{E}}u|(\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{2r}(x_{0}))\Big)

for each ball
\mathrm{B}_{r}(x_{0})
with
\mathrm{B}_{2r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
, with a constant

. Since

,

) and the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Remark 4.11

(
{\rm W}^{1,\mathrm{L}\log\mathrm{L}}
-regularity for

)
.

The above proof yields that every locally bounded

Remark 4.14

.

If one admits

[\tfrac{3}{2},2)
as

h(t)\coloneqq\beta\Phi(t)+(1-\beta)\Phi(\tfrac{3}{2})\geq\Phi(t)\quad\text{for }t\in[\tfrac{3}{2},2)

for some constant
\beta>1
with
\beta\Phi^{\prime}(\frac{3}{2})>2\Phi(\frac{3}{2})
. Based on this choice, we then may perform a similar extension as above to obtain an increasing, convex
\mathrm{C}^{2}

Introduction

We study Cauchy problem for the fractional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (FNLS), namely,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}iu_{t}(x,t)-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u(x,t)+F(u)=0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\end{cases}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

where

and

denotes the fractional Laplacian with
\beta>0

F(u)=\pm(|u|^{\alpha}u)\quad(0<\alpha<\frac{2\beta}{n})

or Hartree type

F(u)=(|\cdot|^{-\nu}\ast|u|^{2})u\quad(0<\nu<\min\{\beta,n\}),

where
\ast
denotes the convolution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The model (
1.1
) was introduced in the
theory of the fractional quantum mechanics by Laskin through an expansion of the Feynman path integral, transitioning from Brownian-like to Lévy-like quantum mechanical paths
[
37

36

) with Hartree type nonlinearity, also known as Boson star equation, arises in the mean-field limit of large system of bosons, see
[
24
]
. This is also referred as Schrödindger-Newton equation or Choquard equation in another physical context
[

]

The goal of this article is to establish local well-posedness (LWP) and global well-posedness (GWP) for (

) having initial data in modulation spaces.

However, there is no useful conservation law in modulation spaces. This raises mathematical challenges to establish GWP for large data. In this paper, we aim to address this for (

Our method of proof is inspired from Bourgain’s high-low frequency decomposition method.
It was introduced by Bourgain
[
11
]
to establish GWP for NLS having power-type nonlinearity in
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{3}{5}
.
See
[
32
, Section 3.2]
,
[
52
]
and
[
50
, Section 3.9]
for more details. The main idea is to break down the initial data into a low-frequency/smoother component and
a high-frequency/rougher component. The choice of the cutoff for high and low-frequency component depends on the regularity of the data. The low-frequency component has finite energy after the cutoff, hence its evolution exists globally. On the other hand, the nonlinear evolution to the difference
equation for the high-frequency component has small energy in an interval of time of existence. Such smallness of the Duhamel term in the smoother energy
space allows the iteration to continue by merging this smoother component with the evolution of the low-frequency component. Chaichenets et al.
[
15
,
14
]
and Manna
[
41
]
have successfully adapted this method for classical NLS (
1.2
)
(\beta=2)
and classical Hartree eqaution (
1.5
) in modulation spaces respectively. In this paper, we extend this work to the case
\frac{2n}{2n-1}<\beta<2
for
n\geq 2.
We note that due to the non-locality and non-smoothness of the fractional dispersion operator
(-\Delta)^{\beta}
, the problem becomes significantly more challenging. Therefore, our analysis involves a new technicalities and we need to make a radial assumption on the initial data.

Generic surface homeomorphisms are almost continuum-wise expansive
Alfonso Artigue

Abstract
We show that for a compact surface without boundary M M the set of cw-expansive homeomorphisms is dense in the set of all the homeomorphisms of M M with respect to the C 0 C^{0} topology. After this we show that for a generic homeomorphism f f of M M it holds that: for all ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 there is a cw-expansive homeomorphism g g of M M which is ε \varepsilon -close to f f and is semiconjugate to f f ; moreover, if π : M → M \pi\colon M\to M is this semiconjugacy then π − 1 ​ ( x ) \pi^{-1}(x) is connected, does not separate M M and has diameter less than ε \varepsilon for all x ∈ M x\in M .

Introduction

It is a recurrent phenomenon in the history of dynamical systems that the chaotic behavior takes a primary role.
This happened in Poincaré’s study of homoclinic orbits, the Smale’s Axiom A and the hyperbolicity of the non-wandering set of structurally stable diffeomorphisms and the robustness of the Lorenz attractor, to name a few examples.
The purpose of this article is to show the generic role played by continuum-wise expansivity in the context of homeomorphisms of compact surfaces with the
C^{0}
topology.

A chaotic topological property which is present in hyperbolic sets and the Lorenz attractor is expansivity.
Recall that a homeomorphism
f
of a metric space is
expansive
if there is
\varepsilon>0
such that if
x\neq y
then
\dist(f^{i}(x),f^{i}(y))>\varepsilon
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In the context of surfaces this property is well understood.
Indeed, Lewowicz
[
13
]
and Hiraide
[
9
]
proved that expansive homeomorphisms of compact surfaces are conjugate to pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphisms, the two-sphere does not admit expansive homeomorphisms and for the two-torus they are conjugate to Anosov diffeomorphisms.
Some of their features are captured by a weaker notion introduced by Kato
[
12
]
called
continuum-wise expansivity
. A
continuum
is a non-empty, closed and connected subset of a given space.
We say that
f
is
cw-expansive
if there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
if
C
is a continuum with more than one point
then
\diam(f^{i}(C))>\varepsilon
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Cw-expansivity is much weaker than expansivity and it is known to show several different features.
For example, it is known that the sphere admits cw-expansive homeomorphisms
[
5
]
. Also, there are cw-expansive diffeomorphisms with wandering points which are robustly cw-expansive in the
C^{r}
topology, for
r\geq 2

[
4
]
.
There are cw-expansive homeomorphisms of the genus 2 surface with a point whose local stable set is connected but not locally connected
[
3
]
.
In
[
1
]
it is shown that such systems may have infinitely many fixed points.
This zoo of examples on surfaces indicates that cw-expansivity is scattered in the set of homeomorphisms of surfaces. The main result of this paper, Theorem
4.7
shows that cw-expansivity is
C^{0}
dense for surfaces. But we have two stronger corollaries.
We show that generic surface homeomorphism are extensions of cw-expansive systems, as explained in detail in the Corollaries
4.8
and
4.9
.

Contents of the paper. In §
2
we study general properties of decompositions, mainly for two-dimensional disks. We introduce the notion of
cw-transversality
(continuum-wise transversality) by requiring a totally disconnected intersection between the objects.
When these objects are local stable and unstable continua, the cw-transversality allows us to conclude cw-expansivity. The main result of this section is Theorem
2.6
which proves that cw-transversality is a generic property for decompositions whose plaques are meagre (have empty interior). The statement gives a precise meaning for this.

In §
3
we recall and develop some concepts of dynamical systems concerning cw-expansivity and almost cw-expansivity. For an arbitrary space we construct a natural
G_{\delta}
subset of homeomorphisms which are almost cw-expansive. If the cw-expansive homeomorphisms are dense, then this
G_{\delta}
subset is dense.
We study some examples. In particular we show that this
G_{\delta}

set may be dense even if the space admits no cw-expansive homeomorphism.

The main results of the paper are stated and proved in §
4
.

Decompositions

This section is related to the theory developed in
[
5
]
concerning
decompositions as local charts of topological foliations to model the distribution of stable and unstable continua of a given homeomorphism.

Let
(X,\dist)
be a compact metric space.
Let
\mathcal{C}(X)
be the space of subcontinua of
X
with the Hausdorff distance.
A
decomposition
of
X
is a partition map
Q\colon X\to\mathcal{C}(X)

which is upper semicontinuous,
i.e.
given
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that if
\dist(y,x)<\delta
then
Q(y)\subset B_{\varepsilon}(Q(x))
.

The
diameter
of a decomposition is

\diam(Q)=\sup_{x\in X}\diam(Q(x))
.
We say that a decomposition
Q
is

meagre
if each
plaque

Q(x)
has empty interior.

2.1
Restrictions and extensions

Suppose that
Y\subset X
is a closed subset.
For
Q
a decomposition of
X
define the
restricted decomposition

Q|_{Y}
of
Y
as

Q|_{Y}(y)=\comp_{y}(Q(y)\cap Y),\text{ for all }y\in Y

where
\comp_{y}(Z)
is the connected component of
Z
that contains
y
.

Suppose that
P
is a decomposition of the subset
Y\subset X
.
In this case we will say that
P
is a
partial decomposition
of
X
.
Define the
singleton extension
of
P
as the partition
P_{se}
of
X
given by
P_{se}(x)=P(x)
for all
x\in Y
and
P_{se}(x)=\{x\}
otherwise.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
P
is a partial decomposition of
X
then
P_{se}
is a decomposition of
X
.
If in addition,
X
has no isolated point and
P
is meagre then its singleton extension

is meagre.

Proof.

Suppose that
P
is defined in the closed subset
Y\subset X
.
To show that
P_{se}
is semicontinuous consider
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
given.
If
x\notin Y
, as
Y
is closed there is
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that
B_{\delta}(x)
is disjoint from
Y
. Thus,
\dist(y,x)<\delta
implies
P_{se}(y)=\{y\}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(x)=B_{\varepsilon}(P_{se}(x))
.
If
x\in Y
then there is
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
from the semicontinuity of
P
over
Y
.
Take
y\in B_{\delta}(x)
. If
y\in Y
then

from the semicontinuity over

.
If

then

.

Assume that
P
is meagre and
X
has no isolated point. For

we have that
P_{se}(x)=\{x\}
has empty interior because

has no isolated point.
For

, if

is open then
U\subset P(x)
. Since
P
is meagre,
U
is empty and the proof ends.
∎

2.2
Cw-transversality

Given a compact metric space
Y
and a continuous map
h\colon Y\to X
define the
pullback decomposition

h^{*}Q
of
Y
as

h^{*}Q(y)=\comp_{y}[h^{-1}(Q(h(y)))]

for all
y\in Y
.

Definition 2.1
.

An arc is
cw-transverse
to a decomposition if
it intersects each plaque in a totally disconnected subset.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to X
is a parametrization of an arc (
i.e.
it is continuous and injective)
then
\alpha
is cw-transverse to a decomposition
Q
if and only if

\diam(\alpha^{*}(Q))=0
.

For
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p\in B
let
\cont_{p}([0,1],B)
be the space of continuous functions
\phi\colon[0,1]\to B
such that

\phi(0)=\phi(1)=p
with the
C^{0}
topology.
The next result will be applied for
X
a two-dimensional disk (
n=1
in the statement).

Proposition 2.3
.

For
n\geq 1
let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed Euclidean ball centered at the origin
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
Q
is a meagre decomposition of
X=[0,1]\times B
then a generic
\phi\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
is cw-transverse to
Q
.

Proof.

For
\phi\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
define
\tilde{\phi}(x)=(x,\phi(x))
.
For
\varepsilon>0
let

U_{\varepsilon}=\{\phi\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B):\diam(\tilde{\phi}^{*}Q)<\varepsilon\}.

We will show that
U_{\varepsilon}
is open and dense in
\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
.
We start proving that the complement of each
U_{\varepsilon}
is closed.
Take a convergent sequence
\phi_{k}\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
such that

\diam(\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{*}Q)\geq\varepsilon
for all
k\geq 1
and
\phi_{k}\to\psi\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
.
This implies that there is a sequence
x_{k}\in[0,1]
such that

\diam(\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{*}Q(x_{k}))=\diam(\comp_{x_{k}}[\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{-1}(Q(\tilde{\phi}_{k}(x_{k})])\geq\varepsilon\text{ for all }k\geq 1.

Taking a subsequence we can suppose that
x_{k}\to y\in[0,1]
. The semicontinuity of the maps involved in the previous equation implies that

\diam(\tilde{\psi}^{*}Q(y))\geq\varepsilon
and
U_{\varepsilon}
is open.

We will show that
U_{\varepsilon}
is dense in
\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
.
Take any
\phi\in\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
.
By definition,
\tilde{\phi}^{*}(Q)
is a decomposition of the interval
[0,1]
into closed subintervals. Thus, only a finite number of such subintervals can have diameter greater than
\varepsilon
. Suppose that for
x\in[0,1]
we have

\diam(\tilde{\phi}^{*}Q(x))\geq\varepsilon
.
This means that for some subinterval
[y,z]\subset[0,1]
,
y\leq x\leq z
, the curve

\tilde{\phi}([y,z])\subset X
is contained in the plaque
Q(x,\phi(x))
.
Let
u=(y+z)/2
. Since
Q
is meagre the plaques of
Q
have empty interior and we can take
p\in B
close to
\phi(u)
and so that
(u,p)\notin Q(x,\phi(x))=Q(u,\phi(u))
.
Therefore, we can define
\phi_{1}\in C_{0}([0,1],B)
, close to
\phi
, in such a way that

\phi_{1}(a)=\phi(a)
for
a\in[0,y]\cup[z,1]
and
\phi_{1}(u)=p
.
For
\phi_{1}
we have that neither
\tilde{\phi}_{1}([y,u])
nor

\tilde{\phi}_{1}([u,z])
are contained in a plaque of
Q
.
Repeating this procedure finitely many times we obtain a map in
U_{\varepsilon}
which is close to the given
\phi
.

Therefore,
G=\cap_{j\geq 1}U_{1/j}
is a residual subset of

\cont_{0}([0,1],B)
.
By Remark
2.2
, each
\phi\in G
is cw-transverse to
Q
and the proof ends.
∎

2.3
Decompositions of disks

Define the sets:

•

the disk
D=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\leq 1\}
,

•

the circle

•

Let

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
\partial D

Q
.
For all
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that if

1-\delta\leq r<1

then

\diam(\pi(Q|_{A_{r}}(x)))<\varepsilon
for all

. This diameter of subsets of

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that for some
\varepsilon>0
there are a sequence

,

x_{n}\in A_{r_{n}}

\diam(\pi(Q|_{A_{r}}(x)))\geq\varepsilon\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

(1)

Taking a subsequence we can assume that

and

, where

is a subcontinuum of
\partial D
.
From (

) we have that

.
The semicontinuity of

.
This contradicts that

and the proof ends.

Fix

From Lemma

and

The homeomorphism

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that there is

We have that

, containg

.
As

, there is

.
We can take a subcontinuum

Take

On the other hand, since

Since

Also

.

.

are meagre decompositions of

\homeo_{\partial}(D)
.

.

and

For each

.
Applying Lemma

[(g_{j}^{*}P_{j})\cap Q_{j}](x)\cap\partial D_{j}=\emptyset\text{ for all }x\in\interior(D_{j}).

(2)

Define

.
Since

, we have that

3.1

4
Dynamics on surfaces

In this section we prove the main results of the article.
Let us first give some ideas about the proofs. To prove the density of cw-expansive homeomorphisms we use that structurally stable diffeomorphisms are
C^{0}
dense. It is known that a cw-expansive homeomorphism cannot have stable points, thus we need a small perturbation around attracting and repelling periodic points. For this purpose we combine two well known constructions. On the one hand, we consider the derived from Anosov diffeomorphism, that gives rise to an expanding attractor on the two-torus.
On the other hand, we have the antipodal quotient of the two-torus giving rise to a pseudo-Anosov map with 1-prong singularities on the two-sphere. From these examples we obtain an expanding attractor with 1-prong singularities which is contained in a disk. Each attracting and repelling periodic orbit is replaced by this expanding attractor, which is cw-expansive. At this point each stable and unstable manifold is one-dimensional. To obtain cw-expansivity we need another perturbation such that the intersections of local stable and unstable sets are totally disconnected. Such results on perturbations were collected in §
2
, the key is Theorem
2.6
.
To prove the results about genericity of almost cw-expansivity we mainly apply known results from
[
1
]
.

In this section we assume that
M
is a smooth, compact surface without boundary.

4.1

4.3
Density and genericity on surfaces

We state and prove the main results of the paper.

Theorem 4.7
.

If
M
is a closed surface then the set of cw-expansive homeomorphisms of

is
C^{0}
dense in
\homeo(M)
.

Proof.

From §
4.2
we start with a diffeomorphism

f_{0}
satisfying Smale’s Axiom A, the transversality condition on
stable and unstable manifolds and the no cycle condition. We will
C^{0}
approximate

by a homeomorphism in

Suppose that

is an attracting periodic point of

of period

.
Then, performing a suitable small

perturbation of

supported in a small neighborhood of the orbit of

defined in §

in the neighborhood of

Repeating this procedure around each attracting periodic orbit.
For repelling periodic orbits we do the same but considering the inverse
f_{PDA}^{-1}

In this way we obtain a homeomorphism

which is

Let us call

to the new pieces introduced by

The other pieces, the basic set common to

Thus, the non-wandering set of

In what follows we will approximate

by a

From what we know about the non-wandering set of

we can take

(5)

From the no cycle condition we have that

We have that all the invariant manifolds (stable and unstable) of

(those comming from

Let

such that each point of

Thus, to finish the proof it is sufficient to perform a perturbation supported on wandering points
near the singular attracting and repelling basic sets

Suppose that

Let

Each

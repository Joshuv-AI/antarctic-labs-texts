Farewell
Authors: Gregory J. Golladay
Published: 2024

This marks my final editorial for Arthroplasty Today, after a 5-year term. I’m proud of how much the journal has grown and how much we have accomplished, from increasing the volume and quality of published manuscripts, to developing a social media presence, to getting Clarivate indexing, and diversifying our board. It has been an incredible journey of growth for me as well, and I am grateful for all the people who have worked tirelessly to make Arthroplasty Today an increasingly relevant and cited journal.

Congratulations to the Program Committee, chaired by Jonathan Vigdorchik, MD, for a well-attended and highly informative American Association of Hip and Knee Surgeons Annual Meeting last month. It is certainly one of, if not the most important meetings for our specialty each year, and it takes a tremendous amount of volunteer work to put together. The staff at American Association of Hip and Knee Surgeons do so much in the background and deserve kudos for their efforts as well.

It is perhaps fitting that this issue is the largest we’ve ever compiled. In addition to a host of original research articles, there are several case reports, surgical technique articles, and systematic reviews of this issue. I would like to point out a few of the highlights. Telang et al [1] did a systematic review of activity restrictions following total hip arthroplasty. They found no significant differences in revision or dislocation rates based on activity but advocated for prospective studies to provide better evidence upon which to make activity recommendations. Patellar resurfacing remains a controversial topic, with resurfacing rates diminishing in the United States in recent years. Woelfle and Cooper [2] report on a single-surgeon experience with selective patellar resurfacing in total knee arthroplasty. They found poorer outcomes and higher reoperation rate, particularly among women, when selective resurfacing was employed. They recommended caution to surgeons considering this approach. Ramkumar et al [3] found a 14.4% difference in pay for female arthroplasty surgeons compared to their male counterparts, suggesting more needs to be done for gender equity within our specialty. Murray et al [4] describe an intraoperative radiographic view that can help identify liner malseating in dual-mobility constructs, potentially minimizing risk and future reoperation. Finally, the value of registry data is demonstrated in the article by Frisch et al [5], who used data from the Michigan Arthroplasty Registry Collaborative Quality Initiative to identify an underperforming implant. Such signals in registry date, when publicized, have the potential to protect patients and are of paramount relevance and importance.

Thank you for the honor of serving, first as Deputy Editor, then as Editor-in-Chief of Arthroplasty Today. I could not be more grateful to all those who helped the journal accomplish so much over this time. Brett Levine, MD, MS and Anna Cohen-Rosenblum are poised to assume duties as Editor-in-Chief and Deputy Editor, respectively, and Elizabeth Lieberman, MD will be taking over as our Social Media Editor. I anticipate a bright future ahead for the journal with the new leaders and wish them great success.

Quasilinear Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for parabolic equations with V ​ M ​ O x VMO_{x} coefficients
Rosamaria Rescigno

Abstract
. We study the strong solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for parabolic quasilinear equations with discontinuous data. The principal coefficients depend on the point ( x , t ) (x,t) and on the solution u , u, the dependence on x x is of V ​ M ​ O VMO type while these are only measurable with respect to t . t. Assuming suitable structural conditions on the nonlinear terms, we prove existence and uniqueness of the strong solution, which turns out to be also Hölder continuous.

1.
Introduction

Let
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
be a cylinder in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a
C^{1,1}
-smooth and bounded domain,
T>0,
and
\partial_{p}Q=\{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\}\cup\{\overline{\Omega}\times\{0\}\}
stands for the parabolic boundary of
Q.

We consider the following Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}u:=D_{t}u-a^{ij}(x,t,u)D_{ij}u=f(x,t,u,Du)\qquad&\text{a.e in }Q\,,\\
\phantom{\mathcal{P}u:=}u(x,t)=0&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q\,.\end{cases}

The classical theory concerning quasilinear elliptic and parabolic PDEs is well developed and can be found in various books (cf.
[
10
,
16
,
17
]
).
After the seminal result of Chiarenza, Frasca, and Longo
[
7
]
, that extends the unique solvability theory for linear elliptic PDEs with smooth coefficients to equations with discontinuous
VMO
coefficients, a lot of studies appeared dedicated to linear and quasilinear equations with such coefficients (cf.
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
14
,
19
]
) and nonlinear operators with coefficients close to
VMO
(see
[
6
,
23
,
24
]
and the references therein). Regarding the parabolic case, Krylov
[
13
]
weakened the
VMO
condition on the coefficient

\{a^{ij}(x,t)\}
assuming
partially

VMO
, that means that the discontinuity is of
VMO
-type
only
with respect to the space variables
x,
while the coefficients are allowed to be
only measurable
with respect to the time variable
t.
Later, his results have been used to study various initial-boundary value problems for linear parabolic operators with partially
VMO
principal coefficients, see
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
11
]
.

As it is known, the linear theory concerning existence and uniqueness of solitions to various boundary value problems is a starting point for the study of quasilinear and nonlinear PDEs under different conditions on the boundary. In this context, we can mention the work of Palagachev
[
22
]
where quasilinear elliptic equations with
VMO
-type discontinuity assumption on the coefficients have been studied for a first time. In that work the author evaluates the
VMO
modulus of the composition
a^{ij}(\cdot,u(\cdot))
that allows to apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem in order to get strong solvability of quasilinear Dirichlet problem. Similar result has been obtained later in
[
25
]
for parabolic quasilinear oprerators with Dirichlet and oblique derivative conditions on the boundary.

Our goal here is to extend these results to the case of quasilinear parabolic operators with partially
VMO
principal coefficients, relying on the linear results of Krylov
[
13
]
and the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem (cf.
[
22
,
25
]
).

Problems like (
1.1
) arise in various applications such as for instance the heat transfer, mathematical modeling of semiconductor devices, stock market modelling, etc.
Many elliptic and parabolic equations with discontinuous coefficients are often proposed in models of the deformation of composite materials, in the mechanics of membranes and films of simple non-homogeneous materials which form a linear laminated medium.

In what follows we use the standard notation:

for any measurable function
f\in L^{p}(Q)
we indicate the norm by
\|f\|_{p,Q};

denote by
D_{i}u
the partial derivatives
\partial u/\partial x_{i},

i=1,\ldots,n;

Df=(D_{1}f,\ldots,D_{n}f)
is the gradient of
f
and
D^{2}f=\{D_{ij}f\}_{i,j=}^{n}
is the Hessian matrix of
f;

the Sobolev space
W^{2,1}_{p}(Q)
consists of functions heaving distributional derivatives in
x
up to second order and one derivative in
t,
endowed by the norm

\|f\|_{W^{2,1}_{p}}(Q)=\|f\|_{p,Q}+\|Df\|_{p,Q}+\|D^{2}f\|_{p,Q}+\|u_{t}\|_{p,Q};

the letter
C
indicates a positive constant which value varies from one appearance to another and the standard summation on the repeated lower and upper indexes is adopted.

2.
Definitions and main assumptions

Let
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ |x-y|<r\}
be a ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
I_{r}(x,t)=\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)\times(t,t+r^{2})
be a
parabolic cylinder
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}.

For an arbitrary function
a\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
we consider the
mean oscillation
of
a
with respect to
x
in
I_{r}(x,t)
(see
[
2
,
13
]
)

{\rm osc\,}_{x}(a,I_{r}(x,t))=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{t}^{t+r^{2}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)}|a(y,\tau)-a(z,\tau)|\,dy\,dz\,d\tau

and denote

a_{R}^{\#(x)}=\sup_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\,\sup_{r\leq R}\,{\rm osc\,}_{x}(a,I_{r}(x,t)).

We say that
a\in VMO_{x}
if
\lim_{R\to 0}a_{R}^{\#(x)}=0
.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u(x,t)
is a strong solution of (
1.1
) if it is twice weakly differentiable in
x
and once in
t
with derivatives belonging to
L^{n+1}(Q)
, that is

u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q}),

u|_{\partial Q}=0,
and satisfies
\mathcal{P}u=f
almost everywhere in
Q
.

For our convenience we suppose that the data
a^{ij}
and
f
are extended as
0
for
(x,t)\not\in\overline{Q}
. This permits us to extend the assumptions on the coefficients in the whole space. Let
Q_{r}(x,t)=I_{r}(x,t)\cap Q
with
(x,t)\in Q
and
\Omega_{r}=\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
.
We study the strong solvability of (
1.1
), imposing the following conditions on the data.

(1)

a^{ij}(x,t,u)
and
f(x,t,u,p)
are
Carathéodory’s functions
, that is, these are measurable in
(x,t)\in Q
for all
(u,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and continuous with respect to the other variables for a.a.
(x,t)\in Q;

(2)

Strict parabolicity
of the operator
\mathcal{P}
: there exists a positive and non-increasing function
\lambda
such that

a^{ij}(x,t,u)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq\lambda(|u|)|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in }Q,\ \forall\,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

and the matrix
{\mathcal{A}}=\{a^{ij}\}
is symmetric;

(3)

a^{ij}(\cdot,u)\in VMO_{x}(Q)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)
locally uniformly in
u\in[-M,M]
for all
M>0,
that is, setting

\begin{split}a_{R}^{ij\#(x)}:=\sup_{|u|\leq M}\sup_{r\leq R}\sup_{(x,t)\in Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}(x,t)}\bigg(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{\Omega_{r}(x)}|a^{ij}(y,t,u)\\
-a^{ij}(z,t,u)|\,dz\bigg)dy\,dt,\end{split}

\gamma_{M}^{\#(x)}(R):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{R}^{ij\#(x)},

we require
\lim_{R\rightarrow{0}}\gamma_{M}(R)\rightarrow 0;

(4)

Local uniform continuity
of
a^{ij}(x,t,u)
with respect to
u
, uniformly in
(x,t)
:

|a^{ij}(x,t,u)-a^{ij}(x,t,\overline{u})|\leq\mu_{M}(|u-\overline{u}|)\quad\text{a.e. in $Q$}

for any
u,\overline{u}\in[-M;M]
. The function
\mu_{M}(\cdot)
is non-decreasing and such that
\mu_{M}(\delta)\to 0
for
\delta\rightarrow 0
. In addition
a^{ij}(x,t,0)\in L^{\infty}(Q);

(5)

Quadratic gradient growth
:

|f(x,t,u,p)|\leq\nu(|u|)(f_{1}(x,t)+|p|^{2})\qquad\text{a.e. in $Q$}

where
f_{1}\in L^{n+1}(Q)
and
\nu(\cdot)
is a positive and non-decreasing function;

(6)

Sign condition
on
f
with respect to
u
:

\dfrac{{\rm sign\,}u\,f(x,t,u,p)}{\lambda(|u|)}\leq\nu_{1}(x,t)|p|+\nu_{2}(x,t)\quad\text{a.e. in Q}

where
|u|>0
and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in L^{n+1}(Q)
are non-negative functions.

We are in a position now to announce our main results.

Theorem 2.2

(Existence)
.

Suppose that the conditions
(1)-(6)
are fulfilled.
Then the problem (
1.1
) has a strong solution
u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
.

Theorem 2.3

(Uniqueness)
.

Let the coefficients
a^{ij}
of
\mathcal{P}
be independent of
u
and the conditions
(1)-(6)
be fulfilled. Suppose, in addition, that
f(x,t,u,p)
is non-increasing with respect to
u
and Lipschitz continuous in
p,
that is, for all
p,p^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
it satisfies

(2.1)

|f(x,t,u,p)-f(x,t,u,p^{\prime})|\leq f_{2}(x,t,u)|p-p^{\prime}|\quad a.e.\text{ in }Q,

with
\sup_{|u|\leq M}f_{2}(x,t,u)\in L^{n+1}(Q)
for any constant
M>0
.

Then the problem (
1.1
) cannot have more than one solution.

3.
Auxiliary Lemmata

The first step in our considerations is to obtain boundedness of the solution, that is essential for the proving of existence . For this goal we use the maximum principle for nonlinear parabolic operators obtained by Krylov in
[
15
]
and developed further by Nazarov and Ural’tseva in
[
20
]
(see also
[
27
]
.)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
, be a solution of (
1.1
) under the assumptions (1),(2) and (6). Then
u
is essentially bounded and

(3.1)

\|u\|_{\infty,Q}\leq C(n)d^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{d}\left\|\nu_{1}\right\|^{n+1}_{n+1,Q}\Big)\|\nu_{2}\|_{{n+1},Q}

where
d={\rm diam\,}\Omega
.

Proof.

Set
Q^{+}=\{(x,t)\in Q\colon\ u(x,t)>0\}
for the subset of
Q
where the solution
u
is positive. In order to apply the maximum principle
[
20
,
27
]
we need to linearize the operator
\mathcal{P}
.
Dividing the both sides of the equation by
\lambda(|u|)
and applying the conditions (2) and (6)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{u_{t}}{\lambda(|u|)}-\frac{a^{ij}(x,t,u)D_{ij}u}{\lambda(|u|)}

\displaystyle\leq|Du|\nu_{1}(x,t)+\nu_{2}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{1}(x,t){\rm sign\,}D_{i}u\cdot D_{i}u+\nu_{2}(x,t)

where we have used that
|Du|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{i}u|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm sign\,}D_{i}u\cdot D_{i}u.

Consider now the linear operator

\overline{\mathcal{P}}u:=\sigma u_{t}-\overline{a}^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}u-\overline{b}^{i}(x,t)D_{i}u\leq\nu_{2}(x,t)

where
\sigma=1/\lambda(|u|),

\overline{a}^{ij}=a^{ij}(x,t,u)/\lambda(|u|),
and
\overline{b}^{i}=\nu_{1}\cdot{\rm sign\,}D_{i}u\in L^{n+1}(Q)
. It is strictly parabolic and denoting
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}=\{\overline{a}^{ij}\}
we have
\det\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}\geq 1
that implies
Tr\,\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}+\sigma>0.
Direct application of the Maximum principle
[
20
]
gives

\sup_{Q^{+}}u\leq C(n)d^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{d}\|\nu_{1}\|^{n+1}_{n+1,Q^{+}}\bigg)\|\nu_{2}\|_{{n+1},Q^{+}}.

Analogously, considering the subset
Q^{-}\subset Q
of points in which
u(x,t)<0,
we can estimate
\sup_{Q^{-}}(-u)
in the same way. Unifying the both estimates we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

The interior and boundary Harnack inequalities (cf.
[
12
,
18
]
) imply Hölder continuity of the solution of (
1.1
) and give an a priori estimate for it. This estimate allows us to control the modulus of continuity
\omega(r)
of
u
in
Q
that is,

(3.2)

|\omega(r)|\leq Cr^{\alpha}

where the constants
C
and
\alpha
depend on known quantities but not on
u
.

The assumption (3) implies discontinuity of the coefficients
a^{ij}
in
x
and
t.

In order to describe the discontinuity of the composition

a^{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,u(\cdot,\cdot)),

we need to estimate its
VMO_{x}
modulus. For this goal we use the ideas developed in
[
22
]
(see also
[
19
,
25
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(1),(3),(4)
hold and
u\in C^{0}(\overline{Q})
. Then

a^{ij}(x,t,u(x,t))\in VMO_{x}\cap L^{\infty}(Q)

with
VMO
modulus
\eta(r)
bounded in terms of
\|u\|_{\infty,Q}
and the modulus of continuity
\omega(r)
of
u
.

Proof.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q,
denote by
M_{u}=\|u\|_{\infty,Q}
and by
(3)
we have

\displaystyle J^{ij}:=

\displaystyle\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{\Omega_{r}(x_{0})}\big|a^{ij}(y,t,u(y,t))-a^{ij}(z,t,u(z,t))\,dz\big|\,dydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{\Omega_{r}(x_{0})}\big|a^{ij}(y,t,u(y,t))-a^{ij}(y,t,u(x_{0},t))\big|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\big|a^{ij}(y,t,u(x_{0},t))-a^{ij}(z,t,u(x_{0},t))\big|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\big|a^{ij}(z,t,u(z,t))-a^{ij}(z,t,u(x_{0},t))\big|\,dzdydt:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

after adding and subtracting suitable terms.
We note that
J_{1}
does not depend on
z
and by (
3.2
) we get

\displaystyle J_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}|a^{ij}(y,t,u(x,t))-a^{ij}(y,t,u(x_{0},t))\big|\,dydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}\mu_{M}\big(|u(y,t)-u(x_{0},t)|\big)\,dydt\leq\mu_{M}(\omega(r))

where we have used
(4).

Analogously,
J_{3}
does not depend on
y
and we can estimate it in the same way as
J_{1}
, i.e.,

J_{3}\leq\mu_{M}(\omega(r))

while
J_{2}
can be estimated via (3) obtaining
J_{2}\leq a^{ij\#(x)}_{R}.

Unifying all these estimates we obtain

\eta(R)=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sup_{|u|\leq M_{u}}\sup_{(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{r}}\sup_{r\leq R}J^{ij}\leq\mu_{M}(\omega(R))+a^{ij\#(x)}_{R},

where the right-hand side tends to
0
as
R\to 0
.
∎

The following a priori estimate is essential and permit us to determine the space in which we can apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem (see
[
19
,
22
,
25
]
and the references therein).

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume the condition (1)-(6) to be fulfilled. Then there exists a constant
C
independent of
u
such that

(3.3)

\|Du\|_{2(n+1),Q}\leq C

for any solution
u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
of the problem (
1.1
).

Proof.

Making use of the condition (5) and Lemmata
3.1
and
3.2
, we transform the equation
\mathcal{P}u=f
into

u_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}u+B(x,t)|Du|^{2}+f_{1}(x,t)u=G(x,t)

where

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&A^{ij}(x,t)=a^{ij}(x,t,u(x,t)),\qquad A^{ij}\in VMO_{x}\cap L^{\infty}(Q)\\[5.0pt]
&B(x,t)=-\frac{f(x,t,u,Du)}{f_{1}(x,t)+|Du|^{2}},\qquad\|B\|_{\infty,Q}\leq\nu(M_{u})<\infty,\\[5.0pt]
&G(x,t)=f_{1}(x,t)u+\frac{f(x,t,u,Du)}{f_{1}(x,t)+|Du|^{2}}\,f_{1}(x,t),\\[5.0pt]
&\|G\|_{n+1,Q}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{n+1,Q}\big(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})\big).\end{split}

Fixing
u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
at the data of the operator, we can consider the one-parameter family of problems

(
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
)

\begin{cases}D_{t}v_{\sigma}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}v_{\sigma}+B(x,t)|Dv_{\sigma}|^{2}&\\
\phantom{D_{t}v_{\sigma}}+f_{1}(x,t)v_{\sigma}=\sigma G(x,t)&\text{ a.e. in }Q,\\[5.0pt]
v_{\sigma}(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q\end{cases}

with
\sigma\in[0,1].

Suppose that there exists a solution
v_{\sigma}\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
of (

F
σ

) and write formally
v_{\sigma}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma)
to indicate it.

Then the question of the uniqueness of that solution for any
\sigma\in[0,1]
arises naturally.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}\leq 1
,
v_{1}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{1}),
and
v_{2}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{2})
. Then

(3.5)

\|v_{2}-v_{1}\|_{\infty,Q}\leq(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})).

Proof.

Let
w=v_{2}-v_{1}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{2})-\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{1}),
that is

\begin{cases}w_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}w+B(x,t)(|Dv_{2}|^{2}-|Dv_{1}|^{2})&\\
\qquad+f_{1}(x,t)w=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})G(x,t)&\text{ a.e. in }Q\\[5.0pt]
w(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q.\end{cases}

Further, we linearize the equation transforming the nonlinear term as follows

(3.6)

\begin{split}B(x,t)(|Dv_{2}|^{2}-|Dv_{1}|^{2})&=B(x,t)\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{i}v_{2}+D_{i}v_{1})D_{i}w\\
&=:B^{i}(x,t)D_{i}w\end{split}

where
B^{i}(x,t)=B(x,t)D_{i}(v_{2}+v_{1})\in L^{n+1}(Q).

Then we consider the linear problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}_{1}w:=w_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}w+B^{i}(x,t)D_{i}w&\\
\phantom{\mathcal{P}_{1}w:=}\qquad+f_{1}(x,t)w=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})G(x,t)&\text{a.e. in }Q\\[5.0pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{P}_{1}w:=}w(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q.\end{cases}

By (
3.4
) we deduce that

G(x,t)\leq f_{1}(x,t)(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})).

Putting
M=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u}))
we obtain immediately that

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}_{1}(w-M)\leq 0&\quad\text{ a.e. in }Q\\[5.0pt]
w-M\leq 0&\quad\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q\end{cases}

and hence the maximum principle (cf.
[
20
]
) gives
w\leq M
a.e. in
Q
by the maximum principle
[
20
]
.

Taking now
\overline{w}=v_{1}-v_{2}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{1})-\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{2})
and repeating the above considerations we obtain

\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}_{1}\overline{w}=-(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})G(x,t)\leq(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})f_{1}(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u}))=\mathcal{P}_{1}M\\
\overline{w}-M\leq 0\end{cases}

and by the maximum principle it follows that
\overline{w}\leq M
a.e. in
Q
and hence (
3.5
).
∎

An immediate consequence of the Proposition
3.4
is the uniqueness of the solution of (

F
σ

), providing that it exists. In fact, putting
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
in (
3.5
) we get immediately
v_{1}=v_{2}
.
Moreover, (
3.5
) gives an estimate for the norm
\|v_{\sigma}\|_{\infty,Q}
of any solution of the problem (

F
σ

).
In fact, setting
\sigma_{1}=0
in (
3.5
), one gets immediately

(3.8)

\|v_{\sigma}\|_{\infty,Q}\leq M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})\qquad\forall\ \sigma\in(0,1].

We can also notice that
v_{0}=\mathcal{F}(0)=0
and
v_{1}=\mathcal{F}(1)=u.

Our goal now is to estimate the norm of the gradient of the solution of (

F
σ

), providing that it exists.

Consider again two solutions
v_{1}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{1})
and
v_{2}=\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{2})
of (

F
σ

) such that
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}\leq 1
. Their difference
w=v_{2}-v_{1}
solves the linear problem

(3.9)

\begin{cases}w_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}w+f_{1}(x,t)w=F(x,t)&\text{ a.e in }Q\\[5.0pt]
w(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q,\end{cases}

where

F(x,t)=(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})G(x,t)-B(x,t)\big(|Dv_{2}|^{2}-|Dv_{1}|^{2}\big)

with a norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{n+1,Q}\leq

\displaystyle\,(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})\|G\|_{n+1,Q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\|B\|_{\infty,Q}\big(\|Dw\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}+2\|Dv_{1}\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}\big).

Choosing
\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}=\tau
small enough and using (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|F\|_{n+1,Q}

\displaystyle\leq\,\tau\|f_{1}\|_{n+1,Q}\big(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})\big)

\displaystyle+\,\nu(M_{u})\big(\|Dw\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}+2\|Dv_{1}\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\,C_{1}\big(1+\|Dw\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}+2\|Dv_{1}\|_{2(n+1),Q}^{2}\big),

where
C_{1}:=\max\{\tau\|f_{1}\|_{n+1,Q}(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})),\nu(M_{u})\}
. As it was already shown, the coefficients
A^{ij}(x,t)
belong to
VMO_{x}\cap L^{\infty}(Q)
and therefore the problem (
3.9
) has a unique solution which satisfies the a priori estimate

\displaystyle\|w\|_{W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)}\leq C\|F\|_{n+1,Q}\leq C_{1}(1+\|Dw\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}+2\|Dv_{1}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q})

with a constant
C_{1}
depending on the data of the problem but not on the solution. In order to approximate the norm of the gradient we adopt the Solonnikov inequality that is an anisotropic version of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality (cf.
[
21
,
26
]
) to the parabolic case, obtaining

\displaystyle\|Dw\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w\|_{\infty,Q}\|D^{2}w\|_{n+1,Q}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\big(M_{u}+\nu(M_{u})\big)\|D^{2}w\|_{n+1,Q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\tau\|w\|_{W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)}\leq C_{1}\tau\big(1+\|Dw\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}+\|Dv_{1}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}\big)

with a constant
C_{2}
depending on known quantities.

Choosing
\tau
smaller, if necessary, such that
C_{2}\tau<1,
we get

\|Dw\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}\leq C_{3}\big(1+\|Dv_{1}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}\big).

Since
v_{2}=w+v_{1}
, we deduce that

(3.10)

\begin{split}\|Dv_{2}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}&\leq\|Dw\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}+\|Dv_{1}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}\\
&\leq C_{4}(1+\|Dv_{1}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}).\end{split}

Taking
\sigma_{1}=0
and
\sigma_{2}=\tau,
that is
v_{1}=0
and
v_{2}=v_{\tau}
we obtain the a priori estimate

(3.11)

\|Dv_{\tau}\|^{2}_{2(n+1),Q}\leq C_{4}.

To prove the solvability of (

F
σ

) under the assumption
\sigma=\tau
, we consider the operator

\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{S}(Q)\longrightarrow W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})

which associates to any
z\in\mathcal{S}(Q)=\{z\in C^{0}(\overline{Q}):Dz\in L^{2(n+1)}(Q)\}
the unique solution
v\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
of the linear problem

(
\mathfrak{F}
)

\begin{cases}v_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}v=\tau G(x,t)-B(x,t)|Dz|^{2}-f_{1}(x,t)z&\text{ a.e in }Q,\\
v(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q,\end{cases}

and we adopt the notion
\mathfrak{F}(z)=v
to indicate this solution.
The existence of the last one follows by (
3.4
) and
[
13
]
.

The Sobolev embedding theorem implies a compact embedding of the space
W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
into
\mathcal{S}(Q)
that gives also compactness of the operator
\mathfrak{F}
considered as a mapping from the Banach space
\mathcal{S}(Q)
into itself.
Let us consider now the one parameter family of problems
\mathfrak{F}_{\rho}:\mathcal{S}(Q)\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{S}(Q)
defined as

(
\mathfrak{F}_{\rho}
)

\begin{cases}v_{t}-A^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}v&\\
\phantom{v_{t}}=\rho(\tau G(x,t)-B(x,t)|Dv|^{2}-f_{1}(x,t)v)&\text{ a.e in }Q,\\[5.0pt]
v(x,0)=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q.\end{cases}

If we can show that for any
\rho\in[0,1]
the norm of the solution of
\mathfrak{F}_{\rho}(v)=v
can be estimated via a constant independent of
\rho
than we will by able to apply the Leray-Schauder theorem that ensures the existence of a fixed point of the problem
\mathfrak{F}_{1}(v)=v,
which is exactly the solution of (

F
σ

) with
\sigma=\tau.

In fact, the desired estimate follows as (
3.8
) and (
3.11
), that is
\|v\|_{\mathcal{S}(Q)}\leq C
with a constant independent of
v
and
\rho\in[0,1]
.

To conclude the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we cover the interval
[0,1]
by a finite number of subintervals each one of length
\delta\leq\tau
. Then, iterating the above procedure and employing (
3.10
), we estimate
\|Dv_{k+1}\|_{2(n+1),Q}
in terms of
\|Dv_{k}\|_{2(n+1),Q}
for
k=0,1,\dots,m-1
with
m\delta=1
. Since (

F
σ

) with
\sigma=1
coincides with the original problem (
1.1
), having in mind the uniqueness of the solution to (

F
σ

) already proved, we obtain the desired estimate (
3.3
).
∎

4.
Main results

Theorem
2.2
(Existence).

To prove existence of solution of (
1.1
), we use one more time the Leray-Schauder theorem. Fixing an arbitrary function
v\in\mathcal{S}(Q)
in the data
a^{ij}(x,t,v)
and
f(x,t,v,Dv)
of (
1.1
), we linearize the problem obtaining

(
\mathfrak{F}_{2}
)

\begin{cases}D_{t}u-a^{ij}(x,t,v)D_{ij}u=f(x,t,v,Dv)\quad&\text{a.e in }Q,\\
u(x,0)=0&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q.\end{cases}

Then me obtain a linear strictly parabolic equation with bounded
VMO_{x}
principal coefficients and right-hand side belonging to
L^{n+1}(Q)
. Therefore, by
[
13
]
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
and we indicate it with
u=\mathfrak{F}_{2}(v)
.

The compactness of
\mathfrak{F}_{2}
acting from
\mathcal{S}(Q)
into itself, does follow in the same way as the compactness of
\mathfrak{F}
above.

To prove the continuity of
\mathfrak{F}_{2}
we take a sequence

\{v_{h}\}_{h=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)
such that
\|v_{h}-v\|_{\mathcal{S}(Q)}\to 0
and set
u_{h}=\mathfrak{F}_{2}(v_{h})
. Then we consider the difference
u_{h}-u
which is a solution of
\mathfrak{F}_{2}(v_{h})-\mathfrak{F}_{2}(v)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D_{t}(u_{h}-u)-a^{ij}(x,t,v_{h})D_{ij}(u_{h}-u)&\\
\phantom{D_{t}(u_{h}-u)}=\big(a^{ij}(x,t,v_{h})-a^{ij}(x,t,v)\big)D_{ij}u&\\
\phantom{D_{t}(u_{h}-u)}+f(x,t,v_{h},Dv_{h})-f(x,t,v,Dv)&\text{a.e in }Q\,,\\[6.0pt]
u_{h}(x,0)-u(x,0)=0&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q\,.\end{cases}

Further,
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
yields an a priori estimate of the solution

(4.1)

\begin{split}\|u_{h}-u\|_{W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)}&\leq C\Big[\|(a^{ij}(\cdot,v_{h})-a^{ij}(\cdot,v))D_{ij}u\|_{n+1,Q}\\
&+\|f(\cdot,v_{h},Dv_{h})-f(\cdot,v,Dv)\|_{n+1,Q}\Big]\\
&\leq\ C\Big[\|\mu(v_{h}-v)D_{ij}u\|_{n+1,Q}\\
&+\|f(\cdot,v_{h},Dv_{h})-f(\cdot,v,Dv)\|_{n+1,Q}\Big]\end{split}

Condition (4) ensures the convergence of the first term while condition (5) ensures the continuity of the Nemytskii operator

\mathcal{S}(Q)\ni v\mapsto f(x,t,v,Dv)\in L^{n+1}(Q).

Hence, the right-hand side in (
4.1
) tends to zero when
h\rightarrow\infty
which gives continuity of
\mathfrak{F}_{2}
.
Further on, the estimates (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) ensure
\|u\|_{\mathcal{S}(Q)}\leq C
with a constant independent of
u
and
\rho\in[0,1]
. This ensures existence of a fixed point
u\in\mathcal{S}(Q)
of the operator
\mathfrak{F}_{2},
which belongs to
W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
by the definition of
\mathfrak{F}_{2},
and this implies existence of solution to (
1.1
).
∎

Theorem
2.3
(Uniqueness).

We argue by contradiction.
Supposing that
u,v\in W^{2,1}_{n+1}(Q)\cap C(\overline{Q})
are two solutions of (
1.1
), then

\begin{cases}D_{t}(u-v)-a^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}(u-v)=f(x,t,u,Du)-f(x,t,v,Dv)&\text{ a.e in }Q\,,\\
u-v=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q\,.\end{cases}

Let
Q^{+}=\{(x,t)\in Q:u(x,t)>v(x,t)\}
. Since
f(x,t,z,p)
is non-increasing in
z
, it follows that
f(x,t,u,Du)-f(x,t,v,Du)\leq 0
for a.a.
(x,t)\in Q^{+}.
Later on, using the condition (
2.1
) and denoting
M_{v}=\|v\|_{\infty,Q}
, we can rewrite the equation above as follows

\displaystyle D_{t}(u-v)

\displaystyle-a^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}(u-v)=f(x,t,u,Du)-f(x,t,v,Du)

\displaystyle+f(x,t,v,Du)-f(x,t,v,Dv)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|v|\leq M_{v}}f_{2}(x,t,v)|Du-Dv|

\displaystyle=\sup_{|v|\leq M_{v}}f_{2}(x,t,v)\sum_{i=1}^{n}({\rm sign\,}D_{i}(u-v))D_{i}(u-v).

Therefore taking
b^{i}=-\sup_{|v|\leq M_{v}}f_{2}(x,t,v)\cdot{\rm sign\,}D_{i}(u-v)
we obtain the problem

\begin{cases}D_{t}(u-v)-a^{ij}(x,t)D_{ij}(u-v)+b^{i}D_{i}(u-v)\leq 0&\text{ a.e in }Q\\
u-v=0&\text{ on }\partial_{p}Q\,.\end{cases}

The maximum principle gives
\max_{Q^{+}}(u-v)\leq 0
. Analogously, taking the set
Q^{-}=\{(x,t)\in Q:v>u\}
we get
\max_{Q^{-}}(v-u)\leq 0
which gives
u=v
in
Q
and thus the claim of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Stochastic unravelings for trace-nonpreserving open quantum system dynamics
Federico Settimo, Kimmo Luoma, Dariusz Chruściński, Bassano Vacchini, Andrea Smirne, Jyrki Piilo

Abstract
Stochastic unravelings allow to efficiently simulate open system dynamics, yet their application has traditionally been restricted to master equations that preserve both Hermiticity and trace. In this work, we introduce a general framework that extends piecewise-deterministic unravelings to arbitrary trace-nonpreserving master equations, requiring only positivity and Hermiticity of the dynamics. Our approach includes, as special cases, unravelings of arbitrary dynamics in the Heisenberg picture, evolutions interpolating between fully Lindblad and non-Hermitian Hamiltonian generators, and equations employed in the derivation of full counting statistics, for which we show it can be used to obtain the moments of the associated probability distribution. The framework is suitable for both trace-decreasing and trace-increasing processes through stochastic disappearance and replication of the stochastic realizations, and it is compatible with different unraveling schemes and with reverse jumps in the non-Markovian regime. Thereby, our approach provides a powerful and versatile simulation method that significantly broadens the applicability of stochastic techniques for open system dynamics.

Trace-nonpreserving master equations.

Any ME preserving positivity and Hermiticity can be written as

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\mathcal{L}[\rho]=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}L_{j}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\Gamma,\rho\right\},

(1)

where
\Gamma
is an arbitrary self-adjoint operator and all rates and operators can depend explicitly on time.
If all rates
\gamma_{j}
are non-negative the dynamics is guaranteed to preserve positivity, however this is not also a necessary condition.
Such ME preserves the trace iff
\Gamma=\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j}\eqqcolon\Gamma_{L}
, as in the generalized Lindblad ME
31
;
41
.
In the general case, the time evolution of the trace reads

\frac{d}{dt}{\operatorname{tr}}[\rho]={\operatorname{tr}}\mathcal{L}[\rho]={\operatorname{tr}}\left[\left(\Gamma_{L}-\Gamma\right)\rho\right].

(2)

We refer to equations of the form (
1
) as TNP MEs.

Let
\Lambda_{t}
be the solution of the ME (
1
), then the resulting dynamics is said to be (C)P divisible if the map
\Lambda_{t,s}\coloneqq\Lambda_{t}\Lambda_{s}^{-1}
describing the time evolution from time
s
to time
t>s
is (completely) positive.
Divisibility has been widely studied in open systems and connected to memory effects and quantum non-Markovianity, both for TP

;
38
;
58
;
13

61
.
Furthermore, (C)P divisibility determines whether a particular unraveling scheme is suitable or not for simulating the underlying dynamics.

Unraveling schemes for the trace decreasing case have been proposed by dealing with non-linear MEs

Other examples in which TNP MEs naturally appear encompass situations in which the loss of particles is taken into account

Unravelings for trace-nonpreserving master equations.

When applying unraveling to trace-preserving MEs, i.e. for
\Gamma=\Gamma_{L}=\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j}
the time evolution of the density matrix is obtained as the average over pure state realizations

\rho(t)=\sum_{i}\frac{N_{i}(t)}{N}{\ket{\psi_{i}(t)}\bra{\psi_{i}(t)}},

(3)

where
N_{i}(t)
is the number of realizations in the state
\psi_{i}(t)
and
N=\sum_{i}N_{i}(0)
.
Trace-preservation follows from the conservation of the total number of stochastic realizations
\sum_{i}N_{i}(t)=N
.
In order to generalize the unravelings to TNP MEs (
1
) while keeping the normalization of the state vector, one needs to allow for the total number of realizations to vary in time.
The evolution of the trace is then given by the ratio between the number of realizations at time
t
and the number at the initial time:
{\operatorname{tr}}\rho(t)=\sum_{i}N_{i}(t)/N\neq 1
.

In order to extend unravelings to TNP MEs, let us consider the well-known Monte-Carlo wave function (MCWF) unravelings
23
;
19
;
48
;
55
.
Notice that our method does not depend on the particular unraveling scheme considered, but it is general.
Let
\psi
denote the current state of the stochastic realization; the MCWF method is a PDP in which the jumps are of the form

\ket{\psi}\mapsto\frac{L_{j}\ket{\psi}}{\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi}\right\rVert}\eqqcolon\ket{\psi_{j}}

(4)

happening between time
t
and
t+dt
with probability

p_{j}=\gamma_{j}\,\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}\,dt.

(5)

The deterministic evolution is given by

\ket{\psi}\mapsto\frac{({\mathbbm{1}}-i\,K\,dt)\ket{\psi}}{\left\lVert({\mathbbm{1}}-i\,K\,dt)\ket{\psi}\right\rVert}\eqqcolon\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}},

(6)

happening with probability

p_{\text{det}}=\left\lVert({\mathbbm{1}}-i\,K\,dt)\ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2},

(7)

where we introduced the non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian

K=H-\frac{i}{2}\Gamma.

(8)

Notice that the MCWF method can be applied only when all rates
\gamma_{j}
are non-negative at all times, otherwise it would lead to negative probabilities.
Such condition is equivalent to CP divisibility of the dynamical map.

Let
p_{J}\coloneqq\sum_{j}p_{j}
denote the total jump probability.
Whenever the ME (
1
) is TP, i.e. whenever
\Gamma=\Gamma_{L}
, then
p_{\text{det}}+p_{J}=1
.
In the general case, however, the probabilities do not sum to one

p_{T}=p_{\text{det}}+p_{J}=1+dt\,\braket{\Gamma_{L}-\Gamma}_{\psi}\neq 1,

(9)

where
\braket{X}_{\psi}=\braket{\psi|X|\psi}
.
This total probability
p_{T}
can generally be smaller or greater than
1
, with the difference corresponding to the change in trace for the particular realization.

p_{T}-1=dt\,\frac{d}{dt}{\operatorname{tr}}[{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}].

(10)

Notice that
p_{T}<1
(
p_{T}>1
) for one particular realization does not imply it for all other realizations, nor a decrease (increase) of trace for the average state
\rho
.

Let us first consider the case
p_{T}<1
.
Since the sum of jump and deterministic probability is smaller than one, we can think of adding an extra process happening with the remaining probability.
Since the trace is decreasing, it is natural for this process to be the disappearance of the stochastic realization
\ket{\psi}\mapsto 0
with probability

p_{\text{d}}=1-p_{T}=\braket{\Gamma-\Gamma_{L}}_{\psi}\,dt\geq 0.

(11)

Therefore, the realization
\psi
can either jump, evolve deterministically, or disappear and therefore not counting anymore in the average of Eq. (
3
).
The corresponding probabilities are positive and correctly normalized to one.

For the case
p_{T}>1
, one cannot indeed add an extra event, since the sum of all probabilities is larger than
1
.
Instead, we include a new process, corresponding to the creation of a new copy in the same state
\psi
.
Such process happens with probability

p_{\text{c}}=p_{T}-1=\braket{\Gamma_{L}-\Gamma}_{\psi}\,dt\geq 0

(12)

and is statistically independent of the jump or deterministic evolution.
Accordingly, the probability of not creating a copy is
p_{\text{n}}=1-p_{\text{c}}
.
Once one has evaluated whether a copy is created or not, the original realization
\psi
can evolve either via a jump or deterministically, but the corresponding probabilities must be renormalized in order to sum to 1 as

p_{j}^{\prime}=p_{j},\qquad p_{\text{det}}^{\prime}=1-p_{J}.

(13)

Notice that the two copies evolve independently and that the event in which the creation of a copy is followed by a jump happens with probability
O(dt^{2})
and, as it is usually the case for stochastic unravelings, we only compute terms up to
dt
, so one can assume that new copy is created in the state
\psi_{\text{det}}
.
Therefore, the three possible evolutions for
\psi
are

1.

no copy and deterministic:
\ket{\psi}\mapsto\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}}
with probability
p_{\text{n}}^{\text{det}}=p_{\text{n}}p_{\text{det}}^{\prime}=1-p_{J}-\braket{\Gamma_{L}-\Gamma}_{\psi}\,dt+O(dt^{2})
;

2.

no copy and jump:
\ket{\psi}\mapsto\ket{\psi_{j}}
with probability
p_{\text{n}}^{j}=p_{\text{n}}p_{j}=p_{j}+O(dt^{2})
;

3.

copy and deterministic: the original copy evolves deterministically and a new copy is created in the ensemble

{{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\mapsto 2\,{\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}}\bra{\psi_{\text{det}}}}}

(14)

with probability
p_{\text{c}}^{\text{det}}=p_{\text{c}}p_{\text{det}}^{\prime}=\braket{\Gamma_{L}-\Gamma}_{\psi}\,dt+O(dt^{2})
.

In
2
, we show that such unraveling scheme matches on average the TNP ME (
1
).

p_{T}<1

Our method does not depend on the MCWF, but it can be applied to any PDP stochastic unraveling scheme.
To stress this feature, in

So far, we have assumed that all jump probabilities
p_{j}
are positive.
However, our unraveling method for TNP MEs can be equipped with reverse jumps
54
;
53
.
The probabilities for deterministic evolution and for the creation or destruction of a copy are left unchanged and computed using the (possibly negative) probabilities
p_{j}
.
The jump, instead, are modified allowing for reverse jumps: if a given realization in the state
\psi
gives a negative probability
p_{j}(\psi)<0
, then the reverse jump
\ket{\psi}\mapsto\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
can happen whenever
\ket{\psi}\propto L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
.
The probability of such reverse jump reads

p_{j}^{\text{rev}}=-p_{j}(\psi)\frac{N_{\psi}}{N_{\psi^{\prime}}}\,dt,

(19)

where
N_{\psi}
and
N_{\psi^{\prime}}
are the number of realization in the states
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
respectively.
The rest of the process is left unchanged.
The proof that the unravelings agree with the ME on average is presented in

Examples.

As a first example of our method, we consider a special case of tilted Lindbladian, in which the TNP ME reads
56

\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{\zeta}=\mathcal{L}[\rho_{\zeta}]+\zeta\,\mathcal{J}[\rho_{\zeta}],

(20)

where
\mathcal{L}
is a TP Lindbladian

\mathcal{L}[\rho]=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma(\bar{n}+1)a\rho a^{\dagger}+\gamma\bar{n}a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}\{\Gamma_{L},\rho\},

(21)

with
a^{\dagger}
,
a
bosonic creation and annihilation operators,
\zeta
,
\gamma
,
\bar{n}
positive real numbers and Hamiltonian

H=\frac{\Omega}{2}\left(ae^{2i\phi}+a^{\dagger}e^{-2i\phi}\right),

(22)

where
\phi
and
\Omega
are real parameters, while
\mathcal{J}[\rho]=\gamma(\bar{n}+1)a\rho a^{\dagger}
.
MEs of this form are typically used to describe the photon counting statistics
6
.
In particular, the trace of
\rho_{\zeta}
allows to compute the factorial moments of the probability distribution
P(n,t)
of observing
n
photons at time
t
as

\mu_{k}=\frac{d^{k}}{d\zeta^{k}}{\operatorname{tr}}\left[\rho_{\zeta}\right]\Big|_{\zeta=0}.

(23)

In principle, one can directly apply the derivative to the estimate of
{\operatorname{tr}}\rho_{\zeta}
obtained by unraveling the TNP ME (
20
) for values of
\zeta
around 0, but the obtained results are too noisy, since the derivative increases the fluctuations due to the creation or destruction of extra copies.
An alternative approach is that of defining
\tau_{k}\coloneqq d^{k}\rho_{\zeta}/d\zeta^{k}|_{\zeta=0}
, which is obtaned by stochastic average according to the TNP ME
51
;
66

\frac{d}{dt}\tau_{k}=\mathcal{L}[\tau_{k}]+k\,\mathcal{J}[\tau_{k-1}]

(24)

and the moments can be obtained as
\mu_{k}={\operatorname{tr}}[\tau_{k}]
.
This system of MEs can be simulated iteratively, with
\tau_{0}=\rho
and using the solution of the
k
-th operator
\tau_{k-1}
in the ME for
\tau_{k}
.
Notice that this does not impact drastically the numerical requirements, since at each time only the trajectories for
\tau_{k}
need to be stored, as well as the average
\tau_{k-1}
.
Typically only the first few moments are of interest, which are sufficient for example to determine whether the distribution is Poissonian or not
29
.
The resulting MEs are inhomogeneous due to the term
k\,\mathcal{J}[\tau_{k-1}]
, which does not depend on
\tau_{k}
.
By using its spectral decomposition
k\,\mathcal{J}[\tau_{k-1}]=\sum_{i}\eta_{i}^{k}{\ket{\xi_{i}^{k}}\bra{\xi_{i}^{k}}}
, our method can be extended to deal with it by adding an extra event, corresponding to the creations of copies in the eigenstates
\ket{\xi_{i}^{k}}
of the inhomogeneous term, with rate equal to the corresponding eigenvalue
\eta_{i}^{k}
, which are positive since
\mathcal{J}
is CP.
Such extra copies and the rate of creation do not depend on the state
\psi
of the realization.
The initial value for
k\geq 1
is
\tau_{k}(0)=0
, and thus initially only the inhomogeneous term is relevant for the unravelings.

we present the first four moments obtained by unraveling the TNP ME (

As a second example we show that our method allows us to extend stochastic unravelings also to dynamics in the Heisenberg picture.
For the sake of the example, we fix

\begin{split}\frac{dX}{dt}=&{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{t}[X]=i\epsilon(t)[\sigma_{x},X]+\gamma_{-}(t)\left(\sigma_{+}X\sigma_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-},X\}\right)\\
&+\gamma_{+}(t)\left(\sigma_{-}X\sigma_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{-}\sigma_{+},X\}\right),\end{split}

(25)

where
\epsilon(t)
and
\gamma_{\pm}(t)
are time-dependent functions.
We consider the strongly-driven regime
\left\lvert\epsilon(t)\right\rvert\gg\gamma_{\pm}(t)>0
.
Notice that divisibility in the two pictures are independent
61
, and it’s possible to have
\epsilon(t)
and
\gamma_{\pm}(t)
in such a way that the resulting dynamics is CP divisible in the Heisenberg picture (
\gamma_{\pm}(t)\geq 0
) but P indivisible in the Schrödinger picture.
In particular, in the strongly-driven regime, the dynamics is not Schrödinger P nor CP-divisible since the initial time, thus making the reverse jumps fail.
See
2
for further details.
This happens because of the driving
H=\epsilon(t)\sigma_{x}
, which breaks the commutativity of
{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{t}
at different times
61
.
Because of divisibility, the unravelings in the Heisenberg picture do not require reverse jumps, while if one unraveled the same dynamics in the Schrödinger picture they would be necessary, thus making the simulations less efficient.
Instead, the method we introduced allow us to easily unravel also this dynamics in the Heisenberg picture.

To do so, we first note that Heisenberg-picture master equations act on arbitrary operators which don’t need to be positive nor self-adjoint, and therefore cannot be written as an average as in Eq. (
3
).
This issue is easily tackled in a similar manner as done in
59
for the extension of unravelings to initially correlated system and environment: an arbitrary operator
X
can first be separated in an Hermitian and anti-Hermitian part
X=X_{h}+iX_{a}
,
X_{h,a}^{\dagger}=X_{h,a}
, each of which can be written as the difference between two positive operators
X_{h,a}=X_{h,a}^{+}-X_{h,a}^{-}
, where each
X_{h,a}^{\pm}
is positive and therefore proportional to a density matrix
X_{h,a}^{\pm}=\mu_{h,a}^{\pm}\rho_{h,a}^{\pm}
.
This way of separating
X
into the sum of positive operators is preserved by the evolution, since it preserves positivity and Hermiticity.
Therefore, each
\rho_{h,a}^{\pm}
is unraveled separately and
X(t)
is obtained as the weighted sum of the different terms

X(t)=\mu_{h}^{+}\rho_{h}^{+}(t)-\mu_{h}^{-}\rho_{h}^{-}(t)+i\mu_{a}^{+}\rho_{a}^{+}(t)-i\mu_{a}^{-}\rho_{a}^{-}(t).

(26)

The unravelings for Eq. (
25
) are performed using the RO technique and they are shown in Fig.
3
, showing that our method can be used to unravel dynamics in the Heisenberg picture, for which the trace is not preserved.
The use of the RO instead of the MCWF allows us to only have three states in the effective ensemble used to reconstruct the density matrix: the eigenstates
\ket{\pm}
of the driving
\sigma_{x}
(and targets of all jumps), and the deterministically evolving state
\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}}
conditioned to no jumps happening.
This drastically improves the efficiency of the simulations.

Conclusions.

We have introduced a general framework to perform stochastic unravelings for TNP MEs that preserve positivity and Hermiticity. Our method significantly broadens the scope of unravelings beyond the standard Lindblad ME in the Schrödinger picture, enabling simulations of a wide variety of physically relevant dynamics.
This includes, for instance, MEs in the Heisenberg picture, dynamics interpolating between non-Hermitian and Lindblad regimes, and MEs used to extract photon counting statistics.
While we developed the method within the MCWF and RO formalism, it can be easily applied to other piecewise-deterministic schemes, even in the presence of reverse jumps, thus providing a versatile tool for simulating fully general open quantum systems dynamics.

Appendix A
Proof of TNP unravelings

For the case
p_{T}<1
, the proof that the unravelings agree on average with the TNP ME (
1
) follows in a straightforward way.
Given a realization in the state
\psi
, it average evolution is given by

\begin{split}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\mapsto&\sum_{j}p_{j}\frac{L_{j}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}L_{j}^{\dagger}}{\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}}\,dt+p_{\text{det}}{{\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}}\bra{\psi_{\text{det}}}}}+p_{\text{d}}\cdot 0\\
=&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}{L_{j}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}L_{j}^{\dagger}}\,dt+({\mathbbm{1}}-iK\,dt){\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}({\mathbbm{1}}+iK^{\dagger}\,dt)\\
=&{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}+\mathcal{L}[{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}]\,dt.\end{split}

(1)

Similarly, for
p_{T}>1
the average evolution reads

{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\mapsto\sum_{j}p_{j}\frac{L_{j}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}L_{j}^{\dagger}}{\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}}\,dt+(p_{\text{n}}^{\text{det}}+p_{\text{c}}^{\text{det}}){{\ket{\psi_{\text{det}}}\bra{\psi_{\text{det}}}}}+p_{\text{c}}^{\text{det}}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}.

(2)

By using
p_{\text{n}}^{\text{det}}+p_{\text{c}}^{\text{det}}=p_{\text{det}}^{\prime}=1-\braket{\Gamma_{L}}_{\psi}\,dt
and expanding the normalization factor of the deterministic term
1/\left\lVert({\mathbbm{1}}-iK\,dt)\ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}
to the first order in
dt
, then it is straightforward to show that

{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\mapsto{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}+\mathcal{L}[{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}]\,dt

(3)

also in the case in which the trace increases
p_{T}>1
.

The correct behavior of the trace follows in a straightforward way from the linearity of the trace and the fact that each realization evolves on average as prescribed by the TNP ME.

Appendix B
Proof of the reverse jumps

The jump rates
\gamma_{j}
can be decomposed into a positive and negative part
\gamma_{j}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\left\lvert\gamma_{j}\right\rvert\pm\gamma_{j})
.
The presence of negative rates does not alter the deterministic nor the creation/disappearance of copies, therefore it is sufficient to show that the jump process reproduces on average the jump term of the ME (
1
).
The average jump process reads
9

{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\mapsto dt\,\sum_{j}\gamma^{+}_{j}L_{j}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}L_{j}^{\dagger}-dt\,\sum_{j}\int d\psi^{\prime}\,\gamma_{j}^{-}\frac{N_{\psi^{\prime}}}{N_{\psi}}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\ \delta\left(\ket{\psi}-\frac{L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}}{\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\rVert}\right)\,\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\rVert^{2}.

(4)

The first term of r.h.s. is the jump term of the ME for positive rates.
In order to show the agreement on average with the ME, one needs to compute the average jump evolution of the unnormalized state

\rho=\int d\psi\,\frac{N_{\psi}}{N}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}},

(5)

where, unlike for the TP case,
N_{\psi}/N
is not a probability distribution since it is not normalized to
1
.
The average jump evolution then reads

\rho\mapsto\sum_{j}\gamma^{+}_{j}L_{j}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-dt\,\sum_{j}\int d\psi\int d\psi^{\prime}\,\gamma_{j}^{-}\frac{N_{\psi^{\prime}}}{N}{\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}}\ \delta\left(\ket{\psi}-\frac{L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}}{\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\rVert}\right)\,\left\lVert L_{j}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\rVert^{2}.

(6)

For the second term, the integral in
\psi
is readily evaluated thanks to the Dirac delta, and therefore the average evolution due only to the jumps is

\begin{split}\rho\mapsto\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}L_{j}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(7)

Therefore, since deterministic evolution, creation and destriction of a copy are not modified by the presence of negative rates, it follows that the unravelings with NMQJ match with the TNP ME also when reverse jumps are taken into account.

Divisibility of the dynamics of Eq. (
25
)

We proceed to show that the solution of the TNP ME (
25
) gives a dynamical map which is CP-divisible in the Heisenberg picture and P- and CP-indivisible in the Schrödinger picture.

CP-divisibility in the Heisenberg picture follows trivially from the positivity of the rates
\gamma_{\pm}(t)
of the ME.
Let
\Lambda^{*}_{t}
be the solution of the ME (
25
), then the dynamics in the Schrödinger picture is described by its adjoint
\Lambda_{t}
, which is a CPTP map.
(C)P divisibility in the Schrödinger picture is equivalent to the (complete) positivity of the map
\Lambda_{t+dt,t}=\Lambda_{t+dt}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}
for all times
t
.
Complete positivity can be evaluated by checking the positivity of the corresponding Choi state

J_{t}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}\Lambda_{t+dt,t}\big[\ket{i}\bra{j}\big]\otimes\ket{i}\bra{j}.

(21)

Positivity, on the other hand, is equivalent to
\Lambda_{t+dt,t}
, when considered in its Bloch representation, mapping the Bloch sphere into itself, i.e. every vector on the unit sphere must be mapped to vectors with norm smaller than
1
.
In Fig.
S1
, we show that both these conditions are not satisfied.
In particular, they are violated since
t=0
, thus making most unraveling methods fail, even in the presence of reverse jumps.
